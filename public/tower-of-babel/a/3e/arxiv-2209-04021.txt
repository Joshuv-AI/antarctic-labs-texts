Radiant toric varieties and unipotent group actions Thanks: Supported by the grant RSF-DST 22-41-02019
Ivan Arzhantsev, Alexander Perepechko, Kirill Shakhmatov

Abstract
. We consider complete toric varieties X X such that a maximal unipotent subgroup U U of the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{Aut}(X) acts on X X with an open orbit. It turns out that such varieties can be characterized by several remarkable properties. We study the set of Demazure roots of the corresponding complete fan, describe the structure of a maximal unipotent subgroup U U in Aut ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{Aut}(X) , and find all regular subgroups in U U that act on X X with an open orbit.

Introduction

Let
X
be a complete toric variety with an acting torus
T
. All varieties and actions are defined over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. It is proved in
[
9
, Proposition 11]
, see also
[
7
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
2
, Theorem 4.2.4.1]
, that the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is a linear algebraic group and
T
is a maximal torus in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
. An algebraic subgroup
H
in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is called
regular
if
H
is normalized by the torus
T
.

We say that
X
is
radiant
if a maximal unipotent subgroup
U
of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
acts on
X
with an open orbit. The aim of this paper is to show that radiant toric varieties have important distinguishing features that make this class of varieties attractive for study.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
stand for the additive and the multiplicative group of the ground field
\mathbb{K}
, respectively. The groups
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
are commutative one-dimensional linear algebraic groups, and
\mathbb{G}_{a}
is unipotent. Moreover, any commutative unipotent linear algebraic group is isomorphic to the vector group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}=\mathbb{G}_{a}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{G}_{a}
(
n
times). Given an algebraic action
\mathbb{G}_{a}\times X\to X
(resp.
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X\to X
), we call the image of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
(resp.
\mathbb{G}_{m}
) in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup
(resp.
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-subgroup
) in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
. An
additive action
on a complete algebraic variety
X
is a faithful algebraic action
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}\times X\to X
with an open orbit. Since the group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
is isomorphic to the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
as an algebraic variety, we obtain an open embedding of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
into
X
such that the action of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
by translations extends to an action on
X
. This shows that a complete variety with an additive action is an equivariant completion of the affine space, and vice versa; see
[
14
,
4
]
.

If
X
is toric we say that an additive action
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}\times X\to X
is
normalized
if the image of the group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is a regular subgroup. Let us say that a regular

\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is a
root
subgroup. It is easy to see that a normalized additive action is an additive action given by
n
pairwise commuting root subgroups in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.

It is well known that any complete toric variety
X
corresponds to a complete fan
\Sigma=\Sigma_{X}
in the lattice
N
of one-parameter subgroups of the acting torus
T
. Let

\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
be the rays of the fan
\Sigma
. We say that the fan
\Sigma
is
bilateral
if there is a basis
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
of the lattice
N
such that, up to renumbering,
the rays
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{n}
are generated by the vectors
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
, respectively, and the remaining rays
\rho_{n+1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
lie in the negative orthant with respect to this basis.

The following result is proved in
[
3
, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 1]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a complete toric variety. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

the variety
X
is radiant;

(ii)

the variety
X
admits an additive action;

(iii)

the variety
X
admits a normalized additive action;

(iv)

the fan
\Sigma_{X}
is bilateral.

In particular, radiant toric varieties are precisely toric equivariant completions of affine spaces, cf.
[
4
]
.

One more characterization of radiant projective toric varieties was proposed by Fu and Hwang
[
12
]
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}(V)
be a closed subvariety. For a nonsingular point
x\in X
, a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
X
coming from a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(V)
is said to be of
Euler type
at
x
if
x
is an isolated fixed point of the restricted
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
X
and the induced
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on the tangent space
T_{x}X
is by scalar multiplication. The subvariety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}(V)
is
Euler-symmetric
if for a general point
x\in X
, there exists a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
X
of Euler type at
x
. It is proved in
[
12
, Theorem 3.7(i)]
that every Euler-symmetric variety admits an additive action. If
X
is a projective toric variety, it is shown in
[
17
, Theorem 3]
that
X
admits an additive action if and only if
X
is Euler-symmetric with respect to some embedding into a projective space. Moreover, in this case
X
is Euler-symmetric with respect to any linearly non-degenerate linearly normal embedding into a projective space.

Lattice polytopes that correspond to projective radiant toric varieties are described in
[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
.
Examples of non-projective radiant toric varieties are given in
[
18
]
; see also
Example
4.7
below.

It is shown in
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
that a normalized additive action on a radiant toric variety is unique up to isomorphism. Moreover, it follows from
[
11
, Corollary 4]
that any additive action on a radiant toric variety is isomorphic to a normalized additive action if and only if the maximal unipotent subgroup
U
in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is commutative. In this case
U
is the only candidate up to conjugation for a subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
defining an additive action on
X
.

Let us describe the content of the paper. In Section
1
we discuss basic facts on toric varieties and Cox rings and recall a description of the automorphism group of a complete toric variety due to Demazure. Section
2
is devoted to the study of Demazure roots of a bilateral fan. Unlike classical root systems, the set of Demazure roots of a complete polyhedral fan may seem rather random and unsymmetric. However, a more detailed analysis shows that in the case of a bilateral fan, the set of Demazure roots has many nice properties. The study of such properties was started by Dzhunusov
[
10
,
11
]
. We develop these results and find new features of Demazure roots. With any bilateral fan
\Sigma
we associate its ray matrix
A
. We show that Demazure roots of
\Sigma
admit an explicit description in terms of columns of the matrix
A
. This allows both to study properties of Demazure roots and to construct bilateral fans with prescribed Demazure roots.

In Section
3
, using results on Demazure roots, we give a description of a maximal unipotent subgroup of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
of a radiant toric variety
X
as a semidirect product of some explicitly presented unipotent groups. The next step is to find all regular unipotent subgroups in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
which act on
X
with an open orbit. This is done in Section
4
. We define a
principal unipotent subgroup
of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
as a regular commutative unipotent subgroup that acts on
X
with an open orbit. It is proved that a regular unipotent subgroup
U
in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
acts on
X
with an open orbit if and only if
U
contains a principal unipotent subgroup. We come to consider two types of radiant toric varieties: the ones that admit a faithful action of a non-commutative unipotent group and the ones that do not. Specific properties of radiant toric varieties of each type are studied.

In Section
5
we compute the center of a regular unipotent subgroup
U
acting on
X
with an open orbit. With any such
U
we associate a directed graph
\Gamma
and describe the central series and the derived subgroups of
U
in terms of
\Gamma
. These results allow to find the nilpotency class and the derived length of a regular unipotent subgroup.

It is shown in Section
6
that the open orbit of a non-commutative regular unipotent subgroup has smaller dimension than the subgroup itself. This imposes certain restrictions on equivariant toric completions of unipotent groups. Finally, in Section
7
we describe smooth radiant toric surfaces. It turns out that the Picard number of a smooth non-radiant toric surface is at least 4. We expect further classification results on smooth radiant toric varieties of small dimensions.

The authors thank the referee for a careful reading and helpful comments.

1.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall basic facts on toric varieties. Hereinafter we fix an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. By the word ‘variety’ we mean a separated algebraic variety over
\mathbb{K}
.

1.1.
Toric varieties and Cox rings

Let
T=(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{n}
be an algebraic torus of rank
n
. A normal irreducible variety
X
is called
toric
, if there is a faithful action of
T
on
X
with an open orbit. There is a well-known combinatorial description of toric varieties. We briefly recall it in this subsection and refer to standard textbooks
[
8
,
13
]
on toric varieties for details.

Let
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the lattice of one-parameter subgroups of
T
and
M=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the character lattice of
T
. Denote by
N_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
the associated
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces
N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:M_{\mathbb{Q}}\times N_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Q}
be the pairing of dual vector spaces
N_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

A
cone
in
N
is a convex polyhedral cone in
N_{\mathbb{Q}}
. A cone is called
strongly convex
if it contains no nonzero linear subspace. Recall that the
dual cone

\sigma^{\vee}
to a cone
\sigma
in
N
is defined by

\sigma^{\vee}=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}\ |\ \langle u,v\rangle\geq 0\ \forall v\in\sigma\}.

Given a strongly convex cone
\sigma
in
N
one can consider the finitely generated
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
\mathbb{K}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
graded by the semigroup of lattice points of the cone
\sigma^{\vee}
:

\mathbb{K}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]=\bigoplus_{u\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M}\mathbb{K}\chi^{u},

where
\chi^{u}\cdot\chi^{u^{\prime}}=\chi^{u+u^{\prime}}
. Denote by
X(\sigma)
the corresponding affine variety
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{K}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M])
. It is well-known that given an affine variety
X
there is a bijection between faithful
T
-actions on
X
and effective
M
-gradings on
\mathbb{K}[X]
. The aforementioned
\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
-grading on
\mathbb{K}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
corresponds to a faithful
T
-action on
X(\sigma)
with an open orbit. Therefore,
X(\sigma)
is a toric variety. Moreover, every affine toric variety
X
arises in this way.

A
fan
in
N
is a finite collection
\Sigma
of strongly convex cones in
N
such that for all
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
every face of
\sigma_{1}
is an element of
\Sigma
and
the intersection
\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}
is a face of both
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between toric varieties with an acting torus
T
and fans in
N
. Namely, given a fan
\Sigma
in
N
the corresponding toric variety
X(\Sigma)
is a union of affine charts
X(\sigma),\sigma\in\Sigma
, where any two charts
X(\sigma_{1})
and
X(\sigma_{2})
are glued along their open subset
X(\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2})
. Every toric variety arises in this way.

Denote by
|\Sigma|
the
support
of a fan
\Sigma
in
N
:

|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma.

A fan
\Sigma
in
N
is called
complete
if
|\Sigma|=N_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The corresponding toric variety
X(\Sigma)
is complete if and only if the fan
\Sigma
is complete.

Let us recall the basic ingredients of the Cox construction; see
[
2
, Chapter 1]
for more details. Let
X
be a normal variety with free finitely generated divisor class group
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
and only constant invertible regular functions. Let
\mathrm{WDiv}(X)
be the group of Weil divisors on
X
and
K\subseteq\mathrm{WDiv}(X)
be a subgroup which maps onto
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
isomorphically. The
Cox ring
of the variety
X
is defined as a
K
-graded algebra

R(X)=\bigoplus_{D\in K}H^{0}(X,D),

where
H^{0}(X,D)=\{f\in\mathbb{K}(X)^{\times}\ |\ \mathrm{div}(f)+D\geq 0\}\cup\{0\}
, and where the multiplication on homogeneous components coincides with multiplication in
\mathbb{K}(X)
and extends to
R(X)
by linearity. Up to isomorphism the graded ring
R(X)
does not depend on the choice of a subgroup
K
. This construction may also be generalized to the case when
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
is a finitely generated group with torsion.

Consider a fan
\Sigma
in
N
and the corresponding toric variety
X=X(\Sigma)
. One-dimensional cones in
\Sigma
are called
rays
. We denote by
\Sigma(1)=\{\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}\}
the set of rays in
\Sigma
. Similarly, we denote by
\sigma(1)
the set of rays of a cone
\sigma
. Let us denote by
p_{l}
the generator of the semigroup
\rho_{l}\cap N
. Assume that
X
has only constant invertible regular functions. This is equivalent to the condition
\langle p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=N_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between cones
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
T
-orbits
O(\sigma)
in
X
such that
\dim O(\sigma)=n-\dim\langle\sigma\rangle
. In particular, each ray
\rho_{l}\in\Sigma(1)
corresponds to a prime
T
-invariant Weil divisor
D_{l}=\overline{O(\rho_{l})}
on
X
. The divisor class group
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
of
X
is generated by classes
[D_{1}],\dots,[D_{m}]
. Moreover, there is a short exact sequence

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Cl}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

where the map
M\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}
is given by
u\mapsto(\langle u,p_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle u,p_{m}\rangle)
and the map
\mathbb{Z}^{m}\to\mathrm{Cl}(X)
is given by
(c_{1},\dots,c_{m})\mapsto c_{1}[D_{1}]+\dots+c_{m}[D_{m}]
. In
[
7
]
it is proved that the Cox ring
R(X)
is a
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
-graded polynomial algebra
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
with
\deg(x_{l})=[D_{l}]
for each
1\leq l\leq m
.

One can easily characterize radiant toric varieties in terms of Cox rings. Namely, a complete toric variety
X
is radiant if and only if the group
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
is free and there is a positive integer
n<m
such that we can reorder the variables in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
in such a way that
\deg(x_{n+1}),\ldots,\deg(x_{m})
form a basis in
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
and for
1\leq j\leq n
we have
\deg(x_{j})=\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}a_{kj}\deg(x_{k})
for some non-negative integers
a_{kj}
; cf.
[
11
, Corollary 3]
.

The
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
-grading on
R(X)
gives rise to an action of the quasitorus
G=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{Cl}(X),\mathbb{K}^{\times})
on
\mathbb{K}^{m}=\mathrm{Spec}(R(X))
. Note that if we apply the
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-,\mathbb{K}^{\times})
-functor to the short exact sequence (
1
), we obtain an inclusion
G\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{m}
.

Denote by
Z
the closed subset of
\mathbb{K}^{m}
defined by equations

\prod_{\rho_{l}\not\in\sigma(1)}x_{l}=0\quad\forall\sigma\in\Sigma.

These equations generate the so-called
irrelevant ideal
in
R(X)
; see
[
2
, Section 1.6.3]
for more details. The subset
Z
is invariant under the action of the group
G
, thus there is an action of
G
on the variety
\widehat{X}=\mathbb{K}^{m}\setminus Z
. The variety
X
is isomorphic to the good categorical quotient
\widehat{X}/\!/G
and the isomorphism
X\cong\widehat{X}/\!/G
is compatible with torus:
X\supset T\cong(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{m}/\!/G=(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{m}/G
.

1.2.
Demazure roots and root subgroups

Hereinafter we fix a complete fan
\Sigma
in
N
with rays
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}
and their primitive generators
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}
and let
X=X(\Sigma)
,
R=R(X)=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
. Denote by
\mathfrak{R}_{i}\subseteq M,\ 1\leq i\leq m
the set of vectors
e\in M
such that

\langle e,p_{i}\rangle=-1\quad\text{and}\quad\langle e,p_{s}\rangle\geq 0\ \text{for all}\ s\neq i.

The elements of the set
\mathfrak{R}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}\mathfrak{R}_{i}
are called
Demazure roots
of
\Sigma
; cf.
[
9
, Définition 4]
. The roots in
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{R}\cap-\mathfrak{R}
are called
semisimple
, and the ones in
\mathfrak{U}=\mathfrak{R}\setminus\mathfrak{S}
are called
unipotent
.

Let
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
. Denote by
\partial_{e}
the locally nilpotent derivation on
R
:

\partial_{e}=\left(\prod_{s\neq i}x_{s}^{\langle e,p_{s}\rangle}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}=x^{\theta(e)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},

where the map
\theta:\mathfrak{R}_{i}\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}
is defined by

\theta(e)=\big(\langle e,p_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle e,p_{i-1}\rangle,0,\langle e,p_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle e,p_{m}\rangle\big).

The action of operators
\exp(\alpha\partial_{e}),\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
on
R
gives rise to an action of the group
U_{e}\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}
on
\widehat{X}
that commutes with the action of
G
. This induces a
U_{e}
-action on
\widehat{X}/\!/G=X
so
U_{e}\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
. The subgroups
U_{e},\ e\in\mathfrak{R}
are exactly the root subgroups of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
. Indeed, let
e\in\mathfrak{R}
. It is easy to see that as a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{X})
the group
U_{e}
is normalized by
(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{m}
, so
U_{e}\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is normalized by the acting torus
T
. Conversely, it is known that every
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
which is normalized by
T
has the form
U_{e}
for some
e\in\mathfrak{R}
; see
[
9
, Théorème 3]
and
[
16
, Proposition 3.14]
.

In the case of a radiant toric variety
X
there is a normalized additive action on
X
given by the tuple of pairwise commuting homogeneous locally nilpotent derivations
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
on the ring
R(X)=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
, where

\partial_{j}=x_{n+1}^{a_{n+1j}}\ldots x_{m}^{a_{mj}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}};

see
[
3
, Lemma 3.5]
.

1.3.
The automorphism group of a complete toric variety

In addition to the quotient realization, the Cox ring
R
also contains information about the automorphism group of
X
. Let
\mathrm{Aut}_{g}(R)
be the group of graded automorphisms of the algebra
R
. If
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{g}(R)
, then
\phi
commutes with the action of the group
G
and thus
\phi
induces an automorphism of
X
. In fact, there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(X)\cong\mathrm{Aut}_{g}(R)/G
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is a linear algebraic group generated by the acting torus
T
, the root subgroups
U_{e},e\in\mathfrak{R}
and a finite group; see
[
7
, Section 4]
.

We recall basic facts on the structure of the group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
proved by Demazure
[
9
]
; see also
[
16
, Section 3.4]
. The torus
T
is a maximal torus in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
and
\mathfrak{R}
is a root system of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
with respect to
T
. The unipotent radical
G_{u}
of
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(X)
is generated by the subgroups
U_{e},e\in\mathfrak{U}
, while there exists a connected reductive subgroup
G_{\text{red}}
with
T
as a maximal torus such that
\mathfrak{S}
is the root system for
G_{\text{red}}
and
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(X)=G_{u}\rtimes G_{\text{red}}
. It follows that if we pick a system of positive roots
\mathfrak{S}^{+}
with
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{S}^{+}\sqcup\mathfrak{S}^{-}
, then the subgroup generated by
G_{u}
and all subgroups
U_{e},e\in\mathfrak{S}^{+}
is a maximal unipotent subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.

By
[
5
]
, for any connected (not necessarily linear) algebraic group
F
of dimension
n
there exists a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
2n
such that the connected component of the group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is isomorphic to
F
. At the same time, the class of connected linear algebraic groups that can be realized as the connected component of the group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
of a complete toric variety
X
is quite constrained. In particular, it is proved in
[
9
, Proposition 3.3]
that the semisimple part of such a group is a group of type
A
. One of the aims of this work is to study further specific properties of the group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
in the case of a radiant toric variety
X
.

1.4.
Regular subgroups

Recall that an algebraic subgroup
H\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is called regular if it is normalized by the acting torus
T
. In particular, root subgroups in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
are precisely regular
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups.

Note that a regular unipotent subgroup
U
is defined by the root subgroups which are contained in
U
. Denote by
\mathfrak{R}(U)
the set of all roots
e\in\mathfrak{R}
such that
U_{e}\subseteq U
. We call elements of the set
\mathfrak{R}(U)
the
roots
of the group
U
. Conversely, given a subset
\mathfrak{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}
such that the subgroups
U_{e},e\in\mathfrak{M}
generate a unipotent subgroup, we denote this subgroup by
U(\mathfrak{M})
. Clearly, if
U
is a regular unipotent subgroup, then
U(\mathfrak{R}(U))=U
. If
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{R}(U(\mathfrak{M}))
, then we call such a set of roots
saturated
, see Definition
4.1
.

On the other hand, one might have
\mathfrak{R}(U(\mathfrak{M}))\supsetneq\mathfrak{M}
for a subset
\mathfrak{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}
. This can happen due to the fact that if
e,e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}
and
e+e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}
, then
\langle U_{e},U_{e^{\prime}}\rangle\supset U_{e+e^{\prime}}
.

Let
1\leq i\leq m
,
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
denote

u_{e}(\alpha)=\exp(\alpha\partial_{e}).

Each
u_{e}(\alpha)
is an automorphism of the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
R
, and the action of
U_{e}
on
\mathbb{K}^{m}=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
is given by

u_{e}(\alpha)\cdot P=(u_{e}(\alpha)\cdot x_{1}(P),\dots,u_{e}(\alpha)\cdot x_{m}(P)),

where
P=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m})
is a point in
\mathbb{K}^{m}
and
u_{e}(\alpha)\cdot x_{i}\in R
is considered as a function on
\mathbb{K}^{m}
for each
1\leq i\leq m
. Since

u_{e}(\alpha)x_{i}=x_{i}+\alpha x^{\theta(e)}\text{ and }u_{e}(\alpha)x_{j}=x_{j}\text{ for }1\leq j\neq i\leq m,

we see that
u_{e}(\alpha)
changes only the
i
th coordinate.

Consider another pair
e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
. Denote
d=\langle e,p_{j}\rangle
. Then

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)x_{k}=x_{k}\text{ for all }k\neq i,j.

If
i=j
, then

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)x_{i}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})(x_{i}+\alpha x^{\theta(e)})=x_{i}+\alpha^{\prime}x^{\theta(e^{\prime})}+\alpha x^{\theta(e)}.

If
i\neq j
, then

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)x_{j}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})x_{j}=x_{j}+\alpha^{\prime}x^{\theta(e^{\prime})}\text{ and}

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)x_{i}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})(x_{i}+\alpha x^{\theta(e)})=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})\left(x_{i}+\alpha x_{j}^{d}\frac{x^{\theta(e)}}{x_{j}^{d}}\right)=

=x_{i}+\alpha(u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})x_{j})^{d}\frac{x^{\theta(e)}}{x_{j}^{d}}=x_{i}+\sum_{l=0}^{d}\binom{d}{l}x_{j}^{d-l}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{l}x^{l\theta(e^{\prime})}\frac{x^{\theta(e)}}{x_{j}^{d}}=

=x_{i}+\sum_{l=0}^{d}\binom{d}{l}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{l}\frac{x^{\theta(e)+l\theta(e^{\prime})}}{x_{j}^{l}}.

Example 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan of projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
so that
n=2
,
m=3
and
{p_{1}=(1,0)}
,
{p_{2}=(0,1)}
,
p_{3}=(-1,-1)
. Take the dual basis in
M
and consider two roots
\mathfrak{M}=\{(-1,1),(0,-1)\}
. As
(3\times 3)
-matrices acting on homogeneous coordinates
[z_{0}:z_{1}:z_{2}]
, the elements of the subgroups
U_{(-1,1)}
and
U_{(0,-1)}
are

u_{(-1,1)}(\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\ u_{(0,-1)}(\beta)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&\beta\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

respectively. Clearly, these root subgroups generate a unipotent subgroup

. However, one can check that

u_{(-1,1)}(\alpha)u_{(0,-1)}(1)u_{(-1,1)}(-\alpha)u_{(0,-1)}(-1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\alpha\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

which is an element of the subgroup
U_{(-1,0)}
. It shows that

\mathfrak{R}(U(\mathfrak{M}))=\mathfrak{M}\cup\{(-1,0)\}\supsetneq\mathfrak{M}.

Let
U
be a regular unipotent subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
with
\mathfrak{R}(U)=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
, and
U_{e_{1}},\ldots,U_{e_{k}}
be all root subgroups in

. Then the multiplication map

U_{e_{1}}\times\ldots\times U_{e_{k}}\to U,\quad(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\mapsto u_{1}\ldots u_{k}

is an isomorphism of affine varieties. This fact is proved in

for regular unipotent subgroups of a reductive group, but the proof passes without changes in the case we are interested in.

2.
Demazure roots of bilateral fans

In this section we continue the study of Demazure roots (or simply roots) of bilateral fans started in
[
3
,
10
,
11
]
. Let
N
be a lattice of rank
n
and
\Sigma
be a complete fan in
N
which is bilateral. Recall that this means that we can order the rays
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
of
\Sigma
so that the primitive vectors
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
form a basis of
N
and for any
n+1\leq k\leq m
we have

(2)

p_{k}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{kj}p_{j},\text{ where }a_{kj}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Let
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
be the basis of the lattice
M=\mathrm{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
dual to
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
. One can easily see that every root
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
has the form

e=-q_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}b_{j}q_{j},\text{ where }b_{j}=\langle e,p_{j}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Moreover, we have
-q_{i}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

A root
e\in\mathfrak{R}
is called
basic
if
e=-q_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
. We also call a root of the form
-q_{i}+q_{j}

elementary
, where
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
i\neq j
. All other roots in
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{R}_{i}
are called
special
. Finally, roots in
\bigsqcup_{k=n+1}^{m}\mathfrak{R}_{k}
are called
detached
.

Note that if
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{S}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
, then
e
is either basic or elementary. In
[
10
, Proposition 2]
it is proved that
\mathfrak{R}_{k}\subseteq\{q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\}
for all
n+1\leq k\leq m
. So, the set
\mathfrak{R}
is composed of

•

basic roots in
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{R}_{i}
;

•

elementary roots in
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{R}_{i}
;

•

special roots in
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{R}_{i}
, they are unipotent;

•

detached roots in
\bigsqcup_{k=n+1}^{m}\mathfrak{R}_{k}
, they are semisimple.

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider roots
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
and
e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}
for some
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
i\neq j
such that
\langle e^{\prime},p_{i}\rangle=0
. Denote
d=\langle e,p_{j}\rangle
.

(1)

If
d>0
, then
e+e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
and
[\partial_{e},\partial_{e^{\prime}}]=-d\partial_{e+e^{\prime}}
.

(2)

If
d=0
, then
[\partial_{e},\partial_{e^{\prime}}]=0
.

Proof.

Let us prove that
e+e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
when
d>0
. Clearly, the conditions
\langle e+e^{\prime},p_{i}\rangle=-1
and
\langle e+e^{\prime},p_{l}\rangle\geq 0
are satisfied for
l\neq i,j
. We have
\langle e+e^{\prime},p_{j}\rangle=\langle e,p_{j}\rangle+\langle e^{\prime},p_{j}\rangle=d-1\geq 0
so
e+e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
.

Write
\partial_{e}=fx_{j}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
and
\partial_{e^{\prime}}=h\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
for some monomials
f
and
h
that do not depend on
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
. Then
[\partial_{e},\partial_{e^{\prime}}]=fh[x_{j}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}]
. If
d>0
, then
[\partial_{e},\partial_{e^{\prime}}]=-dfhx_{j}^{d-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}=-d\partial_{e+e^{\prime}}
. Otherwise
d=0
and
[\partial_{e},\partial_{e^{\prime}}]=0
.
∎

Now let us consider the following partial preorder on the set
C=\{1,\dots,n\}
. We say that
i\succcurlyeq j
if
a_{ki}\geq a_{kj}
for all
n+1\leq k\leq m
, where the integers
a_{kj}
are defined in (
2
). Further, we let
i\asymp j
if
a_{ki}=a_{kj}
for all
n+1\leq k\leq m
. The relation
\asymp
is an equivalence relation on
C
and the preorder
\succcurlyeq
induces a partial order on the set of equivalence classes.

One can regard the defined preorder as a preorder on the set of columns of a matrix. Namely, we write the coordinates of the vectors
-p_{n+1},\dots,-p_{m}
as rows of an
(m-n)\times n
matrix:

A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{n+1\,1}&\ldots&a_{n+1\,n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{m1}&\ldots&a_{mn}\end{pmatrix}.

We say that
A
is the
ray matrix
of the bilateral fan
\Sigma
. Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
be the columns of the matrix
A
. Then
i\succcurlyeq j
if and only if
v_{i}\geq v_{j}
(coordinate-wise), and
i\asymp j
if and only if
v_{i}=v_{j}
.

The rows of
A
are nonzero pairwise distinct primitive integer vectors with non-negative entries. Since the fan
\Sigma
is complete, the matrix
A
contains no zero column: indeed, if there is
i\in C
such that
a_{ki}=0
for all
k>n
, then every cone from
\Sigma
is contained in the half-space
\{v\in N_{\mathbb{Q}}\ |\ \langle q_{i},v\rangle\geq 0\}
, a contradiction. Conversely, any matrix
A
satisfying these conditions is the ray matrix of some bilateral fan
\Sigma
.

The ray matrix
A
does not define the bilateral fan
\Sigma
. But it determines the set of rays
\Sigma(1)
and thus the set of Demazure roots
\mathfrak{R}
. In fact, it is convenient to describe the set of Demazure roots in terms of the matrix
A
. Let us collect the corresponding results in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a bilateral fan and
A
be its ray matrix. Then

(1)

a vector
e=-q_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}b_{j}q_{j}
,
b_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
belongs to
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
if and only if
-Ae\geq 0
;

(2)

a vector
e\in M
is a detached root in
\mathfrak{R}_{k}
if and only if
e=q_{i}
, where the column
v_{i}
is a vector of length
(m-n)
with a
1
in the
(k-n)^{\text{th}}
coordinate and
0
’s elsewhere.

Proof.

(1)

The equality
\langle e,p_{i}\rangle=-1
follows from the definition of a dual basis. Since

-Ae=v_{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}b_{j}v_{j},

the condition
-Ae\geq 0
is just the system of conditions
\langle e,p_{k}\rangle\geq 0
,
k>n
written in vector form.

(2)

Let
e=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}q_{j}
. Then
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{k}
for some
k>n
if and only if all
w_{j}=\langle e,p_{j}\rangle
are non-negative and the vector
Ae=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}v_{j}
has one coordinate 1 and all other coordinates non-positive. Since all vectors
v_{j}
are nonzero and have non-negative coordinates, this is the case if and only if all coordinates
w_{j}
but one are
0
’s, one coordinate
w_{i}
equals
1
, and the vector
v_{i}
also has one coordinate equal to
1
and others equal to
0
. Then
e=q_{i}
and
\langle e,p_{k}\rangle=-1
means that
v_{i}
has
1
in the
(k-n)^{\text{th}}
coordinate.

∎

Example 2.3
.

Let
X=\mathbb{F}_{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{1}
is the Hirzebruch surface. Taking a basis
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
in
N
, we let
p_{4}=-p_{1}-p_{2}
,
p_{5}=-p_{1}
and
p_{6}=-p_{3}
, and the (bilateral) fan
\Sigma
of
X
is the product of the fan of
\mathbb{F}_{1}
generated by
p_{1},p_{2},p_{4},p_{5}
and the fan of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
generated by
p_{3},p_{6}
. We have
n=3
and
m=6
. The ray matrix of
\Sigma
is

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Using Proposition
2.2
, one can find that

\mathfrak{R}_{1}=\{-q_{1},\ -q_{1}+q_{2}\},\ \mathfrak{R}_{2}=\{-q_{2}\},\ \mathfrak{R}_{3}=\{-q_{3}\},

\mathfrak{R}_{4}=\{q_{2}\},\ \mathfrak{R}_{5}=\emptyset,\ \mathfrak{R}_{6}=\{q_{3}\}.

Consider a fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
obtained from
\Sigma
by deforming the ray
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cdot p_{5}
into
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cdot p^{\prime}_{5}

p^{\prime}_{5}=-p_{1}-p_{3}

\mathrm{Cone}(S)
generated by a subset
S\subseteq\{p_{1},\dots,p_{6}\}

\mathrm{Cone}(S^{\prime})
, where
S^{\prime}
is obtained from

by substituting
p_{5}
with
p^{\prime}_{5}
. The collection
\Sigma^{\prime}
of such cones

Consider a fan
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}

by gluing its cones
\mathrm{Cone}(p_{3},p_{4},p^{\prime}_{5})

\mathrm{Cone}(p_{3},p^{\prime}_{5})

.

The next observation follows from Lemma
2.1
; it is also proved in
[
11
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
1\leq i\leq n
if
\mathfrak{R}_{i}\neq\{-q_{i}\}
, then
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
contains an elementary root. Moreover, if
\langle e,p_{j}\rangle>0
for some
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
and
1\leq j\leq n
, then
-q_{i}+q_{j}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
.

We proceed with the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Take
i,j\in C
with
i\neq j
. Then

(1)

i\succcurlyeq j
if and only if
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
contains the elementary root
-q_{i}+q_{j}
;

(2)

if
i\asymp j
, then the translation map
\xi:M\to M
given by
\xi(e)=-q_{i}+q_{j}+e
induces a bijection
\mathfrak{R}_{j}\setminus\{-q_{j}+q_{i}\}\to\mathfrak{R}_{i}\setminus\{-q_{i}+q_{j}\}
.

Proof.

(1)

By Proposition
2.2
.(1) the vector
-q_{i}+q_{j}
is a root if and only if
-A(-q_{i}+q_{j})=v_{i}-v_{j}\geq 0
and by definition
i\succcurlyeq j
if and only if
v_{i}\geq v_{j}
.

(2)

The conditions
\langle e,p_{i}\rangle=-1
and
\langle e,p_{s}\rangle\geq 0
for any
s\neq i
mean that every root in
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
has the form
e=-q_{i}+\sum_{s\neq i}b_{s}q_{s}
with

b_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Similarly, every root in
\mathfrak{R}_{j}
has the form
e^{\prime}=-q_{j}+\sum_{s\neq j}b_{s}^{\prime}q_{s}
with
b_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Since we have
p_{k}=-\sum_{s=1}^{n}a_{ks}p_{s}
for
n+1\leq k\leq m
, the remaining conditions on elements in
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
are

\langle e,p_{k}\rangle=a_{ki}-a_{kj}b_{j}-\sum_{s\neq i,j}a_{ks}b_{s}\geq 0.

Similarly, we have

\langle e^{\prime},p_{k}\rangle=a_{kj}-a_{ki}b_{i}^{\prime}-\sum_{s\neq i,j}a_{ks}b_{s}^{\prime}\geq 0.

By assumptions, we know that
a_{ki}=a_{kj}
for all
n+1\leq k\leq m
. So if
b_{j}=b_{i}^{\prime}=0
, the conditions imposed on the sets
\{b_{s}\}
and
\{b_{s}^{\prime}\}
are the same, and it remains to prove that if
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
and
\langle e,p_{j}\rangle>0
, then
e=-q_{i}+q_{j}
. We have

-Ae=v_{i}-\sum_{s\neq i}b_{s}v_{s}=-(b_{j}-1)v_{j}-\sum_{s\neq i,j}b_{s}v_{s}

since
v_{i}=v_{j}
by definition of
\asymp
. We obtain that a non-negative vector
-Ae
equals the sum of non-positive vectors. It follows that
(b_{j}-1)v_{j}=0
and
b_{s}v_{s}=0
for all

s\neq i,j
. But all
v_{s}
are nonzero, so
b_{j}=1
and
b_{s}=0
for all
s\neq i,j
. We obtain
e=-q_{i}+q_{j}
.

∎

The following lemma contains one more property of Demazure roots that will be needed in the next section.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
i\in C
we have
q_{i}\in\mathfrak{R}
if and only if all roots in
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
are semisimple. In particular, the set
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
contains no special root.

Proof.

If the root
-q_{i}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
is semisimple, then
q_{i}\in\mathfrak{R}
.

Conversely, assume that
q_{i}\in\mathfrak{R}
. Then
q_{i}
is detached and the vector
v_{i}
is as in Proposition
2.2
.(2). Let
e=-q_{i}+\sum_{s\neq i}b_{s}q_{s}
be a root in
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
. Then

-Ae=v_{i}-\sum_{s\neq i}b_{s}v_{s}\geq 0.

Since all vectors
v_{s}
are nonzero,
\sum_{s\neq i}b_{s}
equals either
0
or
1
. In the first case all
b_{s}
equal
0
and the root
e=-q_{i}
is semisimple. Otherwise we have
v_{i}=v_{j}
and
e=-q_{i}+q_{j}
for some
1\leq j\neq i\leq n
, so
i\asymp j
. In this case the vector
-e
is a root as well, so the root
e
is semisimple.
∎

Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
be the equivalence classes of the relation
\asymp
. By Proposition
2.5
an elementary root
-q_{i}+q_{j}
is semisimple if and only if
i
and
j
are in the same equivalence class. By renumbering the classes we may assume that
C_{l}\succ C_{s}
implies
l<s
. Furthermore, by renumbering the vectors
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
we may assume that
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
is an (element-wise) increasing sequence of segments of
C
. In other words, we have the following:

(3)

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{the sequence }C_{1},\dots,C_{r}\text{ is }\succcurlyeq\text{-non-increasing \ and}\\
\text{there exist numbers }0=c_{0}<c_{1}<\ldots<c_{r}=n\text{ such that for all }1\leq s\leq r\\
\text{we have }C_{s}=\{c_{s-1}+1,c_{s-1}+2,\dots,c_{s}\}.\end{array}

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that the condition (
3
) holds. If
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
is a unipotent root, then
e=-q_{i}+\sum_{j>i}\langle e,p_{j}\rangle q_{j}
.

Proof.

The case of basic
e
is trivial. Assume that
\langle e,p_{j}\rangle>0
for some
j\in C
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
-q_{i}+q_{j}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
. So
i\succcurlyeq j
by Proposition
2.5
.(1).

If
i\asymp j
, then by Proposition
2.5
.(2) and the fact that
e
is unipotent we have
e+q_{i}-q_{j}\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}
. It implies
\langle e,p_{j}\rangle=0
, a contradiction. We conclude that
i\succ j
and thus
j>i
.
∎

3.
The structure of a maximal unipotent subgroup

Let us fix a radiant toric variety
X=X(\Sigma)
with a bilateral fan
\Sigma
and keep the notation of the previous section. Let us also assume the condition (
3
). We are going to describe the structure of a maximal unipotent subgroup of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.

Given
v\in N
such that
\langle e,v\rangle\neq 0\ \forall e\in\mathfrak{S}
, we denote

\mathfrak{S}^{+}_{v}=\{e\in\mathfrak{S}\ |\ \langle e,v\rangle>0\}\text{ and }\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}=\mathfrak{S}^{+}_{v}\sqcup\mathfrak{U}.

Recall that
U(\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v})
is a maximal (regular) unipotent subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
and
\mathfrak{R}(U(\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}))=\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}
. Since any system of positive roots in
\mathfrak{S}
is
\mathfrak{S}^{+}_{v}
for some
v\in N
,
any maximal regular unipotent subgroup is obtained this way for a suitable
v
. Let us define
U_{\mathrm{max}}=U(\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v})
with
v
such that

(4)

\langle q_{1},v\rangle<\dots<\langle q_{n},v\rangle<0.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that the condition (
3
) is satisfied and
v\in N
is chosen so that (
4
) holds. Then a root
e\in\mathfrak{R}
lies in
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}
if and only if it has the form
e=-q_{i}+\sum_{j>i}\langle e,p_{j}\rangle q_{j}
for some
i\in C
.

Proof.

All unipotent roots lie in
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}
and have the desired form by Lemma
2.7
. A semisimple root is basic, elementary or detached. In the first case it is in
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}
and has the desired form. In the second case
-q_{i}+q_{j}\in\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}
if and only if
j>i
due to (
4
). In the third case it is not in
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}
and is not of the corresponding form.
∎

Hereinafter we fix a vector
v
satisfying the condition (
4
). Denote
\mathfrak{R}^{+}=\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{v}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}=\mathfrak{R}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{R}^{+}
for all
i\in C
. In particular, we have
\mathfrak{R}^{+}=\mathfrak{R}(U_{\mathrm{max}})=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}
.

Remark 3.2
.

By Lemma
2.4
, if for
i\in C
there is a root
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
with
\langle e,p_{j}\rangle>0
for some
j\in C
, then
i\succcurlyeq j
. We conclude that if
i
lies in a
\succcurlyeq
-minimal equivalence class
C_{s}
and
i
is a maximal number in
C_{s}
, then
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}=\{-q_{i}\}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{R}_{n}^{+}=\{-q_{n}\}
.

Given a subset
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
, we denote

U(C^{\prime})=U\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in C^{\prime}}\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}\right).

For example,
U(i)=U(\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+})=\langle U_{e}\ |\ e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}\rangle
. Note that if
C^{\prime}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
, then
U(C^{\prime})
acts trivially on coordinates
x_{j}
in the Cox ring
R(X)
for all
j\neq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
; see Subsection
1.2
. In particular, if
I
and
J
are disjoint subsets of
C
, then
U(I)\cap U(J)=\{\mathrm{id}\}
. Recall that for
e\in\mathfrak{R}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
we denote
u_{e}(\alpha)=\exp(\alpha\partial_{e})\in U_{e}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
,
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}
,
e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}^{+}
, and
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
. Denote
d=\langle e,p_{j}\rangle
.

(1)

The sum
e+e^{\prime}
is a root if and only if
i<j
and
d>0
, in which case
e+e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
.

(2)

If
i<j
, then

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)\cdot x_{i}=\prod_{l=0}^{d}u_{e+le^{\prime}}\left(\binom{d}{l}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{l}\right)\cdot x_{i}.

(3)

If
e+e^{\prime}
is not a root, then
u_{e}(\alpha)
commutes with
u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})
.

Proof.

(1)

Assume that
e+e^{\prime}
is a root. Then
i<j
, since otherwise
i=j
and
\langle e+e^{\prime},p_{i}\rangle=-2
. Thus,
e+e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
by Lemma
3.1
and
d-1=\langle e+e^{\prime},p_{j}\rangle\geq 0
. The converse follows from Lemma
2.1
.

(2)

The action of the product
u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)
on
x_{i}
was computed in Subsection
1.4
:

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)x_{i}=x_{i}+\sum_{l=0}^{d}\binom{d}{l}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{l}\frac{x^{\theta(e)+l\theta(e^{\prime})}}{x_{j}^{l}}.

By the first statement of the lemma,
e+le^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}
for each
0\leq l\leq d
. Note that

\frac{x^{\theta(e)+l\theta(e^{\prime})}}{x_{j}^{l}}=x^{\theta(e+l\theta(e^{\prime}))}.

Therefore,

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)x_{i}=x_{i}+\sum_{l=0}^{d}\binom{d}{l}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{l}x^{\theta(e+l\theta(e^{\prime}))}=\prod_{l=0}^{d}u_{e+le^{\prime}}\left(\binom{d}{l}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{l}\right)\cdot x_{i}.

(3)

An automorphism
u\in U_{\mathrm{max}}
, arising from an automorphism of the Cox ring
R(X)
, is defined by its action on the coordinate functions
x_{s}
,
1\leq s\leq m
of
R(X)
. Therefore, if
u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{\mathrm{max}}
, then
u_{1}=u_{2}
if and only if

u_{1}x_{s}=u_{2}x_{s}\text{ for all }1\leq s\leq m.

Denote
u_{1}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)
and
u_{2}=u_{e}(\alpha)u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})
. By the computations in Subsection
1.4
, we know

u_{1}x_{k}=x_{k}=u_{2}x_{k}\text{ for }k\neq i,j,

so we only have to consider the cases
s=i,j
.

If
i=j
, then
u_{e}(\alpha)
clearly commutes with
u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})
. Let
i\neq j
. Since
e+e^{\prime}
is not a root,
d=0
by the first statement of the lemma. So by the second statement of the lemma

u_{1}x_{i}=x_{i}+\alpha x^{\theta(e)}=u_{e}(\alpha)x_{i}=u_{2}x_{i}.

The monomial
\theta(e^{\prime})
does not contain the variable
x_{i}
since
i<j
, so

u_{2}x_{j}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})x_{j}=u_{1}x_{j}.

Therefore,
u_{1}=u_{2}
.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

(1)

The subgroup
U(i)
is commutative of dimension
|\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}|
.

(2)

The subgroup
U(i)
is normalized by the subgroup
U(j)
for
j>i
. Moreover, if
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}
,
e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}^{+}
, and
d=\langle e,p_{j}\rangle
, then

u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})^{-1}=\prod_{k=0}^{d}u_{e+ke^{\prime}}\left(\binom{d}{k}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{k}\right)\in U(i)

for all
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Lemma
3.3
. Denote

u_{1}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})^{-1}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)u_{e^{\prime}}(-\alpha^{\prime}),

u_{2}=\prod_{l=0}^{d}u_{e+le^{\prime}}\left(\binom{d}{l}\alpha(\alpha^{\prime})^{l}\right).

Clearly,
u_{2}\in U(i)
, so
u_{2}
fixes
x_{s}
for all
s\neq i
.

As in Lemma
3.3
, it suffices to prove
u_{1}x_{s}=u_{2}x_{s}
for all
1\leq s\leq m
. This equation clearly holds for all
s\neq i,j
. Since the monomial
\theta(e^{\prime})
does not contain the term
x_{i}
, we have

u_{1}x_{j}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e^{\prime}}(-\alpha^{\prime})x_{j}=x_{j}=u_{2}x_{j}.

By Lemma
3.3

u_{1}x_{i}=u_{2}x_{i}
since

u_{1}x_{i}=u_{e^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})u_{e}(\alpha)x_{i}.

Therefore,
u_{1}=u_{2}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Since all elements of the subgroup
U(i)
change only one coordinate
x_{i}
in the spectrum
\mathbb{K}^{m}=\mathrm{Spec}(R(X))
of the Cox ring
R(X)=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
(see Subsection
1.4
), we conclude that for any
1\leq i\leq n
general orbits of the action of the subgroup
U(i)
on
X
are one-dimensional.

Let
U\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
be a regular unipotent subgroup such that
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{R}(U)\subseteq\mathfrak{R}^{+}
. For each
i=1,\dots,n
denote
\mathfrak{M}_{i}=\mathfrak{M}\cap\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

We have

U=\Big(\big(\cdots(U(\mathfrak{M}_{n})\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{n-1}))\dots\big)\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{2})\Big)\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{1}).

In particular,
U_{\mathrm{max}}\cong\Big(\big(\cdots(\mathbb{G}_{a}\ltimes\mathbb{G}_{a}^{|\mathfrak{R}_{n-1}^{+}|})\dots\big)\ltimes\mathbb{G}_{a}^{|\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{+}|}\Big)\ltimes\mathbb{G}_{a}^{|\mathfrak{R}_{1}^{+}|}
.

Proof.

Applying the second claim of Proposition
3.4
to all pairs
(1,j)
,
j>1
we obtain
U=U(\mathfrak{M}_{2}\cup\ldots\cup\mathfrak{M}_{n})\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{1})
. Similarly,
U(\mathfrak{M}_{2}\cup\ldots\cup\mathfrak{M}_{n})=U(\mathfrak{M}_{3},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{n})\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{2})
, and so on. At the end we obtain

U=\Big(\big(\cdots(U(\mathfrak{M}_{n})\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{n-1}))\dots\big)\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{2})\Big)\ltimes U(\mathfrak{M}_{1}),

which proves the assertion. If
U=U_{\mathrm{max}}
, then
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{R}^{+}
and
U(\mathfrak{M}_{i})=U(i)=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{|\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}|}
by the first assertion of Proposition
3.4
. Moreover,
|\mathfrak{R}_{n}^{+}|=1
by Remark
3.2
.
∎

Now let us fix positive integers
k>l
and consider the subgroup
U_{k,l}
of the group
U_{k}
of all unitriangular
(k\times k)
-matrices such that

(\beta_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{k}\in U_{k,l}\iff\beta_{ij}=0\text{ for all }j>i>l.

In other words, the group
U_{k,l}
consists of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{matrix}1&&&\\
&\ddots&\text{\Huge*}&\\
&&\ddots&\\
\text{\huge 0}&&&1\end{matrix}&\text{\Huge*}\\
\hline\cr\text{\huge 0}&\begin{matrix}1&&&\\
&\ddots&&\text{\huge 0}\\
&&\ddots&\\
\text{\huge 0}&&&1\end{matrix}\end{array}\right)

In particular, the subgroup
U_{k,k-1}
coincides with the group
U_{k}
. Note also that the group
U_{k,l}
is commutative if and only if
l=1
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
C_{s}=\{c_{s-1}+1,c_{s-1}+2,\dots,c_{s}\}
be an equivalence class in
C
. Denote
c=c_{s-1}+1
. Then

U(C_{s})\cong U_{k,l}

with
k=|\mathfrak{R}_{c}^{+}|+1
and
l=|C_{s}|
.

Proof.

Denote by
E
the identity
(k\times k)
-matrix. Let
E_{i,j}
be a
(k\times k)
-matrix with a unique nonzero entry on
i^{\text{th}}
row and
j^{\text{th}}
column equal to
1
. For each
i\leq l
the matrices
E+\sum_{j>i}\beta_{ij}E_{i,j}
form a subgroup
H_{i}
in
U_{k,l}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k-i}
:

\left(E+\sum_{j>i}\beta_{ij}E_{i,j}\right)\cdot\left(E+\sum_{j>i}\beta^{\prime}_{ij}E_{i,j}\right)=E+\sum_{j>i}(\beta_{ij}+\beta^{\prime}_{ij})E_{i,j}.

Further, for
l\geq i^{\prime}>i
we have

\left(E+\sum_{j>i^{\prime}}\beta_{i^{\prime}j}E_{i^{\prime},j}\right)\cdot\left(E+\sum_{j>i}\beta_{ij}E_{i,j}\right)=E+\sum_{j>i^{\prime}}\beta_{i^{\prime}j}E_{i^{\prime},j}+\sum_{j>i}\beta_{ij}E_{i,j},

so every element of
U_{k,l}
is uniquely decomposed as the product
h_{l}h_{l-1}\cdots h_{1}
, where
h_{i}\in H_{i}
for all
i
. Finally, for
1\leq i<j\leq k
and
i<i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq k
write

(E-\beta^{\prime}E_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})(E-\beta E_{i,j})(E-\beta^{\prime}E_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})^{-1}=(E-\beta^{\prime}E_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})(E-\beta E_{i,j})(E+\beta^{\prime}E_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})=

=E-\beta E_{i,j}-\delta_{ji^{\prime}}\beta\beta^{\prime}E_{i,j^{\prime}}.

In particular,
U_{k,l}=\Big(\big(\cdots(H_{l}\ltimes H_{l-1})\dots\big)\ltimes H_{2}\Big)\ltimes H_{1}
.

By Corollary
3.6
the group
U(C_{s})
is a semidirect product of the subgroups
U(c)
,
U(c+1)
,
U(c+2),\dots
with the action on each next normalized subgroup as in Proposition
3.4
. Note that if
i\in C_{s}
, then
|\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}|=|\mathfrak{R}_{c}^{+}|-(i-c)=k-(i-c+1)
by Proposition
2.5
.(2) and Lemma
3.1
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{T}
the set of roots in
\mathfrak{R}^{+}
, which are not elementary semisimple. Let us enumerate the roots
e\in\mathfrak{R}_{c}^{+}\cap\mathfrak{T}
by numbers
j_{e}
,
l+1\leq j_{e}\leq k
. By Proposition
2.5
.(2), for every
i\in C_{s}
the map
e\mapsto e-q_{c}+q_{i}
establishes a bijection between roots in
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}\cap\mathfrak{T}
and roots in
\mathfrak{R}_{c}^{+}\cap\mathfrak{T}
. We let
j_{e}
be equal to
j_{e^{\prime}}
, where the root
e^{\prime}
corresponds to
e
under this bijection.

We conclude that a map
\phi:U(C_{s})\to U_{k,l}
defined on generators as

\phi(u_{-q_{i}+q_{j}}(\alpha))=E-\alpha E_{i-c+1,j-c+1}\text{ with }i,j\in C_{s};

\phi(u_{e}(\alpha))=E-\alpha E_{i-c+1,j_{e}}\text{ for other roots }e\in\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}

is the required isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
U_{\mathrm{max}}
be a maximal unipotent subgroup of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
of a radiant toric variety
X
. Assume that the condition (
3
) holds for the equivalence classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
of the relation
\asymp
. Then

U_{\mathrm{max}}\cong\Big(\big(\cdots\big(U_{k_{r},l_{r}}\ltimes U_{k_{r-1},l_{r-1}}\big)\dots\big)\ltimes U_{k_{2},l_{2}}\Big)\ltimes U_{k_{1},l_{1}},

where
k_{s}=|\mathfrak{R}_{c_{s-1}+1}^{+}|+1
and
l_{s}=|C_{s}|
,
1\leq s\leq r
.

Proof.

By the same argument as in the proof of Corollary
3.6
, grouping together subsets
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}
,
i\in C_{s}
for each
1\leq s\leq r
, we obtain a decomposition

U_{\mathrm{max}}=\Big(\big(\cdots\big(U(C_{r})\ltimes U(C_{r-1})\big)\dots\big)\ltimes U(C_{2})\Big)\ltimes U(C_{1}).

By Proposition
3.7
we have
U(C_{s})\cong U_{k_{s},l_{s}}
. This proves the theorem.
∎

Example 3.9
.

Let
X
be the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
so that the number of rays of the fan
\Sigma
equals
n+1
and
p_{n+1}=-(p_{1}+\dots+p_{n})
. We have
1\asymp 2\asymp\dots\asymp n
, so the whole set
C
is an equivalence class. For each
i\in C
the set of roots
\mathfrak{R}_{i}^{+}
consists of vectors

-q_{i},-q_{i}+q_{i+1},-q_{i}+q_{i+2}\dots,-q_{i}+q_{n}.

In particular, we have
|\mathfrak{R}_{1}^{+}|=n
, so
U_{\mathrm{max}}\cong U_{n+1,n}
is the group
U_{n+1}
. Of course, this also follows from the well-known fact
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})=\mathrm{PGL}(n+1)
.

Example 3.10
.

Let
X
be as in Example
2.3
. Then

U_{\mathrm{max}}=(U(3)\ltimes U(2))\ltimes U(1)\cong(\mathbb{G}_{a}\ltimes\mathbb{G}_{a})\ltimes\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}.

In fact, for this particular case
U(1,2)\cong U_{3}
and the subgroup
U(3)
commutes with the subgroup
U(1,2)
, so we have
U_{\mathrm{max}}=U(3)\times U(1,2)\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}\times U_{3}
.

Finishing this section, let us describe a maximal unipotent subgroup
U_{\mathrm{ss}}
of the semisimple part of the group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
as a subgroup of
U_{\mathrm{max}}
. The roots of
U_{\mathrm{ss}}
are the semisimple roots in
\mathfrak{R}^{+}
. For any subset
S\subseteq C
denote by
\mathfrak{R}(S)
the set of roots
\bigsqcup_{i\in S}\mathfrak{R}_{i}
and let
\mathfrak{R}^{+}(S)=\mathfrak{R}(S)\cap\mathfrak{R}^{+}
. For each
1\leq s\leq r
let
U^{s}\subseteq U(C_{s})
be the subgroup such that
\mathfrak{R}(U^{s})=\mathfrak{R}^{+}(C_{s})\cap\mathfrak{S}
. By Theorem
3.8
there is a decomposition

U_{\mathrm{ss}}=\Big(\big(\cdots\big(U^{r}\ltimes U^{r-1}\big)\dots\big)\ltimes U^{2}\Big)\ltimes U^{1}.

Denote by
U_{k,l}^{(1)}
(resp.
U_{k,l}^{(2)}
) the subgroup of
U_{k,l}
consisting of all matrices that differ from the unit matrix at most in the upper left
(l\times l)
-square (resp. at most in the upper right
(l\times(k-l))
-rectangle). Then
U_{k,l}=U_{k,l}^{(1)}\ltimes U_{k,l}^{(2)}
, the subgroup
U_{k,l}^{(1)}
is isomorphic to
U_{l}
, and the subgroup
U_{k,l}^{(2)}
is commutative.

Proposition 3.11
.

In the above notation, the following holds.

(1)

U_{\mathrm{ss}}=U^{1}\times\ldots\times U^{r}
.

(2)

For each
1\leq s\leq r
we have
U^{s}=U(C_{s})\cong U_{k_{s}}
if all roots in
\mathfrak{R}(C_{s})
are semisimple, and
U^{s}\cong U_{k_{s},l_{s}}^{(1)}\cong U_{l_{s}}
otherwise.

Proof.

(1)

Since we already have a semidirect product decomposition of
U_{\mathrm{ss}}
, it suffices to prove that each two subgroups
U^{s}
and
U^{s^{\prime}}
,
s\neq s^{\prime}
commute with each other. It follows from Lemma
3.3
and the facts that a positive semisimple root is either basic or elementary and the sum of two such roots from different components is not a root.

(2)

If all roots in
\mathfrak{R}(C_{s})
are semisimple, then
U^{s}=U(C_{s})\cong U_{k_{s},l_{s}}\cong U_{k_{s}}
, since
l_{s}=k_{s}-1
by definition. Assume that there are unipotent roots in
\mathfrak{R}(C_{s})
. By Lemma
2.6
for each
i\in C_{s}
we have
-q_{i}\in\mathfrak{U}
and due to the construction of the isomorphism
U(C_{s})\cong U_{k_{s},l_{s}}
in the proof of Proposition
3.7
we see that
U^{s}\cong U_{k_{s},l_{s}}^{(1)}
.

∎

We see that each subgroup
U^{s}
is the group of all unitriangular matrices: it is isomorphic either to
U_{k_{s}}
or to
U_{l_{s}}
. This is a manifestation of a famous theorem due to Demazure: the semisimple part of the automorphism group of a complete toric variety is a group of type
A
; see
[
9
, Proposition 3.3]
. The number of simple components in the semisimple part of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
equals the number of classes
C_{s}
such that either
C_{s}
contains at least two elements or the corresponding basic root
-q_{i}
is semisimple.

The center of
U_{\mathrm{max}}
is described in Remark
5.2
below.

4.
Regular unipotent subgroups

In this section we study regular unipotent subgroups of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
of a radiant toric variety
X
that act on
X
with an open orbit. We keep the notation of the previous sections.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a subset of roots
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}
is
saturated
with respect to a subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}
if for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
the sum
a+b
is either contained in
\mathcal{A}
or is not a root.

Recall that
\mathfrak{R}(U_{\mathrm{max}})=\mathfrak{R}^{+}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

A subset
\mathfrak{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}^{+}
equals
\mathfrak{R}(U)
for some regular subgroup
U\subseteq U_{\mathrm{max}}
if and only if for each
i\in C
the subset
\mathfrak{M}_{i}
is saturated with respect to
\bigsqcup_{j>i}\mathfrak{M}_{j}
.

Proof.

Observe that a subset
\mathfrak{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}^{+}
equals
\mathfrak{R}(U)
for some regular subgroup
U\subseteq U_{\mathrm{max}}
if and only if the linear span of derivations
\partial_{e}
,
e\in\mathfrak{M}
is a Lie subalgebra in the tangent algebra of the group
U_{\mathrm{max}}
. By Lemmas
2.1
and
3.1
, this is the case if and only if for all
e\in\mathfrak{M}_{i}
and
e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{M}_{j}
,
j>i
with
\langle e,p_{j}\rangle>0
we have
e+e^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{M}_{i}
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

A
principal unipotent subgroup
of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
of a radiant toric variety
X
is a regular commutative unipotent subgroup that acts on
X
with an open orbit.

The action of a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
on
X
is faithful, so a principal unipotent subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is precisely a subgroup that defines a normalized additive action on
X
. Since every two such actions are equivalent (see
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
), every two principal unipotent subgroups in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
are conjugate. As we have seen in Subsection
1.2
,
U(\{-q_{1},\ldots,-q_{n}\})
is an example of a principal unipotent subgroup.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a radiant toric variety. A regular unipotent subgroup
U
in
U_{\mathrm{max}}
with
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{R}(U)
acts on
X
with an open orbit if and only if
U
contains a principal unipotent subgroup. More precisely, the subgroup
U
acts on
X
with an open orbit if and only if
-q_{1},\ldots,-q_{n}\in\mathfrak{M}
.

Proof.

If a subgroup
U(\mathfrak{M})
acts on
X
with an open orbit, then the set
\mathfrak{M}_{i}
is nonempty for each
i
. Indeed, if such intersection is empty, then any orbit of the group
U(\mathfrak{M})
on the spectrum of the Cox ring
R(X)
is contained in the subvariety, where the coordinates
x_{i},x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{m}
are constant. So, the dimension of any orbit is less than
n
, and it can not project to an open orbit on
X
.

Let us prove by induction that
-q_{1},\ldots,-q_{n}\in\mathfrak{M}
. By Remark
3.2
, we have
-q_{n}\in\mathfrak{M}
. Assuming for some
i<n
that
-q_{i+1},\ldots,-q_{n}\in\mathfrak{M}
, we take an arbitrary element of
\mathfrak{M}_{i}
, say
-q_{i}+\sum_{j>i}b_{j}q_{j}
, and apply consecutively Lemma
2.1
for each
e^{\prime}=-q_{i+1},\ldots,-q_{n}
. Then
-q_{i}\in\mathfrak{M}
, and the direct implication follows.

In Subsection
1.2
we have already seen that locally nilpotent derivations
\partial_{-q_{1}},\dots,\partial_{-q_{n}}
give rise to an additive action on
X
, so the inverse implication is straightforward.
∎

This fact and the results of
[
11
]
motivate the following definition.

Definition 4.5
.

We say that a radiant toric variety
X
is of
Type I
, if a maximal unipotent subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is commutative. All other radiant toric varieties are assigned to
Type II
.

In Type I, the maximal unipotent subgroup in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is a principal unipotent subgroup. By
[
11
, Corollary 4]
, radiant toric varieties of Type I are precisely complete toric varieties that admit a unique additive action. On the other hand, Type II consists exactly of complete toric varieties that admit a faithful action of a non-commutative unipotent group with an open orbit.

Clearly, the direct product
X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{s}
of radiant toric varieties is again a radiant toric variety, and it is of Type I if and only if all factors
X_{i}
are of Type I. Let us give one more result in this direction.

Proposition 4.6
.

Every radiant toric variety of Type I admits a unique decomposition
{X\cong(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{b}\times Y}
, where
b
is a non-negative integer and
Y
is a radiant toric variety of Type I such that
the connected component
\mathrm{Aut}(Y)^{0}
is solvable.

Proof.

Since
X
is of Type I, besides basic roots
-q_{1},\ldots,-q_{n}
the set
\mathfrak{R}
may contain only some detached roots
q_{i}
.

Assume that
q_{1}\in\mathfrak{R}_{n+1}
. By Proposition
2.2
.(2), in the ray matrix
A
we have
a_{n+1\,1}=1
and
a_{k\,1}=0
for all
n+2\leq k\leq m
. If
a_{n+1\,s}>0
for some
2\leq s\leq n
, then by Proposition
2.2
.(1) we obtain
-q_{s}+q_{1}\in\mathfrak{R}_{s}
, a contradiction. So, we have
a_{n+1\,s}=0
for all
2\leq s\leq n
.

It means that
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}\oplus\Sigma_{2}
, where
\Sigma_{1}
is the fan generated by
p_{1}
and
-p_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
is a complementary subfan. It implies that
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X(\Sigma_{2})
. Repeating this procedure, we come to the decomposition

, where

is a principal unipotent subgroup of

. In particular, the group

is solvable.

In any decomposition of this form,

is the number of pairs of semisimple roots of
\Sigma

is the subfan of

obtained as an intersection with the subspace generated by the vectors

such that

contains no semisimple root. This shows that the decomposition is unique.
∎

We sequentially construct a subset

Assume that we constructed subsets

is saturated with respect to
\bigsqcup_{k>j}\mathfrak{M}_{k}

which are saturated with respect to

Assume that we constructed

4.4

such that

and

with an open orbit. All regular unipotent subgroups that act on

with an open orbit are obtained this way for a suitable choice of intermediate subsets

.

5.
Centers and central series

We proceed with a computation of the center of a regular unipotent subgroup. Let
U
be a regular unipotent subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
that acts on
X
with an open orbit. Denote

C(U)=\{i\in C\mid\langle e,p_{i}\rangle\leq 0\ \forall e\in\mathfrak{R}(U)\}.

Proposition 5.1
.

The center
Z(U)
of
U
equals
\prod_{i\in C(U)}U_{-q_{i}}
. In particular,
Z(U)
is contained in a principal unipotent subgroup.

Proof.

Consider the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{u})
of the tangent algebra
\mathfrak{u}=\mathrm{Lie}(U)
.
Let
\delta=\sum_{e\in\mathfrak{R}(U)}\alpha_{e}\partial_{e}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{u})
.
Then by Lemma
2.1
for all
i\in C
we have

0=[\delta,\partial_{-q_{i}}]=\sum_{e:\langle e,p_{i}\rangle>0}-\alpha_{e}\langle e,p_{i}\rangle\partial_{e-q_{i}}.

So,
\alpha_{e}=0
for any non-basic
e
, hence
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{-q_{i}}\partial_{-q_{i}}
. Furthermore, if
-q_{j}+q_{k}\in\mathfrak{R}(U)
for some
j\neq k
, then by Lemma
2.1

0=[\delta,\partial_{-q_{j}+q_{k}}]=[\alpha_{-q_{k}}\partial_{-q_{k}},\partial_{-q_{j}+q_{k}}]=\alpha_{-q_{k}}\langle-q_{j}+q_{k},p_{k}\rangle\partial_{-q_{j}}=\alpha_{-q_{k}}\partial_{-q_{j}}.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\delta=\sum_{i\in C(U)}\alpha_{i}\partial_{-q_{i}}
. Conversely, every
\delta\in\mathfrak{u}
of such form lies in
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{u})
.
We conclude with the fact that
\mathrm{Lie}(Z(U))=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{u})
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If
U=U_{\mathrm{max}}
, then
C(U)
is the set of minimal numbers of classes
C_{s}
that are maximal with respect to the partial preorder
\succcurlyeq
.

In the rest of this section we describe the lower and upper central series of a regular unipotent subgroup
U
in terms of a directed graph on the set of roots of
U
.

Definition 5.3
.

Given a regular unipotent subgroup
U\subseteq U_{\mathrm{max}}
with
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{R}(U)
, we define a directed graph
\Gamma(\mathfrak{M})
with
\mathfrak{M}
as the set of vertices as follows. There is an arrow from a root
a\in\mathfrak{M}
to a root
b\in\mathfrak{M}
if and only if there exists
e\in\mathfrak{M}
such that
b=a+e
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer. We denote by
\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k}
the subset of roots
b
in
\mathfrak{M}
such that there is a path of length
k
in
\Gamma(\mathfrak{M})
ending in
b
. Further, let us denote by
\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k}
the subset of roots
a
in
\mathfrak{M}
such that any path in
\Gamma(\mathfrak{M})
starting in
a
is of length less than
k
.

Remark 5.4
.

If there is an arrow from
a\in\mathfrak{M}_{i}
to
b=a+e\in\mathfrak{M}_{j}
, then
i\geq j
and
[\partial_{a},\partial_{e}]=d\partial_{b}
for some
d\neq 0
by Lemma
2.1
.

We claim that the graph
\Gamma(\mathfrak{M})
is acyclic. Assume to the contrary that there is a cycle
S=(b_{0}=a,b_{1},\dots,b_{l-1},b_{l}=a)
. Then each vertex of this cycle lies in the same
\mathfrak{M}_{i}
. For each
1\leq k\leq l
denote
e_{k}=b_{k}-b_{k-1}
. We have
a=a+\sum_{k=1}^{l}e_{k}
or, equivalently,
\sum_{k=1}^{l}e_{k}=0
. This is a contradiction, since due to Lemma
3.1
the sum of positive roots cannot be zero.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
U
be a regular unipotent subgroup and
l
be the length of the longest path in
\Gamma(\mathfrak{M})
, where
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{R}(U)
. Then the lower (resp. upper) central series of
U
consists of subgroups
U(\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k})
(resp.
U(\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k})
) for
0\leq k\leq l+1
in this order.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. If
k=0
, then
\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k}=\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k}=\emptyset
, so
U(\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k})=U
and
U(\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k})=\{\mathrm{id}\}
are the first subgroups of the corresponding central series. Assume that
U(\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k})
and
U(\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k})
are the
k^{\text{th}}
subgroups of the lower and upper central series, respectively. We use the fact that the tangent algebra of the commutator of two closed connected subgroups is the commutator of their tangent algebras; see
[
19
, Theorem 24.5.11]
. So the tangent algebra
\mathrm{Lie}([U,U(\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k})])
is spanned by all elements
[\partial_{e},\partial_{a}]
, where
e\in\mathfrak{M}
and
a\in\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k}
. By Remark
5.4
, they span the subspace
\langle\partial_{b}\mid b\in\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k+1}\rangle
, hence
[U,U(\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k})]=U(\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow k+1})
.

Similarly, consider
h\in U
such that
ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\in U(\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k})
for all
g\in U
. Then

for some
\partial\in\mathrm{Lie}(U)
and
[\partial,\partial_{e}]\in\mathrm{Lie}(U(\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k}))
for all

. So,
\partial
is a linear combination of elements
\{\partial_{u}\mid u\in\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow k+1}\}
.

Finally,
\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow l}\supsetneq\mathfrak{M}^{\uparrow l+1}=\emptyset
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow l}\subsetneq\mathfrak{M}^{\downarrow l+1}=\mathfrak{M}
.
∎

Corollary 5.6

The nilpotency class of

Corollary 5.8

6.
Toric equivariant completions of unipotent groups

In this section we consider equivariant completions of unipotent groups by toric varieties. The following proposition shows that a complete toric variety
X
can not be realized as an equivariant completion of a non-commutative regular unipotent subgroup.

Proposition 6.1
.

Consider a complete toric variety
X
with an acting torus
T
. Let a regular unipotent subgroup
U
of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
act on
X
with an open orbit
O
so that the action
U\curvearrowright O
has trivial stabilizers. Then the group
U
is commutative.

Proof.

We may assume that
U
is contained in
U_{\mathrm{max}}
. If
U
is not commutative, then by Theorem
4.4

U
contains a principal unipotent subgroup as a proper subgroup. By Lemma
2.4
there is an elementary root
-q_{i}+q_{j}
in
\mathfrak{R}(U)
. Then the one-parameter subgroups
U_{-q_{i}}
and
U_{-q_{i}+q_{j}}
in
U
acting on the spectrum of the Cox ring
R(X)
change only the coordinate
x_{i}
. This shows that generic orbits of
U_{-q_{i}}
and
U_{-q_{i}+q_{j}}
on
X
coincide, and the action
U\curvearrowright O
has non-trivial stabilizers, a contradiction.
∎

The next example demonstrates that the regularity condition is crucial.

Example 6.2
.

For
n\geq 3
we consider the subgroup

U=\left\{\left.\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
a_{1}&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
a_{2}&a_{n}&1&0&\cdots&0\\
a_{3}&0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots\\
a_{n}&0&0&0&&1\\
\end{array}\right)\right|a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{K}\right\}\subset\mathrm{GL}(n+1,\mathbb{K}).

with the action on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
given in homogeneous coordinates by

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot[z_{0}:\ldots:z_{n}]=[z_{0}:z_{1}+a_{1}z_{0}:z_{2}+a_{2}z_{0}+a_{n}z_{1}:z_{3}+a_{3}z_{0}:\ldots:z_{n}+a_{n}z_{0}].

This subgroup is neither commutative nor regular. It acts on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with an open orbit and generic stabilizers are trivial.

The theory of equivariant completions of commutative unipotent groups is nothing but the theory of additive actions. In the toric case this theory is rather well-developed, see
[
3
,
4
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
. It will be interesting to study (toric) equivariant completions of non-commutative unipotent groups. Observations given above may serve as first steps towards this goal.

It is natural here to draw an analogy with the case of flag varieties. Take a simple linear algebraic group
G
and a parabolic subgroup
P
in
G
. The homogeneous space
G/P
is a projective variety called a flag variety of the group
G
. This variety contains an open Bruhat cell
O
, which is an orbit of the unipotent radical
U
of the opposite parabolic subgroup
P^{-}
, and the action of
U
on
O
has trivial stabilizers. So
G/P
is an equivariant completion of
U
. Moreover,
U
may be regarded as a regular unipotent subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(G/P)
since
U
is normalized by a maximal torus in
G
.

At the same time, if
G
is the connected component of the group
\mathrm{Aut}(G/P)
(this is almost always the case), then
G/P
admits an additive action if and only if the group
U
is commutative, and the list of such cases is very short (see
[
1
]
). In particular, it may happen only if the parabolic subgroup
P
is maximal.

It is proved in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
that
G/P
can be realized as an equivariant completion of the unipotent radical
U
in a unique way up to isomorphism, provided
G/P
is not isomorphic to a projective space.

7.
The case of toric surfaces

In this section we illustrate the results obtained above in the case of surfaces. We keep the notation of the previous sections. Let
X
be a radiant toric surface; here
n=2
and so
C=\{1,2\}
.

If 1 and 2 are
\succcurlyeq
-incomparable, then
\mathfrak{R}
consists of two basic roots and
U_{\mathrm{max}}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
is the only unipotent group that acts faithfully with an open orbit on

. Equivalently,

is a radiant toric variety of type I.

Assume now that
1\succcurlyeq 2
. Then

Now let us take a closer look at smooth toric surfaces. Such a surface is given by primitive vectors
p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},p_{m+1}
with
p_{0}=p_{m}
and
p_{1}=p_{m+1}
such that any pair
p_{s},p_{s+1}
forms a basis of the lattice
N=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. In this case
p_{s-1}+p_{s+1}=c_{s}p_{s}
for some integer numbers
c_{s}
,
{1\leq s\leq m}
; see
[
16
, Section 1.7]
or
[
13
, Section 2.5]
. Clearly, the sequence
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
determines a smooth complete toric surface uniquely.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth complete toric surface given by a sequence
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
. Then
X
is radiant if and only if the sequence
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m},c_{1})
contains at least two standing nearby non-positive numbers.

Proof.

A complete toric surface
X
is radiant if and only if for some
s
all the vectors
p_{i}
,
i\neq s,s+1
are contained in the negative orthant with respect to the basis
p_{s},p_{s+1}
. We have

p_{s-1}=c_{s}p_{s}-p_{s+1},\quad p_{s+2}=c_{s+1}p_{s+1}-p_{s},

and all other vectors
p_{i}
lie between
p_{s-1}
and
p_{s+2}
. This implies the claim.
∎

For
m=3
we have only the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
represented by the sequence
(-1,-1,-1)
. The case
m=4
corresponds to Hirzebruch surfaces
\mathbb{F}_{q}
represented by sequences
(0,q,0,-q)
for some non-negative integer
q
. Since any smooth complete toric surface can be obtained either from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by a sequence of blow-ups at
T
-fixed points, any sequence
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
can be obtained either from
(-1,-1,-1)
or from
(0,q,0,-q)
by a series of operations, which add
1
to some elements
c_{s}
and
c_{s+1}
(we assume that
c_{m+1}=c_{1}
) and insert
1
between them; see
[
16
, Section 1.7]
or
[
13
, Section 2.5]
for details.

Proposition
7.1
and the description of sequences
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
given above imply that for
m\leq 5
all smooth complete toric surfaces are radiant. For
m=6
we have the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at three
T
-fixed points corresponding to the sequence
(1,1,1,1,1,1)
, which is not radiant.

It is an interesting problem to study smooth radiant toric varieties in higher dimensions.

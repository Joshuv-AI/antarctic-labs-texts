1.
Introduction

A
weighted oriented graph
or a
digraph
is a graph
D=(V(D),E(D),w)
, where
V(D)
is the vertex set of
D
,
E(D)=\{(x,y)|\mbox{ there is an edge from vertex $x$ to vertex $y$}\}
is the
edge set
of
D
, and
w:V(D)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a map, called weight function. That is, assign a weight
w(x)
to each vertex
x
of
D
. If
D
is a weighted oriented graph, then the underlying simple graph
G
whose

V(G)=V(D)
and
E(G)=\{\{x,y\}|(x,y)\in E(D)\}
. That is,
G
is the simple graph without orientation and weights in
D
. Let
V(D)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and

R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, a polynomial ring in
n
variable
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field. Then the
edge ideal
of
D
is defined as
the ideal

I(D)=(x_{i}x_{j}^{w(x_{j})}~|~(x_{i},x_{j})\in E(D))\subset R.

Studying the edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs is an active area of research at present times. Currently, many authors are studying various algebraic invariants of

I(D)
, namely, Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, depth, projective dimension, etc.
Weighted oriented graphs are important because of their applications in coding theory; see
[
10
,
17
]
. The connection between the algebraic invariants and the combinatorial invariants of a weighted oriented graph is an important problem in combinatorial Commutative Algebra. In particular, describing an algebraic invariant associated to
I(D)
in terms of certain combinatorial information of
D
is a challenging problem. It is well known that
\operatorname{reg}(I(D)^{k})
is eventually a linear function of
k
, see
[
3
,
15
]
. It is very hard to find this linear function and very few is known in the literature.

Gimenez, Martínez-Bernal, Simis and Villarreal gave a combinatorial characterization of Cohen-Macaulay weighted oriented forests, see
[
7
, Theorem 5]
.
In
[
17
]
, Pitones, Reyes and Toledo characterized associated primes and the unmixed property of
I(D)
. Also, see
[
5
]
for a characterization of unmixedness of certain weighted oriented graphs. The projective dimension and regularity of
I(D)
were explicitly computed by Zhu, Xu, Wang, and Tang in
[
20
]
, where
D
is a weighted rooted forest or an oriented cycle by assuming all the weights of the vertices are at least
2
. The invariants of
I(D)^{k}
, where
D
is a weighted oriented gap-free bipartite graph, have explicit combinatorial formulas provided by Zhu, Xu, Wang, and Zhang in
[
21
]
. Also see
[
18
]
. For weighted oriented rooted forests
D
, the regularity and projective dimension of
I(D)^{k}
are computed in
[
19
]
by assuming all the weights of the vertices are at least
2
. For
m
-partite weighted oriented graphs, formulas for those invariants are explicitly given in
[
22
]
. In
[
4
]
, Betti numbers of
I(D)
are explicitly computed for a class of weighted oriented graphs
D
. Also, see
[
16
]
to refer formulas for regularity of a class of weighted oriented graphs whose vertices have weights at least
2
. For a weighted oriented path or a cycle whose edges are oriented in one direction, then its regularity was computed in
[
14
]
. In this work, we proved that if
D
is a weighted oriented Cohen-Macaulay forest with all its leaves are sinks, then we explicitly compute combinatorial formulas for regularity of
I(D)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
and no assumptions on the weights of the vertices. This formula for
\operatorname{reg}(I(D)^{k})
is eventually a linear function of
k
. Our approach does not have any assumptions on the weights.

Now we give a section-wise description of the paper. In section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall definitions and some results which will be used throughout the paper. Also, we set up notation.

Let
D=(V(D),E(D),w)
be a weighted oriented graph and
V(D)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field.
Let
V^{+}(D):=\{x\in V(D):w(x)\geq 2\}
and simple denoted by
V^{+}
. If

, then
x
is called
head
of
e
and
y
is called
tail
of
e
. For a vertex
x\in V(D)
, its
outer neighbourhood
is defined as

N_{D}(x)^{+}:=\{y\in V(D)|(x,y)\in E(D)\}
and its
inner neighbourhood
is defined as
N_{D}(x)^{-}:=\{z\in V(D)|(z,x)\in E(D)\}
and
N_{D}[x]:=N_{D}(x)^{+}\cup N_{D}(x)^{-}\cup\{x\}
,
N_{D}[x]^{+}:=N_{D}(x)^{+}\cup\{x\}
,
N_{D}[x]^{-}:=N_{D}(x)^{-}\cup\{x\}
. A vertex
x\in V(D)
is called a
source
if
N_{D}(x)^{-}=\emptyset
and
x
is called a
sink
if
N_{D}(x)^{+}=\emptyset
. If
x\in V(D)
is a source, then we set
w(x)=1
.
The degree of a vertex
x\in V(D)
, is defined as
d_{D}(x):=|N_{D}(x)|
.
For any
S\subseteq V(D)
, we denote
D\setminus S
, the induced subgraph on the vertex set
V(D)\setminus S
.
For a monomial
f\in R
, define its
support
as
\operatorname{supp}(f):=\{x_{i}:x_{i}\mid f\}
. We denote
\mathcal{G}(I)=
minimal set of generators of
I
. For a monomial ideal
I
, its support is defined as
\operatorname{supp}(I):=\displaystyle\cup_{f\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\operatorname{supp}(f)
.

Recall that two edges
\{x,y\},\{z,u\}\in E(G)
of a simple graph
G
are said to be
adjacent
if there exists an edge between them, that is there exists an edge
\{a,b\}\in E(G)
, where
a\in\{x,y\}
and
b\in\{z,u\}
. Two edges in a weighted oriented graph are said to be adjacent if they are adjacent in its underlying graph. We denote
[r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}
, for any positive integer
r
. Recall that a weighted oriented graph is said to be a
forest (or tree)
if its underlying graph is a forest(or tree). We say that a weighted oriented graph
D
is Cohen-Macaulay if
R/I(D)
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring.

Definition 2.1
.

For any homogeneous ideal
I
in
R
, define the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (or merely regularity) as

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{max}\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{max}\{j+i\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(I)_{j}\neq 0\},

where
\beta_{i,j}(I)
is the
(i,j)^{th}
graded Betti number of
I
and
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(I)_{j}
denotes the
j^{th}
graded component of the
i^{th}
local cohomology module.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
I
is a monomial ideal such that
I=J+K
, where
\mathcal{G}(I)=\mathcal{G}(J)\cup\mathcal{G}(K)
. Then
I=J+K
is Betti splitting if

\displaystyle\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(J)+\beta_{i,j}(J)+\beta_{i-1,j}(J\cap K)\mbox{ for all }i,j\geq 0,

where
\beta_{i-1,j}(J\cap K)=0
if
i=0.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
m=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
be a monomial in
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then the polarization of
m
is defined to be the squarefree monomial

\mathcal{P}(m)=x_{11}x_{12}\ldots x_{1a_{1}}x_{21}\ldots x_{2a_{2}}\ldots x_{na_{n}}

in the polynomial ring
k[x_{ij}:1\leq j\leq a_{i},1\leq i\leq n].
If
I\subset R
is a monomial ideal with
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{u}\}
and
m_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{ij}}
where each
a_{ij}\geq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,m.
Then polarization of
I
, denoted by
I^{\mathcal{P}}
, is defined as:

I^{\mathcal{P}}=(\mathcal{P}(m_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{P}(m_{u})),

which is a squarefree monomial ideal in the polynomial ring
R^{\mathcal{P}}=k[x_{j1},x_{j2},\ldots,x_{ja_{j}}\mid j=1,\ldots,n]
where
a_{j}=\operatorname{max}\{a_{ij}\mid i=1,\ldots,m\}
for any
1\leq j\leq n.

The following result conveys that polarization preserves some homological invariants.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
13
, Corollary 1.6.3]

Let
I\subset R
be a monomial ideal and
I^{\mathcal{P}}\subset R^{\mathcal{P}}
its polarization. Then,

(1)

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(I^{\mathcal{P}})
for all
i
and
j
,

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(I^{\mathcal{P}})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
19
, Lemma 2.7]

Let
S_{1}=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
and
S_{2}=k[x_{m+1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be two polynomial rings,
I\subset S_{1}
and
J\subset S_{2}
be two non zero homogeneous ideals. Then,

(1)

\operatorname{reg}(I+J)=\operatorname{reg}(I)+\operatorname{reg}(J)-1,

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(IJ)=\operatorname{reg}(I)+\operatorname{reg}(J).

Lemma 2.6
.

[
9
, Corollary 12]

Let
H
be a simple hypergraph and
H^{\prime}
its induced subhypergraph. Then,

\operatorname{reg}(H^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{reg}(H).

The following result follows from Lemma
2.6
and
2.4
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
D^{\prime}
be an induced weighted oriented subgraph of
D
. Then,

\operatorname{reg}(I(D^{\prime})^{k})\leq\operatorname{reg}(I(D)^{k}).

Recall the following lemma, which we frequently use in many proofs.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
11
, Lemma 1.2]
[Regularity lemma]
Let
0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence finitely generated graded
R
-modules. Then

(1)

\operatorname{reg}(B)\leq\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{reg}(A),\operatorname{reg}(C)\}
.

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(A)\leq\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{reg}(B),\operatorname{reg}(C)+1\}
.

(3)

\operatorname{reg}(C)\leq\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{reg}(A)-1,\operatorname{reg}(B)\}
.

(4)

If
\operatorname{reg}(A)>\operatorname{reg}(C)+1
, then
\operatorname{reg}(B)=\operatorname{reg}(A)
.

(5)

If
\operatorname{reg}(C)\geq\operatorname{reg}(A)
, then
\operatorname{reg}(B)=\operatorname{reg}(C)
.

(6)

If
\operatorname{reg}(A)>\operatorname{reg}(B)
, then
\operatorname{reg}(C)=\operatorname{reg}(A)-1
.

(7)

If
\operatorname{reg}(B)>\operatorname{reg}(A)
, then
\operatorname{reg}(C)=\operatorname{reg}(B)
.

The following theorem is a combinatorial characterization of Cohen-Macaulay forests.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
7
, Theorem 5]

Let
D
be a weighted oriented forest without isolated vertices, and let
G
be its underlying forest.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

D
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

I(D)
is unmixed; that is, all its associated primes have the same height.

(3)

G
has a perfect matching
\{x_{1},y_{1}\},\{x_{2},y_{2}\},\ldots,\{x_{r},y_{r}\}
so that

d_{G}(y_{i})=1
for
i=1,2,\ldots,r
and
w(x_{i})=1
if
(x_{i},y_{i})\in E(D)
.

3.
Castelnuovo Mumford Regularity of Cohen-Macaulay weighted oriented forests

In this section we prove a formula for
\operatorname{reg}(I(D))
. To show this, we prove some technical lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D
be a weighted oriented unmixed forest. Let
\{\{x_{1},y_{1}\},\ldots,\{x_{r},y_{r}\}\}
be a perfect matching in the underlying graph
G
of
D
. Suppose
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
are sinks. Then for any edge
(x_{t},y_{t})
and
z\in V(D)
such that
N_{D}(x_{t})=\{y_{t},z\}
and for all
k\geq 1
,

(1)

\displaystyle I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}\bigcap x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D)^{k-1}=zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}+x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}
,

(2)

\displaystyle zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}\bigcap x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}=zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}
.

Proof.

(1) Note that
x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}
and
x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}zI(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}
are contained in
I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}\bigcap x_{t}y_{t}^{w}(y_{t})I(D)^{k-1},
because
x_{t}z\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus y_{t})).
This implies that

zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}+x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}\subseteq I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}\bigcap x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D)^{k-1}.

Now we prove the reverse inclusion.
Let
f\in I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}\bigcap x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D)^{k-1}
. Then
f=x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}f_{1},
where
f_{1}\in R
and
m\mid f,
for some
m\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k})
. Now We will see the proof in three further cases.

Case 1:
Suppose
x_{t}\mid m
. Then
z\mid m
because
zx_{t}\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus y_{t})).
In fact, we have
zx_{t}\mid m
. Then
m=zx_{t}m_{1}
, for some
m_{1}\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1})
. Since
y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}\mid f
,
m\mid f
and
y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}\nmid m
, we get that
m\mid x_{t}f_{1}
. This implies that

zx_{t}m_{1}\mid x_{t}f_{1}
. This gives that
m_{1}\mid f_{1}
. Thus
f_{1}\in I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}
. This implies that
f\in zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}.

Case 2:
Suppose
x_{t}\nmid m
and
z\mid m.
Then we have
m\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k})
. Also,
zu^{w(u)}\mid m
because
zu^{w(u)}\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})),
where
u\in N_{D}(z)\setminus x_{t}
. Then
m=zu^{w(u)}m_{2},
for some
m_{2}\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k-1}).
On the other hand, we have
f=x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}f_{1},
where
f_{1}\in R
. Thus we have
m\mid f_{1}
. Therefore,

f=x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}f_{1}=x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}zu^{w(u)}m_{2}g_{2},

for some
g_{2}\in R
. Thus we have

f\in zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}u^{w(u)}I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k-1}\subseteq zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k-1}\subseteq zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}.

Case 3:
Suppose
x_{t}\nmid m
and
z\nmid m.
Then
m\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}).
Since
f=x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}f_{1},
we get that
m\mid f_{1}
because
m\mid f
and
m\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}).
Therefore,

f=x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}f_{1}=x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}mg_{3},

for some
g_{3}\in R.
This implies that
f\in x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}.

(2) Note that
y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}x_{t}zI(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}\subseteq y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}x_{t}zI(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}\bigcap x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}.
Now, we will prove the other inclusion.
Let
f\in y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}x_{t}zI(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1}\bigcap x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}.
Then
f=y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}x_{t}zf_{1}
, for some
f_{1}\in R
and
x_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}m_{1}\mid f
, for some
m_{1}\in\mathcal{G}(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k})
. This implies that
m_{1}\mid f_{1}.
This gives that
f=y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}x_{t}zm_{1}g,
for some
g\in R.
Thus
f\in zx_{t}y_{t}^{w(y_{t})}I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}
, as required.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D
be a weighted oriented unmixed forest. Let
\{\{x_{1},y_{1}\},\ldots,\{x_{r},y_{r}\}\}
be a perfect matching in the underlying graph
G
of
D
. Suppose
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
are sinks. Then for any edge
(x_{t},y_{t})
and

such that
N_{D}(x_{t})=\{y_{t},z\}

(1)

(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}:zx_{t})=I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1},

(2)

(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k},x_{t})=(I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k},x_{t}),

(3)

((I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}:x_{t}),z)=((I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k}:x_{t}),z)=(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k},z).

Proof.

Note that
x_{t}
is a leaf in
D\setminus y_{t}
and
N_{D\setminus y_{t}}(x_{t})=\{z\}
as
N_{D}(x_{t})=\{y_{t},z\}
. Also, one can write
N_{D\setminus y_{t}}^{-}(x_{t})=\{z\}
and
w(x_{t})=1,w(z)=1
. Thus the proof follows from
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
D
be a weighted oriented unmixed forest. Let
\{\{x_{1},y_{1}\},\ldots,\{x_{r},y_{r}\}\}
be a perfect matching in the underlying graph
G
of
D
. Suppose
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
are sinks. Then

\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}\leq\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1})+2,\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k})+1,\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k})\}.

Proof.

Let us take two exact sequences

(1)

0\rightarrow(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}:x_{t})(-1)\xrightarrow{.x_{t}}I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}\rightarrow(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k},x_{t})\rightarrow 0

(2)

The Lemma

(1),
3.2
(2) and

(2)

(3)

\operatorname{reg}(((I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}:x_{t}),z)=\operatorname{reg}((I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k},z)),

respectively.
Note that we have
\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k},x_{t})=\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k})
and
\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k},z)=\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k})
using Lemma

(1).
Now, we apply regularity Lemma

(1) on exact sequence (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k-1})+1,\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus N_{D}[x_{t}])^{k})\}.

Also, we apply regularity lemma

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{reg}((I(D\setminus y_{t})^{k}):x_{t})+1,\operatorname{reg}((I(D\setminus\{x_{t},y_{t}\})^{k},x_{t}))\}

Lemma 3.5

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
D
be an oriented unmixed forest. Let

where

.

Proof.

Observe that
\Theta(k,D)\leq(k-1)(w+1)+\Theta(1,D)

and from Theorem
3.6

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(I(D)^{k})=\Theta(k,D)\leq(k-1)(w+1)+\Theta(1,D)=(k-1)(w+1)+\operatorname{reg}(I(D)),

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(I(D)^{k})\leq(k-1)(w+1)+\operatorname{reg}(I(D))\mbox{ for any }k\geq 1.

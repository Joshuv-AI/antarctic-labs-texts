Kawamata–Miyaoka-type inequality for Q \mathbb{Q} -Fano varieties with canonical singularities II: Terminal Q \mathbb{Q} -Fano threefolds
Haidong Liu, Jie Liu

Abstract




Abstract.
We prove an optimal Kawamata–Miyaoka-type inequality for terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds with Fano index at least
3
. As an application, any terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X
satisfies the following Kawamata–Miyaoka-type inequality:

c_{1}(X)^{3}<3c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X).

Keywords.
Kawamata–Miyaoka-type inequality,
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds, second Chern class

1. Introduction

A normal projective variety is called
Fano
if its anti-canonical divisor is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and ample. A Fano variety is called
\mathbb Q
-Fano
if it is
\mathbb Q
-factorial and its Picard number is one. According to the minimal model program,
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties with terminal singularities are one of the building blocks of algebraic varieties, and they form a bounded family (see
[
Kaw92
,
KMMT00
,
Bir21
]
).

Thanks to Reid’s orbifold Riemann–Roch formula, see
[
Rei87
]
, terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds have been studied intensively in the past two decades; see
[
ABR02
,
Suz04
,
BS07a
,
BS07b
,
Pro10
,
Pro13
,
CJ16
,
BK22
,
Pro25
,
Pro22
]
and the references therein. In particular, all the possibilities for their numerical types are obtained in the
Graded Ring Database
(
Grdb
for short,
[
BK09
]
).

One of the key ingredients in the computation of possible numerical types of terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds is an effective version of the
Kawamata–Miyaoka-type inequality
(see
[
Kaw92
, Proposition 1]
,
[
Suz04
, Theorem 1.7]
and
[
BK22
, Theorem 2.6]
for more details). In our previous paper
[
LL25
]
, we established such an effective Kawamata–Miyaoka-type inequality for terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties in arbitrary dimension. In particular, using a refined argument and Reid’s orbifold Riemann–Roch formula, we have proved the following inequality in dimension three, which significantly improves the known ones in the literature.

Theorem 1.1

(
cf.

[
LL25
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold. Then we have

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq\frac{25}{8}c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X).

In this paper, we aim to continue in this direction. To give a precise statement, let us recall the definition of Fano indices. In general, let
X
be a singular Fano variety. We can define the Fano index of
X
in two different ways:

\begin{split}q\mathbb Q(X)&\coloneqq\max\{q\mid-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb Q}qA,\;A\in\Cl X\},\\
qW(X)&\coloneqq\max\{q\mid-K_{X}\sim qB,\;B\in\Cl X\}.\end{split}

If we assume in addition that
X
has at worst terminal singularities, then the Weil divisor class group
\Cl(X)
is finitely generated and the numerical equivalence for
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisors coincides with the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalence. This implies that both
q\mathbb Q(X)
and
qW(X)
are positive integers.

The main result of this paper is the following optimal Kawamata–Miyaoka-type inequality for terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds with large
q\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
cf.
Lemma
2.2
, Theorems
3.1
and
5.1
)
.

Let
X
be a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold such that
q\mathbb{Q}(X)\geq 3
. Then we have

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq\frac{121}{41}c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X),

and equality holds if and only if
X
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(1,2,3,5)
.

As an immediate application, combining Theorem
1.2
with
[
LL25
, Theorem 4.4]
yields the following generalisation of Theorem
1.1
, which confirms a recent conjecture of K. Suzuki
[
Suz24
, Conjecture 1]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold. Then we have

c_{1}(X)^{3}<3c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X).

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is as follows. Let
X
be a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold with
q\mathbb{Q}(X)\geq 3
which does not satisfy the inequality in Theorem
1.2
. Using the orbifold Riemann–Roch theorem,
[
LL25
, Corollary 4.3]
and
[
Pro24
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have the following two numerical possibilities for
X
(see Lemma
2.2
):

(1.3.1)

qW(X)=q\mathbb{Q}(X)=4
and
\mathcal{R}_{X}=\{7,13\}
,

(1.3.2)

qW(X)=q\mathbb{Q}(X)=5
and
\mathcal{R}_{X}=\{3,7^{2}\}
,

where
\mathcal{R}_{X}
denotes the
local index basket
of
X
(see Section
2.1
). The main contribution of this paper is to exclude these two cases. To this end, we will introduce two different approaches. In the first one we use the theory of foliations to rule out the second case; it is relatively simple (see Section
3
). The first case is much more difficult; we will follow a second approach developed by Yu. Prokhorov in
[
Pro10
]
by using the Sarkisov link (see Section
5
).

Theorem
1.3
can be directly applied to show that 13559 numerical types in the
Grdb

[
BK09
]
do not actually occur for terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds and the Hilbert series of a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold always lies in the list
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ss}}
; see
[
BK22
, Theorem 1.2]
. On the other hand, if
q\mathbb{Q}(X)\geq 9
, then
[
BS07a
, Section 4]
and
[
Pro10
]
show that there are exactly nine numerical types which can be geometrically realised (№ 23–31 in Table
5
). However, the situation becomes much more complicated for smaller
q\mathbb{Q}
. For instance, by Theorem
1.3
, if
q\mathbb{Q}(X)=8
, there are four possibilities (№ 19–22 in Table
5
) for the numerical type of
X
with corresponding local index baskets as follows:

\{3^{2},5\},\quad\{3,7\},\quad\{5,7\},\quad\{3,5,11\}.

It is known that the first three cases can be geometrically realised by appropriate examples, see
[
BS07a
, Table 1]
, and we will treat the last case in Section
6
(see № 22 in Table
5
and Theorem
6.1
), from which we derive the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold with
q\mathbb{Q}(X)=8
. Then its local index basket
\mathcal{R}_{X}
cannot be
\{3,5,11\}
.

Finally, we remark that Theorem
1.3
cannot be generalised to
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds with canonical singularities as
\mathbb{P}(1,3,7,11)
shows (see
[
BK22
, Section 4.5]
), and our proof of Theorem
1.2
is not completely independent of previously known non-existence results in the literature (see the proof of Lemma
2.2
for more details). Moreover, there are also some non-existence results, which cannot be recovered by Theorem
1.2
(
e.g.
№ 5 and № 8 in
[
Pro13
, Lemma 8.1]
and № 5, № 8 and № 9 in
[
Pro13
, Lemma 9.1]
).

2. Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we work over
\mathbb{C}
, and varieties are always supposed to be irreducible. We will freely use the terminology of
[
KM98
]
for birational geometry, especially the minimal model program (MMP for short).

2.1. Orbifold Riemann–Roch formula

Let
X
be a terminal Fano threefold and
q\coloneqq qW(X)
.
According to Reid
[
Rei87
]
, there is a basket of orbifold points

\mathbf{B}_{X}=\left\{\left(r_{i},b_{i}\right)\mid i=1,\ldots,s;\,0<b_{i}\leq\frac{r_{i}}{2};\,b_{i}\text{ is coprime to }r_{i}\right\}

associated to
X
, where a pair
\left(r_{i},b_{i}\right)
corresponds to an orbifold point
Q_{i}
of type
\frac{1}{r_{i}}\left(1,-1,b_{i}\right)
.
Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{X}
the collection of
r_{i}
(permitting weights) appearing in
\mathbf{B}_{X}
, and simply write it as a set of integers whose weights appear in superscripts, say for example

\mathcal{R}_{X}=\{3,7,7\}=\{3,7^{2}\}.

Note that
r_{X}\coloneqq\lcm(\mathcal{R}_{X})
coincides with the Gorenstein index of
X
.
Let
A
be a
\mathbb Q
-Cartier Weil divisor on
X
such that
-K_{X}\sim qA
. According to
[
Rei87
, Corollary 10.3]
,

(2.1)

\chi(tA)=1+\frac{t(q+t)(q+2t)}{12}A^{3}+\frac{t}{12q}c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X)+\sumop\displaylimits_{Q\in\mathbf{B}_{X}}c_{Q}(tA)

for
t\in\mathbb{Z}
; here if the orbifold point
Q
is of type
\frac{1}{r_{Q}}(1,-1,b_{Q})
and
0\leq i_{Q,t}<r_{Q}
is the integer uniquely determined by
qi_{Q,t}\equiv-t\mod r_{Q}
, then

c_{Q}(tA)=-\frac{i_{Q,t}(r^{2}_{Q}-1)}{12r_{Q}}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{i_{Q,t}-1}\frac{\overline{jb_{Q}}\left(r_{Q}-\overline{jb_{Q}}\right)}{2r_{Q}},

where the symbol
\overline{\bullet}
means the smallest residue mod
r_{Q}
and
\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{-1}\coloneqq 0
.

If
t=-q
, then (
2.1
) and Serre’s duality imply that

(2.2)

1=\frac{1}{24}c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X)+\frac{1}{24}\sumop\displaylimits_{Q\in\mathbf{B}_{X}}\left(r_{Q}-\frac{1}{r_{Q}}\right).

If
q\geq 3
and
-q<t<0
, then
\chi(tA)=0
by the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem. Hence (
2.1
) implies that

(2.3)

1+\frac{t(q+t)(q+2t)}{12}A^{3}+\frac{t}{12q}c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X)+\sumop\displaylimits_{Q\in\mathbf{B}_{X}}c_{Q}(tA)=0

for
-q<t<0
.
In particular, if
q\geq 3
and
t=-1
, then we obtain

(2.4)

A^{3}=\frac{12}{(q-1)(q-2)}\left(1-\frac{c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X)}{12q}+\sumop\displaylimits_{Q\in\mathbf{B}_{X}}c_{Q}(-A)\right).

Moreover, the degree
A^{3}
and the Gorenstein index
r_{X}
have the following relations (see
[
Suz04
, Lemma 1.2]
for example):

(2.5)

\gcd(r_{X},q)=1\quad\text{and}\quad r_{X}\cdot A^{3}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.

2.2. Fano indices

We collect in the following some basic facts about the Fano indices of terminal Fano threefolds. We refer the reader to
[
Suz04
]
,
[
Pro10
, Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3]
and
[
Pro22
, Proposition 3.3]
for more details.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a terminal Fano threefold.

(2.1.1)

We have
qW(X)\mid q\mathbb{Q}(X)
.

(2.1.2)

If
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA
for some Weil divisor
A
, then
q\mid q\mathbb{Q}(X)
.

(2.1.3)

If
-K_{X}\sim qB
for some Weil divisor
B
, then
q\mid qW(X)
.

(2.1.4)

If
q\mathbb{Q}(X)\geq 5
, then
qW(X)=q\mathbb{Q}(X)
.

(2.1.5)

We have
qW(X),q\mathbb{Q}(X)\in\{1,\dots,9,11,13,17,19\}
.

Proof.

The first three statements follow from
[
Pro10
, Lemma 3.2]
.
For the last two statements, without loss of generality we may assume that
q\mathbb{Q}(X)\geq 5
and let
A
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor such that
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}q\mathbb{Q}(X)A
. Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorialization. Then we have
-K_{X^{\prime}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}q\mathbb{Q}(X)A^{\prime}
, where
A^{\prime}=\mu^{*}A
is a nef and big
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor. Running a MMP
g\colon X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow X^{\prime\prime}
yields a Mori fibre space
f\colon X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow Z
. Denote by
A^{\prime\prime}
the strict transform of
A^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime\prime}
. Then we obtain
-K_{X^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}q\mathbb{Q}(X)A^{\prime\prime}
. If
\dim(Z)>0
, then the general fibre
F
of
f
is a non-singular Fano variety such that
-K_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}q\mathbb{Q}(X)A^{\prime\prime}|_{F}
, which is impossible as
\dim F\leq 2
and
A^{\prime\prime}|_{F}
is Cartier. So
Z
is a point and
X^{\prime\prime}
is a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold.

By item (
2.1.2
), we have
q\mathbb{Q}(X)\mid q\mathbb{Q}(X^{\prime\prime})
, and then it follows from
[
Pro10
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
Pro22
, Proposition 3.3]
that we must have

q\mathbb{Q}(X)=qW(X^{\prime\prime})=q\mathbb{Q}(X^{\prime\prime})\in\{5,\dots,9,11,13,17,19\}.

Let
m
be the order of
K_{X^{\prime\prime}}+q\mathbb{Q}(X)A^{\prime\prime}
. By
[
Pro22
, Proposition 3.4]
, we have
(m,q\mathbb{Q}(X^{\prime\prime}))=1
. On the other hand, by the negativity lemma, we also have

g^{*}(K_{X^{\prime\prime}}+q\mathbb{Q}(X)A^{\prime\prime})=K_{X^{\prime}}+q\mathbb{Q}(X)A^{\prime}
.
So we obtain
m(K_{X^{\prime}}+q\mathbb{Q}(X)A^{\prime})\sim 0
and then
m(K_{X}+q\mathbb{Q}(X)A)\sim 0
. Thus it follows from
[
Pro10
, Lemma 3.2(iv)]
that
qW(X)=q\mathbb{Q}(X)
.
∎

3. Case
\boldsymbol{q\mathbb{Q}(X)=5}

The goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold such that
q\mathbb{Q}(X)=5
. Then we have

c_{1}(X)^{3}<\frac{121}{41}c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X).

Assume to the contrary that there exists a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X
such that
q\mathbb{Q}(X)=5
and
b_{X}\geq 121/41
.
Then Lemma
2.2
shows that the numerical type of
X
appears as (
2.2.2
). In particular, the numerical invariants of
X
are as follows (
cf.

[
BK09
, № 41436]
):

\mathbf{B}_{X}=\{(3,1),(7,2),(7,3)\},\quad c_{1}(X)^{3}=\frac{500}{21},\quad c_{2}(X)c_{1}(X)=\frac{160}{21}\quad\textup{and}\quad b_{X}=\frac{25}{8}.

In this case, the tangent sheaf
\scr{T}_{X}
is not semi-stable by the Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality, and it follows from
[
LL25
, Theorem 4.2 and Corollary 4.3]
that the maximal destabilizing subsheaf
\scr{F}
of
\scr{T}_{X}
is of rank two with
c_{1}(\scr{F})\equiv 4A
, where
A
is a generator of
\Cl(X)/\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

3.1. Foliation

Let
X
be a normal variety. A

6.5.2.1.
The subcase where

. Since

, we get

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study a two-phase problem for harmonic measure. In this type of problems one considers two disjoint domains
\Omega^{+},\Omega^{-}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
whose boundaries have non-empty intersection, and whose respective harmonic measures
\omega^{+},\omega^{-}
are usually mutually absolutely continuous in some subset of
\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\partial\Omega^{-}
. The objective is to relate
the analytic properties of the
density
\frac{d\omega^{-}}{d\omega^{+}}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\partial\Omega^{-}}
to the geometric properties of
\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\partial\Omega^{-}
.
For example, the
works
[
AMT1
]
,
[
AMTV
]
show that if
\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
are mutually absolutely continuous in some
subset
E\subset\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\partial\Omega^{-}
, then the measures
\omega^{+}|_{E}
and
\omega^{-}|_{E}
are
n
-rectifiable measures, that is, they are concentrated in an
n
-rectifiable subset of
E
and they are absolutely continuous with respect to the Hausdorff
n
-dimensional measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
.
Recall that a set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
n
-rectifiable if there are Lipschitz maps

f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, such that

(1.1)

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\Big(F\setminus\textstyle\bigcup_{i}f_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\Big)=0.

See
[
KPT
]
for a previous partial related result where
\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
are assumed to be complementary NTA domains (see Section

2.3
for the definition of NTA domain), and

[
AM
]
for a related work involving elliptic measure.

In other works of more quantitative nature, besides assuming that
\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
are complementary NTA domains,
one asks also some quantitative condition about the density
\frac{d\omega^{-}}{d\omega^{+}}
. For example, in

[
KT3
]
the authors prove (among other results) that if
\log\frac{d\omega^{-}}{d\omega^{+}}\in{\operatorname{VMO}}(\omega^{+})
and moreover
\Omega^{+}
is
\delta
-Reifenberg flat for some
\delta>0
small enough, then
\Omega^{+}
is vanishing Reifenberg flat (see again Section
2.3
for the notions of
\delta
-Reifenberg flatness and
vanishing Reifenberg flatness). Later on, Engelstein
[
En
]
showed that if one strengthens the
{\operatorname{VMO}}
condition on
\omega^{+}
by
asking
\log\frac{d\omega^{-}}{d\omega^{+}}\in C^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
(still under
the
\delta
-Reifenberg flat assumption) then the inner unit normal
N
of
\Omega^{+}
belongs to
C^{\alpha}
and
\Omega^{+}
is a
C^{1+\alpha}
domain. On the other hand, in
[
AMT2
]
it is shown that the condition

\omega^{-}\in A_{\infty}(\omega^{+})

is equivalent to the fact that
\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
have joint big pieces of chord-arc subdomains. See Section
2.3
for the precise definition.
See also
[
BH
]
for a related result involving the conditions
\log\frac{d\omega^{+}}{d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}}}\in{\operatorname{VMO}}({\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}})
and
\log\frac{d\omega^{-}}{d{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}}}\in{\operatorname{VMO}}({\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}})
; and see
the papers
[
BET1
]
and
[
BET2
]
for results about the structure of the singular set of the boundary
under the assumption that
\log\frac{d\omega^{-}}{d\omega^{+}}
belongs either to
{\operatorname{VMO}}(\omega^{+})
or to
C^{\alpha}
.

In connection with the precise results that we obtain in this work, the following theorem of Prats and the first author of this paper is particularly relevant.

Theorem A

(
[
PT
]
)
.

Let
\Omega^{+}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a bounded NTA domain and let
\Omega^{-}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{+}}
be an NTA domain as well.
Denote by
\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
the respective harmonic measures with poles
p^{+}\in\Omega^{+}
and
p^{-}\in\Omega^{-}
. Suppose that
\Omega^{+}
is a
\delta
-Reifenberg flat domain, with
\delta>0
small enough.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
are mutually absolutely continuous and
\log\dfrac{d\omega^{-}}{d\omega^{+}}\in{\operatorname{VMO}}(\omega^{+}).

(b)

\Omega^{+}
is vanishing Reifenberg flat,
\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
have joint big pieces of chord-arc subdomains, and

(1.2)

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\sup_{r(B)\leq\rho}\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.71397pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.05457pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.4484pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.27722pt}}\!\int_{B}|N-N_{B}|\,d\omega^{+}=0,

where
N
is the inner unit normal to
\partial\Omega^{+}
and
N_{B}
is the unit normal to the
n
-plane
L_{B}
pointing to
\Omega^{+}
and minimizing

(1.3)

\max\Big\{\sup_{y\in\partial\Omega^{+}\cap B}{\rm dist}(y,L_{B}),\sup_{y\in L_{B}\cap B}{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega^{+})\Big\}.

Notice that the condition (b) above provides a geometric characterization of the analytic condition
\log\dfrac{d\omega^{-}}{d\omega^{+}}\in{\operatorname{VMO}}(\omega^{+})
, under the assumption that
\Omega^{+}
is a
\delta
-Reifenberg flat domain, with
\delta>0
small enough.
Remark that the Reifenberg flatness condition on the domain is necessary in the theorem above. This can be easily seen by taking a suitable smooth truncation of the cone
\Omega^{+}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}<x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}\}
, for which the harmonic measures
\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
with pole at
\infty
coincide.

The main result of the current paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega^{+}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a bounded NTA domain and let
\Omega^{-}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{+}}
be an NTA domain as well.
Denote by
\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
the respective harmonic measures with poles
p^{+}\in\Omega^{+}
and
p^{-}\in\Omega^{-}
. Suppose that
\Omega^{+}
is a
\delta
-Reifenberg flat domain, with
\delta>0
small enough.
Then the conditions (a) and (b) in Theorem A are equivalent to the following:

(c)

\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
have joint big pieces of chord-arc subdomains and (
1.2
) holds.

(d)

\Omega^{+}
is a chord-arc domain and the inner unit normal
N
to
\partial\Omega^{+}
belongs to
{\operatorname{VMO}}({\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}})
.

Recall that a chord-arc domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is an NTA domain with
n
-AD regular boundary (see (
2.1
)).
It is trivial that (b) from Theorem A implies (c). On the other hand,
the fact that the condition (d) above implies (a) from Theorem A is essentially known.
See Section
3.1
for more details.
So the main novelty lies in the fact that (c) implies (d).

Notice that neither the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
nor the conditions (a) and (b) in Theorem A contain the fact the surface measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}}
is locally finite.
So the conclusion that
\Omega^{+}
is chord-arc, and thus with locally finite surface measure
may appear rather surprising at first glance. Indeed, this result contrasts with the fact that, as shown in
[
AMT2
]
, there are two-sided NTA domains
\Omega^{\pm}
with non-
\sigma
-finite surface measure such that
\omega^{-}\in A_{\infty}(\omega^{+})
.

It is worth comparing the results from Theorems A and
1.1
with previous results obtained by Kenig and the second author of this paper in connection with the one-phase problem for harmonic measure in chord-arc domains.
By combining some of the results from

[
KT1
]
,
[
KT2
]
,
[
KT4
]
, one gets the following.

Theorem B
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a bounded chord-arc domain which is
\delta
-Reifenberg flat, with
\delta>0
small enough.
Denote by
\omega
the harmonic measure in
\Omega
with pole
p\in\Omega
and write
\sigma={\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\log\dfrac{d\omega}{d\sigma}\in{\operatorname{VMO}}(\sigma).

(ii)

\Omega
is vanishing Reifenberg flat and the inner normal
N
to
\partial\Omega
belongs to
{\operatorname{VMO}}(\sigma)
.

(iii)

The inner normal
N
to
\partial\Omega
exists
\sigma
-a.e. and it belongs to
{\operatorname{VMO}}(\sigma)
.

Notice the analogies between the conditions (a), (b), (c) from Theorems A and
1.1
with the preceding conditions (i), (ii), (iii).
In fact, in view of these results, it is natural to ask if the conditions (a), (b), (c), (d) from Theorems A and
1.1
are equivalent to the fact that the inner normal
N
to
\partial\Omega^{+}
(which exists
\omega^{+}
-a.e.) belongs to
{\operatorname{VMO}}(\omega^{+})

together with the vanishing Reifenberg flatness of
\Omega_{+}
.
Indeed, we remark that the condition (
1.2
) implies that
N\in{\operatorname{VMO}}(\omega^{+})
.
For the moment, the converse implication is an open problem.

To prove the main implication of Theorem
1.1
, namely that
\Omega^{+}
is a chord-arc domain with
N\in{\operatorname{VMO}}(\sigma)
if (c) holds, we will use a geometric corona decomposition which will allow us to estimate the surface measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}}
. We will split some dyadic lattice of “cubes” from
\partial\Omega^{+}
into trees, so that, in each tree,
\partial\Omega^{+}
is well approximated
by a Lipschitz graph at the location and scales of the cubes from the tree. A key step consists in showing that each tree does not have too many stopping cubes (see (
3.4
) in Lemma
3.3
).

2.
Preliminaries

We denote by
C
or
c
some constants that may depend on the dimension and perhaps other fixed parameters. Their value may change at different occurrences. On the contrary, constants with subscripts, like
C_{0}
, retain their values.
For
a,b\geq 0
, we write
a\lesssim b
if there is
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
. We write
a\approx b
to mean
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
. If we want to emphasize that the implicit constant depends on some parameter
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we write
a\approx_{\eta}b

2.1.
Measures and rectifiability

All measures in this paper are assumed to be Borel measures.
A measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
doubling
if there is some constant
C>0
such that

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\,\mu(B(x,r))\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$.}

The measure
\mu
is called
n
-AD regular
(or
n
-Ahlfors-David regular) if

(2.1)

C^{-1}r^{n}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{n}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and $0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)$.}

Note that
n
-AD regular measures are doubling.
A set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
n
-AD regular if
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}
is
n
-AD regular. In case that
\mu
satisfies the second inequality in (
2.1
), but not necessarily the first one, we say that
\mu
has
n
-polynomial growth. We write “
C
-doubling” or “
C
-n-AD regular” if we wish to mention the constant
C
involved in the definitions.

A set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
n
-rectifiable if there are Lipschitz maps
f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\Big(E\setminus\textstyle\bigcup_{i}f_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\Big)=0.

Analogously, one says that a measure
\mu
is

n
-rectifiable

if there are Lipschitz maps

f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, such that

(2.2)

\mu\Bigl({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\textstyle\bigcup_{i}f_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\Big)=0,

and moreover
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
. An equivalent definition for rectifiability of sets and measures is obtained if we replace Lipschitz images of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by possibly rotated
n
-dimensional graphs of
C^{1}
functions.

A measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
uniformly
n
-rectifiable
(UR) if it is
n
-AD-regular and
there exist constants
\theta,M>0
such that for all
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and all
0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)

there is a Lipschitz mapping
g
from the ball
B_{n}(0,r)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\text{Lip}(g)\leq M
such that

\mu(B(x,r)\cap g(B_{n}(0,r)))\geq\theta r^{n}.

A set
E
is called uniformly
n
-rectifiable if the measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable.
The notion of uniform
n
-rectifiability is a quantitative version of
n
-rectifiability introduced
by David and Semmes (see
[
DS
]
). It is easy to check that uniform
n
-rectifiability implies
n
-rectifiability.

Given two doubling measures
\mu
,
\nu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the same support, we write
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\mu)

if there are constants
\delta,{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
such that,
for any ball
B
centered in
\operatorname{supp}\mu
and any Borel set
E\subset B
, the following holds:

\mu(E)\geq\delta\,\mu(B)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\nu(E)\geq{\varepsilon}\,\nu(B).

2.2.
Harmonic measure

For a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
x\in\Omega
, one can construct the harmonic measure
\omega^{x}_{\Omega}
(see
[
He
, p. 217]
, for example). In fact, for any continuous function
f
, the Perron solution for the boundary function
f
is given by

H_{f}(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(y)\,d\omega_{\Omega}^{x}(y),

Remark that constant functions are continuous and since
H_{1}(x)=1
, for any
x\in\Omega
, we have that
\omega_{\Omega}^{x}(\partial\Omega)=1
, for any
x\in\Omega
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
denote the fundamental solution for the Laplace equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, for
n\geq 2
, so that
\mathcal{E}(x)=c_{n}\,|x|^{1-n}
for
n\geq 2
,
c_{n}>0
.
The Newtonian potential of a measure
\mu\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
is defined by

U\mu(x)={\mathcal{E}}*\mu(x),

and the Newtonian capacity of a compact set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
equals

\operatorname{Cap}(F)=\sup\big\{\mu(F):\mu\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}),\,\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset F,\|U\mu\|_{\infty}\leq 1\big\},

where
M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
is the space of finite Borel measures from
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.

The following result is standard. See
[
To1
, Lemma 2.1]
for example.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
n\geq 2
, let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be open and let
B
be a closed ball centered at
\partial\Omega
. Then

\omega_{\Omega}^{x}(B)\geq c(n)\frac{\operatorname{Cap}(\tfrac{1}{4}B\setminus\Omega)}{r(B)^{n-1}}\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\tfrac{1}{4}B\cap\Omega,

with
c(n)>0
.

A similar result holds in the case
n=1
, involving logarithmic capacity instead of Newtonian capacity.

2.3.
NTA and Reifenberg flat domains

Given
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
C\geq 2
,
we say that
\Omega
satisfies the
C
-Harnack chain condition
if
for every
\rho>0
,
k\geq 1
, and every pair of points

x,y\in\Omega

with
{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega),\,{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\geq\rho
and

|x-y|<2^{k}\rho
, there is a chain of
open balls

B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\subset\Omega
,
m\leq C\,k
,
with
x\in B_{1},\,y\in B_{m},

B_{k}\cap B_{k+1}\neq\varnothing

and
C^{-1}{\rm diam}(B_{k})\leq{\rm dist}(B_{k},\partial\Omega)\leq C\,{\rm diam}(B_{k}).
The chain of balls is called
a
Harnack chain
. Note that if such a chain exists, then any positive harmonic function
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

u(x)\approx u(y),

with the implicit constant depending on
m
and
n
. We say that
\Omega
is a
C
-corkscrew domain
if for every
\xi\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,{\rm diam}(\partial\Omega))
there is a ball of radius
r/C
contained in
B(\xi,r)\cap\Omega
. If
B(x,r/C)\subset B(\xi,r)\cap\Omega
, we call
x
a
corkscrew point
for the ball
B(\xi,r)
. Finally, we say that
\Omega
is
C
-non-tangentially accessible (or
C
-NTA, or just NTA)
if it satisfies the
C
-Harnack chain condition and both
\Omega
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
are
C
-corkscrew domains.
We say that
\Omega
is

C
-chord-arc
if,
additionally,
\partial\Omega
is
C
-
n
-AD-regular. Also,
\Omega
is

two-sided
C
-NTA
if both
\Omega
and
\Omega_{\rm{ext}}:=(\overline{\Omega})^{c}
are
C
-NTA.

NTA domains were introduced by Jerison and Kenig in
[
JK
]
. In that work, the behavior of harmonic measure in this type of domains was studied in detail. Among other results, the authors showed that harmonic measure
is doubling in NTA domains, and its support
coincides with the whole boundary.
They also proved that harmonic measure in these domains satisfies the following important change of pole formula.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
\Omega
be a
C
-NTA domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
B
be a ball centered in
\partial\Omega
.
Let
p_{1},p_{2}\in\Omega
be such that
{\rm dist}(p_{i},B\cap\partial\Omega)\geq c_{0}^{-1}\,r(B)
for
i=1,2
.
Then, for any Borel set
E\subset B\cap\partial\Omega
,

(2.3)

\frac{\omega^{p_{1}}(E)}{\omega^{p_{1}}(B)}\approx\frac{\omega^{p_{2}}(E)}{\omega^{p_{2}}(B)},

with the implicit constant depending only on
n
,
c_{0}
and
C
.

For the proof, see Lemma 4.11 from
[
JK
]
. See also
[
MT
]
for an extension of this result to the so-called uniform domains.

Given two NTA domains
\Omega^{+}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
\Omega^{-}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{+}}
,
we say that
\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
have joint big pieces of chord-arc subdomains if for any ball
B
centered in
\partial\Omega^{+}
with radius at most
{\rm diam}(\partial\Omega^{+})
there are two
C
-chord-arc domains

\Omega_{B}^{s}\subset\Omega^{s}
, with
s=+,-
, such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\partial\Omega_{B}^{+}\cap\partial\Omega_{B}^{-}\cap B)\gtrsim r(B)^{n}
, uniformly on
B
.

Given a set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
r>0
, and
P
an
n
-plane, we set

(2.4)

D_{E}(x,r,P)=r^{-1}\max\left\{\sup_{y\in E\cap B(x,r)}{\rm dist}(y,P),\sup_{y\in P\cap B(x,r)}{\rm dist}(y,E)\right\}.

We also define

(2.5)

D_{E}(x,r)=\inf_{P}D_{E}(x,r,P),

where the infimum is over all
n
-planes
P
. For a given ball
B=B(x,r)
, we will also write

D_{E}(B)
instead of
D_{E}(x,r)
.
Given
\delta,R>0
, the set
E
is
(\delta,R)
-Reifenberg flat
(or just
\delta
-Reifenberg flat) if
D_{E}(x,r)<\delta
for all
x\in E
and
0<r\leq R
, and it is
vanishing Reifenberg flat
if

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\sup_{x\in E}D_{E}(x,r)=0.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be an open set, and let
0<\delta<1/2
. We say that
\Omega

is a
(\delta,R)
-Reifenberg flat domain (or just
\delta
-Reifenberg flat) if it satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

\partial\Omega
is
(\delta,R)
-Reifenberg flat.

(b)

For every
x\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r\leq R
, denote by
P(x,r)
an
n
-plane that minimizes
D_{E}(x,r)
. Then one of the connected components of

B(x,r)\cap\bigl\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:{\rm dist}(x,P(x,r))\geq 2\delta\,r\bigr\}

is contained in
\Omega
and the other is contained in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\Omega
.

If, additionally,
\partial\Omega
is vanishing Reifenberg flat, then
\Omega
is said to be vanishing Reifenberg flat, too.
It is well known that if
\Omega
is a
\delta
-Reifenberg flat domain, with
\delta
small enough,
then it is also an NTA domain (see
[
KT1
]
).

2.4.
The space
{\operatorname{VMO}}

Given a Radon measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
, and
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we write

m_{\mu,A}(f)=\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{A}f\,d\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}f\,d\mu.

Assume
\mu
to be doubling.
We say that
f\in{\operatorname{VMO}}(\mu)
if

(2.6)

p_{0}
and
p_{1}
(to this end notice that
the distance from the segment with end-points
p_{0},p_{1}
to
(\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0})^{c}
is comparable to
\ell(R_{0})
). Thus,

\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})\approx\omega^{p_{1}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})\gtrsim 1.

From (
4.23
), the maximum principle, and the last estimate, we infer

\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}\setminus G(S_{0}))\leq\omega^{+,p_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}\setminus G(S_{0}))\lesssim{\varepsilon}\approx{\varepsilon}\,\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}).

Hence,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}\cap G(S_{0}))

\displaystyle=\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})-\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}\setminus G(S_{0}))

\displaystyle\geq(1-C{\varepsilon})\,\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}).

Next we claim that

(4.25)

\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})\geq\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{p_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}).

Indeed, for
x\in K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}
, we have
\omega^{x}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})=1\geq\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{x}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+})
. For
x\in\partial K_{0}\cap\overline{\Omega^{+}}
,
since
\partial K_{0}\cap\overline{\Omega^{+}}\subset\partial K_{0}\cap\overline{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}
, it follows also that

\omega^{x}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})=0=\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{x}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}).

So our claim follows from the maximum principle, since both
\omega^{x}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})
and

\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{x}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+})
are harmonic functions of
x\in\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}
.

By the maximum principle again, recalling that
G(S_{0})\subset\partial\Omega^{+}\cap\partial U^{+}
, and by (
4.24
) and (
4.25
), we get

\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{p_{0}}(K_{0}\cap G(S_{0}))\geq\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap G(S_{0}))\geq(1-C{\varepsilon})\,\omega^{p_{0}}_{\Omega^{+}\cap K_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial\Omega^{+})\geq(1-C{\varepsilon})\,\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{p_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}).

Equivalently,

\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{p_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}\setminus G(S_{0}))\leq C{\varepsilon}\,\omega_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}^{p_{0}}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}).

Now, since
{\widetilde{U}}^{+}
is a Lipschitz domain, by Dahlberg’s theorem
[
Dah
]

\omega^{p_{0}}_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}
is an
A_{\infty}
weight with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}
, and thus
we have

(4.26)

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\big(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}\setminus G(S_{0})\big)\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{a}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\big(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}\big)\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{a}\,\ell(R_{0})^{n},

for some
a>0
(depending only on the
A_{\infty}
character of
\omega^{p_{0}}_{{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}
with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\partial{\widetilde{U}}^{+}}
).

Our next objective consists of showing that

(4.27)

\sum_{Q\in{\rm Stop}(R_{0})}\ell(Q)^{n}\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\big(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}\setminus G(S_{0})\big).

Clearly, this estimate, together with (
4.26
), implies (
3.4
) for
{\varepsilon}
small enough. To prove (
4.27
), notice that, for each
Q\in{\rm Stop}(R_{0})
,
\frac{1}{4}{\widetilde{B}}_{Q}\cap\Gamma\neq\varnothing
by
Lemma
4.12
(recall that
{\widetilde{B}}_{Q}
is defined in Theorem
2.3
(d)). Hence,

\ell(Q)^{n}\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\tfrac{1}{2}{\widetilde{B}}_{Q}\cap\Gamma).

Observe also that the balls
\frac{1}{2}{\widetilde{B}}_{Q}
, with
Q\in{\rm Stop}(R_{0})
, are disjoint from
G(S_{0})
. Indeed, by definition

G(S_{0})=S_{0}\setminus\bigcup_{P\in{\rm Stop}(S_{0})}P
and so
{\widetilde{B}}_{Q}\cap\partial\Omega^{+}\subset Q\subset\partial\Omega^{+}\setminus G(S_{0})
.
Then we deduce

(4.28)

\sum_{Q\in{\rm Stop}(R_{0})}\ell(Q)^{n}\lesssim\sum_{Q\in{\rm Stop}(R_{0})}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\tfrac{1}{2}{\widetilde{B}}_{Q}\cap\Gamma)\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{R_{0}}\cap\Gamma\setminus G(S_{0})),

where in the last inequality we took into account that the balls
\frac{1}{2}{\widetilde{B}}_{Q}
, with
Q\in{\rm Stop}(R_{0})
, are pairwise disjoint, contained in
B_{R_{0}}
, and disjoint from
G(S_{0})
. Using the fact that
G(S_{0})\subset\Gamma\cap\partial{\widetilde{U}}^{+}
an that
\Gamma
and
\partial U^{+}
are Lipschitz graphs, we derive

(4.29)

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(B_{R_{0}}\cap\Gamma\setminus G(S_{0}))\approx{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Pi(B_{R_{0}}\cap\Gamma\setminus G(S_{0})))\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(K_{0}\cap\partial U^{+}\setminus G(S_{0})).

By combining (
4.28
) and (
4.29
) we get (
4.27
). Finally, just note again that (
4.26
) and (
4.27
) yield (
3.4
)
and then the proof of
Lemma
3.3
is concluded.

\square

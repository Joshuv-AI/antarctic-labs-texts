Energy magnetization and transport in systems with a non-zero Berry curvature in a magnetic field
Archisman Panigrahi, Subroto Mukerjee

Abstract
We demonstrate that the well-known expression for the charge magnetization of a sample with a non-zero Berry curvature can be obtained by demanding that the Einstein relation holds for the electric transport current. We extend this formalism to the transport energy current and show that the energy magnetization must satisfy a particular condition. We provide a physical interpretation of this condition, and relate the energy magnetization to circulating energy currents in Chern insulators due to chiral edge states. We further recover the expression for the energy magnetization with this alternative formalism. We also solve the Boltzmann Transport Equation for the non-equilibrium distribution function in 2D for systems with a non-zero Berry curvature in a magnetic field. This distribution function can be used to obtain the regular Hall response in time-reversal invariant samples with a non-zero Berry curvature, for which there is no anomalous Hall response.

V
Conclusions

The Einstein relation has been shown to hold from certain microscopic theories for the electric, energy, and heat transport current in systems with non-zero Berry curvature
Xiao et al. 2006
;
Xiao and Niu 2020
. In this paper, we employ a complementary approach to first demonstrate that, assuming that the Einstein relation holds (whose validity can be established from thermodynamic arguments
Onsager 1931a
;
Onsager 1931b
, irrespective of the underlying microscopic theory), an expression for the charge magnetization can be obtained in a relatively straightforward manner which agrees with the expression obtained for this quantity previously. We then extend this argument to the transport energy current and the heat current, to obtain a condition that the energy magnetization has to obey. We have used it to obtain an expression for the energy magnetization, which has been previously obtained using other methods. Moreover, we have found a physical interpretation of this condition, in terms of the circulating chiral edge modes in a Chern insulator. We have also solved the Boltzmann transport equation up to linear order in potential and temperature gradients for a two-dimensional system, which can be used to obtain the regular Hall response in systems like bilayer graphene, which possess a non-zero Berry curvature, but display no anomalous Hall Effect due to time reversal invariance.

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\hat{\mathcal{O}}}(\bm{r}_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\Bigg[\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(f+g)\left(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega}\right)\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}

(31)

\displaystyle-\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}_{1}}\cdot\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(f+g)\left(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega}\right)\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{\mathcal{O}},(\bm{\hat{r}}-\bm{r}_{1})\}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\Bigg]

We would work with this leading order expansion of the Delta function. For the current density operator, we need to separately take the three components of
\bm{\hat{j}}=-e\frac{\hat{\bm{p}}}{m}
to be
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
.

Let us define the tensor,

\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}=\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\frac{1}{2}\{-e\frac{\hat{p}_{\nu}}{m},(\bm{\hat{r}}-\bm{r}_{1})_{\mu}\}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}

(32)

where
\mu,\nu
runs from
1,2,\dots d
. It can be shown that
\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}
is completely anti-symmetric (see Appendix
B.1
for proof), a fact stated without proof in
Dong et al. 2020
. Due to anti-symmetry of
\mathcal{M}
, we can further write this as (see Appendix
B.2
and
B.3
for proof),

\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\nu}m_{\alpha},

(33)

where
m_{\alpha}
is the
\alpha
-th component of the orbital magnetic moment
\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}
, defined in Eq. (
6
).

Note that we can rewrite the second term in Eq. (
31
) as,

\displaystyle-\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{r}_{1}}\cdot\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(f+g)\left(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega}\right)\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{\mathcal{O}},(\bm{\hat{r}}-\bm{r}_{1})\}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}

(34)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\alpha}\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(f+g)\left(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega}\right)\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{\mathcal{O}},(\bm{\hat{r}}-\bm{r}_{1})_{\alpha}\}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}},

with sum over
\alpha
implied.

Now, let us calculate the
\mu
-th component of the electric current,
\expectationvalue{\vec{\hat{j}}^e}
. We take
\mathcal{\hat{O}}=-e\frac{\hat{{p}}_{\mu}}{m}
. We denote
[dk]=\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega})
. We get,

\displaystyle-\partial_{\alpha}\int[dk](f+g)\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\mu}

(35)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\alpha}\int[dk](f+g)m_{\nu}\epsilon_{\nu\alpha\mu}

\displaystyle=+\partial_{\alpha}\int[dk](f+g)m_{\nu}\epsilon_{\alpha\nu\mu}

This is the
\mu
-th component of
\bm{\nabla}\times\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(f+g)(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega})\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}
. When the electric field, the magnetic field, the chemical potential gradient and the temperature gradients are constant, we can drop the term
\bm{\nabla}\times\int d\bm{k}g(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega})\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}
, because
g
is already a linear function in the spatially uniform fields
\bm{E}
,
\gradient{\mu}
and
\gradient{T}
.

While calculating electric current using Eq. (
31
) (with
\mathcal{\hat{O}}
taken to be the current operator,
\hat{\bm{j}^{e}}=-e\frac{\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{\mu}}{m}
), the first term, which is the contribution of the center of the wavepacket, can be simplified (up to linear order),

\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(f+g)(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega})\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\hat{\bm{j}^{e}}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}=-e\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left[{g}_{\bm{k}}\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}}{\partial\bm{k}}+{f}_{\bm{k}}\frac{e}{\hbar}(\bm{E}\times\bm{\Omega}(\bm{k}))\right].

(36)

Note that the phase space correction factor
(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega})
gets canceled.
Finally we obtain, up to linear order,

~\expectationvalue{\hat{\vec{j}}^e}=\bm{j}^{e}_{\text{total }}=\sum_{s}\Bigg[-e\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left[{g}_{\bm{k}}\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}}{\partial\bm{k}}+{f}_{\bm{k}}\frac{e}{\hbar}(\bm{E}\times\bm{\Omega}(\bm{k}))\right]+\bm{\nabla}\times\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}f_{\bm{k}}\left(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega}\right)\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}\Bigg].

(37)

This equation has a simple interpretation. The net current is the contribution of the movement of the center the wavepackets, as well as due to the rotation about their individual centers.

Appendix B
Demonstration that
m_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}

GOAL
:
The goal of this appendix is to demonstrate that the tensor
\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}
defined in Eq. (
32
) is related to the components of the orbital magnetic moment
\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}
defined in Eq. (
6
) as,
m_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}
. This result is crucial in obtaining Eq. (
37
).

B.1
We demonstrate that
\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}=\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}\frac{1}{2}\{-e\frac{\hat{p}_{\nu}}{m},(\bm{\hat{r}}-\bm{r}_{1})_{\mu}\}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_1}}
is a totally anti-symmetric tensor

The effective Hamiltonian
Ashcroft and Mermin 1976
acting on
u_{n,\bm{k}}
in its effective Schrodinger equation is,

H(\bm{k})=\left[\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}(\bm{k}-i\bm{\nabla})^{2}+V(\bm{r})\right]=e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}\hat{H}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}},

(38)

with
\hat{H}
being the original Hamiltonian. Let us define

\tilde{\bm{P}}(\bm{k})=\frac{m}{\hbar}\frac{\partial H(\bm{k})}{\partial\bm{k}}=\hbar(\bm{k}-i\bm{\nabla})=e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}\hat{\bm{P}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(39)

We would first prove some preliminary results which we would need later on.

B.1.1
Calculation of
\langle{u_{\bm{k}}}|\tilde{\bm{P}}(\bm{k})|{u_{\bm{k}}}\rangle

The idea of this calculation is based on
Chang and Niu 1996
.

~\begin{aligned} \expectationvalue{\tilde{\vec{P}}(\vec{k})}=\bra{u_{\vec{k}}}\tilde{\bm{P}}(\bm{k})\ket{u_{\vec{k}}}&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\bra{u_{\vec{k}}}\frac{\partial H(\bm{k})}{\partial\bm{k}}\ket{u_{\vec{k}}}\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{u}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{k}}\left(H(\bm{k})\ket{u}\right)-\bra{u}H(\bm{k})\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{k}} u}\right]\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{u}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{k}}\left(\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}\ket{u}\right)-\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}\bra{u}\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{k}} u}\right]\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{u}\left(\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{k}}u}\right)+\bra{u}\left(\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\ket{u}\right)-\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}\bra{u}\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{k}} u}\right]\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\end{aligned}

(40)

which is just the (bare) electron mass times its velocity. This expression is nothing but an application of the Hellmann–Feynman theorem
Griffiths 2004
. Note that the Berry curvature term does not appear here because we took a single Bloch eigenstate, not an wavepacket.

B.1.2
Calculation of
\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{k}} u_{\vec{k}}}\tilde{\bm{P}}(\bm{k})\ket{u_{\vec{k}}}

This calculation is based on
Chang and Niu 1996
.

~\begin{aligned} \bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u_{\vec{k}}}\tilde{P}_{\nu}(\bm{k})\ket{u_{\vec{k}}}&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u_{\vec{k}}}\frac{\partial H(\bm{k})}{\partial k_{\nu}}\ket{u_{\vec{k}}}\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{\nu}}\left(H(\bm{k})\ket{u}\right)-\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}H(\bm{k})\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\nu} u}\right]\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{\nu}}\left(\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}\ket{u}\right)-\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}H(\bm{k})\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\nu} u}\right]\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\left(\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\nu}u}\right)+\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\left(\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}}{\partial k_{\nu}}\ket{u}\right)-\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\nu} u}\right]\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}-H(\bm{k})\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\nu}u}\right]+\frac{m}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}}{\partial k_{\nu}}\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\ket{u}\\
&=\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}-H(\bm{k})\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\nu}u}\right]+i{A}_{\mu}(\bm{k})\expectationvalue{{\tilde{P}}_\nu(\vec{k})}\end{aligned}

(41)

B.1.3
Calculation of
\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\hat{\bm{r}}-\bm{r}_{0})}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu}\hat{\bm{P}}_{\nu}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2}}_{\text{all space}}
(let us denote this quantity as
\mathcal{S}
)

Notation
: We define,

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_1}\ket{\psi_2}_{\text{all space}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\text{all space}}d\bm{r}\psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{2}

(42a)

and,

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_1}\ket{\psi_2}_{\text{cell}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\text{unit cell}}d\bm{r}\psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{2}

(42b)

If
\psi_{1}
,
\psi_{2}
are periodic over unit cells, then

\bra{\psi_1}\ket{\psi_2}_{\text{all space}}=N\bra{\psi_1}\ket{\psi_2}_{\text{cell}},

(43)

where

We use the relation,

\hat{\bm{P}}(e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2}})=e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\tilde{\bm{P}}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2}}.

(44)

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\hat{\bm{r}}-\bm{r}_{0})}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu}\hat{\bm{P}}_{\nu}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}_{\text{all space}}

(45)

\displaystyle=\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k}_{1})\cdot(\hat{\bm{r}}-\bm{r}_{0})}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}_{\text{all space}}

Since
\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2, n}}
for different values of

are eigenstates of
H(\bm{k}_{2})
for fixed value of

\mathcal{I}=\sum_{n}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2, n}}{}_{\text{cell }}{}_{\text{cell}}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2, n}}

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2, n}}{}_{\text{all space }}{}_{\text{all space}}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2, n}}=\mathcal{I}
. We insert this in the expression above.

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k}_{1})\cdot(\hat{\bm{r}}-\bm{r}_{0})}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}_{\text{all space}}

where we used the periodicity of the Bloch wavefunctions to absorb the normalization factor. Now we would manipulate the derivative with respect to
{\bm{k}_{1}}_{\mu}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{N}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k}_{1})\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\right)\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}_{\text{all space}}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{all space}}

(47)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{N}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_1}_\mu}u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k}_{1})\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}{}_{\text{all space }}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{all space}}

We make use of the following two identities respectively, to simplify the first and the second terms in the right hand side of Eq.(

\frac{1}{N}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{all space}}=\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell}}

(48)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2, n}}{}_{\text{all space }}{}_{\text{all space}}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2, n}}=\mathcal{I}.

(49)

Then, from Eq. (
47
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=i\sum_{n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\left({}_{\text{all space}}\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k}_{1})\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}_{\text{all space}}\right)\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

(50)

\displaystyle-i\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_1}_\mu}u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k}_{1})\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{all space}}

When the number of sites in a lattice is very large, the points in reciprocal space are dense, and we can approximate the sum over the real space lattice sites,

\sum_{\bm{R}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\cdot\bm{R}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{V_{c}}\int d\bm{r}e^{i(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\cdot\bm{r}}=\frac{(2\pi)^{d}}{V_{c}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2}),

(51)

where
V_{c}
is the volume of the
d

We can further write
\frac{(2\pi)^{d}}{V_{c}}=V_{\text{BZ}}

{1^{\textit{st}}}
Brillouin zone.

For any periodic function over the unit cells,

\int_{\text{all space}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\cdot\bm{r}}f(\text{periodic})=\left(\sum_{\bm{R}\in\text{ lattice sites}}e^{i(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\cdot\bm{R}}\right)\left(\int_{\text{unit cell}}f(\text{periodic})\right)=\left[V_{\text{BZ}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\right]\left(\int_{\text{unit cell}}f(\text{periodic})\right).

(52)

Now we choose units such that

for the simplicity of the subsequent calculations (anyway it can always be absorbed in the normalization). From Eq. (
50

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{n^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\left(\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}_{\text{cell}}\right)\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

(53)

\displaystyle-i\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_1}_\mu}u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell}}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{n^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\left(\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\right)\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}_{\text{cell}}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

\displaystyle+i\sum_{n^{\prime}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_1}_\mu} u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}_{\text{cell}}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

\displaystyle-i\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_1}_\mu}u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell}}

After summing over
n^{\prime}
in the second term of the final line in Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{n^{\prime}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\right]\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}_{\text{cell}}\bra{u_{\vec{k}_2,n'}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

(54)

\displaystyle=i\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\right]\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

Therefore,

\boxed{\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\hat{\bm{r}}-\bm{r}_{0})}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu}\hat{\bm{P}}_{\nu}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})}\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2}}_{\text{all space}}=i\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\right]\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}}

(55)

B.1.4
To show that
\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_0}}\{(\hat{\bm{r}}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_0}}
is anti-symmetric in
\mu
,
\nu
for a Bloch wavepacket
\psi_{\bm{k},\bm{r}_{0}}
, which implies
\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}
is totally anti-symmetric

It is mentioned in Ref.
Dong et al. 2020

\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_0}}\frac{1}{2}\{(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_0}}

Chang and Niu 1996
.
First we calculate the quantity without the anticommutator, and denote is as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_0}}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0})_{\mu}\hat{P}_{\nu}\ket{\psi_{\vec{k},\vec{r}_0}}

(56)

\displaystyle=\int\int

\displaystyle d\bm{k}_{1}d\bm{k}_{2}w(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})w(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k})e^{-i\bm{A}(\bm{k})\cdot(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})}e^{i\bm{A}(\bm{k})\cdot(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k})}

Now we substitute the boxed result (Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=\int\int

\displaystyle d\bm{k}_{1}d\bm{k}_{2}w(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})w(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k})e^{-i\bm{A}(\bm{k})\cdot(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})}e^{i\bm{A}(\bm{k})\cdot(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k})}

(57)

\displaystyle\times i\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\right]\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

After integrating by parts to shift the derivative from the delta function to the other quantities,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\int d\bm{k}_{1}d\bm{k}_{2}w(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})w(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k}_{0})e^{-i\bm{A}(\bm{k})\cdot(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})}e^{i\bm{A}(\bm{k})\cdot(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k})}

(58)

\displaystyle\times i\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_{1}}_{\mu}}\delta(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2})\right]\bra{ u_{\vec{k}_1,n}}\tilde{\bm{P}}_{\nu}(\bm{k}_{2})\ket{u_{\vec{k}_2,n}}{}_{\text{cell }}

\displaystyle=-i\int\int d\bm{k}_{1}d\bm{k}_{2}w(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})w(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k})e^{-i\bm{A}(\bm{k}_{0})\cdot(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{0})}e^{i\bm{A}(\bm{k})\cdot(\bm{k}_{2}-\bm{k})}

Integrating over the delta function, and using Eq.(
41

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=-i\int d\bm{k}_{1}[w(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k})]^{2}\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_1}_\mu} u}\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}_{1}}}-H(\bm{k}_{1})\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial{k_1}_\nu}u}\right]

(59)

\displaystyle=-i\frac{m}{\hbar}\left[\bra{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\mu} u}\varepsilon_{{}_{\bm{k}}}-H(\bm{k})\ket{\frac{\partial}{\partial k_\nu}u}\right]

The last line follows because

E.1

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bm{r}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{\gradient{T}}{T}(\varepsilon-\mu)-\gradient{\mu}\right],

We will now calculate the quantity

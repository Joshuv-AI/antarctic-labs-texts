Gap-Dependent Hydrodynamic Lubrication in Conformal Contacts
James A. Richards, Patrick B. Warren, Wilson C. K. Poon

Abstract
We show that the hydrodynamic lubrication of contacting conformal surfaces with a typical texture height gives rise to a universal behaviour in the Stribeck curve in which the friction coefficient shows an anomalous power-law dependence on the Sommerfeld number, μ ∼ S 2 / 3 \mu\sim S^{2/3} . When the gap height drops below the ‘texture length scale’, deviations from S 2 / 3 S^{2/3} occur, which may resemble the onset of elasto-hydrodynamic and boundary lubrication. Within this framework, we analyse literature data for oral processing and find S 2 / 3 S^{2/3} scaling with deviations consistent with measured lengthscales.

Hydrodynamic lubrication originates from volume conservation in the convergent thin-film flow of an incompressible lubricant

. Consider a ball-on-flat geometry, Fig.
2

Uh/2
(volume per unit width, here and throughout) in the convergent gap due to the ball sliding along
x
relative to the plane at velocity
-U

2

-\frac{h^{3}}{12\eta}\nabla p

2

Mathematically, balancing the gap-dependent Couette (
\mathbf{J}_{c}

\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{J}_{c}+\mathbf{J}_{p})=0

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Bigl(\frac{Uh}{2}\Bigr)-\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Bigl(\frac{h^{3}}{12\eta}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\Bigr)+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Bigl(\frac{h^{3}}{12\eta}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}\Bigr)\right]=0\,.

(1)

In this Reynolds lubrication equation
Reynolds 1886

p(x,y)
is independent of the vertical coordinate because the gap
h(x,y)
is everywhere small compared to the flow’s length and width. Over the bearing area, this pressure supports
N
, while
F
has Couette and Poiseuille components:

N=\int\!\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\,p\,,\quad F=\int\!\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\left[\frac{\eta U}{h}+\frac{h}{2}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}\right)\right].

(2)

Analytic solutions to Eq. (
1

R
) on a flat surface with minimum gap
h_{0}\ll R

2

h_{0}=(72\pi^{2}/25)RS^{2}\approx 28.4RS^{2}

Hirani 2016
;
Hamrock et al. 2004
;
Faber 1995
;
Guyon et al. 2015

in the HL regime. So, while HL is widely thought to be understood, much of the theory remains untested by direct experiments.

In an accompanying paper
PRL 2023

II
Scaling theory of non-conformal contacts

The two surfaces in a non-conformal contact have different curvatures. Kapitza
Kapitza 1955
solved the sphere on flat, Fig.
2
a, analytically. The physics emerges from a scaling argument
Warren 2016
.

First, expand the convergent gap around its narrowest point,
h(r)\approx h_{0}+r^{2}/2R
, Fig.
2
b
. In a region of radius
r_{0}\sim\sqrt{h_{0}R}
, the gap remains narrow, and we expect from Eq. (
1
) that

\frac{Uh_{0}}{r_{0}}\sim\frac{1}{r_{0}}\times\frac{h_{0}^{3}}{\eta}\frac{p}{r_{0}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad p\sim\frac{\eta Ur_{0}}{h_{0}^{2}}\,.

(3)

This supports load
N\sim pr_{0}^{2}\sim\eta UR^{3/2}/h_{0}^{1/2}
, or
h_{0}/R\sim(\eta UR/N)^{2}\sim S^{2}
, confirming Kapitza
Kapitza 1955
.
Symmetry predicts equal but opposite pressure in the diverging gap. If this negative pressure is significantly below atmospheric and the lubricant cavitates, then the ‘half Sommerfeld boundary condition’ of
p=0
applies, giving a net normal force.

The narrow-gap shear rate
\dot{\gamma}\sim U/h_{0}
gives a shear stress
\sigma\sim\eta U/h_{0}
and lateral force
F\sim\sigma r_{0}^{2}\sim\eta UR
, so that

\mu=\frac{F}{N}\sim\frac{\eta UR}{N}\sim S\,.

(4)

Such linear scaling is often assumed in sketches of the Stribeck curve in the HL regime (
e.g.
, Fig. 2 in Ref.
Woydt and Wäsche 2010
).

For a long cylinder,
x_{0}\sim\sqrt{h_{0}R}
in the narrow gap region. If its length
L
satisfies
L\gg x_{0}
, then lubricant flow is exclusively along the direction of motion rather than through side leakage. The pressure has the same form as the ball-on-flat case, but now acts over area
\sim x_{0}L
, not
r_{0}^{2}
, which support a load
N\sim x_{0}Lp_{0}\sim x_{0}^{2}\eta UL/h_{0}^{2}\sim\eta UL(R/h_{0})
, with associated drag
F\sim\sigma x_{0}L\sim\eta UL\sqrt{R/h_{0}}
. Now we predict

S\equiv\frac{\eta UL}{N}\sim\frac{h_{0}}{R}\,,\quad\mu\sim\sqrt{\frac{h_{0}}{R}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mu\sim S^{1/2}\,.

(5)

Apparently, the HL of non-conformal contacts gives
\mu\sim S^{\alpha}
with
\alpha
set by the spatial dimension. However, few published Stribeck curves show either of these scalings.

III.2.3
Rayleigh step

The gap in the Rayleigh step is piecewise continuous,

h=\Bigl\{\begin{array}[]{lll}h_{0}+d&&0\leq x/D<\chi\,,\\
h_{0}&&\chi\leq x/D\leq 1\,.\end{array}

(21)

For a long-bearing with this profile in the small-gap limit (
h_{0}\ll d
), the Couette flux in the thin gap region is negligible, and the compensating Poiseuile flux exists almost entirely to balance the Couette flux in the inlet of length
\chi D

. From Eq. (

\frac{Ud}{\chi D}\sim\frac{1}{\chi D}\times\frac{d^{3}}{12\eta}\times\frac{p}{\chi D}\quad\Rightarrow\quad p\sim\frac{\eta U\chi D}{d^{2}}\,.

(22)

This acts over the whole length of the bearing (even though the flow in the narrow gap is negligible) to give the normal force, while the tangential load is dominated by the narrow gap:

N\sim\frac{\eta U\!\chi D^{2}L}{d^{2}}\,,\quad F\sim\frac{\eta U}{h_{0}}(1-\chi)DL\,,

(23)

i.e.
,

is a constant, while

diverges as the gap narrows. So, we expect that as

approaches some constant value
\sim\chi^{-1}(d/D)^{2}
, Fig.
5

and
b

In the short Rayleigh step, sideways Poisueille leakage occurs over the infinitesimal width of the step itself, so that the conditions necessary for the validity of the Reynolds lubrication formalism in Eq. (
1
) no longer apply.

III.3
Summary of scaling analysis

Our scaling analysis gives is a universal ‘anomalous’ regime of
\mu\sim S^{2/3}
at large
S
for all three geometries in Fig.
3
. When
h_{0}\to d
, deviations from this scaling occurs. The details are geometry dependent, with the
S\to 0
limit derivable by scaling analysis except in the pathological case of the short Rayleigh step.
Solving Eq. (
1
) by quadrature in Sections
IV
and
V
will verify these scaling conclusions and provide closed forms for plotting the behaviours shown in Fig.
5
and Table
1
(and Fig. 3 of Ref.
PRL 2023

scaling is upwards as
S

VI
, a general way of analysing experimental data to extract a surface texturing length scale from the Stribeck curve.

IV.1
General lubrication calculations

We start by deriving a general expression for the Reynolds lubrication pressure,

IV.1.1
Long slider bearings (

With negligible edge effects and

, and we can integrate Eq. (

Assuming equal inlet and outlet pressures,

\xi_{m}=\langle{\xi^{-2}}\rangle/\langle{\xi^{-3}}\rangle

) by parts,
\int\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,p=-\int\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,x(\partial p/\partial x)
, again assuming
\Delta p=0
. After some algebra, we find

\displaystyle N=\frac{6\eta U\!LD^{2}}{d^{2}}\,\frac{\langle{u\xi^{-3}}\rangle\langle{\xi^{-2}}\rangle-\langle{u\xi^{-2}}\rangle\langle{\xi^{-3}}\rangle}{\langle{\xi^{-3}}\rangle}=\frac{\eta U\!LD^{2}}{d^{2}}g_{\delta}(z)\,,

(27)

\displaystyle F=\frac{\eta U\!LD}{d}\,\frac{4\langle{\xi^{-1}}\rangle\langle{\xi^{-3}}\rangle-3\langle{\xi^{-2}}\rangle^{2}}{\langle{\xi^{-3}}\rangle}=\frac{\eta U\!LD}{d}f_{\delta}(z)\,,

(28)

where the subscript in the dimensionless normal load,
g(z)
, and drag,
f(z)
, points to the dependence on the gap profile
\delta(u)=\xi(u)-z
. These expressions imply
\mu=F/N\sim d/D
and

, reproducing the geometric factors in Eq. (

) established by scaling analysis.

The Stribeck curve,
\mu(S)
, then follows parametrically as the gap
z
(and so
\langle{\xi^{-n}}\rangle
) varies as a control parameter. Note that the higher-order averages required in Eqs. (

\langle{\xi^{-1}}\rangle

from recurrence:

\langle{\xi^{-n-1}}\rangle=-\frac{1}{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}{\langle{\xi^{-n}}\rangle}}{\mathrm{d}{z}}\,,\quad\langle{u\,\xi^{-n-1}}\rangle=-\frac{1}{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}{\langle{u\,\xi^{-n}}\rangle}}{\mathrm{d}{z}}\,,

(29)

which follow by differentiating Eq. (

\xi=z+\delta

IV.1.2
Short slider bearings (
D\ll L
)

In this limit, we neglect
\partial p/\partial x
in Eq. (
1
) and, recalling that
h
is
y

h^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial y^{2}}=6\eta U\frac{\mathrm{d}{h}}{\mathrm{d}{x}}\,.

(30)

Fixing
p(y=0)=p(y=L)=0
,
i.e.
taking
p
as the excess pressure, this integrates to

p=-\frac{3\eta U}{h^{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}{h}}{\mathrm{d}{x}}\times y(L-y)\,,

(31)

giving the normal load

\begin{split}N=3\eta U\,\frac{L^{3}}{6}\int_{0}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}{u}}{\xi^{3}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}{\xi}}{\mathrm{d}{u}}=\frac{\eta U\!L^{3}}{d^{2}}\,\frac{(2z+1)}{4z^{2}(z+1)^{2}}=\frac{\eta U\!L^{3}}{d^{2}}g(z)\,.\end{split}

(32)

Now,
g(z)
does not depend on the bearing profile,
\delta

z
. The drag also simplifies due to the absence of Poiseuille flow along the sliding direction,

F=L\int_{0}^{D}\!\!dx\,\frac{\eta U}{h}=\frac{\eta U\!LD}{d}\,f_{\delta}(z)\,,

(33)

with

From Eqs. (

IV.1.3
Power-law expansion

In the large-gap limit,
z\gg 1
. For the short bearing, expanding Eq. (
32
) gives
g(z)\sim z^{-3}
. For the long bearing, we need to expand the
\xi
-dependent numerators in Eqs. (
27
) in the small quantity
\delta(u)/z\sim\mathcal{O}({1/z})
, whereupon we find

\displaystyle\langle{\xi^{-n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}{u}\,z^{-n}}{[1+\delta(u)/z]^{-n}}\approx z^{-n}(1-n\langle{\delta}\rangle/z)\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\langle{u\xi^{-n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}{u}\,z^{-n}u}{[1+\delta(u)/z]^{-n}}\approx z^{-n}(1/2-n\langle{u\delta}\rangle/z)\,.

(35)

The leading order terms cancel, leaving

g(z)\approx\frac{6z^{-5}(\langle{\delta}\rangle/2z-\langle{u\delta}\rangle/z}{z^{-3}}=\frac{3(\langle{\delta}\rangle-2\langle{u\delta}\rangle)}{z^{3}}\sim\mathcal{O}({\frac{1}{z^{3}}})\,,

(36)

as in the short-bearing limit. In the final step we have taken the gap profile averages to be of order the ‘step’ height and not cancel, which is correct for all profiles apart from the case of
\chi=1
, which violates the lubrication approximation. With the tangential load

in all cases (as
\langle{\xi^{-n}}\rangle\rightarrow z^{-n}

with
\mu\propto z^{2}\propto S^{2/3}

In the small-gap limit,
z+1\rightarrow 1
and

from Eqs. (
27

32

S\propto z^{2}
and
\mu\propto z
,
i.e.

\mu\propto S^{1/2}
for all bearing profiles and types, contradicting our scaling predictions. A more rigorous analysis is needed.

V
Transition from large-gap scaling

To probe how deviations from
\mu\sim S^{2/3}
scaling first emerges as
h_{0}\to d
, which will be important for analysing experiments, consider the ‘running exponent’ of the Stribeck curve,

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln\mu}}{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln S}}=1-\frac{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln F}}{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln N}}=1-\frac{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln f}}{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln g}}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln f}}{\mathrm{d}{z}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}{\,\ln g}}{\mathrm{d}{z}}\right)^{-1}\!\!=1-\frac{1}{f}\frac{\mathrm{d}{f}}{\mathrm{d}{z}}\left(\frac{1}{g}\frac{\mathrm{d}{g}}{\mathrm{d}{z}}\right)^{-1}\!\!.

V.1
Short-bearing limit

Given
g(z)
for all profiles, Eq. (
32
), and the recurrence relation for the derivative of
f(z)
, Eq. (
29
), we find

{\alpha}=1-\frac{z(z+1)(2z+1)}{2(3z^{2}+3z+1)}\times\frac{\langle{\xi^{-2}}\rangle}{\langle{\xi^{-1}}\rangle}\,.

(38)

The moments of
\xi
can be recast explicitly in terms of the gap profile,
\xi(u)=z+\delta(u)
. In the asymptotic limit of large

,

\Bigr\langle{\frac{1}{\xi^{n}}}\Bigl\rangle=\frac{1}{z^{n}}\Bigl[1-\frac{n\langle{\delta}\rangle}{z}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr]\,.

(39)

Then, within the short bearing approximation,

\alpha=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{\langle{\delta}\rangle-1/2}{3z}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

(40)

The sign of initial deviations from
\alpha=2/3
depends on the average profile height
\langle{\delta}\rangle
. The short inlet–half-cylinder is ‘blunt’,
\langle{\delta}\rangle<1/2
, only when
\chi
is small enough, Fig.
8
a
[light (gold) to dark (blue) for decreasing
\chi
], in which case
\alpha
initially drops below 2/3 and the Stribeck curve bends upwards from the region of universal

scaling, mimicking the onset of EHL. The onset of deviations from
\alpha=2/3
for the short wedge, Fig.
8
a
(black dashed), is at significantly smaller
z
than for the inlet–half-cylinder because
\langle{\delta}\rangle=1/2
, so that deviations, which are always positive, scale as
z^{-2}
.

V.2
Long-bearing limit

In the long-bearing limit, we find a more complex expression,

\alpha=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2(\langle{u}\rangle\langle{\delta^{2}}\rangle-\langle{u\delta}\rangle\langle{\delta}\rangle)+\langle{u\delta}\rangle^{2}-\langle{u}\rangle\langle{\delta^{2}}\rangle}{3d(\langle{u}\rangle\langle{\delta}\rangle-\langle{u\delta}\rangle)z}{}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

(41)

To highlight the structure of the answer we have retained
\langle{u}\rangle=1/2
, rather than substitute its value. The sign of the initial deviation from
\alpha=2/3
is therefore controlled by the numerator of the second term in this expansion
Note2
.
For the wedge, this reduces to

\alpha=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{17}{90z^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\Bigr(\frac{1}{z^{3}}\Bigl)\,.

(42)

So,
\alpha
drops below 2/3, Fig.
8
b
, leading to an upwards deviation of the Stribeck curve from
S^{2/3}
scaling. Again, this effect is second order in
1/z
, as in the short-bearing case.

For the inlet–half-cylinder,

\alpha=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{2(1+5\chi-30\chi^{2})}{45(1+3\chi)}\frac{1}{z}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

(43)

For sufficiently short inlet lengths,
i.e.

\chi<(5+\sqrt{145})/60\approx 0.284
, in this narrow-gap limit,
\alpha
is lowered, Fig.

(dark lines), and the Stribeck curve deviates upwards from the large-gap scaling with decreasing

.

Finally, for the Rayleigh step, Fig.
8

\alpha=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1-2\chi}{3z}+\mathcal{O}\Bigr(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\Bigl)\,,

so that in a similar manner, a suitably ‘blunt’ step,

We therefore arrive at a conclusion for all slider bearing types and profiles: large-gap scaling is
\mu\sim S^{2}/3
and upwards deviation with decreasing

\langle{\delta}\rangle
,
i.e.
a small enough inlet.

VI
Interpretation of experimental data

We have shown that for a broad class of surface profiles and bearing dimensions, upwards deviation from
\mu\sim S^{2/3}
scaling in the HL regime starts when the gap approaches the step height,
i.e.

h_{0}/d\sim 1
. This also apply to various textured surfaces, Fig.
3
(a), so that

can be estimated from measured Stribeck curves. We end by giving a worked example of this procedure.

Soltanahmadi
et al.

VII
Conclusions

We have shown that the HL of non-conformal and conformal contacts differ fundamentally. For former, the problem has a single length scale, the minimum gap, giving ‘trivial’ scaling in the Stribeck curve,
e.g.

\mu\sim S
for ball-on-flat, independent of the absolute gap. In contrast, the HL of conformal contacts is non-trivial due to the presence of two independent length scales, the minimum gap and a surface texturing or ‘step’ height, giving anomalous
\mu\sim S^{2/3}
large-gap scaling. As the gap decreases below the step height, deviation from
S^{2/3}
occurs. ‘Blunt’ surface profiles, where the gap remains small over more of the bearing area, show an ‘upwards’ deviation as
S
drops, reminiscent of entry into the EHL regime. We used our approach to deduce the length scale of surface texturing from the data in a recent triborheological study.

Large-gap HL is of limited application in engineering applications, which are typically designed to function near the minimum of the Stribeck curve. However, this regime becomes important whenever soft matter is involved in lubrication,
e.g.
, in the oral processing of food
Soltanahmadi et al. 2023
, the application of skin cream
Lee et al. 2022
, and the extrusion of ceramic ‘green bodies’
Jiang et al. 2009
(a ceramic ‘green body’ is the formed object before firing). Future work may therefore fruitfully extend our analysis to non-Newtonian fluids with,
e.g.
, rate-dependent rheology and normal stress differences.

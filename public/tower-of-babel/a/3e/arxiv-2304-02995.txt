Growth of sobolev norms for 2d cubic nls with partial harmonic potential
Mingming Deng, Xiaoyan Su, Jiqiang Zheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the 2 2 D cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) with the partial harmonic potential. First, we prove the local well-posedness in Bourgain spaces by establishing a key bilinear estimate associated with the partial harmonic oscillator. Then, we give the polynomial bound of the Sobolev norms for the solutions using the method of the Planchon, Tzvetkov, and Visciglia in [ 11 ] .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35A01; 35B40; 35Q55

1.
introduction

We consider the following cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with the partial harmonic potential in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+Au\pm|u|^{2}u=0,\quad(t,x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\
u(0,x,y)=\varphi(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
A=-\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{y}^{2}+y^{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is the Sobolev space adapted to the operator
A
. This operator models magnetic traps in the Bose–Einstein condensation
[
2
,
15
]
and has been extensively studied in
[
2
,
3
,
13
,
14
]
. The partial harmonic oscillator has an intermediate behavior between the Laplacian operator which has a continuous spectrum, and the (full) harmonic oscillator which has discrete spectrum, and shares the properties of both operators. For example, for the linear evolution operator
e^{itA}
, the
x
-part can yield large time dispersion, but the
y
-part only provide local time dispersion due to the potential confinement. This operator also serves as an example which has infinite many embedded eigenvalues.

In this paper, we focus on the growth of Sobolev norms for the solutions to the nonlinear equation (
1.1
). This problem was first investigated by Bourgain for the Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
in
[
4
]
, where he used the Fourier multiplier method to prove an iteration bound

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leqslant\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}+C\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}^{1-\delta},\quad\delta^{-1}=(s-1)+

(1.2)

for any
t\in[\tau,\tau+T]
with
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}})
. Here and in the sequel, we write
a+
(
a-
) to denote any number larger (smaller) than
a
. This implies the following polynomial bound

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leqslant C|t|^{2(s-1)+},

and improved the exponential bound derived by a pacing argument. The growth of Sobolev norms reflected the weak turbulence phenomena for the nonlinear dispersive equations.

For the cubic NLS on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a similar result has been derived by Staffilani in
[
12
]
. She showed the bound (
1.2
) by using bilinear estimates in Bourgain spaces and derived

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leqslant C|t|^{(s-1)+}.

The growth of Sobolev norms also have been investigated for the NLS on compact Riemannian manifold, see
[
9
]
for more information.

One natural question to ask is that whether we can get similar results for the NLS with a potential.
One recent result on this field was given by Planchon–Tzvetkov–Visciglia in
[
11
]
where they discussed the Schrödinger operator with the (full) harmonic potential, and obtained polynomial bounds on the growth of solutions to cubic NLS in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Their results highly depended on the following bilinear estimate established in
[
9
]
:

\|P_{N}(u)P_{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}\leqslant C(\min{(M,N)})^{0+}\Big(\frac{\min{(M,N)}}{\max{(M,N)}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-}\|P_{N}(u)\|_{X^{0,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|P_{M}(v)\|_{X^{0,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

(1.3)

where
b<1/2
,
P_{N},P_{M}
are the Littlewood–Paley localization associated with the Hermite operator,
N,M
are dyadic integers, and
X_{T}^{s,b}
denotes the (local) Bourgain spaces adapted to the Hermite operator. Using this, they proved the growth of Sobolev norms of order
s
with
s\geqslant 1
is

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\langle x\rangle^{s}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leqslant Ct^{\frac{2}{3}(s-1)+}.

Inspired by the work of Planchon–Tzvetkov–Visciglia
[
9
]
and the work of Staffilani
[
12
]
, it is natural to conjecture that similar results should be valid for the Schrödinger equation with the partial harmonic potential
A
. The aim of this paper is to confirm this conjecture and give the growth rate.

The key step to gain the growth rate is to establish the following bilinear estimate.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\delta_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and assume that
1\leqslant M\leqslant N
are dyadic integers. Then there exists
0<b<\frac{1}{2}
and
C>0
such that for every
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]

\displaystyle\big\|\Delta_{N}u\Delta_{M}v\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leqslant CM^{\delta}\Big(\frac{M}{N}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\big\|\Delta_{N}u\big\|_{X^{0,b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\big\|\Delta_{M}v\big\|_{X^{0,b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

(1.4)

where
\Delta_{N}
is given in Subsection
2.3
and
X^{s,b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is the Bourgain space defined in Section
2.4
.

To get the above result, we first give some Bernstein type inequalities associated to the Schrödinger operator
A
. Then, using a Morawetz type estimate and some interpolation inequalities, we obtain the bilinear estimate (
1.4
) for the operator
A
. We refer the details to Section
3
.

Proposition
1.1
and a trilinear estimate yield the local well-posedness result in Bourgain spaces. Then, by defining suitable modified energies
\mathcal{S}_{2k+2}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2k+2}
, we prove our main result:

Theorem 1.1

(Growth of Sobolev norms)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For every global solution
u
to (
1.1
) such that
u\in\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}},\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
and

\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|u(t,x,y)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<\infty,

(1.5)

there exists a constant
C
such that

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{3}(2k-1)+\varepsilon}.

(1.6)

Remark 1.1
.

By conservation of energy, the assumption (
1.5
) is naturally satisfied for the defocusing case, but not for the focusing case.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the preliminaries which includes the definition of Hermite functions, some function spaces adapted to the operator
A
, and Bernstein-type inequalities and a multiplier theorem. In Section
3
, we prove the Proposition 1.1 and the local well-posedness for the equation (
1.1
) using Bourgain spaces; In Section
4
, we obtain the growth of high order Sobolev norms for solution to (
1.1
).

2.
preliminaries

Let
z=(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be the space variable, and
\zeta=(\xi,\eta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be the frequency variable. Denote
A_{x}=-\partial_{x}^{2}
and
A_{y}=-\partial_{y}^{2}+y^{2}
.

2.1.
Hermite functions (See
[
16
]
)

The Hermite functions
h_{k}
are defined on
\mathbb{R}
by the formula

\displaystyle h_{k}(y)=(2^{k}k!\pi^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}H_{k}(y)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}},\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
H_{k}
denotes the Hermite polynomial of degree
k
.
It is the eigenfunction of
A_{y}
with eigenvalues
2k+1
, that is,

A_{y}h_{k}=(2k+1)h_{k}.

In addition, the functions
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
form an orthonormal family in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Hence, any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, has an expansion in Hermite functions

f(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{k}f(y),

where
P_{k}
is the projection to the span of
h_{k}
, defined by

\displaystyle P_{k}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(y)h_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}yh_{k}(x).

The next lemma gives the almost orthogonality for the product of Hermite functions with different frequencies.

Lemma 2.1

(See
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\delta>0
and
K\geqslant 0
. Assume
N_{0}\geqslant N_{1}^{1+\delta}
and
N_{1}\geqslant N_{2}\geqslant N_{3}
, there exists
C_{K}>0
such that for every
k_{i}
satisfying
N_{i}\leqslant\sqrt{2k_{i}+1}\leqslant 2N_{i}
,
i=0,1,2,3
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{k_{0}}h_{k_{1}}h_{k_{2}}h_{k_{3}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Big|\leqslant C_{K}(1+N_{0})^{-K}.

2.2.
Sobolev spaces adapted to
A
(See
[
14
]
)

Using the Fourier transform with respect to
x
and the Hermite expansion with respect to
y
, the operator
A
when acting on a function
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
can be written as

\displaystyle Au(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}(\xi^{2}+2k+1)\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}P_{k}u(\xi,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

By the functional calculus, we define the fractional power
A^{s/2}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by

\displaystyle A^{s/2}u(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}(\xi^{2}+2k+1)^{s/2}\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}P_{k}u(\xi,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

The Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
adapted to
A
with index
s\in\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})=D(A^{s/2})=\big\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):A^{s/2}u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\big\}

with the norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\big\|A^{s/2}u\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
.

Remark 2.1
.

From
[
6
]
, we have the following equivalence of the Sobolev norms:
for every
s\geqslant 0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}\big(\big\|D^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}+\big\|\langle y\rangle^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\big)\leqslant\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant C\big(\big\|D^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}+\big\|\langle y\rangle^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\big),

(2.1)

where
D^{s}
is the operator associated with the Fourier multiplier
|\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}|^{s/2}
and
\langle y\rangle=\big(1+y^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Using this result, obtaining (
1.6
) is reduced to the estimate

\big\|D^{2k}u\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\big\|\langle y\rangle^{2k}u\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2k-1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

(2.2)

For later use, we recall a Mikhlin-type multiplier theorem for the operator
A
in
[
13
]
. Let
\mathcal{D}^{N}_{k}
be the forward finite difference with respect to the variable
k
of order
N
.

Lemma 2.2

(Multiplier theorem for
A
)
.

Suppose that a function
m(\xi,k)
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies that for all
0\leqslant N\leqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial^{N}}{\partial{\xi}^{N}}m(\xi,k)\Big|\leqslant C(\xi^{2}+2k+1)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\big|\mathcal{D}^{N}_{k}m(\xi,k)\big|\leqslant C(\xi^{2}+2k+1)^{-N}.

Then, the operator
T_{m}
defined by

\displaystyle T_{m}f(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}m(\xi,k)\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}P_{k}f(\xi,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi

is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for any
1<p<\infty
.

2.3.
Littlewood–Paley theory for
A

Let
\phi
be a smooth function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\phi(\lambda)=1
for
0\leqslant\lambda\leqslant 1
, and
\phi(\lambda)=0
for
\lambda\geqslant 2
. For each dyadic integer
N\in 2^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we define

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(\lambda):=\phi\Big(\frac{\lambda}{N^{2}}\Big),\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{N}(\lambda):=\phi_{N}(\lambda)-\phi_{\frac{N}{2}}(\lambda).

Clearly,
\{\psi_{N}(\lambda)\}_{N\in 2^{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
forms a partition of unity for
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
. Then we define the Littlewood-Paley projection as follows:

\displaystyle S_{N}u(z)

\displaystyle:=\phi_{N}(A)u(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}\phi\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}+2k+1}{N^{2}}\Big)\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}P_{k}u(\xi,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi,

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}u(z)

\displaystyle:=\psi_{N}(A)u(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}\psi\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}+2k+1}{N^{2}}\Big)\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}P_{k}u(\xi,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

For the frequency localized operator
S_{N}
and
\Delta_{N}
, we have the following estimates.

Lemma 2.3

(Bernstein-type estimates)
.

Let
1<p\leqslant q<\infty
and
s\geqslant 0
.

(1)

\|A^{s/2}S_{N}u\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim_{s}N^{s+(\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{q})}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
;

(2)

N^{s}\|\Delta_{N}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim_{s}\|A^{s/2}\Delta_{N}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim_{s}N^{s}\|\Delta_{N}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
;

Proof.

Let
e^{-tA}
be the heat semigroup associated to the operator
A
.
We prove (1) as follows. Using Lemma
2.2
, we have for
1<p<\infty
that

\displaystyle\Big\|A^{s/2}\phi\Big(\frac{A}{N^{2}}\Big)e^{\frac{A}{N^{2}}}e^{-\frac{A}{N^{2}}}f\Big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant CN^{s}\|\lambda^{s/2}\phi(\lambda)e^{\lambda}\|_{C^{2}(0,2)}\|e^{-\frac{A}{N^{2}}}f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

By Mehler’s formula, the Schwartz kernel of
e^{-tA}
is given by

\displaystyle K(t,z,z^{\prime})=2^{-\frac{3}{2}}\pi^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sinh 2t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}{4t}+\frac{1}{4}(2\coth 2t-\tanh t)(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\tanh t(y+y^{\prime})^{2}}.

Since
\sinh(2N^{-2})\leqslant C2N^{-2}
,
\coth(2N^{-2})\leqslant C\frac{1}{2}N^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big|K(N^{-2},z,z^{\prime})\big|\leqslant&~CN^{2}e^{-\big(\frac{1}{4}N^{2}(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\coth(2N^{-2})(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\tanh(N^{-2})(y+y^{\prime})^{2}\big)}\\
\leqslant&~CN^{2}e^{-\big(\frac{1}{4}N^{2}(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}N^{2}(y-y^{\prime})^{2}\big)}.\end{aligned}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big\|e^{-\frac{A}{N^{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}&\leqslant CN^{2}\Big\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}N^{2}(z-z^{\prime})^{2}}f(z^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\\
&\leqslant CN^{2}\big\|e^{-\frac{1}{4}N^{2}z^{2}}\big\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant CN^{2(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})},\end{aligned}

where we used Young’s inequality in the last step.

To prove
(2)
, we only need to prove the first inequality as the second inequality is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.2
. Let
\tilde{\psi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
\tilde{\psi}\psi=\psi
. Then
using (

(
1
)

) and Lemma
2.2
again,

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{N}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|A^{-s/2}\tilde{\psi}\Big(\frac{A}{N^{2}}\Big)A^{s/2}\Delta_{N}u\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-s}\|A^{s/2}\Delta_{N}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-(s+(\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{q}))}\|A^{s/2}\Delta_{N}u\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The restriction on
p
and
q
(
p,q\neq 1,\infty
) in Lemma
2.3
comes from the restriction of the exponents in Lemma
2.2
. We believe the inequalities are valid for
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
, but the lemma suffices for our needs.

Next, for any integer
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the functional of the indicator function of
[\lambda,\lambda+1)
associated to
A
as

\mathbf{1}_{\lambda}
, that is

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\lambda}f(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}\textbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+1)}(\sqrt{\xi^{2}+2k+1})\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}P_{k}u(\xi,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

Our next lemma shows that functions spectrally localized in different regions are almost orthogonal.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If
C_{0}
is sufficiently large, then for any
\nu>0
, there exists
C_{\nu}>0

such that for
\lambda_{j}
(
j=1,2,3
) satisfies
C_{0}\lambda_{j}\leqslant\lambda_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}}f_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{3}}f_{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\right|\leqslant C_{\nu}\lambda_{0}^{-\nu}\prod_{j=0}^{3}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

(2.3)

Proof.

By definition, the spectrum of
\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}}f_{0}
is localized near
\lambda_{0}^{2}
. To be precise,
\xi_{0}^{2}+2k_{0}+1\in[\lambda_{0}^{2},(\lambda_{0}+1)^{2})
. Then either
\xi_{0}^{2}\in[\lambda_{0}^{2}/{2},2\lambda_{0}^{2}]
or
2k_{0}+1\in[\lambda_{0}^{2}/{2},2\lambda_{0}^{2}]
.

In the first case, since
\xi_{j}\leqslant\lambda_{j}
for
j=1,2,3
, we can choose
C_{0}
large enough such that
\sum_{j=1}^{3}\lambda_{j}<\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}<|\xi_{0}|
. Thus, the Fourier support in
x
direction of
\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{1}}*\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{2}}f_{2}}*\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{3}}f_{3}}(\xi,y)\subset\{\xi:|\xi|<|\xi_{0}|\}
. Hence, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}}f_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{3}}f_{3}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}}f_{0}}(\xi,y)\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{1}}*\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{2}}f_{2}}*\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{3}}f_{3}}(\xi,y)d\xi=0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}}f_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{3}}f_{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=0.

In the second case, if
2k_{0}+1\in[\lambda_{0}^{2}/{2},2\lambda_{0}^{2}]
, then we have
\sum_{k=1}^{3}2k_{i}+3\leqslant\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}}{2}<2k_{0}+1
. Then by Lemma
2.1
, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}}f_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{3}}f_{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right|\leqslant C_{\nu}\lambda_{0}^{-\nu}\prod_{i=0}^{3}\|\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{i}}f_{i}\|_{L_{y}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}}f_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}}f_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{3}}f_{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\nu}\lambda_{0}^{-\nu}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\prod_{i=0}^{3}\|\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{i}}f_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{R})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\nu}\lambda_{0}^{-\nu}\prod_{i=0}^{3}\|\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{i}}f_{i}\|_{L^{4}_{x}L_{y}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\nu}\lambda_{0}^{-\nu+2}\prod_{i=0}^{3}\|\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{i}}f_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L_{y}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}})},

where the last inequality follows from the Bernstein inequality.
Thus, we have finished the proof.
∎

2.4.
The Bourgain space
X^{s,b}
adapted to
A

Let
e^{itA}
be the solution operator for the following linear Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x,y)+Au(t,x,y)=0,\quad(t,x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\
u(0,x,y)=\varphi(x,y).\end{cases}

(2.4)

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. The Bourgain space
X^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{t};\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
in the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}=

\displaystyle~\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big\|\langle\xi^{2}+2k+1\rangle^{s/2}\langle\tau+\xi^{2}+(2k+1)\rangle^{b}\mathcal{F}_{t,x}P_{k}u(\tau,\xi,y)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\|e^{itA}u(t,\cdot)\|_{H_{t}^{b}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}^{2},

where
\mathcal{F}_{t,x}
denotes the Fourier transform with respect to
t
and
x
.

For
0<T\leqslant 1
, we denote by
X^{s,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
the subspace of functions
u\in X^{s,b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\inf\big\{\|\tilde{u}\|_{X^{s,b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},~\tilde{u}|_{(-T,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}=u\big\}<\infty.

(2.5)

For the Bourgain spaces, we have the following basic properties.

Proposition 2.1
.

(1)

For
s_{1}\leqslant s_{2},b_{1}\leqslant b_{2}
,
X^{s_{2},b_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\hookrightarrow X^{s_{1},b_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

(2)

For
b>\frac{1}{2}
,
X^{s,b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\hookrightarrow C({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
, and
X^{s,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\hookrightarrow C((-T,T);\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
.

(3)

For any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

Bilinear estimates

In this subsection, we are going to prove Proposition

Lemma 3.1

(3.1)

Proof.

Denote
u_{N}:=e^{itA}\Delta_{N}\varphi_{1},v_{M}:=e^{-itA}\Delta_{M}\varphi_{2}
for simplicity. Notice that
\bar{v}_{M}:=e^{itA}\Delta_{M}\varphi_{2}

2.1

(3.4)

For (

Proposition 3.1

\displaystyle\big\|e^{itA}\Delta_{N}\varphi_{1}e^{itA}\Delta_{M}\varphi_{2}\big\|_{L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}^{2}\leqslant C_{T}MN^{-1}\|\Delta_{N}\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\|\Delta_{M}\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}.

Denote

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}.

Low frequency:
For
I_{1}
, using Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\quad I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|S_{N}(v_{M}u_{N})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant N^{-2}\|NA^{-1/2}S_{N}A^{1/2}(v_{M}u_{N})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant N^{-2}\|A^{1/2}(v_{M}u_{N})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{T}\big\|S_{N}(v_{M}u_{N})\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leqslant&~C(MN^{-1}+M^{2}N^{-2})\|\Delta_{N}\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\|\Delta_{M}\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}.\end{aligned}

High frequency:
For

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{2}\leqslant\sum_{K\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\big\|\Delta_{K}(v_{M}u_{N})\big\|_{L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}^{2}\leqslant&~C\sum_{K\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}(1+K)^{-2}\|\Delta_{K}(v_{M}u_{N})\|_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathcal{H}_{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}^{2}\\

This completes the proof.

Proof of Proposition

.

First, we claim that Lemma

3.2.
Trilinear estimate

In this subsection, we are going to prove a trilinear estimate in Bourgain spaces using the bilinear estimates and the almost-orthogonality for the products of functions spectrally localized to different regions.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
C>0,b
and
b^{\prime}
satisfying
0<b^{\prime}<1/2<b,\ b+b^{\prime}<1,
such that for any
s\geqslant\varepsilon
and for every triple
(u_{j}),j=1,2,3
in
X^{s,b}
, we have

\big\|u_{1}u_{2}\overline{u}_{3}\big\|_{X^{s,-b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\|u_{1}\|_{X^{s,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|u_{2}\|_{X^{\varepsilon,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|u_{3}\|_{X^{\varepsilon,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

(3.16)

Proof.

By duality, to prove (
3.16
) is equivalent to prove

\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{1}u_{2}\bar{u}_{3}\bar{u}_{0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big|\leqslant C\|u_{0}\|_{X^{-s,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|u_{1}\|_{X^{s,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|u_{2}\|_{X^{\varepsilon,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|u_{3}\|_{X^{\varepsilon,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

(3.17)

Then performing a Littlewood–Paley decomposition on each function
u_{i}
and using a symmetry argument, it suffices to estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{N_{1}\geqslant N_{2}\geqslant N_{3}}\sum_{N_{0}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta_{N_{0}}\bar{u}_{0}\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{1}\Delta_{N_{2}}u_{2}\Delta_{N_{3}}\bar{u}_{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big|,

where the summation is meant over dyadic values of
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
and
N_{0}
. The other possible orders of magnitude of
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
are similar.

Case 1:

N_{0}\geqslant N_{1}^{1+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
.
Since
\Delta_{N}=\sum_{k\in[\frac{N^{2}}{2},2N^{2}]}\textbf{1}_{k}\Delta_{N},

\displaystyle\Delta_{N_{0}}\bar{u}_{0}\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{1}\Delta_{N_{2}}u_{2}\Delta_{N_{3}}\bar{u}_{3}=\sum_{i=0}^{3}\sum_{k_{i}\in[\frac{N_{i}^{2}}{2},2N_{i}^{2}]}\textbf{1}_{k_{0}}\Delta_{N_{0}}\bar{u}_{0}\textbf{1}_{k_{1}}\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{1}\textbf{1}_{k_{2}}\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{2}\textbf{1}_{k_{3}}\Delta_{N_{1}}\bar{u}_{k_{3}}.

2.4

Integrating in the variable
t
, using Hölder’s inequality and
X^{0,b^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow L^{4}_{t}L^{2}_{z}

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta_{N_{0}}\bar{u}_{0}\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{1}\Delta_{N_{2}}u_{2}\Delta_{N_{3}}\bar{u}_{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big|\leqslant C_{K}N_{0}^{-K}\prod_{i=0}^{3}\big\|\Delta_{N_{i}}u_{i}\big\|_{X^{0,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

where
b^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}
. The sum in
N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
is finite due to the large negative power of
N_{0}
.

Case 2:

N_{0}\leqslant N_{1}^{1+\delta}
, with
\delta>0
to be chosen later depending on
\varepsilon
. By Proposition
1.1
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta_{N_{0}}\bar{u}_{0}\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{1}\Delta_{N_{2}}u_{2}\Delta_{N_{3}}\bar{u}_{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big|\\
\leqslant&\big\|\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{1}\Delta_{N_{2}}u_{2}\big\|_{L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}\big\|\Delta_{N_{0}}u_{0}\Delta_{N_{3}}u_{3}\big\|_{L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}\\
\leqslant&~C(N_{2}N_{3})^{\delta}\Big(\frac{N_{2}N_{3}}{N_{0}N_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\prod_{j=0}^{3}\big\|\Delta_{N_{j}}u_{j}\big\|_{X^{0,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.\end{aligned}

Since
N_{0}\leqslant N_{1}^{1+\delta}
, we have for
\delta>0
sufficiently small so that
\kappa=2\epsilon-2\delta
is positive,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (N_{2}N_{3})^{\delta}\Big(\frac{N_{2}N_{3}}{N_{0}N_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}N_{0}^{s}N_{1}^{-s}(N_{2}N_{3})^{-\varepsilon}&=N_{0}^{s-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(N_{2}N_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}N_{1}^{-s-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\\
&\leqslant N_{1}^{-2\varepsilon+2\delta}=N_{1}^{-\kappa},\end{aligned}

so we can sum over
N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
.
Using property
(3)
of Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{0}\leqslant N_{1}^{1+\delta}\\
N_{1}\geqslant N_{2}\geqslant N_{3}\end{subarray}}\!\!(N_{2}N_{3})^{\delta}\Big(\frac{N_{2}N_{3}}{N_{0}N_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}N_{0}^{s}N_{1}^{-s}(N_{2}N_{3})^{-\varepsilon}\big\|\Delta_{N_{0}}u_{0}\big\|_{X^{-s,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\big\|\Delta_{N_{1}}u_{1}\big\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\prod_{j=2}^{3}\big\|\Delta_{N_{j}}u_{j}\big\|_{X^{\varepsilon,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\\
&\leqslant~C\big(\sum_{N}\big\|\Delta_{N}u\big\|_{X^{-s,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\sum_{N}\big\|\Delta_{N}u\big\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=2}^{3}\big(\sum_{N_{j}}\big\|\Delta_{N_{j}}u_{j}\big\|_{X^{\varepsilon,b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{aligned}

This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Local well-posedness in Bourgain spaces

In this section, we give the well-posedness for the equation (
1.1
) in Bourgain spaces.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R>0
and
s_{0}\geqslant\varepsilon>0
be given. Then there exist
T>0
and
b>\frac{1}{2}
such that (
1.1
) has a unique local solution in
X^{s_{0},b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with
\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<R
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s_{0},b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

Proof.

To prove this, we apply a fixed point argument for the integral equation

u(t)=e^{itA}\varphi\pm i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)A}(|u|^{2}u)(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.18)

Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be a cut-off function such that
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subset(-2,2)
and
\chi\equiv 1
on the interval
[-1,1]
. For
T\leqslant 1
, we consider the following truncated integral equation

u(t)=\chi(t)e^{itA}\varphi\pm i\chi\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)A}(|u|^{2}u)(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.19)

By the definition of
X^{s_{0},b}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\chi(t)e^{itA}\varphi\|_{X^{s_{0},b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\|\chi(t)\|_{H^{b}_{t}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\Big\|\chi\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)A}\big(|u|^{2}u\big)(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\Big\|_{X^{s_{0},b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant CT^{1-b-b^{\prime}}\big\||u|^{2}u\big\|_{X^{s_{0},-b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

holds for
T\leqslant 1
and
b>\frac{1}{2},b^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}
such that
b+b^{\prime}<1
. By Proposition
3.2
, we obtain

\big\||u|^{2}u\big\|_{X^{s_{0},-b^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\|u\|_{X^{s_{0},b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|u\|_{X^{\varepsilon,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}

(3.20)

for some
b>\frac{1}{2},b^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}
such that
b+b^{\prime}<1
. Note that
T\leqslant 1
, the nonlinear part can be absorbed by the left-hand side and thus we have completed the proof.
∎

4.
Growth of Sobolev norms

In this section, we will write
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}
instead of
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for the ease of notation.
This section is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
. Since
\partial_{t}
has better commutation properties with the nonlinear Schrödinger flow than the operator
A
, we first prove that the
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k}
norm of the solution
u
and
\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}
are comparable.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
k,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
R>0
be given. Let
T>0
be associated with
R
and
s_{0}=2k+s
as in Theorem
3.1
and let
u\in X^{2k+s,b}_{T}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be the unique solution to (
1.1
) with initial condition
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+s}
with
\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{1}}<R
. Assume moreover that
\sup_{(-T,T)}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{1}}<R
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}u(t)-i^{k}A^{k}u(t)\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}}\leqslant C\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s+2k-1}},\quad\forall~t\in(-T,T).

(4.1)

Proof.

Fixed
t
, and using the induction argument on
m
, we obtain

\partial_{t}^{m}u=i^{m}A^{m}u+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{j}\partial_{t}^{j}A^{m-j-1}(|u|^{2}u),\quad\forall m\geqslant 1

(4.2)

for some
c_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Next, we use the induction on
k
to prove (
4.1
). In fact, for
k=0
, it is easy to verify. Therefore, assuming (
4.1
) holds for
k
, we only need to show that the same estimate is true for
k+1
. Using (
4.2
) with
m=k+1
, we want to show

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \big\|\partial_{t}^{k+1}u(t)-i^{k+1}A^{k+1}u(t)\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}}&=\big\|\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}\partial_{t}^{j}A^{k-j}(|u|^{2}u)\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s}}\\
&\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{k}\big\|\partial_{t}^{j}(|u|^{2}u)\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k-2j+s}}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s+2k+1}}.\end{aligned}

Then by (
2.1
), it suffices to prove

\big\|D_{x,y}^{2k-2j+s}\partial_{t}^{j}(|u|^{2}u)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s+2k+1}},\quad j=0,1,...,k,

(4.3)

and

\big\|\langle y\rangle^{2k-2j+s}\partial_{t}^{j}(|u|^{2}u)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s+2k+1}},\quad j=0,1,...,k.

(4.4)

For (
4.3
), we use Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding, and the induction hypothesis to write

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}=j,\\
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2k-2j+s\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1,2,3}\big\|\partial^{j_{l}}_{t}u\big\|_{W^{s_{l},6}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}=j,\\
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2k-2j+s\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1,2,3}\big\|\partial^{j_{l}}_{t}u\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s_{l}+1}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}=j,\\
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2k-2j+s\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1,2,3}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2j_{l}+s_{l}+1}},

where
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
. The interpolation argument implies

\displaystyle\eqref{eq-derivative1}\leqslant C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}=j,\\
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2k-2j+s\end{subarray}}\big(\prod_{l=1,2,3}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s+2k+1}}^{\theta_{l}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s+2k+1}}^{1-\theta_{l}}\big),

where
\theta_{l}(s+2k+1)+(1-\theta_{l})=2j_{l}+s_{l}+1
and
\sum_{l=1}^{3}\theta_{l}=1
for every
j=0,...,k
. Therefore, we conclude the proof of (
4.3
).

Now let us prove (
4.4
). By Leibniz rule, Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\langle y\rangle^{2k-2j+s}\partial_{t}^{j}(|u|^{2}u)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}=j,\\
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2k-2j+s\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1,2,3}\big\|\langle y\rangle^{s_{l}}\partial^{j_{l}}_{t}u\big\|_{L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}=j,\\
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2k-2j+s\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1,2,3}\big\|\partial^{j_{l}}_{t}u\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s_{l}+1}}.

Then similarly to the proof of (
4.3
), we get (
4.4
).
∎

(1-\delta)\theta+(1-\delta)\theta_{1}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta\right)\gamma_{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\eta_{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta\right)\eta_{3}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\theta_{3}+\delta\alpha_{1}+\delta=\frac{4k}{2k+1}+\frac{4\delta}{2k+1},

while for
n_{3}=0
,

(1-\delta)\theta+(1-\delta)\theta_{1}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\gamma_{2}+(\frac{1}{2}+\delta)\eta_{2}+\delta\alpha_{1}+\delta=\frac{8k+1}{4k+2}+\frac{3\delta}{2k+1}.

This completes the proof of (
4.20
), after relabeling
\frac{3\delta}{2k+1}
to
\delta
.
∎

Finally, by using suitable energies and estimates, we prove the Sobolev norms growth provided in Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By integrating the identity (
4.18
) on the strip
(0,T)
in Proposition
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au(T,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{2k+2}(u)(T)=\frac{1}{2}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au(0,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{2k+2}(u)(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{R}_{2k+2}(u)(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau,

where
T
is the one defined in Proposition
4.3
and
R
is defined as

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{1}}=R,

(4.28)

and
R<\infty
by (
1.5
) in Theorem
1.1
. Then we use (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au(T,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au(0,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant C\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}^{\frac{8k+1}{4k+2}+\delta}.

Next by (
4.28
), we can iterate the bound above with the same constants,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au\big((n+1)T,\cdot\big)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au(nT,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant C\|u(nT,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}^{\frac{8k+1}{4k+2}+\delta}

for all
t\in\big(nT,(n+1)T\big)
with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By summing up for
n\in[0,N-1]
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au(NT,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant C\sum_{n\in\{0,...,N-1\}}\|u(nT,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}^{\frac{8k+1}{4k+2}+\delta},

and it implies

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in[0,N]}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k}Au(nT,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant CN\Big(\sup_{n\in[0,N]}\|u(nT,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}\Big)^{\frac{8k+1}{4k+2}+\delta}.

Then using Proposition
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in[0,N]}\|u(nT,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}\leqslant CN^{\frac{2}{3}(2k+1)+\delta}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\|u(NT,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}\leqslant CN^{\frac{2}{3}(2k+1)+\delta},

for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Using Theorem
3.1
, it is easy to deduce that the estimate above implies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[NT,(N+1)T]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}\leqslant CN^{\frac{2}{3}(2k+1)+\delta}

provided that we suitably modify the constant
C
. Summarizing, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{2k+2}}\leqslant Ct^{\frac{2}{3}(2k+1)+\delta}

for all
t>0
. This finishes the proof of Theorem 1.
∎

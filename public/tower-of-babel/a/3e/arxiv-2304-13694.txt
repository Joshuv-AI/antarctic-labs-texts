Fully discrete pointwise smoothing error estimates for measure valued initial data
Dmitriy Leykekhman, Boris Vexler, Jakob Wagner

Abstract
. In this paper we analyze a homogeneous parabolic problem with initial data in the space of regular Borel measures. The problem is discretized in time with a discontinuous Galerkin scheme of arbitrary degree and in space with continuous finite elements of orders one or two. We show parabolic smoothing results for the continuous, semidiscrete and fully discrete problems. Our main results are interior L ∞ L^{\infty} error estimates for the evaluation at the endtime, in cases where the initial data is supported in a subdomain. In order to obtain these, we additionally show interior L ∞ L^{\infty} error estimates for L 2 L^{2} initial data and quadratic finite elements, which extends the corresponding result previously established by the authors for linear finite elements.

1.
Introduction

In this work we discuss smoothing properties of the fully discrete approximation of the homogeneous parabolic problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\Delta v

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}I}\times\Omega,\;

(1)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}I}\times\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle v(0)

\displaystyle=v_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N=2,3
is a bounded, convex, polygonal/polyhedral domain,
and
I=(0,T]
a bounded time interval.
In particular we are interested in pointwise error estimates in the case when the initial condition
v_{0}
is a regular Borel measure
v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
supported in some subdomain
\Omega_{0}
such that

\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset\Omega
,
for example a linear combination of Dirac delta functions,

v_{0}=\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}
. Our main result of this paper establishes the fully discrete error estimate of the form

\|(v-v_{kh})(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(\Omega_{0},T)\left({k^{2r+1}}+\ell_{kh}{h^{s+1}}\right)\|{v_{0}}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}.

(2)

Here
r\geq 0
is the order of time discretization,
s=1,2
is the order of the space discretization, and
\ell_{kh}
is a logarithmic term that depends on the mesh size
h
and the maximum time step
k
.
In order to simplify the presentation, we assume that
v_{0}
is supported in the same subdomain
\Omega_{0}
,
in which the
L^{\infty}
error is estimated, wheareas in general, two different subdomains could be chosen.
We would like to point out that the piecewise linear case
s=1
does not require any additional smoothness assumptions beyond regularity results available on convex domains
\Omega
. The higher order convergence of
s=2
requires some additional smoothness assumptions, which are available for example on rectangular domains (cf. Section
6
). In this case, the logarithmic term
\ell_{kh}
only depends
on
k
.

The above problem is classical and many important results are available in the literature. The
L^{2}
theory for a uniform time partition is well presented in the classical textbook of Thomeé
[
20
]
. Extensions to variable time steps are available in Eriksson et al.
[
7
]
. The
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
stability results, are technically more difficult and one of the first papers in this direction was the work of Schatz et al.
[
18
]
, where such results were established in two space dimensions for piecewise linear elements and strongly A-stable single step methods with uniform time steps. The sharpest result in the case of smooth domains and uniform time steps was obtained by A. Hansbo in
[
9
]
.

In our previous paper
[
15
]
, for piecewise linear space discretizations on a convex polygonal/polyhedral domain
\Omega
and
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have obtained

\|(v-v_{kh})(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(T)\left({k^{2r+1}}+\ell_{kh}{h^{2}}\right)\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(3)

with explicit form of the constant
C(T)
. Such results were required for obtaining sharp results in initial data estimation of the parabolic problems from final time observation
[
15
]
.
However, in order to extend the results to the situation when the final time observation
is taken at a finite number of points
[
14
]
, which is more relevant in applications, we require the results of the form (
2
).
This yields an error estimate for the adjoint state, which satisfies a backwards-in-time problem,
with a final time condition given by a measure supported in the observation points.
Since these points are fixed, this support is contained in a proper subdomain, and hence
the assumptions for (
2
) are satisfied.
In summary, the main contribution of our paper is the establishment of fully discrete error estimates (
2
) for Galerkin methods on potentially highly varying time partitions and quasi-uniform meshes on convex polygonal/polyhedral domains, without any additional smoothness assumptions in the case of piecewise linear case and with additional smoothness assumptions in the case of quadratic elements.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the notion of very weak solutions for parabolic homogeneous problems with initial data given in the space of regular
Borel measures.
In Section
3
, we discuss space-time discretization schemes and introduce the semidiscrete and fully discrete Galerkin solutions to (
1
). In Section
4
, we review and show continuous and discrete smoothing estimates for the continuous, semidiscrete and fully discrete solutions. Our main result will be the pointwise fully discrete error estimate for initial data in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, see Theorem
5.2
, which we establish in Section
5
. Finally, in Section
6
we extend our main result to a higher order space discretization.

2.
Very weak solutions and regularity

We begin by introducing the proper setup for the existence and regularity of the solution with measure valued initial data.
Throughout this work, we use standard notations
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
,
W^{k,p}_{0}(\Omega)

for the Lebesque and Sobolev spaces and abbreviate them by
H^{k}(\Omega)
,
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
, in case
p=2
.
The
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product will be denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
.
We denote the Bochner spaces of
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
valued,
q-
integrable functions over the time interval

I
by
L^{q}(I;W^{k,p}(\Omega))
, and denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{I\times\Omega}

the inner product on
L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))\cong L^{2}(I\times\Omega)

The space
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
of regular Borel measures can be identified with the dual space
of
C_{0}(\Omega):=\{v\in C(\bar{\Omega}):v|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}
, i.e. it holds
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\cong(C_{0}(\Omega))^{*}

The norm on
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is then given as

\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{0\neq v\in C_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\langle v,\mu\rangle}{\|v\|_{C_{0}(\Omega)}}
.
Note that this norm is equivalent to the total variation norm
|\mu|(\Omega)=\mu^{+}(\Omega)+\mu^{-}(\Omega)
, where

\mu=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}
is the Jordan decomposition of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
.
With theses notations fixed, we can state the very weak formulation of (
1
).

{defin}

Let
v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
be given. A function

is called a very weak solution to the
heat equation (

-\int_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}I}\times\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\Delta\varphi\right)v\ dx\ dt=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(0)\ dv_{0}\qquad\text{for all }\varphi\in\Phi_{T},

(4)

where the space
\Phi_{T}
of all test functions is given by

\Phi_{T}=\left\{\varphi\in\Phi:\ \varphi(T)=0\ \text{in }\Omega\right\}\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}I};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)):\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\partial_{t}}\varphi+\Delta\varphi\in L^{\infty}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}I}\times\Omega)\land\varphi(T)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\}.

(5)

With this definition, we have the following result (see
[

, Lemma 2.2]
):

.

For a given

(6)

For any

(7)

In the second estimate of the above theorem, the constant

depends on
T
. We shall make this dependence

4.2
.
For the error analysis below, we will require the following result.

\tau\in(0,T)

with initial

. Then

defined as

v(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{1}(t)&t\in(0,\tau]\\
v_{2}(t)&t\in(\tau,T)\end{array}\right.

(8)

is the very weak solution in the sense of (

Proof 2.3

The proof is straightforward.

3.
Discretization

In this section we describe the semidiscrete and fully discrete finite element discretizations of the
homogeneous equation (
1
) and present smoothing type error estimates. To discretize the problem,
we use continuous Lagrange finite elements of order
s\geq 1
in space and discontinuous Galerkin methods of order
r\geq 0
in time.

3.1.
Time discretization

To be more precise, we partition
I=(0,T]
into subintervals
I_{m}=(t_{m-1},t_{m}]
of length

k_{m}=t_{m}-t_{m-1}
, where
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{M-1}<t_{M}=T
. The maximal and minimal time steps
are denoted by
k=\max_{m}k_{m}
and
k_{\min}=\min_{m}k_{m}
, respectively.
We impose the following conditions on the time mesh (as in
[
12
]

or
[
16
]
):

(i)

There are constants
c,\beta>0
independent on
k
such that

k_{\min}\geq ck^{\beta}.

(ii)

There is a constant
\kappa>0
independent on
k
such that for all
m=1,2,\dots,M-1

\kappa^{-1}\leq\frac{k_{m}}{k_{m+1}}\leq\kappa.

(iii)

It holds
k\leq\frac{T}{2r+2}
.

The semidiscrete space
X_{k}^{r}
for the case
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is taken as

X_{k}^{r}=\Set{\varphi_{k}\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}{\varphi_{k}|_{I_{m}}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(I_{m};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),\ m=1,2,\dots,M},

where
\mathbb{P}_{r}(I_{m};V)
is the space of polynomial functions of degree
r
in time on
I_{m}
with values
in a Banach space
V
.
However, for the semidiscrete formulation of (
1
) with measure valued initial data
v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
to be well defined, at initial time the test functions
\varphi_{k}
need to be in
C_{0}(\Omega)
.
Since for
N\geq 2
the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is not embedded in the space of continuous functions, we need to modify the spaces of
trial and test functions, as

\widetilde{X}_{k}^{r}=\left\{\varphi_{k}\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega):\varphi_{k}|_{I_{m}}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(I_{m};W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)),\ m=1,2,\dots,M\right\}

and

\widehat{X}_{k}^{r}=\left\{\varphi_{k}\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega):\varphi_{k}|_{I_{m}}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(I_{m};W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)),\ m=1,2,\dots,M\right\},

for some
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{2N}{N+2}}<p<\frac{N}{N-1}
and

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{2N}{N-2}}>p^{\prime}>N
the dual exponent satisfying

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. In this setting, the embedding

W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega)
, yields that
\langle v_{0},\varphi_{k,0}^{+}\rangle
is well
defined for all test functions
\varphi_{k}\in\widehat{X}_{k}^{r}
,
while every trial function
v_{k}\in\widetilde{X}_{k}^{r}
satisfies

v_{k}(t)\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
for every
t\in I
.
With these spaces, the dG(
r
)
semidiscrete (in time) solution
v_{k}
of (
1
) for

v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is given by
v_{k}\in\widetilde{X}_{k}^{r}
that satisfies

B(v_{k},\varphi_{k})=\left\langle v_{0},\varphi_{k,0}^{+}\right\rangle\quad\text{for all }\;\varphi_{k}\in\widehat{X}_{k}^{r}.

(9)

Here the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by

B(w,\varphi)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\partial_{t}w},\varphi\rangle_{I_{m}\times\Omega}+({\rm NewA}w,{\rm NewA}\varphi)_{I\times\Omega}+\sum_{m=2}^{M}([w]_{m-1},\varphi_{m-1}^{+})_{\Omega}+(w_{0}^{+},\varphi_{0}^{+})_{\Omega},

(10)

where

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{I_{m}\times\Omega}
is the duality product between
L^{2}(I_{m};W^{-1,p}(\Omega))

and
L^{2}(I_{m};W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega))
.
In the above definition we use the usual notation for functions with possible discontinuities at the nodes
t_{m}

w^{+}_{m}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}w(t_{m}+\varepsilon),\quad w^{-}_{m}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}w(t_{m}-\varepsilon),\quad[w]_{m}=w^{+}_{m}-w^{-}_{m}.

(11)

Remark 3.1
.

Note that whenever
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
the formulation (

equivalent to searching
v_{k}\in X_{k}^{r}

B(v_{k},\varphi_{k})={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(v_{0},\varphi_{k,0}^{+}\right)_{\Omega}}\quad\text{for all }\;\varphi_{k}\in X_{k}^{r}.

(12)

Remark 3.2
.

Since we are dealing with a homogeneous parabolic problem with constant coefficients,
the discontinuous Galerkin method actually coincides with subdiagonal Padé approximations
and one can use, for example, a rational representation of the semidiscrete solution.
While it is more convenient for our analysis to use the definition based on the
bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)

this rational expression allows us to show wellposedness of the semidiscrete problem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. Then the semidiscrete problem (
9
)
has a unique solution
v_{k}\in\widetilde{X}_{k}^{r}

.

It is sufficient to show the claim on the first time interval.
Let

denote a basis of

It is well known, that in the setting of (

),
there exist polynomials

and

of degrees

Here

The polynomial

These all satisfy

By

By the elliptic theory, we can show, that

To show wellposedness of the elliptic problems, we employ the following construction.
For any

From this, we can construct

4.
Parabolic smoothing

In this section we review and establish smoothing properties of the continuous and discrete solutions, which are essential
for the establishment of our main results.

4.1.
Smoothing estimates for the continuous problem

It is well known that homogeneous parabolic problems have a strong smoothing effect. In particular, for
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the solution
v
to the problem (
1
) has the following smoothing property,
see
[
3
, Chapter 1, Eq. 1.14]

\|\partial_{t}^{l}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|(-\Delta)^{l}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{t^{l}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad t>0,\quad l=0,1,\dots.

(23)

Remark 4.1
.

In many situations it is sufficient to have smoothing type estimates in
L^{2}
norms and the corresponding smoothing results, for example in
L^{p}
norms, can obtained by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

In

.

(41)

The second error term in (

which followed from (see

6.
Higher order space discretizations

Our main result from the previous section, Theorem
5.2
, was established for piecewise linear finite elements only and does not require any additional smoothness assumptions on the solutions beyond
H^{2}
regularity that is provided by the convexity of the domain. If additional regularity is available, for example,

|v|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta v\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

(49)

for any
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\Delta v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
then the results of Proposition
1

can be extended (with an improved rate)
to the case of
quadratic Lagrange finite elements which we will denote by
V_{h}^{2}
in this section.

Remark 6.1
.

Since due to Remark
3.5
for each
t\in(t_{1},T]
, the solution

v_{k}
to the semidiscrete problem (
9
)
satisfies
v_{k}(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, one can also show straightforwardly that

\displaystyle\Delta v_{k}(t),\ \partial_{t}\Delta v_{k}(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\ \text{ for all }t\in I_{m},\ m\geq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta^{2}v_{k}(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\ \text{ for all }t\in I_{m},\ m\geq 3.

Additional regularity is available on special domains, for example on rectangles,
right or equilateral triangles. We make the following assumption of the domain
\Omega
.

Assumption 1
.

For every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\Delta u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
there holds
u\in H^{3}(\Omega)
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C
independent of
u
such that

\|u\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\rm NewA}\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(50)

Example 6.2
.

This assumption holds for example on a rectangle, see
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
.
In this case the solution
u
to the elliptic equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f\quad\text{in}\ \Omega

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,

with
f\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
possesses the
H^{3}(\Omega)
regularity with the estimate

\|u\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\rm NewA}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

holds. Thus, Assumption
1
is satisfied in this case.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\Omega
satisfy Assumption
1
.

(1)

Let
u,\Delta u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and
\Delta^{2}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then there holds

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(51)

(2)

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a subdomain with
\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset\Omega
, let
u,\Delta u,\Delta^{2}u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and
\Delta^{3}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then there holds

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{5}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C\|\Delta^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta^{3}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(52)

Proof 6.4
.

(1)

For
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\Delta v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
one directly obtains

\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Due to
\Delta u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\Delta^{2}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
this inequality can be applied to
v=\Delta u
leading to

\|\nabla\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus, Assumption
1
implies the desired estimate.

(2)

Using a higher interior regularity result, see
[
8
, Chapter 6.3, Theorem 2]
,
we obtain

\|u\|_{H^{5}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(\|\Delta u\|_{H^{3}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Omega})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Since
\Delta^{2}u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\Delta^{3}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we can apply (
51
) to
\Delta u
leading to

\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta^{3}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This leads to

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{5}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C\|\Delta^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta^{3}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which proves the desired result by
\|\Delta^{j}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta^{j+1}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for
j=1,2
.

Complementing the standard error estimates for the Ritz projection in the
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
norms, under Assumption
1
,
we also have the following negative norm estimate. Note that even though no
H^{3}
regularity of the
solution
u
is used explicitly in the
estimates, the duality argument used to prove the result, requires the assumption to hold true for
any
H^{1}_{0}
right hand side.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and Assumption
1
hold true. Then it holds

\|u-R_{h}u\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{2}\|\nabla(u-R_{h}u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

If further
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, then it holds

\|u-R_{h}u\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{3}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{3}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof 6.6
.

The first estimate is proved by a duality argument in
[
4
, Theorem 5.8.3]
.
The second estimate then follows with the standard
H^{1}
error estimate and
H^{2}
regularity.

Under Assumption
1
we can establish the main results of this section.
We first consider again the case of
L^{2}
initial data. The extension to
v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)

then follows analogously to the case of linear finite elements.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
v
and
v_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
satisfy (
1
) and
(
20
), respectively. Then for any subdomain
\Omega_{0}
with
\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset\Omega
there holds

\|(v-v_{kh})(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(T,d)\left(\ell_{k}h^{3}+k^{2r+1}\right)\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(53)

where
\ell_{k}=\ln{\frac{T}{k}}
,
d=\dist(\Omega_{0},\partial\Omega)

and
C(T,d)
is a constant depending on
T
and
d
.

6.1.
Proof of Theorem
6.7

The exact dependence of the constant
C
on
T
and
d
is available in the proof of this result.
The rest of this section is devoted to the establishment of the above theorem.
The proof for the quadratic case is similar to the proof for the piecewise linear case, but requires some modifications. As it was done in
[
15
]
, we split the error as

(v-v_{kh})(T)=(v-v_{k})(T)+(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k})(T)+(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T):=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}.

(54)

The first time semidiscrete term
T_{1}
is already estimated in
[
15
, Theorem 3.8]
. The second
term
T_{2}
can again be estimated by the interior pointwise error estimates of

[
19
, Theorem 5.1]
,

\|(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k})(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C\|v_{k}(T)-\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{d})}+Cd^{-N/2}\|(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(55)

for any
\chi\in V^{2}_{h}
, where
\Omega_{d}
is a subdomain satisfying
\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset\Omega_{d}\subset\overline{\Omega}_{d}\subset\Omega

and
d=\dist(\Omega_{0},\partial\Omega_{d})
. We note that in contrast to the linear elements, in the above estimate the logarithmic term is not needed.
By the approximation theory and the Sobolev embedding

H^{5}(\Omega_{d})\hookrightarrow W^{3,\infty}(\Omega_{d})
(see e.g.

[
2
, Theorem 4.12]
),
Lemma
6.3
and the discrete parabolic smoothing result of Lemma

4.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{k}(T)-\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{d})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3}\|v_{k}(T)\|_{W^{3,\infty}(\Omega_{d})}\leq Ch^{3}\|v_{k}(T)\|_{H^{5}(\Omega_{d})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3}\|\Delta^{2}v_{k}(T)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta^{3}v_{k}(T)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq\frac{Ch^{3}}{T^{\frac{5}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The pollution term
\|(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
from (
55
), can be estimated using global elliptic estimates in
L^{2}
norm, Lemma
6.3
and
Lemma
4.10
as

\|(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{3}\|v_{k}(T)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{3}\|\Delta v_{k}(T)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta^{2}v_{k}(T)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq\ \frac{Ch^{3}}{T^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(56)

Thus,

\|(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k})(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(T,\Omega_{0})h^{3}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and it remains to estimate the last term
T_{3}
of (
54
).
As done in
[
15
, Lemmas 8.2–8.3]
, this will be achieved by estimating

\|(-\Delta_{h})^{j}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad j=0,1,

(57)

and the discrete Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
(
34
).
The proof of the above estimates was facilitated by the following technical lemma,
see
[
15
, Lemma 8.1]
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let

, let

and

satisfy (

)
and (

), respectively.
There exists a constant

independent of

,

, and

\|\Delta_{h}^{-1}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{2}\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In order to prove Theorem
6.7
we thus first extend Lemma

6.8
to quadratic finite elements in space, in order to estimate
the terms of (

).

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
v_{k}\in X_{k}^{r}
and
v_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
be the semidiscrete
and fully discrete solutions of (

) and
(
20
), respectively for

. Then there exists a constant

independent
of

\|\Delta_{h}^{-2}\left(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\right)(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{3}\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(58)

Proof 6.10
.

Let
z_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
be the solution to a dual problem with

z_{kh}(T)=\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)
, i.e.

B(\chi_{kh},z_{kh})=\left(\chi_{kh}(T),\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\right)\quad\text{for all}\quad\chi_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}.

Choosing
\chi_{kh}=\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})
,
and using the Galerkin orthogonality
(
21
) of
v_{k}
and
v_{kh}
, we obtain

\mathcal{Z}:=\|\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=B(\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}),z_{kh})=B(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh},\Delta_{h}^{-2}z_{kh}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=B(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{k},\Delta_{h}^{-2}z_{kh}).}

Note, that since
z_{kh}
is piecewise polynomial in time, with values in
V_{h}^{2}
,
for every
t\in I
, it holds
\Delta_{h}^{-2}z_{kh}(t)\in V_{h}^{2}
and for every

t\in I\setminus\{t_{0},t_{1},...,t_{M}\}
it holds
\partial_{t}\Delta_{h}^{-2}z_{kh}(t)\in V_{h}^{2}
.
Using the dual representation of
B
, given in (
15
), and
the definition
P_{h}
, all
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner products vanish, and it holds

\mathcal{Z}=(\nabla(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}),\nabla(\Delta_{h}^{-2}z_{kh}))_{I\times\Omega}.

In this inner product, we can replace
v_{k}
by its Ritz projection
R_{h}v_{k}
and obtain after
applying the definitions of
\Delta_{h}
,
P_{h}
and the duality pairing

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=(\nabla(P_{h}v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k}),\nabla(\Delta_{h}^{-2}z_{kh}))_{I\times\Omega}

\displaystyle=-(P_{h}v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k},\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh})_{I\times\Omega}=-(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k},\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh})_{I\times\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\|(v_{k}-R_{h}v_{k})(t)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\ dt.

The second term in the integral for each fixed
t
, can be estimated as follows,

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\nabla\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t),\nabla\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t)\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-C\left(z_{kh}(t),\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t)\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\|z_{kh}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|z_{kh}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

yielding
\|\Delta_{h}^{-1}z_{kh}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\|z_{kh}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for almost all
t
.
Using this estimate together with Lemma
6.5
, we get

\mathcal{Z}\leq Ch^{3}\int_{I}\|\Delta v_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z_{kh}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\ dt\leq Ch^{3}\|\Delta v_{k}\|_{L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\|z_{kh}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

Using Corollary
4.2
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3}\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z_{kh}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{3}\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Canceling
\|\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
gives the result.

Using this auxiliary result, we can prove the next lemmas estimating
R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}
.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
v_{k}\in X_{k}^{r}
and
v_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
be the semidiscrete and fully discrete
solutions of (

) and (

), respectively for

.
There exists a constant
C
independent of

,

, and

such that

\|\left(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\right)(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq Ch^{3}\left(\frac{1}{T^{2}}+\frac{1}{T^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof 6.12
.

Let
y_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
be the solution to a dual problem with

y_{kh}(T)=\left(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\right)(T)
, i.e
y_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}

satisfies

B(\varphi_{kh},y_{kh})=\left(\varphi_{kh}(T),(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\right)\quad\text{for all}\quad\varphi_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}.

To simplify notation, we define
\psi_{kh}:=R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
.
We introduce
\tilde{\psi}_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
to be zero on

I_{1}\cup...\cup I_{\tilde{m}}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{kh}=\psi_{kh}
on

I_{\tilde{m}+1}\cup...I_{M}
for
\tilde{m}
chosen such that

\frac{T}{2}\in I_{\tilde{m}}
. Analogously we define
\tilde{y}_{kh}
.
Choosing
\tilde{\psi}_{kh}
as test function in the definition of

y_{kh}
and transfering the
cutoff from one argument to the other, by (
22
), we get

\displaystyle\|\left(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\right)(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}

\displaystyle=B(\tilde{\psi}_{kh},y_{kh})

\displaystyle=B(\psi_{kh},\tilde{y}_{kh})+\left(\psi_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{-},y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=B(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh},\tilde{y}_{kh})+\left((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=B(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k},\tilde{y}_{kh})+\left((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}.

Here we also have used the Galerkin orthogonality (
21
) with respect
to the bilinear form
B
. By the definition of the Ritz projection the terms

\left(\nabla(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}),\nabla\tilde{y}_{kh}\right)_{I_{m}\times\Omega}

vanish from the form
B
, such that the remaining terms in
J_{1}
are

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=\tilde{m}+1}^{M}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k},\partial_{t}y_{kh})_{I_{m}\times\Omega}-\sum_{m=\tilde{m}+1}^{M}(R_{h}v_{k,m}^{-}-v_{k,m}^{-},[y_{kh}]_{m})-\left(R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}^{-}-v_{k,\tilde{m}}^{-},y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{\tilde{m}-1},T);{L^{2}(\Omega)})}\left(\|\partial_{t}y_{kh}\|_{L^{1}(I;{L^{2}(\Omega)})}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|[y_{kh}]_{m}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}+\|y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right),

Applying Corollary
4.2
, gives

\|\partial_{t}y_{kh}\|_{L^{1}(I;{L^{2}(\Omega)})}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|[y_{kh}]_{m}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}+\|y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

y_{kh}

y_{kh}(T)
as bound. Using the
L^{2}
error

\ \|(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k})(t)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq Ch^{3}\|v_{k}(t)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{3}\|\Delta v_{k}(t)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta^{2}v_{k}(t)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the smoothing results of Lemma

, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(t_{\tilde{m}-1},T]}\|(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k})(t)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3}\sup_{t\in(t_{\tilde{m}-1},T]}\|\Delta v_{k}(t)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta^{2}v_{k}(t)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(59)

\displaystyle\leq C\dfrac{h^{3}}{t_{\tilde{m}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\dfrac{h^{3}}{T^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

In the last step, we used the estimate

To estimate

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+})=((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{k,\tilde{m}})^{-},y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+})+((v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}):=J_{21}+J_{22}.

In the last step, we have used Lemma

t_{\tilde{m}}\leq\frac{T}{2}+k

and
\frac{T}{2k}>2
, yields

\ln\left(\frac{t_{\tilde{m}}}{k}\right)\leq\ln\left(\frac{\frac{T}{2}+k}{k}\right)=\ln\left(\frac{T}{2k}+1\right)\leq 2\ln\left(\frac{T}{2k}\right)\leq 2\ln\left(\frac{T}{k}\right).

This gives a bound for
J_{22}
, depending on the final time
T
,

J_{22}\leq C\frac{h^{3}}{T^{2}}\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Dividing all considered terms by
\|(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}
gives the
proposed estimate.

We now show a similar result for the discrete Laplacian.

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
v_{k}\in X_{k}^{r}
and
v_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
be the semidiscrete and fully
discrete solutions of (
9
) and (
20
),
respectively. Then there exists a constant independent of
k
,
h
, and
T
such that

\|\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq Ch^{3}\left(\frac{1}{T^{3}}+\frac{1}{T^{\frac{5}{2}}}\right)\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Proof 6.14
.

Let
y_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
be the solution to a dual problem with

y_{kh}(T)=\Delta_{h}\left(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\right)(T)
, i.e
y_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}

satisfies

B(\varphi_{kh},y_{kh})=\left(\varphi_{kh}(T),\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\right)\quad\text{for all}\quad\varphi_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}.

(60)

As in the previous lemma, in order to simplify notation,
we define
{\psi_{kh}:=R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}}
.
We introduce
\tilde{\psi}_{kh}\in X_{k,h}^{r,2}
to be zero on

I_{1}\cup...\cup I_{\tilde{m}}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{kh}=\psi_{kh}
on

I_{\tilde{m}+1}\cup...I_{M}
for
\tilde{m}
chosen such that

\frac{T}{2}\in I_{\tilde{m}}
. We define
\tilde{y}_{kh}
analogously.
Choosing
\Delta_{h}\tilde{\psi}_{kh}
as test function in the definition of
y_{kh}
,
and transfering the cutoff from the first argument of
B
to the second, by applying
(
22
), we get

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{h}\left(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh}\right)(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}

\displaystyle=B(\Delta_{h}\tilde{\psi}_{kh},y_{kh})

\displaystyle=B(\tilde{\psi}_{kh},\Delta_{h}y_{kh})

\displaystyle=B(\psi_{kh},\Delta_{h}\tilde{y}_{kh})+\left(\psi_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{-},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=B(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh},\Delta_{h}\tilde{y}_{kh})+\left((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=B(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k},\Delta_{h}\tilde{y}_{kh})+\left((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}.

Here we have used the Galerkin orthogonality (
21
)
with respect to the bilinear form
B
. By the definition of the Ritz projection the terms

\left(\nabla(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}),\nabla\Delta_{h}\tilde{y}_{kh}\right)_{I_{m}\times\Omega}

vanish from the form
B
, such that the remaining terms of
J_{1}
are

J_{1}=\sum_{m=\tilde{m}+1}^{M}(\partial_{t}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}),\Delta_{h}y_{kh})_{I_{m}\times\Omega}+\sum_{m=\tilde{m}+1}^{M}([R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}]_{m},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,m}^{+}).

Applying Hölder’s inequality in space and time gives

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq\|\partial_{t}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{\tilde{m}},T);{L^{2}(\Omega)})}\|\Delta_{h}y_{kh}\|_{L^{1}((t_{\tilde{m}},T);{L^{2}(\Omega)})}+\sum_{m=\tilde{m}+1}^{M}\|[R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}]_{m}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta_{h}y_{kh,m}^{+}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Introducing an artificial factor
1
as
k_{m}\cdot k_{m}^{-1}
in the sum allows us to extract
the term
\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\left\{k_{m}^{-1}\|[R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}]_{m}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right\}

out of the sum. This gives

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\|\partial_{t}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{m};{L^{2}(\Omega)})}\|\Delta_{h}y_{kh}\|_{L^{1}((t_{\tilde{m}},T);{L^{2}(\Omega)})}

\displaystyle+\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\left\{k_{m}^{-1}\|[R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k}]_{m}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right\}\left(\sum_{m=\tilde{m}+1}^{M}k_{m}\|\Delta_{h}y_{kh,m}^{+}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right).

Using Corollary
4.2
and the
L^{2}
error estimate for the Ritz projection for the other terms, gives the following estimate,

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq Ch^{3}\left(\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\|\partial_{t}v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{m};H^{3}(\Omega))}+\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\left\{k_{m}^{-1}\|[v_{k}]_{m}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega))}\right\}\right)\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Similar to the previous lemma, by the estimate (
51
) of Lemma
6.3

and Remark
6.1
, yielding
\partial_{t}\Delta v_{k}(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

for
t\in(t_{\tilde{m}-1},T]
, we obtain

\|\partial_{t}v_{k}(t)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\partial_{t}\Delta v_{k}(t)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\partial_{t}\Delta^{2}v_{k}(t)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Taking the supremum over
(t_{\tilde{m}-1},T]
and using Lemma
4.7
then yields

\displaystyle\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\|\partial_{t}v_{k}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{m};H^{3}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta v_{k}(t)\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}(I_{m};{L^{2}(\Omega)})}\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta^{2}v_{k}(t)\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}(I_{m};{L^{2}(\Omega)})}

\displaystyle\leq C\dfrac{1}{t_{\tilde{m}}^{\frac{5}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\dfrac{1}{T^{\frac{5}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Applying the same arguments, using
\Delta v_{k}(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for

t\in(t_{\tilde{m}-1},T)
, and Lemma
4.7
gives

\displaystyle\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\left\{k_{m}^{-1}\|[v_{k}]_{m}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\right\}\leq\max_{\tilde{m}\leq m\leq M}\left\{k_{m}^{-1}\left(\|\Delta[v_{k}]_{m}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta^{2}[v_{k}]_{m}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\leq C\dfrac{1}{t_{\tilde{m}}^{\frac{5}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\dfrac{1}{T^{\frac{5}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Summarizing all above results yields the final bound for
J_{1}
:

J_{1}\leq Ch^{3}\dfrac{1}{T^{\frac{5}{2}}}\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

To estimate
J_{2}
we insert an artificial zero like before by adding and subtracting

v_{k,\tilde{m}}^{-}
:

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+})=((R_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{k,\tilde{m}})^{-},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+})+((v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}):=J_{21}+J_{22}.

The term
J_{21}
can be estimated similarly to the previous lemma,
applying (
59
), the discrete smoothing result of Lemma

4.7
for
\|\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and using the special
choice of
\tilde{m}
:

\displaystyle J_{21}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in(t_{\tilde{m}-1},T)}\|(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{k})(t)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\dfrac{h^{3}}{t_{\tilde{m}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\frac{1}{T-t_{\tilde{m}}}\|y_{kh}(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\dfrac{h^{3}}{T^{\frac{5}{2}}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

We estimate
J_{22}
by replacing
v_{k,\tilde{m}}
with its
L^{2}
-projection:

\displaystyle J_{22}

\displaystyle=((P_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},\Delta_{h}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+})

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-},\Delta_{h}^{3}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta_{h}^{3}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\dfrac{h^{3}}{(T-t_{\tilde{m}})^{3}}\ln\left(\frac{t_{\tilde{m}}}{k}\right)\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

In the last step, we have used Lemma
6.9
for

\|\Delta_{h}^{-2}(P_{h}v_{k,\tilde{m}}-v_{kh,\tilde{m}})^{-}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

and the discrete smoothing result of Lemma
4.7
for

\|\Delta_{h}^{3}y_{kh,\tilde{m}}^{+}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}
. Since
y_{kh}
is the solution to a
problem backward in time, we use
\frac{1}{T-t_{\tilde{m}}}
instead of

\frac{1}{t_{\tilde{m}}}
in the application of this result.
Analogously to the previous lemma, we can replace the terms involving
t_{\tilde{m}}

by ones dependent only of
T
because of the special choice of
t_{\tilde{m}}
,
thus giving the final bound for
J_{22}
,

J_{22}\leq C\frac{h^{3}}{T^{3}}\ln{\frac{T}{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Dividing all considered terms by
\|\Delta_{h}(R_{h}v_{k}-v_{kh})(T)\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}
gives the
proposed estimate.

A General Regularized Distributed Solution for System State Estimation from Relative Measurements
Marco Fabris, Giulia Michieletto and Angelo Cenedese

Abstract
This work presents a novel general regularized distributed solution for the state estimation problem in networked systems. Resting on the graph-based representation of sensor networks and adopting a multivariate least-squares approach, the designed solution exploits the set of the available inter-sensor relative measurements and leverages a general regularization framework, whose parameter selection is shown to control the estimation procedure convergence performance. As confirmed by the numerical results, this new estimation scheme allows ( i ) (i) the extension of other approaches investigated in the literature and ( i ​ i ) (ii) the convergence optimization in correspondence to any (undirected) graph modeling the given sensor network.



I
Introduction

A
Sensor Network
(SN) can be generically defined as a system composed of multiple devices (sensor nodes) endowed with limited computation, sensing and communication capabilities, which interact in order to solve problems that are beyond the capacity and knowledge of each single element, accomplishing complex global tasks through the realization of simple local rules. Characterized by a cooperative nature, in the latest years, SNs have become a very popular enabling technology in several cutting-edge research areas, including, e.g., Internet-of-Things
[
1
]
, and Smart Sensing
[
2
]
.

A canonical as well as fundamental problem within this context consists in the
state estimation from relative measurements
(SERM), namely in the determination of the state of each sensor node composing the network, generally resting on the exploitation of a set of noisy relative measurements through a distributed approach
[
3
]
. This issue emerges as a general problem in many diverse SN applications, as, for instance, clock synchronization in wireless SNs, self-calibration of visual SNs, power state estimation in smart grids (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and the references therein).

Related works
-
Motivated by the pervasiveness of this task, many algorithms have been proposed over the years.
These mainly rely on filtering strategies (particle or extended Kalman filters), on maximum a posteriori or maximum likelihood estimation criteria, on the resolution of least-squares (LS) problems basing on consensus-based agreement protocols (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
and the references therein).

In particular, this latter optimization-based method allows to investigate the SERM algorithm performance in connection with the topological features of the SN, especially in relation to the quality and quantity of the available measurements.
In this direction, for instance, the convergence properties of a LS-based SERM algorithm are examined in
[
10
]
given that the relative measurements are characterized by heterogeneous and uncertain quality.
Similarly, in
[
11
]
, the estimation performance is studied in the light of the amount of the available measurements, also corrupted by a constant bias; different behavior are identified for bipartite and nonbipartite tolopogies.
This distinction emerges also in
[
12
]
, where a distributed parametric iterative SERM strategy is presented, and such method is similar to the solution proposed in
[
13
]
: both these algorithms involve a penalty parameter that acts as a regularization coefficient and allows to improve the convergence performance by prioritizing information sharing through certain network links.

Contributions
-
Inspired by the strategies in
[
12
,
13
]
, this work provides a comprehensive point on the SERM problem and, then, formalizes a
general regularized distributed solution
(GRDS) by broadening the groundwork already established in the field.
Indeed, both the consensus-like iterative schemes
\Sigma_{\eta}
and
\Sigma_{\rho}
devised in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
, respectively, can be revisited as specific cases of the here proposed GRDS framework.
In addition, this holds also for the well-known consensus-based scheme
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
detailed in
[
14
]
.
Finally, such reformulation allows the procedure performance optimization for any kind of connected undirected network.

Specifically, the purpose of this work is

(i)
to design a general framework encompassing the (linear) SERM problem and its convergence properties analysis;

(ii)
to extend the parametric regularized estimation approaches investigated in the literature, leading to improved versatility for the parameter selection through the definition of wider domains;

(iii)
to yield optimal solutions in order to compute the desired distributed estimates for any given topology.
This turns particularly beneficial for a class of small-world networks, previously lacking of an effective optimal tuning method.

- In this work,

\mathds{1}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{0}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the
n

\mathbf{I}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
identifies the identity matrix of dimension
n
. The sets
\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{I}_{0}
and
\mathcal{I}_{1}
correspond to the indexes collections
\{1\ldots n\}
,
\{0\ldots n-1\}
, and
\{1\ldots n-1\}
respectively.
Then, for any square matrix
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,

and
\varsigma_{\mathbf{M}}

\varsigma_{\mathbf{M}}=(\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{M}}+\lambda_{n-1}^{\mathbf{M}})/2
. The notation
\mathbf{M}\succeq 0
(
\mathbf{M}\succ 0
) implies that
\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{M}}\geq 0
(
\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{M}}>0
) for all
i\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
; if
\mathbf{M}

Given
\mathbf{M}_{1},\mathbf{M}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\mathbf{M}_{1}\succeq\mathbf{M}_{2}
stands for
\mathbf{M}_{1}-\mathbf{M}_{2}\succeq 0
. Finally,
\mathbf{M}=\diag\left(\mathbf{m}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

II
The SERM Problem

In this section, the SERM problem is formally stated after the introduction of the graph-based model for any given SN. Moreover, both the centralized and decentralized solution approaches derived in
[
12
,
13
]
are recalled.

II-A

Graph-based SN model

As from the literature,
a SN composed of
n\geq 2
devices can be modeled as a graph
\mathcal{G}=\left(\mathcal{V},\>\mathcal{E}\right)
wherein each element in the
nodes set

\mathcal{V}=\left\{v_{1}\ldots v_{n}\right\}
corresponds to a sensor node in the group, and the
edges set

\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
describes the nodes’ interplay in terms of unidirectional/bidirectional sensing and/or communication interactions. Hereafter, it is assumed that there exists the edge
e_{ij}=\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in\mathcal{E}
, i.e., nodes
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are adjacent, if and only if the
i
-th and
j
-th sensor nodes can sense each other and are able to reciprocally exchange information according to some predetermined communication protocol. This implies that the graph
\mathcal{G}
representing the given SN is
undirected
.
In addition, w.l.o.g., it is also assumed to be
connected
.

According to the graph-based SN model, the nodes interplay can be summarized through the
adjacency matrix

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that
[\mathbf{A}]_{ij}=1
, if the
i
-th and
j
-th nodes can interact;
[\mathbf{A}]_{ij}=0
, otherwise.
Thus, for each node
v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
, the set
\mathcal{N}_{i}=\left\{j\;|\;v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathcal{V},v_{j}\neq v_{i},e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
, namely its
neighborhood
, represents the set of nodes interacting with the
i
-th one.
The
degree

d_{i}=|\mathcal{N}_{i}|
of node
v_{i}
, then, corresponds to the
i
-th element of the main diagonal of the
degree matrix

\mathbf{D}=\diag(\mathbf{A}\mathds{1}_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
The latter is, in turns, involved in the definition of both the
Laplacian matrix

\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
normalized Laplacian matrix

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}=\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.

Finally,
d_{m}={\min}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\{d_{i}\}
and
d_{M}={\max}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\{d_{i}\}
identify the
minimum
and
maximum graph degree
, respectively, while
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{G})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}d_{i}
corresponds to the graph volume.
Note that, for
regular
graphs it holds that
d_{m}=d_{M}=c>0
, and furthermore
c=n-1
for
complete
graphs.

II-B

Problem statement

Given a SN modeled as in Section
II-A
, each
i
-th sensor node,
i\in\mathcal{I}
, is assumed to be characterized by a one-dimensional scalar attribute
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
identifying the
i
-th node state
and consisting in a measurable physical quantity, and by a set of
relative measurements

\mathcal{M}_{i}~=~\{\tilde{x}_{ij}~\in~\mathbb{R}\;|\;j\!\in\!\mathcal{N}_{i}\}
where the recorded
\tilde{x}_{ij}
corresponds to the difference between the neighbor state
x_{j}
and the sensing node state
x_{i}
corrupted by some noise.

In this scenario, the SERM problem consists in the determination of the set
\{\hat{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\forall i\in\mathcal{I}\}
that allows to best approximate the true system state
\{{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\forall i\in\mathcal{I}\}
and to be consistent with the set of the existing relative measurements. Such an issue can be solved resorting to the popular LS paradigm as formalized in the following.

Problem 1

Consider a
n
-nodes SN modeled by an undirected connected graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
and characterized by the set
\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
of relative measurements. Introducing the vectors
\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\scalebox{.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}\hat{x}_{1}\;\ldots\;\hat{x}_{n}\end{bmatrix}$}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbf{x}=\scalebox{.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\;\ldots\;x_{n}\end{bmatrix}$}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that identify respectively the system state estimate and the true system state, the SERM problem consists in the resolution of the following convex minimization

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\underset{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\arg\min}\;h(\mathbf{x}),

(1)

where the (convex) cost function
h(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is such that

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\textstyle\sum}_{i\in\mathcal{V}}{\textstyle\sum}_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}(x_{i}-x_{j}+\tilde{x}_{ij})^{2}.

(2)

II-C

Centralized vs distributed solution

As proven in
[
12
]
, the (minimum norm)
centralized solution

\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}_{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the minimization (
1
) is given by

\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}_{C}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}}.

(3)

with
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\scalebox{.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}\textstyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}(\tilde{x}_{j1}-\tilde{x}_{1j})\;\cdots\;\ \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}(\tilde{x}_{jn}-\tilde{x}_{nj})\end{bmatrix}$}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\notin\ker(\mathbf{L})\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}_{n}\right\}
, and
\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denoting the pseudo-inverse of the Laplacian matrix.
Note that the computation of (
3
) rests upon the knowledge of all the nodes interactions and all the relative measurements.

On the other hand,
the first order optimality condition
[\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}h(\hat{\mathbf{x}})]_{i}=0
involving the gradient
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}h(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of cost function (
2
) with respect to
\mathbf{x}
yields

\hat{x}_{i}=d_{i}^{-1}\left({\textstyle\sum}_{j\in{\cal N}_{i}}\hat{x}_{j}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\textstyle\sum}_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}(\tilde{x}_{ji}-\tilde{x}_{ij})\right).

(4)

The estimation of
i
-th sensor node state in (
4
) depends exclusively on the state estimate of its neighbors and on its set of relative measurements, thus suggesting the adoption of a distributed paradigm in the minimization (
1
). In this direction, the following discrete system can be derived from (
4
) as an update rule for
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
driven by the measurements:

\Sigma_{0}:\quad\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k+1)=\mathbf{F}_{0}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k)+\mathbf{u}_{0}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}),

(5)

where
\mathbf{F}_{0}=\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
correspond to the adjacency matrix and the measurements vector normalized by the degree matrix, respectively.
Note that, applying (
5
), the
i
-th sensor node estimate
\hat{x}_{i}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
at the
k
-th
step affects its neighbors’ estimate at the
(k+1)
-th step, but it is not considered for the recursive self-estimate.

As explained in
[
13
]
, the convergence behavior of the scheme
\Sigma_{0}
is determined by the second largest eigenvalue (in modulus) of the state matrix
\mathbf{F}_{0}
. Such a row-stochastic matrix has real spectrum

\Lambda^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}\!=\!\{\lambda^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}_{i}\in\left[-1,1\right]\;|\;1\!=\!\lambda^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}_{0}\!>\!\lambda^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}_{1}\!\geq\!\cdots\!\geq\!\lambda^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}_{n-1}\},

(6)

and this is related the (real) spectrum of normalized Laplacian matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}
, since

\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}=\{\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{i}=1-\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}\;|\;0=\lambda_{0}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}<\lambda_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}\}.

(7)

In detail, the system state estimation driven by (
5
) is guaranteed to converge toward the minimum norm solution (
3
) if
\lambda_{n-1}^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}\neq-1
, namely
\lambda_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}\neq 2
, and this condition is fulfilled if and only if the graph
\mathcal{G}
representing the SN is not bipartite
[
15
]
.
In the bipartite case, instead, the estimation convergence can be achieved only if it exists the possibility to act on the nodes interplay through
\mathbf{A}
or, alternatively, by means of the introduction of a regularization parameter.

III
A General Regularized Distributed Solution

Regularization techniques can in general be exploited to improve the convergence properties also in non-bipartite scenarios. In the light of this fact, a novel GRDS for the SERM problem is presented here, as a first original contribution.

Based on the introduction of some suitable weights acting as regularization parameters, the proposed iterative approach entails the recursive use of the self-estimate, opportunely combined with the neighbors estimate and the set of locally available relative measurements.
From a graph-based perspective, the designed solution implies the insertion of weighted self-loops in the topology modeling a given SN.

Formally, the
real regularization parameter

q_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is introduced, associated to the
i
-th sensor node,
\forall i\in\mathcal{I}
. Assuming
\mathcal{Q}=(-1,1)
and multiplying both sides of (
4
) by term
(1-q_{i})
it follows that

\hat{x}_{i}=q_{i}\hat{x}_{i}+\frac{1-q_{i}}{d_{i}}\left(\sum\limits_{j\in{\cal N}_{i}}\hat{x}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}(\tilde{x}_{ji}-\tilde{x}_{ij})\right).

(8)

The resulting (
8
) still optimizes
h(\mathbf{x})
in (
2
) and can be interpreted as a filter.
This leads to the definition of the following iterative update rule

\Sigma_{\mathbf{Q}}:\quad\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k+1)=\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k)+\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{Q}}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right),

(9)

where both the state matrix
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and the input vector
\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{Q}}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
depends on the (diagonal)
regularization matrix

\mathbf{Q}=\diag{\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
\mathbf{q}=\scalebox{.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\;\cdots\;q_{n}\end{bmatrix}$}^{\top}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Specifically, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathbf{Q}+(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{F}_{0},

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{Q}}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)=(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{u}_{0}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}).

(11)

Note that the regularized scheme
\Sigma_{\mathbf{Q}}
generalizes
\Sigma_{0}
: the update rules (
4
) and (
8
) coincide when
q_{i}=0
,
\forall i\in\mathcal{I}
.

III-A

Convergence properties

The convergence of the scheme
\Sigma_{\mathbf{Q}}
is affected by the spectral properties of the state matrix (
10
), which, in turns, depends on the regularization matrix
\mathbf{Q}
. In particular, the distributed update rule (
9
) is not ensured to be convergent for any choice of
\{q_{i}\in\mathcal{Q},i\in\mathcal{I}\}
as clarified in the following.

First, observe that, for any selection of
\mathbf{Q}
, the spectrum of the matrix
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}
includes the eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}^{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}}=1
. In addition, the following properties also hold.

Lemma 1

For any
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, all the eigenvalues of the corresponding matrix
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}
are real, i.e.,
\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}}\in\mathbb{R}

\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
.

Proof:

The spectrum of a matrix
\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is real if it exists a positive definite diagonal matrix
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

such that
\mathbf{M}\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}^{\top}\mathbf{M}
.
Therefore, since
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q})^{-1}\mathbf{D}\succ 0
for any choice of
\mathbf{Q}
, the thesis is proven selecting
\mathbf{M}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q})^{-1}\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}
.
∎

Lemma 2

For any
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, the eigenvalues of the corresponding matrix
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}
are bounded. In particular, it holds

{\lambda}_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\underline{q}}}\leq\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}}\leq{\lambda}_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\overline{q}}},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{i},

(12)

with
\mathbf{F}_{\underline{q}}=\underline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}+(1-\underline{q})\mathbf{F}_{0}
and
\mathbf{F}_{\overline{q}}=\overline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}+(1-\overline{q})\mathbf{F}_{0}
, given

\underline{q}=\min_{i\in\mathcal{I}}q_{i}
and
\overline{q}=\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}}q_{i}
.

Proof:

Considering the matrix
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}
as a function of
\mathbf{Q}
and exploiting the relation (
7
), the Gershgorin circle theorem
ensures that the condition
\overline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}\succeq\mathbf{Q}\succeq\underline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}
implies

\begin{cases}(\mathbf{Q}-\underline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n})(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{F}_{0})\succeq 0,\\
(\overline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{Q})(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{F}_{0})\succeq 0.\end{cases}

(13)

Then, adding
\mathbf{F}_{0}
to both sides of inequalities (
13
) and applying Lemma
1
, it follows that

\hskip-6.0pt\begin{cases}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathbf{Q}+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{F}_{0}\succeq\underline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}+(1-\underline{q})\mathbf{F}_{0}=\mathbf{F}_{\underline{q}},\\
\mathbf{F}_{\overline{q}}=\overline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}+(1-\overline{q})\mathbf{F}_{0}\succeq\mathbf{Q}+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{F}_{0}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}.\end{cases}

(14)

As a consequence, for any
\mathbf{Q}
, it holds that
\mathbf{F}_{\overline{q}}\succeq\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}\succeq\mathbf{F}_{\underline{q}}
and, thus, the spectral relation (
12
) is guaranteed.
∎

Then, next theorem shows that, adopting the scheme
\Sigma_{\mathbf{Q}}
, the system state estimate asymptotically converges towards the centralized solution (
3
) only for a suitable selection of the regularization parameters set that ensures some spectral properties of the state matrix
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

Theorem 1

If matrix
\mathbf{Q}
is selected so that for the corresponding matrix
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}
it results

|\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}}|<1,\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{1},

(15)

then the system state estimation driven by (
9
) asymptotically converges to the centralized solution (
3
) in terms of relative differences.
Specifically, under the given condition (
15
), it holds that
\hat{x}_{\mathbf{Q},i}^{\star}-\hat{x}_{\mathbf{Q},j}^{\star}=\hat{x}_{C,i}^{\star}-\hat{x}_{C,j}^{\star}
,
\forall i,j\in\mathcal{I}
such that
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}
, with
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{C}^{\star}=\scalebox{.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}\hat{x}_{C,1}^{\star}\;\cdots\;\hat{x}_{C,n}^{\star}\end{bmatrix}$}^{\top}
defined as in (
3
) and
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}_{\mathbf{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}_{\mathbf{Q}}=\scalebox{.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}\hat{x}_{\mathbf{Q},1}^{\star}\;\ldots\;\hat{x}_{\mathbf{Q},n}^{\star}\end{bmatrix}$}^{\top}=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k)
with
\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k)
evolving according to (
9
).

Proof:

Accounting for the scheme
\Sigma_{\mathbf{Q}}
, one can verify that, given the initial condition
\hat{\mathbf{x}}(0)=\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
, it holds

\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k)=\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{k}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\left(\textstyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{l}\right)\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{Q}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})

(16)

and, at the equilibrium, one has
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{eq}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{eq}+\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{Q}}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)
. Taking into account (
10
)-(
11
), this implies

2\mathbf{L}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{eq}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}.

(17)

In particular, condition (
17
) ensures that a generic equilibrium solution for the scheme
\Sigma_{\mathbf{Q}}
can be expressed as
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{eq}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}_{C}+\alpha{\mathds{1}}
, where
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}_{C}
is the minimum norm centralized solution (
3
), while
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{{\mathds{1}}\}=\ker(\mathbf{L})
.

Now, by the Perron-Frobenius theory, condition (
15
) is sufficient to ensure that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{k}={\mathds{1}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\top}
,
where
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

Then, under the equilibrium condition (

converges towards a unique

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(k)

\displaystyle=\left({\mathds{1}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\top}\right)\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\!+\!\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left((\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{k})\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{eq}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle=\left({\mathds{1}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\top}\right)\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\!+\!\left(\mathbf{I}_{n}-{\mathds{1}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\top}\right)\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{eq}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\star}

Regularization parameter selection

In the rest of the section, the attention is focused on the conditions on the set of parameters

In this direction, in the light of Lemma

\mathbf{Q}=q\mathbf{I}_{n}
(with

\Sigma_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

Theorem 2

Condition (
15
) in Theorem
1
is satisfied for any diagonal matrix
\mathbf{Q}
defined by selecting the
q_{i},\forall i\in~\mathcal{I}

q_{i}\in\widecheck{\mathcal{Q}}=\left(\mu,1\right),\;\;\mu=1-\frac{2}{\lambda_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}}\in\left[-1+\frac{2}{n},0\right],

(22)

where
\lambda_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}
is the largest eigenvalue of the normalized Laplacian, and
\mu=-1+2/n
and
\mu=0
hold for the complete and bipartite graphs, respectively.

Proof:

Based on (
12
), condition (
15
) is fulfilled only if
\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\underline{q}}}>-1
and
\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\overline{q}}}<1
,
\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
. In the limit case
\mathbf{Q}=\overline{q}\mathbf{I}_{n}

\overline{q}<1

\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{\underline{q}}}=\underline{q}+(1-\underline{q})\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}>-1

that, given (

\underline{q}>\mu=(\lambda^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}_{n-1}+1)/(\lambda^{\mathbf{F}_{0}}_{n-1}-1)=1-2/\lambda_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}.

To conclude, the parameter

IV-A

Discussion over graph topologies with
\varsigma_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}<1

The property
\varsigma_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}<1
is not characterizing a unique class of graphs, however it suggests some interesting considerations on a variety of topologies.
Indeed,
for graphs whose eigenvalues can be computed in closed form, the above condition can be verified straightforwardly. For example, it is possible to assess that for any circulant graph
C_{n}(1,2)
wherein
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1}\ldots v_{n}\}

More in general,

Specifically,
it holds that

\diamond

\lambda_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}\leq 2C_{\mathcal{G}}
, where
C_{\mathcal{G}}=\min_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\frac{|\bar{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathcal{G}_{1})|}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{G}_{0}),\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{G}_{1})\}}
is the
Cheeger constant
of the graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
.
C_{\mathcal{G}}
is defined accounting for the subgraph
\mathcal{G}_{0}=(\mathcal{V}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0})
of
\mathcal{G}
and its complement
\mathcal{G}_{1}=(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1}),\mathcal{V}_{1}=\mathcal{V}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{0}
, through the cardinality of their cut set
\bar{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathcal{G}_{1})=\{(v_{i},v_{j})\in\mathcal{E}\;|\;v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{0},v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{1}\}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
and their volume
[
15

\diamond

\lambda_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}\leq 2(1-H_{\mathcal{G}})

H_{\mathcal{G}}=\min_{\mathcal{H}}\frac{|\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap\mathcal{N}_{j}|}{2\max\{d_{i},d_{j}\}}

\mathcal{H}=\{i,j\in\mathcal{I}\;|\;i<j,(v_{i},v_{j})\in\mathcal{E}\}

[
16
]
.

It follows by simple substitution that the property
\varsigma_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}<1
is ensured for all the topologies such that
C_{\mathcal{G}}<H_{\mathcal{G}}

VI
Concluding Remarks

This paper addresses the SERM problem in a regularized LS minimization framework proposing a GRDS approach that encompasses the state-of-the-art solutions
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
via the introduction of the regularization matrix
\mathbf{Q}
.
A (sufficient) condition on the
\mathbf{Q}
selection is stated ensuring the convergence of the system state estimation towards the centralized solution.
Numerical results highlight the strengths of the novel GRDS framework with respect to the convergence performance, especially in the case of SNs modeled by graphs having
\varsigma_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}<1
.
Possible future research directions involve, for instance, the introduction of some weights
w_{ij}\geq 0
in the cost function (
2
): these may represent the reliability on the communication link between the
i
-th and the
j
-th node or the trust/confidence level on the measurement
\tilde{x}_{ij}
. In the former case, it is reasonable to assume
w_{ij}=w_{ji},\forall e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}
and the work outcome are still valid with minor modifications, including the use of weighted Laplacian matrix. In the latter case, it will be necessary to account for directed graphs.

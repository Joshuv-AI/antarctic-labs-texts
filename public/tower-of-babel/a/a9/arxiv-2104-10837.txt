Robust Certification for Laplace Learning on Geometric Graphs
Matthew Thorpe, Bao Wang

Abstract
Graph Laplacian (GL)-based semi-supervised learning is one of the most used approaches for classifying nodes in a graph. Understanding and certifying the adversarial robustness of machine learning (ML) algorithms has attracted large amounts of attention from different research communities due to its crucial importance in many security-critical applied domains. There is great interest in the theoretical certification of adversarial robustness for popular ML algorithms. In this paper, we provide the first adversarial robust certification for the GL classifier. More precisely we quantitatively bound the difference in the classification accuracy of the GL classifier before and after an adversarial attack. Numerically, we validate our theoretical certification results and show that leveraging existing adversarial defenses for the k k -nearest neighbor classifier can remarkably improve the robustness of the GL classifier.

Introduction

Let
\Omega_{N}:=\{{\bm{x}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a set of feature vectors with a subset of
\Gamma_{N}:=\{{\bm{x}}_{i}\}_{i\in Z_{N}\subset[N]}

being labeled. If

then
{\bm{x}}_{i}
is labeled
\ell({\bm{x}}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}
and we denote
\ell_{N}:=\ell|_{\Gamma_{N}}
.
The Graph Laplacian (GL) framework encodes the geometry of the feature vectors
\Omega_{N}
by constructing an undirected graph,
G_{N}=(\Omega_{N},{\mathbf{W}}_{N})
, where
\Omega_{N}
forms the nodes of the graph and
{\mathbf{W}}_{N}:=({\mathbf{W}}_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}})_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in\Omega_{N}}
is the set of edge weights with
{\mathbf{W}}_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}
being the weight of the edge between
{\bm{x}}
and
{\bm{y}}
. The graph Dirichlet energy is defined by

{\mathcal{E}(u;\Omega_{N})=\sum_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in\Omega_{N}}{\mathbf{W}}_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}(u({\bm{x}})-u({\bm{y}}))^{2},}

where
u
is a function defined on the nodes
\Omega_{N}
of the graph.
We can then predict the label for the unlabeled data by solving the following constrained energy minimization problem

{\text{minimize }\mathcal{E}(u;\Omega_{N})\text{ over }u:\Omega_{N}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ subject to }u({\bm{x}})=\ell_{N}({\bm{x}})\,\forall{\bm{x}}\in\Gamma_{N}.}

(1)

Laplacian regression is the solution to (
1

To go from regression to (binary) classification one thresholds

\{0,1\}
then the GL classifier predicts the label

1

0

Note that the GL classifier classifies any unlabeled data leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data. As a comparison, for any unlabeled
{\bm{x}}

k
-nearest neighbor (
k

{\bm{x}}

2.1
Preliminaries and Assumptions

To certify the robustness of the GL classifier, we make the following assumptions on the dataset:

(A1)

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open connected and bounded with Lipschitz boundary;

(A2)

{\bm{x}}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
where
\mu
has density
\rho\in\textrm{{C}}^{2}(\Omega)
that is bounded below by a positive constant, i.e.
\inf_{{\bm{x}}\in\Omega}\rho({\bm{x}})=:\rho_{\min}>0
;

(A3)

\mathbb{P}({\bm{x}}\in\Gamma_{N}|{\bm{x}}\in\Omega_{N})=\mathbb{P}(i\in Z_{N}|{\bm{x}}_{i}\in\Omega_{n})=\beta
and if
{\bm{x}}\in\Gamma_{N}
, then
{\bm{x}}
is labeled as
\ell({\bm{x}})
for a Lipschitz function
\ell:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
.

It will be convenient to define
\ell_{N}=\ell|_{\Gamma_{N}}
. Note that
\beta
is the probability of a data point being labeled and so (in the notation of Table
1
) we can make the formal association
\beta\sim(N-M)/N
.
For convenience, we introduce the following constrained graph Dirichlet energy functional:

{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{con}}(u;D_{N})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{E}(u;\Omega_{N})&\text{if }u({\bm{x}})=\ell_{N}({\bm{x}})\,\forall{\bm{x}}\in\Gamma_{N}\\
+\infty&\text{else.}\end{array}\right.}

The Euler-Lagrange equation corresponding to minimizing
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{con}}(\cdot;D_{N})
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N}(u;\Omega_{N})({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{for }{\bm{x}}\in\Omega_{N}\setminus\Gamma_{N}

\displaystyle u({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\ell_{N}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\text{for }{\bm{x}}\in\Gamma_{N},

where
\mathcal{L}_{N}(\cdot;\Omega_{N})
is the graph Laplacian defined by

{\mathcal{L}_{N}(u;\Omega_{N})({\bm{x}})=\sum_{{\bm{y}}\in\Omega_{N}}{\mathbf{W}}_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}\left(u({\bm{x}})-u({\bm{y}})\right).}

We have made explicit the dependence of the domain
\Omega_{N}
on the functionals
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{con}}
and the operator
\mathcal{L}_{N}
. Although this notation may feel cumbersome at this stage, it will aid clarity when we have two sets of data; the original dataset
\Omega_{N}
and the (adversarially-) perturbed dataset
\hat{\Omega}_{N}
.

We will consider
Geometric Random
graphs.
This construction involves weighting edges between all pairs of nodes as a function of the distance between nodes (and we say there is no edge between two nodes if the edge weight is zero).
We use a parameter
\varepsilon
, which is often chosen relative to
N
, to control the length scale in the graph.
This is summarised below:

(A4)

{\mathbf{W}}_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}={\mathbf{W}}_{\varepsilon,{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}
where

{\mathbf{W}}_{\varepsilon,{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}=\eta_{\varepsilon}(|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}|)
and
\eta_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\eta(\cdot/\varepsilon)
and
\eta:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is non-increasing, positive,
\eta(t)\geq 1
for all
t\leq 1
and
\eta(t)=0
for all
t\geq 2
.
In addition, either
\eta
is Lipschitz continuous, or
\eta(t)=\mathds{1}_{t\leq 1}
.

We note that whilst we use the geometric random graph construction in (A4) and we use the
k
NN graph in our experiments. The parameters
k
and
\varepsilon
are related as follows
k\sim N\varepsilon^{d}
(cf Lemma
). There are additional technical challenges when addressing the
k
NN constructions, however, we believe our results carry through to this setting (see also Remark
3
below).

The assumptions in (A4) allow us to bound the degrees of nodes and, letting
\hat{{\bm{x}}},\hat{{\bm{y}}}
be the adversarial perturbations of
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}
, show that either (i)
{\mathbf{W}}_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}
is always close to
{\mathbf{W}}_{\hat{{\bm{x}}},\hat{{\bm{y}}}}
(when
\eta
is Lipschitz) or (ii) we can control the number of
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}
such that
{\mathbf{W}}_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}
is not close to
{\mathbf{W}}_{\hat{{\bm{x}}},\hat{{\bm{y}}}}
(when
\eta=\mathds{1}_{\cdot\leq 1}
).

2.2
Robustness of Semi-Supervised Learning with Graph Laplacian

In this subsection, we give a theoretical bound of the following question:
how is the classification estimate affected if an adversary replaces the clean dataset
D_{N}=(\Omega_{N},\ell_{N})
with a new, corrupted, dataset
\hat{D}_{N}=(\hat{\Omega}_{N},\hat{\ell}_{N})
?
Following
wang2017analyzing
, we assume that the adversary can corrupt features by adding a small perturbation to the unlabeled data; the question of robustness under poisoning attacks
(
dalvi2004adversarial
;
lowd2005good
)
is an interesting question we leave open.
We assume the adversary can corrupt the unlabeled data by moving each point a maximum distance of
r
in
\ell_{2}
-norm. That is, the adversary can replace the set
\Omega_{N}
with a corrupted dataset
\hat{\Omega}_{N}
by, for each
i=1,\dots,N
, choosing
\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{i}\in B({\bm{x}}_{i},r)
thus defining
\hat{\Omega}_{N}=\{\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Here, and in the sequel,
\hat{{\bm{x}}},\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{i}
is understood to be a perturbation of
{\bm{x}},{\bm{x}}_{i}
, respectively. Although the labels are not perturbed, the domain of the labeling function
\ell_{N}
is, i.e. the perturbed domain is
\hat{\Gamma}_{N}=\{\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{i}\}_{\{i\,:\,x_{i}\in\Gamma_{N}\}}
, and so we define
\hat{\ell}_{N}:\hat{\Gamma}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\hat{\ell}_{N}(\hat{{\bm{x}}})=\ell_{N}({\bm{x}})
for all
\hat{{\bm{x}}}\in\hat{\Gamma}_{N}
.
Note that
\hat{\ell}_{N}(\hat{{\bm{x}}})=\ell_{N}({\bm{x}})
is precisely the condition that the adversary doesn’t corrupt labels.

A
learning strategy
is a map from the dataset
D_{N}=(\Omega_{N},\ell_{N})
to a function
u:\Omega_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
. For example, in the previous section we defined the learning strategy

{D_{N}=(\Omega_{N},\ell_{N})\mapsto u(\cdot;D_{N}):=\mathrm{argmin}_{u:\Omega_{N}\to\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{con}}(\cdot;D_{N}).}

(2)

This is the learning strategy we will analyse.

Given a dataset
D_{N}=(\Omega_{N},\ell_{N})
and a perturbation
\hat{D}_{N}=(\hat{\Omega}_{N},\hat{\ell}_{N})
we will compare
u(\cdot;D_{N})
with
u(\cdot;\hat{D}_{N})
by
|u({\bm{x}};D_{N})-u(\hat{{\bm{x}}};\hat{D}_{N})|
. The
\textrm{{L}}^{\infty}

distance between
u(\cdot;D_{N})
and
u(\cdot;\hat{D}_{N})
can be defined as
\max_{{\bm{x}}\in\Omega_{N}}|u({\bm{x}};D_{N})-u(\hat{{\bm{x}}};\hat{D}_{N})|
.

We let
\delta>0
be a prescribed tolerance then the robustness radius is the smallest
r
such that it is possible to perturb
u(\cdot;D_{N})
by more than
\delta
. More precisely, we define the
\delta
-robustness radius below which is a modification of the robustness radius in
(
wang2017analyzing
)
.

Definition 1
.

\delta
-Robustness Radius.

Let
D_{N}\mapsto u(\cdot;D_{N})
be a learning strategy.
The
\delta
-robustness radius
{\mathcal{R}}_{\delta}(\Omega^{\prime},u,D_{N})
of
u
over a subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
given the data
D_{N}
is the smallest radius
r
such that
\sup_{{\bm{x}}\in\Omega_{N}}|u({\bm{x}};D_{N})-u(\hat{{\bm{x}}};\hat{D}_{N})|>\delta
where
|\hat{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}}|<r
for all
{\bm{x}}\in\Omega^{\prime}
, i.e.

{{\mathcal{R}}_{\delta}(\Omega^{\prime},u,D_{N})=\inf_{r>0}\left\{\forall{\bm{x}}_{i}\in\Omega_{N}\cap\Omega^{\prime}\,\exists\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{i}\in B({\bm{x}}_{i},r)\text{ s.t. }\sup_{{\bm{x}}\in\Omega_{N}}|u({\bm{x}};D_{N})-u(\hat{{\bm{x}}};\hat{D}_{N})|>\delta\right\}.}

We prove
\delta
-robustness over
\Omega^{\prime}
in order to avoid problems at the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
In particular, we take
\Omega^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
is sufficiently large.
We believe our arguments can be extended to the boundary but the techniques to do so are more involved and will involve estimates between the GL and its continuum analogue at the boundary.
In particular, our proof uses a bound between the graph Laplacian and its continuum analogue, for which there are quantitative bounds away from the boundary, e.g.
singer06
;
calder18
.
Near the boundary the bound between the graph Laplacian and its continuum counterpart deteriorates to
O(1)
, i.e. there are currently no established rates of convergence close to the boundary, see
calder20AAA
.

Our main theoretical results are the following, the proofs can be found in Appendix
.

Theorem 2
.

\delta
-Robustness of GL-based
Regression.

Under Assumptions (A1-A4)
define

by (

).
There exists constants

,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,

such that if
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,

Remark 3
.

The comparison with

NN given in Table

can be derived from the above theorem as follows.
With probability at least

{\mathbf{W}}_{N,k,{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}}=\frac{N}{k}\mathds{1}_{{\bm{x}}\sim_{k}{\bm{y}}}

Remark 4
.

Theorem

shows the
\delta

We can restate this in terms of the number of labels,

Typically, one uses Laplacian regularisation for labeling by projecting the solution

of (

For simplicity we consider the binary classification problem, that is we seek a function

As is common, we define

{v({\bm{x}};D_{N})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if }u({\bm{x}};D_{N})\geq\frac{1}{2}\\

.

Let
\delta
be given by (

In addition to the assumptions of Theorem

{\sup_{\xi>0}\frac{1}{\xi}\mathrm{Vol}\left(\left\{{\bm{x}}\,:\,\frac{1}{2}-\xi\leq\ell({\bm{x}})\leq\frac{1}{2}+\xi\right\}\right)\leq A,}

(4)

The classification decision boundary is

When

Our proof shows that if

then we can consider the perturbed data points
\hat{\Omega}_{N}=\{\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
to be close to iid and hence apply the result to infer a high probability bound between

and

Theorem 6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2
we have that
\max_{\hat{{\bm{x}}}\in\hat{\Omega}_{N}}|u(\hat{{\bm{x}}};\hat{D}_{N})-\ell({\bm{x}})|\leq\delta

Theorem

quantifies the robustness of GL regression. More precisely, it establishes an upper bound of the maximum allowed adversarial perturbation under which the GL solution,

, of (

) is close to the ground truth label function

we infer the robustness of GL classification.

3
Computational Complexity Analysis

We ignore the common pre-processing time for both
k
NN and GL.
The computational complexity of
k
NN is dominated by nearest neighbor searching,
and the total computational complexity of searching for the nearest neighbors for all unlabeled points is
O(kM\log(N-M))

(
muja2014scalable
)
.
For the GL classifier, if we use the top
k(k\ll N)
-nearest neighbors, the total computational complexity for constructing the weight matrix would be
O(kN\log N)
. The additional computational complexity of GL comes from solving a sparse linear system of the size
N\times N
, which can be solved by using the conjugate gradient method in
O(Nk\sqrt{\kappa})
time, with
\kappa
being the condition number of

On Generators of the Hardy and the Bergman Spaces
Valentin V. Andreev, Miron B. Bekker, Joseph A. Cima

Abstract
A function φ \varphi which is analytic and bounded in the unit disk 𝔻 {\mathbb{D}} is called a generator for the Hardy space H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}) or the Bergman space A 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}) if polynomials in φ \varphi are dense in the corresponding space. We characterize generators in terms of φ − \varphi- invariant subspaces which are also z − z- invariant and study wandering properties of such subspaces. Density of bounded analytic functions in the φ − \varphi- invariant subspaces is also investigated.



Keywords:
Hardy space, Bergman space, generator, invariant subspace.

MSC2010: 46E20, 30H10, 30H20

Introduction

It is well known that any (closed) subspace of the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
or the Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
which is invariant under multiplication by
z
(the
z-
invariant subspace) is also an invariant subspace of any bounded analytic Toeplitz operator. The converse is not in general true.

In this article we consider analytic Toeplitz operators
M_{\varphi}
of multiplication by
\varphi
on the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
and the Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
with the property that the function which is a constant (e.g.
f(z)\equiv 1
) is a cyclic vector for such operators. Such a function
\varphi
we call a generator. We characterize such generators in terms of
M_{\varphi}-
invariant subspaces which are also
z-
invariant.

The problem of determining whether a given bounded analytic function in the the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
is a generator is very hard. Some important results in this direction in the case of Hardy space were obtained in the paper by B. M. Solomyak
[
10
]
.

In
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
Donald Sarason introduced a notion of a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
. We show that each weak
∗
generator is a generator in our sense. If
\psi
is a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
then
\psi-
invariant subspaces have some additional property, see Theorem
7
below.

Weak
∗
generators often occur in the process of studying wandering property of invariant subspaces of analytic Toeplitz operators.
In this article we also consider this question for the case when the symbol of the Toeplitz operator is a generator, see Theorem
3
.

The condition that
H^{\infty}\cap\EuFrak{M}
is dense in the invariant subspace
\EuFrak{M}
plays the crucial role in our work. In Section 3 we study this condition in the case of invariant subspaces of the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
and the Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
.

2
Generators

In what follows
{\mathbb{D}}
denotes the unit disk and
\EuFrak{H}
denotes either Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
or Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
.

Definition 1
.

A function
\varphi\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
is called a generator for
\EuFrak{H}
if polynomials in
\varphi
are dense in
\EuFrak{H}
, i.e.

\overline{l.h.\{\varphi^{n}:n=0,1,\ldots\}}=\EuFrak{H}

(1)

where
l.h.
means linear hull and bar means closure in the norm of
\EuFrak{H}
.

Condition (
1
) implies that a vector
{\mathbf{1}}
(the constant function
1
) is a cyclic vector for the analytic Toeplitz operator
M_{\varphi}
of multiplication by
\varphi
.

Since in a locally convex space the weak closure of a subspace coincides with its norm closure, a function
\varphi
is a generator if polynomials in
\varphi
are weakly dense in
\EuFrak{H}
.

Clearly,
\varphi
is univalent. Indeed, if
\varphi(z_{1})=\varphi(z_{2})
,
z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
, then

P(\varphi(z_{1}))=P(\varphi(z_{2}))
for any polynomial
P
. Consequently,
f(z_{1})=f(z_{2})
for any
f\in\overline{l.h.\{\varphi^{n}:n=0,1,\ldots\}}
which contradicts (
1
).

A notion of a generator is closely related to the notion of weak
∗
generator introduced by D. Sarason in
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
. A function

\psi\in H^{\infty}
is called a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
if polynomials in
\psi
are dense in the weak-star topology of
H^{\infty}
.

Theorem 1
.

1.

Every weak
∗
generator is a generator in the sense of Definition
1
.

2.

There exist a bounded univalent function
\varphi
which is a generator in the sense of Definition
1
but is not a weak
∗
generator.

Proof.

1.
Recall that the space
H^{\infty}
is the dual to the quotient space
L^{1}/H^{1}_{0}
,
H^{\infty}=(L^{1}/H_{0}^{1})^{*}
. A local basis of the weak
∗
topology on
H^{\infty}
at
h_{0}\in H^{\infty}

is formed by the following sets

\left\{h\in H^{\infty}:|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}[h(e^{it})-h_{0}(e^{it})]g_{j}(e^{it})dt|<\epsilon,j=1,2,\ldots,n,g_{j}\in L^{1}\right\}

(2)

Let
\psi
be a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
. Then any set of the form (
2
) contains a polynomial in
\psi
for any
h_{0}\in H^{\infty}
. Taking
g_{j}=\bar{f_{j}}
,
f_{j}\in H^{1}
,
we obtain that
for any
h_{0}\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
, for any
\epsilon>0
, and for any
f_{j}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{D}})
,
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, there is a polynomial
P(\psi)
in
\psi
such that

|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left[P(\psi(e^{it}))-h_{0}(e^{it})\right]\overline{f_{j}(e^{it})}dt|<\epsilon,\quad j=1.2.\ldots,n.

(3)

Since
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})\subset H^{1}({\mathbb{D}})
we take
f_{j}\in H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
and (
3
) gives

|<P(\psi)-h_{0},f_{j}>_{H^{2}}|<\epsilon,

that is the weak closure of polynomials in
\psi
contains all
H^{\infty}
. In particular that closure contains all polynomials in
z
. Therefore weak closure of polynomials in
\psi
coincides with
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
, hence the closure of polynomials in
\psi
in
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
-norm is
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
.
Consequently any weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
is a generator of
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
.

2.
Let
\varphi
be a bounded univalent function and put
\Omega=\varphi({\mathbb{D}})
. It is easily seen that the function
\varphi
is a generator of
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
if and only if the polynomials are dense in
H^{2}(\Omega)
in the norm of
H^{2}(\Omega)
. In
[
1
]
J. Akeroyd constructed a bounded simply connected domain

such that the polynomials are dense in
H^{2}(\Omega)
. At the same time the mapping function
\varphi
is not a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
. This statement follows from the fact that Akeroyd’s domain is a subset of the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
and the following result of D. Sarason (see [8], Corollary 2, page 527):

The function
\varphi
fails to be a weak
∗
generator if there is a domain
B
containing
G
properly such that

\sup\limits_{z\in B}|f(z)|=\sup\limits_{z\in G}|f(z)|.

\Box

Remark 1
.

Using similar arguments one can show that if
\psi
is a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
, then the of the set of polynomials in
\psi
is dense in
H^{p}
for any
1<p<\infty
.

In the case of the Bergman space we note that the space
(A^{1}({\mathbb{D}}))^{*}
properly contains
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
. Let
\tau
be the topology on
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
defined by the family of seminorms

p_{f}(\psi)=|\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}\psi(z)\overline{f(\zeta)}d\sigma(z)|,\qquad\psi\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}}),\quad f\in A^{1}({\mathbb{D}}),

(4)

where
\sigma
is the normalized Lebesque measure of the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
. A function
\psi\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
is called a
\tau
-generator of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
if polynomials in
\psi
are dense in
H^{\infty}
in
\tau-
topology.
Clearly a
\tau-
generator of
H^{\infty}
is the Bergman space version of a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
.

Applying the same arguments as above one concludes that every
\tau-
generator of
H^{\infty}
is a generator of the Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
in the sense of Definition
1
. Suppose now that
\varphi
is a generator of
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
in the sense
of Definition
1
. Therefore polynomials in
\varphi
are dense in
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
in the Bergman norm. In particular, polynomials in
\varphi
are dense in
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
in the weak topology. This means that for any
\epsilon>0
and any
h_{0}\in H^{\infty}
, and for any finite set of functions
f_{j}\in A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
there is a polynomial
P
such that

|\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}(P(\varphi(z))-h(z))\overline{f_{j}(z)}d\sigma(z)|<\epsilon.

Since
A^{1}({\mathbb{D}})
contains functions that do not belong to
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
,

\varphi
is not a
\tau-
generator of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
.

A simple example of a generator for the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
and the Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
is a function

\varphi(z)=\frac{z_{0}-z}{1-\bar{z_{0}}z},\quad|z_{0}|<1.

The statement that
\varphi
is a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
follows from the facts that
\varphi
is a conformal mapping
of the unit disk onto itslef and that the unit disk is a Caratheodory domain. Direct proof of the fact that the linear hull of the functions
\varphi^{k}(z)
,
k=0,1,\ldots
is dense in
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
is also simple. Interesting examples of weak
∗
generators, related to close-to-convex mapping are discussed in our article
[
3
]
.

For the case of the Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
we need to show that from

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{D}}}f(z)\overline{\left(\frac{z_{0}-z}{1-\bar{z_{0}}z}\right)^{k}}dA(z)=0,\quad f\in A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}),\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

it follows that
f=0
. After the change of variable
\zeta=\varphi(z)
the last condition takes the form

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{D}}}g(\zeta)\overline{\frac{\zeta^{k}}{(1-\bar{z_{0}}\zeta)^{2}}}dA(\zeta)=0,\quad k=0,1,\ldots,

where
g(\zeta)=-f(\varphi(\zeta))\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)\in A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
and
\|f\|=\|g\|
, that is for any polynomial

P(\zeta)

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{D}}}g(\zeta)\overline{\frac{P(\zeta)}{(1-\bar{z_{0}}\zeta)^{2}}}dA(\zeta)=0,\quad k=0,1,\ldots.

Taking
P(\zeta)=\zeta^{k}(1-\bar{z_{0}}\zeta)^{2}
one concludes that
g
is orthogonal to
\zeta^{k}
for
k=0,1,\ldots
. Hence
g=0
and, consequently,
f=0
.

A (closed) subspace of
\EuFrak{H}
is called
\varphi
-invariant if it is invariant under the operator
M_{\varphi}
. We also denote by
Lat_{\EuFrak{H}}(\varphi)
the lattice of
\varphi
-invariant subspaces of
\EuFrak{H}
.

For a set
S\subset\EuFrak{H}
we denote
\left[S\right]_{\varphi}
the smallest
\varphi-
invariant subspace containing
S
. Similarly,
\left[S\right]_{z}
is the smallest
z-
invariant subspace containing
S
.

The next theorem gives a characterization of a generator in terms of its invariant subspaces.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varphi
be a bounded univalent function in the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
. In order for
\varphi
to be a generator it is necessary that every
\varphi-
invariant subspace
\EuFrak{M}\subset\EuFrak{H}
such that
{\overline{\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}}}=\EuFrak{M}
is also
z-
invariant, and sufficient that the invariant subspace

[\mathbf{1}]_{\varphi}
be
z-
invariant.

Proof.
At first we recall that a Toeplitz operator
T_{g}
with symbol
g
on
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
(
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
) is defined by the formula

T_{g}h=P(gh)

where
P
is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
onto
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
(from
L^{2}({\mathbb{D}},dA)
onto
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
). If
g\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
then the operator
T_{g}
is bounded.

Suppose
\varphi
is a generator,
f\in\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}
and
h\in\EuFrak{M}^{\perp}
. Since
\EuFrak{M}
is
\varphi-
invariant,
P(\varphi)f\in\EuFrak{M}
for any polynomial

P
, that is

<P(\varphi)f,h>=0.

The left side of the last equality can be written as
<P(\varphi),\bar{f}h>=<P(\varphi),T_{\bar{f}}h>
, where
T_{\bar{f}}
is a Toeplitz operator on
\EuFrak{H}
. Since
f\in H^{\infty}
the operator
T_{\bar{f}}
is bounded and
T_{\bar{f}}h\in\EuFrak{H}
. Thus we have

0=<P(\varphi)f,h>=<P(\varphi),T_{\bar{f}}h>.

Now pick
\epsilon>0
. Because
\varphi
is a generator there is a polynomial
P
such that

|<z-P(\varphi),T_{\bar{f}}h>|<\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary it follows that

0=<z,T_{\bar{f}}h>=<M_{z}f,h>.

Hence
M_{z}f\in\EuFrak{M}
for any
f\in\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}
and, because this intersection is dense in
\EuFrak{M}
one concludes
that
M_{z}\EuFrak{M}\subset\EuFrak{M}
.

To prove the converse statement consider the
\varphi-
invariant subspace
[\mathbf{1}]_{\varphi}
. Since this subspace is the closure in
\EuFrak{H}
of polynomials in
\varphi
, functions from
H^{\infty}
are dense in it. Consequently,
[\mathbf{1}]_{\varphi}
is also
z-
invariant. But any
z-
invariant subspace of
\EuFrak{H}
which contains the function
{\mathbf{1}}
coincides with the whole
\EuFrak{H}
. Therefore
[\mathbf{1}]_{\varphi}=\EuFrak{H}
, that is
\varphi
is a generator.
\Box

Remark 2
.

It was proved earlier that a bounded univalent function

is a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
if and only if

Lat_{\EuFrak{H}}(\psi)=Lat_{\EuFrak{H}}(z)
. For the case
\EuFrak{H}=H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
it was proved by D. Sarason in
[

Corollary 1

Let
\varphi
be a generator of
\EuFrak{H}
and let
\EuFrak{M}
be a
\varphi-

{\rm dim}\;\EuFrak{M}^{\perp}<\infty

\EuFrak{M}
is

-invariant.

The corollary follows from the fact that

is dense in
\EuFrak{H}
and the statement below that was proved for the more general situation in

7
]
, Lemma 2.1:

Let a Banach space

be decomposed as the direct sum of a subspace

and a finite-dimensional subspace

X=Y\dot{+}Z,

and
L
is a dense linear subset of

is dense in

The following statement was proved in
[
5
]
, Lemma 4.1.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\varphi
be a bounded univalent function on the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
with
\varphi(0)=0
. If
\EuFrak{M}
is a
z-
invariant subspace of
\EuFrak{H}
, then
\EuFrak{M}\ominus M_{z}\EuFrak{M}=\EuFrak{M}\ominus M_{\varphi}\EuFrak{M}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\varphi
be a bounded univalent function on the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
with
\varphi(0)=0
. Assume that
\varphi
is a generator of
\EuFrak{H}
and
\EuFrak{M}
is a
\varphi-

\EuFrak{H}

(a)
\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})

\EuFrak{M}
;

(b)
(\EuFrak{M}\ominus\varphi\EuFrak{M})\cap H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})

\EuFrak{M}\ominus\varphi\EuFrak{M}

Then

\EuFrak{M}=\left[\EuFrak{M}\ominus\varphi\EuFrak{M}\right]_{\varphi}.

(5)

Proof.
From Theorem
2
it follows that the subspace
\EuFrak{M}
is

Remark 3
.

If in Theorem
3

\dim(\EuFrak{M}\ominus\varphi\EuFrak{M})<\infty
, then condition (a) implies condition (b).

Let
\EuFrak{M}
be a
\varphi-
invariant subspace of
\EuFrak{H}
. In what follows the quantity

Theorem 4
.

Let
\varphi
be a generator of
\EuFrak{H}
and
\varphi(0)=0
. Assume

and
\EuFrak{M}=[f]_{\varphi}
. Then the

equals 1 and

is also 1.

Proof
. The proof of the first statement of the theorem is, in fact, repetition of the corresponding proof of Theorem 4, Chapter 8, of
[

]
. We include it for completeness.

Represent

in the form

, where

and
f_{2}\in\varphi\EuFrak{M}
. Choose an arbitrary

. Then

, consequently there is a sequence of polynomials of
\varphi

, such that

Define

and and

\displaystyle h_{n}^{(1)}=Q_{n}(\varphi(0))f_{1}-g\in\EuFrak{M}\ominus\varphi\EuFrak{M},

\displaystyle h_{n}^{(2)}=(Q_{n}(\varphi)-Q_{n}(\varphi(0)))f_{1}+Q_{n}(\varphi)f_{2}\in\varphi\EuFrak{M}.

The second inclusion follows from the fact that
Q_{n}(\varphi)-Q_{n}(\varphi(0))=(\varphi-\varphi(0))R_{n}(\varphi)
,

is a polynomial of

and
\varphi(0)=0
.
We have

In particular, the property

3
Density of
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
in the Invariant Subspaces of a Generator

The previous considerations raise the following questions:

Let
\varphi
be a generator of
\EuFrak{H}
and
\EuFrak{M}
is a
\varphi-
invariant subspace of
\EuFrak{H}
such that
dim
(\EuFrak{H}\ominus\EuFrak{M})=\infty
.

Is it true that

\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}\neq\{0\}
?

Is
\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}
dense in
\EuFrak{M}
?

For the case of
\EuFrak{H}=A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
the answer is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For any function
\psi
analytic and bounded in the unit disk
D
there is a singly-generated
\psi-
invariant subspace
\EuFrak{M}\subset A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
such that
\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})=\{0\}
.

Proof.
Let
f\in A^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
be such that its zeros do not satisfy the Blaschke condition and put
\EuFrak{M}=[f]_{\psi}
. Clearly, zeros of any function which belong to
\EuFrak{M}
also do not satisfy the Blaschke condition, consequently,

\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})=\{0\}
.
\Box

Now we consider the case
\EuFrak{H}=H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
. Let
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})\setminus H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
. Then a function
f_{T}\in H^{\infty}
is called
f-
truncating, if
f_{T}f\in H^{\infty}
.
There are many ways to construct an
f-
truncating function. One of them is the following.
Define a real valued function
\omega(e^{it})
as follows:

\omega(e^{it})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\;|f(e^{it})|\leq 1\\
1/|f(e^{it})|&\text{if}\;|f(e^{it})|\geq 1.\end{cases}

We have
|\omega(e^{it})|\leq 1
and
\log{\omega}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
. Therefore there is an outer function
f_{T}\in H^{\infty}\subset H^{2}
such that

|f_{T}(e^{it})|=\omega(e^{it})
. It is obvious that
ff_{T}\in H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
and
\sup\{|f(z)f_{T}(z)|:|z|<1\}\leq 1
.

Assume now that
\varphi
is a generator. Therefore, there is a sequence of polynomials of
\varphi
, say
\{P_{n}(\varphi)\}
, such that
\|P_{n}(\phi)-f_{T}\|_{2}\to 0
. Such a sequence

\{P_{n}(\varphi)\}
we shall call an
f-
truncating sequence.
Since

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|P_{n}(\varphi(e^{it}))-f_{T}(e^{it})|^{2}dt\to 0

there is a subsequence
P_{n_{k}}(\varphi)
such that

P_{n_{k}}(\varphi(e^{it}))\to f_{T}(e^{it})
for a.e.
t\in[0,2\pi]
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
\{P_{n}(\varphi)\}
converges to
f_{T}
almost everywhere on
{\mathbb{T}}
. In particular
P_{n}(\varphi(e^{it}))f(e^{it})\to f_{T}(e^{it})f(e^{it})
on the set of full measure on
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\varphi
be a generator and let
\EuFrak{M}
be a
\varphi-
invariant subspace of the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
.
Suppose
f\in\EuFrak{M}
is not in
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})
and an
f
-truncating sequence
\{P_{n}(\varphi)\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{\infty}
-norm, that is
\sup_{n}{\|P_{n}(\varphi)\|_{\infty}}\leq C<\infty
. Then
\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}})\neq\{0\}
.

Proof.
Let
f_{T}\in H^{2}\cap H^{\infty}
be the
f-
truncating function constructed in the previous paragraph. We need to show that
P_{n}(\varphi)f\to f_{T}f
in
H^{2}
-norm. Since

P_{n}(\varphi)f\in\EuFrak{M}
this will prove the statement.
We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P_{n}(\varphi(e^{it}))f(e^{it})-f_{T}(e^{it})f(e^{it})|^{2}dt=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P_{n}(\varphi(e^{it}))-f_{T}(e^{it})|^{2}|f(e^{it})|^{2}dt.

The expression under integral sign converges to zero almost everywhere and is dominated by
C|f|^{2}
where
C
is a positive constant. Now Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem gives the desired result.
\Box

Theorem 7
.

Let
\psi
be a weak-star generator of
H^{\infty}
and let
\EuFrak{M}
be a
\psi-
invariant subspace of
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
. Then
\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}
is dense in
\EuFrak{M}
in
H^{2}
norm.

Proof.
Let the functions
f\in\EuFrak{M}
and
f_{T}
be as in the previous theorem. For any
h\in H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
and any polynomial
P
we have

<P(\psi)f-f_{T}f,h>=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(P(\psi(e^{it}))-f_{T}(e^{it}))f(e^{it})\overline{h(e^{it})}dt.

Now pick
\epsilon>0
. Since
f\bar{h}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
and
\psi
is a weak-star generator of
H^{\infty}
, there is a polynomial
P
in
\psi
such that the absolute value of the last integral is less than
\epsilon
. Since
P(\psi)f\in\EuFrak{M}
it means that
f_{T}f
belongs to the weak closure of
\EuFrak{M}
. Since weak closure of a subspace coincides with its norm closure, it proves that
\EuFrak{M}\cap H^{\infty}\neq\{0\}
.
Put
\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}=c.l.h.\{P(\psi)f_{T}f:P\text{ is a polynomial}\}
. Then the subspace

\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}
has the following properties:

1.

\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}\subset\EuFrak{M};

2.

\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}
is
\psi-
invariant;

3.

\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}\cap H^{\infty}
is dense in
\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}
in
H^{2}
norm.

Let
\Omega
is the collection of all subspaces of
\EuFrak{M}
which have these three properties. We have shown that
\Omega\neq\emptyset
. Partially order
\Omega
by the set inclusion. By the Hausdorff’s maximal principle there exists a maximal totally ordered subcollection
\Omega^{\prime}
of
\Omega
. Denote by
\EuFrak{M}_{0}
the union of all
\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}
, where
\EuFrak{M}^{\prime}
is a member of
\Omega^{\prime}
. Then
\EuFrak{M}_{0}
is a maximal subspace of
\EuFrak{M}
which satisfies the three conditions above.

We claim that
\EuFrak{M}_{0}=\EuFrak{M}
. Observe first that

\EuFrak{M}\setminus\EuFrak{M}_{0}
does not contain any
H^{\infty}
vectors. Suppose
\EuFrak{M}_{0}\neq\EuFrak{M}
and pick
h\in\EuFrak{M}\ominus\EuFrak{M}_{0}
. Then
h\notin H^{\infty}
. Let
h_{T}
be the
h
-truncating function. Then
h_{T}h\in H^{\infty}
, hence
h_{T}h\in\EuFrak{M}_{0}
and
P(\psi)h_{T}h\in\EuFrak{M}_{0}
for any polynomial
P
. Consequently,

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}P(\psi(e^{it}))h_{T}(e^{it})|h(e^{it})|^{2}dt=0

for any polynomial
P
. Since
h_{T}|h|\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
and
\psi
is a weak-star generator of
H^{\infty}
from the last equality one easily deduces that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e^{int}h_{T}(e^{it})|h(e^{it})|^{2}dt=0,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots

from which it follows that

h_{T}|h|^{2}=k,

(6)

where
k\in H^{1}_{0}
. Therefore

|h|^{2}=\frac{k}{h_{T}}.

Since the left side is a real-valued function and right side is a function of the Nevanlina class and
k(0)=0
one concludes that that equality (
3
) is possible only for
h=0
and
k=0
. It proves our claim and the theorem.

\Box

Combining the last theorem with Theorem
2
we obtain the following statement.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\psi
be a bounded univalent function in the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
. Then
\psi
is a weak
∗
generator of
H^{\infty}
if and only if every
\psi-
invariant subspace of
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
is also
z-
invariant.

[
8
]
using another method.

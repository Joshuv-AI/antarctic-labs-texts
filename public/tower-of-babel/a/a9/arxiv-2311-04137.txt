Stochastic quantization of two-dimensional P ⁡ ( Φ ) P(\Phi) Quantum Field Theory
Paweł Duch, Wojciech Dybalski, Azam Jahandideh

Abstract
We give a simple and self-contained construction of of the P ⁡ ( Φ ) P(\Phi) Euclidean Quantum Field Theory in the plane and verify the Osterwalder-Schrader axioms: translational and rotational invariance, reflection positivity and regularity. In the intermediate steps of the construction we study measures on spheres. In order to control the infinite volume limit we use the parabolic stochastic quantization equation and the energy method. To prove the translational and rotational invariance of the limit measure we take advantage of the fact that the symmetry groups of the plane and the sphere have the same dimension.

Introduction

We revisit the construction of the Euclidean two-dimensional
P(\Phi)
quantum field theory model also known as the
P(\Phi)_{2}
model. The main new contribution is a simple construction of the infinite volume measure of this model using the stochastic quantization technique
[
27
]
and the verification of all Osterwalder-Schrader axioms with the exception of clustering. By the Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction theorem
[
26
]
this yields the existence of the theory in the Lorentzian signature that satisfies all the Wightman axioms possibly with the exception of the uniqueness of the vacuum. Let us point out that the proof of the invariance of the infinite volume measure under all of the Euclidean transformations of the plane, which is one of the Osterwalder-Schrader axioms, is quite non-trivial. In fact, finite-volume measures, which are typically introduced in the intermediate steps of the construction, are usually invariant only under a certain subset of the Euclidean transformations. The novelty of the approach taken in the present work is to study finite-volume
P(\Phi)_{2}
measures defined on spheres in the intermediate steps of the construction. Such measures are invariant under the action of the three-dimensional Lie group of the rotations of the sphere (in contrast, measures defined on a torus are only invariant under the action of the two-dimensional Lie group). To prove the Euclidean invariance of the infinite volume measure we crucially use the fact that the symmetry groups of the plane and the sphere have the same dimension.

,
17
]

\lambda
positive and polynomials

, which was originally developed in

(see also

18
]
). We stress that the method of our paper works for all
\lambda
positive and all polynomials

bounded from below. In order to control the infinite volume limit we have to prove certain bounds for moments of the regularized measures uniform in both the ultraviolet and infrared cutoffs. To this end, we use the parabolic stochastic quantization and the energy method. Let us note that a similar approach has already been used, for example, in
[
1
,
19
,
2
]
to construct the

the infinite volume measure is constructed as a limit of a sequence of measures defined on tori of increasing size. The symmetry group of the torus consists of translations, reflections and rotations by a multiple of

2

an infrared cutoff preserving the rotational invariance was used. The rotational invariance of the infinite volume limit is then obvious. However, the translational invariance is far from clear as it is explicitly broken by the infrared cutoff.
In

In the present work we study

3
Stochastic quantization

In order to show the existence of the infinite volume limit of the
P(\Phi)_{2}
model and prove the bound (
1.3
) we have to establish appropriate bounds for moments of the regularized measure
\mu^{g}_{R,N}
uniform in
R,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. To this end, we shall use the so-called parabolic stochastic quantization technique. We study a certain stochastic process evolving in fictitious time whose stationary distribution coincides with the Euclidean QFT measure. The process satisfies a non-linear stochastic PDE that is called the stochastic quantization equation. More specifically, to prove desired uniform bounds we apply the energy method, which relies on testing the equation by the solution itself and estimating the terms that are not positive. Because of the UV problem the stochastic quantization equation of the measure
\mu^{g}_{R,N}
, that is Eq. (
3.2

\mathbb{S}_{R,N}^{\pm}:=\{(\mathrm{x}_{1},\mathrm{x}_{2},\mathrm{x}_{3})\in\mathbb{S}_{R}\,|\,\pm\mathrm{x}_{1}>1/N\},\quad\mathbb{S}_{R,N}:=\mathbb{S}_{R,N}^{+}\cup\mathbb{S}_{R,N}^{-},\quad\mathbb{S}_{R}^{\pm}:=\cup_{N\in[1,\infty)}\mathbb{S}_{R,N}^{\pm}.

(8.1)

Definition 8.2
.

Let
R\in[1,\infty)
. A functional
F\,:\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}_{R})\to\mathbb{C}
is called cylindrical iff there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
G\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
and
f_{l}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{S}_{R}):=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{R})

F(\phi)=G(\phi(f_{1}),\ldots,\phi(f_{k})).

(8.2)

The algebra of cylindrical functions is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{R}
. The subalgebras of
\mathcal{F}_{R}
consisting of functionals of the form (

) with

,

,

and

and

\mathcal{F}_{R}
and
\mathcal{F}_{R}^{\pm}
with

and
\mathbb{S}_{R}^{\pm}
replaced by
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the half-plane
\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|\pm x_{1}>0\}
, respectively.

Definition 8.3
.

Let
R\in[1,\infty)
. For

.

Remark 8.4
.

Note that
\jmath_{R}^{*}\Theta_{R}\phi=\Theta\jmath_{R}^{*}\phi
for all
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
.

Proposition 8.5
.

Let
\mu
be a weak limit of a subsequence of the sequence of measures
(\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R})_{R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For all

it holds

\int\overline{F(\Theta\phi)}F(\phi)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\phi)\geq 0
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that

\int\overline{F(\Theta\phi)}F(\phi)\,(\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R})(\mathrm{d}\phi)=\int\overline{F(\Theta\jmath_{R}^{*}\phi)}F(\jmath_{R}^{*}\phi)\,\mu_{R}(\mathrm{d}\phi)=\int\overline{F(\jmath_{R}^{*}\Theta_{R}\phi)}F(\jmath_{R}^{*}\phi)\,\mu_{R}(\mathrm{d}\phi)\geq 0

(8.3)

for all
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
. By Def.
8.2
and Remark
4.3
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
it holds
F\circ\jmath_{R}^{*}\in\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R}
. Hence, the last bound above follows from the reflection positivity of the measure
\mu_{R}
.
∎

For completeness, we prove below the reflection positivity of the measure

Definition 8.6
.

Fix

such that

,

on

(8.4)

Remark 8.7
.

Note that formally for

and

Definition 8.8
.

Let
\hat{c}_{R,N}:=\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{K}_{R,N}G_{R}\hat{K}_{R,N})/4\pi R^{2}
. By definition
\hat{X}_{R,N}:=\hat{K}_{R,N}X_{R}
,

\begin{gathered}\hat{X}_{R,N}^{:m:}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}m!}{(m-2k)!k!2^{k}}(\hat{c}_{R,N})^{k}\hat{X}_{R,N}^{m-2k},\qquad\hat{X}_{R,N}^{:m:}(h):=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{R}}\hat{X}_{R,N}^{:m:}(\mathrm{x})\,h(\mathrm{x})\,\rho_{R}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{x}),\\

(8.5)

where

and

.

Lemma 8.11
.

For all
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and all bounded and continuous

it holds

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}_{R,N})=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}F(X_{R,N})\exp(-Y_{R,N}).

(8.6)

. By Lemmas

and

the sequences

∎

Lemma 8.12
.

The following statements hold true for all
R,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
:

(A)

If
F\in\mathcal{F}_{R}^{+}
, then
\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}X_{R})}F(X_{R})\geq 0
.

(B)

If
F\in\mathcal{F}_{R,N}^{+}
, then
\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\geq 0
.

(C)

If
F\in\mathcal{F}_{R,N}^{+}
, then
\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}_{R,N})\geq 0
.

(D)

For all
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R}
it holds

\int\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\phi)}F(\phi)\,\mu_{R}(\mathrm{d}\phi)\geq 0
.

Proof.

For the proof of Item (A) see
[
12
, Theorem 2]
. To prove Item (B), observe that

\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})=\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\hat{K}_{R,N}(\Theta_{R}X_{R}))}F(\hat{K}_{R,N}X_{R})\,,

(8.7)

where we have used the fact that
\Theta_{R}\hat{K}_{R,N}X_{R}=\hat{K}_{R,N}\Theta_{R}X_{R}
. By the support property of the kernel
\hat{K}_{R,N}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})
if
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R,N}
, then the functional
\phi\mapsto F(\hat{K}_{R,N}\phi)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{R}^{+}
. Consequently, the statement follows from Item (A). To prove Item (C), note that

\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}_{R,N})=\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}^{-}_{R,N})}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}^{+}_{R,N})\,.

(8.8)

Denote
H(\hat{X}_{R,N}):=F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}_{R,N}^{+})
. It holds

\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}_{R,N})=\mathbb{E}\overline{H(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})}H(\hat{X}_{R,N})\,.

(8.9)

The RHS of the above equality can be approximated by a similar expression with
H
replaced by some functional belonging to
\mathcal{F}_{R,N}^{+}
. As a result, Item (C) follows from Item (B). Let us turn to the proof of Item (D). First note that for any
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R}
there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R,M}
. Hence, it suffices to show that
\int\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\phi)}F(\phi)\,\mu_{R}(\mathrm{d}\phi)\geq 0
for all
R,M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R,M}
. To establish this claim we note that by Lemma
8.11

\int\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\phi)}F(\phi)\,\mu_{R}(\mathrm{d}\phi)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\overline{F(\Theta_{R}\hat{X}_{R,N})}F(\hat{X}_{R,N})\exp(-\tilde{Y}_{R,N})}{\mathbb{E}\exp(-\tilde{Y}_{R,N})}

(8.10)

and use Item (C) together with the fact that
\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R,M}\subset\mathcal{F}^{+}_{R,N}
for all
N\geq M
.
∎

9
Euclidean invariance

In this section we establish the invariance under the Euclidean transformations of the plane of every accumulation point
\mu
of the sequence
(\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R})_{R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
of measures on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We use the fact that for every
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
the measure
\mu_{R}
is invariant under the rotations of the sphere
\mathbb{S}_{R}
. The proof of the rotational invariance of
\mu
is straight-forward as the rotations
\mathcal{R}_{R,\alpha}
of the sphere
\mathbb{S}_{R}
around the
\mathrm{x}_{3}
axis are mapped under the stereographic projection to the rotations
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
of the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
around the origin. Hence, for every
R\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
the measure
\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is invariant under the rotations around the origin and the same is true for every accumulation point
\mu
. The proof of the translational invariance of
\mu
is more complicated. There is no rotation of the sphere
\mathbb{S}_{R}
that is mapped under the stereographic projection to the translation
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
of the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the
x_{1}
direction. In particular, for every
R\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
the measure
\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is not invariant under the translations. In order to establish the translational invariance of an accumulation point
\mu
we first prove that the rotations
\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}
of the sphere
\mathbb{S}_{R}
around the
\mathrm{x}_{2}
axis are mapped under the stereographic projection to certain transformations
\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}
of the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and subsequently show that the transformations
\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}
converge to the translations
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
of the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the
x_{1}
direction as
R\to\infty
.

Definition 9.1
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
the maps
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
are defined by

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x_{1},x_{2}):=(x_{1}\cos\alpha+x_{2}\sin\alpha,x_{1}\sin\alpha-x_{2}\cos\alpha),\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}(x_{1},x_{2}):=(x_{1}+\alpha,x_{2}),

(9.1)

For
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
the maps
\mathcal{R}_{R,\alpha},\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}\,:\,\mathbb{S}_{R}\to\mathbb{S}_{R}
are defined by

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{R}_{R,\alpha}(\mathrm{x})=(\mathrm{x}_{1}\cos\alpha+\mathrm{x}_{2}\sin\alpha,\mathrm{x}_{1}\sin\alpha-\mathrm{x}_{2}\cos\alpha,\mathrm{x}_{3}),\\
\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}(\mathrm{x})=(\mathrm{x}_{1}\cos(\alpha/R)-\mathrm{x}_{3}\sin(\alpha/R),\mathrm{x}_{2},\mathrm{x}_{1}\sin(\alpha/R)+\mathrm{x}_{3}\cos(\alpha/R)),\end{gathered}

(9.2)

where
\mathrm{x}=(\mathrm{x}_{1},\mathrm{x}_{2},\mathrm{x}_{3})\in\mathbb{S}_{R}
. For
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\alpha\in(-R,R)
the map
\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}\,:\,B_{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is defined by

\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\frac{2(R\sin(\alpha/R)(1-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})/4R^{2})+x_{1}\cos(\alpha/R),x_{2})}{1+\cos(\alpha/R)+(1-\cos(\alpha/R))(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})/4R^{2}-x_{1}/R\sin(\alpha/R)},

(9.3)

where
B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,|x|<R\}
.

Remark 9.2
.

For all
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
it holds
\mathcal{R}_{R,\alpha}\jmath_{R}=\jmath_{R}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
. For all
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\alpha\in(-R,R)
it holds
\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}\jmath_{R}=\jmath_{R}\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}
on
B_{R}
.

Definition 9.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. For
f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we set

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}^{*}f:=f\circ\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{*}f:=f\circ\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

(9.4)

and for
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we set

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}^{*}\phi:=\phi\circ\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}^{*}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{*}\phi:=\phi\circ\mathcal{T}_{-\alpha}^{*}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(9.5)

For
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
,
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
we define
\mathcal{R}_{R,\alpha}^{*}f,\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}^{*}f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
and
\mathcal{R}_{R,\alpha}^{*}\phi,\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}^{*}\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
by analogous formulas.

Definition 9.4
.

Let
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\alpha\in(-R,R)
. For
f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we set

\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}^{*}f:=f\circ\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(B_{R}).

For
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi\subset B_{R}
, we define
\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}^{*}\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by

\langle\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}^{*}\phi,f\rangle:=\langle\phi,\det(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{S}_{R,-\alpha})\mathcal{S}_{R,-\alpha}^{*}f\rangle

(9.6)

for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where
\det(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{S}_{R,-\alpha})
denotes the Jacobian, i.e. the determinant of the tangent map of
\mathcal{S}_{R,-\alpha}
.

Remark 9.5
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
and
M\in(0,\infty)
there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
x\in B_{M}
and
R\in(|\alpha|\vee M,\infty)
it holds

\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}\jmath_{R}(x)=\jmath_{R}\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}(x)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad|\partial^{a}\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}(x)-\partial^{a}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}(x)|\leq C/R.

(9.7)

Noting that
\mathrm{T}\mathcal{T}_{-\alpha}=1
we conclude that for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
there exists
C
such that for all sufficiently large
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
it holds

\|\det(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{S}_{R,-\alpha})\mathcal{S}_{R,-\alpha}^{*}f-\mathcal{T}_{-\alpha}^{*}f\|_{L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{-1/2})}\leq C\,/R.

(9.8)

Remark 9.6
.

Let us note that the algebra of cylindrical functionals
\mathcal{F}
separates points in
L^{-1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/2})\subset\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Hence, if
\mu_{j}
,
j=1,2
, are Borel probability measures on
L^{-1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/2})
such that
\mu_{1}(F)=\mu_{2}(F)
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
by
[
14
, Theorem 4.5(a), Ch. 3]
.

Proposition 9.7
.

Let
\mu
be a weak limit of a subsequence of the sequence of measures
(\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R})_{R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. It holds
\mu(F)=\mu(F\circ\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}^{*})
and
\mu(F)=\mu(F\circ\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{*})
for all bounded and measurable
F\,:\,\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}
and all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Suppose that the sequence of measures
\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
converges to
\mu
along the subsequence
(R_{M})_{M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
. By Remark
6.2
the measure
\mu
is concentrated on
L^{-1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/2})
. Hence, by Remark
9.6
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
F\in\mathcal{F}
is a cylindrical functional. Note that by the rotational invariance of the measure
\mu_{R}
it holds

\mu_{R}(F_{R})=\mu_{R}(F_{R}\circ\mathcal{R}_{R,\alpha}^{*}),\qquad\mu_{R}(F_{R})=\mu_{R}(F_{R}\circ\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}^{*})

(9.9)

for every
F_{R}\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. By Remark
9.2
we have
\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\alpha}\circ\jmath_{R}^{*}=\jmath_{R}^{*}\circ\mathcal{R}^{*}_{R,\alpha}
. Hence, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
we obtain

\mu(F\circ\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\alpha}-F)=\lim_{M\to\infty}\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R_{M}}(F\circ\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\alpha}-F)=\lim_{M\to\infty}\mu_{R_{M}}(F\circ\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\alpha}\circ\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*}-F\circ\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*})\\
=\lim_{M\to\infty}\mu_{R_{M}}(F\circ\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*}\circ\mathcal{R}_{R_{M},\alpha}^{*}-F\circ\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*})=\lim_{M\to\infty}(\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R_{M}}-\mu)(F)=0.

(9.10)

This finishes the proof of the rotational invariance.

Let us turn to the proof of the translational invariance. Note that by Remark
9.2
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and all sufficiently large
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
it holds

\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R}(F)=\mu_{R}(F\circ\jmath_{R}^{*})=\mu_{R}(F\circ\jmath_{R}^{*}\circ\mathcal{T}_{R,\alpha}^{*})=\mu_{R}(F\circ\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}^{*}\circ\jmath_{R}^{*})=\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R}(F\circ\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}^{*}).

(9.11)

Remark
9.5
implies that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\psi\in L^{-1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/2})
and all sufficiently large
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
it holds

|F(\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}^{*}\psi)-F(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{*}\psi)|\leq(C/R)~\|\psi\|_{L^{-1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/2})}.

(9.12)

By Proposition
6.1
and Hölder’s inequality we obtain that

\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}_{R})}\|\jmath_{R}^{*}\phi\|_{L^{-1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/2})}\,\mu_{R}(\mathrm{d}\phi)

(9.13)

is uniformly bounded in
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Hence, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
it holds

\lim_{R\to\infty}\jmath_{R}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R}(F\circ\mathcal{S}_{R,\alpha}^{*}-F\circ\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{*})=0.

(9.14)

Consequently,

\mu(F\circ\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\alpha}-F)=\lim_{M\to\infty}\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R_{M}}(F\circ\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\alpha}-F)\\
=\lim_{M\to\infty}\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R_{M}}(F\circ\mathcal{S}_{R_{M},\alpha}^{*}-F)=\lim_{M\to\infty}(\jmath_{R_{M}}^{*}\sharp\mu_{R_{M}}-\mu)(F)=0.

(9.15)

This finishes the proof.
∎

Appendix A

Definition A.1
.

We say that
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an admissible weight iff there exist
b\in[0,\infty)
and
c\in(0,\infty)
such that

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every

such that

for all

.

Definition A.2
.

Let
w
be an admissible weight,
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

. By definition

is the Banach space with the norm

(A.1)

The weighted Bessel potential space
L^{\alpha}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
is the Banach space with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{\alpha}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}:=\|(1-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}.

(A.2)

We also set
L^{\alpha}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{\alpha}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},1)
. The weighted Sobolev space

is the Banach space with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{n}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}=\textstyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|a|\leq n\end{subarray}}\|\partial^{a}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}.

(A.3)

For
R\in(0,\infty)
the Bessel potential space
L^{\alpha}_{p}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
on the round sphere
\mathbb{S}_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
R
is the Banach space with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{\alpha}_{p}(\mathbb{S}_{R})}:=\|(1-\Delta_{R})^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{S}_{R})},

(A.4)

where
L_{p}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
is the
L_{p}
space on
\mathbb{S}_{R}
with respect to the canonical measure
\rho_{R}
on
\mathbb{S}_{R}
.

Remark A.3
.

The following facts are standard: Let
w
be an admissible weight,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The norms

Appendix B
Mathematical preliminaries

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
. There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
R,N\in[1,\infty)
it holds

-C\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2l+1)}{2R^{2}\,(1+l(l+1)/R^{2})\,(1+l(l+1)/(NR)^{2})^{\kappa}}-\log(N+1)\leq C.

(B.1)

Proof.

Observe that the expression in the statement of the lemma coincides with

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\lfloor l\rfloor+1)\,\mathrm{d}l}{2R^{2}\,(1+\lfloor l\rfloor(\lfloor l\rfloor+1)/R^{2})\,(1+\lfloor l\rfloor(\lfloor l\rfloor+1)/(NR)^{2})^{\kappa}}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}l}{(1+l)(1+(1+l)/N)}.

(B.2)

The absolute value of the above expression is bounded by

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{(2\lfloor Rl\rfloor+1)/R}{2(1+\lfloor Rl\rfloor(\lfloor Rl\rfloor+1)/R^{2})\,(1+\lfloor Rl\rfloor(\lfloor Rl\rfloor+1)/(NR)^{2})^{\kappa}}-\frac{1}{(1+l)(1+(1+l)/N)}\right|\mathrm{d}l.

(B.3)

Using
0\leq l-\lfloor Rl\rfloor/R\leq 1
we show that there exists
\hat{C}\in(0,\infty)
such that the above expression is bounded by

\hat{C}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{(1+l)(1+l^{2}/N^{2})^{\kappa}}-\frac{1}{(1+l)(1+(1+l)/N)}\right|\mathrm{d}l\leq C.

(B.4)

This finishes the proof.
∎

Definition B.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a topological space and let
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be a sequence of probability measures defined on
(\mathcal{X},\mathrm{Borel}(\mathcal{X}))
. The sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}

is tight iff for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K_{\epsilon}\subset\mathcal{X}
such that
\mu_{n}(K_{\epsilon})\geq 1-\epsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. The sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
converges weakly if for every bounded
F\in C(\mathcal{X})
the sequence of real numbers
(\mu_{n}(F))_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
converges.

Theorem B.3

(Prokhorov’s theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a separable metric space. A sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathrm{Borel}(\mathcal{X}))
is tight iff there exists a diverging sequence of natural numbers
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
such that the sequence
(\mu_{a_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
converges weakly.

Lemma B.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be separable normed spaces such that
\imath:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a compact embedding and let
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be a sequence of probability measures on
(\mathcal{X},\mathrm{Borel}(\mathcal{X}))
. Assume that there exists
M\in(0,\infty)
such that
\int_{\mathcal{X}}\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\,\mu_{n}(\mathrm{d}x)\leq M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Then the sequence of measures
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
on
(\mathcal{Y},\mathrm{Borel}(\mathcal{Y}))
defined by

\nu_{n}(A):=\mu_{n}(\imath^{-1}(A)),\qquad\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\quad A\in\mathrm{Borel}(\mathcal{Y}),

(B.5)

is tight.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
,
L_{\epsilon}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\,|\,\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq M/\epsilon\}
and
K_{\epsilon}:=\overline{\imath(L_{\epsilon})}
. Observe that
K_{\epsilon}\subset\mathcal{Y}
is compact. It holds

1-\nu_{n}(K_{\epsilon})\leq 1-\mu_{n}(L_{\epsilon})=\mu_{n}(\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}>M/\epsilon)\leq\epsilon/M~\int_{\mathcal{X}}\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\,\mu_{n}(\mathrm{d}x)\leq\epsilon.

(B.6)

This finishes the proof.

Lemma C.4

There exists

(C.3)

Remark C.5
.

Recall that

,
G_{R}=(1-\Delta_{R})^{-1}
and the counterterms

,
\hat{c}_{R,N}
were introduced in Eq. (

) and Def.

. Note that the operators

commute. Using the above lemma we obtain

Consequently, it holds

|\hat{c}_{R,N}-c_{R,N}|\leq\mathrm{Tr}(|\hat{K}_{R,N}^{2}-K_{R,N}^{2}|G_{R})/4\pi R^{2}\leq 2C(C+1)\,\big[\mathrm{Tr}((1-\Delta_{R}/N^{2})^{-1}(1-\Delta_{R})^{-1})\\

(C.5)

By Lemma
B.1
the RHS of the last inequality above is bounded by a constant independent of
R,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

Proof.

Note that
\hat{K}_{R,N}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l+1)\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{K}_{R,N}\mathcal{P}_{R,l})\,\mathcal{P}_{R,l}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{R,l}\,:\,L_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{R})
is defined such that
(2l+1)\mathcal{P}_{R,l}
is the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace of the operator
-\Delta_{R}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
l(l+1)/R^{2}
. Consequently, by the triangle inequality for the commuting self-adjoint operators it is enough to show that there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
R,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds

\begin{gathered}(1+l(l+1)/R^{2}N^{2})\,|\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{K}_{R,N}\mathcal{P}_{R,l})|\leq C,\\
|\mathrm{Tr}((1-\hat{K}_{R,N})\mathcal{P}_{R,l})|\leq C\,(1+l(l+1))/R^{2}N^{2}.\end{gathered}

(C.6)

(To obtain the second bound in the statement of the lemma one combines both estimates in (
C.6
).)
Recall that
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
the integral kernel of
\mathcal{P}_{R,l}
is given by

\mathcal{P}_{R,l}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})=P_{l}(\mathrm{x}\cdot\mathrm{y}/R^{2})/4\pi R^{2},

where
P_{l}
is the
l

\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{K}_{R,N}\mathcal{P}_{R,l})=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}P_{l}(\cos(\theta/RN))\,RN\sin(\theta/RN)\,h(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(C.7)

Using the fact that
RN\sin(\theta/RN)\leq\theta
,
|P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)|\leq 1
(cf.
[
4
, Sec. 2.7.5]
) and

l(l+1)P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)\sin\vartheta=-\partial_{\vartheta}^{2}(\sin\vartheta\,P_{l}(\cos\vartheta))+\partial_{\vartheta}(\cos\vartheta\,P_{l}(\cos\vartheta))

(C.8)

(cf.
[
4
, Sec. 2.7.2]
) we show the first of the bounds (
C.6
). Next, using that
2\pi\int\theta h(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta=1
, we obtain that

\mathrm{Tr}((1-\hat{K}_{R,N})\mathcal{P}_{R,l})=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}(P_{l}(\cos(\theta/RN))\,RN\,\sin(\theta/RN)-\theta)\,h(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(C.9)

We note the estimates

0\leq 1-P_{l}(\cos(\vartheta))\leq l(l+1)\,(1-\cos(\vartheta))/2\leq l(l+1)\,\vartheta^{2}/4,\quad 0\leq 1-\sin(\vartheta)/\vartheta\leq\vartheta^{2}/6,

(C.10)

where the second inequality follows from

1-P_{l}(u)=P_{l}(1)-P_{l}(u)=\int^{1}_{u}\frac{d}{dv}P_{l}(v)dv\leq(1-u)\,l(l+1)/2

(cf.
[
4
, Sec. 2.7.5]
). This shows the second bound in (
C.6
) and finishes the proof.

Lemma C.10
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
and
L\in[1,\infty)
. There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
R,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
R\geq L
, it holds

\mathbb{E}\|\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}\|_{L^{-\kappa}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/p})}^{p}\leq C,\qquad\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|\jmath_{R}^{*}(X_{R}-X_{R,N})\|_{L^{-\kappa}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/p})}^{p}=0.

(C.21)

Proof.

By Jensen’s inequality it suffices to prove the statement for
p\in 2\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Let
q=(4/\kappa)\vee 4
. There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
depending on
p
and
\kappa
such that for all
R,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
it holds

\mathbb{E}\|\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}\|_{L^{-\kappa}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{L}^{1/p})}^{p}\leq\|v_{L}w_{L}^{-1/q}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\,\|\mathbb{E}((1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}}))^{p}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},w_{L}^{1/q})}\\
\leq C\,\|\mathbb{E}((1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}}))^{2}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},w_{L}^{1/q})}^{p/2}\,,

(C.22)

where the last bound is a consequence of Lemma
C.2
. Recall that
\mathbb{E}X_{R,N}\otimes X_{R,N}=G_{R,N}({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}},{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}})
, where
G_{R,N}=K_{R,N}G_{R}K_{R,N}
. By Lemma
C.1

\mathbb{E}\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}\otimes\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}=m!\,\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m},\qquad\tilde{G}_{R,N}:=(\jmath_{R}^{*}\otimes\jmath_{R}^{*})G_{R,N}({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}},{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}})\,.

(C.23)

Hence, by Fubini’s theorem and explicit formula for the kernel in terms of spherical harmonics

\mathbb{E}(1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}\otimes\,(1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}\\
=m!\,\big((1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\otimes(1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\big)\,\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(C.24)

Since for
F\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
it holds
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,x)\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,y)
we obtain

\|\mathbb{E}((1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\jmath_{R}^{*}X^{:m:}_{R,N}({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}}))^{2}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},w_{L}^{1/q})}\\
\leq m!\,\sup_{y}w_{L}^{1/q}(y)\,\|((1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2}\otimes 1)\big(1\otimes(1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2})\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m}\big)({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}},y)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\\
=m!\,\sup_{y}w_{L}^{1/q}(y)\,\|\big(1\otimes(1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2})\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m}\big)({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}},y)\|_{L^{-\kappa}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(C.25)

By Theorem
A.5
(B) there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
R,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
the above expression is bounded by

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{L}^{1/q}(y)\,\|\big(1\otimes(1-\Delta)^{-\kappa/2})\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m}\big)({\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}},y)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\\

=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m}(x,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}})\|_{L_{\infty}^{-\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{2},w_{L}^{1/q})}

(C.26)

up to a multiplicative constant
C
, which depends on
m
. The first equality above follows from the fact that for
F\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
it holds
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,y)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,y)
. By Theorem
A.5
(B), since
q>2/\kappa
, the above expression is bounded by

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m}(x,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}})\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2},w_{L}^{1/q})}\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|\tilde{G}_{R,N}^{m}(x,{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}})\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2},w_{R}^{1/q})}=\sup_{\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{S}_{R}}\|G_{R,N}(\mathrm{x},{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}})^{m}\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{S}_{R})}\\

One-loop W W boson contributions to the decay H → Z ​ γ H\rightarrow Z\gamma in the general R ξ R_{\xi} gauge
Dzung Tri Tran, Le Tho Hue, Khiem Hong Phan

Abstract
One-loop W W boson contributions to the decay H → Z ​ γ H\rightarrow Z\gamma in the general R ξ R_{\xi} gauge are presented. The analytical results are expressed in terms of well-known Passarino-Veltman functions which their numerical evaluations can be generated using LoopTools . In the limit d → 4 d\rightarrow 4 , we have shown that these analytical results are independent of the unphysical parameter ξ \xi and consistent with previous results. The gauge parameter independence are also checked numerically for consistence. Our results are also well stable with different values of ξ = 0 , 1 , 100 , \xi=0,1,100, and ξ → ∞ \xi\rightarrow\infty .

2
Calculations

In general, an one-loop decay amplitude is decomposed
into one-loop tensor integrals which can be reduced
frequently to the final forms being sums of only scalar functions.
Our calculation will follow the tensor reduction method
for one-loop integrals developed in Ref.
[
37
]
.
This technique is described briefly in the following.

The notations of one-loop one-,
two- and three-point tensor integrals with rank
P

are given by

\displaystyle\{A;B;C\}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{P}}=\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\dfrac{k^{\mu_{1}}k^{\mu_{2}}\cdots k^{\mu_{P}}}{\{D_{1};D_{1}D_{2};D_{1}D_{2}D_{3}\}}.

(1)

In this formula,

D_{j}
(
j=1,2,3
) are the inverse Feynman propagators

\displaystyle D_{j}=(k+q_{j})^{2}-m_{j}^{2}+i\rho,

(2)

q_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}p_{i}
,

p_{i}
are the external momenta, and

m_{j}
are internal masses in the loops.

The explicit reduction formulas for
one-loop one-, two-, three-points tensor integrals
up to rank
P=3
are written as
follows
[
37
]
:

\displaystyle A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(3)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}A_{00},

(4)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(5)

\displaystyle B^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\mu}B_{1},

(6)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}B_{00}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}B_{11},

(7)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{g,q\}^{\mu\nu\rho}B_{001}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}q^{\rho}B_{111},

(8)

\displaystyle C^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\mu}C_{1}+q_{2}^{\mu}C_{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1,2}q_{i}^{\mu}C_{i},

(9)

\displaystyle C^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}C_{00}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1,2}q_{i}^{\mu}q_{j}^{\nu}C_{ij},

(10)

\displaystyle C^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}C_{00i}+\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}q^{\mu}_{i}q^{\nu}_{j}q^{\rho}_{k}C_{ijk}.

(11)

For convenience, another short notation
[
37
]

\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}
will be used
as follows:
\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}=g^{\mu\nu}q^{\rho}_{i}+g^{\nu\rho}q^{\mu}_{i}+g^{\mu\rho}q^{\nu}_{i}
.
Following this approach, the scalar coefficients

A_{00},B_{1},\cdots,C_{222}

in the right hand sides of the above equations
are so-called Passarino-Veltman functions
[
37
]
.
Their analytic formulas for numerical calculations are
well-known. More convenience, these functions can be calculated
numerically using the available package

LoopTools

[
39
]
.

\displaystyle p_{1}p_{2}=\frac{M_{H}^{2}-M_{Z}^{2}}{2}.

4
Conclusions

The analytical results for the form factors presenting one-loop
contributions of the
W
boson to the decay amplitude

H\rightarrow Z\gamma
in the
R_{\xi}
gauge have been collected.
They are expressed as functions of the Passarino-Veltman functions
that numerical calculations are easily generated with
LoopTools
. In the limit of

d\rightarrow 4
, we have shown that these analytic results
are independent of the unphysical parameter
\xi

and consistent with those given in previous works. Numerical checks
for the
\xi
-independence of the form factors
has also discussed. The results are also in
good stability with varying
\xi=0,1,100
and

\xi\rightarrow\infty
. We emphasize that
the results in this paper will be applied to
calculate one-loop contributions of new charged
gauge bosons appearing in many BSMs. They can
be used to cross-checks for consistence with
well-known results given in the unitary gauge.
This is another indirect way to confirm the
new goldstone boson couplings which often
have complicated forms in the BSMs.

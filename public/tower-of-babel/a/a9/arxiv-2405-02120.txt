Second radial eigenfunctions to a fractional Dirichlet problem and uniqueness for a semilinear equation
Mouhamed Moustapha Fall and Tobias Weth

Abstract
. We analyze the shape of radial second Dirichlet eigenfunctions of fractional Schrödinger type operators of the form ( − Δ ) s + V (-\Delta)^{s}+V in the unit ball B B in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} with a nondecreasing radial potential V V . Specifically, we show that the eigenspace corresponding to the second radial eigenvalue is simple and spanned by an eigenfunction u u which changes sign precisely once in the radial variable and does not have zeroes anywhere else in B B . Moreover, by a new Hopf type lemma for supersolutions to a class of degenerate mixed boundary value problems, we show that u u has a nonvanishing fractional boundary derivative on ∂ B \partial B . We apply this result to prove uniqueness and nondegeneracy of positive ground state solutions to the problem ( − Δ ) s ​ u + λ ​ u = u p (-\Delta)^{s}u+\lambda u=u^{p} on B {B} , u = 0 \;u=0 on ℝ N ∖ B \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B . Here s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , λ ≥ 0 \lambda\geq 0 and p > 1 p>1 is strictly smaller than the critical Sobolev exponent.



1.
Introduction

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
B:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,|x|<1\}
denote the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The present paper is devoted to oscillation estimates of the radial second eigenfunctions in the eigenvalue problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}w+Vw=\sigma w\quad\textrm{ in ${B}$},\qquad w\equiv 0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B$.}

Here
(-\Delta)^{s}
denotes the fractional Laplacian of order
s
, which, under appropriate smoothness and integrability assumptions on the function
w
, is pointwisely given by

(1.2)

(-\Delta)^{s}w(x)=c_{N,s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

with
c_{N,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}
. Moreover, we consider (
1.1
) in weak sense. So, by definition, an eigenfunction
u
of (
1.1
) is contained in the Sobolev space

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B):=\{w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\>:\>w\equiv 0\text{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B$}\},

and it satisfies

[w,v]_{s}+\int_{B}Vwv\,dx=\sigma\int_{B}wv\,dx\qquad\text{for all $v\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)$.}

Here

(1.3)

(v_{1},v_{2})\mapsto[v_{1},v_{2}]_{s}=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(v_{1}(x)-v_{1}(y))(v_{2}(x)-v_{2}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy

denotes the bilinear form associated with the fractional Laplacian, and we shall also write
[v]_{s}^{2}:=[v,v]_{s}
in the following. Moreover,
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the usual fractional Sobolev space of functions
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
[w]_{s}^{2}<\infty
. Here we note that the bilinear form
[\cdot,\cdot]_{s}
can also be represented via Fourier transform by

(1.4)

[v_{1},v_{2}]_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{v_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{v_{2}}(\xi)\,d\xi,

and this allows to extend the definition of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{s}
to all
s\geq 0
.

If
V\in L^{q}(B)
for some
q>\max(\frac{N}{2s},1)
, then

(1.5)

the embedding
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B;((1+|V|)dx)
is compact,

where, here and in the following,
L^{2}(B;(1+|V|)dx)
denotes the space of measurable functions
u:B\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int\limits_{B}|u|^{2}(1+|V|)dx<\infty
.
This follows since
2q^{\prime}<2_{s}^{*}
in this case, where
2^{*}_{s}
is the critical fractional Sobolev exponent given by

2^{*}_{s}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}\,\,\textrm{ if }2s<N\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad 2^{*}_{s}=+\infty\,\,\textrm{ if }2s\geq 1=N.

Indeed, we then have a compact Sobolev embedding
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)\hookrightarrow L^{2q^{\prime}}(B)
and a continuous embedding

L^{2q^{\prime}}(B)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B,(1+|V|)dx)
, the latter being a consequence of Hölder’s inequality.

If, in addition,
V
is a radially symmetric function, then it follows from (
1.5
) and a classical argument that there exists a sequence of discrete eigenvalues of (
1.1
) corresponding to radial eigenfunctions. These eigenvalues are given through the min-max characterization

(1.6)

\sigma_{k}(V)=\inf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{rad}(B)}}{{\text{dim}({\mathcal{S}})=k}}}\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{S}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{[w]^{2}_{s}+\int_{B}Vw^{2}\,dx}{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}},\qquad k\geq 1,

where
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{rad}(B)
is the closed subspace of radial functions in
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
. It is well-known that the first eigenvalue
\sigma_{1}(V)
is simple, and the corresponding eigenspace is spanned by a positive eigenfunction
w_{1}
.

Moreover, from (
1.6
) one may, by a standard argument, obtain the alternative useful representation

(1.7)

\sigma_{2}(V)=\inf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{rad}(B)}}{{\langle w,w_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}(B)}=0}}}\frac{[w]^{2}_{s}+\int_{B}Vw^{2}\,dx}{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}}.

In the following, we wish to derive qualitative properties of eigenfunctions of (
1.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\sigma_{2}(V)
. Up to now, few results about simplicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues and oscillation estimates of Dirichlet eigenfunctions

in

V\equiv 0
. Indeed, for

for

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that, for some

Let

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega

, and let

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

, and let

, for some

Let

. Since

, we have

, with

\int_{B}bu^{2}\,dx\leq\|b\|_{L^{q}(B)}\|u\|_{L^{2q^{\prime}}(B)}^{2}\leq C\|b\|_{L^{q}(B)}[u]^{2}_{s}\qquad\textrm{for all $u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)$}

with a constant

. Since

) and (

\sigma_{k}(V_{n})\to\sigma_{k}(V)
as

. In particular, this implies that

is bounded in

converges, up to a subsequence, weakly in

\lambda_{2,s}
from (
1.6
) with
V=0
, which is given by

(3.18)

\lambda_{2,s}=\inf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{rad}(B)}}{{\text{dim}({\mathcal{S}})=2}}}\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{S}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{[w]^{2}_{s}}{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}}.

We claim that

(3.19)

\lambda_{2,s}\leq C(N)\qquad\text{for all $s\in(0,1]$ with a constant $C(N)>0$.}

To see this, we choose an arbitrary two-dimensional subspace
{\mathcal{S}}
of radial functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
, and we consider the compact subset
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}:=\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}\>:\>\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B)}=1\}
. From
3.18
, we then deduce that

\lambda_{2,s}\leq\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}[\varphi]_{s}^{2}=\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{\varphi}|^{2}d\xi\leq\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\xi|)^{2}|\widehat{\varphi}|^{2}d\xi=\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(B)}^{2}=:C(N)

for all
s\in(0,1]
, so (
3.19
) is proved.

Next, we let
\lambda_{1,s}
be the first radial eigenvalue of the Dirichlet fractional Laplacian and
\varphi_{1,s}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
be the corresponding positive eigenfunctions, normalized such that

(3.20)

\|\varphi_{1,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}=1\qquad\text{for $s\in(0,1]$.}

Since
(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi_{1,s}=\lambda_{1,s}\varphi_{1,s}
in
B
and
0\leq\lambda_{1,s}\leq\lambda_{2,s}\leq C(N)
, we may apply
[
33
, Theorem 1.3]
to see that, for all
s_{0}\in(0,1)
, there exists
C=C(N,s_{0})>0
such that

(3.21)

\|\varphi_{1,s}\|_{C^{s}(\overline{B})}\leq C\qquad\textrm{ for all $s\in[s_{0},1)$.}

Hence, if
{s_{*}}\in(0,1]
and
(s_{n})_{n}\subset(0,1)
is a sequence with
s_{n}\to{s_{*}}
, then, up to passing to a subsequence, we have
\lambda_{1,s_{n}}\to\lambda_{*}
and
\varphi_{1,s_{n}}\to{v_{*}}
in
C(\overline{B})
for some function
{v_{*}}\in C(\overline{B})
satisfying
\|{v_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}=1
and
|{v_{*}}(x)|\leq C(1-|x|)^{s}_{+}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, identifying
{v_{*}}
with its trivial extension to all of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have
(-\Delta)^{{s_{*}}}{v_{*}}=\lambda_{*}{v_{*}}
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(B)
. Since
\varphi_{1,s_{n}}\to{v_{*}}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have
\widehat{\varphi_{1,s_{n}}}\to\widehat{{v_{*}}}
pointwisely on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and therefore, by Fatou’s lemma and (
1.4
),

\displaystyle[{v_{*}}]_{{s_{*}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{{v_{*}}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s_{n}}|\widehat{\varphi_{1,s_{n}}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\liminf_{n\to\infty}[\varphi_{1,s_{n}}]_{s_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{1,s_{n}}\|\varphi_{1,s_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}\leq C(N)|B|.

We stress that this not only holds for
{s_{*}}<1
but also in the case
{s_{*}}=1
in which we have

[{v_{*}}]_{{s_{*}}}^{2}=\int_{B}|\nabla{v_{*}}|^{2}\,dx.

Hence
{v_{*}}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{{s_{*}}}({\bf B})
, and
{v_{*}}
satisfies the eigenvalue equation
(-\Delta)^{{s_{*}}}{v_{*}}=\lambda_{*}{v_{*}}
in weak sense. Since
{v_{*}}
is nonnegative and
\|{v_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}=1
, we then obtain that
\lambda_{*}=\lambda_{1,{s_{*}}}
is the first Dirichlet radial eigenvalue of
(-\Delta)^{{s_{*}}}
in
B
and
{v_{*}}=\varphi_{\overline{s},1}
. It thus follows that for any
{s_{*}}\in(0,1]
we have

(3.22)

\varphi_{1,s}\to\varphi_{1,\overline{s}}\quad\textrm{in $C(\overline{B})$ as $s\to{s_{*}}$.}

In the following, we may, by normalization and Theorem
3.2
, assume that

(3.23)

\|\varphi_{2,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{2,s}(0)>0\qquad\text{for all $s\in(0,1]$.}

Consider again
{s_{*}}\in(0,1]
and a sequence
(s_{n})_{n}\subset(0,1)
with
s_{n}\to{s_{*}}
. Then we have

(3.24)

\lambda_{2,{s_{*}}}\geq\limsup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{2,s_{n}}

Indeed, if
\varepsilon>0
is given, we may, by the variational characterization (
3.18
) and the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
, find a two-dimensional subspace
{\mathcal{S}}
of radial functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
with the property that

\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}[\varphi]_{s}^{2}\leq\lambda_{2,{s_{*}}}+\varepsilon,\qquad\text{where $\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}:=\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}\>:\>\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B)}=1\}$.}

Applying (
3.18
) again, we thus deduce that

\lambda_{2,s_{n}}\leq\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}[\varphi]_{s_{n}}^{2}=\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}[\varphi]_{s}^{2}+o(1)\leq\lambda_{2,{s_{*}}}+\varepsilon+o(1)\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty.$}

Thus (
3.24
) follows. Using the regularity estimate given in
[
33
, Theorem 1.3]
together with the facts that
(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi_{2,s_{n}}=\lambda_{2,s_{n}}\varphi_{s_{n},2}
in
B
and
\lambda_{2,s_{n}}\leq C(N)
, we may now argue as above to see that, up to passing to a subsequence, we have
\lambda_{2,s_{n}}\to\lambda_{*}
and
\varphi_{2,s_{n}}\to{v_{*}}
in
C(\overline{B})
for some function
{v_{*}}\in C(\overline{B})\cap{\mathcal{H}}^{{s_{*}}}(B)
satisfying
(-\Delta)^{{s_{*}}}{v_{*}}=\lambda_{*}{v_{*}}
, while also

(3.25)

\lambda_{*}=\frac{[{v_{*}}]_{{s_{*}}}^{2}}{\|{v_{*}}\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{[\varphi_{2,s_{n}}]_{s_{n}}^{2}}{\|\varphi_{2,s_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{2,s_{n}}.

Moreover, by (
3.23
) we have
\|{v_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}=1
and
{v_{*}}(0)>0
, whereas

\int_{B}{v_{*}}\varphi_{1,\overline{s}}\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B}\varphi_{2,s_{n}}\varphi_{1,s_{n}}\,dx=0

by
3.22
. Hence
v
is sign changing, which implies that
\lambda_{*}\geq\lambda_{2,{s_{*}}}
. On the other hand,

\lambda_{*}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{2,s_{n}}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{2,s_{n}}\leq\lambda_{2,{s_{*}}}

by (
3.24
) and (
3.25
), so equality holds. Since
\|{v_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}=1
and
{v_{*}}(0)>0
, it thus follows from (
3.23
) and the simplicity of
\lambda_{2,{s_{*}}}
that
{v_{*}}=\varphi_{2,\overline{s}}
.

Consequently, we have shown that for any
{s_{*}}\in(0,1]
we have

(3.26)

\varphi_{2,s}\to\varphi_{2,{s_{*}}}\quad\textrm{ in $C(\overline{B})$}\qquad\textrm{ as $s\to{s_{*}}$.}

We now recall that
\varphi_{2,s}
changes at most sign twice in the radial variable for all
s\in(0,1)
by Corollary
2.7
. By (
3.16
), we have
\varphi_{2,1}(0)\int_{B}\varphi_{2,1}\,dx<0
. Hence by (
3.26
) and Proposition
3.3
, there exists
s_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\varphi_{2,s}
changes sign precisely once in the radial variable for all
s\in(s_{0},1)
. We define

s_{*}:=\inf\{s_{0}\in(0,1]\,:\,\textrm{ $\varphi_{2,s}$ changes sign only once in the radial variable for all $s\in(s_{0},1)$}\}.

The proof finishes once we show that
s_{*}=0
. Assume on the contrary that
s_{*}>0
.
Then by (
3.26
) and the definition of
s_{*}
,
\varphi_{2,s_{*}}
changes sign only once in the radial variable and thus by Proposition
3.3

\varphi_{2,s_{*}}(0)\int_{B}\varphi_{2,s_{*}}\,dx<0.

On the other hand Proposition
3.3
implies that
\varphi_{2,\tau}(0)\int_{B}\varphi_{2,\tau}\,dx\geq 0
for all
\tau\in(0,s_{*})
. Hence letting
\tau\nearrow s_{*}
and using (
3.26
), we find that
\varphi_{2,s_{*}}(0)\int_{B}\varphi_{2,s_{*}}\,dx\geq 0
. This leads to a contradiction and thus
s_{*}=0
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
V
satisfy (
1.8
), and let
w_{2}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{rad}(B)
be a nontrivial solution to (
1.1
) with
\sigma=\sigma_{2}(V)
.
Then
w_{2}
changes sign precisely once in the radial variable.

Proof.

For
\tau\in[0,1]
, we define

V_{\tau}:B\to\mathbb{R},\qquad V_{\tau}(x)=\tau V(x),

and we let
w_{2,\tau}
be an eigenfunction associated to
\sigma_{2}(V_{\tau})
. By Theorem
3.2
, we may normalize
w_{2,\tau}
such that

(3.27)

\|w_{2,\tau}\|_{L^{2}(B)}=1\quad\text{and}\quad w_{2,\tau}(0)>0\qquad\text{for all $\tau\in[0,1]$.}

Applying Lemma
2.2
, Theorem
3.2
and (
3.27
), we find that, for every
\overline{\tau}\in[0,1]
,

(3.28)

w_{2,\tau}\to w_{2,\overline{\tau}}\quad\textrm{ in $C(\overline{B})$ }\quad\quad\textrm{ as $\tau\to\overline{\tau}$.}

Moreover, for all
\tau\in[0,1]
, the function
w_{2,\tau}
changes sign at most twice in the radial variable by Corollary
2.7
. In addition
w_{2,0}(0)\int_{B}w_{2,0}dx<0
by Proposition
3.4
.
Therefore from (
3.28
) and Proposition
3.3
, there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
such that
w_{2,\tau}
changes sign only once in the radial variable for all
\tau\in[0,\varepsilon]
.
We define

\tau_{*}:=\sup\{\varepsilon\in[0,1]\,:\,\textrm{ $w_{2,\tau}$ changes sign only once for all $\tau\in[0,\varepsilon]$}\}.

By definition of
\tau_{*}
, (
3.28
) and Proposition
3.3
, we see that
w_{2,\tau_{*}}
changes sign only once. In particular

(3.29)

w_{2,\tau_{*}}(0)\int_{B}w_{2,\tau_{*}}dx<0.

We claim that
\tau_{*}=1
. Indeed, if we had
\tau_{*}<1
, then Proposition
3.3
would yield

w_{2,\tau}(0)\int_{B}w_{2,\tau}dx\geq 0\quad\textrm{ for all $\tau\in(\tau_{*},1)$. }

Letting
\tau\searrow\tau_{*}
in the above inequality and using (
3.28
), we get
w_{2,\tau_{*}}(0)\int_{B}w_{2,\tau_{*}}dx\geq 0
which contradicts (
3.29
). As consequence (
3.29
) holds with
\tau_{*}=1
. Combining this with Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
3.2
, we conclude that
w_{2,1}\in\bigl\{\frac{w_{2}}{\|w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(B)}};\frac{-w_{2}}{\|w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(B)}}\bigr\}
changes sign precisely once in the radial variable, as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The continuity argument we use in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is inspired by the work of Frank, Lenzmann and Silvestre
[
21
]
. However, our arguments here allow to simplify the proof in
[
21
]
of the property that, if
V
is a nondecreasing radial Hölder continuous potential, a simple second radial eigenfunction of
(-\Delta)^{s}+V
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
changes sign precisely once . Note that this property is established in
[
21
]
via a continuity argument along a one parameter family of equations
(-\Delta)^{s_{\tau}}+V_{\tau}
which interpolates between
(-\Delta)^{s}+V
and
-\Delta+V_{\varepsilon}
with
V_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon e^{-|x|^{2}})
for some

Considering the corresponding branch of eigenfunctions

, the expansion of the Green function of the operator

on

is used in
[

]
to derive an open condition given by the sign of

3.3
that this sign is given by
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t^{N}W_{2,\tau}(0,t)
where

is the

-harmonic extension of

.

With the help of a new local Hopf-type Lemma for the

-harmonic extension given in Theorem

in the appendix, we shall now prove that the fractional normal derivative of a radial second eigenfunction of (

) is nontrivial.

4.
Nondegeneracy and uniqueness of ground state solutions

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
. For a radial function
v\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
v\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B
, we define

\psi_{v}\in L^{\infty}(0,1),\qquad\psi_{v}(|x|):=\frac{v(x)}{\text{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B)^{s}}=\frac{v(x)}{(1-|x|)^{s}}\qquad\text{for $x\in B$.}

and, as before, we define
\psi_{v}(1):=\liminf\limits_{\rho\nearrow 1}\psi_{v}(\rho)
.

We start by collecting some properties of solutions to (
1.13
). Throughout this section, we let
p\in(1,2^{*}_{s}-1)
and
\lambda\geq 0
be fixed, and we let
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
denote a fixed solution of (
1.13
).

We recall the following well-known properties of
u
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

u\in C^{\infty}(B)\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u
is radially symmetric and strictly decreasing.

(ii)

\psi_{u}
extends to a continuous function on
[0,1]
, and
\psi_{u}(1)>0
.

Proof.

As noted in
[
14
]
, we can apply
[
35
, Proposition 3.1]
to get
u\in L^{\infty}(B)
. Then, by a classical bootstrap argument using interior and boundary regularity (see
[
36
]
and
[
32
]
), we find that
u\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}(B)
, and that
\psi_{u}
extends to a continuous function on
[0,1]
. From
[
23
, Corollary 1.2]
we deduce that
u
is radially symmetric and strictly decreasing in the radial variable. Finally,
\psi_{u}(1)>0
follows from the fractional Hopf lemma, see e.g.
[
18
, Proposition 3.3]
.
∎

As a consequence, we note that
V=-pu^{p-1}
satisfies assumption (
1.8
), with
q=+\infty
. The following lemma has been proved in
[
14
]
in the case
N=1
. The proof is almost the same in the multidimensional case, but we prefer to give the details for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
be a solution to (
1.13
), and let
w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{rad}(B)
be a radial solution of

(4.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}w-pu^{p-1}w=-\lambda w\qquad\textrm{ in $B$.}

Then
\psi_{w}\in C([0,1])
and

(4.2)

\int_{B}u^{p}wdx=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad[u,w]_{s}=-\lambda\int_{B}wu\,dx.

Moreover, the fractional normal derivatives
\psi_{u}(1)
and
\psi_{w}(1)
of
u
and
w
satisfy the identity

(4.3)

2s\lambda\int_{B}uwdx=-\Gamma^{2}(1+s)|\partial B|\psi_{u}(1)\psi_{w}(1).

Proof.

We first note that it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
w\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{2s+\alpha}_{loc}(B)
and

In fact, this has been proved independently and simultaneously in the very recent papers

Assume by contradiction that a solution

For

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Gamma(1+s)^{2}\int_{\partial B}\frac{u}{\delta^{s}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}}{\delta^{s}}\nu_{1}d\sigma=\int_{B}\Bigl(\partial_{x_{1}}u(-\Delta)^{s}v_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{1}}v(-\Delta)^{s}u\Bigr)dx

Moreover, by (

W>0\qquad\text{in ${\bf B}_{r/2}^{+}(0)$.}

For fixed

as above, we let

We note that for

Appendix II: Some topological lemmas on curve intersection

In this appendix, we collect curve intersection properties which we have used in the previous sections. We start by citing the following lemma from
[
14
, Lemma 7.4]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}
be real numbers. Suppose that
\gamma,\eta:[0,1]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
are continuous curves such that
\gamma(0)=(x_{1},0)
,
\gamma(1)=(x_{3},0)
,
\eta(0)=(x_{2},0)
,
\eta(1)=(x_{4},0)
. Then the curves
\gamma
and
\eta
intersect, i.e. there exists
t,\tilde{t}\in(0,1)
with
\gamma(t)=\eta(\tilde{t})
.

We have also used the following slight generalization.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}
be real numbers, and let
\gamma,\eta:[0,1]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
be continuous curves. Moreover, suppose that one of the following is satisfied.

(i)

We have
\gamma(0)=(x_{1},0)
,
\gamma(1)=(x_{3},0)
,

|\eta(0)-(x_{2},0)|<{\rm dist}((x_{2},0),\gamma([0,1]))
and
|\eta(1)-(x_{4},0)|<{\rm dist}((x_{4},0),\gamma([0,1]))

(ii)

We have
\eta(0)=(x_{2},0)
,
\eta(1)=(x_{4},0)
,

|\gamma(0)-(x_{1},0)|<{\rm dist}((x_{1},0),\eta([0,1]))
and
|\gamma(1)-(x_{3},0)|<{\rm dist}((x_{3},0),\eta([0,1]))

Then the curves
\gamma
and
\eta
intersect.

Proof.

We only prove (i), the proof of (ii) is very similar. For two points
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we let
[a,b]:=\{ta+(1-t)b\,:\,t\in[0,1]\}
denote the closed line segment joining
a
and
b
. Then assumption (i) implies that the line segments
[(x_{2},0),\eta(0)]
and
[\eta(1),(x_{4},0)]
do not intersect the curve
\gamma
. On the other hand, adding these line segments to the curve
\eta
, we obtain a curve
\widetilde{\eta}:[0,1]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
joining the points
(x_{2},0)
and
(x_{4},0)
, so by Lemma
6.1
the curve
\widetilde{\eta}
does intersect
\gamma
. It therefore follows that also the original curve
\eta
must intersect
\gamma
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
t_{0}>0
, and let
0\leq x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}
. Suppose that
\gamma,\eta:[0,1]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}
are continuous curves such that
\gamma(0)=(0,t_{0})
,
\gamma(1)=(x_{3},0)
,
\eta(0)=(x_{2},0)
,
\eta(1)=(x_{4},0)
. Then the curves
\gamma
and
\eta
intersect.

Proof.

We define the continuous curve

\widetilde{\gamma}:[-1,1]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}},\qquad\widetilde{\gamma}(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\gamma_{1}(|t|),\gamma_{2}(|t|))&&\qquad\text{if $t<0$,}\\
&(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))&&\qquad\text{if $t\geq 0$.}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

This curve joins the points
(-x_{3},0)
and
(x_{3},0)
. Since
-x_{3}<x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}
, the curve
\widetilde{\gamma}
must intersect
\eta
by Lemma
6.1
. Since
\eta([0,1])\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, this implies that
\eta
intersects
\gamma
, as claimed.
∎

By the same argument as for Corollary
6.2
, we can weaken the assumptions slightly to obtain the following statement.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
t_{0}>0
, and let
0\leq x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}
. Suppose that
\gamma,\eta:[0,1]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}
are continuous curves such that
\gamma(0)=(0,t_{0})
,
\gamma(1)=(x_{3},0)
and

|\eta(0)-(x_{2},0)|<{\rm dist}((x_{2},0),\gamma([0,1])),\qquad|\eta(1)-(x_{4},0)|<\\
dist((x_{4},0),\gamma([0,1])).

Then the curves
\gamma
and
\eta
intersect.

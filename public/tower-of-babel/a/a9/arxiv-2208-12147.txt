For

-local cohomology module of

3.
Some Properties of
L^{I}(M)

In this section we collect some of the properties of
L^{I}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M/I^{n+1}M
which we proved in
[
5
]
. Throughout this section

(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a local ring with infinite residue field,
M
is a
Cohen-Macaulay
module of dimension
r\geq 1
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.

3.1
.

Set
\mathcal{R}(I)=A[It]
; the Rees Algebra of
I
. In
[
5
, 4.2]
we proved that

L^{I}(M)
is a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module. Note that
L^{I}(M)
is
not finitely generated

\mathcal{R}(I)
-module.

3.2
.

Let
H^{i}(-)=H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}}
denote the
i^{th}
-local cohomology functor with respect to
\mathfrak{M}
.
Recall a graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module
V
is said to be

*-Artinian
if
every descending chain of graded submodules of
V
terminates. For example if
E
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module then
H^{i}(E)
is *-Artinian for all

i\geq 0
.

3.3
.

In
[
5
, 4.7]
we proved that

H^{0}(L^{I}(M))=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\frac{\widetilde{I^{n+1}M}}{I^{n+1}M}.

Here
\widetilde{KM}
denotes the Ratliff-Rush closure of
M
with respect to an ideal
K
. Recall

\widetilde{KM}=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}K^{i+1}M\colon K^{i}.

3.4
.

For
L^{I}(M)
we proved that for
0\leq i\leq r-1

(a)

H^{i}(L^{I}(M))
are *-Artinian; see
[
5
, 4.4]
.

(b)

H^{i}(L^{I}(M))_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
; see
[
5
, 1.10 ]
.

(c)

H^{i}(L^{I}(M))_{n}
has finite length
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
; see
[
5
, 6.4]
.

(d)

For
0\leq i\leq r-1

there exists polynomial
q_{i}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}[z]
such that
q_{i}(n)=\ell(H^{i}(L^{I}(M))_{n})
for all
n\ll 0
.

3.5
.

Let
x
be
M
-superficial with respect to
I
, i.e.,
(I^{n+1}M\colon x)=I^{n}M
for all
n\gg 0
. Set
N=M/xM
and
u=xt\in\mathcal{R}(I)_{1}
. Notice
L^{I}(M)/uL^{I}(M)=L^{I}(N)
.
For each
n\geq 1
we have the following exact sequence of
A
-modules:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{I^{n+1}M\colon x}{I^{n}M}\longrightarrow\frac{M}{I^{n}M}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\psi_{n}}\frac{M}{I^{n+1}M}\longrightarrow\frac{N}{I^{n+1}N}\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad\psi_{n}(m+I^{n}M)

\displaystyle=xm+I^{n+1}M.

This sequence induces the following exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules:

(1)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)\longrightarrow L^{I}(M)(-1)\xrightarrow{\Psi_{u}}L^{I}(M)\xrightarrow{\rho^{x}}L^{I}(N)\longrightarrow 0,

where
\Psi_{u}
is left multiplication by
u
and

\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\frac{(I^{n+1}M\colon_{M}x)}{I^{n}M}.

We call (
1
) the
second fundamental exact sequence.

3.6
.

Notice
\lambda\left(\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)\right)<\infty
. A standard trick yields the following long exact sequence connecting
the local cohomology of
L^{I}(M)
and

L^{I}(N)
:

(2)

\begin{split}0\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)&\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(M))\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(N))\\
&\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(M))\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(N))\\
&\cdots\cdots\\
\end{split}

3.7
.

One huge advantage of considering
L^{I}(M)
is that it behaves well with respect to the Veronese functor. Notice

\left(L^{I}(M)(-1)\right)^{<t>}=L^{I^{t}}(M)(-1)\quad\text{for all}\ t\geq 1.

Also note that
\mathcal{R}(I)^{<t>}=\mathcal{R}(I^{t})
and that
(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I)})^{<t>}=\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I^{t})}
. It follows that for all
i\geq 0

\left(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I)}}(L^{I}(M)(-1)\right)^{<t>}\cong H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I^{t})}}(L^{I^{t}}(M)(-1).

4.
results on dimensions of dual’s of local cohomology modules

Throughout this section
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a complete Noetherian local ring with infinite
residue field and
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module of dimension
r\geq 1
. Furthermore we will assume that
I
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.
In this section we prove some results regarding dimensions of dual’s of graded local cohomology modules of
G_{I}(M)
and
L^{I}(M)
. Throughout we compute local cohomology with respect to to the graded maximal ideal of the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=A[It]
. If
E
is a graded
\mathcal{R}
-module then we denote its Matlis dual by
E^{\vee}
.

4.1
.

Let
mod^{f}(\mathcal{R}(I))
be the category of finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules
M
with
\lambda(M)_{n}<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
A^{f}(\mathcal{R}(I))
be the category of
*
-Artinian graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules
L
with
\lambda(L)_{n}<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The usual Matlis duality between finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}
-modules and
*
-Artinian
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules (see
[
2
, 3.6.17]
) restricts to a duality between

mod^{f}(\mathcal{R}(I))
and
A^{f}(\mathcal{R}(I))
.

4.2
.

Let
M\in mod^{f}(\mathcal{R}(I))
. Then it is not difficult to show that there exists a non-empty Zariski open subset
U
of
I/\mathfrak{m}I
such that if
x\in I
such that
\overline{x}\in U
then
\ker(M(-1)\xrightarrow{xt}M)
has finite length. By Matlis duality we also obtain that

\operatorname{coker}(M^{\vee}(-1)\xrightarrow{xt}M^{\vee})
has finite length.

4.3
.

Let
E\in A^{f}(\mathcal{R}(I))
. Then by Matlis duality it follows that there exists a polynomial
q_{E}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}[z]
such that
q_{E}(n)=\lambda(E_{n})
for all
n\ll 0
. Furthermore if
E\neq 0
then
\deg q_{E}(z)=\dim E^{\vee}-1
. We call
q_{E}(z)
as the
dual Hilbert-polynomial
of
E
.

Our first result is

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
M
be a Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module of dimension
r\geq 1
. Then
\dim H^{i}(L^{I}(M))^{\vee}\leq i
for
0\leq i\leq r-1
.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
r=\dim M
. If
r=1
then by
3.3

we get that
H^{0}(L^{I}(M))
has finite length. So we have nothing to show.
Now assume
r\geq 2
and the result has been shown for Cohen-Macaulay modules of dimension
=r-1
. By
4.2
we may choose
x\in I
which is
M
-superficial and

\operatorname{coker}H^{i}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\xrightarrow{xt}H^{i}(L^{I}(M)))
has finite length for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
.
Let
q_{i}^{M}(z)
be the dual Hilbert-polynomial of
H^{i}(L^{I}(M))
.
By
3.3

we get that
H^{0}(L^{I}(M))
has finite length. Set
N=M/xM
. Now assume that

1\leq i\leq r-1
. Then by our construction and
3.6
there exists finite length modules
U_{i},V_{i}
such that we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow U_{i}\rightarrow H^{i-1}(L^{I}(N))\rightarrow H^{i}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\xrightarrow{xt}H^{i}(L^{I}(M)))\rightarrow V_{i}\rightarrow 0.

By our induction hypothesis it follows that
\deg(q_{i}^{M}(z)-q_{i}^{M}(z-1))\leq i-2
. So
\deg q_{i}^{M}(z)\leq i-1
. So
\dim H^{i}(L^{I}(M))^{\vee}\leq i
. The result follows.
∎

.

Let
M
be a Cohen-Macaulay

-module of dimension

. Assume that there is
s
with

\dim H^{i}(G_{I}(M))^{\vee}\leq i-1
. Then there exists a non-empty Zariski-open subset
W
of

such that if

and
\overline{x}\in W
we have
\dim H^{i}(G_{I}(M/xM))^{\vee}\leq i-1
for

Proof.

By

it follows that we may choose
a non-empty Zariski-open subset
W
of
I/\mathfrak{m}I
such that if
x\in I
and
\overline{x}\in U
then
x
which is

M
-superficial and

\operatorname{coker}H^{i}(G_{I}(M))(-1)\xrightarrow{xt}H^{i}(G_{I}(M)))
has finite length for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
.
Set
N=M/xM
.
Then by our construction there exists finite length modules
U_{i},V_{i}
such that we have an exact sequence for

Using dual Hilbert polynomials
it follows that for

H^{i}(G_{I}(N))\cong H^{i}(G_{I}(M)/xtG_{I}(M))\quad\text{ for $i\geq 1$}.

∎

(2)

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we prove our main result.

5.1
.

For our arguments we have to go to the completion. The Ratliff-Rush closure and integral closure of an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal behave well with respect to completion. However to the best of the authors knowledge it is not known whether other coefficient ideals behave well with respect to completion.
Set

E_{i}^{\prime}(I)=\{J\mid J\supset\widetilde{I}\ \text{and}\ e_{j}(J)=e_{j}(I)\ \ \text{for}\ 0\leq j\leq i\}.

It is clear that if
J\in E_{i}^{\prime}(I)
then
J\widehat{A}\in E_{i}^{\prime}(I\widehat{A})
.

5.2
.

Fix
i
with
1\leq i\leq d
. Let
J\in E_{i}^{\prime}(I)
then note
J\subseteq\overline{I}
. So
\mathcal{R}(J)
is a finite
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module. Set
W(J)=\mathcal{R}(J)/\mathcal{R}(I)
. Note
W(J)_{n}
has finite length for all
n
. We show

Proposition 5.3
.

(with setup as in
5.2
)
\dim W(J)\leq d-i
.

Proof.

Set
W=W(J)
.
From the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow W(+1)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\frac{A}{I^{n+1}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\frac{A}{I_{i}^{n+1}}\rightarrow 0

we have

\displaystyle\lambda(W_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\lambda(A/I^{n+1})-\lambda(A/I_{i}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\{e_{0}(I)\binom{n+d}{d}+\cdots+(-1)^{j}e_{j}(I)\binom{n+d-j}{d-j}+\cdots\}

\displaystyle\quad-\{e_{0}(J)\binom{n+d}{d}+\cdots+(-1)^{j}e_{j}(J)\binom{n+d-j}{d-j}+\cdots\}

for all
n\gg 0
. As
e_{j}(I)=e_{j}(J)
for all
0\leq j\leq i
so we get

\lambda(W_{n+1})=(-1)^{i+1}\left(e_{i+1}(I)-e_{i+1}(J)\right)\binom{n+d-i-1}{d-i-1}+\mbox{ lower terms}.

Thus
n\mapsto\lambda(W_{n+1})
is a polynomial of degree at most
d-i-1
. Hence
\dim W\leq d-i
.
∎

Theorem
1.1
is an easy consequence of the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
and with infinite residue field. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
A
.
Fix an integer
r
with
1\leq r\leq d-1
. Assume
\dim H^{i}(L^{I}(A))^{\vee}\leq i-1
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Then for
all
n\geq 1
and
E_{d-r}^{\prime}(I^{n})=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}
.

Proof.

We will first prove the result when
n=1
. For the convenience of the reader we prove the case when
r=1
. It suffices to show that

E_{d-1}^{\prime}(I)=\{\widetilde{I}\}
. As discussed earlier we may assume that
A
is complete. Let
J\in E_{d-1}^{\prime}(I)
. Set
W=W(J)=\mathcal{R}(J)/\mathcal{R}(I)
.
By
5.3
we get
\dim W\leq 1
.
We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow W(+1)\rightarrow L^{I}(A)\rightarrow L^{J}(A)\rightarrow 0.

This induces a long exact sequence in cohomology

\cdots\rightarrow H^{0}(L^{J}(A))\rightarrow H^{1}(W(+1))\rightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(A))\rightarrow\cdots.

Taking Matlis duals we obtain an exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(A))^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{1}(W(+1))^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{0}(L^{J}(A))^{\vee}\rightarrow\cdots.

By our assumption and
3.3
it follows that
\dim H^{1}(W(+1))\leq 0
, As
\dim W\leq 1

it follows from
2.7
that
W
is zero-dimensional. Thus
J^{m}=I^{m}
for all
m\gg 0
. Therefore
J\subseteq\widetilde{I}
. But by definition of
E_{d-1}^{\prime}(I)

we have
J\supseteq\widetilde{I}
. Thus
E_{d-1}^{\prime}(I)=\{\widetilde{I}\}
.

Now assume
r\geq 2
. Let
J\in E_{d-r}^{\prime}(I)
. Set Set
W=W(J)=\mathcal{R}(J)/\mathcal{R}(I)
.
By
5.3
we get
\dim W\leq r
. We assert
\dim W=0
. Suppose if possible
\dim W=c>0
. Note
c\leq r
.
We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow W(+1)\rightarrow L^{I}(A)\rightarrow L^{J}(A)\rightarrow 0.

This induces a long exact sequence in cohomology

\cdots\rightarrow H^{c-1}(L^{J}(A))\rightarrow H^{c}(W(+1))\rightarrow H^{c}(L^{I}(A))\rightarrow\cdots.

Taking Matlis duals we obtain an exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow H^{c}(L^{I}(A))^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{c}(W(+1))^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{c-1}(L^{J}(A))^{\vee}\rightarrow\cdots.

By our assumption and
4.4
it follows that
\dim H^{c}(W(+1))\leq c-1
. This contradicts
2.7
. So
\dim W=0
. Thus
J^{m}=I^{m}
for all
m\gg 0
. Therefore
J\subseteq\widetilde{I}
. But by definition of
E_{d-r}^{\prime}(I)

we have
J\supseteq\widetilde{I}
. Thus
E_{d-r}^{\prime}(I)=\{\widetilde{I}\}
.
Thus we have proved the result when
n=1
.

Now assume that
n>1
. We note that

L^{I^{n}}(A)(-1)
is the
n^{th}
-Veronese module of
L^{I}(A)(-1)
; see
3.7
. As local cohomology module commutes with Veronese it follows that

\dim H^{i}(L^{I^{n}}(A))\leq i-1
for
1\leq i\leq r
. The result now follows from the
n=1
case.
∎

We now give

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It suffices to prove that
E_{d-r}^{\prime}(I^{n})=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}
for all
n\geq 1
. We note that
G(I)=G(I\widehat{A})
. By
5.1
we may assume
that
A
is complete. By
4.6
it follows that
\dim H^{i}(L^{I}(A))^{\vee}\leq i-1
for
1\leq i\leq r
. The result now follows from Theorem

.

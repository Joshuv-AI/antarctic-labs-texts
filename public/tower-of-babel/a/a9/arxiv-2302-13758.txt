Non-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms and Katz p p -adic L L -functions
Luis Santiago Eduardo Palacios Moyano

Abstract
. Let K K be an imaginary quadratic field. In this article, we construct p p -adic L L -functions of non-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms by introducing the notions of C C -cuspidality and partial Bianchi modular symbols. When p p splits in K K , we focus on p p -adic L L -functions of non-cuspidal base change Bianchi modular forms, showing that they factor as products of two Katz p p -adic L L -functions.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime number. The study of
p
-adic
L
-functions has become a cornerstone of modern number theory, as these functions often serve as a vital link between algebraic number theory, arithmetic geometry, and analysis.

In
[
PS11
]
, Pollack and Stevens constructed the
p
-adic
L
-function of cuspidal modular forms using the theory of overconvergent modular symbols. Their work has since inspired numerous generalizations (see for instance
[
BS18
]
,
[
Wil17
]
,
[
BW19
]
, for the case of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
). For non-cuspidal modular forms, Bellaïche and Dasgupta introduced the notions of
C
-cuspidality and partial modular symbols in
[
BD15
]
, which allowed them to construct the
p
-adic
L
-function of Eisenstein series.

Now, let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field, and consider automorphic forms for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
over
K
, commonly referred to as Bianchi modular forms. For the cuspidal case, Williams constructed the
p
-adic
L
-function in
[
Wil17
]
by developing the theory of Bianchi modular symbols.

In this article, we extend these ideas to the non-cuspidal setting, constructing the
p
-adic
L
-function for non-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms by combining and adapting the approaches of Williams and Bellaïche-Dasgupta. Additionally, we establish a significant connection between Katz’s
p
-adic
L
-functions and our construction in the context of non-cuspidal base change Bianchi modular forms.

1.1.
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms and partial symbols

For our study of non-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms, in Section
2.3
we generalize to the Bianchi setting the notion of
C
-cuspidality
given in
[
BD15
]
. Such property is related with the vanishing of constant terms of Fourier expansions at suitable cusps and for Bianchi modular forms with level at
p
we have:

\{\text{cuspidal}\}\subset\{C\textrm{-cuspidal}\}\subset\{\text{Bianchi modular forms}\}.

For cuspidal Bianchi modular forms, an integral formula is known for their complex
L
-functions. In Proposition
2.19
, we establish a similar formula for
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms.

In Section
3
, we introduce algebraic analogues of
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms, called
partial Bianchi modular symbols
, that are easier to study
p
-adically. To define them, we generalize the classical partial modular symbols introduced in
[
BD15
]
and adapt the Bianchi modular symbols of parallel weight used in
[
Wil17
]
. In Proposition
3.9
, we prove that we can attach, in a Hecke-equivariant way, a partial Bianchi modular symbol to a
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular form.

To link partial Bianchi modular symbols with spaces of
p
-adic distributions, we introduce the
overconvergent partial Bianchi modular symbols
in Section
4.2
and generalizing
[
Wil17
]
, we obtain a classicality result in Proposition
4.3
.

In Section
4.4
, we construct the
p
-adic
L
-function of a
C
-cuspidal Bianchi eigenform
\mathcal{F}
of weight
(k,\ell)
and level
K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
, with
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
-eigenvalues
\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
having suitable
p
-adic valuation (see Definition
4.5
). For this, we first attach to
\mathcal{F}
a complex-valued partial Bianchi modular eigensymbol
\phi_{\mathcal{F}}
using Proposition
3.9
. Then, using an isomorphism
\iota:\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
(satisfying
\iota\circ\iota_{\infty}=\iota_{p}
with some previously fixed embeddings
\iota_{\infty}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C},\iota_{p}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
), we view
p
-adically the values of
\phi_{\mathcal{F}}
and then lift it uniquely to an overconvergent partial Bianchi modular eigensymbol
\Psi_{\mathcal{F}}
using Proposition
4.3
. After taking the Mellin transform of
\Psi_{\mathcal{F}}
, we obtain (see Theorem
4.12
for the precise statement):

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a locally analytic distribution
L_{p}^{\iota}(\mathcal{F},-)
on the ray class group
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
, such that for any Hecke character
\psi
of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}|(p^{\infty})
and infinity type
(q,r)
satisfying
0\leqslant q\leqslant k,0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
, we have

L_{p}^{\iota}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=\left[\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\psi(\mathfrak{p})}\right)\right]\left[\frac{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}{(-1)^{\ell+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi),

where
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}
is the
p
-adic avatar of
\psi
,
-D_{K}
is the discriminant of
K
,
w_{K}=|\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|
,
\tau(\cdot)
is a Gauss sum,
\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}
is the eigenvalue of
U_{\mathfrak{f}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{r_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
\Lambda(\mathcal{F},-)
is the normalized
L
-function of
\mathcal{F}
as in (
2.8
).

The distribution
L_{p}^{\iota}(\mathcal{F},-)
satisfies suitable growth conditions (see Section
4.3
) and therefore is unique.

By part ii) of Remark
4.13
, we can construct the
p
-adic
L
-function of certain non-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms by turning them into
C
-cuspidal forms and applying the previous theorem.

1.2.
Non-cuspidal base change and Katz
p
-adic
L
-functions

A case of historical interest where we can apply the methods outlined in Section
1.1
to construct
p
-adic
L
-functions is the non-cuspidal base change scenario.

Suppose that
p
splits in
K
and let
\varphi
be a Hecke character of
K
with conductor coprime to
p
and infinity type
(-k-1,0)
with
k\geqslant 0
. Denote by
\theta_{\varphi}
the theta series attached to
\varphi
, which is a cuspidal modular form of weight
k+2
and let
\theta_{\varphi/K}
be the base change to
K
of
\theta_{\varphi}
, which is known to be a non-cuspidal Bianchi modular form of weight
(k,k)
. In Proposition
5.3
, we prove that the ordinary
p
-stabilization of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
, which we denote by
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
, is a
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular form. In particular, by Theorem
1.1
, we can obtain its
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}^{\iota}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)
.

Part of our interest in the Bianchi modular form
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
relies on the fact that we can avoid the use of an isomorphism
\iota:\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
to construct its
p
-adic
L
-function, and additionally we can factorize its
p
-adic
L
-function as the product of two Katz
p
-adic
L
-functions.

In Section
5.2
, we study the
L
-function of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
and prove that it factors as the product of two Hecke
L
-functions in Lemma
5.4
. Using such factorization, combined with an algebraicity result of Hecke
L
-functions, we show the existence of a
complex period

\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}
, which allows us to prove algebraicity of critical
L
-values of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
and
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
in Proposition
5.7
and Corollary
5.8
, respectively.

Let
\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}
be the complex-valued partial Bianchi modular symbol attached to
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
in Proposition
3.9
, then by defining
\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}^{\mathrm{alg}}:=\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}
we prove in Proposition
5.9
that
\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}^{\mathrm{alg}}
has algebraic values and consequently, we can view it as having
p
-adic values. Finally, using Proposition
4.3
, we can lift
\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}^{\mathrm{alg}}
to an overconvergent Bianchi modular symbol and taking its Mellin transform we obtain the following (see Theorem
5.10
):

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a unique measure
L_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)
on the ray class group
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
such that for any Hecke character
\psi
of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{p}^{t}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{s}
and infinity type
(q,r)
satisfying
0\leqslant q,r\leqslant k
, we have

L_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=\left[\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|p}\left(1-\frac{1}{\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})\psi(\mathfrak{q})}\right)\right]\left[\frac{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})^{t+s}\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}}\right]\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi).

The
p
-adic
L
-functions
L_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)
and
L_{p}^{\iota}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)
, can be related, more precisely, in Proposition
5.11
we prove the following equality of measures:

L_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)=\frac{L_{p}^{\iota}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)}{\iota(\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}})}.

The factorization of the
L
-function of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
in Lemma
5.4
translates to the
p
-adic side. In fact, consider the
p
-adic
L
-function of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
of Theorem
1.2
and the
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}(-)
constructed by Katz in
[
Kat78
]
and generalized by Hida and Tilouine in
[
HT93
]
. We obtain the following (see Theorem
5.13
for more details):

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.2
, for all
\kappa
we have

L_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\kappa)=\frac{L_{p}(\varphi_{p-\rm{fin}}^{c}\kappa\chi_{p})L_{p}(\varphi_{p-\rm{fin}}^{c}\kappa^{c}\lambda_{p}\chi_{p})}{\Omega_{p}^{2k+2}},

where
\lambda_{p},\chi_{p}
are the
p
-adic avatars of the character
\lambda_{K}
(see Lemma
5.4
) and the adelic norm
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}
respectively, and
\Omega_{p}
is the
p
-adic period in Theorem
5.12
.

2.
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms

In this section, we recall basic properties of Bianchi modular forms and define a suitable vanishing condition on its constant terms called
C
-cuspidality. In Section
2.4
we study
L
-functions of
C
-cuspidal forms.

2.1.
Notations

Let
p
be a rational prime and fix throughout the paper embeddings
\iota_{\infty}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\iota_{p}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, note that
\iota_{p}
fixes a
p
-adic valuation
v_{p}
on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field with discriminant
-D_{K}
, ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and different ideal
\mathcal{D}_{K}
generated by
\delta=\sqrt{-D_{K}}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}=(p)\mathfrak{m}
be an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
\mathfrak{m}
coprime to
(p)
. We denote by
id
and
c
the two embeddings of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
and identify them with
\mathrm{Hom}(K,\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
under
\iota_{\infty}
. Henceforth, we write
(k,\ell)
for
k\cdot id+\ell\cdot c\in\mathbb{Z}[{id,c}]
, with
k,\ell\geqslant 0
. Denote by
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
the completion of
K
with respect to the prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}
the ring of integers of
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
and fix a uniformizer
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
at
\mathfrak{q}
. Denote the adele ring of
K
by
\mathbb{A}_{K}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}
where
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}
are the finite adeles. Furthermore, denote the class group of
K
by
\mathrm{Cl}(K)
and the class number of
K
by
h
, and -once and for all- fix a set of representatives
I_{1},...,I_{h}
for
\mathrm{Cl}(K)
, with
I_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
and each
I_{i}
for
2\leqslant i\leqslant h
integral and prime, with each
I_{i}
coprime to
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{K}
.
Let
V_{n}(R)
denote the space of homogeneous polynomials over a ring
R
in two variables of degree
n\geqslant 0
. Note that
V_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is an irreducible complex right representation of
\mathrm{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and denote it by
\rho_{n}
.

For a general Hecke character
\psi
of
K
we denote by
\psi_{\infty}
,
\psi_{f}
the restriction of
\psi
to
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f,\times}
, respectively. Likewise, for a prime
\mathfrak{q}
, we denote
\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}
the restriction of
\psi
to
K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}
, and for an ideal
\mathfrak{f}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
we write
\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}:=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{f}}\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

2.2.
Bianchi modular forms

We write
K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
for the open compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
of matrices
(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{smallmatrix})
modulo
\mathfrak{n}
. Let
\varphi
be a Hecke character, with infinity type
(-k,-\ell)
and conductor dividing
\mathfrak{n}
. For
u_{f}=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
we set
\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(u_{f})=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(d)=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{n}}\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}(d_{\mathfrak{q}})
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say a function
\mathcal{F}:\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K})\rightarrow V_{k+\ell+2}(\mathbb{C})
is a
Bianchi modular form
of weight
(k,\ell)
, level
K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
and central action
\varphi
if it satisfies:

i)
\mathcal{F}
is left-invariant under
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K)
;

ii)
\mathcal{F}(zg)=\varphi(z)\mathcal{F}(g)
for
z\in\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}\cong Z(\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K}))
, where
Z(G)
denotes the center of the group
G
;

iii)
\mathcal{F}(gu)=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(u_{f})\mathcal{F}(g)\rho_{k+\ell+2}(u_{\infty})
for
u=u_{f}\cdot u_{\infty}\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})\times\mathrm{SU_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
;

iv)
\mathcal{F}
is an eigenfunction of the operators
D_{id}
,
D_{c}
, with eigenvalues

k^{2}/2+k
and
\ell^{2}/2+\ell
respectively. Here
D_{\sigma}/4
, for
\sigma\in\{id,c\}
, denotes a component of the Casimir operator in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
(see
[
Hid94
, §2.4]
), and we are considering
\mathcal{F}(g_{\infty}g_{f})
as a function of
g_{\infty}\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C}).

The space of such functions will be denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
. We say
\mathcal{F}
is a
cuspidal
Bianchi modular form if also satisfies:

v) for all
g\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K})

\int_{K\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}}\mathcal{F}\left(\left(\begin{matrix}1&u\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)g\right)du=0,

where
du
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{A}_{K}
. The space of such functions will be denoted by
S_{(k,\ell)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
.

Remark 2.2
.

From
[
Hid94
, Cor 2.2]
) we have
S_{(k,\ell)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)=0
if
k\neq\ell
i.e., all non-trivial cuspidal Bianchi modular forms have parallel weight
(k,k)
.

Recall the set of representatives
I_{1},...,I_{h}
for
\mathrm{Cl}(K)
fixed in Section
2.1
and denote by
\varpi_{i}
their corresponding fixed uniformizer. Set
g_{i}=(\begin{smallmatrix}t_{i}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})
where
t_{1}=1
and for each
i\geqslant 2
, define
t_{i}=(1,...,1,\varpi_{i},1,...)\in\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}
. Since
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K})=\coprod_{i=1}^{h}\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K)\cdot g_{i}\cdot[\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\times K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})]
, a Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
, descends to a collection of
h
functions
F^{i}:\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow V_{k+\ell+2}(\mathbb{C})
via
F^{i}(g):=\mathcal{F}(g_{i}g)
.

Since
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\mathrm{SU_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
is isomorphic to the hyperbolic 3-space
\mathcal{H}_{3}:=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we can descend further using properties ii) and iii) in Definition
2.1
to obtain
h
functions
f^{i}:\mathcal{H}_{3}\longrightarrow V_{k+\ell+2}(\mathbb{C})
via
f^{i}(z,t):=t^{-1}F^{i}(\begin{smallmatrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})
.

Let
\gamma=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}):=\mathrm{SL_{2}}(K)\cap g_{i}K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})g_{i}^{-1}\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
, then each
f^{i}
satisfies the automorphy condition

(2.1)

f^{i}(\gamma\cdot(z,t))=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(d)^{-1}f^{i}(z,t)\rho_{k+\ell+2}(J(\gamma;(z,t))),

where
\gamma\cdot(z,t):=\left(\frac{(az+b)\overline{(cz+d)}+a\overline{c}|t|^{2}}{|cz+d|^{2}+|ct|^{2}},\frac{|ad-bc|t}{|cz+d|^{2}+|ct|^{2}}\right)
is the standard action of
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathcal{H}_{3}
, in which
|\cdot|
denotes the norm in
\mathbb{C}
; and
J(\gamma;(z,t)):=(\begin{smallmatrix}cz+d&\overline{ct}\\
-ct&\overline{cz+d}\end{smallmatrix})
. Thus
f^{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
, the space of
Bianchi modular forms
on
\mathcal{H}_{3}
satisfying the automorphy condition (
2.1
). If
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
is cuspidal then we say
f^{i}
is a cuspidal Bianchi modular form and the space of such forms is denoted by
S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
, for
f^{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
, define the function
f^{i}|_{\gamma}:\mathcal{H}_{3}\rightarrow V_{k+\ell+2}(\mathbb{C})
by

(2.2)

(f^{i}|_{\gamma})(z,t)=det(\gamma)^{-k/2}\overline{det(\gamma)}^{-\ell/2}f^{i}(\gamma\cdot(z,t))\rho_{k+\ell+2}^{-1}\left(J\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{det(\gamma)}};(z,t)\right)\right).

Remark 2.4
.

Note
f^{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
satisfies:

i)
f^{i}|_{g}(0,1)=F^{i}(g)
for
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, in particular, if
g=(\begin{smallmatrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})
with
(z,t)\in\mathcal{H}_{3}
, we recover
f^{i}(z,t)=t^{-1}F^{i}(\begin{smallmatrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})
.

ii)
\left(f^{i}|_{\gamma}\right)(z,t)=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(d)^{-1}f^{i}(z,t)
for
\gamma=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})
.

2.3.
Fourier expansion and cuspidal conditions

Recall that
k,\ell\geqslant 0
, consider the set
R_{k,\ell}=\{(\pm(k+1),\pm(\ell+1))\}
and for
r=(r_{1},r_{2})\in R_{k,\ell}
define

h(r)=\frac{k+\ell+2}{2}+\frac{r_{2}-r_{1}}{2},\;\;\text{and}\;\;g(r)=\left(\frac{1}{2}[r_{1}-(k+1)],\frac{1}{2}[r_{2}-(\ell+1)]\right).

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Bianchi modular form of weight
(k,\ell)
, level
K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
and central action
\varphi
, then it has a Fourier expansion given by (see
[
Hid94
, Thm 6.7]
):

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\left(\begin{matrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\right]=|t|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}\bigg[

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in R_{k,\ell}}t_{\infty}^{g(r)}\binom{k+\ell+2}{h(r)}c_{r}(t\mathcal{D}_{K},\mathcal{F})X^{k+\ell+2-h(r)}Y^{h(r)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}c(\alpha t\mathcal{D}_{K},\mathcal{F})W(\alpha t_{\infty})\textbf{e}_{K}(\alpha z)\bigg],

where

i)

The Fourier coefficients
c(\cdot,\mathcal{F})
, and
c_{r}(\cdot,\mathcal{F})
for each
r\in R_{k,\ell}
, are functions on the fractional ideals of
K
that vanish outside the integral ideals.

ii)

\textbf{e}_{K}
is an additive character of
K\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}
defined by

\textbf{e}_{K}=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\mathrm{prime}}(\textbf{e}_{q}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathbb{Q}_{q}})\right)\cdot(\textbf{e}_{\infty}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}),

for

\textbf{e}_{q}\left(\sum_{j}d_{j}q^{j}\right)=e^{-2\pi i\sum_{j<0}d_{j}q^{j}}\;\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;\;\textbf{e}_{\infty}(r)=e^{2\pi ir};

iii)

W:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow V_{k+\ell+2}(\mathbb{C})
is the Whittaker function

W(s):=\sum_{n=0}^{k+\ell+2}\binom{k+\ell+2}{n}\left(\frac{s}{i|s|}\right)^{\ell+1-n}K_{n-\ell-1}(4\pi|s|)X^{k+\ell+2-n}Y^{n},

where
K_{n}(x)
is the modified Bessel function of order
n
as in
[
Hid94
, §6]
.

Remark 2.5
.

i) Note that
W(s)
is not symmetric in
k
and
\ell
, this comes from the definition of the Whittaker function in
[
Hid94
, (6.1)]
after fixing the weight
(k,\ell)
.

ii) Let
\mathcal{F}=\sum_{n=0}^{k+\ell+2}\mathcal{F}_{n}X^{k+\ell+2-n}Y^{n}
be a Bianchi modular form, then by (
2.3
), the constant term in the Fourier expansion of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is trivial if
n\notin\{h(r):r\in R_{(k,\ell)}\}=\{0,k+1,\ell+1,k+\ell+2\}
. This fact will be used in the proof of Proposition
5.3
.

The Fourier expansion of
\mathcal{F}
descends to
\mathcal{H}_{3}
by

(2.4)

f^{j}\left((z,t);\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{k+\ell+2}f_{n}^{j}(z,t)X^{k+\ell+2-n}Y^{n},

where

\displaystyle f_{n}^{j}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\left[t^{\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}-k-\ell}{2}}\binom{k+\ell+2}{n}c_{r}(t_{j}\mathcal{D}_{K})\right]\delta_{n,h(r)}

\displaystyle+|t_{j}|_{f}t\binom{k+\ell+2}{n}\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}\left[c(\alpha t_{j}\mathcal{D}_{K})\left(\frac{\alpha}{i|\alpha|}\right)^{\ell+1-n}K_{n-\ell-1}(4\pi|\alpha|t)e^{2\pi i(\alpha z+\overline{\alpha z})}\right]

with
r=(r_{1},r_{2})\in R_{k,\ell}
as above and
\delta_{n,h(r)}=1
if
n=h(r)
and
\delta_{n,h(r)}=0
otherwise. Also note that to ease notation, we have written
c_{r}(t_{j}\mathcal{D}_{K})
(resp.
c(\alpha t_{j}\mathcal{D}_{K})
) instead
c_{r}(t_{j}\mathcal{D}_{K},\mathcal{F})
(resp.
c(\alpha t_{j}\mathcal{D}_{K},\mathcal{F})
).

For each
i=1,...,h
, equation (
2.3
) may be thought of as the Fourier expansion of
f^{i}
at the cusp of infinity, which by Remark
2.5
, satisfies that the constant term in the Fourier expansion of

is trivial if

.

We must consider Fourier expansions at all the “
K
-rational” cusps
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)=K\cup\{\infty\}
, for this, let
\sigma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
sending
\infty
to the cusp
s
. For each
i=1,...,h
, since
f^{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
then
f^{i}|_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(\sigma^{-1}\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})\sigma,\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
and hence
f^{i}|_{\sigma}
has a Fourier expansion as in (
2.3
).

Definition 2.6
.

We say that
f^{i}

vanishes
at the cusp
s
if
(f^{i}|_{\sigma})_{n}
has trivial constant term for
0\leqslant n\leqslant k+\ell+2
, and
quasi-vanishes
at the cusp
s
if
(f^{i}|_{\sigma})_{n}
has trivial constant term for
1\leqslant n\leqslant k+\ell+1
.

Remark 2.7
.

i) The property of vanishing and quasi-vanishing at the cusp
s
are well-defined, i.e., are independent of the choice of
\sigma
; for the vanishing case see
[
Byg98
, §6.2.2]
and note that the same argument works for quasi-vanishing.

ii) Let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
be a cuspidal Bianchi modular form, then the cuspidal condition v) in Definition
2.1
is equivalent to the vanishing of
f^{i}
at all cusps for each
0\leqslant i\leqslant h
(see
[
Zha93
, Prop 3.2]
).

Recall that
\mathfrak{n}=(p)\mathfrak{m}
with
\mathfrak{m}
coprime to
(p)
and define for each
i=1,...,h
the set of cusps

C_{i}:=\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})\infty\cup\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})0.

Since
\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})=\{(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(K):b\in I_{i},\;c\in\mathfrak{m}I_{i}^{-1}\}
, we have

C_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
contains
\infty
and elements
\frac{x}{y}\in K
with
x\in I_{i}
and either
y\in\mathfrak{m}
or
y\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that
f^{i}
is
C_{i}
-cuspidal
if quasi-vanishes at all cusps in
C_{i}
.

The previous definition of
C_{i}
-cuspidality differs from the one given in
[
BD15
]
for modular forms. We are not asking for the vanishing of
f^{i}
at the cusps
C_{i}
, instead, we just need quasi-vanishing, i.e., we do not care about the vanishing of the functions
f^{i}_{0}
and
f^{i}_{k+\ell+2}
at the cusps
C_{i}
. The motivation for considering quasi-vanishing instead of vanishing will become clear in Proposition
2.19
.

To state
C_{i}
-cuspidality for all
i
as a property of
\mathcal{F}
, we write
C:=(C_{1},...,C_{h})
and introduce the notion of
C
-cuspidality.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
C-cuspidal
if
f^{i}
is
C_{i}
-cuspidal for
i=1,...,h
.

Remark 2.10
.

Note that for Bianchi modular forms with level at
p
we have

\{\text{cuspidal}\}\subset\{C\text{-cuspidal}\}\subset\{\text{Bianchi modular forms}\}.

We now define Hecke operators acting on
\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
. For this, recall our fixed representatives
I_{1},...,I_{h}
for the class group, and note that for a prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
and each
i\in\{1,...,h\}
there is a unique
j_{i}\in\{1,...,h\}
such that
\mathfrak{q}I_{i}=(\alpha_{i})I_{j_{i}}
, for
\alpha_{i}\in K
. Then we define the Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
acting on Bianchi modular forms
\mathcal{F}=(f^{1},...,f^{h})
by double cosets on each component by

(2.5)

{}\mathcal{F}|_{T_{\mathfrak{q}}}:=\left(\alpha_{1}^{k}\overline{\alpha_{1}}^{\ell}f^{j_{1}}|_{\left[\Gamma_{0}^{j_{1}}(\mathfrak{n})(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{1}\end{smallmatrix})\Gamma_{0}^{1}(\mathfrak{n})\right]},...,\alpha_{h}^{k}\overline{\alpha_{h}}^{\ell}f^{j_{h}}|_{\left[\Gamma_{0}^{j_{h}}(\mathfrak{n})(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{h}\end{smallmatrix})\Gamma_{0}^{h}(\mathfrak{n})\right]}\right).

If
\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{n}
we denote the Hecke operator by
U_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Note that when
K
has class number one and
k=\ell
, the previous action is the same from
[
Pal23
, §2.4]
.

An eigenform is a Bianchi modular form that is a simultaneous eigenvector for all the Hecke operators, in this case, the eigenvalues and Fourier coefficients of the eigenform at each prime
\mathfrak{q}
coincides.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}
denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by the Hecke operators
\{T_{\mathfrak{q}}:(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})=1\}
and
\{U_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}|p\}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}=(p)\mathfrak{m}
be an ideal with
(\mathfrak{m},(p))=1
,
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime ideal not dividing
\mathfrak{m}
and
C_{i}=\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})\infty\cup\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})0
for each
i=1,...,h
, then:

i)
\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})
stabilizes
C_{i}
.

ii)
(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\cdot c_{i}\in C_{j_{i}}
for all
c_{i}\in C_{i}
, where
\mathfrak{q}I_{i}=(\alpha_{i})I_{j_{i}}
.

Proof.

For i) just note that
\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})
(and hence its subgroup
\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})
) stabilizes
C_{i}
.

To prove ii), since
(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{m})=1
and
(I_{i},\mathfrak{m})=1
, there exists
y_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathfrak{q}
and
y_{i}\in I_{i}
, such that
y_{\mathfrak{q}},y_{i}\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
. By the identity
\mathfrak{q}I_{i}=(\alpha_{i})I_{j_{i}}
, there exist an element
t_{j_{i}}\in I_{j_{i}}
such that

(2.6)

{}\alpha_{i}=\frac{y_{\mathfrak{q}}y_{i}}{t_{j_{i}}}.

Let
c_{i}=x/y
, then
x\in I_{i}
and either
y\in\mathfrak{m}
or
y\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
, then we have

(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\cdot\frac{x}{y}=\frac{x}{\alpha_{i}y}=\frac{t_{j_{i}}x}{y_{\mathfrak{q}}y_{i}y}

with
t_{j_{i}}x\in I_{j_{i}}
and either
y_{\mathfrak{q}}y_{i}y\in\mathfrak{m}
or
y_{\mathfrak{q}}y_{i}y\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
then
(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\cdot c_{i}\in C_{j_{i}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
be a
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular form, with
\mathfrak{n}=(p)\mathfrak{m}
and
(\mathfrak{m},(p))=1
; then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}
acts on
C
-cuspidal forms.

Proof.

By (
2.5
) we have to show that for all prime
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{m}
with
\mathfrak{q}I_{i}=(\alpha_{i})I_{j_{i}}
the function
f^{j_{i}}|{\left[\Gamma_{0}^{j_{i}}(\mathfrak{n})(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})\right]}
is
C_{i}
-cuspidal. For this, take
s_{i}\in C_{i}
,
\sigma_{s_{i}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
such that
\sigma_{s_{i}}\cdot\infty=s_{i}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}^{j_{i}}(\mathfrak{n})(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})
. We have to show that the constant term of
(f^{j_{i}}|_{\gamma\sigma_{s_{i}}})_{n}
vanishes for
1\leqslant n\leqslant k+\ell+1
.

Write
\gamma=\gamma_{j_{i}}(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\gamma_{i}
with
\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma_{0}^{j}(\mathfrak{n})
, then using Lemma
2.12
we have
\gamma_{i}\cdot s_{i}=s_{i}^{\prime}\in C_{i}
by part i);
(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\cdot s_{i}^{\prime}=s_{j_{i}}\in C_{j_{i}}
by part ii); and
\gamma_{j_{i}}\cdot s_{j_{i}}=s_{j_{i}}^{\prime}\in C_{j_{i}}
by part i). Then
\gamma\sigma_{s_{i}}\cdot\infty=\gamma_{j_{i}}(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\gamma_{i}\sigma_{s_{i}}\cdot\infty=\gamma_{j_{i}}(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\gamma_{i}\cdot s_{i}=\gamma_{j_{i}}(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\alpha_{i}\end{smallmatrix})\cdot s_{i}^{\prime}=\gamma_{j_{i}}\cdot s_{j_{i}}=s_{j_{i}}^{\prime}\in C_{j_{i}}
.

The result now follows since
f^{j_{i}}
is
C_{j_{i}}
-cuspidal and
\gamma\sigma_{s_{i}}\cdot\infty\in C_{j_{i}}
.
∎

2.4.
L
-function of
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms

Henceforth, we will consider
\psi
to be a Hecke character over
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
with
(\mathfrak{f},I_{i})=1
for each
i
. For each ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{a}}\mathfrak{q}^{n_{\mathfrak{q}}}
coprime to
\mathfrak{f}
, we define
\psi(\mathfrak{a})=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{a}}\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})^{n_{\mathfrak{q}}}
and
\psi(\mathfrak{a})=0
otherwise. In an abuse of notation, we write
\psi
for both the idelic Hecke character and the function it determines on ideals.

Definition 2.14
.

The
L
-function of a Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}
twisted by
\psi
is defined by

L(\mathcal{F},\psi,s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{f})=1\end{subarray}}c(\mathfrak{a},\mathcal{F})\psi(\mathfrak{a})N(\mathfrak{a})^{-s}\;\;\;(s\in\mathbb{C}).

Let
f_{1},...,f_{h}
be the
h
Bianchi modular forms corresponding to
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
I_{1},..,I_{h}
, then
L
-function of
\mathcal{F}
can be written as

(2.7)

{}L(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)=L^{1}(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)+\cdot\cdot\cdot+L^{h}(\mathcal{F},\psi,s),

where

L^{i}(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)=L(f^{i},\psi,s):=w_{K}^{-1}\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}c(\alpha\delta I_{i},\mathcal{F})\psi(\alpha\delta I_{i})N(\alpha\delta I_{i})^{-s}

with
w_{K}=|\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|
.

Remark 2.15
.

In
[
Wei71
, Chap. II]
it is proved that the
L
-functions of
\mathcal{F}
and each
f^{i}
converge absolutely on some right-half plane.

Note that for a complex number
s
, we have
L(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)=L(\mathcal{F},\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{s-1},1)
which allows us following
[
Wil17
, §2.6]
, consider the
L
-function of a
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular form as a function on Hecke characters
\psi
by setting

L(\mathcal{F},\psi):=L(\mathcal{F},\psi,1).

We now complete the
L
-function by adding Deligne’s
\Gamma
-factors at infinity. If
\psi
has infinity type
(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
we define the
renormalized

L
-function of
\mathcal{F}
by:

(2.8)

{}\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi):=\frac{\Gamma(u+1)\Gamma(v+1)}{(2\pi i)^{u+1}(2\pi i)^{v+1}}L(\mathcal{F},\psi).

The
L
-function of
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms can be written as a finite sum of Mellin transforms similarly as in
[
Wil17
, §2.6]
. Before stating the integral expression of the
L
-function, we recall some facts about Gauss sums and define suitable coefficients to link
\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)
with partial modular symbols in Section
3.2
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character of
K
with conductor
\mathfrak{f}
, then we define the Gauss sum of
\psi
to be

\tau(\psi)=\psi_{\infty}(\delta)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
((a)\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(a)e^{2\pi i\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a/\delta)}.

Note that our Gauss sum
\tau
is related with the Gauss sum
\tilde{\tau}
defined in
[
Wil17
, §2.6]
. More precisely, by denoting
x_{\mathfrak{f}}
the idele associated to the ideal
\mathfrak{f}
as in
[
Wil17
, §2.6]
we have that
\psi(a\mathfrak{f})^{-1}\psi_{\infty}(a)^{-1}=\psi(x_{\mathfrak{f}})^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(ax_{\mathfrak{f}})
for
a\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
(a\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1
, then we deduce that

\tau(\psi)=\psi(x_{\mathfrak{f}})\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(x_{\mathfrak{f}})^{-1}\tilde{\tau}(\psi^{-1}).

Remark 2.16
.

i) In the same way as in
[
Wil17
, Prop 1.7]
, for all
c\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we have

\frac{\psi_{\infty}(\delta)}{\tau(\psi)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
((a)\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(a)e^{2\pi i\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ac/\delta)}=\begin{cases}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(c)^{-1}&:((c)\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1,\\
0&:\rm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

ii) Suppose
\mathfrak{f}
coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
and let
i\in\{1,...,h\}
, then we can choose a representative
a
for each
[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}
, such that
a\in C_{i}
: let
I_{k_{i}}
be the unique ideal such that
\mathfrak{f}I_{k_{i}}=(\alpha_{k_{i}})I_{i}
for
\alpha_{k_{i}}\in K
, then we put
a=d_{b}/\alpha_{k_{i}}
where
d_{b}\in I_{k_{i}}
and
d_{b}\equiv b
(mod
\mathfrak{f}
). Note that
\alpha_{k_{i}}^{-1}\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}I_{k_{i}}^{-1}I_{i}\subset\mathfrak{f}^{-1}I_{k_{i}}^{-1}
, so in particular, as
b
ranges over all classes of
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
and as
d_{b}\in I_{k_{i}}
, we see that
d_{b}/\alpha_{i}
ranges over a full set of coset representatives
[a]
for
\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}
with

(a)\mathfrak{f}
coprime to
\mathfrak{f}
. Similarly to (
2.6
), we have that

\alpha_{k_{i}}=y_{\mathfrak{f}}y_{k_{i}}/t_{i}
with

y_{\mathfrak{f}}\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
,
y_{k_{i}}\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
and
t_{i}\in I_{i}
. Since
y_{\mathfrak{f}}y_{k_{i}}\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
, then we obtain

a=d_{b}/\alpha_{k_{i}}=d_{b}t_{i}/y_{\mathfrak{f}}y_{k_{i}}\in C_{i}
.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
C
-cuspidal form of weight
(k,\ell)
, for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant h
,
0\leqslant q\leqslant k,0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
and
a\in C_{i}
we define

c_{q,r}^{i}(a)=2\binom{k+\ell+2}{\ell+q-r+1}^{-1}(-1)^{\ell-r+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}f_{\ell+q-r+1}^{i}(a,t)dt.

Remark 2.18
.

The integral in the above definition converges since
1\leqslant\ell+q-r+1\leqslant k+\ell+1
and
f^{i}
is
C_{i}
-cuspidal. Also, note that
c_{q,r}^{i}
is not symmetric in
k
and
\ell
, this comes from part i) of Remark
2.5
.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
be a
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular form with
\mathfrak{n}=(p)\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{m}
coprime to
(p)
, then for a Hecke character
\psi
of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
and infinity type
(q,r)
satisfying
0\leqslant q\leqslant k,0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
, we have

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{(-1)^{\ell+q+r}2}{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{h}\psi(t_{j})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
((a)\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1,\;a\in C_{j}\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(a)c_{q,r}^{j}(a)\right].

Proof.

Let
f^{1},...,f^{h}
be the Bianchi modular forms corresponding to
\mathcal{F}
. Expanding the
L
-function of
f^{j}
we have

(2.9)

L(f^{j},\psi,1)=\frac{\psi(t_{j})|t_{j}|_{f}}{w_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in K^{\times}\\
(\alpha\delta I_{j},\mathfrak{f})=1\end{subarray}}c(\alpha\delta I_{j},\mathcal{F})\psi_{\infty}(\alpha\delta)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\alpha\delta)^{-1}|\alpha\delta|^{-2}.

We now proceed to rewrite
\psi_{\infty}(\delta)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\alpha\delta)^{-1}|\delta|^{-2}
in terms of a Gauss sum and
\psi_{\infty}(\alpha)^{-1}|\alpha|^{-2}
as an integral.

By parts i) and ii) of Remark
2.16
we have

(2.10)

\psi_{\infty}(\delta)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\alpha\delta)^{-1}|\delta|^{-2}=\frac{1}{D_{K}\tau(\psi)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
((a)\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1,\;a\in C_{j}\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(a)e^{2\pi i\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a\alpha)}.

Likewise, we obtain

(2.11)

\displaystyle\psi_{\infty}(\alpha)^{-1}|\alpha|^{-2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\right)^{-q+r}|\alpha|^{-q-r-2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\right)^{-q+r}\frac{4(2\pi)^{q+r+2}}{\Gamma(q+1)\Gamma(r+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r+1}K_{q-r}(4\pi|\alpha|t)dt,

where the last integral comes by setting
\lambda=4\pi|\alpha|
,
a=q+r+2
and
b=q-r
in the standard integral (see
[
Hid94
, §7]
)

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{a-1}K_{b}(\lambda t)dt=\lambda^{-a}2^{a-2}\Gamma\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right).

Substituting (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) in (
2.9
) and rearranging, we obtain the Fourier expansion of
f_{\ell+q-r+1}^{j}
, more precisely we have

(2.12)

L(f^{j},\psi,1)=A(j,\psi)\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
((a)\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1,\;a\in C_{j}\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(a)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}f_{\ell+q-r+1}^{j}(a,t)dt\right],

where

A(j,\psi)=\frac{\psi(t_{j})(-1)^{q+1}4(2\pi i)^{q+r+2}\binom{k+\ell+2}{\ell+q-r+1}^{-1}}{D_{K}w_{K}\Gamma(q+1)\Gamma(r+1)\tau(\psi)},

and the integral converges by the
C
-cuspidality of
\mathcal{F}
, since the conditions on the infinity type
(q,r)
of
\psi
implies
1\leqslant\ell+q-r+1\leqslant k+\ell+1
.

The result follows by recalling that
\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{\Gamma(q+1)\Gamma(r+1)}{(2\pi i)^{q+r+2}}\sum_{j=1}^{h}L(f^{j},\psi,1)
.
∎

We finish this section with some remarks about algebraicity of

For cuspidal Bianchi modular forms, the “critical” values of this

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
\mathcal{F}

(k,k)

\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}

satisfying

\frac{\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)}{\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\in E(\psi),

where
E(\psi)\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is the extension of
E
generated by the values of
\psi_{f}
.

Remark 2.21
.

In the non-cuspidal case, depending on the Bianchi modular forms we are interested, we can prove algebraicity of critical
L
-values (see Proposition
5.7
for an example). For the construction of the
p
-adic

-function, we are interested in view

and

.

3.1.
Partial modular symbols

Let
\Gamma

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(K)
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-empty

4.1.
Locally analytic distributions

Suppose

and define
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
to be the residue class degree of
\mathfrak{p}
. Note that
\sum f_{\mathfrak{p}}e_{\mathfrak{p}}=2
. Using the embedding
\iota_{p}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
from Section
2.1
, for each prime
\mathfrak{p}|p

\sigma:K\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\times\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
given by
\sigma(a)=(\sigma_{1}(a),\sigma_{2}(a))

For
r,s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, define the rigid analytic
(r,s)
-neighborhood of
\mathcal{O}_{K,p}
in
\mathbb{C}_{p}^{2}
to be

B(\mathcal{O}_{K,p},r,s):=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{2}:\exists u\in\mathcal{O}_{K,p}\;\text{such that}\;|x-\sigma_{1}(u)|\leqslant r,|y-\sigma_{2}(u)|\leqslant s\},

where
|\cdot|
is the norm of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
which is normalized as
|p|=p^{-1}
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
containing the image of
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
. We equip
L
with a valuation
v_{p}
, normalized so that
v_{p}(p)=1
. For
(r,s)
as above, we write
\mathbb{A}[L,r,s]
for the

-Banach space of rigid analytic functions on

and
\mathbb{D}[L,r,s]
for its Banach dual (see
[
Wil17
, §5.1]
).

Define the space of
L
-valued locally analytic distributions to be the projective limit

\mathcal{D}(L)=\lim_{\longleftarrow}\mathbb{D}[L,r,s]=\bigcap_{r,s}\mathbb{D}[L,r,s].

We endow

with a weight
(k,\ell)
-action of the semigroup

\Sigma_{0}(p):=\left\{(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K,p}):p|c,a\in\mathcal{O}_{K,p}^{\times},ad-bc\nequal 0\right\}

by setting

\gamma\cdot_{k,\ell}\zeta(x,y)=(a_{1}+c_{1}x)^{k}(a_{2}+c_{2}y)^{\ell}\zeta\left(\frac{b_{1}+d_{1}x}{a_{1}+c_{1}x},\frac{b_{2}+d_{2}y}{a_{2}+c_{2}y}\right);\,\sigma_{i}(\gamma)=(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{smallmatrix}),

which is well-defined.

These actions are compatible for various

. By duality, they induce actions on

. We write
\mathbb{D}_{k,\ell}[L,r,s]
(resp.

) for the spaces

(resp.

) together with the weight

.

.

(Williams’ Bianchi control theorem). For each prime
\mathfrak{p}
above

, let

-eigenspaces of the

This result is proved like its counterpart for Bianchi modular symbols of parallel weight

in

4.3.
Admissible distributions

For each pair

, the space
\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell}[L,r,s]
from Section
4.1
admits an operator norm
||\cdot||_{r,s}
via

||\mu||_{r,s}=\sup_{0\neq f\in\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell}[L,r,s]}\frac{|\mu(f)|_{p}}{|f|_{r,s}},

where
|\cdot|_{p}
is the usual
p
-adic absolute value on
L
and
|\cdot|_{r,s}
is the sup norm on
\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell}[L,r,s]
. Note that if
r\leqslant r^{\prime}
,
s\leqslant s^{\prime}
, then
||\mu||_{r,s}\geqslant||\mu||_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
for
\mu\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell}[L,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}]
.

These norms give rise to a family of norms on the space of locally analytic functions that allow us to classify locally analytic distributions by growth properties as we vary in this family.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell}(L)
be a locally analytic distribution.

i) Suppose
p
is inert or ramified in
K
, we say
\mu
is
h
-admissible if
||\mu||_{r,r}=O(r^{-h})
as
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

ii) Suppose
p
splits in
K
, we say
\mu
is
(h_{1},h_{2})
-admissible if
||\mu||_{r,s}=O(r^{-h_{1}})
uniformly in
s
as
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
, and
||\mu||_{r,s}=O(r^{-h_{2}})
uniformly in
r
as
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

We say that
\mu
is a measure, if it is bounded, i.e., is
0
-admissible or
(0,0)
-admissible, depending on the splitting behavior of
p
in
K
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),C_{i}}(\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell}(L))
, and
r,s\leqslant 1
. Defining

||\Psi||_{r,s}:=\sup_{D\in\Delta_{C_{i}}^{0}}||\Psi(D)||_{r,s}

gives a well-defined norm on
\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),C_{i}}(\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell}(L))
.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is almost identical to the proof of
[
Wil17
, Def-Prop 5.12]
. In fact, first we write
D\in\Delta_{C_{i}}^{0}
as
D=\alpha_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}D_{n}
for a finite set of generators
D_{j}
and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})]
using Lemma
4.2
. Then for every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})
and
f\in\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell}[L,r,s]
we can prove (in the same way of
[
Wil17
, Lem 5.11]
) that exist positive constants
A
and
A^{\prime}
such that

A|\gamma\cdot_{k,\ell}f|_{r,s}\leqslant|f|_{r,s}\leqslant A^{\prime}|\gamma\cdot_{k,\ell}f|_{r,s}.

Finally, by the above there exists a constant
B
such that (without loss of generality)

||\Psi(D)||_{r,s}\leqslant B||\Psi(D_{1})||_{r,s}.

In particular, the supremum is finite and hence gives a well-defined norm as required.
∎

The following proposition is the analog of
[
Wil17
, Props 5.12, 6.15]
for overconvergent partial Bianchi modular symbols.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n}),C_{i}}(\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell}(L))
.

i) Suppose
p
is inert in
K
and
\Psi
is a
U_{p}
-eigensymbol with eigenvalue
\lambda
and slope
h=v_{p}(\lambda)
. Then, for every
D\in\Delta_{C_{i}}^{0}
, the distribution
\Psi(D)
is
h
-admissible.

ii) Suppose
p
splits in
K
as
\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\Psi
is simultaneously a
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
- and
U_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{n}
-eigensymbol for some
n
, with non-zero eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}^{n}
and
\lambda_{2}^{n}
with slopes
h_{1}=v_{p}(\lambda_{1})
and
h_{2}=v_{p}(\lambda_{2})
. Then, for every
D\in\Delta_{C_{i}}^{0}
, the distribution
\Psi(D)
is
(h_{1},h_{2})
-admissible.

Proof.

To prove part i) we follow line by line the proof of
[
Wil17
, Prop 5.13]
and conclude that for any
r
and positive integer
m
, we have

||\Psi(D)||_{r/p^{m},r/p^{m}}\leqslant|\lambda|_{p}^{-m}||\Psi||_{r,r}.

To prove part ii) we use the same idea of part i) and conclude that for any
r,s
using
m
-th powers of the operators
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
and
U_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{n}
we have

||\Psi(D)||_{r/p^{mn},s}\leqslant|\lambda_{1}|_{p}^{-mn}||\Psi||_{r,s}\;\;\text{and}\;\;||\Psi(D)||_{r,s/p^{mn}}\leqslant|\lambda_{2}|_{p}^{-mn}||\Psi||_{r,s},

and combining both, we obtain the result.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

Define the ray class group of level

-adic characters on

Note that

under the canonical surjection

, also note that, the choice of

in Section

identifies

Let
\Psi=\{\Psi_{x}\}_{x\in\mathrm{Cl}_{K}}\in\mathrm{Symb}_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),C}(\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell}(L))
be an overconvergent partial Bianchi modular symbol, we define for
i,x\in\mathrm{Cl}_{K}
a distribution
\mu_{i}(\Psi_{x})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}^{i}(p^{\infty}),L)
as follows.

Since
\{0\}-\{\infty\}\in\Delta_{C_{i}}^{0}
for all

, we have a distribution

on

on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}^{i}(p^{\infty})
under the identification above, for more details see

.

Definition 4.10
.

The

of

Remark 4.11
.

The distribution

is independent of the choice of class group representatives.

The theory of partial Bianchi modular symbols developed in Sections

and
4
, allows us to construct the

-adic

-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a small slope

-cuspidal Bianchi eigenform of weight
(k,\ell)
and level

. Let
\iota
be an isomorphism
\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
satisfying
\iota\circ\iota_{\infty}=\iota_{p}
. Then there exists a locally analytic distribution

on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
such that for any Hecke character of

of conductor
\mathfrak{f}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\mathfrak{p}^{r_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and infinity type

satisfying

(4.1)

{}L_{p}^{\iota}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}(\psi)\right)\left[\frac{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}{(-1)^{\ell+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi),

where
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}
is the

-adic avatar of
\psi
as in

, §7.3]
,

is
(h_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}|p}
-admissible, where

We call

-adic

-function of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

The small slope
C
-cuspidal Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}
corresponds to a collection of
C_{i}
-cuspidal forms

on
\mathcal{H}_{3}

We first attach to

By applying

Finally, we define the

The interpolation property in (

in Remark

∎

Remark 4.13

i) Note that in the above theorem we define the

5.

Throughout this section, suppose

as

with

being the prime corresponding to the embedding

from Section

In this section, we prove algebraicity of the critical

-values of a non-cuspidal base change Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}
, we attach a complex partial Bianchi modular symbol to

and view it

-adically without using an isomorphism between

and

5.1.
Base change and
C
-cuspidality

Let
\varphi
be a Hecke character of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{M}
coprime to
p
and infinity type
(-k-1,0)
with
k\geqslant 0
. Let
\theta_{\varphi}
be the theta series associated to
\varphi
, which is a newform of weight
k+2
, level
\Gamma_{0}(D_{K}N(\mathfrak{M}))
and nebentypus
\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi}}=\chi_{K}\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}
where
\chi_{K}
is the quadratic character of
K/\mathbb{Q}
and
\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the Dirichlet character modulo
M=N(\mathfrak{M})
given by
\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}(a)=\varphi(a\mathcal{O}_{K})a^{-k-1}
, for an integer
a
coprime to
M
.

Let
\pi
be the automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by
\theta_{\varphi}
and let BC(
\pi
) be the base change of
\pi
to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
(see
[
Lan80
]
). The
base change
of
\theta_{\varphi}
to
K
is the normalized new vector
\theta_{\varphi/K}
in BC(
\pi
) which is a non-cuspidal Bianchi modular form of weight
(k,k)
, level
K_{0}(\mathfrak{m})
with
M\mathcal{O}_{K}|\mathfrak{m}|D_{K}M\mathcal{O}_{K}
(see
[
Fri83
, §2.3]
) and nebentypus
\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}=\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi}}\circ N
.

Remark 5.1
.

The Hecke eigenvalues of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
can be described in terms of the eigenvalues
a_{q}
of
\theta_{\varphi}
, which are described in turn in terms of
\varphi
: for every prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
above
q
,

a_{\mathfrak{q}}=\begin{cases}a_{q}=\varphi(\mathfrak{q})+\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{q}}),&\text{if $q=\mathfrak{q}\overline{\mathfrak{q}}$}\\
a_{q}=\varphi(\mathfrak{q}),&\text{if $q$ ramifies}\\
a_{q}^{2}-2\chi_{K}(q)\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}(q)q^{k+1}=2\varphi(\mathfrak{q})&\text{if $q$ is inert.}\end{cases}

Note that the Bianchi modular form
\theta_{\varphi/K}
has level
\mathfrak{m}
coprime to
p
, however, to construct its
p
-adic
L
-function we need
p
in the level, then, we consider its
p
-stabilizations. We are interested in the
p
-stabilization satisfying the small slope condition of Definition
4.5
, i.e., the
p
-adic valuation of the eigenvalues of
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
U_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
are both less than
k+1
.

The
p
-stabilizations of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
can be explicitly described from the
p
-stabilizations of
\theta_{\varphi}
by considering its Hecke polynomial at
p
given by
x^{2}-a_{p}x+\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi}}(p)p^{k+1}
. Since
p=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
, we have that

a_{p}=\varphi(\mathfrak{p})+\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})
and
\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi}}(p)=\chi_{K}(p)\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}(p)=\varphi(p\mathcal{O}_{K})/p^{k+1}=\varphi(\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}})/p^{k+1}
,
thus the roots of the Hecke polynomial of
\theta_{\varphi}
at
p
are
\alpha=\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})
and
\beta=\varphi(\mathfrak{p})
.

Consider the Hecke polynomial of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
at
\mathfrak{q}|p
given by
x^{2}-a_{\mathfrak{q}}x+\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}(\mathfrak{q})N(\mathfrak{q})^{k+1}
. Since

a_{\mathfrak{q}}=a_{p}
by Remark
5.1
,
N(\mathfrak{q})=p
and
\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}(\mathfrak{q})=\chi_{K}(N(\mathfrak{q}))\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}(N(\mathfrak{q}))=\chi_{K}(p)\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}(p)=\epsilon_{\theta_{\varphi}}(p)
; then the Hecke polynomial of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
at each
\mathfrak{q}|p
and the Hecke polynomial of
\theta_{\varphi}
at
p
are equal.

Therefore, we can take the roots of the Hecke polynomial of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
at
\mathfrak{p}
and at
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
, to be
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}=\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}=\alpha=\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})
and
\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}=\beta_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}=\beta=\varphi(\mathfrak{p})
, respectively.

If
\theta_{\varphi}^{\alpha}
(resp.
\theta_{\varphi}^{\beta}
) is the
p
-stabilization of
\theta_{\varphi}
corresponding to
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
), we define its base change to
K
to be the
p
-stabilization
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{\alpha\alpha}
(resp.
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{\beta\beta}
) of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
corresponding to
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
(resp.
\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
\beta_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
).

Remark 5.2
.

Note that
v_{p}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})=v_{p}(\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})=v_{p}(\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}}))=0
and
v_{p}(\beta_{\mathfrak{p}})=v_{p}(\beta_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})=v_{p}(\varphi(\mathfrak{p}))=k+1
. Since we are interested in the small slope
p
-stabilization of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
, henceforth we will work with
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}:=\theta_{\varphi/K}^{\alpha\alpha}
.

Recall the level of
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
given by
K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
with
\mathfrak{n}=(p)\mathfrak{m}
and consider
C=(C_{1},...,C_{h})
with
C_{i}=\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})\infty\cup\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{m})0
as in Section
2.3
.

The following proposition is the analog of
[
BD15
, Def 6.2]
, however, in our case we do not need to produce a linear combination, since we can take advantage of the nice vanishing properties of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The Bianchi modular form
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
is
C
-cuspidal.

Proof.

Suppose that
K
has class number
1
. By part ii) of Remark
2.5
, the constant term of the Fourier expansion at
\infty
of
\theta_{\varphi/K}=(f_{0},...,f_{n},...,f_{2k+2})
is trivial for
n\notin\{0,k+1,2k+2\}
and by
[
Fri83
, Thm 3.1]
, we also have that the constant Fourier coefficient of

is trivial and therefore
\theta_{\varphi/K}
quasi-vanishes at
\infty
. Likewise, since
\theta_{\varphi/K}
is an eigenform for the Hecke operators, then it is also an eigenform for the Fricke involution
W_{\mathfrak{m}}
, with eigenvalue
\pm 1
(see
[
CW94
, §2]
,
[
Pal23
, §2.4]
). Note that
W_{\mathfrak{m}}
sends the cusp
\infty
to the cusp

, then
\theta_{\varphi/K}
quasi-vanishes at
0
, therefore
\theta_{\varphi/K}
quasi-vanishes at
C
. To conclude that
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
is
C
-cuspidal, we notice that
C
is stable under multiplication by the matrix
(\begin{smallmatrix}\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})
(resp.
(\begin{smallmatrix}\overline{\varpi}_{\mathfrak{p}}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})
) used in the
\mathfrak{p}
-stabilization (resp.
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
-stabilization) of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
.

When
K
has a higher class number, we proceed similarly as above and obtain that
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{i}
quasi-vanishes at
C_{i}
for each
i
. Then we note that the set
of cusps
C_{i}

is stable under multiplication by the matrix
{(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{i}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})}
with
\mathfrak{p}I_{i}=(\alpha_{i})I_{j_{i}}

(resp.
{(\begin{smallmatrix}\beta_{i}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})}
with
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}I_{i}=(\beta_{i})I_{j_{i}}
) appearing in the

-component of the
\mathfrak{p}
-stabilization (resp.
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
-stabilization)
of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
.
∎

5.2.
L
-function

The fact that
\theta_{\varphi/K}
is constructed from a Hecke character, allows us to relate its complex
L
-function with Hecke
L
-functions and prove algebraicity of critical
L
-values of
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character of
K
and let
\psi^{c}(\mathfrak{q}):=\psi(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})
where
\mathfrak{q}
is an ideal of
K
and
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}
is its conjugate ideal. Then

L(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi,s)=L(\varphi^{c}\psi,s)L(\varphi^{c}\psi^{c}\lambda_{K},s)=L(\varphi^{c}\psi\lambda_{K},s)L(\varphi^{c}\psi^{c},s)

where
\lambda_{K}=\chi_{K}\circ N
.

Proof.

This follows by comparing the Euler factors on both sides, using Remark
5.1
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

There are 6 more ways to factor
L(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi,s)
as product of two Hecke
L
-functions, this comes from the fact that for a Hecke character
\nu
we have
L(\nu,s)=L(\nu^{c},s)
. Similar factorizations in the
p
-adic setting do not necessarily hold, because if
\nu
has infinity type
(q,r)
the involution
\nu\rightarrow\nu^{c}

corresponds to the map
(q,r)\rightarrow(r,q)
on weight space and therefore does not preserve the lower right quadrant of weights of Hecke characters that lie in the range
of classical interpolation of the Katz
p
-adic
L
-functions (see
[
BDP12
, Figure 1]
and Theorem
5.12
).

We now prove algebraicity of the critical
L
-values of
\theta_{\varphi/K}
. For this, we first follow
[
Ber08
, §2.3]
to recall algebraicity of the critical
L
-value
L(\chi,0)
, of a Hecke character
\chi
of infinity type
(a,b)
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
a>0\geqslant b
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\omega
its Néron differential. Suppose also that
E
has good reduction at the place above
p
and
\overline{\omega}
is a non-vanishing invariant differential on the reduced curve
\overline{E}
, then Damerell showed that
\pi^{-b}\Omega_{\infty}^{-a+b}L(\chi,0)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
\Omega_{\infty}\in\mathbb{C}
is the complex period of
\omega
.

Definition 5.6
.

Define the complex period
\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
attached to
\theta_{\varphi/K}
to be

\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}=\left(\frac{\Omega_{\infty}}{\pi}\right)^{2k+2}

Proposition 5.7
.

For all Hecke character
\psi
of
K
with infinity type
(q,r)
with
q,r\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
0\leqslant q,r\leqslant k
, we have
\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi)/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
we have

L(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi,1)=L(\varphi^{c}\psi,1)L(\varphi^{c}\psi^{c}\lambda_{K},1)=L(\varphi^{c}\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}},0)L(\varphi^{c}\psi^{c}\lambda_{K}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}},0).

Note that
\varphi^{c}\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}
has infinity type
(q+1,r-k)
with
q+1>0\geqslant r-k
and
\varphi^{c}\psi^{c}\lambda_{K}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}
has infinity type
(r+1,q-k)
with
r+1>0\geqslant q-k
, therefore

\frac{L(\varphi^{c}\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}},0)}{\pi^{r-k}\Omega_{\infty}^{q+1-r+k}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\;\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\;\;\frac{L(\varphi^{c}\psi^{c}\lambda_{K}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}},0)}{\pi^{q-k}\Omega_{\infty}^{r+1-q+k}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}.

The result follows by multiplying the two numbers above and noting that

\frac{L(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi,1)}{\pi^{q+r-2k}\Omega_{\infty}^{2k+2}}=\frac{(2i)^{q+r+2}}{q!r!}\frac{\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi)}{\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}}.

∎

Suppose
\theta_{\varphi/K}
does not have level at
p
and consider its
p
-stabilization
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
as in Remark
5.2
.

Corollary 5.8
.

For all Hecke character
\psi
of
K
with infinity type
(q,r)
satisfying
0\leqslant q,r\leqslant k
, we have
\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

The
L
-functions of
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
and
\theta_{\varphi/K}
are related (see
[
Pal23
, §3.3]
for Bianchi modular forms with trivial nebentypus and

with class number 1) by

\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|p}\left(1-\frac{\varphi(\mathfrak{p})\psi(\mathfrak{q})}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\right)\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi).

By Proposition

we obtain the result.
∎

5.3.
p
-adic
L
-function

Let
f_{1},...,f_{h}
be the collection of descents of
\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}
to
\mathcal{H}_{3}
. By Proposition

is

-cuspidal, then we can consider the coefficients

from Definition

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}
be the period in Proposition
5.7
, then the partial Bianchi modular symbol
\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}^{\mathrm{alg}}:=\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}
takes values in
V_{k,k}^{*}(E)
for some number field

Proof.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character of
K
of infinity type
(q,r)
satisfying
0\leqslant q,r\leqslant k
, then by Proposition
2.19
we have

\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)=\left[\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2}{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{h}\left[\psi(t_{i})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
((a)\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f})=1,\;a\in C_{i}\end{subarray}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(a)c_{q,r}^{i}(a)\right].

Dividing both sides by
\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}

(5.1)

{}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2}{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}\right]^{-1}\frac{\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)}{\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\left[\psi(t_{i})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a]\in\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K}\\

By Corollary
5.8

\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, then the left-hand side of (
5.1
) is an algebraic number and consequently by linear independence of characters we have that
c_{q,r}^{i}(a)/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
for each

.

Consider the polynomial
(X+aY)^{q}Y^{k-q}(\overline{X}+\overline{a}\overline{Y})^{r}\overline{Y}^{k-r}

\phi_{f^{i}}(\{a\}-\{\infty\})\left[(X+aY)^{q}Y^{k-q}(\overline{X}+\overline{a}\overline{Y})^{r}\overline{Y}^{k-r}\right]=c_{q,r}^{i}(a).

\phi_{f^{i}}^{\mathrm{alg}}:=\phi_{f^{i}}/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}

(5.2)

{}\phi_{f^{i}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\{a\}-\{\infty\})\left[(X+aY)^{q}Y^{k-q}(\overline{X}+\overline{a}\overline{Y})^{r}\overline{Y}^{k-r}\right]=c_{q,r}^{i}(a)/\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}.

Now, using (
5.2
), we will show

and a polynomial

, we want to show

.

By Lemma
4.2
,
\Delta_{C_{i}}^{0}
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_{0}^{i}(\mathfrak{n})]
-module by divisors
\{a\}-\{\infty\}
, with

, then, it is enough to prove
[\phi_{f^{i}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\{a\}-\{\infty\})](P)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

Let
P\left[\binom{X}{Y}\binom{\overline{X}}{\overline{Y}}\right]=\sum_{b,d=0}^{k}t_{b,d}X^{b}Y^{k-b}\overline{X}^{d}\overline{Y}^{k-d}

, we can write each

as a homogeneous polynomial

by
(X+aY)-aY
and
\overline{X}

) on each

Finally, using again that

is finitely generated as a

\phi_{\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p}}^{\mathrm{alg}}\in\mathrm{Symb}_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),C}(V_{k,k}^{*}(E))
.
∎

Theorem 5.10

Proposition 5.11
.

We have the following equality of measures on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})

L_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)=\frac{L_{p}^{\iota}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},-)}{\iota(\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}})}.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}|(p^{\infty})
and infinity type
(q,r)
satisfying
0\leqslant q,r\leqslant k
, we write

B(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)=\left[\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\psi(\mathfrak{p})}\right)\right]\left[\frac{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right],

for the factor appearing in Theorem
4.12
.

4.12
we have the interpolation property

\displaystyle L_{p}^{\iota}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})

\displaystyle=\iota\left(B(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=\iota\left(B(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)\frac{\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)}{\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}}\right)\iota(\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}).

Since

\frac{\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi)}{\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}}
as well by Corollary

{}L_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=E_{p}(\theta_{\varphi/K}^{p})\left[\frac{D_{K}w_{K}\tau(\psi)}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})^{t+s}\Omega_{\theta_{\varphi/K}}}\right]\Lambda(\theta_{\varphi/K},\psi),

Theorem 5.13
.

\varphi

Remark 5.14

Proof.

Families of Homomorphic Mappings in the Polydisk Thanks: The authors were partially supported by Fondecyt Grants 1150115, 1150284, 1190380, and 1190756. Key words: Schwarzian operator, polydisk, locally biholomorphic, norm, covering. MCS 2020 . Primary: 32H02, 32A17; Secondary: 30C45.
Martin Chuaqui and Rodrigo Hernández

Abstract
. We study classes of locally biholomorphic mappings defined in the polydisk ℙ n {\mathbb{P}}^{n} that have bounded Schwarzian operator in the Bergman metric. We establish important properties of specific solutions of the associated system of differential equations, and show a geometric connection between the order of the classes and a covering property. We show for modified and slightly larger classes that the order is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the bound on the Schwarzian, and use this to estimate the order of the original classes.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate families of locally biholomorphic mappings in the polydisk
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined by the Schwarzian operator. This is a classical theme in one complex variable, where the simply-connected case corresponds to mapping in the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
with bounded Schwarzian in the hyperbolic norm. The nature of the group of automorphisms in
{\mathbb{D}}
yields families that are
linearly invariant
, with results that are often invariant under the group. The original papers by Pommerenke have become a landmark in this
[
19
]
. In several variables, linear invariance is present for the families in the ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
, and allows for a fairly detailed analysis that includes a generalization of the Ahlfors-Weill extension and examples of locally linearly convex domains
[
4
]
,
[
1
,
10
]
. Our aim here is to study the case for the polydisk, a domain for which the explicit nature of the automorphisms and the Bergman metric partially compensates for the absence of linear invariance. Nevertheless, we were unable reproduce a
density function
associated to the mappings treated that was both invariant and convex relative to the Bergman metric, missing thus a crucial step for the aforementioned Ahlfors-Weill extension.

In Section 2 we present preliminary facts about the Schwarzian operator and specific bounds that are satisfied in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. In Section 3 we present the important notion of
normalization
that gives a geometric insight for the norm of the families. We also develop techniques based on comparison to study specific solutions of the linear system related to the Schwarzian operator. In particular, for sufficiently small values of the parameter in the family, we show that certain normalized solutions at the origin do vanish in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. This can be thought as having the Ahlfors-Weill extension at one point, and implies that for
f
in the family, the image
f({\mathbb{P}}^{n})
misses the complex hyperplane
1-(a_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}w_{n})=0
, where
(n+1)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=(\nabla J_{f})(0)
,
J_{f}
the complex jacobian of
f
. In one complex variable, this corresponds to the fact that
-2/f^{\prime\prime}(0)\notin f({\mathbb{D}})
when
(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq 2
.

In the final section we estimate the
order
of the families treated. In
[
3
]
, a generalization of Pommerenke’s variational methods was employed to estimate the order of the families in
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
, but linear invariance was required. Here instead, we appeal to a dilation argument that yields Lipschitz continuity for the order of slightly larger families. Some of our methods can be considered extensions of techniques that in one variable yield optimal results
[
5
,
6
,
16
]
, and although sharp constants in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
are in many cases still far out of reach, we have made an effort to be as precise as possible. The comparison techniques we develop are likely applicable on more general domains, and our results fit into a broader class of mappings in several variables studied by different authors, see for example
[
8
,
11
,
18
,
20
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
f:\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be a locally biholomorphic mapping
defined on some domain
\Omega
. There are several equivalent definitions of a Schwarzian derivative in several complex variables
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
, and we will follow the notion offered in
[
17
]
because of its explicit connection with differential equations.
The author defines a family of Schwarzian derivatives of
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
as

{S^{k}_{ij}}f=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{l=1}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{l}}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{k}}{\partial f_{l}}-\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\delta^{k}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+\delta^{k}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\right)\log(J_{f})\,,

where
i,j,k=1,2,\ldots,n,

and
\delta^{k}_{i}
are the Kronecker symbols. For
n>1
the Schwarzian derivatives
have the properties
{S^{k}_{ij}}f=0
for
all
i,j,k=1,2,\ldots,n
iff
f(z)=M(z),
for some Möbius transformation

M(z)=\left(\frac{l_{1}(z)}{l_{0}(z)},\ldots,\frac{l_{n}(z)}{l_{0}(z)}\right)\,,

where
l_{i}(z)=a_{i0}+a_{i1}z_{1}+\cdots+a_{in}z_{n}
with

\det(a_{ij})\neq 0
. Furthermore, for a composition one has

(2.1)

{S^{k}_{ij}}(g\circ f)(z)={S^{k}_{ij}}f(z)+\sum^{n}_{l,m,r=1}S^{r}_{lm}g(w)\frac{\partial w_{l}}{\partial z_{i}}\frac{\partial w_{m}}{\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{k}}{\partial w_{r}}\;,\;w=f(z)\,.

Thus, if
g
is a Möbius transformation then
{S^{k}_{ij}}(g\circ f)={S^{k}_{ij}}f
. The coefficients
S^{0}_{ij}f

are given by

S^{0}_{ij}f(z)=(J_{f})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}(J_{f})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}(J_{f})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}S^{k}_{ij}f(z)\right)\,.

In
[
17
]
we can also find a description of the functions with prescribed Schwarzian derivatives
{S^{k}_{ij}}f
.
Consider the following over-determined system of partial differential equations,

(2.2)

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}=\sum^{n}_{k=1}P^{k}_{ij}(z)\frac{\partial u}{\partial z_{k}}+P^{0}_{ij}(z)u\;,\quad i,j=1,2,\ldots,n\,,

where
z=(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})\in\Omega
and
P^{k}_{ij}(z)
are
holomorphic functions in
\Omega
, for
i,j,k=0,\ldots,n
. The system (
2.2
) is called

completely integrable
if there are
n+1
(maximun)
linearly independent solutions, and is said to be in
canonical
form
(see
[
21
]
) if the coefficients satisfy

\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{ij}^{j}(z)=0\;,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n\,.

It was shown that (
2.2
) is a completely integrable system in canonical form
if and only if
P^{k}_{ij}={S^{k}_{ij}}f
for a locally biholomorphic mapping
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
,
where
f_{i}=u_{i}/u_{0}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
is a set of linearly independent
solutions of the system. For a given mapping
f
,
u_{0}=(J_{f})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}
is always
a solution of (
2.2
) with
P_{ij}^{k}={S^{k}_{ij}}f
.

We recall the following definitions from
[
9
]
, where the individual Schwarzians
S^{k}f
are conformed to define the following operators.

Definition 2.1
.

For each
k=1,\ldots,n
we let
S^{k}_{f}
be the
n\times n
matrix

S^{k}_{f}=({S^{k}_{ij}}f)\,,\quad i,j=1,\ldots,n\,.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be locally holomorphic, and let
T_{z}\Omega
stand for the tangent space at
z\in\Omega
. We define the
Schwarzian derivative operator
as the mapping
S_{f}(z):T_{z}\Omega\to T_{z}\Omega
given by

S_{f}(z)(\vec{v})=\left(S^{1}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})\,,\ldots,S^{n}_{f}(z)\vec{v}\right)\,,

where

\vec{v}\in T_{z}\Omega
and
S^{k}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})=\vec{v}^{\,t}S^{k}_{f}(z)\vec{v}
.

With this, we can rewrite (
2.2
) with
P^{k}_{ij}={S^{k}_{ij}}f
as

(2.3)

\mbox{Hess}(u)(z)(\vec{v},\vec{v})=S_{f}(z)(\vec{v})\cdot\nabla u(z)+S^{0}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})u\,,

where
S^{0}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})=\vec{v}\,^{t}S^{0}_{f}(z)\vec{v}
.

The Bergman metric in the polydisk
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}):|z_{i}|<1,\,i=1,\ldots,n\}
is the hermitian product
defined by the diagonal matrix

g_{ii}(z)=\frac{2}{(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{2}}\;,

see, e.g.,
[
12
]
.
The norm of the Schwarzian operator is defined by

\|S_{f}(z)\|=\displaystyle\sup_{\|\vec{v}\|=1}\|S_{f}(z)(\vec{v}\,)\|\,,

where

\|\,\vec{v}\,\|=\left[\,2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|v_{i}|^{2}}{(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{2}}\,\right]^{1/2}

is the Bergman norm of
\vec{v}\in T_{z}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.
Finally, let

\|S_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}\|S_{f}(z)\|\,.

For a system of the form (
2.2
) that is completely integrable and in canonical form, we define
\|P\|
to be
\|S_{f}\|
of any mapping with Schwarzian
derivatives given by the coefficients
P^{k}_{ij}
. Let now

{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}=\{f:{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\,\mid\;f(0)=0\;,\;Df(0)=\textsl{I}\;,\;\|S_{f}\|\leq\alpha\}\,.

It is well known that up to multiplication by a diagonal unitary transformation and a
permutation of the coordinates, the automorphisms of the polydisk are given by

(2.4)

\psi(z)=\psi_{a}(z)=(\psi_{a_{1}}(z_{1}),\ldots,\psi_{a_{n}}(z_{n}))\,,\;\;z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\,,

where
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and

\psi_{a_{j}}(z_{j})=\displaystyle\frac{z_{j}-a_{j}}{1-\overline{a_{j}}z_{j}}\;,\,1\leq j\leq n\,.

Because the automorphisms of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
are not Möbius transformations unless the origin is fixed, the bound on
\|S_{f}\|
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
will generally not be preserved when composing with them. This poses a significant difference regarding
the analysis of the corresponding family in
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
, and it will be useful to estimate the norm of
\|S_{\psi}\|
for an automorphism of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.

The components
\psi_{j}=\psi_{a_{j}}
of the mapping
\psi=\psi_{a}
satisfy

\begin{array}[]{lll}S^{k}_{kk}\psi(z)&=&\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{n+1}\,\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\psi^{\prime}_{k}}(z_{k})\,,\\[14.22636pt]
S^{k}_{jj}\psi(z)&=&0\;,\;\;j\neq k\,,\\[8.5359pt]
S^{k}_{kj}\psi(z)&=&-\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{j}}{\psi^{\prime}_{j}}(z_{j})\;,\;\;j\neq k\,,\\
S^{k}_{ij}\psi(z)&=&0\;,\;i\neq j\,,\,k\neq j\,.\end{array}

It follows that for
\vec{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
we have

\vec{v}^{\,t}S^{k}_{\psi}(z)\vec{v}=\frac{n-1}{n+1}\cdot\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\psi^{\prime}_{k}}(z_{k})v_{k}^{2}-\frac{2}{n+1}\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{j}}{\psi^{\prime}_{j}}(z_{j})v_{k}v_{j}=\frac{v_{k}}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{kj}\,,

where
d_{kj}=-2\displaystyle\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{j}}{\psi^{\prime}_{j}}(z_{j})v_{j}
for
j\neq k
and
d_{kk}=(n-1)\displaystyle\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\psi^{\prime}_{k}}(z_{k})v_{k}
. Therefore

(2.5)

S_{\psi}(z)(\vec{v})=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(v_{1}D_{1},\ldots,v_{n}D_{n}\right)\,,

where

D_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{kj}=(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{\psi^{\prime}_{k}}(z_{k})v_{k}-2\sum_{j\neq k}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{j}}{\psi^{\prime}_{j}}(z_{j})v_{j}\,.

The square of the Bergman norm of
S_{\psi}(z)(\vec{v})
is given by

A=\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2|v_{k}|^{2}}{(1-|z_{k}|^{2})^{2}}|D_{k}|^{2}\,,

and if we assume now that
||\vec{v}||=1
, we see that the sum on the right hand-side is a convex combination of the
|D_{k}|^{2}
.
Hence

A\leq\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}\max_{i}|D_{i}|^{2}\,.

Since
|\psi_{i}^{\prime\prime}/\psi_{i}^{\prime}(z_{i})|\leq 2|a_{i}|/|1-\overline{a_{i}}z_{i}|
we see from (
2.4
) that

|D_{k}|\,\leq\,2(n-1)\frac{|a_{k}||v_{k}|}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}z_{k}|}+4\sum_{j\neq k}\frac{|a_{j}||v_{j}|}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z_{j}|}\,,

and using that
\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}z_{k}|}\leq\frac{2}{1-|z_{k}|^{2}}
together with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}|D_{k}|&\leq&4\left(2|a_{1}|,\dots,(n-1)|a_{k}|,\ldots,2|a_{n}|\right)\cdot\displaystyle\left(\frac{|v_{1}|}{1-|z_{1}|^{2}},\ldots,\frac{|v_{n}|}{1-|z_{n}|^{2}}\right)\\
&&\\
&\leq&2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{4|a_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+(n-1)^{2}|a_{k}|^{2}+\cdots+4|a_{n}|^{2}}\\
&&\\
&\leq&2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{(n+3)(n-1)}\max_{i}|a_{i}|\;\,\leq\;\,2\sqrt{2}(n+1)\max_{i}|a_{i}|\,.\end{array}

With
|z|_{\infty}=\max_{i}|z_{i}|
denoting the polydisk norm, we see that
A\leq 8|\psi(0)|_{\infty}^{2}
, hence

\|S_{\psi}\|\leq 2\sqrt{2}|\psi(0)|_{\infty}\,.

If
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
and
\psi
is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, then we see from (
2.1
) and the invariance of the Bergman metric under
\psi
that

(2.6)

\|S_{f\circ\psi}\|\leq\|S_{f}\|+\|S_{\psi}\|\leq\alpha+2\sqrt{2}|\psi(0)|_{\infty}\,.

The notation
|z|
will be kept to refer to the Euclidean norm.
The following result on estimates for the derivative of a holomorphic function in
{\mathbb{D}}
will be used in the paper, as well as two lemmas on
specific bounds for the Schwarzians of a mapping
f
with bounded norm
\|Sf\|
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g
be holomorphic in
{\mathbb{D}}
.

(i) If
\,\displaystyle|g(z)|\leq C
then
\,\displaystyle|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{C}{1-|z|^{2}}\,.

(ii) If
\,\displaystyle|g(z)|\leq\frac{C}{1-|z|^{2}}
then
\,\displaystyle|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{4C}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\,.

Proof.

For the proof, we may assume that
C=1
. The first part then follows at once from the Schwarz-Pick lemma.

For the second part, fix
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
, which after rotation may be assumed to real and non-negative. The Cauchy integral formula applied on the circle
C_{r}:\;|\zeta-a|=r<1-a
gives

|g^{\prime}(a)|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{C_{r}}\frac{|d\zeta|}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})|\zeta-a|^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{1-a^{2}-r^{2}-2ar\cos(\theta)}\,.

The last integral can be computed using the Weierstrass substitution
\theta=2\arctan(x)
, and gives

|g^{\prime}(a)|\leq\frac{1}{r\sqrt{U-V}}\,,

where
U=(1-a^{2}-r^{2})^{2},\,V=4a^{2}r^{2}
. Evaluating the expression
r\sqrt{U-V}
at the value
r=r(a)
where it attains its maximum is rather cumbersome, but the simple expression
r=(1-a)/\sqrt{2}
is close enough. At this value

U-V=\frac{(1-a)^{2}}{4}\left(a^{2}+6a+1\right)\,,

and thus

|g^{\prime}(a)|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{(1-a)^{2}\sqrt{a^{2}+6a+1}}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}(1+a)^{2}}{(1-a^{2})^{2}\sqrt{a^{2}+6a+1}}\,.

The quantity
(1+a)^{2}/\sqrt{a^{2}+6a+1}
decreases to its minimum value at
a=\sqrt{17}-4
, and is increasing thereafter. From this we can see that the maximum value is attained at
a=1
and is equal to
\sqrt{2}
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
and
|\vec{v}|_{\infty}=1
then

(2.7)

\left|S^{k}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})\right|\leq\frac{3n\alpha}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}\,,

(2.8)

\left|S^{0}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})\right|\leq\frac{5n^{2}\alpha+2n(n+1)\alpha^{2}}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}\,.

Proof.

For the first part, let
\vec{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
be a vector with
|\vec{v}|_{\infty}=1
. It was shown in
[
2
]
that when
\|S_{f}\|\leq\alpha
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, one has
{S^{k}_{ij}}f\equiv 0
for
k\neq i,j
and
(1-|z_{i}|^{2})|S^{k}_{ik}f(z)|\leq\sqrt{2}\alpha
. Hence

\hskip 28.45274pt\left|S^{k}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})\right|=|v_{k}|\displaystyle\left|\left(2\sum_{i=1}^{n}S^{k}_{ik}f(z)v_{i}-S^{k}_{kk}f(z)v_{k}\right)\right|

\hskip 86.72377pt\leq\displaystyle{\frac{2\sqrt{2}\alpha}{(1-|z_{1}|^{2})}+\cdots\frac{\sqrt{2}\alpha}{(1-|z_{k}|^{2})}+\cdots\frac{2\sqrt{2}\alpha}{(1-|z_{n}|^{2})}}

\hskip 86.72377pt=\displaystyle{\frac{2\sqrt{2}(n-1)\alpha+\sqrt{2}\alpha}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}\,=\,\frac{\sqrt{2}(2n-1)\alpha}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}\,\leq\,\frac{3n\alpha}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}}\,.

For the second part and the abbreviated notation
\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial z_{i}
, it was shown in
[
9
]
that

S^{0}_{ii}f(z)=-\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{k}S^{k}_{ii}f(z)+\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k,j=1}^{n}S^{k}_{ij}f(z)S^{j}_{ki}f(z)\,,

and for
i\neq j

S^{0}_{ij}f(z)=\partial_{j}S^{i}_{ii}f(z)-\partial_{i}S^{i}_{ij}f(z)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}S^{k}_{ii}f(z)S^{i}_{kj}f(z)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}S^{k}_{ij}f(z)S^{i}_{ki}f(z)\,.

Since the
{S^{k}_{ij}}f
vanish unless
k=i
or
k=j
, we find that

S^{0}_{ii}f(z)=-\frac{1}{n-1}\partial_{i}S^{i}_{ii}f(z)+\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(S^{k}_{ki}f(z)\right)^{2}\,.

and for
i\neq j

S^{0}_{ij}f(z)=\partial_{j}S^{i}_{ii}f(z)-\partial_{i}S^{i}_{ji}f(z)-S^{j}_{ij}f(z)S^{i}_{ij}f(z)\,.

From Lemma 2.3 we see that

(2.9)

|S^{0}_{ii}f(z)|\leq\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{4\sqrt{2}\alpha+2n\alpha^{2}}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}\,,

and for
i\neq j

(2.10)

|S^{0}_{ij}f(z)|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}\alpha+2\alpha^{2}}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}\,,

because the bounds for
S^{i}_{ii}
and
S^{i}_{ji}
do not depend on
z_{j}
and
z_{i}
, respectively.
Therefore

\hskip 19.91684pt\left|S^{0}_{f}(z)(\vec{v})\right|=\displaystyle\left|v_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S^{0}_{1k}f(z)v_{k}+\cdots+v_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S^{0}_{nk}f(z)v_{k}\right|

\hskip 76.82234pt\leq\displaystyle n\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{4\sqrt{2}\alpha+2n\alpha^{2}}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}+(n-1)\frac{2\sqrt{2}\alpha+2\alpha^{2}}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}\right)

\hskip 76.82234pt=\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n-1}\frac{\sqrt{2}\left[2+(n-1)^{2}\right]\alpha+\left[n+(n-1)^{2}\right]\alpha^{2}}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}\,\leq\,\displaystyle\frac{5n^{2}\alpha+2n(n+1)\alpha^{2}}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}\;\,.

For the last inequality we have used that
2\left[2+(n-1)^{2}\right]\leq 3n(n-1)
and
n+(n-1)^{2}\leq(n+1)(n-1)
because

n\geq 2
, and that
3\sqrt{2}\leq 5
.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let (
2.2
) be a completely integrable system in canonical form with
\|P\|\leq\alpha
.
For fixed
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}
let
A=(P^{k}_{j})_{k,j}
be the matrix with components

P^{k}_{j}=\zeta_{1}P^{k}_{1j}+\cdots+\zeta_{n}P^{k}_{nj}\,,

and let
B=(P^{0}_{j})
be the vector with components

P^{0}_{j}=\zeta_{1}P^{0}_{1j}+\cdots+\zeta_{n}P^{0}_{nj}\,.

Then with
|A|=\max_{|v|=1}|Av|
we have

(2.11)

(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})|A|\leq 1.6\,n\sqrt{n}\alpha\,,

(2.12)

(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}|B|\leq c_{1}(n)\alpha+c_{2}(n)\alpha^{2}\,,

where

(2.13)

c_{1}(n)=\frac{2\sqrt{2n}}{n-1}\left[2+(n-1)^{2}\right]\;\,,\;\,c_{2}(n)=\frac{2\sqrt{n}}{n-1}\left[n+(n-1)^{2}\right]\,.

Furthermore, if
n\sqrt{n}\alpha\leq 3\sqrt{2}-4=0.2426...
then

(2.14)

(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}|B|\leq 4.5\,n\sqrt{n}\alpha\,.

Proof.

We first estimate
|P^{k}_{j}|
for
P^{k}_{ij}={S^{k}_{ij}}f
. For
k\neq j
the only term contributing in the sum is
\zeta_{k}P^{k}_{kj}
, and thus

(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})|P^{k}_{j}|\leq\sqrt{2}\alpha
. When
k=j
all summands may contribute and we see that
(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})|P^{k}_{j}|\leq\sqrt{2}n\alpha
. Therefore, for every column
C
of
A
we have

(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})|C|\leq\sqrt{2}\sqrt{n^{2}+(n-1)}\,\alpha\,.

The bound (
2.11
) follows from the estimate
\sqrt{2}\sqrt{n^{2}+n-1}\leq 1.6n
.

For the second part, we use (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) to find that

(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{2}|P^{0}_{j}|\leq\frac{2}{n-1}\left(\sqrt{2}\left[2+(n-1)^{2}\right]\alpha+\left[n+(n-1)^{2}\right]\alpha^{2}\right)\,,

which establishes (
2.12
)-(
2.13
) because
|B|\leq\sqrt{n}\max_{j}|P^{0}_{j}|
.

For the last claim, fix
\epsilon>0
and suppose that
n\sqrt{n}\alpha\leq\epsilon
. Then

c_{1}(n)\alpha+c_{2}(n)\alpha^{2}\leq\left(c_{1}(n)+\frac{c_{2}(n)}{n\sqrt{n}}\epsilon\right)\alpha\,.

The inequality (
2.14
) will then hold if

2\sqrt{2}\left[2+(n-1)^{2}\right]+2\frac{n+(n-1)^{2}}{n\sqrt{n}}\epsilon\leq 4.5\,n(n-1)\,,

or equivalently, after dividing by
2
and shifting the term
\sqrt{2}(n-1)^{2}
to the right, if

2\sqrt{2}+\frac{n+(n-1)^{2}}{n\sqrt{n}}\epsilon\leq(n-1)\left[2.25n-\sqrt{2}(n-1)\right]\,,

that is, if

\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{n-1}+\frac{n+(n-1)^{2}}{n(n-1)\sqrt{n}}\epsilon\leq(2.25-\sqrt{2})n+\sqrt{2}\,.

Because the left hand-side is decreasing in
n
while the right hand-side is increasing, we see that (
2.14
)
will hold provided it holds for
n=2
, that is, if

3\sqrt{2}+\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{2}\epsilon\leq 4.5\,,

which gives the constant
3\sqrt{2}-4
as the maximum possible value for
\epsilon
. This finishes the proof.
∎

It will become useful to compare the constant

with the bound

in simple terms.
We have that

3.
Normalization and Finite Norm

In this section we will establish important bounds for mappings in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
. In particular, we will show that the family
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
is compact and will exhibit a connection between the order

{\lambda}_{\alpha}=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}}|\nabla J_{f}(0)|

and the size of the largest Euclidean ball centered at the origin that is contained in every image
f({\mathbb{P}}^{n})
,
\,f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
.
To this end, we will consider the associated family
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{0}
of mappings
g=T(f)
for
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
and
T
a specific Möbius transformation that ensures the additional normalization
\nabla J_{g}(0)=0
. Any such mapping will remain locally biholomorphic away from a possibly existing singular set in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
that depends on
g
itself. In any case, such a set will stay away some fixed distance
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,\alpha)
from the origin, and will not exist in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
altogether when
\alpha
is sufficiently small. The procedure of normalizing is described in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be locally biholomorphic, with
f(0)=0
and
Df(0)=\textsl{I}
. Then there exists a Möbius map
T
such
g=T\circ f
satisfies that
g(0)=0
,
Dg(0)=\rm{Id}
and
\nabla J_{g}(0)=0.

Proof.

For
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
let
T
be a Möbius map of the form

(3.1)

T_{a}(w)=\left(\frac{w_{1}}{1+a_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}w_{n}},\ldots,\frac{w_{n}}{1+a_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}w_{n}}\right)\,.

The mapping
g=T(f)
has
g(0)=0
and
Dg(0)=\textsl{I}
. Also,
Dg(z)=DT(f(z))\cdot Df(z)
hence
J_{g}(z)=J_{T}(f(z))\cdot J_{f}(z)
and

\nabla J_{g}(z)=\nabla J_{T}(f(z))\cdot Df(z)\cdot J_{f}(z)+J_{T}(f(z))\cdot\nabla J_{f}(z)\,.

Evaluating at the origin we obtain that

\nabla J_{g}(0)=\nabla J_{T}(0)+\nabla J_{f}(0)=-(n+1)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})+\nabla J_{f}(0)\,,

so that
the lemma holds if
(n+1)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\nabla J_{f}(0)
.
∎

It is important to observe that
u=(J_{g})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}=(1+a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}f_{n})(J_{f})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}
is a solution of (
2.2
) with
P^{k}_{ij}={S^{k}_{ij}}f
and the initial conditions
u(0)=1,\nabla u(0)=0
. It vanishes precisely when
1+a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}f_{n}=0
, that is, when the image
f({\mathbb{P}}^{n})
meets the hyperplane
1+a_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}w_{n}=0
, or
g
becomes infinite.

In order to analyze normalization, we will require estimates obtained from comparison techniques for differential equation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
h:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative function satisfying

h^{\prime\prime}\leq\frac{2a}{1-x^{2}}h^{\prime}+\frac{b}{(1-x^{2})^{2}}h\;\,,\;h(0)=1\,,\,h^{\prime}(0)=0\,,

where
a,b>0
are constants. Then

(3.2)

h^{\prime}\leq\frac{a+c}{1-x^{2}}\,h\,,

and

(3.3)

h\leq 2\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^{\frac{a+c-1}{2}}\,,

where
c=\sqrt{1+b+a^{2}}
.

Proof.

We will analyze the function
h
as a function of the variable
s=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}
defined on

[0,\infty)
. The change of variable
x=x(s)
has
x^{\prime}=1-x^{2}
. Let

w(s)=\frac{h}{u}(x(s))\,,

where
u(x)=\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
. Then
w(0)=1,w^{\prime}(0)=0
and
w^{\prime}=uh^{\prime}-u^{\prime}h
, with the agreement that derivatives of
w
are with respect to
s
, while those of
u,h
are with respect to
x
. One further derivative yields
w^{\prime\prime}=(1-x^{2})(uh^{\prime\prime}-hu^{\prime\prime})
, and because
u^{\prime\prime}=-u/(1-x^{2})^{2}
we see that

w^{\prime\prime}\leq 2a(uh^{\prime})+\frac{1+b}{1-x^{2}}\,uh\,.

We replace the term
uh^{\prime}
in the right hand side with
w^{\prime}+u^{\prime}h=w^{\prime}-(x/u)h=w^{\prime}-xw
, and because
h
, and thus
w
, are non-negative, we obtain

w^{\prime\prime}\leq 2a(w^{\prime}-xw)+(1+b)w\leq 2aw^{\prime}+(1+b)w\,.

Let
{\lambda}_{1,2}=-a\pm\sqrt{1+b+a^{2}}=-a\pm c
be the roots of the equation
{\lambda}({\lambda}+2a)=1+b
, where
{\lambda}_{1}>0>{\lambda}_{2}
. Then

w^{\prime\prime}+{\lambda}_{1}w^{\prime}\leq(2a+{\lambda}_{1})w^{\prime}+(1+b)w=(2a+{\lambda}_{1})(w^{\prime}+{\lambda}_{1}w)=-{\lambda}_{2}(w^{\prime}+{\lambda}_{1}w)\,,

which upon amplification by
e^{{\lambda}_{1}s}
yields

\left(e^{{\lambda}_{1}s}w^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}\leq-{\lambda}_{2}\left(e^{{\lambda}_{1}s}w\right)^{\prime}\,.

The initial conditions
w(0)=1,w^{\prime}(0)=0
imply that

e^{{\lambda}_{1}s}w^{\prime}\leq-{\lambda}_{2}\left(e^{{\lambda}_{1}s}w-1\right)\,,

hence

(3.4)

e^{{\lambda}_{1}s}(w^{\prime}+{\lambda}_{2}w)\leq{\lambda}_{2}\,.

Multiplication now by
e^{({\lambda}_{2}-{\lambda}_{1})s}
and integration leads to

w\,\leq\,\frac{{\lambda}_{2}e^{-{\lambda}_{1}s}-{\lambda}_{1}e^{-{\lambda}_{2}s}}{{\lambda}_{2}-{\lambda}_{1}}\,=\,\frac{1}{2c}\left((c-a)e^{(a+c)s}+(c+a)e^{(a-c)s}\right)

=\,\frac{e^{(a+c)s}}{2c}\,\left(c-a+(c+a)e^{-2cs}\right)\,\leq\,e^{(a+c)s}\,.

In terms of the function
h
we see that

h\leq\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^{\frac{a+c}{2}}=(1+x)\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^{\frac{a+c-1}{2}}\leq 2\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^{\frac{a+c-1}{2}}\,.

Finally, we see from (
3.4
) that
w^{\prime}+{\lambda}_{2}w\leq 0
, which yields

h^{\prime}u+\frac{x}{u}h\leq(a+c)\frac{h}{u}\,,

hence

h^{\prime}\leq\frac{a+c}{1-x^{2}}\,h\,.

This proves (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let (
2.2
) be a completely integrable system in canonical form with
\|P\|\leq\alpha
. Then the solution
u
with
u(0)=1,\nabla u(0)=0
satisfies

(3.5)

|u|\leq 2\left(\frac{1+|z|_{\infty}}{1-|z|_{\infty}}\right)^{\gamma}\,,

(3.6)

|\nabla u|\leq 2\,\frac{1+2\gamma}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}\left(\frac{1+|z|_{\infty}}{1-|z|_{\infty}}\right)^{\gamma}\,,

where
\displaystyle 2\gamma=\sqrt{1+b+a^{2}}-1
,
a=0.8n\sqrt{n}\alpha
and
b=2(3+2\alpha)\sqrt{n}a
.

Proof.

For
\zeta\in\partial{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
we let
v(t)=u(t\zeta)
,
t\in[0,1]
. We seek to estimate the functions

w_{j}(t)=\partial u/\partial z_{j}(t\zeta)
. The
t
-derivatives of these quantities are given by

\begin{array}[]{lll}w_{j}^{\prime}&=&\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{1}\partial z_{j}}\zeta_{1}+\cdots+\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{n}\partial z_{j}}\zeta_{n}\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{k}_{1j}\frac{\partial u}{\partial z_{k}}+P^{0}_{1j}u\right)\zeta_{1}+\cdots+\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{k}_{nj}\frac{\partial u}{\partial z_{k}}+P^{0}_{nj}u\right)\zeta_{n}\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(P^{k}_{1j}\zeta_{1}+\cdots P^{k}_{nj}\zeta_{n}\right)\frac{\partial u}{\partial z_{k}}+\left(P^{0}_{1j}\zeta_{1}+\cdots P^{0}_{nj}\zeta_{n}\right)u\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(P^{k}_{1j}\zeta_{1}+\cdots P^{k}_{nj}\zeta_{n}\right)w_{k}+\left(P^{0}_{1j}\zeta_{1}+\cdots P^{0}_{nj}\zeta_{n}\right)u=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{k}_{j}w_{k}+P^{0}_{j}u\,.\end{array}

Let
\varphi(t)
the vector function in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with coordinates
\varphi_{i}=w_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Since
v^{\prime}=\nabla u\cdot\zeta
we can see that

\varphi^{\prime}=A\cdot\varphi+uB\,,

where
A=(P^{k}_{j})_{k,j}
is an
n\times n
matrix and
B
is the vector with components
P^{0}_{j}
.
Since
|\varphi|^{\prime}\leq|\varphi^{\prime}|
we see that

|\varphi|^{\prime}\leq|A||\varphi|+|B||u|\,,

where
|A|=\max_{|v|=1}|Av|
. The function
\phi(t)=\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{t}|\varphi(s)|ds+1
is an upper bound for
|u|
with
\phi(0)=1,\phi^{\prime}(0)=0

and satisfies

\phi^{\prime\prime}\leq|A|\phi^{\prime}+\sqrt{n}|B|\phi\,.

The inequalities (
2.11
) and (
2.15
) show that
(1-t^{2})|A|\leq 2a
and

(1-t^{2})^{2}|B|\leq b=2\sqrt{n}(3+2\alpha)a
, where
a=0.8n\sqrt{n}\alpha
. It follows now from Lemma 3.2 that

\hskip 22.76228pt\phi\leq 2\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^{\gamma}\;\;,\quad\;\phi^{\prime}\leq\,2\,\frac{1+2\gamma}{1-t^{2}}\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^{\gamma}\,,

for
2\gamma=a+\sqrt{1+b+a^{2}}-1
. This finishes the proof.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let

be fixed. The set of solutions

of completely integrable systems (

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

. Then there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,\alpha)>0
such that

is holomorphic in

Proof.

Let
g=T(f)\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{0}
with

as before.
Then
u=(J_{g})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}=(1+a_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}w_{n})(J_{f})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}

2.2
) with
P^{k}_{ij}={S^{k}_{ij}}f,P^{0}_{ij}=S^{0}_{ij}
that vanishes precisely when

ceases to be holomorphic. We will show that

in some neighborhood of the origin.

For
\zeta\in\partial{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
we define
v(t)=|u(t\zeta)|
,

. Therefore

Since

v(t)v^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq\mbox{Re}\left\{\overline{u}S_{f}(t\zeta)(\zeta)\cdot\nabla u(t\zeta)+|u|^{2}S^{0}_{f}(t\zeta)(\zeta)\right\}\,,

(3.7)

The bound (

v^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}v\geq-\frac{\delta}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^{\gamma}\,,

with

are positive constants. We rewrite this inequality as

for some
\eta(t)\geq 0
, and claim that
v\geq y
for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
, where
t_{0}
is the first zero of the solution

of

y^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}y=-\frac{\delta}{1-t^{2}}\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^{\gamma},\quad y(0)=1\,,\;y^{\prime}(0)=0\,.

For this, observe first that

, hence from Sturm comparison we have that

on

for the solution of

For

The functions

. Since difference

we see, once more, from Sturm comparison that

on

. Therefore,

on
[0,t_{0}]
, which proves the claim by letting

.
We conclude finally that

for all

with

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

The family
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
has finite order bounded by

for some

{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{0}
covers a Euclidean ball of radius

centered at the origin, and every mapping in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}

Proof.

Corollary 3.4 and Lemma 3.5 show that the family

is compact in any ball

for

. We conclude that there exists

such that

for every

. An estimate for

in terms of

will be given in the final section. The mapping

Theorem 3.8
.

Let (

) be a completely integrable system in canonical form with
\|P\|\leq\alpha
, and let

be the solution with
u(0)=1,\nabla u(0)=0
. If

(3.11)

n\sqrt{n}\alpha\leq\frac{1}{6.1}

{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

(3.12)

\left|\frac{\nabla u}{u}\right|\leq\frac{|z|_{\infty}}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}\,.

Let
u

\zeta\in\partial{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
we let

\varphi(t)=\frac{\nabla u}{u}(t\zeta)\,,

. We claim that

\alpha

We see that

\varphi^{\prime}(t)=\left[\frac{1}{u}\mbox{Hess }u(\zeta,\cdot)\right](t\zeta)-\left[\frac{\nabla u\cdot\zeta}{u^{2}}\nabla u\right](t\zeta)\,,

\mbox{Hess }u(\zeta,\cdot)=A\cdot\nabla u+uB\,,

where
A=(a_{ij})
is the
n\times n
matrix given by
a_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{ik}^{j}f\zeta_{k}
, and
B=(b_{i})
is the vector with

b_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S^{0}_{ik}\zeta_{k}
. Hence the function
g(t)=|\varphi(t)|
has
g(0)=0
and satisfies

(3.13)

g^{\prime}\leq|\varphi^{\prime}|\leq\frac{ag}{1-t^{2}}+\frac{b}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}+g^{2}\,,

where, as before, we use (2.11) and (2.14) that see that
a\leq 1.6n\sqrt{n}\alpha
and
b\leq 4.5n\sqrt{n}\alpha
. The theorem now follows from Lemma 3.7 with
c=n\sqrt{n}\alpha
.
∎

We draw the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 3.9
.

If
n\sqrt{n}\alpha\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{6.1}
then all mappings in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{0}
are holomorphic in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.

The next result is also a direct consequence of Theorem 3.8.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let (
2.2
) be a completely integrable system in canonical form with
\|P\|\leq\alpha
, and let
u
be the solution with
u(0)=1,\nabla u(0)=0
. If

n\sqrt{n}\alpha\leq\frac{1}{6.1}

then

\left(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}}\leq|u|\leq\frac{1}{\left(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}}}\,.

Proof.

Since
u(0)=1
, we find from integrating the bound in (
3.12
) that

|\log u|\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\,\log\frac{1}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}\,,

which gives the result.
∎

The following corollary follows at once from the previous theorem, and mimics results for mappings in linearly invariant families
[
7
]
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{0}
for
n\sqrt{n}\alpha\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{6.1}
. Then

(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{\frac{\sqrt{n}(n+1)}{2}}\leq|J_{f}|\leq\frac{1}{(1-|z|_{\infty}^{2})^{\frac{\sqrt{n}(n+1)}{2}}}\,.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
. For
z\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
we let
\eta(z)=\min_{|v|=1}|Df(z)v|
and
\beta=\alpha+2\sqrt{2}|z|_{\infty}
.
Then the image
f({\mathbb{P}}^{n})
covers a Euclidean ball centered at
f(z)
of radius
at least

\frac{1}{2}(1-|z|_{\infty})\eta(z)s_{0}(n,\beta)\,,

and

|\nabla J_{f}(z)|_{\infty}\leq\frac{{\lambda}_{\beta}+2|z|_{\infty}}{1-|z|_{\infty}^{2}}\left|J_{f}(z)\right|\,,

where
s_{0}(n,\beta)
is the constant defined in Theorem 3.6.

Proof.

For
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
fixed, let
g
be defined by

g(z)=[D\psi(0)]^{-1}[Df(z_{0})]^{-1}(f(\psi(z))-f(z_{0})),\quad\psi(0)=z_{0}\,,

where
\psi=\psi_{z_{0}}
is an automorphism as given by (
2.4
). Then
g(0)=0
,
Dg(0)=\textsl{I}
and we have that

\|S_{g}\|\leq\alpha+2\sqrt{2}|z_{0}|_{\infty}
. Hence by (
2.6
)
g\in\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
for
\beta=\alpha+2\sqrt{2}|z_{0}|_{\infty}
, and by Theorem 3.6,
g({\mathbb{P}}^{n})
contains
a ball centered at the origin of Euclidean radius at least
\frac{1}{2}s_{0}(n,\beta)
. Since
D\psi(0)
is a diagonal matrix of elements
1-|z^{0}_{k}|^{2}
,
we see that
\min_{|v|=1}|D\psi(0)v|\geq 1-|z_{0}|_{\infty}^{2}
. The first statement of the theorem follows now from the definition of
g
.

On the other hand,

J_{g}(z)=J_{\psi}(0)^{-1}\cdot J_{f}(z_{0})^{-1}\cdot J_{f}(\psi(z))\cdot J_{\psi}(z)\,,

which implies that
\log J_{g}(z)=\log J_{f}(\psi(z))+\log J_{\psi}(z)+\mbox{constant}
, and

(3.14)

\nabla J_{g}(0)=\frac{\nabla J_{f}}{J_{f}}(z_{0})D\psi(0)+\frac{\nabla J_{\psi}}{J_{\psi}}(0)\,.

Because
g\in\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
we must have that
|\nabla J_{g}(0)|\leq{\lambda}_{\beta}
. In addition, with
z_{0}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})

J_{\psi}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{(1-\overline{a_{j}}z_{j})^{2}}\,,

therefore

\frac{\nabla J_{\psi}}{J_{\psi}}(z)=\left(\frac{2\overline{a_{1}}}{1-\overline{a_{1}}z_{1}},\ldots,\frac{2\overline{a}_{n}}{1-\overline{a_{n}}z_{n}}\right)\,,

so that
\displaystyle\frac{\nabla J_{\psi}}{J_{\psi}}(0)=2\overline{z}_{0}
.
From (
3.14
) we obtain

\left|\frac{\nabla J_{f}}{J_{f}}(z_{0})D\psi(0)\right|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{\beta}+2|z_{0}|_{\infty}\,.

Since
D\psi(0)
is a diagonal matrix with elements
1-|a_{k}|^{2}
, we have

(1-|z_{0}|_{\infty}^{2})|\nabla J_{f}(z_{0})|_{\infty}\leq(\lambda_{\beta}+2|z_{0}|_{\infty})|J_{f}(z_{0})|\,,

which shows that

|\nabla J_{f}(z_{0})|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\lambda_{\beta}+2|z_{0}|_{\infty}}{1-|z_{0}|_{\infty}^{2}}\left|J_{f}(z_{0})\right|\,.

∎

4.
Order

The purpose in this section is to establish an explicit upper bound for the order of the family
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
. We will consider the families
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,r}
consisting of all mappings
f
that are defined and locally biholomorphic for
|z|_{\infty}<r<1
, which have
f(0)=0,Df(0)=\textsl{I}
, and
\|{S^{k}_{ij}}f\|\leq\alpha
; here
\|\cdot\|
stands for the Bergman norm in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
restricted to the set
|z|_{\infty}<r
. We see that
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,r}
, and it follows as before that
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,r}
is compact. For fixed
r
we see that

(4.1)

{\mu_{r}(\alpha)}=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,r}}{|\nabla J_{f}(0)|}<\infty\,,

which is attained for some mapping in the class. In addition,
{\lambda}_{\alpha}\leq\mu_{r}(\alpha)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
r^{2}<1/5
. Then the function
\mu_{r}={\mu_{r}(\alpha)}
is locally Lipschitz for
\alpha>0
, and whenever the derivative exists,

(4.2)

\alpha\mu_{r}^{\prime}(\alpha)\leq C(r)\mu_{r}(\alpha)\,,

with
\displaystyle C(r)=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-5r^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha>0
be fixed, and let
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,r}
be extremal for (
4.1
). We consider the dilation of
f
given by
g(z)=\frac{1}{s}f(sz)
for
0<s<1
. It is easy to see that
|\nabla J_{g}(0)|=s|\nabla J_{f}(0)|=s\mu_{r}(\alpha)
, and we claim that
g\in\mathcal{F}_{\beta,r}
for

\beta=s\left(\frac{1-s^{2}r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\right)^{2}\alpha<\alpha\,.

From the composition law (
2.1
) we see that
{S^{k}_{ij}}g(z)=s{S^{k}_{ij}}f(sz)
, hence in order to estimate
\|S_{g}(z)\|
we must consider how the Bergman norm of given vector
\vec{v}
changes when measured at
z
and
sz
. Let
\vec{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
be a Bergman unit vector at
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
. The square of the Bergman norm
\|\vec{v}\|_{sz}
at
sz
is given by

\sum_{i}\frac{2|v_{i}|^{2}}{(1-s^{2}|z_{i}|^{2})}=\sum_{i}t_{i}\left(\frac{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}{1-s^{2}|z_{i}|^{2}}\right)^{2}\,,

where
t_{i}=2|v_{i}|^{2}/(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{2}
are non-negative numbers with
\sum_{i}t_{i}=1
. It follows that

\|\vec{v}\|^{2}_{sz}
is a number between the maximum and minimum value of the quantities
\left[(1-|z_{i}|^{2})/(1-s^{2}|z_{i}|^{2})\right]^{2}
.
Because the function
(1-x^{2})/(1-s^{2}x^{2})
is decreasing for
x\in[0,1)
we see that

\left(\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-s^{2}r^{2}}\right)^{2}\leq\|\vec{v}\|_{sz}^{2}\leq 1\,,

therefore

\|S_{g}(z)\|\leq s\left(\frac{1-s^{2}r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\right)^{2}\alpha\,.

Simple algebra shows that
s\left(\frac{1-s^{2}r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\right)^{2}<1
for
r^{2}<1/5
, hence
g\in\mathcal{F}_{\beta,r}
and
\beta<\alpha
as claimed.

Finally, since
|\nabla J_{g}(0)|=s{\mu_{r}(\alpha)}
we conclude that
\mu_{r}(\beta)\geq s{\mu_{r}(\alpha)}
, and so

{\mu_{r}(\alpha)}-\mu_{r}(\beta)\leq(1-s){\mu_{r}(\alpha)}\leq C(r)(\alpha-\beta)\frac{{\mu_{r}(\alpha)}}{\alpha}\,.

This proves the theorem.
∎

For our next theorem, recall the order
{\lambda}_{\alpha}=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}}|\nabla J_{f}(0)|
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For each
\sigma>0
there exists
m(\sigma)>0
such that

{\lambda}_{\alpha}\leq 2(n+1)+m(\sigma,n)\alpha^{1+\sigma}\,,

for some
m(\sigma,n)
depending on
\sigma
and
n
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{1}>0
be fixed. It follows from (
4.2
) that for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}

{\mu_{r}(\alpha)}\leq\mu_{r}(\alpha_{1})\left(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha_{1}}\right)^{C(r)}\,,

where
C(r)=(1-r^{2})/(1-5r^{2})>1
approaches
1
as
r\rightarrow 0
. Hence, for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}
we also have

(4.3)

{\lambda}_{\alpha}\leq\frac{\mu_{r}(\alpha_{1})}{\alpha_{1}^{C(r)}}\,\alpha^{C(r)}\,.

We now estimate
{\lambda}_{0}
, that is, the order of the family
\mathcal{F}_{0}
consisting of Möbius mappings of the form

f(z)=\left(\frac{z_{1}}{l(z)},\ldots,\frac{z_{n}}{l(z)}\right)\;,\;\,l(z)=1-a_{1}z_{1}-\cdots-a_{n}z_{n}\,,

with the requirement that
l(z)\neq 0
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. This happens iff
\sum_{i}|a_{i}|\leq 1
. Hence

|\nabla J_{f}(0)|=(n+1)\sqrt{|a_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|a_{n}|^{2}}\leq(n+1)\left(\sum_{i}|a_{i}|\right)^{2}\leq n+1\,,

with equality when a single
a_{i}
has absolute value 1. This shows that
{\lambda}_{0}=n+1
,
and the theorem follows now from (
4.3
) by choosing
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{1}(n)
so that, for example,
{\lambda}_{\alpha_{1}}=2{\lambda}_{0}
.

∎

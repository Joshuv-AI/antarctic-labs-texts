Variable metric backward-forward dynamical systems for monotone inclusion problems
Pankaj Gautam, D. R. Sahu, J. C. Yao

Abstract
. This paper investigates first-order variable metric backward forward dynamical systems associated with monotone inclusion and convex minimization problems in real Hilbert space. The operators are chosen so that the backward-forward dynamical system is closely related to the forward-backward dynamical system and has the same computational complexity. We show existence, uniqueness, and weak asymptotic convergence of the generated trajectories and strong convergence if one of the operators is uniformly monotone. We also establish that an equilibrium point of the trajectory is globally exponentially stable and monotone attractor. As a particular case, we explore similar perspectives of the trajectories generated by a dynamical system related to the minimization of the sum of a nonsmooth convex and a smooth convex function. Numerical examples are given to illustrate the convergence of trajectories.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper
H
denotes a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
and
I
denote the identity operator.

Let
T:H\to 2^{H}
be a set-valued operator.
T^{-1}:H\to 2^{H}
is inverse of
T
which is defined by the relation
y\in Tx\Leftrightarrow x\in T^{-1}y
. The graph of
T
is the set
\operatorname{gra}(T):=\{(x,y)\in H\times H:y\in Tx\}
. The resolvent of an operator
T
of index
\gamma
is defined by
J_{\gamma T}:=(I+\gamma T)^{-1}
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. The Yosida approximation of
T
of index
\gamma
is defined by
T_{\gamma}=(T^{-1}+\gamma I)^{-1}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. For
\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
(T_{\gamma})_{\delta}=T_{\gamma+\delta}
; in particular,
(T_{-\gamma})_{\gamma}=T
(see
[
5
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

[
7
]

A set-valued operator
T:H\to 2^{H}
is said to be

(i)

monotone if

\left\langle x-y,u-v\right\rangle\geq 0~\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in\operatorname{gra}(T);

(ii)

maximal monotone if there exist no monotone operator
S:H\rightarrow 2^{H}
such that
\operatorname{gra}(S)
properly contains
\operatorname{gra}(T)
;

(iii)

maximally
\rho
-cohypomonotone if
T_{\rho}=(T^{-1}+\rho I)^{-1}
is maximally monotone, where
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
;

(iv)

uniformly monotone with modulus function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, if
\phi
is increasing, vanishes only at
0
, and

\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\phi(\|x-y\|)~\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in\operatorname{gra}(T);

(v)

strongly monotone with constant
\rho\in[0,\infty)
if
T-\rho I
is monotone, i.e.,

\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\rho\|x-y\|^{2}~\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in\operatorname{gra}(T).

On cohypomonotonicity, its useful and related notions see

[

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
T:H\to 2^{H}
be a set-valued operator and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Then we have the following:

(a)

J_{\gamma T}^{M}=I-\gamma M^{-1}T_{\gamma}
.

(b)

T_{\gamma}
is defined everywhere and single-valued whenever
J_{\gamma T}^{M}
is.

(c)

J_{\gamma(T_{-\gamma})}^{M}=I-\gamma M^{-1}T
.

(d)

T
is defined everywhere and single-valued whenever
J_{\gamma(T_{-\gamma})}^{M}
is.

Proof.

(a) Since
\gamma\neq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle y\in J_{\gamma T}^{M}x

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x\in y+\gamma M^{-1}Ty

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{x-y}{\gamma}\in M^{-1}T(x-\gamma\frac{x-y}{\gamma})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y\in x-\gamma M^{-1}T_{\gamma}x.

(b) It follows from (a).

(c) Replace
T
by
T_{-\gamma}
in (a), we get

J_{\gamma(T_{-\gamma})}^{M}=I-\gamma M^{-1}(T_{-\gamma})_{\gamma}=I-\gamma M^{-1}T.

(d) It follows from (c).

∎

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
A:H\to 2^{H}
be maximally
(\gamma-\alpha)
-cohypomonotone,
B:H\to H
be
\beta
-cocoercive and
M:H\to H
be a strongly positive such that
\|M^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{\kappa}\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
, where
\kappa>0
. Then we have the following:

(a)

M^{-1}B
is
\kappa
-cocoercive.

(b)

M^{-1}A_{\gamma}
is
\kappa
-cocoercive.

Proof.

(a) Let
x,y\in H
.
Since
B
is
\beta
-cocercive, we have

\langle Bx-By,x-y\rangle\geq\beta\|Bx-By\|^{2}\\
\Leftrightarrow\langle M^{-1}Bx-M^{-1}By,x-y\rangle_{M}\geq\beta\langle Bx-By,Bx-By\rangle.

Also,
\kappa\|M^{-1}Bx-M^{-1}By\|_{M}=\kappa\langle Bx-By,M^{-1}(Bx-By)\rangle
. Altogether, denoting
w:=(Bx-By)
, we obtain

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle M^{-1}Bx-M^{-1}By,x-y\rangle_{M}-\kappa\|M^{-1}Bx-M^{-1}By\|_{M}\geq\beta\|w\|^{2}-\kappa\langle w,M^{-1}w\rangle.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\kappa\langle w,M^{-1}w\rangle\leq\kappa\|M^{-1}\|\|w\|^{2}.

Hence, from (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\langle M^{-1}Bx-M^{-1}By,x-y\rangle_{M}\geq\kappa\|M^{-1}Bx-M^{-1}By\|_{M},

which implies that
M^{-1}B
is
\kappa
-cocoercive.

(b) From Lemma
2.8
,
A_{\gamma}
is
\alpha
-cocoercive operator. So, in the similar manner, one can show
M^{-1}A_{\gamma}
is
\kappa
-cocoercive.
∎

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
\gamma\neq 0.
Let
A:H\to 2^{H}
be a set-valued operator such that
J_{\gamma A}^{M}
is single-valued and everywhere defined and
B:H\to H
be an operator. Define
T_{1}:=(I+\gamma M^{-1}A)^{-1}(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)
and
T_{2}:=(I+\gamma M^{-1}B_{-\gamma})^{-1}(I-\gamma M^{-1}A_{\gamma})
, where
M:H\to H
is a strongly positive operator. Then the following statements hold:

(a)

x\in\operatorname{Fix}(T_{1})\Leftrightarrow x\in\operatorname{Zer}(A+B)\Leftrightarrow A_{\gamma}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x+Bx=0
.

(b)

y\in\operatorname{Fix}(T_{2})\Leftrightarrow y\in\operatorname{Zer}(B_{-\gamma}+A_{\gamma})\Leftrightarrow B\circ J_{\gamma A}^{M}y+A_{\gamma}y=0
.

(c)

I-\gamma M^{-1}B:\operatorname{Zer}(A+B)\to\operatorname{Zer}(B_{-\gamma}+A_{\gamma})
is a bijection with inverse
J_{\gamma A}^{M}
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose that
x\in\operatorname{Zer}(A+B)
. Then

\displaystyle 0\in(A+B)x

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 0\in\gamma M^{-1}Ax+\gamma M^{-1}Bx

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x\in(I+\gamma M^{-1}A)x

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x=(I+\gamma M^{-1}A)^{-1}(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x\in\operatorname{Fix}(T_{1}).

Also,

\displaystyle x\in\operatorname{Fix}(T_{1})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x\in\operatorname{Fix}[(I-\gamma M^{-1}A_{\gamma})(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x=x-\gamma M^{-1}Bx-\gamma M^{-1}A_{\gamma}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow A_{\gamma}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x+Bx=0.

In (a) apply
(B_{-\gamma},A_{\gamma})
in place of
(A,B)
and using Proposition
2.18
, we have the result.

Let
x\in\operatorname{Zer}(A+B)
. By (a), we have

\displaystyle A_{\gamma}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x+B\circ J_{\gamma A}^{M}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(A_{\gamma}+B\circ J_{\gamma A}^{M})\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x\in\operatorname{Zer}(B_{-\gamma}+A_{\gamma}).

Also, for
y\in\operatorname{Zer}(B_{-\gamma}+A_{\gamma})
, from (b), we deduce

\displaystyle B\circ J_{\gamma A}^{M}y+A_{\gamma}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)\circ J_{\gamma A}^{M}y=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(B+A_{\gamma}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B))\circ J_{\gamma A}^{M}y=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow J_{\gamma A}^{M}y\in\operatorname{Zer}(A+B).

Finally,
J_{\gamma A}^{M}\circ(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)x=x

(I-\gamma M^{-1}B)\circ J_{\gamma A}^{M}y=y
for

and
y\in\operatorname{Zer}(B_{-\gamma}+A_{\gamma})
.
∎

3.
Convergence of trajectories generated by first order dynamical systems

3.1.
Operator Framework

Consider the dynamical system

\displaystyle{\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc@{}c@{}r}\dot{u}(t)=\lambda(t)[T(u(t))-u(t)]\\[6.0pt]
u(0)=u_{0},\end{array}\right.}

where
u_{0}\in H
,
T:H\to H
is an
\alpha
-averaged operator and
\lambda:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a Lebesgue measurable function satisfying

(3.4)

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\lambda}\leq\inf\limits_{t\geq 0}\lambda(t)\leq\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\lambda(t)\leq\overline{\lambda},

where
\underline{\lambda},\overline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
.
First we establish the following result for the dynamical system (
3.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T:H\to H
be an
\alpha
-averaged operator for
\alpha\in(0,1)
with
\operatorname{Fix}(T)\neq\emptyset
. Let
u:[0,\infty)\to H
be the unique strong global solution of the dynamical system (
3.1
).
Then we have the following:

(i)

The trajectory
u
is bounded and
\dot{u}
,
(I-T)u\in L^{2}([0,\infty);H)
.

(ii)

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dot{u}(t)=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}(I-T)(u(t))=0
.

(iii)

u(t)\rightharpoonup\bar{u}\in\operatorname{Fix}(T)
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

(i) Let
u^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(T)
. Define
k(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)-u^{*}\|^{2}
,
t\in[0,\infty)
. Then
\dot{k}(t)=\langle u(t)-u^{*},\dot{u}(t)\rangle
. From (
3.1
), and the fact that
(I-T)u^{*}=0
, we have for every
t\in[0,\infty)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\dot{k}(t)+\lambda(t)\langle u(t)-u^{*},(I-T)(u(t))-(I-T)u^{*}\rangle=0.

Since
T
is
\alpha
-averaged operator, so there exists a nonexpansive operator
R:H\to H
such that
T=(1-\alpha)I+\alpha R
and
\operatorname{Fix}(T)=\operatorname{Fix}(R)
. From (
3.5
) we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\dot{k}(t)+\alpha\lambda(t)\langle u(t)-u^{*},(I-R)(u(t))-(I-R)u^{*}\rangle=0.

Remark
2.5
shows that
I-R
is
\frac{1}{2}
-cocoercive operator. From (
3.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{k}(t)+\frac{\alpha\lambda(t)}{2}\|(I-R)(u(t))\|^{2}\leq 0,

which implies that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\dot{k}(t)+\frac{\lambda(t)}{2\alpha}\|(I-T)(u(t))\|^{2}\leq 0.

From (
3.1
) and
(\ref{2.4a})
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{k}(t)+\frac{1}{2\alpha\lambda(t)}\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0~for~all~t\in[0,\infty).

Using condition (
3.4
), we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle\dot{k}(t)+\frac{1}{2\alpha\overline{\lambda}}\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0~for~every~t\in[0,\infty).

From (
3.8
), we get that the function
t\mapsto k(t)
is monotonically decreasing. Also, the map
t\mapsto k(t)
is locally absolutely continuous. Hence there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

k(t)\leq N_{1}~for~all~t\in[0,\infty).

Thus,
k
is bounded, and hence
u
is bounded.

To integrate the inequality (
3.8
), we get that there is
N_{2}\in\mathbb{{R}}
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle k(t)+\frac{1}{2\alpha\overline{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}\leq N_{2}~~for~all~t\in[0,\infty).

Since
k
is bounded, so from (
3.9
), we conclude that
\dot{u}(t)\in L^{2}([0,\infty);H)
. Hence, from
(\ref{2.10})
and (
3.4
), we get that
(I-T)u\in L^{2}([0,\infty);H)
.

(ii) Using Remark
2.11
(b) and cocoercivity of
I-R
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|(I-T)(u(t))\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|(I-R)(u(t))\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\left\langle(I-R)(u(t)),\frac{d}{dt}((I-R)(u(t)))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\|(I-R)(u(t))\|^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|(I-T)(u(t))\|^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}~~\forall t\in[0,\infty).

By using Lemma
2.15
and part (i) we obtain
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}(I-T)(u(t))=0
, and from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we conclude that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dot{u}(t)=0.

(iii) We show that both the assumptions of Lemma
2.16
are satisfied.

As
k
is bounded and
t\mapsto k(t)
is monotonically decreasing, we observe that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}k(t)
exists and belongs to the set of real numbers. So,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)-u^{*}\|
exists.

Let
\bar{u}
be a weak sequential cluster point of
u(t)
, i.e, there exists a sequence

(as
n\to\infty
) such that
\{u(t_{n})\}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
. Using Lemma
2.17
and part (ii), we deduce that
\bar{u}\in\operatorname{Fix(R)}=\operatorname{Fix(T)}
and the conclusion follows.
∎

\exists\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\Phi(t,u^{*})-\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\Phi(t,v^{*})\in\mathbb{R}
, where

\Phi(t,u^{*})=\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|u(t)-u^{*}\|^{2}+f(u(t))-f(u^{*})-\langle\nabla f_{\gamma}(u^{*}),u(t)-u^{*}\rangle.

So, we get

(3.28)

\displaystyle\exists\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}\langle u(t),v^{*}-u^{*}\rangle+\langle\nabla f_{\gamma}(u^{*})-\nabla f_{\gamma}(v^{*}),u(t)\rangle\right)\in\mathbb{R}.

If we express (
3.28
) by the means of sequences
\{t_{n}\}
and
\{t^{\prime}_{n}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\langle u^{*},v^{*}-u^{*}\rangle+\langle \nabla f_{\gamma}(v^{*})-\nabla f_{\gamma}(u^{*}),u^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\langle v^{*},v^{*}-u^{*}\rangle+\langle \nabla f_{\gamma}(v^{*})-\nabla f_{\gamma}(u^{*}),v^{*}\rangle,

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\|u^{*}-v^{*}\|+\langle \nabla f_{\gamma}(v^{*})-\nabla f_{\gamma}(u^{*}),v^{*}-u^{*}\rangle=0,

and by the monotonicity of
\nabla f_{\gamma}
we conclude that
u^{*}=v^{*}
.

(v) The proof follows by using
A=\partial f
and
B=\nabla g
in Theorem
3.2
(vii).
∎

Conclusions

In this paper, first-order variable metric backward-forward dynamical systems associated with monotone inclusion, and convex minimization problems have been studied. Existence, uniqueness, weak and strong convergence of the trajectories of dynamical systems (

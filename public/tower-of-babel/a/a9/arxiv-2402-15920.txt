On an inequality of Lin, Kim and Hsieh and Strong Subadditivity
Eric A. Carlen, Michael P. Loss

Abstract
We give an elementary proof of an inequality of Lin, Kim and Hsieh that implies strong subadditivity of the non Neumann entropy.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{3}
be finite dimensional Hilbert spaces. For a density matrix
\rho_{123}
on

\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
,
let
\rho_{12}:=\textnormal{Tr}_{3}[\rho_{123}]
, the partial trace of
\rho_{123}
over
\mathcal{H}_{3}
, and
\rho_{1}:=\textnormal{Tr}_{2}[\rho_{12}]=\textnormal{Tr}_{23}[\rho_{123}]
, etc. In 1968, Lanford and Robinson
[
6
]
conjectured that the von Neumann entropies of
\rho_{123}
,
\rho_{2}
,
\rho_{12}
and
\rho_{23}
are related by

S(\rho_{12})+S(\rho_{23})\geqslant S(\rho_{123})+S(\rho_{2})\ .

(1)

In 1970, while the conjecture was still open, Araki and Lieb
[
3
]
showed by an elementary purification argument that (
1
) is valid for all tripartite density matrices
\rho_{123}
if and only if

S(\rho_{12})+S(\rho_{23})-S(\rho_{1})-S(\rho_{3})\geqslant 0

(2)

holds for all tripartite density matrices
\rho_{123}
. They did prove a weaker form of (
1
), with

S(\rho_{2})
replaced by
\log(\textnormal{Tr}[\rho_{2}^{2}])
, and showed that this sufficed to prove the thermodynamic limit that Lanford and Robinson had sought to prove.
However, (
1
) is of interest for many reasons, and work towards proving it continued.

To see that (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent, let
\mathcal{H}_{4}
be a fourth Hilbert space, and let
\Psi
be a unit vector in
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{4}
such that

\textnormal{Tr}_{4}[|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|]=\rho_{123}\ .

Such a unit vector
\Psi
always exists. By a 1935 theorem of Schrödinger
[
9
]
, if
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
are two finite dimensional Hilbert spaces,
and
\Phi
is any unit vector in
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}
, then there are orthonormal sets
\{{\bf u}_{1},\dots,{\bf u}_{r}\}
and
\{{\bf v}_{1},\dots,{\bf v}_{r}\}
in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
respectively, and positive numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}=1
such that

\Phi:=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}{\bf u}_{j}\otimes{\bf v}_{j}\ .

(3)

The bi-orthogonal decomposition (
3
) is usually called the
Schmidt decomposition
. What Schmidt actually wrote
about
[
8
]
was the closely related singular value decomposition for integral operators.

Taking the two partial traces of the pure state
|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|
yields

\rho_{\mathcal{H}}:=\textnormal{Tr}_{\mathcal{K}}[|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}|{\bf u}_{j}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{j}|\quad{\rm and}\quad\rho_{\mathcal{K}}:=\textnormal{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}[|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}|{\bf v}_{j}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{j}|\ .

(4)

Thus, as observed in
[
3
]
,
\rho_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
have the same non-zero spectrum, including multiplicities. In particular,

S(\rho_{\mathcal{H}})=S(\rho_{\mathcal{K}})\ .

(5)

To “purify”
\rho_{123}
, let
\rho_{123}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}|{\bf u}_{j}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{j}|
be a spectral decomposition of
\rho_{123}
, and let
\{{\bf v}_{1},\dots,{\bf v}_{r}\}
be an orthonormal set in
a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{4}
of dimension at least
r
. By (
4
),

\Psi=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}{\bf u}_{j}\otimes{\bf v}_{j}
has the property that
\textnormal{Tr}_{4}[|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|]=\rho_{123}
.
Now one can consider
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{4}
as a bipartite space
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}
in various ways. By (
4
) we have

S(\rho_{123})=S(\rho_{4})\quad{\rm and}\quad S(\rho_{23})=S(\rho_{14})\ .

(6)

Thus, (
1
) becomes

S(\rho_{12})+S(\rho_{14})\geqslant S(\rho_{4})+S(\rho_{2})\ ,

(7)

which, up to relabeling, is the same as (
2
). Conversely, because of (
6
), once one has proved (
7
), one has proved (
1
), as noted in
[
3
]
. However, until the recent paper
[
7
]
there was no direct proof of (
7
). The proof of (
1
) was given by Lieb and Ruskai
[
6
]
in 1973. Their proof used a deep convexity result of Lieb, proved in the same year
[
5
]
, that resolved another famous conjecture, the Wigner-Yanase-Dyson Conjecture. The following, rather surprising theorem was proved in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Lin, Kim and Hsieh)
.

Let
\rho_{12}
be an invertible density matrix on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and let
\sigma_{23}
be any density matrix on

\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
. Then

\rho_{1}^{-1}\otimes\sigma_{23}\leqslant\rho_{12}^{-1}\otimes\sigma_{3}\ .

(8)

As a direct corollary, one obtains (
2
), and hence strong subadditivity.
Since the logarithm is operator monotone,

\log\rho_{12}+\log\sigma_{23}-\log\rho_{1}-\log\sigma_{3}\leqslant 0\ .

(9)

Now let
\rho_{123}
be a density matrix on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
, and apply (
9
) with
\rho_{12}=\textnormal{Tr}_{3}[\rho_{123}]
and
\sigma_{23}=\textnormal{Tr}_{1}[\rho_{123}]=\rho_{23}
. Then taking the trace against
-\rho_{123}
yields (
2
). The condition that
\rho_{12}
be invertible is removed by a simple continuity argument
[
2
]
.

The elegant proof of (
9
) in
[
7
]
uses a “twisted” pair of Stinespring factorizations of a completely positive map to prove that

\rho_{12}^{1/2}\rho_{1}^{-1/2}(\sigma_{23}^{1/2}\sigma_{3}^{-1/2})^{*}=\rho_{12}^{1/2}\sigma_{3}^{-1/2}\rho_{1}^{-1/2}\sigma_{23}^{1/2}

is a contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
, and this is equivalent to the statement that

(\rho_{1}^{-1}\sigma_{23})^{1/2}\rho_{12}\sigma_{3}^{-1}(\rho_{1}^{-1}\sigma_{23})^{1/2}\leqslant\mathds{1}\ ,

which in turn is equivalent to (
8
). In this note we give an elementary direct proof of (
8
) using little more than the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Schmidt decompositions.

Remark 1.1
.

The inequality (
8
) extends to an inequality for positive operators
X
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
Y
on
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
since
we may normalize these to form
\rho_{12}=(\textnormal{Tr}[X])^{-1}X
and
\sigma_{23}:=(\textnormal{Tr}[Y])^{-1}Y
, and the normalization factors may be cancelled from both sides. The normalization is only relevant to the application.

Next, while there is no restriction on the finite dimensions of the Hilbert spaces
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
and
d_{3}
, we may freely assume that

d_{2}\geqslant\max\{d_{1},\textnormal{d}_{3}\}
. To see this, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
be a Hilbert space that contains
\mathcal{H}_{2}
as a proper subspace.
Let
P
be the projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{2}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
. If
\rho_{12}
is invertible on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
,
\rho_{12}(\epsilon):=\rho_{12}+\epsilon\mathds{1}_{1}\otimes P
is invertible on

\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
for all
\epsilon>0
. Of course, any density matrix
\sigma_{23}
on
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
may be regarded as a density matrix on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
. Then the restriction of
\rho_{12}^{-1}(\epsilon)\otimes\sigma_{3}\geqslant\rho_{1}^{-1}(\epsilon)\otimes\sigma_{23}

to
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
reduces to (
8
) as
\epsilon
decreases to zero.

2
Proof of the inequality of Lin, Kim and Hsieh

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Psi
and
\Phi
be unit vectors in
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
respectively, and let
\rho_{12}=|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|
and
\sigma_{23}=|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|
. Suppose that
\sigma_{3}
is invertible.
Let
P
denote the projection into the orthogonal complement of
\Phi
in
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
. For
\epsilon>0
, define

X_{23}(\epsilon)=|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|+\epsilon P\ .

(10)

Then for all
\epsilon
sufficiently small,

\rho_{12}\otimes\sigma_{3}^{-1}\leqslant\left(1+\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)\rho_{1}\otimes X_{23}(\epsilon)^{-1}\ .

(11)

Remark 2.1
.

Since
\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma_{3}
have the same rank,
\sigma_{3}
can only be invertible if
d_{2}\geqslant d_{3}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Remark
1.1
, we may assume
d_{2}\geqslant d_{3}
.
Since both sides of (
11
) are linear in
\rho_{12}
, and since any density matrix is a convex combination of pure states, (
11
) is valid for all density matrices
\rho_{12}
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Then by the monotonicity of the inverse function, if
\rho_{12}
is invertible,

\rho_{12}^{-1}\otimes\sigma_{3}\geqslant\left(1+\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)^{-1}\rho_{1}^{-1}\otimes X_{23}(\epsilon)\ .

(12)

Now taking
\epsilon
to zero, we recover (
8
) for pure states
\sigma_{23}
such that
\sigma_{3}
is invertible. Such pure states are dense by (
3
) and (
4
), and again, since any density matrix on
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
is a convex combination of pure states, it follows that (
8
) is valid for all density matrices
\sigma_{23}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d_{2}d_{3}}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
in which
\Phi_{1}=\Phi
.
The general unit vector
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
has the form
\varphi=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{2}d_{3}}{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell}
where

\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\|{\bf w}_{\ell}\|^{2}=1
. Then with
B:=\rho_{1}\otimes X_{23}(\epsilon)^{-1}
,

\langle\varphi\ ,\ B\varphi\rangle=\langle{\bf w}_{1},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{1}\rangle+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{\ell=2}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{\ell}\rangle\ .

(13)

We must compare this with
\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle
, where
A:=\rho_{12}\otimes\sigma_{3}^{-1}
. Recall that for all
a,b,\delta>0
,

ab\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(\delta a^{2}+\delta^{-1}b^{2})
. Hence for
any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=1}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell},A{\bf w}_{\ell^{\prime}}\otimes\Phi_{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi,A{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi\rangle+2\sum_{\ell=2}^{d_{2}d_{3}}{\rm Re}\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi,A{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell}\rangle+\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=2}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell},A{\bf w}_{\ell^{\prime}}\otimes\Phi_{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(1+\delta d_{2}d_{3})\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi,A{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi\rangle+\left(\frac{1}{\delta}+d_{2}d_{3}\right)\sum_{\ell=2}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell},A{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell}\rangle\ .

Next, with
\mu
denoting the least eigenvalue of
\sigma_{3}
,
A\leqslant\mu^{-1}\rho_{12}\otimes\mathds{1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell},A{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mu^{-1}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell},\rho_{12}\otimes\mathds{1}{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mu^{-1}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell^{\prime}},\rho_{12}\otimes\mathds{1}{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle\leqslant\mu^{-1}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell},\textnormal{Tr}_{23}[\rho_{12}\otimes\mathds{1}]{\bf w}_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{3}\mu^{-1}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{\ell}\rangle\ ,

where final inequality comes from
\langle{\bf w}_{\ell},\textnormal{Tr}_{23}[\rho_{12}\otimes\mathds{1}]{\bf w}_{\ell}\rangle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell^{\prime}},\rho_{12}\otimes\mathds{1}{\bf w}_{\ell}\otimes\Phi_{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle
.
Combining this with (
14
) yields

\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle\leqslant(1+\delta d_{2}d_{3})\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi,A{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi\rangle+d_{3}\mu^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}+d_{2}d_{3}\right)\sum_{\ell=2}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{\ell}\rangle\ .

(15)

We now claim that

\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi,A{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi\rangle\leqslant\langle{\bf w}_{1},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{1}\rangle\ .

(16)

Accepting this, choose
\delta=\sqrt{\epsilon}/d_{2}d_{3}
and then (
15
) becomes

\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle\leqslant(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\langle{\bf w}_{1},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{1}\rangle+\frac{d_{2}d_{3}^{2}}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}+1\right)\sum_{\ell=2}^{d_{2}d_{3}}\langle{\bf w}_{\ell},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{\ell}\rangle\ .

(17)

Then from (
13
),

{\displaystyle\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle\leqslant(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\langle\varphi,B\varphi\rangle}
for all sufficiently small
\epsilon
.

To prove (
16
),
let
{\displaystyle\Psi=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}{\bf u}_{j}\otimes{\bf v}_{j}}
and
{\displaystyle\Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{3}}\mu_{k}^{1/2}{\bf x}_{k}\otimes{\bf y}_{k}}

be Schmidt decompositions of
\Psi
and
\Phi
.
Then
\rho_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\lambda_{j}|{\bf u}_{j}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{j}|
, and

{\displaystyle A=\sum_{j,j^{\prime},k}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{1/2}\mu_{k}^{-1}|{\bf u}_{j}\otimes{\bf v}_{j}\otimes{\bf y}_{k}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes{\bf v}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes{\bf y}_{k}|}
.
Writing out
\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi,A{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi\rangle
using the Schmidt decomposition for
\Phi
yields the sum

\sum_{i,i^{\prime}j,j^{\prime},k}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{1/2}\mu_{k}^{-1}\mu_{i}^{1/2}\mu_{i^{\prime}}^{1/2}\langle{\bf u}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes{\bf v}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes{\bf y}_{k},{\bf w}_{1}\otimes{\bf x}_{i}\otimes{\bf y}_{i}\rangle\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes{\bf x}_{i^{\prime}}\otimes{\bf y}_{i^{\prime}},{\bf u}_{j}\otimes{\bf v}_{j}\otimes{\bf y}_{k}\rangle\

in which
only the terms with
i=i^{\prime}=k
can be non-zero, and hence the sum reduces to

\sum_{j,j^{\prime},k}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{1/2}\langle{\bf w}_{1},{\bf u}_{j}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{j^{\prime}},{\bf w}_{1}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{j^{\prime}},{\bf x}_{k}\rangle\langle{\bf x}_{k},{\bf v}_{j}\rangle\ .

Define the
d_{1}\times d_{1}
matrix
M
with entries
M_{j^{\prime},j}:=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{3}}\langle{\bf v}_{j^{\prime}},{\bf x}_{k}\rangle\langle{\bf x}_{k},{\bf v}_{j}\rangle
and the vector
{\bf a}\in\mathbbm{C}^{d_{1}}
with entries
a_{j}:=\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\langle{\bf w}_{1},{\bf u}_{j}\rangle
. Then
0<M\leqslant\mathds{1}
(with equality on the right if
d_{3}=d_{2}
), and therefore,

\langle{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi,A{\bf w}_{1}\otimes\Phi\rangle=\langle{\bf a},M{\bf a}\rangle\leqslant\|{\bf a}\|^{2}=\langle{\bf w}_{1},\rho_{1}{\bf w}_{1}\rangle
.
∎

The original proof of strong subadditivity
[
6
]
showed that strong subadditivity, the joint convexity of the relative entropy, and the
concavity of the conditional entropy were all equivalent, and then used the convexity results from
[
5
]
to prove strong subadditivity. The proof of strong subadditivity using Theorem
1.1
sidesteps deep convexity issues altogether, and indeed,
using the equivalence results, provides simple proofs of the joint convexity of the relative entropy, and the
concavity of the conditional entropy. We thank Trung Nghia Nguyen for pointing out a flaw in an earlier proof of the main lemma.

We close by showing that there are no cases of equality for the operator inequality (
8
).

Theorem 2.2
.

If
{\rm dim}(\mathcal{H}_{2})>1
, there are no density matrices
\rho_{12}
and
\sigma_{23}
for which there is equality in (
8
).

Proof.

Suppose there is equality in (
8
). Discarding the kernel of
\sigma_{3}
from
\mathcal{H}_{3}
as needed, we may suppose

\sigma_{3}
is invertible. (Note that as operators on
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
,
{\rm ker}(\sigma_{3})\subseteq{\rm ker}(\sigma_{23})
). Then with equality in (
8
),
\sigma_{23}
is in fact invertible, and of course
\rho_{1}
is invertible whenever
\rho_{12}
is invertible.

Define
X=\sigma_{3}^{-1/2}\sigma_{23}\sigma_{3}^{-1/2}
and
Y=\rho_{1}^{-1/2}\rho_{12}\rho^{-1/2}
. Then as operators on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
,

X=Y^{-1}

(18)

Since
X
acts trivially on
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
Y
acts trivially on
\mathcal{H}_{3}
, there are
A,B\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
such that
X=\mathds{1}\otimes A\otimes\mathds{1}
and

Y=\mathds{1}\otimes B\otimes\mathds{1}
.
From the definitions of
X
and
Y
,
\textnormal{Tr}_{2}[X]=\textnormal{Tr}_{2}[Y]=\mathds{1}
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
, and this means that
\textnormal{Tr}_{2}[A]=\textnormal{Tr}_{2}[B]=1
.
However then
\textnormal{Tr}_{2}[B^{-1}]\geqslant(\textnormal{Tr}_{2}[B])^{-1}
, and equality is only possible if
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is one dimensional.
Hence equality in (
8
) is not possible when
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is non-trivial.
∎

Thermodynamics of z = 4 z=4 Hořava-Lifshitz black holes
Mohammad Bagher Jahani Poshteh, Robert B. Mann

Abstract
Thermodynamics of z = 4 z=4 Hořava-Lifshitz black holes in 3+1 dimensions is studied in extended phase space. By using the scaling argument we find the Smarr relation and the first law for the black hole solutions of z = 4 z=4 Hořava-Lifshitz gravity. We find that it is necessary to take into account the variation of dimensionful parameters of the theory in the first law. We find that the reverse isoperimetric inequality can be violated for spherical, flat, and hyperbolic horizons and in all such cases we have black holes for which the specific heat at constant pressure and volume are positive. This provides a counterexample to a recent conjecture stating that black holes violating the reverse isoperimetric inequality are thermodynamically unstable. We find for z = 4 z=4 Hořava-Lifshitz black holes with hyperbolic horizons that there are two critical points: one showing Van der Waals behavior, the other reverse Van der Waals behavior.

The investigations of black hole thermodynamics in extended phase space have revealed a deep resemblance between charged AdS black holes and Van der Waals fluid

IV.1
Reverse isoperimetric inequality

Here we consider the reverse isoperimetric inequality for
z=4
Hořava-Lifshitz black holes. The reverse isoperimetric inequality

\mathcal{R}=\bigg(\frac{(d-1)V}{\omega_{d-2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{d-2}}{A}\Big)^{\frac{1}{d-2}}\geq 1,

(46)

is a conjecture in
d
dimensions, whose implication is that for a given thermodynamic volume, the entropy is maximized for Schwarzschild-AdS black holes
Cvetic et al. 2011
. This interpretation will not hold for the Hořava-Lifshitz case since the entropy (
24
) is no longer proportional to the area of the black hole.

In
3+1
dimensions we obtain

\mathcal{R}=\frac{\sqrt[3]{3V}}{r_{+}}\geq 1,

(47)

where the horizon area
A=\omega_{k}r_{+}^{2}

Cai and Soh 1999
. Using eq. (
34
) this becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=

\displaystyle\sqrt[3]{\frac{32\pi\left(3k^{2}+12km^{2}r_{+}^{2}+64\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{4}\right)}{3\kappa^{2}\mu m^{2}r_{+}^{4}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sqrt[3]{1+\frac{3\left(5k^{2}+4km^{2}r_{+}^{2}-64\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{4}\right)\bigl\{3km^{2}+8\pi kP\left[1+\ln\left(\frac{16}{3}\pi Pr_{+}^{2}\right)\right]+16\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{2}\bigr\}}{32\pi P\left(k+2m^{2}r_{+}^{2}\right)\left(3k^{2}+12km^{2}r_{+}^{2}+64\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{4}\right)}},

(48)

We find that eq. (
47
) can be violated for any
k=1,\,0,\,-1
. For large black holes we find that

\lim_{r_{+}\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{R}_{k=\pm 1}=8\sqrt[3]{\frac{\pi^{2}P}{3\mu\kappa^{2}}}=\mathcal{R}_{k=0},

(49)

where the latter equality holds for all values of
r_{+}
. For large black holes with
k=\pm 1
and for all sized black holes with
k=0
, the reverse isoperimetric inequality is violated for

P<\bar{P}=\frac{3\mu\kappa^{2}}{512\pi^{2}}=\frac{\mu\kappa^{2}}{32\pi m^{2}}P_{max},

(50)

which is independent of
r_{+}
and
m
, where we have used (
25
). We see that for
\mu\kappa^{2}/m^{2}>32\pi
the upper bound

on the pressure is larger than

, and so the reverse isoperimetric inequality is always violated for the planar
k=0

r_{+}
limit for

IV.2
Thermodynamic instabilities

We have seen that for
z=4
Hořava-Lifshitz gravity, there are black holes that violate the reverse isoperimetric inequality (
46
). Such violations have been noted before (
47
)
Hennigar et al. 2015b
, with the associated black holes being called super-entropic, since their entropy exceeds the maximum implied from the relation (
46
) (see also
Boudet et al. 2020
). However for the black holes we are considering entropy is not proportional to area; we shall therefore refer to black holes violating (
46
) as
RII-violating black holes.

It was recently conjectured
Johnson 2020
that for super-entropic black holes the specific heat at constant volume,
C_{V}
, is negative. This conjecture was subsequently followed up with a counterexample
Cong and Mann 2019
, suggesting a broader version of the conjecture:
black holes violating the reverse isoperimetric inequality (
47
) are thermodynamically unstable. In specific terms, for such black holes, the specific heat at constant pressure,
C_{P}
is negative whenever
C_{V}>0
.

We consider this conjecture for the
z=4
Hořava-Lifshitz RII-violating black holes. The specific heat at constant pressure and volume are

\displaystyle C_{P}=T\left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}\right|_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi\left(k+2m^{2}r_{+}^{2}\right)^{2}\left(-5k^{2}-4km^{2}r_{+}^{2}+64\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{4}\right)\left(3k^{2}+12km^{2}r_{+}^{2}+64\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{4}\right)}{\left(3\mu\kappa^{2}m^{4}r_{+}^{4}\right)\left[5k^{3}+26k^{2}m^{2}r_{+}^{2}+8km^{2}r_{+}^{4}\left(m^{2}+24\pi P\right)+128\pi m^{4}Pr_{+}^{6}\right]},

(51)

\displaystyle C_{V}=T\left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}\right|_{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\left(\left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}\right|_{P}+\left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial P}\right|_{T}\left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right|_{V}\right)=\frac{4\pi\left(-5k^{2}-4km^{2}r_{+}^{2}+64\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{4}\right)}{3\mu\kappa^{2}m^{4}r_{+}^{4}}

(52)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left[\frac{\left(k+2m^{2}r_{+}^{2}\right)^{2}\left(3k^{2}+12km^{2}r_{+}^{2}+64\pi m^{2}Pr_{+}^{4}\right)}{\left(5k^{3}+26k^{2}m^{2}r_{+}^{2}+8km^{2}r_{+}^{4}\left(m^{2}+24\pi P\right)+128\pi m^{4}Pr_{+}^{6}\right)}-\frac{I}{J}\right],

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 12m^{2}r_{+}^{4}\left\{k^{4}\left(15m^{2}-8\pi P\right)+6k^{3}m^{2}r_{+}^{2}\left(13m^{2}-16\pi P\right)+8k^{2}m^{2}r_{+}^{4}\left(3m^{4}+12\pi m^{2}P+64\pi^{2}P^{2}\right)+8\pi kP\right.

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{16}{3}\pi Pr_{+}^{2}\right)\left[5k^{3}+26k^{2}m^{2}r_{+}^{2}+8km^{2}r_{+}^{4}\left(m^{2}+24\pi P\right)+128\pi m^{4}Pr_{+}^{6}\right]-\left.256\pi km^{6}Pr_{+}^{6}-2048\pi^{2}m^{6}P^{2}r_{+}^{8}\right\}^{2},

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 75k^{6}\left(3m^{2}-8\pi P\right)+450k^{5}m^{2}r_{+}^{2}\left(3m^{2}-8\pi P\right)+16k^{4}m^{2}r_{+}^{4}\left(81m^{4}+144\pi m^{2}P-1024\pi^{2}P^{2}\right)+96k^{3}m^{4}r_{+}^{6}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(m^{2}-8\pi P\right)\left(3m^{2}-8\pi P\right)-49152\pi^{2}k^{2}m^{4}P^{2}r_{+}^{8}\left(m^{2}-2\pi P\right)+8192\pi^{2}km^{6}P^{2}r_{+}^{10}\left(5m^{2}-8\pi P\right)+262144\pi^{3}m^{8}P^{3}r_{+}^{12}.

V
Critical behavior

In this section we investigate the critical behavior of
z=4
Hořava-Lifshitz black holes. To find the equation of state we use eq. (
23
) to obtain

P=\frac{T}{v}+\frac{k}{4\pi v^{2}}+\frac{2kT}{m^{2}v^{3}}+\frac{5k^{2}}{4\pi m^{2}v^{4}},

(54)

where we take
v=2r_{+}
as the specific volume so that the above relation is similar to the equation of state of the Van der Waals system.

Contrary to typical behaviour in Einstein gravity
Kubiznak and Mann 2012
;
Kubiznak et al. 2017
, the
k=1
case has no interesting phase behaviour or critical behaviour.
Critical behavior, if any, takes place at an inflection point of the equation of state (
54
), namely

\frac{\partial P}{\partial v}=0,\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial v^{2}}=0\;.

(55)

No real positive solutions to both of the above equations exist for
k=1,\,0
, and so there is no critical behavior for black holes with spherical or planar horizons.

However for hyperbolic horizons with
k=-1
we find two positive solutions

\displaystyle v_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{m^{2}}\left(6+\sqrt{21}\right)},

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{m^{2}}\left(6-\sqrt{21}\right)},

(56)

to (
55
).
Hence, in contrast to AdS black holes in general relativity
Kubiznak et al. 2017
, critical behavior in
z=4
Hořava-Lifshitz black holes takes place only for

VI
Concluding remarks

We have shown that
z=4
Hořava-Lifshitz black holes have a rich thermodynamic structure, with a number of surprising features. They exhibit both Van der Waals and reverse Van der Waals behaviour, but with notably distinct stability features. The Van der Waals behaviour is characterized by an inverted swallowtail structure in the free energy, where the first-order phase transition is between stable/unstable large and unstable small black holes with
C_{P}<0
, and an isolated branch of stable intermediate-sized black holes with
C_{P}>0
. The reverse Van der Waals behaviour consists of a first-order phase transition is between a large stable black holes with
C_{P}>0
and a small unstable black holes with
C_{P}<0
; the intermediate-sized black holes also have
C_{P}<0
.

We also find that these black holes provide counterexamples to stability conjectures posited earlier
Johnson 2020
;
Cong and Mann 2019
that were based on the reverse isoperimetric inequality. It would be interesting to see if some generalization of these conjectures could be formulated (and proved) that would include the broader class of Hořava-Lifshitz black holes.

On Gaussian interpolation inequalities
\firstname Giovanni \lastname Brigati \CDRorcid 0000-0002-3698-9995, \firstname Jean \lastname Dolbeault \CDRorcid 0000-0003-4234-2298, \firstname Nikita \lastname Simonov \CDRorcid 0000-0002-3241-190X

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to Gaussian interpolation inequalities with endpoint cases corresponding to the Gaussian Poincaré and the logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, seen as limits in large dimensions of Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities on spheres. Entropy methods are investigated using not only heat flow techniques but also nonlinear diffusion equations as on spheres. A new stability result is established for the Gaussian measure, which is directly inspired by recent results for spheres.

Cet article est consacré à des inégalités d’interpolation Gaussiennes, avec comme cas extrêmes l’inégalité de Poincaré Gaussienne et l’inégalité de Sobolev logarithmique, vues comme limites en grandes dimensions des inégalités de Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev sur les sphères. Les méthodes d’entropie sont abordées en utilisant non seulement des techniques basée sur l’équation de la chaleur mais aussi sur des équations de diffusion non-linéaires, comme pour les sphères. Un nouveau résultat de stabilité est établi pour les mesures Gaussiennes, qui s’inspire directement de résultats récents sur les sphères.

Let
d\gamma(y):=(2\,\pi)^{-n/2}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\,|y|^{2}}\,dy
be the centred normalized Gaussian probability measure. We recall that
|{\mathbb{S}}^{k-1}|=2\,\pi^{k/2}/\Gamma(k/2)
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
and

\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{a-1}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,r^{2}\right)^{-b}\,dr=d^{\frac{a}{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)\,\Gamma\left(b-\frac{a}{2}\right)}{2\,\Gamma(b)}

if
0<a<2\,b
. Applying these formulas with
a=k=d-n
and
b=d-2>2-n
, we find that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-n}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|\zeta|^{2}\right)^{2-d}\,d\zeta=(d\,\pi)^{\frac{d-n}{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+n}{2}-2\right)}{\Gamma(d-2)}\,.

Applying these formulas with
a=b=k=d\geq 2
, we find that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|x|^{2}\right)^{-d}}\,dx=c_{d}

and, as a consequence

\lim_{d\to+\infty}\frac{1}{c_{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-n}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|\zeta|^{2}\right)^{2-d}\,d\zeta=\frac{4}{(2\,\pi)^{n/2}}

using Stirling’s formula. Since

\lim_{d\to+\infty}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|y|^{2}\right)^{2-\frac{d+n}{2}}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\,|y|^{2}}\,,

we obtain

\lim_{d\to+\infty}\frac{1}{c_{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|\nabla g_{d}(y)|^{2}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|x|^{2}\right)^{2-d}}\,dx=4\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\gamma\,.

Similar computations show that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|g_{d}(y)|^{2}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|x|^{2}\right)^{-d}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|g_{d}(y)|^{2}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|y|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d+n}{2}}}\,dy\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-n}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|\zeta|^{2}\right)^{-d}\,d\zeta\,,\\
\lim_{d\to+\infty}\frac{1}{c_{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|g_{d}(y)|^{2}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|x|^{2}\right)^{-d}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|v|^{2}}\,d\gamma\,,

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|g_{d}(y)|^{p}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|x|^{2}\right)^{-d}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|g_{d}(y)|^{p}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|y|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d+n}{2}}}\,dy\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-n}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|\zeta|^{2}\right)^{-d}\,d\zeta\,,\\
\lim_{d\to+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{d,p}\,d^{d\frac{p-2}{2\,p}}}{4\,d\,c_{d}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|g_{d}(y)|^{p}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{d}\,|x|^{2}\right)^{-d}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|v|^{p}}\,d\gamma\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\,.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

3.
Entropy methods and nonlinear flows for Gaussian measures

In this section, we prove the result of Theorem
1.3
for the Gaussian measure
d\gamma
and extend it to the slightly more general framework of a uniformly strictly log-concave measure
d\mu
, before drawing some consequences. Most of the results are similar to computations usually done on the sphere, but we are not aware of the use of nonlinear flows (
m\neq 1
) in the context of Gaussian measures. This approach is very natural in the perspective of spheres in the large-dimensional limit.

3.1.
Gaussian interpolation inequalities : a proof by the
carré du champ
method

On
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let us consider the probability measure

d\mu=Z^{-1}\,e^{-\phi}\,dy\quad\mbox{with}\quad Z=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{e^{-\phi}}\,dy

(10)

and redefine the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator

by

\mathcal{L}:=\Delta-\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla

(11)

on
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)
. This generalizes the case of the harmonic potential
\phi(y)=\frac{1}{2}\,|y|^{2}
considered in the introduction. We assume that
\phi
satisfies the
Bakry-Emery condition

\mathrm{Hess}\,\phi\geq\lambda_{\star}\,\mathrm{Id}\quad\mbox{a.e.}

(12)

for some
\lambda_{\star}>0
. The harmonic potential corresponds to the equality case with
\lambda_{\star}=1
. Under Assumption (
12
), it is well known (see for instance
[
7
, Section 7.6.2]
) that, with
\lambda=\lambda_{\star}
and for any
p\in[1,2)
,

\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2-p}\left(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\right)\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)

(13)

and also, by taking the limit as
p\to 2_{-}
, that

\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|f|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|f|^{2}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)\,.

The classical proof by the carré du champ, as in
[
5
,
4
,
3
]
, relies on the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck flow

\partial_{t}\rho=\mathcal{L}\rho
applied to the solution with initial datum
\rho(t=0,\cdot)=|f|^{p}
. Here we consider a more general strategy and compute as in the carré du champ method using the
nonlinear diffusion flow

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{m}\,\mathcal{L}\rho^{m}\quad\forall\,t\geq 0\,,\quad\rho(t=0,\cdot)=|f|^{p}\,.

(14)

There is no difficulty in proving global existence of a positive bounded solution. The case of a non-negative solution is also covered by a standard approximation of the initial datum. In the case of the harmonic potential, the key of the existence proof is to combine an
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-contraction property and the Maximum Principle as in the classical theory of nonlinear diffusions : see for instance
[
42
, Chapter 9]
for a general reference on this topic. In the case of a general potential
\phi
, for existence and uniqueness we refer to
[
19
, Section 3]
and to
[
33
]
for a general overview of the topic. Concerning the
t\to+\infty
limit, it is enough to consider initial data bounded from above and from below by two positive constants : this property is preserved along the flow and the nonlinear diffusion term can then be estimated using linear operators, so that the solution converges to a unique constant determined by mass conservation. The proofs can be adapted from
[
17
,
19
]
and from the results on the sphere on the other hand : see
[
18
]
and references therein. As they present no essential difficulty, they will be omitted. Our goal here is to understand the range of
m
for which we have a monotonicity property of the deficit as in the case
m=1
. Let
m_{\pm}(p)
be defined as in (
5
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
n\geq 1
,
p\in[1,2)
and
m\in[m_{-}(p),m_{+}(p)]
. We consider the measure
d\mu
as in (
10
) such that (
12
) holds for some
\lambda_{\star}>0
. If
\rho>0
solves (
14
) with
\mathcal{L}
defined by (
11
) for an initial datum
\rho(t=0,\cdot)=|f|^{p}
in
\mathrm{L}^{2/p}\cap\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)
, then

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla\rho^{1/p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{2-p}\left(\|\rho^{1/p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2/p}\right)\right)\leq 0\quad\forall\,t>0\,.

The result of Theorem
3.1
is new for
m\neq 1
. Theorem
1.3
corresponds to the special case of the harmonic potential
\phi(y)=\frac{1}{2}\,|y|^{2}
with

in Theorem
3.1
. The range of the admissible parameters
(m,p)
is the same in Theorems
1.3
and
3.1
, and shown in Fig.

\rho(t,\cdot)
converges to a constant as

so that the limit of the deficit is

provides us with a proof of (

Proof 3.2

(Proof of Theorem
3.1
)
.

In order to do computations, a very convenient reformulation is obtained with the substitution

w^{\beta\,p}=\rho\,.

Then,
w
is a solution of the PDE

\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}=w^{2-2\beta}\left(\mathcal{L}w+\kappa\,\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{w}\right)

(15)

for any
t\geq 0
, with initial datum
w(t=0,\cdot)=|f|^{1/\beta}
, where

\kappa:=\beta\,(p-2)+1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta:=\frac{2}{2-p\,(1-m)}\,.

(16)

A first computation shows that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{w^{\beta p}}\,d\mu=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|f|^{p}}\,d\mu
is independent of
t
because

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{w^{\beta p}}\,d\mu=\beta\,p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{w^{\kappa}\left(\mathcal{L}w+\kappa\,\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{w}\right)}\,d\mu=0\,.

A second useful computation goes as follows :

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\,\beta^{2}}\,

\displaystyle\,\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\left(\big|\nabla w^{\beta}\big|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{p-2}\,w^{2\beta}\right)}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\left(\mathcal{L}w+(\beta-1)\,\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{w}-\frac{\lambda_{\star}\,w}{\beta\,(p-2)}\right)\left(\mathcal{L}w+\kappa\,\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{w}\right)}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{(\mathcal{L}w)^{2}}\,d\mu+(\kappa+\beta-1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{(\mathcal{L}w)\,\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{w}}\,d\mu+\kappa\,(\beta-1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\frac{|\nabla w|^{4}}{w^{2}}}\,d\mu-\lambda_{\star}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}\,d\mu\,.

The purely technical purpose of introducing the exponent
\beta
is to make the last line of the above computation
2
-homogeneous in
w
, which makes the discussion easier to read. Inserting the two following estimates,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{(\mathcal{L}w)^{2}}\,d\mu=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\nabla w\cdot\nabla(\mathcal{L}w)}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\nabla w\cdot(\mathcal{L}\nabla w)}\,d\mu+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\nabla w\cdot[\mathcal{L},\nabla]\,w}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\|\mathrm{Hess}w\|^{2}}\,d\mu+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\nabla w\cdot[\mathcal{L},\nabla]\,w}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\|\mathrm{Hess}w\|^{2}}\,d\mu+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\mathrm{Hess}\,\phi:\nabla w\otimes\nabla w}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\|\mathrm{Hess}w\|^{2}}\,d\mu+\lambda_{\star}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}\,d\mu

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{(\mathcal{L}w)\,\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{w}}\,d\mu=-\,2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\mathrm{Hess}w:\frac{\nabla w\otimes\nabla w}{w}}\,d\mu+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\frac{|\nabla w|^{4}}{w^{2}}}\,d\mu\,,

we obtain that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\left(\big|\nabla w^{\beta}\big|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{p-2}\,w^{2\beta}\right)}\,d\mu\leq 0

if, for any function
w
, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}[w]:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\|\mathrm{Hess}w\|^{2}}\,d\mu-2\,(\kappa+\beta-1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\mathrm{Hess}w:\frac{\nabla w\otimes\nabla w}{w}}\,d\mu\\
+\big(\kappa\,(\beta-1)+\kappa+\beta-1\big)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\frac{|\nabla w|^{4}}{w^{2}}}\,d\mu\geq 0\,.

A sufficient condition is obtained if the reduced discriminant is negative, that is, if

(\kappa+\beta-1)^{2}-\big(\kappa\,(\beta-1)+\kappa+\beta-1\big)\leq 0\,.

Altogether, this gives the condition

\beta_{-}(p)\leq\beta\leq\beta_{+}(p)\quad\mbox{with}\quad\beta_{\pm}(p):=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{(p-1)\,(2-p)}}{1-(p-1)\,(2-p)}\,.

(17)

See Fig.
3
. Equivalently, written in terms of
m

m_{-}(p)\leq m\leq m_{+}(p)
with
m_{\pm}(p)
defined by (
5

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{p-2}\left(\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\right)\right)=-\,2\,\beta^{2}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}[w]\leq 0

(18)

because
\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}[w]
is non-negative under Condition (
17

To make these computations rigorous, one has to justify all integrations by parts. This is by now rather standard and can be done using the following scheme.

(1)

Let

be large enough and consider
\Omega_{h}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,:\,\phi(x)<h\right\}
. We can consider the evolution equation restricted to
\Omega_{h}
, with no flux boundary conditions. Then apply the
carré du champ
method and keep track of the boundary terms. Since
\Omega_{h}
is a bounded convex domain, these terms have a sign due to Grisvard’s lemma : see for instance
[

[

[

Extend Inequality (

3.2.
Improved inequalities based on the
carré du champ
method

In the proof of Theorem
3.1
, using only
\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}[w]\geq 0
is a crude estimate. Let us explain how one can obtain improved estimates by making a better use of
\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}[w]\geq 0
. Under Condition (
17
), we can indeed rewrite
\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}[w]
as an integral of a sum of squares,

\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}[w]=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\left\|\mathrm{Hess}w-(\kappa+\beta-1)\,\frac{\nabla w\otimes\nabla w}{w}\right\|^{2}}\,d\mu+\delta\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\frac{|\nabla w|^{4}}{w^{2}}}\,d\mu

(19)

\delta:=\kappa\,(\beta-1)+\kappa+\beta-1-(\kappa+\beta-1)^{2}=\big(\beta-\beta_{-}(p)\big)\,\big(\beta_{+}(p)-\beta\big)>0\,.

(20)

As in
[
2

, let us consider the special case
m=\beta=1
of the linear flow. Let us define the
entropy
and the

by

\mathcal{E}[w]:=\frac{1}{2-p}\left(\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{I}[w]:=\|\nabla w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\,.

Inequality (
13
) amounts simply to

\mathcal{I}[w]-\lambda_{\star}\,\mathcal{E}[w]\geq 0\,.

We can now state a first
improved entropy–entropy production inequality

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
n
be any positive integer. We consider the measure
d\mu
as in (
10
) such that (
12
) holds for some
\lambda_{\star}>0
. For any

\varphi(s):=1+s-(1+s)^{p-1}

\mathcal{I}[f]\geq\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{(2-p)^{2}}\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\,\varphi\left(\frac{(2-p)\,\mathcal{E}[f]}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}}\right)\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)\,.

(21)

Since

, the function

is convex with
\varphi(0)=0

\varphi^{\prime}(0)=2-p

\varphi(s)\geq(2-p)\,s

, so that Inequality (
) is stronger than (

) amounts to

\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{(2-p)^{2}}\left(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2\,(p-1)}\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2\,(2-p)}\right)

and can also be rewritten as

\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{2-p}\left(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}\right)\\
\geq\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{(2-p)^{2}}\left((p-1)\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}+(2-p)\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2}-\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2\,(p-1)}\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\mu)}^{2\,(2-p)}\right)\,.

(22)

We claim no originality in either Proposition

Proof 3.5

(Sketch of a proof)
.

With
\beta=1

. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\big(\mathcal{I}[w]\big)^{2}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{2}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\frac{|\nabla w|^{4}}{w^{2}}}\,d\mu

and, with

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mu=(2-p)\,\mathcal{E}[w]+M\,.

Altogether, with
\mathsf{e}(t):=(2-p)\,M^{-1}\,\mathcal{E}[w(t,\cdot)]
, if

solves (

Proof 3.9

\geq\frac{\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}}{2-p}\,\frac{1}{2}\,\psi\left((2-p)\,\theta\right)\left((2-p)\,\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}}\right)^{2}\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}\,(2-p)\,\psi\left((2-p)\,\theta\right)\,\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{4}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}}\geq\kappa_{\star}(\theta)\,\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}

with
\kappa_{\star}(\theta):=\frac{1}{2}\,(2-p)\,\psi\left((2-p)\,\theta\right)\,\theta
because
t\mapsto\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)
is non-increasing,
\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\geq\theta
and
\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}
.

② From now on we work in a neighbourhood of the constants which, by homogeneity of the inequalities, is defined as

\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\leq\theta\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\,.

With
\theta>0
small, we claim that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{f}\,d\gamma
is close to
1
if
\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}=1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. For any non-negative function

f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)\quad\mbox{such that}\quad\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\leq\theta\,,

(30)

we have the estimate

\sqrt{1-\theta}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{f}\,d\gamma\leq 1\,.

Proof 4.4
.

With
\bar{f}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{f}\,d\gamma
, the result follows from the Gaussian Poincaré inequality according to

1=\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|f-\bar{f}|^{2}}\,d\gamma+\bar{f}^{2}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}+\bar{f}^{2}\leq\theta+\bar{f}^{2}\,.

③ Assume that
f
is as in (
30
) and let us decompose
u_{f}(x):=f(x)/\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{f}\,d\gamma
as

u_{f}(x)=1+\varepsilon\,x\cdot\nu+\eta\,r(x)

where
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is such that
\varepsilon\,\nu=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{x\,u_{f}(x)}\,d\gamma
with
\varepsilon>0
,
\eta
is a positive number and
r
is a function in
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)\cap(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)
such that
\|\nabla r\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}=1
and
\|r\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}\leq 1/2
by the Gaussian Poincaré inequality after taking into account the additional orthogonality condition
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{r\,x_{i}}\,d\gamma=0
for any
i=1
,
2
,…,
n
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Let
f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)
be such that (

) holds. With the above notation, we have

\|u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}=1+\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}\,\|r\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\leq 1+\theta\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\nabla u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}\leq\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}

and, if
\,\eta>t\,\varepsilon^{2}
for some

\|\nabla u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2-p}\left(\|u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\|u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\,(2-p)\left(\eta^{2}+\frac{t^{2}\,\varepsilon^{4}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}}\right)\,.

By homogeneity, if (
30
) holds and
\eta>t\,\varepsilon^{2}
for some given

4.3
, we obtain

\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2-p}\left(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\right)\\
\geq\frac{1}{4}\,(2-p)\,(1-\theta)\left(\|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\,f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}+\frac{t^{2}\,\|\nabla(\Pi_{1}\,f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{4}}{\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}+\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}}\right)\,.

This case already covers the result of Theorem

with

c_{n,p}\leq c_{n,p}^{(1)}:=\frac{1}{4}\,(2-p)\,(1-\theta)\min\big\{t,1\big\}\,.

(31)

Proof 4.6
.

The result follows from Proposition
3.14
and from the chain of elementary inequalities

\eta^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta^{2}+t^{2}\,\varepsilon^{4}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta^{2}+\frac{t^{2}\,\varepsilon^{4}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}}\right)\,.

④ The next part of the proof relies on a Taylor expansion of
\|u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}
. With no loss of generality, by rotational invariance, we can assume that
\nu=(1,0,\ldots,0)
so that with Cartesian coordinates

, we write
u_{f}(x)=1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}+\eta\,r(x)
. The following result is at the core of our strategy. It heavily relies on the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality and new estimates for the remainder terms based on the boundedness of

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
n\geq 1
and

be a non-negative function such that (

\|u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\geq 1+(p-1)\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}\|r\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\right)\\
+(p-1)\,(2-p)\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\varepsilon^{4}+\varepsilon^{2}\,\eta\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{x_{1}^{2}\,r(x)}\,d\gamma\right)-\frac{\mathcal{C}\,\varepsilon^{4}}{\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)}\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0_{+}\,.

Proof 4.8
.

This proof is elementary although a little bit lengthy. Several steps are similar to those of
[
18

and will be only sketched. Let us split it into three steps.

Step 1.
Let us start with a list of preliminary remarks.

\bullet
Let
B_{\varepsilon}
be the centred ball of radius

, that is,

B_{\varepsilon}:=\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,:\,2\,\varepsilon\,|x|<1\big\}

and let
B_{\varepsilon}^{c}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
. We observe that

\gamma(B_{\varepsilon}^{c})=\big|{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\big|\int_{1/(2\,\varepsilon)}^{+\infty}r^{n-1}\,e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\,dr=c_{n}\,\varepsilon^{2-n}\,e^{-\frac{1}{8\,\varepsilon^{2}}}\left(1+O\left(\varepsilon^{2}\right)\right)\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0_{+}

with
c_{n}=2^{3\,(2-n)/2}/\Gamma(n/2)
. Let
\xi_{p}:=\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)}\varepsilon^{-5}\big(\gamma(B_{\varepsilon}^{c})\big)^{(2-p)/2}
. Hence we have

\int_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}|g|^{p}\,d\gamma\leq\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{p}\,\big(\gamma(B_{\varepsilon}^{c})\big)^{(2-p)/2}\leq\xi_{p}\,\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{p}\,\varepsilon^{5}

for any

, by Hölder’s inequality and, as a consequence,

\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}|g|^{p}\,d\gamma\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|g|^{p}}\,d\gamma\leq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}|g|^{p}\,d\gamma+\xi_{p}\,\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{p}\,\varepsilon^{5}\quad\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\,.

(32)

From now on, we assume without further notice that
x\in B_{\varepsilon}
unless it is specified.

\bullet
An expansion in Taylor series of
(1+s)^{p}-1-p\,s

shows that all terms are non-negative :

(1+s)^{p}\geq 1+p\,s+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,s^{2}\quad\forall\,s\in(-1,0]\,.

Applied to

|u_{f}|^{p}=\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}+\eta\,r\right)^{p}=\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p}\left(1+\frac{\eta\,r}{1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}}\right)^{p}\\
\geq\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p}\left(1+p\,\frac{\eta\,r}{1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\left(\frac{\eta\,r}{1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}}\right)^{2}\right)\\
=\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p}+p\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-1}\,\eta\,r+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-2}\,\eta^{2}\,r^{2}\,.

\bullet
Let us consider the case
1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}>0
and

. The function

\rho(s):=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left((1+s)^{p}-1-p\,s-\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,s^{2}\right)\quad\,\forall\,s\geq 0

is bounded. Let us extend
\rho
by
0
on
(-1,0)
and define

\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}(x):=\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-2}\,\rho\left(\frac{\eta\,r(x)}{1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}}\right)\,.

With this definition, using
u_{f}\geq 0
by hypothesis, we obtain

\displaystyle|u_{f}|^{p}\,

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}+\eta\,r\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p}+p\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-1}\,\eta\,r+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-2}\,\eta^{2}\,r^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\eta^{2}\,r(x)^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}(x)

with equality whenever
r\geq 0
.

As
\eta\to 0_{+}
,
\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}
converges a.e. to
0
on
B_{\varepsilon}
uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
. The dominated convergence theorem is enough to conclude that

\lim_{\eta\to 0_{+}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(x)^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}(x)\,d\gamma=0

for a given function
r
, but this is not enough to conclude uniformly with respect to
r
. To do this, we need more detailed estimates. Notice however that

\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon})}\leq M:=2^{2-p}\,\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})}

where
M
is independent of
\varepsilon
,
\eta
and
r
.

\bullet
Since
\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}x_{1}^{2\,k}\,d\gamma\geq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}x_{1}^{2\,k+1}\,d\gamma=0
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{x_{1}^{2\,k}}\,d\gamma\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{x_{1}^{2\,k+1}}\,d\gamma=0
for any

, an expansion in Taylor series of

gives

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p}}\,d\gamma\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\varepsilon^{2}\,x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{24}\,p\,(p-1)\,(p-2)\,(p-3)\,x_{1}^{4}\,\varepsilon^{4}\right)}\,d\gamma\\
=1+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\,p\,(p-1)\,(p-2)\,(p-3)\,\varepsilon^{4}\,.

By applying (
32
) with

, we obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}|^{p}}\,d\gamma\leq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}|1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}|^{p}\,d\gamma+\xi_{p}\left(1+\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{p/2}\,\varepsilon^{5}\,.

Summing up with
\varepsilon^{2}\leq\theta
, we have

\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}|1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}|^{p}\,d\gamma\geq 1+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\,p\,(p-1)\,(p-2)\,(p-3)\,\varepsilon^{4}-\xi_{p}\,(1+\theta)^{p/2}\,\varepsilon^{5}\,.

\bullet
Let us estimate

We obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|u_{f}|^{p}}\,d\gamma\geq 1+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\,p\,(p-1)\,(p-2)\,(p-3)\,\varepsilon^{4}-\xi_{p}\,(1+\theta)^{p/2}\,\varepsilon^{5}\\
+p\,\eta\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}{\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-1}\,r}\,d\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\eta^{2}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}{\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-2}\,r^{2}}\,d\gamma\\

Step 2.
We prove that

\bullet
By the logarithmic Sobolev inequality,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{h^{2}\,\log h^{2}}\,d\gamma\leq 2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|\nabla r|^{2}}\,d\gamma+\left(1+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{r^{2}}\,d\gamma\right)\,\log\left(1+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{r^{2}}\,d\gamma\right)\leq 2\,\log(2\,e)\,.

Let
\chi:=\mathbb{1}_{\{\eta\,h>s_{0}\}}

. Then we have

because

and, as a consequence,

\eta^{2}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}\setminus A_{\varepsilon,\eta,r,s_{0}}}r(x)^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}(x)\,d\gamma\geq-\,\frac{\log(2\,e)\,M}{\log s_{0}+\log\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)}\,\eta^{2}\,.

\bullet
Let us notice that

\mathcal{M}=\sup_{s>0}\frac{|\rho(s)|}{\log(1+s)}

\eta^{2}\int_{A_{\varepsilon,\eta,r,s_{0}}}r(x)^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}(x)\,d\gamma\geq-\,2^{2-p}\,\mathcal{M}\int_{A_{\varepsilon,\eta,r,s_{0}}}\eta^{2}\,r^{2}\,\log(1+2\,\eta\,r)\,\mathbb{1}_{\{r>0\}}\,d\gamma\,,

whenever

\displaystyle\log(1+2\,\eta\,r)\leq\log\left(1+2\,\sqrt{\eta}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad 0\leq r\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta}}\,,

\displaystyle\log(1+2\,\eta\,r)\leq\frac{\log(1+2\,s_{0})}{\log\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)}\,\log r^{2}\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta}}\leq r\leq\frac{s_{0}}{\eta}\,,

and conclude using

\eta^{2}\int_{A_{\varepsilon,\eta,r,s_{0}}}r(x)^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta,r}(x)\,d\gamma\geq-\,2^{2-p}\,\mathcal{M}\,\eta^{2}\left(\theta\,\log\left(1+2\,\sqrt{\eta}\right)+\frac{2\,\log(1+2\,s_{0})}{\log\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)}\right)\,.

Step 3.
We compute the contribution of

p\,\eta\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}{\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-1}\,r}\,d\gamma\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\eta^{2}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}{\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-2}\,r^{2}}\,d\gamma

\bullet
Using (

p\,\eta\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1})^{p-1}\,r\,d\gamma\geq p\,\eta\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1})^{p-1}\,r}\,d\gamma-2^{1-p}\,\xi_{p}\,\varepsilon^{5}\right)\\
\geq p\,\eta\,\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\,(p-1)\,(p-2)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{x_{1}^{2}\,r}\,d\gamma-\mathsf{c}_{1}\,\varepsilon^{3}-2^{1-p}\,\xi_{p}\,\varepsilon^{5}\right)

\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\eta^{2}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}{\left(1+\varepsilon\,x_{1}\right)^{p-2}\,r^{2}}\,d\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\,p\,(p-1)\,\eta^{2}\left(\|r\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\mathsf{c}_{2}\,\varepsilon\right)

\mathsf{c}_{2}:=\sup_{s\in(-1,0)\cup(0,+\infty)}\left|(1+s)^{p-2}-1\right|/s

Step 4.

Proof 4.9

(Proof of Theorem
1.4
)
.

The strategy of proof is similar to
[

\eta\leq t\,\varepsilon^{2}

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\|u_{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\geq 1+(p-1)\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}\left(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\right)\right)+(p-1)\,(2-p)\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\varepsilon^{4}+\alpha\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\eta\right)-\frac{\mathcal{C}\,\varepsilon^{4}}{\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)}\,,

where the estimate of

\lambda>0

The Distribution of polynomials in monotone independent elements
Marwa Banna, Pei-Lun Tseng

Abstract
. Building on the work of Arizmendi and Celestino (2021), we derive the ∗ * -distributions of polynomials in monotone independent and infinitesimally monotone independent elements. For non-zero complex numbers α \alpha and β \beta , we derive explicitly the ∗ * -distribution of p α , β = α ​ a ​ b + β ​ b ​ a p_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha ab+\beta ba whenever a a and b b are monotone or infinitesimally monotone independent elements. This encompasses both cases of the commutator and anti-commutator. This approach can be pushed to study more general polynomials. As applications, we derive the limiting distribution with respect to the partial trace of polynomials in a certain class of random matrices.

1.
Introduction

In non-commutative probability theory, diverse notions of independence arise through the examination of purely algebraic representations of fundamental concepts in classical probability theory. Five notions of independence were identified to satisfy natural axioms of probability: tensor (classical), free, Boolean, monotone, and its mirror image, anti-monotone. These notions of independence were classified by Speicher
[
31
]
and Muraki
[
23
,
24
]
and also by Ben Ghorbal and Schürmann
[
6
]
in the framework of category theory.

Following Voiculescu’s introduction of the concept of free independence, numerous extensions and generalizations of freeness have arisen within the non-commutative setting. Among these, one notable generalization is infinitesimal freeness, see
[
15
]
and
[
8
]
. This extension has found practical applications in investigating the occurrence of spikes in diverse deformed random matrix models, as shown in
[
3
,
30
,
9
]
. Consequently, extensions of infinitesimal Boolean and monotone independence have also been introduced in
[
27
,
17
]
.

Just like the classical counterpart, these notions of independence enable the computation of probability distributions for sums and products of independent random variables when we have knowledge of their individual distributions. This is achieved through the corresponding operations of additive and multiplicative convolutions. For more details, we refer to
[
36
,
35
]
for free convolutions,
[
32
,
16
]
for Boolean convolutions, and
[
22
,
7
]
for monotone convolutions. Similarly, analogous convolutions, known as infinitesimal additive and multiplicative convolutions, have been developed in
[
5
,
19
,
28
,
34
]
for the free case and
[
27
,
33
]
for the Boolean and monotone cases.

Beyond sums and products, there is a keen interest in determining distributions of general polynomials in independent variables. A natural starting point involves deriving the distributions of two particular polynomials: the commutator
i(x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{1})
, and the anti-commutator,
x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}x_{1}
, for independent variables
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. While the distribution of the commutator in the free setting was derived combinatorially using cumulants in prior works
[
25
]
, the distribution of the anti-commutator was only obtained recently in
[
14
,
26
]
. This is also the case for Boolean independent variables where the distributions of both the commutator and anti-commutator were determined recently in
[
21
]
. Furthermore, the infinitesimal free and Boolean distributions of commutators and anti-commutators were, under certain assumptions, derived recently in
[
21
]
.

On the other hand, distributions of commutators and anti-commutators for monotone independent variables and infinitesimally monotone independent remain unexplored. One of our primary objectives in this paper is to address this gap. As polynomials, we study closely linear spans in Section
3
for which we obtain explicit results. In fact, let
(\mathcal{C},\varphi)
be a
*
-probability space and
a
,
b\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{a}\prec\mathring{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
where
\mathcal{A}_{a}
is the
*
-algebra generated by
a
and
\mathring{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
is the
*
-algebra generated by
b
and
1
. We determine explicitly in Theorem

The framework of Theorem

Moreover, using the upper triangular technique developed in

In the work of Lenczewski
[

]
, random matrix models for asymptotic monotone independence with respect to the partial trace were given and were further discussed in
[

,
10
]
. On the other hand, Mingo and Tseng
[

Our approach builds upon the work of Arizmendi and Celestino
[
1
]
who study distributions of polynomials with cyclic monotone elements. The notion of cyclic monotone independence was introduced by Collins, Hasebe, and Sakuma
[
11
]
to describe the joint limiting spectral distribution of some pairs of matrices. They derived spectral formulas for certain polynomials of degree
2
and
3

[
1

(1,1)
-entry of matrix products, allowing for the derivation of the distribution by examining the corresponding product matrices. Building on this idea, we extend this approach to study polynomials in monotone independent elements. Moreover, this approach can be further pushed to investigate more general polynomials which lead to computing the precise distributions of a wide range of polynomials in random matrices as Proposition
5.4

Preliminaries

2.1.
Monotone Independence

A
non-commutative probability space
(or ncps for short) is a pair
(\mathcal{C},\varphi)
such that
\mathcal{C}
is a unital algebra and
\varphi
is a linear functional on
\mathcal{C}
that
\varphi(1)=1
. Moreover, a ncps
(\mathcal{C},\varphi)
is called a
*
-probability space
if we further assume
\mathcal{C}
is a
*
-algebra and
\varphi(a^{*}a)\geq 0
for all
a\in\mathcal{C}
.

For
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
, the
*
-distribution
of
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
is the set of all possible joints moments

\mu_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}=\{\varphi(a_{i_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}^{m_{n}})\mid n\geq 1,\ 1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\leq k\text{ and }m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\{1,*\}\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{C},\varphi)
is a
*
-probability space, and
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
*
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}
that are not necessarily unital. We say that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
monotone independent
and write
\mathcal{A}\prec\mathcal{B}
if

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(b_{0}a_{1}b_{1}\cdots a_{n}b_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})\varphi(b_{0})\varphi(b_{1})\cdots\varphi(b_{n}),

for all
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
,
b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1}\in\mathcal{B}
and
b_{0},b_{n}
are either
1
or elements in
\mathcal{B}
.

For two sets
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
of elements in
\mathcal{C}
, we say
(A_{1},A_{2})
is monotone independent and write
A_{1}\prec A_{2}
if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\prec\mathring{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
where
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is the
*
-algebra generated by
A_{1}
and
\mathring{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
is the
*
-algebra generated by
A_{2}
and
1
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that under the notion of monotone independence of pair algebras
(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})
, we usually don’t consider
\mathcal{A}_{1}
to be unital; otherwise, for all

we have

\varphi(a^{n})=\varphi(1\cdot a\cdot 1\cdot a\cdots 1\cdot a)=\varphi(a)^{n}\varphi(1\cdot 1\cdots 1\cdot 1)=\varphi(a)^{n},

which is not the case that we are interested in.

Let

\displaystyle\varphi(b_{0}a_{1}b_{1}\cdots a_{n}b_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})\varphi(b_{0})\varphi(b_{1})\cdots\varphi(b_{n}),

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(b_{0}a_{1}b_{1}\cdots a_{n}b_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})\varphi(b_{0})\varphi(b_{1})\cdots\varphi(b_{n})

(\mathcal{C},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})

-probability space and define

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\Big\{\begin{bmatrix}a&a^{\prime}\\

0&a\end{bmatrix}\Big|a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}\Big\}.

2.3.
Random Matrices for Monotone independence

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{C}_{n},\varphi_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of ncps and
(a_{n},b_{n})
be a pair of elements in
\mathcal{C}_{n}
for each
n
. We say that the sequence
(a_{n},b_{n})_{n}
is
asymptotically monotone independent
with respect to
\varphi_{n}
if there exist a ncps
(\mathcal{C},\varphi)
and elements
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
with
a\prec b
such that

(a_{n},b_{n})
converges to
(a,b)
in distribution; that is,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}(p(a_{n},b_{n}))=\varphi(p(a,b))\text{ for any polynomial }p

and
a\prec b
with respect to
\varphi
.

We present the random matrix model given by Lenczewski
[
18
]
where asymptotic monotone independence holds with respect to the partial trace. Let
A_{N}
be an
N\times N
random matrix and
N_{0}\leq N
be a fixed natural number.
A_{N}
can be represented as a composition of four block matrices in the following manner:

A_{N}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{(1,1)}&A^{(1,2)}\\
A^{(2,1)}&A^{(2,2)}\end{bmatrix},

where
A^{(1,1)}
corresponds to a square submatrix of size
N_{0}\times N_{0}
,
A^{(2,2)}
to a square submatrix of size
(N-N_{0})\times(N-N_{0})
,
A^{(1,2)}
to a submatrix of size
N_{0}\times(N-N_{0})
, and
A^{(2,1)}
to a submatrix of size
(N-N_{0})\times N_{0}
. The partial trace
\psi_{N}
of
A_{N}
is then defined by

(2.2)

\psi_{N}(A_{N}):=\frac{1}{N_{0}}(\mathbb{E}\circ Tr_{N_{0}})(A_{N}^{(1,1)})

where
Tr_{N_{0}}
is the non-normalized trace.
Here we note that the partial trace
\psi_{N}
depends on
N_{0}
. Lenczewski showed that if
A_{N}
and
B_{N}
are independent GUE matrices, then (
T_{A_{N}},B_{N})
is asymptotically monotone independent with respect to
\psi_{N}
where

T_{A_{N}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&A^{(1,2)}\\
A^{(2,1)}&0\end{bmatrix}.

In
[
21
]
, Mingo and Tseng considered the notion of infinitesimal freeness and showed a method for utilizing infinitesimal idempotents to construct monotone independent random matrix models. By this construction, they recover the random matrix models in
[

Let
(\mathcal{C},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
be an infinitesimal probability space, and let

be an

\varphi(j^{k})=0
for all

and

. Assume furthermore that
\varphi^{\prime}(j)\neq 0
and let

. Finally, define

\psi(a)=\frac{1}{\varphi^{\prime}(j)}\varphi^{\prime}(aj)\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{C},

and note that
\psi(a)=\varphi(a)
for all

It is clear that

is a linear functional on

with

is a ncps. Finally, set

and

, and define
\mathcal{J}_{a}(\mathcal{A})
to be the algebra generated by

\{j^{(\varepsilon_{1})}a_{1}j^{(\varepsilon_{2})}a_{2}\cdots j^{(\varepsilon_{k-1})}a_{k-1}j^{(\varepsilon_{k})}\mid k\geq 0,\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}\in\{\pm 1\}\text{ with }\varepsilon_{1}\neq\cdots\neq\varepsilon_{k},a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\}.

We refer to
[

We recall now real and complex Wigner matrices and consider certain assumptions on their moments. We will provide applications of our main results to polynomials in such matrices in Section

the

Distribution of Polynomials in Monotone Independent Elements

The Linear Span of

Let
(\mathcal{C},\varphi)
be a

-probability space. For any

\mathcal{C}
, we denote by

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{C},\varphi)
is a
*

\varphi(b_{1})=\tilde{b}_{1},\varphi(b_{2})=\tilde{b}_{2},\varphi(c_{1})=\tilde{c}_{1},\varphi(c_{2})=\tilde{c}_{2}
, and

\varphi(b_{i}b_{j})=\begin{cases}b_{1,1}&\text{ if }(i,j)=(1,1),\\
b_{2,2}&\text{ if }(i,j)=(2,2),\\
b_{1,2}&\text{ if }(i,j)=(1,2)\text{ or }(2,1).\end{cases}

We set

\alpha\mu_{a^{2}}\delta_{\vartheta+\zeta}+(1-\alpha)\mu_{a^{2}}\delta_{\vartheta-\zeta}

where

,

\alpha=\frac{1}{2\zeta}(\zeta-\vartheta+\tilde{c}_{1}\tilde{b}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{c}_{2}\tilde{b}_{1}^{2})
with

\vartheta=\frac{1}{2}(b_{2,2}\tilde{c}_{2}+b_{1,1}\tilde{c}_{1})\text{ and }\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(b_{2,2}\tilde{c}_{2}-b_{1,1}\tilde{c}_{1})^{2}+4b_{1,2}^{2}\tilde{c}_{2}\tilde{c}_{1}}.

Proof.

By Remark
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(p^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\Big([b_{1}\cdot a\varphi(c_{1})a\cdot b_{1}+b_{2}\cdot a\varphi(c_{2})a\cdot b_{2}]^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=1}^{2}\varphi(a_{j_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{k}})\varphi(b_{j_{1}})\varphi(b_{j_{1}}b_{j_{2}})\varphi(b_{j_{2}}b_{j_{3}})\cdots\varphi(b_{j_{k-1}}b_{j_{k}})\varphi(b_{j_{k}})

where
a_{1}=\varphi(c_{1})a^{2}
and
a_{2}=\varphi(c_{2})a^{2}
. Note that for all

\varphi(a_{j_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{k}})=\varphi(a^{2k})\varphi(c_{j_{1}})\varphi(c_{j_{2}})\cdots\varphi(c_{j_{k}}).

Thus, if for each

, we set

, and

\alpha_{k}^{(i)}=\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k-1}\in[2]}\varphi(b_{i}\cdot b_{j_{1}})\varphi(b_{j_{1}}b_{j_{2}})\cdots\varphi(b_{j_{k-2}}b_{j_{k-1}})\varphi(b_{j_{k-1}})\varphi(c_{j_{1}})\varphi(c_{j_{2}})\cdots\varphi(c_{j_{k-1}})\text{ for }k\geq 2

\displaystyle\varphi(p^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(a^{2k})\alpha_{k}\quad\text{ where }\quad\alpha_{k}:=\varphi(b_{1})\varphi(c_{1})\alpha_{k}^{(1)}+\varphi(b_{2})\varphi(c_{2})\alpha_{k}^{(2)}.

We observe that for each

\alpha_{l}^{(2)}=\varphi(b_{2}b_{1})\varphi(c_{1})\alpha_{l-1}^{(1)}+\varphi(b_{2}b_{2})\varphi(c_{2})\alpha_{l-1}^{(2)}=b_{1,2}\tilde{c}_{1}\alpha_{l-1}^{(1)}+b_{2,2}\tilde{c}_{2}\alpha_{l-1}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\alpha_{l}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{1,2}\tilde{c}_{1}\alpha_{l-1}^{(1)}+b_{1,2}\tilde{c}_{1}b_{2,2}\tilde{c}_{2}\alpha_{l-2}^{(1)}+b_{2,2}^{2}\tilde{c}_{2}^{2}\alpha_{l-2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{1,2}\tilde{c}_{1}\Big(\alpha_{l-1}^{(1)}+b_{2,2}\tilde{c}_{2}\alpha_{l-2}^{(1)}+b_{2,2}^{2}\tilde{c}_{2}^{2}\alpha_{l-3}^{(1)}\Big)+b_{2,2}^{3}\tilde{c}_{2}^{3}\alpha_{l-3}^{(2)}

\displaystyle b_{1,2}\tilde{c}_{1}\Big(\alpha_{l-1}^{(1)}+\sum_{j=1}^{l-3}b_{2,2}^{j}\tilde{c}_{2}^{j}\alpha_{l-(j+1)}^{(1)}\Big)+b_{2,2}^{l-2}\tilde{c}_{2}^{l-2}(b_{1,2}\tilde{b}_{1}\tilde{c}_{1}+b_{2,2}\tilde{b}_{2}\tilde{c}_{2}).

Using the expression obtained for

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\varphi(b_{1}b_{1})\varphi(c_{1})\alpha_{k-1}^{(1)}+\varphi(b_{1}b_{2})\varphi(c_{2})\alpha_{k-1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=b_{1,1}\tilde{c}_{1}\alpha_{k-1}^{(1)}+b_{1,2}\tilde{c}_{2}\alpha_{k-1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=b_{1,1}\tilde{c}_{1}\alpha_{k-1}^{(1)}+b_{1,2}^{2}\tilde{c}_{1}\Big(\tilde{c}_{2}\alpha_{k-2}^{(1)}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-4}b_{2,2}^{j}\tilde{c}_{2}^{j+1}\alpha_{k-j-2}^{(1)}+b_{2,2}^{k-3}\tilde{c}_{2}^{k-2}\tilde{b}_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+b_{1,2}b_{2,2}^{k-2}\tilde{c}_{2}^{k-1}\tilde{b}_{2}.

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{(1)}-b_{2,2}\tilde{c}_{2}\alpha_{k-1}^{(1)}=b_{1,1}\tilde{c}_{1}\alpha_{k-1}^{(1)}+(b_{1,2}^{2}-b_{2,2}b_{1,1})\tilde{c}_{2}\tilde{c}_{1}\alpha_{k-2}^{(1)},

and hence we obtain the following linear homogeneous recurrences

\alpha_{k}^{(1)}-(b_{2,2}\tilde{c}_{2}+b_{1,1}\tilde{c}_{1})\alpha_{k-1}^{(1)}+(b_{2,2}b_{1,1}-b_{1,2}^{2})\tilde{c}_{2}\tilde{c}_{1}\alpha_{k-2}^{(1)}=0.

and

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{1}\tilde{c}_{1}s_{1}^{(1)}+\tilde{b}_{2}\tilde{c}_{2}s_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\zeta}(\zeta-\vartheta+\tilde{c}_{1}\tilde{b}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{c}_{2}\tilde{b}_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{1}\tilde{c}_{1}s_{2}^{(1)}+\tilde{b}_{2}\tilde{c}_{2}s_{2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\zeta}(\zeta+\vartheta-\tilde{c}_{1}\tilde{b}_{1}^{2}-\tilde{c}_{2}\tilde{b}_{2}^{2})

by solving a system of equations which concludes the proof by obtaining for any

\displaystyle\varphi(p^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(a^{2k})\alpha_{k}

Theorem 4.2

5.
Applications to Random Matrices

We show in the section the utility of our results to derive the distributions of some random matrices with respect to the partial trace. Let us first recall that the partial trace is defined for a given
N\times N
random matrix
A_{N}
by

\psi_{N}(A_{N}):=\frac{1}{N_{0}}(\mathbb{E}\circ Tr_{N_{0}})(A^{(1,1)})

where
Tr_{N_{0}}
is the non-normalized trace and
A^{(1,1)}
is the first block of
A_{N}
. To draw the connection to random matrices, we recall the notation in Subsection
2.3
and the results stated therein and prove the following:

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
A_{N}
and
B_{N}
are independent
N\times N
Wigner matrices as defined in Section
2.3
, then
(T_{Q(A_{N})},B_{N})_{N}
is asymptotically monotone independent with respect to
\psi_{N}
for any polynomial
Q
whose constant term is zero.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a polynomial that has no constant term. We note that
A_{N}
and
B_{N}
are asymptotically free with respect to
\mathbb{E}\circ Tr_{N}
where we recall that
tr_{N}
is the normalized trace. Also note that
T_{Q(A_{N})}=j_{N}Q(A_{N})(1-j_{N})+(1-j_{N})Q(A_{N})j_{N}
where
j_{N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}}E_{N}^{j,j}
and that by Theorem
2.7
,
Q(A_{N})
and
B_{N}
are asymptotically infinitesimally free from
j_{N}
. Then it follows by Theorem
2.6
that
(T_{Q(A_{N})},B_{N})_{N}
is asymptotically monotone independent with respect to
\psi_{N}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
A_{N}
and
B_{N}
be independent
N\times N
Wigner matrices and let for some
m\geq 0
and non-zero
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
P_{N}
be the polynomial defined by

(5.1)

P_{N}=\alpha T_{A_{N}^{m}}B_{N}+\widebar{\alpha}B_{N}T_{A_{N}^{m}}.

Then the limiting distribution of
P_{N}
with respect to
\psi_{N}
is
\mu=\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{\omega}+\delta_{-\omega})
where
\delta
is the dirac measure and
\omega=\sqrt{|\alpha|D_{m}}
with

D_{m}=\begin{cases}C_{m}-C^{2}_{m/2}&\text{ if }m\text{ is even,}\\
C_{m}&\text{ if }m\text{ is odd,}\end{cases}

and
C_{k}
being the
k
-th Catalan number.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
,
(T_{A_{N}^{m}},B_{N})_{N}
is asymptotically monotone independent with respect to
\psi_{N}
. In addition, we note that the limiting distribution of
T_{A_{N}^{m}}
with respect to
\psi_{N}
can be easily computed following
[
21
, Proposition 5.3]
. Indeed, we have for any
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\psi_{N}(T_{A_{N}^{m}}^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Big[\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\big((\mathbb{E}\circ Tr_{N})(A_{N}^{2m})-(\mathbb{E}\circ Tr_{N})(A_{N}^{m})^{2}\big)\Big]^{k/2}&\text{ if }k\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{ if }k\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

By the Wigner semicircular law, we infer that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\psi_{N}(T_{A_{N}^{m}}^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D_{m}^{k/2}&\text{ if }k\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{ if }k\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Finally, observing that
\lim_{N\to\infty}(\mathbb{E}\circ Tr_{N})(B_{N})=0
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}(\mathbb{E}\circ Tr_{N})(B_{N}^{2})=1
, we get by applying Theorem
3.1
and taking into account Remark
3.2
that for any
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\psi_{N}(P_{N}^{k})=\begin{cases}\big(|\alpha|D_{m}\big)^{k/2}&\text{ if }k\text{ is even}\\
0&\text{ if }k\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

∎

Finally, we give another example around the polynomial in Proposition

Proposition 5.4

Let

and

\delta

\alpha=\frac{1}{2\zeta}(\zeta-\vartheta+C_{h}C_{n}^{2}+C_{s}C_{m}^{2})

\vartheta=\frac{1}{2}(C_{2m}C_{s}+C_{2n}C_{h}),\qquad\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(C_{2m}C_{s}-C_{2n}C_{h})^{2}+4b_{1,2}^{2}C_{s}C_{h}},

C_{k}

k
-th Catalan number.

Proof.

It is notable that
(T_{A_{N}},B_{N})_{N}
is asymptotically monotone independent with respect to
\psi_{N}
by Proposition
5.1
. Then there is a
*
-probability space
(\mathcal{C},\psi)
and elements
a
and
b
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
a\prec b
and
(T_{A_{N}},B_{N})
converges to
(a,b)
in
*
-distribution. In other words, for any
k\geq 1

\lim_{N\to\infty}\psi_{N}(P_{N}^{k})=\psi(p^{k})\text{ where }p=b_{1}ac_{1}ab_{1}+b_{2}ac_{2}ab_{2}.

with
b_{1}=b^{2n},b_{2}=b^{2m},c_{1}=b^{2h},c_{2}=b^{2s}.

Following the notation in Proposition
3.6
, we have

\tilde{b}_{1}=C_{n},\tilde{b}_{2}=C_{m},\tilde{c}_{1}=C_{h},\tilde{c}_{2}=C_{s}
, and

(5.2)

b_{1,2}=\begin{cases}C_{2n}&\text{ if }(i,j)=(1,1),\\
C_{2m}&\text{ if }(i,j)=(2,2),\\
C_{n+m}&\text{ if }(i,j)=(1,2)\text{ or }(2,1).\end{cases}

Then by applying Proposition

\psi(p^{k})=\frac{1}{2\zeta}(\zeta-\vartheta+C_{h}C_{n}^{2}+C_{s}C_{m}^{2})(\vartheta+\zeta)^{k}+\frac{1}{2\zeta}(\zeta+\vartheta-C_{h}C_{n}^{2}-C_{s}C_{m}^{2})(\vartheta-\zeta)^{k}

where

\vartheta=\frac{1}{2}(C_{2m}C_{s}+C_{2n}C_{h}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(C_{2m}C_{s}-C_{2n}C_{h})^{2}+4C_{n+m}^{2}C_{s}C_{h}}.

∎

Remark 5.5
.

\mu=\alpha\delta_{\vartheta+\zeta}+(1-\alpha)\delta_{\vartheta-\zeta}
in Proposition
5.4
is a probability measure. One could also check that indeed

using the fact that

2C_{n}^{2}C_{m}^{2}\leq C_{n+m}^{2}\leq C_{2n}C_{2m}\text{ for all }n,m\geq 1.

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int f(x)g(x)d\mu\text{ where }d\mu(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-t^{2}}dt.

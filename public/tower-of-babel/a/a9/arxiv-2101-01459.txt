Geometry of almost contact metrics as almost ∗ * -Ricci solitons
Dhriti Sundar Patra, Akram Ali, Fatemah Mofarreh

Abstract
. In the present paper, we give some characterizations by considering ∗ * -Ricci soliton as a Kenmotsu metric. We prove that if a Kenmotsu manifold represents an almost ∗ * -Ricci soliton with the potential vector field V V is a Jacobi along the Reeb vector field, then it is a steady ∗ * -Ricci soliton. Next, we show that a Kenmotsu matric endowed an almost ∗ * -Ricci soliton is Einstein metric if it is η \eta -Einstein or the potential vector field V V is collinear to the Reeb vector field or V V is an infinitesimal contact transformation.

Introduction

In
[
23
]
, Pigola et al. introduced the notion of almost Ricci soliton as a generalization of Ricci soliton by adding the condition on the parameter
\lambda
to be a variable function. More precisely, a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be a Ricci soliton if there exist
a vector field
V
and a constant
\lambda
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\pounds_{V}g+\operatorname{Ric_{g}}=\lambda\,g,

where
\pounds_{V}
denotes the Lie-derivative in the direction of
V
and
\operatorname{Ric_{g}}
is the Ricci tensor of
g
. The study on the Ricci solitons and almost Ricci solitons have a long history and a lot of achievements were acquired (see
[
10
,
20
,
24
,
23
]
). On the other hand, the almost contact manifolds admitting Ricci solitons or almost Ricci solitons were also been studied by many researchers, see
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
21
,
25
,
26
,
30
]
.

The notion of
*
-Ricci tensor was
introduced by Tachibana
[
27
]
on almost Hermitian manifolds and studied by Hamada and Inoguchi
[
15
]
on real hypersurfaces of non-flat complex space forms(for details see
[
17
]
) and defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric_{g}}^{*}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr_{g}}\{\varphi oR(X,\varphi\,Y)\}

for all vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
and
\varphi
is a
(1,1)
-tensor field. Furthermore, Hamada gave a complete classification of
*
-Einstein Hopf hypersurfaces in non-flat complex space form. If the
*
-Ricci tensor is a constant multiple of the Riemannian metric
g
, then
M
is said to be a
*
-Einstein manifold. As a generalization of
*
-Einstein metric, Kaimakamis et al.
[
18
]
introduced a so-called
*
-Ricci soliton, where essentially they modified the definition of Ricci soliton by replacing the Ricci tensor
\operatorname{Ric_{g}}
in soliton equation (
1.1
) with the
*
-Ricci tensor
\operatorname{Ric_{g}}^{*}
and defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\pounds_{V}g+\operatorname{Ric_{g}}^{*}=\lambda\,g.

Note that a
*
-Ricci soliton is trivial if the vector field
V
is Killing, and in this case, the manifold becomes
*
-Einstein
.
Thus, it is considered as a natural generalization of
*
-Einstein metric. A
*
-Ricci soliton is said to be almost
*
-Ricci soliton if
\lambda
is a smooth function on
M
. Moreover, an almost
*
-Ricci soliton is called shrinking, steady and expanding according to as
\lambda
is positive, zero and negative, respectively. In
[
18
]
, it was studied a real hypersurfaces of a non-flat complex space form admitting a
*
-Ricci soliton whose potential vector field is the structure vector field and proved that a real hypersurface in a complex projective space does not admit a
*
-Ricci soliton. They have also shown that a real hypersurface of complex hyperbolic space admitting a
*
-Ricci soltion is locally congruent to a geodesic hypersphere.

In
[
19
]
, Kenmotsu first introduced and studied a particular class of almost contact metric manifolds, known as Kenmotsu manifolds, characterized by the terms of warped products.
The warped product
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}N
(of the real line
\mathbb{R}
and a Kähler manifold
N
) with the warping function

(1.3)

\displaystyle f(t)=ce^{t},

given on an open interval
J=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
,
admits Kenmotsu structure. Conversely, every point of a Kenmotsu manifold has a neighbourhood, which is locally a warped product
J\times_{f}N
, where
f
is given by (
1.3
).
In recent years, several anthers studied Ricci soliton and almost Ricci soliton on contact metric manifolds
[
25
,
26
]
, Kenmotsu manifolds
[
11
,
13
]
, cosymplectic manifold
[

. First, Ghosh
[

proved that a Kenmotsu metric as a Ricci soliton is Einstein if the metric is
\eta
-Einstein

]
or the potential vector field

is contact. Recently, Patra-Rovenski
[

proved that a Kenmotsu metric as an

-Ricci soliton is Einstein if either it is
\eta
-Einstein or the potential vector field
V
is an infinitesimal contact transformation or
V
is collinear to the Reeb vector field.

Next, Chen
[
3
]
considered a real hypersurface of a non-flat complex space form which admits a
*
-Ricci soliton whose potential vector field belongs to the principal curvature space and the holomorphic distribution.
Recently, Ghosh-Patra
[
14

studied
*
-Ricci soliton and gradient almost
*
-Ricci soliton on contact metric manifolds. Later on, several mathematician studied

-Ricci soliton on contact and almost contact metric manifolds (e.g., see

2.
Preliminaries

Let
M^{2n+1}
be a
(2\,n+1)
-dimensional smooth manifold. An
almost contact structure
on
M^{2n+1}
is a triple
(\varphi,\xi,\eta)
, where
\varphi
is a
(1,1)
-tensor,
\xi
is a vector field (called
Reeb vector field
) and
\eta
is a 1-form, satisfying

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi^{2}=-id+\eta\otimes\xi,\quad\eta(\xi)=1,

where
id
denotes the identity endomorphism. A smooth manifold
M
with such a structure is called an
almost contact manifold
. It follows from (
2.1
) that

\varphi(\xi)=0,\quad\eta\circ\varphi=0,\quad{\rm rank}(\varphi)=2n.

A Riemannian metric
g
on
M
is said to be
compatible
with an almost contact structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta)
if

g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\,\eta(Y)

for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
, where
\mathfrak{X}_{M}
is the Lie algebra of all vector fields on
M
.
An almost contact manifold endowed with a compatible Riemannian metric
g
is said to be an
almost contact metric manifold
and is denoted by
M(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
.
The
fundamental
2
-form

\Phi
on
M(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
is defined by
\Phi(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi Y)
for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
.
An almost contact metric manifold satisfying
d\eta=0
and
d\Phi=2\,\eta\wedge\Phi
is said to be an
almost Kenmotsu manifold
(e.g.,
[
9
,
8
]
).
An almost contact metric structure on
M
is said to be
normal
if
the almost complex structure
J
on
M^{2n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
defined by

J(X,f\frac{d}{dt})=(\varphi\,X-f\xi,\eta(X)\frac{d}{dt}),

where
f
is a real function on
M\times\mathbb{R}
, is
integrable
, or the tensor
N_{\varphi}=[\varphi,\varphi]+2d\eta\otimes\xi
vanishes on
M
, where
[\varphi,\varphi]
denotes the
Nijenhuis tensor
of
\varphi
.
A normal almost Kenmotsu manifold is said to be a
Kenmotsu manifold
(see
[
19
]
); equivalently, an almost contact metric manifold is said to be a
Kenmotsu manifold
(see
[
19
]
) if

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y=g(\varphi X,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)\,\varphi X

for any
X,\,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
, where
\nabla
is the
Levi-Civita connection
of
g
.
The following formulas hold on Kenmotsu manifolds, see
[
13
,
19
]
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\,\xi

\displaystyle=X-\eta(X)\xi,

(2.4)

\displaystyle R(X,Y)\xi

\displaystyle=\eta(X)Y-\eta(Y)X,

(2.5)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\xi}Q)X

\displaystyle=-2\,QX-4nX,

(2.6)

\displaystyle Q\xi

\displaystyle=-2n\xi

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
, where
R
and
Q
denote, respectively, the
curvature tensor
and the
Ricci operator
of
g
associated with the Ricci tensor and given by

\operatorname{Ric_{g}}(X,Y)=g(QX,Y)
for

X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Lemma 4.2 of
[
12
]
) Let
M^{2n+1}(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
be a Kenmotsu manifold. If
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq 2n+1}
be an orthonormal frame on
M
then we have for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}

\displaystyle(i)\,\,\,\sum_{\,i=1}^{2n+1}g((\nabla_{X}Q)\varphi\,e_{i},e_{i})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(ii)\,\,\,\sum_{\,i=1}^{2n+1}g((\nabla_{\varphi\,e_{i}}Q)\varphi X,e_{i})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}X(r)-\{r+2n(2n+1)\}\eta(X).

In view of the first Bianchi’s identity, the
*
-Ricci tensor
on almost contact metric manifold can be written as (see
[
2
]
)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric_{g}}^{*}(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}g(R(X,\varphi Y)e_{i},\varphi e_{i})=\sum_{i}g(R(e_{i},X)\varphi X,\varphi e_{i})

for all
X
,
Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
. Note that
\operatorname{Ric_{g}}^{*}
is not symmetric. By contraction of (
2.7
), the
*
-scalar curvature
is given by
r^{*}=\sum_{i}\operatorname{Ric_{g}}^{*}(e_{i},e_{i})
. If the
*
-Ricci tensor
\operatorname{Ric_{g}}^{*}
is a constant multiple of the Riemannian metric
g
we say that the manifold is
*
-Einstein
. Moreover, the
*
-scalar curvature is not constant on a
*
-Einstein manifold
.
A contact metric manifold
M^{2n+1}
is called
\eta
-Einstein
, if its Ricci tensor has the following form

(2.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric_{g}}=\alpha\,g+\beta\,\eta\otimes\eta

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are smooth functions on
M
.
For an
\eta
-Einstein
K
-contact manifold of dimension
>3
, these
\alpha
and
\beta
are constant (e.g.,
[
32
]
), but for an
\eta
-Einstein Kenmotsu manifold this is not true (see details
[
19
]
).
The
divergence of a tensor field

K
of type
(1,s)
is defined by

(\operatorname{div}K)(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{s})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{2n+1}g((\nabla_{e_{i}}K)(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{s}),e_{i})

for all
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{s}\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
. It is well known that for an arbitrary
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
,

\pounds_{X}f=X(f)=g(X,Df)

for some smooth function
f
, where
D
is the gradient operator of
g
. The
Hessian
of a smooth function
f
on
M
is defined
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
by

{\rm Hess}_{f}(X,Y)=\nabla^{2}f(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}Df,Y).

.

Let

\displaystyle R(X,Y)\varphi\,Z-\varphi\,R(X,Y)Z=g(Y,Z)\,\varphi X-g(X,Z)\,\varphi Y+g(X,\varphi Z)Y-g(Y,\varphi Z)X.

\displaystyle g(R(X,Y)\varphi\,Z,\varphi U)-g(R(X,Y)Z,U)

\displaystyle-g(Y,\varphi Z)\,g(X,\varphi U)

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\,g)(X,Y)+2\,\operatorname{Ric_{g}}(X,Y)=2\,\{\lambda-2n+1\}\,g(X,Y)-2\,\eta(X)\eta(Y).

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}\,\pounds_{V}\,g)(X,Y)+2\,(\nabla_{Z}\,\operatorname{Ric_{g}})(X,Y)

\displaystyle-2\,\eta(X)\eta(Y)\eta(Z)\bigg\}.

Recall the commutation formula, [see p. 23 in

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\,\nabla_{Z}\,g-\nabla_{Z}\pounds_{V}\,g-\nabla_{[V,Z]}\,g)(X,Y)=

\displaystyle-g((\pounds_{V}\,\nabla)(Z,X),Y)

\displaystyle g((\pounds_{V}\,\nabla)(Z,X),Y)+g((\pounds_{V}\,\nabla)(Z,Y),X)=

\displaystyle 2\,Z(\lambda)\,g(X,Y)-2\,(\nabla_{Z}\operatorname{Ric_{g}})(X,Y)

\displaystyle-2\,\eta(X)\eta(Y)\eta(Z)\bigg\}.

Cyclically rearranging

\displaystyle g((\pounds_{V}\,\nabla)(X,Y),Z)=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}\operatorname{Ric_{g}})(X,Y)-(\nabla_{X}\operatorname{Ric_{g}})(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}\operatorname{Ric_{g}})(Z,X)

\displaystyle X(\lambda)\,g(Y,Z)-Y(\lambda)\,g(X,Z)-Z(\lambda)\,g(X,Y)

\displaystyle 2\bigg\{\eta(Z)g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)\eta(Z)\bigg\}.

2.3

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}Q)\xi=-QX-2nX

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\,\nabla)(X,\xi)+(\pounds_{V}\,\nabla)(X,Y)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M^{2n+1}(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
,
n>1
, be a Kenmotsu manifold. If
g
represents an almost
*
-Ricci soliton with the potential vector field
V
is a Jacobi along
\xi
, then it is a steady
*
-Ricci soliton.

Proof.

First of all, invoking Lemma
3.2
, we derive the following expression

\displaystyle R(X,Y)D\lambda=

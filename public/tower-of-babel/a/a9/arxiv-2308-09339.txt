Minimaxity under half-Cauchy type priors
Yuzo Maruyama ??,??, Takeru Matsuda ??,??

Abstract
This is a follow-up paper of Polson and Scott (2012, Bayesian Analysis), which claimed that the half-Cauchy prior is a sensible default prior for a scale parameter in hierarchical models. For estimation of a normal mean vector under the quadratic loss, they showed that the Bayes estimator with respect to the half-Cauchy prior seems to be minimax through numerical experiments. In terms of the shrinkage coefficient, the half-Cauchy prior has a U-shape and can be interpreted as a continuous spike and slab prior. In this paper, we consider a general class of priors with U-shapes and theoretically establish sufficient conditions for the minimaxity of the corresponding (generalized) Bayes estimators. We also develop an algorithm for posterior sampling and present numerical results.

Introduction

Consider a normal hierarchical model

\displaystyle y\mid\beta

\displaystyle\sim N_{p}(\beta,I_{p}),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\beta\mid\kappa

\displaystyle\sim N_{p}(0,\kappa^{-1}(1-\kappa)I_{p}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle\sim\pi(\kappa),

(1.3)

where the hyperparameter
\kappa\in(0,1)
specifies the shrinkage coefficient of the Bayes estimator (posterior mean) of
\beta
:

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}(y)={\rm E}[\beta\mid y]=(1-{\rm E}[\kappa\mid y])y.

(1.4)

Polson and Scott 2012
claimed that the hyperprior

\pi(\kappa)\propto\kappa^{-1/2}(1-\kappa)^{-1/2},

(1.5)

is a sensible default choice.
Since it has a U-shape with

\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\pi(\kappa)=\lim_{\kappa\to 1}\pi(\kappa)=\infty,

(1.6)

it may be regarded as a continuous spike and slab prior.
See
Carvalho, Polson and
Scott 2010
for a related discussion in the context of horseshoe priors.
For the parameterization

\lambda=\sqrt{\frac{1-\kappa}{\kappa}}\in(0,\infty),

(1.7)

the prior (
1.5
) is expressed as

\pi(\lambda)\propto\frac{1}{1+\lambda^{2}}I_{(0,\infty)}(\lambda),

(1.8)

which is the reason why the prior (
1.5
) is called the half-Cauchy prior.

Polson and Scott 2012
introduced a class of “hypergeometric inverted-beta priors” with the density

\pi(\kappa)\propto\kappa^{a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}(1+c\kappa)^{-1}\exp(d\kappa),

(1.9)

which is a generalization of the half-Cauchy prior (
1.5
).
For estimation of
p
-variate normal mean
\beta
under the quadratic loss

, they derived expressions for the risk of Bayes estimators with respect to the prior (
1.9
).
Recall that the usual estimator
\hat{\beta}=y
is inadmissible for

although it is minimax for any

(
Stein 1974
;
Fourdrinier, Strawderman and
Wells 2018
)
.
Through numerical experiments,
Polson and Scott 2012
discussed the minimaxity of the Bayes estimators under (
1.9
) for
p\geq 3
and compared them with the James–Stein estimator.

In this paper, we consider more general (possibly improper) prior

\pi(\kappa)=\kappa^{a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa),

(1.10)

where we assume

A.1

[around
\kappa=0
]

Furthermore, the first author of this paper,

.
Hence this paper can be also viewed as an extension of two papers

In Section

Minimaxity

&\geq\min\left\{0,p/2+a+2+H_{1}(1)\right\}-2\max_{\kappa\in[0,1]}H_{2}(\kappa).\end{split}

(3.30)

Recall

\Delta(w)=\frac{p}{2}-a-2+(1-b)\Delta_{1}(w)+\Delta_{2}(w),

(3.31)

as in (
3.13
).
Further, as in (
3.20
) and (
3.30
),
the lower bound of
\Delta_{1}(w)
and
\Delta_{2}(w)
is negative and non-positive respectively.
Hence the sufficient condition
\Delta(w)\geq 0
cannot hold if
a\geq p/2-2
.
Let
a<p/2-2
. Then, by (
3.13
), (
3.20
) and (
3.30
),
we have

\Delta(w)\geq\frac{3p}{2}+a-\frac{p+2a+2+2\max_{\kappa\in[0,1]}H_{2}(\kappa)}{b}+\min\left\{0,p/2+a+2+H_{1}(1)\right\},

(3.32)

which completes the proof.
∎

When
H(\kappa)
is monotone non-increasing, it follows that

H_{1}(\kappa)=H(\kappa)\text{ and }H_{2}(\kappa)\equiv 0

(3.33)

for (
3.1
).
When
H(\kappa)
is monotone non-decreasing, it follows that

H_{1}(\kappa)=0,\ H_{2}(\kappa)=H(\kappa)\text{ and }\max_{\kappa\in[0,1]}H_{2}(\kappa)=H(1),

(3.34)

for (
3.1
).
Then we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose
-p/2\leq a<p/2-2
.

1.

Suppose
H(\kappa)
is monotone non-increasing with
H(1)\geq-(p/2+a+2)
.
Then the (generalized) Bayes estimator with respect to the prior (
1.10
) is minimax if

\displaystyle\frac{p+2a+2}{3p/2+a}\leq b<1.

(3.35)

2.

Suppose
H(\kappa)
is monotone non-decreasing with
H(1)<(p/2-a-2)/2
.
Then the (generalized) Bayes estimator with respect to the prior (
1.10
) is minimax if

\frac{p+2a+2+2H(1)}{3p/2+a}\leq b<1.

(3.36)

Before
Fourdrinier, Strawderman and
Wells 1998
, minimaxity of estimators was typically shown through so-called

Baranchik 1970
’s (
Baranchik 1970
) sufficient condition, which states that
the shrinkage estimator

\left(1-\frac{\phi(\|y\|^{2})}{\|y\|^{2}}\right)y

(3.37)

is minimax if
\phi
is monotone non-decreasing and
0\leq\phi(w)\leq 2(p-2)
.
For the (generalized) Bayes estimator with respect to the prior (
1.10
), the function
\phi
is obtained from Tweedie’s formula (
2.9
) as

\phi(\|y\|^{2})=\|y\|^{2}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+1}\exp(-\kappa\|y\|^{2}/2)\kappa^{a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2}\exp(-\kappa\|y\|^{2}/2)\kappa^{a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa}.

(3.38)

Then, we have the following result on the non-monotonicity of
\phi
, which means that we cannot employ
Baranchik 1970
’s (
Baranchik 1970
) sufficient condition.

Lemma 3.2
.

1.

\lim_{\|y\|^{2}\to\infty}\phi(\|y\|^{2})=p+2a
.

2.

Suppose

1-b+\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\frac{h^{\prime}(\kappa)}{h(\kappa)}>0.

(3.39)

Then
\phi
is not monotone and approaches
p+2a
from the above.

Suppose
H(\kappa)=\kappa h^{\prime}(\kappa)/h(\kappa)

is monotone non-decreasing. Since
H(0)=0
by
A.1
and (
3.1
),
both
H(\kappa)
and
h^{\prime}(\kappa)/h(\kappa)=H(\kappa)/\kappa
are non-negative for
\kappa\in[0,1]
.
Then we have

\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\frac{h^{\prime}(\kappa)}{h(\kappa)}\geq 0\ \text{ and }\ 1-b+\liminf_{\kappa\to 0}\frac{h^{\prime}(\kappa)}{h(\kappa)}>0.

(3.40)

Thus, from Part
2
of Lemma
3.2
,
\phi
is non-monotone.
Hence, Part
2
of Corollary
3.1
provides the minimaxity of
(generalized) Bayes estimators with non-monotone
\phi
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Let
w=\|y\|^{2}/2
. Then

\phi(w)=2w\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a}\exp(-\kappa w)(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a-1}\exp(-\kappa w)(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa}.

(3.41)

A Tauberian theorem (see, e.g., Theorem 13.5.4 in
Feller 1971
) gives

\begin{split}\lim_{w\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a}\exp(-\kappa w)(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa}{w^{-p/2-a-1}h(1/w)}&=\Gamma(p/2+a+1)\\
\lim_{w\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a-1}\exp(-\kappa w)(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa}{w^{-p/2-a}h(1/w)}&=\Gamma(p/2+a),\end{split}

(3.42)

which implies that

\lim_{\|y\|^{2}\to\infty}\phi(\|y\|^{2})=p+2a.

(3.43)

The way to approach to
p+2a
determines
(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)
as follows.
Under the condition (
3.39
),
there exists
0<\kappa_{0}<1
and
\epsilon>0
such that

\frac{1-b}{1-\kappa}+\frac{h^{\prime}(\kappa)}{h(\kappa)}\geq\epsilon

(3.44)

for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
.
An integration by parts with (
3.8
) and (
3.10
)
gives

\begin{split}&w\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a}\exp(-\kappa w)(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa\\
&=(p/2+a)\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\left\{\exp(-\kappa w)-\exp(-w)\right\}\mathrm{d}\kappa\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\left\{\exp(-\kappa w)-\exp(-w)\right\}\mathrm{d}\kappa\\
&=(p/2+a)\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\exp(-\kappa w)\mathrm{d}\kappa\\
&\quad-\frac{(p/2+a)\int_{0}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\exp(w)}\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\exp(-\kappa w)\mathrm{d}\kappa\\
&\quad+\int_{\epsilon_{0}}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\left\{\exp(-\kappa w)-\exp(-w)\right\}\mathrm{d}\kappa.\end{split}

(3.45)

For the last term, we have

\begin{split}\exp(-\kappa w)-\exp(-w)&=\exp(-\kappa w)\left\{1-\exp(-\{1-\kappa\}w)\right\}\\
&\leq\exp(-\kappa w)(1-\kappa)w\end{split}

(3.46)

and hence

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\epsilon_{0}}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\left\{\exp(-\kappa w)-\exp(-w)\right\}\mathrm{d}\kappa\right|\\
&\leq\frac{w}{\exp(\kappa_{0}w)}\int_{\epsilon_{0}}^{1}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left|\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\right|(1-\kappa)\mathrm{d}\kappa.\end{split}

(3.47)

For the third term,

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\exp(-\kappa w)\mathrm{d}\kappa\\
&=\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{\frac{1-b}{1-\kappa}+\frac{h^{\prime}(\kappa)}{h(\kappa)}\right\}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\exp(-\kappa w)\mathrm{d}\kappa\\
&\geq\epsilon\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\kappa^{p/2+a}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\exp(-\kappa w)\mathrm{d}\kappa.\end{split}

(3.48)

Thus, by (
3.44
), we have

\lim_{w\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\kappa^{p/2+a}\left\{(1-\kappa)^{b-1}h(\kappa)\right\}^{\prime}\exp(-\kappa w)\mathrm{d}\kappa}{w^{-p/2-a-1}h(1/w)}\geq\epsilon\Gamma(p/2+a+1)

(3.49)

and

\liminf_{\|y\|^{2}\to\infty}\|y\|^{2}\left\{\phi(\|y\|^{2})-(p+2a)\right\}>0,

(3.50)

which implies that
\phi
is not monotone and approaches
p+2a
from the above.
∎

5.1
Algorithm for posterior sampling

We develop an algorithm for sampling from the posterior
\pi(\beta,\kappa\mid y)
corresponding to our prior
\pi(\beta,\kappa)
in (
1.2
) and (
1.10
).
When
h(\kappa)\equiv 1
(e.g. half-Cauchy prior), the Gibbs sampler can be written down in closed-form by using the fact that the beta-prime (or Pearson Type VI) distribution is given by an inverse-Gamma mixture of inverse-Gamma
(
Polson and Scott 2012
)
.
However, it seems difficult to derive such a data-augmentation technique for general
h(\kappa)
.
Thus, we employ the Metropolis-within-Gibbs algorithm
(
Robert and
Casella 2004
)
here.

First, the conditional distribution
\pi(\beta\mid\kappa,y)
is Gaussian with mean
(1-\kappa)y
and covariance
(1-\kappa)I_{p}
and thus easy to sample.
Next, the conditional distribution
\pi(\kappa\mid\beta,y)
is

\pi(\kappa\mid\beta,y)\propto\kappa^{a+p/2-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}\exp\left(-\frac{\|y\|^{2}}{2}\kappa\right)h(\kappa),\quad 0\leq\kappa\leq 1.

To sample from it, we use the independent Metropolis–Hastings algorithm
(
Robert and
Casella 2004
)
with the proposal distribution given by
{\rm Beta}(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
, where we set
\tilde{a}=\max(a,0.5)
and
\tilde{b}=0.5
.
In summary, our algorithm is given as follows:

1.

Set
\kappa_{0}=0.5
and
t=1

2.

Sample
\beta_{t}\sim{\rm N}_{p}((1-\kappa_{t-1})y,(1-\kappa_{t-1})I_{p})

3.

Sample
\tilde{\kappa}\sim{\rm Beta}(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
and set

\kappa_{t}=\begin{cases}\tilde{\kappa}&{\rm with\ probability}\ q\\
\kappa_{t-1}&{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}

where

q=\min\left(\left(\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{\kappa}\right)^{a-\tilde{a}+p/2}\left(\frac{1-\tilde{\kappa}}{1-\kappa}\right)^{b-\tilde{b}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|y\|^{2}}{2}(\tilde{\kappa}-\kappa)\right)\frac{h(\tilde{\kappa})}{h(\kappa)},1\right)

4.

Update
t
to
t+1
and return to 2

In computing the generalized Bayes estimator (posterior mean of
\beta
), we applied the Rao–Blackwellization technique for variance reduction
(
Robert and
Roberts 2021
)
.
Namely, we replaced step 2 of the above algorithm with
\beta_{t}=(1-\kappa_{t-1})y
and took average of sampled
\beta
after burn-in.

In the following, we examine two priors.
The first one is

\displaystyle\pi(\kappa)=\kappa^{a_{*}-1}(1-\kappa)^{a_{*}-1}

(5.1)

where
a_{*}\in(0,1]
is given in (
4.1
).
From Corollary
3.1
,
the Bayes estimator with respect to this prior is minimax when
p\geq 7
.
The second one is

\displaystyle\pi(\kappa)=\kappa^{a-1}(1-\kappa)^{b-1}(1+c_{1}\log(1/\kappa))^{c_{2}},

(5.2)

where
a=0,b=(5p+4)/(6p),c_{1}=(p-4)/16,c_{2}=-2
as in (
4.12
).
From the discussion in Section
4.2
, this prior is proper and the Bayes estimator with respect to it is minimax when
p\geq 5

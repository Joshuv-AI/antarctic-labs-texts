Fluctuations of eigenvector overlaps and the Berry conjecture for Wigner matrices
L. Benigni, G. Cipolloni

Abstract
We prove that any finite collection of quadratic forms (overlaps) of general deterministic matrices and eigenvectors of an N × N N\times N Wigner matrix has joint Gaussian fluctuations. This can be viewed as the random matrix analogue of the Berry random wave conjecture.

The groundbreaking discovery of Wigner

In this article, we study joint fluctuations of the overlaps
\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle
. For simplicity, consider
A

to be two deterministic

\mathds{E}\left[\frac{N^{2}}{|I|}\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle\langle\mathbf{u}_{j},A\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle\right]\ll 1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathds{E}\left[\frac{N^{2}}{|I|}\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle^{2}\right]\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}{1}.

The main difficulty for this result at the time was to understand correlations between different entries of different eigenvectors and was handled by introducing new moment observables along the Dyson Brownian motion. For the same specific family of observables, and introducing again other symmetrized observables, convergence of the diagonal overlap to the Gaussian distribution was proved in
[
3

\sqrt{\frac{N}{\langle|A|^{2}\rangle}}\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

This convergence was then extended to all observables
A
such that

has effective rank
\gg 1

[
15

by refining these multi-resolvent local laws. Note that the condition is necessary to obtain some averaging in light of the central limit theorem making the result optimal. Our goal in this article is to obtain similar convergence, not just for the diagonal overlaps, but jointly for any finite collections of eigenvectors and observables
A
using the machinery set up developed in
[

[

We prove that
(\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle)_{i,j,A}

\mathbb{E}\left[N\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle\langle\mathbf{u}_{k},B\mathbf{u}_{l}\rangle\right]\approx\left[\delta_{kj}\delta_{li}+\left(\frac{2}{\beta}-1\right)\delta_{ki}\delta_{lj}\right]\langle AB\rangle,

(1.2)

where
\beta
is the Dyson index such that
\beta=1
in the symmetric case and
\beta=2
in the Hermitian case. In particular, note that
\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle,\langle\mathbf{u}_{j},B\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle
are asymptotically independent for any
j\neq i
and, even more interestingly, that
\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},A\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle,\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},B\mathbf{u}_{i}\rangle
are also asymptotically independent as long as
\langle AB\rangle\ll\sqrt{\langle A^{2}\rangle\langle B^{2}\rangle}
. The limiting statement in (
1.2

W
drawn from the GOE/GUE ensembles by Weingarten calculus as the eigenbasis of these matrices are distributed according to the Haar measure on the orthogonal or unitary group, respectively. Thus (
1.2

\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{k}
behaves as if they were Haar distributed on the corresponding compact group. While the covariance structure is different due to eigenvectors being close to Haar distributed on unitary or othgonal group, our result proves joint Gaussian fluctuations around the QUE property of eigenvectors and thus can be understood as an equivalent to the Berry conjecture in the context of random matrices.

Our result, and many mentioned above, rely on a careful analysis of the Dyson vector flow, the flow of eigenvectors induced by the Dyson Brownian motion (DBM). This flow was first computed in
[
10
]
and first used in this setting in the seminal paper
[
9

where it was used to compute asymptotic moments of individual eigenvector entries. The dynamical method to prove universality results as introduced in the groundbreaking paper
[

consists in three steps. The first step establishes an (almost) optimal a priori bound for the quantity of interest and the second step use the DBM, combined with our a priori bound, to prove our joint fluctuations of eigenvectors along the dynamics. Finally, the third step is based on a comparison step between the dynamical model and Wigner matrices.

•

Suppose that the particle at site
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
jump respectively to the sites
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
. If at the site
i_{1}
, the second chosen particle is attached to a
particle at a site
i_{p}
with
i_{p}\neq i_{2}
. Then after the jumps, the edge
\{j_{1},j_{2}\}
will be a single edge and thus
F(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf a}{\bf b}}^{{\bf i}{\bf j}})=0.
For the observable

f_{t}
, this creates a cycle of a certain order as in the second point of the Case 1 above and we get that, for some
p>0
,

\sum_{\bf j}^{*}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{2n}a_{i_{r},j_{r}}\right)\left(f_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}^{{\bf i}{\bf j}})-F(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}^{{\bf i}{\bf j}})\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{(n-p+1)\xi}}{\sqrt{N\eta}}\Phi_{\bf A}\right).

•

Suppose again that the particle at site
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
jump respectively to the sites
j_{1}
and
j_{2}.
Suppose now that the other chosen particle at site
i_{1}
belongs to an occurrence of the edge
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
and that its attached vertex is also jumping to site
j_{2},
then we are exactly in the same case as the third item of Case 1.

Thus, we see that if we are in the first or second item of Case 1 or the first item of Case 2 for at least one edge, we obtain that

\sum_{\bf j}^{*}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{2n}a_{i_{r},j_{r}}\right)\left(f_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}^{{\bf i}{\bf j}})-F(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}^{{\bf i}{\bf j}})\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{n\xi}}{\sqrt{N\eta}}\Phi_{\bf A}\right).

If we perform for all particles in the configuration, jumps as in the third item of Case 1 or the second of Case 2 (if possible), we obtain an error of

\sum_{\bf j}^{*}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{2n}a_{i_{r},j_{r}}\right)\left(f_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}^{{\bf i}{\bf j}})-F(\mathbf{x}_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}^{{\bf i}{\bf j}})\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{n\xi}}{\sqrt{N\eta}}\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\langle A_{k}^{2}\rangle}{N}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{n\xi}}{\sqrt{N\eta}}\Phi_{\bf A}\right)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Green’s Function Comparison (GFT)

Consider the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck flow

\mathrm{d}\widehat{W}_{t}=-\frac{\widehat{W}_{t}}{2}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{B}_{t}}{\sqrt{N}},\qquad\widehat{W}_{0}=W,

(A.1)

with
\widehat{B}_{t}
being a matrix valued real symmetric or complex Hermitian Brownian motion, and
W
being a Wigner matrix satisfying Assumption
2.1
. Note that along the flow (
A.1
) the first two moments of
\widehat{W}_{t}
are preserved. Note also that the dynamics is slightly different from (
3.1
) but it is chosen as an initial condition for (
3.1
) such that at time
T
, their distributions are the same, see
[
15
]
*(B.1)–(B.3) for more details. We denote the resolvent of
\widehat{W}_{t}
by
G_{t}(z):=(\widehat{W}_{t}-z)^{-1}
, with
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.

In this section we show that for
t
sufficiently small the distribution of the eigenvectors of
\widehat{W}_{t}
is very close to their distribution at time
t=0
. The next proposition shows that as long as
t\ll N^{1/2}
the change in the distribution of eigenvectors is negligible. We remark that for the purpose of proving Theorem
2.2
we need to choose
t=N^{-1+\varepsilon}
, for some small fixed
\varepsilon>0
, hence much smaller that the threshold
N^{-1/2}
. The fact that to prove Theorem
2.2
it is enough to prove Proposition
A.1
for resolvents at a spectral parameter
\eta=N^{-1-\zeta}
follows by level repulsion
[
25
]
.

Proposition A.1
.

Fix any
p\in\mathbb{N},\zeta,\delta>0
,and consider a smooth function
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\mathbb{E}F\left(N^{-(k-2)/2}C_{k}^{-1}\langle\Im G_{t}(z_{1})A_{1}\dots\Im G_{t}(z_{k})A_{k}\rangle\right)_{k\in[p]}-\mathbb{E}F\left(\Im G_{t}\to\Im G_{0}\right)_{k\in[p]}\right|\lesssim tN^{1/2+C\zeta}.

(A.2)

Here
z_{i}=E_{i}+\mathrm{i}\eta
, with
\eta=N^{-1-\zeta}
and
E_{i}\in[-2+\delta,2-\delta]
,
A_{1},A_{2},\dots
are Hermitian matrices with
\langle A_{i}\rangle=0
, and

C_{k}=C_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{k}):=\prod_{i\in[k]}\langle A_{i}^{2}\rangle^{1/2}.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is similar to
[
15
]
*Appendix B, we thus only present an outline of it. To keep the notation short we define

R_{t}:=F\left(N^{-(k-2)/2}C_{k}^{-1}\langle\Im G_{t}(z_{1})A_{1}\dots\Im G_{t}(z_{k})A_{k}\rangle\right)_{k\in[p]}.

Then, by Itô’s formula, we compute

\mathbb{E}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}R_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}w_{\alpha}(t)\partial_{\alpha}R_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\kappa_{t}(\alpha,\beta)\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}R_{t}\right],

(A.3)

where
\alpha,\beta\in[N]^{2}
are double indices,
w_{\alpha}(t)
are the entries of
\widehat{W}_{t}
, and
\partial_{\alpha}:=\partial_{w_{\alpha}}
. Here

\kappa_{t}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l}):=\kappa(w_{\alpha_{1}}(t),\dots,w_{\alpha_{l}}(t))

denotes the joint cumulant of
w_{\alpha_{1}}(t),\dots,w_{\alpha_{l}}(t)
, with
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that by (
2.1
) it follows
|\kappa_{t}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l})|\lesssim N^{-l/2}
uniformly in
t\geqslant 0
. Performing a cumulant expansion in (
A.3
) we are left with

\mathbb{E}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}R_{t}=\mathbb{E}\sum_{l=3}^{L}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l}}\kappa_{t}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l})\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\alpha_{l}}R_{t}+\Omega(L),

(A.4)

with
\Omega(L)
a negligible error.

Next, we note that by
[
15
]
*Corollary 2.4 it follows that

\big|\langle{\bm{x}},G_{t}(z_{1})A_{1}\dots G_{t}(z_{k})A_{k}G_{t}(z_{k+1}){\bm{y}}\rangle\big|\lesssim N^{C\zeta}N^{k/2}\prod_{i\in[k]}\langle A_{i}^{2}\rangle^{1/2},

(A.5)

for some constant
C>0
possibly depending on
k
. We point out that in
[
15
]
*Corollary 2.4 the bound (
A.5
) is proven for
\Im z_{i}\geqslant N^{-1+\varepsilon}
, for any small
\varepsilon>0
; the fact that it can be extended to
\Im z_{i}=\eta
, i.e. a bit below the level spacing
N^{-1}
, follows similarly to
[
11
]
*Appendix A. We now show this considering a specific example, the general case is completely analogous and so omitted; we explain the details at the end of this section.

Finally, by (
A.5
) and
|(G)_{ab}|\leqslant N^{\zeta}
it readily follows that for any
k\in[p]
we have

\left|\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\alpha_{l}}N^{-(k-2)/2}C_{k}^{-1}\langle\Im G_{t}(z_{1})A_{1}\dots\Im G_{t}(z_{k})A_{k}\rangle\right|\leqslant N^{C\zeta},

(A.6)

for some large fixed constant
C>0
. We thus conclude that

\left|\mathbb{E}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}R_{t}\right|\lesssim\sum_{l=3}^{L}N^{-l/2+2}N^{C\zeta}=N^{1/2+C\zeta}.

(A.7)

Integrating in time we conclude (
A.2
).

∎

Proof of (
A.5
).

For simplicity we do not distinguish the indices, i.e. we write

(GA)^{l}=G_{t}(z_{1})A_{1}\dots G_{t}(z_{l})A_{l}.

Assume that
\Im z_{l}=\eta
and
|\Im z_{j}|\geqslant\eta_{*}:=N^{-1+\varepsilon}
for
j\neq l
, then

\begin{split}\langle{\bm{x}},(GA)^{l-1}G(z_{l})A(GA)^{k-l}G{\bm{y}}\rangle&=\langle{\bm{x}},(GA)^{l-1}G(E_{l}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{*})A(GA)^{k-l}G{\bm{y}}\rangle\\
&\quad-\int_{\eta}^{\eta_{*}}\langle{\bm{x}},(GA)^{l-1}G(E_{q_{0}}+\mathrm{i}\tau)^{2}A(GA)^{k-l}G{\bm{y}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

(A.8)

For the first term in the rhs. we use the local law from
[
15
]
*Corollary 2.4 obtaining a bound as in (
A.5
). For the second term we estimate (neglecting
\log N
–factors)

\begin{split}&\int_{\eta}^{\eta_{*}}\langle{\bm{x}},(GA)^{l-1}G(E_{l}+\mathrm{i}\tau)^{2}A(GA)^{k-l}G{\bm{y}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\lesssim\int_{\eta}^{\eta_{*}}\frac{1}{\tau}\langle{\bm{x}},(GA)^{l-1}\Im G(E_{l}+\mathrm{i}\tau)((GA)^{l-1})^{*}{\bm{x}}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{\bm{y}},(A(GA)^{k-l}G)^{*}\Im G(E_{l}+\mathrm{i}\tau)A(GA)^{k-l}G{\bm{y}}\rangle^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\lesssim\frac{\eta_{*}}{\eta}\int_{\eta}^{\eta_{*}}\frac{1}{\tau}\langle{\bm{x}},(GA)^{l-1}\Im G(E_{l}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{*})((GA)^{l-1})^{*}{\bm{x}}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{\bm{y}},(A(GA)^{k-l}G)^{*}\Im G(E_{l}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{*})A(GA)^{k-l}G{\bm{y}}\rangle^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\lesssim\frac{\eta_{*}}{\eta}\sqrt{N^{l-1}N^{k-l+1}\prod_{i\in[k]}\langle A_{i}^{2}\rangle}\lesssim N^{\zeta}N^{k/2}\prod_{i\in[k]}\langle A_{i}^{2}\rangle^{1/2}.\end{split}

(A.9)

This proves (
A.5
) choosing
\varepsilon\leqslant\zeta
. We remark that in the second inequality we used the monotonicity of the map
\tau\mapsto\tau\langle{\bm{v}},\Im G(E+\mathrm{i}\tau){\bm{w}}\rangle
, and that in the third inequality we used the local law from
[
15
]
*Corollary 2.4.

∎

Interpolation inequalities on the sphere and phase transition: rigidity, symmetry and symmetry breaking
Esther Bou Dagher, Jean Dolbeault

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the study of phase transitions associated to a large family of Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev interpolation inequalities on the sphere depending on one parameter. We characterize symmetry and symmetry breaking regimes, with a phase transition that can be of first or second order. We establish various new results and study the qualitative properties of the branches of solutions to the Euler-Lagrange equations.



keywords
Interpolation; Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities; best constants; branches of solutions; optimal functions; bifurcations; symmetry; symmetry breaking; uniqueness; rigidity; phase transition.

Introduction

On the
d
-dimensional unit sphere, the classical
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev interpolation inequality
,

\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{d}{p-2}\left(\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)

(GNS)

for any
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
, was proved with optimal constant by M.-F. Bidaut-Véron and L. Véron
[
5
]
, and also by W. Beckner
[
4
]
using another method, for
p>2
. The case
p<2
and some cases with
p>2
are covered by the
carré du champ
method coupled with the heat flow
[
1
,
2
,
27
]
. Here
d\geq 1
is an integer,
d\sigma=\big|\mathbb{S}^{d}\big|^{-1}\,dv_{g}
denotes the uniform probability measure on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, and
dv_{g}
can be seen as the measure induced on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
by Lebesgue’s measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
. The space
\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
with
q\in[1,+\infty)
is the standard Lebesgue space with norm

\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{q}}\,d\sigma\right)^{1/q}.

The fact that
d\sigma
is a probability measure means that
\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
if
u=1
a.e. on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
and we also have
\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{1}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}\leq\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{2}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}
for any
u\in\mathrm{L}^{q_{2}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
as soon as
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}
. The space
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
is obtained by completion of the space of smooth functions on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
with respect to the norm
u\mapsto\big(\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\big)^{1/2}
. Inequality (
GNS
) holds in the range

p\in[1,2)\cup(2,\infty)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd=1,\,2\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptp\in[1,2)\cup(2,2^{*}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd\geq 3\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt2^{*}:=\frac{2\,d}{d-2}\,.

By convention, we take
2^{*}=+\infty
if
d=1
,
2
. The
carré du champ
method has been extended to nonlinear diffusion flows
[
13
,
24
]
in order to cover the whole range of admissible exponents on manifolds with positive curvature, with applications to the sphere
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. Inequality (
GNS
) can be considered as a special case of the family of interpolation inequalities in
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)

\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}

(1)

which depends on a positive parameter
\lambda
, with optimal constant
\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)
. One can indeed check that

d=\max\left\{\lambda>0\,:\,\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)=\lambda\right\}\,.

By construction, the function
\lambda\mapsto\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)
is monotone non-decreasing and concave. Branches of critical functions, parametrized by
\lambda
, which realize the equality case in (
1
), have been characterized by the
carré du champ
method
[
22
,
27
,
18
]
. In this paper, we investigate the larger family of inequalities

\left(\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{\theta}\Bigg(\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\Bigg)^{1-\theta}\geq\left(\frac{\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)}{p-2}\right)^{\theta}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,,

(2)

for any
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
, where
\theta>0
and
\lambda>0
are two parameters and
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
is the corresponding optimal constant. Depending on the value of
\theta
, we observe several scenarios of
phase transition
. The case
\theta=1
corresponds to (
1
) and the range
\theta>1
is admissible. The range
\theta<1
deserves some additional care, as there is another limitation,
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
, where

\theta_{\star}:=d\,\frac{p-2}{2\,p}

is the exponent in the
Euclidean Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality

\|{\nabla f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\theta_{\star}}\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1-\theta_{\star}}\geq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},dx)\,.

(3)

Our purpose is to study
\lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
and characterize whenever possible the optimal functions. Let us define the
Bakry-Emery exponent

2^{\#}
, the parameters
\gamma^{\#}
and
\theta^{\#}
, such that
2<2^{\#}\leq 2^{*}
and
\theta_{\star}<\theta^{\#}<1
, by

\displaystyle 2^{\#}:=\frac{2\,d^{2}+1}{(d-1)^{2}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma^{\#}:=\left(\frac{d-1}{d+2}\right)^{2}(p-1)\,(2^{\#}-p)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\,\theta^{\#}:=\left(\frac{\gamma^{\#}}{p-2}+1\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd\geq 2\,,

\displaystyle 2^{\#}=+\infty\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma^{\#}=\frac{p-1}{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\,\theta^{\#}:=3\,\frac{p-2}{4\,p-7}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd=1\,.

Inequality (
2
) is known, for any
p\in(2,2^{\#})
but only for
\theta=\theta^{\#}
, from Inequality (2.4) of
[
18
]
. This inequality arises as a consequence of improvements of (
1
) based on the
carré du champ
method, written in the form

\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{d\,\theta^{\#}}{p-2}\left(\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2/\theta^{\#}}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2\,(\theta^{\#}-1)/\theta^{\#}}-\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)

(4)

for any
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
. Our first result deals with Inequality (
2
) and properties of
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
p\in(2,+\infty)
if
d=1
,
2
,
p\in(2,2^{*}]
if
d\geq 3
, and
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
. Inequality (
2
) holds for some optimal
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)>0
, for any
\lambda>0
and equality is achieved if
p<2^{*}
and
\theta>\theta_{\star}
. The function
\lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
is monotone non-decreasing, monotone increasing if
\theta>\theta_{\star}
, concave and such that, for any
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)\leq\lambda\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)<\lambda\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda>d\,\theta\,,

\displaystyle\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=\lambda\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\leq d\,\theta\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\theta\geq\theta^{\#}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptp\in(2,2^{\#}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd\geq 2\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{or}\hskip 10.00002ptp>2\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd=1\,.

Moreover, for any
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
, there is some explicit
\kappa>0
such that

\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)\sim\kappa\,\lambda^{1-\theta_{\star}/\theta}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\to+\infty\,.

(5)

Whenever
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=\lambda
, constant functions realize the equality case in (
2
). If these constant functions are the only optimal functions, we shall say that there is
symmetry
because none of the optimal functions points in a privileged direction. The equality case is achieved by non-constant functions if
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)<\lambda
, in which case rotations and multiplications by a constant generate a
d
-dimensional set of optimal functions. In that case, we shall say that there is
symmetry breaking
. The key issue is to understand for which value of
\lambda
the
phase transition
from a regime with symmetry to a regime of symmetry breaking occurs, that is, to determine

\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta):=\inf\big\{\lambda>0\,:\,\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)<\lambda\big\}\,.

We read from Theorem
1.1
that
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta)\leq d\,\theta
, and
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta)=d\,\theta
if
\theta\geq\theta^{\#}
and
p\in(2,2^{\#})
. Our second statement goes as follows. Explicit estimates of
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}
will be given later, but the general picture goes as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
p\in(2,+\infty)
if
d=1
,
2
,
p\in(2,2^{*}]
if
d\geq 3
and
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
. Then
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta)
is positive and
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta)<d\,\theta
if
\theta-\theta_{\star}\geq 0
is taken small enough and
p<p_{\ast}
for some
p_{\ast}>2
.

Inequalities (
2
) provide us with a simple model of nonlinear
phase transition
, which fits into the
Ehrenfest classification

[
36
]
. For given values of
p
and
\theta
, the transition from a regime with symmetry to a regime of symmetry breaking is driven by the parameter
\lambda
. The curve
\lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
can either be smooth (
second order phase transition
), or give rise to a jump in the derivative with respect to
\lambda
(
first order phase transition
). It turns out that a detailed description can be obtained in the simplest case,
d=1
, which is particularly enlightening: see Section
5
for details and also Section
6
for various numerical illustrations. As we shall see with less details, similar patterns are also present in higher dimensions.

A key tool for the analysis of (
1
) is a generalized
carré du champ
method which amounts to evolve the
deficit
in (
1
), that is, the difference of the two sides of the inequality, by a nonlinear diffusion equation. This allows us to reduce the issue of the optimality from a global nonlinear variational problem to a local problem that can be studied with spectral tools. Such a strategy enters in the class of the
entropy methods
. From the point of view of physics, the evolution equation that we use is a diffusion at fixed temperature and the notion of (generalized) entropy is in fact a
free energy
.

The
symmetry versus symmetry breaking
issue in (
2
) provides us with a rather simple mathematical model, in which both types of phase transition occur depending on the choice of the parameter
\theta
. Similar results on phase transitions have been observed in

[

,
11
]
, a well known example of functional inequalities in which symmetry breaking plays an important role, but which also raises various technical difficulties and is for this reason less understood
[

,
7
]
. The sharp threshold for the symmetry range of the parameters in such inequalities is known in the critical case
[
34
,
26
]
and in the subcritical case
[
6
,
29
]
, under a condition which corresponds to
\theta=1
in (
2
). However, a complete parabolic proof based on entropy methods is so far missing and results rely on

results: see
[

,
33
,
7
]
for partial results based on flows. The analogy between our interpolation inequalities on the sphere and Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities can be pushed further, as one can study branches of critical functions as in (
1
), see for instance
[

,
30
,
15
]
. There is also a family of inequalities involving a parameter
\theta<1
as in (
2
) which has several qualitative properties
[

,
31
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
that are similar to the properties that we can observe on the sphere. However Inequality (
2
) provides us with a much more detailed example of the phenomenon of phase transition, especially in dimension

, in a simple functional framework. Moreover, the regularity of the solution of the nonlinear flow on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
raises no difficulty

]
(to be compared with

2.2
Stereographic projection, scalings and interpolation inequalities

Let us explain how we define and use the stereographic projection. On
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\ni x
, let
r=|x|
and
\omega=x/|x|
denote spherical coordinates. On the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, we consider cylindrical coordinates
(\rho\,\omega,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times(-1,1)
with
\rho^{2}+z^{2}=1
. The
stereographic projection

\mathsf{S}:{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\setminus\{\mathsf{N}\}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
\mathsf{N}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
is the North Pole defined by
z=+1
, is such that
\mathsf{S}(\rho\,\omega,z)=r\,\omega=x
where

z=\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{2}+1}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\rho=\frac{2\,r}{1+r^{2}}\,.

If
v
is a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let us consider its counterpart
u:=\mathsf{S}^{-1}\,v
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
obtained using the inverse stereographic projection as

\left(u\circ\mathsf{S}^{-1}\right)(x)=\mathsf{m}(r)^{d-2}\,v(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{m}(r)=\sqrt{(1+r^{2})/2}\,.

For any
q\geq 1
, with
\delta(q):=2\,d-q\,(d-2)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{q}}\,d\sigma=\big|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\big|^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|v|^{q}}{\mathsf{m}(r)^{\delta(q)}}}\,dx\,,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\sigma+\frac{1}{4}\,d\,(d-2)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\sigma=\big|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\big|^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,dx\,.

Notice that
\delta(2)=4
, and
\delta(2^{*})=0
if
d\geq 3
. We refer to Theorem 2.1 of
[
18
]
for a statement concerning the inequality obtained from (
1
) and a special case of (
2
) by the stereographic projection.

Using the stereographic projection, we learn that

\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{-\frac{p-2}{p\,\theta}}\,\inf\,\frac{(p-2)\,\|{\nabla v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+4\,(\lambda-\mathsf{A})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|v|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\big(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\big)^{\delta/2}\,|v|^{p}}\,dx\right)^{2/(p\,\theta)}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{4\,|v|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx\right)^{1-1/\theta}}

where the infimum is taken on
\left\{v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d},(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}\,dx\big)\setminus\{0\}\,:\,\nabla v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},dx)\right\}
and
\delta=\delta(p)
. Either
v
is constant, in which case
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u]=\lambda
, or
\|{\nabla v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>0
, which we assume from now on. With the rescaling

v(x)=w\left(\frac{x}{\tau}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\tau=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda-\mathsf{A}}}

for any
\lambda>\mathsf{A}
, we find that

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u]:=|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{-\frac{2\,\theta_{\star}}{d\,\theta}}\,\frac{(p-2)\,\|{\nabla v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+4\,(\lambda-\mathsf{A})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|v|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\big(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\big)^{\delta/2}\,|v|^{p}}\,dx\right)^{2/(p\,\theta)}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{4\,|v|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx\right)^{1-1/\theta}}\\
=\left(\frac{\tau}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{1/d}}\right)^{\frac{2\,\theta_{\star}}{\theta}}\,\frac{\tau^{-2}\,(p-2)\,\|{\nabla w}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+4\,(\lambda-\mathsf{A})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|w|^{2}}{(1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\big(\frac{2}{1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2}}\big)^{\delta/2}\,|w|^{p}}\,dx\right)^{2/(p\,\theta)}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{4\,|w|^{2}}{(1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx\right)^{1-1/\theta}}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=\frac{(\lambda-\mathsf{A})^{1-\theta_{\star}/\theta}}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{2\,\theta_{\star}/(d\,\theta)}}\,\frac{(p-2)\,\|{\nabla w}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+4\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|w|^{2}}{(1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\big(\frac{2}{1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2}}\big)^{\delta/2}\,|w|^{p}}\,dx\right)^{2/(p\,\theta)}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{4\,|w|^{2}}{(1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx\right)^{1-1/\theta}}\,.

Assuming that
X
and
Y
are positive numbers and using the identity

\left(\frac{X+Y}{Y^{1-1/\theta}}\right)^{\theta}\geq\mathsf{c}_{\theta}\,X^{\theta_{\star}}\,Y^{1-\theta_{\star}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{c}_{\theta}:=\frac{\theta^{\theta}}{\theta_{\star}^{\,\theta_{\star}}\,\,(\theta-\theta_{\star})^{\theta-\theta_{\star}}}

(18)

with

and

, we obtain

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u]\geq\frac{(p-2)^{\theta_{\star}/\theta}\,(\lambda-\mathsf{A})^{1-\theta_{\star}/\theta}}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{2\,\theta_{\star}/(d\,\theta)}}\left(c_{\theta}\,\frac{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{\theta_{\star}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|w|^{2}}{(1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2})^{2}}}\,dx\right)^{1-\theta_{\star}}}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\big(\frac{|w|^{p}}{(1+\tau^{2}\,|x|^{2})^{\delta/2}}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}}\right)^{1/\theta}\,.

By yet another scaling, we can get rid of

and find that, after minimization,

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u]\geq\kappa_{2}(p,\theta)\,(\lambda-\mathsf{A})^{1-\theta_{\star}/\theta}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\kappa_{2}(p,\theta):=(p-2)^{\theta_{\star}/\theta}\,{c_{\theta}^{1/\theta}}\left(\tfrac{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{\theta_{\star}/d}}\right)^{2/\theta}\,,

(19)

where

is the optimal constant in (

).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
d\geq 1
. If

,

if

2.3
Existence of an optimal function

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

,
p\in(2,+\infty)
if

,
2
,
p\in(2,2^{*}]
if

\theta>\theta_{\star}
. For any

, there is an optimal function for (
2

, or

Proof 2.10
.

If
d\geq 3
, Hölder’s inequality (
9
) applies with
\theta=\theta_{\star}
and
q(p,\theta_{\star})=2^{*}
. Combined with (
GNS
) written with

, we obtain

\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}\leq\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{\theta_{\star}}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{1-\theta_{\star}}\leq\left(\frac{4}{d\,(d-2)}\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{\theta_{\star}/2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{1-\theta_{\star}},

and, as a consequence, using (
6
),

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u]\geq\frac{(p-2)\,\nu+\lambda}{\left(\frac{4}{d\,(d-2)}\,\nu+1\right)^{\theta_{\star}/\theta}}=:\mathsf{q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[\nu]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\nu=\nu[u]:=\frac{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\,.

The existence of an optimal function follows by a standard minimization argument. If
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a minimizing sequence for
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\|{u_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
by homogeneity of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}
. Using

, it follows from the above estimate that

is bounded, thus proving that
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

up to the extraction of a subsequence to some

. From the compactness of the embedding

, we conclude that

is a minimizer of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}
. In dimension

.

Notice that an optimization of

with respect to

gives an explicit lower estimate of

which is similar to the optimization on
\mathsf{x}
in the proof of Lemma

.

2.4
Symmetry breaking

We obtain an estimate of the symmetry breaking range by Taylor expanding around a constant perturbed by a spherical harmonic function.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
p\in(2,2^{*})
if
d=1
,
2
,
p\in(2,2^{*}]
if

and

. There is

in (
2
), that is,

, if
\lambda>d\,\theta

Proof 2.12
.

Let us consider

with
\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\varphi}\,d\sigma=0
as a test function:

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\,\|{\nabla\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\left\|{u_{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=1+\varepsilon^{2}\,\|{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,,

As a consequence, we obtain that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u_{\varepsilon}]-\lambda}{p-2}=\|{\nabla\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\theta}\,\|{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\left(d-\frac{\lambda}{\theta}\right)\|{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}<0

\varphi

and
\lambda>d\,\theta
.

2.5

Using a simple monotonicity observation, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
d\geq 1
,

in (
2

Proof 2.14
.

We start by the following observation. Let

\mathsf{f}
on

defined by

It is elementary to compute
-\,\mathsf{f}^{\prime}(\theta)=X^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\,\log(X^{\frac{1}{\theta}})+1-X^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\geq 0
.

Let

. If

, we consider two cases:

and we read from (

(2) Or
\theta\in(\theta^{\#},1)
and we know from (
4

\displaystyle\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\;\frac{d}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,\mathsf{f}(\theta^{\#})

\displaystyle\geq\;\frac{d}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,\mathsf{f}(\theta)=\frac{d\,\theta}{p-2}\left(\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{-2\,\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}-\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,.

\displaystyle\;\frac{d\,\theta}{p-2}\left(\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{-2\,\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}-\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)

\varepsilon>0

\left(\mathsf{A}\,\varepsilon^{-\mathsf{a}}+\frac{\lambda\,\mathsf{B}}{p-2}\,\varepsilon^{\mathsf{b}}\right)^{\theta}=c_{\theta}\left(\frac{\lambda}{p-2}\right)^{\theta-\theta_{\star}}\,\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2\,\theta_{\star}}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2\,(1-\theta_{\star})}\geq c_{\theta}\left(\frac{\lambda}{p-2}\right)^{\theta-\theta_{\star}}\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)^{2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}

(21)

using (
3
). Taking
\lambda=p-2
, we find that
\mathcal{K}_{p,d,\theta}\geq c_{\theta}^{1/\theta}\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}(p)}^{2/\theta}
. There is in fact equality because we can choose for
u
an optimal function for (
3
). The case
\theta=\theta_{\star}
is obtained as a limit case using
\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to\theta_{\star}}c_{\theta}=1
.

The above computation is also interesting if
\lambda\neq p-2
. On
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, in the limit as
\lambda\to+\infty
, so that
\varepsilon\to 0
, heuristics are simple as soon as we know that optimal functions concentrate: the behaviour of
\lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
as
\lambda\to+\infty
is determined by the interpolation problem on the tangent hyperplane to the sphere at the point of concentration,
i.e.
, by (
20
), up to the normalization factor in the definition of
d\sigma
. This was proved for
\theta=1
in
[
22
]
. By taking for
u
an approximation of the optimal function for (
3
) with compact support in a small ball, which can be done because of the scaling invariance of (
3
), we can guess from (
21
) that
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=O(\lambda^{\theta-\theta_{\star}})
as
\lambda\to+\infty
. The purpose of the following result is to extend the results for (
1
) of Proposition 9 and 10 of
[
22
]
to Inequality (
2
) with
\theta\neq 1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
on
d
,
p
and
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
, we have

\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=\kappa(p,\theta)\,\lambda^{1-\theta_{\star}/\theta}\,\big(1+o(1)\big)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\to+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\kappa(p,\theta)=\left(\tfrac{p-2}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{2/d}}\right)^{\theta_{\star}/\theta}\mathcal{K}_{p,d,\theta}\,.

Proof 3.4
.

We do a
blow-up analysis
based on a change of variables depending on
\lambda\to+\infty
. As in Section
2.2
, let
\mathsf{m}(r)=\sqrt{(1+r^{2})/2}
,
\delta(p)=2\,d-p\,(d-2)
and
u_{\lambda}:=\mathsf{S}^{-1}\,v_{\lambda}
where

v_{\lambda}(x):=v\left(\frac{x}{\tau}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\tau=\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{\frac{p-2}{\lambda-\overline{\lambda}}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\overline{\lambda}:=(p-2)\,\mathsf{A}=\frac{1}{4}\,d\,(d-2)\,(p-2)\,.

We take
v
as an optimal function for (
20
). With
r=|x|
and
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}
defined in (
6
), we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u_{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-2)\left(\|{\nabla u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,d\,(d-2)\,\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)+(\lambda-\overline{\lambda})\,\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\,\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2-\frac{2}{\theta}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-2)\,\|{\nabla v_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+(\lambda-\overline{\lambda})\,\|{\mathsf{m}(r)^{-2}\,v_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{\frac{1}{\theta}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\,\|{\mathsf{m}(r)^{-\frac{\delta(p)}{p}}\,v_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\,\|{\mathsf{m}(r)^{-2}\,v_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2-\frac{2}{\theta}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda-\overline{\lambda})^{1-\frac{\theta_{\star}}{\theta}}\,(p-2)^{\frac{\theta_{\star}}{\theta}}\,\left(\|{\nabla v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,\|{\mathsf{m}(\tau\,r)^{-2}\,v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\right)}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{\frac{1}{\theta}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\,2^{-\frac{\delta(p)}{p\,\theta}-2\left(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\right)}\,\|{\mathsf{m}(\tau\,r)^{-\frac{\delta(p)}{p}}\,v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\,\|{\mathsf{m}(\tau\,r)^{-2}\,v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2-\frac{2}{\theta}}}\,.

By taking the limit as
\lambda\to+\infty
and using Lebesgue’s theorem of dominated convergence with
\mathsf{m}(\tau\,r)\to 1/\sqrt{2}
a.e., and using the fact that the quotient becomes proportional to the quotient that defines the constant in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality up to a numerical constant, we conclude that

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u_{\lambda}]=(\lambda-\overline{\lambda})^{1-\theta_{\star}/\theta}\kappa(p,\theta)\big(1+o(1)\big)

as
\lambda\to+\infty
, for a constant
\kappa(p,\theta)
that can be computed explicitly, up to tedious but elementary considerations. Consistently with Proposition
2.7
(we notice that
\kappa(p,\theta)=\kappa_{2}(p,\theta)
in (
19
)), this proves that

\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\lambda^{\theta_{\star}/\theta-1}\,\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)\leq\kappa(p,\theta).

We have now to prove the reverse inequality. As in Proposition 10 of
[
22
]
, we argue by contradiction. Let
({\lambda}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
({u}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be such that
u_{n}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
with
\|{u_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}=+\infty
,

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda_{n}}[u_{n}]=\mu(p,\theta,\lambda_{n})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\theta_{\star}/\theta-1}\,\mu(p,\theta,\lambda_{n})\leq\kappa(p,\theta)-\eta

(22)

for some
\eta>0
. We learn from the expression of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}
that
\lambda_{n}\,\|{u_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2/\theta}\leq\kappa(p,\theta)\,\lambda_{n}^{1-\theta_{\star}/\,\theta}
as
n\to+\infty
so that
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|{u_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=0
. Concentration occurs: there exists a sequence
({y}_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of points in
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
with
i\in I\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, sequences of positive numbers
({\zeta}_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(r_{i,n})_{i,n\in I\times{\mathbb{N}}}
and functions
u_{i,n}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\sigma)
with

u_{i,n}=u_{n}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on}\hskip 10.00002pt{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\cap B(y_{i},r_{i,n})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{supp }u_{i,n}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\cap B(y_{i},2\,r_{i,n})

such that, as
n\to+\infty
,

\sum_{i\in I}\|{\nabla u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\sim\|{\nabla u_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{i\in I}\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\sim\|{u_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}

with, for all
i\in I
,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}r_{i,n}=0\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\,\zeta_{i}=1\ \hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\cap B(y_{i},r_{i,n})}|u_{i,n}|^{p}\,d\sigma=\zeta_{i}\,.

With a
blow up
argument applied to
(u_{i,n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, we prove for all
i\in I
that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{\theta_{\star}}{\theta}-1}\left((p-2)\,\|{\nabla u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\lambda_{n}\,\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{-2-\frac{2}{\theta}}\geq\kappa(p,\theta)\,\zeta_{i}^{\frac{2}{p\,\theta}}\,.

We may notice that

\frac{(p-2)\,\|{\nabla u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\lambda_{n}\,\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2-\frac{2}{\theta}}}\geq\frac{(p-2)\,\|{\nabla u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\lambda_{n}\,\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2-\frac{2}{\theta}}}\,.

Let us choose an integer
N
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\zeta_{i}^{\frac{2}{p\,\theta}}\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\zeta_{i}\right)^{\frac{2}{p\,\theta}}>1-\frac{\eta}{2\,\kappa(p,\theta)}\,.

For
n
large enough, by writing

\lambda_{n}^{\frac{\theta_{\star}}{\theta}-1}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(p-2)\,\|{\nabla u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\lambda_{n}\,\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u_{i,n}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2-\frac{2}{\theta}}}\geq\kappa(p,\theta)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\zeta_{i}^{\frac{2}{p\,\theta}}\,\big(1+o(1)\big)\geq\kappa(p,\theta)-\frac{\eta}{2}\,,

we obtain a contradiction with (
22
). This concludes the proof.

We learn from Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
that

\mu(p,\theta_{\star},\lambda)\leq\kappa(p,\theta_{\star})=\tfrac{p-2}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{2/d}}\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)^{2/\theta_{\star}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\lambda>0\,.

(23)

This estimate is very useful. Taking into account various other results, here is one of the consequences.

Corollary 3.5
.

With the notation of Theorem
1.2
, we have
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta)\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})
for any
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
and

\displaystyle(p-2)\,\mathsf{A}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})\leq\kappa(p,\theta_{\star})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd\geq 3\,,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})=\kappa(p,\theta_{\star})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002ptd=1\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{or}\hskip 10.00002ptd=2\,.

Proof 3.6
.

By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, using the monotonicity of
\theta\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
, we know that
\mu(p,\theta_{\star},\lambda)\leq\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)\leq\lambda
if
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
. This proves
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta)\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})
. If
d\geq 3
, the lower estimate on
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})
follows from (
16
), while the upper estimate
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})\leq\kappa(p,\theta_{\star})
is a consequence of (
23
). If
d=1
or
d=2
,
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})=\kappa(p,\theta_{\star})
is deduced from Proposition
2.7
.

Remark 3.7
.

In view of Corollary
3.5
, one can conjecture that
\lambda_{\mathrm{T}}(p,\theta_{\star})=\kappa(p,\theta_{\star})
if
d\geq 3
, as it is the case if
d=1
or
d=2
. One the other hand, one may wonder if Lemma
3.3
is compatible with (
15
), because for a given
p
and a given
\theta
,
\lambda/\Lambda=(p-2)\,(d-2\,\theta)/4\,\theta
is independent of
\Lambda>0
. We know by (
23
) that
\mu(p,\theta_{\star},\lambda)
is bounded uniformly with respect to

, while
\mu\left(q,\theta,\Lambda\right)\sim\frac{4\,\theta\,\kappa(q,\theta)}{(p-2)\,(d-2\,\theta)}\,\lambda^{1-\theta_{\star}(q)/\theta}\to+\infty
as

if

. There is no paradox because we obtain

if we take

.

3.2
Reparametrization

As in
[
9
,
12
,
31
,
16
,
17
,

]
,
the set of optimal functions for (
6
) for
\theta\neq 1
can be parametrized by the critical points of the problem corresponding to
\theta=1
. The precise statement goes as follows.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
p\in(2,2^{*})
,
\theta\geq\theta_{\star}
and
\Lambda>0
, given. If
u
is an optimal function for (
6
), then, up to a multiplicative constant, the function
u
solves

-\,\Delta u+\frac{\lambda}{p-2}\,u=u^{p-1}

(24)

with

\lambda=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\Lambda+(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\tfrac{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{p}=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\Lambda}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,.

(25)

Proof 3.9
.

Any optimal function
u
for (
6
) is such that

\left(\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\Lambda}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{\theta}\Bigg(\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\Bigg)^{1-\theta}=\left(\frac{\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)}{p-2}\right)^{\theta}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}

solves the Euler-Lagrange equation

\frac{\theta\,\big(-\,\Delta u+\frac{\Lambda}{p-2}\,u\big)}{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\Lambda}{p-2}\,\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}+\frac{(1-\theta)\,u}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}=\frac{u^{p-1}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{p}}\,,

(26)

that is, Equation (
24
) with
\lambda
and the multiplicative constant given by (
25
).

An interesting consequence is the fact that the curve
\Lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)
can be parametrized by
\lambda
using

\Lambda=\theta\,\lambda-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\frac{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)=\theta\left(\lambda+(p-2)\,\frac{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}}\right)^{2/\theta}\,,

where
u
is as in the above proof, because

\left((p-2)\,\frac{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}+\Lambda\right)^{\theta}\,\frac{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}=\big(\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)\big)^{\theta}\;\mbox{and}\;(p-2)\,\frac{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}+\Lambda=\theta\left(\lambda+(p-2)\,\frac{\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right).

However, it is not
a priori
granted that any positive non-constant solution to (
24
) gives rise to an optimal function for (
6
). We recall that
\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)=\mu(p,1,\lambda)
. Since Proposition
3.8
applies to
\theta=1
, in that case an optimal function solves (
24
), up to a multiplicative constant. Let us assume that there is some continuous branch
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
of optimal functions in the case
\theta=1
, which solve (
24
). Assume moreover that there is no other non-constant solution of (
24
). Then

\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)=(p-2)\,\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{p-2}

and we can introduce

\nu(p,\lambda):=\frac{\|{\nabla u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\,.

Here we assume that
\lambda
is in some interval
\mathcal{I}
. By testing (
24
) with
u_{\lambda}
, we know that

\lambda+(p-2)\,\nu(p,\lambda)=\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)\,\frac{\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}

and learn that the curve
\Lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)
is contained in the set

\Big\{\big(\Lambda(p,\theta,\lambda),M(p,\theta,\lambda)\big)\,:\,\lambda\in\mathcal{I}\Big\}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Lambda(p,\theta,\lambda):=\theta\,\lambda-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\nu(p,\lambda)\,,\\[6.0pt]
M(p,\theta,\lambda):=\theta\,\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)^{1/\theta}\left(\lambda+(p-2)\,\nu(p,\lambda)\right)^{1-1/\theta}\,.\end{array}\right.

(27)

Remark 3.10
.

If we consider the bifurcation from constant functions, which occurs for
\theta=1
at
\lambda=\mu=d
, then by considering the corresponding branch
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
of positive non-constant solutions to (
24
), we may notice that
\lim_{\lambda\to d_{+}}\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)=d
,
\lim_{\lambda\to d_{+}}\nu(p,\lambda)=0
and, as a consequence

\lim_{\lambda\to d_{+}}\Lambda(p,\theta,\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\to d_{+}}M(p,\theta,\lambda)=d\,\theta\,.

(28)

The reparametrization (
27
) is also consistent with Lemma
3.3
as
\lambda\to+\infty
. From the case
\theta=1
, we know that
\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)\sim\kappa(p,1)\,\lambda^{1-\theta_{\star}}
so that
\nu(p,\lambda)\sim c\,\lambda
for some

and recover that
\Lambda(p,\theta,\lambda)\sim\kappa(p,\theta)\,M(p,\theta,\lambda)^{1-\theta_{\star}/\theta}
as

using
\big(\kappa(p,\theta)\big)^{\theta}=\kappa(p,1)\,\theta^{\theta}/(\theta-\theta_{\star})^{\theta-\theta_{\star}}
.

The case
d=1
is of particular interest as all positive non-constant solutions of (

) are known, exist only in
\mathcal{I}=(d,+\infty)
and are uniquely for any given
\lambda\in\mathcal{I}
. However, for a given value of
\Lambda
, (

or up to a rotation in dimension

. Hence the reparametrization in terms of

has to be reserved for numerical computations or a local analysis of the branches and
\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)
can be estimated only after optimizing
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\lambda}[u]
on all admissible solutions
u
of (

).

3.3
Comparison with inequalities on the Euclidean space

We investigate the regime of
\theta
close to
\theta_{\star}
and prove that for
p>2
but not too large, symmetry breaking occurs for
\lambda<d\,\theta
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
d\geq 1
. There is some
p_{\ast}\in(2,2^{*})
such that for any
p\in(2,p_{\ast})
and any
\theta\in(\theta_{\star},\theta_{\star}+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon(p)>0
, we have

\frac{\mu(p,\theta,d\,\theta)}{d\,\theta}<1\,.

Proof 3.12
.

Taking the logarithm of both sides in (
3
), we have

\frac{\theta_{\star}}{2}\,\log\left(\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\right)+\frac{1-\theta_{\star}}{2}\,\log\left(\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\geq\log\left(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)\right)+\frac{1}{p}\,\log\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|f|^{p}}\,dx\right)\,.

This inequality for
p>2
becomes an equality at
p=2
, with
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(2)=1
. Using
\theta_{\star}^{\prime}(2)=d/4
where
′
denotes the derivative with respect to
p
, we obtain

\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{\|{\nabla f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{2}{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|f|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|f|^{2}}{\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,dx+\frac{8}{d}\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}^{\prime}(2)\,\|{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}

by taking the derivative with respect to
p
at
p=2
, which the logarithmic Sobolev in scale invariant form with optimal constant: see for instance Theorem 2 of
[
40
]
, Inequality (2.3) of
[
38
]
or Inequality (26) of
[
10
]
. Hence we deduce that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}^{\prime}(2)=\frac{d}{8}\,\log(\pi\,d\,e/2)
. Altogether, this means that

\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\frac{\mu(p,\theta_{\star},\lambda)}{d\,\theta_{\star}}\sim\frac{2\,\pi\,e}{d\,|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{2/d}}=\frac{2\,e}{d}\left(\frac{\Gamma\big(\tfrac{d+1}{2}\big)}{2\,\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^{2/d}\,.

As a function of
d
, the right-hand side is increasing with limit
1
as
d\to+\infty
according to Stirling’s formula, which proves that

\lim_{p\to 2^{+}}\frac{\mu(p,\theta_{\star},\lambda)}{d\,\theta_{\star}}<1\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,(d,\lambda)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times(0,+\infty)\,.

(29)

We apply (
29
) with
\lambda=d\,\theta
and use the continuity of
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
first with respect to
p
and then with respect to
\theta
, as a consequence of the reparametrization.

To get an upper estimate of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)
in an arbitrary dimension
d\geq 1
, we can use concentrating Gaussian test functions. Using (
23
), we obtain

\mu(p,\theta_{\star},\lambda)\leq\kappa(p,\theta_{\star})=\tfrac{p-2}{|{\mathbb{S}}^{d}|^{2/d}}\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)^{2/\theta_{\star}}\leq\mathsf{g}(p,d):=\tfrac{d\,(p-2)\,p^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}{2^{\frac{(d+2)\,p-4}{d\,(p-2)}}\,\pi^{\frac{1}{d}}}\,\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d+1}{2}\right)^{2/d}

(30)

for any
\lambda>0
. The condition
\mathsf{g}(p,d)<d\,\theta_{\star}
shows that for
\theta-\theta_{\star}>0
small, symmetry breaking occurs for
\lambda<d\,\theta
. Checking the condition
\mathsf{g}(p,d)<d\,\theta_{\star}
is easy to implement numerically. Solving
\mathsf{g}(p,d)=d\,\theta_{\star}
gives a lower estimate on
p_{\ast}
. See Section
6.6
for more details.

3.4
Consequences for the main results

With the estimates of Section
3
, we can complete the proofs of Section
2.6
.

Proof of Theorem

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

,
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)<0
,

\mathsf{s}^{\prime}(\lambda)>0

Proof 4.2
.

The proof relies on a formal Taylor expansion in which we neglect higher order terms without justification, based on our analyticity assumption. Let us start by introducing the lowest spherical harmonic function that are needed to obtain the leading order terms.

On the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, we consider the Laplace-Beltrami operator restricted to the functions depending only on the azimuthal angle
\zeta\in[0,\pi]

\mathcal{L}f:=(\sin\zeta)^{1-d}\,\frac{d}{d\zeta}\left((\sin\zeta)^{d-1}\,\frac{df}{d\zeta}\right)\,.

On
[0,\pi]
, we consider the probability measure

d\sigma_{\zeta}=\frac{1}{Z_{d}}\,(\sin\zeta)^{d-1}\,d\zeta\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002ptZ_{d}:=\int_{0}^{\pi}(\sin\zeta)^{d-1}\,d\zeta=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\;\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}\,.

The spherical harmonic functions

\varphi_{0}(\zeta):=1\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi_{1}(\zeta)=\sqrt{d+1}\,\cos\zeta\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{d+3}{2\,d}}\,(d+1)\,\left((\cos\zeta)^{2}-1\right)\,,

are normalised eigenfunctions in
\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,\pi],d\sigma_{\zeta})
of
\mathcal{L}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{0}=0
,
\lambda_{1}=d
,
\lambda_{2}=2\,d+2
such that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\varphi_{1}|^{4}}\,d\sigma=3\,\frac{d+1}{d+3}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\,\varphi_{2}}\,d\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{2\,d}{d+3}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\varphi_{i}|^{2}}\,d\sigma=1\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,i=0\,,\,1\,,\,2\,,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\varphi_{1}}\,d\sigma=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\varphi_{2}}\,d\sigma=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\varphi_{1}\,\varphi_{2}}\,d\sigma=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\varphi_{2}|^{2}\,\varphi_{1}}\,d\sigma=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\varphi_{1}^{3}}\,d\sigma=0\,.

According to Section
2.4
, the Taylor expansion of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\Lambda}[1+\varepsilon\,\varphi_{1}]
at order
\varepsilon^{2}
shows that
\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)<\Lambda
if
\Lambda>d\,\theta
, while
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\theta,\Lambda}[u]
is linearly stable at
u=1
if
\Lambda<d\,\theta
. Moreover, we know from (
28
) that
\Lambda(d)=M(d)=d\,\theta
, so that
\mu(p,\theta,d\,\theta)=d\,\theta
. In order to understand the bifurcation of
\Lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\Lambda)
at
\Lambda=d\,\theta
, we have to perform a Taylor expansion at higher order. The unique positive solution of (
24
) which is constant is
u=\big(\lambda/(p-2)\big)^{1/(p-2)}
and we consider the expansion with respect to
\lambda
in a neighbourhood of
\lambda=d
. Let

u_{\varepsilon}=\big(\tfrac{d}{p-2}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}\left(1+\varepsilon\,\mathsf{a}\,\varphi_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\,\mathsf{b}\,\varphi_{2}\right)\,.

(33)

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{d}{p-2}\big)^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}\,\|{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=d\,\mathsf{a}^{2}+2\,(d+1)\,\mathsf{b}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{d}{p-2}\big)^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}\,\|{u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=1+\mathsf{a}^{2}+\mathsf{b}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{d}{p-2}\big)^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}\,\|{u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=1+(p-1)\left(\mathsf{a}^{2}\,\varepsilon^{2}+\mathsf{c}\,\varepsilon^{4}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{c}:=\mathsf{b}^{2}+(p-2)\,\sqrt{\tfrac{2\,d}{d+3}}\,\mathsf{a}^{2}\,\mathsf{b}-\tfrac{(p-2)\,(d+p)}{2\,(d+3)}\,\mathsf{a}^{4}\,.

With these preliminaries in hand, our strategy can be decomposed into three steps and goes as follows.

1.

If
\theta=1
, we know that the curve
\mathcal{C}
bifurcates from
(\lambda,\mu)=(d,d)
as a concave function
\lambda\mapsto\mu(\lambda)
, to the right and below the line
\mu=\lambda
. We can compute an approximation on the lowest spherical harmonic function by minimizing
\mathcal{Q}_{p,1,\lambda}
with respect to the corresponding coefficients.

2.

We use the reparametrization of Section
3.2
to obtain a curve which bifurcates from
(\lambda,\mu)=(d\,\theta,d\,\theta)
.

3.

We discuss the behaviour of
\mathcal{C}
in the neighbourhood of the bifurcation point depending on
\theta
.

Step 1: the case
\theta=1
. In view of the Taylor expansion of Section
2.4
, let us take
\lambda=\lambda(\varepsilon):=d+\varepsilon^{2}
and consider
u_{\varepsilon}
given by (
33
). A minimization of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,1,\lambda(\varepsilon)}[u_{\varepsilon}]
with respect to
\mathsf{a}
and
\mathsf{b}
shows that

\mathcal{Q}_{p,1,\lambda(\varepsilon)}[u_{\varepsilon}]=\lambda(\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+2)\,(d+3)\,(p-2)}{2\,d\,(d+1)\,(p-1)\,(2\,d-p\,(d-2))}\,\varepsilon^{4}+o(\varepsilon^{4})

for the optimal choice of
\mathsf{a}
and
\mathsf{b}
given by

\mathsf{a}=\sqrt{\frac{(d+2)\,(d+3)}{d\,(d+1)\,(p-1)\,(2\,d-p\,(d-2))}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{b}=\frac{\sqrt{d\,(d+3)}}{\sqrt{2}\,(d+1)\,(2\,d-p\,(d-2))}\,.

Step 2: the reparametrization, the case
\theta\neq 1
. According to Section
3.2
and with the simplified notation
\mu(\lambda)=\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)
and
\nu(\lambda)=\nu(p,\lambda)
, we have

\nu\big(\lambda(\varepsilon)\big)=\frac{\|{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\|{u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}+o(\varepsilon^{4})=\alpha\,\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\beta\,\varepsilon^{4}+o(\varepsilon^{4})

(34)

where

\alpha=\frac{(d+2)\,(d+3)}{(d+1)\,(p-1)\,(2\,d-p\,(d-2))}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\beta=\frac{(d+3)\,\big(d^{2}\,(d+1)\,(p-1)^{2}-(d+2)^{2}\,(d+3)\big)}{d\,(d+1)^{2}\,(p-1)^{2}\,(2\,d-p\,(d-2))^{2}}\,,

and
\mu\big(\lambda(\varepsilon)\big)=\mathcal{Q}_{p,1,\lambda(\varepsilon)}[u_{\varepsilon}]
, that is,

\mu\big(\lambda(\varepsilon)\big)=d+\varepsilon^{2}-\gamma\,\varepsilon^{4}+o(\varepsilon^{4})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma=\frac{(d+2)\,(d+3)\,(p-2)}{2\,d\,(d+1)\,(p-1)\,(2\,d-p\,(d-2))}\,.

(35)

Step 3: discussion. In our simplified notation, the reparametrization (
27
) can be rewritten as

\Lambda(\lambda)=\theta\,\lambda-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\nu(\lambda)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptM(\lambda)=\theta\,\mu(\lambda)^{1/\theta}\left(\lambda+(p-2)\,\nu(\lambda)\right)^{1-1/\theta}\,.

From the analyticity assumption, the derivatives can be computed order by order in terms of
\varepsilon^{2}
and we obtain that

\Lambda^{\prime}(d)=\theta-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\alpha>0

if and only if
\theta>\theta_{0}
. By direct computation, we have

M^{\prime}(\lambda)=\mu^{\prime}(\lambda)\left(\frac{\lambda+(p-2)\,\nu(\lambda)}{\mu(\lambda)}\right)^{1-1/\theta}+(\theta-1)\left(\frac{\mu(\lambda)}{\lambda+(p-2)\,\nu(\lambda)}\right)^{1/\theta}\,\big(1+(p-2)\,\nu^{\prime}(\lambda)\big)

and for
\lambda=d

\mu(d)=d
, while we learn that
\nu(d)=0
and
\mu^{\prime}(d)=1
from (
34

35

M^{\prime}(d)=\theta-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\alpha=\Lambda^{\prime}(d)\,.

As a consequence, the slope
\mathsf{s}
of the curve
\mathcal{C}
defined by (
31

\mathsf{s}(d)=1\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002pt\theta\neq\theta_{0}\,.

If
\theta\neq\theta_{0}
, let us observe that

\mathsf{s}^{\prime}(d)=\frac{\mathsf{s}^{\prime}(d)}{\mathsf{s}(d)}=\frac{M^{\prime\prime}(d)}{M^{\prime}(d)}-\frac{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(d)}{\Lambda^{\prime}(d)}=\frac{M^{\prime\prime}(d)-\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(d)}{\Lambda^{\prime}(d)}

where

\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(d)=-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\beta\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptM^{\prime\prime}(d)=-\mu^{\prime\prime}(d)+(p-2)\,\frac{1-\theta}{d\,\theta}\left((p-2)\,\alpha^{2}-d\,\theta\,\beta\right)\,.

Altogether, we have the identity

\mathsf{s}^{\prime}(d)\,\Lambda^{\prime}(d)=-\,(p-2)^{2}\,\frac{1-\theta}{d\,\theta}\,\alpha^{2}

from which we deduce the concavity (resp. convexity) property of
\mathcal{C}
if
\theta>\theta_{0}
(resp.
\theta<\theta_{0}
).

|v^{\prime}|={\sqrt{2\left(E+\mathcal{V}(v)\right)}}

T(E)=2\int_{0}^{T(E)/2}dx=2\int_{I(E)}{\frac{dw}{\sqrt{2\left(E+\mathcal{V}(w)\right)}}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\,\pi}{|u|^{q}}\,d\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{p-2}}{2\,\pi\,\sqrt{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{T(E)}|v|^{q}\,dx=\frac{\sqrt{p-2}}{\pi\,\sqrt{\lambda}}\,J_{q}(E)\,,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\,\pi}{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2\,\pi\,\sqrt{p-2}}\int_{0}^{T(E)}|v^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\pi\,\sqrt{p-2}}\,K(E)\,,

with

According to

\lim_{E\to(-\,\theta_{\star})_{+}}T(E)=2\,\pi\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{E\to 0_{-}}T(E)=+\infty\,.

\overline{\lambda}
and
\overline{\mu}
as functions of
E
and write

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}(E)=(p-2)\left(\frac{T(E)}{2\,\pi}\right)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}(E)=\frac{(p-2)\int_{0}^{2\,\pi}{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\sigma+\overline{\lambda}(E)\int_{0}^{2\,\pi}{|u|^{2}}\,d\sigma}{\left(\int_{0}^{2\,\pi}{|u|^{p}}\,d\sigma\right)^{2/p}}=\overline{\lambda}(E)\left(\frac{\sqrt{p-2}}{\pi\,\sqrt{\overline{\lambda}(E)}}\right)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\frac{K(E)+J_{2}(E)}{J_{p}(E)^{2/p}}\,,

For later purpose, it is also convenient to define

\overline{\nu}(E):=\frac{\|{u^{\prime}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}=\frac{\overline{\lambda}(E)}{p-2}\,\frac{K(E)}{J_{2}(E)}\,.

Altogether, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}(E)=(p-2)\left(\frac{T(E)}{2\,\pi}\right)^{2}\,,

(40)

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}(E)=(p-2)\left(\frac{T(E)}{2\,\pi}\right)^{2}\,\left(\frac{T(E)}{2}\right)^{-\frac{p-2}{p}}\,\frac{K(E)+J_{2}(E)}{J_{p}(E)^{2/p}}\,,

\displaystyle\overline{\nu}(E)=\left(\frac{T(E)}{2\,\pi}\right)^{2}\,\frac{K(E)}{J_{2}(E)}\,.

Remark 5.1

Notice that

\overline{\mu}(E)=\big(\overline{\lambda}(E)\big)^{1-\theta_{\star}}\left(\frac{p-2}{\pi^{2}}\right)^{\theta_{\star}}\,\frac{K(E)+J_{2}(E)}{J_{p}(E)^{2/p}}\,.

(41)

Since
\lim_{E\to 0_{-}}T(E)=+\infty
and
J_{2}(E)
,
J_{p}(E)
and
K(E)
converge as
E\to 0_{+}
respectively to

J_{2}(0)=\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{p+2}{p-2}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\;\Gamma\big(\tfrac{p}{p-2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\tfrac{p}{p-2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptJ_{p}(0)=\frac{2\,p}{p+2}\,J_{2}(0)\,,\hskip 10.00002ptK(0)=\frac{p-2}{p+2}\,J_{2}(0)\,,

(42)

we recover the result of Lemma
3.3
in dimension
d=1
if
\theta=1
. For details on this computation, see for instance the proof of Lemma 3 in
[
12
]
.

\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)<\lambda
if
\lambda>d\,\theta
and
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=\lambda
if

,
\theta\geq\theta^{\#}
and

and
\theta^{\#}
is the corresponding exponent. If
d=1
, then
2^{\#}=+\infty
and
\theta^{\#}=3\,(p-2)/(4\,p-7)
. If
d\geq 2
, we also have
\mu(2^{\#},\theta,\lambda)=\lambda
if
\lambda\leq d\,\theta
and
\theta\geq\theta^{\#}
. If
p\in(2^{\#},2^{*}
and
d\geq 3
, or
p\in(2^{\#},+\infty)
if
d=2
, we also have
\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=\lambda
if
\lambda\leq d\,\theta
,
\theta\geq 1
. See Fig.
1
.

The function
\lambda\mapsto\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)
is monotone non-decreasing and concave. We distinguish two regimes:

\bullet

Symmetry:

\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)=\lambda
, the equality case in (
2
) is achieved only by constant functions,

\bullet

Symmetry breaking:

\mu(p,\theta,\lambda)<\lambda
, the equality case in (
2
) is not achieved by constant functions.

Our goal is to understand whether the
phase transition
from symmetry to symmetry breaking occurs at
\lambda=d\,\theta
and determines a
second order phase transition
, or for some
\lambda<d\,\theta
, which corresponds to a
first order phase transition
. Notice that a first order phase transition can occur also at
\lambda=d\,\theta
. In Fig.
1
, dark grey regions correspond to
\theta\geq\min\big\{1,\theta^{\#}\big\}
in which we have a second order phase transition.

6.2
The branch of positive non-constant solutions for
d=1

In dimension
d=1
, obtaining all positive non-constant solutions to (
36
) can be done by solving this Hamiltonian ODE using elliptic integrals involving the Hamiltonian energy
E
as a parameter, which parametrizes monotonically
\lambda=\overline{\lambda}(E)
while
\overline{\mu}(E)=\mu\big(p,1,\lambda(\theta,E)\big)
is obtained by computing
\overline{\mu}(E)=\overline{\lambda}(E)\,\|{u_{E}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{p-2}
where
u_{E}
denotes the
2\,\pi
-periodic solution of energy
E
. The curve
E\mapsto\big(\overline{\lambda}(E),\overline{\mu}(E)\big)
given by (
40
) represents all solutions corresponding to the Euler-Lagrange equations associated with (
2
) for
\theta=1
. If
d=1
but
\theta\neq 1
, all solutions corresponding to the Euler-Lagrange equations associated with (
2
) can be represented by a curve
E\mapsto\big(\lambda(\theta,E),\mu(\theta,E)\big)
given by (
44
). Typical patterns are shown in Fig.
2
. Depending on the values of
\theta
, the bifurcating curve goes either to the right (higher values of
\lambda
) or to the right. This first qualitative property is explained next.

6.3
The first bifurcation from constant solutions for
d=1

The deficit associated with (
2
) is linearly stable if
\lambda<d\,\theta
, and linearly unstable if
\lambda>d\,\theta
. As a consequence, a branch of non-constant solutions of the Euler equation bifurcates at
\lambda=d\,\theta
. The case of dimension
d=1
enters in the general setting of Section
4
, but one can give a simpler analysis in that case. Let us start with the case
\theta=1
. The simple
ansatz

u_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)=1+\mathsf{a}\,\varepsilon\,\sqrt{2}\,\cos\zeta+\mathsf{b}\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\sqrt{2}\,\cos(2\,\zeta)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\zeta\in[0,2\,\pi)\approx{\mathbb{S}}^{1}

allows us to describe the branch of positive non-constant solutions to (
24
) near the bifurcation point, at leading order for
\varepsilon>0
small, as a parametric curve in
(\lambda,\mu)
coordinates parametrized by
\varepsilon>0
such that

\lambda(\varepsilon)=1+\varepsilon^{2}\,.

This
ansatz
has been justified in Section
4
. Here we only collect the observations needed for numerical computations in dimension
d=1
. At formal level, we do the expansion of

\mu(\varepsilon):=\frac{(p-2)\,\|{u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}+\lambda(\varepsilon)\,\|{u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}{\|{u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}

and simply observe that
\lambda(\varepsilon)-\mu(\varepsilon)
achieves its maximum for

\mathsf{a}=\frac{6}{(p-1)\,(p+2)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{b}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,(p+2)}\,,

in which case we obtain

\mu(\varepsilon)=1+\varepsilon^{2}-\gamma\,\varepsilon^{4}+o\big(\varepsilon^{4}\big)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma=\frac{3\,(p-2)}{(p-1)\,(p+2)}\,.

Notice that we can eliminate
\varepsilon^{2}=\lambda-1
and find that
\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)=\lambda-\gamma\,(\lambda-1)^{2}+o\big((\lambda-1)^{2}\big)
,
i.e.
, the parabola which interpolates
\lambda\mapsto\overline{\mu}(p,\lambda)
at the bifurcation point. With a similar computation, we can compute

\nu(\varepsilon):=\frac{\|{u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}{\|{u_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}=\alpha\,\varepsilon^{2}+\beta\,\varepsilon^{4}+o\big(\varepsilon^{4}\big)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha=\frac{6}{(p-1)\,(p+2)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\beta=\frac{2\,(p^{2}-2\,p-17)}{(p-1)^{2}\,(p+2)^{2}}\,.

So far, these computations are done in the case
\theta=1
. If
\theta\neq 1
, we can use them to get an approximation of
\lambda(\theta,E)
and
\mu(\theta,E)
using (
44
). For
\varepsilon>0
small, we obtain the reparametrization

\displaystyle\lambda=\theta\,(1+\varepsilon^{2})-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\left(\alpha\,\varepsilon^{2}+\beta\,\varepsilon^{4}\right)+o\big(\varepsilon^{4}\big)\,,

\displaystyle\mu=\theta\left((p-2)\left(\alpha\,\varepsilon^{2}+\beta\,\varepsilon^{4}\right)+1+\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\theta}}\left(1+\varepsilon^{2}-\gamma(p)\,\varepsilon^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+o\big(\varepsilon^{4}\big)\,.

Expanding again around
\varepsilon=0
, we notice that

\begin{aligned} &\lambda=\theta+A(\theta,p)\,\varepsilon^{2}+B(\theta,p)\,\varepsilon^{4}+o\big(\varepsilon^{4}\big)\\
&\mu=\theta+A(\theta,p)\,\varepsilon^{2}+C(\theta,p)\,\varepsilon^{4}+o\big(\varepsilon^{4}\big)\end{aligned}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\left\{\begin{aligned} &A(\theta,p)=\theta-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\alpha=\theta-(1-\theta)\,\tfrac{6\,(p-2)}{(p-1)\,(p+2)}\,,\\
&B(\theta,p)=-\,(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\beta\,,\\
&C(\theta,p)=\tfrac{1-\theta}{2\,\theta}\,(p-2)^{2}\,\alpha^{2}-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\beta-\gamma\,.\end{aligned}\right.

To understand the behaviour of the branch in the
(\lambda,\mu)
representation, it is interesting to compute the sign of
A(\theta,p)
. An elementary computation shows that
A(\theta,p)
is negative if and only if

\theta<\theta_{0}(p):=\frac{6\,(p-2)}{p^{2}+7\,p-14}<\theta_{\star}\,.

This is consistent with (
32
). As in Section
4
, we obtain that
\mathsf{s}^{\prime}(\lambda)<0
for
\lambda>d
, close enough to
d
if
\theta<\theta_{0}
, so that bifurcations always take place
to the left
in that case. This happens if
2<p<p_{0}=7
. See Fig.
3
.

6.5
A summary for
d=1

Our symmetry breaking results are summarized in Fig.
7
. A numerical estimate of the range in which a first order phase transition holds is obtained, in terms of
\theta
by finding, for each value of
p
, for which
E\in(-\,\theta_{\star},0)
we can solve

\lambda(\theta,E)=\mu(\theta,E)=\theta

(46)

where
\lambda(\theta,E)
and
\mu(\theta,E)
are defined by (
43
). As in Section
5.2
, since the solution
u_{E}
of (
36
) corresponding to
\lambda=\overline{\lambda}(E)
is optimal for (
1
), we know that

\frac{\|{u_{E}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}{\|{u_{E}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}^{2}}=\frac{(p-2)\,\overline{\nu}(E)+\overline{\lambda}(E)}{\overline{\mu}(E)}

Altogether, we obtain

\lambda(\theta,E)=\theta\,\overline{\lambda}(E)-(1-\theta)\,(p-2)\,\overline{\nu}(E)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mu(\theta,E)=\theta\,\big((p-2)\,\overline{\nu}(E)+\overline{\lambda}(E)\big)^{1-\frac{1}{\theta}}\,\overline{\mu}(E)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\,.

Solving (
46
) amounts to
\theta=\widetilde{\theta}(E):=\frac{(p-2)\,\overline{\nu}(E)}{(p-2)\,\overline{\nu}(E)+\overline{\lambda}(E)-1}
while
E
is obtained as the solution of

(\overline{\lambda}(E)-1)\,\log\big((p-2)\,\overline{\nu}(E)+\overline{\lambda}(E)\big)=\big((p-2)\,\overline{\nu}(E)+\overline{\lambda}(E)-1\big)\,\log\overline{\mu}(E)\,.

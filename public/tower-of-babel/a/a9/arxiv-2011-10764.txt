Nonlocal reaction preventing blow-up in the supercritical case of chemotaxis Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Evangelos A. Latos

Abstract
This paper studies the non-negative solutions of the Keller-Segel model with a nonlocal nonlinear source in a bounded domain. The competition between the aggregation and the nonlocal reaction term is highlighted: when the growth factor is stronger than the dampening effect, with the help of the nonlocal term, the model admits a classical solution which is uniformly bounded. Moreover, when the growth factor is of the same order compared to the dampening effect, the nonlocal nonlinear exponents can prevent the chemotactic collapse. Global existence of classical solutions is shown for an appropriate range of the exponents as well as convergence to the constant equilibrium state.

Introduction

Chemotaxis was originally described by the Keller-Segel model which was introduced in
[
16
,
17
]
. The model describes the characteristic movement of cells where there are two options: the cells can be attracted (move toward) by the increasing signal concentration or they can be repulsive by this signal. Afterwards, many mathematical models have been proposed to describe chemotaxis in the last few years. The simplest version, see for example
[
28
]
, describes the competition between the diffusion, the reproduction and the nonlocal aggregation satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u^{m}\nabla c)+\lambda f(u),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
-\Delta c+c=u^{\gamma},&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu=\nabla c\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ (n\geq 3)
,
m,\lambda,\gamma,\chi>0
and
\nu
is the outer unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
. The initial data are assumed to be
u_{0}\geq 0
,
u_{0}\in C^{\theta}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. The reaction term is taken to be

f(u)=u^{\alpha}\left(1-\sigma\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}dx\right)

with
\alpha\geq 1,\beta>1,\sigma>0

In the context of biological aggregation,

represents the bacteria density while

is the concentration of the chemical substance. The reaction term describes the reproduction rate of the bacteria where the resources of the environment can be consumed either locally or non-locally. The Keller-Segel system we consider describes the situation where chemicals diffuse much faster than cells
[
14
]
, thus the original parabolic-parabolic problem can be reduced into (

Logistic growth described by nonlocal terms has been investigated in the recent years. In
[
27
]
the authors focused on the parabolic-elliptic system with a linear competitive effect:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla c)+u\left(a_{0}-a_{1}u-\frac{a_{2}}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}udx\right),\\

where

and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla c)+u^{\alpha}\left(1-\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}dx\right)),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\

They showed that the nonlocal term

can actually prevent the chemotactic collapse. The result for the bounded domain case is the following:

(

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}).

At this point we highlight the benefits of considering a nonlocal Fischer-KPP (Kolmogorov-Petrovsky-Piskunov) reaction term instead of a logistic one. The classical chemotaxis system (parabolic-elliptic) is actually a nonlocal problem therefore, in order to control the behavior of its solution it only seems natural to consider a nonlocal reaction term. As it was stated in
[
27
]
that “it seems to conjecture that the dampening effect of the nonlocal terms might lead to an even more effective homogenization". Moreover, logistic growth described by nonlocal terms has already been used in competitive systems modelling cancer cells behavior which considers the influence of the surrounding area of a cell
[
27
,
29
]
and it can also describe Darwinian evolution of a structured population
[
18
]
or nuclear reaction processes
[
12
,
34
]
. Therefore the effect of the nonlocal term on the diffusion-aggregation-reaction equation is quite attractive.

Additionally, the analytical results on chemotaxis with logistic sources mostly concentrate on local reaction terms (
au-bu^{\alpha}
) where
a,b>0
:

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla u^{m}\cdot\nabla c-\chi u^{m}c+\chi u^{m+\gamma}+au-bu^{\alpha}.

(17)

Model (
17
) possesses a global classical solution under the consideration that either the dampening effect is stronger than the growth factor
[
6
,
33
]
, i.e.
\alpha>m+\gamma
, or that the dampening effect is of the same order compared to the growth factor
[
27
,
30
]
, i.e.
\alpha=m+\gamma
, combining some constraints on the coefficients
b
and
\chi
. To the best of our knowledge, when the growth factor is stronger than the dampening effect, whether the model admits a global solution is still open. Moreover, most of the known results focus on reaction terms of the type
g(u)-h(u)
where
g(u)\sim u^{a}
and
h(u)\sim u^{b}
with
b>a
. The essential difference between previous problems and the present one is that the reaction term now is of the form
u^{\alpha}-u^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}dx
. Actually, model (
1
) can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla u^{m}\cdot\nabla c+\chi u^{m+\gamma}-\chi cu^{m}+\lambda u^{\alpha}-\lambda u^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}dx.

Here, If the growth factor
u^{m+\gamma}
is stronger than the dampening effect
u^{\alpha}
then, the nonlocal term
\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}dx
can actually help preventing the chemotactic collapse.

Main results

Our work focuses on the study of the global-in-time existence of classical solutions and their asymptotic behaviour. We prove the following results:

1.

The existence of classical solutions to (
1
) under appropriate conditions on the parameters.

2.

The convergence to the constant equilibrium state.

The first result on the global existence extends the result found in
[
3
]
. The extra nonlinearities
u^{m},\ u^{\gamma}
that now appear make the procedure a bit more technical, but most importantly they point out the role of these two terms on the balance between global existence and blow-up. The second one, which deals with the convergence to the constant equilibrium state, is a new result and it is the main result of this work. The difficulty of this lies mostly on controlling the nonlinear nonlocal reaction terms (the choice of the solution pair which was used in
[
27
]
does not apply here because of the nonlinearities that appear in our problem) which is handled by choosing the appropriate pair of upper-lower solutions and then using the "rectangle method" to get the convergence.

More precisely, our first result reads as follows:

Theorem 1

(Global existence)
.

Let
n\geq 3,\ \alpha,\gamma,m\geq 1,\ \beta>1
satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma+m\leq\alpha<1+2\beta/n\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{n+4}{2}-\beta<\alpha<\gamma+m,

(18)

then, problem (
1
) admits a unique global classical solution which is uniformly bounded. Moreover, the following estimate holds, then for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}).

(19)

Remark 2
.

Our results are indeed different from the case
f(u)\equiv 0
, we have proven that the solution is uniformly bounded in
L^{k}(\Omega)
for
1\leq k\leq\infty
without any restriction on the initial data. While in chemotaxis systems with
f(u)\equiv 0,
the solution will exist globally only if one imposes a smallness condition on the initial data.

Remark 3
.

It is well known, for example in
[
14
]
, that the solution of chemotaxis system without reaction blows up in finite time for large initial data. Comparing this result with Theorem 1 shows that an appropriate nonlocal nonlinear dampening effect could give a global in time solution without any restriction on the initial data. The condition of Theorem 1 implies
\beta>\frac{n}{2}(\gamma+m-1)
can prevent chemotactic collapse. However, whether there exists a blow-up solution to our model under the assumption that
\beta\leq\frac{n}{2}(\gamma+m-1)
for higher dimension is still open.

Now we turn to the asymptotic behavior of the solution. We consider the spatial homogeneous version of our system (the elliptic-elliptic system) for example the integral in this case translates into:
\sigma\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Omega}\overline{u}^{\beta}\;dx=\sigma\overline{u}^{\beta}.

Also, we denote

(u,c)=(\xi,\xi),\quad\text{with}\quad\xi=\sigma^{-\frac{1}{\beta}},

(20)

the constant solution to the corresponding to (
1
) elliptic-elliptic system.

We will use a rectangle method, see for exmple
[

]
. We start by constructing a sub- and super-solution that are homogeneous in space. Therefore, we consider the following ODE system:

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{t}

\displaystyle A_{0}+\left(\frac{A_{1}}{p}-A_{0}\right)k^{\prime}>\left(\frac{A_{1}}{2}-A_{0}\right)k

which contradicts relation (

Next, if

\displaystyle A_{0}+\left(A_{0}-\frac{A_{1}}{2}\right)k>\left(A_{0}-\frac{A_{1}}{2^{*}}\right)k^{\prime}>\left(A_{0}-\frac{A_{1}}{2^{*}}\right)\frac{k}{2}.

Therefore, (

\displaystyle\frac{(2-\alpha)}{\beta}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\beta}\Leftrightarrow\alpha+\beta>\frac{n+4}{2}.

(49)

and again following along the lines of

First we examine the order of the solution to (

Lemma 8

(ODE-ordering)
.

Let

Since the right hand sides of (

, also since the right hand sides are

Next, to show the ordering of these solutions we argue by contradiction. Assume that there exists a

\underline{u}(t_{0})=\xi
.
With similar arguments we look into the cases

Next, we prove the convergence of the solution to (

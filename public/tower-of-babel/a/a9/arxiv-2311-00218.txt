Abstract
. We study the solutions to a nonlocal 1-Laplacian equation given by 2 ​ P.V. ​ ∫ ℝ N u ⁡ ( x ) − u ⁡ ( y ) | u ⁡ ( x ) − u ⁡ ( y ) | ​ d ​ y | x − y | N + s = f ⁡ ( x ) for ​ x ∈ Ω , \displaystyle 2\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s}}=f(x)\quad\textmd{for }x\in\Omega, with Dirichlet boundary condition u ⁡ ( x ) = 0 u(x)=0 in ℝ N \ Ω \mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega and nonnegative L 1 L^{1} -data. By investigating the asymptotic behaviour of renormalized solutions u p u_{p} to the nonlocal p p -Laplacian equations as p p goes to 1 + 1^{+} , we introduce a suitable definition of solutions and prove that the limit function u u of { u p } \{u_{p}\} is a solution of the nonlocal 1 1 -Laplacian equation above.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a smooth bounded domain. In this paper, we consider the following nonlocal 1-Laplacian Dirichlet problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{1}u=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
0\leq f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and the operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p}u(x):=

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy,\quad x\in\Omega,

with
p\in[1,+\infty)
,
s\in(0,1)
and
P.V.
being a commonly used abbreviation in the principal value sense.

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}
, being known as the celebrated fractional (nonlocal)
p
-Laplacian, emphasizes the Lévy process which indicates the emergence of the jump diffusion. In recent years, with the development of fractional Sobolev space, there is an extensive literature related to nonlocal
p
-Laplacian equations. Existence, uniqueness, regularity and the properties of solutions are treated under different situations; see
[
2
,
29
,
32
,
1
,
30
]
and references therein.

Problem (
1.1
) can be seen as a nonlocal counterpart of the following
1
-Laplacian Dirichlet problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta_{1}u:=-\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

This equation has been usually addressed as the limit problem for
p
-Laplacian type equations
when
p
goes to
1^{+}
. When the right-hand side term
f
belongs to different settings, it was studied by Kawohl in
[
28
]
for constant data,
by Cicalese and Trombetti in
[
21
]
for data belonging to the Lorentz space
L^{N,\infty}(\Omega)
and by Mercaldo, Segura de León and Trombetti in
[
35
]
for general data belonging to
W^{-1,\infty}(\Omega)
(see also
[
24
]
for the case with critical Sobolev exponent). In the framework of
L^{1}
setting, since no almost everywhere finite solution can be expected, Mercaldo, Segura de León S and Trombetti
[
36
]
investigated the behaviour, as
p
goes to
1^{+},
of the renormalized solutions to the
p
-Laplacian problems and proved the existence of renormalized solutions.

The notion of renormalized solution was first introduced by DiPerna and Lions in the study of the Boltzmann equation in
[
26
]
. There are plenty of results on renormalized solutions of
p
-Laplacian type equations based on the existence of weak solutions. For the nonlinear elliptic problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\text{div}(a(x,\nabla u))=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.3)

under the assumption that
p>2-\frac{1}{N}
, even in the case of the data is a measure, Boccardo and Gallouët gave the existence of solutions in the sense of distribution and proved that the renormalized solution of (
1.3
) is a distributed solution in
[
15
,
16
]
. Maso, Murat, Orsina and Prignet showed in
[
23
]
that, the restriction on
p
could be eliminated, that is the renormalized solution of (
1.3
) is a distributed solution for all
p>1
. At the same time, the study of renormalized solutions was also adapted to general nonlinear elliptic or parabolic problems
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
23
,
40
]
.

As far as the parabolic
1
-Laplacian operator are regarded, the pioneering works can be found in a series of papers by Andreu, Ballesteler, Caselles and Mazón
[
4
,
3
,
5
,
7
]
. Indeed, in
[
3
]
, the authors characterized the imprecise quotient
\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
, through the Anzellotti-Frid-Chen’s Pairing Theory (see
[
10
,
20
]
). This theory allows them to introduce a vector field
z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
which plays the role of
\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
. We also remark that in
[
31
]
, the authors investigated the inhomogeneous
1
-Laplace evolution equation in another way.

Finally, we would like to turn to the nonlocal equations. To the best of our knowledge, it seems that there are few results on this issue. In particular, Andreu, Mazón, Rossi and Toledo
[
9
,
8
]
considered the following nonlocal diffusion equation with different boundary conditions

u_{t}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J(x-y)|u(t,y)-u(t,x)|^{p-2}(u(t,y)-u(t,x))\,dy,

where
J:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative, radial, continuous function, strictly positive in
B(0,1)
, vanishing in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B(0,1)
and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}J(z)\,dz=1
. The authors studied
1<p<+\infty
as well as the extreme cases
p=1

and limits
p\to+\infty
, see also
[
34
]
. Novaga and Onoue
[
37
]
obtained the regularity of solutions to a nonlocal variational problem related to the image denoising model:

\min\{\mathcal{F}_{K,f}(u):u\in BV_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},

where the functional
\mathcal{F}_{K,f}
is defined as

\mathcal{F}_{K,f}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x-y)|u(x)-u(y)|\,dxdy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x)-f(x))^{2}\,dx,

the function
K
is a kernel singular at the origin, and a typical example is the function
|x|^{-(N+s)}
.
The authors showed that, in two dimensions, minimizers have the same Hölder regularity as the original image. We also refer to
[
11
,
27
,
39
]
for the nonlocal equations with variable exponents.

Concerning the nonlocal
1
-Laplacian equation (
1.1
), the existence of
(s,1)
-harmonic function and the equivalence between those functions and minimizers were investigated in
[
19
]
. Moreover, under the assumption that
f
belongs to a certain closed ball in
L^{\frac{N}{s}}(\Omega)
, Bucur in
[
18
]
studied the minimizer of
\mathcal{F}^{s_{p}}_{p}
(which is equivalent to the weak solution of nonlocal
p
-Laplacian)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{s_{p}}_{p}(u):=\frac{1}{2p}\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{p}p}}dxdy-\int_{\Omega}fu\,dx,

where
p
is small enough and
s_{p}=N+s-\frac{N}{p}\in(s,1)
, and obtained the existence, asymptotics and flatness results.

Motivated by the paper
[
36
]
, our main aim is to deal with the solutions to the nonlocal 1-Laplacian equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (
1.1
) and nonnegative
L^{1}
-data. To be more precise, we concentrate on the behaviour of the renormalized solutions
u_{p}
when
p
goes to
1^{+}
, and prove that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle u_{p}\rightarrow u\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\rightharpoonup Z(x,y)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(A_{\Omega})

and

\displaystyle|u_{p}(\cdot)|^{p-2}u_{p}(\cdot)\rightharpoonup Z(\cdot,y)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(\Omega)

for any fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0}
, where
1\leq q<\frac{N}{N-s}
,
A_{\Omega}:=(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cup(\Omega_{0}\times\Omega)
and

\Omega_{0}:=\Omega+B_{1}(0)
with
B_{1}(0)
donating the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered on the origin. Different from the local case, the normal vector field
\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
in classical framework is taken by the fractional ratio
\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}
, which leads to the singular set is more difficult to interpret and describe. After finding a suitable function
Z
with
Z\in\text{sgn}(u(x)-u(y))
, where sgn is the generalized sign function satisfying
\text{sgn}(0)=[-1,1]
, we show that this function
u
is a solution to problem (
1.1
).

To state our result, we introduce now the notion of solution to such a problem in the sense of renormalization.

Definition 1.1
.

A measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be a solution to problem (
1.1
) if the following conditions hold:

(1)

T_{k}(u)\in W^{s,1}_{0}(\Omega)
for any fixed
k>0;

(2)

There exists a function
Z
such that
Z_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\|Z_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 1,
where
Z_{k}
is defined as

\displaystyle Z_{k}:=Z\mathcal{X}_{\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)<k\text{ and }u(y)<k\right\}};

(3)

For fixed
k>0
, there holds

\displaystyle Z\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)=\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|

in the domain

\displaystyle\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)<k\text{ and }u(y)<k\right\};

(4)

Z
is an anti-symmetry function, that is

\displaystyle Z(x,y)=-Z(y,x)

for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N};

(5)

For all
\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
and
S\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with compact support, satisfying
S(t)
is monotone in
(0,+\infty)
,
S^{\prime}(t)\varphi(x)\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\Omega
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}Z_{\infty}\frac{\left(S(u)\varphi\right)(x)-\left(S(u)\varphi\right)(y)}{|x-y|^{N+s}}dxdy\leq\int_{\Omega}fS(u)\varphi dx,

(1.4)

where
Z_{\infty}=Z\mathcal{X}_{\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)<+\infty\ and\ u(y)<+\infty\right\}}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}:=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\left[(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\right].

Next we introduce our main result as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a measurable function
u
such that it is a solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

Remark 1.3
.

We remark that we have to impose the monotonicity and nonnegativity conditions in (5) of Definition
1.1
due the technical reason from the Fatou’s lemma. Another approximation method has been proposed to study the nonlocal
1
-Laplacian in
[
18
]
, which may be useful to solve this problem. However, there are still some difficulties in dealing with the
L^{1}
-data problem.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we state some notations and useful tools regarding to the fractional Sobolev spaces and renormalized solutions. Section
3
is devoted to studing the relationship between fractional Sobolev space with different parameters and the asymptotic behaviour of renormalized solutions
u_{p}
to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian equations as
p
goes to
1^{+}
. We will give the proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some notation used later. We will say that
\left\{u_{p}\right\}
is a sequence and consider subsequences of it. We denote by
|z|
the Euclidean norm of
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\text{meas}\left(E\right)
donates the Lebesgue measure of a measurable subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

For
1<q<+\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{q,\infty}(\Omega)
is the space of Lebesgue measurable functions
u
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{t\left(\text{meas}\left(\left\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|>t\right\}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\}<+\infty.

For
0<\alpha<1
and
1\leq p<+\infty
, the fractional Sobolev space
W^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty\right\}.

For a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we denote by
W^{\alpha,p}_{0}(\Omega)
a fractional Sobolev space, which is

\displaystyle W^{\alpha,p}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0\ \text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega\right\}.

We refer to
[
25
]
for more information about the space
W^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

From
[
39
]
, we give the definition of renormalized solution of the following fractional
p
-Laplacian problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}u=f&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
f
is a nonnegative function belonging to
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
p>1
.

Definition 2.1
.

u_{p}
is said to be a renormalized solution to problem (
2.1
) if
u_{p}
satisfies:

(1)

For any fixed
k>0
,
T_{k}(u_{p})\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where the truncation function
T_{k}
is defined by

\displaystyle T_{k}(t)=\max\left\{-k,\min\left\{k,t\right\}\right\}

for any
t\in\mathbb{R};

(2)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\left\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}:\left(u_{p}(x),u_{p}(y)\right)\in R_{h}\right\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy=0,

where

\displaystyle R_{h}=\left\{(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\max\left\{|v|,|w|\right\}\geq h+1\ \text{and }\min\left\{|v|,|w|\right\}\leq h\right\};

(3)

For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
and
S\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with compact support, there holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)\left[\left(S(u_{p})\varphi\right)(x)-\left(S(u_{p})\varphi\right)(y)\right]}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\\
&=\int_{\Omega}fS(u_{p})\varphi dx.\end{aligned}

Remark 2.2
.

In (3) of Definition
2.1
, it is enough for us to assume that
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
S\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with compact support, such that
S(u_{p})\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
which could be proved by the method in the proof of
[
39
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Next, for the convenience of the readers, we give some lemmas that can be proved by following the arguments given in
[
39
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u_{p}
be a renormalized solution to problem (
2.1
), then

\displaystyle T_{k}(u_{p})\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

and for fixed
\delta,\gamma>0
, set

\displaystyle S_{\delta,\gamma,h}(t)=T_{\delta}(\gamma G_{h}(t)),

where

\displaystyle G_{h}(t)=t-T_{h}(t),

we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\left[S_{\delta,\gamma,h}(u_{p})(x)-S_{\delta,\gamma,h}(u_{p})(y)\right]\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy=0.

Lemma 2.4
.

The renormalized solution
u_{p}
to problem (
2.1
) for fixed
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left[T_{k}(u_{p})\right]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leq k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

for fixed
k>0
.

Proof.

For the function
0\leq f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, taking a sequence
f_{n}=T_{n}(f)
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq f_{n}\leq f

and

\displaystyle f_{n}\rightarrow f\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega),

then, by calculus of variations, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
, there exists a nonnegative weak solution
u_{n,p}
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to problem (
2.1
), where
f
is replaced by
f_{n}
. That is, for any
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u_{n,p}(x)-u_{n,p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{n,p}(x)-u_{n,p}(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy=\int_{\Omega}f_{n}vdx.\end{aligned}

We take
v=T_{k}(u_{n,p})
as a test function to have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{|T_{k}(u_{n,p})(x)-T_{k}(u_{n,p})(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\\
&\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u_{n,p}(x)-u_{n,p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{n,p}(x)-u_{n,p}(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\left[T_{k}(u_{n,p})(x)-T_{k}(u_{n,p})(y)\right]dxdy\\
&=\int_{\Omega}f_{n}T_{k}(u_{n,p})dx\leq k\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.\end{aligned}

In addition, similar to the proof of
[
39
, Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3]
, we could prove

\displaystyle T_{k}(u_{n,p})\rightarrow T_{k}(u_{p})\quad\text{in }W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\ \text{as }n\rightarrow+\infty.

Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left[T_{k}(u_{p})\right]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leq k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

∎

3.
The asymptotic behaviour

In this section, we study the asymptotic behaviour of the renormalized solutions sequence
\left\{u_{p}\right\}
as
p
goes to
1^{+}
. We not only find a measurable function
u
satisfying
u_{p}\rightarrow u
by applying property of Lorentz space, but also find a function
Z
, which will play the role of
\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}
in a imprecise way.

We first show that the functions in fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
belong to the ones in
W^{s^{\prime},1}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
1<p<2
and
0<s^{\prime}<s<1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a function
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
p\in(1,2)
, there holds

\displaystyle u\in W^{s^{\prime},1}_{0}(\Omega)

for all
0<s^{\prime}<s<1
, moreover,

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{s^{\prime},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+C,

where the constant
C
depends on
s,s^{\prime},\Omega,N
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
can be divided into
\Omega\times\Omega_{0}
,
\Omega_{0}\times\Omega
,
\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0})
,
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0})\times\Omega
and
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}=(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cup(\Omega_{0}\times\Omega)\cup(\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0}))\cup((\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0})\times\Omega)\cup((\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)),

where
\Omega_{0}:=\Omega+B_{1}(0)
. We prove the lemma in different subdomains.

In
\Omega\times\Omega_{0}
, from Young’s inequality and the similar arguments in
[
25
, Section 6]
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega\times\Omega_{0}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cap\left\{|x-y|\leq 1\right\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy+\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cap\left\{|x-y|>1\right\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cap\left\{|x-y|\leq 1\right\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy+\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cap\left\{|x-y|>1\right\}}(|u(x)|+|u(y)|)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cap\left\{|x-y|\leq 1\right\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{p}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{sp}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(s^{\prime}-s)p^{\prime}}}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+C(\Omega)\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cap\left\{|x-y|\leq 1\right\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy+C(\Omega)\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cap\left\{|x-y|\leq 1\right\}}|x-y|^{(s-s^{\prime})p^{\prime}-N}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C(\Omega,N,s)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+\int_{\Omega}dx\int_{0}^{1}r^{(s-s^{\prime})p^{\prime}-1}dr

\displaystyle\leq C(\Omega,N,s)[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C(\Omega,N)\frac{r^{(s-s^{\prime})p^{\prime}}}{(s-s^{\prime})p^{\prime}}\Bigg|_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\leq C(\Omega,N,s)[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C(\Omega,N,s,s^{\prime}),

(3.1)

where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate exponent of
p
, that is
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

In
\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|dx\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{s^{\prime}+1}}dr

\displaystyle\leq C(N,s^{\prime},\Omega)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.2)

Similar to (1) and (2) there holds

\displaystyle\int_{(\Omega_{0}\times\Omega)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy\leq C(\Omega)[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C(\Omega,N,s,s^{\prime})

(3.3)

and

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0})\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy\leq C(N,s^{\prime},\Omega)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.4)

In
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)
, from the definition of

, we get

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy=0.

(3.5)

Therefore, combining (
3
)–(
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{s^{\prime},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s^{\prime}}}dxdy\leq C(\Omega,N,s^{\prime})[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C(\Omega,s,s^{\prime},N).

(3.6)

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

By using H
\ddot{o}

In the following part of this section, we study the renormalized solutions

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\right\},

and

as

goes to

.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
u_{p}
be the renormalized solution to problem (
2.1
), then

\displaystyle u_{p}\in L^{\frac{N(p-1)}{N-sp},\infty}(\Omega)

and

\displaystyle{\rm meas}\left(\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}}>h\right\}\right)\leq Ch^{\frac{N(1-p)}{N-s}}

for all
h>0
, where
C
depends only on
f,N,s
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.4
and fractional Sobolev’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{k}(u_{p})\|_{L^{p*}(\Omega)}\leq C\left[T_{k}(u_{p})\right]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\left(Mk\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
M:=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
and
C
depends only on
N,s,p
and
p*=\frac{Np}{N-sp}
. Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\text{meas}\left(\left\{x\in\Omega:u_{p}>\varepsilon\right\}\right)\leq\left(\frac{\|T_{k}(u_{p})\|_{L^{p*}(\Omega)}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p*}\leq\left(C(Mk)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{\frac{Np}{N-sp}}\varepsilon^{-p*},

letting
\varepsilon=k
, we have

\displaystyle\text{meas}\left(\left\{x\in\Omega:u_{p}>k\right\}\right)\leq CM^{\frac{N}{N-sp}}k^{\frac{N(1-p)}{N-sp}},

which means, by definition of Lorentz space,

\displaystyle u_{p}\in L^{\frac{N(p-1)}{N-sp},\infty}(\Omega).

Subsequently, we show

\displaystyle\text{meas}\left(\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}}>h\right\}\right)\leq Ch^{\frac{N(1-p)}{N-s}}.

Similar to the arguments used in
[
17
]
, set

\displaystyle\varPhi(k,\lambda)=\text{meas}\left(\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}>\lambda,\ \max\left\{u_{p}(x),u_{p}(y)\right\}>k\right\}\right).

Obviously, we have

\displaystyle\varPhi(k,0)\leq CM^{\frac{N}{N-sp}}k^{\frac{N(1-p)}{N-sp}}

(3.7)

and
\lambda\mapsto\varPhi(k,\lambda)
decreasing in
(0,+\infty)
. In addition, note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\left(\varPhi(0,\mu)-\varPhi(k,0)\right)d\mu\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\left(\varPhi(0,\mu)-\varPhi(k,\mu)\right)d\mu,

there holds

\displaystyle\varPhi(0,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varPhi(0,\mu)d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\varPhi(k,0)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\left(\varPhi(0,\mu)-\varPhi(k,\mu)\right)d\mu

\displaystyle=:\varPhi(k,0)+I.

(3.8)

For
I
, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\lambda}\text{meas}\left(\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}>\mu,\ \max\left\{u_{p}(x),u_{p}(y)\right\}\leq k\right\}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\left\{|u_{p}|\leq k\right\}\times\left\{|u_{p}|\leq k\right\}}\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|T_{k}(u_{p})(x)-T_{k}(u_{p})(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq kM.

(3.9)

Combining (
3.7
), (
3
) and (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\varPhi(0,\lambda)\leq\frac{kM}{\lambda}+CM^{\frac{N}{N-sp}}k^{\frac{N(1-p)}{N-sp}}

for all
k>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\varPhi(0,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\min\limits_{k>0}\left\{\frac{kM}{\lambda}+CM^{\frac{N}{N-sp}}k^{\frac{N(1-p)}{N-sp}}\right\}

\displaystyle=C\lambda^{\frac{N(1-p)}{p(N-s)}},

where
C
is a constant depending only on
N,s,M,\Omega
. Letting
\lambda=h^{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{meas}\left(\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}}>h\right\}\right)\leq Ch^{\frac{N(1-p)}{N-s}}.

∎

Remark 3.4

We point out that, in Proposition

, although the constant

depends on

, we could limit

, and we may choose a constant

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
u_{p}
be the renormalized solution to problem (
2.1
), there exists a measurable function
u
and a function
Z
, such that

\displaystyle u_{p}\rightarrow u\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\rightharpoonup Z(x,y)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(A_{\Omega})

and

\displaystyle|u_{p}(\cdot)|^{p-2}u_{p}(\cdot)\rightharpoonup Z(\cdot,y)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(\Omega)

for any fixed
y\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0}\right)
and
q\in\left[1,\frac{N}{N-s}\right)
,
A_{\Omega}=(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cup(\Omega_{0}\times\Omega)
,
\Omega_{0}=\Omega+B_{1}(0).

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle S_{n}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }t\leq n,\\
n-t+1&\text{if }n<t\leq n+1,\\
0&\text{if }t>n\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\varphi(t)=\frac{t}{1+t}.

We have

\displaystyle\varphi(u_{p})\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega).

Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{n}(u_{p})\varphi(u_{p})\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)\left[\left(S_{n}(u_{p})\varphi(u_{p})\right)(x)-\left(S_{n}(u_{p})\varphi(u_{p})\right)(y)\right]}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fS_{n}(u_{p})\varphi(u_{p})dx.

(3.10)

Note that

\displaystyle\left(S_{n}(u_{p})\varphi(u_{p})\right)(x)-\left(S_{n}(u_{p})\varphi(u_{p})\right)(y)

\displaystyle=\left[S_{n}(u_{p})(x)-S_{n}(u_{p})(y)\right]\frac{\varphi(u_{p})(x)+\varphi(u_{p})(y)}{2}

\displaystyle\ \quad+\left[\varphi(u_{p})(x)-\varphi(u_{p})(y)\right]\frac{S_{n}(u_{p})(x)+S_{n}(u_{p})(y)}{2},

we can divide the left-hand side of (
3
) into two parts, by denoting

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}&\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)\left[S_{n}(u_{p})(x)-S_{n}(u_{p})(y)\right]}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\\
&\times\frac{\varphi(u_{p})(x)+\varphi(u_{p})(y)}{2}dxdy\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{2}=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}&\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)\left[\varphi(u_{p})(x)-\varphi(u_{p})(y)\right]}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\\
&\times\frac{S_{n}(u_{p})(x)+S_{n}(u_{p})(y)}{2}dxdy.\end{aligned}

By Lemma
2.3
and
I_{1}+I_{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
, we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}I_{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

that is

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}\left(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)\right)\left[\varphi(u_{p})(x)-\varphi(u_{p})(y)\right]}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Since

\displaystyle\left|\varphi(u_{p})(x)-\varphi(u_{p})(y)\right|\leq|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|

and

\displaystyle\left(\varphi(u_{p})(x)-\varphi(u_{p})(y)\right)(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))\geq 0,

we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{\left|\varphi(u_{p})(x)-\varphi(u_{p})(y)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Taking into account
\left[\varphi(u_{p})\right]_{W^{s^{\prime},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\left[\varphi(u_{p})\right]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C
for any fixed
s^{\prime}\in(0,s)
, we have
\left\{\varphi(u_{p})\right\}
is bounded in
W^{s^{\prime},1}_{0}(\Omega)
. As a consequence, up to a subsequence, still denote by
\left\{\varphi(u_{p})\right\}
,

\displaystyle\varphi(u_{p})\rightarrow\varphi(u)\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega)\text{ and a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Recalling
\varphi(t)
is strictly increasing, the sequence
\left\{u_{p}\right\}
tends to a measurable function
u
for almost all
x\in\Omega
. In particular, if
\varphi(u)=1
, we set
u=+\infty
.

Next, we try to find a function
Z
, defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Note that Proposition
3.3
and Remark
3.4
imply that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A_{\Omega}}\left(\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C,\quad q\in\left[1,\frac{N}{N-s}\right),

where
A_{\Omega}=(\Omega\times\Omega_{0})\cup(\Omega_{0}\times\Omega)
and
C
depends only on
N,s,\Omega
. Thereby, up to a subsequence, still denote by

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\right\},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\rightharpoonup Z_{q}(x,y)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(A_{\Omega}).

By a diagonal argument, we find the limit

does not depend on

. Hence, we write
Z_{q}
as
\bar{Z}
, defined on
A_{\Omega}
.

Similarly, we could find a function
\hat{Z}(x)
, such that for fixed

,

\displaystyle|u_{p}(x)|^{p-2}u_{p}(x)\rightharpoonup\hat{Z}(x)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(\Omega),\quad q\in\left[1,\frac{N}{N-s}\right),

where we utilized

.

By setting

\displaystyle Z(x,y)=\begin{cases}\bar{Z}(x,y)&\text{if }(x,y)\in A_{\Omega},\\[2.84526pt]
\hat{Z}(x)&\text{if }(x,y)\in\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0}),\\[2.84526pt]

∎

4.
The limit problem

In this section, we show the connection between the function
u,Z
and the “limit problem” of (
2.1
), which is

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{1}u=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{cases}

(4.1)

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We prove Theorem
1.2
by several steps. Some of the reasoning is based on the ideas developed in
[
35
,
33
]
.

Step 1.
We shall prove that
T_{k}(u)\in W^{s,1}_{0}(\Omega)
for any fixed
k>0
.

For fixed
k>0
, we have

\displaystyle\left[T_{k}(u_{p})\right]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leq k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Note that
T_{k}(u_{p})\rightarrow T_{k}(u)\text{ a.e. in }\Omega
and from Fatou’s lemma, there holds

\displaystyle[T_{k}(u)]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}[T_{k}(u_{p})]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leq k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

where Fatou’s lemma is utilized. Moreover,
u_{p}=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega
implies that
u=0\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega
. Therefore, it follows that
T_{k}(u)\in W^{s,1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Step 2.
We shall prove that
Z_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\|Z_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 1
.

For any fixed
k>0
, the sequence

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\mathcal{X}_{\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u_{p}(x)<k\text{ and }u_{p}(y)<k\right\}}\right\}

is bounded in
L^{q}(A_{\Omega}),q\in\left[1,\frac{N}{N-s}\right)
. Thus, as
p\rightarrow 1^{+}
, we have, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\mathcal{X}_{\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u_{p}(x)<k\text{ and }u_{p}(y)<k\right\}}\rightharpoonup w_{k}

in
L^{1}(A_{\Omega})
.

Denote

\displaystyle B_{p,h,k}=\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:\frac{|T_{k}(u_{p})(x)-T_{k}(u_{p})(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}}>h\right\}.

Similar to the same arguments as before, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\mathcal{X}_{B_{p,h,k}\cap\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u_{p}(x)<k\text{ and }u_{p}(y)<k\right\}}\rightharpoonup g_{h,k}

and

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\mathcal{X}_{\left(A_{\Omega}\backslash B_{p,h,k}\right)\cap\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u_{p}(x)<k\text{ and }u_{p}(y)<k\right\}}\rightharpoonup f_{h,k}

in
L^{1}(A_{\Omega})
for some
g_{h,k}
and
f_{h,k}
.

By the definition of
B_{p,h,k}
, the following inequality holds,

\displaystyle\text{meas}\left(B_{p,h,k}\right)\leq\frac{1}{h^{p}}\int_{A_{\Omega}}\frac{|T_{k}(u_{p})(x)-T_{k}(u_{p})(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\leq\frac{k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{h^{p}}.

Therefore, from Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int_{B_{p,h,k}\cap\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u_{p}(x)<k\text{ and }u_{p}(y)<k\right\}}\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}dxdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A_{\Omega}}\left[\frac{|T_{k}(u_{p})(x)-T_{k}(u_{p})(y)|^{p-2}\left(T_{k}(u_{p})(x)-T_{k}(u_{p})(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\right]^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\ \times\left(\text{meas}\left\{B_{p,h,k}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\frac{k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{h^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{h^{p}}.

(4.2)

By letting
p
goes to
1^{+}
, for fixed
k,h>0
, it follows from (
4
)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{A_{\Omega}}g_{h,k}\phi dxdy\right|\leq\frac{k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{h}

for any
\phi\in L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})
with
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})}\leq 1
, hence we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\Omega}}\left|g_{h,k}\right|dxdy\leq\frac{k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{h}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{p}(x)-u_{p}(y))}{|x-y|^{\left(\frac{N}{p}+s\right)(p-1)}}\mathcal{X}_{\left(A_{\Omega}\backslash B_{p,h,k}\right)\cap\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u_{p}(x)<k\text{ and }u_{p}(y)<k\right\}}\leq h^{p-1}

a.e. in
A_{\Omega}
. This inequality implies the following pointwise estimate for

,

\displaystyle\left|f_{h,k}\right|\leq\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 1}h^{p-1}=1\quad\text{a.e. in }A_{\Omega}.

From

\displaystyle w_{k}=f_{h,k}+g_{h,k}

with

\displaystyle\|f_{h,k}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})}\leq 1,\quad\int_{A_{\Omega}}\left|g_{h,k}\right|dxdy\leq\frac{k\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{h},

we obtain

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow+\infty}g_{h,k}=0

and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow+\infty}f_{h,k}=\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow+\infty}(w_{k}-g_{h,k})=w_{k}.

Since
\|f_{h,k}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})}\leq 1
, we obtain
w_{k}\in L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})
and
\|w_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})}\leq 1
. Next we show the connection between
w_{k}
and
Z_{k}
. Taking

into account, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u_{p}(x)<k\text{ and }u_{p}(y)<k\right\}}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}\text{ and }u(y)<k\right\}}

in

for all

. By means of

, the set of
k
such that

\displaystyle\text{meas}\left(\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u(x)=k\text{ or }u(y)=k\right\}\right)>0

is countable, which means, for almost all
k>0
, there holds
w_{k}=Z_{k}
. Observing that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}w_{k}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}Z_{k}=Z_{\infty}=Z\mathcal{X}_{\left\{(x,y)\in A_{\Omega}:u(x)<+\infty\text{ and }u(y)<+\infty\right\}}

holds almost everywhere in

, and combining with

, we have

\displaystyle\|Z_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})}\leq\|Z_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})}\leq 1

for all
k>0
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle Z_{k}\rightharpoonup Z_{\infty}\quad\text{in }L^{q}(A_{\Omega})

(4.3)

for any
q\in[1,+\infty)
.

Next, we study the property of
Z
on
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\backslash A_{\Omega}
. From Theorem
3.5
, we know for any fixed

,

\displaystyle|u_{p}(\cdot)|^{p-2}u_{p}(\cdot)\rightharpoonup Z(\cdot,y)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(\Omega),\quad q\in\left[1,\frac{N}{N-s}\right).

Similar to the discussion of
Z
in
A_{\Omega}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle Z_{k}(\cdot,y)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\quad\|Z_{k}(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1

and

\displaystyle Z_{k}(\cdot,y)\rightharpoonup Z_{\infty}(\cdot,y)\quad\text{in }L^{q}(\Omega)

(4.4)

for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{0}
,
q\in[1,+\infty)
and fixed

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle Z_{k}(x,y)\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\backslash A_{\Omega}\right),\quad\|Z_{k}(x,y)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\backslash A_{\Omega}\right)}\leq 1.

Step 3.
We shall prove that for fixed
k>0
, there holds

\displaystyle Z\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)=\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\quad\text{a.e. in}\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:u(x)<k\text{ and }u(y)<k\right\}.

Firstly, we discuss it in
A_{\Omega}
. By Step 2,
\|Z_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\Omega})}\leq 1
, hence

\displaystyle Z_{k}(T_{k}(u)(x)-T_{k}(u)(y))\leq|T_{k}(u)(x)-T_{k}(u)(y)|.

Now we prove

\displaystyle|T_{k}(u)(x)-T_{k}(u)(y)|\leq Z_{k}(T_{k}(u)(x)-T_{k}(u)(y)).

For fixed
k>0

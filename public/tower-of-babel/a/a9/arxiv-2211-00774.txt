Distribution of runs and patterns in four state trials
Jungtaek Oh

Abstract
From a mathematical and statistical perspective, a segment of a DNA strand can be viewed as a sequence of four-state (A, C, G, T) trials. In thispaper, we consider the distributions of runs and patterns related to the run lengths of four states (A, B, C, and D). Let X 1 , X 2 , … X_{1},X_{2},\ldots be a sequence of four state i.i.d. trials taking values in the set 𝒮 = { A , B , C , D } \mathscr{S}=\{A,\ B,\ C,\ D\} of four symbols with probability P ⁡ ( A ) = P a P(A)=P_{a} , P ⁡ ( B ) = P b P(B)=P_{b} , P ⁡ ( C ) = P c , P(C)=P_{c}, and P ⁡ ( D ) = P d . P(D)=P_{d}. We obtain exact formulas for the probability mass functions of the distributions of runs of B’s of order k k , for longest run, shortest run, waiting time, and run lengths.



Keywords:
runs and patterns, multistate trials, discrete distribution of order
k
, waiting time distribution, distribution of run length, longest run, shortest run, DNA sequence.

Introduction

Runs and runs-related statistics have been intensively studied in the literature because of their wide range of applications in various areas, including statistics (e.g., hypothesis testing), engineering (e.g. system reliability, health services monitoring and quality control), molecular biology and bioinformatics (e.g., population genetics and deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) sequence homology), physics, psychology, radar astronomy, computer science (e.g., encoding/decoding and transmission of digital information), and finance (e.g., financial engineering, risk analysis, and risk prediction). Significant progress on runs and related statistics in the past few decades has been comprehensively surveyed by
Balakrishnan, N. and Koutras, M. V. 2003
and
Fu, J. C. and Lou, W. W. 2003
. Moreover, there are various more recent contributions on the topic, such as
Arapis, A. N., Makri, F. S., and Psillakis, Z. M. 2018
,
Eryilmaz, S. 2018
,
Kong, Y. 2019
,
Makri, F. S., Psillakis, Z. M. and Arapis, A. N. 2019
, and
Aki, S. 2019
.

There are two main types of problems concerning runs and runs-related statistics.

(1)

The number of trials until the first occurrence of a run or pattern (or until the
r
-th occurrence of a run or pattern), which is called a waiting time problem.

(2)

The number of occurrences of a run or pattern until the
n
-th trial.

Waiting time distributions have attracted considerable interest in applied probability. Properties of waiting time distributions are extensively analyzed in
Ebneshahrashoob, M. and Sobel, M. 1990
,
Aki, S. 1992
,
Balasubramanian. K. 1993
,
Koutras, M. V. 1996
,
Antzoulakos, D. L. and Philippou, A. N. 1997
,
Antzoulakos, D. L. 1999
,
Kim, S., Park, C. and Oh, J. 2013
, and
Inoue, K. and Aki, S. 2014
.

The geometric distribution of order
k
is a well-known waiting time distribution. This is defined as the distribution of the number of trials until the first consecutive
k
successes. This definition is based on
Philippou, A. N., Georghiou, C., and Philippou, G. N. 1983
. This distribution becomes the classical geometric distribution for the case
k=1
(the number of Bernoulli trials needed to get one success) with probability mass function
f(x)=q^{x-1}p

(x\geq 1).

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of four state independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) trials that take values in the set
\mathscr{S}=\{A,\ B,\ C,\ D\}
of four symbols. For the number of trials
W_{1}^{k}
until the first consecutive
k
successes we have the following equivalent definitions of
W_{1}^{k}
:

\begin{split}W_{1}^{k}\ =&\ \text{min}\{n:X_{n-k+1}=\cdots=X_{n}=1\}\\
=&\ \text{min}\left\{n:\prod_{n-k+1}^{n}X_{j}=1\right\}\\
=&\ \text{min}\left\{n:\sum_{n-k+1}^{n}X_{j}=k\right\}.\end{split}

Various statistical properties of the geometric distribution of order
k
have been applied to multiple areas, ranging from quality control to reliability. In particular, the consecutive-
k
-out-of-
n
:
F
system (see,
Chao, MT and Fu, JC and Koutras, MV. 1995
,
Chang, GJLR and In-Hang, Chung and Cui, Lirong and Hwang, FK. 2000
,
Kuo, W and Zuo, M. 2003
,
Eryilmaz, S. 2010
, and
Triantafyllou, Ioannis S. 2015
) is closely related to this distribution. Another closely related random variable is the length
L_{n}
of the longest success run in
n
four state trials. Because
W_{1}^{k}\leq n
if and only if
L_{n}\geq k
, we have the following between the probability distribution functions of
W_{1}^{k}
and
L_{n}
:
\mathbf{P(}W_{1}^{k}\leq n)=\mathbf{P}(L_{n}\geq k).

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
be random variables i.i.d.
W_{1}^{k}
. The distribution of the number
W_{r}^{k}
of trials until the
r
-th appearance of a success run of length
k
is a negative binomial distribution of order
k.
This follows from the fact that it is the distribution of the
r
-fold convolution of the geometric distribution of order
k
:

W_{r}^{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}X_{i}.

Some main aspects of the occurrences of runs and patterns are: where, when, and how many times they occur. To study these, statistics that count runs and patterns according to various enumerating schemes are defined.
Feller, W. 1968
presented a classical counting scheme for enumerating runs of fixed length : once
k
consecutive successes occur, the number of occurrences of
k
consecutive successes is incremented and the counting procedure starts anew, referred to as a
nonoverlapping
counting scheme, which follows a binomial distribution of order
k
. In another counting scheme, we count only the number
E_{n}^{k}
of success runs of length exactly
k
preceded and succeeded either by failures or by nothing (
Mood, A. M. 1940
).

In a sequence of
n
four state trials, we can define other statistics via runs and patterns, such as the longest (maximal) and the shortest (minimal) run lengths (see,
Sen, K., Agarwal, M. L. and Chakraborty, S. 2002
). The longest and shortest runs are the upper and lower bounds of the number of consecutive successes that appear in a sequence of
n
four state trials, respectively. These distributions can be applied in DNA-type sequence comparison by observing the frequencies of the longest runs of matches or mismatches.

Sequences of categorical outcomes frequently arise in biomedical research, representing, for example, DNA strand segments or outcomes of healthcare evaluations. They are typically analyzed by defining problem-specific statistics involving runs and patterns. A DNA molecule is a chain or sequence of pairs of nucleotides with four base structures: adenine, cytosine, guanine, and thymine (or A, C, G, and T, respectively). The occurrence of a specified nucleotides sequence in some portion of the chain is the event that the specified run of A, C, G, and T occurs. Mathematically, a random DNA strand segment can be viewed as a sequence of four-state (A, C, G, T) trials. We consider some distributions of runs and patterns related to run lengths for multistate trials, particularly four-state trials.

2
Discrete distribution of order
k

We consider a sequence
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
of multistate trials defined on the state space
\mathscr{S}=\{A,\ B,\ C,\ D\}
with probabilities
P(A)=P_{a}
,
P(B)=P_{b}
,
P(C)=P_{c}
and
P(D)=P_{d},
such that
P_{a}+P_{b}+P_{c}+P_{d}=1,
.
In this section, we obtain the PMFs of a binomial distribution of order
k,
and the distribution of runs of length exactly
k
using combinatorial analysis.

2.1
Binomial distribution of order
k

First, we consider the number
N_{n}^{k}
of runs of B’s of length
k
in
n
i.i.d. four state trials. We establish the PMF of the random variable
N_{n}^{k}
using combinatorial analysis.

Theorem 2.1
.

The
PMF
of
N_{n}^{k}
, for
0\leq x\leq\left[\frac{n}{k}\right]
, is given by

\begin{split}P(N_{n}^{k}=x)=P_{b}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{\star i}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})+x\choose x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},x}\left(\frac{P_{a}}{P_{b}}\right)^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}x_{t}}\left(\frac{P_{c}}{P_{b}}\right)^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}}\left(\frac{P_{d}}{P_{b}}\right)^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}z_{t}},\end{split}

where the inner summation
\sum_{\star}
is over all nonnegative integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}
for which

\sum_{t=1}^{k}t(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})+kx+i=n,
for
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1.

Proof.

Let
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}A=O_{t}
,
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}C=J_{t}
,
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}D=T_{t}
, where
1\leq t\leq k
.
A typical element of the event
\left\{N_{n}^{k}=x\right\}
is a sequence

\begin{split}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\overset{1}{\boxed{{\underbrace{B\dots B}_{k}}}}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\overset{2}{\boxed{{\underbrace{B\ldots B}_{k}}}}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\overset{x}{\boxed{{\underbrace{B\ldots B}_{k}}}}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\underbrace{B\ldots B}_{i}\end{split},

where
i\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
, and
{\boxed{R_{t}}}
represents any combination of the strings
O_{t},J_{t}
, and
T_{t}
appearing altogether
x_{t}
,
y_{t}
, and
z_{t}
times, respectively, in the sequence, satisfying

\begin{split}\sum_{t=1}^{k}t(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})+kx+i=n.\end{split}

(2.1)

The number of different ways of arranging this sequence equals

\begin{split}{\sum_{t=1}^{k}x_{t}+\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}+\sum_{t=1}^{k}z_{t}+x\choose x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},x}.\end{split}

(2.2)

Because of the independence of the trials, the probability of the above sequence is

\begin{split}P_{a}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}x_{t}}P_{b}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}(t-1)(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})+kx}P_{c}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}}P_{d}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}z_{t}}.\end{split}

(2.3)

The probability of the run of B’s of length
i
at the end of each of these sequences is
P_{b}^{i}

(0\leq i<k)
, which leads to the overall probability

\begin{split}P_{a}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}x_{t}}P_{b}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}(t-1)(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})+kx+i}P_{c}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}}P_{d}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}z_{t}}.\end{split}

Summing over
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
the result follows.
∎

Remark 1
.

For
P_{a}=q
,
P_{b}=p
such that
p+q=1
,
P_{c}=P_{d}=0
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}=0
, Theorem
2.1
reduces to Theorem 2.1 of
Philippou, A. N. and Makri, F. S. 1986
.

where the inner summation
\sum_{\star(k,i)}
is over all nonnegative integers
x_{1}
,
\ldots
,
x_{n-x(k+1)}
,
y_{1}
,
\ldots

y_{n-x(k+1)}
,

\ldots

for which
\sum_{t=1}^{n-x(k+1)}t(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})=n-i

x_{k+1}+y_{k+1}+z_{k+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x&\text{if $i\neq k,$}\\

\end{array}\right.

Let
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}A=O_{t}
,
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}C=J_{t}
,
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}D=T_{t}
, for

\left\{E_{n}^{k}=x\right\}
is a sequence

\begin{split}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\overset{1}{\boxed{O_{k+1}\ or\ J_{k+1}\ or\ T_{k+1}}}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\overset{x}{\boxed{O_{k+1}\ or\ J_{k+1}\ or\ T_{k+1}}}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\underbrace{B\ldots B}_{i}\end{split},

where
0\leq i\leq n-x(k+1)
, and
{\boxed{R_{t}}}
represents any string
O_{t}
,
J_{t}
, and
T_{t}
appearing
x_{t}
,
y_{t}
, and
z_{t}
times, respectively, in the sequence, satisfying

\sum_{t=1}^{n-x(k+1)}t(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})=n-i,

(2.4)

subject to the condition
x_{k+1}+y_{k+1}+z_{k+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x&\text{if $i\neq k,$}\\

{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}A=O_{t}

{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}C=J_{t}

{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}D=T_{t}

(3.3)

\begin{split}P_{a}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}x_{t}}P_{b}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}(t-1)(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})+k(r-1)}P_{c}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}}P_{d}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}z_{t}}.\end{split}

(3.4)

The probability of the run of B’s of length
k
at the end of each of these sequences is
P_{b}^{k}
, which leads to the overall probability

\begin{split}P_{a}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}x_{t}}P_{b}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}(t-1)(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})+kr}P_{c}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}}P_{d}^{\sum_{t=1}^{k}z_{t}}.\end{split}

∎

Remark 3
.

For
P_{a}=q
,
P_{b}=p
such that
p+q=1
,
P_{c}=P_{d}=0
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}=0
, Theorem
3.2
reduces to Theorem 3.1 (a) of
Philippou, A. N. 1984
.

4

We consider a sequence
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}

\mathscr{S}=\{A,\ B,\ C,\ D\}
with probabilities
P(A)=P_{a}
,

,

and
P(D)=P_{d},
such that
P_{a}+P_{b}+P_{c}+P_{d}=1,
. In this section, we obtain the PMFs of the distribuions of the longest and shortest runs and establish the PMFs and their joint distributions using combinatorial analysis.

n
four state i.i.d. trials, which is called longest run statistics. We establish the PMF of the random variable

Theorem 4.1
.

The PMF of

\begin{split}P(L_{n}=\ell)=P_{b}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{\star}{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})\choose x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell+1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell+1},z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell+1}}\left(\frac{P_{a}}{P_{b}}\right)^{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}x_{t}}\left(\frac{P_{c}}{P_{b}}\right)^{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}y_{t}}\left(\frac{P_{d}}{P_{b}}\right)^{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}z_{t}},\end{split}

\sum_{\star}
is over all nonnegative integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell+1}
,
y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell+1}
,
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell+1}
such that
\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}t(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})=n-i
, for
0\leq i\leq\ell,
and satisfying at least one of the conditions
x_{\ell+1}\geq 1
,
y_{\ell+1}\geq 1
,
z_{\ell+1}\geq 1
, and
i=\ell.

Proof.

Let
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}A=O_{t}
,
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}C=J_{t}
,
{\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{t-1}}D=T_{t}
, where
t=1,\ldots,

\min(\ell+1,n)
.
A typical element of the event
\left\{L_{n}=\ell\right\}
is a sequence

\begin{split}\cdots{\boxed{R_{t}}}\cdots\underbrace{B\cdots B}_{i},\end{split}

where
{\boxed{R_{t}}}
represents any of the strings
O_{t}
,
J_{t}
, and
T_{t}
appearing
x_{t}
,
y_{t}
, and
z_{t}
times, respectively, in the sequence, satisfying
\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}t(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})=n-i
, for
0\leq i\leq\ell,
and satisfying at least one of the conditions:

,
y_{\ell+1}\geq 1
,
z_{\ell+1}\geq 1
, and
i=\ell.

The number of different ways of arranging the sequence equals

\begin{split}{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})\choose x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell+1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell+1},z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell+1}}.\end{split}

Because of the independence of the trials, the probability of the above sequence is

\begin{split}P_{a}^{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}x_{t}}P_{b}^{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}(t-1)(x_{t}+y_{t}+z_{t})}P_{c}^{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}y_{t}}P_{d}^{\sum_{t=1}^{\ell+1}z_{t}}.\end{split}

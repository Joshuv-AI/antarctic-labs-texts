A Refined Lifting Theorem for Supersingular Galois Representations
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 be a prime number, 𝔽 \mathbb{F} a finite field of characteristic p p and let χ ¯ \bar{\chi} be the mod- p p cyclotomic character. Let ρ ¯ : G ℚ → GL 2 ⁡ ( 𝔽 ) \bar{\rho}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}) be a Galois representation such that the local representation ρ ¯ ↾ G ℚ p \bar{\rho}_{\restriction\text{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}} is flat and irreducible. Further, assume that det ​ ρ ¯ = χ ¯ \text{det}\bar{\rho}=\bar{\chi} . The celebrated theorem of Khare and Wintenberger asserts that if ρ ¯ \bar{\rho} satisfies some natural conditions, there exists a normalized Hecke-eigencuspform f = ∑ n ≥ 1 a n ​ q n f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n} and a prime 𝔭 | p \mathfrak{p}|p in its field of Fourier coefficients such that the associated 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} -adic representation ρ f , 𝔭 {\rho}_{f,\mathfrak{p}} lifts ρ ¯ \bar{\rho} . In this manuscript we prove a refined version of this theorem, namely, that one may control the valuation of the p p -th Fourier coefficient of f f . The main result is of interest from the perspective of the p p -adic Langlands program.

3.
Overview

Let
\bar{\rho}
be a Galois representation
\bar{\rho}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
which satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.1
. It follows from the well known results in
[

,
Ram02
]
that
\bar{\rho}
lifts to a continuous Galois representation
\rho:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}))
with

which is geometric in the sense of Fontaine and Mazur. This means that
\rho
is odd, unramified away from finitely many primes and the local representation
\rho_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is de Rham. We remark that at the time Ramakrishna proved his results on lifting Galois representations, the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture had not yet been settled. One step in the construction is to analyze suitable local deformation conditions associated to the local representations
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{l}}}
at each prime
l
at which
\bar{\rho}
is ramified. Local at
p
conditions are comprehensively studied in
[
Ram02
, pp. 124-138]
. In
[
Ram93
]
, he analyzed the
flat deformation condition
at
p
, cf. Definition
4.10
. Since

is assumed to be flat, it follows that
\rho_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
can be arranged to be flat, and in particular, crystalline. The analysis of flat deformations via the theory of Fontaine-Laffaille modules aided in the calculations made in
[
Ram93
]
, and this method is revisited in sections
4
and
5
of this paper. Since

is assumed to be irreducible, the Fontaine Mazur conjecture is known for geometric lifts of

and it follows from the main theorem in
[

]
that

does arise from a Hecke eigencuspform

of weight
2
on
\Gamma_{1}(N)
. Furthermore,
f
is supersingular at
p
and

is prime to

. In this section we explain the general lifting strategy used in proving Theorem

, after reviewing a few preliminary notions.

3.2.
The Lifting Strategy

Fix an integer

with the following properties:

(1)

Let

be a normalized Hecke eigencuspform and
\mathfrak{p}|p
a prime of

. Assume that the

(2)

The deformation functor

(3)

Let

be the tangent space to the flat deformation functor

. We require that

stabilizes mod

in

shows this property is satisfied.

Recall that for
v\in S\backslash\{p\}
, the functor
\mathcal{C}_{v}
is specified as in Proposition
3.3
. The main theorem is proved by lifting
\bar{\rho}
to a geometric representation
\rho
by successively lifting
\rho_{m}
to
\rho_{m+1}
as depicted

\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}),
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})/p^{m})
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})/p^{m+1})
\bar{\rho}
\rho_{m}
\rho_{m+1}

so that
\rho_{m\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

at each prime

and the auxiliary set of primes

Definition 3.4

(2)

&1\\
\end{array}}\right),

Let

\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

be a nice prime. The space
\mathcal{N}_{v}
consists of cohomology classes

Proof.

Recall that

∎

Let

for

\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
at

for

for

for the tangent space of
\mathcal{C}_{v}
. Set
\mathcal{N}_{p}
to be the tangent space of the functor of flat deformations
\mathcal{C}_{p}
. For

with respect to the non-degenerate Tate pairing

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\times H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\rightarrow H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\mathbb{F}(\bar{\chi}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{F}.

and

H^{1}_{\mathcal{N}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}):=\text{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{S\cup X}}\bigoplus_{v\in S\cup X}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{v}}\right\}

Lemma 3.6
.

The dimensions of the Selmer group and dual Selmer group coincide, i.e.,

(3.1)

\operatorname{dim}H^{1}_{\mathcal{N}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\operatorname{dim}H^{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}).

Proof.

The following formula is due to Wiles (see
[
NSW13
, Theorem 8.7.9]
):

(3.2)

\begin{split}&\operatorname{dim}H^{1}_{\mathcal{N}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\operatorname{dim}H^{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\\
&=\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\\
&+\sum_{v\in S\cup X\cup\{\infty\}}\left(\dim\mathcal{N}_{v}-\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\right).\\
\end{split}

Since
\det\bar{\rho}=\bar{\chi}
is odd, one has that
\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\infty},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=1
. It follows from Proposition
3.3
that

\dim\mathcal{N}_{v}-\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }v\neq p,\infty,\\
1&\text{ if }v=p,\\
-1&\text{ if }v=\infty.\\
\end{cases}

Recall that the image of
\bar{\rho}
contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. It is an easy exercise to check that

H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=0\text{, and }H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0.

Putting it all together, the result follows.
∎

The Selmer and dual Selmer groups fit into a five-term exact sequence called the Poitou-Tate sequence (see the proof of
[

, Lemma 1.1]
). We need only point out that the cokernel of

injects into

\operatorname{res}_{S\cup X}
is an isomorphism. All deformations
\rho_{m}
discussed in this paper will have similitude character equal to
\chi\mod{p^{m}}
.

The three main steps are as follows:

(1)

Corollary

and a mod

of

is furthermore arranged to satisfy the conditions

at the primes

and

at

.
This strategy for producing such a lift is based on the method developed by Khare, Larsen and Ramakrishna in
[

]
.

(2)

We show that there is a finite set of nice primes

containing

such that the Selmer group

[

(3)

The space
\mathcal{N}_{p}
stabilizes the mod-

. The method of Ramakrishna produces a lift

which satisfies the condition

. The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture predicts that

arises from a normalized Hecke eigencuspform

. This has been proved by Kisin, cf.
[
Kis09
]
. Since the condition

is satisfied at

is equal to

(cf. condition
(
1
)
on p.6).

Lifting

to characteristic zero involves a standard argument which goes back to the original works of Ramakrishna. We review this construction in complete detail. Since

is chosen so that the dual Selmer group

is zero, it follows from the Poitou Tate long exact sequence that the restriction map

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{S\cup X}}\bigoplus_{v\in S\cup X}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{v}}

is surjective. Let

and

be a lift of

which is unramified outside

and satisfies the conditions

at each prime

and the condition

at

We show that
\rho_{m}
may be lifted to

which satisfies the same conditions. One may always choose a continuous lift

as depicted

\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}

4.
Recollections on the Fontaine-Laffaille Functor

Let
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})
denote the ring of Witt-vectors with residue field
\mathbb{F}
and let
K
denote its fraction field
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})[p^{-1}]
. Let
\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be the Frobenius element and let
\mathbb{C}_{p}
denote the completion of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
w.r.t the valuation
v_{p}
. Recall the notion of a (filtered)
\varphi
-module.

Definition 4.1
.

A filtered
\varphi
-module
M
is a
K
vector space equipped with a semilinear bijective map
\varphi
with respect to
\sigma
and a decreasing filtration
\{F^{i}M\}
. For this filtration,
F^{i}M=M
for
i\ll 0
and
F^{i}M=0
for
i\gg 0
.

The
p
-adic Galois representations that arise from the étale cohomology of varieties over
\mathbb{Q}
satisfy some natural conditions at
p
. From this perspective, there are two predominant conditions at
p
, the ordinary condition and the crystalline condition. The ordinary condition may be described in concrete representation theoretic terms. In this paper, we assume that
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is irreducible, and thus we are not in the ordinary setup. The crystalline condition on the other hand is rather subtle, as these are the representations that arise from the Galois action on the generic fibre of a finite flat group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The crystalline period functor associates a
\varphi
-module to a crystalline representation
\varrho:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K)
. This allows one to classify crystalline Galois representations by classifying the associated
\varphi
-modules. The Fontaine-Laffaille functor on the other hand will allow us to parametrize flat deformations of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
. This approach helps reduce the study of finite flat group schemes to linear algebra in the category of Fontaine-Laffaille modules. Ramakrishna used the properties of the Fontaine-Laffaille functor to explicitly characterize flat deformation rings. In this section, we review some properties of the Fontaine-Laffaille functor and make preparations to describe a fixed slope subfunctor of the functor of flat deformations of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\text{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
. The standard references for this section are
[
FL82
,
Ram93
,
Pat06
]
. For an introduction to the flat deformation functor, see
[
Con97
]
.

Let us recall the construction of Fontaine’s crystalline period ring
\text{B}_{\rm{cris}}
and the crystalline period functor
\text{D}_{\rm{cris}}
. Set
\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
to denote the characteristic-
p
ring given by the inverse limit
\varprojlim\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}/p
w.r.t the
p
-power maps
x\mapsto x^{p}
. We choose a distinguished element
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{i})
in
\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
for which
\varepsilon_{0}
is a primitive
p
-th root of unity. For
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
, denote by
[x]
the Teichmüller lift of
x
in
\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})
. The continuous Galois-equivariant ring homomorphism

\theta:\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
, defined by
\theta\left(\sum_{i}[x^{(i)}]p^{i}\right):=\sum_{i}x^{(i)}_{0}p^{i}
, is open and surjective. The kernel of
\theta
is a principal ideal generated by a special element
\xi\in\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})
with some key properties stated in
[
BC09
, Proposition 4.4.3]
. Let
\text{A}_{\rm{cris}}
denote the divided power envelope of
\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})
with respect to the ideal
\ker\theta

The category of finite dimensional continuous

Definition 4.2

Fontaine’s crystalline period

In order to study flat deformations of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

Definition 4.3
.

A Fontaine-Laffaille module

is a finitely generated
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})
-module that is furnished with a decreasing, exhaustive, separated filtration of
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})
-submodules
\{F^{i}M\}
and for each integer

. Furthermore, for each
i
, there is a
\sigma
-semilinear map

\varphi^{i}=\varphi_{M}^{i}:F^{i}M\rightarrow M.

Furthermore, the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

there exists
j_{0}\geq 0
such that
F^{-j}M=M
and
F^{j}M=0
for all
j\geq j_{0}
,

(2)

\varphi^{i+1}=p\varphi^{i}
,

(3)

\sum_{i}\varphi^{i}(F^{i}M)=M
.

Denote by
\varphi
the map
\varphi^{0}:F^{0}M\rightarrow M
.

A map

of Fontaine-Laffaille modules is a
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})
-module map such that
f(F^{i}M)\subseteq F^{i}N
and

\varphi^{i}_{N}\circ f_{\restriction F^{i}M}=f\circ\varphi^{i}_{M}
.

Following

\text{U}_{S}\simeq\text{T}_{\rm{cris}}^{*}

=&{\rm{Hom}}_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}({\rm{Hom}}_{\text{MF}}(M,\varinjlim_{n}\text{A}_{\rm{cris}}/p^{n}),K/\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})(1)).\end{split}

)
by simplifying both sides of the isomorphism. We observe that

\begin{split}&{\rm{Hom}}_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}(\hat{\text{T}}^{*}_{\rm{cris}}(\mathcal{M}),\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})(1))\\
=&{\rm{Hom}}_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}({\rm{Hom}}_{MF}(\mathcal{M},\text{A}_{\rm{cris}}),\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})(1))\\
\simeq&{\rm{Hom}}_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}({\rm{Hom}}_{MF}(\mathcal{M},\text{A}_{\rm{cris}})\otimes_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}K/\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}),K/\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})(1))\\
\simeq&{\rm{Hom}}_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}(\varinjlim_{n}{\rm{Hom}}_{MF}(\mathcal{M},\text{A}_{\rm{cris}})/p^{n},K/\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})(1)).\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}L\simeq&\varprojlim_{m}\text{T}_{1}(\mathcal{M}/p^{m})\\
\simeq&\varprojlim_{m}{\rm{Hom}}_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}({\rm{Hom}}_{MF}(\mathcal{M}/p^{m},\varinjlim_{n}\text{A}_{\rm{cris}}/p^{n}),K/\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})(1))\\
\simeq&{\rm{Hom}}_{\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})}(\varinjlim_{m}{\rm{Hom}}_{MF}(\mathcal{M}/p^{m},\varinjlim_{n}\text{A}_{\rm{cris}}/p^{n}),K/\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})(1))\\

On the other hand, we observe that

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X_{m+1}^{\prime}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S\cup X_{m}}H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})

is an isomorphism.

Let
v
be a prime in
S\cup X_{m}
. There is a local cohomology class

z_{v}\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})

such that the twist
(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}z_{v})\rho_{m+1}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{v}
for
v\neq p
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
for
v=p
. Since the map
(
6.1
)
is an isomorphism, there exists
z\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X_{m+1}^{\prime}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
such that
z_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}=z_{v}
for each prime
v\in S\cup X_{m}
. Thus, by construction, the twist
\rho_{m+1}^{\prime}:=(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}z)\rho_{m+1}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{v}
at each prime
v\in(S\backslash\{p\})\cup X_{m}
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
at
p
. We are not done, since the twist is not known to satisfy the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{v}
for
v\in X_{m+1}^{\prime}\backslash X_{m}
. At each prime
v\in X_{m+1}^{\prime}\backslash X_{m}
, choose a cohomology class
h_{v}\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
such that
(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}h_{v})\rho_{m+1|\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}^{\prime}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{v}
. Note that
h_{v}
is determined up to
\mathcal{N}_{v}
, i.e. if
g_{v}\in\mathcal{N}_{v}
then
(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}(h_{v}+g_{v}))\rho_{m+1|\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}^{\prime}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{v}
. According to Lemma
3.5
, the space
\mathcal{N}_{v}
consists of cohomology classes
h
such that
h(\sigma_{v})=0
. According to
[
KLR05
, Corollary 11]
there is a finite set of nice primes
X_{m+1}
containing
X_{m+1}^{\prime}
such that
\rho_{m+1}^{\prime}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{v}
at each prime
v\in X_{m+1}\backslash X_{m+1}^{\prime}
and there is a cohomology class
g\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X_{m+1}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
such that

(1)

g_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}=0
for
v\in X_{m}
,

(2)

g(\sigma_{v})=h_{v}(\sigma_{v})
for
v\in X_{m+1}^{\prime}\backslash X_{m}
,

(3)

g(\sigma_{v})=0
for
v\in X_{m+1}\backslash X_{m+1}^{\prime}
.

In other words,
g_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}\in h_{v}+\mathcal{N}_{v}
for
v\in X_{m+1}^{\prime}\backslash X_{m}
and
g_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}\in\mathcal{N}_{v}
for
v\in X_{m+1}\backslash X_{m+1}^{\prime}
. Recall that
\rho_{m+1}^{\prime}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{v}
for
v\in X_{m+1}\backslash X_{m+1}^{\prime}
, thus,
(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}g)\rho_{m+1}^{\prime}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{v}
at
v
as well. The twist
(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}g)\rho_{m+1}^{\prime}
satisfies
(\star)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof.

(of Theorem
1.1
)
Corollary
6.3
asserts that there is a finite set of nice primes
Z
containing
V
such that there is a deformation

\rho_{\lambda+2}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup Z}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})/p^{\lambda+2})

of
\bar{\rho}
satisfying
(\star)
. There is a finite set of nice primes
X
containing
Z
such that the dual Selmer group
H^{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})
is zero. This is a standard argument, see the proofs of
[
Tay03
, Lemma 1.2]
or
[
Pat16
, Proposition 5.2, Lemma 5.3]
.

We show that
\rho_{\lambda+2}
may be inductively lifted to a characteristic zero representation

\rho:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}))

satisfying the condition
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
at
p
. Moreover,
\rho
shall satisfy
\mathcal{C}_{v}
at the primes
v\in S\backslash\{p\}\cup X
as well. Here, we recall that for
v\in S\backslash\{p\}
, the deformation functor
\mathcal{C}_{v}
is chosen as in Proposition
3.3
, and for
v\in X
, it is specified by Definition
3.4
.

We show by induction that
\rho_{\lambda+2}
may be lifted to a characteristic zero representation
\rho
.
Since
H^{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0
, by Poitou-Tate, it follows that the restriction map

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{S\cup X}}\bigoplus_{v\in S\cup X}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{v}}

is surjective. Let
m\geq\lambda+2
and
\rho_{m}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})/p^{m})
be a lift of
\rho_{\lambda+2}
which satisfies the specified local conditions
(\star)
at the primes
S\cup X
(see Definition
6.1
).
The inductive argument involves lifting
\rho_{m}
one more step to
\rho_{m+1}
, so that the same conditions are satisfied. Recall from section
3.2
, that if the obstruction class
\mathcal{O}(\rho_{m})
is equal to zero, then
\rho_{m}
must lift one more step. Since
\rho_{m}
satisfies a liftable deformation condition at each prime
v\in S\cup X
, the obstruction class
\mathcal{O}(\rho_{m})
is trivial when restricted to a prime
v\in S\cup X
. Therefore, it belongs to
\Sha^{2}_{S\cup X}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
. As explained in section
3.2
, since the dual Selmer group is zero, so is
\Sha^{2}_{S\cup X}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
. Hence
\rho_{m}
lifts to a representation

\rho_{m+1}^{\prime}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})/p^{m+1}).

It is shown that there is a suitable global cohomology class
z\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
, such that the twist

\rho_{m+1}:=(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}z)\rho_{m+1}^{\prime}

satisfies the specified local conditions
(\star)
at the primes
S\cup X
. At each prime
v\in(S\backslash\{p\})\cup X
, there is a cohomology class
z_{v}\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
such that the twist

(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}z_{v}){\rho_{m+1}}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}\in\mathcal{C}_{v}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})/p^{m+1})

and a class
z_{p}\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
such that

(\operatorname{Id}+p^{m}z_{p}){\rho_{m+1}}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}(\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F})/p^{m+1}).

Since
m\geq\lambda+2
, it follows from Proposition
5.8
that
\mathcal{N}_{p}
stabilizes
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
. The surjectivity of
\operatorname{res}_{S\cup X}
implies that the tuple

(z_{v})\in\bigoplus_{v\in S\cup X}H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})/\mathcal{N}_{v}

arises from a global cohomology class
z
which is unramified outside
S\cup X
. Therefore,
\rho_{m+1}
satisfies the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{v}
at each prime
v\in(S\backslash\{p\})\cup X
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
at
p
. In other words, it satisfies the condition
(\star)
. This completes the inductive lifting argument. Thus, there is a characteristic zero lift
\rho
unramified away from
S\cup X
, satisfying
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
at
p
.

Recall that all deformations are assumed to have determinant
\chi
and thus, it follows from the main theorem of
[
Kis09
]
that
\rho
arises from a normalized eigencuspform
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
. Since
\rho
is crystalline, the level
N
is prime to
p
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the prime above
p
in
\mathbb{Q}(f)
so that
\rho=\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
\rho
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\lambda}
at
p
, it follows from Proposition
5.2
that

v_{p}(\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(a_{p}))=\lambda.

The proof is complete.
∎

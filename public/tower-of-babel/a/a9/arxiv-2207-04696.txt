Generation of maximally-entangled long-lived states with giant atoms in a waveguide
Alan C. Santos, R. Bachelard

Abstract
In this paper we show how to generate efficiently entanglement between two artificial giant atoms with photon-mediated interactions in a waveguide. Taking advantage of the adjustable decay processes of giant atoms into the waveguide, and of the interference processes, spontaneous sudden birth of entanglement can be strongly enhanced with giant atoms. Highly entangled states can also be generated in the steady-state regime when the system is driven by a resonant classical field. We show that the statistics of the light emitted by the system can be used as a witness of the presence of entanglement in the system, since giant photon bunching is observed close to the regime of maximal entanglement. Given the degree of quantum correlations incoherently generated in this system, our results open a broad avenue for the generation of quantum correlations and manipulation of photon statistics in systems of giant atoms.



Driven artificial atoms in a waveguide constitute the heart of superconducting quantum circuits
Wallraff et al. 2004
, in which the photon-mediated interaction between distant atoms can lead to collective effects such as sub-radiance/superradiance

;
Mlynek et al. 2014

;
Zheng et al. 2012

N
two-level atoms

;
DiCarlo et al. 2009
and quantum communication

This has lead to the development of strategies to harness this decoherence and generate entanglement between sub-systems, for instance, by coupling the system whether to thermal baths of negative temperature

In this scenario, identifying configurations where the effect of the environment on the entanglement is reduced to its minimum is an important task for the development of quantum technologies. In the frame of waveguide quantum electrodynamics, a promising alternative to small emitters has been introduced:
giant atoms
are emitters whose coupling with a guided wave extends over such a length that their interaction cannot be described as that of point-like emitters

;
Kockum 2020
. In particular, the dipole approximation does not hold any longer

;
Noguchi et al. 2017
;
Bolgar et al. 2018

;
Vadiraj et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Du et al. 2022a
;
Zhao and Wang 2020
and decoherence-free mediated interaction between two atoms

;
Soro and Kockum 2022
, make this system an ideal platform for the robust generation of entanglement
Kannan et al. 2020
;
Yu et al. 2021
. The multiple connections between each atom and the waveguide, and the possibility to intertwine the different atoms through their connection points, opens a broad field of perspectives for the manipulation of entangled states and for information processing, which has barely been scratched up to now

In this paper we study the generation of entanglement for two giant atoms through non-unitary (incoherent) decay processes. More specifically, we show how two giant atoms within a tunable frequency
\omega_{0}
of the light in the waveguide, see sketch in Fig.

\omega_{0}

Finally, we show that the statistics of the light emitted by the two giant atoms in the waveguide can be used as a entanglement witness in the system, so the entanglement can be monitored in a non-destructive way through the scattered light.

Maximally entangled steady state –

Conclusion.
Considering a system of two giant atoms driven by photon-mediated interaction and external fields, we have studied entanglement generation, and quantified it by the concurrence between the atoms. We showed that the phase acquired by the traveling photon inside the waveguide (related to the frequency of the light in the waveguide
Frisk Kockum et al. 2014
) allows us to design a two-qubit system with both frequency-tunable collective decay rates and transition rates between the states of the system. Consequently, frequency-tunable collective effects of two giant atoms can be achieved. We have taken advantage of these properties to create a high degree of entanglement in the system through two different processes: free decay and pump-driven dynamics. The amount of concurrence obtained for giant atoms is much larger than for small (pointlike) atoms. In particular, it is possible to create a quasi-maximally-entangled steady state with giant atoms, by adequately adjusting the Rabi frequency of the classical external field which drives the system. Finally, we observe that the statistics of the light emitted in the waveguide are a witness of the concurrence generated in the system, so it can be used as a non-destructive measurement for entanglement. By adjusting the external field detuning
\Delta_{\text{p}}
, giant photon bunching emission
Jahnke et al. 2016
is observed as the entanglement increases.

. Therefore, experimental realization and the immediate extension of our analysis for systems of many giant atoms are left for future prospects.

Appendix A
Derivation of the master equation

In this section we present a brief derivation of the master equation which describes the giant atom dynamics when driven by an external field. First of all, we shortly describe how to obtain the master equation for the case where no external pump is applied to the atoms (see
Kockum et al. 2018
for a detailed discussion). Inspired by the experimental realization in superconducting atoms
Kannan et al. 2020
, Fig.
S1
a
shows the schematic representation of two nested giant atoms in superconducting circuit. Two artificial atoms (transmon qubits) are capacitively connected to a common waveguide. The master equation for this system is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\rho}(t)=\frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{H}_{1}^{0}+\hat{H}_{2}^{0},\hat{\rho}(t)]+\mathcal{D}(\hat{\rho}),

(S1)

where we define the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{n}^{0}=\hbar(\omega_{n}+\delta_{n})\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{n}\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{n}
for each atom
n
, and
\mathcal{D}(\hat{\rho})
describes the effects of the interaction between the atoms and the waveguide (as defined in the main text). Now, we need to compute the term
\mathcal{D}(\hat{\rho})
to properly describe the dynamics of the system. To this end, we can use the SLH formalism
Combes et al. 2017
, which is also the approach considered in Ref.
Kockum et al. 2018
. In this formalism we need the number
n
of input and output ports to find the triplet of elements
S
(a
n\times n
scattering matrix),
L
(a vector with
n
matrices describing the system-environment interaction), and
\hat{H}
the Hamiltonian of the system (including interactions
Combes et al. 2017
). The triplet is then denoted by
G=\left(\textbf{S},\textbf{L},\hat{H}\right)
, and the system is completely described by the resulting triplet
G_{\mathrm{res}}
, obtained using SLH composition rules for
G_{k}

Combes et al. 2017
. For example, for the case of two-nested atoms (with no pump) the SLH diagram is given by Fig.
S1
a
, and the resulting triplet is obtained from
right
- and
left
-moving photons through their
independent
contributions encoded as

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{R}}

\displaystyle=G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,4}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{3}}\triangleleft G_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,3}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{2}}\triangleleft G_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,2}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{1}}\triangleleft G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,1},

(S2)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{L}}

\displaystyle=G_{\mathrm{L}}^{1,1}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{1}}\triangleleft G_{\mathrm{L}}^{2,2}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{2}}\triangleleft G_{\mathrm{L}}^{2,3}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{3}}\triangleleft G_{\mathrm{L}}^{1,4},

(S3)

where we have defined

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{1}^{-},\hat{H}_{1}^{0}\right),\penalty\ G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,4}=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{4}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{1}^{-},0\right),

(S4)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{L}}^{1,1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{1}^{-},0\right),\penalty\ G_{\mathrm{L}}^{1,4}=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{4}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{1}^{-},0\right),

(S5)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{2}^{-},\hat{H}_{2}^{0}\right),\penalty\ G_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,3}=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{3}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{2}^{-},0\right),

(S6)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{L}}^{2,2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{2}^{-},0\right),\penalty\ G_{\mathrm{L}}^{2,3}=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{3}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{2}^{-},0\right),

(S7)

and the elements
G_{\varphi_{n}}
are the phase acquired by the photon traveling into the waveguide. The notation
G_{n}\triangleleft G_{k}
refers to the
cascade rule
of the SLH formalism
Combes et al. 2017
. Therefore, the resulting triplet reads
G_{\mathrm{res}}=G_{\mathrm{R}}\boxplus G_{\mathrm{L}}
, in which
G_{n}\boxplus G_{k}
denotes the
concatenation product
.

(a) Sketch of two nested giant superconducting atoms, where the atom 1 and 2 connect to waveguide, though a capacitive coupling, at points

Now, let us use the same methodology to include the local control of the atoms. In superconducting giant atoms the individual and local control of the atoms can be done by the direct capacitive coupling of each atom with their own XY-drive line
Barends et al. 2014
;
Krantz et al. 2019
, as shown in Fig.
S1
b
. In this case, the Hamiltonian for the atoms, including control, is given by
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{1}+\hat{H}_{2}
, with

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{n}=\hat{H}_{n}^{0}+\hat{H}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}=\hat{H}_{n}^{0}+\hbar\Omega_{0}\left(\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}+\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}\right),

(S8)

with
\Omega_{0}
the Rabi frequency of this pump. Here we highlight two important points: i) the above Hamiltonian does not depend on the driving field in the waveguide, and ii) the SLH rules are valid for any
H
on the localized system Hilbert space
Combes et al. 2017
. It means that the local external field can be suitably included by changing the local Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{n}^{0}
for another local Hamiltonian of the form
\hat{H}_{n}
. In fact, by using the SLH cascade rule given by
Gough and James 2009
;
Azodi et al. 2019

\displaystyle\left(\textbf{S}_{2},\textbf{L}_{2},\hat{H}_{2}\right)\triangleleft\left(\textbf{S}_{1},\textbf{L}_{1},\hat{H}_{1}\right)=\left(\textbf{S}_{2}\textbf{S}_{1},\textbf{L}_{2}+\textbf{S}_{2}\textbf{L}_{1},\hat{H}_{\mathrm{res}}\right),

(S9)

where the resulting Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{res}}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{res}}=\hat{H}_{1}+\hat{H}_{2}+\mathrm{Im}\left(\textbf{L}^{\dagger}_{2}\textbf{S}_{2}\textbf{L}_{1}\right),

(S10)

it is possible to conclude that

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,1}\triangleleft\left(\mathbbm{1},\textbf{0},\hat{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{c}}\right)=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{1}^{-},\hat{H}_{1}^{0}+\hat{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{c}}\right)=\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,1}.

(S11)

Therefore, by including an external local control
\hat{H}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}
, we do not change the sector of the triplet
G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,1}
associated to the dissipative term
\mathcal{D}(\hat{\rho})
and responsible for any interaction of the atoms with the waveguide. Only the Hamiltonian sector of
G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,1}
is affected, but it does not promote any new interaction in the system. A similar calculation can be done to show that

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,2}\triangleleft\left(\mathbbm{1},\textbf{0},\hat{H}_{2}^{\mathrm{c}}\right)=\left(\mathbbm{1},\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{2}^{-},\hat{H}_{2}^{0}+\hat{H}_{2}^{\mathrm{c}}\right)=\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,2},

(S12)

such that the new resulting SLH triplet is
\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{res}}=\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{R}}\boxplus G_{\mathrm{L}}
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{R}}

\displaystyle=G_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,4}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{3}}\triangleleft G_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,3}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{2}}\triangleleft\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{R}}^{2,2}\triangleleft G_{\varphi_{1}}\triangleleft\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{R}}^{1,1}.

(S13)

In Fig.
S1
b
we show the change promoted in the SLH diagram due to the local control on each atom.

Appendix B
Computing decay rates

Let us consider the two-atom system governed by the master equation given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\rho}(t)=\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}(t)],

(S14)

where
\mathcal{L}[\bullet]
is a Louvillian that depends on the system we are dealing with. We consider a complete basis for the two-atom system, composed of the double excited state
\ket{ee}
, ground-state
\ket{gg}
, and
\ket{\psi_{\pm}}
(defined in the main text). We are here interested in the decay from a state
\rho(0)
to one of the target states
\{\ket{ee},\ket{gg},\ket{\psi_{\pm}}\}
. In this case, the evolved state can be computed as

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{g}(t)\hat{\rho}_{gg}+p_{e}(t)\hat{\rho}_{ee}+p_{\psi_{+}}(t)\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{+}}+p_{\psi_{-}}(t)\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{-}}.

(S15)

with
\hat{\rho}_{x}=\ket{x}\bra{x}
. Then, using these equations, the dynamics of each population in the above equation is

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{g}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{g}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{gg}]\hat{\rho}_{gg})+p_{e}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{ee}]\hat{\rho}_{gg})

\displaystyle+p_{\psi_{+}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{+}}]\hat{\rho}_{gg})+p_{\psi_{-}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{-}}]\hat{\rho}_{gg}),

(S16a)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{e}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{g}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{gg}]\hat{\rho}_{ee})+p_{e}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{ee}]\hat{\rho}_{ee})

\displaystyle+p_{\psi_{+}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{+}}]\hat{\rho}_{ee})+p_{\psi_{-}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{-}}]\hat{\rho}_{ee}),

(S16b)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{\psi_{\pm}}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{g}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{gg}]\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{\pm}})+p_{e}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{ee}]\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{\pm}})

\displaystyle+p_{\psi_{+}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{+}}]\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{\pm}})+p_{\psi_{-}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{-}}]\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{\pm}}).

(S16c)

Let us discuss the meaning of these dynamical equations through the example of Eq. (
S16a
). Now, to follow our claim, we need to take a particular form to
\mathcal{L}[\bullet]
. For the master equation considered in the main text, defined in Eq. (
2
), it is possible to show that

\displaystyle\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{gg}]\hat{\rho}_{gg})=\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{ee}]\hat{\rho}_{gg})=0,

(S17)

such that

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{g}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{\psi_{+}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{+}}]\hat{\rho}_{gg})+p_{\psi_{-}}(t)\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{\psi_{-}}]\hat{\rho}_{gg}).

(S18)

The gain of population in the ground state depends on the populations
p_{\psi_{+}}(t)
and
p_{\psi_{-}}(t)
, as well as the coefficients that set the decay rate of the states
\ket{\psi_{+}}
. Then, without loss of generality, we find the decay rate from the state
\ket{x}
to
\ket{gg}
in according with the above equation as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{x\rightarrow g}=\tr(\Lcal[\hat{\rho}_{x}]\hat{\rho}_{g}).

(S19)

We use this analysis to compute the decay rates shown in the main text. It is worth mentioning that the analysis done here is only valid for decay dynamics, as given by the master equation considered in the main text. Other kinds of decoherence, such as bit-flip or dephasing, for example, need to be studied on a case-by-case basis..

Appendix E
Light emitted by giant atoms into a waveguide

Let us consider the Hamiltonian given by
H=H_{\text{a}}+H_{\text{f}}+H_{\text{c}}
, where
H_{\text{a}}\!=\!\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hbar\omega_{n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}
is the bare Hamiltonian for the atoms (two-level systems),

\displaystyle H_{\text{f}}=\sum_{\nu}\hbar\varpi_{\nu}(a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\nu}a_{\mathbf{r}\nu}+a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}a_{\mathbf{l}\nu}),

(S22)

is the field Hamiltonian with creation
a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\nu}
(
a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}
) and annihilation
a_{\mathbf{r}\nu}
(
a_{\mathbf{l}\nu}
) operators for right-moving (left-moving) bosonic modes with frequency
\varpi_{\nu}
. Finally, the atom-waveguide interaction is described by the coupling Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\text{c}}=\hbar\sum_{\nu,n}g_{\nu,n}\left[\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}+\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}\right]\left[\alpha^{\ast}_{n\nu}a_{\mathbf{l}\nu}+\alpha_{n\nu}a_{\mathbf{r}\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right],

(S23)

where
g_{\nu,n}
is coupling strength of the
n
-th atom with
\nu
-th mode, and
\alpha_{n\nu}\!=\!\sum_{j=1}^{K_{n}}e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}x_{j}^{(n)}/c}
are complex amplitudes that take into account the phase acquired by the bosonic mode at the point
x_{j}^{(n)}
of the
j
-th connection point of the
n
-th atom with the waveguide.

As a first approximation, let us neglect the terms of the form
\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}a_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}
and
\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}
, since they can be eliminated by RWA approximation. Then, in the Heisenberg picture we can write the dynamical equation for each mode
a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}
and
a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\nu}
as

\displaystyle\dot{a}^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}=\frac{i}{\hbar}[H,a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}]=\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{\text{f}},a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}]+\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{\text{c}},a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}].

(S24)

By using that

\displaystyle[a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu},a_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\mu}]=\delta_{\nu\mu}\mathbbm{1},\penalty\ \penalty\ [a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{r}/\mathbf{l})\nu},a_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\mu}]=0,

(S25)

we obtain

\displaystyle[H_{\text{f}},a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\hbar\varpi_{\mu}[(a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\mu}a_{\mathbf{r}\mu}+a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\mu}a_{\mathbf{l}\mu}),a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}]

\displaystyle=\hbar\varpi_{\mu}a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}.

(S26)

In addition, the following commutation relation is derived:

\displaystyle[H_{\text{c}},a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}]

\displaystyle=\hbar\sum_{\mu,n}g_{\mu,n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}\left[\left(\alpha_{n\mu}^{\ast}a_{\mathbf{l}\mu}+\alpha_{n\mu}a_{\mathbf{r}\mu}\right),a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}\right]

\displaystyle=\hbar\sum_{\mu,n}g_{\mu,n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}\left(\alpha_{n\mu}^{\ast}\delta_{\mathbf{l}(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})}\delta_{\mu\nu}+\alpha_{n\mu}\delta_{\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})}\delta_{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\hbar\sum_{n=1}^{N}g_{\nu,n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}\left(\alpha_{n\nu}^{\ast}\delta_{\mathbf{l}(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})}+\alpha_{n\nu}\delta_{\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})}\right).

(S27)

where we have used that
\left[a_{\mathbf{l}\mu},a^{\dagger}_{(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})\nu}\right]\!=\!\delta_{\mathbf{l}(\mathbf{l}/\mathbf{r})}\delta_{\mu\nu}\mathbbm{1}
. Therefore, we find

\displaystyle\dot{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}

\displaystyle=i\varpi_{\mu}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}+i\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n\nu}^{\ast}g_{\nu,n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+},

(S28a)

\displaystyle\dot{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\nu}

\displaystyle=i\varpi_{\mu}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\nu}+i\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n\nu}g_{\nu,n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}.

(S28b)

Now, we can use that

\displaystyle\dot{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}-i\varpi_{\mu}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}

\displaystyle=e^{i\varpi t}\left[e^{-i\varpi t}\frac{da^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}}{dt}-i\varpi e^{-i\varpi t}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{i\varpi t}\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-i\varpi t}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}\right)

(S29)

to write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}t}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}\right)=ie^{-i\varpi_{\nu}t}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n\nu}^{\ast}g_{\nu,n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}.

(S30)

It is then integrated to get

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}(t)=a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{l}\nu}(0)e^{i\varpi_{\nu}t}+i\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n\nu}^{\ast}g_{\nu,n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}(t^{\prime}-t)}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

(S31)

In a similar way, we find that

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\nu}(t)=a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}\nu}(0)e^{i\varpi_{\nu}t}+i\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n\nu}g_{\nu,n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}(t^{\prime}-t)}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(S32)

and thus

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{l}\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=a_{\mathbf{l}\nu}(0)e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}t}-i\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n\nu}g_{\nu,n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}(t-t^{\prime})}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(S33a)

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{r}\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=a_{\mathbf{r}\nu}(0)e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}t}-i\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha^{\ast}_{n\nu}g_{\nu,n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}(t-t^{\prime})}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

(S33b)

Now, by integrating over the frequency
k
, one gets

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{E}}(t)

\displaystyle=L\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a_{\mathbf{l}\nu}(t)dk

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(t)-i\sum_{n=1}^{N}g_{n}\int_{0}^{t}L\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}(k)e^{ikc(t-t^{\prime})}dk\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(S34)

with
\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(t)\!=\!L\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a_{\mathbf{l}\nu}(0)e^{-i\varpi_{\nu}t}
,
L
a normalization parameter. We have here used
\varpi_{\nu}\!=\!-kc
for the left traveling photon, while the coefficients
\alpha_{n\nu}
and
g_{n\nu}
are now functions of
k
denoted as
\alpha_{n}(k)
and
g_{n}(k)
, respectively. Now, we assume that the phase-shift at the connection point of the atom varies very slowly with
k
, so that
|\partial_{k}\alpha_{n}(k)|\!\ll\!1
, so we can simplify the above equation as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{E}}(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(t)-i\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{n}}g_{n}e^{-ikx_{j}^{(n)}}\int_{0}^{t}L\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ikc(t-t^{\prime})}dk\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(t)-i\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{n}}g_{n}e^{-ikx_{j}^{(n)}}\int_{0}^{t}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(t)-i\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{n}}g_{n}e^{-ikx_{j}^{(n)}}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t).

(S35)

Then, the above equation shows that the field in the waveguide is a combination of the input field
\hat{E}_{0}(t)\!\propto\!\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(t)
and the field emitted by the atoms
\hat{E}_{\mathbf{l}}(t)
, which we identify as being the second term in right-hand side. Therefore, we write that the operator of the field emitted by the
N
atom system is given by

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{\mathbf{l}}(t)\propto\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{n}}e^{-ikx_{j}^{(n)}}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t),

(S36)

so that the case of small atoms can be recovered by taking
K_{n}\!=\!1
. Now, by considering the operator
a_{\mathbf{r}\nu}(t)
we can show that

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{\mathbf{r}}(t)\propto\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{n}}e^{ikx_{j}^{(n)}}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{-}(t).

(S37)

Appendix F
The
Q
Mandel parameter

The
Q
Mandel parameter was originally proposed as
Mandel 1979
;
Mandel and Wolf 1995

\displaystyle Q_{\text{M}}=\frac{\langle\hat{n}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{n}\rangle^{2}}{\langle\hat{n}\rangle}=\frac{\langle\hat{n}^{2}\rangle}{\langle\hat{n}\rangle}-\langle\hat{n}\rangle,

(S38)

with
\hat{n}\!=\!a^{\dagger}a
, in which we can use function
g^{(2)}(0)\!=\!\langle\hat{n}^{2}\rangle/\langle\hat{n}\rangle^{2}
, so that

\displaystyle Q_{\text{M}}=\langle\hat{n}\rangle\left(g^{(2)}(0)-1\right).

(S39)

Traces of Hecke operators on Drinfeld modular forms for 𝔽 q ​ [ T ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
Sjoerd de Vries

Abstract
In this paper, we study traces of Hecke operators on Drinfeld modular forms of level 1 in the case A = 𝔽 q ​ [ T ] A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T] . We deduce closed-form expressions for traces of Hecke operators corresponding to primes of degree at most 2 and provide algorithms for primes of higher degree. We improve the Ramanujan bound and deduce the decomposition of cusp forms of level Γ 0 ​ ( 𝔭 ) \Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}) into oldforms and newforms, as conjectured by Bandini-Valentino, under the hypothesis that each Hecke eigenvalue has multiplicity less than p p .

Introduction

Drinfeld modular forms are function field analogues of elliptic modular forms. At the heart of this analogy lies the one between Drinfeld modules of rank 2 and elliptic curves, both of which can be realised as rank 2 lattices in the algebraic closure of the completion of a global field at infinity. To fix ideas, let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of order
q
and characteristic
p
, let
K
be the function field of a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
\infty
be a place of
K
, and let
A
be the ring of integers in
K
(the elements of
K
with poles only at
\infty
). Drinfeld modular forms are then defined as certain functions on the moduli space of Drinfeld
A
-modules.

The theory of Drinfeld modular forms bears, as expected, many similarities to the classical theory of modular forms. For instance, every modular form has a weight, and the space of modular forms of fixed weight is finite-dimensional. However, there are also notable differences. Let us mention two examples. First, Drinfeld modular forms
come equipped with a type, which can be seen as an element of
\mathbb{Z}/(q-1)\mathbb{Z}
and which is absent in the classical setting. Second, since everything happens in positive characteristic, there is no obvious analogue of a Petersson inner product, and as such there is also no straightforward analogue of notions derived from it. Chief among these is the decomposition of modular forms into oldforms and newforms. There is recent work initiated by Bandini-Valentino which aims to achieve a decomposition into oldforms and newforms via non-classical methods
[
BV22
]
.

In both settings, Hecke operators play a central role in the study of modular forms. Again, one finds striking similarities and differences. In both settings, there is a Hecke operator for each prime, and each Hecke operator respects the modular forms of a given weight. On the other hand, eigenvalues of Hecke operators classically arise as coefficients of
q
-expansions of modular forms; in the Drinfeld setting, there is no such relationship, except for some special modular forms (those with
A
-expansions). Even more jarringly, classical Hecke operators are always diagonalisable (they are orthogonal with respect to the Petersson inner product), but for Drinfeld modular forms this is not always the case
[
LM08
]
.

This mixture between familiarity and strangeness makes Drinfeld modular forms interesting objects of study, and much is yet to be understood, even in the simplest case
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. In this paper we study the Hecke operators
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, when
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, using a trace formula proved in
[
Vri24
]
. The trace formula is geometric, in the sense that it expresses the trace of a Hecke operator as a sum over points on the moduli space of Drinfeld modules. The number of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules over a finite field in a given isogeny class has been studied by Yu and Gekeler
[
Yu95
,
Gek08
]
, allowing for an explicit evaluation of the trace formula in some cases.

We now state our main results. Denote by
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
the space of Drinfeld cusp forms of weight
k
and type
l
, so that
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\neq 0
only if
k\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. Firstly, we obtain a closed-form expression for traces of Hecke operators for primes of degree 1.

Theorem 1.1

(Thm.
3.11
)
.

Fix
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. Then we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}{\binom{k-j}{j}}T^{j}.

In the appendix, we completely describe the number of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules over finite fields in a fixed isogeny class in terms of Hurwitz class numbers, extending the aforementioned results by Yu and Gekeler. The main application of this is the following: if
q
is even, then Theorem
1.1
models the traces of every Hecke operator.

Theorem 1.2

(Thm.
3.22
)
.

Suppose
2\mid q
. Fix
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. Then for any Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and any
n\geq 1
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}{\binom{k-j}{j}}\wp^{nj}.

Since Theorem
1.2
is valid for any
n\geq 1
, we can draw conclusions about the Hecke eigenvalues.

Theorem 1.3

(Thm.
5.9
and Thm.
5.13
)
.

Suppose
2\mid q
. Write
\wp
for the monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
. Then
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}
with odd algebraic multiplicity if and only if
\lambda=\wp^{j}
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
0\leq j<k/2
,
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}
, and
\binom{k-j}{j}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
.
Moreover, there are only finitely many weights in which the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
has no repeated eigenvalues.

If
q
is odd, the traces of Hecke operators are more complicated for primes of higher degree; see for instance Thm.
3.29
for primes of degree 2. Nonetheless, the traces can be effectively computed, as described in Algorithm
3.34
.

Generally speaking, it is a difficult task to determine whether a given Hecke operator is injective. Bandini and Valentino conjectured
[
BV22a
]
that the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{T}
is always injective on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
. We note that a result by Petrov and Joshi
[
JP14
]
in fact implies this result for any Hecke operator on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Thm.
5.3
)
.

The Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is injective on
\bigoplus_{k,l}\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
for any prime
\mathfrak{p}
.

Further conjectures of Bandini and Valentino aim to establish a decomposition of Drinfeld cusp forms of level
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
into oldforms and newforms. This is related to bounds on slopes of Hecke operators on Drinfeld cusp forms of level 1. We study analogous bounds on traces of Hecke operators in Section
4
. We have a precise guess for a sharp bound in this case (Conj.
4.3
), which we can prove in some special cases (Thm.
4.9
).

Although we are unable to prove Conjecture
4.3
in general, let alone the stronger version for slopes (Conj.
5.23
), we are able to improve the Ramanujan bound from
[
Vri24
]
(see Prop.
4.1
). This already has implications for the decomposition of cusp forms into oldforms and newforms.

Theorem 1.5

(Cor.
5.22
)
.

If
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}<p
, then
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))=\operatorname{S}^{\text{new}}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))\oplus\operatorname{S}^{\text{old}}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))
.

Using the trace formula, we also prove the following theorem, which addresses an open question about Drinfeld modular forms with
A
-expansions. Namely, given an eigenform
f\in\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
and an integer
n\geq 1
such that
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}f=\wp^{n-1}f
for all
\mathfrak{p}
, it does not necessarily follow that
f
has an
A
-expansion with
A
-exponent
n
.

Theorem 1.6

(Thm.
6.1
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{3}[T]
be a non-zero prime ideal with monic generator
\wp
. Then the
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-eigenvalue of
E^{2}h^{2}\in\operatorname{S}_{12,0}
equals
\wp^{3}
.

Many of our results have been implemented in
Magma
. The code can be found at
https://github.com/Sjoerd-deVries/DMF_Trace_Formula.git
.

]
.

Outline of the paper

In Section
2
, we recall the basic theory of Drinfeld modules and Drinfeld modular forms, including the necessary background on
A
-expansions.

In Section
3
, we begin our study of the trace formula. One of the first observations is a certain symmetry in weights of the form
k=p^{m}+1
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
which turns out to be very useful. We prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
and deduce several consequences. We obtain some results on traces of Hecke operators for primes of higher degree and describe an algorithm to compute these. We also obtain results on the traces of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
.

In Section
4
, we study the Ramanujan bound from
[
Vri24
]
. We prove that it is not sharp at level 1 and propose a stronger version. We prove the strong Ramanujan bound in some cases and give a sufficient condition for it to hold in general.

In Section
5
, we explore to what extent our results give information about the Hecke eigenvalues. In characteristic 2, we reach a satisfactory answer: we can explicitly determine all eigenvalues which occur with odd multiplicity, and we get no information whatsoever about the ones which occur with even multiplicity. We also prove Thm.
1.4
, and discuss the relationship between traces and slopes. In particular, we deduce the decomposition into oldforms and newforms at level
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
under the condition that no Hecke eigenvalue is repeated
p
times. We also formulate several conjectures based on our computations.

Section
6
is of a more computational nature. We mostly focus on 1-dimensional spaces of cusp forms. A computation in weight
12
for
q=3
yields the aforementioned result on
A
-expansions. We explicitly compute the
\mathbf{T}_{T}
-eigenvalues in types 0 and 2. For other types, we have partial results relating
\mathbf{T}_{T}
-eigenvalues through symmetry; in particular, this lets us compute the
\mathbf{T}_{T}
-eigenvalues on some eigenforms without
A
-expansions. We also briefly consider the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
when
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of degree 2.

In Appendix
A
, we make explicit the connection between Hurwitz class numbers, points on Jacobians of hyperelliptic curves, and isogeny classes of Drinfeld modules over finite fields.

Background

2.1

Drinfeld modular forms for
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]

Drinfeld modular forms are function field analogues of modular forms, originally defined by Goss in his PhD thesis
[
Gos80
]
and further studied by himself and Gekeler
[
Gos80a
,
Gek86
,
Gek88
]
; all the material of this section can be found in more detail there. Let
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
K=\Frac(A)
. Let
K_{\infty}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(T^{-1})\!)
be the completion of
K
at the place
\infty
, and let
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
be the completion of an algebraic closure of
K_{\infty}
. Denote by
\Omega:=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus K_{\infty}
the Drinfeld upper half-plane, seen as a rigid-analytic space. It has an action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(K_{\infty})
by Möbius transformations.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. A
Drinfeld modular form of weight
k
and type
l
is a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
satisfying the following properties:

1.

For each
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
, we have

f(\gamma z)=\det(\gamma)^{-l}(cz+d)^{k}f(z).

(1)

2.

f
is holomorphic on
\Omega
and at infinity.

If additionally
f
vanishes at infinity, it is called a
cusp form
. A cusp form which vanishes to order at least 2 at infinity is called a
double cusp form
. Denote the
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-vector space of Drinfeld modular forms of weight
k
and type
l
by
\operatorname{M}_{k,l}
, the subspace of cusp forms by
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
, and the subspace of double cusp forms by
\operatorname{S}^{2}_{k,l}
.

Note that
\operatorname{M}_{k,l}=\operatorname{M}_{k,l^{\prime}}
whenever
l\equiv l^{\prime}\pmod{q-1}
, since
\det(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]))=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Moreover,
\operatorname{M}_{k,l}=0
unless
k\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Classically, holomorphicity of a modular form at infinity implies that its
q
-series is a power series. For Drinfeld modular forms, an analogous property holds, but instead of a
q
-series one can define a so-called
t
-expansion; holomorphicity at infinity is then equivalent to the
t
-expansion being a power series, and being a cusp form is equivalent to the constant term being 0. For details, see
[
Gek88
, Section 5]
.

Multiplication induces maps

\operatorname{M}_{k,l}\times\operatorname{M}_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{M}_{k+k^{\prime},l+l^{\prime}}.

This turns the space of all Drinfeld modular forms into a doubly graded algebra, which we denote by
\operatorname{M}:=\bigoplus_{k,l}\operatorname{M}_{k,l}
. The doubly graded ideal of cusp forms is denoted
\operatorname{S}:=\bigoplus_{k,l}\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
.

When
k>0
is a multiple of
q-1
, there exists a non-zero Drinfeld modular form of weight
k
, non-vanishing at the cusp, called an Eisenstein series. For
k=k^{\prime}(q-1)
, denote this modular form by
E_{k}\in\operatorname{M}_{k,0}
. The first cusp form of type zero is denoted by
\Delta\in\operatorname{S}_{q^{2}-1,0}
. Moreover, there exists a modular form
h\in\operatorname{S}_{q+1,1}
such that
h^{q-1}=-\Delta
. The following theorem shows that the algebra of Drinfeld modular forms has a very simple structure
[
Gek88
, Thm. 5.13]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

We have an isomorphism of doubly graded algebras

\operatorname{M}=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[E_{q-1},h].

In particular, the ideal
\operatorname{S}
of cusp forms is the principal ideal generated by
h
, and similarly
\operatorname{S}^{2}
is generated by
h^{2}
. Theorem
2.3
implies that each
\operatorname{M}_{k,l}
is finite-dimensional and yields the following dimension formulae for the spaces of (double) cusp forms.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
1\leq l\leq q-1
. Then we have

\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}=\begin{cases}1+\left\lfloor\frac{k-l(q+1)}{q^{2}-1}\right\rfloor&\text{if }k\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}\text{ and }k\geq l(q+1);\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Moreover, the dimension of the space of double cusp forms is

\dim\operatorname{S}^{2}_{k,l}=\begin{cases}\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}-1&\text{if }l=1\text{ and }\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\neq 0;\\
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

One can also define Drinfeld modular forms of higher level. For a non-zero ideal
\mathfrak{n}\trianglelefteq A
, define

\Gamma(\mathfrak{n})=\left\{M\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)\ \bigg|\ M\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\pmod{\mathfrak{n}}\right\}.

Definition 2.5
.

A subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
is called a
congruence subgroup
if
\Gamma(\mathfrak{n})\subseteq\Gamma
for some ideal
\mathfrak{n}
.

In particular,

\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}):=\left\{M\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)\ \bigg|\ M\equiv\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{pmatrix}\pmod{\mathfrak{n}}\right\}

is a congruence subgroup for any
\mathfrak{n}
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
be a congruence subgroup. A
Drinfeld modular form of level
\Gamma
is a rigid-analytic function on
\Omega
, holomorphic at the cusps of
\Gamma

[
Gek86
, V.2.4]
, which satisfies the transformation property (
1
) for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. By a modular form of level 1, we mean a modular form for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
.

The space of modular forms of weight
k
, type
l
, and level
\Gamma
is denoted by
\operatorname{M}_{k,l}(\Gamma)
, and the subspace of cusp forms by
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma)
.

The set of all Drinfeld modular forms of level
\Gamma
is again a doubly graded algebra, which we denote by
\operatorname{M}(\Gamma)
. The doubly graded ideal of cusp forms, resp. double cusp forms is denoted
\operatorname{S}(\Gamma)
, resp.
\operatorname{S}^{2}(\Gamma)
.

2.2

Hecke operators

As in the classical case, Drinfeld modular forms admit actions of Hecke operators.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\wp\in A
be monic and irreducible, and write
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
for the maximal ideal generated by
\wp
. Define the
Hecke operator associated to
\mathfrak{p}
to be the linear map
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{M}_{k,l}\to\operatorname{M}_{k,l}
given by

(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}f)(z):=\wp^{k-1}f(\wp z)+\wp^{-1}\sum_{\deg(b)<\deg(\wp)}f\left(\frac{z+b}{\wp}\right).

The subalgebra of
\End_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\operatorname{M})
generated by all Hecke operators is called the
Hecke algebra
. A non-zero modular form
f\in\operatorname{M}
is called an
eigenform
if
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
acts as a scalar on
f
for every
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
.

Remark 2.7
.

1.

If
\wp
is a generator of
\mathfrak{p}
, we sometimes also write
\mathbf{T}_{\wp}
for the Hecke operator associated to
\mathfrak{p}
.

2.

Our definition of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a rescaled version of the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}
defined in
[
Gos80
,
Gek88
]
. More precisely,
we have

\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\wp^{-1}\mathbf{T}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

This normalisation is made purely to simplify computations and should not cause confusion. Note that the same normalisation is used in e.g.
[
Hat21
,
NR21
]
.

3.

The Hecke operators are linear endomorphisms of
\operatorname{M}
but do not preserve the ring structure in general. In particular, the Hecke eigenvalues of the generators
h
and
E_{q-1}
a priori say nothing about the Hecke eigenvalues of other modular forms.

4.

One can extend the definition of Hecke operators from prime ideals to arbitrary ideals. In doing so, one obtains the relation
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{n}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}=\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}
for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\trianglelefteq A
. In the classical setting, this relation only holds when
\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{Z}
are coprime. This difference can be explained by the fact that the Galois representation associated to a cuspidal eigenform
[
Böc02
, Section 14]
is one-dimensional, as opposed to two-dimensional in the classical setting.

The Hecke operators preserve the spaces
\operatorname{M}_{k,l}
and
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
for any
k
and
l
. Moreover, they preserve the space
\operatorname{S}^{2}_{k,l}
of double cusp forms (the modular forms which vanish to order two at infinity), a feature unique to the function field setting.

2.3

A
-expansions

Every Drinfeld modular form has a
t
-expansion, analogous to the
q
-series of elliptic modular forms. The
t
-expansion of
f\in\operatorname{M}
is an expansion of the form

f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n},\qquad a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty},

where
t=t(z)
is a parameter at infinity. In the spirit of function field arithmetic, one would like to replace the sum over
\mathbb{Z}
by a sum over
A
. In
[
Pet13
]
, Petrov showed that this can be done for certain Drinfeld modular forms
f
, but that such modular forms are very special. We recall some key points of the theory of
A
-expansions here.

Denote by
A_{+}
the set of monic polynomials in
A
. For
a\in A_{+}
, write
t_{a}:=t(az)
. For
n\geq 1
, denote by
G_{n}(X)
the
n
-th Goss polynomial (suitably normalised; see
[
Pet13
]
).

Definition 2.8
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{S}
be a Drinfeld cusp form. Then
f
has an
A
-expansion
if there exists an integer
n\geq 1
and constants
c_{a}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
such that

f=\sum_{a\in A_{+}}c_{a}G_{n}(t_{a}).

In this case,
n
is called an
A-exponent
of
f
.

If
f\in\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
has an
A
-expansion with
A
-exponent
n
, then necessarily
n\equiv l\pmod{q-1}
. If
f
is an eigenform with
A
-expansion, then its
A
-exponent is uniquely determined, but in general it is not known whether a non-zero cusp form can have
A
-expansions with different
A
-exponents.

The main theorem of
[
Pet13
]
gives infinitely many examples of eigenforms with
A
-expansions; moreover, all known examples of modular forms with
A
-expansions can be obtained by applying Petrov’s theorem. We highlight the following cases.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
q=p^{r}
. The following cusp forms are eigenforms with
A
-expansions.

1.

The forms
E^{n}h^{q}
for
0\leq n\leq q-1
, with
A
-exponent
(n+1)(q-1)+1
.

2.

The forms
E^{ap^{s}}h^{l}
for
1\leq s\leq r
,
0\leq a\leq p^{r-s}
, and
1\leq l\leq p^{s}
, with
A
-exponent
l
.

Moreover, for all
k
such that
\operatorname{S}_{k,1}\neq 0
, the space
S_{k,1}/S^{2}_{k,1}
is spanned by an eigenform with
A
-expansion and
A
-exponent 1.

Proof.

The statement about single cusp forms is
[
Pet13
, Thm. 3.2]
. In the other cases, note that each cusp form is the unique (up to scalar multiplication) double cusp form of the given weight and type. Hence it suffices to show that there is a non-zero doubly cuspidal eigenform with
A
-expansion and the claimed
A
-exponent in that weight and type. In each case, this follows from
[
Pet13
, Thm. 1.3]
.
∎

The main reason why eigenforms with
A
-expansions are relevant for our purposes is that their Hecke eigensystems are particularly simple.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
f
be an eigenform with
A
-expansion and
A
-exponent
n
. Then

\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}f=\wp^{n-1}f

for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
with monic generator
\wp
.

The Hecke eigensystems arising from eigenforms with
A
-expansions are called
power eigensystems
. In Theorem
6.1
, we show that an eigenform can have a power eigensystem even though it does not have an
A
-expansion.

2.4

Drinfeld modules over finite fields

Recall that
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. We give a quick introduction to Drinfeld
A
-modules over finite fields in order to understand the terms involved in the trace formula from
[
Vri24
]
.
We also recall some results from
[
Yu95
]
about isogeny classes of Drinfeld modules of rank 2.
For proofs of the results in this section, see
[
Pap23
, Chapter 4]
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
F
be a field containing
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The
ring of additive polynomials over
F
, denoted
F\{\tau\}
, is defined as the non-commutative polynomial ring over
F
in the variable
\tau
satisfying
\tau x=x^{q}\tau
for all
x\in F
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}\subseteq F\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a field. A
Drinfeld
A
-module of rank
r
over
F
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi\colon A\to F\{\tau\}
such that

\varphi_{T}:=\varphi(T)=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\tau+\ldots+\alpha_{r}\tau^{r},\quad\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in F,\quad\alpha_{r}\neq 0.

Given a Drinfeld module
\varphi
with
\varphi_{T}
as above, the
characteristic
of
\varphi
is the prime ideal of
A
generated by the minimal polynomial of
\alpha_{0}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A
morphism of Drinfeld modules
\varphi\to\psi
is an element
f\in F\{\tau\}
such that
f\varphi_{T}=\psi_{T}f
. We say that
\varphi
is
isogenous
to
\psi
if there exists a non-zero morphism
\varphi\to\psi
. The ring of endomorphisms of
\varphi
is denoted by
\End(\varphi)\subseteq F\{\tau\}
.

Remark 2.13
.

1.

Clearly,
\varphi
is determined by
\varphi_{T}
.

2.

It follows from the definition that if
\varphi
is a Drinfeld module with characteristic
\mathfrak{p}
, then
F
is an extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}=A/\mathfrak{p}
. We tacitly assume that a Drinfeld module over
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}
has characteristic
\mathfrak{p}
.

3.

Isogeny is an equivalence relation.

Definition 2.14
.

Suppose
F=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is finite, and let
\varphi
be a Drinfeld module over
F
. The
Frobenius endomorphism
of
\varphi
is defined to be
\pi_{\varphi}:=\tau^{m}\in F\{\tau\}
.

Since
\tau^{m}
lies in the center of
F\{\tau\}
, the Frobenius endomorphism is indeed an endomorphism of
\varphi
. To understand its importance, we consider
\End(\varphi)
as a finitely generated free
A
-module via the map
\varphi
. Tensoring with
K=\Frac(A)
yields the
K
-algebra
\End^{0}(\varphi):=K\otimes_{A}\End(\varphi)
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\varphi
be a Drinfeld
A
-module of rank
r
over a finite field.

1.

\End^{0}(\varphi)
is a division algebra, which depends up to isomorphism only on the isogeny class of
\varphi
.

2.

The center
Z(\End^{0}(\varphi))
of
\End^{0}(\varphi)
equals
K(\pi_{\varphi})
.

3.

The reduced degree of
\End^{0}(\varphi)
equals

[\End^{0}(\varphi):K]^{\text{red}}:=[\End^{0}(\varphi):K(\pi_{\varphi})]^{1/2}[K(\pi_{\varphi}):K]=r.

In particular, we see that
r\leq\text{rk}_{A}\End(\varphi)\leq r^{2}
. Moreover, the minimal polynomial of
\pi_{\varphi}
has degree
[K(\pi_{\varphi}):K]
, which divides
r
. Hence the following definition makes sense.

Definition 2.16
.

The
characteristic polynomial of Frobenius
is the unique monic polynomial
c_{\varphi}(X)\in A[X]
of degree
r
which is a power of the minimal polynomial of
\pi_{\varphi}\in\End(\varphi)
. A polynomial
f(X)\in A[X]
is called a
Weil polynomial of rank
r
over
F
if there exists a Drinfeld module
\varphi
of rank
r
over
F
such that
f(X)=c_{\varphi}(X)
.

Weil polynomials have remarkable properties
[
Pap23
, Thm. 4.2.7]
.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be Drinfeld modules of rank
r
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with characteristic
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
\wp
be the monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
. Consider the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius

c_{\varphi}(X)=X^{r}+a_{r-1}X^{r-1}+\ldots+a_{1}X+a_{0}\in A[X].

Then
c_{\varphi}(X)=c_{\psi}(X)
if and only if
\varphi
is isogenous to
\psi
. Moreover, the following properties hold:

1.

(Riemann Hypothesis) Any root
\pi\in\bar{K}
of
c_{\varphi}(X)
satisfies
|\pi|_{\infty}=q^{m/r}
.

2.

For each
0\leq i<r
, we have

\deg(a_{i})\leq\frac{(r-i)m}{r}.

3.

There exists some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
such that
a_{0}=\lambda\wp^{m/\deg(\wp)}
.

Let us now consider the case of rank
r=2
. Then for any Drinfeld module
\varphi
over a finite field, we have

c_{\varphi}(X)=X^{2}+a_{1}X+a_{0}=(X-\pi_{\varphi})(X-\bar{\pi}_{\varphi}),

where
\bar{\pi}_{\varphi}
from now on denotes the Galois conjugate of
\pi_{\varphi}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, denote by
h_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},X_{2}]
the
k
-th homogeneous symmetric polynomial in two variables. Concretely,

h_{k}(X_{1},X_{2})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}X_{1}^{i}X_{2}^{k-i}.

We can now state the trace formula
[
Vri24
, Thm. 4.10]
.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
\wp\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be monic and irreducible and write
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
. For any
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{[\varphi]/\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}}h_{k}(\pi_{\varphi},\bar{\pi}_{\varphi})\cdot\left(\frac{\pi_{\varphi}\bar{\pi}_{\varphi}}{\wp^{n}}\right)^{l-k-1},

(2)

where the sum is over isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules of rank 2 over
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}
.

In the remainder of this section, we recall some results on isogeny classes. By Prop.
2.15
, the endomorphism algebra
\End^{0}(\varphi)
of a Drinfeld module only depends on its isogeny class. Moreover, given a division algebra
D_{\pi}
which is the endomorphism algebra of some Drinfeld module
\varphi
, one can determine the number of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules isogenous to
\varphi
from
D_{\pi}
. Indeed, any Drinfeld module
\varphi^{\prime}
isogenous to
\varphi
will give rise to an
A
-order
A[\pi]\subseteq\End(E,\varphi^{\prime})\subset D_{\pi}
, which depends only on the isomorphism class of
\varphi^{\prime}
. In this way, counting isomorphism classes in a given isogeny class becomes a problem of counting certain
A
-ideals in division algebras.

In general, this is a difficult problem; we refer the reader to
[
KKP24
]
for details. For Drinfeld modules of rank 2, however, one can completely describe the isomorphism classes in a given isogeny class (see Appendix
A
for details). In what follows, we describe the possible Weil polynomials of Drinfeld modules over finite fields.

Starting from the characterization of Weil polynomials in
[
Yu95
, Thm. 3]
it is straightforward to generalize the classification of these in
[
Yu95
, Prop. 4]
to also include even characteristic. Recall that a quadratic extension
L/K
of function fields is called
imaginary
if there is only one place of
L
lying over
\infty
.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
\wp\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be monic and irreducible and write
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
.
Then the Weil polynomials of rank 2 over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
are precisely the following:

1.

c(X)=X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}
such that
a\in A
,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,
(a,\wp)=1
,
\deg(a)\leq n\deg(\wp)/2
, and the splitting field of
c(X)
over
K
is imaginary;

2.

if
n
is odd:
c(X)=X^{2}+b\wp^{n}
such that
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and the splitting field of
c(X)
over
K
is imaginary;

3.

if
n
is even and
\deg(\wp)
is odd:
c(X)=X^{2}-\lambda\wp^{n/2}X+b\wp^{n}
where
X^{2}-\lambda X+b
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
;

4.

if
n
is even:
c(X)=(X-\mu\wp^{n/2})^{2}
where
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

Definition 2.20
.

Fix a monic irreducible polynomial
\wp\in A
and write
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
. Let
a\in A
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. For any

, we define

\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b):=\{\text{Drinfeld modules }\varphi\text{ of rank 2 over }\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}\ |\ c_{\varphi}(X)=X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}\}/\cong,

the set of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules with characteristic polynomial
X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}
.

Remark 2.21
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Then

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(ca,c^{2}b).

To see this, let
F:=\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
and fix
\lambda\in F^{\times}
such that
\mathrm{Nm}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\lambda)=c
.
Then the ring isomorphism
m_{\lambda}:F\{\tau\}\to F\{\tau\}
defined by
\tau\mapsto\lambda\tau
induces a bijection
[\varphi]\mapsto[m^{-1}_{\lambda}\circ\varphi]
between
\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)
and
\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(ca,c^{2}b)
.

Remark 2.22
.

The cardinalities

are intimately related to Hurwitz class numbers. We refer the reader to Appendix
A
for details.

3

Traces

3.1

Rewriting the trace formula

Suppose that
\pi
is the Frobenius endomorphism of some Drinfeld module
\varphi
over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
, where
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
for some monic irreducible polynomial
\wp
of degree
d
. Then we have seen that the characteristic polynomial is of the form

c_{\varphi}(X)=(X-\pi)(X-\bar{\pi})=X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n},

where
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
has degree at most
nd/2
. It will be convenient to rewrite the trace formula (
2
) in terms of
a
,
b
, and
\wp
.

For an integer
m\geq 0
, we let
e_{m},p_{m},h_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},X_{2}]
denote the elementary symmetric, resp. power sum, resp. homogeneous symmetric polynomials of degree
m
in 2 variables. In particular,
e_{0}=h_{0}=1
and
p_{0}=2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
m\geq 1
, we have (under the convention that
0^{0}=1
):

p_{m}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=\pi^{m}+\bar{\pi}^{m}=m\sum_{r_{1}+2r_{2}=m}(-1)^{r_{2}}\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-1)!}{r_{1}!r_{2}!}a^{r_{1}}(b\wp^{n})^{r_{2}}.

Proof.

One can express the power polynomials in terms of the elementary symmetric polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}
using Newton’s identities:

p_{m}=(-1)^{m}m\sum_{r_{1}+2r_{2}+\ldots+mr_{m}=m}\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}+\ldots+r_{m}-1)!}{r_{1}!r_{2}!\ldots r_{m}!}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(-e_{i})^{r_{i}}.

The result follows because
e_{1}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=a
,
e_{2}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=b\wp^{n}
, and
e_{m}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=0
for
m>2
. The convention
0^{0}=1
has to be adopted since
e_{i}^{0}
is the constant polynomial
1
, which evaluates to 1 even if the input is 0.
∎

To get the trace formula into its desired form, we will need a binomial identity. We believe this identity to be known, but include a proof for lack of reference.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
k,j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k>2j\geq 0
. Then we have

\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\frac{k-2i}{k-j-i}\binom{k-j-i}{j-i}=\binom{k-j}{j}.

Proof.

We will prove more generally that for integers
j\leq n
, we have

\binom{n}{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\frac{n+j-2i}{n-i}\binom{n-i}{j-i}.

(3)

For this to make sense, we take on the convention that the term for
i=j=n
equals
(-1)^{j}
.

We use induction. Our base case consists of the formula for
\binom{n}{0}
and
\binom{n}{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
; one can check directly that these indeed equal 1. Now suppose we know that the formula holds for all
m<n
. Let
j<n
. Note that the term for
i=j
in (
3
) contributes
(-1)^{j}
. By induction, we obtain

\displaystyle\binom{n}{j}-(-1)^{j}

\displaystyle=\binom{n-1}{j-1}+\left(\binom{n-1}{j}-(-1)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{i}\left(\frac{n+j-2(i+1)}{n-1-i}\binom{n-1-i}{j-1-i}+\frac{n-1+j-2i}{n-1-i}{\binom{n-1-i}{j-i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{n+j-2i}{n-i}{\binom{n-i}{j-i}}.

The last equality above follows from the fact that
\binom{x}{y}+\binom{x}{y+1}=\binom{x+1}{y+1}
and the equality

(n+j-2(i+1))(j-i)+(n-1+j-2i)(n-j)=(n-1-i)(n+j-2i),

which is true for all
i,j,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
\epsilon_{k}=0
if
k
is odd, and
\epsilon_{k}=(-b\wp^{n})^{k/2}
if
k
is even. Then we have (under the convention that
0^{0}=1
):

h_{k}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=\epsilon_{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}a^{k-2j}(b\wp^{n})^{j}.

(4)

Proof.

Clearly
h_{0}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=1
and
h_{1}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=\pi+\bar{\pi}=a
. For
k\geq 2
, we have
h_{k}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=p_{k}(\pi,\bar{\pi})+b\wp^{n}h_{k-2}(\pi,\bar{\pi})
,
so we see inductively that

h_{k}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=(-1)^{k/2}\epsilon_{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}(b\wp^{n})^{i}p_{k-2i}(\pi,\bar{\pi}).

Combining this with Lemma
3.1
gives

h_{k}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=(-1)^{k/2}\epsilon_{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}(b\wp^{n})^{i}(k-2i)\sum_{r_{1}+2r_{2}=k-2i}(-1)^{r_{2}}\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-1)!}{r_{1}!r_{2}!}a^{r_{1}}(b\wp^{n})^{r_{2}}.

Noting that
r_{1}
and
k
have the same parity, we substitute
r_{1}=k-2j
for
j=i,\ldots,\lfloor k/2\rfloor
, which gives
r_{2}=j-i
. Thus,

h_{k}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=(-1)^{k/2}\epsilon_{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}(k-2i)\sum_{j=i}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{(k-j-i-1)!}{(k-2j)!(j-i)!}a^{k-2j}(b\wp^{n})^{j}.

Changing the order of the sums, we get

h_{k}(\pi,\bar{\pi})=(-1)^{k/2}\epsilon_{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}c_{k,j}a^{k-2j}(b\wp^{n})^{j},

(5)

where

c_{k,j}=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(j,\lceil k/2\rceil-1)}(-1)^{j-i}(k-2i)\frac{(k-j-i-1)!}{(k-2j)!(j-i)!}.

If
j\leq\lceil k/2\rceil-1
, then
c_{k,j}=(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}
by Lemma
3.2
. If
j=\lfloor k/2\rfloor>\lceil k/2\rceil-1
, then
k
is even and we see directly that

c_{k,k/2}=2\sum_{i=0}^{k/2-1}(-1)^{k/2-i}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }k/2\text{ is even;}\\
-2&\text{if }k/2\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Hence the term corresponding to
j=k/2
in equation (
5
) becomes
\epsilon_{k}
. This yields the desired expression.
∎

By Proposition
2.17
, the
pair
(a,b)
of coefficients of
c_{\varphi}(X)
determines the Drinfeld module
\varphi
up to isogeny. Because the trace formula is a sum over isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules, the numbers
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)
(see Definition
2.20
) naturally show up when rewriting the trace formula in terms of
a
and
b
. The following lemma, which is a partial generalization of
[
Yu95
, Prop. 3]
, will help to simplify the resulting trace formula.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be a maximal ideal. Then for any
n\geq 1
and any
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)b^{t}=0

as elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

Since there are no cusp forms of weight
2
, this follows from the trace formula (
2
) by setting
k=0
and
l=t+1
.
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Trace formula)
.

For any
k\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
, and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}c_{k,j}a^{k-2j}b^{j+l-k-1}\wp^{nj},

(6)

where
c_{k,j}=(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}
.

Proof.

Combining the trace formula (
2
) with Lemma
3.3
gives the required formula (
6
), up to an additional term involving
\epsilon_{k}
. To see why the
\epsilon_{k}
-term disappears, suppose
k
is even. Then

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\epsilon_{k}=(-\wp^{n})^{k/2}\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)b^{k/2},

which is zero by Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Combining Proposition
3.5
with Remark
2.21
, one sees that
\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})=0
unless
k\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
, in accordance with Lemma
2.4
.

3.2

Symmetry

The trace formula contains the binomial coefficients
c_{k,j}=(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}
. These coefficients exhibit a certain symmetry (Prop.
3.8
.1), which extends to a symmetry of traces of Hecke operators around weights of the form
p^{m}+1
.

In what follows, we will repeatedly use Lucas’s theorem, which we recall here for convenience.

Theorem 3.7

(Lucas’s theorem)
.

Fix a prime power
q
and integers
x,y\geq 0
. Suppose
x=\sum x_{i}q^{i}
,
k=\sum y_{i}q^{i}
are the
q
-ary expansions of
x
and
y
respectively. Then as elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have

\binom{x}{y}=\prod_{i\geq 0}\binom{x_{i}}{y_{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Proof.

The statement is well-known when
q
is a prime. If
q
is a power of a prime
p
, the statement follows by writing
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
in
p
-ary form.
∎

We record some consequences for later use.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
k=p^{m}-1
. The following hold:

1.

For all
1\leq N\leq k
and
j\geq N
, we have
c_{k+N,j}\equiv_{p}c_{k-N,j-N}
. That is,

(-1)^{j}\binom{k+N-j}{j}\equiv(-1)^{j-N}\binom{k-j}{j-N}\pmod{p}.

2.

For all
0\leq y\leq x\leq k
, we have

\binom{x}{y}\equiv\binom{p^{m}+x}{y}\pmod{p}.

3.

If
p=2
, we have

\binom{2^{m}-1-j}{j}\equiv 0\pmod{2}\quad\text{for all }j>0.

Proof.

1.

Let
0\leq y\leq x\leq k
. Then Lucas’s theorem implies
[
Mat06
, p. 480]
,

(-1)^{x}\binom{x}{y}\equiv_{p}(-1)^{y}\binom{k-y}{k-x}.

(7)

If
2j\leq k+N
, the claimed identity follows by letting
x=k+N-j
and
y=j
. If
2j>k+N
, the identity also holds, as both sides of the equation are zero.

2.

This follows because the
p^{m}
-digits of
x
and
y
are both zero, so by
Lucas’s theorem
,

\binom{p^{m}+x}{y}\equiv_{p}\binom{1}{0}\binom{x}{y}.

3.

The statement is obvious if
m=1
, so let
m>1
. If
j
is odd, the statement follows from Lucas’s theorem by considering the
2^{0}
-digits of
2^{m}-1-j
and
j
. If
j=2i
is even, the
2^{0}
-digits give a contribution of
\binom{1}{0}
modulo
2
, so we can remove them. Since removing the
2^{0}
-digit of
n\in\mathbb{N}
corresponds to the function
n\mapsto\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, this gives

\binom{2^{m}-1-2i}{2i}\equiv_{2}\binom{2^{m-1}-1-i}{i}\equiv_{2}0

by induction on
m
.

∎

Theorem 3.9

(Symmetry)
.

Let
m\geq 1
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a maximal ideal with monic generator
\wp
, and let
n\geq 1
. Then for any
1\leq N\leq p^{m}
and any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1+N,l})=\wp^{Nn}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1-N,l-N})+\epsilon,

where

\epsilon=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)(ab^{-1})^{p^{m}}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor}c_{N-1,j}a^{N-1-2j}b^{j+l-N}\wp^{nj}.

Proof.

Write
k=p^{m}-1
. We compute, using
the trace formula
and Prop.
3.8
.1:

\displaystyle\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt

\displaystyle|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2+N,l})=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil(k+N)/2\rceil-1}c_{k+N,j}a^{k+N-2j}b^{j+l-(k+N)-1}\wp^{nj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{j=N}^{\lceil(k+N)/2\rceil-1}c_{k-N,j-N}a^{k-N-2(j-N)}b^{j-N+(l-N)-(k-N)-1}\wp^{nj}+\epsilon_{0}

\displaystyle=\wp^{Nn}\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil(k-N)/2\rceil-1}c_{k-N,j}a^{k-N-2j}b^{j+(l-N)-(k-N)-1}\wp^{nj}+\epsilon_{0}

\displaystyle=\wp^{Nn}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2-N,l-N})+\epsilon_{0},

where

\epsilon_{0}=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{k+N,j}a^{k+N-2j}b^{j+l-(k+N)-1}\wp^{nj}.

We simplify
\epsilon_{0}
as follows. Note that for
0\leq j\leq N-1
, both
j
and
N-1-j
are less than
p^{m}
. By Prop.
3.8
.2, we obtain

c_{k+N,j}=(-1)^{j}\binom{k+N-j}{j}=(-1)^{j}\binom{p^{m}+N-1-j}{j}\equiv_{p}(-1)^{j}\binom{N-1-j}{j}=c_{N-1,j}.

The latter binomial coefficient vanishes for all
j>N-1-j
, which gives the desired
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

In Thm.
3.9
, the term
\epsilon
in
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1+N,l})
in general has roughly the same complexity (in terms of degree and amount of non-zero terms) as the whole trace. However, note that the sum over
j
in
\epsilon
has much fewer terms than the corresponding sum in the trace formula for
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1+N,l})
. Moreover, in certain cases
\epsilon
becomes negligible compared to the term
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1-N,l-N})
. For instance, if
nd=1
, we will see in Thm.
3.15
that
\epsilon
often does not contribute to the degree of the trace. It also seems like
\epsilon
is trivial for small values of
N
even when the coefficients
c_{N-1,j}
are non-zero.

3.3

Primes of degree 1

In this section, we apply the trace formula when
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of degree 1. The main result is the following.

Theorem 3.11
.

Fix
k\geq 0
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
\mathfrak{p}=(T-x)
. If
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}{\binom{k-j}{j}}(T-x)^{j},

and
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=0
otherwise.

The proof of Thm.
3.11
relies on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be a prime of degree 1. Then for
a\in A
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }a\in\mathbb{F}_{q};\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

A Drinfeld module
\varphi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
(T-x)
is determined by

\varphi_{T}=x+\alpha\tau+\beta\tau^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\{\tau\},

with
\beta\neq 0
. One easily checks that its characteristic polynomial is given by

c_{\varphi}(X)=X^{2}+\beta^{-1}\alpha X-\beta^{-1}(T-x),

so the Weil polynomial
X^{2}+aX+b(T-x)
occurs only if
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, in which case it occurs precisely once.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.11
.

Let
\wp=T-x
denote the monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
. By Prop.
3.5
and Lemma
3.12
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}\wp^{j}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}a^{k-2j}b^{j+l-k-1}.

(8)

Recall that for
n\geq 1
and any prime power
q
, we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}x^{n}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}x^{n}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }{q-1}\mid n;\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Applying the above identity to the sums over
a
and
b
, we see that the summand corresponding to a given
j
vanishes unless

0\leq j<k/2\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptk\equiv 2j\pmod{q-1}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptj\equiv k+1-l\pmod{q-1}.

(9)

Hence the expression (
8
) can be rewritten as

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}\wp^{j},

as desired.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Let
n\geq 1
, let
\wp
be a monic irreducible polynomial, and let
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
. Suppose that for any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\equiv_{p}\begin{cases}1&\text{if }a\in\mathbb{F}_{q};\\
0&\text{if }\deg(a)>0.\end{cases}

Define

f(X):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}X^{j}.

Then the proof of Thm.
3.11
shows that
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=f(\wp^{n})
.
One example in which the assumptions are satisfied is
q=3
,
n=1
, and
\wp=T^{3}+2T+1
.

Theorem 3.15

(Symmetry for degree 1 primes)
.

Let
m\geq 1
. Then for any
1\leq N\leq p^{m}
and any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p^{m}+1+N\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1+N,l})=T^{N}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1-N,l-N})+\epsilon(T),

where

\epsilon(T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq 2j\leq N-1\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}{\binom{N-1-j}{j}}T^{j}.

In particular, if
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1-N,l-N})\neq 0
, we have

\deg\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1+N,l})=N+\deg\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{p^{m}+1-N,l-N}).

Proof.

This follows from Thm.
3.9
, the expression of
\epsilon=\epsilon(T)
being due to Lemma
3.12
. The consequence about the degrees follows because
\deg(\epsilon)<N
.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Let

. Then for any

since there are no cusp forms of weight less than

Thus the corollary follows from the explicit description of
\epsilon(T)
.

Proposition 3.19
.

Fix

3.4

Traces in characteristic 2

In characteristic 2, traces of Hecke operators turn out to be much more well-behaved than in odd characteristic: every prime behaves like a prime of degree 1 (see Thm.
3.22
). On the other hand, the traces also contain less information: there are infinitely many weights in which the Hecke eigenvalues of a fixed Hecke operator occur with multiplicity divisible by 2 (see Thm.
5.13
) and so these eigenvalues cannot be studied via trace methods. In contrast, for odd
q
the traces are harder to pin down, but there is no known example of repeated eigenvalues at level 1.

Remark 3.21
.

In characteristic 2, types are in some sense superfluous. If
q=2
, we omit them completely since
\mathbb{Z}/(q-1)\mathbb{Z}=0
. If
q=2^{r}
with
r>1
, then 2 is a unit modulo
q-1
, which implies that for every
k\geq 2
, there is exactly one type
l
modulo
q-1
satisfying
k\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
(namely
l=2^{r-1}k
).

Theorem 3.22
.

Suppose
2\mid q
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be a maximal ideal, let
\wp
be a monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
and let
n\geq 1
. Then for any
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}{\binom{k-j}{j}}\wp^{nj}\qquad\text{if }k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1},

and
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=0
otherwise.

Proof.

By Propositions
A.7
and
A.2
, we have for
a\in A
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\equiv_{2}\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\deg(a)\leq 0;\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus the result follows from Rmk.
3.13
.
∎

Remark 3.23
.

Theorem
3.22
implies that any result proved for
\mathbf{T}_{T}
extends to the analogous statement for
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
simply by substituting
\wp^{n}
for
T
.

Corollary 3.24
.

Suppose
q=2
. Then as elements of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[T][\![X]\!]
, we have

\sum_{k\geq 0}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2})X^{k}=\frac{X}{(1-X-TX^{2})(1-TX^{2})}

Proof.

Consider the generating series

g(X,Y):=\frac{1}{1-(1+Y)X}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n}{j}Y^{j}X^{n}.

Setting
Y=TX
gives

\frac{1}{1-X-TX^{2}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}{j\geq 0}\binom{n}{j}T^{j}X^{n+j}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-j}{j}T^{j}\right)X^{k}.

By Theorem
3.22
, we have

\frac{1}{1-X-TX^{2}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2})X^{k}+\frac{1}{1-TX^{2}},

which yields the claimed expression.
∎

The simple version of the trace formula combined with the calculus of binomial coefficients modulo 2 allow us to explicitly compute traces of Hecke operators in special weights.

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
q=2^{r}
. Fix
1\leq s\leq r
and
m\geq 0
. Write
\delta_{q,2}
for the Kronecker delta, i.e.,
\delta_{q,2}=1
if
q=2
and
\delta_{q,2}=0
otherwise.

1.

If
k+2=2^{s}q^{m}+1
and
l=2^{r-1}(k+2)
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }l\equiv 1\pmod{q-1};\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

2.

If
k+2=2^{s}q^{m}
and
l=2^{s-1}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\wp^{-n}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\wp^{2^{s-1}q^{j}n}.

3.

If
k+2=2^{s}q^{m}+2
and
l=2^{s-1}+1
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\delta_{q,2}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\wp^{2^{s-1}q^{j}n}.

4.

If
k+2=2^{s+1}q^{m}+2^{s}q^{m}+1
and
l=2^{r-1}(k+2)
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\begin{cases}\delta_{q,2}+\wp^{q^{m+1}n}&\text{if }s=r;\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Thm.
3.22
and Prop.
3.8
.3. The proofs of the other identities are left to the reader.
∎

Theorem 3.26

(Symmetry for
q=2
)
.

Suppose
q=2
. Let
m\geq 1
and
1\leq N\leq 2^{m}
. Then for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]
and any
n\geq 1
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{2^{m}+1+N})=\wp^{Nn}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{2^{m}+1-N})+\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{N+1})+N\wp^{n(N-1)/2}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case
\wp^{n}=T
; the general case follows by substituting
\wp^{n}
for
T
.
By Thm.
3.15
, we only need to show that

\sum_{0\leq 2j\leq N-1}{\binom{N-1-j}{j}}T^{j}=\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{N+1})+NT^{\frac{N-1}{2}},

but this is clear from Thm.
3.11
.
∎

Remark 3.27
.

If
q=2

3.26
allows for the computation of the trace of any Hecke operator in weight

3.5

Traces in odd characteristic: primes of degree 2

As the characteristic 2 case is now settled, we may safely assume that
q
is odd. We turn to the study of traces of powers of Hecke operators
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
when
nd>1
, where
d=\deg(\mathfrak{p})
. When
nd=2
, we obtain the following explicit expression.

Theorem 3.29
.

Suppose
2\nmid q
and
\wp
is a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
d\in\{1,2\}
. Write
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
and let
nd=2
. Suppose
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}\wp^{nj}\ +

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=(q-1)/2}^{q-2}4^{-m}\binom{m}{(q-1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1+m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<i<k-2j\\
i\equiv-2m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j+\frac{q-1}{2}}\binom{k-j}{i,j,k-2j-i}T^{i}\wp^{nj},

where the congruences imposed on
i
and
j
are modulo
q-1
.

The first step towards proving Theorem
3.29
is to understand the cardinalities
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)
.
We fix the following notation. For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, define the Legendre symbol via

\left(\frac{\alpha,\beta}{q}\right):=\begin{cases}1&X^{2}-\alpha X+\beta\text{ has two distinct roots in $\mathbb{F}_{q}$};\\
0&X^{2}-\alpha X+\beta\text{ is a square};\\
-1&X^{2}-\alpha X+\beta\text{ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$}.\end{cases}

Note that this is the same as the Legendre symbol for
D=\alpha^{2}-4\beta
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.,
\left(\frac{\alpha,\beta}{q}\right)=D^{(q-1)/2}
. If
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is a polynomial of degree
\leq 1
, we denote by
a^{+}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
the coefficient of
T
in
a
.

Proposition 3.30
.

Suppose
2\nmid q
and let
nd=2
. Then for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
with
\deg(a)\leq 1
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\equiv 1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)\pmod{p}.

Proof.

We prove this case by case, comparing each time an explicit computation to the answer provided by Prop.
A.2
. Throughout, we abbreviate
H(D):=H(A[\sqrt{D}])
and
\chi_{D}:=\chi_{K(\sqrt{D})}
.

In case 1, let
f(X,T)=X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}
, with
\wp\nmid a=a^{+}T+a^{-}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Let
D=a^{2}-4b\wp^{n}
be the discriminant of
f
. In this case, Proposition
A.2
implies that
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=H(D)
if the splitting field of
f
is imaginary, and
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=0
otherwise. We distinguish three subcases.

i)

D
is not square-free. In this case, we have
D=\mu Q^{2}
for a monic prime
Q
, necessarily of degree 1, and
\mu=(a^{+})^{2}-4b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. In this case,
\mu
is a non-square. Indeed, if
\mu=\lambda^{2}
, then
-4b\wp^{n}=(\lambda Q+a)(\lambda Q-a)
. Since
\wp
is prime, this implies
a=0
, which contradicts
\wp\nmid a
. Thus,
K(\sqrt{D})=K(\sqrt{\mu})=K\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, so both
\infty
and
Q
are inert and Lemma
A.1
gives

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=H(D)\equiv_{p}1-\chi_{D}(Q)=2,

The fact that
\mu
is a non-square also gives

1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)=2,

which is what we wanted.

ii)

D
is square-free and
\deg(D)>0
. In this case, the ramification at
\infty
is determined by the splitting behaviour of
\tilde{f}(X,s)=s^{2}f(X/s,1/s)
at
s=0
; that is,

\tilde{f}(X,0)=X^{2}-a^{+}X+b.

Hence
f
is a Weil polynomial if and only if
\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)\neq 1
. Since
\deg(D)\leq 2
, the curve
Y^{2}=D
has genus 0, so its Jacobian has one point. Thus, Lemma
A.5
gives

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=H(D)=1-\chi_{D}(\infty)=1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right).

iii)

D\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
H(D)=1
. On the other hand, the coefficient of
T^{2}
in
D
equals
(a^{+})^{2}-4b=0
. Hence
X^{2}-a^{+}X+b
is a square, so again
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)
.

In case 2, let
f(X,T)
be as before, except now
\wp\mid a
. We again consider subcases.

i)

d=2
. Since
\deg(a)\leq 1
, this implies
a=0
, so let
f(X)=X^{2}-b\wp
with
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Clearly
\left(\frac{0,b}{q}\right)=b^{(q-1)/2}=\chi_{D}(\infty)
; in particular, if
f
is a Weil polynomial, then
\infty
is inert.
Since
Y^{2}=\mu\wp
is a curve of genus 0, we obtain

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0,b)=H(b\wp)=1-\chi_{D}(\infty)=1-\left(\frac{0,b}{q}\right).

ii)

d=1
. Then
f(X,T)=X^{2}-a^{+}\wp X+b\wp^{2}
with
a^{+}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and the splitting behaviour of
f
is equivalent to the splitting behaviour of
X^{2}-a^{+}X+b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. In particular, if
X^{2}-a^{+}X+b
splits, then
f(X,T)
is not a Weil polynomial as it does not occur as a case in Prop.
2.19
. Comparing each of the possibilities for
\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)
with the corresponding case in Prop.
A.2
gives
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)
every time.

∎

Lemma 3.31
.

For
1\leq m\leq q-1
, we have

\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(\frac{\alpha,\beta}{q}\right)(\alpha^{-2}\beta)^{m}\equiv(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}4^{-m}\binom{m}{(q-1)/2}\pmod{p}.

Proof.

Note that for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

\left(\frac{\alpha,\beta}{q}\right)=(\alpha^{2}-4\beta)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}=(1-4\alpha^{-2}\beta)^{\frac{q-1}{2}},

and hence

\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(\frac{\alpha,\beta}{q}\right)(\alpha^{-2}\beta)^{m}=-\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}(1-4\gamma)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\gamma^{m}=-\sum_{i=0}^{(q-1)/2}\binom{(q-1)/2}{i}(-4)^{i}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\gamma^{m+i}.

The final sum is zero unless
m+i\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}
, in which case it contributes a factor of
-1
. This gives

\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(\frac{\alpha,\beta}{q}\right)(\alpha^{-2}\beta)^{m}=(-4)^{-m}\binom{(q-1)/2}{q-1-m},

and the latter equals the claimed expression by Eq. (
7
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.29
.

Combining
the trace formula
with Prop.
3.30
gives

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}c_{k,j}\sum_{a^{+}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{a^{-}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)\right)(a^{+}T+a^{-})^{k-2j}b^{j-l+1}\wp^{nj}.

Expanding
(a^{+}T+a^{-})^{k-2j}
via the binomial theorem and simplifying gives

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\equiv k-2j\end{subarray}}^{k-2j-1}(-1)^{j+1}\binom{k-j}{i,j,k-2j-i}s_{i,j}T^{i}\wp^{nj},

where

\displaystyle s_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a^{+}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)\right)(a^{+})^{i}b^{j-l+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a^{+},b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)\right)(a^{+})^{i}b^{j-l+1}+\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(1-\left(\frac{0,b}{q}\right)\right)0^{i}b^{j-l+1}.

Write
m:=j-l+1
. Since
i\equiv k-2j\equiv-2m\pmod{q-1}
, the first sum can be computed via Lemma
3.31
. In particular, if
i=0
, we find that

s_{0,j}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1};\\
0&\text{if }j\equiv l-1+(q-1)/2\pmod{q-1}.\end{cases}

If
i>0
, the second sum vanishes. Let
[m]
denote the representative of
m
modulo
q-1
satisfying
1\leq[m]\leq q-1
. Then Lemma
3.31
yields

s_{i,j}=\epsilon_{j}+(-1)^{1+\frac{q-1}{2}}4^{-m}\binom{[m]}{(q-1)/2},

where
\epsilon_{j}=1
if
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}
and
\epsilon_{j}=0
otherwise. In particular,
s_{i,j}=0
if
i>0
and
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}
. Note also that the binomial coefficient vanishes whenever
2[m]<q-1
. Combining the above yields the claimed formula.
∎

It is worth noting that
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)
is independent of
\mathfrak{p}
and
n
as long as
nd=2
.

Corollary 3.32
.

Let
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then there exists a polynomial
f_{k+2,l}(X)\in A[X]
of degree
\deg(f_{k+2,l})<k/2
such that

f_{k+2,l}(\wp^{n})=\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\wp}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})

for every pair
(\wp,n)
such that
\wp\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
d
and
nd=2
.

Proof.

If
2\mid q
, this is obvious from Thm.
3.22
. If
2\nmid q
, then by Propositions
3.5
and
3.30
, the polynomial in question is given by

f_{k+2,l}(X)=\sum_{0\leq j<k/2}c_{k,j}\sum_{\deg(a)\leq 1}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(1-\left(\frac{a^{+},b}{q}\right)\right)a^{k-2j}b^{j+l-k-1}X^{j}.

∎

Remark 3.33
.

The polynomial
f_{k+2,l}
is non-unique in general: adding any multiple of

\prod_{nd=2}(X-\wp^{n}),\qquad\wp\text{ monic prime of degree }d\leq 2,

to
f_{k+2,l}
will give a polynomial in
A[X]
which also interpolates the traces.
The bound on the degree does ensure unicity of
f_{k+2,l}
for small values of
k
.

See Section
6.3
for a computation of the polynomials
f_{k+2,l}
in some special cases.

3.6

Odd characteristic: primes of higher degree

Suppose we want to compute traces when
nd>2
. It gets increasingly harder to obtain explicit formulas, for example because the Hurwitz class number
H(D)
is now related to the number of points on a Jacobian of positive dimension (see Lemma
A.5
).
Instead, we will combine the results from this paper into an algorithm to compute the trace of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
in
Magma

[
BCP97
]
.

Algorithm 3.34
.

Input:

•

An odd prime power
q
;

•

Integers
k,l,n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
;

•

A monic irreducible polynomial
\wp\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
.

Output:

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})
for
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)
.

1.

Using Propositions
2.17
and
2.19
, create a list
L_{0}
containing all the characteristic polynomials of Drinfeld modules of rank 2 over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
. For instance,

L_{0}=\left\{X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}\ \bigg|\ \deg(a)\leq\frac{n\deg(\wp)}{2},\ b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\right\}

is such a list.

2.

Since
Magma
can determine whether or not a field extension is imaginary
[
Bos+13
, Section 42.13]
, one can algorithmically find the sublist
L\subseteq L_{0}
of Weil polynomials.

3.

Since
Magma
can compute the class number of the finite maximal order in an imaginary quadratic extension of function fields
[
Bos+13
, Section 42.2]
, as well as compute how a prime ramifies in an extension, one can use Proposition
A.2
and Lemma
A.1
to compute
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)
for all polynomials
f=X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}\in L
.

4.

Set
k^{\prime}=k-2
and compute

H=\sum_{f\in L}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{0\leq j<k^{\prime}/2}(-1)^{j}\binom{k^{\prime}-j}{j}a^{k^{\prime}-2j}b^{j+l-k^{\prime}+1}\wp^{nj}.

By Prop.
3.5
,
H
is the desired Hecke trace.

An implementation of Algorithm
3.34
in
Magma
can be found at
https://github.com/Sjoerd-deVries/DMF_Trace_Formula.git
. The results in the appendix (especially Rmk.
A.6
) give an alternative way to algorithmically determine the numbers
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)
in terms of point counts on hyperelliptic curves.

Example 3.35
.

Figure
3
shows the degrees of the traces of the prime
T^{5}+2T+1\in\mathbb{F}_{3}[T]
for
l=0
and
2\leq k\leq 200
obtained using Algorithm
3.34
. The dotted line is the strong Ramanujan bound from Conj.
4.3
, in this case given by
\deg=\frac{5(k-4)}{2}
. The computation of these traces in
Magma
took 4.8 seconds on a standard laptop.

3.7

Traces modulo
\mathfrak{p}

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be a maximal ideal with monic generator
\wp
of degree
d
. The trace formula for
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
becomes much simpler modulo
\mathfrak{p}
.

Proposition 3.36
.

For any
k\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
, and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})\equiv\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{k}b^{l-k-1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}.

Proof.

Modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
, all terms with
j>0
in Equation (
6
) are zero.
∎

Lemma 3.37
.

We have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})\equiv\sum_{(a,\wp)=1}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{k}b^{l-k-1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{n}},

i.e. only the ordinary Drinfeld modules contribute.

Proof.

We may assume that
k\geq 2
is even, as otherwise
\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}=0
. In cases 2, 3 and 4 of Prop.
2.19
, we have
\wp^{n/2}\mid a
and hence
\wp^{n}\mid a^{k}
. Hence only the Weil polynomials from case 1 contribute, which are precisely the ones for which
(a,\wp)=1
.
∎

Proposition 3.38
.

For any
k\geq 1
,
n\geq 1
, and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})\equiv\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+(q^{d}-1)+2,l})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

Moreover, let
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and let
m:=\min(q^{e},n)
.
Then we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})\equiv\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+q^{e}(q^{d}-1)+2,l})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{m}}.

Proof.

The first congruence follows from the second by setting
e=0
. By Prop.
3.36
, it suffices to show that
a^{k}\equiv a^{k+q^{e}(q^{d}-1)}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{m}}
for any
a\in A
, as it is clear that
b^{q^{e}(q^{d}-1)}=1
. This follows because
A/\mathfrak{p}^{m}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}[\wp]/(\wp^{m})
and in this ring, we have

f=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f_{i}\wp^{i}\implies f^{q^{e}}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f_{i}^{q^{e}}\wp^{iq^{e}}=f_{0}^{q^{e}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}},

since
q^{e}\geq m
. Hence, if
f_{0}\neq 0
, we have
f^{q^{e}(q^{d}-1)}=1
. But for
a\in A
with image
f\in A/{\mathfrak{p}^{m}}
, we have
f_{0}\neq 0\iff(a,\wp)=1
, so we are done by Lemma
3.37
.
∎

Proposition 3.39
.

For any
k\geq 1
,
n\geq 1
, and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{kq+2,l})\equiv\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})^{q}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}.

Proof.

By Prop.
3.36
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{kq+2,l})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{kq}b^{l-kq-1}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)^{q}a^{kq}b^{(l-k-1)q}\equiv\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})^{q}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{n}},

as claimed.
∎

Recall that there is a Frobenius map on modular forms. It is given by

F_{q}\colon\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\to\operatorname{S}_{kq,l},\qquad F_{q}(f)=f^{q}.

Frobenius sends Hecke eigenforms to Hecke eigenforms: more precisely, if
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}f=\lambda f
, then
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}f^{q}=\wp^{q-1}\lambda^{q}f^{q}
. This leads to the following congruences.

Proposition 3.40
.

If
k\geq 3
is such that
q^{d}-1\mid k(q-1)
, then for any
n\geq 1
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{kq,l})\equiv\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{kq,l}/F_{q}(\operatorname{S}_{k,l}))\equiv\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

Proof.

Since
F_{q}(\operatorname{S}_{k,l})\subseteq\operatorname{S}_{kq,l}
is Hecke-stable, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{kq,l})=\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006ptF_{q}(\operatorname{S}_{k,l}))+\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{kq,l}/F_{q}(\operatorname{S}_{k,l})).

Since
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}f^{q}=\wp^{q-1}\lambda^{q}f^{q}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006ptF_{q}(\operatorname{S}_{k,l}))=\wp^{q-1}\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})^{q}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

This gives the first congruence. On the other hand, by Prop.
3.38
and the assumption on
k
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{kq,l})=\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+k(q-1),l})\equiv\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

∎

4

The strong Ramanujan bound

The Ramanujan bound from
[
Vri24
]
, with our scaling conventions, says that

\deg\Tr\left(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}\right)\leq\frac{ndk}{2},

(10)

where
d=\deg(\mathfrak{p})
. In level 1, this bound is not sharp (see Prop.
4.1
).
Based on much computational evidence, we propose an improved bound (the strong Ramanujan bound) in the form of Conjecture
4.3
, which we prove in some special cases (see Thm.
4.9
).

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
k,l,n
and
\mathfrak{p}
, the Ramanujan bound (
10
) is a strict inequality.

Proof.

Denote by
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})^{+}
the term of degree
ndk/2
in
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})
; we will show that it is zero. Given a Weil polynomial
X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T][X]
, write

a=a^{+}T^{nd/2}+\text{lower order terms}.

Then we have by
the trace formula
and the fact that
\wp
is monic,

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})^{+}=\sum_{\deg(a)=nd/2}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}c_{k,j}(a^{+}T^{nd/2})^{k-2j}b^{j+l-k-1}T^{ndj}.

The summand corresponding to a given
j
is thus

c_{k,j}T^{ndk/2}\sum_{\deg(a)=nd/2}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)(a^{+})^{k-2j}b^{j+l-k-1}.

Note that
a^{+}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
for all
a
appearing in the sum above, so it suffices to show that

\sum_{\deg(a)=nd/2}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)(a^{+})^{m}b^{t}=0

(11)

for all integers
1\leq m\leq q-1
and all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. We do this by induction. Note that
\operatorname{S}_{m+2,t}
contains no double cusp forms for
m\leq q-1
and any
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, and since single cusp forms have trivial Hecke eigenvalues, we have
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n})^{+}=0
in the entire range of
m
and
t
we consider.

The base cases are
m=1
and
m=2
. Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{3,t})^{+}=T^{nd}\sum_{\deg(a)=nd/2}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{+}b^{t-2};

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{4,t})^{+}=T^{nd}\sum_{\deg(a)=nd/2}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)(a^{+})^{2}b^{t-3},

which gives (
11
) for
m\in\{1,2\}
and any
t
.

Next, let
m^{\prime}\leq q-1
and suppose (
11
) holds for all
m=1,2,\ldots,m^{\prime}-2
and any
t
. Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{m^{\prime}+2,t})^{+}

\displaystyle=T^{ndm^{\prime}/2}\sum_{\deg(a)=nd/2}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\left((a^{+})^{m^{\prime}}b^{t-m^{\prime}-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil m^{\prime}/2\rceil-1}c_{m^{\prime},j}(a^{+})^{m^{\prime}-2j}b^{j+t-m^{\prime}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=T^{ndm^{\prime}/2}\sum_{\deg(a)=nd/2}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)(a^{+})^{m^{\prime}}b^{t-m^{\prime}-1},

which gives (
11
) for
m=m^{\prime}
and any
t
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Assuming the Tate conjecture, Coleman and Edixhoven have shown that the Ramanujan bound on elliptic modular forms is likewise not sharp
[
CE98
, Thm. 4.1]
.

Conjecture 4.3

(Strong Ramanujan bound)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be a maximal ideal of degree
d
and let
n\geq 1
. Then for any
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\deg\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})\leq\frac{nd(k-(q+1))}{2}.

(12)

Throughout the remainder of this paper, we will say that
the strong Ramanujan bound holds for
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
if the inequality (
12
) holds for all
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 4.4
.

1.

If
nd=1
, the strong Ramanujan bound is sharp infinitely often for any type. To see this, fix a type
1\leq l\leq q-1
and put
k^{(l)}_{n}:=q-1+2q(l-1+n(q-1))
for
n\geq 0
. Then
k_{n}^{(l)}+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
, and the trace of
\mathbf{T}_{T}
on the space
\operatorname{S}_{k^{(l)}_{n}+2,l}
can be computed using Thm.
3.11
. In particular, by
Lucas’s theorem
, the trace has leading term
T^{q(l-1+n(q-1))}
with coefficient

\displaystyle c_{k^{(l)}_{n},q(l-1+n(q-1))}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q(l-1+n(q-1))}\binom{q-1+q(l-1+n(q-1))}{q(l-1+n(q-1))}

\displaystyle\equiv_{p}(-1)^{l-1}\binom{l-1+n(q-1)}{l-1+n(q-1)}\binom{q-1}{0}=(-1)^{l-1}\neq 0.

2.

With notation as above, we have
\operatorname{S}_{k_{0}^{(l)}+2,l}=\langle E^{l-1}h^{l}\rangle
. The eigenforms
E^{l-1}h^{l}
are the first forms to attain the strong Ramanujan bound for
\mathbf{T}_{T}
in each type. In other words, the strong Ramanujan bound is not attained for
\mathbf{T}_{T}
acting on
\operatorname{S}_{k^{\prime}+2,l}
for any
k^{\prime}<k^{(l)}_{0}
.

3.

In the other direction, Corollary
3.17
shows that the distance of a trace to the strong Ramanujan bound is unbounded as the weight goes to infinity.

Recall that
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=0
whenever
\deg(a)>\lfloor\frac{nd}{2}\rfloor=:N
. If
X^{2}-aX+b\wp^{n}
is the Weil polynomial of a Drinfeld module over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
, write

a=a_{0}+a_{1}T+\ldots+a_{N}T^{N},\qquad a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Proposition 4.5
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}
and
n
, and let
N=\lfloor nd/2\rfloor
. The following are equivalent:

1.

The strong Ramanujan bound holds for
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
.

2.

For all
k\geq 0
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\deg\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{k}b^{t}\leq\frac{nd(k-(q-1))}{2}.

(13)

3.

For all
k\geq 0
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)b^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{0}+\ldots+v_{N}=k\\
v_{1}+2v_{2}+\ldots+Nv_{N}=m\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}}a_{0}^{v_{0}}a_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots a_{N}^{v_{N}}=0

for all
m
such that
2m>nd(k-(q-1))
.

Proof.

We use the formula

\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}c_{k,j}a^{k-2j}b^{j+l-k-1}\wp^{nj}

(14)

from Prop.
3.5
. This immediately yields
2\implies 1
, since the summand corresponding to a fixed
j
then has degree bounded by

\frac{nd(k-2j-(q-1))}{2}+ndj=\frac{nd(k+2-(q+1))}{2},

as desired.

For
1\implies 2
, note that (
13
) holds for
k\in\{0,1\}
and any
t
, since there are no double cusp forms of weight
2
or
3
for any
q
. Suppose by induction that (
13
) holds for any
k^{\prime}<k
and any
t
. Then (
14
) implies that, for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{k}b^{l-k-1}=\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}nd(k-(q-1))}\right),

which by the strong Ramanujan bound implies (
13
) for
k
and any
t
.

The equivalence
2\iff 3
follows from the computation

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{k}b^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}T^{i}\right)^{k}b^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{kN}T^{m}\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)b^{t}\sum\binom{k}{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}}a_{0}^{v_{0}}a_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots a_{N}^{v_{N}},

where the last sum is over
N
-tuples
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
such that
\sum v_{i}=k
and
\sum iv_{i}=m
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Suppose
nd
is odd. Then the strong Ramanujan bound holds for
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
if it holds for all pairs
(k,t)
with
k<nd(q-1)
and
1\leq t\leq q-1
.

Proof.

If
nd
is odd, then
N=(nd-1)/2
. Hence
\deg a^{k}b^{t}\leq k(nd-1)/2
for any pair
(a,b)
such that
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)\neq 0
. Now if
k\geq nd(q-1)
, we have

\deg\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a^{k}b^{t}\leq\frac{k(nd-1)}{2}\leq\frac{nd(k-(q-1))}{2},

so by Prop.
4.5
, it only remains to check
k<nd(q-1)
.
∎

More generally, we have the following sufficient condition for the Ramanujan bound to hold, which for each fixed
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
is a finite computation. It is worth mentioning that we have not found any examples where this sufficient condition is not satisfied.

Proposition 4.7
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
and
n\geq 1
. Suppose that

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)b^{t}a_{0}^{v_{0}}a_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots a_{N}^{v_{N}}=0

(15)

under the following conditions:

1.

0\leq v_{i}\leq q-1
for all
i=0,\ldots,N
;

2.

2\sum iv_{i}>nd(k-(q-1))
, where
k:=\sum v_{i}
;

3.

1\leq t\leq q-1
and
2t\equiv-k\pmod{q-1}
.

Then the strong Ramanujan bound holds for
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
.

Proof.

By the third statement in Prop.
4.5
, it suffices to show that Equation (
15
) holds for all
k:=\sum v_{i}\geq 0
,
2\sum iv_{i}>nd(k-(q-1))
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we may assume
1\leq t\leq q-1
. Since
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(ca,c^{2}b)
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, the sum is automatically zero if
2t\not\equiv-k\pmod{q-1}
. Note also that all
a_{i}
lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and hence
a_{i}^{s}=a_{i}^{s-(q-1)}
for all
s\geq q
. Hence if some
v_{j}\geq q
, we can apply the above identity to reduce to the case
k^{\prime}=k-(q-1)
, where we note that

2\sum_{i=1}^{N}iv_{i}>nd(k-(q-1))\implies 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}iv_{i}-2j(q-1)>nd(k^{\prime}-(q-1)),

since
2j\leq nd
. One may keep doing this until
0\leq v_{i}\leq q-1
for all
i
, in which case we are in the setting of the proposition.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The condition
2\sum iv_{i}>nd(k-(q-1))
implies that
v_{0}<q-1
. By looking at the term of lowest degree in
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})
for all
k\leq q+1
, we find that for any
k,t\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)a_{0}^{k}b^{t}=\begin{cases}1&k\neq 0,\ k\equiv t\equiv 0\pmod{q-1};\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This settles the cases of Prop.
4.7
in which
v_{i}=0
for all
i>0
.

Theorem 4.9
.

The strong Ramanujan bound holds for all
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
such that
nd\leq 3
. If
2\mid q
, the strong Ramanujan bound holds for any
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
.

Proof.

If
nd=1
we have
N=0
, so the claim follows from Rmk.
4.8
. If
nd=2
, then by Prop.
3.30
we can write each sum in Prop.
4.7
as

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)b^{t}a_{0}^{v_{0}}a_{1}^{v_{1}}=\sum_{a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}a_{0}^{v_{0}}\sum_{a_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(1-\left(\frac{a_{1},b}{q}\right)\right)b^{t}a_{1}^{v_{1}}=0,

since the condition
2\sum iv_{i}>nd(k-(q-1))
implies that
v_{0}<q-1
.

If
nd=3
we apply Cor.
4.6
. Note that the spaces
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
for
k<3(q-1)
contain no double cusp forms if
q\leq 5
, so in this case we are done. If
q>5
, there is only one double cusp form of weight
k<3(q-1)
, namely
h^{2}\in\operatorname{S}_{2(q+1),2}
. This cusp form has an
A
-expansion with
A
-exponent 2, which implies that

\deg\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}|\operatorname{S}_{2(q+1),2})=nd<\frac{nd(q+1)}{2}.

Finally, suppose
2\mid q
. By Thm.
3.22
, the trace of any Hecke operator behaves like the trace of
\mathbf{T}_{T}
, so the result follows from the case
nd=1
.
∎

5

Eigenvalues

In positive characteristic
p
, it is not obvious that trace methods are sufficiently powerful to make deductions about eigenvalues. The problem is the following: if the linear operator
\mathbf{T}
has an eigenvalue
\alpha
with algebraic multiplicity
p
, then there is no way to recover
\alpha
from the sequence
(\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}))_{n\geq 1}
: the contribution of these
p
eigenvalues to
\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n})
is
p\alpha^{n}=0
.

This problem is studied in more detail in
[
Vri25
]
. In many situations, one can recover information about the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{T}
if one assumes that no eigenvalue is repeated
p
times, by which we mean that the algebraic multiplicity of any eigenvalue is not a positive multiple of
p
.

Throughout this section, we fix the following notation.
Let
V
be a
K
-vector space and let
\mathbf{T}\colon V\to V
be a linear map. We denote by
\text{Sp}(\mathbf{T})\subset\bar{K}
the set of eigenvalues of
\mathbf{T}
. For any
\alpha\in\text{Sp}(\mathbf{T})
, we write
m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}
for the algebraic multiplicity of
\alpha
, i.e.,

m_{\alpha}=\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ (X-\alpha)^{n}\text{ divides }\det(\mathds{1}X-\mathbf{T})\}.

5.1

Detecting repeated eigenvalues

We noted that any eigenvalue which is repeated
p
times is impossible to recover from the sequence of traces. What the traces do detect is whether or not there is a repeated eigenvalue, assuming that the dimension of the space is known.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
d=\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
. The action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
has a repeated eigenvalue if and only if the matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}})&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})&\cdots&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{d-1})\\
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}})&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{3})&\cdots&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{d})\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{d-1})&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{d})&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{d+1})&\cdots&\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2d-2})\end{pmatrix}

has zero determinant.

Proof.

Denote the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
by
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\in\bar{K}
. For any
n\geq 0
, we have
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})=\alpha_{1}^{n}+\ldots+\alpha_{d}^{n}
. Hence, if we let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{1}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{d-1}\\
1&\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{2}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{2}^{d-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\alpha_{d}&\alpha_{d}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{d}^{d-1}\end{pmatrix},

then
A^{t}A=M
. But
A
is a Vandermonde matrix, so

\det M=\det A^{t}A=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{2}.

Thus
\det M=0
if and only if
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}
for some
i\neq j
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If
p=2
, the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
almost always has repeated eigenvalues by Thm.
5.13
. If
p>2
, we suspect that the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
never has
p
repeated eigenvalues at level 1. At higher levels, though, there can be repeated eigenvalues for any
q

[
LM08
,
Hat22
]
. This is analogous to the classical setting, where Buzzard’s conjecture
[
Mae15
, Conj. 2.1]
predicts that each Hecke operator acts irreducibly on
\operatorname{S}_{k}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
for any
k
. This would in particular imply that eigenvalues are not repeated at level 1.

5.2

Injectivity

In their study of oldforms and newforms, Bandini and Valentino conjectured that the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{T}
acting on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
is always injective
[
BV22a
, Conj. 1.1]
. This is related to the diagonalisability of the Atkin operator
\mathbf{U}_{T}
at level
\Gamma_{0}(T)
. Since then, the conjecture has been proven in the case that the space of cusp forms has dimension 1
[
BV23
,
DK23
]
.

It turns out that a result by Joshi and Petrov
[
JP14
]
on the structure of Hecke operators modulo a prime of degree 1 immediately implies the following stronger version of the conjecture.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be any maximal ideal and let
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is injective on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
.

Proof.

Let
\wp
denote the monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
and pick
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\wp(x)\neq 0
.
By
[
JP14
, Cor. 3.3]
, the reduction
\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
modulo the prime
(T-x)
is well-defined and has eigenvalue
\wp(x)^{l-1}
with multiplicity
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
. But if
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
were not injective, then 0 would be among the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

For elliptic modular forms, we believe the injectivity of Hecke operators at level 1 to be an interesting question. Despite a wealth of computational data, there are no known examples of Hecke operators
\mathbf{T}_{p}
acting on
\operatorname{S}_{k}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
with a zero eigenvalue. Lehmer’s conjecture on the Ramanujan
\tau
-function asserts that if
k=12
, there is no such Hecke operator.

5.3

Eigenvalues in characteristic 2

In this section we assume that
q
is a power of 2. We have seen in Thm.
3.22
that the traces of Hecke operators are exceptionally simple. We use this to determine many eigenvalues of the Hecke operators explicitly (Thm.
5.9
). On the other hand, the Hecke action almost always has repeated eigenvalues (Thm.
5.13
). The simple expression of the traces is therefore partly due to the fact that not all eigenvalues contribute to the traces.

Definition 5.5
.

Suppose
2\mid q
. For any
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the sets

P(k+2,l,q):=\left\{0\leq j<k/2\ \bigg|\ j\equiv_{q-1}l-1\text{ and }\binom{k-j}{j}\equiv_{2}1\right\},

and write
N(k+2,l,q):=\#P(k+2,l,q)
.

Remark 5.6
.

If
q=2
, the sequence
N(k+2,1,2)
is closely related to the
Stern-Brocot sequence

(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined as follows:
a_{0}=a_{1}=1
and for
k\geq 2
,

a_{k}=\begin{cases}a_{k/2}+a_{k/2-1}&\text{if }k\text{ is even};\\
a_{(k-1)/2}&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Then we have

N(k+2,1,2)=a_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+(-1)^{k}\right).

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose
2\mid q
. If
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
, then

N(k+2,l,q)\equiv\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}\pmod{2}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
\mathfrak{p}=(T-x)
be a prime of degree 1. In
[
JP14
]
, the authors define, for any
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
, Hecke operators
\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
on Drinfeld modular forms mod
\mathfrak{p}
in such a way that

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{q}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})\equiv\Tr(\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997pt\widetilde{\operatorname{S}}_{k,l})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

Moreover, if
Q
is the monic generator of
\mathfrak{q}
, they show that the only eigenvalue of
\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
on
\widetilde{\operatorname{S}}_{k,l}
is
Q(x)^{l-1}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{q}
. Since
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\widetilde{\operatorname{S}}_{k,l}
, this implies that

\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\ (\text{mod }p)=\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})\ (\text{mod }T+1)

(16)

as elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Now suppose
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. Then by Thm.
3.11
,

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}\pmod{T+1}

(17)

Setting
p=2
and combining (
16
) and (
17
) gives the result.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

If
q=2
, Lemma
5.7
can also be proved combinatorially using the recursions satisfied by
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2}
and by the Stern-Brocot sequence. If
q>2
, the analogous recursion satisfied by the numbers
N(k+2,l,q)
gets more involved as it involves a change in the type
l
, which makes a direct calculation difficult.

The following theorem completely describes the eigenvalues of Hecke operators in characteristic 2 which occur with algebraic multiplicity not divisible by 2.

Theorem 5.9
.

Suppose
2\mid q
. Let
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be any maximal ideal. Then the set of eigenvalues with odd multiplicity of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
acting on
\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}
is given by

\{\alpha\in\text{Sp}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}})\ |\ m_{\alpha}\equiv_{2}1\}=\{\wp^{j}\ |\ j\in P(k+2,l,q)\}.

Proof.

Theorem
3.22
implies that for all
n\geq 1
,

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<k/2\\
j\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\end{subarray}}{\binom{k-j}{j}}\wp^{nj}=\sum_{j\in P(k+2,l,q)}\wp^{nj}.

Write
P(k+2,l,q)=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}\}
with
N=N(k+2,l,q)
. It follows that for all
n\geq 1
,

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=p_{n}(\wp^{j_{1}},\ldots,\wp^{j_{N}}),

where
p_{n}
denotes the
n
-th power sum symmetric polynomial in
N
variables.

On the other hand, let
E=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{e}\}
denote the set of eigenvalues with odd algebraic multiplicity, with
e=\#E
. Then for all
n\geq 1
, we also have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})=p_{n}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{e}).

For
c\geq e
, write
A_{c}
for the
e\times c
Vandermonde matrix on
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{e}\}
, i.e.,

A_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{1}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{c-1}\\
1&\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{2}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{2}^{c-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\alpha_{e}&\alpha_{e}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{e}^{c-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Then
A_{c}^{t}A_{c}
is the matrix
M_{c}=(\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i+j-2}))_{i,j=1}^{c}
; the equality for
i=j=1
follows because
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}\equiv_{2}e
. Hence the rank of
M_{c}
is at most
e
. Since the
\alpha_{i}
are distinct, the upper leftmost
e\times e
minor
A_{e}^{t}A_{e}
has non-zero determinant, so in fact
\rk(M_{c})=e
.

For
c\geq N
, write
B_{c}
for the
N\times c
Vandermonde matrix on
\{\wp^{j_{1}},\ldots,\wp^{j_{N}}\}
. By Lemma
5.7
, we know that
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}\equiv_{2}N
. Therefore it follows in the same way that
B_{c}^{t}B_{c}=M_{c}
and that
\rk(M_{c})=N
. Comparing the ranks of
M_{c}
for
c\geq\max\{N,e\}
gives
N=\#E
. But knowing this, we can in fact deduce that
E=\{\wp^{j}\ |\ j\in P(k+2,l,q)\}
; in words, the elements
\wp^{j_{1}},\ldots,\wp^{j_{N}}
are precisely the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
with odd multiplicity. This follows because the sequence of all power sums of distinct elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\bar{K}
uniquely determines these elements
[
Vri25
, Prop. 3.17]
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

If
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}\equiv_{2}N+1
, then the matrix
B_{c}
in the proof of Thm.
5.9
would have to be replaced by the
(N+1)\times c
Vandermonde matrix on
\{0,\wp^{j_{1}},\ldots,\wp^{j_{N}}\}
. Hence, Lemma
5.7
is equivalent to the statement that 0 occurs with even multiplicity as an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which we know to be true by Thm.
5.3
.

Remark 5.11
.

It follows immediately from Thm.
5.9
that
N(k+2,l,q)\leq\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}
if
k+2\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. The dimension of the space of cusp forms is given by Lemma
2.4
, and implies the weaker inequality

N(k+2,l,q)\leq\left\lceil\frac{k}{q^{2}-1}\right\rceil.

This inequality is not obvious for small values of
k
, and there are in fact plenty of counterexamples if one considers
k
and
l
such that
k+2\not\equiv 2l\pmod{q-1}
. For instance, if
(k+2,l,q)=(12,1,4)
, we have
P(k+2,l,q)=\{0,3\}
whereas
\lceil 10/15\rceil=1
.

Example 5.12
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. By Prop.
3.25
.1 and Thm.
5.9
, the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{2^{m}+1,l}
has at most one non-repeated eigenvalue, namely 1. All other eigenvalues are repeated.

Theorem
5.9
implies that
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
has a repeated eigenvalue as soon as
N(k+2,l,q)<\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}
. We now show that this almost always happens.

Theorem 5.13
.

Suppose
2\mid q
. Then there are only finitely many weights in which the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
has no repeated eigenvalues.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}=\Theta(k)
, i.e., the dimension grows linearly with
k
. We will show that
N(k+2,l,q)=O(k^{\log_{2}(\varphi)})
, where
\varphi
is the golden ratio. Since
2>\varphi
, Thm.
5.9
then implies that some eigenvalue must be repeated if
k
is large enough.

Let
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the Stern-Brocot sequence, given by

a_{k}=\#\left\{0\leq j\leq k/2\ \bigg|\ \binom{k-j}{k}\equiv_{2}1\right\};

then certainly
N(k+2,l,q)\leq a_{k}
for all
k
. In
[
Leh29
]
, it is proven that for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\max\left\{a_{k}\ |\ k\leq 2^{N}-1\right\}=F_{N+1},

(18)

where
F_{i}
is the
i
-th Fibonacci number (with
F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1
). Since the Fibonacci sequence is
O(\varphi^{k})
with
\varphi
the golden ratio, we have

N(k+2,l,q)\leq a_{k}=O\left(\varphi^{\log_{2}(k)}\right)=O\left(k^{\log_{2}(\varphi)}\right),

which is what we wanted.
∎

Remark 5.14
.

If the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
has repeated eigenvalues for some
\mathfrak{p}
, then it has repeated eigenvalues for all
\mathfrak{p}
. However, it should be noted that the repeated eigenvalues may well depend on
\mathfrak{p}
in some non-trivial way (unlike the eigenvalues with odd multiplicity).

For any given value of
q
, the constants can be made explicit. This is demonstrated by the following theorem, which sheds light on the computations done for primes of degree
\leq 5
in
[
JP14
, Ex. 4.6]
.

Theorem 5.15
.

For
q=2
, the only values of
k
such that the Hecke action on
\operatorname{S}_{k}
has no repeated eigenvalues are
3\leq k\leq 8
and
k\in\{10,11,12,14,20,22\}
.

Proof.

We need to show that for all
k\geq 1
, the inequality

N(k,1,2)\leq\dim\operatorname{S}_{k}=\left\lceil\frac{k-2}{3}\right\rceil

is strict for all weights
k
except those listed in the theorem.

By Rmk.
5.6
, we have

N(k,1,2)=a_{k-2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+(-1)^{k}\right)\leq a_{k-2},

with
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
the Stern-Brocot sequence. By Lehmer’s bound (
18
), if
k_{0}=2^{N-1}
satisfies

F_{N+1}<\frac{2^{N-1}-2}{3}\leq\dim\operatorname{S}_{k_{0}},

then
a_{k}<\dim\operatorname{S}_{k}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
.
This inequality is achieved for
N=8
, as
34<42=126/3
. We conclude that there is a strict inequality

N(k,1,2)<\dim\operatorname{S}_{k}\text{ for all }k\geq 128,

and checking the cases
1\leq k\leq 127
by hand gives the theorem.
∎

5.4

Slopes

A popular way to study the Hecke action is via slopes, due in part to the relationship (in the classical setting) between slopes and
p
-adic families of modular forms. We use a slightly more general definition than the one usually found in the literature by allowing different valuations
v
.

Definition 5.16
.

Let
V
be a
K
-vector space and let
v
be a valuation on
K
. Let
\mathbf{T}\colon V\to V
be a linear map. An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
is a
v
-adic slope of
\mathbf{T}
if there exists an eigenvalue
\lambda\in\text{Sp}(\mathbf{T})
and a valuation
w
on
\bar{K}
such that
w|_{K}=v
and
w(\lambda)=\alpha
.
A slope
\alpha
is called
finite
if
\alpha\neq\infty
. The
multiplicity
of a slope
\alpha
is defined to be the integer

d_{v}(\alpha)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\text{Sp}(\mathbf{T})\\
w(\lambda)=\alpha\end{subarray}}m_{\lambda},

where
w
is a fixed valuation on
\bar{K}
extending
v
.
A
v
-adic slope of weight
k
and type
l
is a
v
-adic slope of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\to\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
. In this case, we also denote the multiplicity by
d_{v}(k,l,\mathfrak{p},\alpha):=d_{v}(\alpha)
.

Equivalently,
\alpha
is a slope of multiplicity
d
if and only if the
v
-adic Newton polygon of the characteristic polynomial
\det(\mathds{1}X-\mathbf{T})\in K[X]
of
\mathbf{T}
has a line segment of slope
\alpha
and projected length
d
.

Remark 5.17
.

Classically, the eigenvalues of the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{p}
acting on elliptic modular forms of weight
k
are algebraic integers of complex absolute value
p^{(k-1)/2}
. In particular, if
\ell\neq p
is a prime number, then the
\ell
-adic slopes of
\mathbf{T}_{p}
are all zero. For this reason, a slope of
\mathbf{T}_{p}
(or the Atkin operator
\operatorname{U}_{p}
, at higher level) is defined to be a
p
-adic slope. For Hecke operators on Drinfeld modular forms, it appears that non-zero
v
-adic slopes of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
may occur for any place
v
. In particular, we believe the case
v=v_{\infty}
to be of much interest. One reason for this is that a bound on
\infty
-adic slopes implies bounds on
v
-adic slopes for all other places
v
.

Proposition 5.18
.

(
[
Vri25
, Prop. 4.2]
)
Suppose
\mathbf{T}\colon V\to V
is a
K
-linear map such that no eigenvalue of
\mathbf{T}
is repeated
p
times. Then for any valuation
v
on
K
and any
c\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, the following are equivalent:

1.

v(\lambda)\geq c
for all
\lambda\in\text{Sp}(\mathbf{T})
;

2.

v(\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997ptV))\geq cn
for all
n\geq 1
.

In particular, under the condition that the Hecke eigenvalues have multiplicity less than
p
, Proposition
4.1
implies bounds on the
\infty
-adic slopes. Recall that the
\infty
-adic valuation on
K
is given by
v_{\infty}(f/g)=\deg(g)-\deg(f)
for
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
.

Theorem 5.19

(Ramanujan bound for slopes)
.

Suppose that the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
does not have
p
repeated eigenvalues. Then any
\infty
-adic slope
\alpha
of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
acting on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
satisfies

0\leq-\alpha<\deg(\mathfrak{p})\cdot\frac{k-2}{2}.

Since the Hecke operators are defined over
A
, we can also conclude (weaker) bounds on the
v
-adic slopes for valuations
v\neq v_{\infty}
.

Corollary 5.20
.

Let
v\neq v_{\infty}
be a valuation on
K
. Consider the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
.
Let
\lambda\in\Sp(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}})
be an eigenvalue which is not repeated
p
times, i.e.,
p\nmid m_{\lambda}
. Then we have

\frac{v(N_{K(\lambda)/K}(\lambda))}{[K(\lambda):K]}<\frac{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}{\deg(v)}\frac{k-2}{2}.

Proof.

Let
f:=N_{K(\lambda)/K}(\lambda)\in A
be the constant term of the minimal polynomial
m(X)
of
\lambda
. Let
w_{\infty}
be a valuation on a normal closure of
K(\lambda)
such that
w_{\infty}|_{K}=v_{\infty}
. By Theorem
5.19
, we have

-v_{\infty}(f)=-w_{\infty}(f)=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\sim\lambda}-w_{\infty}(\lambda^{\prime})<[K(\lambda):K]\deg(\mathfrak{p})\frac{k-2}{2},

where the sum runs over all roots
\lambda^{\prime}
of
m(X)
, counted with multiplicity.
By the product formula for valuations, we have
\deg(v)v(f)\leq-v_{\infty}(f)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

5.5

Oldforms and newforms

So far, we have focused on Drinfeld cusp forms of level 1, but there is no particular reason to restrict to this case; many results in this paper can be extended to higher level as well. There are some differences, however. For instance, to apply Böckle’s Eichler-Shimura theory at level
\Gamma
, one should work with Hecke operators
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
\mathfrak{p}
not dividing the level. In exchange, one has an interesting new operator available at level
\mathfrak{p}
: the
Atkin operator

\operatorname{U}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which is closely related to the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
at level 1
[
BV19
]
.

Another interesting phenomenon at higher level is the decomposition of cusp forms into oldforms and newforms. Classically, there is a natural way to decompose the space of elliptic cusp forms
\operatorname{S}_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(Np))
into oldforms (cusp forms which come from
\operatorname{S}_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
) and newforms (those which do not). The Petersson inner product plays a key role in proving this decomposition, but in the Drinfeld setting, we have no such inner product at our disposal.

In
[
BV22
]
, Bandini and Valentino propose a decomposition of
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))
into oldforms and newforms, where the oldforms are spanned by the image of certain degeneracy maps
\delta_{1},\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\to\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))
. They conjecture that this always yields a direct sum decomposition and prove it in some special cases. They moreover note
[
BV22
, Cor. 2.10]
that the direct sum decomposition holds if and only if
\pm\wp^{(k-2)/2}
does not occur as an eigenvalue for the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
. Combined with our results, this can be strengthened.

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
k\geq 0
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and suppose that

\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))\neq\operatorname{S}^{\text{new}}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))\oplus\operatorname{S}^{\text{old}}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})).

Then the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
has eigenvalue
\wp^{(k-2)/2}
or
-\wp^{(k-2)/2}
with multiplicity a positive multiple of
p
.

Proof.

By assumption, one of the eigenvalues
\pm\wp^{(k-2)/2}
must occur at level 1. But if this eigenvalue is not repeated
p
times, this would (by Prop.
5.18
) contradict Prop.
4.1
.
∎

Corollary 5.22
.

If
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}<p
, then
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))=\operatorname{S}^{\text{new}}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))\oplus\operatorname{S}^{\text{old}}_{k,l}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p}))
. If
l\equiv 1\pmod{q-1}
, then the same holds if
\dim\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\leq p
.

Proof.

The first part is immediate from Theorem
5.21
, and the second part follows because the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,1}
has Hecke eigenvalue 1 with multiplicity 1
[
Hat21
, Lem. 2.4]
.
∎

5.6

Conjectures

It is possible to compute the characteristic polynomial of an operator from its traces under the condition that no eigenvalue is repeated
p
times. For
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, this condition has in practice always been fulfilled, so we have been able to compute slopes from the traces obtained via the trace formula. The resulting data contained some notable patterns. We state the most convincing of these here in the form of conjectures.

Our first conjecture is the strong Ramanujan bound, which we have already stated for traces. The variant for slopes is a priori stronger.

Conjecture 5.23

(Strong Ramanujan bound)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a maximal ideal. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
be a
v
-adic slope of weight
k
for
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then

|\alpha|\leq\frac{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}{\deg(v)}\cdot\frac{k-(q+1)}{2}.

Remark 5.24
.

By Prop.
5.18
, it is equivalent (assuming there are no repeated eigenvalues) to ask that

\deg\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})\leq\frac{nd(k-(q+1))}{2},

where
d=\deg(\mathfrak{p})
; hence Thm.
4.9
provides some evidence towards the conjecture.

Going further, our data suggests that the
\infty
-adic slopes attain the strong Ramanujan bound periodically, and with predictable multiplicity.

Conjecture 5.25
.

Suppose
2\nmid q
. Fix
1\leq l\leq q-1
and let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a maximal ideal of degree
d
. For
n\geq 1
, we set
k_{n}=(n-1)q^{2}+(2l-n)q+1
. Then the
\infty
-adic slopes of weight
k
and type
l
for
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
attain the strong Ramanujan bound if and only if
k=k_{n}
for some
n
and
(n,l)\neq(2,1)
. Moreover, the multiplicity with which the bound is attained is given by

d_{l}(n):=d_{\infty}\left(k_{n},l,\mathfrak{p},-\frac{d(k_{n}-(q+1))}{2}\right)=n-2\left\lceil\frac{n-l}{q+1}\right\rceil.

Remark 5.26
.

1.

In Conj.
5.25
, the exception for
(n,l)=(2,1)
is necessary: one easily verifies that
\operatorname{S}_{q^{2}+1,1}
is a one-dimensional space spanned by
E^{q}h
, which is a single cusp form and thus has eigenvalue
1
, which does not attain the strong Ramanujan bound. This is consistent with the fact that
d_{l}(n)=0
in this (and only this) case.

2.

Note that for
1\leq l\leq q-1
, the space
\operatorname{S}_{k_{1},l}
is spanned by
E^{l-1}h^{l}
. According to the conjecture, this is the first eigenform to attain the strong Ramanujan bound in type
l
, which is true for primes of degree 1 by Rmk.
4.4
.

3.

A more intuitive description of
d_{l}(n)
is the following: we have
d_{l}(1)=1
for any
l
, and for
n\geq 1
we have

d_{l}(n+1)=\begin{cases}d_{l}(n)+1&\text{ if }\dim\operatorname{S}_{k_{n+1},l}=\dim\operatorname{S}_{k_{n},l}+1;\\
d_{l}(n)-1&\text{ if }\dim\operatorname{S}_{k_{n+1},l}=\dim\operatorname{S}_{k_{n},l}.\end{cases}

4.

If
2\mid q
, we still believe Conjecture
5.23
, but Conjecture
5.25
is certainly false. For
q=2
, for instance, data suggests that the strong Ramanujan bound is attained for all
k\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, and the multiplicity with which it does so is always 1.

Finally, we consider the following question: which
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
can appear as a
v
-adic slope of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
? For primes of degree 1 and
v\in\{v_{\mathfrak{p}},v_{\infty}\}
, the answer seems to depend on the type in a precise way.

Conjecture 5.27

(The slopes which appear)
.

Suppose
2\nmid q
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a prime of degree 1. Let
v\in\{v_{\mathfrak{p}},-v_{\infty}\}
, and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
be a
v
-adic slope of type
l
. Then
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
is an integer, and moreover

\alpha\equiv l-1\pmod{q-1}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\alpha\equiv l-1+\frac{q-1}{2}\pmod{q-1}.

6

Computations for low weights

In this section, we collect some consequences of
the trace formula
when applied to spaces of cusp forms
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
of small dimension.

6.1

Power eigensystems and
A
-expansions

Theorem
2.10
shows that the Hecke eigensystem of an eigenform with
A
-expansion is of a very particular form. It is natural to ask whether the converse is true: if
f
is an eigenform such that
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}f=\wp^{n-1}f
for all
\mathfrak{p}
, does
f
admit an
A
-expansion?

In
[
Pet13
, Ex. 2.7]
, Petrov considers the eigenform
E^{2}h^{2}\in\operatorname{S}_{12,0}
for
q=3
. By considering its
t
-expansion, one sees that it cannot have an
A
-expansion, but Petrov’s computations indicate that its Hecke eigensystem looks like that of a form with
A
-expansion. This suggests that the answer to the above question is negative. We confirm Petrov’s suspicion here.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{3}[T]
be a non-zero prime ideal with monic generator
\wp
. Then the
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-eigenvalue of
E^{2}h^{2}
equals
\wp^{3}
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{S}_{12,0}=\langle E^{2}h^{2}\rangle
is one-dimensional, the
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-eigenvalue of
E^{2}h^{2}
equals the trace of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{12,0}
. Using
the trace formula
twice, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{12,0})=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)a^{10}b+\wp^{2}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{8,0})=\wp^{3}+\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)a^{10}b,

using that
\operatorname{S}_{8,0}=\langle h^{2}\rangle
and
h^{2}
has an
A
-expansion by Prop.
2.9
.
On the other hand, consider

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{14,1})=\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)\left(a^{12}+a^{10}b\wp+a^{6}\wp^{4}\right).

(19)

Since
\operatorname{S}_{14,1}
is spanned by eigenforms with
A
-expansions, we have
\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{14,1})=1+\wp^{4}
for all
\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\operatorname{S}_{6,1}=\langle Eh\rangle
and
\operatorname{S}_{8,1}=\langle E^{2}h\rangle
, we can also deduce that

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)a^{12}=\bigg(\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)a^{4}\bigg)^{3}=1,\qquad\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)a^{6}=1.

Plugging this into (
19
) gives the desired

\sum_{a,b}\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a,b)a^{10}b=0.

∎

Remark 6.2
.

If
q=3
, the eigensystem of
Eh^{2}\in\operatorname{S}_{10,0}
remains elusive. As already observed by Petrov in his thesis, computations suggest that

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{10,0})=\wp(-T-T^{3})\quad\text{for all }\mathfrak{p}=(\wp).

Computing the trace by mimicking the proof of Thm.
6.1
does not work.
Indeed,
the trace formula
gives

Proposition 6.4

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
2\leq l\leq q-1
and
0\leq n\leq q-1
. Then

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997ptE^{n}h^{l})=T^{q(l+n-q)+l-n-1}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997ptE^{q-1-n}h^{q+1-l}).

Proof.

First note that

\langle E^{n}h^{l}\rangle=\operatorname{S}_{q^{2}+1+q(l+n-q)+l-n-1,l}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle E^{q-n-1}h^{q+1-l}\rangle=\operatorname{S}_{q^{2}+1-(q(l+n-q)+l-n-1),q+1-l},

and both of these spaces are one-dimensional. Hence the modular forms from the statement are eigenforms. Write
N:=q(l+n-q)+l-n-1
.

Suppose first that
N\geq 0
, i.e.
l+n>q
or
l+n=q
and
n\leq(q-1)/2
. Then Theorem
3.15
with
p^{m}=q^{2}
yields

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997ptE^{n}h^{l})=T^{q(l+n-q)+l-n-1}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997ptE^{q-1-n}h^{q+1-l})+\epsilon(T),

where

\epsilon(T)=(-1)^{l-1}\sum_{0\leq 2i<l+n-q-1}\binom{q(l+n-q)-n-1-i(q-1)}{l-1+i(q-1)}T^{l-1+i(q-1)}.

By
Lucas’s theorem
, we have

\binom{q(l+n-q)-n-1-i(q-1)}{l-1+i(q-1)}\equiv_{p}\binom{l+n-q-1-i}{i}\binom{q-n-1+i}{l-1-i}

as long as
q-n-1+i<q
and
l-1-i\geq 0
and
l+n-q-1-i\geq 0
. In the range
0\leq 2i<l+n-q-1
, all of these conditions hold. In addition, for these values of
i
we have
q-n-1+i<l-1-i
, so the latter binomial coefficient is zero. Thus
\epsilon(T)=0
.

If instead
N<0
, then the statement follows from the first case after replacing
l
by
q+1-l
and
n
by
q-1-n
.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Write
q=p^{r}
. Then the following hold:

1.

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997ptE^{q-1}h^{l})=T^{q(l-1)}.

, and

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.99997ptE^{q-1-ap^{s}}h^{l})=T^{q(l-1)+(1-q)ap^{s}}.

The first statement is trivial if

. If

, it follows from Prop.
6.5
by setting

, since

has an

. The second statement follows similarly by setting

.
∎

Proposition 6.10
.

Suppose
2\nmid q
and let
k+2=2q^{2}-2
. Then the characteristic polynomial of
\mathbf{T}_{T}
acting on the two-dimensional space
\operatorname{S}_{k+2,0}
is irreducible and given by

\det(\mathds{1}X-\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,0})=X^{2}-(3T^{2q-3}-T^{q-2})X+2T^{q^{2}+3q-6}-2T^{q^{2}+2q-5}+T^{3q-5}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.11
and
Lucas’s theorem
, we obtain

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,0})=-\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{2q^{2}-q-2-i(q-1)}{q-2+i(q-1)}T^{q-2+i(q-1)}=3T^{2q-3}-T^{q-2}.

By Theorem
3.29
and the above computation, we have

\displaystyle\Tr

\displaystyle(\mathbf{T}_{T}^{2}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,0})=-T^{2q-4}+3T^{4q-6}+\sum_{m=(q-1)/2}^{q-2}(-1)^{m-1+\frac{q-1}{2}}4^{-m}\binom{m}{(q-1)/2}S_{m},

\displaystyle S_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{2q-1}\sum_{0\leq 2a\leq N}\binom{2q^{2}-3-m-a(q-1)}{m-1+a(q-1)}\binom{2(q^{2}-1-m-a(q-1))}{2(q-1-m)+(N-2a)(q-1)}T^{2q-4+N(q-1)}.

By
Lucas’s theorem
, we see that most of the binomial coefficients vanish,
and after computing the non-zero terms we are left with

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{T}^{2}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k+2,0})=-4T^{q^{2}+3q-6}+4T^{q^{2}+2q-5}+9T^{4q-6}-8T^{3q-5}+T^{2q-4}.

and the linear term is

, which gives the claimed expression. To see that the characteristic polynomial is irreducible, we make the change of variables

to obtain

c(Y)=Y^{2}-T^{q-2}(T^{q-1}-1)X+2T^{3q-5}(1-T^{q-1})^{q+1}.

Suppose for contradiction that

for some

. The Newton polygon of

then shows that there exists a sequence of elements
(\epsilon_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\in\{0,1\}^{q-1}

where

. This gives

On the other hand, we have

, and hence

Let

\deg(Q)=0

N(q-1)=q-1+(q-1-N)(q-1)

∎

Appendix A
The number of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules in a fixed isogeny class

Jonas Bergström and Sjoerd de Vries

A.1

Hurwitz class numbers

We begin by defining Hurwitz class numbers (alternatively called Gauß class numbers) following
[
Gek08
, Section 6]
.

Let
L
be an imaginary extension of degree two of the quotient field
K
of
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{L}
denote the integral closure of
A
in
L
. An
A
-order
in
L
is a subring
B
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
which contains
A
and is free of rank 2 as an
A
-module. Any
A
-order is of the form
A+f\mathcal{O}_{L}
for some monic
f\in A
.

A
fractional ideal
of
B
is a non-zero finitely generated
B
-submodule of
L
. Two fractional ideals
I,J
are said to be
equivalent
if they are related by
I=gJ
for some
g\in L^{\times}
. Let
H(B)
denote the number of equivalence classes of fractional ideals of
B
.
Define the Kronecker symbol
\chi_{L}
via

\displaystyle\chi_{L}(\mathfrak{p})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ splits in }L;\\
0&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ ramifies in }L;\\
-1&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ is inert in }L,\end{cases}

for any prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
. If
\mathfrak{p}=(\wp)\subset A
, we also write
\chi_{L}(\wp)
for the Kronecker symbol of
\mathfrak{p}
.

For any monic
f\in A
, let
\mathbb{P}(f)
denote the set of monic prime divisors of
f
and put
B_{f}=A+f\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
For any
f\in A
, we then have

H(B_{f})=\sum_{g|f}\frac{H(\mathcal{O}_{L})\cdot q^{\deg(g)}}{[\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:B^{\times}_{g}]}\prod_{\wp\in\mathbb{P}(g)}(1-\chi_{L}(\wp)q^{-\deg(\wp)}),

(20)

where the sum is over monic
g
in
A
, see
[
Gek08
, Section 6]
.

Equation (
20
) simplifies modulo
p
to the following.

Lemma A.1
.

For any monic
f\in A
,

H(B_{f})\equiv_{p}H(\mathcal{O}_{L})\prod_{\wp\in\mathbb{P}(f)}(1-\chi_{L}(\wp)).

Proof.

Since
L
is a quadratic extension, any
A
-order has unit group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
or
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
. This tells us that
[\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:B_{g}^{\times}]\in\{1,q+1\}
and so
[\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:B_{g}^{\times}]\equiv_{p}1
.
If a monic
g\in A
is not square-free, then

\frac{H(\mathcal{O}_{L})\cdot q^{\deg(g)}}{[\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:B^{\times}_{g}]}\prod_{\wp\in\mathbb{P}(g)}(1-\chi_{L}(\wp)q^{-\deg(\wp)})\equiv_{p}0.

Since there is a
1
-to-
1
correspondence between subsets of
\mathbb{P}(f)
and monic square-free divisors of
f
, we find from Equation (
20
) that

H(B_{f})\equiv_{p}H(\mathcal{O}_{L})\prod_{S\subseteq\mathbb{P}(f)}\prod_{\wp\in S}-\chi_{L}(\wp)=H(\mathcal{O}_{L})\prod_{\wp\in\mathbb{P}(f)}(1-\chi_{L}(\wp)),

as claimed.
∎

A.2

Isomorphism classes

The following result gives a formula for the number of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules over a finite field in a fixed isogeny class. In the cases of a commutative endomorphism algebra, this number can be expressed in terms of Hurwitz class numbers.

Proposition A.2
.

Given a characteristic polynomial as in Prop.
2.19
, we have

\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=\begin{cases}H(A[\pi])&\text{in case 1;}\\
H(A[\sqrt{-b\wp}])&\text{in case 2;}\\
2&\text{in case 3;}\\
(q^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}-1)/(q-1)&\text{in case 4.}\end{cases}

Remark A.3
.

For
q
odd, this result follows from
[
Yu95
, Prop. 7]
. We note that each isomorphism class of Drinfeld modules in loc. cit. is weighted by the factor
q-1
divided by the number of automorphisms of any representative of this isomorphism class. For
q
even and
n=1
, this result follows from
[
Gek08
, Prop. 6.8]
.

Proof.

The endomorphism algebra of any Drinfeld module in an isogeny class in case 1, 2, 3 will be commutative, and more precisely an imaginary extension
L
of
K
of degree two.
By
[
KKP24
, Prop. 5.1]
there is a Drinfeld module with endomorphism ring
B
precisely if
B
is a ring such that
A[\pi]\subset B\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
B
is locally maximal at
\pi
. In cases 2 and 3 we see directly that only
B=\mathcal{O}_{L}
with
A[\pi]\subset B\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
is locally maximal at
\pi
, and in case 1, by
[
KKP24
, Cor. 2.9]
, every
A[\pi]\subset B\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
is locally maximal at
\pi
.

It follows from an upcoming erratum to Theorem 5.4 of
[
KKP24
]
that
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=H(A[\pi])
in case 1, that
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
in case 2 and that
\#\text{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}(a,b)=2
in case 3 (which in case 3 is due to the fact that there are two Frobenius-stable lattices inside the Dieudonné module at
\mathfrak{p}
).

For case 4, let
\Sigma(2,\mathfrak{p})
denote the set of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-isomorphism classes of supersingular Drinfeld modules of rank 2 with characteristic
\mathfrak{p}
. Then we have (see
[
Gek91
, Ex. 4.4]
or
[
Pap23
, Cor. 4.4.12]
),

\#\Sigma(2,\mathfrak{p})=\begin{cases}(q^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}-1)/(q^{2}-1)&\text{if }{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}\equiv_{2}0;\\
(q^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}-1)/(q^{2}-1)+\frac{q}{q+1}&\text{if }{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}\equiv_{2}1.\end{cases}

On the other hand, since
n
is even, every supersingular Drinfeld module is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}

[
Gek91
, Prop. 4.2]
. Therefore we also have

\#\Sigma(2,\mathfrak{p})=\sum_{[\varphi]/\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}}\frac{1}{\#\Aut(\varphi)},

where the sum is taken over the isomorphism classes of supersingular Drinfeld modules over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
. If
\deg(\mathfrak{p})\equiv_{2}1
, the supersingular Drinfeld modules in case 3 of Prop.
2.19
contribute

2\cdot\frac{q^{2}-q}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}=\frac{q}{q+1}

to
\#\Sigma(2,\mathfrak{p})
. The only other contribution comes from the supersingular Drinfeld modules
\varphi
of case 4. These come in
q-1
isogeny classes, each of which contains the same number
N
of isomorphism classes by Remark
2.21
. Moreover, any Drinfeld module in case 4 has automorphism group
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
. Putting this together gives

\frac{q-1}{q^{2}-1}N=\frac{q^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}-1}{q^{2}-1}.

∎

Remark A.4
.

If
q
is even then
L/K
is inseparable in case 2. Moreover,
A[\sqrt{-b\wp}]=\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
H(A[\sqrt{-b\wp}])=1
by Lemma
A.5
.

A.3

Hyperelliptic curves

In this subsection, we describe a way to make the computation of the Hurwitz class number of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
explicit in terms of hyperelliptic curves (and their Jacobians).

Fix a monic irreducible polynomial
c(X)
of degree two with coefficients in
A
.
Denote its splitting field over
K
by
L
.
Let
\pi
be a fixed root of
c(X)
in
L
.

Let us first assume
q
to be odd.
Factor the discriminant of
c(X)
into a product
Df^{2}
with
D
square-free and
f
monic. The extension
L/K
is then the splitting field of
\tilde{c}(X,T)=X^{2}-D
. Put
g=\lceil\deg(D)/2-1\rceil
.

Secondly, let us assume
q
to be even. Say that
c(X)=c_{1}(X)=X^{2}+r_{1}X+s_{1}
. If
r_{1}=0
, then
L/K
is inseparable, and all places of
K
ramify in
L
. Assume therefore that
r_{1}\neq 0
. Let
g_{1}
be the integer such that
2g_{1}+1\leq\max(2\deg(r_{1}),\deg(s_{1}))\leq 2g_{1}+2
. Put

f_{1}=\gcd(r_{1},(s_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+s_{1}(r_{1}^{\prime})^{2})
,

\hat{f}_{1}=\prod_{\wp\in\mathbb{P}(f_{1})}\wp
and
m=\deg(\hat{f}_{1})
.
If
\deg(f_{1})>0
, then let
l
be the remainder of
s_{1}^{q^{m}/2}
divided by
r_{1}
. Finally put
r_{2}=r_{1}/\hat{f}_{1}
and
s_{2}=(s_{1}+r_{1}l+l^{2})/\hat{f}_{1}^{2}
. Then
r_{2}
and
s_{2}
will be in
A
, see
[
Ber09
, Lemma 9.3]
.
Let
g_{2}
be the integer such that
2g_{2}+1\leq\max(2\deg(r_{2}),\deg(s_{2}))\leq 2g_{2}+2
and note that
g_{2}<g_{1}
.
Repeat this process with
c_{2}(X)=X^{2}+r_{2}X+s_{2}
, continuing until
f_{k}=\gcd(r_{k},(s_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+s_{k}(r_{k}^{\prime})^{2})
has degree
0
, for some
k\geq 1
.
Then put
f=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\hat{f}_{i}
,
\tilde{c}(X)=\tilde{c}(X,T)=X^{2}+r_{k}X+s_{k}
and
g=g_{k}
.

Let again
q
be arbitrary, and
L/K
separable. If
g\geq 0
, then
\tilde{c}(X,T)
is an affine equation for the (geometrically irreducible, projective and non-singular) curve
C_{L}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus
g
that comes with a degree two cover of the projective line. So if
g\geq 2
then
C_{L}
will be a hyperelliptic curve.

Let
\tilde{\pi}
be a root of
\tilde{c}(X)
. Then
A[\tilde{\pi}]=\mathcal{O}_{L}
, and by the Jacobian criterion for smoothness of
c(X)
(see for instance
[
Ber09
, Section 8]
for the characteristic two case) we see that
A[\pi]=B_{f}
.
For
\wp\in\mathbb{P}(f)
, we find that

\displaystyle\chi_{L}(\wp)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\tilde{c}(X,T)\text{ has two distinct linear factors in }(A/(\wp))[X];\\
0&\text{if }\tilde{c}(X,T)\text{ is a square in }(A/(\wp))[X];\\
-1&\text{if }\tilde{c}(X,T)\text{ is irreducible in }(A/(\wp))[X].\end{cases}

If we put
\tilde{c}_{\infty}(Y,s):=s^{2g+2}\tilde{c}(s^{-(g+1)}Y,1/s)
, then
\tilde{c}_{\infty}(Y,s)
lies in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[s,Y]
. Thus
\tilde{c}_{\infty}(Y,0)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[Y]
, and the Kronecker symbol of the prime
\infty=(1/T)
of
K
is given by

\chi_{L}(\infty)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\tilde{c}_{\infty}(Y,0)\text{ has two distinct factors in }\mathbb{F}_{q}[Y];\\
0&\text{if }\tilde{c}_{\infty}(Y,0)\text{ is a square in }\mathbb{F}_{q}[Y];\\
-1&\text{if }\tilde{c}_{\infty}(Y,0)\text{ is irreducible in }\mathbb{F}_{q}[Y].\end{cases}

Note that the extension
L/K
is imaginary if and only if
\chi_{L}(\infty)\neq 1
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
L/K
be an imaginary quadratic extension.

1.

If
L/K
is inseparable, then
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})=1
.

2.

If
L/K
is separable and
g=-1
, then
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})=1
.

3.

If
L/K
is separable and
g\geq 0
,
then

H(\mathcal{O}_{L})=(1-\chi_{L}(\infty))\#J_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

where
J_{L}
denotes the Jacobian of
C_{L}
.

Proof.

If
L/K
is inseparable, then
q
is even and
L=K(\sqrt{T})
, so the map
L\to K
induced by
\sqrt{T}\mapsto T
gives an isomorphism
L\cong K
of abstract fields. Since
\mathcal{O}_{L}\cong A
under this isomorphism, we have
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})=1
.

If
L/K
is separable and
g=-1
, then
L\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(T)
and
\mathcal{O}_{L}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
, so
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})=1
.

Finally, assume that
L/K
is separable and that
g\geq 0
.
The divisor class group of
L
will then be isomorphic to
J_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
The Hurwitz class number
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
is equal to the
S
-class number of
L
, with
S
the set containing only the place above
\infty
.
If
\infty
is ramified in
L
then
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
is equal to the divisor class number of
L
, and if
\infty
is inert in
L
then
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
is twice the divisor class number, see
[
Ros02
, Prop. 14.1]
or
[
Sti09
, Exerc. 5.10]
.
∎

Remark A.6
.

The number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points on the Jacobian of a curve can be computed in terms of the number of points on the curve as follows. If
\psi_{L}(z)
is the characteristic polynomial of the geometric Frobenius acting on the first
\ell
-adic étale cohomology of
C_{L}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then
\#J_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\psi_{L}(1)
. Moreover, if
e_{i}
denotes the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial and
p_{i}
the
i
-th power sum polynomial, then there are rational numbers
r_{i,\lambda}
for each partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i})
of the integer
i\geq 1
, such that

e_{i}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash i}r_{i,\lambda}\prod_{j=1}^{i}p_{j}^{\lambda_{j}}

and

\psi_{L}(z)=z^{2g}+q^{g}+(-1)^{g}\left(\sum_{\lambda\vdash g}r_{g,\lambda}\prod_{j=1}^{g}a_{j}^{\lambda_{j}}\right)z^{g}+\sum_{i=1}^{g-1}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{\lambda\vdash i}r_{i,\lambda}\prod_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}^{\lambda_{j}}\right)(z^{2g-i}+q^{i}z^{i})

with
a_{j}=q^{j}+1-\#C_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}})
for
j=1,\ldots,g
.

Finally, if
q
is even, let us classify when
H(A[\pi])
is even. This result is used in Section
3.4
.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
q
be even and put
c(X)=X^{2}+rX+s
. Assume that
L/K
is imaginary. Then
H(A[\pi])
is even if and only if
\deg(r)>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
,
H(A[\pi])
is even if
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
is even. Let us first consider the parity of
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
.

If
L/K
is inseparable, which happens only if
r=0
, then
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})=1
by Lemma
A.5
.1.
If
g=-1
, then
\deg(r)=0
and
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})=1
by Lemma
A.5
.2.

Assume now that
g\geq 0
and
L/K
is separable.
By Lemma
A.5
.3, we know that
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
is even if
\infty
is inert. We also find that if
\infty
is inert then
\deg(r)\geq\deg(\tilde{r})>0
.
Assume therefore that
\infty
is ramified.
Since any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point of the Jacobian
J_{L}
of
C_{L}
is a torsion point and
J_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is finite, we have that

J_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\prod_{\ell}J_{L}[\ell^{N}](\mathbb{F}_{q})

for some
N\geq 0
, where the product is over all prime numbers
\ell
.
Moreover,
J_{L}[\ell^{N}](\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a finite
\ell
-group, so
\#J_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is even if and only if
\#J_{L}[2^{N}](\mathbb{F}_{q})
is even.
This is equivalent to
J_{L}[2^{N}](\mathbb{F}_{q})
being non-trivial which is in turn equivalent to
J_{L}[2](\mathbb{F}_{q})
being non-trivial.

Let
P_{\infty}
denote the unique point of
C_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\infty
.
Let
\tilde{c}(X,T)
be the affine equation for
C_{L}
as above.
For any point
P_{(\alpha,\beta)}
of
C_{L}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\setminus\{P_{\infty}\}
with
T
-coordinate
\alpha
and
X
-coordinate
\beta
, we have that
[P]+[P_{(\alpha,\beta+\tilde{r}(\alpha))}]-2[P_{\infty}]=0\in J_{L}[2](\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
Hence, if
\tilde{r}(\alpha)=0
then
\beta
is the unique square root of
\tilde{s}(\alpha)
and the divisor
[P_{(\alpha,\sqrt{\tilde{s}(\alpha)})}]-[P_{\infty}]
is in
J_{L}[2](\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
Thus, for any
h\in\mathbb{P}(\tilde{r})
we get a divisor

D_{h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
h(\alpha)=0\end{subarray}}[P_{(\alpha,\sqrt{\tilde{s}(\alpha)})}]-\deg(h)[P_{\infty}]\in J_{L}[2](\mathbb{F}_{q})\subset J_{L}[2](\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

For any non-empty
S\subset\mathbb{P}(\tilde{r})
,
D_{S}=\sum_{h\in S}D_{h}
is a reduced divisor, and hence will be non-zero.
Moreover, for any non-empty distinct
S,T\subset\mathbb{P}(\tilde{r})
,
D_{S}+D_{T}=D_{S\cup T\setminus S\cap T}\neq 0
.
It then follows from the Deuring-Shafarevich formula (see for instance
[
Shi11
, Thm. 1.2]
) that the
2^{\#\mathbb{P}(\tilde{r})}
elements
D_{S}
for
S\subset\mathbb{P}(\tilde{r})
are precisely the elements of
J_{L}[2](\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
We conclude that
H(\mathcal{O}_{L})
will be odd precisely if
\deg(\tilde{r})=0
and
\deg(\tilde{s})>0
.

Finally, let
A[\pi]=B_{f}
for some
f\in A
. For any
\wp\in\mathbb{P}(f)
,
1-\chi_{L}(\wp)
is odd precisely if
\wp
ramifies in
L
. If
L
is inseparable then this happens for all
\wp\in\mathbb{P}(f)
, so in this case it follows from Lemma
A.1
and the above that
H(A[\pi])
is odd. If
\deg(\tilde{r})=0
then no
\wp\in\mathbb{P}(f)
ramifies in
L
. We conclude that if
\deg(\tilde{r})=0
then, by Lemma
A.1
and the above,
H(A[\pi])
is odd precisely if
f=1
, that is, if
\deg(r)=0
.
∎

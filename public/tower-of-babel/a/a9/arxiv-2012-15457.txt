Topological R R -pressure and topological pressure of free semigroup actions
Yinan Zheng, Qian Xiao

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the definition of topological r r -pressure of free semigroup actions on compact metric space and provide some properties of it. Through skew-product transformation into a medium, we can obtain the following two main results. 1. We extend the result that the topological pressure is the limit of topological r r -pressure in [ 6 ] to free semigroup actions ( r → 0 r\to 0 ). 2. Let f i , f_{i}, i = 0 , 1 , ⋯ , m − 1 i=0,1,\cdots,m-1 , be homeomorphisms on a compact metric space. For any continuous function, we verify that the topological pressure of f 0 , ⋯ , f m − 1 f_{0},\cdots,f_{m-1} equals the topological pressure of f 0 − 1 , ⋯ , f m − 1 − 1 . f_{0}^{-1},\cdots,f_{m-1}^{-1}.

1.
Introduction

The topological entropy was introduced by Adler et al
[
1
]
as an invariant of topological conjugacy, which describes the complexity of a system. Later, using spanning sets and separated sets, Bowen
[
3
]
defined topological entropy for a uniformly continuous map on metric space and proved that for a compact space, they coincide with that defined by Adler et al.
As a natural extension of topological entropy, topological pressure is a rich source of dynamical systems. Ruelle
[
14
]
first introduced the concept of topological pressure of additive potentials for expansive dynamical systems. Walters
[
16
,
17
]
then extended this concept to a compact space with the continuous transformation. Since topological pressure plays an important role in dynamical systems, some researchers try to find some generalization of topological pressure applicable to other systems (see, for example,
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
18
]
).

In 1980, Feldman
[
8
]
introduced the concept of
r
-entropy for the interaction between
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. On this basis, the notion of topological
r
-entropy and measure-theoretic
r
-entropy of a continuous map were introduced in
[
13
]
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and continuous map
f
from
X
into itself. For a finite set
A
, we denote the cardinality of
A
by Card
A
. Now for
x\in X,~n\geq 0,~\varepsilon>0
,

0<r<1
and
\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
, let

B(x,n,\varepsilon,r,f)=\left\{y\in X:\frac{1}{n}Card\{0\leq i\leq n-1:d(f^{i}x,f^{i}y)<\varepsilon\}>1-r\right\}.

A subset
F
is called an
(n,\varepsilon,r,f)
-spanning set of
X
(with respect to
f
), if for arbitrary
x\in X
, there exists
y\in F
, such that
x\in B(y,n,\varepsilon,r,f)
.

Denote by
r_{n}(f,\varepsilon,r)
the smallest cardinality of any
(n,\varepsilon,r,f)
-spanning set of
X
. In
[
13
]
, Ren et al defined

h_{r}{(f,\varepsilon)}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{n}(f,\varepsilon,r),

and

h_{r}{(f)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{r}{(f,\varepsilon)},

h_{r}{(f)}
is called the topological
r
-entropy of
f
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
13
]
, Corollary 2.5)
Suppose that
(X,d)
is a compact space with the metric
d
,
f:X\rightarrow X
is a continuous map. Then

\lim_{r\to 0}h_{r}{(f)}=h{(f)},

where
h{(f)}
denotes the topological entropy of
f
.

Moreover, Zhu et al
[
19
]
introduced the notion of topological
r
-entropy of free semigroup actions on a compact metric space and proved the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
19
]
, Theorem 1.1)
Let
f_{0},\dotsc,f_{m-1}
be continuous transformations of a compact metric space
(X,d)
to itself. Then

\lim_{r\to 0}h_{r}(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1})=h(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1}),

where
h(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1})
denotes the topological entropy of
f_{0},\cdots,f_{m-1}
and

h_{r}(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1})
denotes the topological
r
-entropy of
f_{0},\cdots,f_{m-1}
(
[
19
]
,definition 3.1).

In
[
6
]
, Chen further introduced the concept of topological
r
-pressure in compact metric space and gave relevant properties.

For
\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
, put

Q_{n}\left(f,\varphi,\varepsilon,r\right)=\inf\left\{\sum_{x\in F}e^{\left(S_{n}\varphi\right)(x)}:F\text{ is }a\left(n,\varepsilon,r,f\right)\text{-spanning set of $X$}\right\}.

In
[
6
]
, Chen defined

P_{r}(f,\varphi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}\left(f,\varphi,\varepsilon,r\right),

P_{r}(f,\varphi)
is called the topological
r
-pressure of
f
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
6
]
, Corollary 3.2.2)
Let
f\colon X\to X
be a continuous map of a compact metric space
(X,d)
, and
\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
. Then

\lim_{r\to 0}P_{r}(f,\varphi)=P(f,\varphi),

where
P(f,\varphi)
denotes the topological pressure of
f
.

Based on the above results, we introduce the notion of topological
r
-pressure for a free semigroup action and give some properties of the notion in this paper. The main results of this paper are the following two theorems.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f_{0},\dotsc,f_{m-1}
be continuous transformations of a compact metric space
(X,d)
to itself, and
\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
. Then

(1.1)

\lim_{r\to 0}P_{r}(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=P(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1},\varphi),

where
P(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1},\varphi)
denotes the topological pressure of
f_{0},\cdots,f_{m-1}
and
P_{r}(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1},\varphi)
denotes the topological
r
-pressure of
f_{0},\cdots,f_{m-1}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f_{0},\dotsc,f_{m-1}
be homeomorphisms of a compact metric space
(X,d)
to itself, and
\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
. Then

(1.2)

P(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=P(f_{0}^{-1},\cdots,f_{m-1}^{-1},\varphi).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Topological pressure of free semigroup actions

Let
F_{m}^{+}
be the set of all finite words of symbols
0,1,\cdots,m-1
. For every
w\in F_{m}^{+}
,
|w|
denotes the length of
w
, i.e., the number of symbols in
w
. If
w,w^{\prime}\in F_{m}^{+}
, define
ww^{\prime}
be the word obtained by writing
w^{\prime}
to the right of
w
. With respect to this law of composition,
F_{m}^{+}
is a free semigroup with
m
generators. We write
w\leq w^{\prime}
if there exists a word
w^{\prime\prime}
such that
w^{\prime}=w^{\prime\prime}w
.

Denote the set of all two-side infinite sequences of symbols
0,1,\ldots,m-1
by
\Sigma_{m}
, that is,

\Sigma_{m}=\{\omega=(\cdots,\omega_{-1},\overset{*}{\omega_{0}},\omega_{1},\cdots):\omega_{i}=0,1,\ldots,m-1~for~all~integer~i\}.

A metric on
\Sigma_{m}
is introduced by setting

d^{\prime}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=1/2^{k},~where~k=\inf\{|n|:\omega_{n}\neq\omega_{n}^{\prime}\}.

Obviously,
\Sigma_{m}
is compact with respect to this metric. The Bernoulli shift
\sigma:\Sigma_{m}\rightarrow\Sigma_{m}
is a homeomorphism of
\Sigma_{m}
given by the formula:

(\sigma\omega)_{i}=\omega_{i+1}.

Assume that
\omega\in\Sigma_{m},w\in F_{m}^{+}
,
a,b
are integers, and
a\leq b
. We write
\omega|_{[a,b]}=w
if
w=\omega_{a}\omega_{a+1}\ldots\omega_{b-1}\omega_{b}
.

Suppose that a free semigroup with
m
generators acts on
X
, denote the maps corresponding to the generators by
f_{0},\dotsc,f_{m-1}
, we assume that these maps are continuous. If
w\in F_{m}^{+}
,
w=w_{k}w_{k-1}\cdots w_{1}
where
w_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
for all
i=1,2,\cdots,k
, denote
f_{w}=f_{w_{k}}f_{w_{k-1}}\cdots f_{w_{1}}
. Obviously,
f_{ww^{\prime}}=f_{w}f_{w^{\prime}}
for any
w,w^{\prime}\in F_{m}^{+}
.

For every
w\in F_{m}^{+}
, define a metric
d_{w}
on
X
by

d_{w}(x_{1},x_{2})=\max_{w^{\prime}\leq w}d(f_{w^{\prime}}(x_{1}),f_{w^{\prime}}(x_{2})),\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

Obviously, if
w\leq w^{\prime}
, then
d_{w}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq d_{w^{\prime}}(x_{1},x_{2})
for all
x_{1},~x_{2}\in X
.

For every
w\in F_{m}^{+},~{w^{\prime}}\leq w,~\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
and we denote
\sum_{w^{\prime}\leq w}\varphi\left(f_{w^{\prime}}x\right)
by
\left(S_{w}\varphi\right)\left(x\right)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0,
a subset
E
of
X
is said to be a
(w,\varepsilon,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-separated set of
X
for any
x,y\in E
,
x\neq y
, implies
d_{w}(x,y)>\varepsilon
.

In
[
10
]
, Lin et al defined

\displaystyle Q_{w}^{s}\left(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1},\varphi,\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{\left(S_{w}\varphi\right)(x)}:E\text{ is }a\left(w,\varepsilon,f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1}\right)\text{-separated set of $X$}\right\},

Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{|w|=n}Q_{w}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon).

Let
\varepsilon>0,
a subset
F
of
X
is called a
(w,\varepsilon,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-spanning set of
X
if for every
x\in X
there exists
y\in F
such that
d_{w}(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
.

Let

\displaystyle Q_{w}\left(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1},\varphi,\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\sum_{x\in F}e^{\left(S_{w}\varphi\right)(x)}:F\text{ is }a\left(w,\varepsilon,f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1}\right)\text{-spanning set of $X$}\right\}

Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{|w|=n}Q_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon),

and defined the topological pressure of a free semigroup action by the formula

\displaystyle P(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon).

Remark 2.1
.

When
m=1
, it coincides with the topological pressure defined in
[
16
,
17
]
. Sometimes, in order to emphasize metric
d
, the topological pressure is always denoted as
P_{d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)
.

2.2.
Skew-product transformation

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and suppose
f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1}
are continuous mappings of
X
to itself.

To this action, we assign the following a skew-product transformation. Its base is
\Sigma_{m}
, its fiber is
X
, and the maps
F:\Sigma_{m}\times X\to\Sigma_{m}\times X
and
g:\Sigma_{m}\times X\to\mathbb{R}
are defined by the formula

F(\omega,x)=(\sigma\omega,f_{\omega_{0}}(x)),

g(\omega,x)=\varphi(x),

where
\omega=(\cdots,\omega_{-1},\overset{*}{\omega_{0}},\omega_{1},\cdots)
,
\sigma
is the Bernoulli shift and
\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
.
Here
f_{\omega_{0}}
stands for
f_{0}
if
\omega_{0}=0
, and for
f_{1}
if
\omega_{0}=1
, and so on. For
w=i_{1}\cdots i_{k}\in F_{m}^{+}
, denote
\overline{w}=i_{k}\cdots i_{1}
. Let
\omega=(\cdots,\omega_{-1},\overset{*}{\omega_{0}},\omega_{1},\cdots)\in\Sigma_{m}
, then

\displaystyle F^{n}(\omega,x)

\displaystyle=(\sigma^{n}\omega,f_{\omega_{n-1}}f_{\omega_{n-2}}\cdots f_{\omega_{0}}(x))

\displaystyle=(\sigma^{n}\omega,f_{\overline{\omega|_{[0,n-1]}}}(x)).

Let
P(F,g)
denote the topological pressure of
F
with respect to
g
for the dynamical system
(\Sigma_{m}\times X,F)
.

In
[
10
]
, Lin et al proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
10
]
, Theorem 1.1 )
The topological pressure of the skew product transformation
F
with respect to
g
, satisfies

P_{D}(F,g)=\log m+P_{d}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{m-1},\varphi),

where the metric
D
on
\Sigma_{m}\times X
is defined as

D((\omega,x),(\omega^{\prime},x^{\prime}))=\max\left\{d^{\prime}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}),d(x,x^{\prime})\right\}

and the metric
d^{\prime}
on
\Sigma_{m}
is introduced by setting
d^{\prime}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=1/2^{k}
, and
k=\inf\{|n|:\omega_{n}\neq\omega_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\}
.

3.
topological
R
-pressure of free semigroup actions

In this section, we introduce the definition of topological
r
-pressure of free semigroup actions and give some properties of the concept.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{m-1}
are continuous maps from
X
into itself. For
x\in X
,
w\in F_{m}^{+}
,
\varepsilon>0
and
0<r<1
, let

\displaystyle B(x,w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0}\ldots f_{m-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{y\in X:\frac{1}{|w|}Card\{w^{\prime}:d(f_{w^{\prime}}x,f_{w^{\prime}}y)<\varepsilon,and~w^{\prime}\leq w\}>1-r\right\}.

A subset
F
of
X
is called a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0}\ldots f_{m-1})
-spanning set of
X
, if for every
x\in X
, there exists
y\in F
such that
x\in B(y,w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0}\ldots f_{m-1}).

Let

\displaystyle Q_{w}\left(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\sum_{x\in F}e^{\left(S_{w}\varphi\right)(x)}:F\text{ is a}\left(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1}\right)\text{-spanning set of}~X\right\},

Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{|w|=n}Q_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

Remark 3.1
.

Let
r_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varepsilon,r)
denote the smallest cardinality of any

(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-spanning set of
X
. Then let

r_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varepsilon,r)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{|w|=n}r_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varepsilon,r).

\left(1\right)
If
\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}
then
Q_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{1},r)\geq Q_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{2},r)
. Hence,

Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{1},r)\geq Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{2},r).

\left(2\right)

0<Q_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r)\leq\parallel e^{S_{w}\varphi}\parallel r_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varepsilon,r)
. Hence,

0<Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r)\leq e^{n\parallel\varphi\parallel}r_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varepsilon,r).

\left(3\right)

Q_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},0,\varepsilon,r)=r_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varepsilon,r)
. Hence,

Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},0,\varepsilon,r)=r_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varepsilon,r).

Definition 3.1
.

For
0<r<1,

\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
, the topological
r
-pressure of free semigroup actions is defined by the formula

P_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

We sometimes write
P_{r,d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)
to emphasise the dependence on
d
.

Remark 3.2
.

It’s obvious that
P(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)\geq P_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).
If
m=1
, it coincides with the topological
r
-pressure defined in
[
6
]
and if
\varphi=0
, it coincides with the topological
r
-entropy of free semigroup actions defined in
[
19
]
.

Now we make a brief introduction to the separated set.

A subset
E
of
X
is called a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-separated set of
X
for any
x,y\in E
,
x\neq y
, implies

\frac{1}{|w|}Card\{w^{\prime}:d(f_{w^{\prime}}x,f_{w^{\prime}}y)\geq\varepsilon,and~w^{\prime}\leq w\}>r.

Put

\displaystyle Q_{w}^{s}\left(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{\left(S_{w}\varphi\right)(x)}:E\text{ is a}\left(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{\mathrm{m}-1}\right)\text{-separated set of $X$}\right\}

Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{|w|=n}Q_{w}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

Remark 3.3
.

If
\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}
then
Q_{w}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{1},r)\geq Q_{w}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{2},r)
. Hence,

Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{1},r)\geq Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon_{2},r).

Put

P^{s}_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

We sometimes write
P^{s}_{r,d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)
to emphasise the dependence on
d
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
n\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
and
0<r<1
, we have

\left(1\right)~Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r,)\leq Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

\left(2\right)
Let
M=\max\limits_{x\in X}{|\varphi(x)|}
, and if
\delta=\sup\{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|:d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon\}
, then

Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,2\varepsilon,2r)\leq e^{n\delta+2nrM}Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

Proof.

\left(1\right)
Let
E
be a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-separated set of
X
of maximum cardinality, then
E
is a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-spanning set of
X
.

Suppose
E
is not a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-spanning set, then there exists at least one point
x\in X
such that for all
y\in E
, we have

\frac{1}{|w|}Card\{w^{\prime}:d(f_{w^{\prime}}x,f_{w^{\prime}}y)<\varepsilon,and~w^{\prime}\leq w\}<1-r,

that is

\frac{1}{|w|}Card\{w^{\prime}:d(f_{w^{\prime}}x,f_{w^{\prime}}y)\geq\varepsilon,and~w^{\prime}\leq w\}>r,

which contradicts the separated set of maximum cardinality.

So we obtain that
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-separated set which cannot be enlarge to a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-separated set must be a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-spanning set of
X
. Therefore we have

Q_{w}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r)\leq Q_{w}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

Moreover,

Q_{n}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r)\leq Q_{n}^{s}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon,r).

\left(2\right)
To show the other lemma, suppose
E
is a
(w,2\varepsilon,2r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-separated set of
X
and

F
is a
(w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
-spanning set of
X
.
Define
\phi
:
E\rightarrow F
by choosing, for each
x\in E
, some point
\phi(x)\in F
with
x\in B(\phi(x),w,\varepsilon,r,f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})
.
Then
\phi(x)
is injective (details are given in
[
19
]
).
Let
z\in E
, such that

S_{w}\varphi(\phi(z))-S_{w}\varphi(z)=\min_{x\in E}\{S_{w}\varphi(\phi(x))-S_{w}\varphi(x)\}.

Let
A=\{w^{\prime}:d(f_{w^{\prime}}z,f_{w^{\prime}}\phi(z))<\varepsilon,w^{\prime}\leq w\}
, then
CardA>|w|(1-r)
.
Denote
CardA=|w|(1-r)\cdot k
,
1<k\leq\frac{1}{1-r}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in F}e^{S_{w}\varphi(y)}\geq\sum_{y\in\phi E}e^{S_{w}\varphi(y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in E}e^{S_{w}\varphi(\phi(x))-S_{w}\varphi(x)}e^{S_{w}\varphi(x)}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{x\in E}\{e^{{S_{w}\varphi(\phi(x))-S_{w}\varphi(x)}}\}\sum_{x\in E}e^{S_{w}\varphi(x)}

\displaystyle=e^{{S_{w}\varphi(\phi(z))-S_{w}\varphi(z)}}\sum_{x\in E}e^{S_{w}\varphi(z)}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-|w|(1-r)k\delta-|w|(1-(1-r)\cdot k)\cdot 2M}\sum_{x\in E}e^{S_{w}\varphi(x)}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-|w|\delta-|w|r\cdot 2M}\sum_{x\in E}e^{S_{w}\varphi(x)}.

Therefore,

\limsup_{r\to 0}P_{r,d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)\leq P_{d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)\leq\liminf_{r\to 0}P_{r,d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).

Hence

P_{d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=\lim_{r\to 0}P_{r,d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{m-1}
are continuous maps from
X
into itself. Then

\lim_{r\to 0}P^{s}_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=P(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).

Proof.

From Theorem
3.2
, we have

P^{s}_{2r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)-2Mr\leq P_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)\leq P^{s}_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi),

then

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}P^{s}_{2r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{r\to 0}P_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi),

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}P_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{r\to 0}P^{s}_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).

By Theorem
1.4
,

\lim_{r\to 0}P_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=P(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi),

then

\limsup_{r\to 0}P^{s}_{2r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)\leq P(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)\leq\liminf_{r\to 0}P^{s}_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).

This implies the existence of the limit, therefore

\lim_{r\to 0}P^{s}_{r}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=P(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).

∎

5.
The proof of Theorem
1.5

Before we prove Theorem
1.5
, we should make some preparations.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space, suppose a free semigroup with
m
generators acts on
X
and the generators
f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{m-1}
of
X
are homeomorphisms. It’s easy to see that the skew-product transformation

F:\Sigma_{m}\times X\to\Sigma_{m}\times X
is homeomorphism.

We can see that the skew-product inverse transformation
F^{-1}:\Sigma_{m}\times X\to\Sigma_{m}\times X
and
g:\Sigma_{m}\times X\to\mathbb{R}
are defined by the formula

F^{-1}(\omega,x)=(\sigma^{-1}\omega,f^{-1}_{\omega_{-1}}(x)),

g(\omega,x)=\varphi(x),

where
\varphi\in C\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
and
\omega=(\ldots,\omega_{-1},\overset{*}{\omega}_{0},\omega_{1},\cdots)\in\Sigma_{m}
. Here
f_{\omega_{-1}}
stands for
f_{0}
if
\omega_{-1}=0
, and for
f_{1}
if
\omega_{-1}=1
, and so on. Moreover

\displaystyle F^{-n}(\omega,x)

\displaystyle=(\sigma^{-n}\omega,f^{-1}_{\omega_{-n}}f^{-1}_{\omega_{-n+1}}\cdots f^{-1}_{\omega_{-1}}(x))

\displaystyle=(\sigma^{-n}\omega,f^{-1}_{\overline{\omega|_{[-n,-1]}}}(x)).

In order to prove Theorem
1.5
, we give the following property of
P(F^{-1},g).

Theorem 5.1
.

The topological pressure of the skew product inverse transformation
F^{-1}
satisfies

(5.1)

P_{D}(F^{-1},g)=\log m+P_{d}(f^{-1}_{0},\ldots,f^{-1}_{m-1},\varphi),

where the metric
D
on
\Sigma_{m}\times X
is defined as

D((\omega,x),(\omega^{\prime},x^{\prime}))=\max\{d^{\prime}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}),d(x,x^{\prime})\}

and the metric
d^{\prime}
on
\Sigma_{m}
is introduced by setting
d^{\prime}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=1/2^{k}
, and
k=\inf\{|n|:\omega_{n}\neq\omega_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\}
.

The proof of the theorem is similar to that of Lin et al
[
10
]
, so we omit the proofs.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then

(5.2)

Q_{n}^{s}(F^{-1},g,\varepsilon)\geq m^{n}Q_{n}^{s}(f^{-1}_{0},\ldots,f^{-1}_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon).

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
, then

(5.3)

Q_{n}(F^{-1},g,\varepsilon)\leq K(\varepsilon)m^{n}Q_{n}(f^{-1}_{0},\ldots,f^{-1}_{m-1},\varphi,\varepsilon),

where
K(\varepsilon)
is a positive constant that depends only on
\varepsilon
.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
.

From Lemma
5.2
, we have

P_{D}(F^{-1},g)\geq\log m+P_{d}(f^{-1}_{0},\ldots,f^{-1}_{m-1},\varphi).

From Lemma
5.3
, we have

P_{D}(F^{-1},g)\leq\log m+P_{d}(f^{-1}_{0},\ldots,f^{-1}_{m-1},\varphi),

and the proof is complete.
∎

Now we give the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Since
F
is homeomorphism, we have

P_{D}(F^{-1},g)=P_{D}(F,g).

From Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle P_{D}(F^{-1},g)

\displaystyle=\log m+P_{d}(f^{-1}_{0},\ldots,f^{-1}_{m-1},\varphi),

\displaystyle P_{D}(F,g)

\displaystyle=\log m+P_{d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi).

Therefore

P_{d}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\varphi)=P_{d}(f^{-1}_{0},\ldots,f^{-1}_{m-1},\varphi).

∎

Remark 5.1
.

In
[
11
]
, the authors gave an example to show that topological pressure in the sense of article
[
11
]
does not have the property similar to Theorem 1.5.

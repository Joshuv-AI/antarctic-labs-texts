Counting solutions to quadratic polynomials
V. Vinay Kumaraswamy

Abstract
. Using the circle method, we obtain asymptotic formulae for the number of integer solutions to certain quadratic polynomials that are uniform in the coefficients of the polynomial.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11D45, 11P55

1.
Introduction

Let
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a quadratic polynomial with integer coefficients. We write

F(\mathbf{x})=Q(\mathbf{x})+L(\mathbf{x})+N,

(1.1)

where
Q(\mathbf{x})
is an indefinite quadratic form of rank
r
with
5\leqslant r\leqslant n
,
L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a linear form and
N
is an integer. In this article we will give an asymptotic formula for the counting function

N(P,w)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}\\
F(\mathbf{x})=0\end{subarray}}w(P^{-1}\mathbf{x})

as
P\to\infty
, where
w(\mathbf{x})
is a smooth function with compact support. Importantly, this result is uniform in the size of the coefficients of
F
, and it
is a key ingredient in
[
8
]
to count rational points on cubic hypersurfaces. Our result is a generalisation of work of Browning and Dietmann
[
1
, Proposition 1]
to
the setting of general quadratic equations.

1.1.
Statements of the main results

Let
M=\left(M_{i,j}\right)\in\mat_{n\times n}(\mathbf{Q})
be a symmetric matrix such that
Q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\mathbf{x}^{t}M\mathbf{x}
. Since
M
is symmetric, there exists an orthogonal
transformation
R\in O_{n}(\mathbf{R})
such that
R^{t}MR=\diag(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r},0,\ldots,0)
, where
\lambda_{i}
are the non-
zero eigenvalues of
M
. Set
\|F\|=\max_{i,j}|M_{i,j}|.
Since the matrix entries
of
M
are bounded by
\|F\|
we have the estimate
|\lambda_{i}|\leqslant n\|F\|
.

Let
c>0
be a small real number. Let
w_{0}
be a non-negative smooth function with compact support in
[-2c,2c]
such that

w_{0}(x)=1
for
x\in[-c,c]
.

Let
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a non-singular solution to the equation
Q(\mathbf{x})=0
. Define

w_{2}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}w_{0}(x_{i}-\xi_{i}).

(1.2)

If
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}
are the non-zero eigenvalues of
Q(\mathbf{x})
,
let
\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{r})\in\mathbf{R}^{r}
be a non-zero real solution to

Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{|\lambda_{i}|}x_{i}^{2}=0.

Observe that such a solution exists because
Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x})
is indefinite.
Set

w_{1}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{n-h}w_{0}(x_{i}-\tau_{i}).

Also, let

w_{Q}(\mathbf{x})=w_{3}(R^{t}\mathbf{x}),

(1.3)

where

w_{3}(\mathbf{x})=w_{1}(|\lambda_{1}|^{1/2}x_{1},\ldots,|\lambda_{r}|^{1/2}x_{r},\|F\|^{1/2}x_{r+1},\ldots,\|F\|^{1/2}x_{n}).

Let

\sigma_{\infty}(Q_{\sgn},w_{1})\int_{\mathbf{R}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})e(-\theta Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x}))\,d\mathbf{x}\,d\theta

and

\sigma_{\infty}(Q,w_{2})=\int_{\mathbf{R}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{2}(\mathbf{x})e(-\theta Q(\mathbf{x}))\,d\mathbf{x}\,d\theta.

denote the singular integrals associated with
Q_{\sgn}
and
Q
. Let

\mathfrak{S}(F)=\prod_{p}\lim_{t\to\infty}p^{-t(n-1)}\#\left\{\mathbf{x}\bmod{p^{t}}:F(\mathbf{x})\equiv 0\bmod{p^{t}}\right\}

(1.4)

denote the singular series.

Define

\kappa=\kappa_{n}=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if $2\mid r,$}\\
1&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
F(\mathbf{x})
is as in (
1.1
) where
Q(\mathbf{x})
is an indefinite quadratic form of rank
r\geqslant 5
. Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}

be the non-zero eigenvalues of
Q
and let
\Delta=\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|
.
Assume that the equation
F(\mathbf{x})\equiv 0\bmod{p^{t}}
is soluble for all prime powers
p^{t}
.
Assume for any
A\geqslant 1
we have

\sup_{|\mathbf{x}|\leqslant A}|L(\mathbf{x})|\ll\|F\|^{2},

where the implied constant depends only on
A
.
Let
w_{Q}(\mathbf{x})
be as in (
1.3
). Let
\eta>0
. Suppose that
P\geqslant 1
is such that

\|F\|\ll\min\left\{P^{2/3-\eta},|\lambda_{i}|^{1/4}P^{1/2-\eta}\right\}\text{ and }|N|\ll P^{2-\eta}.

Define

\mathscr{E}(P)=\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2r}}\|F\|^{\frac{r}{2}}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}+\Delta^{-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\kappa}{2r}}\|F\|^{\frac{r}{2}-1}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}}.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

N(P,w_{Q})=\frac{\sigma_{\infty}(Q_{\sgn},w_{1})\mathfrak{S}(F)P^{n-2}}{\|F\|^{\frac{n-r}{2}}\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O_{\varepsilon,\eta}\left(\|F\|^{\varepsilon}P^{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}(P)\right).

Moreover, we have that
\sigma_{\infty}(Q_{\sgn},w_{1})\gg 1
and
\mathfrak{S}(F)\gg_{\varepsilon}\Delta^{-\frac{r}{r-4}-\varepsilon}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The estimate
\mathfrak{S}(F)\gg_{\varepsilon}\Delta^{-\frac{r}{r-4}-\varepsilon}
follows from a straightforward modification of
[
1
, Proposition 2]
.

Note that Theorem
1.1
implies, in particular, that a solution to the equation
F=0
exists, provided that

Q
is indefinite of rank at least
5
and that the equation
F\equiv 0\bmod{m}
is soluble for every
integer
m
, thereby establishing the Hasse principle for such quadratic polynomials. This recovers a classical result of Siegel
[
9
]
(see also work
of Watson
[
10
]
for
a more elementary proof).

We also draw the reader’s attention to important work of Dietmann
[
3
]
that established a very general form of Theorem
1.1
. Whereas
Dietmann requires only that
r\geqslant 3
and that
F
is everywhere locally soluble, our result
is stronger for those quadratic polynomials
F
for which the product of the non-zero
eigenvalues of
Q
is ‘large’ in absolute value. This is precisely what has been ustilised in
[
8
]
.

We prove Theorem
1.1
by using the smooth
\delta
-function form of the circle method due to Heath-Brown
[
5
, Theorem 3]
that
is based on earlier work of Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
4
]
. The weight function
w_{Q}
is modelled on the analogous weight function
constructed in
[
1
]
, which not only yields a good lower bound for the main term in
N(P,w_{Q})
, but it also minimises the size of the error term.

We end this introduction by remarking that by counting solutions
to the equation
F=0
weighted by
w_{2}
, we obtain the following generalisation of
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
, whose proof
we omit on account of its similarity to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
r\geqslant 5
and let
w
be as in (
1.2
). Assume that
|N|\ll\|F\|^{3}
and that for any
A\geqslant 1
we have

\sup_{|\mathbf{x}|\leqslant A}|L(\mathbf{x})|\ll\|F\|^{2},

where the implied constant depends only on
A
. Suppose that
\|F\|\leqslant P
. Define

\mathscr{E}(P)=\|F\|^{\frac{n}{2}+\frac{r}{4}-1-\frac{\kappa}{4}}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}}+\|F\|^{\frac{n}{2}+\frac{r}{4}-\frac{1}{4}}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

N(P,w_{2})=\sigma_{\infty}(F,w)\mathfrak{S}(F)P^{n-2}+O\left(\|F\|^{\varepsilon}P^{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}(P)\right).

It is interesting to note that while Theorem
1.1
is useful in obtaining lower bounds, one can obtain good upper bounds for the number of
integer solutions to quadratic polynomials using Theorem
1.3
, provided that
\|F\|
is not too large in terms of
P
. Removing this
constraint would have striking applications to the Dimension Growth Conjecture for cubic hypersurfaces, which remains unsolved.

2.
Counting solutions using the smooth
\delta
function

Using
[
5
, Theorem 3]
with
Q^{2}=P^{2}
to detect the equation
F(\mathbf{x})=0
, we get

N(P,w)=\frac{1}{P^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{-n}S_{q}(\mathbf{c})I_{q}(\mathbf{c})+O(1),

(2.1)

where

\begin{split}S_{q}(\mathbf{c})&=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{q}}\sum_{\mathbf{b}\bmod{q}}e_{q}(aF(\mathbf{b})+\mathbf{b}.\mathbf{c})\\
I_{q}(\mathbf{c})&=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{Q}(P^{-1}\mathbf{x})h\left(\frac{q}{P},\frac{F(\mathbf{x})}{P^{2}}\right)e_{q}(-\mathbf{c}.\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}.\end{split}

2.1.
Estimates for
I_{q}(\mathbf{c})

Let
r=q/P
and
\mathbf{v}=P\mathbf{c}/q
. We begin by writing

I_{q}(\mathbf{c})=P^{n}I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v}),

where

\displaystyle I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{Q}(\mathbf{x})h\left(r,Q(\mathbf{x})+\frac{L(\mathbf{x})}{P}+\frac{N}{P^{2}}\right)e(-\mathbf{x}.\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{x}

and
w_{Q}(\mathbf{x})
is given in (
1.3
). Let
G(\mathbf{x})=Q(\mathbf{x})+L(\mathbf{x})/P+N/P^{2}
. Observe that for
\mathbf{x}
lying in the support of
w_{Q}
, we have

L(\mathbf{x})\ll\|F\|^{3/2}+\|F\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{-1/2}.

Since
\|F\|^{3/2}+\|F\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{-1/2}\ll P
by the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
, we have that

|G(\mathbf{x})|\leqslant c_{w,F}

for some constant
c_{w,F}>0
, whenever
\mathbf{x}\in\supp w_{Q}
. Let
U:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
be a smooth function supported on

[-1-c_{w,F},1+c_{w,F}]
such that
U(x)=1
for
x\in[-c_{w,F},c_{w,F}]
. Then

\displaystyle I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v})=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{Q}(\mathbf{x})h\left(r,G(\mathbf{x})\right)U(G(\mathbf{x}))e(-\mathbf{x}.\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{x}.

(2.2)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}
be the non-zero eigenvalues of
Q
. Suppose that

\sup_{|\mathbf{x}|\leqslant A}|L(\mathbf{x})|\ll_{A}\|F\|^{2},

for any
A\geqslant 1
. If
\mathbf{c}\neq\mathbf{0}
then we have the following estimates for
I_{q}(\mathbf{c})
. We have for any
N\geqslant 0
that

I_{q}(\mathbf{c})\ll\Delta^{-1/2}\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\frac{P^{n+1}}{q}\frac{\|F\|^{\frac{N}{2}}}{|\mathbf{c}|^{N}}.

(2.3)

In addition, we have

I_{q}(\mathbf{c})\ll\Delta^{-1/2}\|F\|^{-n/2+3r/4-1/2}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}+1}q^{\frac{r}{2}-1}|\mathbf{c}|^{-\frac{r}{2}+1}.

(2.4)

Proof.

Let
D\in\mat_{n\times n}
denote the diagonal matrix

D=\diag(|\lambda_{1}|^{-1/2},\ldots,|\lambda_{r}|^{-1/2},\|F\|^{-1/2},\ldots,\|F\|^{-1/2}).

Making the change of variables
R^{t}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{z}
in (
2.2
), we get that

\begin{split}I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v})&=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{3}(\mathbf{z})h\left(r,G(R\mathbf{z})\right)e(-\mathbf{z}.R^{t}\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{z}.\\
\end{split}

Recalling the definition of
D
, we get that

\begin{split}I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v})&=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{-1/2}\times\\
&\quad\quad\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{z})h\left(r,G(DR\mathbf{z})\right)e(-\mathbf{z}.R^{t}D\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{z}.\end{split}

If
\mathbf{c}\neq\mathbf{0}
, then it follows from the estimate
|\lambda_{i}|\ll\|F\|
that
|R^{t}D\mathbf{v}|\gg\|F\|^{-1/2}|\mathbf{v}|
. Integration by parts and
[
5
, Lemma 5]
yield the estimate

\displaystyle I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\ll_{A}\Delta^{-1/2}\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}|\mathbf{v}|^{-A}r^{-1-A}

\displaystyle\ll\Delta^{-1/2}\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}r^{-1}\left(|\mathbf{c}|/\sqrt{\|F\|}\right)^{-A},

for any
A>0
. This completes the proof of (
2.3
)

Let
p(t)
denote the Fourier transform of the compactly supported smooth function
g(x)=h(r,x)U(x)
. Then we have

\displaystyle I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}\times

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbf{R}}p(t)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})e(tG(DR\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{x}.R^{t}D\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{x}\,dt

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}}p(t)J(t,\mathbf{v})\,dt,

where we have set

J(t,\mathbf{v})=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})e(tG(DR\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{x}.R^{t}D\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{x}.

Appealing to
[
5
, Lemma 10]
, we see that

J(t,\mathbf{v})\ll_{M}\|F\|^{\frac{M}{2}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-M}

(2.5)

if
|t|\ll|\mathbf{v}|/\|F\|^{1/2}.

In the remaining range we proceed as follows. Applying Parseval’s theorem as in
[
7
, Lemma 3.2]
we get that

J(t,\mathbf{v})\ll\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|\widehat{w_{1}}(\mathbf{x})|\,d\mathbf{x}\right)\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\int_{[-1,1]^{n}}e(tG(DR\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{x}.\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{x}.

It is easy to see that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|\widehat{w_{1}}(\mathbf{x})|\,d\mathbf{x}\ll 1
.
Observe that
Q(DR\mathbf{x})=Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x})
. As a result,

\displaystyle J(t,\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sup_{y\in\mathbf{R}}\int_{-1}^{1}e(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{|\lambda_{i}|}tx^{2}+xy)\,dx\,\times

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{i={r}+1}^{n}\sup_{y\in\mathbf{R}}\int_{-1}^{1}e(xy)\,dx.

Therefore, we obtain the bound

J(t,\mathbf{v})\ll\min\left\{1,|t|^{-\frac{r}{2}}\right\}.

(2.6)

By the preceding estimate and (
2.5
) we get for any integer
N>0
that

\displaystyle I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\ll\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}\left\{\|F\|^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{|t|\ll|\mathbf{v}|/\|F\|^{1/2}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-N}\,dt\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad+\int_{|t|\gg|\mathbf{v}|/\|F\|^{1/2}}|t|^{-\frac{r}{2}}\,dt\right\}.

The estimate (
2.4
) now follows by choosing
N
as follows. Set
N=r/2
if
r
is even. If
r
is odd, choose
N=\frac{r-1}{2}
if
|\mathbf{v}|\ll\sqrt{Y}
and
N=\frac{r+1}{2}
otherwise.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that

\sup_{|\mathbf{x}|\leqslant A}|L(\mathbf{x})|\ll_{A}\|F\|^{3/2}+\|F\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{-1/2}.

for any
A\geqslant 1
. Let
0<N<r/2-1
be an integer. Suppose also that

\|F\|^{3/2}+\|F\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{-1/2}\leqslant P^{1-\eta}\text{ and }\quad|N|\leqslant P^{2-\eta}

for some
\eta>0
. Then

I_{q}(\mathbf{0})=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}P^{n}\left(\sigma_{\infty}(Q_{\sgn},w_{1})+O(P^{-\eta/2})+O(q^{N}P^{-N})\right).

Moreover,

1\ll\sigma_{\infty}(Q_{\sgn},w_{1})\ll 1
.

Proof.

We begin by observing that the singular integral satisfies the bounds
1\ll\sigma_{\infty}(Q_{\sgn},w_{1})\ll 1

by construction of the weight function
w_{1}
. We will now show that the expression for
I_{q}(\mathbf{0})
holds.

We have

I_{q}(\mathbf{0})=P^{n}I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{0}),

with

\displaystyle I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{Q}(\mathbf{x})h\left(r,Q(\mathbf{x})+\frac{L(\mathbf{x})}{P}+\frac{N}{P^{2}}\right)\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{-1/2}\times

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})h\left(r,Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x})+\frac{L(DR\mathbf{x})}{P}+\frac{N}{P^{2}}\right)\,d\mathbf{x},

where we have used the definition of
w_{Q}
from (
1.3
) in the last line.

For
\theta\in\mathbf{R}
let

L(\theta)=\int_{\mathbf{R}}U(t)h(r,t)e(\theta t)\,dt.

Then by
[
2
, Equation 2.10]
, we have for any
N>0
that

L(\theta)=1+O_{N}((q/P)^{N})+O_{N}((q/P)^{N}|\theta|^{N}).

Also let

\displaystyle J(\theta,\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})e\left(-\theta\left(Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x})+\frac{L(DR\mathbf{x})}{P}+\frac{N}{P^{2}}\right)\right)\,d\mathbf{x}\,.

Appealing to
[
2
, Equation 2.9]
, we deduce that

\displaystyle I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{-1/2}\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbf{R}}\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\delta\theta)}{\pi\delta\theta}\right)^{2}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})L(\theta)\,d\theta.

As a result, for any
N<r/2-1
we have that

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbf{R}}\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\delta\theta)}{\pi\delta\theta}\right)^{2}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})\,d\theta\,+

\displaystyle\quad O\left((q/P)^{N}\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbf{R}}(1+|\theta|^{N})\min\left\{1,|\theta|^{-r/2}\right\}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbf{R}}\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\delta\theta)}{\pi\delta\theta}\right)^{2}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})\,d\theta\,+

\displaystyle\quad\quad O\left(\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}q^{N}P^{-N}\right).

Letting
\delta\to 0
we get that

\begin{split}I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{0})&=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})\,d\theta\,\\
&\quad+O\left(\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-1/2}q^{N}P^{-N}\right).\end{split}

(2.7)

By (
2.6
), we have that

\int_{\mathbf{R}}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})\,d\theta=\int_{|\theta|\leqslant P^{\eta}}J(\theta,0)\,d\theta+O(P^{-\eta(r/2-1)}).

As a result,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})\times

\displaystyle\quad\int_{|\theta|\leqslant P^{\eta}}e\left(-\theta\left(Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x})+\frac{L(DR\mathbf{x})}{P}+\frac{N}{P^{2}}\right)\right)\,d\theta\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O(p^{-\eta(r/2-1)}).

By the hypothesis of the lemma, we have that

L(DR\mathbf{x})/P+N/P^{2}\ll P^{-\eta},

for
any
\mathbf{x}\in\supp w_{1}
. As a result,

e(-\theta(Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x})+L(DR\mathbf{x})/P+N/P^{2}))=e(-\theta Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x}))(1+O(P^{-\eta})),

whence

\int_{|\theta|\leqslant P^{\eta}}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})\,d\theta=\int_{|\theta|\leqslant P^{\eta}/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})e(-\theta Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x}))\,d\theta\,d\mathbf{x}+O(P^{-\eta/2}).

Notice that the argument used to deduce (
2.6
) also shows that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})e(-\theta Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x}))\,\ll\min\left\{1,|\theta|^{-r/2}\right\}.

Therefore, we have

\int_{\mathbf{R}}J(\theta,\mathbf{0})\,d\theta=\int_{\mathbf{R}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}w_{1}(\mathbf{x})e(-\theta Q_{\sgn}(\mathbf{x}))\,d\theta\,d\mathbf{x}+O(P^{-\eta/2}).

As
I_{q}(\mathbf{0})=P^{n}I_{r}^{*}(\mathbf{0})
, by (
2.7
) we may conclude for any
0<N<r-1/2
that

I_{q}(\mathbf{0})=\|F\|^{-(n-r)/2}\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}P^{n}\left(\sigma_{\infty}(Q_{\sgn},w_{1})+P^{-\eta/2}+q^{N}P^{-N}\right).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.2.
Analysis of the exponential sum

In this section, we will analyse the exponential sum
S_{q}(\mathbf{c})
. We begin by recording some notation that will be used repeatedly in this
section.

Let
r
denote the rank of the quadratic form
Q(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}^{t}M\mathbf{x}
.
Completing squares, we find that there exists a matrix
S\in\sln(\mathbf{Q})
such that
S^{t}MS=\diag(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r},0,\ldots,0),
where
\beta_{i}
are non-zero integers. Let

Q^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}^{t}S^{-1}\diag(\beta_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\beta_{r}^{-1},0,\ldots,0)(S^{t})^{-1}\mathbf{x}

(2.8)

denote the quadratic form dual to
Q(\mathbf{x})
.
Put

\mathscr{D}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\beta_{i}
. Then
\mathscr{D}\ll\|F\|^{r}
.

Clearing denominators, we may suppose that
S
has integer entries (with a potentially different set of
\beta_{i})
. Then if
p\nmid 2\mathscr{D}
, we have for any
k\geqslant 1
that
S^{t}MS\equiv\diag(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r},0,\ldots,0)\bmod{p^{k}}
, with
(\beta_{i},p)=1
. Also recall
Q^{*}(\mathbf{x})
from (
2.8
).
We begin by recording the following multiplicativity result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
q=uv
with
(u,v)=1
. Then

S_{q}(\mathbf{c})=S_{u}(\overline{v}\mathbf{c})S_{v}(\overline{u}\mathbf{c}),

where
\overline{u}
(resp.
\overline{v}
) is the multiplicative inverse of
u
(resp.
v
) modulo
v
(resp.
u
).

Next we record the following estimate for
S_{q}(\mathbf{c})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
q\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\Delta^{-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\kappa}{2r}}\|F\|^{-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3r}{4}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<|\mathbf{c}|\ll C\\
(d_{r+1},\ldots,d_{n})=\mathbf{0}\\
Q^{*}(\mathbf{c})=0\end{subarray}}|\mathbf{c}|^{1-\frac{r}{2}}.

By
[
6

, the number of integer solutions
|\mathbf{c}|\ll C
to
Q^{*}(\mathbf{c})=0
is
O_{\varepsilon}(C^{n-2+\varepsilon})
, whence we get that

E_{1,2}(P)\ll_{\varepsilon}\Delta^{-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\kappa}{2r}}\|F\|^{\frac{r}{2}-1+\varepsilon}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}+\varepsilon}.

(2.13)

Finally, turning to
E_{2}(P)
, we get

\displaystyle E_{2}(P)

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|^{-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3r}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\ll P\\
x\text{ dyadic}\end{subarray}}x^{-n+\frac{r}{2}-1}\times

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<|\mathbf{c}|\ll C\\
d_{j}\neq 0\text{ for some}\\

Then by Lemma

we get that

\begin{split}E_{2}(P)&\ll_{\varepsilon}\|F\|^{\frac{r}{2}+\varepsilon}P^{n-\frac{r}{2}-1+\varepsilon}.\end{split}

Note that
E_{2}(P)\ll E_{1,1}(P)
as
\|F\|\leqslant P
. It is also clear that the error terms in (
2.11
) are smaller than
E_{1,1}(P)
and
E_{1,2}(P)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
when
(m_{r+1},\ldots,m_{n})=\mathbf{0}

For the error term

\displaystyle E(P)

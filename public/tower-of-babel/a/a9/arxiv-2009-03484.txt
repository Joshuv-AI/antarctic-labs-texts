Fiber cones of rational normal scrolls are Cohen–Macaulay Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 13C40, 13A30, 13P10; Secondary 14M12 Thanks: Keyword: Rational normal scroll, Rees algebra, Fiber cone, Regularity, Cohen–Macaulay, Singularity
Kuei-Nuan Lin and Yi-Huang Shen

Abstract
. In this short paper, we show that the fiber cones of rational normal scrolls are Cohen–Macaulay. As an application, we compute their Castelnuovo–Mumford regularities and 𝒂 \bm{a} -invariants, as well as the reduction number of the defining ideals of the rational normal scrolls. We also characterize the Gorensteinness of the fiber cone.



1.
Introduction

In this short note, we show that the fiber cones of rational normal scrolls are Cohen–Macaulay. Let
R
be a standard graded polynomial ring over some field
{\mathbb{K}}
and
I
be an ideal of
R
minimally generated by some forms
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}
of the same degree. Those forms define a rational map
\phi
whose image is a variety
X
. The bi-homogeneous coordinate ring of the graph of
\phi
is the Rees algebra of the ideal
I
, and the homogeneous coordinate ring of the image is the fiber cone (special fiber ring) of this ideal. Here, we are mostly interested in these two types of blow-up algebras when the ideal
I
describes the rational normal scroll.

Rational normal scrolls are typical determinantal varieties, central in the study of algebraic varieties. The study of determinantal varieties has attracted earnest attentions of algebraic geometers and commutative algebraists, partly due to the beautiful structures involved and the interesting applications to the applied mathematics and statistics; see, for instance,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
26
]
, to name but a few.

The study of Cohen–Macaulay property of the Rees algebra and the related fiber cone is an active research area. Most of the work is focusing on giving certain assumptions on the ground ring and the ideal;
see for example,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
, and
[
28
]
. However, not much is known about the Cohen–Macaulayness of these blow-up algebras of the determinantal varieties. The first well-known case is probably the maximal minors of a generic matrix where the fiber cone is the Grassmannian variety. Hochster
[
19
]
in 1973 showed that the Grassmannian is Cohen–Macaulay. In 1983, Eisenbud and Huneke
[
12
]
showed that the posets defining the Rees algebra and fiber cone are wonderful, and obtained the Cohen–Macaulayness of those algebras. Recently, the first author of this paper and her coauthors proved the Cohen–Macaulayness of Rees algebras and its fibers cones of closed determinantal facet ideals in
[
1
]
and sparse determinantal ideals in
[
6
]
.

All the work mentioned above came from generic matrices and their specializations. Blow-up algebras of rational normal scrolls were first studied by Conca, Herzog and Valla
[
7
]
in 1996. And they proved that the Rees algebra and the fiber cone of a balanced rational normal scroll are Cohen–Macaulay normal domains. The difficulty of determining the Cohen–Macaulayness of those blow-up algebras is partly due to the lack of necessary information regarding the defining ideals of those algebras. Fortunately, Sammartano solved this implicitization problem in full generality for the rational normal scrolls
in
[
25
]
recently. And this opened the door of understanding those algebras for us.

We need to be more precise now. In the following, let
n_{1},\dots,n_{d}
be a sequence of positive integers and
c=\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}
. For each
i=1,\dots,d
, let
\mathcal{C}_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{c+d-1}
be a rational normal curve of degree
n_{i}
with complementary linear spans and let
\varphi_{i}:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the corresponding isomorphism. Whence, the related
rational normal scroll
is simply

\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\overline{\{\varphi_{1}(p),\varphi_{2}(p),\dots,\varphi_{d}(p)\}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{c+d-1}.

It is well-known (
[
17
]
) that
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
is uniquely determined by the sequence
n_{1},\dots,n_{d}
up to projective equivalence. And in suitable coordinates, the ideal
I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
of
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
is generated by the maximal minors of the matrix

(1)

\mathbf{X}\coloneqq\scalebox{0.9}{$\begin{pNiceArray}.$}

In the following, we will write
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{i,j}\mid 0\leq j\leq n_{i}-1]
for the ground ring, and
I=I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}=I_{2}(\mathbf{X})
for this ideal. Recall that the
Rees algebra
of
I
is
\mathcal{R}(I):=\oplus_{i\geq 0}I^{i}t^{t}\subseteq R[t]
, and the related
fiber cone
will be
\mathcal{F}(I)=\mathcal{R}(I)\otimes_{R}\mathbb{K}\cong\mathbb{K}[I]\subseteq R
, where
t
is a new variable. As mentioned above, Sammartano gave the implicit equations of the defining ideals of these blow-up algebras in
[
25
]
. Meanwhile, in the
balanced case
, i.e., when
|n_{i}-n_{j}|\leq 1
for all
i,j
, Conca, Herzog, and Valla showed in
[
7
]
that both
\mathcal{R}(I)
and
\mathcal{F}(I)
are Cohen–Macaulay normal domains.

In this paper, we will
drop
the balanced assumption. We can still obtain the Cohen–Macaulayness of the fiber cone

\mathcal{F}(I)
in
2.1
. By this, we generalize the corresponding result of Conca, Herzog, and Valla. As a quick application, we compute the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity and
\bm{a}
-invariant of
\mathcal{F}(I)
in
3.4
. And the reduction number of the ideal
I
is provided as well. As the second application, we characterize the Gorensteinness of the fiber cone.

2.
Cohen–Macaulayness of the fiber cones of rational normal scrolls

This section is devoted to prove
that the fiber cone associated with the rational normal
scroll is Cohen–Macaulay. To achieve that, we associate the initial ideal of the defining
ideal of the fiber cone
with its Stanley–Reisner complex
\Delta
.
We will show that the ideal of the Alexander
dual of this complex has linear quotients. Consequently, the original simplicial complex is shellable and Cohen–Macaulay.
Hence, the fiber cone is Cohen–Macaulay as well.

To establish the expected linear-quotients property, we have to resort to the binary-tree description of the facets of the simplicial complex
\Delta
in
[
25
]
. To be more accurate, instead of just using the lexicographic order on the minimal monomial generators of the Alexander dual ideal, we need to group these generators first using the leaves of the binary trees; see
2.4
.
The subsequent proof is also quite involved, since we have to analyze the binary trees in more detail and pin down those linear generators of the successive colon ideals.

Recall that a rational normal scroll is uniquely determined by a sequence of positive integers
{\bm{n}}=(n_{1},...,n_{d})
with
n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{d}
by
[
17
]
.
Using the terminology stated in the above introduction, we are ready to present the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
be the defining ideal of the rational normal scroll
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{c+d-1}
for
c=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
. Then the fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})
is Cohen–Macaulay.

We need some preparations before laying out its proof.
Let us start by recalling the indispensable constructions in
[
25
]
for the fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I)
where
I=I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
. First of all, a matrix
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
was introduced in
[
25
, Section 2]
as follows. One starts with the first column of the
i
-th catalecticant block
\mathbf{X}_{i}
of the original matrix
\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{X}_{1},\mathbf{X}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{d})
for each
i
increasingly in
i
, then the second column, and so on until one has used all columns except the last one for each block; when a block runs out of columns one simply skips it. The first
c-d
columns of
\mathbf{M}
form the submatrix

\begin{pmatrix}x_{i(2),0}&x_{i(2)+1,0}&\cdots&x_{d,0}&x_{i(3),1}&x_{i(3)+1,1}&\cdots&x_{d,1}&x_{i(4),2}&\cdots&\cdots&x_{d,n_{d}-2}\\
x_{i(2),1}&x_{i(2)+1,1}&\cdots&x_{d,0}&x_{i(3),2}&x_{i(3)+1,2}&\cdots&x_{d,1}&x_{i(4),3}&\cdots&\cdots&x_{d,n_{d}-1}\end{pmatrix},

where the
i(l)
denotes the least integer

such that
n_{i}\geq l
.
The last
d
columns of
\mathbf{M}
are of the last column of the
i
-th block of
\mathbf{X}
for each

, but ordered decreasingly in

\begin{pmatrix}x_{d,n_{d}-1}&x_{d-1,n_{d-1}-1}&\cdots&x_{2,n_{2}-1}&x_{1,n_{1}-1}\\
x_{d,n_{d}}&x_{d-1,n_{d}-1}&\cdots&x_{2,n_{2}}&x_{1,n_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.

Interested readers are invited to go through the examples in

\operatorname{in}(P)

Proof of
2.1
.

When

, the fiber cone is the coordinate ring of the Grassmann variety
\mathbb{G}(1,c-1)\subset P^{\binom{c}{2}-1}
; see

Now, it remains to consider the case when

. Notice that
\mathcal{F}(I)
being Cohen–Macaulay is equivalent to saying that the defining ideal

has this property. By

, or more strongly, by

\operatorname{in}(P)

In the following, we shall assume that

Before we really start proving the linear quotients property, we still need some preparations. Under the natural identification
T_{\alpha,\beta}\leftrightarrow(\alpha,\beta)

V=\Set{(\alpha,\beta)}{1\leq\alpha<\beta\leq c}.

Following

\Delta

with integral endpoints. And we will use the familiar notions of length, intersection, and containment of intervals.

As usual, if

\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Then, with respect to the above matrix
\mathbf{M}
, for each
\alpha\in[c-d-2]
and each

\gamma_{\alpha,i}
be the least index

\mathbf{M}
involves variables from the block

. Then, there exists some

(2)

\Set{\gamma_{\alpha,1},\dots,\gamma_{\alpha,d}}=\Set{\alpha+2,\dots,\alpha+\ell_{\alpha}}\cup\Set{c-d+\ell_{\alpha},\dots,c};

Lemma 2.2

(
[

Suppose that

(3)

\Set{(\beta,\beta+1)}{\beta\in\{\alpha,\ldots,\alpha+\ell_{\alpha}\}\cup\{c-d+\ell_{\alpha}-1,\ldots,c-1\}};

Proposition 2.6
.

When
c\geq d+4
, the ideal
(\operatorname{in}(P))^{\vee}
has linear quotients with respect to the given ordering
\succ

Proof.

Let
F
be a facet of
\Delta
and suppose that
F\in\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
. We want to show that the minimal monomial generating set
G_{F}
\coloneqq G(I_{F})
of the colon ideal

I_{F}\coloneqq\braket{\widehat{{\bm{T}}^{F^{\prime}}}\mid F^{\prime}\succ F}:\widehat{{\bm{T}}^{F}}

is linear. Notice that

\braket{\widehat{{\bm{T}}^{F^{\prime}}}}:\widehat{{\bm{T}}^{F}}={\bm{T}}^{F\setminus F^{\prime}}\coloneqq\prod_{v\in F\setminus F^{\prime}}T_{v}.

Suppose that
F
is the first facet within
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
with respect to the designated linear order
\succ
. If
\alpha=c-d-2
, then
F
is actually the first facet of
\Delta
and there is nothing to show in this case. Otherwise,
\alpha<c-d-2
. Let
F^{\prime}
be the first facet of
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}
. It is clear that
F^{\prime}\succ F
and

F\setminus F^{\prime}=\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}\setminus\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha+1}=\{({\alpha},{\alpha}+1)\}

by
2.3
and
2.5
. Notice that for any facet
F^{\prime\prime}
preceding
F
,
({\alpha},{\alpha}+1)\in F\setminus F^{\prime\prime}
. Whence, the colon ideal
I_{F}
is principal and linear.

Suppose instead that
F
is not the first facet in
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
. This situation is more involved. We will break the long proof into three parts, according to the following plans.

(1)

Firstly, we describe the expected linear generating set
G_{F}
.

(2)

Next, we show that the variables described above really show up as some colons of monomials, and they are the only variables possessing this property.

(3)

Finally, we show that we don’t have minimal monomial generators in
G_{F}
of higher degrees.

After showing the above three parts, it is clear then that the ideal
(\operatorname{in}(P))^{\vee}
has linear quotients. At this point, one may suggest only proving the last step. But to successfully give the proof, we have to clear higher-degree generators by the linear ones.

2.1.
Linear generators of the colon ideal
I_{F}

We separate the vertices in
F
into collections for each fixed facet
F\in\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
. For

, let

(4)

F_{b}\coloneqq\Set{v\in F\mid v=(b,*)}.

If the cardinality

.
It is clear that

when

.
Now, assume that

(1)

If

.

(2)

(ii)

be a graded Cohen–Macaulay module over the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
and
P_{M}(t)
the numerator Laurent polynomial of the Hilbert series of
M
. Then
\operatorname{reg}(M)=\operatorname{deg}(P_{M}(t))
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
10
, Proposition 6.6]
or
[
14
, Proposition 1.2]
)
.

Let
I\subset R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
be a homogeneous ideal that is generated in one degree, say
d
. Assume that the fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I)
is Cohen–Macaulay. Then each minimal reduction of
I
is generated by
\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{F}(I))
homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
, and
I
has the reduction number
\operatorname{r}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{F}(I))
.

Recall that the rational normal scroll
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
is
balanced
precisely when
|n_{i}-n_{j}|\leq 1
for all
i,j
. Whence, we can rearrange the columns of the matrix
\mathbf{X}
in the equation (
1
) and rewrite it as the
Hankel matrix

{\bm{H}}_{2,c,d}
for
c=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
. And here is the last piece that we need for the regularity result.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d})
be the defining ideal of the rational normal scroll
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{c+d-1}
in the balanced case. Then, the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of the fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d}))
is

\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{F}(I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d})))=\begin{cases}\left\lceil(c+d-1)/2\right\rceil,&\text{if $4+d\leq c$},\\
c-3,&\text{if $2<c\leq 4+d$}.\end{cases}

It is time to describe the first result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
be the defining ideal of the rational normal scroll
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{c+d-1}
for
c=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
.

(1)

The Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of the fiber cone is given by

\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}))=\begin{cases}\left\lceil(c+d-1)/2\right\rceil,&\text{if $4+d\leq c$},\\
c-3,&\text{if $2<c<4+d$}.\end{cases}

(2)

The reduction number of
I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
is given by

\operatorname{r}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})=\begin{cases}\left\lceil(c+d-1)/2\right\rceil,&\text{if $4+d\leq c$},\\
c-3,&\text{if $2<c<4+d$}.\end{cases}

(3)

The
{\bm{a}}
-invariant of the fiber cone is given by

{\bm{a}}(\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}))=\begin{cases}\left\lceil(c+d-1)/2\right\rceil-c-d,&\text{if $4+d\leq c$},\\
-c,&\text{if $2<c<4+d$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Notice that the fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})
is Cohen–Macaulay by
2.1
. Thus, the item
2
follows directly from the item
1
and
3.2
. And similarly, the item
3
follows from the item
1
, Equation (
9
) and the fact

\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}))=\begin{cases}c+d,&\text{if $4+d\leq c$,}\\
2c-3,&\text{if $2<c<4+d$,}\end{cases}

by
[
25
, Proposition 3.1, Corollary 3.10]
.

As for the item
1
, just as observed in the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 3.13]
, the Hilbert function of the fiber cone of the rational normal scroll
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
depends only on
c
and
d
by
[
2
, Theorem 3.7]
.
Whence, the fiber cones of the ideal
I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
above and the ideal
I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d})
in
[
21
, Section 3]
have the same Laurent polynomial. Now, by the Cohen–Macaulayness of
\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})
established in
2.1
, these fiber cones have the same Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity by
3.1
.

Now, it remains to apply
3.3
.
∎

As the second application to our main result
2.1
, we characterize the Gorensteinness of the fiber cone. The corresponding numerical condition in the balanced case is the following.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
21
, Proposition 2.10]
)
.

Let
I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d})
be the defining ideal of the rational normal scroll
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{c+d-1}
in the balanced case. Then, the fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d}))
is Gorenstein if and only if
c\in\Set{2,3,2+d,3+d,4+d}
.

And here is the last result that this paper wants to describe.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
be the defining ideal of the rational normal scroll
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{c+d-1}
for
c=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
. Then, the fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})
is Gorenstein if and only if
c\in\Set{2,3,2+d,3+d,4+d}
.

Proof.

The fiber cone
\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})
is Cohen–Macaulay by
2.1
. And since it is isomorphic to the toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}]
, it is an integral domain.
As mentioned in the proof of
3.4
, the fiber cones
\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})
and
\mathcal{F}(I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d}))
have identical Hilbert series. Thus, by both (b) and (c) of
[
4
, Corollary 4.4.6]
,
\mathcal{F}(I_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})
is Gorenstein, if and only
\mathcal{F}(I_{2}({\bm{H}}_{2,c,d}))
is so. Now, it suffices to apply
3.5
.
∎

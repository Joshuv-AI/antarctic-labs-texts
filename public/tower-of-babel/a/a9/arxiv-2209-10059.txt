Comparing direct limit and inverse limit of even K K -groups in multiple ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions
Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
Iwasawa first established a duality relating the direct limit and the inverse limit of class groups in a ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension, and this result has recently been extended to multiple ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions by many authors. In this paper, we establish an analogous duality for the direct limit and the inverse limit of higher even K K -groups in a ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -extension. Keywords and Phrases : Even K K -groups, ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -extension. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020 : 11R23, 11R70, 11S25.

Introduction

Let
F
be a number field and let
K
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
. For every finite extension
L
of
F
contained in
K
, we let
A_{L}
denote the
p
-primary part of the ideal class group of
L
. The ring of integer of
L
is then denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Suppose that
L^{\prime}
is an extension of
L
contained in
K
. Then there is a natural map
A_{L}\longrightarrow A_{L^{\prime}}
induced by the natural inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{L}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, we have a map
A_{L^{\prime}}\longrightarrow A_{L}

going the other way which is induced by the (ideal) norm. Then one has

\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}A_{L}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}A_{L},

where the direct limit (resp., the inverse limit) is taken with respect to the inclusion maps (resp., the norm maps). These two limit modules come naturally equipped with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]
-module structures, where
\Gamma=\Gal(K/F)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]
-module
M
, we write
M^{\iota}
for the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]
-module which is the same underlying
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
M
but whose
\Gamma
-action is given by

\gamma\cdot_{\iota}x=\gamma^{-1}x,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma\in\Gamma,x\in M.

A classical theorem of Iwasawa
[
5
]
then asserts that there is a pseudo-isomorphism

\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}A_{L}\right)^{\iota}\sim\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}A_{L}\right)^{\vee}

of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]
-modules. Here
(-)^{\vee}
is the Pontryagin dual. This result of Iwasawa has been generalized to the context of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extension (see the works of Nekovář
[
12
]
, Vauclair
[
23
]
and, more recently, that of Lai and Tan
[
7
]
).

In this paper, we will consider the situation of the higher even
K
-groups. As before, let
p
be a prime, and let
F
be a number field. In the event that
p=2
, we shall assume further that the number field
F
has no real primes. Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extension of
F
. Fix an integer
i\geq 2
. For each finite intermediate extension
L
of
F_{\infty}/F
, the works of Quillen
[
22
]
and Borel
[
3
]
tell us that the higher even
K
-group
K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
is finite. As is standard in Iwasawa theory, we are interested in the Sylow
p
-subgroup
K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]
of
K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
. Now, for two finite subextensions
L\subseteq L^{\prime}
, the inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\prime}
induces a map
\jmath_{L/L^{\prime}}:K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]
by functoriality. In the other direction, there is the norm map (also called the transfer map)
\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{\prime}/L}:K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]
. Similar to the class groups situation, we consider the following direct limit and inverse limit

\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}],

whose transition maps are given by the maps
\jmath_{L/L^{\prime}}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{\prime}/L}
respectively. Again, these limit modules come equipped with natural
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module structures, where
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module
M
, the module
M^{\iota}
is defined similarly as before. The main result of this paper is then as follows.

Theorem

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

Retain the notation as above. Then there is a pseudo-isomorphism

\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\right)^{\iota}\sim\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\right)^{\vee}

of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-modules.

Since
K_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]=A_{L}
, our result may therefore be interpreted as a generalization of the previous results of Iwasawa
et al
to the higher even
K
-groups. For the remainder of the introductional section, we shall sketch the ideas of our proof, leaving the details to the body of the paper.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module. For two open subgroups
V\subseteq U
of
G
, the norm map
N_{U/V}
on
M_{V}
factors through
M_{U}=(M_{V})_{U/V}
to yield a map
M_{U}\longrightarrow M_{V}
, which by abuse of notation is also denoted by
N_{U/V}
. Then
\{M_{U}\}
forms a direct system.
We then say that the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module
M
is
systematically coinvariant-finite
if
M_{U}
is finite for every open subgroup
U
of
G
. For such a systematically coinvariant-finite module
M
, the direct limit
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}
is naturally a discrete
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module. The following algebraic result, which will be proved in Section
2
, is an important ingredient for the proof of our main theorem.

Theorem

(Theorem
2.3
)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module which is systematically coinvariant-finite. Then we have an isomorphism

\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]])\cong\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}\Big)^{\vee}

(1.1)

of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-modules.

Since a systematically coinvariant-finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module is automatically torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
(see Lemma
2.2
below), the left hand of the isomorphism (
1.1
) is pseudo-isomorphic to
M^{\iota}
(for instance, see
[
15
, Proposition 8]
). In view of this, to prove our Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to take
M=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]
, and show that this module satisfies the following properties:

(I)

For every open subgroup
U
of
G
, we have an isomorphism

t_{U}:\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\right)_{U}\cong K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L_{U}})[p^{\infty}],

where
L_{U}
is the fixed field of
U
. In particular, the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module
\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]
is systematically coinvariant-finite.

(II)

For open subgroups
V\subseteq U
of
G
, the following diagram

\textstyle{\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\right)_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{N_{U/V}}
\textstyle{K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L_{U}})[p^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\jmath_{L_{U}/L_{V}}}
\textstyle{\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\right)_{V}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L_{V}})[p^{\infty}]}

(1.2)

commutes.

The verification of the above two properties will be dealt with in Section
3
(in particular, see Proposition
3.2
).
As a corollary, we have the following result which is reminiscent to that for the class groups (see
[
2
, Proposition 2]
,
[
7
, Corollary 1.2.1]
and
[
8
, Théorème 4.1 and Théorème 4.4]
).

Corollary

(Corollary
3.3
)
.

Retain the above notation. Then
\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]
is pseudo-null over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
if and only if
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]=0
.

2
Systematically coinvariant-finite modules

Throughout this section,
p
will always denote a fixed prime. Let
d\geq 1
. We shall denote by
G
the group isomorphic to the
d
-copies of the additive group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The completed group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{U}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U],

where
U
runs through all open subgroups of
G
and the transition maps are given by the natural projection
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
for
V\subseteq U
. It is well-known that the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
can be identified with the power series ring in
d
variables over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. In particular, it is a local ring.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module. For an open subgroup
U
of
G
, we write
M_{U}
for the largest quotient
of
M
on which
U
acts trivially. The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module
M
is then said to be
systematically coinvariant-finite
if
M_{U}
is finite for every open subgroup
U
of
G
. As seen in the introduction,
\{M_{U}\}_{U}
forms a direct system of finite modules with transition maps given by

N_{U/V}:M_{U}\longrightarrow M_{V}

for
V\subseteq U
. In particular,
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}
is a discrete
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module.

Lemma 2.2
.

A systematically coinvariant-finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module
M
is finitely generated torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the (unique) maximal ideal of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
. Then it contains the augmentation ideal
I_{G}
(for instances, see
[
13
, Proposition 5.2.16]
). Thus,
M/\mathfrak{m}M
is a quotient of
M_{G}
and hence finite. By the Nakayama lemma, this in turn implies that
M
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
. Finally, since
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
, it is in particular a solvable
p
-adic Lie group. Therefore, we may apply the main result of
[
1
]
to conclude that
M
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
.
∎

We can now state the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
M
be a systematically coinvariant-finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module. Then we have an isomorphism

\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]])\cong\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}\Big)^{\vee}.

Before giving the proof, we make two remarks.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that the conclusion of theorem is false if we remove the “systematically coinvariant-finite” hypothesis. We give a counterexample to illustrate this. Suppose that
d\geq 2
. Let
M=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the module with trivial
G
-action. Then one has
\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]])=0
but
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Remark 2.5
.

It is instructive to specialize our theorem to the context of
G=\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. In this context,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]
identifies with the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
in one variable. Denote by
w_{n}:=w_{n}(T)
the polynomial
(1+T)^{p^{n}}-1
. For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
-module, one has a natural identification
M/w_{n}M\cong M_{\Gamma_{n}}
, where
\Gamma_{n}
is the subgroup of
\Gamma
of index
p^{n}
. If
M
is systematically coinvariant-finite, then the prime ideals dividing
w_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, are disjoint to the set of prime ideals of height one in the support of
M
. Our Theorem
2.3
therefore coincides with the isomorphism

\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]])\cong\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{n}M/w_{n}\Big)^{\vee}

obtained via the theory of Iwasawa adjoint as in
[
13
, Proposition 5.5.6]
.

We split the proof of Theorem
2.3
into a few lemmas. The first of which is the following general observation.

Lemma 2.6
.

For every finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module
M
, there is an isomorphism

\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]])\cong\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{U}\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]),

where the inverse limit is taken with respect to the canonical projection maps.

Proof.

The isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]])\cong\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{U}\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]).

plainly holds when
M
is free of finite rank. But since the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
is Noetherian, every finitely generated module
M
has a resolution consisting of finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-modules. Applying the above isomorphism to this free resolution and taking homology, we obtain the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Before continuing, we make another remark.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
U
be an open (normal) subgroup of
G
and let
M
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module
M
. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module homomorphism
M\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
naturally factors through
M_{U}
to yield a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module homomorphism
M_{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
. Furthermore, one sees easily that this latter map can be viewed as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
-module homomorphism. Conversely, given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
-module homomorphism
M_{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
, by considering the composition
M\longrightarrow M_{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
, we obtain a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module homomorphism. In conclusion, we have identifications

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]).

These identifications will be frequently utilized in the subsequent discussion of this paper without any further mention.

For the next two lemmas, we shall require the module
M
to be systematically coinvariant-finite. In particular, the next lemma also makes use of the property of
G
being commutative.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that
M
is a systematically coinvariant-finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-module. Then we have an isomorphism

\theta_{U}:\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])\cong\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]).

Furthermore, if
V
is another open subgroup of
G
which is contained in
U
, then we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]}(M_{V},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])}

where the vertical maps are induced by the projection
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
.

Proof.

Since
M
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
, we can find a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]^{r}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

of finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
-modules. Taking
U

0\longrightarrow H_{1}(U,M)\longrightarrow N_{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]^{r}\longrightarrow M_{U}\longrightarrow 0

of

-modules. Let

denote the kernel of the map
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]^{r}\longrightarrow M_{U}
. Consider the following diagram

\textstyle{\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]^{r},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(C,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{U}}
\textstyle{\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta_{U}}
\textstyle{0}

(2.2)

with exact rows. Here the middle vertical map
\alpha_{U}
is given by

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(C,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(N_{U},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]}(N,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]).

It is straightforward to check that the leftmost square is commutative and this in turn induces the rightmost vertical map which is our
\theta_{U}
. Furthermore, the map
\alpha_{U}
is injective with cokernel contained in

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}\big(H_{1}(U,M),\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]\big).

Since
U
is an open subgroup of

, it is also isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}

\theta_{U}
is natural in

. The proof of the lemma is therefore completed. ∎

\scriptstyle{\eta_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{(M_{U})^{\vee}}

(2.4)

where the left vertical map is induced by the projection
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
and the right vertical map is induced by the norm map
N_{U/V}:M_{U}\longrightarrow M_{V}
.
To simplify notation, we shall write
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Now, for each
f\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])
and
x\in M_{U}
, we write

f(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in G/U}f_{\sigma}(x)\sigma,

where
f_{\sigma}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We then define the map

\eta_{U}:\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])\longrightarrow(M_{U})^{\vee}

by sending
f\mapsto f_{1}
, where “
1
” denotes the identity of the group
G/U
. Let
\tau\in G/U
. Since
f
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
-homomorphism, we have the identity
\tau f(x)=f(\tau x)
which in turn yields

f_{\sigma}(\tau x)=f_{\tau^{-1}\sigma}(x)

for every
\sigma\in G/U
. In particular, we have

f_{1}(\tau x)=f_{\tau^{-1}}(x)

for every
\tau\in G/U
. Hence
f
is uniquely determined by
f_{1}
, and therefore,
\eta_{U}
is an isomorphism. It remains to show that
\eta_{U}
is natural in
U
. Let
V
be an open subgroup of
G
which is contained in
U
. The projection
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]
induces a map

\rho_{VU}:\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]}(M_{V},\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V])\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]}(M_{V},\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U])=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]}(M_{U},\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]).

Let
x\in M_{U}
and
h\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]}(M_{V},\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/V]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. By abuse of notation, we write
x\in M_{V}
for a preimage of

under natural surjection
M_{V}\longrightarrow(M_{V})_{U/V}=M_{U}
. Let
\sigma_{1}=1,\sigma_{2},...,\sigma_{r}\in G/V
be a complete set of coset representatives of
U/V
in
G/V
. Then one has

h(x)=\sum_{\tau\in U/V}\sum_{i=1}^{r}h_{\tau\sigma_{i}}(x)\tau\sigma_{i}.

From which, we have

\rho_{VU}(h)(x)=\rho_{VU}(h(x))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Big(\sum_{\tau\in U/V}h_{\tau\sigma_{i}}(x)\Big)\sigma_{i},

which in turn implies that

\rho_{VU}(h)_{1}(x)=\sum_{\tau\in U/V}h_{\tau}(x)=\sum_{\tau\in U/V}h_{1}(\tau^{-1}x)=h_{1}\left(\sum_{\tau\in U/V}\tau^{-1}x\right)=h_{1}\big(N_{U/V}(x)\big).

This establishes the commutativity of the diagram (

of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
. Now, by the Poitou-Tate sequence, we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Gal(L^{\prime}_{\infty}/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{2}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))\longrightarrow\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}\bigoplus_{w_{L}\in S(L)}H^{2}(L_{w_{L}},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)),

where
S(L)
is the set of primes of
L
above
p
.
Since the decomposition group of
F_{\infty}/F
at the prime of
F
above
p
has dimension
\geq 2
(cf.
[
8
, Théorème 3.2]
), we may apply a similar argument to that in
[
9
, Lemma 5.3]
to conclude that
\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}\bigoplus_{w_{L}\in S(L)}H^{2}(L_{w_{L}},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))
is pseudo-null over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
, and whence,
H_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{2}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))
is pseudo-null over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
. On the other hand, as
F_{\infty}
contains
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
, an application of
[
19
, Lemma 2.5.1(c)]
tells us that

H_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{2}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))\cong H_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{2}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i-1)

for every
i\geq 2
. Thus, it follows that
H_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{2}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))
is pseudo-null over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]]
for every
i\geq 2
. Combining this latter observation with Lemma
3.4
and Corollary
3.3
, we obtain the conclusion of the proposition. ∎

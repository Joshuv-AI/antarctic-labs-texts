Kempe Classes and Almost Bipartite Graphs
Daniel W. Cranston, Carl Feghali

Abstract
Let G G be a graph and k k be a positive integer, and let Kc ​ ( G , k ) \textrm{Kc}(G,k) denote the number of Kempe equivalence classes for the k k -colorings of G G . In 2006, Mohar noted that Kc ​ ( G , k ) = 1 \textrm{Kc}(G,k)=1 if G G is bipartite. As a generalization, we show that Kc ​ ( G , k ) = 1 \textrm{Kc}(G,k)=1 if G G is formed from a bipartite graph by adding any number of edges less than ( ⌈ k / 2 ⌉ 2 ) + ( ⌊ k / 2 ⌋ 2 ) \binom{\lceil k/2\rceil}{2}+\binom{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}{2} . We show that our result is tight (up to lower order terms) by constructing, for each k ≥ 8 k\geq 8 , a graph G G formed from a bipartite graph by adding ( k 2 + 8 ​ k − 45 + 1 ) / 4 (k^{2}+8k-45+1)/4 edges such that Kc ​ ( G , k ) ≥ 2 \textrm{Kc}(G,k)\geq 2 . This refutes a recent conjecture of Higashitani–Matsumoto.

Introduction

In a
(proper)
k
-coloring
of a graph
G
, each vertex is assigned a color from

\{1,\ldots,k\}
and adjacent vertices receive distinct colors. Given a

k
-coloring
\varphi
, an
i,j
-Kempe swap
interchanges the colors of some component of the subgraph
induced under
\varphi
by colors
i
and
j
. Clearly, this
yields a new proper
k
-coloring.

Kempe swaps were introduced in the late
1800s by Alfred Kempe, in a failed attempt to prove the 4 Color Theorem.
However, the idea was largely salvaged by Heawood, who used this technique to
prove the 5 Color Theorem. Kempe swaps did play a crucial role in the eventual
proof of the 4 Color Theorem
[
1
]
and its later
reproof
[
24
]
. Kempe swaps have also played a central role in the study
of edge-coloring, including work of Vizing
[
28
,
29
]
,
Kierstead
[
16
,
11
]
, Tashkinov
[
26
]
,
and many others
[
9
,
5
,
4
]
.
All of this work asks one meta-question “Does a given graph (or line graph)
admit a
k
-coloring?”

However, this sustained focus on the use of Kempe swaps eventually prompted two
interesting related questions: “Given a graph
G
and two
k
-colorings

\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
, is it possible to transform
\varphi_{1}
into
\varphi_{2}
by a
sequence of Kempe swaps?” and “Is it possible to transform
every

k
-coloring into every
other
k
-coloring by a sequence of Kempe swaps?”
For a graph
G
and a positive integer
k
, two
k
-colorings are

equivalent
if one can be transformed to the other by a sequence of Kempe
swaps. Let
\textrm{Kc}(G,k)
denote the number of equivalence classes.
Now the latter question can be rephrased as asking whether or not
\textrm{Kc}(G,k)=1
.
(Such questions fall, more broadly, under the heading of
reconfiguration
,
which is surveyed by van den Heuvel
[
27
]
and
Nishimura
[
23
]
.)

Early work in this area was done by
Vizing
[
28
,
29
]

(for edge-coloring), Meyniel
[
19
]
, Fisk
[
14
]
, and Las Vergnas and
Meyniel
[
17
]
. In 2006 this line of study was reinvigorated by a
beautiful survey of Mohar
[
20
]
, which proposed a variety of open
questions. The abundance of results that has followed includes work on regular
graphs
[
2
,
13
]
, planar graphs
[
12
,
10
]
,
grids
[
21
,

,
7
]
, line graphs

[

]
, and much

,

.

Mohar
[

]
noted (and it is easy to check) that
\textrm{Kc}(B,k)=1
when

is any
bipartite graph and

. This motivated Higashitani and Matsumoto
[

]
to study

\textrm{Kc}(B+E_{\ell},k)
, where

is a graph formed from

by adding

graph for some

Conjecture 1

(Higashitani–Matsumoto; now disproved for
k\geq 8
)
.

If
G
is a
(k-1)
-colorable
B+E_{\ell}
graph with
k\geq 4
and
\ell<\binom{k}{2}
, then
\textrm{Kc}(G,k)=1
.

The assumptions on
\ell
and the chromatic number of
G
in Conjecture
1

are necessary
[
15
]
. Here we show that Conjecture
1
is false for
all
k\geq 8
(we suspect that it is true for
k\in\{5,6,7\}
). Let
\mathbf{1}_{x}
denote
the function on positive integers
x
that is 1 if
x

is odd and 0 if
x
is even. That is,
\mathbf{1}_{x}
is equal to
x\bmod{2}
.

Theorem 1
.

For all
k\geq 8
, Conjecture
1
is false.
Furthermore, for all
k\geq 12
, there exist
(k-1)
-colorable graphs
G_{k}

with
\textrm{Kc}(G_{k},k)\geq 2
and
G_{k}=B+E_{\ell}
where
\ell=(k^{2}+8k-45+\mathbf{1}_{k-1})/4
.

In our next result, we show that, in a certain sense, our constructions are the worst
possible (up to lower order terms).

Theorem 2
.

If
G=B+E_{\ell}
with

\ell<\binom{\lceil k/2\rceil}{2}+\binom{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}{2}=\frac{k^{2}-2k+\mathbf{1}_{k}}{4}

\textrm{Kc}(G,k)=1

For easy reference, we record the following observation.

Lemma 1
.

If
f(x):=\binom{\lceil x/2\rceil}{2}+\binom{\lfloor x/2\rfloor}{2}
,
then
f(x)=(x^{2}-2x+\mathbf{1}_{x})/4
.

Proof.

If

f(x)=2\binom{x/2}{2}=\frac{x}{2}\frac{x-2}{2}=\frac{x^{2}-2x}{4}=\frac{x^{2}-2x+\mathbf{1}_{x}}{4}
.
If

is odd, then
f(x)=\binom{(x+1)/2}{2}+\binom{(x-1)/2}{2}=\frac{x^{2}-1+x^{2}-4x+3}{8}=\frac{x^{2}-2x+1}{4}=\frac{x^{2}-2x+\mathbf{1}_{x}}{4}

Theorem 1

For all

Theorem 2

Suppose the theorem is false, and let

Now

Claim 1

Every

is

-equivalent to some
(k-1)
-coloring.

Proof.

An

We first show that if there exists a pair of colors

does not have any

or

and

(Specifically, color

To do this, whenever

appears on

contains no

Each time that we perform the steps in the previous paragraph, the number

Thus, we can assume from now on, that for every pair of colors
i,j
of

\varphi

E_{\ell}
has no
i,j
-edge, then in
\varphi
colors
i
and

each appear on at most one part.

Suppose colors
i
and

each appear only on the same one part, and that

and

can be “merged” by recoloring

with

To prove the claim, it suffices to show that

must have a mergeable pair,

By hypothesis,
we have
\ell\leq\frac{k^{2}-2k+\mathbf{1}_{k}}{4}-1=\frac{k^{2}-2k-4+\mathbf{1}_{k}}{4}
. Thus, the number of pairs

and

.

from

has no

identify all vertices colored

under
\varphi
(as shown above, all

has an

\frac{k^{2}-\mathbf{1}_{k}}{4}

Thus, the number of mergable pairs is at least

\frac{k^{2}+4-\mathbf{1}_{k}}{4}-\frac{k^{2}-\mathbf{1}_{k}}{4}=1

\diamondsuit

We end this note with the proof of the following aforementioned result.

Theorem 3
.

If
\textrm{Kc}(G,\chi(G)+1)\geq 2
, then
|V(G)|\geq 9
.

Proof.

We prove that if
|V(G)|\leq 8

\textrm{Kc}(G,\chi(G)+1)=1

We proceed by induction on
|V(G)|

G
or
its complement
\overline{G}
is a complete graph, which includes the base case

|V(G)|\leq 2

Mohar

\chi(G)=2

\textrm{Kc}(G,k)=1

for all

\chi(G)\geq 3

\textrm{Kc}(G-v,\chi(G)+1)=1

proved by Las Vergnas and Meyniel
[
17
]
; Mohar also includes a

[
20

henceforth that

If
G
has a dominating vertex

\textrm{Kc}(G,\chi(G)+1)=1

\textrm{Kc}(G-v,\chi(G))=\textrm{Kc}(G-v,\chi(G-v)+1)=1

induction hypothesis. However, we prove something stronger: If there exists
an independent set

and there exists

To see this, note that an arbitrary
(\chi(G)+1)
-coloring of

\overline{G}

simplicial vertex, i.e., its neighborhood is a clique. Thus, we call

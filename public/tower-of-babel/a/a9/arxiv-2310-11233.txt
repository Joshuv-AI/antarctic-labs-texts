Nearly half-flat SU(3) structures on S 3 × S 3 S^{3}\times S^{3}
Ragini Singhal

Abstract




Abstract

We study the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure induced on an oriented hypersurface of a 7-dimensional manifold with a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. Such
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures are called
nearly half-flat
.
We characterise the left invariant nearly half-flat structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
. This characterisation then helps us to systematically analyse nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on an interval times
S^{3}\times S^{3}
.

1 Introduction

A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a
7
-dimensional manifold
M
is defined by a non-degenerate 3-form
\varphi
that induces a metric
g_{\varphi}
, a cross-product
\times_{\varphi}
, an orientation
\vol_{\varphi}
and thus a Hodge star
*_{\varphi}
on
M
(see
[
12
]
). The Riemannian manifold
M
with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
is called nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
if
\varphi
is a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure that is, for some
\lambda\neq 0

(1.1)

\displaystyle d\varphi

\displaystyle=\lambda*_{\varphi}\varphi.

They were described as manifolds with weak holonomy
\mathrm{G}_{2}
by Gray in
[
33
]
and are positive Einstein
[
30
]
. The cone over a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifold has holonomy contained in
\textup{Spin}(7)
. Manifolds with nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure have been studied for various reasons in mathematics as well as physics: in addition to the papers described in the introduction, see also
[
9
,
10
,
3
,
4
,
31
,
39
,
6
,
5
,
23
,
45
,
47
,
29
,
1
,
8
]
.

So far the only known examples of nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifolds are homogeneous
[
30
]
or they come from 3-Sasakian geometry
[
11
,
34
]
. Thus, in order to find new examples of nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifolds one has to look for non-homogeneous examples. Finding an inhomogeneous Einstein metric coming from the nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is quite challenging since the field equations for an appropriate metric ansatz are highly nonlinear partial differential equations. As a first step towards this goal, it would be natural to look for cohomogeneity-one examples of nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifolds, that is, nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures that has a Lie group
G
action preserving
\varphi
and the generic orbits have dimension
7-1=6
. These
6
-dimensional orbits then carry an invariant
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure defined by a 2-form
\omega
and a
3
-form
\gamma
. In this article we describe the invariant
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure
(\omega,\gamma)
on the six-dimensional oriented hypersurface
M^{6}
of a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifold
N^{7}
. The condition that the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi=dt\wedge\omega+\gamma
on
I\times M
satisfies (
1.1
) imposes some conditions on the intrinsic torsion of the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure
(\omega,\gamma)
on
M
. The
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure
(\omega,\gamma)
thus obtained is known as
nearly half-flat
and is defined by the condition

\displaystyle d\gamma=\frac{\lambda}{2}\omega^{2}

where
\lambda
is the non-zero constant in (
1.1
).

The term nearly half-flat originates from the analogous situation where the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure on the hypersurface of a torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifold is referred as
half-flat
. Nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures were first introduced in
[
26
]
in the context of
evolution equations on six-manifolds
M
leading to nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on the product of
M
and an interval. In
[
22
,
48
,
46
,
21
]
the authors showed that one can construct nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures by lifting certain nearly half-flat structures. This result is analogous to Hitchin’s result in
[
37
]
that half-flat

\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on a six-dimensional manifold
M
can be lifted to parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on the product
M\times\mathbb{R}
under certain conditions. In fact the nearly half-flat is a slight generalisation of the half-flat where the
3
-form
\gamma
is not closed. Moreover if we impose
\gamma
to be closed, the nearly half-flat structure becomes half-flat. This is the same as setting
\lambda=0
in (
1.1
).

In
[
19
]
the authors classified the connected Lie groups
G
that can act as cohomogeneity-one on nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifolds. In the case when
G
is simple, no new solutions were found. One of the possible non-simple Lie groups that can act by cohomogeneity-one on nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifolds is
\rm{SU}(2)^{2}
up to finite quotients. In this case the generic orbit of a complete cohomogeneity-one nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifold is isomorphic to
S^{3}\times S^{3}
or its finite quotient. In this article we describe the
\rm{SU}(2)^{2}
-invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
. In
[
42
]
the authors describe the left-invariant half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
using the representation theory of
\rm{SO}(4)
and matrix algebra. In the present article we follow a similar approach and show that we can describe any invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure in terms of two real
3\times 3
matrices and two real constants satisfying some normalization and commutativity relations (see Theorem
4.1
).

In
[
28
]
the authors constructed the first complete examples of cohomogeneity-one nearly Kähler structures in six-dimensions. Hopefully the characterization of invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
can also be used to achieve something similar in the nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure case.

The study of the hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{R}^{7}
with its associated
\mathrm{G}_{2}
cross product was initiated by Calabi
[
16
]
, Gray
[
32
]
and later extended to manifolds with
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
[
18
,
17
,
20
,
42
,
2
,
24
,
43
]
. The Weingarten map of an oriented hypersurface inside a manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure can be completely described in terms of the intrinsic torsion forms of the hypersurface. This relation was used in
[
15
]
and
[
2
]
to describe the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure on an oriented hypersurface of a manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

In §
2
we give a brief introduction of
\rm{SU}(3)
-,
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on manifolds of dimension
6
,
7
respectively. We define the respective intrinsic torsion forms and alienate the classes of our particular interests.

In §
3
we derive the evolution equations that describe the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
M\times I
evolving from a family of nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
M
. We then use the evolution equations to describe the nearly half-flat condition in terms of the intrinsic torsion forms.

In §
4
we specialise the theory curated in the previous sections to
S^{3}\times S^{3}
which constitutes the heart of this article. Using matrix algebra we describe the space of invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
and show that it can be parameterized using two real
3\times 3
matrices
P,Q
and real constants
(a,b)
satisfying some commutativity and normalization conditions. Further we use this parameterization to describe the moduli space of invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
, in terms of which it turns out to be a finite-dimensional symplectic quotient. This
description is rather similar to the half-flat case as described in
[
42
]
. The description of the invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure
(\omega,\gamma)
in term of elementary matrices makes it rather elegant to construct
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures of specific torsion classes as we do in §
4
. Using this terminology we are able to produce nearly half-flat structures with strictly positive scalar curvature as well as with zero scalar curvature.

In §
5
we describe the evolution equations in the special case of
S^{3}\times S^{3}
in terms of the parameterization. The equations we have are in contrast with the half-flat case as presented in
[
42
]
as they are no longer Painlevé equations. In fact the constants
a
and
b
evolve with
t
as opposed to the half-flat case (see (
5.1
)). We also represent some of the known examples of nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures obtained from the invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
such as the homogeneous nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on the Berger space
\rm{SO}(5)/SO(3)
and the sine cone metric on
S^{1}\times S^{3}\times S^{3}
. The two solutions we present here has extra
Z_{2}^{2}
and
\rm{SU}(2)
-symmetry respectively. The algebraic setup introduced makes the description of these known nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures much more elegant and efficient to use.

2
\rm{SU}(3)
and
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures

We are interested in studying invariant
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
. An
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure on a
6
-dimensional manifold
M
is defined by a pair
\omega,\Omega
where
\omega
is a symplectic form and
\Omega
is a complex
(3,0)
form which satisfies the normalization condition

\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\bar{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4i}{3}\omega^{3}.

An
\rm{SU}(3)
reduction defines a circle of real 3-forms
\Gamma:=\{\cos\theta\rm{Re}\Omega+\sin\theta\rm{Im}\Omega,\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
, and any
\gamma\in\Gamma
along with the
2
-form
\omega
defines an almost complex structure
J
, the metric
g
, and the orientation
\vol_{g}
. Note that an
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure on a
6
-dimensional manifold can be defined by a pair
(\omega,\gamma)
where
\gamma\in\Gamma
. indeed
\gamma
can determine
J
as well
J\gamma
such that
\gamma+iJ\gamma
is a complex holomorphic volume form of type
(3,0)
. See
[
36
]
for more details.

Using
\omega
one can define the symplectic Hodge star
\star:\Omega^{r}M\to\Omega^{6-r}M
via the relation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\star\beta

\displaystyle=\omega(\alpha,\beta)\ \frac{\omega^{3}}{6}.

Using the above defined symplectic Hodge star for
p\in M
we have
P_{\gamma}\in\rm{End}(T^{*}_{p}M)
defined by

\displaystyle P_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\colon\alpha\mapsto-\frac{1}{2}\star(\gamma\wedge\star(\gamma\wedge\alpha).

Then the endomorphism
J_{\gamma}=(\det{P_{\gamma}})^{-\frac{1}{6}}P_{\gamma}
defines an almost complex structure on
M
. We write
J
instead of
J_{\gamma}
when there is no scope for confusion. On any point
p\in M
an almost complex structure
J_{\gamma}
on
T_{p}M
can be defined using any
\gamma\in\Gamma
as described in
[
37
, Section 8.2]
. We define
K_{\gamma}\in\rm{End}(T_{p}M)\otimes\Lambda_{p}^{6}M\cong\rm{End}(T_{p}M)
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle X\mapsto K(X):=(X\lrcorner\gamma)\wedge\gamma\in\Lambda^{5}_{p}M\cong T_{p}M\otimes\Lambda^{6}_{p}M.

Then
J_{\gamma}=6K_{\gamma}/\omega^{3}
.

The natural action of the Lie group
\rm{SU}(3)
on the tangent space of a
6
-dimensional manifold induces the following decomposition on the space of differential forms
\Omega^{p}
into irreducible
\rm{SU}(3)
-representations
\Omega^{p}_{k}
with pointwise dimension
k
(see
[
7
]
):

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Omega^{2}&=\Omega^{2}_{1}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{6}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{8},\\
\Omega^{3}&=\Omega^{3}_{\rm{Re}}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{\rm{Im}}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{6}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{12},\end{split}

where each summand can be described in terms of the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure as follows,

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\omega,

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}_{6}

\displaystyle=\{\star(\alpha\wedge\gamma)\ |\ \alpha\in\Omega^{1}\}=\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}\ |\ J\beta=-\beta\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}_{8}

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}\ |\ \beta\wedge\gamma=0,\star\beta=-\beta\wedge\omega\}

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}\ |\ J\beta=\beta,\beta\wedge\omega^{2}=0\},

and

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}_{\rm{Re}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\gamma,

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}_{\rm{Im}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}J\gamma,

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}_{6}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\wedge\omega\ |\ \alpha\in\Omega^{1}\}=\{\xi\in\Omega^{3}\ |\ \star\xi=\xi\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}_{12}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in\Omega^{3}\ |\ \xi\wedge\omega=0,\xi\wedge\gamma=0,\xi\wedge J\gamma=0\}.

The space of
1,6
-forms is irreducible and we can describe the space of
4,5
-forms via the isomorphism described by the Hodge star operator
*
.
Using the decomposition in (
2.3
) and the relations between
\omega,\gamma,J\gamma
one can compute the derivatives of the forms
\omega,\gamma,J\gamma
. For some
w_{1}^{\pm}\in\Omega^{0},w_{2}^{\pm}\in\Omega^{2}_{8}
,
w_{3}\in\Omega^{3}_{12}
and
w_{4},w_{5}\in\Omega^{1}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\omega&=w_{1}^{+}\gamma+w_{1}^{-}J\gamma+w_{4}\wedge\omega+w_{3},\\
d\gamma&=\frac{2w_{1}^{-}}{3}\omega^{2}+w_{5}\wedge\gamma+w_{2}^{+}\wedge\omega,\\
dJ\gamma&=-\frac{2w_{1}^{+}}{3}\omega^{2}+Jw_{5}\wedge\gamma+w_{2}^{-}\wedge\omega.\end{split}

The forms
w_{i}
are the intrinsic torsion forms of the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures
[
2
,
7
,
18
]
and define the torsion
T
of the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure. The vanishing of
T
implies
\rm{Hol}(\nabla^{LC})\subseteq\rm{SU}(3)
.

The torsion
T
of a
G
-structure is the space
T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}
which for
G=\rm{SU}(3)
turns out to be a
42
-dimensional space. As irreducible
\rm{SU}(3)
-modules the torsion space decomposes as follows

\displaystyle T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{su}(3)^{\perp}\cong\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\pm}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\pm}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{3}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{4}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{5}.

The
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure is torsion-free or Calabi–Yau if and only if
T=0
and is nearly Kähler if
T\in\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\pm}
. For a more thorough description of these torsion classes see
[
18
]
,
[
2
]
. In this article we are interested in a special torsion class of
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures known as nearly half-flat which arise when the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
M\times I
is a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. The torsion for a nearly half-flat structure lies is
\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}^{-}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{3}
. We describe this torsion class in detail in the following section.

A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is defined by the reduction of the structure group of the frame bundle of a
7
-dimensional manifold
N
to the Lie group
\mathrm{G}_{2}\subset\rm{SO}(7)
. The existence of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
N
is characterized by a positive
3
-form
\varphi
preserved by the action of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
on
\Omega^{3}(N)

[
33
]
. Such a structure exists if and only if the manifold is orientable and spin, conditions which are respectively equivalent to the vanishing
of the first and second Stiefel–Whitney classes. The
3
-form
\varphi
nonlinearly induces a Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
and an orientation
\vol_{\varphi}
on
N
and hence a Hodge
star operator
*_{\varphi}
. We denote the Hodge dual
4
-form
*_{\varphi}\varphi
by
\psi
. Pointwise we have
\|\varphi\|^{2}=\|\psi\|^{2}=7
, where the norm is taken with respect to the metric induced by
\varphi
.

Similar to
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure, a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
N
induces a splitting of the spaces of differential forms into irreducible
\mathrm{G}_{2}
representations. The space of
2
-forms
\Omega^{2}
and
3
-forms
\Omega^{3}
decompose as

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{2}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{14},

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{3}_{1}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{27}.

More precisely, we have the following description of the space of forms :

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}_{7}

\displaystyle=\{X\lrcorner\varphi\mid X\in\Gamma(TN)\}=\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}\mid*(\varphi\wedge\beta)=2\beta\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}_{14}

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}(N)\mid\beta\wedge\psi=0\}=\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}\mid*(\varphi\wedge\beta)=-\beta\}.

Similarly, for
3
-forms

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{f\varphi\mid f\in C^{\infty}(N)\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}_{7}

\displaystyle=\{X\lrcorner\psi\mid X\in\Gamma(TN)\}=\{*(\alpha\wedge\varphi)\mid\alpha\in\Omega^{1}\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{3}_{27}

\displaystyle=\{\eta\in\Omega^{3}(N)\ \mid\ \eta\wedge\varphi=0=\eta\wedge\psi\}.

The decomposition of
\Omega^{4}
and
\Omega^{5}
are obtained by taking the respective Hodge stars with respect
*_{\varphi}
.

Given a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
on
M
, we can decompose
d\varphi
and
d\psi
according to the above decomposition. This defines the
torsion forms
, which are unique differential forms
\tau_{0}\in\Omega^{0}
,
\tau_{1}\in\Omega^{1}
,
\tau_{2}\in\Omega^{2}_{14}
and
\tau_{3}\in\Omega^{3}_{27}
such that (see
[
38
]
)

\displaystyle d\varphi

\displaystyle=\tau_{0}\psi+3\tau_{1}\wedge\varphi+*_{\varphi}\tau_{3},

\displaystyle d\psi

\displaystyle=4\tau_{1}\wedge\psi+*_{\varphi}\tau_{2}.

Here the torsion lives in the
49
-dimensional space
T^{*}N\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\perp}
and decomposes into irreducible
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-modules as follows

\displaystyle T^{*}N\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{\perp}\cong\mathcal{X}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{X}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{X}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{X}_{3}.

These torsion forms give rise to the sixteen classes of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures and
T=0
if and only if
d\varphi=d\psi=0
(see
[
25
]
,
[
14
]
). The torsion class for which
T\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
is called nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
is
nearly parallel
if and only if there exists
\lambda\neq 0
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle d\varphi=\lambda\psi\ \ \ \ \textup{and}\ \ \ \ d\psi=0.

In this case,
T_{ij}=\dfrac{\lambda}{4}(g_{\varphi})_{ij}
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\varphi
is a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure differentiating (
2.5
) gives
d\lambda\wedge\psi=0
which implies
d\lambda=0
, as wedge product with
\psi
is an isomorphism from
\Omega^{1}_{7}
to
\Omega^{5}_{7}
. Thus, if
N
is connected
\lambda
is a constant.

In this article we are interested in parameterizing the
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures that arise on the equidistant orientable hypersurfaces of manifolds with nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures.

3 Evolution equations from
\rm{SU}(3)
to
\mathrm{G}_{2}

The exceptional Lie group
\mathrm{G}_{2}
is the group of automorphisms of the Octonions
\mathbb{O}
that preserves the splitting
\mathbb{O}\cong\mathbb{R}+\rm{Im}\mathbb{O}
. Then one can define the Lie group
\rm{SU}(3)
as the subgroup of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
that preserves an imaginary unit Octonion. This fact indicates the presence of an
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure on an orientable hypersurface of a manifold with
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure which led Calabi
[
16
]
and Gray
[
32
]
study the induced
\rm{SU}(3)
-structure on orientable hypersurfaces of
\rm{Im}\mathbb{O}
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval. Given a one-parameter family of
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures
(\omega(t),\gamma(t))
on
M
, one can define a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
(\varphi,\psi)
on
I\times M
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi&=dt\wedge\omega(t)+\gamma(t)\\
\psi=*_{\varphi}\varphi&=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}(t)-dt\wedge J\gamma(t).\end{split}

Conditions on
\omega,\gamma
for nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.
Now suppose
\varphi,\psi
define a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, that is for some non-zero constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle d\varphi

\displaystyle=\lambda\psi.

From (
3.1
) we get that

(3.2)

\displaystyle d\varphi

\displaystyle=dt\wedge(-d\omega(t)+\gamma^{\prime}(t))+d\gamma(t),

(3.3)

\displaystyle d\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{d(\omega^{2}(t))}{2}+dt\wedge(\frac{1}{2}(\omega^{2})^{\prime}(t)+dJ\gamma(t)).

Thus
(\varphi,\psi)
defines a nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\omega(t)&=\gamma^{\prime}(t)+{\lambda}J\gamma(t),\\
d\gamma(t)&=\frac{\lambda}{2}\omega^{2}(t)\\
dJ\gamma(t)&=-\frac{(\omega^{2})^{\prime}}{2}.\end{split}

For some
\alpha,\beta\in C^{\infty}(M),Z\in\Omega^{1}(M),\gamma^{\prime}_{12}\in\Omega^{3}_{12}
the
3
-form
\gamma^{\prime}=\alpha\gamma+\beta J\gamma+Z\wedge\omega+\gamma^{\prime}_{12}
.

Equation (
3.4
) immediately imply
d\omega^{2}=0
which further implies that
\omega\wedge d\omega=0
. Thus we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega\wedge d\omega=\omega\wedge\gamma^{\prime}+\lambda\omega\wedge J\gamma.

Since
\omega\wedge J\gamma=0
the above implies that
\omega\wedge\gamma^{\prime}=0
hence
\beta=0
. Since
\gamma\wedge\omega=0
we have

\displaystyle d\gamma\wedge\omega

\displaystyle=\gamma\wedge d\omega,

which from (
3.4
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\omega^{3}

\displaystyle=\gamma\wedge\gamma^{\prime}+\lambda(\gamma\wedge J\gamma)=\gamma\wedge\gamma^{\prime}+\frac{2}{3}\lambda\omega^{3}.

Thus,
\gamma\wedge\gamma^{\prime}=(-1/6)\lambda\omega^{3}
which implies
\alpha=-\frac{\lambda}{4}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\alpha\gamma-\frac{\lambda J\gamma}{4}+\gamma^{\prime}_{12}.

Now let
\omega^{\prime}=p\omega+X\lrcorner\gamma+\omega^{\prime}_{8}
for some
p\in C^{\infty}(M),X\in\Gamma(TM),\omega^{\prime}_{8}\in\Omega^{2}_{8}
.
The equation
d\psi=0
implies
dJ\gamma=-\omega^{\prime}\wedge\omega
which further implies
p=2\alpha/3
and
X=0
.
Substituting
\alpha=w_{1}^{+},\gamma^{\prime}_{12}=w_{3},
and
\omega^{\prime}_{8}={w}_{2}^{-}
in (

\displaystyle=\frac{2(2p^{2}-q)^{2}}{p^{2}}e^{2i-1}\otimes e^{2i-1}+\frac{2(2p^{2}+q)^{2}}{p^{2}}e^{2i}\otimes e^{2i}+\frac{4p^{4}-q^{2}}{p^{2}}(e^{2i-1}\otimes e^{2i}+e^{2i}\otimes e^{2i-1}).

One can compute the scalar curvature of the nearly half-flat structure metric by (
3.7
). For
a=b=0
and
q=\pm\frac{p\sqrt{36p^{2}+3\sqrt{3}p}}{3}
the scalar curvature takes the form

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{2(72p^{4}+105p+5\sqrt{3})}{3p}.

There are two values of
p
for which
s=0
but for only one of them
q=\pm\frac{p\sqrt{36p^{2}+3\sqrt{3}p}}{3}\in\mathbb{R}
hence we get one solution from this case.

However for
q=\pm\frac{p\sqrt{36p^{2}-3\sqrt{3}p}}{3}
the scalar curvature turns out to be

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{2(72p^{4}+105p-5\sqrt{3})}{3p},

and both the solutions for
s=0
are admissible.

5  The
S^{3}\times S^{3}

We can now describe the flow equations for the nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
S^{3}\times S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
compatible with (
3.1
) in this matrix framework. From Proposition
3.2
we know that an invariant nearly half-flat structure
(\omega,\Omega)\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,a,b}
. on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
evolve to a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if it satisfies (
3.6
).

In terms of matrix
P,Q
used to parameterize invariant nearly half-flat
\rm{SU}(3)
-structures on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
the evolution equations take the following form where
Q_{i}
s and
A,B,R_{i}
s are defined in (
4.1
) and (
4.4
) respectively.

Proposition 5.1
.

The evolution equations for the flow
t\mapsto(P(t),Q(t))\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda.a(t),b(t)}
are given by

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a^{\prime}&=-\frac{2\lambda}{\det P}A,\ \ \ b^{\prime}=-\frac{2\lambda}{\det P}B\\
Q_{1}^{\prime}&=-\frac{2\lambda}{\det P}R_{1}+P\\
Q_{2}^{\prime}&=-\frac{2\lambda}{\det P}R_{2}-P\end{split}

Remark 5.2
.

In the half-flat case that is when
\lambda=0
the parameters

are constant in

The homogeneous nearly
\mathrm{G}_{2}
metric on the Berger space:

Let

be the Berger space. The homogeneous metric on
B
has a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. There is a cohomogeneity-one action of
\rm{SO}(4)
on
B
as first described by

B
isomorphic to
\rm{SO}(4)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\cong\frac{S^{3}\times S^{3}}{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}}
. The Lie group
\mathrm{SO}(3)
is embedded into
\mathrm{SO}(5)
via the
5
dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

S_{i,j}
the symmetric
3\times 3
matrix with 1 at the
(i,j)
and
(j,i)
entry and
0
elsewhere then

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\rm{diag}(1,1,-2)}{\sqrt{6}},\quad E_{2}:=\frac{\rm{diag}(1,-1,0)}{\sqrt{2}},\quad E_{3}:=S_{12},\quad E_{4}:=S_{13},\quad E_{5}:=S_{23}

defines a basis of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cong\mathbb{R}^{5}
. The embedding of
\rm{SO}(3)
in
\rm{SO}(5)
by the conjugate action of
\rm{SO}(3)
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cong\mathbb{R}^{5}
.

The group
\rm{SO}(5)
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{5}
via the usual left multiplication. We can define the group
\rm{SO}(4)=\rm{SO}(4)_{E_{1}}\subset\rm{SO}(5)
as the subgroup preserving the
E_{1}
direction in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
. Thus there is an action of
\rm{SO}(4)
on
\mathbb{R}^{5}
. The generic stabilizer group for the group
\rm{SO}(4)_{E_{1}}
is also given by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\cong\rm{diag}(1,1,ab,b,a)
that preserves the
E_{1},E_{2}
directions in
\mathbb{R}^{5}

\rm{SO}(4)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
. The stabilizer of the identity coset
x_{-}=\textup{id}.\rm{SO}(3)
in
B
is the group
K^{-}\cong\rm{O}(2)
such that
K_{0}^{-}\cong\rm{SO}(2)
acts by angle
2\theta
in the
E_{2},E_{3}
plane and by angle
\theta
in the
E_{4},E_{5}
plane. Thus
x_{-}.\rm{SO}(4)=\rm{SO}(4)/(\rm{SO}(2)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is a singular orbit for the action. For the other singular orbit, we need to follow the geodesic
\gamma(t):=\cos(t)E_{1}+\sin(t)E_{2}
transverse to all orbits. At

we get the second singular orbit again isomorphic to
\rm{SO}(4)/(\rm{SO}(2)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Thus the singular stabilizer groups for the action are both isomorphic to
S^{1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. By some simple calculations one can compute that

\displaystyle\rm{Stab}(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}&t\in(0,\pi/3),\\
S(\mathrm{O}(2)\mathrm{O}(1))&t=0,\\
S(\mathrm{O}(1)\mathrm{O}(2))&t=\pi/3.\end{cases}

With respect to the basis
\{e^{1},e^{2},e^{3},e^{4},e^{5},e^{6},dt\}

\rm{SO}(4)/Z_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{5\sqrt{5}}\left(\cos(t)(e^{235}-e^{146})-3\sin(t-2\pi/3)\sin(t+2\pi/3)(e^{235}+e^{146})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{5\sqrt{5}}\left(\cos(t-2\pi/3)(e^{145}-e^{236})-3\sin(t)\sin(t+2\pi/3)(e^{145}+e^{236})\right)

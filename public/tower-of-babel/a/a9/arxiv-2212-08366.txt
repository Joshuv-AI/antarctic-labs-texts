Abstract




Abstract
: In this paper, we introduce and study a stochastic differential variational inequality (SDVI) which consists of a stochastic differential equation and a stochastic variational inequality. We obtain the existence and uniqueness of the solutions for SDVI by using the iteration method and Gronwall’s inequality. Moreover, we show the convergence of Euler scheme for solving SDVI under some mild conditions. Finally, we apply the obtained results to solve the electrical circuits with diodes and the collapse of the bridge problems in stochastic environment.

Keywords
: Stochastic differential variational inequality; Stochastic differential equation; Stochastic variational inequality; Euler scheme; Convergence.

Preliminaries

This section recalls some necessary notions, symbols and lemmas.

Definition 2.1
.

[
23
,
2
,
17
]

•

Denote the norm and inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or
\mathbb{R}^{m}
) by
\|\cdot\|
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
.

•

B_{t}
is an
l
-dimensional standard Brownian motion with probability distribution
N(0,t)
.

•

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\left\{B_{t}:0\leq s\leq t\right\}
is a filtration generated by
B_{t}
.

•

L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the set which contains all the
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued
measurable square-integrable random variables, is a Hilbert space fitted out the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}=(E|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

•

H_{[a,b]}=L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of all stochastic processes
f(t,\omega)
,
0\leq a\leq b\leq T
valued in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(t,\omega)
is adapted to the filtration
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}
and
\int_{a}^{b}\mathbb{E}\|f(t,\omega)\|^{2}dt<\infty
. Moreover,
L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{H_{[a,b]}}=\int_{a}^{b}\mathbb{E}[\langle u(t,\omega),v(t,\omega)\rangle]dt,\quad\forall u,v\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

and norm

\|u\|_{H_{[a,b]}}=\left[\int_{a}^{b}\mathbb{E}\|u(t,\omega)\|^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall u\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be nonempty. For any given given interval
[a,b]\subset[0,T]
, set

U[a,b]=\left\{u\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}):u(s,\omega)\in K,a.e.\;s\in[a,b],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega\right\}

and

U=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}):u(\omega)\in K,a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega\right\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be nonempty, closed and convex. Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

for any given interval
[a,b]\subset[0,T]
,
U[a,b]
is nonempty, closed and convex in
L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
;

(ii)

U
is nonempty, closed and convex in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Proof.

For any
u(s,\omega)\in U[a,b]
, we have
u(s,\omega)\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and so it is
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable for all
s\in[a,b]
. It follows from the fact
K\not=\emptyset
that
U[a,b]\not=\emptyset
. Now we show that
U[a,b]
is convex in
L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Indeed, for any
u_{1}(s,\omega),u_{2}(s,\omega)\in U[a,b]
and
0<\lambda<1
, one has
u_{1}(s,\omega),u_{2}(s,\omega)\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
u_{1}(s,\omega),u_{2}(s,\omega)\in K
for a.e.
t\in[a,b]
, a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
. Since
K
is nonempty closed convex, it is easy to check that

\lambda u_{1}(s,\omega)+(1-\lambda)u_{2}(s,\omega)\in K,\;a.e.\;t\in[a,b],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega

and so
U[0,t]
is convex.

Next we prove that
U[a,b]
is closed in
L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. In fact, let
\left\{u_{n}(s,\omega)\right\}\subset U[a,b]
be a sequence such that
\|u_{n}(s,\omega)-u^{*}(s,\omega)\|_{H[a,b]}\to 0
. Then it is easy to see that
u^{*}(t,\omega)\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Moreover, for any
s\in[a,b]
,

\|u_{n}(s,\omega)-u^{*}(s,\omega)\|^{2}_{H[a,b]}=\int_{a}^{b}\mathbb{E}\|u_{n}(s,\omega)-u^{*}(s,\omega)\|^{2}ds\to 0,

which implies that

\|u_{n}(s,\omega)-u^{*}(s,\omega)\|\to 0,\;a.e.\;s\in[a,b],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega.

Now, the closedness of
K
shows that
u^{*}(s,\omega)\in U[a,b]
and so
U[a,b]
is closed. Thus the statement
(i)
holds. Similarily, we can prove
(ii)
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

[
6
]

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
K\subset H
be nonempty, closed and convex. Then, for each
m\in H
, there exists a unique
u\in K
, named as the projection of
m
onto
K
and denoted by

, such that

\displaystyle\|m-u\|_{H}=\min_{v\in K}\|m-v\|_{H}=dist(m,K).

Moreover,

if and only if

u\in K,\quad\left\langle m-u,v-u\right\rangle_{H}\leq 0,\quad\forall v\in K.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
27
]

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
K\subset H
be nonempty, closed and convex, and
T:H\rightarrow H
be a mapping. Then
x\in K
is a solution of the following variational inequality (VI):

\left\langle T(x),y-x\right\rangle_{H}\geq 0,\quad\forall y\in K

if and only if
x=P_{K}(I-\alpha T)(x)
, where
\alpha
is a positive constant and
I
is an identity operator.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
8
,
43
]

If there exist a non-negative sequence
\left\{z_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
and a non-negative and non-decreasing sequence
\left\{y_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
such that

\displaystyle z_{n}\leq\frac{Q_{A}}{N^{\beta}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{z_{i}}{(n-i)^{1-\beta}}+y_{n},\quad 1\leq n\leq N,0<\beta<1,Q_{A}>0,

then the sequence
\left\{z_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
can be bounded from above by

\displaystyle z_{n}\leq y_{n}(1+\mathbb{G}_{\beta}[Q_{A}\Gamma(\beta)],1\leq n\leq N,

where
\Gamma(x)=2\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{2x-1}e^{-t^{2}}dt
is the
\Gamma
-function and
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{\Gamma(\beta k+1)}
with
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
23
]

Let
\left\{y_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions in
L^{1}[a,b]
satisfying equation

\displaystyle y_{n+1}(t)\leq\alpha+\beta\int_{a}^{t}y_{n}(s)ds,\quad\forall t\in[a,b],

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are constants. If
y_{1}(t)\equiv c

\displaystyle y_{n+1}(t)\leq\alpha e^{\beta(t-a)}+c\frac{\beta^{n}(t-a)^{n}}{n!}.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any fixed
x\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if
u\in U[a,b]
, then the following SVI

\displaystyle\langle F(t,\omega,x(t,\omega),u(t,\omega)),v-u(t,\omega)\rangle\geq 0,,\quad\forall v\in K,a.e.\;t\in[a,b],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega

(2.1)

is equivalent to that, for all
v^{\prime}\in U[a,b]
with
t\in(0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\tilde{F}(x,u),v^{\prime}-u\right\rangle_{H_{[a,b]}}\geq 0,

(2.2)

where

\tilde{F}:L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\times U[a,b]\to L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})

is defined by

\tilde{F}(x,u)(s,\omega):=F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega))

for all
(x,u)\in L^{2}_{ad}([a,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\times U[a,b]
, for all
s\in[a,b]
, and all
\omega\in\Omega
.

Proof.

We first show that (
2.1
) leads to (
2.2
). If (
2.2
) false, then there exists a
v_{0}\in U[a,b]
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{a}^{b}\langle F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega)),v_{0}(s,\omega)-u(s,\omega)\rangle ds<0,\quad\forall(s,\omega)\in[a,b]\times\Omega,

which implies that

\displaystyle\Lambda^{0}:=\left\{(s,\omega)\in[a,b]\times\Omega:\langle F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega)),v_{0}(s,\omega)-u(s,\omega)\rangle<0\right\}\neq\emptyset

with
\int_{\Omega}\int_{a}^{b}I_{\Lambda^{0}}dsdP(\omega)>0
. From the definition of
U[a,b]
, we know
v_{0}(s,\omega)\in K
for a.e.
t\in[a,b]
and a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
.
Thus, there exists
v^{\prime}\in K
such that

\displaystyle\langle F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega)),v^{\prime}-u(s,\omega)\rangle<0,\forall(s,\omega)\in\Lambda^{0},

which contradicts with (
2.1
).

Next we prove that (
2.2
) leads to (
2.1
). On the contrary, suppose that (
2.1
) fails. Then there exists a
u_{0}\in K
and
\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\epsilon}:=\left\{(s,\omega)\in[a,b]\times\Omega:\langle F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega)),v_{0}(s,\omega)-u(s,\omega)\rangle<\epsilon\right\}

with
\int_{\Omega}\int_{a}^{b}I_{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}dsdP(\omega)>0
. Since
\langle F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega)),v_{0}(s,\omega)-u(s,\omega)\rangle
is
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-adapted, so is
I_{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}(s,\omega)
. Let

\tilde{u}(s,\omega)=u_{0}I_{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}(s,\omega)+u(s,\omega)I_{\Lambda_{\epsilon}^{c}}(s,\omega),\quad\forall(t,\omega)\in[a,b]\times\Omega.

Clearly,
\tilde{u}\in U[a,b]
. By taking
v=\tilde{u}
in (
2.2
) , one has

\displaystyle\quad\;\mathbb{E}\int_{a}^{b}\langle F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega)),\tilde{u}(s,\omega)-u(s,\omega)\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{a}^{b}I_{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}(s,\omega)\langle F(s,\omega,x(s,\omega),u(s,\omega)),u_{0}-u(s,\omega)\rangle dsdP(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq-\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\int_{a}^{b}I_{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}dsdP(\omega)<0,

which is a contradiction.

3
Existence and uniqueness of the solutions for SDVI

In this section, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the solutions for SDVI. To this end, we need the following assumption and lemmas.

Assumption 3.1
.

Suppose that there exist a few positive constants
C
,
L_{f}
,
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
,
L_{g}
,
L_{F}
with
L_{F}>C
such that

(
i
)

\|f(t,x,u)\|\leq K_{1}(1+\|x\|+\|u\|)
;

(
ii
)

\|g(t,x,u)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}}\leq K_{2}(1+\|x\|+\|u\|)
;

(
iii
)

\|f(t_{1},x_{1},u_{1})-f(t_{2},x_{2},u_{2})\|\leq L_{f}(|t_{1}-t_{2}|+\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|)
;

(
iv
)

\|g(t_{1},x_{1},u_{1})-g(t_{2},x_{2},u_{2})\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}}\leq L_{g}(|t_{1}-t_{2}|+\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|)
;

(
v
)

\|\tilde{F}(x^{\prime}_{1},u^{\prime}_{1})-\tilde{F}(x^{\prime}_{2},u^{\prime}_{2})\|_{H[0,t]}\leq L_{F}(\|x^{\prime}_{1}-x^{\prime}_{2}\|_{H[0,t]}+\|u^{\prime}_{1}-u^{\prime}_{2}\|_{H[0,t]})
;

(
vi
)

\left\langle\tilde{F}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}_{1})-\tilde{F}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}_{2}),u^{\prime}_{1}-u^{\prime}_{2}\right\rangle_{H[0,t]}\geq C\|u^{\prime}_{1}-u^{\prime}_{2}\|_{H[0,t]}^{2}
,

for all
t,t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]
, all
x,x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, all
u,u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, all
x^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2}\in L^{2}_{ad}([0,t]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, all
u^{\prime}_{1},u^{\prime}_{2}\in U[0,t]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any fixed
x\in L^{2}_{ad}([0,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

(0<b\leq T)
, if
\tilde{F}
satisfies the conditions
(v)
and
(vi)
in Assumption
3.1
, then there exists a unique
u\in U[0,b]
such that

\langle F(t,\omega,x(t,\omega),u(t,\omega)),v-u(t,\omega)\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,a.e.\;t\in[0,b],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.6
, we know that the problem is equivalent to finding a unique
u\in U[0,b]
such that

\left\langle\tilde{F}(x,u),v^{\prime}-u\right\rangle_{H_{[0,b]}}\geq 0,\quad\forall v^{\prime}\in U[0,b].

Consider the following iterative sequence:

u_{n+1}=P_{U[0,b]}[u_{n}-\rho\tilde{F}(x,u_{n})],\quad\rho>0.

By the conditions
(v)
and
(vi)
in Assumption 3.1, one has

\displaystyle\|u_{n+1}-u_{n}\|^{2}_{H_{[0,b]}}

\displaystyle=\|P_{U[0,b]}[u_{n}-\rho\tilde{F}(x,u_{n})]-P_{U[0,b]}[u_{n-1}-\rho\tilde{F}(x,u_{n-1})]\|^{2}_{H_{[0,b]}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{n}-u_{n-1}-\rho\tilde{F}(x,u_{n})+\rho\tilde{F}(x,u_{n-1})\|^{2}_{H_{[0,b]}}

\displaystyle=\|u_{n}-u_{n-1}\|^{2}_{H_{[0,b]}}+\rho^{2}\|\tilde{F}(x,u_{n-1})-\tilde{F}(x,u_{n})\|^{2}_{H_{[0,b]}}

\displaystyle\quad\>-2\rho\langle\tilde{F}(x,u_{n})-\tilde{F}(x,u_{n-1}),u_{n}-u_{n-1}\rangle_{H_{[0,b]}}

\displaystyle\leq(1-2\rho C+\rho^{2}L^{2}_{F})\|u_{n+1}-u_{n}\|^{2}_{H_{[0,b]}},

where
0<\rho<\frac{2C}{L^{2}_{F}}
, which implies that there exists a unique
u^{*}\in L^{2}_{ad}([0,b]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
u^{*}=P_{U[0,b]}[u^{*}-\rho\tilde{F}(x,u^{*})]
. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\left\langle\tilde{F}(x,u^{*}),v^{\prime}-u^{*}\right\rangle_{H_{[0,b]}}\geq 0,\quad\forall v^{\prime}\in U[0,b].

This ends the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For each

Moreover, there exists a constant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{1}(s,\omega)-u_{2}(s,\omega)\|^{2}ds\leq M^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{1}(s,\omega)-x_{2}(s,\omega)\|^{2}ds,\forall t\in[0,T].

Proof.

For each
j=1,2
, it follows from Lemma

that there exists a unique
u_{j}\in U[0,T]
such that

\langle F(s,\omega,x_{j}(s,\omega),u_{j}(s,\omega)),v-u_{j}(s,\omega)\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,a.e.\;s\in[0,T],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega.

By Lemmas

u_{j}=P_{U[0,t]}[u_{j}-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{j},u_{j})]
for all

Now the conditions

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}+\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{2})-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{1})\|^{2}_{H[0,t]}

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}_{H[0,t]}+\rho^{2}\|\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{2})-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{1})\|^{2}_{H[0,t]}

\displaystyle(1-2\rho C+\rho^{2}L^{2}_{F})\mathbb{E}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}_{H[0,t]},

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{H[0,t]}

\displaystyle\|P_{U[0,t]}[u_{1}-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{1})]-P_{U[0,t]}[u_{1}-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{1})]\|_{H[0,t]}

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{1})+\rho\tilde{F}(x_{2},u_{2})\|_{H[0,t]}

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}+\rho\tilde{F}(x_{2},u_{2})-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{2})+rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{2})-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{2},u_{2})\|_{H[0,t]}

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}+\rho\tilde{F}(x_{2},u_{2})-\rho\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{2})\|_{H[0,t]}+\rho\|\tilde{F}(x_{1},u_{2})-\tilde{F}(x_{2},u_{2})\|_{H[0,t]}

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-2\rho C+\rho^{2}L^{2}_{F}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{H[0,t]}+\rho L_{F}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{H[0,t]},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{1}(s,\omega)-u_{2}(s,\omega)\|^{2}ds\leq M^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{1}(s,\omega)-x_{2}(s,\omega)\|^{2}ds,\forall t\in[0,T],

where

∎

In the sequel, we omit
\omega

sometimes without ambiguity.

Let

\displaystyle\left\langle F(t,\omega,x(t),u(t)),v-u(t)\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,a.e.\;t\in[0,T],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1
.

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle\quad\;\mathbb{E}\|x^{(n+1)}_{t}\|^{2}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left[x_{0}^{2}+\left[\int_{0}^{T}f(t,x^{(n)}_{t},u^{(n)}_{t})dt\right]^{2}+\left[\int_{0}^{T}g(t,x^{(n)}_{t},u^{(n)}_{t})dB_{t}\right]^{2}\right].

At the end of this section, we show our main results as follows.

Theorem 4.1

Let

We first show the measurability of

0,&\quad\mbox{else}.\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &x_{h}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(\hat{s},x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s}))ds+\int_{0}^{t}g(\hat{s},x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s}))dB_{s},\\
&\left\langle F(\hat{s},\omega,x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s})),v-u_{h}(\hat{s})\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.7)

, we know that

-measurable and

Define a mapping

by

for all

, for all

, and for all
\omega\in\Omega
. Then, by (

) Lemmas
2.3
and
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle u_{h}(\hat{s})=P_{U[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}[u_{h}(\hat{s})-\rho\tilde{F}_{n}(x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s}))],

(4.8)

where

) and (

\mathcal{F}_{t_{i}}
-measurable for all

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{h}(t)\|^{2}dt<\infty
.

Next we consider
\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t)-x(t)\|^{2}
. To this end, by employing (
1.2
), one has

\displaystyle x_{h}(t_{i})=x_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}f(t_{k-1},x_{h}(t_{k-1}),u_{h}(t_{k-1}))(t_{k}-t_{k-1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mbox{}+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}g(t_{k-1},x_{h}(t_{k-1}),u_{h}(t_{k-1}))(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}}),

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t_{i})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\|x_{0}\|^{2}+3\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}f(t_{k-1},x_{h}(t_{k-1}),u_{h}(t_{k-1}))(t_{k}-t_{k-1})\right]^{2}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad+3\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}g(t_{k-1},x_{h}(t_{k-1}),u_{h}(t_{k-1}))(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}(x_{0})^{2}+3H_{1}+3H_{2},

where

\displaystyle H_{1}\leq

\displaystyle h^{2}K_{1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}(1+\|x_{h}(t_{k-1})\|+\|u_{h}(t_{k-1})\|)\right]^{2}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h^{2}K_{1}^{2}N\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{E}\left[1+\|x_{h}(t_{k-1})\|+\|u_{h}(t_{k-1})\|\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3ThK_{1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{E}[1+\|x_{h}(t_{k-1})\|^{2}+\|u_{h}(t_{k-1})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3ThK_{1}^{2}+3ThK_{1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t_{k-1})\|^{2}+3ThK_{1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{E}\|u_{h}(t_{k-1})\|^{2}

and

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3hK_{2}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{E}[1+\|x_{h}(t_{k-1})\|^{2}+\|u_{h}(t_{k-1})\|^{2}]

(4.11)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t_{i})\|^{2}\leq 3\mathbb{E}\|x_{0}\|^{2}+CT+C^{\prime}h\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t_{k-1})\|^{2},

(4.13)

where

4.14
) and Assumption

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t)-x_{h}(t_{i-1})\|^{2}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}f(t_{i-1},x_{h}(t_{i-1}),u_{h}(t_{i-1}))ds+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}g(t_{i-1},x_{h}(t_{i-1}),u_{h}(t_{i-1}))dB_{s}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}f(t_{i-1},x_{h}(t_{i-1}),u_{h}(t_{i-1}))ds\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[6K_{2}^{2}(1+\|x_{h}(t_{i-1})\|^{2}+\|u_{h}(t_{i-1})\|^{2})(t-t_{i-1})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B_{1}h^{2}+B_{2}h,

where

and

. Thus, by (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t)-x(t)\|^{2}\leq I_{1}+I_{2},

&I_{2}=2\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}(g(s,x(s),u(s))-g(\hat{s},x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s})))dB_{s}\right]^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

From (
4.15

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}(f(s,x(s),u(s))-f(\hat{s},x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s})))ds\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad+12TL_{f}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{h}(s)-x_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]+6TL_{f}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}(g(s,x(s),u(s))-g(\hat{s},x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s})))dB_{s}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 6TL_{g}^{2}h^{2}+12L_{g}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|x(s)-x_{h}(s)\|^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{\quad}+12L_{g}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{h}(s)-x_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]+6L_{g}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq 6TL_{g}^{2}h^{2}+12TL_{g}^{2}B_{1}^{2}h^{2}+12TL_{g}^{2}B_{2}h+12L_{g}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|x(s)-x_{h}(s)\|^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{\quad}+6L_{g}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right].

Combining (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t)-x(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}h^{2}+C_{2}h+C_{3}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|x(s)-x_{h}(s)\|^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\quad\;+C_{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right],

,

Finally, we estimate
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]

) and Assumption

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad\;\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|u(s)-u(\hat{s})+\rho F(s,\omega,x(s),u_{h}(\hat{s}))-\rho F(s,\omega,x(s),u(s))\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|u(s)-u(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad+\rho^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|F(s,\omega,x(s),u_{h}(\hat{s}))-F(s,\omega,x(s),u(s))\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad-2\rho\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\langle u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s}),F(s,\omega,x(s),u(s))-F(s,\omega,x(s),u_{h}(\hat{s}))\rangle ds\right]

4.20

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad\;\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})+\rho F(\hat{s},\omega,x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s}))-\rho F(s,\omega,x(s),u_{h}(\hat{s}))

\displaystyle\leq\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})+\rho F(s,\omega,x(s),u_{h}(\hat{s}))-\rho F(s,\omega,x(s),u(s))\|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad+\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|\rho F(\hat{s},\omega,x_{h}(\hat{s}),u_{h}(\hat{s}))-\rho F(s,\omega,x(s),u_{h}(\hat{s}))\|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(1-2\rho C+\rho^{2}L^{2}_{F})}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{2}\rho L_{F}h^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad+\sqrt{2}\rho L_{F}\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|x(s)-x_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds

(4.21)

\displaystyle\quad\;+\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds

4.19

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|x_{h}(t)-x(t)\|^{2}\leq C^{\prime}_{1}h^{2}+C^{\prime}_{2}h+C^{\prime}_{3}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|x(s)-x_{h}(s)\|^{2}ds\right],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)-u_{h}(\hat{s})\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4A^{2}_{F}TB_{1}+2A^{2}_{F}T)h^{2}+4A^{2}_{F}TB_{2}h+4A^{2}_{F}\|x_{h}(s)-x(s)\|_{H_{[0,T]}}^{2}

Clearly,

and
\|x_{h}-x\|_{H_{[0,T]}}\rightarrow 0

From

Now we propose the following algorithm for solving SDVI (

Algorithm 5.1
.

Step 0

Step 2

For

Z(t_{k+1})
be a standard normal random variable obeying
N(0,t_{k+1})
and

\displaystyle x_{h}(t_{k+1})=x_{h}(t_{k})+h\left[f(t_{k},x_{h}(t_{k}),u_{h}(t_{k}))\right]+\left[g(t_{k},x_{h}(t_{k}),u_{h}(t_{k}))\right]\sqrt{h}Z_{t_{k+1}}.

Compute
u_{h}(t_{k+1})\in K
satisfying the following stochastic variational inequality problem:

\displaystyle\left\langle F(t_{k+1},\omega,x_{h}(t_{k+1}),u_{h}(t_{k+1})),v-u_{h}(t_{k+1})\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega.

Step 3
:

By the recursion, for
k=0,1,2,\cdots,N-1
, we link the points of
x_{h}(t_{k})
when
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})
by the following procedure

\displaystyle x_{h}(t)=

\displaystyle x_{h}(t_{k})+\left[f(t_{k},x_{h}(t_{k}),u_{h}(t_{k}))\right](t-t_{k})

\displaystyle+\left[g(t_{k},x_{h}(t_{k}),u_{h}(t_{k}))\right]\sqrt{t-t_{k}}Z_{t_{k+1}}

and link the points of
u_{h}(t_{k})
by the piecewise constant method.

Clearly, Theorem
4.1
ensures the convergence of Euler scheme constructed by Algorithm
5.1
.

5.1
The electrical circuits with diodes

In this subsection, we consider the electrical circuits with (ideal) diodes in the stochastic environment. Following
[
34
,
20
,
14
]
, it is well known that DVIs can be used to model the electrical circuits with (ideal) diodes. Assume that
x_{1}
is the electric current across the inductor and
x_{2}
is the electric tension through the capacitor. Let
(v_{D_{i}},i_{D_{i}})
(i=1,2,3,4) denote the voltage-current pairs associated to the
i
-th diode. Setting
x(t)=(x_{1}(t),x_{2}(t))^{T}
and
u(t)=(u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t),u_{3}(t),u_{4}(t))^{T}=(i_{D_{1}},v_{D_{2}},v_{D_{3}},i_{D_{4}})^{T}
, then the electrical circuits with (ideal) diodes can be described as the following DVI (see
[
20
,
14
]
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dx(t)=[Ax(t)+Bu(t)+f(t)]dt,\quad x(0)=x_{0},\\
&\left\langle Qx(t)+Mu(t),v-u(t)\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,a.e.\;t\in[0,T],\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

where

\displaystyle A=\left(\begin{matrix}-\frac{2}{3}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{5}\end{matrix}\right),\quad B=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\frac{1}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}&0\\
1&0&0&1\end{matrix}\right),\quad f(t)=(2\sin(3t-\frac{\pi}{3}),0)^{T},

\displaystyle Q=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
1&0\\
-1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),\quad M=\left(\begin{matrix}\epsilon&0&-1&0\\
0&\epsilon&0&1\\
1&0&\epsilon&0\\
0&-1&0&\epsilon\end{matrix}\right)(\epsilon>0),

\displaystyle K=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:-10\leq y_{1},y_{2}\leq 10,\;0\leq y_{3},y_{4}\leq 20\right\},\quad x_{0}=(-1,0)^{T},\quad T=1.5.

Here the coefficient
\epsilon
describes the level of the strong monotonicity. By taking into account some stochastic environmental effects in the electrical circuits with (ideal) diodes, DVI (
5.2
) should be changed into the following SDVI:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dx(t)=[Ax(t)+Bu(t)+f(t)]dt+[Cx(t)+Du(t)+g(t)]dB_{t},\quad x(0)=x_{0},\\
&\left\langle Qx(t)+Mu(t),v-u(t)\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,\;a.e.\;t\in[0,T],\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.3)

where
C=\left(\begin{matrix}a&0\\
0&a\end{matrix}\right)
,
D=\left(\begin{matrix}b&b&b&b\\
b&b&b&b\end{matrix}\right)
,
g(t)=(c\sin(t),0)^{T}
, and
B_{t}
is a
1
-dimensional standard Brownian motion. Here the constants

are the diffusion coefficients related to
x(t)
,
u(t)

3.1
that (
5.3
) admits a unique Carathéodory solution
(x(t),u(t))
for fixed

and
\epsilon>0
. Moreover, since (
5.3
) satisfies Assumption
4.1
, we can use Algorithm
5.1
to simulate paths for (
5.3
). Especially, let

,

5.2
The collapse of the bridge

In this subsection, we consider an SDVI to model the collapse of the bridge problem in stochastic environment. The following model was introduced in
[
13
]
to describe the collapse of bridge:

\displaystyle m\ddot{x}+q(x)=g(x),\quad t\in[0,T],

(5.4)

where
m
is the mass of the bridge floor,
g
is the applied force, and

q(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\alpha x,\quad\mbox{if}\quad x\geq 0,\\
&\beta x,\quad\mbox{if}\quad x<0\end{aligned}\right.

is the restoring force for displacement in different directions. Here
\alpha
and
\beta
are Hooke’s constants for the tension and compression, respectively. From
[
16
,
4
,
3
]
, by taking into account the plasticity and white noise in (
5.4
), one has

\displaystyle m\ddot{x}+\tau\dot{x}+q(x)=g(x)+k\frac{dB_{t}}{dt},\quad t\in[0,T],

(5.5)

where
k\geq 0
is the diffusion coefficient to describe the degree of the white noise,
\tau\geq 0
is the viscous damping coefficient to describe the level of the plasticity of the bridge.
If
\alpha\geq\beta
, then

\displaystyle q(x)=\alpha x+u,

(5.6)

where
u=\max\left\{0,(\beta-\alpha)x\right\}
. Thus,

\displaystyle 0\leq u\bot u+(\alpha-\beta)x\geq 0.

(5.7)

Let
y=(y_{1},y_{2})^{T}
with
y_{1}=x
and
y_{2}=\frac{dy_{1}}{dt}
. Take
\theta\geq 0
,
\alpha=4
,
m=10
,
\beta=1
, and
T=1
. Then (
5.5
) can be changed into the following SDVI:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dy(t)=f(t,y(t),u(t))dt+g(t,y(t),u(t))dB_{t},\\
&0\leq u\bot u+3y_{1}\geq 0,\\
&y(0)=(0,\theta)^{T},\quad t\in[0,1],\end{aligned}\right.

(5.8)

where

\begin{cases}f(t,y(t),u(t))=\left(y_{2}(t),-\frac{2}{5}y_{1}(t)-\tau y_{2}(t)-\frac{1}{10}u(t)+sin(4t)\right)^{T},\\
g(t,y(t),u(t))=(0,k)^{T},\quad\tau\geq 0,\quad k\geq 0,\quad\theta\geq 0.\end{cases}

Here
\theta
is initial value of
y_{2}
which describes the initial velocity of the oscillation of the bridge.

It follows from Theorem
3.1
that (
5.8
) admits a unique Carathéodory solution
(x(t),u(t))
for fixed
\tau\geq 0
,
k\geq 0
and
\theta\geq 0
. Moreover, since (
5.8
) satisfies Assumption
4.1
, we can use Algorithm
5.1
to simulate paths for (
5.3
). Particularly, take
T=1
,
N=50(h=0.02)
,
x_{0.02}(0)=(0,\theta)^{T}
and
u_{0.02}(0)=0
in Algorithm

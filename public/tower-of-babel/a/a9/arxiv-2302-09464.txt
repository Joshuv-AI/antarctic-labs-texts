New Objects in Scattering Theory with Symmetries
Andrey S. Losev, Tim V. Sulimov

Abstract
. We consider 1D quantum scattering problem for a Hamiltonian with symmetries. We show that the proper treatment of symmetries in the spirit of homological algebra leads to new objects, generalizing the well known T- and K-matrices. Homological treatment implies that old objects and new ones are be combined in a differential. This differential arises from homotopy transfer of induced interaction and symmetries on solutions of free equations of motion. Therefore, old and new objects satisfy remarkable quadratic equations. We construct an explicit example in SUSY QM on S 1 S^{1} demonstrating nontriviality of the above relation.

1.
Introduction and Results

Consider nonrelativistic 1D quantum mechanics for a particle that has internal
degrees of freedom, e.g. spin or isospin. Its space of states is described by a
tensor product of functions on a circle or a line and a finite dimensional
vector space
I
corresponding to internal degrees of freedom. The Hamiltonian
is taken to be
H=H_{0}+V
, where
H_{0}
is the
free Hamiltonian
that
acts by
-\partial_{x}^{2}
and does not rotate the internal space
I
, and the

interaction

V
that in general rotates the space
I
. We shall use the
perturbative approach, i.e., start with a solution to
H_{0}
and consider the
corresponding solution of
H
as a series in interaction
V
. Since we will be
interested in solutions of
H_{0}
with fixed energy
E
, it is convenient to
define a shifted Hamiltonian
H_{0,E}=H_{0}-E
.

Consider the problem on a line and assume that interaction falls off fast enough
at space infinities. Define the
perturbative T-matrix
as follows. Its
matrix element between two states of the continuous spectrum

|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle,|\varphi_{\beta}\rangle\in\ker{H_{0,E}}
is equal to

(1.1)

\displaystyle T_{\beta,\alpha}=\langle\varphi_{\beta}|T|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle=\langle\varphi_{\beta}|V+VG_{\text{caus}}V+VG_{\text{caus}}VG_{\text{caus}}V+\ldots|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle,

where
G_{\text{caus}}
is the
causal Green’s function

(1.2)

G_{\text{caus}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}(E-H_{0}+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\varepsilon)^{-1}.

It is known that
T
is the perturbative solution to the Lippman-Schwinger
equation for the (nonperturbative) T-matrix
T^{\text{np}}
:

(1.3)

T^{\text{np}}=V+VG_{\text{caus}}T^{\text{np}}.

It is also well-known that all questions about scattering of particles may be
expressed through
T^{\text{np}}
(see, for example,
[
5
]
).

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a symmetry (super)algebra of
H
with generators
S_{a}
:

(1.4)

[S_{a},H]=0,\qquad\{S_{a},S_{b}\}=f_{ab}^{d}S_{d},

where
\{,\}
denotes the supercommutator.
Note, that Hamiltonian itself is also a symmetry, because
[H,H]=0
.
One is prompted to ask if the symmetries of the Hamiltonian are reflected in
any kind of symmetries of
T^{\text{np}}
, or at least in symmetries of
T
.

In this paper we will attempt to answer this question. Surprisingly, the answer
is very nontrivial. In order to formulate it we have to introduce
new
objects
that (to the best of our knowledge) have not been introduced in
scattering theory before. We will also show that they are related by quadratic
equations. In our current understanding these objects may be seen as coming from
the problem on a circle of asymptotically big radius. However, we believe that
they could be defined directly in the theory on the line, we just do not know of
such a definition yet and we are working on it.

To formulate the answer we will use
ghosts
for symmetries. The ghosts

c^{a}
are coordinates on
\mathfrak{g}
with inverted parity. In simple words it
means that if
S_{a}
is even then
c^{a}
is odd and vice versa. The ghost for the
Hamiltonian will be denoted by
c
and will be odd while the rest of the ghosts
will be denoted by
\tilde{c}^{a}
.

It turns out that
T
is just a particular component of the differential

Q_{\text{ind}}
acting on the space
\ker{H_{0,E}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[[c^{a},R^{-1}]]
.
Here
R^{-1}
is an even variable. We call it
R^{-1}
because it is related to
the problem on the circle of radius
R
which is discussed in
subsections
3.2

and
3.3
.

Appearance of the differential
Q_{\text{ind}}
is novel in the context of scattering
theory, but is natural in the context of homological algebra.

Quadratic equations are just

(1.5)

Q_{\text{ind}}^{2}=0.

Now we will proceed to details of construction of
Q
out of symmetries.
First we assume that like Hamiltonian symmetries
S_{a}
are split into two pieces:

(1.6)

S_{a}=S_{0,a}+S_{1,a}

where
S_{0,a}
is a symmetry of the free Hamiltonian and
S_{1,a}
(like
interaction of Hamiltonian) vanish at space infinities.

Then,

(1.7)

Q_{\text{ind}}=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{ab}^{d}c^{a}c^{b}\partial_{c^{d}}+c^{a}S_{a}(R^{-1})+c^{a}c^{b}S_{ab}(R^{-1})+\ldots

In the zeroth order in
R^{-1}
expansion the second term in equation above
equals to the symmetry of free Hamiltonian acting on solutions to free problem

(1.8)

Q_{\text{ind}}^{(0)}=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{ab}^{d}c^{a}c^{b}\partial_{c^{d}}+c^{a}S_{0,a}\big|_{\ker{H_{0,E}}}.

In the first order in
R^{-1}
expansion we have

(1.9)

Q_{\text{ind}}^{(1)}=c^{a}S_{a}^{(1)}+c^{a}c^{b}S_{ab}^{(1)}+\ldots=cK+\tilde{c}^{a}S_{a}^{(1)}+c^{a}c^{b}S_{ab}^{(1)}+\ldots

It is remarkable that the coefficient in front of the distinguished ghost
c
is
the well known K-matrix (see, for instance,
[
4
]
), which is related
to T-matrix by

(1.10)

K=T+\frac{{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}}{2\sqrt{E}}TK.

Example
.

Consider
V(x)=\lambda\delta(x)
. The T-matrix can be easily acquired
through any of the formulae above giving

(1.11)

T(p,q)=\frac{\lambda}{1-\frac{\lambda}{2{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa}},

which is independent of the incoming or outgoing momenta. Using (
1.10
)
we get

(1.12)

K(p,q)=\lambda.

Coincidently,
K
turned out to be equal to
V
. One can easily see that
this will not be the case in general.

The perturbative expansion for
K
can be acquired from (
1.1
) by replacing
the causal Green’s function with the standing wave one.

Other terms in the expansion of
Q_{\text{ind}}
are novel objects in scattering
theory, related to standard objects by
Q_{\text{ind}}^{2}=0
.

2.1.

In this paper we shall illustrate ideas of

where
\pi
and

are the projection and inclusion operators. Composing them

that can be written using the homotopy

(2.6)

We can now write down the central object of homotopy transfer — the

(see, for example,
[

[

]
)

is actually

, but since

we simplify the notation. Additionally,

(2.9)

(2.10)

(2.11)

S_{\text{L}}=\pi\left(S+SG_{\text{st}}V+SG_{\text{st}}VG_{\text{st}}V+\ldots\right)i,

(2.12)

S_{\text{LR}}=\pi\left(SG_{\text{st}}S+SG_{\text{st}}VG_{\text{st}}S+SG_{\text{st}}VG_{\text{st}}VG_{\text{st}}S+\ldots\right)i.

3.1.
SUSY QM in More Detail

Supersymmetric quantum mechanics can be formulated as follows

(3.1)

Q_{+}=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{W},\quad Q_{-}=(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{W})^{*},\quad H=\{Q_{+},Q_{-}\},

where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}=\psi\partial_{x}
is the de Rham differential,
\psi^{*}=-\partial_{\psi}
and
\psi
is the
Grassmann variable associated with supersymmetry (for a broad review
see
[
2
]
).

Let us write down the Hamiltonian and its symmetries explicitly:

(3.2)

H=-\partial_{x}^{2}+W^{\prime 2}-W^{\prime\prime}(1-2\psi\partial_{\psi}),

(3.3)

Q_{+}=\psi(\partial_{x}+W^{\prime}),\qquad Q_{-}=\partial_{\psi}(-\partial_{x}+W^{\prime}).

Remark
.

Both
Q_{+}
and
Q_{-}
are symmetries of the full Hamiltonian but not of
any of its subparts.

Let us choose
S=Q_{+}
. As we can see,
S_{\text{LR}}\sim\psi^{2}=0
, so
equations (
)-(
2.18
) are of no use in this case.
On the other hand, equation (
) presents an interesting symmetry of

V_{\text{ind}}
which, as we have shown earlier, is the K-matrix.

Remark
.

If we were to consider

,

would surely appear.

We shall proceed to studying this equation for a particular potential on the

and then acquire the results for the real line by taking

3.2.
Double
\delta
-potential on
S^{1}

The reason for studying the problem on a circle rather than on a line will
become apparent later. For now we say that this case is easier to understand due

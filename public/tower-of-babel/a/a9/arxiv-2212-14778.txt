Generic diagonal conic bundles revisited
Alexei N. Skorobogatov, Efthymios Sofos

Abstract
We prove a stronger form of our previous result that Schinzel’s Hypothesis holds for 100% of n n -tuples of integer polynomials satisfying the usual necessary conditions, where the primes represented by the polynomials are subject to additional constraints in terms of Legendre symbols, as well as upper and lower bounds. We establish the triviality of the Brauer group of generic diagonal conic bundles over the projective line. Finally, we give an explicit lower bound for the probability that diagonal conic bundles in certain natural families have rational points.



Introduction

n

Here we give some improvements, complements and further applications of the

In the first section we prove a stronger form of

Using this result, in Corollary

Let

.

be an algebraic closure of

Each polynomial

For distinct pairs

Write

(17)

we have compatible
specialisation maps

{\rm sp}_{\bf m}\colon{\rm{Pic}}(X)\to{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bf m})\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm sp}_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}\colon{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\overline{F}})\to{\rm{Pic}}(X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}),

see
[
H94
, §3.1, §3.4]
.
Since
X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
is rational, the map
{\rm sp}_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
is an isomorphism
by
[
H94
, Prop. 3.4.2]
.
Arguments based on Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem
(see
[
H94
, p. 240]
) imply that there is a hilbertian subset

{\mathcal{H}}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^{d+n}
such that for
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{H}
the map

{\rm sp}_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
induces isomorphisms

{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\overline{F}})^{{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{F}/F)}\tilde{\longrightarrow}{\rm{Pic}}(X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})^{{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})}\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm H}^{1}(F,{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\overline{F}}))\tilde{\longrightarrow}{\rm H}^{1}({\mathbb{Q}},{\rm{Pic}}(X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})).

Lemma
2.2
implies that for
{\bf m}\in{\mathcal{H}}
we have
{\rm H}^{1}({\mathbb{Q}},{\rm{Pic}}(X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}))=0
.
Next, we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\rm{Pic}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\rm sp}_{\bf m}}
\textstyle{{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\overline{F}})^{{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{F}/F)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\rm sp}_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bf m})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm{Pic}}(X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})^{{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})}}

The exact sequence (
18
) and Theorem
2.1
imply that the top horizontal
map is an isomorphism. It follows that for
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{H}
the bottom map is also an isomorphism.
This map fits into an exact sequence

0\to{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bf m})\to{\rm{Pic}}(X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})^{{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})}\to{\rm{Br}}({\mathbb{Q}})\to{\rm{Br}}(X_{\bf m})\to{\rm H}^{1}({\mathbb{Q}},{\rm{Pic}}(X_{{\bf m},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})),

from which we conclude that for
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{H}

the natural map
{\rm{Br}}({\mathbb{Q}})\to{\rm{Br}}(X_{\bf m})
is an isomorphism. The complement

{\mathbb{Q}}^{d+n}\setminus{\mathcal{H}}
is a thin set
[
S97
, Section 9.2, Prop. 1]
.
The classical theorem of S.D. Cohen (see, e.g.
[
S97
, Section 13.1, Thm. 1]
) implies that

{\mathcal{H}}
contains 100% of points of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+n}
, when they are
ordered by height.
\Box

3
Explicit densities

Densities of Schinzel
n
-tuples

Let
\ell
be a prime. For
P(t)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}[t]
we denote by
Z_{P}(\ell)

the number of zeros of
P(t)
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\bf d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, let

\delta_{n}(\ell,{\bf d}):=\frac{\#\{{\bf P}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}[t]^{n}:{\rm{deg}}(P_{i})\leq d_{i},Z_{P_{1}\cdots P_{n}}(\ell)\neq\ell\}}{\ell^{n+d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}}}

be the density of Schinzel
n
-tuples modulo
\ell
. Write
d=d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}
.
An explicit expression for
\delta_{n}(\ell,{\bf d})
is given in
[
SS23
, Eq. (2.6)]
, but
when
\ell
is large or small compared to the degrees
d_{i}

it is easy to calculate
\delta_{n}(\ell,{\bf d})
directly. For example, if

\ell>d
, then
Z_{P_{1}\cdots P_{n}}(\ell)\neq\ell
if and only if each
P_{i}(t)

is a non-zero polynomial, thus

\delta_{n}(\ell,{\bf d})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{d_{i}+1}}\right).

Lemma 3.1

If
\ell\leq 1+\min\{d_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
, then

\delta_{n}(\ell,{\bf d})=1-\left(1-(1-1/\ell)^{n}\right)^{\ell}.

Proof.
The probability for a given polynomial of positive degree to have a non-zero value
at a given point of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
is
1-1/\ell
. The product of
n
polynomials of positive degrees
does not vanish at a given point of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
with probability
(1-1/\ell)^{n}
, so it vanishes
with probability
1-(1-1/\ell)^{n}
. If
\ell\leq d_{i}
for all
i
, these events are independent,
so the product of polynomials vanishes everywhere on
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
with probability

1-\delta_{n}(\ell,{\bf d})=\big(1-(1-1/\ell)^{n}\big)^{\ell}
.
\Box

Remark 3.2

For
\ell=2
the assumptions of the lemma are
always met, so we have

\delta_{n}(2,{\bf d})=1/2^{n-1}-1/4^{n}
.
In particular, the density of Schinzel
n
-tuples is always at most
1/2^{n-1}
, hence
goes to
0
when
n\to\infty
.

The case
n=2

Expression
[
SS23
, Eq. (2.6)]
is complicated
to evaluate if
1+\min\{d_{i}\}<\ell\leq d
.
We now give a more practical lower bound in the case
n=2
.

Lemma 3.3

We have

\delta_{2}(\ell,{\bf d})\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{1+d_{1}}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{1+d_{2}}}\right)-\frac{2^{\ell}}{\ell^{\ell}}.

Proof.

In the counting function in the definition of
\delta_{2}(\ell,{\bf d})
we can assume that neither polynomial
is identically zero, thus

\delta_{2}(\ell,{\bf d})=\frac{(\ell^{d_{1}+1}-1)(\ell^{d_{2}+1}-1)-N}{\ell^{d_{1}+d_{2}+2}},

where

N=\#\{(P_{1},P_{2})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}[t]\setminus\{0\})^{2}:{\rm{deg}}(P_{i})\leq d_{i},Z_{P_{1}P_{2}}(\ell)=\ell\}.

Since
P_{1}(t)P_{2}(t)
vanishes everywhere on
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, we see that

N\leq\sum_{S\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}\#\{(P_{1},P_{2})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}[t]\setminus\{0\})^{2}:{\rm{deg}}(P_{i})\leq d_{i},\ P_{1}(S)=P_{2}(S^{c})=0\},

where
S^{c}={\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\setminus S
.
Since
\#S\leq d_{1}
and
\#S^{c}\leq d_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle N\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\\
\ell-d_{2}\leq\#S\leq d_{1}\end{subarray}}\#\{(P_{1},P_{2})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}[t]\setminus\{0\})^{2}:{\rm{deg}}(P_{i})\leq d_{i},\ P_{1}(S)=P_{2}(S^{c})=0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\\
\ell-d_{2}\leq\#S\leq d_{1}\end{subarray}}(-1+\ell^{1+d_{1}-\#S})(-1+\ell^{1+d_{2}-\ell+\#S})\leq\ell^{2+d_{1}+d_{2}-\ell}\,2^{\ell},

which gives the desired bound.
\Box

Equation
P_{1}(t)x^{2}+P_{2}(t)y^{2}=z^{2}

The main goal of this section is to give an explicit lower bound for
the density of pairs of integer polynomials
P_{1}(t)
,
P_{2}(t)
of fixed degrees
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
,
respectively, such that the above equation is solvable in
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We use Corollary
1.3
with
n_{1}=n_{2}=1
,
n_{3}=0
,
a_{1}=a_{2}=1
, and
a_{3}=-1
.
We only need to deal with the prime
\ell=2
.
If
a
and
b
are odd integers, then the Hilbert symbol

(a,b)_{2}
equals 1 if and only if
a
or
b
is
1\bmod 4
.
Thus we need to calculate
the probability
\sigma_{2}
that a pair of polynomials
P_{1}(t),P_{2}(t)\in({\mathbb{Z}}/4)[t]

of degrees
{\rm{deg}}(P_{1})\leq d_{1}
,
{\rm{deg}}(P_{2})\leq d_{2}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

P_{1}(0)
and
P_{2}(0)
are both odd, or
P_{1}(1)
and
P_{2}(1)
are both odd, and

(b)

P_{i}(n)\equiv 1\bmod 4
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
and some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}/4
.

Condition (a) is the Schinzel condition at
2
, and condition (b) is
the triviality of the Hilbert symbol at
2
. Recall that
d=d_{1}+d_{2}
. We have

\sigma_{2}=\delta_{2}(2,{\bf d})-4^{-d-2}\#T=1/2-1/4^{2}-4^{-d-2}\#T,

where
T
is the set of pairs
P_{1}(t),P_{2}(t)\in({\mathbb{Z}}/4)[t]

of degrees
{\rm{deg}}(P_{1})\leq d_{1}
,
{\rm{deg}}(P_{2})\leq d_{2}
satisfying condition (a) and
taking only values
0
,
2
, and
3
modulo
4
.

There are
2^{e-1}
polynomials of degree at most
e
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[t]
with given values
at
0
and
1
. Each of these can be lifted to exactly
2^{e-3}
polynomials of degree at most
e

in
({\mathbb{Z}}/4)[t]
with given values at the elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
that are compatible with
the values at
0
and
1
modulo
2
. Thus there are
4^{e-2}
polynomials
P(t)\in({\mathbb{Z}}/4)[t]

of degree at most
e
with given values at the elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
such that the values at
0 and 2 have the same parity, and similarly for the values at 1 and 3.

For
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/4
we write
v_{i}=P_{1}(i)

and
v^{\prime}_{i}=P_{2}(i)
. Condition (a) is equivalent to the condition that
v_{0}v^{\prime}_{0}
is odd
or
v_{1}v^{\prime}_{1}
is odd.
We obtain that
\#T
is the product of
4^{d-4}
and the number of

(v_{j})\in\{0,2,3\}^{4}
,
(v^{\prime}_{j})\in\{0,2,3\}^{4}
such that

v_{0}v^{\prime}_{0}\text{ or }v_{1}v^{\prime}_{1}\,\text{is odd, and}\,v_{0}-v_{2},v_{1}-v_{3},v^{\prime}_{0}-v^{\prime}_{2},v_{1}^{\prime}-v_{3}^{\prime}\ \text{are even}.

Since
2\mid v_{0}-v_{2}
, the values of
(v_{0},v_{2})
can only be

(0,0),(0,2),(2,0),(2,2),(3,3).

(19)

The same can be said about
(v_{1},v_{3}),(v^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{2}),(v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{3})
.
If
v_{0}v^{\prime}_{0}
is odd, then
v_{0}=v_{2}=v^{\prime}_{0}=v^{\prime}_{2}=3
.
Then there are 25 possibilities for
(v_{1},v_{3},v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{3})
since both
(v_{1},v_{3})
and
(v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{3})
can be chosen arbitrarily from the list in (
19
).
A similar counting gives another
25
cases when
v_{1}v^{\prime}_{1}
is odd.
We have counted twice the cases where both
v_{0}v^{\prime}_{0}
and
v_{1}v^{\prime}_{1}
are odd.
This implies that all
v_{i}
and
v^{\prime}_{i}
equal 3, so there is in fact only one such case.
We obtain
\#T=25+25-1=49
, and hence

\sigma_{2}=1/2-1/4^{2}-\frac{49}{4^{6}}=\frac{1743}{4096}=42.553\ldots.

Evaluating the product
\sigma_{2}\prod_{\ell\geq 3}\delta_{2}(\ell,{\bf d})
using Lemmas

3.1
and
3.3
gives the following bound.

Proposition 3.4

Let
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
be positive integers. The density of pairs of integer polynomials
P_{1}(t)
,
P_{2}(t)
of degrees
{\rm{deg}}(P_{1})=d_{1}
and
{\rm{deg}}(P_{2})=d_{2}
with positive leading
coefficients, ordered by height,
such that the equation

P_{1}(t)x^{2}+P_{2}(t)y^{2}=z^{2}

has a solution
(x,y,z,t)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}
with
\max\{x,y,z,t\}\leq(\log|\mathbf{P}|)^{3\max\{d_{i}\}}|\mathbf{P}|
, is at least

\frac{1743}{4096}\prod_{3\leq\ell\leq 1+\min d_{i}}\left(1-\left(\frac{2\ell-1}{\ell^{2}}\right)^{\ell}\right)\prod_{\ell>1+\min d_{i}}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{1+d_{1}}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{1+d_{2}}}\right)+O\left(\frac{2^{\ell}}{\ell^{\ell}}\right)\right).

When
\min\{d_{i}\}\to\infty
this product becomes asymptotic to

\mathcal{P}:=\frac{1743}{4096}\prod_{\text{prime }\ell\geq 3}\left(1-\left(2/\ell-1/\ell^{2}\right)^{\ell}\right).

This is strictly exceeding
0.35
. Verifying
\mathcal{P}>0.35
with any
computer package will lead to troubles related to accumulating
rounding errors owing to
(2/\ell-1/\ell^{2})^{\ell}
. Thus, it is better to use the bound

\left(2/\ell-1/\ell^{2}\right)^{\ell}<(2/\ell)^{\ell}\leq 1/\ell^{4},\quad\text{for}\ \ \ell\geq 17,

which implies that

\mathcal{P}_{0}\prod_{\text{prime }\ell\geq 17}\left(1-1/\ell^{4}\right)<\mathcal{P}<\mathcal{P}_{0},

where

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\frac{1743}{4096}\prod_{\text{prime }\ell\in[3,13]}\left(1-\left(2/\ell-1/\ell^{2}\right)^{\ell}\right).

We can easily calculate that

\prod_{\ell\geq 17}(1-1/\ell^{4})=0.9999723\ldots\text{ and }\mathcal{P}_{0}=0.3504\ldots,

therefore, we have
0.3503<\mathcal{P}<0.3504
.

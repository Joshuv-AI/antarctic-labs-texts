The Haagerup property and actions on von Neumann algebras
Thiebout Delabie, Alexandre Zumbrunnen

Abstract
In Chapter 2 2 of ”Groups with the Haagerup Property” [ CCJ+12 ] , Jolissaint gives on the one hand a characterization of the Haagerup property in terms of strongly mixing actions on standard probability spaces; on the other hand he gives a noncommutative analogue of this result in terms of actions on factors. In the recent paper ”A new characterization of the Haagerup property by actions on infinite measure spaces” [ DJZ20 ] , the authors give a characterization of the Haagerup property but this time dealing with C 0 C_{0} -actions on infinite measure spaces. Following the spirit of this section, we give a noncommutative analogue in terms of C 0 C_{0} -actions on von Neumann algebras. Next we discuss some natural questions which remained open around C 0 C_{0} -dynamical systems. In particular we give examples of C 0 C_{0} -dynamical systems for groups acting properly on trees. Finally, we give a positive answer to the question of the ergodicity of such systems for non-periodic groups.



1
Introduction

The Haagerup property is a property of topological groups that has many different characterizations. Throughout this article,
G
denotes a locally compact, second countable group which is assumed non-compact because the compact case is not relevant to this property
[
CCJ+12
]
.
Haagerup defined what is now called the H property in
[
Haa78
]
: motivated by the characterizations of nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras and injective von Neumann algebras in terms of existence of completely positive linear maps, and aiming to prove that complete positivity is essential in these results.

Another characterization, first called a-
(\operatorname{T})
-menability, was defined by Gromov in
[
Gro88
]
, as a strong negation for property
(\operatorname{T})
.
It was later shown that these properties are equivalent.
For more information we refer to
[
CCJ+12
]
.

Recall that
G
has the Haagerup property if and only if there exists a sequence of normalized, positive definite functions
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
on
G
such that
\varphi_{n}
tends to the constant function
1
uniformly on compact subsets of
G
as
n\to\infty
, and each
\varphi_{n}\in C_{0}(G)
. In turn,
G
is amenable if and only if each
\varphi_{n}
can be chosen with compact support. Hence the Haagerup property is a weaker property than amenability, which itself generalizes both abelian and finite groups. Even though, there exists groups with the Haagerup property that are not amenable, free groups for example, there are a lot of properties of amenable groups that still hold for groups with the Haagerup property.
For example, groups with the Haagerup property satisfy the Baum-Connes conjecture
[
HK01
]
.

As von Neumann algebras are often seen as a non commutative version of measure spaces, since the space
\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
is a von Neumann algebra for every measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
, let us consider the following characterization of the Haagerup property :

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 2.1.3,
[
CCJ+12
]
)
.

A locally compact second countable group has the Haagerup property if and only if it has a measure preserving action on a standard probability space that is strongly mixing and has a nontrivial asymptotically invariant sequence.

This can be generalized to include the non-commutative case.
This is done in Chapter 2 of
[
CCJ+12
]
, where theorem 2.1.5 states the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable group. If there exists an action of
G
on a von Neumann algebra
N
with a faithful normal state
\varphi
that is
G
-invariant, such that the action is strongly mixing and has a nontrivial asymptotically invariant sequence, then
G
has the Haagerup property.

Conversely, if
G
has the Haagerup property, then it has such an action on the following von Neumann algebras:

1.

N=R
(where
R
is the hyperfinite
II
1
factor);

2.

N=R\otimes\operatorname{B}(\ell^{2})
;

3.

N=R_{\lambda}
(where
R_{\lambda}
is the Powers factor).

Note that in the list above we have von Neumann algebras of types II
1
, II
∞
and III.

Similarly, we can consider Theorem 1.5 in
[
DJZ20
]
, which states the following (will be specified in
Theorem

2.2
) :

Theorem 1.3
.

A locally compact second countable group has the Haagerup property if and only if it has a measure preserving action on a measure space that is
C_{0}
and has an almost invariant sequence.

In this article we provide a new characterization of the Haagerup property that is the non commutative version of the characterization in
Theorem

1.3
.
We prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable group and let
N
be a von Neumann algebra. If there exists an action of
G
on
N
that is
C_{0}
and has an almost invariant sequence, then
G
has the Haagerup property.
Conversely, if
G
has the Haagerup property, then there exists von Neumann algebras
N
of every type for which there exists an action of
G
on
N
that is
C_{0}
and has an almost invariant sequence.

In section
2
we show how
Theorem

1.3
translates to the language of von Neumann algebras. That is, we show that a group has the Haagerup property if and only if it has an action on
\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
that is
C_{0}
and has almost invariant vectors.
We also show that the existence of such an action implies the Haagerup property for any von Neumann algebra
N
.

In section
3
we show that such an action exists for every group with the Haagerup property and every von Neumann algebra listed below:

1.

N=\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
(where
(\Omega,\mu)
is the measure space in
Theorem

1.3
);

2.

N=\operatorname{B}(\operatorname{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mu))
(see
Proposition

3.3
);

3.

When
G
admits a discrete subgroup
\Gamma
satisfying the hypothesis of
Proposition

3.10
,

N=\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\rtimes\Gamma;

4.

When
G
is unimodular,
N=\operatorname{B}(\operatorname{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mu))\rtimes G
(see
Proposition

3.11
);

5.

Take
R_{\lambda}
, the Powers factor,
N=\operatorname{B}(\operatorname{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mu))\otimes R_{\lambda}
(see
Proposition

3.13
).

In the last section, we discuss some natural questions which remained open related to
Theorem

1.3
. When
G
is amenable, then

has an invariant mean and it suffices to consider the regular representation of

What can be an explicit example of such a dynamical system

In the case of the free group on two generators, we have :

Example 1.6
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{F}_{2}\curvearrowright T_{4}

(\Omega(T_{4})\times\mathbb{R},\mu\otimes\lambda),

where

The second topic which is relevant in this context is the ergodicity of the

-dynamical system.

Let us recall that a locally compact group is periodic if every cyclic subgroup is relatively compact. So a locally compact group is non-periodic if and only if it contains a discrete subgroup isomorphic to

2
From actions on von Neumann algebras to the Haagerup property

Throughout this section,
G
denotes a non-compact locally compact, second countable group. In this article we will use two characterizations of the Haagerup property. For more informations on the Haagerup property we refer to
[
CCJ+12
]
. The first characterization comes from
[
Gro88
]
section
4.5.C
:

Definition 2.1
.

G
has the Haagerup property if and only if there exists a
C_{0}
unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of
G
, that is, all the matrix coefficients
\varphi_{\xi,\eta}:g\mapsto\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle
belong to
C_{0}(G)
, which weakly contains the trivial representation
1_{G}
.

The second characterization was introduced in
[
DJZ20
]
, which contains a dynamical characterization of the Haagerup property in terms of actions on infinite measure spaces.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact, second countable group. Then it has the Haagerup property if and only if it admits an action by Borel automorphisms on a measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
such that
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite,
G
-invariant and satisfies the following two additional properties:

1.

for all measurable subsets
A,B\subset\Omega
such that
0<\mu(A),\mu(B)<\infty
, the function
g\mapsto\mu(gA\cap B)
is
C_{0}
, i.e.
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mu,G)
is a
C_{0}
-dynamical system;

2.

There exists a sequence of subsets
(A_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{B}
with measure
\mu(A_{n})=1
for all
n
such that for all compact subsets
K
of
G
we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{g\in K}\mu(gA_{n}\Delta A_{n})=0.

(1)

Note that for the second condition one has the equality
\mu(gA_{n}\Delta A_{n})=\|\pi_{\Omega}(g)\chi_{A_{n}}-\chi_{A_{n}}\|^{2}
. Hence, the second condition is equivalent to the existence of such sets
A_{n}
with

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{g\in K}\langle\pi_{\Omega}(g)\chi_{A_{n}},\chi_{A_{n}}\rangle=1.

Now,
Theorem

2.2
is equivalent to Theorem 1.5 of
[
DJZ20
]
, as the vectors
\xi_{n}
in the proof are indicator functions, see
[
DJZ20
]
on bottom of page 11.

Theorem
2.2
can be expressed in terms of actions on the commutative von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it turns out that this theorem has a noncommutative analogue. This section is devoted to state it and prove how the noncommutative analogue implies the Haagerup property. In order to translate theorem
2.2
in terms of von Neumann algebras, we need some preliminaries.

Let
N
be a von Neumann algebra with separable predual, and let
\varphi
be a
faithful normal semifinite weight
on
N
. A weight on
N
is the linear extension of a map
\varphi:N^{+}\to[0,+\infty]
satisfying the following conditions:

\varphi(x+y)=\varphi(x)+\varphi(y),\quad x,y\in N^{+};\quad\varphi(\lambda x)=\lambda\varphi(x)\;\lambda\geq 0.

It is said to be semifinite if

\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}=\{x\in N^{+}:\varphi(x)<\infty\}

generates
N
and faithful if
\varphi(x)\neq 0
for every non-zero
x\in N^{+}
. For more details we refer to
[
Tak13
]
. Let

\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}=\left\{x\in N:\;\varphi(x^{*}x)<\infty\right\},

and we denote
L^{2}(N,\varphi)
the completion of
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
with respect to the norm

\|x\|_{\varphi}^{2}=\varphi(x^{*}x)\quad x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}.

Thus by extending the left multiplication on
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
we obtain an action of
N
on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(N,\varphi)
. We will suppose that
L^{2}(N,\varphi)
is separable. Moreover, we want an action
\alpha\colon G\to\text{Aut}(N)
such that its extension to
\operatorname{L}^{2}(N,\varphi)
is a unitary representation. This prerequisite is satisfied with a few simple assumptions:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G
be an l.c.s.c. group with an action
\alpha
on a von Neumann algebra
N
.
The action
\alpha
extends to a unitary representation on
\operatorname{L}^{2}(N,\varphi)
if and only if
\alpha
preserves the weight
\varphi
, that is
\varphi\circ\alpha_{g}=\varphi
, and for every
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
we have that the maps
g\mapsto\varphi(x^{*}\alpha_{g}(x))
are measurable.

Proof.

It is clear that the given action extends to a unitary representation on
L^{2}(N,\varphi)
. This follows from the fact that
G
acts by isometries on
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
and then it suffices to consider their extension. In other words, to obtain an unitary representation it suffices to set for every
g\in G

\pi(g)(x)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{g}(x_{n})\quad x\in L^{2}(N,\varphi),

where
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
is any sequence such that
\|x_{n}-x\|_{\varphi}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.
The strong continuity of
\pi
follows from Lemma
A.6.2
on page
309
of
[
BdLHV08
]
. Indeed we assumed that
L^{2}(N,\varphi)
is separable and the following sequence of functions defined by

f_{n}\colon G\to\mathbb{C}\colon g\mapsto\langle\pi(g)x_{n},x_{n}\rangle_{\varphi}=\varphi(x_{n}^{*}\alpha_{g}(x_{n}))

where
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, is a sequence of measurable functions. Therefore for every
\xi\in L^{2}(N,\varphi)
fixed, we have that the map
g\mapsto\langle\pi(g)\xi,\xi\rangle_{\varphi}
is measurable as it is the pointwise limit of the sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
.

Next, we show the converse. Note that
\varphi(x^{*}\alpha_{g}(x))=\langle\pi_{g}(x),x\rangle
for every
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
. As
\pi
is strongly continuous, we have that the maps
g\mapsto\varphi(x^{*}\alpha_{g}(x))
are continuous and hence measurable. So, it suffices to show that
\alpha
preserves the weight
\varphi
. Suppose it is not preserved. As it is the linear extension of a map on the positive elements of
N
, we can take
x\in N
and
g\in G
such that
\varphi(x^{*}x)\neq\varphi\circ\alpha_{g}(x^{*}x)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\varphi(x^{*}x)
is finite. However, that implies that
\|x\|_{\varphi}^{2}\neq\varphi\circ\alpha_{g}(x^{*}x)=\|\alpha_{g}(x)\|_{\varphi}^{2}
, which contradicts the fact that
\pi
is unitary.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G
be an l.c.s.c. group with an action
\alpha
on a von Neumann algebra
N
.
The action
\alpha
is
C_{0}
for
\varphi
if its extension to
\operatorname{L}^{2}(N,\varphi)
is a unitary representation and

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(\alpha_{g}(x)y)=0\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}.

(2)

A sequence of projections
(e_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
is said to be an almost invariant sequence for
\alpha
and
\varphi
if
\|e_{k}\|_{\varphi}=1
for all
k
and

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{g\in K}\|\alpha_{g}(e_{k})-e_{k}\|_{\varphi}=0,

(3)

for all compact subsets
K
of
G
.

Next we show that if
\alpha
is
C_{0}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
contains an almost invariant sequence, then
G
has the Haagerup property.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that there exists an action
\alpha:G\to\text{Aut}(N)
which is
C_{0}
for
\varphi
and that
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
contains an almost invariant sequence for
\alpha
and
\varphi
. Then the representation defined in
Proposition

2.3
weakly contains the trivial representation of
G
and is
C_{0}
. In particular
G
has the Haagerup property.

Proof.

The set
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
is total in
L^{2}(N,\varphi)
by construction, and for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}

\langle\pi(g)x,y\rangle_{\varphi}=\varphi(y^{*}\alpha_{g}(x))\rightarrow 0,

as
g\rightarrow\infty
. This proves that
\pi
is of class
C_{0}
. Moreover,
1_{G}\prec\pi
by the existence of an almost invariant sequence.
∎

We can now translate theorem
2.2
in terms of actions on von Neumann algebras.

Corollary 2.6
.

G
has the Haagerup property if and only if there exists a commutative algebra
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
with its canonical semifinite, but infinite, trace
\tau
and an action
\alpha
of
G
on this von Neumann algebra which is
C_{0}
for
\tau
and contains an almost invariant sequence for
\alpha
and
\tau
.

Proof.

If
G
has such an action on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then
G
has the Haagerup property by Theorem
2.5
. Now, if
G
has the Haagerup property, then by theorem
2.2
there exists a
C_{0}
dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mu,G)
which almost has invariant vectors.
Now take the commutative von Neumann algebra
N=L^{\infty}(\Omega)
endowed with the canonical trace given by
\tau=\int_{\Omega}d\mu
. Then
L^{2}(N,\tau)=L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is separable as
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite. The measurable action of
G
on
N
is given by the permutation representation
\pi_{\Omega}
which is invariant for
\tau
as
\mu
is
G
-invariant. Furthermore the condition
(1)
in the theorem
2.2
implies that the function
g\mapsto\tau(x^{*}\alpha_{g}(x))
is measurable for every
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tau}
fixed, as well as the action is
C_{0}
for
\tau
. Finally from the family
(A_{n})
we consider the associated sequence of unit vectors
\xi_{n}=\chi_{A_{n}}
in
N
. As it satisfies the property (
1
), then this sequence is an almost invariant sequence for
\alpha
and
\tau
.
∎

In the next section we construct such actions on noncommutative von Neumann algebras.

3
Constructing actions on von Neumann Algebras

In this section
G
will denote a locally compact, second countable group with the Haagerup property. This section consists in three parts. First, we begin by creating an action on the
{{I}}_{\infty}
factor
\operatorname{B}(\mathcal{H})
endowed with the canonical trace
\operatorname{Tr}
and we prove this action is
C_{0}
for
\operatorname{Tr}
and there is an almost invariant sequence.
Next, we give some actions on crossed products both on
N\rtimes_{\alpha}G
and on von Neumann algebras of the form
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma
, with
\Gamma
a discrete group. Finally we investigate some actions on tensor products.

3.1
Action on the bounded operators of a Hilbert space

Let
G
be a locally compact, second countable group with the Haagerup property. Then using definition
2.1
there exists a
C_{0}
representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of
G
which weakly contains the trivial representation. Let us recall that we can assume without lost of generality that
\mathcal{H}
is separable. Note that we can use
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\Omega)
as in Theorem
2.2
, with the permutation representation.

Thus consider the
{{I}}_{\infty}
factor
N=\operatorname{B}(\mathcal{H})
endowed with the canonical trace

\operatorname{Tr}(T)=\sum_{n}\langle Te_{n},e_{n}\rangle,

where
(e_{n})_{n}
is a Hilbert basis of
\mathcal{H}
, and
T\in\operatorname{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\alpha
be the action of
G
on
N
given by

\alpha_{g}(T)=\pi(g)T\pi(g)^{*}.

Then we have that
\operatorname{Tr}
is
\alpha
-invariant

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_{g}(T))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\langle\pi(g)T\pi(g)^{*}e_{n},e_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\langle T\pi(g^{-1})e_{n},\pi(g^{-1})e_{n}\rangle.

The trace being invariant by change of basis, therefore we have
\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_{g}(T))=\operatorname{Tr}(T).

The following result can be deduced from
[
BdLHV08
]
page 294.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space. Then the following map
\Psi
is an isomorphism of Hilbert spaces from
\mathcal{H}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}}
to
\operatorname{L}^{2}(\operatorname{B}(\mathcal{H}),\operatorname{Tr})
defined by

\Psi(\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime})(\eta)=\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\xi^{\prime}.

We are grateful to Alain Valette for the following remark. With
Proposition

3.1
in mind, it is then easy to see that the tensor product
\pi\otimes\overline{\pi}
is unitary equivalent to the adjoint representation of
\pi
. Therefore
Proposition

3.3
follows from two remarks:

1.

Since
\pi
is
C_{0}
, then
\pi\otimes\overline{\pi}
is
C_{0}
.

2.

Since
\pi
and
\overline{\pi}
admit almost invariant vectors, then
\pi\otimes\overline{\pi}
also.

Nevertheless for the sake of completeness we give a proof of
Proposition

3.3
which has the advantage to construct ”by hand” an almost invariant sequence for the adjoint action of
G
. Moreover, it will complete and justify remark
3.4
.

It still remains to prove that the action
\alpha
is a measurable action.

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix
T\in\mathfrak{n}_{\operatorname{Tr}}
. Then the map form
G
to
\mathbb{C}
defined by
g\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(T^{*}\alpha_{g}(T))
is measurable.

Proof.

As we have

g\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(T^{*}\alpha_{g}(T))=\sum_{k}\langle\pi(g)T\pi(g)^{*}e_{k},Te_{k}\rangle,

it suffices to show that the maps
f_{k}\colon g\mapsto\langle\pi(g)T\pi(g)^{*}e_{k},Te_{k}\rangle
are measurable for all
k
. Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have:

\displaystyle|f_{k}(g)-f_{k}(e)|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{k}(g)-\langle\pi(g)Te_{k},Te_{k}\rangle|+|\langle\pi(g)Te_{k},Te_{k}\rangle-\langle Te_{k},Te_{k}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi(g)\|\|T\|\|(\pi(g)^{*}-\text{Id})e_{k}\|\|Te_{k}\|+|\langle\pi(g)Te_{k},Te_{k}\rangle-\langle Te_{k},Te_{k}\rangle|.

So, as
\text{SOT}\lim_{g\rightarrow e}\pi(g)=\text{Id}
, then
f_{k}(g)\rightarrow_{g\rightarrow e}f_{k}(e)
. Thus for every
k
we have that
f_{k}
is continuous. In particular
f_{k}
is measurable for every
k
.
Hence, the map
g\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(T^{*}\alpha_{g}(T))
is measurable as it is the simple limit of measurable functions.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be an l.c.s.c. group with the Haagerup property. Then there exists an action of
G
on
(\operatorname{B}(\mathcal{H}),\operatorname{Tr})
, which is
C_{0}
for the trace
\operatorname{Tr}
and it has an almost invariant sequence.

Proof.

We begin to show the existence of a sequence of almost invariant projections. As
G
has the Haagerup property, there exists, by
Definition

2.1
, a sequence of unit vectors
(\xi_{n})_{n}
in
\mathcal{H}
such that for every compact sets
K
of
G
:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi_{n}-\xi_{n}\|=0.

Set
P_{n}=\Psi(\xi_{n}\otimes\xi_{n})
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(P_{n}^{*}P_{n})=\sum_{k}\langle P_{n}e_{k},P_{n}e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\langle\langle e_{k},\xi_{n}\rangle\xi_{n},\langle e_{k},\xi_{n}\rangle\xi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}|\langle e_{k},\xi_{n}\rangle|^{2}=\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}=1,

where we used Parseval’s identity. Moreover

\displaystyle\alpha_{g}(P_{n})(\eta)

\displaystyle=\pi(g)P_{n}\pi(g^{-1})(\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle\eta,\pi(g)\xi_{n}\rangle\pi(g)\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=\Psi(\pi(g)\xi_{n}\otimes\pi(g)\xi_{n})(\eta).

Therefore:

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{g}(P_{n})-P_{n}\|_{\operatorname{Tr}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\pi(g)\xi_{n}\otimes\pi(g)\xi_{n}-\xi_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\|\pi(g)\xi_{n}\|^{4}-2\text{Re}\left(\langle\pi(g)\xi_{n},\xi_{n}\rangle^{2}\right)+\|\xi_{n}\|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(1-\text{Re}\left(\langle\pi(g)\xi_{n},\xi_{n}\rangle^{2}\right)\right).

Hence for every compact sets
K
of
G
we have:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{g\in K}\|\alpha_{g}(P_{n})-P_{n}\|_{\operatorname{Tr}}=0.

It remains to show that
\alpha
is
C_{0}
for
\operatorname{Tr}
. First take elements of the form
\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{H}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{H}
and consider their image by
\Psi
denoted
T_{\xi_{1},\eta_{1}},T_{\xi_{2},\eta_{2}}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\operatorname{Tr}}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(T_{\xi_{1},\eta_{1}}^{*}\alpha_{g}(T_{\xi_{2},\eta_{2}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\langle\pi(g)T_{\xi_{2},\eta_{2}}\pi(g)^{*}e_{k},T_{\xi_{1},\eta_{1}}e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\langle\pi(g)\langle\pi(g^{-1})e_{k},\xi_{2}\rangle\eta_{2},\langle e_{k},\xi_{1}\rangle\eta_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi(g)\eta_{2},\eta_{1}\rangle\sum_{k}\langle\pi(g^{-1})e_{k},\xi_{2}\rangle\langle e_{k},\xi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\pi(g)\eta_{2},\eta_{1}\rangle\|\xi_{1}\|\|\pi(g)\xi_{2}\|.

As
\pi
is a unitary
C_{0}
-representation, then we have

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Tr}(T_{\xi_{1},\eta_{1}}^{*}\alpha_{g}(T_{\xi_{2},\eta_{2}}))=0.

Now take
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\operatorname{Tr}}
. By Proposition
3.1
there exists
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{H}
which represent
T_{1},T_{2}
. Moreover for
\varepsilon>0
fixed, there exists sequences
(\xi_{n}),(\eta_{n})\subset\mathcal{H}
such that:

1.

\|\xi-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\|\leq\varepsilon;

2.

\|\eta-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\eta_{n}\|\leq\varepsilon.

Thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(T_{1}^{*}\alpha_{g}(T_{2}))

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi\left(\xi-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\right),\alpha_{g}(\Psi(\eta))\rangle_{\operatorname{Tr}}+\langle\Psi\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\right),\alpha_{g}(\Psi(\eta))\rangle_{\operatorname{Tr}}

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi\left(\xi-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\right),\alpha_{g}(\Psi(\eta))\rangle_{\operatorname{Tr}}+

\displaystyle\langle\Psi\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\right),\alpha_{g}\left(\Psi\left(\eta-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\eta_{n}\right)\right)\rangle_{\operatorname{Tr}}

\displaystyle+\langle\Psi\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\right),\alpha_{g}\left(\Psi\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}e_{n}\otimes\eta_{n}\right)\right)\rangle_{\operatorname{Tr}}

Hence by Cauchy Schwarz and using that
\alpha
is
C_{0}
for the elements
T_{\xi_{i},\eta_{i}}
, there exists a compact set
K
of
G
such that for all
g\in G\setminus K
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(T_{1}^{*}\alpha_{g}(T_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|T_{2}\|_{\operatorname{Tr}}+\varepsilon\sqrt{\|T_{1}\|_{\operatorname{Tr}}+\varepsilon}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\varepsilon}{N_{\varepsilon}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(1+\|T_{2}\|_{\operatorname{Tr}}+\sqrt{\|T_{1}\|_{\operatorname{Tr}}+\varepsilon}).

∎

3.2
Actions on crossed products

We begin this subsection with a brief recall on crossed products. Let
N
be a von Neumann algebra acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group and consider
\alpha\colon\Gamma\to\text{Aut}(N)
an action of
\Gamma
on
N
. We denote by
\mathcal{K}=l^{2}(\Gamma,\mathcal{H})
the Hilbert space of all square integrable
\mathcal{H}
-valued functions. Now take the normal representation
\pi_{\alpha}
of
N
defined by

(\pi_{\alpha}(x)\xi)(s)=\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x)\xi(s),\;\xi\in\mathcal{K},\;x\in N,\;s\in\Gamma.

Moreover consider the unitary representation
\lambda
of
\Gamma
:

(\lambda(t)\xi)(s)=\xi(t^{-1}s),\;\xi\in\mathcal{K},\;s,t\in\Gamma,

which is covariant with
\pi_{\alpha}
, that is

\lambda(s)\pi_{\alpha}(x)\lambda(s)^{*}=\pi_{\alpha}\circ\alpha_{s}(x),\;x\in N,\;s\in\Gamma.

Definition 3.5
.

The von Neumann algebra generated by
\pi_{\alpha}(N)
and
\lambda(\Gamma)
on
\mathcal{K}
is called the crossed product of
N
by
\alpha
and denoted by
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma
.

To each
x\in N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma
one can associate a function
x:\Gamma\to N
with the idea that
x
is represented by
\sum\pi_{\alpha}(x(g))\lambda(g)
. It is well known that this function uniquely determines
x
(
[
Tak79
]
, p.366,
[
Ped79
]
, p.284). However the convergence of the series does not take place in any of the usual topologies on
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma
(
[
Mer85
]
).

Let
\varphi
a faithful normal semi-finite weight on
N
and consider
\hat{\varphi}
the canonical induced weight on
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma
:

\hat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x(e)),\;x\in N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma.

Furthermore we suppose that
\varphi
is
\alpha
-invariant.

Proposition 3.6

(Corollary
4
,
[
Mer85
]
)
.

For every
x\in N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma
we can find a unique
N
valued function on
\Gamma
, also denoted by

, such that

\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\pi_{\alpha}(x(s))\lambda(s)
converges in

to

.

Recall that by ”
\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\pi_{\alpha}(x(s))\lambda(s)
converges in topology
T

\{\sum_{g\in F}\pi_{\alpha}(x(g))\lambda(g)\}_{F}

on the directed set of all finite subsets
F
of
\Gamma
converges in the topology
T
. For any
x\in N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma
we denote
x_{F}=\sum_{g\in F}\pi_{\alpha}(x(g))\lambda(g)
. Then applying Proposition
3
of
[
Mer85
]
to
\hat{\varphi}\circ\pi_{\alpha}
one has:

(\hat{\varphi}(xx^{*})-\hat{\varphi}(x_{F}x_{F}^{*}))\rightarrow 0.

(4)

Lemma 3.7
.

The function
x
of
\Gamma
in
N
representing
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
is
\operatorname{L}^{2}
-square summable.

Proof.

For any finite set
F\subset\Gamma
one has :

\displaystyle\|x_{F}\|_{\hat{\varphi}}^{2}=\hat{\varphi}(x_{F}^{*}x_{F})

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}\left(\sum_{s,t\in F}\pi_{\alpha}(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s)^{*}))\lambda(s)^{*}\pi_{\alpha}(x(t))\lambda(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}\left(\sum_{s,t\in F}\pi_{\alpha}(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s)^{*}x(t)))\lambda(s^{-1}t)\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\sum_{s\in F}\alpha_{s^{-1}}(x(s)^{*}x(s))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in F}\varphi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(x(s)^{*}x(s)))

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in F}\|x(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}.

We conclude the proof using the normality of the weight and equation (
4
).
∎

Now suppose that
G
has a
C_{0}
action with an almost invariant sequence on
(N,\varphi)
, denoted
\beta
, which commutes with
\alpha

for every

,

\iota
of

\iota_{g}(x)=\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\pi_{\alpha}(\beta_{g}(x(s)))\lambda(s),\;x\in N\rtimes_{\alpha}\Gamma.

Then
\hat{\varphi}
is
\iota
-invariant as
\varphi
is
\beta
-invariant. Moreover this action is measurable in the following sense:

Proposition 3.8
.

For every
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
fixed, the map from
G
to
\mathbb{C}
defined by
g\mapsto\hat{\varphi}(x^{*}\iota_{g}(x))
is measurable.

Remark 3.9
.

To prove this proposition we need an observation. Let
F
be a finite subset of
\Gamma
and consider an element in
N
of the form

\sum_{s\in F}x(s)y(s).

Then we have that:

\varphi\left(\sum_{s\in F}x(s)y(s)\right)=\sum_{s\in F}\varphi(x(s)y(s)).

(5)

As
\varphi\left(\sum_{s\in F}x(s)y(s)\right)
can be derived from:

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\sum_{s\in F}(x(s)+y(s))^{*}(x(s)+y(s))\right),\;\varphi\left(\sum_{s\in F}y(s)^{*}y(s)\right),\;\varphi\left(\sum_{s\in F}x(s)^{*}x(s)\right),

we can prove that the real and the imaginary parts of the left hand side are equal to those in the right hand side in (
5
).

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
fixed. Then we have for any finite
F\subset\Gamma
:

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(x_{F}^{*}\iota_{g}(x_{F}))

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}\left(\sum_{s,t\in F}\pi_{\alpha}(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s)^{*}))\lambda(s)^{*}\pi_{\alpha}(\beta_{g}(x(t)))\lambda(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}\left(\sum_{s,t\in F}\pi_{\alpha}(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s)^{*})\beta_{g}(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(t))))\lambda(s^{-1}t)\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\sum_{s\in F}\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s))^{*}\beta_{g}(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s)))\right)

By the previous remark and equation (
4
), we have:

\hat{\varphi}(x^{*}\iota_{g}(x))=\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\varphi\left(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s))^{*}\beta_{g}(\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x(s)))\right).

As the map

is measurable for every

fixed, then

is a simple limit of measurable functions. Therefore this map is measurable.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
G
be an l.c.s.c. group with the Haagerup property, let
\Gamma
be a discrete group and let
N
be a von Neumann algebra, with a faithful normal semi-finite weight
\varphi
, such that
G
and
\Gamma
have commuting actions
\beta
and
\alpha

\beta
is
C_{0}
for
\varphi

C_{0}
-action on

\hat{\varphi}
.

Proof.

First we prove that the action
\iota
of
G
on

is
C_{0}
for
\hat{\varphi}
. Let

with

and

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(\iota_{g}(x)y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\varphi\left(\beta_{g}(x(s))\alpha_{s}^{-1}(y(s))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\|x(s)\|_{\varphi}\|y(s)\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\left(\|x(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|y(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}\right).

Then by Lemma
3.7
we deduce

\sum_{s\in\Gamma}\left(\|x(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|y(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}\right)<\infty.

Thus for
\varepsilon>0
fixed, there exists a finite subset
K
of
\Gamma
such that

\sum_{s\in K^{c}}\left(\|x(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|y(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}\right)<\varepsilon.

Moreover for every

, as
\beta
is a
C_{0}
-action for
\varphi
there exists a compact subset

\varphi(\beta_{g}(x(h))\alpha_{h}^{-1}(y(h)))<\frac{\varepsilon}{2|K|}\quad\forall g\in G\setminus Q_{h}.

Then set
Q=\bigcup_{h\in K}Q_{h}
which is a compact of
G
such that for all

\displaystyle|\hat{\varphi}(\iota_{g}(x)y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in K^{c}}\left(\|x(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|y(s)\|_{\varphi}^{2}\right)+\sum_{h\in K}|\varphi(\beta_{g}(x(h))\alpha_{h}^{-1}(y(h)))|

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+|K|\frac{\varepsilon}{2|K|}=\varepsilon.

Now by assumption there exists an almost invariant sequence
(e_{k})_{k\geq 1}
for
\beta
and
\varphi
. Hence considering the following sequence:

we obtain an almost invariant sequence for
\iota
and
\hat{\varphi}
as for all

\|\iota_{g}(x_{k})-x_{k}\|_{\hat{\varphi}}=\|\beta_{g}(e_{k})-e_{k}\|_{\varphi}.

∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let

.

Remark 3.12
.

Since
G
is unimodular then
\Delta\equiv 1.
However in order to emphasize where the hypothesis of unimodularity is necessary in the following proof, it was decided to start this proof as if we consider any group l.c.s.c.

Proof.

First we prove that the action
\iota
of
G
on
N\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is
C_{0}
. Let
x,y\in K(G,N)\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
and suppose without lost of generality that
\|x\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}=\|y\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}=1
. Then, recalling that
G
is first assumed to be a general l.c.s.c. group, consider:

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}((x+\iota_{g}(y))^{*}(x+\iota_{g}(y)))

\displaystyle=\varphi\left((x+\iota_{g}(y))^{*}\times(x+\iota_{g}(y))(e)\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\int(x+\iota_{g}(y))^{*}(s)\alpha_{s}((x+\iota_{g}(y))(s^{-1}))ds\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\int\Delta(s)^{-1}\alpha_{s}((x+\iota_{g}(y))(s^{-1})^{*})\alpha_{s}((x+\iota_{g}(y))(s^{-1}))ds\right)

Hence we have that it is equal to :

\varphi\left(\int\Delta(s)^{-1}\alpha_{s}(x(s^{-1})^{*}+\Delta(g)^{-1}\lambda(g)y(s^{-1})^{*})\lambda(g)^{*})\alpha_{s}(x(s^{-1})+\Delta(g)^{-1}\lambda(g)y(s^{-1})\lambda(g)^{*})ds\right).

Using the covariance relation and making the change of variables
u=s^{-1}
we obtain :

\varphi\left(\int\left[\alpha_{u^{-1}}(x(u)^{*})+\Delta(g)^{-1}\alpha_{u^{-1}g}(y(u)^{*})\right]\left[\alpha_{u^{-1}}(x(u))+\Delta(g)^{-1}\alpha_{u^{-1}g}(y(s))\right]du\right).

As
\varphi
is normal, then we have :

\displaystyle\int\|x(u)+\Delta(g)^{-1}\alpha_{g}(y(u))\|_{\varphi}^{2}du

\displaystyle=\int\|x(u)\|_{\varphi}^{2}+2\text{Re}\left(\langle x(u),\Delta(g)^{-1}\alpha_{g}(y(u))\rangle_{\varphi}\right)+\Delta(g)^{-1}\|y(u)\|_{\varphi}^{2}du.

Now when
G
is unimodular then
\Delta=1
and therefore:

\Delta(g^{-1})\int\|y(u)\|_{\varphi}^{2}du=\tilde{\varphi}(y^{*}y).

So we have that :

\tilde{\varphi}((x+\iota_{g}(y))^{*}(x+\iota_{g}(y))=\tilde{\varphi}(x^{*}x)+\tilde{\varphi}(y^{*}y)+2\Delta(g)^{-1}\text{Re}\left(\int\varphi(x(u)^{*}\alpha_{g}(y(u)))du\right).

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have that :

\langle x(u),\alpha_{g}(y(u))\rangle_{\varphi}\leq\|x(u)\|_{\varphi}\|y(u)\|_{\varphi}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\|x(u)\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|y(u)\|_{\varphi}^{2}).

As
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
, then their respective functions are square integrable ( this is the analogue of lemma
3.7
for locally compact groups). Hence we can use the dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
x,y
have compact supports to conclude that :

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}2\Delta(g)^{-1}\text{Re}\left(\int\varphi(x(u)^{*}\alpha_{g}(y(u)))du\right)=0,

as
\Delta(g)=1
for all

. It follows that

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(x\iota_{g}(y))=0\quad\forall x,y\in K(G,N),

which proves that
\iota
is
C_{0}
for
\tilde{\varphi}
.

Now by assumption there exists an almost invariant sequence
(e_{k})_{k\geq 1}
for
\alpha
and
\varphi
. Consider a continuous function

with compact support and take

x_{n}=e_{n}f.

Then we have :

\displaystyle\|\iota_{g}(x_{n})-x_{n}\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\|\alpha_{g}(x_{n}(u))-x_{n}(u)\|_{\varphi}^{2}du

\displaystyle=\int\|\alpha_{g}(e_{n})-e_{n}\|_{\varphi}^{2}|f(u)|^{2}du.

Hence for each compact

we have :

\sup_{g\in K}\|\iota_{g}(x_{n})-x_{n}\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{2}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0.

∎

3.3
Actions on tensor products

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
G
be an l.c.s.c. group with the Haagerup property and let
N
and
M
be von Neumann algebras with faithful, normal, semi-finite weights
\varphi_{N}
and
\varphi_{M}
such that
G
has a
C_{0}
-action on
N
with an almost invariant sequence and
G
has an action on
M
that also has an almost invariant sequence and that extends to a unitary representation on
\operatorname{L}^{2}(M,\varphi_{M})
.
Then
G
has a
C_{0}
-action on
N\overline{\otimes}M
with an almost invariant sequence.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be the action of
G
on
N
and
\beta
be the action of
G
on
M
. Take
\varphi=\varphi_{N}\otimes\varphi_{M}
. By Definition 4.2 and Proposition 4.3 of
[
Tak13
]
, we know that
\varphi
is a weight on
N\overline{\otimes}M
.
We show that the action
\gamma=\alpha\otimes\beta
of
G
on
N\overline{\otimes}M
is
C_{0}
and has an almost invariant sequence.

First, note that
\operatorname{L}^{2}(N\overline{\otimes}M,\varphi_{N}\otimes\varphi_{M})=\operatorname{L}^{2}(N,\varphi_{N})\overline{\otimes}\operatorname{L}^{2}(M,\varphi_{M})
. As both the extension of
\alpha
and
\beta
to
\operatorname{L}^{2}(N,\varphi_{N})
and
\operatorname{L}^{2}(M,\varphi_{M})
respectively are unitary representation, we have that
\gamma
extends to a unitary representation as well. As
\alpha
is
C_{0}
for
\varphi_{N}
, then by proposition
2.5
its extension is
C_{0}
. Since the tensor product of a
C_{0}
-representation with any representation is
C_{0}
, then
\gamma
is
C_{0}
for
\varphi
.

Finally, by proposition
2.5
both the extension of
\alpha
and
\beta
have almost invariant vectors. Since the tensor product of representations with almost invariant vectors has almost invariant vectors, then
\gamma
has an almost invariant sequence.
∎

With this result we can construct many new examples of von Neumann algebras that have a
C_{0}
-action of
G
with an almost invariant sequence.
From
Corollary

2.6

4
Discussion on Ergodicity

Theorem
2.2
characterizes the Haagerup property of

in terms of actions on infinite measure spaces. In this section we investigate if such actions can always be taken ergodic. First we will give an another proof of this theorem using a skew product action introduce in section 2.5 in
[
MV20
]
. Via tools of
[
MV20
]
and
[

]
this new approach will allow us to assume that such dynamical system is ergodic. Secondly we give non trivial examples of such dynamical systems for groups acting properly on trees. In particular, it will give an example of a measure space with a

-action for the free group on two generators.

We begin by a brief recall of the Gaussian process. We refer to
[
AIM19
]
for more information. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real separable Hilbert space. Then it is already known that there exists a standard probability space
(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\mu)
and a linear isometry
\iota:\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\mu)
such that :

1)

For all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
,
\iota(\xi)
is a centered Gaussian random variable of variance
\|\xi\|^{2}
;

2)

The family of random variables
(\iota(\xi))_{\xi\in\mathcal{H}}
generates the
\sigma
-algebra of measurable subsets of
(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\mu)
.

Moreover this random process is unique in the following sense.
Suppose there exists another triple
(X,\nu,\iota^{\prime})
satisfying
(1)
and
(2)
, then there exists a measurable bijection
\varphi:(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\mu)\to(X,\nu)
such that
\varphi_{*}\mu=\nu
and almost everywhere

\iota^{\prime}(\xi)\circ\varphi=\iota(\xi),

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. We use the same notation as
[
MV20
]
and denote this unique Gaussian process
(\hat{\xi})_{\xi\in\mathcal{H}}
. Note that from the uniqueness of the Gaussian process, it follows that for an orthogonal transformation
U\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{H})
, there exists a measure preserving automorphism
\hat{U}
of
(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\mu)
such that

\widehat{U\xi}=\hat{\xi}\circ\hat{U}^{-1}.

Now defining a new measure on
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
by the formula
d\mu_{\eta}=\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\|\eta\|^{2}+\hat{\eta})d\mu
for a fixed vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, we can see that the random variable

\hat{\xi}-\langle\xi,\eta\rangle

has a normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\|\xi\|^{2})
with respect to
\mu_{\eta}
. Thus using uniqueness there exists a nonsingular automorphism
\hat{T}_{\eta}
of
(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\mu)
such that

\hat{\xi}\circ\hat{T}_{\eta}^{-1}=\hat{\xi}-\langle\xi,\eta\rangle,

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. We will use the notation
\hat{T}_{\eta}(\omega)=\omega+\eta
so that the intuitive formula
\langle\omega+\eta,\xi\rangle=\langle\omega,\xi\rangle+\langle\eta,\xi\rangle
holds, where
\langle\omega,\xi\rangle
denotes
\hat{\xi}(\omega)
. Then a simple computation shows that for
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{H})
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
we have

\hat{V}(\omega+\eta)=\hat{V}(\omega)+V(\eta).

Therefore we get a morphism from
\text{Isom}(\mathcal{H})
to
\text{Aut}(\hat{\mathcal{H}},[\mu])
. In particular, for every action
\alpha:G\curvearrowright\mathcal{H}
by affine isometries, we obtain a Gaussian action
\hat{\alpha}:G\curvearrowright(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\mu)
.

Now let
\pi:G\to\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal representation of
G
with the Haagerup property. Let
c\in Z^{1}(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be a
1
-cocycle which is proper and take
\alpha:G\curvearrowright\mathcal{H}
the associated proper affine isometric action. Consider the measurable space
\Omega=\hat{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathbb{R}
endowed with the measure
\nu:=\mu\otimes\lambda
, where
\lambda
denotes the Lebesgue measure. Then we define the skew product infinite measure preserving action
\beta:G\curvearrowright(\Omega,\nu)
by the formula:

\beta_{g}(\omega,t)=(\hat{\pi}(g)w,t+\langle\omega,c(g^{-1})\rangle).

(6)

First we prove that the Koopman representation associated to

is of class
C_{0}
using the following proposition taken from

]

Proposition 4.1

Let

(i)

\lim_{g\to\infty}\langle\pi(g)\xi,\xi\rangle=0
for some faithful
\xi\in L^{2}(X)^{+}

(ii)

\pi
is mixing.

Let

\|c(g_{n})\|\geq A>0\;\forall n\geq N.

Remark 4.3

4.1
Examples of dynamical systems for groups acting properly on trees

A typical example of groups which it is easy to construct a

-dynamical system with an invariant mean as in theorem

is the amenable groups. Indeed it suffices to consider the left regular representation (see proposition 1.4
[

]
for example). The advantage of using Gaussian functor to prove this theorem is that it gives us a way to make explicit such system when we are considering groups acting properly on trees. We begin by some recalls in order to fix the notation. We refer to Chapter
2
and Appendix

of
[
BdLHV08
]
for more details.

Let
T=(V,E)
a locally finite tree. We will identify
T
with its set of vertices. We denote by
\mathbb{E}
the set of oriented edges, where each geometric edge comes with two possible orientations. For
x,y\in V
, let
[x,y]
be the set of oriented edges on the unique geodesic path from
x
to
y
. We say that
e\in[x,y]
is coherent if
e
points from
x
to
y
and incoherent otherwise. There exists a unique embedding
\iota:T\to H
into an affine Hilbert space
H
such that :

1.

d(x,y)=\|\iota(x)-\iota(y)\|^{2}
;

2.

H
is the closed affine span of
\iota(T)
.

We will view
T
directly as a subset of
H
.
Suppose that
G
acts properly on
T
by automorphisms, then by the uniqueness of the embedding, the action of
G
extends uniquely to an affine isometric action
\alpha:G\curvearrowright H
with
\alpha_{g}(\iota(x))=\iota(gx).
Moreover as the action of
G
on
T
is proper, then combining proposition
8.6
and
9.3
of
[
AIM19
]
, we deduce that
\alpha
is proper. Hence we get:

Example 4.13
.

Let
G
be a group acting properly on a locally finite tree
T
.
Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright H
be the affine isometric proper action associated. Therefore

(\hat{H},\mu)=\left(\mathbb{R},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)dx\right)^{\otimes T}.

This comes from the standard construction, thanks to property
(2)
of the embedding, which can be found in
[
CCJ+12
]
Chapter
2
. Thus for the free group on two generators,
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, let
\alpha:\mathbb{F}_{2}\curvearrowright H
be the affine isometric action associated to its action on its Cayley graph. Then an example of infinite measure space as in theorem
2.2
is given by :

\left(\mathbb{R},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)dx\right)^{\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}}\otimes(\mathbb{R},\lambda).

This answers to a question from Y.Stalder.

For these groups, it is possible to give an alternative way to find such a measure space.
Consider
\Omega(T)
the compact space of all possible orientations of
T
. Recall that an orientation of
T
is a map
\omega:E(T)\to\left\{-1,1\right\}
. Then we define for every
x,y\in T
the continuous function
c(x,y)
on
\Omega(T)
by the formula:

c(x,y)(\omega)=\sum_{e\in[x,y]}\omega(e)=n_{e}-m_{e},

where
n_{e}
(respectively

) is the number of edges of
[x,y]
which is coherent (respectively incoherent) by the orientation
\omega\in\Omega(T)
. Observe that we have the cocycle relation, i.e.
c(x,y)+c(y,z)=c(x,z)
for all
x,y,z\in T
. Fix a base-point
x_{0}\in T
and define a random orientation of
T
as follows: each edge
e\in E(T)
independently is oriented towards
x_{0}
with probability
p
and away from
x_{0}
with probability

. This defines a probability measure on
\Omega(T)
denoted
\mu_{x_{0}}^{p}
.
Thus we can see
(\Omega(T),\mu_{x_{0}}^{p})=\prod_{e\in E(T_{4})}(\left\{-1,1\right\},p\delta_{1}+(1-p)\delta_{-1})
. Now we consider the cocycle

defined by:

c(g,\omega)=c(x_{0},gx_{0})(\omega).

Therefore we have
c(gh,\omega)=c(g,\omega)+c(h,g^{-1}\omega).

Finally take the skew product action of
G
on
\left(\Omega(T)\times\mathbb{R},\mu\otimes\lambda\right)
, given by :

\beta_{g}(\omega,t)=(\omega,t+c(g^{-1},\omega)).

To prove that the permutation representation is
C_{0}
, denote
X
a Bernoulli random variable with values in
\left\{-1,1\right\}
with parameter

and let
(X_{e})_{e\in E(T)}

(g_{n})
be a sequence in

As the action of

on

which is nothing but a kind of random walk on

. Put

in order to have that

can be simulated by a Markov chain, which is a transient walk. Thus :

\mathbb{P}(\lim_{n\to\infty}|S_{n}|=+\infty)=1.

Hence for almost every
\omega
, the cocycle

is proper. Then it suffices to consider for example the vector

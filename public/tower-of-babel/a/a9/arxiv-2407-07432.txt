Spectrum of group vertex magic graphs
S. Balamoorthy †, N. Kamatchi ‡, S.V. Bharanedhar †

Abstract
Let G G be a simple undirected graph and let A A be an additive Abelian group with identity 0. In this paper, we introduce the concept of group magic spectrum of a graph G G with respect to a given Abelian group A A and is defined as s ​ p ​ e ​ c ​ ( G , A ) spec(G,A) := { λ : λ \{\lambda:\lambda is a magic constant of some A A -vertex magic labeling f } f\} . In their recent work, K. M. Sabeel et al. in Australas. J. Combin. 85(1) (2023), 49-60 proved a forbidden subgraph characterization for the group vertex magic graph. In this work, we present a new method which uses minimum number of vertices required for this graph. We obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for the spectrum of a graph G G to be a subgroup when A = V 4 A=V_{4} or ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} , where p p is a prime number. Also we introduce the notion of reduced spectrum r ​ e ​ d ​ s ​ p ​ e ​ c ​ ( G , A ) redspec(G,A) and study the relation between s ​ p ​ e ​ c ​ ( G , A ) spec(G,A) and r ​ e ​ d ​ s ​ p ​ e ​ c ​ ( G , A ) redspec(G,A) . Keywords: Group vertex magic; magic spectrum; reduced graph; tensor product. AMS Subject classification: 05C25, 05C78, 05C76.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we consider finite, simple and connected graphs with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set

. For a vertex

G

Let

for any vertex

of

admits such a labeling, then it is called an

-vertex magic graph. If
G
is
A
-vertex magic for any non-trivial Abelian group

is called a group vertex magic graph. Several works have been done on A-vertex magic graphs in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
]
.

Let
H
be a graph with vertex set
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}
and let
\mathcal{F}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\}
be a family of graphs. The
H
-join operation of the graphs
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}
is denoted as
G=H[G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}]
, is obtained by replacing the vertex
v_{i}
of

G_{i}

and every vertex of

is made adjacent with every vertex of

, whenever

is adjacent to

in

and the edge set of

E(G)=\displaystyle\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}E(G_{i})\big)\cup\big(\bigcup_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(H)}\{uv:u\in V(G_{i}),v\in V(G_{j})\}\big)
(see
[
5
]
and
[

). If

It is always interesting to study graphs arising from algebraic structures. In this connection, one can see the recent survey articles

If

For given a graph

.

[

Main Results

In this section, we present a few necessary conditions for

From the definition of tensor product one can observe the following.

Observation 2
.

Let
G_{1}
and

be two simple graphs. The graphs
G_{1}
and

have odd degree vertices if and only if
G_{1}\otimes G_{2}
has odd degree vertices.

Proof.

From the definition of tensor product, we have
deg_{G_{1}\otimes G_{2}}(u,v)=deg_{G_{1}}(u)\cdot deg_{G_{2}}(v)
, where
(u,v)\in V(G_{1}\otimes G_{2})
. Hence the result follows. ∎

Observation 3
.

Let
G_{i}

(1\leq i\leq k\,;\,k\geq 2)
be simple graphs and

. Then

is Eulerian if and only if at least one of the
{G_{i}}^{\prime}s
is Eulerian.

Lemma 2

Let

-vertex magic labeling of

Assume that

Corollary 1

Let

\mu\in spec(G,A)

Let

-vertex magic graph. If

Proof.

Suppose

-vertex magic labeling of

Observation 4

Let

is not a subgroup of

.

Remark 1

For no tree

is a subgroup of

.

Theorem 2

Let

-vertex magic graph. Then the

Corollary 2

There is no graph

Theorem 3
.

Let

(1\leq i\leq k\,;\,k\geq 2)

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a
V_{4}
-vertex magic graph. Then the
spec(G,V_{4})
is a subgroup of
V_{4}
if and only if
0\in spec(G,V_{4})
.

Proof.

Clearly, it is enough to prove the sufficiency part. If
spec(G,V_{4})=\{0\},
then there is nothing to prove. Suppose
spec(G,V_{4})\neq\{0\}
, then there exists a labeling
l
with a non-zero magic constant. Assume that the magic constant of
l
is
a
.
Let
v\in V(G)
. Then
w(v)=r_{1}a+r_{2}b+r_{3}c,
where
r_{i}\in\{0,1\},
for all
i=1,2,3
. If
r_{1}a\neq 0,
then
r_{2}b+r_{3}c=0
and also
r_{1}a=a.
If
r_{1}a=0,
then
r_{2}b+r_{3}c=b+c=a
. We replace the labels
a,b,c
in

by

. Now,
w(v)=r_{1}b+r_{2}c+r_{3}a=b
. By a similar argument, we get

.
∎

Corollary 3
.

There is no graph
G,
whose

is a proper subgroup of
V_{4}
.

The following theorem shows that
spec(G,A)
is symmetric about the origin.

Theorem 5
.

If
a\in spec(G,A)
, then
-a\in spec(G,A)
.

Proof.

Assume that
l
is a
A
-vertex magic labeling of
G
with magic constant
a
. Define
l^{\prime}:V\to A\setminus\{0\}
by
l^{\prime}(v)=-l(v),
for all

. Now,

\displaystyle w(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)}l^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)}l(u)

\displaystyle=-a.

Since
v
is arbitrary, therefore,
w(v)=-a,
for all
v\in V(G)
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Thus by the above theorem to check spectrum is a subgroup it is enough to check it is closed.

Theorem 6
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
. If
a\in spec(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}\subset spec(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

Let
a
be a non-zero element such that
a\in spec(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Then there exists a labeling
l
such that the label of every vertex is a multiple of
a
and
w(v)=a
, for all
v\in V(G)
. Let
v\in V(G)
. Since
a
generates
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we have

w(v)=r_{1}a+r_{2}2a+\cdots+r_{i}ia+\cdots+r_{p-1}(p-1)a,\,0\leq r_{j}\leq deg_{G}(v),

for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
.

Hence,

w(v)=(r_{1}+2r_{2}+\cdots+ir_{i}+\cdots+(p-1)r_{p-1})a=a
.

Therefore,
(r_{1}+2r_{2}+\cdots+ir_{i}+\cdots+(p-1)r_{p-1})\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,p)
.

Hence
(r_{1}+2r_{2}+\cdots+ir_{i}+\cdots+(p-1)r_{p-1})ka=ka
, where
0<k\leq p-1
.
Now, define
l^{\prime}:V\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
by
l^{\prime}(v_{i})=kl(v_{i})({\rm mod}\,p)
. Hence we have
ka\in spec(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
∎

Theorem 10

The class of group vertex magic graphs is universal. In other words, every graph can be embedded as an induced subgraph of a group vertex magic graph.

Proof.

In the following definition we introduce the reduced spectrum of a graph

Definition 3
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
H
be its reduced graph. We say that
H
is
A^{\prime}
magic if there exists a labeling
l:V(H)\to A
such that
0
can also be used to label the vertex
[u]\in V(H)

whenever
|[u]|\geq 2
and
\displaystyle\sum_{[u]\in N_{H}([v])}l([u])=\mu
, for all
[v]\in V(H)
. The collection of all such
\mu
is called the reduced spectrum of the graph
G
with respect to the Abelian group
A
and is denoted by
redspec(G,A)
.

The following Proposition shows that to compute the spectrum of a graph it is enough to compute its reduced spectrum whenever the Abelian group has at least three elemenets.

Proposition 4
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. Then

spec(G,A)=redspec(G,A)
, where
A
has at least three elements.

Proof.

Let
a\in spec(G,A)
.
Assume that
l
is an
A
-vertex magic labeling of
G
with the magic constant
a
. Now, define
l^{\prime}:V(H)\to A
by
l^{\prime}([u])=\displaystyle\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\in V(G)}}{{v\in[u]}}}l(v)
. Then
w([u])=a
, for all
[u]\in V(H)
. Conversely, let
l^{\prime}
be a
A^{\prime}
-vertex magic labeling of the reduced graph
H
of
G
with magic constant
a
, where
a\in redspec(G,A)
. Define
l:V(G)\to A\setminus\{0\}
by if
|[v]|=1
, then
l(v)=l^{\prime}([v])
and if
|[v]|\geq 2
, then we label the vertices of the class using Lemma
1
in such a way that sum of the labels of all vertices in this equivalence class is equal to
l^{\prime}([v])
in the reduced graph
H
of G. Let
v\in V(G)
. Then

\displaystyle w(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)}l(u)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{[u]\in N_{H}([v])}l^{\prime}([u])

\displaystyle=a.

Since
v
is arbitrary, we get the required result.
∎

Lemma 4

Let

Theorem 12
.

Let
P_{k}
be the path on
k
vertices and
G=P_{k}[{K_{n_{1}}}^{c},{K_{n_{2}}}^{c},\ldots,{K_{n_{k}}}^{c}]
be a graph, where

is even. Then
0\in spec(G,A)
, where
|A|>2
if and only if
n_{j}>1
, for each

.

Proof.

For

denote the equivalence classes of

\sim_{G}
on

with
|[u_{j}]|=n_{j}

n_{k-1}>1
. Now,
v\in[u_{k-2}]

w(v)=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in[u_{k-1}]}l(u)+\sum_{u\in[u_{k-3}]}l(u)=0

n_{k-3}>1
. Continuing this argument, we get
n_{i}>1
, where

is odd and
1\leq i\leq k
. Let
v\in[u_{1}]
. Since
w(v)=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in[u_{2}]}l(u)=0
, which implies
n_{2}>1
. Continuing this argument, we get
n_{i}>1
, where

is even and

. The converse part follows from Lemma

.
∎

Theorem 14

Let

Assume that

Without loss of generality, we assume that

In our investigation, we have the following natural question: Does there exist a graph whose spectrum contains all the elements of the abelian group A except zero?. The following result answers the question affirmatively by providing infinite class of such graphs.

Theorem 17
.

Let
H
be a graph of order
k
.
Let
G=H\tilde{\circ}\wedge_{i=1}^{k}K_{n_{i}}^{c}
be a graph, where
n_{i}>1
, for all
i
. Then
spec(G,A)=A\setminus\{0\}
, where
|A|>2
.

Proof.

Let
V(G)=V(H)\cup\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(K_{n_{i}}^{c})\big)
, where
V(H)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}
and
V(K_{n_{i}}^{c})=\{v_{1}^{i},v_{2}^{i},\ldots,v_{n_{i}}^{i}\}
, for all
i
. Let
a\in A\setminus\{0\}
. Define
l:V(G)\to A\setminus\{0\}
by
l(v_{i})=a
and using Lemma
1
, we label the vertices
v_{j}^{i}
with the condition
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}l(v_{j}^{i})=-(deg_{H}(v_{i})-1)a
. Thus
w(v)=a
for all
v\in V(G)
. Since
a
is arbitrary and from observation
4
, we get the required result.
∎

4
Conclusion and scope

In this paper, we have introduced the concept of
A
- vertex magic spectrum of a graph. Furthermore, we have constructed an infinite family of graphs with spectra containing zero. Additionally, we have established sufficient conditions for the presence of
0
in
spec(G,A)
, specifically in certain product graphs. We propose the following problems for further investigation.

1) Identify family of graphs whose spectrum forms a subgroup.

2) Establish necessary conditions for a graph’s spectrum to be a subgroup.

3) Characterize graph
G
for which
spec(G,A)=A
, where
|A|\geq 3.

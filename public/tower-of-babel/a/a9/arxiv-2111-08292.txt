Large deviation principle for stochastic generalized Ginzburg-Landau equation driven by jump noise
Ran Wang, Beibei Zhang

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we establish a large deviation principle for the stochastic generalized Ginzburg-Landau equation driven by jump noise. The main difficulties come from the highly non-linear coefficient. Here we adopt a new sufficient condition for the weak convergence criteria, which is proposed by Matoussi, Sabbagh and Zhang (2021).

Keyword:
Large deviation principle; Weak convergence method; stochastic generalized Ginzburg-Landau equation; Poisson random measure.

MSC:
60H10; 60H15

1.
Introduction

Consider the following stochastic generalized Ginzburg-Landau equation (SGGLE in short) driven by jump noise:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}du^{\varepsilon}(t)=\big((1+i\alpha)\Delta u^{\varepsilon}(t)-(1-i\beta)|u^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2\sigma}u^{\varepsilon}(t)+\gamma u^{\varepsilon}(t)+F(u^{\varepsilon}(t))\big)dt\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\varepsilon\int_{\mathcal{Z}}u^{\varepsilon}(t-)g(z)\tilde{\eta}^{\varepsilon^{-1}}(dz,dt),\\
u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ t\geq 0,\,x\in\partial D,\\
u^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u(0),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ x\in D,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
D=(0,L_{1})\times(0,L_{2}),L_{1},~L_{2}>0,~i=\sqrt{-1},\gamma>0
,
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator on
D
with the Dirichlet boundary condition, the parameters
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are real-valued constants,
u
is a complex-valued scalar function. The derivative term
F
is defined by
F(u)=\lambda_{1}\cdot\nabla(|u|^{2}u)+(\lambda_{2}\cdot\nabla u)|u|^{2}
with two complex constant vectors
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
.
\eta^{\varepsilon^{-1}}
is a Poisson random measure on
[0,T]\times\mathcal{Z}
with a
\sigma
-finite intensity measure
\varepsilon^{-1}\lambda_{T}\otimes\nu
,
\lambda_{T}
is the Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]
and
\nu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\mathcal{Z}
.
\tilde{\eta}^{\varepsilon^{-1}}(ds,dz)=\eta^{\varepsilon^{-1}}(dsdz)-\varepsilon^{-1}\nu(dz)ds

is a compensated Poisson random measure.

The generalized Ginzburg-Landau equation, as a basis model of the nonlinear phenomena, is used in various areas of the physics, such as the Rayleigh-Benard convection, the Taylor-Couette flow in fluid mechanics, the drift dissipative wave in plasma physics and the turbulent flow in chemical reaction

[

For the stochastic Ginzburg-Landau equation (SGLE in short), a long list of studies on the existence and uniqueness, as well as the asymptotic behavior of solutions, have been studied, such as

This new sufficient condition is recently successfully applied to study the large deviation principle in

,

There are many results about the LDPs related to stochastic partial differential equations driven by jump noise (see, e.g.,

Recall that

For any

Let

is an

(i) for each

(ii) for disjoint

For notational simplicity, we write from now on

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}=\mathcal{M}_{FC}(\mathcal{Z}_{T}),

(2.1)

and denote by
\mathbb{P}
the probability measure induced by

on

.
Under

\nu_{T}

Denote

\mathbb{Y}=\mathcal{Z}\times[0,\infty),\ \ \mathbb{Y}_{T}=[0,T]\times\mathbb{Y},\ \ \bar{\mathbb{M}}=\mathcal{M}_{FC}(\mathbb{Y}_{T}).

(2.2)

.

. Let

2.2.
A general criterion for large deviation principle

Let
\{u^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of random variables defined on a probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

and taking values in a Polish space
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Rate function)
A function
I:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is called a rate function on

\mathcal{E}
,
if for each
M<\infty
the level set
\{y\in\mathcal{E}:I(y)\leq M\}
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 2.2
.

(Large deviation principle)
Let
I
be a rate function on
\mathcal{E}
. The sequence

\{u^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

is said to satisfy a large deviation principle (LDP) on
\mathcal{E}
with the rate function
I
if the following two
conditions
hold:

(a).

For each closed subset
F
of
\mathcal{E}
,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(u^{\varepsilon}\in F)\leq\inf_{y\in F}I(y);

(b).

For each open subset
G
of
\mathcal{E}
,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(u^{\varepsilon}\in G)\geq-\inf_{y\in G}I(y).

Define
l:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by

l(r)=r\log r-r+1,\ \ r\in[0,\infty).

For any
\psi\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}
, the quantity

\displaystyle L_{T}(\varphi)=\int_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}l(\psi(t,x,\omega))\nu_{T}(dtdx)

(2.4)

is well defined as a
[0,\infty]
-valued random variable.
Define

\displaystyle S^{N}=\Big\{\varphi:\mathcal{Z}_{T}\rightarrow[0,\infty),\,L_{T}(\varphi)\leq N\Big\}.

(2.5)

A function
\varphi\in S^{N}
can be identified with a measure
\nu_{T}^{\varphi}\in\mathbb{M}
, defined by

\displaystyle\nu_{T}^{\varphi}(A)=\int_{A}\varphi(s,x)\nu_{T}(dsdx),\ \ A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Z}_{T}).

This identification induces a topology on
S^{N}
under which
S^{N}
is a compact space. Throughout this paper we use this topology on
S^{N}
. Denote

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}:=\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}S^{N}

(2.6)

and
\bar{\mathbb{A}}^{N}:=\{\psi\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}\ \text{and}\ \psi(\omega)\in S^{N},\ \bar{\mathbb{P}}\text{-}a.s.\}
. Let
\{K_{n}\subset\mathcal{Z},\ n=1,2,\cdots\}

be an increasing sequence of compact sets such that
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n}=\mathcal{Z}
. For each
n
, let

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{b,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\psi\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}:{\rm\ for\ all\ }(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\bar{\mathbb{M}},\ n\geq\psi(t,x,\omega)\geq 1/n\ {\rm if}\ x\in K_{n}\

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm and}\ \psi(t,x,\omega)=1\ {\rm if}\ x\in K_{n}^{c}\Big\},

and let
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{b}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{b,n}
. Define

\tilde{\mathbb{A}}^{N}=\bar{\mathbb{A}}^{N}\cap\Big\{\phi:\phi\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\Big\}
.

Let

\{\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

be a family of measurable maps from
\mathbb{M}
to
\mathbb{U}
, where
\mathbb{M}
is given in (
2.1

Suppose that there exists a measurable map

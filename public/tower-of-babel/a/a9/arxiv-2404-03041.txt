1.
Introduction

The topological complexity of a configuration space
X
is a numerical invariant
\TC(X)
which appeared in topological robotics
[
Fa2
]
.
It is closely related to an old numerical invariant called the Lusternik-Schnirelmann categpory
[
LS
]
of a space
X
,
\operatorname{cat}(X)
. Since
\TC(X)
and
\operatorname{cat}(X)
are homotopy
invariant they bring numerical invariant of discrete groups defined as
\TC(\Gamma)=\TC(B\Gamma)
and
\operatorname{cat}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{cat}(B\Gamma)
where
B\Gamma=K(\Gamma,1)
is a classifying space for
the group
\Gamma
.

In 50s Eilenberg and Ganea proved
[
EG
]
that the LS-category of a group
\Gamma
coincides with its cohomological dimension
\operatorname{cat}(\Gamma)=\cd(\Gamma)
.
An algebraic description of
\TC(\Gamma)
is still missing. What is known that
\cd(\Gamma)\leq\TC(\Gamma)\leq\cd(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
. This implies in particular that

\TC(\Gamma)=\infty
for groups with torsions. It turns out that it is quite difficult to compute the topological complexity of groups
\TC(\Gamma)

[
FM
]
,
[
GLO
]
.
There are very few exact computations are known:
\TC(A)=\cd(A)
for a free abelian group
A
,
\TC(F)=2
for free non-abelian groups, and

\TC(M_{g})=4
,
g>1
for the fundamental group of orientable surfaces (all done in
[
Fa1
]
), for the fundamental group of non-orientable surfaces of genus
g>1
,
\TC(N_{g})=4

[
CV
]
(and
[
Dr1
]
for
g>3
),
\TC(\Gamma)=2\cd(\Gamma)
for torsion free hyperbolic groups
[
Dr2
]
and for certain toral relatively hyperbolic groups
[
Li
]
.

Recently we in
[
DJ
]
and independently Ben Knudsen and Shmuel Weinberger in
[
KW
]
have defined a new probabilistic versions of the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category and the topological complexity of a topological space
X
. Though we were motivated by topological robotics, we introduced new numerical homotopy invariants of spaces and, hence,
new numerical invariants of discrete groups. In this paper we attempted a further study of these numerical invariants for groups. We call our invariants the distributional topological complexity, denoted as
d\TC
, and the distributional LS-category, denoted as
d\operatorname{cat}
. Knudsen and Weinberger called them analog topological complexity and the analog LS-category and denoted them by

\ATC
and
\operatorname{acat}
respectively. Formally there is a difference between our definitions which is in the choice of topology on the space
\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
of probability measures
\mu
on a topological space
Z
with the cardinality of supports bounded by
n
,
|\supp(\mu)|\leq n
. The elements of
\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
can be viewed as finite linear combinations
\mu=\sum_{z\in Z}\lambda_{z}z
of points in
Z

with
\sum\lambda_{z}=1
and
\lambda_{z}\geq 0
.

There are several ways to introduce topology on
\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
. The finest topology is the quotient topology which comes from the symmetric join product
by means of a quotient map
q:Symm^{\ast n}(X)\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)

[
KK
]
. By the definition

Symm^{\ast n}(Z):=\ast^{n}Z/S_{n}
is the orbit space of action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
on the iterated join product
\ast^{n}Z=Z\ast\cdots\ast Z
.
Elements of
Symm^{\ast n}(Z)
can be seen as formal sums
t_{1}x_{1}+\cdots t_{n}x_{n}
with no order
on the summands and the convention
0x=0y
for each summand. Then the quotient map
q:Symm^{\ast n}(X)\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
is defined by the condition
tx+t^{\prime}x=(t+t^{\prime})x
. We note that
q
is a map with compact contractible fibers.

Knudesn and Weinberger used the quotient topology which is non-metrizable
but it is the standard choice in the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
(see
[
KK
]
). We decided that a metric topology is more appropriate for robotics and chose
the Levy-Prokhorov metric
d_{LP}
from several known metrics on measures. Clearly the identity map
(\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z),\tau_{q})\to(\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z),d_{LP})
is continuous
[
J
]
.
Moreover, it is possible to show that this map is a homotopy equivalence when
Z
is a locally finite CW-complex. We are not giving a proof of it, since in all proofs of our results in this paper any choice of topology is good. In this paper we will be using the notations from
[
DJ
]
for the invariants.

We note when
Z
is a discrete space,
\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
is the
n
-skeleton of the simplex
\Delta(Z)
spanned by
Z

with the CW-complex topology in the first case and with the metric topology taken from the Hilbert space
\Delta(Z)\subset\ell_{2}(Z)
in the second case. Since these topologies agree on finite subcomplexes, the identity map is a weak homotopy equivalence and, since we are dealing with ANR-spaces, it is a homotopy equivalence.

Here are the definitions.

The distributional topological complexity
,
d\TC(X)
, of a space
X
is the minimal number
n
such that there is a continuous map

s:X\times X\to\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(X))

satisfying
s(x,y)\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(x,y))
for all
(x,y)\in X\times X
where
P(x,y)\subset P(X)
is the set of all paths in
X
from
x
to
y
.

The distributional LS-category
,
d\operatorname{cat}(X)
, of a space
X
is the minimal number
n
such that there is a continuous map

s:X\to\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(X))

satisfying
s(x)\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(x,x_{0}))
for all
x\in X
.

Like in the case of classical invariants
\TC
and
\operatorname{cat}
there are inequalities

d\operatorname{cat}(\Gamma)\leq d\TC(\Gamma)\leq d\operatorname{cat}(\Gamma\times\Gamma).

Knudsen and Weinberger pointed out on more similarity by proving the Eilenberg-Ganea equality

for torsion free groups.
The classical Eilenberg-Ganea theorem
[
EG
]
gives the equality
\operatorname{cat}\Gamma=\cd(\Gamma)
for all groups
\Gamma
.
The striking difference was found in both
[
DJ
]
,
[
KW
]

d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})=d\TC(\mathbb{Z}_{2})=1.

We recall that
\cd(\Gamma)=\infty

In contrast, it was proven in
[

that

for any finite group

In

The LS-category.

.
Then by Theorem
4.3
, in view of Proposition
5.3
and the definitions of

5.7 Proposition
.

Let

Proof.

F

for

\mathcal{B}_{4}(F)

\kappa\in H^{4}(N\times N;\mathcal{F})
to the section of
\mathcal{B}_{4}(\bar{p}):P(N)_{4}\to N\times N
is nontrivial,
\kappa\neq 0
.
Since

is orientable, we have the equality
{\mathcal{O}}_{M\#N}=q^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{N}
for the orientation sheaves
where
q:M\#N\to N
is a map collapsing

to a point.
Then
{\mathcal{O}}_{(M\#N)\times(M\#N)}=(q\times q)^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{N\times N}

By the Poincare Duality with local coefficients

]
for

(q\times q)_{*}([(M\#N)\times(M\#N)]\cap(q\times q)^{*}\kappa)=[N\times N]\cap\kappa\neq 0

[X]
denotes a fundamental class of
X
.
Thus, we obtain
(q\times q)^{*}\kappa\neq 0
.
Hence
q\times q
does not admit a lift with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{4}(\bar{p})
. By Proposition
2.8
,
d\TC(M\#N)\geq d\TC(q)\geq 4

∎

5.10 Theorem

(Knudsen-Weinberger
[
KW
]
)
.

For a finite group
G
,
d\operatorname{cat}(G)\leq|G|-1
and
d\TC(G)\leq|G|-1
.

Proof.

Let
u:EG\to BG
be the universal cover and let
n=|G|

s:BG\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(EG)
sending
x
to the evenly distributed measure on

5.11 Theorem
.

Suppose that
p
is prime, then

d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=d\TC(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=p-1.

Proof.

Let
L_{p}^{\infty}=K(\mathbb{Z}_{p},1)
be the infinite lens space and
L^{n}_{p}
denote its
n
-dimensional skeleton for
n=p-1
.
We show that
d\operatorname{cat}(L^{n}_{p})\geq n
. Assume the contrary,
d\operatorname{cat}(L^{n}_{p})\leq n-1
. Then by Lemma
2.10
there is an open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
L^{n}_{p}
such that each of the
diagonal inclusions
\delta_{i}:U_{i}\to SP^{i}(L^{n}_{p})
is null-homotopic. By Theorem
2.18
(see the remark after it) there is a partition
L^{n}_{p}=P_{1}\cup\dots\cup P_{n}
into polyhedra such that
P_{i}\subset U_{i}
and each
P_{i}
admits a deformation retract onto a complex
K_{i}
of
\dim K_{i}\leq 1
. We claim that the base point inclusion
\xi_{i}:L^{n}_{p}\to SP^{i}(L^{n}_{p})
restricted to
P_{i}
is null-homotopic. Note that
K_{i}
is homotopy equivalent to finite disjoint union of points and wedges of circles
\vee^{k}S^{1}
. Let
h_{i}:\coprod\vee_{j=1}^{k}S^{1}\to K_{i}
be such homotopy equivalence. We show that
\xi_{i}h_{i}
is null-homotopic.
It suffices to show that the restriction of
\xi_{i}h_{i}
to each circle in every wedge is null-homotopic. By Proposition
2.13

on the level of the fundamental groups we have the equality
[\delta_{i}h_{i}|_{S^{1}}]=i[\xi_{i}h_{i}|_{S^{i}}]
. Since
\delta_{i}
is null-homotopic on
P_{i}
, we obtain the equality

i[\xi_{i}h_{i}|_{S^{i}}]=0
in the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(SP^{i}(L^{n}_{p}))=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Since
p
is prime, this implies that
[\xi_{i}h_{i}|_{S^{i}}]=0
.
We obtain that
\operatorname{cat}(\xi_{n})\leq n-1
which contradicts with Proposition
2.14
. Thus,
d\operatorname{cat}(L^{n}_{p})\geq n
.

Assume that
d\operatorname{cat}(L^{\infty}_{p})\leq n-1
. Then
by Proposition
4.1
,
d\operatorname{cat}(L^{n}_{p})\leq n-1
and we obtain a contradiction.
Thus,

d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=d\operatorname{cat}(L^{\infty}_{p})\geq n=p-1
. Then in view of Theorem
5.10
we obtain

p-1\geq d\TC(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p-1.

∎

6.
Further generalization of
d\TC

For a metric space
X
we call a path
f:[0,1]\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(X)

resolvable
if there is a continuous map
F:I\times\{1,\dots,n\}\to X
and a probability measure
\mu
on the set
\{1,\dots,n\}

such that
f(t)=\mathcal{B}_{n}(F)(t\times\mu)
for all
t\in I
.

We denote by
RP(\mathcal{B}_{n}(X))\subset P(\mathcal{B}_{n}(X))
the subspace of resolvable paths.
Then the
n
-intertwined navigation algorithm
on a space
X
is a continuous map

m:X\times X\to RP(\mathcal{B}_{n}(X))

such that
m(x,y)(0)=\delta_{x}
and
m(x,y)(1)=\delta_{y}
for all
x,y\in X
.
We define
the intertwining topological complexity

i\TC(X)
of
X
to be the minimal
n
such that there is an
(n+1)
-intertwined navigation algorithm on
X
.

We define
the intertwining LS-category

i\operatorname{cat}(X)
of
X
as the minimal number
n
such that there is a continuous map

h:X\to RP(\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(X))
satisfying
h(x)(0)=\delta_{x}
and
h(x)(1)=\delta_{x_{0}}
for all
x\in X

[
DJ
]
.

The following are straightforward.

6.1 Proposition
.

i\TC(X)\leq d\TC(X)
.

6.2 Proposition
.

i\TC(X)
is homotopy invariant.

6.3 Corollary
.

The invariant
i\TC(\Gamma):=i\TC(K(\Gamma,1))
is well-defined for discrete groups
\Gamma
.

6.4 Proposition
.

For the Higman group we have
i\TC(H)=1
.

Proof.

Since
K=K(H,1)
is acyclic, by Dold’s theorem
[
D
]

\tilde{H}_{i}(SP^{2}(K))=\tilde{H}_{i}(SP^{2}(pt))=0
fro all
i
.
The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(SP^{2}(K))=H_{1}(K)=0
. Then by Hurewicz theorem the space

SP^{2}(K)
is contractible. We denote by
[x,y]\in SP^{2}(K)
the orbit of
(x,y)\in K\times K
under permutation of coordinates.
Let
\delta:K\to SP^{2}(K)
,
\delta(x)=[x,x]
, denote the diagonal embedding.
Since
SP^{2}(K)
is a contractible CW complex
[
Mi
]
, it is an absolute extensor. Therefore, the map
K\coprod K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\coprod\delta}}{{\to}}SP^{2}(K)
,
K\coprod K\subset_{\text{Cl}}K\ast K
, can be extended to a map
f:K\ast K\to SP^{2}(K)

of the join product. Let
q:K\times K\times[0,1]\to K\ast K
be the quotient map
from the definition of the join product. The composition
f\circ q
produces the map
\psi:K\times K\to P(SP^{2}(K))
to the path space.
We define an embedding
\phi:SP^{2}(K)\to\mathcal{B}_{2}(K)
by the formula

\phi([x,y])=\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{2}y.

The map
\phi
induces the map of path spaces

P(\phi):P(SP^{2}(K))\to P(\mathcal{B}_{2}(K)).

Note that every path in
SP^{2}(K)
is the image
of a map
I\times\{1,2\}\to X
. It implies that
P(\phi)
has its image in resolvable paths.
Then

m=P(\phi)\circ\psi:K\times K\to RP(\mathcal{B}_{2}(K))

is a requested navigation algorithm.
∎

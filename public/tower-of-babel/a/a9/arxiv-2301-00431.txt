On Wonderful compactifications of SL ⁡ ( 2 , 𝔽 ) \operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}) for non-Archimedean local fields 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} Thanks: corina.ciobotaru@gmail.com
Corina Ciobotaru

Abstract
. We compute the wonderful compactification of symmetric varieties of SL ⁡ ( 2 , 𝔽 ) \operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}) , where 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} is a finite field-extension of ℚ p {\mathbb{Q}}_{p} with p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 , that comes from either an abstract or 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} -involutions of SL ⁡ ( 2 , 𝔽 ) \operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}) . For each of those wonderful compactifications we find the SL ⁡ ( 2 , 𝔽 ) \operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}) -stabilizers of the accumulation points of the corresponding symmetric varieties and compare them to the Chabauty limits found in [ 4 ] .



1.
Introduction

The wonderful compactifications emerged from specific problems in enumerative geometry. In laymen’s terms a wonderful compactification is a way of compactifying a symmetric variety
G/H
via an irreducible linear representation of the group
G
, where
G
is an algebraic group and
H\leq G
is the fixed point set of an involutorial automorphism of
G
. This procedure will provide remarkable properties of the
G
-closed orbits of that compactification. In principal, the wonderful compactification of
G/H
does not depend on the chosen irreducible linear representation of
G
, and a Borel subgroup of
G
will only have one open and dense orbit in
G/H
. The study of wonderful compactifications was initiated by DeConcini and Procesi
[
5
]
in the case of a complex semi-simple group
G
of adjoint type (i.e. a semi-simple group with trivial center). Since then wonderful compactifications have been extensively studied in algebraic geometry and have important applications in many fields of Mathematics (see the Introduction of
[
7
]
). If one replaces the complex numbers by any (not necessarily algebraically closed) field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2
, the results of
[
5
]
were further expanded by DeConcini and Springer in
[
6
]
to any adjoint semi-simple group
G
defined over
k
and
H
the fixed point group of an involutorial automorphism of
G
defined over
k
.

In this article we focus on the wonderful compactifications of symmetric varieties of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
, where
{\mathbb{F}}
a finite field-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with
p\neq 2
, that come from various abstract and
{\mathbb{F}}
-involutions of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
. Notice that
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
is not of adjoint type. In particular, we are interested in computing the
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
-stabilizers of the accumulation points in the wonderful compactifications of those symmetric varieties and we compare them to the Chabauty limits found in
[
4
]
. Although some of the techniques used in this article to build the wonderful compactifications are similar to the well established literature (e.g.
[
7
]
), we will make extensive use of some results from
[
3
]
, the Bruhat–Tits tree and its visual boundary associated with
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
, which contrast with the methods employed in
[
6
]
.

Let us first fix the notation. Throughout this article we restrict to a prime
p\neq 2
. Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{E}}
be any quadratic extension of
{\mathbb{F}}
. Let

\omega_{\mathbb{F}},\omega_{\mathbb{E}}
be uniformizers of
{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{E}}
, respectively. Recall

, for some non-square

. Then
{\mathbb{F}}^{*}/({\mathbb{F}}^{*})^{2}=\{1,\omega_{\mathbb{F}},S,S\omega_{\mathbb{F}}\}
(
[
13
, Corollaries to Theorems 3 and 4]
or
[
12
, page 41, Section 12]
). If
{\mathbb{E}}
is
ramified
then
{\mathbb{E}}={\mathbb{F}}(\sqrt{\omega_{\mathbb{F}}})
, or
{\mathbb{E}}={\mathbb{F}}(\sqrt{S\omega_{\mathbb{F}}})
(where
\omega_{\mathbb{F}}\neq\omega_{\mathbb{E}}
), or if
{\mathbb{E}}
is

(where
\omega_{\mathbb{F}}=\omega_{\mathbb{E}}
). We choose the unique valuation
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{E}}
on
{\mathbb{E}}
that extends the given valuation
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{F}}
on
{\mathbb{F}}
. Choose

and so

{\mathbb{E}}={\mathbb{F}}(\alpha)
. Notice each element

can be uniquely written as

, with

. For the ramified extensions we can consider
\omega_{\mathbb{E}}^{2}=\omega_{\mathbb{F}}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}\;|\;|x|_{\mathbb{F}}\leq 1\}

{\mathbb{F}}
, then
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
is compact and open in
{\mathbb{F}}
. For

we have
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
\omega_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}=p

,
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}/({\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*})^{2}=\{1,S_{p}\}

We denote by
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathbb{F}}
the Bruhat–Tits tree for
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
whose vertices are equivalence classes of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
-lattices in
{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
(for its construction see
[
13

). The tree

is a regular, infinite tree with valence

at every vertex. The boundary at infinity

of

is the projective space

. Moreover, the end
\infty\in\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
corresponds to the vector
\big[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\big]\in P^{1}({\mathbb{F}})
. The rest of the ends
\xi\in\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathbb{F}}
correspond to the vectors
\big[\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\big]\in P^{1}({\mathbb{F}})
, where
x\in{\mathbb{F}}
. To give a concrete example, the Bruhat–Tits tree of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is the
p+1=|{\mathbb{F}}_{p}|+1
-regular tree. The boundary at infinity
\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
of

is the projective space
P^{1}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})={\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Let
\rho:\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\to\operatorname{GL}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})
be the representation of

over
{\mathbb{F}}
given by

One can easily prove

\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
with respect to the compact-open topologies of the locally compact groups
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
and
\operatorname{GL}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})

has a dominant weight (see
[

For an abstract involution
\theta:\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\to\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
, i.e.
\theta
is an abstract automorphism of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
with
\theta^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
, we consider:

(1)

the action of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
on
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})
given by

, for every
g\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
and

,

(2)

the continuous map
\psi_{\theta}:\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\to{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
given by
g\mapsto\psi_{\theta}(g):=[\rho(g)\rho(\theta(g^{-1}))]=[\rho(g\theta(g^{-1}))]
,

(3)

and the fixed point group of
\theta
denoted by
H_{\theta}:=\{g\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\;|\;\theta(g)=g\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

We take
X_{\theta}:=\overline{\psi_{\theta}(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))}=\overline{[\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
and, by abuse of terminology, call it the wonderful compactification of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})/H_{\theta}
with respect to the involution

.

We will see in the proofs below that a Borel subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
might have more open orbits on

\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})/H_{\theta}

is not of adjoint type. By abuse of notation, for any

and any

we use

to mean
[\rho(g)D\rho(\theta(g^{-1}))]\in X_{\theta}
for some, thus any, representative

of
[D]\in{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
.

By the known theory, those wonderful compactifications do not depend on the irreducible representation
\rho
of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
(for a proof see
[
1

or
[

2.
The wonderful compactification of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})

First recall the usual wonderful compactification associated with the involution
\theta(x,y):=(y,x)
of
G:=\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
, having
Diag(G)=\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
as a fixed point group of the involution
\theta
.
We compactify
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})=G/Diag(G)
in the following way. Consider
\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))\leq\operatorname{GL}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})
and
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
acting on
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})
by
(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot_{\theta}A=\rho(g_{1})A\rho(g_{2}^{-1})
, where
(g_{1},g_{2})\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
and
A\in\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})
. Then the stabilizer in
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
of
\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}
is exactly
Diag(G)=\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
, and
\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))=(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}
.

Next we take the map

\psi_{\theta}:\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\to{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2})),\;\text{ given by }(g_{1},g_{2})\mapsto\psi_{\theta}(g_{1},g_{2}):=[\rho(g_{1}g_{2}^{-1})]

and we want to understand the closure

X_{\theta}:=\overline{\psi_{\theta}(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))}=\overline{[(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]}=\overline{[\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))]}\text{ in }{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))

called
the wonderful compactification of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})=G/Diag(G)
with respect to the involution
\theta
, where
{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
is endowed with the usual topology.

Denote

(1)

\begin{split}&T:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}|\;x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{*}\right\}\qquad U^{+}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}|\;y\in{\mathbb{F}}\right\}\qquad U^{-}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
y&1\end{pmatrix}|\;y\in{\mathbb{F}}\right\}\\
&B^{+}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}|\;y\in{\mathbb{F}},x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{*}\right\}\qquad B^{-}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
y&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}|\;y\in{\mathbb{F}},x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{*}\right\},\\
\end{split}

where
T
is the maximal tori of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
,
U^{+}
is the unipotent radical of the Borel subgroup
B^{+}
, and
U^{-}
is the unipotent radical of the opposite Borel
B^{-}
. By the definition of
X_{\theta}
:

Lemma 2.1
.

The
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
orbit of
\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
with respect to the action
\cdot_{\theta}
is dense in
X_{\theta}
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
we need the following notation. Let

{\mathbb{P}}_{0}:=\{x=[1,x_{2};x_{3},x_{4}]\in{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))\}

and notice this is open in
{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
. Denote
X_{\theta,0}:={\mathbb{P}}_{0}\cap X_{\theta}
and notice this is again open in
X_{\theta}
. The set
X_{\theta,0}
is called the big cell in
X_{\theta}
.

Recall that
B^{+}=TU^{+}
as well as the following decomposition in
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})

(2)

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c/a&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&b/a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\text{ for }a\neq 0.

From here it is easy to verify the following facts.

Lemma 2.2
.

The set
U^{-}TU^{+}
is dense in
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
, and in particular
[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]
is dense in
\psi_{\theta}(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))=[\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))]
, and so in
\overline{[\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))]}
.

Proof.

The first part of the lemma follows from an easy approximation of matrices having
a=0
using the decomposition given in (
2
). The rest of the lemma follows directly from the density of
U^{-}TU^{+}
in
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

We have that

X_{\theta,0}\cap[\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))]=[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]

X_{\theta}=\bigcup\limits_{(g_{1},g_{2})\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})}(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot_{\theta}X_{\theta,0}

X_{\theta,0}=\overline{[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]}\cap{\mathbb{P}}_{0}.

Proof.

By the decomposition
2
it is clear that

[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]=X_{\theta,0}\cap[\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))]={\mathbb{P}}_{0}\cap[\rho(\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}))].

Since
\overline{[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]}=X_{\theta}
we immediately have
X_{\theta,0}=\overline{[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]}\cap{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
, i.e. the closure of the set
[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
.

Let
C\in X_{\theta}
. As
C\in{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
we have
C=[x_{1},x_{2};x_{3},x_{4}]
. If
x_{1}\neq 0
then
C\in X_{\theta,0}
and we are done. If
x_{1}=0
, then by some easy calculations one can arrange for some
(g_{1},g_{2})\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
such that
\rho(g_{1})C\rho(g_{2}^{-1})\in{\mathbb{P}}_{0}

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
2.3
it is enough to compute the set
(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}
, and then to precisely compute its closure
\overline{[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]}\cap{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
.
We have

(3)

\begin{split}(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}&=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
a&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&-b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}|\;x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{*},a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}\right\}\\
&\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&-xb\\
ax&-axb+x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}|\;x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{*},a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}\right\}\Rightarrow\\
&\Rightarrow[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]=\{[1,-b;a,-ab+x^{-2}]\;|\;x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{*},a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}\}.\\
\end{split}

Then the only accumulation points of the set
[(U^{-}T\times U^{+})\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
are the points of the form
[1,-b;a,-ab]
, for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}
. Moreover, it is very easy to see that the only accumulation point of the set
[T\cdot_{\theta}\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}]
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
is just
[1,0;0,0]
.
Notice, an element
[1,-b;a,-ab]
respresents a matrix in
{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
of determinant zero. As well,
\{[1,-b;a,-ab]\;|\;a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}\}
is the
(U^{-}T\times U^{+})
-orbit of the element
[1,0;0,0]\in{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
. From the above calculation and since the action
\cdot_{\theta}
is continuous, it is immediate that the
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
-orbit of
\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}
is open. It is also clear that the
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
-orbit of
[1,0;0,0]
in
{\mathbb{P}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{F}}^{2}))
is closed and, by in easy computation, the stabilizer of
[1,0;0,0]
in
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})\times\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})
is exactly
B^{+}\times B^{-}
.
The theorem follows.
∎

3.

\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{E}})/\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{F}})

{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{F}}

Let
{\mathbb{F}}

{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

{\mathbb{E}}={\mathbb{F}}(\alpha)

{\mathbb{F}}

\theta:{\mathbb{E}}\to{\mathbb{E}}

with respect to
{\mathbb{F}}
,
\theta(a+\alpha b):=a-\alpha b
, for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}
. This map
\theta
induces an abstract involution of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{E}})

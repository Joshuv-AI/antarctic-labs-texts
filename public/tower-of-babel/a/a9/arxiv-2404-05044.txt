Clinical Trials Protocol Authoring using LLMs
Morteza Maleki, SeyedAli Ghahari

Abstract
This report embarks on a mission to revolutionize clinical trial protocol development through the integration of advanced AI technologies. With a focus on leveraging the capabilities of generative AI, specifically GPT-4 models, this initiative aimed to streamline and enhance the efficiency and accuracy of clinical trial protocols. The methodology encompassed a detailed analysis and preparation of comprehensive drug and study level metadata, followed by the deployment of generative AI models for automated protocol section generation. Results demonstrated a significant improvement in protocol authoring, highlighted by increases in efficiency, accuracy, and the customization of protocols to specific trial requirements. Additionally, an extensive economic and cost analysis was performed to evaluate the practical feasibility of AI-assisted protocol generation, revealing substantial cost savings and resource optimization. Challenges encountered during model selection and prompt engineering were systematically addressed, leading to refined methodologies that capitalized on the advanced text generation capabilities of the AI models. This project not only showcases the practical applications and benefits of generative AI in clinical trial design but also sets a foundation for future innovations in the field.

In this research project, it was realized that the text generation task using these models were not efficient and accurate in any way, and the output generated text was found to be extremely concise (400 characters) and insufficient and predictably did not encapsulate the full purpose of text generation. The work on this venue was immediately halted and a prompt engineering path was explored as an alternative approach.

One of the reasons the T5 and BioBart models were not successful in the task of text generation can be attributed to their design which is better suited for classification tasks in that they can successfully learn long format texts and be trained to classify the data into different categories with small output generation required, making them less suitable to generate long format text generation such as in clinical protocol authoring. The OpenAI GPT models, on the other hand, are designed not for the classification tasks but for conversational and long text format responses enabling them to be a good candidate for study protocol sections authoring based on given style, format, and examples provided.

3.1
Evaluation Metrics

Since the predicted (generated) output consists of hundreds of words in a specific style and format, the evaluation of the model performance is significantly different from a typical classification or regression task. To that end, we employed the the following evaluation metrics to assess the quality and accuracy of generated output in our study.

3.1.1
Cosine Similarity

Cosine similarity measures the cosine of the angle between two non-zero vectors in a
multi-dimensional space. In text analysis, these vectors often represent text documents. It operates in the following paradigm:

Text data is first converted into vectors, often using techniques like Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) or word embedding. Cosine similarity is then calculated between these vectors. The value ranges from -1 (completely different) to 1 (exactly the same). It’s used to compare the similarity between the generated text and a reference text. High cosine similarity indicates that the generated text closely matches the reference in terms of content and context.

3.1.2
BLEU Scores

Bilingual Evaluation Understudy (BLEU) is a metric for evaluating a generated sentence to a set of reference sentences. It operates according to the following paradigm:

BLEU-n Score: Measures the co-occurrence of n-grams (sequence of n words) between the generated text and reference text. BLEU1 refers to unigrams (1 word), BLEU2 to bigrams (2 words), and so forth. BLEU score calculates the precision of n-grams in the generated text relative to the reference text, then combines the precision using a geometric mean. A brevity penalty is applied if the generated text is shorter than the reference, to discourage overly terse outputs. A higher BLEU score indicates better agreement between the machine’s output and the human reference. Used extensively in machine translation and can be applied to other text generation tasks.

3.1.3
Rogue Scores

Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation (ROUGE) is a set of metrics used for
evaluating automatic summarization and machine translation. It operates according to the following paradigm:

•

ROUGE-N: Measures the overlap of n-grams between the generated text and reference text.

•

ROUGE-L: Considers the longest common subsequence between the generated and reference text.

•

Recall: The proportion of the reference text’s n-grams found in the generated text.

•

Precision: The proportion of the generated text’s n-grams that are found in the reference text.

•

F1 Score: The harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balance between the two.

ROUGE is particularly useful in evaluating summarization, where capturing the essence of the original text is crucial. A higher ROUGE score indicates that the generated text more effectively captures the key points of the reference text.

These metrics are valuable tools in assessing the quality of text generated by AI models. They provide objective ways to measure aspects like similarity, fluency, and relevance of the generated text compared to a human-written reference.

Discussion

The presented results underscore the substantial impact of providing examples in the prompt on enhancing the quality and coherence of text generation. Our analysis revealed that models such as GPT-4-turbo and GPT-4o significantly outperform others in almost all measured metrics. Notably, the inclusion of one to two examples proved optimal for helping the models grasp the required writing style and content for the introduction section, achieving high cosine similarity and ROUGE precision scores.

Our cost analysis experiment highlighted the economic efficiency of different models, revealing that GPT-4-turbo and GPT-4o offer a balanced trade-off between cost and performance. Despite the higher computational costs associated with advanced models like GPT-4, their superior output quality justifies the investment, especially in scenarios requiring high accuracy and adherence to complex regulatory standards. The annual cost analysis demonstrated substantial potential savings and resource optimization when utilizing these generative AI models for clinical protocol development.

The data analysis confirms the transformative potential of generative AI in clinical trial protocol authoring. GPT-4 models, in particular, demonstrated an exceptional ability to produce text that closely aligns with human expert outputs, as evidenced by high cosine similarity and ROUGE scores. These findings suggest that with adequate training and fine-tuning, generative AI can significantly reduce the time required for protocol development while maintaining or enhancing the quality and compliance of the documents produced.

Further examination of the figures and tables reveals detailed insights into model performance across different settings. The incremental improvements observed with an increasing number of examples highlight the importance of context-rich input data for training AI systems. This is crucial for future applications of AI in clinical research, suggesting that a more extensive dataset, possibly encompassing a broader variety of trial types and therapeutic areas, could further enhance the model’s accuracy and versatility. The adaptability of GPT-4 models to the provided metadata, as demonstrated by various metrics, illustrates the potential for personalized medicine applications where protocols are tailored to specific patient populations or disease characteristics.

Looking ahead, the integration of AI in clinical trials could revolutionize other aspects of clinical research, such as patient recruitment and data analysis. AI’s ability to learn from vast datasets and identify patterns can be leveraged to predict trial outcomes, optimize resource allocation, and enhance patient safety through more precise and individualized trial protocols. However, as we push the boundaries of AI applications in clinical settings, continuous evaluations of ethical implications, transparency, and data privacy must be prioritized to ensure that the adoption of these technologies contributes positively to patient outcomes and public trust in medical research.

4.1
Challenges and Resolutions

The initial use of T5 models highlighted challenges in generating long and contextually rich content. By shifting focus to the GPT-4 model and refining prompt engineering techniques, we significantly improved the output quality for the introduction section. Token limits for input or output in earlier models like GPT-3.5 were resolved in newer versions of GPT models, alleviating previous constraints. This study’s scope was limited to Type II Diabetes and US-based sponsors, using clinical protocols and associated metadata to predict select protocol sections. Future research could expand to include international sponsors, various medical drugs, and different clinical trial protocol sections.

4.2
Impact and Future Directions

The successful implementation of this work marks a significant step forward in clinical trial design. Integrating AI-generated protocol sections into existing toolkits for trial design and study planning can lead to extensive time and financial savings, along with higher accuracy and efficiency. Future efforts may include expanding the range of automated sections, refining the model’s accuracy, and exploring further integration within clinical research frameworks.

5.4. Scientific Rationale for Study Design

This study will evaluate 2 dosing regimens of LY3209590 in study participants with T2DM who are already treated with a daily basal insulin. Study participants will take their last dose of daily basal insulin the day prior to randomization. Treatment will be initiated after randomization with either LY3209590 or insulin degludec as an active comparator. As LY3209590 has a long half-life, a weekly dosing regimen is expected to reach steady-state of PK in over CCI of treatment. Therefore, the withdrawal of a daily basal insulin would result in loss of glycemic control for several weeks after randomization. Therefore, a loading-dose regimen approach, by which an exposure to achieve comparable glycemic control to prior basal insulin dose can be achieved CCI, followed by CCI starting at Week 2 to maintain steady-state exposure. Subsequently, dose adjustments can be made in the initial dose individualization phase of the study. See Appendix 7 and section 7.2.1.2 for dosing and administration details. The 32-week treatment duration is planned as the slow dose escalation approach is necessary for LY3209590 to achieve near comparable glycemic efficacy to prior daily basal insulin.

Insulin degludec is an unblinded active comparator in this study and will be used to compare the effects of LY3209590 on glycemic control, hypoglycemia, and weight gain with a daily basal insulin.

5.5. Justification for Dose

Following a once-weekly SC administration of LY3209590, the time to reach steady-state glucose level is estimated between 4 to 10 weeks based on the long half-life of LY3209590. Therefore, weekly dose and gradual dose adjustment is recommended for LY3209590 dosing regimen.

Alterations to the doses recommended by these dose adjustment algorithms are also under discretion of the investigator and will take hypoglycemia and other study participant safety concerns into account.

The starting dose for insulin degludec is the same dose as the basal insulin that the study participant used previously before entering the study. The study participant will start a titration phase based on fasting glucose achievements under this therapy and the presence of hypoglycemia or other safety concerns. The titration will be performed each week. The dose adjustment algorithm is adapted from the well-established Riddle algorithm (Section 7.2.1.2) under discretion of the investigator.

Guidance is provided for the two algorithms used in dosing LY3209590. There is also guidance for conversion of prior basal insulin dose to LY3209590 loading dose, as well as dosing suggestions for investigators based on fasting glucose and hypoglycemia of the study participant (Appendix 7).

Safety of study participants will be closely monitored during the early stages of dose titration to determine whether adjustments to the conversion and dose adjustment algorithms are needed. As additional data emerges, guidance from the Sponsor on these dosing algorithms may be modified (Appendix 7).

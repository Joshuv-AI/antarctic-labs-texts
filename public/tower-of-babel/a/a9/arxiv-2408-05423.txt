Unveiling subarcsecond multiplicity in the Pleiades with Gaia multicolor photometry
Dmitry Chulkov

Abstract
The list of 409 probable cluster members down to G = 15 mag G=15^{\rm mag} ( m ≳ 0.5 ​ M ⊙ m\gtrsim 0.5M_{\odot} ) is compiled for the two degree radius of the Pleiades, based on astrometric data from Gaia DR3 and the PPMXL catalog, along with several radial velocity surveys, including APOGEE and LAMOST. This approach allows for the inclusion of binary stars with unreliable Gaia solutions, thereby eliminating associated bias. Thus, the often-neglected 14 sources with Gaia two-parameter solutions are included. The subsequent analysis of color-magnitude and color-color diagrams exploits artifacts in Gaia photometric data, caused by the different field sizes used to measure fluxes in the G G , B p B_{p} , and R p R_{p} passbands, to reveal binary stars with subarcsecond angular separation. The findings are validated with prior high-resolution observations. Overall, 24 ± 3 24\pm 3 cluster members with angular separation between 0.1 and 1 arcsec (13.5 to 135 AU projected distance) and mass ratio q > 0.5 q>0.5 are deemed binary, indicating a binarity fraction of 6 ± 1 6\pm 1 %.



I
Introduction

Young clusters are important tracers of the structure and properties of their host galaxies
[
2
]
, including our own Milky Way
[
55
,
10
]
. As coeval and chemically homogeneous groups, clusters are valuable for developing stellar evolution models
[
18
]
. The topic of stellar multiplicity is intimately related to the study of open clusters, as most stars are born in dense stellar environments, and their dissolution gives rise to one of the binary formation mechanisms
[
61
]
. Binarity complicates the determination of the initial and present-day mass functions, which are key characteristics of stellar populations
[
62
]
. Despite major efforts in recent years, the statistical properties of binary stars and their variations in different environments remain controversial due to numerous selection effects associated with observational data
[
21
]
. The Gaia mission
[
34
]
recently opened a new chapter both for open cluster and multiplicity studies
[
31
]
, with a number of works dedicated to the characterization of multiplicity in the Galactic clusters (see references in Section
IV
). The Pleiades has the highest number of members within 400 pc of the Sun
[
84
]
, making it an important benchmark for astronomers. Its relative proximity allows for in-depth analysis that is currently unattainable for more distant objects. This paper begins with an inventory of the cluster members in Section
II
, followed by the adoption of a theoretical isochrone used for mass and binary mass ratio calculations in Section
III
. The analysis of multiplicity, based on the interpretation of Gaia multicolor photometry, is presented in Section
IV
, with the key results summarized in Section
V
.

II
Member selection

II.1
Motivation and basic principles

The key principles for selecting cluster members, outlined by
[
104
]
over a century ago, remain relevant. The contributing factors include angular distance from the cluster center, proper motion, and position in the color–magnitude diagram. While radial velocities can be utilized, they are less reliable. Significant advancement lies in the availability of precise parallax measurements, which add another dimension to the parameter space under consideration. A combination of large proper motion, high galactic latitude, and low interstellar reddening helps in selecting Pleiades members
[
66
]
, which are well-separated from field stars compared to other clusters
[
42
]
. Therefore, the approach described below would likely be less fruitful for other clusters. For a few stars, membership status remains doubtful. Due to their low number, they have been kept for further analysis.

The Gaia mission
[
34
]
has had a tremendous impact on the census of open star clusters
[
17
,
16
]
. Numerous papers have been dedicated to the identification of clusters and their members using various algorithms. Studies based on the Gaia EDR3
[

and DR3
[

[

[

\nu

Analysis of heliocentric radial velocities
\nu

\nu

[
101

9
]

G<15^{\rm mag}
sample, the factor of low signal-to-noise ratio dominates. The presence of a bright neighbor within a few arcseconds may cause the spurious results
[
11
]
. Stellar activity causes intrinsic radial velocity wobble
[
98
]
. Finally, binary and multiple stars show variation of
\nu
due to the motion of components around a common center of mass. Thus, some reported radial velocities can be misleading and should be treated with caution.

While one catalog does not have adequate completeness for the entire sample, combining data sets allows achieving it. Radial velocities from Gaia DR3
[
57

-1

-1

14<G<15
magnitude range. Data reliability is particularly questionable for young, active low-mass stars. A comparison by
[

, or incorrect multicolor photometry. Regarding the latter, color estimates in Gaia are distorted by blending effect and should be treated carefully.
B_{P}
and

fluxes are efficiently measured through aperture photometry in a fairly large

[

[

and
R_{P}
magnitudes represent the combined flux from a system, while an angular resolution in
G

B_{P}-G
and
G-R_{P}
colors, rendering them impractical for member selection. Nonetheless, the analysis of color-color diagrams is valuable for subarcsecond multiplicity diagnostics (Section
IV
).

III
Theoretical isochrone

III.1
Isochrone selection

The defining feature of star clusters is the common origin of their members, which provides an excellent opportunity to test stellar evolution models
[
16
]
or the quality of catalog data
[
13
]
. While an intracluster age gradient
[
41
]
and multiple star formation episodes are observed in the youngest clusters with an age of several Myr
[
54
]
, the corresponding effect remains unnoticeable for older objects, such as the Pleiades (
t\sim 100
Myr). Therefore, the sample is treated as a simple, equal-age population.
Although age is notably hard to determine for a lone star
[
94
]
, there are a variety of age-dating methods for star clusters
[
5
]
. Among them, the lithium depletion technique appears to be promising for the Pleiades-age clusters
[
52
]
.
Recent estimates of the Pleiades’ age with this method are rather consistent:
t=112\pm 5

[
25
]
,
127^{+6}_{-10}

[
40
]
,
93^{+9}_{-10}
Myr
[
53
]
. Isochrone fitting
[
72
]
tends to bring larger uncertainty, but largely does not contradict
t\sim 100-125
Myr estimate
[
36
,
13
]
. However, despite the Pleiades representing a benchmark for isochrone testing, the sequence for low-mass stars (
M\lesssim 0.6M_{\odot}
) is not well-fitted by the existing models
[
8
]
.

Besides age, other parameters such as metallicity, distance, and interstellar extinction should be defined, as they alter the cluster’s appearance in the color-magnitude diagram (Figure
6
). Open clusters are expected to be metallicity-homogeneous within [Fe/H]
<
0.05
[
65
,
96
]
.
The Pleiades show a solar or marginally enhanced metallicity according to spectroscopic observations
[
95
,
33
]
. A distance of 135 pc, which converts into a
5.65^{\rm mag}
modulus, is applied to all stars. The interstellar medium around the Pleiades has a complex structure
[
110
]
, possibly contributing to extinction
A_{V}
and reddening non-uniformity within the cluster
[
100
]
.
This factor potentially introduces additional dispersion to the observed magnitudes on a scale of a few hundredths of a magnitude for sources in different parts of the cluster but should not affect the relative flux of binary star components.

Following
[
74
]
,
A_{V}=0.15^{\rm mag}
is adopted. Extinction in different wavelengths is then calculated according to
[
19
]
and
[
78
]
through the CMD
7

interface.

[

. The PARSEC model accounts for this; however, the estimated impact of moderate spin with
\Omega/\Omega_{c}=0.3
is within

in comparison to non-rotating stars for

\Omega_{c}
is the breakup angular velocity. An inclination of
60^{\circ}

[
45

. Generally, the choice of a theoretical isochrone is somewhat arbitrary, and models with slightly different parameters can be justified as well.

III.2
Mass estimation

After selecting the isochrone, a mock population of single and binary stars is generated. Since some of the considered sources have poorly defined or missing parallaxes, a uniform distance of
d=135
pc is applied. Assuming a radius of
r\sim 10
pc, an object at the cluster’s near edge appears
5\log_{10}\frac{d+r}{d}\sim 0.15^{\rm mag}
brighter than an identical star near the center; this value is taken as the uncertainty of the
G
. Monte Carlo sampling with error margins of quantiles 0.15 and 0.85 is utilized to accommodate uncertainties. Using the chosen theoretical isochrone (Figure
6
), apparent magnitudes in various passbands are computed across a dense mass grid, linear interpolation is applied when necessary. The
G
magnitude thus directly constrains the mass of isolated stars. Subsequently, the mass ratio of components
q=m_{2}/m_{1}
,
0<q<1
, is introduced. Depending on the angular separation and possibly other factors, the reported Gaia magnitude may correspond to individual component flux or the total brightness of the binary system. This distinction can be discerned from the source’s position in the color–magnitude diagram (Figure
4
) if the flux from the secondary star is large enough. Primary, secondary, and combined magnitudes are computed across a two-dimensional grid of
m_{1}
and
q
. For passbands other than
G
, the relative magnitude contrast between the primary and secondary components is calculated. The values of
m_{1}
and
q
that agree with observed magnitude and contrast are selected. This methodology is validated on V1282 Tau, a spectroscopic binary with high-quality multicolor contrast data and an independent mass ratio estimate from orbital solutions by
[
103
]
. The adopted magnitude cutoff of
G<15^{\rm mag}
corresponds to masses
m\gtrsim 0.47M_{\odot}
for single stars or
m_{1}\gtrsim 0.38M_{\odot}
in the case of unresolved twin binaries.

IV
Multiplicity analysis

As discussed in Section
II.6
, the Pleiades stars form a distinct relation in the color-magnitude diagram, and an offset from the main sequence is regarded as a marker of peculiarity, such as stellar multiplicity, incorrect photometric data, or a wrong assessment of membership status. The related analysis is an essential part of numerous papers
[
77
,
51
,
30
,
23
,
67
,
3
]
concerning the binary population of open clusters. Remarkably, at least
[
28
]
and
[
81
]
emphasize the often-neglected contribution of resolved binary stars in nearby clusters. While the mentioned works focus on Gaia photometric data,
[
69
]
introduce a multicolor approach. However, the common assumption that
angular separation, and hence the division between resolved and unresolved sources, does not depend on the chosen passband, is commonly used by authors. Below, the legitimacy of such an approach is challenged.

The Pleiades cluster analysis provides an opportunity to complement Gaia data with long-term studies of the binary stars. Known double-lined spectroscopic systems are used to probe the behavior of unresolved systems in Figure
7
. As expected, spectroscopic binaries are shifted above the main sequence, which is populated by single stars, with the offset depending on the mass ratio. When the
G-R_{p}
color is plotted instead of the
G
magnitude, these systems fall on the main sequence. Simulations with the theoretical isochrone (Section
III
) confirm that populations of single and unresolved binary stars should be indistinguishable in the color-color plot. Yet, at least 54 entries stand out in Figure
8
. Analysis shows that the vast majority of these sources are resolved binary stars with angular separation within 2 arcsec. Differences in field size for flux measurements in
B_{p}
,
R_{p}
, and
G
passbands likely explain the outliers. Thus, the reported
G
magnitude refers to an individual star, while the
B_{p}
and
R_{p}
values represent the total system’s flux.

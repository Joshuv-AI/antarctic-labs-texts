Quasi-Monte Carlo for unbounded integrands with importance sampling Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Du Ouyang, Xiaoqun Wang, Zhijian He

Abstract
We consider the problem of estimating an expectation 𝔼 ⁡ [ h ⁡ ( W ) ] \mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right] by quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods, where h h is an unbounded smooth function on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and W W is a standard normal distributed random variable. To study rates of convergence for QMC on unbounded integrands, we use a smoothed projection operator to project the output of W W to a bounded region, which differs from the strategy of avoiding the singularities along the boundary of the unit cube [ 0 , 1 ] d [0,1]^{d} in [ 24 ] . The error is then bounded by the quadrature error of the transformed integrand and the projection error. If the function h ⁡ ( 𝒙 ) h(\bm{x}) and its mixed partial derivatives do not grow too fast as the Euclidean norm | 𝒙 | \lvert\bm{x}\rvert goes to infinity, we obtain an error rate of O ⁡ ( n − 1 + ε ) O(n^{-1+\varepsilon}) for QMC and randomized QMC (RQMC) with a sample size n n and an arbitrarily small ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . However, the rate turns out to be O ⁡ ( n − 1 + 2 ​ M + ε ) O(n^{-1+2M+\varepsilon}) if the functions grow exponentially with a rate of O ⁡ ( exp ⁡ { M ​ | 𝒙 | 2 } ) O(\exp\{M\lvert\bm{x}\rvert^{2}\}) for a constant M ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) M\in(0,1/2) . Superisingly, we find that using importance sampling with t distribution as the proposal can improve the root mean squared error of RQMC from O ⁡ ( n − 1 + 2 ​ M + ε ) O(n^{-1+2M+\varepsilon}) to O ( n − 3 / 2 + ε ) O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon}) for any M ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) M\in(0,1/2) .

Introduction

Quasi-Monte Calro (QMC) is an efficient quadrature method to numerically solve the integral problems on the unit cube
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
. Unlike the Monte Carlo (MC) method, the QMC method uses low-discrepancy sequences instead of random sequences (see
[
3
,
19
,
4
,
6
,
26
]
). If the integrand has bounded
variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause (BVHK), then by using the Koksma-Hlawka inequality, QMC with
n
quadrature points yields a deterministic error of
O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{d})
, which is asymptotically faster than the MC rate
O(n^{-1/2})
.

In many problems of financial engineering and stochastic control, the underlying solutions can be formulated as expectations of the form
\mathbb{E}[h(W)]
with respect to a standard normal distribution
W
(see
[
13
,
8
,
27
]
). To estimate
\mathbb{E}[h(W)]
, QMC quadrature rule takes

\widehat{I}_{n}(h)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j}),

(1)

where
\{\bm{y}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a low-discrepancy point set in
[0,1]^{d}
,
\Phi(x)
is the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
N(0,1)
satisfying

\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}du,

(2)

\circ
is the composite operator and
\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y})
is the inverse of
\Phi
acting on each component of the argument
\bm{y}
.
Since unbounded functions cannot be BVHK, the Koksma-Hlawka inequality fails to provide the
O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{d})
error rate for unbounded functions. This paper is devoted to providing comprehensive error analysis for (
1
), in which the integrand
h\circ\Phi^{-1}
may have singularities along the boundary of the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
.

Owen
[
24
]
studied the QMC error for unbounded functions on
\left(0,1\right)^{d}
. He found that QMC error attains a rate of
O(n^{-1+\max_{j}A_{j}+\varepsilon})
if the integrand satisfies the boundary growth condition

\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}f(\bm{y})\right|\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\min\left(y_{j},1-y_{j}\right)^{-A_{j}-\mathbf{1}_{j\in\bm{u}}}

for some
A_{j}>0
,
0<B<\infty
, and all
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d=\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}
, where
\partial^{\bm{u}}f(\bm{y})
denotes the mixed partial derivative of
f(\bm{y})
with respect to
y_{j}
with
j\in\bm{u}
and
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small.
Moreover, randomized QMC (RQMC) method yields the same rate of
O(n^{-1+\max_{j}A_{j}+\varepsilon})
for the mean error. Recently, He et al.
[
11
]
found that this rate also holds for root mean squared error (RMSE) when using scrambled nets (a commonly used RQMC method
[
22
]
). It is easy to see that when
A_{j}
are all arbitrarily small (we call it the “QMC-friendly” growth condition), the convergence rate achieves the optimal case
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
. In this article, we propose a projection based quasi-Monte Carlo (P-QMC) method and further refine the boundary growth conditions considered in Owen
[
24
]
. Using P-QMC or RQMC method, we obtain better convergence results for different boundary growth conditions.

There are some related work on studying unbounded integrands in the context of QMC. Kuo et al.
[
17
]
studied the problem of multivariate integration over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. They considered the case where the intergrand belongs to some weighted tensor product reproducing kernel Hilbert space and proved that good randomly shifted lattice rules can be constructed component by component to achieve a worst case error of order
O(n^{-1/2})
. Moreover, Kuo et al.
[
16
]
improved the results by proving that a rate of convergence close to the optimal order
O(n^{-1})
can be achieved with an appropriate choice of parameters for the function
space. Based on this, Nichols and Kuo
[
18
]
extended the theory of Kuo et al.
[
16
]
in several non-trivial directions. In the above work, the constants in the big-
O
bounds can be independent
of dimension
d
under appropriate conditions on the weights of the function space. Nuyens and Suzuki
[
20
]
introduced a method that scales lattice rules from the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
to properly sized
boxes on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and achieved higher-order convergence that matches the
smoothness of the integrand in a certain Sobolev space. Basu and Owen
[
1
]

studied three quadrature methods for integrands on the square

[0,1]^{2}
that may become singular as the point approaches the diagonal line
x_{1}=x_{2}
.

Importance sampling (IS) is an efficient variance reduction method in the context of MC (see
[
9
,
15
,
21
,
7
,
28
]
). However, IS cannot improve the convergence rate of MC. It is natural to ask a question: “Can IS accelerate the convergence rate in QMC?”. He et al.
[
11
]
followed the framework of Owen
[
24
]
to show that using a proper IS in RQMC can retain the RMSE rate of
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
under the “QMC-friendly” growth condition. In our framework, by employing suitable IS to slow down the growth of the integrand, the error rate of RQMC is improved from
O(n^{-1+\max_{j}A_{j}+\varepsilon})
to
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon})
, in which the growth condition is not necessarily “QMC-friendly”.

A key strategy in
[
24
]
is to employ an auxiliary function that has a low variation to approximate the unbounded integrand. However, the auxiliary function used in
[
24
]
is not smooth enough so that it does not meet the smoothness requirement in Owen
[
25
]
for establishing the
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon})
error rate. Differently, the function constructed based on the projection method is smooth, allowing us to address this issue and obtain the desired rate of convergence when using IS.

In this paper, we prove that when the smooth integrand grows at rate of

O(\exp\left\{M|\bm{x}|^{k}\right\})
with
M>0
and
0<k<2
, the convergence rates of the P-QMC and RQMC methods is
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
. By Owen
[
24
]
, the convergence rate of RQMC turns out to be
O(n^{-1+2M+\varepsilon})
if the integrand grows extremely fast with a rate of
O(\exp\left\{M|\bm{x}|^{2}\right\})
for a constant
0<M<1/2
. However, we demonstrate that with an appropriate IS, the convergence rate of RQMC improves from
O(n^{-1+2M+\varepsilon})
to
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon})
. Our work theoretically establishes that suitable IS can accelerate the convergence rate of QMC. Some integrands after pre-integration (also known as conditioning)
[
10
,
27
]
arsing from option pricing and smooth loss functions in deep learning both satisfy our growth conditions. Leveraging the findings in this paper, we can obtain the convergence rates of the QMC methods for these problems.

The structure of this paper is as follows. Section
2
introduces the basic concepts of QMC and RQMC methods. Section
3
focuses on the function growth conditions as described in Owen
[
24
]
, and categorizes these into specific function growth classes. In Section
4
, we propose the P-QMC method and obtain the convergence results with respect to different growth classes. Section
5
discusses the P-QMC and RQMC methods using importance sampling, demonstrating that the convergence rates can be significantly improved by using a proper IS. Section
6
provides numerical experiments to confirm the theoretical results. Section
7
concludes the paper. We put lengthy but useful results about the operations of growth classes in Appendix.

Preliminaries

In this article, all norms that appear are Euclidean norms. In order to avoid ambiguity, bold symbols, such as
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
, are used to represent vectors, and normal symbols are used to represent scalars. For example, we use
\bm{y}_{j}
to represent an element of a low-discrepancy point set, and use
y_{j}
to represent a component of a
d
-dimensional point
\bm{y}
. Also,
\bm{x}
is always used to represent a point in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\bm{y}
is always used to represent a point in
[0,1]^{d}
. Denote
1{:}d=\left\{1,2,\dots,d\right\}.
Let
\bm{u}
be a subset of
1{:}d
, and
|\bm{u}|
be the number of elements in
\bm{u}
. Let
\bm{a}_{\bm{u}}{:}\bm{b}_{-\bm{u}}
be a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose
j
-th component is
a_{j}
if
j\in\bm{u}
and
b_{j}
otherwise.

2.1
Quasi-Monte Carlo methods

QMC method is a quadrature rule for approximating the integration of functions over the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
. Instead of using random points in Monte Carlo method, QMC method uses deterministic low-discrepancy sequences.
The uniformity of a point set is measured by discrepancy defined below.

Definition 1
.

For the point set
P=\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}
in the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
, the star discrepancy of
P
is defined as

D^{*}_{n}(P):=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{B}(\bm{y}_{j})-\lambda(B)\right|,

where the
\lambda(\cdot)
is the Lebesgue measure and
\mathcal{B}
is the family of all subintervals in
[0,1]^{d}
of the form
\prod_{j=1}^{d}[0,u_{j})\subset[0,1]^{d}
.

In this paper, we work on smooth functions, which are defined below.

Definition 2
.

A function
f(\bm{x})
defined over
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a
smooth function
if for any
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
,
\partial^{\bm{u}}f
is continuous. Let
\mathcal{S}^{d}(K)
be the class of such smooth functions.

For smooth functions, we have a brief definition for the variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause.

Definition 3
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{d}([0,1]^{d})
, then the variation of
f
in the sense of Hardy and Krause is

V_{\mathrm{HK}}(f)=\sum_{\varnothing\neq\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\int_{\left[0,1\right]^{d}}\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}f\left(\bm{y_{u}}{:}\mathbf{1}_{-\bm{u}}\right)\right|d\bm{y}.

The Koksma-Hlawka inequality
[
12
]
provides an error bound for QMC quadrature rule, i.e.,

\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\bm{y}_{j})-\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f(\bm{y})d\bm{y}\right|\leq V_{\mathrm{HK}}(f)D^{*}_{n}\left(\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}\right).

Note that the error bound depends on the star discrepancy of the sequence used for QMC methods. There are several kinds of low-discrepancy sequences, such as Sobol’ sequece, Halton sequence and Faure sequence (see
[
19
,
8
]
) with the star discrepancy of
O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{d})
for the first
n
points. Therefore, if the function
f
is BVHK, then QMC can attain a rate of convergence
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
, where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small for hiding the logarithm term. In this paper, we focus on digital nets.

Definition 4
.

An elementary interval of
[0,1)^{d}
in base b is an interval of the form

E=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left[\frac{t_{j}}{b^{k_{j}}},\frac{t_{j}+1}{b^{k_{j}}}\right)

for nonnegative integers
k_{j}
and
t_{j}<b^{k_{j}}
.

Definition 5
.

Let
\lambda,t,m
be integers with
m\geq 0,\ 0\leq t\leq m
, and
1\leq\lambda<b
. A point set
\left\{\bm{y}_{j}\right\}
of
\lambda b^{m}
points is called a
\left(\lambda,t,m,d\right)
-net in base b if every elementary interval in base
b
of volume
b^{t-m}
contains
\lambda b^{t}
points of the point set and no elementary interval in base b of volume
b^{t-m-1}
contains more than
b^{t}
points of the point set.

If
\lambda=1
, then we use the notation
(t,m,d)
-net instead. Every
(t,m,d)
-net in base
b\geq 2
is a low-discrepancy point set with
n=b^{m}
, whose star discrepancy becomes
O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{d-1})
. We refer to Niederreiter
[
19
]
for more details.

Owen
[
22
]
provided a scrambling method to randomize the QMC points, resulting in a kind of RQMC methods. The randomized points satisfies
\bm{y}_{j}\sim U[0,1]^{d}
and retain the net property. Owen
[
23
,
25
]
showed that the scrambled net achieves a convergence rate of
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon})
for smooth integrands, which is better than the rate
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
for the unscrambled net. The following two propositions are taken from Owen
[
23
]
.

Proposition 6
.

If
\left\{\bm{a}_{j}\right\}
is a
(\lambda,t,m,d)
-net in base b and
\left\{\bm{y}_{j}\right\}
is the scrambled version of
\left\{\bm{a}_{j}\right\}
, then
\left\{\bm{y}_{j}\right\}
is a
(\lambda,t,m,d)
-net in base b with probability
1
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\bm{a}
be a point in
[0,1]^{d}
and
\bm{y}
is the scrambled version of
\bm{a}
. Then
\bm{y}
has the uniform distribution on
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
.

3
Growth conditions

To drive the convergence rate of RQMC method for unbounded functions over the unit cube
(0,1)^{d}
, Owen
[
24
]
introduced the boundary growth condition for smooth functions defined on
(0,1)^{d}
,

\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}f(\bm{y})\right|\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\min\left(y_{j},1-y_{j}\right)^{-A_{j}-\mathbf{1}_{j\in\bm{u}}}

(3)

for some
A_{j}>0
,
0<B<\infty
, and all
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
.
By compositing the inverse distribution function, our target integrand is then
f(\bm{y})=h\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y})
, which is defined in
(0,1)^{d}
. Therefore, the growth condition for function
h
is

\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}\left(h\circ\Phi^{-1}\right)(\bm{y})\bigg|\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\min\left(y_{j},1-y_{j}\right)^{-A_{j}-\mathbf{1}_{j\in\bm{u}}}.

(4)

Since
\Phi
is a specific function, we now work out an equivalent condition on
h(\bm{y})
for ensuring (
4
).
Let
\bm{x}=\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y})
. Then the left hand side of (
4
) is

\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}\left(h\circ\Phi^{-1}\right)(\bm{y})\bigg|=\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}h(\bm{x})\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\frac{d\Phi^{-1}(y_{j})}{dy_{j}}\bigg|=\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}h(\bm{x})\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\frac{1}{\varphi(x_{j})}\bigg|,

where
\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-x^{2}/2\right\}
is the Gaussian probability density. Since

\displaystyle\min\left(y_{j},1-y_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\Phi(x_{j}),1-\Phi(x_{j})\right\}=1-\Phi(|x_{j}|),

the inequality (
4
) is equivalent to

\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}h\left(\bm{x}\right)\bigg|\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-\Phi(|x_{j}|)\right)^{-A_{j}-\mathbf{1}_{j\in\bm{u}}}\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\varphi(x_{j}):=G(\bm{x}).

Note that for any
\varepsilon>0
, if
|x_{j}|
is large enough then

\exp\left\{x_{j}^{2}/2\right\}\leq\left(1-\Phi(|x_{j}|)\right)^{-1}\leq\exp\left\{(1+\varepsilon)x_{j}^{2}/2\right\}.

Consequently, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
such that

\displaystyle B_{1}\exp\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{A_{j}}{2}x_{j}^{2}\right\}\leq G(\bm{x})\leq B_{2}\exp\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{A_{j}+\varepsilon}{2}x_{j}^{2}\right\}.

(5)

As a result, the inequality (
4
) implies
|\partial^{\bm{u}}h(\bm{x})|=O(\exp\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{A_{j}+\varepsilon}{2}x_{j}^{2}\})
. On the other hand, if
|\partial^{\bm{u}}h(\bm{x})|=O(\exp\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{A_{j}}{2}x_{j}^{2}\})
, then the inequality (
4
) holds, leading to a mean error rate of
O(n^{-1+\max_{j}A_{j}+\varepsilon})
for RQMC
[
24
]
. To achieve the convergence rate
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
, all
A_{j}
must be arbitrarily small. This leads to the definition of the “QMC-friendly” condition.

Definition 8
.

We say that
h(\bm{x})
satisfies the “QMC-friendly” condition if for any fixed
M>0
, there exists
B>0
such that

\sup_{\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h(\bm{x})\right|\leq Be^{M|\bm{x}|^{2}}.

We next refine the growth conditions in Owen
[
24
]
and define some slower-growing function classes (relatively smaller), and use the P-QMC method to obtain better convergence results.

Definition 9
.

For
M>0,\ B>0
and
k>0
, define polynomial growth class,

G_{p}(M,B,k):=\left\{h\in\mathcal{S}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\sup_{\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h(\bm{x})\right|\leq M|\bm{x}|^{k}+B\right\},

and define exponential growth class,

G_{e}(M,B,k):=\left\{h\in\mathcal{S}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\sup_{\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h(\bm{x})\right|\leq Be^{M|\bm{x}|^{k}}\right\},

where
\mathcal{S}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the class of smooth functions given in Definition
2
.
We say that
h
has
polynomial growth
if there exists
M>0,B>0,k>0
such that
h\in G_{p}(M,B,k)
, and
h
has
exponential growth of order
k
if there exists
M>0,B>0
such that
h\in G_{e}(M,B,k)
.

When the order
k=2
, there are functions in the growth class that grow too fast to satisfy the “QMC-friendly” condition. This leads to the definition of fast growth class.

Definition 10
.

If
M<1/2,\ B>0
, then we call
G_{e}(M,B,2)
the
fast growth class
. We say that
h
has
fast growth
, if
h
belongs to the fast growth class.

Note that in the definition of fast growth class, we have reached the minimum restriction on
M
. Because if
M\geq 1/2
, there are cases where the integral is infinity. And when
M\geq 1/4
, the variance can be infinity, therefore, the MC method does not converge.

The growth conditions we have defined are a further subdivision of Owen’s growth condition. If
h
satisfies Owen’s growth condition (
4
), then there are three situations,

I)

h
has polynomial growth;

II)

h
has exponential growth of order
k<2
;

III)

h
has fast growth.

Note that
I)
and
II)
constitute the “QMC-friendly” condition. With the help of the projection operator defined later, if
h
satisfies condition
I)
or
II)
, P-QMC and RQMC method will yield better results.

In option pricing, under the well known Black-Scholes model
[
8
]
, the payoff functions can be written as

h(W)=f(\exp\left\{AW\right\}),

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a constant matrix. If
f
and its derivatives up to order
d
have polynomial growth, then by Theorem
38
in Appendix, we obtain
h\in G_{e}(M^{\prime},B,1)
for some
M^{\prime}>0
and
B>0
. However, the function
f
usually has kinks or jumps in option pricing. To reclaim the efficiency of QMC, it was suggested to use the conditioning method to smooth the integrand to obtain a new function
f\in C^{d-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
. We refer to
[
10
,
27
]
for more details. From this perspective, our findings in this paper can be applied for many problems in financial engineering.

Now, we focus on the situation where
h
satisfies the fast growth. By relation (
5
), if
h\in G_{e}(M,B,2)
then
h
satisfies (
4
) with
\max_{j}A_{j}=2M
, and therefore, by the results of Owen
[
24
]
, RQMC achieves a mean error rate of
O\left(n^{-1+2M+\varepsilon}\right)
. From this standpoint,
M<1/2
is a very weak constraint; otherwise, it would not converge. In Section
5
, we will prove that with a suitable IS, the P-QMC method achieves the convergence rate of
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
and the RQMC method achieves the convergence rate of
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon})
for
M<1/2
. As a result, we can conclude that using IS accelerates the convergence rate of QMC.

4
The P-QMC method

In this section, we propose a method, called projection based quasi-Monte Carlo method (P-QMC). After composite the projection operator and inverse distribution function, the modified integrand defined on the unit cube
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
is of BVHK, and therefore error analysis can be achieved when using QMC method to the modified integrand.

4.1
Projection operator

We introduce the concept of a projection operator, which projects the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
space to a bounded region
\left[-R,R\right]^{d}
for a constant
R>0
.

Definition 11
.

If
x\in\mathbb{R}
, for any
R>0
, we define the projection operator
\widehat{P}_{R}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\widehat{P}_{R}(x)={\rm arg}\min_{y\in[-R,R]}|y-x|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}x,&x\in\left[-R,R\right]\\
R,&x>R\\
-R,&x<-R\end{array}\right..

If
\bm{x}=\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\widehat{P}_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
acts on each component of
\bm{x}
, i.e.,
\widehat{P}_{R}(\bm{x})

=

(\widehat{P}_{R}(x_{1}),\ldots,\widehat{P}_{R}(x_{d}))
.

From the definition of
\widehat{P}_{R}
, we can see that
\widehat{P}_{R}
is a function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the cube
[-R,R]^{d}
and that it projects each component of a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
from
\mathbb{R}
to
[-R,R]
. The operator
\widehat{P}_{R}
is continuous but not differentiable at some points. We next modify
\widehat{P}_{R}
to make it smooth. The following lemma gives us a modification for the one-dimensional case, and the multidimensional case can be defined in the component-wise way.

Lemma 12
.

(One-dimensional case)

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,R)
, there exists a modification of the projection operator
\widehat{P}_{R}
, denoted as
P_{R}
, which is defined on
[-\infty,\infty]
and satisfies

i)

P_{R}(x)=\widehat{P}_{R}(x)=x\ \ for\ x\in[-R+\varepsilon,R-\varepsilon]
;

ii)

P_{R}
has continuous derivative of order 1, i.e.,
P_{R}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
;

iii)

|\frac{dP_{R}(x)}{dx}|\leq\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq R\}}
;

iv)

|x|\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq R-\varepsilon\}}+(R-\varepsilon)\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|>R-\varepsilon\}}\leq|P_{R}(x)|\leq|x|\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq R\}}+R\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|>R\}}
;

v)

P_{R}(\infty)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}P_{R}(x)
and
P_{R}(-\infty)=\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}P_{R}(x)
.

Proof.

There are many constructive methods for proving this lemma. For instance, we adopt a quadratic function to replace the part that
P_{R}\neq\widehat{P}_{R}
, so that the piecewise function constructed is smooth. Define
P_{R}
as:

P_{R}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-R+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},&\ x\in[-\infty,-R]\\
\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}x^{2}+\frac{R}{\varepsilon}x+\frac{(R-\varepsilon)^{2}}{2\varepsilon},&\ x\in(-R,-R+\varepsilon)\\
x,&\ x\in[-R+\varepsilon,R-\varepsilon]\\
-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}x^{2}+\frac{R}{\varepsilon}x-\frac{(R-\varepsilon)^{2}}{2\varepsilon},&\ x\in(R-\varepsilon,R)\\
R-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},&\ x\in[R,\infty]\end{array}\right..

(6)

It is easy to verify that the
P_{R}
satisfies the conditions in Lemma
12
.

\varepsilon=1

From now on, we will call
P_{R}
the
projection operator
if each component of
P_{R}
satisfies the conditions in Lemma
12
and with a slight abuse of terminology, call
R
the
projection radius
. For convenience, we take
\varepsilon=1
in Lemma
12
and drop the dimension notation if there is no misunderstanding. Figure
1
shows the one-dimensional smoothed projection operator with
\varepsilon=1
.

Proposition 13
.

Denote
H=\left[-R+1,R-1\right]^{d}
. If
\bm{x}\in H
, then
\bm{x}=P_{R}(\bm{x})
. If
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus H
, then for any point
\bm{\xi}
lies on the line segment connecting points
\bm{x}
and
P_{R}(\bm{x})
, we have
|\bm{\xi}|\geq R-1
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the second statement. There is a
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, such that
\bm{\xi}=(1-\lambda)\bm{x}+\lambda P_{R}(\bm{x})
. Let
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d})
. For any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\left[-R+1,R-1\right]^{d}
, there is an index
j\in 1{:}d
such that
|x_{j}|\geq|P_{R}(x_{j})|\geq R-1
. Therefore,

|\bm{\xi}|\geq|\xi_{j}|=|(1-\lambda)x_{j}+\lambda P_{R}(x_{j})|=(1-\lambda)|x_{j}|+\lambda|P_{R}(x_{j})|\geq R-1,

where the second equality holds because
x_{j}
and
P_{R}(x_{j})
have the same sign.

4.2
Error analysis for the P-QMC method

In this section, we discuss how to modify the integrand through the projection operator
P_{R}
to achieve BVHK and prove the corresponding convergence property. To this end, we modify the integrand
h
by compositing the projection operator
P_{R}
, and the integrand changes from
h\circ\Phi^{-1}
to the modified integrand
h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}
. The following lemma shows that the modified integrand is a smooth function on
[0,1]^{d}
.

Lemma 14
.

If
h\in\mathcal{S}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the modified integrand
h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}([0,1]^{d})
.

Proof.

Note that our projection operator
P_{R}
is defined on
[-\infty,\infty]^{d}
, so the modified integrand
h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}
is well-defined on
[0,1]^{d}
. For any
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
,

\displaystyle\partial^{\bm{u}}\left(h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial^{\bm{u}}h\right)\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y})\cdot\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\frac{dP_{R}(\Phi^{-1}(y_{j}))}{d\Phi^{-1}(y_{j})}\frac{d\Phi^{-1}(y_{j})}{dy_{j}}.

The right hand side of the above equality is well-defined on
[0,1]^{d}
because the projection operator
P_{R}
satisfies condition
iii)
of Lemma
12
and the derivative vanishes when
y_{j}
approaches
0
or
1
. Therefore, it is continuous on
[0,1]^{d}
.

To deal with the singularities, Owen
[
24
]
used the low variation extension due to Sobol’,

\widehat{f}(\bm{y})=f(\bm{c})+\sum_{\bm{u}\neq\varnothing}\int_{[\bm{c}_{\bm{u}},\bm{y}_{\bm{u}}]}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\bm{z}_{\bm{u}}{:}\bm{c}_{-\bm{u}}\in K\right\}}\partial^{\bm{u}}f(\bm{z}_{\bm{u}}{:}\bm{c}_{-\bm{u}})d\bm{z}_{\bm{u}},

where
K\subseteq[0,1]^{d}
is Sobol’s extensible with anchor
\bm{c}
.
This extension is not differentiable at some points, implying
\widehat{f}\notin\mathcal{S}^{d}([0,1]^{d})
. Our modified integrand
h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}
is smooth, allowing to calculate the variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause simply by Definition
3
. Furthermore, we can apply the results in Owen
[
25
]
to achieve higher convergence rate for such a smooth function.

Our P-QMC method uses the modified quadrature method

\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j}),

where
\left\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\right\}
is a low-discrepancy point set. We claim that the modified method overcomes the problem of infinite variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause, and moreover, its convergence rate is better than those in Owen
[
24
]
and He et al.
[
11
]
.

The total error of the proposed P-QMC method can be decomposed into the following two parts

\bigg|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\bigg|\leq\underbrace{\bigg|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h\circ P_{R}(W)\right]\bigg|}_{\text{QMC error}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigg|h\circ P_{R}(W)-h(W)\bigg|}_{\text{projection error}}.

(7)

We are going to bound the error
\mathbb{E}\left|h\circ P_{R}(W)-h(W)\right|
due to using the projection operator, which is called the
projection error
. Then, for the
QMC error

\left|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h\circ P_{R}(W)\right]\right|
, we calculate the variation of the modified integrand in the sense of Hardy and Krause, and use the Koksma-Hlawka inequality to obtain an upper bound. Both of these errors depend on the projection radius
R
. Finally, we choose an appropriate
R
to balance the two sources of errors and obtain the convergence rate for the total error.

In contrast, if we use the original estimator
\widehat{I}_{n}(h)
defined in (
1
), we will have an additional term called
sample error
, as shown below

\bigg|\widehat{I}_{n}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\bigg|\leq\underbrace{\bigg|\widehat{I}_{n}(h)-\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)\bigg|}_{\text{sample error}}+\left|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\right|.

(8)

The magnitude of sample error depends on how far the low-discrepancy points we use deviate from the boundaries. In this regard, Owen
[
24
]
specifically studied the case of Halton sequences and gave several regions that avoid the origin and other corners so that the sample error vanishes. Calculating sample error is generally a cumbersome task for the P-QMC method. However, if we use the RQMC method and consider the expected error, the sample error can be bounded by the projection error, thereby avoiding the estimation of this term. The next lemma will be used several times in the following proofs.

Lemma 15
.

If
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+},M>0
and
T\geq 1/\sqrt{2M}
, then

\int_{T}^{\infty}x^{d}\exp\left\{-Mx^{2}\right\}dx\leq\frac{(d+2)!!}{4M}T^{d-1}\exp\left\{-MT^{2}\right\}.

(9)

Proof.

With
u=\sqrt{2M}x
, the left hand side of (
9
) is equal to

\displaystyle\left(2M\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\int_{\sqrt{2M}T}^{\infty}u^{d}e^{-u^{2}/2}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2M\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\left(\left(\sqrt{2M}T\right)^{d-1}e^{-MT^{2}}+(d-1)\int_{\sqrt{2M}T}^{\infty}u^{d-2}e^{-u^{2}/2}du\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2M\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\left((\sqrt{2M}T)^{d-1}+(d-1)(\sqrt{2M}T)^{d-3}+\cdots+(d-1)!!\right)\exp\left\{-MT^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2M\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{(d+2)}{2}(d-1)!!(\sqrt{2M}T)^{d-1}\exp\left\{-MT^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(d+2)!!}{4M}T^{d-1}\exp\left\{-MT^{2}\right\},

where in the first inequality we use

\int_{\sqrt{2M}T}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}/2}du\leq\int_{\sqrt{2M}T}^{\infty}ue^{-u^{2}/2}du=\exp\left\{-MT^{2}\right\}

for the case
d
is even, and in the last inequality, we use
(d-1)!!\leq d!!
.

When
h
falls into different growth classes, there are corresponding worst case estimates for the projection error. Note that the mean error can be bounded by the root mean squared error.

Lemma 16
.

Assume
M>0
and
B>0
. For polynomial growth class
G_{p}(M,B,k)
with
k>0
, if
R>2
, then

\sup_{h\in G_{p}(M,B,k)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h\left(W\right)-h\circ P_{R}(W)\right)^{2}\right]\leq C_{1}(R-1)^{[2k]+d-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}(R-1)^{2}\right\},

(10)

where
C_{1}=(\frac{\pi}{2})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\left(M^{2}+B^{2}\right)([2k]+d+2)!!
and
[2k]
is the integer part of
2k
. And for exponential growth class
G_{e}(M,B,k)
with
0<k<2
, if
R>1+\sqrt{2}
, then

\sup_{h\in G_{e}(M,B,k)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h\left(W\right)-h\circ P_{R}(W)\right)^{2}\right]\leq C_{2}(R-1)^{d-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}(R-1)^{2}\right\},

(11)

where
C_{2}=(\frac{\pi}{2})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}B^{2}\exp\left\{(4kM)^{\frac{k}{2-k}}\right\}(d+2)!!
.

Proof.

We first prove (
10
). For any
h\in G_{p}(M,B,k)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(h\left(W\right)-h\circ P_{R}(W))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{|\bm{x}|\geq R-1}\left(\left|h(\bm{x})\right|+\left|h\circ P_{R}(\bm{x})\right|\right)^{2}e^{-|\bm{x}|^{2}/2}d\bm{x}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{|\bm{x}|\geq R-1}\left(2M|\bm{x}|^{k}+2B\right)^{2}e^{-|\bm{x}|^{2}/2}d\bm{x}

(12)

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{|\bm{x}|\geq R-1}8\left(M^{2}|\bm{x}|^{2k}+B^{2}\right)e^{-|\bm{x}|^{2}/2}d\bm{x},

where we use
|h(\bm{x})|\leq M|\bm{x}|^{k}+B
and
|h\circ P_{R}(\bm{x})|\leq M|P_{R}(\bm{x})|^{k}+B\leq M|\bm{x}|^{k}+B
by Lemma
12
. Using the polar coordinates transformation, the right hand side of (
12
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{8}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{j=1}^{d-2}|\sin\psi_{j}|^{d-1-j}\int_{R-1}^{\infty}|\bm{x}|^{d-1}\left(M^{2}|\bm{x}|^{2k}+B^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{|\bm{x}|^{2}}{2}}d|\bm{x}|d\psi_{1{:}(d-1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\int_{R-1}^{\infty}\left(M^{2}|\bm{x}|^{2k+d-1}+B^{2}|\bm{x}|^{d-1}\right)e^{-|\bm{x}|^{2}/2}d|\bm{x}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\int_{R-1}^{\infty}\left(M^{2}+B^{2}\right)|\bm{x}|^{[2k]+d}e^{-|\bm{x}|^{2}/2}d|\bm{x}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\left(M^{2}+B^{2}\right)([2k]+d+2)!!(R-1)^{[2k]+d-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}(R-1)^{2}\right\},

where
[k]
is the the integer part of
k
, at the first inequality we use the fact that
|\sin\psi|^{j}\leq 1
and at the second inequality we use
|\bm{x}|\geq 1
and
2k-1\leq[2k]
, and at the last inequality, we use Lemma
15
.

For the proof of (
11
), we start at (
12
). Replacing the term
2M|\bm{x}|^{k}+2B
with
2B\exp\left\{M|\bm{x}|^{k}\right\}
and using the same method, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(h\left(W\right)-h\circ P_{R}(W))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\int_{R-1}^{\infty}B^{2}|\bm{x}|^{d-1}\exp\left\{2M|\bm{x}|^{k}\right\}e^{-|\bm{x}|^{2}/2}d|\bm{x}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}B^{2}\int_{R-1}^{\infty}|\bm{x}|^{d}\exp\left\{2M|\bm{x}|^{k}\right\}e^{-|\bm{x}|^{2}/2}d|\bm{x}|,

(13)

where we use the fact that
|\bm{x}|\geq 1
. Note that by Young’s inequality, for any
\varepsilon>0

|\bm{x}|^{k}\leq\frac{\left(\varepsilon|\bm{x}|^{k}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}}{\frac{2}{k}}+\frac{\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-k}}}{\frac{2}{2-k}}=\frac{k}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{k}}|\bm{x}|^{2}+\frac{2-k}{2\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{2-k}}}.

Choose
\varepsilon=(4kM)^{-\frac{k}{2}}
, so that

2M|\bm{x}|^{k}\leq\frac{1}{4}|\bm{x}|^{2}+(4kM)^{\frac{k}{2-k}}.

Therefore, the right hand side of (
13
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}

\displaystyle B^{2}\exp\left\{(4kM)^{\frac{k}{2-k}}\right\}\int_{R-1}^{\infty}|\bm{x}|^{d}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bm{x}|^{2}}{4}\right\}d|\bm{x}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}B^{2}\exp\left\{(4kM)^{\frac{k}{2-k}}\right\}(d+2)!!(R-1)^{d-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}(R-1)^{2}\right\},

where in the above inequality, we use Lemma
15
.

The key to bound the QMC error lies in calculating the variation of the modified integrand
h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}
in the sense of Hardy and Krause. For
h
in different growth classes, corresponding upper bound estimates for the variation are given in the following lemma.

Lemma 17
.

For every fixed
M>0,B>0,k>0
and projection radius
R>0
,

\sup_{h\in G_{p}(M,B,k)}V_{\mathrm{HK}}(h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1})\leq 2^{2d}\left(Md^{\frac{k}{2}}R^{k+d}+BR^{d}\right),

(14)

\sup_{h\in G_{e}(M,B,k)}V_{\mathrm{HK}}(h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1})\leq 2^{2d}BR^{d}\exp\left\{M(\sqrt{d}R)^{k}\right\}.

(15)

Proof.

For the proof of (
14
), note that by Lemma
14
, the modified integrand
h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}([0,1]^{d})
. Therefore, we can use the definition of the variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause for smooth functions, i.e.,

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{HK}}(h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1})=\sum_{\varnothing\neq\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\int_{\left[0,1\right]^{d}}\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}\left(\bm{y_{u}}{:}\mathbf{1}_{-\bm{u}}\right)\right|d\bm{y}.

It suffices to analyze every term in the right hand side of the above equality. Recall the relation (
2
) for distribution function
\Phi
.

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}\left(\bm{y_{u}}{:}\mathbf{1}_{-\bm{u}}\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}h\left(P_{R}\left(\bm{x}_{\bm{u}}\right){:}P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}\left(\mathbf{1}_{-\bm{u}}\right)\right)\bigg|\cdot\left|\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\frac{dP_{R}(\Phi^{-1}(y_{j}))}{d\Phi^{-1}(y_{j})}\frac{d\Phi^{-1}(y_{j})}{dy_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(Md^{\frac{k}{2}}|R|^{k}+B\right)\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x_{j}|\leq R\}}\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left\{\frac{|x_{j}|^{2}}{2}\right\},

(16)

where we use
h\in G_{p}(M,B,k)
and the conditions
iii)
and
iv)
in Lemma
12
for the smooth projection operator
P_{R}
. Integrating both sides of (
16
) and changing the variable from
\bm{y}
to
\bm{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\left[0,1\right]^{d}}

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}\left(\bm{y_{u}};\mathbf{1}_{-\bm{u}}\right)\right|d\bm{y}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \leq\left(Md^{\frac{k}{2}}R^{k}+B\right)\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\int_{-R}^{R}dx_{j}\prod_{j\in\overline{\bm{u}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|x_{j}|^{2}}{2}\right\}dx_{j}

(17)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \leq 2^{d}\left(Md^{\frac{k}{2}}R^{k+d}+BR^{d}\right),

where
\overline{\bm{u}}
is the complement of
\bm{u}
. It follows from the above inequality that

V_{\mathrm{HK}}(h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1})\leq 2^{2d}\left(Md^{\frac{k}{2}}R^{k+d}+BR^{d}\right).

Therefore, (
14
) holds. For the proof of (
15
), it suffices to replace the
Md^{\frac{k}{2}}R^{k}+B
in (
17
) with
B\exp\left\{M(\sqrt{d}R)^{k}\right\}
.

The projection operator plays a crucial role in computing the variation of functions in the sense of Hardy and Krause. Through compositing the projection operator, the non-zero region of the mixed partial derivative of the modified integrand is restricted by
R
, so the variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause can be bounded. By combining the two lemmas above, we obtain the following error bounds.

Theorem 18
.

Let
\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}
be a low-discrepancy point set. The P-QMC method to approximate the integral
\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]
is

\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j}).

(18)

(I) For polynomial growth class
G_{p}(M,B,k)
, by choosing
R=\sqrt{4\log n}+1
, we obtain

\sup_{h\in G_{p}(M,B,k)}\left|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\right|=O\left(n^{-1}(\log n)^{\frac{3d}{2}+\frac{k}{2}-1}\right).

(19)

(II) For exponential growth class
G_{e}(M,B,k)
with order
0<k<2
, by choosing
R=\sqrt{8\log n}+1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in G_{e}(M,B,k)}\left|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\right|=O\left(n^{-1}(\log n)^{\frac{3d}{2}-1}\exp\left\{M(8d\log n)^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\}\right).

(20)

Proof.

For the proof of (
19
), note that

\displaystyle\bigg|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\bigg|\leq\bigg|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h\circ P_{R}(W)\right]\bigg|+\mathbb{E}\bigg|h\circ P_{R}(W)-h(W)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{HK}}(h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1})D^{*}_{n}\left(\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}\right)+\sqrt{C_{1}}(R-1)^{\frac{[2k]+d-1}{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}(R-1)^{2}\right\}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2d}C\left(Md^{\frac{k}{2}}R^{k+d}+BR^{d}\right)\frac{(\log n)^{d-1}}{n}+\sqrt{C_{1}}(R-1)^{\frac{[2k]+d-1}{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}(R-1)^{2}\right\},

(22)

where
C
and
C_{1}
are constants. The first term of (
21
) is obtained by Koksma-Hlawka inequality and the second term is due to (
10
) in Lemma
16
. To make magnitude of the both term of (
22
) approximately the same, we choose
R=\sqrt{4\log n}+1
, and then the second term is
O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{([2k]+d-1)/4})
and the first term is
O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{3d/2+k/2-1})
. Therefore, by taking supreme at the both sides of (
22
), we obtain (
19
). The proof for (
20
) is straight forward.

Remark 19
.

In both cases of Theorem
18
, the convergence rate is
O(n^{-1+\varepsilon})
, and all we need to notice is that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
0<k<2
, with
t=\log n
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\exp\left\{M\left(\sqrt{8d\log n}\right)^{k}\right\}}{n^{\varepsilon}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left\{M(8d)^{\frac{k}{2}}t^{\frac{k}{2}}-\varepsilon t\right\}=0.

However, the results in Theorem
18
offer a more detailed analysis compared to the results presented in Owen
[
24
]
. These findings indicate that as the function grows faster, the performance of the QMC method becomes worse.

Corollary 20
.

Let
\left\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\right\}
be an RQMC point set used in the estimator
\widehat{I}_{n}(h)
given by (
1
) such that each
\bm{y}_{j}\sim U[0,1]^{d}
and

\mathbb{E}\left[D^{*}_{n}\left(\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}\right)\right]\leq C\frac{(\log n)^{d-1}}{n},

where
C
is a constant independent of
n
.

(I) For the polynomial growth class
G_{p}\left(M,B,k\right)
with
k>0
,

\sup_{h\in G_{p}(M,B,k)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h)-\mathbb{E}[h(W)]\right)^{2}\right]=O\left(n^{-2}(\log n)^{3d+k-2}\right).

(II) For the exponential growth class
G_{e}\left(M,B,k\right)
with order
0<k<2
,

\sup_{h\in G_{e}(M,B,k)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h)-\mathbb{E}[h(W)]\right)^{2}\right]=O\left(n^{-2}(\log n)^{3d-2}\exp\left\{2M(8d\log n)^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\}\right).

Proof.

Recall the estimator
\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)
defined by (
18
). It suffices to note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h)-\mathbb{E}[h(W)]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left[(\widehat{I}_{n}(h)-\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h))^{2}\right]+3\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)-\mathbb{E}\left[h\circ P_{R}(W)\right]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+3\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h\circ P_{R}(W)-h(W)\right)^{2}\right],

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h)-\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j})-h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h(W)-h\circ P_{R}(W)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h(W)-h\circ P_{R}(W)\right)^{2}\right].

The desired results can be obtained by the same way as in Theorem
18
.

Remark 21
.

When using the RQMC point set as samples, we can drop the projection operator and simply use
\widehat{I}_{n}(h)
as the estimator. The RMSE rate is still the same. Due to Propositions
6
and
7
, a scrambled
(t,m,d)
-net satisfies the condition in Corollary
20
. Owen
[
24
]
only provided the convergence rate in the sense of mean error. He et al.
[
11
]
showed that this rate holds also for RMSE by using scrambled digital nets. But this is not true if using other randomization methods, such as digitally shifted nets. In contrast, we use a different framework to provide the RMSE rate for general RQMC point sets satisfying
\mathbb{E}\left[D^{*}_{n}\left(\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}\right)\right]=O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{d-1})
, including both digitally shifted nets and scrambled nets.

5
Importance sampling based methods

Importance sampling methods reduce variance and speed up convergence in Monte Carlo (MC) methods by choosing a suitable importance density. However, in QMC methods, there is no intuitive way to theoretically justify the role of importance sampling as in MC methods.

We will show theoretically how importance sampling improves QMC. We use a distribution with heavier tails than normal to achieve two things. First, as the projection radius
R
goes to infinity, importance sampling preserves the convergence rate of the projection error to zero. Second, the heavy-tailed density slows down the QMC error divergence to infinity significantly. Moreover, by applying the importance sampling and replacing the low-discrepancy points with scrambled net, we obtain the convergence rate
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon})
which is better than Owen
[
24
]
and He et al.
[
11
]
.

We give a brief introduction to the importance sampling method. Suppose that
\varphi
and
g
are the density functions of
d
-dimensional random variables
W
and
\tau
, respectively, then for any
h
is integrable under the measure induced by
W
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(W\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\bm{x})\varphi(\bm{x})d\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\bm{x})\frac{h(\bm{x})}{g(\bm{x})}g(\bm{x})d\bm{x}=\mathbb{E}\left[h_{\mathrm{IS}}\left(\tau\right)\right],

where

h_{\mathrm{IS}}:=\varphi\frac{h}{g}

(23)

is the weighted function obtained after using importance sampling.
Moreover, suppose that
\tau
is a
d
-dimensional random variable with each component being independent of each other, i.e., the density function of
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{d})
has the form

g(\bm{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(x_{j}),

(24)

where
g_{j}
is the marginal density function of
\tau_{j}
. Let
F_{j}
be the distribution function of
\tau_{j}
. For
\bm{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})
, denote
F^{-1}(\bm{y})=(F_{1}^{-1}(y_{1}),\dots,F_{d}^{-1}(y_{d}))
.

Our method is to apply the projection operator to
h_{\mathrm{IS}}
and using the IS-based P-QMC method to obtain the quadrature rule

\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j}),

(25)

where
\{\bm{y}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a low-discrepancy point set. Moreover, if
\left\{\bm{y}_{j}\right\}
is a scrambled
(\lambda,t,m,d)
-net, then we drop the projection operator and use IS-based RQMC method to obtain the estimator

\widehat{I}_{n}(h_{\mathrm{IS}}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ F^{-1}\left(\bm{y}_{j}\right).

(26)

We claim that our IS-based P-QMC and IS-based RQMC method can give the convergence rate for integrands in fast growth classes (see Definition
10
), which are not “QMC-friendly”, and achieve better convergence rates of
O\left(n^{-1+\varepsilon}\right)
and
O\left(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon}\right)
, respectively. Note that
G_{e}(M,B,2)
is a larger set, so our convergence rate still holds for other growth conditions. In the following parts, we consider that
h/g
has the fast growth, i.e.,
h/g\in G_{e}(M,B,2)\ \text{where}\ M<1/2
.

5.1
Importance sampling based P-QMC methods

In this part, we derive the projection error caused by the projection method for the integrand after importance sampling, and then we derive the upper bound of variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause. By selecting an appropriate projection radius
R
, we obtain the convergence rate of importance sampling based P-QMC method. The following lemma provides an estimate for the derivatives of the weighted function
h_{\mathrm{IS}}
.

Lemma 22
.

If
h/g\in G_{e}\left(M,B,2\right),\ M<1/2
and
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
, then

\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{x})\right|\leq 2^{|\bm{u}|}B|\bm{x}|^{|\bm{u}|}\exp\left\{-(\frac{1}{2}-M)|\bm{x}|^{2}\right\},

(27)

Proof.

For any
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
, note that

\displaystyle\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}

\displaystyle=\partial^{\bm{u}}\left(\varphi\frac{h}{g}\right)=\sum_{\bm{u_{1}}+\bm{u_{2}}=\bm{u}}\partial^{\bm{u_{1}}}\varphi\partial^{\bm{u_{2}}}\frac{h}{g}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{|\bm{x}|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{\bm{u_{1}}+\bm{u_{2}}=\bm{u}}\partial^{\bm{u_{2}}}\frac{h}{g}\prod_{j\in\bm{u_{1}}}(-x_{j}),

where
\bm{u}_{1}+\bm{u}_{2}=\bm{u}
means that
\bm{u}_{1}\cup\bm{u}_{2}=\bm{u}
and
\bm{u}_{1}\cap\bm{u}_{2}=\varnothing
. Taking the modulus on both sides of the above formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{x})\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{|\bm{x}|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{\bm{u_{1}}+\bm{u_{2}}=\bm{u}}Be^{M|\bm{x}|^{2}}\prod_{j\in\bm{u_{1}}}\left|x_{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{|\bm{x}|^{2}}{2}}\left(\sum_{\bm{u_{1}}+\bm{u_{2}}=\bm{u}}1\right)Be^{M|\bm{x}|^{2}}|\bm{x}|^{|\bm{u}|}

(28)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{|\bm{x}|^{2}}{2}}2^{|\bm{u}|}B\exp\left\{M|\bm{x}|^{2}\right\}|\bm{x}|^{|\bm{u}|}.

(29)

In (
28
) we use the fact that
|x_{j}|\leq|\bm{x}|
, and in (
29
) we use

\sum_{\bm{u_{1}}+\bm{u_{2}}=\bm{u}}1=2^{|\bm{u}|}.

Remark 23
.

Note that the right hand side of (
27
) is monotonically decreasing with respect to
|\bm{x}|
when
|\bm{x}|\geq\sqrt{\frac{|\bm{u}|}{1-2M}}
and converge to 0 when
|\bm{x}|\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, there exists a constant
C(M,B,d)
depending only on
M,B,d
, which dominates
\sup_{\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{x})\right|
.

The following lemma estimates the mean squared error which dominates the projection error.

Lemma 24
.

Assume
M<1/2
and
R\geq 1+1/\sqrt{1-2M}
. For every
h
and
g
satisfying
h/g\in G_{e}\left(M,B,2\right)
and
\mathbb{E}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\leq A
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\left(\tau\right)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}\left(\tau\right))^{2}\right]\leq 16AB^{2}d(R-1)^{2}\exp\left\{-(1-2M)(R-1)^{2}\right\}.

(30)

Proof.

Denote
H=[-R+1,R-1]^{d}
. Note that by the definition of projection operator
P_{R}
in (
6
),
P_{R}(\bm{x})=\bm{x}
for any
\bm{x}\in H
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\left(\tau\right)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}\left(\tau\right))^{2}\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus H}\bigg|h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{x})-h_{\mathrm{IS}}(P_{R}(\bm{x}))\bigg|^{2}g(\bm{x})d\bm{x},

(31)

where
g
is the density function of
\tau
. By the Lagrange mean value theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{x})-h_{\mathrm{IS}}(P_{R}(\bm{x}))\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\nabla h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{\xi_{x}})\cdot\left(\bm{x}-P_{R}(\bm{x})\right)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\nabla h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{\xi_{x}})\bigg|^{2}\bigg|\bm{x}-P_{R}(\bm{x})\bigg|^{2}.

(32)

where
\bm{\xi_{x}}
lies on the line segment connecting points
\bm{x}
and
P_{R}(\bm{x})
, and
\nabla
represents the gradient operator. Note that

\bigg|\bm{x}-P_{R}(\bm{x})\bigg|^{2}\leq 2\left(|\bm{x}|^{2}+|P_{R}(\bm{x})|^{2}\right)\leq 4|\bm{x}|^{2}.

(33)

And if
|\bm{u}|=1
, then by Lemma
22
and Remark
23
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{\xi_{x}})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2B|\bm{\xi_{x}}|\exp\left\{-(\frac{1}{2}-M)|\bm{\xi_{x}}|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2B(R-1)\exp\left\{-(\frac{1}{2}-M)(R-1)^{2}\right\}.

(34)

In (
34
), note that by Proposition
13
,
|\bm{\xi_{x}}|\geq R-1
for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus H
and by Remark
23
, the function is decreasing with respect to
|\bm{x}|
when
|\bm{x}|\geq 1/\sqrt{1-2M}
. Therefore,

\bigg|\nabla h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\bm{\xi_{x}})\bigg|\leq 2B\sqrt{d}(R-1)\exp\left\{-(\frac{1}{2}-M)(R-1)^{2}\right\}.

(35)

As a result, (
30
) follows from (
31
), (
33
) and (
35
).

Remark 25
.

By Lemma
24
, we can drive the estimate of projection error. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg|h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}(\tau)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\tau)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\left(\tau\right)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}\left(\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{Ad}B(R-1)\exp\left\{-\left(\frac{1}{2}-M\right)\left(R-1\right)^{2}\right\}.

Next, we aim to demonstrate that importance sampling can decelerate the rate at which the variation, in the sense of Hardy and Krause, diverges towards infinity. Combining the results of Lemma
22
and Remark
23
, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 26
.

After using importance sampling, the variation of the modified integrand
h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}
in the sense of Hardy and Krause has a uniform upper bound for every
h
and
g
satisfying
h/g\in G_{e}(M,B,2)

\sup_{h/g\in G_{e}(M,B,2)}V_{\mathrm{HK}}(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1})\leq C(M,B,d)2^{2d}R^{d}.

(36)

Proof.

The proof is nearly the same as in Lemma
17
, and all we need is to note that by Lemma
22
and Remark
23
, every mixed derivative of
h_{\mathrm{IS}}
is dominated by some constant only depends on
M,B
and
k
. Therefore, for any
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
, we can rewrite (
17
) as

\displaystyle\int_{\left[0,1\right]^{d}}

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}\left(\bm{y}_{\bm{u}};\mathbf{1}_{-\bm{u}}\right)\right|d\bm{y}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \leq C\left(M,B,d\right)\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\int_{-R}^{R}dx_{j}\prod_{j\in\overline{\bm{u}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{j}(x_{j})dx_{j}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \leq C\left(M,B,d\right)2^{d}R^{d},

and (
36
) follows from that there are at most
2^{d}
such terms in the definition of
V_{\mathrm{HK}}
(see Definition
3
).

Combining Remark
25
and Lemma
26
and using the same method in the proof of Theorem
18
, we obtain the convergence rate for importance sampling based P-QMC method.

Theorem 27
.

Assume
M<1/2
and
\mathbb{E}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\leq A
. Let
\left\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\right\}
be a low-discrepancy point set.
By choosing
R=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-2M}\log n}+1
, we obtain

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h/g\in G_{e}(M,B,2)\\
\mathbb{E}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\leq A\end{subarray}}\bigg|\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\bigg|=O\left(n^{-1}(\log n)^{\frac{3d}{2}-1}\right).

Remark 28
.

Note that this theorem tells us that by combining importance sampling, for polynomial growth classes, the degree of
\log n
on the denominator of the convergence rate has been reduced from
3d/2+k/2-1
to
3d/2-1
. For exponential growth classes, there is no exponential term on the denominator of the convergence rate. Moverover, for fast growth class, without IS, the convergence rate is
O\left(n^{-1+2M+\varepsilon}\right)
, and by applying IS, we achieve the convergence rate
O(n^{-1}(\log n)^{3d/2-1})
. This shows that after combining importance sampling, theoretically we obtain a faster convergence rate.

5.2
Importance sampling based RQMC methods

The results of the previous sections are based on the framework of the Koksma-Hlawka inequality, so they hold for any low-discrepancy point set. When replacing the QMC points with randomized QMC (RQMC) points, we obtain the importance sampling based RQMC method. This method can achieve a faster convergence rate of
O(n^{-3/2+\epsilon})
.

Owen
[
23
,
25
]
gave a variance estimation of scrambled net for smooth functions on
[0,1]^{d}
, and the variance is related to the infinity norm of the integrand. One thing to note is that the original integrand
h\circ\Phi^{-1}
does not satisfy the condition, because it has singularities. However, when we use the projection method, that is, the original integrand is composed with the projection operator, the modified integrand
h\circ P_{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}
or
h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}
is smooth and we can estimate its infinity norm. The following result is from Owen
[
25
]
, which consider the scrambled
\left(\lambda,t,m,d\right)
-net (see Definition
5
).

Lemma 29
.

Let
f(\bm{x})
be a smooth function. Suppose
that
\{\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{n}\}
is a
(\lambda,t,m,d)
-net in base
b\geq 2
with
n=\lambda b^{m}
. If
\{\bm{y}_{j}\}
is the
scrambled version of
\{\bm{a}_{j}\}
, then with
1\leq\lambda<b
and
m>t+d
,

\mathrm{Var}\bigg(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}f(\bm{y}_{j})\bigg)\leq C\frac{(\log n)^{d-1}}{n^{3}}\max_{\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\|\partial^{\bm{u}}f\|_{\infty}^{2},

(37)

where
C
is a constant that depends only on
b,\lambda,t,d
.

Recall that
h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}([0,1]^{d})
. Therefore, it satisfies the condition of the Lemma
29
. Next, we give an upper bound of the infinity norm of the modified integrand
h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}
.

Lemma 30
.

Suppose that
M<1/2
, then for
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
and
h
and
g
satisfying
h/g\in G_{e}(M,B,2)
, there exists a constant
C(M,B,d)
such that

\|\partial^{\bm{u}}(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1})\|_{\infty}\leq C(M,B,d)\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\sup_{|x_{j}|\leq R}\frac{1}{g_{j}(x_{j})}.

(38)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\partial^{\bm{u}}\left(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}\right)\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y})\cdot\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\left(P_{R}^{\prime}(x_{j})\frac{1}{g_{j}(x_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\partial^{\bm{u}}h_{\mathrm{IS}}\right)\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y})\cdot\prod_{j\in\bm{u}}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\left\{|x_{j}|\leq R\right\}}\frac{1}{g_{j}(x_{j})}\right),

(39)

where
\bm{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})
and
x_{j}=F_{j}^{-1}(y_{j})
. By Lemma
22
and Remark
23
, (
38
) follows from (
39
).

Furthermore, we impose some specific restrictions on the importance density. Assumme
\tau
is square integrable. We consider separately the cases where the importance density has polynomial and exponential growth. Define the following two sets:

\displaystyle V_{p}(k):=\bigg\{(h,\tau):\frac{h}{g}\in G_{e}(M,B,2),\ \max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{1}{g_{j}(x)}\leq M_{1}|x|^{k}+B_{1}\ \text{and}\ \mathbb{E}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\leq A\bigg\},

\displaystyle V_{e}(k):=\bigg\{(h,\tau):\frac{h}{g}\in G_{e}(M,B,2),\ \max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{1}{g_{j}(x)}\leq B_{2}e^{M_{2}|x|^{k}}\ \text{and}\ \mathbb{E}\left|\tau\right|^{2}\leq A\bigg\}.

As before, we can balance the errors by choosing an appropriate projection radius
R
and drive the convergence rate for the IS-based RMQC method.

Theorem 31
.

Let
\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}
be a scrambled
(\lambda,t,m,d)
-net in base
b
with
n=\lambda b^{m}
, and suppose
M<1/2
. We have

\sup_{(h,\tau)\in V_{p}(k)}\mathbb{E}\left[(\widehat{I}_{n}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right])^{2}\right]=O\left(n^{-3}(\log n)^{(k+1)d}\right),

(40)

and

\sup_{(h,\tau)\in V_{e}(k)}\mathbb{E}\left[(\widehat{I}_{n}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right])^{2}\right]=O\left(n^{-3}(\log n)^{d}\exp\left\{2dM_{2}\left(\frac{3\log n}{1-2M}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\}\right).

Proof.

It suffices to prove (
40
). Let
(h,\tau)\in V_{p}(k)
and let
\{\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n}\}
be a scrambled
\left(\lambda,t,m,d\right)
-net. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\right)^{2}\bigg]\leq 3\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})\right)^{2}\bigg]

(41)

\displaystyle+3\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\mathbb{E}\left[h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}(\tau)\right]\right)^{2}\bigg]+3\left(\mathbb{E}\left[h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}(\tau)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\right)^{2}.

For the first term of the right hand side of (
41
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})\right)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j})-h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j})\right)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j})-h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ F^{-1}(\bm{y}_{j})\right)^{2}\right]

(42)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}(\tau)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right],

(43)

where in (
42
), we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and (
43
) follows from that every
\bm{y}_{j}
has the uniform distribution on
[0,1]^{d}
(see Proposition
7
).

Note that the second term of the right hand side of (
41
) is
3\mathrm{Var}\left(\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})\right)
and the third term is bounded by
3\mathbb{E}\left[(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}(\tau)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\tau))^{2}\right]
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\widehat{I}_{n}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})-\mathbb{E}\left[h(W)\right]\right)^{2}\bigg]\leq 3\mathrm{Var}\left(\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})\right)+6\mathbb{E}\left[\big(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}(\tau)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\tau)\big)^{2}\right].

(44)

Using the results in Lemma
29
and Lemma
30
, when
n
is large enough so that
m>t+d
, the first term of the right hand side of (
44
) has the following upper bounds.

\displaystyle 3\mathrm{Var}\left(\widehat{I}_{n}^{R}(h_{\mathrm{IS}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d}\|\partial^{\bm{u}}(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}\circ F^{-1})\|_{\infty}^{2}\frac{(\log n)^{d-1}}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{d}\sup_{|x_{j}|\leq R}\frac{1}{g_{j}^{2}(x_{j})}\frac{(\log n)^{d-1}}{n^{3}}

(45)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(M_{1}R^{k}+B_{1}\right)^{2d}\frac{(\log n)^{d-1}}{n^{3}},

(46)

where
C
is a constant only depends on
M,B,M_{1},t,b,d
. The inequality (
45
) follows from Lemma
30
, and (
46
) follows from
\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{1}{g_{j}}\leq M_{1}|x|^{k}+B_{1}
.

By Lemma
24
,

\displaystyle 6\mathbb{E}\left[\left(h_{\mathrm{IS}}\circ P_{R}(\tau)-h_{\mathrm{IS}}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right]\leq 96AB^{2}d(R-1)^{2}\exp\left\{-(1-2M)(R-1)^{2}\right\}.

(47)

To balance two terms (

) and (

), we choose

Therefore, (
46
) achieves
O\left(n^{-3}(\log n)^{(k+1)d-1}\right)
, and (
47
) achieves
O\left(n^{-3}\log n\right)
. This proves the desired result.

Remark 32
.

It is important to note that without importance sampling, the RQMC method cannot achieve a higher convergence rate of
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon})
, due to the infinity norm of the derivatives of the integrand cannot be controlled by a constant and the right hand side of (
38
) is
O(\exp\left\{dR^{2}\right\})
, thus we can not find a projection radius
R
to make the projection error and QMC error both converge at a rate of
O(n^{-3/2+\varepsilon}).

The conditions for Theorems
27
and
31
are about
h
and
g
. If we fix an appropriate importance density, the conditions of the theorems can be directly restricted to
h
. The following section will provide a detailed discussion on this perspective. Note that, the next section uses the
t
-distribution as an example of an IS proposal. However, our framework is highly versatile, and many distributions with heavier tails than the normal distribution may meet the conditions of Theorem
31
and be used as an IS proposal.

5.3
The choice of the IS density

We use a heavy-tailed distribution as the IS proposal to accelerate the convergence rate. We introduce a class of importance densities that satisfy the conditions of the above theorems. Take the
t
-distribution as the proposal of importance sampling. The density function of
\tau
is

g(\bm{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(x_{j})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\nu_{j}+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\nu_{j}\pi}\Gamma(\frac{\nu_{j}}{2})}\left(1+\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\nu_{j}}\right)^{-(\nu_{j}+1)/2}.

(48)

Each component of
\tau
is a
t
-distribution with parameter
\nu_{j}
. Let
\nu=\max_{j}\nu_{j}
. We can easily verify that there exist
M(\nu)>0
and
B(\nu)>0
, such that

7
Conclusion

Using the projection based quasi-Monte Carlo method on various growth classes, we attained more refined results than Owen
[
24
]
’s. Our framework dictates that the convergence rate is contingent upon the growth condition of the integrand. For integrand with fast growth, both QMC and MC methods manifest inferior performance. Nevertheless, by using importance sampling with a heavy-tailed proposal, we achieved better convergence rates. This assertion is corroborated by our numerical experiments. In this paper, we do not focus on reducing the Monte Carlo variance through importance sampling. It is desirable to develop a good importance sampling to reduce Monte Carlo variance while retaining the faster convergence rate of QMC. This is left for future work.

Appendix

In many problems, the integrand that we consider, such as the loss function in deep learning, has a very complex form (it is obtained by some basic operations on different functions). We refer to Huré et al.
[
14
]
and Beck et al.
[
2
]
for details. For such a complex integrand, it is not easy to verify directly which growth class it belongs to, but the growth conditions of the functions that compose the integrand are easy to verify. Therefore, we need to discuss what kind of growth class we get after applying some operations on the basic growth classes. We use the following notation.

Definition 35
.

If
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are two function classes, then for
\otimes=+,\times,\circ
, we define

S_{1}\otimes S_{2}:=\left\{h_{1}\otimes h_{2}:\ h_{1}\in S_{1},\ h_{2}\in S_{2}\right\}.

and we define the corresponding scalar operations for the case that
S_{1}
is a constant.

The growth classes that we defined have properties similar to those of a linear space, except that the corresponding parameters may change. From Theorem
18
, we know that for polynomial case, the convergence rate is determined by
k
, and for exponential case, the convergence rate is determined by
M
and
k
. Therefore, in the following calculations, we will focus on the changes of these coefficients, and the specific forms of other coefficients are irrelevant for our analysis.

Theorem 36
.

Assume
M,M_{1},M_{2},B,B_{1},B_{2},k,k_{1},k_{2}
are all positive.

(1)

Scalar multiplication.

Assume
c\neq 0
is a constant. For the polynomial case,

c\times G_{p}(M,B,k)=G_{p}(|c|M,|c|B,k).

For the exponential case,

c\times G_{e}(M,B,k)=G_{e}(M,|c|B,k).

(2)

Addition.

The first case is the addition of two polynomial growth classes. There exist
M_{3}>0
and
B_{3}>0
, such that

G_{p}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1})+G_{p}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2})\subseteq G_{p}(M_{3},B_{3},\max\{k_{1},k_{2}\}).

The second case is the addition of two exponential growth classes. If
k_{1}>k_{2}
, then there exists
B_{3}>0
, such that

G_{e}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1})+G_{e}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2})\subseteq G_{e}(M_{1},B_{3},k_{1}).

If
k_{1}=k_{2}=k
, then

G_{e}(M_{1},B_{1},k)+G_{e}(M_{2},B_{2},k)\subseteq G_{e}(\max\{M_{1},M_{2}\},B_{1}+B_{2},k).

The third case is the addition of an exponential growth class and a polynomial growth class. There exists
B_{3}>0
, such that

G_{e}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1})+G_{p}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2})\subseteq G_{e}(M_{1},B_{3},k_{1}).

(3)

Multiplication.

The first case is the multiplication of two polynomial growth classes. There exist
M_{3}>0
and
B_{3}>0
, such that

G_{p}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1})\times G_{p}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2})\subseteq G_{p}(M_{3},B_{3},k_{1}+k_{2}).

The second case is the multiplication of two exponential growth classes. If
k_{1}>k_{2}
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
B(\varepsilon)>0
, such that

G_{e}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1})\times G_{e}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2})\subseteq G_{e}(M_{1}+\varepsilon,B(\varepsilon),k_{1}).

If
k_{1}=k_{2}
, then there exists
B_{3}>0
, such that

G_{e}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1})\times G_{e}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2})\subseteq G_{e}(M_{1}+M_{2},B_{3},k_{1}).

The third case is the multiplication of an exponential growth class and a polynomial growth class. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
B(\varepsilon)>0
, such that

G_{e}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1})\times G_{p}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2})\subseteq G_{e}(M_{1}+\varepsilon,B(\varepsilon),k_{1}).

Proof.

The results of scalar multiplication are easy to verify. We briefly prove the other results. For any

f\in G_{p}(M_{1},B_{1},k_{1}),\ g\in G_{p}(M_{2},B_{2},k_{2}),

it is easy to verify that the result holds by choosing appropriate
(M_{3},B_{3})
for addition
+
. For multiplication
\times
, it suffices to note that for fixed
\bm{u}\subseteq 1{:}d
,

|\partial^{\bm{u}}(f\times g)|=\left|\sum_{\bm{u}_{1}+\bm{u}_{2}=\bm{u}}\partial^{\bm{u}_{1}}f\partial^{\bm{u}_{2}}g\right|\leq\sum_{\bm{u}_{1}+\bm{u}_{2}=\bm{u}}\left|\partial^{\bm{u}_{1}}f\partial^{\bm{u}_{2}}g\right|.

As for exponential case, note that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists constant
B
which depends on
\varepsilon
, such that

|\bm{x}|^{k_{2}}e^{M|\bm{x}|^{k_{1}}}\leq Be^{(M+\varepsilon)|\bm{x}|^{k_{1}}}.

It is easy to verify the conclusion of the theorem through these calculations.

For investigating composition operations, we need additional symbols. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
be the
n

\mathbb{Z}_{d}:=\left\{\bm{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d}\right):\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{N},\ j=1,\cdots,d\right\},

The module of

|\bm{\alpha}|:=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d},

and for

We take

The derivative notation to be used is then

D^{\bm{\alpha}}h(\bm{x}):=\frac{\partial^{|\bm{\alpha}|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}h\left(\bm{x}\right).

Definition 37
.

For

and

Eigenstate Fluctuation Theorem in the Short and Long Time Regimes
Eiki Iyoda 1 , Kazuya Kaneko 2 , Takahiro Sagawa 2,3

Abstract
The canonical ensemble plays a crucial role in statistical mechanics in and out of equilibrium. For example, the standard derivation of the fluctuation theorem relies on the assumption that the initial state of the heat bath is the canonical ensemble. On the other hand, the recent progress in the foundation of statistical mechanics has revealed that a thermal equilibrium state is not necessarily described by the canonical ensemble but can be a quantum pure state or even a single energy eigenstate, as formulated by the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH). Then, a question raised is how these two pictures, the canonical ensemble and a single energy eigenstate as a thermal equilibrium state, are compatible in the fluctuation theorem. In this paper, we theoretically and numerically show that the fluctuation theorem holds in both of the long and short-time regimes, even when the initial state of the bath is a single energy eigenstate of a many-body system. Our proof of the fluctuation theorem in the long-time regime is based on the ETH, while it was previously shown in the short-time regime on the basis of the Lieb-Robinson bound and the ETH [Phys. Rev. Lett. 119 , 100601 (2017)]. The proofs for these time regimes are theoretically independent and complementary, implying the fluctuation theorem in the entire time domain. We also perform a systematic numerical simulation of hard-core bosons by exact diagonalization and verify the fluctuation theorem in both of the time regimes by focusing on the finite-size scaling. Our results contribute to the understanding of the mechanism that the fluctuation theorem emerges from unitary dynamics of quantum many-body systems, and can be tested by experiments with, e.g., ultracold atoms.

Conventional statistical mechanics relies on the concept of ensembles, such as the microcanonical ensemble and the canonical ensemble. These are characterized as the maximum entropy states under certain energy constraints

The canonical ensemble plays a crucial role even beyond equilibrium situations.
For example, we can prove the second law of thermodynamics and the fluctuation theorem

Here, the fluctuation theorem is a universal relation that incorporates the role of fluctuations of the entropy production, represented as

The fluctuation theorem implies the second law at the average level, stating that the average entropy production is nonnegative:

\Delta S_{\mathrm{S}}

\beta

We note that the fluctuation theorem reproduces the second law through the Jensen inequality
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle\geq e^{-\langle\sigma\rangle}

A critical question here is how universally the thermodynamic properties appear without canonical ensembles, even in non-equilibrium situations.
In particular, it is interesting to investigate the validity of the fluctuation theorem: the question focuses on whether it is possible to understand how the fluctuation theorem and the second law emerge from quantum mechanics without assuming the conventional statistical ensembles.

A partial understanding of the above question has been addressed in recent papers.
Specifically, Ref.
Iyoda2017
theoretically showed the fluctuation theorem (and the second law) in the short-time regime, which is based on the ETH and the Lieb-Robinson bound
Lieb1972
;
Hastings2006
;
Haah2018

We note that in the numerical simulation of Ref.
Iyoda2017
, the initial state of the system was chosen to be a pure state, and the fluctuation theorem was apparently broken in the long-time regime.
This apparent breakdown originates from so-called absolute irreversibility
Murashita2014
;
Funo2015
induced by the pure initial state, and as will be shown in this paper, the fluctuation theorem still holds in the long-time regime if absolute irreversibility is properly treated.
Moreover, Ref.
Kaneko2017
theoretically showed the second law at the average level in the long-time regime on the basis of the ETH.
Also in previous papers
Jin2016
;
Schmidtke2018
, it has been numerically suggested that the fluctuation theorem holds even in the long-time regime.
Given these researches, it is desirable to make a comprehensive understanding of the validity of the fluctuation theorem in the entire time domain.

The main result of this paper is to show, analytically and numerically, that the fluctuation theorem holds with the non-canonical bath in both of the short and long-time regimes.
Specifically, the initial state of the bath is supposed to be a single energy eigenstate sampled from an energy shell.
This establishes that the universal property of non-equilibrium fluctuations in entropy production emerges even without the canonical ensemble.
In particular, since we prove the fluctuation theorem for the extreme case with a single energy eigenstate, the fluctuation theorem holds when the initial state of the bath is any mixture of energy eigenstates in the energy shell.

We discuss the entire time domain by considering the long and short-time regimes.
We theoretically show that the long-time average of the deviation from the fluctuation theorem vanishes in the thermodynamic limit of the heat bath.
To confirm the theory, we perform numerical simulations and show that the deviation decreases with the bath size
N
.
On the other hand, Ref.
Iyoda2017
theoretically showed the fluctuation theorem for the short-time regime in the thermodynamic limit on the basis of the Lieb-Robinson bound and the ETH.
While the theory of Ref.
Iyoda2017
is valid in the thermodynamic limit, the fluctuation theorem in the short-time regime for the numerically-accessible system size was not fully established in Ref.
Iyoda2017
because of the large finite-size effect.
In the present paper, we perform systematic numerical calculations to clarify whether the theoretical scenario is indeed relevant for the numerically-accessible system size.
In particular, we numerically investigate the bath-size dependence of the error term of the fluctuation theorem, which eventually excludes other scenarios than the theoretical scenario of Ref.
Iyoda2017
.

II.1
Fluctuation theorem for the canonical bath

As a preliminary, we consider the conventional setup of the fluctuation theorem.
The total system is composed of the system S and the heat bath B.
The Hamiltonian of the total system is written as

\displaystyle H:=H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{I}}+H_{\mathrm{B}},

(2)

where
H_{\mathrm{S}}
,
H_{\mathrm{B}}
are the Hamiltonian of system S and bath B respectively, and
H_{\mathrm{I}}(\neq 0)
describes the interaction between S and B.
The initial state of the total system SB is assumed to be a product state:

\displaystyle\rho(0):=\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0).

(3)

We also define the canonical ensembles of system S and bath B as
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}:=e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}/Z_{\mathrm{S}}
with
Z_{\mathrm{S}}:={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{S}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}]
and
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}:=e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}/Z_{\mathrm{B}}
with
Z_{\mathrm{B}}:={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}]
respectively.

The time evolution of the total system is given by
\rho(t)=U\rho_{{\mathrm{S}}}(0)\otimes\rho_{{\mathrm{B}}}(0)U^{\dagger}
, where
U(t):=e^{-iHt/\hbar}
is the unitary time evolution operator.
We write the reduced density operator of system S and bath B at time
t
as
\rho_{{\mathrm{S}}}(t):={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[\rho(t)],\rho_{{\mathrm{B}}}(t):={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{S}}[\rho(t)]
, respectively.

In this setup, we explain the concept of the stochastic entropy production
\sigma

Esposito2009
.
To introduce it,
let us consider an operator
\sigma(t):=-\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)+\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}
(see
Sagawa2012
;
Manzano2018
;
Landi2021
for the same approach to the stochastic entropy production).
The first term on the right-hand side is the informational contribution, whose average is the von Neumann entropy of the system
S_{\mathrm{S}}(t):=-{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{S}}[\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]
.
The second term of
\sigma(t)
is the thermal contribution that gives the heat term.

While
\sigma(t)
is not an ordinary physical observable because it depends on the state
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)
,
the above definition of
\sigma(t)
enables us to treat various physically-relevant situations in a unified manner. One of typical situations is that the initial state of system S is the canonical ensemble, in which the measurement of
\sigma(t)
is equivalent to the measurement of energy, because
-\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}=\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}+\ln Z_{\mathrm{S}}
.
If we do not have any prior knowledge about the state of the system, we can measure
-\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)
by performing quantum state tomography of
\rho_{S}(t)
beforehand
Vogel1989
. In fact, by quantum state tomography, we obtain the information about the eigenbasis of
\rho_{S}(t)
and its eigenvalues. Then, we can perform the projection measurement of the eigenbasis and associate an eigenvalue with the outcome.

We consider the projection measurement of
\sigma(t)
at time
0
and
t
.
Let the measurement outcomes be

\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}
at
t=0
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}
at
t
.
We then define the stochastic entropy production
\sigma
as
\sigma:=\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}-\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}
, whose average
\langle\sigma\rangle=\Delta S_{\mathrm{S}}-\beta Q
is the average entropy production of the total system.
Here,
Q
is the heat emitted from bath B to system S, defined as
Q:=-{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[H_{\mathrm{B}}(\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(t)-\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0))].

For the conventional fluctuation theorem, the initial state of bath B is assumed to be the canonical ensemble
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)=\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
.
The fluctuation theorem states that

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle=1.

(4)

It is straightforward to show
Esposito2009
that
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle=\mathrm{tr}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}Ue^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}U^{\dagger}\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)].

(5)

Substituting
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}=e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}/Z_{\mathrm{B}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}Ue^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\frac{e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}}{Z_{\mathrm{B}}}U^{\dagger}\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\right]

(6)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}}{Z_{\mathrm{B}}}UU^{\dagger}\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\right]

(7)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}\right]

(8)

\displaystyle=1.

(9)

We note that if absolute irreversibility
Murashita2014
;
Funo2015
occurs, the fluctuation theorem is modified (see details in Appendix
A
).
Absolute irreversibility is an apparent violation of the fluctuation theorem that occurs when
\rho_{S}(0)
does not have the full rank (e.g.,
\rho_{S}(0)
is a pure state).
The modification term appears in the form of

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle=1-\lambda(t).

(10)

See Appendix
A
for details.
Absolute irreversibility can be understood as a kind of singularity about the initial state of system S. We emphasize that this is not a purely mathematical problem, but can be observed experimentally
Masuyama2018
.

\displaystyle\delta n_{\mathrm{S}}:={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{S}}[n_{0}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)-\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0))].

The initial state of bath B is an energy eigenstate of

We perform the calculation of the fluctuation theorem for each energy eigenstate in the energy shell and investigate the dependence of the error of the fluctuation theorem on the bath size and the initial state of bath B.
We define the inverse temperature of

as
\beta_{\alpha}
satisfying
E_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}(\beta_{\alpha})H_{\mathrm{B}}]

The onsite potential
\omega
is determined such that the canonical expectation of the particle number of the bath equals
N_{\mathrm{P}}
:
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[n_{\mathrm{B}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}]=N_{\mathrm{P}}
.
We set
\omega
as above, because the ETH is satisfied only within each particle number sector, and
N_{\mathrm{P}}
should be close to the canonical expectation number.
To investigate the bath size dependence, we set
L_{x}=3
,
L_{y}=3,4,5

III
Overview of the Results

In this section, we give an overview of the main results of this paper: the fluctuation theorem holds in both the long and short-time regimes (as defined below) in the setting of the previous section.
The proof of the fluctuation theorem in the long-time regime is based on the ETH of
H
(Sec.
IV

Also, the fluctuation theorem in the short-time regime has been shown on the basis of the ETH of
H_{\mathrm{B}}
and the Lieb-Robinson bound
Iyoda2017
(Sec.

The proofs of the fluctuation theorem in these time regimes are theoretically independent of each other and play complementary roles.
Our systematic numerical calculations about the bath size dependence support the theories in both of the long and short-time regimes.

This section is organized as follows.
In Sec.

In Secs.

In Sec.
III.4
, we comment on the two time regimes.

III.2
Long-time regime

We consider the fluctuation theorem in the long-time regime.
In this paper, we prove that
\overline{\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle}
, the long-time average of
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
, nearly equals
1
in the large-bath limit.
Note that we denote the long-time average of any quantity
O(t)
as

\displaystyle\overline{O(t)}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}O(t)dt.

(37)

In the special case that the condition
(
17
) is satisfied, we can prove that
|\overline{\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle}-1|=o(1)
holds, where the right-hand side represents the asymptotic behavior with respect to the bath size
N
.

If Eq. (
17
) is not strictly satisfied, the interaction-induced error
\delta G_{\mathrm{I}}
defined in Sec.
III.1
can generally appear in the fluctuation theorem.
However, the interaction-induced error vanishes in the thermodynamic limit under the rotating wave approximation, which holds well in the long-time regime (see Appendix
C
for details).

In the absence of localization
Pal2010
;
Nandkishore2015
or persistent oscillations
Turner2018
,
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
relaxes to the long-time average
\overline{\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle}
after the finite relaxation time
\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}
.
We refer to the regime of
t
satisfying
\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}\lesssim t
as the long-time regime.
We argue that the relaxation time satisfies
\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}=\Theta(1)
with respect to the bath size
N
for the following reason.
The relaxation of
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
can be associated with observables of system S, because under the condition (
17
), the heat
Q
is replaced by the energy change of system S.
Under physically reasonable conditions, the relaxation time of observables of system S is
\Theta(1)
when the initial state of bath B is the microcanonical ensemble
Garcia2017
.
We note that even when the condition (
17
) does not strictly hold,
we can replace the heat Q under the rotating wave approximation, which again implies the relaxation time of
\Theta(1)
.
We note that Ref.
Reimann2016
showed that the relaxation time is independent of the size of the total system if the Hamiltonian is random, and that numerical results consistent with the random Hamiltonians have been obtained with realistic models
Hetterich2015
;
Rigol2009
.
In summary, we argue that
\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}=\Theta(1)
holds in our setup, which is consistent with our own numerics as well.

We briefly remark on absolute irreversibility (see Appendices
A
and
F.3
for details).
As mentioned in the introduction, the numerical calculations in Ref.
Iyoda2017
showed that
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
is smaller than
1
in the long-time regime, which was argued to be a violation of the fluctuation theorem.
As a matter of fact, this is due to the numerical setup of Ref.
Iyoda2017
that the initial state of system S is a pure state, causing absolute irreversibility.
That is, the reason why the fluctuation theorem in the long-time regime appeared to be broken in the numerical calculations of Ref.
Iyoda2017
is that the correction term
\lambda
in Eq. (
10
) was not taken into account, while the numerical calculation itself is correct.
In the present paper, we prove and numerically confirm that the fluctuation theorem holds in the long-time regime with absolute irreversibility and the energy eigenstate bath, if the correction term
\lambda
is taken into account.
Finally, we remark that there is an alternative approach to make the fluctuation theorem hold by regularizing the pure initial state
HevelingPC
.

We next consider the short-time regime.
To define the short-time regime in line with Ref.
Iyoda2017
, we divide bath B into
{\mathrm{B}}_{0}
and
{\mathrm{B}}_{1}

{\mathrm{B}}_{0}

{\mathrm{B}}_{1}

Specifically, under the above choice of the size of

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{LR}}=\Theta(N^{\mu}).

(38)

We note that the error of the fluctuation theorem is

III.4
On the two time regimes

We emphasize that the short and long time regimes are defined and analyzed theoretically independently.
The short-time regime is defined by the Lieb-Robinson time, which increases at most linearly with the bath size, while the long-time regime is defined by coincidence with the long-time average, which covers a very long time scale characterized by the quantum recurrence time that increases doubly exponentially with the bath size
Bocchieri1957
;
Percival1961
;
Venuti2015
.
From the argument in Secs.
III.2
and
III.3
, the short and long-time regimes have an overlap and thus cover the entire time domain as shown in Fig.
3
, when the bath size is sufficiently large (see also Sec.
V.1
).

Suppose that the time evolution of the error is given as in Fig.
3
, i.e., the error of the fluctuation theorem initially increases in
t^{2}
and relaxes monotonically to the long-time average after the
N
-independent relaxation time. Then, the fluctuation theorem in the long-time regime implies that in the short-time regime, and vice versa.
In general, however, the fluctuation theorem in these time regimes are theoretically shown independently, and they together show the fluctuation theorem in the entire time domain.
In fact, only from the fluctuation theorem in the long-time regime, we cannot exclude the situation that the error is not monotonic like Fig.
3
, but overshoots to a value larger than
o(1)
after a time evolution of
t^{2}
and then relaxes to the long-time average.
This possibility can be excluded from the fluctuation theorem in the short-time regime.

Let us discuss the time scales of real experimental setups.
For example,
in Ref.
Choi2016MBL
, real-time dynamics of ultracold atoms has been experimentally studied over about
300\tau
with
\tau
being the tunneling time.
On the other hand, the Lieb-Robinson time
\tau_{\mathrm{LR}}
is estimated as
\tau_{\mathrm{LR}}\sim\ell\tau
, where
\ell=N^{1/4}
is the distance between
\mathrm{S}
and
\mathrm{B_{1}}
.
As the experiment is performed in the two-dimensional system where the site number is
N\sim 250
, the Lieb-Robinson time is evaluated as
4\tau
.
Thus, the Lieb-Robinson time is reasonably longer than the experimental time resolution, while it is much shorter than the experimentally tractable time regime.
In such an experimental setup, therefore, the theories for the short and long-time regimes are both necessary to address the entire time domain.
On the other hand, we note that the Lieb-Robinson time is too short in our numerical setup of Sec.
II.3
for the short-time regime to be clearly visible, because our system size is small due to the limitation of numerical accessibility (
N\sim 15
).
Our theory for the short-time regime as well as for the long-time regime is more relevant to real-experimental setups with current or the near-future technologies, where the system sizes can be much bigger than numerics.

is
\Theta(N^{1/2})

Tasaki2018
.
Both of these energy widths are smaller than
\Delta=\Theta(N^{\alpha})~(1/2<\alpha<1)
.
Thus, we can define an energy shell with the energy width
\Delta
, which includes both of the supports of
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)
and
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
and the contribution out of the shell is negligible in the thermodynamic limit.

When the energy eigenstates of
H
satisfy the strong diagonal ETH for any observable of system S,
O_{\mathrm{S}}\simeq O_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}
and
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[\rho^{\mathrm{DE}}]\simeq{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[\rho^{{\mathrm{can}},{\mathrm{DE}}}]
hold, implying that any observable of system S relaxes to the long-time average and the initial states of bath B cannot be distinguished by looking at system S alone. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{G_{\mathrm{S1}}}={\mathrm{tr}}[O_{\mathrm{S}}\rho^{\mathrm{DE}}]\simeq{\mathrm{tr}}[O_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}\rho^{{\mathrm{can}},{\mathrm{DE}}}]=\overline{G_{\mathrm{S1}}^{\mathrm{can}}},

(51)

which leads to
\left|\overline{G_{\mathrm{S1}}}-\overline{G_{\mathrm{S1}}^{\mathrm{can}}}\right|=o(1)
.

When the interaction between system S and bath B is weak, the above result can also be interpreted as follows.
We rewrite
O_{\mathrm{S}}
and
O_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}
as
O_{\mathrm{S}}=\overline{\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}})^{-1}
and
O^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}=\overline{\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}})^{-1}
, respectively.
If the state of S relaxes to the canonical ensemble of
H_{\mathrm{S}}
, i.e.,
\overline{\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)}\simeq\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}
and
\overline{\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)}\simeq\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}
,
O_{\mathrm{S}}\simeq 1_{\mathrm{S}}
and
O_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}\simeq 1_{\mathrm{S}}
hold, which leads to
\overline{G_{\mathrm{S1}}}\simeq\overline{G^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S1}}}
.
That is,
\left|\overline{G_{\mathrm{S1}}}-\overline{G_{\mathrm{S1}}^{\mathrm{can}}}\right|=o(1)
holds when the system-bath interaction is weak and the state of system S relaxes to the canonical ensemble.

We next prove

|\overline{G_{\mathrm{S2}}}|=o(1)
and

|\overline{G_{\mathrm{S2}}^{\mathrm{can}}}|=o(1)
by using the off-diagonal ETH for the energy eigenstates of
H
and observables in S.
We first approximate
\overline{G_{\mathrm{S2}}}
as

\displaystyle\overline{G_{\mathrm{S2}}}\simeq Z_{\mathrm{S}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in{\mathrm{sh}}\\
a\neq b\\
i,j\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle(q_{\mathrm{S}}^{ij})_{ab}(q_{\mathrm{S}}^{ji})_{ba}

\displaystyle(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0))_{ab}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0))_{ba},

(52)

where the summation over the energy eigenstates of
H
is restricted to the energy shell.
If the off-diagonal ETH for

holds, the bath size dependence of the off-diagonal matrix elements

\displaystyle(q_{\mathrm{S}}^{ij})_{ab}=\mathcal{O}(1)/\sqrt{D^{\prime}}.

(53)

In addition, by using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the fact that the purity of any state

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Theta(1)}{D^{\prime}}=o(1).

(54)

See details in Appendix
F.2
.
In a similar manner,
\left|\overline{G^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S2}}}\right|=o(1)
is proved.

We here summarize the assumptions used in the foregoing proofs.
In the proof of

Short-time regime

In this section, we investigate the fluctuation theorem with the energy eigenstate bath in the short time regime.
While Ref.
Iyoda2017
theoretically showed that
|{\delta G}_{\mathrm{S}}|=o(1)
holds on the basis of the Lieb-Robinson bound and the ETH, no quantitative numerical calculations of the bath size dependence of the error was made.
In the present paper, we numerically investigate the dependence of the error of the fluctuation theorem on the bath size and the initial state.
In Sec.

Iyoda2017
.
In Sec.
V.2

V.1
Definition of the error

In this subsection, we discuss the error of the fluctuation theorem in the short-time regime.
Specifically, we decompose
{\delta G}_{\mathrm{S}}
introduced in Eq. (
30
) into the term that can be evaluated by the Lieb-Robinson bound and the term that can be evaluated by the ETH.

From the Lieb-Robinson bound, when considering the dynamics of the short-time regime, the effect of the far side of bath B (
{\mathrm{B}}_{1}
in Fig.
4
) on system S is negligible.
Using
H_{{\mathrm{B}}_{0}}
which is given by restricting
H_{\mathrm{B}}
to
{\mathrm{B}}_{0}
, we define the truncated Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{T}}
and the corresponding time evolution operator

H_{\mathrm{T}}:=H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{I}}+H_{{\mathrm{B}}_{0}}

Then, we define

and

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{tr}[e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}U_{\mathrm{T}}e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)U_{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)],

(56)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{S}}^{{\mathrm{can}},{\mathrm{T}}}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{tr}[e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}U_{\mathrm{T}}e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}U_{\mathrm{T}}^{\dagger}\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)].

G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{T}}\simeq G_{\mathrm{S}}
and

Using these arguments, we decompose

\displaystyle{\delta G}_{\mathrm{S}}={\delta G}_{\mathrm{LR}}+{\delta G}_{\mathrm{ETH}},

(58)

\displaystyle{\delta G}_{\mathrm{LR}}

\displaystyle:={\delta G}_{\mathrm{LR}}^{(1)}+{\delta G}_{\mathrm{LR}}^{(2)},

(59)

\displaystyle{\delta G}_{\mathrm{LR}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle:=G_{\mathrm{S}}-G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{T}},

(60)

\displaystyle{\delta G}_{\mathrm{LR}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle:=G_{\mathrm{S}}^{{\mathrm{can}},{\mathrm{T}}}-G_{\mathrm{S}}^{{\mathrm{can}}},

(61)

\displaystyle{\delta G}_{\mathrm{ETH}}

\displaystyle:=\delta G_{\mathrm{S}}-\delta G_{\mathrm{LR}}=G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{T}}-G^{{\mathrm{can}},{\mathrm{T}}}_{\mathrm{S}}.

Using the Lieb-Robinson bound

and

VI
Summary and discussion

In this study, we have investigated the fluctuation theorem in the long and short-time regimes, when the initial state of bath B is an energy eigenstate and the time evolution of the total system is unitary.
Our results theoretically and numerically show that the fluctuation theorem holds in the entire time domain.

In the long-time regime, we have considered the long-time average of
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
.
We theoretically showed that the error of the fluctuation theorem introduced in Eq. (
30
) vanishes in the thermodynamic limit of the heat bath (Sec.
IV
).

Appendix A
Absolute irreversibility

Absolute irreversibility means a violation of the fluctuation theorem, which occurs when the initial state of system S is singular in the sense that it does not have the full support in the Hilbert space (e.g., the initial state is pure)
Murashita2014
;
Funo2015
.
This is because the final state of the reverse process is not necessarily in the support of the initial state of the forward process.
The violation of the fluctuation theorem can be described by a correction term as described later.
In this Appendix, we consider the generalized fluctuation theorem including the correction term in the presence of absolute irreversibility.

First, we consider the conventional case where the initial state of bath B is the canonical ensemble.
We denote the projection operator onto the subspace which supports
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)
by
P_{\mathrm{ini}}^{\mathrm{S}}
.
When
D_{\mathrm{S}}>\mathrm{rank}[P_{\mathrm{ini}}^{\mathrm{S}}]
, the fluctuation theorem is accompanied by the correction term due to absolute irreversibility.
In particular, if
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)
is pure,
\mathrm{rank}[P_{\mathrm{ini}}^{\mathrm{S}}]=1<D_{\mathrm{S}}
holds.
Then, it has been shown in Ref.
Murashita2014
;
Funo2015
that

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle=1-\lambda^{\mathrm{can}}(t),

(75)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\mathrm{can}}(t)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{tr}[(1-P_{\mathrm{ini}}^{\mathrm{S}})U^{\dagger}\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{R}}(0)U],

(76)

where
\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{R}}(0):=\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\otimes\rho^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{B}}
is the initial state of the reverse process.
We note that one can measure
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
and
\lambda^{\mathrm{can}}(t)
in the forward and the reverse processes independently.
In fact, Eq. (77) has been confirmed experimentally using projection measurements on the system
Masuyama2018
.

The correction term (
76
) does not appear if the singularity is removed by regularizing the initial state of system S as follows:

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{reg}}(0):=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(0)}{{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{S}}[\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(0)]},

(77)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(0):=

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)+\varepsilon(1-P_{\mathrm{ini}}^{\mathrm{S}})~~~(\varepsilon>0),

(78)

which is essentially the same approach as adopted in Ref.
HevelingPC
(see also Ref.
Sagawa2012Reg
).
If we take the limit of
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
after calculating
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
, then
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle=1
holds.
On the other hand, if we take the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
first and then calculate
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle
, Eq. (
75
) holds.
This is not just the problem of the (unphysical) order of the limits.
As mentioned above, however, Eq. (
75
) has been experimentally observed, and thus the effect of absolute irreversibility is physically relevant.
On the other hand, in the case where small noise is unavoidable in the initial state, the regularization approach would be relevant.
Therefore, these two approaches are both physically reasonable and play complementary roles.

We next consider the fluctuation theorem with the energy eigenstate bath in the long-time regime in the presence of absolute irreversibility.
We define the correction term as

\displaystyle\lambda(t)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{tr}[(1-P_{\mathrm{ini}}^{\mathrm{S}})U^{\dagger}\rho_{\mathrm{R}}(0)U],

(79)

where
\rho_{\mathrm{R}}(0):=\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)
.
We will prove in Appendix
F
that

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle=1-\lambda(t)

(80)

holds in the thermodynamic limit.
Because we have already discussed the long-time average of the left-hand side in Sec.
IV.2
, we only need to show

\displaystyle|\overline{\lambda^{\mathrm{can}}(t)}-\overline{\lambda(t)}|=o(1).

(81)

We will prove Eq. (
81
) by using the ETH of the total system SB in Appendix
F
.

We here show the validity of Eq. (

) by numerical simulation.

shows the time dependence of

Appendix B
Supplement on the interaction-induced error

In this Appendix, we discuss the form of the interaction-induced error.
Specifically, we compare
{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}
defined in Eq. (
32
) and the interaction-induced error defined in Ref.
Iyoda2017
.
We show that
{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}
reflects how thermal the initial state of bath B is.

First, we consider the characteristic function of the entropy production:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(u;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}):={\mathrm{tr}}[

\displaystyle Ue^{-iu\beta H_{{\mathrm{B}}}}e^{iu\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}U^{\dagger}e^{-iu\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)}e^{iu\beta H_{{\mathrm{B}}}}],

(82)

which is the Fourier transformation of the probability distribution of the stochastic entropy production
\sigma
. We note that
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle=\mathcal{G}(i;\rho_{\mathrm{B}})
holds.
Let us next consider the following function by replacing
H_{\mathrm{B}}
by
-H_{\mathrm{S}}
, as in
G_{S}
defined in Eq. (
26
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{S}}(u;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}):={\mathrm{tr}}[

\displaystyle Ue^{iu\beta H_{{\mathrm{S}}}}e^{iu\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}U^{\dagger}e^{-iu\ln\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)}e^{-iu\beta H_{{\mathrm{S}}}}].

(83)

The interaction-induced error comes from the difference between Eqs. (
82
) and (
83
). By noting that the fluctuation theorem holds if the initial state of B is canonical, we define the interaction-induced error as

\displaystyle{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(i;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0))-\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{S}}(i;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{S}}(i;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}})-\mathcal{G}(i;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}).

(84)

By definition,
{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}=0
holds when the initial state of bath B is the canonical ensemble (
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)=\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
).

As an alternative definition, in the supplemental information of Ref.
Iyoda2017
, the interaction-induced error is introduced using the operator norm as

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{I}}(u)

\displaystyle:=\|e^{iu\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}Ue^{-iu\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}-e^{-iu\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}Ue^{iu\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}\|.

(85)

Using
\delta_{\mathrm{I}}(u)
, the error of the fluctuation theorem is evaluated as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}(u;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0))-\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{S}}(i;\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0))|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{I}}(u)C(u),

(86)

where
C(u)
is an
N
-independent constant and written as

\displaystyle C(u)=\begin{cases}(p_{b}(0))^{-|u_{\mathrm{I}}-1|}>1&(1<u_{\mathrm{I}})\\
1&(0\leq u_{\mathrm{I}}\leq 1)\\
(p_{b}(t))^{-|u_{\mathrm{I}}|}>1&(u_{\mathrm{I}}<0),\end{cases}

(87)

where
p_{b}(t)
is the minimum eigenvalue of
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)
and
u_{\mathrm{I}}:=\mathrm{Im}[u]
.
In the supplemental information of Ref.
Iyoda2017
,

C(u)
in the right-hand side of (
86
) was considered to be
1
, while the
modification as above is required for
1<u_{\mathrm{I}}
and
u_{\mathrm{I}}<0
.
We note that only the case of
u_{\mathrm{I}}=1
was used in the proof of the fluctuation theorem of the form (
4
) in Ref.
Iyoda2017
, and therefore the proof of it is correct as it is.

In the above evaluation,
\delta_{\mathrm{I}}(u)
is independent of the initial state of bath B.
On the other hand,
{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}=0
holds when the initial state of bath B is the canonical ensemble.
Therefore,
{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}
is a more proper measure of the error, because it can reflect how thermal the initial state of bath B is.

Appendix C
Rotating wave approximation and the interaction-induced error

In this Appendix, we show that Eqs. (
18
) and (
36
) hold under the rotating wave approximation and the off-diagonal ETH.
We first introduce the rotating wave approximation and show Eq. (
18
) in Appendix
C.1
.
Then, we show Eq. (
36
) in Appendix
C.2
.

C.1
Rotating wave approximation and the proof of Eq. (
18
)

We write the interaction Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{I}}=\sum_{ab}(H_{\mathrm{I}})_{ab}|E_{a}\rangle\langle E_{b}|.

(88)

Let
\Omega
be the cutoff frequency for the rotating wave approximation, which is assumed to be independent of the system size.
We then introduce the rotating wave approximation of
H_{\mathrm{I}}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}={\sum_{ab}}^{\prime}(H_{\mathrm{I}})_{ab}|E_{a}\rangle\langle E_{b}|,

(89)

where the sum is taken over
(a,b)
satisfying
|E_{a}-E_{b}|<\Omega
.
The rotating wave approximation holds in a long-time regime with
t\gg\Omega^{-1}
.

To show Eq. (
18
), we assume the off-diagonal ETH in the following form
Srednicki1994
;
Mondaini2017
:

\displaystyle(\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}})_{ab}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D^{\prime}}}g_{ab}r_{ab},

(90)

where
g_{ab}
characterizes the dependence of
(\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}})_{ab}
on
E_{a}
and
E_{b}
, and
r_{ab}
is a random variable whose mean is
0
and variance is
1
.
We note that
Eq. (
90
) is stronger than the off-diagonal ETH in the form of Eq. (
15
).
We use Eq. (
90
) only in this Appendix.

Under the rotating wave approximation and the off-diagonal ETH,
the commutator in Eq. (
18
) is written as

\displaystyle[H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{B}},\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}]={\sum_{ab}}^{\prime}|E_{a}\rangle\langle E_{b}|\frac{R_{ab}}{\sqrt{D^{\prime}}},

(91)

where
R_{ab}:=(E_{a}-E_{b})g_{ab}r_{ab}
. We note that
R_{ab}
is the matrix elements of the band random matrix and the variance of
R_{ab}
is less than
\Omega^{2}\|\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}\|^{2}
.
From the Wigner semicircle law,
the maximum eigenvalue of a random matrix, whose dimension is
D
and variance of the matrix elements is
\sigma^{2}
, is about
2\sqrt{D}\sigma
.
Similarly, the maximum eigenvalue of a band random matrix with band width
W
is evaluated to be
2\sqrt{W}\sigma
, which is mathematically shown for some band random matrix
Sodin2010
.
We evaluate the band width of the band random matrix
R
as
D^{\prime}\Omega/\|\tilde{H}\|
, where we defined
\tilde{H}:=H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{B}}+\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}
.
Then, the operator norm of Eq. (
91
) is evaluated as

\displaystyle\|[H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{B}},\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}]\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c\Omega\|\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}\|}{\sqrt{N}},

(92)

where we used
\|\tilde{H}\|=\Theta(N)
and introduced
c:=\Theta(1)
.
Thus, Eq. (
18
) is proved.

In the same manner, we can show that

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{N}}\|\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}\|\Omega^{n},

(93)

\displaystyle\|g^{a}_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{ca}{\sqrt{N}}\|\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}\|^{a}\Omega^{n},

(94)

where we inductively define

\displaystyle f_{n+1}

\displaystyle:=[\tilde{H},f_{n}],~~f_{0}=A,

(95)

\displaystyle g^{a}_{n+1}

\displaystyle:=[\tilde{H},g^{a}_{n}],~~g^{a}_{0}=\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}^{a},

(96)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=[\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}},\tilde{U}^{\dagger}],

(97)

where
\tilde{U}:=\exp(-i\tilde{H}t)
.
We note that Eq. (
92
) is a special case (
a=n=1
) of Eq. (
94
).

In this subsection, we show Eq. (

).
We first focus on
\delta G^{(1)}_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle\delta G^{(1)}_{\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{tr}[\delta\tilde{U}_{\beta}\rho\tilde{U}^{\dagger}\Lambda_{\mathrm{S}}(t)],

Appendix D
Naive approach to evaluate the error of the fluctuation theorem

In this Appendix, we discuss another naive approach to evaluate the error of the fluctuation without Eq. (
29
).
In this approach, however, we cannot show that the error of the fluctuation theorem vanishes in the thermodynamic limit.

We note that
G-G^{\mathrm{can}}
can be written as the difference between the expectation values of
O_{\mathrm{B}}(t)
, which is non-local for
t>0
, as follows:

\displaystyle G-G^{\mathrm{can}}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{B}}\left[O_{\mathrm{B}}(t)(\rho_{\mathrm{B}}-\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{MC}})\right],

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{B}}\left[O_{\mathrm{B}}(t)(\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{MC}}-\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}})\right]

(105)

\displaystyle O_{\mathrm{B}}(t):=

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{S}}\left[U^{\dagger}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}U\right]e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}.

(106)

The first and second terms on the right-hand side of Eq. (
D
) are regarded as the errors associated with the ETH and the equivalence of ensembles for
O_{\mathrm{B}}(t)
, respectively. Below, we discuss them in detail.

The equivalence of ensembles has been theoretically shown for (quasi-)local operators, whose support is at most
N^{1/2}
and operator norm does not increase with respect to
N

Tasaki2018
. The error of the equivalence of ensembles only polynomially decreases with respect to
N
. If we assume that the equivalence of ensembles in the same form as
Tasaki2018
holds for
O_{\mathrm{B}}(t)
, we cannot show the error of the equivalence of ensembles vanishes in the thermodynamic limit due to the exponential increase of the norm of
O_{\mathrm{B}}(t)
.

The ETH for highly non-local operators has been investigated theoretically
Hamazaki2018
and numerically
Kaneko2019
. In Ref.
Hamazaki2018
, the ETH is theoretically shown to hold with the error decreasing as
1/\sqrt{D}
for typical many-body operators, whose operator norm does not grow exponentially. In Ref.
Kaneko2019
, the same scaling as
Hamazaki2018
was numerically observed. However, the operator norm of
O_{\mathrm{B}}(t)
exponentially increases and is bounded by
D\sqrt{N}

Tasaki2018
(see also Eq. (
146
)). Then, the error associated with the ETH is bounded by
\sqrt{DN}
and we cannot show that the error of the fluctuation theorem associated with the ETH vanishes in the thermodynamic limit.

For the above reasons, instead of the direct evaluation of
G-G_{\mathrm{can}}
, in the main text we have adopted the decomposition (
29
) and show the fluctuation theorem based on plausible assumptions such as the ETH and the equivalence of ensembles for (quasi-)local physical quantities.

Appendix E
Initial rise of the error of the fluctuation theorem

In this Appendix, we show that the errors of the fluctuation theorem initially rises in proportionate to
t^{2}
as mentioned in Secs.
III
and
V
.

The error of the fluctuation theorem
\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle-1
initially rises in proportionate to

\displaystyle\langle e^{-\sigma}\rangle-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}Ue^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)U^{\dagger}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}\left[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\left(-\frac{itH}{\hbar}\right)e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)\right]

\displaystyle+

\rho=\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
and

\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(0)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
.

We next evaluate
\Delta_{T}(G)
.
As in Eq. (
139

\overline{G^{2}}
as

\displaystyle\overline{G^{2}}=

\displaystyle(D_{\mathrm{S}}Z_{\mathrm{B}}e^{\beta E^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathrm{ini}}})^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4})\\
i,j,k,l\end{subarray}}\tilde{\rho}_{a_{1},b_{1}}\rho^{\prime}_{a_{2},b_{2}}\tilde{\rho}_{a_{3},b_{3}}\rho^{\prime}_{a_{4},b_{4}}

\displaystyle(\rho^{ij})_{b_{1},a_{1}}(B^{ij}_{\mathrm{S}})_{b_{2},a_{2}}(\rho^{kl})_{b_{3},a_{3}}(B^{kl}_{\mathrm{S}})_{b_{4},a_{4}},

(145)

where

E^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathrm{ini}}
is the energy of the initial state of bath B,

\tilde{\rho}:=(1_{\mathrm{S}}/D_{\mathrm{S}})\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)
and

\rho^{ij}:=q_{\mathrm{S}}^{ij}\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
.

Regarding the partition function, the following relation holds
Tasaki2018
:

\displaystyle(Z_{\mathrm{B}}e^{\beta E^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathrm{ini}}})^{2}\leq D^{2}N.

(146)

We also have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[(\rho^{ij})^{2}]=\delta_{ij}/D_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}],

(147)

where
D_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}]
is the effective dimension of
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
with respect to
H_{\mathrm{B}}
. For simplicity, when there is no degeneracy in the eigenenergy and the state is diagonal with the energy eigenstates as
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|E_{i}\rangle\langle E_{i}|
, the effective dimension is written as

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho]:=\left(\sum_{i}|p_{i}|^{2}\right)^{-1}.

(148)

We note that
D_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}]
increases exponentially with respect to the bath size
N

Kaneko2017
.
Besides, we assume that the matrix elements of the density operator
\rho^{\prime}
and
\tilde{\rho}
are
\mathcal{O}({D^{\prime}}^{-1})
:

\displaystyle|(\rho^{\prime})_{ab}|=\mathcal{O}({D^{\prime}}^{-1}),

(149)

\displaystyle|(\tilde{\rho})_{ab}|=\mathcal{O}({D^{\prime}}^{-1}).

(150)

This is not satisfied when the density operators
\rho^{\prime}
and
\tilde{\rho}
, which are product states as in Eq. (
3
), are localized to some specific eigenstates of
H
.
When the initial state is in the form of Eq. (
3
), we argue that
\mathcal{O}({D^{\prime}}^{-1})
is naturally satisfied if the Hamiltonian
H
is chaotic and mixes system S and bath B sufficiently.

In
\Delta_{T}(G_{\mathrm{S}})
, the term corresponding to Fig.
12
(b) is evaluated as

\displaystyle\left|(D_{\mathrm{S}}Z_{\mathrm{B}}e^{\beta E^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathrm{ini}}})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\\
i,j,k,l\end{subarray}}\tilde{\rho}_{a_{1},a_{3}}\rho^{\prime}_{a_{2},a_{2}}\tilde{\rho}_{a_{3},a_{1}}\rho^{\prime}_{a_{4},a_{4}}

\displaystyle\left.(\rho^{ij})_{a_{3},a_{1}}(B^{ij}_{\mathrm{S}})_{a_{2},a_{2}}(\rho^{kl})_{a_{1},a_{3}}(B^{kl}_{\mathrm{S}})_{a_{4},a_{4}}\right|

(151)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Theta(N)\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\\
i,j,k,l\end{subarray}}\rho^{\prime}_{a_{2},a_{2}}\rho^{\prime}_{a_{4},a_{4}}(\rho^{ij})_{a_{3},a_{1}}(\rho^{kl})_{a_{1},a_{3}}\right|

(152)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Theta(N)({\mathrm{tr}}[\rho^{\prime}])^{2}\sum_{i,j,k,l}\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}[(\rho^{ij})^{2}]{\mathrm{tr}}[(\rho^{kl})^{2}]}

(153)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Theta(N)}{D_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}]}=o(1).

(154)

In the first inequality, we used Eqs. (

)(
146
) and (

).
In the second inequality, we used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. In the last inequality, we used Eq. (

Appendix G
Perturbation theory on the interaction-induced error

In this Appendix, by using the perturbation theory, we show that the interaction-induced error does not grow significantly in the long-time regime and vanishes in the thermodynamic limit in the short-time regime.

G.1
The long-time regime

We show that
|\overline{{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}}|\leq\Theta(1)
holds at high temperature using the perturbation theory.
The long-time average of the interaction-induced error (
32
) is written as

\displaystyle\overline{{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle:=\overline{G}-\overline{G_{\mathrm{S}}},

(155)

\displaystyle\overline{{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(2)}}

\displaystyle:=\overline{G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}}}-\overline{G^{\mathrm{can}}}.

(156)

First, we divide
\overline{{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(1)}}
into the diagonal term
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(1)}}
and the off-diagonal term
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}2}^{(1)}}
.
The diagonal term is written as

\displaystyle\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(1)}}=\sum_{a}

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\pi_{a}e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)\pi_{a}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}]

\displaystyle-\sum_{a}

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}\pi_{a}e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)\pi_{a}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}],

(157)

where
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}:={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[\rho^{\mathrm{DE}}]
. By using the first order perturbation theory for the eigenstates, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\alpha}{\sum_{j\beta}}^{\prime}{\sum_{k\gamma}}^{\prime}\frac{|\langle E_{j}^{\mathrm{S}}E_{\beta}^{\mathrm{B}}|H_{\mathrm{I}}|E_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}E_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}\rangle|^{2}}{(\delta E_{ji}^{\mathrm{S}}+\delta E_{\beta\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}})(\delta E_{ki}^{\mathrm{S}}+\delta E_{\beta\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}})}\delta_{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle\left[\langle E_{j}^{\mathrm{S}}|\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}|E_{k}^{\mathrm{S}}\rangle\delta_{\alpha,\mathrm{ini}}(e^{-\beta\delta E_{\beta\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}}-e^{-\beta\delta E_{ki}^{\mathrm{S}}})\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\langle E_{j}^{\mathrm{S}}|\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}|E_{k}^{\mathrm{S}}\rangle\delta_{\alpha,\mathrm{ini}}(e^{-\beta\delta E_{\beta\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}}-e^{-\beta\delta E_{ki}^{\mathrm{S}}})\right],

(158)

where the summation over
j,k,\beta,\gamma
are restricted to
j\neq i
,
k\neq i
,
\beta\neq\alpha
,
\gamma\neq\alpha
and
\delta_{\alpha,\mathrm{ini}}
means
\alpha
equals the index of the initial state of bath B. We also define
\delta E^{\mathrm{S}}_{ij}:=E^{\mathrm{S}}_{i}-E^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}
and
\delta E^{\mathrm{B}}_{\beta\alpha}:=E^{\mathrm{B}}_{\beta}-E^{\mathrm{B}}_{\alpha}
.

Here, we assume the following relation as the off-diagonal ETH instead of Eq. (
53
).

\displaystyle|\langle E_{j}^{\mathrm{S}}E_{\beta}^{\mathrm{B}}|H_{\mathrm{I}}|E_{i}^{\mathrm{S}}E_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}\rangle|^{2}\sim\frac{\Theta(1)}{D}e^{-\nu|\delta E_{\beta\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}|},

(159)

where
\nu
is an
N
-independent constant.
The exponential decay with respect to energy difference
\delta E_{\beta\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}
in Eq. (
159
) has been observed in numerical calculations of the off-diagonal ETH
Mondaini2017
. Besides, the exponential decay is theoretically shown when the Hamiltonian is local
Arad2016
.
Then, the terms that can diverge in Eq. (
158
) are written as

e^{-(\nu\pm\beta)|\delta E_{\beta\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}|}
. At high temperature
\nu>\beta
,
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(1)}}\leq\Theta(1)
is shown.

We next show that the off-diagonal term vanishes as
N
increases:
\left|\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}2}^{(1)}}\right|=o(1)
. Since
\left|\overline{G_{{\mathrm{S}}2}}\right|=o(1)
is already shown, it is sufficient to show
|\overline{G_{2}}|=o(1)
, where
\overline{G_{2}}
is the off-diagonal contribution of
\overline{G}
. In the same manner as Appendix
F
, we show
|\overline{G_{2}}|=o(1)
by using the off-diagonal ETH (
53
) and the conditions on the density operators (
149
) and (
150
).

We divide
\overline{{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(2)}}
into the diagonal term
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(2)}}
and the off-diagonal term
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}2}^{(2)}}
.
Since
\overline{G^{\mathrm{can}}_{{\mathrm{S}}2}}=o(1)
is already shown,
\left|\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}2}^{(2)}}\right|=o(1)
holds. By using the diagonal ETH for the operator of system S, the diagonal term
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(2)}}
is evaluated as

\displaystyle\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(2)}}\simeq 1-\langle\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{can}})^{-1}\rangle_{\mathrm{MC}}.

(160)

If the density operator of system S in the long-time regime
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}
relaxes to the canonical ensemble of
H_{\mathrm{S}}
, the second term on the right-hand side equals
1
. Then, we argue that
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(2)}}=o(1)
holds when
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}
relaxes to the canonical ensemble.
On the other hand,

\rho_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{DE}}
does not necessarily relax to the canonical ensemble of

for general interactions. Then, we assume
\overline{{\delta G}_{{\mathrm{I}}1}^{(2)}}\leq\Theta(1)

From the foregoing argument,
|\overline{{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}}|\leq\Theta(1)
is shown.

G.2
The short-time regime

We show that
|{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}|=o(1)
holds in the short-time regime (
t\ll\tau_{\mathrm{LR}}
) at high temperature using the perturbation theory.

The interaction-induced error is defined in Eq. (
32

Because

in

\displaystyle{\delta G}_{\mathrm{I}}\simeq

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}Ue^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}U^{\dagger}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}Ue^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{S}}}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}U^{\dagger}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)],

(161)

where
\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}:=\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)-\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
.
By using the Lieb-Robinson bound and the ETH again, the second line is shown to be
o(1)
. Below, we evaluate the first line using the perturbation calculation for the time evolution operator.

From the first order perturbation theory, we obtain

\displaystyle U\simeq

\displaystyle U_{0}+U_{1},

(162)

\displaystyle U_{0}:=

\displaystyle e^{-iH_{0}t/\hbar},

(163)

\displaystyle U_{1}:=

\displaystyle-iU_{0}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau e^{-iH_{0}\tau/\hbar}H_{\mathrm{I}}e^{iH_{0}\tau/\hbar},

(164)

where we define
H_{0}:=H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{B}}
. We also define

\displaystyle\delta G_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{B}}[U,V^{\dagger}]:=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}Ue^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}V^{\dagger}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)],

(165)

\displaystyle\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(i,j)}^{\mathrm{B}}:=

\displaystyle\delta G_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{B}}[U_{i},U_{j}^{\dagger}].

(166)

Then the first line of Eq. (
161
) is approximated as
\sum_{i=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(i,j)}^{\mathrm{B}}
.

We first evaluate
\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(0,0)}^{\mathrm{B}}
as

\displaystyle\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(0,0)}^{\mathrm{B}}=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}U_{0}e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}U^{\dagger}_{0}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}[\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\otimes\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(167)

where we used
[U_{0},H_{\mathrm{B}}]=0
and
[U_{0},\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}]=0
.
We next calculate
\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(0,1)}^{\mathrm{B}}
as

\displaystyle|\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(0,1)}^{\mathrm{B}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}U_{0}e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}U^{\dagger}_{1}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}d\tau{\mathrm{tr}}[U_{0}H_{\mathrm{I}}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau)\otimes\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\|U_{0}\|\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau)\otimes\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}\|_{1},

(168)

where we define
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau):=e^{iH_{\mathrm{S}}(t-\tau)}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)e^{-iH_{\mathrm{S}}(t-\tau)}
. We note that
\|U_{0}\|=1
holds and
\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau)\otimes\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}\|_{1}=\mathcal{O}(D^{-1/2})
is satisfied from the ETH.
Then, in the short-time regime
t\ll\tau_{\mathrm{LR}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(0,1)}^{\mathrm{B}}|\leq\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{LR}}}{\Theta(D^{1/2})}=\frac{\Theta(N^{\mu})}{\Theta(D^{1/2})}=o(1).

(169)

Besides,
\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(1,0)}^{\mathrm{B}}
is evaluated as

\displaystyle\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(1,0)}^{\mathrm{B}}=-i\int_{0}^{t}d\tau{\mathrm{tr}}[U_{0}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau)\otimes\delta\rho H_{\mathrm{I}}].

(170)

Then,
\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(1,0)}^{\mathrm{B}}=o(1)
in
t\ll\tau_{\mathrm{LR}}
is shown to hold in the same manner as
\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(0,1)}^{\mathrm{B}}
.

We finally calculate
\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(1,1)}^{\mathrm{B}}
as

\displaystyle\delta G_{{\mathrm{I}},(1,1)}^{\mathrm{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau d\tau^{\prime}{\mathrm{tr}}[e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}e^{iH_{0}(t-\tau)}H_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle~~~~~e^{-iH_{0}\tau}e^{\beta H_{\mathrm{B}}}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}e^{iH_{0}\tau^{\prime}}H_{\mathrm{I}}e^{iH_{0}(t-\tau^{\prime})}\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{2}\left|\sum_{a,b,c}e^{-\beta\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}}}(H_{\mathrm{I}})_{ab}(H_{\mathrm{I}})_{bc}(\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}})_{bb}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t))_{ca}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{2}\left|\sum_{a,b,c}M(a,b,c,t)\right|,

(171)

where

\displaystyle M(a,b,c,t):=

\displaystyle e^{-\beta\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}}}(H_{\mathrm{I}})_{ab}(H_{\mathrm{I}})_{bc}(\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}})_{bb}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t))_{ca}.

(172)

We note that the index

represents a pair of indexes of the eigenstates of system S and bath B and is written as

We write the corresponding eigenenergy as

The same applies to
b

c

We write the matrix elements as

(A)_{ab}=\langle E_{\mathrm{S}}^{a_{\mathrm{S}}}E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}}|A|E_{\mathrm{S}}^{b_{\mathrm{S}}}E_{\mathrm{B}}^{b_{\mathrm{B}}}\rangle

(A)_{a_{\mathrm{S}}b_{\mathrm{S}}}=\langle E_{\mathrm{S}}^{a_{\mathrm{S}}}|A|E_{\mathrm{S}}^{b_{\mathrm{S}}}\rangle

(A)_{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}}=\langle E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}}|A|E_{\mathrm{B}}^{b_{\mathrm{B}}}\rangle
.
We also write the energy change of B as
\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}}
.

We define the energy ranges of bath B as follows.
First, we define
\Lambda_{1}:=[E-\Delta,E+\Delta]
,
E={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[H_{\mathrm{B}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)]={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{B}}[H_{\mathrm{B}}\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}]

(1/2<a<1)

We note that the energy widths of
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(0)
and
\rho_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{can}}
are included in
\Lambda_{1}
.
We also define

\overline{\Lambda_{2}}
.
We write the sum range as
a\in\Lambda_{1}
, which means that
\sum_{a\in\Lambda_{1}}=\sum_{a_{\mathrm{S}}}\sum_{a_{\mathrm{B}}\in\Lambda_{1}}
.

We evaluate
\sum_{a,b,c}M(a,b,c,t)
for the following three cases: (i)

, (ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in{\overline{\Lambda_{2}}}\\
b\in\Lambda_{1}\\
c;c_{\mathrm{B}}=a_{\mathrm{B}}\end{subarray}}M(a,b,c,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Theta(1)}{D}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in{\overline{\Lambda_{2}}}\\

c;c_{\mathrm{B}}=a_{\mathrm{B}}\end{subarray}}(\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}})_{bb}(\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(t))_{ca}

\displaystyle~~~~e^{-\nu|\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}}|}e^{-\nu|\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{b_{\mathrm{B}}c_{\mathrm{B}}}|}e^{-\beta\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}}},

(173)

where we used the off-diagonal ETH for

.
We write

\displaystyle\frac{D_{\mathrm{S}}D_{\overline{\Lambda_{2}}}}{D}\max_{a_{\mathrm{B}}}(e^{-\nu|\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}^{\nu}}|}e^{+\beta|\delta E_{\mathrm{B}}^{a_{\mathrm{B}}b_{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}}|}),

(176)

where we define

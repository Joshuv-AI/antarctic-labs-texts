M.Sc., I.I.T. Delhi, India

Abstract




On irrationality of Euler’s constant and related asymptotic formulas

Abstract:
By defining

I_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(x(1-x)y(1-y))^{n}}{(1-xy)(-\log xy)}\ dxdy

Sondow (see [2]) proved that

I_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\gamma+L_{n}-A_{n}

We prove asymptotic formula for
L_{n}
and
A_{n}
as
n\to\infty
,

L_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\left(\log\left({\frac{{3n}}{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left({\frac{1}{n}}\right)\right)

and

A_{n}\sim\frac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\left(\gamma+\ln\frac{3}{2}+\ln n\right)

Using the sufficient condition for irrationality criteria of Euler’s constant due to Sondow, we prove that
\gamma
is irrational.

Keywords and Phrases:
Euler’s constant, Harmonic number, asymptotic equality, rising and falling factorial, Stirling numbers, Beta function

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
.

1. Introduction and Definitions
The Euler’s constant is defined by the limit,

\gamma:=\lim_{n\to\infty}(H_{n}-\log n)

(1)

where
H_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}
is the
n
th Harmonic number. Euler’s constant has a double integral representation (see [1]),

\gamma=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x}{(1-xy)(-\log xy)}\ dxdy

(2)

Sondow (see [2]) gave criteria for irrationality of Euler’s constant where he defined

I_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(x(1-x)y(1-y))^{n}}{(1-xy)(-\log xy)}\ dxdy

(3)

If
d_{n}=\text{LCM}(1,2,...,n)
then Sondow proved (see [2])

I_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\gamma+L_{n}-A_{n}

(4)

where

L_{n}=d^{-1}_{2n}\log S_{n}

(5)

S_{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{i=0}^{\min{(k-1,n-k)}}\prod_{j=i+1}^{n-i}(n+k)^{\frac{2d_{2n}}{j}\binom{n}{i}^{2}}

(6)

and

A_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{2}H_{n+j}

(7)

Clearly then we have
d_{2n}A_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
It was proved that a sufficient condition for irrationality of
\gamma
is (see [2])

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}\neq\frac{\pi}{6\log 2}

(8)

where
\{x\}
denotes the fractional part of
x
.

2. Main Theorems
Using Laplace’s method (see [3], p.322), Sondow proved as
n\to\infty
(see [2]),

I_{n}\sim\left(\frac{\pi}{6\log 2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{n4^{2n}}\right)

(9)

I_{n}
can be also represented as (see [2])

I_{n}=\sum_{v=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{v}^{\infty}\left(\frac{n!}{x(x+1)...(x+n)}\right)^{2}\ dx

(10)

The goal of this article is to prove the following result.

Theorem :
We have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}\neq\frac{\pi}{6\log 2}

(11)

Proof
: We prove a few Lemma:

Lemma 1:
We have the following partial fraction decomposition for
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\frac{1}{(x(x+1)(x+2)...(x+n))^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{a_{k}}{x+k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{b_{k}}{(x+k)^{2}}

(12)

where

a_{k}=2\frac{H_{k}-H_{n-k}}{\left(k!(n-k)!\right)^{2}}

(13)

and

b_{k}=\frac{1}{\left(k!(n-k)!\right)^{2}}

(14)

Proof
: Since every
k
,
k=-n,...,-2,-1,0
is a pole of order two of the given fraction so its decomposition looks like

\frac{1}{(x(x+1)(x+2)...(x+n))^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{a_{k}}{x+k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{b_{k}}{(x+k)^{2}}

(15)

Next we find
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
: For finding
b_{k}
, we multiply each side of (15) by
(x+k)^{2}
, simplify, and set
x=-k
to get

b_{k}=\left(\frac{1}{(-k)(-(k-1))\cdots(-1)(1)(2)\cdots(n-k)}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{\left(k!(n-k)!\right)^{2}}

(16)

Deriving a formula for
a_{k}
is some what lengthy. The rising and falling factorial functions may be expanded as polynomials whose coefficients are the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind:

x^{(n)}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\brack j}x^{j}

(17)

and

(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-(n-1))=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}{n\brack j}x^{j}

(18)

The goal is to find coefficients of an expansion of
\left(x^{(n+1)}\right)^{-2}
in powers of
y\doteq x+k
. Therefore we write

x^{(n+1)}=(y-k)(y-(k-1))\cdots(y-1)y(y+1)\cdots(y+n-k)=\frac{y^{(n-k+1)}(y)_{k+1}}{y}

(19)

Expanding this in powers of
y
yields

\displaystyle x^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y}\left({n-k+1\brack 0}+{n-k+1\brack 1}y+{n-k+1\brack 2}y^{2}+O(y^{3})\right)

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\left(-{k+1\brack 0}+{k+1\brack 1}y-{k+1\brack 2}y^{2}+O(y^{3})\right)

(20)

The formulas for the Stirling numbers involved are, for all
n\geq 0
,

{n+1\brack 0}=0,\quad{n+1\brack 1}=n!,\quad\text{and}\quad{n+1\brack 2}=n!H_{n},

(21)

where
H_{n}
is the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
harmonic number. Therefore,

x^{(n+1)}=(-1)^{k}k!(n-k)!\,y\left(1+(H_{n-k}-H_{k})y+O(y^{2})\right).

(22)

So we have

\left(x^{(n+1)}\right)^{-2}=\frac{1}{\left(k!(n-k)!\right)^{2}}\,\left(y^{-2}+2(H_{k}-H_{n-k})y^{-1}+O(1)\right).

(23)

From this we may read off the formula for
b_{k}
given above and this formula for
a_{k}
:

a_{k}=2\frac{H_{k}-H_{n-k}}{\left(k!(n-k)!\right)^{2}}.

(24)

This completes the proof of Lemma
1
.

Lemma 2:
We have the following representation for
I_{n}
,

I_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(B(x+n+k,n+1))^{2}\ dx

(25)

where
B(p,q)
is the Beta function.

Proof
: Can be derived from (10).

Lemma 3:
We have the following representation for
I_{n}
,

I_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\gamma-\sum_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}(2(H_{n-j}-H_{j})\log((n+j)!)+H_{n+j})}

(26)

Proof
: Since from (25)

I_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(B(x+n+k,n+1))^{2}\ dx

(27)

and we have

(B(x+n+k,n+1))^{2}=\frac{n!^{2}}{\prod_{j=n}^{2n}(x+k+j)^{2}}

(28)

By using partial fractions as obtained in (12) we get

I_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{2}\left(2\frac{H_{j}-H_{n-j}}{x+j+k+n}+\frac{1}{x+j+k+n}\right)\ dx

(29)

Integrating and evaluating the negative integrand at
x=0
and summing over
k
with a limit gives

I_{n}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{2}\left(2(H_{n-j}-H_{j})\log\left(\frac{(n+j+r)!}{(n+j)!}\right)+H_{n+j+r}-H_{n+j}\right)

(30)

It is well known that as
r\to+\infty

H_{n+j+r}=\log(n+j+r)+\gamma+o(1)

(31)

and

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}}=\binom{2n}{n},

(32)

we can derive the explicit formula

I_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\gamma-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}(2(H_{n-j}-H_{j})\log((n+j)!)+H_{n+j})}

(33)

provided that

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{r\to+\infty}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\left({2(H_{n-j}-H_{j})\log((n+j+r)!)+\log(n+j+r)}\right)}=0.

(34)

Denote the expression under the limit by
S_{n}(r)
. Performing the change of summation index
j\to n-j
and taking the average with the original expression, we find

S_{n}(r)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\left({(H_{n-j}-H_{j})\log\frac{{(n+j+r)!}}{{(2n-j+r)!}}+\frac{1}{2}\log((2n-j+r)(n+j+r))}\right)}.

(35)

Stirling’s formula and the Maclaurin series of the logarithm then yields

S_{n}(r)=(\log r)\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\left({(H_{n-j}-H_{j})(2j-n)+1}\right)}+o(1)

(36)

as
r\to+\infty
. Therefore, it remains to show that

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}((H_{n-j}-H_{j})(2j-n)+1)}=0

(37)

for all
n\geq 1
. Because of the symmetry in
H_{n-j}-H_{j}
, this may be further simplifed to the claim that

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}(2j(H_{n-j}-H_{j})+1)}=0

(38)

for all
n\geq 1
. Computer algebra software confirms this for
n=1,2,3\ldots,200
. This completes the proof for Lemma 3.

Comparing equations (4) and (26)

L_{n}=-\sum_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}(2(H_{n-j}-H_{j})\log(n+j)!)}

(39)

Lemma 4:
We have the following asymptotic formula for
L_{n}
and
A_{n}
as
n\to\infty
,

L_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\left(\log\left({\frac{{3n}}{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left({\frac{1}{n}}\right)\right)

(40)

and

A_{n}\sim\frac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\left(\gamma+\ln\frac{3}{2}+\ln n\right)

(41)

Proof
: This answers the question about the asymptotics of
a_{n}
provided that the conjectured formula for
I_{n}
in my other answer is correct. Note that since
H_{k}=\psi(k+1)+\gamma
, we can write

L_{n}=I_{n}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\psi(n+j+1)}=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\psi(n+j+1)}+o(1).

(42)

The
o
-term follows from equation (9). Now by the asymptotic result

\psi(k+1)=\log k+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})

(43)

we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\psi(n+j+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\log(n+j)}+\mathcal{O}(1)\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\frac{1}{{n+j+1}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\log(n+j)}+\mathcal{O}\!\left({\frac{1}{n}}\right)\binom{2n}{n}.

Since
\log n\leq\log(n+j)\leq\log n+\log 2
, it follows that as
n\to+\infty

L_{n}\sim\binom{2n}{n}\log n

(44)

Also by the change of summation index from
j
to
n-j
and taking the average with the original expression, we find

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\log(n+j)}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\log\sqrt{(n+j)(2n-j)}}.

(45)

Now

\displaystyle\log\sqrt{(n+j)(2n-j)}

\displaystyle=\log\left({\frac{{3n}}{2}}\right)+\log\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{9}\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j}{n}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\log\left({\frac{{3n}}{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(1)\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j}{n}}\right)^{2}.

Numerics suggest that as
n\to+\infty

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{j}^{2}\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j}{n}}\right)^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{{8n}}\binom{2n}{n},

(46)

This would lead to the more precise result that as
n\to+\infty

L_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\left(\log\left({\frac{{3n}}{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left({\frac{1}{n}}\right)\right)

(47)

Now to find asymptotics for
L_{n}
and
A_{n}
:
We use Stirling’s formula

I_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}\gamma-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{2}(2(H_{n-j}-H_{j})\ln((j+n)!)+H_{n+j})=\binom{2n}{n}\gamma+L_{n}-A_{n}

(48)

where
A_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{2}H_{n+j}

As
\binom{n}{j}^{2}
reaches a sharp maximum near
j=\frac{n}{2}
, we can choose
n
even for a while,
j=\frac{n}{2}+k
and present
A_{n}
as

A_{n}=\sum_{k=-n/2}^{n/2}\left(\frac{n!}{\big(\frac{n}{2}-k\big)!\big(\frac{n}{2}+k\big)!}\right)^{2}H_{\frac{3n}{2}+k}

(49)

Using the Stirling’s formula for
p!
( for
p\gg 1
)

A_{n}\sim\sum_{k=-n/2}^{n/2}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}H_{\frac{3n}{2}+k}}{\sqrt{2\pi(\frac{n}{2}+k)}\sqrt{2\pi(\frac{n}{2}-k)}\Big(\frac{\frac{n}{2}+k}{e}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}+k}\Big(\frac{\frac{n}{2}-k}{e}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}-k}}

(50)

The terms decline sharply as soon as
k
excides
\sqrt{n}\,
, so we can switch from summation to integration. Given that
H_{\frac{3n}{2}+k}
is slowly changing function, we are allowed just to take its value at
k=0
and use the asymptotics

H_{\frac{3n}{2}}=\gamma+\ln\frac{3n}{2}+O\big(\frac{1}{n}\big)

(51)

After manipulations we get

A_{n}\sim\frac{2\cdot 4^{n}\big(\gamma+\ln\frac{3}{2}+\ln n\big)}{\pi\sqrt{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-4t^{2}}dt=\frac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\left(\gamma+\ln\frac{3}{2}+\ln n\right)

(52)

Due to the fact that

\binom{2n}{n}\sim\frac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\to\infty\,\,\text{at}\,\,n\to\infty

(53)

and
I_{n}\to 0
at
n\to\infty
, we conclude that

L_{n}\sim\frac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\left(\ln\frac{3}{2}+\ln n\right)

(54)

This proves Lemma
4
.

Now we are ready to prove the Theorem: Since
\log S_{n}>0
(see [2]) and
0\leq\{\log S_{n}\}<1
so we get in LHS of
(8)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}\geq 0

(55)

Since by (5)

\log S_{n}=d_{2n}L_{n}

(56)

so we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{d_{2n}L_{n}\}

(57)

By (47), we can write (57) as

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\left\{d_{2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left(\log\left({\frac{{3n}}{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left({\frac{1}{n}}\right)\right)\right\}

(58)

Since by Prime number theorem, as
n\to\infty
,
d_{2n}\sim e^{2n}
and we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{e^{2n}}\right)\left\{e^{2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left(\log\left({\frac{{3n}}{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left({\frac{1}{n}}\right)\right)\right\}

(59)

So we claim that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}=0

(60)

Mathematica also hints the answer that the above limit in (60) is
0
. So we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{4^{2n}n}{d_{2n}}\right)\{\log S_{n}\}\neq\frac{\pi}{6\log 2}

(61)

This settles the proof of the Theorem.

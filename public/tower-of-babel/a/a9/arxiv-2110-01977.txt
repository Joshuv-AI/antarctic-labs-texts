Analytic scattering parameters at low energies.
E. Z. Liverts

Abstract
The scattering of two and more particles at low energies is described by the so called effective-range expansion. The leading terms of this expansion are the scattering length and effective range. The analytic expressions for both of the aforementioned scattering parameters are presented for the inverse-power potential and the Woods-Saxon potential. A technique for calculating the approximate scattering parameters is proposed. Approximate analytic formulas representing the scattering length and effective range are obtained for the Yukawa potential. The corresponding figures demonstrate a few interesting features of the effective range. All analytic formulas, both exact and approximate, were verified by comparing with the corresponding results obtained by direct numerical calculations. Wolfram Mathematica is heavily used. The presented results can be used with advantage in the fields of nuclear physics, atomic and molecular physics, quantum chemistry and many others.



I
Introduction

The scattering of two particles with short-range interactions at sufficiently low energy is determined by their
S
-wave scattering length
a
.
The energy is sufficiently low if the de Broglie wavelengths of the particles are large compared to the range of the interaction.
The scattering length is important not only for two-body systems, but also for few-body and many-body systems. If all the constituents of a few-body system have sufficiently low energy, its scattering properties are determined primarily by
a
. A many-body system has properties determined by
a
if its components have not only sufficiently low energies, but also separations that are large in comparison with the range of interactions.

At sufficiently low energies, the
S
-wave phase shift
\delta_{0}(k)
can be expanded in powers of the wave number
k
.
The expansion

k~\cot\delta_{0}(k)=-\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{2}r_{e}k^{2}+...

(1)

is called the
effective-range expansion
, and its the first two terms define the
S
-wave scattering length
a
and the effective range
r_{e}

EBH
;
FGR
.
These two scattering parameters provide a useful way to parametrize information, e. g., on low-energy nucleon-nucleon scattering. Furthermore, these characteristics may be related to observations other than
nn
-scattering, such as, e. g., deuteron binding energy. Very accurate results can be obtained for the
np
-system by scattering slow neutrons off protons in hydrogen atoms bound in
H_{2}
molecules.
Low-energy scattering parameters are widely applicable in the effective field theory (see, e. g.,
HAM
) to calculate the properties of nuclear matter and finite nuclei.
For these reasons, a great deal of attention is devoted to the measurements, calculations and understanding of these parameters.

In order to calculate the scattering parameters such as
a
or
r_{e}
, one needs to solve the radial Schr
\ddot{\textrm{o}}
dinger equation for
S
-partial waves
LAL
;
FGR
at zero energy

\chi^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}V(r)\chi(r),

(2)

under the boundary condition

\chi(0)=0.

(3)

Here

is reduced mass of the considered particles moving in the field

of
a fixed center of force,
\hbar
is reduced Planck constant.
Using the asymptotic representation

\chi(r)\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\simeq}A+Br

(4)

for the solution of Eq.(

) at large enough

one can find the scattering length

by a simple formula

a=-\frac{A}{B}.

In turn, the effective range

can be calculated by the integral (see, e.g., Ref.

r_{e}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\chi_{as}^{2}(r)-\chi^{2}(r)\right]dr,

where

is a physical solution of the Schr
\ddot{\textrm{o}}
dinger equation (

is the specified asymptotic form for

The Woods-Saxon potential

IV
Approximate analytical solution

There are many potentials, such as, e. g., the Yukawa potential, that do not give exact analytic solutions to the Schr
\ddot{\textrm{o}}
dinger equation (
2
).
To calculate the scattering parameters for these potentials, we propose a technique that will be described below.

Using the definition (
5
) for the scattering length, let us present a solution of Eq.(
2
) in the form:

\chi(r)=f(r)-1+\frac{r}{a},

(59)

where according to the boundary conditions (
3
) and (
7
) the function
f(r)
must satisfy the conditions:

f(0)=1,~~~~f(\infty)=0.

(60)

This function, in turn, can be represented as an expansion in some basis
\left\{g_{n}(r)\right\}
:

f(r)\simeq\sum_{n=0}^{N}C_{n}g_{n}(r).

(61)

In this paper we will use the simplest well-known basis functions of the form

g_{n}(r)=e^{-\beta r}r^{n},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(\beta>0)

(62)

which enables us to satisfy automatically the second of conditions (
60
),
whereas one should put
C_{0}=1
to satisfy the first one.

In this section, the system of units with
m=\hbar=1
will be used.
In order to calculate the coefficients
C_{i}
, one can apply the well-known method of projecting the initial Eq.(
2
) onto the subspace of the basis functions (
62
).
Let the approximate solution
\chi(r)
of this equation is presented by Eqs.(
59
)-(
62
).
Multiplication of both sides of the equation (
2
) by the basis function
g_{k}(r)
followed by
integration over the whole space yields:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi^{\prime\prime}(r)g_{k}(r)dr=2\int_{0}^{\infty}V(r)\chi(r)g_{k}(r)dr~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(k=1,2,...,N).

(63)

Substituting functions (
59
) with
f(r)
of the form (
61
) into the equation (
63
),
one obtains the following system of linear equations for
C_{n}
:

\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}S_{nk}=\frac{q_{k}}{a}-h_{k},

(64)

where

S_{nk}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(r)-2V(r)g_{n}(r)\right]g_{k}(r)dr,

(65)

q_{k}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}V(r)g_{k}(r)rdr,

(66)

h_{k}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{2V(r)\left[1-g_{0}(r)\right]+g_{0}^{\prime\prime}(r)\right\}g_{k}(r)dr.

(67)

Solution of Eq.(
64
) can be written in the form

C_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{q_{k}}{a}+h_{k}\right)S_{kn}^{-1},

(68)

where,
S_{kn}^{-1}
are the elements of the inverse matrix in respect to the matrix with the elements
S_{nk}
.

Using the asymptotic behavior (
4
), it is easy to show, that the scattering length (
5
) can be presented in the form:

a=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left[R-\frac{1}{y(R)}\right],

(69)

where
y(R)=\chi^{\prime}(R)/\chi(R)
is the logarithmic derivative of the radial wave function
\chi(R)
.

At this stage we propose to apply the quasilinearization method (QLM)
LEZ
, which enables us to calculate
y(R)
using the analytic but very accurate approximation. The QLM is iterative one.
It was shown
LEZ
that already at the first iteration the QLM can produce the analytical logarithmic derivative
y_{1}(R)
, which can be very accurate in case of making the correct choice of the wave function
\chi_{0}(R)
of zero iteration (initial guess).
Thus, according to QLM the logarithmic derivative
y_{1}(R)=\chi_{1}^{\prime}(R)/\chi_{1}(R)
of the solution
\chi_{1}(R)
to the Schr
\ddot{\textrm{o}}
dinger equation (
2
) can be presented in the form:

y_{1}(R)=\frac{G(R)}{\chi_{0}^{2}(R)},

(70)

where

G(R)=\int_{0}^{R}\left[\chi_{0}^{\prime 2}(r)+2V(r)\chi_{0}^{2}(r)\right]dr.

(71)

Subsequent consideration is dependent on the choice of potential
V(r)
.

As an example, let us consider the Yukawa potential of the form:

V(r)=-\lambda\frac{e^{-r}}{r}.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(\lambda>0)

(72)

This potential, also called the ”screened Coulomb potential”, is used in various fields of physics to model singular but short-range interactions.
Note, that the scale transformation
PAT
for the scattering length

a(\lambda,r_{0})=r_{0}a(\lambda r_{0},1)

(73)

enables us to investigate the Yukawa potential of the simplified form (
72
) instead of the general form corresponding to substitution of the exponent
-r
by
-r/r_{0}
.

Thus, using the function defined by Eqs.(
59
)-(
62
) as the initial guess
\chi_{0}(r)
, one obtains for the integral (
71
) with the Yukawa potential (
72
):

\displaystyle G(R)=\frac{R}{a^{2}}-b_{0}+b_{1}(R)e^{-R}+b_{2}(R)e^{-R\beta}+b_{3}e^{-R(1+\beta)}-\frac{\beta}{2}e^{-2R\beta}+~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle+2\lambda\left\{\Gamma(0,R)-2\Gamma[0,R(1+\beta)]+\Gamma[0,R(1+2\beta)]\right\}+~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}\left\{-p_{0,n}+p_{1,n}(R)e^{-2R\beta}+p_{2,n}(R)e^{-R(1+\beta)}+p_{3,n}\Gamma(n,2R\beta)+p_{4,n}\Gamma[n,R(1+2\beta)]+\right.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\left.+p_{5,n}\Gamma[n,R(1+\beta)]\right\}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{n}C_{k}\left\{s_{0,nk}+s_{1,nk}\Gamma[n+k,R(1+2\beta)]+s_{2,nk}\Gamma[n+k-1,2R\beta]+\right.~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\left.+s_{3,nk}\Gamma[n+k,2R\beta]+s_{4,nk}\Gamma[n+k+1,2R\beta]\right\},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(74)

where

\displaystyle b_{0}=\frac{2\lambda}{a^{2}}-\frac{2}{a}\left(2\lambda-1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+\beta}\right)-\frac{\beta}{2}+2\lambda\ln\left[\frac{(1+\beta)^{2}}{1+2\beta}\right];~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle p_{0,n}=(n-1)!\left\{\frac{n\beta}{(2\beta)^{n}}+4\lambda\left[\frac{1}{(1+2\beta)^{n}}+\frac{n-a(1+\beta)}{a(1+\beta)^{n+1}}\right]\right\};~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle s_{0,nk}=\frac{2\lambda(n+k-1)!}{(1+2\beta)^{n+k}}+\frac{\left[(n-k)^{2}-n-k\right](n+k-2)!}{4(2\beta)^{n+k-1}};~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(75)

\displaystyle b_{1}(R)=\frac{2\lambda(1-2a+R)}{a^{2}};~~b_{2}(R)=\frac{2}{a}\left(1+\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}R^{n}\right);~~b_{3}=\frac{4\lambda}{a(1+\beta)};~~p_{1,n}(R)=-\beta R^{n};~~~

\displaystyle p_{2,n}(R)=\frac{4\lambda R^{n}}{a(1+\beta)};~~p_{3,n}=\frac{n\beta}{(2\beta)^{n}};~~p_{4,n}=\frac{4\lambda}{(1+2\beta)^{n}};~~p_{5,n}=\frac{4\lambda\left[n-a(1+\beta)\right]}{a(1+\beta)^{n+1}};~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle s_{1,nk}=-\frac{2\lambda}{(1+2\beta)^{n+k}};~~s_{2,nk}=\frac{nk}{(2\beta)^{n+k-1}};~~s_{3,nk}=-\frac{(n+k)}{2(2\beta)^{n+k-1}};~~s_{4,nk}=\frac{1}{4(2\beta)^{n+k-1}}.~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(76)

Taking into account, that the incomplete gamma functions
\Gamma(x,y)

for integer
x=n
can be presented in the form

\displaystyle\Gamma(n,x)=(n-1)!e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!},~~~~~~~~~(n=1,2,...)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\Gamma(0,x)=\frac{e^{-x}}{x+1-\frac{1}{x+3-...}},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(77)

one can write the following asymptotic expression for the integral (
71
):

G(R)\underset{R\rightarrow\infty}{\simeq}\frac{R}{a^{2}}-b_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}p_{0,n}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{n}C_{k}s_{0,nk}.

(78)

To obtain the last equation, the terms with exponential factors were neglected.

It is clear, that the asymptotic expression for the initial guess function (
59
) is of the form:

\chi_{0}(R)\underset{R\rightarrow\infty}{\simeq}\frac{R}{a}-1.

(79)

Substituting the asymptotic expressions (
78
) and (
79
) into the RHS of Eq.(
70
), one obtains for the RHS of Eq.(
69
):

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left[R-\frac{\chi_{0}^{2}(R)}{G(R)}\right]=a\left[2-a\left(b_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}p_{0,n}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{n}C_{k}s_{0,nk}\right)\right].

(80)

Thus, Eq.(
69
) can be rewritten in the form:

b_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}p_{0,n}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{n}C_{k}s_{0,nk}=\frac{1}{a}.

(81)

Inserting the explicit expressions (
IV
) for
b_{0},p_{0,n}
and
s_{0,nk}

into Eq.(
81
), one obtains the equation

A_{2}a^{2}+A_{1}a+2\lambda=0,

(82)

where

\displaystyle A_{1}=1-\frac{4\lambda}{1+\beta}\left[\beta-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{C_{n}n(n-1)!}{(1+\beta)^{n}}\right],~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle A_{2}=-\frac{\beta}{2}+2\lambda\ln\left[\frac{(1+\beta)^{2}}{1+2\beta}\right]+\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}(n-1)!\left\{\frac{n\beta}{(2\beta)^{n}}+4\lambda\left[\frac{1}{(1+2\beta)^{n}}-\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{n}}\right]\right\}+~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{n}C_{k}(n+k-2)!\left\{\frac{2\lambda(n+k-1)}{(1+2\beta)^{n+k}}-\frac{\beta\left[n+k-(n-k)^{2}\right]}{2(2\beta)^{n+k}}\right\}.~~~~~~~~~~~

(83)

In fact, Eq.(
82
) represents a transcendental equation for the scattering length
a
, because in general case both linear coefficients
C_{i}
and the exponent factor
\beta
can be functions of
a
.
However, in the case of
\beta
is independent on
a
, one obtains a quadratic equation for
a
, and hence, an explicit analytic expression for the scattering length.
Let us demonstrate this point on examples of the simplest expansions with
N=1,2
.

In order to derive an analytic solutions to Eq.(
64
) corresponding to the Yukawa potential (
72
), let us write the explicit expressions for
S_{nk},q_{k}
and
h_{k}
according to Eqs.(
65
), (
66
) and (
67
), respectively:

\displaystyle S_{nk}=(n+k-2)!\left\{\frac{2\lambda(n+k-1)}{(1+2\beta)^{n+k}}-\frac{\beta\left[n+k-(n-k)^{2}\right]}{2(2\beta)^{n+k}}\right\},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle q_{k}=-\frac{2\lambda k!}{(1+\beta)^{1+k}},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle h_{k}=(k-1)!\left\{2\lambda\left[\frac{1}{(1+2\beta)^{k}}-\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{k}}\right]+\frac{k\beta}{2(2\beta)^{k}}\right\}.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(84)

Thus, solution of Eq.(
64
) for the two-term expansion (

C_{1}^{(1)}=\frac{\beta(1+2\beta)\left\{a(1+\beta)\left[1+\beta(3+2\beta-8\lambda)\right]+8\lambda(1+2\beta)\right\}}{a(1+\beta)^{2}\left[1+4\beta(1+\beta-2\lambda)\right]}.

(85)

The superscript denotes that this expression corresponds to the expansion with
N=1
.
Inserting this expression into Eqs.(
IV

B_{2}^{(1)}a^{2}+B_{1}^{(1)}a+B_{0}^{(1)}=0,

(86)

\displaystyle 2\lambda(1+\beta)^{2}\left[1+4\beta(1+\beta-2\lambda)\right]\ln\left[\frac{(1+\beta)^{2}}{1+2\beta}\right],~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle B_{1}^{(1)}=(1+\beta)^{2}(1+2\beta)^{2}-4\lambda\beta\left[2+\beta(5+6\beta+4\beta^{2})\right]+\dfrac{32\lambda^{2}\beta^{4}}{1+\beta},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle B_{0}^{(1)}=\frac{2\lambda}{(1+\beta)^{2}}\left\{(1+\beta)^{4}(1+2\beta)^{2}-8\lambda\beta^{3}\left[2(1+2\beta)+\beta^{2}\right]\right\}.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(87)

Solving Eq.(

) for the three-term expansion (

), one can derive the proper expressions for the linear coefficients

and

. Inserting then those expressions into Eqs.(

instead of

.
Note, that the coefficients

(

Therefore, if the exponent

is independent on

.

V
Conclusions

The properties of the effective-range expansion have been studied.
The analytic expressions for the leading terms of this expansion, the scattering length and effective range, have been derived for the inverse-power potential and the Woods-Saxon potential.

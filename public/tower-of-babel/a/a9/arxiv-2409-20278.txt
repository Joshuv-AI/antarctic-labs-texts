Width Parameters for Minimum Flow Decomposition
Andreas Grigorjew, Wanchote Po Jiamjitrak, Brendan Mumey, Alexandru I. Tomescu

Abstract
Minimum flow decomposition ( MFD ) is the strongly 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard problem of finding a smallest set of integer weighted s s - t t paths in an s s - t t DAG G G whose weighted sum is equal to a given flow f f on G G . Despite its many practical applications, we lack an understanding of graph structures that make MFD easy or hard. Recent progress is due to Cáceres et al. [ACM TALG 2024], who showed that the DAG width , the minimum number of paths to cover all edges, plays an essential role in the approximation of the problem. Our first set of results regard the computational complexity of MFD parameterised by the width. This question was previously open, because MFD on width-1 DAGs (i.e. paths) is trivially solvable, and the existing 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hardness proofs use DAGs of unbounded width. We show that MFD on width-2 DAGs is already 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard and that MFD on width-3 DAGs is strongly 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard. Our main contribution complements these hardness bounds, as we show that weak 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hardness is the best we can hope for on width-2 DAGs. In fact, we prove the more general statement that MFD with unary coded input can be solved in quasi-polynomial time on DAGs of constant parallel-width, which includes width-2 DAGs. The parallel-width of a DAG G G ( par-width ​ ( G ) \textsf{par-width}(G) ) was defined by Deligkas and Meir [MFCS 2017] as the size of the largest minimal s s - t t cut-set. We obtain these results by, a) interpreting flow decompositions as a sequence of certain digraph minor operations defined by Deligkas and Meir [MFCS 2017], and b) defining a new notion of width of a flow network, flow-width of ( G , f ) (G,f) , defined as the minimum number of paths covering all edges of G G , where every edge e e can be covered by at most f ⁡ ( e ) f(e) paths. Using (a) and (b), we show as an intermediate result, an improved upper bound ( ⌊ log ⁡ ‖ f ‖ ⌋ + 1 ) ⋅ par-width ​ ( G ) (\lfloor\log\|f\|\rfloor+1)\cdot\textsf{par-width}(G) for MFD , where ‖ f ‖ \|f\| is the largest flow weight of all edges.

Introduction

Minimum Flow Decomposition (
MFD
) of a given flow on a directed graph is the problem of finding a minimum sized set of weighted paths, such that for every edge, the sum of the weights of the paths crossing this edge is equal to the flow on the edge. It is a standard result that every flow on a directed acyclic graph can be decomposed to at most
m-n+2
weighted paths
[
1
,
27
]
, where
n
is the number of nodes and
m
is the number of edges. In this paper, all graphs will be directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) that are multigraphs (that is, we allow parallel edges) with single source
s
and single sink
t
, and we use the term
flow network
to refer to a pair
(G,f)
of a DAG
G
and a flow
f
on
G
. This paper addresses the variant
MFD
N
, where the flow and path weights are non-negative integers. We distinguish it from
MFD
Z
, where the flow and path weights can also be negative; we use
MFD
to refer to both variants and we write
\mathsf{mfd}_{\mathbb{Y}}(G,f)
for the size of the minimum flow decomposition using path weights in a set
\mathbb{Y}
.

MFD
is strongly
\mathsf{NP}
-hard, even on DAGs, and even when the flow values come from
\{1,2,4\}

[
14
]
. However, the problem has a wide range of applications, e.g. in Bioinformatics
[
3
,
9
,
12
,
22
,
24
,
23
,
28
,
2
,
21
]
, transportation planning
[
20
]
or network design
[
14
,
19
,
27
]
.
Despite this widespread use, we lack a good theoretical understanding behind the complexity of
MFD
N
.
Moreover, as opposed to other
\mathsf{NP}
-hard problems which have been extensively studied on restricted classes of graphs, we have only little knowledge of graph classes that make the problem tractable or any other structural properties that make
MFD
N
easier. Below, we describe in more detail the current state of the art around
MFD
.

1.1
Related work

Kloster et al.
[
16
]
showed that

N
is in linear FPT time
2^{O(k^{2})}\cdot(n+\log\|f\|)

k=\mathsf{mfd}_{\mathbb{N}}(G,f)

is the maximum norm of

\varepsilon

O(1/\varepsilon^{2})

[
14
]
.

Recent theoretical progress is due to Cáceres et al.
[
4

, who showed that the

s
-

MFD
, since every flow decomposition is also such a path cover. For example, using width, they improved the approximation factor lower bound of the widely-used greedy approach for
MFD

-

– to
\Omega(m/\log m)

[

. To obtain this, they exploited the fact that the width can grow exponentially after subtracting flow and removing edges of zero flow.

To partially address this issue, Cáceres et al.
[
4
]
proved that restricting the input to

graphs, which Cáceres et al.
[
4

defined as the class of

-

-

A notable example of width-stable DAGs are

\mathsf{NP}

[

, and it is not known whether it can be decided in polynomial time whether a graph is width-stable.
Moreover, Cáceres et al.
[

used the width to define an approximation algorithm for

by repeatedly decomposing the flow using

As we show in this work,

is related to the

problem, in which we are given a set

of positive integers and a parameter

O^{*}(s^{\log s})

1.2
Our contributions

As opposed to other
\mathsf{NP}
-hard problems, we have little understanding of what makes
MFD
easy to solve or hard. As such, in this paper we establish further connections between structural properties or parameters of the graph, and the tractability status of
MFD
N
in relation to these parameters.
We first show that instances of small width remain
\mathsf{NP}
-hard. This was previously open, because all reductions showing the hardness of
MFD
use instances of arbitrary widths.
We then show how using two related parameters, the
flow-width
of a DAG
G
and a flow
f
, which we introduce in
Section

4
, and the
parallel-width

\textsf{par-width}(G)
, recently introduced by Deligkas and Meir
[
7
]
, can make
MFD
N
easier to solve.

Investigating width parameters is motivated by the following. On width-1 graphs,
MFD
is trivially solvable. Moreover, we observe that some real-world instances from Bioinformatics
[
25
]
admit small widths. Further, we reveal structural features of flow decompositions, as we demonstrate in the following.

Improved hardness results.

In
Section

3
we show that
MFD
N
remains
\mathsf{NP}
-hard on small width instances. It is known that it remains
\mathsf{NP}
-hard on width-stable graphs, since the
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness reduction from
[
27
]
uses series-parallel graphs, which are a subclass of width-stable graphs
[
4
]
. However, their width is not bounded and it remained open whether having a bounded width makes the problem easier. We address this by proving the following.

Theorem 1
.

MFD
N
on a DAG
G
is strongly
\mathsf{NP}
-hard even when
0pt{G}=3
.

To prove this theorem, we devise a construction where, even if the initial graph width is
3
, the width can grow arbitrarily large after removing a path of any MFD.
Our next theorem shows that the problem remains
\mathsf{NP}
-hard on width-2 graphs when the flow is coded in binary with a reduction from the
generating set
problem
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2
.

MFD
N
on a DAG
G
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard when
f
is coded in binary, even when
0pt{G}=2
.

We will also show that this result is tight by addressing the case when the flow is coded in unary (i.e., the natural number
a
is stored using
a+1
bits) in the following.

Improved parameterised runtime.

Our main result regards an improved parameterised runtime upper bound for
MFD
. We use the parallel-width of
G
(written
\textsf{par-width}(G)
),
which is defined as the size of the largest minimal cut-set of
G
and was introduced in the context of a directed graph minor notion on
s
-
t
digraphs
[
7
]
. The main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{N}
be a constant.
MFD
N
on
(G,f)
can be solved in time
\|f\|^{O(\log\|f\|)}\cdot n
when
\textsf{par-width}(G)\leq c
, which is quasi-polynomial if
f
is coded in unary.

Note that, contrary to graphs of width at least 3, all width-2 graphs admit a parallel-width of 2.

Theorem

1
,
Theorem

2
and
Theorem

3
provide a tight description of
MFD
N
’s computational complexity on constant width. Moreover, the results show that
MFD
N
on constant parallel-width naturally generalizes the
generating set
problem. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that the parallel-width has been used in the analysis of the computational complexity of a problem.

Kloster et al.
[
16
]
stated the open problem, whether there exists a
O^{*}(2^{o(k^{2})})
time FPT algorithm for
MFD
. Our results make partial progress on this question, as the reduction used for
Theorem

2
shows that the existence of such an algorithm implies that
generating set
can be solved in time
O^{*}(2^{o((\log s)^{2})}))
. This is an unsolved problem, as previous solvers for
generating set
do not resolve this question
[
17
,
6
]
.

Flow-width and graph minors.

We now explain how we achieve
Theorem

3
.
Our main tool is to analyse the flow network structure in
Section

4
, using the
flow-width

\textsf{fw}(G,f)
, a new parameter that we define and which might be of independent interest. In contrast to
MFD
Z
, the DAG width faces the drawback of covering an edge too often: given a positive flow
f
on
G
, it might be necessary for all minimum path covers to cover an edge more than
f(e)
times. The flow-width uses the given flow
f
as upper bounds for the number of times covering paths can use edges, and satisfies
0pt{G}\leq\textsf{fw}(G,f)
for all positive flows
f
. Moreover, the flow-width is still a natural lower bound to the optimal solution of
MFD
N
.

We begin by showing that the flow-width is monotone on the flow, if and only if the underlying DAG is width-stable, and that the flow-width is a more fine-grained parameter to analyse the width-stability compared to width alone. This implies the following corollary, improving the best known approximation ratio of
MFD
N
on width-stable DAGs from
O(\log\textsf{Val}(f))

[
4
]
to
O(\log\|f\|)
:

Corollary 4
.

On width-stable DAGs we can approximate
MFD
N
given the input
(G,f)
with ratio
\lfloor\log\|f\|\rfloor+1
in time
O(nm\log\|f\|)
.

Next, we argue that the graph minors defined by Deligkas and Meir
[
7
]
correspond to possible edge saturations of flow decompositions in
Section

5
. This enables us to use the parallel-width as parameter, which was defined in the context of these graph minors.
The flow-width plays a central role in this context, as it connects the width and the parallel-width:

Lemma 5
.

For all
s
-
t
DAGs
G
, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle 0pt{G}

\displaystyle=\min\{\textsf{fw}(G,f)\mid f>0\},

\displaystyle\textsf{par-width}(G)

\displaystyle=\max\{\textsf{fw}(G,f)\mid f\geq 0\}.

While
\textsf{par-width}(G)
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard to compute, it admits a polynomial-time algorithm if it is constant
[
7
]
.
Using
Lemma

5
, we obtain a new upper bound for
MFD
N
, which we will use to prove
Theorem

3
and is of independent interest:

Lemma 6
.

Given a flow network
(G,f)
with
f>0
, we can decompose the flow using at most
\textsf{par-width}(G)\cdot(\lfloor\log\|f\|\rfloor+1)
paths in time
O(nm\log\|f\|)
.

Decomposing via flow-width

Suppose we are decomposing a flow path by path. During this decomposition, flow is subtracted from edges until they are saturated and cannot be traversed by further weighted paths any more. 4.1

Definition 11

-

4.2
Parity fixing with minimally covering flows

Cáceres et al.
[

used the width to define an approximation algorithm for

, where

This algorithm follows a “parity fixing” approach: it constructs a unitary flow
(that is, a flow with values in

which, when subtracted from

with weights in

The resulting flow can then be divided by two, to repeat the procedure until the flow is zero. To sum up, Cáceres et al.
[

proved that it is possible to express

as

f=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\log\|f\|\rfloor}2^{i}f_{i},\text{~~~with~~~}\mathsf{mfd}_{\{-1,1\}}(G,f_{i})\leq 0pt{G}.

(2)

We now present the parity-fixing heuristic for
MFD
N
by Mumey et al.
[
19

in order to theoretically analyse its performance.
Given a flow

such that

[

. In other words, they showed that the following LP formulation solves the problem for a given flow network

\displaystyle\min\textsf{Val}(g),\text{subject to}

(3)

\displaystyle g\text{ is a flow on $G$},

\displaystyle 0\leq g\leq f,

\displaystyle g(e)\geq 1\text{ for all $e\in E(G)$ where $f(e)$ is odd}.

In the
i

be an optimal solution to the LP formulation
3

and subtract

.
See

5

MFD

We now present a novel view on flow decompositions, which leads to the main result of the paper. We first show that, despite being used in a different context, there exists a close connection of a certain notion of directed graph minors defined by Deligkas and Meir
[
7
]
to the decomposition of flows, in the sense that contractions of flow-subgraphs are equivalent to the digraph minor operations. Next, we use this connection to parameterise

by the parallel-width, which was defined by Deligkas and Meir by the largest minimal cut-set
[
7

. They showed that the class of graphs of

at most

is hereditary. We then show that the flow-width generalises the

and the

. This gives us an upper bound for
MFD

and consequently an improved parameterised complexity for
MFD

6
Conclusions

In this article we answered problems regarding the computational complexity of
MFD
N
parameterised by DAG width notions. We showed that
MFD
N
on graphs
G
with
0pt{G}=2
or
\textsf{par-width}(G)\leq c
obtains a quasi-polynomial runtime when the flow is coded in unary and is NP-hard for binary coded flows. This shows that
MFD
N
with constant parallel-width naturally generalises the
generating set
problem. When the width is equal to 3, the problem remains even strongly NP-hard.

We achieved this result using two techniques. First, by introducing the flow-width of a flow network, we relate minimally covering flows to graph structure. Second, by connecting flow decompositions to directed minors. We showed an intermediate result that expressing the input flow as the sum of minimally covering flows can lead to efficient upper bounds for minimum flow decompositions.

An interesting open question is, whether
generating set
can be solved in time
O^{*}(2^{o(\log(s)^{2})})
, where
s
is the maximum integer in the input, or can the existence of such an algorithm be ruled out under classical computational complexity assumptions? This paper shows that a negative answer would also show that there is no
O^{*}(2^{o(k^{2})})
time algorithm for
MFD
N
.

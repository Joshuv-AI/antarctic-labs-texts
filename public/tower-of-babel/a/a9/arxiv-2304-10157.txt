On p − p- Rationality of Number Fields and Greenberg’s Generalized Conjecture
Hang Li and Derong Qiu

Abstract




Abstract
In this paper, a new criteria is given to determine
the
p-
rationality of number fields, some illustrated examples are constructed,
the relations between Greenberg’s Generalized Conjecture (GGC, for short),
generalized
abc-
conjecture and the
p-
rationality are discussed, from which
some explicit fields satisfying GGC are obtained.

Keywords
:
p-
rationality, Greenberg’s Generalized Conjecture (GGC),
generalized
abc-
conjecture, class group.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2020
: 11R23 ,
11R29.

1. Introduction and Main Results

Let
F
be a number field,
p
a prime number. Let
S_{p}
denote the set of
all places of
F
above
p,\ M_{S_{p}}(F)
the maximal pro-
p
extension unramified
outside
S_{p},
and
F_{S_{p}}
the maximal Abelian pro-
p
extension unramified
outside
S_{p}.
Write
G_{S_{p}}(F):=\text{Gal}(M_{S_{p}}(F)/F),
then

G_{S_{p}}(F)^{ab}=\text{Gal}(F_{S_{p}}/F).
In the following, we denote by

\mathcal{T}_{F}=\text{tor}(G_{S_{p}}(F)^{ab})
the maximal
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
torsion
submodule of
G_{S_{p}}(F)^{ab}.

F
is called
p-
rational if
G_{S_{p}}(F)
is a free
pro-
p
group (see [Mov1, 2]).

It is known ([Gr2],section 3) that a number field
F
is
p-
rational if and only if the
following two conditions hold

(1)
\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(G_{S_{p}}(F)^{ab})=r_{2}(F)+1;

(2)
G_{S_{p}}(F)^{ab}
is a torsion free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
module, i.e.,

\mathcal{T}_{F}=\{1\}.

Condition (1) above is equivalent to say that Leopoldt’s conjecture holds for
(F,p),

while condition (2) means that
F_{S_{p}}=\widetilde{F},
the composite field of
all the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
extension of
F.

The
p-
rationality of number fields has been extensively studied, one of its important
application is on constructing non-abelian number fields satisfying Leopoldt’s conjecture
(e.g. [Mov1, 2]). Besides, much questions about it are still open, such as

Conjecture 1.1
(Gras [G3, Conjecture 8.11]).
Every number field
F
is

p-
rational if
p
is sufficiently large.

Conjecture 1.2
(Greenberg [Gr2]).
For any odd prime number
p
and positive integer

t,
there exists a Galois extension
F
of the rational number field
{\mathbb{Q}}

such that
F
is
p-
rational and
\text{Gal}(F/{\mathbb{Q}})\cong({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{t}.

When
F
is a quadratic number field or some of their composite, much progress have
been made on the Greenberg’s conjecture above (see e.g., [AB],[BM], [Ko], [CLS]).

There are a series of work on determining the
p-
rationality of totally real number fields
and quadratic imaginary number fields (see e.g., [Gr2], [Li]). In this paper, we will study
similar questions on some cases that
F
is neither totally real nor quadratic, more
precisely, we consider the
p-
rationality of complex cubic and pure imaginary quartic Number
Fields, and the connection of
p-
rationalality of certain CM number fields with GGC.
Our main results are the following

Theorem 1.3.

Let
K
be a complex cubic or pure imaginary quartic number field,

p
a prime number (when
p=3,
assume it is unramified in
K/{\mathbb{Q}},
when

p=5
and
K
is quartic, assume
5
is not totally ramifed in
K
).

Then
K
is
p-
rational if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(1)
H_{p}(K)\subseteq\widetilde{K},
where
H_{p}(K)
is the Hilbert
p-
class
field of
K,
and
\widetilde{K}
is the composite of
all
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
extension of
K.

(2) There is a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
lying over
p
such that

\varepsilon^{p^{f}-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}^{e+1})
for
any fundamental unit
\varepsilon
of
K,
where
e
and
f

are the ramification index and residue degree of
\mathfrak{p}
in
K/{\mathbb{Q}},
respectively.

Theorem 1.4.

Let
K
be a CM field,
K^{+}
be its maximal real subfield,
let
p
be an odd prime number which splits completely in
K.
Let
K_{\infty}^{+}

be the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
extension of
K^{+}.
Assume
p\nmid h(K),

where
h(K)
is the class number of
K.
Then

(1) The Iwasawa invariants
\lambda,\mu,\nu
of
K_{\infty}^{+}/K^{+}
all vanish
and the Leopoldt’s conjecture holds for
(K^{+},p)
if and only if
K^{+}
is

p-
rational.

(2) If
K^{+}
is
p-
rational, then GGC holds for
(K,p).

Theorem 1.5.

Let
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a complex cubic number field,

\varepsilon>0
be its fundamental unit,
f(x)=x^{3}-a_{2}x^{2}-a_{1}x-a_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\varepsilon
over
{\mathbb{Q}}.

Let
p
be an odd prime number with
p\nmid d(f)
(the discriminant of
f(x)
).
Define the arithmetic third order sequence of rational integers

F_{n+3}=a_{2}F_{n+2}+a_{1}F_{n+1}+a_{0}F_{n}\ (n\geq 0),\ F_{0}=F_{1}=0,F_{2}=1.

Then
p
is unramified over
K/{\mathbb{Q}}.

Furthermore,

(1) if
p
splits completely over
K/{\mathbb{Q}},\ F_{p-1}\not\equiv 0(\text{mod}\ p^{2}),

then there is a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
lying over
p
such that

\varepsilon^{p-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}^{2}).

(2) if
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
for two different
prime ideals in
K
with
f(\mathfrak{p}_{1}/p)=1
and
f(\mathfrak{p}_{2}/p)=2,

then

F_{p^{2}-1}\not\equiv 0(\text{mod}\ p^{2})\Rightarrow\varepsilon^{p-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})
or

\varepsilon^{p^{2}-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2}).

(3) if
p
is inertia over
K/{\mathbb{Q}},
i.e.,
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}

is a prime ideal in
K,
then

F_{p^{3}-1}\not\equiv 0(\text{mod}\ p^{2})\Rightarrow\varepsilon^{p^{3}-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}^{2}).

Theorem 1.6.

Let
K
be a complex cubic or pure imaginary quartic number field.
If the generalized
abc-
conjecture holds for
K,
then as
x\rightarrow\infty,
we have

\sharp\{\text{prime numbers}\ p\leq x,K\ \text{is}\ p-\text{rational}\}\geq c\log x

for some constant
c>0
depending only on
K.

Remark 1.7.
Under the assumption of Theorem 1.4 above, by the proof of Prop.2.14
of [BM], one can obtain that,
K^{+}
is
p-
rational
\Leftrightarrow

K
is
p-
rational. So the conclusion (2) of Theorem 1.4 above can also be
stated as: If
K
is
p-
rational, then GGC holds for
(K,p).
This partially
answers the following open question in [ABo]:

Do
p-
rational number fields satisfy Greenberg’s generalized conjecture ?

In the following, for a number field
F,
as usual, we denote by
r_{1}
and

r_{2}
the number of real and complex infinite places, respectively.
So
r_{1}+2r_{2}=[F:{\mathbb{Q}}].
Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
F

and
E_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
denote the group of units. For any place
v

of
F,
let
F_{v}
denote the completion of
F
at
v,
and
U_{v}^{(1)}

denote the group of principal units of
F_{v}.
Also, we let
h(F)
denote the class
number of
F.

In general, for a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
module
M,
let
\text{tor}(M)

denote its maximal torsion
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
submodule.

2. Proof of Theorem 1.3

Let
K
be a complex cubic number field, then
[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]=3
and
r_{1}=r_{2}=1.

Let
\varepsilon>1
be its fundamental unit. For any prime number
p,
it is easy to
see that the Leopoldt’s conjecture ([Ka]) holds for
(K,p).

Proposition 2.1.

Let
K
be a complex cubic number field,

p\geq 3
a prime number which splits completely in
K/{\mathbb{Q}}.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{i}(i=1,2,3)
be all the primes of
K
lying over
p.
Then

\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)}}{\overline{E^{\prime}}})=\{1\}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \varepsilon^{p-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ p^{2}\mathcal{O}_{K}),\quad(2.1)

where
U_{i}^{(1)}
is the group of principal units of the local field

K_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}},E^{\prime}=\{\varepsilon\in E_{K}:\varepsilon\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{i})(i=1,2,3)\},
and
\overline{E^{\prime}}
is the closure of
the image of
E^{\prime}
in
\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)}
via
the diagonal imbedding.

Proof.
To begin with, we have the exact sequence ([G1, Chapt.3, Lemma4.2.4])

1\rightarrow\frac{\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\overline{E^{\prime}})}\rightarrow\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)}}{\overline{E^{\prime}}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{log}_{p}}}{{\rightarrow}}\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\frac{\log_{p}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})})\rightarrow 0.\quad(2.2)

By assumption,
E_{K}=<-1>\times<\varepsilon>,e(\mathfrak{p}_{i}/p)=1,i=1,2,3.
So
\frac{e}{p-1}=\frac{1}{p}<1.

By [N, Chapt2, prop.5.5],
\log_{p}:U_{i}^{(1)}\rightarrow\mathfrak{p}_{i}

are isomorphisms, so each
U_{i}^{(1)}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
torsion free
(i=1,2,3),

and then
\frac{\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\overline{E^{\prime}})}=\{1\}.
Therefore, to show (2.1), we only need to show that

\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\frac{\log_{p}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})})=\{0\}\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon^{p-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ p^{2}\mathcal{O}_{K}).

Note that
-1\notin E^{\prime}
because
p>2.

Let
i_{p}:\ E^{\prime}\rightarrow U_{1}^{(1)}\times U_{2}^{(1)}\times U_{3}^{(1)},x\mapsto(i_{1}(x),i_{2}(x),i_{3}(x))

be the diagonal imbedding. Then
\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(i_{p}(E^{\prime}))\leq\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(E^{\prime})=1.
On the other hand,
p
splits completely in

k/{\mathbb{Q}},
so
\varepsilon^{p-1}\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{i}),i=1,2,3.
hence
i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1})\in U_{s}^{(1)}(s=1,2,3),

i.e.,
\varepsilon^{p-1}\in E^{\prime},i_{p}(\varepsilon^{p-1})\in i_{p}(E^{\prime}).
So
<i_{p}(\varepsilon^{p-1})>\subset i_{p}(E^{\prime}),
which implies
\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(i_{p}(E^{\prime}))\geq 1,

and then
\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(i_{p}(E^{\prime}))=1.
Since each element in

i_{p}(E^{\prime})
is of the form
i_{p}(\varepsilon^{t}),\varepsilon^{t}\in E^{\prime},
we may take a smallest positive integer
t_{0}
such that

i_{p}(E^{\prime})=<i_{p}(\varepsilon^{t_{0}})>.
Then
t_{0}\mid(p-1).

Let
\overline{E^{\prime}}
be the closure of
i_{p}(E^{\prime})
in

\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)}.
Then

\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\overline{E^{\prime}})=1,
and as a

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
module,
\overline{E^{\prime}}
is generated by

i_{p}(\varepsilon^{t_{0}}),
so
\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})
is
generated by
\log_{p}(i_{p}(\varepsilon^{t_{0}})).

Hence for any
(a,b,c)\in\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})\subset\log_{p}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)}),
there is
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
such that

(a,b,c)=d\cdot(\log_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{t_{0}})),\log_{p}(i_{2}(\varepsilon^{t_{0}})),\log_{p}(i_{3}(\varepsilon^{t_{0}}))).
On the other hand, since

t_{0}\mid p-1,
we may let
p-1=r_{0}t_{0}.

Then
(r_{0},p)=1
and
\frac{1}{r_{0}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}.
Hence

d\cdot\log_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{t_{0}}))=\frac{d}{r_{0}}\cdot\log_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\cdot\log_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),
which
implies
(a,b,c)\in\prod_{s=1}^{3}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\cdot\log_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1})).

Now we come to show the assertion (2.1) above.

\Rightarrow:
Suppose

\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\frac{\log_{p}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})})=\{0\}

but
\varepsilon^{p-1}\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ p^{2}\mathcal{O}_{K}).
Then

\varepsilon^{p-1}\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{s}^{2})(s=1,2,3)

because
p^{2}\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{3}^{2}.

So
i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1})\in U_{s}^{(2)}=(U_{s}^{(1)})^{p}\ (s=1,2,3)

because
\frac{e}{p-1}=\frac{1}{p-1}<1
(see [S, chat.14,prop9]). Hence
there is
(x,y,z)\in U_{1}^{(1)}\times U_{2}^{(1)}\times U_{3}^{(1)}
such that

(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}),i_{2}(\varepsilon^{p-1}),i_{3}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))=(x^{p},y^{p},z^{p})\in U_{1}^{(2)}\times U_{2}^{(2)}\times U_{3}^{(2)}.

If
\log_{p}x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\cdot\log_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),

then there exists
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
such that

\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))=p\log_{p}x=pa\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),
so

\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))=pa\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),
which implies that
pa=1
(notice that

\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))\neq 0

because
i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})\neq 1
), hence
p
is invertible in

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},
a contradiction! Similarly for
y,z.
So we have

(\log_{p}x,\log_{p}y,\log_{p}z)\notin\prod_{s=1}^{3}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\cdot\log_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),

but
p\cdot(\log_{p}x,\log_{p}y,\log_{p}z)=(p\log_{p}x,p\log_{p}y,p\log_{p}z)=(\log_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),\log_{p}(i_{2}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),\log_{p}(i_{3}(\varepsilon^{p-1})))\in\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}}).
So

(\log_{p}x,\log_{p}y,\log_{p}z)\ \text{mod}

\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})
is a torsion element in

\frac{\log_{p}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})},
i.e.,

\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\frac{\log_{p}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})})\neq 0,

contradicts to the assumption!

\Leftarrow:
Suppose

\text{tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(\frac{\log_{p}(\prod_{i=1}^{3}U_{i}^{(1)})}{\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})})\neq\{0\}.

Then there is an element
(\log_{p}x_{1},\log_{p}x_{2},\log_{p}x_{3})

\in\prod_{i=1}^{3}\log_{p}(U_{i}^{(1)})\setminus\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}})
such that

p\cdot(\log_{p}x_{1},\log_{p}x_{2},\log_{p}x_{3})\in\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}}).
We assert that

\log_{p}x_{s}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\text{log}_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))

(s=1,2,3).
If otherwise, then there are two cases discussed as follows:

(1)
\log_{p}x_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\text{log}_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))\ (s=1,2,3).
Then

\log_{p}x_{s}=a_{s}\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))
for some
a_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(s=1,2,3).
Since

p\cdot(\log_{p}x_{1},\log_{p}x_{2},\log_{p}x_{3})\in\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}}),
there is
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
such that

p\cdot(\log_{p}x_{1},\log_{p}x_{2},\log_{p}x_{3})=(d\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))),d\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{2}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))),d\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))),
from which we get

pa_{1}=pa_{2}=pa_{3},
so
a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}
and then

(\log_{p}x_{1},\log_{p}x_{2},\log_{p}x_{3})\in\log_{p}(\overline{E^{\prime}}).

A contradiction!

(2) For the remained case, we may as well assume that
\log_{p}x_{1}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),
but

\log_{p}x_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\text{log}_{p}(i_{2}(\varepsilon^{p-1})).

Let
\log_{p}x_{2}=b\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{2}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))\ (b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).
As discussed in case (1) above,

p\cdot\log_{p}x_{s}=d\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))(s=1,2).
Thus
d=pb,
so
p\cdot\log_{p}x_{1}=pb\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})).
We get

\log_{p}x_{1}=b\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})).
A contradiction!

This shows the above assertion, i.e.,
\log_{p}x_{s}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\text{log}_{p}(i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))\ (s=1,2,3).
As discussed above,
p\cdot\log_{p}x_{1}=b\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))
for some

0\neq b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}.
Let
b=p^{n}\cdot u,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},u\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}.
If
n>0,
then
p\cdot\log_{p}x_{1}=p\cdot(p^{n-1}\cdot u\cdot\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})))

which implies
\log_{p}x_{1}=p^{n-1}\cdot u\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1}))\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),
a contradiction!
If
n=0,
then
p\cdot\log_{p}x_{1}=u\text{log}_{p}(i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})),

so
i_{1}(\varepsilon^{p-1})=\text{exp}_{p}(u^{-1}\text{log}_{p}(x_{1}^{p}))=(\text{exp}_{p}(u^{-1}\text{log}_{p}(x_{1})))^{p}\in(U_{1}^{(1)})^{p}=U_{1}^{(2)}.

By similar discussion for
x_{2},x_{3}
above, we also get
i_{s}(\varepsilon^{p-1})\in U_{s}^{(2)}\ (s=2,3),
which implies
\varepsilon^{p-1}\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ p^{2}\mathcal{O}_{K}),
and the proof of Proposition.2.1
is completed.
\Box

Proposition 2.2.

Let
K
be a complex cubic number field,
p
an
odd prime number (when
p=3,
assume it is unramified in
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
). If there is
only one prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
over
p,
then

\text{tor}(\frac{U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}}{\overline{E^{\prime}}})=\{1\}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \varepsilon^{p^{f}-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}^{e+1}),\quad(2.2)

where
e=e(\mathfrak{p}/p),f=f(\mathfrak{p}/p)
are the ramification index and
residue degree of
\mathfrak{p}
in
K/{\mathbb{Q}},
respectively.

Proof.
It is easy to see that, as a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
submodule of
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)},\ \overline{E^{\prime}}
is generated by
i_{p}(\varepsilon^{p^{f}-1}).
Also

\frac{e(\mathfrak{p}/p)}{p-1}<1,
so
(U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)})^{p}=U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(e+1)}.
Since
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
torsion free,
similar to the discussion in the proof of Prop.2.1 above, we are done, and the proof is
completed.
\Box

Proposition 2.3.

Let
K
be a complex cubic number field,
p\geq 5
a
prime number. If
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
with prime

\mathfrak{p}_{1}\neq\mathfrak{p}_{2}

Proposition 2.4.

Let
K
be a complex cubic number field,
p\geq 3
a
prime number. If
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
with
residue degree
f(\mathfrak{p}_{1}/p)=2,f(\mathfrak{p}_{2}/p)=1
for prime
ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\neq\mathfrak{p}_{2}
in
K.
Then

\text{tor}(\frac{U_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{(1)}\times U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{(1)}}{\overline{E^{\prime}}})=\{1\}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \varepsilon^{p^{2}-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})\ \text{or}\ \varepsilon^{p-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2}).\quad(2.4)

Proof.
Similar to the proof of Prop.2.1 above, we can show that, as

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
modules,
\overline{E^{\prime}}
is generated by
i_{p}(\varepsilon^{p^{2}-1}),

both
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{(1)}
and
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{(1)}
are

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}-
torsion free, as well as

\text{tor}(\frac{U_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{(1)}\times U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{(1)}}{\overline{E^{\prime}}})=\{1\}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \varepsilon^{p^{2}-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})\ \text{or}\ \varepsilon^{p^{2}-1}\not\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2}).

Notice that for the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{2},
we have

\varepsilon^{p-1}\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2})\ \Leftrightarrow\ \varepsilon^{p^{2}-1}\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2}).

In fact, the necessity is obvious. Conversely,
\varepsilon^{p-1}=\varepsilon^{p^{2}-1}/(\varepsilon^{p-1})^{p}\in U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{(2)}

because
(\varepsilon^{p-1})^{p}\in(U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{(1)})^{p}=U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{(2)}.
The proof is completed.
\Box

Now we come to prove our Theorem 1.3.

4. Proof of Theorem 1.5

Proof of Theorem 1.5.
We have

K.

p

into
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}.
Then

By the general

\begin{array}[]{l}F_{n}=A\varepsilon^{n}+B\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon)^{n}+C\sigma_{3}(\varepsilon)^{n},\ \text{where}\\

\begin{array}[]{l}(\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon)-\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon))(\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon)-\sigma_{3}(\varepsilon))(\sigma_{3}(\varepsilon)-\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon))F_{n}\\
=(\sigma_{3}(\varepsilon)-\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon))(\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon)^{n}-\varepsilon^{n})-(\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon)-\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon))(\sigma_{3}(\varepsilon)^{n}-\varepsilon^{n}).\end{array}

Unramified case of Grothendieck–Serre for totally isotropic simply-connected group schemes
Roman Fedorov

Abstract
. We prove a case of the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture: let R R be a Noetherian semilocal flat algebra over a Dedekind domain such that all fibers of R R are geometrically regular; let 𝐆 \mathbf{G} be a simply-connected reductive R R -group scheme having a strictly proper parabolic subgroup scheme. Then a 𝐆 \mathbf{G} -torsor is trivial, provided that it is trivial over the total ring of fractions of R R . We also simplify the proof of the conjecture in the quasisplit unramified case. The argument is based on the notion of a unipotent chain of torsors that we introduce. We also prove that if R R is a Noetherian normal domain and 𝐆 \mathbf{G} is as above, then for any generically trivial torsor over an open subset U U of Spec R \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}R , there is a closed Z ⊂ Spec R Z\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}R of codimension at least two such the torsor trivializes over any affine open of U − Z U-Z .



1.
Introduction and Main results

1.1.

Let
R
be a regular local ring; let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive group scheme over
R
. A conjecture of Grothendieck and Serre (see
[
Ser
, remarque, p.31]
,
[
Gro1
, remarque 3, p.26-27]
, and
[
Gro2
, remarque 1.11.a]
) predicts that a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
R
is trivial, if it is trivial over the fraction field of
R
. Recently this has been proved in the case when
R
contains an infinite field in
[
FP
]
, it was extended to the case of finite fields in
[
Pan3
]
. In the mixed characteristic case the conjecture was previously known if
R
is unramified (that is, the fibers of the projection
\Spec R\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}
are regular) and
{\mathbf{G}}
is quasisplit, see
[
Čes1
]
. Assuming that the group scheme is simply-connected (so, necessarily semisimple), we generalize this result from the quasisplit to the totally isotropic case. We will also reprove the aforementioned result (see Remark
2.6
(
2.6
)). Also, we show that, for
{\mathbf{G}}
as above and any normal Noetherian domain
R
, every generically trivial
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor is “almost trivial” away from codimension two; see Section
1.2
.

Following
[
GS
]
, we make the following definition (cf. Remark
1.2
(
1.2
)).

Definition 1.1
.

A parabolic subgroup scheme
{\mathbf{P}}
of a reductive group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
is called
strictly proper,
if it intersects non-trivially every normal semisimple subgroup scheme of
{\mathbf{G}}
.

We prove the following theorem, which is a generalization of
[
Čes1
, Thm. 9.1]
in the simply-connected case.

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian semilocal flat
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra, where
{\mathcal{O}}
is a semilocal Dedekind domain. Assume that all fibers of
\Spec R\to\Spec{\mathcal{O}}
are geometrically regular. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simply-connected reductive
R
-group scheme having a strictly proper parabolic subgroup scheme. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
R
. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial over the total ring of fractions of
R
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial.

The theorem will be derived from Theorems
4
and
5
in Section
2
.

Remarks 1.2
.

(a) It is proved in
[
ČF
, Prop. 5.1]
that the unramified Grothendieck–Serre conjecture is true for a group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
, provided it is true for the simply-connected cover of its derived group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathrm{der}}
. Thus, the unramified Grothendieck–Serre conjecture is known for all totally isotropic group schemes
{\mathbf{G}}
. In fact, a completely different argument, not relying on a reduction to the simply-connected case, is given in
[
ČF
]
. It is, however, unlikely that Theorem
2
below can be proved in full generality by methods of
[
ČF
]
.

(b) In the situation of Theorem
1
,
R
is automatically regular. Indeed, Popescu’s Theorem
[
SP
, Tag07GC]
shows that
R
is a filtered colimit of smooth finitely generated
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebras, which are regular by
[
SP
, Tag07NF]
.

(c) Let
R
be local, then a reductive
R
-group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
has a strictly proper parabolic subgroup scheme if and only if it is totally isotropic in the terminology of
[
Čes2
]
(or strongly locally isotropic in the terminology of
[
Fed2
]
).

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a regular local ring. Recall that
R
is
unramified
if
R/pR
is regular, where
p
is the characteristic of the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
. It follows from Theorem
1
that the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture holds for simply-connected totally isotropic reductive group schemes over unramified regular local rings:

Corollary 1
.

Let
R
be an unramified regular local ring and
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simply-connected totally isotropic reductive
R
-group scheme. Then a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
R
is trivial, provided that it is trivial over the fraction field of
R
.

Proof.

If
R
contains a field, then it coincides with its fiber
R/pR
, and the statement follows from
[
FP
,
Pan3
]
. Otherwise,
R
is a flat
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra, where
{\mathcal{O}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p\mathbb{Z}}
. The special fiber of the projection
\Spec R\to\Spec{\mathcal{O}}
is equal to
R/pR
; by definition of unramifiedness it is regular, thus it is geometrically regular over
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
because
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is perfect. Since
R
is regular, the generic fiber of
\Spec R\to\Spec{\mathcal{O}}
is regular (and thus geometrically regular). Hence we can apply Theorem
1
.
∎

We note that, as follows from the above proof and Remark
1.2
(
1.2
), every unramified Noetherian local ring is regular.

1.2.
“Almost trivial” torsors

We now explain the main idea of our argument (and, in fact, of most proofs of the cases of the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture in mixed characteristic). Using Popescu’s Theorem, we may assume that
{\mathbf{G}}
and its generically trivial torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
are defined over a scheme
X
smooth and of finite type over a Dedekind domain
{\mathcal{O}}
. The
X
-torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
, being generically trivial, is trivial away from a closed codimension one subset
T\subset X
. In the case, when
{\mathcal{O}}
is a field (that is, in the equal characteristic case), this allows us to fiber
X
(after shrinking it) over an open subset of an affine space, so that
T
is finite over the base. As explained below in Section
1.3
(
1.3
), the main problem in mixed characteristic is that we “loose one dimension”, so we would need
T
to be of codimension two. This is, however, impossible: by Hartogs principle the trivialization over
X-T
would extend to
X
. However, we can find
T
of codimension two in
X
such that
{\mathcal{E}}
is “almost trivial” over
X-T
in the following sense:

Theorem 2
.

Let
R
be a normal Noetherian domain and
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simply-connected reductive
R
-group scheme having a strictly proper parabolic subgroup scheme. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a generically trivial
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over an open subscheme
U\subset\Spec R
. Then there is a closed subset
T\subset\Spec R
of codimension at least two such that for any morphism
\Spec A\to U-T
the pullback of
{\mathcal{E}}
to
\Spec A
is trivial.

We emphasize that this theorem has very modest assumptions on
R
: it is only required to be normal and Noetherian. The author does not expect this statement to hold beyond the simply-connected isotropic case (e.g., even for tori). We also emphasize that
{\mathcal{E}}
is not required to be defined over
\Spec R
but only over an open subset thereof.

1.3.
History and overview of the proof

Note that the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture was initially formulated for local rings but it has been generalized to semilocal rings; most of the papers cited deal with the semilocal case.

(a) The conjecture was proved in the case when the semilocal ring contains an infinite field in 2012 by Fedorov and Panin who completed the work of many people; see
[
FP
]
and the historical remarks therein. The conjecture was proved when the semilocal ring contains a finite field by Panin in 2014; see
[
Pan3
]
.

(b) We briefly explain the strategy of the proof in the equicharacteristic case. The first step is to use Popescu’s Theorem to reduce to the case when
R
is the semilocal ring of a finite set
{\mathbf{x}}
of closed points on an integral affine scheme
X
smooth and of finite type over a field
k
. By spreading out we may assume that the group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
and the
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
are defined over
X
. We may also assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial away from a proper closed subset
Y\subset X
.

(c) The second step is to fiber an open neighborhood
X^{\prime}\subset X
of
{\mathbf{x}}
over an open subset
S\subset\mathbb{A}^{\dim X-1}_{k}
in such a way that the fibers are smooth curves and
Y\cap X^{\prime}
is finite over the base. In fact, any generic projection does the job. Formally, this is accomplished by compactifying
X
and using Bertini’s Theorem. As we will see momentarily, this is where the situation becomes drastically different in the mixed characteristic case. We note that the finite field case became approachable after finite field Bertini’s Theorem became available (see
[
Poo
]
).

(d) The third step is to replace
X^{\prime}\to S
with
C:=X^{\prime}\times_{S}\Spec R\to\Spec R
and
{\mathcal{E}}
with
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}:=p_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
. The original torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
is recovered as
\Delta^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
, where
\Delta\colon\Spec R\to C
is the diagonal embedding. The data
(C\to\Spec R,\Delta,{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
can be improved until
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
can be descended onto
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}
.

(e) The last step is to show that
if
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is a torsor over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}
trivial away from an
R
-finite subscheme, then
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is trivial along any section of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}\to\Spec R
. We note that originally this statement was only available in the case when
{\mathbf{G}}
is simple and simply-connected, so one had to reduce to this case. One of the main results of
[
Fed2
]
shows that the statement is true for any reductive
{\mathbf{G}}
.

(f) Very little was known about the mixed characteristic case until 2015 (see
[
Fed3
]
and the historical remarks in loc. cit.) Here are the main ideas of loc. cit. Assume that
R
is unramified. Then the main difficulty is that the argument in (
1.3
) fails. For example, if
\dim X=2
, then the fibers of the projection
X\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}_{p\mathbb{Z}}
are already one-dimensional, so there is nothing to fiber. On the other hand,
Y
may not be finite over
\Spec\mathbb{Z}_{p\mathbb{Z}}
, which is crucial for the following. In general, we “loose” one dimension because the projection
X\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}_{p\mathbb{Z}}
cannot be deformed.

Here is the main idea of
[
Fed3
]
: let
{\mathbf{G}}
be quasisplit with a Borel subgroup scheme
{\mathbf{B}}
. The generic trivialization of
{\mathcal{E}}
induces a generic reduction to
{\mathbf{B}}
. Since
{\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{B}}
is projective, this reduction can be extended to a complement of a subset of codimension two; call it
Z
. Now we recover the lost dimension: there is a smooth morphism
X^{\prime}\to S
similar to the above such that
Z\cap X^{\prime}
is
S
-finite (for example, if
\dim X=2
, then
Z
is a finite scheme, so it is automatically finite over
S
). One shows that this can be performed in such a way that there is
Y\subset X^{\prime}
, also
S
-finite, such that
Y\supset Z\cap X^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime}-Y
is affine. One then shows that
{\mathcal{E}}
can be reduced to the unipotent radical
\Ru{\mathbf{B}}
of
{\mathbf{B}}
on
X^{\prime}-Y
, which shows that
{\mathcal{E}}_{X^{\prime}-Y}
is trivial (because
X^{\prime}-Y
is affine). The rest of the proof is very similar to the equal characteristic case.

To make the above ideas work, it is required in
[
Fed3
]
that
X
has a projective compactification satisfying some technical conditions. It is also required that
{\mathbf{G}}
is split and only the local (rather than semilocal) case is considered.

(g) In 2020 Česnavičius (see
[
Čes1
]
) was able to get rid of the assumptions on the compactification, as well as to generalize to quasisplit group schemes and to the semilocal rings. Also, the proof was streamlined in loc. cit., so we will generally follow it in this paper. One of the main ideas is that one need not choose
Y\subset X^{\prime}
as above at all. In fact, Česnavičius descends
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}
by showing that
\Ru{\mathbf{B}}
-torsors can be descended along certain affine morphisms.

(h) The first main idea of the current paper is to introduce a notion of a
unipotent chain
, see Section
2
. Roughly speaking, this is a sequence of torsors such that every torsor is obtained from the previous one via a unipotent modification. We prove (see Theorem
4
) that in the situation of Step (
1.3
), one can find a codimension two closed subset
T
of
X
such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{X-T}
is connected to the trivial torsor via a unipotent chain, provided that
{\mathbf{G}}
is simple, simply-connected, and isotropic. In this situation,
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial over any open affine subscheme of
X
disjoint from
T
; see Theorem
2
above and its proof.

Unfortunately, the above property of
{\mathcal{E}}
and
T
is not enough to perform the descent of Step (
1.3
). To make this step work, we introduce a notion of a trivial unipotent chain with respect to a pair of opposite parabolic subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{G}}
; the above unipotent chain over
X-T
is trivial away from a closed subset that is of positive codimension fiberwise over the Dedekind domain.

(i) Next, we prove the unramified case of the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture for any torsor that can be connected to a trivial torsor via a unipotent chain away from a subset of codimension two (there is also a local triviality condition for the chain). We follow the usual strategy. As in Step (
1.3
), following essentially
[
Fed3
]
and
[
Čes1
]
, one finds a smooth curve
C\to\Spec R
with a section
\Delta
, a torsor
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
over
C
such that
\Delta^{*}{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\approx{\mathcal{E}}
, and an
R
-finite closed subset
Z\subset C
such that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{C-Z}
is connected to the trivial torsor via a unipotent chain. Moreover, we may choose the data so that the unipotent chain is trivial away from a closed subscheme quasifinite over
R
.

(j) Initially,
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is a torsor over a
C
-group scheme. However, replacing
C
with an étale cover we reduce to the case when the group scheme is a pullback from
\Spec R
. This step, known as
equating group schemes
, requires some additional work in our case (see Section
4.2
). Since the notion of trivial unipotent chain depends on a pair of opposite parabolic subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{G}}
, we also need to make sure that this equating is compatible with parabolic subgroup schemes.

(k) Let the unipotent chain connecting
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{C-Z}
to the trivial torsor be trivial away from a closed
R
-quasifinite subset
Y\subset C
containing
Z
. After improving our data, we get an étale morphism
\varphi\colon C\to W\subset\mathbb{A}_{R}^{1}
such that
Y
maps isomorphically onto a closed subscheme
\varphi(Y)\subset W
such that the preimage of
\varphi(Y)
in
C
is equal to
Y
. We descend
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
to a torsor
{\mathcal{E}}_{W}
over
W
. The triviality of the chain over
C-Y
lets us descend the chain to a chain over
C-\varphi(Z)
. In particular,
{\mathcal{E}}_{W}
is trivial away from any closed subscheme
Y^{\prime}
containing
\varphi(Z)
and having affine complement. Such a subscheme can be chosen
R
-finite. By patching
{\mathcal{E}}_{W}
with the trivial torsor over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}-Y^{\prime}
, we obtain a torsor over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}
trivial away from an
R
-finite closed subscheme.

(l) The last step is to generalize Step (
1.3
) to the mixed characteristic case. This is accomplished by the following theorem of independent interest. We note that this theorem is closely related to the results of
[
PS
]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
U
be an affine semilocal scheme. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive group scheme over
U
. Assume that
Z
is a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}
finite over
U
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}
trivial over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}-Z
. Then for every section
\Delta\colon U\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}
of the projection
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\to U
the
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor
\Delta^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial.

This theorem will be proved in Section
5
. If
U
is a scheme over a field, then this is a slight generalization of
[
Fed2
, Thm. 4]
. The general case needs only minor modifications because most of the work is happening over the closed points of
U
anyways.

(m) We would like to mention some recent results concerning the mixed characteristic case of the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture: in
[
Pan1
]
the conjecture is proved for
\SL_{1}(D)
, where
D
is an Azumaya algebra over an unramified regular local ring. In
[
GL
]
the conjecture is proved in the unramified case, provided that
{\mathbf{G}}
is a constant group scheme. In
[
GP
]
the conjecture is proved when
R
is a semilocal ring of a scheme that is smooth and projective over a discrete valuation ring.

1.4.
Notations

For a group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
we denote by
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ad}
its adjoint group scheme, and by
\Ru{\mathbf{G}}
its unipotent radical. By a simple group scheme we always mean a simple reductive group scheme.

In this paper we work with right torsors; we only consider torsors for flat and finitely presented group schemes. If
{\mathbf{G}}
is a
T
-group scheme and
T^{\prime}\to T
is a morphism, we say “a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
T^{\prime}
” to mean a
{\mathbf{G}}_{T^{\prime}}
-torsor. If
T^{\prime\prime}\to T^{\prime}
is another morphism and
{\mathcal{E}}
is a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
T^{\prime}
, then
{\mathcal{E}}\times_{T^{\prime}}T^{\prime\prime}
is naturally a torsor over
T^{\prime\prime}
called “the pullback of
{\mathcal{E}}
”. Note that every section of a torsor gives a trivialization of this torsor. We say that a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
T^{\prime}
is
generically trivial
if its restriction to a dense open subscheme of
T^{\prime}
is trivial.

Notations as above, let
{\mathbf{H}}
be a subgroup scheme of
{\mathbf{G}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
be a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
T^{\prime}
. By an
{\mathbf{H}}
-reduction
of
{\mathcal{E}}
we mean a pair
({\mathcal{H}},\varphi)
, where
{\mathcal{H}}
is an
{\mathbf{H}}
-torsor and
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{H}}\times^{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
is an isomorphism of
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors. If such a reduction exists, we say that
{\mathcal{E}}

can be reduced to
{\mathbf{H}}
.

Finally, if
R
is a ring and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in R
, we denote by
V(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\subset\Spec R
the common zero locus of
f_{i}
.

2.
Chains of torsors

In this section, we define unipotent chains of torsors. The idea is that every torsor in the chain is obtained from the previous by “modifying along a unipotent subgroup scheme”.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a group scheme and
{\mathcal{E}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
be
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors. The scheme of
{\mathbf{G}}
-automorphisms of
{\mathcal{E}}
is a group scheme
\Aut({\mathcal{E}})
; such a group is called a strongly inner form of
{\mathbf{G}}
. The scheme of
{\mathbf{G}}
-isomorphisms
\Iso({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
is naturally an
\Aut({\mathcal{E}})
-torsor. It is also a left
\Aut({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
-torsor, though we can identify left and right torsors using the inversion in
{\mathbf{G}}
. Recall that
\Ru
stands for the unipotent radical.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a scheme;
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive
X
-group scheme.
A unipotent chain of
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors
is a sequence

(1)

{\mathcal{E}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{E}}_{n},{\mathbf{P}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{P}}_{n-1},\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1},

where
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
is a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor,
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}={\mathbf{G}}
is a trivial
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor,
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\subset\Aut({\mathcal{E}}_{i})
is a parabolic subgroup scheme, and
\tau_{i}
is a reduction of the
\Aut({\mathcal{E}}_{i})
-torsor
\Iso({\mathcal{E}}_{i},{\mathcal{E}}_{i+1})
to
\Ru{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
. We also say that the chain
connects
the trivial torsor to
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
.

Note an obvious lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

In the notation of the definition, let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphisms of schemes. Then
f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
, …,
f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
,
f^{*}{\mathbf{P}}_{1}
, …,
f^{*}{\mathbf{P}}_{n-1}
,
f^{*}\tau_{1}
, …,
f^{*}\tau_{n-1}
is a unipotent chain of
f^{*}{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors.

Lemma 2.3
.

In the notation of the definition, assume that
X
is affine. Then all the torsors
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
are trivial.

Proof.

Induction on
n
reduces the lemma to showing that
{\mathcal{E}}_{n-1}\approx{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
. Since
X
is affine, every
\Ru{\mathbf{P}}_{n-1}
-torsor is trivial by
[
SGA33
, exp. XXVI, cor. 2.2]
. Thus,
\Iso({\mathcal{E}}_{n-1},{\mathcal{E}}_{n})
is a trivial
\Aut({\mathcal{E}}_{n-1})
-torsor. Hence, it has a section, which is the sought-for isomorphism.
∎

We note that the trivialized
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor has a canonical reduction to any subgroup scheme of
{\mathbf{G}}
; we call this reduction
standard
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a scheme;
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive
X
-group scheme with opposite parabolic subgroup schemes
{\mathbf{P}}_{\pm}
. We say that a unipotent chain (
1
) is
trivial
if
{\mathbf{P}}_{1}={\mathbf{P}}_{\pm}\subset{\mathbf{G}}={\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and for
i=2,\ldots,n
there are trivializations of
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
identifying
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
either with
{\mathbf{P}}_{-}
or with
{\mathbf{P}}_{+}
and such that under these trivializations
\tau_{i}
become the standard reductions of
\Iso({\mathcal{E}}_{i-1},{\mathcal{E}}_{i})={\mathbf{G}}
to
\Ru{\mathbf{P}}_{i-1}=\Ru{\mathbf{P}}_{\pm}
.

Note that the notion of a unipotent chain does not depend on a choice of opposite parabolic subgroup schemes
{\mathbf{P}}_{\pm}
but the notion of a trivial chain does. The pullback of a trivial chain is trivial. Theorem
1
is a simple corollary of the following two theorems, Popescu’s approximation theorem, and the Dedekind (that is, 1-dimensional) case.

Theorem 4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian normal domain;
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simply-connected simple
R
-group scheme with opposite proper parabolic subgroup schemes
{\mathbf{P}}_{\pm}\subset{\mathbf{G}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}
be a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over an open subscheme
U\subset\Spec R
trivial away from
V(f)\cap U
, where
f\in R-\{0\}
. Then there is a closed subset
T\subset V(f)
of codimension at least two in
\Spec R
and a unipotent chain of
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors over
\Spec R-T
:

Theorem 5
.

Let

Derivation of Theorem
1
from Theorems
4
and
5
.

Since

is regular,

is the disjoint union of its irreducible components, so we may assume that
R
is integral.
Assume first that
{\mathbf{G}}
is a simple group scheme. By Popescu’s Theorem (
[
SP
, Tago07GC]
) we may assume that
R
is a semilocal ring of a finite set
{\mathbf{x}}
of points on an integral affine scheme
X
smooth and of finite type over a semilocal Dedekind domain. By
[
Guo
]
, we may assume that
X
is of positive dimension over this Dedekind domain. Spreading out and replacing
X
with an appropriate affine neighborhood of
{\mathbf{x}}
, we may assume that
{\mathbf{G}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
are defined over
X
and that
{\mathbf{G}}
has a proper parabolic subgroup scheme
{\mathbf{P}}_{+}
. Let
{\mathbf{P}}_{-}
be an opposite parabolic subgroup scheme (existing because
X
is affine).

Applying again
[
Guo
]
to the semilocalizaton of
X
at the generic points of special fibers and spreading out, we find a closed subscheme
Y\subset X
fiberwise of positive codimension over the Dedekind domain such that
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial away from
Y
. Moreover, we may assume that
Y=V(f)
for

.

Applying Theorem
4
with
R=\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
,
U=X
, we get a closed subset
T\subset Y
of codimension at least two in
X
and a unipotent chain of
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors over
X-T
connecting
{\mathbf{G}}_{X-T}
to
{\mathcal{E}}_{X-T}
. Moreover, the chain is trivial over
X-Y
. It remains to apply Theorem
5
.

Consider now the general case. Recall that a semisimple simply-connected group scheme can be written as the product of Weil restrictions of simple group schemes along finite connected étale covers (see
[
SGA33
, exp. XXIV, prop. 5.10]
). Write
{\mathbf{G}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbf{G}}_{i}
, where
{\mathbf{G}}_{i}
is the Weil restriction of a simple
R_{i}
-group scheme
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
, where the integral domain
R_{i}
is a finite étale
R
-algebra. We need a lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

The simply-connected group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
has a strictly proper parabolic subgroup scheme if and only if each
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
has a proper parabolic subgroup scheme.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the scheme of parabolic subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{G}}
, let
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
be the scheme of parabolic subgroup schemes of
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
(cf.
[
SGA33
, exp. XXVI, cor. 3.5]
). Since a parabolic subgroup scheme of a reductive group scheme gives rise to a parabolic subgroup scheme of its Weil restriction, we get a morphism
\prod_{i}\Res_{R_{i}/R}{\mathcal{P}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{P}}
, where
\Res_{R_{i}/R}
is the Weil restriction functor. Let us check that this morphism is an isomorphism. Since this is enough to check after an étale base change, we may assume that for all
i
we have
R_{i}=R
and that each
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
is split. Then the statement reduces to the statement that the parabolic subgroup schemes of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
are exactly subgroup schemes of the form
{\mathbf{P}}_{1}\times\ldots\times{\mathbf{P}}_{r}
, where each
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
is a parabolic subgroup scheme of
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
. Choosing a split maximal torus and a Borel subgroup scheme in
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
, we get a split maximal torus and a Borel subgroup scheme in
\prod_{i}\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
. Then we have a notion of standard parabolic subgroup schemes, and it is clear that every standard parabolic subgroup scheme in
\prod_{i}\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
is the product of standard parabolic subgroup schemes in
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
. It remains to note that every parabolic subgroup scheme is locally conjugate to a standard one.

We see that parabolic subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{G}}
correspond to collections
({\mathbf{P}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{P}}_{r})
, where
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\subset\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
is a parabolic subgroup scheme. It is clear that strictly proper parabolic subgroup schemes correspond to collections of proper parabolic subgroup schemes.
∎

By
[
SGA33
, exp. XXIV, prop. 8.4]
,
{\mathcal{E}}
corresponds to a sequence
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
, …,
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
of
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
-torsors. These torsors are generically trivial. By the previous lemma, each
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}}_{i}
contains a proper parabolic subgroup scheme. Thus, by the already proved case,
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
is trivial, so
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial as well. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

from Theorems

As in the proof above, using Lemma

, we may assume that
{\mathbf{G}}
is simple, simply-connected, and isotropic. By Theorem

3.1.
Gluing and reductions of torsors

Let
X
be a scheme and fix a Zariski cover
X=W_{1}\cup W_{2}
. Let
{\mathbf{H}}
be a flat and finitely presented
X
-group scheme and let
g\in{\mathbf{H}}(W_{1}\cap W_{2})
. Then we can use

to glue the trivial
{\mathbf{H}}
-torsor over
W_{1}
with the trivial
{\mathbf{H}}
-torsor over
W_{2}
. We obtain an
{\mathbf{H}}
-torsor
\Glue({\mathbf{H}},g)
over

. More precisely, the torsor

has canonical trivializations
s_{1}
over
W_{1}
and
s_{2}
over
W_{2}
such that
s_{2}|_{W_{1}\cap W_{2}}=s_{1}|_{W_{1}\cap W_{2}}g
. Being trivialized over
W_{1}
,

has a canonical reduction to any subgroup scheme of
{\mathbf{H}}
over
W_{1}
. We recall that such a reduction is called standard.

Lemma 3.1
.

Notations as above, assume that
{\mathbf{U}}\subset{\mathbf{H}}
is a subgroup scheme and
u\in{\mathbf{U}}(W_{1}\cap W_{2})
.

(i) The
{\mathbf{H}}
-torsor
\Glue({\mathbf{H}},u)
has a
{\mathbf{U}}
-reduction that extends the standard reduction over
W_{1}
.

(ii) Assume that
g\in{\mathbf{H}}(W_{1}\cap W_{2})
and set
{\mathcal{F}}:=\Glue({\mathbf{H}},g)
,
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}:=\Glue({\mathbf{H}},ug)
. We use the trivialization of
{\mathcal{F}}
over
W_{1}
to identify the restrictions of
{\mathbf{H}}
and
\Aut({\mathcal{F}})
to
W_{1}
. Assume that there is a subgroup scheme
{\mathbf{U}}^{\prime}\subset\Aut({\mathcal{F}})
such that under the above identification we have
{\mathbf{U}}_{W_{1}}={\mathbf{U}}^{\prime}_{W_{1}}
. Then the
\Aut({\mathcal{F}})
-torsor
\Iso({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
has a
{\mathbf{U}}^{\prime}
-reduction that extends the standard reduction over
W_{1}
.

Proof.

(i) Consider the
{\mathbf{U}}
-torsor
\Glue({\mathbf{U}},u)
. It follows from the definitions that
\Glue({\mathbf{U}},u)\times^{\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{H}}\simeq\Glue({\mathbf{H}},u)
.

(ii) Let
s_{i}
be the canonical trivialization of
{\mathcal{F}}
over
W_{i}
, while
s^{\prime}_{i}
be the canonical trivialization of
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
over
W_{i}
. These trivializations give sections (=trivializations)

of
\Iso({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
over
W_{i}
sending
s_{i}
to
s^{\prime}_{i}
. Then, on

, we have
t_{2}(s_{1})=t_{1}(s_{1})u
.

Next, on
W_{1}\cap W_{2}
, we use the trivializations
s_{1}
and
t_{1}
to identify
\Aut({\mathcal{F}})
and
\Iso({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
with
{\mathbf{G}}
. Then the action of
\Aut({\mathcal{F}})
on
\Iso({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
is identified with the action of
{\mathbf{G}}
on itself via the multiplication on the right. Thus we have on
W_{1}\cap W_{2}
:
t_{2}=t_{1}u
. Therefore,
\Iso({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})=\Glue(\Aut({\mathcal{F}}),u)
. It remains to apply part (i) with
{\mathbf{U}}^{\prime}\subset\Aut({\mathcal{F}})
instead of
{\mathbf{U}}\subset{\mathbf{H}}
.
∎

3.2.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R
be a normal domain and

. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{m}\subset R
be distinct minimal prime ideals containing

be the completion of the discrete valuation ring

and let

be the fraction field of

. Let

be an

-module of finite rank. Assume that for all

. Then for all

there is

and

such that for all

we have

and

, where we view

as an element of

via the composition

Proof.

For

we have

for all

. Consider the semilocal Dedekind domain

and the module

such that for all

we have

, where

is the valuation of

in
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}}
. Write

, where
x\in M
and
h\notin{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for all
j
. It remains to take

.
∎

We will keep the notation
R
,
f
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
,
\overline{{\mathcal{O}}}_{j}
and
\overline{K}_{j}
through the end of the section. For an

-group scheme

, we denote by

the

{\mathbf{H}}(\overline{{\mathcal{O}}}_{j})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
,
f
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
,
\overline{{\mathcal{O}}}_{j}
and
\overline{K}_{j}
be as above, let
{\mathbf{U}}
be an affine

-group scheme with a filtration
{\mathbf{U}}={\mathbf{U}}_{n}\supset{\mathbf{U}}_{n-1}\supset\ldots\supset{\mathbf{U}}_{0}=\{e\}
such that the successive quotients
{\mathbf{U}}_{i}/{\mathbf{U}}_{i-1}
are isomorphic to additive group schemes of locally free
R
-modules of finite rank. Let
u_{j}\in{\mathbf{U}}(\overline{K}_{j})
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. Then for
N>0
there is
h\in R
and
u\in{\mathbf{U}}(R_{fh})
such that for all
j
we have
h\notin{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
and
u_{j}\in{\mathbf{U}}^{(N)}_{j}u
.

Proof.

Let

be an integer such that

. Assume that for all

N>0
we can find

and

such that for all

we have

We induct on

. Note that

for all

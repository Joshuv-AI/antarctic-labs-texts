Abstract
. In this paper we establish the L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} estimates for global pseudo-differential operators on graded Lie groups. We provide both necessary and sufficient conditions for the L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} boundedness of pseudo-differential operators associated with the global Hörmander symbol classes on graded Lie groups, within the range 1 < p ≤ 2 ≤ q < ∞ 1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty . Additionally, we present a sufficient condition for the L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} estimates of pseudo-differential operators within the range 1 < p ≤ q ≤ 2 1<p\leq q\leq 2 or 2 ≤ p ≤ q < ∞ 2\leq p\leq q<\infty . The proofs rely on estimates of the Riesz and Bessel potentials associated with Rockland operators, along with previously established results on L p L^{p} -boundedness of global pseudo-differential operators on graded Lie groups. Notably, as a byproduct, we also establish the sharpness of the Sobolev embedding theorem for the inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces on graded Lie groups.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Outline

The boundedness of pseudo-differential operators is a fundamental problem in harmonic analysis that arises due to their diverse applications in harmonic analysis and partial differential equations
[
25
,
44
]
. Several contributions have been made in the Euclidean space setting towards establishing the
L^{p}
-boundedness of pseudo-differential operators associated with the Hörmander symbol class
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

[
6
,
18
,
44
]
. For non-commutative groups, recent papers
[
9
,
13
]
have investigated the
L^{p}
-boundedness of pseudo-differential calculus associated with global Hörmander classes on compact Lie groups and graded Lie groups. Furthermore, Hörmander
[
26
]
established the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of pseudo-differential operators associated with the symbol class
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the range
1<p\leq q<\infty
(see also
[
5
]
). It is worth noting that Hörmander provided a sufficient condition for the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of Fourier multipliers in his seminal paper
[
27
]
for the range
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty
, and this result has recently been extended to various settings such as compact homogeneous manifolds
[
1
,
3
]
, graded Lie groups
[
32
]
, general locally compact unimodular groups
[
4
,
36
]
, quantum groups
[
2
]
, and for the generalised Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and hypergroups
[
29
,
30
]
, as well as for eigenfunction expansions of certain operators
[
12
,
11
]
. However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, no attempts have been made to establish the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of pseudo-differential operators on noncommutative Lie groups. The primary objective of this article is to fill this gap and make progress towards the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of pseudo-differential operators for the range
1<p,q<\infty
associated with the global Hörmander symbol classes
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
on graded Lie groups
G
with its unitary dual
\widehat{G},
introduced by V. Fischer and the third author in the monograph
[
16
]
.

Graded Lie groups, which include significant examples like the Heisenberg group, Heisenberg-type groups, and stratified Lie groups, are a notable class within the realm of homogeneous nilpotent Lie groups
[
21
,
16
]
. What sets them apart from other nilpotent Lie groups is the existence of hypoelliptic left-invariant homogeneous partial differential operators known as Rockland operators
[
33
,
45
]
. The Helffer and Nourrigat solution to the Rockland conjecture sheds light on this distinguishing characteristic
[
22
]
. These groups have significant applications in analysis, representation theory, and geometry
[
23
]
. A visionary program for studying analysis and PDEs on graded Lie groups was presented by Stein in his 1970 lecture at the ICM in Nice
[
42
]
. In the influential paper by Rothschild and Stein
[
34
]
, it was demonstrated how one can learn a great deal about
certain differential operators on manifolds, by approximation using operators on certain
homogeneous nilpotent groups
[
20
,
35
]
. Inspired by the setup of global pseudo-differential operators on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
suggested by M. Taylor
[
43
]
, V. Fischer and the third author
[
16
]
developed a Kohn-Nirenberg type calculus on general graded Lie groups for a suitably defined operator-valued global Hörmander symbols classes
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
on a graded Lie group
G
. Recently, this calculus was extended by Federico, Rottensteiner, and the third author
[
14
]
using the so-called
\tau
-quantisation on graded Lie groups, which, in particular, gives rise to Weyl-type pseudo-differential calculus on graded Lie groups. This construction is based on the general setup of pseudo-differential operators on unimodular type-I locally compact groups
[
31
]
. The
L^{p}
-boundedness of pseudo-differential operators associated with operator-valued Hörmander symbol class
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
was established in
[
9
]
and its validity for
\tau
-quantisations was discussed in
[
14
]
. We also refer to
[
10
]
for further developments and applications to PDEs.

1.2.
Main results

To state our main results, some notation, and basic knowledge should be in order. We refer to Section
2
for a detailed exposition. Let
G
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q.

We denote the positive Rockland operator on
G
by
\mathcal{R},
that is, a positive left-invariant hypoelliptic differential operator which is homogeneous. The existence of such an operator on
G
is assured by the resolution of the Rockland conjecture by Helffer and Nourrigat
[
22
]
. Some examples of the Rockland operators in particular situations are as follows:

•

For the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}:
the positive sub-Laplacian
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})
and its powers.

•

For a stratified Lie group
G:
let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
be a basis of the first stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of the stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G,
then the operator

\mathcal{R}:=(-1)^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}X_{i}^{2k},\,\,c_{i}>0

is an example of a Rockland operator for any
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Note that the case
k=1
corresponds to Hörmander type sub-Laplacians.

•

In a more general setup, that is, on a graded Lie group
G
with a basis
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
satisfying
D_{r}X_{i}=r^{\nu_{i}}X_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,n,\,r>0,
with the dilation weights
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n},
denoting by
\nu_{0}
any common multiple of
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n},
the operator

\mathcal{R}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{i}}}c_{i}X^{2\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{i}}},\,c_{i}>0

is a Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
2\nu_{0}
on the graded Lie group
G.

There have been several studies dealing with function spaces such as homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces on graded Lie groups associated with Rockland operators. We refer to
[
17
,
16
,
8
]
and references therein for more detail. In particular, a sufficient condition for the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of the Riesz operator
I_{a}:=\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}
with
0<a<Q
is given in
[
16
]
.

The following theorem presents the result that establishes a sufficient condition and in some cases a necessary condition, for the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of pseudo-differential operators on graded Lie groups associated with the global Hörmander symbol classes
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).
We denote the class of operators associated with
\textnormal{Op}({S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}))
by
\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty.
Let
G
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
and let
0\leq\delta\leq\rho\leq 1,

\delta\neq 1.
Then, the following statements hold.

•

Let
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty.
Every pseudo-differential operator
A\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
admits a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G),
that is

\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(G),\,\|Af\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\,\,\,

(1.1)

holds, if and only if,

m\leq-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).

(1.2)

•

Every pseudo-differential operator
A\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
admits a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G),
that is (
1.1
) holds, in the following cases:

(i)

if
1<p\leq q\leq 2
and

m\leq-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

(1.3)

(ii)

if
2\leq p\leq q<\infty
and

m\leq-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right).

(1.4)

Remark 1.2
.

The order conditions in (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) can be written in a simplified way for
1<p,q<\infty
as follows:

m\leq-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2},0\right\}\right),

(1.5)

where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of the group
G
.

Remark 1.3
.

If
G=\mathbb{R}^{n},
the order condition in (
1.5
) is sharp for Fourier multipliers, see Hörmander
[
26
, Page 163]
.

Remark 1.4
.

For the proof of the necessary and sufficient condition in (
1.2
), an essential tool is the sharpness of the Sobolev embedding theorem on graded Lie groups. More precisely, in Lemma
3.2
we prove that for
1<p,q<\infty,
and for a Rokland operator
\mathcal{R}
of homogeneous degree
\nu>0
on a graded Lie group
G,
the Bessel operator

B_{a}=(1+\mathcal{R})^{-\frac{a}{\nu}},

admits a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G),
that is, the estimate

\|B_{a}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,\,\,

(1.6)

holds, if and only if,
1<p<q<\infty
and

a\geq Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).

(1.7)

That (
1.7
) is a sufficient condition for the boundedness of
B_{a}
was proved in
[
16
]
. Our contribution in Lemma
3.2
is then the proof of the
reverse
statement.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by reviewing some basic definitions and results from
the theory of pseudo-differential operators on graded Lie groups as developed in
[
16
]
used in our analysis of
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
-bounds in the setting of graded Lie groups. We introduce the necessary tools on the subject in the next subsections.

2.1.
Hörmander classes on graded Lie groups

For the facts below we follow
[
16
]
.

Let
G
be a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}.
We always assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a real vector space of finite dimension. In this case, the exponential map

\textnormal{exp}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow G

is a global diffeomorphism that allows us to make the identification
G\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The corresponding mapping
(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
is a polynomial mapping and the Haar measure on
G
is induced by the exponential mapping from the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n},
where
n=\dim(G)
is the topological dimension of
G.

A
family of dilations
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a family of endomorphisms
D_{r}\in\textnormal{End}(\mathfrak{g}),

r>0,
satisfying the following properties:

•

\forall r>0,

D_{r}
is an algebra automorphism, i.e. if
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g},
then, for any
r>0,
we have that

D_{r}(X+Y)=D_{r}X+D_{r}(Y),

and

D_{r}[X,Y]=[D_{r}X,D_{r}Y],

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}.

•

There exists a diagonalisable linear operator
A:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that

D_{r}X=e^{\ln(r)A}X,\,\,\forall X\in\mathfrak{g}.

Such
A
is called the
dilation matrix
of the group.

A
homogeneous group
is a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G

such that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is endowed with a family of dilations
\{D_{r}\}_{r>0}.
In this case the mappings

\textnormal{exp}\circ D_{r}\circ\textnormal{exp}^{-1}:G\rightarrow G

are group automorphisms on
G
that we also denote by
D_{r}.
Moreover, if

\textnormal{Spectrum}(A)=\{\nu_{1},\cdots\nu_{n}\}

is the set of eigenvalues of the dilation matrix
A
, the number

Q=\textnormal{Tr}(A):=\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}

is called the
homogeneous dimension of

G.

Let
\pi
be a continuous, unitary and irreducible representation of
G
on a separable Hilbert space
H_{\pi},
this means that,

1.

\pi\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(H_{\pi})),
i.e.
\pi(gg^{\prime})=\pi(g)\pi(g^{\prime})
and for the adjoint of
\pi(g),

\pi(g)^{*}=\pi(g^{-1}),
for every
g,g^{\prime}\in G.

2.

The function
(g,v)\mapsto\pi(g)v,
from
G\times H_{\pi}
into
H_{\pi}
is a continuous mapping.

3.

For every
g\in G,
and for a vector subspace
W_{\pi}\subset H_{\pi},
if
\pi(g)W_{\pi}\subset W_{\pi},
then
W_{\pi}=H_{\pi}
or
W_{\pi}=\{0\}.

Let
\textnormal{Rep}(G)
be the family of unitary, continuous and irreducible representations of
G.
The relation,

\pi_{1}\sim\pi_{2}\Longleftrightarrow\exists B\in\mathscr{B}(H_{\pi_{1}},H_{\pi_{2}}):\,\forall g\in G,\,B\pi_{1}(g)B^{-1}=\pi_{2}(g)

is an equivalence relation and the unitary dual of
G,
denoted by
\widehat{G}
is defined via

\widehat{G}:={\textnormal{Rep}(G)}/{\sim}.
We always identify any representation with its equivalence class
\pi\simeq[\pi].
One reason to do this is that in the setting of nilpotent Lie groups the unitary dual is a continuous set.

We are going to denote by
d\pi
the
Plancherel measure
on
\widehat{G}.

The group Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}_{G}(f):=\widehat{f}
of
f\in\mathscr{S}(G)\cong\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
at
\pi\in\widehat{G},
is defined by

\widehat{f}(\pi)=\int\limits_{G}f(g)\pi(g)^{*}dg:H_{\pi}\rightarrow H_{\pi},\textnormal{ and }\mathscr{F}_{G}:\mathscr{S}(G)\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G}):=\mathscr{F}_{G}(\mathscr{S}(G)).

Under the identification,
\pi\simeq[\pi]=\{\pi^{\prime}:\pi\sim\pi^{\prime}\}
, the Kirillov trace character
\Theta_{\pi}
defined by

f\in\mathscr{S}(G)\mapsto(\Theta_{\pi},f):=\textnormal{ Tr }(\widehat{f}(\pi)),

is a tempered distribution on
G.
In particular, the identity

f(e_{G})=\int\limits_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{ Tr }(\widehat{f}(\pi))d\pi,

implies the
Fourier inversion formula

f=\mathscr{F}_{G}^{-1}(\widehat{f}),
where

(\mathscr{F}_{G}^{-1}\sigma)(g):=\int\limits_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{ Tr }[\pi(g)\sigma(\pi)]d\pi,\,\,g\in G,\,\,\,\,\mathscr{F}_{G}^{-1}:\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(G),

is the
inverse Fourier transform
. We recall that the Plancherel theorem takes the form

\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})},

where we have denoted by

L^{2}(\widehat{G}):=\int\limits_{\widehat{G}}H_{\pi}\otimes H_{\pi}^{*}d\pi,

the Hilbert space endowed with the norm:

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}=\left(\int_{\widehat{G}}\|\sigma(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}d\pi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

An important class of operators that interact with the dilations on the group
G
are
homogeneous operators
. We recall that a linear operator
T:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(G)
is
homogeneous
of degree
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
if for every
r>0
the identity

T(f\circ D_{r})=r^{\nu}(Tf)\circ D_{r}

holds for every
f\in\mathscr{D}(G).

For every unitary representation
\pi\in\widehat{G},
acting as
\pi:G\rightarrow U({H}_{\pi}),
on a representation space
{H}_{\pi},
we denote by
{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}
the set of
smooth vectors
on
{H}_{\pi},
that consists of all elements
v\in{H}_{\pi}
such that the function
x\mapsto\pi(x)v,

\pi\in\widehat{G},
is smooth.

A
Rockland operator
is a positive left-invariant differential operator
\mathcal{R}
which is homogeneous of positive degree
\nu=\nu_{\mathcal{R}}
and such that, for every unitary irreducible non-trivial representation
\pi\in\widehat{G},

\pi(\mathcal{R})
is an injective operator on the space of smooth vectors
{H}_{\pi}^{\infty};
Here,
\sigma_{\mathcal{R}}(\pi)=\pi(\mathcal{R})
is the symbol associated to
\mathcal{R}.
It coincides with the infinitesimal representation of
\mathcal{R}
as an element of the universal enveloping algebra.

It can be shown that a Lie group
G
is graded if and only if there exists a differential Rockland operator on
G.

Remark 2.1
.

If the Rockland operator

\mathcal{R}:=\sum_{[\alpha]=\nu}a_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}

is formally self-adjoint, then
\mathcal{R}
and

\pi(\mathcal{R})=\sum_{[\alpha]=\nu}a_{\alpha}\pi(X^{\alpha})

admit self-adjoint extensions on
L^{2}(G)
and on
{H}_{\pi},
respectively, for a.e.
\pi\in\widehat{G}
. Now if we use the same notation for their (unbounded) self-adjoint
extensions and we denote by
E
and
E_{\pi}
their respective spectral measures, we will denote by

\phi(\mathcal{R})=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(\lambda)dE(\lambda),\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,\pi(\phi(\mathcal{R}))\equiv\phi(\pi(\mathcal{R}))=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(\lambda)dE_{\pi}(\lambda),

the measurable functions defined by the spectral functional calculus associated with
\mathcal{R}
and
\pi(\mathcal{R}),
respectively.
In general, we will reserve the notation
{E_{A}(\lambda)}_{0\leq\lambda<\infty}
for the spectral measure associated with a
positive
and self-adjoint operator
A
on a Hilbert space
H.

For any multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},
and for an arbitrary family
\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\},
of left-invariant vector-fields compatible with respect to the graded structure of the Lie algebra we will use the notation

[\alpha]:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{j}\alpha_{j},

(2.1)

for the homogeneous degree of the operator
X^{\alpha}:=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

In order to present a consistent definition of pseudo-differential operators, namely, a quantisation formula, as developed in
[
16
]
(see (
2.5
)), we need to define a suitable class of Sobolev spaces on the unitary dual
\widehat{G}
acting on the set of smooth vectors
H_{\pi}^{\infty},
for every representation space
H_{\pi}.
To record this notion, let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator on
G
of homogeneous degree
\nu>0.

Definition 2.2

(Sobolev spaces on
H_{\pi}^{\infty}
)
.

Let
\pi_{1}\in\textnormal{Rep}(G),
and let
a\in\mathbb{R}.
We denote by
H_{\pi_{1}}^{a},
the Hilbert space obtained by the completion of
H_{\pi_{1}}^{\infty}
with respect to the norm

\|v\|_{H_{\pi_{1}}^{a}}=\|\pi_{1}(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}v\|_{H_{\pi_{1}}}.

In order to introduce the general notion of a symbol, that in our contexts are operators acting on Hilbert spaces (the corresponding representation spaces), as the one developed in
[
16
]
, we require the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

A
\widehat{G}
-field of operators

\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi):\pi\in\widehat{G}\}

defined on the class of smooth vectors is defined on some Sobolev space
{H}_{\pi}^{a}
when for each unitary representation
\pi_{1}\in\textnormal{Rep}(G),
such that
\pi_{1}\in\pi\in\widehat{G},
the operator
\sigma(\pi_{1})
is bounded from
H^{a}_{\pi_{1}}
to
H_{\pi_{1}}
in the sense that

\sup_{\|v\|_{H_{\pi_{1}}^{a}}=1}\|\sigma(\pi_{1})v\|_{H_{\pi}}<\infty,

holds, and satisfying for every two elements
\sigma(\pi_{1})
and
\sigma(\pi_{2})
in
\sigma(\pi)
the property

\textnormal{If }\pi_{1}\sim\pi_{2}\textnormal{ then }\sigma(\pi_{1})\sim\sigma(\pi_{2}).

We recall that the
inhomogenous Sobolev space

L^{p}_{a}(G)
is defined by the completion of
\mathscr{D}(G)
with respect to the norm (see
[
16
, Chapter 4]
)

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{a}(G)}=\|(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}f\|_{L^{p}(G)},

(2.2)

for
a\in\mathbb{R},
and for any
0<p<\infty.
On the other hand, the
homogenous Sobolev space

\dot{L}^{p}_{a}(G)
is defined by the completion of
\mathscr{D}(G)\cap\textnormal{Dom}(\mathcal{R}^{\frac{a}{\nu}}),
with respect to the norm (see
[
16
, Chapter 4]
)

\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{a}(G)}=\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{a}{\nu}}f\|_{L^{p}(G)},

(2.3)

for
a\in\mathbb{R},
and for any
0<p<\infty.

Definition 2.4
.

A
\widehat{G}
-field of operators defined on smooth vectors with range in the Sobolev space
H_{\pi}^{a}
is a family of classes of operators
\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi):\pi\in\widehat{G}\}
where

\sigma(\pi):=\{\sigma(\pi_{1}):H^{\infty}_{\pi_{1}}\rightarrow H_{\pi}^{a},\,\,\pi_{1}\in\pi\},

for every
\pi\in\widehat{G}
viewed as a subset of
\textnormal{Rep}(G),
satisfying for every two elements
\sigma(\pi_{1})
and
\sigma(\pi_{2})
in
\sigma(\pi):

\textnormal{If }\pi_{1}\sim\pi_{2}\textnormal{ then }\sigma(\pi_{1})\sim\sigma(\pi_{2}).

The following notion will be useful in order to use the general theory of non-commutative integration.

Definition 2.5
.

A
\widehat{G}
-field of operators defined on smooth vectors with range in the Sobolev space
H_{\pi}^{a}
is measurable when for some (and hence for any)
\pi_{1}\in\pi
and any vector
v_{\pi_{1}}\in H_{\pi_{1}}^{\infty},
as
\pi\in\widehat{G},
the resulting field
\{\sigma(\pi)v_{\pi}:\pi\in\widehat{G}\},

is
d\pi
-measurable and

\int\limits_{\widehat{G}}\|v_{\pi}\|^{2}_{H_{\pi}^{a}}d\pi=\int\limits_{\widehat{G}}\|\pi(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}v_{\pi}\|^{2}_{H_{\pi}}d\pi<\infty.

Remark 2.6
.

We always assume that a
\widehat{G}
-field of operators defined on smooth vectors with range in the Sobolev space
H_{\pi}^{a}
is
d\pi
-measurable.

The
\widehat{G}
-fields of operators associated to Rockland operators can be defined as follows.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
L^{2}_{a}(\widehat{G})
denote the space of measurable fields of operators
\sigma
with range in
H_{\pi}^{a},
that is,

\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi):H_{\pi}^{\infty}\rightarrow H_{\pi}^{a}\},\textnormal{ with }\{\pi(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}\sigma(\pi):\pi\in\widehat{G}\}\in L^{2}(\widehat{G}),

for one (and hence for any) Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu.
We also denote

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{a}(\widehat{G})}:=\|\pi(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}\sigma(\pi)\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}.

By using the notation above, we will introduce a family of function spaces required to define
\widehat{G}
-fields of operators (that will be used to define the symbol of a pseudo-differential operator, see Definition
2.9
).

Definition 2.8

(The spaces
\mathscr{L}_{L}(L^{2}_{a}(G),L^{2}_{b}(G)),

\mathcal{K}_{a,b}(G)
and
L^{\infty}_{a,b}(\widehat{G})
)
.

•

The space
\mathscr{L}_{L}(L^{2}_{a}(G),L^{2}_{b}(G))
consists of all left-invariant linear operators

such that

extends to a bounded operator.

•

The space

is the family of all right convolution kernels of elements in

i.e.

if and only if

•

We also define the space

of symbols by the following condition:
\sigma\in L^{\infty}_{a,b}(\widehat{G})
if

\|\pi(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{b}{\nu}}\sigma(\pi)\pi(1+\mathcal{R})^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})}:=\sup_{\pi\in\widehat{G}}\|\pi(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{b}{\nu}}\sigma(\pi)\pi(1+\mathcal{R})^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}\|_{\mathscr{B}(H_{\pi})}<\infty.

In this case

extends to a bounded operator with

\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}_{a,b}(\widehat{G})}=\|T_{\sigma}\|_{\mathscr{L}(L^{2}_{a}(G),L^{2}_{b}(G))},

and note that
\sigma\in L^{\infty}_{a,b}(\widehat{G})
if and only if

Definition 2.9

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\pi^{\prime}(D_{r}(z)))\phi_{0}(2^{-j}((r\cdot\pi^{\prime})(\mathcal{R}))^{1/\nu})\widehat{f}(\pi^{\prime})]d\pi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\pi^{\prime}(D_{r}(z)))\phi_{0}(2^{-j}r\pi^{\prime}(\mathcal{R})^{1/\nu})\widehat{f}(\pi^{\prime})]d\pi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\pi^{\prime}(D_{r}(z)))\phi_{0}(2^{-j-M}\pi^{\prime}(\mathcal{R})^{1/\nu})\widehat{f}(\pi^{\prime})]d\pi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(\psi_{j+M}(\mathcal{R}^{\frac{1}{\nu}})f)\circ D_{r}(z).

In consequence

\displaystyle[E(\lambda,\infty)(f\circ D_{r})](x)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\geq N}\phi_{j}(\mathcal{R}^{1/\nu})(f\circ D_{r})\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\geq N}(\phi_{j+M}(\mathcal{R}^{\frac{1}{\nu}})f)\circ D_{r}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\geq N+M}(\phi_{j}(\mathcal{R}^{\frac{1}{\nu}})f)\circ D_{r}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=(f-E(0,2^{N+M})f)\circ D_{r}(x)

\displaystyle=(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{r}.

We have proved that

\displaystyle\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{0,\lambda}(G),\,\forall r=2^{-M},\,E(\lambda,\infty)(f\circ D_{r})=(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{r}.

(3.14)

Let us consider the set of dilations

\mathbb{D}_{0}:=\{D_{r}:r=2^{-M},\,M\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

In what follows let us consider
R\in\mathbb{D}_{0},
and let us consider the function

By applying (
3.13
) with
E(2^{N},\infty)(f\circ D_{R}),
we have the inequality

\displaystyle C\|E(2^{N},\infty)(f\circ D_{R})\|_{L^{p}(G)}

\displaystyle\geq C_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\|E(2^{N},\infty)(f\circ D_{R})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}}(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}E(2^{N},\infty)(f\circ D_{R}),E(2^{N},\infty)(f\circ D_{R}))_{L^{2}}.

Using that

•

\|(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{R}\|_{L^{p}(G)}=R^{-Q/p}\|E(2^{N+M},\infty)f\|_{L^{p}(G)},

•

\|(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(G)}=R^{-Q/q^{\prime}}\|E(2^{N+M},\infty)f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(G)},

that the change of variables
y=D_{R}(x)
gives the volume element

and the identities below

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{R},(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{R})_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\smallint_{G}\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{R}(x)\overline{(E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{R}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\smallint_{G}R^{-a}(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)\circ D_{R}(x))\overline{E(2^{N+M},\infty)f\circ D_{R}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=R^{-a-Q}\smallint_{G}(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)(y))\overline{E(2^{N+M},\infty)f(y)}dy

\displaystyle=R^{-a-Q}(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}E(2^{N+M},\infty)f,E(2^{N+M},\infty)f)_{L^{2}},

we have that for any
R\in\mathbb{D}_{0},
and with
h=E(2^{N+M},\infty)f,

we get the following lower-bound estimate

\displaystyle CR^{-Q/p}\|h\|_{L^{p}(G)}\geq C_{\lambda}R^{Q/q^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\|h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}}R^{-a-Q}(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}h,h)_{L^{2}}.

Let us observe that the previous inequality is equivalent to the following one

CR^{-Q/p+a+Q-Q/q^{\prime}}\|h\|_{L^{p}(G)}\|h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}C_{\lambda}^{-1}\geq(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}h,h)_{L^{2}}>0.

Using (
3.11
) we have the inequality

CR^{-Q/p+a+Q-Q/q^{\prime}}\|h\|_{L^{p}(G)}\|h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{1+2^{N}}\right)^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}\geq(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}h,h)_{L^{2}}>0,\,R=2^{-M}.

(3.15)

If
\varkappa:=-Q/p+a+Q-Q/q^{\prime}<0,
we take
M<0,

N=-M.
Then

is independent of the pair

and consequently (

) takes the form

C2^{-M\varkappa}\|h\|_{L^{p}(G)}\|h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{1+2^{-M}}\right)^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}\geq(\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}h,h)_{L^{2}}>0.

(3.16)

Since
M<0,

2^{-M}>1,
we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{1+2^{-M}}\right)^{-\frac{a}{\nu}}=2^{\frac{Ma}{\nu}}(1+2^{-M})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}\leq 2^{\frac{Ma}{\nu}}(2\times 2^{-M})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}=2^{\frac{a}{\nu}}.

In consequence, letting

2^{-M\varkappa}\rightarrow 0^{+}
implying that the inequality in (
3.16
) is only possible if

\varkappa=-Q/p+a+Q-Q/q^{\prime}=a-Q/p+Q/q\geq 0,

(3.17)

as desired.
Now, in order to prove the converse, that is if (
3.4
) holds, then
B_{a}
is bounded from
L^{p}(G)
into

let us proceed as follows. If

the boundedness of

from

is a consequence of the Sobolev embedding theorem proved in

, page 246]

holds for

Certainly, this means that

\forall f\in\mathscr{S}(G),\,\,\|B_{-u}f\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C\|B_{-v}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

(3.18)

With
a=-u
and

that is when

we have the boundedness operator inequality

\forall f\in\mathscr{S}(G),\,\,\|B_{a}f\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

(3.19)

Now, if

3.2.
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
-boundedness of pseudo-differential operators I

The aim of this subsection is to extend the results in the previous subsection to the pseudo-differential setting. The following result presents the necessary and sufficient criteria for a pseudo-differential operator to be bounded from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G)
for the range
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{R}.
Let
G
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q.
Then, every pseudo-differential operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
with
0\leq\delta\leq\rho\leq 1
and
\delta\neq 1,
admits a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G),
that is

\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(G),\,\|Af\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,\,\,

(3.21)

holds, if and only if,

m\leq-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).

(3.22)

Proof.

Assume that
m>-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).
We are going to show that there exists
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
which is not bounded from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G).
We consider

A=B_{-m}=(1+\mathcal{R})^{\frac{m}{\nu}}\in\Psi^{m}_{1,0}(G\times\widehat{G})\subset\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

Since

-m<Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right),

from Lemma
3.2
, we have that
A=B_{-m}
is not bounded from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G).
So, we have proved the necessity of the order condition (
3.22
). Now, in order to prove the reverse statement, we consider
m
satisfying (
3.22
) and
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
satisfying the conditions

m=m_{1}+m_{2},\,m_{1}\leq-Q(1/p-1/2),\,m_{2}\leq-Q(1/2-1/q).

(3.23)

If
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}),
we factorise
A
as follows,

\displaystyle A=B_{-m_{2}}A_{0}B_{-m_{1}},\,\,A_{0}=B_{m_{2}}AB_{m_{1}}.

Note that
A_{0}\in\Psi^{0}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).
The Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem (Theorem
2.13
(iii)) implies that
A_{0}
is bounded from
L^{2}(G)
into
L^{2}(G).
On the other hand, from Lemma
3.2
we have that
B_{m_{2}}:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{q}(G),
and
B_{m_{1}}:L^{p}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(G),
are bounded operators. In consequence, we have proved that
A
admits a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G).
The proof is complete.
∎

3.3.
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
-boundedness of pseudo-differential operators II

In this subsection, we consider the
L^{p}-L^{q}
boundedness of pseudo-differential operators on graded Lie groups for a wider range of indices
p
and

Our main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1<p\leq q<\infty,m\in\mathbb{R},
and let
G
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q.
Then, every pseudo-differential operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
with
0\leq\delta\leq\rho\leq 1
and
\delta\neq 1,
admits a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G),
that is

\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(G),\,\|Af\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,\,\,

(3.24)

holds in the following cases:

(i)

if
1<p\leq q\leq 2
and

m\leq-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

(3.25)

(ii)

if
2\leq p\leq q<\infty
and

m\leq-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right);

(3.26)

Proof.

(i)

Let us consider
p,q
and
m
satisfying the conditions given in (i). Choose
m^{\prime}=-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)
and this implies that the Bessel potential
B_{-m^{\prime}}
is bounded from
L^{p}(G)
to
L^{q}(G)
as a consequence of Lemma
3.2
. For
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}),
we decompose it as follows:

A=(AB_{m^{\prime}})B_{-m^{\prime}}.

Now, we note that operator
AB_{m^{\prime}}\in\Psi^{m-m^{\prime}}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
with
m-m^{\prime}
satisfying
m-m^{\prime}\leq-Q(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right).
Then, Theorem
2.14
shows that
AB_{m^{\prime}}
is bounded operator from
L^{q}(G)
into
L^{q}(G).
Therefore, we conclude that the operator
A
has a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G).

(ii)

To prove this part we follow the same strategy as in Part (i). We factorise the operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
in the following manner:

A=B_{-m^{\prime}}(B_{m^{\prime}}A),

where
m^{\prime}=-Q(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}).
Again, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that the operator
B_{-m^{\prime}}
is a bounded from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G).
On the other hand, the operator
B_{m^{\prime}}A\in\Psi^{m-m^{\prime}}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
with
m-m^{\prime}\leq-Q(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right),
which, as a consequence of Theorem
2.14
, yields that the operator
B_{-m^{\prime}}A
is bounded from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{p}(G).
Hence, we conclude that the operator
A
has a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G).

This completes the proof of this theorem. ∎

Example 3.6
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\in C^{\infty}(G)
be complex-valued smooth functions with bounded derivatives, i.e. such that for any

X_{x}^{\alpha}L_{1}
and

are bounded functions and such that for some
\Lambda\geq 0,
there exists a constant

such that they satisfy the following

\forall x\in G,\,\forall\lambda\geq\Lambda,\,|L_{1}(x)+L_{2}(x)\lambda|\geq c_{\Lambda}(1+\lambda).

(3.27)

Then the operator

A(x,\mathcal{R}):=L_{1}(x)+L_{2}(x)\mathcal{R}\in\Psi^{\nu}_{1,0}(G\times\widehat{G})

is a non-invariant elliptic operator with a left-parametrix
P(x,\mathcal{R})\in\Psi^{-\nu}_{1,0}(G\times\widehat{G})
(see
[
16
, Proposition 5.8.2]
and
[
10
]
). According to the order condition in (
1.5
),
if

\nu\geq Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right),

(3.28)

where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of the group
G,

P(x,\mathcal{R}):L^{p}(G)\rightarrow L^{q}(G)

is bounded.

We note that the inequality in (

) is not sharp in the sense that the difference

\nu-Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)

could be strictly positive. However, if
r,a\in\mathbb{R}
are real parameters in such a way that

4.1.
Subelliptic regularity on graded Lie groups

This subsection is devoted to applying the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
-boundedness of pseudo-differential operators on graded Lie groups to the validity of subelliptic estimates for elliptic operators. For the main aspects of this subject in the Euclidean setting and on compact manifolds we refer to Hörmander
[
24
,
25
]
and Kohn and Nirenberg
[
28
]
, see also Taylor
[
44
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\in C^{\infty}(G)
be complex functions satisfying the growth property in (
3.27
). Let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu>0,
and assume that
u\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(G)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
are solutions of the equation

A(x,\mathcal{R})u=f,\,A(x,\mathcal{R}):=L_{1}(x)+L_{2}(x)\mathcal{R}.

(4.1)

If
f\in L^{p}_{r}(G),
for some
r\in\mathbb{R},
with
1<p<\infty,
then for any
s\in\mathbb{R},
there exists
C_{s}>0,
such that the a-priori estimate

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{a}}\leq C_{s}(\|f\|_{L^{p}_{r}(G)}+\|u\|_{L^{q}_{-s}(G)}),

(4.2)

holds for
a\in\mathbb{R}
provided that
1<p\leq q<\infty
and that

\nu\geq a-r+Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).

(4.3)

Proof.

Let
P:=P(x,\mathcal{R})
be a left-parametrix of
A:=A(x,\mathcal{R}).
Then, we have that

S:=PA-I\in\Psi^{-\infty}_{1,0}(G\times\widehat{G}).
Let
a,r,s\in\mathbb{R}.
From the identity
Au=f,
we have that

PAu=u+Su=Pf,

(4.4)

and then

\|B_{-a}u\|_{L^{q}(G)}=\|B_{-a}Pf-B_{-a}Su\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq\|B_{-a}PB_{r}B_{-r}u\|_{L^{q}(G)}+\|B_{-s}Su\|_{L^{q}(G)}.

(4.5)

Since the operator
B_{-a}S
is smoothing, we have that, for any
s\in\mathbb{R},
there exists
C_{s}>0
such that the following inequality holds

\|B_{-a}Su\|_{L^{q}(G)}=\|B_{-a}SB_{-s}B_{s}u\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C_{s}\|B_{s}u\|_{L^{q}(G)}=C_{s}\|u\|_{L^{q}_{-s}(G)}.

On the other hand, the operator
B_{-a}PB_{r}
has order
a-\nu-r
and it is bounded from
L^{p}(G)
into
L^{q}(G)
(see (
1.5
) and Remark
1.2
) if

-a+\nu+r\geq Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).

In this case we have that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{a}(G)}=\|B_{-a}u\|_{L^{q}(G)}\lesssim_{s}\|B_{-r}u\|_{L^{p}(G)}+\|u\|_{L^{q}_{-s}(G)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}_{r}(G)}+\|u\|_{L^{q}_{-s}(G)}.

The proof is complete.
∎

This subsection is devoted to discussing the implications of our results in the setting of newly developed

We will here briefly recall the relationship between the
\tau
-quatisation and the global Kohn-Nirenberg quantisation on graded Lie groups.

We say that a measurable function
\tau:G\rightarrow G
often referred as

satisfies the property (HP) if, by representing it via exponential coordinates as
\tau(x)=(c_{1}^{\tau}(x),\ldots,c_{n}^{\tau}(x))\in G,

of
\tau(x)

for some nonzero
C_{i}^{\tau}
and some homogeneous polynomials
d_{i}^{\tau}
of degree
\nu_{i}
depending only on
x_{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,i-1.
Having this quantisation function
\tau,
one defines the
\tau
-quantisation on a graded Lie group
G
by

\text{Op}^{\tau}(\sigma)f(x)=\int_{\widehat{G}}\text{Tr}\left(\int_{G}\pi(y^{-1}x)\sigma(x\tau(y^{-1}x)^{-1},\pi)f(y)\,dy\right)d\pi.

(4.6)

In the following result of
[
14
]
, the authors established the relationship between the Kohn-Nirenberg quantisation and the
\tau
-quantisation on graded Lie groups.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
14
, Theorem 4.12]

Let
\tau
be quantising function different from the constant function
\tau\equiv e_{G}
on
G
which satisfies the property (HP) and let
A
be a continuous operator from
\mathscr{S}(G)
to
\mathscr{S}(G)
. Assume that
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq\delta<\min\{\rho,\frac{1}{\nu_{n}}\}\leq 1
and there exist two symbols
\sigma
and
\sigma_{\tau}
such that
A=\text{Op}^{\tau}(\sigma_{\tau})=\text{Op}(\sigma).
Then
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
if and only if
\sigma_{\tau}\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

By combining Theorem
4.2
, Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
we obtain the following
L^{p}
-

\tau
-quantisation of operators on
G.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R},

0\leq\delta<\min\{\rho,\frac{1}{\nu_{n}}\}\leq 1,
and let
\tau
be a quantising function satisfying (HP). Then, for

the operator
T=\text{Op}^{\tau}(\sigma)
has a bounded extension from
L^{p}(G)
to

for
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})

\text{Op}^{\tau}(\sigma)

Proof.

It follows from Theorem

for some

and Theorem

.
∎

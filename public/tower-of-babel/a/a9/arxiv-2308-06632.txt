1.
Introduction

The twin prime conjecture states that there are infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes
p_{n}
and
p_{n+1}
which differ exactly by
2
. Although the twin prime conjecture remains unproven, partial progress has been made over the past several decades. A consequence of the prime number theorem is that
p_{n}\sim n\log p_{n}
, so the average gap
p_{n+1}-p_{n}
is asymptotically
\log p_{n}
, i.e., unbounded. In 2005, Goldston, Pintz, and Yıldırım

[
16
]
developed a sieve method that detects tuples of primes in short intervals, demonstrating that

(1.1)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}-p_{n}}{\log p_{n}}=0.

In particular, the result (
1.1
) says that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exist infinitely many
n
such that
p_{n+1}-p_{n}\leq\varepsilon\log p_{n}
. That is, there exist infinitely many consecutive primes whose gap is an arbitrarily small multiple of the mean gap.
In 2013, Zhang
[
49
]
significantly improved upon the GPY argument, showing that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+1}-p_{n})\leq 70{,}000{,}000.

Later that year, Maynard brought the value of
70{,}000{,}000
down to
600

[
29
]
. He also proved that if
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+m}-p_{n})\ll m^{3}e^{4m}.

(Tao developed the underlying ideas independently, but arrived at slightly different conclusions.) Later that year, the Polymath Research Project
[
36
]
synthesized the arguments of Zhang, Maynard, and Tao, proving that

\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+1}-p_{n})\leq 246.

More generally, the existence of infinitely many bounded gaps has been established within special sets of primes of number-theoretic interest. See
[
2
,
43
,
45
]
, for example.

In this paper, we are interested in bounded gaps between primes related to the Sato–Tate conjecture.
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)e^{2\pi inz}\in S_{k}^{\rm new}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a holomorphic cusp form without complex multiplication (CM) and with trivial nebentypus, level
N
, and even integral weight
k\geq 2
. We normalize
f
so that
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
, and the trivial nebentypus condition then implies that
\lambda_{f}(n)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
n
.
If
f
is also an eigenform for all of the Hecke operators and all of the Atkin–Lehner involutions
|_{k}W(N)
and
|_{k}W(Q_{p})
for each prime
p\mid N
, then
f
is a newform (see
[
33
, Section 2.5]
).
For any prime
p
, Deligne’s proof of the Weil conjectures implies that
|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq 2p^{(k-1)/2}
.
Thus, there exists a unique angle
\theta_{f}(p)\in[0,\pi]
such that
\lambda_{f}(p)={2p^{(k-1)/2}}\cos{\theta_{f}(p)}
.

It is natural to ask how the angles
\theta_{f}(p)
distribute as the prime
p
varies.
Given an interval
I\subseteq[0,\pi]
, we consider the set of primes

\mathcal{P}_{f,I}:=\{p:p\nmid N,\theta_{f}(p)\in I\}.

One may ask how many primes
p\leq x
belong to
\mathcal{P}_{f,I}
. Specifically, we are interested in the prime counting function

\pi_{f,I}(x):=\#(\mathcal{P}_{f,I}\cap[1,x]).

The Sato–Tate conjecture, in the form proposed by Serre
[
40
]
which holds for all non-CM newforms as above, is equivalent to the statement that for the measure
d\mu_{\rm ST}:=\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta
,

(1.2)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{f,I}(x)}{\pi(x)}=\mu_{\rm ST}(I).

This was proven by Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, and Taylor
[
3
]
.
See the works of Thorner
[
44
]
and Hoey, Iskander, Jin, and Trejos-Suárez
[
21
]
for effective bounds on the rate of convergence in (
1.2
).

Given a pair of distinct non-CM holomorphic newforms
f_{j}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}(n)e^{2\pi inz}\in S_{k_{j}}^{\textrm{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{j}))
,
j=1,2
, with
\lambda_{1}(1)=\lambda_{2}(1)=1
, it is natural to ask what is the joint distribution of the angles
\theta_{1}(p)
and
\theta_{2}(p)
. For a pair of subintervals
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq[0,\pi]
, we consider the set of primes

(1.3)

\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}:=\{p:p\nmid N_{1}N_{2},\ \theta_{1}(p)\in I_{1},\ \theta_{2}(p)\in I_{2}\}.

Next, we define the joint prime counting function

\pi_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}(x):=\#(\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}\cap[1,x]).

A natural question to ask, first posed by Mazur and Katz, is
whether

(1.4)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}(x)}{\pi(x)}=\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{1})\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{2}).

That is, when do the angles
\theta_{1}(p)
and
\theta_{2}(p)
behave asymptotically like independent random variables?

We impose a technical condition on the relationship between
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in order to obtain the asymptotic (
1.4
). We say that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
twist-inequivalent
, denoted by
f_{1}\not\sim f_{2}
, if for every primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
one has that
f_{1}\neq f_{2}\otimes\chi
.
Building on the work of Harris
[
20
]
, Wong
[
48
]
proved that (
1.4
) holds when
f_{1}\not\sim f_{2}
. See Thorner
[
44
]
for an effective bound on the rate of convergence. In this paper, we establish the infinitude of bounded gaps within the set of primes
\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}
defined in (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For
j=1,2
, let
f_{j}(z)\in S_{k_{j}}^{\rm new}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{j}))
be non-CM holomorphic cuspidal newforms with trivial nebentypus that are unrelated by twist. If
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq[0,\pi]
are subintervals and
P_{n}
denotes the
n
-th prime in
\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}
, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}(P_{n+m}-P_{n})\ll\frac{N_{1}N_{2}m}{\varphi(N_{1}N_{2})(\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{1})\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{2}))^{8/3}}\exp\Big(\frac{4581N_{1}N_{2}m}{\varphi(N_{1}N_{2})(\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{1})\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{2}))^{8/3}}\Big).

The implied constant is absolute and effective.

In 2004, Green and Tao
[
17
]
proved that the primes form infinitely many arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length. This was achieved by extending Szemerédi’s theorem to the primes via a transference principle. Subsequently, many simplifications and improvements have been made to this argument, including a criterion of Pintz
[
34
]
.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}
has positive density in the primes, the Green–Tao theorem implies the existence of infinitely many arithmetic progressions of arbitrarily length in
\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}
. In this paper, we prove a common generalization of infinitely many bounded gaps with the Green–Tao theorem.
That is, there exist infinitely many arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length in
\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}
which occur simultaneously as the shifts of an admissible set. We postpone the full statement of this result until
Theorem

4.8
in
Section

4
.

2.
Conventions and Notation

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of positive integers. Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the set of prime numbers
\{2,3,5,\ldots\}
. Throughout the paper,
p
always denotes a prime. Given a set
S
, we let
\mathbbm{1}_{S}
denote the indicator function of
S
.

We let
a\mid b
denote the proposition that
a
divides
b
, and
a\nmid b
to be its negation.
Although the notation
[a,b]
is also used for intervals in the paper, we also use
[a,b]
to denote the least common multiple of
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
. Similarly, we will also use
(a,b)
to denote the greatest common divisor. In both cases, it will be clear from context which of the two meanings is intended.
We say that a finite subset

is admissible if for every prime
p
, the reduction of
\mathcal{H}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. The diameter of an admissible set

is
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{H}):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq k}|h_{j}-h_{j}|
.
We assume without loss of generality in our results that
0\in\mathcal{H}
.

Given a natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it admits a prime factorization
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
where
p_{1}<\cdots<p_{r}
. We then define the following arithmetic functions:

•

P^{-}(n):=p_{1}
, is the smallest prime divisor of
n
;

•

\rad(n):=p_{1}\cdots p_{r}
, the product of the distinct primes dividing
n
;

•

\Lambda(n)
is the von Mangoldt function, defined as
\Lambda(n)=\log{p}
if
n=p^{\ell}
for some prime
p
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\Lambda(n)=0
otherwise;

•

d_{k}(n):=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\binom{k+\alpha_{j}-1}{k-1}
is the
k
-fold divisor function; i.e., the number of ways
n
can be expressed as the product of
k
natural numbers;

•

\varphi(n):=n\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-1/p_{j})
is the Euler totient function;

•

\mu(n)
is the Möbius function, defined as
\mu(n)=(-1)^{r}
if
n
is squarefree and
\mu(n)=0
otherwise.

We write
f\ll g
, or
f=O(g)
, to indicate that in a range of
z
(clear from context), there exists a positive constant
C
that depends only on
k
,
\theta
,
\delta
,
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{H})
,
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
,
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
A
, and
B
such that
|f(z)|\leq C|g(z)|
.
Also, the use of the term “sufficiently large” may depend on
k
,
\theta
,
\delta
,
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{H})
,
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
,
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
A
, and
B
. The dependence of the implied constant on any additional parameters
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
is denoted by the symbol
\ll_{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}
.
We write
f\asymp g
to denote that
f\ll g
and
f\gg g
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
let
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
. Given a locally integrable function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
we define

\int_{(\sigma)}f(s)\,ds:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\sigma-iT}^{\sigma+iT}f(s)\,ds

provided that the limit exists.

For every
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we define the
m
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind
U_{m}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
by the recurrence relation

U_{0}(t):=1,\qquad U_{1}(t):=2t,\qquad U_{m+2}(t):=2tU_{m+1}(t)-U_{m}(t).

In particular, we have that the Chebyshev polynomials satisfy

U_{m}(\cos\theta)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}e^{i(m-2j)\theta},\qquad\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

3.
A Minorizing Fourier Expansion

In order to prove bounded gaps in the prime sets
\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}
, one must be able to detect when
(\theta_{1}(p),\theta_{2}(p))\in I_{1}\times I_{2}
. We proceed as in Rouse and Thorner
[
39
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq[0,\pi]
be subintervals. Given
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a real trigonometric polynomial of the form

(3.1)

\mathcal{F}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\sum_{0\leq m_{1}\leq M_{1}}\sum_{0\leq m_{2}\leq M_{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(m_{1},m_{2})U_{m_{1}}(\cos\theta_{1})U_{m_{2}}(\cos\theta_{2})

with the following properties.

(1)

If
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,\pi]
, then
\mathcal{F}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\mathbbm{1}_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
.

(2)

The inequality

|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(0,0)-\mu_{{\rm ST}}(I_{1})\mu_{{\rm ST}}(I_{2})|\leq 8\Big(\frac{\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{2})^{1/3}}{M_{1}+1}+\frac{\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{1})^{1/3}}{M_{2}+1}+\frac{6}{(M_{1}+1)(M_{2}+1)}\Big)

holds.

Proof.

We begin with some notation. Let
I_{1}=[a_{1},b_{1}]
,
I_{2}=[a_{2},b_{2}]
, and
J_{1}:=[a_{1}/(2\pi),b_{1}/(2\pi)]

J_{2}:=[a_{2}/(2\pi),b_{2}/(2\pi)]
.
Given an interval
J\subseteq[0,1]
, the Fourier expansion of the indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{J}
is given by

\mathbbm{1}_{J}(\theta)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J}(n)e(n\theta),\qquad\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J}(n)=\int_{J}e(-nt)\,dt.

Cochrane
[
8
]
proved that for
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a trigonometric polynomial

S(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq|m_{1}|\leq M_{1}\\
0\leq|m_{2}|\leq M_{2}\end{subarray}}\widehat{S}(m_{1},m_{2})\cdot e(m_{1}x_{1}+m_{2}x_{2})

with the following properties:

(1)

for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in[0,1]^{2}
,
S(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\mathbbm{1}_{J_{1}\times J_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
;

(2)

for all
(m_{1},m_{2})
, we have that
\widehat{S}(m_{1},m_{2})+\widehat{S}(-m_{1},-m_{2})=2\operatorname{Re}\widehat{S}(m_{1},m_{2})
;

(3)

for all
(m_{1},m_{2})
,
we have that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Big|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{1}}(m_{1})\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{2}}(m_{2})-\widehat{S}(m_{1},m_{2})\Big|\leq\frac{|J_{2}|}{M_{1}+1}+\frac{|J_{1}|}{M_{2}+1}+\frac{3}{(M_{1}+1)(M_{2}+1)}.

If we define
U_{-1}(\cos\theta)=0
and
U_{-2}(\cos\theta)=-1
, then

(3.3)

\displaystyle\cos(n\theta)=\frac{1}{2}(U_{n}(\cos\theta)-U_{n-2}(\cos\theta)),\hskip 28.45274ptn\geq 0.

We use these identities to obtain the Chebyshev polynomial approximation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=S\Big(\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi},\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\Big)+S\Big(-\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi},\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\Big)+S\Big(\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi},-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\Big)+S\Big(-\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi},-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\Big)

for
\mathbbm{1}_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
. Notice that
\mathcal{F}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}
is real-valued. We see for

that

\Big(-\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi},\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\Big),\Big(\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi},-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\Big),\Big(-\frac{\theta_{1}}{2\pi},-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\Big)\notin(J_{1}\times J_{2})\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1).

Therefore,

(3.4)

By continuity of
\mathcal{F}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}
, the inequality
Equation

3.4

We now write

\mathcal{F}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=4\widehat{S}(0,0)+4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|m_{1}|\leq M_{1}\\
|m_{2}|\leq M_{2}\\
\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{0}\end{subarray}}\widehat{S}(m_{1},m_{2})\cos(|m_{1}|\theta_{1})\cos(|m_{2}|\theta_{2}).

By

3.3

\displaystyle 4

\displaystyle\widehat{S}(0,0)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|m_{1}|\leq M_{1}\\

We compute

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(0,0)=4\operatorname{Re}(\widehat{S}(0,0)-\widehat{S}(2,0)-\widehat{S}(0,2)+\widehat{S}(2,2))

and

\mu_{\rm ST}([a,b])=2\operatorname{Re}\Big(\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{[\frac{a}{2\pi},\frac{b}{2\pi}]}(0)-\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{[\frac{a}{2\pi},\frac{b}{2\pi}]}(2)\Big).

Therefore, it follows from
Item

3
for
S
that

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{I_{1},I_{2},M_{1},M_{2}}(0,0)-\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{1})\mu_{\rm ST}(I_{2})|

\displaystyle=4|\operatorname{Re}(\widehat{S}(0,0)-\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{1}}(0)\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{2}}(0)-\widehat{S}(2,0)+\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{1}}(2)\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{2}}(0)

\displaystyle-\widehat{S}(0,2)+\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{1}}(0)\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{2}}(2)+\widehat{S}(2,2)-\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}_{J_{1}}(2)\widehat{\mathbbm{1}}(2))|

\displaystyle\leq 16\Big(\frac{|J_{2}|}{M_{1}+1}+\frac{|J_{1}|}{M_{2}+1}+\frac{3}{(M_{1}+1)(M_{2}+1)}\Big).

We claim that if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{\theta}\\

4.1.
Modified Maynard–Tao Sieve

We present the necessary sieve theory for our proofs, following Maynard
[
29
]
, and Vatwani and Wong
[
45
]
.
Let
F_{I_{1},I_{2}}
be the minorizing polynomial given in
Corollary

3.2
with degrees
M_{1},M_{2}
.
We define several parameters which may depend on a uniform choice of
x\geq 16
:

\widetilde{\theta}:=\frac{1}{(M_{1}+1)(M_{2}+1)+2},\qquad 0<\theta<\widetilde{\theta},\qquad\delta\in(0,\theta/2),

R:=x^{\theta/2-\delta},\qquad D_{0}>0,\qquad W:=\prod_{p\leq D_{0}}p,\qquad U:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq D_{0}\\
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\end{subarray}}p.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
be an admissible set. For all primes
p
there exists an integer
a_{p}
such that
a_{p}\not\equiv h_{j}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
for all
1\leq j\leq k
. By the Chinese Remainder theorem, we can choose
u_{0}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ U)
such that
u_{0}\equiv-a_{p}\mod p
for all
p\mid U
. This way, we have that
u_{0}+h_{j}\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
for all
p\mid U
and
1\leq j\leq k
. Therefore, we deduce that
(u_{0}+h_{j},U)=1
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.
For every
k
-tuple of natural numbers
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
, we introduce a parameter

\lambda_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}

that satisfies the following support condition.
The parameter
\lambda_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}}
is nonzero only if
d:=\prod_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}
satisfies
d<R
,
\mu^{2}(d)=1
, and we also have that
(d_{j},W)=1
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Notice that the condition
\mu^{2}(d)=1
implies that the
d_{j}
are squarefree and pairwise coprime.
We define for each

a nonnegative weight

w_{n}:=\begin{cases}\Big(\sum\limits_{d_{j}\mid n+h_{j}\,\forall j}\lambda_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}}\Big)^{2}&\mbox{if $n\equiv u_{0}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ U)$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We also define the sums

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq 2x\\
n\equiv u_{0}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ U)\end{subarray}}w_{n},

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq 2x\\
n\equiv u_{0}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ U)\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{j})F_{I_{1},I_{2}}(\cos\theta_{1}(n+h_{j}),\cos\theta_{2}(n+h_{j}))\Big)w_{n},

\displaystyle S(x,\rho)

\displaystyle:=S_{2}-\rho S_{1}.

Although
\theta_{j}(p)
is defined only for
p
prime, by abuse of notation this is acceptable because the indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{j})
is non-vanishing if and only if
n+h_{j}
is prime.
Note that because
F_{I_{1},I_{2}}
satisfies
Item

1
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{j})\mathbbm{1}_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}(\theta_{1}(n+h_{j}),\theta_{2}(n+h_{j}))-\rho\Big)w_{n}\geq S(x,\rho).

Thus, it suffices to show that
S(x,\rho)>0
for all

sufficiently large in order to deduce by the pigeonhole principle that there are infinitely many integers
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that the set
\{n+h_{1},\ldots,n+h_{k}\}
contains at least
\lfloor\rho+1\rfloor
primes which lie in
\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2},I_{1},I_{2}}
.

4.2.
Bounded Gaps

In this section, we prove

The first proposition expresses the asymptotics for the sums

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\theta\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{(M_{1}+1)(M_{2}+1)+2}\Big)

Let

Let

(4.1)

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\rad(N_{1}N_{2})\cdot\frac{(1+O(1/D_{0}))\varphi(W)^{k}x(\log R)^{k}}{W^{k+1}}I_{k}(G),

(4.2)

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\varphi(\rad(N_{1}N_{2}))\cdot\widehat{F}_{I_{1},I_{2}}(0,0)\cdot\frac{(1+O(1/D_{0}))\varphi(W)^{k}x(\log R)^{k+1}}{W^{k+1}\log x}\sum_{m=1}^{k}J_{k}^{(m)}(G).

Using

4.6
and

Theorem 4.8
.

Let

be subintervals of
[0,\pi]
with positive Lebesgue measure.
Let
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
be an admissible set.
If

Proof.

We follow the argument of
[
15
]
.
By

and

, the set

Let

be the set of all

7.
Automorphic
L
-functions

In this section, we recall some of the theory of automorphic representations and their associated
L
-functions.

7.1.
Automorphic representations

Let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the adele ring over
\mathbb{Q}
. For every natural number
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathfrak{F}_{m}
be the set of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GL_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with trivial central character. Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{m}
. There exist smooth admissible representations
\pi_{\infty}
of
\GL_{m}(\mathbb{R})
and
\pi_{p}
of
\GL_{m}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for every prime
p
such that
\pi
equals the restricted tensor product

\pi=\pi_{\infty}\otimes\Big(\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\bigotimes}_{p}\pi_{p}\Big).

Moreover,
\pi_{p}
is ramified for at most finitely many
p
. For every prime
p
, there exist
m
complex numbers
(\alpha_{j,\pi}(p))_{j=1}^{m}
that form the local
L
-function

L(s,\pi_{p}):=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)p^{-s})^{-1}.

In particular, the
L
-function associated to
\pi
has an absolutely convergent Euler product

L(s,\pi):=\prod_{p}L(s,\pi_{p})=\prod_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)p^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\pi}(n)n^{-s},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

There exist
m
complex Langlands parameters
(\kappa_{j,\pi})_{j=1}^{m}
that form the local
L
-function

L(s,\pi_{\infty}):=\pi^{-ms/2}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+\kappa_{j,\pi}}{2}\Big).

There also exists a conductor
q_{\pi}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
r_{\pi}\in\{0,1\}
which is
1
if and only if
\pi
is the trivial representation, such that the completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\pi):=(s(1-s))^{r_{\pi}}q_{\pi}^{s/2}L(s,\pi_{\infty})L(s,\pi)

is entire of order
1
. If
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{m}
is the dual of
\pi
, then

\{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(p)\}=\{\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}\},\qquad\{\kappa_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}\}=\{\overline{\kappa_{j,\pi}}\},\qquad q_{\widetilde{\pi}}=q_{\pi},\qquad r_{\widetilde{\pi}}=r_{\pi}.

There exists
W(\pi)\in\mathbb{C}
of modulus
1
such that the functional equation

\Lambda(s,\pi)=W(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi})

holds for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
.
The nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi)
are the zeros of
\Lambda(s,\pi)
, and they lie in the critical strip
0\leq\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq 1
.
Finally, the analytic conductor is defined as

C(s,\pi):=q_{\pi}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(|s+\kappa_{j,\pi}|+3),\qquad C(\pi):=C(0,\pi).

7.2.
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{m}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{m^{\prime}}
. Let
r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=1
if
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}
, and otherwise
r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=0
.
For every prime
p\nmid q_{\pi}q_{\pi^{\prime}}
, we define the local
L
-function

L(s,\pi_{p}\times\pi_{p}^{\prime}):=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m^{\prime}}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\alpha_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(p)p^{-s})^{-1}.

For each
p\mid q_{\pi}q_{\pi^{\prime}}
and each pair of integers
(j,j^{\prime})\in[1,m]\times[1,m^{\prime}]
, Jacquet, Piatetskii-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
18
]
defined complex numbers
\alpha_{j,j,^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)
and
\kappa_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}
such that if

L(s,\pi_{p}\times\pi_{p}^{\prime}):=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m^{\prime}}(1-\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi,\pi^{\prime}}(p)p^{-s})^{-1}

and

L(s,\pi_{\infty}\times\pi_{\infty}^{\prime}):=\pi^{-mm^{\prime}s/2}\prod_{j,j^{\prime}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+\kappa_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}{2}\Big),

then the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}):=\prod_{p}L(s,\pi_{p}\times\pi_{p}^{\prime})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)n^{-s}

converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
and satisfies the following theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{m}\times\mathfrak{F}_{m^{\prime}}
. There exists a conductor
q_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the completed
L
-function

(7.1)

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}):=(s(1-s))^{r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}q_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}^{s/2}L(s,\pi_{\infty}\times\pi_{\infty}^{\prime})L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})

is entire of order
1
. Moreover, there exists
W(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}
of modulus
1
such that

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=W(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}).

Proof.

See
[
18
]
.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Moreover,
[
18
]
shows that
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is holomorphic away from a possible pole at
s=1
, which occurs if and only if
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{m}
,
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{m^{\prime}}
, and
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q_{\chi}
.
The nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
are the zeros of
\Lambda(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
, and they lie in the critical strip
0\leq\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq 1
.
For the conductor
q_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}
, Bushnell and Henniart
[
5
]
proved that

(7.2)

q_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}\mid q_{\pi}^{m^{\prime}}q_{\pi^{\prime}}^{m}q_{\chi}^{mm^{\prime}}.

Define

C(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)):=q_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m^{\prime}}(|s+\kappa_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}|+3).\quad C(\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)):=C(0,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)),

Brumley
[
23
, Appendix]
proved that

(7.3)

C(\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\ll C(\pi)^{m^{\prime}}C(\pi^{\prime})^{m}q_{\chi}^{mm^{\prime}},

where the implied constant only depends on
m,m^{\prime}
. One can also check that

(7.4)

C(it,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\leq C(\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))(|t|+3)^{mm^{\prime}}.

If
\chi
is a primitive Dirichlet character, we denote the following Dirichlet coefficients

\displaystyle L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(n)n^{-s},\qquad\frac{1}{L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(n)n^{-s},

\displaystyle-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(n)n^{-s}.

Note, if
\chi
is the trivial character then
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
and if
\pi^{\prime}
is the trivial representation then
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=L(s,\pi)
, so we are setting notation for these
L
-functions as well. Using computations analogous to those from Luo, Rudnick, and Sarnak
[
46
, Lemma 2.1]
, we find for
(q_{\pi}q_{\pi^{\prime}},q_{\chi})=1
that
\alpha_{j,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(p)=\alpha_{j,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)\chi(p)
. In particular, if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(n)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)\chi(n),

(7.5)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(n)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)\chi(n),

\displaystyle\mu_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(n)

\displaystyle=\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)\chi(n).

We note that finding an upper bound on the absolute value of one of these numbers is independent of
\chi
.
Furthermore, there exist theorems which allow decoupling of the dependencies on
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
.
For example, Brumley
[
41
, Appendix]
proved that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(7.6)

|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\leq\sqrt{\Lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)}.

Also, Jiang, Lü, and Wang
[
25
, Lemma 3.1]
later proved that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(7.7)

|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)}.

7.3.
Isobaric sums

For
t\in\mathbb{R}

|\cdot|^{it}

|\cdot|
is the adelic norm. The
L

whose

8.1.
Automorphy of symmetric powers

Let
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a newform as in
Section

. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a local factor at the primes
p\nmid N
of the form

(8.1)

L_{p}(s,\Sym^{m}f):=\prod_{j=0}^{m}(1-\alpha_{j,\Sym^{m}f}(p)p^{-s})^{-1},\qquad\alpha_{j,\Sym^{m}f}(p):=e^{i(m-2j)\theta_{f}(p)}.

For each prime
p\mid N
, there exists a local factor
L_{p}(s,\Sym^{m}f)
such that the
m
-th symmetric power
L
-function of
f
is given by

L(s,\Sym^{m}f):=\prod_{p\mid N}L_{p}(s,\Sym^{m}f)\prod_{p\nmid N}\prod_{j=0}^{m}(1-e^{i(m-2j)\theta_{f}(p)}p^{-s})^{-1}=\prod_{p}\prod_{j=0}^{m}(1-\alpha_{j,\Sym^{m}f}(p)p^{-s})^{-1},

which converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
.

Theorem 8.1

(Newton and Thorne,
[
30
,
31
]
)
.

Let
f
be a non-CM holomorphic newform with trivial nebentypus. Then, there exists
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{m+1}
such that
L(s,\Sym^{m}f)=L(s,\pi)
.

For non-CM holomorphic newforms
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, recall that the notation
f_{1}\not\sim f_{2}
means that if
\psi
is a primitive Dirichlet character, then
f_{1}\neq f_{2}\otimes\psi
. Since the nebentypus characters of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are trivial, it follows from
Theorem

8.1
that there exist self-dual representations
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{m_{1}+1}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{m_{2}+1}
such that

L(s,\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi))=L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)).

In order to determine whether
L(s,\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi))
has a pole at
s=1
or not, we have the following proposition which follows from the work of Rajan
[
38
, Corollary 5.1]
.

Proposition 8.2
.

If
f_{1}\not\sim f_{2}
and
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(0,0)
, then
L(s,\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi))
is entire.

Proof.

By
Theorem

8.1
, let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be the cuspidal automorphic representations associated to
\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}
and
\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}
respectively. Notice that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are self-dual because our newforms have trivial nebentypus. Then, by
Theorem

7.1
, it suffices to show that
\pi\ncong\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.
If
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
, then
\pi\ncong\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
because
\pi
has degree
m_{1}+1
and
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
has degree
m_{2}+1
.
Therefore, we may assume without loss of generality that
m_{1}=m_{2}=\ell>0
.

Rajan
[
38
, Corollary 5.1]
proved that if

(8.2)

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\#\{p\leq x:U_{\ell}(\cos\theta_{1}(p))=U_{\ell}(\cos\theta_{2}(p))\}}{\pi(x)}>0,

then there exists a Dirichlet character
\psi
such that
f_{1}=f_{2}\otimes\psi
.
Thus, assume for the sake of contradiction that
\pi=\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.
This implies that for all but finitely many primes
p
, one has

U_{\ell}(\cos\theta_{1}(p))=U_{\ell}(\cos\theta_{2}(p))\chi(p).

Therefore, (
8.2
) holds because the (natural) density of primes
p
such that
\chi(p)=1
equals
1/\varphi(q_{\chi})
.
This implies that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are related by twist, which is a contradiction.
The proposition follows.
∎

The following lemma will be useful later.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic newform with level
N
and trivial nebentypus. For all

, one has

L(s,\Sym^{m_{1}}f\times\Sym^{m_{2}}f)=\prod_{j=0}^{\min(m_{1},m_{2})}L(s,\Sym^{|m_{1}-m_{2}|+2j}f).

Proof.

This follows from the Clebsch–Gordan identities for
{\rm SU}(2)
.

8.2.
Estimates on Dirichlet Coefficients

Note that in applications (
7.5
) holds, which allows us to assume for our purposes of bounding coefficients that
\chi
is trivial. We say that an
L
-function satisfies the Generalized Ramanujan Conjecture (GRC) if its Satake parameters are bounded in modulus by
1
, and its Langlands parameters have nonnegative real parts. We have the following two lemmata.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic newform with level
N
and trivial nebentypus. Then, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
L(s,\Sym^{m}f)
satisfies GRC.

Proof.

For
m=0
,
L(s,\Sym^{0}f)=\zeta(s)
satisfies GRC. Therefore, let us assume
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If
p
is a prime not dividing
N
, then the Satake parameters of
L_{p}(s,\Sym^{m}f)
are bounded in modulus by
1
by considering (
8.1
).
To show that the other primes
p\mid N
have local factors whose parameters are bounded in modulus by
1
, as well as showing that the Langlands parameters have nonnegative real parts, we proceed as in
[
1
, Proof of Corollary 7.1.15]
.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be non-CM holomorphic newforms.
For all
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(8.3)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)\Lambda(n),

(8.4)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq d_{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}(n),

(8.5)

\displaystyle|\mu_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}}(n)|

\displaystyle\ll_{m_{1},m_{2}}d_{2}(n)^{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}.

Proof.

We apply (
7.7
) with
\pi=\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}
,
\pi^{\prime}=\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}
in order to obtain

|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\leq\sqrt{\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi}(n)\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)}.

When
n
is not a prime power,
\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)=0
.
When
n=p^{\ell}
is a prime power,
Lemma

8.3
implies that

\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi}(p^{\ell})=\sum_{j=0}^{m_{1}}\Lambda_{\Sym^{2j}f_{1}}(p^{\ell})=\sum_{j=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{r=0}^{2j}\alpha_{r,\Sym^{2j}f_{1}}(p)^{\ell}\log p,

and similarly for
\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}
. The bound (
8.3
) now follows from
Lemma

8.4
.

For the proof of (
8.4
), one relates
\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)
to
\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)
by formally relating the Dirichlet series
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
and
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
. We reduce to the case of prime powers, since
\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)
is a multiplicative function. For
n=p^{\ell}
, applying formal integration and exponentiation to
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
yields the relation between Dirichlet coefficients

(8.6)

\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{\ell})=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{r!}\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}=\ell}\frac{\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{j_{1}})\cdots\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{j_{r}})}{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}(\log p)^{r}},\qquad j_{i}\in\mathbb{N}.

Applying (
8.3
), we obtain (
8.4
) since

|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{\ell})|\leq\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\frac{[(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)]^{r}}{r!}\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}=\ell}\frac{1}{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}=d_{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}(p^{\ell}).

Lastly, we prove (
8.5
). For all prime powers
p^{\ell}
, we have that

\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{\ell})=(-1)^{\ell}\sum_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{\ell}}\alpha_{j_{1},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)\cdots\alpha_{j_{\ell},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p),\qquad 1\leq j_{i}\leq(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1).

In particular,
\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{\ell})=0
for
\ell>(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)
. Also, if
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
, then

|\mu(p^{\ell})|\leq\binom{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}{\ell}\leq(\ell+1)^{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}=d_{2}(p^{\ell})^{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}.

Thus, because there are only finitely many
p\mid N_{1}N_{2}
, we deduce by the multiplicativity of
\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)
and
d_{2}(n)
that (
8.5
) holds.
∎

8.3.
Sums of Dirichlet Coefficients

We now address sums of the form

\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(n).

We have the following lemma that is sufficient for the regime of conductors used in Vaughan’s method in
Section

9
.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q_{\chi}
with
(q_{\chi},N_{1}N_{2})=1
.
Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{2}{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)})
and
c(\theta)=\frac{2-(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)\theta}{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)+1}

We have that if
q_{\chi}\leq x^{\theta}
, then for all
\varepsilon>0

\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi)}(n)\ll_{m_{1},m_{2},\theta,\varepsilon}x^{1-c(\theta)+\varepsilon}

holds uniformly over all such
\chi
.

Proof.

By
Equation

7.5
,
Equation

8.4
, and the fact that
d_{r}(n)\ll_{r,\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}
, one has that

|\lambda_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi)}(n)|\leq d_{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}(n)\ll_{m_{1},m_{2},\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}.

Therefore,

satisfies the hypotheses of
[
13
, Proposition 1.1]
.
Therefore, we have for
x\geq q_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi)}^{1/2}
that

\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi)}\ll q_{\chi}^{\frac{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)}{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)+1}}x^{\frac{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)-1}{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)+1}+\varepsilon}\leq x^{1-c(\theta)+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant only depends on

, and the Langlands parameters of
L(s,\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi))
.
One may observe that the Langlands parameters only depend on the weights

of

respectively and
\chi(-1)=\pm 1
.
Therefore, the implied constant only depends on
f_{1},f_{2},m_{1},m_{2},\varepsilon
.

In order to handle

) that

q_{\Sym^{m_{1}}f_{1}\times(\Sym^{m_{2}}f_{2}\otimes\chi)}^{1/2}\ll_{m_{1},m_{2}}q_{\chi}^{(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)/2}\leq x^{\theta(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)/2}.

Since
\theta(m_{1}+1)(m_{2}+1)/2<1
, this range of

is bounded in terms of

\theta
, we are done.
∎

.

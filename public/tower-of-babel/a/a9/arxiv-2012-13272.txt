Abstract
. This note intends to demonstrate how to discuss scalar curvature functions’ admissibility on bundles by directly applying some of the Kazdan–Warner results. Proofs of the concept include determining which functions are realizable as scalar curvature functions on the total space of several bundles over exotic manifolds. In addition, we employ traditional variational methods to provide reasonable conditions to smooth functions on the total spaces of some fiber bundles to be realized as scalar curvature functions for some Riemannian submersions metrics. We apply these last results to bundles over Calabi–Yau manifolds.

3.
Prescribing scalar curvature via general vertical warpings

Our goal in this section is to study how to prescribe scalar curvature in fiber bundles which are locally isometric to products. To this aim, we resort to a natural generalization of the
canonical variation
, called
general vertical warping
. Given a Riemannian submersion
\pi:(\overline{M},\ga)\to(M,\textsl{h})
and a smooth function
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the metric
\ga_{\phi}
along the same lines as the canonical variation:

\ga_{\phi}=\ga\Big|_{\mathcal{H}{\times}\mathcal{H}}+e^{2\phi}\ga\Big|_{\mathcal{V}{\times}\mathcal{V}}.

The submersion
\pi:(\overline{M},\ga_{\phi})\to(M,\textsl{h})
is Riemannian and its fibers are
e^{2\phi}
-homothetic to the original fibers in
(\overline{M},\ga)
. The main result in this section is Proposition
3.1
, from which
Theorem
F
follows.

In order to well introduce its statement, define

(8)

\displaystyle b_{k}:=\dfrac{k+1}{8k},~c_{k}:=\dfrac{(k+1)^{2}}{8(k-1)k},~\theta_{k}:=2\dfrac{k-1}{k+1}.

In what follows, the superscripts consistently denote the dimensions of the underlined manifolds.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\pi:F^{k}\hookrightarrow(\overline{M}^{n+k},\ga)\to(M^{n},\textsl{h})
be a Riemannian submersion from a closed oriented manifold
\overline{M}
. Assume that
(\overline{M},\ga)
is locally isometric to a product whose fibers have constant scalar curvature
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

(a)

If
c>0
and
\min_{\overline{M}}\,\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}>0
then any basic smooth function
is the scalar curvature of some general vertical warping metric;

(b)

Assume
c\leq 0
and suppose that a basic function
f:\overline{M}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the condition:

(9)

\displaystyle-b_{k}\left(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}\right)+c_{k}c\left(\mathrm{vol}(M)\right)^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\geq 0.

If
|c|>0
, then
f
is the scalar curvature of some general vertical warping metric. In contrast, if
c=0
, there exists some
A\geq 0
such that
f+A
is the scalar curvature of a general vertical warping metric.

Before proceeding to the proof, we observe that the scope of application of Proposition
3.1
is broader than local products of the form
M\times F
. Indeed, every warped product
(\overline{M},\ga)=(M\times F,\textsl{h}\times_{\phi}\ga_{f})
admits a metric where it is locally a product. Lemma
3.1
also holds for submersions which are locally warped products (see Section
4
for further details).

3.1.
The case
c\leq 0

To begin with, assume that
(M^{n},\textsl{h})
is a closed Riemannian manifold and
(F,\ga_{F})
is a Riemannian manifold with constant scalar curvature
c\leq 0
. Let
f:M\times F\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth basic function. We start by discussing the proof of Proposition
3.1
to the case where
\overline{M}
is a Riemannian product. To do so, we apply standard variational methods to guarantee the existence of positive solutions for the following PDE:

(10)

\Delta_{\textsl{h}}u+\dfrac{k+1}{4k}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})u-\dfrac{k+1}{4k}u^{\frac{k-3}{k+1}}c=0.

Equation (
10
) is obtained from a general vertical warping through Lemma
3.2
, a proof of that is obtained as a consequence of the proof of Lemma
3.4
, to be stated and proved later in this paper.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M,\textsl{h})
and
(F,\ga_{F})
be Riemannian manifolds and
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Denote by
\ga
the warped metric of
M\times_{e^{2\phi}}F
. Then the scalar curvature of
\ga
is given by

\mathrm{scal}_{\ga}=\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}+e^{-2\phi}\mathrm{scal}_{\ga_{F}}-k(k-1)|\nabla\phi|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}-2k|\nabla\phi|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}-2k\Delta_{\textsl{h}}\phi.

Indeed, given a basic function
f\in C^{\infty}(M\times F;\mathbb{R})
, consider the PDE

(11)

\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}+e^{-2\phi}\mathrm{scal}_{\ga_{F}}-f=k(k-1)|\nabla\phi|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}+2k\left\{|\nabla\phi|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}+\Delta_{\textsl{h}}\phi\right\}.

The solution
\phi
is such that
f
is the scalar curvature of the warped metric
\widetilde{\ga}=\textsl{h}+e^{2\phi}\ga_{F}
(this is a consequence of the to-be-proved Lemma
3.4
). By setting
\phi=\log u^{\frac{2}{k+1}}
a direct computation shows that equation (
11
) is reduced to equation (
10
).

To show that the PDE (
10
) has a positive solution, assume that
f,\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}
are continuous functions. Define the following functional
J:W^{1,2}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
by

(12)

\displaystyle J(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}-b_{k}\int_{M}\left(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}\right)u^{2}+c_{k}\int_{M}cu^{\theta_{k}}.

Note that
J
is well defined and, according to the classical Sobolev Embedding Theorem, it is of class
C^{1}
.

3.1.1.
The case
c<0

Assume that
c<0
. We will obtain the desired solution
u
to equation (
10
) as a minimum for
J
restricted to the set
\mathbf{M}:=\{u\in W^{1,2}(M):u\geq 0,~\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}=1\}
. This is the content of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Define
\mathbf{M}:=\{u\in W^{1,2}(M):u\geq 0,~\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}=1\}.
Assume that
f,\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}
are continuous functions, that
c<0
and that the following inequality holds

(13)

\displaystyle-b_{k}\left(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}\right)+c_{k}c\left(\mathrm{vol}(M)\right)^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\geq 0.

Then,

(1)

there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that
J\big|_{\mathbf{M}}>c_{0},

(2)

J\big|_{\mathbf{M}}
is coercive,

(3)

\mathbf{M}
is weakly closed and
J\big|_{\mathbf{M}}
is weakly lower semi-continuous.

Before proving it, let us show how Proposition
3.1
follows from it. Lemma
3.3
provides that
J\big|_{\mathbf{M}}
has a minimum point in
\mathbf{M}
. Therefore, we have a Lagrange Multiplier problem: there exists
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(14)

\int_{M}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle-2b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})uv+cc_{k}\theta_{k}\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}-1}v=\theta_{k}\Lambda\left(\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}-1}v\right),

\forall v\in W^{1,2}(M)
.
We claim that
\Lambda\geq 0
: by taking
v=u
, we have

\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}-2b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})u^{2}+cc_{k}\theta_{k}\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}=\theta_{k}\Lambda\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}

However, since
\theta_{k}\leq 2
the Hölder’s inequality implies

\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}=\left(\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}\right)^{2/\theta_{k}}\leq\left(\mathrm{vol}(M)\right)^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\int_{M}u^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\theta_{k}\Lambda\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\int_{M}\left(-b_{k}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})+\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}cc_{k}(\mathrm{vol}(M))^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\right)u^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{M}\left(-b_{k}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})+cc_{k}(\mathrm{vol}(M))^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\right)u^{2}\geq 0,

so
\Lambda\geq 0
.

We now multiply both sides of (
14
) by
\zeta>0
to get

\int_{M}\langle\nabla(\zeta u),\nabla v\rangle-2b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})(\zeta u)v+\zeta^{-\theta_{k}+2}(cc_{k}-\Lambda)\theta_{k}\int_{M}(\zeta u)^{\theta_{k}-1}v=0,

\forall v\in W^{1,2}(M)
. But since
\Lambda\geq 0
we can assume that
\zeta
is such that
\zeta^{-\theta_{k}+2}(cc_{k}-\Lambda)=cc_{k}
, concluding that
\tilde{u}=\zeta u
is a weak solution for equation (
10
).

To finish the argument, note that if
f
and
\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}
are smooth functions, the classical regularity theory for elliptic PDEs (see
[
Aub98
, Theorem 3.58, p. 87]
) guarantees that the solution
u
is smooth. Finally, we observe that the solution
u
is positive. Otherwise, a Maximum Principle argument, as in
[
Aub98
, Proposition 3.75, p.98]
guarantees
u\equiv 0
.
We thus have proved that any basic smooth function
f:M\times F\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
9
) can be realized as the scalar curvature of a warped product metric, thus concluding item
(2)
of Proposition
3.1
in the case of Riemannian products. It is left to prove Lemma
3.3
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

It is easy to see
\mathbf{M}\neq\emptyset.
Moreover, Poincaré’s Inequality implies that

(15)

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{M}\left(-b_{k}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})+cc_{k}(\mathrm{vol}(M))^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\right)u^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}-b_{k}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})+cc_{k}(\mathrm{vol}(M))^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\right)u^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\mathrm{vol}(M)}\left(\int_{M}u\right)^{2},

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first positive eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\textsl{h}}
. On the other hand, the Hölder’s Inequality gives:

\displaystyle\int_{M}u\leq\mathrm{vol}(M)\left(\int_{M}u^{\theta_{k}}\right)^{1/\theta_{k}}=\mathrm{vol}(M),

leading to an explicit bound for the last term above. We thus conclude that
J(u)
has a lower bound, since
-b_{k}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})+cc_{k}(\mathrm{vol}(M))^{2/\theta_{k}-1}\geq 0
by hypothesis. Likewise, coercivity follows directly from (
15
): If
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\infty
then equation (
15
) implies that
J(u)\rightarrow\infty
. If
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded but
\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\infty
, the same equation ensures the result.

To see that
\mathbf{M}
is weakly closed, take
\{u_{m}\}\subset\mathbf{M}
weakly converging to
u\in W^{1,2}(M).
According to the Rellich–Kondrachov’s Theorem, the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(M)
compactly embeds in
L^{\theta_{k}}(M)
. Hence, since
1=\int_{M}|u_{m}|^{\theta_{k}}\rightarrow\int_{M}|u|^{\theta_{k}}
, we have the result: not only
\int_{M}|u|^{\theta_{k}}=1
but since we have the strong
L^{\theta_{k}}(M)
convergence, hence strong
L^{1}(M)
convergence, it holds that, up to passing to a subsequence,
\{u_{m}\}
pointwise converges to
u
, so
u\geq 0
almost everywhere. These imply that
\mathbf{M}
is weakly closed, as desired.

Finally, to ensure that
J
is weakly lower semi-continuous, note that since
\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}},~\mathrm{scal}_{\ga_{F}}
and
f
are continuous functions and

\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{m}|_{\textsl{h}}^{2},

we conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\to\infty}J(u_{m})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u_{m}|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}-b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})u_{m}^{2}+c_{k}\int_{M}cu_{m}^{\theta_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}-b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})u^{2}+c_{k}\int_{M}cu^{\theta_{k}}=J(u).\qed

3.1.2.
The case
c=0

We finally deal with the case
c=0
. We only sketch it since it follows closely to the proof of Lemma
3.3
as we shall make clear.

In this case, the functional
J
is reduced to

(16)

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{\textsl{h}}^{2}-b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})u^{2}

For this case, we can search for a minimum of
J
restricted to the set

\mathbf{M}=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}(M):\int_{M}u^{2}=1\right\}.

Since the Hölder’s Inequality implies that there is
C>0
such that for any
u\in\mathbf{M}
we have
(\int_{M}u)^{2}\leq C\left(\int u^{2}\right)=C
, it is immediate to see that the Poincaré’s Inequality implies that
J
has a lower-bound restricted to
M
. Moreover, the coercivity also follows under the assumption that
f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}\leq 0
. The same routine argument used in Lemma
3.3
implies that
M
is weakly closed. Therefore,
J
has a critical point in
M
.

Once more, given any function
v\in W^{1,2}(M)
tangent to
\mathbf{M}
, one has a Lagrange Multiplier problem:

(17)

\int_{M}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle-2b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})uv=2\Lambda\int_{M}uv,

for every
v\in W^{1,2}(M)
. Making
v=u
we see that since
-2b_{k}\max_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}})\geq 0
and
\int_{M}u^{2}=1
then
\Lambda\geq 0
.

Now let us rewrite equation (
17
) as

(18)

\int_{M}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle-2b_{k}\int_{M}(f-\mathrm{scal}_{\textsl{h}}+\Lambda b_{k}^{-1})uv=0.

Note that equation (
18
) is the weak formulation of the warped product approach to prescribing the function

f+\Lambda b_{k}^{-1}
, which includes the result defining
A:=+\Lambda b_{k}^{-1}
.

We finish this subsection by sketching the proof of the general case of item
(2)
of Proposition
3.1
– it follows via straightforward modifications of the previous arguments. We start by recalling the following result:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F^{k}\hookrightarrow(\overline{M}^{n+k},\ga)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}(M^{n},\textsl{h})
be a Riemannian submersion. Let
\widetilde{\ga}
be a general vertical warping metric on
M
via the function
u^{\frac{4}{k+1}}
, where
u:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth basic function. Then the scalar curvature of
\widetilde{\ga}
is given by

(19)

\mathrm{scal}_{\widetilde{\ga}}=\mathrm{scal}_{\ga}-\dfrac{4k}{k+1}u^{-1}\Delta_{\textsl{h}}u+\left(4+2(k-1)\right)\frac{2}{k+1}u^{-1}\operatorname{d}\!{u}(H)+\left(u^{-\frac{4}{k+1}}-1\right)\mathrm{scal}_{\ga_{F}}-\left(1-u^{\frac{4}{k+1}}\right)\|A\|^{2},

where
\|A\|^{2}
is a term that depends on the non-integrability of the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}=(\ker\operatorname{d}\!{\pi})^{\perp}
. In particular,
\|A\|^{2}=0
if
\pi
is locally a warped product.

Proof.

We shall use the formulae described in Chapter 2 in
[
GW09
]
. Next,
K
always describes the
non-reduced sectional curvature
for the subscripted metric. Let
T_{i}=e^{-\phi}v_{i},~v_{i}\in\ker\operatorname{d}\!{\pi}
and
X\in\mathcal{H}.
Then,

\displaystyle{\widetilde{K}}(e^{-\phi}v_{1},e^{-\phi}v_{2})

\displaystyle=(e^{-2\phi}-1)K_{\ga_{F}}(v_{1},v_{2})+K_{\ga}(v_{1},v_{2})-|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle+\operatorname{d}\!{\phi}(\sigma(v_{1},v_{1})+\sigma(v_{2},v_{2})),

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}(X,e^{-\phi}v)

\displaystyle=K_{\ga}(X,v)-(1-e^{2\phi})|A^{*}_{X}v|^{2}-\mathrm{Hess}~\phi(X,X)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{d}\!{\phi}(X)^{2}+2\operatorname{d}\!{\phi}(X)g(S_{X}v,v).

Take a
\ga
-orthonormal basis

4.2.
Polar foliations and Calabi–Yau bundles

Observe that a manifold is locally a metric product if it admits some cover that splits as a metric product. Specifically,
(\overline{M},\ga)

We recall a result in Gromoll–Walschap

]

(Proposition 2.2.1,
[
GW09
]
)
.

be a Riemannian submersion. Then

(2)

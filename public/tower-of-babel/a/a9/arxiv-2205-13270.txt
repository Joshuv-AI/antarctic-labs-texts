Abstract
For graphs G G and H H , an H H -coloring of G G is an edge-preserving mapping from V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) to V ⁡ ( H ) V(H) . In the H H -Coloring problem the graph H H is fixed and we ask whether an instance graph G G admits an H H -coloring. A generalization of this problem is H H -ColoringExt , where some vertices of G G are already mapped to vertices of H H and we ask if this partial mapping can be extended to an H H -coloring. We study the complexity of variants of H H -Coloring in F F -free graphs, i.e., graphs excluding a fixed graph F F as an induced subgraph. For integers a , b , c ⩾ 1 a,b,c\geqslant 1 , by S a , b , c S_{a,b,c} we denote the graph obtained by identifying one endvertex of three paths on a + 1 a+1 , b + 1 b+1 , and c + 1 c+1 vertices, respectively. For odd k ⩾ 5 k\geqslant 5 , by W k W_{k} we denote the graph obtained from the k k -cycle by adding a universal vertex. As our main algorithmic result we show that W 5 W_{5} -ColoringExt is polynomial-time solvable in S 2 , 1 , 1 S_{2,1,1} -free graphs. This result exhibits an interesting non-monotonicity of H H -ColoringExt with respect to taking induced subgraphs of H H . Indeed, W 5 W_{5} contains a triangle, and K 3 K_{3} -Coloring , i.e., classical 3-coloring, is NP -hard already in claw-free (i.e., S 1 , 1 , 1 S_{1,1,1} -free) graphs. Our algorithm is based on two main observations: 1. W 5 W_{5} -ColoringExt in S 2 , 1 , 1 S_{2,1,1} -free graphs can be in polynomial time reduced to a variant of the problem of finding an independent set intersecting all triangles, and 2. the latter problem can be solved in polynomial time in S 2 , 1 , 1 S_{2,1,1} -free graphs. We complement this algorithmic result with several negative ones. In particular, we show that W 5 W_{5} -ColoringExt is NP -hard in S 3 , 3 , 3 S_{3,3,3} -free graphs. This is again uncommon, as usually problems that are NP -hard in S a , b , c S_{a,b,c} -free graphs for some constant a , b , c a,b,c are already hard in claw-free graphs.

Introduction

Many computationally hard problems become tractable when restricted to instances with some special properties.
In case of graph problems, typical families of such special instances come from forbidding certain substructures.
For example, for a family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs, a graph
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free
if it does not contain any graph from
\mathcal{F}
as an induced subgraph. If
\mathcal{F}
contains a single graph
F
, then we write
F
-free instead of
\{F\}
-free.
Classes defined by forbidden induced subgraphs are
hereditary
, i.e., closed under vertex deletion.
Conversely, every hereditary class of graphs can be uniquely characterized by a (possibly infinite) set of minimal forbidden induced subgraphs.

Let us define two families of graphs that play a special role as forbidden induced subgraphs.
For
t\geqslant 1
, by
P_{t}
we denote the path in
t
vertices.
For
a,b,c\geqslant 1
, by
S_{a,b,c}
we mean the graph consisting of three paths
P_{a+1},P_{b+1},P_{c+1}
with one endvertex identified. Each graph
S_{a,b,c}
is called a
subdivided claw
.
The smallest subdivided claw, i.e.,
S_{1,1,1}
is the
claw
, and the graph
S_{2,1,1}
is sometimes called the
fork
or the
chair
.
Finally, let
\mathcal{S}
denote the class of graphs whose every component is a path or a subdivided claw.

MIS
and
k
-Coloring
in
F
-free graphs.

Two problems, whose complexity in hereditary graph classes attracts significant attention, are
Max (Weighted) Independent Set
(denoted by
MIS
) and
k
-Coloring
. Let us briefly survey known results, focusing on
F
-free graphs for connected
F
.

By the observation of Alekseev
[
2
]
,
MIS
is
NP
-hard in
F
-free graphs, unless
F\in\mathcal{S}
.
On the positive side, polynomial-time algorithms are known for some small graphs
F\in\mathcal{S}
.
If
F=P_{t}
, then the polynomial-time algorithm for
t\leqslant 5
was provided by Lokshtanov et al.
[
38
]
,
which was later extended to
t\leqslant 6
by Grzesik et al.
[
28
]
.
The case of
t=7
remains open and the general belief is that for every
t
the problem is polynomial-time solvable.
The evidence is given by the existence of quasipolynomial-time algorithms
[
25
,
46
]
.

The polynomial-time algorithm for
MIS
in claw-free graphs
[
47
,
42
]
can be obtained by an extension of the augmenting path approach used for finding largest matchings
[
16
]
; note that a maximum matching is precisely a largest independent set in the line graph, and line graphs are in particular claw-free.
There are also more modern approaches, based on certain decompositions of claw-free graphs
[
18
,
43
]
.
A polynomial-time algorithm for
MIS
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs was first obtained by Alekseev
[
3
]
(only for the unweighted case),
and later an arguably simpler algorithm was provided by Lozin and Milanič
[
40
]
(also for the weighted case).
Again, the complexity of
MIS
in
F
-free graphs for larger subdivided claws
F
remains open, but all these cases are believed to be tractable.
This belief is supported by the existence of a subexponential-time algorithm
[
11
,
41
]
, a QPTAS
[
12
,
11
,
41
]
, or a polynomial-time algorithm in the bounded-degree case
[
1
]
.

If it comes to
k
-Coloring
, then it follows from known results that if
F
is not a forest of paths,
then for every
k\geqslant 3
the problem is
NP
-hard in
F
-free graphs
[
26
,
37
,
17
,
33
]
.
The complexity of
k
-Coloring
in
P_{t}
-free graphs is quite well understood.
For
t=5
, the problem is polynomial-time solvable for every constant
k

[
31
]
.
If
k\geqslant 5
, then the problem is
NP
-hard already in
P_{6}
-free graphs
[
34
]
.
The case of
k=4
is polynomial-time solvable for
t\leqslant 6

[
49
]
and
NP
-hard for
t\geqslant 7

[
34
]
.
The case of
k=3
is much more elusive.
We know a polynomial-time algorithm for
P_{7}
-free graphs
[
5
]
and the cases for all
t\geqslant 8
are open.
The general belief that they should be tractable is again supported by the existence of a quasipolynomial-time algorithm
[
46
]
.

Let us mention two generalizations of
k
-Coloring
.
In the
k
-ColoringExt
problem we are given a graph
G
with a subset of its vertices colored using
k
colors,
and we ask whether this partial coloring can be extended to a proper
k
-coloring of
G
.
In the even more general
List

k
-Coloring
problem, each vertex of the instance graph
G
is equipped with a subset of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
called
list
, and we ask whether there exists a proper
k
-coloring of
G
respecting all lists.
Clearly any tractability result for a more general problem implies the same result for a less general one,
and any hardness result for a less general problem implies the same hardness for more general variants.
In almost all mentioned cases the algorithms for
k
-Coloring
generalize to
List

k
-Coloring
.
The only exception is the case
k=4
and
t=6
: the polynomial time algorithm for
4
-Coloring
in
P_{6}
-free graphs can be generalized to
4
-ColoringExt

[
49
]
, but
List

4
-Coloring
in this class is
NP
-hard
[
27
]

Minimal obstructions.

One of the ways of designing polynomial-time algorithms for
k
-Coloring
is to check is the instance graph contains some (hopefully small) subgraph that is not
k
-colorable. This approach is formalized by the notion of
critical graphs
.
A graph
G
is
(k+1)
-vertex critical
if it is not
k
-colorable, but its every induced subgraph is. Such graphs can be thought of
minimal obstructions
to
k
coloring: a graph
G
is
k
-colorable if and only if it does not contain any
(k+1)
-vertex-critical graph as an induced subgraph.
Thus if for some hereditary class
\mathcal{G}
of graphs, the number of
(k+1)
-vertex critical graphs is finite,
we immediately obtain a polynomial-time algorithm for
k
-Coloring
in graphs from
\mathcal{G}
.
Indeed, it is sufficient
to check if the instance graph contains any
(k+1)
-vertex-critical induced subgraph, which can be done in polynomial time by brute-force.
Such an algorithm, in addition to solving the instance, provides a
certificate
in case of the negative answer – a constant-size subgraph which does not admit a proper
k
-coloring.
Thus the question whether for some class
\mathcal{G}
, the number of
(k+1)
-vertex critical graphs is finite,
can be seen as a refined analysis of the polynomial cases of
k
-Coloring
.

The finiteness of the families of
(k+1)
-vertex critical in
F
-free graphs is fully understood.
Recall that the only interesting (i.e., not known to be
NP
-hard) cases are for
F
being a forest of paths.
Again focusing on connected
F
, i.e.,
k
-Coloring
of
P_{t}
-free graphs,
we know that the families of minimal obstructions are finite for
t\leqslant 6
and
k=4

[
8
]
,
and for
t\leqslant 4
and any
k
. The latter result follows from the fact that
P_{4}
-free graphs are perfect and thus the only obstruction for
k
-coloring is
K_{k+1}
.
In all other cases there are constructions of infinite families of minimal obstructions
[
8
,
32
]
.

Graph homomorphisms in
F
-free graphs.

A homomorphism from a graph
G
to a graph
H
is a mapping from
V(G)
to
V(H)
that preserves edges, i.e.,
the image of every edge of
G
is an edge of
H
.
Note that if
H
is the complete graph on
k
vertices, then homomorphisms to
H=K_{k}
are exactly proper
k
-colorings.
For this reason homomorphisms to
H
are called
H
-colorings
, we will also refer to vertices of
H
as
colors
.
In the
H
-Coloring
problem the graph
H
is fixed and we need to decide whether an instance graph
G
admits a homomorphism to
H
.
By the analogy to coloring, we also define more general variants, i.e.,
H
-ColoringExt
and
List

H
-Coloring
.
In the former problem we ask whether a given partial mapping from vertices of an instance graph
G
to
V(H)
can be extended to a homomorphism,
and in the latter one each vertex of
G
is equipped with a list which is a subset of
V(H)
and we look for a homomorphism respecting all lists.

The complexity of
H
-Coloring
was settled by Hell and Nešetřil
[
30
]
: the problem is polynomial-time solvable if
H
is bipartite or has a vertex with a loop, and
NP
-hard otherwise. The dichotomy is also known for
List

H
-Coloring

[
21
,
22
,
23
]
: this time the tractable cases are the so-called
bi-arc graphs
.
The case of
H
-ColoringExt
is more tricky. The classification follows from the celebrated proof of the CSP complexity dichotomy
[
7
,
53
]
,
but the graph-theoretic description of polynomial cases is unknown (and probably difficult to obtain).

We are very far from understanding the complexity of variants of
H
-Coloring
in
F
-free graphs.
Chudnovsky et al.
[
9
]
showed that
List

C_{k}
-Coloring
for
k\in\{5,7\}\cup[9,\infty)
is polynomial-time solvable in
P_{9}
-free graphs. On the negative side, they showed that for every
k\geqslant 5
the problem is
NP
-hard in
F
-free graphs,
unless
F\in\mathcal{S}
.
This negative result was later extended by Piecyk and Rzążewski
[
45
]
who showed that if
H
is not a bi-arc graph, then
List

H
-Coloring
is
NP
-hard and cannot be solved in subexponential time (assuming the ETH) in
F
-free graphs, unless
F\in\mathcal{S}
.

The case of forbidden path or subdivided claw was later investigated by Okrasa and Rzążewski
[
44
]
.
They defined a class of
predacious graphs

and showed that if
H
is not predacious, then for every
t
, the
List

H
-Coloring
problem
can be solved in quasipolynomial time in
P_{t}
-free graphs.
On the other hand, for every predacious
H
there exists
t

for which
List

H
-Coloring
cannot be solved in subexponential time in
P_{t}
-free graphs unless the ETH fails.
They also provided some partial results for the case of forbidden subdivided claws.

Finally, Chudnovsky et al.
[
10
]
considered a generalization of
List

H
-Coloring
in
P_{5}
-free graphs and related classes.

The notion of vertex-critical graphs can be naturally translated to
H
-colorings.
A graph
G
is a
minimal
H
-obstruction
if it is not
H
-colorable, but its every induced subgraph is.
Minimal
H
-obstructions in restricted graph classes were studied by some authors, but the results are rather scattered
[
35
,
4
]
.

Our motivation.

Let us point a substantial difference between working with
H
-Coloring
and working with
List

H
-Coloring
(with
H
-ColoringExt
being somewhere between, but closer to
H
-Coloring
).
The
List

H
-Coloring
problem enjoys certain monotonicity: if
H^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
H
,
then every instance of
List

H^{\prime}
-Coloring
can be seen as an instance of
List

H
-Coloring
, where no vertex from
V(H)-V(H^{\prime})
appears in any list. Thus any tractability result for
List

H
-Coloring
implies the analogous result for
List

H^{\prime}
-Coloring
,
while any hardness result for
List

H^{\prime}
-Coloring
applies also to
List

H
-Coloring
.
In particular, all hardness proofs for
List

H
-Coloring
follow the same pattern: first we identify a (possibly infinite) family
\mathcal{H}
of “minimal hard cases”
and then show hardness of
List

H^{\prime}
-Coloring
for every
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
. This implies that
List

H
-Coloring
is hard unless
H
is
\mathcal{H}
-free.
The lists are also useful in the design of algorithms: for example if for some reason we decide that some vertex
v\in V(G)
must be mapped to
x\in V(H)
, we can remove from the lists of neighbors of
v
all non-neighbors of
x
, and then delete
v
from the instance graph.
This combines well with e.g. branching algorithms or divide-&-conquer algorithms based on the existence of separators.

In contrast, when coping with
H
-Coloring
we need to think about the global structure of
H
.
This makes working with this variant of the problem much more complicated. In particular, hardness proofs often employ certain algebraic tools
[
30
,
6
]
, which in turn do not combine well with the world of
F
-free graphs.
However, note that the complexity dichotomy for
H
-Coloring

is still monotone
with respect to taking induced subgraphs of
H
:
the minimal
NP
-hard cases are odd cycles.

An interesting example of non-monotonicity of
H
-Coloring
was provided by Feder and Hell in an unpublished manuscript
[
20
]
.
For an odd integer
k\geqslant 5
, let
W_{k}
denote the
k
-wheel
, i.e., the graph obtained from the
k
-cycle by adding a universal vertex.
Feder and Hell proved that
W_{5}
-Coloring
is polynomial-time solvable in line graphs.
This is quite surprising as
W_{5}
contains a triangle and
K_{3}
-Coloring
, i.e.,
3
-Coloring
, is
NP
-hard in line graphs
[
33
]
.
(Note that this implies that
List

W_{5}
-Coloring
is
NP
-hard in line graphs.)
Feder and Hell also proved that for any odd
k\geqslant 7
, the
W_{k}
-Coloring
problem is
NP
-hard in line graphs
[
20
]
.

Our contribution.

In this paper we study to which extent the result of Feder and Hell
[
20
]
can be generalized.
We provide an algorithm and a number of lower bounds, each of a different kind.

The main algorithmic contribution of our paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

The
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
problem can be solved in polynomial time in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs.

Let us sketch the outline of the proof.
Perhaps surprisingly, despite the fact that graph homomorphisms generalize graph colorings,
our approach is much closer to the algorithms for
MIS
.

Consider any homomorphism
\varphi
from
G
to
W_{5}
and let
X
be the set of vertices of
G
mapped by
\varphi
to the universal vertex of
W_{5}
.
We notice that
X
is independent, and
G-X
admits a homomorphism to
C_{5}
.
This is exactly how we look at the problem: we aim to find an independent set
X
whose removal makes the graph
C_{5}
-colorable
(and make sure that the precoloring of vertices is respected).

So let us focus on describing the structure of
G-X
, i.e., recognizing
C_{5}
-colorable graphs.
Note that here we need to use the fact that our graph is
S_{2,1,1}
-free,
as
C_{5}
-Coloring
is
NP
-hard in general graphs.
As a warm-up let us assume that our instance is claw-free and forget about precolored vertices.
We observe that every
C_{5}
-colorable graph must be triangle-free, as there is no homomorphism from
K_{3}
to
C_{5}
.
But since
G-X
is
\{S_{1,1,1},K_{3}\}
-free, it must be of maximum degree at most 2, i.e.,
every component of
G-X
is a path or a cycle with at least 4 vertices.
It is straightforward to verify that such graphs always admit a homomorphism to
C_{5}
.
Therefore in claw-free graphs, solving
W_{5}
-Coloring
boils down to finding an independent set intersecting all triangles.

We extend this simple observation in two ways. First, we show that the same phenomenon occurs in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs:
the only
S_{2,1,1}
-free minimal
C_{5}
-obstruction is the triangle.
Second, we show that the same way we can handle precolored vertices:
the only no-instances of
C_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
\{S_{2,1,1},K_{3}\}
-free graphs can be easily recognized.
Based on this result we show that
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs can be in polynomial time
reduced to the
Independent Triangle Transversal Extension
(
ITTE
) problem on an induced subgraph of the original instance.
Here, the “extension” means that some vertices of our instance can be prescribed to be in
X
or outside
X
.
Thus from now on we focus on solving
ITTE
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs.
Note that we still need to use the fact that our instances are
S_{2,1,1}
-free, as
ITTE
is
NP
-hard in general graphs
[
19
]
.

We start with the case that our instance graph
G
is claw-free.
We use the result of Chudnovsky and Seymour
[
13
]
who show that each claw-free graph admits certain decomposition called a
strip structure
.
Roughly speaking, this means that
G
“resembles” the line graph of some graph
D
: the vertices of
G
can be partitioned
into sets
\eta(e)
assigned to the edges
e
of
D
, such that (i) for each
e\in E(D)
the set
\eta(e)
induces a subgraph of
G
with a simple structure
and (ii) the interactions between sets
\eta(e)
and
\eta(f)
for distinct edges
e,f
are well-defined.
Due to property (i), for each edge
e
of
D
we can solve the problem in the subgraph of
G
induced by
\eta(e)
in polynomial time.
Then we can use property (ii) to combine these partial solutions into the final one by finding an appropriate matching in an auxiliary graph derived from
D
.

As the final step, we lift our algorithm for claw-free graphs to the class of
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs.
We use the observation of Lozin and Milanič
[
40
]
:
they show that if
G
is
S_{2,1,1}
-free and
prime
, then every
prime
induced subgraph of the graph obtained from
G
by removing any vertex and its neighbors is claw-free (the exact definition of prime graphs can be found in
section

4
).
Using this observation we can reduce the
ITTE
problem on
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs to the same problem in the class of claw-free graphs,
which we already know how to solve in polynomial time.

Combining the reduction from
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs to
ITTE
in
S_{2,1,1,}
-free graphs
and the polynomial-time algorithm for
ITTE
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs we obtain
theorem

1
.
Let us point out that the frontier of the complexity of
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
S_{a,b,c}
-free graphs is the same as for
MIS
: the minimal open cases are
S_{3,1,1}
and
S_{2,2,1}
.

In the remainder of the paper we investigate several possible generalizations of
theorem

1
and show a number of negative results.

First, one can ask whether a simpler algorithm, at least for
W_{5}
-Coloring
, can be obtained by showing that the family of minimal
S_{2,1,1}
-free
W_{5}
-obstructions is finite. We show that this is not the case: we construct an infinite family of minimal
W_{5}
-obstructions (actually,
W_{k}
-obstructions for every odd
k\geqslant 5
) which are even claw-free.

Next, we show that
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
S_{3,3,3}
-free graphs of maximum degree 5 is
NP
-hard and cannot be solved in subexponential time, unless the ETH fails.
This should be contrasted with the fact that for any
a,b,c
, the
MIS
problem in
S_{a,b,c}
-free graphs can be solved in subexponential time
[
11
,
41
]
,
and even in polynomial time, if the instance is of bounded maximum degree
[
1
]
.
These facts are an evidence that
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
S_{a,b,c}
-free graphs for large
a,b,c
cannot be solved with the tools developed for
MIS
, as it is the case for
a=2
and
b=c=1
.
Again, we find our hardness result quite surprising, as typically problems that are hard in
S_{a,b,c}
-free graphs for some fixed
a,b,c
are already had in claw-free graphs.

Finally, we consider the complexity of variants of
W_{k}
-Coloring
in
F
-free graphs.
We remark that for every
k
, the
List

W_{k}
-Coloring
problem can be solved in polynomial time in
P_{5}
-free graphs
[
10
]

Furthermore for every
k
the graph
W_{k}
is not predacious, so by the result of Okrasa and Rzążewski
[
44
]
,

List

W_{k}
-Coloring
can be solved in quasipolynomial time in
P_{t}
-free graphs, for every fixed
t
.
We show that forbidding any other connected graph leads to an
NP
-hard problem,
with possible exception of
W_{5}\textsc{-Coloring}
in
S_{a,b,c}
-free graphs.

Theorem 2
.

Let
F
be a connected graph.

1.

If
F
is not a path nor a subdivided claw, then the
W_{5}
-Coloring
problem is
NP
-hard in
F
-free graphs and cannot be solved in subexponential time, unless the ETH fails.

2.

Let
k\geqslant 7
be odd. If
F
is not a path, then the
W_{k}
-Coloring
problem is
NP
-hard in
F
-free graphs and cannot be solved in subexponential time, unless the ETH fails.

The paper is concluded with pointing our some open problems and possible directions for future research.

Remark
.

The curious reader might wonder why we only consider
k
-wheels for odd
k\geqslant 5
.
The 3-wheel is exactly
K_{4}
, and homomorphisms to
K_{4}
are exactly proper 4-colorings.
As mentioned above, both
4
-Coloring
and
4
-ColoringExt
are well studied in hereditary graph classes and behave substantially differently than
W_{k}
-Coloring
for odd

We start with the analysis of

-free instances of

-ColoringExt

3.1
C_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
\{S_{2,1,1},K_{3}\}
-free graphs

A connected bipartite graph is called

if it is complete or can be obtained from a complete bipartite graph by removing the edges of some matching.

Observation 5
.

Let
G
be a connected
\{S_{2,1,1},K_{3}\}
-free graph.
Then either (i)
G
is a path, or (ii)
G
a cycle of length at least 5, or (iii)
G
is an almost complete bipartite graph.

Proof.

We consider the following cases.

Case 1:
G
does not have cycles.

Hence, if
G
is not a path, it has a vertex
v
of degree at least 3.
However, if now there exists a vertex that is non-adjacent to
v
, then, as
G
is connected, there exists a vertex
u
at distance 2 from
v
.
If
w
is the common neighbor of
u
and
v
, and
v_{1},v_{2}
are some other two neighbors of
v
(that are non-adjacent to
u
as
G
does not have cycles), then
\{v,v_{1},v_{2},u,w\}
induces a copy of
S_{2,1,1}
.
Hence,
G
is a tree that has a universal vertex
v
, so

must be a star, i.e., an almost complete bipartite graph.

Case 2:
G
is non-bipartite or
C_{4}
-free.

If
G
is bipartite, let
k\geqslant 6
be the length of a shortest induced cycle in
G
.
Such a cycle must exist, since
G
is
C_{4}
-free and contains a cycle of length at least 6, so also an induced cycle of length at least 6.
If
G
is non-bipartite, let

be the length of a shortest induced odd cycle in
G
.
Let

be an induced cycle of length

in
G
, clearly

.
Denote by
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}
the consecutive vertices of
C
.

We claim that
V(C)=V(G)
.
Suppose there exists a vertex in
G
that does not belong to
C
. Since
G
is connected, some vertex, say
v_{1}
, of
C
has a neighbor, say
u
, which is not in
C
.
Since
G
is
K_{3}
-free and
C
is an induced cycle of length
k\geqslant 5
, the graph induced by the set of vertices
\{u,v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{k}\}
is a copy of
S_{2,1,1}
unless
uv_{3}
is an edge in
G
.
So since now assume that
uv_{3}
is an edge in
G
. Then in particular
\{u,v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
induces a cycle
C_{4}
. Hence if
G
is bipartite, we get a contradiction and in this case we conclude that
G
is a cycle of length at least
6
.
If
G
is not bipartite, we similarly show that
uv_{k-1}
is an edge in G. Thus,
u,v_{3},v_{4},\ldots,v_{k-1}
are consecutive vertices of a cycle of length
k-2
in
G
.
We found an odd cycle in

of length smaller than

.
Consequently, there is an induced odd cycle in
G
which is shorter than the cycle

, a contradiction.
Thus,

is an odd cycle of length at least
5
, and that completes the proof.

Case 3:
G
is bipartite and contains a cycle
C_{4}
.

Let us denote the bipartition classes of
G
by
V
and
U
and let
C
be a copy of
C_{4}
contained in

. Denote by
v_{1},v_{2}
(respectively,

) the vertices of

in
V
(resp. in

).

Suppose there is a vertex in
G
which is not a neighbor of any vertex in
C
. By connectivity of

there is a vertex, say
x
, at distance
2
from
C
. We can assume without loss of generality that
v_{1}
is a vertex in
C
at distance
2
from
x
. Let
y
be a common neighbor of
v_{1}
and
x
. It is easy to see that the graph induced by the set of vertices
\{x,y,v_{1},u_{1},u_{2}\}
is a copy of
S_{2,1,1}
, a contradiction. Thus, every vertex in
G
is a neighbor of some vertex of
C
. Consequently, every vertex of
U
(resp.

) is adjacent to
v_{1}
or
v_{2}
(resp.
u_{1}
or
u_{2}
).

Assume now that
v_{1}
has at least two neighbors, say
w_{1},w_{2}
which are not neighbors of
v_{2}
. Then the set of vertices
\{v_{2},u_{1},v_{1},w_{1},w_{2}\}
induces a copy of

, a contradiction. Thus, there is at most one vertex which is adjacent to

but not to
v_{2}
. We denote this vertex by

if it exists. Similarly, there is at most one vertex adjacent to
v_{2}
(resp.

) but not to

(resp.

). We denote this vertex by
u_{2}^{\prime}
(resp.

) if it exists.
We have shown that each vertex
v_{1},v_{2}
(resp.

) is adjacent to all but at most one vertex of

(resp.

).

Suppose now that

is non-adjacent not only to

but to some other vertex

as well. Clearly, at least one of the vertices

is adjacent to

because otherwise

is not a neighbor of any vertex of

. Assume without loss of generality that
u_{1}
is adjacent to

. Since

is adjacent to

, the set of vertices

, a contradiction. Thus,

is adjacent to all vertices of

. We similarly show that

(resp.

(resp.

).

Finally, suppose that some vertex

is non-adjacent to some two vertices, say

of

. Then, the set of vertices
\{v,u_{1},v_{1},x_{1},x_{2}\}
induces a copy of

, a contradiction. Thus, every vertex of

is adjacent to all vertices of

except possibly one. We similarly show that every vertex of

is adjacent to all vertices of

except possibly one. Thus,

is an almost complete bipartite graph.

We denote the consecutive vertices of
C_{5}
by
1,2,3,4,5
. We will refer to the vertices of
C_{5}
as colors. We will say that two colors are

if they are neighbors on the cycle

.

Let
G
be a graph, let

, and let
\varphi:W\to V(C_{5})
be a coloring of vertices of
W
.
We say that a pair of vertices
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)
is

if
u,v\in W
and there is an

-

of length at most
3
such that

and

cannot be extended to a homomorphism from

to
C_{5}
.
Equivalently, a pair
\{u,v\}\subseteq W
of vertices of
G
is conflicted in a coloring
\varphi
of

if and only if

(i)

v\in N_{G}(u)
, and
\varphi(v)
is non-adjacent to
\varphi(u)
in
C_{5}
, or

(ii)

there exists a path
u,w,v
in
G
with

, and
\varphi(v)
is adjacent to
\varphi(u)
, or

(iii)

there exists a path
u,w_{1},w_{2},v
in
G
with
w_{1},w_{2}\not\in W
, and
\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)
.

We say that
\varphi
is

, if there is no pair of conflicted vertices in

(with respect to
\varphi
).

Clearly, being triangle-free and conflict-free are necessary conditions to be a yes-instance of
C_{5}
-ColoringExt
.
The following lemma say that in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs they are also sufficient.

6
.

Let
G
be an
\{S_{2,1,1},K_{3}\}
-free graph, let
W\subseteq V(G)
, and let
\varphi:W\to V(C_{5})
.
If
\varphi
is conflict-free, then
\varphi
can be extended to a homomorphism
\psi:G\to C_{5}
.

Proof.

Clearly, by the definition of a conflict-free coloring, it suffices to show that every conflict-free coloring can be extended to a conflict-free coloring of the whole graph
G
. Assume otherwise, i.e. that there is a conflict-free coloring
\varphi:W\to V(C_{5})
with
W\not=V(G)
which cannot be extended to a conflict-free coloring of
G
. We can assume without loss of generality that
\varphi
is maximal with respect to this property, i.e. it is not possible to color any uncolored vertex of
G
so that the resulting coloring is still conflict-free.

By
5
,
G
is a cycle, a path or an almost complete bipartite graph. We consider two cases.

Case 1:
G
is a cycle or a path.

Clearly, we can assume that at least one vertex of
G
is colored.
By maximality of the coloring
\varphi
, if
G
is a path, then each end
v
of this path is colored. Indeed, assume that
v
does not have a color and let
u
be the closest colored vertex. Depending on the distance from
v
to
u
we can always choose a color for
v
so that the pair
\{u,v\}
is not conflicted.

We proved that any uncolored vertex of
G
is an internal vertex of a path in
G
whose only colored vertices are the ends of this path. Consider such a path
P
of the largest length and let
u
and
v
be the colored ends of this path. Let
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
) be the color of
u
(resp.
v
) in the coloring
\varphi
. Denote by
w
the neighbor of
v
on
P
. If
P
has more than
2
internal vertices then we color
w
with a color which is a neighbor of the color
\beta
and is different from the color
\alpha
. Obviously, such a color exists. The resulting extension of the coloring
\varphi
is not conflicted, because
\{u,w\}
is not a conflicted pair in this extension, a contradiction with maximality of
\varphi
.
If
P
has at most
2
internal vertices, then by the fact that
u,v
are not conflicted and the definition of conflicted pair,
\varphi|_{\{u,v\}}
can be extended to a homomorphism from
P
to
C_{5}
. Therefore
\varphi
can be extended to internal vertices of
P
which contradicts the maximality of
\varphi
.
We have shown the lemma if
G
is a path or a cycle.

Case 2:
G
is an almost complete bipartite graph.

We will prove first the following statement.

Claim 6.1
.

A coloring
\varphi:W\to V(C_{5})
of an almost complete bipartite graph
G
is conflict-free if and only if the following conditions are satisfied

(i)

\varphi
is a
C_{5}
-coloring of the induced graph
G[W]
, and

(ii)

if some two vertices
u,v\in W
in the same bipartition class have a common neighbor in
G
then the colors of
u
and
v
are not neighbors, and

(iii)

vertices in different bipartition classes have different colors.

Proof of Claim.
First assume that that
\varphi
is conflict-free. If some two adjacent vertices in
G[W]
are not colored with colors which are neighbors then these vertices form a conflicted pair, a contradiction. Suppose that some two vertices
u,v\in W
are in the same bipartition class and have a common neighbor, say
w
, in
G
. If the colors of
u
and
v
are neighbors and
w\not\in W
then the pair
\{u,v\}
is conflicted by condition (ii) in the definition of a conflicted pair, a contradiction. If the colors of
u
and
v
are neighbors and
w\in W
then the pair
\{u,w\}
or
\{w,v\}
is conflicted by condition (i) in the definition of a conflicted pair, a contradiction again. Finally, assume that some two vertices
u,v
in different bipartition classes have the same color. If
u
and
v
are joined by an edge then the pair
\{u,v\}
is conflicted. Otherwise there is a path
u,w_{1},w_{2},v
in
G
. In this case the pair
\{u,v\}
is conflicted by the condition (iii) or (ii) or (i) in the definition of a conflicted pair, a contradiction. We proved that the conditions (i)-(iii) are satisfied.

Conversely, assume that the conditions (i)-(iii) are satisfied for a coloring
\varphi
. One can readily verify that condition (i) (resp. (ii) and (iii)) implies that condition (i) (resp. (ii) and (iii)) in the definition of a conflicted pair is not satisfied. Thus,
\varphi
is conflict-free which completes the proof of the claim.

\blacksquare

Let
U
and
V
be the bipartition classes of the graph
G
. To prove the lemma for almost complete bipartite graphs we consider two subcases.

Subcase (i): Each two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
in the same bipartition class have a common neighbor.

By
6.1
vertices of
V\cap W
(resp.
U\cap W
) are colored in
\varphi
with at most two colors and if both colors are used then these colors are not neighbors. Moreover, the colors used on vertices of
V\cap W
are different from the colors used on vertices of
U\cap W
. Thus, without loss of generality, we can assume that the colors of vertices of
V\cap W
are elements of the set
\{1,3\}
and the colors of vertices of
U\cap W
are elements of the set
\{2,4\}
. We observe that if both colors

and

are used by the coloring
\varphi
then each of these colors is used on one vertex only and the vertices colored with these colors are non-adjacent in

. Otherwise the coloring
\varphi
is not conflict-free. If some vertex of

(resp.

) is not colored in the coloring
\varphi
, then we color it with color

(resp.

). By

this extension of

is conflict-free, a contradiction with maximality of
\varphi
.

Subcase (ii): Some two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
in the same bipartition class do not have a common neighbor.

We observe that if both bipartition classes of an almost complete bipartite graph

have at least three vertices or

is a star then
each two vertices

in the same bipartition class have a common neighbor in

. So,

has exactly two vertices in some bipartition class, say

, and has at least three vertices in the bipartition class

(recall that

is connected). Moreover, if

is obtained from a complete bipartite graph by removing only one edge then the condition in subcase (ii) is not satisfied either. Thus,

is obtained from a complete bipartite graph with

and

by removing a

, of degree

. This is the only pair of vertices in

which does not have a common neighbor. If, in the coloring

,

or

is not colored or the colors of

and

are not neighbors, then we proceed as in subcase (i). However, it may happen that in the coloring

the colors of the vertices

and

are neighbors, say

and

, respectively. Let

(resp.

) be the neighbor of

(resp.

) in

. It follows from

that if

and

then

is conflict-free if and only if

(see condition (ii) in

),

(see conditions (i) and (iii) in

3.2
The
Independent Triangle Transversal Extension
problem

The following auxiliary problem plays a crucial role in our algorithm.

Independent Triangle Transversal Extension (ITTE)

Instance:

A graph
G
, sets
X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
, and
E^{\prime}\subseteq E(G)

Question:

Is there an independent set
X\subseteq V(G)
, such that (i)
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
, (ii) for every
e\in E^{\prime}
it holds that
e\cap X\neq\emptyset
, (iii)
Y^{\prime}\cap X=\emptyset
, (iv)
G-X
is triangle-free?

We observe that if
G
contains a
K_{4}
, then at most one vertex of such a clique can belong to an independent set, and the graph induced by the remaining part is not triangle-free.
Thus we immediately obtain the following observation which will be used throughout the paper implicitly.

Observation 7
.

If
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is an instance of
ITTE
such that
G
is not
K_{4}
-free, then it is a no-instance.

As the final result of this section we show that
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs
can be reduced in polynomial time to
ITTE
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs.

Theorem 8
.

The
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs can be reduced in polynomial time to the
ITTE
problem in
S_{2,1,1}
-free graphs.

Proof.

Let
(G,U,\varphi)
be an instance of
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
. Note that we can assume that there are no vertices
u,v\in U
such that
uv\in E(G)
and
\varphi(u)\varphi(v)\notin E(W_{5})
, otherwise we immediately report a no-instance (formally, we can return a trivial no-instance of
ITTE
, e.g.,
(K_{4},\emptyset,\emptyset,\emptyset)
). This can be verified in polynomial time.

We define the instance
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
ITTE
as follows. We initialize
X^{\prime}
,
Y^{\prime}
and
E^{\prime}
as empty sets. We add to
X^{\prime}
every vertex precolored with
0
and we add every vertex precolored with vertex other than
0
to
Y^{\prime}
.
To construct
E^{\prime}
, consider each pair
u,v\in U
of conflicted vertices with respect to
\varphi:Y^{\prime}\to[5]
, and let
P
be a witness of
u
and
v
.
By our first assumption,
|P|\geqslant 2
.
If the consecutive vertices of
P
are
u,w,v
, then we add
w
to
X^{\prime}
.
If the consecutive vertices of
P
are
u,w_{1},w_{2},v
, then we add the edge
w_{1}w_{2}
to
E^{\prime}
. This completes the construction of the instance
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
ITTE
. Clearly the reduction is done in polynomial time.

Let us verify that the instance
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
ITTE
is equivalent to the instance
(G,U,\varphi)
of
W_{5}
-ColoringExt
. First assume that there is a set
X\subseteq V(G)
that is a solution to the instance
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
ITTE
. Then
G-X
is triangle-free. Suppose that there is a conflicted pair of vertices
u,v
in
G-X
and let
P
be the path such that the precoloring of
u,v
cannot be extended to
P
. Observe that by the construction of
E^{\prime}
at least one vertex of
P
must be in
X

So now suppose that

-ColoringExt

. Then either

4
Solving
ITTE
: basic toolbox

In this seciton we provide some basic tools to deal with

.
In particular, we prove that the problem behaves well under standard graph decompositions: modular decomposition, clique-cutset decomposition, and tree decomposition.

First, let us prove that in order to solve

it is sufficient to solve instances

of

.

Proposition 9
.

Let
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be an instance of

. We can construct in polynomial time an instance

of

and such that

is an induced subgraph of

Proof.

We initialize
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}:=X^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}:=E^{\prime}
. If there is an edge

with

, then we conclude that we are dealing with a no-instance. If there is an edge

with

and

, then we add

to

. Consider a triangle

. If

and

to

. If

and

, then we add the edge

to
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
. Finally, we set

.
This completes the construction of the instance
(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
. Clearly the construction is performed in polynomial time.

Let us verify the equivalence. First, assume that
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a yes-instance and
X
is a solution. Let us show that

is also a solution for
(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
. Consider a vertex
v\in\widetilde{X}^{\prime}
. If

, then

since

is a solution for
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
. If there is an edge
uv\in E^{\prime}
with

, then it must hold that

since we cannot have
u\in X
. If there is a triangle
uvw
with

, then
y\in X
since

is triangle-free and
u,w
cannot be in

. Now let

. If
uv\in E^{\prime}
, then clearly one of
u,v
must be in
X
. If there is a triangle
uvw
with
w\in Y^{\prime}
, then one of
u,v
must be in
X
since
G-X
is triangle-free. Finally, observe that if
G-X
is triangle-free, then
\widetilde{G}-X
, as an induced subgraph of
G-X
, is also triangle-free.

So now assume that there is a solution
X
for
(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
. We will verify that
X
is also a solution for
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
. First, observe that since

, we have that
X\cap Y^{\prime}=\emptyset
. Consider
u\in X^{\prime}
. Note that

, so

. Now consider

. If
u,v\notin Y^{\prime}
, then

and one of

is in

. If
u\in Y^{\prime}
, then
v\in\widetilde{X}^{\prime}
and thus

. It remains to verify that

is triangle-free. Suppose to the contrary that there is a triangle
uvw
in

. Since
\widetilde{G}-X
is triangle-free, then at least one of
u,v,w
, say

, must be in

. Note that if all vertices

, then we already reported a no-instance. Therefore, either we added an edge
vw
to
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
and one of
v,w
is in

, or we added one of
v,w
, say
v
, to

, and thus

, a contradiction.

4.1
Modular decomposition and prime graphs

Let
G
be a graph and let
M
be a proper subset of
V(G)
. We say that
M
is a
module
of
G
if for every vertex
v\in V(G)-M
either
v
is adjacent to every vertex of
M
or is non-adjacent to every vertex of
M
. We say that a module
M
is
non-trivial
if
|M|>1
, otherwise
M
is
trivial
. We say that
G
is
prime
if every module of
G
is trivial.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a partition of
V(G)
into modules. We define the
quotient
graph
Q(\mathcal{M})
to be a graph such that there is a bijection between the modules in
\mathcal{M}
and vertices of
Q(\mathcal{M})
, and for vertices
m,m^{\prime}
of
Q(\mathcal{M})
, there is an edge
mm^{\prime}
if and only if the corresponding modules
M,M^{\prime}
are adjacent (i.e., all edges between
M
and
M^{\prime}
are present). Observe that
Q(\mathcal{M})
is an induced subgraph of
G
since it can be obtained by picking exactly one vertex from each module in
\mathcal{M}
.

A classic result of Gallai
[
24
]
asserts that if
G
and
\overline{G}
are both connected, then the partition
\mathcal{M}
of
V(G)
into inclusion-wise maximal modules is uniquely determined and can be found in polynomial time. In this case the quotient graph
Q(\mathcal{M})
is prime.

In the next lemma we show that
ITTE
combines well with modular decompositions of graphs.

10
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a hereditary class of graphs and let
\mathcal{X}^{*}
be the class of all induced subgraphs of the graphs in
\mathcal{X}
that are either prime or cliques. If
ITTE
can be solved in polynomial time on
\mathcal{X}^{*}
, then it can be solved in polynomial time on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Let
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be an instance of
ITTE
such that
G\in\mathcal{X}
. By
proposition

9
, we can assume that
Y^{\prime}=\emptyset
. Note that by
proposition

9
, we obtain an instance that is still in
\mathcal{X}
since the class is hereditary. Clearly, if
G
contains only one vertex, the problem is trivial and can be solved in constant time. If
G
is disconnected, then we can solve the problem on every instance separately. If
\overline{G}
is disconnected, then observe that the vertex set of a connected component of
\overline{G}
is a module. In this case we define the partition
\mathcal{M}
of
V(G)
into modules so that the modules are the vertex sets of connected components in
\overline{G}
. So now assume that both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected and
G
contains at least two vertices. In this case we define
\mathcal{M}
as a partition of
V(G)
into maximal modules – recall that if
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected, then
\mathcal{M}
is uniquely determined and can be found in polynomial time
[
24
]
.

Let
Q:=Q(\mathcal{M})
be the quotient graph of
G
. Note that
Q
contains at least two vertices. If
\overline{G}
is disconnected, then
Q
is a clique and if
\overline{G}
is connected, then
Q
is prime. Hence, we have that
Q\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
. For a module
M
of
\mathcal{M}
we will denote by
m
the vertex representing
M
in the graph
Q
.

We will construct an instance
(Q,\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
of
ITTE
equivalent to the instance
(G,X^{\prime},\emptyset,E^{\prime})
.

Since there are at least two vertices in
G
and
G
is connected, the graph
Q
is also connected and therefore there are no isolated vertices in
Q
. We can conclude that no module corresponding to a vertex of
Q
contains a triangle since, by
7
,
G
is
K_{4}
-free.

Types of modules.

We distinguish three types of modules: (1) independent sets, (2) bipartite with at least one edge, and (3) non-bipartite. We observe that there are no edges in
G
joining any two modules of type (2) or (3) because
G
is
K_{4}
-free. Consequently, in the graph
Q
the vertices representing modules of type (2) or (3) are adjacent only to vertices representing modules of type (1).

For a given instance
(G,X^{\prime},\emptyset,E^{\prime})
, we distinguish two subtypes of modules of type (2). A module
M
of type (2) is of type (2b) if there is a bipartition class in
G[M]
which contains
X^{\prime}\cap M
and a vertex of every edge of
E^{\prime}
intersecting
M
. A module of type (2) is of type (2a) if it is not of type (2b).

Note that in any solution
X
of the
ITTE
problem, for a module
M
of type (3), we have
X\cap M=\emptyset
. Suppose to the contrary that
X\cap M\neq\emptyset
. Then for any module
M^{\prime}
adjacent to
M
, we have
M^{\prime}\cap X=\emptyset
. Moreover, since
G[M]
is non-bipartite, there is an edge
uv
in
G[M]-X
. Therefore, for any vertex
w
in an adjacent module,
uvw
is a triangle in
G-X
, a contradiction. Similarly, we can observe that if for a module
M
of type (2) we have
M\cap X\neq\emptyset
, then one bipartition class of
G[M]
is fully taken to
X
. So if
M
of type (3) and
X^{\prime}\cap M\neq\emptyset
, or
M
of type (2) and there is a connected component
C
of
G[M^{\prime}]
with two vertices of
X^{\prime}
in distinct bipartition classes, then we can report a no-instance.

Construction of the equivalent instance.

The set
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}
consists of the vertices
m
of
Q
such that the corresponding module
M
of
G
satisfies at least one of the following conditions:

1.

M
contains some vertex of
X^{\prime}
,

2.

G[M]
contains some edge of
E^{\prime}
,

3.

some neighbor of
M
is of type (3),

4.

some neighbor of
M
is of type (2a).

The set
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
consists of the edges
mm^{\prime}
of
Q
such that for the corresponding modules
M,M^{\prime}
of
G
satisfy at least one of the following conditions:

1.

both
M
and
M^{\prime}
are of type (1) and there is an edge in
E^{\prime}
joining a vertex in
M
with a vertex in
M^{\prime}
,

2.

one of the modules
M
or
M^{\prime}
is of type (2b).

This completes the construction of
(Q,\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
.

Correctness.

Let us verify the correctness, i.e., that the created instance
(Q,\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
is equivalent to the instance
(G,X^{\prime},\emptyset,E^{\prime})
.

First assume that
X
is a solution for the instance
(G,X^{\prime},\emptyset,E^{\prime})
. We will prove that
\widetilde{X}=\{m\in V(Q):M\cap X\not=\emptyset\}
is a solution for the instance
(Q,\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
. Since
X
is an independent set of vertices of
G
,
\widetilde{X}
is an independent set of vertices of
Q
. Suppose the set
\widetilde{X}
does not intersect some triangle
m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}
in
Q
. Then each triangle in
G
which has one vertex in
M_{1}
, another in
M_{2}
and the last one
M_{3}
has no vertex in
X
, a contradiction.

Let us show now that
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{X}
. Obviously, every module
M
containing a vertex of
X^{\prime}
or an edge of
E^{\prime}
has a non-empty intersection with
X
, so the corresponding vertex
m
in
Q
is in
\widetilde{X}
. Consider now a module
M
whose neighbor, say
M^{\prime}
, is of type (3) or (2a) and suppose no vertex of
M
is in
X
. Then, every triangle with one vertex in
M
and the other two in
M^{\prime}
has a common vertex with
X
in
M^{\prime}
. Moreover, all modules which are neighbors of
M^{\prime}
contain no vertex of
X
, because
X
is independent. The set
X\cap M^{\prime}
must be an independent set of vertices intersecting every edge of
G[M]
, otherwise vertices of some edge in
M^{\prime}
together with any vertex of
M
form a triangle with no vertex in
X
. This is, however, impossible if
M^{\prime}
is of type (3) because

is a non-bipartite graph, a contradiction. Hence,
X
intersects the module
M
if
M^{\prime}
is a module of type (3). If
M^{\prime}
is of type (2a) then
X\cap M^{\prime}
must be a bipartition class of
M^{\prime}
that contains all vertices of
X^{\prime}\cap M^{\prime}
. By the definition of a module of type (2a), there is an edge
e\in E^{\prime}
with an endvertex in
M^{\prime}
and no endvertex in

. Thus, the other endvertex of

is a member of some module which is a neighbor of
M^{\prime}
. We have already observed that no vertex of
X
is a member of such module. Thus,

is disjoint from
X
, a contradiction. Hence,
X
intersects the module
M
if
M^{\prime}
is a module of type (2a). We have shown that
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{X}
.

Let us prove that
\widetilde{X}
intersects every edge of
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
.
Consider an edge
mm^{\prime}\in\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
. If the corresponding modules
M
and
M^{\prime}
are of type (1) and there is an edge in

joining a vertex in

with a vertex in
M^{\prime}
, then one of the ends of this edge is in

, so
X
intersects
M
or
M^{\prime}
and, consequently,

or
m^{\prime}\in\widetilde{X}
. If one of the modules
M
or
M^{\prime}
, say
M^{\prime}
, is of type (2b) then vertices of any edge in
M^{\prime}
together with a vertex of
M
form a triangle, so
X
contains a vertex of
M
or
M^{\prime}
. Consequently, one of the ends of the edge
mm^{\prime}\in\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
is in
\widetilde{X}
. We have shown that
\widetilde{X}
intersects each edge of
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
so
\widetilde{X}
is indeed a solution for the instance
(Q,\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
.

Assume now that
\widetilde{X}
is a solution for the instance
(Q,\widetilde{X}^{\prime},\emptyset,\widetilde{E}^{\prime})
of
ITTE
. We define
X
to be the set consisting of all vertices of every module
M
of type (1) such that

and all vertices of a bipartition class, say

, of each module
M^{\prime}
of type (2b) such that

and

intersects every edge of
E^{\prime}
with an endvertex in
M^{\prime}
and contains all vertices of the set
X^{\prime}\cap M^{\prime}
. Notice that by the definition of a module of type (2b) such a bipartition class exists. We will prove that
X
is a solution for the instance
(G,X^{\prime},\emptyset,E^{\prime})
.

Since
\widetilde{X}
is an independent set of vertices of
Q
, it is clear that
X
is an independent set of vertices of
G
. As we have already observed, no module corresponding to a vertex of
Q
contains a triangle. If a triangle
T
in
G
has two vertices
u,w
in the same module
M
and the third one, say

, in another module, say

, then

must be of type (2) or (3) and

must be of type (1). If

is of type (2a) or (3) then

because, by the definition of

,

. If

is of type (2b) then, by the definition of

,

, so

or

. By definition of

, in the former case, we have

, as

is of type (1), and in the latter one, either

or

. Last, if a triangle

in

has its vertices in three different modules

. Since the vertices

. Consequently the vertex of

. We have shown that every triangle in

has a vertex in

.

Let us prove now that

intersects every edge

. If

is contained in some module

then

. Notice that

is of type neither (3) nor (2a) because the vertices of

and

. Thus,

. If

.

Running time.

Classifying the modules, i.e., verifying if they are independent or bipartite, clearly can be done in polynomial time. Classifying modules of type (2) into subtypes, for each module

of type (2) can be done in polynomial time. Indeed, it is enough to verify the condition for each connected component of

\widetilde{X}^{\prime}
and

. Therefore the instance
(G,X^{\prime},\emptyset,E^{\prime})
can be solved in polynomial time. That completes the proof.

4.2
Clique cutsets and atoms

A (possibly empty) set
C\subseteq V(G)
is a
cutset
in
G
, if
V(G)-C
is disconnected.
We say that
C
is a
clique cutset
if
C
is a cutset and a clique.
We say that
G
is an
atom
if it does not contain clique cutsets. Note that, in particular, every atom is connected.
A triple
(A,C,B)
is a
clique cutset partition
if it is a partition of
V(G)
such that
C
is a clique cutset, there is no edge between
A
and
B
, and
G[A\cup C]
is an atom.

We will need the following.

Theorem 11
(Tarjan
[
50
]
).

Let
G
be a graph.
Then in polynomial time we can find a clique cutset partition or correctly conclude that
G
is an atom.

We show that we can reduce
ITTE
problem to instances
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
such that
G
is an atom.
The algorithm is a standard recursion that exploits the existence of clique cutsets
[
50
]
.

12
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a family of graphs, and let
\mathcal{X}^{a}
be the family of all atoms in
\mathcal{X}
.
If
ITTE
can be solved in polynomial time in
\mathcal{X}^{a}
, then
ITTE
can be solved in polynomial time in
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}=(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be an instance of
ITTE
such that
G\in\mathcal{X}
.
We compute the solution recursively.

If
G
is an atom,
\mathcal{I}
can be solved in polynomial time by our assumption.
Otherwise, we find a clique cutset partition
(A,C,B)
of
G
.
Define
G_{AC}:=G[A\cup C],G_{CB}:=G[C\cup B]
.
If
|C|>3
, or
|X^{\prime}\cap C|\geqslant 2
, we report that
G
is a no-instance.
Otherwise, for every
C^{\prime}\subseteq C-Y^{\prime}
, such that
|C^{\prime}|\leqslant 1
, we solve the instance
\mathcal{I}_{C^{\prime}}=(G_{AC},(X^{\prime}\cap A)\cup C^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\cap A,E^{\prime}\cap E(G_{AC}))
.
Let
M_{A}
be the set of these sets

for which
\mathcal{I}_{C^{\prime}}
is a yes-instance.
If
M_{A}=\emptyset
, we report that
G
is a no-instance.
If
|C|=2
, and
\emptyset\notin M_{A}
, we add the edge from
C
to
E^{\prime}
.
If
|C|=1
, and
\emptyset\notin M_{A}
, we add the vertex from
C
to
X^{\prime}
.
Then, we recursively solve the instance
\mathcal{I}_{B}=(G_{CB},X^{\prime}-A,(Y^{\prime}-A)\cup C_{Y},E^{\prime}\cap E(G_{CB}))
, where
C_{Y}=\{x\in C:\{x\}\notin M_{A}\}
.

Correctness.

First, we prove that if the algorithm returns that
\mathcal{I}
is a no-instance, then the solution cannot exist.
Indeed, it means that in some recursive call (i)
|C|>3
, or (ii)
|X^{\prime}\cap C|\geqslant 2
, or (iii)
M_{A}=\emptyset
, or (iv)
\mathcal{I}_{B}
is a no-instance.
In the first case,
G
contains a copy of
K_{4}
, so by
7
,
\mathcal{I}
is a no-instance.
In the second case, the set
X^{\prime}
is not independent, so again
\mathcal{I}
is a no-instance.
For the remaining cases, suppose that
\mathcal{I}
is a yes-instance, and
X
is a solution to
\mathcal{I}
.
However, then
X\cap(A\cup C)
is a solution to
(G_{AC},X^{\prime}\cap(A\cup C),Y^{\prime}\cap(A\cup C),E^{\prime}\cap E(G_{AC}))
, so
C^{\prime}=X\cap C\in M_{A}
, a contradiction with (iii).
Similarly,
X\cap(B\cup C)
is a solution to
\mathcal{I}_{B}
, a contradiction with (iv).

Now observe that the solution returned by a recursive call for
\mathcal{I}_{B}
can be always extended to
G
.
Indeed, assume that
X
is a solution to
\mathcal{I}_{B}
, and let
C^{\prime}:=X\cap C
.
As
C
is a clique, we must have
|C^{\prime}|\leqslant 1
.
If
C^{\prime}=\emptyset
, we must have that
|C|\leqslant 2
, as otherwise
C
is triangle in
G-X

However, then we have in particular that
E^{\prime}\cap E(G[C])=\emptyset
and
X^{\prime}\cap C=\emptyset
, so if
|C|\leqslant 2
, then
\emptyset\in M_{A}
and the solution can be extended to
G
.
On the other hand, if
C^{\prime}=\{x\}
for some
x\in C
, then
x\notin C_{Y}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{I}_{C^{\prime}}
is a yes-instance, so the solution also can be extended to
G
.

Running time.

Concluding that
G
is an atom or finding the clique cutset partition
(A,B,C)
can be done in polynomial time by
theorem

11
.
As
|C|\leqslant 3
, at every step we call the algorithm for instances that are atoms at most four times, so this step also takes polynomial time.
Since the set
A
is non-empty, in every recursion call our instance is strictly smaller, so after at most
n
calls, we obtain the solution.
Hence, the running time of the algorithm is polynomial in
|V(G)|
.

4.3
Bounded-treewidth graphs

Monadic Second-Order Logic
(
\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
) over graphs consists of formulas with vertex variables, edge variables, vertex set variables, and edge set variables, quantifiers, and standard logic operators. We also have a predicate
\mathsf{inc}(v,e)
, indicating that the vertex
v
belongs to the edge
e
.

For a graph
G
, let
{\operatorname{tw}(G)}
denote the treewidth of
G
.
The classic result of Courcelle
[
14
]
asserts that problems that can be expressed in
\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
can be efficiently solved on graphs of bounded treewidth. The statement of the following version of Courcelle’s theorem comes from Cygan et al.
[
15
, Theorem 7.11]
.

Theorem 13
(Courcelle
[
14
]
).

Let
\Psi
be a formula of
\textsf{MSO}_{2}
and
G
be an
n
-vertex graph equipped with evaluation of all the free variables of
\Psi
.
Moreover,
G
is given along with a tree decomposition of width
t
.
Then tverifying whether
\Psi
is satisfied in
G
can be done in time
f(||\Psi||,t)\cdot n
, for some computable function
f
.

It is straightforward to verify that
ITTE
can be expressed as a formula of
\textsf{MSO}_{2}
with free variables where the sets
X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}
, and
E^{\prime}
are free variables of
\Psi
. Furthermore, given a graph of bounded treewidth,
one can find a tree decomposition of bounded width in polynomial time, using one of FPT approximation algorithms for treewidth
[
36
]
. Summing up, we obtain the following.

Corollary 14
.

The
ITTE
problem can be solved in polynomial time on graphs with bounded treewidth.

5
Solving
ITTE
in claw-free graphs

Let
G
be a connected graph.
A
strip structure of
G
is a pair
(D,\eta)
, that consists of a simple graph
D
, a set
\eta(xy)\subseteq V(G)
for every
xy\in E(D)
, and its non-empty subsets
\eta(xy,x),\eta(xy,y)\subseteq\eta(xy)
, satisfying the following conditions:

(S1)

|E(D)|\geqslant 3
, and there are no vertices of degree 2 in
D
,

(S2)

the sets
\{\eta(e):e\in E(D)\}
form a partition of
V(G)
,

(S3)

if
u\in\eta(e),v\in\eta(f)
, for some
e,f\in E(D)
, then
uv\in E(G)
if and only if there exists
x\in V(D)
such that
e
and
f
are incident to
x
,
u\in\eta(e,x)
and
w\in\eta(f,x)
,

(S4)

for every
x\in V(D)
, the set
\bigcup_{y\in N(x)}\eta(xy,x)
induces a clique in
G
.

Chudnovsky and Seymour
[
13
]
proved that every claw-free
G
is either very simple
or admits a strip structure where the subgraphs induced by vertices assigned to a single edge are simple.
If
G
is additionally of bounded maximum degree, then this result becomes particularly usuful.
The following corollary of the result of Chudnovsky and Seymour comes from the paper of Abrishami et al.
[
1
, Corollary 3.5]
.

Theorem 15
(Chudnovsky, Seymour
[
13
]
).

If
G
is a connected claw-free graph with maximum degree at most
\Delta
, then either

{\operatorname{tw}(G)}\leqslant 4\Delta+3
, or
G
admits a strip structure
(D,\eta)
such that for every
e\in E(D)
, we have
{\operatorname{tw}(G[\eta(e)])}\leqslant 4\Delta+4
.
Moreover,
(D,\eta)
can be found in time polynomial in
|V(G)|
.

The main result of this section is that
ITTE
can be solved in polynomial time in claw-free graphs.

Theorem 16
.

ITTE
can be solved in polynomial time in claw-free graphs.

Proof.

Let
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be an instance of
ITTE
, such that
G
is claw-free.
By
lemma

12
, we can assume that
G
is an atom, and, in particular,
G
is connected.

First, we check whether
G
contains
K_{4}
.
If so, by
7
, we can safely return that we deal with a no-instance.
Hence, we assume that
G
is
K_{4}
-free.
This implies that
\Delta(G)\leqslant 5
.
Indeed, if there exists a vertex
v
of degree at least six, then
v
either has three pairwise non-adjacent neighbors (and hence a claw), or three pairwise adjacent neighbors (and hence a
K_{4}
).

Since
G
is claw-free and
\Delta(G)\leqslant 5
, by
Theorem

15
, either (i)
{\operatorname{tw}(G)}\leqslant 23
or (ii) there exists a strip structure
(D,\eta)
such that for every
e\in E(D)
we have
{\operatorname{tw}(G[\eta(e)])}\leqslant 24
.
By
corollary

14
, if (i) holds, it can be checked in polynomial time if
G
is a yes-instance.
Therefore, we will assume that (ii) holds.
By
Theorem

15
the strip structure
(D,\eta)
can be computed in polynomial time.
As
G
is connected, so is
D
.

Recall that by the definition of a strip structure, if
y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}
are neighbors of some
x\in V(D)
, the set
\bigcup_{i\in[d]}\eta(xy_{i},x)
induces a clique in
G
.
Hence, as
G
is
K_{4}
-free, we have
\Delta(D)\leqslant 3
, which implies that the vertices of
D
are either of degree 1 or 3.
Moreover, if
x
is of degree 3, let
N(x)=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}
and note that since
G
is
K_{4}
-free,
|\eta(xy_{1},x)|=|\eta(xy_{2},x)|=|\eta(xy_{3},x)|=1
.
In this case, we denote the single member of
\eta(xy,x)
by
v_{x,y}
.

On the other hand, if
D
contains a vertex
y
of degree 1, two things can happen: either
D
is isomorphic to
K_{2}
, or
y
is adjacent to a vertex
x
that is of degree 3 in
D
.
In the first case, if we denote by
e
the edge of
D
, we have
{\operatorname{tw}(G)}={\operatorname{tw}(G[\eta(e)])}\leqslant 24
, so again, we can solve the problem in polynomial time by
corollary

14
.
In the second case, let
y_{1},y_{2}
be the other (than
y
) neighbors of
x
in
D
.
We observe that
\{v_{x,y_{1}},v_{x,y_{2}}\}
or
\{v_{x,y}\}
is a clique cutset in

, a contradiction with
G
being an atom.
Hence, we can assume that
D
is 3-regular.

For
x\in V(D)
and
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in N_{D}(x)
, let
E(x):=\{v_{x,y_{1}}v_{x,y_{2}},v_{x,y_{2}}v_{x,y_{3}},v_{x,y_{3}}v_{x,y_{1}}\}
.
Recall that by the definition of a strip structure,
G[E(x)]
is a triangle.
The following reduction rule is straightforward.

Reduction Rule 16.1
.

If
E(x)\subseteq E^{\prime}
, then
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a no-instance.
If
v_{x,y_{1}}v_{x,y_{2}},v_{x,y_{2}}v_{x,y_{3}}\in E^{\prime},
but
v_{x,y_{3}}v_{x,y_{1}}\notin E^{\prime}
, we can safely add
v_{x,y_{2}}
to
X^{\prime}
and remove
v_{x,y_{1}}v_{x,y_{2}},v_{x,y_{2}}v_{x,y_{3}}
from
E^{\prime}
.
Hence, for every
x\in V(D)
it holds that
|E(x)\cap E^{\prime}|\leqslant 1
.

Consider an edge
xy
of
D
.
Let
G_{xy}:=G[\eta(xy)]
, and let
I_{xy}=\{v_{x,y},v_{y,x}\}-Y^{\prime}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}(xy)
be the (possibly empty) set of these sets
A
which satisfy
X^{\prime}\cap I_{xy}\subseteq A\subseteq I_{xy}
and

\mathcal{I}_{A}=(G_{xy},(X^{\prime}\cap V(G_{xy}))\cup A,(Y^{\prime}\cap V(G_{xy}))\cup(I_{xy}-A),E^{\prime}\cap E(G_{xy}))

is a yes-instance of
ITTE
.
Observe that if there exists a solution
X
of
ITTE
for
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
, then
X\cap\eta(xy)
is a solution to
\mathcal{I}_{A^{\prime}}
for
A^{\prime}=\{v_{x,y},v_{y,x}\}\cap X
, and in this case
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(xy)
.
We obtain the following.

Reduction Rule 16.2
.

If
\mathcal{A}(xy)=\emptyset
for some
xy\in E(D)
, then
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a no-instance.

Our aim is to reduce the
ITTE
problem to the
MWM
∗
problem.
Let
\mathcal{E}:=\{xy\in E(D):\emptyset\notin\mathcal{A}(x,y)\}
.
We construct an instance
(D^{\prime},U,\mathfrak{w},|\mathcal{E}|)
of
MWM
∗
as follows.
To obtain
D^{\prime}
, we take a copy of the set
V(D)
, and for every
xy_{1}\in E(D)
we introduce an additional vertex

.
Then, for every
xy\in E(D)
, we do the following (below we always use the notation

).

(1)

if
\{v_{x,y},v_{y,x}\}\in\mathcal{A}(xy)
, we add
xy
to
E(D^{\prime})
,

(2)

if
\{v_{x,y}\}\in\mathcal{A}(xy)
, we add
xt_{xy}
to
E(D^{\prime})
,

(3)

if
\{v_{y,x}\}\in\mathcal{A}(xy)
, we add

to
E(D^{\prime})
.

(4)

if
\{v_{x,y^{\prime}}v_{x,y^{\prime\prime}}\}=E^{\prime}\cap E(x)
, we remove
xy
and

from

(if they were introduced).

Let
U=V(D)\subseteq V(D^{\prime})
, i.e., the set of original vertices of

.
For
xy\in E(D)
, we define
T(xy):=\{xy,xt_{xy},yt_{xy}\}\cap E(D^{\prime})
.
We set
\mathfrak{w}:E(D^{\prime})\to\{0,1\}
to be
\mathfrak{w}(uv)=1
if

for some
xy\in\mathcal{E}
, and
\mathfrak{w}(uv)=0
otherwise.
This concludes the construction of
(D^{\prime},U,\mathfrak{w},|\mathcal{E}|)
.

By the definition of the strip structure, each edge
uv\in V(G)
is either contained in
E(G_{xy})
, for some
xy\in E(D)
, or
u=v_{x,y^{\prime}}
and
v=v_{x,y^{\prime\prime}}
for some

.
In the first case, we say that

is of
type I for
xy
, in the latter – that
uv
is of
type II for
\{xy^{\prime},xy^{\prime\prime}\}
.
Similarly, each triangle
uvt\in V(G)
is is either contained in
E(G_{xy})
, for some
xy\in E(D)
(so it is of
type I for
xy
), or
u=v_{x,y}
,
v=v_{x,y^{\prime}}
and
t=v_{x,y^{\prime\prime}}
for some
x\in U
(so it is of
type II for
x
).
We claim the following.

Claim 16.1
.

(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a yes-instance of

if and only if
(D^{\prime},U,\mathfrak{w},|\mathcal{E}|)
is a yes-instance of
MWM
∗
.

Proof of Claim.
First, assume that
(D^{\prime},U,\mathfrak{w},|\mathcal{E}|)
is a yes-instance of
MWM
∗
, i.e., there exists a matching
M
such that
\mathfrak{w}(M)\geqslant|\mathcal{E}|
and
M
covers
U
.

We note that since
M
is a matching, in particular, for every
xy\in E(D)
it holds that
|T(xy)\cap M|\leqslant 1
.
We set
T:=\bigcup_{xy\in\mathcal{E}}T(xy)
.
By the definition of
\mathfrak{w}
, we have
\sum_{e\in M}\mathfrak{w}(e)=\sum_{e\in M\cap T}\mathfrak{w}(e)=|M\cap T|
.
Since
\sum_{e\in M}\mathfrak{w}(e)\geqslant|\mathcal{E}|
, we obtain that
|M\cap T|\geqslant|\mathcal{E}|
, i.e.,
M
contains at least
|\mathcal{E}|
edges from
T
.
However, as
|T(xy)\cap M|\leqslant 1
for every

, we know that the following property is satisfied:

\displaystyle(*)\textrm{ The matching $M$ contains exactly one edge from each $T(xy)$, where $xy\in\mathcal{E}$.}

Each
x\in U
is incident to exactly one edge of
M
, denote that edge by

.
We define the solution
X
to
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
by defining
\eta(xy)\cap X
for every
xy\in E(D)
.
Let
A(xy)
be as follows.

\displaystyle A(xy):=\begin{cases}\{v_{x,y},v_{y,x}\}&\textrm{if }m(x)=xy,\\
\{v_{x,y}\}&\textrm{if }m(x)=xt_{xy},\\
\{v_{y,x}\}&\textrm{if }m(y)=yt_{xy},\\
\emptyset&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

As
M
is a matching, we cannot have

and
m(y)=yt_{xy}
, or
m(x)=xt_{xy}
and
m(y)=yt_{xy}
at the same time, so
A(xy)
is well-defined.

Observe that
A(xy)\in\mathcal{A}(xy)
, by definition of
E(D^{\prime})
.
Indeed, first three cases in the definition of

correspond precisely to conditions (1)-(3) in the construction of
E(D^{\prime})
.
For the last case, assume that the
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{A}(xy)
, i.e.,
xy\in\mathcal{E}
.
However, by
(*)
, if
xy\in\mathcal{E}
, then
M\cap T(xy)\neq\emptyset
, i.e., one of first three cases in the definition of
A(xy)
holds.

As
A(xy)\in\mathcal{A}(xy)
, let
X(xy)
be a solution to
\mathcal{I}_{A(xy)}
.
We claim that
X=\bigcup_{xy\in E(D)}X(xy)
is a solution to the instance
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of

.
For that, we need to verify that (i)
X
is an independent set, (ii)

, (iii)

, (iv) for every

it holds that

, and (v)

is triangle-free.

For (i), assume that

If

.
Hence,

.
This means that

, and

However, this implies, respectively, that

If

Hence, by step (4) of the construction of

and

.

.

Finally, to see that (v) holds, consider a triangle

in

.

.
If

is of type I for some

, then again, since

, we know that at least one of

.
So assume that

is of type II for some

.
As

.
Hence, by definition of sets

,

, and

For the other implication, assume that
(G,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})

, so there exists a solution

.
For each

We note that

, where

We construct a solution

for the instance

.

\displaystyle M(xy):=\begin{cases}\{xy\}&\textrm{if }A(xy)=\{v_{x,y},v_{y,x}\},\\
\{xt_{xy}\}&\textrm{if }v_{x,y}\neq v_{y,x}\textrm{ and }A(xy)=\{v_{x,y}\},\\

, respectively) is removed in step (4), then

.
We claim that

is a matching
M
in

\mathfrak{w}(M)\geqslant|\mathcal{E}|
.

.
For (i), note that the only elements of

, are vertices of form

.

Since

Recall that by

The following observation can be easily verified.

\ell

It is straightforward to verify that the instance constructed in this way is equivalent to

3-Sat

2^{o(n+m)}

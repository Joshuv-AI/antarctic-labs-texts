On the characterization of Riemannian warped product Einstein metrics
Sayed Mohammad Reza Hashemi

Abstract




ABSTRACT. In
[
HPW12
]
C. He, P. Petersen and W. Wylie investigate warped product Einstein spaces using the concept of
(\lambda,m+n)
-Einstein metrics in the Riemannian setting. The authors in
[
HPW12
]
extend the works of
[
CSW11
]
,
[
Cat12
]
and that of
[
KK03
]
on
(\lambda,n+m)
-Einstein manifolds with boundary.
(\lambda,n+m)
-Einstein structure is globally characterized in
[
HPW12
]
, but it is not correctly stated. In this paper I present a series of results, including local characterizations, which are necessary for the restatement of the main result in
[
HPW12
]
. Finally, I restate their global characterization, i.e.
[
HPW12
, Theorem 7.2]
.

1
Introduction

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
which has no boundary point is said to be geodesically complete provided every maximal geodesic
\gamma
:
I\rightarrow M
is defined on
the whole
\mathbb{R}
, i.e.
I=\mathbb{R}
. Moreover a Riemannian manifold with non-empty boundary is said to be geodesically complete provided every maximal geodesic
\gamma:

I\rightarrow M
maps each finite end point of
I
to a boundary point of
\partial M
, i.e. every maximal geodesic
\gamma:

I\rightarrow M
satisfies in one of the following conditions:

1)
\gamma:

[a,\infty)\rightarrow M
then
\gamma(a)\in\partial M

2)
\gamma:

(-\infty,b]\rightarrow M
then
\gamma(b)\in\partial M

3)
\gamma:

[a,b]\rightarrow M
then
\gamma(a)
,
\gamma(b)\in\partial M

4)
\gamma:

(-\infty,\infty)\rightarrow M
then
\gamma(-\infty,\infty)\cap\partial M=\emptyset

By completeness, we mean geodesically completeness unless we specifically mention some other sense of completeness.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
HPW12
]
)
.

A (
\lambda
,n+m)-Einstein manifold
(M,g,f)
is a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
which may have boundary together with a smooth function
f
(here smooth means of
class
C^{2}
) on
M
satisfying

\displaystyle Hessf

\displaystyle=\frac{f}{m}(Ric-\lambda g)

(1)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle>0\text{ on int}(M)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=0\text{ on }\partial M.

If
m=1
we additionally assume that
\Delta f=-\lambda f
. A
(\lambda,n+m)
-Einstein manifold
(M,g,f)
itself is not necessarily Einstein. A simple case of
(\lambda,n+m)
-Eintstein manifolds happens when

is constant. Then from Equation (
1
) one obtains
Ric=\lambda g
. Moreover as
f
is constant it can not be identically zero, hence
\partial M=\emptyset
. In
[
HPW12
]
this case is called a
\lambda
-Einstein manifold or a trivial
(\lambda,n+m)
-Einstein manifold.

Reminder: For the case
m=1
see
[
Cor00
]
. In this paper and in particular in the main results, i.e. theorems

,
4.3
and
5.1
we focus on

. For a Riemannian manifold

without boundary compare
[

]
.

Our motivation to study

-Einstein manifolds is due to

.

(

]

)

Suppose

,

-Einstein

\mu

\displaystyle\mu=f\Delta f+(m-1)\lvert\nabla f\lvert^{2}+\lambda f^{2}

(2)

In the definition of a

-Einstein manifold

for the case

. This is necessary for the existence of

If we define

e^{-\frac{u}{m}}=f

-Einstein manifold

, Equation (

\displaystyle Ric_{u}^{m}=Ric+Hessu-\frac{du\otimes du}{m}=\lambda g,

(3)

is sometimes called the m-Bakry-Emery tensor. The so-called m-quasi-Einstein manifolds were introduced by J. Case, Y-J. Shu and G. Wei in
[

]
as a triple
(M,g,u)
consisting of a Riemannian manifold

and

. By the results of
[

]
,
[

characterizes m-quasi Einstein manifolds

as the base manifold of an Einstein warped product for which

is the warping function. Additionally through
[

, they proved a rigidity property for scalar curvature, Scal, by giving lower and upper bounds respecting the sign of

A bit later, Catino in
[

considered the following extended form of the equation (

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}+\mathrm{Hess}u-\varsigma du\otimes du=\lambda g

for the so-called generalized quasi-Einstein manifolds. Here

are three smooth functions on a complete Riemannian manifold

,

.
Equation (

are constant, and a quasi-Einstein manifold when
\varsigma
and

are constant. Catino proves the following.

Proposition 1.3
.

(
[

]
, Theorem 1.1.
)
.
Let

,

, be a generalized quasi-Einstein manifold with harmonic Weyl tensor and

. Then around any regular point of

G. Catino, C. Mantegazza, L. Mazzieri and M. Rimoldi in

also consider Equation (

. They prove the following result for an arbitrary

.

Proposition 1.4
.

(
[
CMMR13
]
, Theorem 1.1.
)
.
Let

, be a complete locally conformally flat quasi Einstein manifold. Then the following hold:

-Einstein metrics in the Riemannian case. These metrics can also be seen as generalizations of gradient Ricci solitons, which are invariant under the Ricci flow. Our work is mostly based on
[

where the authors extend the works of
[

]
,
[

]
and that of
[

]
on

-Einstein manifolds with boundary. In
[

]
setting new quantities

and

, the authors get control on the number of corresponding eigenvalues of the tensor P with respect to
\nabla f
(hence for the Schouten tensor and Hess

Lemma 1.5
.

(
[
HPW12
]
, Lemma

.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a Riemannian

(\lambda,n+m)
-Einstein manifold with harmonic Weyl tensor and

. Then at a point

where

, the tensor

(hence Ricci and Schouten tensors) has at most two eigenvalues. If it has two eigenvalues then one has multiplicity

with eigenvector
\nabla f
,
and the other one has multiplicity

with vectors orthogonal to
\nabla f
. If it has one eigenvalue then

Then the authors define

\sigma_{1}
and

are the eigenvalues of the Schouten tensor. If
(M,g)
is Einstein, then there is only one eigenvalue, i.e.

. In this case Hess

is proportional to the metric, cf. Example
2.2
for the relevant results. Using Lemma
1.5
they decompose the metric

in a neighborhood of a point

into a warped product

, further they show that

:

Theorem 1.6
.

(
[
HPW12
]
, Theorem

.

Suppose

and
(M,g,f)
is a
(\lambda,n+m)
-Einstein metric with harmonic Weyl tensor and

. Then

f=f(t)

in a neighborhood of

, where

is an Einstein metric. Moreover if the metric is locally conformally flat around

, then

is a space of constant sectional curvature.

-Einstein metrics in the Riemannian case as their main result:

.

(
[
HPW12
]
, Theorem

.

Let

As the main results of this paper, we first formulate relation between the warping function of a local warped product form of

and the function

of a

-Einstein metric

This paper is organized in the following way. In the second section we bring some examples. In the third section, first we obtain the formula describing the relation between the warping function of a one-dimensional basis warped product and the function

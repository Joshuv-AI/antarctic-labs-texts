Abstract

Let
\Omega

(X,d,\mathfrak{m})

K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
, and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a complete metric space. A map
u:\Omega\to Y
is called an
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
-map if for some
P\in Y
the function
d_{Y}(P,u(x))
is in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Given a map
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y),
for each
r>0
, the
approximating energy

E^{\Omega}_{2,r}[u]
at scale
r
is defined as a functional on
C_{0}(\Omega),

E^{\Omega}_{2,r}[u](\phi):=\int_{\Omega}\phi\cdot{\rm ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega]d\mathfrak{m},

where

(1.1)

{\rm ks}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x):=\begin{cases}\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\frac{d_{Y}^{2}(u(x),u(y))}{r^{2}}d\mathfrak{m}(y)\right)^{1/2}&{\rm if}\ B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega,\\
0&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

A map
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
is called in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
if

E_{2}^{\Omega}(u):=\sup_{\phi\in C_{0}(\Omega),\ 0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1}\Big(\limsup_{r\to 0}E^{\Omega}_{2,r}[u](\phi)\Big)<+\infty.

We consider the Dirichlet problem as follows. Given any
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, we want to find the minimizer of

\min_{u\in W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)}E_{2}^{\Omega}(u),

where

(1.2)

W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y):=\big\{u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y):\ d_{Y}\big(u(x),w(x)\big)\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\big\}.

Such an energy-minimizing map is called a
harmonic map
.

Theorem 1.2

(Mondino-Semola, Gigli)

Let
\Omega\subset X

(X,d,\mathfrak{m})

However, they did not address boundary regularity questions. Recall that the boundary regularity for harmonic maps between smooth manifolds has been established by Schoen-Uhlenbeck
[
41

.

Our goal in the present paper is to study the boundary regularity for harmonic maps into/between singular metric spaces. Up to now, there are only a few answers for several special cases.
The first is the case where the domain
\Omega
is smooth. In 1994, Serbinowski
[
42
]
proved that the harmonic map
u
is Hölder continuous near the boundary, assuming that its trace is so and that
\Omega
satisfies a condition in terms of locally exterior cones. In particular, he proved the following

Theorem 1.3

(Serbinowski)
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded in a smooth Riemannian manifold, and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space. Suppose that
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
and
u\in W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)
is a harmonic map. Then

(A) if
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz (i.e., it can be locally written as a graph of a Lipschitz function) and if
w\in C^{\alpha}({\overline{\Omega}},Y)
, then
u\in C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega},Y)
for some small
\beta\in(0,\alpha]
.

(B) if
\partial\Omega
is of
C^{1}
and
w\in Lip({\overline{\Omega}},Y)
, then
u\in C^{1-\epsilon}(\overline{\Omega},Y)
for every
\epsilon>0
.

Here a map
w\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega},Y)
means

d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)\leqslant Ld^{\alpha}d(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\overline{\Omega}

for some constant
L>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1).

The second is the case where the domain
\Omega
is in an Alexandrov space with curvature bounded below. Huang-Zhang
[
25
]
proved that the harmonic map
u\in W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)
is Hölder continuous (with a small Hölder index) near the boundary, assuming that
w\in Lip(\overline{\Omega},Y)
and that
\Omega
satisfies a condition in terms of locally exterior balls.

In this paper, we will be concerned with the boundary regularity of harmonic maps on
RCD
spaces. We first recall some classical conditions as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in a metric measure space
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
. It is said to satisfy an
exterior density condition
if there exist two numbers
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
R_{0}>0
such that

(1.3)

\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega)\geqslant\lambda\cdot\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))\quad\forall\ x\in\partial{\Omega},\ \ \forall r\in(0,R_{0}).

It is said to satisfy a
uniformly exterior sphere condition
if there exists a number
R_{0}>0
such that for each
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
there exists a ball

B_{R_{0}}(y_{0})
satisfying

(1.4)

\Omega\cap B_{R_{0}}(y_{0})=\emptyset\quad{\rm and}\quad x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial B_{R_{0}}(y_{0}).

From the volume doubling property, one knows if
\Omega
satisfies a uniformly exterior sphere condition then it also satisfies an exterior density condition.
It is well-known that if
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz then it satisfies an exterior density condition, and if
\partial\Omega
is of
C^{2}
on a smooth manifold then it satisfies an
exterior sphere condition.

The main result in this paper is the following boundary regularity of harmonic maps.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega,Y
be as in the above Theorem
1.2
. Let
w\in W^{1,2}({\Omega},Y)
and
u\in W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)
be a harmonic map. Then

(A) If
\partial\Omega
satisfies an exterior density condition and if
w\in C^{\alpha}({\overline{\Omega}},Y)
, then
u\in C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega},Y)
for some small
\beta\in(0,\alpha)
.

(B) If
\partial\Omega
satisfies a uniformly exterior sphere condition and if
w\in Lip({\overline{\Omega}},Y)
, then
u\in C^{1-\epsilon}(\overline{\Omega},Y)
for every
\epsilon>0
.

Remark 1.6
.

In the case when
Y=\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
u
is a harmonic function, the conclusion of Theorem
1.5
(A) has been proved by Björn in
[
5
]
, under the weaker assumption that the measure
\mathfrak{m}
is doubling and that
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
supports an
L^{2}
-Poincaré inequality.

Remark 1.7
.

The conclusion of Theorem
1.5
(B) is sharp. One can not improve the Hölder regularity of Theorem
1.5
(B) to Lipschitz regularity. Indeed, let us recall some known results about harmonic functions on an Euclidean space (see, for example,
[
21
,
33
]
). Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain,
n\geqslant 2,
and let
u\in W^{1,2}(D)
be a harmonic function on
D
with boundary data
\phi\in Lip(\overline{D})
( i.e.,
u=\phi
on
\partial D
). Then

(1) if
\partial D
satisfies an exterior density condition, then
u\in C^{\beta}(\overline{D})
, for some small
\beta>0
,

(2) if
\partial D
is of
C^{1}
, then
u\in C^{1-\epsilon}(\overline{D})
for every
\epsilon>0
,

(3) One can not expect that
u\in Lip(\overline{D})
, even
\partial D
is of
C^{\infty}
(see the following Example
1.8
).

Example 1.8
.

Consider the upper semi-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}=\{(x,t)|\ t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Let
\phi
be defined on
\mathbb{R}
by

\phi(x)=|x|,\quad{\rm if}\quad|x|\leqslant 1\qquad{\rm and}\quad\phi(x)=(2-|x|)^{+}\quad{\rm otherwise}.

The harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with boundary data
\phi
is uniquely determined by the Poisson integral formula,

u(x,s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{s\phi(x)}{|x-y|^{2}+s^{2}}dy.

By directly calculating, we have

\begin{split}\frac{\partial u(0,s)}{\partial s}&=\int_{|y|\leqslant 1}\frac{y^{2}-s^{2}}{(y^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}|y|dy+\int_{2\geqslant|y|\geqslant 1}\frac{y^{2}-s^{2}}{(y^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}(2-|y|)dy\\
&:=I_{1}(s)+I_{2}(s)\end{split}

where

I_{1}(s)=\int_{|y|\leqslant 1}\frac{y^{2}-s^{2}}{(y^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}|y|dy=2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y^{2}-s^{2}}{(y^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}ydy=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y^{2}-s^{2}}{(y^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}d(y^{2})=O(\log(1/s))

as
s\to 0^{+}
, and

|I_{2}(s)|\leqslant\int_{2\geqslant|y|\geqslant 1}\frac{|y^{2}-s^{2}|}{(y^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}(2-|y|)dy\leqslant 8\quad\forall s\in(0,1).

Therefore, we conclude

|\nabla u(0,s)|\geqslant\left|\frac{\partial u(0,s)}{\partial s}\right|=O(\log(1/s)),\quad{\rm as}\ s\to 0^{+}.

to the proof of the main Theorem
1.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, i.e.,
(X,d)
is a complete and separable metric space equipped with a non-negative Borel measure which is finite on any ball
B_{r}(x)\subset X
and
{\rm supp}(\mathfrak{m})=X
. We will denote by
B_{r}(x_{0})
the open ball in
X
. Given an open domain
\Omega\subset X
, we denote by
Lip(\Omega)
(resp.
C_{0}(\Omega)
,
Lip_{0}(\Omega)
,
Lip_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
) the space of Lipschitz continuous (resp. continuous with compact support, Lipschitz continuous with compact support, locally Lipschitz continuous) functions on
\Omega
, and by
L^{p}(\Omega):=L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{m})
for any
p\in[1,+\infty]
. For any
f:E\to\mathbb{R}
on a measurable subset
E\subset X
, we denote by

\fint_{E}fd\mathfrak{m}:=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(E)}\int_{E}fd\mathfrak{m}.

Let
f\in Lip_{\rm loc}(X)
, the
pointwise Lipschitz constant
or
slope
is given by

{\rm Lip}f(x):=\begin{cases}\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{d(x,y)}=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{r}&{\rm if}x\ {\rm is\ not\ isolated},\\
0&\quad{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

The
Cheeger energy

{\rm Ch}:L^{2}(X)\to[0,+\infty]
is

{\rm Ch}(f):=\frac{1}{2}\inf\Big\{\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{X}({\rm Lip}f_{j})^{2}d\mathfrak{m}:\ f_{j}\in Lip_{\rm loc}(X)\cap L^{2}(X),\ \ f_{j}\overset{L^{2}}{\to}f\Big\}.

For any
f\in W^{1,2}(X)
, it was showed
[
8
,
2
]
that there exists a minimal weak upper gradient
|\nabla f|
such that

{\rm Ch}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mathfrak{m},

and for any
f\in Lip(X)
that
|\nabla f|={\rm Lip}f

\mathfrak{m}-
a.e. in
X
.
The Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(X):=\{f\in L^{2}(X):\ {\rm Ch}(f)<+\infty\}
.

There are several equivalent definitions for the
Riemannian curvature-dimension condition
on metric measure spaces
[
13
,
4
,
7
,
3
]
. We state one as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,+\infty]
, a metric measure space
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
is called an
RCD(K,N)
-space
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

The Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(X)
is a Hilbert space. In this case, it was proved
[
17
]
that for any
f,g\in W^{1,2}(X)
the limit

(2.1)

\left<{\nabla f},{\nabla g}\right>:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{|\nabla(f+\epsilon g)|^{2}-|\nabla f|^{2}}{2\epsilon}

exists and is in
L^{1}(X)
. This provides a canonical Dirichlet form

\mathscr{E}(f,g):=\int_{X}\left<{\nabla f},{\nabla g}\right>d\mathfrak{m}
for any
f,g\in W^{1,2}(X).

We denote by
\Delta
the infinitesimal generator of
\mathscr{E}
and by
\{H_{t}:=e^{t\Delta}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
the corresponding semi-group (heat flow). The domain of
\Delta
is denoted by
D(\Delta)
.

(2)

For any
f\in D(\Delta)
with
\Delta f\in W^{1,2}(X)
, it holds the weak Bochner inequality

(2.2)

\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{2}\Delta gd\mathfrak{m}\geqslant\int_{X}\Big(\frac{(\Delta f)^{2}}{N}+\left<{\nabla f},{\nabla\Delta f}\right>+K|\nabla f|^{2}\Big)gd\mathfrak{m}

for any
g\in D(\Delta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
with
\Delta g\in L^{\infty}(X)
and
g\geqslant 0
.

(3)

There are some point
x_{0}\in X
and constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leqslant c_{1}e^{c_{2}r^{2}}
for all
r>0
.

(4)

For any
f\in W^{1,2}(X)
with
|\nabla f|\leqslant 1

\mathfrak{m}-
a.e. in
X
, there exists a representative
g\in Lip(X)
with Lipschitz constant 1 such that
g=f

\mathfrak{m}-
a.e. in
X
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
. It is a geodesic space (see
[
3
]
), i.e., for any two points
x,y\in X
, there exists a curve
\gamma
connecting them such that the length
L(\gamma)=d(x,y).
Recall that the length of a continuous curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
is defined by

L(\gamma)=\sup_{a=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{m}=b}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d\big(\gamma(a_{i}),\gamma(a_{i+1})\big).

Any closed ball
\overline{B_{R}(x)}\subset X
satisfies a standard assumption that
(\overline{B_{R}(x)},d,\mathfrak{m})
has a volume doubling property and supports an
L^{2}
-Poincaré inequality, both the doubling constant
C_{D}
and the Poincaré constant
C_{P}
depend only on
N,K,R
(see
[
46
,
37
]
).

The semi group
\{H_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
on
L^{2}(X)
admits a heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
on
X
(see
[
43
,
2
]
) such that

H_{t}f(x)=\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)f(y)d\mathfrak{m}(y),\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(X).

The heat kernel is Lipschitz continuous (see
[
26
]
) and satisfies the stochastic completeness, i.e.,

(2.3)

\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)d\mathfrak{m}(y)=1

for all
x\in X
. An upper bound of
p_{t}(x,y)
was proved in
[
27
]
that

p_{t}(x,y)\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K}}{m\big(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x)\big)}\exp\Big(-\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{5t}+C_{N,K}\cdot t\Big)

for some constant
C_{N,K}>0
depending only on
N
and
K
. Erbar–Kuwada–Sturm
[
13
]
proved the Bakry-Ledoux’ estimate: for every
f\in W^{1,2}(X)
and every
t>0
it holds

(2.4)

|\nabla(H_{t}f)|^{2}+\frac{4Kt^{2}}{N(e^{2Kt}-1)}|\Delta H_{t}f|^{2}\leqslant e^{-2Kt}H_{t}(|\nabla f|^{2})\quad\mathfrak{m}{\rm-a.e.\ \ in}\ X.

Definition 2.2

(Local Sobolev Space)
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set. A function
f\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
belongs to

W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, provided, for any
\chi\in Lip_{0}(\Omega)
it holds

f\chi\in W^{1,2}(X)
, where
f\chi
is understood to be
0
outside from
\Omega
. In this case, the function
|\nabla f|:\Omega\to[0,\infty]
is
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. defined by

|\nabla f|:=|\nabla(\chi f)|,\ \ \mathfrak{m}-a.e.\,\mathrm{on}\ \{\chi=1\},

for any
\chi
as above.

The space

W^{1,2}(\Omega):=\{f\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega):\ f,|\nabla f|\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}:=(\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||\nabla f|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)})^{1/2}.

Finally, the space
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
is defined as the
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
-closure of the space of functions
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
with
{\rm supp}(f)\subset\Omega.

Recall the notion of the measure-valued Laplacian in
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open subset. A function
g\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is called to be in
D({\bf\Delta},\Omega)
if there exists a signed Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
such that for any

\phi\in Lip_{0}(\Omega)
it holds

(2.5)

-\int_{\Omega}\left<{\nabla f},{\nabla\phi}\right>d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{\Omega}\phi d\mu.

If such
\mu
exists, then it must be unique. In this case, we will write
{\bf\Delta}g=\mu
.

It was shown
[
16
]
that the operator
{\bf\Delta}
is linear, and both the Leibniz rule and the chain rule hold.
In general, the measure-valued Laplacian
{\bf\Delta}f
for a function
f\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
may not be absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. We consider its Radon-Nikodym decomposition

{\bf\Delta}f=({\bf\Delta}f)^{\rm ac}\cdot\mathfrak{m}+({\bf\Delta}f)^{\rm sing}.

When
\Omega=X
, the compatibility of
{\bf\Delta}
and the
\Delta
has been shown in
[
17
]
, which states that if
f\in W^{1,2}(X)
and
g\in L^{2}(X)
, then

{\bf\Delta}f=g\cdot\mathfrak{m}\Longleftrightarrow f\in D(\Delta)\ {\rm and}\ \Delta f=g.

Let
f\in D({\bf\Delta},\Omega)
and
g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. We denote by
{\bf\Delta}f\geqslant g
as measures (or in the sense of distributions) if
-\int_{\Omega}\left<{\nabla f},{\nabla\phi}\right>d\mathfrak{m}\geqslant\int_{\Omega}g\phi d\mathfrak{m}
for all nonnegative
\phi\in Lip_{0}(\Omega)
.
When
f\in D({\bf\Delta},\Omega)
and
{\bf\Delta}f\geqslant 0
as measures, we have
f^{+}:=\max\{f,0\}\in D({\bf\Delta},\Omega)
and
{\bf\Delta}f^{+}\geqslant 0
as measures. We have also the Maximum principle as follows (see
[
8
]
). Let
f\in D({\bf\Delta},\Omega)
and
{\bf\Delta}f\geqslant 0
as measures. If
f\leqslant c
on
\partial\Omega
in the sense of
(f-c)^{+}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
then
f\leqslant c
almost
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
\Omega.
In particular, if
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
, if
f\leqslant c
on
\partial\Omega
, and if
{\bf\Delta}f\geqslant 0
as measures, then
f\leqslant c
on
\Omega.

Given a function
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
g\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we can solve the (relaxed) Dirichlet problem of the Poisson equation (see, for example,
[
8
]
)

\begin{cases}{\bf\Delta}f=h\\
f-g\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).\end{cases}

The following boundary regularity result for the harmonic functions has been given in
[

]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
. Assume that

\Omega
satisfies an exterior density condition. Then for each harmonic function
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that
f-g\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
for some
g\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Then, after a redefinition on a
set of measure zero,
f
is
C^{\beta}
-continuous at each
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, for some small
\beta\in(0,\alpha)
. Moreover, there exist constants
r_{0}\in(0,R_{0})
and
C>0
such that

\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}|f(x)-f(x_{0})|\leqslant Cr^{\beta}

for all
r\in(0,r_{0})
and all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega,
where
R_{0}
is the number given in the definition of exterior density condition, and
C
depends only on
N,K,{\rm diam}(\Omega),
and a modulus of the Hölder continuity of
g
, i.e., a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

|g(x)-g(x_{0})|\leqslant C_{1}d^{\alpha}(x_{0},x)
for all
x_{0},x\in\overline{\Omega}
. (See Equation (16) in
[
5
]
for the details).

The following corollary of the Laplacian comparison theorem
[
16
]
will be used to construct the barriers.

Lemma 2.5
.

For each
y_{0}\in X
, the distance function
d_{y_{0}}(x):=d(x,y_{0})
is in
D({\bf\Delta},X\setminus\{y_{0}\})
, and there exists a number
m_{N,K}\geqslant 1
such that

(2.6)

{\bf\Delta}d^{-m}_{y_{0}}\geqslant\frac{m^{2}}{2R^{m+2}}\quad{\rm on}\quad B_{R}(y_{0})\setminus\{y_{0}\}

for all
R\in(0,1)
and all
m\geqslant m_{N,K}
, in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

From the Laplacian comparison, we have
d_{y_{0}}(x)\in D({\bf\Delta},X\setminus\{y_{0}\})
and

d_{y_{0}}\cdot{\bf\Delta}d_{y_{0}}\leqslant C_{N,K}\quad{\rm on}\quad B_{1}(y_{0})\setminus\{y_{0}\}

in the sense of distributions, for some constant
C_{N,K}
depending only on
N,K
.

Notice that
d^{-m}_{y_{0}}
is in
Lip_{\rm loc}(B_{1}(y_{0})\setminus\{y_{0}\})
. It follows from the Chain rule
[
16
]
that

\begin{split}{\bf\Delta}d^{-m}_{y_{0}}&=(-m)d_{y_{0}}^{-m-2}\cdot\big(d_{y_{0}}\cdot{\bf\Delta}d_{y_{0}}-(m+1)|\nabla d_{y_{0}}|^{2}\big)\\
&\geqslant-m\big(C_{N,K}-(m+1)\big)d_{y_{0}}^{-m-2}\\
&=(m^{2}-mC_{N,K}+m)\frac{1}{d_{y_{0}}^{m+2}}\geqslant\frac{m^{2}-mC_{N,K}+m}{R^{m+2}}\end{split}

on
B_{R}(y_{0})\setminus\{y_{0}\}
in the sense of distributions, where we have used
d_{y_{0}}\leqslant R
and
|\nabla d_{y_{0}}|=1
almost everywhere in
B_{R}(y_{0}).

Now the desired estimate holds provided
m\geqslant m_{N,K}:=\max\{2C_{N,K},1\}
.
∎

3.
Sobolev spaces for maps with metric targets and harmonic maps

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open domain of an
RCD(K,N)
space
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
such that
\mathfrak{m}(X\backslash\Omega)>0
, and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a complete metric space.

A Borel measurable map
u:\ \Omega\to Y
is said to be in the space
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
if it has a separable range and, for some (hence, for all)
P\in Y
,

\int_{\Omega}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)d\mathfrak{m}(x)<\infty.

We equip with a distance in
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
by

d^{2}_{L^{2}}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),v(x)\big)d\mathfrak{m}(x)

for all
u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y).

Several notions of Sobolev energy for maps into metric targets have been introduced in
[
34
,
29
,
24
,
35
,
40
,
20
]
. We will recall the notions in
[
34
,
35
,
20
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a map
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y),
for each
r>0
, the
approximating energy

E^{\Omega}_{2,r}[u]
at scale
r
is defined as a functional on
C_{0}(\Omega),

E^{\Omega}_{2,r}[u](\phi):=\int_{\Omega}\phi\cdot{\rm ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega]d\mathfrak{m},

where
{\rm ks}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)
is given in (
1.1
).
A map
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
is called in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
if

E_{2}^{\Omega}(u):=\sup_{\phi\in C_{0}(\Omega),\ 0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1}\Big(\limsup_{r\to 0}E^{\Omega}_{2,r}[u](\phi)\Big)<+\infty.

The following properties have been proved in
[
20
]
:

(1)

if
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, there is a function
e_{u}:=e_{2}[u,\Omega]\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, called
energy density
, such that

(3.1)

E^{\Omega}_{2}(u):=\int_{\Omega}e_{u}d\mathfrak{m},

(2)

If
Y=\mathbb{R}
, then the above space
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})

is equivalent to the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
given in §2.

(3)

if
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
and
\phi\in Lip(Y,Z)
, then
\phi\circ u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Z)
. In particular,
d_{Y}(P,u(x))\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
|\nabla d_{Y}(P,u(x))|\leqslant e^{1/2}_{u}
almost everywhere.

Given any
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, we consider the minimizer of

\min_{u\in W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)}E_{2}^{\Omega}(u),

where
W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)
is given in (
1.2
).
Such an energy-minimizing map is called a
harmonic map
.

Let us recall the concept of non-positive curvature in the sense of Alexandrov.

Definition 3.2

(see, for example,
[
6
,
12
]
)
.

A complete space
(Y,d_{Y})
is called to be a
CAT(0)
space if it is a geodesic space and if it holds the following comparison property.
Given any triangle
\triangle PQR\subset Y
and point
S\in QR
with

d_{Y}(Q,S)=d_{Y}(R,S)=\frac{1}{2}d_{Y}(Q,R),

then for a triangle
\triangle\bar{P}\bar{Q}\bar{R}
in 2-dimensional plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

d_{Y}(P,Q)=|\bar{P}-\bar{Q}|,\quad d_{Y}(Q,R)=|\bar{Q}-\bar{R}|,\quad d_{Y}(R,P)=|\bar{R}-\bar{P}|

and a point
\bar{S}\in\bar{Q}\bar{R}
with

|\bar{Q}-\bar{S}|=|\bar{R}-\bar{S}|=\frac{1}{2}|\bar{Q}-\bar{R}|,

we have

d_{Y}(P,S)\leqslant|\bar{P}-\bar{S}|.

It has been proved in
[
20
, Theorem 6.4]
that when
Y
is a
CAT(0)
-space, there always exists a (unique) harmonic map
u\in W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)
for any
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
. Recently, Mondino-Semola
[
39
]
and Gigli
[
18
]
, independently, obtained the following interior regularity result.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
39
,
18
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in an
RCD(K,N)
space for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
with
\mathfrak{m}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
, and let
Y be a
CAT(0)
space. Suppose that
u:\Omega\to Y
is a harmonic map. Then the following two properties are satisfied:

(1)
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
\Omega
, and moreover

(3.2)

\sup_{x,y\in B_{r}(z_{0}),\ x\not=y}\frac{d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)}{d(x,y)}\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K,{\rm diam}(\Omega)}}{r}\inf_{O\in Y}\sqrt{\fint_{B_{2r}(z_{0})}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(\cdot),O\big)d\mathfrak{m}}

for any ball
B_{r}(z_{0})
such that
B_{2r}(z_{0})\subset\Omega
;

(2) for any
P\in Y
it holds

(3.3)

{\bf\Delta}d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)\geqslant 0

in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

The conclusion (1) is proved in
[
39
,
18
]
, and the explicit estimate (
3.2
) is in
[
18
]
.

To show (2), we fix any
\epsilon>0
and put

f_{\epsilon}(x):=(d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}\geqslant\epsilon.

Then we have
f^{2}_{\epsilon}=d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)+\epsilon^{2}
,

(3.4)

|\nabla f_{\epsilon}|=\frac{d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)}{f_{\epsilon}}|\nabla d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)|\leqslant|\nabla d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)|

almost everywhere in
\Omega
, and, by the chain rule
[
16
]
,

2f_{\epsilon}\cdot{\bf{\Delta}}f_{\epsilon}+2|\nabla f_{\epsilon}|^{2}={\bf\Delta}f^{2}_{\epsilon}={\bf\Delta}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)

in the sense of distributions.
Thus, by combining with

{\bf\Delta}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)\geqslant 2e_{u}

as measures (see
[
31
,
39
,
18
]
), and
|\nabla d_{Y}(P,u(x))|^{2}\leqslant e_{u}
, we have

f_{\epsilon}\cdot{\bf\Delta}f_{\epsilon}\geqslant 0

as measures. Notice that
d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)\in C(\Omega)
(see
[
31
,
39
,
18
]
). Hence, we have
f_{\epsilon}\in C(\Omega)
and
f_{\epsilon}\geqslant\epsilon
. Therefore, we conclude
{\bf\Delta}f_{\epsilon}\geqslant 0
as measures for any
\epsilon>0
. That is,

(3.5)

-\int_{\Omega}\left<{\nabla f_{\epsilon}},{\nabla\phi}\right>d\mathfrak{m}\geqslant 0,\quad\forall\phi\in Lip_{0}(\Omega),\ \ \phi\geqslant 0.

From the fact
f_{\epsilon}^{2}=d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)+\epsilon^{2}
and (
3.4
), we know that
\{f_{\epsilon}\}_{0<\epsilon<1}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. (i.e. for each
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
,
\{f_{\epsilon}\}_{0<\epsilon<1}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\prime})
.) Then there exists a sequence of numbers
\{\epsilon_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\epsilon_{j}\to 0^{+}
, such that
f_{\epsilon_{j}}
converges weakly to
d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)
in
W_{\rm loc}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Letting now
\epsilon_{j}\to 0^{+}
in (
3.5
), we conclude that
{\bf\Delta}d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)\geqslant 0
in the sense of distributions.
∎

4.
Boundary regularity of harmonic maps

We will give a proof of Theorem
1.5
in this section. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of an
RCD(K,N)
metric measure
space
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
with
\mathfrak{m}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Without loss of the generality, we assume always
K\leqslant 0
.

4.1.
Boundary estimates for harmonic functions

In this subsection, we will deal with a special case,
Y=\mathbb{R}
, i.e., the harmonic function.
We need a simple lemma as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(X)
be a Lipschitz function with
|\nabla f|\leqslant L
for some
L>0
. There exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(N,K)\geqslant 0
, depending only on
N,K
, such that

(4.1)

|\nabla H_{t}f|(z)\leqslant C_{1}L,

(4.2)

|\Delta H_{t}f|(z)\leqslant C_{1}Lt^{-1/2},

(4.3)

|H_{t}f(z)-f(z)|\leqslant C_{1}Lt^{1/2}

for almost all
z\in X
and all
0<t\leqslant 1.

Proof.

Since
f\in L^{2}(X)
, we know
H_{t}f\in D(\Delta)
.
From the Bakry-Ledoux’s estimate (
2.4
) and the
L^{\infty}(X)
-contraction of
H_{t}
, we have

|\nabla H_{t}f|^{2}\leqslant e^{-2K}H_{t}(|\nabla f|^{2})\leqslant e^{-2K}L^{2}

and

|\Delta H_{t}f|^{2}\leqslant\frac{N(e^{2Kt}-1)}{4Kt^{2}}H_{t}(|\nabla f|^{2})\leqslant\frac{N(e^{2Kt}-1)}{4Kt^{2}}L^{2}

for almost all
z\in X
and all
0<t\leqslant 1
. It follows the desired estimate (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), by using an elementary inequality

e^{s}-1\leqslant s,\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}

and letting
s=2Kt.
(keeping in mind
K\leqslant 0
).
Finally, we have (
4.3
) by

|H_{t}f(z)-f(z)|\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\left|\frac{\partial H_{\tau}f(z)}{\partial s}\right|d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\left|\Delta H_{\tau}f(z)\right|d\tau\leqslant CLt^{1/2}

for almost all
z\in X
and
0<t\leqslant 1
.
The proof is finished.
∎

We consider the growth of a harmonic function near the boundary.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain and satisfy a uniformly exterior condition with constant
R_{0}
. Suppose that
f\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega)
is a harmonic function on
\Omega
with boundary data
g\in Lip(\overline{\Omega})
. Suppose that
g
has a Lipschitz constant
L
and that
g(z_{0})=0
for some
z_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}.
Given any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
R_{\epsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\min\{R_{0},1\})
(depending only on
\epsilon,N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
) such that

(4.4)

M_{x_{0}}(r)\leqslant\max\big\{C_{2}M_{x_{0}}(2r^{\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}})\cdot r^{\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}}+C_{2}Lr,\ Lr^{1-\epsilon}\big\}

for all
r\in(0,R_{\epsilon})
and for all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,
where

M_{x_{0}}(r):=\max\big\{\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}\left(f(x)-f(x_{0})\right),\ Lr^{1-\epsilon}\big\},

and the constant
C_{2}\geqslant 1
depends only on
N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{g}
be a Lipschitz extension of
g
on
X
with
\tilde{g}\in L^{2}(X)
and the same Lipschitz constant
L
. Let
H_{t}\tilde{g}
be the heat flow with
H_{0}\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}
.
For any
t\in(0,1)
, we solve the Dirichlet problems

{\bf\Delta}f_{t}=0\quad{\rm on}\quad\Omega\quad{\rm with}\quad f_{t}-H_{t}\tilde{g}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Since
\Omega
satisfies a uniformly exterior sphere condition, in particular, an exterior density condition, we know from Lemma
2.4
that
f,f_{t}\in C(\overline{\Omega}).

Notice that
f_{t}-f
is harmonic on
\Omega
with the boundary value
H_{t}\tilde{g}-g
. It follows

|f_{t}-f|\big|_{\partial\Omega}=|H_{t}\tilde{g}-\tilde{g}|\big|_{\partial\Omega}\leqslant C_{1}Lt^{1/2}

for all
t\in(0,1)
, where
C_{1}
is given in Lemma
4.1
. The maximum principle yields that

(4.5)

\left|f_{t}-f\right|\leqslant C_{1}Lt^{1/2}\quad\forall x\in\Omega,\ \ \forall t\in(0,1).

Without loss of the generality, we can assume that
C_{1}\geqslant\max\{1,{\rm diam}(\Omega)\}
.
Here and in the sequel of this proof, we denote
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,
as constants depending only on
N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
.

Putting

(4.6)

v_{t}=f_{t}-H_{t}\tilde{g}\quad{\rm on}\ \Omega,

it follows that
v_{t}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
for all
t\in(0,1)
, and for any
x\in B_{2R}(x_{0})\cap{\overline{\Omega}}
,

v_{t}(x)\leqslant f(x)-g(x)+2C_{1}Lt^{1/2}\leqslant M_{x_{0}}(2R)+2LR+2C_{1}Lt^{1/2},

where we have used the definition of
M_{x_{0}}(2R)
, (
4.5
), (
4.3
),
g(x_{0})=f(x_{0})
, and that
g
is
L
-Lipschitz on
\Omega
. From the definition of
M_{x_{0}}(2R)
, we have
M_{x_{0}}(2R)\geqslant L(2R)^{1-\epsilon}\geqslant 2LR
provided
2R\leqslant 1
.
Therefore, for any
x\in B_{2R}(x_{0})\cap{\overline{\Omega}}
and
t<1
we have

(4.7)

v_{t}(x)\leqslant 2M_{x_{0}}(2R)+2C_{1}Lt^{1/2}

provided
2R\leqslant 1
.

Fix some
m\geqslant m_{N,K}
given in Lemma
2.5
such that
\frac{2^{m}\cdot C_{1}^{2}}{m^{2}}\geqslant 8
, (say, for example,
m=\max\{m_{N,K},12\}
and notice that
C_{1}\geqslant 1
). Let
\tilde{R}_{\epsilon}
be determined by

(4.8)

\frac{4}{C^{2}_{1}\tilde{R}^{2\epsilon}_{\epsilon}}=\frac{2^{m}}{m^{2}}.\quad\qquad({\rm It\ implies}\ \ 2\tilde{R}_{\epsilon}\leqslant 1.)

For any
R<\tilde{R}_{\epsilon}
, we choose
t
such that

(4.9)

\frac{4M}{R^{2}}\cdot\frac{m^{2}}{2^{m}}=C_{1}Lt^{-1/2},\qquad{\rm and\ let}\quad B=\frac{4MR^{m}}{2^{m-1}},

where
M:=M_{x_{0}}(2R).

Noticing that
\frac{R}{M}\leqslant\frac{R}{L(2R)^{1-\epsilon}}\leqslant\frac{R^{\epsilon}}{L}
, we have

(4.10)

t^{1/2}=\frac{C_{1}LM}{4}\cdot\frac{R^{2}}{M^{2}}\cdot\frac{2^{m}}{m^{2}}\leqslant\frac{M}{C_{1}L}\left(\frac{R^{2\epsilon}}{\tilde{R}_{\epsilon}^{2\epsilon}}\right)\leqslant\frac{M}{C_{1}L}.

We first check that
t\leqslant 1
. Indeed, it follows from
C_{1}\geqslant\max\{1,{\rm diam}(\Omega)\}
and
M=M(2R)\leqslant\max\{{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}f,L\}\leqslant\max\{{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}g,L\}\leqslant L\cdot\max\{{\rm diam}(\Omega),1\}
(since the maximal principle and
g(z_{0})=0
for some
z_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
).

Since
\Omega
satisfies a uniformly exterior sphere condition (and
X
is a geodesic space), for any
R\in(0,R_{0}/2)
, there exists a point
y_{0}
such that

d(y_{0},\partial\Omega)=d(y_{0},x_{0})=R/2.

We consider a barrier

h(x)=B\big((R/2)^{-m}-d_{y_{0}}^{-m}(x)\big)

on
B_{R}(y_{0}).
From Lemma
2.5
and (
4.9
), we have

{\bf\Delta}h\leqslant-B\frac{m^{2}}{2R^{m+2}}=-\frac{4MR^{m}}{2^{m-1}}\cdot\frac{m^{2}}{2R^{m+2}}=-\frac{4M}{R^{2}}\cdot\frac{m^{2}}{2^{m}}=-C_{1}Lt^{-1/2}

on
B_{R}(y_{0}).
Consider
\Omega^{\prime}:=\Omega\cap B_{R}(y_{0})
, then
\partial\Omega^{\prime}=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
, where
\Gamma_{1}=\partial B_{R}(y_{0})\cap\overline{\Omega}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\partial\Omega\cap\overline{B_{R}(y_{0})}.
It follows from (
4.7
) and (
4.10
) that

v_{t}\leqslant 4M\leqslant\frac{2^{m}-1}{2^{m-1}}\frac{4MR^{m}}{R^{m}}=h\quad{\rm on}\ \Gamma_{1},

and

v_{t}=0\leqslant h\quad{\rm on}\ \Gamma_{2}.

By (
4.2
), we have

{\bf\Delta}v_{t}=-\Delta H_{t}\tilde{g}\geqslant-C_{1}Lt^{-1/2},\quad v_{t}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

for all
t\in(0,1)
.
Using the maximum principle, we conclude that
v_{t}\leqslant h
on
\Omega^{\prime}.
Hence, noticing that
h(x_{0})=0
, we have

(4.11)

v_{t}(x)\leqslant h(x)\leqslant d(x_{0},x)\cdot|\nabla h|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leqslant d(x_{0},x)\cdot\frac{mB}{(R/2)^{m+1}}=16Mm\frac{d(x,x_{0})}{R}

for all
x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
. Now for any
r<R/2
and
x\in B_{r}(x_{0})
, we conclude that

\begin{split}f(x)&\leqslant f_{t}(x)+C_{1}Lt^{1/2}=v_{t}(x)+H_{t}\tilde{g}(x)+C_{1}Lt^{1/2}\\
&\leqslant 16mM\frac{r}{R}+g(x)+2C_{1}Lt^{1/2}\\
&\leqslant 16mM\frac{r}{R}+Lr+g(x_{0})+2\frac{(C_{1}L)^{2}R^{2}}{4M}\cdot\frac{2^{m}}{m^{2}},\end{split}

where we have used (
4.5
), (
4.3
), (
4.11
), (
4.6
) and the Lipschitz continuous of
g
. Therefore, by using
f(x_{0})=g(x_{0})
and
R/M\leqslant R^{\epsilon}/L
, we conclude that

M(r)\leqslant 16m\frac{M}{R}r+Lr+C_{3}LR^{1+\epsilon}

or
M(r)\leqslant Lr^{1-\epsilon}
for any
r<R/2
, where
C_{3}=C^{2}_{1}2^{m-1}/(m^{2})
. Now we choose
r=R^{1+\epsilon}
to conclude the desired (
4.4
) with
C_{2}=\max\{16m,C_{3}+1\}
. To ensure
r<R/2
, we assume
r<2^{-1/(\epsilon+\epsilon^{2})}
. The constant

R_{\epsilon}:=\min\big\{\tilde{R}_{\epsilon}^{1/(1+\epsilon)},\ (R_{0}/2)^{1/(1+\epsilon)},\ 2^{-1/(\epsilon+\epsilon^{2})}\big\},

where
\tilde{R}_{\epsilon}
is given in (
4.8
).
This finishes the proof.
∎

The main result in this subsection is given as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain and satisfy a uniformly exterior condition with constant
R_{0}
. Suppose that
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
is a harmonic function on
\Omega
with boundary data
g\in Lip(\overline{\Omega})
. Suppose
g(z_{0})=0
for some
z_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a number
R^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\min\{1,R_{0}\})
(depending only on
\epsilon,N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
) such that for any ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,R^{\prime}_{\epsilon})
it holds

(4.12)

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}|f(x)-f(x_{0})|\leqslant C_{\epsilon}L\cdot r^{1-\epsilon},

where the constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
depends only on
\epsilon,N,K
,
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
, and the constant
L
is a Lipschitz constant of
g
.

Proof.

The maximum principle implies

\max_{\overline{\Omega}}|f|\leqslant\max_{\overline{\Omega}}|g|\leqslant L\cdot{\rm diam}(\Omega),

since
g(z_{0})=0
for some point
z_{0}
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\epsilon_{j}:=2^{-j}
, and we define a sequence of numbers
\{a_{j}\}
by

(4.13)

a_{1}=\frac{1}{3},\quad a_{j+1}=\frac{a_{j}}{1+\epsilon_{j}}+\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{1+\epsilon_{j}}=\frac{a_{j}+2^{-j}}{1+2^{-j}}.

It is easy to check

(4.14)

a_{j+1}\leqslant 1-\epsilon_{j},\ \quad a_{j+1}\geqslant a_{j}\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}a_{j}=1.

Since
-f
is harmonic function with boundary data
-g
, by replacing
f
with
-f
, and noting
\lim_{j\to\infty}a_{j}\to 1
, it suffices to show that
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
R_{k}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\min\{R_{0},1\})
such that

(4.15)

M_{x_{0}}(r)\leqslant C_{4}^{k}Lr^{a_{k}}

for all
r\in(0,R_{k})
and all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, where the constant
C_{4}:=3C_{2}({\rm diam}(\Omega)+1)
, and
C_{2}
is as in Lemma
4.2
.

Let
\epsilon_{1}=1/2
. By using Lemma
4.2
and
\max_{\overline{\Omega}}|f|\leqslant L\cdot{\rm diam}(\Omega),
there exists
R_{\epsilon_{1}}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\min\{1,R_{0}\})
such that

M_{x_{0}}(r)\leqslant\max\big\{C_{2}\cdot(L\cdot{\rm diam}(\Omega))\cdot r^{1/3}+C_{2}Lr,\ Lr^{1/2}\big\}\leqslant C_{4}L\cdot r^{1/3}

for all all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,R_{\epsilon_{1}})
,
r<1
.

We now argue by induction. Given
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we assume that
M_{x_{0}}(r)\leqslant C_{4}^{j}L\cdot r^{a_{j}}
for all all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,R_{\epsilon_{j}})
. By using Lemma
4.2
again, there exists
R_{\epsilon_{j+1}}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\min\{1,R_{0}\})
such that

\begin{split}M_{x_{0}}(r)&\leqslant\max\big\{C_{2}C_{4}^{j}L\cdot(2r^{\frac{1}{1+\epsilon_{j}}})^{a_{j}}r^{\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{1+\epsilon_{j}}}+C_{2}Lr,\ Lr^{1-\epsilon_{j}}\big\}\\
&=\max\big\{C_{2}C_{4}^{j}L\cdot 2^{a_{j}}r^{a_{j+1}}+C_{2}Lr,\ Lr^{1-\epsilon_{j}}\big\}\qquad\quad({\rm by}\ \ \eqref{equ-4.13})\\
&\leqslant(2C_{2}C_{4}^{j}+C_{2})L\cdot r^{a_{j+1}},\qquad\quad({\rm by}\ \ \eqref{equ-4.14})\end{split}

for all
r\in(0,R_{\epsilon_{j+1}})
and all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Notice that
2C_{2}C_{4}^{j}+C_{2}\leqslant C_{4}^{j+1}
. Now the proof of (
4.15
), and hence the proof of the theorem, is finished.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The above estimate implies
u\in C^{1-\epsilon}(\overline{\Omega})
. See the next subsection for the general case of harmonic maps.

4.2.
Boundary regularity for harmonic maps

This subsection will deal with the general case that
Y
is a
CAT(0)
-space. Let
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
and let
u\in W^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega,Y)
be a harmonic map with boundary data
w
.

We begin with the following global boundedness.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
w\in C(\overline{\Omega},Y)
and denote by

{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w:=\max_{x,y\in\Omega}d_{Y}\big(w(x),w(y)\big).

Then there exists
P\in Y
such that
d_{Y}(P,u(x))\leqslant{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w
for all
x\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Notice that

d_{Y}(u(x),w(x))\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and the triangle inequality imply

[d_{Y}(u(x),P)-d_{Y}(w(x),P)]^{+}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

for any
P\in Y
.
Fixing arbitrarily
P\in w(\Omega)
, we have
d_{Y}(P,w(x))\leqslant{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w,

and hence

[d_{Y}(u(x),P)-{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w]^{+}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Now, by the maximum principle and the sub-harmonicity of
d_{Y}(u(x),P)
(see Theorem
3.3
(2)), we get

d_{Y}(u(x),P)\leqslant{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w,\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ \ \Omega.

From Theorem
3.3
(1), we know that
u\in C(\Omega)
. Therefore, we have

d_{Y}(u(x),P)\leqslant{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w
for all
x\in\Omega.

∎

We now extend the boundary estimate in Theorem
4.3
from harmonic functions to harmonic maps.

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset X
satisfies a uniformly exterior condition with constant
R_{0}
and
w\in Lip(\overline{\Omega},Y)
. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
it holds

(4.16)

d_{Y}\big(u(x),w(x_{0})\big)\leqslant C_{\epsilon}L_{w}d^{1-\epsilon}(x,x_{0})

for all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
x\in\Omega
with
d(x,x_{0})<R^{\prime}_{\epsilon}
, where
R^{\prime}_{\epsilon}
and
C_{\epsilon}
are as in Theorem
4.3
, and

L_{w}:=\sup_{x,y\in\overline{\Omega}}\frac{d_{Y}\big(w(x),w(y)\big)}{d(x,y)}.

In particular,
u
is continuous at
x_{0}
and
u(x_{0})=w(x_{0})
.

Proof.

Fix any point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. We solve the Dirichlet problem

{\bf\Delta}f(x)=0\quad{\rm on}\ \Omega\qquad{\rm and}\qquad f(x)-d_{Y}(w(x_{0}),w(x))\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Notice that, by the triangle inequality, the function
g_{x_{0}}(x):=d_{Y}(w(x_{0}),w(x))
is Lipschitz continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
with a Lipschitz constant

L_{g_{x_{0}}}\leqslant L_{w}\quad{\rm and}\quad g_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=0.

According to Theorem
4.3
, we have

(4.17)

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}|f(x)-f(x_{0})|\leqslant C_{\epsilon}L_{w}r^{1-\epsilon},

for any ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,R^{\prime}_{\epsilon})
.

By using Theorem
3.3
(2), we know that
d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))-f(x)
is subharmonic on
\Omega
. Now we consider its boundary value on
\partial\Omega
.
From

d_{Y}(u(x),w(x))\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and the triangle inequality, we have

[d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))-d_{Y}(w(x),w(x_{0}))]^{+}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Hence, by
[d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))-f(x)]^{+}\leqslant[d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))-d_{Y}(w(x),w(x_{0}))]^{+}+[d_{Y}(w(x),w(x_{0}))-f(x)]^{+}
, we obtain

[d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))-f(x)]^{+}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Now the maximum principle implies that

d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))\leqslant f(x),\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ \ \Omega.

Notice that
u\in C(\Omega)
(by Theorem
3.3
) and
f\in C(\Omega)
, we get

d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))\leqslant f(x),\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

By combining this and (
4.17
) we have

d_{Y}(u(x),w(x_{0}))\leqslant f(x)\leqslant C_{\epsilon}L_{w}r^{1-\epsilon},\qquad\forall\ x\in B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega

for any ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,R^{\prime}_{\epsilon})
. Hence we have finished the proof.
∎

When the boundary data
w
is Hölder continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
, the same proof of Theorem
4.6
implies the following result.

Corollary 4.7
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset X
satisfies an exterior density condition with constant
R_{0}
, and
w\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega},Y)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then, there exist two constants
\beta\in(0,\alpha)
and
r_{0}\in(0,R_{0})
such that

d_{Y}\big(u(x),w(x_{0})\big)\leqslant Cd^{\beta}(x,x_{0})

for all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
x\in\Omega
with
d(x,x_{0})<r_{0}
,
where
R_{0}
is the number given in the definition of exterior density condition, and
C
depends only on
N,K,{\rm diam}(\Omega),
and the modulus of a Hölder continuity of
w
.

Proof.

The same argument is as in the proof of Theorem
4.6
, by replacing Theorem
4.3
with Lemma
2.4
. Notice also that a modulus of the Hölder continuity of
w
dominates the modulus of the Hölder continuity of
g_{x_{0}}
for all
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega.

∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We first prove (B). From Lemma
4.5
, we have
d_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big)\leqslant{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w:=\bar{M}
for all
x\in\Omega
.

For any two point
x,y\in\overline{\Omega}
with
d(x,y):=r
. We can assume

r<R^{\prime}_{\epsilon}/20:=R_{1},

where
R^{\prime}_{\epsilon}
is as in Theorem
4.6
. Suppose not, from Lemma
4.5
, we get
d_{Y}(u(x),u(y))\leqslant 2\bar{M}/R_{1}\cdot r.

We split the following argument into three cases: (i)
d(x,\partial\Omega)>4R_{1}
, (ii)
4r<d(x,\partial\Omega)<5R_{1}
, and (iii)
d(x,\partial\Omega)<5r.

(i)
d(x,\partial\Omega)>4R_{1}
. It follows
B_{2R_{1}}(x)\subset\Omega
, by using Theorem
3.3
(1) to the ball
B_{R_{1}}(x)
, we have (notice that
y\in B_{R_{1}}(x)
)

(4.18)

d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K,{\rm diam}(\Omega)}}{R_{1}}\bar{M}\cdot d(x,y).

(ii)
4r<d(x,\partial\Omega)<5R_{1}
. Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
such that
d(x,x_{0})=d(x,\partial\Omega):=\bar{d}
. It follows
B_{\bar{d}/2}(x)\subset\Omega
, by using Theorem
3.3
(1) to the ball
B_{\bar{d}/4}(x)
and Theorem
4.6
, we have

(4.19)

\begin{split}d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)&\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K,{\rm diam}(\Omega)}}{\bar{d}/4}\sup_{B_{\bar{d}/2}(x)}d_{Y}((u(x),u(x_{0})\big)\cdot d(x,y)\\
&\leqslant C_{\epsilon,w}\frac{r^{\epsilon}}{\bar{d}^{\epsilon}}\cdot r^{1-\epsilon}\leqslant C_{\epsilon,w}r^{1-\epsilon},\qquad({\rm since}\ \ r\leqslant\bar{d},)\end{split}

where
C_{\epsilon,w}
depends only on
\epsilon,L_{w},N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
.

(iii)
d(x,\partial\Omega)<5r
. Let
x_{0},y_{0}
be two points in
\partial\Omega
such that
d(x,x_{0})=d(x,\partial\Omega)
and
d(y,y_{0})=d(y,\partial\Omega)
. By using Theorem
4.6
to both
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
, we have

(4.20)

\begin{split}d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)&\leqslant d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(x_{0})\big)+d_{Y}\big(u(y),u(y_{0})\big)+d_{Y}\big(u(x_{0}),u(y_{0})\big)\\
&\leqslant C_{\epsilon,w}\big(d^{1-\epsilon}(x,x_{0})+d^{1-\epsilon}(y,y_{0})\big)+L_{w}d(x_{0},y_{0})\\
&\leqslant C_{\epsilon,w}(2\cdot(5r)^{1-\epsilon})+L_{w}\big(d(x,x_{0})+d(x,y)+d(y,y_{0})\big)\\
&\leqslant 10C_{\epsilon,w}\cdot r^{1-\epsilon}+11L_{w}\cdot r,\end{split}

where
C_{\epsilon,w}
depends only on
\epsilon,L_{w},N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
.

Combining (
4.18
)-(
4.20
) in the above three cases, we obtain

(4.21)

\begin{split}d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)\leqslant C\cdot r^{1-\epsilon},\end{split}

for all
x,y
with
d(x,y)\leqslant R_{\epsilon}^{\prime}/20
, where
C_{\epsilon,w}
depends only on
\epsilon,{\rm osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}w,L_{w},R_{\epsilon}^{\prime},N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
. This finishes the proof of (B).

The proof of (A) is the same as the above, by replacing Theorem
4.6
with Corollary
4.7
. The proof is completed.
∎

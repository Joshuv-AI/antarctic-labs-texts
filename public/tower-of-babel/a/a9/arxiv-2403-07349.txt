Andreas Malmendier, Michael T. Schultz

Abstract
. A fundamental object of study in mirror symmetry of n n -dimensional Fano varieties is the A-side connection on small quantum cohomology. When the Picard rank is 1, the Borel transform relates the quantum differential operator of the Fano to the Picard-Fuchs operator of the mirror to the associated pencil of anticanonical Calabi-Yau ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -folds on the Fano variety. Expanding on related work by W. Yang on the Beukers-Peters pencil of K3 surfaces associated with Apéry’s proof for the irrationality of ζ ⁡ ( 3 ) \zeta(3) , for such operators we define holomorphic prepotentials, virtual Yukawa couplings, and virtual instanton numbers, analogous to the usual ingredients of Calabi-Yau mirror symmetry. We prove that when the underlying Calabi-Yau operator is modular, the virtual Yukawa coupling is a modular form of weight- ( n + 1 ) (n+1) , with the holomorphic prepotential as an Eichler integral. We then analyze the quantum differential operators for modular pencils of K3 surfaces arising as Dolgachev-Nikulin-Pinkham mirrors for the anticanonical linear systems for the 17 deformation classes of Fano threefolds of Picard rank-1 classified by Iskovskikh from the perspective of Golyshev & Zagier’s proof of the Gamma conjecture for such Fanos, the natural setting of Yang’s work. Here, the virtual instanton numbers are proven to be periodic integers with period equal to the level of the modular subgroup. Finally, we conjecture that the geometric nature of these virtual instanton numbers can be understood in terms of relative genus zero Gromov-Witten invariants of the associated log Calabi-Yau pair from the Fano threefold and anticanonical K3.

1.
Introduction

Mirror symmetry has allowed both mathematicians and physicists deep insight into the geometry of both complex algebraic varieties and supersymmetric field theories, in particular through the development and study of Gromov-Witten invariants. In geometry, Gromov-Witten invariants are numbers
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},\dots
that, in certain situations, count pseudoholomorphic curves of appropriate genus and degree meeting prescribed intersection theoretic data in a given symplectic manifold, while in physics we may think of these invariants as arising through instanton corrections of Type-II string theories compactified on certain complex manifolds. Gromov-Witten invariants for Calabi-Yau threefolds are encoded in the Lambert expansion of a generating function known as Yukawa coupling
\mathscr{Y}
. Standard methods with both mathematical and physical footing show that the genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants of elliptic curves and K3 surfaces are trivial, or equivalently, that the associated Yukawa couplings are constant.

About twelve years before the seminal calculation in mirror symmetry for Calabi-Yau threefolds by Candelas, de la Ossa, Green & Parkes
[
15
]
, Apéry stunned the mathematical community by showing that
\zeta(3)
is irrational
[
4
]
, where
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function. Apéry’s proof of the irrationality of
\zeta(3)
utilized two sequences
A_{n},B_{n}
of rational numbers that simultaneously satisfy the linear recursion relation, given by

(1.1)

n^{3}u_{n-1}-(2n+1)(17n^{2}+17n+5)u_{n}+(n+1)^{3}u_{n+1}=0,

with initial conditions
(A_{0}=1,A_{1}=5)
and
(B_{0}=0,B_{1}=6)
, beginning as

A_{n}=1,\;5,\;73,\;1445,\;\dots,\qquad B_{n}=0,\;6,\;\frac{351}{4},\;\frac{62531}{36},\;\dots\;.

Apéry showed in fact that
A_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
n\geq 0
. A similar technique, involving a second order linear recursion, also yielded a new proof for the irrationality of
\zeta(2)=\pi^{2}/6
. While direct, these results were so surprising that it lead many in the following generation to uncover the mechanisms lurking behind Apéry’s proof. In particular, Beukers
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
and Beukers & Peters
[
11
]
showed that there are fundamental connections between these irrationality proofs and elliptic curves, K3 surfaces, and modular forms. Yet deeper connections continue to emerge to more modern concepts like Calabi-Yau motives and Landau-Ginzburg models, for example in work by Kerr
[
41
]
, and in work of Golyshev, Kerr, & Sasaki
[
27
]
.

Amazingly, there is a confluence of the aforementioned ideas: on the one hand, through open-string mirror symmetry (see references in §
3.3
), and on the other hand through the relation between mirror symmetry for Fano varieties, as in Golyshev
[
30
]
, and the Gamma conjecture, put forward by Galkey, Golyshev, & Iritani
[
26
]
. Most relevant for this article is work by Golyshev & Zagier
[
31
]
, where this conjecture was proved directly for the precisely 17 deformation classes of smooth Fano threefolds of Picard rank-1, classified by Iskovskikh
[
39
,
40
]
. Roughly speaking, a key observation is that the certain families of elliptic curves or K3 surfaces admit modular parameterizations
[
31
,
68
]
, similar to those discovered by Beukers as underlying Apéry’s proof, and moreover that these elliptic or K3 pencils also naturally arise as suitable mirror varieties to anticanonical linear systems on the Fano varieties in question, to give special cases of the Gamma conjecture. Let us describe the situation for
\zeta(3)
in some preliminary detail.

In
[
64
]
, W. Yang investigated the geometry of the pencil of K3 surfaces
X_{t}
, discovered by Beukers & Peters
[
11
]
in their investigation of the geometry underlying Apéry’s irrationality proof for
\zeta(3)
. The pencil is described in affine coordinates
(x,y,z)
as the locus of the equation

X_{t}\colon\quad 1-(1-xy)z-txyz(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)=0

with
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and was shown to generically have Picard rank-19. Assembling the sequences
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
in generating functions,

\omega(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}t^{n}\;,\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}t^{n}\;,

Beukers and Peters showed that
\omega(t)
is the unique holomorphic period of
X_{t}
near
t=0
such that
\omega(0)=1
. The Picard-Fuchs operator annihilating the period integrals is the third-order linear differential operator

\mathcal{L}_{3}=\theta^{3}-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^{2}+17\theta+5)+t^{2}(\theta+1)^{3}\,

with
\theta=t\frac{d}{dt}
. Via the Frobenius method, the differential equation
\mathcal{L}_{3}(\omega(t))=0
is equivalent to the linear recursion (
1.1
). Beukers showed
[
9
]
that the parameter
t=t(\tau)
is a Hauptmodul for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(6)^{+}
, which is the subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
generated by
\Gamma_{0}(6)\subset\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
and the associated Fricke involution. Here,
\tau
lies in the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
and is identified as the ratio
\tau(t)=\omega_{1}(t)/\omega_{0}(t)
of canonical logarithmic solutions near
t=0
, with
\omega(t)=\omega_{0}(t)
. In other words, the functional inverse
t(\tau)
is the mirror map of the pencil
X_{t}
of K3 surfaces (§
2.1
). In fact, Beukers showed that

\displaystyle t(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{12}\eta(6\tau)^{12}}{\eta(2\tau)^{12}\eta(3\tau)^{12}}\;,

\displaystyle\omega(t(\tau))

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta(2\tau)^{7}\eta(3\tau)^{7}}{\eta(\tau)^{5}\eta(6\tau)^{5}}=F(\tau)\,,

where
\eta(\tau)
is the Dedekind eta function, so that
\omega
becomes a modular form of weight-two for
\Gamma_{0}(6)^{+}
.

The generating function
\gamma(t)
satisfies a
non
-homogeneous Picard-Fuchs equation, namely
\mathcal{L}_{3}(\gamma(t))=6t
. There are numerous physical and mathematical interpretations of such equations which have occupied a growing body in the literature going back to Griffith’s work
[
35
]
on normal functions, and more recently in string theoretic considerations by Morrison & Walcher
[
54
]
, Li, Lian, & Yau
[
47
]
, and by Chen, Doran, Kerr, & Lewis
[
17
]
. Indeed, in large part the genesis of this present work can be seen as having roots in these perspectives.

Furthermore, Yang showed that
\gamma(t)
is a solution of a homogeneous
fourth
order equation, and is a fundamental solution of the differential operator
\mathcal{D}_{4}=\theta\cdot\mathcal{L}_{3}
at
t=\infty
, with

\mathcal{D}_{4}=\theta^{4}-t(\theta+1)(2\theta+1)(17\theta^{2}+17\theta+5)+t^{2}(\theta+1)^{3}(\theta+2)\,.

This operator behaves in many respects like the Picard-Fuchs operator of a pencil of Calabi-Yau threefolds, and so Yang imitated the famous mirror symmetry calculation by Candelas, de la Ossa, Green & Parkes
[
15
]
which relied on such an operator. In doing so, Yang defined a holomorphic prepotential
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{D}_{4}}=\omega_{4}(t)/\omega_{0}(t)
, where
\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{4}(t)
form the canonical logarithmic basis of solutions at
t=0
for
\mathcal{D}_{4}
, and a Yukawa coupling defined by the equation

\mathscr{Y}=\frac{d\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{D}_{4}}}{d\tau^{3}}=6+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{3}N_{k}\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\,.

Here,
\tau
is the inverse of the mirror map of the K3 pencil
X_{t}
,
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
, and the numbers
N_{k}
in the Lambert expansion of
\mathscr{Y}
play the role of the genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants of a pencil Calabi-Yau threefolds. In fact, it is straightforward to show that
\mathcal{D}_{4}
is not the Picard-Fuchs operator of
any
pencil of Calabi-Yau threefolds with
h^{2,1}=1
(Remark
3.3
). We will refer to this kind of operator as an almost Calabi-Yau operator (Definition
4.1
). Although the genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants of K3 surfaces are known to be trivial, on an experimental basis, Yang determined that the numbers
N_{k}
appearing in the expansion of the prepotential were integers, and moreover that they exhibited six-fold periodicity,

\displaystyle N_{1}=-42,\hskip 14.22636ptN_{2}=-39

\displaystyle,\hskip 14.22636ptN_{3}=-44,\hskip 14.22636ptN_{4}=-39,

\displaystyle N_{5}=-42,\hskip 14.22636ptN_{6}

\displaystyle=-34,\hskip 14.22636ptN_{k+6}=N_{k}\,.

We will refer to these as
virtual
instanton numbers. Furthermore, the Yukawa coupling
\mathscr{Y}
was shown to be a modular form of weight-four, that factorizes into the product of two modular forms of weight-two, specifically
\mathscr{Y}=F(\tau)H(\tau)
, where
F(\tau)
is the eta product of weight-two for the holomorphic period of
X_{t}
near
t=0
, and a weight-two modular form

H(\tau)=2\Theta^{2}_{\mathrm{hex}}(2\tau)-\Theta^{2}_{\mathrm{hex}}(\tau)\,,

which is built from standard theta constants of the planar hexagonal lattice,

\Theta_{\mathrm{hex}}(\tau)=\theta_{3}(0,\tau)\theta_{3}(0,3\tau)-\theta_{2}(0,\tau)\theta_{2}(0,3\tau)\,.

However, Yang found no explanation for these curious results.

Yang also found similar results for the famous pencil of Picard rank-19 deformed Fermat K3 surfaces, constituting the mirror to family of generic quartic hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. This family admits a hypergeometric Picard-Fuchs operator
\mathcal{L}_{3}
, and Yang again studied the behavior of the almost Calabi-Yau threefold operator
\mathcal{D}_{4}=\theta\cdot\mathcal{L}_{3}
(listed respectively in Tables
1
and
2
under the entry
N=2
), finding experimentally in this case two-fold periodic integers

N_{1}=-480,\hskip 14.22636ptN_{2}=-240,\hskip 14.22636ptN_{k+2}=N_{k}\,.

However, no explanation for this behavior or an interpretation of these virtual instanton numbers was found, and in this case Yang was not able to determine if the associated Yukawa coupling was a modular form.

The purpose of this article is to fill this gap, clarify the underlying structures behind Yang’s results, and answer the majority of questions posed by Yang’s work, resulting in a conjecture that we believe refines Yang’s remaining quesions and places them in a more suitable realm for future analysis. In doing so, we find and prove analogous results for all families of K3 surfaces of Picard rank-19 that arise as Dolgachev-Nikulin-Pinkham mirrors of anticanonical linear systems of the 17 deformation classes of Fano threefolds of Picard rank-1. We suggest that our results are closely related to the Gamma conjecture, and in particular to Iritani’s recent work
[
38
]
conjecturing a connection between mirror symmetry and the Gamma conjecture for Fano varieties, mirrors of their anticanonical divisors, and the local Calabi-Yau variety formed as total space of the canonical bundle of the Fano. Particularly, we conjecture, following work of van Garrel, Graber, & Ruddat
[
61
]
, Fan, Tseng, You
[
25
]
, and You
[
66
]
, that the virtual instanton numbers encode relative invariants of the log Calabi-Yau pair formed from the ambient Fano and a smooth anticanonical divisor.

This article is structured as follows: In §
2

\zeta(2)

\mathcal{D}_{4}=\theta\cdot\mathcal{L}_{3}
. This is a geometric reason to back up Yang’s rationale to study such an operator. We call the operator
\mathcal{D}_{4}
the quantum differential operator associated with the pencil of K3 surfaces. We supply relevant definitions and prove basic properties of these operators for any order
n\geq 2
.

Section §
4

\mathcal{D}_{n+1}
. For such operators we define a notion of a holomorphic prepotential, and virtual Yukawa couplings and instanton numbers in Definition
4.2
; notice that we employ a slightly different normalization than Yang in
[

that when the underlying Calabi-Yau operator admits a modular parameterization, the virtual Yukawa coupling is a modular form of weight-

that the associated virtual instanton numbers are integers, and are always
N

is the level of the modular subgroup. Our results depend crucially on Golyshev & Zagier’s work
[

In §

As we will show, we can also compute this data for all families of elliptic curves that are related to the K3 pencils discussed above through a combination of twists and rational pullback. A crucial insight into the utility of these virtual instanton numbers comes from work of Stienstra

The precise mechanism behind Stienstra’s observation appears to be the Iritani’s work on the Gamma conjecture

Inspired by this, our discussion culminates in a conjecture that we state in §

Geometry of maximally unipotent operators

In this section we review some properties of Calabi-Yau varieties and Calabi-Yau operators
[
2
,
12
,
13
]
relevant to classical mirror symmetry of Calabi-Yau threefolds. We review geometry of so-called modular differential operators
[
31
,
67
]
in §
2.2
, and review the twist construction of Doran & Malmendier
[
24
]
in §
2.3
that allows for the explicit construction of Calabi-Yau varieties, realizing certain Calabi-Yau operators as their Picard-Fuchs systems.

2.1.
Calabi-Yau varieties and operators

In this article we will be concerned with ordinary linear differential operators
\mathcal{L}_{n}
of order
n=2,3,4
of the form

\mathcal{L}_{n}=a_{n}(t)\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}+a_{n-1}(t)\,\frac{d^{n-1}}{dt^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}(t)\,\frac{d}{dt}+a_{0}(t)

of Fuchsian type, i.e., with only regular singular points. The coefficients
a_{n},\dots,a_{0}\in\mathbb{C}[t]
will be polynomials in
t
. We will often represent
\mathcal{L}_{n}
as a polynomial in the logarithmic derivative
\theta=t\frac{d}{dt}
,

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}_{n}=P_{0}(\theta)+tP_{1}(\theta)+\cdots+t^{k}P_{k}(\theta)

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where each
P_{i}(\theta)
is a polynomial of degree
n
. This assures that the coefficients
a_{0}(t),\dots,a_{n}(t)
are algebraic functions. A small but important point that becomes relevant in §
3
about this notation is that by writing
\mathcal{L}_{n}
in the form of Equation (
2.1
) implies that each
a_{i}(t)
is divisible by
t
.

In particular, we will be concerned with Fuchsian operators
\mathcal{L}_{n}
associated to certain algebraic varieties that have strong integrality properties.

Definition 2.1
.

An irreducible Fuchsian operator
\mathcal{L}_{n}
of order
n
is said to be a
Calabi-Yau operator
if the following conditions hold:
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is self-adjoint, and is normalized to have a regular singular point at
t=0
of maximally unipotent monodromy (MUM); the holomorphic solution of
\mathcal{L}_{n}(\omega(t))=0
is
N
-integral for some integer
N>1
, with integral
q
-coordinate and integral genus-zero instanton numbers
N_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
k\geq 1
.

Let us explain the some aspects of Definition
2.1
. To say that
t=0
is a point of maximally unipotent monodromy means that the differential equation
\mathcal{L}_{n}(\omega(t))=0
has a basis of solutions
\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{n-1}
at
t=0
of the form

(2.2)

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=h_{0}(t)\,,

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(h_{0}(t)\log(t)+h_{1}(t)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\omega_{n-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n-1}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}h_{k}(t)\log^{n-1-k}(t)\right)\,,

where
h_{0},\dots,h_{n-1}
are holomorphic functions satisfying
h_{0}(0)=1,h_{1}(0)=h_{2}(0)=\dots=h_{n-1}(0)=0
. Such a basis is canonical and is called a
Frobenius basis
. We will always have
h_{0}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[[t]]
and
h_{i}(t)\in t\mathbb{Q}[[t]]
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
.

The existence of a Frobenius basis is equivalent to all roots of the indicial equation (determining a power series solution of the differential equation) vanishing at
t=0
, and, additionally, the monodromy matrix
M_{t=0}\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
satisfying
(M_{t=0}-\mathbb{I}_{n})^{n}=0
and
(M_{t=0}-\mathbb{I}_{n})^{k}\neq 0
for
0<k<n
. Here,
\mathbb{I}_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. Hence,
M_{t=0}
is
maximally unipotent
. It is easy to recognize when
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is MUM at
t=0
using Equation (
2.1
): this is equivalent to leading polynomial
P_{0}(\theta)
having the form

P_{0}(\theta)=\theta^{n}\,.

The self-adjoint condition means that there is a nonzero function
h(t)
, algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}(t)
, such that
\mathcal{L}_{n}h(t)=(-1)^{n}h(t)\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}
where
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}
denotes the adjoint of
\mathcal{L}_{n}
. The condition of being self-adjoint implies, as a necessary condition, that the function
h(t)
satisfies the differential equation

(2.3)

h^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{2}{n}\frac{a_{n-1}(t)}{a_{n}(t)}h(t)\,,

see for example
[
24
, §3.3]
.

The meaning of the three strong integrality requirements characterizing the Calabi-Yau condition in Definition
2.1
is as follows
[
2
,
3
]
:

a) The holomorphic solution at
t=0

\omega_{0}(t)=h_{0}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}t^{n}

is
N
-integral, i.e., there is an integer
N>1
such that
\omega_{0}(Nt)\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]]
.

b) The
q
-coordinate is integral, that is,

(2.4)

q(t)\equiv\exp\left(2\pi i\frac{\omega_{1}(t)}{\omega_{0}(t)}\right)=t\exp\left(\frac{h_{1}(t)}{h_{0}(t)}\right)\in t\mathbb{Z}[[t]]\,.

The local inverse of the
q
-coordinate is called the
mirror map
, and will be of frequent use in this article. Notice crucially that the definition of the mirror map depends only on the operator having a MUM point, and does not depend on the operator being self-adjoint.

c) The instanton numbers
N_{k}
satisfy
N_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
. These numbers are intimately related to genus zero Gromov-Witten invariants and can be computed in several ways. A natural definition motivated by physics
[
52
]
is the following: consider the second logarithmic derivative of
\omega_{2}/\omega_{0}
expressed in terms of the (local inverse of the)
q
-coordinate. This quantity is known as the
Yukawa coupling
,

(2.5)

\mathscr{Y}\equiv\left(q\frac{d}{dq}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{0}}\right)\,.

The instanton numbers
N_{k}
are then computed by expanding the Yukawa coupling in a Lambert series about
q=0
,

(2.6)

\mathscr{Y}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{3}N_{k}\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\,.

When the order of the irreducible Fuchsian operator
\mathcal{L}_{n}
satisfies
n=4
, the instanton numbers can be computed from a generating function, called the
holomorphic prepotential

[
16
]
. In fact, the mirror symmetry conjecture states that the holomorphic prepotential
\mathfrak{F}(\tau)
is given in terms of a Frobenius basis of solutions
\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
for a fourth order Calabi-Yau operator
\mathcal{L}_{4}
by the following expression:

(2.7)

\mathfrak{F}=-\frac{1}{12}Y_{111}\left(-\frac{\omega_{3}}{\omega_{0}}+\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{\omega_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}Y_{011}\frac{\omega_{1}^{2}}{\omega_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}Y_{001}\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{0}}-\frac{1}{6}Y_{000}\,,

where
Y_{111},Y_{011},Y_{001},Y_{000}
are constants. One then obtains the Yukawa coupling

(2.8)

\mathscr{Y}=\frac{d^{3}\mathfrak{F}}{d\tau^{3}}\,,

and, in turn, this can be used to compute the instanton numbers
N_{k}
; see Morrison
[
52
, §2, §5]
.

It is straightforward to show that if

As the name suggests, the differential operators introduced above are intimately connected to Calabi-Yau varieties.

2.3.
Weierstrass models and the twist construction

The twist construction of Doran & Malmendier
[
24

provides a geometric mechanism that is useful for simultaneously constructing Calabi-Yau varieties iteratively that admit fibrations useful in string dualities, while also realizing monodromy tuples determined by Bogner & Reiter
[
14
]
that are associated to certain subfamilies of known Calabi-Yau operators
[
2
]
. More specifically, they show how Doran’s generalized functional invariant
[
21
]
(defined in §
2.3.2
) can be used in a “bottom-up” fashion to iteratively construct families of
m
-dimensional Calabi-Yau varieties
X^{(m)}
that are simultaneously elliptically fibered over a rational base and fibered by the family of
(m-1)
-dimensional Calabi-Yau varieties
X^{(m-1)}
over a punctured
\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
t=0
is a MUM point for each family. Additionally, their construction naturally shows that the Picard-Fuchs operator
\mathcal{L}_{n}
for
X^{(m)}
described in §
2.1
is given by the Hadamard product of the Picard-Fuchs operator
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
for
X^{(m-1)}
and a certain hypergeometric operator determined by the generalized functional invariant.

2.3.1.
Weierstrass models

Central to the twist construction are elliptic fibrations and associated Weierstrass models. If
X
,
S
are normal complex algebraic varieties of respective dimensions
m
and
m-1
, an elliptic fibration of
X
over
S
is a proper surjective morphism
\pi\colon X\to S
with connected, irreducible fibers of arithmetic genus one, i.e., each fibre is either a smooth elliptic curve, a rational curve with a node, or a rational curve with a cusp. We assume that
\pi
is smooth over a Zariski open subset
S_{0}\subset S
such that the complement
D_{\pi}=S-S_{0}
is a divisor with at worst normal crossings, and that the nodal or cuspidal fibers occur over
D_{\pi}
. Hence, the generic fibre of
\pi
is a smooth elliptic curve. Moreover, we assume the fibration is a
Jacobian
elliptic fibration, that is, we assume existence of a section
\sigma\colon S\to X
marking the neutral point of the elliptic curve group law on each smooth fibre. The fibration is assumed to be minimal in the following way in that there are no
(-1)
-curves in the fibers of
\pi
. A more general notion holds for higher dimensional elliptic fibrations that we will not need here.

Many geometric aspects of elliptic fibrations are determined by the fundamental line bundle
\mathsf{L}:=(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{-1}\in\mathrm{Pic}(S)
, which is related to the situation above to the canonical bundles
K_{X},K_{S}
of
X
and
S
by the adjunction formula as

(2.24)

K_{X}\cong\pi^{*}(K_{S}\otimes\mathsf{L})\,.

Thus, the canonical bundle
K_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
is trivial when
\mathsf{L}\cong K_{S}^{-1}
is the anticanonical bundle of
S
, giving us a way to determine necessary conditions for when the total space
X
of the fibration is a Calabi-Yau variety. Additionally, it is well known that the fundamental line bundle determines a Weierstrass model
\hat{\pi}\colon W\to S
for the fibration as follows.

Given an elliptic fibration
\pi\colon X\to S
with fundamental line bundle
\mathsf{L}
, let
g_{2},\,g_{3}
be sections of
\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 4},\,\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 6}
such that the discriminant section
\Delta=g_{2}^{3}-27g_{3}^{2}
of
\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 12}
does not vanish identically. The Weierstrass model
W
for the fibration is defined as the subvariety

(2.25)

y^{2}z=4x^{3}-g_{2}xz^{2}-g_{3}z^{3}

of the
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle
\mathbf{P}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{S}\oplus\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 2}\oplus\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 3})
over
S
in the following way. Let
p\colon\mathbf{P}\to S
be the natural projection, and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(1)
be the tautological line bundle. The variables
x,y,z
in Equation (
2.25
) are respectively sections of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(1)\otimes\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 2},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(1)\otimes\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 3}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(1)
that correspond to the natural injections of
\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 2},\,\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 3}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{S}
into
p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(1)=\mathcal{O}_{S}\oplus\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 2}\oplus\mathsf{L}^{\otimes 3}
. Then
\hat{\pi}\colon W\to S
is naturally an elliptic fibration over
S
in the sense outlined above, where
\hat{\pi}
is the natural projection, and the discriminant divisor
D_{\hat{\pi}}
is given by
\mathcal{O}_{S}(\Delta)
. The Weierstrass model naturally is a Jacobian elliptic fibration, with canonical section
\hat{\sigma}\colon S\to W
given by the point
[x\,:\,y\,:\,z]=[0\,:\,1\,:\,0]
in each fiber. Moreover, the image
\Sigma:=\hat{\sigma}(S)\subset W\subset\mathbf{P}
is a Cartier divisor, whose normal bundle is isomorphic to the fundamental line bundle as
p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(-\Sigma)\cong\mathsf{L}
.

However, the Weierstrass model
W
may be singular. Miranda
[
50
]
studied the resolution of the singularities of
W
in the case that
X
is a threefold, and shows there is a natural birational map
X\dashrightarrow W
compatible with the sections
\sigma,\hat{\sigma}
of the respective fibrations by blowing down all components of the fibers that do not meet
\sigma(S)
. If
\pi\colon X\to S
is minimal in the sense described above, then the inverse map
W\dashrightarrow X
is a resolution of the singularities of
W
. In this way, all relevant geometry of the Jacobian elliptic fibration is captured by its associated Weierstrass model.

The twist construction of Doran & Malmendier is concerned with 1-parameter
families
of Weierstrass models
\mathcal{W}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where each
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
corresponds to a Weierstrass model
\hat{\pi}\colon W_{t}\to S
for a 1-parameter family
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of
m
-dimensional elliptic Calabi-Yau varieties
\pi\colon X_{t}\to S
. Then taking local coordinates
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m-1})\in S
and
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the Weierstrass model in Equation (
2.25
) for
W_{t}
becomes

(2.26)

y^{2}=4x^{3}-g_{2}(u,t)x-g_{3}(u,t)\,,

where we have taken the affine chart
z=1
in
W_{t}
. In addition, a holomorphic
m
-form
\Omega_{t}\in H^{m,0}(X_{t})
can be represented on the Weierstrass model by the closed differential form

(2.27)

\Omega_{t}=du_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge du_{m-1}\wedge\frac{dx}{y}\,,

where we suppress the pullback by the birational map
X\dashrightarrow W
. As described in §
2.1
, this form can then be integrated over a suitable basis of
m
-cycles to obtain the period integrals
\omega_{i}(t)
that satisfy the Picard-Fuchs equation as a consequence of Griffiths transversality. See
[
24
, §5.3]
and
[
49
, §2.1]
for more details. As a result of this discussion, we will blur the lines between
X_{t}
and the Weierstrass model
W_{t}
and hence the families
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\mathcal{W}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
through the duration of this article.

2.3.2.
Twisted families of Weierstrass models

We first recall Doran’s
generalized functional invariant

[
22
]
. A generalized functional invariant is a triple
(i,j,\alpha)
with
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\left\{\frac{1}{2},1\right\}
such that
1\leq i,j\leq 6
. The generalized functional invariant encodes a 1-parameter family of degree
i+j
covering maps
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which is totally ramified over
0
, ramified to degrees
i
and
j
over
\infty
, and simply ramified over another point
\mathsf{t}
. For homogeneous coordinates
[v_{0}:v_{1}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the relevant family of maps parameterized by
\mathsf{t}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}-\{0,1,\infty\}
is given by

(2.28)

[v_{0},v_{1}]\mapsto[c_{ij}v_{1}^{i+j}\mathsf{t}:v_{0}^{i}(v_{0}+v_{1})^{j}],

for some constant
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. For a family
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of elliptic Calabi-Yau varieties with Weierstrass models given by Equation (
2.26
) with complex
m
-dimensional fibers and a generalized functional invariant
(i,j,\alpha)
such that

(2.29)

0\leq\mathrm{deg}_{t}(g_{2})\leq\mathrm{min}\left(\frac{4}{i},\frac{4\alpha}{j}\right),\hskip 14.22636pt0\leq\mathrm{deg}_{t}(g_{3})\leq\mathrm{min}\left(\frac{6}{i},\frac{6\alpha}{j}\right).

Doran and Malmendier showed that a new family
\tilde{\pi}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
can be constructed such that the general fiber
\tilde{X}_{\mathsf{t}}=\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(\mathsf{t})
is a compact, complex
(m+1)
-manifold equipped with a Jacobian elliptic fibration over
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times S
. In the coordinate chart
\{[v_{0}:v_{1}],(u_{1},\dots,u_{m-1})\}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\times S
the family of Weierstrass models
W_{\mathsf{t}}
, in the affine chart
\tilde{z}=1
, is given by

(2.30)

\begin{split}\tilde{y}^{2}=4\tilde{x}^{3}&-g_{2}\left(\frac{c_{ij}\mathsf{t}v_{1}^{i+j}}{v_{0}^{i}(v_{0}+v_{1})^{j}},u\right)v_{0}^{4}v_{1}^{4-4\alpha}(v_{0}+v_{1})^{4\alpha}\tilde{x}\\
&-g_{3}\left(\frac{c_{ij}\mathsf{t}v_{1}^{i+j}}{v_{0}^{i}(v_{0}+v_{1})^{j}},u\right)v_{0}^{6}v_{1}^{6-6\alpha}(v_{0}+v_{1})^{6\alpha}\end{split}

with
c_{ij}=(-1)^{i}i^{i}j^{j}/(i+j)^{i+j}
and

\tilde{x}=v_{0}^{2}v_{1}^{2-2\alpha}\left(v_{0}+v_{1}\right)^{2\alpha}x\,,\qquad\tilde{y}=v_{0}^{3}v_{1}^{3-3\alpha}\left(v_{0}+v_{1}\right)^{3\alpha}y.

The new family is called the
twisted family with generalized functional invariant
(i,j,\alpha)
of
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It follows that conditions (
2.29
) guarantee that the twisted family is minimal and normal if the original family is. Moreover, they showed that if the Calabi-Yau condition is satisfied for the fibers of the twisted family if it is satisfied for the fibers of the original.

Additionally, a holomorphic
(m+1)
-form
\tilde{\Omega}_{\mathsf{t}}\in H^{m+1,0}(\tilde{X}_{\mathsf{t}})
for the twisted family, in the affine chart
[v_{0}\,:\,v_{1}]=[v\,:\,1]
and
\tilde{z}=1
, is given by

(2.31)

\tilde{\Omega}_{\mathsf{t}}=\frac{dv}{v(v+1)^{\alpha}}\wedge\Omega_{\mathsf{t}}\,,

where
\Omega_{\mathsf{t}}
is the pullback of the holomorphic
m
-form in Equation (
2.27
), pulled back via the map defining the generalized functional invariant in Equation (
2.28
). It follows that if
\omega(t)
is a period integral for the family
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, a period integral
\tilde{\omega}(\mathsf{t})
for the twisted family is as follows:

(2.32)

\tilde{\omega}(\mathsf{t})=\oint_{C}\frac{dv}{v(v+1)^{\alpha}}\,\omega\left(\frac{c_{ij}\mathsf{t}}{v^{i}(v+1)^{j}}\right)\,,

where
C
is a suitably small non-contractible loop in the
v
-plane. Doran & Malmendier showed from this integral representation that the period integral for the twisted family with
\alpha=1
is the following Hadamard product (
[
24
, Proposition 5.1]
):

(2.33)

\tilde{\omega}(\mathsf{t})=(2\pi i)\,_{i+j-1}F_{i+j-2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{i+j}&\cdots&\frac{i+j-1}{i+j}\\
\frac{1}{i}\cdots&\frac{i-1}{i}\,\frac{1}{j}&\cdots\frac{j-1}{j}\end{array}\Bigg|\mathsf{t}\right)\star\omega(\mathsf{t})\,.

A similar formula holds for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, though we will not need it in this article. Here,
{}_{p}F_{q}
is the generalized univariate hypergeometric function, and the Hadamard product
(f\star g)(t)
of two power series
f(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}t^{n}
and
g(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}b_{n}t^{n}
is given by
(f\star g)(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}b_{n}t^{n}
.

We will be primarily interested in the twist construction for the generalized functional invariant
(i,j,\alpha)=(1,1,1)
. In this case, the relevant hypergeometric function that appears as the twist function in the Hadamard product is

(2.34)

_{1}F_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\Bigg|\,4t\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4t}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\,t^{n}\,,

where we have chosen the argument
4t
in order to produce an integral power series. This holomorphic function is annihilated by the differential operator

(2.35)

\mathcal{L}_{1}=(4t-1)\frac{d}{dt}+2=t(2+4\theta)-\theta\,.

These results shows immediately that the Picard-Fuchs operator
\mathcal{L}_{n+1}
of the twisted family
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
decomposes as the Hadamard product

(2.36)

\mathcal{L}_{n+1}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\star\mathcal{L}_{n}

of the hypergeometric operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and Picard-Fuchs operator
\mathcal{L}_{n}
for the family
\mathcal{X}\to~\mathbb{P}^{1}
. One also has that
\mathcal{L}_{n+1}
is MUM at
t=0
if
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is. A similar statement holds for any generalized functional invariant
(i,j,\alpha)
.

3.
Irrationality proofs and mirror symmetry

In 1978, Roger Apéry stunned the mathematics community by showing that
\zeta(3)
is irrational
[
4
]
. This was accomplished by producing two mysterious sequences of rational numbers
A_{n},B_{n}
that satisfy the same linear recurrence relation with different initial conditions, such that
B_{n}/A_{n}\to\zeta(3)
fast enough to apply Dirichlet’s irrationality criterion. In the same article, Apéry also provided a new proof of the irrationality of
\zeta(2)
along the exact same lines. Much effort was then expended in attempting to understand the mysterious nature of the sequences of rational numbers appearing in these proofs. Shortly thereafter, Beukers
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
and Beukers & Peters
[
11
]
found some surprising geometry behind these sequences fueling the irrationality proofs, which we review now.

3.1.
Irrationality of
\zeta(2)
and elliptic curves with rational 5-torsion

Apéry’s proof of the irrationality of
\zeta(2)
utilized the two sequences
a_{n},b_{n}
of rational numbers that simultaneously satisfy the linear recursion relation

(3.1)

n^{2}u_{n-1}-(11n^{2}+11n+3)u_{n}-(n+1)^{2}u_{n+1}=0\,

with initial conditions
(a_{0}=1,a_{1}=3)
, and
(b_{0}=0,b_{1}=5)
. The first few numbers of these sequences are determined to be

(3.2)

a_{n}=1,\;3,\;19,\;147,\;\dots,\qquad b_{n}=0,\;5,\;\frac{125}{4},\;\frac{8705}{36},\;\dots\;,

and these numbers have some remarkable properties. It was shown that
a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
n\geq 0
. In fact, there is the closed form

(3.3)

a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{n+k}{k}\,.

There is a similar, but more complicated form for the numbers
b_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and the denominators are bounded tightly as
d_{n}^{2}\,b_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
d_{n}=\mathrm{LCM}\,\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Using standard techniques, if one concatenates the above sequences into generating functions

(3.4)

\omega(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}\;,\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}t^{n}\;,

the recursion in Equation (
3.1
) becomes the second order linear differential operator

(3.5)

\mathcal{L}_{2}=\theta^{2}-t(11\theta^{2}+11\theta+3)-t^{2}(\theta+1)^{2}\;,

and one finds the differential relations

(3.6)

\mathcal{L}_{2}(\omega(t))=0\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{L}_{2}(\gamma(t))=5t\,.

Notice that by remarks in §
2.1
, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is MUM at
t=0
.
3
3

3

The extra factor of
t
in the right-hand side of the non-homogeneous equation not usually seen in the literature (e.g., Beukers
[
8
]
) is due to our representation of
\mathcal{L}_{2}
in terms of
\theta
, per our remarks preceding Definition
2.1
.
Surprisingly,
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is a Calabi-Yau operator; Beukers showed in
[
7
]
that
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is the Picard-Fuchs operator of a family of elliptic curves with arithmetic significance. In particular, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is modular, as in §
2.2
.

Let
X_{1}(5)
be the genus-zero modular curve
\Gamma_{1}(5)\backslash\mathbb{H}^{*}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
\pi\colon\mathcal{E}\to~X_{1}(5)
be the modular elliptic surface parameterizing elliptic curves with rational five-torsion. The generating function
\omega(t)
of the
a_{n}
sequence in Equation (
3.4
) is convergent near
t=0
, and it was shown directly that a)
t\in X_{1}(5)
is a Hauptmodul, and b)
\omega(t)
is the period integral of the generic fibre
E_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
,

\omega(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{dx}{y}\,,

where
\Sigma_{0}\in H_{1}(E_{t},\mathbb{Z})
is a suitable 1-cycle of the elliptic curve
E_{t}
. The elliptic curve is represented by a Weierstrass model

y^{2}=4x^{3}-g_{2}(t)x-g_{3}(t)

that is given explicitly in §
5.5
. Results of Beukers show
[
9
]
that the mirror map
t(\tau)
is Hauptmodul, and has a
q
-expansion

(3.7)

t(\tau)=q\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{n}\right)^{5\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)}\;.

Further, the period integral
\omega(t(\tau))
becomes a weight-one modular form for
\Gamma_{1}(5)
, expressed as

(3.8)

\omega(t(\tau))=1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left((3-i)\chi(n)+(3+i)\overline{\chi(n)}\right)\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\in M_{1}(\Gamma_{1}(5))\,.

Here
i^{2}=-1
, and
\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)
,
\chi(n)
are respectively the quadratic and quartic characters

\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)=(-1)^{j}\;,\hskip 14.22636pt\chi(n)=i^{j}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{if}\;\;n\equiv 2^{j}\mod 5\;.

3.2.
Irrationality of
\zeta(3)
and a family of K3 surfaces

Similarly, Apéry’s proof of the irrationality of
\zeta(3)
utilizes two sequences
A_{n},B_{n}
of rational numbers that simultaneously satisfy the linear recursion relation

(3.9)

n^{3}u_{n-1}-(2n+1)(17n^{2}+17n+5)u_{n}+(n+1)^{3}u_{n+1}=0

with initial conditions
(A_{0}=1,A_{1}=5)
and
(B_{0}=0,B_{1}=6)
. These sequences begin as

(3.10)

A_{n}=1,\;5,\;73,\;1445,\;\dots,\qquad B_{n}=0,\;6,\;\frac{351}{4},\;\frac{62531}{36},\;\dots\;.

As before, these numbers have remarkable properties. The numbers
A_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
are integral for
n\geq 0
. Again, there is a closed form expression, closely related toEquation (
3.3
), given as

(3.11)

A_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}\,.

There is a more complicated closed form for the rational numbers
B_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and again the denominators are bound by
d_{n}^{3}\,B_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
d_{n}=\mathrm{LCM}\,\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

We again concatenate these sequences into generating functions

(3.12)

\omega(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}t^{n}\;,\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}t^{n}\;,

and the linear recursion in Equation (
3.9
) becomes the linear differential operator

(3.13)

\mathcal{L}_{3}=\theta^{3}-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^{2}+17\theta+5)+t^{2}(\theta+1)^{3}\,,

leading to the differential relations

(3.14)

\mathcal{L}_{3}(\omega(t))=0\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{L}_{3}(\gamma(t))=6t\,.

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{3}
is MUM at
t=0
, and again surprisingly,
\mathcal{L}_{3}
a Calabi-Yau operator. Based on Beukers’ work in
[
7
]
, Beukers & Peters showed explicitly in
[
11
]
that
\mathcal{L}_{3}
is the Picard-Fuchs operator of a pencil
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of K3 surfaces of Picard rank-19, represented by the affine equation

(3.15)

X_{t}\colon\quad 1-(1-xy)z-txyz(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)=0\,

with
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Later, Beukers showed
[
9
]
that
\mathcal{L}_{3}
is also modular.

Specifically, they showed that for
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
sufficiently close to zero the generic fibre
X_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
admits a basis of locally constant transcendental 2-cycles
\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}\in H_{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})
and a holomorphic 2-form
\Omega_{t}\in H^{2,0}(X_{t})
such that the period integral

\omega(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Omega_{t}

realizes the generating function of the
A_{n}
sequence. More details will be provided in §
5.6
. Again results of Beukers in
[
9
]
show that the mirror map
t(\tau)
is a Hauptmodul, admits a representation as the following
\eta
-product

(3.16)

t(\tau)=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{12}\eta(6\tau)^{12}}{\eta(2\tau)^{12}\eta(3\tau)^{12}}\;,

where
\eta(\tau)
is the usual Dedekind eta function,

\eta(\tau)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})\;.

Moreover, the period integral
\omega(t(\tau))
is a weight-two modular form with respect to the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(6)^{+}\subset\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, realized explicitly as the
\eta
-product

(3.17)

\omega(t(\tau))=\frac{\eta(2\tau)^{7}\eta(3\tau)^{7}}{\eta(\tau)^{5}\eta(6\tau)^{5}}\,.

3.3.
Mirror symmetry of Fano varieties and quantum differential equations

A natural question at this point is that of the geometric content of the generating functions for the auxiliary sequences
b_{n},B_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
for
\zeta(2)
and
\zeta(3)
. Notice that these functions are solutions to
non-homogeneous
Picard-Fuchs equations that appear in Equations (
3.6
,
3.14
). The solutions to non-homogeneous Picard-Fuchs equations are important in the context of open string mirror symmetry, as studied for example by Morrison & Walcher
[
54
]
, Li, Lian, & Yau
[
47
]
, and Zhou
[
71
]
, building off earlier classical work of Griffiths
[
35
]
on normal functions. A number of authors have connected such considerations to a motivic perspective and higher Chow groups of algebraic cycles, such as Chen, Doran, Kerr, & Lewis
[
17
]
, Golyshev, Kerr, & Sasaki
[
27
]
, and Kerr
[
41
]
. Moreover, the modular differential operators we consider in this article are closely related via mirror symmetry
[
69
]
to the quantum differential equations of certain del Pezzo surfaces and Fano threefolds.

In the context of mirror symmetry of Picard rank-1 Fano threefolds, Golyshev
[
29
]
introduced a class D
n
of
n^{th}
-order Fuchsian ordinary linear differential operators
\mathcal{L}_{n}
. There, the differential operators are relevant to the (small) quantum cohomology of smooth Fano varieties. The operators were then studied in depth by Golyshev & Stienstra
[
28
]
. A lengthy, precise definition of such operators is not needed here. However, let us summarize some relevant aspects: each determinantal D
n
operator
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is defined in a combinatorial way via determinants from an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrix
A=[a_{ij}]
in such a way that
\mathcal{L}_{n}
always arises as the rightmost factor of a Fuchsian operator
\mathcal{D}_{n+1}
of order
(n+1)
, given by

(3.18)

\mathcal{D}_{n+1}=\theta\cdot\mathcal{L}_{n}\,\,.

See Golyshev
[
30

for the precise definition and relevant matrices
A=[a_{ij}]
.

Determinantal D3 operators
\mathcal{L}_{3}
were studied by Golyshev in the context of mirror symmetry of the precisely seventeen algebraic deformation classes of rank-1 Fano threefolds, i.e., algebraic threefolds with ample anticanonical class such that the Picard lattice has rank-1 that were classified by Iskovskikh
[
39
,
40
]
. We will not need a geometric description of these varieties and defer the interested reader to Iskovskikh’s work; see also Golyshev
[
30
, §6.3]
. For each deformation class
F
, the corresponding operator
\mathcal{L}_{3}
was shown to be modular, and to be the Picard-Fuchs operator of the pencil
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of K3 surfaces of Picard rank-19 that are Dolgachev-Nikulin-Pinkham mirror
[
19
]
to the anticanonical linear system on
F
. In fact, each family is determined by a congruence subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, such that the base
\mathbb{P}^{1}\cong\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
is the associated genus-zero modular curve, and the point
t=0\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is always MUM. In other words,
\mathcal{L}_{3}
is a third order Calabi-Yau operator, and at
t=0
the holomorphic period
\omega_{0}(t)
is integral, i.e.,

\omega_{0}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]]\,\,.

Let us provide some more details: a deformation class
F=F_{N,d}
of a rank-1 Fano threefold is determined by two numerical invariants, the index
d
and the level
N
. Each class in
H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Z})
is algebraic, so we have
H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. The index is given by

, where
c_{1}
is the anticanonical class, and the level is
N=\frac{1}{2d^{2}}\left\langle c_{1}^{3},[F]\right\rangle
. Both are easily shown to be positive integers. The seventeen possible combinations of invariants
(N,d)
determined by Iskovskikh are as follows:

\displaystyle(1,1),\;\;(2,1),\;\;(3,1),\;\;(4,1),\;\;

\displaystyle(5,1),\;\;(6,1),\;\;(8,1),\;\;(9,1),\;\;(11,1),

(3.19)

\displaystyle(1,2),\;\;(2,2),\;\;(3,2),\;\;

\displaystyle(4,2),\;\;(5,2),\;\;(3,3),\;\;(2,4)\,.

For each deformation class, the generic anticanonical divisor is mirror to a smooth K3 surface of Picard rank-19 whose Picard-Fuchs operator
\mathcal{L}_{3,N}
is modular, as shown by Golyshev
[
30
]
. Each
\mathcal{L}_{3,N}
is normalized to have a MUM point at

. For example, the pair
(6,1)
corresponds to the family of K3 surfaces, introduced by Beukers & Peters

that is associated with the irrationality proof of

and was discussed in §

\mathcal{L}_{3,6}

4.
Modularity of virtual Yukawa couplings

We have encountered a somewhat delightful tension – the Yukawa coupling associated with elliptic curves and K3 surfaces is trivial, but we can compute an analogue of a Yukawa coupling from their respective quantum differential operators from Definition
3.1
using a certain holomorphic prepotential inspired by Equation (
2.7
). These turn out to have interesting properties such as modularity that makes them worth studying in detail.

4.1.
Almost Calabi-Yau Operators & Virtual Yukawa Couplings

The fact that quantum differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{n+1}=\theta\cdot\mathcal{L}_{n}
of Calabi-Yau operators are MUM and possess the strong integrality properties described in §

, leads us to make the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

An

-order Fuchsian operator
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is an

Our Definition

2.1

[
3

3.3

We require the same integrality conditions as discussed in §

2.5

For example, when

refer to the coefficients in the Lambert expansion

\imath(\mathrm{L})

A conjecture and future directions

In this article we have have given a complete answer to the questions put forth by Yang
[
64
]
through the lens of modular parameterizations of Picard-Fuchs ODEs. Yang was initially motivated by holomorphic prepotentials of fourth order operators and classical mirror symmetry for Calabi-Yau threefolds. We conclude that Yang’s results say more perhaps about the mirror symmetry of
Fano
threefolds of Picard rank-1 via the results of Golyshev & Zagier
[
31
]
, and appear to be in perfect agreement with Iritani’s work on the Gamma conjecture
[
38
]
. Through the twist construction of Doran & Malmendier
[
24
]
, almost all of these operators are related to rational modular elliptic surfaces, where similar results about third almost Calabi-Yau quantum differential operators and modularity of virtual Yukawa couplings hold.

Stienstra observed
[
58
]
that a natural functional inverse to these virtual Yukawa couplings encoded actual instanton counts in certain string compactifications on the canonical bundle of relevant del Pezzo surfaces as computed by physcists
[
42
,
46
]
. Inspired by this, we defined dual instanton numbers for the virtual Yukawa couplings of K3 surfaces of Picard rank-19. We have checked that similar experimental results on the integrality of dual instanton numbers (as in Equation (
5.7
)) hold for all operators in Table
1
and their virtual Yukawa couplings in Table
3
. In fact, it is clear from Proposition
3.2
and Golyshev
[
30
, §5.8]
that such a virtual Yukawa coupling can be defined for each deformation class of type
(N,d)
of rank-1 Fano threefold in Equation (
3.3
). However, we may generically lose modularity and periodicity of virtual instanton numbers whenever
N=1
.

Based off the discussion in §
5.2
preceding Definition
5.1
, we make the following conjecture about the dual instanton numbers for virtual Yukawa couplings associated with type
(N,d)
anti-canonical K3 surfaces of Picard rank-19.

Conjecture.
a) For each deformation class
F_{N,d}
of rank-1 Fano threefold of type
(N,d)
in Equation (
3.3
), there are rational numbers
\beta,\mu\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
and a positive integer
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
such that the normalized dual instanton numbers
\mathsf{N}_{k}:=\mu\widetilde{N}_{k}
from Equation (
5.7
) of the virtual Yukawa coupling expressed in
\mathsf{Q}=Q^{\nu}
with choice of B-field determined by
\beta
are integeral, relative genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants of the log Calabi-Yau pair
(F_{N,d},D)
formed from the associated rank-1 Fano threefold
F_{N,d}
and smooth anticanonical divisor
D\in|-K_{F_{N,d}}|
. Moreover, b) the
\mathsf{Q}
-expansions in Equation (
5.7
) are expressible in terms of quasi-modular forms for some discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
commensurable with
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

The evidence for such a conjecture is as follows: arguments similar to those in
[
42
,
46
]
justifying Stienstra’s computations for the instanton numbers of local Calabi-Yau threefolds show that our quantum differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{4,N}
are naturally related to geometry of the local Calabi-Yau fourfold
K_{F_{N,d}}
, the total space of the canonical bundle of the respective rank-1 Fano threefold
F_{N,d}
. The local-relative correspondence of van Garrel, Graber, & Ruddat
[
61
]
, together with the work on relative invariants by Fan, Tseng, & You
[
25
]
, and You
[
66
]
would then imply that our computations of appropriately normalized dual instantons acutally compute relative invariants, up to a sign. It remains to show that the normalized dual instantons are in fact local invariants of
K_{F_{N,d}}
; in this sense, Iritani’s work on the mirror symmetric Gamma conjecture seems relevant
[
38
]
. Quasi-modularity of instanton expansions in string theory, especially within the framework of local mirror symmetry, has been observed for local Calabi-Yau threefolds by Alim, Scheidegger, Yau, & Zhou
[
1
]
, and also for Calabi-Yau fourfolds in
[
36
]
by Haghighat, Movasati, & Yau.

In fact, part a) of the conjecture appears to be true for the case
(N,d)=(2,4)
. In this case, the Fano threefold is
\mathbb{P}^{3}
(whose deformation space is trivial), and the relevant Picard-Fuchs operator is the pullback of
\mathcal{L}_{3,2}
in Table
1
under the map
t\mapsto t^{4}
; see Golyshev
[
30
, §5.8]
. In this case, it is natural to take
\nu=4
, and after computing the relevant expansions from Equations (
5.6
,
5.7
), one determines that by choosing
\beta=1
and
\mu=-\frac{1}{4}
, the following normalized dual instanton numbers
\mathsf{N}_{k}=-\frac{1}{4}\widetilde{N}_{k}
are obtained:

Then for each
k=1,\dots,12
, the integers
\mathsf{N}_{k}
agree precisely with the genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants for the local Calabi-Yau fourfold
K_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, the total space of the canonical bundle of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. These were computed for example by Klemm & Pandharipande
[
43
, Table 1]
using the Aspinwall-Morrison formula
[
5
]
, and the bound of
k=12
comes from loc. cit., the most extensive list we could find in the literature relevant to this case. We expect that agreement holds for
all
normalized dual instanton numbers with this choice of B-field
\beta=1
. It follows from
[
61
]
then that the normalized dual instanton numbers in (
6.1
) agree with the relative invariants of
(\mathbb{P}^{3},D)
up to a sign, where
D\in|-K_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}|
is a generic anticanonical quartic hypersurface. This is completely analogous to the case of the conifold singularity on the local Calabi-Yau threefold
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
, see
[
42
,
46
]
and
[
58
,
\#4
]
.

We remark that independent of our conjecture, the dual instanton numbers of both the modular elliptic pencils and their twisted
\mathrm{M}_{N}
-polarized K3 surface counterparts computed here satisfy highly nontrivial divisibility conditions. Since we have shown the virtual instantons of all modular families in this article are periodic (Corollaries
4.5
,
5.2
), this implies that intricate nonlinear combinations of dual instantons form periodic sequences, with period determined by the level of the congruence subgroup. For the modular elliptic families
\#1-4
in
[
58
]
, and those listed above for
K_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, the dual instantons are genus zero Gromov-Witten invariants. To illustrate the striking nature of this implication, we list general formulae first few periodic virtual instantons of K3 pencils in terms of dual instantons, here listed with B-field
\beta=-1
:

It would be very interesting to further investigate the ramifications and interpretations of the virtual instanton numbers.

Moreover, we note that the modular results in §
5
represent a modular counterpart to the special function identities for holomorphic periods, induced by the composition into Hadamard products in Equation (
2.33
). This suggests that there may be a way to understand the twist construction between modular families of elliptic curves and K3 surfaces purely in terms of modular forms. On the other hand, each respectively represents a mirror variety to a smooth anticanonical divisor on either a del Pezzo surface or Fano threefold, and the twist construction may offer yet another interpretation in terms of Fano varieties. This, along with modularity of all Landau-Ginzburg models associated to Fano threefolds, is also the theme of a recent deep investigation
[
20
]
by Doran, Harder, Katzarkov, Ovcharenko, & Przyjalkowski.

Finally, it is interesting to note that Zhou
[
71
, §1.1, 3.1]
studied the period integrals of the Hesse pencil – not its mirror, as in §
5.3
– directly from the GKZ perspective and recovered essentially the operator
\mathcal{D}_{3,3}
and weight-three modular form
\mathscr{Y}_{\mathcal{D}_{3,3}}
, but from a completely different point of view. Similarly, several quantities that we have studied in this article also appear in the context of open string mirror symmetry, by studying chain integrals rather than period integrals in a manner similar to Li, Lian & Yau in
[
47
]
and Morrison & Walcher
[
54
]
. Finding a connection between these perspectives may offer more applications for quantum differential operators and their dual instanton numbers.

A Complete Set of Quadratic Constraints for Repeated ReLU and Generalizations
Sahel Vahedi Noori, Bin Hu, Geir Dullerud, Peter Seiler

Abstract
This paper derives a complete set of quadratic constraints (QCs) for the repeated ReLU. The complete set of QCs is described by a collection of matrix copositivity conditions. We also show that only two functions satisfy all QCs in our complete set: the repeated ReLU and flipped ReLU. Thus our complete set of QCs bounds the repeated ReLU as tight as possible up to the sign invariance inherent in quadratic forms. We derive a similar complete set of incremental QCs for repeated ReLU, which can potentially lead to less conservative Lipschitz bounds for ReLU networks than the standard LipSDP approach. The basic constructions are also used to derive the complete sets of QCs for other piecewise linear activation functions such as leaky ReLU, MaxMin, and HouseHolder. Finally, we illustrate the use of the complete set of QCs to assess stability and performance for recurrent neural networks with ReLU activation functions. We rely on a standard copositivity relaxation to formulate the stability/performance condition as a semidefinite program. Simple examples are provided to illustrate that the complete sets of QCs and incremental QCs can yield less conservative bounds than existing sets.

This paper mainly focuses on QCs satisfied by the ReLU. The notion of a QC is formally defined next.

Definition 1

A function
F:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
satisfies the
Quadratic Constraint (QC)
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v\\
F(v)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}v\\
F(v)\end{bmatrix}\geq 0.

(3)

We denote by

the set of all functions which satisfy the QC defined by
M\in\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}
. Furthermore, given a subset of matrices
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}
we define

; namely,
F\in QC(\mathcal{M})
means

satisfies every QC defined by the elements of
\mathcal{M}
.

QCs are, in general, conservative bounds on the graph of a function in the sense that both
QC(M)
and
QC(\mathcal{M})
will typically contain more than one function. However, these bounds are useful as they can be easily incorporated into stability and performance conditions for dynamical systems. Section
V
will provide one such application of QCs for stability and performance analysis.

QCs for ReLU can be derived using simple properties of the scalar ReLU. Specifically, the
scalar ReLU satisfies the following useful properties that have been used previously in the literature
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
:

1.

Positivity:
The scalar ReLU is non-negative for all inputs:
\phi(v)\geq 0

\forall v\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

Positive Complement:
The scalar ReLU satisfies
\phi(v)\geq v

\forall v\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.

Complementarity:
The graph of the scalar ReLU is identically on the line of slope 0 (when
v\leq 0
) or the line of slope 1 (when
v\geq 0
). Thus,
\phi(v)\,(v-\phi(v))=0

\forall v\in\mathbb{R}
.

4.

Positive Homogeneity:
The scalar ReLU is homogeneous for all non-negative constants:
\phi(\beta v)=\beta\phi(v)

\forall v\in\mathbb{R}
and
\forall\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

We can express equivalent properties for ReLU, e.g. the positivity and positive complement properties are
\Phi(v)\geq 0
and
\Phi(v)\geq v
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
, respectively.
The next lemma gives two QCs for the ReLU based on these properties. Both QCs are known in the literature and the second one, defined by

Lemma 1

Define the following subsets of
\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}0&Q_{1}\\
Q_{1}&-2Q_{1}\end{bmatrix}\,:\,Q_{1}\in D^{n_{v}}\right\},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}-I&I\\

(5)

satisfies the QC defined by any

\Phi(v)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M_{2}\begin{bmatrix}v\\

note that quadratic forms are even functions:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-v\\
-w\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}-v\\
-w\end{bmatrix}.

(17)

With this observation we can conclude the following.

Proposition 1

Given a subset
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}
. Then
\Phi\in QC(\mathcal{M})
if and only if
{\Phi}^{\mbox{\footnotesize flip}}\in QC(\mathcal{M})
.

In words, this says there is a fundamental limit on how finely QCs can specify the ReLU: the flipped ReLU must always also be included. The next theorem
states that ReLU and flipped ReLU are, in fact, the only functions that satisfy all QCs defined by
\mathcal{M}_{c}
. Namely, the set of QCs defined by
\mathcal{M}_{c}
is as tight as possible for ReLU up to the sign-invariance inherent in quadratic forms.

Theorem 2

A function
F:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}

satisfies all QCs defined by
\mathcal{M}_{c}
if and only if
F
is either
\Phi
or
{\Phi}^{\mbox{\footnotesize flip}}
.
Namely: the set
QC(\mathcal{M}_{c})=\{\Phi,\,{\Phi}^{\mbox{\footnotesize flip}}\}
.

Proof:

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume
F\in\{\Phi,\,{\Phi}^{\mbox{\footnotesize flip}}\}
. If
F=\Phi
then Theorem
1
implies that
F
satisfies all QCs defined by
\mathcal{M}_{c}
. The same is true
when
F={\Phi}^{\mbox{\footnotesize flip}}
because quadratic forms are even functions.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
F\in QC(\mathcal{M}_{c})
. By Lemma
3
,
\mathcal{M}_{c}
contains
any
M_{1}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&Q_{1}\\
Q_{1}&-2Q_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
with
Q_{1}
diagonal.
Select
Q_{1}
to have all zero entries except for entry
(i,i)
.
Then all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
,
w=F(v)
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M_{1}\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}=2(Q_{1})_{ii}w_{i}(v_{i}-w_{i})\geq 0.

(18)

(Q_{1})_{ii}
can be chosen to be positive or negative. Thus
(
18
) implies that
w_{i}=0
or
w_{i}=v_{i}
. In other words, the allowable values of
F_{i}(v)
only depend on
v_{i}
and thus we write
F_{i}(v_{i})\in\{0,v_{i}\}
for all
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma
3
also implies that
\mathcal{M}_{c}
contains any
M_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{2}
.
Define
M_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{2}
by

\displaystyle M_{2}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-I&I\\
0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{\top}Q_{2}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-I&I\\
0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mbox{ with }Q_{2}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&e_{i}e_{j}^{\top}+e_{j}e_{i}^{\top}\end{smallmatrix}\right],\,i\neq j.

(19)

Then all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
,
w=F(v)
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M_{2}\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}=2w_{i}w_{j}\geq 0.

(20)

This condition is violated if
F_{i}(v_{i})
and
F_{j}(v_{j})
have opposite sign for some
v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.
This implies: (i)
F_{i}
is globally nonnegative for all
i=1,\ldots n_{v}
, or (ii)

F_{i}
is globally non-positive for all
i=1,\ldots n_{v}
. The remainder of the proof shows that case (i) implies
F=\Phi
while case (ii) implies
F={\Phi}^{\mbox{\footnotesize flip}}
.

First consider case (i), and note that in order to show
F=\Phi
it is sufficient to demonstrate
F_{i}(v_{i})\geq v_{i}
for all
i
and
v
. Define
M_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{2}
with

Q_{2}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}e_{i}e_{j}^{\top}+e_{j}e_{i}^{\top}&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
for some
i\neq j
. Then all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
,
w=F(v)
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M_{2}\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}=2(w_{i}-v_{i})(w_{j}-v_{j})\geq 0.

(21)

Now set
v_{j}=-1
and then by the non-negativity of
F_{j}
we have
w_{j}-v_{j}>0
. Then (
21
) implies

w_{i}\geq v_{i}
, namely
F_{i}(v_{i})\geq v_{i}
as required.

Case (ii) leads to
F={\Phi}^{\mbox{\footnotesize flip}}
following a similar argument.

∎

III-C

Complete Set of Incremental QCs for ReLU

Next, we build on our results
in Section
III-A

to define the largest class of incremental QCs for ReLU.
Let matrices
M\in\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}
and
D_{1}
,

D_{2}\in D_{\pm 1}^{n_{v}}
be given. Let
M_{D_{1},D_{2}}\in\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}
denote the following matrix:

\displaystyle M_{D_{1}D_{2}}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}D_{1}&-D_{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}(I+D_{1})&-\frac{1}{2}(I+D_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{\top}M\left[\begin{smallmatrix}D_{1}&-D_{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}(I+D_{1})&-\frac{1}{2}(I+D_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\right].

(22)

Given this notation, define the following set of matrices:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}

\displaystyle:=\left\{M\in\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}\,:\,M_{D_{1}D_{2}}\in COP^{2n_{v}}\,\,\forall D_{1},\,D_{2}\in D_{\pm 1}^{n_{v}}\right\}.

(23)

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
involves a collection
2^{2n_{v}}=4^{n_{v}}
copositivity
conditions; one condition for each pair
D_{1}
,
D_{2}\in D^{n_{v}}_{\pm 1}
. The next theorem states that
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
defines the complete class of incremental QCs for ReLU.

Theorem 3

The ReLU
\Phi
satisfies an incremental QC defined by
M\in\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}
if and only if
M\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
. Equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}=\left\{M\in\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}|\ \Phi\in QC^{inc}(M)\right\}.

(24)

Proof:

The definition of an incremental QC involves
a quadratic form (
8
) that can be equivalently written as:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bar{v}-\hat{v}\\
\bar{w}-\hat{w}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}\bar{v}-\hat{v}\\
\bar{w}-\hat{w}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{v}\\
\hat{v}\\
\bar{w}\\
\hat{w}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}R^{\top}MR\begin{bmatrix}\bar{v}\\
\hat{v}\\
\bar{w}\\
\hat{w}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\mbox{ where }R:=\begin{bmatrix}I&-I&0&0\\
0&0&I&-I\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n_{v}\times 4n_{v}}.

Thus
M
defines an incremental constraint for a ReLU of dimension
n_{v}
if and only if

R^{\top}MR
defines a normal (non-incremental) QC
for a ReLU of dimension
2n_{v}
. By Theorem
1
, this is equivalent to the following condition:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D\\
\frac{1}{2}(I+D)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}(R^{\top}MR)\begin{bmatrix}D\\
\frac{1}{2}(I+D)\end{bmatrix}\in COP^{2n_{v}}\,\,\forall D\in D^{2n_{v}}_{\pm 1}.

(25)

Block partition
D=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}D_{1}&0\\
0&D_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.
Then (
25
) simplifies, by direct multiplication, to
M_{D_{1}D_{2}}\in COP^{2n_{v}}

for all
D_{1}
,
D_{2}\in D^{n_{v}}_{\pm 1}
.
In summary,
M
defines an incremental QC for
ReLU if and only if
M\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
.
∎

III-D

New Incremental QCs for ReLU

Section
II-C
introduced a well-known set of ReLU incremental QCs, denoted
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
, based on
[0,1]
slope constraint for scalar ReLU. We initially conjectured that
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
might contain all possible incremental QCs for ReLU, i.e
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}=\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
. However, we found examples where the use of
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
yields more conservative bounds than
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
(See Section
VI-B
). Such examples imply that
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
is a strict subset of
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
.

The purpose of this subsection is to derive a new set of ReLU incremental QCs that we found while studying these examples. This new set is interesting on its own. Moreover, this illustrates that
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
can be explored to help derive new sets of QCs that may have computational advantages. Our new set of incremental QCs is based on the following property for scalar ReLU.

Proposition 2

Any
\bar{v}_{i}
,
\hat{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}

(i=1,2)
with
\bar{w}_{i}=\phi(\bar{v}_{i})
and
\hat{w}_{i}=\phi(\hat{v}_{i})
satisfy:

\displaystyle\left[(\bar{v}_{1}-\hat{v}_{1})-(\bar{v}_{2}-\hat{v}_{2})\right]^{2}+2(\bar{w}_{1}-\hat{w}_{1})\cdot(\bar{w}_{2}-\hat{w}_{2})\geq 0.

(26)

Proof:

To simplify notation, let

dv_{i}:=\bar{v}_{i}-\hat{v}_{i}
and

dw_{i}:=\bar{w}_{i}-\hat{w}_{i}
. With this notation, we need to show:

\displaystyle\left[dv_{1}-dv_{2}\right]^{2}+2dw_{1}\cdot dw_{2}\geq 0.

(27)

The
[0,1]
slope constraint implies that if
dv_{i}\geq 0
then
dw_{i}\in[0,dv_{i}]
. Similarly, if
dv_{i}\leq 0
then
dw_{i}\in[dv_{i},0]
.

If
dv_{1}dv_{2}\geq 0
then
dw_{1}dw_{2}\geq 0
and thus
(
27
) holds. On the other hand, if
dv_{1}dv_{2}\leq 0
then
dw_{1}dw_{2}\geq dv_{1}dv_{2}
. This case implies
(dv_{1}-dv_{2})^{2}+2dw_{1}dw_{2}\geq dv_{1}^{2}+dv_{2}^{2}\geq 0
.
∎

Having established this new property, we now state the following lemma about doubly hyperdominant matrices.

Lemma 4

Let
T_{2}=T_{2}^{\top}\in DH^{n_{v}}
be given
with
\sum_{k=1}^{n_{v}}T_{ik}=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n_{v}
. Then there exists

\{\lambda_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle T_{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\lambda_{i,j}(e_{i}-e_{j})(e_{i}-e_{j})^{\top}.

(28)

Proof:

The rows
T_{2}
of sum to zero and hence the columns also sum to zero by symmetry. Hence,
T_{2}
is a symmetric, doubly hyperdominant matrix with zero excess. Theorem 3.7 in
[
33
]
gives a decomposition similar to (
28
)
for nonsymmetric, doubly hyperdominant matrices with zero excess. The argument in
[
33
]
can be adapted as follows for the symmetric case. The proof is given Appendix
-B
.
∎

Next we state a new set of incremental QCs for ReLU using the decomposition
in Lemma
4
and the property for scalar ReLU in Proposition
2
.

Theorem 4

The ReLU
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n_{v}}

satisfies the incremental QC defined by any matrix in the
following set:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}T_{2}&0\\
0&S_{2}-T_{2}\end{bmatrix}\,:\,T_{2}=T_{2}^{\top}\in DH^{n_{v}},\,\right.

(29)

\displaystyle\left.S_{2}\in D^{n_{v}},\ (S_{2})_{ii}=(T_{2})_{ii},\,\sum_{k=1}^{n_{v}}T_{ik}=0\mbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,n_{v}\right\}.

Proof:

Take any
M_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{2}
. The upper left block
T_{2}
is a symmetric, doubly hyperdominant matrix with zero excess. By Lemma
4
, there exists

\{\lambda_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle T_{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\lambda_{i,j}(e_{i}-e_{j})(e_{i}-e_{j})^{\top}.

(30)

This further implies that
S_{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\lambda_{ij}(e_{i}e_{i}^{\top}+e_{j}e_{j}^{\top})
and hence
S_{2}-T_{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\lambda_{ij}(e_{i}e_{j}^{\top}+e_{j}e_{i}^{\top})
. Therefore

M_{2}
can be decomposed as:

\displaystyle M_{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\lambda_{ij}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}(e_{i}-e_{j})(e_{i}-e_{j})^{\top}&0\\
0&e_{i}e_{j}^{\top}+e_{j}e_{i}^{\top}\end{smallmatrix}\right].

(31)

Then for any

\bar{v},\,\hat{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
and

\bar{w}=\Phi(\bar{v})
,
\hat{w}=\Phi(\hat{v})
,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bar{v}-\hat{v}\\
\bar{w}-\hat{w}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M_{2}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{v}-\hat{v}\\
\bar{w}-\hat{w}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{v}}\lambda_{ij}\left[\left((\bar{v}_{i}-\hat{v}_{i})-(\bar{v}_{j}-\hat{v}_{j})\right)^{2}+2(\bar{w}_{i}-\hat{w}_{i})\cdot(\bar{w}_{j}-\hat{w}_{j})\right].

Each term of this sum is nonnegative by property (
26
)
of the scalar ReLU and
\lambda_{ij}\geq 0
.
Thus
\Phi
satisfies all incremental QCs defined by
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{2}
.
∎

ReLU satisfies the incremental QCs defined by
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{2}
. Numerical implementations may favor these subsets over the complete set
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
for computational reasons.

IV
Related Extensions

The key results in the previous sections can be generalized in various ways. This section presents two specific extensions leading to the complete set of QCs for other nonlinear functions appearing in the literature.

IV-A

Affine Transformations

First we consider the effect of affine transformations on QCs. The next result is stated for general functions and is not specific to ReLU.

Lemma 5

Let a function
F:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{w}}
and matrices
A_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{w}\times n_{v}}
,
A_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{w}\times n_{w}}
, and
A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}\times n_{w}}
be given. Define a new function
G:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{w}}
by:

\displaystyle G(\hat{v})=A_{0}\hat{v}+A_{1}F(A_{2}\hat{v}).

(32)

(a)

Given
M\in\mathbb{S}^{n_{v}+n_{w}}
. If
A_{1}
is nonsingular and
F
satisfies the QC defined by
M
then
G
satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}:=R_{A}^{\top}MR_{A}\in\mathbb{S}^{n_{v}+n_{w}}
with

\displaystyle R_{A}:=\begin{bmatrix}A_{2}&0\\
-A_{1}^{-1}A_{0}&A_{1}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(33)

(b)

Given
\hat{M}\in\mathbb{S}^{n_{v}+n_{w}}
. If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are nonsingular then
G
satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}
if and only if
F
satisfies the QC defined by
M:=R_{A}^{-\top}\hat{M}R_{A}^{-1}
.

Proof:

(a)
F
satisfies the QC defined by
M
and hence,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}A_{2}\hat{v}\\
F(A_{2}\hat{v})\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}A_{2}\hat{v}\\
F(A_{2}\hat{v})\end{bmatrix}\geq 0\,\,\,\,\forall\hat{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}.

(34)

Next, rearranging (
32
) we get

\displaystyle F(A_{2}\hat{v})=A_{1}^{-1}\left(G(\hat{v})-A_{0}\hat{v}\right).

(35)

Substitute this expression into (
34
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\hat{v}\\
G(\hat{v})\end{bmatrix}^{\top}R_{A}^{\top}MR_{A}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{v}\\
G(\hat{v})\end{bmatrix}\geq 0\,\,\,\,\forall\hat{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}.

(36)

Hence
G
satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}
as required.

(b) First, note that if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are nonsingular then
R_{A}
is nonsingular with the following inverse:

\displaystyle R_{A}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{2}^{-1}&0\\
A_{0}A_{2}^{-1}&A_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

(37)

It follows from (a) that if
F
satisfies the QC defined by
M=R_{A}^{-\top}\hat{M}R_{A}^{-1}
then
G
satisfies the QC defined by
R_{A}^{\top}MR_{A}=\hat{M}
. Thus it remains to show the “only if” direction. Assume
G
satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}
. Since
A_{2}
is nonsingular, this implies:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}A_{2}^{-1}v\\
G(A_{2}^{-1}v)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\hat{M}\begin{bmatrix}A_{2}^{-1}v\\
G(A_{2}^{-1}v)\end{bmatrix}\geq 0\,\,\,\,\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}.

(38)

Substitute for
G(A_{2}^{-1}v)
using the definition of
G
in (
32
). Then
the QC (
38
) simplifies to:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v\\
F(v)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}R_{A}^{-\top}\hat{M}R_{A}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}v\\
F(v)\end{bmatrix}\geq 0\,\,\,\,\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}.

(39)

Hence
F
satisfies the QC defined by
M=R_{A}^{-\top}\hat{M}R_{A}^{-1}
.
∎

We can use affine transformations to give the complete set of QCs for another class of functions. Define
g_{\alpha\beta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
for
\alpha\neq\beta
as follows:

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha v&\mbox{if }v<0\\
\beta v&\mbox{if }v\geq 0\end{array}\right..

g_{\alpha\beta}
corresponds to the scalar ReLU
\phi
when
\alpha=0
and
\beta=1
. It corresponds to leaky ReLU
[
35
]
when
0<\alpha<1

and
\beta=1
. In general,
g_{\alpha\beta}
is a piecewise linear function with a slope change at
v=0
.
The corresponding repeated function

G_{\alpha\beta}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
is defined elementwise by

G_{\alpha\beta}(v)=(g_{\alpha\beta}(v_{1}),\ldots,\,g_{\alpha\beta}(v_{n_{v}}))
.

Corollary
2
below states that the complete
set of QCs for
G_{\alpha\beta}
is defined
by the following set of matrices:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\hat{M}\in\mathbb{S}^{2n_{v}}\,:\,\right.

(42)

\displaystyle\left.\left[\begin{smallmatrix}D\\
\alpha D+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}(I+D)\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{\top}\hat{M}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}D\\
\alpha D+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}(I+D)\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in COP^{n_{v}}\,\,\forall D\in D_{\pm 1}^{n_{v}}\right\}.

Corollary 2

The function
G_{\alpha\beta}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
with
\alpha\neq\beta

satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}
if and only if
\hat{M}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
.

Proof:

G_{\alpha\beta}
can be written in terms of the ReLU as follows:

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}(v)=\alpha v+(\beta-\alpha)\Phi(v).

(43)

This corresponds to an affine transformation
as in (
32
) with
A_{0}=\alpha I
,
A_{1}=(\beta-\alpha)I
, and
A_{2}=I
. Both
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are nonsingular and thus we have:

\displaystyle R_{A}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
-\frac{\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}I&\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}I\end{bmatrix}\mbox{ and }R_{A}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
\alpha I&(\beta-\alpha)I\end{bmatrix}.

(44)

It follows from Lemma
5
(b) that
G
satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}
if and only if
ReLU
\Phi
satisfies the QC defined by
M=R_{A}^{-\top}\hat{M}R_{A}^{-1}
.
Moreover, Theorem
1
states that
\Phi
satisfies a QC defined by
M
if and only if
M\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
. Combining these equivalences,
G
satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}
if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D\\
\frac{1}{2}(I+D)\end{bmatrix}^{\top}R_{A}^{-\top}\hat{M}R_{A}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}D\\
\frac{1}{2}(I+D)\end{bmatrix}\in COP^{n_{v}}\,\,\forall D\in D_{\pm 1}^{n_{v}}.

(45)

This simplifies to
\hat{M}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
.
∎

IV-B

Application to Householder and Max/Min Activations

Several gradient norm preserving activation functions have appeared in the literature
for the design of Lipschitz neural networks

[
36
,
37
]
. One example is the Householder activation function
[
38
]
.
Given a vector
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
with
\|h\|_{2}=1
, the Householder activation
G_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
is defined by:

\displaystyle G_{h}(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v&\mbox{if }h^{\top}v\geq 0\\
(I-2hh^{\top})v&\mbox{if }h^{\top}v<0\end{array}\right..

As one example, if
h=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{\top}
then
G_{h}(v)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\max(v_{1},v_{2})\\
\min(v_{1},v_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. This special case of the Householder activation is called the MaxMin activation
[
36
,
37
]
. A more general ”groupwise” Householder and MaxMin activation functions are used in
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
but we will focus on the (single group) Householder in (
IV-B
) for simplicity.

The Householder activation function can be expressed with an affine transformation on ReLU. Let
1_{n_{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
be the vector of all ones and
N\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}\times n_{v}}
be any matrix with
N1_{n_{v}}=0
. Then
G_{h}
can be written as:

\displaystyle G_{h}(x)=v+\left(\frac{2}{n_{v}}h1_{n_{v}}^{\top}+N\right)\,\Phi\left(-(h^{\top}v)\cdot 1_{n_{v}}\right).

(48)

Equation
48
corresponds to an affine transformation as in (
32
) with
A_{0}=I
,
A_{1}=\frac{2}{n_{v}}h1_{n_{v}}^{\top}+N
, and
A_{2}=-1_{n_{v}}h^{\top}
. This transformation is not unique due to the choice of
N
. A similar affine transformation appeared in
[
13
]
for the MaxMin activation.

It is possible to choose
N
such that
A_{1}
is nonsingular. By Lemma
5
(a), if ReLU
\Phi
satisfies a QC defined by
M
then the Householder activation
G_{h}
satisfies the QC defined by
\hat{M}:=R_{A}^{\top}MR_{A}

with
R_{A}
defined in (
33
). Thus we can use the complete set of QCs for ReLU to define QCs for
G_{h}
. However,
A_{2}
is singular in this affine transformation. Hence Lemma
5
(b) does not apply, i.e. we cannot equivalently map between QCs for
\Phi
and
G_{h}
. Therefore, the complete set of QCs for
G_{h}
are not necessarily constructed from the complete set for
\Phi
.

We can provide a direct construction for the complete set of QCs of the Householder activation
G_{h}
. This uses a similar technique as used to define
\mathcal{M}_{c}
for ReLU. First, define the following set of matrices:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{h}

This section focuses on the numerical issues associated with the complete set

Let

VI-B

Lipschitz Bounds With Incremental QCs

This section provides a simple example to bound the Lipschitz constant of a simple Neural Network (NN). We use the LipSDP condition
[
5
]
but combined with our complete set of
incremental QCs for ReLU. Consider
an
\ell
-layer NN
f:\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{\ell+1}}
defined by weights
W^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k+1}\times n_{k}}

and biases
b^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k+1}}
. The NN maps
x\in R^{n_{0}}
to
y=f(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\ell+1}}
by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{0}&=x\\
x^{k+1}&=\Phi^{k}(W^{k}x^{k}+b^{k})\mbox{ for }k=0,\ldots,\ell-1\\
y&=W^{\ell}x^{\ell}+b^{\ell}.\end{split}

(61)

Here we consider the case where
k^{th}
activation function
\Phi^{k}
is a ReLU of dimension
n_{k+1}
.
L
is a Lipschitz bound (in the
2
-norm) for the NN i
\|f(x)-f(\hat{x})\|_{2}\leq L\|x-\hat{x}\|_{2}
for all
x,\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}
.

We can stack
the activation functions into a single
ReLU
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
with total dimension
m:=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}n_{k}
.
The NN can be expressed compactly as

\displaystyle Bx

\displaystyle=\Phi(Ax+b)

(62)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=Cx+b^{\ell},

(63)

where
5
5

5

blkdiag(
\cdots
) denotes the block diagonal augmentation of matrices.

\displaystyle x:=\begin{bmatrix}x^{0}\\
\vdots\\
x^{\ell}\end{bmatrix},\,\,\,b:=\begin{bmatrix}b^{0}\\
\vdots\\
b^{\ell-1}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle A:=\begin{bmatrix}\mbox{blkdiag}(W^{0},\ldots,W^{\ell-1})&0_{m\times n_{\ell}}\end{bmatrix},\,\,\,B:=\begin{bmatrix}0_{m\times n_{0}}&I_{m}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle C:=\begin{bmatrix}0_{n_{\ell+1}\times(n_{0}+m-n_{\ell})}&W^{\ell}\end{bmatrix},\,\,\,D=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n_{0}}&0_{n_{0}\times m}\end{bmatrix}

It follows from Theorem 2 in
[
5
]
that
L
is a Lipschitz bound for
f
if there exists
an incremental QC, defined by
M
, for
\Phi
such that the following LMI holds:

\displaystyle LMI_{Lip}(M,L):=\begin{bmatrix}A\\
B\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}A\\
B\end{bmatrix}+C^{\top}C-L^{2}\,D^{\top}D\preceq 0

(64)

We can find the smallest Lipschitz bound by searching over convex classes of incremental QCs. An SDP can be formulated to
minimize
L^{2}
subject to

LMI_{Lip}(M,L)\preceq 0
with
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
or the relaxed version of the complete set
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
using similar steps to those given in Section
V-C
.

We used the SDP condition to compute Lipschitz bounds for a small two-layer NN with the following weights

\displaystyle W^{0}=\begin{bmatrix}-0.575&0.420&0.050\\
-0.730&0.200&-1.020\end{bmatrix},

(65)

\displaystyle W^{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1.120&-0.630\end{bmatrix},\,\,W^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}-0.700\\
-1.300\end{bmatrix}.

(66)

The biases are not specified since they do not enter into the LMI condition.

We obtain a Lipschitz bound
L=1.2528
when searching over
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
. We also obtain
L=1.2528
when searching over both
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
and our new set
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{2}
. Thus
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{2}
does not improve the bound on this specific example. However, the bound is improved to
L=1.1817
when we use the complete set of incremental constraints
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
. The times to compute the bounds with
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
,
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}+\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
was 0.3sec, 0.3 sec, and 3.6sec, respectively.

A lower bound on the Lipschitz constant was obtained by randomly sampling
N=10^{7}
pairs of NN inputs
(x,\hat{x})
from a zero-mean, unit variance Gaussian distribution and computing the corresponding NN outputs
(y,\hat{y})
. The largest value of the ratio

\frac{\|y-\hat{y}\|_{2}}{\|x-\hat{x}\|_{2}}
over all the samples (assuming
x\neq\hat{x}
) is a lower bound on the Lipschitz constant. This sampled lower bound is
1.1817
. This matches the upper bound with
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
up to the reported digits.

This is an academic example illustrating that it is possible to improve upon the Lipschitz bounds obtained only using
\mathcal{M}^{inc}_{1}
. Importantly, the complete set
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{inc}
provides a unifying view that may aid in the search of additional useful subsets of incremental QCs.
More realistic NNs would have activation functions where the total dimension
m
can be very large, requiring future study on possible combinations of our proposed method and existing SDP scaling techniques
[
12
]
.

VII
Conclusions

This paper derived a complete set of quadratic constraints (QCs) for the ReLU. The complete set of QCs is described by a collection of
2^{n_{v}}
matrix copositivity conditions where
n_{v}
is the dimension of the ReLU. The relationship between our complete set and existing QCs has been carefully discussed. We also derived a similar complete set of incremental QCs for ReLU.
We illustrate the use of the complete set of QCs to assess stability and performance for recurrent neural networks with ReLU activation functions. We will study, as future work, the conservatism (if any) and the scalability of using the complete set of ReLU QCs for stability/performance analysis.

Homological mirror symmetry for the symmetric squares of punctured spheres
Yankı Lekili, Alexander Polishchuk

Abstract
. For an appropriate choice of a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -grading structure, we prove that the wrapped Fukaya category of the symmetric square of a ( k + 3 ) (k+3) -punctured sphere, i.e. the Weinstein manifold given as the complement of ( k + 3 ) (k+3) generic lines in ℂ ​ P 2 \mathbb{C}P^{2} is quasi-equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on a singular surface 𝒵 2 , k \mathcal{Z}_{2,k} constructed as the boundary of a toric Landau-Ginzburg model ( 𝒳 2 , k , 𝐰 2 , k ) (\mathcal{X}_{2,k},{\mathbf{w}}_{2,k}) . We do this by first constructing a quasi-equivalence between certain categorical resolutions of both sides and then localising. We also provide a general homological mirror symmetry conjecture concerning all the higher symmetric powers of punctured spheres. The corresponding toric LG-models ( 𝒳 n , k , 𝐰 n , k ) (\mathcal{X}_{n,k},{\mathbf{w}}_{n,k}) are constructed from the combinatorics of curves on the punctured sphere and are related to small toric resolutions of the singularity x 1 ​ … ​ x n + 1 = v 1 ​ … ​ v k x_{1}\ldots x_{n+1}=v_{1}\ldots v_{k} .

1.2.
Formulation of the main result

In this paper, we give an explicit candidate for the
B
-side for any
n
and
k
. To that end, inspired by the work of Abouzaid-Auroux-Katzarkov
[
1
]
, we introduce a smooth toric variety
\mathcal{Y}_{n,k}
over
\mathbb{A}^{k}=\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbf{k}[V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}]
with the general fiber isomorphic to
the torus
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
and the special fiber
\mathcal{Z}_{n,k}
over
V_{1}=V_{2}=\ldots=V_{k}=0
given by a union of toric varieties glued along toric strata

1.3

In fact,
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n,k}
is a small toric resolution of the singularity
X_{1}\ldots X_{n+1}=V_{1}\ldots V_{k}

(see Sec.
2.4

We also consider a Landau-Ginzburg version by considering the variety

\mathcal{X}_{n,k}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}_{n,k}\times\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbf{k}[U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}]

with the potential

\mathbf{w}_{n,k}=U_{1}V_{1}+U_{2}V_{2}+\ldots+U_{k}V_{k}

There is a universal grading of the Fukaya category of
V_{n,k}
with values in an abelian group

{\mathbb{L}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n+k+1}
which is compatible with a certain natural
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading structure. In order to get a relation with the B-side we identify
{\mathbb{L}}
with a natural grading group
{\mathbb{L}}_{B}
on the B-side, which is a sublattice in
the character group of an algebraic torus acting on
{\mathcal{X}}_{n,k}
in a way that rescales

\mathbf{w}_{n,k}
according to the given character
l_{0}\in{\mathbb{L}}_{B}
.

The possible
\mathbb{Z}
-grading structures on
V_{n,k}
is a torsor over
H^{1}(V_{n,k})=\mathbb{Z}^{n+k}
. Among these there is a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-grading structure
\eta_{0}
on
V_{n,k}
which roughly speaking is determined by the property that the primitive chords around the

have degree 0, and the primitive chords around
v_{i}
have degree 2. This corresponds to the unique subgroup
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
in the above torus, such that all
x_{i}
and
V_{j}
have weight
0
(and
U_{j}
have weight
1

-graded equivariant matrix factorizations of

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n},D_{n,k},\eta_{0})\simeq D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathcal{Y}_{n,k})

2.1.
(Multi)gradings of

Let

{\mathbb{L}}
-grading to a

The usual notion of an

-algebra which is a

-graded vector space with some operations

Definition 2.1.1
.

An
{\mathbb{L}}
-graded

-algebra
is an
{\mathbb{L}}
-graded vector space

equipped with operations

\mathfrak{m}_{n}:A^{\otimes n}\to A,\ n\geq 1,

where the
{\mathbb{L}}
-degree of

-axioms

\sum_{m,n}(-1)^{|a_{1}|+\ldots+|a_{n}|-n}\mathfrak{m}_{d-m+1}(a_{d},\ldots,a_{n+m+1},\mathfrak{m}_{m}(a_{n+m},\ldots,a_{n+1}),a_{n},\ldots a_{1})=0.

where we use
the induced
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading on
A
. Similarly, we can define the notion of an
A_{\infty}
-homomorphism between
{\mathbb{L}}
-graded
A_{\infty}
-algebras.

We will call a homomorphism
f:{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbb{Z}

admissible
if
f(l_{0})=1
and
f(x)\equiv|x|\operatorname{mod}(2)
.
Given an
{\mathbb{L}}
-graded
A_{\infty}
-algebra

and an admissible homomorphism

,
we can collapse the
{\mathbb{L}}
-grading on
A
to a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading and get a usual
A_{\infty}
-algebra
A/f
.
Note that an admissible homomorphism

induces a decomposition

{\mathbb{L}}=\mathbb{Z}\cdot l_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{L}}_{0},

where
{\mathbb{L}}_{0}=\operatorname{ker}(f)
. Then the
{\mathbb{L}}
-grading on

can be viewed as an
{\mathbb{L}}_{0}
-grading on

compatible with the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading,
such that all
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
on

preserve the
{\mathbb{L}}_{0}
-degree. The same is true about
A_{\infty}
-homomorphisms (in particular, gauge equivalences)
between
{\mathbb{L}}
-graded
A_{\infty}
-algebras
A
and
B
: they can be viewed as usual

-homomorphisms between the usual
A_{\infty}
-algebras

and

, preserving the

-degree.

Note that when

is finitely generated, an

Let

-graded
A_{\infty}
-algebras.

Lemma 2.1.3

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{\bullet})

-graded

2.2.
Multigraded Fukaya categories

Multigradings appear naturally in the context of Fukaya categories: they have been used in
[
23
]
,
[
4
]
and studied extensively by Sheridan
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
. Given a symplectic manifold
V
, we consider
the relative Lagrangian Grassmannian of the tangent bundle
TV
,

\mathcal{G}V\to V.

The exact sequence associated to this fibration gives the following exact sequence

\mathbb{Z}=H_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{p}V)\to H_{1}(\mathcal{G}V)\to H_{1}(V)\to 0

where
{\mathcal{G}}_{p}V
is the Lagrangian Grassmannian of the tangent space at a point
p\in V
, and
the identification
\mathbb{Z}=H_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{p}V)
is given by the Maslov index. We set

{\mathbb{L}}:=H_{1}(\mathcal{G}V)

and let
l_{0}
be the image of
1\in\mathbb{Z}
under the homomorphism
H_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{p}V)\to H_{1}(\mathcal{G}V)
.

In addition, we choose a homomorphism

{\mathbb{L}}=H_{1}(\mathcal{G}V)\to\mathbb{Z}/2

which sends
l_{0}
to 1.

In
[
24
]

[
25
]
, a canonical choice of such a homomorphism is given by the pairing with the first Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{1}(\mathcal{L})\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}V;\mathbb{Z}/2)
of the universal bundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{G}V
whose fiber over a point is the Lagrangian subspace at that point. This corresponds to the canonical
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading structure on Fukaya categories corresponding to the fibrewise double cover of
\mathcal{G}V
given by the oriented Lagrangian Grassmannian of the tangent bundle
TV
.

To allow for other choices of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-gradings, we consider pairings with

w_{1}(\mathcal{L})+p^{*}\sigma\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}V;\mathbb{Z}/2)

for
\sigma\in H^{1}(V;\mathbb{Z}/2)
, where
p:\mathcal{G}V\to V
is the natural projection. By a slight abuse of notation, we denote the corresponding homomorphism by

\sigma:\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{Z}/2

Note that as we obtained this by pulling back a class from
V
, this homomorphism still satisfies
\sigma(l_{0})=1
.

Sheridan explains in
[
24
, Section 3]
that the gluing formulae for index theory of Cauchy-Riemann operators allow one to define an
{\mathbb{L}}
-graded Fukaya category and in
[
25
, Appendix B.3]
provides careful elaborations of the arguments by Seidel in
[
22
, Sections 11 & 12 ]
. The key topological observation that makes this work is that if there is a holomorphic disk contributing to some
A_{\infty}
-product, then its boundary is nullhomologous. The proof given in
[
25
, Appendix B.3]
applies in the same way if we use the more general
\mathbb{Z}/2
-gradings corresponding to
\sigma:\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{Z}/2
after modifying the definition in
[
25
, Definition B.4]
so that
\mathbb{Z}/2
degree of a generator is computed with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}/2
grading associated to
\sigma
.

The objects of this Fukaya category are
{\mathbb{L}}
-graded Lagrangian submanifolds of
V
. This means that, for a Lagrangian submanifold
L
, we require a lift of its Gauss map
L\to\mathcal{G}V
to the universal abelian cover
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}V\to\mathcal{G}V
and a Pin structure on
L
. If
H_{1}(L)\to H_{1}(V)
is trivial, then such a lift exists (and is unique up to translation by
{\mathbb{L}}
for connected
L
). A Pin structure on
L
exists if and only if the second Stiefel-Whitney class vanishes, and the space of Pin structures on
L
is a torsor over
H^{1}(L;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. In this paper, we work only with contractible Lagrangians, so they will always be gradable (uniquely up to translation by
{\mathbb{L}}
) and will have canonical Pin structures on them.

In summary, for each
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading structure
\sigma
on
V
, which can be prescribed by exhibiting a homomorphism
\sigma:\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
\sigma(l_{0})=1
, we can construct a
\mathbb{L}
-graded Fukaya category of
V
, where
\mathbb{L}=H_{1}(\mathcal{G}V)
. There is an effective
H^{1}(V;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
worth of choices for the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading structures, but once we fix such a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading, there is a canonical
\mathbb{L}
-graded Fukaya category.

Remark 2.2.1
.

i) Vanishing of
2c_{1}(V)
guarantees that the morphism
\mathbb{Z}=H_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{p}V)\to H_{1}(\mathcal{G}V)
is injective, or equivalently
l_{0}\in{\mathbb{L}}
is non-torsion.

ii) Recall that the usual way of equipping Fukaya categories with a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading goes via similar construction (see
[
20
]
) however instead of using the universal abelian cover of
\mathcal{G}V
, one considers a fibrewise universal cover of
\mathcal{G}V
. Such a cover exists if and only if
2c_{1}(V)=0
. Moreover, if non-empty, the space of such covers is an
H^{1}(V)
-torsor.

E=(E,d_{E})
,
F=(F,d_{F})
of
(G,l_{0})
-equivariant matrix factorizations
we define the
L(l_{0})
-graded space of morphisms

\hom_{L(l_{0})}(E,F):=R\Gamma(X,\operatorname{com}_{L(l_{0})}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E,F)))^{G},

where on the right we take the totalization of the
\mathbb{Z}\times L(l_{0})
-graded complex with
respect to the homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\times L(l_{0})\to L(l_{0}):(n,l)\mapsto nl_{0}+l
.
We denote by
\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}
the corresponding
L(l_{0})
-graded dg-category
(sometimes we will omit
l_{0}
from the notation).

Note that we have a natural shift operation
(E,d_{E})\mapsto(E[1],d_{E[1]})
, where

E[1]_{0}=E_{1}\otimes l_{0},\ E[1]_{1}=E_{0}\otimes l_{0},

and
d_{E[1]}
is induced by
-d_{E}
. This shift operation is compatible with the shift of degree by
l_{0}
on the spaces
of morphisms.

Remark 2.3.2
.

It is clear from the definition that
if a global function
f
on
X
has weight
l
with respect to the
G
-action, then the multiplication by
f

gives an endomorphism of degree
2l
of any object of
\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}
.
Thus, the natural
G
-weights get doubled when passing to the endomorphisms in this category.

Lemma 2.3.3
.

(i) There is an equivalence of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded dg-categories

\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{\mathbb{Z}l_{0}}\simeq\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{\mathbb{Z}},

where on the left we consider the subcategory corresponding to the
subcomplexes of
\hom
-spaces that have grading in
\mathbb{Z}l_{0}\subset L(l_{0})

(and identify the grading group
\mathbb{Z}l_{0}
with
\mathbb{Z}
).

(ii) Assume that
G
is a split algebraic torus:
G\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
.
Then for a subgroup
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\subset G
, such that
l_{0}
restricts to the identity character of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, the
natural forgetful functor

\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}\to\operatorname{MF}_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}(X,W)

acts as identity on morphism spaces by collapsing the
L(l_{0})
-grading to the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading via the homomorphism

L(l_{0})\hookrightarrow L\to\mathbb{Z}
dual to the embedding
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows immediately from the definitions. For part (ii), we first observe that the homomorphism
p_{\mathbb{Z}}:L\to\mathbb{Z}
induces a splitting

L=\mathbb{Z}l_{0}\oplus K,

where
K=\operatorname{ker}(p_{\mathbb{Z}})
. From this we get an induced splitting

L(l_{0})=\mathbb{Z}l_{0}\oplus 2K.

Next, let
(H,d_{H})=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E,F)
, for some

(G,l_{0})
-equivariant matrix factorizations
E
and
F
.
Then we observe that

\operatorname{com}_{L(l_{0})}(H)=\bigoplus_{k\in K}\operatorname{com}(H)\otimes k.

Due to our assumption on
G
, this implies that

R\Gamma(X,\operatorname{com}_{L(l_{0})}(H))^{G}\simeq R\Gamma(X,\operatorname{com}(H))^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

as claimed.
∎

From Lemma
2.3.3
(i) we see that one can recover all morphisms
in
\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}
from those in
\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{\mathbb{Z}}
using the isomorphism
of morphism spaces in
\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}
,

\operatorname{Hom}^{2l+ml_{0}}(E,F)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}^{ml_{0}}(E,F\otimes l).

Assume now that
W
is not a zero divisor.
Then using the equivalence (
2.2
)
we get an equivalence

\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{\mathbb{Z}l_{0}}\simeq\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq D_{\operatorname{sing}}([X_{0}/G]).

Thus, given a
G
-equivariant coherent sheaf

on

, we get from the corresponding object of the

-equivariant singularity category an object of
\operatorname{MF}_{G,l_{0}}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}
.

2.3.4.
Grothendieck-Serre duality and a vanishing criterion for morphisms

Assume now that
G={\mathbb{G}}_{m}
and
l_{0}
is the identity character.
Recall that for a pair of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant matrix factorizations of
W
,
E
and
F
, the cohomology of the morphism
space are given by

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}(E,F)\simeq H^{*}(X,\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E,F)))^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}.

Now assume that we have a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant proper
morphism
\pi:X\to S
, where
S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is a regular Noetherian affine scheme with a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action.
Then we can define a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant sheaf on
S
by

R\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)_{S}:=R\pi_{*}\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E,F)).

In addition, we will need the following construction from

[
17
, Sec. 1.1]
.
For a bounded complex
(C^{\bullet},\delta)
of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant vector bundles on
X
, we define
a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant matrix factorization
\operatorname{mf}(C)
of
0
, by setting

\operatorname{mf}(C)_{0}=\bigoplus_{n}C^{2n}\otimes l_{0}^{-n},\ \ \operatorname{mf}(C)_{1}=\bigoplus_{n}C^{2n-1}\otimes l_{0}^{-n},

with the differential induced by
\delta
.
It is easy to see that there is a natural isomorphism of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant complexes

\operatorname{com}(\operatorname{mf}(C))=\bigoplus_{n}C\otimes l_{0}^{n}[-2n].

Lemma 2.3.4
.

(i) In the above situation,
for
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant matrix factorizations
E
and
F
,
one has an isomorphism

R\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)^{\vee}_{S}\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}(F,E\otimes\omega_{X/S}[n])_{S},

where
n
is the relative dimension of
\pi
, and
M^{\vee}=R\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathcal{O}}_{S})
, for
M\in D_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}(S)
.

(ii) In the same situation assume that
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(F,E)\simeq\operatorname{mf}(i_{*}H)
for a closed
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-invariant
subscheme
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
and
H\in D_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}(Z)
, such that

i^{*}\omega_{X/S}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\otimes l_{0}^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the vanishing of
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}(E,F)
implies the vanishing of
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}(F,E)
.

Proof.

(i) Since
\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(F,E\otimes\omega_{X/S}))\simeq\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E,F))^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X/S}
,
this follows immediately from the usual (
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant) Grothendieck-Serre duality applied to
\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(E,F))
.

(ii) Set
{\mathcal{H}}:=R\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)_{S}
. By assumption, we have
R\Gamma(S,{\mathcal{H}})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}=0
. But we also have

{\mathcal{H}}\otimes l_{0}^{n}\simeq{\mathcal{H}}[2n],

hence, we get
R\Gamma(S,{\mathcal{H}}\otimes l_{0}^{n})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
S
is affine, this implies that
{\mathcal{H}}=0
.
Therefore, by part (i), we deduce that

R\pi_{*}\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(F,E\otimes\omega_{X/S}[n]))\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}(F,E\otimes\omega_{X/S}[n])_{S}=0.

By assumption, we have an isomorphism

\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(F,E))\otimes\omega_{X/S}\simeq\bigoplus_{n}i_{*}H\otimes l_{0}^{n}[-2n]\otimes\omega_{X/S}\simeq\bigoplus_{n}i_{*}H\otimes l_{0}^{n+m}[-2n]\simeq\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(F,E))\otimes l_{0}^{m}.

Therefore, we deduce the vanishing of
R\pi_{*}\operatorname{com}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(F,E))
, and hence, of
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}(F,E)
.
∎

2.3.5.
Generation

We will use the standard generation result for the singularity categories (see
[
15
, Thm. 3.5]
).
However, we need to adapt this result to the multigraded categories we work with.

Let us assume that we are in the setup of Sec.
2.3.3
. Assume in addition that
G
is a split algebraic torus,
and let us fix a subgroup
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\subset G
, such that
l_{0}|_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is the identity character
\chi
.

Lemma 2.3.5
.

Let
Z\subset X_{0}
be a
G
-invariant closed subscheme containing the singular locus of
X_{0}
, and let

(E_{i})_{i\in I}
be a collection of
G
-equivariant coherent sheaves on

, which we view as objects of

and as objects of
\operatorname{MF}_{G}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}

Assume that

generate the

. Then
(E_{i})
generate
\operatorname{MF}_{G}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}
.

Proof.

The category
\operatorname{MF}:=\operatorname{MF}_{G}(X,W)_{L(l_{0})}
has a natural quasicoherent extension
\operatorname{MF}^{\operatorname{qcoh}}
, such that the objects of
\operatorname{MF}
are compact.
Thus, to prove that
(E_{i})
generate
\operatorname{MF}
, it is enough to check that for
X\in\operatorname{MF}^{\operatorname{qcoh}}
,
the vanishing
\operatorname{Hom}(E_{i},X)=0
for all

implies that

.
It is enough to know the same assertion after collapsing the

-grading to the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading via some homomorphism

f:L(l_{0})\to\mathbb{Z}
sending
l_{0}
to
1
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.3.3
(ii),
we can replace
G
with our fixed subgroup
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.
Thus, we are reduced to showing that
(E_{i})
generate
\operatorname{MF}_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}(X,W)
. Note that

E_{i}\otimes l_{0}^{n}\simeq E_{i}[2n]

in this category. Hence, using equivalence with the singularity category (
2.3
), it is enough to check that

(E_{i}\otimes\chi^{n})
generate
D_{\operatorname{sing}}([X_{0}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])
. By the equivariant version of
[
15
, Thm. 3.5]
, it is enough to check that
the subcategory of
D^{b}\operatorname{Coh}(X_{0}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
generated by
(E_{i}\otimes\chi^{n})
contains
i_{*}D^{b}\operatorname{Coh}(\operatorname{Sing}X_{0})

But this follows from our assumption on
(E_{i})
.
∎

2.3.6.
Elementary computations with Koszul matrix factorizations

Let us denote by
\{a,b\}
the Koszul matrix factorization of
a\cdot b
, where
a
and
b
are possibly sections of
line bundles,

E_{1}={\mathcal{O}}(-b)\rTo{b}E_{0}={\mathcal{O}}\rTo{a}{\mathcal{O}}(a)=E_{1}(ab)

where
a
(resp.,
b
) is a section of a line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}(a)
(resp.,
{\mathcal{O}}(b)
).
In the computations below we view coherent sheaves as matrix factorizations of
0
via the functor
\operatorname{mf}(\cdot)

described in Sec.
2.3.4
.

Lemma 2.3.6
.

(i) Assume
(a,b)
is a regular sequence. Then one has natural quasiisomorphisms

\underline{\operatorname{End}}(\{a,b\})\simeq\{a,b\}^{\vee}|_{b=0}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}/(a,b).

(ii) Assume
(a,b,c)
is a regular sequence. Then one has natural quasiisomorphisms

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\{bc,a\},\{ca,b\})\simeq\{bc,a\}^{\vee}|_{b=0}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}(a)/(a,b)[-1].

The generating morphism comes from the exact triangle

{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(b)\rTo{a}{\mathcal{O}}/(ab)\to{\mathcal{O}}/(a)\to{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(b)[1].

(iii) Assume
(a,b,c)
is a regular sequence. Then one has natural quasiisomorphisms

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\{c,ab\},\{ca,b\})\simeq\{c,ab\}^{\vee}|_{b=0}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}/(b,c),

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\{ca,b\},\{c,ab\})\simeq\{ca,b\}^{\vee}|_{ab=0}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(b,c).

The generating morphism for the former is the natural projection
{\mathcal{O}}/(ab)\to{\mathcal{O}}/(b)
,
while the generating morphism for the latter corresponds to the natural embedding

{\mathcal{O}}/(b)\rTo{a}{\mathcal{O}}(a)/(ab).

Proof.

We use the standard quasiisomorphism of matrix factorizations of zero,

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}((E,d_{E}),\{a,b\})\simeq(E,d_{E})^{\vee}\otimes\{a,b\}\simeq(E,d_{E})^{\vee}|_{b=0}

(see
[
18
, Prop. 1.6.3(ii)]
).

In (i) this leads to

{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(b)\rTo{a}{\mathcal{O}}/(b)\rTo{0}\ldots

which is quasiisomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}/(a,b)
.

In (ii) we get

{\mathcal{O}}(-bc)/(b)\rTo{0}{\mathcal{O}}/(b)\rTo{a}{\mathcal{O}}(a)/(b)

which is quasiisomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}(a)/(a,b)[-1]
.

In (iii), for the first
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
we get

{\mathcal{O}}(-c)/(b)\rTo{c}{\mathcal{O}}/(b)\rTo{0}\ldots

which is quasiisomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}/(b,c)
.

Finally, for second
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
in (iii) we get

{\mathcal{O}}(-ca)/(ab)\rTo{ca}{\mathcal{O}}/(ab)\rTo{b}{\mathcal{O}}(b)/(ab)\to\ldots

We observe that

\operatorname{ker}(ca:{\mathcal{O}}/(ab)\to{\mathcal{O}}(ca)/(ab))=\operatorname{im}(b:{\mathcal{O}}(-b)/(ab)\to{\mathcal{O}}/(ab)),

\operatorname{ker}(b:{\mathcal{O}}/(ab)\to{\mathcal{O}}(b)/ab)=\operatorname{im}(a:{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(ab)\to{\mathcal{O}}/(ab)).

Hence, the above matrix factorization of
0
is quasiisomorphic to

\operatorname{coker}({\mathcal{O}}(-ca)/(ca)\rTo{ca}a{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(ab))\simeq\operatorname{coker}({\mathcal{O}}(-ca)/(b)\rTo{c}{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(b))\simeq{\mathcal{O}}(-a)/(b,c).

∎

2.4.
Toric geometry of
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n,k}

Below we use the notation from the description of the dual cones of
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n,k}
from Sec.
1.3
.
Let us denote by
\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
the linear combinations of vectors in
S_{\phi}
with nonnegative integer coefficients.

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Fix a nonincreasing map
\phi:[1,n]\to[1,k]
.

(i) Each subset
S_{\phi}\subset H
is a basis of
H
.

(ii) One has
c_{i,j}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
whenever
j<\phi(i)
and
-c_{i,j}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
whenever
j\geq\phi(i)
.

(iii) For each
i=1,\ldots,k
and each
j=1,\ldots,n+1
, the vectors
v_{i}
and
x_{j}
are in
\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
.

Proof.

(i) This follows immediately from the fact that the natural map of lattices

H_{1}(\Sigma_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus H_{1}(\Sigma_{r})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\not\in\operatorname{im}(\phi)}\mathbb{Z}\cdot v_{i}\to H

is an isomorphism.

(ii) The relations
c_{i,j-1}=c_{i,j}+v_{j}
and
-c_{i,j+1}=-c_{i,j}+v_{j+1}
reduce this to

c_{i,\phi(i)-1}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}},\ \ -c_{i,\phi(i)}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}.

Let us prove the first inclusion—the proof of the second is similar. We use descending induction on
i
.
If
\phi(i+1)<\phi(i)
or
i=n
then

c_{i,\phi(i)-1}\in S_{\phi}
by the definition. If
\phi(i+1)=\phi(i)
then
x_{i+1}\in S_{\phi}
and we can write

c_{i,\phi(i)-1}=c_{i+1,\phi(i+1)-1}+x_{i+1}
, and the assertion follows from the induction assumption.

(iii) The fact that
v_{i}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

follows from the first definition of
S_{\phi}
(to get
S_{\phi}
from
S
we keep all
v_{i}
for
i\not\in\operatorname{im}(\phi)
and replace each
v_{i}

for
i\in\operatorname{im}(\phi)
with a collection of vectors with the sum
v_{i}
).

Next, let us prove that
x_{i}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
. For
i=1
this follows from the equality
x_{1}=-c_{1,k}
.
For
i>1
we use the equality
x_{i}=c_{i-1,j}-c_{i,j}
.
If
\phi(i)<\phi(i-1)
then we set
j=\phi(i)
and note that
-c_{i,j}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
and

c_{i-1,j}\in\langle S_{\phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
since
j<\phi(i-1)
. In the case
\phi(i)=\phi(i-1)
we have
x_{i}\in S_{\phi}
by the definition.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{n,k}
denote the toric hypersurface
X_{1}\ldots X_{n+1}=V_{1}\ldots V_{k}
in the affine space with coordinates

(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n+1},V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})
.
We have a natural toric birational morphism

\pi:{\mathcal{Y}}_{n,k}\to{\mathcal{S}}_{n,k},

which is a resolution of singularities since
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n,k}
is smooth.

Note that
{\mathcal{S}}_{n,k}
is the affine toric variety associated with the cone

C\subset{\mathbb{R}}H^{\vee}
spanned by the vectors
\omega_{ij}=e_{i}+f_{j}
,
i=1,\ldots,n+1
,
j=1,\ldots,k
.
Here we use the presentation

H=(\mathbb{Z}x_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}x_{n+1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}v_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}v_{k})/(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n+1}-v_{1}-\ldots-v_{k}),

which allows to identify
{\mathbb{R}}H^{\vee}
with the hyperplane
\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n+1}=\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{k}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, and we
denote by
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+1})
and
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
the standard bases in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
.

Note that the vectors
\omega_{ij}
are precisely the vertices of the polytope

\Pi:=\Delta_{n}\times\Delta_{k-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{k},

where
\Delta_{m}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
is the standard simplex spanned by the basis vectors.
Thus,
C
is the cone generated by this polytope. Now taking cones over simplices of a standard triangulation of
\Pi

(see e.g.,
[
5
, Ch. 7, Sec. 3D]
) one gets a toric fan, and the corresponding toric variety is a small resolution of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n,k}

(since we do not add new
1
-dimensional cones). We claim that this precisely leads to our variety
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n,k}
.

Proposition 2.4.2
.

The map
\pi
is the small resolution, associated with the standard triangulation of
\Pi
corresponding to the usual ordering of the vertices of

\Delta_{n}
and the reverse ordering of the vertices of
\Delta_{k-1}
.

Proof.

We need to identify the dual simplices to
\sigma^{\vee}_{\phi}
, where
\phi
runs through nonincreasing maps
[1,n]\to[1,k]
,
with the simplices of the standard triangulation of
\Pi
described in
[
5
, Ch. 7, Sec. 3D]
.
Note that due to our ordering of the vertices of
\Delta_{k-1}
, the latter simplices
are numbered by lattice paths from
(1,k)
to
(n+1,1)
, i.e., paths obtained by starting at the point
(1,k)
and moving either to the right or down
along the grid until reaching the point
(n+1,1)
. Alternatively, they can be numbered by shuffles
w
of the word

A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n}B_{1}B_{2}\ldots B_{k-1}
(preserving the order of
A_{i}
’s and of
B_{j}
’s), where
A_{i}
stands for the
i
th horizontal move
and
B_{j}
stands for the
j
th vertical move.

The simplex
\Delta_{L}
of the triangulation associated with the lattice path
L
is given inside
\Pi
by the collection of inequalities:

\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{i}\leq\beta_{k}+\ldots+\beta_{j}
whenever
A_{i}
precedes
B_{j}
in the corresponding shuffled word
w_{L}
, and

\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{i}\geq\beta_{k}+\ldots+\beta_{j}
whenever
B_{j}
precedes
A_{i}
.

We have a natural bijection between lattice paths and nonincreasing functions
\phi:[1,n]\to[1,k]
: for a lattice path
L

we let
\phi_{L}
to be the unique function such that

(i+1/2,\phi_{L}(i))
lies on the path
L
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. Now it is easy to see that
A_{i}
precedes
B_{j}
in the shuffled word
w_{L}

if and only if
\phi_{L}(i)\geq j
. Thus, due to formula (
1.8
)
the simplex
\Delta_{L}
is given inside
\Pi
by the inequalities
\langle v,c_{i,j-1}\rangle\geq 0
whenever
\phi_{L}(i)\geq j
, and

\langle v,-c_{i,j-1}\rangle\geq 0
whenever
\phi_{L}(i)<j
. Hence, the same inequalities give the cone
\sigma_{L}
generated by
\Delta_{L}
inside
the cone
C
.

It remains to observe that the cone
C
is dual to the cone generated by all vectors
x_{i}
and
v_{j}
in
H
. Hence,
Lemma
2.4.1
implies that the cone
\sigma_{L}
is dual to the cone
\sigma^{\vee}_{\phi_{L}}
. Thus,
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n,k}
is precisely the
toric variety associated with the fan with the maximal cones
\sigma_{L}
.
∎

6.

6.1.

Here we prove Theorem

.

First, we observe that we can match the grading groups. Namely, for this we use canonical decompositions

In the case

, we consider the correspondence between the generating objects

It is easy to see that it extends to the isomorphism between the corresponding (cohomology) endomorphism algebras,
so that endomorphisms

and

and

on the

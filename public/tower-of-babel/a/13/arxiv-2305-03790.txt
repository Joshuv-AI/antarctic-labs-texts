Hidden regularity in singular optimal control of port-Hamiltonian systems Thanks: Preprint submitted to Arxiv on 11/20/23
Timm Faulwasser, Jonas Kirchhoff, Volker Mehrmann, Friedrich Philipp, Manuel Schaller, Karl Worthmann

Abstract
We study the problem of state transition on a finite time interval with minimal energy supply for linear port-Hamiltonian systems. While the cost functional of minimal energy supply is intrinsic to the port-Hamiltonian structure, the necessary conditions of optimality resulting from Pontryagin’s maximum principle may yield singular arcs. The underlying reason is the linear dependence on the control, which makes the problem of determining the optimal control as a function of the state and the adjoint more complicated or even impossible. To resolve this issue, we fully characterize regularity of the (differential-algebraic) optimality system by using the interplay of the cost functional and the dynamics. In case of the optimality DAE being characterized by a regular matrix pencil, we fully determine the control on the singular arc. In case of singular matrix pencils of the optimality system, we propose an approach to compute rank-minimal quadratic perturbations of the objective such that the optimal control problem becomes regular. We illustrate the applicability of our results by a general second-order mechanical system and a discretized boundary-controlled heat equation.

This weaker condition is related to the regularity of the matrix pencil, which is associated with the necessary optimality condition arising from a Pontryagin maximum principle. The derivation of this general existence condition using the port-Hamiltonian structure of the OCP is at the center of our work. In the case where this existence condition fails to hold, we provide a regularization procedure via minimal-rank perturbations of the cost functional such that the pencil associated with the optimality condition becomes regular.

Typically, only some (and not all) of the control constraints are inactive. Then, denoting the active and inactive set corresponding to the control constraints by

The approach in
[

studies the optimality conditions associated with (

When this optimality system is regular in the DAE sense, then the optimal control is linearly determined by the optimal state trajectory and associated adjoint. Moreover, criteria on the existence and uniqueness of an optimal control can be deduced, see also
[

For cases where this regularity is not given, one can resort to regularizations of the OCP via “small” perturbations of the original cost functional by a “small” quadratic term in the control and obtains the regularized optimal control problem

\displaystyle x(t_{0})

\displaystyle=x^{0},\ x(t_{1})=x^{1},

\displaystyle y(t)

This work is structured as follows. First, we recall the necessary and sufficient optimality conditions obtained from the Pontryagin maximum principle. These conditions are given as a linear DAE in the state, the adjoint, and the control. Solution formulas for this DAE in terms of the Drazin inverse can be applied if the associated pencil is regular. Therefore, we study the properties of this pencil in Section
3
. Our results are summarized in Figure
1
.
In particular, in Subsection
3.1

[
8
]
and relate them to the sufficient regularity condition

In Section

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{x}=\Bigg(\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}0_{\ell\times\ell}&-I_{\ell}\\
I_{\ell}&0_{\ell\times\ell}\end{bmatrix}}_{J}-\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}D&0_{\ell\times\ell}\\
0_{\ell\times\ell}&0_{\ell\times\ell}\end{bmatrix}}_{R}\Bigg)\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}M^{-1}&0_{\ell\times\ell}\\
0_{\ell\times\ell}&K\end{bmatrix}}_{Q}x+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}I_{\ell}\\
0_{\ell\times\ell}\end{bmatrix}}_{B}u,

see for example
[
45
]
for an application in the control of high-rise buildings. We observe that
\ker RQ\cap\mathrm{im}\,B=\{0\}
holds; that is, the sufficient condition for regularity derived in
[
37
]
is satisfied. Thus, we can inspect the corresponding optimal control problem (
5
) without regularization term, i.e.
S=0
. Further, using the
Hautus Lemma
, see e.g.
[
41
]
, it is immediate that the pair
((J-R)Q,B)
is controllable. Hence, we may assume that
\lambda_{0}=1
in (
9
).

Decomposing
also
\lambda=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{2\ell}

analogously to the state, (
9c
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(t)=0\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}].

(12)

The adjoint equation (
9b
) reads

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}M^{-1}D&-M^{-1}\\
K&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{2}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}M^{-1}DM^{-1}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}

and inserting (
12
) yields

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\lambda}_{2}(t)=0\quad\mbox{for a.e.}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\lambda_{2}(t)=-DM^{-1}x_{1}(t)\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}].

From this, we further conclude

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{x}_{1}(t)=0\quad\mbox{for a.e.}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]\quad\text{with}\quad x_{1}(t)\equiv x_{1}^{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad x_{1}(t)=x_{1}^{1}\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}].

This already yields the condition
x_{1}^{0}=x_{1}^{1}
on the entry and exit point of the singular arc and
\lambda_{2}(t)=-DM^{-1}x_{1}^{0}
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
. Inserting this into the second row of the state equation yields
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{x_{2}}(t)=M^{-1}x_{1}^{0}
for a.e.
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
and thus

x_{2}(t)=x_{2}^{0}+(t-t_{0})M^{-1}x_{1}^{0}\ \quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}].

From this we get the additional condition
x_{2}^{1}=x_{2}^{0}+(t_{1}-t_{0})M^{-1}x_{1}^{0}
, coupling the entry and exit point with the time at which the solution leaves the singular arc. In particular, if
x^{0}\neq x^{1}
, an optimal solution only exists for one particular length
t_{1}-t_{0}
of the singular arc.

Finally, the first row of the state equation implies the formula for the optimal control, which is the
feedback law

\displaystyle u(t)=DM^{-1}x_{1}(t)+Kx_{2}(t)=(D+(t-t_{0})K)M^{-1}x_{1}^{0}+Kx_{2}^{0}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}],

where the last part of the equation is an explicit solution formula in terms of the initial value.

In Example
2.3
we observed that, for second-order mechanical systems, the control on the singular arc can be expressed directly via the entry point
x^{0}
. The aim of this paper is to provide a generalisation of this approach to optimal control problems of the form (
4
) or, if a regularization is necessary, its regularized counterparts (
5
), or (
8
).
To derive an explicit solution formula, we recall the concept of the Drazin inverse.

Definition 2.4
(Drazin inverse, see
[
18
, Definition 2.17]
).

Let
M\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
. The unique matrix
M^{D}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
with the properties

(i)

M^{D}M=MM^{D}
,

(ii)

M^{D}MM^{D}=M^{D}
,

(iii)

there exists
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:M^{D}M^{\nu+1}=M^{\nu}
,

is called the
Drazin inverse
of
M
. The minimal
\nu
satisfying (iii) is called the
(Riesz) index
of
M
and coincides with the nilpotency index of
M
, which is the size of the largest Jordan block associated with the eigenvalue zero.

Recall further the well-known solution formula for initial value problems associated with linear time-invariant differential-algebraic systems with commuting coefficients using the Drazin inverse, see e.g.
[
18
, Lemma 2.25 and Theorem 2.27]
for a proof.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval with
t_{0}\in I
and consider the homogeneous system

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E-A\right)z=0,

(13)

with
E,A\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
satisfying
EA=AE
and
\ker E\cap\ker A=\left\{0\right\}
. A function
z\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(I,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution of (
13
)
if, and only if,
z(t_{0})\in\mathrm{im}\,E^{D}E
and

\displaystyle z=e^{E^{D}A(\cdot-t_{0})}z(t_{0}).

(14)

Unfortunately, the matrices
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{A}_{S}
in the optimality system (
10
) commute if, and only if,
B=0
in which case the optimal control problem (
8
) becomes trivial, since the boundary value problem either possesses no solution (which is generically the case) or every control is optimal. However, if the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular (i.e.
\det(s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S})\in\mathbb{K}[s]
is not the zero-polynomial), then there exists
\mu\in\mathbb{K}
so that
\mu\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is invertible and the coefficient matrices of the transformed pencil
(\mu\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S})^{-1}(s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S})
commute as the following elementary result shows.

Lemma 2.6
(
[
9
, Lemma 2, p. 418]
).

Let
E,A\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
. Then for every
\mu\in\mathbb{K}
such that
\mu E-A
is invertible,

\displaystyle[(\mu E-A)^{-1}E][(\mu E-A)^{-1}A]=[(\mu E-A)^{-1}A][(\mu E-A)^{-1}E].

It is then clear that the interplay of Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
leads to a solution formula for (
10
) if the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular, see
[
18
]
for a detailed analysis. However, it should be noted that this formula characterises only continuously differentiable solutions, while we initially considered weak solutions in, e.g., (
5
). On the other hand, we ultimately seek for an optimal control which is a linear feedback of the state or the state and associated adjoint. In this case, the solution of the optimality system (
10
) is smooth and can be obtained from the solution formula.

3
Analysis of the optimality pencil
\bm{s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}}

In this section, we study properties of the pencil associated to the optimality DAE (
10
). In particular, we consider its regularity, which is strongly connected with the solution formula in terms of the Drazin inverse, the index and rank-minimal choice of the regularization term
S
. The main results of this section are summarised in the Figure
1
.

3.1
Regularity

Conditions that characterize the regularity of the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
via normal and staircase forms are well studied, see
[
7
,
8
,
29
]
.
The next proposition contains such conditions in the special case of port-Hamiltonian systems.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S^{H}=S\geq 0
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular.

(ii)

There exists
\mu\in\mathbb{K}\setminus(\sigma(-((J-R)Q)^{H})\cup\sigma((J-R)Q))
such that the matrix

\displaystyle S_{\mu}:=S-\begin{bmatrix}B^{H}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&(J-R)Q-\mu I\\
((J-R)Q)^{H}+\mu I&QRQ\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}B\\
0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}

(15)

is invertible.

(iii)

There exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle i\omega\notin\sigma((J-R)Q)\quad\text{and}\quad\ker S\cap\ker RQ\big((J-R)Q-i\omega I\big)^{-1}B=\left\{0\right\}.

(16)

(iv)

There exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)\quad\text{and}\quad\ker S\cap\ker RQ\big(JQ-i\omega I\big)^{-1}B=\left\{0\right\}.

(17)

(v)

There exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)\quad\text{and}\quad\ker S\cap B^{-1}(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ=\left\{0\right\},

(18)

Proof.

The equivalence of (i)–(iii) follows directly from Proposition 1 and Theorem 5 from
[
8
]
, and the equivalence of (iv) and (v) is straightforward. It remains to prove that
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular if, and only if, (iv) holds. To this end, we first show that for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\big(\sigma(JQ)\cup\sigma\big((J-R)Q\big)\big)
we have

\displaystyle\big(JQ-zI\big)^{-1}RQ\big((J-R)Q-zI\big)^{-1}=\big((J-R)Q-zI\big)^{-1}RQ\big(JQ-zI\big)^{-1}.

This follows immediately by applying
\big(JQ-zI\big)^{-1}
from the left and from the right to the equality

\displaystyle RQ\big((J-R)Q-zI\big)^{-1}\big(JQ-zI\big)

\displaystyle=RQ(JQ-RQ-zI)^{-1}(JQ-zI)

\displaystyle=RQ(JQ-RQ-zI)^{-1}(JQ-RQ-zI+RQ)

\displaystyle=RQ+RQ(JQ-RQ-zI)^{-1}RQ

\displaystyle=(JQ-RQ-zI+RQ)(JQ-RQ-zI)^{-1}RQ

\displaystyle=(JQ-zI)(JQ-RQ-zI)^{-1}RQ

\displaystyle=\big(JQ-zI\big)\big((J-R)Q-zI\big)^{-1}RQ.

Hence, for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\big(\sigma(JQ)\cup\sigma\big((J-R)Q\big)\big)
,

\displaystyle\ker RQ((J-R)Q-zI)^{-1}B

\displaystyle=\ker\big((J-R)Q-zI)\big)^{-1}RQ(JQ-zI)^{-1}B

\displaystyle=\ker RQ(JQ-zI)^{-1}B.

Now, note that if (
16
) holds for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, then it holds in a neighborhood of this
\omega
. The same is true for (
17
). Since the spectrum of a matrix is finite, this proves the claim.

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
8
, Theorem 7]
it is proven that regularity of
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is also equivalent to the uniqueness of optimal controls for the OCP (
8
), if they exist.

Typically, the necessary and sufficient conditions for the regularity of the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
in Proposition
3.1
are hard to verify in applications, since all of them are existence statements which contain resolvents.
Therefore, we seek to find explicit or resolvent-free conditions.
A
necessary
condition,
that meets both requirements is the following result inspired by
[
8
, Proposition 6]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S^{H}=S\geq 0
. If
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular, then

\displaystyle\ker S\,\cap\,\bigcap_{r=0}^{n-1}\ker RQ(JQ)^{r}B=\left\{0\right\}.

(19)

The converse implication holds for single-input-single-output systems (i.e. the case
m=1
), but not in general.

Proof.

If
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular, then (
17
) holds for all but finitely many
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
due to Proposition
3.1
(iv), and since the set of all
\mu\in\mathbb{K}
so that
\mu\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is invertible
is either empty or co-finite. Thus, there exists some
\Omega>\|JQ\|
so that for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-\Omega,\Omega)
one has that
i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)
and
\ker S\cap\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B=\left\{0\right\}
.
Hence, for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-\Omega,\Omega)
the Neumann series

(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{-i}{\omega}\right)^{k+1}(JQ)^{k}

converges absolutely and

\displaystyle RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{-i}{\omega}\right)^{k+1}RQ(JQ)^{k}B.

(20)

Using the Cayley-Hamilton Theorem, see e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 8.6]
, we have that for all
r\geq n
there exist coefficients
a_{0}^{r},\ldots,a_{n-1}^{r}\in\mathbb{K}
, such that

(JQ)^{r}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}^{r}(JQ)^{j}.

Hence, we conclude that for all
r\geq n

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\rho=0}^{n-1}\ker RQ(JQ)^{\rho}B\,\subset\,\ker\sum_{\rho=0}^{n-1}a_{\rho}^{r}RQ(JQ)^{\rho}B=\ker RQ(JQ)^{r}B.

This shows that
\ker S\cap\bigcap_{r=0}^{n-1}\ker RQ(JQ)^{r}B=\ker S\cap\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}\ker R(JQ)^{r}B
. Therefore, for all
v\in\ker S\cap\bigcap_{r=0}^{n-1}\ker RQ(JQ)^{r}B
and for all

\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-\Omega,\Omega)
, we have that

\displaystyle RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}Bv=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}RQ(JQ)^{k}Bv\left(\frac{-i}{\omega}\right)^{k+1}=0

and hence

v=0
.

Consider the case that
m=1
, i.e.
S\in\mathbb{K}^{1\times 1}

and
B\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times 1}
.
If
S
is non-zero, then the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is obviously regular. If
S=0
then
Proposition
3.1
(iv) yields that
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular if, and only if, there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, with

i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)
and
RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B\neq 0
.
Seeking a contradiction, assume that (
19
) holds and that
RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B=0
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with
i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)
. In this case, the representation (
20
) and the identity theorem for power series
[
17
, Corollaries 1.2.4 and 1.2.7]
yield that
RQ(JQ)^{r}B=0
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, which
contradicts our assumption. Therefore
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is indeed regular.

The following example shows that the converse implication does not hold in general.
Let
n=m=2
,
Q=B=I_{2}
,
S=0_{2\times 2}
and

\displaystyle J=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad R=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}.

Then for

\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{\pm 1\right\}

we have

\displaystyle\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}=(J-i\omega I)\ker R=\left\{(z,i\omega z)^{\top}\,\big|\,z\in\mathbb{C}\right\}\neq\left\{0\right\}.

Since
\ker S=\mathbb{C}^{m}
, Proposition
3.1
(iv) yields that
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is singular. On the other hand, we have, for all
\omega,\mu\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{\pm 1\right\}
with
\omega\neq\mu

\displaystyle\ker R(J-i\omega I)^{-1}\cap\ker R(J-i\mu I)^{-1}=\left\{0\right\}.

Using the Neumann series,
this yields that (
19
) holds; for details see the upcoming Lemma
3.4
. This shows that (
19
) is, in general, not sufficient for regularity of
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
.

We have seen that the condition (
19
) is necessary but not sufficient for regularity of
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
. The following lemma reveals the underlying reason for this shortcoming:
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular if, and only if,
\ker S\cap\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B=\left\{0\right\}
for one fixed
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, whereas the condition (
19
) holds if and only if the intersection of the kernel of
RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B
over infinitely many
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
has trivial intersection with
\ker S
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S^{H}=S\geq 0
. Then (
19
) holds if, and only if, there exists
\Omega>0
such that all
\omega_{1}>\omega_{0}>\Omega
fulfil the property

\displaystyle\ker S\ \cap\bigcap_{\omega_{0}<\omega<\omega_{1}}\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B=\left\{0\right\}.

(21)

Proof.

Let
\phi:=\frac{1}{\omega}
. Then, the series (
20
) is a formal power series
F\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times m}[[\phi]]
with non-vanishing convergence radius
\rho\geq\frac{1}{\left\|JQ\right\|}
, where we use the convention
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
. Each
v\in\mathbb{K}^{m}
induces then the formal power series
F(\cdot)v\in\mathbb{K}^{n}[[\phi]]
whose convergence radius is at least
\rho
. By definition of
F
, we then have for all
\phi\in(-\rho,\rho)
and for all
v\in\mathbb{K}^{m}
that

\displaystyle F(\phi)v=RQ\left(JQ-\frac{i}{\phi}I\right)^{-1}Bv.

Therefore, the identity theorem for power series
[
17
, Corollaries 1.2.4 and 1.2.7]
yields the equivalence

\displaystyle v\in\ker S\cap\bigcap_{r=0}^{n-1}\ker RQ(JQ)^{r}B\iff v\in\ker S\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ F(\cdot)v\equiv 0.

The latter is equivalent to

\displaystyle v\in\ker S\ \cap\bigcap_{\omega_{0}<\left|\omega\right|<\omega_{1}}\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B

for all
\omega_{0}<\omega_{1}\in(1/\rho,\infty)
.
This shows that (
19
) and (
21
) are indeed equivalent.

As we have demonstrated, the inverse-free necessary condition (
19
) is not sufficient for regularity of
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
. However, the next proposition presents an inverse-free necessary and sufficient condition.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S^{H}=S\geq 0
. The pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular if, and only if, there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)\quad\text{and}\quad B\ker S\cap(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ=\left\{0\right\}=\ker B\cap\ker S.

(22)

Proof.

Assume that the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular. By Proposition
3.1
(iv), there exists some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with
i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)
such that
\ker S\cap\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B=\left\{0\right\}
. In particular, this implies that
\ker S\cap\ker B=\left\{0\right\}
. Let
v\in B\ker S\cap(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ
. Then, there exists
u\in\ker S
so that
v=Bu
and
(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}Bu\in\ker RQ
or, equivalently,
u\in\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B
. Thus,
u=0
and hence
v=0
, so that (
22
) holds.

Conversely, let
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
be such that (
22
) holds for this particular
\omega
. Let
u\in\ker S\cap\ker RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}B
and set
v:=Bu\in B\ker S
. Then
RQ(JQ-i\omega I)^{-1}v=0
, i.e.
v\in(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ
. Hence, we obtain
v=0
or, equivalently,
u\in\ker B
. Since
\ker B\cap\ker S=\{0\}
, we conclude that
u=0
and Proposition
3.1
(iv) yields that
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular.

Condition (
22
) implies, in particular, that the block matrix
[B,JQ-i\omega I]
acts injectively on the cartesian product
\ker S\times\ker RQ
. This immediately yields a rank criterion for the regularity of
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S=S^{H}\geq 0
. Then the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular if, and only if, there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\dim\big(B\ker S+(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ\big)=m+n-(\mathrm{rk}\,S+\mathrm{rk}\,RQ).

(23)

Proof.

Let
Z_{S}\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times(m-\mathrm{rk}\,S)}
and
Z_{R}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times(n-\mathrm{rk}\,RQ)}
be matrices with full column rank such that
\mathrm{im}\,Z_{S}=\ker S
and
\mathrm{im}\,Z_{R}=\ker RQ

and we have the formula

\dim(B\ker S+(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ)=\mathrm{rk}\,[BZ_{S},(JQ-i\omega I)Z_{R}].

Let
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
be regular. In view of Proposition
3.5
, this is the case if, and only if, the restriction of
B
to
\ker S
is injective and
B\ker S\cap(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ=\left\{0\right\}
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
so that
JQ-i\omega I
is invertible. Hence, for this particular
\omega
we have
\mathrm{rk}\,BZ_{S}=\dim B\ker S=\dim\ker S=m-\mathrm{rk}\,S
and
\mathrm{rk}\,(JQ-i\omega I)Z_{R}=\dim(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ=\dim\ker RQ=n-\mathrm{rk}\,RQ
and the sum
B\ker S+(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ
is a direct sum. Thus, (
23
) holds, i.e.

\displaystyle\dim(B\ker S+(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ)

\displaystyle=\dim(B\ker S)+\dim((JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ)

\displaystyle=n+m-(\mathrm{rk}\,S+\mathrm{rk}\,RQ).

Conversely, assume that (
23
) is satisfied. The set of all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathrm{rk}\,[BZ_{S},(JQ-i\omega I)Z_{R}]<n+m-(\mathrm{rk}\,S+\mathrm{rk}\,RQ)

either coincides with
\mathbb{R}
or is finite. By condition (
23
), the former does not hold.
In particular, there is some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
so that
i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)
such that (
23
) holds at this
\omega
. Then, we can conclude analogously that (
22
) holds. From Proposition
3.5
, we conclude that the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular.

Whereas the previous characterizations of regularity required the choice
i\omega\notin\sigma(JQ)
, condition (
23
) can be verified without computing the spectrum of
JQ
.

3.2
Rank-minimal regularization of the cost functional

Recall that one of our goals is to choose a positive semidefinite matrix
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
of minimal rank,
to achieve
regularity of the optimality system. In the following we construct a regularization term of minimal rank in the cost functional of (
8
) such that the resulting pencil in the optimality system is regular.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
be such that the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is singular for
S=0
. Let

\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with

and consider
the subspace

\displaystyle V:=B^{-1}(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ.

Let

with

and

(e.g. the orthogonal projection onto

). Then

\displaystyle\dim B^{-1}(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ=\min\left\{\dim B^{-1}(JQ-zI)\ker RQ\,\big|\,z\in\mathbb{C}\right\},

(24)

which holds for all but finitely many
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}\,S=\min\left\{\mathrm{rk}\,\widehat{S}\,\left|\,s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{\widehat{S}}\penalty\ \text{regular}\right.\right\}.

(25)

Proof.

Since

, Proposition
3.1
(v) yields that
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular. Assume now that (
24
) holds. We show that
S
is rank-minimal in the sense of (

\widehat{S}\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
\mathrm{rk}\,\widehat{S}\leq\mathrm{rk}\,S
so that

and

is regular. By Proposition

(v), we find some
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
so that

. Since (

\displaystyle\dim\left(\mathrm{im}\,B+(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ\right)=\max\left\{\dim\left(\mathrm{im}\,B+(JQ-zI)\ker RQ\right)\,\big|\,z\in\mathbb{C}\right\},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\geq\dim\big(\ker\widehat{S}+B^{-1}(JQ-i\omega_{0}I)\ker RQ\big)

\displaystyle=\dim\ker\widehat{S}+\dim B^{-1}(JQ-i\omega_{0}I)\ker RQ

The following result extends
[

, Theorem 8]
to regularized cost functionals and not necessarily invertible matrices

.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and

with

. If

\displaystyle B\ker S\cap\ker RQ=\left\{0\right\}=\ker B\cap\ker S,

then
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular. The converse is false, in general. Moreover, if (

) holds, then optimal controls for the OCP (

Proof.

In the following, denote by
M_{S,T}(A)
the minor of a matrix
A
with row and column index sets
S
and
T
, respectively, where
|S|=|T|
.

Let

and
Z_{R}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times(n-\mathrm{rk}\,RQ)}
such that
\mathrm{im}\,Z_{Q}=\ker S
and
\mathrm{im}\,Z_{R}=\ker RQ
. Then assumption (

) yields that

has full column rank
\tau=n+m-(\mathrm{rk}\,S+\mathrm{rk}\,RQ)
. Hence, there exists a non-zero minor

with

. By continuity, there exists

which is (

) with

.

and

and let

The previous result included a condition which is sufficient but not necessary for regularity. In the following we show that it is indeed necessary for regularity with an additional condition on the index.

3.3
Index of the optimality DAE (
9
)

An important property for the numerical solution of the optimality boundary value problem (
9

Kronecker index
, i.e. the size of the largest block associated with the eigenvalue
\infty
in the Kronecker canonical form
[
12
]
of the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
. The next proposition characterizes condition (
26
) in the case
S=0
in terms of the Kronecker index.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
,
and
S=0_{m\times m}
. Then

\mathrm{im}\,B\cap\ker RQ=\{0\}=\ker B

(27)

holds if, and only if, the pencil
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular with Kronecker index three.

Proof.

First of all, since
S=0
we have that (
27
) is equivalent to (
26
). Hence, by Proposition
3.8
, the pencil is regular in both logical directions of the proof. Therefore, we may assume that the pencil is regular. By Proposition
3.5
, there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with

such that

\mathrm{im}\,B\cap(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ=\mathrm{im}\,B\cap(JQ+i\omega I)\ker RQ=\{0\}=\ker B.

We now have to prove that
V:=\mathrm{im}\,B\cap\ker RQ=\{0\}
if, and only if, the pencil is of index three. We set
A:=(J-R)Q
.

One easily computes the first four elements of the increasing

(see
[
4
, Definition 2.1]
or
[

), defined by
\mathcal{W}_{0}=\{0\}
and

for

\mathcal{W}_{1}=\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{K}^{m}
,
\mathcal{W}_{2}=\{0\}\times\mathrm{im}\,B\times\mathbb{K}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}-QRQv\\
Av+w\end{bmatrix}:v,w\in\mathrm{im}\,B\right\}\times\mathbb{K}^{m}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{4}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}-QRQJQv-QRQw\\
A^{2}v+Aw+y\end{bmatrix}:v\in V,\,w,y\in\mathrm{im}\,B\right\}\times\mathbb{K}^{m}.

Note that here
\mathcal{E}^{-1}
denotes the preimage of the map defined by
\mathcal{E}
and not the inverse of
\mathcal{E}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{W}_{0}\neq\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{1}\neq\mathcal{W}_{2}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{W}_{2}\neq\mathcal{W}_{3}
, since
\mathcal{W}_{2}=\mathcal{W}_{3}
is equivalent to
\mathrm{im}\,B\subset\ker RQ
and

, which leads to

i.e.,
B=0
, contradicting
\ker B=\{0\}
. From
[
4

, we conclude that the Kronecker index of the pencil index is at least three, and that the index is three if, and only if,
\mathcal{W}_{3}=\mathcal{W}_{4}
.

Now,
\mathcal{W}_{3}=\mathcal{W}_{4}
is easily seen to be equivalent to the statement that for each
x\in V
there exist

such that
RQJQx=RQv
and
JQJQx=JQv+w
. Hence,

obviously implies
\mathcal{W}_{3}=\mathcal{W}_{4}
. Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{W}_{3}=\mathcal{W}_{4}
and let

.
Then there exist
v,w\in\mathrm{im}\,B
such that
RQJQx=RQv
and
(JQ)^{2}x=JQv+w
. Since
RQx=0
and thus
RQ[(JQ+i\omega I)x-v]=0
, we have

\displaystyle(JQ-i\omega I)[(JQ+i\omega I)x-v]

\displaystyle=((JQ)^{2}+\omega^{2}I)x-(JQ-i\omega I)v

\displaystyle=w+\omega^{2}x+i\omega v\in\mathrm{im}\,B\cap(JQ-i\omega I)\ker RQ=\{0\},

and hence

(JQ+i\omega I)x=v\in\mathrm{im}\,B\cap(JQ+i\omega I)\ker RQ=\{0\}.

Thus,

follows which shows that
V=\{0\}
.

4
Existence of an optimal trajectory

In our considerations so far, we have only focused on the regularity of the pencil associated with the necessary and sufficient optimality conditions of the port-Hamiltonian optimal control problem (
5
). Using the general solution formula that we presented in Proposition
2.5
, we can derive a solution formula for (
10
) along the lines of
[
8
, pp. 1096]
.

Recall the Drazin inverse from Definition
2.4
and note that
M^{D}M=MM^{D}
coincides with the spectral projection onto the direct sum of the generalized eigenspaces (root subspaces) of
M
corresponding to its non-zero eigenvalues.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S^{H}=S\geq 0
be such that
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular. Choose
\mu\in\mathbb{K}\setminus(\sigma((J-R)Q)\cup\sigma(-((J-R)Q)^{H}))
such that
\mu\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is invertible, define

as in (
15
) and

\displaystyle E_{\mu}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}0&(J-R)Q-\mu I\\
((J-R)Q)^{H}+\mu I&QRQ\end{bmatrix}^{-1},

\displaystyle N_{\mu}

\displaystyle:=\left(E_{\mu}+E_{\mu}\begin{bmatrix}B\\
0\end{bmatrix}S_{\mu}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}B^{H}&0\end{bmatrix}E_{\mu}\right)\begin{bmatrix}0&-I\\
I&0\end{bmatrix},

(28)

\displaystyle M_{\mu}

\displaystyle:=S_{\mu}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}B^{H}&0\end{bmatrix}E_{\mu}\begin{bmatrix}0&-I\\
I&0\end{bmatrix}.

(29)

A function
\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^{T}&x^{T}&u^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{K}^{n+n+m})
is a solution of (
10
) if, and only if,
\begin{bmatrix}\lambda(t_{0})\\
x(t_{0}))\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{im}\,(N_{\mu}^{D}N_{\mu})
and

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\lambda\\
x\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=e^{-[N_{\mu}^{D}(I-\mu N_{\mu})](\,\cdot\,-t_{0})}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda(t_{0})\\
x(t_{0})\end{bmatrix},

(30)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=M_{\mu}N_{\mu}^{D}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda\\
x\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

In view of the calculations in
[
8
, pp. 1096]
and Proposition
2.5
,
\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^{T}&x^{T}&u^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
is a solution of (
10
) if, and only if, there are
v_{\lambda},v_{x}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda\\
x\end{bmatrix}&=e^{-[N_{\mu}^{D}(I-\mu N_{\mu})](\cdot-t_{0})}N_{\mu}^{D}N_{\mu}\begin{bmatrix}v_{\lambda}\\
v_{x}\end{bmatrix},\\
u&=M_{\mu}N_{\mu}^{D}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda\\
x\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

(31)

Note that

is the spectral projection onto the sum of the root subspaces of
N_{\mu}
corresponding to the non-zero eigenvalues.
Therefore, every function with
\begin{bmatrix}\lambda(t_{0})\\
x(t_{0}))\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{im}\,(N_{\mu}^{D}N_{\mu})

that has the representation (

) is indeed a solution of (

). Conversely, let
\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^{T}&x^{T}&u^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{K}^{n+n+m})
be a solution of (

) and let

) holds.

\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^{T}(t_{0})\\

, which proves the assertion.

The statement of Proposition
4.1
shows that the subspace
\mathrm{im}\,(N_{\mu}^{D}N_{\mu})
determines the set of admissible initial values for the boundary value problem (

) does not depend on the particular choice of

, the subspace

does not either, which is shown in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S^{H}=S\geq 0
be such that
s\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is regular. Choose

and

are invertible. Then we have
\mathrm{im}\,(N_{\mu}^{D}N_{\mu})=\mathrm{im}\,(N_{\nu}^{D}N_{\nu})
.

The following proposition finally characterizes the existence of a solution of the optimality system with prescribed initial and terminal values for the state, cf.
[
8
, Theorem 4]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with
S^{H}=S\geq 0
. Let
\mu\in\mathbb{K}\setminus(\sigma((J-R)Q)\cup\sigma(-(J-RQ)^{H}))
be such that
\mu\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{A}_{S}
is invertible and let

be such that for all

\displaystyle e^{-[N_{\mu}^{D}(I-\mu N_{\mu})](t-t_{0})}=\begin{bmatrix}E_{1}(t)&E_{2}(t)\\
E_{3}(t)&E_{4}(t)\end{bmatrix}.

(32)

If
E_{3}(t_{1})
is invertible, then (
10
) possesses a solution
\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^{T}&x^{T}&u^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{K}^{n+n+m})
with

and

if, and only if,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}E_{3}(t_{1})^{-1}[x^{1}-E_{4}(t_{1})x^{0}]\\
x^{0}\end{bmatrix}\,\in\,\mathrm{im}\,(N_{\mu}^{D}N_{\mu}).

(33)

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, the system (

) has a solution
(x,\lambda,u)
with

if, and only if,
\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^{T}(t_{0})\\

, we obtain

\displaystyle x(t_{1})=E_{3}(t_{1})\lambda(t_{0})+E_{4}(t_{1})x^{0},

and hence

if, and only if,
\lambda(t_{0})=E_{3}(t_{1})^{-1}[x^{1}-E_{4}(t_{1})x^{0}]
.

The optimal control that we have obtained in Proposition

, cf. (

), is not a state feedback solution, as it depends additionally on the adjoint. However, Proposition

enables us to eliminate this dependence and to define the optimal control as a state feedback.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(J,R,Q,B)\in\Sigma_{n,m}
and
S\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
with

. Let

is invertible and let the condition (

\displaystyle u(t)=M_{\mu}N_{\mu}^{D}e^{-[N_{\mu}^{D}(I-\mu N_{\mu})](t-t_{0})}\begin{bmatrix}E_{3}^{\mu}(t_{1})^{-1}[x^{1}-E_{4}^{\mu}(t_{1})x^{0}]\\
x^{0}\end{bmatrix}

for all

with

and

) and (

).

The formula (

\displaystyle u=M_{\mu}N_{\mu}^{D}e^{-[N_{\mu}^{D}(I-\mu N_{\mu})](\cdot-t_{0})}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda(t_{0})\\

Inserting the formula

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}^{0}\\

Example 5.4
.

We consider the temperature distribution in a domain

,

with a Dirichlet boundary control on a part of the boundary

\displaystyle\theta(s,\gamma)

\displaystyle=0,\

\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}},t\in[0,T],

\omega\in\Omega

Discretizing in space by central finite differences, see e.g.

where

consists of the nodal values of the temperature profile, i.e.

are zero, such that

We briefly illustrate this for the one-dimensional case with

\displaystyle\Delta\theta(t,\omega_{j})\approx\frac{\theta(t,\omega_{j+1})-2\theta(t,\omega_{j})+\theta(t,\omega_{j-1})}{h^{2}},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-2.

6
Conclusions and outlook

The optimality system associated with the problem of steering a port-Hamiltonian system from an initial state to a terminal state on a given time interval with minimal energy supply has been studied. Regularity of this differential-algebraic equation has been characterized exploiting the port-Hamiltonian structure. Minimal rank quadratic perturbations of the cost functional have been characterized so that the perturbed problem produces a regular optimality system. Assuming regularity, the solution formula for regular linear time-invariant differential-algebraic equations leads to a characterization of optimal solutions. Moreover, we have derived conditions which allow to characterize the optimal control as a state feedback. Last, the results are illustrated by means of examples, including second-order mechanical systems and a discretized heat equation.

Future works consider extensions to control of nonlinear or infinite-dimensional port-Hamiltonian systems, such as beam equations, building upon
[
31
]
are subject to future research. In the nonlinear case, first steps were conducted by
[
15
,
27
]
. In the infinite-dimensional case, we expect that the structure of the descriptor matrix in the optimality DAE can help to generalize the results.

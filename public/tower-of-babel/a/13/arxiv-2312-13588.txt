A few new oddtown and eventown problems
Griffin Johnston, Jason O’Neill

Abstract
Given a vector α = ( α 1 , … , α k ) ∈ 𝔽 2 k \alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k} , we say a collection of subsets ℱ \mathcal{F} satisfies α \alpha -intersection pattern modulo 2 2 if all i i -wise intersections consisting of i i distinct sets from ℱ \mathcal{F} have size α i ( mod 2 ) \alpha_{i}\pmod{2} . In this language, the classical oddtown and eventown problems correspond to vectors α = ( 1 , 0 ) \alpha=(1,0) and α = ( 0 , 0 ) \alpha=(0,0) respectively. In this paper, we determine the largest such set families of subsets on a n n -element set with α \alpha -intersection pattern modulo 2 2 for all α ∈ 𝔽 2 3 \alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3} and all α ∈ 𝔽 2 4 \alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{4} asymptotically. Lastly, we consider the corresponding problem with restrictions modulo 3 3 .

In this section, we will prove three lemmas that we will utilize throughout. The following lemma establishes a connection between
f_{\alpha}(n)
and

f_{\beta}(n)
when
\alpha

\beta

Lemma 1

(Trace Lemma)
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
let
\beta=(\alpha_{t+1},\dots,\alpha_{t+r})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
where

t+r\leq k
,
f_{\alpha}(n)>k
, and
\alpha_{t+1}\neq\alpha_{t+2}
.
Then, if
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
\alpha
-intersection pattern modulo
2
and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\in\mathcal{F}
,

|\mathcal{F}|\leq f_{\beta}(|F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{t}|)+t

Let
\mathcal{F}
and

F_{1},\dots,F_{t}\in\mathcal{F}
be as in the lemma statement and set

T=F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{t}
. Then

\mathcal{F}_{T}:=\{F\cap T\ :\ F\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{F_{1},\dots,F_{t}\}\}

is a
\beta
-intersecting family. Note also that if

A,B\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{F_{1},\dots,F_{t}\}
with

A\neq B

and

A\cap T=B\cap T
, then

A\cap B\cap T=(A\cap T)\cap(B\cap T)=A\cap T
. But this
is impossible as it implies
\alpha_{t+1}=\alpha_{t+2}
, a contradiction. Since
|\mathcal{F}|=|\mathcal{F}_{T}|+t
and
\mathcal{F}_{T}
satisfies
\beta
-intersection pattern modulo
2
with ground set
|T|
, the desired bound holds.
∎

We call the intersection pattern

\alpha

Lemma 2

(Dual Lemma)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\setminus\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\}
. Then
f_{\alpha}(n-1)\leq f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n)\leq f_{\alpha}(n+1)
. Thus, if
f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n+1)/f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n)\to 1

f_{\alpha}(n)\sim f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n)

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be an
\alpha
-intersecting family.
Consider the family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq 2^{[n+1]}

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\{F\cup\{n+1\}\ :\ F\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Note that the size of each of the
m
-wise intersection also increases by exactly one for each

1\leq m\leq k
. Thus
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
satisfies

(\alpha+\mathbf{1})
-intersection pattern modulo
2

f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n-1)\leq f_{\alpha}(n)\leq f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n+1)

\frac{f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n-1)}{f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n)}\leq\frac{f_{\alpha}(n)}{f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n)}\leq\frac{f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n+1)}{f_{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}(n)}.

(1)

By (
1

Lemma 3

(Partition Sum Lemma)
.

Let

3.2  The
(0,1,1)

Here we show that

Note that

\mathcal{A}=\{F\cap F_{i}^{C}\ :\ F\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{F_{i}\}\}.

Note

(1,0)

As
\mathcal{F}

.
Hence

|\mathcal{F}|-1\leq|F_{i}^{C}|\leq n-2

as desired.

Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we studied families satisfying

The

-intersection pattern modulo

-intersection patterns modulo

For problems with intersection conditions modulo

Theorem A.1

(Folklore)
.

In this appendix, we will prove that

f_{(1,1)}(n)=\begin{cases}2^{\frac{n-2}{2}}&\text{$n$ even}\\
2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}&\text{$n$ odd}.\end{cases}

The extremal family consists of adding an auxiliary element to a canonical eventown construction. Let
2k+1\in\mathbb{Z}
be an odd integer and set
e_{i}=\{2i-1,2i\}
for
i\in[k]
. Define

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\Bigg\{\{2k+1\}\cup\;\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}e_{i}\;:I\in 2^{[k]}\Bigg\}.

Observe that for
n=2k+1
odd,
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subset 2^{[n]}
satisfies
(1,1)
-intersection pattern modulo
2
and for
n=2k
even,
\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\subset 2^{[n-1]}\subset 2^{[n]}
satisfies
(1,1)
-intersection pattern modulo
2
. Moreover, both constructions have the desired number of sets, establishing the lower bound.

For the upper bound, we proceed in a manner similar to the classical eventown proof. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
satisfies
(1,1)
-intersection pattern modulo
2
and set
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to be the corresponding characteristic vectors. Define
W=\mathrm{Span}(V)
and consider the following map
\phi:W\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
where
w\mapsto w\cdot w
. This is a surjective homomorphism, so

W/\ker\phi\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
giving us that

|\ker\phi|=\frac{1}{2}|W|
. Moreover, as
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
(1,1)
-intersection pattern modulo
2
,
\ker\phi\cap V=\emptyset
, which gives
|\mathcal{F}|=|V|\leq\frac{1}{2}|W|
. The proof is finished by the below claim:

Claim A.1
.

\dim(W)\leq(n+1)/2
. Hence, for
n
even,
\dim(W)\leq n/2
.

Let
B:=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
be a maximal linearly independent subset of
V
. We now consider the
set
B_{0}:=\{v_{1}+v_{2},v_{1}+v_{3},\dots,v_{1}+v_{m}\}
, and will show this is a linearly independent subset of
W^{\perp}
.

Indeed, for any
v\in V
,
v\cdot(v_{1}+v_{i})=1+1=0\pmod{2}
for all
i\in\{2,\dots,m\}
since
V
consists of the characteristic vectors of
\mathcal{F}
. Since
W=\mathrm{Span}(V)
, it follows that
w\cdot(v_{1}+v_{i})=0\pmod{2}
for all
w\in W
and for all
i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
. Therefore,
B_{0}\subseteq W^{\perp}
.

To see linear independence, observe that we may rewrite any non-trivial combination of scalars
\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=2}^{m}\lambda_{i}(v_{1}+v_{i})=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{m}\lambda_{i}\right)v_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}\lambda_{i}v_{i}.

Thus, since
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
is linearly independent subset of
W
, it follows that
\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i
. This means that
B_{0}\subseteq W^{\perp}
is linearly independent and therefore
\dim(W^{\perp})\geq m-1
. Hence,

n=\dim(W)+\dim(W^{\perp})\geq 2m-1\implies m\leq\frac{n+1}{2}.\qed

This completes the proof.

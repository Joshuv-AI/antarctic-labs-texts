Homogenization of Boundary Value Problems in Perforated Lipschitz Domains
Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
This paper is concerned with boundary regularity estimates in the homogenization of elliptic equations with rapidly oscillating and high-contrast coefficients. We establish uniform nontangential-maximal-function estimates for the Dirichlet, regularity, and Neumann problems with L 2 L^{2} boundary data in a periodically perforated Lipschitz domain. Keywords : Homogenization; Perforated Domain; Boundary Estimate. MR (2020) Subject Classification : 35B27; 35J25.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with boundary regularity estimates in the homogenization of elliptic equations with rapidly oscillating and
high-contrast coefficients in perforated Lipschitz domains.
Let
A=A(y)
be a real-valued
d\times d
matrix
satisfying the ellipticity condition,

\mu|\xi|^{2}\leq(A\xi)\cdot\xi\quad\text{ and }\quad\|A\|_{\infty}\leq\mu^{-1},

(1.1)

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some
\mu>0
, and the periodicity condition,

A(y+z)=A(y)\quad\text{ for any }y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(1.2)

Let
\omega
be a connected and unbounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
\omega
is 1-periodic; i.e.,
its characteristic function is periodic with respect to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
We also assume that
each of connected components of

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega

is the closure of a bounded open set
F_{k}
with Lipschitz boundary,
and that

\min_{k\neq\ell}\text{dist}(F_{k},F_{\ell})>0.

(1.3)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\Omega^{\varepsilon}=\Omega\cap\varepsilon\omega=\Omega\setminus\overline{\varepsilon F},

(1.4)

where
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
F=\cup_{k}F_{k}
. We will assume that

\text{\rm dist}(\partial\Omega,\varepsilon F)\geq\kappa\varepsilon

(1.5)

for some
\kappa\in(0,1)
.
As a result,
\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega\cup\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
, where

\Gamma^{\varepsilon}=\Omega\cap\partial(\varepsilon\omega)
, and
dist
(\partial\Omega,\Gamma^{\varepsilon})\geq\kappa\varepsilon
.
For
0\leq\delta\leq 1
, define

\Lambda_{\delta}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\overline{\omega},\\
&\delta&\quad&\text{ if }x\in F.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

We are interested in the regularity estimates, which are uniform in
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
0\leq\delta\leq 1
,
for the elliptic operator,

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}=-\text{\rm div}\big(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(x)\nabla\big)

(1.7)

in
\Omega
,
where

A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(x)=\big[\Lambda_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)\big]^{2}A(x/\varepsilon).

(1.8)

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
arises naturally in the modeling of acoustic propagation in porous media, periodic electromagnetic structures, and
soft inclusions in composite materials
[
14
,
26
]
.

In the case
\delta=1
,
the regularity estimates for
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=-\text{\rm div}(A(x/\varepsilon)\nabla)

have been studied extensively in recent years.
Using a compactness method,
the interior Lipschitz estimate and the boundary Lipschitz estimate for the Dirichlet problem in
a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain were established by M. Avellaneda and F. Lin in a seminal work
[
5
]
.
The boundary Lipschitz estimate for the Neumann problem in a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain
was obtained in
[
10
]
.
In
[
11
,
12
]
, C. Kenig and the present author investigated the
L^{p}
Dirichlet, regularity,
and Neumann problems in Lipschitz domains.
We obtain nontangentail-maximal-function estimates for the sharp ranges of
p
’s in the scalar case
[
11
]
,
and for
p
close to
2
in the case of elliptic systems
[
12
]
.
The results in
[
11
,
12
]
extend an earlier work of B. Dahlberg (unpublished) on the
L^{p}
Dirichlet problem in the scalar case.
They also extend the classical work of B. Dahlberg, E. Fabes, D. Jerison, C. Kenig, J. Pipher, G. Verchota, and many others
on the
L^{p}
boundary value problems
for elliptic equations and systems in Lipschitz domains to the periodic homogenization setting.
We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for references on boundary value problems in nonsmooth domains,
to
[
19
]
for further references on periodic homogenization, and to
[
2
]

for related work on large-scale regularity in stochastic homogenization.

In this paper we will be concerned with the case
0\leq\delta<1
in perforated domains,
where
\delta^{2}
represents the conductivity ratio of the disconnected matrix block subset
\Omega\cap\varepsilon F
to the connected subregion
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
.
Notice that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is not elliptic uniformly in
\delta
.
In the case where
A=I
,
\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
and

sufficiently smooth, using the compactness method in
[

]
,

,
24

]

Also see earlier work in
[

,

]
for uniform estimates in the case
\delta=0
.
In
[

[

In the stochastic setting with

[

In this paper we shall be mainly interested in the nontangential-maximal-function estimates, which are uniform in
\varepsilon
and
\delta

for the Dirichlet, regularity, and Neumann problems,
under the assumption that both

More precisely, we consider the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \text{div}\big(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\big)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}&=f&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.9)

and the Neumann problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \text{div}\big(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\big)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}&=g&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.10)

where
\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=n(x)\cdot A(x/\varepsilon)\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and

n
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Our main results in this paper give nontangential-maximal-function estimates, which are uniform in both
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\delta\in[0,1]
,
for (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).
These estimates
are new even for the case where
A=I

and
\omega
,
\Omega
are smooth.

For a function
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
let
N(u)
denote the (generalized) nontangential maximal function of

N(u)(x)=\sup\left\{\left(\fint_{B(y,d(y)/4)}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}:\ y\in\Omega\text{ and }|y-x|<C_{0}\,d(y)\right\}

(1.11)

for

, where

d(y)=\text{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega)
and
C_{0}=C_{0}(\Omega)>1
is sufficiently large.
We impose a Hölder continuity condition on
A=A(y)
in
\omega
,

\|A\|_{C^{0,\sigma}(\omega)}\leq M\quad\text{ for some $\sigma\in(0,1)$ and $M>0$.}

(1.12)

No smoothness condition is needed for
A
in
F
.

.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
0\leq\delta\leq 1
.
Assume that
A
satisfies conditions (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (

Then, for any

, the unique solution

of (
1.9
)

N(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
satisfies the estimate,

\|N(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(1.13)

where
C

depend only on
d
,
\mu
,
(M,\sigma)
in (
1.12
),
\omega
,
\kappa
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

.

Let

and

.

,

\|N(\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\,\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

(1.14)

where

depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
(M,\sigma)
,
\omega
,
\kappa
, and the Lipschitz character of

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

and

.
Suppose

,
\omega
and

.

\|N(\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\,\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(1.15)

,

,

,

We now describe our approaches to Theorems

1.2

Our main tool is the Rellich estimates,

\Big\|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\approx\|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(1.20)

for weak solutions of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})=0
in
\Omega
.
To prove (
1.20
),
we first use local Rellich estimates for the elliptic operator

\text{\rm div}(A\nabla
) and condition (
1.5
) as well as a covering argument to reduce the problem to the boundary layer estimates,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma_{\kappa\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,d\sigma+C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,dx,

(1.21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma_{\kappa\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}\Big|^{2}\,d\sigma+C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,dx,

where
\Sigma_{t}=\big\{x\in\Omega:\ \text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<t\big\}
.
The estimates in (
1.21
) are then proved by establishing a sharp convergence rate in
H^{1}(\Omega)

for a two-scale expansion.
We remark that a similar approach has been used in
[
18
,
6
]
in the case
\delta=1
for elliptic systems of elasticity.
To deal with the fact that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is not uniformly elliptic in
\delta
,
techniques of extension are used to treat the block regions
\varepsilon F_{k}
with small ellipticity constants.

With the Rellich estimates (
1.20
) at our disposal, we follow an approach used in
[
11
]

for scalar elliptic equations with periodic coefficients.
To prove Theorem
1.1
,
we show that

N(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\leq C\big[\mathcal{M}_{\partial\Omega}(|f|^{p_{0}})\big]^{1/p_{0}}\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega

(1.22)

for some
p_{0}<2
, where
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is a solution of (
1.9
) and

\mathcal{M}_{\partial\Omega}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on
\partial\Omega
.
This is done by applying a localized version of (
1.20
) to the Green function
G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,y)
and
using the estimate,

|G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C[\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)]^{\sigma}}{|x-y|^{d-2+\sigma}},

(1.23)

which holds if either
x,y\in\Omega^{\varepsilon}
or

with
|x-y|\geq c_{d}\varepsilon
.
Estimate (
1.23
) follows from the boundary Hölder estimate for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in Lipschitz domains, which is proved by
an approximation argument. A great amount of efforts, involving a reverse Hölder argument above the scale
\varepsilon
, is also needed to
lower the exponent in (
1.22
) to some
p_{0}<2
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we exploit the fact that
the operator

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is invariant under the translation
x\to x+\varepsilon z
, where
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
As in
[
11
]
, this allows us to dominate

for dist
(x,\partial\Omega)\geq C\varepsilon
by

\mathcal{M}_{\partial\Omega}\big\{|\nabla_{\tan}f|+M_{rad}(Q_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon\,\delta}))\big\},

where

is a radial maximal operator and

Q_{\varepsilon}(u)=\varepsilon^{-1}\big\{u(x+\varepsilon e_{d})-u(x)\big\},

with

e_{d}=(0,\dots,0,1)
.
Since
Q_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})
is a solution,
the term involving
Q_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})
may be handled by using a localized version of Theorem
1.1
and
the boundary layer estimates in (
1.21
).
Finally, Theorem
1.3

1.2
and (
1.21

\Omega

, and

.

Throughout this paper we assume that the matrix
A=A(y)

satisfies conditions (

\omega

(2.1)

\overline{F_{k}}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(1/100)\kappa\leq\text{dist}(F_{k},\partial\widetilde{F}_{k}),\\

(2.2)

Since diam
(F_{k})\leq\text{\rm diam}(Y)=\sqrt{d}

(\widetilde{F}_{k})\leq d
.
By the periodicity of
\omega

\widetilde{F}_{k}

2.1
Extension operators

Since
\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}}

is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
there exists an extension operator
T_{k}
from
\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}}
to
\widetilde{F}_{k}

such that

\displaystyle\|T_{k}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{F}_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}})},

\displaystyle\|T_{k}(u)\|_{W^{1,p}(\widetilde{F}_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}})},

for
1<p<\infty

[
21
]
.
Let

\widetilde{T}_{k}(u)=\fint_{\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus F_{k}}u+T_{k}\Big(u-\fint_{\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus F_{k}}u\Big).

Then
\widetilde{T}_{k}
is an extension operator from
\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}}
to
\widetilde{F}_{k}
,

\|\widetilde{T}_{k}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{F}_{k})}\leq C_{p}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}})},

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widetilde{T}_{k}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{F}_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\,\|u-\fint_{\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}}}u\|_{W^{1,p}(\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}})}\leq C_{p}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}})},

where we have used a Poincaré inequality.
By dilation there exist extension operators
E_{\varepsilon,k}
from
\varepsilon\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\varepsilon\overline{F_{k}}
to
\varepsilon\widetilde{F}_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\|E_{\varepsilon,k}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\varepsilon\widetilde{F}_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\varepsilon\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\varepsilon\overline{F_{k}})},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla E_{\varepsilon,k}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\varepsilon\widetilde{F}_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\varepsilon\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\varepsilon\overline{F_{k}})},

for
1<p<\infty
and
\varepsilon>0
, where
C_{p}
depends only on
d
,
p
, and
\omega
.
As a result, we obtain the following.

.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain satisfying (

\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})},

(2.4)

where

depends only on
d
,

,
\kappa
, and
\omega

The next lemma will be used to treat regions where the ellipticity constant is small.

Lemma 2.2

Suppose

and
\text{\rm div}(A(x/\varepsilon)\nabla u)=0

Then

where

depends only on

,
\mu

Proof.

By dilation we may assume

Let
\widetilde{u}\in H^{1}(\widetilde{F}_{k})
be an extension of

such that

\|\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(F_{k})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus\overline{F_{k}})}

Since
\widetilde{u}-u\in H_{0}^{1}(F_{k})
and
\text{\rm div}(A\nabla(\widetilde{u}-u))=\text{\rm div}(A\nabla\widetilde{u})
in

from which the inequality (

Lemma 2.3

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain satisfying (
1.5
).
Let

be a weak solution of (
1.9
) with

.
Then

(2.6)

where

depends only on

,
\mu
,
\kappa
,
\omega
, and
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let

be a function such that

on
\partial\Omega
and

.
Since

and

\text{\rm div}(A_{\delta}^{\delta}\nabla(u-v))=-\text{\rm div}(A_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}\nabla v)
in
\Omega

(2.7)

for any

in (

) and the Cauchy inequality,

This, together with Poincar’e’s inequality

2.2
Correctors

For
0\leq\delta\leq 1
, let

A_{\delta}(y)=\Lambda_{\delta^{2}}(y)A(y)=[\Lambda_{\delta}(y)]^{2}A(y)
, where
\Lambda_{\delta}
is defined by (
1.6
).
Observe that the
d\times d
matrix
A_{\delta}(y)
is 1-periodic.
Let
\chi_{\delta}(y)=(\chi_{\delta,1}(y),\dots,\chi_{\delta,d}(y))
be the corrector for the operator

-\text{div}(A_{\delta}\nabla)
, where, for
1\leq j\leq d
, the function

\chi_{\delta,j}\in H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a weak solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\text{\rm div}(A_{\delta}\nabla\chi_{\delta,j})=\text{\rm div}(A_{\delta}\nabla y_{j})\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\chi_{\delta,j}\text{ is 1-periodic},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.12)

with

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{Y}\chi_{\delta,j}\,dy=0&\quad&\text{ if }\delta>0,\\
&\int_{Y\cap\omega}\chi_{\delta,j}\,dy=0&\quad&\text{ if }\delta=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.13)

and
Y=[0,1]^{d}
.
If
\delta>0
, the existence and uniqueness of correctors follow readily from the Lax-Milgram Theorem
by using the bilinear form,

\int_{Y}A_{\delta}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,dy,

on the Hilbert space
H^{1}_{\text{per}}(Y)
,
the closure of 1-periodic
C^{\infty}
functions in
H^{1}(Y)
.
In the case
\delta=0
, one uses the bilinear form

\int_{Y\cap\omega}A\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,dy

on the Hilbert space
H^{1}_{\text{per}}(Y\cap\omega)
,
the closure of 1-periodic
C^{\infty}
functions in
H^{1}(Y\cap\omega)
.
This gives the definition of
\chi_{0,j}
on
\omega
.
Recall that

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\omega}=F=\cup_{k}{F_{k}}
.
We extend
\chi_{0,j}
to each
F_{k}
by using the weak solution in
H^{1}(F_{k})
of

-\text{\rm div}(A\nabla u)=\text{\rm div}(A\nabla y_{j})\ \text{ in }F_{k},

with Dirichlet data
u=\chi_{0,j}
on
\partial F_{k}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
0\leq\delta\leq 1
.
Then

\int_{Y}\Big(|\nabla\chi_{\delta}|^{2}+|\chi_{\delta}|^{2}\Big)\,dy\leq C,

(2.14)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
, and
\omega
.

Proof.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
.
By energy estimates,

\int_{Y}|\Lambda_{\delta}\nabla\chi_{\delta}|^{2}\,dy\leq C\int_{Y}|\Lambda_{\delta}A|^{2}\,dy\leq C.

This gives
\|\nabla\chi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap\omega)}\leq C
.
Next, note that

\text{\rm div}(A\nabla(\chi_{\delta,j}+y_{j}))=0

in
F_{k}
.
It follows by Lemma
2.2
that

\|\nabla\chi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(F_{k})}\leq C+C\|\nabla\chi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap\omega)}\leq C.

Since
Y\cap F_{k}\neq\emptyset
only for a finite number of
k
’s,
we obtain
\|\nabla\chi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(Y\setminus\omega)}\leq C
.
As a result, we have proved that
\|\nabla\chi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C
.
In view of (
2.13
), the estimate
\|\chi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C
follows by Poincaré’s inequality.

If
\delta=0
, the energy estimate gives
\|\nabla\chi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap\omega)}\leq C
.
By Poincaré’s inequality and (
2.13
), we obtain

\|\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(Y\cap\omega)}\leq C
.
In view of the definition of
\chi_{0}
on
F_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(F_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C+C\|\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial F_{k})}\leq C+C\|\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{F}_{k}\setminus F_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C+C\|\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(Y\cap\omega)}\leq C.

It follows that
\|\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(Y\setminus\omega)}\leq C
.
∎

2.3
Homogenized operator

The homogenized matrix for the operator
-\text{\rm div}(A_{\delta}\nabla)
is given by

\widehat{A_{\delta}}=\fint_{Y}\Big\{A_{\delta}+A_{\delta}\nabla\chi_{\delta}\Big\}\,dy.

(2.15)

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
.
Then

|\widehat{A_{\delta}}-\widehat{A_{0}}|\leq C\delta^{2},

(2.16)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
, and
\omega
.

Proof.

We provide a proof, which also may be found in
[
15
]
, for the reader’s convenience.
Note that

\displaystyle|\widehat{A_{\delta}}-\widehat{A_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Y}|\Lambda_{\delta^{2}}-\Lambda_{0}||A|\,dy+\int_{Y}|\Lambda_{\delta^{2}}A\nabla\chi_{\delta}-\Lambda_{0}A\nabla\chi_{0}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}+C\int_{Y\cap\omega}|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})|\,dy+C\delta^{2}\int_{Y\setminus\omega}|\nabla\chi_{\delta}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}+C\int_{Y\cap\omega}|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})|\,dy,

where we have used (
2.14
) for the last inequality.
To bound
|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})|
, we observe that

-\text{\rm div}(\Lambda_{\delta^{2}}A\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0}))=\text{\rm div}((\Lambda_{\delta^{2}}-\Lambda_{0})A\nabla(y+\chi_{0})).

Thus, for any
\psi\in H^{1}_{\text{per}}(Y)
,

\int_{Y}\Lambda_{\delta^{2}}A\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})\cdot\nabla\psi\,dy=-\delta^{2}\int_{Y\setminus\omega}A\nabla(y+\chi_{0})\cdot\nabla\psi\,dy.

(2.17)

We now choose
\psi\in H^{1}_{\text{p}er}(Y)
such that

\psi=\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0}
on
Y\cap\omega
and

\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap\omega)}.

(2.18)

It follows from (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) that

\displaystyle\int_{Y\cap\omega}|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\int_{Y\setminus\omega}|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})||\nabla\psi|\,dy+C\delta^{2}\int_{Y\setminus\omega}|\nabla(y+\chi_{0})||\nabla\psi|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap\omega)}.

Hence,

\|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})\|_{L^{1}(Y\cap\omega)}\leq\|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap\omega)}\leq C\delta^{2}.

∎

Note that for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\big(\widehat{A_{\delta}}\xi\big)\cdot\xi=\int_{Y}A_{\delta}\nabla\big((y+\chi_{\delta})\cdot\xi\big)\cdot\nabla\big((y+\chi_{\delta})\cdot\xi\big)\,dy.

(2.19)

It follows that

\widehat{A_{\delta}}
is symmetric if
A
is symmetric.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
0\leq\delta\leq 1
. Then for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mu_{0}|\xi|^{2}\leq\big(\widehat{A_{\delta}}\xi\big)\cdot\xi\quad\text{ and }\quad|\widehat{A_{\delta}}|\leq\mu_{0}^{-1},

(2.20)

where
\mu_{0}>0
depends only on
d
,
\mu
, and
\omega
.

Proof.

The second inequality in (
2.20
) follows readily from (
2.14
).
To see the first, note that

\displaystyle\big(\widehat{A_{\delta}}\xi\big)\cdot\xi

\displaystyle\geq\mu\min(1,\delta^{2})\int_{Y}|\nabla((y+\chi_{\delta})\cdot\xi)|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\mu\min(1,\delta^{2})\int_{Y}\Big\{|\nabla(\chi_{\delta}\cdot\xi)|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+2(\nabla\chi_{\delta})\xi\cdot\xi\Big\}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\mu\min(1,\delta^{2})|\xi|^{2}.

Thus, in view of (
2.16
),
it suffices to consider the case
\delta=0
.
To this end,
suppose there exists a sequence
\{A^{\ell}\}
of 1-periodic matrices
satisfying (
1.1
) and a sequence
\{\xi^{\ell}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|\xi^{\ell}|=1

such that
(\widehat{A^{\ell}_{0}}\xi^{\ell})\cdot\xi^{\ell}\to 0
,
as
\ell\to\infty
.
By passing to a subsequence we may assume
\xi^{\ell}\to\xi
.
It follows that
(\widehat{A^{\ell}_{0}}\xi)\cdot\xi\to 0
,
as
\ell\to\infty
, where
|\xi|=1
.
Let
\chi^{\ell}_{0}
denote the corrector for the matrix
A^{\ell}_{0}
.
Then

\int_{Y\cap\omega}|\nabla\big((\chi^{\ell}_{0}+y)\cdot\xi\big)|^{2}\,dy\to 0,

as
\ell\to\infty
.
Let
E_{\ell}
denote the average of
(\chi^{\ell}_{0}+y)\cdot\xi
over
Y\cap\omega
.
Then,
(\chi^{\ell}_{0}+y)\cdot\xi-E_{\ell}
converges to zero in
H^{1}(Y\cap\omega)
.
Since the sequence
\{\chi^{\ell}_{0}\cdot\xi-E_{\ell}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{\text{per}}(Y\cap\omega)
, by
passing to a subsequence,
we may assume it converges weakly in
H^{1}_{\text{per}}(Y\cap\omega)
.
This implies that
y\cdot\xi\in H^{1}_{\text{p}er}(Y\cap\omega)
.
However, since all connected components of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega
are bounded,
for each
1\leq j\leq d
,
there exists
y\in\partial Y\cap\omega
such that
y+e_{j}\in\partial Y\cap\omega
.
It follows that
(y+e_{j})\cdot\xi=y\cdot\xi
.
Consequently, we obtain
\xi=0
, which contradicts with the fact
|\xi|=1
.
The argument above shows that
(\widehat{A_{0}}\xi)\cdot\xi\geq\mu_{0}|\xi|^{2}

for some
\mu_{0}>0
depending only on
d
,
\mu
, and
\omega
(not directly on
A
).
∎

2.4
Flux correctors

Let

B_{\delta}=A_{\delta}+A_{\delta}\nabla\chi_{\delta}-\widehat{A_{\delta}}.

(2.21)

Write

(2.22)

where the repeated index is summed.

Lemma 2.9
.

There exist
\phi_{\delta,kij}\in H^{1}_{\text{per}}(Y)
, where

, such that

\int_{Y}\phi_{\delta,kij}\,dy=0

where
C

,
\mu
, and
\omega
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the case
\delta=1

[
26
,
9
]
.
Since
\int_{Y}b_{\delta,ij}\,dy=0
, there exists

such that

\Delta f_{ij}=b_{\delta,ij}\quad\text{ in }Y\quad\text{ and }\quad\|f_{ij}\|_{H^{2}_{\text{per}}(Y)}\leq C\|b_{\delta,ij}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|b_{\delta,ij}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}+\|b_{\delta,kj}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq C,

where we have used (
2.14
) for the last inequality.
∎

2.5
Small-scale Hölder estimates in Lipschitz domains

Let
B_{r}=B(0,r)
,

B_{r}^{+}=B_{r}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{d}>\psi(x^{\prime})\},\quad B_{r}^{-}=B_{r}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{d}<\psi(x^{\prime})\},

and

\Delta_{r}=B_{r}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}=\psi(x^{\prime})\}
,
where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with

\psi(0)=0
and
\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\leq M_{1}
.
Consider the weak solution of
\text{\rm div}(\widetilde{A}_{\delta}\nabla u)=0
in
B_{1}
,
where
\widetilde{A}_{\delta}(x)=A(x)
for
x\in B_{1}^{+}
and
\widetilde{A}_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{2}A(x)
for
x\in B_{1}^{-}
.
In the case
\delta=0
we also assume
\text{\rm div}(A\nabla u)=0
in
B_{1}^{-}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose
A
satisfies the ellipticity condition (
1.1
).
Let
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be a weak solution of
\text{\rm div}(\widetilde{A}_{\delta}\nabla u)=0
in
B_{1}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{\sigma}(B^{+}_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\left(\fint_{B_{1}^{+}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\delta\left(\fint_{B^{-}_{1}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\},

(2.25)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{\sigma}(B^{-}_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.26)

where
C>0
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
depend only on
d
,
\mu
, and
M_{1}
.

Proof.

We first note that if
0<\delta_{0}\leq\delta\leq 1
,
the estimates (
2.25
)-(
2.26
) follow directly from the De Giorgi - Nash estimates,
with
C
and
\sigma
depending on
d
,
\mu
, and
\delta_{0}
.
Next, consider the case
\delta=0
.
Since
\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right)_{+}=0
on
\Delta_{1}
,
it follows from the De Giorgi - Nash theory by a reflection argument that there exists
\rho>0
, depending only on
d
,
\mu
,
and
M_{1}
, such that

\|u\|_{C^{\rho}(B^{+}_{3/4})}\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{1}^{+}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.27)

Using Theorem 8.29 in
[
7
]
and (
2.27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{\rho/2}(B^{-}_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{C^{\rho}(\Delta_{3/4})}+C\left(\fint_{B_{1}^{-}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

To treat the case
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
, we use a perturbation argument.
Define

\Phi(r;u)=\inf_{k}\left\{\left(\fint_{B^{+}_{r}}|u-k|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\delta\left(\fint_{B^{-}_{r}}|u-k|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\}.

Let
v\in H^{1}(B_{R})
be a solution of
\text{\rm div}(A\nabla v)=0
in
B_{R}^{+}
and
B_{R}^{-}

such that

v=u
on
\partial B_{R}
,
\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\right)_{+}=0
on
\Delta_{R}
, and

v_{+}=v_{-}
on
\Delta_{R}
.
By the estimates for the case
\delta=0
,

\Phi(r;v)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\rho/2}\Phi(R;v)

(2.29)

for
0<r<R
.
Also, note that for any
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{R})
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}^{+}}A\nabla(u-v)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=-\delta^{2}\int_{B_{R}^{-}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx.

(2.30)

We now choose
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
in (
2.30
) to be an extension of
(u-v)|_{B_{R}^{+}}

such that

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}^{-})}\leq C\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}^{+})}.

This gives

\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}^{+})}\leq C\delta^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}^{-})}.

(2.31)

Since
\varphi=u-v
on
\partial B_{R}^{-}
and
\text{\rm div}(A\nabla(u-v))=0
in
B_{R}^{-}
,
by the energy estimate,

\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}^{-})}\leq C\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}^{-})}\leq C\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}^{+})}.

As a consequence, we have proved that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{R}}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\left(\fint_{B_{R}^{-}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\delta}{R}\left\{\left(\fint_{B^{+}_{2R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\delta\left(\fint_{B^{-}_{2R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\},

where we have used Caccioppoli’s inequality (
2.11
) for the last step.
By Poincaré’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{R}}|u-v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\delta\left\{\left(\fint_{B^{+}_{2R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\delta\left(\fint_{B^{-}_{2R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\Phi(\theta R;u)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(\theta R;v)+C_{\theta}\left(\fint_{B_{\theta R}}|u-v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\theta^{\rho/2}\Phi(R;v)+C_{\theta}\left(\fint_{B_{\theta R}}|u-v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\theta^{\rho/2}\Phi(R;u)+C_{\theta}\delta\left\{\left(\fint_{B^{+}_{2R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\delta\left(\fint_{B^{-}_{2R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\}.

Since
u-k
is also a solution, we obtain

\Phi(\theta R;u)\leq\left\{C_{0}\theta^{\rho/2}+C_{\theta}\delta\right\}\Phi(2R;u).

Choose
\theta\in(0,1/4)
so small that
C_{0}\theta^{\rho/2}\leq(1/4)
.
With
\theta
chosen, we then choose
\delta_{0}
so small that

C_{\theta}\delta_{0}\leq(1/4)
.
This yields that if
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
, then

\Phi(\theta R;u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Phi(2R;u).

It follows that for
0<r<R\leq 1
,

\Phi(r;u)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\sigma}\Phi(R;u),

(2.32)

where
\sigma
depends only on
d
,
\mu
and
M_{1}
.
The inequality (
2.32
), together with the interior Hölder estimates for
u
,
gives (
2.25
).
As in (
2.28
), since
\text{\rm div}(A\nabla u)=0
in
B_{1}^{-}
,
the estimate (
2.26
) follows from (
2.25
).
∎

Theorem 2.11
.

Suppose
A
satisfies (
1.1
).
Let
u=u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
be a weak solution of

\text{\rm div}(A_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}\nabla u)=0
in
B_{2r}=B(x_{0},2r)
,
where

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in

.
In this section we establish a sub-optimal convergence rate in

,
without the condition (

).
Let
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and

Suppose that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \text{\rm div}({A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})&=\text{\rm div}(\widehat{A_{\delta}}\nabla v_{\delta})&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}&=v_{\delta}&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where

(3.2)

Consider the function

(3.3)

where
\chi_{\delta}
is the corrector defined by (

\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=1&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{4d\varepsilon},\\
&\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=0&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\Sigma_{3d\varepsilon}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

The smoothing operator
S_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.3
) is defined by

S_{\varepsilon}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,

(3.5)

where
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)=\varepsilon^{-d}\varphi(y/\varepsilon)
and
\varphi
is a (fixed) function in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,1/2))
such that

\varphi\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,dx=1

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
S_{\varepsilon}
be the operator given by (
3.5

Let

Then

\displaystyle\|g^{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\|g^{\varepsilon}\nabla S_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\fint_{B(x,1/2)}|g|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
g^{\varepsilon}(x)=g(x/\varepsilon)
,
C
depends only on
d
and

\|f-S_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.7)

Proof.

See e.g.
[
19
, pp.37-38]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
0\leq\delta\leq 1
,
let
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
,
v_{\delta}
,

be given as above.
Then, for any

\Big|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\Big|\leq C\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big\{\|\nabla v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{4d\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{3d\varepsilon})}\Big\},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\omega
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega

Proof.

The proof is similar to that for the case

[

Let
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

Using

\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{A_{\delta}}\nabla v_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx,

(3.9)

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\widehat{A_{\delta}}-A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\right)\nabla v_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

(3.10)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla\chi_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\chi_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)\nabla S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\widehat{A_{\delta}}-A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\right)\left(\nabla v_{\delta}-S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta})\right)\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}+A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla\chi_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)-\widehat{A_{\delta}}\right)S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\chi_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)\nabla S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

By the Cauchy inequality and (

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v_{\delta}-S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{4d\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\{\|\nabla v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{4d\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{3d\varepsilon})}\big\}.

It follows from the Cauchy inequality, (
3.6
) and (

) that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\chi_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)\nabla S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\{\|\nabla v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{4d\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{3d\varepsilon})}\big\}.

Finally, to bound

A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}+A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla\chi_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)-\widehat{A_{\delta}}=B_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon),

where the matrix
B_{\delta}
is given by (
2.21
).
Since supp
(S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla v_{\delta}))\subset\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{2d\varepsilon}

it follows by Lemma

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}b_{\delta,ij}(x/\varepsilon)S_{\varepsilon}\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial v_{\delta}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\Big(\phi_{\delta,kij}(x/\varepsilon)\Big)S_{\varepsilon}\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial v_{\delta}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\delta,kij}(x/\varepsilon)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}S_{\varepsilon}\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial v_{\delta}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{i}}\,dx.

Thus, as in the case of

\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2d\varepsilon)}|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\right)^{12}\leq C\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\varepsilon\omega}|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\right)^{12},

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0})|

where we have used (
5.11
) for the last inequality.

6
Estimates of Green’s functions

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, satisfying (
1.5
).
For
0<\delta\leq 1
, let
G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,y)
denote the Green’s function for the
operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}=-\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla)
in
\Omega
, where
A
satisfies (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).
No smoothness condition on
A
is needed.
The goal of this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
d\geq 3
and
0<\delta\leq 1
.
Then, for
x,y\in\Omega
and
x\neq y
,

|G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\quad\text{ if }\ x,y\in\Omega^{\varepsilon}\text{ or }|x-y|\geq 4d\varepsilon,

(6.1)

where

C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\omega
,
\kappa
, and the Lipschitz
character of
\Omega
.

We begin with some energy estimates.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
d\geq 3
.
Let
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of

-\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})=f
in
\Omega
, where
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
support in
B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega
, where
y_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}

and
0<r<\text{\rm diam}(\Omega)
.
Then

\|[\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}]^{2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Cr\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(6.2)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\omega
,
\kappa
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Then

\int_{\Omega}[\Lambda_{\delta}(x/\varepsilon)]^{2}A(x/\varepsilon)\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega}f\cdot\varphi\,dx.

(6.3)

By letting
\varphi=u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in (
6.3
) and using

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}\leq Cr\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Cr\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
p=\frac{2d}{d-2}
,
we obtain

\delta^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Cr\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(6.4)

Next, in (
6.3
), we let
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be an extension of
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}
such that

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}

(6.5)

(see Lemma
2.1
).
It follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\varepsilon})}+Cr\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Cr\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})},

where we have used (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) for the last inequality.
This, together with (
6.4
), gives (
6.2
).
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
d\geq 3
. Let
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of

-\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})=\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}f

in
\Omega
, where
f\in L^{2}(B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega)
.
Then

\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Cr\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(6.6)

where

C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\omega
,
\kappa
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

By using the test function
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and Cauchy inequality, we obtain

\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}.

(6.7)

Note that

\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}\leq Cr\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Cr\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used Hölder’s inequality as well as the Sobolev type inequality,

\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(6.8)

with
p=\frac{2d}{d-2}
,
for
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
This, together with (
6.7
), gives (
6.6
).
Finally, to see (
6.8
, we let
\widetilde{\varphi}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be an extension
of
\varphi|_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}
such that

\|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\delta\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\delta\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used the usual Sobolev inequality for the second inequality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Fix
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
and let
r=|x_{0}-y_{0}|/2
.
We first consider the case
r\geq 2d\varepsilon
.
Let
f\in L^{2}(B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega)
and

u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,y)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,y)f(y)\,dy.

Then
-\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})=f
in
\Omega
and
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
It follows from Lemma
6.2
that

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}\leq Cr\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let
\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be an extension of
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}
such that

\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}
.
By (
5.6
),

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B(x_{0},r))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\cap B(x_{0},2r))}\leq Cr\|\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Cr\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Cr^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
p=\frac{2d}{d-2}
and we have used a Sobolev inequality for
\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
.
Since
\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta})=0
in
B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega
,

u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and
r>2d\varepsilon
,
it follows from (
5.13
) that

|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0})|\leq C\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq Cr^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}.

By duality this implies that

\left(\int_{B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},y)|^{2}\,dy\right)^{1/2}\leq Cr^{2-\frac{d}{2}}.

Since
\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon*}_{\delta}\nabla_{y}G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},\cdot))=0
in
B(y_{0},r)

and
G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},\cdot)=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we obtain

|G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},y_{0})|\leq C\left(\fint_{B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},y)|^{2}\,dy\right)^{1/2}\leq Cr^{2-d}.

We now consider the case where
r=(1/2)|x_{0}-y_{0}|<2d\varepsilon
.
Let
g\in L^{2}(B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega)
and

w_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\int_{\Omega}G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,y)\Lambda_{\delta}(y/\varepsilon)g(y)\,dy.

Then
-\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta})=\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\,g
in
\Omega
and

w_{\varepsilon,\delta}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
By Lemma
6.3
,

\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Cr\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Cr\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Cr\|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Cr^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used (
6.8
) for the second inequality.
Hence, by Theorem
2.11
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(x_{0})|w_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By duality we obtain

\left(\fint_{B(y_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(x_{0})G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},y)\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(y)|^{2}\,dy\right)^{1/2}\leq Cr^{2-d},

which, by Theorem
2.11
again, leads to

\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(x_{0})\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(y_{0})G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq Cr^{2-d},

since
\text{\rm div}(A^{\varepsilon*}_{\delta}\nabla G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},\cdot))=0
in
\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\}
and
G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{0},\cdot)=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
∎

7

Throughout this section we assume that

),

)-(

We use the scale-invariant

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of (
1.9
)
with

where

and

depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\omega
,
\kappa
,
\alpha
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega

Proof.

Let
v
be the solution of the homogenized problem:

\text{\rm div}(\widehat{A_{\delta}}\nabla v)=0
in
\Omega
and

v=f
on
\partial\Omega
.
Recall that for
0<t<1

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}+t\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Sigma_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{1/2}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.
Let
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}
be defined by (
3.3
), with
v
in the place of

.
The cut-off function
\eta_{\varepsilon}
in (

\Big|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\Big|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}

in (

Using the ellipticity condition (

\delta^{2}\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

(7.4)

By Lemma

, there exists
\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

Let

in (

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{2})}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(D_{2})}\big\},

Defining quantum divergences via convex optimization
Hamza Fawzi, Omar Fawzi

Abstract
We introduce a new quantum Rényi divergence D α # D^{\#}_{\alpha} for α ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) \alpha\in(1,\infty) defined in terms of a convex optimization program. This divergence has several desirable computational and operational properties such as an efficient semidefinite programming representation for states and channels, and a chain rule property. An important property of this new divergence is that its regularization is equal to the sandwiched (also known as the minimal) quantum Rényi divergence. This allows us to prove several results. First, we use it to get a converging hierarchy of upper bounds on the regularized sandwiched α \alpha -Rényi divergence between quantum channels for α > 1 \alpha>1 . Second it allows us to prove a chain rule property for the sandwiched α \alpha -Rényi divergence for α > 1 \alpha>1 which we use to characterize the strong converse exponent for channel discrimination. Finally it allows us to get improved bounds on quantum channel capacities.

Introduction

Given nonnegative vectors
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}
, the
\alpha
-Rényi divergence is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(P\|Q)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\sum_{x\in\Sigma}P(x)^{\alpha}Q(x)^{1-\alpha}&\text{if }P\ll Q\\
\infty&\text{else}.\end{array}\right.

for
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
. Here
P\ll Q
means that for any
x\in\Sigma
,
Q(x)=0
implies that
P(x)=0
and the
\log
is taken to be base
2
. This definition has found many applications in information theory and beyond, we refer to the survey paper
[
37
]
for more general definitions and properties of this quantity. To generalize this notion to quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
which are now positive semidefinite operators on
\mathbb{C}^{\Sigma}
the interpretation of the multiplication appearing in the definition matters and multiple definitions exist. Such definitions are systematically studied in
[
35

\rho
and
\sigma

\mathbb{C}^{\Sigma}
, provided
\rho\ll\sigma
(i.e., the support of
\rho
is contained in the support of

), the geometric and sandwiched divergences

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{\alpha}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)\ ,

(4)

were respectively defined by
[
26
]
and by
[

,

]
.
The inverses here should be understood as generalized inverses i.e., the inverse on the support. When
\rho\ll\sigma
is not satisfied, both quantities are set to

commute, both definitions agree and as

Another natural definition is the measured Rényi divergence, which is obtained by performing the same measurement on

\displaystyle D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\{|v_{x}\rangle\}_{x\in\Sigma}}D_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{x}|v_{x}\rangle\!\langle v_{x}|\rho|v_{x}\rangle\!\langle v_{x}|\;\Big\|\;\sum_{x}|v_{x}\rangle\!\langle v_{x}|\sigma|v_{x}\rangle\!\langle v_{x}|\right)\ ,

Contributions

In this paper, we put forward another way of defining quantum Rényi divergences through a convex optimization program. Even if such divergences may not have operational interpretations in terms of some information processing task, we demonstrate in this paper that they can nonetheless be useful tools for proofs and for computations.
Given
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, we define the
\#
Rényi divergence of order
\alpha
between two positive semidefinite operators
\rho,\sigma
as

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\ ,

\displaystyle Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{A\geq 0}\mathrm{tr}\,(A)\quad\text{ s.t. }\quad\rho\leq\sigma\#_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}A\ .

(6)

Here
\sigma\#_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}A
denotes the
\frac{1}{\alpha}
-geometric mean of
\sigma
and
A
. We recall the definitions and properties of the matrix geometric mean in Section
2
below. Using the joint concavity of the matrix geometric mean, the optimization program in (
6
) is convex and for rational values of
\alpha
it can be expressed as a semidefinite program
[
33
,
14
]
.
We show the following properties:

•

We prove in Section
3
that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
satisfies the data processing inequality and it matches with
D_{\alpha}
for commuting operators. In addition, it is subadditive under tensor product and when regularized, it is equal to the sandwiched divergence i.e., we have (Proposition
3.4
)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\;\;=\;\;\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma).

•

We establish in Section
4
that the extension of
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
to channels has an expression as a convex optimization program (similar to the one for states (
6
)) and satisfies subadditivity under tensor product as well as a chain rule property. Furthermore, when regularized, it gives the regularized sandwiched divergence between channels.

We then give some applications of
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
in Section
5
.

•

We show that, for
\alpha>1
, the regularized sandwiched
\alpha
-Rényi divergence between quantum channels can be computed to arbitrary precision in finite time (Theorem
5.1
).

•

We prove a new chain rule property for
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha>1
(Corollary
5.2
). In turn, the new chain rule property allows us to characterize the strong converse exponent for channel discrimination and show that in this regime adaptive strategies do not offer an advantage over nonadaptive strategies (Section
5.2.1
).

•

We give improved bounds on amortized entanglement measures, which can be used for example to bound the quantum capacity of channels with free two-way classical communication (Section
5.2.2
). We restrict our focus in this paper to the resource of entanglement, but we expect the same techniques to be applicable in many other resource-theoretic frameworks
[
8
]
.

In addition to these applications, we mention that a close variant of this divergence is introduced in
[
7
]
to bound conditional entropies for quantum correlations.

Remark 1.1

(Convention for unnormalized
\rho
)
.

We note that divergences are most interesting when the first argument is normalized, i.e.,
\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)=1
but it is convenient to keep the definition general. To define it for a general positive semidefinite operator
\rho
, we use a convention which is not standard. We choose for this work to use the exact same expression, e.g., (
1
) for the classical case, (
4
) for the sandwiched Rényi divergence etc… even if
\rho
is not normalized. With this convention,
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\frac{\rho}{\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)}\|\sigma)+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
, which is slightly different from the more standard choice made for example in
[
35
]
where the correction term for normalization is simply
\log\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
. Note however that the difference between these variants only depends on
\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
and
\alpha
, and thus the two variants basically have the same properties even when
\rho
is not normalized. In particular, we will be using the property that the regularized measured divergence is equal to the sandwiched divergence, a property which clearly holds equally well for both conventions.

Properties for positive semidefinite operators

In this section we state and prove basic properties for the new quantity
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
.

Proposition 3.1

(First properties)
.

The feasible set of (
6
) is nonempty iff
\rho\ll\sigma
. Furthermore, if
\rho\ll\sigma
the infimum in (
6
) is attained at some
A\ll\sigma
, more precisely, at some
A\leq\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\|_{\infty}^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)P
where
P
is the projector on
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
and the inverses are generalized inverses.

Proof.

If (
6
) is feasible then
\rho\leq\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A\ll\sigma
by Proposition
2.2
. Conversely, assume
\rho\ll\sigma
then
A=\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\|_{\infty}^{\alpha-1}\rho
is feasible. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\left\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right\|_{\infty}^{\alpha-1}\left(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right)\ .

Using the operator monotonicity of
t\mapsto t^{1/\alpha}
, we get

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\sigma^{1/2}\left(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right)^{1/\alpha}\sigma^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A\ .

Note that this shows the program (
6
) always has a trivial achievable value of
\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\|^{\alpha-1}_{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
.

Let
P
be a projector on
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
, and let
A
be a feasible point of (
6
). Then
PAP
is also feasible and satisfies
\mathrm{tr}\,(PAP)\leq\mathrm{tr}\,(A)
. Indeed, by the transformer inequality we have

\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}PAP\geq P(\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A)P\geq P\rho P=\rho\ .

Thus this means we can restrict
A
to satisfy
A\ll\sigma
. If we in addition assume that
A
achieves a value for the objective function in (
6
) that is at least as good as the trivial value of
\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\|^{\alpha-1}_{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
, we may assume that
\mathrm{tr}\,(A)\leq\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\|^{\alpha-1}_{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
which implies that
A\leq\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\|^{\alpha-1}_{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)P
.

We can thus write
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\inf\left\{\mathrm{tr}\,(A):\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A\geq\rho\text{ and }A\leq\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\|^{\alpha-1}_{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)P\right\}
. The feasible set is closed (by Proposition
2.2
) and bounded, hence the infimum is attained.

We now show that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
satisfies the main properties of a Rényi divergence: it satisfies the data-processing inequality and for commuting states, it matches with the classical Rényi divergence.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha>1
. The function
(\rho,\sigma)\mapsto Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)
is jointly convex. Furthermore
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
is monotone under trace-preserving positive maps. More precisely, let
\rho
and
\sigma
be positive semidefinite operators on the Hilbert space
X
. Then if
\mathcal{N}
is a positive and trace-preserving map from
\mathscr{L}(X)
to
\mathscr{L}(Y)
then

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\ .

In addition, if
\rho
and
\sigma
commute, then
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
.

Proof.

Joint convexity follows directly from the joint concavity property of the matrix geometric mean (
7
). In fact, for any positive semidefinite operators
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},A_{0},A_{1}
satisfying
\rho_{0}\leq\sigma_{0}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{0}
and
\rho_{1}\leq\sigma_{1}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{1}
, we have for
\lambda\in[0,1]

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\rho_{0}+\lambda\rho_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\sigma_{0}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{0}+\lambda\sigma_{1}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{1}

\displaystyle=((1-\lambda)\sigma_{0})\#_{1/\alpha}((1-\lambda)A_{0})+(\lambda\sigma_{1})\#_{1/\alpha}(\lambda A_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\left((1-\lambda)\sigma_{0}+\lambda\sigma_{1}\right)\#_{1/\alpha}\left((1-\lambda)A_{0}+\lambda A_{1}\right)\ .

Taking the minimum over
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
, we obtain the desired result.

The data-processing inequality with positive trace-preserving maps follows immediately from the monotonicity property of
\#_{1/\alpha}
in Proposition
2.1
. In fact, assuming that
\rho\ll\sigma
(otherwise the statement clearly holds) let
A
be an optimal point for
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
so that
\rho\leq\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}(\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}(\sigma)\#_{1/\alpha}\mathcal{N}(A)\ ,

As such
\mathcal{N}(A)
is feasible for
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))
with
\mathrm{tr}\,(\mathcal{N}(A))=\mathrm{tr}\,(A)
, this proves that
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
.

To analyze the commutative case, consider
\rho
and
\sigma
commuting operators. It suffices to assume that
\rho\ll\sigma
in what follows. To show that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
it suffices to take
A=\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}
which commutes with
\rho
and
\sigma
. Then
\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A=\sigma^{1-1/\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha})^{1/\alpha}=\rho
.
To prove
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\geq D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
consider a common eigenbasis
|1\rangle,\dots,|d\rangle
for
\rho
and
\sigma
and consider the map
\mathcal{M}(W)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|i\rangle\!\langle i|W|i\rangle\!\langle i|
. Note that
\mathcal{M}
is completely positive and trace-preserving and we have
\mathcal{M}(\rho)=\rho
and
\mathcal{M}(\sigma)=\sigma
. Given an optimal choice of
A
in the program (
6
) for
\rho
and
\sigma
, we can write as before

\displaystyle\rho=\mathcal{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}(\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}(\sigma)\#_{1/\alpha}\mathcal{M}(A)

\displaystyle=\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}\mathcal{M}(A)\ .

Noting that
\mathrm{tr}\,(\mathcal{M}(A))=\mathrm{tr}\,(A)
, we have constructed another optimal solution where the matrix
A
commutes with
\rho
and
\sigma
. In this case
\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A=\sigma^{1-1/\alpha}A^{1/\alpha}
. Thus, the condition
\rho\leq\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A
translates to
\sigma^{1/\alpha-1}\rho\leq A^{1/\alpha}
. As all the matrices are diagonal in the same basis, we can take both sides of this inequality to the power
\alpha
and get
\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}\leq A
.
Taking the trace, we get that
D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
.
∎

Now we turn to properties of the divergence for tensor products and the relation to other quantum Rényi divergences.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
. The quantity
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
is subadditive under tensor products, i.e., for
\rho_{1},\sigma_{1}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
\rho_{2},\sigma_{2}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})\ .

(12)

In addition, for
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
it can be related to the measured Rényi divergence as follows:

\displaystyle D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|\ .

(13)

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})

(14)

and

\displaystyle D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\ .

(15)

Furthermore
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\rightarrow D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)
when
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

In order to show subadditivity, we may assume
\rho_{1}\ll\sigma_{1}
and
\rho_{2}\ll\sigma_{2}
(the statement clearly holds otherwise). For
b\in\{1,2\}
, let
A_{b}
be a feasible solution for the program (
6
) for
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{b}\|\sigma_{b})
. Then define
A_{12}=A_{1}\otimes A_{2}
. Then, using the tensor product property in Proposition
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})\#_{1/\alpha}A_{12}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{1}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{1})\otimes(\sigma_{2}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\ .

In addition,
\mathrm{tr}\,(A_{12})=\mathrm{tr}\,(A_{1})\mathrm{tr}\,(A_{2})
and we thus get
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})\leq Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})
which proves subadditivity.

Next, to relate the different divergences, note that all of these divergences are finite if and only if
\rho\ll\sigma
. So we focus on the case
\rho\ll\sigma
. To show (
13
), it follows from the data-processing inequality that for any choice of orthonormal basis
\{|v_{x}\rangle\}_{x}
, the measurement map
\mathcal{M}(W)=\sum_{x}|v_{x}\rangle\!\langle v_{x}|W|v_{x}\rangle\!\langle v_{x}|
satisfies
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
. But as
\mathcal{M}(\rho)
and
\mathcal{M}(\sigma)
commute,
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))=D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))
. Taking the supremum over measurements
\mathcal{M}
, we have
D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
.

In the other direction, consider the pinching map
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
defined by
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(W)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)}\Pi_{\lambda}W\Pi_{\lambda}
, where
\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)
denotes the set of eigenvalues of
\sigma
and
\Pi_{\lambda}
is the projector onto the eigenspace of
\lambda
. Consider an optimal solution
A
of (
6
) for the states
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)
and
\sigma
. Then we have
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)\leq\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A
. Using the pinching inequality
\rho\leq|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)
(see e.g.,
[
17
]
or
[
35
, Chapter 2]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\rho\leq|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|\cdot\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A

\displaystyle=\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}(|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|^{\alpha}A)\ .

As such
|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|^{\alpha}A
is feasible for the optimization program (
6
) for
\rho
and
\sigma
and thus
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|^{\alpha}Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)\|\sigma)
. But note that
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\sigma)=\sigma
and it commutes with
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)
, so using Proposition
3.2
, we have
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)\|\sigma)=D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\rho)\|\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\sigma))\leq D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
. Putting everything together, we get

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma)|\ .

As a result, we have that for any integer
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq\frac{1}{n}D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})+\frac{1}{n}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma^{\otimes n})|.

But it is well-known that
|\mathrm{spec}(\sigma^{\otimes n})|\leq(n+1)^{\dim\mathcal{H}-1}
. This shows that the regularization of both
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
and
D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}
give the same value. But the regularization of the measured Rényi divergence is known to be equal to
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}

[
27
]
(see also
[
35
, Theorem 4.1]
). The inequality
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
then follows directly because, by subadditivity of
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
we have

\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma).

We now show that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
.
Recall that
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\sigma\#_{\alpha}\rho\right)
, where, for
\rho\ll\sigma
,
\sigma\#_{\alpha}\rho
is defined by

\sigma\#_{\alpha}\rho=\sigma^{1/2}\left(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right)^{\alpha}\sigma^{1/2}\ ,

where generalized inverses are used if
\sigma
is not invertible.
If we choose
A=\sigma\#_{\alpha}\rho
, it is immediate to verify that

\displaystyle\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A=\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}(\sigma\#_{\alpha}\rho)=\rho

which means that
A
is feasible for (
6
), and so
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
. We note that for
\alpha\in(1,2]
this inequality actually follows immediately from the fact that
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
is the maximal Rényi divergence satisfying the data-processing inequality
[
26
]
. As illustrated in Figure
1
, there can be a large gap between
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
and
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
.

Finally we prove that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\rightarrow D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)
when
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
. We saw in Proposition
3.1
that
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\left\|\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right\|_{\infty}^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)=2^{D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)(\alpha-1)}\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
, which implies that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)
. On the other hand it is known (see e.g.,
[
29
, Proposition 4]
) that
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\rightarrow D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)
when
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
thus we get the desired result.

∎

We conclude this section by establishing additional useful properties of
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}
.

Proposition 3.5

(Additional properties for
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}
)
.

For any
\alpha>1
, the following hold.
(a) Isometric invariance: if
V
is an isometry, i.e.,
V^{*}V=I
then
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(V\rho V^{*}\|V\sigma V^{*})=Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
. (b) Positive homogeneity:
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\lambda\rho\|\lambda\sigma)=\lambda Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
for
\lambda\geq 0
. (c) Block additivity:
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})=Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})
provided
\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{1}+\sigma_{1})\perp\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{2}+\sigma_{2})
.

Proof.

(a) The inequality
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(V\rho V^{*}\|V\sigma V^{*})\leq Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
follows from the data-processing inequality. For the other inequality if
A
is an optimal point for
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(V\rho V^{*}\|V\sigma V^{*})
, using the transformer inequality,
V^{*}AV
is feasible point for the program defining
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
. But as
VV^{*}\leq I
, we have
\mathrm{tr}\,(V^{*}AV)=\mathrm{tr}\,(AVV^{*})\leq\mathrm{tr}\,(A)
, which proves the desired result.

(b) Immediate to verify from the definition of
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
.

(c) We use the fact that
(A_{1}+A_{2})\#_{1/\alpha}(B_{1}+B_{2})=(A_{1}\#_{1/\alpha}B_{1})+(A_{2}\#_{1/\alpha}B_{2})
established in Section
2
. Let
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
be projectors on
\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{1}+\sigma_{1})
and
\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{2}+\sigma_{2})
respectively. For the inequality
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})\leq Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})
, consider
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
two optimal points for
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})
and
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})
, respectively. Using the transformer inequality and the orthogonality condition, note that
P_{1}A_{1}P_{1}
and
P_{2}A_{2}P_{2}
are also optimal points for
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})
and
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})
. Using the property of the geometric mean for orthogonal sums of operators, we get that
P_{1}A_{1}P_{1}+P_{2}A_{2}P_{2}
is feasible for the program defining
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})
.

For the reverse inequality. Let
A
be an optimal solution in the definition of
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})
so that
(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})\#_{1/\alpha}A\geq\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}
.
Let
\pi(A)=P_{1}AP_{1}+P_{2}AP_{2}
be the projection map on “block-diagonal” operators. Then, letting
\sigma=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
and
\rho=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}
we have

\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}\pi(A)=\pi(\sigma)\#_{1/\alpha}\pi(A)\geq\pi(\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A)\geq\pi(\rho)=\rho.

Since
\mathrm{tr}\,\pi(A)=\mathrm{tr}\,A
, it follows that
\pi(A)
is also an optimal solution in the definition of
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})
. Call
A_{1}=P_{1}AP_{1}
and
A_{2}=P_{2}AP_{2}
. By the previous equation and the block additivity of the geometric mean, we see that
\sigma_{1}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{1}\geq\rho_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}\#_{1/\alpha_{2}}A_{2}\geq\rho_{2}
. This implies that
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})\leq\mathrm{tr}\,A_{1}
and
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})\leq\mathrm{tr}\,A_{2}
, which in turn implies
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2})\leq\mathrm{tr}\,\pi(A)=Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
as desired.
∎

An immediate consequence of the proposition above is that if
|\psi\rangle
is a unit normed vector in

then
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\otimes|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\|\sigma\otimes|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|)=Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

V|\phi\rangle=|\phi\rangle\otimes|\psi\rangle

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite set,
p(x)\geq 0
,
\rho(x)
and
\sigma(x)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
for all
x\in\Sigma
. Then,
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}
has the direct-sum property for classical-quantum states:

\displaystyle Q^{\#}_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{x\in\Sigma}p(x)|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes\rho(x)\Big\|\sum_{x\in\Sigma}p(x)|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes\sigma(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Sigma}p(x)Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho(x)\|\sigma(x))\ .

4
Properties for positive maps

The notion of divergence between states can naturally be extended to a divergence between channels by maximizing over the input states. Here we consider the stabilized version where a reference system that is unaffected by the channels is allowed.
Let
X,X^{\prime},Y
be Hilbert spaces with
\dim X=\dim X^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
be completely positive maps from
\mathscr{L}(X^{\prime})
to
\mathscr{L}(Y)
. It will be convenient to write the definition of the channel divergence in terms of the Choi states of the channels. For this, we define
\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}
as an unnormalized maximally entangled state of the form
\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}=\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}|x\rangle\!\langle x^{\prime}|_{X}\otimes|x\rangle\!\langle x^{\prime}|_{X^{\prime}}
, where
\{|x\rangle\}_{x}
labels a fixed basis of
X
and
X^{\prime}
. Then we let

J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}=(\mathcal{I}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\Phi_{XX^{\prime}})
and
J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}=(\mathcal{I}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\Phi_{XX^{\prime}})
be the Choi matrices of these channels. Here
\mathcal{I}_{X}
denotes the identity map on
\mathscr{L}(X)
. Observe that for any density operator
\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)
,
\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}=(\mathcal{I}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\Omega_{XX^{\prime}})
where
\Omega_{XX^{\prime}}
is the pure state
\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For any divergence
\mathbf{D}
, the corresponding channel divergence is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M}):=\sup_{\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X)}\mathbf{D}(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{XY}^{\mathcal{N}}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})\ .

(16)

We refer to
[
25
]
for a more detailed discussion of this definition. For
\mathbf{D}=D^{\#}_{\alpha}
, our first result is an expression for the channel divergence in terms of a convex optimization program.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
and completely positive maps
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, we can write

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

(17)

\displaystyle Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\inf_{A_{XY}\geq 0}\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y}(A_{XY})\|_{\infty}\quad\textup{ s.t. }\quad J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\leq J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{XY}\ ,

(18)

where
\|.\|_{\infty}
denotes the operator norm.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that the constraint in (
18
) is jointly convex in
J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}
and
J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}
.

Proof.

First, if
J_{XY}^{\mathcal{N}}\ll J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}}
is not satisfied, then both quantities are
\infty
: we can take
\omega_{X}=\frac{I}{\dim X}
in (
16
) and have
D^{\#}_{\alpha}({\mathcal{N}}\|{\mathcal{M}})=\infty
, and on the other hand the optimization program in (
18
) is infeasible. So we may assume that
J_{XY}^{\mathcal{N}}\ll J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}}
in what follows.
We have to show that

\sup_{\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X)}Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{XY}^{\mathcal{N}}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})\;=\;\min_{A_{XY}\geq 0}\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y}(A_{XY})\|_{\infty}\;\;\text{ s.t. }\;\;J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\leq J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{XY}.

(19)

We will start by showing that, if we strengthen the condition
\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X)
with
\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X),\omega_{X}>0
in the left-hand side, equality holds. We will later show that the condition
\omega_{X}>0
leads to the same quantity. With this additional condition, the left-hand side of (
19
) is

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega_{X}>0\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\min_{B_{XY}\geq 0}\mathrm{tr}\,(B_{XY})

(20)

\displaystyle\quad\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})\#_{1/\alpha}B_{XY}\ .

(21)

Now using the fact that
\omega_{X}
is invertible together with the transformer inequality, we get

\displaystyle(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})\#_{1/\alpha}B_{XY}

\displaystyle=\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\#_{1/\alpha}(\omega_{X}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{XY}\omega_{X}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\right)\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}

Thus, the constraint in (
21
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\leq J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\#_{1/\alpha}(\omega_{X}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{XY}\omega_{X}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\ .

Thus, by performing a change of variable
A_{XY}=\omega_{X}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{XY}\omega_{X}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, the program in (
20
) becomes

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega_{X}>0\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\min_{A_{XY}\geq 0}\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\leq J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{XY}\ .

Now using Sion’s minmax theorem, observe that we can exchange the minimization and the maximization. In fact, the objective function is linear in both
\omega_{X}
and in
A_{XY}
, and the set of invertible density operators is convex. In addition, as we assumed that
J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\ll J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}
, using Proposition
3.1
, we may restrict the set of
A_{XY}
we optimize over to be convex and compact. To conclude, it suffices to observe that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega_{X}>0\end{subarray}}\mathrm{tr}\,(\omega_{X}A_{XY})=\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y}A_{XY}\|_{\infty}
.

Since replacing the condition
\omega_{X}\geq 0
by
\omega_{X}>0
can only decrease the LHS of (
19
), we have shown the direction
\geq
of (
19
). It thus remains to show the direction
\leq
. Take an optimal feasible solution
A_{XY}
of (
18
) and let us write
\lambda
for its value. Now consider an
\omega_{X}\in\mathscr{D}(X)
and define
A_{XY}^{\omega}=\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. By construction
\mathrm{tr}\,(A^{\omega}_{XY})\leq\lambda
. In addition, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\#_{1/\alpha}A_{XY}\right)\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})\#_{1/\alpha}A^{\omega}_{XY}\ ,

where we used the transformer inequality. As such
A_{XY}^{\omega}
is feasible for the defining optimization program for
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})
, and this implies
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq\lambda
. Taking the supremum over
\omega_{X}
completes the proof.
∎

An immediate corollary is that the channel divergence is subadditive.

Corollary 4.3
.

For any
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
and completely positive maps
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\|\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{1}\|\mathcal{M}_{1})+D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{2}\|\mathcal{M}_{2})

Proof.

Let
A^{1}_{X_{1}Y_{1}}
be a feasible solution for the program (
18
) for the channels
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
A^{2}_{X_{2}Y_{2}}
for
\mathcal{N}_{2}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
. Then using the fact that
J^{\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}}=J^{\mathcal{N}_{1}}\otimes J^{\mathcal{N}_{2}}
and the tensor product property of the mean (Proposition
2.2
), we have that
A^{12}_{X_{1}X_{2}Y_{1}Y_{2}}=A^{1}_{X_{1}Y_{1}}\otimes A^{2}_{X_{2}Y_{2}}
is feasible for
\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y_{1}Y_{2}}A^{12}_{X_{1}X_{2}Y_{1}Y_{2}}\|_{\infty}=\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y_{1}}A^{1}_{X_{1}Y_{1}}\|_{\infty}\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y_{2}}A^{2}_{X_{2}Y_{2}}\|_{\infty}
.
∎

Next, we prove that as for states, the regularized channel divergence is equal to the regularized sandwiched Rényi divergence.
Note that unlike for states, the sandwiched Rényi divergence of channels is not additive in general, see
[
13
]
for an example.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
be completely positive maps from
\mathscr{L}(X)
to
\mathscr{L}(Y)
. For any
n\geq 1
and
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})-\frac{1}{n}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(d^{2}+d)\log(n+d)\leq\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})\leq\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})\ ,

where
d=\dim X\dim Y
.

Proof.

The second inequality follows immediately from the fact that
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}
; see Proposition
3.4
.

For the other direction, the channels
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
are covariant with respect to the representation of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. In fact, for
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, if we denote
P_{X}(\pi)
the operator on the space
X^{\otimes n}
that permutes the
n
tensor factors according to
\pi
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(P_{X}(\pi)\ .\ P_{X}(\pi)^{*})=P_{Y}(\pi)\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(\ .\ )P_{Y}(\pi)^{*}\ ,

and similarly the same relation holds for
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
. Using the definition (
16
), we have

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\omega_{X^{n}}\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\otimes n})}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J_{XY}^{\mathcal{N}})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}})\ .

Using
[
25
, Proposition II.4]
for the divergence
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
(which satisfies the data-processing inequality as shown in Proposition
3.2
), we may restrict the optimization to permutation-invariant states and get

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega_{X^{n}}\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\otimes n})\\
[P_{X}(\pi),\omega_{X^{n}}]=0\>\forall\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\end{subarray}}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J_{XY}^{\mathcal{N}})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}})\ .

Now consider such a permutation-invariant
\omega_{X^{n}}
and we use the relation to the measured Rényi divergence in (
13
):

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log|\mathrm{spec}(\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}})|\ .

Now note that if
\omega_{X^{n}}
is permutation-invariant, then so is the operator
\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
on
(X\otimes Y)^{\otimes n}
. As such, using Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle|\mathrm{spec}(\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY})^{\otimes n}\omega_{X^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}})|\leq(n+1)^{d}(n+d)^{d^{2}}\ ,

where
d:=\dim X\dim Y
. Taking the supremum over all
\omega_{X^{n}}
, we get

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\leq D^{\mathbb{M}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(d^{2}+d)\log(n+d)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(d^{2}+d)\log(n+d)\ .

This gives the desired result.
∎

The channel divergence satisfies a chain rule property for any
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, as the one satisfied for the geometric divergence
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}

[
12
]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\rho_{RX^{\prime}},\sigma_{RX^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{P}(R\otimes X^{\prime})
,
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
be completely positive maps from
\mathscr{L}(X^{\prime})
to
\mathscr{L}(Y)
. Then

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\rho_{RX^{\prime}})\|(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})+D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{RX^{\prime}}\|\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})\ .

Proof.

Let
A_{XY}
be an optimal solution for (
18
) for the maps
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
and
\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}}
be an optimal solution for (
6
) for
\rho_{RX^{\prime}}
and
\sigma_{RX^{\prime}}
. Then note that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\rho_{RX^{\prime}})=\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\otimes\rho_{RX^{\prime}}|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle\quad(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})=\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\otimes\sigma_{RX^{\prime}}|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle\ .

Combining the properties of
A_{XY}
and
\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}}
using Proposition
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\otimes\rho_{RX^{\prime}}\leq(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\otimes\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})\#_{1/\alpha}(A_{XY}\otimes\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}})\ .

Then using the transformer inequality, we have

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|J^{\mathcal{N}}_{XY}\otimes\rho_{RX^{\prime}}|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|\big((J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\otimes\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})\#_{1/\alpha}(A_{XY}\otimes\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}})\big)|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\big(\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|(J^{\mathcal{M}}_{XY}\otimes\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle\big)\#_{1/\alpha}\big(\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|(A_{XY}\otimes\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}})|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle\big)\ .

To conclude it suffices to compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,\big(\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|(A_{XY}\otimes\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}})|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle\big)

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y}(A_{XY})\otimes\mathrm{tr}\,_{R}(\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}})|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y}A_{XY}\|_{\infty}\langle\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}|I_{X}\otimes\mathrm{tr}\,_{R}(\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}})|\Phi_{XX^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y}A_{XY}\|_{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\,(\bar{A}_{RX^{\prime}})\ ,

which after taking the logarithm establishes the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Note that the chain rule can be seen as a generalization of the data processing inequality. In fact, we can take the
R
system to be trivial and if the maps are the same
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}
and in addition trace-preserving, then
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=0
.

5
Applications

In this section we present some example applications of the newly introduced divergences. Most of these applications are related to the regularized sandwiched divergence between channels. For
\alpha>1
, we denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})\ .

(22)

We note that as the sequence
\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})
is superadditive, using Fekete’s lemma, the limit exists and can be replaced by a supremum over
n
. Regularized entropic quantities appear extensively in quantum information theory but it is unclear how to compute them (or even whether they are computable to start with) as we do not have control on the convergence speed in the regularization. Using
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
, one can quantify the convergence speed explicitly for
\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}
and thus show that this quantity is computable.

5.1
Converging hierarchy of upper bounds on the regularized divergence of channels

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
and
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
be completely positive maps from
\mathscr{L}(X)
to
\mathscr{L}(Y)
. Then for any
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})-\frac{1}{m}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(d^{2}+d)\log(m+d)\leq\>\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\>\leq\frac{1}{m}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})\ ,

where
d=\dim X\dim Y
. We can also write

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})-\frac{1}{m}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})\leq\frac{1}{m}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(d^{2}+d)\log(m+d)\ .

(23)

Proof.

The lower bound follows immediately from Lemma
4.4
with
m=n
. For the upper bound, using the fact that
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}
and the subadditivity property in Corollary
4.3
we have for any
n,m

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes mn}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes mn})

\displaystyle\leq D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes mn}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes mn})

\displaystyle\leq nD^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})\ .

Dividing by
mn
and taking the limit as
n\to\infty
concludes the proof of the upper bound.
The inequality (
23
) follows from this upper bound together with Lemma
4.4
(more specifically, the lower bound there applied for
m=n
).
∎

Note that for any finite
m
, the quantity
\frac{1}{m}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})
can be approximated to arbitrary accuracy and this shows that
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}
can be approximated within additive
\varepsilon
in finite time.
The precise analysis of the running time as a function of the bit size of the input is a subtle question that is outside the scope of this work.
But staying at a high level, the running time of this algorithm will be exponential in the input and output dimensions of the channels. In fact, we can take
m=\lceil\frac{8\alpha d^{3}}{(\alpha-1)\varepsilon}\rceil
where
d
is the dimension of the Choi state of the channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, and then compute
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})
. The channels
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}
have a Choi state of dimension
d^{m}
and thus the convex program defining
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})
can be approximated in time that is polynomial in
d^{m}
using the ellipsoid algorithm. Overall, the running time is exponential in
d
.

As the regularized divergence between channels appears in the analysis of many information processing tasks, we believe this result will be useful in obtaining improved characterizations of such tasks. An example is the task of channel discrimination, for which the regularized Umegaki channel divergence governs the asymptotic error rate
[
43
,
39
,
13
]
. One could also obtain upper bounds on quantum channel capacities, such as the classical capacity, in terms of regularized divergence between channels (see e.g.,
[
41
]
). In fact, closely following the approach of
[
12
]
to upper bound the classical capacity of a quantum channel and replacing
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
with
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
, one does obtain improved bounds, including for the amplitude damping channel. However, the improvements obtained by such a direct application of
[
12
]
were small, typically less than 1%. To give an example, for a damping parameter
\gamma=0.5
, we obtain (using
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
with two copies of the channel) an upper bound on the capacity of
0.7694...
whereas the previous bound (using
D_{\max}
or
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
) was
0.7716...

[
40
,
41
]
. We leave the further exploration of this question for future work.

5.2
A chain rule for the sandwiched Rényi divergence

Our second application is a chain rule for the sandwiched Rényi divergence, which once again features the regularized divergence between channels. Such a chain rule was proved in
[
13
]
for the Umegaki relative entropy.

Corollary 5.2

(Chain rule for the sandwiched Rényi divergence)
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\rho_{RX^{\prime}},\sigma_{RX^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{P}(RX^{\prime})
,
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
be completely positive maps from
\mathscr{L}(X^{\prime})
to
\mathscr{L}(Y)
. Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\rho_{RX^{\prime}})\|(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\sigma_{RX^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})+\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{RX^{\prime}}\|\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})\ .

(24)

Proof.

We apply the chain rule in Proposition
4.5
to the states
\rho_{RX^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}
and
\sigma_{RX^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}
and the channels
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
and get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\rho_{RX^{\prime}})^{\otimes n}\|(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\sigma_{RX^{\prime}})^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})+\frac{1}{n}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho_{RX^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}\|\sigma_{RX^{\prime}}^{\otimes n})\ .

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, the state divergences becomes sandwiched divergences using (
14
) and the channel divergence becomes the regularized channel sandwiched divergence using Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

It is unclear whether taking the limit
\alpha\to 1
in this chain rule recovers the chain rule proved in
[
13
]
. The reason for this difficulty is that it remains open whether
\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 1}\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
.

It is also possible to phrase the chain rule in terms of amortized divergences as introduced in
[
43
]
. For a divergence
\mathbf{D}
, the amortized divergence is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\sup_{\rho_{RX}\in\mathscr{D}(RX),\sigma_{RX}\in\mathscr{D}(RX)}\mathbf{D}((\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho_{RX})\|(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M})(\sigma_{RX}))-\mathbf{D}(\rho_{RX}\|\sigma_{RX})\ ,

(25)

where the supremum also runs over all finite dimensional spaces
R
. When
\mathbf{D}
is the sandwiched Rényi divergence
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}
, note that for positive real numbers
\beta
and
\gamma
, we have
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\beta\rho\|\gamma\sigma)=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\beta-\log\gamma
. As a result, for any nonzero
\rho_{RX},\sigma_{RX}\in\mathscr{P}(RX)
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho_{RX})\|(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M})(\sigma_{RX}))-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{RX}\|\sigma_{RX})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\left((\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N})\left(\frac{\rho_{RX}}{\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)}\right)\|(\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{M})\left(\frac{\sigma_{RX}}{\mathrm{tr}\,(\sigma)}\right)\right)-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\rho_{RX}}{\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho)}\|\frac{\sigma_{RX}}{\mathrm{tr}\,(\sigma)}\right)\ ,

(26)

which means that in (
25
), we can also take the supremum over all nonzero positive semidefinite operators.

Theorem 5.4

(Amortization = regularization for sandwiched divergence)
.

For any completely positive maps
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
and any
\alpha>1
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\ .

(27)

Proof.

The inequality
\leq
follows immediately from the chain rule in (
24
).

The inequality
\geq
is actually true for any generalized divergence and was observed in previous works
[
43
,
39
]
. Note that we can equivalently write the channel divergence as

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\sup_{\phi_{XX^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{D}(X\otimes X^{\prime})}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\phi)\|(\mathcal{I}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y})(\phi))\ ,

where as usual
X^{\prime}
and
X
have the same dimension. Thus, denoting the
n
copies of
X
by
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
and using the shorthand
X_{i}^{j}
to denote
X_{i}X_{i+1}\dots X_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi_{X_{1}^{n}{X^{\prime}}^{n}_{1}}\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\otimes n}\otimes{X^{\prime}}^{\otimes n})}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes n})(\phi)\|(\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes n})(\phi))

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi_{X_{1}^{n}{X^{\prime}}^{n}_{1}}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Bigg(\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes(i+1)}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+2}^{n}})(\phi)\|(\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}{X^{\prime}}_{i+2}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes(i+1)}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+2}^{n}})(\phi))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+1}^{n}})(\phi)\|(\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}{X^{\prime}}_{i+1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+1}^{n}})(\phi))\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sup_{\phi_{X_{1}^{n}{X^{\prime}}^{n}_{1}}}\Bigg(\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes(i+1)}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+2}^{n}})(\phi)\|(\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}{X^{\prime}}_{i+2}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes(i+1)}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+2}^{n}})(\phi))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+1}^{n}})(\phi)\|(\mathcal{I}_{X_{1}^{n}{X^{\prime}}_{i+1}^{n}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}\to Y}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{{X^{\prime}}_{i+1}^{n}})(\phi))\Bigg)\ .

Note that in the
i
-th term, we subtract two expressions that differ by an application of the channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
on the system
X_{i+1}
and so the remaining systems can be considered as the
R
system in the definition (
25
). Using in addition the observation in (
26
) saying that
\rho
and
\sigma
need not be normalized, we get that each term is bounded by
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\leq n\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\ ,

which gives the desired result.
∎

The concept of amortization is particularly useful when analyzing information processing tasks that have an adaptive aspect. We discuss some examples below.

5.2.1
Channel discrimination

We discuss the example of channel discrimination, referring to
[
10
,
43
]
for a more detailed and precise presentation of the problem and the relevant references on the topic. Imagine we would like to distinguish between two quantum channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
having black box access to
n
uses of one of them. The task of adaptive channel discrimination is to decide which channel we are dealing with. The word adaptive here refers to the fact that our use of one of the black boxes can depend on the outcomes of a previously used black box. By contrast, a strategy is called parallel (or nonadaptive) if the
n
black boxes are used in parallel on a fixed input state. As is common in hypothesis testing, we call the type I error the probability
\alpha_{n}
that the channel is actually
\mathcal{N}
but our procedure says
\mathcal{M}
and the type II error
\beta_{n}
is the other kind of error and the goal is to determine the tradeoff between these two errors. Multiple regimes can be considered, the most studied is the asymmetric or Stein setting where we set
\alpha_{n}\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and consider the asymptotic behavior of the optimal type II error
-\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}
. The works
[
43
,
39
,
13
]
establish that if we take
\varepsilon\to 0
this is given by the regularized Umegaki relative entropy
D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
3
3

3

We recall that the Umegaki quantum relative entropy (also called simply quantum relative entropy) is defined as
D(\rho\|\sigma)=\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma))

[
36
,
18
]
. Our focus here is on the strong converse regime, i.e., we require
\beta_{n}\leq 2^{-rn}
with
r>D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
and we consider the behavior of
\alpha_{n}
. As far as we are aware, it is not known whether in this case we always have
\alpha_{n}\to 1
(this would be a strong converse property). However, we can always consider the following quantity which measures how quickly
\alpha_{n}
goes to
1
when it does so:

\displaystyle\overline{H}(r,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}):=\inf_{\text{adaptive strategies}}\Big\{\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n}):\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}\leq-r\Big\}\ .

Note that if
\alpha_{n}
does not converge to
1
exponentially fast, then this quantity is
0
.

A lower bound is given in
[
43
, Proposition 20]
for this quantity:

\displaystyle\overline{H}(r,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)\ .

(28)

We note that
[
43
, Proposition 20]
actually shows something stronger: for any
n
, any adaptive strategy and any
\alpha>1
, we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n})\geq\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(-\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)\ .

Thus, for any family of strategies satisfying
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}\leq-r
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(-\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)\ ,

which establishes (
28
).

Using equality (
27
) together with the explicit convergence bounds in Theorem
5.1
as well as the strong converse exponent established for states
[
27
]
, we show that this bound is in fact tight. This generalizes the result of
[
10
]
who considered the case where
\mathcal{M}
is a replacer channel, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}(W)=\mathrm{tr}\,(W)\sigma
for some state
\sigma
.

Theorem 5.5
.

For any completely positive and trace-preserving maps
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
and any
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{H}(r,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)\ .

(29)

In addition, the achievability uses a nonadaptive strategy and this shows that adaptive strategies do not offer an advantage in this setting.

Remark 5.6

(Continuity of
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}
when
\alpha\to 1
)
.

Note that this result implies that
\overline{H}(r,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})=0
if
r\leq\inf_{\alpha>1}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
and
\overline{H}(r,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})>0
if

. As the behaviour of
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}
as

remains unclear, we cannot rule out that
D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})<\inf_{\alpha>1}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
for some channels, and so it remains open whether a strong converse property holds in general.

Proof.

As usual, we will assume
J^{\mathcal{N}}\ll J^{\mathcal{M}}
, as otherwise,
D^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\infty
and the statement is void. The lower bound
\geq
follows immediately from (
28
) and equality (
27
).

For the upper bound, the idea is to use the characterization of
[
27
]
for the strong converse exponent for state discrimination. They show that for any states
\rho
and
\sigma
and
r>0
, there is a family of strategies to distinguish between
\rho^{\otimes n}
and
\sigma^{\otimes n}
with type II error probability
\beta_{n}(\rho,\sigma)\leq 2^{-rn}
and achieving a type I error probability
\alpha_{n}(\rho,\sigma)
satisfying

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n}(\rho,\sigma))\leq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\right)\ .

Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose an integer
m
so that
\frac{1}{m}(d^{2}+d)\log(m+d)<\varepsilon
where

. For any state
\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\otimes m})
we can apply this result to the states
\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}
and get a sequence of strategies achieving
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}},\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq-rm
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}},\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}))\leq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(rm-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)\ .

(30)

We choose
\omega_{m}
to achieve up to
\varepsilon

\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\otimes m})
with
\omega>0

\omega_{m}

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{n}(\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}},\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq 2^{-rm\cdot n}

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n}(\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}},\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\otimes m})\\
\omega>0\end{subarray}}\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(rm-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)+\varepsilon\ .

(31)

Now we observe that for any channels
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
such that
J^{\mathcal{A}}\ll J^{\mathcal{B}}
, we can perform the change of variable
u=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}
and get

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega>0\end{subarray}}\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{A}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{B}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega>0\end{subarray}}\sup_{u\in(0,1)}f(\omega,u)\ ,

(32)

where we defined the function

by

. We extend the function

to

and

. Note that as we assumed

, we have

and we even have for any
\omega>0
,

is independent of
\omega
. As we will see shortly, for any
\omega>0
, the function

is thus continuous on
[0,1]
. As such we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega>0\end{subarray}}\sup_{u\in(0,1)}f(\omega,u)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega>0\end{subarray}}\max_{u\in[0,1]}f(\omega,u)\ .

(33)

We are now ready to apply Sion’s minimax theorem. To do this, we check the following conditions:

•

For any
\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)
with
\omega>0
, the function
u\mapsto f(\omega,u)
is concave and continuous on the compact interval
[0,1]
. This follows from
[
27
, Remark IV.13 or the discussion preceding Lemma IV.9]
which shows that
u\mapsto u\widetilde{D}_{\frac{1}{1-u}}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{A}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{B}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
is convex and continuous on
[0,1)
. It is also clear that extending it continuously to
u=1
preserves the two properties.

•

For any
u\in[0,1]
, the function
\omega\mapsto f(\omega,u)
is convex and continuous on the convex set
\{\omega\in\mathcal{D}(X):\omega>0\}
. For
u\in\{0,1\}
, this is trivial as the function is constant. For
u\in(0,1)
, this follows immediately from Lemma
A.2
.

Applying Sion’s minimax theorem, we can exchange the
\inf
and
\max
in (
33
) and get

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega>0\end{subarray}}\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{A}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{B}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{u\in[0,1]}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathscr{D}(X)\\
\omega>0\end{subarray}}ur-u\widetilde{D}_{\frac{1}{1-u}}(\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{A}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{B}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\sup_{u\in(0,1)}ur-u\widetilde{D}_{\frac{1}{1-u}}(\mathcal{A}\|\mathcal{B}),0,r-D_{\max}(\mathcal{A}\|\mathcal{B})\Big\}\ .

Note that for the second equality we used equality (
37
) saying that we can drop the
\omega>0
condition in the infimum. Now as
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\leq D_{\max}
, we have
\sup_{u\in(0,1)}ur-u\widetilde{D}_{\frac{1}{1-u}}(\mathcal{A}\|\mathcal{B})\geq\sup_{u\in(0,1)}u(r-D_{\max}(\mathcal{A}\|\mathcal{B}))\geq\max\{0,r-D_{\max}(\mathcal{A}\|\mathcal{B})\}
so we can drop the terms
0
and
r-D_{\max}(\mathcal{A}\|\mathcal{B})
from the maximization.
Thus, (
31
) becomes

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n}(\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}},\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(rm-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})\right)+\varepsilon\ .

Using the finite convergence bounds in (
23
) for
\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\frac{1}{m}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})+\frac{1}{m}(d^{2}+d)\log(m+d)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\frac{1}{m}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})+\varepsilon

recalling our choice of
m
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\frac{1}{m}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m})\right)\leq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)+\varepsilon\ .

(34)

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{n}(\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}},\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes m}}\omega_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)+2\varepsilon\ .

In other words, we have constructed a sequence of strategies for distinguishing between
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes mn}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes mn}
for
n\geq 1
with a type II error
\beta_{nm}
and a type I error
\alpha_{nm}
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\beta_{nm}

\displaystyle\leq-r\ .

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-\alpha_{nm})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)+2\varepsilon\ .

(35)

To conclude, we define a strategy for distinguishing between
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}
for
k
that is not necessarily of the form
mn
for some
n
. For that, we write
k=mq+p
with
0\leq p<m
and we only use
mq
copies of the channel and apply the above argument. We thus obtain exactly the same type I and type II errors as for
mq
copies, i.e.,
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{mq}
and
\beta_{k}=\beta_{mq}
. With this notation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\log\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{mq+p}\log\beta_{mq}\leq\frac{1}{m(q+1)}\log\beta_{mq}=\frac{q}{q+1}\frac{1}{mq}\log\beta_{mq}\ .

As
k\to\infty
, we have
q\to\infty
so

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\beta_{k}\leq\limsup_{q\to\infty}\frac{1}{mq}\log\beta_{mq}\leq-r\ .

In addition, using the same notation, the type I error satisfies

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}\log(1-\alpha_{k})\leq-\frac{1}{mq}\log(1-\alpha_{mq})\ .

As a result, (
35
) implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}-\frac{1}{k}\log(1-\alpha_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(r-\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\right)+2\varepsilon\ .

As this is valid for any
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain the claimed result.
∎

5.2.2
Bounds on amortized entanglement measures and applications

Another task that has an adaptive nature is the task of quantum communication using free two-way classical communication.
In order to analyze such tasks, one usually considers an entanglement measure and tracks its value during the rounds of the protocol. Here, we will focus on measures of the following form: for
\alpha\in[1,\infty]
and some convex subset
\mathcal{C}(X:Y)\subseteq\mathscr{P}(XY)
, we can define for a bipartite state
\rho_{XY}

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,\mathcal{C}}(X:Y)_{\rho}=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}(X:Y)}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{XY}\|\sigma_{XY})\ .

When it is clear from the context
\alpha
and
\mathcal{C}
will be dropped from the notation.
Note that this quantity is quasiconvex in
\rho_{XY}
. In fact using the joint quasiconvexity of
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}
, we have for
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
\rho^{0},\rho^{1}\in\mathscr{D}(XY)
and
\sigma^{0},\sigma^{1}\in\mathcal{C}(X:Y)

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\lambda\rho^{0}+(1-\lambda)\rho^{1}\|\lambda\sigma^{0}+(1-\lambda)\sigma^{1})\leq\max\{\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho^{0}\|\sigma^{0}),\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho^{1}\|\sigma^{1})\}\ .

Taking the infimum over
\sigma^{0}
and
\sigma^{1}
, we get the quasiconvexity of
E(X:Y)_{\rho}
in
\rho
.
To make this a useful correlation measure, we will assume that
\mathcal{C}(X:Y)
contains all the product states
\phi_{X}\otimes\psi_{Y}
, and as we assumed convexity of
\mathcal{C}(X:Y)
, it also contains the set of all separable states.
Many studied quantum correlation measures are special cases:

•

For the relative entropy of entanglement,
\mathcal{C}
is the set of separable states and
\alpha=1
, but the full range
\alpha\in[1,\infty]
has also been used, in particular for the study of adaptive protocols
[
30
,
42
,
9
]
.

•

For the Rains bound,
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{PPT^{\prime}}:=\{\sigma_{XY}\in\mathscr{P}(XY):\|\sigma_{XY}^{\top_{Y}}\|_{1}\leq 1\}
and
\alpha=1

[
31
]
, and the version with
\alpha=\infty
has also been studied in
[
38
,
4
]
. The notation
\top_{Y}
denotes the partial transpose on system
Y
with respect to some fixed basis.

One can then naturally define the entanglement of a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y}
as

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{N})=\sup_{\rho\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\prime}X)}E(X^{\prime}:Y)_{(\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y})(\rho_{X^{\prime}X})}\ ,

where the supremum also runs over arbitrary finite dimensional systems
X^{\prime}
. Note that using the quasiconvexity of
E
in the state, we may restrict
\rho_{X^{\prime}X}
to be pure. Thus, whenever the set
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under local isometries (which will be the case here), it suffices to take
X^{\prime}
to have the same dimension as
X
. The amortized version is then defined as

\displaystyle E^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N})=\sup_{\rho_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\prime}XY)}E(X^{\prime}:YY^{\prime})_{(\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}Y^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y})(\rho_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}})}-E(X^{\prime}X:Y^{\prime})_{\rho_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}}\ ,

where the supremum runs over arbitrary finite dimensional systems
X^{\prime}Y^{\prime}
. Note that if
Y^{\prime}
is trivial, we recover
E(\mathcal{N})
but in general it is not clear how to bound the dimensions of the systems
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
. Amortized quantities allow one to place upper bounds on the rates of protocols allowing two-way communication, as shown for example
[
3
]
in the context of bidirectional channel capacities and in
[
23
,
4
]
in the context of quantum/private communication with free two-way classical communication.

Lemma 5.7
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{X\to Y}
be a completely positive map satisfying the following property. For any
\rho_{X^{\prime}YY^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\prime}YY^{\prime})

\sigma_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}(X^{\prime}X:Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle E(X^{\prime}:YY^{\prime})_{\rho}\leq\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{X^{\prime}YY^{\prime}}\|(\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}Y^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{X\to Y})(\sigma_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}))\ .

Then

\displaystyle E^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N})\leq\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M}).

Proof.

Consider
\rho_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}

\sigma_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}(X^{\prime}X:Y^{\prime})
. Applying the chain rule, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y}(\rho_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}})\|\mathcal{M}_{X\to Y}(\sigma_{X^{\prime}XY}))\leq\widetilde{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})+\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}\|\sigma_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}})\ .

Thus,

\displaystyle E^{\mathrm{a}}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\rho_{X^{\prime}XY^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{D}(X^{\prime}XY)}E(X^{\prime}:YY^{\prime})_{(\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}Y^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y})(\rho_{X^{\prime}XY})}-E(X^{\prime}X:Y^{\prime})_{\rho_{X^{\prime}XY}}

6
Discussion

We have presented a family of quantum
\alpha
-Rényi divergences for
\alpha>1
based on the geometric mean. The framework is in fact more general and allows us to define quantum divergences in a similar way using a Kubo-Ando mean for any operator monotone function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
. As we mostly used generic properties of operator means to establish properties of
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
, we expect analogous properties for more general functions
g
to hold. For example, for any convex function
f
, the
f
-divergence between distributions
P
and
Q
is defined as
Q_{f}(P\|Q)=\sum_{x}f(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)})Q(x)
. When
f(t)=t^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>1
, we obtain (after applying
\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log
) the
\alpha
-Rényi divergence. Several quantum
f
-divergences have been proposed, see e.g.,
[
19
]
. In the special case where
f
is bijective and its inverse
f^{-1}
is operator monotone, then using the Kubo-Ando mean associated with
g=f^{-1}
, we would obtain a quantum version of the
f
-divergence. Here, we focused on the case
f(t)=t^{\alpha}
and correspondingly
g(t)=t^{1/\alpha}
, but it would be interesting to explore other choices of
g
and potential applications.
In a different direction, a variant of
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
is defined in
[
7
]
, using the
\frac{1}{2}
-geometric mean but one takes the geometric mean
k
times iteratively with different variables. More generally, we hope that our work encourages the study of further quantum divergences that are defined via convex optimization programs.

We leave multiple open questions. A specific question is whether
\lim_{\alpha\to 1}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
is equal to the Belavkin-Staszewski divergence
\widehat{D}(\rho\|\sigma)

[
2
]
? Numerical examples suggest that this should be the case.
Another question is whether it is possible to define
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
when
\alpha<1
with similar properties? The natural extension would be to define
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
with
Q^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\max\{\mathrm{tr}\,(A):\rho\geq\sigma\#_{1/\alpha}A\}
. But with this definition, it is simple to check using the operator monotonicity of
t\mapsto t^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in[0,1]
that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
which means that we cannot have the property (
14
) for example. This argument does not go through when
\alpha>1
as
t\mapsto t^{\alpha}
is not operator monotone in this regime. It would also be interesting to generalize the divergences introduced here to infinite-dimensional spaces or even to von Neumann algebras. Another important question that is left open is whether
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}
converges to
D^{\mathrm{reg}}
when
\alpha\to 1
.

The following standard lemma about permutation invariant operators was used for the proof of Lemma
4.4
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
X
be a permutation-invariant operator on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
, i.e.,
[P({\pi}),X]=0
for any permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
P({\pi})|\psi_{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{n}\rangle=|\psi_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\rangle
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathrm{spec}(X)|\leq(n+1)^{d}(n+d)^{d^{2}}\ .

Proof.

P
defines a representation of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
and its decomposition into irreducible representations is well-known, see e.g.,
[
16
, Section 5.3]
. In fact, its irreducible representations are labelled by the set
\mathcal{I}_{n,d}
of Young diagrams of size
n
with at most
d
rows. For
\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}
, we denote by
p_{\lambda}
the corresponding irreducible representation acting on the space
V_{\lambda}
. Each
p_{\lambda}
appears in general multiple times in
P
and this is taken into account by introducing the multiplicity space
U_{\lambda}
(which happens to correspond to an irreducible representation of the unitary group but we will not use this here). Summarizing, the operator
P(\pi)
can in the Schur basis be written as

\displaystyle P(\pi)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}}|\lambda\rangle\!\langle\lambda|\otimes I_{U_{\lambda}}\otimes p_{\lambda}(\pi)\ .

We now express the operator
X
in the Schur basis

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}\\
i\in[m(\lambda)],i^{\prime}\in[m(\lambda^{\prime})]\end{subarray}}|\lambda\rangle\!\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\otimes|u_{\lambda,i}\rangle\!\langle u_{\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime}}|\otimes X_{(\lambda,i),(\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime})}\ ,

where we have introduced orthonormal bases
\{u_{\lambda,i}\}_{i\in m(\lambda)}
of the spaces
U_{\lambda}
(
m(\lambda)
is the dimension of
U_{\lambda}
) and
X_{(\lambda,i),(\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime})}
can be seen as an operator from
V_{\lambda^{\prime}}
to
V_{\lambda}
. We can now write the products
P(\pi)X
and
XP(\pi)
as

\displaystyle P(\pi)X

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}\\
i\in[m(\lambda)],i^{\prime}\in[m(\lambda^{\prime})]\end{subarray}}|\lambda\rangle\!\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\otimes|u_{\lambda,i}\rangle\!\langle u_{\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime}}|\otimes p_{\lambda}(\pi)X_{(\lambda,i),(\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime})}

\displaystyle XP(\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}\\
i\in[m(\lambda)],i^{\prime}\in[m(\lambda^{\prime})]\end{subarray}}|\lambda\rangle\!\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\otimes|u_{\lambda,i}\rangle\!\langle u_{\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime}}|\otimes X_{(\lambda,i),(\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime})}p_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\pi)\ .

Applying Schur’s lemma, we get that
X_{(\lambda,i),(\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime})}=0
if
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
and
X_{(\lambda,i),(\lambda,i^{\prime})}=x_{\lambda,i,i^{\prime}}I_{V_{\lambda}}
for some scalar
x_{\lambda,i,i^{\prime}}
. Defining the operator
X_{U_{\lambda}}=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}\in m(\lambda)}x_{\lambda,i,i^{\prime}}|u_{\lambda,i}\rangle\!\langle u_{\lambda,i^{\prime}}|
, we can write
X
as

\displaystyle X=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{n,d}}|\lambda\rangle\!\langle\lambda|\otimes X_{U_{\lambda}}\otimes I_{V_{\lambda}}\ .

As a result,
|\mathrm{spec}(X)|\leq|\mathcal{I}_{n,d}|\max_{\lambda}m(\lambda)
. But it is well-known that
|\mathcal{I}_{n,d}|\leq(n+1)^{d}
and
\max_{\lambda}m(\lambda)\leq(n+d)^{d^{2}}
(see e.g.,
[
16
, Section 6.2]
). This concludes the proof of the claim.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

For any
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, the function

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\>:\>

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

\displaystyle(\rho,\sigma)\mapsto\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

is lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

Let
(\rho,\sigma)
be such that
\rho\ll\sigma
. Our objective is to show that for any sequence
(\rho_{n},\sigma_{n})
converging to
(\rho,\sigma)
, we have
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})\geq\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
. We first observe that if we restrict ourselves to the set
\mathscr{P}(\mathrm{supp}(\sigma))\times\mathscr{P}(\mathrm{supp}(\sigma))
, then
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}
is continuous at
(\rho,\sigma)
as a composition of continuous functions. Now let
P
be the projector onto
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)

P(I-P)=0

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{\alpha}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{Q}_{\alpha}\left(P\rho_{n}P+(I-P)\rho_{n}(I-P)\|P\sigma_{n}P+(I-P)\sigma_{n}(I-P)\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{Q}_{\alpha}(P\rho_{n}P\|P\sigma_{n}P)+\widetilde{Q}_{\alpha}((I-P)\rho_{n}(I-P)\|(I-P)\sigma_{n}(I-P))

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{Q}_{\alpha}(P\rho_{n}P\|P\sigma_{n}P)\ .

Now the sequence
(P\rho_{n}P,P\sigma_{n}P)
is in
\mathscr{P}(\mathrm{supp}(\sigma))\times\mathscr{P}(\mathrm{supp}(\sigma))
and converges to
(\rho,\sigma)
. Using continuity of the function restricted to this set we obtain the desired result. We remark that
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}
is not continuous in general even in the classical case: consider for example
\rho_{n}=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+n^{-1}|1\rangle\!\langle 1|
and
\sigma_{n}=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+n^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}|1\rangle\!\langle 1|
with
\rho=\sigma=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|
then
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=0
but
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(1+n^{-\alpha}n^{+\alpha}\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
is bounded away from
0
.

Now assume that we do not have
\rho\ll\sigma
. In this case, our objective is to show that for any sequence
(\rho_{n},\sigma_{n})
converging to
(\rho,\sigma)
, we have
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Note that
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\not\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
implies that
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)^{\perp}\not\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\rho)^{\perp}
. Let
|v_{1}\rangle\in\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)^{\perp}
but not in
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)^{\perp}
. Then complete it
\{|v_{1}\rangle,\dots,|v_{d}\rangle\}
into an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
and define the completely positive and trace-preserving map
\mathcal{M}
by
\mathcal{M}(W)=\sum_{i}|v_{i}\rangle\!\langle v_{i}|W|v_{i}\rangle\!\langle v_{i}|
. By the data-processing inequality, we have for any
n
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{n})\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma_{n}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(\langle v_{1}|\rho_{n}|v_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha}(\langle v_{1}|\sigma_{n}|v_{1}\rangle)^{1-\alpha}\ .

But then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle v_{1}|\rho_{n}|v_{1}\rangle=\langle v_{1}|\rho|v_{1}\rangle>0
(as
|v_{1}\rangle\not\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho)^{\perp}
) and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle v_{1}|\sigma_{n}|v_{1}\rangle=\langle v_{1}|\sigma|v_{1}\rangle=0
, which leads to the desired result.
∎

Mark Alford \orcidicon, Arus Harutyunyan * \orcidicon, Armen Sedrakian \orcidicon

Abstract
We discuss the bulk viscosity of hot and dense n ​ p ​ e ​ μ npe\mu matter arising from weak-interaction direct Urca processes. We consider two regimes of interest: (a) the neutrino-transparent regime with T ≤ T tr T\leq T_{\rm tr} ( T tr ≃ 5 ÷ 10 T_{\rm tr}\simeq 5\div 10 MeV is the neutrino-trapping temperature); and (b) the neutrino-trapped regime with T ≥ T tr T\geq T_{\rm tr} . Nuclear matter is modeled in relativistic density functional approach with density-dependent parametrization DDME2. The maximum of the bulk viscosity is achieved at temperatures T ≃ 5 ÷ 6 T\simeq 5\div 6 MeV in the neutrino-transparent regime, then it drops rapidly at higher temperatures where neutrino-trapping occurs. As an astrophysical application, we estimate the damping timescales of density oscillations by the bulk viscosity in neutron star mergers and find that, e.g., at the oscillation frequency f = 10 f=10 kHz, the damping will be very efficient at temperatures 4 ≤ T ≤ 7 4\leq T\leq 7 MeV where the bulk viscosity might affect the evolution of the post-merger object.

Introduction

The recent detections of gravitational waves from binary neutron-star
(BNS) mergers by the LIGO-Virgo collaboration motivate studies of the
transport properties of hot and dense nuclear matter. Numerical
simulations of BNS mergers predict intense emission of gravitational
waves during the first tens of milliseconds after the merger in the
kHz frequency range (see, e.g.,
Refs.
Endrizzi et al. 2018
;
Most et al. 2019
;
Ciolfi et al. 2019
;
Tsokaros et al. 2019
for recent
simulations). The dissipation of matter flows in the
post-merger object might affect the gravitational wave spectra emitted during this
stage of BNS merger evolution. In particular, indications of bulk viscous dissipation were seen in a recent BNS simulation
incorporating beta equilibrating processes
Most et al. 2022
, confirming earlier estimates of its
likely importance.

There exist extensive studies of the bulk viscosity of
neutron–proton–electron (hereafter
npe
) and
npe\mu
(where
\mu

stands for muons) matters in low-temperature (cold) neutron
stars
Sawyer and Soni 1979
;
Sawyer 1980
;
Sawyer 1989
;
Haensel and Schaeffer 1992
;
Haensel et al. 2000
;
Haensel et al. 2001
;
Haensel et al. 2002
;
Dong et al. 2007
;
Alford et al. 2010
;
Alford and Good 2010
;
Kolomeitsev and Voskresensky 2015
. The bulk viscosity
of the dense matter at high temperatures (up to tens of MeV) was computed in
recent works which covered various regimes of temperature and density,
as well as neutrino trapping/un-trapping, in strongly interacting
hadronic matter
Alford and Harris 2019
;
Alford et al. 2019
;
Alford and Haber 2021
;
Alford et al. 2021
.

In this contribution, we review briefly the results of
Ref.
Alford et al. 2021
for the bulk viscosity of the
neutrino-trapped, relativistic
npe\mu
matter, as well as complement
them with novel results for the neutrino-transparent regime. The
impact of purely leptonic weak processes on the bulk viscosity is
discussed. We use the DDME2 parametrization
Lalazissis et al. 2005
of
relativistic density functional theory with density-dependent
couplings to model the background nuclear matter. It provides
very reasonable values of such characteristics of symmetric nuclear
matter, as the energy per nucleon
E_{\rm sat}=-16.14
MeV and
compressibility
K_{\rm sat}~=~251.15
MeV at saturation density

n_{0}=0.152
fm
-3
, as well as characteristics of asymmetric
nuclear matter such as the symmetry energy

E_{\rm sym}=32.31
MeV and its slope
L_{\rm sym}=51.27

MeV. In our previous work
Alford et al. 2019
;
Alford et al. 2021
we used
the NL3 parameterization (along with the DDME2) where the couplings
are density-independent but meson fields acquire additional
self-interactions terms. This functional differs significantly from the DDME2
functional used in this study in the properties of
asymmetric matter with
E_{\rm sym}=37.4
and
L_{\rm sym}=118.9

MeV. Specifically, these two functionals cover well the range for the
parameters
E_{\rm sym}
and
L_{\rm sym}
that have been inferred
from the PREX-II experiment by two alternative
analysis
Reed et al. 2021
;
Reinhard et al. 2021
. Thus, using our previous
results one can assess the impact of the variations of the important
characteristics of nuclear matter on the various quantities of
interest, such as bulk viscosity and damping time scales. A full
analysis of the sensitivity of the results on the input of various
density functionals goes beyond the present study.

With the results obtained
for the bulk viscosity, we estimate the bulk viscous dissipation
timescales of density oscillations in BNS mergers. For typical
oscillation frequencies
1\leq f\leq 10
kHz the bulk viscous damping
timescales reach down to tens of milliseconds (at
n_{B}\simeq 3n_{0}
) or
milliseconds (at
n_{B}\simeq n_{0}
) at temperatures
4\leq T\leq 7
MeV.
Here the bulk viscous damping can have a significant impact on the
initial phase of post-merger dynamics with a typical timescale

\sim
10 ms. At high temperatures above the neutrino-trapping, the
bulk viscosity falls rapidly by orders of magnitude, and the damping
timescales become too long to affect the dynamics of BNS mergers.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we
discuss the weak processes in nuclear matter. In Section
3
,
we discuss the bulk viscosity produced by the Urca processes.
Section
4
collects the numerical results for
the equilibration rates, the bulk viscosity, and the dissipation
damping timescales in the regimes of neutrino-transparent and
neutrino-trapped matter. Section
5
provides
a brief summary of our results. We work with natural (Gaussian)
units where
\hbar=c=k_{B}=1
.

2
Weak Processes in Neutron Star Matter

We consider relativistic
npe\mu
matter in the range of densities

0.5n_{0}\leq n_{B}\leq 5n_{0}
, where
n_{0}\simeq 0.152
fm
-3
is the
nuclear saturation density and temperatures
1\leq T\simeq 100
MeV. Neutrinos are trapped in the matter above the
neutrino-trapping temperature
T_{\rm tr}\simeq 5\div 10
MeV and
un-trapped (free-streaming) below this temperature
Alford and Harris 2018
.

Consider now the simplest semi-baryonic
\beta
-equilibration
processes—the direct Urca processes of neutron decay and
lepton capture, respectively

\displaystyle n\rightleftarrows p+l^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{l},

(1)

\displaystyle p+l^{-}\rightleftarrows n+{\nu}_{l},

(2)

where
l=\{e,\mu\}
is electron or muon,
\nu_{l}
is the
corresponding neutrino. In the
\nu
-transparent regime, these
processes proceed only in one direction from left to right as
neutrinos/anti-neutrinos can appear only in the final state.

If muons are present in matter, the following leptonic processes of
muon decay, neutrino absorption and antineutrino absorption,
respectively, may occur additionally

\displaystyle\mu^{-}\rightleftarrows e^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{e}+{\nu}_{\mu},

(3)

\displaystyle\mu^{-}+{\nu}_{e}\rightleftarrows e^{-}+{\nu}_{\mu},

(4)

\displaystyle\mu^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\rightleftarrows e^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{e}.

(5)

In the
\nu
-transparent regime, (
4
) and (
5
) are not possible, and (
3
) can only occur in the forward direction when the temperature is high enough to open up enough phase space around the muon and electron Fermi surfaces. We neglect modified-Urca-type processes involving electromagnetic interaction with a spectator particle
Alford and Good 2010
; these are suppressed by a factor of
\alpha^{2}
.

In
npe\mu
matter the baryon number given by
n_{B}=n_{n}+n_{p}
is
conserved. The matter is also charge neutral,
i.e.,
n_{p}=n_{e}+n_{\mu}
. In the neutrino-trapped case the lepton numbers

n_{L_{l}}=n_{l}+n_{\nu_{l}}=Y_{L_{l}}n_{B}
(with
Y_{L_{l}}
being the lepton
fractions) are also conserved. In neutron star mergers one can adopt
the values
Y_{Le}=Y_{L\mu}=0.1
for the neutrino-trapped
case
Baiotti 2019
.

The
\beta
-equilibration rates of the processes (
1
)
and (
2
) are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n\to pl\bar{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Omega_{p}\sum|{\cal M}_{\rm Urca}|^{2}\bar{f}(k)\bar{f}(p)\bar{f}(k^{\prime})f(p^{\prime})(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k+p+k^{\prime}-p^{\prime}),

(6)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{pl\to n\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Omega_{p}\sum|{\cal M}_{\rm Urca}|^{2}{f}(k){f}(p)\bar{f}(k^{\prime})\bar{f}(p^{\prime})(2\pi)^{4}\delta(k+p-k^{\prime}-p^{\prime}).

(7)

where

\displaystyle\int d\Omega_{p}=\int\!\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}2p_{0}}\int\!\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}2p^{\prime}_{0}}\int\!\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2k_{0}}\int\!\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}2k^{\prime}_{0}}

(8)

is the Lorentz-invariant momentum phase-space element,
f(p)

is the Fermi distribution of particles, and
\bar{f}(p)=1-f(p)
.
The particles are assigned momenta as follows:
(l)\to k
,

(\nu_{l}/\bar{\nu}_{l})\to k^{\prime}
,
(p)\to p
, and
(n)\to p^{\prime}
. Note that in neutrino-transparent
matter one should replace
\bar{f}(k^{\prime})\to 1
in these expressions.

The spin-averaged relativistic matrix element of the Urca processes
reads

\displaystyle\sum|{\cal M}_{\rm Urca}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 32G_{F}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{c}\Big[(1+g_{A})^{2}(k\cdot p)(k^{\prime}\cdot p^{\prime})

(9)

\displaystyle+(1-g_{A})^{2}(k\cdot p^{\prime})(k^{\prime}\cdot p)+(g_{A}^{2}-1)m^{*2}(k\cdot k^{\prime})\Big],

where
G_{F}=1.166\cdot 10^{-5}
GeV
-2
is the Fermi coupling
constant,
\theta_{c}
is the Cabibbo angle with
\cos\theta_{c}=0.974
,

g_{A}=1.26
is the axial-vector coupling constant, and
m^{*}
is the
effective nucleon mass. We will keep only the first term of this
expression in the following as the second and the third terms are
negligible for
g_{A}
values close to the vacuum value quoted
above. The twelve-dimensional phase-space integrals in
Equations (
6
) and (
7
) can be reduced to
the following four-dimensional integrals which are then computed
numerically

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n\to pl\bar{\nu}}(\mu_{\Delta_{l}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{{G}^{2}T^{4}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!dy\,\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dx\,\left[(\mu_{\nu_{l}}+\mu_{n}^{*}+yT)^{2}-m_{n}^{*2}-x^{2}T^{2}\right]

(10)

\displaystyle\times\left[(\mu_{l}+\mu_{p}^{*}+\bar{y}_{l}T)^{2}-m_{l}^{2}-m_{p}^{*2}-x^{2}T^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\Gamma_{pl\to n\nu}(\mu_{\Delta_{l}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{G}^{2}T^{4}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!dy\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dx\,\left[(\mu_{\nu_{l}}+\mu_{n}^{*}+yT)^{2}-m_{n}^{*2}-x^{2}T^{2}\right]

(11)

\displaystyle\times\left[(\mu_{l}+\mu_{p}^{*}+\bar{y}_{l}T)^{2}-m_{l}^{2}-m_{p}^{*2}-x^{2}T^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\times\int_{m_{l}/T-\alpha_{l}}^{\alpha_{p}+\bar{y}_{l}}\!dz\,f(z)f(\bar{y}_{l}-z)\,\theta_{x}\!\int_{-\alpha_{\nu_{l}}}^{\alpha_{n}+y}\!dz^{\prime}\,{f}(z^{\prime}-y)\bar{f}(z^{\prime})\,\theta_{z},

where
G=G_{F}\cos\theta_{c}(1+g_{A})
,
m_{l}
is the lepton mass,

\alpha_{l}=\mu_{l}/T
,
\alpha_{N}=\mu_{N}^{*}/T
for
N=\{n,p\}
with

\mu_{N}^{*}
being the nucleon effective chemical potential, see
Section
4.1

\bar{y}_{l}=y+\mu_{\Delta_{l}}/T

\mu_{\Delta_{l}}=\mu_{n}+\mu_{\nu_{l}}-\mu_{p}-\mu_{l}

f(x)\equiv[1+e^{x}]^{-1}
. The
\theta

Equations (
10
) and (
11
) imply

\displaystyle\theta_{x}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle(z_{k}-x)^{2}\leq\left(z-\alpha_{p}-\bar{y}_{l}\right)^{2}-m_{p}^{*2}/T^{2}\leq(z_{k}+x)^{2},

(12)

\displaystyle\theta_{y}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle(z_{k}^{\prime}-x)^{2}\leq\left(z^{\prime}+\alpha_{n}+y\right)^{2}-m_{n}^{*2}/T^{2}\leq(z_{k}^{\prime}+x)^{2},

(13)

\displaystyle\theta_{z}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle(z_{k}^{\prime}-x)^{2}\leq\left(z^{\prime}-\alpha_{n}-y\right)^{2}-m_{n}^{*2}/T^{2}\leq(z_{k}^{\prime}+x)^{2}.

(14)

The integration variables
y
and
x
are the transferred energy

variables
z
and
z^{\prime}
are the normalized-by-temperature lepton

z_{k}=\sqrt{(z+\alpha_{l})^{2}-m_{l}^{2}/T^{2}}

In the case ofneutrino-trapped matter, the rates of the inverse
processes are obtained fromEquations (
10
) and
(
11
) by replacing
f(p_{i})\rightarrow\bar{f}(p_{i})

for all particles. In the case of
\nu

\mu_{\nu_{l}}=0
and
\bar{f}(z^{\prime})\to 1
in the direct processes.

We will work in the low-temperature approximation where
beta equilibrium corresponds to
\mu_{\Delta_{l}}=0
. In the case
of deviations from
\beta
-equilibrium, there is a net rate of proton
production/annihilation due to each of the processes (
1
)
and (
2
), which in the linear-response regime

\mu_{\Delta_{l}}\ll T
can be written as

\Gamma_{n\to pl\bar{\nu}}-\Gamma_{pl\bar{\nu}\to n}=\lambda_{n\leftrightarrow pl\bar{\nu}}\,\mu_{\Delta_{l}}
, and

\Gamma_{n\nu\to pl}-\Gamma_{pl\to n\nu}=\lambda_{pl\leftrightarrow n\nu}\,\mu_{\Delta_{l}}
, with the coefficients
\lambda_{n\leftrightarrow pl\bar{\nu}}

and
\lambda_{pl\leftrightarrow n\nu}
given by
Alford et al. 2021

\displaystyle\lambda_{n\leftrightarrow pl\bar{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\Gamma_{n\to pl\bar{\nu}}}{\partial\mu_{\Delta_{l}}}-\frac{\partial\Gamma_{pl\bar{\nu}\to n}}{\partial\mu_{\Delta_{l}}}\right)\bigg|_{\mu_{\Delta_{l}}=0}=\frac{\Gamma_{n\leftrightarrow pl\bar{\nu}}}{T},

(15)

\displaystyle\lambda_{pl\leftrightarrow n\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\Gamma_{n\nu\to pl}}{\partial\mu_{\Delta_{l}}}-\frac{\partial\Gamma_{pl\to n\nu}}{\partial\mu_{\Delta_{l}}}\right)\bigg|_{\mu_{\Delta_{l}}=0}=\frac{\Gamma_{pl\leftrightarrow n\nu}}{T}.

(16)

Note that at temperatures
T\gtrsim 1\,{\rm MeV}
and at densities
where direct Urca would be forbidden at
T=0
, the neutron decay
and lepton capture processes are Boltzmann-suppressed by different
factors, arising from their different phase spaces
Alford and Harris 2018
.
This means that the coefficients should be evaluated at a nonzero

\mu_{\Delta_{l}}=\mu_{\Delta_{l}}^{\rm eq}
, but we work in the
approximation
\mu_{\Delta_{l}}^{\rm eq}=0
: this is discussed
in Section
4
.

Similar to the Urca reaction rates, the lepton reaction
rates can be written in the following form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\to e\bar{\nu}\nu}=\int d\Omega_{k}\sum|{\cal M}_{\rm lep}|^{2}f(k_{\mu})\bar{f}(k_{e})\bar{f}(k_{\bar{\nu}_{e}})\bar{f}(k_{\nu_{\mu}})(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k_{e}+k_{\bar{\nu}_{e}}+k_{\nu_{\mu}}-k_{\mu}),

(17)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\nu\to e{\nu}}=\int d\Omega_{k}\sum|{\cal M}_{\rm lep}|^{2}f(k_{\mu}){f}(k_{\nu_{e}})\bar{f}(k_{e})\bar{f}(k_{\nu_{\mu}})(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k_{e}+k_{\nu_{\mu}}-k_{\nu_{e}}-k_{\mu}),

(18)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\bar{\nu}\to e\bar{\nu}}=\int d\Omega_{k}\sum|{\cal M}_{\rm lep}|^{2}f(k_{\mu}){f}(k_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}})\bar{f}(k_{e})\bar{f}(k_{\bar{\nu}_{e}})(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k_{e}+k_{\bar{\nu}_{e}}-k_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}-k_{\mu}),

(19)

where
d\Omega_{k}
is defined analogously to Equation (
8
).
The spin-averaged relativistic matrix element of lepton reactions
reads
Guo et al. 2020

\sum|{\cal M}_{\rm lep}|^{2}=128G_{F}^{2}\left(k_{e}\cdot k_{\nu_{\mu}/\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}\right)\left(k_{\nu_{e}/\bar{\nu}_{e}}\cdot k_{\mu}\right).

(20)

The final expressions for the lepton reaction rates are
very similar to the Urca process rates (
10
) and
(
11
) and are given in Ref.
Alford et al. 2021
.

3
Bulk Viscosity of
npe\mu
Matter

In this section, we briefly review the bulk viscosity of
relativistic
npe\mu
matter arising from the Urca
processes (
1
) and (
2
).
For this, we consider small-amplitude density oscillations
with frequency
\omega
. Separating the oscillating parts
from the static equilibrium values of particle densities
we can write
n_{j}(t)=n_{j0}+\delta n_{j}(t)
, where
\delta n_{j}(t)\sim e^{i\omega t}
with
j=\{n,p,l,\nu_{l}\}
.
Oscillations drive the system out of chemical equilibrium
leading to nonzero chemical imbalances
\mu_{\Delta_{l}}=\delta\mu_{n}+\delta\mu_{\nu_{l}}-\delta\mu_{p}-\delta\mu_{l}
,
which can be written as

\displaystyle\mu_{\Delta_{l}}=A_{n}\delta n_{n}+A_{\nu_{e}}\delta n_{\nu_{e}}-A_{p}\delta n_{p}-A_{l}\delta n_{l},

(21)

where the particle susceptibilites are defined as
A_{n}=A_{nn}-A_{pn}
,

A_{p}=A_{pp}-A_{np}
, and
A_{l}=A_{ll}
,
A_{\nu_{l}}=A_{\nu_{l}\nu_{l}}
with

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\frac{\partial\mu_{i}}{\partial n_{j}},

(22)

where the derivatives are computed in
\beta
-equilibrium state.

If the weak processes were switched off, then the number of
all particle species would conserve separately, which implies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\delta{n}^{0}_{j}(t)+\theta n_{j0}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\delta{n}^{0}_{j}(t)=-\frac{\theta}{i\omega}\,n_{j0},

(23)

where
\theta=\partial_{i}v^{i}
is the fluid expansion rate.
Once the weak reactions are switched on, there is a net production
of particles which should be included in the balance equations.
To linear order in chemical imbalances, these equations read

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\delta n_{n}(t)+\theta n_{n0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{e}\mu_{\Delta_{e}}(t)-\lambda_{\mu}\mu_{\Delta_{\mu}}(t),

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\delta n_{p}(t)+\theta n_{p0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{e}\mu_{\Delta_{e}}(t)+\lambda_{\mu}\mu_{\Delta_{\mu}}(t),

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\delta n_{e}(t)+\theta n_{e0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{e}\mu_{\Delta_{e}}(t)+\lambda_{L}\mu_{\Delta}^{L}(t),

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\delta n_{\mu}(t)+\theta n_{\mu 0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu}\mu_{\Delta_{\mu}}(t)-\lambda_{L}\mu_{\Delta}^{L}(t),

(27)

where
\mu_{\Delta}^{L}\equiv\mu_{\mu}+\mu_{\nu_{e}}-\mu_{e}-\mu_{\nu_{\mu}}=\mu_{\Delta_{e}}-\mu_{\Delta_{\mu}}
is the chemical imbalance for leptons,
and
\lambda_{l}=\lambda_{n\leftrightarrow pl\bar{\nu}}+\lambda_{pl\leftrightarrow n\nu}
.
The coefficient
\lambda_{L}
is the purely leptonic analog to
\lambda_{l}
.

Solving the system of Equations (
24
)–(
27
)
is generally quite cumbersome. However, as shown in Section
4.1
,
the lepton processes proceed typically much slower than the Urca
processes in both regimes of neutrino-transparent and neutrino-trapped
matter, i.e.,
\lambda_{L}\ll\lambda_{l}
(slow lepton-equilibration
limit). As a result, the terms
\propto\lambda_{L}
can be dropped from
the balance Equations (
24
)–(
27
).
In other words, the Urca-process-driven bulk viscosity can be
computed by assuming that the weak leptonic processes are frozen.

Substituting now Equation (
21
) in Equations (
24
)
and (
26
) and putting
\lambda_{L}=0
we find

\displaystyle i\omega\delta n_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n_{n0}\theta-(\lambda_{e}+\lambda_{\mu})A_{n}\delta n_{n}+(\lambda_{e}+\lambda_{\mu})A_{p}\delta n_{p}+\lambda_{e}A_{e}\delta n_{e}

(28)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu}A_{\mu}\delta n_{\mu}-\lambda_{e}A_{\nu_{e}}\delta n_{\nu_{e}}-\lambda_{\mu}A_{\nu_{\mu}}\delta n_{\nu_{\mu}},

\displaystyle i\omega\delta n_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n_{e0}\theta+\lambda_{e}A_{n}\delta n_{n}-\lambda_{e}A_{p}\delta n_{p}-\lambda_{e}A_{e}\delta n_{e}+\lambda_{e}A_{\nu_{e}}\delta n_{\nu_{e}}.

(29)

Using the relations
\delta n_{p}+\delta n_{n}=\delta n_{B}
,

\delta n_{e}+\delta n_{\mu}=\delta n_{p}
,
\delta n_{L_{e}}=\delta n_{e}+\delta n_{\nu_{e}}
, and
\delta n_{L_{\mu}}=\delta n_{\mu}+\delta n_{\nu_{\mu}}
and solving the coupled
Equations (
28
) and (
29
) we
find (
\lambda\equiv\lambda_{e}+\lambda_{\mu}
)

\displaystyle D\delta n_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta}{i\omega}\bigg\{i\omega\left[n_{n0}(i\omega+\lambda_{e}A_{e}+\lambda_{e}A_{\nu_{e}})+n_{e0}(\lambda_{e}A_{e}+\lambda_{e}A_{\nu_{e}}-\lambda_{\mu}A_{\mu}-\lambda_{\mu}A_{\nu_{\mu}})\right]

(30)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[i\omega(\lambda A_{p}+\lambda_{\mu}A_{\mu}+\lambda_{\mu}A_{\nu_{\mu}})+\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}((A_{1}-A_{n})(A_{2}-A_{n})-A_{p}^{2})\right]n_{B0}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\lambda_{e}A_{\nu_{e}}\left[i\omega+\lambda_{\mu}(A_{\mu}+A_{\nu_{\mu}})\right]n_{L_{e}0}-\lambda_{\mu}A_{\nu_{\mu}}\left[i\omega+\lambda_{e}(A_{e}+A_{\nu_{e}})\right]n_{L_{\mu}0}\bigg\},

\displaystyle D\delta n_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta}{i\omega}\bigg\{i\omega n_{e0}\Big[i\omega+\lambda_{\mu}A_{2}+\lambda_{e}(A_{n}+A_{p})\Big]

(31)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\lambda_{e}n_{B0}\Big[A_{p}(i\omega+\lambda_{\mu}A_{2})-\lambda_{\mu}(A_{n}+A_{p})(A_{2}-A_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda_{e}n_{L_{e}0}A_{\nu_{e}}(i\omega+\lambda_{\mu}A_{2})+\lambda_{e}(A_{n}+A_{p})i\omega n_{n0}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}(A_{n}+A_{p})A_{\nu_{\mu}}n_{L_{\mu}0}\bigg\},

where we used the baryon and lepton number conservation

\delta n_{B}=-n_{B0}(\theta/i\omega)
and
\delta n_{L_{l}}=-n_{L_{l}0}(\theta/i\omega)
, and defined

\displaystyle D=(i\omega+\lambda_{e}A_{1})(i\omega+\lambda_{\mu}A_{2})-\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}(A_{n}+A_{p})^{2}

(32)

with

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}+A_{p}+A_{e}+A_{\nu_{e}},

(33)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}+A_{p}+A_{\mu}+A_{\nu_{\mu}}.

(34)

In order to find the bulk viscosity we still need to separate
the instantaneous equilibrium parts of particle densities from
perturbations (
30
) and (
31
).
Equilibrium shifts can be obtained from Equations (
30
)
and (
31
) either in the limit of
\lambda_{l}\to\infty
(fast equilibration), or in the limit of
\lambda_{l}\to 0

(slow equilibration). Both choices lead us to the same result for
the bulk viscosity as the latter vanishes in both limits of
fast or slow equilibration.
Subtracting thus the local quasi-equilibrium shifts
\delta n_{j}^{0}

from Equations (
30
) and (
31
) we find the
required nonequilibrium parts
\delta n^{\prime}_{j}=\delta n_{j}-\delta n^{0}_{j}
.
After this the nonequilibrium part of the pressure,
referred to as bulk viscous pressure, will be given by

\displaystyle\Pi=\sum_{j}c_{j}\delta n^{\prime}_{j},

(35)

with

\displaystyle c_{j}\equiv\frac{\partial p}{\partial n_{j}}=\sum_{i}n_{i0}\frac{\partial\mu_{i}}{\partial n_{j}}=\sum_{i}n_{i0}A_{ij}.

(36)

Here we used the Gibbs–Duhem relation
dp=sdT+\sum_{i}n_{i}d\mu_{i}
,
and recalled the definitions (
22
).
The bulk viscous pressure then reads

\displaystyle\Pi=\frac{\theta}{i\omega}\frac{i\omega(\lambda_{e}C_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{\mu}C_{2}^{2})+\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}\big[A_{1}C_{2}^{2}+A_{2}C_{1}^{2}-2(A_{n}+A_{p})C_{1}C_{2}\big]}{(i\omega+\lambda_{e}A_{1})(i\omega+\lambda_{\mu}A_{2})-\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}(A_{n}+A_{p})^{2}},

(37)

where we defined

\displaystyle c_{n}-c_{p}-c_{e}+c_{\nu_{e}}=n_{n0}A_{n}-n_{p0}A_{p}-n_{e0}A_{e}+n_{\nu_{e}0}A_{\nu_{e}}\equiv C_{1},

(38)

\displaystyle c_{n}-c_{p}-c_{\mu}+c_{\nu_{\mu}}=n_{n0}A_{n}-n_{p0}A_{p}-n_{\mu 0}A_{\mu}+n_{\nu_{\mu}0}A_{\nu_{\mu}}\equiv C_{2}.

(39)

Extracting the real part of Equation (
37
) and recalling the
definition of the bulk viscosity
{\rm Re}\Pi=-\zeta\theta
we find

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}\Big\{\lambda_{e}\left[(A_{n}+A_{p})C_{1}-A_{1}C_{2}\right]^{2}+\lambda_{\mu}\left[(A_{n}+A_{p})C_{2}-A_{2}C_{1}\right]^{2}\Big\}+\omega^{2}(\lambda_{e}C_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{\mu}C_{2}^{2})}{\Big\{\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}\left[A_{1}A_{2}-(A_{n}+A_{p})^{2}\right]-\omega^{2}\Big\}^{2}+\omega^{2}(\lambda_{e}A_{1}+\lambda_{\mu}A_{2})^{2}}.

(40)

If we neglect the muonic contribution then we arrive at

\displaystyle\zeta_{e}=\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{A_{1}}\frac{\gamma_{e}}{\omega^{2}+\gamma_{e}^{2}},

(41)

with
\gamma_{e}=\lambda_{e}A_{1}
, which coincides with
the result of Ref.
Alford et al. 2019
.

In the limit of high frequencies
\omega\gg\lambda_{l}A_{i}

we find from Equation (
40
)

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{\lambda_{e}C_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{\mu}C_{2}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}=\zeta_{e}+\zeta_{\mu},

(42)

where
\zeta_{e}
and
\zeta_{\mu}
are the partial bulk viscosities by
electronic and muonic Urca processes, respectively
Haensel et al. 2000
.

In the opposite limit of low frequencies we find

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{\lambda_{e}(C_{1}-a_{1}C_{2})^{2}+\lambda_{\mu}(C_{2}-a_{2}C_{1})^{2}}{\lambda_{e}\lambda_{\mu}(A_{n}+A_{p})^{2}(a_{1}a_{2}-1)^{2}},

(43)

with
a_{1}=A_{1}/(A_{n}+A_{p})
and
a_{2}=A_{2}/(A_{n}+A_{p})
.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we provided a brief review of our work on the
Urca-process-driven bulk viscosity of relativistic
npe\mu
matter in
the parameter range relevant to binary neutron star mergers. We
focused on the semi-leptonic Urca processes as well as leptonic
processes in
npe\mu
matter in two different regimes of interest:
(a) neutrino-transparent regime where
T\leq T_{\rm tr}
with

T_{\rm tr}\simeq 5\div 10
MeV being the neutrino-trapping
temperature; and (b) neutrino-trapped regime at
T\geq T_{\rm tr}
.
Along with the results for the bulk viscosity in the
neutrino-trapped regime obtained earlier in Ref.
Alford et al. 2021
,
we showed novel results for the neutrino-transparent matter as well
as some results pertaining to the case of isentropic instead of
isothermal oscillations.

Our main observations can be summarized as follows:

(a)

We observe that the leptonic reactions proceed much slower than the
Urca process in the entire temperature-density range.
In the neutrino-transparent matter the dominant leptonic reaction
is the muon decay, whereas
in the neutrino-trapped regime, the dominant leptonic reactions
are the neutrino and antineutrino absorption processes.

(b)

As a result, the bulk viscosity of
npe\mu
matter can be computed assuming
that the leptonic processes are frozen. Qualitatively, the bulk viscosity of

npe\mu

npe
matter by factors from 2.5 to 8 above
the maximum temperature in the
\nu

\nu

(c)

The bulk viscosity features its resonant maximum at a temperature
where the average
\beta
-relaxation rate of electronic and muonic Urca
processes coincides with the angular frequency of density oscillations.
This resonant maximum appears around
T\simeq 5
MeV where the matter is
still transparent to neutrinos. At higher temperatures, bulk viscosity drops
rapidly once the matter enters the neutrino-trapped regime. There
appear sharp minima in the bulk viscosity at
T\geq 30
MeV where the
lepton fractions become independent of the density.

(d)

Using our results for the bulk viscosity we estimate the bulk viscous
damping timescales of density oscillations in neutron star mergers. We
find that for typical oscillation frequencies
1\leq f\leq 10
kHz there
is a finite temperature-density range where the bulk viscous dissipation
can affect the short-term evolution (
\simeq
10 ms) of BNS mergers
significantly. The damping timescale features a minimum at
T\simeq 5
MeV
with minimum values of the order of ms at very low densities
n_{B}\leq n_{0}
.
At higher densities, the damping timescales of density oscillations are larger
and can affect the post-merger evolution only on a long-time scale. At high
temperatures where neutrinos are trapped in the matter, the damping timescales
are much longer; therefore, the Urca processes are not the dominant
channels by which
to damp the density oscillations in BNS mergers.

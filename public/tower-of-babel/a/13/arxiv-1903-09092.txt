Variation of the first eigenvalue of ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian along the Ricci-harmonic flow
Shahroud Azami

Abstract
. In this paper, we study monotonicity for the first eigenvalue of a class of ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian. We find the first variation formula for the first eigenvalue of ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian on a closed Riemannian manifold evolving by the Ricci-harmonic flow and construct various monotic quantities by imposing some conditions on initial manifold.

1.
Introduction

On the other hand, the study on eigenvalue problem has received remarkable attention. Recently, many mathematicians considered the eigenvalue problem of geometric operators under various geometric flows, because it is a very powerful tool for the understanding Riemannain manifold. The fundamental study of this works began when Perelman
[
10
]
showed that the functional

F=\int_{M}(R+|\nabla f|^{2})e^{-f}\,d\mu

is nondecreasing along the Ricci flow coupled to a backward heat-type equation, where
R
is the scalar curvature with respect to the metric
g(t)
and
d\mu
denotes the volume form of the metric
g(t)
.
The nondecreasing of the functional
F
implies that the first eigenvalue of the geometric operator
-4\Delta+R
is nondecreasing under the Ricci flow.
Then, Li
[
7
]
and Zeng with et’al
[
12
]
extended the geometric operator
-4\Delta+R
to the operator
-\Delta+cR
and studied the monotonicity of eigenvalues of the operator
-\Delta+cR
along Ricci flow and the Ricci-Bourguignon flow, respectively.

Also, in
[
1
,
11
,
13
]
has been investigated the evolution for the first eigenvalue of
p
-Laplacian along the Ricci-harmonic flow, Ricci flow and mth mean curvature flow, respectively. A generalization of
p
-Laplacian is a class of
(p,q)
-Laplacian which has applications in physic and related sciences such as non-Newtonnian fluids, pseudoplastics
[
4
,
5
]
and we introduce it in later section.

On the other hand, geometric flows for instance, Ricci-harmonic flow have been a topic of active research interest in mathematics and physics. A geometric flow is an evolution of a geometric structure. Let
M
be a closed
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian metric
g_{0}
. Hamilton for the first time in
1982
introduced the Ricci flow as follows

\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2Ric(g(t)),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,g(0)=g_{0},

where
Ric
is the Ricci tensor of
g(t)
. The Ricci flow has been proved to be a very useful tool to improve metrics in Riemannian geometry, when
M
is compact. Now, let
(M^{m},g)
and
(N^{n},\gamma)
be closed Riemannain manifolds. By Nash’s embedding theorem, assume that
N
is isometrically embedded into Euclidean space
e_{N}:(N^{n},\gamma)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for a sufficiently large
d
. We identify maps
\phi:M\to N
with
e_{N}\circ\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Müller
[
9
]
considered a generalization of Ricci flow as

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2Ric(g(t))+2\alpha\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi,&g(0)=g_{0},\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=\tau_{g}\phi&\phi(0)=\phi_{0},\end{cases}

where
\alpha
is a positive coupling constant,
\phi(t)
is a family of smooth maps from
M
to some closed target manifold
N
and
\tau_{g}\phi
is the intrinsic Laplacian of
\phi
which denotes the tension field of
\phi
with respect to the evolving metric

. This evolution equation system called Ricci flow coupled with harmonic map flow or
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow for short. Müller in
[

]
shown that system (

) has unique solution with initial data
(g(0),\phi(0))=(g_{0},\phi_{0})
. Also, the normalized

flow defined as

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2Ric(g(t))+2\alpha\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi+\frac{2}{m}rg(t),&g(0)=g_{0},\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=\tau_{g}\phi&\phi(0)=\phi_{0},\end{cases}

where
r=\frac{\int_{M}(R-\alpha|\nabla\phi|^{2})d\mu}{\int_{M}d\mu}
is the
average of
R-\alpha|\nabla\phi|^{2}
. Under this normalized flow, the
volume of the solution metrics remains constant in time.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Eigenvalues of
p
-Laplacian

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold and
f:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function on
M
or
f\in W^{1,p}(M)
. The Laplace-Beltrami operator acting on a smooth function
f
on
M
is the divergence of gradient of
f
, written as

\Delta f=div(grad\,f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\partial_{i}(\sqrt{\det g}\,\partial_{j}f)

where
\partial_{i}f=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{i}}
.
The
p
-Laplacian of
f
for
1<p<\infty
is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\triangle_{p}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle div(|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla f|^{p-2}\Delta f+(p-2)|\nabla f|^{p-4}(Hessf)(\nabla f,\nabla f)

where

(Hessf)(X,Y)=\nabla(\nabla f)(X,Y)=Y.(X.f)-(\nabla_{Y}X).f,\,\,\,\,X,Y\in\mathcal{X}(M)

and in local coordinate, we get

(Hessf)(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k}f.

Notice that when
p=2
,
p
-Laplacian is the Laplace-Beltrami operator.
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold.
In this paper, we consider the nonlinear system introduced in
[
6
]
, that is

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u=-\lambda|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}v\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,M\\
\Delta_{q}v=-\lambda|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}u\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,M\\
(u,v)\in W^{1,p}(M)\times W^{1,q}(M)\end{cases}

where
p>1,\,\,q>1
and
\alpha,\beta
are real numbers satisfying

(2.3)

\alpha>0,\,\beta>0,\,\,\,\,\frac{\alpha+1}{p}+\frac{\beta+1}{q}=1.

In (
2.2
),
we say that

\lambda
is an eigenvalue whenever for some
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(M)
and
v\in W_{0}^{1,q}(M)
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p-2}<\nabla u,\nabla\phi>d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}v\phi d\mu,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q-2}<\nabla v,\nabla\psi>d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}u\psi d\mu,

where
\phi\in W^{1,p}(M)
,
\psi\in W^{1,q}(M)
and
W_{0}^{1,p}(M)
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
in Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(M)
. The pair
(u,v)
is called a eigenfunctions corresponding to eigenvalue
\lambda
. A first positive eigenvalue of (
2.2
) obtained as

inf\{A(u,v):(u,v)\in W_{0}^{1,p}(M)\times W_{0}^{1,q}(M),\,\,B(u,v)=1\}

where

\displaystyle A(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q}d\mu,

\displaystyle B(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu.

Let
(M^{m},g(t),\phi(t))
be a solution of the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow (
1.1
) on
the smooth manifold
(M^{m},g_{0},\phi_{0})
in the interval
[0,T)
then

(2.6)

\lambda(t)=\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu_{t}+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q}d\mu_{t},

defines the evolution of an eigenvalue of (
2.2
), under the
variation of
(g(t),\phi(t))
where the eigenfunctions associated to
\lambda(t)

is normalized that is
B(u,v)=1
.
Motivated by the above works, in this paper we will study the first eigenvalue of a class of
(p,q)
-Laplacian (
2.2
) whose metric satisfies the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow. Throughout of paper we write
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\partial_{t}u=u^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{S}=Ric_{g}-\alpha\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi
,
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=Ric_{ij}-\alpha\nabla_{i}\phi\nabla_{j}\phi
and
S=R-\alpha|\nabla\phi|^{2}
.

3.
Variation of
\lambda(t)

In this section, we will give some useful evolution formulas for
\lambda(t)
under the Ricci-harmonic flow. Now, we give a useful statement about the variation of the first eigenvalue of (
2.2
) under the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are two metrics on Riemannian manifold
M^{m}
which satisfy
(1+\epsilon)^{-1}g_{1}<g_{2}<(1+\epsilon)g_{1}
then for any
p\geq q>1
, we have

\lambda(g_{2})-\lambda(g_{1})\leq\left((1+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+m}{2}}-(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{m}{2}}\right)\lambda(g_{1})

in particular,
\lambda(t)
is a continues function respect to
t
-variable.

Proof.

By direct computation we complete the proof of lemma. In local coordinate we have
d\mu=\sqrt{\det g}\,dx^{1}\wedge...\wedge dx^{m}
, therefore

(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{m}{2}}d\mu_{g_{1}}<d\mu_{g_{2}}<(1+\epsilon)^{\frac{m}{2}}d\mu_{g_{1}}.

Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle G(g,u,v)=\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{g}^{p}d\mu_{g}+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla v|_{g}^{q}d\mu_{g},

then

\displaystyle\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}G(g_{2},u,v)-\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{2}}G(g_{1},u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{g_{2}}^{p}d\mu_{g_{2}}-\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{g_{1}}^{p}d\mu_{g_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}-\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{2}}\right)\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{g_{1}}^{p}d\mu_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla v|_{g_{2}}^{q}d\mu_{g_{2}}-\int_{M}|\nabla v|_{g_{1}}^{q}d\mu_{g_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}-\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{2}}\right)\int_{M}|\nabla v|_{g_{1}}^{q}d\mu_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\left((1+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+m}{2}}-(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{m}{2}}\right)\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|_{g_{1}}^{p}d\mu_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\left((1+\epsilon)^{\frac{q+m}{2}}-(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{m}{2}}\right)\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}\int_{M}|\nabla v|_{g_{1}}^{q}d\mu_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left((1+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+m}{2}}-(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{m}{2}}\right)G(g_{1},u,v)\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu_{g_{1}}.

Since the eigenfunctions corresponding to
\lambda(t)
are normalized, thus we get

\lambda(g_{2})-\lambda(g_{1})\leq\left((1+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+m}{2}}-(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{m}{2}}\right)\lambda(g_{1})

this completes the proof of Lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(g(t),\phi(t)),\,\,t\in[0,T)
, be a solution of the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow on a closed manifold
M^{m}
and let
\lambda(t)
be the first eigenvalue of the
(p,q)
-Laplacian along this flow. Then for any
t_{0},\,t_{1}\in[0,T)
and
t_{1}>t_{0}
, we have

(3.2)

\lambda(t_{1})\geq\lambda(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\mathcal{G}(g(\tau),u(\tau),v(\tau))d\tau

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g(t),u(t),v(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha+1)\int_{M}\left(\mathcal{S}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+<\nabla u^{\prime},\nabla u>\right)|\nabla u|^{p-2}d\mu

\displaystyle+(\beta+1)\int_{M}\left(\mathcal{S}(\nabla v,\nabla v)+<\nabla v^{\prime},\nabla v>\right)|\nabla v|^{q-2}d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}Sd\mu-\frac{\beta+1}{q}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q}Sd\mu.

Proof.

Assume that

G(g(t),u(t),v(t))=\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla u(t)|_{g(t)}^{p}d\mu_{g(t)}+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla v(t)|_{g(t)}^{q}d\mu_{g(t)},

at time
t_{1}
we first let
(u_{1},v_{1})=(u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))
be the eigenfunctions for the eigenvalue
\lambda(t_{1})
of
(p,q)
-Laplacian. We consider the following smooth functions

h(t)=u_{1}\left[\frac{\det[g_{ij}(t_{1})]}{\det[g_{ij}(t)]}\right]^{\frac{1}{2(\alpha+\beta+1)}},\qquad l(t)=v_{1}\left[\frac{\det[g_{ij}(t_{1})]}{\det[g_{ij}(t)]}\right]^{\frac{1}{2(\alpha+\beta+1)}},

along the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow. We define

u(t)=\frac{h(t)}{\left(\int_{M}|h(t)|^{\alpha}|l(t)|^{\beta}h(t)l(t)d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}},\qquad u(t)=\frac{l(t)}{\left(\int_{M}|h(t)|^{\alpha}|l(t)|^{\beta}h(t)l(t)d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}

which
u(t),\,v(t)
are smooth functions under the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow, satisfy

\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu=1,

and at time
t_{1}
,
(u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))
are the eigenfunctions for
\lambda(t_{1})
of
(p,q)
-Laplacian at time
t_{1}
i.e.

\lambda(t_{1})=G(g(t_{1}),u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))
. If
f
is a smooth function respect to time
t
then along the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left(|\nabla f|^{p}\right)=\frac{p}{2}\left[\partial_{t}g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f+2g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f^{\prime}\nabla_{j}f\right]|\nabla f|^{p-2}

by (
1.1
) we have
\partial_{t}g^{ij}=2g^{ik}g^{jl}\mathcal{S}_{kl}
, therefore

(3.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(|\nabla f|^{p}\right)=p|\nabla f|^{p-2}\bigg(\mathcal{S}(\nabla f,\nabla f)+<\nabla f^{\prime},\nabla f>\bigg),

and

\partial_{t}d\mu=\frac{1}{2}tr_{g}(\partial_{t}g)d\mu=-Sd\mu.

Since
u(t)
and
v(t)
are smooth functions, hence
G(g(t),u(t),v(t))
is a smooth function with respect to
t
. If we set

(3.5)

\mathcal{G}(g(t),u(t),v(t)):=\frac{d}{dt}G(g(t),u(t),v(t)),

then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g(t),u(t),v(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha+1)\int_{M}\left(\mathcal{S}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+<\nabla u^{\prime},\nabla u>\right)|\nabla u|^{p-2}d\mu

\displaystyle+(\beta+1)\int_{M}\left(\mathcal{S}(\nabla v,\nabla v)+<\nabla v^{\prime},\nabla v>\right)|\nabla v|^{q-2}d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}Sd\mu-\frac{\beta+1}{q}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q}Sd\mu.

Taking integration on the both sides of (
3.5
) between
t_{0}
and
t_{1}
, we conclude that

(3.7)

G(g(t_{1}),u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))-G(g(t_{0}),u(t_{0}),v(t_{0}))=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\mathcal{G}(g(\tau),u(\tau),v(\tau))d\tau

where
t_{0}\in[0,T)
and
t_{1}>t_{0}
. Noticing
G(g(t_{0}),u(t_{0}),v(t_{0}))\geq\lambda(t_{0})
and plugin
\lambda(t_{1})=G(g(t_{1}),u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))
in (
3.7
), yields (
3.2
) and
\mathcal{G}(g(t),u(t),v(t))
satisfies in (
3.3
).
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(M^{m},g(t),\phi(t))
be a solution of the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow on the smooth closed manifold
(M^{m},g_{0},\phi_{0})
and
\lambda(t)
denotes the
evolution of the first eigenvalue under the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow. Suppose that
k=\min\{p,q\}
and

(3.8)

\mathcal{S}-\frac{1}{k}Sg\geq 0\,\,\text{in}\,\,M^{m}\times[0,T).

If
S_{\min}(0)\geq 0
, then

\lambda(t)
is nondecreasing and differentiable almost everywhere along the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow on
[0,T)
.

Proof.

Let for any
t_{1}\in[0,T)
,

u(t_{1}),\,\,\,v(t_{1})
be the eigenfunctions for
\lambda(t_{1})
of
(p,q)
-Laplacian. Then
\int_{M}|u(t_{1})|^{\alpha}|v(t_{1})|^{\beta}u(t_{1})v(t_{1})d\mu_{g(t_{1})}=1
and

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g(t_{1}),u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha+1)\int_{M}\left(\mathcal{S}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+<\nabla u^{\prime},\nabla u>\right)|\nabla u|^{p-2}d\mu

\displaystyle+(\beta+1)\int_{M}\left(\mathcal{S}(\nabla v,\nabla v)+<\nabla v^{\prime},\nabla v>\right)|\nabla v|^{q-2}d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}Sd\mu-\frac{\beta+1}{q}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q}Sd\mu.

Now, by the time derivative of the condition

\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu=1

we can get

(3.10)

(\alpha+1)\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}u^{\prime}vd\mu+(\beta+1)\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uv^{\prime}d\mu=\int_{M}S|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu.

On the other hand, (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) imply that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{M}<\nabla u^{\prime},\nabla u>|\nabla u|^{p-2}d\mu=\lambda(t_{1})\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}u^{\prime}vd\mu,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{M}<\nabla v^{\prime},\nabla v>|\nabla v|^{q-2}d\mu=\lambda(t_{1})\int_{M}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uv^{\prime}d\mu.

Therefore from (
3.10
), (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) we have

\displaystyle(\alpha+1)\int_{M}<\nabla u^{\prime},\nabla u>|\nabla u|^{p-2}d\mu+(\beta+1)\int_{M}<\nabla v^{\prime},\nabla v>|\nabla v|^{q-2}d\mu

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\lambda(t_{1})\int_{M}S|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu,

and the replacing (
3.13
) in (
3.9
), results that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g(t_{1}),u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(t_{1})\int_{M}S|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu+(\alpha+1)\int_{M}\mathcal{S}(\nabla u,\nabla u)|\nabla u|^{p-2}d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}Sd\mu+(\beta+1)\int_{M}\mathcal{S}(\nabla v,\nabla v)|\nabla v|^{q-2}d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta+1}{q}\Large\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q}Sd\mu.

From (
3.14
) and (
3.8
) we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g(t_{1}),u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda(t_{1})\int_{M}S|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}uvd\mu+(\alpha+1)(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p})\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}Sd\mu

\displaystyle+(\beta+1)(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{q})\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{q}Sd\mu.

Since

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}S=\Delta S+2|\mathcal{S}_{ij}|^{2}+2\alpha|\tau_{g}\phi|^{2}

and
|\mathcal{S}_{ij}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{m}S^{2}
, it follows that

(3.16)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}S\geq\Delta S+\frac{2}{m}S^{2}.

The solution to

\frac{d}{dt}y(t)=\frac{2}{m}y^{2}(t),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,y(t)=S_{\min}(0),

is

(3.17)

y(t)=\frac{S_{\min}(0)}{1-\frac{2}{m}S_{\min}(0)t},\,\,\,\,\,\,t\in[0,T^{\prime}),

where
T^{\prime}=\min\{T,\frac{m}{2S_{\min}(0)}\}
. Using maximum principle to (
3.16
), we get

along the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow. If

then the nonnegativity of

preserved along the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow. Therefore (
3.15
) becomes
\mathcal{G}(g(t_{1}),u(t_{1}),v(t_{1}))\geq 0
.
Thus we get
\mathcal{G}(g(t),u(t),v(t))>0
in any small enough neighborhood of
t_{1}
. Hence
\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\mathcal{G}(g(\tau),u(\tau),v(\tau))d\tau>0
for any
t_{0}<t_{1}
sufficiently close to
t_{1}
. Since
t_{1}\in[0,T)
is arbitrary the Proposition
3.2

is increasing and continues on the interval
[0,T)
, the classical Lebesgue’s theorem (see
[
8

),
\lambda(t)
is differentiable almost everywhere on
[0,T)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(M^{m},g(t),\phi(t))
be a solution of the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow
on the smooth closed manifold
(M^{m},g_{0},\phi_{0})
. If
\lambda(t)
denotes the
evolution of the first eigenvalue under the
(RH)_{\alpha}
flow, then

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{d\lambda}{dt}(u,v,t)|_{t=t_{1}}

\displaystyle=

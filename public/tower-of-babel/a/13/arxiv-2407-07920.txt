On generalized Stirling numbers and zeta values
Kamel Mezlini & Tahar Moumni & Najib Ouled Azaiez

Abstract
The generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind together with the Stirling numbers of the first kind are used to present a novel method to approximate the Riemann zeta function at integer values by rationals. We show that the error committed in such approximation decay exponentially to zero.

Introduction:

Zeta functions of various sorts are all-pervasive objects in modern number theory
and an ever recurring-theme is the role played by their special values at integral arguments. First introduced by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, the zeta function extends
complex analysis to investigate the distribution of prime numbers
and holds paramount significance in the study of the distribution of
prime numbers.

The prototypical result on special values
is the theorem that

\zeta(2k)={\rm rational\;number}\times\pi^{2k},

for
k

a non-negative integer.
While the analytical properties of the Riemann zeta function have
been extensively studied, practical computation of its values,
especially at odd integer arguments, remains a challenging task due to
the intricate nature of the function. The problem of finding
efficient methods to evaluate the Riemann zeta function at integers
has been considered by many authors (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
3
,
8
,
12
,
15
]
). Particularly,
approximating zeta values by rationals, remains one of the most
subject investigated by many authors.
But all approximations results deals
with some particular and finite sets of zeta values.

This paper introduces a novel method for approximating
zeta values
\zeta(p)
by rationals
for all and not particular positive integer
p
. Our approach
draws inspiration from the rich field of combinatorics, and uses surprising
Stirling numbers of the first kind together with generalized Stiriling numbers of the second kind, proven to be powerful tools in expressing and
manipulating many usueful mathematical functions. Due to this fact, Stiriling numbers form an important
sequence of rational approximation scheme for the Riemann zeta
function.
In
[
11
]
the only approximation of
\zeta(p)
for odd positive integer
p
deals with
\zeta(5)
with an error term which decay to zero as
\exp(-\log(2)N)
. However, our Theorem
7
provide an approximation of zeta values

\zeta(p)
for any odd positive integer
p
with an error term which decay to zero as
N\exp(-N)

and so more faster.

In section
2
, we give the horizontal and vertical generating function associated to the generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind, we also give their integral representation, and a recurrence relation.

In Section
3
, we present an important relationship between the generalized Stirling numbers and zeta functions. Our aim is to approximate zeta values by sequence of rationals,
See Theorem
7
.

2
Generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind

The classical Stirling numbers of the first kind
s(n,k)
, and of
the second kind
S(n,k)
can be defined by the following generating
functions (see [
[
7
]
, Chapter 5]):

\frac{\ln^{k}(1+t)}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}s(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\;|t|<1,\;\;k=0,1,2,....

(1)

\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\;t\in\mathbb{R},\;\;k=0,1,2,....

(2)

The generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by the following explicit formula:

S_{n}^{p}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}}{(k+1)^{p}},\;\;n=0,1,2,...,p=1,2,....

(3)

Theorem 1
.

The horizontal generating function associated to the generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{n}^{p}
is given by:

e^{-t}{}{}_{p}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;t\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}S_{n}^{p}t^{n},\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

(4)

Proof.

Recall that the generalized hypergeometric
function is defined by,
see
[
5
]

{}_{p}F_{q}\left[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{p};b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{q};z\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{m=1}^{p}\left(a_{m}\right)_{k}z^{k}}{\prod_{m=1}^{q}\left(b_{m}\right)_{k}k!},

(5)

where
(c)_{n}
is the shifted factorial defined by

(c)_{0}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad(c)_{n}=c(c+1)\cdots(c+n-1)\quad\text{ for }n=1,2,\ldots

Expanding both functions of the left hand side of
(
4
) into their entire series to obtain

\displaystyle e^{-t}{}_{p}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;t\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}t^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!(k+1)^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{n+k}}{k!(k+1)^{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!k!(k+1)^{p}})t^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}S_{n}^{p}t^{n}.

∎

Using the fact that

\binom{n}{k-1}=\frac{k}{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k},

we may write

S_{n}^{p}=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}}{k^{p-1}}=S(1-p,n+1),

where
S(\alpha,n)
is the Stirling number of negative order defined by Butzer, Kilbas and Trujillo (see
[
6
]
).

Using (
5
) we can check that
S_{n}^{p}
can be expressed as a hypergeometric function as:

S_{n}^{p}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}{}_{p+1}F_{p}\left[-n,1,...,1;2,...,2;1\right].

Theorem 2
.

The generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind
have the integral representation

S_{n}^{p}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!(p-1)!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-e^{-t})^{n}e^{-t}t^{p-1}dt.

(6)

Proof.

By substituting Euler’s integral:

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t(k+1)}t^{p-1}dt=\frac{(p-1)!}{(k+1)^{p}},

(7)

in (
3
), the result (
6
) follows.∎

Remark 1
.

From the integral representation (
6
),we have the following properties:

\forall\;\;n\in\mathbb{N},\;\;|S_{n}^{p}|\leq\frac{1}{n!}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n!S_{n}^{p}=0.

(8)

Integration by parts of the integral in (
6
)
produces the recurrence for
S_{n}^{p}.

Theorem 3
.

The generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind
satisfy the recurrence relation

(n+1)S_{n}^{p+1}=S_{n}^{p}-S_{n-1}^{p+1},\;p=2,3,...,

with

S_{0}^{p}=1;\;S_{n}^{1}=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}.

Proof.

Integration by parts of the integral in
(
6
) produces

S_{n}^{p}=-\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n-1)!p!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-e^{-t})^{n-1}e^{-2t}t^{p}dt+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!p!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-e^{-t})^{n}e^{-t}t^{p}dt.

(9)

So,

S_{n}^{p}=-\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n-1)!p!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-e^{-t})^{n-1}(e^{-t}-1+1)e^{-t}t^{p}dt+S_{n}^{p+1}

(10)

then
S_{n}^{p}
can be expressed in the form

S_{n}^{p}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n-1)!p!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-e^{-t})^{n}e^{-t}t^{p}dt-\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n-1)!p!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-e^{-t})^{n-1}e^{-t}t^{p}dt+S_{n}^{p+1}

(11)

From (
11

6
), we deduce

S_{n}^{p}=(n+1)S_{n}^{p+1}+S_{n-1}^{p+1}.

∎

Theorem 4
.

The generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind
can be defined by the following vertical generating function:

The
S_{n}^{p}
sequence and zeta function

In this section, we present a relation between the generalized Stirling numbers and zeta functions.

3.1
The
S_{n}^{p}
sequence and Riemann zeta values

To establish the relation between the
S_{n}^{p}
-sequence and zeta
values we recall first the following result established in

[
10
]

Theorem 5
.

For
p\geq 2
and for all
t\geq 0,
we have

{}_{p}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;t\right]=e^{t}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)!t^{n+p}}\gamma(n+p,t).

(18)

where
\gamma
denoting the incomplete Gamma function.

We note also that the Laplace transform of the generalized
hypergeometric function is given by, see
[
9
]

\begin{gathered}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}{}_{p}F_{q}\left[a_{1},...,a_{p};b_{1},...,b_{q};t\right]dt=\frac{1}{s}{}_{p+1}F_{q}\left[1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{p};b_{1},\ldots,b_{q};s^{-1}\right].\\
{[p\leq q]}\end{gathered}

(19)

We recall also that the polylogarithm is related to the generalized
hypergeometric function as the following, (see
[
1
]
page
106)

\mathrm{Li}_{p}(x)=x{}_{p+1}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;x\right]

(20)

and it is related to the Riemann zeta function as follows,

\zeta(p)=\mathrm{Li}_{p}(1).

(21)

Theorem 6
.

The following equality holds true for all integer
p\geq 2
and for
all positive real parameter
R.

\zeta(p)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{S_{n}^{p}R^{n+1}}{n+1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)!}\left(\frac{\gamma(n+p,R)}{(n+p-1)R^{n+p-1}}+e^{-R}\right).

(22)

Proof.

To prove (
22
) we use (
21
) and
(
20
) together with (
19
), to obtain

\displaystyle\zeta(p)\;=\;\mathrm{Li}_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{p+1}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;1\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}{}_{p}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;t\right]dt,

or equivalently, using (
4
) and (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}e^{-t}{}_{p}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;t\right]dt+\int_{R}^{+\infty}e^{-t}{}_{p}F_{p}\left[1,...,1;2,...,2;t\right]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{0}^{R}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}S_{n}^{p}t^{n}dt}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{R}^{+\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)!t^{n+p}}\gamma(n+p,t)dt}_{I_{2}}.

By (
8
), we have
|S_{n}^{p}|\leq\frac{1}{n!}
, from which
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|S_{n}^{p}|t^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}dt=\int_{0}^{R}e^{t}dt=e^{R}-1.

This fact justifies the interchange of integration and summation in
I_{1}
, and we have

I_{1}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{S_{n}^{p}}{n+1}R^{n+1}.

By the monotone convergence theorem, we interchange summation and
integration in
I_{2}
to obtain:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)!}\underbrace{\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{\gamma(n+p,t)}{t^{n+p}}dt}_{J}.

Integrating
J
by part, we obtain

\displaystyle{J=\frac{\gamma(n+p,R)}{(n+p-1)R^{n+p-1}}+\frac{e^{-R}}{n+p-1}}.

(23)

Consequently

\displaystyle{I_{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!}\left(\frac{\gamma(n+p,R)}{R^{n+p-1}}+e^{-R}\right)}

(24)

which complete the proof of
(
22
).
∎

3.2
Approximating zeta values by rationals

Our aim now is to use (
22
) to approximate zeta values by
sequence of rationals. To do so, we define the sequence as follows:
For each integer
N\geq p
,

\zeta_{N}(p)=\sum_{n=0}^{4N}\frac{S_{n}^{p}}{n+1}N^{n+1}+\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)N^{n+p-1}},

(25)

and we show that the error term
|\zeta(p)-\zeta_{N}(p)|
decay exponentially to zero for large
N
. Hence the value of
\zeta(p)
is approximated by
(
25
).

Theorem 7
.

Their exist a positive constant
C
such that for large
N
,
we have the estimation

|\zeta(p)-\zeta_{N}(p)|\leq CNe^{-N}

Proof.

For larger integer
N
, and by taking
R=N
in (
22
), we get

\begin{split}\zeta(p)-\zeta_{N}(p)=\underbrace{\sum_{n=4N+1}^{+\infty}\frac{S_{n}^{p}N^{n+1}}{n+1}}_{\epsilon_{1}}-\underbrace{\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1){N^{n+p-1}}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma(n+p,N)}{(n+p-1)!}\right)}_{\epsilon_{2}}&+\\
\underbrace{\sum_{n=N-p+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)N^{n+p-1}}\frac{\gamma(n+p,N)}{(n+p-1)!}}_{\epsilon_{3}}+\underbrace{e^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!}}_{\epsilon_{4}}.\end{split}

(26)

Using (
8
), we deduce that the first series in the right hand side of
(
26
) is estimated as follows

\epsilon_{1}=\displaystyle{\left|\sum_{n=4N+1}^{+\infty}\frac{S_{n}^{p}}{n+1}N^{n+1}\right|}\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{n=4N+1}^{+\infty}\frac{N^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}},

and the error term is bounded by

\epsilon_{1}\leq\displaystyle{\frac{N^{4N+2}}{(4N+2)!}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{n}}=\displaystyle{\frac{4N^{4N+2}}{3(4N+2)!}}.

Using the following well known bounds, valid for all positive integers
n

\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n+1}}<n!<\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n}},

we have

\displaystyle{\frac{4N^{4N+2}}{3(4N+2)!}}\leq\frac{4}{3\sqrt{2\pi(4N+2)}}\left(\frac{eN}{4N+2}\right)^{4N+2}e^{-\frac{1}{12(4N+2)+1}},

(27)

\epsilon_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\frac{e}{4}\right)^{4N}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-N}.

Hence
\epsilon_{1}
decay exponentially to
0
when
N
goes to infinity.
To proceed further, we recall the following upper bound of the
Stirling number of the first kind valid for
m\;=\;1,\;\cdots\;,\;n-1,
see
[
2
]

\displaystyle{|s(n+1,m+1)|\leq\frac{n!(\log n)^{m}}{m!}\left(1+\frac{m}{\log n}\right)}.

(28)

Using the following equality found in
[
14
]

\frac{\gamma(n+p,N)}{(n+p-1)!}=e^{-N}N^{n+p}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{N^{j}}{(n+p+j)!}.

(29)

Since
n>N-p
in series defining
\epsilon_{3}
, and using the
following inequality

(n+p+j)!\geq(n+p)^{j}(n+p-1)!,

(30)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(n+p,N)}{(n+p-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-N}N^{n+p}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{N^{j}}{(n+p)^{j}(n+p-1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-N}N^{n+p}}{(n+p-1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{N}{n+p}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-N}N^{n+p}(n+p)}{(n+p-1)!(n+p-N)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-N}\sum_{n=N-p+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)N^{n+p}(n+p)}{(n+p-1)N^{n+p-1}(n+p-1)!(n+p-N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ne^{-N}\sum_{n=N-p+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)(n+p)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!(n+p-N)}.

By (
28
),

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{Ne^{-N}}{(p-2)!}\sum_{n=N-p+1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+p)(\log(n+p-2))^{p-2}}{(n+p-1)^{2}(n+p-N)}\left(1+\frac{p-2}{\log(n+p-2)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{\beta Ne^{-N}}{(p-2)!}},

where

\displaystyle{\beta=\sum_{n=N-p+1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+p)(\log(n+p-2))^{p-2}}{(n+p-1)^{2}(n+p-N)}\left(1+\frac{p-2}{\log(n+p-2)}\right)}.
This prove that
\epsilon_{3}
decay exponentially
to
0.

Using again (
28
) and by similar technics used to give
an upper bound for
\epsilon_{3}
we can show that

\displaystyle\epsilon_{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{e^{-N}}{(p-2)!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\log(n+p-2))^{p-2}}{(n+p-1)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{p-2}{\log(n+p-2)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{\mu e^{-N}}{(p-2)!}},

where

\displaystyle{\mu=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\log(n+p-2))^{p-2}}{(n+p-1)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{p-2}{\log(n+p-2)}\right)}.
This prove that
\epsilon_{4}
decay exponentially
to
0.

Let now study the decay rate of

. Using

]

\displaystyle{1-\frac{\gamma(n,x)}{\Gamma(n)}=e^{-x}\sum_{n=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1){N^{n+p-1}}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n+p-1}\frac{N^{j}}{j!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n+p-1}\frac{(n+p-1)!}{j!{N^{n+p-1-j}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n+p-1}\frac{(j+1)(j+2)\cdots(n+p-1)}{{N^{n+p-1-j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n+p-1}\left(\frac{n+p-1}{N}\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!}\left(\frac{1-\left(\frac{n+p-1}{N}\right)^{n+p}}{1-\frac{n+p-1}{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ne^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(-1)^{n}s(n+p-1,p-1)}{(n+p-1)(n+p-1)!}.

Once again using (
28
) we obtain the following upper
bound of
\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{Ne^{-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-p}\frac{(\log(n+p-2))^{p-2}}{(n+p-1)^{2}(p-2)!}\left(1+\frac{p-2}{\log(n+p-2)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{\nu Ne^{-N}}{(p-2)!}},

where

\displaystyle{\nu=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\log(n+p-2))^{p-2}}{(n+p-1)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{p-2}{\log(n+p-2)}\right)}.

∎

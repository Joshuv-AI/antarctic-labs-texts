Variational and Strong Variational Convexity in Infinite-Dimensional Variational Analysis
Pham Duy Khanh, Vu Vinh Huy Khoa, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Vo Thanh Phat

Abstract




Abstract
. This paper is devoted to a systematic study and characterizations of the fundamental notions of variational and strong variational convexity for lower semicontinuous functions. While these notions have been quite recently introduced by Rockafellar, the importance of them has been already recognized and documented in finite-dimensional variational analysis and optimization. Here we address general infinite-dimensional settings and derive comprehensive characterizations of both variational and strong variational convexity notions by developing novel techniques, which are essentially different from finite-dimensional counterparts. As a consequence of the obtained characterizations, we establish new quantitative and qualitative relationships between strong variational convexity and tilt stability of local minimizers in appropriate frameworks of Banach spaces.

Key words
. variational analysis and optimization, variational and strong variational convexity, local maximal monotonicity, proximal mappings and Moreau envelopes, tilt-stable local minimizers, generalized differentiation

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
49J52, 49J53, 47H05, 90C30, 90C45

1
Introduction

The notions of
variational
and
strong variational
convexity of lower semicontinuous functions have been recently introduced by Rockafellar
[
29
]
in finite-dimensional spaces, and then they have been applied by him to the design and justification of numerical algorithms of the proximal and augmented Lagrangian types for solving some classes of nonsmooth optimization problems; see
[
30
,
31
]
. The motivation behind these notions was the fact that just certain
local
(vs. global)
monotonicity
properties of (limiting)
subgradient mappings
were needed for developing numerical algorithms; cf. also Pennanen
[
25
]
. While it has been well recognized in variational analysis that the crucial
global
maximal monotonicity of subgradient mappings is
equivalent
to convexity,
local
maximal monotonicity opens the door to introducing the new class of
variationally convex
functions, and similarly for the strong counterparts.

On the other lines of research, the usefulness of variational convexity has been recently revealed in the developments of the
generalized Newton methods

[
12
,
14
,
23
]
to solve subgradient inclusions and optimization problems generated by a large class of
prox-regular
functions via reducing them to problems of
{\cal C}^{1,1}
optimization by using
Moreau envelopes
. It is shown in
[
13
]
that the desired convexity (strong convexity) of Moreau envelopes is
equivalent
to the variational convexity (strong variational convexity) of the corresponding cost functions. We also mention remarkable applications of variational convexity to
variational sufficiency
in constrained optimization
[
13
,
29
,
30
,
34
]
with its algorithmic implementations.

All the aforementioned developments concern problems in finite-dimensional spaces with the essential usage of finite-dimensional geometry in formulations and proofs of the obtained results. For its own sake and having in mind applications to infinite-dimensional optimization problems, including dynamic optimization and optimal control of ODE and PDE systems, we now aim at developing variational convexity theory in appropriate settings of
Banach spaces
. This is done below by using powerful tools of infinite-dimensional variational analysis and generalized differentiation, epigraphical convergence of extended-real-valued functions, and Banach space geometry. The major results obtained in this way include
complete characterizations
of (strong) variational convexity via local (strong) maximal monotonicity of subgradient mappings, via (strong) convexity of tilted and usual Moreau envelopes, relationships between strong variational convexity and tilt stability of local minimizers, etc. The reader will see below that different characterizations require appropriate geometric assumptions on the Banach spaces in question, which all hold in the case of
Hilbert spaces
.

The rest of the material is organized as follows. Section
2
contains those definitions and
preliminaries
, which are broadly used throughout the entire paper. In Section
3
, we discuss several notions of
strong convexity
for extended-real-valued functions and
strong monotonicity
for set-valued mappings with highlighting relationships between them in the case of subgradient operators. Section
4
concerns
local monotonicity
of set-valued mappings together with its strong counterpart and presents the
resolvent characterization
(of a local Minty type) of local maximal monotonicity in reflexive Banach spaces.

In Section
5
, we define both notions of variational and strong variational convexity for functions on Banach spaces and derive explicit descriptions of their
subdifferential graphs
. This result is instrumental to establish in Section
6
our major characterizations of
variational convexity
via
local maximal monotonicity of subgradient mappings
and
local convexity of Moreau envelopes
under various geometric assumptions on the Banach spaces in question. In this section, we also derive
quantitative
characterizations of
strong variational convexity
with establishing relationships between the corresponding moduli.

Section
7
involves tools of
generalized differentiation
in variational analysis to study variational and strong variational convexity. Our characterizations here are obtained in Hilbert spaces via
second-order subdifferentials
. In Section
8
, we establish relationships between
strong variational convexity
and
tilt stability
of local minimizers for a general class of optimization problems. The
concluding
Section
9
summarizes the obtained results and discusses some directions of our future research.

2
Preliminaries from Variational Analysis

In this section, we define and discuss some notions from variational analysis and generalized differentiation that are frequently used in what follows. More details and references can be found in the books
[
18
,
32
]
and
[
17
,
33
]
in finite and infinite dimensions, respectively.

Unless otherwise stated, all the spaces under consideration are assumed to be
Banach
with the generic symbols
\|\cdot\|
for norms and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the canonical pairing between a space
X
and its topological dual
X^{*}
. We use ‘
\rightarrow
’, ‘
\overset{w}{\rightarrow}
’, and ‘
\overset{w^{*}}{\rightarrow}
’ to indicate the strong/norm, weak, and weak
∗
convergence, respectively. Product spaces
X\times Y
are normed by
\|(x,y)\|:=\|x\|+\|y\|
whenever
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. The symbols
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
and
B_{r}(x)
, signify, respectively, the closed and open balls centered at
x
with radius
r>0
. Throughout the paper, we consider set-valued mappings/multifunctions
F\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
with the
domain

\mbox{\rm dom}\,F:=\{x\in X\;|\;F(x)\neq\emptyset\}
and the
graph

\mbox{\rm gph}\,F:=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\;|\;y\in F(x)\}
. As usual,
{\rm I\!N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
.

The (Painlevé-Kuratowski)
sequential outer limit
of a set-valued mapping
F:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
as
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
is

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\exists\text{ sequences }x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},\;x_{k}^{*}\overset{w^{*}}{\rightarrow}x^{*}\text{ with }x_{k}^{*}\in F(x_{k}),\;k\in{\rm I\!N}\big\}.

(2.1)

For a
proper
extended-real-valued function
\varphi\colon X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
with
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi:=\{x\in X\;|\;\varphi(x)<\infty\}\neq\emptyset
and for any given
\varepsilon\geq 0
, define the
\varepsilon
-regular subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
by

\widehat{\partial}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x}):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\Big|\;\liminf\limits_{x\to\bar{x}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq-\varepsilon\Big\}.

(2.2)

When
\varepsilon=0
in (
2.2
), this construction is known as the (Fréchet)
regular subdifferential

of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
and is denoted by
\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x})
. The (Mordukhovich)
limiting/basic subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is defined via the sequential outer limit (
2.1
) by

\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\overset{\varphi}{\to}\bar{x},\,\varepsilon\downarrow 0\end{subarray}}\;\widehat{\partial}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x)

(2.3)

with
x\overset{\varphi}{\to}\bar{x}
meaning that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
\varphi(x)\rightarrow\varphi(\bar{x})
. Both constructions
\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x})
and
\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
reduce to the gradient
\{\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})\}
if
\varphi
is strictly differentiable at
\bar{x}
and to the subdifferential of convex analysis

\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\;\text{ for all }\;x\in X\big\}

when
\varphi
is convex. It has been well recognized that
\varepsilon>0
can be equivalently dismissed in (
2.3
) provided that
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) around
\bar{x}
and the space
X
is
Asplund
, i.e., a Banach space where every separable subspace has a separable dual; this class is sufficiently broad including, in particular, all reflexive spaces. In Asplund spaces, the limiting subdifferential (
2.3
) and the related normal and coderivatives constructions for sets and set-valued mappings enjoy comprehensive
calculus rules
, which are based on
variational/extremal principles
of variational analysis. Some important properties of (
2.3
) with using
\varepsilon>0
in the limiting procedure are also available in general Banach spaces.

The next piece of variational analysis needed in what follows concerns
variational convergence
of extended-real-valued functions and sets. Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{N\subset{\rm I\!N}\;\big|\;{\rm I\!N}\setminus N\;\text{ finite}\big\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\mathcal{N}_{\infty}^{\#}:=\big\{N\subset{\rm I\!N}\;\big|\;N\;\text{ infinite}\big\}.

Given a sequence of sets
\{C^{k}\}\subset X
, its
outer limit
and
inner limit
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}C^{k}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;\exists N\in\mathcal{N}^{\#}_{\infty},\;\exists x^{k}\in C^{k}\;\text{ with }\;x^{k}\xrightarrow{N}x\big\},

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}C^{k}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;\exists N\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty},\ \exists x^{k}\in C^{k}\;\mbox{ with }\;x^{k}\xrightarrow{N}x\big\}.

For a sequence
\left\{\varphi^{k}\right\}
of extended-real-valued functions on
X
, its
lower epi-limit
and
upper epi-limit
are defined, respectively, by

\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\text{e-}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k})\;\mbox{ and }\;\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\text{e-}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}).

If these two functions agree on
X
, then the
epi-limit
function e-
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}
exists, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\text{e-}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}:=\text{e-}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}=\text{e-}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}\;\mbox{ on }\;X.

In this case, it is said that the functions
\varphi^{k}

epi-converge
to
\varphi
, which is denoted by
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
. The next characterization of epi-convergence taken from
[
1
, Proposition 1.14]
plays an important role to verify the characterizations of local maximal monotonicity established in Section
5
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
\varphi^{k}\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
as
k\in{\rm I\!N}
and

\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be given functions. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
as
k\to\infty
.

(ii)
At each point
x\in X
, we have the relationship

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varphi^{k}(x^{k})\geq\varphi(x)\ \text{ for every sequence }\;x^{k}\rightarrow x,\\
\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\varphi^{k}(x^{k})\leq\varphi(x)\ \text{ for some sequence }\;x^{k}\rightarrow x.\end{cases}

Finally in this section, recall that a function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is
weakly sequentially lower semicontinuous
(weakly sequentially l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
if for any sequence
\{x_{k}\}
which weakly converges to
\bar{x}
, it holds that
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(x_{k})\geq\varphi(\bar{x})
. On the other hand,
\varphi
is
weakly lower semicontinuous
(weakly l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
in the weak topology of
X
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in U.

It is easily to see that the weak l.s.c. yields the weak sequential l.s.c., while the reverse implication is not true even in Hilbert spaces; see a counterexample in
[
15
, Example 2.1]
.

3
Strong Monotonicity and Strong Convexity

This section is manly devoted to the study of various notions of (global)
strong monotonicity
for set-valued mappings and
strong convexity
for extended-real-valued functions with
given moduli
, and to establishing relationships between them in the case of subdifferential operators.

Recall first that a set-valued mapping
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is (globally)
monotone
on
X
if

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ whenever }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T.

(3.1)

In what follows, we address the
two notions
of strong monotonocity. The first one involves the
duality mapping

J:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
between
X
and
X^{*}
defined by

J(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\langle x^{*},x\rangle=\|x\|^{2}=\|x^{*}\|^{2}\big\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X,

(3.2)

which plays a significant role in our study. It is well known that
J
is the subgradient mapping of the quadratic function
\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
, and thus it is maximal monotone on
X
with
\mbox{\rm dom}\,J=X
. It is also well known that the duality mapping is
single-valued
on
X
if and only if the space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
Gâteaux smooth
. i.e., its norm is Gâteaux differentiable on
X\setminus\{0\}
. This subclass of Banach is very large including, after an equivalent renorming, every separable and every reflexive space; see, e.g., the comprehensive book
[
6
]
, where the reader can find these and other facts from geometric theory of Banach spaces used below. If
X
is Hilbert, then the duality mapping
J
reduces to the usual identity mapping
I
on
X
.

Employing (
3.2
), we say that
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
on a Banach space
X
is (globally)
strongly monotone
on
X
with
modulus
\sigma>0
(or
\sigma
-strongly monotone
) if the shift operator
T-\sigma J
is monotone on
X
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\sigma\langle j(x_{1})-j(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle

(3.3)

whenever
(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
and
(x_{1},j(x_{1})),(x_{2},j(x_{2}))\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,J
. Another notion of strong monotonicity, which is also broadly employed in applications, is defined by using only the norm on
X
. Given
\sigma>0
, we say that
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is
norm
\sigma
-strongly monotone
on
X
if

\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\sigma\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}\;\mbox{ for all }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T.

(3.4)

It is clear that the notions of
\sigma
-strong monotonicity and norm
\sigma
-strong monotonicity agree if
X
is a Hilbert space, but it is not the case in general. If the modulus
\sigma>0
is not specified, we simply use the terms “strong monotonicity” and “norm strong monotonicity” for the above notions. The
maximality
versions of (
3.1
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
) are understood as usual with respect to the graph inclusion. Note also the defined monotonicity notions for set-valued mappings induce the corresponding ones for
sets
by associating them with the
graphs
of such mappings.

The following proposition verified in
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
is needed in the subsequent material.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, for any
\sigma>0
we have the assertions:

(i)
If
T
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone, then
T-\sigma J
is maximal monotone on
X
.

(ii)
If
X
is reflexive and
T
is maximal monotone, then
T+\sigma J
is maximal monotone.

(iii)
If
T=\partial\varphi
for an l.s.c. convex function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
, then
T+\sigma J
is maximal monotone.

(iv)
If in the settings of
(ii)
and
(iii)
, the norm
\|\cdot\|
is Gâteaux smooth, then the shifted operator
T+\sigma J
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone.

Next we turn to extended-real-valued functions
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
and consider for them two notions of
strong convexity
. For any
\sigma>0
, define
quadratically
\sigma
-shifted function

\psi(x):=\varphi(x)-(\sigma/2)\|x\|^{2}\ \text{ for all }\ x\in X

(3.5)

and say that
\varphi
is
strongly convex
with
modulus

\sigma>0
, or
\sigma
-
strongly convex
, if its quadratic shift (
3.5
) is convex. We also use another notion of strong convexity introduced by Boris Polyak
[
27
]
in the form

\varphi\big(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\big)+\sigma\lambda(1-\lambda)\dfrac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2}\leq\lambda\varphi(x)+(1-\lambda)\varphi(y)\;\mbox{for all }\;x,y\in X,\;\lambda\in(0,1),

(3.6)

which has been studied and applied in many publications; see, e.g., the books
[
19
,
36
]
and the references therein. We label (
3.6
) as
Polyak
\sigma
-strong convexity
and use the terms “strong convexity” and “Polyak strong convexity” for the notions above if the modulus
\sigma>0
is not specified.

It is proved in
[
24
]
that the two strong convexity notions above
agree if and only if

X
is an
inner product space
. It also follows from
[
24
]
that in any non-Hilbert space, the quadratic function
x\mapsto\|x\|^{2}
is
2
-strongly convex but not Polyak 2-strongly convex. On the other hand, the following example in the space
X:=L^{1}([0,1];{\rm I\!R})
shows that a function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
may be Polyak 2-strongly convex while not being 2-strongly convex. Indeed, define
\varphi(x):=\int_{0}^{1}|x(t)|^{2}dt
for
\int_{0}^{1}|x(t)|^{2}dt<\infty
and
\varphi(x):=\infty
otherwise. To verify that
\varphi
is Polyak 2-strongly convex, consider
u,v\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi=L^{2}([0,1];{\rm I\!R})
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. We clearly have for a.e.
t\in[0,1]
that

|\lambda u(t)+(1-\lambda)v(t)|^{2}+\lambda(1-\lambda)|u(t)-v(t)|^{2}\leq\lambda|u(t)|^{2}+(1-\lambda)|v(t)|^{2}.

(3.7)

Integrating both sides of (
3.7
) over
[0,1]
gives us

\varphi\big(\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v\big)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\lambda\varphi(u)+(1-\lambda)\varphi(v).

Therefore, the classical Hölder inequality ensures that

\varphi\big(\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v\big)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|u-v\|_{X}^{2}\leq\lambda\varphi(u)+(1-\lambda)\varphi(v),

i.e.,
\varphi
is Polyak 2-strongly convex on
X
. To see that the function
\varphi
is not 2-strongly convex on
X
, the reader can check the violation of the underlying inequality
\psi(\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v)\leq\lambda\psi(u)+(1-\lambda)\psi(v)
for the quadratic shift (
3.5
) with
u(t):={\rm log}(t^{100}+0.5)
,
v(t):={\rm log}(1.5-t)
, and
\lambda:=0.5
.

It follows from
[
36
, Corollary 3.5.11]
that for any l.s.c. convex function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on an arbitrary Banach space and any
\sigma>0
, the Polyak
\sigma
-strong convexity of
\varphi
is
equivalent
to the norm
\sigma
-strong monotonicity (
3.4
) of the
subgradient mapping

\partial\varphi
. Regarding relationships between the
\sigma
-strong convexity of
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
defined via the convexity of (
3.5
) and the
\sigma
-strong monotonicity (
3.3
) of
\partial\varphi
in generality, we present the following proposition, which involves the limiting subgradient mapping (
2.3
).

Proposition 3.2
.

For a proper extended-real-valued function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
defined on a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
and for any
\sigma>0
, we have the assertions:

(i)
If
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly convex, then for all
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\partial\varphi
, it holds the representation

\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial\varphi(x)=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|&\exists j(x)\in J(x)\;\mbox{ with }\;\varphi(u)\geq\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},u-x\rangle\\
&+(\sigma/2)(\|x\|^{2}-2\langle j(x),u\rangle+\|u\|^{2})\;\mbox{ whenever }\;u\in X\big\}.\end{array}

(3.8)

(ii)
Suppose that
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is Gâteaux smooth and that
\varphi
is l.s.c. If
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly convex, then
\partial\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone (in fact,
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone). The converse implication holds if
X
is an Asplund space.

[
Proof.]
To verify (i), assume that
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly convex, i.e.,
\psi
from (
3.5
) is convex. Pick
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\partial\varphi
and deduce from the Moreau-Rockafellar theorem that

\partial\varphi(x)=\partial\big(\psi+(\sigma/2)\|\cdot\|^{2}\big)(x)=\partial\psi(x)+\sigma J(x),

which ensures the claimed representation in (i) by the subdifferential construction of convex analysis.

To justify the first assertion in (ii), we clearly have

\partial\varphi=\partial\big(\varphi-(\sigma/2)\|\cdot\|^{2}\big)+\partial\big(\sigma/2)\|\cdot\|^{2}\big)=\partial\big(\varphi-(\sigma/2)\|\cdot\|^{2}\big)+\sigma J,

and thus deduce the result from Proposition
3.1
(iv).

It remains to verify the converse statement of (ii) provided that the space
X
is Asplund. By the
\sigma
-strong monotonicity of
\partial\varphi
, we get that the operator
\widehat{\partial}\varphi-\sigma J
is monotone on
X
. It follows from the difference rule for regular subgradients (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma 6.22]
or
[
16
, Theorem 3.4]
) that

\widehat{\partial}\big(\varphi-(\sigma/2)\|\cdot\|^{2}\big)\subset\widehat{\partial}\varphi-\sigma J,

and hence
\widehat{\partial}\big(\varphi-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\big)
is monotone. Applying finally
[
17
, Theorem 3.56]
in Asplund spaces yields the convexity of
\varphi-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
, and thus the function
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly convex.

4
Local Monotonicity of Set-Valued Operators

We begin with defining
local monotonicity
,
local strong monotonicity
, and their
maximality
versions used below. Recall that by definition a “neighborhood” is an
open
set around a given point.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. We say that:

(i)

T
is
locally monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if there exist a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a globally monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

(4.1)

T
is
locally maximal monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if there exist a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a maximal monotone mapping
\bar{T}
on
X
such that (
4.1
) holds.

(ii)

T
is
locally strongly monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma>0
if there are neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
,
V
of
\bar{x}^{*}
and a globally
\sigma
-strongly monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

(4.2)

T
is
locally strongly maximal monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma>0
if there exist a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone mapping
\bar{T}
such that (
4.2
) holds.

We say that
T
is monotone
with respect to

W\subset X\times X^{*}
(
W
may not be open) if (
4.1
) holds with replacing
U\times V
by
W
. Similar terminology is used for
\sigma
-strong monotonicity and maximality versions.

In our preceding paper
[
11
]
, we established various characterizations of the local maximality monotonicity properties of set-valued operators formulated in Definition
4.1
. To present a crucial characterization of local maximal monotonicity, let us recall the needed notions and facts from Banach space theory that can be found, e.g., in
[
6
]
and the references therein.

A norm
\|\cdot\|
on
X
is
Fréchet smooth
if it is Fréchet differentiable on
X\setminus\{0\}
. This important class of Banach spaces is a bit smaller than their Gâteaux smooth counterpart, but still includes, under equivalent renorming, all reflexive spaces and spaces with separable duals. A space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
strictly convex
if for all
x,y\in S_{X}:=\{x\in X\mid\|x\|=1\}
,
x\neq y
and all
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

\|\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\|<1
. Note that the duality mapping
J
from (
3.2
) is single-valued and norm-to-weak
∗
continuous if
X^{*}
is strictly convex. The 2-
uniform convexity
of
(X,\||\cdot\|)
can be defined as

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}+c\|x-y\|^{2}\leq 2\big(\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}\big)\ \text{ for all }\;x,y\in X,

(4.3)

with some
c\leq 1
, where the case of
c=1
corresponds to Hilbert spaces. It follows from
[
35
, Corollary 1]
that the
2
-uniform convexity of a Banach space is equivalent to the norm strong monotonicity (
3.4
) of the duality mapping
J
with some modulus
0<c_{1}\leq 1
meaning that for every
x,y\in X
,
j(x)\in J(x)
, and
j(y)\in J(y)
we have the inequality

\langle j(x)-j(y),x-y\rangle\geq c_{1}\|x-y\|^{2}.

(4.4)

The reverse inequality to (
4.3
) with ‘
\geq
’ replacing ‘
\leq
’ and
c\geq 1
defines 2-
uniformly smooth
spaces. The latter class of spaces can be equivalently described as

c\|x-y\|^{2}\geq\|x\|^{2}-2\langle j(x),y\rangle+\|y\|^{2}\;\mbox{ for each }\;x,y\in X\;\mbox{ and }\;j(x)\in J(x).

(4.5)

If
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is a Gâteaux smooth space (i.e.,
J(x)=\{j(x)\}
), then
the right-hand side of inequality (
4.5
) is called the
Lyapunov functional
and is denoted by
\Lambda(x,y)
. Finally in this brief tour of Banach space geometry, we mention the
Kadec-Klee property
of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
postulating that for any sequence
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}x
with
\|x_{k}\|\to\|x\|
we have
\|x_{k}-x\|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

Having in hand the Banach space notions discussed above, recall a fundamental renorming result saying that any
reflexive
Banach space admits an equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|
such that

\|\cdot\|\text{ is Fr\'{e}chet smooth, strictly convex, and has the Kadec-Klee property.}

(4.6)

The following
resolvent characterization
of local maximal monotonicity of set-valued operators taken from
[
11
, Theotem 3.3]
can be viewed as a far-going local counterpart of the classical Minty characterization of global maximal monotonicity. Given a set-valued mapping
T\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
between Banach spaces, we say
g\colon X\to Y
is a
single-valued continuous localization
of
T
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
if there exists a neighborhood
U\times V
of this point such that
\mbox{\rm dom}\,g=U
,
g
is continuous on
U
, and
\mbox{\rm gph}\,g=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping defined on a reflexive Banach space endowed with the equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|
satisfying the properties listed in (
4.6
), let
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
, and let
J\colon X\to X^{*}
be the duality mapping (
3.2
). Then
T
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if and only if it is locally monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and its resolvent
(T+\lambda J)^{-1}
admits a single-valued continuous localization around
(\bar{x}^{*}+\lambda J(\bar{x}),\bar{x})
for any
\lambda>0
.

5
Variational Convexity via Subdifferential Graphs

In this section, we define the underlying notions of
variational
and
strong variational convexity
of extended-real-valued functions on Banach spaces by following the pattern of Rockafellar
[
29
]
in finite dimensions. The main result of this section provides a characterization of both notions via the corresponding descriptions of the subdifferential graph.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function on a Banach space
X
, and let
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. Then:

(i)

\varphi
is
variationally convex
at
\bar{x}
for a limiting subgradient
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if there exist a convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a convex l.s.c. function
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
such that

\left(U_{\varepsilon}\times V\right)\cap\operatorname{gph}\partial\varphi=(U\times V)\cap\operatorname{gph}\partial\widehat{\varphi}\text{ and }\varphi(x)=\widehat{\varphi}(x)\text{ at the common elements }(x,x^{*})

(5.1)

for some
\varepsilon>0
, where
U_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in U\mid\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\}
.

(ii)
Given
\sigma>0
, we say that
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly variationally convex
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if the convex function
\psi
in
(i)
is assumed to be
\sigma
-strongly convex.

(iii)
Given
\sigma\geq 0
, we use the term
variational
\sigma
-convexity
for
\varphi
to unify both variational convexity
(\sigma=0)
and
\sigma
-strong variational convexity
(\sigma>0)
cases.

The following auxiliary construction provides a useful technical tool to deal with strong variational convexity below. For any
\sigma\neq 0
, define the
vertical shear mapping

\Phi_{\sigma}:X\times X^{*}\rightarrow X\times X^{*}
by

\Phi_{\sigma}(x,x^{*})=\big(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x)\big)\;\text{ whenever }\;(x,x^{*})\in X\times X^{*},

(5.2)

which is a homeomorphism from
X\times X^{*}
to itself provided that
X
is Fréchet smooth. It is easy to check that for any
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
we have the transformations

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\overset{\Phi_{\sigma}}{\longmapsto}\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma J)\overset{\Phi_{-\sigma}}{\longmapsto}\mbox{\rm gph}\,T.

(5.3)

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a Fréchet smooth space, and let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be a proper function with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. Given a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
as well as constants
\varepsilon>0
and
\sigma\neq 0
, there is a convex neighborhood
Q\times W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
such that we have the implication

\big[(x,x^{*})\in(Q_{\varepsilon/2}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\big]\Longrightarrow\big[(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\big],

(5.4)

where
Q_{\varepsilon/2}:=\{x\in Q\mid\psi(x)<\psi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon/2\}
and
U_{\varepsilon}
is taken from Definition
5.1
.

[
Proof.]
The quadratic function
\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is continuously differentiable on
X
with
\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\right)=J
. The subdifferential sum rule from
[
17
, Proposition 1.107(ii)]
gives us the equality

\partial\varphi=\partial\psi+\sigma J\ \ \text{ on }\ X,

(5.5)

which yields
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(\partial\psi+\sigma J)
. Furthermore, it follows from (
5.3
) that

\Phi_{\sigma}(\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi

(5.6)

via the homeomorphism
\Phi_{\sigma}
defined in (
5.2
). As
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))=\Phi_{-\sigma}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, the set
\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
is a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
. This allows us to find convex neighborhoods
Q\subset U
of
\bar{x}
and
W
of
\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})
such that
Q\times W\subset\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
together with
Q\subset B_{r_{1}}(\bar{x})
and
W\subset B_{r_{2}}(\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
, where
r_{2}>0
and
r_{1}>0
satisfy the estimates

r_{1}<\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2(\|\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})\|+r_{2})}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002ptr_{1}(r_{1}+2\|\bar{x}\|)<\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}.

It follows from the above that
(\sigma/2)(\|x\|^{2}-\|\bar{x}\|^{2})<\varepsilon/2
for all
x\in Q
yielding

\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})<\psi(x)-\psi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon/2\ \text{ for all }\ x\in Q

and thus
Q_{\varepsilon/2}\subset U_{\varepsilon}
. To verify now (
5.4
), consider
(x,x^{*})\in(Q_{\varepsilon/2}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi
and claim that
(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
. Indeed, (
5.6
) implies that

(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))=\Phi_{\sigma}(x,x^{*})\in\Phi_{\sigma}(\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi.

(5.7)

Since
Q\times W\subset\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
, we also have

(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))=\Phi_{\sigma}(x,x^{*})\in\Phi_{\sigma}(Q\times W)\subset\Phi_{\sigma}(\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V))=U\times V.

(5.8)

Combining
Q_{\varepsilon/2}\subset U_{\varepsilon}
with (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) gives us

(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi,

which therefore verifies (
5.4
) and completes the proof.

The next proposition establishes the equivalence between the strong variational convexity of a function and variational convexity of its quadratic shift.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function defined on a Fréchet smooth space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
, and let
\sigma>0
. Then
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if and only if its
\sigma
-shift

\psi(x):=\varphi(x)-(\sigma/2)\|x\|^{2},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X,

is variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})\in\partial\psi(\bar{x})
.

[
Proof.]
It follows from (
5.5
) that
\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})\in\partial\psi(\bar{x})
. The
\sigma
-strong variational convexity of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
gives us a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a
\sigma
-strongly convex function
\widehat{\varphi}
satisfying (
5.1
). By Lemma
5.2
, we find a convex neighborhood
Q\times W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
on which (
5.4
) holds. Considering the convex function
\widehat{\psi}:=\widehat{\varphi}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
, deduce from the subdifferential sum rule that
\partial\widehat{\varphi}=\partial\widehat{\psi}+\sigma J
. It is easy to see that
\widehat{\psi}
is convex and l.s.c. with
\widehat{\psi}\leq\psi
on
U
. To verify the variational convexity of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})
, it suffices to show that

(Q_{\varepsilon/2}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi=(Q\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\psi}\text{ and }\psi(x)=\widehat{\psi}(x)\text{ at the common elements }(x,x^{*}),

(5.9)

where
Q_{\varepsilon/2}
is taken from Lemma
5.2
. To furnish this, consider
(x,x^{*})\in(Q_{\varepsilon/2}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi
and deduce from (
5.4
) the fulfillment of inclusion

(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi.

(5.10)

It follows from (
5.10
) and (
5.1
) that

(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi(x)=\widehat{\varphi}(x).

The above representation of
\partial\widehat{\varphi}(x)
implies that
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\psi}
with
\psi(x)=\widehat{\psi}(x)
.

On the other hand, consider
(x,x^{*})\in(Q\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\psi}
. Utilizing the obtained representation of
\partial\widehat{\varphi}
and the transformation for
\Phi_{\sigma}
in (
5.3
), we have
(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))\in(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
. Then (
5.1
) brings us to
(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x))\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
and
\varphi(x)=\widehat{\varphi}(x)
, and therefore

(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\psi(x)=\widehat{\psi}(x).

It remains to show that
x\in Q_{\varepsilon/2}
, i.e.,
\psi(x)<\psi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon/2
. Indeed, it follows from
(x,x^{*})\in(Q\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\psi}
and the choice of
Q,W
above that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi(x)=\widehat{\psi}(x)\leq\widehat{\psi}(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-x\rangle\leq\psi(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-x\rangle\leq\psi(\bar{x})+\|x^{*}\|\cdot\|\bar{x}-x\|\\
\leq\psi(\bar{x})+(\|x^{*}-(\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))\|+\|\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})\|)\cdot\|\bar{x}-x\|\leq\psi(\bar{x})+(r_{2}+\|\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})\|)r_{1}<\psi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon/2\end{array}

where
r_{1},r_{2}
are taken from the proof of Lemma
5.2
. This yields
(x,x^{*})\in(Q_{\varepsilon/2}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi
and thus justifies (
5.9
), which justifies the variational convexity of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x})
. The reverse implication of the proposition can be verified similarly.

As a part of the proof of the main result of this section, we present the next lemma about the
epi-convergence
of extended-real-valued functions.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
X
be an arbitrary Banach space, and let
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
and
\varphi^{k}\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
for
k\in{\rm I\!N}
be l.s.c. functions. The following assertions hold:

(i)
If
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
as
k\to\infty
, then

\inf\varphi\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}(\inf\varphi^{k}).

(5.11)

(ii)
If in addition to
(i)
, we have

\argmin\varphi\neq\emptyset
and

\argmin\varphi^{k}\neq\emptyset
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
, then

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}(\argmin\varphi^{k})\subset\argmin\varphi.

Thus
x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
if
\argmin\varphi
consists of a unique point
\bar{x}
.

[
Proof.]
Assertion (i) is proved in
[
1
, Proposition 2.9]
. To verify assertion (ii), pick
x\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}(\argmin\varphi^{k})
and find subsequences
\{k_{n}\}\subset{\rm I\!N}
and
x^{k_{n}}\in\argmin\varphi^{k_{n}}
such that
x^{k_{n}}\rightarrow x
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. It follows from
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
that
\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k})=\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi
. Using further

\big(x,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})\big)=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}(x^{k_{n_{l}}},\varphi^{k_{n_{l}}}(x^{k_{n_{l}}}))\;\text{ for some subsequence }\;\{k_{n_{l}}\}\;\text{ of }\;\{k_{n}\}

and
(x^{k_{n_{l}}},\varphi^{k_{n_{l}}}(x^{k_{n_{l}}}))\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\varphi^{k_{n_{l}}})
as
l\in{\rm I\!N}
tells us that

\big(x,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})\big)\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}).

Therefore, we have
\big(x,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})\big)\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi
meaning that

\varphi(x)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}}).

Getting all the above together brings us to

\varphi(x)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}(\inf\varphi^{k_{n}})\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}(\inf\varphi^{k})\leq\inf\varphi,

where the last inequality follows from (
5.11
). This yields
x\in\argmin\varphi
and thus completes the proof.

Now we get the main characterizations of this sections for both notions of variational convexity and strong variational convexity of functions on Banach spaces. In finite dimensions, the following theorem is contained in
[
29
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
with the proof based on finite-dimensional geometry.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function defined on a Banach space
X
, and let
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
be a basic subgradient from (
2.3
). For any
\sigma\geq 0
, consider the following statements:

(i)

\varphi
is variationally
\sigma
-convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
.

(ii)
There exist a convex neighborhood

of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})

for which

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}(u,u^{*})\in\left(U_{\varepsilon}\times V\right)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\Longrightarrow\exists\,j(u)\in J(u)\;\mbox{ such that for all }\;x\in U\;\mbox{ we have}\\
\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle+(\sigma/2)\big(\|u\|^{2}-2\langle j(u),x\rangle+\|x\|^{2}\big).\end{array}

Then implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
is valid in general Banach spaces. The converse implication
(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i)
fulfills whenever
\sigma\geq 0
if
X
is reflexive and if
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
.

[
Proof.]
We split the proof of the theorem into
eight steps
as follows.

Step 1:
Implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
holds in an arbitrary Banach space
. Indeed, suppose that
\varphi
is variationally
\sigma
-convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
and find a convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and an
l.s.c. function
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
, which is convex for
\sigma=0
and
\sigma
-strongly convex for
\sigma>0
,
such that the conditions in (
5.1
) are satisfied for some
\varepsilon>0
. Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\widehat{\varphi}
enjoys the above properties on the whole space
X
(i.e., associate
U
with
X
), which allows us to employ Proposition
3.2
on
U
. Pick any
(u,u^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
and get
(u,u^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
. Applying Proposition

(i) tells us that

\exists j(u)\in J(u)\;\mbox{ with }\;\widehat{\varphi}(x)\geq\widehat{\varphi}(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle+(\sigma/2)\big(\|u\|^{2}-2\langle j(u),x\rangle+\|x\|^{2}\big)\;\text{ for all }\;x\in U.

Since
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on

\widehat{\varphi}(u)=\varphi(u)

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle+(\sigma/2)\big(\|u\|^{2}-2\langle j(u),x\rangle+\|x\|^{2}\big)

whenever
x\in U
, which justifies implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii).

Next we start verifying the opposite implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) under the additional assumptions imposed where they are needed. By (ii), take a convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a number
\varepsilon>0
such that (
5.5

and

to get

\widehat{\varphi}:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(x):=\sup_{(u,u^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap{\rm\small gph}\,\partial\varphi}\big\{\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle+(\sigma/2)\|u\|^{2}-\sigma\langle\bar{j}(u),x\rangle\big\}+(\sigma/2)\|x\|^{2}.

(5.15)

It is easy to see that
\widehat{\varphi}
is l.s.c. and

\widehat{\varphi}(x)>-\infty

on
X
. Moreover, it follows from (

.

Step 2:
For any l.s.c. function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}

\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)

\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
. Indeed,

\varphi(x)=\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},x-x\rangle+(\sigma/2)\|x\|^{2}-\sigma\langle\bar{j}(x),x\rangle+(\sigma/2)\|x\|^{2}\;\text{ with }\;(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi,

which yields
\widehat{\varphi}(x)\geq\varphi(x)
. The latter ensures together with
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
that
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
. Further, we deduce from
(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
and
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
that

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle=\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+(\sigma/2)\left(\|x\|^{2}-2\langle\bar{j}(x),x^{\prime}\rangle+\|x^{\prime}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(x^{\prime})\;\mbox{ for any }\;x^{\prime}\in X,

where the last inequality follows from (
5.15
). Therefore,

\widehat{\varphi}(x^{\prime})\geq\widehat{\varphi}(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x^{\prime}\in X,

which tells us that
x^{*}\in\partial\widehat{\varphi}(x)
by the convexity of
\widehat{\varphi}
.

Now we check the opposite implication claimed in Step 2. Take

(x,x^{*})\in(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}

with
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
and get, by the convexity of
\widehat{\varphi}
and
x^{*}\in\partial\widehat{\varphi}(x)
, that

\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\widehat{\varphi}(x)\leq\widehat{\varphi}(x^{\prime})\;\text{ for all }\;x^{\prime}\in X.

Then we deduce from
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
and
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
that

\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(x^{\prime})\ \text{ for all }\ x^{\prime}\in U,

(5.16)

which implies in turn that

\liminf_{x^{\prime}\to x}\frac{\varphi(x^{\prime})-\varphi(x)-\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle}{\|x^{\prime}-x\|}\geq 0.

This tells us that
x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x)
and hence yields
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x)
. To finish the proof in Step 2, it remains to check that
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
. To verify this, deduce from (
5.16
) and the above choice of
U
and
V
that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-x\rangle\leq\varphi(\bar{x})+\|x^{*}\|\cdot\|\bar{x}-x\|\\
\leq\varphi(\bar{x})+(\|x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*}\|+\|\bar{x}^{*}\|)\cdot\|\bar{x}-x\|\leq\varphi(\bar{x})+(r_{2}+\|\bar{x}^{*}\|)r_{1}<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,\end{array}

which shows that
x\in U_{\varepsilon}
, and hence we are done in this step.

Let
\Omega\subset U
be a convex, closed, and bounded set such that
\bar{x}\in{\rm int}\,\Omega
and that
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. on
\Omega
. Using the indicator function
\delta_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
, for each
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
define

\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y):=\varphi(y)-\widehat{\varphi}(x)-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle+(1/2)\|y-x\|^{2}+\delta_{\Omega}(y),\hskip 10.00002pty\in X.

(5.17)

Step 3:
In any Banach space
X
, we have

\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y)\geq 0
,

argmin
\,\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=\{\bar{x}\}
, and
\min\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=0
.
To verify these properties, observe that
\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle+\widehat{\varphi}(x)\leq\widehat{\varphi}(y)\leq\varphi(y)
for any
y\in U
and
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
. By
\Omega\subset U
, we get that
\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y)\geq 0
whenever
y\in X
. The latter ensures that
\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}(y)=0
if and only if
y=\bar{x}
. This implies in turn that
\argmin\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=\{\bar{x}\}
and that
\min\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=0
.

Step 4:
Assume that
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
and that
X
is reflexive. Then the function
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
from (
5.17
) attains its absolute minimum on
X
.
It follows from Mazur’s weak closure theorem that the set
\Omega
under consideration is weakly sequentially closed. Furthermore, we easily deduce from the weak sequential l.s.c. of
\varphi
on
\Omega
and the structure of
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
in (
5.17
) that the latter function is weakly sequentially l.s.c. on the bounded and weakly sequentially closed subset
\Omega
of the reflexive Banach space
X
. Then the appropriate version of the Weierstrass existence theorem in this setting ensures that
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
attains its absolute minimum on
\Omega
.

Step 5:
For a general l.s.c. function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on a Banach space
X
, the function
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
from (
5.17
) is epicontinuous in
(x,x^{*})
with respect to
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
, i.e.,
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\xrightarrow{e}\psi_{x,x^{*}}
whenever
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\xrightarrow{{\rm\small gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}}(x,x^{*})
as
k\to\infty
.
To verify this, we use the characterization of epi-convergence presented in Proposition
2.1
(ii). Take
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\xrightarrow{{\rm\small gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}}(x,x^{*})
and then fix any
y\in X
and
y_{k}\rightarrow y
as
k\to\infty
. It easily follows from the convexity of the l.s.c. function
\widehat{\varphi}
that
\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\rightarrow\widehat{\varphi}(x)
as
k\to\infty
. This yields

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(y_{k})\geq\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varphi(y_{k})+\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(y_{k})-\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\\
+\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\big[(1/2)\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\langle x^{*}_{k},y_{k}-x_{k}\rangle\big]\geq\varphi(y)+\delta_{\Omega}(y)-\widehat{\varphi}(x)+(1/2)\|y-x\|^{2}-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle=\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y).\end{array}

We can also check directly that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(y)=\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\varphi(y)+\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(y)-\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\\
+\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\big[(1/2)\|y-x_{k}\|^{2}-\langle x^{*}_{k},y-x_{k}\rangle\big]=\varphi(y)+\delta_{\Omega}(y)-\widehat{\varphi}(x)+(1/2)\|y-x\|^{2}-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle=\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y)\end{array}

and conclude therefore that
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\xrightarrow{e}\psi_{x,x^{*}}
as
k\to\infty
, which verifies the claimed epicontinuity.

Step 6:

Let
\varphi
be a weakly sequentially l.s.c. function around
\bar{x}
defined on a reflexive space
X
, and let the pairs

(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
converge to some
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
as
k\to\infty
. There for all large
k
, there is a pair
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
such that
\widehat{\varphi}(\widetilde{x}_{k})=\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})
and
x_{k}^{*}-\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k}\in J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
.
Employing the result of Step 5 and remembering from Step 4 that
\argmin\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\neq\emptyset
allow us to deduce from Lemma
5.4
(ii) the existence of
\{\widetilde{x}_{k}\}
with
\widetilde{x}_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
and such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{x}_{k}\in\argmin\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}\cap\mathrm{int}\,\Omega\;\text{ for all large }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

Furthermore, we get by Lemma
5.4
(i) that

0=\min\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}(\inf\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}})=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\big(\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\big),

which tells us in turn that
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\downarrow 0
as

since
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\geq 0
. Therefore,

\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})-\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})=\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})+\langle x^{*}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\rangle-(1/2)\|\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}<\varepsilon/2

from which we derive the conditions

\displaystyle\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})+\varepsilon/2\leq\widehat{\varphi}(\bar{x})+\varepsilon=\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,

and thus
\widetilde{x}_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon}
for large
k
. Since
\widetilde{x}_{k}
is a minimizer of
\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}
on

, it satisfies
0\in\partial\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})
. Taking into account that
\varphi
is l.s.c. while the other functions in (

) are locally Lipschitzian around
\bar{x}
, we apply the subdifferential sum rule from
[
17
, Theorem 2.33]
to
\partial\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})
giving us

\displaystyle 0\in\partial\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\subset\partial\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})-x^{*}_{k}+J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\;\mbox{ for large }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}

since
\partial(\|\cdot-x_{k}\|^{2})(\widetilde{x}_{k})=J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
and
\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\widetilde{x}_{k})=N(\widetilde{x}_{k},\Omega)=\{0\}
due to
\bar{x}\in{\rm int}\,\Omega
. Thus we find
\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k}\in\partial\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})
and
j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\in J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
with
\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k}=x^{*}_{k}-j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
. It follows from
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and
\widetilde{x}_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
that
\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\rightarrow 0
. By
\|j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\|=\|\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\|
, this tells us that
j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\rightarrow 0
, and hence
\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*}\rightarrow\bar{x}^{*}
. Thus we have
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
for large
k
. Applying finally the statement of Step 2 ensures that
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
and
\widehat{\varphi}(\widetilde{x}_{k})=\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})
for large
k
, which verifies the claimed assertion.

Step 7:
We actually have
\widetilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}
for large
k\in{\rm I\!N}
in the setting of Step
6
.
Indeed, the convexity of the l.s.c. function
\widehat{\varphi}
yields the monotonicity of
\partial\widehat{\varphi}
.
Due to
(x_{k},x^{*}_{k}),(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
, we get by Step 6 that

0\leq\langle x^{*}_{k}-\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k},x_{k}-\widetilde{x}_{k}\rangle=-\|x_{k}-\widetilde{x}_{k}\|^{2},

which clearly tells us that
\widetilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}
.

Step 8:
In the setting of Step
7
, there is a neighborhood
(U,\bar{V})
of
\bar{x}
and
\bar{x}^{*}
such that for all
(x,x^{*})\in(U\times\bar{V})\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
it holds
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
. Supposing the contrary gives us a sequence

converging to

and

. It follows from Steps 6 and 7 that

\Longrightarrow
(i) and completes the proof.

6
Variational Convexity via Subdifferential Local Monotonicity and Moreau Envelopes

In this section, we investigate the notions of variational convexity and its strong counterpart for extended-real-valued functions on Banach spaces from different viewpoints in comparison with Section
5
; namely, by using subdifferential local monotonicity and Moreau envelopes.

The
Moreau envelope
of an l.s.c. function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on a Banach space
X
is defined by

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\inf_{w\in X}\big\{\varphi(w)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|w-x\|^{2}\big\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X,

(6.1)

where
\lambda>0
is a parameter. The corresponding
proximal mapping
is given by

P_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\text{\rm argmin}_{w\in X}\big\{\varphi(w)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|w-x\|^{2}\big\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X.

(6.2)

As in
[
33
]
, we say that
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is
prox-bounded
if

\varphi(x)\geq\alpha\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\beta\;\mbox{ for some }\;\alpha,\beta\in{\rm I\!R}\;\mbox{ and }\;\bar{x}\in X.

(6.3)

This notion can be characterized in terms of Moreau envelopes.

Proposition 6.1
.

For an l.s.c. function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on a Banach space
X
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is prox-bounded in the sense of (
6.3
).

(ii)
For some
\lambda>0
and
x\in X
, we have
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
.

(iii)
There is
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
for any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
and any
x\in X
.

[
Proof.]
To get (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), take
\alpha,\beta
in (
6.3
) and observe that for any
\lambda>0
with
\alpha+(2\lambda)^{-1}\geq 0
, we have

\varphi(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|y-x\|^{2}\geq\big(\alpha+(2\lambda)^{-1}\big)\|y-x\|^{2}+\beta\;\text{ whenever }\;y\in X.

Then (ii) is justified by taking the infimum on both sides of the above inequality with respect to
y\in X
.

Supposing now that (ii) is satisfied, denote

\lambda_{0}:=\sup\big\{\lambda>0\;\big|\;\exists x\in X\;\text{ and }\;e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty\big\}

and get from (ii) that
\lambda_{0}>0
. Picking any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
and
x\in X
, find

\bar{\lambda}>\lambda
and
\bar{x}\in X
such that
e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})>-\infty
. Whenever
y\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|y-x\|^{2}=\varphi(y)+(2\bar{\lambda})^{-1}\|y-\bar{x}\|^{2}+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|y-x\|^{2}-(2\bar{\lambda})^{-1}\|y-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
\geq e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|y-x\|^{2}-(2\bar{\lambda})^{-1}(\|y-x\|+\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2})\\
=e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})+\big((2\lambda)^{-1}-(2\bar{\lambda})^{-1}\big)\cdot\|y-x\|^{2}-\bar{\lambda}^{-1}\|y-x\|\cdot\|x-\bar{x}\|-(2\bar{\lambda})^{-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}.\end{array}

By
e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})>-\infty
in (ii) and by
\bar{\lambda}>\lambda
, the term
\varphi(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|y-x\|^{2}
is bounded from below uniformly in
y\in X
. This tells us that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
for any
x\in X
and
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
, i.e., (iii) holds. The last implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) is obvious, and hence the proof is complete.

To proceed further with functions on general Banach spaces, we need the following modifications of Moreau envelopes and proximal mappings depending on a fixed dual vector
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. The
x^{*}
-tilted Moreau envelope
for
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
with a parameter
\lambda>0
is defined by

e^{x^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\inf_{w\in X}\big\{\varphi(w)-\langle x^{*},w\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|w-x\|^{2}\big\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X.

(6.5)

It is easy to see that (
6.5
) is the usual Moreau envelope (
6.1
) of the
tilted function

\varphi(x)-\langle x^{*},x\rangle
. The corresponding

x^{*}
-tilted proximal mapping
is

P_{\lambda}^{x^{*}}\varphi(x):=\text{\rm argmin}_{w\in X}\big\{\varphi(w)-\langle x^{*},w\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|w-x\|^{2}\big\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X.

(6.6)

In the case of Hilbert spaces, we have the relationships (see, e.g.,
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
):

e_{\lambda}^{x^{*}}\varphi(x)=e_{\lambda}\varphi(x+\lambda x^{*})-\langle x^{*},x\rangle-(\lambda/2)\|x^{*}\|^{2},\hskip 10.00002ptP_{\lambda}^{x^{*}}\varphi(x)=P_{\lambda}\varphi(x+\lambda x^{*}).

(6.7)

The next proposition addresses
fixed points
of the
x^{*}
-tilted proximal mappings
P_{\lambda}^{x^{*}}
in the case where
x^{*}:=\bar{x}^{*}
in (
6.6
) is a
proximal subgradient
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
that is defined as follows: there are positive numbers
r
and
\varepsilon
for which

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-(r/2)\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

(6.8)

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function on a Banach space with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
, and let
\bar{x}^{*}\in X^{*}
. Then we have the equivalent assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is prox-bounded, and
\bar{x}^{*}
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
.

(ii)

P^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
for some
\lambda>0
.

(iii)

P^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small.

[
Proof.]
To verify (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), fix
\bar{x}^{*},r,\varepsilon
satisfying (
6.8
) and check that we can make (
6.8
) holding for all
x\in X
if
r
is properly modified. By the prox-boundedness of
\varphi
, it follows from Proposition
6.1
(iii) that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})>-\infty
for some
\lambda>0
. Choosing
\bar{\lambda}>0
to be sufficiently small tells us that

\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-(2\bar{\lambda})^{-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})-(2\lambda)^{-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq\varphi(x)

(6.9)

holds for all
x
with
\|x-\bar{x}\|>\varepsilon
. Denoting
\bar{r}:=2\max\{r,1/\bar{\lambda}\}
, deduce from (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) that

\varphi(x)-\langle\bar{x}^{*},x\rangle+(\bar{r}/2)\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}>\varphi(\bar{x})-\langle\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x}\rangle\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\neq\bar{x},

which is equivalent to
P_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{x}^{*}}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
with
\bar{\lambda}:=1/\bar{r}
and hence justifies (ii).

Since implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) is obvious, it remains to verify (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). Indeed, (iii) ensures by Proposition
6.1
(ii) that
\varphi
is prox-bounded. The fact that
\bar{x}^{*}
satisfies (
6.8
) follows from
\bar{x}
being a global minimizer of the function
\varphi(\cdot)-\langle\bar{x}^{*},\cdot\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|\cdot-\bar{x}\|^{2}
on
X
.

Next we recall some important notions of variational analysis broadly used in what follows. They can be found in the books
[
32
]
in finite dimensions and
[
33
]
in Banach spaces. An l.s.c. function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is
prox-regular
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if there are numbers
\varepsilon>0,r\geq 0
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle-(r/2)\|x-u\|^{2}

(6.10)

for all
x\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
and
(u,u^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\times\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}^{*}))
with
\varphi(u)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
.
If (
6.10
) holds for all
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, then
\varphi
is called to be
prox-regular at

\bar{x}
. Further, we say that
\varphi
is
subdifferentially continuous
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
with
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|<\varepsilon
whenever
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\bar{x})\times\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\bar{x}^{*}))
. If the latter holds for all
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, then
\varphi
is called to be
subdifferentially continuous at

\bar{x}
. Observe that for subdifferentially continuous functions, the condition “
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
” in the definition of prox-regularity can be dropped.

The
\varphi
-attentive
\varepsilon
-localization
of
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
is the mapping
T^{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
defined by

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T^{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}:=\big\{(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\;\big|\;\|x-\bar{x}\|<\varepsilon,\;|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|<\varepsilon\;\text{ and }\;\|x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*}\|<\varepsilon\big\}.

(6.11)

If
\varphi
is l.s.c., a localization can be taken with
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
instead of the absolute value in (
6.11
).

The proposition below, taken from
[
33
]
, concerns the tilted mappings (
6.5
) and (
6.6
).

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c., prox-bounded, and prox-regular function at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
defined on a
2
-uniformly convex and Gâteaux smooth space. Then there are
\lambda_{0},\varepsilon>0
such that for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
we can find a neighborhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\bar{x}
on which
e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth with the derivative

\nabla e_{\lambda}^{\bar{x}^{*}}\varphi=\lambda^{-1}J\circ(I-P_{\lambda}^{\bar{x}^{*}}\varphi).

(6.12)

Furthermore, the tilted proximal mapping (
6.6
) is single-valued and continuous being represented as

Now we present the needed properties of the duality mapping (

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a
2
-uniformly convex Banach space whose norm is Gâteaux smooth. Then the corresponding duality mapping
J
is single-valued and norm-to-norm continuous on the whole space
X
, i.e., the norm

is Fréchet smooth.

[
Proof.]
Fix

as

and the Gâteaux smoothness of its norm, the dual space

The following slightly modified notions of monotonicity and strong monotonicity of subgradient operators

Definition 6.5
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}

-locally monotone

(

-locally maximal monotone

and a number
\varepsilon>0
such that the mapping
\partial\varphi
is monotone
(
resp. maximal monotone
)
relative to
U_{\varepsilon}\times V
, where

is taken from Definition
5.1
. The notions of
\varphi
-local strong monotonicity
and its
\varphi
-local strong maximal monotonicity
counterpart with some modulus

are defined similarly.

Having in hand the above propositions and definitions, we are now ready to establish the new characterizations of variational and strong variational convexity of extended-real-valued functions on Banach spaces. Our major characterizations are given in the following theorem, which concerns the notion of variational convexity and then will be used for characterizing its strong counterpart. In finite dimensions, the equivalence between assertions (i), (ii), and (iii) of the theorem below has been obtained in
[
29

by a different proof, which is based on finite-dimensional geometry and doesn’t require prox-regularity and prox-boundedness of
\varphi

\Longleftrightarrow

[

. This together with (

) implies that the mapping
\partial\varphi
is maximal monotone with respect to

, which gives us (ii) by Definition
6.5
. Implication (ii)

As a part of verifying implications (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv) and (iv)

(6.14)

(6.15)

\Longrightarrow
(iv), apply Definition

to

B_{r}(\bar{x})^{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\;|\;\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\}

\bar{x}^{*}

by the assumed prox-regularity of

. Due to the prox-boundedness of

Using the

(6.17)

and employ (

\psi_{\lambda}(\bar{x})=e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},P^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\rangle-(2\lambda)^{-1}\|P_{\lambda}^{\bar{x}^{*}}\varphi(\bar{x})-\bar{x}\|^{2}=\varphi(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\big\langle\nabla e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{1})-\nabla e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\big\rangle=\big\langle\nabla e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{1})-\nabla e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\big\rangle\\
\\

\Longrightarrow

that

is prox-regular, prox-bounded. Under (iv), we have that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(x^{\prime})-\langle\bar{x}^{*},x^{\prime}\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\big\|x^{\prime}-\big(x^{\prime}+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)\big\|^{2}\geq e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi\big(x^{\prime}+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)\\

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}

(i)

\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}

\varphi

\bar{x}

\Longrightarrow
(ii)

\Longrightarrow
(i)
holds when
\varphi

[
Proof.]
Our intention is to check the equivalence:

6.23

\Longrightarrow
(v) in Theorem
6.6
gives us a convex neighborhood
Q\times W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
along with a positive number
\delta
such that

\big[(u,u^{*})\in(Q^{\psi}_{\delta}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\Longrightarrow\psi(x)\geq\psi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle\big]\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in Q,

(6.26)

where
Q_{\delta}^{\psi}:=\{x\in Q\mid\psi(x)<\psi(\bar{x})+\delta\}
. By Lemma
5.2
, we find convex neighborhoods
U\subset Q
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{x}^{*}
ensuring the implication

\big[(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\delta/2}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\big]\Longrightarrow\big[(x,x^{*}-\sigma J(x))\in(Q^{\psi}_{\delta}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\big].

(6.27)

To verify (
5.5
), let
\varepsilon:=\delta/2
and take
(u,u^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
,
x\in U
. Then we get by
(
6.27
) that

(u,u^{*}-\sigma J(u))\in(Q^{\psi}_{\delta}\times W)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi.

(6.28)

Since
x\in U\subset Q
, it follows from (
6.26
) and (
6.28
) that

\psi(x)\geq\psi(u)+\langle u^{*}-\sigma J(u),x-u\rangle,

which brings us to (
5.5
) and hence justifies (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv). The last implication (iv)
\Longrightarrow
(i) is a result of Theorem
5.5
, and thus we are done with the proof.

It is easy to check that for
Hilbert
spaces
X
, the prox-regularity assumption on
\psi
in Theorem
6.10
is
equivalent
to the prox-regularity of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Furthermore, the Hilbert space setting allows us to establish the following
quantitative characterization
of strong variational convexity via the strong convexity of usual Moreau envelope (
6.1
) with modulus interplay.

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be a prox-bounded function on a Hilbert space
X
that is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Then for any
\sigma>0
, the function
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
if and only if the Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}\varphi
is locally
\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma\lambda}
-strongly convex around
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{x}^{*}
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small.

[
Proof.]
For the “only if” part, deduce from Theorem
6.10
and Proposition
6.9
that

\partial\psi
is
\psi
-locally monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma\bar{x})
. By using (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv) in Theorem
6.6
, we know that the tilted Moreau envelope
e_{\theta}^{\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma\bar{x}}\psi
is locally convex around
\bar{x}
for small
\theta>0
. This is equivalent to the local convexity of
e_{\theta}\psi
around
\bar{x}+\theta(\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma\bar{x})=(1-\theta\sigma)\bar{x}+\theta\bar{x}^{*}
for such
\theta>0
due to (
6.7
). Proposition
6.8
yields

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)=e_{\lambda/(1+\sigma\lambda)}\psi\left(\dfrac{x}{1+\sigma\lambda}\right)+\dfrac{\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma\lambda}}{2}\|x\|^{2}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in X\;\mbox{ and small }\;\lambda,

(6.29)

where
\lambda>0
can be chosen so that
\lambda/(1+\sigma\lambda)>0
is also small. Combining this with (
6.29
) and the local convexity of
e_{\theta}\psi
when
\theta>0
is sufficiently small, we get that

e_{\lambda}\varphi\text{ is locally }\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma\lambda}\text{-strongly convex around }\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{x}^{*}\;\text{ for small }\;\lambda>0.

The “if” part is verified similarly using Proposition
5.3
, Theorem
6.6
and Proposition
6.8
.

7
Second-Order Characterizations of Variational Convexity

In this section, we involve tools of generalized differentiation in variational analysis to provide further characterizations of variational
\sigma
-convexity
(\sigma\geq 0)
for extended-real-valued functions on
Hilbert spaces
, which is assumed unless otherwise stated. Along with the notions of regular and limiting subdifferentials defined in Section
2
, we need some other constructions recalled as follows.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a nonempty set, and let
\delta(\cdot;\Omega)
be its indicator function equal to 0 if
x\in\Omega
and
\infty
otherwise. The
regular normal
and
limiting normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}\in\Omega
are defined, respectively, as the regular and limiting subdifferentials of the indicator function

\widehat{N}(\bar{x};\Omega):=\widehat{\partial}\delta(\bar{x};\Omega)\;\mbox{ and }\;N(\bar{x};\Omega):=\partial\delta(\bar{x};\Omega).

Note that, while the regular normal cone is always convex but the limiting normal cone is often nonconvex, the latter enjoys much better calculus in both finite and infinite dimensions; see, e.g.,
[
17
,
32
]
.

Next we recall the two notions of coderivatives for a multifunction
F\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
between Hilbert spaces. The
regular coderivative

of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
is defined by

\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F\big)\big\},\hskip 10.00002pty^{*}\in Y

(7.1)

via the regular normal cone to the graph of
F
. The
mixed coderivative
of
F
is given in the limiting form

D^{*}_{M}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\bar{y}^{*}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{(x,y,y^{*})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{y}^{*})}\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,y)(y^{*}),\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{y}^{*}\in Y^{*},

(7.2)

where the outer limit ‘
\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}
’ defined in (
2.1
) is taken with respect to the norm convergence on
Y^{*}=Y
and the weak convergence on
X^{*}=X
; see
[
17
]
for more details and comprehensive calculus rules available for (
7.2
) in more general settings. Invoking the coderivatives (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) applied to the limiting subdifferential (
2.3
), we get the second-order subdifferential constructions for
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
defined by

\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})(u):=(\widehat{D}^{*}\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})(u)\;\mbox{ and }\;{\partial}^{2}_{M}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})(u):=({D}^{*}_{M}{\partial}\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})(u),\hskip 10.00002ptu\in X,

(7.3)

known as the
combined second-order subdifferential
and the
mixed second-order subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
relative to
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, respectively. It has been well recognized in variational analysis and optimization that both second-order subdifferentials in (
7.3
) can be explicitly computed for broad classes of extended-real-valued functions, admit reasonable calculus rules (especially for
\partial^{2}_{M}\varphi
), provide characterizations of major tilt and full stability properties of optimal solutions and maximal monotonicity of subgradient operators with efficient applications to Newtonian algorithms of nonsmooth optimization and practical models of machine learning, etc.; see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
and the references therein.

Prior to deriving the main second-order subdifferential characterizations of variational
\sigma
-convexity, we present two technical results of their own interest. The first proposition shows among other things that in the setting under consideration below, the prox-boundedness of the function in question is a consequence of prox-regularity and subdifferential continuity.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function on a Banach space. Assume that
\varphi
is subdifferentially continuous and prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
relative to a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, and let
\sigma\geq 0
. Denoting
\psi:=\varphi+\delta_{U}
, we have the following assertions:

(i)

\psi
is prox-bounded on
X
as well as prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
.

(ii)

\partial\psi(x)=\partial\varphi(x)
for all
x\in U
. As a consequence,
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V)
and
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\cap(U^{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U^{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\times V)
for any
\varepsilon>0
, where
U^{\psi}_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in U\mid\psi(x)<\psi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\}
.

(iii)

\breve{\partial}^{2}\psi(x,x^{*})=\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,x^{*})
and
\partial^{2}_{M}\psi(x,x^{*})=\partial^{2}_{M}\varphi(x,x^{*})
for all
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\cap(U\times V)
.

(iv)
If
X
is reflexive,
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
, and
\psi
is
\sigma
-strongly variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
, then
\varphi
is also
\sigma
-strongly variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
.

[
Proof.]
The prox-regularity and subdiffential continuity of
\psi
in (i) immediately follow from these properties assumed for
\varphi
. Let us verify the prox-boundedness of
\psi
. It follows from the prox-regularity and subdifferential continuity of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
and from the construction of
\psi
that there is
r\geq 0
for which

\psi(x)\geq\psi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-(r/2)\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}

whenever
x\in U
. But since
\psi(x)=\infty
when
x\in X\setminus U
, the latter holds for all
x\in X
, which yields the prox-boundedness of
\psi
and hence justifies (i).

The properties in (ii) follow directly from the definitions. To verify (iii), we get from
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V)
in (ii) with the open set
U\times V
and from (
7.3
) that

\breve{\partial}^{2}\psi(x,x^{*})=\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,x^{*})\;\mbox{ and }\;\partial^{2}_{M}\psi(x,x^{*})=\partial^{2}_{M}\varphi(x,x^{*})\;\mbox{ for any }\;(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\cap(U\times V).

It remains to verify (iv) under the additional assumptions imposed therein, which ensure characterization (
5.5
) of variational
\sigma
-convexity in Theorem
5.5
. Applying (
5.5
) to
\psi
and remembering the definition of this function, we easily conclude that (
5.5
) holds for
\varphi
as well, which therefore justifies (iv) by Theorem
5.5
and hence completes the proof of the proposition.

The next proposition extends the well-known from finite dimensions to Hilbert spaces.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function defined on a Hilbert space
X
, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
. If
\varphi
is prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
, then the graphical set
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
is locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
.

[
Proof.]
Proposition
7.1
allows us to assume that
\varphi
is prox-bounded. Then it follows from
[
3
, Proposition 4.4]
under the subdifferential continuity and prox-regularity of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
that there exist constant
\lambda_{0},\varepsilon>0
and an
\varepsilon
-localization
T_{\varepsilon}
of
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
defined by

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T_{\varepsilon}:=\big\{(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\;\big|\;x\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\;\text{ and }\;x^{*}\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}^{*})\big\}

such that for any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
we get a closed neighborhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{x}^{*}
with

P_{\lambda}\varphi=(I+\lambda T_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\ \text{ on }U_{\lambda},

(7.4)

where
P_{\lambda}\varphi
single-valued and continuous on
U_{\lambda}
. Consider further the set

\displaystyle W:=\big\{(x,x^{*})\in X\times X\;\big|\;x\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}),\;x^{*}\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}^{*}),\;\text{ and }\;x+\lambda x^{*}\in U_{\lambda}\}.

Clearly,
W=\big(\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\times\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}^{*})\big)\cap\vartheta^{-1}\big(\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\times U_{\lambda}\big)
with
\vartheta(x,x^{*}):=(x,x+\lambda x^{*})
for all
(x,x^{*})\in X\times X
. Since
\vartheta
is a homeomorphism on
X\times X
, we get that
W
is a closed set with
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in{\rm int}\,W
. Let us check that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap W
is closed as well. Indeed, pick
\{(x_{k},x^{*}_{k})\}\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap W
such that
(x_{k},x^{*}_{k})\rightarrow(x,x^{*})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and get that
(x,x^{*})\in W
. Since
(x_{k},x^{*}_{k})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap W
, we have

(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T_{\varepsilon}\;\mbox{ and }\;x_{k}+\lambda x^{*}_{k}\in U_{\lambda}\;\mbox{ for all }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

(7.5)

Combining (
7.4
) with (
7.5
) and the single-valuedness of
P_{\lambda}\varphi
on
U_{\lambda}
yields

x_{k}=P_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k}+\lambda x^{*}_{k})
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
. Since
P_{\lambda}\varphi
is continuous on
U_{\lambda}
, we get from the latter that
x=P_{\lambda}\varphi(x+\lambda x^{*})
. As
(x,x^{*})\in W
, it follows that
x+\lambda x^{*}\in U_{\lambda}
, and thus from (
7.4
) we have

x=P_{\lambda}\varphi(x+\lambda x^{*})=(I+\lambda T_{\varepsilon})^{-1}(x+\lambda x^{*}).

This immediately implies that
x^{*}\in T_{\varepsilon}(x)
, which yields
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap W
and shows that the set
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
is locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
.

Now we are ready to derive second-order subdifferential characterizations of variational
\sigma
-convexity.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space, and let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
and weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
. Then for any
\sigma\geq 0
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is variationally
\sigma
-convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
.

(ii)

\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
and there exist neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{x}^{*}
such that

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\;\text{ whenever}\;z\in\partial^{2}_{M}\varphi(x,y)(w),\;(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V),\;w\in X.

(7.6)

(iii)

\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
and there exist neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
, and
V
of
\bar{x}^{*}
such that

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,y)(w),\;(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V),\;w\in X.

(7.7)

[
Proof.]
Regarding (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), observe first that the prox-regularity of the function
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
is a direct consequence of its
\sigma
-strong variational convexity. On the other hand, it follows from Theorem
6.6
for
\sigma=0
(resp. Theorem
6.10
for
\sigma>0
) that the variational
\sigma
-convexity and subdifferential continuity of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
yields the local (resp. local
\sigma
-strong) maximal monotonicity of
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
. Then we get (
7.6
) by
[
11
, Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 6.4]
.

Implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) is trivial since the mixed second-order subdifferential always contains the combined one. To verify the remaining implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i), we deduce from Proposition
7.1
that (iii) ensures that the function
\psi=\varphi+\delta_{U}
is prox-bounded, subdifferentially continuous and prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
while satisfying the condition

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\psi(x,y)(w),\;(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\cap(U\times V),\;w\in X.

(7.8)

As follows from Proposition
7.2
,
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi
is closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
. To apply now the local maximal monotonicity characterizations from
[
11
, Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 6.4]
to
\partial\psi
, we need the local hypomonotonicity property of
\partial\psi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, which easily follows from the prox-regularity of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
due to Proposition
7.1
(i). The aforementioned characterizations of the local maximal monotonicity from
[
11
]
tell us that (
7.8
) guarantees the local maximal monotonicity (resp. local
\sigma
-strong maximal monotonicity) of
\partial\psi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
. Hence we deduce from Theorem
6.6
and Theorem
6.10
that
\psi
is variationally
\sigma
-convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
, which together with
Proposition
7.1
(iii) justifies the claimed assertion (i).

Finally, we present the following example, which shows that the
subdifferential continuity
is
essential
for the characterizations of variational
\sigma
-convexity in Theorem
7.3
even for
univariate
functions.

Example 7.4
.

Consider the l.s.c. function
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
given by
\varphi(x):=0
if
x=0
and
\varphi(x):=1
otherwise. The subgradient mapping
\partial\varphi
is calculated as

\partial\varphi(x)={\rm I\!R}
for
x=0
and
\partial\varphi(x)=\{0\}
otherwise. This shows that
\varphi
is prox-regular while not subdifferentially continuous at
0
for
0\in\partial\varphi(0)
. Denoting
U_{1}:=\{x\in{\rm I\!R}\mid\varphi(x)<\varphi(0)+1\}=\{x\in{\rm I\!R}\mid\varphi(x)<1\}=\{0\}
, we see that

(U_{1}\times{\rm I\!R})\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi=(\{0\}\times{\rm I\!R})\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi=\{0\}\times{\rm I\!R},

and thus
\partial\varphi
is
\varphi
-locally strongly monotone around
(0,0)
with modulus
1
. Theorem
6.10
tells us that
\varphi
is
1
-strongly variationally convex at
0
for
0
. Now we check that condition (
7.7
) fails for
\varphi
. Indeed, fix any neighborhood
U\times V
of
(0,0)
and pick
(x,0)\in U\times V
with
x<0
. Locally around
(x,0)
, the graph of
\partial\varphi
agrees with the graph of the function
f\equiv 0
, and therefore

\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,0)(1)=(\widehat{D}^{*}\partial\varphi)(x,0)(1)=(\widehat{D}^{*}f)(x)(1)=\{0\}.

This confirms the failure of (
7.7
) for
\sigma=1
and
w=1
.

Strong Variational Convexity and Tilt Stability

This section is devoted to establishing relationships between strong variational convexity of extended-real-valued functions on Banach spaces and tilt stability of local minimizers in the associated minimization problems. First we recall the fundamental notion of tilt-stable local minimizers introduced and studied by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
26
]
in finite-dimensional spaces.

Definition 8.1
.

For an l.s.c. function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on a Banach space,
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is a
tilt-stable local minimizer
of
\varphi
if there are neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
0\in X
such that the mapping

M_{V,U}(x^{*}):=\argmin\big\{\varphi(x)-\langle x^{*},x\rangle\;\big|\;x\in U\big\}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }\ x^{*}\in V

(8.1)

is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous with
M_{V,U}(0)=\{\bar{x}\}
. We also say that
\sigma\geq 0
is a
modulus
of tilt stability for
\bar{x}
if
\sigma
is a constant of Lipschitz continuity of (
8.1
).

Starting with
[
26
]
, where tilt-stable minimizers of prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous functions
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
were characterized via the positive-definiteness of the
basic second-order subdifferential/generalized Hessian
by Mordukhovich
\partial^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0):=\partial^{2}_{M}\varphi(\bar{x},0)
in (
7.3
), so much has been done for characterizations and applications of tilt stability in various classes of optimization problems in finite and infinite dimensions; see
[
5
,
7
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
22
,
23
,
29
,
33
]
for just a few references. The next theorem establishes close relationships between tilt stability and strong variational convexity in Banach spaces.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function on a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Consider the following assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is strongly variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}=0
.

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable local minimizer of
\varphi
.

Then we have implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
if
X
is
2
-uniformly convex and Gâteaux smooth. The reverse implication
(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i)
holds if
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
, subdifferentially continuous and prox-regular with a constant
r\geq 0
at
\bar{x}
for
0
, and if either
X
is
2
-uniformly smooth with
r<\sigma
where
\sigma^{-1}
is the modulus of tilt-stability of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
, or
X
is a Hilbert space.

[
Proof.]
To verify (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), suppose that
X
is
2
-uniformly convex and Gâteaux smooth. This implies that
\|\cdot\|
is strictly convex, Fréchet smooth by Proposition
6.4
, and Kadec-Klee, i.e., it satisfies (
4.6
). By definition of strong variational convexity and its characterization in Theorem
5.5
, the strong variational convexity of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}=0

Claim 1:
For

To conclude this section, we present two remarks about the role of prox-regularity and subdifferential continuity assumptions in the obtained characterizations.

Remark 8.3
.

When

, it is proved in

Remark 8.4

Due to the equivalence in Theorem

characterize tilt stability of local minimizers for prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous functions in Hilbert spaces. This has already been known from

\langle z,w\rangle>0\;\mbox{ whenever }\;z\in\partial^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w),\;w\neq 0

Example 8.5
.

Consider the (nonconvex) function

9
Concluding Remarks

This paper provides a systematic study of variational and strong variational convexity of nonsmooth functions in Banach spaces. Although these notions have been recently introduced and studied in finite dimensions, their importance has been already realized in variational analysis, optimization, and applications. We derive various characterizations of these and related notions in infinite-dimensional spaces and establish their relationships between local maximal monotonicity of subgradient operators, positive-semidefiniteness of second-order subdifferentials, tilt stability of local minimizers, etc.

Among major open questions of our future research, we mention applications of variational and strong variational convexity to
variational sufficiency
in structured optimization, which has been recently studied in finite dimensions
[
13
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
34
]
. Then we aim at applying the obtained results to numerical algorithms of nonsmooth optimization in finite-dimensional and infinite-dimensional spaces.

Diffusion Approximation for Transport Equations with Dissipative Drifts for Time Dependent Coefficients
Luca Di Persio, Yuri Kondratiev, Viktorya Vardanyan

Abstract
We consider a generalization of classical results of Freidlin and Wentzell to the case of time dependent dissipative drifts. We show the convergence of diffusions with multiplicative noise in the zero limit of a diffusivity parameter to the related dynamical systems. The solution to the associated transport equation is obtained as an application.



Keywords:
diffusion process, dynamical system, perturbation parameter, local Lipschitz condition, time dependent dissipative drift, zeroth order approximation, Cauchy problem, transport equation

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification 2010:
47D07, 37P30, 60J65, 60G55

Introduction

Dynamical systems with small random perturbations are the subject of well know studies by Freidlin and Wentzell
[
1
]
.
Their classical results essentially cover the case of diffusions with regular drifts and small parameters in multiplicative noise. The main problem within latter scenario, which is the focus of our study, is the convergence of such diffusions to deterministic dynamical systems and related evolution equation of transport type.

We study stochastic differential equations(SDEs) with a small perturbation parameter, in which the coefficients depend on the time.
The dependence on time is the main novelty in our considerations comparing with
[
1
]
.

Let us also underline that in
[
1
]

the authors work under standard probabilistic assumptions on the drift in the considered diffusions, namely: global Lipschitz
condition and linear bound on the growth. Nevertheless, in several applications in mathematical physics, these assumptions are too restrictive, as in the case of, e.g., the stochastic quantisation method.

We consider the dissipative condition on the drift coefficient and the local Lipschitz condition on the drift and diffusion coefficients formulated for
0\leq t\leq T
, where
0<T<+\infty
is a finite time horizon, with constants depending on
T
. Under latter conditions, we prove both existence and uniqueness for the perturbed SDE. Moreover, we show the convergence result for the solution of the perturbed system to the solution of the unperturbed system, when the perturbation parameter approaches zero. We then consider an application of the above-mentioned results to the Cauchy problem and the transport equations.

The model

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be a reference filtered probability space. For the sake of completeness, let us recall that the latter means that
\Omega
is a nonempty set, interpreted as the space of elementary events, while
\mathcal{F}
, is a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
\Omega
,
\mathbb{P}
is a probability measure on the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
, and

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
,
0<T<+\infty
being a finite horizon time, is a filtration over
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Finally, we define
W=\{W_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
as a given
l
-dimensional standard Brownian motion adapted to the just defined filtration.

We consider an equation

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=b(t,x(t)),\ x(0)=x_{0}

(1)

and the perturbed stochastic differential equation

dX^{\varepsilon}(t)=b(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t))dt+\varepsilon\sigma(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t))dW_{t},\ X^{\varepsilon}(0)=x_{0}

(2)

in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
,
\varepsilon
representing a fixed,
small
, multiplicative constant. The functions
b(t,x)
and
\sigma(t,x)
are measurable functions on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{r}
, taking, respectively, values in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
and
\mathbb{R}^{r\times l}
,

x_{0}
is a given
\mathbb{R}^{r}
-valued random variable independent of
W_{t}
.

The stochastic process
X^{\varepsilon}(t)
is considered as a result of small perturbations of (
1
), with perturbation parameter
\varepsilon
.
Solving (
2
), means finding a random process
X_{t}=X_{t}(W)
which satisfies the relation

X^{\varepsilon}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))ds+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))dW_{s},

with probability 1 for every
t\in[0,T]
.
Let us first assume that the coefficients of our diffusion are Lipschitz continuous and with linear growth bound. This implies that (
2
) admits a uniquely determined solution. Equations of the form (
2
) were studied, e.g.,
by Gihman and Skorohod
[
4
]
in Section 2 of Chapter 2 and in Section 3 of Chapter 3.

We want to prove that such (sequence of) solution(s) of SDE (
2
) converges to the solution of (
1
) uniformly, when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, also estimating the convergence rate.

Before proving main results, let us recall the following result that will be then exploited in our proofs.

Lemma 2.1
.

[Gronwall]
Let
m(t),t\in[0,T]
, be a non-negative function satisfying

m(t)\leq C+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}m(s)ds,\ t\in[0,T]

(3)

with
C,\alpha\geq 0
.

Then
m(t)\leq Ce^{\alpha t},
for
t\in[0,T]
.

3
Zeroth Order Approximation

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the coefficients of (
2
) are linearly bounded and satisfy a uniform Lipschitz condition, i.e. for every
T>0
there exist constants
K_{T}
,
L_{T}
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
,
t\in[0,T]
:

|b(t,x)|^{2}+|\sigma(t,x)|^{2}\leq K_{T}^{2}(1+|x|^{2})

(4)

|b(t,x)-b(t,y)|^{2}+|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)|^{2}\leq L_{T}^{2}|x-y|^{2}

(5)

Then for all
t>0
and
\delta>0
we have:

1.

E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)|^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}a(t)
, and

2.

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}P\{\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|>\delta\}=0

where a(t) is a monotone increasing function expressed in terms of
E|x_{0}|
,
L_{T}
and
K_{T}
.

Proof.

We start showing that
1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. To this end, we use Ito’s formula, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the condition (
4
) of linear boundedness for the SDE (
2
) coefficients.

\displaystyle 1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=1+E|x_{0}|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}E<X^{\varepsilon}(s),b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))>ds+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}E|\sigma(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq 1+E|x_{0}|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}E\sqrt{|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))^{2}}ds+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}E|\sigma(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq 1+E|x_{0}|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}E\sqrt{|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}K_{T}^{2}(1+|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})}ds

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}E[K_{T}^{2}(1+|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})]ds

\displaystyle\leq 1+E|x_{0}|^{2}+(2K_{T}+\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2})\int_{0}^{t}(1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})ds

Then, in view of applying the Gronwall Lemma (
3
), we choose
m(t)=1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}
,
C=1+E|x_{0}|^{2}
and
\alpha=2K_{T}+\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2}
, then
obtaining

1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\leq(1+E|x_{0}|^{2})\exp[(2K_{T}+\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2})t].

(6)

In what follows, we use the above result to show that
E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)|^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}a(t)
.
Proceeding as before, using Lipschitz condition (
5
) and the boundedness of
1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}
i.e.(
6
), we have:

\displaystyle E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}E<X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s),b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))>ds

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}E|\sigma(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}E\sqrt{|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|^{2}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|^{2}}ds

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}E|\sigma(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}E\sqrt{|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|^{2}L_{T}^{2}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|^{2}}ds

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})ds

\displaystyle\leq 2L_{T}\int_{0}^{t}E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|^{2}ds+\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})ds

By Lemma (
3
), choosing
m(t)=E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)|^{2}
,
\alpha=2L_{T}
,
C=\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})ds
, we derive
the following inequality

\displaystyle E|X^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{2L_{T}t}\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})ds

\displaystyle\leq e^{2L_{T}t}\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+E|x_{0}|^{2})\exp[(2K_{T}+\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2})s]ds

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}a(t)\,,

and we conclude choosing
a(t)

such that it is a monotone increasing function.

Concerning the second part of the theorem, we first use the definition of our diffusion processes (
1
) and (
2
) to get

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|

\displaystyle=\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}b(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dv+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dw_{v}-\int_{0}^{s}b(v,x(v))dv|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}b(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dv-\int_{0}^{s}b(v,x(v)dv|

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dw_{v}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|ds+\varepsilon\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dw_{v}|.

(7)

Then, by the Chebyshev inequality, setting

\xi(\omega)=\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|ds,\ a=\frac{\delta}{2},\ f(x)=x^{2}\,,

we estimate the first term on the right hand side of (
7
), i.e.:

\displaystyle P\{\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|ds>\frac{\delta}{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{E[\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|ds]^{2}}{(\frac{\delta}{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\delta^{-2}tE\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq 4t\delta^{-2}L_{T}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq 4tL_{T}^{2}\delta^{-2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\delta^{-2}a_{1}(t)

(8)

where in the second step we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, in the third we applied the Lipschitz condition for the drift, then concluding exploiting the first assertion of the theorem.
Having obtained an estimate for the first term on the right hand side, we move forward to estimate the second term on the right hand side of (
7
).
To this aim we first use the generalized Kolmogorov inequality for stochastic integrals, namely:

P\{\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}|\int_{a}^{t}f(s,\omega)dw_{s}|>C|\mathcal{N}_{a}\}\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}}E(\int_{a}^{b}f^{2}(s,\omega)ds|\mathcal{N}_{a})\,,

(9)

then, setting
f(v,\omega)=\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v)),\ C=\frac{\delta}{2\varepsilon},\mathcal{N}_{a}=\Omega,\ a=0,b=t
, we can rewrite (
9
) as follows

\displaystyle P\{\varepsilon\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dw_{v}|>\frac{\delta}{2}\}

\displaystyle=P\{\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dw_{v}|>\frac{\delta}{2\epsilon}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\frac{\delta}{2\varepsilon})^{2}}E(\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}ds)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}K_{T}^{2}(1+E|X^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})ds

\displaystyle\leq 4\varepsilon^{2}\delta^{-2}K_{T}^{2}(1+E|x_{0}|^{2})\int_{0}^{t}\exp[(2K_{T}+\varepsilon^{2}K_{T}^{2})s]ds

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\delta^{-2}a_{2}(t)\,

(10)

so that, combining (
7
), (
8
) and (
10
), we have:

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}P\{\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-x(s)|>\delta\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}P\{\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|ds

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dw_{v}|>\delta\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}P\{\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,x(s))|ds>\frac{\delta}{2}\}

\displaystyle+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}P\{\varepsilon\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(v,X^{\varepsilon}(v))dw_{v}|>\frac{\delta}{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}[\varepsilon^{2}\delta^{-2}a_{1}(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\delta^{-2}a_{2}(t)]=0.

which concludes the proof.
∎

Zeroth Order Approximation for Dissipative Case

4.1
Existence and Uniqueness of a Solution of the SDE with Dissipative Drift

We aim at generalizing what obtained within the previous section to consider weaker conditions on the coefficients of eq. (
2
). Namely, we aim at finding that eq. (
2

Moreover, after proving both existence and uniqueness, we will also derive the zeroth order approximation for the process solving (
2

Let us start proving a result similar to Theorem 2, Chapter 2, Section 6,
[
2
]
,
where the Authors considered a similar SDE-problem in dimension

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the coefficients

,

Proof.

Define
\gamma_{1}(t):=1

Then we get

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)[X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)]

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}(t)\int_{0}^{t}[b_{1}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-b_{2}(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))]ds

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}(t)\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma_{1}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-\sigma_{2}(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))]dW_{s}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}(t)\int_{0}^{t}[b_{1}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-b_{2}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))]ds

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}(t)\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma_{1}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-\sigma_{2}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))]dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}(t)\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma_{2}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-\sigma_{2}(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))]dW_{s}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}(t)\int_{0}^{t}[b_{2}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-b_{2}(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))]ds

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)[X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\gamma_{1}(t)\Big[\int_{0}^{t}[b_{2}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-b_{2}(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))]ds\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle+2\gamma_{1}(t)\varepsilon^{2}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma_{1}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-\sigma_{2}(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))]dW_{s}\Big]^{2}.

\gamma_{1}(t)=1

\gamma_{1}(s)=1

we can write the
\gamma_{1}(s)
inside the brackets. Taking the expectation and then using the Lipschitz condition and Cauchy-Schwarz-inequality, we can show that for

\displaystyle E\Big[\gamma_{1}(t)[X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)]^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq E\Big[4[\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{1}(s)L_{T}|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)|ds]^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 4L_{T}^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}E[\gamma_{1}(s)|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}]ds

\displaystyle=P\{\psi(x_{0})\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{N}^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}>N^{2}\psi(x_{0})\}

\overline{\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}}P\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{N}^{\varepsilon}(t)|>N\}\leq P\{\psi(x_{0})\leq\delta\}.

results from the preceding relation, which completes the proof of the existence of a solution to (

Next we want to prove the uniqueness of the solution.
Let

be two solutions of (

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)|\leq N
and equal to 0 otherwise, then by the second condition, we have:

\displaystyle E|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\phi(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2E[\phi(t)(\int_{0}^{t}|b(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))|ds)^{2}]

\displaystyle+2E[\phi(t)(\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-\sigma(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))|dW_{s})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq 2tE[(\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)|b(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}ds)]

\displaystyle+2E[(\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)|\sigma(s,X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s))-\sigma(s,X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s))|^{2}ds)]

\displaystyle\leq(2T+2)L_{N,T}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}E(\phi(s)|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2})ds\,,

where we used that

Then, by Lemma

with

, we obtain

, implying:

indeed,

Hence the probability on the right side of this inequality tend to zero as N

∎

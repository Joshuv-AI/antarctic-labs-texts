Recurrence, rigidity, and popular differences
John T. Griesmer

Abstract
. We construct a set of integers S S such that every translate of S S is a set of recurrence and a set of rigidity for a weak mixing measure preserving system. Here “set of rigidity” means that enumerating S S as ( s n ) n ∈ ℕ (s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}} produces a rigidity sequence. This construction generalizes or strengthens results of Katznelson, Saeki (on equidistribution and the Bohr topology), Forrest (on sets of recurrence and strong recurrence), and Fayad and Kanigowski (on rigidity sequences). The construction also provides a density analogue of Julia Wolf’s results on popular differences in finite abelian groups.

2.4.

3.
Fourier transforms of measures, Proof of Theorem
2.1

Proposition
3.2

Here
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is the torus with the usual topology. We identify
\mathbb{T}
with the interval
[0,1)

e_{n}:[0,1)\to\mathbb{C}
given by

A Borel measure
\mu

If

\sigma
on

and a sequence of integers

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int|\exp(2\pi is_{n}\theta)-1|\,d\sigma(\theta)=0

is continuous, these conditions imply that the sequence

is a rigidity sequence for a weak mixing system, as the following lemma states.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)

A sequence
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a rigidity sequence for a weak mixing system if and only if there is a continuous measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|e_{s_{n}}-1\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}=0
. Consequently:

(ii)

A translated sequence
(s_{n}-m)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a rigidity sequence for a weak mixing system if and only if there is a continuous probability measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|e_{s_{n}}-e_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}=0
.

See Proposition 2.10 of
[
1
]
for a proof of Lemma

(i) and further exposition. Part (ii) follows from part (i) and multiplying the integrand in

A

is isomorphic to

Below we list some well-known properties of uniquely ergodic topological systems. Let

be a uniquely ergodic topological system with invariant probability measure

is continuous, so that

(U1)

If

(U2)

If

, and

(U3)

For all

.

[

, starting with a clopen set
A
in the proof.

We say a set

5.
The Bohr topology and recurrence

The
Bohr topology
on
\mathbb{Z}
is the weakest topology on
\mathbb{Z}
such that every homomorphism
\chi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{T}
is continuous. See Chapter 5 of
[

for more on the Bohr topology and difference sets.

Remark 5.1
.

A subbase for the Bohr topology consists of sets of the form
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,U):=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\alpha\in U\}
, where
U\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is open and
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
. In other words, the Bohr topology consists of arbitrary unions of finite intersections of sets of the form
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,U)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If every translate of

is a set of recurrence, then
S
is dense in the Bohr topology on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

This is a standard fact, so we only outline the proof. The following standard facts imply the conclusion:

(i)

Every neighborhood in the Bohr topology has positive upper Banach density.

(ii)

Every Bohr neighborhood
U
of the identity
0_{\mathbb{Z}}
contains a difference set
V-V
of a Bohr neighborhood
V
of the identity.

(iii)

Every Bohr neighborhood of a point contains a translate of a Bohr neighborhood of
0_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

∎

Whether the converse of Proposition
5.2
holds is an open problem, equivalent to Question
1.1
: if the answer to Question
1.1
is “yes,” then every set
S
dense in the Bohr topology of
\mathbb{Z}
is a set of recurrence, hence every translate of such a set is a set of recurrence.

5.2.
Equidistribution

A set

is

if there is a sequence of finite subsets

such that for every

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{|S_{j}|}\sum_{n\in S_{j}}\exp(2\pi in\alpha)=0.

It follows easily from the definition that

is equidistributed iff every translate of

is equidistributed. Furthermore, standard arguments show that if

is a sequence of finite sets satisfying equation (

and every

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{|S_{j}|}\sum_{n\in S_{j}}\mu(D\cap T^{n}D)\geq\mu(D)^{2}.

Consequently, every translate of every equidistributed set is a set of optimal recurrence.

such that

(E)

9.
Characterization of upper Banach density

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be finite and let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. There is a
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|(A-n)\cap F|\geq d^{*}(A)\cdot|F|
.

Proof.

We outline the proof in this paragraph and present an explicit proof in the next. First, find a long interval
I
having
\frac{1}{|I|}|I\cap A|\approx d^{*}(A)
. We may assume that
I=\{1,\dots,N\}
for some large
N
,
F\subseteq I
, and
(\max F)/N
is small. Consider the intersections
I\cap(A-n)\cap F
. Summing their cardinalities, we get
\sum_{n=1}^{N}|I\cap(A-n)\cap F|\approx|F|\cdot|I\cap A|
, as each element of
I\cap A
is counted approximately
|F|
times in the sum. So on average,
|I\cap(A-n)\cap F|
is approximately
\frac{1}{N}\cdot|F|\cdot|I\cap A|\approx|F|\cdot d^{*}(A)
, and there must be an
n
such that
|(A-n)\cap F|\geq d^{*}(A)
.

Here is an explicit proof: Let
\mathbf{\Phi}=(\Phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Følner sequence such that
d^{*}(A)=d_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(A)
. Let
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. For sufficiently large
j
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Phi_{j}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{j}}|(A-n)\cap F|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{j}|}\sum_{n^{\prime}\in F}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{j}}1_{A-n}(n^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{j}|}\sum_{n^{\prime}\in F}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{j}}1_{A}(n+n^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n^{\prime}\in F}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{j}|}|A\cap(\Phi_{j}-n^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n^{\prime}\in F}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{j}|}(|A\cap\Phi_{j}|-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{j}|}(|A\cap\Phi_{j}|-\varepsilon)|F|

\displaystyle\geq(d^{*}(A)-\varepsilon)|F|.

From the above inequalities, we see that for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, the average
\frac{1}{|\Phi_{j}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{j}}|(A-n)\cap F|
is at least
d^{*}(A)|F|-\varepsilon^{\prime}
. The pigeonhole principle then implies
|(A-n)\cap F|\geq d^{*}(A)|F|-\varepsilon^{\prime}
for some
n
. Using the integrality of
|(A-n)\cap F|
, we conclude that
|(A-n)\cap F|\geq d^{*}(A)|F|
for some
n
. ∎

Deriving the following corollary from Lemma
9.1
is an easy exercise.

Corollary 9.2
.

For
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
d^{*}(A)
is the largest number
\delta
satisfying the following condition: for all finite sets
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, there exists
n
such that
|(A-n)\cap F|\geq\delta|F|
.

The following version of Furstenberg’s correspondence principle is a special case of Proposition 3.1 in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. Then there is an ergodic measure preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
and a set
D\subseteq X
having
\mu(D)=d^{*}(A)
, and

d^{*}(A\cap(A-m))\geq\mu(D\cap T^{-m}D)

The next lemma is a partial converse of the correspondence principle. It is a special case of Proposition 3.2.7 in

]

.

(X,\mu,T)

. Then there is a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
having
d^{*}(A)\geq\mu(D)
such that

Combining Lemmas
10.1
and

Lemma 10.3
.

\delta>0
and let

contains

, where

.

contains

.

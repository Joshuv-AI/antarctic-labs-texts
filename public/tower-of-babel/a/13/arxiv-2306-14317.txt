Topological expansion for posets and the homological k k -connectivity of random q q -complexes
Ran Tessler, Elad Tzalik

Abstract
We study high dimensional expansion beyond simplicial complexes (posets) and focus on q q -complexes which are complexes whose basic building blocks are linear spaces over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . We show that the complete q q -complex (consists of all subspaces of a given linear space) may have non-trivial homology groups and therefore some techniques for simplicial complexes fail. We develop new techniques to bypass this. In particular: 1. We describe a new construction of cones and use it to determine when the homology of the complete q q -complex is trivial. We use this construction to prove the ”projective support dimension conjecture” conjectured by Mnukhin and Siemons [ 20 ] . 2. We define topological high dimensional expansion and topological overlap for posets, and show that the complete q q -complex has linear (in the number of lines) coboundary expansion and satisfies the topological overlap property. 3. We define the q q -Linial-Meshulam model of random q q complexes and prove a sharp threshold for the connectivity of random q q -complexes.

1.3
Coboundary expansion

Coboundary expansion was first defined in the foundational works of Linial, Meshulam and Wallach (
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
) on the
k

[
11
]
.

For a cochain
\alpha
its size is defined by
|\alpha|=|{\rm supp}(\alpha)|
. Define the size of the cohomology class of
\alpha
to be the minimum size over elements in the class of
\alpha
, that is

\lVert[\alpha]\rVert=\min\{|\alpha-d_{k-1}\beta|\text{ for }\beta\in C^{k-1}\}.

With those two notions, one can generalize the coboundary expansion of graphs to other cochain complexes. The coboundary expansion at dimension
k

h_{k}
is defined by:

h_{k}=\min_{\alpha\in C^{k}(X)\setminus B^{k}(X)}\left\{\frac{|d_{k}\alpha|}{\lVert[\alpha]\rVert}\right\}

In
Section

2.2
we show (with explicit bound on
C_{k}
):

Theorem
23
.

Let
h_{k,n}
be the expansion constant of
k
cochains of
Gr(V)
where dim
V=n>2k
. There exist a constant
C_{k}
that depends on
k,q
such that:

h_{k,n}\geq C_{k}{n\choose 1}_{q}

We remark that our proof also applies in the case
q=1
, though the bound on the expansion constant is noticeably worse than the optimal one obtained by Meshulam and Wallach
[
19
]
.

We also propose, in Section
2.4
, a generalization of Gromov’s topological overlap property
[
11
]
, and prove that the complete Grassmannian complex

satisfies it (for all
q
).

We consider the connectivity of Linial-Meshulam random

Let

This section studies the homological properties of

If

Proof.

The term

Notice that

\displaystyle[\partial_{k+1}d_{k}(U)-d_{k-1}\partial_{k}(U)]_{U}=(n-k\mod 2)-(k\mod 2)=(-1)^{k}

(1)

Proposition 13

For

It is enough to prove the identity for a

\displaystyle\partial(c_{\vec{b},W})=\partial\left(\left.d_{k}\right|_{W_{\vec{b}}}(-1)^{k}\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)\right)

Since

\displaystyle\partial\left(\left.d_{k}\right|_{W_{\vec{b}}}(-1)^{k}\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left.d_{k-1}\right|_{W_{\vec{b}}}\partial_{k}\left((-1)^{k}\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)\right)+(-1)^{k}\left((-1)^{k}\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left.d_{k-1}\right|_{W_{\vec{b}}}\partial_{k}\left((-1)^{k}\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)\right)+\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)

\displaystyle\left.d_{k-1}\right|_{W_{\vec{b}}}\partial_{k}\left((-1)^{k}\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)\right)=0

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\left((-1)^{k}\left(W-c_{\vec{b},\partial(W)}\right)\right)

We now use the cones constructed for

We use this technique together with the cone construction of

We remark that there is small set expansion, meaning that if a chain has a few subspaces then it must expand by simple counting:

Claim 19

\displaystyle{n-k\choose 1}_{q}|\alpha|-{|\alpha|\choose 2}

\displaystyle|d_{k}\alpha|\geq{n-k\choose 1}_{q}|\alpha|-{|\alpha|\choose 2}-{|\alpha|\choose 2}\geq{n-k\choose 1}_{q}|\alpha|-|\alpha|\left(|\alpha|-1\right)

2.2.1

We now turn to prove high dimensional expansion of

Claim 20

\iota_{s}d_{k}\alpha+d_{k-1}\iota_{s}\alpha=\alpha

Claim 21

Let

\displaystyle|c_{\vec{b},W}|\leq[k+1]_{q}\left([k]_{q}!^{2}\cdot\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{[j]_{q}!^{2}}\right)\right)

(2)

Remark 22

Theorem 23
.

Let

be the expansion constant of

where dim

. Let

be the bound of the cone size for
k

1.

, where

2.

Proof.

For the first item, let

be the set of all bases of

then

\displaystyle|S|\cdot\lVert[\alpha]\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in S}\lVert\alpha-d_{k-1}\iota_{s}\alpha\rVert

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in S}\lVert\iota_{s}d_{k}\alpha\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{W\in Gr(k,n)}\mathds{1}_{d_{k}\alpha(c_{s,W})\neq 0}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{W\in Gr(k,n)}\mathds{1}_{{\rm supp}(c_{s,W})\cap{\rm supp}(d_{k}\alpha)\neq\emptyset}

By symmetry each element of

\displaystyle|S|\cdot\lVert[\alpha]\rVert\leq\frac{|S|\cdot|Gr_{k}(n)|\cdot b}{|Gr_{k+1}(n)|}\cdot\lVert d_{k}\alpha\rVert

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert d_{k}\alpha\rVert}{\lVert[\alpha]\rVert}\geq\frac{|Gr_{k+1}(n)|}{|Gr_{k}(n)|\cdot b}=\frac{[n-k]_{q}}{[k+1]_{q}}\cdot\frac{1}{b}

For the second item, each closed chain

can be filled by

We now define topological overlap for posets and show the complete Grassmannian is a poset that satisfies the topological overlap property.

A ranked poset

3

The aim of this section is to prove a

[
17
]

[
19

) on random simplicial complexes. They studied the probability measure

on simplicial complexes

that contain subsets of size at most

for some parameter

and contain all

subsets in their support. For each such complex

the probability to get

\mathbb{P}[X]=p^{|X(k+1)|}\cdot(1-p)^{{n\choose k+1}_{q}-|X(k+1)|}

Their motivation was to generalize the result of Erdős–Rényi on the threshold for connectivity of a random graph to a higher dimension. They considered homological connectivity, which simply is defined by having
H^{k}(X)=0
. Their main result is that a complex sampled from
\triangle(n,k,p)
the for connectivity is sharp around
p=\frac{k\ln(n)}{n}
in the sense of
Theorem

5
.

The strategy of their proof is to union bound over the probability that a cochain
\alpha\in C^{k}(X)\setminus B^{k-1}(X)
is a cocycle in
X
. Each such
\alpha
is a cocycle in
X
exactly when
{\rm supp}(d_{k1}\alpha)\cap X=\emptyset
which happens with probability

.

In order to bound these probabilities, they proved that the complete complex is a coboundary expander. With this observation, the probability that
\alpha
will be a cocycle in the random complex can be bounded by
\exp({-\lVert[\alpha]\rVert\cdot cn})
for some constant
c>0
. Even with this estimate, there are still too many cochains to union-bound over. The way they overcome this is by looking at the Down-Up connected components of
\alpha
, specifically considering the graph
G_{\alpha}
which vertex set is
{\rm supp}(\alpha)
and two sets
v,u
are connected if
|v\cap u|=k-1
. The main observation is that if
G_{\alpha}
is disconnected, and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
is a decomposition of
\alpha
with respect to these components, then
{\rm supp}\left(d_{k}\alpha_{1}\right)\cap{\rm supp}\left(d_{k}\alpha_{2}\right)=\emptyset
. In particular
\alpha
is a cocycle iff
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are cocycles. Therefore it is enough to union bound over
\alpha
which are minimal in their cohomology class and have connected support.

We use the same strategy to prove the phase transition in connectivity of random
q
-complexes. We already proved in

4
Concluding Remarks

In this work, we studied the homology of Grassmannian posets, its topological expansion, and a generalization of the Linial-Meshulam model for random
q
-complexes.

The Grassmannian poset is the
q\neq 1-
analog of the simplex, hence it should be the building block for more general Grassmannian complexes. Unlike its
q=1
analog, the simplex, its homology is non-trivial. This non-triviality may imply a very rich homological picture for more complicated Grassmannian complexes.

Despite this complication, the topological expansion of the simplex generalizes to general
q,
as well as the phase transition in homological connectivity. It makes sense to ask which other topological and probabilistic properties of complexes generalize well.

Does the giant component phenomena in random graphs generalize to
q
-complexes sampled in the
q
-Linial-Meshulam model? A possible definition of a giant component is a subspace of codimension
O(1)
such that the induced complex on it has trivial homology.

Question 42
.

What is the threshold for having a giant connected component? Which properties of the random graph
G(n,\frac{c}{n})
transfer to random
q
graphs? (E.g. scale of a critical window, decorated expander structure, etc.)

Another natural question, which may have application to error correcting codes, is whether, for all dimensions
d
, there is a family of bounded degree (each line is contained in at most
C
faces for some constant
C(d)
)
q
-complexes which are coboundary expanders in dimension
d
.

Finally, we believe a very interesting question is to find a signed boundary map for the Grassmannian poset which satisfies
\partial^{2}=0
over a ring of characteristic
0
. Specifically, we believe it is intriguing to decide whether there is a boundary signing map for the complete
q
-complex with signs in the subring of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of the
q+1
roots of unity.

Expansion by restrictions

Claim 43
.

There exist
c>0
that depend only on

such that for any 1-co-chain
\alpha
on

|d_{1}\alpha|\geq c|\alpha|\cdot\left({n\choose 1}_{q}-|\alpha|\right)

Proof.

The proof is by induction. Assume that for a co-chain
\alpha
on

:

|d_{1}\alpha|\geq c|\alpha|\cdot\left({n\choose 1}_{q}-|\alpha|\right)

Let
\alpha
be a co-chain on

, then we can calculate
|d_{0}\alpha|
by calculating its restrictions on hyperplanes of codimension 1. i.e.

\displaystyle|d_{1}\alpha|=\frac{1}{|Gr_{n-2}(n-1)|}\sum_{H\in Gr_{n}(n+1)}\left|\left.d_{1}\alpha\right|_{H}\right|\geq\frac{1}{{n-1\choose 1}_{q}}\sum_{H\in Gr_{n-2}(n-1)}\left|\left.d_{1}\right|_{H}\left.\alpha\right|_{H}\right|

Where we used the fact that every plane in

is counted for each hyperplane that contains it and this set is in bijection with

. Now we use the induction hypothesis on each

. Notice that if
\alpha=\sum_{v_{i}\in{\rm supp}(\alpha)}\mathbbm{1}_{v_{i}}
then
\left.\alpha\right|_{H}=\sum_{v_{i}\in{\rm supp}(\alpha);v_{i}\subseteq H}\mathbbm{1}_{v_{i}}
, therefore:

\displaystyle{n-1\choose 1}_{q}|d_{1}\alpha|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{H\in Gr_{n}(n+1)}\left|\left.d_{1}\right|_{H}\left.\alpha\right|_{H}\right|\geq\sum_{H\in Gr_{n}(n+1)}c\left(\sum_{v_{i}\in H}1\right)\cdot\left({n\choose 1}_{q}-\left(\sum_{v_{i}\in H}1\right)\right)

(9)

For the second term we get that:

\displaystyle\sum_{H\in Gr_{n}(n+1)}\left(\sum_{v_{i}\in H}1\right)^{2}=\sum_{H\in Gr_{n}(n+1)}\sum_{v_{i}\in H}1+\sum_{v_{i}\neq v_{j}}\sum_{v_{i}\oplus v_{j}\subseteq H}1={n\choose 1}_{q}|\alpha|+|\alpha|\cdot(|\alpha|-1){n-1\choose 1}_{q}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}{n-1\choose 1}_{q}|d_{1}\alpha|

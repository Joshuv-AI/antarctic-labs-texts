Abstract
The paper deals with motion planning for a spin-rolling sphere when the sphere follows a straight path on a plane. Since the motion of the sphere is constrained by the straight line, the control of the sphere’s spin motion is essential to converge to a desired configuration of the sphere. In this paper, we show a new geometric-based planning approach that is based on a full-state description of this nonlinear system. First, the problem statement of the motion planning is posed. Next, we develop a geometric controller implemented as a virtual surface by using the Darboux frame kinematics. This virtual surface generates arc-length-based inputs for controlling the trajectories of the sphere. Then, an iterative algorithm is designed to tune these inputs for the desired configurations. The feasibility of the proposed approach is verified by simulations.

In this paper, we approach the motion planning problem with two main motivations: considering a spin-rolling sphere on the plane and developing optimal smooth trajectories for the arbitrary desired states. Our approach is based on parameterizing the spin-rolling sphere motion by using the Darboux-frame-based kinematics of the ball-plate system originally proposed by Cui and Dai
[

In this paper, after explaining the motion planning problem, we introduce a virtual surface to manipulate the control inputs of the Darboux-frame-based kinematic model to a desired configuration. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first proposed geometric arc-length-based control strategy for the motion planning of a spin-rolling sphere. This control strategy separates the time scale from the kinematic equations, which allows constructing motion with different convergence rates in a given time. Finally, we propose a tuning algorithm to iteratively solve the differential kinematic model until achieving a successful final full configuration. It is important to note that the designed Darboux-frame parametrization increases the number of planning parameters that simplify the developed algorithm.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the motion planning problem is stated and a distance constraint due to the no-sliding constraint is explained. Next, Section 3 includes the design of the geometric controller for a Darboux frame kinematics. In Section 4, the iterative algorithm with included tuning variables is described. Simulations of this new approach are demonstrated and discussed in Section 5. Finally, we conclude our findings in Section 6.

the path on the sphere, connecting the initial and final configurations, must be established.

5
Global Convergence of The Planning Algorithm

To prove the convergence of proposed iterative Algorithm
2
, one can utilize Zangwill’s convergence theorem
[
44
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let the iterative algorithm
{\bm{\mathbf{A}}}:\;\mathbb{R}^{5}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
, showed in the flowchart at Fig.
7
, be on
{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}={\bm{\mathbf{X}}}_{I}\circ{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}_{II}\circ{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}_{III}
compact set where
{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}_{I}
,
{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}_{II}
and
{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}_{III}
are sets of phase I, II and phase III computation steps, respectively. Given
{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{0}\in{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}
, the created sequence
\{{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
satisfies

{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k+1}\in{\bm{\mathbf{A}}}({\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k})
. Then,
{\bm{\mathbf{A}}}
is globally convergent to a solution
\bm{\Gamma}\subset{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}
with following conditions

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bullet\exists\textit{ a descent function ${\bm{\mathbf{z}}}$ for }\bm{\Gamma}\textit{ and }{\bm{\mathbf{A}}},\\
&\bullet\textit{The squence}\;\{{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=0}\subset S\;\textnormal{for}\;S\subset{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}\textit{ is a compact set,}\\
&\bullet\textit{The mapping }{\bm{\mathbf{A}}}\textit{ is closed at all points of }{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}/\bm{\Gamma}.\end{split}

Regarding the first condition in Theorem
1
, the designed line search algorithm
{\bm{\mathbf{A}}}
have descent function
{\bm{\mathbf{z}}}=\{R_{q},\zeta_{q},R_{u},\zeta_{u},\psi_{u}\}
which roughly can be presented as

{\bm{\mathbf{z}}}(x)={\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}+{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}_{k}{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{k}

(26)

where
{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{k}
and
{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}_{k}=\textnormal{diag}\{h(1),h(2),h(3),h(4),h(5)\}
are the direction of iteration and step size of iterations. Note that
\zeta_{u}
is updated in the same way with others, only because
R_{u}
changes the curve
{\bm{\mathbf{L}}}_{o}
, the new angular location shift happens by
\zeta_{u}
. Next, the given descent function
{\bm{\mathbf{z}}}(x)
has following properties:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\textnormal{If }{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}\notin\bm{\Gamma}\textnormal{ and }{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}\in{\bm{\mathbf{X}}},\;{\bm{\mathbf{z}}}(y)<{\bm{\mathbf{z}}}(x),\\
&\textnormal{If }{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}\in\bm{\Gamma}\textnormal{ and }{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}\in{\bm{\mathbf{X}}},\;{\bm{\mathbf{z}}}(y)\leq{\bm{\mathbf{z}}}(x),\end{cases}

hence, the first condition of Theorem
1
is satisfied. The algorithm
{\bm{\mathbf{A}}}
is on the compact manifold of the sphere
U_{C}
and the plane
U_{S}
so
{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}_{k}
norms as the error e.g.,
e_{r}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
become a compact set as well. This can be clearly extended for the second condition about all produced sequences
\{{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=0}
on
{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}
. Now, we provide a proposition to satisfy final condition as

Proposition 1
.

Let
f
be a real continues function on
{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}
. Then, Algorithm
{\bm{\mathbf{A}}}
with solution set
\bm{\Gamma}
of

\bm{\Gamma}({\bm{\mathbf{x}}},{\bm{\mathbf{d}}})=\{{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;|\;{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}={\bm{\mathbf{x}}}+{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}{\bm{\mathbf{d}}},\;{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}>0,\;\textnormal{and }f({\bm{\mathbf{y}}})=\min{\;f({\bm{\mathbf{x}}}+{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}{\bm{\mathbf{d}}})}\}

is closed at any point
({\bm{\mathbf{x}}},{\bm{\mathbf{d}}})
at which always
{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}\neq 0
.

D
presents the proof of the given proposition. By satisfying all the three conditions in Theorem
1
, the proposed line search algorithm is always convergent to a solution for the desired configuration.

Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a new approach for path planning of the spin-rolling sphere on a straight plane trajectory. The planning was mainly constructed by the introduced geometric control. In the beginning, the path planning problem is explained. Next, a virtual surface was designed to produce arc-length-based control inputs for the fully-actuated transformed model in
[
18
]
. The problem is imagined as manipulating a flexible rope-like curve on the sphere with a constant length. Thus, we proposed an iterative algorithm to tune the traversed curve toward desired configurations. The achieved simulations clarified the performance of the proposed planning approach.

The proposed algorithm produces smooth trajectories of the spin-rolling sphere. In this connection, it should be noted that the feedback-based planning algorithms
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
results in piece-wise smooth trajectories. Also, our approach does not require decomposition of the planning strategy into motion steps as featured in geometric phase shift methods
[
30
,
28
,
24
,
38
]
. Another advantage is that the resulting angular velocities of the spin-rolling sphere can shaped to different desired convergence rates.

Proof of Closed Set

Let
{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}\rightarrow{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{0}
and
{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{k}\rightarrow{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{0}
be the convergence to desired values as
k\rightarrow\infty
via sequences of
\{{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
and
\{{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
. Also, suppose
\{{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
is a sequence where
{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}\in\Gamma({\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k},{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{k})
for all
k

{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{k}\rightarrow{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{0}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. We want to illustrate that
{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{0}\in\Gamma({\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{0},{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{0})
for having a closed set.

In each iteration

for

. Thus, by knowing fact that

, there is

\small{\begin{split}&{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}_{k}=||{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{k}-{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}||=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}||{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{k}(1)-{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}(1)||&...\\
...&||{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{k}(n)-{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}(n)||\end{array}\right]\rightarrow{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}^{*}=||{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{0}-{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{0}||,\end{split}}

hence it implies that
{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{0}={\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{0}+{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}^{*}{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{0}
.

Now, to show
{\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{0}
minimizes the
f
along the
{\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{0}+{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{0}
, we know for each k and
{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}
,
0<{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}<\infty
, there is

f({\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{k})\leq f({\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}+{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{k}).

Therefore, by continuity of

f({\bm{\mathbf{y}}}_{0})\leq f({\bm{\mathbf{x}}}_{0}+{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}{\bm{\mathbf{d}}}_{0})
for all
{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}
in which shows

On the μ \mu equals zero Conjecture for Fine Selmer groups in Iwasawa theory
Shaunak V. Deo, Anwesh Ray, R. Sujatha

Abstract
. We study the Iwasawa theory of the fine Selmer group associated to Galois representations arising from modular forms. The vanishing of the μ \mu -invariant is shown to follow in some cases from a natural property satisfied by Galois deformation rings. We outline conditions under which the μ = 0 \mu=0 conjecture is shown to hold for various Galois representations of interest.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a number field and
p
be an odd prime. The
p
-primary roots of unity, considered
as a module over the absolute Galois group of
F
, is denoted by
\mu_{p^{\infty}}.
Set
F_{n}
to be the unique field
contained in
F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
such that
[F_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F]=p^{n}
.
The cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{cyc}
of
F
is the subextension of
F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
obtained
as the union of the fields
F_{n}.

In his seminal work
[
Iwa73
]
, Iwasawa studied the growth of the
p
-part of the class groups over certain towers of number fields. In particular, for the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
, Iwasawa proved that there are invariants
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\#\text{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})=p^{p^{n}\mu+\lambda n+\nu}\text{ for }n\gg 0,

where
\text{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})
is the
p
-Sylow subgroup of the class group of
F_{n}
.
The invariants
\mu
and
\lambda
are the Iwasawa invariants associated to the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\operatorname{cyc}}/F
.

The genesis of Iwasawa theory arose from the study
of these invariants for a broad class of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of a number field.
Iwasawa formulated various conjectures about these invariants.
Amongst them is a famous conjecture,
henceforth referred to as
Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture
, which asserts that the
\mu
-invariant of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\operatorname{\mathrm{cyc}}}/F
of any number field
F
is zero.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be the maximal abelian, unramified, pro-
p
extension of
F_{\operatorname{\mathrm{cyc}}}
, let
X(F_{\operatorname{\mathrm{cyc}}})=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{F}/F_{\operatorname{\mathrm{cyc}}})
and let
\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\operatorname{\mathrm{cyc}}}/F)
.
Then an equivalent formulation of this conjecture is the statement that
X(F_{\operatorname{\mathrm{cyc}}})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.
When
F
is an abelian number field, the
\mu
-invariant is known to vanish by the work of Ferrero and Washington
[
FW79
]
. We remark that no general result in this direction is known for any broader class
of non-abelian number fields.

In the modern language of Galois representations and arithmetic geometry, Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture can be interpreted in terms of structural invariants associated to certain modules over the Iwasawa algebra of
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\operatorname{cyc}}/F)
.
These modules arise naturally in the context of the Galois representation associated to the Tate motive
\mu_{p^{\infty}}.
Subsequently, Iwasawa theory evolved to cover the study of a range of modules over Iwasawa algebras that arise from other Galois representations. An important instance of this evolution is the work of Mazur
[
Maz72
]
who initiated the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves and abelian varieties. In this pathbreaking work, Mazur studied the
p
-primary Selmer group of an elliptic curve (or abelian variety)
E_{/F}
which has good, ordinary reduction at all primes dividing
p
over
F_{\operatorname{cyc}}
, and defined analogous Iwasawa
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants in this context.
The
\mu
-invariant of the
p
-primary Selmer group need not vanish. For example, for
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}=X_{0}(11)
, the
\mu
-invariant of the
5
-primary Selmer group is positive. Given the analogy between class groups and Selmer groups of elliptic curves, it is natural to frame an analogue of Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture in this setting which we describe below.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
F
and
p
be a prime above which
E
has potential good reduction. Set
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\operatorname{cyc}}/F)
and let
\Lambda
denote the corresponding Iwasawa algebra, which is defined to be the completed group algebra
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
. Then, the
p
-primary
fine Selmer group
consists of the Galois cohomology classes in
H^{1}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/F_{\operatorname{cyc}}),E[p^{\infty}]\right)
that are trivial at all primes above
p
and the primes at which
E
has bad reduction.
The fine Selmer group is believed to be cotorsion as an Iwasawa module and this is known to be true for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
CS05
]
).
In contrast, the usual Selmer group need not satisfy this condition unless the elliptic curve is assumed to have ordinary reduction at all primes above
p
.

The properties of the fine Selmer group were systematically studied by Coates and the third named author who showed that the Iwasawa
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group is related to the classical Iwasawa
\mu
-invariant of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of the number field
F(E[p])
cut out by the
p
-torsion in
E
. We refer to
[
CS05
, Corollary 3.5]
for the precise statement of this result. This relationship shows that the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group is a consequence of Iwasawa’s classical
\mu=0
conjecture. This led Coates and the third named author to formulate the
\mu=0
conjecture for the fine Selmer group of an elliptic curve (see
[
CS05
, Conjecture A]
), as the analogue of Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture for Galois representations associated to elliptic curves.

Iwasawa theory of
p
-adic Galois representations is currently a central theme in number theory.
The fine Selmer group can be defined in the more general context of
p
-adic
Galois representations (see Definition
2.1
and Definition
2.2
).
We remark that the
\mu=0
conjecture can be formulated in this
broader framework (see Conjecture
2.6
and
[
LS18
, Conjecture A, p.74]
). It has interesting applications to the study of Selmer groups associated to Galois representations of interest.
The goal of this article is to study the
\mu=0
conjecture in this broader setting. The main results formulate explicit conditions under which the
\mu
-invariant vanishes for the fine Selmer groups associated to important classes of
p
-adic Galois representations. The cases of interest are the Galois representations arising from elliptic curves defined over number fields, Hecke eigencuspforms of weight at least
2
, Tate twists of Artin representations and Tate twists of the adjoint representations associated to modular Galois representations.

In the study of Galois representations, the theme of
p
-adic variation plays a key role. More precisely, the deformation theory of Galois representations, introduced and developed by Mazur in
[
Maz89
]
, allows us to study
p
-adic families of lifts of a fixed mod-
p
Galois representation. Hida
[
Hid00
]
studied the relationship between certain modular deformation rings defined over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and the Iwasawa theory of the classical Selmer group of the adjoint representation of a modular form.
In this article, the
\mu=0
conjecture for the fine Selmer group of the first Tate twist of the adjoint representation of a
p
-adic Galois representation
\rho
is related to

unobstructedness
of the residual representation of
\rho
(see Definition
4.1
).
Note that the notion of unobstructedness appears naturally in Galois deformation theory in connection with the structure of the universal deformation ring (in the sense of Mazur).
This provides a new approach in investigating the fine Selmer group.
It is our belief that the linkage established here will help advance the current state of art in both the Iwasawa theory of the fine Selmer group as well as deformation theory of Galois representations. In particular, our methods provide a large class of new examples of fine Selmer groups of Galois representations for which the
\mu
-invariant vanishes.

Let us now briefly outline the results proved in this article. Section
2
is preliminary in nature. In section
3
, we establish a criterion for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of a Galois representation (see Theorem
3.7
).
To be precise, we prove that the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of a
p
-adic Galois representation
\rho
vanishes if the
second
global Galois cohomology group of the first Tate twist of the dual of the residual representation of
\rho
vanishes.
Thus, this criterion is determined purely in terms of the residual representation. Theorem
3.8
gives explicit conditions for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group, which we specialize to the case of elliptic curves in Theorem
3.9
.

Section
4
establishes the linkage between deformation theory of Galois representations and the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group. We study the fine Selmer group of the first Tate twist of the
n^{2}
-dimensional adjoint Galois representation
\operatorname{Ad}\rho
associated to an
n
-dimensional Galois representation
\rho
. An important class of such Galois representations arises naturally from automorphic forms. More precisely, Theorem
4.3
shows that the
\mu
-invariant vanishes for the fine Selmer group of
\operatorname{Ad}\rho(1)
if
\rho
is
unobstructed
. This concept was initially introduced by Mazur in
[
Maz89
]
, and it was shown by Weston in
[
Wes04
]
that Galois representations arising from modular forms are unobstructed under some explicit conditions.

Let
f
be a normalized Hecke newform of weight
k\geq 2
. Given a prime
\mathfrak{p}
in the field of Fourier coefficients of
f
, let
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
be the associated
2
-dimensional
\mathfrak{p}
-adic Galois representation. Theorem
4.6
shows that the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}(1)
vanishes for a set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of Dirichlet density
1
.
Moreover, if
k>2
, then it is shown to vanish for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
. Furthermore, if the level of
f
is squarefree, then there is an explicit set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
outside of which the associated
\mu
-invariant is
0
.
When
k=2
and
f
has trivial nebentypus, our results imply that the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
vanishes for a set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of Dirichlet density
1
.
We also prove a similar result in the setting of Hilbert modular forms (see Theorem
4.11
).
On the other hand, we prove that if
E
is a rational elliptic curve with squarefree conductor, then there are infinitely many primes
p
such that the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}
vanishes (see Theorem
4.12
).
Here,
\rho_{E,p}
is the
2
-dimensional
p
-adic Galois representation attached to
E
and
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}
is the subrepresentation of
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{E,p}
consisting of matrices with trace
0
.

We then turn our attention to Artin representations. Note that we can realize an Artin representation as a
p
-adic Galois representation for all primes
p
.
We prove that if
\rho
is an Artin representation such that
H^{0}(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}),\rho)=0
, then the
\mu
-invariants of the fine Selmer groups of the first Tate twists of the
p
-adic realizations of the dual of
\rho
vanish for all but finitely many primes
p
(see Theorem
4.14
).

We also connect the
\mu=0
conjecture with the notion of
neatness
introduced by Mazur in
[
Maz89
]
to prove that the
\mu=0
conjecture is true for first Tate twist of the adjoint representation of a
p
-adic Galois representation
\rho
whose residual representation is
neat
(see Corollary
4.19
). Leveraging a construction of Mazur and Böckle, we end section
4
with explicit families of odd and even
S_{3}
-representations such that the
\mu=0
-conjecture holds for the fine Selmer groups of first Tate twists of all their characteristic
0
lifts.

In section
5
, we study the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group associated to
2
-dimensional residually dihedral Galois representations. The conditions are stated purely in terms of the residual representation and the set of primes at which the characteristic zero representation ramifies. In Theorem
5.6
we give explicit conditions for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant and obtain a large class of examples for which our results apply. Finally, we remark that our results are illustrated with concrete numerical examples.

3.
A Criterion for
\mu=0
for the fine Selmer group of a Galois representation

In this section, we shall discuss various criteria for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group associated to a Galois representation. As in the previous section, let
p
be an odd prime,
F
be a number field and
S
be a finite set of primes containing all archimedean primes and the primes above
p
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the valuation ring of a finite extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with uniformizer
\varpi
and let
\mathbb{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{O}/\varpi
be its residue field. Fix an integral Galois representation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
on an
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
\mathbf{T}_{\rho}
. Let
\bar{\rho}
denote the residual representation
\rho\mod{\varpi}
and set
\mathbf{V}_{\bar{\rho}}=\mathbf{T}_{\rho}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbb{F}
to be the underlying vector space on which
\operatorname{G}_{F,S}
acts via
\bar{\rho}
. Setting
\mathbf{V}_{\rho}=\mathbf{T}_{\rho}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
, recall that
\mathbf{A}(\rho)=\mathbf{V}_{\rho}/\mathbf{T}_{\rho}
. We identify
\mathbf{V}_{\bar{\rho}}
with
\mathbf{A}(\rho)[\varpi]
. Denote by
\rho^{\wedge}
the dual of the representation
\rho
and by
\chi_{p}
be the
p
-adic cyclotomic character. We set
\rho^{*}
to denote the representation
\rho^{\wedge}\otimes\chi_{p}
. Recall that
\bar{\chi}_{p}
is the mod-
p
cyclotomic character, and
\bar{\rho}^{\wedge}
is the dual representation to
\bar{\rho}
. Identify
\bar{\rho}^{*}
with
\bar{\rho}^{\wedge}\otimes\bar{\chi}_{p}
.
We note that
\mathbf{T}_{\rho^{*}}
is isomorphic to the twist
\mathbf{T}_{{\rho}^{\wedge}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}(\chi_{p})
.
Therefore, to summarize,
\rho
and
\rho^{*}
are integral representations acting on the
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\mathbf{T}_{\rho}
and
\mathbf{T}_{\rho^{*}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}\left(\mathbf{T}_{\rho},\mathcal{O}(\chi_{p})\right)
, respectively. The representations
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{\rho}^{*}
act on the
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces
\mathbf{V}_{\bar{\rho}}

K
containing all primes dividing
p
and all archimedean primes.
Let
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{K,S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be a continuous representation.
Recall, from §
2
, that
K_{S}
is the maximal extension of
K
unramified at all primes of
K
lying outside
S
and
\operatorname{G}_{K,S}
is the corresponding Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{S}/F)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with residue field
\mathbb{F}
.
Let
\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
be the category of complete local noetherian
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
R
with residue field isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}
. Given
R\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
, let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
R
and fix a residual isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
R/\mathfrak{m}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{F}
.
As
R
is noetherian, there exist
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m}\in R
such that
\mathfrak{m}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m})
.
Since
R
is a complete local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, there exists a morphism
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}\rrbracket\to R
which sends
X_{i}
to
\alpha_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
(see
[
Eis95
, Theorem 7.16 (a)]
).
Note that the map
\mathcal{O}\to R/\mathfrak{m}
induced by
\phi
is surjective and
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
generate
\mathfrak{m}
.
Therefore, it follows, from
[
Eis95
, Theorem 7.16 (b)]
, that
\phi
is surjective. Hence, we get a presentation for
R
of the form

R\simeq\frac{\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\rrbracket}{\left(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\right)}.

An
R
-lift of
\bar{\rho}
is a Galois representation
\rho_{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
such that
\bar{\rho}=\rho_{R}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
. The subgroup of matrices
A\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
that reduce to the identity modulo the maximal ideal of
R
is denoted by
\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{n}(R)
. Two
R
-lifts
\rho_{R}
and
\rho_{R}^{\prime}
of
\bar{\rho}
are
strictly equivalent
if
\rho_{R}=A\rho_{R}^{\prime}A^{-1}
for some matrix
A\in\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{n}(R)
. An
R
-deformation
of
\bar{\rho}
is a strict equivalence class of
R
-lifts.

Given a Galois representation
\rho_{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
, set
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{R}
to be the adjoint representation of
\rho_{R}
. As an
R
-module,
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{R}
consists of all
n\times n
matrices with entries in
R
. The Galois action on
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{R}
is defined by the adjoint action

\sigma\cdot v=\rho_{R}(\sigma)v\rho_{R}(\sigma)^{-1},

where,
\sigma\in\operatorname{G}_{K,S}
and
v\in\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{R}
. Thus, we may view
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{R}
as a Galois representation

\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n^{2}}(R).

In particular, specializing this construction to the case
R=\mathbb{F}
and
\rho_{R}=\bar{\rho}
, we get the adjoint representation of
\bar{\rho}
:

\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n^{2}}(\mathbb{F}).

Definition 4.1
.

A representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is said to be
unobstructed
if
H^{2}(K_{S}/K,\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
.
Following this definition, we say that
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
is
unobstructed
if the corresponding residual representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
(obtained by composing
\rho
with the natural surjective map
\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{F}
) is unobstructed.

Associated to
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{K,S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, consider a
functor of deformations

\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sets},

which takes
R\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
to the set
\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}}(R)
consisting of
R
-deformations of
\bar{\rho}
considered as a representation of the group
\operatorname{G}_{K,S}
.
When
\bar{\rho}
is absolutely irreducible, there is a
universal Galois representation
unramified away from
S
(see
[
Maz89
, Proposition 1]
), which we denote by

\rho^{\operatorname{univ}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}\left(R_{\bar{\rho}}\right).

Here,
R_{\bar{\rho}}
is the
universal deformation ring
associated to
\bar{\rho}
and the group
\operatorname{G}_{K,S}
(the set
S
being suppressed in our notation). It is universal in the sense that for any
R
-deformation
\rho_{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
of
\bar{\rho}
, there exists a unique homomorphism of complete noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R_{\bar{\rho}}\rightarrow R
such that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{GL}_{n}(R_{\bar{\rho}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{G}_{K,S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{GL}_{n}(R),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\operatorname{univ}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{R}}

where the vertical map is induced by
\psi
.

In general, finding the explicit structure of the universal deformation ring
R_{\bar{\rho}}
is a very difficult problem.
However, if
\bar{\rho}
is unobstructed, then we know that

R_{\bar{\rho}}\simeq\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket

where
d=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(H^{1}(G_{K,S},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}))
(see
[
Maz89
, Proposition 2]
).
Indeed,
[
Maz89
, Section 1.6]
implies that
R_{\bar{\rho}}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket
. Since
\bar{\rho}
is unobstructed,
[
Maz89
, Proposition 2]
implies that the Krull dimension of
R_{\bar{\rho}}
is at least
d+1
.
This allows us to conclude that
R_{\bar{\rho}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket
.
In particular, specializing to the case
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
n=2
, we get:

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
is absolutely irreducible and unobstructed. Then:

(1)

If
\bar{\rho}
is odd, then
R_{\bar{\rho}}
is isomorphic (as a complete noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra) to the formal power series ring
\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\rrbracket
.

(2)

If
\bar{\rho}
is even, then
R_{\bar{\rho}}
is isomorphic (as a complete noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra) to the formal power series ring
\mathcal{O}\llbracket X\rrbracket
.

Proof.

If
\bar{\rho}
is odd, then the global Euler characteristic formula implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}))=3
.
If
\bar{\rho}
is even, then the global Euler characteristic formula implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}))=1
.
The result now follows from
[
Maz89
, Proposition 2]
and the explanations given above.
∎

Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
be a representation. As we are assuming that
p
is odd, we get a direct sum decomposition

\operatorname{Ad}\rho=\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho\oplus\mathcal{O}

as
G_{\mathbb{Q},S}
-representations with summands
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho
and
\mathcal{O}
corresponding to trace
0
matrices and scalars, respectively. We have a similar decomposition for
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
as well.
Recall that we denoted the
p
-adic cyclotomic character by
\chi_{p}
.
So we have

\operatorname{Ad}\rho(1)=\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(\chi_{p})=\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho)\otimes\det(\rho)^{-1}\chi_{p}\oplus\mathcal{O}(\chi_{p}).

Thus, if
\det(\rho)=\chi_{p}
, then
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho)
is a subrepresentation of
\operatorname{Ad}\rho(1)
.
We obtain the following criterion for the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of the first Tate-twist of the adjoint representation (without assuming
\dim(\rho)=2
):

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
be a continuous Galois representation. If
\rho
is
unobstructed
, then the following assertions hold:

(1)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\operatorname{Ad}\rho
.

(2)

the fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{A}(\operatorname{Ad}\rho(1))/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
associated to
\operatorname{Ad}\rho(1)
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\operatorname{Ad}\rho(1))=0
.

(3)

If
n=2
and
r=\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho)\otimes(\det(\rho))^{-1}\chi_{p}
, then the fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{A}(r)/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
associated to
r
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(r)=0
.

Proof.

Observe that
\left(\operatorname{Ad}\rho(1)\right)^{*}=\operatorname{Ad}\rho
.
Unobstructedness of
\rho
implies that
H^{2}(K_{S}/K,\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
. Hence, the assertions (
1
) and (
2
) above follow directly from Theorem
3.7
. For (
3
), consider the case when
n=2
and
r=\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho)\otimes(\det(\rho))^{-1}\chi_{p}
. In this case, we find that
r^{*}=\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho
. Hence,
H^{2}(K_{S}/K,\mathbf{V}_{\bar{r}^{*}})
is a direct summand of
H^{2}(K_{S}/K,\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})
, and therefore is equal to
0
. The assertion in this case follows once again from Theorem
3.7
.
∎

The unobstructedness of modular Galois representations
\rho
is studied in greater detail in
[
Wes04
]
.
Before recalling the main result of
[
Wes04
]
, we briefly describe its setup.
Let
f
be a newform (i.e. a new cuspidal eigenform) of weight
k\geq 2
on
\Gamma_{1}(N)
, where
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
containing the primes dividing
N
and
\infty
.
Denote by
F
the field of Fourier coefficients of
f
and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Set
\mathcal{O}
to be the completion of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
at
\mathfrak{p}
. Fix a uniformizer
\varpi
of
\mathcal{O}
and set
\mathbb{F}
to denote the residue field
\mathcal{O}/\varpi
. Let
p
be the prime of
\mathbb{Q}
lying below
\mathfrak{p}
and let
S_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=S\cup\{p\}
. Let

\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})

be an integral Galois representation associated to
f
and the prime
\mathfrak{p}
by the construction of Eichler-Shimura and Deligne. Let
\bar{\rho}_{f,\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
be the residual representation obtained by reducing
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
modulo
\varpi
. Note that the construction of Eichler-Shimura and Deligne gives a Galois representation over the fraction field of
\mathcal{O}
and the Galois representation
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
arises from a choice of a Galois stable
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
\mathbf{T}
in it. When the residual representation is absolutely irreducible, there is a unique choice of
\mathbf{T}
.
In all the examples that we consider, the residual representation will be absolutely irreducible.
We will mostly be working with this setup in the rest of the section.

Denote by
\operatorname{Obs}(f)
the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
at which
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
is
obstructed
(i.e., not unobstructed). An important notion in this context is that of a
congruence prime
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
d
be a divisor of
N
. We say that
\mathfrak{p}
is a
congruence prime of level
d
for
f
if there is a newform
f^{\prime}
such that

•

f^{\prime}
has weight
k
and level
\Gamma_{1}(d)
;

•

f^{\prime}
is
not
Galois conjugate to
f
;

•

\bar{\rho}_{f^{\prime},\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f,\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}
for some prime
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
above
\mathfrak{p}
.

Denote by
\operatorname{Cong}(f)
the set of all congruence primes of
f
(as
d
ranges through all divisors of
N
).

Since there are only finitely many newforms whose level is a divisor of
N
, it is easy to see, by comparing their Hecke eigenvalues which are not equal, that
\operatorname{Cong}(f)
is finite. Let
\varphi
denote Euler’s totient function.

The following explicit result is due to Weston and has been subsequently generalized in various directions.

Theorem 4.5

(Weston)
.

Let
f
be a Hecke newform of weight
k\geq 2
on
\Gamma_{1}(N)
and let
M
be the conductor of the nebentypus of
f
. Consider two cases.

(1)

First, consider the case
k>2
. Then, for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the Galois representation
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
is unobstructed. Furthermore, if
N
is assumed to be squarefree, we have that

\operatorname{Obs}(f)\subseteq\left\{\mathfrak{p}|p;p\leq k+1\right\}\cup\left\{\mathfrak{p}|p;p|\left(N\varphi(N)\prod_{\ell|(N/M)}(\ell+1)\right)\right\}\cup\operatorname{Cong}(f).

(2)

Consider the case
k=2
. Then, the set
\operatorname{Obs}(f)
of obstructed primes
\mathfrak{p}
has Dirichlet density zero.

Proof.

The first part is obtained by combining
[
Wes04
, Theorem 5.4]
with
[
Wes05
, Theorem 1]
.
The second part follows directly from
[
Wes04
, Theorem 5.5]
(see also
[
Maz97
, Corollary 2]
when
f
is a non-CM modular newform having weight
2
, trivial nebentypus and rational Hecke eigenvalues).
∎

Combining the above theorem with Theorem
4.3
, one obtains an immediate application towards the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
f
be a Hecke newform of weight
k\geq 2
on
\Gamma_{1}(N)
and let
M
be the conductor of the nebentypus
\epsilon
of
f
. Let
F
be the number field generated by its Hecke eigenvalues and let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
F
. Consider two cases.

(1)

If
k>2
, then, for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the following assertions hold:

(a)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture holds for
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
.

(b)

Let
r
denote any one of the representations
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes(\det(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))^{-1}\chi_{p}
or
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}(1)
. Then the fine Selmer group associated to
r
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(r)=0
.

Furthermore, if
N
is squarefree, then the above assertions hold for all primes outside the finite set

\operatorname{Obs}(f)\subseteq\left\{\mathfrak{p}|p;p\leq k+1\right\}\cup\left\{\mathfrak{p}|p;p|\left(N\varphi(N)\prod_{\ell|(N/M)}(\ell+1)\right)\right\}\cup\operatorname{Cong}(f).

(2)

Consider the case
k=2
. Then, for a set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
of Dirichlet density one, the following assertions hold:

(a)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
.

(b)

For
r\in\{\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes\epsilon^{-1},\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}(1)\}
, the fine Selmer group associated to
r
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(r)=0
.

Proof.

The result follows directly from Theorems
4.3
and
4.5
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

If
f
is a newform of weight
2
with trivial nebentypus, then, under the notation established above, Theorem
4.6
implies that for a set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
of Dirichlet density one, the fine Selmer group associated to the symmetric square representation
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
is co-torsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))=0
.

Remark 4.8
.

For Galois representations associated to elliptic curves of rank
0
(not the adjoint or symmetric square representation, but the representation itself), similar results can be proved for a set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of Dirichlet density
1
, see
[
Wut07
]
.
On the other hand, for modular forms of weight
k>2
, Theorem
4.6
proves that the
\mu
-invariant of the first Tate twist of the adjoint representation vanishes for all but finitely many primes, which is stronger than showing that it vanishes for a set of primes of Dirichlet density
1
. Moreover, the set of primes outside which the
\mu
-invariant is known to vanish, is made explicit in the squarefree level case by the above result. Also, in the weight
2
case, when
f
coincides with an abelian variety of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
-type, there is no assumption made on the rank of this abelian variety.

Remark 4.9
.

Hatley
[
Hat16
]
has generalized
[
Wes05
, Theorem 1]
to modular newforms of arbitrary level.
Thus, combining
[
Hat16
, Theorem 3.6]
with Theorem
3.7
, one can explicitly describe a finite set of primes of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
outside of which
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}({\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes(\det(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))^{-1}\chi_{p}})
and
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}(1))
vanish even when the level of
f
is not squarefree.
Since the description of this set is tedious, we will not give it here.
We refer the reader to
[
Hat16
, Theorem 3.6]
for more details.

Specializing to the case when
N=1
,
S=\{\infty\}
and
f
is the unique normalized cusp form of level
1
and weight

,

\rho_{f,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

attached to

.
Let

\bar{\rho}_{f,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

be the reduction of

modulo
p
.

Combining
[
Wes04
, Theorem 2]
with Theorem
4.3
, we get:

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
f
be the unique normalized cusp form of level
1
and weight

.
If

is a prime such that
\bar{\rho}_{f,p}
is absolutely irreducible, then

(1)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{f,p}
.

(2)

For

, the fine Selmer group associated to

is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with

.

In the setting of the theorem above, Weston obtains
[
Wes04
, Theorem 2]
by proving that

is unobstructed i.e.

proves the theorem.

.
Hence, we have
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes(\det(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))^{-1}\chi_{p}=\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,p})(2-k)
.
∎

If

is one of the newforms considered in Theorem

, then the set of primes

is

.
In particular if

, then the hypotheses of Theorem

are satisfied by primes

and

.

Weston’s work on unobstructedness of modular Galois representations has been generalized in several directions by various authors.
In the setting of Hilbert modular forms, such results have been obtained by Gamzon
[
Gam16
]
.
Using his main result, we get:

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
K
be a totally real field,
f
be a Hilbert modular newform over
K
and
S
be a finite set of primes of
K
containing all prime divisors of the level of
f
and all archimedean primes of
K
.
Let
F
be the number field generated by its Hecke eigenvalues and let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
F
.
For a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, let
p
be the rational prime lying below
\mathfrak{p}
,
S_{\mathfrak{p}}
be
S\cup\{\text{primes of }K\text{ lying above }p\}
and

\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{K,S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{p}})

be the corresponding Galois representation attached to
f
.
Suppose the following hypotheses hold:

(1)

f
has no CM,

(2)

f
is not a twist of a base change of a Hilbert newform over a proper subfield

of
K
,

(3)

All weights of
f
are greater than
2
.

Then, for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the following assertions hold:

(a)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
.

(b)

The fine Selmer group associated to
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes(\det(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))^{-1}\chi_{p}
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes(\det(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))^{-1}\chi_{p})=0
.

Proof.

Note that, under the hypotheses of the theorem above,
[
Gam16
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that
H^{2}(K_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}/K,\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{f,\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. We note here that
\left(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes(\det(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))^{-1}\chi_{p}\right)^{*}
is identified with
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
.
Theorem
3.7
gives the result.
∎

The analogous problem for
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
-representations is studied by Broshi, Mullath, Sorensen and Weston
[
BMSW20
]
.
On the other hand, Guiraud in
[
Gui20
]
has established a related generalization (in the spirit of Gamzon) in the setting of regular algebraic conjugate self-dual cuspidal (RACSDC) automorphic representations (see
[
Gui20
, Theorem 1.2]
for more details).
We expect that Theorem
4.6
can be suitably generalized to such settings.

We end this subsection by proving an analogue of Theorem
4.6
for the vanishing of
\mu
-invariant of
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho
, where
\rho
is the Galois representation associated to an elliptic curve.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with squarefree conductor
N
and let
S
be the set of primes dividing
N
and
\infty
. For a prime
p
, let

\rho_{E,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S_{p}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

be the
p
-adic Galois representation attached to
E
.
Then, for infinitely many primes
p
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture holds for
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}(1)=\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{E,p})
.

(2)

The fine Selmer group associated to
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p})=0
.

Proof.

Recall that
(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p})^{*}=\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}(1)
.
So, by Theorem
3.7
, it suffices to prove that for infinitely many primes p, we have
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{p}}/\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p}(1))=0
.
Using Poitou-Tate duality, we get the following exact sequence (see
[
Was97
, Proposition 10]
):

(4.1)

0\to H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{p}}/\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})\to\oplus_{\ell\in S}H^{0}(G_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})\oplus H^{0}(G_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})\to\\
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{p}}/\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p}(1))^{*}\to\Sh^{1}_{S_{p}}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})\to 0.

Hence, we conclude that
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{p}}/\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p}(1))=0
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

H^{0}(G_{\ell},{\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p}})=0
for all
\ell\in S
,

(2)

H^{0}(G_{p},{\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p}})=0
,

(3)

\Sh^{1}_{S_{p}}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})=0
.

Suppose
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is absolutely irreducible.
Hence, it follows that
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{p}}/\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})=0
.
Note that, in
[
Wes05
]
, Weston defines a suitable Selmer group
H^{1}_{\emptyset}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbf{A}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}))
of
\mathbf{A}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p})
(see
[
Wes05
, p. 204]
).
This Selmer group is also extensively studied in
[
DFG05
]
.
Since
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{p}}/\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})=0
, we get, using the proof of
[
Wes05
, Lemma 7]
and
[
Wes05
, Equation 2.3]
, that
\Sh^{1}_{S_{p}}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})
is a subgroup of
H^{1}_{\emptyset}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbf{A}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}))
.
Let
f_{E}
be the weight
2
modular form associated to
E
.
From
[
DFG05
, Theorem 3.7]
, we get that the length of
H^{1}_{\emptyset}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbf{A}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}))
is the
p
-valuation of the congruence ideal
\eta_{f_{E}}^{\emptyset}
defined in
[
DFG05
, Section 1.7.3]
. As
\eta_{f_{E}}^{\emptyset}
is an ideal of
\mathbb{Z}
, we conclude that
H^{1}_{\emptyset}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbf{A}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\rho_{E,p}))=0
for all but finitely many primes
p
(see the proofs of
[
Wes04
, Theorem 5.4, Theorem 5.6]
for more details).
Note that
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is absolutely irreducible for all but finitely many primes
p
.
Hence, we conclude that
\Sh^{1}_{S_{p}}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})=0
for all but finitely many primes
p
.

Now
E
is a rational elliptic curve of conductor
N
. So it follows, from the modularity of rational elliptic curves, that there exists a newform
f_{E}
of level
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and weight
2
with rational Fourier coefficients such that
\rho_{E,p}=\rho_{f_{E},p}
for all primes
p
.

Now, in addition to irreducibility of
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
, suppose
p>N^{2}
and
H^{0}(G_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})\neq 0
for some
\ell\in S
.
As
N
is squarefree and
f_{E}
has trivial nebentypus, it follows that
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is reducible, semi-simple and hence, unramified at
\ell
(see the proof of
[
Wes05
, Lemma 11]
for more details).
So, we conclude, from
[
Edi97
, (B) of p. 221]
, that
p
is a congruence prime of level dividing
\dfrac{N}{\ell}
(see Definition
4.4
for the definition of congruence primes).
Since
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is absolutely irreducible for all but finitely many primes
p
and there are only finitely many congruence primes, we conclude that for all but finitely many primes
p
,
H^{0}(G_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})=0
for all
\ell\in S
.

Note that if
p
is a supersingular prime of
E
, then the restriction of
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
to
G_{p}
is absolutely irreducible and hence,
H^{0}(G_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}_{E,p})=0
.
A celebrated theorem of Elkies (
[
Elk87
, Theorem 2]
) implies that
E
has infinitely many supersingular primes.
Combining this with all the analysis given above proves the theorem.
∎

4.2.
Artin representations

Note that, in Theorem
4.5
, the weight of the modular newforms is always assumed to be greater than
1
.
Therefore, it does not shed any light on the unobstructedness of Artin representations as they arise only in the setting of weight
1
modular forms.
Moreover, an Artin representation can be considered as a
p
-adic representation for every prime
p
(as we will see below).
So it is easy to formulate the question studied by Weston
[
Wes04
]
for Artin representations of arbitrary dimensions.
This question is studied by Böckle, Guiraud, Kalyanswamy and Khare in
[
BGKK18
]
in more generality.
In particular, they focus on the vanishing of
H^{2}
for arbitrary Artin representations rather than restricting to the case of adjoint of Artin representations.
We will now briefly describe their setup following
[
BGKK18
, Section 6.1]
.

Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a non-trivial irreducible Artin representation (which is not necessarily odd).
So it is a continuous, irreducible representation with finite non-trivial image.
Let
E
be the finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
fixed by
\ker(\rho)
. Let
G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
and
h_{E}
be the class number of
E
.
Let
S
be the set of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
consisting of primes which are ramified in
E
and
\infty
.

As
\operatorname{Im}(\rho)
is finite, there exists a number field
F
such that, under a suitable basis,
\operatorname{Im}(\rho)\subset\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
F
and
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.
Let
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the completion of
F
at
\mathfrak{p}
,
\mathcal{O}
be its ring of integers and
\mathbb{F}
be its residue field.
Let
p
be the rational prime lying below
\mathfrak{p}
and let
S_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=S\cup\{p\}
.
Then, under a suitable basis, the representation
\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F_{\mathfrak{p}})
, obtained by composing
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
with the map
F\to F_{\mathfrak{p}}
induced by completion, takes values in
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
.

Recall that we have fixed
p
to be an odd prime. Moreover, assume that
p\nmid|G|.h_{E}
,
p
is unramified in
E
and
p\geq\text{max}\{\ell^{|G|}\mid\ell\in S\}
.
Then, the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
obtained by reducing
\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
modulo the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
is absolutely irreducible (see
[
BGKK18
, Section 6.1]
for more details).
Thus it is natural to ask whether
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathbb{Q},\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all but finitely many such primes
\mathfrak{p}
.
This question is studied in
[
BGKK18
]
.

To be precise, in the setup described above, they prove:

Theorem 4.13

(Böckle-Guiraud-Kalyanswamy-Khare)
.

Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a non-trivial irreducible Artin representation and let
F
be a number field over which
\rho
is defined as above. Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
F
.
Let
S
be the set of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
consisting of primes at which
\rho
is ramified and
\infty
.
If
H^{0}(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}),\rho)=0
, then
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathbb{Q},\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Proof.

This is a part of Proposition
6.6
of
[
BGKK18
]
.
∎

Combining this theorem with Theorem
3.7
, we get:

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a non-trivial irreducible Artin representation and let
F
be a number field over which
\rho
is defined as above. Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
F
.
Suppose
H^{0}(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}),\rho)=0
.
Then, for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the following assertions hold:

(a)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(b)

The fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{A}(\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*})/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
associated to
\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*})=0
.

Remark 4.15
.

Note that, in
[
BGKK18
, Proposition 6.6]
, it is also proved that if

0<\dim(H^{0}(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}),\rho))<\dim(\rho)

and Heuristic
6.5
of
[
BGKK18
]
holds, then
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathbb{Q},\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.
Combining this result with Theorem
3.7
, we conclude that
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*})=0
for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
in these cases as well.

Note that the same question for Artin representations of arbitrary number fields is analyzed in Section
6.2
of
[
BGKK18
]
.

4.3.
Neat Representations

Let
K
be a number field,
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
and
S
be a finite set of primes of
K
containing all primes of
K
dividing
p
and all archimedean primes.
Let
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{K,S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be a continuous, absolutely irreducible representation.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with residue field
\mathbb{F}
.
Fix a uniformizer
\varpi
of
\mathcal{O}
.

Suppose
\bar{\rho}
is unobstructed and let
d=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(H^{1}(K_{S}/K,\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}))
.
Then, from
[
Maz89
, Proposition 2]
, it follows that the residual representation
\bar{\rho}
lifts to the universal representation unramified away from
S

\rho^{\operatorname{univ}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}\left(\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket\right),

which we view as a family of Galois representations. Indeed, given any
d
-tuple
a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{d})\in\mathcal{O}^{d}
, the
\mathcal{O}
-valued homomorphism

\varphi_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
sending
X_{i}
to
\varpi a_{i}
gives rise to a Galois representation
\rho_{a}
given by the composite

(4.2)

\rho_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\xrightarrow{\rho^{\operatorname{univ}}}\operatorname{GL}_{n}\left(\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rrbracket\right)\xrightarrow{\varphi_{a}^{*}}\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}),

where the second homomorphism is induced by
\varphi_{a}
.
Since
\bar{\rho}
is unobstructed, it follows from Theorem
4.3
that the weak Leopoldt conjecture holds for
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{a}
, and moreover,
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{a}(1))=0
. Thus, we get a family of Galois representations parametrized by
\mathcal{O}^{d}
for which the
\mu
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of the adjoint representation vanishes.

Recall that if
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
n=2
, then we get that
d=3
if
\bar{\rho}
is odd and
d=1
if
\bar{\rho}
is even (see Proposition
4.2
).

In
[
Maz89
]
, Mazur introduced the notion of
neatness
and constructed odd Galois representations of
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
that are unobstructed and unramified away from a single prime
p
. Böckle
[
B9̈9
]
gave a similar construction for
even
, unobstructed Galois representations of
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. We relate the notion of neatness to vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of fine Selmer groups.

Fix an absolutely irreducible residual representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
as above, let
L/K
be the splitting field cut out by
\bar{\rho}
. Denote by
S^{\prime}
the set of primes of
L
that lie above the non-archimedean primes of
S
and set
G
to be the Galois group
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
.
For a prime
w\in S^{\prime}
, denote by
L_{w}
the completion of
L
at
w
.
Denote the subgroups of
L^{\times}
and
L_{w}^{\times}
consisting of of
p
-th roots of unity by
\mu_{p}(L)
and
\mu_{p}(L_{w})
, respectively.
Denote the ring of integers of
L
and
L_{w}
by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
\mathcal{O}_{L_{w}}
, respectively.

An
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module
M
is said to be
coprime
to
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
if the tensor product
M\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})^{\vee}
does not contain the identity representation.

Definition 4.16
.

We define three Galois modules
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
and
M_{3}
associated to
L
as follows:

(1)

M_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{coker}\left\{\mu_{p}(L)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S^{\prime}}\mu_{p}(L_{w})\right\}
;

(2)

M_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{\mathcal{O}_{L}^{*}/(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{*})^{p}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{L_{w}}^{*}/(\mathcal{O}_{L_{w}}^{*})^{p}\right\}
;

(3)

M_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Cl}(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
, where
\operatorname{Cl}(L)
is the class group of
L
.

Definition 4.17

(Mazur)
.

With notation as above,
\bar{\rho}
is said to be
neat
if
p
does not divide the cardinality of
\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\rho})
and
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
and
M_{3}
are coprime to
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
.

See
[
Maz89
, Section 1.12]
for more details.

Theorem 4.18

(Mazur)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
,
K
be a number field and
S
be a set of primes of
K
containing all primes above
p
and all archimedean primes. Let
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be a
neat
residual representation. Then
\bar{\rho}
is
unobstructed
.

Proof.

The reader is referred to the proof of
[
Maz89
, Proposition 7]
.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Let
\bar{\rho}
be a neat Galois representation as in Theorem
4.18
, let
d=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(H^{1}(K_{S}/K,\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}))
and let
\rho_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
be the
\mathcal{O}
-deformation of
\bar{\rho}
associated to
a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{d})\in\mathcal{O}^{d}
as above (see (
4.2
) for the definition of
\rho_{a}
). Then, the following assertions hold:

(1)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{a}
,

(2)

the fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{A}(\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{a}(1))/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
associated to
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{a}(1)
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{a}(1))=0
. If
n=2
, then the same assertion holds for
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\rho_{a})\otimes(\det(\rho_{a}))^{-1}\chi_{p}
.

Proof.

The result is a direct consequence of Theorem
4.18
and Theorem
4.3
.
∎

In section 1.13 of
loc.cit.
, Mazur constructs examples of
2
-dimensional neat, odd Galois representations associated to certain
S_{3}
-extensions of
L/\mathbb{Q}
( which means
d=3
). The examples give a family of odd representations
\bar{\rho}
for which the above Corollary applies. More specifically, let
p
be a prime number which can be represented as
27+4a^{3}
, where
a
is an integer. Let
L
be the splitting field of
f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x^{3}+ax+1
, the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
S_{3}
. The discriminant of
f(x)
is
-p
and therefore,
p
is the only prime that is ramified in
L
. There is a natural inclusion of
S_{3}
into
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, via which we obtain a Galois representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. We refer to
loc. cit.
for further details. Such Galois representations are referred to as
special
S_{3}
-representations
since they are unobstructed, i.e.,
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
.
Since
\bar{\rho}
is an
S_{3}
-representation, it is easy to see that
\bar{\rho}
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
. Hence, it follows that
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}},{\bar{\rho}})=0
.
Note that
\bar{\rho}
is self-dual i.e.
\bar{\rho}^{\vee}=\bar{\rho}
.
Therefore, it follows from Theorem
3.7
that for any representation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
that lifts
\bar{\rho}
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\rho
,

(2)

the fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{A}(\rho(1))/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
associated to
\rho(1)
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\rho(1))=0
.

Similarly in
[
B9̈9
, Section 3.1]
, Böckle constructs examples of
2
-dimensional neat, even Galois representations associated to certain totally real
S_{3}
-extensions of
L/\mathbb{Q}
(which means
d=1
).
The examples give a family of even representations
\bar{\rho}
for which the above Corollary applies.
To be precise, let
p
be a prime number which can be represented as
4a^{6}-27
, where
a\geq 2
is an integer and
L
be the splitting field of
f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x^{3}-a^{2}x-1
. So the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
S_{3}
. The discriminant of
f(x)
is
p
and therefore,
p
is the only prime that is ramified in
L
. Using the arguments of the previous paragraph, we obtain a Galois representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
In
[
B9̈9
, Theorem 3.1]
, Böckle proves that
\bar{\rho}
is neat and hence, unobstructed if the following hypotheses are satisfied:

(1)

p
satisfies the Ankeney-Artin-Chowla conjecture,

(2)

\left(u\left(\dfrac{3}{2a^{2}}\right)-1\right)(2a^{3}+9)/6-\left(u\left(\dfrac{3}{3-2a^{3}}\right)-1\right)\not\equiv\dfrac{1}{243}(4a^{6}-27)(9/4+a^{3})\pmod{(4a^{6}-27)^{2}}
, where for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
u(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x/x^{p}
.

It is verified in
loc.cit.
that these hypotheses are satisfied for all
108
primes
p
of the form
4a^{6}-27
with
2\leq a\leq 1000
(see
[
B9̈9
, Section 3.1]
for more details).
As noted above,
\bar{\rho}
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
in this case as well. Therefore, we conclude, using the same arguments as above, that for any representation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},\{p,\infty\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
that lifts
\bar{\rho}
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\rho
.

(2)

The fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{A}(\rho(1))/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
associated to
\rho(1)
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\rho(1))=0
.

5.
Residually dihedral representations

In this section, we study residually dihedral Galois representations that arise from modular forms to illustrate Theorem
3.8
.

To be precise, let
p
be an odd prime,
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
,
S
be a finite set of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
containing
p
and
\infty
and
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
be a continuous representation.
We assume that
\bar{\rho}
is dihedral, which is to say that there exists a quadratic extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
and a character
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K,S^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
, where
S^{\prime}
is the set of primes of
K
that lie above the primes in
S
, such that
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
is isomorphic to the induced representation
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K,S^{\prime}}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}}(\psi)
.
For the ease of notation, we will denote this representation by
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
be the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with residue field
\mathbb{F}
and fix a uniformizer
\varpi
of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.
We say that a representation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
is
residually dihedral
if the residual representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
obtained by reducing
\rho
modulo
\varpi
is dihedral as described above.
Note that a residually dihedral representation
\rho
is not necessarily dihedral.

Now suppose
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
is dihedral which means
\bar{\rho}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)
for some character
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{K,S^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. Then the projective image of
\bar{\rho}
(i.e. the image of
\bar{\rho}
under the natural surjective map
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
obtained by going modulo scalars) is either a dihedral group or a cyclic group.
Moreover,
\bar{\rho}
is absolutely irreducible if and only if the projective image of
\bar{\rho}
is a dihedral group.

In addition to being dihedral, now assume
\bar{\rho}
is also odd and absolutely irreducible. Combining the work of Khare and Wintenberger on Serre’s conjecture (
[
KW09
, Theorem 9.1]
) and work of Kisin (
[
Kis09
, Theorem 0.1]
), we see that
\bar{\rho}
lifts to a
p
-adic modular Galois representation
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
without increasing the set
S
of ramified primes for the representation. There is however, always a natural choice of lift
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(W(\mathbb{F}))
(where
W(\mathbb{F})
is the ring of Witt vectors of
\mathbb{F}
), letting
\tilde{\psi}
denote the Teichmüller lift of
\psi
, we see that
\rho=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\tilde{\psi})
is a lift of
\bar{\rho}
.

In the setting of dihedral representations
\bar{\rho}
as above, we will provide explicit criteria for the conditions in Theorem
3.8
to be satisfied.
As a result, we obtain that if
\bar{\rho}
satisfies these criteria, then for all characteristic
0
lifts
\rho
of
\bar{\rho}
, and in particular for the lifts arising from modular newforms, the weak Leopoldt conjecture holds for
\rho^{*}
and
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\rho)=0
(see Theorem
5.8
).
Note that lifts of such representations arising from CM modular forms have been studied in
[
BN18
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
\bar{\rho}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})

. Let

be an irreducible

-module such that

. Then, with respect to notation above,

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
\bar{\rho}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)
.
Then the projective image of
\bar{\rho}
is a dihedral group if and only if
\psi\neq\psi^{\nu}
.
Moreover, the image of
\bar{\rho}
is itself a non-abelian dihedral group if and only if
\psi^{-1}=\psi^{\nu}
and
\psi^{\nu}\neq\psi
.

Proof.

Recall that the projective image of
\bar{\rho}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)
is dihedral if and only if
\bar{\rho}
is irreducible.

Let
V
be the vector space underlying
\bar{\rho}
.
As
\bar{\rho}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)
, we get, after choosing a suitable basis of
V
, that
\bar{\rho}(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi(g)&0\\
0&\psi^{\nu}(g)\end{pmatrix}
for all
g\in G_{K}
and
\bar{\rho}(\tilde{\nu})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
a&0\end{pmatrix}
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}.

Now
G_{\mathbb{Q}}=G_{K}\cup\tilde{\nu}G_{K}
.
Therefore,
\bar{\rho}
is reducible if and only if there exists a non-zero
v\in V
which is a common eigenvector for
\bar{\rho}(\tilde{\nu})
and
\bar{\rho}(g)
for all
g\in G_{K}
.
If
\psi=\psi^{\nu}
, then such an eigenvector clearly exists and hence,
\bar{\rho}
is reducible.

If
\psi\neq\psi^{\nu}
, then there exists a
g_{0}\in G_{K}
such that
\psi(g_{0})\neq\psi^{\nu}(g_{0})
.
So if a non-zero vector
v\in V
is an eigenvector for
\bar{\rho}(g_{0})
, then either
\bar{\rho}(g_{0})v=\psi(g_{0})v
or
\bar{\rho}(g_{0})v=\psi^{\nu}(g_{0})v
.
Let
w=\bar{\rho}(\tilde{\nu})v
. Then
w\neq 0
.
If
\bar{\rho}(g_{0})v=\psi(g_{0})v
, then
\bar{\rho}(g_{0})w=\psi^{\nu}(g_{0})w
.
As
\psi(g_{0})\neq\psi^{\nu}(g_{0})
and
w\neq 0
, it follows that
w
is not a scalar multiple of
v
. This means that
v
is not an eigenvector of
\bar{\rho}(\tilde{\nu})
.
If
\bar{\rho}(g_{0})v=\psi^{\nu}(g_{0})v
, then we similarly conclude that
v
is not an eigenvector of
\bar{\rho}(\tilde{\nu})
.
Therefore, if
\psi\neq\psi^{\nu}
, then
\bar{\rho}
is irreducible.
This finishes the proof of the first part of the lemma.

Note that the image of
\bar{\rho}
is a non-abelian dihedral group if and only if
\bar{\rho}(G_{K})
is a cyclic group of order
>2
and
\bar{\rho}(\tilde{\nu}g\tilde{\nu}^{-1})=\bar{\rho}(g^{-1})
for all
g\in G_{K}
.
Further,
\bar{\rho}(\tilde{\nu}g\tilde{\nu}^{-1})=\bar{\rho}(g^{-1})
for all
g\in G_{K}
if and only if
\psi^{\nu}=\psi^{-1}
. Moreover, if this holds, then
\bar{\rho}(G_{K})
is cyclic. If
\psi^{\nu}=\psi^{-1}
, then
\bar{\rho}(G_{K})
is cyclic of order
>2
if and only if
\psi^{-1}\neq\psi
.
Therefore, we conclude that the image of
\bar{\rho}
is a non-abelian dihedral group if and only if
\psi^{\nu}=\psi^{-1}
and
\psi^{\nu}\neq\psi
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
\bar{\rho}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)
.
Let
\ell
be a prime and
L
be the extension of
\mathbb{Q}
cut out by
\ker(\bar{\rho})
. Suppose that the trivial representation is a subrepresentation of
\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}
. Let
w\mid\ell
be a choice of prime of
K
that lies above
\ell
. Then, at least one of the following conditions hold:

(1)

\ell
splits in
K
and either
\psi_{|w}=1
or
\psi^{\nu}_{|w}=1
,

(2)

\ell
is either inert or ramified in
K
, and the unique prime
w
of
K
lying above
\ell
splits completely in

.

Proof.

Assume that
\ell
splits in
K
. Then, we have that
\bar{\rho}_{|w}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{|w}&0\\
0&\psi^{\nu}_{|w}\\
\end{array}}\right)
, and the result follows in this case.

Now suppose
\ell
is either inert or ramified in
K
.
Then
w
is the unique prime of
K
lying above
\ell
and we can choose
\tilde{\nu}\in G_{\ell}
.
As the trivial representation is a subrepresentation of
\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}
, it follows that either
\psi_{|w}=1
or
\psi^{\nu}_{|w}=1
.

As
\tilde{\nu}\in G_{\ell}
and
G_{w}
is normal in
G_{\ell}
, it follows that
\tilde{\nu}G_{w}\tilde{\nu}^{-1}=G_{w}
.
Therefore, it follows that if
\psi_{|w}=1
, then
\psi^{\nu}_{|w}=1
and vice versa. This finishes the proof of the Lemma.

∎

Let

Note that
\operatorname{Cl}_{2}(K)
is stable under the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})

Therefore, using class field theory, we conclude that

is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}

Let
\nu
be a lift of the generator of

in

Since

Let

.

Lemma 5.7
.

Under the notation and set-up established above,
\nu g\nu^{-1}=g^{-1}
for all
g\in H_{0}
.

Proof.

As
H_{0}
is normal in
G_{0}
,
\nu H_{0}\nu^{-1}=H_{0}
.
Let
H_{1}=\{h\in H_{0}\mid\nu h\nu^{-1}=h\}
and
H_{2}=\{h\in H_{0}\mid\nu h\nu^{-1}=h^{-1}\}
.
So
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are subgroups of
H_{0}
and moreover, they are normal in
G_{0}
. As
H_{0}
has odd order, we have
H_{1}\cap H_{2}=\{1\}
.
We now claim that
H_{0}=H_{1}H_{2}
.

Let
g\in H_{0}
and suppose
\nu g\nu^{-1}=g^{\prime}\in H_{0}
.
Let
g_{1}=gg^{\prime}
and
g_{2}=g(g^{\prime})^{-1}
. So
g^{2}=g_{1}g_{2}
.
As
\nu^{2}=1
, we get that
\nu g_{1}\nu^{-1}=g_{1}
and
\nu g_{2}\nu^{-1}=g_{2}^{-1}
.
So
g_{1}\in H_{1}
and
g_{2}\in H_{2}
.
Thus
g^{2}\in H_{1}H_{2}
for all
g\in H_{0}
.
As
H_{0}
is abelian and has odd order, every element of
H_{0}
is a square and hence,
H_{0}=H_{1}H_{2}
.

Suppose
|H_{1}|=k>1
. Note that
G^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G_{0}/H_{2}
is an abelian group of order
2|H_{1}|=2k
.
So if
N^{\prime}\subset N_{0}
is the fixed field of
H_{2}
, then
N^{\prime}
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(N^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})=G^{\prime}
.
Note that
N^{\prime}
is an unramified extension of
K
.
Therefore, a prime
\ell
of
\mathbb{Q}
is ramified in
N^{\prime}
if and only if it is ramified in
K
.
As
K
is quadratic over
\mathbb{Q}
, the order of the inertia group at any rational prime
\ell
in
\operatorname{Gal}(N^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})
is at most
2
.

As
k
is odd and
G^{\prime}
is abelian,
G^{\prime}
contains a unique subgroup
H^{\prime}
of order
2
.
Thus the subfield of
N^{\prime}
fixed by
H^{\prime}
is an unramified abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
k>1
. This gives us a contradiction.
Hence,
|H_{1}|=1
which means
H_{0}=H_{2}
proving the lemma.
∎

Let
L^{\prime}
be a cyclic, unramified extension of
K
with
[L^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]=n>1
,

) and

.
Note that such an extension exists as we are assuming that the class number of

is divisible by an odd prime

.
From Lemma

, we conclude that
L^{\prime}
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
, with
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})=D_{n}
, the dihedral group with
2n
elements.
As
n
is odd,
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})
is non-abelian.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic
p
. Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field with class number divisible by an odd prime different from
p
.
Let
L^{\prime}
be a cyclic extension of
K
contained in the Hilbert class field of
K
with
[L^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]=n>1
,
n
odd and
p\nmid n
.
Let
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/K)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
and
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be non-trivial characters.
Set
\bar{\rho}
to denote the induced representation
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{G}_{K}}^{\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi\eta)
of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
L
be the extension of
\mathbb{Q}
cut out by
\bar{\rho}
.
Let
S
be a set of prime numbers containing
p
,
\infty
and the primes which are ramified in
L
.
Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
be a lift of
\bar{\rho}
.
Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

If
\ell\in S
,
\ell
is split in
K
and
w
is a fixed place of
K
lying above
\ell
, then
|\psi(G_{w})|\neq|\eta(G_{\ell})|
,

(2)

If
\ell
is either inert or ramified in
K
and
w
is the unique place of
K
lying above
\ell
, then
\eta_{|\ell}
is non-trivial and is not the quadratic character of
G_{\ell}
corresponding to
K_{w}
,

(3)

L_{1,S}=L\cdot K_{1,S}
.

Then, the following assertions hold:

(a)

the weak Leopoldt conjecture is true for
\rho^{*}
,

(b)

the fine Selmer group
\mathcal{R}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{A}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
associated to
\rho
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu^{\operatorname{fn}}(\rho)=0
.

Proof.

Note that, under the hypotheses of the theorem above, conditions (
1
) and (
2
) of Theorem
5.6
hold.
Therefore, by Theorem
5.6
, it suffices to check that
no
condition of Lemma
5.5
is satisfied.
Let
\ell\in S
and fix a place
w
of
K
lying above
\ell
.

Suppose
\ell
is split in
K
. Now condition (
1
) implies that
\psi\eta_{|w}\neq 1
.
As
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})
is a non-abelian dihedral group, it follows that
\psi^{\nu}=\psi^{-1}
.
As
\eta
is a character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we get that
\eta^{\nu}=\eta
.
Therefore, condition (
1
) implies that
(\psi\eta)^{\nu}_{|w}=\psi^{-1}\eta_{|w}\neq 1
.
Thus condition (
1
) of Lemma
5.5
is not satisfied.

Now suppose
\ell
is either inert or ramified in
K
. As
L^{\prime}
is an unramified extension of
K
and
K_{w}
is a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, it follows that the image of
G_{\ell}
in
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})
is abelian.
As
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})=D_{n}
with
n
odd, it follows that the image of
G_{\ell}
in
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})
is an abelian group of order
2
.
Hence,
w
is totally split in
L^{\prime}/K
.
Now as
\eta_{|\ell}
is not the quadratic character corresponding to
K_{w}
and
\eta_{|\ell}\neq 1
, it follows that
\eta_{|w}\neq 1
and hence,
w
is not completely split in
L/K
.
Therefore, condition (
2
) of Lemma
5.5
is not satisfied. This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

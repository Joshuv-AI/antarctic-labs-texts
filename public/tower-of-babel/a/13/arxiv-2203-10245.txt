Extremal spectral radius of nonregular graphs with prescribed maximum degree
Lele Liu

Abstract
Let G G be a graph attaining the maximum spectral radius among all connected nonregular graphs of order n n with maximum degree Δ \Delta . Let λ 1 ​ ( G ) \lambda_{1}(G) be the spectral radius of G G . A nice conjecture due to Liu, Shen and Wang [On the largest eigenvalue of non-regular graphs, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B, 97 (2007) 1010–1018] asserts that lim n → ∞ n 2 ​ ( Δ − λ 1 ​ ( G ) ) Δ − 1 = π 2 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))}{\Delta-1}=\pi^{2} for each fixed Δ \Delta . Concerning an important structural property of the extremal graphs G G , Liu and Li present another conjecture which states that G G has degree sequence Δ , … , Δ , δ \Delta,\ldots,\Delta,\delta . Here, δ = Δ − 1 \delta=\Delta-1 or δ = Δ − 2 \delta=\Delta-2 depending on the parity of n ​ Δ n\Delta . In this paper, we make progress on the two conjectures. To be precise, we disprove the first conjecture for all Δ ≥ 3 \Delta\geq 3 by showing that the limit superior is at most π 2 / 2 \pi^{2}/2 . For small Δ \Delta , we determine the precise asymptotic behavior of Δ − λ 1 ​ ( G ) \Delta-\lambda_{1}(G) . In particular, we show that lim n → ∞ n 2 ​ ( Δ − λ 1 ​ ( G ) ) / ( Δ − 1 ) = π 2 / 4 \lim\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))/(\Delta-1)=\pi^{2}/4 if Δ = 3 \Delta=3 ; and lim n → ∞ n 2 ​ ( Δ − λ 1 ​ ( G ) ) / ( Δ − 2 ) = π 2 / 2 \lim\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))/(\Delta-2)=\pi^{2}/2 if Δ = 4 \Delta=4 . We also confirm the second conjecture for Δ = 3 \Delta=3 and Δ = 4 \Delta=4 by determining the precise structure of extremal graphs. Particularly, we show that the extremal graphs for Δ ∈ { 3 , 4 } \Delta\in\{3,4\} must have a path-like structure built from specific blocks. Keywords: Spectral radius; Nonregular graph; Maximum degree. AMS Classification: 05C35; 05C50; 15A18.

Introduction

One of the best known classes of graphs is regular graphs, which have been studied extensively
in a variety of contexts. For a nonregular graph in which not all vertices have equal degrees,
it can be viewed as somehow deviating from regularity. There are several measures on graphs
which are used to determine how close a given graph is to being regular. One such measure is
the difference between the maximum degree and the largest eigenvalue of a graph. To account
for this, we denote by
\lambda_{1}(G)
the largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix
A(G)

of a graph
G
, which is also called the spectral radius of
G
. We also denote by
\Delta(G)

and
\delta(G)
the maximum degree and minimum degree of
G
, respectively. Given a connected
graph
G
, it is well-known that
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\Delta(G)
, with equality if and only if
G

is regular. Hence, this fact allows us to consider the difference
\Delta(G)-\lambda_{1}(G)

as a relevant measure of irregularity of a graph
G
(see
[
10
, p. 242]
).
Also, it is natural to ask how small
\Delta(G)-\lambda_{1}(G)
can be when
G
is nonregular.
In the past decades the study of this parameter of nonregular graphs has attracted the interests
of a large number of scholars.

Let
G
be a connected nonregular graph on
n
vertices with maximum degree
\Delta
and minimum
degree
\delta
. In 2004, Stevanović
[
21
]
first proved that

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)>\frac{1}{2n(n\Delta-1)\Delta^{2}}.

He also asked whether or not the power of
\Delta
appearing in the fraction could be improved.
Using the ratio of components of the Perron vector, Zhang
[
24
]
obtained a finer
bound as follows

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)>\frac{(\sqrt{\Delta}-\sqrt{\delta})^{2}}{nD\Delta},

where
D
is the diameter of
G
. Correlatively, Alon and Sudakov
[
3
]
proved
that for a connected nonbipartite graph
G
,

\Delta+\lambda_{n}(G)>\frac{1}{n(D+1)},

where
\lambda_{n}(G)
is the least eigenvalue of
A(G)
. By taking the number of edges
m
of

G
into account, Cioabă, Gregory and Nikiforov
[
9
]
further
improved Zhang’s bound
[
24
]
, which stated that

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)>\frac{n\Delta-2m}{n(D(n\Delta-2m)+1)}\geq\frac{1}{n(D+1)}.

Let us remark that the above inequalities also refine Alon-Sudakov’s bound

[
3
]
as
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq-\lambda_{n}(G)
. Moreover, the
authors
[
9
]
also conjectured that
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)>1/(nD)

for all connected nonregular graphs with maximum degree
\Delta
. This was subsequently
proved by Cioabă
[
8
]
. In 2007, Liu, Shen and Wang
[
13
]

proved a lower bound of
\Delta(G)-\lambda_{1}(G)
in terms of
\Delta
and
n
as follows

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\frac{\Delta+1}{n(3n+2\Delta-4)},

which is asymptotically best possible up to a constant factor. Later, Liu, Huang and
You
[
15
]
presented a slight improvement for the aforementioned bound.
Shi
[
19
]
established another strong inequality by introducing more parameters as follows

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)>\Big[(n-\delta)D+\frac{1}{\Delta-2m/n}-\binom{D}{2}\Big]^{-1},

which improved Cioabă’s bound
[
8
]
in some cases. Recently, Zhang
[
23
]

and Feng-Zhang
[
11
]
furthermore improve Cioabă’s result
[
8
]

in different forms. For more results on this topic, we refer to

[
7
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
and references therein for details.
It is worth mentioning that any lower bound on
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)
also gives an upper bound
on
\lambda_{1}(G)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
denote the set of graphs attaining the maximum spectral radius
among all connected nonregular graphs with
n
vertices and maximum degree
\Delta
, and let

\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)
denote the maximum spectral radius. For a graph
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
,
Liu, Shen and Wang
[
13
]
investigated the order of magnitude of

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)
, and posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
. For each fixed
\Delta
, the limit of

n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))/(\Delta-1)
exists. Furthermore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))}{\Delta-1}=\pi^{2}.

Remark 1.1
.

This conjecture is trivially true for
\Delta=2
. Indeed, if
\Delta=2
, then
G
is
necessarily a path and
\lambda_{1}(G)=2\cos\pi/(n+1)
. Obviously,
2-2\cos\pi/(n+1)
is
asymptotic to
\pi^{2}/n^{2}
. Therefore,
Conjecture 1.1
holding for
\Delta=2
.

The following example shows that
Conjecture 1.1
no longer hold if the condition on

\Delta
was dropped.

Example 1.1
.

Let
\Delta=n-1
. Then
G
must be the graph obtained by removing an edge from the complete
graph
K_{n}
. Evidently,

\lambda_{1}(G)=\frac{n-3+\sqrt{n^{2}+2n-7}}{2}.

By direct computation we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))}{\Delta-1}=2.

Therefore the condition that
\Delta
is fixed is crucial in
Conjecture 1.1
.

Intuitively, the graphs attaining the maximum spectral radius among all connected nonregular
graphs with prescribed maximum degree must be close to regular graphs. In particular, Liu
and Li
[
14
]
posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
3\leq\Delta\leq n-2
and
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
. Then
G
has degree sequence

(\Delta,\ldots,\Delta,\delta)
, where

\delta=\begin{cases}\Delta-1,&n\Delta\ \text{is odd},\\
\Delta-2,&n\Delta\ \text{is even}.\end{cases}

Although the statement of
Conjecture 1.2
is intuitive, it appears difficult
to prove or disprove even for small
\Delta
. An indication to the difficulty of this
conjecture, as well as
Conjecture 1.1
, is that graphs having bounded degree are sparse
graphs, whose spectral radius are bounded by a constant. Hence, numerous tools from spectral
graph theory are ineffective. In this paper we make the first progress on these two conjectures.
More precisely, we disprove
Conjecture 1.1
for all
\Delta\geq 3
, and confirm

Conjecture 1.2
for
\Delta=3
and
\Delta=4
. In addition, we determine the
exact structure of extremal graphs in
\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
, as well as the leading term of

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)
for
\Delta\in\{3,4\}
. Particularly, we show that the extremal
graphs must have a path-like structure for
\Delta\in\{3,4\}
. To prove our results, we use the
local switching operations (see in Section
2
) and forbidden induced subgraphs
to deduce structural properties of the extremal graphs. The original ideas come from

[
1
,
2
,
5
,
12
]
,
which study the minimum algebraic connectivity of regular graphs.

The present paper is built up as follows. Preliminary definitions and notation are collected
in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we present some
general results on the extremal graphs that will be used frequently in the sequel. We
prove
Conjecture 1.2
and determine the structure of extremal graphs for

\Delta=3
and
\Delta=4
in Section
4
and Section
5
,
respectively. In Section
6
, we give an upper bound of
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)
,
which allows us to disprove
Conjecture 1.1
. In addition, we determine the precise asymptotic
behavior of
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)
for
\Delta\in\{3,4\}
. We conclude this paper in
Section
7
with some remarks and open problems.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce notation and preliminary lemmas that will be used in the sequel.

Throughout this paper we consider only simple graphs, i.e, undirected graphs without multiple
edges or loops. Given a subset
X
of the vertex set
V(G)
of a graph
G
, the subgraph of

G
induced by
X
is denoted by
G[X]
, and the graph obtained from
G
by deleting
X
is
denoted by
G\setminus X
. As usual, for a vertex
v
of
G
we write
d_{G}(v)
and
N_{G}(v)

for the degree of
v
and the set of neighbors of
v
in
G
, respectively. If the underlying
graph
G
is clear from the context, simply
d(v)
and
N(v)
. Let
N_{X}(v)
denote the set of
vertices in
X
that adjacent to
v
, i.e.,
N_{X}(v)=N_{G}(v)\cap X
. Given two vertices

u
,
v
, we use
u\sim v
(resp.
u\nsim v
) to indicate that vertices
u
and
v
are adjacent
(resp. nonadjacent). For a positive integer
n
, let
[n]
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a vector, we use
x_{\max}
and
x_{\min}
to denote the maximum
and minimum components of
\bm{x}
, respectively.

The Perron–Frobenius theorem implies that the adjacency matrix
A(G)
of a connected graph
G

has a positive eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(G)
, and this is called the
Perron vector

of
G
. Let
\bm{x}
and
\Delta
be the Perron vector and maximum degree of
G
, respectively.
A short argument shows that

(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))\cdot\|\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}(\Delta-d(v))x_{v}^{2}+\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}.

(2.1)

Recall that the Laplacian matrix of
G
is defined as
L(G):=D(G)-A(G)
, where
D(G)
is
the diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are the vertex degrees of
G
. Hence, by Rayleigh
principle, for any vector
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{|V(G)|}
we have

(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))\cdot\|\bm{y}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}(\Delta-d(v))y_{v}^{2}+\bm{y}^{\mathrm{T}}L(G)\bm{y}.

(2.2)

On the other hand, noting that
A(G)\bm{x}=\lambda_{1}(G)\bm{x}
, we have

\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}A(G)\bm{x}=\lambda_{1}(G)\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{x}
,
where
\bm{1}
is the all-ones vector. That is,

\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(u)x_{v}=\lambda_{1}(G)\sum_{v\in V(G)}x_{v}.

We immediately obtain that

\sum_{v\in V(G)}(\Delta-d(v))x_{v}=(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))\sum_{v\in V(G)}x_{v}.

(2.3)

The following two local operations on edges of graphs are well-known, from which one can obtain
perturbation results in spectral radius under edge operations.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
10
]
, Theorem 8.1.3)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
uv\in E(G)
and
uw\notin E(G)
. Let

\widetilde{G}:=G+uw-uv
and
\bm{x}
be the Perron vector of
G
. If

x_{w}\geq x_{v}
, then
\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{G})>\lambda_{1}(G)
.

Given a graph
G
, the
local switching
is the replace of a pair of edges
uv
and
st

in
G
by the edges
sv
and
tu
, which is denoted by
{\bf LS}(s,t,v,u)
. In addition, we use

{\bf LS}(G;s,t,v,u)
to denote the resulting graph obtained by the local switching operation

{\bf LS}(s,t,v,u)
. Note that local switching preserves degrees.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
10
]
, Theorem 8.1.10)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph, and
\widetilde{G}={\bf LS}(G;s,t,v,u)
. If
(x_{s}-x_{u})(x_{v}-x_{t})\geq 0
,
then
\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{G})\geq\lambda_{1}(G)
, with equality if and only if
x_{s}=x_{u}
and
x_{v}=x_{t}
.

3
General results for graphs in
\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)

In this section, we present some general results on the extremal graphs in
\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)

that will be useful at various points in this paper. In order to state our results, we should
first introduce more symbol. Let
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
. We denote

S=\{v\in V(G):d(v)<\Delta\},~~T=\{v\in V(G):d(v)=\Delta\}.

Throughout this section and the next two sections, we always assume that
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)

and
\bm{x}
is the Perron vector of
G
with
\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1
. Denote
V(G):=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
,
and write
x_{i}:=x_{v_{i}}
for brevity. For convenience, we assume
\bm{x}
is decreasing, i.e.,

x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}
.

3.1
The size of
S
is small

The proof of the following lemma can be found in
[
14
]
and
[
16
]
, we include
it here in order to keep this paper complete and self-contained.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
14
,
16
]
)
.

The induced subgraph
G[S]
is a complete graph.

Proof.

The assertion is clear for
|S|\geq 3
by the monotonicity of
\lambda_{1}(G)
with respect to edge
addition. So in the following we assume
|S|=2
.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
S=\{u,v\}
and
u\nsim v
. Then
d(u)=d(v)=\Delta-1

due to the maximality of
\lambda_{1}(G)
. Without loss of generality, we assume
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
.
We first show that
N(u)=N(v)
by contradiction. If
N(u)\cap N(v)\neq\emptyset
, we choose
a vertex
w\in N(v)\setminus N(u)
. Let
G^{\prime}:=G+uw-vw
. Obviously,
G^{\prime}
is still a connected
nonregular graph, and
\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})>\lambda_{1}(G)
by
Lemma 2.1
, a contradiction.
If
N(u)\cap N(v)=\emptyset
, let
P
be a shortest path from
v
to
u
. Since

d(v)=\Delta-1\geq 2
, there exists a vertex
w\in N(v)
such that
w\notin V(P)
.
Let
G^{\prime\prime}:=G+uw-vw
. Then
G^{\prime\prime}
is connected and
\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime\prime})>\lambda_{1}(G)
, a contradiction.
It follows that
N(u)=N(v)
.

Since
G
is connected and
d(u)=d(v)=\Delta-1
, there exits two vertices
s,t\in N(u)=N(v)

such that
st\notin E(G)
. In what follows we shall prove
x_{s}>x_{u}
. If
|N_{T}(s)\cap N(u)|=\Delta-2
,
by eigenvalue equations we have

(\lambda_{1}(G)+1)(x_{s}-x_{u})=x_{v},

which yields
x_{s}>x_{u}
. If
|N_{T}(s)\cap N(u)|<\Delta-2
, there must be a vertex
w\in N_{T}(s)

such that
wu\notin E(G)
. If
x_{s}\leq x_{u}
, then the graph
G+uw-ws
has larger spectral radius
than
G
, a contradiction. Therefore, we have
x_{s}>x_{u}
. Similarly, we have
x_{t}>x_{v}
. Finally,
we deduce that
\lambda_{1}({\bf LS}(G;u,t,v,s))>\lambda_{1}(G)
by
Lemma 2.2
,
a contradiction completing the proof.
∎

We immediately obtain an upper bound on
|S|
as follows.

Corollary 3.1
.

|S|\leq\Delta-1
.

3.2
The components of Perron vector on
S
are small

Roughly speaking, the next lemma states that for any
u
,
v\in S
, the neighbors of
u
and
v
in
T

are nested, i.e., one of sets is contained in the other one.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
and
u,v\in S
. Then
x_{u}\leq x_{v}
if and only if
N_{T}(u)\subset N_{T}(v)
.

Proof.

We first show the necessity. Suppose contrary that there exists
w\in N_{T}(u)
and
w\notin N_{T}(v)
.
Let
G^{\prime}:=G-uw+vw
. Then
\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})>\lambda_{1}(G)
by
Lemma 2.1
, a contradiction.
Hence, we have
N_{T}(u)\subset N_{T}(v)
.

For the other direction, by eigenvalues equations for
u
,
v
and
Lemma 3.1
, we have

\lambda_{1}(G)x_{u}=\sum_{w\in S\setminus\{u\}}x_{w}+\sum_{w\in N_{T}(u)}x_{w},~~\lambda_{1}(G)x_{v}=\sum_{w\in S\setminus\{v\}}x_{w}+\sum_{w\in N_{T}(v)}x_{w}.

Therefore, we derive that

(\lambda_{1}(G)+1)(x_{v}-x_{u})=\sum_{w\in N_{T}(v)\setminus N_{T}(u)}x_{w}\geq 0,

which implies that
x_{v}\geq x_{u}
, as desired.
∎

A vertex
v\in V(G)
is called
local minimum
if
x_{w}\geq x_{v}
for any
w\in N(v)
.
Obviously, any vertex in
T
is not local minimum, for otherwise,
G
has largest eigenvalue
at least
\Delta
, a contradiction.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in S
and
v\in T
be two vertices such that
x_{u}=\max\{x_{w}:w\in S\}
and

x_{v}=\min\{x_{w}:w\in T\}
. Then
x_{u}<x_{v}
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
. Since
v
is not local minimum, we have

N_{S}(v)\neq\emptyset
. Using
Lemma 3.2
, we find that
uv\in E(G)
.
If there exists
w\in N_{T}(v)
such that
uw\notin E(G)
, we let
G^{\prime}:=G+uw-vw
. Then

G^{\prime}
is connected and
\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})>\lambda_{1}(G)
, a contradiction. So we assume

N_{T}(v)\subset N_{T}(u)
below. Now we consider the degree of
u
. In light of
Lemma 3.1

we deduce that

d(u)=(|S|-1)+|N_{T}(u)|\geq(|S|+|N_{T}(v)|)-1\geq d(v)-1=\Delta-1.

On the other hand,
d(u)\leq\Delta-1
due to
u\in S
. Hence, all inequalities above
must be equalities, we immediately obtain
N_{T}(v)=N_{T}(u)
. By the eigenvalue equations
for
u
and
v
, we have
(\lambda_{1}(G)+1)x_{u}=\lambda_{1}(G)x_{v}
, contrary to the
assumption
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
. This completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

3.3
Local adjacency relations of vertices

Given an integer
k
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{k}
the collection of the first
k
vertices
(with respect to the components of Perron vector
\bm{x}
) of

, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}_{k}:=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}

The following lemma will be used frequently in the subsequent proofs.

Lemma 3.4

(Principle of Proximity)
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
and
d=d_{G[\mathcal{M}_{k}]}(v)<\Delta
. If
v
is not adjacent

for

, then we can connect

v
to
v_{k+s+1},\ldots,v_{k+s+\Delta-d}
, not decreasing
\lambda_{1}(G)
.

Proof.

Assume that
v
is not adjacent to some
u\in\{v_{k+s+1},\ldots,v_{k+s+\Delta-d}\}
, and

v_{j}\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}\cup\{v_{k+s+1},\ldots,v_{k+s+\Delta-d}\}
is a neighbor of
v

with
j>k+s+\Delta-d
. Since
N(u)\cap\mathcal{M}_{k}=\emptyset
, we deduce that there
exists a neighbor
w
of

such that
w\nsim v_{j}
. By
Lemma 2.2
,
we may apply the operation
{\bf LS}(v,v_{j},u,w)
to

, not decreasing the largest eigenvalue
and leaving

adjacent to

, as desired.
∎

If
s=0
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
and
d=d_{G[\mathcal{M}_{k}]}(v)<\Delta
.
If
N(v_{i})\cap\mathcal{M}_{k}=\emptyset
for

,
then we can connect
v
to
v_{k+1},\ldots,v_{k+\Delta-d}
,
not decreasing
\lambda_{1}(G)
.

The next conclusion then follows immediately by setting

in

.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
. Then we can connect
v_{1}
to both
v_{2}
,
v_{3},\ldots,v_{\Delta+1}
,
not decreasing the largest eigenvalue of
G
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any local switching to
G
that preserving non-decreasing of

Lemma 3.5
.

For any positive integer

Proof.

We prove this lemma by contradiction. Recall that
S

. Hence,

. In what

\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)>\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}(x_{u_{i}}-x_{u_{i+1}})^{2}\geq\frac{(x_{v}-x_{u})^{2}}{\ell}>\frac{(x_{v}-x_{u})^{2}}{n}.

On the other hand,
Theorem 6.1
implies that
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G)=O(n^{-2})
,
from which and the above inequality we have
x_{\max}=O(n^{-1/2})
.
∎

With
Theorem 6.1
and
Lemma 6.2
in hand, we are ready
to give an exact coefficient for the leading term of
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)
for
\Delta\in\{3,4\}
.
Let
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
. Observe that
G\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(1)}
for
\Delta=3
and
G\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(2)}

for
\Delta=4
. So we need to consider the asymptotic behavior of
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p}\odot F)
,
where
p\in\{\Delta-1,\Delta-2\}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

The following conclusions hold:

(1)

Let
\Delta
be odd and
G_{\Delta,k-1}^{\Delta-1}\odot F\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(1)}
. Then

\Delta-\lambda_{1}\big(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{\Delta-1}\odot F\big)=(1+o(1))\frac{(\Delta-1)\pi^{2}}{4n^{2}}.

(2)

Let
\Delta
be even and
G_{\Delta,k-1}^{\Delta-2}\odot F\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(2)}
. Then

\Delta-\lambda_{1}\big(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{\Delta-2}\odot F\big)=(1+o(1))\frac{(\Delta-2)\pi^{2}}{2n^{2}}.

Proof.

Referring to the proof of
Theorem 6.1
, it is enough to give a matched lower bound
on
\Delta-\lambda_{1}\big(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p}\odot F\big)
for
p\in\{\Delta-1,\Delta-2\}
. Furthermore,
by the monotonicity of the spectral radius of graphs with respect to vertex addition, we only need to
consider the graph shown in Fig.
18
, whose components of Perron vector
\bm{x}

are also labeled in Fig.
18
.

From (
2.1
), a routine computation gives rise to

\Delta-\lambda_{1}\big(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p}\odot F\big)>p\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(z_{i}-a_{i})^{2}+p(\Delta-p)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(a_{i}-b_{i})^{2}+(\Delta-p)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(b_{i}-z_{i+1})^{2}.

Consider the function
f_{i}(x,y):=p(z_{i}-x)^{2}+p(\Delta-p)(x-y)^{2}+(\Delta-p)(y-z_{i+1})^{2}
.
One can check that
f_{i}(x,y)
attaining minimum at

x=\frac{(p+1)z_{i}+(\Delta-p)z_{i+1}}{\Delta+1},~~y=\frac{pz_{i}+(\Delta-p+1)z_{i+1}}{\Delta+1}.

Putting all these together, we conclude that

\Delta-\lambda_{1}\big(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p}\odot F\big)>\frac{p(\Delta-p)}{\Delta+1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(z_{i}-z_{i+1})^{2}.

(6.7)

Below we shall give an estimation for the right-hand side of the above inequality.
By eigenvalue equations, we find that

a_{i}\leq b_{i}\leq z_{i+1},~~i\in[k-1].

Noting the fact that
\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}^{2}+p\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{i}^{2}+(\Delta-p)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}^{2}+\sum_{u\in V(F)\setminus V(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p})}x_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\Delta+1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}^{2}+\sum_{u\in V(F)\setminus V(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p})}x_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\Delta+1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}^{2}+O(n^{-1}).

The last equality uses the fact that
x_{\max}=O(n^{-1/2})
. It follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\Delta+1}-O(n^{-1}).

(6.8)

For each
i\in[k]
, let
z_{-i}=z_{i}
, and denote
\bm{z}:=(z_{-1},\ldots,z_{-k},z_{k},\ldots,z_{1})^{\mathrm{T}}
.
Consider the path
P_{2k}
on
2k
vertices, and notice that
z_{-1}=z_{1}=O(n^{-3/2})
by
Lemma 6.2
.
According to (
6.7
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\Delta-\lambda_{1}\big(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p}\odot F\big)

\displaystyle>\frac{p(\Delta-p)}{2(\Delta+1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=-k,\\
i\notin\{-1,0\}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}(z_{i}-z_{i+1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p(\Delta-p)}{2(\Delta+1)}\big(z_{-1}^{2}+z_{1}^{2}+\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}L(P_{2k})\bm{z}\big)-O(n^{-3})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p(\Delta-p)}{2(\Delta+1)}\big(2-\lambda_{1}(P_{2k})\big)\cdot\|\bm{z}\|_{2}^{2}-O(n^{-3}).

The last inequality follows from (
2.2
). Recall that
\lambda_{1}(P_{2k})=2\cos\pi/(2k+1)
(see, e.g.
[
6

).
It follows from (
6.8
) that

\displaystyle\Delta-\lambda_{1}\big(G_{\Delta,k-1}^{p}\odot F\big)

\displaystyle>\frac{p(\Delta-p)\pi^{2}}{(\Delta+1)^{2}(2k+1)^{2}}-O(n^{-3})

\displaystyle=\frac{p(\Delta-p)\pi^{2}}{4n^{2}}-O(n^{-3}),

where the last equality use the fact
k=(n-\alpha)/(\Delta+1)
. Finally, we obtain the desired
results by letting

for odd
\Delta
and
p=\Delta-2
for even
\Delta
, respectively.
∎

We immediately obtain the asymptotic behavior of
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)
for
\Delta\in\{3,4\}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

If
\Delta=3
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(3-\lambda_{1}(n,3))=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}.

If
\Delta=4
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(4-\lambda_{1}(n,4))=\pi^{2}.

Concluding remarks and open problems

In this paper, we study the structural properties of extremal graphs in
\mathcal{G}(n,\Delta)
, which disprove

Conjecture 1.1
for
\Delta\geq 3
and confirm
Conjecture 1.2
when
\Delta\in\{3,4\}
.
Although
Conjecture 1.1
is not true as the inconsistent asymptotic behavior of
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)

for
\Delta
that have different parity, we can still ask what is the exact leading term of
\Delta-\lambda_{1}(n,\Delta)
.

Theorem 6.3
answers this problem for
\Delta=3
and
\Delta=4
. However,
for general
\Delta
, it seems to be difficult to solve it. Based on some numerical experiments and heuristic
arguments, we present the following conjecture.

Conjecture 7.1
.

Let
G
be a graph attaining the maximum spectral radius among all connected
nonregular graph with
n
vertices and maximum degree
\Delta
. Then
the limit of
n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))
always exists. Furthermore,

(1)

if
\Delta
is odd, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))}{\Delta-1}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}.

(2)

if
\Delta

(\Delta>2)
is even, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{2}(\Delta-\lambda_{1}(G))}{\Delta-2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}.

By analyzing the structural properties of the extremal graphs, we confirm
Conjecture 1.2

for small
\Delta
. However, we cannot expect an affirmative answer to
Conjecture 1.2
for
general
\Delta
. In fact, we have some evidence for the following speculation.

Conjecture 7.2
.

Let
G
be a graph attaining the maximum spectral radius among all connected
nonregular graph with
n
vertices and maximum degree
\Delta

(\Delta\geq 3)
.
For each fixed
\Delta
and sufficiently large
n
,
G
has degree sequence
(\Delta,\ldots,\Delta,\delta)
,
where

\delta=\begin{cases}\Delta-1,&\Delta\ \text{is odd},\ n\ \text{is odd},\\
1,&\Delta\ \text{is odd},\ n\ \text{is even},\\
\Delta-2,&\Delta\ \text{is even}.\end{cases}

Finally, we remark that for a connected nonregular graph

of order
n

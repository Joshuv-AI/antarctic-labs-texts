Commuting varieties and cohomological complexity theory
Paul D. Levy, Nham V. Ngo, Klemen Šivic

Abstract
. In this paper we determine, for all r r sufficiently large, the irreducible component(s) of maximal dimension of the variety of commuting r r -tuples of nilpotent elements of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} . Our main result is that in characteristic ≠ 2 , 3 \neq 2,3 , this nilpotent commuting variety has dimension ( r + 1 ) ​ ⌊ n 2 4 ⌋ (r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , r ≥ 7 r\geq 7 . We use this to find the dimension of the (ordinary) r r -th commuting varieties of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} and 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{sl}_{n} for the same range of values of r r and n n . Our principal motivation is the connection between nilpotent commuting varieties and cohomological complexity of finite group schemes, which we exploit in the last section of the paper to obtain explicit values for complexities of a large family of modules over the r r -th Frobenius kernel ( GL n ) ( r ) (\GL_{n})_{(r)} . These results indicate an inequality between the complexities of a rational G G -module M M when restricted to G ( r ) G_{(r)} or to G ⁡ ( 𝔽 p r ) G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}) ; we subsequently establish this inequality for every simple algebraic group G G defined over an algebraically closed field of good characteristic, significantly extending the main theorem in [ LN99 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Commuting Varieties

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field and let
r
be a positive integer.
Let
G
be a linear algebraic group defined over
k
and
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(G)
.
For each closed subvariety
V
of
\mathfrak{g}
, denote by

C_{r}(V)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in V^{r}:[x_{i},x_{j}]=0,1\leq i,j\leq r\}

the variety of commuting
r
-tuples of elements of
V
.
For brevity, we will call
C_{r}(V)
the
r
-th commuting variety of
V
, or just the commuting variety of
V
if there is no potential for confusion.
Two special cases which have received considerable interest are the
ordinary commuting variety

C_{r}(\mathfrak{g})
, and the
nilpotent commuting variety

C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
, where
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
is the nilpotent cone of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Basic geometric properties of these varieties, such as the number and dimensions of irreducible components, are unknown except for small values of
r
or
n
.

The first results on ordinary commuting varieties were the independent proofs by Motzkin-Taussky
[
MT55
]
and Gerstenhaber
[
Ger61
]
that
C_{2}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is irreducible.
Subsequently, this was extended to the Lie algebra of an arbitrary reductive algebraic group by Richardson
[
Ric79
]
in characteristic zero, and by the first author
[
Lev02
]
in positive characteristic (under mild hypotheses).
It was observed in
[
Ger61
]
that the irreducibility of
C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
can fail for
r\geq 4
.
Guralnick showed
[
Gur92
]
that
C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is irreducible for
n\leq 3
and any
r
, while it is reducible for all
n,r\geq 4
.

Although nilpotent commuting varieties have received rather less attention until relatively recently, interest in
C_{2}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
has been stimulated by a close relationship with the punctual Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the plane.
This connection was exploited by Baranovsky in his proof
[
Bar01
]
of the irreducibility of
C_{2}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
for
{\rm char}(k)=0
or
{\rm char}(k)\geq n
.
Baranovsky’s result was extended to
{\rm char}(k)\geq\frac{n}{2}
by Basili
[
Bas03
]
, and to arbitrary characteristic by Premet
[
Pre03a
]
, as a special case of equidimensionality of the (second) nilpotent commuting variety of the Lie algebra of a reductive algebraic group.
For larger values of
r
, work of the second and third authors shows that
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
is irreducible for
n\leq 3
and any
r
, and is reducible for all
r,n\geq 4

[
Ngo14
]
,
[
NŠ14
]
.
Apart from these reducibility results, little is known about these varieties for general
r
and
n
.

In this paper, we study the dimensions of the ordinary and nilpotent commuting varieties for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
The main initial idea is the following: let
{\mathfrak{m}}
be a commutative nil (i.e. consisting of nilpotent elements) subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
of maximal dimension.
It is clear that for
r
large enough, the dimension of the linear span of a general
r
-tuple from
G\cdot\mathfrak{m}^{r}
is not smaller than the dimension of the linear span of any
r
-tuple from
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
.
Since this is an open condition and there are, up to conjugacy, at most two commutative nil subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
of maximal dimension, the subvariety
\overline{G\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}}
is an irreducible component of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
of dimension
r\dim{\mathfrak{m}}+\dim G-\dim N_{G}({\mathfrak{m}})
.
For
\mathfrak{g}
of type
A
, the commutative nil subalgebras of maximal dimension were studied in
[
CFP15
]
; the remaining types have been dealt with in the subsequent paper
[
PS16
]
, generalizing results of Malcev in characteristic zero
[
Mal45
]
.
Subsets
G\cdot\mathfrak{m}^{r}
for various classical
\mathfrak{g}
were also investigated by the second author in
[
Ngo15
]
.
Most of our effort in the first part of this paper will be directed towards showing that (under mild conditions on
r
) no irreducible component of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
can have dimension greater than
\dim(G\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}^{r})
.
As a consequence, we obtain (see Theorem
4.8
):

Theorem
.

Assume
{\rm char}\,k\neq 2,3
,
n\geq 4
, and
r\geq 7
.
Let
G=\GL_{n}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
Then

\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))=(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor,

and the irreducible components of maximal dimension are the subsets of the form
G\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}
where
{\mathfrak{m}}
is a commutative nil subalgebra of maximal dimension; there is one such component if
n
is even, and two if
n
is odd.

We can apply this result to determine the irreducible components of maximal dimension of the
r
-th ordinary commuting varieties of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, for the same range of values of
r
and
n
(see Cor.
4.9
and Cor.
4.10
).

Corollary
.

Assume
{\rm char}\,k\neq 2,3
,
n\geq 4
and
r\geq 7
.
Exclude the case
(n,r)=(4,7)
.
Then
\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})=(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor+r
and
\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})=\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
unless
{\rm char}\,k|n
, in which case
\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})=\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.

In a subsequent paper
[
LNŠ21
]
, we will apply a similar analysis to the
r
-th nilpotent commuting variety of the symplectic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
The dimension is
(r+1)n(n+1)/2
for large enough
n
and
r
.
Note that in this case
{\mathfrak{m}}
has dimension
n(n+1)/2
, and this also equals the codimension (in
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
) of the normalizer of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
In general we make the following (see
[
PS16
]
for background on the variety
{\mathbb{E}}(d,\mathfrak{g})
):

Conjecture
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}={\rm Lie}(G)
where
G
is a simple algebraic group.
For
r
sufficiently large, we have

\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))=dr+m

where
d
is the maximum dimension of an elementary subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and
m=\dim{\mathbb{E}}(d,\mathfrak{g})
is the dimension of the projective variety of all
d
-dimensional elementary subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
.

This conjecture should be seen in the context of Friedlander’s support varieties for rational representations, see
[
Fri15
]
; the formula
dr+m
can be thought of as (conjecturally, in general) the asymptotic dimension of the topological space
V(G)
of 1-parameter subgroups of
G
.

1.2.
Complexity of modules over Frobenius kernels

After establishing our main theorem, we apply our results on nilpotent commuting varieties to cohomology of infinitesimal subgroups of
G
. Assuming
p>0
, let
F_{r}:G\to G
be the
r
-th Frobenius morphism of
G
, defined on matrices as
(a_{ij})\mapsto(a^{p^{r}}_{ij})
.
Then the
r
-th
Frobenius kernel
of
G
, denoted by
G_{(r)}
, is defined to be the scheme-theoretic kernel of
F_{r}
.
Note that
G_{(r)}
is an
infinitesimal
group scheme, that is, it has only one rational point over the base field
k
.
The group structure comes into play when considering points over arbitrary
k
-algebras, or (equivalently) in the Hopf algebra structure of the (dual of the) finite-dimensional coordinate ring. (See
[
Jan03
]
for an account of the background theory.)

The theory of support varieties for finite groups, and later for finite group schemes, was motivated by the idea that geometric methods might help to shed light on their (modular) representation theory.
In our case, one considers the even cohomology ring
\opH^{ev}(G_{(r)},k)
, which turns out to be commutative and finitely generated over
k

[
FS97
, Theorem 1.1]
, so its maximal ideal spectrum is an affine variety, the
support variety
of the trivial
G_{(r)}
-module.
By
[
SFB97b
, Theorem 5.2]
and
[
SFB97a
, Lemma 1.7]
this variety is homeomorphic to the
r
-th commuting variety
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g}))
, where
\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g})
is the set of matrices in
\mathfrak{g}
with
p
-th power zero.
We therefore obtain, from our results on
C_{r}({\mathcal{N}}(\mathfrak{g}))
, the Krull dimension of the ring
\opH^{ev}(G_{(r)},k)
for
G=\GL_{n}(k)
.
This dimension is the
complexity
of the trivial module over
G_{(r)}
.
More generally, to an arbitrary
G_{(r)}
-module
M
one associates a certain subvariety of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g}))
; the dimension of this subvariety is the complexity of
M
.
In Theorem
5.3
, we deduce a criterion on the highest weight (assuming
p{>}n^{3}/4
) for the restriction to
G_{(r)}
of a simple
G
-module to have maximal complexity.
Up to a point, this reduces the determination of the complexity of a given simple
G_{(r)}
-module to the study of the support varieties of the various simple restricted
\mathfrak{g}
-modules arising from the
p
-adic decomposition of the highest weight.

Inspired by the results outlined in the previous paragraph, at the end of the paper we explore further a connection between the complexities of Frobenius kernels and finite groups of Lie type.
To explain this, let
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
be the group consisting of all
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
-rational points of
G
, a finite Chevalley group.
Similarly to
G_{(r)}
, these subgroups inherit certain cohomological properties from the ambient algebraic group
G
.
The complexity theory of
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
is well-known from work of Quillen et al, see e.g.
[
Ben91
, Ch. 5]
for further details.
The complexity of the trivial module for any finite group
\Gamma
is equal to the
p
-rank, i.e. the maximal rank of an elementary abelian
p
-subgroup of
\Gamma
.
The
p
-ranks of the finite Chevalley groups are known, see e.g.
[
Hum06
, S 15.4]
.
In particular, if
G=\SL_{n}
then

c_{G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(k)=r\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor

which (by our main Theorem) equals
\tfrac{r}{r+1}c_{G_{(r)}}(k)
for
r\geq 7
and
n\geq 4
.
In the final section of the paper, we prove the inequality

c_{G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M)\leq\frac{r}{r+1}c_{G_{(r)}}(M)

for an arbitrary finite-dimensional rational module
M
over a simple algebraic group
G
in good positive characteristic (see Theorem
5.7
).
This significantly generalizes a result of Lin and Nakano
[
LN99
, Thm. 3.4(b)]
(which considers the case
r=1
) and provides a new proof of the result of Drupieski
[
Dru13
, Thm. 2.3]
that
M|_{G({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{r}})}
is projective if
M|_{G_{(r)}}
is.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

In §
2
we collect some general results on nilpotent orbits and involutions which will be useful in the rest of the paper.
The main results concerning the nilpotent commuting variety are established in §
4
.
We first consider a certain subset
C^{\prime}(x)
of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
related to the commuting variety of
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
, where
x
is a nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{g}
with no Jordan blocks of order greater than 4.
(See §
4.0
for the definition of
C^{\prime}(x)
.)
In this special case, we analyse the subset
C^{\prime}(x)
in some detail, proving with the aid of various technical results from §
3
that
\dim C^{\prime}(x)
is no greater than
(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor
.
(To preserve the sanity of the reader, details of some computations are deferred to the Appendix.)
Once this case is settled, we can then prove the required inequality for an arbitrary
x
by an induction argument.
Taken on their own, the technical results in §
3
are unlikely to be of much interest, so a reader who does not want to get bogged down in the details might prefer to go straight to §
4
, only occasionally referring to the earlier sections for the auxiliary results.
Finally, §
5
deals with the connections with cohomological support and complexity of modules over finite group schemes.

2.
Some background

In this section we collect some standard results on orbital varieties and involutions.

2.1.
Gradings of nilpotent centralizers and orbital varieties

Let
G
be a simple algebraic group or
\GL_{n}
, let
{\mathfrak{g}}=\Lie(G)
and let
e
be a nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In characteristic zero, we can embed
e
in an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{h,e,f\}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)\subseteq\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}(h;i)
where
\mathfrak{g}(h;i)=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:[h,x]=ix\}
.
In positive characteristic, one has to be careful about the use of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples because, for example, the grading of
\mathfrak{g}
according to
(\ad h)
-eigenspaces becomes an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-grading rather than a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading.
Under mild conditions one can replace the machinery of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples by
associated cocharacters
.
An associated cocharacter for
e
is a cocharacter
\lambda:k^{\times}\rightarrow G
such that:

-
\lambda(t)\cdot e=t^{2}e
for all
t\in k^{\times}
;

-
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)\subseteq\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}(\lambda;i)
, where
\mathfrak{g}(\lambda;i)=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:\lambda(t)\cdot x=t^{i}x\;\mbox{for all}\;t\in k^{\times}\}
;

- there is a Levi subgroup
L
of
G
such that
e\in\Lie(L)
is distinguished and
\lambda(k^{\times})\subseteq L^{(1)}=(L,L)
.

If the characteristic of the base field is a
good prime
for
G

[
SS70
, §4]
then every nilpotent element
e
of
\mathfrak{g}
has an associated cocharacter, and any two such are conjugate by an element of
Z_{G}(e)

[
Pre03b
]
.
(For the purposes of the present paper we only need to know that all primes are good in type
A
; only
2
is a bad prime for the other classical types.)
For a nilpotent Jordan block of order
m
in
G=\GL_{m}
, the standard choice of associated cocharacter is
\lambda:k^{\times}\rightarrow G
,
\lambda(t)={\rm diag}(t^{m-1},t^{m-3},\ldots,t^{-(m-1)})
.
For
G=\GL_{n}
and an arbitrary nilpotent matrix in Jordan normal form, we can construct an associated cocharacter by defining
\lambda
in each block separately.
We now have
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)=\sum_{i\geq 0}{\mathfrak{z}}(e;i)
where
{\mathfrak{z}}(e;i)=\mathfrak{g}(\lambda;i)\cap{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)
. The same holds in any reductive algebraic group
G
such that
(G,G)
is simply connected and
p
is good prime for
G

[
Jan04
, Proposition 5.8]
.
The subalgebra
{\mathfrak{z}}(e;0)
is called the
reductive part
of
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)
.
(It is the Lie algebra of the reductive group
Z_{G}(e)\cap Z_{G}(\lambda(k^{\times}))
.)

To any nilpotent orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
in a classical Lie algebra, one associates a partition
[m^{a_{m}},\ldots,1^{a_{1}}]
given by the sizes of the Jordan blocks of an element of
{\mathcal{O}}
.
Let
e\in{\mathcal{O}}
.
It is now well-known (see
[
Pre03b
]
for a unified proof) that when
G
is a reductive group satisfying the
standard hypotheses

[
Jan04
, 2.9]
, the set of nilpotent orbits as well as the data on dimensions and reductive parts are the same as for the corresponding complex simple Lie algebra, and
\Lie(Z_{G}(e))={\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)
for any nilpotent element
e
.
(The standard hypotheses are always satisfied for
G=\GL_{n}
.)
In particular, if
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
then
{\mathfrak{z}}(e;0)
is isomorphic to
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathfrak{gl}_{a_{i}}
and
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+\ldots+a_{m})^{2}
.
(In general, the dimension of the centralizer is equal to
\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda;0)+\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda;1)
.)
For later reference we will be interested in the subset
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)\subseteq{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)
of elements
y
such that
ke+ky\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be a non-zero nilpotent orbit in the classical Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and let
e\in{\mathcal{O}}
have associated partition
[m^{a_{m}},\ldots,1^{a_{1}}]
.
Suppose the characteristic of
k
is either zero or strictly greater than
m
.
Then
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)\subseteq\sum_{i\geq 1}{\mathfrak{z}}(e;i)
.

Proof.

In type
D
we can replace the special orthogonal group
\SO_{2n}
by the full orthogonal group
{\rm O}_{2n}
.
This ensures that the condition
ke+ky\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}}
is equivalent to the set of rank conditions:
{\rm rank}(\xi e+\eta y)^{r}\leq{\rm rank}\,e^{r}
for all
r\geq 1
and all
\xi,\eta\in k
.
Hence we can assume that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
(since any classical Lie algebra has a standard embedding in some
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, and an associated cocharacter
\lambda
for
e
in
\mathfrak{g}
is also an associated cocharacter for
e
in
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
).
Let
y=y_{0}+y_{1}+\ldots\in{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
where
y_{i}\in{\mathfrak{z}}(e;i)
.
Then any scalar multiple of
y
is in
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
, and hence (applying
\Ad\lambda(t)
to
(e,t^{2}y)
and scaling by
t^{-2}
) we have
ke+k(y_{0}+ty_{1}+\ldots)\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}}
for any
t\in k^{\times}
.
In other words,
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
is
\Ad\lambda(k^{\times})
-stable.
The various rank conditions can be expressed via various determinants of submatrices of
(\xi e+\eta y)^{r}
, and therefore
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
is also a Zariski closed subset of
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)
.
Hence
y_{0}\in{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
.
We wish to show that
y_{0}=0
.

We recall that
{\mathfrak{z}}(e;0)\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{a_{m}}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{a_{1}}
.
After conjugating if necessary, we may assume that

e=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{J_{m}}&&&\\
&\widetilde{J_{m-1}}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\widetilde{J_{1}}\end{bmatrix}\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\widetilde{J_{i}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&I_{a_{i}}\\
&0&\ddots\\
&&\ddots&I_{a_{i}}\\
&&&0\end{array}\right]\in\mathfrak{gl}_{ia_{i}}\,\mathrm{for}\,1\leq i\leq m.

Then the reductive part of the centralizer is the set of all matrices of the form:

\begin{bmatrix}\Delta_{m}(A_{m})&&&\\
&\Delta_{m-1}(A_{m-1})&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\Delta_{1}(A_{1})\end{bmatrix}\quad\mathrm{where}\;A_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{a_{i}}\;\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{j}(B)=\begin{bmatrix}B&&&\\
&B&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&B\end{bmatrix}.

It is now clear that
y_{0}
of the above form belongs to
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
if and only if each
\Delta_{i}(A_{i})
belongs to
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{ia_{i}}}^{\prime}(\widetilde{J}_{i})
.
To see that we must have
A_{i}=0
, we simply compute
(\Delta_{i}(A_{i})+\widetilde{J}_{i})^{i}
, noting that the submatrix in the top right-hand corner is
iA_{i}
.
By our assumption on the characteristic, the requirement
iA_{i}=0
implies
A_{i}=0
.

Remark 2.2
.

a) Continuing the argument, we can be more precise: if
y=y_{0}+y_{1}+\ldots
is in
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
, then
y_{0}=0
and
y_{1}\in{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
.
(These are necessary but not in general sufficient conditions.)

b) The condition
p>m
is necessary, as the following example
[
Pre03a
, Rk. 3.1(3)]
shows.
Let
e\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2p}
be nilpotent with associated partition
[p,p]
.
Then
G\cdot e={\mathcal{O}}
is the (unique) maximal orbit in
{\mathcal{N}}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2p})
, that is,
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
is the set of all
2p\times 2p
matrices with
p
-th power zero.
In this case the reductive part of the centralizer of
e
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
.
Letting
e_{0}\in{\mathfrak{z}}(e;0)
be a non-zero nilpotent, we see that
e_{0}^{[p]}=0
.
By standard facts about the
p
-operation on a restricted Lie algebra, we have
(ae_{0}+be)^{[p]}=a^{p}e_{0}^{[p]}+b^{p}e^{[p]}=0
, whence
ae_{0}+be\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
for any
a,b\in k
.
Thus
e_{0}\in\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
.

Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
with unipotent radical
U
, and let
{\mathfrak{b}}
, resp.
{\mathfrak{u}}
be the Lie algebra of
B
, resp.
U
.
Given a nilpotent orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
, the intersection
{\mathcal{O}}\cap{\mathfrak{u}}
is an
orbital variety
.
The following fact will be very useful:

-
{\mathcal{O}}\cap{\mathfrak{u}}
is equidimensional of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathcal{O}}
.

(See
[
Jan04
, Thm. 1, 10.6 and Thm., 10.11]
. The proof in
[
Jan04
]
applies in characteristic zero or good positive characteristic, which suffices for our purposes.)
We now apply this to prove a useful fact about the dimensions of certain subsets of centralizers of nilpotent elements.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a classical Lie algebra, let
{\mathcal{O}}
be a nilpotent orbit with associated partition
[m^{a_{m}},\ldots,1^{a_{1}}]
and let
e\in{\mathcal{O}}
.
Suppose the characteristic of
k
is either zero or greater than
m
.
Then
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
,
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)\subseteq\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}(\lambda;i)
, which is the nilradical of a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and is therefore contained in the nilradical
{\mathfrak{u}}
of a Borel.
Thus

{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}}\cap{\mathfrak{u}}=\bigcup_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathfrak{u}}

which is a finite union of locally closed subsets of dimension at most
\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathcal{O}}
, so we are done.
∎

For clarity we recall our standing assumption that
k
is not of characteristic 2.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G=\GL_{n}
and let
e\in{\mathcal{N}}
be a square zero matrix of rank
s
.
Let
t=n-2s
.
Then
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)=s(s+t)
.

Proof.

By standard results on dimensions of nilpotent orbits, the dimension of the orbit of
e
is
(2s+t)^{2}-(s+t)^{2}-s^{2}=2s^{2}+2st
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.3
we have
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)\leq s(s+t)
.
To show that we have equality, it only remains to exhibit a closed subset of
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
of dimension
s(s+t)
.
We can assume after conjugation that

(1)

e=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&I_{s}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix};\;\;\;\;\mbox{then}\;\;\;{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)\supseteq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}0&y&w\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}:y\in M_{s\times t},w\in\mathfrak{gl}_{s}\right\}

which has dimension
s(s+t)
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We note that the subset on the right-hand side of (
1
) is the nilradical of a maximal parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted
{\mathfrak{u}}_{s,s+t}
and identified in
[
CFP15
]
as a maximal commutative nil subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
It was proved in
[
CFP15
]
that if
n=2m
(resp.
n=2m+1
) then up to conjugacy
{\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m}
is the unique, resp.
{\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m+1},{\mathfrak{u}}_{m+1,m}
are the only, commutative nil subalgebra(s) of
\mathfrak{g}
of maximal dimension.

2.2.
Involutions of reductive groups

In this subsection we will state some standard results about involutions of (Lie algebras of) reductive groups.
These were established in characteristic zero in
[
KR71
]
, and in odd positive characteristic (under mild hypotheses) by the first author in
[
Lev07
]
.
Let
G
be reductive and let
\theta:G\rightarrow G
be an automorphism of order 2.
Then
d\theta
is an involution of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{k}}
, resp.
{\mathfrak{p}}
denote the
(+1)
, resp.
(-1)
eigenspace for
d\theta
on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
is stable under the adjoint action of the identity component of the fixed point subgroup
K=(G^{\theta})^{\circ}
; moreover,
\Lie(K)={\mathfrak{k}}
.

In the next section we will require some facts about the orbits of
K
on
{\mathfrak{p}}
, summarized in the following proposition and established in
[
KR71
, Prop. 16, Thm. 1, Prop. 5 and Thm. 9]
and
[
Lev07
, Thm. 2.11, Cor. 2.10, Lemma 4.1, Thm. 4.9 and Thm. 5.1]
.
We recall that a subspace of
{\mathfrak{p}}
which is maximal among the commutative subspaces consisting of semisimple elements is called a
Cartan subspace
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
G
,
\theta
,
K
,
{\mathfrak{k}}
,
{\mathfrak{p}}
be as above, and assume that the characteristic of the ground field is either zero or odd and good for
G
.

a) The semisimple elements are dense in
{\mathfrak{p}}
, and the
K
-orbit of
x\in{\mathfrak{p}}
is closed if and only if
x
is semisimple.
Any semisimple element of
{\mathfrak{p}}
is contained in a Cartan subspace, and any two Cartan subspaces of
{\mathfrak{p}}
are
K
-conjugate.

b) In positive characteristic, assume further that G is separably isogenous to a group satisfying the standard hypotheses.
For any
x\in{\mathfrak{p}}
, the intersection
(G\cdot x)\cap{\mathfrak{p}}
consists of finitely many
K
-orbits, each of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\dim G\cdot x
.

3.
Technical lemmas

This section contains estimates of the dimensions of various varieties which are related to the nilpotent commuting variety
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

\mathcal{U}_{s,t}=\{(y,z)\in M_{s\times t}\times M_{t\times s}:yz=0,zy=0\}.

Then
\dim\mathcal{U}_{s,t}=st
.

Proof.

Conjugation by the diagonal matrix
g=\begin{bmatrix}I_{s}&0\\
0&-I_{t}\end{bmatrix}
is an involutive automorphism of
\GL_{s+t}
. The fixed point subgroup for this automorphism is
\GL_{s}\times\GL_{t}
and the
(-1)
-eigenspace in
\mathfrak{gl}_{s+t}
is the set of all matrices of the form
M=\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
z&0\end{bmatrix}
where
y\in M_{s\times t}
and
z\in M_{t\times s}
. The conditions
yz=0,zy=0
are equivalent to
M^{2}=0
, so
\mathcal{U}={\mathcal{U}}_{s,t}
is isomorphic to the variety of all square zero matrices in the
(-1)
eigenspace
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
m=\min\{s,t\}
. The conditions
yz=0,zy=0
are equivalent also to
\mathrm{im}\,z\subseteq\ker y,\mathrm{im}\,y\subseteq\ker z
, which imply
\mathrm{rank}(M)=\mathrm{rank}(y)+\mathrm{rank}(z)\leq m
. The variety
\mathcal{U}
is therefore a subset of the closure of the nilpotent
\GL_{s+t}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
corresponding to the partition
[2^{m},1^{s+t-2m}]
.
There are finitely many
\GL_{s+t}
-orbits in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, and by Proposition
2.6
each such orbit
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
intersects
\mathfrak{p}
in finitely many
\GL_{s}\times\GL_{t}
-orbits, each of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. Therefore
\dim\mathcal{U}\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathcal{O}
. A short calculation shows that
\dim\mathcal{O}=2st
, hence
\dim\mathcal{U}\leq st
. To show the equality observe that
\{(y,0):y\in M_{s\times t}\}
is a subvariety of
\mathcal{U}
of dimension
st
.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Using results on orbit closures in symmetric spaces one can show that the variety
\mathcal{U}_{s,t}
is equidimensional.
Its irreducible components are

\{(y,z)\in\mathcal{U}_{s,t}:\rank(y)\leq m,\rank(z)\leq\min\{s,t\}-m\}

for
m=0,\ldots,\min\{s,t\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
r
be a positive integer,
s,t
non-negative integers, and let

\mathcal{Y}_{r,s,t}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in M_{s\times t}^{r}\times M_{t\times s}^{r}:y_{i}z_{j}=0,1\leq i,j\leq r\}.

Then
\dim\mathcal{Y}_{r,s,t}\leq rst+\lfloor\frac{t^{2}}{4}\rfloor
.

Proof.

We note that
\GL_{t}
acts on
{\mathcal{Y}}={\mathcal{Y}}_{r,s,t}
by

g\cdot(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})=(y_{1}g^{-1},\ldots,y_{r}g^{-1},gz_{1},\ldots,gz_{r}).

Let
(y_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathcal{Y}}
and let
W
be the subspace of
k^{t}
spanned by the columns of the
z_{i}
.
Then
z_{i}\in W\otimes(k^{s})^{T}
and
y_{i}\in k^{s}\otimes W^{\perp}
, where
W^{\perp}\subseteq(k^{t})^{T}
is the subspace of all linear forms which kill
W
.
(Here we identify
M_{s\times t}
with
k^{s}\otimes(k^{t})^{T}
, and we identify
(k^{t})^{T}
with
(k^{t})^{*}
via left multiplication.)
For a fixed
W
of dimension
m
, the space of such tuples is therefore of dimension
rsm+rs(t-m)=rst
.
Now any two subspaces of
k^{t}
of dimension
m
are conjugate by the action of
\GL_{t}
, so fixing a subspace
W_{m}
of dimension
m
for each
m\in\{0,\ldots,t\}
, we obtain:

{\mathcal{Y}}=\cup_{m=0}^{t}\GL_{t}\cdot\left((k^{s}\otimes W_{m}^{\perp})^{r}\oplus(W_{m}\otimes(k^{s})^{T})^{r}\right).

Since the stabilizer of
W_{m}
is a maximal parabolic subgroup of
\GL_{t}
of dimension
t^{2}-tm+m^{2}
, we therefore have
\dim{\mathcal{Y}}\leq rst+{\rm max}_{0\leq m\leq t}\,m(t-m)=rst+\lfloor\frac{t^{2}}{4}\rfloor
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
r
be a positive integer,
s,t
nonnegative integers and let

\mathcal{W}_{r,s,t}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in M_{s\times t}^{r}\times M_{t\times s}^{r}:y_{i}z_{j}=y_{j}z_{i},~1\leq i,j\leq r\}.

Then
\dim\mathcal{W}_{r,s,t}\leq(r+1)st+\lfloor\frac{t^{2}}{2}\rfloor
.
Moreover, for all
r\geq 2
we have

\dim\mathcal{W}_{r,s,1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}rs&\mbox{if $s\geq 2$ and $r\geq 3$,}\\
2s+1&\mbox{if $s\geq 2$ and $r=2$,}\\
r+1&\mbox{if $s=1$,}\\
0&\mbox{if $s=0$.}\end{array}\right.

In particular,
\dim\mathcal{W}_{r,s,1}\leq rs+1
for each
r\geq 2
and each
s\geq{0}
.

Proof.

The lemma clearly holds if
s=0
or
t=0
, so we assume that
s,t\geq 1
.
We start with the following observation. A short computation shows that the action of
\GL_{r}
on
M_{s\times t}^{r}\times M_{t\times s}^{r}
defined by

(a_{ij})\bullet(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{1i}y_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{ri}y_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{1i}z_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{ri}z_{i}\right)

stabilizes the variety
\mathcal{W}_{r,s,t}
.
Since
\GL_{r}
is connected, it also stabilizes each irreducible component of
\mathcal{W}_{r,s,t}
.
Therefore the subset
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{r,s,t}
of all
(2r)
-tuples
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathcal{W}_{r,s,t}
such that
the rank of
y_{1}
is not less than the rank of any linear combination of
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
(*) is dense.

We first examine the case
t=1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an irreducible component of
{\mathcal{W}_{r,s,1}}
.
If there exists a
(2r)
-tuple in
\mathcal{C}
with
y_{1}\neq 0
and
z_{1}\neq 0
, then the set of such elements is dense in
{\mathcal{C}}
and the relations
y_{1}z_{i}=y_{i}z_{1},2\leq i\leq r
imply that for each
2\leq i\leq r
there exists
\lambda_{i}\in k
such that
(y_{i},z_{i})=\lambda_{i}(y_{1},z_{1})
.
It follows that
{\mathcal{C}}
is the closure of the image of a morphism
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{s}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r-1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{r,s,1}
that is injective on the open dense subset
(\mathbb{A}^{s}\backslash\{0\})\times(\mathbb{A}^{s}\backslash\{0\})\times\mathbb{A}^{r-1}
, hence
\dim\mathcal{C}=2s+r-1
.
On the other hand, if
y_{1}=0
or
z_{1}=0
for each
(2r)
-tuple in
\mathcal{C}
, then by considering the
\GL_{r}
-action we see that we have either
y_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq r
or
z_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq r
in each
(2r)
-tuple in
\mathcal{C}
, yielding
\dim\mathcal{C}=rs
.
The description of the dimension of
{\mathcal{W}}_{r,s,1}
follows by considering the difference
rs-(2s+r-1)=(r-2)(s-1)-1
.

Now we prove the general statement by induction on
s
and
t
. We have already proved that
\dim\mathcal{W}_{r,1,1}=r+1
, therefore we assume that
s>1
or
t>1
and that
\dim\mathcal{W}_{\rho,\sigma,\tau}\leq(\rho+1)\sigma\tau+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}
for all positive integers
\rho
and nonnegative integers
\sigma
and
\tau
satisfying
\sigma\leq s
and
\tau<t
or
\sigma<s
and
\tau\leq t
.
For
0\leq m\leq\min\,\{s,t\}
let
{\mathcal{W}}_{(m)}
be the set of all tuples
(y_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathcal{W}}_{r,s,t}
with
{\rm rank}\,y_{1}=m
.
Clearly
{\mathcal{W}}_{(m)}
and the intersection
{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{(m)}={\mathcal{W}}_{(m)}\cap{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{r,s,t}
are quasi-affine.
Moreover,
{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{r,s,t}
is the disjoint union of the
{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{(m)}
and so it will suffice to prove that
\dim{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{(m)}\leq(r+1)st+\frac{t^{2}}{2}
for all
m
.

Using the theorem on the dimension of fibres for the projection

\displaystyle\pi_{r+1}:~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{(m)}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\to~~~~~M_{t\times s}

\displaystyle(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},

\displaystyle z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\mapsto z_{1},

one gets
\dim\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{(m)}\leq st+\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m}
where

\mathcal{Z}_{m}={\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{(m)}\cap\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{r}):y_{i}\in M_{s\times t},z_{i}\in M_{t\times s}\}.

(We remark that every irreducible component of
{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{(m)}
intersects non-trivially with
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}
, since if
(y_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{(m)}
then clearly
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},\xi z_{1},\ldots,\xi z_{r})\in{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}_{(m)}
for all
\xi\in k
.)

For any tuple
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathcal{Z}_{m}
there exist bases of
k^{s}
and
k^{t}
with respect to which

y_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{m}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right],

therefore
\mathcal{Z}_{m}=(\GL_{s}\times\GL_{t})\cdot\mathcal{V}_{m}
where
\mathcal{V}_{m}=\left\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathcal{Z}_{m};y_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{m}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\right\}
and the actions of
\GL_{s}\times\GL_{t}
on
M_{s\times t}
and on
M_{t\times s}
are respectively defined by
(g,h)\cdot y=gyh^{-1}
and
(g,h)\cdot z=hzg^{-1}
for all
(g,h)\in\GL_{s}\times\GL_{t},y\in M_{s\times t},z\in M_{t\times s}
. It follows that

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m}=\dim(\GL_{s}\times\GL_{t})\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{m}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]+\dim\mathcal{V}_{m}=m(s+t-m)+\dim\mathcal{V}_{m}.

We now examine
\mathcal{V}_{m}
.
Since
y_{1}z_{i}=0
for each
2\leq i\leq r
, we obtain

z_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
z_{i}^{\prime}&z_{i}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right]

for some
z_{i}^{\prime}\in M_{(t-m)\times m}
and
z_{i}^{\prime\prime}\in M_{(t-m)\times(s-m)}
for
2\leq i\leq r
. On the other hand, the condition (*) implies that for each
2\leq i\leq r
one has

y_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}y_{i}^{\prime}&y_{i}^{\prime\prime}\\
y_{i}^{\prime\prime\prime}&0\end{array}\right]

for some
y_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
,
y_{i}^{\prime\prime}\in M_{m\times(t-m)}
and
y_{i}^{\prime\prime\prime}\in M_{(s-m)\times m}
. Moreover, the conditions
y_{i}z_{j}=y_{j}z_{i}
yield
y_{i}^{\prime\prime}z_{j}^{\prime}=y_{j}^{\prime\prime}z_{i}^{\prime}
for all
2\leq i,j\leq r
, therefore

\mathcal{V}_{m}\subseteq\bigg\{\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{m}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}y_{2}^{\prime}&y_{2}^{\prime\prime}\\
y_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}&0\end{array}\right],\ldots,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}y_{r}^{\prime}&y_{r}^{\prime\prime}\\
y_{r}^{\prime\prime\prime}&0\end{array}\right],0,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
z_{2}^{\prime}&z_{2}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right],\ldots,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
z_{r}^{\prime}&z_{r}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right]\right):\\
\quad\quad y^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{m},y_{i}^{\prime\prime\prime}\in M_{(s-m)\times m},z_{i}^{\prime\prime}\in M_{(t-m)\times(s-m)},~2\leq i\leq r,(y_{2}^{\prime\prime},\ldots,y_{r}^{\prime\prime},z_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{r}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{r-1,m,t-m}\bigg\}.

Using the induction hypothesis we have

\dim\mathcal{V}_{m}\leq(r-1)(m^{2}+(s-m)m+(t-m)(s-m))+rm(t-m)+\frac{(t-m)^{2}}{2}{=(r-1)st+\frac{t^{2}-m^{2}}{2}},

so

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m}\leq(r-1)st+\frac{t^{2}-m^{2}}{2}+m(s+t-m)\leq rst+\frac{t^{2}}{2}

for each
m\leq\min\{s,t\}
. Hence, we obtain
\dim\mathcal{W}_{r,s,t}=\dim\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{r,s,t}\leq st+\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m}\leq(r+1)st+\frac{t^{2}}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
c,m,l
be non-negative integers such that
2m+l\leq c
.
Then the subset

{\mathcal{V}}_{c,m,l}=\{u\in\mathfrak{gl}_{c}:{\rm rank}(u)=c-m-l,{\rm rank}(u^{2})=c-2m-l\}

has dimension
c^{2}-(2m^{2}+2ml+l^{2})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
{\mathcal{V}}_{c,m,l}
is locally closed subset since the rank conditions
{\rm rank}(u)\leq a
and
{\rm rank}(u^{2})\leq b
are closed conditions for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Any
u\in{\mathcal{V}}_{c,m,l}
is
\GL_{c}
-conjugate to a matrix in Jordan normal form
\begin{bmatrix}u^{\prime}&0\\
0&u^{\prime\prime}\end{bmatrix}
where
u^{\prime\prime}
is invertible and
u^{\prime}
is nilpotent, with
(m+l)
Jordan blocks, exactly
l
of which are of order 1.
Given
\lambda=[\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{m}]
such that
\lambda_{m}>1
and
n_{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}+l\leq c
we choose a nilpotent
n_{\lambda}\times n_{\lambda}
matrix
u^{\prime}_{\lambda}
with Jordan blocks of sizes
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m},1,\ldots,1
.
Then the set

{\mathcal{V}}_{c,\lambda}:=\GL_{c}\cdot\left\{\begin{bmatrix}u^{\prime}_{\lambda}&0\\
0&u^{\prime\prime}\end{bmatrix}:u^{\prime\prime}\in\GL_{c-n_{\lambda}}\right\}

is Zariski constructible (and irreducible) and
{\mathcal{V}}_{c,m,l}
is the (disjoint) union of these subsets, taken over all relevant partitions
\lambda
.
To determine dimensions we note that
\GL_{c}\cdot{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}
is dense in
{\mathcal{V}}_{c,\lambda}
where

{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}u^{\prime}_{\lambda}&0\\
0&u^{\prime\prime}\end{bmatrix}:u^{\prime\prime}\in\GL_{c-n_{\lambda}}\;\mbox{diagonal with distinct eigenvalues}\;\right\}

and that any
\GL_{c}
-orbit in
{\mathcal{V}}_{c,\lambda}
has finite (possibly empty) intersection with
{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}
, by the uniqueness of the Jordan normal form.
The transpose of the partition
[\lambda,1^{l}]
is
[m+l,m,\ldots]
, where there are non-zero terms after
m
if and only if
\lambda_{1}>2
.
Since
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{n_{\lambda}}}(u_{\lambda}^{\prime})
is the sum of the squares of the parts of
[\lambda,1^{l}]^{T}
, it follows that

\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{c,\lambda}=c^{2}-\dim N_{\GL_{c}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda})+(c-n_{\lambda})=c^{2}-\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{n_{\lambda}}}(u^{\prime}_{\lambda})=c^{2}-((m+l)^{2}+m^{2}+\ldots)

which is maximal precisely when
\lambda=[2^{m}]
.
The statement on the dimension follows.
∎

The following lemma plays a crucial role in establishing the dimension of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
, see the proof of Lemma
4.7
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
r>5
be a positive integer and
a,b,c
nonnegative integers. Let

\mathcal{Z}_{r,a,b,c}
be the subvariety of
M_{a\times c}^{r}\times M_{b\times c}^{r}\times M_{c\times a}^{r}\times M_{c\times b}^{r}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{c}^{r}
consisting of all tuples

(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r},w_{1},\ldots,w_{r},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})

satisfying the following equations:

y_{i}w_{j}=y_{j}w_{i},z_{i}v_{j}=0,y_{i}u_{j}=y_{j}u_{i},z_{i}u_{j}=z_{j}u_{i},u_{i}w_{j}=u_{j}w_{i},u_{i}v_{j}=u_{j}v_{i}

for each
1\leq i,j\leq r
. Then:

a) We have
\dim\mathcal{Z}_{r,a,b,0}=0
,
\dim{\mathcal{Z}}_{r,1,0,1}=r+2
and
\dim{\mathcal{Z}}_{r,0,0,1}=r
; if
(a,b)\not\in\{(1,0),(0,0)\}
then
\dim\mathcal{Z}_{r,a,b,1}\leq r(a+b)+1
.

b) If
c\geq 2
then

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{r,a,b,c}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{a^{2}}{11}+c^{2}+2ac+2bc+(r-1)(c^{2}+\frac{(a+b)^{2}}{2})&\mbox{if $c\geq a+b$,}\\
\frac{a^{2}}{11}+c^{2}+2ac+2bc+(r-1)(\frac{c^{2}}{2}+(a+b)c)&\mbox{if $c\leq a+b$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

As in Lemma
3.4
, the irreducible components of
\mathcal{Z}={\mathcal{Z}}_{r,a,b,c}
are invariant under the action of
\GL_{r}
, hence the subset
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{Z}}
consisting of all tuples
(y_{1},\ldots,u_{r})\in\mathcal{Z}
such that the rank of
u_{1}
is not less than the rank of any linear combination of
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
and the rank of
u_{1}^{2}
is not less than the rank of the square of any linear combination of
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}

is dense.
In particular,
\dim\mathcal{Z}=\dim\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
.

As the case
c=0
is trivial, we first consider the case
c=1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be any component of
\mathcal{Z}
. The condition
z_{i}v_{j}=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq r
implies
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{r}=0
or
v_{1}=\cdots=v_{r}=0
. By symmetry we can assume that the latter holds on
\mathcal{C}
. If there exists a
(5r)
-tuple in
\mathcal{C}
such that
u_{i}\neq 0
for some
i
, then
y_{j}=\frac{u_{j}}{u_{i}}y_{i}
,
z_{j}=\frac{u_{j}}{u_{i}}z_{i}
and
w_{j}=\frac{u_{j}}{u_{i}}w_{i}
for
1\leq j\leq r
, which yields
\dim\mathcal{C}=r+2a+b
. On the other hand, if
u_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
and all tuples in
\mathcal{C}
, then Lemma
3.4
implies
\dim\mathcal{C}\leq r(a+b)+1
. This proves (a).

In the rest of the proof we assume
c\geq 2
.
For all nonnegative integers
m
and
l
with
2m+l\leq c
we define
\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}
as the subset of
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
consisting of all tuples
(y_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
satisfying
u_{1}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{c,m,l}
. (See Lemma
3.5
.) This is clearly a locally closed set and the union of all
\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}
is
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
. Moreover, if
(y_{1},\ldots,u_{r})\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}
, then the
\GL_{r}
-action gives us
u_{i}\in\overline{\mathcal{V}_{c,m,l}}
for
i=2,\ldots,r
.
We consider the projection

\pi\colon\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}\to M_{a\times c}\times M_{b\times c}\times M_{c\times a}\times M_{c\times b}\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{2(a+b)c}

defined by

\pi(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r},w_{1},\ldots,w_{r},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})=(y_{1},z_{1},w_{1},v_{1}).

The preimage
\pi^{-1}(0,0,0,0)
intersects every irreducible component of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m,l}
, since, for any
t\in k
and any
(y_{1},\ldots,u_{r})\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{m,l}
, we clearly have
(ty_{1},\ldots,tv_{r},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{m,l}
.
Hence
\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}\leq 2ac+2bc+\dim\pi^{-1}(0,0,0,0)
.
Next, we consider the projection
\pi^{\prime}\colon\pi^{-1}(0,0,0,0)\to\overline{\mathcal{V}_{c,m,l}}^{r-1}
defined by

\pi^{\prime}(0,y_{2},\ldots,y_{r},0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{r},0,w_{2},\ldots,w_{r},0,v_{2},\ldots,v_{r},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})=(u_{2},\ldots,u_{r}).

As above, we observe that
\pi^{-1}(0,0,0,0)
is invariant under multiplying the last
r-1
components by an arbitrary scalar, hence
\pi^{\prime-1}(0,\ldots,0)
intersects every irreducible component of
\pi^{-1}(0,0,0,0)
. Consequently,
\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}\leq 2ac+2bc+(r-1)(c^{2}-2m^{2}-2ml-l^{2})+\dim\mathcal{W}_{m,l}
where

\mathcal{W}_{m,l}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&(y_{2},..,y_{r},z_{2},..,z_{r},w_{2},..,w_{r},v_{2},..,v_{r},u_{1})\in M_{a\times c}^{r-1}\times M_{b\times c}^{r-1}\times M_{c\times a}^{r-1}\times M_{c\times b}^{r-1}\times\mathcal{V}_{c,m,l}:\\
&y_{i}u_{1}=0,z_{i}u_{1}=0,u_{1}w_{i}=0,u_{1}v_{i}=0,y_{i}w_{j}=y_{j}w_{i},z_{i}v_{j}=0,2\leq i,j\leq r.\end{array}\right\}

For each tuple
(y_{2},\ldots,y_{r},z_{2},\ldots,z_{r},w_{2},\ldots,w_{r},v_{2},\ldots,v_{r},u_{1})\in\mathcal{W}_{m,l}
, with respect to a basis of
k^{c}
, we can assume that there exists nonnegative integer
t\leq c-2m-l
such that

u_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}u_{1}^{\prime}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&u_{1}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right]

for some invertible
t\times t
matrix
u_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and some nilpotent
(c-t-l)\times(c-t-l)
matrix
u_{1}^{\prime}
in the Jordan canonical form which has
m
Jordan blocks, all of them of order more than 1. Then

y_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{i}^{\prime}&y_{i}^{\prime\prime}&0\end{array}\right],\quad z_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}z_{i}^{\prime}&z_{i}^{\prime\prime}&0\end{array}\right],\quad w_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{i}^{\prime}\\
w_{i}^{\prime\prime}\\
0\end{array}\right],\quad v_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}v_{i}^{\prime}\\
v_{i}^{\prime\prime}\\
0\end{array}\right]

for
2\leq i\leq r
where
y_{i}^{\prime}\in M_{a\times(c-t-l)},y_{i}^{\prime\prime}\in M_{a\times l},z_{i}^{\prime}\in M_{b\times(c-t-l)},z_{i}^{\prime\prime}\in M_{b\times l},w_{i}^{\prime}\in M_{(c-t-l)\times a},w_{i}^{\prime\prime}\in M_{l\times a},v_{i}^{\prime}\in M_{(c-t-l)\times b},v_{i}^{\prime\prime}\in M_{l\times b}
, the transposes of the rows of the matrices
y_{i}^{\prime}
and
z_{i}^{\prime}
belong to the
m
-dimensional kernel of
u_{1}^{\prime T}
and the columns of the matrices
w_{i}^{\prime}
and
v_{i}^{\prime}
belong to the
m
-dimensional kernel of
u_{1}^{\prime}
.
Therefore
y_{i}^{\prime}w_{j}^{\prime}=0
and
z_{i}^{\prime}v_{j}^{\prime}=0
for all
2\leq i,j\leq r
.
The conditions
y_{i}w_{j}=y_{j}w_{i}
and
z_{i}v_{j}=0
are then equivalent to
y_{i}^{\prime\prime}w_{j}^{\prime\prime}=y_{j}^{\prime\prime}w_{i}^{\prime\prime}
and
z_{i}^{\prime\prime}v_{j}^{\prime\prime}=0
for
2\leq i,j\leq r
.
Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.5
now imply

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{W}_{m,l}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\mathcal{V}_{c,m,l}+2(r-1)(a+b)m+ral+\left\lfloor\frac{l^{2}}{2}\right\rfloor+(r-1)bl+\left\lfloor\frac{l^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=c^{2}-2m^{2}-2ml+\left\lfloor\frac{l^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-\left\lceil\frac{l^{2}}{2}\right\rceil+al+(r-1)(a+b)(2m+l),

therefore

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}\leq al+c^{2}+2ac+2bc-2m^{2}-2ml+\left\lfloor\frac{l^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-\left\lceil\frac{l^{2}}{2}\right\rceil+(r-1)(c^{2}-2m^{2}-2ml-l^{2}+(a+b)(2m+l)).

Since
r\geq 6
, we clearly have

\left\lfloor\frac{l^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{(2r-11)l^{2}}{4}=\frac{(r-1)l^{2}}{2}-\frac{9l^{2}}{4}.

Furthermore,
al-\left\lceil\frac{l^{2}}{2}\right\rceil-\frac{9l^{2}}{4}\leq al-\frac{11l^{2}}{4}\leq\frac{a^{2}}{11}
and therefore

al-\left\lceil\frac{l^{2}}{2}\right\rceil-\frac{9l^{2}}{4}+c^{2}+2ac+2bc-2m^{2}-2ml\leq\frac{a^{2}}{11}+c^{2}+2ac+2bc.

On the other hand, since
2m+l\leq c
, the expression

\displaystyle c^{2}-2m^{2}-2ml-\frac{l^{2}}{2}+(a+b)(2m+l)

\displaystyle=c^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(2m+l)^{2}+(a+b)(2m+l)

is maximal if
2m+l=\min\{a+b,c\}
, so

c^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(2m+l)^{2}+(a+b)(2m+l)\leq\begin{cases}c^{2}+\frac{(a+b)^{2}}{2}\quad&\mathrm{if~~}a+b\leq c,\\
\frac{c^{2}}{2}+(a+b)c&\mathrm{if~~}a+b\geq c.\end{cases}

It follows that

\dim\mathcal{Z^{\prime}}=\max_{m,l}\{\dim\mathcal{Z}_{m,l}\}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{a^{2}}{11}+c^{2}+2ac+2bc+(r-1)\left(c^{2}+\frac{(a+b)^{2}}{2}\right)&\text{~if~~}a+b\leq c,\\
\frac{a^{2}}{11}+c^{2}+2ac+2bc+(r-1)\left(\frac{c^{2}}{2}+(a+b)c\right)&\text{~if~~}a+b\geq c,\end{cases}

hence completing our proof.
∎

4.
Dimension of the commuting variety

In this section, we use the estimates established in the previous two sections to get upper bounds for
\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
.
When
r
is large enough, we are able to exactly describe the dimension of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
and the irreducible components of maximal dimension.
We assume from now on that the characteristic of
k
is not equal to 2 or 3.
We first explain our general strategy.

4.0.
General stategy

Although we focus in this paper on the group
\GL_{n}
, the present strategy is applicable to any reductive algebraic group.
Recall that
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
is the nilpotent cone of
\mathfrak{g}
.
For each element
e
in
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, set
C(e)=\overline{G\cdot(e,C_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})))}
.
Then we have for all
r\geq 2

C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))\>=\bigcup_{\mathcal{O}_{e}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})/G}C(e).

Since this union is finite, each irreducible component of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
is an irreducible component of some
C(e)
with
e\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Generalizing an idea in
[
Pre03a
, Prop. 2.1]
, there is an action of
\GL_{r}
on
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
defined by

(a_{ij})\bullet(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{1i}x_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{ri}x_{i}\right)

which stabilizes each irreducible component of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
.
Thus each irreducible component of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
is a subset of
C^{\prime}(e)=\overline{G\cdot(e,C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)))}
for some
e\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, where

C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e))=\left\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r-1})\in C_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})):e+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}ky_{i}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}_{e}}\right\}.

We earlier (before Lemma
2.1
) introduced the subset
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e)
of
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e)
consisting of all
y
such that
ke+ky\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}_{e}}
.
Clearly
C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e))\subseteq C_{r-1}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(e))
.
We can obtain an upper bound for
\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
by determining bounds for
\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e))
or
\dim C^{\prime}(e)
, for
e
belonging to each orbit in
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
be a square zero matrix and let
s=\rank(x)
and
t=n-2s
. Then for
r\geq 1
, we have

\dim C^{\prime}_{r}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))=rs(s+t).

Proof.

We have
C^{\prime}_{r}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\subseteq C_{r}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(x))
and therefore
\dim C^{\prime}_{r}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\leq r\dim{\mathfrak{z}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
.
By Lemma
2.4
we have
\dim C^{\prime}_{r}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\leq rs(s+t)
.
To obtain equality, write
x=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&I_{s}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}
as in the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
Then
{\mathfrak{u}}_{s,s+t}^{r}
(see Remark
2.5
)
is a subset of
C^{\prime}_{r}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))
of dimension
rs(s+t)
, hence we have
\dim C^{\prime}_{r}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\geq rs(s+t)
too.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
be a square zero matrix and let
s=\rank(x)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
and
t=n-2s
. Then
\dim C^{\prime}(x)=(r+1)s(s+t)
. In particular, if we assume further that
\rank(x)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, then

\dim C^{\prime}(x)=(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

Specifically, if
n=2m
then
C^{\prime}(x)=G\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m}^{r}
is irreducible; if
n=2m+1
then
C^{\prime}(x)=G\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}_{m+1,m}^{r}\cup G\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m+1}^{r}
is equidimensional.

Proof.

The first statement follows immediately from Proposition
4.1
and the equality

(2)

\dim C^{\prime}(x)=n^{2}-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)+\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)).

The statements about the maximal rank case can be deduced immediately from the fact that, if
x
is in the form indicated in the proof of Prop.
4.1
, then
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}(x)
equals
{\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m}
, resp.
{\mathfrak{u}}_{m+1,m}\cup{\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m+1}
if
n=2m
, resp,
2m+1
. Note that each set
G\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}_{l,n-l}^{r}
is indeed closed, since it is defined by equations
x_{i}x_{j}=0
,
1\leq i,j\leq r
, and the closed conditions that the rows of all
x_{i}
span at most an
(n-l)
-dimensional space and their columns span at most an
l
-dimensional space.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
, we have
\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))\leq(r+1)\frac{n^{2}-1}{3}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
be a nilpotent matrix with associated partition
[m^{a_{m}},\ldots,2^{a_{2}},1^{a_{1}}]
, where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\geq 0
and
a_{m}>0
.
It suffices to show that

\dim C^{\prime}(x)\leq(r+1)\frac{n^{2}-1}{3}
for each such
x
,
which is equivalent to

\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\leq(r+1)\frac{n^{2}-1}{3}-\dim GL_{n}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)=\frac{r-2}{3}n^{2}+\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\frac{r+1}{3}.

If
m\leq 2
, then the inequality follows from Corollary
4.2
.
We now assume
m\geq 3
and proceed by induction on
m
.
Write
x
in the form indicated in the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
Consider a tuple
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r-1})
in
C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))
, where each
y_{l}
, for
1\leq l\leq r-1
, is of the form

y_{l}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}y_{mm}^{(l)}&y_{m,m-1}^{(l)}&\cdots&y_{m1}^{(l)}\\
y_{m-1,m}^{(l)}&y_{m-1,m-1}^{(l)}&\cdots&y_{m-1,1}^{(l)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
y_{1m}^{(l)}&y_{1,m-1}^{(l)}&\cdots&y_{11}^{(l)}\end{array}\right]\quad\mathrm{where}\quad y_{ii}^{(l)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}y_{ii1}^{(l)}&y_{ii2}^{(l)}&\cdots&y_{iii}^{(l)}\\
0&y_{ii1}^{(l)}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&y_{ii2}^{(l)}\\
0&\cdots&0&y_{ii1}^{(l)}\end{array}\right]

for some matrices
y_{ii1}^{(l)},\ldots,y_{iii}^{(l)}
of appropriate size, and for
i\neq j
the matrix
y_{ij}^{(l)}
has the same form with some additional block rows of zeros at the end or some additional block columns of zeros at the beginning (see e.g.
[
NŠ14
, Proposition 14]
).

Let

x^{\prime}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widetilde{J_{m-1}}\\
&\ddots\\
&&\widetilde{J_{1}}\end{array}\right],

and
n^{\prime}=n-ma_{m}
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n^{\prime}}
.
Assume first that
n^{\prime}=0
, i.e.
n=ma_{m}
. Then
\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)=ma_{m}^{2}
. Since
y_{l}
is nilpotent for each
l
,
y_{mm1}^{(l)}
is nilpotent for each
l
. Recall that
\dim\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{a_{m}})=a_{m}^{2}-a_{m}
. Then we have

\dim C_{r-1}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\leq(r-1)(ma_{m}^{2}-a_{m})\leq(r-2)\frac{m}{3}ma_{m}^{2}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-(r-1)\leq\frac{r-2}{3}n^{2}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\frac{r+1}{3}

for all
r\geq 2
,
m\geq 3
and
a_{m}\geq 1
.

Assume now that
n^{\prime}>0
.
By the induction hypothesis we have

\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))\leq\frac{r-2}{3}{n^{\prime}}^{2}+\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})-\frac{r+1}{3}.

We note that
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=ma_{m}^{2}+2n^{\prime}a_{m}=a_{m}(2n-ma_{m})
.
Applying Krull’s Principal ideal theorem to the coordinate ring of any irreducible component of
C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))
and its ideal generated by all entries of
y_{mi}^{(l)}
and
y_{im}^{(l)}
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
1\leq l\leq r-1
, we have

\displaystyle\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))

\displaystyle\leq(r-1)(\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))+\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq(r-2)\left(\frac{{n^{\prime}}^{2}}{3}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\right)+\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\frac{r+1}{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{r-2}{3}\left((n-ma_{m})^{2}+3a_{m}(2n-ma_{m})\right)+\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\frac{r+1}{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{r-2}{3}n^{2}+\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\frac{r-2}{3}(m-3)a_{m}(2n-ma_{m})-\frac{r+1}{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r-2}{3}n^{2}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\frac{r+1}{3}

for all
m\geq 3
, as required.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In the block form indicated in the above proof, the component of
y_{l}
in the reductive part of the centralizer (see §
2
) is given by the submatrices
y_{ii1}^{(l)}
.
Since
y_{l}
is nilpotent then in fact
y_{mm1}^{(l)}
is nilpotent for
1\leq l\leq r-1
.
Since
\dim\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{a_{m}})=a_{m}^{2}-a_{m}
,
we have the stronger inequality:

\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))-\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))\leq(r-1)(\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))-(r-1)a_{m}.

(Although we used this fact in the proof only in the case
n^{\prime}=0
, it will be needed to establish the stronger inequality in our main Theorem
4.8
.)
Note that if the characteristic of the ground field is zero or greater than
m
then in fact
y_{mm1}^{(l)}=0
by Lemma
2.1
.

Corollary 4.5
.

For all
n\geq 3
and
r\geq 5
, we have
\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\leq(r+1)\frac{n^{2}-1}{3}+r
.

Proof.

For the duration of this proof, denote
{\mathcal{N}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{a})
by
{\mathcal{N}}_{a}
.
Let
r\geq 5
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be an irreducible component of
C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.
For each partition
\lambda=[\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{m}]
of
n
let
X_{\lambda}
denote the set of elements of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
having
m
distinct eigenvalues of multiplicities
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
.
The subset
X_{\lambda}
is Zariski constructible and
\GL_{n}
-stable, since it is the image of a morphism
\GL_{n}\times Y\rightarrow{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
, where
Y
is the open subset of
m
-tuples
((a_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,(a_{m},x_{m}))\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}(k\times{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda_{i}})
such that the
a_{i}
are distinct.
As there are finitely many of the subsets
X_{\lambda}
, and since
{\mathcal{C}}
is stable under the dot action of
\GL_{r}
(see the proof of Lemma
3.4
), it follows that for some
\lambda
,
X_{\lambda}^{r}
contains a non-empty open subset
{\mathcal{U}}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Here
m
is the maximum number of distinct eigenvalues of an element of an
r
-tuple belonging to
{\mathcal{C}}
.
By the dot action, this is also the maximum number of distinct eigenvalues of any linear combination of the elements of an
r
-tuple belonging to
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Thus if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in{\mathcal{U}}
then the eigenspace decomposition of
k^{n}
for the semisimple part of
x_{1}
must be the same as for the semisimple parts of
x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}
.
We therefore have

\mathcal{C}=\overline{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\overline{GL_{n}\cdot(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{\lambda_{1}}+kI_{\lambda_{1}})\times\cdots\times C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{\lambda_{m}}+kI_{\lambda_{m}}))}.

Thus

(3)

\dim\mathcal{C}\leq n^{2}-\dim N_{\GL_{n}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\lambda_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{gl}_{\lambda_{m}})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{\lambda_{i}})+r\right).

By Lemma
4.3
we therefore have to show that

n^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(r+1)\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}{3}+(m-1)r\leq(r+1)\frac{n^{2}-1}{3}.

Rearranging and using
n=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}
we get an equivalent inequality

(4)

\frac{2r-1}{3}(m-1)\leq\frac{2(r-2)}{3}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}.

The right hand-side of the above inequality is at least
\frac{r-2}{3}m(m-1)
, so it suffices to show
(2r-1)(m-1)\leq(r-2)m(m-1)
. If
m\geq 3
and
r\geq 5
, then
(r-2)m-(2r-1)=(m-2)r+1-2m\geq 5(m-2)+1-2m=3(m-3)\geq 0
. The inequality (
4
) therefore holds for
r\geq 5
if
m=1
or
m\geq 3
. On the other hand, if
m=2
, then it is equivalent to
2r-1\leq 2(r-2)\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
, which holds for
r\geq 4
, as
n\geq 3
and consequently
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\geq 2
.

∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Assume
{\rm char}\,k\neq 2,3
.
For each

and

\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))=(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor

and the irreducible component(s) of maximal dimension is, resp. are:

if

, resp.

G\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}_{m+1,m}^{r}
if

Proof.

By Cor.
4.2

\dim C_{r}({\mathcal{N}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))\geq(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor
.
Suppose
x\in{\mathcal{N}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
has associated partition
[m^{a_{m}},\ldots,1^{a_{1}}]
where
a_{m}>0
.
By Lemma
4.7
and Cor.
4.2
, it will suffice to show that
\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))<(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor-n^{2}+\dim{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
whenever
m>4
.
Write
x
in the form indicated in Lemma
2.1
, let
n^{\prime}=n-ma_{m}
, let
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n^{\prime}}
and let
x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
be the submatrix of
x
as indicated in the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
Suppose by induction that
\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))\leq(r+1)\frac{{n^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}-n^{\prime 2}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})
.
Recall from the proof of Lemma
4.3
that
\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{g^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=a_{m}(2n-ma_{m})
and
n^{2}-{n^{\prime}}^{2}=ma_{m}(2n-ma_{m})
.
By Remark
4.4
we have:

\displaystyle\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))+(r-1)(\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))-(r-1)a_{m}

\displaystyle\leq(r+1)\frac{{n^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}-{n^{\prime}}^{2}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})+(r-1)(\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}))-(r-1)a_{m}

\displaystyle=(r+1)\frac{n^{2}}{4}-n^{2}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-a_{m}(2n-ma_{m})\left(\frac{r-3}{4}m-(r-2)\right)-(r-1)a_{m}.

Since
\frac{r-3}{4}m-(r-2)\geq\frac{r-7}{4}\geq 0
, it follows that
\dim C^{\prime}_{r-1}({\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\leq(r+1)\frac{n^{2}}{4}-n^{2}+\dim\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)-(r-1)a_{m}
, and therefore

.
As

Corollary 4.9
.

Assume
{\rm char}\,k\neq 2,3
,
n\geq 4
and
r\geq 7
.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}
be a nil subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
of maximal dimension.
Then

\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor+r&\mbox{if $(n,r)\neq(4,7)$,}\\
40&\mbox{if $(n,r)=(4,7)$.}\end{array}\right.

If
(n,r)\neq(4,7)
then
G\cdot(kI_{n}+{\mathfrak{m}})^{r}
is an irreducible component of maximal dimension; if
(n,r)\in\{(4,7),(5,7),(4,8)\}
then the generic component
\overline{\GL_{n}\cdot\mathfrak{h}^{r}}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, is an irreducible component of maximal dimension.

Proof.

Clearly
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))+(kI_{n})^{r}\subseteq C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, therefore

\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\geq\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))+r=(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+r

for all
n\geq 4
and
r\geq 7
.

Conversely, arguing as in Corollary
4.5
, and using Theorem
4.8
instead of Lemma
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}-\dim N_{GL_{n}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\lambda_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{gl}_{\lambda_{m}})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\lambda_{i}}))+r\right)

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}+mr+\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\lambda^{2}_{i}-2\lambda_{i}+1+r(\lambda_{i}-1))+\sum_{i=l+1}^{m}(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor

for any component
\mathcal{C}
of
C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, where
\lambda_{i}\leq 3
for
i\leq l
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 4
for
i>l
.
If
l=m
, i.e.
\lambda_{i}\leq 3
for all
i
,
then all the varieties
C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\lambda_{i}})
are irreducible by
[
Gur92
]
, which implies that
\mathcal{C}
is the generic component (see e.g. the proof of Lemma 5 in
[
Šiv12
]
). Hence
\dim\mathcal{C}=n^{2}+(r-1)n
. It is easy to see that
n^{2}+(r-1)n\leq(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor+r
for all
r\geq 3+\frac{8}{n-3}
if
n\geq 5
is odd and for all
r\geq 3+\frac{8}{n-2}+\frac{4}{(n-2)^{2}}
if
n\geq 4
is even. Hence,
\dim\mathcal{C}\leq(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor+r
if
r\geq 7
, except if
r=7
and
n=4
. Moreover, we have the equality
\dim\mathcal{C}=(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor+r
if and only if
(n,r)\in\{(4,8),(5,7)\}
.

Now we suppose that there is some
\lambda_{i}\geq 4
.
We need to prove the following

\displaystyle n^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}+mr+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\lambda^{2}_{i}-2\lambda_{i}+1+r(\lambda_{i}-1)\right)+\sum_{i=l+1}^{m}(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\leq(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+r.

Since
n\geq 4
, this inequality is obvious for
m=1
(in fact, we have equality in this case), therefore we assume
m\geq 2
. Observe that
\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{4}\rfloor\leq\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{4}
for each
i
and
\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor\geq\frac{n^{2}-1}{4}
, and that we have shown above that
\lambda_{i}=1
for
i\leq l
.
So it suffices to show that

(r-3)\sum_{i=l+1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{4}+(m-1)r+\frac{r+1}{4}-l<(r-3)\frac{n^{2}}{4}.

Since
n=l+\sum_{i=l+1}^{m}\lambda_{i}
,
the last inequality can be deduced from the following

(14)

\displaystyle(m-1)r+\frac{r+1}{4}-l<(r-3)\Big(\sum_{l+1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{2}+\frac{l}{2}\sum_{i=l+1}^{m}\lambda_{i}+\frac{l^{2}}{4}\Big).

Assume first that
l\geq 1
. Since
\lambda_{i_{0}}\geq 4
for some
i_{0}
, we have

\sum_{l+1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{2}+\frac{l}{2}\sum_{i=l+1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\geq 2(m-1).

To prove (

and
m\geq 2
.

On the other hand, if
l=0
, then
\lambda_{i}\geq 4
for each

\sum_{l+1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{2}\geq 4m(m-1),

which implies that (
14
) holds for any
m\geq 2
and
r\geq 4
.
∎

kI_{n}+\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, so
{\rm SL}_{n}\cdot(kI_{n}+\mathfrak{m})^{r}=\GL_{n}\cdot(kI_{n}+{\mathfrak{m}})^{r}
is contained in
C_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
.
The statement about the dimension in this case follows.
(Since
{\rm char}\,k\neq 2
, we cannot have
n=4
here.)
For the remaining cases, we argue as in the proof of Cor.
4.9
.
Note that our assumption on the characteristic implies that some
\lambda_{i}
is not divisible by the characteristic.
Consequently,

\dim\mathcal{C}\leq n^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\lambda_{i}}))+r)-r

and the conclusion follows by exactly the same argument as above if we slightly adapt the proofs of
[
Gur92
]
and
[
Šiv12
, Lemma 5]
to hold also in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

a) The inequalities
\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))\leq(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor
and
\dim C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\leq(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor+r
do not hold for small values of

has dimension

(see

) which for

is greater than
(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor
if and only if

and

.
Similarly, the generic component of
C_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
has dimension
n^{2}+(r-1)n
which, for

is greater than
(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor+r
if and only if

(n,r)\in\{(4,4),(4,5),(4,6),(4,7),(5,4),(5,5),(5,6),(6,4),(6,5),(7,4),(8,4),(9,4),(10,4)\}.

We conjecture that these are the only values of

such that

resp.

b) The proof of of Thm.
4.8
starts with the ‘base case’ of a nilpotent element

5.
Cohomological complexity theory

In this section we will discuss some applications of our earlier results to complexity of modules over finite group schemes, using a powerful result of Suslin-Friedlander-Bendel linking nilpotent commuting varieties with cohomological support varieties.
While the main results of this paper concern the type
A
only, these applications suggested a more general statement about complexities of rational
G
-modules (Theorem
5.7
) which we prove in all types.
The groups we are most interested in are Frobenius kernels and finite Chevalley groups.
We start with some notation and background for group schemes.

5.1.
Notation and background

We assume from now on that
{\rm char}\,k=p>0
and
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Let
G
be a reductive group scheme defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
(See
[
Jan03
, I.2]
for a definition.)
For each positive integer
r
, let
F_{r}:G\to G^{(r)}
be the
r
-th Frobenius morphism (see
[
Jan03
, I.9]
).
The scheme-theoretic kernel
G_{(r)}=\ker(F_{r})
is called the
r
-th Frobenius kernel
of
G
.
On the other hand, the fixed-point subgroup
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})=G^{F_{r}}
is a finite Chevalley group.
The subgroup schemes
G_{(r)}
and
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
are important examples of
finite group schemes
, i.e. group schemes with a finite-dimensional coordinate algebra.
Fix
B\subseteq G
a Borel subgroup of
G
and let
U\subseteq B
be the unipotent radical of
B
.
We also have the corresponding notation
B_{(r)},U_{(r)},B(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
, and
U(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
. Recall that
\mathfrak{g}=\text{Lie}(G)
, and let
\mathfrak{u}=\text{Lie}(U)
.

By convention, our
G
-modules are assumed to be finite-dimensional and rational over
G
.
The cohomological background below is valid for any finite group scheme
H
, but we will only consider
H
to be one of
G_{(r)},B_{(r)},U_{(r)},G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}),B({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{r}}),U({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{r}})
.
For any
H
-module
M
, the
m
-th cohomology of
H
with coefficients in
M
is defined as

\opH^{m}(H,M)=\Ext^{m}_{H}(k,M).

We further denote

\opH^{\bullet}(H,M)=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\opH^{m}(H,M)

and

\opH^{ev}(H,k)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\opH^{2m}(H,k)}~&\text{if}~p>2,\\
\opH^{\bullet}(H,k)~&\text{if}~p=2.\end{cases}

This is a commutative
k
-algebra under the cup product.
On the other hand, Yoneda composition provides an
\opH^{ev}(H,k)
-module structure on
\Ext_{H}^{\bullet}(M,M)
. Let
J_{M}
be the annihilator in
\opH^{ev}(H,k)
under this action.
We define
{\mathcal{V}}_{H}(M)
, the
support variety
of
M
over
H
to be the maximal ideal spectrum of the quotient ring
\frac{\opH^{ev}(H,k)}{\sqrt{J_{M}}}
.
In particular,
{\mathcal{V}}_{H}(M)
is a closed subvariety of
{\mathcal{V}}_{H}(k)=\text{Spec}\,{\opH^{ev}(H,k)_{\red}}
.

Given a graded vector space
\{V_{i}\}
, the
growth rate
of
\{V_{i}\}
is defined to be the least integer
s\geq 0
satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim V_{n}}{n^{s}}=0.

If no such
s
exists, one says that the growth rate for
\{V_{i}\}
is infinite.
The
complexity
of
M
, denoted
c_{H}(M)
, is defined to be the growth rate of
\Ext^{\bullet}_{H}(M,M)
.
It is well known that
c_{H}(M)=\dim\mathcal{V}_{H}(M)
for each finite dimensional
M
, see
[
NPV02
]
. In particular,
c_{H}(k)=\dim\opH^{ev}(H,k)
.

5.2.
Complexity of modules over Frobenius kernels

In the two papers
[
SFB97a
]
and
[
SFB97b
]
, Suslin, Friedlander, and Bendel determined support varieties of modules over
G_{(r)}
in terms of nilpotent commuting varieties.
In particular, they showed that if
G
has a structure of
exponential type
(see
[
Fri15
, 1.6]
for a definition) then

{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(r)}}(k)=C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g})),

and for each
G
-module
M
the support variety
{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(r)}}(M)
is a closed,
conical
G
-subvariety of
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g}))
. We recall that the restricted
nullcone
\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g})
is a closed subvariety of
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, hence

C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g}))\subseteq C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
.
Our calculation of the dimension of

C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g}))
provides insight into the complexity of
G
-modules.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G=\GL_{n}
and suppose
p>3
. If
n\geq 4,r\geq 7
, then for any
G_{(r)}
-module
M
, we have

c_{G_{(r)}}(M)\leq(r+1)\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor

with equality if the dimension of
M
is not divisible by
p
.

Proof.

As
\GL_{n}
is of exponential type, we have from the result of Suslin-Friedlander-Bendel that

c_{G_{(r)}}(M)\leq\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g}))\leq\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})).

By
[
SFB97b
, Example 6.9]
, we have

c_{G_{(r)}}(M)=c_{G_{(r)}}(k)=\dim C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g}))

for every
G
-module
M
whose dimension is not divisible by
p
.
On the other hand, if
{\mathfrak{m}}
is a commutative nil subalgebra of maximal dimension (i.e.
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m}
if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2m}
,
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{u}}_{m,m+1}
or
{\mathfrak{u}}_{m+1,m}
if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2m+1}
) then we observe that

G\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}\subseteq C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g})).

Hence, our assertion follows from Theorem
4.8
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

For the case
p=2
, the second author already proved the equality for
G=\GL_{n}
and
n,r\geq 1
, see
[
Ngo15
, Proposition 3.3.4]
.
We expect the theorem also to hold for the case
p=3
.

We now consider what can be said about the complexity of the restriction of an arbitrary rational
G
-module to
G_{(r)}
.
If
M
has dimension coprime to
p
then the support variety of
M
over
G_{(r)}
is the same as the support variety of the trivial module over
G_{(r)}
. On the other hand, there are in general many other rational
G
-modules
M
for which
c_{G_{(r)}}(M)=c_{G_{(r)}}(k)
but
{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(r)}}(M)\subsetneq{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(r)}}(k)
.

To explain this, assume in the following discussion that
G=\SL_{n}
.
(Restriction from
\GL_{n}
to
\SL_{n}
clearly has no effect on support varieties.)
Fix a maximal torus
T
contained in
B
, and let
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
be the corresponding basis of simple roots (numbered in the standard way).
Let
X(T)
, resp.
Y(T)
, be the lattice of characters, resp. cocharacters in
T
.
Since
G
is simply-connected,
Y(T)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
.
Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}
be the fundamental weights, i.e. such that
\langle\omega_{j},\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
, where
\langle.\,,.\rangle
denotes the perfect pairing
X(T)\times Y(T)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then
X(T)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n-1}{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{i}
.
A weight
\lambda\in X(T)
is
p^{r}
-restricted
(dominant) if
0\leq\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle<p^{r}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
; then
\lambda=\lambda_{0}+p\lambda_{1}+\ldots+p^{r-1}\lambda_{r-1}
where
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{r-1}
are
p
-restricted.
For a dominant weight
\lambda
let
L(\lambda)
denote the simple (finite-dimensional)
G
-module with highest weight
\lambda
.
If
\lambda
is
p^{r}
-restricted then
L(\lambda)|_{G_{(r)}}
is also irreducible.
Sobaje proved
[
Sob12
, Thm. 3.2]
that for
p
large enough, we have:

{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(r)}}(L(\lambda))=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r-1})\in C_{r}({\mathcal{N}}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g})):x_{i}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(1)}}(L(\lambda_{i}))\right\}

and therefore that
\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(r)}}(L(\lambda))=\dim C_{r}({\mathcal{N}}(\mathfrak{g}))
if and only if
{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(1)}}(L(\lambda_{i}))\supseteq G\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}=\overline{\mathcal{O}}
for
0\leq i\leq r-1
, where
{\mathcal{O}}
is the maximal nilpotent orbit for which all parts of the partition have order
\leq 2
.
(Recall that
{\mathfrak{m}}
is a commutative nil subalgebra of maximal dimension.) This is equivalent to the fact that each
\mathcal{V}_{G_{(1)}}(L(\lambda_{i}))
contains an element
e\in\mathcal{O}
. Now combining with the algebraic description of
\mathcal{V}_{G_{(1)}}(L(\lambda_{i}))
in
[
FP11
, Example 1.18(1)]
, we have proved the following.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
G=\SL_{n}
with
n\geq 3
. Suppose
p>n^{3}/4
and
r\geq 7
. Let
\lambda=\lambda_{0}+p\lambda_{1}+\ldots+p^{r-1}\lambda_{r-1}
where
\lambda_{i}
is
p
-restricted for
0\leq i\leq r-1
. Then
c_{G_{(r)}}(L(\lambda))=c_{G_{(r)}}(k)
if and only if for each
i
an element e of the maximal square-zero orbit has at least one Jordan block of size
<p
when acting on
L(\lambda_{i})
.

It would be nice if a geometric description of
{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(1)}}(L(\lambda_{i}))
could be obtained.
Unfortunately, it is in general a hard problem. If
p
is extremely large, then one can determine the support variety
{\mathcal{V}}_{G_{(1)}}(L(\lambda_{i}))
combinatorially using
[
DNP12
, Thm. 4.1]
and the fact that Lusztig’s character formula holds for all restricted dominant weights. Note also that Lusztig’s conjecture has been discussed in detail elsewhere, so we only mention Andersen-Jantzen-Soergel’s proof (for an unknown but extremely large bound)
[
AJS94
]
and Williamson’s discovery of various counter-examples
[
Wil17
]
for moderately large
p
, indicating that the required bound on
p
is at least exponential in the rank.

5.3.
Connection to finite Chevalley groups

In the rest of the paper we discuss some observations concerning support varieties and cohomological complexity for modules over the Chevalley group
G({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{r}})
, considered in connection with support varieties for
G_{(r)}
.
The main result here was inspired by our calculations on complexity of
G_{(r)}
-modules in the previous subsection.

Interactions between the three categories of
G
-,
G_{(r)}
-, and
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules have been studied for over fifty years, see for example
[
Hum06
, Ch. 10]
.
The restriction functors from
G
-modules to
G_{(r)}
- or
G({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{r}})
-modules allow us to relate the cohomology of
G_{(r)}
to that of
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
.
However, very little has been established in the way of a direct connection between the categories of
G_{(r)}
- and
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules.
A distinguished contribution in this direction is a result of Lin and
Nakano
[
LN99
, Thm. 3.4(b)]
stating that

c_{G(\mathbb{F}_{p})}(M)\leq\frac{1}{2}c_{G_{(1)}}(M)

for any
G
-module
M
.
We are going to generalize this to
G_{(r)}
and
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
for all
r\geq 1
.
This extension was inspired by the observation for
p>3
that:

c_{G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(k)=\frac{r}{r+1}c_{G_{(r)}}(k)

for
n\geq 4,r\geq 7
if
G=GL_{n}
, as follows easily by comparing Theorem
5.1
with
[
Hum06
, Table 1, p. 154]
.
In subsequent work
[
LNŠ21
]
we will show that this also holds for
G={\rm Sp}_{2n}
and a similar range of values of
n
,
r
.
In any case (and for any
G
), we have

(15)

\displaystyle c_{G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(k)\leq\frac{r}{r+1}c_{G_{(r)}}(k)

at least for large enough
r
.

In the rest of this paper, we proceed to prove the inequality (
15
) for an arbitrary simple algebraic group
G
and
G
-module
M
.
The strategy is to first establish an inequality of the complexities over the Borel subgroups, and then induce up to
G
.
The induction stage requires us to analyze some special geometric properties of
G
-saturation varieties.

For the next two results, we shall assume that
G
is a simple algebraic group and
p
is good for
G
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
W
be a
B
-stable closed subvariety of
\mathfrak{u}^{r}
for some positive integer
r
. Then we have

\dim G\cdot W\geq\frac{r+1}{r}\dim W.

Proof.

We can clearly assume that
W
is irreducible.
Let
\pi_{1}
be the restriction to
W
of the projection from
{\mathfrak{u}}^{r}
onto the first factor, and let
V_{1}=\overline{\pi_{1}(W)}
.
We may also assume that
\dim V_{1}\geq\frac{1}{r}\dim W
.
By the theorem on the dimensions of fibres, we have
\dim G\cdot W=\dim G+\dim W-\min_{w\in W}\dim\{g\in G:g^{-1}\cdot w\in W\}
.
The inequality
\dim G\cdot W\geq\frac{r+1}{r}\dim W
is therefore equivalent to:

\dim G-\min_{w\in W}\dim\{g\in G:g^{-1}\cdot w\in W\}\geq\frac{1}{r}\dim W.

We first consider the case
r=1
, i.e.
W=V_{1}
.
Since
G\cdot V_{1}
is closed and
G
-stable in
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, we can write
G\cdot V_{1}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\mathcal{O}_{j}}
where each
\mathcal{O}_{j}
is a nilpotent orbit in
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then

V_{1}\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\mathcal{O}_{j}}\right)\cap\mathfrak{u}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\left(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{j}}\cap\mathfrak{u}\right).

By
[
Jan04
, Theorem 10.11(1)]
, for each
j
,
\dim\left(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{j}}\cap\mathfrak{u}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathcal{O}_{j}
and therefore

2\dim V_{1}\leq\dim G\cdot V_{1}
.
Hence we have
\dim G-\min_{v\in V_{1}}\dim\{g\in G:g^{-1}\cdot v\in V_{1}\}\geq\dim V_{1}
.

In the general case, let
\mathcal{U}_{1}
be the open subset of
V_{1}
such that
\dim\{g\in G:g^{-1}\cdot u_{1}\in V_{1}\}
is minimal for all
u_{1}\in\mathcal{U}_{1}
, and let
\mathcal{U}
be the corresponding open subset of
W
.
Since
W
,
V_{1}
are irreducible and
\pi_{1}:W\rightarrow V_{1}
is dominant, it follows that there exists
u\in\mathcal{U}
such that
u_{1}=\pi_{1}(u)\in\mathcal{U}_{1}
.
Then we clearly have

\displaystyle\dim G-\min_{w\in W}\dim\{g\in G:g^{-1}\cdot w\in W\}

\displaystyle=\dim G-\dim\{g\in G:g^{-1}\cdot u\in W\}

\displaystyle\geq\dim G-\dim\{g\in G:g^{-1}\cdot u_{1}\in V_{1}\}

\displaystyle\geq\dim V_{1}\geq\frac{1}{r}\dim W.

∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
M
be a
G
-module. For all
r\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle{\dim\mathcal{V}_{G_{(r)}}(M)\geq\frac{r+1}{r}\dim\mathcal{V}_{B_{(r)}}(M)}.

In other words,

c_{G_{(r)}}(M)\geq\frac{r+1}{r}c_{B_{(r)}}(M).

Proof.

We first show that
\mathcal{V}_{B_{(r)}}(M)
can be identified with a subvariety of
\mathfrak{u}^{r}
. If
G
is of classical type, then this is done by Lemmas 1.7 and 1.8 in
[
SFB97a
]
. For other types of
G
, we can make use of Sobaje’s results in
[
Sob15a
]
[
Sob15b
]
.
Explicitly, consider the exponential map exp
:\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{U}_{1}(G)
defined in
[
Sob15a
, Theorem 3.4]
.
From
[
Sob15b
, Corollary 4.3]
, this map is in fact the restriction of a Springer isomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{U}(G)
.
Then it is well-known that
\phi
restricts to the unipotent radical of any parabolic subgroup of
G
, see for example
[
Sob15b
, Theorem 1.1]
.
In other words, the map exp restricts to
\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{u})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{U}_{1}(U)
giving that
U
has an exponential map defined in
[
Sob15a
, Definition 2.3]
(see
[
Sob15a
, Theorem 3.5]
).
Hence, Theorem 2.5 of
[
Sob15a
]
implies an identification between
\mathcal{V}_{U_{(r)}}(k)
and
C_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{[p]}(\mathfrak{u}))
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{B_{(r)}}(k)=\mathcal{V}_{U_{(r)}}(k)
(see for example the last line of page 23
[
NPV02
]
),
\mathcal{V}_{B_{r}}(M)
can be considered as a
B
-stable subvariety of
\mathfrak{u}^{r}
.
We note that those results of Sobaje depend on various types of prime
p
including
good
,
pretty good
, and
separably good
. These types of prime are only different when
G
is of type
A
, so it does not affect our proof. Finally, the corollary follows immediately from the above theorem and the fact that
G\cdot\mathcal{V}_{B_{(r)}}(M)=\mathcal{V}_{G_{(r)}}(M)

[
Ben96
, Proposition 4.5.2]
.
∎

We first prove our main result in the simplest case.

Lemma 5.6
.

For positive integers
r
and
m
,

c_{\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M)\leq c_{(\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a})_{(r)}}(M)

for any
\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a}
-module
M
.

Proof.

As
\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
is an elementary abelian group, we have

\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{mr}\cong\mathbb{G}^{mr}_{a}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Then applying
[
LN99
, Theorem 3.4(3.4.2)]
, we obtain

c_{\mathbb{G}^{mr}_{a}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}(M)\leq c_{\mathbb{G}^{mr}_{a(1)}}(M)=c_{(\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a})_{(r)}}(M),

where the last equality follows from the equivalence between categories of
\mathbb{G}_{a(r)}
-modules and
\mathbb{G}^{r}_{a(1)}
-modules,
[
SFB97b
, Proof of Prop. 6.5]
. Hence, we have proved the inequality.
∎

Here comes the main theorem.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
G
be a simple algebraic group.
Suppose
p
is a good prime for
G
.
For any rational
G
-module
M
, we have

c_{U(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M)\leq c_{U_{(r)}}(M),

and

c_{G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M)\leq\frac{r}{r+1}c_{G_{(r)}}(M).

Proof.

Since
U(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
is a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
B(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
and
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
,

c_{G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M)=c_{B(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M)=c_{U(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M).

Note further that
c_{U_{(r)}}(M)=c_{B_{(r)}}(M)
, see for example
[
NPV02
, §2.3]
.
Hence, it suffices to prove the first inequality as the other one follows immediately from Corollary
5.5
.
Observe for any finite group
K
that
c_{K}(M)
equals to the maximum complexity of
M
over all maximal elementary abelian subgroups of
K
, so we just need to prove the inequality for each maximal elementary subgroup of
U(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
.
Let
E
be such a subgroup of
U(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
.
Then
E\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{m}
for some positive integer
m
and is generated by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
.
Using
[
Sei00
, Theorems 1.3 or 1.4]
, for each
x_{i}
, we can find a monomorphism from
\mathbb{G}_{a}
to
G
which sends
1
to
x_{i}
.
In other words, there is an abelian unipotent
U_{i}
in
G
which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
U_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
contains
x_{i}
for each

. Now let
U_{max}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}
, we have
U_{max}\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{m}
and
U_{max}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})=E
. Now Lemma
5.6
gives us

c_{E}(M)=c_{U_{max}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})}(M)\leq c_{U_{max(r)}}(M).

Since
U_{max(r)}
is a closed subgroup of
U_{(r)}
, we have

\mathcal{V}_{U_{max(r)}}(M)=\mathcal{V}_{U_{max(r)}}(k)~\bigcap~\mathcal{V}_{U_{(r)}}(M)

by
[
NPV02
, 2.2.10]
. It follows that
c_{U_{max(r)}}(M)\leq c_{U_{(r)}}(M)
, therefore completing our proof.
∎

From the fact that the complexity of a module is zero if and only if the module is projective, we obtain an alternative proof to the main result of Drupieski in
[
Dru13
, Theorem 2.3]
as follows.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
M
be a rational
G
-module. If
M
is projective over
G_{(r)}
, then it is also projective as a module over
G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
.

Our last theorem also indicates that commuting varieties may be a source of information about complexity over finite Chevalley groups, in addition to the known relationship with support varieties over Frobenius kernels.
This suggests the following question, a positive answer to which would provide further context for Corollary
5.8

-module

over

and over
G_{(r)}
(or perhaps

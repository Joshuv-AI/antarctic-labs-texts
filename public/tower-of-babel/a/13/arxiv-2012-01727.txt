Classification of ancient flows by sub-affine-critical powers of curvature in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Kyeongsu Choi, Liming Sun

Abstract
. We classify closed convex ancient α \alpha -curve shortening flows for sub-affine-critical powers α ≤ 1 3 \alpha\leq\frac{1}{3} . In addition, we show that closed convex smooth finite entropy ancient α \alpha -curve shortening flows with 1 3 < α \frac{1}{3}<\alpha are shrinking circles. After rescaling, the ancient flows satisfying the above conditions converge exponentially fast to smooth closed convex shrinkers as the time goes to negative infinity. In particular, when α = 1 k 2 − 1 \alpha=\frac{1}{k^{2}-1} with 3 ≤ k ∈ ℕ 3\leq k\in\mathbb{N} , the round circle shrinker has non-trivial Jacobi fields, but the ancient flows asymptotic to shrinking circles do not evolve along the Jacobi fields.

Uniqueness of tangent flow at infinity

2.1.
Preliminary

Suppose
\Gamma

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, namely
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. The
support function
of
\Gamma
in the direction
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
with respect to some fixed point

(2.1)

\displaystyle u_{z_{0}}(\theta):=\max_{z\in\Omega}\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),z-z_{0}\rangle.

When

is the origin, we write

for the purpose of simplicity.

If
\Gamma

\theta

\Gamma

of strictly convex closed differentiable curve

\displaystyle u(\theta)=-\langle\mathbf{N}(\theta),\mathbf{X}(\theta)\rangle.

We can derive

\displaystyle u_{\theta\theta}+u=\langle\tfrac{d}{d\theta}\mathbf{X},\mathbf{T}\rangle=\langle\tfrac{ds}{d\theta}\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T}\rangle=\kappa^{-1},

where

Now, we consider an evolution

that

\displaystyle u_{t}=-\kappa^{\alpha}=-(u_{\theta\theta}+u)^{-\alpha}.

Also, differentiating (

(\kappa^{\alpha})_{t}=\alpha(\kappa^{\alpha})^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\kappa^{\alpha})_{\theta\theta}+\alpha(\kappa^{\alpha})^{2+\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\kappa^{\alpha}

Next, from (

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}_{\tau}=(1+\alpha)^{-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}(-t)^{-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\Gamma_{t},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\,\;\tau=-\tfrac{1}{1+\alpha}\log(-t).

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\bar{\boldsymbol{X}}={\bar{\kappa}^{\alpha}}\mathbf{N}+\bar{\boldsymbol{X}},

We also use
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{k}^{\alpha}}
for clarification to denote the linearized operator above, when we consider the operators for different shrinkers.

Thus, the remaining quadratic error term is given by

(3.7)

\displaystyle E(v)=-{(h^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}+v_{\theta\theta}+v)^{-\alpha}}+h-\alpha h^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}(v_{\theta\theta}+v).

We now introduce the space
L^{2}_{h}(\mathbb{S}^{1})=\{f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}:\|f\|_{h}^{2}<+\infty\}
, where
\|f\|_{h}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}f^{2}h^{-1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. The space is equipped with the inner product

(3.8)

\displaystyle(f,g)_{h}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}fgh^{-1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}d\theta.

Since
h>0
, this norm is equivalent to the standard
L^{2}
norm over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. More importantly,
\mathcal{L}
is a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{h}
over the compact space
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Thus we have eigenfunctions
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\text{span}\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\cdots\}=L^{2}_{h}
,
(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})_{h}=\delta_{ij}
, and
\mathcal{L}\varphi_{j}+\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}=0
, where the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy
\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{j+1}
and
\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{j}=+\infty
.

Given
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define projection operators
P_{j}
by
P_{j}f=(f,\varphi_{j})_{h}\varphi_{j}
and

(3.9)

\displaystyle P_{<\lambda}=\sum_{\{j:\lambda_{j}<\lambda\}}P_{j},

\displaystyle P_{=\lambda}=\sum_{\{j:\lambda_{j}=\lambda\}}P_{j},

\displaystyle P_{>\lambda}=\sum_{\{j:\lambda_{j}>\lambda\}}P_{j}.

For
\lambda=0
, we will frequently use the following notations for the purpose of brevity.

(3.10)

\displaystyle P_{+}=\sum_{\{j:\lambda_{j}<0\}}P_{j},

\displaystyle P_{*}=\sum_{\{j:\lambda_{j}=0\}}P_{j},

\displaystyle P_{-}=\sum_{\{j:\lambda_{j}>0\}}P_{j}.

We denote the projection to the negative eigensapce by
P_{+}
instead of
P_{-}
, because
\|P_{+}v\|_{h}
would increase in time due to its unstability.

Proposition 3.1

(non-vanishing)
.

Let
v
be a solution to (
3.5
) for some positive
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
satisfying (
3.2
). Then,
\|v(\cdot,\tau)\|_{h}>0
holds for every
\tau
unless
v\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\|v(\cdot,\tau_{0})\|_{h}=0
at some
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and denote by
\overline{\Omega}_{\tau}
the region enclosed by the rescaled flow
\overline{\Gamma}_{\tau}
. Since the entropy is invariant under rotations,
\overline{\Gamma}_{\tau_{0}}
has the same entropy to the backward limit shrinker, namely

(3.11)

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{\tau_{0}})=\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{\tau}).

Therefore, Proposition
2.6
implies
v\equiv 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(error estimates)
.

Suppose that
v
and
h
are solutions to (
3.5
) and (
3.2
), respectively, satisfying (
3.3
) for some
\tau_{0}\leq-1
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, there exists some
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
depending on
\alpha,h
such that if
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
then for each
j\in\mathbb{N}

(3.12)

\displaystyle|(E,P_{-}v)_{h}|+\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq 0}|(E,P_{j}v)_{h}|\leq C\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2},

holds for
\tau\in[\tau_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1},\tau_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}]
and
E
defined in (
3.7
), where
C
is some constant depending on
\alpha,h
and
\{\varphi_{j}:\lambda_{j}\leq 0\}
.

Proof.

We consider a function
f(x)
defined by

(3.13)

x^{2}f(x)=-(1+x)^{-\alpha}+1-\alpha x.

By the Taylor expansion of
(1+x)^{-\alpha}
,
f(x)
is smooth near
x=0
. Hence, by the definition of
E
in (
3.5
), we get
E=h^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}(v+v_{\theta\theta})^{2}\rho
, where
\rho(v)=h^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}f(h^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(v+v_{\theta\theta}))
is a smooth positive function. Since
v\to 0
in the
C^{20}
-topology, there exists a constant
C
only depending on
h,f
such that
|\partial_{\theta}^{m}\rho|\leq C
holds for
m=0,1,2,3
and sufficiently negative
\tau
.

Now, by using
E=(v+v_{\theta\theta})^{2}h^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}\rho
we can obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(E,v)_{h}=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(v_{\theta\theta}+v)^{2}v\rho d\theta=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta}^{2}v\rho+v^{2}(v+2v_{\theta\theta})\rho d\theta\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta}^{2}v\rho d\theta+O(1)\|v\|_{C^{2}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

Here
O(1)
denotes a quantity bounded by some numeric constant
C
for negative enough
\tau
. In addition,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta}^{2}v\rho d\theta=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta\theta}v_{\theta}v\rho+v_{\theta\theta}v_{\theta}^{2}\rho+v_{\theta\theta}v_{\theta}v\rho_{\theta}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta\theta\theta}(\partial_{\theta}v^{2})\rho+\tfrac{1}{3}\partial_{\theta}(v_{\theta}^{3})\rho+\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta\theta}(\partial_{\theta}v^{2})\rho_{\theta}d\theta.

Hence, by using integration by parts we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta}^{2}v\rho d\theta=&\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta}^{3}\rho_{\theta}d\theta+O(1)\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}\\
=&-\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}2v_{\theta\theta}v_{\theta}v\rho_{\theta}+v_{\theta}^{2}v\rho_{\theta\theta}d\theta+O(1)\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}\\
=&-\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta}(\partial_{\theta}v^{2})\rho_{\theta}+\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}(\partial_{\theta}v^{2})\rho_{\theta\theta}d\theta+O(1)\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}\\
=&O(1)\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

Combine the above inequalities,

(3.18)

\displaystyle|(E,v)_{h}|\leq C\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}.

On the other hand, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle(E,P_{j}v)_{h}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(v_{\theta\theta}+v)^{2}P_{j}v\rho d\theta=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta}^{2}P_{j}v\rho d\theta+O(1)\|v\|_{C^{2}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}.

Moreover, using integration by parts

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta}^{2}P_{j}v\rho d\theta=&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta\theta\theta}v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)\rho+v_{\theta\theta}v_{\theta}[(P_{j}v)\rho]_{\theta}\ d\theta\\
=&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta}[v_{\theta\theta\theta}(P_{j}v)\rho]+\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{\theta}(v_{\theta}^{2})[(P_{j}v)\rho]_{\theta}d\theta\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v[v_{\theta\theta\theta}(P_{j}v)\rho]_{\theta}+\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}^{2}[(P_{j}v)\rho]_{\theta\theta}d\theta\\
=&O(1)\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}\|P_{j}v\|_{H^{1}}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}^{2}[(P_{j}v)\rho]_{\theta\theta}d\theta.\end{split}

For the second term, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}^{2}[(P_{j}v)\rho]_{\theta\theta}d\theta\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{\theta}[\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)_{\theta\theta}\rho+v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}+\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)\rho_{\theta\theta}]d\theta\\
=&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v\left[\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta\theta}(P_{j}v)_{\theta\theta}\rho+\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)_{\theta\theta\theta}\rho+\tfrac{3}{2}v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)_{\theta\theta}\rho_{\theta}+v_{\theta\theta}(P_{j}v)_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.+\tfrac{3}{2}v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)_{\theta}\rho_{\theta\theta}+\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta\theta}(P_{j}v)\rho_{\theta\theta}+\tfrac{1}{2}v_{\theta}(P_{j}v)\rho_{\theta\theta\theta}\right]d\theta\\
=&\,O(1)\|v\|_{C^{2}}\|v\|_{h}\|P_{j}v\|_{H^{3}}.\end{split}

Thus,

(3.22)

|(E,P_{j}v)_{h}|\leq C\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}\|P_{j}v\|_{H^{3}}.

We observe that
P_{j}v=(v,\varphi_{j})_{h}\varphi_{j}
gives
\partial_{\theta}^{m}P_{j}v=(v,\varphi_{j})_{h}\partial_{\theta}^{m}\varphi_{j}
, and thus

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}P_{j}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{j}\|P_{j}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
C_{j}
only depends on
m
and
\varphi_{j}
. Therefore,

(3.24)

|(E,P_{j}v)_{h}|\leq C\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}\|P_{j}v\|_{H^{3}}\leq C_{j}\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}.

Thus, combining (
3.18
) and the above estimates for all
\lambda_{j}\leq 0
yields

(3.25)

|(E,P_{-}v)_{h}|\leq C\|v\|_{C^{4}}\|v\|_{h}^{2}.

∎

Theorem 3.5

(spectral dichotomy)
.

Suppose that an ancient solution
v
to (

-topology as

and negative enough

\displaystyle\|P_{-}v\|_{C^{8}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|P_{+}v\|_{C^{8}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\left|\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\|P_{*}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right|=o(\|P_{*}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}),

or

(3.39)

.

, we have

for all
\tau

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\|P_{+}\partial_{\theta}^{m}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\left|\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\|P_{*}\partial_{\theta}^{m}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|

4.
Radial asymptotic behavior

In this section, we will show that a rescaled ancient flow with the radial asymptotic behavior converges exponentially fast to the unit circle. We state the goal of this section below.

Theorem 4.1

(fast convergence to the circle)
.

Suppose that a rescaled ancient solution
\overline{\Gamma}_{\tau}
smoothly converges to the unit circle
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\infty}
as
\tau\to-\infty
. Then, there exist some
C>0
and
T\ll-1
such that (
3.39
) and

(4.1)

\|v(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{I}\tau}

hold for
\tau\leq T
, where
1+v(\cdot,\tau)
is the support function of
\overline{\Gamma}_{\tau}
and
\lambda_{I}
is the greatest negative eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{L}=\alpha\partial_{\theta}^{2}+\alpha+1
.

We recall the evolution equation (
3.5
) of
v
with
h\equiv 1
that

(4.2)

\partial_{\tau}v=\mathcal{L}v+E(v),

where

(4.3)

\mathcal{L}v=\alpha v_{\theta\theta}+(\alpha+1)v,

and

(4.4)

E(v)=-(1+v_{\theta\theta}+v)^{-\alpha}+1-\alpha(v_{\theta\theta}+v).

To show the exponential decay of
v
, we assume the neutral mode dominance (
3.38
) in Theorem
3.5
, towards a contradiction. First, we should have
\ker\mathcal{L}\neq\emptyset
unless
v\equiv 0
. Thus, Theorem
A.1
implies that there exists some integer
k\geq 3
such that

(4.5)

\alpha=1/(k^{2}-1).

Next, we consider the Fourier expansion of
v
.

(4.6)

\displaystyle v(\theta,\tau)=A_{0}(\tau)+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{m}(\tau)\cos m\theta+B_{m}(\tau)\sin m\theta,

where

(4.7)

\displaystyle A_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v(\theta,\tau)d\theta,\hskip 10.00002ptA_{m}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v\cos m\theta d\theta,B_{m}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v\sin m\theta d\theta.

We also define
\rho(\tau)
and
Q(\tau)
by

(4.8)

\displaystyle\rho=A_{k}^{2}+B_{k}^{2},

\displaystyle Q=(A_{k}^{2}-B_{k}^{2})A_{2k}+2A_{k}B_{k}B_{2k}.

In order to exclude the trivial case
v\equiv 0
, we assume that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\rho(\tau)=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{but}\hskip 10.00002pt\rho(\tau)>0

holds for negative enough
\tau
. Moreover, to have the neutral mode dominance (
3.38
), we assume that

(4.10)

\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\rho(\tau)=o(\rho),

and

(4.11)

\|P_{-}v\|_{C^{8}}+\|P_{+}v\|_{C^{8}}=o(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}).

We will verify in the proof of Theorem
4.1
that these assumptions are required for a contradiction argument.

For the convenience of notation, only in this section, we abuse the notation of the projection operators in (
3.10
) as
P_{0}v=A_{0}
and
P_{m}v=A_{m}\cos m\theta+B_{m}\sin m\theta
for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. However, we will use the same definition of
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
, namely

(4.12)

\displaystyle P_{-}v=\sum_{m>k}P_{m}v,

\displaystyle P_{+}v=\sum^{k-1}_{m=0}P_{m}v.

Notice that
P_{k}
denotes the projection to neutral space, namely
P_{*}=P_{k}
. Notice that
\|P_{*}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\rho
.
Furthermore, we will relabel the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\infty}}
by

(4.13)

\lambda_{l}=\alpha(l^{2}-1)-1,

with
l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\cup\{0\}
so that
P_{l}
denotes the projection to the eigenspace of
\lambda_{l}
.

In this section, we will show that
P_{2k}v
and
P_{0}v
dominate
P_{-}v
and
P_{+}v
, respectively. Thus, we consider the following projections for convenience.

(4.14)

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{-}v:=P_{-}v-P_{2k}v,

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{+}v:=P_{+}v-P_{0}v.

We begin by observing the slow decay property of
\rho
.

Proposition 4.2

(slow decay)
.

If we have (
4.10
) and (
4.9
), then

(4.15)

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\rho(\tau)e^{-\delta\tau}=+\infty

holds for every
\delta>0
.

Proof.

Combining (
4.10
) and (
4.9
) yields
\frac{d}{d\tau}\log\rho=o(1)
. Hence, given
\delta>0
there exists some
T
such that
\frac{d}{d\tau}\log\rho<\frac{\delta}{2}
holds for
\tau\leq T
. Thus,

(4.16)

5.

In this section, we will show that a rescaled ancient flow asymptotic to
k
-fold shrinkers converges exponentially fast.

We recall the kernel
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma^{\alpha}_{k}}=\text{span}\{h_{\theta}\}
from Theorem
A.1
. Since
h_{\theta}
corresponds to the rotation of
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{k}
, the shrinker
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{k}
is an
integrable critical point
of the entropy
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
. This notion of integrability and the fast convergence were pioneered by
Allard and Almgren 1981
in their study of tangent cones of minimal surfaces with isolated singularities.

Definition 5.1

(
\varepsilon
-shrinking flow)
.

We say that a rescaled ancient flow
\overline{\Gamma}_{\tau}
with the support function
\bar{u}(\theta,\tau)
is
\varepsilon
-close to
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{k}
with the support function

(up to rotation) at time
\tau_{0}
if there exists some
\theta_{0}\in[0,2\pi)
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{\theta_{0}}(\cdot,\tau)-h\|_{C^{4,\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\varepsilon,

holds for
\tau\in[\tau_{0}-1,\tau_{0}+1]
, where
\bar{u}_{\theta_{0}}(\theta,\tau)=\bar{u}(\theta_{0}+\theta,\tau)
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that a rescaled flow
\overline{\Gamma}_{\tau}
converges to the shrinker
\Gamma_{k}^{\alpha}
with
k\geq 3
up to rotations as

]

Theorem 6.4

unless

.

Since each

(

\partial_{\tau}v_{i}-\mathcal{L}v_{i}=E(v_{i})=h^{1+\frac{2}{\alpha}}(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{i}+v_{i})^{2}f(h^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{i}+v_{i})),

w=v_{1}-v_{2}

w_{\tau}-\mathcal{L}w=E(v_{1})-E(v_{2})=:F(w;v_{1},v_{2})=:(\partial_{\theta}^{2}w+w)G(v_{1},v_{2})h^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}},

(6.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G:=&\,h^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{1}+v_{1}+\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{2}+v_{2})f(h^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{1}+v_{1}))\\
&+h^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{2}+v_{2})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(h^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(s(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{1}+v_{1})+(1-s)(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{2}+v_{2})))ds.\end{split}

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose that
v_{1},v_{2}
are smooth ancient solutions to (
6.2
), and given
\varepsilon>0
there is
T_{\varepsilon}
such that the difference
w=v_{1}-v_{2}
satisfies
\|v_{1}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^{4}},\|v_{2}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^{4}}\leq\varepsilon
for
\tau\leq T_{\varepsilon}
. Then, there are some constants
\varepsilon_{0},C_{0}
only depending on
\alpha,h
such that

(6.15)

\displaystyle|(F,w)_{h}|(\tau)\leq C_{0}(\|v_{1}\|_{C^{4}}+\|v_{2}\|_{C^{4}})\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{h}^{2},

holds for
\tau\leq T_{\varepsilon_{0}}
.

Proof.

While
\|v_{1}\|_{C^{4}},\|v_{2}\|_{C^{4}}
are small enough,

solves the linear PDE (
6.13
)

(6.16)

w_{\tau}=\mathcal{L}w+h^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}G(w_{\theta\theta}+w).

Hence, the standard interior regularity theory for linear parabolic PDEs yields

(6.17)

\|w(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{h}

for some constant

only depending on

\displaystyle(F,w)_{h}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(w_{\theta\theta}+w)wGd\theta=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}-w_{\theta}^{2}G-w_{\theta}wG_{\theta}+w^{2}Gd\theta,

Therefore, combining the two inequalities above completes the proof.

Lemma 6.6
.

Suppose that
v_{1},v_{2}
are smooth ancient solutions to (
6.2
) undergoing exponential decay (
6.1
). Then, for every negative value
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, the difference
w=v_{1}-v_{2}
satisfies

(6.20)

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}e^{\lambda\tau}\|w(\cdot,\tau)\|_{h}=+\infty,

unless
v_{1}\equiv v_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\limsup_{\tau\to-\infty}e^{\lambda\tau}\|w\|_{h}\leq C
holds for some
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
. Then, we have
\delta^{\prime}=\min\{\delta,\lambda_{1}-\lambda\}>0
. We recall
\varepsilon_{0},T_{\varepsilon_{0}}
in Lemma
6.5
, and then we find some
C_{1}
and
T_{1}\leq T_{\varepsilon_{0}}
such that

\|v_{1}\|_{C^{4}}+\|v_{2}\|_{C^{4}}\leq C_{1}e^{\delta^{\prime}\tau}
and
e^{\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}\leq C_{1}e^{\delta^{\prime}\tau}

hold for
\tau\leq T_{1}
. Hence, Lemma
6.5
yields

(6.21)

|(F,w)_{h}|\leq C_{0}C_{1}e^{\delta^{\prime}\tau}\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{h}^{2}.

Since we have

(6.22)

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\|w\|_{h}^{2}=(w,\mathcal{L}w+F)_{h}\leq-\lambda_{1}\|w\|^{2}_{h}+(w,F)_{h},

combining with (
6.21
) and
e^{\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}\leq C_{1}e^{\delta^{\prime}\tau}
yields

(6.23)

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{d\tau}e^{2\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}^{2}\leq C_{0}C_{1}e^{(2\lambda_{1}+\delta^{\prime})\tau}\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{h}^{2}\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{3}e^{3\delta^{\prime}\tau}

for
\tau\leq T_{1}
. Remembering
\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}e^{\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}\leq\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}C_{1}e^{\delta^{\prime}\tau}=0
, we have

(6.24)

e^{2\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\tfrac{d}{ds}e^{2\lambda_{1}s}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{h}^{2}ds\leq\frac{2C_{0}C_{1}^{3}}{3\delta^{\prime}}e^{3\delta^{\prime}\tau}=\frac{2^{2}C_{0}C_{1}^{3}}{3!\delta^{\prime}}e^{3\delta^{\prime}\tau}

for
\tau\leq T_{1}
. We repeat this process so that we have

(6.25)

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{d\tau}e^{2\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}^{2}\leq C_{0}C_{1}e^{(2\lambda_{1}+\delta^{\prime})\tau}\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{h}^{2}\leq\frac{2^{2}C_{0}^{2}C_{1}^{4}}{3!\delta^{\prime}}e^{4\delta^{\prime}\tau},

and thus

(6.26)

e^{2\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\tfrac{d}{ds}e^{2\lambda_{1}s}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{h}^{2}ds\leq\frac{2^{3}C_{0}^{2}C_{1}^{4}}{4!(\delta^{\prime})^{2}}e^{4\delta^{\prime}\tau}.

By iterating the same process, we obtain

(6.27)

e^{2\lambda_{1}\tau}\|w\|_{h}^{2}\leq\frac{2^{k-1}C_{0}^{k-2}C_{1}^{k}}{k!(\delta^{\prime})^{k-2}}e^{k\delta^{\prime}\tau}

for
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tau\leq T_{1}
. Passing
k\to\infty
yields
w\equiv 0
for
\tau\leq T_{1}
, and therefore
w\equiv 0
holds for all
\tau
.
∎

Lemma 6.7

for

(6.57)

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\|e^{\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{I}}\tau}\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{a}_{\bar{I}}^{\prime})-\vec{a}_{\bar{I}}^{\prime}\cdot\vec{\varphi}_{\bar{I}}\|_{h}=0.

We repeat this process at most

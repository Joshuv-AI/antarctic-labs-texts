On the spectrum of the double-layer operator on locally-dilation-invariant Lipschitz domains
Simon N. Chandler-Wilde, Raffael Hagger, Karl-Mikael Perfekt, Jani A. Virtanen

Abstract
We say that Γ \Gamma , the boundary of a bounded Lipschitz domain, is locally dilation invariant if, at each x ∈ Γ x\in\Gamma , Γ \Gamma is either locally C 1 C^{1} or locally coincides (in some coordinate system centred at x x ) with a Lipschitz graph Γ x \Gamma_{x} such that Γ x = α x ​ Γ x \Gamma_{x}=\alpha_{x}\Gamma_{x} , for some α x ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha_{x}\in(0,1) . In this paper we study, for such Γ \Gamma , the essential spectrum of D Γ D_{\Gamma} , the double-layer (or Neumann-Poincaré) operator of potential theory, on L 2 ​ ( Γ ) L^{2}(\Gamma) . We show, via localisation and Floquet-Bloch-type arguments, that this essential spectrum is the union of the spectra of related continuous families of operators K t K_{t} , for t ∈ [ − π , π ] t\in[-\pi,\pi] ; moreover, each K t K_{t} is compact if Γ \Gamma is C 1 C^{1} except at finitely many points. For the 2D case where, additionally, Γ \Gamma is piecewise analytic, we construct convergent sequences of approximations to the essential spectrum of D Γ D_{\Gamma} ; each approximation is the union of the eigenvalues of finitely many finite matrices arising from Nyström-method approximations to the operators K t K_{t} . Through error estimates with explicit constants, we also construct functionals that determine whether any particular locally-dilation-invariant piecewise-analytic Γ \Gamma satisfies the well-known spectral radius conjecture, that the essential spectral radius of D Γ D_{\Gamma} on L 2 ​ ( Γ ) L^{2}(\Gamma) is < 1 / 2 <1/2 for all Lipschitz Γ \Gamma . We illustrate this theory with examples; for each we show that the essential spectral radius is < 1 / 2 <1/2 , providing additional support for the conjecture. We also, via new results on the invariance of the essential spectral radius under locally-conformal C 1 , β C^{1,\beta} diffeomorphisms, show that the spectral radius conjecture holds for all Lipschitz curvilinear polyhedra.

The Nyström method

In this section, with a view to applications in §

12

12

\omega_{q,N}\geq 0

4
The Double-Layer Operator on Dilation invariant graphs

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
be an open cone,
f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
a Lipschitz continuous function, and consider the graph
\Gamma=\left\{(x,f(x)):x\in A\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the case that
\alpha\Gamma=\Gamma
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
; that is, in the case that

f(\alpha x)=\alpha f(x),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in A.

(4.1)

We will term such graphs
dilation invariant
.

In this section, the largest of the paper, we study the spectrum and essential spectrum of the double-layer (DL) operator
D_{\Gamma}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
given by (
1.2
) on such graphs. (The case

is of particular interest for later applications.) In §
4.1
we show, for general dimension

)
\sigma(D_{\Gamma})=\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})
(and, similarly, that
W(D_{\Gamma})=W_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})
) and, by Floquet-Bloch-transform arguments, that

\sigma(D_{\Gamma})
is the union of the spectra of a family of operators
K_{t}:L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})
, for
t\in[-\pi,\pi]
, where
\Gamma_{0}
is a particular relatively closed and bounded subset of
\Gamma
. Moreover, helpful for the later computation of
\sigma(K_{t})
, each
K_{t}
is compact, and so has a discrete spectrum, if
f\in C^{1}(A\setminus\{0\})
(Corollary
4.6
).

In the remaining subsections, §
4.2
–§
4.4
, we focus on the 2D case, considering the Nyström approximation of spectral properties of
K_{t}
, combining the general results of §
3.2
with explicit estimates for the particular operators
K_{t}
. The case
A=\mathbb{R}
, with
f
real-analytic on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, is treated in §
4.3
. It is this case that is relevant, via, e.g., (
1.12
), to the computation of
\sigma(D_{\Gamma})
and the spectral radius conjecture when
\Gamma
is the boundary of a bounded Lipschitz domain. But this case is rather complex; the operator
K_{t}
is studied by reducing it to a

operator matrix, corresponding to the split of

into the two half-axes

4.2
, the easier case

4.1
Floquet-Bloch transform results

Let
V_{\alpha}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
be dilation by
\alpha
, that is,

V_{\alpha}\phi(x)=\alpha^{(d-1)/2}\phi(\alpha x),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\Gamma.

(4.2)

V_{\alpha}
is unitary and commutes with
D_{\Gamma}
: noting that
n(y)=n(\alpha y)
, for
y\in\Gamma
, we see that, for all
\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and almost every
x\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle D_{\Gamma}V_{\alpha}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{d}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{(x-y)\cdot n(y)}{|x-y|^{d}}\alpha^{(d-1)/2}\phi(\alpha y)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{(d-1)/2}}{c_{d}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{(x-\alpha^{-1}y)\cdot n(\alpha^{-1}y)}{|x-\alpha^{-1}y|^{d}}\phi(y)\,\alpha^{-(d-1)}\mathrm{d}s(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{(d-1)/2}}{c_{d}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{(\alpha x-y)\cdot n(y)}{|\alpha x-y|^{d}}\phi(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)

\displaystyle=V_{\alpha}D_{\Gamma}\phi(x).

This already implies that the spectrum and the essential spectrum of
D_{\Gamma}
coincide, as implied by the following simple proposition (cf.
[
11
, Lemma 2.7]
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
. If
T
commutes with a sequence of unitary operators
(U_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges weakly to
0
, then
\sigma(T)=\sigma_{\ess}(T)
.

Proof.

Assume there exists
\phi\in H\setminus\left\{0\right\}
such that
T\phi=0
. As
T
and
U_{j}
commute, also
TU_{j}\phi=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular,
\left\{U_{j}\phi:j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subseteq\ker(T)
. As the operators
U_{j}
are unitary and
U_{j}\to 0
weakly, the sequence
(U_{j}\phi)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
cannot have a convergent subsequence. Hence,
\ker(T)
is either trivial or infinite-dimensional. Similarly,
\ker(T^{\prime})
is either trivial or infinite-dimensional. Thus, if
T
is Fredholm, it is invertible. Considering
T-\lambda I
instead of
T
yields the result.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

be a dilation invariant graph. Then
\sigma(D_{\Gamma})=\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})
,
\overline{W(D_{\Gamma})}=W_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})
, and
\left\lVert D_{\Gamma}\right\rVert_{\ess}=\left\lVert D_{\Gamma}\right\rVert
.

Proof.

Assume that
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
have compact support and
0\notin\supp\phi\cup\supp\psi
. Then

\left\langle V_{\alpha}^{j}\phi,\psi\right\rangle=\int_{\Gamma}\alpha^{j(d-1)/2}\phi(\alpha^{j}x)\overline{\psi(x)}\,\mathrm{d}s(x).

If
\left\lvert j\right\rvert
is sufficiently large, the integrand vanishes. Because compactly supported functions are dense in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
, it follows that
V_{\alpha}^{j}\to 0
weakly as
\left\lvert j\right\rvert\to\infty
. The equality of spectrum and essential spectrum follows from Proposition
4.1
. The results for the numerical range and norm follow from
[
11
, Lemma 2.7]
.
∎

To make use of standard Floquet-Bloch/Fourier transform results, it is convenient to view
D_{\Gamma}
as a discrete convolution operator. For

\Gamma_{j}:=\left\{(\tilde{x},f(\tilde{x}))\in\Gamma:|\tilde{x}|\in[\alpha^{j+1},\alpha^{j}]\right\}

L^{2}(\Gamma_{j})
with a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\Gamma)
by extending by

. Let

denote orthogonal projection. Clearly,

P_{j}\phi(x)=\begin{cases}\phi(x)&\text{if }x\in\Gamma_{j},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and we note that
P_{j}V_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha}P_{j+1}
, so that also
P_{j}V_{\alpha}^{k}=V_{\alpha}^{k}P_{j+k}
,

.
Let

\Gamma=\cup_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}
,
E_{j}\cap E_{k}=\emptyset
for
j\neq k
and
\Gamma
is locally
C^{1}
at every
x\in\cup_{j=1}^{N}\partial E_{j}
, then

D_{\Gamma}\sim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{E_{j}}D_{\Gamma}\chi_{E_{j}}

and thus

\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{\ess}(\chi_{E_{j}}D_{\Gamma}\chi_{E_{j}}).

(5.2)

Equation (
5.2
) is a localisation result. We have also the following, more substantial localisation result (cf.
[
16
,
47
,
15
,
11
]
) for the case where
\Gamma\in\mathscr{D}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\Omega_{-}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain such that
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{-}\in\mathscr{D}
, and pick
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\Gamma
for which
\Gamma\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}B(x_{j},\delta(x_{j}))
. Then

\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})=\bigcup\limits_{x\in\Gamma}\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma_{x}})=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma_{x_{j}}}).

Proof.

Let
x\in\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
is locally
C^{1}
at
x
, then, by (
5.1
) and since
D_{\Gamma_{x}}
is compact,
\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma_{x}})=\left\{0\right\}\subset\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})
. So assume that
\Gamma
is locally dilation invariant at
x
. Then there is an
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\alpha\Gamma_{x}=\Gamma_{x}
. Let
\eta\colon\Gamma\to[0,1]
be a Lipschitz continuous function with support in
B(x,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma
that is equal to
1
in a neighbourhood of
x
. Let
\psi_{x}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an isometry with
\psi_{x}(x)=0
and
\psi_{x}(B(x,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma)=B(0,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma_{x}
. Define
\Psi_{x}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
by

\Psi_{x}\phi(y)=\begin{cases}\phi(\psi_{x}(y))&\text{if }y\in B(x,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma,\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

which has adjoint
\Psi_{x}^{\prime}:L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
given by

\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\phi(y)=\begin{cases}\phi(\psi_{x}^{-1}(y))&\text{if }y\in B(0,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma_{x},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\Psi_{x}
and
\Psi_{x}^{\prime}
are partial isometries with initial spaces
L^{2}\big(B(0,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma_{x}\big)
and
L^{2}\big(B(x,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma\big)
, respectively. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
the composition
\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta^{m}\Psi_{x}:L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
is the operation of multiplication by
(\eta^{\prime})^{m}
, where
\eta^{\prime}(y):=\eta(\psi^{-1}_{x}(y))
, for
y\in B(x,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma_{x}
,
\eta^{\prime}(y):=0
,
y\in\Gamma_{x}\setminus B(x,\delta(x))
.

Let
V_{\alpha}
be the unitary dilation as defined in (
4.2
) but with
\Gamma
replaced by
\Gamma_{x}
. Then

V_{\alpha}^{n}\eta^{\prime}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi(y)=\begin{cases}\eta(\psi_{x}^{-1}(\alpha^{n}y))\phi(y)&\text{if }\alpha^{n}y\in B(0,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma_{x},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
y\in\Gamma_{x}
and
\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
. By dominated convergence, it follows that

V_{\alpha}^{n}\eta^{\prime}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\to\phi
as
n\to\infty

for all
\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
. (We can see, similarly, that, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
V_{\alpha}^{n}(\eta^{\prime})^{m}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\to\phi
as
n\to\infty

for all
\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
.) Using that
\psi_{x}
is an isometry, we further observe that
\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta D_{\Gamma}\eta\Psi_{x}=\eta^{\prime}D_{\Gamma_{x}}\eta^{\prime}
. This implies, since also
D_{\Gamma_{x}}V_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha}D_{\Gamma_{x}}
, that

V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta D_{\Gamma}\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}=V_{\alpha}^{n}\eta^{\prime}D_{\Gamma_{x}}\eta^{\prime}V_{\alpha}^{-n}=(V_{\alpha}^{n}\eta^{\prime}V_{\alpha}^{-n})D_{\Gamma_{x}}(V_{\alpha}^{n}\eta^{\prime}V_{\alpha}^{-n})\to D_{\Gamma_{x}}

(5.3)

in the strong operator topology.

Now assume that
D_{\Gamma}-\lambda
is Fredholm and let
A\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
be a Fredholm regularizer of
D_{\Gamma}-\lambda
, so that the products
A(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)
and
(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)A
are compact perturbations of the identity. Then, for any
\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
,

\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta A\eta(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert+\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}(I-\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta A\eta(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta\Psi_{x})V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert.

(5.4)

The first term can be estimated as

\displaystyle\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta A\eta(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert\eta(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert,

where we used that
V_{\alpha}
is an isometry,
\Psi_{x}^{\prime}
is an isometry on the range of the multiplication operator
\eta
, and
\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert\leq 1
. By (
5.3
),

\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert\to\left\lVert(D_{\Gamma_{x}}-\lambda)\phi\right\rVert,

as
n\to\infty
. The second term in (
5.4
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}(I-\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta A\eta(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta\Psi_{x})V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}(I-\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta A(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta^{2}\Psi_{x})V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert+\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta A[\eta,D_{\Gamma}]\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}(I-\eta^{\prime 3})V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert+\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta(A(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)-I)\eta^{2}\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert+\left\lVert V_{\alpha}^{n}\Psi_{x}^{\prime}\eta A[\eta,D_{\Gamma}]\eta\Psi_{x}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\phi\right\rVert.

Note that both
A(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)-I
and
[\eta,D_{\Gamma}]
are compact. We also observe that for any compact operator
K\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
we have
V_{\alpha}^{n}KV_{\alpha}^{-n}\to 0
in the strong operator topology,
since
V_{\alpha}^{-n}\to 0
in the weak operator topology.
Therefore, and using that
V_{\alpha}^{n}\eta^{\prime 3}V_{\alpha}^{-n}\to I
, we obtain that the second term in (
5.4
) tends to zero as
n\to\infty
. We conclude that
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert(D_{\Gamma_{x}}-\lambda)\phi\right\rVert
for every
\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
. The same argument also shows that
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert(D_{\Gamma_{x}}-\lambda)^{\prime}\phi\right\rVert
for every
\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{x})
. It follows that
D_{\Gamma_{x}}-\lambda
is invertible and thus Fredholm.

Conversely, choose
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\Gamma
such that

\Gamma\subset\cup_{i=1}^{N}B(x_{i},\delta(x_{i}))
, and suppose that
D_{\Gamma_{x_{j}}}-\lambda
is Fredholm for
j=1,\ldots,N
, and let
A_{j}
be a Fredholm regularizer of
D_{\Gamma_{x_{j}}}-\lambda
.
For every
j
let
\psi_{x_{j}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an isometry with
\psi_{x_{j}}(x_{j})=0
and

\psi_{x_{j}}(B(x_{j},\delta(x_{j}))\cap\Gamma)=B(0,\delta(x_{j}))\cap\Gamma_{x_{j}}
.
Choose Lipschitz continuous functions
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{N}
such that
\left\{\eta_{j}^{2}:j=1,\ldots,N\right\}
is a partition of unity of
\Gamma
subordinate to the sets
B(x_{j},\delta(x_{j}))
. Now note that, since
D_{\Gamma_{x_{j}}}
essentially commutes with
\eta_{j}^{\prime}
,
A_{j}
essentially commutes with
\eta_{j}^{\prime}
as well. This implies that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Psi_{x_{j}}\eta_{j}^{\prime}A_{j}\Psi_{x_{j}}^{\prime}\eta_{j}(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)

\displaystyle\sim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Psi_{x_{j}}A_{j}\eta_{j}^{\prime}\Psi_{x_{j}}^{\prime}(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta_{j}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Psi_{x_{j}}A_{j}\Psi_{x_{j}}^{\prime}\eta_{j}(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\eta_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Psi_{x_{j}}A_{j}\eta_{j}^{\prime}(D_{\Gamma_{x_{j}}}-\lambda)\eta_{j}^{\prime}\Psi_{x_{j}}^{\prime}\sim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Psi_{x_{j}}\eta_{j}^{\prime}A_{j}(D_{\Gamma_{x_{j}}}-\lambda)\eta_{j}^{\prime}\Psi_{x_{j}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Psi_{x_{j}}\eta_{j}^{\prime 2}\Psi_{x_{j}}^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\eta_{j}^{2}=I.

Similarly,
(D_{\Gamma}-\lambda)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\eta_{j}\Psi_{x_{j}}A_{j}\eta_{j}^{\prime}\Psi_{x_{j}}^{\prime}\sim I
. Thus
D_{\Gamma}-\lambda
is Fredholm.
∎

5.2
Deformations

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
. We will now consider the situation where a Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{-}
with boundary
\Gamma
is deformed by a
C^{1,\beta}
-diffeomorphism

\psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is conformal or anti-conformal at a point
x\in\Gamma
, meaning that the Jacobian
J\psi(x)
of
\psi
at
x
lies in
\mathbb{R}O(d)
, where
\mathbb{R}O(d):=\left\{\lambda A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R},A\text{ orthogonal}\right\}
.
Due to the invariance of the double-layer potential under transformations from
\mathbb{R}O(d)
, we will for our purposes be able to assume that
J\psi(x)=I
. Let
D_{\Gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the kernel of
D_{\Gamma}
. Likewise,
V_{j}
will be the integral operator with kernel
V_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
|D_{\Gamma}|
will be the integral operator with kernel
|D_{\Gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)|
, and so on. We write

\displaystyle D_{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(z),\psi(y))

\displaystyle=D_{\Gamma}(z,y)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{D_{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(z),\psi(y))-\frac{(z-y)\cdot n^{\Gamma}(y)}{c_{d}|\psi(z)-\psi(y)|^{d}}n^{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(y))\cdot J\psi(y)n^{\Gamma}(y)}_{V_{1}(z,y)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{D_{\Gamma}(z,y)(n^{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(y))\cdot J\psi(y)n^{\Gamma}(y)-1)}_{V_{2}(z,y)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{D_{\Gamma}(z,y)n^{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(y))\cdot J\psi(y)n^{\Gamma}(y)\left[\frac{|z-y|^{d}}{|\psi(z)-\psi(y)|^{d}}-1\right]}_{V_{3}(z,y)}.

(5.5)

The term
V_{1}
is weakly singular, satisfying an estimate of the form
|V_{1}(z,y)|\leq C_{\beta}|z-y|^{1+\beta-d}
for
\beta\in(0,1)
and almost every
z,y\in\Gamma
, see
[
43
, Lemma 6]
. (In order to apply Medková’s results from
[
43
]
, note that the reduced boundary
\partial_{\ast}\Omega_{-}\subset\Gamma
has full surface measure for a Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{-}
, cf.
[
17
, p. 209]
.) Therefore the operator
V_{1}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
is compact.

We view
V_{2}
as the composition of
D_{\Gamma}
with a multiplication operator, noting that by
[
43
, Lemma 3]
we have

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\esssup\limits_{y\in\Gamma\cap B(x,\rho)}|n^{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(y))\cdot J\psi(y)n^{\Gamma}(y)-1|=0.

In particular, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\rho>0
such that the operator norm of

V_{2}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma\cap B(x,\rho))\to L^{2}(\Gamma)

is smaller than
\varepsilon
.

For a bounded Lipschitz domain with boundary
\Gamma
, let
\mathscr{A}_{2}=\mathscr{A}_{2}(\Gamma)
denote the algebra of integral operators
K
such that
|K|\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
is bounded.
We will work within the class of domains such that
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
in this subsection. The preceding considerations show that
D_{\psi(\Gamma)}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}(\psi(\Gamma))
if
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}(\Gamma)
. Since the relationship between
\Gamma
and
\psi(\Gamma)
is symmetric, we see that

D_{\psi(\Gamma)}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}(\psi(\Gamma))\textrm{ if and only if }D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}(\Gamma).

(5.6)

Note that
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
for any
C^{1,\beta}
-boundary
\Gamma
, since
D_{\Gamma}
has a weakly singular kernel in this case.
It is also known that
|D_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}|\colon L^{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma})\to L^{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
is bounded for any of the following
graphs

\widetilde{\Gamma}
:

(i)

a 2D wedge (modelling a polygonal corner)
[
48
]
;

(ii)

a 3D wedge (modelling a polyhedral edge)
[
51
]
;

(iii)

a 3D polyhedral cone
[
16
]
;

(iv)

a 3D smooth cone (such as a circular cone)
[
52
]
.

Furthermore, this statement may be extended to dilation-invariant domains which are built from such graphs.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is a dilation-invariant graph, i.e.,
\widetilde{\Gamma}=\alpha\widetilde{\Gamma}
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and suppose that, at every
x\in\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is either locally
C^{1,\beta}
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
, or coincides locally with a graph of type (i), (ii), (iii), or (iv). Then
|D_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}|
is bounded on
L^{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

By hypothesis, we know that
\left\lvert D_{j}\right\rvert\colon L^{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0})\to L^{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0})
is bounded for
j=-1,0,1
, where
D_{j}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
are as in §
4.1
. The lemma therefore follows from Proposition
4.3
and (
4.4
).
∎

A simple localization argument now demonstrates that the condition
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
is quite general.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that at every
x\in\Gamma
,
\Gamma
is locally
C^{1,\beta}
, for some
\beta\in(0,1)
, or coincides locally
with a graph satisfying the hypothesis of Lemma
5.3
. Then
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}(\Gamma)
.

Returning to the decomposition of
D_{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(z),\psi(y))
, we note, for the third term
V_{3}
, the following consequence of the fact that
J\psi(x)=I
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
. Then

|V_{3}|(z,y)=|D_{\Gamma}|(z,y)Q(z,y)
,
where
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
is non-negative and

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\esssup_{y,z\in\Gamma\cap B(x,\rho)}Q(z,y)=0
,
where the essential supremum is taken with respect to the product measure on
\Gamma\times\Gamma
.

Let
\eta_{\rho}\colon\Gamma\to[0,1]
be a Lipschitz continuous function with
\supp\eta_{\rho}\subset B(x,\rho)
such that
\eta_{\rho}
is equal to
1
in a neighbourhood of
x
. Furthermore, denote the operator of composition with
\psi
by

C_{\psi}\colon L^{2}(\psi(\Gamma))\to L^{2}(\Gamma),\hskip 10.00002ptC_{\psi}\phi(z)=\phi(\psi(z)),

and let
\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}=\eta_{\rho}\circ\psi^{-1}
. Note also that we have the change of variables formula

\int_{\psi(\Gamma)}\phi(y)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\psi(\Gamma)}(y)=\int_{\Gamma}(1+T(y))\phi(\psi(y))\,\mathrm{d}s_{\Gamma}(y),

(5.7)

where
T\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and
\lim\limits_{y\to x}T(y)=0
. The operator

\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}D_{\psi(\Gamma)}\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}\colon L^{2}(\psi(\Gamma))\to L^{2}(\psi(\Gamma))

is then similar to

C_{\psi}\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}D_{\psi(\Gamma)}\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}C_{\psi^{-1}}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma),

which, by (
5.7
), is an integral operator with kernel

(1+T(y))\eta_{\rho}(z)D_{\psi(\Gamma)}(\psi(z),\psi(y))\eta_{\rho}(y)
.
By the previous calculations, if
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
, we therefore find that

C_{\psi}\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}D_{\psi(\Gamma)}\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}C_{\psi^{-1}}\sim\eta_{\rho}D_{\Gamma}\eta_{\rho}+\eta_{\rho}\tilde{V}\eta_{\rho},

(5.8)

where
\tilde{V}
is an integral operator induced by a kernel of the form
\tilde{V}(z,y)=D_{\Gamma}(z,y)\tilde{Q}(z,y)
with
\tilde{Q}\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
such that

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\esssup\limits_{y,z\in\Gamma\cap B(x,\rho)}|\tilde{Q}(z,y)|=0.

(5.9)

For
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
with support in
B(x,\rho)
we have

\left\lvert\left\langle\tilde{V}\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}\left\lvert\phi_{2}(z)\tilde{V}(z,y)\phi_{1}(y)\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}s(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(z)\leq\esssup\limits_{y,z\in\Gamma\cap B(x,\rho)}|\tilde{Q}(z,y)|\left\lVert\left\lvert D_{\Gamma}\right\rvert\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi_{2}\right\rVert.

Since
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
, (
5.9
) implies that

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\|\eta_{\rho}\tilde{V}\eta_{\rho}\|=0.

(5.10)

Taking powers, we conclude that

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\|(\eta_{\rho}D_{\Gamma}\eta_{\rho})^{n}-C_{\psi}(\eta_{\rho}^{\prime}D_{\psi(\Gamma)}\eta_{\rho}^{\prime})^{n}C_{\psi^{-1}}\|_{\ess}=0

(5.11)

for every
n\geq 1
.

We now prove the main theorem of this section. When
R=1/2
, it says the following: if, for every point
x
,
\Gamma
is locally obtained by deforming a domain that satisfies the spectral radius conjecture, then
\Gamma
satisfies the spectral radius conjecture as well.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
R>0
and
\beta>0
. Suppose that for every
x\in\Gamma
there exists a
C^{1,\beta}
-diffeomorphism
\psi_{x}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, conformal or anti-conformal at
x
, and a Lipschitz cut-off function
\eta_{x}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
such that
\eta_{x}
is equal to
1
in a neighbourhood of
x
,
D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
, and

\sigma_{\ess}(\eta_{x}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\eta_{x}^{\prime})\subset B(0,R),

where
\eta_{x}^{\prime}=\eta_{x}\circ\psi_{x}^{-1}
. Then
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
and

\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})\subset B(0,R)
.

Proof.

As noted previously, the condition on
\Gamma
, even for just one
x
, implies that
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
.

Fix
x\in\Gamma
. Since the commutator
[D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)},\eta_{x}^{\prime}]
is compact, we see that
(\eta_{x}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\eta_{x}^{\prime})^{n}-\eta_{x}^{\prime n}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}^{n}\eta_{x}^{\prime n}
is compact for every
n\geq 1
. By the hypothesis, choose
n=n(x)
sufficiently large so that

\|(\eta_{x}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\eta_{x}^{\prime})^{n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}=\|\eta_{x}^{\prime n}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}^{n}\eta_{x}^{\prime n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}<R\|C_{\psi_{x}}\|^{-\frac{1}{n}}\|C_{\psi_{x}^{-1}}\|^{-\frac{1}{n}}.

If we choose a new cut-off function
\tilde{\eta}_{x}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
such that
\supp\tilde{\eta}_{x}\subset\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\eta_{x}(y)=1\right\}
, note that, since
(\eta_{x}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\eta_{x}^{\prime})^{n}-\eta_{x}^{\prime n}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}^{n}\eta_{x}^{\prime n}
is compact, we have

\|(\tilde{\eta}_{x}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\tilde{\eta}_{x}^{\prime})^{n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}=\|\tilde{\eta}_{x}^{\prime n}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}^{n}\tilde{\eta}_{x}^{\prime n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\|\eta_{x}^{\prime n}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}^{n}\eta_{x}^{\prime n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}<R\|C_{\psi_{x}}\|^{-\frac{1}{n}}\|C_{\psi_{x}^{-1}}\|^{-\frac{1}{n}},

so that

\|C_{\psi_{x}}(\tilde{\eta}_{x}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\tilde{\eta}_{x}^{\prime})^{n}C_{\psi_{x}^{-1}}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\|\eta_{x}^{\prime n}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}^{n}\eta_{x}^{\prime n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}\|C_{\psi_{x}}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\|C_{\psi_{x}^{-1}}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}<R.

Hence, by making the support of
\eta_{x}
sufficiently small and applying (
5.11
), we obtain that

\|(\eta_{x}D_{\Gamma}\eta_{x})^{n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}=\|\eta_{x}^{n}D_{\Gamma}^{n}\eta_{x}^{n}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n}}<R.

Now choose
\delta(x)
sufficiently small so that
\eta_{x}\equiv 1
in a neighbourhood of
B(x,\delta(x))\cap\Gamma
. By compactness, we may pick points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
so that

\Gamma\subset\cup_{j=1}^{N}B(x_{j},\delta(x_{j}))
.
Let

r:=\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}\|(\eta_{x_{j}}D_{\Gamma}\eta_{x_{j}})^{n(x_{j})}\|_{\ess}^{\frac{1}{n(x_{j})}}<R

and let
\left\{\tau_{j}:j=1,\ldots,N\right\}
be a Lipschitz partition of unity of
\Gamma
subordinate to the sets
B(x_{j},\delta(x_{j}))
. Choose
m
to be a large integer such that
n(x_{j})
divides
m
for all
j
. Then

D^{m}_{\Gamma}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}D_{\Gamma}^{m}=\sum\tau_{j}\eta_{x_{j}}^{2m}D_{\Gamma}^{m}

and therefore, recalling that
[D_{\Gamma},\eta_{x_{j}}]
is compact,

\displaystyle\|D_{\Gamma}^{m}\|_{\ess}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}(\eta_{x_{j}}D_{\Gamma}\eta_{x_{j}})^{m}\Bigg\|_{\ess}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert(\eta_{x_{j}}D_{\Gamma}\eta_{x_{j}})^{m}\right\rVert_{\ess}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert(\eta_{x_{j}}D_{\Gamma}\eta_{x_{j}})^{n(x_{j})}\right\rVert^{\frac{m}{n(x_{j})}}_{\ess}

Therefore

.

Referring back to Lemma

, all graphs

of type (i)-(iii) satisfy (the graph version of) the spectral radius conjecture, results which can also be found in the corresponding references. Graphs of type (iv) that are convex also satisfy the spectral radius conjecture, by
[

]
. If

is a compactly supported Lipschitz function, we thus have that

for

sufficiently large. Applying Theorem

we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
d=3
and let
\Gamma
be the boundary of a Lipschitz domain. Assume that for every
x\in\Gamma
there exists a

-diffeomorphism

, conformal or anti-conformal at

, such that
\psi_{x}(\Gamma)
is locally a subset of the boundary of a polyhedral cone or a convex smooth cone. Then

satisfies the spectral radius conjecture.

The class of domains considered in Corollary

encompasses all domains in 3D that may reasonably be referred to as a Lipschitz curvilinear polyhedra. From Theorem
5.6
we of course also obtain the analogous corollary for 2D domains, a well known result; the corresponding class of curves precisely describes the
C^{1,\beta}
-curvilinear polygons in 2D.

5.3
Possible extensions and further questions

Theorem
5.6
in particular shows that the spectral radius conjecture only depends on the local behaviour of
\Gamma
, under the extraneous assumption that
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
. To achieve the same result for a general Lipschitz domain, we need a different tool to treat the term
V_{3}
which does not rely on the introduction of absolute values. Since
V_{3}(z,y)=\widetilde{Q}(z,y)D_{\Gamma}(z,y)
, for a specific kernel
\widetilde{Q}(z,y)
, we would like an answer to the following question.

Question 5.8
.

Describe suitable classes of kernels
B
such that the kernel
B(z,y)D_{\Gamma}(z,y)
defines a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\Gamma)
, and estimate the norm of the corresponding operator.

When
B
is of the form
B(z,y)=a(z)-a(y)
, for some function
a
on
\Gamma
, the resulting operator is the commutator
[a,D_{\Gamma}]
. Commutators of singular integral operators are very well studied, but we have been unable to identify or correctly apply existing results to the kernel multiplier of interest to us, namely,

B(z,y)=\eta_{\rho}(z)\left(\frac{|z-y|^{d}}{|\psi(z)-\psi(y)|^{d}}-1\right)\eta_{\rho}(y).

Other versions of Question
5.8
also seem interesting. For example, one could ask for the stronger property that
B(z,y)
be a kernel multiplier of each of the Riesz transforms of
\Gamma
. We also note the similarity between the term
V_{3}
and the kernel of the Clifford–Cauchy integral operator, as presented in
[
4
, Consequence 3.6]
.

Of course, one would like to know that not only the spectral radius conjecture is local, but that the entire essential spectrum is as well.

Question 5.9
.

For a domain
\Omega_{-}
with boundary
\Gamma
, is the essential spectrum local and invariant under locally conformal deformations?

We can give a positive answer to this question if we, in addition to the hypotheses of Theorem
5.6
, assume that we do not have too many singular points, by which we mean points where the boundary is not
C^{1}
. In the statement, we let
\chi_{x,\rho}:=\chi_{B(x,\rho)}
and
\chi_{x,\rho}^{\prime}:=\chi_{x,\rho}\circ\psi_{x}^{-1}
for
x\in\Gamma
and
\rho>0
.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\beta>0
and assume that
\Gamma
has at most countably many singular points. Further suppose that for every
x\in\Gamma
there exists a
C^{1,\beta}
-diffeomorphism
\psi_{x}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, conformal or anti-conformal at
x
, such that
D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
. Then
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
and

\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})\subset\bigcup\limits_{x\in\Gamma}\sigma_{\ess}(\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime})

for arbitrary
\rho_{x}>0
. Moreover, there exist
\rho_{x}>0
such that

\sigma_{\ess}(D_{\Gamma})=\bigcup\limits_{x\in\Gamma}\sigma_{\ess}(\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}).

Proof.

By (
5.6
),
D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
for just one
x\in\Gamma
already implies
D_{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
.

Let
x\in\Gamma
,
\rho_{x}>0
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. If
\lambda=0
, then
0\in\sigma_{\ess}(\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime})
either by (
5.1
) (if
\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}\equiv 1
) or by construction (otherwise). So assume that
\lambda\neq 0
and suppose that
\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}-\lambda
is Fredholm for every
x\in\Gamma
.

Fix
x
for a moment. As
\Gamma
has at most countably many singular points and
\psi_{x}
is a diffeomorphism, we know that
\chi_{x,\tilde{\rho}_{x}}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\chi_{x,\tilde{\rho}_{x}}^{\prime}-\lambda
is Fredholm for all but at most countably many
\tilde{\rho}_{x}\in(0,\rho_{x}]
. In this case the essential norm of the Fredholm regularizer
A_{x,\tilde{\rho}_{x}}
of
\chi_{x,\tilde{\rho}_{x}}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\chi_{x,\tilde{\rho}_{x}}^{\prime}-\lambda
is bounded by

\left\lVert A_{x,\tilde{\rho_{x}}}\right\rVert_{\ess}\leq\max\left\{\left\lVert A_{x,\rho_{x}}\right\rVert_{\ess},\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert^{-1}\right\}.

Therefore, by (
5.8
) and (
5.10
), for every
x\in\Gamma
we can choose
\tilde{\rho}_{x}
sufficiently small such that
\chi_{x,\tilde{\rho}_{x}}D_{\Gamma}\chi_{x,\tilde{\rho}_{x}}-\lambda
is also Fredholm, and such that
\partial B(x,\tilde{\rho}_{x})
contains none of the singularities of
\Gamma
.

By compactness, we may choose finitely many points
x_{j}
and corresponding radii
\tilde{\rho}_{x_{j}}
such that the balls
B(x_{j},\tilde{\rho}_{x_{j}})
cover
\Gamma
. Define recursively

E_{1}:=B(x_{1},\tilde{\rho}_{x_{1}})\cap\Gamma,\hskip 10.00002ptE_{j+1}=\big(B(x_{j+1},\tilde{\rho}_{x_{j+1}})\cap\Gamma\big)\setminus\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{j}E_{k}.

Now we can apply (
5.2
) to obtain that
D_{\Gamma}-\lambda
is Fredholm.

Conversely, assume that
D_{\Gamma}-\lambda
is Fredholm and fix
x\in\Gamma
. By (
5.1
), we must have
\lambda\neq 0
. As we only have countably many singularities,
\chi_{x,\rho}D_{\Gamma}\chi_{x,\rho}-\lambda
is also Fredholm for all but at most countably many
\rho>0
by (
5.2
). Now note that the situation between
D_{\Gamma}
and
D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}
is symmetric. Hence, the same argument as above shows that
\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}D_{\psi_{x}(\Gamma)}\chi_{x,\rho_{x}}^{\prime}-\lambda
is also Fredholm for a sufficiently small
\rho_{x}>0
.
∎

In particular, we may extend Theorem

to domains that are only

locally dilation invariant in the following sense.

.

Let
\beta>0
and assume that
\Gamma

. Suppose that for every

there exists a

-diffeomorphism

Remark 6.2

(

.

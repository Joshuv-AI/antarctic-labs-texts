Explaining high-braking indice of magnetars SGR 0501 + + 4516 and 1E 2259 + + 586 using the double magnetic-dipole model Publication type: Article Type
Fang-Zhou Yan, Zhi-Fu Gao*, Wen-Shen Yang, Ai-Jun Dong

Abstract
In this paper, we attribute high braking indices n > 3 n>3 of two magnetars SGR 0501 + + 4516 and 1E 2259 + + 586 to the decrease in their inclination angles using the double magnetic-dipole model proposed by [ 11 ] . In this model, there are two magnetic moments inside a neutron star, one is generated by the rotation effect of a charged sphere, M 1 M_{1} , and the other is generated by the magnetization of ferromagnetically ordered material, M 2 M_{2} . Our calculations indicate that the magnetic moment M 2 M_{2} would evolve towards alignment with the spin axis of the two magnetars, and cause their magnetic inclination angles to decrease. We also define a ratio η = M 2 / M 1 \eta=M_{2}/M_{1} , which reflects the magnetization degree, and find that the values of η \eta of the two magnetars are about two-orders of magnitude higher than that of rotationally powered pulsar PSR J1640-4631 with n = 3.15 ​ ( 3 ) n=3.15(3) , assuming that they have the same rate of decrease in their inclination angles.

Introduction

The spin-down evolution of pulsars has become an important research hot spot in the field of compact objects
(
Dang \BOthers. \APACyear2020
;
Kou \BOthers. \APACyear2018
;
Magalhaes \BOthers. \APACyear2012
;
Magalhaes \BOthers. \APACyear2016
;
Oliveira \BOthers. \APACyear2018
;
Wen, Chen\BCBL \BOthers. \APACyear2020
;
Yan, Manchester, van Straten\BCBL \BOthers. \APACyear2011
;
Yan, Manchester, Hobbs\BCBL \BOthers. \APACyear2011
;
Yan \BOthers. \APACyear2018
;
Zhao \APACyear2019
;
Zhao \APACyear2020
)
.
Allen \BBA Horvath \APACyear1997
proposed that the long-term slowdown of a pulsar’s rotation follows a power-law form
\tau_{\rm ext}\propto\Omega^{n}
with
\tau_{\rm ext}
being the external torque acting on the crust,
\Omega
the rotational angular velocity, and
n
the braking index, and gave an expression

\dot{\Omega}=-K\Omega^{n},

(1)

where
\dot{\Omega}
is the derivative of
\Omega
, and
K
is a positive proportional parameter. The braking index of a pulsar is defined as

n=\frac{\Omega\ddot{\Omega}}{\dot{\Omega}^{2}}=\frac{\nu\ddot{\nu}}{\dot{\nu}^{2}}=2-\frac{P\ddot{P}}{\dot{P}^{2}},

(2)

where
\ddot{\Omega}
is the second derivative of
\Omega
,
\nu=\Omega/2\pi
the spin frequency, and
P=1/\nu
the spin period. A pure magneto-dipole radiation (MDR) model predicts the braking index
n=3
. However, the observed braking indices of pulsars always deviate from three predicted by the pure MDR model.
Michel \BBA Goldwire \APACyear1970
proposed the origin of the alignment and slowing down torque with the corresponding equations of motion

I\dot{\alpha}=-\frac{2M^{2}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\Omega^{2}}{3c^{3}}

(3)

when pulsars are slowed down, where
I
is the star’s moment of inertia,
\alpha
is the inclination angle between the rotation and magnetic axes,
\dot{\alpha}
is the derivative of
\alpha
,
M=B_{d}R^{3}
is the magnetic moment (
B_{d}
is the surface dipole magnetic field strength at the equator, and
R
the radius of the star), and
c
is the speed of light in vacuum. In addition, a similar model of the alignment or counteralignment of the magnetic axis of a pulsar with its spin axis was proposed by
Macy \APACyear1974
. A change in the inclination
angle
\alpha
of a pulsar is usually companied with a change in
pulse profiles
(
Wen \BOthers. \APACyear2016\APACexlab\BCnt1
;
Wen \BOthers. \APACyear2016\APACexlab\BCnt2
;
Yan \BOthers. \APACyear2019
;
Yan \BOthers. \APACyear2020
;
Yuan \BOthers. \APACyear2017
;
Wen, Yan\BCBL \BBA Yuan \APACyear2020
)
. A dipole rotating in
vacuum is subject to the radiation field torque
(
Davis \BBA Goldstein \APACyear1970
)

I\dot{\Omega}=-\frac{2M^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha\Omega^{3}}{3c^{3}}

(4)

The starting point of this work comes from
Michel \BBA Goldwire \APACyear1970
,
Macy \APACyear1974
and
Allen \BBA Horvath \APACyear1997
. The so-called “vacuum model” includes the alignment torque and radiation field torque, described by Eq. (3) and
Eq. (4), respectively, and allows a change in
\alpha
and/or a change in the magnetic momentum
M
of a pulsar. From
Eq (4), we get the second derivative of
\Omega
,

\displaystyle\ddot{\Omega}=-\frac{2}{3c^{3}}(3M^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha\Omega^{2}\dot{\Omega}+

\displaystyle~~~~2M\dot{M}\sin^{2}\alpha\Omega^{3}+2M^{2}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\dot{\alpha}\Omega^{3}).

(5)

Substituting Eqs.(3),(4) and (5) into Eq. (2), the observed braking index
n_{\rm obs}
in vacuum model is expressed as

n_{\rm obs}=3+\frac{2\Omega}{\dot{\Omega}}(\frac{\dot{\alpha}}{\tan\alpha}+\frac{\dot{M}}{M}).

(6)

If we ignore the change in
M
, the above equation becomes

n_{\rm obs}=3+\frac{2\Omega}{\dot{\Omega}}\frac{\dot{\alpha}}{\tan\alpha}=3+\frac{2\nu}{\dot{\nu}}\frac{\dot{\alpha}}{\tan\alpha}.

(7)

The rotationally powered pulsar PSR J1640
-
4631 has

\nu
=4.843 Hz and
\dot{\nu}=-2.28\times 10^{-11}
Hz s
-1

(Archibald et al. 2016), implying a characteristic age of

\tau_{c}=-\nu/2\dot{\nu}
=3370 yr and surface dipole field
B_{d}=1.43\times 10^{13}I^{1/2}_{45}
G, where
I_{45}
is the
moment of inertia in units of
10^{45}
g cm
2

(
Gotthelf \BOthers. \APACyear2014
)
. It is the first to have a braking index greater
than 3,
n=3.15(3)
(the number in parentheses denotes the error)
(
Archibald \BOthers. \APACyear2016
)
, measured with
high precision.
Ekşi \BOthers. \APACyear2016
explained the braking index of PSR J1640-4631
in plasma-filled model and inferred the change in the inclination angle to be at the rate
\dot{\alpha}=-0.23(5)^{\circ}
/century, about 2.5 times smaller in absolute value than the rate
\dot{\alpha}=-0.56^{\circ}
/century of the Crab pulsar through 45 years of observations
(
Lyne \BOthers. \APACyear2015
)
.

Because of lack of long-term pulsed emission in quiescence and strong timing noise, it is impossible to directly measure the braking index of a magnetar, powered by superhigh multiple magnetic fields. Based on the estimated ages of their potentially associated supernova remnants (SNR) and the timing parameter,
Gao \BOthers. \APACyear2016
measured the mean braking indices of
n=13(4)
, 6.3(1.7) and 32(10) for 1E 1841-045, SGR 0501
+
4516 and 1E 2259
+
586, respectively, using the expression
n=1+P/(\dot{P}t_{SNR})
, where
t_{SNR}
is the SNR age. Since the uncertainty in estimating the SNR age of 1E 1841-045 is too large, 1E 1841-045 will not be considered in this work. The high braking indices of pulsars have been explained by different models (see
Gao \BOthers. \APACyear2017
for a brief review). We attribute high braking indices
n>3
of SGR 0501
+
4516 and 1E 2259
+
586 to the decrease in their inclination angles, and estimate their initial magnetic moments, initial inclination angles by using a novel double magnetic-dipole model proposed by
Hamil \BOthers. \APACyear2016
(hereafter the HSS model).

2
The HSS model

The pulsar’s magnetic field was thought to originate from
the following mechanisms: the dynamo effect and the magnetization of a ferromagnetic core in the liquid interior of neutron stars (NSs)
(
Sliverstein \APACyear1969
)
. Since there is no internal source of energy to generate convection and activate dynamo, it is very difficult to produce a continuous dynamo action in the pulsar. In the HSS model, the pulsar has two dipoles with magnetic moments
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, and the distance between two dipole centers is a constant
r
. The second magnetic moment
M_{2}
is thought to be off-centered dipole, and the inclination angle between the
M_{1}
and the dipole-dipole axis is
\theta_{1}
. Because the NS interior is in superfluidity phase, the friction received by the rotational region supporting
M_{2}
is neglected. Due to the interaction between two dipoles, the change rate of
\theta_{2}
between the
M_{2}
and the dipole-dipole axis is

\dot{\theta_{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2M_{1}M_{2}}{I_{2}r^{3}}F(\Theta)},

(8)

where

F(\Theta)=(sin\theta_{1i}\sin\theta_{2i}-2\cos\theta_{1i}\cos\theta_{2i})-(\sin\theta_{1f}\sin\theta_{2f}-2\cos\theta_{1f}\cos\theta_{2f})
,
I_{2}
is the moment of inertia of the rotating region,
i
and
f
represent the initial and final values, respectively.

During the motion of the
M_{2}
, its potential energy would convert into the kinetic energy. By taking
C=0

in Eq. (10) of
Hamil \BOthers. \APACyear2016
, we can obtain the direction of
M_{2}
when its potential
energy is minimum,

\theta_{2}^{\rm min}=-\arctan(\frac{\tan\theta_{1}}{2}).

(9)

From, Eq.(9), it is obvious that
\theta_{2}^{\rm min}
should be negative, meaning that the minimum energy position should locate the clockwise direction of the dipole-dipole axis. For an arbitrary initial
\theta_{2}
, the physical law indicates that the
M_{2}
will freely rotate around the minimum energy position, which is similar to a simple pendulum.

According to the geometric relationship, the inclination angle and its change rate are determined by

~~~~~\alpha=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2},~~~{\rm and}~~\dot{\alpha}=-\dot{\theta}_{2},

(10)

respectively, where
\theta_{1}
keeps constant, positive (negative) angles
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are measured from the dipole-dipole axis counterclockwise (clockwise), the rotation axis is taken as
\alpha=0
, an increase (decrease) in
\alpha
and a consequent
movement away (toward) the axis of rotation.
In this work, for simplicity, we take range of
-90^{\circ}<\theta_{2}<0
in our simulation.

From Eq. (13), it is obvious that the degree of magnetization of a magnetar is far higher that that of a common radio pulsar. The above equation implies that there is a difference in the magnetic field formation mechanism (including paramagnetic magnetization) between rotationally powered pulsars and magnetic-field powered magnetars during their early ferromagnetic phases. However, the cause of such a difference is not yet known to date. I hope that this work will be helpful in understanding the nature of magnatars.

4
Summary

We have applied a toy two-dipole model to the two high-
n
magnetars SGR0501
+
4516 and 1E 2259
+
586, and ignored other factors influencing their braking indices, which are attributed to the decrease in inclination angles. For these two sources, their initial magnetic moments, initial inclination angles and average decrease rates of inclination angles are estimated and comparisons with rotationally powered pulsars are presented. Our results could be useful in understanding the nature of magnetars, and will be tested by future observations.

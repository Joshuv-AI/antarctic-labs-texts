A regularity criterion for a 3D tropical climate model with damping
Diego Berti ∗, Luca Bisconti ∗, Davide Catania ∗∗

Abstract
. In this paper we deal with the 3D tropical climate model with damping terms in the equation of the barotropic mode u u and in the equation of the first baroclinic mode v v of the velocity, and we establish a regularity criterion for this system thanks to which the local smooth solution ( u , v , θ ) (u,v,\theta) can actually be extended globally in time.

1.
Introduction

We consider the following 3D tropical climate model with damping, i.e.

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nu\Delta u+\sigma_{1}|u|^{\alpha-1}u+\nabla\pi+\mathrm{div}\,(v\otimes v)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+(u\cdot\nabla)v-\eta\Delta v+\sigma_{2}|v|^{\beta-1}v+(v\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\theta=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta+(u\cdot\nabla)\theta-\mu\Delta\theta+\mathrm{div}\,v=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,u=0,

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0},\,\,v(x,0)=v_{0},\,\,\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0},

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
t\geq 0
,
u=(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t),u_{3}(x,t))

and
v=(v_{1}(x,t),v_{2}(x,t),v_{3}(x,t))
denote the barotropic mode
and the first baroclinic mode of the velocity, respectively,

\pi=\pi(x,t)
indicates the pressure,
\theta=\theta(x,t)
the
temperature, and
\nu>0
,
\eta>0
and
\mu>0
. Here,

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>0
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 1
are the damping
coefficients (further appropriate restrictions will be introduced later on).

When
\nu=\eta=\mu=0
(and
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=0
), the above system gives the original
tropical climate model derived by Frierson, Majda and Pauluis

[
10
]
(see also
[
28
]
). Instead, in the case of
\nu>0
,
\eta>0
and

\mu=0
, (
1
) reduces to the viscous version of the
same model that has been analyzed by Li and Titi
[
22
]
(see also
[
23
]
).

Local existence of strong solutions to the considered 3D model, without damping, and with

\nu=1
,
\eta=1
and
\mu=0
, has been established by Ma, Jiang and
Wan in
[
26
]
. Global well-posedness of solutions to a
tropical climate model with dissipation in the equation of the first
baroclinic mode of the velocity, under the hypotheses of small initial
data, was studied by Wan
[
31
]
, Ma and Wan
[
27
]
.
In fact, the issue of global regularity has been investigated in a
number of articles and (partially) addressed, by introducing
suitable hypotheses on the initial data, modified viscosity and diffusivity, or
by inserting damping terms in the equations for
u
,
\nu
, and
\theta
.
Some of these studies, whose contents are related to the present analysis,
are briefly recalled here below.

For the 3D case with damping, Yuan and Chen
[
36
]

studied the global regularity of strong solutions assuming

\sigma_{1}>0
and
\alpha\geq 4
, and removing the damping term from
the equation
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{2}
, i.e. setting
\sigma_{2}=0
(see also
Yuan and Zhang, in
[
37
]
). In

[
37
]
, the authors proved global regularity assuming that
one of the following three conditions holds true:

(i)

\displaystyle\qquad\alpha,\beta\geq 4,

(ii)

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{7}{2}\leq\alpha<4,\,\,\,\,\beta\geq\frac{5\alpha+7}{2\alpha},\,\,\,\,\varpi\geq\frac{7}{2\alpha-5},

(iii)

\displaystyle\qquad 3<\alpha\leq\frac{7}{2},\,\,\,\,\beta,\varpi\geq\frac{7}{2\alpha-5},

where
\varpi>0
refers to an extra damping term,
i.e.
\sigma_{3}|\theta|^{\varpi-1}\theta
, with
\sigma_{3}\geq 0
, inserted in the left-hand
side of
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{3}
.

In
[
7
]
the authors analyze the
d
-dimensional
(
1
),
d=2,3
, without damping terms, with only the
standard dissipation (i.e.
-\eta\Delta v
) of the first baroclinic
model of the velocity (and substituting
-\nu\Delta u
with
\nu u
as
well as
-\mu\Delta\theta
with
\mu\theta
) and choosing a special class of initial data

(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})
with
H^{s}
-norm arbitrarily large. Let us also
recall the recent papers
[
24
,
25
]
where the
authors prove well-posedness for the tropical climate model upon
selecting special classes of initial data.

We also mention an article of Zhu
[
40
]
, in which the 3D system
(
1
) is considered with
\sigma_{i}=0
,
i=1,2,3
, and
fractional diffusion on the barotropic mode, with initial data in

H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The author proves global existence of strong solutions

(u,v,\theta)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, for any
T>0
,
removing
-\eta\Delta v
in
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{2}
, and replacing

-\nu\Delta u
with
\nu\Lambda^{2\chi}u=\nu(-\Delta^{1/2})^{2\chi}u
in

\eqref{TCM-gen}_{1}
, with
\chi\geq 5/2
.

In
[
32
]
the authors give a regularity criterion on the gradient of
u
for the
system (
1
), assuming
\sigma_{i}=0
,
i=1,2,3
, and initial data in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
A further regularity criterion, for the local-in-time smooth solution to the 3D
tropical climate model in the Morrey–Campanato space, is given in
[
33
]
.

Analyses yielding criteria similar to the one developed in the present
work are carried out in
[
9
]
for the 3D MHD equations
(see also
[
2
,
13
,
11
]
). We also mention some
regularity criteria for the 3D Boussineq equations having connection
with our analysis, i.e.
[
30
,
34
,
38
,
39
]
.

In the present paper we consider problem (
1
) with

3\leq\alpha,\beta<4
, and
(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})\in H^{s}
,
3/2<s\leq 2
,
providing a regularity criterion to obtain the smoothness of the
solutions. This case, which is rather far from verifying the
hypotheses used to prove global existence in the previous works,
highlights somehow the relevance of the choice
\alpha\geq 4
in
order to obtain smooth solutions: without such a condition, the
introduction of suitable constraints seems to be necessary in order to
obtain regular global solutions.

In our main result (see Theorem
2.1
, below) we provide a
regularity criterion involving the barotropic mode and the first
baroclinic mode of the velocity in the homogeneous Besov space

{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}
(see, e.g.
[
19
,
20
]
), that is: if

\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|u(t)\|^{\delta}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|v(t)\|^{\gamma}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\,dt<\infty,

with
\delta=\delta(\alpha)
and
\gamma=\gamma(\beta)
defined in
(
10
) below, then the solution

(u,v,\theta)(t)
can be smoothly extended after
T>0
.

2.
Preliminaries and main result

For
p\geq 1
, we indicate by
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the usual Lebesgue
space, endowed with norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{p}=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{p}}
, with moreover

\|\,\cdot\,\|=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{2}
, when
p=2
. For
s>0
, we denote by

W^{s,p}=W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{s,p}=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{W^{s,p}}
the Sobolev space and
its norm, respectively (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
). When
p=2
, we use the
notation
H^{s}=W^{s,2}
and

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{H^{s}}=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{s,2}
. Here
\dot{H}^{s}
denotes
the standard homogeneous Sobolev space with norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
. In terms of the Fourier transform, homogeneous and inhomogeneous
Sobolev spaces can be written as follows

\dot{H}^{s}=\Big\{f\,:\,\|f\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty\Big\},

and

H^{s}=\Big\{f\,:\,\|f\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{2s})|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty\Big\}.

Note that
H^{s}
is equal to
\dot{H}^{s}\cap L^{2}
. For
s>0
, we also
introduce the operator
\Lambda^{s}
, formally
defined as
\Lambda^{s}f=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f
, that is the Fourier
multiplier such that
\widehat{\Lambda^{s}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\hat{f}(\xi)
, for

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Plainly,
\Lambda^{2}f=-\Delta f
.

Most of the estimates involving Besov and
BMO
spaces, that we
use in the following, have been established in
[
20
]
.
We refer to this paper for a detailed overview (see also

[
19
,
21
]
) on the theory of Besov spaces

B^{s}_{p,q}=B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(and homogeneous Besov spaces

\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}=\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
), with
0<p,q\leq\infty

and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and also on the
BMO=BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of functions of
bounded mean oscillation
.

In the sequel we will use the symbols
C
to denote generic
constants, which may change from line–to–line, but are not dependent
on the specific functions under consideration.

2.1.
Some estimates

Here below, we make explicit those key tools (from literature) which are instrumental in order to prove Theorem
2.1
.

We need interpolation’s inequalities for Sobolev spaces and the well-known Gagliardo–Nirenberg’s inequality (see
[
29
]
).
Besides this estimate in the standard form, we also make use and recall two recent fractional versions of it:
as a consequence of
[
16
, Corollary 2.4]
, we have

(2)

\|\Lambda^{r}f\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{p_{1}}^{1-\kappa}\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{p_{2}}^{\kappa},

in the case
r,s\geq 0
,
1<q,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
and
0\leq\kappa\leq 1
satisfying the conditions

(3)

\frac{1}{q}=\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{p_{1}}+\frac{\kappa}{p_{2}}\right)-\frac{\kappa s-r}{n},\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,r\leq\kappa s.

In the following, when we refer to Gagliardo–Nirenberg’s
inequality, we mean formula (
2
).

For the extremal case
q=\infty
, we use
[
6
, Corollary 1]
in the form

(4)

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq C\|f\|_{p_{1}}^{1-\kappa}\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{p_{2}}^{\kappa},

where, as before,
0=\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{p_{1}}+\frac{\kappa}{p_{2}}\right)-\frac{\kappa s}{n}.

Notice that the above Gagliardo–Nirenberg-type estimates allow for further fractional generalizations, like those in
[
5
, Theorem 1]
and
[
6
, Theorem 1]
, along with their direct consequences.

In the sequel, we will also use the Kato–Ponce product estimate
[
17
]
(see also
[
14
,
15
,
18
]
), i.e.

(5)

\|\Lambda^{s}(fg)\|_{p}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{p_{1}}\|\Lambda^{s}g\|_{q_{1}}+\|g\|_{p_{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{q_{2}}\big),

with
s>0
,
1<p<\infty
,
1<q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
and
1<p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
, such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
. We highlight that, in the right-hand side of (
5
),

L^{\infty}
-norms are allowed only for zero-order terms with respect to
\Lambda^{s}
.

Also, for
1<p<\infty
, we will use (see
[
19
]
)

(6)

\|f\,\cdot\,g\|_{p}\leq C(\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{BMO}+\|f\|_{BMO}\|g\|_{p}).

At last, we recall the following logarithmic inequality
(see, e.g.,
[
12
, Lemma 1.4, pp. 4 and 6]
):

(7)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{BMO}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|f\|_{H^{s}})\big),

where, in particular, we used the embedding

\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,2}\subset BMO
, in the case
s>n/2
(see, e.g.,

[
19
,
20
,
21
]
, for more details).

From now on, it will always be assumed
n=3
.

2.2.
Regularity result

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})\in H^{s}\times H^{s}\times H^{s}
,
3/2<s\leq 2
,
with
\mathrm{div}\,u_{0}=0
. Assume that
3\leq\alpha,\beta<4
.
Let
(u,v,\theta)
be a local solution of the system
(
1
), defined on some time interval
[0,T)
, with

0<T<\infty
, and having the following regularity

(8)

u,v,\theta\in C([0,\tilde{T}];H^{s})\,\,\textrm{ and }\,\,u,v\in L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};H^{s+1}),

for any
0<\tilde{T}<T
. Then
(u,v,\theta)(t)
can be extended
beyond time
T
, with the same regularity as in
(
8
), and hence as a smooth solution, provided that

(9)

\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|u(t)\|^{\delta}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|v(t)\|^{\gamma}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\,dt<\infty,

where

(10)

\delta=\frac{6(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-5}>2\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\gamma=\frac{6(\beta-1)}{3\beta-5}>2.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

The proof consists in proving suitable energy estimates for the
considered solution
(u,v,\theta)(t)
showing explicitly that it can be
extended after time
T>0
. Thus, the procedure is divided in a number
of steps in which we establish the needed bounds in
L^{2}
,
H^{1}
and

H^{s}
, with
3/2<s\leq 2
. These steps parallel the formal estimates in the
global existence results given in
[
36
]
and

[
37
]
, although in our case they are carried out with
different techniques borrowed from
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
30
,
35
,
38
]
.

3.1.
L^{2}
-estimates

Taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{1}
,

\eqref{TCM-gen}_{2}
and
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{3}
with
u
,
v
and

\theta
, respectively, adding them up and integrating with respect to

t
, we reach

(11)

\displaystyle\|(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\nu\|\nabla u\|^{2}+

\displaystyle\eta\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\mu\|\nabla\theta\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sigma_{1}\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\sigma_{2}\|v\|_{\beta+1}^{\beta+1}\Big)\,d\ell=\|(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})\|^{2},

where we used the notation

\|(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\doteq\|u(t)\|^{2}+\|v(t)\|^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|^{2},\,\,{\textrm{with $0\leq t<T$},}

which will be adapted to the case of higher order norms, and the
following identities have been exploited

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}\,(v\otimes v)\cdot u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v\cdot\nabla)u\cdot v\,dx=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\theta\cdot v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}\,v\cdot\theta\,dx=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u\,dx=0,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)v\cdot v\,dx=0\textrm{ and }\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)\theta\cdot\theta\,dx=0.

Thanks to (
11
), for any
0<t<T
, it follows that

u,v,\theta\in L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,t;H^{1})
.

3.2.
\dot{H}^{1}
-estimates

Multiplying
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{1}
by
-\Delta u
, integrating by
parts, we obtain

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}+

\displaystyle\nu\|\Delta u(t)\|^{2}-\sigma_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u|u|^{\alpha-1}\cdot\Delta u\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\Delta u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}\,(v\otimes v)\cdot\Delta u\,dx.

Multiplying
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{2}
by
-\Delta v
, we obtain

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla v(t)\|^{2}+

\displaystyle\eta\|\Delta v(t)\|^{2}-\sigma_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v|v|^{\beta-1}\cdot\Delta v\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)v\cdot\Delta v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta v\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\theta\cdot\Delta v\,dx.

Taking the
L^{2}
-product of
\eqref{TCM-gen}_{3}
with
-\Delta\theta
,
we find

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|^{2}+\mu\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)\theta\cdot\Delta\theta\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}\,v\,\Delta\theta\,dx.

Using calculations similar to those in
[
37
,
36
]
, adding
(
12
) and (
13
), we have that

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\big(

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}\big)+\nu\|\Delta u\|^{2}+\eta\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\mu\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}+\sigma_{1}\||u|^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\nabla u\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\sigma_{1}(\alpha-1)}{(\alpha+1)^{2}}\|\nabla|u|^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\|^{2}+\sigma_{2}\||v|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\nabla v\|^{2}+\frac{4\sigma_{2}(\beta-1)}{(\beta+1)^{2}}\|\nabla|v|^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}\,(v\otimes v)\cdot\Delta u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)v\cdot\Delta v\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\theta\cdot\Delta v\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)\theta\cdot\Delta\theta\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}\,v\,\Delta\theta\,dx\doteq\sum_{i=1}^{7}J_{i},

where

\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\doteq\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}+\|\nabla v(t)\|^{2}+\|\nabla\theta(t)\|^{2},\,\,\,\textrm{ with }\,\,\,0\leq t<T.

Let us use
w
to represent
u
,
v
or even
\theta
. By applying Hölder’s, Young’s and Gagliardo–Nirenberg’s inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\doteq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u||\nabla v||\Delta w|\,dx\leq C\|u\|_{6}\|\nabla v\|_{3}\|\Delta w\|\leq C\|u\|_{6}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{3}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{6}^{2}\|\nabla v\|\|\Delta v\|+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}=C\||u|^{2}\|_{3}\|\nabla v\|\|\Delta v\|+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\||u|^{2}\|_{3}^{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}.

From (
6
), the interpolation’s inequality

\|u\|_{3}\leq C\|u\|^{\frac{2(\alpha-2)}{3(\alpha-1)}}\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\frac{\alpha+1}{3(\alpha-1)}}

and (
7
), we have then

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{3}^{2}\|u\|_{BMO}^{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|^{\frac{4(\alpha-2)}{3(\alpha-1)}}\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{3(\alpha-1)}}\|u\|_{BMO}^{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{3(\alpha-1)}}\big(1+\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\ln(e+\|u\|_{H^{s}})\big)\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}.

To estimate lower order terms we used
(
11
), that, in particular, provide an uniform
bound in time on
\|(u,v,\theta)(t)\|
, i.e
\sup_{0\leq t<T}\|(u,v,\theta)(t)\|\leq\|(u_{0},v_{0},\theta_{0})\|.

Finally, from direct manipulations, the fact that
\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{3(\alpha-1)}<\alpha+1
,
and Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{2}
and
\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-5}
, we get

\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{3(\alpha-1)}}\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\leq C\big(\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}^{2\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-5}}\big),

and so

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|^{2}(1+\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{3(\alpha-1)}}\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\ln(e+\|u\|_{H^{s}}))+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|^{2}\Big(1+\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\big(\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\|u\|^{\frac{6(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-5}}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\ln(e+\|u\|_{H^{s}})\Big)+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}.

Proceeding as for
J
, we deduce:

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u||\nabla u||\Delta u|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|^{2}\Big(1+\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\big(\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\|u\|^{\frac{6(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-5}}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\ln(e+\|u\|_{H^{s}})\Big)+2\varepsilon\|\Delta u\|^{2},

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v||\nabla v||\Delta u|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|^{2}\Big(1+\|v\|_{\beta+1}^{\beta+1}+\big(\|v\|_{\beta+1}^{\beta+1}+\|v\|^{\frac{6(\beta-1)}{3\beta-5}}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\ln(e+\|v\|_{H^{s}})\Big)+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta u\|^{2},

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u||\nabla v||\Delta v|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|^{2}\Big(1+\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\big(\|u\|_{\alpha+1}^{\alpha+1}+\|u\|^{\frac{6(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-5}}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\ln(e+\|u\|_{H^{s}})\Big)+2\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2},

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v||\nabla u||\Delta v|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|^{2}\Big(1+\|v\|_{\beta+1}^{\beta+1}+\big(\|v\|_{\beta+1}^{\beta+1}+\|v\|^{\frac{6(\beta-1)}{3\beta-5}}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\ln(e+\|v\|_{H^{s}})\Big)+\varepsilon\|\Delta u\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|^{2},

and

\displaystyle J_{6}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u||\nabla\theta||\Delta\theta|\,dx

Plugging the above estimates into (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\big(\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}\big)+{\frac{\nu}{2}}\|\Delta u\|^{2}+{\frac{\eta}{2}}\|\Delta v\|^{2}+{\frac{\mu}{2}}\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}

\displaystyle+{\sigma_{2}}\||v|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\nabla v\|^{2}+\frac{4\sigma_{2}(\beta-1)}{(\beta+1)^{2}}\|\nabla|v|^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)\|^{2}\Big(1+\mathcal{A}+\big(\mathcal{A}+\|v\|^{\gamma}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|u\|^{\delta}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\times

\displaystyle\times\ln(e+\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\|v\|_{H^{s}})\Big)

where

In particular, for any

y(t)\doteq\sup_{t_{*}\leq\ell\leq t}\big(\|u(\ell)\|_{H^{s}}+\|v(\ell)\|_{H^{s}}\big),

(16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)

\displaystyle(t)\|^{2}+\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\left({\frac{\nu}{2}}\|\Delta u\|^{2}+{\frac{\eta}{2}}\|\Delta v\|^{2}+{\frac{\mu}{2}}\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}\right)d\ell

\displaystyle+{\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}\||u|^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\nabla u\|^{2}+\frac{4\sigma_{1}(\alpha-1)}{(\alpha+1)^{2}}\|\nabla|u|^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\|^{2}\Big)\,d\ell}

\displaystyle+{\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\Big({\sigma_{2}}\||v|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\nabla v\|^{2}+\frac{4\sigma_{2}(\beta-1)}{(\beta+1)^{2}}\|\nabla|v|^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}}\|^{2}\Big)\,d\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)(t_{*})\|^{2}\,e^{C\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\big(1+\|u\|^{\delta}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|v\|^{\gamma}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\big)\ln(e+\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\|v\|_{H^{s}})d\ell}

with

positive constant only depending on

, we reach

\displaystyle e+\|\Lambda^{s}(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}+\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\left((\nu-\varepsilon)\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|^{2}+(\eta-\varepsilon)\|\Lambda^{s+1}v\|^{2}+(\mu-\varepsilon)\|\Lambda^{s+1}\theta\|^{2}\right)\,d\ell

\displaystyle\leq e+\|\Lambda^{s}(u,v,\theta)(t_{*})\|^{2}+\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\mathfrak{C}(\ell)\,d\ell\,.

Recalling the estimate (
16
), the definition of
y=y(t)
introduced in (
15
),
and actually re-defining such a quantity as

y(t)\doteq\sup_{t_{*}\leq\ell\leq t}\big(\|u(\ell)\|_{H^{s}}+\|v(\ell)\|_{H^{s}}+\|\theta(\ell)\|_{H^{s}}\big),

and using

\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\mathfrak{C}(\ell)\,d\ell\leq C_{\ast}\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\big(e+y(t)\big)^{C\varepsilon}\,d\ell\leq C_{\ast}(t-t_{*})\big(e+y(t)\big)^{C\varepsilon},

with
C_{\ast}
positive constant only depending
\|\nabla(u,v,\theta)(t_{*})\|^{2}
and
t_{\ast}
, we obtain

(37)

e+\|\Lambda^{s}(u,v,\theta)(t)\|^{2}\\
\leq e+\|\Lambda^{s}(u,v,\theta)(t_{*})\|^{2}+C_{\ast}(t-t_{*})(e+y(t))^{C\varepsilon},

and taking the supremum over
0\leq\ell\leq t
(up to introducing an additional
positive constant on the right-hand side of the above inequality, we can exploit relation (
16
), on the
left-hand side of (
37
), to reconstruct the
H^{s}
-norm and also bound
the lower-order terms), we have

\displaystyle e+y(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e+\|\Lambda^{s}(u,v,\theta)(t_{*})\|^{2}+C_{\ast}(e+y(t)^{2})^{C\varepsilon},

with
C_{\ast}
large enough.
Now, taking
t_{*}
sufficiently close to
t
so that
\varepsilon
is such
that
C\varepsilon<1
, we conclude that

(e+y(t)^{2})^{1-C\varepsilon}\leq e+\|\Lambda^{s}(u,v,\theta)(t_{*})\|^{2}+C_{\ast}<+\infty

and hence

\sup_{t_{*}\leq\ell\leq t}\big(e+\|\Lambda^{s}(u,v,\theta)(\ell)\|^{2}\big)<+\infty,\ \mbox{ for every $t\in(0,T)$.}

Since
t
is arbitrary in the interval
(0,T)
, this concludes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

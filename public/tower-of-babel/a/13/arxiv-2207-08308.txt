Abstract
. We obtain the strong asymptotics of multiple orthogonal polynomials which arise in a mixed type Hermite-Padé approximation problem defined on a Nikishin system of functions. The results obtained allow to give exact estimates of the rate of convergence of the approximating rational functions and the strong asymptotics of Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials.



Keywords:
multiple orthogonal polynomials, biorthogonal polynomials, Hermite-Padé approximation, strong asymptotics, fixed point theorems

AMS classification:
Primary: 42C05; 30E10, Secondary: 41A21, 47H10

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Szegő’s theory of orthogonal polynomials
[
27
, Chap. X-XII]
is a gem of real and complex analysis not only for its intrinsic beauty but also because of its many consequences, extensions, and applications in different fields (see
[
24
,
25
]
). The natural framework in which the theory is developed is for polynomials orthogonal with respect to measures supported on the unit circle. Then, it may be transposed to polynomials orthogonal with respect to measures supported on bounded intervals of the real line. To illustrate, we present what is perhaps the most important result of Szegő’s theory in the context of orthogonal polynomials on segments of the real line. First, we need some notation.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure supported on an interval
\Delta=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
. We say that
\mu
satisfies Szegő’s condition and write
\mu\in\mathcal{S}(\Delta)
if

(1.1)

\int_{\Delta}{\ln\mu^{\prime}(x)}\D\eta_{\Delta}(x)>-\infty,

where

\D\eta_{\Delta}(x)=\frac{\D x}{\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}},\qquad x\in\Delta,

and
\mu^{\prime}
denotes the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\mu
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. When
\mu\in\mathcal{S}(\Delta)

\mathsf{G}(\mu,z):=\exp\left[\frac{\sqrt{(z-b)(z-a)}}{2\pi}\int_{\Delta}\frac{\ln(\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}\,\mu^{\prime}(x))}{x-z}\D\eta_{\Delta}(x)\right],

is called the Szegő function of
\mu
. The square root outside the integral is taken to be positive for
z>b
and those inside the integral are positive when
x\in(a,b)
. The Szegő function verifies

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\mathsf{G}(\mu,z)\neq 0\textrm{ for }z\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta,\\
\mathsf{G}(\mu,\infty)>0,\\
\lim_{y\to 0}|\mathsf{G}(\mu,x+iy)|^{2}=(\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}\,\mu^{\prime}(x))^{-1},\quad\textrm{for a.e.}\,\,x\in\Delta.\end{cases}

Let
\Psi
be the conformal map from
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta
onto the complement (in
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
) of the closed unit disk such that
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
,
\Psi^{\prime}(\infty)>0
.
Let
l_{n}(z)=\kappa_{n}z^{n}+\cdots
be the
n
-th orthonormal polynomial with respect to
\mu
with positive leading coefficient; that is,
\kappa_{n}>0
and

\int l_{n}(x)l_{k}(x)\D\mu(x)=\delta_{n,k},\qquad n,k\geq 0

where
\delta_{n,k}
denotes the Kronecker
\delta
. We have

Theorem 1.1

(G. Szegő)
.

Assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{S}(\Delta)
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{l_{n}(z)}{\Psi^{n}(z)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathsf{G}(\mu,z),

uniformly on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta
, and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\kappa_{n}}{C^{n}}=\frac{\mathsf{G}(\mu,\infty)}{\sqrt{2\pi}},

where
C^{-1}=(b-a)/4
is the logarithmic capacity of
\Delta
.

The second formula follows directly from the first one applied at
z=\infty
. The first formula expresses that asymptotically, the
n
-th orthonormal polynomial behaves like the
n
-th power of the fixed function
\Psi
.

Our goal is to obtain a similar result for the multiple orthogonal polynomials (which share orthogonality relations with a system of measures) arising in a certain mixed type approximation problem. Previously, this type of extension of Szegő’s theory was considered by A.I. Aptekarev
[
1
,
2
]
for type
ii
Hermite-Padé polynomials of so called Angelesco and Nikishin systems of measures. Our work is related with Nikishin systems of measures but the orthogonality relations differ from the type
ii
case. However, as we explain later, our main result Theorem
1.4

generalizes in several directions that of A.I. Aptekarev in
[
2
, Theorem 2]
.

In the study of multi-peakon solutions of the Degasperis-Procesi equation, a mixed type Hermite-Padé approximation problem was introduced in
[
17
]
. The same construction showed to be useful in the analysis of the two matrix model
[
6
]
and Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials
[
5
]
. The approximated objects are systems of two functions, defined in terms of Cauchy transforms of two measures supported on disjoint intervals of the real line. This application motivated the introduction in
[
15
]
of an approximation problem for an arbitrary Nikishin system generated by
m
measures (which reduces to the previous case when
m=2
) and study the convergence of the method.

Recently, the approximation problem was generalized further by V.G. Lysov in
[
18
]
where he studied the asymptotic zero distribution and the logarithmic asymptotics of the associated multiple orthogonal polynomials. The ratio asymptotics of these multiple orthogonal polynomials was given in
[
12
]
. Here, we wish to find their strong asymptotic behavior. Before stating our main result, let us briefly present the so called Nikishin systems of measures and Lysov’s definition of multi-level Hermite-Padé approximants.

1.2.
Nikishin systems and their multi-level Hermite-Padé polynomials

Nikishin systems of measures were first introduced by E.M. Nikishin in
[
20
]
. Throughout the years, it has been shown that polynomials which share orthogonality
relations with respect to such systems of measures behave asymptotically similar to
standard orthogonal polynomials (with respect to a single measure). A brief overview of the subject may be found in
[
16
]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\Delta)
denote the class of all finite positive Borel measures
s
with compact support
\supp s\subset\Delta=[a,b]
containing an infinite set of points. If
s
satisfies Szegő’s condition (
1.1
) we write
s\in\mathcal{S}(\Delta)
. By

\widehat{s}(z):=\int\frac{\D s(x)}{z-x}

we denote the Cauchy transform of the measure
s
. Obviously,
\widehat{s}\in\mathcal{H}(\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta)
(holomorphic on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta
).

Let
\Delta_{\alpha}
,
\Delta_{\beta}
be two intervals contained in the real line such that
\Delta_{\alpha}\cap\Delta_{\beta}=\varnothing
. Consider the measures
\sigma_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta_{\alpha})
and
\sigma_{\beta}\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta_{\beta})
. With these two measures we construct a third one as follows (using differential notation)

\D\langle\sigma_{\alpha},\sigma_{\beta}\rangle(x):=\widehat{\sigma}_{\beta}(x)\D\sigma_{\alpha}(x).

Notice that
\langle\sigma_{\alpha},\sigma_{\beta}\rangle\neq\langle\sigma_{\beta},\sigma_{\alpha}\rangle
. We can extend this operation inductively to more than two measures. For example, if
\Delta_{\gamma}\cap\Delta_{\alpha}=\varnothing
and

\sigma_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta_{\gamma})
, we define

\langle\sigma_{\gamma},\sigma_{\alpha},\sigma_{\beta}\rangle:=\langle\sigma_{\gamma},\langle\sigma_{\alpha},\sigma_{\beta}\rangle\rangle.

Definition 1.2
.

Take a collection
\Delta_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m,m\geq 2,
of intervals such that

(1.3)

\Delta_{j}\cap\Delta_{j+1}=\varnothing,\qquad j=1,\ldots,m-1.

Let
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
be a system of measures such that
\sigma_{j}\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,m
. We say that
(s_{1,1},\ldots,s_{1,m})=\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
, where

s_{1,1}=\sigma_{1},\;s_{1,2}=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle,\;s_{1,3}=\langle\sigma_{1},\langle\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\rangle\rangle,\,\ldots,\;s_{1,m}=\langle\sigma_{1},\langle\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\rangle\rangle

is the Nikishin system of measures generated by
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
. The vector
(\widehat{s}_{1,1},\ldots,\widehat{s}_{1,m})
is called the Nikishin system of functions.

Notice that any sub-system of
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
of consecutive measures is also a generator of some Nikishin system. In the sequel, for
1\leq j\leq k\leq m
, we will write

s_{j,k}:=\langle\sigma_{j},\sigma_{j+1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\rangle,\qquad s_{k,j}:=\langle\sigma_{k},\sigma_{k-1},\ldots,\sigma_{j}\rangle.

Without further notice, throughout the paper we consider that
m\geq 2
.

Let
(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
be the set of all
m
-dimensional vectors with non-negative integer components, not all equal to zero. For
\vec{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
we define
|\vec{n}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
and
\eta_{\vec{n},j}=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Consider the Nikishin system
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
and
\vec{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
. There exist polynomials
a_{\vec{n},0},a_{\vec{n},1},\ldots,a_{\vec{n},m}
, where
\deg a_{\vec{n},j}\leq|\vec{n}|-1
,
j=0,1,\ldots,m-1,
and
\deg a_{\vec{n},m}\leq|\vec{n}|
, not all identically equal to zero, called multi-level (ML) Hermite-Padé polynomials of
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
with respect to
\vec{n}
, that verify

(1.4)

\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}(z):=\left((-1)^{j}a_{\vec{n},j}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m}(-1)^{k}a_{\vec{n},k}\widehat{s}_{j+1,k}\right)(z)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{z^{n_{j+1}+1}}\right),\qquad z\to\infty,

where
j=0,\ldots,m-1
(the asymptotic expansion of
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
at
\infty
begins with
z^{-n_{j+1}-1}
, or higher). For completeness, set
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m}:=(-1)^{m}a_{\vec{n},m}
.

We warn the reader that with our terminology in
[
18
, Problem A]
the ML Hermite-Padé polynomials were defined with respect to the system
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{m},\ldots,\sigma_{1})
. Initially, in
[
15
]
ML Hermite-Padé approximants were defined only for multi-indices of the form
(n,0,\ldots,0)
.

Following Mahler’s denomination
[
19
]
, a multi-index
\vec{n}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
is said to be normal if
\deg a_{{\vec{n}},j}=|\vec{n}|-1
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1,
and
\deg a_{{\vec{n}},m}=|\vec{n}|
. The system of functions is said to be perfect when all the multi-indices are normal. In
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
, it was proved that Nikishin systems of functions are perfect for this approximation problem. Normality implies that the vector
(a_{\vec{n},0},\ldots,a_{\vec{n},m})
is uniquely determined up to a multiplicative factor. In the sequel, we normalize this vector so that
a_{\vec{n},m}
has leading coefficient equal to one.

The zeros of the forms
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
have some interesting properties (see
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
or Subsection
2.1
below). The form
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},0}
has no zero in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{1}
. For
j=1,\ldots,m,

\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
has exactly
\eta_{\vec{n},j}
zeros in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j+1}
,
(\Delta_{m+1}=\varnothing)
, they are all simple and lie in
\mathring{\Delta}_{j}
(the interior of
\Delta_{j}
with the Euclidean topology of
\mathbb{R}
). If
Q_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m,
denotes the monic polynomial whose roots are the simple zeros which
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
has in
\mathring{\Delta}_{j}
, then

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{z^{\eta_{\vec{n},j+1}+1}}\right)\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j+1}),\qquad z\to\infty,\qquad j=1,\ldots,m-1.

For each
j=0,\ldots,m-1
the order of interpolation at infinity prescribed in (
1.4
) is exact.

A sequence of distinct multi-indices
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
is said to be a ray sequence if

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}n_{j}/|\vec{n}|=p_{j},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m.

(Consequetly,
\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}=1
.)
The main results in
[
18
, Theorem 1.2, Corollary 1.1]
give the asymptotic zero distribution and the logarithmic asymptotics of ray sequences of the polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=1,\ldots.m,
when
\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\neq 0
,
j=1,\ldots,m
(this assumption can be relaxed to requiring that the measures
\sigma_{j}
be regular, see
[
26
, Chap. 3]
for the definition of regular measure). With the assumptions in Lysov’s paper it is possible to prove ratio asymptotics (see
[
12
, Theorem 3.4, Corollary 3.5]
).

\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
is said to be a straight ray sequence when

(1.5)

\frac{n_{j}}{|\vec{n}|}=p_{j},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m,\qquad\vec{n}\in\Lambda.

Our goal is to give the strong asymptotics of sequences of polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
,
a_{\vec{n},j-1}
,
j=1,\ldots,m,
and forms
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=0,\ldots,m
, for
\vec{n}
belonging to a straight ray sequence of multi-indices assuming that
\sigma_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\Delta_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,m
.

1.3.
Statement of the main results

Let
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}
,
p_{1}>0.
Set

(1.6)

P_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{j}p_{k},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m.

Notice that
P_{j}>0
,
j=1,\ldots,m
. Let
\vec{\Delta}=(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m})
be a collection of bounded intervals
\Delta_{j}=[a_{j},b_{j}]
of the real line which satisfies (
1.3
). By
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\vec{\Delta})
we denote the cone of all vector probability measures
\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m})
such that
\supp(\mu_{j})\subset\Delta_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Given a finite Borel measure
\mu
with compact support its logarithmic potential is defined as

V^{\mu}(z):=\int\ln\frac{1}{|z-x|}\D\mu(x).

In the present paper, we need the solution
\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\vec{\Delta})
of the vector equilibrium problem

(1.7)

V^{\lambda_{j}}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{P_{j-1}}{P_{j}}\,V^{\lambda_{j-1}}(x)+\frac{P_{j+1}}{P_{j}}\,V^{\lambda_{j+1}}(x)\right]\begin{cases}=\omega_{j},&x\in\supp\lambda_{j},\\
\geq\omega_{j},&x\in\Delta_{j},\end{cases}

for
j=1,\ldots,m
. Here,
V^{\lambda_{m+1}}\equiv V^{\lambda_{0}}\equiv 0
,
P_{0}=P_{m+1}=0
.

The problem (
1.7
) is equivalent to

(1.8)

P_{j}^{2}V^{\lambda_{j}}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\left[P_{j}P_{j-1}\,V^{\lambda_{j-1}}(x)+P_{j}P_{j+1}\,V^{\lambda_{j+1}}(x)\right]\begin{cases}=P_{j}^{2}\omega_{j},&x\in\supp\lambda_{j},\\
\geq P_{j}^{2}\omega_{j},&x\in\Delta_{j},\end{cases}

for
j=1,\ldots,m
. It is well known that there exists a unique
\vec{\lambda}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\vec{\Delta})
such that (
1.8
) holds for each
j=1,\ldots,m
.
\vec{\lambda}
is called the unitary vector equilibrium measure and
\vec{\omega}:=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m})
the vector equilibrium constant corresponding to the vector equilibrium problem with interaction matrix

(1.9)

\mathcal{C}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}P_{1}^{2}&-\frac{P_{1}P_{2}}{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
-\frac{P_{2}P_{1}}{2}&P_{2}^{2}&-\frac{P_{2}P_{3}}{2}&\ddots&0\\
0&-\frac{P_{3}P_{2}}{2}&P_{3}^{2}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&P_{m}^{2}\end{array}\right)

on the system of intervals
\vec{\Delta}
.

The proof of the existence of unique
\vec{\lambda}
and
\vec{\omega}
in the present situation follows from results contained in
[
21
, Chap. 5, §4]
(see also
[
3
, Section 4]
) due to the fact that
\mathcal{C}
is a symmetric positive definite matrix whose entries
c_{j,k}
are non-negative whenever
\Delta_{j}\cap\Delta_{k}\neq\varnothing
. We will need that
\supp(\lambda_{j})=\Delta_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, in order to have equality in (
1.7
) throughout all
\Delta_{j}
. It will be shown in Lemma
2.4
that this is true whenever
p_{1}>0
and
p_{1}\geq\cdots\geq p_{m}
.

In the sequel,

(1.10)

\Phi_{j}:=e^{-v_{j}},\qquad v_{j}:=V^{\lambda_{j}}+i\widetilde{V}^{\lambda_{j}},\qquad C_{j}=e^{\omega_{j}},

where
\widetilde{V}^{\lambda_{j}}
denotes the harmonic conjugate of
V^{\lambda_{j}}
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m,
which equals zero on
(b_{j},+\infty)
. The function
\Phi_{j}
is a single-valued analytic function in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j}
(notice that the values of
-v_{j}
vary by a multiple of
2\pi i
as we circulate around
\Delta_{j}
along a closed Jordan curve) with a simple pole at
\infty
. These functions will play the same role as
\Psi
in Szegő’s Theorem
1.1
. The functions
\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}
will be used to compare the polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
. We still need to introduce the limiting functions.

Assume that
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})\in\mathcal{S}(\vec{\Delta})
; that is,
\sigma_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\Delta_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,m
. There exists a unique system of Szegő functions
\vec{\mathsf{G}}=(\mathsf{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{G}_{m})
(see Theorem
1.6
below), where
\mathsf{G}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}(\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{k})
, which verifies the system of boundary value equations

(1.11)

|\mathsf{G}_{j}(x)|^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{|x-b_{j+1}||x-a_{j+1}|}\,\mathsf{G}_{j-1}(x)\mathsf{G}_{j+1}(x)}{\sqrt{(b_{j}-x)(x-a_{j})}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}(x)},\quad\mbox{a.e. on}\,\,\Delta_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,m.

Here,
\mathsf{G}_{0}\equiv\mathsf{G}_{m+1}\equiv 1
and when
j=m
,
\sqrt{|x-b_{m+1}||x-a_{m+1}|}
is substituted by
1
. Notice that
\mathsf{G}_{j}(z)
is the Szegő function with respect to a measure on
\Delta_{j}
whose Radon-Nikodym devivative is (compare (
1.11
) and (
1.2
))

\frac{\sigma_{j}^{\prime}(x)}{\sqrt{|x-b_{j+1}||x-a_{j+1}|}\,\mathsf{G}_{j-1}(x)\mathsf{G}_{j+1}(x)}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Lambda=\Lambda(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
be a straight ray sequence of multi-indices such that (
1.5
) takes place, and
p_{1}\geq\cdots\geq p_{m}
. Assume that
\vec{\sigma}\in\mathcal{S}(\vec{\Delta})
.
Then

(1.12)

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}(z)}=\frac{\mathsf{G}_{j}(z)}{\mathsf{G}_{j}(\infty)},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m,

uniformly on each compact subset of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{j}
, where
\vec{\mathsf{G}}=(\mathsf{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{G}_{m})
is the vector of Szegő functions whose components are determined by the boundary conditions (
1.11
) and the
\Phi_{j}
are as defined in (
1.10
) constructed from the vector equilibrium problem associated with the given vector
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
. The normalizing constants
\kappa_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m,
defined in (
2.7
) verify

(1.13)

\lim_{n\in\Lambda}\frac{\kappa_{\vec{n},j}}{C_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}}=\frac{\mathsf{G}_{j}(\infty)}{\sqrt{2\pi}},

where the
C_{j}
were given in (
1.10
). Additionally, the ML Hermite-Padé polynomials satisfy

(1.14)

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}\frac{a_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{\Phi_{m}^{|\vec{n}|}(z)}=\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m}(z)}{\mathsf{G}_{m}(\infty)}\widehat{s}_{m,j+1}(z),\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1,

uniformly on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{m}
.

The asymptotic formulas satisfied by the forms
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1,
are more entangled and we give them in Corollary
3.1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
makes use of fixed point theorems. This method was employed by A.I. Aptekarev in
[
1
]
for the study of the strong asymptotics of type
ii
Hermite-Padé polynomials of Angelesco systems of two measures and used again in
[
2
]
to obtain the strong asymptotics of type
ii
Hermite-Padé polynomials of Nikishin systems with an arbitrary number of measures. Here, we adapt the scheme proposed in
[
2
]
to the case of ML Hermite-Padé approximation, simplify some proofs, and correct some statements.

Type II Hermite-Padé polynomials and ML Hermite-Padé polynomials in general do not coincide, but for the multi-indices considered in Theorem
1.4
for which
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{m}
their construction is equivalent as follows from V.G. Lysov’s result
[
18
, Proposition 1.1]
. In
[
2
]
the measures are
assumed to be absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure and the multi-indices are of the form
\vec{n}=(n,n,\ldots,n),n\geq 1
. Therefore, our result covers that of A.I. Aptekarev with weaker assumptions. Theorem
1.4
extends Szegő’s classical result on the strong asymptotic of orthogonal polynomials to the case when
m>1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}({\vec{\Delta}})
be the linear space of all vector functions
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
such that
f_{j}
is continuous on
\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}
,
(\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{m+1}=\varnothing)
. Set

\|\vec{f}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left\{\|f_{j}\|_{\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}}\right\}

where
\|\cdot\|_{\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}}
denotes the sup norm on the indicated set. It is well known that

(\mathcal{C}({\vec{\Delta}}),\|\cdot\|_{\vec{\Delta}})
is a Banach space.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})
denote the set of all vector functions
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
such that
f_{j}
is positive and continuous on
\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}
. On
\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})
we define the distance

d(\vec{f},\vec{g})=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left\{\|\ln(f_{j}/g_{j})\|_{\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}}\right\}.

Since the logarithm establishes a homeomorphism between
\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})
and
\mathcal{C}({\vec{\Delta}})
, it is easy to verify that

(\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}}),d)
is a complete metric space. Furthermore, we have an important relation between the distance
d(\cdot,\cdot)
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
both acting on
\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})
; namely, for any sequence of vector functions
(\vec{g}_{n})_{n\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})
and
\vec{g}\in\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})
, we have

(1.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\vec{g}_{n}-\vec{g}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}d(\vec{g}_{n},\vec{g})=0.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
be a vector of non-negative functions such that
\ln w_{j}\in L_{1}(\eta_{\Delta_{j}})
,
j=1,\ldots,m
. Define

\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}):\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}}),

where for each
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
,
T_{j}\vec{f}
is the Szegő function on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{j}
whose boundary values on
\Delta_{j}
verify

(1.16)

|T_{j}\vec{f}(x)|^{2}=\frac{f_{j-1}(x)f_{j+1}(x)}{w_{j}(x)},\qquad\mbox{a.e. on}\,\,\Delta_{j},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m

(
f_{0}\equiv f_{m+1}\equiv 1
).

Recall that Szegő functions are positive on the real line outside of the support of the defining measure. Let
{\overline{m}}:=\lceil m/2\rceil
be the smallest integer greater or equal to
m/2
. As usual,
\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}^{j}
denotes the composition of
\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}
with itself
j
times.

Theorem 1.6
.

The map
\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}^{\overline{m}}
is a contraction on the complete metric space
({C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}}),d)
; more precisely,

(1.17)

d(\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f},\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g})\leq\gamma_{m}\,d(\vec{f},\vec{g})

where

\gamma_{m}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(2^{\overline{m}}-1)/2^{\overline{m}},&m\,\,\mathrm{even},\\
(2^{\overline{m}}-2)/2^{\overline{m}},&m\,\,\mathrm{odd}.\end{array}\right.

The map
\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}
has a unique fixed point.

In
[
2
, Proposition 1.1]
the author proves that

d(\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}\vec{f},\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}\vec{g})<d(\vec{f},\vec{g})
showing that
\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}
is non expansive and draws the erroneous conclusion that

\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}
is contractive. Theorem
1.6
corrects this last statement.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

In Subsections 2.1 and 2.2, we present some properties of the forms
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
and of the polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
which carry their zeros. In particular, it is shown that for each
\vec{n}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
the polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
are orthogonal with respect to certain varying measures (see (
2.6
)). Notice that in the orthogonality relations the polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
appear at the same time as orthogonal and in the denominator of the varying part of the measure (with the subindices shifted). This inconvenience is settled introducing
an operator
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
on a set of vector polynomials, denoted
\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
, for which
(Q_{\vec{n},1},\ldots,Q_{\vec{n},m})
is a unique fixed point. In Subsection 2.3 we normalize the orthogonal polynomials and the varying measures allowing us to use some known results of the theory of orthogonal polynomials with respect to varying measures developed in
[
7
,
8
,
11
]
to obtain in Subsection 2.5 certain asymptotic formulas for multiple orthogonal polynomials which are capital in the proof of Theorem
1.4
for the following reasons. Lemma
2.5
allows to reduce the proof of the strong asymptotics on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j}
to the strong asymptotics on
\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}
(since (
2.24
)
implies (
2.25
)) and hints the introduction of the operator
\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}
(with
\vec{w}
as in (
3.4
)) whose fixed point determines the limiting functions of the strong limits of Theorem
1.4
. In order to show that the fixed point of
\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}
attracts fixed points of the operators
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
a local fixed point theorem due
to Brouwer is used and Lemma
2.6
is needed to guarantee that the neighborhoods on which Brouwer’s Theorem is applied are non empty. In Subsection 2.4 the solution of an equilibrium problem, which allows to construct the comparison functions, is studied. Theorem
1.6
is proved in Subsection 3.1 with assumptions more general than needed in this paper having in mind future applications. Theorem
1.4
is obtained in Subsection 3.2. Subsections 3.3 and 3.4 contain applications of the main result to give precise estimates of the rate of convergence of ML Hermite-Padé approximations and the strong asymptotics of Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials, respectively.

2.1.
Some useful relations

The polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
play an important role throughout the paper so for the benefit of the reader we summarize their construction and some of its properties (for details, see
[
12
, Lemmas 2.1, 2.2, and 3.1]
).

Let
(s_{1,1},\ldots,s_{1,m})=\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
be given. Assume that there exist polynomials with real coefficients
a_{0},\ldots,a_{m}
and a monic polynomial
Q
with real coefficients whose roots lie in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{1}
such that

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)}{Q(z)}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{1})\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)}{Q(z)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{z^{N}}\right),\quad z\rightarrow\infty,

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}:=a_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}\widehat{s}_{1,k}
and
N\geq 1
. Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}:=a_{1}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}a_{k}\widehat{s}_{2,k}
. Using Cauchy’s integral formula on a contour surrounding
\Delta_{1}
and Fubini’s Theorem it readily follows (see
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
) that

(2.1)

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)}{Q(z)}=\int\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)}{z-x}\frac{\D\sigma_{1}(x)}{Q(x)},

and if
N\geq 2
, from Cauchy’s theorem, Fubini’s theorem, and Cauchy’s integral formula, we have

(2.2)

\int x^{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\frac{\D\sigma_{1}(x)}{Q(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,1,\ldots,N-2.

In particular,
\mathcal{A}_{1}
has at least
N-1
sign changes in
\mathring{\Delta}_{1}
or it is identically equal to zero.

Fix
\vec{n}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
. The forms
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
in Definition
1.3
are symmetric with
respect to the real line (that is,
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}(\overline{z})=\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}({z})}
); therefore, their roots come in conjugate pairs. Fix
\vec{n}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
. If
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},0}
has a zero in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{1}
take
Q_{\vec{n},0}
as a monic polynomial of degree
\geq 1
with real coefficients whose roots are zeros of
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},0}
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{1}
; otherwise, take
Q_{\vec{n},0}\equiv 1
. Using (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) and (
1.4
) for
j=0
, it follows that

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},0}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},0}(z)}=\int\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},1}(x)}{z-x}\frac{\D\sigma_{1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},0}(x)},

and

\int x^{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},1}(x)\frac{\D\sigma_{1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},0}(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,1,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},1}-1.

In the second relation there is at least one more orthogonality relation if either the order in (
1.4
) for
j=0
is higher than
\eta_{\vec{n},1}
or
\deg Q_{\vec{n},0}\geq 1
.

Therefore, there exists a monic polynomial with real coefficients
Q_{\vec{n},1}
of degree
\geq\eta_{\vec{n},1}
(or strictly greater if either the order in (
1.4
) for
j=0
is higher than
\eta_{\vec{n},1}
or
\deg Q_{\vec{n},0}\geq 1
) whose roots are zeros of
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},1}
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{2}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},1}/Q_{\vec{n},1}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{2}
. Using (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) and (
1.4
) for
j=1
, it follows that

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},1}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},1}(z)}=\int\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},2}(x)}{z-x}\frac{\D\sigma_{2}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},1}(x)}

and

\int x^{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},2}(x)\frac{\D\sigma_{2}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},1}(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,1,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},2}-1.

There is at least one more orthogonality relation if either the order in (
1.4
) for
j=0,1
is higher than prescribed,
\deg Q_{\vec{n},0}\geq 1
, or
\deg Q_{\vec{n},1}>\eta_{\vec{n},1}
.

One can repeat the arguments proving that there exist monic polynomials
Q_{\vec{n},j}
,
\deg Q_{\vec{n},j}\geq\eta_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1
,
(\eta_{\vec{n},0}=0)
with real coefficients whose roots lie in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j+1}
and are zeros of
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
, such that

(2.3)

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}=\int\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)}{z-x}\frac{\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)},

and

(2.4)

\int x^{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\frac{\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,1,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},j+1}-1,

with an additional orthogonality relation if for some
j
either the order in (
1.4
) is greater than that prescribed or
\deg Q_{\vec{n},j}>\eta_{\vec{n},j}
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m}=(-1)^{m}a_{\vec{n},m}
and
\deg a_{\vec{n},m}\leq|\vec{n}|=\eta_{\vec{n},m}
the orthogonality relations for

imply that there can be no additional orthogonality relation unless

. But this is not possible because (
1.4
) would imply that

,

and the solution of that system of equations would be the trivial one against our assumption. Consequently, all the orders in (

\deg Q_{\vec{n},j}=\eta_{\vec{n},j}

. and

. In the sequel,
Q_{\vec{n},m}:=a_{\vec{n},m}
.

Set

\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j}(z):=\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j+1}(z)\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)},\quad j=0,\ldots,m-1,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},m}(z)\equiv(-1)^{m}.

From (
2.3
)-(
2.4
), for
j=0,\ldots,m-1
, we get

(2.5)

\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j}(z)=\int\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j+1}^{2}(x)}{z-x}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)Q_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)},

where
Q_{\vec{n},0}\equiv Q_{\vec{n},m+1}\equiv 1
, and

(2.6)

\int x^{\nu}Q_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)Q_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,1,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},j+1}-1.

Define
K_{\vec{n},m}:=1
,

K_{\vec{n},j}:=\left(\int(Q_{\vec{n},j+1}(x))^{2}\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)|}{|{Q}_{\vec{n},j}(x){Q}_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)|}\right)^{-1/2},\quad j=0,\ldots,m-1

and

(2.7)

\kappa_{\vec{n},j+1}:=\frac{K_{\vec{n},j}}{K_{\vec{n},j+1}},\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1.

Set

q_{\vec{n},j}:=\kappa_{\vec{n},j}Q_{\vec{n},j},\quad j=1,\ldots,m,\qquad{h}_{\vec{n},j}:=(K_{n,j})^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j},\quad j=0,\ldots,m.

With the new notation, (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) become

(2.8)

h_{\vec{n},j}(z)=\varepsilon_{\vec{n},j+1}\int\frac{q_{\vec{n},j+1}^{2}(x)}{z-x}\frac{|h_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)|}{|Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)Q_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)|},\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1

where
\varepsilon_{\vec{n},j+1}
denotes the constant sign which the varying measure
\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)Q_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)}
adopts on
\Delta_{j+1}
, and

\int x^{\nu}q_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\frac{|h_{n,j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)|}{|Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)Q_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)|}=0,\qquad\nu=0,1,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},j+1}-1.

Notice that
q_{\vec{n},j+1}
is orthonormal with respect to
\frac{|h_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)|}{|Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)Q_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)|}

2.2.
The operator
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}

Fix
\vec{n}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
denote the set of all vector polynomials
(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m})
where
Q_{j},j=1,\ldots,m,
is a monic polynomial with real coefficients of degree
\eta_{\vec{n},j}

whose zeros lie in
\mathbb{C}\setminus(\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1})
. Recall that
\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{m+1}\equiv\varnothing
.

Definition 2.1
.

Define
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}:\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
such that for each
{\vec{Q}}=({Q}_{1},\ldots,{Q}_{m})\in\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
,
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}(\vec{Q})=\vec{Q}^{*}=({Q}^{*}_{1},\ldots,{Q}^{*}_{m})\in\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
satisfies

(2.9)

\int x^{\nu}Q_{j}^{*}(x)\frac{\mathcal{H}_{j}({\vec{Q}};x)\D\sigma_{j}(x)}{{Q}_{j-1}(x){Q}_{j+1}(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},j}-1,\qquad j=1,\ldots m,

where
\mathcal{H}_{m}({\vec{Q}};x)\equiv(-1)^{m}
,
Q_{0}=Q_{m+1}\equiv 1
, and

(2.10)

\mathcal{H}_{j}({\vec{Q}};z)=\int\frac{(Q^{*}_{j+1}(x))^{2}}{z-x}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{j+1}({\vec{Q}};x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{{Q}_{j}(x){Q}_{j+2}(x)},\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1.

The components of
\vec{Q}^{*}
as well as the weights
\mathcal{H}_{j}({\vec{Q}};z)
must be calculated inductively starting from the value
j=m
down to
j=0
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{m}({\vec{Q}};x)
is given, with (
2.9
)-(
2.10
) you can determine
Q_{m}^{*}
and
\mathcal{H}_{m-1}({\vec{Q}};x)
univocally. With this information, (
2.9
)-(
2.10
) determine
Q_{m-1}^{*}
and
\mathcal{H}_{m-2}({\vec{Q}};x)
and so forth. Therefore,
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
is correctly defined.

We can define a distance
d_{\vec{n}}:\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}\times\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as follows.

d_{\vec{n}}(\vec{Q}_{1},\vec{Q}_{2})=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\{\|Q_{1,j}-Q_{2,j}\|_{\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}}\},

where
\vec{Q}_{1}=(Q_{1,1},\ldots,Q_{1,m})
and
\vec{Q}_{2}=(Q_{2,1},\ldots,Q_{2,m})
are arbitrary vector polynomials in
\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
and the norms are uniform on the indicated sets (recall that by convention
\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{m+1}=\varnothing
). It is not difficult to prove that
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
is continuous on
\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
with this distance (for details see the corresponding result in
[
11
, Subsection 3.6]
). This property is used when we apply Brouwer’s Theorem in Subsection 3.2 below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
\vec{n}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
. The operator
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
has a unique fixed point which coincides with
\vec{Q}_{\vec{n}}:=(Q_{\vec{n},1},\ldots,Q_{\vec{n},m})
.

Proof.

Indeed,
\vec{Q}_{\vec{n}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\vec{n}}
and (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) show that it is a fixed point of
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
(compare with (
2.9
)-(
2.10
)). Let
\vec{\tilde{Q}}=(\tilde{Q}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{Q}_{m})
be an arbitrary fixed point of
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
. We must show that
\vec{\tilde{Q}}=\vec{Q}_{\vec{n}}
.

From the definition of the operator
\tilde{T}_{\vec{n}}
and of a fixed point, it follows that

(2.11)

\int x^{\nu}\tilde{Q}_{j}(x)\frac{\mathcal{H}_{j}({\vec{\tilde{Q}}};x)\D\sigma_{j}(x)}{\tilde{Q}_{j-1}(x)\tilde{Q}_{j+1}(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},j}-1,\qquad j=1,\ldots m,

where
\mathcal{H}_{m}({\vec{\tilde{Q}}};x)\equiv(-1)^{m}
,
\tilde{Q}_{0}=\tilde{{Q}}_{m+1}\equiv 1
, and

(2.12)

\mathcal{H}_{j}({\vec{\tilde{Q}}};z)=\int\frac{(\tilde{Q}_{j+1}(x))^{2}}{z-x}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{j+1}({\vec{\tilde{Q}}};x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{\tilde{Q}_{j}(x)\tilde{Q}_{j+2}(x)},\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1.

Notice that
\mathcal{H}_{j}({\vec{\tilde{Q}}};z)
,
j=0,\ldots,m
is analytic, symmetric with respect to the real line, and never equals zero in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j+1}\,(\Delta_{m+1}=\varnothing)
. For
j=m
this is trivial. Then, from (
2.12
) for
j=m-1
the assertion readily follows. If for some
j+1
the statement holds, from (
2.12
) the same is true for
j
.

Define

(2.13)

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}(z):=\frac{\tilde{Q}_{j}(z)\mathcal{H}_{j}(\vec{\tilde{Q}};z)}{\tilde{Q}_{j+1}(z)},\qquad j=0,\ldots,m.

From this definition and the properties of
\mathcal{H}_{j}(\vec{\tilde{Q}};z)
, it follows that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
,
j=0,\ldots,m
is analytic and symmetric in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{j+1}
, and its zeros in this region coincide with the roots of
\tilde{Q}_{j}
all of which are simple and lie on
\Delta_{j}
. Let us show that there exist polynomials
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
, where
\deg a_{j}\leq|\vec{n}|-1
,
j=0,1,\ldots,m-1,

a_{m}
monic, and
\deg a_{m}\leq|\vec{n}|
, not all identically equal to zero, such that for each
j=0,\ldots,m-1

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}(z)=\left((-1)^{j}a_{j}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m}(-1)^{k}a_{k}\widehat{s}_{j+1,k}\right)(z)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{z^{n_{j+1}+1}}\right),\qquad z\to\infty.

If this is so, from the uniqueness of the multi-level Hermite-Padé approximants it follows that

(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=(a_{\vec{n},0},a_{\vec{n},1},\ldots,a_{\vec{n},m}),\qquad(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{m-1})=({\mathcal{A}}_{\vec{n},0},{\mathcal{A}}_{\vec{n},1},\ldots,{\mathcal{A}}_{m-1})

and

(\tilde{Q}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{Q}_{m})=(Q_{\vec{n},1},\ldots,Q_{\vec{n},m}).

Take
a_{m}:=\tilde{Q}_{m}=(-1)^{m}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
. Obviously,
a_{m}
is a monic polynomial of degree
|\vec{n}|
. With the notation introduced in (
2.13
), (
2.11
)-(
2.12
) adopt the form

(2.14)

\int x^{\nu}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}(x)\frac{\D\sigma_{j}(x)}{\tilde{Q}_{j-1}(x)}=0,\qquad\nu=0,\ldots,\eta_{\vec{n},j}-1,\qquad j=1,\ldots m,

and

(2.15)

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}(z)=\frac{\tilde{Q}_{j}(z)}{\tilde{Q}_{j+1}(z)}\int\frac{\tilde{Q}_{j+1}(x)}{z-x}\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{\tilde{Q}_{j}(x)},\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1.

Since
\deg\tilde{Q}_{j}=\eta_{\vec{n},j}
and
\deg\tilde{Q}_{j+1}=\eta_{\vec{n},j+1}
this relation entails that

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(1/z^{n_{j+1}+1}),\qquad z\to\infty,

as needed. Using (
2.14
)-(
2.15
), we get

(2.16)

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}(z)=\int\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j+1}(x)}{z-x}\D\sigma_{j+1}(x).

For
j=m-1
, (
2.16
) gives

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{m-1}(z)=\int\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{m}(x)-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{m}(z)}{z-x}\D\sigma_{m}(x)+\int\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{m}(z)}{z-x}\D\sigma_{m}(x)

=(-1)^{m-1}a_{m-1}(z)+(-1)^{m}a_{m}(z)\widehat{s}_{m,m},

where

a_{m-1}(z):=\int\frac{{a}_{m}(z)-{a}_{m}(x)}{z-x}\D\sigma_{m}(x),\qquad\deg a_{m-1}=|\vec{n}|-1.

Assume that there exist polynomials
a_{j+1},\ldots,a_{m-1}
,
\deg a_{k}=|\vec{n}|-1
,
k=j+1,\ldots,m-1,
such that

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j+1}=(-1)^{j+1}a_{j+1}+\sum_{k=j+2}^{m}(-1)^{k}a_{k}\widehat{s}_{j+2,k}.

Substituting this expression inside the integral in (
2.16
), we get

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}(z)=\int\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j+1}(x)\mp\left((-1)^{j+1}a_{j+1}(z)+\sum_{k=j+2}^{m}(-1)^{k}a_{k}(z)\widehat{s}_{j+2,k}(x)\right)}{z-x}\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)=

(-1)^{j+1}\int\frac{a_{j+1}(x)-a_{j+1}(z)}{z-x}\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)+\sum_{k=j+2}^{m}(-1)^{k}\int\frac{a_{k}(x)-a_{k}(z)}{z-x}\D s_{j+1,k}(x)+

\sum_{k=j+1}^{m}(-1)^{k}a_{k}(z)\widehat{s}_{j+1,k}(z).

obtaining for
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
an expression similar to the one we had for
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j+1}
with the same
a_{k}
,
k=j+1,\ldots,m
plus

a_{j}(z):=\int\frac{a_{j+1}(z)-a_{j+1}(x)}{z-x}\D{s}_{j+1,j+1}(x)+\sum_{k=j+2}^{m}(-1)^{k-j+1}\int\frac{a_{k}(z)-a_{k}(x)}{z-x}\D s_{j+1,k}(x),

which obviously is a polynomial of degree
|\vec{n}|-1
. We have concluded the proof.
∎

one could be tempted to replace the weights

2.3.
Normalization

Fix
\vec{n}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
. Let

\vec{\Delta}=(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
. We have

d(\vec{T}\vec{f},\vec{T}\vec{g}):=\max\left\{\|\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{2}},\|\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{1}}\right\}.

Now,
\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|)
and
\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|)
are harmonic functions on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{1}
and
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{2}
, respectively. Using the maximum principle for harmonic functions it follows that

\max\left\{\|\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{2}},\|\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{1}}\right\}\\
\leq\max\left\{\sup_{\Delta_{1}}|\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|)|,\sup_{\Delta_{2}}|\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|)|\right\}.

Due to (
1.16
)

\max\left\{\sup_{\Delta_{1}}|\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|)|,\sup_{\Delta_{2}}|\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|)|\right\}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{\|\ln(f_{2}/g_{2})\|_{\Delta_{1}},\|\ln(f_{1}/g_{1})\|_{\Delta_{2}}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}d(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

Notice that in the quotient the weights cancel out and what remains is a continuous function on the corresponding intervals and the supremum becomes a maximum. The last equality is simply by definition. Adding up these relations (
1.17
) follows with the value prescribed on
\gamma_{2}
. In the sequel, we assume that
m\geq 3
and, therefore,
\overline{m}>1
.

Now, let us see why
\vec{T}
is not contractive when
m=3
. Proceeding as above with
\vec{f}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}),\;\vec{g}=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\in{C}^{+}({\vec{\Delta}})
, and
\vec{\Delta}=(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3})
, we get

\displaystyle d(\vec{T}\vec{f},\vec{T}\vec{g})=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{2}},\|\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{3}},\|\ln(|T_{3}\vec{f}|/|T_{3}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{1}},\|\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{2}},\|\ln(|T_{3}\vec{f}|/|T_{3}\vec{g}|)\|_{\Delta_{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{\|\ln(f_{2}/g_{2})\|_{\Delta_{1}},\|\ln(f_{1}/g_{1})+\ln(f_{3}/g_{3})\|_{\Delta_{2}},\|\ln(f_{2}/g_{2})\|_{\Delta_{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{d(\vec{f},\vec{g}),\|\ln(f_{1}/g_{1})+\ln(f_{3}/g_{3})\|_{\Delta_{2}},d(\vec{f},\vec{g})\right\}.

The problem comes with the norm appearing in the last line because the natural way to bound it is to use the triangle inequality obtaining

\|\ln(f_{1}/g_{1})+\ln(f_{3}/g_{3})\|_{\Delta_{2}}\leq\|\ln(f_{1}/g_{1})\|_{\Delta_{2}}+\|\ln(f_{3}/g_{3})\|_{\Delta_{2}}\leq 2d(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

Putting everything together, we get that
T
is non expansive; that is,

(3.1)

d(\vec{T}\vec{f},\vec{T}\vec{g})\leq d(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

(The previous arguments allow to deduce that
\vec{T}
is non expansive for any
m\geq 3
.)

Let us see that equality is actually attained. Indeed, taking
\vec{f}\equiv(e,e,e)
,
\vec{g}\equiv(1,1,1)
, where
e
denotes Euler’s constant, the vector of harmonic functions

\left(\ln(|T_{1}\vec{f}|/|T_{1}\vec{g}|),\ln(|T_{2}\vec{f}|/|T_{2}\vec{g}|),\ln(|T_{3}\vec{f}|/|T_{3}\vec{g}|)\right)

in
\prod_{k=1}^{3}(\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{k})

reduces to the constant vector function
(1/2,1,1/2)
in which case

d(\vec{T}\vec{f},\vec{T}\vec{g})=1=d(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

Similarly, one can prove that
\vec{T}
is not contractive for any
m\geq 3
. Therefore, there is an error in the statement of
[
2
, Proposition 1.1]
. However, the conclusion that
\vec{T}
has a unique fixed point is correct since (
1.17
) is true as we show next.

We return to the general case. Take two functions
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}),\vec{g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\in{C}^{+}(\vec{\Delta})
. For simplicity in writing, in the sequel we use the notation

\ln\vec{f}:=(\ln f_{1},\ldots,\ln f_{m}),\qquad{\vec{f}}/{\vec{g}}:=(f_{1}/g_{1},\ldots,f_{m}/g_{m}),

and

|{\vec{f}}/{\vec{g}}|:=(|f_{1}/g_{1}|,\ldots,|f_{m}/g_{m}|),\qquad\|\ln({\vec{f}}/{\vec{g}})\|_{\vec{\Delta}}:=d(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

According to the definition of the metric
d
, we have

(3.2)

d(\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f},\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g})=\|\ln(|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f}|/|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g}|)\|_{\vec{\Delta}}.

Indeed, the
k
-th component
T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}
of
\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f}
is a Szegő function in
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{k}
which is real and positive on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\Delta_{k}
. Consequently,

\ln T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}=\ln|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}|\qquad\mbox{on}\qquad{\mathbb{R}\setminus\Delta_{k}}.

However,
\ln|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}|
extends to a harmonic function on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{k}
and

\|\ln(|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}|/|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{g}|)\|_{(\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1})}=\|\ln|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}|-\ln|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{g}|\|_{(\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1})}.

Consequently, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
of
\ln(\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f}/\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g})
coincides with the norm of
\ln(|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f}|/|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g}|)
whose components are the harmonic functions
\ln(|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}|/|T_{k}\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{g}|)
. We must find these harmonic functions.

Fix
k=1,\ldots,m
. For each
u_{k}\in\mathcal{C}({\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}})
(the space of continuous functions on
{\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}
), we define
P_{j,k}(u_{k})
,
j=k-1,k+1
, to be the harmonic function on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{j}
whose boundary values on
\Delta_{j}
coincide with the values of
u_{k}
on
\Delta_{j}
(recall that
\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{m+1}=\varnothing
). Let us introduce the matrix map

(3.3)

P:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&P_{1,2}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
P_{2,1}&0&P_{2,3}&\cdots&0&0\\
0&P_{3,2}&0&\ddots&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\ddots&0&P_{m-1,m}\\
0&0&0&\cdots&P_{m,m-1}&0\end{array}\right).

(Only the entries in the sub-diagonal and supra-diagonal differ from
0
.) Set
u_{k}=\ln(f_{k}/g_{k})
.

In the following,
(\cdot)^{t}
denotes the transpose of the vector
(\cdot)
. Using (
1.16
), we have

(\ln|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f}|/|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g}|)^{t}=\frac{1}{2}P(\ln|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{f}|/|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}-1}\vec{g}|)^{t}=\cdots=

\frac{1}{2^{\overline{m}-1}}P^{\overline{m}-1}(\ln|\vec{T}\vec{f}/\vec{T}\vec{g}|)^{t}=\frac{1}{2^{\overline{m}}}P^{\overline{m}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})^{t}.

This formula allows us to bound
\|\ln(|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f}|/|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g}|)\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
.

To illustrate, let us consider first the cases when
m=3,4
and thus
\overline{m}=2
. We have

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&P_{1,2}&0\\
P_{2,1}&0&P_{2,3}\\
0&P_{3,2}&0\end{array}\right)^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\\
u_{3}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{1,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{1,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\\
P_{2,1}P_{1,2}u_{2}+P_{2,3}P_{3,2}u_{2}\\
P_{3,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{3,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\end{array}\right)

and

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&P_{1,2}&0&0\\
P_{2,1}&0&P_{2,3}&0\\
0&P_{3,2}&0&P_{3,4}\\
0&0&P_{4,3}&0\end{array}\right)^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\\
u_{3}\\
u_{4}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{1,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{1,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\\
P_{2,1}P_{1,2}u_{2}+P_{2,3}P_{3,2}u_{2}+P_{2,3}P_{3,4}u_{4}\\
P_{3,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{3,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}+P_{3,4}P_{4,3}u_{3}\\
P_{4,3}P_{3,2}u_{2}+P_{4,3}P_{3,4}u_{4}\end{array}\right).

Using (
3.2
), the maximum principle for harmonic functions, and the triangle inequality, when
m=3
we get

4d(\vec{T}^{2}\vec{f},\vec{T}^{2}\vec{g})=

\max\{\|P_{1,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{1,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{2}},\|P_{2,1}P_{1,2}u_{2}+P_{2,3}P_{3,2}u_{2}\|_{\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{3}},\|P_{3,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{3,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{2}}\}

\leq\max\{\|P_{1,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{1,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{1}},\|P_{2,1}P_{1,2}u_{2}+P_{2,3}P_{3,2}u_{2}\|_{\Delta_{2}},\|P_{3,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}+P_{3,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{3}}\}

\leq\max\{\|P_{1,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}\|_{\Delta_{1}}+\|P_{1,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{1}},\|P_{2,1}P_{1,2}u_{2}\|_{\Delta_{2}}+\|P_{2,3}P_{3,2}u_{2}\|_{\Delta_{2}},

\|P_{3,2}P_{2,1}u_{1}\|_{\Delta_{3}}+\|P_{3,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{3}}\}

Using the maximum principle for harmonic functions it is easy to deduce that any one of the norms appearing in the last two lines can be bounded by
d(\vec{f},\vec{g})
. For example,

\|P_{1,2}P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{1}}\leq\|P_{2,3}u_{3}\|_{\Delta_{2}}\leq\|\ln(f_{3}/g_{3})\|_{\Delta_{3}}\leq d(\vec{f},\vec{g})

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\ln(f_{3}/g_{3})
is one of the components of the vector
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
whose norm coincides with
d(\vec{f},\vec{g})
. Consequently,

d(\vec{T}^{2}\vec{f},\vec{T}^{2}\vec{g})\leq\frac{1}{2}d(\vec{f},\vec{g})

and
1/2
is the value assigned to
\gamma_{3}
.

When
m=4
, reasoning as above you get

4d(\vec{T}^{2}\vec{f},\vec{T}^{2}\vec{g})\leq 3d(\vec{f},\vec{g})

which is equivalent to (
1.17
) with
\gamma_{4}=3/4
. The
3
in the previous inequality comes from the fact that the second and third entries of
P^{2}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{4})^{t}
have three terms while the first and last only two.

Following the ideas expressed in the two examples, in order to estimate
\|\ln(|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{f}|/|\vec{T}^{\overline{m}}\vec{g}|)\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
all we have to do is to count the number of terms in each component of the vector
P^{\overline{m}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})^{t}
and divide the largest number by
2^{\overline{m}}
to get
\gamma_{m}
.

The component
r
of
P^{\overline{m}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})^{t}
is the sum of all the expressions of the form

P_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}P_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\cdots P_{\alpha_{\overline{m}},\beta_{\overline{m}}}u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}

satisfying the rules:

•

\alpha_{1}=r
,

•

\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}\pm 1,1\leq\beta_{i}\leq m,i=1,\ldots,{\overline{m}}
,

•

\alpha_{i+1}=\beta_{i},i=1,\ldots,{\overline{m}}-1
.

If this is so, notice that using the maximum principle for harmonic functions and (
1.16
) we have

\|P_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}P_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\cdots P_{\alpha_{\overline{m}},\beta_{\overline{m}}}u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}\|_{\Delta_{r-1}\cup\Delta_{r+1}}\leq\|P_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}P_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\cdots P_{\alpha_{\overline{m}},\beta_{\overline{m}}}u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}\|_{\Delta_{\alpha_{2}}}\\
=\|P_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\cdots P_{\alpha_{\overline{m}},\beta_{\overline{m}}}u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}\|_{\Delta_{\alpha_{2}}}\leq\|P_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\cdots P_{\alpha_{\overline{m}},\beta_{\overline{m}}}u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}\|_{\Delta_{\beta_{2}}}\leq\cdots\leq\\
\|P_{\alpha_{\overline{m}},\beta_{\overline{m}}}u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}\|_{\Delta_{\alpha_{\overline{m}}}}\leq\|P_{\alpha_{\overline{m}},\beta_{\overline{m}}}u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}\|_{\Delta_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}}=\|u_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}\|_{\Delta_{\beta_{\overline{m}}}}\leq d(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

The assertion of the previous sentence is a consequence of the definition of the product of two matrices and the structure of the matrix
P
. To prove it we introduce some terminology. We say that an expression of the form
P_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}P_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\cdots P_{\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}}
is a chain with
k
links. Only the entries of the form
P_{k,k\pm 1}
, where
k+1\leq m,k-1\geq 1
, of
P
are different from zero. The first subindex indicates the row and the second subindex the column where they are located. When we multiply
P
by itself the entry
P_{k,k+1}
with
k+1\leq m
gets multiplied by all the entries of
P
in row
k+1
, but the only products that survive (are different from zero) are the chains with two links
P_{k,k+1}P_{k+1,k+2}
(provided
k+2\leq m
) and
P_{k,k+1}P_{k+1,k}
which are located in
P^{2}
in the positions
(k,k+2)
and
(k,k)
, respectively. The entry
P_{k,k-1}
with
k-1\geq 1
gets multiplied by all the entries of
P
in row
k-1
, but the only products that survive are the chains with two links
P_{k,k-1}P_{k-1,k}
and
P_{k,k-1}P_{k-1,k-2}
(provided
k-2\geq 1
) which are located in
P^{2}
in the positions
(k,k)
and
(k,k-2)
, respectively.
We can go on in the same manner arriving to the rules stated above.

Summarizing, whenever
2\leq\beta_{i}\leq{m}-1
, the link
P_{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}}
may be followed by
P_{\beta_{i},\beta_{i}+1}
or
P_{\beta_{i},\beta_{i}-1}
; otherwise, there is only one option, either
P_{1,2}
when
\beta_{i}=1
or
P_{m,m-1}
when
\beta_{i}=m
. Consequently, the central components of
P^{\overline{m}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})^{t}
are the ones with the largest number of terms since
1
and
m
are furthest away from the initial value
r
determined by the component in question.

When
m
is odd, the central component is the
{\overline{m}}
-th one. In that case, the number of terms in the
{\overline{m}}
-th component of
P^{\overline{m}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})^{t}
equals
2^{\overline{m}}-2
because there are only two chains with
{\overline{m}}-1
links (namely
P_{{\overline{m}},{\overline{m}}-1}P_{{\overline{m}}-1,{\overline{m}}-2}\cdots P_{2,1}
and
P_{{\overline{m}},{\overline{m}}+1}P_{{\overline{m}}+1,{\overline{m}}+2}\cdots P_{m-1,m}
), which can be
extended with only one link. Therefore,
\gamma_{m}=(2^{\overline{m}}-2)/2^{\overline{m}}
.

If
m
is even the central components are the
{\overline{m}}
and
{\overline{m}}+1
ones. In the first case, only one chain with
{\overline{m}}-1
links can be extended with only one link, namely
P_{{\overline{m}},{\overline{m}}-1}P_{{\overline{m}}-1,{\overline{m}}-2}\cdots P_{2,1}
. In the second case, the only chain with
{\overline{m}}-1
links which can be extended with only one link is
P_{{\overline{m}}+1,{\overline{m}}+2}P_{{\overline{m}}+2,{\overline{m}}+3}\cdots P_{m-1,m}
. Thus, the total number of terms in these two components is
2^{\overline{m}}-1
. This implies that
\gamma_{m}=(2^{\overline{m}}-1)/2^{\overline{m}}
. With this we conclude the proof.

\Box

Incidentally, for any fixed

the maps

can be defined. They are contractive if and only if
{\overline{m}}^{\prime}\geq{\overline{m}}
. The constants of contraction are (not necessarily strictly) decreasing with

.

\|\vec{g}\|_{\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}}:=\max\{\sup\{|g_{k}(z)|:z\in\Omega_{k,\delta}\}:k=1,\ldots,m\}\,\,(\leq\infty)

\min_{\vec{\Delta}}\vec{g}:=\min\{\min\{g_{k}(z):z\in\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}\}:k=1,\ldots,m\},

where

Fix a constant

. Define

Obviously,

.

Let
(\vec{\rho}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary sequence of elements in

. For each

the sequence of functions made up by the

-th component of

is uniformly bounded on

. Therefore, there exists
\mathbb{I}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
(\vec{\rho}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{I}}
converges componentwise to some vector function
\vec{\rho}
uniformly on each compact subset of
\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}
, The
k
-th component of
\vec{\rho}
is, therefore, holomorphic and symmetric on
\Omega_{k,\delta}
, and non-negative on
\Omega_{k,\delta}\cap\mathbb{R},k=1,\ldots,m
. Additionally,

\min_{\vec{\Delta}}\vec{\rho}=\lim_{n\in\mathbb{I}}\min_{\vec{\Delta}}\vec{\rho}_{n}\geq C^{-1},

and

\|\vec{\rho}\|_{\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}}=\lim_{n\in\mathbb{I}}\|\vec{\rho}_{n}\|_{\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}}\leq C.

Consequently,
\vec{\rho}\in H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)
. We conclude that
H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)
is compact with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets on the space
H(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta})
of vector analytic functions on
\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}
.

Fix an arbitrary

. Let

\omega(\theta)=\{\vec{g}\in H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C):\|\vec{g}-\vec{\mathsf{G}}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}\leq\theta\}.

Analogously, for every

, set

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\omega(\theta),\qquad 0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0},

for, otherwise, we could find a sequence of vector functions in
H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)\subset\mathcal{C}^{+}(\vec{\Delta})
which converges to
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
in the
d
metric but not in the
\|\cdot\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
norm which would contradict (
1.15
).

Consider

\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}:=\omega_{\varepsilon}\cap H^{+,\vec{n}}.

Let
\mu_{k}
be the representing measure of
\mathsf{G}_{k}
; that is,
\mathsf{G}_{k}(z)=\mathsf{G}_{k}(\mu_{k},z)
,

. Taking into account Lemma

, in particular (

) and (

), we get

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}d(\vec{\tilde{g}}_{1}^{(\vec{n})},\vec{\mathsf{G}})=0,

where

\vec{\tilde{g}}_{1}^{(\vec{n})}:=\left(\frac{\mathsf{G}_{1}(\infty)\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n},1}}{\Phi_{1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},1}}},\cdots,\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m}(\infty)\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n},m}}{\Phi_{m}^{\eta_{\vec{n},m}}}\right)

and the
\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n},j}
,

are the orthonormal polynomials
\tilde{q}_{\vec{n},j}

given in (
2.26
) re-normalized to be monic. Because of (
2.28
) we conclude that, for every
\varepsilon
,
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},
there exists
n_{0}
such that
\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}\neq\varnothing
for
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
,
|\vec{n}|>n_{0}
.

Let
H(\vec{\Omega})
be the locally convex space of all vector analytic functions on
\vec{\Omega}=(\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{m})
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of
\vec{\Omega}
.
Let us show that for each
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
fixed,
\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}
is a compact subset of

with this topology.

For
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
fixed, consider an arbitrary sequence

We must show that it has a subsequence which converges to an element of
\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}
.

Fix

. Consider the sequence of monic polynomials with real coefficients

P_{n,k}

remain uniformly bounded away from
\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}\cup\{\infty\}
in

.
Therefore, there exists a subsequence of indices

and a monic polynomial

with real coefficients of degree

, whose zeros lie on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1})
, such that

. Since this is true for every

, it readily follows that there exists a subsequence

\lim_{n\in I^{\prime}}(\rho_{n,1},\ldots,\rho_{n,m})=\left(\frac{\mathsf{G}_{1}(\infty){P}_{1}}{\Phi_{1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},1}}},\cdots,\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m}(\infty){P}_{m}}{\Phi_{m}^{\eta_{\vec{n},m}}}\right)\in\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}},

\vec{\Omega}
. With this we conclude the proof that

Fix
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
. Let us show that
\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}
is convex. Take two arbitrary vector functions in

\vec{p}_{j}=\left(\frac{\mathsf{G}_{1}(\infty){P}_{j,1}}{\Phi_{1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},1}}},\cdots,\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m}(\infty){P}_{j,m}}{\Phi_{m}^{\eta_{\vec{n},m}}}\right),\qquad j=1,2.

Let us show that for every
\beta,0\leq\beta\leq 1

\beta\vec{p}_{1}+(1-\beta)\vec{p}_{2}\in\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}.

Obviously,
\beta\vec{p}_{1}+(1-\beta)\vec{p}_{2}\in H^{+,\vec{n}}\cap H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)
. In particular, for each
k=1,\ldots,m,

(3.6)

{\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty)(\beta{P}_{1,k}(x)+(1-\beta)P_{2,k}(x))}{\Phi_{k}^{-\eta_{\vec{n},k}}(x)}>0,\qquad x\in\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}.

It remains to prove that

(3.7)

\left\|\ln\left(\frac{\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty)(\beta{P}_{1,k}+(1-\beta)P_{2,k})}{\Phi_{k}^{\eta_{\vec{n},k}}G_{k}}\right)\right\|_{\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}\leq\varepsilon.

For each
x\in\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}
fixed,
\beta{P}_{1,k}(x)+(1-\beta)P_{2,k}(x)
is an intermediate
point between
{P}_{1,k}(x)
and
{P}_{2,k}(x)
. This fact, together with (
3.6
), implies that for
x\in\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}

0<\min\left\{\frac{\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty)({P}_{1,k}(x))}{\Phi_{k}^{\eta_{\vec{n},k}(x)}G_{k}(x)},\frac{\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty)({P}_{2,k}(x))}{\Phi_{k}^{\eta_{\vec{n},k}(x)}G_{k}(x)}\right\}\leq

\frac{\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty)(\beta{P}_{1,k}(x)+(1-\beta)P_{2,k}(x))}{\Phi_{k}^{\eta_{\vec{n},k}(x)}G_{k}(x)}\leq\max\left\{\frac{\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty)({P}_{1,k}(x))}{\Phi_{k}^{\eta_{\vec{n},k}(x)}G_{k}(x)},\frac{\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty)({P}_{2,k}(x))}{\Phi_{k}^{\eta_{\vec{n},k}(x)}G_{k}(x)}\right\}.

Using the monotonicity of the logarithm (
3.7
) readily follows.

Now we need to show that
T_{\vec{n}}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}})\subset\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}
for all
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
with
|\vec{n}|
large enough. Indeed, there exist
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
and
n_{0}
such that for all
\vec{n}\in\Lambda,|\vec{n}|\geq n_{0}
, we have

(3.8)

T_{\vec{n}}(\omega_{\varepsilon^{\prime},\vec{n}})\subset\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}.

If we assume the contrary, we could find a sequence
(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})})_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
,
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
,

\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}=\left(\frac{\mathsf{G}_{1}(\infty)\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n},1}}{\Phi_{1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},1}}},\cdots,\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m}(\infty)\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n},m}}{\Phi_{m}^{\eta_{\vec{n},m}}}\right)\in\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}},

such that

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})})=0,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},T_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}))=d(T(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),T_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}))\geq\varepsilon.

Since
(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})})_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\subset H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)
it follows that for every
j=1,\ldots,m
the zeros of
(\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n},j})_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
remain uniformly bounded away from
\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}
. Indeed, if we assume the contrary, taking a convenient subsequence, we would conclude that
\mathsf{G}_{j}
must have some zero in
\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}
. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
2.5
and from
(
2.25
) and (
1.15
) it follows that

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}d(T(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),{T}_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}))=0.

which is not possible because the limit value should have to be
\geq\varepsilon
. So, such
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
exists.

It remains to prove that

(3.9)

T_{\vec{n}}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon^{\prime},\vec{n}})\subset\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}.

for all
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
with
|\vec{n}|
sufficiently large. An inmediate consequence of the maximum principle is that
the operator
\vec{T}
is non-expansive, see
[
2
, Proposition 1.1]
or (
3.1
) for the special case
m=3
. Therefore,

d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{T}(\vec{g}))=d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))\leq d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g}),\qquad\vec{g}\in\omega_{\varepsilon}.

Consequently,

\sup_{\vec{g}\in\omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\frac{d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))}{d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g})}\leq 1.

Consider

\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\{\vec{g}\in\omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}:g_{k}(\infty)=\mathsf{G}_{k}(\infty),k=1,\ldots,m\}.

Suppose that

\sup_{\vec{g}\in\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\frac{d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))}{d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g})}=1.

Then, there exist a sequence

(\vec{g}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}_{n}))}{d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g}_{n})}=1,

a subsequence
(\vec{g}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{I}\subset\mathbb{N}}
, and

\vec{g}\in H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)
such that

\lim_{n\in\mathbb{I}}\|\vec{g}_{n}-\vec{g}\|_{\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}}=0,\qquad\lim_{n\in\mathbb{I}}d(\vec{T}(\vec{g}_{n}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))=0,

and

{d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))}={d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g})}.

That is

(3.10)

\|\ln(\vec{T}(\vec{g})/\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}))\|_{\vec{\Delta}}=\|\ln(\vec{g}/\vec{\mathsf{G}})\|_{\vec{\Delta}}.

We will show that this implies that
\vec{g}=\vec{\mathsf{G}}
which is not possible because

d(\vec{g},\vec{\mathsf{G}})\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
.

Let
\vec{u}:=\ln(\vec{g}/\vec{\mathsf{G}})=(\ln(g_{1}/\mathsf{G}_{1}),\ldots,\ln(g_{m}/\mathsf{G}_{m}))
be the vector function whose
k
-th component
u_{k}
is the restriction to
\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}
of
\ln(g_{k}/G_{k})
. By the definition of the operator
\vec{T}
, we have
\ln(|\vec{T}(\vec{g})|/|\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}})|)
is the vector function whose
j
-th component is the harmonic function on
\Omega_{j}
whose boundary values on
\Delta_{j}
coincide with the restriction on
\Delta_{j}
of
(u_{j-1}+u_{j+1})/2
. If (
3.10
) takes place, there exists
k
,
1\leq k\leq m
such that (recall the definition of the operators
P_{j,k}
just before the introduction of the matrix (
3.3
))

\displaystyle\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|P_{k,k-1}u_{k-1}+P_{k,k+1}u_{k+1}\|_{\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\|P_{k,k-1}u_{k-1}\|_{\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}+\|P_{k,k+1}u_{k+1}\|_{\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\|P_{k,k-1}u_{k-1}\|_{\Delta_{k}}+\|P_{k,k+1}u_{k+1}\|_{\Delta_{k}}\right)\leq\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}.

If

or

, you actually get
\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
because in all the intermediate steps there is only one term, so
\|u\|_{\vec{\Delta}}=0
and we would be done.

Now, for any other value of

the chain of inequalities implies that there is equality on each step; in particular

\|P_{k,k-1}u_{k-1}\|_{\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}=\|P_{k,k-1}u_{k-1}\|_{\Delta_{k}}=\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}},\qquad

and

\|P_{k,k+1}u_{k+1}\|_{\Delta_{k-1}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}=\|P_{k,k+1}u_{k+1}\|_{\Delta_{k}}=\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}.

We conclude that
P_{k,k-1}u_{k-1}
and
P_{k,k+1}u_{k+1}
are constant because they are harmonic functions in
\Omega_{k}
which attain their maximum at interior points. The constant value they take is
\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
(or
-\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
). In turn this implies that
u_{k+1}
and
u_{k-1}
restricted to
\Delta_{k}
are constantly equal to
\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
(or
-\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
). Consequently,

\ln(g_{k+1}(x)/\mathsf{G}_{k+1}(x))=\ln(g_{k-1}(x)/\mathsf{G}_{k-1}(x))=\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}},\qquad x\in\Delta_{k}.

However,
\ln(g_{k+1}/\mathsf{G}_{k+1})\in H^{+}(\Omega_{\delta,k+1}),\ln(g_{k-1}/\mathsf{G}_{k-1})\in H^{+}(\Omega_{\delta,k-1})
. Since they are constant on the interval
\Delta_{k}
of their regions of holomorphy, they are constantly equal to
\|u\|_{\vec{\Delta}}
.
Now,

g_{k+1}(\infty)=\mathsf{G}_{k+1}(\infty)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad g_{k-1}(\infty)=\mathsf{G}_{k-1}(\infty).

It follows that

\|\vec{u}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}=\ln(g_{k+1}(\infty)/\mathsf{G}_{k+1}(\infty))=\ln(g_{k-1}(\infty)/\mathsf{G}_{k-1}(\infty))=0

Therefore,
\vec{g}\equiv\vec{\mathsf{G}}
, which is impossible as pointed out above. This means that

\sup_{\vec{g}\in\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\frac{d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))}{d(\mathsf{G},\vec{g})}=\gamma<1.

In other words,

(3.11)

d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))\leq\gamma d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g}),\qquad\vec{g}\in\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}.

Now, we are going to show that there exists
n_{0}
such that for all
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
with
|\vec{n}|>n_{0}
and
\vec{g}\in\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}
, we have

(3.12)

d(T_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))<(1-\gamma)\varepsilon.

Indeed, if we assume the contrary, we can find
(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})})_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
,
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
,
\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}\in\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}
such that

(3.13)

d(T_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}))\geq(1-\gamma)\varepsilon.

Since
\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}\subset H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)
the sequence
(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})})_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is uniformly bounded in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}}
. Therefore, there exists a function
\vec{g}\in H^{+}(\vec{\Omega}_{\delta},C)
and a subsequence
\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
such that

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}\|\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}-\vec{g}\|_{\vec{\Omega}_{\delta}}=0.

In particular,

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}\|\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}-\vec{g}\|_{\vec{\Delta}}=0,

and (
2.24
) in Lemma
2.5
takes place with
\vec{f}_{\vec{n}}=\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}
,
\vec{f}=\vec{g}
, and
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,m
, the zeros of the
j
-th component of the vector
functions
\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})}
remain uniformly bounded away from
\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}
for
\vec{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
. Indeed, the contrary implies that the
j
-th component of
\vec{g}
has a zero on
\Delta_{j-1}\cup\Delta_{j+1}
which is not the case. According to (
2.25
)

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\prime\prime}}\|T_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}^{(\vec{n})})-\vec{T}(\vec{g})\|_{\vec{\Delta}}=0

which contradicts (

.

For
\vec{g}\in\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon^{\prime},\vec{n}}\subset\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, on account of (

) and (

), we obtain

d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},T_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}))\leq d(\vec{T}(\vec{\mathsf{G}}),\vec{T}(\vec{g}))+d(\vec{T}(\vec{g}),T_{\vec{n}}(\vec{g}))\leq\gamma d(\vec{\mathsf{G}},\vec{g})+(1-\gamma)\varepsilon<\varepsilon.

This implies (
3.9
) which together with (

) give that there exists
n_{0}
such that

T_{\vec{n}}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}})\subset\omega_{\varepsilon,\vec{n}},\qquad\vec{n}\in\Lambda,\qquad|\vec{n}|\geq n_{0}.

We have shown that for each fixed
\vec{n}\in\Lambda
,

,

is a non-empty convex, compact subset of

,

. Since

) and (

) of Theorem

.

From

and

. Since

Now, using (
1.12
), (

Proof.

From (

\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j}
we have

\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}(z)=\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},j+1}(z)}\int\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j+1}^{2}(x)}{z-x}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\vec{n},j+1}(x)\D\sigma_{j+1}(x)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(x)Q_{\vec{n},j+2}(x)},\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1,

where the equality holds in
\Omega_{j+1}
. Due to (
2.8
),

\frac{\varepsilon_{\vec{n},j+1}K_{\vec{n},j}^{2}\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{(\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}/\Phi_{j+1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j+1}})(z)}=\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}(z)}\frac{\Phi_{j+1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j+1}}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},j+1}(z)}h_{\vec{n},j}(z).

Now, from Lemma
2.3
and Theorem
1.4
, (
3.15
) is immediate. For (
3.14
) take into account that
Q_{\vec{n},0}\equiv 1,\Phi_{0}\equiv 1,
and
\Delta_{0}=\varnothing
.
∎

One may be tempted to think that condition (
1.5
) on the sequence of multi-indices
\Lambda
in Theorem
1.4
is too restrictive. The fact is that the strong asymptotics of multilevel Hermite-Padé polynomials is very sensitive to the way the sequence of multi-indices is taken. For example, if
\Lambda
verifies (
1.5
) and we define
{\Lambda}^{l}
as

{\Lambda}^{l}:=\{{\vec{n}}^{l}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}:{\vec{n}}^{l}=\vec{n}+\vec{e}_{l},\vec{n}\in\Lambda\},

where
\vec{e}_{l}
is the unitary vector with
1
in
l
-th component, then for each
l
fixed there exists

(3.16)

\lim_{\vec{n}^{l}\in\Lambda^{l}}\frac{Q_{\vec{n}^{l},j}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n}^{l},j}}(z)},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m,

but the limiting functions will depend on
l
. To prove this, write

\frac{Q_{\vec{n}^{l},j}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n}^{l},j}}(z)}=\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}(z)}\frac{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n}^{l},j}}(z)}\frac{Q_{\vec{n}^{l},j}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}.

Since
\Lambda
verifies (
1.5
)

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}\frac{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}(z)}=\frac{\mathsf{G}_{j}(z)}{\mathsf{G}_{j}(\infty)},

uniformly on each compact subset of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{j}
.
The second factor satisfies

\frac{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n},j}}(z)}{\Phi_{j}^{\eta_{\vec{n}^{l},j}}(z)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&j=1,\ldots,l-1,\\
1/\Phi_{j}(z),&j=l,\ldots,m.\end{array}\right.

Finally, using
[
12
, Theorem 3.4]

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}\frac{Q_{\vec{n}^{l},j}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},j}(z)}=\frac{{F}_{j}^{(l)}(z)}{{F}_{j}^{(l)}(\infty)},

uniformly on each compact subset of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Delta_{j}
, where

{F}_{j}^{(l)},j=1,\ldots,m,
is the solution of a system of boundary value problems defined in
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
. Consequently, the limit in (
3.16
) can be given explicitly. We will not dwell into more details and simply mention that using
[
12
, Theorem 3.4]
one can extend Theorem
1.4
to sequences of multi-indices of the form

\Lambda^{\vec{v}}:=\{\vec{n}+\vec{v}:\vec{n}\in\Lambda\},

where
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{*}
verifies (
1.5
) and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}
is fixed.

3.3.
Rate of convergence of ML Hermite-Padé approximants

In
[
18
]
, the author obtains the logarithmic asymptotics of the linear forms
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1,
and with it estimates the rate of convergence of the rational functions
\frac{a_{\vec{n},j}}{a_{\vec{n},m}}
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1,
as
|\vec{n}|\to\infty
, along ray sequences of multi-indices. For straight ray sequences and Nikishin systems with Szegő’s condition, using the results on strong asymptotics, we are able to give the exact rate of convergence.

Corollary 3.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
, we have

(3.17)

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}\frac{\varepsilon_{\vec{n},m}K_{\vec{n},m-1}^{2}\Phi_{m}^{2|\vec{n}|}(z)}{\Phi_{m-1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},m-1}}(z)}\left(\frac{a_{\vec{n},j}}{a_{\vec{n},m}}-\widehat{s}_{m,j+1}\right)(z)=\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m-1}(z)\mathsf{G}_{m}^{2}(\infty)}{\mathsf{G}_{m}^{2}(z)\mathsf{G}_{m-1}(\infty)}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}\widehat{s}_{m-1,j+1}(z)}{\sqrt{(z-a_{m})(z-b_{m})}},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\cup_{i=j+1}^{m}\Delta_{i}
.

Proof.

Taking into account
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
with
\mathcal{L}_{j}=\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,m-2
and
r=m-1
, we have (for all points where the expression is meaningful)

\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k-j}\widehat{s}_{k,j+1}\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},k}=(-1)^{j}(a_{\vec{n},j}-a_{\vec{n},m}\widehat{s}_{m,j+1}).

Though
[
15
]
is dedicated to a special case of ML Hermite-Padé approximation the forms
\mathcal{L}_{j}
have a general character and that lemma is also applicable in the present situation.
Dividing the above equality by
Q_{\vec{n},m}=a_{\vec{n},m}
, we obtain

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}}{Q_{\vec{n},m}}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k-j}\widehat{s}_{k,j+1}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},k}}{Q_{\vec{n},m}}=(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{a_{\vec{n},j}}{a_{\vec{n},m}}-\widehat{s}_{m,j+1}\right).

This is equivalent to

(3.18)

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}}{Q_{\vec{n},m}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}}{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k-j}\widehat{s}_{k,j+1}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},k}}{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}}\right)=(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{a_{\vec{n},j}}{a_{\vec{n},m}}-\widehat{s}_{m,j+1}\right).

From formula (1.8) in
[
18
, Proposition 1.2]
, it follows that the ratios
\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},k}/\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}
,
k=j,\ldots,m-2
, converge uniformly to zero on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\cup_{i=j+1}^{m}\Delta_{i}
; consequently,

(3.19)

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}}{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k-j}\widehat{s}_{k,j+1}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},k}}{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}}=(-1)^{m-1-j}\widehat{s}_{m-1,j+1},

on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\cup_{i=j+1}^{m}\Delta_{i}
. On the other hand, from Corollary
3.1
and (
1.12
) we have

(3.20)

\lim_{n\in\Lambda}\frac{\varepsilon_{\vec{n},m}K_{\vec{n},m-1}^{2}\Phi_{m}^{2|\vec{n}|}(z)}{\Phi_{m-1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},m-1}}(z)}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},m}(z)}=\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m-1}(z)\mathsf{G}_{m}^{2}(\infty)}{\mathsf{G}_{m}^{2}(z)\mathsf{G}_{m-1}(\infty)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(z-a_{m})(z-b_{m})}},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\Delta_{m-1}\cup\Delta_{m})
.

Therefore, from (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), we get

\lim_{\vec{n}\in\Lambda}\frac{\varepsilon_{\vec{n},m}K_{\vec{n},m-1}^{2}\Phi_{m}^{2|\vec{n}|}(z)}{\Phi_{m-1}^{\eta_{\vec{n},m-1}}(z)}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}(z)}{Q_{\vec{n},m}(z)}\left(\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},j}}{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k-j}\widehat{s}_{k,j+1}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},k}}{\mathcal{A}_{\vec{n},m-1}}\right)(z)=

\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m-1}(z)\mathsf{G}_{m}^{2}(\infty)}{\mathsf{G}_{m}^{2}(z)\mathsf{G}_{m-1}(\infty)}\frac{(-1)^{m-1-j}\widehat{s}_{m-1,j+1}(z)}{\sqrt{(z-a_{m})(z-b_{m})}},

on compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\cup_{i=j+1}^{m}
which together with (
3.18
) give us (
3.17
).
∎

3.4.
Strong asymptotics of Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials

In this subsection, we apply Theorem
1.4
to obtain the strong asymptotics of Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials.

As above consider the Nikishin system
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
where
\supp\sigma_{k}\subset\Delta_{k}
and the intervals
\Delta_{k}
are bounded. Define the kernel function

K(x_{1},x_{m})=\int_{\Delta_{2}}\int_{\Delta_{3}}\cdots\int_{\Delta_{m-1}}\frac{\,\D\sigma_{m-1}(x_{m-1})\cdots\,\D\sigma_{3}(x_{3})\D\sigma_{2}(x_{2})}{(x_{m-1}-x_{m})(x_{m-2}-x_{m-1})\cdots\,(x_{2}-x_{3})(x_{1}-x_{2})}.

When
m=2
, take

K(x_{1},x_{2})\equiv 1.

There exist two sequences of monic polynomials
(P_{n}),(Q_{n}),n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},
such that for each
n
,
\deg(P_{n})=\deg(Q_{n})=n
and

\int_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{m}}P_{k}(x_{1})K(x_{1},x_{m})Q_{n}(x_{m})\,\mbox{d}\sigma_{1}(x_{1})\,\mbox{d}\sigma_{m}(x_{m})=C_{n}\delta_{k,n},\qquad C_{n}\neq 0.

As usual,
\delta_{k,n}=0
,
k\neq n
,
\delta_{n,n}=1
. The polynomials
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
are called Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials with respect to
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
.

When
m=2
biorthogonal polynomials appear in the analysis of the two matrix model
[
4
,
6
]
and
in the search of discrete solutions of the Degasperis-Procesi equation
[
5
]
through a Hermite-Padé approximation problem for two discrete measures. In
[
6
]
a result on the strong asymptotics of Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials was obtained for a pair of Laguerre type weights with unbounded support and exponential decay at infinity. Using Theorem
1.4
we can give the strong asymptotics of general Cauchy biorthogonal polynomials with respect to a system of measures with compact support.

In
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
it was proved that the
n
-th biorthogonal polynomial
Q_{n}
with respect to
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
coincides with the ML Hermite-Padé polynomial
a_{\vec{n},m}
of the Nikishin system
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
associated with the multi-index
\vec{n}=(n,0,\ldots,0)
. Because of the symmetry of the kernel it turns out that the biorthogonal polynomial
P_{n}
coincides with the corresponding
a_{\vec{n},m}
constructed for the reversed Nikishin system
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{m},\ldots,\sigma_{1})
and the multi-index
\vec{n}=(n,0,\ldots,0)
(see
[
10
, Subsection 2.3]
. Let us focus on
Q_{n}
. From Theorem
1.4
, we obtain

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that
\vec{\sigma}\in S(\vec{\Delta})
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{Q_{n}(z)}{\Phi_{m}^{n}(z)}=\frac{\mathsf{G}_{m}(z)}{\mathsf{G}_{m}(\infty)},

where
\Phi_{m}
is given by (
1.10
) for the vector equilibrium problem on the system of intervals
\vec{\Delta}
with interaction matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{r r r r r}1&-1/2&0&\cdots&0\\
-1/2&1&-1/2&\ddots&0\\
0&-1/2&1&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\end{array}\right)_{m\times m}

and
\mathsf{G}_{m}
is the last component of the fixed point of the operator

\vec{T}_{\vec{w}}
for the vector weight (
3.4
)
where
\tilde{h}_{m}\equiv 1
and the functions
\tilde{h}_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m-1

An example related to Whitney’s extension problem for L 2 , p ​ ( ℝ 2 ) L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) when 1 < p < 2 1<p<2
Jacob Carruth, Arie Israel

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the existence of a bounded linear extension operator T : L 2 , p ​ ( E ) → L 2 , p ​ ( ℝ 2 ) T:L^{2,p}(E)\rightarrow L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) when 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 , where E ⊂ ℝ 2 E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} is a certain discrete set with fractal structure. Our proof makes use of a theorem of Fefferman-Klartag [ 3 ] on the existence of linear extension operators for radially symmetric binary trees.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a space of continuous, real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with a norm (or seminorm)
||\cdot||
. For any subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define the trace norm (or seminorm) of a continuous function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

||f||_{\mathbb{X}(\Omega)}=\inf\{||F||:F|_{\Omega}=f\}.

The
trace space

\mathbb{X}(\Omega)
is then defined to be the space of continuous functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with finite trace norm.

We say that a linear map
T:\mathbb{X}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
linear extension operator for
\mathbb{X}(\Omega)
provided that
(Tf)|_{\Omega}=f
for all
f\in\mathbb{X}(\Omega)
. We say that
\mathbb{X}(\Omega)
admits a bounded linear extension operator
if there exists a constant
C
(determined only by the function space
\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) such that there exists a bounded linear extension operator for
\mathbb{X}(\Omega)
with operator norm at most
C
. One formulation of the
Whitney extension problem
for
\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
asks whether
\mathbb{X}(\Omega)
admits a bounded linear extension operator for every subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the (homogeneous) Sobolev space of all functions
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
whose (distributional) partial derivatives of order
m
belong to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, equipped with the seminorm
\|F\|_{L^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\nabla^{m}F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. We assume that
\frac{n}{m}<p<\infty
, so that functions
F\in L^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are continuous – this ensures that the trace space
L^{m,p}(\Omega)
is well-defined for an arbitrary subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In
[
2
]
, the second-named author, C. Fefferman, and G.K. Luli proved that
L^{m,p}(\Omega)
admits a bounded linear extension operator when
p>n
, with operator norm at most a constant
C=C(m,n,p)
, for an arbitrary subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When
p
is in the range
\frac{m}{n}<p\leq n
, however, it is unknown whether such an operator exists in general.

The first nontrivial case in this range is the space
\mathbb{X}=L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
1<p<2
(when
p=2
, it is easy to construct a bounded linear extension operator thanks to the Hilbert space structure). In this paper, we construct a bounded linear extension operator for
L^{2,p}(E)
for a particular set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
to be defined below. This set
E
appears to be exceptional. For a variety of sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
it is possible to construct a bounded linear extension operator for
L^{2,p}(\Omega)
by making certain analogies with the case
p>2
. Our construction for
L^{2,p}(E)
, however, requires completely new ideas.

We now define the set
E
. We introduce a real number
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
. We assume that
\varepsilon
is smaller than some absolute constant. For an integer
L\geq 1
, we define

\Delta=\varepsilon^{L}.

The set
E
is of the form

E=E_{1}\cup E_{2},

where

\displaystyle E_{1}=\{(\Delta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-1,1]\}\times\{0\}\;\mbox{and}

\displaystyle E_{2}=\Big\{(z,\Delta):z=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}s_{\ell}\varepsilon^{\ell},s_{\ell}\in\{-1,+1\}\Big\},

Accordingly,
E_{1}
is a set of equispaced
\Delta
-separated points on the line
x_{2}=0
, and
E_{2}
is a set of points of separation
\geq\Delta
on the line
x_{2}=\Delta
. For convenience, we assume that for every
(x,\Delta)\in E_{2}
we have
(x,0)\in E_{1}
. We can ensure this by, e.g., taking
\varepsilon=1/N
for a large, positive integer
N
.

In this paper, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<2
. There exists a bounded linear extension operator
T:L^{2,p}(E)\rightarrow L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, depending on
p
, satisfying

\|Tf\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2,p}(E)}\;\text{for any}\;f\in L^{2,p}(E)

for some constant
C
depending only on
p
.

We now say a bit about the construction of the operator
T
. We fix a square
Q^{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
containing
E
with diameter
\sim 1
. Given
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we will describe how to produce a function
F\in L^{2,p}(Q^{0})
satisfying
F|_{E}=f
and having the property that

||F||_{L^{2,p}(Q^{0})}\leq C||G||_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(1)

for any
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for which
G|_{E}=f
. Once we accomplish this, it is not difficult to deduce Theorem
1
.

We first partition the square
Q^{\circ}
into a Calderón-Zygmund decomposition
CZ
, which is a finite family of dyadic squares
Q\subset Q^{0}
with pairwise disjoint interiors. Our decomposition is a version of the Whitney decomposition of
Q^{0}\setminus E
truncated at scale
\sim\Delta
. Briefly, every
Q\in\text{CZ}
has sidelength
\delta_{Q}\approx\Delta+\text{dist}(Q,E)
and satisfies
\#(1.1Q\cap E)\leq 1
. In particular, the smallest cubes in
CZ
have sidelength proportional to
\Delta
and distance to
E
bounded by
C\Delta
– see section
3
for further details. Our function
F\in L^{2,p}(Q^{0})
takes the form

F(x)=\sum_{Q\in\text{CZ}}\theta_{Q}(x)\cdot P_{Q}(x),

(2)

where
\{\theta_{Q}\}_{Q\in\text{CZ}}
is a Whitney partition of unity subordinate to
CZ
– more precisely, (1)
\sum_{Q}\theta_{Q}=1
on
Q^{0}
, (2)
\text{supp}(\theta_{Q})\subset 1.1Q
, and (3)
|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}|\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{-k}
for each
Q\in\text{CZ}
– and each
P_{Q}
is an affine polynomial satisfying

P_{Q}=f\mbox{ on }1.1Q\cap E.

(3)

Using (
3
) and the support condition on
\theta_{Q}
, we see that the function
F
will satisfy
F|_{E}=f
. For each
Q\in\text{CZ}
,
P_{Q}
is of the form

P_{Q}(z)=L_{Q}(z)+\eta_{Q}\cdot z^{(2)}\;\text{for}\;z=(z^{(1)},z^{(2)})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\eta_{Q}
is a real number and
L_{Q}
is an affine polynomial satisfying
\partial_{2}L_{Q}=0
. Due to the structure of the set
E_{1}
, our hand is essentially forced when it comes to choosing the
L_{Q}
’s – specifically, we demand that each
L_{Q}
agrees with the function
f
at two suitably chosen points of the set
E_{1}
. The bulk of the work, then, is in choosing the numbers
\{\eta_{Q}\}_{Q\in\text{CZ}}
to ensure a certain natural
compatibility condition
on the family
\{P_{Q}\}_{Q\in\text{CZ}}
, namely, the condition:

\sum_{Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ},Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}}\delta_{Q}^{2-p}|\nabla P_{Q}-\nabla P_{Q^{\prime}}|^{p}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p},

(4)

where
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}
denotes the property that
1.1Q\cap 1.1Q^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
for two squares
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}
(we say that
Q
“touches”
Q^{\prime}
), and
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is any function satisfying
G|_{E}=f
. Estimate (
4
) is the essential ingredient in the proof of the norm bound (
1
) for the function
F
defined in (
2
).

Roughly speaking, each
\eta_{Q}
corresponds to the average
x^{(2)}
-partial derivative of
F
on the square
Q
. Since the value of
f
near points of the set
E_{2}
is our only source of information about the
x^{(2)}
-partial derivative of
F
, we make use of a natural hierarchical clustering
\mathcal{C}
of the set
E_{2}
to choose the
\eta_{Q}
’s.

Elements
C\in\mathcal{C}
correspond to the subsets of
E_{2}
of the form
C(s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}):=\{(z,\Delta):z=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}s_{\ell}\epsilon^{\ell},\;s_{k+1},\cdots,s_{L}\in\{-1,+1\}\}
for fixed
(s_{1},\cdots,s_{k})\in\{-1,+1\}^{k}
. The smallest clusters correspond to the
2^{L}
different singleton sets in
E_{2}
(when
k=L
), and the largest cluster corresponds to
C=E_{2}
(when
k=0
). Distinct clusters are “well-separated” in the sense that
\text{dist}(C_{1},C_{2})\gg\max\{\mathrm{diam}(C_{1}),\mathrm{diam}(C_{2})\}
for
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
,
C_{1}\neq C_{2}
.

The set of clusters
\mathcal{C}
has the structure of a rooted binary tree of depth
L
. The clusters corresponding to the leaves of the tree are the singletons
\{z\}
(
z\in E_{2}
), and
E_{2}
is the root of
\mathcal{C}
.

To every square
Q\in\text{CZ}
, we associate a cluster
C_{Q}\in\mathcal{C}
. If
1.1Q\cap E_{2}\neq\emptyset
then
Q
is associated to the singleton cluster
C_{Q}=\{x_{Q}\}
where
x_{Q}
is a point of
1.1Q\cap E_{2}
. To every other square
Q
we associate a cluster
C_{Q}\in\mathcal{C}
that is nearby to
Q
.

We then introduce real numbers
\{\eta_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
and postulate that

\eta_{Q}=\eta_{C_{Q}}\;\text{for every}\;Q\in\text{CZ}.

(5)

For each
z=(z^{(1)},\Delta)\in E_{2}
, we define

\eta_{\{z\}}=\frac{f(z)-f(z^{(1)},0)}{\Delta}.

This is natural, as for squares
Q\in\text{CZ}
satisfying
1.1Q\cap E_{2}\neq\emptyset
we will need to choose
\eta_{Q}=\eta_{\{x_{Q}\}}
where
x_{Q}\in 1.1Q\cap E_{2}
, to ensure that
P_{Q}
satisfies (
3
).

Now that we have chosen
(\eta_{\{z\}})_{z\in E_{2}}
, we need to choose the remaining
\eta_{C}
so as to establish the compatibility condition (
4
) for the
P_{Q}
. We use the following theorem of C. Fefferman and B. Klartag, proved in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2

(Fefferman-Klartag, 2023)
.

Consider a full, binary tree of depth
N
with vertices
V
. Let
V_{k}
denote the set of vertices of depth
k
. For any
v\in V_{k}
(
k=1,\dots,N
), let
\pi(v)
denote the parent of
v
.

Write
\partial V\subset V
to denote the set of leaves of
V
(i.e.,
\partial V=V_{N}
). Let
\mathbb{R}^{V}
and
\mathbb{R}^{\partial V}
denote the sets of real-valued functions on
V
and
\partial V
, respectively.

Given weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{N}>0
, define the
L^{1,p}(V)
-seminorm by

\|\Phi\|_{L^{1,p}(V)}=\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}w_{k}\sum_{v\in V_{k}}|\Phi(\pi(v))-\Phi(v)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\;\text{for any}\;\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{V}.

(6)

Define the
L^{1,p}
trace seminorm by

\|\phi\|_{L^{1,p}(\partial V)}=\inf\{\|\Phi\|_{L^{1,p}(V)}:\Phi|_{E}=\phi\}\;\text{for any}\;\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{\partial V}.

There exists a linear operator
H:\mathbb{R}^{\partial V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{V}
with the following properties:

1.

H
is an extension operator, i.e.,

H\phi|_{\partial V}=\phi\;\text{for any}\;\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{\partial V}.

2.

For any
\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{\partial V}
, we have

\|H\phi\|_{L^{1,p}(V)}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{1,p}(\partial V)}.

We apply Theorem
2
to the binary tree
\mathcal{C}
equipped with a natural set of edge weights, and choose the
\eta_{C}
by applying the extension operator
H
to the data
\{\eta_{\{z\}}\}_{z\in E_{2}}
; because the resulting choice of
\{\eta_{C}\}
minimizes the seminorm (
6
), one can show the resulting polynomials
\{P_{Q}\}
defined in terms of the
\{\eta_{Q}\}
(which are related to the
\{\eta_{C}\}
in (
5
)) will satisfy (
4
). As mentioned before, the norm bound (
1
) follows as a consequence of this condition.

Because the
\eta_{\{z\}}
depend linearly on
f
, and the
\eta_{C}
depend linearly on
\{\eta_{\{z\}}\}_{z\in E_{2}}
, it is obvious that the
P_{Q}
depend linearly on
f
, and so,
F
depends linearly on
f
.

It is finally trivial to extend
F:Q^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to a function
\widetilde{F}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2,p}(Q^{0})}
. Since
F\in L^{2,p}(Q^{0})
is an extension of
f
satisfying the norm bound (
1
), we deduce that
\widetilde{F}\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is an extension of
f
satisfying
\|\widetilde{F}\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|G\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
for any
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
G|_{E}=f
. Thus, we cam set
Tf=\widetilde{F}
, and
T
is a bounded linear extension operator for
L^{2,p}(E)
.

This concludes our overview of the proof of Theorem
1
.

We remark that when
\varepsilon
is bounded away from a certain “critical value”
\varepsilon_{0}=(1/2)^{1/(2-p)}
, it is possible to prove Theorem
1
without invoking Theorem
2
. In particular, one can construct an extension operator for
L^{2,p}(E)
by making certain analogies with the case
p>2
. When
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
, however, we know of no such construction.

Throughout this paper we write
C,C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime},\dots
to denote positive constants. Such constants are allowed to depend only on
p
(but not on
\varepsilon,L
). We write
C_{X},C^{\prime}_{X},\dots
for positive constants depending on some quantity
X
. For positive real numbers
A,B
we write
A\lesssim B
if there exists a constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
and
A\lesssim_{X}B
if
A\leq C_{X}B
. We write
A\approx B
if
A\lesssim B
and
A\lesssim B
.

Preliminaries

For a (Lebesgue) measurable function
F
defined on a measurable set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|S|>0
, we write
(F)_{S}:=|S|^{-1}\int_{S}F\ dx
. We begin by stating a couple of standard inequalities for Sobolev spaces. For details see, e.g.,
[
4
]
.

Given an annulus
A=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:r\leq|x-x_{0}|\leq R\}
with inner radius
r
and outer radius
R
, the
thickness ratio
of
A
is defined to be the quantity
R/r
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a square, a ball, or an annulus with thickness ratio at most
C_{0}\in[1,\infty)
. Then the following hold.

1.

Let
1<r<2
, and
r^{\prime}=2r/(2-r)
. Then for any
F\in L^{2,r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we have

\bigg(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F(x)-(\nabla F)_{\Omega}|^{r^{\prime}}\ dx\bigg)^{1/r^{\prime}}\lesssim_{r,C_{0}}\|F\|_{L^{2,r}(\Omega)}.

2.

Let
q>2
. Then for any
F\in L^{1,q}(\Omega)
we have (after potentially redefining
F
on a set of measure 0) that

|F(x)-F(y)|\lesssim_{q,C_{0}}|x-y|^{1-2/q}\|F\|_{L^{1,q}}\;\text{for any}\;x,y\in\Omega.

We use these inequalities to prove the following basic lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a square, a ball, or an annulus with thickness ratio at most
C_{0}\in[1,\infty)
and let
1<r<2
. For any
F\in L^{2,r}(\Omega)
and any
y\in\Omega
, we define a function
T_{y,\Omega}(F):\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

T_{y,\Omega}(F)(x)=F(y)+(\nabla F)_{\Omega}\cdot(x-y).

We then have

|T_{x,\Omega}(F)(y)-T_{z,\Omega}(F)(y)|\lesssim_{r,C_{0}}\|F\|_{L^{2,r}(\Omega)}|x-z|^{2-2/r}\;\text{for any}\;x,z\in\Omega.

In particular,

\|F-T_{y,\Omega}(F)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{r,C_{0}}\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{2-2/r}\|F\|_{L^{2,r}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
r^{\prime}=2r/(2-r)
. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
define
G_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

G_{x}(y)=T_{y,\Omega}(F)(x).

By Part 1 of Lemma
1
, we have

\|G_{x}\|_{L^{1,r^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{r,C_{0}}\|F\|_{L^{2,r}(\Omega)}.

Note that
r^{\prime}>2
, since
r<2
. Applying Part 2 of Lemma
1
to the function
G_{x}
proves the lemma.
∎

Let
B(z,r)
denote the ball of radius
r>0
centered at
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and let
\mathcal{M}
denote the uncentered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, i.e.,

(\mathcal{M}f)(x)=\sup_{B(z,r)\ni x}\frac{1}{|B(z,r)|}\int_{B(z,r)}f(y)\ dy\;\text{for any}\;f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Recall that
\mathcal{M}
is a bounded operator from
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
1<q\leq\infty
(see, e.g.,
[
5
]
).

3
The CZ decomposition

We will work with squares in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; by this we mean an axis parallel square of the form
Q=[a_{1},b_{1})\times[a_{2},b_{2})
. We let
\delta_{Q}
denote the sidelength of such a square
Q
. To
bisect
a square
Q
is to partition
Q
into squares
Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3},Q_{4}
, where
\delta_{Q_{i}}=\delta_{Q}/2
for each
i=1,2,3,4
. We refer to the
Q_{i}
as the
children
of
Q
.

We define a square
Q^{0}=[-4,4)\times[-4,4)
; note that
E\subset Q^{0}
. A
dyadic
square
Q
is one that arises from repeated bisection of
Q^{0}
. Every dyadic square
Q\neq Q^{0}
is the child of some square
Q^{\prime}
; we call
Q^{\prime}
the
parent
of
Q
and denote this by
(Q)^{+}=Q^{\prime}
.

We say that two dyadic square
Q,Q^{\prime}

touch
if
(1.1Q\cap 1.1Q^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. We write
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}
to denote that
Q
touches
Q^{\prime}
.

For any dyadic square
Q
, we define a collection
\text{CZ}(Q)
, called the
Calderón-Zygmund decomposition of

Q
, by setting

\text{CZ}(Q)=\{Q\}\;\text{if }\#(3Q\cap E)\leq 1,

and

\text{CZ}(Q)=\bigcup\{\text{CZ}(Q^{\prime}):(Q^{\prime})^{+}=Q\}\;\text{if}\;\#(3Q\cap E)\geq 2.

We write
\text{CZ}=\text{CZ}(Q^{0})
. Note that
\text{CZ}\neq\{Q^{0}\}
because
\#(3Q^{0}\cap E)=\#E\geq 2
. Then
CZ
is a partition of
Q^{0}
into dyadic squares
Q
satisfying

\delta_{Q}\geq\frac{\Delta}{9}.

(7)

To see this, observe that for any
Q\in\text{CZ}
we have
\#(3Q^{+}\cap E)\geq 2
and
3Q^{+}\subset 9Q
. Since points of
E
are
\Delta
-separated, we have
\delta_{Q}\geq\Delta/9
, as claimed.

We now summarize some basic properties of the collection
CZ
.

Lemma 3
.

The collection
\emph{CZ}
has the following properties:

1.

For any
Q\in\emph{CZ}
, we have
\#(1.1Q\cap E)\leq 1
and
\#(3Q^{+}\cap E)\geq 2
.

2.

For any
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\emph{CZ}
with
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\delta_{Q}\leq\delta_{Q^{\prime}}\leq 2\delta_{Q}
.

3.

For any
Q\in\emph{CZ}
, we have

\#\{Q^{\prime}:Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}\}\lesssim 1.

4.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\#\{Q\in\emph{CZ}:x\in 1.1Q\}\lesssim 1.

5.

For any
Q\in\emph{CZ}
with
\#(1.1Q\cap E)=0
, we have
\delta_{Q}\approx\emph{dist}(Q,E)
.

We omit the proof of Lemma
3
, as this type of decomposition is standard in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
1
]
.

We say that
Q\in\text{CZ}
is a
boundary square
if
1.1Q\cap\partial Q^{0}\neq\emptyset
. Denote the set of boundary squares by
\partial\text{CZ}
.

Observe that every
Q\in\partial\text{CZ}
satisfies
\delta_{Q}\geq 1
. Indeed, this follows because
E\subset[-1,1]\times[-1,1]
, and if
Q
is a dyadic square intersecting the boundary of
Q^{0}
with
\delta_{Q}=1
then
3Q
is disjoint from
E
. We denote
z_{0}:=(-1,0)
,
w_{0}:=(1,0)
to be the points of maximal separation in
E_{1}
. Note that

z_{0},w_{0}\in 50Q\mbox{ for all }Q\in\partial\text{CZ}.

(8)

We say that a square
Q\in\text{CZ}
is of
Type I
if
\#(1.1Q\cap E_{1})=1
,
Type II
if
\#(1.1Q\cap E_{2})=1
, and
Type III
if
\#(1.1Q\cap E)=0
. We denote the collection of squares of Type I, II, and III by
\text{CZ}_{I}
,
\text{CZ}_{II}
, and
\text{CZ}_{III}
, respectively. These collections form a partition of
CZ
because
\#(3Q\cap E)\leq 1
for any
Q\in\text{CZ}
by construction.

Observe that
\partial\text{CZ}\subset\text{CZ}_{III}
.

From Property 1 of Lemma
3
, it is easy to deduce that

\delta_{Q}\approx\Delta\;\text{for every}\;Q\in\text{CZ}_{I}\cup\text{CZ}_{II}.

(9)

From Property 5 of Lemma
3
we have

\delta_{Q}\approx\text{dist}(Q,E)\;\text{for every}\;Q\in\text{CZ}_{III}.

Combining this with (
7

4
Constructing the interpolant

Let
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. In this section, we will construct a function
F:Q^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
F|_{E}=f
.

Let
\{\theta_{Q}\}_{Q\in\text{CZ}}
be a partition of unity subordinate to
CZ
constructed so that the following properties hold. For any
Q\in\text{CZ}
, we have:

(POU1)

\text{supp}(\theta_{Q})\subset 1.1Q
.

(POU2)

For any
|\alpha|\leq 2
,
\|\partial^{\alpha}\theta_{Q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{-|\alpha|}.

(POU3)

0\leq\theta_{Q}\leq 1
.

For any
x\in Q^{0}
, we have

(POU4)

\sum_{Q\in\text{CZ}}\theta_{Q}(x)=1.

Our interpolant
F
will then be of the form

F=\sum_{Q\in\text{CZ}}P_{Q}\theta_{Q},

(11)

where each
P_{Q}
is an affine polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the form

P_{Q}(x)=L_{Q}(x)+\eta_{Q}\cdot x^{(2)}\;\text{for}\;x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Here,
L_{Q}
is an affine polynomial satisfying
\partial_{2}L_{Q}=0
and
\eta_{Q}
is a real number.

In the previous section we have associated to each square
Q\in\text{CZ}
the points
z_{Q},w_{Q}\in E_{1}
. We now define
L_{Q}
to be the unique affine polynomial satisfying:

L_{Q}|_{\{z_{Q},w_{Q}\}}=f|_{\{z_{Q},w_{Q}\}}\mbox{ and }\partial_{2}L_{Q}=0.

(12)

Thanks to Property 4 of Lemma
4
there exists an affine polynomial
L_{0}
for which

L_{Q}=L_{0}\;\text{for all}\;Q\in\partial\text{CZ}.

We will choose the
\eta_{Q}
later in Sections
6
and
7
.

We now compute a simple upper bound for the
L^{2,p}
-seminorm of
F
.

Lemma 5

(The Patching Lemma)
.

The function
F
satisfies

\|F\|^{p}_{L^{2,p}(Q^{0})}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}:\\
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\Big\{\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}^{p}\delta_{Q}^{2-2p}+|\eta_{Q}-\eta_{Q^{\prime}}|^{p}\delta_{Q}^{2-p}\Big\}.

Proof.

Fix a square
Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}
. Observe that

F(x)=\sum_{Q\in\text{CZ}}\theta_{Q}(x)[P_{Q}(x)-P_{Q^{\prime}}(x)]+P_{Q^{\prime}}(x).

By Property 4 of Lemma
3
, there are a bounded number of squares
Q\in\text{CZ}
for which
x\in(1.1Q)\cap Q^{\prime}
. Therefore, by
(POU1)
, there are a bounded number of
Q\in\text{CZ}
with
\text{supp}(\theta_{Q})\cap Q^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Taking
2^{\text{nd}}
derivatives, using
(POU2)
, and integrating
p^{\text{th}}
powers then gives

\|F\|_{L^{2,p}(Q^{\prime})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\text{CZ}:\\
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\big\{\delta_{Q}^{2-2p}\|P_{Q}-P_{Q^{\prime}}\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+\delta_{Q}^{2-p}|\nabla(P_{Q}-P_{Q^{\prime}})|\big\}.

For any affine polynomial
P
we have
|\nabla P|\leq\delta_{Q}^{-1}||P||_{L^{\infty}(Q)}
, and thus

\|F\|_{L^{2,p}(Q^{\prime})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\text{CZ}:\\
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\delta_{Q}^{2-2p}\|P_{Q}-P_{Q^{\prime}}\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

By the triangle inequality, and the fact that
|z^{(2)}|\leq C\delta_{Q}
whenever
z\in 1.1Q
,

\displaystyle\|P_{Q}-P_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+|\eta_{Q}-\eta_{Q^{\prime}}|\|z^{(2)}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+|\eta_{Q}-\eta_{Q^{\prime}}|\delta_{Q}.

Therefore,

\|F\|_{L^{2,p}(Q^{\prime})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\text{CZ}:\\
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left\{\delta_{Q}^{2-2p}\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+\delta_{Q}^{2-p}|\eta_{Q}-\eta_{Q^{\prime}}|^{p}\right\}.

Since
CZ
is partition of
Q^{0}
, summing over
Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}
proves the lemma.
∎

5
Establishing compatibility of the
L_{Q}

In this section we will prove that for any function
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
G|_{E}=f
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}:\\
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}^{p}\delta_{Q}^{2-2p}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}.

(13)

Fix some
r
satisfying
1<r<p
. We claim that for any
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}
with
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}
we have

\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}^{r}\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{2r-2}\|G\|_{L^{2,r}(250Q)}^{r}.

(14)

Observe that for any
x\in Q
we have

L_{Q}(x)-L_{Q^{\prime}}(x)=f(z_{Q})+\partial_{1}L_{Q}\cdot(x-z_{Q})-f(z_{Q^{\prime}})-\partial_{1}L_{Q^{\prime}}\cdot(x-z_{Q^{\prime}}).

Therefore, since
G(z_{Q^{\prime}})=f(z_{Q^{\prime}})
and
\partial_{2}L_{Q}=\partial_{2}L_{Q^{\prime}}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq

\displaystyle|f(z_{Q})-T_{z_{Q^{\prime}},250Q}(G)(z_{Q})|

\displaystyle+|(\partial_{1}G)_{250Q}-\partial_{1}L_{Q}|\cdot\delta_{Q}+|(\partial_{1}G)_{250Q}-\partial_{1}L_{Q^{\prime}}|\cdot\delta_{Q}.

Recall that for any
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}
with
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}
we have
\frac{1}{2}\delta_{Q^{\prime}}\leq\delta_{Q}\leq 2\delta_{Q^{\prime}}
(see Lemma
3
), and therefore
50Q^{\prime}\subset 250Q
. In particular, we have

\{z_{Q},w_{Q},z_{Q^{\prime}},w_{Q^{\prime}}\}\subset(250Q)\cap E_{1}.

Since
G|_{E}=f
, Lemma
2
then implies

\displaystyle|f(z_{Q})-T_{z_{Q^{\prime}},250Q}(G)(z_{Q})|\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{2-2/r}\|G\|_{L^{2,r}(250Q)},

\displaystyle|f(z_{Q})-f(w_{Q})-(\partial_{1}G)_{250Q}\cdot(z_{Q}-w_{Q})|\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{2-2/r}\|G\|_{L^{2,r}(250Q)},

\displaystyle|f(z_{Q^{\prime}})-f(w_{Q^{\prime}})-(\partial_{1}G)_{250Q}\cdot(z_{Q^{\prime}}-w_{Q^{\prime}})|\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{2-2/r}\|G\|_{L^{2,r}(250Q)};

we deduce (
14
).

The inequality (
14
) implies that

\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}^{r}\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{2r}\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})(z)\;\text{for any}\;z\in Q,

where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator (see Section
2
). Taking
(p/r)
-th powers and integrating gives

\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}^{p}\lesssim\delta_{Q}^{2p-2}\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(Q)}^{p/r}.

By Properties 2 and 3 of Lemma
3
, we deduce

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{CZ}:\\
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\|L_{Q}-L_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}^{p}\delta_{Q}^{2-2p}\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\text{CZ}}\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(Q)}^{p/r}.

Since
CZ
is a pairwise disjoint collection of squares, we have

\sum_{Q\in\text{CZ}}\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(Q)}^{p/r}\leq\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p/r}.

We use the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator from
L^{q}
to
L^{q}
for
1<q\leq\infty
to deduce (
13
).

, and
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}
, it must be the case that
Q,Q^{\prime}
are contained in a
c\delta_{Q}
-neighborhood of the boundary of
B_{C}
for some absolute constant
c
. By (
10
), it is also the case that
Q,Q^{\prime}
are at most distance
c^{\prime}\delta_{Q}
from the
x^{(1)}
-axis for another absolute constant
c^{\prime}
. Here,
\delta_{Q}=\delta
is fixed. A simple packing argument then yields (
23
). Combining (
21
) and (
23
), we show that

\displaystyle\sum_{(Q,Q^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Q}_{C}}\delta_{Q}^{2-p}\leq\sum_{k\leq\log_{2}(c\epsilon^{\ell+1})}\sum_{(Q,Q^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Q}_{C},\delta_{Q}=2^{k}}(2^{k})^{2-p}

(24)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\leq\log_{2}(c\epsilon^{\ell+1})}2^{k(2-p)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\quad(C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\ell=1,\cdots,L-1).

(Note that we’ve used the assumption
(2-p)>0
.)

Combining (
20
) with (
22
), (
24
), allows us to continue the bound in (
19
),

\begin{split}\sum_{Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}}|\eta_{Q}-\eta_{Q^{\prime}}|^{p}\delta_{Q}^{2-p}\lesssim&\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|\eta_{\pi(C)}-\eta_{C}|^{p}\\
&+\varepsilon^{L(2-p)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}}|\eta_{\pi(C)}-\eta_{C}|^{p}.\end{split}

(25)

For
\ell=1,\dots,L
we define

\nu_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}&\text{for}\;\ell=1,\dots,L-1,\\
\varepsilon^{L(2-p)}&\text{for}\;\ell=L.\end{cases}

(26)

We then rewrite inequality (
25
) as

\sum_{Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\prime}}|\eta_{Q}-\eta_{Q^{\prime}}|^{p}\delta_{Q}^{2-p}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\nu_{\ell}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|\eta_{\pi(C)}-\eta_{C}|^{p}.

(27)

In the next section we will choose the remaining
\eta_{C}
(for
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
with
\ell=0,1,\dots,L-1
) and prove that our choices satisfy suitable estimates. Finally, in the last section we will explain how to use those estimates to complete the proof of Theorem
1
.

7
Step II of choosing the
\eta_{Q}
: Minimizing
\|F\|_{L^{2,p}}

Recall that we have already defined
\eta_{C}
for
C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}
in (
17
).

The goal of this section is to choose
\eta_{C}
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
(
\ell=0,1,\dots,L-1)
so that for any
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
G|_{E}=f
we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\nu_{\ell}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|\eta_{\pi(C)}-\eta_{C}|^{p}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}.

(28)

This is easy to do, thanks to Theorem
2
. Recall
\nu_{\ell}
are defined in (
26
).

The following is an immediate corollary of Theorem
2
.

Corollary 1
.

There exist
\eta_{C}
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
(
\ell=1,\dots,L-1)
, depending linearly on
\{\eta_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}},
so that the following holds. Let
\{\gamma_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
be a collection of real numbers with
\gamma_{C}=\eta_{C}
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}
. Then

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\nu_{\ell}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|\eta_{\pi(C)}-\eta_{C}|^{p}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\nu_{\ell}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|\gamma_{\pi(C)}-\gamma_{C}|^{p}.

We fix the definition of
\{\eta_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
as in Corollary
1
. Recall that
\{\eta_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}}
depend linearly on the data
f
– see (
17
) and (
15
). Thus,
\eta_{C}
depends linearly on
f
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}
.

We claim that for any
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
G|_{E}=f
we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}|^{p}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}.

(29)

We will later show that (
29
) and Corollary
1
allow us to deduce estimate (
28
) for the
\{\eta_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
, completing the task of this section.

For every
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,\dots,L-1
, let
r_{C},x_{C}
denote the radius and center, respectively, of the ball
B_{C}
. For
k\geq 0
define the annulus

A_{C,k}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:10^{k}r_{C}\leq|x-x_{C}|<10^{k+1}r_{C}\}.

Recall (see Section
6
) that
\hat{B}_{C}
is also centered at
x_{C}
and that

\text{radius}(\hat{B}_{C})=10^{M+1}r_{C}.

for some positive integer
M
. Define another annulus

A_{C}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{M}A_{C,k}.

Note that the collection of annuli
\{A_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\ell=1,\dots L-1}
is pairwise disjoint.

We claim that for any
1<r<p
and for every
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
(
\ell=1,\dots,L-1
) we have

|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}|^{p}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(A_{C})}^{p/r}.

(30)

Since the
A_{C}
are pairwise disjoint, (
30
) implies

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}|^{p}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p/r};

we use the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator from
L^{p/r}
to
L^{p/r}
to deduce (
29
).

To prove (
30
), use Lemma
2
to get

\begin{split}|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{A_{C,0}}|&\lesssim\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(1-2/r)}\|G\|_{L^{2,r}(B_{C}\cup A_{C,0})}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{\ell+1}(\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r}(z))^{1/r}\end{split}

for any
z\in A_{C,0}
. (Recall that
r_{C}\approx\varepsilon^{\ell+1}
.) Taking
p
-th powers and integrating over
A_{C,0}
, we get

|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{A_{C,0}}|^{p}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(A_{C,0})}^{p/r}.

Similarly, we can show

\displaystyle|(\partial_{2}G)_{A_{C,k}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{A_{C,k+1}}|^{p}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\lesssim 10^{k(p-2)}\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(A_{C,k})}^{p/r},

\displaystyle|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{A_{C,M}}|^{p}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\lesssim 10^{M(p-2)}\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(A_{C,M})}^{p/r}.

We apply the triangle inequality and use that
p<2
to get

\begin{split}|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}|\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2/p-1)}&\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|^{1/r}_{L^{p/r}(A_{C})}\sum_{k=0}^{M}10^{k(1-2/p)}\\
&\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}(|\nabla^{2}G|^{r})\|^{1/r}_{L^{p/r}(A_{C})},\end{split}

proving (
30
), and thus, proving (
29
).

We now explain how to prove (
28
). We fix
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
G|_{E}=f
and define
(\gamma_{C})_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
in terms of
G
and
(\eta_{C})_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
. We take
\gamma_{C}=(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}
if
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
for
\ell=0,\dots,L-1
and
\gamma_{C}=\eta_{C}
if
C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}
. Now, we apply Corollary 1 to deduce that

\begin{split}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\nu_{\ell}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|\eta_{\pi(C)}-\eta_{C}|^{p}&\lesssim\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\varepsilon^{(\ell+1)(2-p)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{C}}|^{p}\\
&\qquad+\varepsilon^{L(2-p)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}}|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-\eta_{C}|^{p}.\end{split}

(31)

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}
. Observe that
\eta_{C}=\eta_{x_{C}}=\frac{f(x_{C})-f(x_{C}^{(1)},0)}{\Delta}=\frac{G(x_{C})-G(x_{C}^{(1)},0)}{\Delta}
. By Lemma
2
, we easily get that

|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-\eta_{C}|^{p}\varepsilon^{L(2-p)}\lesssim||G||_{L^{2,p}(B_{\pi(C)})}^{p}\;\text{for any}\;C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}.

The collection of balls
\{B_{\pi(C)}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}}
has bounded overlap, so

\varepsilon^{L(2-p)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{L}}|(\partial_{2}G)_{B_{\pi(C)}}-\eta_{C}|^{p}\lesssim||G||_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}.

Combining this estimate with (
31
) and (
29
), we complete the proof of (
28
).

8
Proof of Main Theorem

Combining Lemma
5
and the inequalities (
13
), (
27
), and (
28
) proves the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\{\eta_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
be defined via (
17
) and Corollary
1
, and let
\{\eta_{Q}\}_{Q\in\text{CZ}}
be defined in terms of
\{\eta_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}}
in (
18
). Then the function
F\in L^{2,p}(Q^{0})
defined by (
11
) satisfies:

•

F|_{E}=f

•

For any
G\in L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
G|_{E}=f
, we have

\|F\|_{L^{2,p}(Q^{0})}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

It is easy to deduce Theorem
1
from Lemma
6
. Recall that there is some affine polynomial
L_{0}
such that

L_{Q}=L_{0}\;\text{for every}\;Q\in\partial\text{CZ}.

Similarly, recall that for every
Q\in\partial\text{CZ}
we have
C_{Q}=E_{2}
. Writing
\eta_{0}=\eta_{E_{2}}
, we thus have

\eta_{Q}=\eta_{0}\;\text{for every}\;Q\in\partial\text{CZ}.

We define a function
\tilde{F}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by setting

\tilde{F}(x)=\begin{cases}F(x)&\text{if }x\in Q^{0},\\
L_{0}(x)+\eta_{0}\cdot x^{(2)}&\text{if }x\notin Q^{0}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\tilde{F}|_{E}=f
and
\|\tilde{F}\|_{L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|F\|_{L^{2,p}(Q^{0})}
. This proves Theorem
1
.

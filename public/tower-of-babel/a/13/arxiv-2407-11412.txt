Singular Nakano positivity of direct image sheaves of adjoint bundles
Takahiro INAYAMA, Shin-ichi MATSUMURA, Yuta WATANABE ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a proper Kähler fibration f : X → Y f\colon X\to Y and a singular Hermitian line bundle ( L , h ) (L,h) on X X with semi-positive curvature. We prove that the canonical L 2 L^{2} -metric on the direct image sheaf f ∗ ​ ( 𝒪 X ​ ( K X / Y + L ) ⊗ ℐ ⁡ ( h ) ) f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L)\otimes\mathcal{I}(h)) is singular Nakano semi-positive in the sense that the ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} -equation can be solved with optimal L 2 L^{2} -estimate. Our proof does not rely on the theory of Griffiths positivity for the direct image sheaf.

1.
Introduction

The theory of positivity of direct image sheaves of adjoint bundles originates
from Griffiths’ work
[
Gri70
]
,
which is based on the theory of variation of Hodge structures.
This theory has been further developed in subsequent works
[
Fuj78
,
Kaw81
,
Vie83
]

and other subsequent contributions from the viewpoint of algebraic geometry.
On the other hand, the analytic theory of the positivity of direct image sheaves
was initiated by the theory of variation of Bergman kernels by
[
MY04
]
and further advanced in
[
Ber09
]
.
This analytic approach has been extended by
[
HPS18
,
PT18
]

to establish the theory of
singular Griffiths positivity
of direct image sheaves
as a more applicable framework.
The main result of this paper (see Theorem
1.1
below)
establishes
singular Nakano positivity
of direct image sheaves
in the same framework as in
[
HPS18
,
PT18
]
, and further generalizes
the previous works
[
Wat23
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
DNWZ23
, Theorem 1.5]

on singular Nakano positivity.
Here, we adopt the definition of singular Nakano positivity based on
[
DNWZ23
,
Ina22
]
,
which uses solutions of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation with optimal
L^{2}
-estimates of Hörmander type
(see Definition
2.1
for the precise definition).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper Kähler fibration between complex manifolds
X
and
Y

(
i.e., a proper surjective morphism with connected fibers such that
the inverse image
f^{-1}(U_{Y})
is a Kähler manifold
for a sufficiently small open set
U_{Y}\subset Y
)
.
Let
(L,h)
be a singular Hermitian line bundle on
X
such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq f^{*}\theta
holds on
Y
in the sense of currents,
where
\theta
is a continuous
(1,1)
-form on
Y
.
Then, we have the following
:

(1)

The induced canonical
L^{2}
-metric
H
on the direct image sheaf
f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L)\otimes\mathcal{I}(h))

satisfies that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{H}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{loc.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}\text{ on }Y\text{ in the sense of Definition }\ref{def-dbarNak-sheaf}.

(2)
If we further assume that
X
is Kähler, then the canonical
L^{2}
-metric
H
satisfies that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{H}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{glo.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}\text{ on }Y\text{ in the sense of Definition }\ref{def-dbarNak-sheaf}.

Here
K_{X/Y}
is the relative canonical bundle and
\mathcal{I}(h)
is the multiplier ideal sheaf
associated with
h
.

To clarify our contribution, we review and compare the previous works to Theorem
1.1
.
Consider the setting of Theorem
1.1
and suppose that
\theta=0
.
When
h
is a smooth Hermitian metric and
f\colon X\to Y
is a smooth fibration (i.e., a holomorphic submersion),
the breakthrough work
[
Ber09
]
proved that
the canonical
L^{2}
-metric
H

is a smooth Hermitian metric on
f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L))

whose Chern curvature is Nakano positive.
This result has been generalized to the “non-smooth” setting by
[
HPS18
,
PT18
]
.
More specifically, building upon the work
[
PT18
]
,
the work
[
HPS18
]
proved that
H
is a (possibly) singular Hermitian metric on

f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L)\otimes\mathcal{I}(h))
satisfying Griffiths positivity.
This is an elegant application of the Ohsawa-Takegoshi
L^{2}
-extension theorem with optimal
L^{2}
-estimate.
For smooth Hermitian metrics, Nakano positivity is a stronger notion than Griffiths positivity.
Therefore, the study of Nakano positivity in the “non-smooth” setting is a natural and important problem.
In tackling this problem,
we face the difficulty that the Chern curvature cannot be defined for singular Hermitian metrics (see
[
Rau15
]
), and the correct definition of Nakano positivity is not immediately obvious.
The work
[
DNWZ23
]
showed that Nakano positivity of smooth Hermitian metrics
can be characterized by the
\overline{\partial}
-equation with optimal
L^{2}
-estimate.
In this paper, by adopting the definition based on this characterization,
we prove the main result, generalizing not only
[
Ber09
]
to the “non-smooth” setting
but also
[
HPS18
,
PT18
]
to singular Nakano positivity.
We emphasize that our proof does not rely on the Griffiths positivity.

This paper presents two applications of Theorem
1.1
.
The first application proves the singular Nakano positivity of the Narasimhan–Simha metric
on direct image sheaves of relative pluri-canonical bundles (see Corollary
4.1
).
In this proof, as opposed to Theorem
1.1
,
we use the Griffiths positivity results established in
[
HPS18
]
and
[
PT18
]
.

A vanishing theorem of Kodaira type can be expected for singular Nakano positive vector bundles,
which is an advantage of considering Nakano positivity compared to Griffiths positivity.
In this context, as the second application,
we prove a vanishing theorem of Kollár-Ohsawa type
(see Corollary
1.2
and cf.
[
Mat16
,
Ohs84
,
Kol86a
,
Kol86b
]
).
Corollary
1.2
follows directly from Theorem
1.1
and
[
Ina22
, Theorem 1.5]

when
Y
is projective and
f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+L)\otimes\mathcal{I}(h))
is a locally free sheaf.
However, in the general case,
we utilize the proof of Theorem
1.1

to solve the
\overline{\partial}
-equation with optimal
L^{2}
-estimates,
thereby deducing the vanishing theorem.
In this regard, Corollary
1.2
can be viewed as a quantitative version
of the vanishing theorem of Kollár-Ohsawa type.

Corollary 1.2

(cf.
[
Mat16
,
Ohs84
,
Kol86a
,
Kol86b
]
)
.

Consider the same situation as in Theorem
1.1
.
Assume that
X
is a weakly pseudo-convex Kähler manifold and
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq f^{*}\omega_{Y}
holds on
Y

for some Kähler form
\omega_{Y}
on
Y
.

Then, we have the following vanishing theorem
:

H^{q}(Y,f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+L)\otimes\mathcal{I}(h)))=0\quad\text{ for all $q>0$.}

Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
, we review the definition of singular Nakano positivity
and prove the technical lemmas necessary for the proof of the main result.
Section
3
is divided into two parts:
In the first half, we solve the
\bar{\partial}
-equation with optimal
L^{2}
-estimates
in an appropriate setting.
In the latter half, we derive Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
.
In Section
4
, as a corollary of Theorem
1.1
,
we prove the singular Nakano positivity of the Narasimhan–Simha metric on direct image sheaves.
In Appendix
A
, we summarize useful results on the approximation and extension of singular Hermitian metrics, which may already be known to experts.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by reviewing the definition of singular Nakano positivity.
The direct image sheaf treated in this paper is a torsion-free sheaf, but not necessarily locally free.
Therefore, for our purpose, we require Definition
2.2
.

Definition 2.1

(Singular Nakano positivity for vector bundles,
[
DNWZ23
,
Ina22
]
)
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on a complex manifold
X
and

\theta
be a continuous
(1,1)
-form on
X
.
Consider a singular Hermitian metric
h
on
E
on
X
with
h^{*}
being upper semi-continuous
(i.e., the function
|u|^{2}_{h^{*}}
is upper semi-continuous for any local section
u
of
E^{*}
).

(1) We define
the global
\theta
-Nakano positivity in the sense of
L^{2}
-estimates
, denoted by

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{glo.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\text{ on }X,

as follows:
For any data consisting of:

•

a Stein coordinate
\Omega\subset X
admitting a trivialization
E|_{\Omega}\cong\Omega\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
;

•

a Kähler form
\omega_{\Omega}
on
\Omega
;

•

a smooth function
\psi
on
\Omega
such that
\theta+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi>0
;

•

a positive integer
q
with
1\leq q\leq n
;

•

a
\overline{\partial}
-closed
g\in L^{2}_{n,q}(\Omega,E;\omega_{\Omega},he^{-\psi})

satisfying

\int_{\Omega}\langle B^{-1}_{\omega_{\Omega},\psi,\theta}g,g\rangle_{\omega_{\Omega},h}e^{-\psi}\,dV_{\omega_{\Omega}}<\infty,

there exists
u\in L^{2}_{n,q-1}(\Omega,E;\omega_{\Omega},he^{-\psi})
such that

\overline{\partial}u=g\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}_{\omega_{\Omega},h}e^{-\psi}\,dV_{\omega_{\Omega}}\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle B^{-1}_{\omega_{\Omega},\psi,\theta}g,g\rangle_{\omega_{\Omega},h}e^{-\psi}\,dV_{\omega_{\Omega}},

where
B_{\omega_{\Omega},\psi,\theta}=[(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi+\theta)\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E},\Lambda_{\omega_{\Omega}}]
.

(2)
We define
the local
\theta
-Nakano positivity in the sense of
L^{2}
-estimates
, denoted by

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{loc.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\text{ on }X,

as follows: For any point
x\in X
, there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x
such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{glo.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\text{ on }U.

Definition 2.2

(Singular Nakano positivity for torsion-free sheaves)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a torsion-free sheaf and
\theta
be a continuous
(1,1)
-form
on a complex manifold
X
.
Let
X_{\mathcal{E}}
denote the largest Zariski open set where
\mathcal{E}
is locally free.
Consider a singular Hermitian metric
h
on
\mathcal{E}

(i.e., a singular Hermitian metric on the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}|_{X_{\mathcal{E}}}
).
Assume that the function
|u|^{2}_{h^{*}}
is upper semi-continuous for any local section
u
of
(\mathcal{E}|_{X_{\mathcal{E}}})^{*}
.
We say that the sheaf
(\mathcal{E},h)
is global
\theta
-Nakano positive in the sense of

-estimates if
(\mathcal{E}|_{X_{\mathcal{E}}},h)

-Nakano positive.
We also say that the sheaf
(\mathcal{E},h)
is local
\theta
-Nakano positive in the sense of

-estimates
if for any point

of
x
such that

-Nakano positive.

In this paper, we need a generalization of this lemma to vector bundles. While the proof is straightforward,

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(E,h)
be a singular Hermitian vector bundle on a complex manifold
X
.
Assume that the norm
|t|_{h^{*}}
with respect to
h^{*}
is locally bounded
for any smooth section
t
of
E^{*}
,
where
h
is the dual metric on the dual vector bundle
E^{*}
.
Then, we have:

(1)

Let
s=\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}e_{i}
be a
(
not necessarily holomorphic or smooth
)

section of
E
, where
\{e_{i}\}_{{i=1}}^{r}
is a local frame of
E
.
For any
p

1\leq p\leq\infty
,
the following holds: If
|s|_{h}
is locally
L^{p}
-integrable,
then the absolute value
|s_{i}|
of each component

is also locally
L^{p}
-integrable.

(2)

Let
\omega_{X}
be a Hermitian form on
X
.
Let

be

-valued-differential forms on
X

|u|_{h,\omega_{X}}

The norm

Proof of the main results

Setting 3.1

Theorem 3.2

Under Setting

Proof.

The proof can be divided into four steps.

Step 1

(Strategy of the proof)
.

The purpose of this step is to explain the notation and the strategy of the proof.

Let
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
be a coordinate on an open subset

.
Then, the
E
-valued
(m,q)
-form
g

g=\sum_{|J|=q}g_{J}\,dt\wedge d\overline{t}_{J}\text{ and }g_{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{J,i}e_{i},

where

\bullet

runs through the multi-indices of degree

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

The section

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}:=\sum_{|J|=q}\big(g_{J}\otimes f^{*}dt\big)\wedge f^{*}d\overline{t}_{J}

Following

\bullet

\omega_{X_{y}}:={\omega_{X}}|_{X_{y}}

By the property of

In particular, the integrand does not depend on the choice of Kähler forms on

.

(1) It is obvious that
\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{t}\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|J|=q}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\partial G_{J}(t,z)}{\partial\overline{t}_{k}}\,d\overline{t}_{k}\wedge dz\wedge\,dt\wedge d\overline{t}_{J}

(see

(2)

of the left-hand side in Claim

converges to

Appendix A

In this section, we summarize a standard approximation method for almost everywhere (a.e.) Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metrics, as described in
[
PT18
, Definition 2.2.2]
. This result can be seen as a generalization of Berndtsson-Paun’s regularization theorem for singular Hermitian metrics with Griffiths semi-negative curvature. As an application of this result, we obtain an extension theorem for Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metrics. While these results may be familiar to experts, we provide detailed explanations for the convenience of readers.

Lemma A.1

(cf.
[
BP08
, Proposition 3.1]
,
[
PT18
, Remark 2.2.3]
,
[
Wat24
, Proof of Theorem 5.10]
)
.

Let
h
be an a.e. Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric on the trivial bundle
U\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
on a domain
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
h_{i\bar{j}}
denote
h(e_{i},e_{j})
,
where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
is the standard frame of
U\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
.
For a standard approximate identity
\rho_{\varepsilon}
, we define a sequence of smooth Hermitian metrics
\{h_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
by the convolution

h_{\varepsilon,i\bar{j}}=h_{i\bar{j}}*\rho_{e}.

Then, the following hold:

(1)

h_{\varepsilon}
is a Griffiths semi-negative smooth Hermitian metric defined on
U_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in U\mid\mathbb{B}(x;\varepsilon)\subset U\}
;

(2)

there exists a Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric
H
on U such that
h_{\varepsilon}\searrow H
pointwise as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
;

(3)

H=h
almost everywhere.

Proof.

For any constant section
c
, we get
|c|^{2}_{h}*\rho_{\varepsilon}=|c|^{2}_{h_{\varepsilon}}
and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}|c|^{2}_{h}\geq 0
, which means that
h_{\varepsilon}
is decreasing as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. To prove
h_{\varepsilon}
to be Griffiths semi-negative, we take an arbitrary open subset
V\Subset U_{\varepsilon}
and a local holomorphic section
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
on
V
.
It is enough to show that
\Delta_{\xi}(|u|^{2}_{h_{\varepsilon}})\geq 0
for any
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
\Delta_{\xi}=\sum\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}\xi_{j}\bar{\xi}_{k}
.
For any test function
\phi\in\cal{D}(V)_{\geq 0}
, by Fubini’s theorem we have

\displaystyle\int_{V}\Delta_{\xi}(|u|^{2}_{h_{\varepsilon}})\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{V}|u|^{2}_{h_{\varepsilon}}(z)\Delta_{\xi}(\phi)(z)dV_{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{V}\sum_{i,j}\biggl(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon,i\bar{j}}(z-w)\rho_{\varepsilon}(w)dV_{w}\biggr)u_{i}(z)\overline{u}_{j}(z)\Delta_{\xi}(\phi)(z)dV_{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}}\biggl(\int_{V}|u|^{2}_{h^{w}}(z)\Delta_{\xi}(\phi)(z)dV_{z}\biggr)\rho_{\varepsilon}(w)dV_{w}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where
h^{w}(z):=h(z-w)
. Since it is only a parallel transport,
h^{w}
is also a.e. Griffiths semi-negative, i.e.
\Delta_{\xi}(|u|^{2}_{h^{w}})\phi\geq 0
.

Define the singular Hermitian metric
H
by
H(z):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to+\infty}h_{\varepsilon}(z)
. In detail,
H
is defined as follows. Let
H(z):=(H_{i\bar{j}}(z))
be the local matrix representation for
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
.
For constant sections
v_{1}={}^{t}(1,0,\cdots,0),\cdots,v_{r}

={}^{t}(0,\cdots,0,1)
, define
H_{jj}
as the decreasing limit of
|v_{j}|^{2}_{h_{\varepsilon}}
converging pointwise, i.e.
H_{jj}(z):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}|v_{j}|^{2}_{h_{\varepsilon}}(z)
, then
H_{jj}
is a function with plurisubharmonicity and coincides with
h_{jj}
a.e. For constant vectors
v={}^{t}(1,1,\cdots,0)
and
v^{\prime}={}^{t}(1,i,\cdots,0)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to+\infty}|v|^{2}_{H_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle:=|v|^{2}_{H}=H_{11}+H_{22}+2\mathrm{Re}H_{12},

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to+\infty}|v^{\prime}|^{2}_{H_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle:=|v^{\prime}|^{2}_{H}=H_{11}+H_{22}+2\mathrm{Im}H_{12}.

From this,
H_{12}
can be defined as a function and coincides with
h_{12}
a.e. By finitely repeating this process, we can define
H
that coincides with
h
a.e.
Since
h_{\varepsilon}
is decreasing as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, it follows that
\log|u|^{2}_{H}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\log|u|^{2}_{h_{\varepsilon}}
is plurisubharmonic for any local holomorphic section
u
, which means that
H
is Griffiths semi-negative.
∎

This lemma naturally leads to the following result.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold,
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
and
h
be an a.e. Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric on
E
. Then, there exists a Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric
H
on
E
satisfying
H=h
almost everywhere.

Proof.

Take a sufficiently fine open covering
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
.
Then we regard
h
as a collection of singular Hermitian metrics
\{h_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
defined on
U_{\alpha}
satisfying
{}^{t}\overline{g_{\alpha\beta}}h_{\alpha}g_{\alpha\beta}=h_{\beta}
a.e. on
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
, where each
h_{\alpha}
is a.e. Griffiths semi-negative and
\{g_{\alpha\beta}\}_{\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda}
is a collection of transition functions of
E
.
By using Lemma
A.1
, we can obtain a Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric
H_{\alpha}
on
U_{\alpha}
. Since
\{H_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
also satisfies
{}^{t}\overline{g_{\alpha\beta}}H_{\alpha}g_{\alpha\beta}=H_{\beta}
a.e. on
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
, the collection
\{H_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
defines a singular Hermitian metric
H
on
E
.
Since each
g_{\alpha\beta}
is holomorphic, the function
\log|s_{\beta}|^{2}_{H}=\log|s_{\beta}|^{2}_{H_{\beta}}=\log|g_{\alpha\beta}s_{\beta}|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}}
is plurisubharmonic on
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
for any
s_{\beta}\in H^{0}(U_{\beta},E)
, where
g_{\alpha\beta}s_{\beta}\in H^{0}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta},E)
. Then
H
is globally Griffiths semi-negative.
∎

At the end of this section, we show the aforementioned extension result, which demonstrates that Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metrics behave like plurisubharmonic functions.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain,
Z\subsetneq U
be a
(
closed
)
analytic subset
and
E
be a vector bundle on
U
.
Let
h
be a Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric defined on
E|_{U\setminus Z}
.
Assume one of the following conditions:

(1)

there exists an upper semi-continuous singular Hermitian metric
h^{\prime}
on E satisfying
h^{\prime}|_{U\setminus Z}=h
;

(2)

\mathrm{codim}\,Z\geq 2
.

Then, there exists a Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric
H
on
E
such that
H|_{U\setminus Z}=h
.

Proof.

Regarding
h
as a metric on
E
by the trivial extension, we can say that
h
is a.e. Griffiths semi-negative thanks to the assumption.
Then, Lemma
A.1
implies that there exists a Griffiths semi-negative singular Hermitian metric
H
on
E
such that
H=h
almost everywhere.
Since both
H
and
h
are Griffiths semi-negative on
U\setminus Z
, it follows that
H=h
on
U\setminus Z
.
∎

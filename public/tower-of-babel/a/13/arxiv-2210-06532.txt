Relaxed many-body optimal transport and related asymptotics
Ugo Bindini, Guy Bouchitté

Abstract
. Optimization problems on probability measures in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} are considered where the cost functional involves multi-marginal optimal transport. In a model of N N interacting particles, like in Density Functional Theory, the interaction cost is repulsive and described by a two-point function c ⁡ ( x , y ) = ℓ ⁡ ( | x − y | ) c(x,y)=\ell(|x-y|) where ℓ : ℝ + → [ 0 , ∞ ] \ell:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to[0,\infty] is decreasing to zero at infinity. Due to a possible loss of mass at infinity, non existence may occur and relaxing the initial problem over sub-probabilities becomes necessary. In this paper we characterize the relaxed functional generalizing the results of [ 4 ] and present a duality method which allows to compute the Γ − \Gamma- limit as N → ∞ N\to\infty under very general assumptions on the cost ℓ ⁡ ( r ) \ell(r) . We show that this limit coincides with the convex hull of the so-called direct energy. Then we study the limit optimization problem when a continuous external potential is applied. Conditions are given with explicit examples under which minimizers are probabilities or have a mass < 1 <1 . In a last part we study the case of a small range interaction ℓ N ​ ( r ) = ℓ ⁡ ( r / ε ) \ell_{N}(r)=\ell(r/\varepsilon) ( ε ≪ 1 \varepsilon\ll 1 ) and we show how the duality approach can be also used to determine the limit energy as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 of a very large number N ε N_{\varepsilon} of particles.



Keywords:
N-body optimal transport, infinite-body optimal transport, relaxation and
\Gamma
-convergence, optimization on sub-probabilities, mean-field limits.

1.
Introduction

Given a probability measure
\rho\in\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\geq 2
, we consider the multi-marginal Optimal Transport (OT) problem defined by

(1.1)

C_{N}(\rho)=\inf\left\{{\int c_{N}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})dP(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\;\colon P\in\Pi_{N}(\rho)}\right\}

where
\Pi_{N}(\rho)
denotes the set of multi-marginal transport plans

\Pi_{N}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\left\{{P\in\mathcal{P}\left((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\right)\;\colon(\pi_{j})_{\#}(P)=\rho\;\forall j=1,\dotsc,N}\right\}.

being
\pi_{j}
the projections from
\mathbb{R}^{Nd}
on the
j
-th factor
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
{\pi_{j}}^{\#}
the push-forward operator

{\pi_{j}}^{\#}P(E)=P\big(\pi_{j}^{-1}(E)\big)\qquad\hbox{for all Borel sets }E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

As a cost function
c_{N}
we consider a two-particle interaction of the form

(1.2)

c_{N}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})=\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\ell(\left|{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right|)

where
\ell\colon[0,+\infty]\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
satisfies the following standing assumptions:

(H1)

\ell(r)\geq 0
and
\ell(0)>0
;

(H2)

\ell
is lower semi-continuous;

(H3)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to+\infty}\ell(r)=0
;

In addition to the standing assumptions, we will sometimes assume that

(H4)

\ell
is locally integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
i.e.
,
\displaystyle\int_{\left\{{|z|\leq R}\right\}}\ell(\left|{z}\right|)dz<+\infty,\forall R>0
; or

(H5)

\ell(0)<+\infty
and
\ell
is positive semi-definite in the following sense: for every
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and every subset
\left\{{x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{m}}\right\}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m}
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\ell(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)t_{i}t_{j}\geq 0\quad\forall t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc,t_{m}\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that, by applying above with
m=2
, we get
\displaystyle\sup\ell=\ell(0)<+\infty
; hence (H5) implies (H4) while, as well known, the Fourier transform of
\ell
satisfies
\hat{\ell}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

It is common in many applications as in Density Functional Theory, crowd motion models and statistics, to encounter minimum problems of the form

\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{C_{N}(\rho)+\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}
is a suitable density functional. In this context it is important to understand the behaviour of this value and the structure of the minimizers for a large number
N
of particles/people, as this can be used to approximate the behaviour of large systems which are often out of reach through numerical methods.

The first step in order to treat rigorously these instances is to understand the limit as
N\to\infty
of the multi-marginal OT functional. In this setting, a natural tool is the notion of
\Gamma
-convergence with respect to the weak* topology of Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In particular if

\displaystyle C_{\infty}\vcentcolon=\Glim_{n\to\infty}C_{N}
exists and can be identified,
it will possible to pass to the limit in minimum problems of the kind
\inf_{\rho}\left\{{C_{N}(\rho)+\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\right\}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{F}
is weakly continuous, by applying a celebrated theorem of De Giorgi, we will obtain the convergence of the infima

\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\rho}\left\{{C_{N}(\rho)+\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\right\}=\min_{\rho}\left\{{C_{\infty}(\rho)+\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\right\}

and the weak* convergence of minimizing sequences in
\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
to minimizers of the functional
C_{\infty}+\mathcal{F}.

However, as far as the minimum problem involves measures on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this result applies only in the case where minimizing sequences are tight (
i.e.
do not
undergo a loss of mass at infinity). Actually such a condition rules out very simple cases, as
for instance when
\mathcal{F}
a linear functional of the form
\mathcal{F}(\rho)=\int v\,d\rho
being
v
a continuous potential with compact support.
To overcome this difficulty, we need to extend the
N
-particles problem and its limit
N\to\infty
on the larger space
\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consisting of all sub-probabilities. This requires a relaxion procedure
for the multi-marginal OT cost in the same line as in
[
4
]
.

The contributions of this paper are presented according to the following plan:

In Section
2
, we extend to general costs
\ell
the relaxation and duality framework recently developed in the case of the Coulomb interaction energy (see
[

]
). As an application, assuming that
\ell(0)<+\infty
, we derive a recipe for computing the relaxed energy
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)
for
\rho
being a finitely supported in
\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Explicit expressions are given
when
\rho
is a combination of two Dirac masses

In Section

, we prove the

-convergence of

as

and provide a characterization of the limit functional

. This representation
relies on a duality argument allowing to compute the Legendre-Fenchel conjugate of
C_{\infty}
as a functional on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This result stated in
4.2
is the natural generalization of
[
9
]
for general two-point particle interaction.
We remark that the pointwise convergence of

for

being a given probability was studied by B. Pass et al. in
[
11
]

[
22
]
in case of a positive definite cost function

to recent works devoted to the next order asymptotics

In all these works the simple limit on

is identified as the so called direct energy

D(\rho)=\int\ell(\left|{x-y}\right|)d\rho(x)d\rho(y),

which, in the Coulomb’s case (

), represents the potential energy due to the self-interaction of the density of charge

In Section
4
, we tackle the natural question of the relation between the

, the two-homogeneous extension

In Section

, we specialize in the minimum problem

\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{C_{\infty}(\rho)-\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle}\right\}\ ,

where
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a given external potential and

a positive parameter.
Denoting by
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
the non empty set of solutions,
we show the existence of threshold values
\lambda_{*}(v)\leq\lambda^{*}(v)<+\infty
such that
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\cap\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

. On the other hand, for

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)=\{\rho_{\lambda}\}
and
\lambda_{*}(v)=\lambda^{*}(v)
. In this case, the behavior

is shown to be linear below the common threshold.
These results are illustrated in the last Subsection

In Section

which depends on a infinitesimal interaction distance parameter

. This is motivated by the justification of the passage from discrete to continuous in some multi-particle models (mean-field limit), as those that are used for instance in crowd motion theory (see

2.
Relaxed multimarginal energy

Let
\ell
be a cost satisfying the standing assumptions. Then it is straightforward to check that the functional
C_{N}\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,+\infty]
is convex, proper and lower semi-continuous on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
endowed with the weak* topology. However, the latter property is not very useful in general when dealing with a sequence
(\rho_{n})
such that
\sup_{n}C_{N}(\rho_{n})<+\infty
. Indeed, such a sequence may show-up a loss of mass at infinity and then be weakly converging to a sub-probability.

Following the direct method of calculus of variations, it is then natural to extend the definition of the multi-marginal energy to elements
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by introducing the relaxed cost:

\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)=\inf\left\{{\liminf_{n}C_{N}(\rho_{n})\;\colon\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup\rho,\ \rho_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\}

Several characterizations of
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)
are available. Two of them, relying on a direct approach, are given in the next subsection
and will be used for some explicit computations (
Subsection 2.3
). A third one, very useful for studying the limit behavior of
C_{N}
as
N\to\infty
, relies on duality theory and will be presented in Section
3
.

2.1.
Two characterizations of
\overline{C_{N}}

A direct approach for characterizing
\overline{C_{N}}
consists
in embedding the elements
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}
as probabilities

\tilde{\rho}=i^{\sharp}(\rho)+(1-\left|{\rho}\right|)\delta_{\omega}
over the Alexandrov’s compactification

X=\mathbb{R}^{d}\cup\left\{{\omega}\right\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\omega
is the point at infinity
and
i\colon x\mapsto x
the identity embedding of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into
X
. The cost
c_{N}
is then extended to
X^{N}

by setting
\ell(\left|{a-b}\right|)=0
whenever
a
or
b
equals
\omega
. Note that, with this convention,
the extension (still denoted
c_{N}
) is lower semi-continuous on
X
thanks to (H3).
With these notations, a first expression for the relaxed cost
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)
in terms of
\tilde{\rho}=i^{\sharp}(\rho)+(1-\|\rho\|)\delta_{\omega}
can be derived similarly as in
[
4
, Proposition 2.2]
:

(2.1)

\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\min\left\{{\int_{X^{N}}c_{N}dP\;\colon P\in\mathcal{P}(X^{N}),P\in\Pi(\tilde{\rho})}\right\}.

Note that the existence of a minimum in (
2.1
) follows from the lower semi-continuity of the map
P\mapsto\int_{X^{N}}c_{N}dP
and of the compactness of
\Pi(\tilde{\rho})
with respect to the narrow convergence on
\mathcal{P}(X^{N})
.

Next considering (
2.1
) and splitting the contributions of
\int_{X^{N}}c_{N}dP
on each set of the form
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}\times{\omega}^{N-k}
(see the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
), we are led to a second characterization of
\overline{C_{N}}
namely:

(2.2)

\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dotsc,a_{N}\geq 0\\
\rho_{1},\dotsc,\rho_{N}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\end{subarray}}\left\{{\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{i(i-1)}{N(N-1)}a_{i}C_{i}(\rho_{i})\;\colon\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\leq 1,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{i}{N}a_{i}\rho_{i}=\rho}\right\}.

Let us mention that the expression above, which we call
stratification formula
, has, in a different context, some relationship with the grand-canonical formulation of optimal transport as it appears in
[
12
]
.

It is easy to check that the infimum in (
2.2
) is attained. Moreover, if
\|\rho\|\leq\frac{1}{N}
, we
get
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)=0
by choosing
a_{1}=1
,
a_{2}=\dotsb=a_{N}=0
while, if
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the only possible choice
a_{1}=\dotsb=a_{N-1}=0
,
a_{N}=1
yields
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)=C_{N}(\rho)
. More generally, the case of a fractional mass
\|\rho\|=\frac{K}{N}
is interesting in the study of molecular structures, as it encodes a ionization phenomenon where exactly
K
electrons among
N
stay at finite distance, while the others
N-K
go away to infinity. In this case, by taking all
a_{i}
vanishing for
i\neq K
and
a_{K}=1
, we obtain the upper bound

(2.3)

\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)\leq\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}C_{K}\left({\frac{N}{K}\rho}\right),\quad\text{whenever $\|\rho\|=\frac{K}{N}$}.

In view of the explicit formula given in
2.8
, it turns out that the inequality above is in fact an equality when
\rho
is a combination of two Dirac masses and
\ell(0)<+\infty
. We expect that the equality holds true also in a larger class of
\rho
under suitable conditions on the cost function
\ell
. For further comments and a conjecture related to this issue, we refer to
Subsection 2.2
.

2.2.
The vanishing gap conjecture

Let us fix
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, and apply (
2.2
) to the evaluation of
\overline{C_{N}}(\theta\rho).

In an optimal choice
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{N}
there will be a minimum and a maximum index
i
such that
a_{i}>0
. Among all possible optimal choices, we select the one for which the difference between maximum and minimum index is the lowest, and we denote those indices respectively by
\overline{K}(N,\theta\rho)
and
\underline{K}(N,\theta\rho)
. Observe that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{i}{N}a_{i}\rho_{i}=\theta\rho\implies\sum_{i=1}^{N}ia_{i}=\theta N,

hence necessarily
\underline{K}(N,\theta\rho)\leq\theta N\leq\overline{K}(N,\theta\rho)
.

Conjecture 2.1
.

The gap between
\underline{K}(N,\theta\rho)
and
\overline{K}(N,\theta\rho)
vanishes (with respect to
N
) as
N\to\infty
,
i.e.
,

(2.4)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\underline{K}(N,\theta\rho)}{N}=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\overline{K}(N,\theta\rho)}{N}=\theta.

This is a weak version of the statement

\underline{K}(N,\theta\rho)=\lfloor{\theta N}\rfloor,\quad\overline{K}(N,\theta\rho)=\lceil{\theta N}\rceil,

where
\lfloor{x}\rfloor
(resp.
\lceil{x}\rceil
) denotes the largest integer smaller (resp. the smallest integer greater) than
x
.
Having a gap bounded not larger than
1
is true for a two points supported
\rho
according to Subsection
2.3
, but
possibly not true in general. Estimates on this gap are available in the recent work by S. Di Marino, M. Lewin and L. Nenna

[
19
]
. An immediate consequence of the validity of the asymptotic statement (
2.4
) will appear in the forthcoming
3.14
.

2.3.
A recipe for computing
\overline{C_{N}}

An explicit computation of the relaxed transport cost
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)
for a general sub-probability
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is often much involved, and in most cases impossible to carry out. In this subsection we propose a recipe for deriving
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)
when
\rho
is a combination of Dirac masses assuming that
\ell(0)<+\infty
.

Let
\Sigma\vcentcolon=\left\{{x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{m}}\right\}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m}
be a finite set supporting the atomic measure
\rho
. We associate with such
\Sigma
the quadratic form defined by

q_{\Sigma}(t)\vcentcolon=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\ell(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)t_{i}t_{j},\quad t=(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Then we define the convex function

(2.5)

f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}(t)\vcentcolon=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(I_{m}^{(N)})}\left\{{\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\gamma(k)q_{\Sigma}(k)\;\colon\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\gamma(k)k=t}\right\},

where
I_{m}^{(N)}\vcentcolon=\left\{{k\in\mathbb{N}^{m}:|k|\vcentcolon=\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}\leq N}\right\}
and we implicitly assume that
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}(t)=+\infty
if
t\notin\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
, being
\Delta_{m}^{(N)}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\cap\{|t|\leq N\}
.

It is easy to check that
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}
coincides with the largest convex l.s.c. function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
such that
g=q_{\Sigma}
on
I_{m}^{(N)}
. Moreover,
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}(t)
is non-increasing with respect to
N
with a limit as
N\to\infty
given by

(2.6)

f_{\Sigma}(t)=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{m})}\left\{{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\gamma(k)q_{\Sigma}(k)\;\colon\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\gamma(k)k=t}\right\}.

On the other hand, due to the finiteness of the set
I_{m}^{(N)}
, the infimum in the linear programming problem (
2.5
) is actually a minimum and the convex funcion
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}(t)
is expected to be piecewise affine in
\Delta_{N}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\ell
satisfy the standing assumptions and
\ell(0)<+\infty
. Let
\Sigma\vcentcolon=\left\{{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}}\right\}
and
s\in[0,1]^{m}
such that
|s|:=\sum_{i}s_{i}\leq 1
. Then, for every
N\geq 2
:

(2.7)

\overline{C_{N}}\left({\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}}\right)=\frac{f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}(Ns)}{N(N-1)}-\frac{\ell(0)}{N-1}\sum s_{i}.

In particular, if
q_{\Sigma}
is non negative, then for every
k\in I_{m}^{(N)}
:

(2.8)

\overline{C_{N}}\left({\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{k_{i}}{N}\delta_{x_{i}}}\right)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}(k_{i}-1)}{N(N-1)}\ell(0)+\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}k_{j}\ell(|x_{i}-x_{j}|).

Proof.

By the characterization in (
2.1
),
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)
is the minimal cost of symmetric
N
-transport plan
P_{N}
on the compactified space
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}\cup\left\{{\omega}\right\}
with marginals
\tilde{\rho}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}+(1-|s|)\delta_{\omega}
.
The generic form of such a
N
-transport plan
P
is given by

P=\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\gamma(k)p_{\Sigma}(k)\quad\text{being}\quad p_{\Sigma}(k)\vcentcolon=\Sym\left(\delta_{x_{1}}^{\otimes k_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\delta_{x_{m}}^{\otimes k_{m}}\otimes\delta_{\omega}^{\otimes(N-|k|)}\right)

and where
\gamma(k)\geq 0
satisfies
\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\gamma(k)=1
(
i.e.
,
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(I_{m}^{(N)})
) and the marginal condition

(2.9)

\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\gamma(k)k=Ns.

The cost of such an admissible
P
reads
\int_{X^{d}}c_{N}dP=\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\gamma(k)c_{N}(p_{\Sigma}(k)).
After a careful computation one checks that the cost
c_{N}(p_{\Sigma}(k))
is given up to the multiplicative factor
\frac{1}{N(N-1)}
by

\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\left({\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}(k_{i}-1)\ell(0)+\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}k_{j}\ell(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)}\right)=q_{\Sigma}(k)-|k|\ell(0).

Taking into account (
2.9
), the total cost reduces to:

\int_{X^{d}}c_{N}dP=\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}\gamma(k)\frac{q_{\Sigma}(k)}{N(N-1)}-\frac{s}{N-1}\ell(0).

Searching for the minimal cost brings us to solving (
2.5
) and to the expression (
2.7
) for
\overline{C_{N}}\left({\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}}\right)
. Now if
k
denotes a particular element of
I_{m}^{(N)}
, by plugging the value
s=\frac{k}{N}
, we obtain

\overline{C_{N}}\left({\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{k_{i}}{N}\delta_{x_{i}}}\right)=\frac{f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}(k)-\ell(0)|k|}{N(N-1)}.

In view of the definition of
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}
in (
2.5
), if we assume that
q_{\Sigma}\geq 0
, then by convexity it holds
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}(k)\geq q_{\Sigma}(k)
while the opposite inequality is obvious since
k\in I_{m}^{N}
. The equality (
2.8
) follows.
∎

In view of (
2.7
), it is possible to recover an optimal decomposition in the stratification formula (
2.2
)
from an optimal
\gamma
in (
2.5
), as in the following.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\,\delta_{x_{i}}
and let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(I_{m}^{(N)})
be optimal (
2.5
).
Then, setting for every
K\in\{0,1,\dots,N\}
:

(2.10)

a_{K}\vcentcolon=\sum_{\left|{k}\right|=K}\gamma(k),\quad\rho_{K}\vcentcolon=\sum_{\left|{k}\right|=K}\frac{\gamma(k)}{a_{K}}\left({\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{k_{i}}{K}\delta_{x_{i}}}\right),

we obtain an optimal decomposition
\rho=\sum_{K=1}^{N}\frac{K}{N}a_{K}\rho_{K}
in (
2.7
).

Accordingly, with the notation of Subsection
2.2
), we have:

(2.11)

\underline{K}(\rho)=\min\{\left|{k}\right|\;\colon k\in\supp(\gamma)\},\quad\overline{K}(\rho)=\max\{\left|{k}\right|\;\colon k\in\supp(\gamma)\}.

Proof.

The admissibility of
\gamma
in (
2.5
) implies that
Ns_{i}=\sum_{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}}k_{i}\gamma(k)
for every
1\leq i\leq m
. By splitting the sum over the subsets
\{k\in I_{m}^{(N)}\;\colon\left|{k}\right|=K\}
for
0\leq K\leq N
, we derive that

\rho=\sum_{K=1}^{N}\frac{K}{N}a_{K}\rho_{K}
while
\sum_{K=0}^{N}a_{K}=1
, obtaining an admissible decomposition for
(
2.7
). It follows that

\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)\leq\sum_{K=2}^{N}a_{K}\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}C_{K}(\rho_{K}).

The converse inequality is a consequence of the optimality of
\gamma
in (
2.5
). We have:

\displaystyle\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K=1}^{N}\left({\sum_{|k|=K}\frac{q_{\Sigma}(k)-\ell(0)|k|}{N(N-1)}\gamma(k)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{K=2}^{N}a_{K}\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}\left({\sum_{|k|=K}\frac{q_{\Sigma}(k)-\ell(0)K}{K(K-1)}\gamma_{K}(k)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{K=2}^{N}a_{K}\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}C_{K}(\rho_{K}),

where in the second inequality
\gamma_{K}
denotes the probability on
\{|k|=K\}
such that
\gamma_{K}(k)=\frac{\gamma(k)}{a_{K}}
. In the second inequality, we applied (
2.7
) with
N=K
noticing that
\gamma_{K}
is an admissible competitor in

\mathcal{P}(I_{m}^{(K)})
for
f_{\Sigma}^{(K)}(t^{(K)})
being
t^{(K)}=\sum_{|k|=K}\gamma_{K}(k)k
.
In turn, this implies that

C_{K}(\rho_{K})=C_{K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{t^{(K)}_{i}}{K}\delta_{x_{i}}\right)\leq\sum_{|k|=K}\frac{q_{\Sigma}(k)-\ell(0)K}{K(K-1)}\gamma_{K}(k).

Summarizing, we proved that the decomposition of
\rho
given by
a_{K},\rho_{K}
(as defined in (
2.10
)) is optimal in the stratification formula (
2.2
). The relations (
2.11
) follows directly from the expression of
a_{K}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

If
q_{\Sigma}\geq 0

) for
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)

for suitable

such that

.
The extension of previous equality to general

such that

f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}
agrees
on

2.4.
Identification of
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}
(resp.
f_{\Sigma}
) through simplicial partitions

In the spirit of the finite element method, we consider partitions of
\overline{\Omega}=\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
(resp. of
\overline{\Omega}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
)
which consist of a family
\mathcal{F}
of simplices
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that:

i)

\cup\{S:S\in\mathcal{F}\}=\overline{\Omega}
;

ii)

the elements of
\mathcal{F}
have mutually disjoints interiors;

iii)

any face of a simplex
S\in\mathcal{F}
is either a face of another
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
, or a subset of
\partial\overline{\Omega}
.

For the existence of such partitions in any dimension
m
, we refer to
[
6
]
(see references therein).
In our case we will restrict ourselves to partitions
\mathcal{F}
, that we call
admissible simplicial partitions
, consisting of simplices whose vertices are in the integer lattice
\mathbb{N}^{m}
.
A natural guess supported by forthcoming
2.5

is that
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}
and
f_{\Sigma}
should be piecewise affine along such a partition.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
q_{\Sigma}\geq 0
. Let be given an admissible simplicial partition of
\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
(resp
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
) and denote by
g
the unique continuous function
g
which is is affine on each simplice and satisfies
g=q_{\Sigma}
on
I_{m}^{(N)}
(resp. on
\mathbb{N}^{m}
).

Then
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}=g
on
\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
(resp
f_{\Sigma}=g
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
) if and only if
g
is convex on
\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
(resp
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
). If it is the case, then the interior of each simplex
S
does not meet
\mathbb{N}^{m}
.

Proof.

By restricting the infimum in (
2.5
) to elements
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(I_{m}^{(N)})
supported on the vertices of a simplex, we see that the inequality
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}\leq g
is always true. Since
g=q_{\Sigma}=f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}
on
I_{m}^{(N)}
, the opposite inequality
g\geq f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}
holding on
\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
is equivalent to the convexity of
g
. The characterization of
f_{\Sigma}
is obtained in the same way.

Assume now that the interior of a simplex
S
of the partition contains an element of
\mathbb{N}^{m}
. Then the convex quadratic function
q_{\Sigma}
would agree with the affine function
g
not only on the vertices of
S
but also at that interior point forcing
q_{\Sigma}
to be affine over the whole simplex. This is impossible unless
q_{\Sigma}
vanishes identically.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

A consequence of
2.5
is that, given an admissible simplicial partition
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
such that the affine interpolant of
q_{\Sigma}
is convex, then the equality
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}=f_{\Sigma}
is true provided that any simplex
S\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies the flatness criterium
\left|{|s|-|t|}\right|\leq 1
for all
s,t\in S
. Indeed, under the this condition, any such a simplex
S
touching the interior of
\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
satisfies
S\subset\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
. In the same way, if
\mathcal{F}
is an admissible simplicial partition of
\Delta_{m}^{(N)}
providing a convex interpolant of
q_{\Sigma}
, then in view of (
2.11
) we have the bound:

\overline{K}(N,\rho)-\underline{K}(N,\rho)\leq\sup_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\max\left\{{|s|-|t|:s,t\in S}\right\},

holding for every
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported on
\Sigma
.

It turns out that, in the case of two Dirac masses (
i.e.
,
m=2
and
\Sigma=\left\{{x,y}\right\}
), the equality
f_{\Sigma}^{(N)}=f_{\Sigma}
holds in
I_{2}^{(N)}
and we can identify the optimal triangle partition of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
associated with
f_{\Sigma}
. More precisely, let us denote, for every
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
, the square
Y_{kl}:=[k,k+1]\times[l,l+1]
that we split into the two triangles
T_{kl}^{-}=\{(u,v)\in Y_{kl}:u+v\leq k+l+1\}
and
T_{kl}^{+}=\{(u,v)\in Y_{kl}:u+v\geq k+l+1\}.

Accordingly we obtain an admissible simplicial partition of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
satisfying the flatness criterium of
2.6
.
Then let
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the unique continuous function which is affine on each
T_{kl}^{\pm}
and such that

g(k,l)=\ell(0)(k^{2}+l^{2})+2\min\left\{{\ell(0),\ell(|x-y|)}\right\}kl,\quad\forall(k,l)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}.

Note that the positivity of
q_{\Sigma}
here means that
\ell(0)\geq\ell(|x-y|)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The piecewise affine function
g
defined above is convex.

Proof.

Setting
L_{0}\vcentcolon=\ell(0)
and
L_{1}\vcentcolon=\min\left\{{\ell(0),\ell(|x-y|)}\right\}
, the gradient of
g
is piecewise constant and given on
Y_{kl}
by:

(2.12)

\nabla g=\begin{cases}L_{0}(2k+1,2l+1)+L_{1}(l,k)&\text{if $(k,l)\in T_{kl}^{-}$}\\
L_{0}(2k+1,2l+1)+L_{1}(l+1,k+1)&\text{if $(k,l)\in T_{kl}^{+}$.}\end{cases}

The distributional Hessian of
g
will be a rank tensor measure concentrated on the jump set of
\nabla g
. Checking the convexity of
g
reduces then to check that
\nabla^{2}g\geq 0
which means that the normal jumps of
\nabla g
are non-negative
along the sides of each triangle (or equivalently that the distributional Laplacian of
g
is non-negative).

In view of (
2.12
), an easy computation shows that that the normal jumps along the horizontal or vertical sides are all equal
2(L_{0}-L_{1})
, while the normal jumps along the oblique sides are equal to
2\sqrt{2}\,L_{1}
. Accordingly, the distributional Laplacian
\Delta g
is a non-negative periodic measure.
∎

As a direct consequence of
2.2
,
2.5
and
2.7
, we get the following.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\Sigma=\left\{{x,y}\right\}
. Then for every
s,t\in[0,1]
such that
s+t\leq 1
, we have

\overline{C_{N}}(s\delta_{x}+t\delta_{y})=\frac{f_{\Sigma}(Ns,Nt)}{N(N-1)}.

In particular, if
s+t=\frac{K}{N}
with
K\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\overline{C_{N}}(s\delta_{x}+t\delta_{y})=C_{K}\left({\frac{s}{s+t}\delta_{x}+\frac{t}{s+t}\delta_{y}}\right).

Remark 2.9
.

The case of a single Dirac mass
\Sigma=\{x\}
is recovered by taking
t=0
. Then
h(s)\vcentcolon=\overline{C_{N}}(s\delta_{x})
is the monotone continuous function on
[0,1]
such that
h(\frac{k}{N})=\ell(0)\,\frac{k(k-1)}{N(N-1}
and
f
is affine on each interval
[\frac{k}{N},\frac{k+1}{N}].

In the case of two Dirac masses at
x,y
such that
\ell(|x-y|)<\ell(0)
, an easy computation gives
f_{\Sigma}(k,l)=h(\frac{k+l}{N})
and we reduce to the single Dirac case since
\overline{C_{N}}(s\delta_{x}+t\delta_{y})=\overline{C_{N}}((s+t)\delta_{x})
.

3.
Duality and
\Gamma
-convergence

3.1.
Duality

In the following the space of bounded Borel measures
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is identified as the dual of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
we see
C_{N}
as a convex functional defined on the whole space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by setting
C_{N}(\rho)=+\infty
if
\rho\notin\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

By a classical result (see (e) in(
A.1
)), the relaxed functional
\overline{C_{N}}
can be recovered as the Fenchel biconjugate of
C_{N}
, that is:

(3.1)

\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)=M_{N}^{*}(\rho)=\sup_{v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle-M_{N}(v)}\right\}.

where
M_{N}\colon C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[-\infty,+\infty]
the Fenchel conjugate of
C_{N}
, reeds

M_{N}(v)\vcentcolon=C_{N}^{*}(v)=\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle-C_{N}(\rho)}\right\}.

The existence of an optimal Lipschitz potential
v
in (
3.1
) has been proved when
\ell
is a Coulomb type interaction
under mild assumptions (
cf.

[
7
]
for the case
\|\rho\|=1
and
[
4
]
for the case
\|\rho\|<1
).

A characterization of
M_{N}
is available in terms of a maximization problem over configurations of
N
points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Recalling the notation
S_{N}v(x)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v(x_{i})
for
x=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})
, we have the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(3.2)

M_{N}(v)=\sup\left\{{S_{N}v(x)-c_{N}(x)\;\colon x\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}}\right\}.

It follows that
M_{N}(v)=M_{N}(v_{+})\geq 0
. In addition, we have:

(3.3)

\frac{1}{N}\sup v_{+}\leq M_{N}(v)\leq\sup v_{+}.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\ell(0)>0
, we have the strict inequality
M_{N}(v)<\sup v_{+}
for any
v
whose positive maximum is attained at a unique point

\bar{x}
. Indeed the equality
M_{N}(v)=\sup v_{+}
would imply that, at some point
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})
, we have
M_{N}(v)(x)=\max v_{+}
while
c_{N}(x)=0
, which is not possible since all
x_{i}
would coincide with
\bar{x}
so that
c_{N}(x)=\ell(0)
.

Proof.

Given
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let

\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad P=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}}\delta_{x_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\delta_{x_{\sigma(N)}}.

Observe that
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
P\in\Pi_{N}(\rho)
. Hence

\displaystyle S_{N}v(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})-c_{N}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int vd\rho-\int c_{N}dP

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int vd\rho-C_{N}(\rho)\leq M_{N}(v),

which gives an inequality.
On the other hand, for any
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if
P\in\Pi_{N}(\rho)
is optimal in (
1.1
) one has

\int vd\rho-C_{N}(\rho)=\int(S_{N}v-c_{N})dP\leq\sup(S_{N}v-c_{N})

Passing to the supremum on the left-hand side one gets the converse inequality whence (
3.2
).

The fact that
M_{N}(v)\geq 0
is then straightforward by sending all
x_{k}
to infinity in such a way that
c_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\to 0
.

On the other hand, we have obviously
M_{N}(v)\leq M_{N}(v_{+})
. To show the opposite inequality let us fix
\varepsilon>0
and take
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
such that

S_{N}v_{+}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})-c_{N}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\geq M_{N}(v_{+})-\varepsilon.

Then sending to infinity every
x_{k}
such that
v(x_{k})<0
while keeping the other
x_{j}
’s fixed will increase the value of
S_{N}(v)
to
S_{N}(v_{+})
and decrease the value of
c_{N}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})
(since all terms of the kind
\ell(\left|{x_{j}-x_{k}}\right|)
will vanish).
Accordingly we get
M_{N}(v)\geq S_{N}(v_{+})-c_{N}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\geq M_{N}(v_{+})-\varepsilon
, hence the desired inequality since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary.

To show (
3.3
), we may now assume that
v\geq 0
. Then the upperbound
M_{N}(v)\geq\sup v
is trivial. On the other hand, by selecting
x_{1}
such that
v(x_{1})=\max v
and by sending the other
x_{j}
’s to infinity, we conclude with the lower bound
M_{N}(v)\geq\frac{1}{N}v(x_{1})=\frac{1}{N}\sup v.

∎

In the same spirit as of
3.1
, we have an alternative lower bound inequality for
M_{N}(v)
namely:

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
2\leq K\leq N
one has

(3.4)

M_{N}(v)\geq\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}M_{K}\left(\frac{N-1}{K-1}v\right).

Proof.

Let
\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{K}\}
be an optimal
K
-points configuration up to a small
\varepsilon>0
for
M_{K}\left(\frac{N-1}{K-1}v\right)
as defined in (
3.2
). Then we may complete to obtain a
N
-points configuration by adding
x_{K+1},\dotsc,x_{N}
tending to
\infty
so that, in view of
(H3)
, we have:

\displaystyle M_{N}(v)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{K}v(x_{j})-\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq K}\ell(\left|{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}\left({\frac{N-1}{K(K-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{K}v(x_{j})-\frac{2}{K(K-1)}\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq K}\ell(\left|{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right|)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}\left({\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{N-1}{K-1}v(x_{j})-c_{K}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{K})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{K(K-1)}{N(N-1)}\left(M_{K}\left({\frac{N-1}{K-1}v}\right)-\varepsilon\right),

hence (
3.4
) by sending
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

In view of the equality
M_{N}(v)=M_{N}(v_{+})
(see
3.1
), the supremum in (
3.1
) can be restricted to non negative
v
and rewritten in the alternative form

(3.5)

\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0,v\in C_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{\int vd\rho-\lambda\;\colon S_{N}(v)\leq c_{N}+\lambda\quad\text{in $(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}$}}\right\}

from which we deduce the following monotonocity property:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\rho,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\rho\leq\nu
. Then
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)\leq\overline{C_{N}}(\nu).

Proof.

Since
\int vd\rho\geq\int vd\rho
for every
v\in C_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the inequality
\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)\leq\overline{C_{N}}(\nu)
follows from
(
3.5
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

It turns out that, by applying
[
4
, Proposition 3.8]
, the pointwise inequality constraint appearing in (
3.5
) can be drastically reduced in practice and replaced by the same condition
S_{N}(v)\leq c_{N}+\lambda
holding
\tilde{\rho}^{\otimes N}
a.e. on
X^{d}
, where
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}\cup\{\omega\}
is the compactified space introduced in Section
2.1
(with
c_{N}
and
S_{N}(v)
being extended to
X^{d}
).

Accordingly, for a discrete measure
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
, we are led to a linear programming problem involving
m+1
unknowns in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, namely
v_{i}=v(x_{i})
(
1\leq i\leq m
) and
v_{m+1}=\lambda
.
For instance, in the case of a two Dirac masses measure as studied in Subsection
2.3
, we are led to:

\overline{C_{N}}(s\delta_{x}+t\delta_{y})=\sup\left\{{sv_{1}+tv_{2}+v_{3}}\right\},

subject to the following constraints holding for every
k\geq 0,l\geq 0
with
k+l\leq N
:

\frac{k}{N}v_{1}+\frac{l}{N}v_{2}+\left({1-\frac{k+l}{N}}\right)v_{3}\leq\frac{k(k-1)+l(l-1)}{N(N-1)}\ell(0)+\frac{2kl}{N(N-1)}\ell(|x-y|)

3.2.
Pointwise and
\Gamma
-convergence

The asymptotic behavior of the functionals
C_{N}
and
M_{N}
as
N\to\infty
will be a direct consequence of the two following results.

Lemma 3.6

(monotonicity)
.

The sequences
(C_{N})_{N\geq 1}
and
(\overline{C_{N}})_{N\geq 1}
are monotone non-decreasing. The sequence
(M_{N})_{N\geq 1}
is monotone non-increasing.

Note that, in order to get the monotonicity property of
C_{N}
with respect to the number
N
of marginals, the normalization factor
\frac{1}{N(N-1)}
used in the definition (
1.2
) is essential.

Proof.

We observe that

C_{N}(\rho)=\inf_{\sigma\in\Pi_{2,N}(\rho)}\int\ell(\left|{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right|)d\sigma(x_{1},x_{2}),

where
\Pi_{2,N}(\rho)
denotes the set of 2-body marginals of measures in
\Pi_{N}(\rho)
. This

Lemma 3.7
.

The family

is equi-Lipschitz, with Lipschitz constant equal to 1.

Proof.

Let

By letting

∎

From

Lemma 3.8
.

We have the duality relations

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume that
\ell
satisfies the local integrability assumption (H4). Then the domain of

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{M_{\infty}(tv)}{t}=\sup v_{+}\quad\forall v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

By construction the domain of the relaxed functional

\delta_{x}\in\mathcal{K}

∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Assume that
\ell>0
on

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{M_{\infty}(tv)}{t}=0\quad\forall v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Since the map

is monotone non decreasing, we have

Clearly the function

. Therefore

coincides with its biconjugate

An easy computation shows that

while

H_{t}^{*}(\rho)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{t}\,(\left\langle{\rho,tv}\right\rangle-M_{\infty}(tv)):v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}=\frac{C_{\infty}(\rho)}{t},

for every

and

. It follows that

coincides with the indicator function of the

that is of

Therefore we have

∎

Lemma 3.13
.

The convex functional
M_{\infty}
is 1-Lipschitz on

and, for every

,
the map
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto\dfrac{M_{\infty}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}
is monotone non increasing.
In particular, under
(H4)
, we have

for every
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\sup v>0.

Proof.

Let
t\geq 1
,
w\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and choose a sequence
(k_{N})_{N\geq 2}
such that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{k_{N}}{N}=t^{-1}
. Then, by applying the inequality (
3.4
), we get:

M_{N}(v)\geq\frac{k_{N}(k_{N}-1)}{N(N-1)}M_{k_{N}}\left({\frac{N-1}{k_{N}-1}w}\right)\geq\frac{k_{N}(k_{N}-1)}{N(N-1)}M_{\infty}\left({\frac{N-1}{k_{N}-1}w}\right),

thus , after sending

, the inequality

(3.8)

M_{\infty}(tw)\ \leq t^{2}\,M_{\infty}(w)\qquad\forall w\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ ,\ \forall t\geq 1.

Let now
\lambda,\mu
such that
0<\lambda<\mu
. Then, by plugging
t=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}
and
v=\mu w
in (
3.8
), we deduce that

\frac{M_{\infty}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}\leq\frac{M_{\infty}(\mu v)}{\mu^{2}},
hence the desired monotonicity property.

In order to show the last statement, we notice that the convexity of

implies that the set of real numbers
\left\{{t\geq 0:M_{\infty}(tv)\leq 0}\right\}

is a closed non empty interval starting from

. Then the inequality (

) implies that this interval is either

\left\{{0}\right\}
or the half line
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Under (H4), the second alternative is ruled out if

since, by

, the slope at infinity

must be positive.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

The convex functional
C_{\infty}
satisfies the inequality:

(3.9)

C_{\infty}(\theta\rho)\leq\theta^{2}C_{\infty}(\rho)\quad\forall\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\forall\theta\in[0,1].

Moreover, if
2.1
holds true, then
C_{\infty}
is 2-homogeneous,
i.e.
, the inequality (
3.9
) is an equality.

Proof.

In order to show (
3.9
), it is enough to consider a sequence
(k_{N})_{N\geq 2}
such that
\lim\frac{k_{N}}{N}=\theta
and apply the inequality (
2.3
):

\overline{C_{N}}\left({\frac{k_{N}}{N}\rho}\right)\leq\frac{k_{N}(k_{N}-1)}{N(N-1)}C_{k_{N}}(\rho).

Then, by exploiting the
\Gamma
-convergence of
\overline{C_{N}}
to
C_{\infty}
, we get
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\overline{C_{N}}\left({\frac{k_{N}}{N}\rho}\right)\geq C_{\infty}(\theta\rho)
while, by the pointwise convergence
\overline{C_{k_{N}}}(\rho)\to C_{\infty}(\rho)
(recall that
\overline{C_{k_{N}}}(\rho)=C_{k_{N}}(\rho)
when
\|\rho\|=1
), the right hand side converges to
\theta^{2}\,C_{\infty}(\rho)
.

Next let us assume that (
2.4
) holds true and let
\rho_{1},\dotsc,\rho_{N}
and
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{N}
be optimal in (
2.2
) for
\theta\rho
, with
a_{1}=\dotsb=a_{k-1}=0
. Since
C_{j}\geq C_{k}
for
j\geq k
, by the convexity of
C_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\overline{C_{N}}(\theta\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{N}a_{j}\frac{j(j-1)}{N(N-1)}C_{j}(\rho_{j})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{N-1}\sum_{j=k}^{N}a_{j}\frac{j}{N}C_{k}(\rho_{j})\geq\frac{k-1}{N-1}\theta C_{k}(\rho).

The inequality above will hold in particular for
k=\underline{k}(N,\theta\rho)
. Therefore,
by taking a sequence
(N_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{h\to\infty}\frac{\underline{k}(N_{h},\theta\rho)}{N_{h}}=\theta
,
we may conclude that
C_{\infty}(\theta\rho)\geq\theta^{2}C_{\infty}(\rho)
, which is the converse inequality of (
3.9
).
∎

4.
Relations with the direct energy

In Section
3
, we obtained a formal description of the limit functionals
M_{\infty}
and
C_{\infty}
, namely that the
\Gamma
-limit
M_{\infty}
coincides with the pointwise limit of the non increasing sequence
(M_{N})
while
C_{\infty}=\sup\overline{C_{N}}
coincides with the Fenchel conjugate of
M_{\infty}
.
Our aim now is to give a more explicit description of these functionals relying on the fact that
M_{\infty}
coincides with the Fenchel conjugate of the so called
direct energy

D\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,+\infty]
given by (
4.1
)

(4.1)

D(\rho)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int\ell(\left|{x-y}\right|)d\rho(x)d\rho(y)&\text{if $\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The name “direct energy” is inherited from a physical model where
\rho
represents a charge density, as this functional equals (up to constants) the potential energy due to the self-interaction of the density
\rho
.
It is convenient to introduce also the
2
-homogeneous extension of
D
to sub-probabilities
D_{2}\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,+\infty]

i.e.
,

(4.2)

D_{2}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\|\rho\|^{2}D\left(\dfrac{\rho}{\|\rho\|}\right)&\text{if $\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By construction we have
D_{2}\leq D
. Moreover, by observing that
D_{2}(\rho)=\left\langle{c_{2},\rho\otimes\rho}\right\rangle
for every
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is easy to deduce from (H2) that
D_{2}
is weakly* lower semi-continuous as a functional on
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In contrast the functional
D
, whose domain is not closed, is not lower semi-continuous.
Therefore, we will consider the weak* lower semi-continuous envelope of
D
defined for every
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

(4.3)

\overline{D}(\rho)=\inf_{\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup\rho}\liminf_{n\to\infty}D(\rho_{n}).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
D
and
D_{2}
be defined by (
4.1
),(
4.2
) respectively. Then it holds

(4.4)

\overline{D}\leq D_{2}\quad,\quad D^{*}=D_{2}^{*}.

Proof.

In view of the property (d) in
Appendix A
, we have
D^{*}=(\overline{D})^{*}
. Thus the first inequality of
(
4.4
) will imply that
D_{2}^{*}\leq D^{*}
, hence
D_{2}^{*}=D^{*}
since the converse inequality follows trivially from
D_{2}\leq D
.
So it is enough to show that
\overline{D}\leq D_{2}
which is equivalent to the following
claim:

(4.5)

\forall\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\exists\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup\rho\colon\limsup_{n\to\infty}D(\rho_{n})\leq D_{2}(\rho).

First we show (
4.5
) when
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has a compact support. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\|\rho\|\geq 0
and
D_{2}(\rho)<+\infty
. For
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\tau_{h}\rho
the push-forward of
\rho
through the translation map
x\mapsto x+h
. We will exploit several times the following property

(4.6)

\lim_{\left|{h}\right|\to\infty}\left\langle{c_{2},\rho\otimes\tau_{h}\rho}\right\rangle=0.

To check (
4.6
), we simply notice that if
\supp\rho\subset B_{R}
, then for
\left|{h}\right|\geq 3R
we have

\left\langle{c_{2},\rho\otimes\tau_{h}\rho}\right\rangle=\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}\ell(\left|{x-y-h}\right|)d\rho(x)d\rho(y)\leq\sup\left\{{\ell(r)\colon r\geq R}\right\},

whence
\limsup_{\left|{h}\right|\to\infty}\left\langle{c_{2},\rho\otimes\tau_{h}\rho}\right\rangle\leq\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\ell(r)=0,
thanks to (H3).

Accordingly, given a unitary direction
e\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can choose an increasing sequence of positive reals
(R_{n})
such that
R_{n}\to\infty
and

(4.7)

\left\langle{c_{2},\rho\otimes\tau_{re}\rho}\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\quad\forall r\geq R_{n}

For given
n\in N^{*}
, we define
n
collinear vectors
h_{n,j}=R_{n}je
for
j=1,\dotsc,n
, so that
\left|{h_{n,i}-h_{n,j}}\right|\geq R_{n}e
for every
i\neq j
. Then we set:

\rho_{n}=\rho+\frac{1-\|\rho\|}{n\|\rho\|}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{h_{n,j}}\rho.

By construction
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, since
|h_{n,j}|\geq R_{n}\to\infty
,
\rho_{n}
agrees with
\rho
on any compact subset as soon
n
is large enough, hence we have
\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup\rho
. On the other hand, by the invariance of
D_{2}
under translations and the choice of the
h_{n,j}
’s, we get:

\displaystyle D(\rho_{n})

\displaystyle=D_{2}(\rho)+\frac{2(1-\|\rho\|)}{n\|\rho\|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\left\langle{c_{2},\tau_{h_{n,i}}\rho\otimes\tau_{h_{n,j}}\rho}\right\rangle+\frac{(1-\|\rho\|)^{2}}{n^{2}\|\rho\|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\,D_{2}(\tau_{h_{n,j}}\rho)

\displaystyle=D_{2}(\rho)+\frac{2(1-\|\rho\|)}{n\|\rho\|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\left\langle{c_{2},\rho\otimes\tau_{(h_{n,i}-h_{n,j})}\rho}\right\rangle+\frac{(1-\|\rho\|)^{2}}{n^{2}\|\rho\|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{2}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq D_{2}(\rho)+\frac{2(1-\|\rho\|)}{n\|\rho\|}+\frac{(1-\|\rho\|)^{2}}{n\|\rho\|^{2}}D_{2}(\rho),

where, in order to derive the last inequality, we used the fact that, by (
4.7
), we have
\left\langle{c_{2},\rho\otimes\tau_{(h_{n,i}-h_{n,j})}\rho}\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Summarizing, we found a recovering sequence
(\rho_{n})
satisfying (
4.5
) and therefore
\overline{D}(\rho)\leq D_{2}(\rho)
.

To extend this inequality to non compactly supported
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is enough to consider, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the measure
\rho_{n}\vcentcolon=\rho\lfloor_{B(0,n)}
which clearly satisfies
\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup\rho
as
n\to\infty
. Then, since
D_{2}(\rho_{n})\leq D_{2}(\rho)
,
we have

\overline{D}(\rho)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\overline{D}(\rho_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{2}(\rho_{n})\leq D_{2}(\rho).

This concludes the proof of (
4.4
).
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\ell
satisfy the standing assumptions. Then

(i)
It holds
M_{\infty}=D^{*}
, hence for every
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(4.8)

M_{\infty}(v)=\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int\left(\frac{v(x)+v(y)}{2}-\ell(\left|{x-y}\right|)\right)\,d\rho(x)d\rho(y).

Moreover, if the cost
\ell
is bounded, the following estimate holds for

(4.9)

M_{\infty}(v)\leq M_{N}(v)\leq M_{\infty}(v)+\frac{1}{N}(\sup\ell+\sup v_{+}).

(ii)
We have he following duality relations:

(4.10)

C_{\infty}=D^{**}=D_{2}^{**}.

In particular, if
D
is convex, then
C_{\infty}=\overline{D}
while
C_{\infty}(\rho)=D(\rho)
if
\|\rho\|=1
. If moreover
D_{2}
is convex, then
C_{\infty}=D_{2}.

Proof.

It is immediate to check that the right hand side supremum in (

) coincides with
D^{*}(v)
. Let us prove that
D^{*}\leq M_{\infty}
. Due to the pairwise-interaction structure of the cost
c_{N}
and recalling (

\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle-D(\rho)=\left\langle{S_{2}v-c_{2},\rho\otimes\rho}\right\rangle=\langle S_{N}v-c_{N},\underset{N\text{ times}}{\underbrace{\rho\otimes\dotsb\otimes\rho}}\rangle\leq M_{N}(v).

The inequality
D^{*}(v)\leq M_{\infty}(v)
follows by taking the supremum with respect to
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
on the left-hand side and the infimum in
N
on the left-hand side.

Let us prove now the converse inequality
M_{\infty}\leq D^{*}
.
To that aim, we fix
\varepsilon>0
and, for every
N\geq 2
, we pick
x^{(N)}=(x_{1}^{(N)},x_{2}^{(N)},\dotsc,x_{N}^{(N)})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
such that

M_{N}(v)\leq(S_{N}v-c_{N})(x^{(N)})+\varepsilon.

Then we consider the symmetric transport plan
P_{N}=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}}\delta_{x_{\sigma(1)}^{(1)}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\delta_{x_{\sigma(N)}^{(N)}}
and denote by
\gamma_{N}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
the
the 2-marginal projection of
P_{N}
. Note that
P_{N}\in\Pi_{N}(\rho)
being
\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{j}^{(N)}}
, and

(4.11)

M_{N}(v)-\varepsilon\leq\left\langle{S_{N}v-c_{N},P_{N}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{S_{2}v-c_{2},\gamma_{N}}\right\rangle.

Next we approximate
\gamma_{N}
by
\widetilde{\gamma_{N}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
given in the form:

(4.12)

\widetilde{\gamma_{N}}(A)=\int_{A}(Q\otimes Q)dp_{N}(Q),\quad\text{for every Borel subset $A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}$,}

where

is suitably chosen element of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

Owing to a classical result by Diaconis and Freedman
[
14
, Theorem 13]
(see the last statement in the proof therein), we may choose
p_{N}
so that the gap
\mu_{N}:=\gamma_{N}-\widetilde{\gamma_{N}}
is a balanced measure such that:

(4.13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mu_{N})_{+}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mu_{N})_{-}\leq\frac{1}{N}.

In view of (
4.12
), we have

\left\langle{S_{2}v-c_{2},\widetilde{\gamma_{N}}}\right\rangle\leq\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\langle{S_{2}v-c_{2},Q\otimes Q}\right\rangle=D^{*}(v).

Therefore the estimate (
4.11
) implies that:

(4.14)

M_{N}(v)-\varepsilon\leq D^{*}(v)+\left\langle{S_{2}v-c_{2},\mu_{N}}\right\rangle.

In order to conclude that
M_{\infty}\leq D^{*}
, we proceed now in two steps.

Step 1: we assume that
\sup\ell<+\infty
and prove (
4.9
)
.
In this case, we have
\left\langle{S_{2}v-c_{2},\mu_{N}}\right\rangle\to 0
as
N\to+\infty
since
S_{2}v-c_{2}
is bounded and
\mu_{N}\to 0

in total variation owing to (
4.13
). Thus, from (
4.14
), we infer that
M_{\infty}(v)\leq D^{*}(v)
by sending
N\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore we have
M_{\infty}=D^{*}
and (
4.8
)
Next, keeping in mind that
M_{N}(v)=M_{N}(v^{+})
and
M_{\infty}(v)=M_{\infty}(v_{+})
,
it is not restrictive to assume that
v\geq 0
. Then by (
4.14
) and (
4.8
), we get:

M_{N}(v)-\varepsilon\leq M_{\infty}(v)+\left({\int(\mu_{N})_{+}}\right)\sup v+\left({\int(\mu_{N})_{-}}\right)\sup\ell

whence the estimate (
4.9
) thanks to (
4.13
) and since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary small.

Step 2: we remove the assumption
\sup\ell<+\infty
and show that
M_{\infty}\leq D_{2}^{*}
.
We use the truncated cost
\ell_{h}(r)=\ell(r)\wedge h
being
h
a large positive parameter and define accordingly

D_{2,h}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\left\langle{c_{2,h},\rho\otimes\rho}\right\rangle
for
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
c_{2,h}(x,y)\vcentcolon=\ell_{h}(|x-y|)

and
M_{N,h}(v)=\sup\{S_{N}v-c_{N,h}\}
for
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Clearly it holds
M_{N}(v)\leq M_{N,h}(v)
so that
M_{\infty}(v)\leq\lim_{N\searrow\infty}M_{N,h}(v)
.
By applying Step 1 to the bounded cost
\ell_{h}
(which satisfies the standing assumptions), we deduce that
M_{\infty}(v)\leq D_{2,h}^{*}(v)
for every
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
h>0
. Therefore, recalling that
D_{2}^{*}=D^{*}
by
4.1
, we are done if we show that

(4.15)

\limsup_{h\to+\infty}D_{2,h}^{*}(v)\leq D_{2}^{*}(v).

By the definition of the Fenchel conjugate, there exists
\rho_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\limsup_{h\to+\infty}D_{2,h}^{*}(v)\leq\limsup_{h\to+\infty}\ \left\langle{v,\rho_{h}}\right\rangle-D_{2,h}(\rho_{h}).

Without loss of generality, we may assume that there exists
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\rho_{h}\rightharpoonup\rho
.
The wished inequality (
4.15
) follows once we can check that

(4.16)

\liminf_{h\to+\infty}D_{2,h}(\rho_{h})\geq D_{2}(\rho).

Let us fix
h_{0}>0
. Then, since
D_{2,h}\geq D_{2,h_{0}}
for every
h\geq h_{0}
, by the lower semicontinuity of
D_{2,h_{0}}
we have

\liminf_{h\to+\infty}D_{2,h}(\rho_{h})\ \geq\ \liminf_{h\to+\infty}D_{2,h_{0}}(\rho_{h})\ \geq\ D_{2,h_{0}}(\rho).

As
h_{0}
is arbitrary , the claim (
4.16
) follows since by monotone convergence:

\lim_{h_{0}\nearrow+\infty}D_{2,h_{0}}(\rho)=\int\sup_{h_{0}}\ell_{h_{0}}(|x-y|)\ \rho\otimes\rho=\int\ell(|x-y|)\ \rho\otimes\rho=D_{2}(\rho).

The proof of Step 2 and of the assertion (i) of
4.2
is now complete.

(ii) A consequence of the assertion (i) above and of
3.8
is that
C_{\infty}=M_{\infty}^{*}=D^{**}
, hence (
4.10
) since
D^{*}=D_{2}^{*}
.

Assume now that
D
is convex. Then
C_{\infty}=\overline{D}
in virtue of the property
(e)
in
Equation A.1
. Since
D_{2}\leq D
while
D_{2}
is lower semi-continous, we have
D_{2}\leq\overline{D}\leq D
. Noticing that
D_{2}=D
on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by construction, we deduce that
C_{\infty}(\rho)=\overline{D}(\rho)=D(\rho)
whenever
\|\rho\|=1
. If in addition
D_{2}
is assumed to be convex, then recalling that
D_{2}
is l.s.c., we conclude that
D_{2}=D_{2}^{**}=C_{\infty}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For every
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have:

(4.17)

C_{\infty}(\rho)\ =\ \min\left\{\int D_{2}(Q)\,\nu(dQ):\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\ \int Q\,\nu(dQ)=\rho\right\}.

Proof.

The functional
D_{2}
is l.s.c. on
\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is a metrizable weakly* compact space.
Therefore, noticing that the barycenter constraint (namely
\int Q\,\nu(dQ)=\rho
) is closed, the infimum in the right hand side of (
4.17
) is actually a minimum. Moreover the infimum value, as a function of
\rho
, is l.s.c. and coincides with the convex lower semicontinuous enveloppe of
D_{2}
, whence (
4.17
) since
C_{\infty}=D_{2}^{**}
by (
4.10
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Owing to (
4.9
), we see that, for a bounded cost function
\ell
and a given potential
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
M_{N}(v)-M_{\infty}(v)
behaves like
\frac{1}{N}
as
N\to\infty
. The identification of the limit of
N(M_{N}(v)-M_{\infty}(v))
as a function of
v
is an interesting open issue.
Let us stress the fact that the asymptotic behavior of
M_{N}(v)
would be different
in the case of a highly confining potential
v\notin C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, in particular if
V(x)\vcentcolon=-v(x)
blows up to
+\infty
as
|x|\to\infty.

Indeed, as noticed in
[
4
, Remark 5.4]
, a connection can be made with the energy of a system of
N
points subject to an external confining potential as studied in
[
16
,
25
,
23
]
namely:

\mathcal{H}_{N}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\ell(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)+N\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i}).

By extending the definition of
M_{N}(v)
to unbounded (negative)
v
, we see that:

-M_{N}(-V)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\,\inf\left\{{\mathcal{H}_{N}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}):x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right\}.

In the case of a Riesz cost function
\ell(t)=t^{-s}
when
d\geq 3
and
d-2\leq s<d
, the above mentioned works reveals an asymptotic behavior as
N\to\infty
of the form

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\min\mathcal{H}_{N}=\min\{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(\rho):\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}+N^{\frac{s}{d}-1}(C_{d,s}(V)+o(1)),

where the second minimum is reached by a compactly supported
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\min\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=\inf\{C_{\infty}(\rho)+\int Vd\rho\;\colon\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}=-M_{\infty}(v)
, we are led to the estimate

|M_{N}(v)-M_{\infty}(v)|\sim CN^{\frac{s}{d}-1}
where
1-\frac{s}{d}<1
, in contrast with (
4.9
).

Remark 4.5
.

In the aforementioned work by C. Cotar et al.

Moreover considering the

[

]

.

In case of a bounded cost

]

(4.18)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\ell(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)\,t_{i}t_{j}\geq 0\quad\text{whenever }t_{1}+t_{2}+\dotsb+t_{m}=0.

Accordingly, under (

5.
Minimizers and ionization effect

In this section we focus on the minimizers of the problem:

(5.1)

\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{C_{\infty}(\rho)-\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle}\right\}

where
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a given external potential and
\lambda>0
is a strength parameter. As the infimum
-M_{\infty}(\lambda v)
depends only on the positive part of
v
, we will assume without loss of generality that

v\geq 0
and
\sup v>0.

In this case, we know from the
3.14
(ii) that,
under the standing assumptions (including (H4)), we have
M_{\infty}(v)>0
. Therefore the minimum of (
5.1
) is
strictly negative
, hence not reached at
\rho=0
.

We will denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
the set of minimizers: it is a non empty convex weakly* compact subset of
\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Notice that
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)=\partial M_{\infty}(\lambda v)
where
\partial M_{\infty}
denotes the subdifferental of
M_{\infty}
as a functional on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
As stated in the introduction, the problem (
5.1
) arises in the limit
N\to\infty
of

(5.2)

\min_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{\overline{C_{N}}(\rho)-\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle}\right\}.

Indeed, by the
\Gamma
-convergence of
\overline{C_{N}}
(
3.9
), the set of minimizers for the relaxed N-marginal problem (
5.2
) (namely
\partial M_{N}(\lambda v)
) converges to
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
in the sense of Kuratowski.

Our aim is to characterize situations where one of the following happens:

(a)

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(confinement regime);

(b)

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\subset\{\|\rho\|<1\}
(full ionization);

(c)

none of (i) and (ii) (partial ionization).

We expect the existence of nonnegative threshold constants
\lambda_{*}(v)\leq\lambda^{*}(v)
such that
(a)
occurs for
\lambda\geq\lambda^{*}(v)
and
(b)
for
\lambda\leq\lambda_{*}(v)
. This scenario will be confirmed (see subsection
5.1
) with a strict inequality
\lambda_{*}(v)>0
for rapidly decreasing potenials
v
(see Subsection
5.3
). Obviously, in the strictly convex case (
\ell
of strictly positive type), only one of alternatives (a) and (b) are possible since
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
is a singleton and then
\lambda_{*}(v)=\lambda^{*}(v)
.
Explicit computations of this common value are provided in the case of a radial potential
v
,
d=3
and
\ell(r)=\frac{1}{r}
(see Subsection
5.4
).

Remark 5.1
.

Note that (
5.2
) is a particular case of

\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{C_{N}(\rho)+\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\right\}
where
\mathcal{F}
is a weakly continuous perturbation.
It turns out that more general terms can be added to the infinite marginal energy, as for instance

•

\mathcal{F}(\rho)=\frac{3}{5}\int\rho^{5/3}-\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle
(Thomas-Fermi model (TF))

•

\mathcal{F}(\rho)=\int\frac{|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{\rho}-\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle
(Thomas-Fermi-von Weizsäcker model (TFW))

However, since in these cases
\mathcal{F}
is merely weakly l.s.c., the asymptotic analysis requires further technical arguments which are not studied in this paper. On the other hand, the ionization issue for the related minimization problems is, to our knowledge, far to be understood as far as general interaction costs
\ell
and external potential
v
are considered. In case of the TF model in dimension
d=3
(
\ell(r)=\frac{1}{r}
and
v
a Coulomb potential generated by a finite number of nuclei), we refer to the seminal paper by Lieb-Simon
[
18
]
where the regimes (a) (ionic case) and (b) (neutral case) are evidenced.

Remark 5.2
.

A lot of work has been dedicated to the existence issue for probabilities (situation (a)) in the case of positive type cost for which
C_{\infty}=D_{2}
and the solution to (
5.1
) is unique (see for instance
[
15
,
24
]
). In all these works where the external potential
V
appears with the opposite sign (
i.e.
V=-v
), the tightness of minimizing sequences is obtained under the condition that the supremum of
S_{2}v-c_{2}
is stricly negative on the complement of
K^{2}
being
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a large compact subset.
Let us notice that the latter condition implies that
\limsup_{|x|\to+\infty}v<0
which excludes the possibility of considering external potentials
v
vanishing at infinity. In this sense the technical arguments used in the aformentioned works needs to be improved in order to handle the case
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and possibly relaxed solutions
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
such that
\|\rho\|<1
.
In Subsection
5.3
, we propose a criterium of slow decay of
v
at infinity ensuring the confinement scenario (a) for all
\lambda>0
while, in the opposite direction, the ionization scenario (b) is shown for every compactly supported
v
and
\lambda

being small enough.

5.1.
Existence of thresholds

In this subsection, we are in the general framework of a cost function
\ell
satisfying
(H1)-(H4)
.
For futher discussions it is convenient to introduce the function
\mathcal{I}:C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,1]
defined by

\mathcal{I}(v):=\min\{\|\rho\|:\rho\in\partial M_{\infty}(v)\},

where the minimum is attained since
\partial M_{\infty}(v)=\mathcal{S}_{1}(v)
is convex weakly* compact.
Then, for every
\lambda\geq 0
, the occurrence of scenario (a) (
i.e.
,
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
)
is equivalent to
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)=1
. Accordingly, we define the
upper ionization
threshold

(5.3)

\lambda^{*}(v)\vcentcolon=\sup\left\{{\lambda>0\;\colon\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)<1}\right\}

and the
lower ionization threshold
under which scenario (b) occurs:

(5.4)

\lambda_{*}(v)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{{\lambda>0\;\colon S_{\lambda}(v)\cap\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\neq\emptyset}\right\}.

Clearly one has
\lambda_{*}(v)\leq\lambda^{*}(v)
. In case of a strict inequality, scenario (c) (partial ionization) will occur for
\lambda
in between.
Next, recalling the monotonicity property of
3.13
, we associate to each
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the constant:

\mathcal{K}(v):=\sup_{t>0}\frac{M_{\infty}(tv)}{t^{2}}\ =\ \lim_{t\to 0_{+}}\frac{M_{\infty}(tv)}{t^{2}}.

and the additional threshold:

(5.5)

\kappa(v)\ :=\ \sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0\ :\ \frac{M_{\infty}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}=\mathcal{K}(v)\right\},

where, by convention, we set
\kappa(v)=0
if
\mathcal{K}(v)=+\infty
.
Note that, if
\mathcal{K}(v)<+\infty
, the subset of reals
\lambda
appearing above coincides with the interval
[0,\kappa(v)]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
v\geq 0
and
\sup v>0
. Then it holds:

0\leq\ \lambda_{*}(v)\ \leq\ \kappa(v)\leq\ \lambda^{*}(v)\ <\ +\infty.

Furthermore, if
\kappa:=\kappa(v)>0
, then
\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}(v)
is a subset of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
while:

\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)=\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}(v)\subset\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\quad\text{for every}\quad\lambda\in[0,\kappa].

Remark 5.4
.

In case of a stricly convex functional
C_{\infty}
(for instance when
\ell
is of positive type), the situation gets much simpler since the subset
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
reduces to a single element
\rho_{\lambda}
and the inequalities
(
5.3
) become equalities,
i.e.
:

\lambda_{*}(v)=\kappa(v)=\lambda^{*}(v)
for every
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In addition, if
\mathcal{K}(v)<+\infty
, we
recover the linear behavior of
\rho_{\lambda}
observed in the examples of Subsection
5.4
, namely:

\rho_{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{\kappa(v)}\rho_{\kappa(v)}\quad,\quad M_{\infty}(\lambda v)=\mathcal{K}(v)\,\lambda^{2}\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,\kappa(v)]\ ,

while
\|\rho_{\lambda}\|=1\quad\forall\lambda\geq\kappa(v)
.
Unfortunately, the two authors did not succeed in providing any example relying on the non uniqueness of solutions for somes values of
\lambda
where the strict inequality
\lambda_{*}(v)<\lambda^{*}(v)
occurs.

In order to prove
5.3
, some preliminary steps are in order.
First we relate the set
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
to the solutions of another simpler problem sharing the same infimum,namely

\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{{D_{2}(\rho)-\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle}\right\}

(by (
4.10
), the infimum above
i.e.
-D_{2}^{*}(\lambda v)
coincides with
-M_{\infty}(\lambda v)
).
Since
D_{2}
is weakly* l.s.c. on
\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the set of solutions to the latter problem is therefore a non empty compact subset
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)\subset\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
. The counterpart of the function
\mathcal{I}(v)
defined above is then

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(v):=\min\{\|\rho\|:\rho\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(v)\}.

Notice that the equality
C_{\infty}(\rho)=D_{2}(\rho)
holds for any element
\rho\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let be given
\lambda\geq 0
and
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)=\overline{\co}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)\right),\quad\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)=\mathcal{I}(\lambda v).

Proof.

To establish the first equality, we pick up any element
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
to which we associate, by means of the representation formula (
4.17
), a suitable
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that
\rho=\int Q\,\nu(dQ)
. Then we have:

-M_{\infty}(\lambda v)=C_{\infty}(\rho)-\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle=\int\left(D_{2}(Q)-\lambda\left\langle{v,Q}\right\rangle\right)\,\nu(dQ).

Since
D_{2}(Q)-\lambda\left\langle{v,Q}\right\rangle\geq-M_{\infty}(\lambda v)
for every
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the equality above implies that

Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
for
\nu
-almost all
Q
. The inclusion

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\subset\overline{\co}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)\right)
follows. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
is convex and compact, the converse inclusion is obvious as well as the inequality
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)\leq\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)
. It remains to show that
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)\geq\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)
.
To that aim we select an element
\rho\in S_{\lambda}(v)
of minimal mass ,
i.e.
such that that
\|\rho\|=\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)
.
As before, it holds
Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
thus
\|Q\|\geq\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)
for
\nu
-almost all
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By integrating with respect to
\nu
, we arrive to
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)=\|\rho\|=\int\|Q\|\,\nu(dQ)\geq\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Next we give the following constancy result which will be crucial:

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\lambda_{0}>0
and
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
0<\mathcal{I}(\lambda_{0}v)<1
. Then, whenever
\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda<\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\mathcal{I}(\lambda_{0}v)}
, we have

\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)<1\ \text{and}\quad\frac{M_{\infty}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}=\frac{M_{\infty}(\lambda_{0}v)}{\lambda_{0}^{2}}\quad.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{0}\in\widetilde{S_{\lambda_{0}}}(v)
be such that
\|\rho_{0}\|=\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda_{0}v)
and let
\lambda=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\theta}
where
\theta
is any real number such that
\|\rho_{0}\|<\theta\leq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle M_{\infty}(\lambda_{0}v)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}\left\langle{v,\rho_{0}}\right\rangle-D_{2}(\rho_{0})

\displaystyle=\theta^{2}\left({\left\langle{\frac{\lambda_{0}v}{\theta},\frac{\rho_{0}}{\theta}}\right\rangle-D_{2}(\frac{\rho_{0}}{\theta})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ \theta^{2}\,D_{2}^{*}\left({\frac{\lambda_{0}v}{\theta}}\right)\ ,

where in the second line, we exploit the two-homogeneity property of
D_{2}
.
By the monotonicity
3.13
and thanks to the equality
D_{2}^{*}=M_{\infty}
, we infer that the inequality of the last line is actually an equality, whence

M_{\infty}(\lambda v)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda_{0}^{2}}M_{\infty}(\lambda_{0}v)
as claimed . In addition, by setting
\rho:=\frac{\rho_{0}}{\theta}
and after dividing by
\theta^{2}
,
we are led to the relation
\left\langle{\lambda v,\rho}\right\rangle-D_{2}(\rho)=M_{\infty}(\lambda v)
. Therefore
\rho\in\widetilde{S_{\lambda}}(v)
and we get:

\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)\ =\ \tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)\ \leq\ \|\rho\|\ =\ \frac{\|\rho_{0}\|}{\theta}\ <\ 1\ .

∎

Proof of
5.3
.

In a first step, we show that
\lambda^{*}(v)<+\infty
. Let
\lambda>0
such that
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)<1
. Then, as
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)=\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)
by
5.5
,
there exist
\rho\in\widetilde{S_{\lambda}(v)}
such that
0<\|\rho\|<1
. Then the polynomial function

g_{\lambda}(t):=D_{2}(t\rho)-\lambda\,\left\langle{tv,\rho}\right\rangle\ =\ t^{2}\,D_{2}(\rho)-\lambda\,t\,\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle

reaches its minimum on the interval
[0,\|\rho\|^{-1}]
at
t=1
.
The necessary condition
g_{\lambda}^{\prime}(1)=0
implies then the equi-partition of energies:

D_{2}(\rho)=\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle=M_{\infty}(\lambda v),

from which we deduce

\left\{{\lambda>0\;\colon\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)<1}\right\}\subset\left\{{\lambda>0\;\colon M_{\infty}(\lambda v)\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\sup v}\right\}.

Clearly the subset of real numbers in the right hand side above is a finite interval since, by
3.10
, the function
t\mapsto\frac{M_{\infty}(tv)}{t}
is monotone and converges increasingly to
\sup v_{+}
as
t\to\infty
.
The desired inequality
\lambda^{*}(v)<+\infty
follows.

In a second step, we show that
\lambda_{*}(v)\leq\kappa(v)
. It is not restrictive to assume that
\lambda_{*}\vcentcolon=\lambda_{*}(v)>0
.
Then, obviously, we have
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)<1
for every
\lambda<\lambda_{*}
. Since
M_{\infty}
is convex and Lipschitz, the function
k(t)\vcentcolon=\frac{M_{\infty}(tv)}{t^{2}}
is
locally Lipschitz on
(0,+\infty)
where it admits left and right derivatives.
By applying the constancy
5.6
, we infer that
k^{\prime}(\lambda+0)=0
for every
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
which in turn implies that
k
is constant on the whole interval. This constant is finite and coincides with
\mathcal{K}(v)=\lim_{t\to 0_{+}}k(t)
. In virtue of (
5.5
), we deduce that
\lambda_{*}(v)\leq\kappa(v)
.

In a third step, we show that
\kappa(v)\leq\lambda^{*}(v)
. It is not restrictive to assume that
\kappa\vcentcolon=\kappa(v)>0
.
Let
\rho_{*}\in S_{\kappa}(v)
. Then we claim that
\rho_{*}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Indeed, suppose that
\|\rho_{*}\|<1
. Then

\mathcal{I}(\kappa v)<1
and by
5.6
, we would get that

\mathcal{K}(v)=\frac{M_{\infty}(\kappa v)}{\kappa^{2}}=\frac{M_{\infty}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}},

for some
\lambda>\kappa
. This is not possible in view of (
5.5
).
Therefore we have
S_{\kappa}(v)\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{I}(\kappa v)=1
. The desired inequality

\kappa(v)\leq\lambda^{*}(v)
is now straightforward from the definition (
5.3
).

In a last step, we consider a real
\lambda
such that
0\leq\lambda\leq\kappa
and show first that

\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}S_{\kappa}(v)\subset S_{\lambda}(v)
and then that
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)=\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}
.
Letting
\rho_{*}\in S_{\kappa}(v)
and
t\in[0,1]
, by using the constancy of the ratio
\frac{M_{\infty}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}
for
\lambda\in(0,\kappa]
(see
5.6
), we have

D_{2}(t\rho_{*})-\left\langle{\lambda v,t\rho_{*}}\right\rangle=t^{2}\left(D_{2}(\rho_{*})-\left\langle{\kappa v,\rho_{*}}\right\rangle\right)=-t^{2}M_{\infty}(\kappa v)=-M_{\infty}(t\kappa v).

In particular, for
t=\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}
, we infer that
\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}\rho_{*}\in S_{\lambda}(v)
and, thanks to
\|\rho_{*}\|=1
(see third step), that
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}
.
Eventually we claim that a strict inequality
\mathcal{I}(\lambda v)<\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}
is not possible for
\lambda\in(0,\kappa)
.
Indeed, it would imply the existence of
\rho\in S_{\lambda}(v)
such that
\|\rho\|<\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}
. Then, by setting
\mu\vcentcolon=\frac{\rho}{\|\rho\|}
and
\tilde{\lambda}\vcentcolon=\frac{\lambda}{\|\rho\|}
, we would get

M_{\infty}(\lambda v)=\lambda\left\langle{v,\rho}\right\rangle-C_{\infty}(\rho)=\|\rho\|^{2}\,(\tilde{\lambda}\left\langle{v,\mu}\right\rangle-C_{\infty}(\mu))\leq\|\rho\|^{2}\,M_{\infty}(\tilde{\lambda}v),

whence
\mathcal{K}(v)=\frac{M_{\infty}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}\leq\frac{M_{\infty}(\tilde{\lambda}v)}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}
.
In virtue of (
5.5
), the latter inequality is not possible since
\tilde{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{\|\rho\|}>\kappa
.
The proof of
5.3
is now complete.
∎

5.2.
Optimality conditions

To every
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we associate
the convolution potential
u_{\rho}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto[0,+\infty]
defined by

(5.6)

u_{\rho}(x)\vcentcolon=\int\ell(|y-x|)\,\rho(dy).

It is a lower semicontinuous function and, as
D_{2}(\rho)=\int u_{\rho}\,d\rho
, it holds
u_{\rho}\in L^{1}_{\rho}
whenever
D_{2}(\rho)
is finite.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let be given
\lambda\geq 0
and
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(i)

(necessary condition) Let
\rho\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
. Then there exists a constant
c_{\lambda}
such that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(a)&c_{\lambda}\leq 0,\quad c_{\lambda}(1-\|\rho\|)\geq 0,\\
(b)&u_{\rho}-\frac{\lambda}{2}v=c_{\lambda}\quad\text{$\rho$- a.e.},\\
(c)&u_{\rho}-\frac{\lambda}{2}v\geq c_{\lambda}\quad\text{$\nu$- a.e},\ \text{whenever $D_{2}(\nu)+\int u_{\rho}\,d\nu<\infty$.}\end{cases}

(ii)

Assume that
D_{2}
is convex (thus
C_{\infty}=D_{2}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)=\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
). Then:

\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\ \iff\ \exists c_{\lambda}\ \text{such that
\eqref{cnD} holds}.

Remark 5.8
.

By integrating the equality (
5.7
)(b) with respect to
\rho
, we obtain the identities

(5.8)

C_{\infty}(\rho_{\lambda})=\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\langle{v,\rho_{\lambda}}\right\rangle+c_{\lambda}\,\|\rho\|,\quad M_{\infty}(\lambda v)=\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\langle{v,\rho_{\lambda}}\right\rangle-c_{\lambda}\,\|\rho_{\lambda}\|,

for any pair satisfying condition (
5.7
).

Proof.

First we observe that, for every
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u_{\rho}
is a lower semicontinuous function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
[0,+\infty]
such that
D_{2}(\rho)=\int u_{\rho}\,d\rho
. Hence
u_{\rho}\in L^{1}_{\rho}
whenever
D_{2}(\rho)
is finite. It is the case in particular when
\rho
is an element of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
and then, for every non negative measure
\nu
admissible in the sense of condition (c), we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(D_{2}(\rho+\varepsilon(\nu-\rho))-D_{2}(\rho))=2\,\left\langle{u_{\rho},\nu}\right\rangle.

The minimality of
\rho
implies then the following variational inequality:

\left\langle{u_{\rho}-\frac{\lambda}{2}v,\nu}\right\rangle\geq\left\langle{u_{\rho}-\frac{\lambda}{2}v,\rho}\right\rangle=\vcentcolon c_{\lambda}.

This is the exact counterpart of the condition given in
[
15
,
24
]
which we extend to
admissible competitors
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By taking
\nu=0
, we deduce that the constant
c_{\lambda}
defined above
satisfies
c_{\lambda}\leq 0
while, if
\|\rho\|<1
, we get
c_{\lambda}\geq 0
by taking
\nu=\frac{\rho}{\|\rho\|}
.
By localizing , we deduce the necessary optimality conditions (
5.7
) given in assertion i).
Next we observe that, if
D_{2}
is convex, then the inequality
\int u_{\rho}\,d\nu\leq\frac{1}{2}(D_{2}(\rho)+D_{2}(\nu))
ensures the validity of condition (
5.7
)(c) assuming merely that
D_{2}(\nu)<+\infty
.
Then clearly, conditions (
5.7
) imply the variational inequality above which, as well known for convex problems, characterizes an optimal
\rho
.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)
. Then
\supp\rho\subset\supp v
.

Proof.

In view of
5.5
, it is enough to show
the inclusion
\supp\rho\subset\supp v
when
\rho\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
.
By appying the optimality condition in (
5.7
)(b) of
5.7
and since
c_{\lambda}\leq 0
, we infer that
u_{\rho}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\,v
holds
\rho-
a.e.
On the other hand, for any
r>0
, we have the inequality:

u_{\rho}(x)\geq\int_{B(x,r)}\ell(|y-x|)\,\rho(dy)\geq\underline{\ell}(r)\rho(B(x,r)),

being
\underline{\ell}(r)
defined by (
5.10
).
It follows that
A_{r}\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \rho(B(x,r))>\frac{\lambda}{2}\ \frac{v(x)}{\underline{\ell}(r)}\right\}

is
\rho

,

\rho(B(x,r))\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{v(x)}{\underline{\ell}(r)},\quad\forall x\in\supp(\rho).

By

. Therefore
the inclusion

5.9
).
∎

5.3.
Estimates for the threshold values

Let us associate with the cost
\ell

its lower and upper non increasing counterparts:

(5.10)

\underline{\ell}(r)\vcentcolon=\inf\{l(t):t\leq r\},\quad\overline{\ell}(r)\vcentcolon=\sup\{l(t):t\geq r\}.

In the forthcoming estimates, we will also need the average of a radial function
f(|x|)
on a ball, namely:

(5.11)

[f](r)\vcentcolon=\frac{d}{r^{d}}\int_{0}^{r}f(t)\,t^{d-1}\,dt=d\int_{0}^{1}f(rs)\,s^{d-1}\,ds.

By (
3.7
), the function
[\ell](r)
is finite on
(0,+\infty)
and it is easy to check that:

\underline{\ell}(2r)\leq\ K(r,d)\leq 2^{d}\,[\ell](2r).

Lemma 5.10
.

The functions
\underline{\ell},\overline{\ell},[\underline{\ell}],[\overline{\ell}]
are l.s.c. monotone non increasing
and vanish at infinity. Moreover
\underline{\ell}
is positive at least on a small interval
[0,\delta]
.

Proof.

By (H2), the infimum in (
5.10
) is a minimum, thus
\underline{\ell}
is strictly positive on a small intervall
[0,\delta]
since otherwise
we would have
\ell(0)=0
. On the other hand, for any given
\alpha>0
, the inequality
\overline{\ell}(r)\leq\alpha
holds if and only if the open subset
\{\ell>\alpha\}
is contained in
[0,r)
. This property is clearly stable under convergence of a sequence
r_{n}\to r
, whence the lower semicontinuity property of
\overline{\ell}
.
The monotonicity property of
[\underline{\ell}],[\overline{\ell}]
can be deduced from the second equality in (
5.11
) when choosing
f
to be non decreasing. On the other hand, passing to the limit
R\to\infty
in the inequality
[\overline{\ell}](R)\leq\frac{d}{R^{d}}(\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\overline{\ell}(r)\,r^{d-1}\,dr)+\overline{\ell}(R_{0})
, and then as
R_{0}\to+\infty
,
we deduce that
[\overline{\ell}](R)\to 0
as
R\to\infty
. Eventually we get the lower semicontinuity of
[\ell],[\underline{\ell}],[\overline{\ell}]
by applying Fatou’s lemma.
∎

We are now in position to give a lower estimate for the lower threshold
\lambda_{*}(v)
defined in (
5.4
).
For every
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{+})
, we set
\alpha_{v}\in(0,+\infty]
to be defined by

(5.12)

\alpha_{v}\vcentcolon=\sup\left\{\frac{v(x)}{\underline{\ell}(2|x|)},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}6.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\alpha_{v}
given in (
5.12
).
Then we have the inequality:

\lambda_{*}(v)\geq\frac{2}{\alpha_{v}}.

In view of the lower bound given above , the condition
\alpha_{v}<+\infty
is sufficient to predict
full ionization (scenario (b)) for small values of
\lambda
. This applies in particular when
v
is compactly supported
or more generally when
\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{v(x)}{\underline{\ell}(2|x|)}<+\infty.

Note however that the estimate of
5.11
is not optimal in view of the examples given in the radial case (see Subsection
5.4
where
\ell(r)=\frac{1}{r},v(x)=V(|x|)
). In this examples, an exact threshold value (
5.20
) can be derived in term of a constant
\alpha_{V}
similar to
\alpha_{v}
(see (
5.19
)) but not including the doubling factor as in (
5.12
).

Proof.

Assume that
\alpha_{v}<+\infty
and let
\lambda\in(0,\frac{2}{\alpha_{v}})
. Using
5.5
, it is enough to show
that any
\rho\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
satisfies
\|\rho\|<1
.
Let us apply the inequality (
5.9
) at any point
x\in\supp(\rho)

with
r=2|x|
. In view of (
5.12
), we deduce that:

\rho(B(0,|x|))\leq\rho(B(x,2|x|))\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\ \frac{v(x)}{\underline{\ell}(2|x|)}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\alpha_{v}\quad\forall x\in\supp(\rho).

By selecting a sequence
x_{n}\in\supp(\rho)
such that
\rho(B(0,x_{n}))\to\|\rho\|
, we may conclude that

\|\rho\|\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\alpha_{v}<1
.
∎

In the last part of this subsection, our aim is to provide an upperbound for the upper threshold
\lambda^{*}(v)
defined in (
5.3
). By
5.3
, we know already that
\lambda^{*}(v)<+\infty
. An interesting issue would be in particular to characterize potential
v
for which
\lambda^{*}(v)=0
, meaning that
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\lambda>0
(we will say that
v
is
strongly confining
).
From now on, we require an additional assumption ensuring a slow decay of
\ell
at infinity,
namely we assume that it exists a Borel function
k_{\ell}:(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that:

(5.13)

\begin{cases}\ell(sr)\leq k_{\ell}(s)\ell(r),&\forall s\in(0,1],\forall r>0;\\
k_{\ell}(1^{-})=1;&I(k_{\ell})\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{1}k_{\ell}(s)s^{d-1}\,ds<+\infty.\end{cases}

This assumption is satisfied for
\ell(r)=r^{-p}
with
0<p<d
. However it rules out exponentially decreasing
costs (like
\ell(r)=\frac{\exp(-\varepsilon r)}{r}
), for which we expect that
\lambda^{*}(v)=0
is not possible for
v\in C_{0}
unless
v\equiv 0
.

To every
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{+})
, we associate the constant
\beta_{v}\in(0,+\infty]
defined by

(5.14)

\ \beta_{v}\vcentcolon=\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\ \frac{v(x)}{\overline{\ell}(|x|)}.

Note that
\beta_{v}\leq\alpha_{v}
as a consequence of the inequality
\overline{\ell}(r)\geq\underline{\ell}(2r)
.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\ell
satisfy (
5.13
) and
\beta_{v}
be given by (
5.14
). Then

0\leq\lambda^{*}(v)\leq\frac{2}{\beta_{v}}.

Accordingly
v
is strongly confining if
\beta_{v}=+\infty

Proof.

By
5.5
and the definition (
5.3
), we are reduced to prove the following implication:

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda v)<1\ \implies\lambda\leq\frac{2}{\beta_{v}}.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\rho\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}(v)
with
\|\rho\|<1
.
We fix a positive
\eta>1
and denote by
\theta_{R,\eta}
the uniform probability on the crown

S_{R,\eta}:=\{R<|x|<\eta R\}
. By applying the optimality condition (c) and integrating with respect to
\nu=\theta_{R,\eta}
, we get:

(5.15)

\left\langle{u_{\rho},\theta_{R,\eta}}\right\rangle=\int w_{R,\eta}\,d\rho\ =\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\langle{v,\theta_{R}}\right\rangle,

being
w_{R,\eta}(x)\vcentcolon=\int\ell(|x-y|)\,\theta_{R,\eta}(dy)
the radial potential associated with
\theta_{R,\eta}
.
We claim that, for every
\eta>1
,

(5.16)

\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{\left\langle{v,\theta_{R,\eta}}\right\rangle}{\overline{\ell}(R)}\geq\beta_{v}k_{\ell}(\eta^{-1}),\quad\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{\int w_{R,\eta}\,d\rho}{\overline{\ell}(R)}\leq\left\lVert{\rho}\right\rVert.

Under (
5.16
), by passing to the limit as
R\to\infty
in the equality (
5.15
) after dividing by
\overline{\ell}(R)
, we get:

\beta_{v}\ k_{\ell}(\eta^{-1})\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}\left\lVert{\rho}\right\rVert<\frac{2}{\lambda}.

The desired inequality follows ultimately as
\eta\to 1^{+}
since
k(1^{-})=1
.

Proof of claim (
5.16
)
We prove first the left hand side inequality. It is not restrictive to assume
that
\beta_{v}>0
. Let
\beta\in(0,\beta_{v})
. Then, recalling that
\overline{\ell}
is non increasing, we have
for large values of

\left\langle{v,\theta_{R,\eta}}\right\rangle\geq\beta\left\langle{\overline{\ell}(|x|),\theta_{R,\eta}}\right\rangle\geq\beta\overline{\ell}(\eta R)\geq\beta k_{\ell}(\eta^{-1})\overline{\ell}(R),

where, in the last inequality, we used the fact
\overline{\ell}
satisfies the same inequality as
\ell
in (
5.13
) while keeping the same factor
k_{\ell}(s)
(here
s=\eta^{-1}
).
The desired inequality follows as
R\to\infty
after dividing by
\overline{\ell}(R)
and by letting
\beta\nearrow\beta_{v}
.

The proof of the second inequality in (

) is more technical. Let us fix
R_{0}>0
such that
R_{0}<R
.
Then, since
\ell([y-x|)\leq\overline{\ell}(R-R_{0})
whenever

and

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle{w_{R,\eta},\theta_{R,\eta}}\right\rangle}{\overline{\ell}(R)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{\ell}(R-R_{0})}{\overline{\ell}(R)}\rho(|x|\leq R_{0})+\frac{\sup w_{R,\eta}}{\overline{\ell}(R)}\rho(|x|>R_{0})

\displaystyle\leq k\left({1-\frac{R_{0}}{R}}\right)\rho(|x|\leq R_{0})+C_{\eta}\,\rho(|x|>R_{0}),

where we have set
\displaystyle C_{\eta}\vcentcolon=\sup_{R}\frac{\sup\,w_{R,\eta}}{\overline{\ell}(R)}
.
If we can show that
C_{\eta}<+\infty
, then the desired inequality will clearly follow by sending

and then

.
Clearly
w_{R,\eta}\leq\overline{w}_{R,\eta}
where
\overline{w}_{R,\eta}(x)\vcentcolon=\int\overline{\ell}(|x-y|)\,\theta_{R,\eta}(dy)

Since

∎

5.4.
Radial examples

In this subsection we assume that

(5.17)

d=3,\quad\ell(r)=\frac{1}{r},\quad v(x)=V(|x|),

where
V:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to[0,+\infty)
is continuous locally Lipschitz such that

\lim_{r\to\infty}V(r)=0.

As the Coulomb cost
\ell(r)=\frac{1}{r}
is of positive type on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the extended direct energy
D_{2}
is a stricly convex functional on
\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Therefore
C_{\infty}=D_{2}
(see
4.2
(ii)) and, for every
\lambda\geq 0
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(v)=\{\rho_{\lambda}\}
where
\rho_{\lambda}
is the unique minimizer
of (
5.1
). Accordingly, the two thresholds introduced in (
5.4
)(
5.3
) are
equal and coincide with

\lambda_{V}:=\min\{\lambda\geq 0:\|\rho_{\lambda}\|=1\}.

By using the invariance under rotations and te convexity of
C_{\infty}
, it is easy to show that
\rho_{\lambda}
is radial. Therefore
\rho_{\lambda}
is determined by the cumulative distribution function:

F_{\lambda}(r):=\rho_{\lambda}(\{|x|\leq r\}),

which is non increasing in
[0,+\infty]
and satisfies

(5.18)

F_{\lambda}(+\infty)=F(r_{\lambda})=\left\lVert{\rho_{\lambda}}\right\rVert\quad\text{where}\quad r_{\lambda}\vcentcolon=\sup\{|x|:x\in\supp(\rho_{\lambda})\}.

Next we introduce the two parameters
\alpha_{V}
and
r_{V}^{*}
defined by:

(5.19)

\alpha_{V}\vcentcolon=\sup_{r>0}rV(r),\quad r_{V}^{*}\vcentcolon=\inf\{r>0:rV(r)=\alpha_{V}\},

(with the convention that
r_{V}^{*}=+\infty
if the infimum is not attained).

We are going to show that the threshold value
\lambda_{V}
is given by

(5.20)

\lambda_{V}=\frac{2}{\alpha_{V}}

and that, for every
\lambda>\lambda_{V}
, the effective radius
r_{\lambda}
given by (
5.18
)) satisfies
r_{\lambda}<r_{V}^{*}

(hence finite) while
r_{\lambda}=r_{V}^{*}
for every
\lambda\leq\lambda_{V}
.

A key argument of the proof relies on the fact that
\frac{1}{4\pi r}
is a fundamental solution of the 3d Laplacian; then
C_{\infty}(\rho)<+\infty
implies that
\rho\in W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
while

C_{\infty}(\rho)\ =\ D_{2}(\rho)\ =\ \left\langle{u_{\rho},\rho}\right\rangle\ =\ \frac{1}{4\pi}\int|\nabla u_{\rho}|^{2}\,dx,

where
u_{\rho}
is the Coulomb potential generated by
\rho
,
i.e.
, the solution of

(5.21)

-\Delta u_{\rho}=4\pi\rho,\quad u_{\rho}\to 0\quad\text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.

Note that here
u_{\rho}
is defined a.e. in the sense of the Newtonian capacity, hence admits a representative defined
\nu
-a.e., where
\nu
is any element of
\mathcal{P}_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Particularizing to
\rho
being the solution
\rho_{\lambda}
, we get a radial potential in the form

U_{\lambda}(|x|)
, where
U_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is continuous non increasing and
U_{\lambda}(+\infty)=0
.
Then the Laplace equation (
5.21
) holding in the distributional sense in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
implies that
F_{\lambda}
is a primitive of the non negative measure
(-r^{2}U_{\lambda}^{\prime})^{\prime}
. More precisely,
U_{\lambda}
can be deduced from
F_{\lambda}

by the conditions

(5.22)

F_{\lambda}(r)=-r^{2}U_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r-0),\quad F_{\lambda}(r+0)=-r^{2}U_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r+0)\quad\forall r>0.

which, in the case
r_{\lambda}<+\infty
, imply that

U_{\lambda}(r)=\frac{\|\rho_{\lambda}\|}{r}\quad\forall r\geq r_{\lambda}.

In order to simplify the computations, we make the additional assumption that the potential

V
satisfies:

(5.23)

-r^{2}V^{\prime}(r)\text{ is monotone non decreasing on $\{V>0\}$}.

This implies that
V
is non increasing, hence
\{V>0\}
is an open interval
[0,r_{V})
(
r_{V}\in(0,+\infty]
) and
V^{\prime}
admits a left
V^{\prime}(r-0)
at any
r\in(0,r_{V}]
(resp. a right derivative
V^{\prime}(r+0)

6.
Continuum models as limits of small range interaction costs

In this section we study the asymptotic behavior of the multi-marginal cost
C_{N}
when the two-particles repulsive cost
\ell
is rescaled with respect to a small parameter
\varepsilon
which tends to zero while
N\to\infty
. More precisely, we propose to replace
\ell
with
\ell_{\varepsilon}(t)\vcentcolon=\ell(t/\varepsilon)
in the definition (
1.2
) of
c_{N}
, thus leading to the small range interaction cost:

(6.1)

c_{N}^{\varepsilon}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})=\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\ell\left({\frac{\left|{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right|}{\varepsilon}}\right).

Motivated by the passage from discrete to continuous models arising for instance in crowd or traffic congestion, we will assume that the particles stay in the closure
\overline{\Omega}
of a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and we
propose to scale
\varepsilon
so that

(6.2)

N\varepsilon^{d}\to\kappa

where
\kappa
is a positive parameter which roughly represents the limit volume as
\varepsilon\to 0
of a cluster of
N
particles placed at the nodes of a
\varepsilon Q
-periodic grid, where
Q=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
is the unit cube of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Choosing
N
to be the driving parameter as in the previous sections, we set

\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N}\vcentcolon=\left({\frac{\kappa}{N}}\right)^{1/d}.

Accordingly, the functional on
\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})
under study will be

(6.3)

F_{N}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{{\int c_{N}^{\varepsilon_{N}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\ dP(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\;\colon P\in\Pi_{N}(\rho)}\right\}.

Our aim is to characterize the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{N}
as a functional on
\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})
. It is natural to expect such a
\Gamma
-limit to be the restriction to
\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})
of a local functional defined on absolutely continuous measures that is of the form
F_{\infty}(\rho)=\int_{\Omega}f(\frac{d\rho}{dx})\,dx
for a suitable convex l.s.c integrand
f
. This guess is confirmed in a particular case on which we focus now on.
The general case is, in our opinion, a very challenging issue that deserves further studies.

Henceforth we assume that the repulsive potential is given by

(6.4)

\ell(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $t\geq 1$}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that this function
\ell
does not satisfy the local integrability assumption (H4) given in the introduction. The corresponding interaction energy given (
6.1
) is related to the so called
hard sphere model
, in which congruent spheres of diameter
\varepsilon
centered at
x_{i}
are packed in a container in such a way that they do not overlap each other.

Despite its simplicity this hard-sphere repulsive potential is used in physics for understanding the equilibrium and dynamical properties of a variety of materials, including simple fluids, colloids, glasses, granular media
[
26
]
. We suggest that it can be also used in the crowd motion modeling in order to justify the passage from the discrete to the continuous level, with a possible link with congestion transport theory as depicted in
[
20
]
.

In this crowd model the particles or individuals have a given minimal distance
\varepsilon
to each other and are located on discrete subset
\Sigma
of a container
\overline{\Omega}
. To any such configuration we may associate the empirical measure

\mu_{\Sigma}\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{\sharp(\Sigma)}\sum_{x\in\Sigma}\delta_{x}.

Accordingly, we may define another functional on
\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})
by setting

(6.5)

\widetilde{F_{N}}(\rho)=\begin{cases}F_{N}(\rho)&\text{if $\rho=\mu_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma\subset\overline{\Omega}$}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

As can be readily checked,
\widetilde{F_{N}}
is non-convex and larger than
F_{N}
. Observe that both functionals
F_{N}
and
\widetilde{F_{N}}
are indicator functions, meaning that they take values in
\left\{{0,+\infty}\right\}
, due to the particular choice of the cost function
\ell
in (
6.4
). Moreover, they share the same Fenchel conjugate as we have:

(6.6)

F_{N}^{*}(v)=\widetilde{F_{N}}^{*}(v)=\sup\left\{{S_{N}v(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\;\colon x_{i}\in\overline{\Omega},\left|{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right|\geq\varepsilon_{N}\text{ if $i\neq j$}}\right\}.

Therefore, since
F_{N}
is convex and weakly lower semicontinuous, we have
\widetilde{F_{N}}^{**}=F_{N}
, which means that
F_{N}
coincides with the convex l.s.c. envelope of
\widetilde{F_{N}}
. Note that the supremum in (
6.6
) is actually a maximum, as
\overline{\Omega}^{N}
is compact.

Our convergence result involves a constant related to the densest sphere packing volume fraction in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
namely:

(6.7)

\gamma_{d}\vcentcolon=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{S(Q_{k})}{k^{d}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{S(Q_{k})}{k^{d}},

where
Q_{k}=\left[-\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}\right]^{d}
and, for any Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
S(A)
denotes the maximal number of points in
A
with mutual distance larger or equal to
1
, that is (denoting
\Delta
the diagonal of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
)

(6.8)

S(A)\vcentcolon=\sup\left\{{\sharp(\Sigma)\;\colon\Sigma\subset A,\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\ \forall(x,y)\in\Sigma^{2}\setminus\Delta}\right\}

.

For every

as

.

Before presenting the proof, we need some results linked with the packing constant
\gamma_{d}
. First we mention that the equality in (
6.7
) is a consequence of a classical result in ergodic theory (see for instance
[
17
, Thm 2.1]
) which applies to any set function
S
on Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is translation invariant and subadditive on disjoint sets,
i.e.
,
S(A\cup B)\leq S(A)+S(B)
whenever
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Observe moreover that, for our
S
given in (
6.8
), the subadditivity property above applies also for intersecting subsets.

Given a bounded Borel subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define for every
\varepsilon>0
:

\displaystyle n_{\varepsilon}(A)\vcentcolon=\sup\left\{{\sharp(\Sigma)\;\colon\Sigma\subset A,\left|{x-y}\right|\geq\varepsilon\ \forall(x,y)\in\Sigma^{2}\setminus\Delta}\right\}

\displaystyle\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}(A)\vcentcolon=\sup\left\{{\sharp(\Sigma)\;\colon\Sigma\subset A,\left|{x-y}\right|\geq\varepsilon\ \forall(x,y)\in(\Sigma^{2}\setminus\Delta)\cup(\Sigma\times\partial A)}\right\}.

Note that, since
A
is bounded, the suprema above are finite, hence they are both maxima:
n_{\varepsilon}(A)
is the maximal number of points in
A
with mutual distance
\geq\varepsilon
, while
{\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}}(A)
denotes the maximal number of non overlaping open balls of diameter
\varepsilon
contained in
A
. Obviously
n_{\varepsilon}
and
{\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}}
are non decreasing set functions and
{\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}}\leq n_{\varepsilon}
.

It can be readily checked that
n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{a})=S(Q_{a/\varepsilon})
for every
a>0
and that

n_{\varepsilon}(A\cup B)\leq n_{\varepsilon}(A)+n_{\varepsilon}(B)\quad\text{for all Borel sets $A,B$}.

In contrast, the set function
{\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}}
is super-additive on disjoint sets,
i.e.
,

{\tilde{n}}_{\varepsilon}(A\cup B)\geq{\tilde{n}}_{\varepsilon}(A)+{\tilde{n}}_{\varepsilon}(B)\quad\text{ whenever $A\cap B=\emptyset$}.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A
be a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non empty interior such that
|\partial A|=0
. Then

(6.10)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}n_{\varepsilon}(A)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}(A)=\gamma_{d}|A|.

Furthermore, for every
\varepsilon>0
let
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\subset A
be an optimal subset for
n_{\varepsilon}(A)
(resp. for
\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}(A)
).
Then the associated empirical measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{n_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\varepsilon}}\delta_{x_{i}}
converges tightly to the uniform probability density on
A

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

First we show (
6.10
) when
A=Q_{a}
for
a>0
. Observe that
n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{a})=S(Q_{\frac{a}{\varepsilon}})
while
S(Q_{k_{\varepsilon}})\leq S(Q_{\frac{a}{\varepsilon}})\leq S(Q_{k_{\varepsilon}+1})
being
k_{\varepsilon}
the integer part of
a/\varepsilon
. Then the equality
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{a})=\gamma_{d}a^{d}
follows since, by (
6.7
), we have
S(Q_{k_{\varepsilon}})\sim\gamma_{d}\,k_{\varepsilon}^{-d}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

On the other hand, for
\varepsilon<\delta<a
, we have
n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{a-\delta})\leq{\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}}(Q_{a})\leq n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{a})
. Thus by applying the previous convergence to
Q_{a}
and
Q_{a-\delta}
and then sending
\delta\to 0
, we deduce that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}\,{\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}}(Q_{a})=\gamma_{d}a^{d}.

Let now
A
be a Borel set with non empty interior
\mathring{A}
such that
|\partial A|=0
. We consider the family of hypercubes
\mathcal{A}\vcentcolon=\left\{{Q(x,a)\;\colon x\in\overline{A},a>0}\right\}
being
Q(x,a)=x+Q_{a}
. As
\overline{A}
is compact, for every
\delta>0
we may find a finite subfamily
\{Q_{i}:i\in I\}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that
\overline{A}\subset\cup_{i\in I}Q_{i}
and
\sum_{i\in I}|Q_{i}|\leq|\overline{A}|+\delta
. By the subadditivity property of the set function
n_{\varepsilon}
and by using the first step, we get

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}n_{\varepsilon}(A)\leq\sum_{i\in I}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{i})\leq\gamma_{d}\sum_{i\in I}|Q_{i}|\leq\gamma_{d}(|\overline{A}|+\delta)=\gamma_{d}(|A|+\delta),

where we used also that
|\partial A|=0
; by sending
\delta\to 0
we get

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}n_{\varepsilon}(A)\leq\gamma_{d}|A|.

On the other hand by Besicovitch’s Covering Theorem (generalized by Morse to families of hypercubes, see for instance
[
21
]
), there exists a countable family
\left\{{Q_{n}}\right\}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that
Q_{n}\subset\mathring{A}
,
Q_{m}\cap Q_{n}=\emptyset
if
m\neq n
, with
\left|\mathring{A}\setminus\cup_{n}Q_{n}\right|=0.
By the superadditivity of the set function
{\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}}
and by applying first step to each
Q_{n}
, we deduce that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}{\tilde{n}}_{\varepsilon}(A)\geq\sum_{n}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{n})\geq\gamma_{d}\sum_{i\in I}|Q_{i}|\geq\gamma_{d}|\mathring{A}|\ .

Recalling that
\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}(A)\leq n_{\varepsilon}(A)
and that
\partial A
was assumed to be of vanishing Lebesque measure, we deduce
the converse inequality
\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{d}n_{\varepsilon}(A)\geq\gamma_{d}|A|,

whence (
6.10
).

Let us now prove the second assertion of
6.3
. Let
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
be an optimal subset of
A
associated with
n_{\varepsilon}(A)
. Up to a subsequence we may assume that
\mu_{\varepsilon}
converges tightly to a probability measure
\mu
supported on the compact set
\overline{A}
. For every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\delta>0
, by applying (
6.10
), we find that:

\mu(Q_{\delta}(x_{0}))\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\sharp(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap Q_{\delta}(x_{0}))}{n_{\varepsilon}(A)}\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{n_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\delta}(x_{0})\cap A)}{n_{\varepsilon}(A)}=\frac{|Q_{\delta}(x_{0})\cap A|}{|A|}.

Since
x_{0}
and
\delta
are arbitrary, we infer that
\mu
is an absolutely continuous measure with a density
\frac{d\mu}{dx}\leq\frac{1}{|A|}
a.e. in
\overline{A}
. Thus as
\mu(\overline{A})=1
(and
|\overline{A}|=|A|
), we conclude that
\mu
is the uniform probability density on
A
and it is the unique cluster point of
\mu_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
In view of the first equality in (
6.10
), the previous arguments work as well when substituting
n_{\varepsilon}
with
\tilde{n}_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Appendix A
Tools and notation from convex analysis

Let
X
be a topological vector space, and
f\colon X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
. The
lower semi-continuous envelope
of
f
, denoted
\overline{f}
is the greatest lower semi-continuous function below
f
,
i.e.
,

\overline{f}(x)=\sup\left\{{g(x)\;\colon g\leq f,g\ \text{ is l.s.c.}}\right\}=\inf_{x_{n}\to x}\{\liminf_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n})\}.

The
convex hull
of
f
, denoted by
\cl f
, is the largest convex lower semi-continuous function below
f
. It may be defined as the function whose epigraph is the closed convex hull of the epigraph of
f
in
X\times\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Notice that
f
is lower semi-continuous iff
\overline{f}=f
and that if
f
is convex then
\cl f=\overline{f}
.

The
Legendre-Fenchel conjugate
of
f
, denoted by
f^{*}
, is defined on
X^{*}
as

f^{*}(v)=\sup\left\{{\left\langle{v,x}\right\rangle-f(x)\;\colon x\in X}\right\},

while its biconjugate , denoted by
f^{**}
, is given by

f^{**}(x)=\sup\left\{{\left\langle{v,x}\right\rangle-f^{*}(v)\;\colon v\in X^{*}}\right\}.

We list some well-known properties (see for instance
[
3
,
27
]
)

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(a)&\text{$f^{*}$ is convex and lower semi-continuous;}\\
(b)&\text{ if $f\leq g$, then $f^{*}\geq g^{*}$; }\\
(c)&\text{if $\{f^{*}<+\infty\}$ is non empty, then $f^{**}=\cl f$ ;}\\
(d)&\text{$(\overline{f})^{*}=f^{*}$ and $(\overline{f})^{**}=f^{**}$;}\\
(e)&\text{if $f$ is convex, then $\overline{f}=f^{**}$.}\\
\end{cases}

Let
f\colon X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a convex l.s.c. function and
x_{0}\in X
such that
f(x_{0})<+\infty.

Then, for every
z\in X
, the map
t\mapsto\frac{1}{t}(f(x_{0}+tz)-f(x_{0}))
is non decreasing on
(0,+\infty)
. We define the
recession function
of
f
as follows:

f_{\infty}(z):=\sup_{t>0}\frac{f(x_{0}+tz)-f(x_{0})}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(x_{0}+tz)-f(x_{0})}{t}.

The next result shows that the definition above does not depend of
x_{0}
.

Lemma A.1
.

f_{\infty}
coincides with the support function of the subset
{\rm dom}\,f^{*}:=\{v\in X^{*}\;\colon f^{*}(v)<+\infty\}
, that is:

(A.2)

f_{\infty}(z)\ =\ \sup\{\left\langle{v,x}\right\rangle\;\colon v\in{\rm dom}\,f^{*}\}.

Accordingly
f_{\infty}
is convex l.s.c. and positively one-homogeneous on
X
.

Some basic results in
\Gamma
-convergence theory

For an extensive study we refer

. Recall that a sequence of functionals
(F_{n})
defined on a topological space
X
is said to be
\Gamma-
convergent to
F:X\mapsto(-\infty,+\infty]
if the two following conditions hold:

i)

(
\Gamma-\liminf
) If
x_{n}\to x
in
X
, then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(x_{n})\geq F(x)
.

ii)

(
\Gamma-\limsup
) For all
x\in X
, there is a recovering sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}\to x
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(x_{n})\leq F(x)
.

It is easy to show that if
F_{n}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F
, then
F
is l.s.c on
X
. Furhermore we have the following equivalence:

F_{N}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F\iff\overline{F_{n}}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F.

As a consequence, one can readily check that, if
(F_{n})
is monotone non decreasing, then we have

F_{N}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F
where
F=\sup_{n}\overline{F_{n}}.

In addition we will use the two following results:

Proposition B.1

(

13

)
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space,
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of equi-Lipschitz functionals on
X
. Then:

G_{n}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}G\iff G_{n}(x)\to G(x)\ ,\ \forall x\in X.

.

\sup F_{n}(\rho_{n})<+\infty\ \Rightarrow\ \sup_{n}\left\lVert{\rho_{n}}\right\rVert_{X^{*}}<+\infty.

F_{N}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F\quad\text{in $X^{*}$}\ \iff\ F_{N}^{*}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F^{*}\quad\text{in $X$}.

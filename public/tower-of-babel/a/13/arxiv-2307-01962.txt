Oriented spanning trees and stationary distribution of digraphs
Jiang Zhou, Changjiang Bu

Abstract
By using biclique partitions of digraphs, this paper gives reduction formulas for the number of oriented spanning trees, stationary distribution vector and Kemeny’s constant of digraphs. As applications, we give a method for enumerating spanning trees of undirected graphs by vertex degrees and biclique partitions. The biclique partition formula also extends the results of Knuth and Levine from line digraphs to general digraphs.

Oriented spanning trees of digraphs

A biclique is a bipartite digraph
Q
whose vertices can be partitioned into two parts
Q^{(1)}
and
Q^{(2)}
, and
E(Q)=\{ij:i\in Q^{(1)},j\in Q^{(2)}\}
. Let
\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}

\Omega(\varepsilon)

\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}

in

is

w_{Q_{i}Q_{j}}(\Omega(\varepsilon))=w(Q_{j})\sum_{u\in Q_{i}^{(2)}\cap Q_{j}^{(1)}}\frac{w_{u}(G)}{d_{u}(G)},

(3.1)

where
w(Q_{j})=\sum_{u\in Q_{j}^{(2)}}w_{u}(G)
. By Observation
1.4
, we know that the part (1) of the following theorem extends Theorem
1.3
to general digraphs.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}
be a biclique partition of a weighted digraph
G
whose weights are induced by indeterminates
\{w_{i}(G)\}_{i\in V(G)}
, and

for each

. Set

.

(1) For any
u\in V(G)
, we have

\displaystyle t_{u}(G)=\frac{w_{u}(G)\prod_{u\neq v\in V(G)}d_{v}(G)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}w(Q_{i})}\sum_{Q_{i}:u\in Q_{i}^{(2)}}t_{Q_{i}}(\Omega(\varepsilon)).

(2) For any
Q_{i}\in\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle t_{Q_{i}}(\Omega(\varepsilon))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}w(Q_{i})}{\prod_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}(G)}\sum_{u\in Q_{i}^{(1)}}t_{u}(G).

Proof.

For a biclique partition
\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}
in
G

R_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{|V(G)|\times r}
and
S_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times|V(G)|}

\displaystyle(R_{\varepsilon})_{uj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w(Q_{j})~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\in Q_{j}^{(1)},\\
0~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\notin Q_{j}^{(1)},\end{cases}

\displaystyle(S_{\varepsilon})_{iu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{u}(G)~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\in Q_{i}^{(2)},\\
0~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\notin Q_{i}^{(2)},\end{cases}

where
w(Q_{j})=\sum_{u\in Q_{j}^{(2)}}w_{u}(G)
. Let
H
be the bipartite weighted digraph with Laplacian matrix

\displaystyle L_{H}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{1}&-R_{\varepsilon}\\
-S_{\varepsilon}&D_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
D_{1}
is a diagonal matrix satisfying
(D_{1})_{uu}=d_{u}(G)

is a diagonal matrix satisfying

\displaystyle D_{1}-R_{\varepsilon}D_{2}^{-1}S_{\varepsilon}=L_{G},~D_{2}-S_{\varepsilon}D_{1}^{-1}R_{\varepsilon}=L_{\Omega(\varepsilon)}.

For any

and

2.9

t_{u}(H)=\det(L_{H}(u,u))=t_{u}(G)\prod_{i=1}^{r}w(Q_{i}),

(3.2)

t_{Q_{i}}(H)=\det(L_{H}(Q_{i},Q_{i}))=t_{Q_{i}}(\Omega(\varepsilon))\prod_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}(G).

(3.3)

Let
X
be the adjoint matrix of

. Then

XL_{H}=\det(L_{H})I=0.

(3.4)

By Lemma
2.5
, we get

(X)_{uv}=t_{v}(H)~~~~(u,v\in V(H)).

(3.5)

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(H)}t_{v}(H)(L_{H})_{vu}=d_{u}(G)t_{u}(H)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}t_{Q_{i}}(H)(S_{\varepsilon})_{iu}=0.

By (3.2) and (3.3) we get

\displaystyle d_{u}(G)t_{u}(G)\prod_{i=1}^{r}w(Q_{i})-w_{u}(G)\prod_{v\in V(G)}d_{v}(G)\sum_{Q_{i}:u\in Q_{i}^{(2)}}t_{Q_{i}}(\Omega(\varepsilon))=0.

\displaystyle t_{u}(G)=\frac{w_{u}(G)\prod_{u\neq v\in V(G)}d_{v}(G)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}w(Q_{i})}\sum_{Q_{i}:u\in Q_{i}^{(2)}}t_{Q_{i}}(\Omega(\varepsilon)).

By (3.4) and (3.5) we have

\displaystyle w(Q_{i})t_{Q_{i}}(\Omega(\varepsilon))\prod_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}(G)-w(Q_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{r}w(Q_{j})\sum_{u\in Q_{i}^{(1)}}t_{u}(G)=0.

Let
\pi(G)
denote the stationary distribution vector of a digraph
G
. By using biclique partitions of digraphs, we give the following reduction formulas for stationary distribution vector and Kemeny’s constant of digraphs.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
G
be a strongly connected digraph with
n
vertices. For any
u\in V(G)
and biclique partition
\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}
of
G
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{u}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Q_{i}:u\in Q_{i}^{(2)}}|Q_{i}^{(2)}|^{-1}\pi_{Q_{i}}(\Theta(\varepsilon)),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\Theta(\varepsilon))+n-r.

Proof.

For a biclique partition
\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}
in
G
, let
R_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
and
S_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}
be two corresponding incidence matrices with entries

\displaystyle(R_{\varepsilon})_{uj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d_{u}^{+}(G)^{-1}|Q_{j}^{(2)}|~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\in Q_{j}^{(1)},\\
0~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\notin Q_{j}^{(1)}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle(S_{\varepsilon})_{iu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|Q_{i}^{(2)}|^{-1}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\in Q_{i}^{(2)},\\
0~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{if}~u\in V(G),u\notin Q_{i}^{(2)}.\end{cases}

By computation, we have

\displaystyle R_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}=P_{G},~S_{\varepsilon}R_{\varepsilon}=P_{\Theta(\varepsilon)}.

Suppose that
P_{\Theta(\varepsilon)}=S_{\varepsilon}R_{\varepsilon}
has eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}=1,\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r}
, then
P_{G}=R_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}
has eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r},0,\ldots,0
. By Lemma
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(G)=\frac{n-r}{1-0}+\sum_{i=2}^{r}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{i}}=\mathcal{K}(\Theta(\varepsilon))+n-r.

The products of all nonzero eigenvalues of
I-P_{G}
and
I-P_{\Theta(\varepsilon)}
are both

\prod_{i=2}^{r}(1-\lambda_{i})=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\det(I-P_{G}(v,v))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\det(I-P_{\Theta(\varepsilon)}(Q_{i},Q_{i})).

(4.1)

Let
H
be the bipartite weighted digraph with Laplacian matrix

\displaystyle L_{H}=\begin{pmatrix}I&-R_{\varepsilon}\\
-S_{\varepsilon}&I\end{pmatrix}.

For any
u\in V(G)
and
Q_{i}\in\varepsilon
, by Lemmas
2.5
and
2.9
, we have

t_{u}(H)=\det(I-P_{G}(u,u)),~t_{Q_{i}}(H)=\det(I-P_{\Theta(\varepsilon)}(Q_{i},Q_{i})).

(4.2)

Let
X
be the adjoint matrix of
L_{H}
. Then

\displaystyle XL_{H}=\det(L_{H})I=0.

By Lemma
2.5
, we get
(X)_{ij}=t_{j}(H)
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(H)}t_{v}(H)(L_{H})_{vu}=t_{u}(H)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}t_{Q_{i}}(H)(S_{\varepsilon})_{iu}=0.

By (4.2) we get

\displaystyle\det(I-P_{G}(u,u))=\sum_{Q_{i}:u\in Q_{i}^{(2)}}|Q_{i}^{(2)}|^{-1}\det(I-P_{\Theta(\varepsilon)}(Q_{i},Q_{i})).

By Lemma
2.7
and (4.1) we get

\displaystyle\pi_{u}(G)=\sum_{Q_{i}:u\in Q_{i}^{(2)}}|Q_{i}^{(2)}|^{-1}\pi_{Q_{i}}(\Theta(\varepsilon)).

∎

Example 4.15
.

For a digraph
G
with vertex set
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, its
k
-blow up
G(k)
has vertex set
V(G(k))=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{n}
and edge set
E(G(k))=\bigcup_{ij\in E(G)}\{uv:u\in V_{i},v\in V_{j}\}
, where
|V_{1}|=\cdots=|V_{n}|=k
. For a biclique partition
\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}
of
G
,
\eta=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}
is a biclique partition of
G(k)
, where
P_{i}
is the
k
-blow up of
Q_{i}
. Notice that
w_{P_{i}P_{j}}(\Theta(\eta))=kw_{Q_{i}Q_{j}}(\Theta(\varepsilon))
if
Q_{i}^{(2)}\cap Q_{j}^{(1)}\neq\emptyset
. By Theorem
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{u}(G(k))=k^{nk-2}\prod_{v\in V(G)}d_{v}^{+}(G)^{k-1}\kappa_{u}(G)~(u\in V_{i}).

By Theorem
4.14
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{u}(G(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{-1}\pi_{i}(G)~(u\in V_{i}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(G(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(G)+n(k-1).

By Observation
1.4
and Theorem
4.14
, we get the following formulas for stationary distribution vector and Kemeny’s constant of line digraphs.

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
G
be a strongly connected digraph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. For any
e=ij\in E(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{e}(\mathcal{L}(G))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{i}^{+}(G)^{-1}\pi_{i}(G),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}(G))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(G)+m-n.

Take
\mathcal{L}^{0}(G)=G
, and the iterated line digraph
\mathcal{L}^{s}(G)=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{L}^{s-1}(G))
(
s=1,2,3,\ldots
). We can get the following formula involving iterated line digraph from Corollary
4.16
.

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
G
be a strongly connected digraph with
n
vertices. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}^{s}(G))=\mathcal{K}(G)+|V(\mathcal{L}^{s}(G))|-n.

5
Concluding remarks

Let
G
be a weighted digraph with a partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, and let
L_{G}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}&-B\\
-C&L_{2}\end{pmatrix}
, where
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are principal submatrices of
L_{G}
corresponding to
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, respectively. If
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are nonsingular, then
S_{1}=L_{1}-BL_{2}^{-1}C
and
S_{2}=L_{2}-CL_{1}^{-1}B
are Laplacian matrices of some weighted digraphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively (because
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are square matrices whose all row sums are zeros). Similar with the proof of Theorem
3.10
, we can derive the following more general spanning tree identity

\displaystyle d_{u}(G)t_{u}(G_{1})-\sum_{v\in V_{1},vu\in E(G)}w_{vu}(G)t_{v}(G_{1})=\frac{\det(L_{1})}{\det(L_{2})}\sum_{v\in V_{2},vu\in E(G)}w_{vu}(G)t_{v}(G_{2}).

We can also obtain new reduction formula for counting spanning trees in undirected graphs from our results. For a connected undirected graph
H
, let
H_{0}
denote the digraph obtained from
H
by replacing every edge
\{i,j\}\in E(H)
with two directed edges
ij
and
ji
. Then the number of spanning trees in
H
is equal to
\kappa_{u}(H_{0})
for each
u\in V(H)
. For any biclique partition
\varepsilon=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}
of
H_{0}
, by Corollary
3.13
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{u}(H_{0})=\frac{\prod_{v\in V(H)}d_{v}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}|Q_{j}^{(2)}|}\frac{t_{Q_{i}}(\Omega(\varepsilon))}{|Q_{i}^{(1)}|},~i=1,\ldots,r,

where
d_{v}
is the degree of vertex
v
in
H
.

Amine Marrakchi, Mikael De La Salle

Abstract
. The aim of this note is to present a “type III” generalization of a distribution lemma of Connes. We then derive, following Vidick, consequences on infinite-dimensional quantum strategies for non-local games.



Non-local games are the central objects in the recent solution to
Connes’ embedding problem
[
JNV+20
]
. In fact, all the games
considered there have the additional property of being synchronous. Let us recall the vocabulary.

A
synchronous game

[
HMPS19
]
is a tuple
\mathcal{G}=(X,\nu,A,D)
where
X
and
A
are finite sets,
\nu
is a symmetric probability measure on
X\times X
and
D\colon X\times X\times A\times A\to\{0,1\}
is a symmetric function satisfying
D(x,x,a,b)=1_{a=b}
for every
x\in X
and
a,b\in A
. If
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we say that the synchronous game
\mathcal{G}
is
\alpha
-synchronous
if
\nu(x,x)\geq\alpha\sum_{y}\nu(x,y)
for every
x\in X
.

A
commuting strategy
for
\mathcal{G}
is a family of probability
distributions on
A\times A
of the form
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}(a,b)=\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle
where
\xi
is a unit vector in a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
(p^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}
and
(\tilde{p}^{y}_{b})_{b\in A}
are partitions of unity (PVM) on
\mathcal{H}
such that

[p^{x}_{a},\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}]=0
for every
x,y,a,b
. A
synchronous
strategy

[
PSS+16
]
for
\mathcal{G}
is a commuting strategy such that

\mathcal{P}_{x,x}(a,b)=0
for every
x\in X
and
a\neq b
. The

value
of the strategy
\mathcal{P}
at the game
\mathcal{G}
is

\val(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{P})=\int\sum_{a,b}D(x,y,a,b)\mathcal{P}_{x,y}(a,b)d\nu(x,y).

So, given a synchronous game, synchronous strategies are those
strategies that maximize

\int_{X}\sum_{a,b}D(x,y,a,b)\mathcal{P}_{x,y}(a,b)d\nu(x,x)=\int_{X}\sum_{a}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}(a,a)d\nu(x,x).

It is therefore natural to expect that synchronous games
that have a value close to
1
have a synchronous value close to

1
. The next result confirms this expectation.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
\varepsilon,\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\mathcal{G}
be an
\alpha
-synchronous game admitting a commuting strategy with value
\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Then
\mathcal{G}
admits a synchronous strategy with value
\geq 1-c(\varepsilon/\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}}
.

When only finite-dimensionnal Hilbert spaces are allowed,
Theorem
0.1
was proved by Vidick

[
Vid22
]
. The generalization to infinite dimensions has also
independently been obtained by Lin
[
Lin23
]
by a
different route.

Observe (see
[
PSS+16
, Theorem 5.5]
) that synchronous
strategies are exactly the strategies of the form
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}(a,b)=\tau(p^{x}_{a}p^{y}_{b})
for a family of partitions of unity
(p^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}
in a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
with a tracial state

\tau
. Therefore, the elementary statement of
Theorem
0.1
can be equivalently stated in von
Neumann algebraic language as follows.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
\varepsilon,\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\mathcal{G}
be an
\alpha
-synchronous game. Assume that there is a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
, a unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
a two families of partitions of the unity
(p^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset\mathcal{M}
and
(\tilde{p}^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
such that

\int\sum_{a,b}D(x,y,a,b)\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle d\nu(x,y)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Then there is a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
with a tracial state
\tau
and a family of partitions of unity
(r^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset\mathcal{N}
such that

\int\sum_{a,b}D(x,y,a,b)\tau(r^{x}_{a}r^{y}_{b})d\nu(x,y)\geq 1-c(\varepsilon/\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

What is amusing to notice is that Theorem
0.1
is very
elementary to state, but we do not see any elementary proof of
it. Both Lin’s and our proofs rely on quite advanced von Neumann
algebra techniques (the Connes-Tomita-Takesaki theory). More precisely
our proof relies on a new “type III” generalization of a
distribution lemma of Connes
[
Con76
]
. We believe that this
result (Proposition
1.3
) is of independent
interest. Lin does not rely on our type III generalization of Connes’ Lemma, but shows that arbitrary commuting correlations can be approximated by correlations coming from finite von Neumann algebras, and then runs Vidick’s proof in the tracial setting. Our proof has the feature that the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
is completely explicit :
\mathcal{M}
can be taken as a finite corner of the
core
of
\mathcal{M}
appearing in the Connes-Tomita-Takesaki theory (as the crossed product of
\mathcal{M}
by its modular flow), see Corollary
3.1
. In the particular case when
\mathcal{M}
is semi-finite,
\mathcal{N}
can even be taken as a finite corner of
\mathcal{M}
, see Corollary
3.2
.

1.
Connes’ lemma

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra. There is a unique (up to unique isomorphism) tuple
(c(\mathcal{M}),\tau,\theta,\iota)
of a von Neumann algebra
c(\mathcal{M})
with a faithful semifinite trace
\tau
, and continuous group homomorphism
\theta\colon\mathbf{R}\to\Aut(\mathcal{M})
and an embedding
\iota\colon\mathcal{M}\to c(\mathcal{M})
satisfying the conditions
\iota(\mathcal{M})=\{x\in c(\mathcal{M})\mid\theta_{s}(x)=x\forall s\in\mathbf{R}\}
and

(1.1)

\tau\circ\theta_{s}=e^{-s}\tau\forall s\in\mathbf{R}.

It is called the
core
of
\mathcal{M}
. See
[
FT01
, Theorem 3.3, 3.4 and 3.5]
or
[
Tak03
, Theorem 6.11]
.

Example 1.1
.

If
\mathcal{M}=\mathbf{C}
,
c(\mathcal{M})
can be realized as
L_{\infty}(\mathbf{R})
with translation action
\theta^{\mathbf{C}}_{s}f=f(\cdot-s)
and the trace
\tau^{\mathbf{C}}(f)=\int f(s)e^{-s}ds
. If
\mathcal{M}
carries a semifinite trace
\tau_{0}
, then
c(\mathcal{M})=c(\mathbf{C})\otimes\mathcal{M}
with the natural action
\theta^{\mathbf{C}}_{s}\otimes 1
and trace
\tau^{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\tau_{0}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is not semifinite, the construction is much more difficult, it is the accomplishment of the Tomita-Takesaki theory.

Haagerup’s
non-commutative
L_{p}
space

L_{p}(\mathcal{M})

[
Haa79
]
is the space of
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
c(\mathcal{M})
and satisfying
\theta_{s}(x)=e^{-\frac{s}{p}}x
for all
s
. Then for
1\leq p<\infty
,
\|x\|_{p}:=\tau(\chi_{(1,\infty)}(|x|))^{\frac{1}{p}}
is a complete norm on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, and
(L_{2}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{2})
is a Hilbert space.

The fundamental fact due to Haagerup that we will use is that the non-commutative
L_{1}
space
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
is naturally identified with
\mathcal{M}_{*}
. Explicitely, the set of positive elements
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
is in bijection with the positive elements
x\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
, the bijection being characterized by

\varphi\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}}\theta_{s}(z)ds\Big)=\tau(zx)

for every positive
z\in c(\mathcal{M})
. We write
\varphi(1)=\tr(x)
, and the linear map
\tr
extends to a linear map
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathbf{R}
that satisfies
\tr(xy)=\tr(yx)
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,
y\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})
. We will make several uses of the following lemma.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. If
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
and
y\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})
are positive, then

\tr(xy)=\tau(x^{1-p}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(x)y).

Proof.

Define
z=x^{-p}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(x)
with the convention
0^{-p}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(0)=0
. Then we have
\int_{\mathbf{R}}\theta_{s}(z)=\supp(x)
(see the proof of
[
Ter81
, Lemma 5]
for the details). By the definition of
\tr
, we deduce

\tau(zx^{\frac{1}{2}}yx^{\frac{1}{2}})=\tr(x^{\frac{1}{2}}yx^{\frac{1}{2}}).

The lemma follows by the trace property.
∎

A particular case of this lemma is that for
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,
\|x\|_{p}=(\tr(|x|^{p}))^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

We warn the reader that they are two different non-commutative
L_{p}
spaces involved: Haagerup’s
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
that we have just defined, and the tracial space
L_{p}(c(\mathcal{M}),\tau)
, which is the space of operators affiliated with
c(\mathcal{M})
and satisfying
\tau(|x|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty
. They are both made of operators affiliated with
c(\mathcal{M})
(but we have
L_{p}(c(\mathcal{M}),\tau)\cap L_{p}(\mathcal{M})=\{0\}
). To avoid confusion, we will write
\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}
for the first, and
\|x\|_{L_{p}(\tau)}
for the second.

Our goal is precisely to compare these two non-commutative
L_{p}

spaces for
p=2
(this can be combined with results from

[
Ric15
]
to obtain similar inequalities for other values of

p
). This generalizes
[
Con76
, Lemma 1.2.6]
to non-semifinite
von Neumann algebras.

Proposition 1.3
.

For every
x,y\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})^{+}
,

\|x-y\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\leq\|\chi_{(1,\infty)}(x)-\chi_{(1,\infty)}(y)\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}\leq\|x-y\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\|x+y\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}

A moment of thoughts and a look at Example
1.1
reveals for, for
\mathcal{M}
semi-finite, Proposition
1.3
is indeed an equivalent form of
[
Con76
, Lemma 1.2.6]
.

For the proof, we start with the following key observation of Connes
[
Con76
, Proposition 1.1]
that gives the existence of a joint spectral measure for two positive operators in a tracial von Neumann algebra.

Proposition 1.4

(Connes)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a normal semifinite trace
\tau
. Let
x
and
y
be two unbounded positive operators affilitated with
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\nu_{x}:A\mapsto\tau(\chi_{A}(x))
and
\nu_{y}:B\mapsto\tau(\chi_{B}(y))
be the spectral measures of
x
and
y
respectively. Suppose that
\nu_{x}|_{A_{0}}
and
\nu_{y}|_{B_{0}}
are
\sigma
-finite for some Borel subsets
A_{0},B_{0}\subset\mathbf{R}_{+}
. Then there exists a unique
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu
on
(A_{0}\times\mathbf{R}_{+})\cup(\mathbf{R}_{+}\times B_{0})
such that

\tau(\chi_{A}(x)\chi_{B}(y))=\nu(A\times B)

for all Borel sets
A,B\subset\mathbf{R}_{+}
such that
A\subset A_{0}
or
B\subset B_{0}
.

Then for every pair of Borel functions
f,g:\mathbf{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
such that
f
is supported on
A_{0}
or
g
is supported on
B_{0}
, we have

\tau(f(x)g(y))=\int_{\mathbf{R}_{+}^{2}}f(u)g(v)d\nu(u,v).

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
x,y
be a pair of positive elements of
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
. There exists a unique finite measure
\mu=\mu_{x,y}
on
[0,1]
such that for every pair of Borel functions
f,g:\mathbf{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
satisfying
f(0)g(0)=0
, we have

\tau(f(x)g(y))=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}\right)g\left(\frac{1-\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}\right)drd\mu(\lambda).

Moreover, we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\lambda^{2}d\mu(\lambda),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|y\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int(1-\lambda)^{2}d\mu(\lambda),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{(1,\infty)}(x)-\chi_{(1,\infty)}(y)\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int|2\lambda-1|d\mu(\lambda)

(1.5)

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\int\lambda(1-\lambda)d\mu(\lambda).

Proof.

We apply Proposition
1.4
. Note that the spectral measures
\nu_{x}
and
\nu_{y}
are finite on
[\varepsilon,+\infty)
for every
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, there are
\sigma
-finite on
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{*}
(but not necessarily on
\mathbf{R}_{+}
since we have
\nu_{x}(\{0\})=\infty
if
\supp(x)\neq 1
). Therefore, by Proposition
1.4
, there is a unique
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu
on
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
such that

(1.6)

\tau(f(x)g(y))=\int f(u)g(v)d\nu(u,v)

for every pair of Borel functions
f,g:\mathbf{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
such that
f(0)g(0)=0
. In fact
\nu
is even a Radon measure on the locally compact space
\mathbf{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
since it is finite on
([\varepsilon,+\infty)\times\mathbf{R}_{+})\cup(\mathbf{R}_{+}\times[\varepsilon,+\infty))
for every
\varepsilon>0
.

By (
1.1
), we have

e^{-2s}\tau(f(x)g(y))=\tau(\theta_{2s}(f(x)g(y)))=\tau(f(e^{-s}x)g(e^{-s}y)

Since
\nu
is uniquely characterized by
1.6
, we get
d\nu(e^{s}u,e^{s}v)=e^{-2s}d\nu(u,v)
for all
s\in\mathbf{R}
.
Let
\rho
be the Radon measure on
[0,1]\times\mathbf{R}^{*}_{+}
obtained as the pull-back of
\nu
by the homeomorphism

[0,1]\times\mathbf{R}^{*}_{+}\ni(\lambda,r)\mapsto\left(\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{r}},\frac{1-\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

Then we have
d\rho(\lambda,e^{s}r)=e^{s}d\rho(\lambda,r)
. Therefore,
\rho
must be of the form
\rho=\mu\otimes dr
for some finite measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
. Then a change of variable in equation
1.6
allows us to conclude that

\tau(f(x)g(y))=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}\right)g\left(\frac{1-\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}\right)drd\mu(\lambda)

for every pair of Borel functions
f,g:\mathbf{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
satisfying
f(0)g(0)=0
.

Now, we have to prove the second part. The equations (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) are immediate. For example, taking
f=\chi_{(1,\infty)}
and
g=1
, we obtain

\|x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}=\tau(\chi_{(1,\infty)}(x))=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{r<\lambda^{2}}drd\mu(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{2}d\mu(\lambda).

This proves (
1.2
). The equality (
1.3
) is proved in the same way. For (
1.4
), take
f=g=\chi_{(1,\infty)}
:

\displaystyle\|\chi_{(1,\infty)}(x)-\chi_{(1,\infty)}(y)\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\int_{0}^{\infty}|1_{r<\lambda^{2}}-1_{r<(1-\lambda)^{2}}|^{2}drd\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|\lambda^{2}-(1-\lambda)^{2}|d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|2\lambda-1|d\mu(\lambda).

The last equality (
1.5
) is slightly more involved. By Lemma
1.2
, if
f(t)=t^{-1}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)
, we obtain

\langle x,y\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}=\tr(xy)=\tau(f(x)y).

This is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}\right)\frac{1-\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{r}}{\lambda}\frac{1-\lambda}{\sqrt{r}}drd\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda(1-\lambda)d\mu(\lambda).\qed

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

By Proposition
1.5
,

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}+\|y\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\int(\lambda^{2}+(1-\lambda)^{2}-2\lambda(1-\lambda))d\mu(\lambda)=\int(1-2\lambda)^{2}d\mu(\lambda).

In the same way,

\|x+y\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}=\int 1d\mu(\lambda).

So the claimed inequality is equivalent to

\int(1-2\lambda)^{2}d\mu(\lambda)\leq\int|2\lambda-1|d\mu(\lambda)\leq\sqrt{\int(1-2\lambda)^{2}d\mu(\lambda)\int 1d\mu(\lambda)}.

This first inequality is clear because
|1-2\lambda|\leq 1
on
[0,1]
. The second inequality is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

A particular case of Proposition
1.3
is the following. Here and in the whole note, we write
[x,y]
for the commutator
xy-yx
. Also, a
partition of the unity
or
PVM
in
\mathcal{M}
is a finite family
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
of self-adjoint projections that sum to
1
. A finite family
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
of positive operators that sum to
1
is called a
POVM
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
x\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})^{+}
of norm
1
, and
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\subset\mathcal{M}
be a partition of the unity. Let
q=\chi_{(1,\infty)}(x)
. We have

\sum_{k}\|[p_{k},x]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\leq\sum_{k}\|[p_{k},q]\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}\leq 2\|x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\Big(\sum_{k}\|[p_{k},x]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
U=\sum_{k}e^{\frac{2i\pi k}{n}}p_{k}\in U(\mathcal{M})
. Then by the orthogonality of the characters of
\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|U^{k}xU^{-k}-x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|[U^{k},x]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}=\sum_{k}\|[p_{k},x]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}.

and similarly

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|U^{k}qU^{-k}-q\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}=\sum_{k}\|[p_{k},q]\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}.

So the corollary follows from Proposition
1.3
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, because

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|U^{k}xU^{-k}+x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2\|x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}.

∎

2.
Applications to correlations

Let us introduce some notation. If
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
is a von Neumann algebra and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is a unit vector, define
q_{\xi}\in c(\mathcal{M})
by
q_{\xi}=\chi_{(1,\infty)}(h_{\xi})
, where
h_{\xi}\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
is the element corresponding to the the vector state
\langle\cdot\xi,\xi\rangle
on
\mathcal{M}
through the isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{*}
and
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
described previously. It is a trace
1
projection, because
\tau(q_{\xi})=\tr(h_{\xi})=\langle 1\xi,\xi\rangle=1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra,
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be a unit vector.

Let
X,A
be finite sets,
\nu
be a symmetric probability measure on
X\times X
with marginal
\mu
. For every
x\in X
, let
(p^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset\mathcal{M}
and
(\tilde{p}^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be partitions of the unity.

If
\int\sum_{a\in A}\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{x}_{a}\xi,\xi\rangle d\mu(x)\geq 1-\delta
, then

\int\sum_{a,b\in A}\Big|\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle-\tau(q_{\xi}p^{x}_{a}q_{\xi}p^{y}_{b}q_{\xi})\Big|d\nu(x,y)\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Moreover, for every
x\in X
there is a partition of the unity
(r^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset q_{\xi}c(\mathcal{M})q_{\xi}
such that

\int\sum_{a,b\in A}\Big|\tr(p^{x}_{a}h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h^{\frac{1}{2}})-\tau(r^{x}_{a}r^{y}_{b})\Big|d\nu(x,y)\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Given Theorem
1.3
, the proof is essentially the same as the proof of
[
Vid22
, Theorem 3.1]
, but we write the proof for completeness.

We shall use the following elementary Hilbert space lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a Hilbert space,
(\Omega,m)
be a measure space and
\xi,\eta\in L_{2}(\Omega,m;\mathcal{K})
. Then

\int\big|\|\xi(\omega)\|^{2}-\|\eta(\omega)\|^{2}\big|dm(\omega)\leq\|\xi-\eta\|\|\xi+\eta\|.

Proof.

For any vectors in a Hilbert space, the inequality

\big|\|u\|^{2}-\|v\|^{2}\big|\leq\|u-v\|\|u+v\|

is easy: squaring both sides, it is equivalent to

\|u\|^{4}+\|v\|^{4}-2\|u\|^{2}\|v\|^{2}\leq\|u\|^{4}+\|v\|^{4}+2\|u\|^{2}\|v\|^{2}-4|\Re\langle u,v\rangle|^{2},

which is just the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Taking
u=\xi(\omega)
and
v=\eta(\omega)
, integrating and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
L_{2}(\Omega,m)
, we deduce the lemma. ∎

The next lemma is the particular case of Theorem
2.1
for strategies that are symmetric.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra,
h\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})_{+}

1
, and
q=\chi_{(1,\infty)}(h)\in L_{2}(c(\mathcal{M}))

Let
X,A,\nu,\mu
be as in Theorem
2.1
and, for every
x\in X

(p^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset\mathcal{M}

If
\int\sum_{a\in A}\|[p^{x}_{a},h^{\frac{1}{2}}]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x)\leq\delta

\int\sum_{a,b\in A}\Big|\tr(p^{x}_{a}h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h^{\frac{1}{2}})-\tau(qp^{x}_{a}qp^{y}_{b}q)\Big|d\nu(x,y)\leq 4\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Moreover, for every
x\in X

(r^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset qc(\mathcal{M})q
such that

\int\sum_{a,b\in A}\Big|\tr(p^{x}_{a}h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h^{\frac{1}{2}})-\tau(r^{x}_{a}r^{y}_{b})\Big|d\nu(x,y)\leq 38\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Proof.

Let
\Omega=X\times X\times A\times A
and
m
the product of the measure
\nu
and the counting measure on
A\times A
. Consider the norm
1
elements
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}
of
L_{1}(\Omega,m)
defined by

\displaystyle C_{1}(x,y,a,b)

\displaystyle=\tr(h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{x}_{a})=\|p^{x}_{a}h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2},

\displaystyle C_{2}(x,y,a,b)

\displaystyle=\tr(hp^{x}_{a}p^{y}_{b}p^{x}_{a})=\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{x}_{a}p^{y}_{b}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2},

\displaystyle C_{3}(x,y,a,b)

\displaystyle=\tau(qp^{x}_{a}p^{y}_{b}p^{x}_{a})=\|qp^{x}_{a}p^{y}_{b}\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2},

\displaystyle C_{4}(x,y,a,b)

\displaystyle=\tau(qp^{y}_{b}qp^{x}_{a}q)=\|p^{x}_{a}qp^{y}_{b}\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}.

We have
C_{2}=C_{3}
by Lemma
1.2
. Moreover, by
Lemma
2.2
, we can bound

\displaystyle\|C_{1}-C_{2}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{4\int\sum_{a,b}\|[p^{x}_{a},h^{\frac{1}{2}}]p^{y}_{b}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\nu(x,y)}

\displaystyle={4\int\sum_{a}\|[p^{x}_{a},h^{\frac{1}{2}}]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x)}\leq{4\delta}.

In the same way, by Lemma
2.2
and Corollary
1.6
, we obtain

(2.1)

\|C_{3}-C_{4}\|_{1}^{2}\leq 4\int\sum_{a}\|[p^{x}_{a},q]\|_{L_{2}(\tau)}^{2}d\mu(x)\leq 8\sqrt{\delta}.

We deduce

\|C_{1}-C_{4}\|_{1}=\|C_{1}-C_{2}+C_{3}-C_{4}\|_{1}\leq 2\sqrt{\delta}+2\sqrt{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}.

We obtain

\|C_{1}-C_{4}\|_{1}\leq\min(2,2\sqrt{\delta}+2\sqrt{\sqrt{2\delta}})\leq 4\delta^{\frac{1}{4}},

which is precisely the first conclusion of the lemma.

For the second conclusion, we use an orthogonalization result for POVMs that originates from the work of Kempe and Vidick
[
KV11
]
. We use a form that applies to infinite dimensional space and with the right dependance from
[
dlS22
]
. We can rewrite the second inequality in (
2.1
) as

\int\sum_{a}\tau((qp^{x}_{a}q)^{2})d\mu(x)\geq 1-8\sqrt{\delta},

so by
[
dlS22
, Theorem 1.2]
for every
x\in X
, there is a partition of the unity
(r^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}
in
qc(\mathcal{M})q
such that

\sum_{a}\tau(|qp^{x}_{a}q-r^{x}_{a}|^{2})\leq 9(1-\tau((qp^{x}_{a}q)^{2})).

As a consequence,

\int\sum_{a}\tau(|qp^{x}_{a}q-r^{x}_{a}|^{2})d\mu(x)\leq 72\sqrt{\delta}.

Using twice Lemma
2.2
gives

\int\sum_{a,b}|\tau(qp^{y}_{b}qp^{x}_{a}q)-\tau(r^{y}_{b}r^{x}_{a})|d\nu(x,y)\leq 4\sqrt{72\sqrt{\delta}}\leq 34\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}.\qed

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The idea is simple: the assumption of the theorem implies that the correlation
\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle
is close to the symmetric correlation
\tr(p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}})
, so we can apply Lemma
2.3
. We proceed in several steps.

We first observe that for every
y
there is a POVM
(p^{\prime y}_{b})_{b\in A}\subset\mathcal{M}
such that for every
x,a,b
,

(2.2)

\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle=\tr(p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{\prime y}_{b}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle.

This is a standard fact from von Neumann algebras, that we recall for completeness. The map
\langle\cdot\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle
on
\mathcal{M}
is a positive element of
\mathcal{M}_{*}
, so there is a unique positive
h(\xi,y,b)\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})_{*}
such that
\langle\cdot\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle=\tr(\cdot h(\xi,y,b))
. The fact that
\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\leq 1
implies that
0\leq h(\xi,y,b)\leq h_{\xi}
, that is there is a unique
p^{\prime y}_{b}\in c(\mathcal{M})
such that
0\leq p^{\prime y}_{b}\leq\supp(h_{\xi})
and
h(\xi,y,b)=h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{\prime y}_{b}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We have
\theta_{s}(p^{\prime y}_{b})=p^{\prime y}_{b}
, so
p^{\prime y}_{b}\in\mathcal{M}
. By uniqueness, we have
h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{b}p^{\prime y}_{b}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}=h_{\xi}
, that is
\sum_{b}p^{\prime y}_{b}=\supp(h_{\xi})
. Replacing
p^{\prime y}_{b}
by
p^{\prime y}_{b}+(1-\supp(h_{\xi}))
for some
b
turns
p^{\prime y}
to a POVM still satisfying (
2.2
).

We now claim that

(2.3)

\int\sum_{a}\|[p^{x}_{a},h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x)\leq 4\delta.

Before we justify this, observe that by Lemma
2.3
, this implies

(2.4)

\int\sum_{a,b\in A}\Big|\tr(p^{x}_{a}h^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h^{\frac{1}{2}})-\tau(q_{\xi}p^{x}_{a}q_{\xi}p^{y}_{b}q_{\xi})\Big|d\nu(x,y)\leq 4\sqrt{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}.

To prove (
2.3
), we expand

\int\sum_{a}\|[p^{x}_{a},h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x)=2-2\int\sum_{a}\|h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x).

By (
2.2
),
\int\sum_{a\in A}\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{x}_{a}\xi,\xi\rangle d\mu(x)
is the scalar product in
L_{2}(\mu;\ell_{2}(A;L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
of
(x,a)\mapsto h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}
with
(x,a)\mapsto h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}p^{\prime x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}
. These two vectors clearly have norm at most
1
, so by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and our assumption, they both have norm at least
1-\delta
. In particular,

\int\sum_{a}\|h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x)\geq(1-\delta)^{2}\geq 1-2\delta

and (
2.3
) follows.

The next observation is that

(2.5)

\int\sum_{a}\|h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}(p^{x}_{a}-(p^{\prime x}_{a})^{\frac{1}{2}})\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x)\leq 6\sqrt{\delta}.

Expanding the square, we can write the left-hand side of (
2.5
) as

\int\big(2-2\sum_{a}\tr(h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}(p^{\prime x}_{a})^{\frac{1}{2}})+2\Re\sum_{a}\tr(h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}[p^{x}_{a},h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}](p^{\prime x}_{a})^{\frac{1}{2}})\big)d\mu(x).

Using our assumption and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain that the left-hand side of (
2.5
) is less than

2\delta+2\Big(\int\sum_{a}\|[p^{x}_{a},h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}]\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2\delta+4\sqrt{\delta}.

The last inequality is by (
2.3
). This proves (
2.5
).

Now observe that
\tr(p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}})
is the norm of
p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}
in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Similarly, by (
2.2
),
\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle
is the norm of
p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}(p^{\prime y}_{b})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We can therefore apply Lemma
2.2
and obtain

\int\sum_{a,b}\Big|\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle-\tr(p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{y}_{b}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}})|d\nu(x,y)\leq 2\Big(\int\sum_{a,b}\|p^{x}_{a}h_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}(p^{y}_{b}-(p^{\prime y}_{b})^{\frac{1}{2}})\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}d\nu(x,y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is less than
3\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}
by (
2.5
). Together with (
2.4
), this concludes the proof of the first half of the theorem. The second half is immediate from the moreover part of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

3.
Application for strategies and games

We now prove the main results of the introduction, Theorem
0.1
and
0.2
. As announced, we prove a more precise form of them in Corollary
3.1
. Before, we introduce some notation. Given a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
, we denote by
\val_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{G})
the supremum of
\val(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{P})
over all commuting strategies with
p^{x}_{a}\in\mathcal{M}
and
\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.

If
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a von Neumann algebra with a normal tracial state
\tau
, the
tracial (or synchronous) value

\val_{\mathcal{M},\tau}^{tr}(\mathcal{G})
is the supremum of

\int\sum_{a,b}D(x,y,a,b)\tau(p^{x}_{a}p^{y}_{b}),

over all partitions of unity
(p^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset\mathcal{M}
.

Finally, if
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a von Neumann algebra with a semifinite normal trace, we denote by
\val_{\leq\mathcal{M},\tau}^{tr}(\mathcal{G})
the supremum of
\val_{q\mathcal{M}q,\frac{1}{\tau(q)}\tau}^{tr}(\mathcal{G})
over all nonzero projections
q\in\mathcal{M}
with finite trace.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
\alpha
-synchronous game and
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra. If
\val_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{G})\geq 1-\delta
, then there is a trace one projection
q\in c(\mathcal{M})
such that
\val_{qc(\mathcal{M})q,\tau}^{tr}(\mathcal{G})\geq 1-c(\delta/\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}}
for a universal constant
c
.

Proof.

Assume that
\val_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{G})>1-\delta
. This means that there is a commuting strategy
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{H},\xi,p,\tilde{p})
with
p\subset\mathcal{M}
and
\tilde{p}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
such that
\val(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{P})\geq 1-\delta
, that is

\int\sum_{a,b}(1-D(x,y,a,b))\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle d\nu(x,y)\leq\delta.

In particular, restricting the integral to the diagonal
\{(x,x)\mid x\in X\}
and using that
\mathcal{G}
is
\alpha
-synchronous, we obtain

\alpha\int\sum_{a\neq b}\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{x}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle d\mu(x)\leq\delta,

where
\mu
is the first marginal of
\nu
. Equivalently,

\int\sum_{a}\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{x}_{a}\xi,\xi\rangle d\mu(x)\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{\alpha}.

So it follows from Theorem
2.1
that the correlation
\langle p^{x}_{a}\tilde{p}^{y}_{b}\xi,\xi\rangle
is
O((\delta/\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}})
close in
L_{1}
-norm to the correlation
\tau(r^{x}_{a}r^{y}_{b})
. In particular, the value of the game
\mathcal{G}
at these strategies are
O((\delta/\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}})
-close, and the corollary follows.
∎

In the particular case when
\mathcal{M}
is semifinite, we obtain.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
\alpha
-synchronous game and
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful tracial state
\tau_{0}
. If
\val_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{G})\geq 1-\delta
, then
\val_{\leq\mathcal{M},\tau_{0}}^{\tr}(\mathcal{G})\geq 1-c(\delta/\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}}
.

This corollary is the combination of Corollary
3.1
with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be any symmetric game and
(\mathcal{M},\tau_{0})
be von Neumann with a normal faithful semifinite trace. Then
\val_{\leq\mathcal{M},\tau_{0}}^{\tr}(\mathcal{G})=\val_{\leq c(\mathcal{M}),\tau}^{\tr}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

By Example
1.1
,
(c(\mathcal{M}),\tau)
is isomorphic to
(L_{\infty}(\mathbf{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},e^{-s}ds\otimes\tau_{0})
, so a finite projection
q\in c(\mathcal{M})
corresponds to a measurable family
(q_{s})_{s\in\mathbf{R}}
of finite projections satisfying
\int\tau_{0}(q_{s})e^{-s}ds=\tau(q)
. Moreover, if
(r^{x}_{a})_{a\in A}\subset qc(\mathcal{M})q
is a family of PVMs, the correlation
\big(\frac{1}{\tau(q)}\tau(r^{x}_{a}r^{y}_{b})\big)_{x,y,a,b}
is a convex combinations of the correlations
\big(\frac{1}{\tau_{0}(q_{s})}\tau_{0}(r^{x}_{a}(s)r^{y}_{b}(s))\big)_{x,y,a,b}
. The lemma follows.
∎

When
\mathcal{M}=M_{d}(\mathbf{C})
, we recover Vidick’s theorem.

Corollary 3.4
.

[
Vid22
]
If an
\alpha
-synchronous game
\mathcal{G}
admits a commuting strategy with value
\geq 1-\delta
on a Hilbert space of finite dimension
\leq d^{2}
, then
\val_{\leq M_{d}(\mathbf{C}),Tr}^{\tr}(\mathcal{G})\geq 1-c(\delta/\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a strategy on
\mathcal{H}
of dimension
\leq d^{2}
with valued
\geq 1-\delta
. Consider the algebras
\mathcal{M}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
generated by
\{p^{x}_{a}\mid x\in X,a\in A\}
and
\{\tilde{p}^{x}_{a}\mid x\in X,a\in A\}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\dim\mathcal{M}\leq\dim\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. They are commuting sub-algebras of
B(\mathcal{H})
, so
\mathcal{M}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
embeds into
B(\mathcal{H})
. Taking dimensions, we obtain
(\dim\mathcal{M})^{2}\leq\dim\mathcal{M}\dim{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\leq d^{4}
. Therefore,
\dim\mathcal{M}\leq d^{2}
and
\dim\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of algebras
M_{k}(\mathbf{C})
with
k\leq d
and we deduce that
\val_{M_{k}(\mathbf{C})}(\mathcal{G})\geq 1-\delta
for some
k\leq d
. We conclude by Corollary
3.2
.
∎

Multiple Landau level filling for a large magnetic field limit of 2D fermions
Denis

Abstract




Abstract:

Motivated by the quantum hall effect, we study
N
two dimensional interacting fermions in a large magnetic field limit. We work in a bounded domain, ensuring finite degeneracy of the Landau levels. In our regime, several levels are fully filled and inert: the density in these levels is constant. We derive a limiting mean-field and semi classical description of the physics in the last, partially filled Landau level.

I.1 ModelModel

We consider a system of
N
interacting fermionic particles in two dimensions. They are placed under a homogeneous magnetic field perpendicular to the domain. In this context the kinetic energy of the particles is quantized into discrete energy levels called Landau levels, separated by a finite energy gap. This problem has initially been studied by Lieb Solovej and Yngvason in
[
LiebSolovejYngvason
]
,
[
LiebSolovej
]
,
[
LiebSolovejYngvasona
]
,
[
LiebSolovejYngvasonb
]
,
[
LiebSolovejYngvasonc
]
and more recently by Fournais, Lewin and Madsen in
[
FournaisMadsen
]
,
[
FournaisLewinSolovej
]
.

Our goal is to study the mean field and semi-classical limit under high magnetic field so the Landau level quantization plays an important role. This setup is related to that of
[
LiebSolovejYngvason
]
where three regimes are studied. In the first one, the energy gap is small with respect to the potential contributions in the energy so particles occupy all Landau levels and a standard Thomas–Fermi model is obtained in the limit. In the second one, the energy gap is comparable to the potential energy terms, particles optimise both their Landau level and their position in the potentials and the limit is a magnetic Thomas-Fermi model. For the last scaling, the gap is large compared to the potential energies so all particles occupy the lowest Landau level and the limit is described with a classical continuum electrostatic theory in this level. We want to deal with the intermediate situation where only a finite number of Landau levels are completely filled. Precisely, our result is a limit where the
q
first Landau Level are fully filled, the next Landau level is partially filled with filling ratio
r<1
and all higher Landau levels are empty. We also provide a model for the physics in the partially filled Landau level. This setup is physically motivated by the quantum Hall effect which mostly takes place in a partially filled Landau level while lower Landau levels are filled and inert, and higher levels are empty, see
[
Jain
]
.

In this perspective we want to fix the limit ratio of the number of particles to the degeneracy of Landau levels. On the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
this degeneracy is infinite. To ensure finiteness of the degeneracy of the Landau levels (see
section

II.3
), we work on a bounded domain. For simplicity, we would like to consider a torus with periodic boundary conditions. But, in the presence of a magnetic field the periodic boundary conditions must be modified. This is a well known issue, for example see
[

. As explained in
section

II.1

I.2 Semi-classical limit modelSemi-classical limit model

In the limit, we obtain a semi-classical model where the energy no longer depends on the wave-function but on the density in phase space. This comes with a non linearity in the interaction term. The phase space is
\mathbb{N}\times\Omega
. This means that particles have two degrees of freedom: the first one is
n\in\mathbb{N}
the quantum number representing the Landau Level index and
x\in\Omega
representing the position of particles in space. In classical mechanics, one can think of
x
as the center of the cyclotron orbit of the particles and
n
as the index of the quantized angular velocity of the cyclotron orbit. This model is semi-classical in the sense that the Pauli principle still holds as a bound on the density.

Notation I.3:

Semi-classical functional

\displaystyle\eta\coloneq\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\delta_{n}\right)\otimes\lambda_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{sc,\hbar b}\left[m\right]\coloneq\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{N}\times\Omega}E_{n}m(n,R)d\eta(n,R)+\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{N}\times\Omega}Vmd\eta+\int\displaylimits_{(\mathbb{N}\times\Omega)^{2}}wm^{\otimes 2}d\eta^{\otimes 2}

(13)

where, as we will see in

\displaystyle E_{n}\coloneq 2\hbar b\left(n+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{sc}\coloneq\left\{m\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{N}\times\Omega\right)\text{ such that }\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{N}\times\Omega}md\eta=1\text{ and }0\leq m\leq\dfrac{1}{(q+r)L^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle E_{sc,\hbar b}^{0}\coloneq\inf_{m\in\mathcal{D}_{sc}}\mathcal{E}_{sc,\hbar b}\left[m\right]

Notation I.4:

\displaystyle E_{qLL}^{0}\coloneq\inf_{\mathcal{D}_{qLL}}\mathcal{E}_{qLL}

\displaystyle E^{q,r}\coloneq\dfrac{q^{2}+2qr+r}{q+r}\qquad E_{V}^{q,r}\coloneq\dfrac{q}{(q+r)L^{2}}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}V\qquad E_{w}^{q,r}\coloneq\dfrac{q^{2}+2qr}{(q+r)^{2}L^{4}}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega^{2}}w

I.4 ScalingScaling

Another way to obtain the scaling in
section

I.1
is to observe that we have two characteristic length-scales:

•

\dfrac{L}{\sqrt{N}}
, measuring the mean distance between particles

•

l_{b}
, the magnetic length, which, in classical mechanics corresponds to the radius of a cyclotron orbit. Due to the Pauli principle,
l_{b}
will be the order of the minimum distance between particles inside a Landau level. More precisely the Pauli principle takes the form of an upper bound on the density in phase space.

The square ratio of these length is

\displaystyle\dfrac{L^{2}}{Nl_{b}^{2}}=\dfrac{bL^{2}}{\hbar N}

(24)

If this ratio goes to zero, the mean distance between particles is very small compared to the minimal length-scale between two particles in a fixed Landau level. This implies that the particles must fill many Landau levels and this corresponds to the scaling in
[
LiebSolovejYngvason
]
where the energy gap between Landau level is small compared to the potential terms.

If this ratio goes to infinity, the mean distance between particles is very large compared to the minimal length-scale between two particles in a fixed Landau Level. As a consequence, all particles can be placed in the lowest Landau level and this corresponds to the regime in
[
LiebSolovejYngvason
]
where particles only occupy the lowest Landau level and do not feel the Pauli principle.

In the limit we study, we see from
11
that the ratio
24
has been fixed to be

\displaystyle\dfrac{L^{2}}{Nl_{b}^{2}}\limit\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{2\pi}{q+r}

(25)

in order to fill a finite number of Landau levels. In our limit we fixed
L
and took
l_{b}
going to zero, but one can also ensure
25
by fixing a magnetic length
\widetilde{l_{b}}>0
and taking a domain length
\widetilde{L}
going to infinity as

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}\coloneq\dfrac{\widetilde{l_{b}}}{l_{b}}L

(26)

In this limit the density of particles in the domain
\Omega
is fixed:

\displaystyle\dfrac{\widetilde{L}^{2}}{N}\limit\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\widetilde{l_{b}}^{2}\dfrac{2\pi}{q+r}

(27)

Those limits are equivalent in the sense that the
N
-body Hamiltonian
6
is unitarily equivalent to

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\tau}\mathscr{H}_{N,\tau}\coloneq\dfrac{1}{\tau}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\left(i\hbar_{\tau}\nabla_{j}+b_{\tau}A_{\tau}(x_{j})\right)^{2}+V_{\tau}(x_{j})\right)+\dfrac{2}{N-1}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq N}w_{\tau}(x_{j}-x_{k})\right)

(28)

where

\displaystyle\hbar_{\tau}\coloneq\dfrac{\hbar}{\sqrt{\tau}}\qquad A_{\tau}\coloneq\dfrac{1}{\tau}A\left(\tau\bullet\right)\qquad b_{\tau}\coloneq\tau^{\frac{3}{2}}b\qquad V_{\tau}\coloneq\tau V\left(\tau\bullet\right)\qquad w_{\tau}\coloneq\tau w\left(\tau\bullet\right)

Taking,
\tau\coloneq L/\widetilde{L}
and using
28
, we confirm that the new magnetic length is

\displaystyle\sqrt{\dfrac{\hbar_{\tau}}{b_{\tau}}}=\dfrac{\widetilde{L}l_{b}}{L}=\widetilde{l_{b}}

Moreover if one chooses

\displaystyle A=A_{Lan}\qquad V(x)=\varrho*\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|}\qquad w(x)=\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|}

then the vector potential and the interaction potential are not rescaled :

\displaystyle A_{\tau}=A\qquad w_{\tau}=w

If we assume that the external potential is generated by a background charge density
\varrho\in L^{1}(\Omega)
it transforms as

\displaystyle V_{\tau}(x)=\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\tau\varrho(\tau x-y)\dfrac{1}{\left|y\right|}dy=\int\displaylimits_{\left[0,\widetilde{L}\right]^{2}}\tau^{2}\varrho(\tau\left(y-x\right))\dfrac{1}{\left|y\right|}dy\eqcolon\varrho_{\tau}*\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|}

The re-scaling preserves the total charge

\displaystyle\int\displaylimits_{\left[0,\widetilde{L}\right]^{2}}\varrho_{\tau}dx=\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\varrho dx

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{N,\tau}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\left(i\hbar_{\tau}\nabla_{j}+b_{\tau}A(x_{j})\right)^{2}+\rho_{\tau}*\dfrac{1}{\left|x\right|}\right)+\dfrac{2}{N-1}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq N}w(x_{j}-x_{k})

We conclude that our initial scaling is equivalent to a thermodynamic limit.

This subsection is devoted to the usual formalism for the description of the magnetic Laplacian in terms of annihilation and creation operators. More details about these operators and the properties of Landau levels can be found in

Notation II.2

We denote by
\pi_{x},\pi_{y}
the coordinates of the magnetic momentum:

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{\hbar,b}\eqcolon\left(\begin{matrix}i\hbar\partial_{x}+bA_{x}\\
i\hbar\partial_{y}+bA_{y}\end{matrix}\right)\eqcolon\left(\begin{matrix}\pi_{x}\\
\pi_{y}\end{matrix}\right)

and define the annihilation and creation operators respectively as

\displaystyle a\coloneq\dfrac{\pi_{y}-i\pi_{x}}{\sqrt{2\hbar b}}\hskip 28.45274pta^{\dagger}\coloneq\dfrac{\pi_{y}+i\pi_{x}}{\sqrt{2\hbar b}}

(35)

and the number of excitation operator
\mathcal{N}\coloneq a^{\dagger}a
.

The quantization of the magnetic Laplacian comes from the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle\left[\pi_{x},\pi_{y}\right]=i\hbar b

(36)

\displaystyle\left[a,a^{\dagger}\right]=\text{Id}\text{ (canonical commutation relation)}

\displaystyle\left[\tau_{z_{0}},a\right]=\left[\tau_{z_{0}},a^{\dagger}\right]=0

(37)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}=2\hbar b\left(\mathcal{N}+\dfrac{\text{Id}}{2}\right)

(38)

\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}
defines the Sobolev space
\left(\text{Dom}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}\right),\braket{\bullet}_{\mathscr{L}}\right)
with

\displaystyle\braket{\chi|\psi}_{\mathscr{L}}\coloneq\braket{\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}\chi|\psi}

which is equivalent to
(H^{2}_{mp}(\Omega),\braket{\bullet}_{H^{1}})
. The quadratic form defined by
\braket{\bullet}_{\mathscr{L}}
is continuous, Hermitian and coercive on
\text{Dom}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}\right)
. Thus
\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}
is a closed positive self-adjoint operator and the embedding

\displaystyle\text{Dom}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)

is continuous and compact.

H^{2}_{mp}(\Omega)
contains the smooth and compactly supported functions, so it is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We can conclude using the Lax-Milgram theorem
[
CheverryRaymond
, Corollary 4.26]
that the resolvent of
\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}
is well defined and compact. Applying the spectral theorem to the resolvent of
\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}
proves that its spectrum is punctual and
L^{2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbertian direct sum of eigenspaces of
\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}
. The same conclusions also holds for the
N
-body Hamiltonian

since the magnetic Laplacian is of dominant order in it.
\mathcal{N}
inherits the properties of
\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}
and it is well known that

\displaystyle\text{sp}(\mathcal{N})=\mathbb{N}

Lemma IV.2:

Periodic Kato inequality

Define the complex sign

\displaystyle s(u)\coloneq\begin{cases}\begin{matrix}\dfrac{\overline{u}}{\left|u\right|}&\text{ if }&u\neq 0\\
0&\text{ if }&u=0\end{matrix}\end{cases}

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
then
\left|u\right|\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\left|\hbar\nabla\left|u\right|\right|\leq\left|\mathscr{P}_{\hbar,b}u\right|

(62)

Moreover if
\left|u\right|
is periodic, then

\displaystyle-\hbar^{2}\Delta\left|u\right|\leq\text{Re}\left[s(u)\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}u\right]

(63)

in the weak sense on
C^{\infty}_{p}(\Omega)
, or equivalently,
\forall\varphi\in C_{p}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
,

\displaystyle-\hbar^{2}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|\Delta\varphi\leq\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\text{Re}\left[s(u)\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}u\right]\varphi

Proof
:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\hbar\nabla\left|u\right|^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\hbar\nabla(\overline{u}u)=\text{Re}\left[\overline{u}\hbar\nabla u\right]=\text{Re}\left[\overline{u}\left(\hbar\nabla-ibA\right)u\right]

so taking absolute values

\displaystyle\left|\hbar\dfrac{\nabla\left|u\right|^{2}}{2}\right|\leq\left|u\right|\left|\mathscr{P}_{\hbar,b}u\right|

(64)

Define

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}=\sqrt{\left|u\right|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}\in C^{\infty}_{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})

Using
64
,

\displaystyle\left|\hbar\nabla u_{\epsilon}\right|=\dfrac{\left|\hbar\nabla\left|u\right|^{2}\right|}{2u\epsilon}\leq\dfrac{\left|u\right|}{u_{\epsilon}}\left|\mathscr{P}_{\hbar,b}u\right|\leq\left|\mathscr{P}_{\hbar,b}u\right|

(65)

So
\nabla u_{\epsilon}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
and converges weakly to
v\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
up to sequence of
\epsilon
. Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\text{int}(\Omega),\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
, since
\varphi\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
and
0\leq u_{\epsilon}-\left|u\right|\leq\epsilon

\displaystyle\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}v\cdot\varphi=\lim\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\varphi=-\lim\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}u_{\epsilon}\nabla\cdot\varphi=-\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|\nabla\cdot\varphi

so

IV.3 Diamagnetic inequalityDiamagnetic inequality

The diamagnetic inequality in terms of Green functions allows us to restrict ourselves to the non magnetic case.

Notation IV.3:

Green functions

Resolvents of
-\hbar^{2}\Delta
with periodic boundary conditions and
\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}
are well defined for
\lambda>0
:

\displaystyle G_{bA,\lambda}\coloneq(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+\lambda)^{-1}\quad G_{\lambda}\coloneq(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+\lambda)^{-1}

Their integral kernels define the corresponding Green functions.

They have the following properties:

Property IV.4

Let
x\in\Omega
, then
G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet),G_{\lambda}(x,\bullet)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(x,y)=G_{\lambda}(x-y)=G_{\lambda}(y-x)=\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi\hbar}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\dfrac{1}{k^{2}+\lambda}e^{ik\cdot(x-y)}\geq 0

Proof
:
The periodic Laplacian is diagonalizable in the plane wave basis indexed by
k\in\dfrac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z^{2}}
:

\displaystyle e_{k}(x)\coloneq\dfrac{1}{L}e^{ik\cdot x}

Indeed

\displaystyle-\hbar^{2}\Delta+\lambda=\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left(\hbar^{2}k^{2}+\lambda\right)\left|e_{k}\right>\left<e_{k}\right|

so

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(x,y)=\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\dfrac{1}{\hbar^{2}k^{2}+\lambda}e^{ik\cdot\left(x-y\right)}

A change of index
k\coloneq-k
gives
G_{\lambda}(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, since
G_{\lambda}f
solves

(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+\lambda)u=f,u\in H_{p}^{2}(\Omega)

by the Lax-Milgram theorem,
G_{\lambda}f
is the unique minimizer of the following functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)\coloneq\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\left(\hbar^{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+\lambda\left|u\right|^{2}-fu\right)dx

Assuming
f\geq 0
, we see that
\mathcal{J}(u)\geq\mathcal{J}(\left|u\right|)
and conclude that
G_{\lambda}f\geq 0
. This implies that
G_{\lambda}(x,y)\geq 0
. Finally,

\displaystyle-\hbar^{2}\Delta+\lambda\geq\lambda\text{ and }\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+\lambda\geq\lambda

so

\displaystyle\left\lVert G_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\leq\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\text{ and }\left\lVert G_{bA,\lambda}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\leq\dfrac{1}{\lambda}

and
G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet),G_{\lambda}(\bullet,y)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

\square

Now we prove a diamagnetic inequality:

Proposition IV.5:

Diamagnetic inequality for Green functions

For all
x\in\Omega
and for almost every
y\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\left|G_{bA,\lambda}(x,y)\right|\leq G_{\lambda}(x,y)

Proof
:
Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by definition

\displaystyle\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}G_{bA,\lambda}\left(x,\bullet\right)\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+\lambda\right)\varphi=G_{bA,\lambda}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+\lambda\right)\varphi=\varphi

so, in the distributional sense

\displaystyle\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+\lambda\right)G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet)=\delta_{x}

(71)

Let
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}_{+})
radial with support included in the ball
B(0,L/2)
such that
\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{1}}=1
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, define the localizer
\rho_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x)\coloneq\begin{cases}n^{2}\rho(nx)&\text{if }x\in B\left(0,\frac{L}{2n}\right)\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}

Since
\rho_{n}
is periodic, the regularisation

\displaystyle u_{x}\coloneq G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet)*\rho_{n}\in C_{p}^{\infty}(\Omega)

Thus, equation
71
becomes

\displaystyle\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+\lambda\right)u_{x}=\delta_{x}*\rho_{n}=\rho_{n}(x-\bullet)

(72)

We estimate

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left[s(u_{x})\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+\lambda\right)u_{x}\right]=\text{Re}\left[s(u_{x})\rho_{n}(x-\bullet)\right]\leq\rho_{n}(x-\bullet)

Then we apply Kato’s inequality
62
to
u_{x}
, use
s(u_{x})u_{x}=\left|u_{x}\right|
and obtain

\displaystyle\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+\lambda\right)\left|u_{x}\right|\leq\text{Re}\left[s(u_{x})\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}u_{x}\right]+\lambda\left|u_{x}\right|\leq\rho_{n}(x-\bullet)

(73)

in a weak sense on
C_{p}^{\infty}(\Omega)^{*}
.

Similarly as
72
,

\displaystyle(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+\lambda)\rho_{n}*G_{\lambda}(\bullet,y)=\rho_{n}(y-\bullet)

Thus testing inequality
73
on
\rho_{n}*G_{\lambda}(\bullet,y)\in C_{p}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}_{+})
we get

\displaystyle\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\left|u_{x}(z)\right|\rho_{n}(y-z)dz

\displaystyle\leq\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\rho_{n}(x-z)\rho_{n}*G_{\lambda}(\bullet,y)(z)dz

With the changes of variables
t\coloneq t+x-y
,
z\coloneq z+x-y
and
section

IV.3
,

\displaystyle\left|G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet)*\rho_{n}\right|*\rho_{n}(y)\leq

\displaystyle\iint\displaylimits_{\Omega^{2}}\rho_{n}(x-z)\rho_{n}(z-t)G_{\lambda}(t-y)dzdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\displaylimits_{\Omega^{2}}\rho_{n}(2x-y-z)\rho_{n}(z-t)G_{\lambda}(t-x)dzdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{n}*\rho_{n}*G_{\lambda}(x,\bullet)(2x-y)

(74)

If
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, by Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho_{n}*\varphi_{n}-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\rho_{n}*(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\rho_{n}*\varphi-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\rho_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert\varphi_{n}-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\rho_{n}*\varphi-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\limit\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}0

Let
x\in\Omega
, with
section

IV.3
, after extraction of a subsequence,
\left|\rho_{n}*G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet)\right|\to\left|G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet)\right|
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
so

\displaystyle\left|G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet)*\rho_{n}\right|*\rho_{n}\limit\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}\left|G_{bA,\lambda}(x,\bullet)\right|

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and up to another subsequence almost everywhere. Similarly, almost everywhere

\displaystyle\rho_{n}*\rho_{n}*G_{\lambda}(x,\bullet)\limit\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}G_{\lambda}(x,\bullet)

So passing to the limit in

, for almost every

,

\displaystyle\left|G_{bA,\lambda}(x,y)\right|\leq G_{\lambda}(x,2x-y)=G_{\lambda}(y-x)=G_{\lambda}(x,y)

\square

IV.4 Lieb-Thirring inequalityLieb-Thirring inequality

We add a constant to the Laplacian to ensure that the constant function has a non-zero energy.

Theorem IV.6:

Lieb-Thirring Inequality

Let
\mathcal{V}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}_{+})
,

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\leq 0}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\right]\leq\dfrac{C_{LT}}{\hbar^{2}}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\mathcal{V}(x)^{2}dx

(75)

Proof
:
We denote
N_{\lambda}
the number of eigenvalues of
\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1
less than or equal to
\lambda
. From
[
LiebSeiringer
, Section 4.3]
,

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\leq 0}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}N_{\lambda}d\lambda

Define the Birman-Schwinger operator

\displaystyle K_{\lambda}\coloneq\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}G_{bA,\lambda}\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}

and let
B_{\lambda}
be the number of eigenvalues of
K_{\lambda}
greater or equal to
1
. We use the diamagnetic inequality to restrict to the non magnetic case. Since
G_{bA,\lambda}
is positive, we can define its square root. Using the arguments of
[
LiebSeiringer
, Theorem 4.4]
we can deduce from
section

IV.3
the diamagnetic inequality for
\sqrt{G_{bA,\lambda}}
:

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{G_{bA,\lambda}}(x,y)\right|\leq\sqrt{G_{\lambda}}(x,y)

Hence with
section

IV.3
,

\displaystyle\left|G^{\frac{3}{2}}_{bA,\lambda}(x,y)\right|=\left|\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}G_{bA,\lambda}(x,z)\sqrt{G_{bA,\lambda}}(z,y)dz\right|\leq\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}G_{\lambda}(x,z)\sqrt{G_{\lambda}}(z,y)dz=G^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\lambda}(x,y)

So taking
m\coloneq 3/2
and using an inequality on the traces of powers (see
[
LiebSeiringer
, Theorem 4.5]
),

\displaystyle N_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=B_{\lambda}\leq\text{Tr}\left[K_{\lambda}^{m}\right]\leq\text{Tr}\left[\mathcal{V}^{\frac{m}{2}}K_{\lambda}^{m}\mathcal{V}^{\frac{m}{2}}\right]\leq\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\mathcal{V}(x)^{m}\left|G_{A,\lambda+1}^{m}(x,x)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}{\mathcal{V}(x)}^{m}G^{m}_{\lambda+1}(x,x)dx

So we obtain

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\leq 0}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\right]\leq\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\int\displaylimits_{1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{V}(x)}^{m}G^{m}_{\lambda}(x,x)dxd\lambda

(76)

The kernel of
G_{\lambda}^{m}
is

\displaystyle G^{m}_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi\hbar}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\dfrac{1}{\left(k^{2}+\lambda\right)^{m}}e^{i\frac{k\cdot x}{\hbar}}

We use the integral bound for the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{1}{\left(k^{2}+\lambda\right)^{m}}\leq\lambda^{-m}+\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\left(u^{2}+\lambda\right)^{m}}du

so

\displaystyle G^{m}_{\lambda}(0)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{1}{\left(\left(\frac{2\pi\hbar}{L}\right)^{2}(k^{2}+q^{2})+\lambda\right)^{m}}=\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2m}\sum_{k,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{1}{\left(k^{2}+q^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2m}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left(k^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m}}+\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\left(k^{2}+u^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m}}du\right)

We estimate the integral

\displaystyle\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\left(k^{2}+u^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m}}du

\displaystyle=\left(k^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{-m}\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\left(\frac{u^{2}}{k^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda}+1\right)^{m}}du

\displaystyle=\dfrac{I(m)}{\left(k^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}}

with

\displaystyle m>\frac{1}{2}\implies I(m)\coloneq\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\left(1+u^{2}\right)^{m}}du<\infty

Similarly

\displaystyle G^{m}_{\lambda}(0)\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2m}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\left(k^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m}}+\dfrac{I(m)}{\left(k^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda^{-m}}{L^{2}}+\dfrac{I(m)}{2\pi\hbar L}\lambda^{-m+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2m}\left(\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\left(u^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m}}du+\int\displaylimits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{I(m)}{\left(u^{2}+\left(\frac{L}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{2}\lambda\right)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}}du\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda^{-m}}{L^{2}}+\dfrac{I(m)}{\pi\hbar L}\lambda^{-m+\frac{1}{2}}+\dfrac{1}{\left(2\pi\hbar\right)^{2}}I(m)I\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)\lambda^{-m+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C(m)}{\hbar^{2}}\lambda^{-m+1}

since
\lambda\geq 1
. We need
m>1
for the integrals to converge. We use the same trick as
[
LiebSeiringer
]
changing the potential to

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}(x)\coloneq\max\left(\mathcal{V}-\dfrac{\lambda}{2},0\right)

Combining this with

\displaystyle\mu\coloneq\frac{2V(x)}{\lambda},d\lambda=-\dfrac{2V(x)}{\mu^{2}}d\mu

we obtain

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\leq 0}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\hbar,b}+1-\mathcal{V}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(m)}{\hbar^{2}}\int\displaylimits_{1}^{\infty}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\lambda^{-m+1}\max\left(V(x)-\dfrac{\lambda}{2},0\right)^{m}d\lambda dx

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(m)}{\hbar^{2}}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\left(\int\displaylimits_{0}^{2V(x)}\lambda\left(\dfrac{2V(x)}{\lambda}-1\right)^{m}d\lambda\right)dx

\displaystyle=\dfrac{C(m)}{\hbar^{2}}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}\left(\int\displaylimits_{1}^{\infty}\dfrac{2V(x)}{\mu}\left(\mu-1\right)^{m}\cdot\dfrac{2V(x)}{\mu^{2}}d\mu\right)dx

\displaystyle=\dfrac{C(m)}{\hbar^{2}}\int\displaylimits_{\Omega}V(x)^{2}\left(\int\displaylimits_{1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\left(\mu-1\right)^{m}}{\mu^{3}}d\mu\right)dx

The integral in
\mu
converges if
3-m>1
. To conclude we need
1<m<2
hence the choice
m=3/2
is convenient.

\square

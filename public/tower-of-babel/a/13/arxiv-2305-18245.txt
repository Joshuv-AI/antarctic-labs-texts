1.
Introduction

The past few decades have seen significant advances in the dynamics of polynomials and entire functions. Taylor series expansions tell us that any entire function, such as
\exp(z)
, can be approximated by a sequence of polynomials. This often allows us to adapt the techniques of polynomial dynamics to the entire setting. With this philosophy, Devaney, Lyubich, Rempe, Schleicher and others have conducted broad studies of several
entire maps as limits of polynomials. Whether polynomial approximations can be tailor-made to satisfy specific dynamical properties of the limiting entire map is a broad and intriguing question. The authors of
[
BDH+00
]
considered this question in the setting of the exponential functions
p_{\lambda}(z)=\lambda\exp(z)
with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, and showed that hyperbolic components and specific rays in the parameter plane of
\{p_{\lambda}\}
are limits of the corresponding objects in the parameter space of unicritical polynomials
p_{d,\lambda}(z)=\lambda(1+z/d)^{d}
as the degree
d\rightarrow\infty
.
[
Kis95
]
studied convergence of Julia sets of a sequence of entire functions when the sequence has a limit satisfying certain properties. In
[
MB12
]
, the author studied a type of kernel convergence of non-escaping hyperbolic components of general entire families.

In this article, we study approximations of
postsingularly finite
(psf) maps in the
p_{\lambda}
family. An entire map is said to be postsingularly finite if the forward orbit of its set of singular values is finite. In the
p_{\lambda}
family, these are maps for which the point
0
, the unique singular value, has a finite forward orbit under iteration. Since the singular values of polynomials are exactly its critical values, we refer to psf polynomials as postcritically finite (pcf). Postcritically finite polynomials admit various combinatorial descriptions, in terms of
spiders
(see
[
HS94
]
,
[
Hub16
]
),
Hubbard trees
(
[
Poi93
]
), etc. Spiders and
homotopy Hubbard trees
have also been introduced for psf exponentials (see
[
LSV06
]
,
[
PRS23
]
respectively). Polynomials of critically preperiodic type were completely classified in
[
BFH92
]
using purely combinatorial techniques, and psf exponentials in
[
LSV06
]
. Postsingularly finite maps are profitable to study, since they play an important role in understanding the parameter space of both unicritical polynomials and exponentials. The topology of the Mandelbrot set can largely be described by means of pcf quadratic polynomials, as shown in
[
DH84
]
and
[
Sch04
]
. A similar study of the Multibrot sets was conducted in
[
EMS16
]
.

The machinery developed by William Thurston enables us to study topological models for postcritically finite rational maps. The question of when a topological pcf model is equivalent to a rational map is the subject of Thurston’s characterization theorem (
[
DH93
]
), and other results such as Dylan Thurston’s positive characterization of rational maps (
[
Thu20
]
), the Levy-Bernstein theorem (
[
Hub16
, Theorem 10.3.1]
), etc, lean into this theme. In the polynomial setting, by a theorem of Bernstein, Lei, Levy and Rees (
[
Hub16
, Theorem 10.3.9]
), it is known that a topological pcf polynomial is equivalent to a holomorphic one if and only if the topological model does not have a
Levy cycle
(see Section
2.2.1
). Thurston’s theory for postsingularly finite transcendental maps has seen tremendous development in the last decade, kicked off by
[
HSS09
]
for
topological exponential maps
, and continued by
[
Pfr19
]
and
[
PPS21
]
for general transcendental maps. Work by Shemyakov (
[
She22
]
) proves that for Thurston maps of structurally finite type, the only obstructions are Levy cycles.

In the setting of unicritical polynomials, the spider algorithm developed by
[
HS94
]
is an implementation of the Thurston pullback operator in the case of critically periodic parameters and some critically preperiodic ones. This provides a link between the combinatorial data contained in spiders and the rigid geometry of the pcf polynomial. Similarly for exponentials, external addresses (see section
2.7
) play the role of angles in the finite degree case. Our main result illustrates the utility of this relationship between combinatorics and geometry to prove the existence of a dynamical approximation of psf exponentials by pcf polynomials, and pinpoints these approximating polynomials by identifying angles of rays that land on them in parameter space.

In concurrent work with Prochorov and Reinke, we show that we can approximate general psf entire maps by pcf polynomials locally uniformly. Our approach there is to translate combinatorial data contained in line complexes into an analytical approximation of the Thurston pullback operator (defined in Section
2.2.4
) of entire maps. While the more extensive literature on the combinatorics unicriticals and exponentials is mined in order to poinpoint our approximating polynomials as landing points of specific parameter rays, our results here chalk out further questions about approximations of general psf entire maps.

1.1.
Results

Given a Thurston map
f
on the plane (defined in Section
2.2.1
), we denote by
\operatorname{Post}{(f)}
the postsingular set of
f
. When
f
is a unicritical pcf polynomial, its critical value
0
is either periodic or strictly preperiodic under iteration.

We make extensive use of parameter rays to the space of unicriticals. Any map
p_{d,\lambda}
with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is affine conjugate to a monic polynomial
z^{d}+c
, where
c
is unique upto multiplication by a
(d-1)
th root of unity. Moreover, if
\lambda
is a critically preperiodic parameter, any such
c
is the landing point of a positive finite number of parameter rays at rational angles.

We state our main result below.

Theorem A
.

Given a postsingularly finite exponential map
p_{\lambda}
where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, there exists an integer
D
and postcritically finite polynomials
p_{d,\lambda_{d}}
for
d\geq D
such that

(1)

p_{d,\lambda_{d}}\longrightarrow p_{\lambda}
locally uniformly on
{\mathbb{C}}
as
d\rightarrow\infty

(2)

p_{\lambda}|\operatorname{Post}{(p_{\lambda})}
is conjugate to
p_{d,\lambda_{d}}|\operatorname{Post}{(p_{d,\lambda_{d}})}

Furthermore, there exists a polynomial
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
, integers
\ell,k\geq 1
with
\deg q\leq\ell+k-2
depending only on
\lambda
, and a sequence of monic polynomials
z^{d}+c_{d}
conjugate to
p_{d,\lambda_{d}}
, such that
c_{d}
is the landing point of the parameter ray at angle
\theta_{d}
, with
(d-1)\theta_{d}=\frac{(d-1)q(d)}{d^{\ell}(d^{k}-1)}
for all
d
.

Each Thurston map
f:(S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\},\operatorname{Post}(f))\righttoleftarrow
gives rise to an operator
\sigma_{f}:\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},\operatorname{Post}(f))\righttoleftarrow
, called the Thurston pullback operator of
f
(see Section
2.2.4
), which has a fixed point
[\varphi]
in
\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},\operatorname{Post}(f))
if and only if
f
is realized as (ie, is Thurston equivalent to) a psf entire function
g
. If
f
is realized, the function
g=\varphi^{\prime}\circ f\circ\psi
for some
\varphi^{\prime},\psi\in[\varphi]
.

In the case of psf unicriticals and exponentials, such topological models can be constructed using the underlying spiders of these functions. Given a psf polynomial
p_{d,\lambda}
, choose any
z^{d}+c
conjugate to
p_{d,\lambda}
and an angle
\theta
such that the corresponding parameter ray lands at
c
. We recall in Section
2.8
the degree
-d
spider map
\mathcal{F}_{d,\theta}:S_{d}(\theta)\righttoleftarrow
. A certain quotient
[\mathcal{F}_{d,\theta}]:[S_{d}(\theta)]\righttoleftarrow
obtained by identifying points with the same itinerary can be extended to a branched cover of
S^{2}
that is Thurston equivalent to a pcf
p_{d,\lambda}
. We introduce a
spider space

\mathcal{T}^{d}_{\theta}
(a type of Teichmüller space) associated with
[S_{d}(\theta)]
and an operator
\sigma^{d}_{\theta}
on
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{\theta}
(which is highly related to the spider operator in
[
HS94
]
). This operator is conjugate to the Thurston pullback operator of
[\mathcal{F}_{d,\theta}]
, and has a unique fixed point
[\varphi]
in
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{\theta}
, using which we can recover
p_{d,\lambda}
as
\varphi^{\prime}\circ[\mathcal{F}_{d,\theta}]\circ\varphi
.

In direct analogy, there exists a notion of dynamic/parameter rays to the exponential family
p_{\lambda}
. Each ray in the exponential setting is labelled by a sequence
\underline{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
called an external address. It is known that each psf exponential parameter
\lambda
is the landing point of a positive finite number of parameter rays with strictly preperiodic external addresses. Conversely, the parameter ray at every strictly preperiodic external address lands on an psf exponential parameter (see Section
2.7
). Thus, given psf
p_{\lambda}
where
\lambda
has external address
\underline{s}
, following
[
LSV06
]
we construct a planar graph
\Gamma_{\underline{s}}
called an exponential spider, and an associated graph map
\mathcal{F}_{\underline{s}}:\Gamma_{\underline{s}}\righttoleftarrow
. A quotient dynamical system
[\mathcal{F}_{\underline{s}}]:[\Gamma_{\underline{s}}]\righttoleftarrow
can be extended to topological psf map of the plane Thurston equivalent to
p_{\lambda}
. We construct a Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{\underline{s}}
with an associated operator
\sigma_{\underline{s}}
that is conjugate to
\sigma_{[\mathcal{F}_{\underline{s}}]}
, and has a fixed point
[\varphi]
in
\mathcal{T}_{\underline{s}}
. As before, we have
p_{\lambda}=\varphi^{\prime}\circ[\mathcal{F}_{\underline{s}}]\circ\psi
for some
\varphi^{\prime},\psi\in[\varphi]
.

What we have detailed is the iteration of Thurston pullback operators using spiders. We uncover relationships between spiders
\Gamma_{\underline{s}}
for preperiodic
\underline{s}
, and spiders

as

. Our first step is to show that any degree
-d
spider is realised as a degree
-d+1
spider. In other words, the spiders

of rational angles

are

as the degree

Given a degree
d\geq 2
, there exist
d

and

This persistence of spiders underlies a deeper relationship between the space of pcf unicriticals in one degree to the next. Let

denote the collection of

Given a degree

, there exist

,

.

Next, we show that spiders corresponding to preperiodic external addresses are realised as degree

Given an external address
\underline{s}
with preperiod

Background

Throughout this section, fix a degree
d\geq 2
.

2.1.
Operations on angles

All angles in this paper are taken to be elements of
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. Given distinct angles
\alpha,\beta
,
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{\alpha,\beta\}
consists of two connected components or
arcs
. The length of the shorter arc is denoted
d(\alpha,\beta)
. We take the linear order on
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
induced by that on
[0,1)
. The map
\mu_{d}:{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\righttoleftarrow
is defined as
\mu_{d}(x)=dx
, and we let
\mathscr{O}_{d}(\theta)=\{d^{n-1}\theta:n\geq 1\}
.

Every rational angle is preperiodic under
\mu_{d}
with preperiod
\ell\geq 0
and eventual period
k\geq 1
(we will often drop the word ‘eventual’).

2.1.1.
Itineraries and kneading data

Given
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, for
j=0,1,...,d-1
, we define the
j
th static sector with respect to
\theta
as the interval
(\frac{\theta+j}{d},\frac{\theta+j+1}{d})\subset{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, and denote it
T^{stat}_{d,j}(\theta)
. Note that
\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{d-1}T^{stat}_{d,j}(\theta)={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\mu_{d}^{-1}(\theta)
.

Now suppose
\theta\in T^{stat}_{d,i}(\theta)
. For
j=0,1,...,d-1
, we define the
j
th dynamic sector with respect to
\theta
to be
T^{stat}_{d,j+i}(\theta)
, and denote it
T^{dyn}_{d,j}(\theta)
. Dynamic sectors are well-defined if and only if
\theta\not\in\mu_{d}^{-1}(\theta)
.

For any angle
t\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, the itinerary of
t
with respect to
\theta
, denoted
\Sigma_{d,\theta}(t)
, is the sequence
\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\nu_{3}....\in\{0,1,...,d,*\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
where

\displaystyle\nu_{n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}j&d^{n-1}t\in T^{dyn}_{d,j}(\theta)\\
*&d^{n-1}t\in\mu_{d}^{-1}(\theta)\end{cases}

An itinerary is called
*-
periodic if it is periodic under the shift map
\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\nu_{3}\nu_{4}....\mapsto\nu_{2}\nu_{3}\nu_{4}....
with period
k
, and the
*
’s occur exactly at indices
k,2k,3k,
etc. For an angle
\theta
, the itinerary
\Sigma_{d,\theta}(\theta)
is called the kneading sequence of
\theta
. Kneading sequences of rational angles are preperiodic or
*-
periodic under the shift map.

2.2.
Thurston maps on the plane

The theory developed by William Thurston to study postsingularly finite entire functions develops topological models for these functions and asks when such models are ‘equivalent’ in a suitable sense to holomorphic ones. We give the necessary background on Thurston theory in this section.

Let
f:S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}\righttoleftarrow
a topological branched cover. If
\deg f
is finite, then
f
is said to be a
topological polynomial
. A value
a\in S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}
is said to be an
asymptotic value
for
f
if there exists an arc
\gamma:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow t
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}f(\gamma(t))=a
.
The critical and asymptotic values of
f
are called its
singular values
, and we denote the set of singular values by
S(f)
. The
postsingular set

\operatorname{Post}{(f)}
of
f
is the forward orbit of
S(f)
. If
\operatorname{Post}{(f)}
is finite, we say that
f
is
postsingularly finite
(psf). Since topological polynomials do not have asymptotic values, a psf topological polynomial is said to be
postcritically finite
(pcf).

Definition 2.1
.

Given any complex structure
\mu
on
S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}
such that
(S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\},\mu)
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
, the Riemann surface
(S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\},f^{*}\mu)
is isomorphic either to
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{D}}
. If
(S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\},f^{*}\mu)
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
for every choice of
\mu
, we call
f
a
topological entire
function.

We note that any topological polynomial is topologically entire.

2.2.1.
Thurston maps

Definition 2.2
.

A psf topological entire function
f:(S^{2}\setminus{\infty},A)\righttoleftarrow
where
\operatorname{Post}{(f)}\subset A
and
A
is a finite set, is called a
Thurston map
. Unless specified, we assume that
A=\operatorname{Post}{(f)}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Thurston maps
f:(S^{2}\setminus{\infty},A)\righttoleftarrow
and
g:(S^{2}\setminus{\infty},B)\righttoleftarrow
are said to be
Thurston equivalent
if there exist orientation-preserving homeomorphisms
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}:S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}\righttoleftarrow
such that

•

g\circ\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{1}\circ f

•

\varphi_{0}|A\equiv\varphi_{1}|A
and
\varphi_{0}(A)=\varphi_{1}(A)=B

•

\varphi_{0}\sim\varphi_{1}
rel
A

The map
f
is said to be
realized
if
f
is Thurston equivalent to some psf entire function
g
; otherwise it is
obstructed
.

For topological polynomials, obstructions to being realized are well-understood. Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{r},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{r-1}\}
be a collection of essential simple closed curves
\gamma_{i}\subset S^{2}\setminus\operatorname{Post}{(f)}
, which are pairwise disjoint and belong to distinct homotopy classes relative to
\operatorname{Post}{(f)}
. Such a collection is called a
Levy cycle
for
f
if for every
i\in\{0,1,...,r\}
, at least one connected component
\eta
of
f^{-1}(\gamma_{i})
is isotopic to
\gamma_{i-1}
relative to
\operatorname{Post}{(f)}
, and
f|\eta:\eta\longrightarrow\gamma_{i}
has degree
1
.

The following result was proven by Bernstein, Lei, Levy and Rees.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Hub16
, Theorem 10.3.9]
)
.

If
f
is a topological polynomial, it is realized if and only if it does not have a Levy cycle.

Recent work by Shemyakov (
[
She22
]
) extends this result to the structurally finite topologically entire family.

2.2.2.
Topological exponential functions

Note that we will be working only in the context of unicritical polynomials and exponential functions.

Definition 2.5
.

A universal cover
f:S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}\longrightarrow S^{2}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
is called a
topological exponential function
.

For a function
f
as above, there is a unique asymptotic value at
0
, and we have
S(f)=\{0\}
. As proved in
[
HSS09
]
, if
f
is as above, it is topologically entire: given a complex structure on
S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}
such that
(S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\},\mu)
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
, let
\varphi:S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be an integrating map for
\mu
with
\varphi(0)=0
. The holomorphic map
\varphi\circ f:(S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\},f^{*}\mu)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is a universal cover. Since there is no universal cover from
{\mathbb{D}}
onto
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, the Riemann surface
(S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\},f^{*}\mu)
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
.

2.2.3.
Teichmüller spaces of punctured spheres

Let
f:(S^{2},\operatorname{Post}{(f)})\righttoleftarrow
be a Thurston map. If we want to find a psf entire map

that is Thurston equivalent to

, according to Thurston theory we start by looking at complex structures induced by all possible maps
\varphi:S^{2}\setminus\{\infty\}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
and try to guess a suitable

such that

. We describe this process in this section and the next.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a finite set

with
|A|\geq 3
, the

\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},A)
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},A)

\displaystyle=\{\text{Orientation-preserving homeomorphisms }\varphi:S^{2}\longrightarrow\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\}/\sim

where
\varphi\sim\psi
if, for some
M\in\text{Aut}(\hat{{\mathbb{C}}})
,
\varphi
is isotopic to
M\circ\psi
rel
A
.

\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},A)
is a contractible complex manifold of dimension
|A|-3
. The
Teichmüller metric
on
\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},A)
is defined as follows: given
[\varphi],[\psi]\in\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},A)
,

\displaystyle d_{T}([\varphi],[\psi])

\displaystyle=\inf_{q.c.\psi^{\prime}\sim\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}}\ln K(\psi)

where
\psi^{\prime}\sim\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}
means
\psi^{\prime}
is isotopic to
\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}
rel
A
, and
K(\psi)
is the complex dilitation of
\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}
.

2.2.4.
The Thurston pullback operator

.

Let

Given
[\varphi]\in\operatorname{Teich}(S^{2},\operatorname{Post}{(f)}\cup\{\infty\})
, where a representative

2.3.

Given

, we let

denote the polynomial

\displaystyle\varphi_{d,c}\circ f_{d,c}(z)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{d,c}(z)^{d}\hskip 5.0pt\forall z\in\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus K(f_{d,c})

\displaystyle\lim_{z\mapsto\infty}\frac{\varphi_{d,c}(z)}{z}

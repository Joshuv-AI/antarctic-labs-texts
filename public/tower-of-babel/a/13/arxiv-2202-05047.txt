Infinitary Noetherian Constructions II. Transfinite Words and the Regular Subword Topology Thanks: The first and third authors were supported by grant ANR-17-CE40-0028 of the French National Research Agency ANR (project BRAVAS)
Jean Goubault-Larrecq, Simon Halfon, Aliaume Lopez

Abstract
. We show that the spaces of transfinite words, namely ordinal-indexed words, over a Noetherian space, is also Noetherian, under a natural topology which we call the regular subword topology. We characterize its sobrification and its specialization ordering, and we give an upper bound on its sobrification rank and on its stature.

1.
Introduction

Given a well-quasi-order
X
(wqo, for short), the space of infinite
words
X^{\omega}
(of length
\omega
) need not be wqo in the subword
preordering. One way of correcting this anomaly is to turn to the
stronger notion of better quasi-orderings
[
11
]
. Another one
is to turn to the weaker notion of Noetherian space. Noetherian
spaces are a natural, topological generalization of wqos with many
similar properties
[
5
, Section 9.7]
. For example,
there are Noetherian analogues of Higman’s theorem and of Kruskal’s
theorem. Noetherianity is also preserved by some infinitary
constructions such as powerset.

In part I of this work
[
6
]
we have shown that, given a
Noetherian space
X
,
X^{\omega}
is again Noetherian, with a natural
topology, the subword topology. The same works for the set of
finite-or-infinite words
X^{\leq\omega}
. The purpose of the present
paper is to extend this to spaces
X^{<\alpha}
of transfinite words,
namely words indexed by ordinals strictly smaller than a fixed ordinal
bound
\alpha
.

The topology we choose is a natural generalization of
that of part I.
The bulk of the work consists in showing that if
X
is Noetherian,
then so is
X^{<\alpha}
.

Outline.

Section
2

recapitulates a few basic notions, and is also where we state our
basic tool for showing that
X^{<\alpha}
is Noetherian, as
Proposition
2.1
. We define the regular subword topology on

X^{<\alpha}
in Section
3
, based on so-called
transfinite products. After a few basic results on transfinite words
in Section
4
, we show that the

\alpha
-products, a specific kind of transfinite products, form an
irredundant subbase of closed subsets of
X^{<\alpha}
, where
\alpha

is a special kind of ordinal which we call a bound; this is one of the
conditions of Proposition
2.1
. We characterize inclusion of
transfinite products in Section
6
, and we
show that every transfinite product has a canonical
form in Section
7
. This allows us to show that the
collection of
\alpha
-products is well-founded in
Section
8
; this is another condition of
Proposition
2.1
. The final condition requires us to express
finite intersections of
\alpha
-products as finite unions of

\alpha
-products, which we do in
Section
9
. In the process, we obtain an
upper bound on the stature and reduced sobrification rank of

X^{<\alpha}
. Finally, we describe the specialization preordering of

X^{<\alpha}

in Section
10
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Topology.

Most of the following can be found in
[
5
]
.

Every topological space has a
specialization
preordering

\leq
, defined by
x\leq y
if and only if every open neighborhood
of
x
contains
y
. The closure of
\{x\}
is the
principal
ideal

\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits x\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{y\in X\mid y\leq x\}
. We silently consider any topological space as a preordered set
under
\leq
.

The
Alexandroff topology
of a preordering
\leq
is its family
of upwards-closed sets. Among the topologies with a given
specialization preordering
\leq
, it is the finest. The coarsest is
the
upper topology
, whose closed sets are intersections of sets
of the form
\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits E
,
E
finite; we write
\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits E
for

\bigcup_{x\in E}\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits x
.

A
Noetherian
space is a topological space in which every open
set is compact. We do not require compactness to imply separation.

A strict partial ordering
<
is
well-founded
if and only if
there is no infinite strictly descending chain.
By a slight abuse of language, we say that a preordering
\leq
is
well-founded if and only if its strict part (
x<y
if and only if

x\leq y
and
y\not\leq x
) is well-founded. A space is Noetherian
if and only if its set of closed subsets is well-founded under
inclusion.

A closed subset
C
is
irreducible
if and only if
C\neq\emptyset
, and for all
closed sets
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
such that
C\subseteq F_{1}\cup F_{2}
,
C
is
included in
F_{1}
or in
F_{2}
. The closure
\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits x
of every point
x

is irreducible closed. The product
C_{1}\times C_{2}
of two
irreducible closed subsets is irreducible in the product topology. A
space is
sober
if and only if every irreducible closed subset
is the closure
\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits x
of a unique point
x
.

An important property is that, in a Noetherian space
X
, every closed
subset is a
finite
union of irreducible closed subsets.

The
sobrification

\mathcal{S}X
of a topological space
X
is its
set of irreducible closed subsets, with the sets

\diamond U\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{C\in\mathcal{S}X\mid C\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
,

U
open in
X
, as open sets. The specialization ordering of
\mathcal{S}X

is inclusion.
\mathcal{S}X
is sober, and its lattice of open subsets is
order-isomorphic to that of
X
, through
U\mapsto\diamond U
. In particular,
X
is Noetherian if and only
if
\mathcal{S}X
is.

\mathcal{S}
defines a functor: for every continuous map

f\colon X\to Y
,
\mathcal{S}f
maps every
C\in\mathcal{S}X
to the
closure
cl(f[C])
in
Y
of
f[C]
. (We write
f[C]
for the
direct image
\{f(x)\mid x\in C\}
.) In particular,
cl(f[C])

is irreducible closed.

The sober Noetherian spaces can be characterized
order-theoretically: they are exactly the sets
X
with a
well-founded preordering
\leq
such that every finite intersection of
principal ideals can be expressed as a finite union of principal
ideals; the topology of
X
is the upper topology of
\leq
. Then the
closed subsets are exactly the sets
\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits E
with
E
finite.

The following proposition refines this, and will be the core of our
constructions. A family of sets
\mathcal{P}
is
irredundant
if
and only if every element of
\mathcal{P}
is irreducible in

\mathcal{P}
, namely: no element of
\mathcal{P}
is empty, and for all

P,P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
, if
P\subseteq P_{1}\cup P_{2}
then

P\subseteq P_{1}
or
P\subseteq P_{2}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a family of subsets of a set
X
, such that:

(1)

\mathcal{P}
is well-founded under inclusion;

(2)

X
can be written as a finite union of elements of
\mathcal{P}
;

(3)

for all
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}
,
P\cap Q
is a
finite union of elements of
\mathcal{P}
.

Then
X
, with the coarsest topology that makes every element of

\mathcal{P}
closed, is Noetherian.
If
\mathcal{P}
is irredundant, its irreducible closed subsets are
exactly the elements of
\mathcal{P}
, and
\mathcal{S}X
equals

\mathcal{P}
with the upper topology of inclusion.

Proof.

By assumption,
\mathcal{P}
is sober Noetherian in the upper
topology of
\subseteq
.

For every
x\in X
, since
\mathcal{P}
is well-founded
(property (1)), there is a minimal element
P
of
\mathcal{P}
that
contains
x
. For every
Q\in\mathcal{P}
that contains
x
, we
can write
P\cap Q
as
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}
where each
P_{i}
is in

\mathcal{P}
, by (3). Then
x
is in one
P_{i}
, and by minimality
of
P
,
P=P_{i}
. It follows that
P=P_{i}\subseteq P\cap Q
, so

P\subseteq Q
. Hence
P
is the smallest element of
\mathcal{P}

that contains
x
. Let us write that element as
\eta(x)
; for
every
Q\in\mathcal{P}
,
\eta(x)\subseteq Q
if and only if

x\in Q
.

This defines a map
\eta\colon X\to\mathcal{P}
. For every finite
subset
E\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{n}\}
of
\mathcal{P}
,

\eta^{-1}(\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits E)=\{x\in X\mid\exists i,\eta(x)\subseteq P_{i}\}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}
is closed in
X
. Hence
\eta
is
continuous. Taking
n\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}1
, we obtain that every element
P

of
\mathcal{P}
can be written as
\eta^{-1}(\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits\{P\})
. Since

\eta^{-1}
commutes with all intersections and (finite) unions,

\eta
is a
full
map, viz. every closed subset of
X
is the
inverse image of some closed subset of
\mathcal{P}
—this was
called an
initial
map in
[
6
]
. Lemma 8 of
that paper states that any space from which there is a full map to a
Noetherian space is itself Noetherian. Therefore
X
is Noetherian.

By Lemma 9 of the same paper, every irreducible closed subset
C
of

X
is of the form
\eta^{-1}(\mathcal{C})
for some irreducible
closed subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{P}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is
sober,
\mathcal{C}=\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits P
for some unique
P\in\mathcal{P}
,
hence
C=P
.

Conversely, and assuming
\mathcal{P}
irredundant, we claim that
every element
P
of
\mathcal{P}
is irreducible in
X
. By
assumption,
P
is non-empty. Since
\eta
is full, the closed
subsets of
X
are exactly the inverse images of closed subsets of

\mathcal{P}
, namely the finite unions of elements of
P
. Hence it
suffices to show that if
P
is included in a finite union of
elements of
\mathcal{P}
, then it is included in one of them. This
follows directly from the fact that
\mathcal{P}
is irredundant.
∎

2.2.
Ordinals.

An
indecomposable
ordinal is one of the form
\omega^{\beta}
,
where
\beta
is an ordinal; equivalently, an ordinal that cannot be
written as the sum of two strictly smaller ordinals. The other
ordinals are
decomposable
. For all ordinals
\alpha
,
\beta
,

\gamma
, we have:

(1)

if
\gamma
is indecomposable and

\alpha,\beta<\gamma
, then
\alpha+\beta<\gamma
;

(2)

if
\gamma
is indecomposable,

\alpha<\gamma
, and
\beta\leq\gamma
, then

\alpha+\beta\leq\gamma
;

(3)

every ordinal
\alpha
can be written in a unique way as a
finite sum of indecomposable ordinals
\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}

(its
Cantor normal form
), where

\alpha\geq\gamma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\gamma_{m}
;

(4)

the ordering on Cantor normal forms is lexicographic:
\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}<\gamma^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\gamma^{\prime}_{n}
(where both
sides are in Cantor normal form) if and only if for some
i\geq 1
,

\gamma_{1}=\gamma^{\prime}_{1}
, …,

\gamma_{i-1}=\gamma^{\prime}_{i-1}
and either
i=m+1\leq n
, or
i\leq m,n
and

\gamma_{i}<\gamma^{\prime}_{i}
.

3.
Transfinite words

We call
transfinite word
on a set
X
any map from
\alpha
to

X
, where
\alpha
is any ordinal. Such a word
w
has
length

|w|\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\alpha
. Seen as the set of ordinals
\beta
strictly
less than
\alpha
,
|w|
is also the
domain
of
w
, and we
write
{w}({\beta})
for the letter at position
\beta
in
w
, for
every
\beta\in|w|
(equivalently,
\beta<|w|
).

When
X
is preordered by
\leq
,
the
subword preordering

\leq
is defined on transfinite words
by
w\leq_{*}w^{\prime}
if and only if there is a strictly increasing map

f\colon|w|\to|w^{\prime}|
such that

{w}({\beta})\leq{w^{\prime}}({f(\beta)})
for every
\beta
,

0\leq\beta<|w|
.
We also say that
f

exhibits

w
as a subword of
w^{\prime}
.

We write
X^{<\alpha}
for the set of transfinite words
w
of length

|w|<\alpha
.
X^{\leq\alpha}
denotes
X^{<\alpha+1}
. For
example,
X^{<\omega}
is the set
X^{*}
of finite words on
X
, and

X^{<\omega+1}=X^{\leq\omega}
is the set of finite-or-infinite
words studied in Part I
[
6
]
.

The
concatenation

ww^{\prime}
of
w
and
w^{\prime}
is the transfinite word of length
|w|+|w^{\prime}|
such that

{(ww^{\prime})}({\beta})={w}({\beta})
for every
\beta<|w|
, and

{(ww^{\prime})}({|w|+\beta})={w^{\prime}}({\beta})
for every
\beta<|w^{\prime}|
.
We write
AB
for the set
\{ww^{\prime}\mid w\in A,w^{\prime}\in B\}
.

We are interested in the following topology. We cannot work on the
(proper) class of all transfinite words over
X
, for foundational
reasons. Instead we work on
sets

Y
of transfinite words;
this leads us to take intersections with
Y
here and there. Usually,

Y
will be a set of the form
X^{<\alpha}
.

Definition 3.1

(Regular subword topology)
.

The
regular subword topology
on any set of transfinite words

Y
on a space
X
is the coarsest topology that makes the sets

(F_{1}^{<\alpha_{1}}F_{2}^{<\alpha_{2}}\allowbreak\cdots\allowbreak F_{n}^{<\alpha_{n}})\cap Y
closed, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
,
…,
F_{n}
are closed subsets of
X
, and
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
,
…,
\alpha_{n}
are ordinals.

The following class of ordinals will be ubiquitous.

Definition 3.2

(Bound)
.

A
bound
is an ordinal of the form
\omega^{\beta}
or

\omega^{\beta}+1
,
\beta\geq 0
. The
trivial bound
is

\omega^{0}
(
=1
), all others are
non-trivial
. A

proper bound
is one of the form
\omega^{\beta}
or

\omega^{\beta}+1
with
\beta\geq 1
.

Definition 3.3

(Preatom, atom, product)
.

Let
X
be a topological space. A
preatom
is
an expression of the form
F^{<\gamma}
, where
F
is a closed
subset of
X
and
\gamma
is a bound.
An
atom
is a preatom
F^{<\gamma}
such that
\gamma
is
non-trivial, and if
\gamma=\omega^{0}+1
then
F
is irreducible
closed in
X
.

A
transfinite product

P
is any set of the form

A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{n}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and each
A_{i}
is an atom.
We write
\varepsilon
when
n=0
, namely

\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{\epsilon\}
.

When
\beta=0
,
F^{<\omega^{0}}=\varepsilon=\{\epsilon\}
, and

F^{<\omega^{0}+1}
is sometimes written as
F^{?}
: that is the set of
words of length at most
1
, whose only letter if any is in
F
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every set
F
, for every ordinal
\alpha
, one can write

F^{<\alpha}
as

F^{<\gamma_{1}}F^{<\gamma_{2}}\allowbreak\cdots\allowbreak F^{<\gamma_{m}}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
and each
\gamma_{i}
is a
non-trivial bound.

Proof.

If
\alpha=0
, we take
m\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}0
. Otherwise, let us write

\alpha
in Cantor normal form, as a finite sum

\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}
of indecomposable ordinals, where

m\neq 0
and
\alpha\geq\gamma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\gamma_{m}
. We
claim that
F^{<\alpha}
is equal to

F^{\leq\gamma_{1}}F^{\leq\gamma_{2}}\cdots F^{\leq\gamma_{m-1}}F^{<\gamma_{m}}
. The result will follow, since the latter is equal
to

F^{<\gamma_{1}+1}F^{<\gamma_{2}+1}\cdots F^{<\gamma_{m-1}+1}F^{<\gamma_{m}}
: when
\gamma_{m}\neq 1
, all the superscripts are
non-trivial bounds; when
\gamma_{m}=1
, this simplifies to

F^{<\gamma_{1}+1}F^{<\gamma_{2}+1}\allowbreak\cdots\allowbreak F^{<\gamma_{m-1}+1}
, where all superscripts are non trivial
bounds, too.

F^{\leq\gamma_{1}}\cdots F^{\leq\gamma_{m-1}}F^{<\gamma_{m}}
is included in

F^{<\alpha}
. Conversely, let
w\in F^{<\alpha}
, and let us
write
|w|
in Cantor normal form as

\gamma^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\gamma^{\prime}_{n}
. We can write
w
as a
concatenation
w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
where
|w_{1}|=\gamma^{\prime}_{1}
, …,

|w_{n}|=\gamma^{\prime}_{n}
. Since
|w|<\alpha
, there is a number

i\geq 1
such that
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}=\gamma_{1}
, …,

\gamma^{\prime}_{i-1}=\gamma_{i-1}
and either
i=n+1\leq m
, or

i\leq m,n
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}<\gamma_{i}
. In the first case,

w\in F^{\leq\gamma_{1}}\cdots F^{\leq\gamma_{n}}\subseteq F^{\leq\gamma_{1}}\cdots F^{\leq\gamma_{m-1}}F^{<\gamma_{m}}
. In the second case,
since

\gamma_{i}>\gamma^{\prime}_{i}\geq\gamma^{\prime}_{i+1}\geq\cdots\geq\gamma^{\prime}_{n}

and
\gamma_{i}
is indecomposable,

\gamma^{\prime}_{i}+\cdots+\gamma^{\prime}_{n}<\gamma_{i}
. Then

w_{1}\in F^{\leq\gamma_{1}}
, …,

w_{i-1}\in F^{\leq\gamma_{i-1}}
, and
w_{i}w_{i+1}\cdots w_{n}
is
in
F^{<\gamma_{i}}
, hence in

F^{\leq\gamma_{i}}\cdots F^{\leq\gamma_{m-1}}F^{<\gamma_{m}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

The regular subword topology on any set of transfinite words
Y
on
a Noetherian space
X
is the coarsest topology that has the
intersections of
Y
with transfinite products as closed sets.

Proof.

Let us consider a set of the form

F_{1}^{<\alpha_{1}}F_{2}^{<\alpha_{2}}\cdots F_{n}^{<\alpha_{n}}
, where

F_{1}
, …,
F_{n}
are closed subsets of
X
, and
\alpha_{1}
,

\alpha_{2}
, …,
\alpha_{n}
are ordinals. We claim that we can
rewrite it as a finite union of transfinite products.

If some
\alpha_{i}
equals
0
, then

F_{1}^{<\alpha_{1}}F_{2}^{<\alpha_{2}}\cdots F_{n}^{<\alpha_{n}}
is empty.
Otherwise, using Lemma
3.4
, we may assume that every

\alpha_{i}
is a non-trivial bound. We may also remove the preatoms

F_{i}^{<\alpha_{i}}
such that
F_{i}=\emptyset
, since in that case

F_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}=\varepsilon
. Let
I
be the subset of those
indices
i
,
1\leq i\leq n
, such that
\alpha_{i}=\omega^{0}+1
.
For each
i\in I
, we can write
F_{i}
as a finite union of
irreducible closed subsets
C_{i1}
, …,
C_{ik_{i}}
(and

k_{i}\neq 0
since
F_{i}\neq\emptyset
), since
X
is Noetherian.
For every function
f
mapping each
i\in I
to an element of

\{1,\cdots,k_{i}\}
, let
P_{f}
be the transfinite product obtained
from
F_{1}^{<\alpha_{1}}F_{2}^{<\alpha_{2}}\cdots F_{n}^{<\alpha_{n}}
by
replacing each preatom
F_{i}^{<\alpha_{i}}
,
i\in I
, by

C_{if(i)}^{?}
. Then

F_{1}^{<\alpha_{1}}F_{2}^{<\alpha_{2}}\cdots F_{n}^{<\alpha_{n}}
is the
finite union of the transfinite products
P_{f}
, when
f
varies over
the finitely many possible functions.
∎

4.
Elementary combinatorics on transfinite words

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}
be two bounds, and
u
and
v
be
transfinite words.

(1)

If
\gamma
is indecomposable and
|u|,|v|<\gamma
, then

|uv|<\gamma
.

(2)

If
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
and
|u|<\gamma
,
|v|<\gamma^{\prime}
,
then
|uv|<\gamma^{\prime}
.

(3)

If
\gamma
is proper and
|u|<\gamma
, then for every

x\in X
,
|xw|<\gamma
.

Proof.

(1) By definition of indecomposability, using
|uv|=|u|+|v|
.

(2) follows from (1) if
\gamma^{\prime}
is indecomposable. Otherwise, let
us write
\gamma^{\prime}
as
\omega^{\beta}+1
. Then
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}

means
\gamma\leq\omega^{\beta}
. Since
|u|<\gamma
,

|u|<\omega^{\beta}
and since
|v|<\gamma^{\prime}
,

|v|\leq\omega^{\beta}
. Hence

|uv|=|u|+|v|\leq\omega^{\beta}<\gamma^{\prime}
, because

\omega^{\beta}
is indecomposable.

(3) Since
\gamma
is proper, we have
|x|=\omega^{0}<\gamma
;
then (3) follows from (2).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Every closed set in the regular subword topology is
downwards-closed with respect to
\leq_{*}
.

Proof.

Given any two downwards-closed subsets
A
and
B
with respect to

\leq_{*}
, their product
AB
is, too: if
w\leq_{*}w^{\prime}
and

w^{\prime}\in AB
, then we can write
w^{\prime}
as
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
where
u^{\prime}\in A
and

v^{\prime}\in B
, and it is then easy to show that
w=uv
for some

u\leq_{*}u^{\prime}
and
v\leq_{*}v^{\prime}
. It is clear that every atom is
downwards-closed, as well as
\varepsilon
, so every transfinite
product is downwards-closed, hence also every intersection of finite
unions of transfinite products.
∎

For every indecomposable ordinal
\gamma
, there is a so-called

Hessenberg pairing
map

H\colon\gamma\times\gamma\to\gamma
, which is injective; for
all
\alpha<\beta<\gamma
and
\delta<\gamma
,

H(\alpha,\delta)<H(\beta,\delta)
and

H(\delta,\alpha)<H(\delta,\beta)

[
10
, Exercise 2.23 (ii)]
,
and it is easy to see that

H(\alpha,0)\geq\alpha
for every
\alpha<\gamma
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F
be a non-empty subset of a set
X
. For every transfinite
word
w
on
X
such that
|w|
is an indecomposable ordinal

\gamma
, and whose letters are in
F
, there is a word
w^{\prime}
of
length
\gamma
, whose letters are in
F
again, such that for every
way of writing
w^{\prime}

5.
Continuity and irredundancy

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a Noetherian space.

(1)

For any set

(2)

Proof.

(1) For every preatom

(2) First,

As far as continuity is concerned, let

,

are

On spaces of the form

Let

P^{\prime}
, by
Lemma
4.2
.
∎

We turn to sufficient conditions. There are three cases, depending on
the relative positions and indecomposability statuses of
\gamma
and

\gamma^{\prime}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}
be two non-trivial bounds, and let

F^{<\gamma}P
,
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
be two transfinite products.
Assuming that
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
, or that
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is
indecomposable,
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
if and
only if:

(1)

F\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
,

(2)

or
F\not\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ direction is by Lemma
6.1
(1) and
(2). We deal with the ‘if’ direction. Note that

\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
; also, if
\gamma^{\prime}
is
decomposable, then
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
.

(1) For every
w\in F^{<\gamma}P
, let us write
w
as
uv
with

u\in F^{<\gamma}
and
v\in P
. Since

P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
,
v
is in
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
.
Let us write
v
as
v_{1}v_{2}
with
v_{1}\in{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
and

v_{2}\in P^{\prime}
. Then the letters of
uv_{1}
are all in
F^{\prime}
, and

|uv_{1}|<\gamma^{\prime}
by Lemma
4.1
(2). It follows
that
uv_{1}
is in
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
, so
w=uv_{1}v_{2}
is in

{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
.

(2) If
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}
,
then

F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
, where the
last inequality is because every
w\in P^{\prime}
can be written as

\epsilon w\in{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}
be two non-trivial bounds, and

F^{<\gamma}P
,
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
be two transfinite products.
Assuming that
\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}
,

F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
if and only if:

(1)

F
is empty and
P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
,

(2)

or
F
is non-empty and
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(1) If
F=\emptyset
then
F^{<\gamma}P=P
, and the equivalence
is clear.

(2) If
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}
, then

F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
since
P^{\prime}
is trivially
included in
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
. The ‘only if’ direction is by
Lemma
6.1
(3).
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}
be two non-trivial bounds, and let

F^{<\gamma}P
,
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
be two transfinite products.
Assuming that
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is decomposable,

F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
if and only if:

(1)

F
is empty and
P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
,

(2)

or
F
is non-empty,
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
, and
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
,

(3)

or
F\not\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
F
is empty then
F^{<\gamma}P=P
, so
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
is equivalent to
P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
. Henceforth, we assume
F
non-empty.

‘If’ direction. If
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
, then

F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
is obvious (recall that

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
). If
F^{\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}
, then

F^{\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
.

‘Only if’. Let us assume

F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
. If
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
,
then
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
by Lemma
6.1
(4).
Otherwise,
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq P^{\prime}
by Lemma
6.1
(2).
∎

7.
Reduced products

We can write transfinite products in many equivalent ways. For
example,
\emptyset^{<\gamma}=\varepsilon
for every non-trivial
bound
\gamma
. Here are a few other cases.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
F
,
F^{\prime}
be non-empty closed subsets of a topological space

X
, and
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}
be non-trivial bounds. If
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
, then:

(1)

If
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
or if
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is
indecomposable, then

F^{<\gamma}{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}=F^{\prime<\gamma^{\prime}}
.

(2)

If
\gamma^{\prime}
is indecomposable, and
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
, then

{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}F^{<\gamma}={F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

The right-hand sides are always included in the left-hand sides.

(1) By Lemma
6.2
, and remembering that

F\subseteq F^{\prime}
,

F^{<\gamma}{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}\subseteq F^{\prime<\gamma^{\prime}}
if and only if

{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}\subseteq F^{\prime<\gamma^{\prime}}
, which is simply true.

(2) Since
\gamma^{\prime}
is indecomposable, by the same lemma,

{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}F^{<\gamma}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
if and only if

F^{<\gamma}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
, which holds since

F\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

We will see that this leads to canonical forms for transfinite
products. As in
[
7
, Theorem 4.22]
, and to reduce
excessive pedantry related to the difference between syntax and
semantics, we write
\mathtt{A}
,
\mathtt{B}
(resp.,
\mathtt{P}
,
\mathtt{Q}
) to denote
atoms, resp. sequences of atoms (syntax), and
A
,
B
,
P
,

for
their respective semantics. Hence if
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{A}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{2}\cdots\mathtt{A}_{n}

(as a sequence), then

(as a product). The
(syntactic) atoms
\mathtt{A}
are pairs
(F,\gamma)
of a closed set
F

and a non-trivial bound
\gamma
(with
F
irreducible if

\gamma=\omega^{0}+1
), and then

. Note that

\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{Q}
implies

, but the converse may fail.

Definition 7.2

(Reduced)
.

A sequence of atoms
\mathtt{P}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\mathtt{A}_{1},\mathtt{A}_{2}\cdots\mathtt{A}_{n}
on

,
where
\mathtt{A}_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}(F_{i},\gamma_{i})

(1)

is a non-empty closed subset of

(

);

(2)

for every

,
1\leq i<n
, such that
\gamma_{i}<\gamma_{i+1}

is not included in
F_{i+1}
;

(3)

for every

,

, such that
\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i+1}

and

are incomparable;

and for every

, such that

is

.

Lemma 7.3
.

For all non-empty closed subsets
F
and
F^{\prime}
of a space

, for
all non-trivial bounds
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}
,

(1)

F^{<\gamma}\neq\varepsilon

(2)

F^{<\gamma}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
if and only if
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
.

(3)

F^{<\gamma}={F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
if and only if
F=F^{\prime}
and

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(1) Since
F
is non-empty, let us pick
x
in
F
. Since
\gamma

is non-trivial, the one-letter word

is in

, whence
the conclusion.

(2) If
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
or if
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is

,

F^{<\gamma}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
if and only if

F\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
(true),
or
F\not\subseteq F^{\prime}
and

Hence

in this case.
If

, by Lemma

If

F^{<\gamma}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}

and

\varepsilon\subseteq\varepsilon

.

∎

Lemma 7.4
.

The only reduced sequence of atoms with semantics
\{\epsilon\}

is
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
\mathtt{P}
be a reduced sequence of atoms of length

\mathtt{A}_{1}\cdots\mathtt{A}_{n}
. By Lemma

(1), each

contains a non-empty word, and their concatenation is a non-empty

.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

For all atoms
A\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}F^{<\gamma}
,
B\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
,
with
F,F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, and all transfinite products
P
,
Q
, if

AP\subseteq BQ
and
A\not\subseteq B
, then
AP\subseteq Q
.

Proof.

Since
A\not\subseteq B
, we have
F\not\subseteq F^{\prime}
or

\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}
, by Lemma
7.3
(2).

If
\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}
, then Lemma
6.3
and

F^{\gamma}P\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}Q
imply

F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq Q
. Let us therefore assume

\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
, and
F\not\subseteq F^{\prime}
. If

\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
or
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is indecomposable, then by
Lemma
6.2
,
F^{<\gamma}P\subseteq Q
again. If

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is decomposable, then we reach the same
conclusion by using Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Lemma 7.6
.

For any reduced sequence of atoms
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{P}
,
AP=A
implies

\mathtt{P}=\varepsilon
.

Proof.

By contradiction, let us assume that
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{A}^{\prime}\mathtt{Q}
, and let us
write
A
as
F^{<\gamma}
and
A^{\prime}
as
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
. Since

AP\subseteq A
, either
\gamma
is indecomposable and

P\subseteq A
by Lemma
6.2
, or
\gamma
is
decomposable and
P\subseteq\varepsilon
by
Lemma
6.4
. The latter is impossible by
Lemma
7.4
. Hence
\gamma
is indecomposable, and

P=A^{\prime}Q\subseteq A
.

If
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma
, then by Lemma
6.2

F^{\prime}\subseteq F
and
Q\subseteq A
, or
F^{\prime}\not\subseteq F
and

P\subseteq\varepsilon
. The latter is impossible, as above. The
former is impossible, too, because
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{A}^{\prime}\mathtt{Q}
is reduced, using
Definition
7.2
(3), (4). If
\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma
, then

P=A^{\prime}Q\subseteq\varepsilon
by Lemma
6.3
, which is
impossible by Lemma
7.4
.
∎

Lemma 7.7
.

For every reduced sequence of atoms
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{P}
and for every atom

\mathtt{B}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
with
F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, if
AP=B

then
\mathtt{P}=\varepsilon
.

Proof.

By induction on the number
n\geq 1
of atoms in
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{P}
. If

n=1
, this is vacuous, so let
n\geq 2
. If
B\subseteq A
, then

B\subseteq A\subseteq AP=B
, so
A=B
. Then
AP=A
, so

\mathtt{P}=\varepsilon
by Lemma
7.6
.

We now assume
B\not\subseteq A
, and we will show that this is
impossible. By Lemma
7.5
applied to
B\subseteq AP
,
we have
B\subseteq P
. Since
P\subseteq AP=B
,
B=P
. By
induction hypothesis, if we write
\mathtt{P}
as
\mathtt{A}^{\prime}\mathtt{Q}
where
\mathtt{A}^{\prime}

is an atom, then
\mathtt{Q}=\varepsilon
, so
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{A}^{\prime}
. Then
P=B

entails
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{A}^{\prime}=\mathtt{B}
by Lemma
7.3
(3). Let us write

\mathtt{A}
as
(F,\gamma)
and
\mathtt{B}
as
(F^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
. Since

A\subseteq AP=B
, Lemma
7.3
(2) entails that

F\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
. But

\mathtt{A}\mathtt{P}=(F,\gamma)(F^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is reduced, and this
contradicts Definition
7.2
(2) if
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
,
(3) if
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is indecomposable. Hence
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}

is decomposable. Then

AP=F^{<\gamma}{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma}\subseteq B=F^{\prime<\gamma}
implies

{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma}\subseteq\varepsilon
by Lemma
6.4
,
and that is impossible by Lemma
7.3
(1).
∎

Proposition 7.8
.

For all reduced sequences of atoms
\mathtt{P}
and
\mathtt{Q}
,
P=Q
if and
only if
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{Q}
.

Proof.

The ‘if’ direction is trivial. We show that
P=Q
implies
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{Q}

by induction on the sum
|\mathtt{P}|+|\mathtt{Q}|
of the sizes of
\mathtt{P}
and

\mathtt{Q}
, where by
size we mean number of atoms. Henceforth we assume
P=Q
.

If
|\mathtt{P}|=0
, then
P=Q=\{\epsilon\}
. By
Lemma
7.4
,
\mathtt{Q}=\varepsilon=\mathtt{P}
. The situation
is symmetric if
|\mathtt{Q}|=0
. Let us assume
|\mathtt{P}|,|\mathtt{Q}|\geq 1
. If

|\mathtt{Q}|=1
,
\mathtt{Q}
is an atom
\mathtt{B}
. By Lemma
7.7
, we
must have
|\mathtt{P}|=1
. Then
P=Q
implies
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{Q}
by
Lemma
7.3
(3). Similarly if
|\mathtt{P}|=1
. The
interesting case is the remaining one:
|\mathtt{P}|,|\mathtt{Q}|\geq 2
. Let
us write
\mathtt{P}
as
\mathtt{A}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathtt{Q}
as

\mathtt{B}_{1}\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
.

We first claim that if
A_{1}\not\subseteq B_{1}
, then

\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
. Indeed, under that assumption, and since

P=A_{1}A_{2}P^{\prime}\subseteq Q=B_{1}B_{2}Q^{\prime}
, we obtain

P\subseteq B_{2}Q^{\prime}
by Lemma
7.5
. In turn,

B_{2}Q^{\prime}\subseteq B_{1}B_{2}Q^{\prime}=Q=P
, so
P=B_{2}Q^{\prime}
. The
induction hypothesis then yields
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
. Similarly, if

B_{1}\not\subseteq A_{1}
then
\mathtt{Q}=\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}
.

It follows that we cannot have
A_{1}\not\subseteq B_{1}
and

B_{1}\not\subseteq A_{1}
. Otherwise,

\mathtt{Q}=\mathtt{B}_{1}\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}=\mathtt{B}_{1}\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{B}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}=\mathtt{B}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{1}\mathtt{Q}
, which is impossible since

|\mathtt{B}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{1}\mathtt{Q}|\neq|\mathtt{Q}|
.

Hence
A_{1}
is included in
B_{1}
, or conversely. Without loss of
generality, let us assume
B_{1}\subseteq A_{1}
. We claim that, in
fact,
A_{1}=B_{1}
. We reason by contradiction, and we assume

A_{1}\not\subseteq B_{1}
. Then we have seen that
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
.
By syntactic matching,
\mathtt{A}_{1}=\mathtt{B}_{2}
(and
\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}=\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}
).
Let us write
\mathtt{B}_{1}
as
(F,\gamma)
and
\mathtt{B}_{2}
as

(F^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
. Since
B_{1}\subseteq A_{1}=B_{2}
,
F\subseteq F^{\prime}

and
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
by Lemma
7.3
(2). By
Definition
7.2
(2) and (3) applied to

\mathtt{B}_{1}\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
, it is impossible that
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
, or
that
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is indecomposable. Hence
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is
decomposable. Now
Q=B_{1}B_{2}Q^{\prime}=F^{<\gamma}B_{2}Q^{\prime}
is included
in
P=A_{1}A_{2}P^{\prime}=B_{2}A_{2}P^{\prime}={F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma}A_{2}P^{\prime}
(since

\mathtt{A}_{1}=\mathtt{B}_{2}
and
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
), so
B_{2}Q^{\prime}\subseteq A_{2}P^{\prime}
by
Lemma
6.4
(2).
Since
A_{2}P^{\prime}\subseteq P=B_{2}Q^{\prime}
(because
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
),

A_{2}P^{\prime}=B_{2}Q^{\prime}
. By induction hypothesis,

\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}=\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
, so
\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}=\mathtt{P}
. This is impossible
since
\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{A}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}
.
Having reached a contradiction, we
conclude that
A_{1}=B_{1}
, so
\mathtt{A}_{1}=\mathtt{B}_{1}
by
Lemma
7.3
(3).

We now claim that
A_{2}P^{\prime}\subseteq B_{2}Q^{\prime}
. We know that

P=A_{1}A_{2}P^{\prime}
is included in
Q=B_{1}B_{2}Q^{\prime}=A_{1}B_{2}Q^{\prime}
(since

A_{1}=B_{1}
). Let us write
\mathtt{A}_{1}
as
(F,\gamma)
and
\mathtt{A}_{2}
as

(F^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
. If
\gamma
is decomposable, then

A_{2}P^{\prime}\subseteq B_{2}Q^{\prime}
by Lemma
6.4
(2). Let
therefore
\gamma
be indecomposable. Since
\mathtt{A}_{1}\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}
is
reduced, we cannot have
F\supseteq F^{\prime}
and
\gamma\geq\gamma^{\prime}
,
by Definition
7.2
(3) and (4), so

A_{2}\not\subseteq A_{1}
, by Lemma
7.3
(2). Then

A_{2}P^{\prime}\subseteq A_{1}A_{2}P^{\prime}\subseteq A_{1}B_{2}Q^{\prime}
,
so
A_{2}P^{\prime}\subseteq B_{2}Q^{\prime}
by Lemma
7.5
.

Symmetrically,
B_{2}Q^{\prime}
is included in
A_{2}P^{\prime}
, so

A_{2}P^{\prime}=B_{2}Q^{\prime}
. By the induction hypothesis

\mathtt{A}_{2}\mathtt{P}^{\prime}=\mathtt{B}_{2}\mathtt{Q}^{\prime}
. We remember that
\mathtt{A}_{1}=\mathtt{B}_{1}
, so

\mathtt{P}=\mathtt{Q}
. ∎

We can always rewrite any transfinite product into a reduced product
with the same semantics, using Lemma
7.1
, whence the
following.

Corollary 7.9
.

Every transfinite product is equal to
P
for some unique reduced
sequence of atoms
\mathtt{P}
.

Corollary
7.9
allows us to conflate the notions of
transfinite product and of reduced sequence of atoms. By abuse of
language, we will call
reduced product
any transfinite product

P
written in such a way that
\mathtt{P}
is reduced. A
reduced

\alpha
-product
is an
\alpha
-product
that is reduced in this sense.

8.
Well-foundedness

The
rank
(or
height
) of an element
x
in a well-founded
poset
P
is defined by well-founded induction as the least ordinal
strictly larger than the ranks of all elements
y<x
. We write

||F||
for the rank of
F
in the lattice of closed subsets of a
Noetherian space
X
. For any Noetherian space
F
,
||F||
is the

stature
of
F

[
8
]
, generalizing the notion of
the same name on wqos
[
1
]
.

Given any two ordinals

\alpha\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\omega^{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+\omega^{\alpha_{m}}
and

\beta\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\omega^{\beta_{1}}+\cdots+\omega^{\beta_{n}}
in Cantor
normal form, their
natural sum

\alpha\oplus\beta
is defined
as
\omega^{\gamma_{1}}+\cdots+\omega^{\gamma_{m+n}}
, where

\gamma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\gamma_{m+n}
is the list obtained by
sorting the list

\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n}
in decreasing
order. This operation is associative and commutative, and strictly
monotonic in both arguments.

An ordinal
\delta
is
critical
if and only if

\omega^{\delta}=\delta
. For every ordinal
\alpha
, let

{\alpha}^{\circ}
be
\alpha+1
if
\alpha=\delta+n
for some critical
ordinal
\delta
and some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\alpha
otherwise. Then

\alpha<\omega^{{\alpha}^{\circ}}
, and
\alpha\mapsto{\alpha}^{\circ}

is strictly monotonic
[
8
, Lemmata 12.3 and 12.4]
. For
every proper bound
\gamma
, we define
[{\gamma}]
as the rank of

\gamma
in the poset of all proper bounds less than or equal to

Definition 8.1

||F||

Proposition 8.2

Let

be a Noetherian space. For all reduced products

We first claim that

and
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}

,

\varphi(F^{<\gamma})<\varphi({F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}})
. By
Lemma

,
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
and
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
,
and not both are equalities. If

and

\varphi(F^{<\gamma})=||F||<||F^{\prime}||=\varphi({F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}})

and

are proper, then

since

,

and

are strictly monotonic.

We prove the proposition by induction on the sum of the lengths of

and

. Let

. If

. If additionally

\varphi(F^{<\gamma})
, and
when

\varphi(F^{<\gamma})=||F||\neq 0

is a power of
\omega

We now assume that

. We write

, where
A_{1}
, …,

\varphi(P)<\varphi(P^{\prime})
. We turn to the other cases: from

If

9.
Intersections of transfinite products

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space. The intersection of two transfinite
products satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\varepsilon\cap P^{\prime}=P\cap\varepsilon=\varepsilon
.

(2)

If
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
or if
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is
indecomposable, then
F^{<\gamma}P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
is
equal to the union of

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}(P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime})
and of

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}(F^{<\gamma}P\cap P^{\prime})
.

(3)

If
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is decomposable, say

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}=\omega^{\beta}+1
, then

F^{<\gamma}P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
is the union of

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}(P\cap P^{\prime})
, of

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime})
, and of

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(F^{<\gamma}P\cap P^{\prime})
.

Proof.

(1) is clear. Let us deal with the left to right inclusions for the
other cases, as a first step.
For every
w\in F^{<\gamma}P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
, let us
write
w
as
uv
where
u\in F^{<\gamma}
,
v\in P
and also as

u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
where
u^{\prime}\in{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}
,
v^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}
.

(2) If
|u|\leq|u^{\prime}|
,
u
is a prefix of
u^{\prime}
, so its
letters are not just in
F
, but also in
F^{\prime}
. Therefore

u\in(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}
. Also,
v
is in
P
, and is a suffix
of
w
. In particular,
v\leq_{*}w
, so
v\in{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
by
Lemma
4.2
. If
|u^{\prime}|\leq|u|
, then symmetrically

u^{\prime}
is in
(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}
and
v^{\prime}
is in
P^{\prime}
and in

F^{<\gamma}P
.

(3) If
|u|=|u^{\prime}|=\omega^{\beta}
, then
u=u^{\prime}
is in

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}
, and
v=v^{\prime}
is in
P\cap P^{\prime}
, so

w\in(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}(P\cap P^{\prime})
. Otherwise,
w
is in

F^{<\omega^{\beta}}P
and in
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
, or in

F^{<\gamma}P
and in
{F^{\prime}}^{<\omega^{\beta}}P^{\prime}
. In the first
case, by (2) with
\omega^{\beta}
in lieu of
\gamma
,
w
is in

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime})\cup(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(F^{<\omega^{\beta}}P\cap P^{\prime})
, hence in

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime})\cup(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(F^{<\gamma}P\cap P^{\prime})
. In the second case,
a similar argument leads to the same result.

We now deal with the right to left inclusions.

(2)
(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}(P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime})
is included
both in
F^{<\gamma}P
(because
F\cap F^{\prime}\subseteq F
) and in

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}={F^{\prime}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
, by
Lemma
7.1
(1). Similarly for

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}(F^{<\gamma}P\cap P^{\prime})
.

(3)
(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}(P\cap P^{\prime})
is included both in

F^{<\gamma}P
and in
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma}P^{\prime}={F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
.

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime})
is included
both in

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}P\subseteq F^{<\omega^{\beta}}P\subseteq F^{<\gamma}P
and in

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}{F^{\prime}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}={F^{\prime}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}

(by Lemma
7.1
(1)). Finally,

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}(F^{<\gamma}P\cap P^{\prime})
is included
both in
(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}F^{<\gamma}P=F^{<\gamma}P

(by Lemma
7.1
(1)) and in

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}P^{\prime}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\omega^{\beta}}P^{\prime}\subseteq{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary 9.2
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian space. The intersection of any two
transfinite products is a finite union of transfinite products.

Proof.

By induction on the sum of their sizes, using
Lemma
9.1
. The case where one of them is

\varepsilon
is obvious, so we deal with the intersection of two
transfinite products
F^{<\gamma}P
and
{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
.
Without loss of generality,
\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}
.

By induction hypothesis,
P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
,

F^{<\gamma}P\cap P^{\prime}
and
P\cap P^{\prime}
are
finite unions of transfinite products, say
\bigcup_{i}P_{i}
,

\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}
, and
\bigcup_{k}R_{k}
respectively.

The intersection
F^{<\gamma}P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}
can then be
expressed as

\bigcup_{i}(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}P_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j}(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}Q_{j}
when
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
or if
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}

is indecomposable. If
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
is of the form

, then it can be expressed as the union

\bigcup_{k}(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}R_{k}\cup\bigcup_{i}(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}P_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j}(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{\beta}}Q_{j}
.

This is a finite union of transfinite products, except when

\gamma=\omega^{0}+1
. In that case, we need to refine the
expressions above. If
F\cap F^{\prime}
is empty, then

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}=\varepsilon
, so

F^{<\gamma}P\cap{F^{\prime}}^{<\gamma^{\prime}}P^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i}P_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}
when

, and

otherwise.

We now assume that

.

and

are

is Noetherian,

is a

\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{n}C_{\ell}
of irreducible closed
subsets. Since

,

is non-zero. Then

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\gamma}=(F\cap F^{\prime})^{?}=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{n}C_{\ell}^{?}
(an equality that would fail if

were zero), and

(F\cap F^{\prime})^{<\omega^{0}}=\varepsilon
. Therefore

is equal to

\bigcup_{i,\ell}C_{\ell}^{?}P_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j,\ell}C_{\ell}^{?}Q_{j}
when

, or to

\bigcup_{k,\ell}C_{\ell}^{?}R_{k}\cup\bigcup_{i}P_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}
if
\gamma^{\prime}=\omega^{0}+1
.
∎

We can now use Proposition
2.1
. If

is Noetherian, the

\mathcal{P}
of

-products is well-founded under

,

is a finite
union of elements of

since it is, in fact, an

\alpha
-product. The intersection of any two elements of

is a finite union of elements of

by

, and
\mathcal{P}
is irredundant by

. Moreover, the regular subword topology
on

is the coarsest that makes every element of

a closed set, by Proposition

. Since
every set of transfinite words on

is included in

for

, we obtain the following.

Theorem 9.3
.

For every Noetherian space
X
, every space

of transfinite words
on

is Noetherian in the regular subword topology. For every

are
the

Given any non-empty Noetherian space

For

10.
The specialization ordering

A non-empty transfinite word
w
is indecomposable if and only if for
every way of writing
w
as
uv
where
v\neq\epsilon
, we have

w\leq_{*}v
. Pouzet
[
12
]
, confirming a conjecture of
Jullien
[
9
]
, shows that
\leq
is a
better-quasi-ordering if and only if every transfinite word (of
countable length) is a concatenation of finitely many indecomposable
words.

Given a topological space
X
and an ordinal
\alpha
, let us call
w\in X^{<\alpha}

topologically
indecomposable
if and only
w\neq\epsilon
and, for every way of
writing
w
as
uv
where
v\neq\epsilon
,
w
is in the closure

\overline{v}
of
v
in
X^{<\alpha}
. (We write
\overline{v}
instead
of the more cumbersome notation
cl(\{v\})
.) When
w=uv
, we have

v\leq_{*}w
, so
v\in\overline{w}
by Lemma
4.2
.
Hence
w
is topologically indecomposable if and only if for every way
of writing
w
as
uv
where
v\neq\epsilon
,

\overline{v}=\overline{w}
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space. Every indecomposable transfinite
word
w
on
X
is topologically indecomposable.

Proof.

Whenever
w=uv
with
v\neq\epsilon
,

w\leq_{*}v
; so
w\in\overline{v}
by Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Lemma 10.2
.

Every transfinite word
w\in X^{<\alpha}
, where
X
is Noetherian
and
\alpha
is a bound, can be written as a finite concatenation of
topologically indecomposable transfinite words.

Proof.

By induction on
|w|
. The claim is clear if

. Otherwise, for every
\beta<|w|
, we
write
w
as
u_{\beta}v_{\beta}
where
u_{\beta}
is its
prefix of length
\beta
, and
v_{\beta}\neq\epsilon
. For all

\beta<\gamma<|w|

v_{\gamma}\leq_{*}v_{\beta}

by Lemma

and therefore
\overline{v_{\gamma}}\subseteq\overline{v_{\beta}}
.
Hence the closed sets
\overline{v_{\beta}}
with
\beta<|w|
form

is Noetherian
(Theorem

), inclusion is well-founded on its closed

of

\overline{v_{\beta}}

We claim that

\gamma\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\beta+|u|

By induction hypothesis (indeed,

is a finite concatenation

∎

Lemma 10.3
.

The length of a topologically indecomposable transfinite word is
indecomposable.

Proof.

Let
w
be topologically indecomposable in
X^{<\alpha}
,
and let us assume that
|w|
is decomposable. We write
|w|
as

\beta+\gamma
, where
0<\beta,\gamma<|w|
, and then
w
as

uv
where
|u|=\beta
. We have
|v|=\gamma\neq 0
, so

v\neq\epsilon
. Since
w
is topologically indecomposable,
w
is
in
\overline{v}
. Now
v
is in
X^{<\gamma+1}
, which is closed, so

\overline{v}\subseteq X^{<\gamma+1}
. Thus
w
is in
X^{<\gamma+1}
, which is impossible since
\gamma<|w|
.
∎

Lemma 10.4
.

Let

\mathcal{C}_{2}

Proof.

Let
w
be topologically indecomposable. Since

and

. If

,
then

\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Otherwise, since

is

Conversely, let us assume that

or

for every concatenation of two closed sets

\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathcal{C}_{2}

∎

Lemma 10.5

Let

be a bound.
For every topologically indecomposable word

\overline{w}={(\mathrm{supp}\;{w})}^{<\gamma+1}

. Clearly,

is in

, so

\overline{w}\subseteq F^{<\gamma+1}
. Conversely,
\overline{w}
is

-product

. Since

Lemma 10.6
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian space,

be an ordinal, and
\alpha

or

. The map

It is well-defined and continuous by Lemma

. By

-products (resp.,

\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}P_{i}

\mathcal{C}_{2}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}Q_{j}

of

.

Lemma 10.7
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian space, and
\alpha
be a bound. For all sets
of transfinite words

and

.

We use Lemma

. If

Proposition 10.8
.

For every Noetherian space
X
and every bound
\alpha
, the regular
subword topology on
X^{<\alpha}
is the coarsest one such that

F^{<\gamma}
is closed for every closed subset
F
of
X
and every
ordinal
\gamma\leq\alpha
, and such that

\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathcal{C}_{2}
is closed for all closed subsets

\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
such that

\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq X^{<\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let us call admissible any topology
\tau
containing the sets

as closed sets, and closed under concatenations

\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathcal{C}_{2}
, as described above. The regular
subword topology is admissible, since

\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathcal{C}_{2}=cl(\mathcal{C}_{1})cl(\mathcal{C}_{2})=cl(\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathcal{C}_{2})
, by Lemma

. Given
any admissible topology

is finer than the regular subword

∎

There is a full, continuous map
\eta_{Y}\colon y\mapsto\mathop{\downarrow}\nolimits y
from
any space
Y
to
\mathcal{S}Y
. The specialization preordering
\leq
on
Y

is characterized by
y\leq y^{\prime}
if and only if
y\in\eta_{Y}(y^{\prime})
.
We note that
\eta_{X^{<\alpha}}(w)
is simply
\overline{w}
.

Theorem 10.9
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian space,
\alpha
be a bound. Let
\leq^{\mathrm{top}}_{*}
be
the specialization preordering of
X^{<\alpha}
.

(1)

For every

, one can write
w
as a finite
concatenation of topologically indecomposable words

w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
, and then

\eta_{X^{<\alpha}}(w)=\overline{w}=\overline{w_{1}}\cdots\overline{w_{n}}
, and

\overline{w_{i}}={(\mathrm{supp}\;{w_{i}})}^{<|w_{i}|+1}
for every
i
,

1\leq i\leq n
.

(2)

For all transfinite words
w\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}w_{1}\cdots w_{m}
and

w^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}w^{\prime}_{1}\cdots w^{\prime}_{n}
written as finite concatenations of
topologically indecomposable words,
w\leq^{\mathrm{top}}_{*}w^{\prime}
if and only if
there are indices

0=i_{0}\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n-1}\leq i_{n}=m
such that
for every
j
with
1\leq j\leq n
,

\bigcup_{i=i_{j-1}+1}^{i_{j}}\mathrm{supp}\;{w}_{i}\subseteq\mathrm{supp}\;{w^{\prime}_{j}}
and

|w_{i_{j-1}+1}|+\cdots+|w_{i_{j}}|\leq|w^{\prime}_{j}|
.

Proof.

(1) We write
w
as
w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
where each
w_{i}
is
topologically indecomposable by Lemma
10.2
.
By Lemma
10.7
(and since

\overline{\epsilon}=\varepsilon
in the base case

),

\overline{w}=\overline{w_{1}}\cdots\overline{w_{n}}
. Finally,

\overline{w_{i}}={(\mathrm{supp}\;{w_{i}})}^{<|w_{i}|+1}
by
Lemma
10.5
.

(2) Lemma
10.4
has the following consequence.
Given any closed set of the form
\mathcal{C}_{1}\mathcal{C}_{2}
with

\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
closed, for every transfinite word

in

, written as
a finite concatenation of topologically indecomposable words, there
is an index

with

and

w_{i+1}\cdots w_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Indeed, since

, there is an index
j
with

such that one can write

as
uv
, and

. Since

(2)),

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}cat(w_{1}\cdots w_{j-1},\_)^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
and

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{2}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}cat(\_,w_{j}\cdots w_{m})^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{2})

are closed. Now
w_{j}=uv\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{2}
, so

is in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1}
or in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{2}
; we define

as

j
in the first case, as

in the second case.

By induction on

, if

of closed subsets of

, we can find indices

for every

.

Let us assume that
w\leq^{\mathrm{top}}_{*}w^{\prime}
, namely

\overline{w}\subseteq\overline{w^{\prime}}
. By (1),

\overline{w_{1}}\cdots\overline{w_{m}}\subseteq\overline{w^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots\overline{w^{\prime}_{n}}
. Letting

\mathcal{C}_{j}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\overline{w^{\prime}_{j}}

Since

and Lemma

Conversely, if

How does this compare to the subword ordering

? By

,

. The

Example 10.10
.

Let

be

with the cofinite topology. Since its non-trivial

is Noetherian.
In order to see that
\leq^{\mathrm{top}}_{*}
and
\leq_{*}
differ, let

. For every non-empty suffix

of

,

. Hence

is

(1),

\overline{w}=X^{<\omega+1}
, so

.

For spaces

Let

11.
Conclusion and open problems

We have described a Noetherian topology on spaces of transfinite words
over a Noetherian space
X
, in particular on spaces
X^{<\alpha}
,
where
\alpha
is a bound. In the latter situation, we have
characterized its irreducible closed subsets, and given upper bounds
on the stature and reduced sobrification rank of
X^{<\alpha}
. We have also
characterized the specialization preordering
\leq^{\mathrm{top}}_{*}
of
X^{<\alpha}
.

Although we have not stressed it, the syntax of
\alpha
-products
naturally yields an S-representation of
X^{<\alpha}
, in the sense of

[
3
,
6
]
, provided we restrict our bounds to lie
in some class of ordinals with a computable representation, decidable
ordering, and decidable equality. S-representations are important in
forward analysis procedures for well-structured transition systems

[
2
]
.

We finish with two questions. First, it is frustrating that
\leq^{\mathrm{top}}_{*}
and

\leq_{*}
differ in general. Is there a natural, finer
Noetherian topology on
X^{<\alpha}
that
would have
\leq_{*}
as specialization preordering? The specialization
preordering of a Noetherian space is necessarily well-founded. Hence,
if the desired topology exists,
X^{<\alpha}
is well-founded under

\leq_{*}
. By an argument similar to
Lemma
10.2
, every non-empty word

w\in X^{<\alpha}
would have an indecomposable suffix, in other
words
X
would have to be
\beta
-better-quasi-ordered in the sense
of
[
12
, Definition IV-2]
or of
[
4
, Chapter 8,
5.1]
, for every
\beta<\alpha
.

Second, what is the exact stature of
X^{<\alpha}
? its reduced
sobrification rank?

Spectrally Simple Zeros of Zeon Polynomials
G. Stacey Staples

Abstract
Combinatorial properties of zeons have been applied to graph enumeration problems, graph colorings, routing problems in communication networks, partition-dependent stochastic integrals, and Boolean satisfiability. Power series of elementary zeon functions are naturally reduced to finite sums by virtue of the nilpotent properties of zeons. Further, the zeon extension of any analytic complex function has zeon polynomial representations on associated equivalence classes of zeons. In this paper, zeros of polynomials over complex zeons are considered. Existing results for real zeon polynomials are extended to the complex case and new results are established. In particular, a fundamental theorem of zeon algebra is established for spectrally simple zeros of complex zeon polynomials, and an algorithm is presented that allows one to find spectrally simple zeros when they exist. As an application, inverses of zeon extensions of analytic functions are computed using polynomial methods. Keywords: zeons; polynomials; fundamental theorem of algebra; spectral equivalence classes; analytic functions MSC2020: 13B25, 05E40, 15A66, 81R05

Generally, the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra does not hold for zeon polynomials. That is, if

Theorem 5.1

(Zeon Quadratic Formula)

\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\alpha\neq 0

\varphi

\varphi

(i.)

\varphi
are given by

for any
\eta\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}_{n}}
satisfying

.

(ii.)

If
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\Delta_{\varphi}\neq 0

\varphi(u)=0
has two distinct solutions.

(iii.)

If
\Delta_{\varphi}\neq 0
is nilpotent and
\varphi(u)=0
has a solution, then it has infinitely many solutions.

Proof.

Writing

\alpha u^{2}+\beta u+\gamma=\frac{\alpha^{-1}}{4}((2\alpha u+\beta)^{2}-(\beta^{2}-4\alpha\gamma)),

it follows that the zeros of the polynomial are precisely the elements

\varphi^{-1}(0)=\left\{\frac{\alpha^{-1}}{2}(w-\beta):w^{2}=\beta^{2}-4\alpha\gamma\right\}.

Thus, the problem of finding zeros of the polynomial is essentially reduced to finding square roots of the discriminant.

To establish Case (

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{-1}}{2}(w-\beta)\in\varphi^{-1}(0).

In Case (

∎

5.1
Spectrally Simple Zeros of Complex Zeon Polynomials

The main result of this section is a useful version of the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra for zeon polynomials. It not only guarantees the existence of a simple invertible zeon zero of
\varphi(u)
when the complex polynomial
f(u)=\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\varphi(u))
has a simple invertible zero, but it provides an algorithm for computing such a zero.

Letting
\varphi(u)
be a nonconstant monic zeon polynomial, we consider
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}_{n}}
to be a
simple
zero of
\varphi
if
\varphi(u)=(u-\lambda)g(\lambda)
for some zeon polynomial
g
satisfying
g(\lambda)\neq 0
.

Definition 5.3
.

A simple zero
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}_{n}}
of
\varphi(u)
is said to be a
spectrally simple
if
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda
is a simple zero of the complex polynomial
f=\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\varphi
.

With the notion of spectrally simple zeros in hand, a fundamental theorem of algebra for zeon polynomials can be presented.

Theorem 5.4

(Fundamental Theorem of Zeon Algebra)
.

Let
\varphi(u)
be a monic polynomial of degree
m
over
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}_{n}
, and let
f(u)=\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\varphi(u))
be the complex polynomial induced by
\varphi
. If
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
is a simple zero of
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\varphi(u))
, let
g(u)
be the unique complex polynomial satisfying
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\varphi(u))=(u-\lambda_{0})g(u)
. It follows that
\varphi(u)
has a simple zero
\lambda
such that
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda=\lambda_{0}
. In particular, for
1\leq k\leq n
, the grade-
k
part of
\lambda
(denoted
\lambda_{k}
) is given by

\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{g(\lambda_{0})}\left<\varphi\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}\right)\right>_{k}.

Moreover, such a zero
\lambda
is unique.

Proof.

Assuming that
\lambda_{0}
is a simple zero of
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\varphi(u))
, one can write

\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\varphi(u))=(u-\lambda_{0})g(u)

where
g
is a complex polynomial of degree
m-1
satisfying
g(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
.

Applying the polynomial division algorithm, there exist zeon polynomials
q
and
r
such that

\varphi(u)=(u-\lambda_{0})q(u)+r(u)

where
r(u)
is either zero or a zeon constant. If
r(u)=0
, then
\lambda_{0}
is a complex zero of
\varphi(u)
, and the proof is complete. Otherwise, it follows that

\varphi(\lambda_{0})=r(\lambda_{0})

and further, that
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(r(\lambda_{0}))=g(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
, so that
r(\lambda_{0})=r(u)
is invertible.

For convenience, set
\nu=-1/g(\lambda_{0})
, and let
w_{\natural}
denote the minimal grade part of

; i.e.,
w_{\natural}=\langle w\rangle_{\natural u}
. Replacing
\lambda_{0}
with
\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}
in the evaluation, we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}q(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural})+r(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(q(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}))+r(\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}(q(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}).

Letting
\ell_{0}=\natural\varphi(\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural})\rangle_{\ell_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(q(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}))+\langle r(\lambda_{0})\rangle_{\ell_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}g(\lambda_{0})+\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}+\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

It follows that either
\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural})=0
or
\natural\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural})>\natural\varphi(\lambda_{0})
. Proceeding inductively by setting

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0})_{\natural},

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1})_{\natural},\,\,(k>1)

and setting
\ell_{j}=\natural\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{j-1})
for

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k})\rangle_{\ell_{k-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1})_{\natural}\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(q(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1}))

\displaystyle+\langle r(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1})\rangle_{\ell_{k-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1})_{\natural}g(\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle+\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1})_{\natural}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1})_{\natural}+\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1})_{\natural}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Hence,
\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k})=0
or

\natural\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k})>\natural\varphi(\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1}).

Since the number of generators of
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}_{n}
is
n
, the process terminates with
\lambda=\displaystyle\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}
such that
\varphi(\lambda)=0
.

Uniqueness of the zero
\lambda
is trivial if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Uniqueness of nontrivial zeon zero

for monic zeon polynomial

is a simple zero of
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\varphi(u))

\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(q(\lambda_{0}))\neq 0

.
∎

Okounkov bodies associated to abundant divisors and Iitaka fibrations
Sung Rak Choi, Jinhyung Park, Joonyeong Won

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to study the Okounkov bodies associated to abundant divisors. As a main result, we prove that the valuative Okounkov bodies of an abundant divisor encode all the numerical properties. We apply this result to recover the asymptotic base loci of an abundant divisor from the valuative Okounkov bodies. We also give a criterion of when the valuative and limiting Okounkov bodies of an abundant divisor coincide by comparing their Euclidean volumes. To obtain these results, we prove some variants of Fujita’s approximations for Okounkov bodies using Iitaka fibrations.



1.
Introduction

Inspired by the work of Okounkov
[
O1
,
O2
]
, Lazarsfeld–Mustaţă
[
LM
]
and Kaveh–Khovanskii
[
KK
]
independently introduced and studied the convex sets called the Okounkov bodies associated to big divisors. Following their philosophy, there has been a number of attempts to understand the various asymptotic properties of divisors by analyzing the structure of the Okounkov bodies.
The details are as follows. We first let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
. For a divisor
D
on
X
, the
Okounkov body

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
is defined as a convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which clearly depends on
D
and also on the choice of the admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
(see Definition
2.1
). It is expected that one can extract various positivity properties of the divisor
D
from the structure of the Okounkov bodies. Based on the results on the Okounkov bodies of big divisors
[
LM
]
, we extended in
[
CHPW1
,
CPW1
,
CPW2
]
the study of Okounkov bodies to pseudoeffective divisors by introducing the
valuative Okounkov body

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and the
limiting Okounkov body

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
of a pseudoeffective divisor
D
(see Definition
2.2
). By definition,
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\subseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
holds in general and
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
when
D
is a big divisor. See Subsection
2.6
for more details.

By
[
LM
, Proposition 4.1 (i)]
and
[
J
, Theorem A]
, it is known that the Okounkov bodies are numerical in nature, i.e., two big divisors
D,D^{\prime}
on a smooth projective variety
X
are numerically equivalent if and only if
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})
for every admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
. This statement was extended to pseudoeffective divisors using the limiting Okounkov bodies in
[
CHPW1
, Theorem C]
. Thus theoretically one could read off all the numerical information of a given pseudoeffective divisor from its limiting Okounkov bodies. In contrasts, the valuative Okounkov bodies do not reflect the numerical properties of divisors in full as we observed in
[
CHPW1
, Remark 3.13]
.

The first aim of the paper is to show that as is often the case, imposing the “abundance condition” on divisors turns the valuative Okounkov bodies into numerical objects. In this paper, following
[
BDPP
]
,
[
CP
]
, we say that a divisor
D
is
abundant
if
\kappa(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
holds. Since
\kappa(D)\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\sigma}(D)\leq\kappa_{\nu}(D)
holds in general, our definition is weaker than the classical abundance which requires
\kappa(D)=\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
or
\kappa(D)=\kappa_{\nu}(D)
. We refer to Subsection
2.5
for the definitions of numerical Iitaka dimensions
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
,
\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
,
\kappa_{\nu}(D)
and to Subsection
2.7
for abundant divisors.

The following theorem is an extension of
[
LM
, Proposition 4.1 (i)]
and
[
J
, Theorem A]
to valuative Okounkov bodies of abundant divisors.

Theorem A

(=Corollary
4.11
)
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}
be pseudoeffective abundant
\mathbb{R}
-divisors on a smooth projective variety
X
. Then we have:

D\equiv D^{\prime}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ if and only if }\penalty\ \penalty\ \Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})\penalty\ \text{ for every admissible flag $Y_{\bullet}$ on $X$}.

We remark that the ‘only if’ direction of Theorem
A
does not hold when
D,D^{\prime}
are not abundant. It is because
\dim\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime\prime})=\kappa(D^{\prime\prime})
holds for any divisor
D^{\prime\prime}
while we may possibly have
\kappa(D)\neq\kappa(D^{\prime})
even when
D\equiv D^{\prime}
(see
[
CHPW1
, Remark 3.13]
). However, the ‘if’ direction of Theorem
A
holds without the abundance assumption on
D,D^{\prime}
(see Proposition
4.9
). As a consequence, we will also show in Corollary
4.12
that if
\Pic(X)
is finitely generated, then for any divisors
D,D^{\prime}
with
\kappa(D),\kappa(D^{\prime})\geq 0
, we have:

D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ if and only if }\penalty\ \penalty\ \Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})\penalty\ \text{ for every admissible flag $Y_{\bullet}$ on $X$}.

It is natural to ask how to extract the numerical properties of abundant divisors from the valuative Okounkov bodies. To give a partial answer to this question, we study the restricted base locus
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
(see Subsection
2.2
for the definition) of an abundant divisor
D
using the valuative Okounkov bodies. The analogous results of the following theorem for limiting Okounkov bodies was obtained in
[
CHPW2
, Theorem A]
(see also
[
KL1
,
KL2
,
KL3
]
).

Theorem B

(=Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
D
be a pseudoeffective abundant
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
n
, and
x\in X
be a point. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
.

(2)

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
does not contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for every admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
centered at
x
.

(3)

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
does not contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
centered at
x
.

As we observed in
[
CPW1
, Remark 4.10]
, without the abundance condition,
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
may not contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
even if
D
is nef. Note that the analogous statements concerning
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)
as in
[
CHPW2
, Theorem C]
for an abundant divisor
D
easily follow from
[
CHPW2
, Theorem 6.5]
since big divisors are abundant and
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)=X
holds if
D
is not big.

In
[
CHPW1
]
, we have seen that the Okounkov bodies
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
encode a good amount of asymptotic properties of the divisor
D
if the given admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
contains a Nakayama subvariety or a positive volume subvariety of
D
, respectively (see Subsection
2.6
for the definitions of these special subvarieties). For example, we have
\dim\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\kappa(D)
and
\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
for such admissible flags
Y_{\bullet}
.
Thus for the two Okounkov bodies
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
to coincide with each other, it is necessary to assume that
\kappa(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
, i.e.,
D
is an abundant divisor. In this case, we show in Proposition
2.15
that a subvariety is a Nakayama subvariety of
D
if and only if it is a positive volume subvariety of
D
.
However, even under the abundance condition, the inclusion
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\subseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
can be strict as was noticed in
[
CHPW1
, Example 4.2]
. By comparing the Euclidean volumes of the Okounkov bodies
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
, we obtain a criterion for the equality of these bodies.

Theorem C

(=Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
D
be a pseudoeffective abundant
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on an
n
-dimensional smooth projective variety
X
with
\kappa(D)>0
. Fix an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
such that
V=Y_{n-\kappa(D)}
is a Nakayama subvariety of
D
and
Y_{n}
is a general point in
X
.
Consider the Iitaka fibration
\phi\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
of
D
and the strict transform
V^{\prime}
of
V
on
X^{\prime}
. Then we have

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))=\deg(\phi|_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}\to Z)\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)).

In particular,
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
if and only if the map
\phi|_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}\to Z
is generically injective.

We remark that even if
D
is an abundant
\mathbb{R}
-divisor with
\kappa(D)>0
, there may not exist Nakayama subvarieties
V
giving rise to a generically injective map
\phi|_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}\to Z
(see Example
6.3
). See also Section 4 of
[
CHPW1
]
for more related results.

To prove all the above theorems, we use the results on Nakayama subvarieties and Iitaka fibrations (see Subsection
2.7
). Another key ingredients are some versions of Fujita’s approximations for the valuative Okounkov bodies
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
of an effective divisor
D
(Lemma
3.1
) and for the limiting Okounkov bodies
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
of an abundant divisor
D
(Lemma
3.6
). These results may be also regarded as alternative constructions of Okounkov bodies
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. We begin by collecting relevant basic facts on various asymptotic invariants, Iitaka fibrations, Zariski decompositions, Okounkov bodies, numerical Iitaka dimensions, etc. in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we prepare the main ingredients required for the proofs of Theorems
A
and
C
. Sections
4
,
5
, and
6
are devoted to proving Theorems
A
,
B
, and
C
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect relevant facts which will be used later.

2.1.
Conventions

Throughout the paper, we work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers.
By a
(
sub
)
variety
, we mean an irreducible (sub)variety, and
X
denotes a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
. Unless otherwise stated, a
divisor
means an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor. A divisor
D
on
X
is
pseudoeffective
if its numerical class
[D]\in N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
lies in the pseudoeffective cone
\overline{\Eff}(X)
, the closure of the cone spanned by effective divisor classes.
A divisor
D
on
X
is
big
if
[D]
lies in the interior
\text{Big}(X)
of
\overline{\Eff}(X)
.

2.2.
Asymptotic invariants

Let
D
be a divisor on
X
. The
stable base locus
of
D
is defined as
\SB(D):=\bigcap_{D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\geq 0}\Supp(D^{\prime})
.
The
augmented base locus
of
D
is defined as
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D):=\bigcap_{A}\text{SB}(D-A)

where the intersection is taken over all ample divisors
A
.
The
restricted base locus
of
D
is defined as
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D):=\bigcup_{A}\SB(D+A)

where the union is taken over all ample divisors
A
.
It is well known that
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)
and
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
depend only on the numerical class of
D
. Note that
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)=X
(resp.
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)=X
) if and only if
D
is not pseudoeffective (resp. not big), and
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)=\emptyset
(resp.
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)=\emptyset
) if and only if
D
is nef (resp. ample).
For more details, see
[
ELMNP1
]
.

Consider a subvariety
V\subseteq X
of dimension
v
.
The
restricted volume
of
D
along
V
is defined as

\displaystyle\vol_{X|V}(D):=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X|V,\lfloor mD\rfloor)}{m^{v}/v!}

where
h^{0}(X|V,\lfloor mD\rfloor)
is the dimension of the image of the natural restriction map
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mD\rfloor))\to H^{0}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}(\lfloor mD\rfloor|_{V}))
.
If
V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)
, then the restricted volume
\vol_{X|V}(D)
depends only on the numerical class of
D
, and it uniquely extends to a continuous function
\vol_{X|V}\colon\text{Big}^{V}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
where
\text{Big}^{V}(X)
is the set of all
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes
\xi
such that
V
is not properly contained in any irreducible component of
\mathbf{B}_{+}(\xi)
. When
V=X
, we simply let
\vol_{X}(D):=\vol_{X|X}(D)
, and we call it the
volume
of
D
.
For more details, we refer to
[
La
, Section 2.2.C]
,
[
ELMNP2
]
.

Now, assume that
V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
.
The
augmented restricted volume
of
D
along
V
is defined as

\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\vol_{X|V}(D+\varepsilon A)

where
A
is an ample divisor on
X
. The definition is independent of the choice of
A
.
Note that
\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D)=\vol_{X|V}(D)
for
D\in\text{Big}^{V}(X)
.
This also extends uniquely to a continuous function
\vol_{X|V}^{+}\colon\overline{\text{Eff}}^{V}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
where
\overline{\text{Eff}}^{V}(X):=\text{Big}^{V}(X)\cup\{\xi\in\overline{\text{Eff}}(X)\setminus\text{Big}(X)\mid V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{-}(\xi)\}
.
For
D\in\overline{\text{Eff}}^{V}(X)
, we have
\vol_{X|V}(D)\leq\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D)\leq\vol_{V}(D|_{V})
, and both inequalities can be strict in general.
For more details, see
[
CHPW1
, Subsection 2.3]
.

2.3.
Iitaka fibration

Let
D
be a divisor on
X
.
The
Iitaka dimension
of

\kappa(D):=\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\;\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mD\rfloor))}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}

, and

2.4.
Divisorial Zariski decompositions

To define the divisorial Zariski decomposition, we first consider a divisorial valuation
\sigma
on
X
with the center
V:=\operatorname{Cent}_{X}\sigma
on
X
.
If
D
is a big divisor on
X
, we define
the asymptotic valuation
of
\sigma
at
D
as

\ord_{V}(||D||):=\inf\{\sigma(D^{\prime})\mid D\equiv D^{\prime}\geq 0\}
.
If
D
is only a pseudoeffective divisor on
X
, we define

\displaystyle\ord_{V}(||D||):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\ord_{V}(||D+\varepsilon A||)

for some ample divisor
A
on
X
. This definition is independent of the choice of
A
. Note that
\ord_{V}(||D||)
is a numerical invariant of
D
.
The
divisorial Zariski decomposition
of a pseudoeffective divisor
D
is the decomposition

D=P_{\sigma}+N_{\sigma}=P_{\sigma}(D)+N_{\sigma}(D)

into the
negative part

N_{\sigma}=N_{\sigma}(D):=\sum_{E}\ord_{E}(||D||)E

where the summation is over all the finitely many prime divisors
E
of
X
such that
\ord_{E}(||D||)>0
and the
positive part

P_{\sigma}=P_{\sigma}(D):=D-N_{\sigma}
. The positive part
P_{\sigma}(D)
is characterized as the maximal divisor such that
P_{\sigma}\leq D
and
P_{\sigma}(D)
is movable (see
[
N
, Proposition III.1.14]
). Note that by construction
N_{\sigma}(D)
is a numerical invariant of
D
.
For more details, see
[
B1
]
,
[
N
]
,
[
P
]
.

Let
D
be a divisor on
X
with
\kappa(D)\geq 0
.
The
s
-decomposition
of
D
is the decomposition

D=P_{s}+N_{s}=P_{s}(D)+N_{s}(D)

into the
negative part

N_{s}=N_{s}(D):=\inf\{L\mid L\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D,L\geq 0\}
and the
positive part

P_{s}=P_{s}(D):=D-N_{s}
.
The positive part
P_{s}(D)
is characterized as the smallest divisor such that
P_{s}\leq D
and
R(X,P_{s})\simeq R(X,D)
(see
[
P
, Proposition 4.8]
). Note that
N_{s}(D)
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear equivalence invariant of
D
.
Note that
P_{s}(D)\leq P_{\sigma}(D)
and
P_{s}(D),P_{\sigma}(D)
do not coincide in general. If
D
is an abundant divisor (see Definition
2.11
), then
P_{s}(D)=P_{\sigma}(D)
so that
P_{s}(D),N_{s}(D)
become numerical invariants of
D
(see Theorem
2.13
(2)).
For more details, see
[
P
]
.

2.5.
Numerical Iitaka dimensions

Let
D
be a pseudoeffective divisor on
X
. There are several notions of the numerical Iitaka dimensions in the literature defined from different perspectives (see e.g.
[
BDPP
]
,
[
CP
]
,
[
E
]
,
[
Leh1
,
Leh2
]
,
[
Les
]
,
[
N
]
). Among them, the following dimension first introduced by Boucksom–Demailly–Păun–Peternell
[
BDPP
]
is of our most interest:

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D):=\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\langle D^{k}\rangle\neq 0\right.\right\}.

Here
\langle D^{k}\rangle
is the positive intersection product (see
[
Leh1
, Section 4]
for the definition and basic properties). By
[
Leh1
, Theorem 6.2]
(see also
[
CP
, Theorem 1.1]
), we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)&=&\max\left\{\dim W\left|\vol^{+}_{X|W}(L)>0\right.\right\}\\
&=&\max\left\{\dim W\left|\inf\limits_{\phi}\vol_{\widetilde{W}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)|_{\widetilde{W}})>0\right.\right\}\end{array}

where
W
range over all the irreducible subvarieties of
X
not contained in
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
, and
\phi\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{W})\to(X,W)
range over all
W
-birational models, which by definition mean that
W
is not contained in any
\phi
-exceptional center and
\widetilde{W}
is the strict transform of
W
(see
[

, Definition 2.10]
). We have
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\geq 0
whenever

is pseudoeffective. We put
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D):=-\infty
when
D
is not pseudoeffective. We will use the following basic properties of
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
:

(1)

\kappa(D)\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
.

(2)

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq n
, and
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=n
if and only if
D
is big.

(3)

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
is a numerical invariant of
D
, i.e.,
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D^{\prime})
whenever
D\equiv D^{\prime}
.

(4)

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(P_{\sigma}(D))
.

We refer to
[
CP
]
,
[
Leh1
]
,
[
N
]
for more detailed properties.

We recall some other numerical Iitaka dimensions defined for a pseudoeffective
D

\begin{array}[]{rl}\kappa_{\sigma}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\nu}(D)&:=\min\{\dim W\mid\text{$D$ does not numerically dominate $W$}\}\\
\kappa_{\vol}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}\end{array}

where
A
is a sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
. The first two dimensions
\kappa_{\sigma}
and
\kappa_{\nu}
were defined by Nakayama
[
N
, Chapter V]
(see
[
N
, Definition V.2.22]
for the definition of numerical dominance), and the third dimension
\kappa_{\vol}
is defined by Lehmann
[
Leh1
]
. They are also numerical invariants of
D
. Note that
\kappa_{\sigma}(D),\kappa_{\vol}(D),\kappa_{\nu}(D)
are nonnegative integers at most
n=\dim X
when
D
is pseudoeffective and
\kappa_{\sigma}(D),\kappa_{\vol}(D),\kappa_{\nu}(D)=n
if and only if
D
is big. By
[
CP
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\sigma}(D)\leq\kappa_{\nu}(D)\;\text{ and }\;\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol}(D)\leq\kappa_{\nu}(D).

It is worth noting that these numerical Iitaka dimensions
\kappa_{\sigma}(D),\kappa_{\vol}(D),\kappa_{\nu}(D)
can be strictly larger than
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
(see
[
Les
, Theorem 3]
,
[
CP
, Theorem 1.2]
).

2.6.
Okounkov bodies

Here we recall the construction and basic properties of Okounkov bodies associated to pseudoeffective divisors in
[
LM
]
,
[
KK
]
,
[
CHPW1
]
. Throughout this subsection, we fix an
admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}
on
X
, which by definition is a sequence of subvarieties

Y_{\bullet}:X=Y_{0}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Y_{n-1}\supseteq Y_{n}=\{x\}

where each
Y_{i}
is an irreducible subvariety of codimension
i
in
X
and is nonsingular at
x
.
Let
D
be a divisor on
X
with
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}:=\{D^{\prime}\mid D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\geq 0\}\neq\emptyset
.
We define a valuation-like function

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}\colon|D|_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}

as follows:
for
D^{\prime}\in|D|_{\mathbb{R}}
, let
\nu_{1}=\nu_{1}(D^{\prime}):=\ord_{Y_{1}}(D^{\prime})
.
Since
D^{\prime}-\nu_{1}(D^{\prime})Y_{1}
is effective and does not contain
Y_{2}
in the support, we define
\nu_{2}=\nu_{2}(D^{\prime}):=\ord_{Y_{2}}((D^{\prime}-\nu_{1}Y_{1})|_{Y_{1}})

We then inductively define
\nu_{i+1}=\nu_{i+1}(D^{\prime}):=\ord_{Y_{i+1}}((\cdots((D^{\prime}-\nu_{1}Y_{1})|_{Y_{1}}-\nu_{2}Y_{2})|_{Y_{2}}-\cdots-\nu_{i}Y_{i})|_{Y_{i}})
.
Thus we finally obtain

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime}):=(\nu_{1}(D^{\prime}),\nu_{2}(D^{\prime}),\ldots,\nu_{n}(D^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}

Definition 2.1
.

When
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}\neq\emptyset
, the
Okounkov body

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
of a divisor
D
on
X
with respect to an admdissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on

is defined as the closure of the convex hull of
\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(|D|_{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. When
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}=\emptyset
, we set
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D):=\emptyset
.

More generally, a similar construction can be applied to a graded linear series
W_{\bullet}
associated to a
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
to construct the Okounkov body
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(W_{\bullet})
of

with respect to
Y_{\bullet}
. For more details, we refer to
[
LM
]
.

In
[
LM
]
,
[
KK
]
, the Okounkov bodies

were mainly studied for big divisors. When

is not big, the following extension was introduced in
[
CHPW1
]
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
CHPW1
, Definition 1.1]
)
.

(1)

For a divisor
D
which is effective up to
\sim_{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.,
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}\neq\emptyset
, the
valuative Okounkov body

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
of

with respect to an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
is defined as the closure of the convex hull of
\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(|D|_{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.
If
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}=\emptyset
, then we set
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D):=\emptyset
.

(2)

For a pseudoeffective divisor
D
, the
limiting Okounkov body

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
of

with respect to an admissible flag

is defined as

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D+\varepsilon A)=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D+\varepsilon A)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

where

.
(Note that
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
is independent of the choice of

If

Note that we actually have

. However, we will only use the notation

on

.
Let
f_{m}^{*}D=P_{m}+N_{m}
be the
s
-decomposition.

Since
Y_{n}
is general, by taking the strict transforms
Y_{i}^{m}
of
Y_{i}
on
X_{m}
, we obtain an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}^{m}:Y_{0}^{m}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Y_{n}^{m}
on
X_{m}
.
We note that
U_{m}:=Y_{n-\kappa(D)}^{m}
is also a Nakayama subvariety of
f^{*}_{m}D
since
f_{m}
is
U
-birational (see
[
CHPW1
, Proposition 2.15]
). By definition, we see that
U_{m}
is also a Nakayama subvariety of
M_{m}
.

Let
W_{\bullet}
be a graded linear series on
U
associated to
D|_{U}
where
W_{k}
is the image of the natural injective map
H^{0}(X,\lfloor kD\rfloor)\to H^{0}(U,\lfloor kD\rfloor|_{U})
.
We also consider a graded linear series
W_{\bullet}^{m}
on
U_{m}
associated to
M_{m}|_{U_{m}}
where
W_{k}^{m}
is the image of the natural injective map
H^{0}(X_{m},\lfloor kM_{m}\rfloor)\to H^{0}(U_{m},\lfloor kM_{m}\rfloor|_{U_{m}})
.
Note that
\dim W_{m}=\dim W_{m}^{m}
.
Let
\phi_{m}\colon X_{m}\to Z_{m}
be the morphism defined by
|M_{m}^{\prime}|
.
Then there is an ample divisor
H_{m}
on
Z_{m}
such that
\phi_{m}^{*}H_{m}=M_{m}
.
Note that
\phi_{m}|_{U_{m}}\colon U_{m}\to Z_{m}
is a surjective morphism of projective varieties of the same dimension
\kappa(D)
.
Since
Y_{n}
is general, we can assume that
\overline{Y}_{\bullet}^{m}:Z_{m}=\phi_{m}(Y_{n-\kappa(D)}^{m})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\phi_{m}(Y_{n}^{m})
is an admissible flag on
Z_{m}
.

The following lemma is the main result of this subsection.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the same notations as above, we have

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{\overline{Y}_{\bullet}^{m}}(H_{m}).

Proof.

As we noted above, we treat
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D),\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}),
and
\Delta_{\overline{Y}_{\bullet}^{m}}(H_{m})
as the subsets of the same fixed space
\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}
.
By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.5
, we have

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(f_{m}^{*}D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m}),

and by
[
CHPW1
, Remark 3.11]
and
[
CPW2
, Lemma 5.1]
, we have

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})=\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet}^{m})=\Delta_{\overline{Y}_{\bullet}^{m}}(H_{m}).

Note that
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})\subseteq\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m})
.
By
[
CHPW1
, Remark 3.11]
, we also have

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta_{Y_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet}).

By applying
[
LM
, Remark 2.8, Theorems 2.13 and 3.3]
, we see that

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta_{Y_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet}))=\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet}^{m})).

As
\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet}^{m})\subseteq\Delta_{Y_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet})
, we obtain

\Delta_{Y_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\kappa(D)\bullet}}(W_{\bullet}^{m}).

Thus the assertion now follows.
∎

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor)=H^{0}(W,\lfloor\mu^{*}(mD)\rfloor)=H^{0}(W,\lfloor mP^{\prime}\rfloor)\\

.

. By taking pullback via

\displaystyle P_{m}^{W}=\lim_{m^{\prime}\to\infty}f_{m^{\prime},m*}^{W}M_{m^{\prime}}^{W}

H
on

. Then we have

M_{m}^{W}\leq P_{m}^{W}\leq M_{m}^{W}+\frac{1}{m}(f_{m}^{W})^{*}\mu^{*}H.

f_{m}^{\prime}\colon Z\to X
of

and the log resolution

Remark 3.5
.

When
D
is a big divisor, one can easily deduce the classical statement of Fujita’s approximation (see e.g.,
[
La
, Theorem 11.4.4]
) from Lemma
3.4
.

The following is the main result of this subsection. This generalizes
[
LM
, Theorem D]
to the limiting Okounkov body case.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under the same notations as above, we have

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{Y_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}}).

Proof.

We treat
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta_{Y_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})
in the statement as the subsets of the same fixed space
\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}
.
For any sufficiently large
m^{\prime}>0
, by Lemmas
2.3
and
2.5
, we have

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m^{\prime}}}(f_{m^{\prime}}^{*}D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m^{\prime}}}(P_{m^{\prime}}).

Thus
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m^{\prime}}}(P_{m^{\prime}})
is independent of
{m^{\prime}}
.
By
[
CPW2
, Lemma 5.5]
, for any
m>0
, we have

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}}).

To prove the lemma, it is sufficient to verify
\displaystyle\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m^{\prime}}}(P_{m^{\prime}})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})
.

By Lemma
3.4
, for any sufficiently large integer
m>0
, we have

M_{m}\leq P_{m}\leq M_{m}+\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H

for some ample divisor
H
on
X
. Since
x\in X
is general, we may assume
x\not\in\Supp(H)
.
By the subadditivity property of limiting Okounkov bodies,

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m}-M_{m})+\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H+M_{m}-P_{m})\subseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H)=\frac{1}{m}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(H).

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(H)=\{0\}
, it follows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m}-M_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}\left(\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H+M_{m}-P_{m}\right)=\{0\}.

By the subadditivity property of limiting Okounkov bodies,

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})+\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m}-M_{m})\subseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m})\\
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m})+\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H+M_{m}-P_{m})\subseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}+\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H).\end{array}

Since
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}
and
Y^{m}_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}(M_{m}+\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H)
, it follows from Lemma
3.4
and
[
CPW2
, Theorem 1.1]
that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})\subseteq\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m})\subseteq\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\bullet}}\left(M_{m}+\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H\right).

The existence of the limits is guaranteed by the following claim:

(3.3)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}))=\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}}\left(\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\bullet}}\left(M_{m}+\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H\right)\right).

If this claim (
3.3
) holds, then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\bullet}}\left(M_{m}+\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H\right).

As we saw in the beginning of the proof,
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m})
coincide with
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
for all sufficiently large
m>0
. Thus we have

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}).

It now remains to prove the claim (
3.3
). We may assume that
V_{m}:=Y_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}^{m}
is a smooth positive volume subvariety of
M_{m}
, and
f_{m}|_{V_{m}}\colon V_{m}\to V
is a birational contraction. By
[
CPW2
, Lemma 5.5]
, we have

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m}))=\frac{1}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)!}\vol_{V_{m}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})=\frac{1}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)!}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}.

Similarly, by
[
LM
, (2.7) in p.804]
, we also have

\begin{array}[]{l}\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}}\left(\Delta_{Y^{m}_{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\bullet}}\left(M_{m}|_{V_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}(f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}}\right)\right)\\
=\frac{1}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)!}\vol_{V_{m}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}(f_{m}^{*}H)_{V_{m}})\\
=\frac{1}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)!}\left(M_{m}|_{V_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}(f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}}\right)^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}\\
=\frac{1}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)!}\left((M_{m}|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}+\sum_{k=0}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1}\frac{{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\choose k}}{m^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-k}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})^{k}\cdot((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-k}\right).\end{array}

To prove the claim (
3.3
), it is sufficient to show that for each
0\leq k\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1
, there exists a constant
C_{k}
independent of
m
such that

(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})^{k}\cdot((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-k}\leq C_{k}.

If
k=0
, then we have
((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}=((f_{m}|_{V_{m}})^{*}(H|_{V}))^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}=(H|_{V})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}
, which is independent of
m
. Now, suppose that
1\leq k\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1
.
Note that
V_{m}\not\subseteq\SB(f_{m}^{*}D)
and
M_{m}|_{V_{m}}\leq(f_{m}^{*}D)|_{V_{m}}
. Thus

M_{m}|_{V_{m}}\cdot((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1}\leq(f_{m}^{*}D)|_{V_{m}}\cdot((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1}=D|_{V}\cdot(H|_{V})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1}.

By a Hodge-type inequality
[
La
, Corollary 1.6.3 (i)]
, we have

(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})^{k}\cdot((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-k}\leq\frac{(M_{m}|_{V_{m}}\cdot((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1})^{k}}{((f_{m}^{*}H)|_{V_{m}})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)})^{k-1}}\leq\frac{(D|_{V}\cdot(H|_{V})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)-1})^{k}}{((H|_{V})^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)})^{k-1}}.

Note that the right hand side is independent of
m
. This proves the claim (
3.3
). We complete the proof.
∎

4.
Numerical equivalence and Okounkov body

In this section, we prove Theorem
A
as Corollary
4.11
. Throughout the section,
X
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
. First, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be a birational morphism with
\widetilde{X}
smooth, and
D
be a divisor on
X
with
\kappa(D)\geq 0
. Consider an admissible flag

\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}:\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{Y}_{0}\supseteq\widetilde{Y}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\widetilde{Y}_{n-1}\supseteq\widetilde{Y}_{n}=\{x^{\prime}\}

on
\widetilde{X}
and an admissible flag

on
X
such that each restriction
f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}}\colon\widetilde{Y}_{i}\to Y_{i}
is a birational morphism for
0\leq i\leq n
.
Assume that
Y_{i}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
are smooth for
0\leq i\leq n
.
For
1\leq i\leq n
, write
f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}}^{*}Y_{i}=\widetilde{Y}_{i}+E_{i}
for some effective
f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}}
-exceptional divisor

on
\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}
.
Then we have

\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(f^{*}D)=\left\{\mathbf{x}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\cdot\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{i\bullet}}(E_{i}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}})\penalty\ \left|\;\penalty\ \mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\right.\right\}

where we regard
\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{i\bullet}}(E_{i}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}})
as a point in
\{0\}^{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In particular,
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(f^{*}D)
determine each other.

Proof.

We can canonically identify
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}
with
|f^{*}D|_{\mathbb{R}}
. For any
D^{\prime}\in|D|_{\mathbb{R}}
, let

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ \nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(f^{*}D^{\prime})=(\widetilde{\nu}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\nu}_{n}).

Since
\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(|D|_{\mathbb{R}}|
and
\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(|f^{*}D|_{\mathbb{R}})
are dense subsets of
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(f^{*}D)
, respectively, it is sufficient to show that

(4.1)

(\widetilde{\nu}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\nu}_{n})=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\nu_{i}\cdot\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{i\bullet}}(E_{i}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}}).

Let
D_{1}^{\prime}:=D^{\prime}
on
X=Y_{0}
, and define inductively
D_{i}^{\prime}:=(D_{i-1}^{\prime}-\nu_{i-1}Y_{i-1})|_{Y_{i-1}}
on
Y_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq n
. Similarly, let
\widetilde{D}_{1}^{\prime}:=f^{*}D^{\prime}
on
\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{Y}_{0}
, and define inductively
\widetilde{D}_{i}^{\prime}:=(\widetilde{D}_{i-1}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\nu}_{i-1}\widetilde{Y}_{i-1})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}}
on
\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq n
. Then
\nu_{i}=\ord_{Y_{i}}D_{i}^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\nu}_{i}=\ord_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}}\widetilde{D}_{i}^{\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
First of all, observe that the first coordinates of both sides in (
4.1
) are
\widetilde{\nu}_{1}
and
\nu_{1}
and
\widetilde{\nu}_{1}=\nu_{1}
.
As
\widetilde{Y}_{1}=f^{*}Y_{1}-E_{1}
, we get

\widetilde{D}_{2}^{\prime}=(f^{*}D_{1}^{\prime}-\nu_{1}\widetilde{Y}_{1})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}=(f^{*}(D_{1}^{\prime}-\nu_{1}Y_{1})+\nu_{1}E_{1})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}=f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}D_{2}^{\prime}+\nu_{1}E_{1}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}.

Then we have

\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{1\bullet}}(\widetilde{D}_{2}^{\prime})=\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{1\bullet}}(f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}D_{2}^{\prime})+\nu_{1}\cdot\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{1\bullet}}(E_{1}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}).

Note that
\ord_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}\widetilde{D}_{2}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\nu}_{2}
and
\ord_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}D_{2}^{\prime}=\nu_{2}
. Thus (
4.1
) holds for the second coordinates. Now, as
\widetilde{Y}_{2}=f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}Y_{2}-E_{2}
, we get

(f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}D_{2}^{\prime}-\nu_{2}\widetilde{Y}_{2})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}=(f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}(D_{2}^{\prime}-\nu_{2}Y_{2})+\nu_{2}E_{2})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}=f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}^{*}D_{3}^{\prime}+\nu_{2}E_{2}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}},

Then we have

\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{2\bullet}}((f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}D_{2}^{\prime}-\nu_{2}\widetilde{Y}_{2})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}})=\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{2\bullet}}(f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}^{*}D_{3}^{\prime})+\nu_{2}\cdot\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{2\bullet}}(E_{2}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}).

Note that

\ord_{\widetilde{Y}_{3}}(f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}}^{*}D_{2}^{\prime}-\nu_{2}\widetilde{Y}_{2})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}+
the third coordinate of
\nu_{1}\cdot\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{1\bullet}}(E_{1}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{1}})=\widetilde{\nu}_{3}

and
\ord_{\widetilde{Y}_{3}}f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2}}^{*}D_{3}^{\prime}=\nu_{3}
. Thus (
4.1
) holds for the third coordinates.
In general, we have

\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{i\bullet}}((f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}}^{*}D_{i}^{\prime}-\nu_{i}\widetilde{Y}_{i})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}})=\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{i\bullet}}(f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}}^{*}D_{i+1}^{\prime})+\nu_{i}\cdot\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{i\bullet}}(E_{i}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for $2\leq i\leq n-1$. }

Note that

\ord_{\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}}(f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}}^{*}D_{i}^{\prime}-\nu_{i}\widetilde{Y}_{i})|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}}+
the
(i+1)
-th coordinate of
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\nu_{j}\cdot\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{j\bullet}}(E_{j}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{j}})=\widetilde{\nu}_{i+1}

and

\ord_{\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}}f|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i}}^{*}D_{i+1}^{\prime}=\nu_{i+1}
.
Thus we obtain (
4.1
).

We first prove the ‘only if’ direction of Theorem
A
, which is a generalization of
[

to (possibly non-big) abundant divisors.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}
be divisors on
X
with
\kappa(D),\kappa(D^{\prime})\geq 0
. Suppose that
D
or
D^{\prime}
is an abundant divisor. If
D\equiv D^{\prime}
, then
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})
for every admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.13
(1), both
D,D^{\prime}
are abundant divisors.
Fix an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
. Possibly by taking a higher birational model of
X
, we may assume that each subvariety
Y_{i}
in
Y_{\bullet}
is smooth (see Remark
2.4
).
By Theorem
2.12
, there is a birational morphism
\mu\colon W\to X
and a morphism
g\colon W\to T
with connected fibers such that
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}P_{\sigma}(g^{*}B)
for some big divisor
B
on
T
.
Thus
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})|_{F}\equiv 0
for a general fiber
F
of
g
, and hence,
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
since
\kappa(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})=\kappa(D^{\prime})\geq 0
. This implies that
\kappa_{\sigma}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})|_{F})=\kappa(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})|_{F})=0
. By taking a higher birational model of
W
if necessary, by
[
N
, Corollary V.2.26]
(see also
[
Leh2
, Theorem 5.7]
),
we may assume that
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}P_{\sigma}(g^{*}B^{\prime})
for some divisor
B^{\prime}
on
T
.
Applying
[
N
, Lemma III.5.15]
(see also
[
Leh2
, Proof of Corollary 6.3]
), we see that
P_{\sigma}(B)\equiv P_{\sigma}(B^{\prime})
and
B^{\prime}
is also a big divisor on
T
. We also have
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}P_{\sigma}(g^{*}B)=P_{\sigma}(g^{*}P_{\sigma}(B))
and
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}P_{\sigma}(g^{*}B^{\prime})=P_{\sigma}(g^{*}P_{\sigma}(B^{\prime}))
. We write
P_{\sigma}(B)=P_{\sigma}(B^{\prime})+N
for some numerically trivial divisor
N
on
T
.
Then we have

P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}P_{\sigma}(g^{*}P_{\sigma}(B))=P_{\sigma}(g^{*}P_{\sigma}(B^{\prime}))+g^{*}N\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})+g^{*}N.

By successively taking strict transforms
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
of
Y_{i}
under the birational morphisms
\mu|_{\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}}\colon\widetilde{Y}_{i-1}\to Y_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, we obtain an admissible flag

\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}:W=\widetilde{Y}_{0}\supseteq\widetilde{Y}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\widetilde{Y}_{n-1}\supseteq\widetilde{Y}_{n}

on
W
. Possibly by taking a higher birational model of
W
, we may assume that each subvariety of
\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}
is smooth. By Theorem
2.13
(2), we have
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D)=P_{s}(\mu^{*}D)
and
P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})=P_{s}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})
.
By Lemmas
2.5
and
4.1
, it is sufficient to show that

\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D))=\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})).

Now, take an ample divisor
A
on
T
so that
A+kN
is also an ample divisor for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Choose a large integer

such that
aP_{\sigma}(B^{\prime})-A\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E^{\prime}
for some effective divisor
E^{\prime}
on
T
.
Then
aP_{\sigma}(g^{*}P_{\sigma}(B^{\prime}))-g^{*}A\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E
for some effective divisor
E
on
W
.
For any integer
m>0
, we have

(m+a)P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(m+a)(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})+g^{*}N)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}mP_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})+E+g^{*}(A+(m+a)N).

By the subadditivity property of the valuative Okounkov bodies, we have

\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D))\supseteq\frac{m}{m+a}\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime}))+\frac{1}{m+a}\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(E)+\frac{1}{m+a}\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}((g^{*}(A+(m+a)N))).

for

is contained in

\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D))\supseteq\frac{m}{m+a}\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime}))+\frac{1}{m+a}\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(E).

By letting

, we see that

\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D))\supseteq\Delta^{\val}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D^{\prime})).

Similarly by replacing

by
D^{\prime}
and

by
-N

Remark 4.3
.

Obviously, Proposition
4.2
does not hold without the assumption that
D
or
D^{\prime}
is an abundant divisor (see
[
CHPW1
, Remark 3.13]
).

For the converse of Proposition
4.2
, we need several lemmas.

Lemma 4.4
.

Consider two surjective morphisms
f_{1}\colon X\to Z_{1}
and
f_{2}\colon X\to Z_{2}
with connected fibers. Suppose that
\dim Z_{1}=\dim Z_{2}>0
and
f_{1},f_{2}
are not birationally equivalent. Then for a general member
G\in|H|
where
H
is a very ample divisor on
Z_{1}
, the inverse image
f_{1}^{-1}(G)
dominates
Z_{2}
via
f_{2}
, i.e., we have
f_{2}(f_{1}^{-1}(G))=Z_{2}
.

Proof.

Notice that
|f_{1}^{*}H|
is a base point linear system.
Thus we may assume that
f_{1}^{-1}(G)=f_{1}^{*}G\in|f_{1}^{*}H|
is a general member so that
f_{1}^{-1}(G)
is a prime divisor on
X
. Suppose that
f_{2}(f_{1}^{-1}(G))
does not dominate
Z_{2}
via
f_{2}
. Then
f_{2}(f_{1}^{-1}(G))
is contained in a prime divisor
D
on
Z_{2}
. We then have
f_{1}^{*}G\leq f_{2}^{*}D
, so

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mf_{1}^{*}G))\subseteq H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mf_{2}^{*}D))\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for any integer $m>0$.}

In particular,
D
is a big divisor on
Z_{2}
. Consider a rational map
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Z^{\prime}
given by
|mf_{2}^{*}D|
for a sufficiently large and divisible integer
m>0
. The rational map
\varphi
factors through
Z_{2}
via a birational map
\varphi^{\prime}\colon Z_{2}\dashrightarrow Z^{\prime}
given by
|mD|
, and
\varphi
and
f_{2}
are birationally equivalent.
Since
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mf_{1}^{*}G))\subseteq H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mf_{2}^{*}D))
, there is a rational map
\pi\colon Z^{\prime}\dashrightarrow Z_{1}
. Note that
\pi\circ\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Z_{1}
is birationally equivalent to
f_{1}\colon X\to Z_{1}
. As
f_{1}
has connected fibers,
\pi
is birational and so is
\pi\circ\varphi^{\prime}\colon Z_{2}\dashrightarrow Z_{1}
. This implies that
f_{1},f_{2}
are birationally equivalent, so we get a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}
be divisors on
X
with
\kappa(D),\kappa(D^{\prime})>0
. If
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})
for every admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
, then the Iitaka fibrations of

are all birationally equivalent.

Proof.

Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a birational morphism with the Iitaka fibrations
\phi\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
of
D
and
\phi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
of
D^{\prime}
.
Since
\dim\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\kappa(D)
for any admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
, we have
\kappa(D)=\kappa(D^{\prime})
so that
\dim Z=\dim Z^{\prime}
.
To derive a contradiction, suppose that
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
are not birationally equivalent. By Lemma
4.4
, for a general member
G\in|H|
where
H
is a very ample divisor on
Z
, the inverse image
\phi^{-1}(G)
dominates
Z^{\prime}
via
\phi^{\prime}
.
We can take a general subvariety

is a Nakayama subvariety of

.

,

is also a Nakayama subvariety of

. However,

does not dominate

via

.

∎

1\leq i\leq n-1
. By letting
Y_{n}:=\{x\}
where
x
is a general point on
C
, we obtain an admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}:X=Y_{0}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Y_{n-1}\supseteq Y_{n}=\{x\}

on
X
. Since
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n-\kappa(D)}
are general effective very ample divisors,
Y_{n-\kappa(D)}
is a Nakayama subvariety of
D
by
[
CHPW1
, Proposition 2.13]
. Thus this admissible flag

satisfies the conditions in the statement.

For a sufficiently large integer
m>0
, take a log resolution
f_{m}\colon X_{m}\to X
of the base ideal
\mathfrak{b}(\lfloor mD\rfloor)
so that we obtain a decomposition
f_{m}^{*}(\lfloor mD\rfloor)=M_{m}^{\prime}+F_{m}^{\prime}
into a base point free divisor
M_{m}^{\prime}
on
X_{m}
and the fixed part
F_{m}^{\prime}
of
|f_{m}^{*}(\lfloor mD\rfloor)|
. Let
M_{m}:=\frac{1}{m}M_{m}^{\prime}
and
F_{m}:=\frac{1}{m}F_{m}^{\prime}
. Let
\phi_{m}\colon X_{m}\to Z_{m}
be a morphism given by
|M_{m}^{\prime}|
. By taking a higher birational model of
Z_{m}
, we may assume that
Z_{m}
is a smooth variety.
There exists a nef and big divisor
H_{m}
on
Z_{m}
such that
M_{m}=\phi_{m}^{*}H_{m}
.
Since our choice of admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
is independent of this process, we may assume that
f_{m}\colon X_{m}\to X
is isomorphic over a neighborhood of
x
. Let
C_{m}
be the strict transform of
C
on
X_{m}
.
By taking strict transforms
Y_{i}^{m}
of
Y_{i}
on
X_{m}
for each
0\leq i\leq n-1
(note that
Y_{n-1}^{m}=C_{m}
), we obtain an admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}^{m}:X_{m}=Y_{0}^{m}\supseteq Y_{1}^{m}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Y_{n-1}^{m}\supseteq Y_{n}^{m}=\{f_{m}^{-1}(x)\}

on
X_{m}
. We may also assume that

\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}:Z_{m}=\overline{Y}^{m}_{0}=\phi_{m}(Y_{n-\kappa(D)}^{m})\supseteq\overline{Y}^{m}_{1}=\phi_{m}(Y_{n-\kappa(D)-1}^{m})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\overline{Y}^{m}_{\kappa(D)-1}=\phi_{m}(Y_{n-1}^{m})\supseteq\overline{Y}^{m}_{\kappa(D)}=\phi_{m}(Y_{n}^{m})

is an admissible flag on
Z_{m}
. Note that

. Then
M_{m}\cdot C_{m}=d\cdot(H_{m}\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m}))
. By
[
CPW2

, we have

H_{m}\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m})=\vol_{Z_{m}|\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-1}}(H_{m})=\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta_{\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}}(H_{m})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\kappa(D)-1}=0}).

We now prove that
\displaystyle P_{s}\cdot C=\lim_{m\to\infty}M_{m}\cdot C_{m}
. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}
be the divisorial components of
\SB(D)
. Since the closure of
\SB(D)\setminus(E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{k})
has codimension at least two in
X
, we may assume that
C\cap\SB(D)\subseteq E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{k}
. We can also assume that
C
is smooth and meets all
E_{i}
transversally at smooth points of
E_{i}
. Thus
C_{m}
does not meet any effective
f_{m}
-exceptional divisor. We write

f_{m}^{*}\frac{\lfloor mP_{s}\rfloor}{m}=M_{m}+e_{1}^{m}f_{m*}^{-1}E_{1}+\cdots+e_{k}^{m}f_{m*}^{-1}E_{k}+F_{m}^{\prime\prime}

where
F_{m}^{\prime\prime}
is an effective
f_{m}
-exceptional divisor. We have

\frac{\lfloor mP_{s}\rfloor}{m}\cdot C=f_{m}^{*}\frac{\lfloor mP_{s}\rfloor}{m}\cdot C_{m}=M_{m}\cdot C_{m}+e_{1}^{m}E_{1}\cdot C+\cdots+e_{k}^{m}E_{k}\cdot C.

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}e_{i}^{m}=0
for each
1\leq i\leq k
and
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\lfloor mP_{s}\rfloor}{m}\cdot C=P_{s}\cdot C
, it follows that
\displaystyle P_{s}\cdot C=\lim_{m\to\infty}M_{m}\cdot C_{m}
as desired.

Combining what we have obtained above, we find

P_{s}\cdot C=d\cdot\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta_{\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}}(H_{m})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\kappa(D)-1}}).

To prove the lemma, it is sufficient to show that

(4.2)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}}(H_{m})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\kappa(D)-1}=0}=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0}.

By definition,
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}}(H_{m})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\kappa(D)-1}=0}\subseteq\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0}
holds. To derive a contradiction, suppose that this inclusion is strict.
For a sufficiently large integer
m_{0}>0
, we can choose a small ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
A_{m_{0}}
on
Z_{m_{0}}
such that

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta_{\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}}(H_{m})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\kappa(D)-1}})+A_{m_{0}}\cdot\phi_{m_{0}}(C_{m_{0}})<\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0})-\varepsilon

for any sufficiently small number
\varepsilon>0
and any sufficiently large integer
m>m_{0}
.
There exists a sufficiently small number
\delta>0
such that all the following divisors

\begin{array}[]{rcl}A_{m_{0},1}=A_{m_{0},1}(\delta_{1})&\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}&A_{m_{0}}+\delta_{1}\overline{Y}^{m_{0}}_{1},\\
A_{m_{0},2}=A_{m_{0},2}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})&\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}&A_{m_{0},1}|_{\overline{Y}^{m_{0}}_{1}}+\delta_{2}\overline{Y}^{m_{0}}_{2},\\
&\vdots&\\
A_{m_{0},\kappa(D)-1}=A_{m_{0},\kappa(D)-1}(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\kappa(D)-1})&\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}&A_{m_{0},\kappa(D)-2}|_{\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m_{0}}}+\delta_{\kappa(D)-1}\overline{Y}^{m_{0}}_{\kappa(D)-1}\end{array}

are ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors for any nonnegative rational numbers
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{\kappa(D)-1}\leq\delta
.
By Lemma
3.1
,
\displaystyle\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(M_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta_{\overline{Y}_{\bullet}^{m}}(H_{m})
.
Thus there exist a sufficiently large integer
m>0
and an effective divisor
H_{m}^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}H_{m}
on
Z_{m}
such that if we write
\nu_{\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}}(H_{m}^{\prime})=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\kappa(D)-1},b)
, then
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{\kappa(D)-1},b
are nonnegative rational numbers with
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{\kappa(D)-1}\leq\delta
and
\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0})-\varepsilon\leq b
.
We can write

\begin{array}[]{rcl}H_{m}^{\prime}&=&H_{m,1}+\delta_{1}\overline{Y}_{1}^{m},\\
H_{m,1}|_{\overline{Y}_{1}^{m}}&=&H_{m,2}+\delta_{2}\overline{Y}_{2}^{m},\\
&\vdots&\\
H_{m,\kappa(D)-2}|_{\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m}}&=&H_{m,\kappa(D)-1}+\delta_{\kappa(D)-1}\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-1}^{m}\end{array}

where each
H_{m,i}
is an effective divisor on
\overline{Y}_{i-1}^{m}
for
1\leq i\leq\kappa(D)-1
. Notice that

H_{m,\kappa(D)-1}\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m})=H_{m,\kappa(D)-1}\cdot\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-1}^{m}\geq b
.
By taking common resolution, we can assume that there is a birational morphism
g_{m}\colon Z_{m}\to Z_{m_{0}}
such that
\overline{Y}_{i}^{m}=g_{m}|_{\overline{Y}_{i-1}^{m}}^{*}\overline{Y}_{i}^{m_{0}}
for every
1\leq i\leq\kappa(D)
.
Note that
H_{m}+g_{m}^{*}A_{m_{0}}+B
is an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Z_{m}
for any ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
on
Z_{m}
. We may assume that
\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m}\not\subseteq\Supp(B)
. Thus we can find an effective divisor
E\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}H_{m}+g_{m}^{*}A_{m_{0}}+B
such that

E|_{\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m}}=H_{m,\kappa(D)-1}+g_{m}|_{\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m}}^{*}A_{m_{0},\kappa(D)-1}+B|_{\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m}}

where
A_{m_{0},\kappa(D)-1}=A_{m_{0},\kappa(D)-1}(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\kappa(D)-1})
. Then we obtain

(H_{m}+g_{m}^{*}A_{m_{0}}+B)\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m})=E\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m})=(H_{m,\kappa(D)-1}+g_{m}|_{\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m}}^{*}A_{m_{0},\kappa(D)-1}+B|_{\overline{Y}_{\kappa(D)-2}^{m}})\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m})>b.

As

can be an arbitrarily small ample divisor, we get
(H_{m}+g_{m}^{*}A_{m_{0}})\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m})\geq b
. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta_{\overline{Y}^{m}_{\bullet}}(H_{m})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\kappa(D)-1}})+A_{m_{0}}\cdot\phi_{m_{0}}(C_{m_{0}})=(H_{m}+g_{m}^{*}A_{m_{0}})\cdot\phi_{m}(C_{m})\\
\geq b\geq\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0})-\varepsilon,\end{array}

which is a contradiction. Therefore, we obtain (
4.2
) as required.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Here we explain why Lemma
4.6
can be considered as a generalization of Jow’s result
[
J
, Corollary 3.3 and Theorem 3.4 (b)]
, which states that if
D
is a big divisor on
X
and
Y_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
X
whose subvarieties are transversal complete intersections of general effective very ample divisors on
X
, then

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0})=D\cdot Y_{n-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{p\in Y_{n-1}\cap E_{i}}\ord_{E_{i}}(||D||)

where
E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}
are irreducible components of
\SB(D)
. Since
Y_{n-1}
is a sufficiently general curve, we may assume that
Y_{n-1}
is smooth and meets all
E_{i}
transversally at smooth points of
E_{i}
. Thus Jow’s result can be also expressed equivalently as

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{p\in Y_{n-1}\cap E_{i}}\ord_{E_{i}}(||D||)=N_{\sigma}(D)\cdot Y_{n-1}

so that
\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}(\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0})=P_{\sigma}(D)\cdot Y_{n-1}
. Note that for any big divisor
D
,
P_{\sigma}(D)=P_{s}(D)
and the identity map
\text{id}_{X}\colon X\to X
is an Iitaka fibration of
D
. Thus Lemma
4.6
recovers Jow’s result.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
D
be a divisor on
X
with
\kappa(D)>0
, and
D=P_{s}+N_{s}
be the
s
-decomposition. Let
E
be an irreducible component of

. Then we have

\mult_{E}N_{s}=\inf\{x_{1}\mid(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D),\text{ $Y_{\bullet}$ is an admissible flag such that $Y_{1}=E$}\}.

In particular, one can read off the negative part
N_{s}
from the set

\{\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\mid Y_{\bullet}\text{ is an admissible flag on $X$}\}.

Proof.

By the definition of
s
-decomposition, we have

\inf\{x_{1}\mid(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(P_{s}),\text{ $Y_{\bullet}$ is an admissible flag such that $Y_{1}=E$}\}=0.

Note also that
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(N_{s})
consists of a single point
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
with
x_{1}=\mult_{E}N_{s}
. Thus the assertion follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of Theorem
A
by proving the converse of Proposition
4.2
.
The following result is a generalization of
[
J
, Theorem A]
to possibly non-big divisor case.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}
be divisors on
X
with
\kappa(D),\kappa(D^{\prime})\geq 0
. If
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})

on

.

Proof.

Recall that if
D
is a divisor with
\kappa(D)\geq 0
, then any
\kappa(D)
-dimensional general subvariety of

is a Nakayama subvariety of
D
. Thus we can take an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
containing the Nakayama subvarieties of
D,D^{\prime}
with general

. By the assumption, we can deduce from Theorem
2.9
(1) that
\kappa(D)=\kappa(D^{\prime})
.
The assertion is trivial when
\kappa(D)=\kappa(D^{\prime})=0
. Thus, from now on, we assume that
\kappa(D),\kappa(D^{\prime})>0
.
By Theorem
4.5
, we may fix an Iitaka fibration

on

.

on

.

5.
Restricted base locus via Okounkov bodies

We show Theorem
B
as Theorem
5.1
in this section. The idea of the proof is essentially the same as that of
[
CHPW2
, Theorem A]
, but we include the detailed proof for reader’s convenience. Throughout the section,
X
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
D
be a pseudoeffective abundant divisor on
X
, and
x\in X
be a point. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)

(2)

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
does not contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for every admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
centered at
x
.

(3)

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
does not contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
centered at
x
.

Proof.

We may assume that
D
is effective. Since
D
is an abundant divisor, we have
\ord_{V}(||D||)=\inf\{\sigma(D^{\prime})\mid D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\geq 0\}
by Theorem
2.13
(2) for any divisorial valuation
\sigma
with the center
V
on
X
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Assume that
x\in\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
, and fix an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
centered at
x
.
By taking a sufficiently small ample divisor
A
, we may assume that
x\in\mathbf{B}_{-}(D+A)
.
By
[
ELMNP1
, Theorem B]
, we have
\ord_{x}(||D+A||)>0
. Thus it follows that

\delta:=\inf\{\mult_{x}(D^{\prime})\mid D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\geq 0\}=\ord_{x}(||D||)\geq\ord_{x}(||D+A||)>0.

For
D^{\prime}\in|D|_{\mathbb{R}}
, we write
\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})=(\nu_{1}(D^{\prime}),\ldots,\nu_{n}(D^{\prime}))
. Then we obtain

\nu_{1}(D^{\prime})+\cdots+\nu_{n}(D^{\prime})\geq\mult_{x}(D^{\prime})\geq\delta.

This implies that for any point
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
, we have
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}\geq\delta
. In particular,
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
does not contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
Trivial.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
Assume that
x\not\in\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
, and fix an arbitrary admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
centered at
x
. By Remark
2.4
, we may assume that each
Y_{i}
in
Y_{\bullet}
is smooth. We use the notations in Subsection
3.2
. We may take a birational morphism
f_{m}\colon X_{m}\to X
for each sufficiently large integer
m>0
in such a way that there is an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}^{m}
on
X_{m}
such that
f_{m}|_{Y_{i}^{m}}\colon Y_{i}^{m}\to Y_{i}
is a birational morphism for
0\leq i\leq n
.
We can write
f_{m}^{*}D=M_{m}+N_{m}+(P_{m}-M_{m})
. Note that
Y_{n}^{m}\not\subseteq\Supp(N_{m})
and
M_{m}
is semiample. Thus there is an effective divisor
D^{\prime}_{m}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D
such that

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(D^{\prime}_{m})=\nu_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m}-M_{m}).

Now, by Lemma
3.4
, there is an ample divisor
H
on
X
such that
P_{m}-M_{m}\leq\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H
. We then have

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(D^{\prime}_{m})+\mathbf{x}=\nu_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}(P_{m}-M_{m})+\mathbf{x}\in\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}^{m}}\left(\frac{1}{m}f_{m}^{*}H\right)\penalty\ \text{for some $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}$}.

In view of Lemma
4.1
, we see that

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime}_{m})+\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}\left(\frac{1}{m}H\right)\penalty\ \text{for some $\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}$}.

However, since
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}\left(\frac{1}{m}H\right)=\{0\}
, it follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime}_{m})=0
. This means that the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is contained in
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
. We have shown that
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
D
be an abundant divisor on
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

D
is nef.

(2)

For every point
x\in X
, there exists an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
centered at
x
such that
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(3)

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
contain the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for every admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
.

Proof.

Recall that a divisor
D
on
X
is nef if and only if
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)=\emptyset
. Thus the corollary is immediate from Theorem

.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Note that Theorem

6.
Comparing two Okounkov bodies

In this section, we prove Theorem

as Theorem

.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
D

-dimensional smooth projective variety

. Fix an admissible flag

of

X^{\prime}
. Then we have

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))=\deg(\phi|_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}\to Z)\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)).

In particular,
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
if and only if the map
\phi|_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}\to Z
is generically injective.

Proof.

We use the notations in Section
3
. By Proposition
2.15
,
V
is also a positive volume subvariety of
D
.
For a sufficiently large integer
m>0
, we have

\deg(\phi_{m}|_{V_{m}}\colon V_{m}\to Z_{m})=\deg(\phi|_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}\to Z)=:d.

Since
\phi_{m}|_{V_{m}}^{*}H_{m}=M_{m}
, it follows that
\vol_{V_{m}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})=d\cdot\vol_{Z_{m}}(H_{m})
. By Lemmas
3.1
,
3.6
, Theorem
2.9
, and
[
LM
, Theorem A]
, we obtain

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{\kappa(D)!}\vol_{V_{m}}(M_{m}|_{V_{m}})\text{ and }\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{\kappa(D)!}\vol_{Z_{m}}(H_{m}).

Thus the first assertion immediately follows.

Recall that
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\subseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
. Thus

\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
if and only if
\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))=\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))
.

Now the second assertion follows from the first assertion.
∎

Example 6.2
.

Upon obtaining Theorem
6.1
, one may wonder whether under the same settings,
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
and
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
coincide up to rescaling by a constant, i.e.,

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=(\deg(\phi|_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}\to Z))^{\frac{1}{\kappa(D)}}\cdot\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D).

This is not true in general.
For instance, consider a 3-fold
X:=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the projections
f\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
g\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
H:=f^{*}L
and
F:=g^{*}P
where
L
is a line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
P
is a point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then
H
is an abundant divisor with
\kappa(H)=2
.
Note that
f
is the Iitaka fibration of
H
.
Take a general point
x
in
X
and general members
H^{\prime}\in|H|
and
S\in|H+2F|
containing
x
. Note that
S
is a Nakayama subvariety of
H
and
\deg(f|_{S}\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{2})=2
.
We now fix an admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}:X\supseteq S\supseteq S\cap H^{\prime}\supseteq\{x\}

on
X
. It is easy to check that
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(H)
is an isosceles right triangle in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}

and
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(H)
is a non-isosceles right triangle in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
.
In particular, we see that
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(H)\neq\sqrt{2}\cdot\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(H)
.

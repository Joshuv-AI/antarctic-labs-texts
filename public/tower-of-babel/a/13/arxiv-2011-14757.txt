Message Passing Based Structured Sparse Signal Recovery for Estimation of OTFS Channels with Fractional Doppler Shifts
Fei Liu, Zhengdao Yuan, Qinghua Guo, Zhongyong Wang, Peng Sun

Abstract
The orthogonal time frequency space (OTFS) modulation has emerged as a promising modulation scheme for high mobility wireless communications. To enable efficient OTFS detection in the delay-Doppler (DD) domain, the DD domain channels need to be acquired accurately. To achieve the low latency requirement in future wireless communications, the time duration of the OTFS block should be small, therefore fractional Doppler shifts have to be considered to avoid significant modelling errors due to the assumption of integer Doppler shifts. However there lack investigations on the estimation of OTFS channels with fractional Doppler shifts in the literature. In this work, we develop a channel estimator for OTFS with particular attention to fractional Doppler shifts, and both bi-orthogonal waveform and rectangular waveform are considered. Instead of estimating the DD domain channel directly, we estimate the channel gains and (fractional) Doppler shifts that parameterize the DD domain channel. The estimation is formulated as a structured sparse signal recovery problem with a Bayesian treatment. Based on a factor graph representation of the problem, an efficient message passing algorithm is developed to recover the structured sparse signal (thereby the OTFS channel). The Cramer-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) for the estimation is developed and the effectiveness of the algorithm is demonstrated through simulations.

A number of channel estimation methods have been proposed in the literature. In

To solve this problem, pilot and data symbols are placed in the same OTFS block in

[

, it is not clear how to estimate the channel with fractional Doppler shifts for OTFS with a more practical waveform, e.g., the rectangular waveform. In addition, the method estimates the DD domain channel directly, and does not provide the estimates of the channel gains and fractional Doppler shifts.

In this work, we address the issue of OTFS channel estimation with particular attention to fractional Doppler shifts. As the OTFS channel is parameterized by the channel gains and fractional Doppler shifts, we estimate these parameters rather than the DD domain channel directly, mainly due to two reasons. Firstly, this can lead to much better performance as the number of variables to be estimated is significantly smaller. Secondly, the estimates of Doppler shifts can be useful, e.g., for estimating the velocity of mobile users. In addition, both the bi-orthogonal waveform and the rectangular waveform are considered. We formulate the
OTFS
channel estimation as a structured signal recovery problem, which is solved using Bayesian inference. With a factor graph representation of the problem, a message passing algorithm is developed to estimate the channel gains and fractional Doppler shifts jointly. In contrast to
[
13
]
, we show that using a few pilot symbols rather than a single pilot symbol
is
more preferable as the peak to average power ratio (PAPR) of OTFS signals can be significantly reduced. Our proposed algorithm can work with either single pilot symbol or multiple pilot symbols, and significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art method. The Cramer-Rao lower bound (CRLB) for the
channel
estimation is derived to verify the performance of the proposed algorithm. The bit error rates (BER) of the OTFS system with perfect channel and estimated channel are also compared to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

•

The OTFS DD domain channel is acquired by estimating the relevant parameters, i.e., the channel gains and fractional Doppler shifts. The estimation of the parameters is formulated as a novel structured sparse signal recovery problem with a Bayesian treatment.

•

Both the bi-orthogonal waveform and the rectangular waveform are considered in this work. To the best of our knowledge, the investigations on the fractional Doppler shift estimation are very limited in the literature. The work in
[
13
]
, where the DD domain channel is estimated directly, does not provide fractional Doppler estimate explicitly, and it is not applicable to the case of the rectangular waveform.

•

Due to the uniqueness of the formulated structured sparse signal recovery problem, a dedicated message passing algorithm is proposed to efficiently solve the problem. The algorithm is able to work with either a single pilot symbol or multiple pilot symbols, which can significantly reduce the PAPR of the OTFS signal.

•

To evaluate the performance of the estimator, the CRLB is derived. Comparison results with existing methods are provided to demonstrate the advantages of the proposed method.

For simplicity, this work is focused on a plain OTFS system, but the proposed algorithm can be extended for a more complex system, such as MIMO-OTFS.

In this paper, we use the scheme in
[
13
]
and
[

]

As shown in Fig.

To guarantee the channel estimation quality, the power of the pilot symbols should be sufficiently high, which can be much larger than the power of data symbols. In this section, we first focus on OTFS with the bi-orthogonal waveform, and then extend our discussion to the case of the rectangular waveform.

Message Computations in Part I

Assume that the belief of
\bm{c}
is known, which turns out to be Gaussian, i.e.,
b(\bm{c})\propto\mathcal{CN}(\bm{c};\bm{c}^{p},\bm{V}_{c}^{p})
, as given later in (
42

43
). The VMP or mean field (MF)
is used at the function node
f_{\bm{y}}(\bm{c},\gamma)
, and the message from
f_{\bm{y}}(\bm{c},\gamma)
to the variable node
\gamma
can be computed as

\displaystyle m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow\gamma}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\propto\exp\int\ln f_{\bm{y}}(\bm{c},\gamma)b(\bm{c})d\bm{c}

\displaystyle\propto{\gamma^{Z}}\exp\{-\gamma[(\bm{y}-\bm{X}_{bi}\bm{c}^{p})^{H}(\bm{y}-\bm{X}_{bi}\bm{c}^{p})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+Tr\{\bm{X}_{bi}\bm{V_{c}}^{p}\bm{X}_{bi}^{H}\}]\}.

(37)

With the prior
f_{\gamma}(\gamma)\propto 1/\gamma
, the belief
b(\gamma)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle b(\gamma)\propto\gamma^{-1}m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow\gamma}(\gamma).

(38)

Then, with the MF rule and the belief
b(\gamma)
, we can compute the outgoing message
m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow{c}_{jb}}(c_{jb})
as

\displaystyle m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow{c}_{jb}}(c_{jb})

\displaystyle\propto\exp\int\ln f_{\bm{y}}(\bm{c},\gamma)b(\gamma)b(\bm{c}_{\sim c_{jb}})d\gamma d\bm{c}_{\sim c_{jb}},

(39)

where
\bm{c}_{\sim c_{jb}}
denotes a vector obtained by removing
c_{jb}
from
\bm{c}
.

c_{jb}
, then compute the outgoing extrinsic message. According to the MF rule, the message
m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow\bm{c}}(\bm{c})
can be expressed as

\displaystyle m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow\bm{c}}(\bm{c})

\displaystyle\propto\exp\int\ln f_{\bm{y}}(\bm{c},\gamma)b(\gamma)d\gamma

\displaystyle\propto~\mathcal{CN}\left(\bm{c};(\bm{X}_{bi}^{H}\bm{X}_{bi})^{-1}\bm{X}_{bi}^{H}\bm{y},\hat{\gamma}^{-1}(\bm{X}_{bi}^{H}\bm{X}_{bi})^{-1}\right),

(40)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int\gamma b(\gamma)d\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{Z}{(\bm{y}-\bm{X}_{bi}\bm{c}^{p})^{H}(\bm{y}-\bm{X}_{bi}{c}^{p})+Tr\{\bm{X}_{bi}{V_{c}}^{p}\bm{X}_{bi}^{H}\}}.

\bm{c}
is Gaussian with covariance matrix
\bm{V}_{c}^{p}
and mean vector
\bm{c}^{p}
, which can be computed as

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{c}^{p}=

\displaystyle(\bm{V}_{c}^{-1}+\hat{\gamma}\bm{X}_{bi}^{H}\bm{X}_{bi})^{-1},

(42)

\displaystyle\bm{c}^{p}=

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{p}_{c}(\bm{V}_{c}^{-1}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{c}}+\hat{\gamma}\bm{X}_{bi}^{H}\bm{y}),

(43)

where
\bm{V}_{\bm{c}}
is a diagonal matrix with the diagonal elements given by
\{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}\}
and
\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{c}}
is a column vector that consists of
\{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{c}_{jb}\}

\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{c}_{jb}
and the variance
\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}
are delayed to (
89
) and (

).

m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow{c}_{jb}}(c_{jb})

[

\displaystyle m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow{c}_{jb}}(c_{jb})=\mathcal{CN}(c_{jb};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{c}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}})

(44)

with

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{c_{jb}}^{p}}-\frac{1}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}}\right)^{-1},

(45)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{c}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}\left(\frac{{c}_{jb}^{p}}{\nu_{c_{jb}}^{p}}-\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{c}_{jb}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}}\right),

(46)

{\bm{c}}^{p}

th element of the
j
th block of the vector that consists of the diagonal elements of
\bm{V}_{\bm{c}}^{p}
.

IV-B
1
Forward Message Passing

With the incoming message from Part I
n_{c_{jb}\rightarrow f_{c_{jb}}}(c_{jb})=m_{f_{\bm{y}}\rightarrow{c}_{jb}}(c_{jb})=\mathcal{CN}(c_{jb};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{c}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}})
and the factor
f_{c_{jb}}(c_{jb},h_{j},g_{jb})=\delta(c_{jb}-h_{j}g_{jb})
, we can obtain an intermediate function node
\tilde{f}_{c_{jb}}(h_{j},g_{jb})
according to BP
[
18
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{c_{jb}}(h_{j},g_{jb})=

\displaystyle\int f_{c_{jb}}(c_{jb},h_{j},g_{jb})n_{c_{jb}\rightarrow f_{c_{jb}}}(c_{jb})d{c_{jb}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{CN}(h_{j}g_{jb};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{c}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}).

(47)

With the local function
\tilde{f}_{c_{jb}}(h_{j},g_{jb})
, we can compute the message from
f_{c_{jb}}
to
h_{j}
by treating
g_{jb}
as a constant, i.e.,

\displaystyle m_{f_{c_{jb}}\rightarrow h_{j}}(h_{j})=\mathcal{CN}(h_{j};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{h}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}),

(48)

where

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{h}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{c}_{jb}}{\hat{g}_{jb}},

(49)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}}{|\hat{g}_{jb}|^{2}}

(50)

with
\hat{g}_{jb}
being the mean of the Gaussian belief of
g_{jb}
, which is computed in (
88
).

The product of the Gaussian messages
\{m_{f_{c_{jb}}\rightarrow h_{j}}(h_{j}),\forall b\}
is still Gaussian
[
25
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle q_{j}(h_{j})=\prod_{b}m_{f_{c_{jb}}\rightarrow h_{j}}(h_{j})\propto\mathcal{CN}(h_{j};\hat{q}_{j},\nu_{q_{j}}),

(51)

where

\displaystyle{\nu}_{q_{j}}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{b}\frac{1}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}}\right)^{-1},

(52)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{j}=

\displaystyle{\nu}_{q_{j}}\sum_{b}\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{h}_{jb}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}}.

(53)

With the message
m_{f_{h_{j}}\rightarrow h_{j}}(h_{j})\propto\mathcal{CN}(h_{j};0,\widehat{\lambda}_{j}^{-1})
, which is given later in (
76
), the belief
b(h_{j})
of
h_{j}
is obtained as

\displaystyle b(h_{j})=q_{j}(h_{j})m_{f_{h_{j}}\rightarrow h_{j}}(h_{j})\propto\mathcal{CN}(h_{j};\hat{h}_{j},\nu_{h_{j}}),

(54)

where

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{j}=

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{q}_{j}}{1+{\nu}_{q_{j}}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}},

(55)

\displaystyle\nu_{h_{j}}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{{\nu}_{q_{j}}}+\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\right)^{-1}.

(56)

The message
m_{f_{h_{j}}\rightarrow\lambda_{j}}(\lambda_{j})
is then computed by using the MF rule, i.e.,

\displaystyle m_{f_{h_{j}}\rightarrow\lambda_{j}}(\lambda_{j})\propto

\displaystyle exp\left\{\int\ln f_{h_{j}}(h_{j},\lambda_{j})b(h_{j})d{h_{j}}\right\}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}exp\{-\lambda_{j}(|\hat{h}_{j}|^{2}+\nu_{h_{j}})\},

(57)

so the belief
b(\lambda_{j})
of the hyperparameter
\lambda_{j}
is given as

\displaystyle b(\lambda_{j})=

\displaystyle m_{f_{h_{j}}\rightarrow\lambda_{j}}(\lambda_{j})f_{\lambda_{j}}(\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}^{\epsilon_{j}}exp\left\{-\lambda_{j}(\eta_{j}+|\hat{h}_{j}|^{2}+\nu_{h_{j}})\right\}.

(58)

The message from
f_{c_{jb}}
to
g_{jb}
is computed with the MF rule, i.e.,

\displaystyle m_{f_{c_{jb}}\rightarrow g_{jb}}(g_{jb})\propto

\displaystyle exp\left\{\int\ln\tilde{f}_{c_{jb}}(h_{j},g_{jb})b(h_{j})d{h_{j}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{CN}(g_{jb};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{g}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}),

(59)

where

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{g}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{c}_{jb}\hat{h}_{j}^{*}}{|\hat{h}_{j}|^{2}+\nu_{h_{j}}},

(60)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}}{|\hat{h}_{j}|^{2}+\nu_{h_{j}}}.

(61)

It is noted that the local function node
f_{g_{j}b}
includes a nonlinear function
\Phi(q,\kappa_{j})
, which makes the message computation about
\kappa_{j}
intractable.
Inspired by the extended Kalman filter
[
26
]

, to solve this problem,
\Phi(q,\kappa_{j})
is linearized by using the first order Taylor expansion with the estimate of
\kappa_{j}
in last iteration, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Phi(q,\kappa_{j})\approx\Phi(q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})+\Phi^{\prime}(q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})(\kappa_{j}-\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}),

(62)

with

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-j2\pi t}{MN}\right)\Phi(q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})+

\displaystyle e^{-j2\pi\frac{t(d+\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})}{MN}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}j\frac{2n\pi}{N}e^{j\frac{2n\pi}{N}(q+\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})},

(63)

where
\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}
denotes the estimates of
\kappa_{j}
in last iteration. Then, with the BP rule and the approximation in (
62
), the message
m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow\kappa_{j}}(\kappa_{j})
can be expressed as

\displaystyle m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow\kappa_{j}}(\kappa_{j})=

\displaystyle\int f_{g_{jb}}(g_{jb},\kappa_{j})m_{f_{c_{jb}}\rightarrow g_{jb}}(g_{jb})d{g_{jb}}.

(64)

Note that
\kappa_{j}
is a real valued variable. To ensure that the message
m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow\kappa_{j}}(\kappa_{j})
is real, we rewrite the function

as

\displaystyle f_{g_{jb}}(g_{jb},\kappa_{j})

\displaystyle=\delta\left(\mathcal{R}[g_{jb}]-\mathcal{R}[\Phi(Q,\kappa_{j})]\right)\delta\left(\mathcal{I}[g_{jb}]-\mathcal{I}[\Phi(Q,\kappa_{j})]\right),

(65)

where
Q=-\widehat{N}+b-1
. Then the message
m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow\kappa_{j}}(\kappa_{j})
in (
64
) can be obtained as

\displaystyle m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow\kappa_{j}}(\kappa_{j})\propto

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\kappa_{j};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}^{\mathcal{R}},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{R}})\mathcal{N}(\kappa_{j};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}^{\mathcal{I}},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{I}})

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\kappa_{j};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}),

(66)

where

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}^{\mathcal{R}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{R}[\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{g}_{jb}]-\mathcal{R}[\Phi(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]+\mathcal{R}[\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{R}[\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]},

(67)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{R}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}}{2|\mathcal{R}[\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]|^{2}},

(68)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}^{\mathcal{I}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}[\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{g}_{jb}]-\mathcal{I}[\Phi(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]+\mathcal{I}[\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{I}[\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]},

(69)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{I}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}}{2|\mathcal{I}[\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})]|^{2}},

(70)

and

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{R}}}+\frac{1}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{I}}}\right)^{-1},

(71)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}\left(\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}^{\mathcal{R}}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{R}}}+\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}^{\mathcal{I}}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}^{\mathcal{I}}}\right).

(72)

The prior of
\kappa_{j}
is a uniform distribution over
[-0.5,0.5]
, i.e.,
-0.5\leq\kappa_{j}\leq 0.5
. To simplify the computation of the belief
b(\kappa_{j})
, we simply carry out the clipping operation, i.e.,

b(\kappa_{j})\propto\begin{cases}\mathcal{N}(\kappa_{j};-0.5,0);&\hat{\kappa}_{j}\leq-0.5\\
\mathcal{N}(\kappa_{j};0.5,0);&\hat{\kappa}_{j}\geq 0.5\\
\mathcal{N}(\kappa_{j};\hat{\kappa}_{j},\nu_{\kappa_{j}});&otherwise\\
\end{cases}

(73)

where

\nu_{\kappa_{j}}=\left(\sum_{b}\frac{1}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}}\right)^{-1},

(74)

\hat{\kappa}_{j}=\nu_{\kappa_{j}}\sum_{b}\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}}.

(75)

We firstly compute the message
m_{f_{h_{j}}\rightarrow h_{j}}(h_{j})
from the function node
f_{h_{j}}
to
h_{j}
by using the MF rule as follows

\displaystyle m_{f_{h_{j}}\rightarrow h_{j}}(h_{j})\propto

\displaystyle exp\left\{\int\ln f_{h_{j}}(h_{j},\lambda_{j})b(\lambda_{j})d{\lambda_{j}}\right\}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\mathcal{CN}(h_{j};0,\hat{\lambda}_{j}^{-1}),

(76)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{j}=\int\lambda_{j}b(\lambda_{j})d\lambda_{j}=\frac{\epsilon_{j}+1}{\eta_{j}+|\hat{h}_{j}|^{2}+\nu_{h_{j}}}.

Then the message

from variable node

to

\displaystyle n_{h_{j}\rightarrow f_{c_{jb}}}(h_{j})=\frac{b(h_{j})}{m_{f_{c_{jb}}\rightarrow h_{j}}}\propto\mathcal{CN}(h_{j};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{h}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}),

(78)

where

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}=

\displaystyle\left({\frac{1}{\nu_{h_{j}}}}-\frac{1}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}}\right)^{-1},

(79)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{h}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}\left(\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{\nu_{h_{j}}}-\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{h}_{jb}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}}\right).

(80)

The backward message
n_{\kappa_{j}\rightarrow f_{g_{jb}}}(\kappa_{j})
is Gaussian with mean
\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}
and variance
\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}
and can be calculated as

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}=

\displaystyle(1/\nu_{\kappa_{j}}-1/\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}})^{-1},

(81)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}(\hat{\kappa}_{j}/\nu_{\kappa_{j}}-\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}/\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}).

(82)

For the cases that
\hat{\kappa}_{j}=0.5
or
-0.5
and
\nu_{\kappa_{j}}=0
in (
73
), we set

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}=\nu_{\kappa_{j}},\quad\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}=\hat{\kappa}_{j}.

(83)

Then the message
m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow g_{jb}}(g_{jb})
is calculated by using the BP rule, i.e.,

\displaystyle m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow g_{jb}}(g_{jb})=

\displaystyle\int{f_{g_{jb}}(g_{jb},\kappa_{j})n_{\kappa_{j}\rightarrow f_{g_{jb}}}(\kappa_{j})}d{\kappa_{j}}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\mathcal{CN}(g_{jb};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{g}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}),

(84)

where

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{g}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\Phi(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})+\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})(\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\kappa}_{jb}-\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}),

(85)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}=

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{\kappa_{jb}}|\Phi^{\prime}(Q,\hat{\kappa}_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})|^{2}.

(86)

Hence we can obtain the belief

with

(87)

\hat{g}_{jb}=\nu_{g_{jb}}\left(\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{g}_{jb}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}}+\frac{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{g}_{jb}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{g_{jb}}}\right).

(88)

Finally, by combining the incoming message
n_{g_{jb}\rightarrow f_{c_{jb}}}(g_{jb})=m_{f_{g_{jb}}\rightarrow g_{jb}}(g_{jb})
and
n_{h_{j}\rightarrow f_{c_{jb}}}(h_{j})\propto\mathcal{CN}(h_{j};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{h}_{jb},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}})

\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{c}_{jb}
and variance
\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}

[
27
]

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{c}_{jb}=

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{h}_{jb}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{g}_{jb},

(89)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{c_{jb}}=

\displaystyle|\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{h}_{jb}|^{2}+|\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{g}_{jb}|^{2}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}+\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\nu}_{h_{jb}}.

(90)

V
Cramer-Rao Lower Bound

To evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithm, we derive the CRLB for the channel gain and Doppler shift estimation. We assume that the locations of the nonzero elements in
\bm{c}
are known to facilitate the derivation of the CRLB. Hence, the CRLB derived in this section is a loose performance bound for the estimation.

We firstly rewrite (
9
) and (
28
) as

\displaystyle y[k,l]=u[k,l]+\omega[k,l],

(91)

where

\displaystyle u[k,l]=\sum_{i=1}^{P}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{q=-\hat{N}}^{\hat{N}}}

\displaystyle h_{i}f(q,\kappa_{i})e^{-j2\pi\frac{l_{i}(k_{i}+\kappa_{i})}{MN}}

\displaystyle\times x([k-k_{i}+q]_{N},[l-l_{i}]_{M})

(92)

for the bi-orthogonal waveform, and

\displaystyle u[k,l]=\sum_{i=1}^{P}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{q=-\hat{N}}^{\hat{N}}}

\displaystyle h_{i}f(q,\kappa_{i})e^{j2\pi\frac{(l-l_{i})(k_{i}+\kappa_{i})}{MN}}

\displaystyle\times x([k-k_{i}+q]_{N},[l-l_{i}]_{M})

(93)

for the rectangular waveform.
In order to derive the CRLB, we define
\bm{\theta}=[h_{1},h_{p},\cdots,h_{P},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{p},\cdots,\kappa_{P}]^{T}
. According to
[
28
]
, the CRLB of the
j
th element in
\bm{\theta}
is the
j
th diagonal element of the inverse of the Fisher information
matrix, i.e.,

\displaystyle\theta_{j}^{CRLB}=[\bm{I}^{-1}(\bm{\theta})]_{jj},

(94)

where the Fisher information matrix
\bm{I}(\bm{\theta})
has a size of
2P\times 2P
with the
(i,j)
th element given as

\displaystyle[\bm{I}(\bm{\theta})]_{ij}=-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\ln p(\bm{y};\bm{\theta})}{\partial{\theta}_{i}\partial{\theta}_{j}}\right]

(95)

for
i=1,2,\cdots,2P
,
j=1,2,\cdots,2P
, and the expectation is taken with respect to
p(\bm{y};\bm{\theta})
. The logarithm of the likelihood function
\ln p(\bm{y};\bm{\theta})
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\ln p(\bm{y};\bm{\theta})=-Z\ln(\pi\gamma^{-1})-\gamma\sum_{z=0}^{Z-1}|y_{z}-u_{z}|^{2},

(96)

where
y_{z}
denotes the
z
th element in
\bm{y}
and
u_{z}
denotes the corresponding
u[k,l]
. Then, (
95
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle[\bm{I}(\bm{\theta})]_{ij}=\gamma\sum_{z=0}^{Z-1}\left[-\frac{\partial u_{z}}{\partial{\theta}_{i}}\frac{\partial u_{z}^{*}}{\partial{\theta}_{j}}-\frac{\partial u_{z}^{*}}{\partial{\theta}_{i}}\frac{\partial u_{z}}{\partial{\theta}_{j}}\right].

(97)

For simplicity, we define
k^{\prime}\triangleq[k-k_{p}+q]_{N}
and
l^{\prime}\triangleq[l-l_{p}]_{M}
.
When
1\leq p\leq P
(
p
represents
i
or
j
), the derivative
\frac{\partial{u}_{z}}{\partial{\theta}_{p}}
is about the channel gain
h_{p}
. For the bi-orthogonal waveform, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{u}_{z}}{\partial h_{p}}=\sum_{q=-\widehat{N}}^{\widehat{N}}

\displaystyle f(q,\kappa_{p})e^{-j2\pi\frac{l_{p}(k_{p}+\kappa_{p})}{MN}}x[k^{\prime},l^{\prime}],

(98)

while for the rectangular waveform we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{u}_{z}}{\partial h_{p}}=\sum_{q=-\widehat{N}}^{\widehat{N}}

\displaystyle f(q,\kappa_{p})e^{j2\pi\frac{(l-l_{p})(k_{p}+\kappa_{p})}{MN}}x[k^{\prime},l^{\prime}].

(99)

When
P<p^{\prime}\leq 2P
(
p^{\prime}
represents
i
or
j
), the derivative
\frac{\partial{u}_{z}}{\partial{\theta}_{p^{\prime}}}
is about
\kappa_{p}
, where
p=p^{\prime}-P
. For the bi-orthogonal waveform, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{u}_{z}}{\partial\kappa_{p}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=-\widehat{N}}^{\widehat{N}}h_{p}\biggl[\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}j\frac{2n\pi}{N}e^{j\frac{2n\pi}{N}(q+\kappa_{p})}\right)e^{-j2\pi\frac{l_{p}(k_{p}+\kappa_{p})}{MN}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f(q,\kappa_{p})\left(e^{-j2\pi\frac{l_{p}(k_{i}+\kappa_{p})}{MN}}\frac{-j2\pi l_{p}}{MN}\right)\biggl]x[k^{\prime},l^{\prime}],

(100)

while for the rectangular waveform

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{u}_{z}}{\partial\kappa_{p}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=-\widehat{N}}^{\widehat{N}}h_{p}\biggl[\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}j\frac{2n\pi}{N}e^{j\frac{2n\pi}{N}(q+\kappa_{p})}\right)e^{j2\pi\frac{(l-l_{p})(k_{p}+\kappa_{p})}{MN}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f(q,\kappa_{p})\left(e^{j2\pi\frac{(l-l_{p})(k_{p}+\kappa_{p})}{MN}}\frac{j2\pi(l-l_{p})}{MN}\right)\biggl]x[k^{\prime},l^{\prime}].

(101)

To evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithm in terms of the normalized mean squared error (NMSE), we use the average normalized CRLB for the estimation of channel gains and fractional Doppler shifts, which are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{h}}_{bound}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{P}{\theta}_{j}^{CRLB}}{||\bm{h}_{\bm{\theta}}||_{2}^{2}},

(102)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\kappa}}_{bound}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{j=P+1}^{2P}{\theta}_{j}^{CRLB}}{||\bm{\kappa}_{\bm{\theta}}||_{2}^{2}},

(103)

where
\bm{h}_{\bm{\theta}}=(h_{1},h_{2},\cdots,h_{P})^{T}
and
\bm{\kappa}_{\bm{\theta}}=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{P})^{T}
.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have addressed the issue of the estimation of OTFS channels with fractional Doppler shifts, where both the bi-orthogonal waveform and the rectangular waveform are considered. The estimation is formulated as a sparse structured signal recovery problem
and a Bayesian treatment is investigated
. With a factor graph representation of the problem, we have derived an message passing based algorithm to estimate the channel gains and fractional Doppler shifts. The CRLB has also been derived to evaluate the estimation performance of the proposed algorithm. It has been shown that the proposed algorithm significantly outperforms the existing algorithm, and it is able to work with multiple pilot symbols to achieve significant PAPR reduction.

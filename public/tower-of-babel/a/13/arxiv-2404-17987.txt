Spectrum of Weighted Composition Operators Part X The spectrum and essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the polydisc algebra.
Arkady Kitover, Mehmet Orhon

Abstract
. We investigate the spectrum and the essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the polydisc algebra 𝔸 n \mathds{A}^{n} . In the case n = 2 n=2 we provide a detailed and, in most cases, complete description of these spectra.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 47B33; Secondary 47B48, 46B60

1.
Introduction

The spectrum of weighted automorphisms of the disc-algebra was described by Kamowitz in
[
3
]
. In
[
4
]
the first named author obtained a full description of the spectrum of weighted automorphisms of uniform algebras. The paper
[
5
]
contains a description of essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the algebra
C(K)
. In
[
6
]
the authors obtained some general results about the spectrum and essential spectra of weighted isometrical endomorphisms of uniform algebras and applied these results to the problem of describing the essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the disc-algebra. This description is complete in the case of automorphisms generated by elliptic or parabolic Möbius transformations, and partial in the case of hyperbolic transformations (see
[
6
, Problem 3.7]
).

The goal of the current paper is to apply the results obtained in
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
to the problem of describing essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the polydisc algebra
\mathds{A}^{n}
. While our methods can be applied in the case of any
n\in\mathds{N}
, we decided to provide as many details as possible in the case
n=2
. As the reader will see, even in this case the multitude of distinct possibilities is quite large.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

The following notations are used throughout the paper.

\mathfrak{M}_{A}
and
\partial A
are the space of maximal ideals and the Shilov boundary of
a commutative Banach algebra
A
, respectively.

Let
A
be a commutative unital Banach algebra and
U
be an automorphism of
A
. We will often instead of
U
write
T_{\varphi}
, where
\varphi
is the homeomorphism of
\mathfrak{M}_{A}
onto itself generated by
U
.

\mathds{N}
is the set of all positive integers.

\mathds{Z}
is the set of all integers.

\mathds{C}
is the field of all complex numbers.

We denote by
\mathds{D}
and by
\mathds{U}
the closed and the open unit disk, respectively.

\mathds{T}
is the unit circle.

\mathds{AN}(r,R)
, where
0<r<R<\infty
, is the annulus
\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:r\leq|\lambda|\leq R
}.

For
n=2,3,\ldots
,
\mathds{D}^{n}
,
\mathds{U}^{n}
, and
\mathds{T}^{n}
denote the closed unit polydisc, the open unit polydisc, and the
n
-dimensional unit torus, respectively.

All linear spaces are considered over the field
\mathds{C}
.

C(K)
is the space of all complex-valued continuous functions on a Hausdorff compact space
K
endowed with the standard norm.

\mathds{A}^{n},n=2,3,\ldots
is the polydisc algebra: the algebra of all functions analytic in
\mathds{U}^{n}
and continuous in
\mathds{D}^{n}
.

It is well known (see e.g
[
7
]
) that
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathds{A}^{n}}=\mathds{D}^{n}
and
\partial\mathds{A}^{n}=\mathds{T}^{n}
.

Let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space and
\varphi
be a homeomorphism of
K
onto itself. Then,
\varphi^{0}(k)=k,k\in K
, and
\varphi^{n}=\varphi^{n-1}\circ\varphi,n\in\mathds{Z}
.

Let
K
be a Hausdorff compact space,
\varphi
be a continuous map of
K
into itself, and
w\in C(K)
. Then,
w_{1}=w
and
w_{n}=w_{n-1}(w\circ\varphi^{n-1}),n=2,3,\ldots
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
T:X\rightarrow X
be a continuous linear operator.

\sigma(T)
is the spectrum of
T
.

\rho(T)
is the spectral radius of
T
.

\rho_{min}(T)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1/\rho(T^{-1},&\hbox{if the operator $T$ is invertible;}\\
0,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
is the approximate point spectrum of
T
, i.e.

\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists x_{n}\in X,\|x_{n}\|=1,Tx_{n}-\lambda x_{n}\mathop{\rightarrow}_{n\rightarrow\infty}0\}.

According to this definition the point spectrum of
T
is a subset of
\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
.

\sigma_{r}(T)=\sigma(T)\setminus\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
is the residual spectrum of
T
.

Recall that a continuous linear operator
T
on a Banach space
X

is called upper semi-Fredholm (respectively, lower semi-Fredholm) if its image
R(T)
is closed in
X
and
\dim\ker T<\infty
(respectively, if
codimR(T)<\infty
).

The operator
T
is called semi-Fredholm if it is either upper semi-Fredholm or lower semi-Fredholm, and it is called Fredholm if it is both upper semi-Fredholm and lower semi-Fredholm.

The operator
T
is called Weil operator if
\dim\ker T=codimR(T)<\infty
.

\sigma_{sf}(T)
is the semi-Fredholm spectrum of
T
, i.e.
\sigma_{sf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda I-T
is not semi-Fredholm
\}
.

\sigma_{usf}(T)
is the upper semi-Fredholm spectrum of
T
, i.e.
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda I-T
is not an upper semi-Fredholm operator
\}
. It is well known (see e.g.
[
2
]
) that

\begin{split}&\sigma_{usf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists x_{n}\in X,\|x_{n}\|=1,Tx_{n}-\lambda x_{n}\mathop{\rightarrow}_{n\rightarrow\infty}0,\\
&\text{and the sequence}\;x_{n}\;\text{is singular, i.e. it does not contain}\\
&\text{a norm convergent subsequence}\}.\end{split}

\sigma_{lsf}(T)
is the lower semi-Fredholm spectrum of
T
, i.e.
\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda I-T
is not a lower semi-Fredholm operator
\}
.

\begin{split}&\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists x_{n}^{\prime}\in X^{\prime},\|x_{n}^{\prime}\|=1,T^{\prime}x_{n}^{\prime}-\lambda x_{n}^{\prime}\mathop{\rightarrow}_{n\rightarrow\infty}0,\\
&\text{and the sequence}\;x_{n}^{\prime}\;\text{is singular}.\end{split}

\sigma_{f}(T)
is the Fredholm spectrum of
T
, i.e.
\sigma_{f}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda I-T
is not a Fredholm operator
\}
.

\sigma_{w}(T)
is the Weil spectrum of
T
, i.e.
\sigma_{f}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda I-T
is not a Weil operator
\}
.

It is well known (see e.g
[
2
]
) that

•

\sigma_{sf}(T)\subseteq\sigma_{usf}(T)\subseteq\sigma_{f}(T)\subseteq\sigma_{w}(T)
,

•

\sigma_{usf}(T)=\sigma_{lsf}(T^{\prime})
,
\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\sigma_{usf}(T^{\prime})
.

In the case when operator
T
is defined and bounded on distinct Banach spaces,
\sigma(T,X)
means the spectrum of
T
on the Banach space
X
. The same agreement relates to all the spectra considered above.

3.
Axillary results

Our investigation of essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the algebra
\mathds{A}^{2}
is based on several lemmas stated below.

Lemma 3.1
.

(see
[
6
, Proposition 2.8]

Let
A
be a unital uniform algebra. Assume that
\partial A
has no isolated points. Let
T_{\varphi}
be a periodic automorphism of
A
, i.e. there is an
n\in\mathds{N}
such that
T_{\varphi}^{n}=I
. Let
w\in A
and
T=wT_{\varphi}
. For any
t\in\mathfrak{M}_{A}
let
p(t)
be the smallest positive integer such that
\varphi^{p(t)}(t)=t
. Then,

(1)

\sigma(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists t\in\mathfrak{M}_{A},\lambda^{p(t)}=w_{p(t)}(t)\}
.

(2)

\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\sigma_{sf}(t)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists t\in\partial A,\lambda^{p(t)}=w_{p(t)}(t)\}

Let us recall the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

A uniform algebra
A
is called analytic if for any nonempty open subset
U
of
\partial A
and for any
a\in A
we have
a|U\equiv 0\Rightarrow a=0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

(see
[
6
, Theorem 2.9]
)
Let
A
be a unital analytic uniform algebra,
U=T_{\varphi}
be a non-periodic automorphism of
A
, and
T=wT_{\varphi}
. Assume that

(a)

\partial A
is a connected set.

(b)

There are periodic automorphisms,
U_{n}=T_{\varphi_{n}}
of
A
such that for any
t\in\partial A
we have
\varphi_{n}(t)\rightarrow\varphi(t)
.

(c)

Let
\mu\in(C(\partial A))^{\prime}
be a
\varphi
-invariant probability measure. Then,
\mu
is
\varphi_{n}
-invariant,
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Then,

(1)

\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\sigma_{sf}(T)
.

(2)

The sets
\sigma(T)
and
\sigma_{sf}(T)
are connected rotation invariant subsets of
\mathds{C}
.

(3)

If either
w\in A^{-1}
or
\min\limits_{s\in\partial A}|w(s)|=0
, then
\sigma(T)=\sigma_{sf}(T)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

(see
[
4
, Lemma 3.6, Theorem4.2, Corollary 4.4]
and
[
6
, Theorem 2.1, Proposition 2.11]
)

Let
A
be an analytic uniform unital algebra. Let
T_{\varphi}
be a non-periodic automorphism of
A
,
w\in A
, and
T=wT_{\varphi}
. Then,

(1)

\sigma(T)
is a connected rotation invariant subset of
\mathds{C}
.

(2)

\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
is rotation invariant and
\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\sigma_{a.p.}(T,C(\partial A))
.

(3)

Let
\lambda\in\mathds{C}
, then
\lambda\in\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
if and only if there is a
t\in\partial A
such that
|w_{n}(t)|\geq|\lambda|^{n}
and
|w_{n}(\varphi^{-n}(t))\leq|\lambda|^{n}
for any
n\in\mathds{N}
.

(4)

If
\partial A
has no isolated points, then
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
.

(5)

If
\partial A
has no isolated points and there is a
u\in\partial A
such that
|w_{n}(u)|\leq|\lambda|^{n}
and
|w_{n}(\varphi^{-n}(u))\geq|\lambda|^{n}
for any
n\in\mathds{N}
, then
\lambda\in\sigma_{lsf}(T)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

(see
[
5
]
) Let
A
be a unital uniform algebra and
T=wT_{\varphi}
be a weighted automorphism of
A
. Assume that
\partial A
contains no isolated points. Assume also that
\partial A
is the union of
\varphi
and
\varphi^{-1}
invariant nonempty sets
K_{1},K_{2}
and
O
such that the sets
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are closed,
(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\cap O=\emptyset
, and for any
k\in O
we have

\begin{split}&\bigcap\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}cl\{\varphi^{i}(k):i\geq m\}=K_{1},\\
&\bigcap\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}cl\{\varphi^{-i}(k):i\geq m\}=K_{2}.\end{split}

Then, the following statements hold.

(1)

If
0\leq r=\rho(T,C(K_{2}))<R=\rho_{min}(T,C(K_{1}))
, then either
\mathds{AN}(r,R)\subseteq\sigma_{usf}(T)
, or, if
r=0
,
R\mathds{D}\subseteq\sigma_{usf}(T)
.

(2)

If
0\leq r=\rho(T,C(K_{1}))<R=\rho_{min}(T,C(K_{2}))
, then either
\mathds{AN}(r,R)\subseteq\sigma_{lsf}(T)
, or, if
r=0
,

.

(3)

If
\rho_{min}(T,C(K_{1}))=\rho(T,C(K_{1}))=\rho_{min}(T,C(K_{2}))=\rho(T,C(K_{2}))=r
, then

r\mathds{T}\subseteq\sigma_{sf}(T)
.

(4)

Moreover, if there is a
k\in O
such that

and the set

and

\rho(T,C(K_{1}))\mathds{D}\subseteq\sigma_{lsf}(T)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

(see
[
4
]
) Let
A
be a unital uniform algebra and
T=wT_{\varphi}
be a weighted automorphism of
A
. Then,

\rho(T)=\max\limits_{\mu\in M_{\varphi}}\exp\int\ln{|w|}d\mu,

where
M_{\varphi}=\{\mu\in C^{\prime}(\partial A):T_{\varphi}^{\prime}\mu=\mu,\mu\geq 0,\|\mu\|=1
}
.

4.
Essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the algebra
\mathds{A}^{2}

It is well known (see
[
7
]
) that every automorphism of the algebra
\mathds{A}^{2}
is the composition operator
T_{\Phi}
, where the map
\Phi
is on of the following

(a)

\Phi(z_{1},z_{2})=(\varphi(z_{1}),\psi(z_{2})),(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathds{D}^{2}
,

(b)

\Phi(z_{1},z_{2})=(\psi(z_{2}),\varphi(z_{1})),(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathds{D}^{2}
,

where
\varphi
and
\psi
are Möbius transformations of
\mathds{D}
.

In the case (b) the map
\Phi^{2}
is of the form (a) and taking into consideration the spectral mapping theorem for essential spectra (see
[
1
, Corollary 3.61, p. 149]
) we can without loss of generality assume that the map
\Phi
is of the form (a).

We also can and will without loss of generality consider only elliptic Möbius transformations of the form
\varphi(z)=\alpha z,z\in\mathds{D},\alpha\in\mathds{T}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})=(\alpha_{1}z_{1},\alpha_{2}z_{2})
, where
\alpha_{1}
is a primitive
p^{th}
-root of unity and
\alpha_{2}
is a primitive
q^{th}
-root of unity,
p,q\in\mathds{N}
. Let
m=l.c.m.(p,q)
. Then,

(1)

\sigma(T)=\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists t\in\mathfrak{M}_{A},\lambda^{m}=w_{m}(t)\}
.

(2)

\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\sigma_{sf}(t)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists t\in\partial A,\lambda^{m}=w_{m}(t)\}

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
3.1
and the fact that the set of zeros of
w_{m}-\lambda^{m}
in
\mathds{U}^{2}
is either empty or infinite.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})=(\alpha_{1}z_{1},\alpha_{2}z_{2})
, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathds{T}
,
\alpha_{1}
is a primitive
p^{th}
-root of unity and
\alpha_{2}
is not a root of unity. Let
w\in\mathds{A}^{2}
and let

(Tf)(z_{1},z_{2})=w(z_{1},z_{2})f(\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})),(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathds{D}^{2},f\in\mathds{A}^{2}
. We assume that
w(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}^{s}z_{2}^{t}\tilde{w}(z_{1},z_{2})
, where
s,t\geq 0
and
|\tilde{w}(0,0)|>0

(1)

If
w
is either an invertible element of
\mathds{A}^{2}
or
w
is not invertible in
C(\mathds{T}^{2})
, then

\sigma(T)=\sigma_{sf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists z_{1}\in\mathds{T},|\lambda|^{p}=\tilde{w}_{p}|(z_{1},0)|\}.

(1)

(2)

If
w
is invertible in
C(\mathds{T}^{2})
but not invertible in
\mathds{A}^{2}
, then

\begin{split}&\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\sigma_{sf}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\exists z_{1}\in\mathds{T},|\lambda|=|\tilde{w}_{p}(z_{1},0)|\},\\
&\sigma(T)=\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{D}=\max\limits_{z_{1}\in\mathds{T}}|\tilde{w}_{p}(z_{1},0)|^{1/p}\mathds{D}.\\
\end{split}

(2)

Proof.

Let us first assume that
p=1
, i.e. that
\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1},\alpha z_{2})
, where
\alpha
is not a root of unity. It is immediate to see that if
\mu\in C(\mathds{T}^{2})^{\prime}
is an extreme point of the set of all
\varphi
-invariant probability measures, then there is a
z_{1}\in\mathds{T}
such that
\mu
is the normalised Lebesgue measure on the circle
\{(z_{1},z):z\in\mathds{T}\}
, and therefore

\exp\int\ln{|w|}d\mu=|\tilde{w}(z_{1},0)|.

(3)

Therefore, in the case
p=1
the statements (
1
) and (
2
) follow from
[
6
, Corollary 2.7, Theorem 2.9]
and from the obvious observation that in case (2)
def(T)=\infty
.

To finish the proof in general case we consider the operator
T^{p}
and apply the spectral mapping theorem for Fredholm and semi-Fredholm spectra (see
[
1
, Corollary 3.61, p.149]
)
∎

\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})=(\alpha_{1}z_{1},\alpha_{2}z_{2})

(7)

Proposition 4.7

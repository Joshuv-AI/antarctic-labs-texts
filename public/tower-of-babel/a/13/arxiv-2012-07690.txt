A PAC-Bayesian Approach to Generalization Bounds for Graph Neural Networks
Renjie Liao, Raquel Urtasun, Richard Zemel, University of Toronto, Uber ATG, Vector Institute, Canadian Institute for Advanced Research {rjliao, urtasun, zemel}@cs.toronto.edu

Abstract
In this paper, we derive generalization bounds for the two primary classes of graph neural networks (GNNs), namely graph convolutional networks (GCNs) and message passing GNNs (MPGNNs), via a PAC-Bayesian approach. Our result reveals that the maximum node degree and spectral norm of the weights govern the generalization bounds of both models. We also show that our bound for GCNs is a natural generalization of the results developed in ( Neyshabur et al. 2017 ) for fully-connected and convolutional neural networks. For message passing GNNs, our PAC-Bayes bound improves over the Rademacher complexity based bound in ( Garg et al. 2020 ) , showing a tighter dependency on the maximum node degree and the maximum hidden dimension. The key ingredients of our proofs are a perturbation analysis of GNNs and the generalization of PAC-Bayes analysis to non-homogeneous GNNs. We perform an empirical study on several real-world graph datasets and verify that our PAC-Bayes bound is tighter than others.

Introduction

Graph neural networks (GNNs)

(
Gori et al. 2005
;
Scarselli et al. 2008
;
Bronstein et al. 2017
;
Battaglia et al. 2018
)
have become very popular

(

)
, quantum chemistry
(
Gilmer et al. 2017
)
, computer vision
(

(

)
, robotics
(

)
, and physics
(

)

Given a graph along with node/edge features, GNNs learn node/edge representations by propagating information on the graph via local computations shared across the nodes/edges.
Based on the specific form of local computation employed, GNNs can be divided into two categories: graph convolution based GNNs
(
Bruna et al. 2013
;
Duvenaud et al. 2015
;
Kipf & Welling 2016
)
and message passing based GNNs
(
Li et al. 2015
;
Dai et al. 2016
;
Gilmer et al. 2017
)
.
The former generalizes the convolution operator from regular graphs (
e.g
.
, grids) to ones with arbitrary topology, whereas the latter mimics message passing algorithms and parameterizes the shared functions via neural networks.

Due to the tremendous empirical success of GNNs, there is increasing interest in understanding their theoretical properties.
For example, some recent works study their expressiveness
(
Maron et al. 2018
;
Xu et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2019
)
, that is, what class of functions can be represented by GNNs.
However, only few works investigate why GNNs generalize so well to unseen graphs.
They are either
restricted to a specific model variant
(
Verma & Zhang 2019
;
Du et al. 2019
;
Garg et al. 2020
)
or have loose dependencies on graph statistics
(
Scarselli et al. 2018
)
.

On the other hand, GNNs have close ties to standard feedforward neural networks,
e.g
.
, multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs).
In particular, if each i.i.d. sample is viewed as a node, then the whole dataset becomes a graph without edges.
Therefore, GNNs can be seen as generalizations of MLPs/CNNs since they model not only the regularities within a
sample but also the dependencies among samples as defined in the graph.
It is therefore natural to ask if we can generalize the recent advancements on generalization bounds for MLPs/CNNs
(
Harvey et al. 2017
;
Neyshabur et al. 2017
;
Bartlett et al. 2017
;
Dziugaite & Roy 2017
;
Arora et al. 2018
;
Arora et al. 2019
)
to GNNs, and how would graph structures affect the generalization bounds?

In this paper, we answer the above questions by proving generalization bounds for the two primary
classes of GNNs,
i.e
.
, graph convolutional networks (GCNs)
(
Kipf & Welling 2016
)
and message-passing GNNs (MPGNNs)

(
Dai et al. 2016
;
Jin et al. 2018
)
.

Our generalization bound for GCNs shows an intimate relationship with the bounds for MLPs/CNNs with ReLU activations
(
Neyshabur et al. 2017
;
Bartlett et al. 2017
)
.
In particular, they share the same term,
i.e
.
, the product of the spectral norms of the learned weights at each layer multiplied by a factor that is additive across layers.
The bound for GCNs has an additional multiplicative factor
d^{{(l-1)}/{2}}
where

is the maximum node degree and
l
is the network depth.
Since MLPs/CNNs are special GNNs operating on graphs without edges (

,

(
Neyshabur et al. 2017
)
on such degenerated graphs.
Therefore, our result is a natural generalization of the existing results for MLPs/CNNs.

Our generalization bound for message passing GNNs reveals that the governing terms of the bound are similar to the ones of GCNs,
i.e
.
, the geometric series of the learned weights and the multiplicative factor
d^{l-1}
.
The geometric series appears due to the weight sharing across message passing steps, thus corresponding to the product term across layers in GCNs.
The term

encodes the key graph statistics.
Our bound improves the dependency on the maximum node degree and the maximum hidden dimension compared to the recent Rademacher complexity based bound
(
Garg et al. 2020
)
.
Moreover, we compute the bound values on four real-world graph datasets (
e.g
.
, social networks and protein structures) and verify that our bounds are tighter.

In terms of the proof techniques, our analysis follows the PAC-Bayes framework in the seminal work of
(
Neyshabur et al. 2017
)
for MLPs/CNNs with ReLU activations.
However, we make two distinctive contributions which are customized for GNNs.
First, a naive adaptation of the perturbation analysis in
(
Neyshabur et al. 2017
)
does not work for GNNs since ReLU is not 1-Lipschitz under the spectral norm,
i.e
.
,
\|\text{ReLU}(X)\|_{2}\leq\|X\|_{2}

Instead, we construct the recursion on certain node representations of GNNs like the one with maximum
\ell_{2}
norm, so that we can perform perturbation analysis with vector 2-norm.
Second, in contrast to
(
Neyshabur et al. 2017
)
which only handles the homogeneous networks,
i.e

when

This generalizes the analysis to non-homogeneous GNNs like typical MPGNNs.

2.3
Background of PAC-Bayes Analysis

PAC-Bayes
(
McAllester 1999
;
McAllester 2003
;
Langford & Shawe-Taylor 2003
)
takes a Bayesian view of the probably approximately correct (PAC) learning theory
(
Valiant 1984
)
.
In particular, it assumes that we have a prior distribution
P
over the hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}
and obtain a posterior distribution
Q
over the same support through the learning process on the training set.
Therefore, instead of having a deterministic model/hypothesis as in common learning formulations, we have a distribution of models.
Under this Bayesian view, we define the generalization error and the empirical error as,

\displaystyle L_{S,\gamma}(Q)=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}[L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})],\qquad\qquad L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}(Q)=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}[L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}(f_{w})].

Since many interesting models like neural networks are deterministic and the exact form of the posterior
Q
induced by the learning process and the prior
P
is typically unknown, it is unclear how one can perform PAC-Bayes analysis.
Fortunately, we can exploit the following result from the PAC-Bayes theory.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
McAllester 2003
)

(Two-sided)
Let
P
be a prior distribution over
\mathcal{H}
and let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Then, with probability
1-\delta
over the choice of an i.i.d. size-
m
training set
S
according to
\mathcal{D}
, for all distributions
Q
over
\mathcal{H}
and any
\gamma>0
, we have

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}(Q)\leq L_{S,\gamma}(Q)+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|P)+\ln\frac{2m}{\delta}}{2(m-1)}}.

Here
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
is the KL-divergence.
The nice thing about this result is that the inequality holds for all possible prior
P
and posterior
Q
distributions.
Hence, we have the freedom to construct specific priors and posteriors so that we can work out the bound.
Moreover,
McAllester 2003
;
Neyshabur et al. 2017
provide a general recipe to construct the posterior such that for a large class of models, including deterministic ones, the PAC-Bayes bound can be computed.
Taking a neural network as an example, we can choose a prior distribution with some known density,
e.g
.
, a fixed Gaussian, over the initial weights.
After the learning process, we can add random perturbations to the learned weights from another known distribution as long as the KL-divergence permits an analytical form.
This converts the deterministic model into a distribution of models while still obtaining a tractable KL divergence.

obtained the following result which holds for a large class of deterministic models.

(

3
Generalization Bounds

In this section, we present the main results: generalization bounds of GCNs and MPGNNs using a PAC-Bayesian approach.
We then relate them to existing generalization bounds of GNNs and draw connections to the bounds of MLPs/CNNs.
We summarize the key ideas of the proof in the main text and defer the details to the appendix.

3.1
PAC-Bayes Bounds of GCNs

As discussed above, in order to apply Lemma
2.2
, we must ensure that the change of the output brought by the weight perturbations is small with a large probability.
In the following lemma, we bound this change using the product of the spectral norms of learned weights at each layer and a term depending on some statistics of the graph.

Lemma 3.1
.

(GCN Perturbation Bound) For any
B>0,l>1
, let

\displaystyle\left|f_{w+u}(X,A)-f_{w}(X,A)\right|_{2}\leq eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}.

The key idea of the proof is to decompose the change of the network output into two terms which depend on two quantities of GNNs respectively: the maximum change of node representations
\max_{i}\left|H_{l-1}^{\prime}[i,:]-H_{l-1}[i,:]\right|_{2}

\max_{i}\left|H_{l-1}[i,:]\right|_{2}
.
Here the superscript prime denotes the perturbed model.
These two terms can be bounded by an induction on the layer index.
From this lemma, we can see that the most important graph statistic for the stability of GCNs is the maximum node degree,

,

, let

be a

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}d^{l-1}l^{2}h\log(lh)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}({\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}/{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}})+\log\frac{ml}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right).

In particular, we choose the prior distribution

The key steps of the proof are: 1) constructing a quantity of learned weights

Although Lemma

The latter is important since

3.2
PAC-Bayes Bounds of MPGNNs

3.3.2

As described above, MLPs/CNNs can be viewed as special cases of GNNs.

e.g

5
Discussion

In this paper, we present generalization bounds for two primary classes of GNNs,
i.e
.
, GCNs and MPGNNs.
We show that the maximum node degree and the spectral norms of learned weights govern the bound for both models.
Our results for GCNs generalize the bounds for MLPs/CNNs in
(
Neyshabur et al. 2017
)
, while our results for MPGNNs improve over the state-of-the-art Rademacher complexity bound in
(
Garg et al. 2020
)
.
Our PAC-Bayes analysis can be generalized to other graph problems such as node classification and link prediction since our perturbation analysis bounds the maximum change of any node representation.
Other loss functions (
e.g
.
, ones for regression) could also work in our analysis as long as they are bounded.

However, we are far from being able to explain the practical behaviors of GNNs.
Our bound values are still vacuous as shown in the experiments.
Our perturbation analysis is in the worst-case sense which may be loose for most cases.
We introduce Gaussian posterior in the PAC-Bayes framework to obtain an analytical form of the KL divergence.
Nevertheless, the actual posterior induced by the prior and the learning process may likely to be non-Gaussian.
We also do not explicitly consider the optimization algorithm in the analysis which clearly has an impact on the learned weights.

This work leads to a few interesting open problems for future work:
(1) Is the maximum node degree the only graph statistic that has an impact on the generalization ability of GNNs? Investigating other graph statistics may provide more insights on the behavior of GNNs and inspire the development of novel models and algorithms.
(2) Would the analysis still work for other interesting GNN architectures, such as those with attention
(
Veličković et al. 2017
)
and learnable spectral filters
(
Liao et al. 2019
)
?
(3) Can recent advancements for MLPs/CNNs,
e.g
.
, the compression technique in
(
Arora et al. 2018
)
and data-dependent prior of
(
Parrado-Hernández et al. 2012
)
, help further improve the bounds for GNNs?
(4) What is the impact of the optimization algorithms like SGD on the generalization ability of GNNs? Would graph structures play a role in the analysis of optimization?

\mathbb{P}(\max_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|f_{w+u}(x)-f_{w}(x)|_{\infty}<\frac{\gamma}{4})>\frac{1}{2}
.
Then, for any
\gamma,\delta>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over an i.i.d. size-
m
training set
S
according to
\mathcal{D}
, for any
w
, we have:

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\sqrt{\frac{2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q(w+u)\|P)+\log\frac{8m}{\delta}}{2(m-1)}}

Proof.

Let
\tilde{w}=w+u
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of perturbation with the following property,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{w^{\prime}\middle|\max_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|f_{w^{\prime}}(x)-f_{w}(x)|_{\infty}<\frac{\gamma}{4}\right\}.

(12)

\tilde{w}=w+u
(
w
is deterministic and
u
is stochastic) is distributed according to
Q(\tilde{w})
.
We now construct a new posterior
\tilde{Q}
as follows,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(\tilde{w})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{Z}Q(\tilde{w})&\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{C}\\
0&\tilde{w}\in\bar{\mathcal{C}}.\end{cases}

(13)

Here
Z=\int_{\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{d}Q(\tilde{w})=\underset{\tilde{w}\sim Q}{\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{C})
and
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
is the complement set of
\mathcal{C}
.
We know from the assumption that
Z>\frac{1}{2}
.
Therefore, for any
\tilde{w}\sim\tilde{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle\left.\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},j\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},x\in\mathcal{X}}\middle|\left|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[i]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j]\right|-|f_{w}(x)[i]-f_{w}(x)[j]|\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left.\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},j\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},x\in\mathcal{X}}\middle|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[i]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j]-f_{w}(x)[i]+f_{w}(x)[j]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left.\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},j\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},x\in\mathcal{X}}\middle|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[i]-f_{w}(x)[i]\middle|+\middle|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j]-f_{w}(x)[j]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left.\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},x\in\mathcal{X}}\middle|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[i]-f_{w}(x)[i]\middle|+\max_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{k}^{+},x\in\mathcal{X}}\middle|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j]-f_{w}(x)[j]\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{\gamma}{4}+\frac{\gamma}{4}=\frac{\gamma}{2}

(14)

Recall that

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D}}(f_{w},0)

\displaystyle=\underset{z\sim\mathcal{D}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{w}(x)[y]\leq\max_{j\neq y}f_{w}(x)[j]\right)

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\tilde{w}},\frac{\gamma}{2})

\displaystyle=\underset{z\sim\mathcal{D}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}+\max_{j\neq y}f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j]\right).

Denoting
j_{1}^{\ast}=\argmax_{j\neq y}f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j]
and
j_{2}^{\ast}=\argmax_{j\neq y}f_{w}(x)[j]
, from Eq. (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]-f_{w}(x)[y]+f_{w}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]\middle|<\frac{\gamma}{2}\right.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow~

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]<f_{w}(x)[y]-f_{w}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]+\frac{\gamma}{2}

(15)

Note that since
f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]\geq f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]
, we have

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]

\displaystyle\leq f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]

\displaystyle\leq f_{w}(x)[y]-f_{w}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]+\frac{\gamma}{2}\qquad(\text{Eq. (\ref{eq:pac_bayes_deterministic_tmp_2})})

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle f_{w}(x)[y]-f_{w}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]\leq 0~\Rightarrow~f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]\leq\frac{\gamma}{2},

which indicates
\underset{z\sim\mathcal{D}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{w}(x)[y]\leq f_{w}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]\right)\leq\underset{z\sim\mathcal{D}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]\leq f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)
, or equivalently

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{\mathcal{D},\frac{\gamma}{2}}(f_{\tilde{w}}).

(16)

Note that this holds for any perturbation
\tilde{w}\sim\tilde{Q}
.

Again, recall that

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},\frac{\gamma}{2}}(f_{\tilde{w}})

\displaystyle=\underset{z\sim\mathcal{D}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}+\max_{j\neq y}f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j]\right)

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}(f_{w})

\displaystyle=\underset{z\sim\mathcal{D}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{w}(x)[y]\leq\gamma+\max_{j\neq y}f_{w}(x)[j]\right)

From Eq. (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left|f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]-f_{w}(x)[y]+f_{w}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]\middle|<\frac{\gamma}{2}\right.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow~

\displaystyle f_{w}(x)[y]-f_{w}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]<f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]+\frac{\gamma}{2}

(17)

Note that since
f_{{w}}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]\geq f_{{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]
, we have

\displaystyle f_{{w}}(x)[y]-f_{{w}}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]

\displaystyle\leq f_{{w}}(x)[y]-f_{{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]

\displaystyle\leq f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]+\frac{\gamma}{2}\qquad(\text{Eq. (\ref{eq:pac_bayes_deterministic_tmp_4})})

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[y]-f_{\tilde{w}}(x)[j_{1}^{\ast}]\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}~\Rightarrow~f_{{w}}(x)[y]-f_{{w}}(x)[j_{2}^{\ast}]\leq\gamma,

which indicates
L_{\mathcal{D},\frac{\gamma}{2}}(f_{\tilde{w}})\leq L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}(f_{{w}})
.
Therefore, from the perspective of the empirical estimation of the probability, for any
\tilde{w}\sim\tilde{Q}
, we almost surely have

\displaystyle L_{S,\frac{\gamma}{2}}(f_{\tilde{w}})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{{w}}).

(18)

Now with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{w}\sim\tilde{Q}}\left[L_{\mathcal{D},\frac{\gamma}{2}}(f_{\tilde{w}})\right]\qquad(\text{Eq. (\ref{eq:pac_bayes_deterministic_tmp_3})})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{w}\sim\tilde{Q}}\left[L_{S,\frac{\gamma}{2}}(f_{\tilde{w}})\right]+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\tilde{Q}\|P)+\log\frac{2m}{\delta}}{2(m-1)}}\qquad(\text{Theorem \ref{thm:pac_bayes}})

\displaystyle\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\tilde{Q}\|P)+\log\frac{2m}{\delta}}{2(m-1)}}\qquad(\text{Eq. (\ref{eq:pac_bayes_deterministic_tmp_5})})

(19)

Note that

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|P)

\displaystyle=\underset{{\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{C}}}{\int}Q\log\frac{Q}{P}\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}+\underset{\tilde{w}\in\bar{\mathcal{C}}}{\int}Q\log\frac{Q}{P}\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}

\displaystyle=\underset{\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{C}}{\int}\frac{QZ}{Z}\log\frac{Q}{ZP}\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}+\underset{\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{C}}{\int}Q\log Z\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}

\displaystyle\quad+\underset{\tilde{w}\in\bar{\mathcal{C}}}{\int}\frac{Q(1-Z)}{1-Z}\log\frac{Q}{(1-Z)P}\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}+\underset{\tilde{w}\in\bar{\mathcal{C}}}{\int}Q\log(1-Z)\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}

\displaystyle=ZD_{\mathrm{KL}}(\tilde{Q}\|P)+(1-Z)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{Q}\|P)-H(Z),

(20)

where
\bar{Q}
denotes the normalized density of
Q
restricted to
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
.

H(Z)
is the entropy of a Bernoulli random variable with parameter
Z
.
Since we know
\frac{1}{2}\leq Z\leq 1
from the beginning,
0\leq H(Z)\leq\log 2
, and
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
is nonnegative, from Eq. (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\tilde{Q}\|P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|P)+H(Z)-(1-Z)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{Q}\|P)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Z}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|P)+H(Z)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|P)+2\log 2.

(21)

Combining Eq. (
A.1
) and Eq. (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|P)+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{8m}{\delta}}{m-1}},

(22)

which finishes the proof.
∎

A.2
Graph Results

In this part, we provide a result on the graph Laplacian used by GCNs along with the proof.
It is used in the perturbation analysis of GCNs.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
A
be the binary adjacency matrix of an arbitrary simple graph
G=(V,E)
and
\tilde{A}=A+I
.
We define the graph Laplacian
L=D^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{A}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}
where
D
is the degree matrix of
\tilde{A}
. Then we have
\|L\|_{1}=\|L\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{d}
,
\|L\|_{2}\leq 1
, and
\|L\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{r}
where
r
is the rank of
L
and
d-1
is the maximum node degree of
G
.

Proof.

First,
\tilde{A}
is symmetric and element-wise nonnegative.
Denoting
n=|V|
, we have
\tilde{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
D_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{A}[i,j]
, and
1\leq D_{i}\leq d,\forall i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{n}
.
It is easy to show that
L[i,j]={\tilde{A}[i,j]}/{\sqrt{D_{i}D_{j}}}
.

For the infinity norm and 1-norm, we have
\|L\|_{1}=\|L^{\top}\|_{\infty}=\|L\|_{\infty}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|L\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|L[i,j]\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\tilde{A}[i,j]}{\sqrt{D_{i}D_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{D_{i}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{A}[i,j]

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{n}}\sqrt{D_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}

(23)

For the spectral norm, based on the definition, we have

\displaystyle\|L\|_{2}=\sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{|Lx|_{2}}{|x|_{2}}=\sigma_{\max},

(24)

where
\sigma_{\max}
is the maximum singular value of
L
.
Since
L
is symmetric, we have
\sigma_{i}=|\lambda_{i}|
where
\lambda_{i}
is the
i
-th eigenvalue of
L
.
Hence,
\sigma_{\max}=\max_{i}|\lambda_{i}|
.
From Raylaigh quotient and Courant–Fischer minimax theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|L\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\max_{i}|\lambda_{i}|=\max_{x\neq 0}\left|\frac{x^{\top}Lx}{x^{\top}x}\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{x\neq 0}\left|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}L[i,j]x_{i}x_{j}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{x\neq 0}\left|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\tilde{A}[i,j]x_{i}x_{j}}/{\sqrt{D_{i}D_{j}}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{x\neq 0}\left|\frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in\tilde{E}}{x_{i}x_{j}}/{\sqrt{D_{i}D_{j}}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\neq 0}\left|\frac{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\tilde{E}}\left({x_{i}^{2}}/{D_{i}}+{x_{j}^{2}}/{D_{j}}\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{x\neq 0}\left|\frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in\tilde{E}}{x_{i}^{2}}/{D_{i}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}\right|=\max_{x\neq 0}\left|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}{x_{i}^{2}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}\right|=1,

(25)

where
\tilde{E}
is the union of the set of edges
E
in the original graph and the set of self-loops.
For Frobenius norm, we have
\|L\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{r}\|L\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{r}
where
r
is the rank of
L
.
∎

A.3
GCN Results

In this part, we provide the proofs of the main results regarding GCNs.

Lemma
3.1
.

(GCN Perturbation Bound) For any
B>0,l>1
, let
f_{w}\in\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{K}
be a
l
-layer GCN. Then for any
w
, and
x\in\mathcal{X}_{B,h_{0}}
, and any perturbation
u=\text{vec}(\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l})
such that
\forall i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{l}
,
\|U_{i}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}
, the change in the output of GCN is bounded as,

\displaystyle\left|f_{w+u}(X,A)-f_{w}(X,A)\right|_{2}\leq eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}

Proof.

We first perform the recursive perturbation analysis on node representations of all layers except the last one,
i.e
.
, the readout layer.
Then we derive the bound for the graph representation of the last readout layer.

Perturbation Analysis on Node Representations.

In GCN, for any layer
j<l
besides the last readout one, the node representations are,

\displaystyle f_{w}^{j}(X,A)=H_{j}=\sigma_{j}\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right).

(26)

We add perturbation
u
to the weights
w
,
i.e
.
, for the
j
-th layer, the perturbed weights are
W_{j}+U_{j}
.
For the ease of notation, we use the superscript of prime to denote the perturbed node representations,
e.g
.
,
H_{j}^{\prime}=f_{w+u}^{j}(X,A)
.
Let
\Delta_{j}=f_{w+u}^{j}(X,A)-f_{w}^{j}(X,A)=H_{j}^{\prime}-H_{j}
.
Note that
\Delta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times h_{j}}
.
Let
\Psi_{j}=\max\limits_{i}\left|\Delta_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}=\max\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}^{\prime}[i,:]-H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
and
\Phi_{j}=\max\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
.
We denote the
u_{j}^{\ast}=\argmax\limits_{i}\left|\Delta_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
and
v_{j}^{\ast}=\argmax\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
.

Upper Bound on the Max Node Representation

For any layer
j<l
, we can derive an upper bound on the maximum (w.r.t.

\ell_{2}
norm) node representation as follows,

\displaystyle\Phi_{j}

\displaystyle=\max_{i}\left|H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}=\left|\left(\sigma_{j}\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right)\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}=\left|\sigma_{j}\left(\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\qquad(\text{Lipschitz property of ReLU under vector 2-norm})

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]W_{j}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{2}=\left|\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{v_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[v_{j}^{\ast},k]H_{j-1}[k,:]\right|_{2}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{v_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[v_{j}^{\ast},k]\left|H_{j-1}[k,:]\right|_{2}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{v_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[v_{j}^{\ast},k]\Phi_{j-1}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{2}\qquad\left(\text{since }\forall i,\left|H_{j-1}[i,:]\right|_{2}\leq\Phi_{j-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq d^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{j-1}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq d^{\frac{j}{2}}\Phi_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\qquad(\text{unroll the recursion})

\displaystyle\leq d^{\frac{j}{2}}B\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|W_{i}\|_{2},

(27)

where in the last inequality we use the fact
\Phi_{0}=\max_{i}\left|X[i,:]\right|_{2}\leq B
based on the assumption
A3
.

\mathcal{N}_{v_{j}^{\ast}}
is the set of neighboring nodes (including itself) of node
v_{j}^{\ast}
.
In the third from the last inequality, we use the Lemma
A.3
to derive the following fact that
\forall i
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\tilde{L}[i,k]=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\left|\tilde{L}[i,k]\right|\leq\left\|\tilde{L}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{d}.

(28)

Upper Bound on the Max Change of Node Representation.

For any layer
j<l
, we can derive an upper bound on the maximum (w.r.t.

\ell_{2}
norm) change between the representations with and without the weight perturbation for any node as follows,

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}^{\prime}[i,:]-H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}=\left|\sigma_{j}\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}^{\prime}(W_{j}+U_{j})\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]-\sigma_{j}\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\qquad

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}^{\prime}(W_{j}+U_{j})\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]-\left(\tilde{L}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\qquad(\text{Lipschitz property of ReLU})

\displaystyle=\left|\left((\tilde{L}H_{j-1}^{\prime})[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)(W_{j}+U_{j})-\left((\tilde{L}H_{j-1})[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)W_{j}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\left((\tilde{L}H_{j-1}^{\prime})[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)-\left((\tilde{L}H_{j-1})[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)\right)(W_{j}+U_{j})+\left((\tilde{L}H_{j-1})[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)U_{j}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]\left(H_{j-1}^{\prime}[k,:]-H_{j-1}[k,:]\right)\right)(W_{j}+U_{j})+\left(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]H_{j-1}[k,:]\right)U_{j}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]\left(H_{j-1}^{\prime}[k,:]-H_{j-1}[k,:]\right)\right|_{2}\left\|W_{j}+U_{j}\right\|_{2}+\left|\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]H_{j-1}[k,:]\right|_{2}\left\|U_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]\left|H_{j-1}^{\prime}[k,:]-H_{j-1}[k,:]\right|_{2}\left\|W_{j}+U_{j}\right\|_{2}+\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]\left|H_{j-1}[k,:]\right|_{2}\left\|U_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]\Psi_{j-1}\left\|W_{j}+U_{j}\right\|_{2}+\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{u_{j}^{\ast}}}\tilde{L}[u_{j}^{\ast},k]\Phi_{j-1}\left\|U_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}\Psi_{j-1}\left\|W_{j}+U_{j}\right\|_{2}+\sqrt{d}\Phi_{j-1}\left\|U_{j}\right\|_{2},

(29)

where in the second from the last inequality we use the fact
\forall k
,
\left|H_{j-1}^{\prime}[k,:]-H_{j-1}[k,:]\right|_{2}\leq\Psi_{j-1}
and
\forall k
,
\left|H_{j-1}[k,:]\right|_{2}\leq\Phi_{j-1}
.
In the last inequality, we again use the fact in Eq. (
28
).
We can simplify the notations in Eq. (
A.3
) as
\Psi_{j}\leq a_{j-1}\Psi_{j-1}+b_{j-1}
where
a_{j-1}=\sqrt{d}\|W_{j}+U_{j}\|_{2}
and
b_{j-1}=\sqrt{d}\Phi_{j-1}\|U_{j}\|_{2}
.
Since
\Delta_{0}=X-X=\bm{0}
, we have
\Psi_{0}=0
.
It is straightforward to work out the recursion as,

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}b_{k}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}a_{i}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}d^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{k}\|U_{k+1}\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}d^{\frac{1}{2}}\|W_{i+1}+U_{i+1}\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}d^{\frac{j-k}{2}}\Phi_{k}\|U_{k+1}\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\|W_{i}+U_{i}\|_{2}\right).

(30)

Based on Eq. (
A.3
), we can instantiate the bound in Eq. (
A.3
) as

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}d^{\frac{j-k}{2}}\Phi_{k}\|U_{k+1}\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\|W_{i}+U_{i}\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}d^{\frac{j-k}{2}}\left(d^{\frac{k}{2}}B\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\|U_{k+1}\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\left(\|W_{i}\|_{2}+\|U_{i}\|_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq B\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}d^{\frac{j}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\|U_{k+1}\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=B\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}d^{\frac{j}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\frac{\|U_{k+1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k+1}\|_{2}}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=Bd^{\frac{j}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{\|U_{k+1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k+1}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq Bd^{\frac{j}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{j}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j-k}

(31)

Final Bound on the Readout Layer

Now let us consider the average readout function in the last layer,
i.e
.
, the
l
-th layer.
Based on Eq. (
A.3
) and Eq. (
A.3
), we can bound the change of GCN’s output with and without the weight perturbation as follows,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{l}|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}^{\prime}(W_{l}+U_{l})-\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}W_{l}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}\Delta_{l-1}(W_{l}+U_{l})+\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}U_{l}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left|\bm{1}_{n}\Delta_{l-1}(W_{l}+U_{l})\right|_{2}+\frac{1}{n}\left|\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}U_{l}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\|W_{l}+U_{l}\|_{2}|\bm{1}_{n}\Delta_{l-1}|_{2}+\frac{1}{n}\|U_{l}\|_{2}|\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left\|W_{l}+U_{l}\right\|_{2}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{l-1}[i,:]\right|_{2}+\frac{1}{n}\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{l-1}[i,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left\|W_{l}+U_{l}\right\|_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\Delta_{l-1}[i,:]\right|_{2}\right)+\frac{1}{n}\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|H_{l-1}[i,:]\right|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|W_{l}+U_{l}\right\|_{2}\Psi_{l-1}+\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}\Phi_{l-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|W_{l}+U_{l}\right\|_{2}Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1-k}+\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\|W_{i}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left[\left\|W_{l}+U_{l}\right\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1-k}+\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\left[\frac{\left\|W_{l}+U_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1-k}+\frac{\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1-k}+\frac{\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-k}+\frac{\left\|U_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-l}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\left[\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}\right]\qquad\left(\text{Use }1\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\leq e\right)

(32)

which proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem
3.2
.

(GCN Generalization Bound) For any
B>0,l>1
, let
f_{w}\in\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{K}
be a
l
-layer GCN. Then for any
\delta,\gamma>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the choice of an i.i.d. size-
m
training set
S
according to
\mathcal{D}
, for any
w
, we have,

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}d^{l-1}l^{2}h\log(lh)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}+\log\frac{ml}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right)

Proof.

Let
\beta=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)^{1/l}
.
We normalize the weights as
\tilde{W}_{i}=\frac{\beta}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}}W_{i}
.
Due to the homogeneity of ReLU,
i.e
.
,
a\phi(x)=\phi(ax)
,
\forall a\geq 0
, we have
f_{w}=f_{\tilde{w}}
.
We can also verify that
\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|\tilde{W}_{i}\|_{2}
and
\|W_{i}\|_{{F}}/\|W_{i}\|_{2}=\|\tilde{W}_{i}\|_{{F}}/\|\tilde{W}_{i}\|_{2}
,
i.e
.
, the terms appear in the bound stay the same after applying the normalization.
Therefore, w.l.o.g., we assume that the norm is equal across layers,
i.e
.
,
\forall i
,
\|W_{i}\|_{2}=\beta
.

Consider the prior
P=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
and the random perturbation
u\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
.
Note that the
\sigma
of the prior and the perturbation are the same and will be set according to
\beta
.
More precisely, we will set the
\sigma
based on some approximation
\tilde{\beta}
of
\beta
since the prior
P
can not depend on any learned weights directly.
The approximation
\tilde{\beta}
is chosen to be a cover set which covers the meaningful range of
\beta
.
For now, let us assume that we have a fix
\tilde{\beta}
and consider
\beta
which satisfies
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l}\beta
.
Note that this also implies

\displaystyle|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l}\beta

\displaystyle~\Rightarrow~\left(1-\frac{1}{l}\right)\beta\leq\tilde{\beta}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\beta

\displaystyle~\Rightarrow~\left(1-\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1}\beta^{l-1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1}\beta^{l-1}

\displaystyle~\Rightarrow~\left(1-\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\beta^{l-1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\beta^{l-1}

\displaystyle~\Rightarrow~\frac{1}{e}\beta^{l-1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}\leq e\beta^{l-1}

(33)

From
Tropp 2012
, for
U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times h}
and
U_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\sigma^{2}I)
, we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|U_{i}\|_{2}\geq t\right)\leq 2he^{-t^{2}/2h\sigma^{2}}.

(34)

Taking a union bound, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|U_{1}\|_{2}<t~\&~\cdots~\&~\|U_{l}\|_{2}<t\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\exists i,\|U_{i}\|_{2}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathbb{P}\left(\|U_{i}\|_{2}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-2lhe^{-t^{2}/2h\sigma^{2}}.

(35)

Setting
2lhe^{-t^{2}/2h\sigma^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
, we have
t=\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}
.
This implies that the probability that the spectral norm of the perturbation of any layer is no larger than
\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}
holds with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}
.
Plugging this bound into Lemma
3.1
, we have with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\left|f_{w+u}(X,A)-f_{w}(X,A)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\|W_{k}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle=eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\beta^{l}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\|U_{k}\|_{2}}{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\beta^{l-1}l\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{2}Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}l\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}\leq\frac{\gamma}{4},

(36)

where we can set
\sigma=\frac{\gamma}{42Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}l\sqrt{h\log(4lh)}}
to get the last inequality.
Note that Lemma
3.1
also requires
\forall i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{l}
,
\|U_{i}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}
.
The requirement is satisfied if
\sigma\leq\frac{\beta}{l\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}}
which in turn can be satisfied if

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{4eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\beta^{l-1}l\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}}\leq\frac{\beta}{l\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}},

(37)

since the chosen value of
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma\leq\frac{\gamma}{4eBd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\beta^{l-1}l\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}}
.
Note that Eq. (
37
) is equivalent to
\frac{\gamma}{4eB}d^{\frac{1-l}{2}}\leq\beta^{l}
.
We will see how to satisfy this condition later.

We now compute the KL term in the PAC-Bayes bound in Lemma
2.2
.

\displaystyle\text{KL}\left(Q\|P\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|w|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}=\frac{42^{2}B^{2}d^{l-1}\tilde{\beta}^{2l-2}l^{2}h\log(4lh)}{2\gamma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{B^{2}d^{l-1}\beta^{2l}l^{2}h\log(lh)}{\gamma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(B^{2}d^{l-1}l^{2}h\log(lh)\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}\right).

(38)

From Lemma
2.2
, fixing any
\tilde{\beta}
, with probability
1-\delta
and for all
w
such that
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l}\beta
, we have,

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}d^{l-1}l^{2}h\log(lh)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}+\log\frac{m}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right).

(39)

Finally, we need to consider multiple choices of
\tilde{\beta}
so that for any
\beta
, we can bound the generalization error like Eq. (
39
).
First, we only need to consider values of
\beta
in the following range,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{d}}{2B}\right)^{1/l}\leq\beta\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{md}}{2B}\right)^{1/l},

(40)

since otherwise the bound holds trivially as
L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq 1
by definition.
Note that the lower bound in Eq. (
40
) ensures that Eq. (
37
) holds which in turn justifies the applicability of Lemma
3.1
.
If
\beta<\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{d}}{2B}\right)^{1/l}
, then for any
(X,A)
and any
j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}_{K}
,
|f(X,A)[j]|\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}
.
To see this, we have,

\displaystyle\left|f_{w}(X,A)[j]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f_{w}(X,A)\right|_{2}=|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}W_{l}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}|\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}|_{2}\|W_{l}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|W_{l}\|_{2}\max_{i}|H_{l-1}[i,:]|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}=d^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\beta^{l}B\qquad(\text{Use Eq. (\ref{eq:gcn_output_bound})})

\displaystyle=d^{\frac{l-1}{2}}B\frac{\gamma}{2Bd^{\frac{l-1}{2}}}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}.

(41)

Therefore, by the definition in Eq. (
4
), we always have
L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})=1
when
\beta<\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{d}}{2B}\right)^{1/l}
.
Alternatively, if
\beta>\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{md}}{2B}\right)^{1/l}
, the term inside the big-O notation in Eq. (
39
) would be,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}d^{l-1}l^{2}h\log(lh)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}+\log\frac{m}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}h\log(lh)}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}h\log(lh)}{4}}\geq 1,

(42)

where we use the facts that
\|W_{i}\|_{F}\geq\|W_{i}\|_{2}
and we typically choose
h\geq 2
in practice and
l\geq 2
.

Since we only need to consider
\beta
in the range of Eq. (
40
), a sufficient condition to make
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l}\beta
hold would be
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{d}}{2B}\right)^{1/l}
.
Therefore, if we can find a covering of the interval in Eq. (
40
) with radius
\frac{1}{l\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{d}}{2B}\right)^{1/l}
and make sure bounds like Eq. (
39
) holds while
\tilde{\beta}
takes all possible values from the covering, then we can get a bound which holds for all
\beta
.
It is clear that we only need to consider a covering
C
with size
|C|=\frac{l}{2}\left(m^{\frac{1}{2l}}-1\right)
.
Therefore, denoting the event of Eq. (
39
) with
\tilde{\beta}
taking the
i
-th value of the covering as
E_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(E_{1}~\&~\cdots~\&~E_{|C|}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\exists i,\bar{E}_{i}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{|C|}\mathbb{P}\left(\bar{E}_{i}\right)\geq 1-|C|\delta.

(43)

Note
\bar{E}_{i}
denotes the complement of
E_{i}
.
Hence, with probability
1-\delta
and for all
w
, we have,

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})

\displaystyle\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}d^{l-1}l^{2}h\log(lh)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}+\log\frac{m|C|}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right)

\displaystyle=L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}d^{l-1}l^{2}h\log(lh)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}+\log\frac{ml}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right),

(44)

which proves the theorem.
∎

A.4
MPGNNs Results

In this part, we provide the proofs of the main results regarding MPGNNs.

Lemma
3.3
.

(MPGNN Perturbation Bound) For any
B>0,l>1
, let
f_{w}\in\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{K}
be a
l
-step MPGNN.
Then for any
w
, and
x\in\mathcal{X}_{B,h_{0}}
, and any perturbation
u=\text{vec}(\{U_{1},U_{2},U_{l}\})
such that
\eta=\max\left(\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{l}
, the change in the output of MPGNN is bounded as,

\displaystyle|f_{w+u}(

\displaystyle X,A)-f_{w}(X,A)|_{2}\leq\begin{cases}eB\left(l+1\right)^{2}\eta\|W_{1}\|_{2}\|W_{l}\|_{2}C_{\phi},&\text{if}\ d\mathcal{C}=1\\
eBl\eta\|W_{1}\|_{2}\|W_{l}\|_{2}C_{\phi}\frac{\left(d\mathcal{C}\right)^{l-1}-1}{d\mathcal{C}-1},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{C}=C_{\phi}C_{\rho}C_{g}\|W_{2}\|_{2}
.

Proof.

We first perform the recursive perturbation analysis on node representations of all steps except the last one, i.e., the readout step. Then we derive the bound for the graph representation of the last readout step.

Perturbation Analysis on Node Representations.

In message passing GNNs, for any step
j<l
besides the last readout one, the node representations are,

\displaystyle\bar{M}_{j}

\displaystyle=C_{\operatorname{in}}g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}\right)

\displaystyle H_{j}

\displaystyle=\phi\left(XW_{1}+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right),

(45)

where the incidence matrices
C_{\operatorname{in}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times c}
and
C_{\operatorname{out}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times c}
(recall
c
is the number of edges).
Moreover, since each edge only connects one incoming and one outgoing node, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i}\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]=\|C_{\operatorname{in}}\|_{\infty}\leq d

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]\leq\|C_{\operatorname{out}}\|_{1}\leq 1

(46)

where
d-1
is the maximum node degree.
Note that one actually has
\|C_{\operatorname{in}}\|_{\infty}\leq d-1
for simple graphs.
Since some models in the literature pre-process the graphs by adding self-loops, we thus relax it to
\|C_{\operatorname{in}}\|_{\infty}\leq d
which holds in both cases.

We add perturbation
u
to the weights
w
,
i.e
.
, the perturbed weights are
W_{1}+U_{1}
,
W_{2}+U_{2}
and
W_{l}+U_{l}
.
For the ease of notation, we use the superscript of prime to denote the perturbed node representations,
e.g
.
,
H_{j}^{\prime}=f_{w+u}^{j}(X,A)
.
Let
\Delta_{j}=f_{w+u}^{j}(X,A)-f_{w}^{j}(X,A)=H_{j}^{\prime}-H_{j}
.
Note that
\Delta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times h_{j}}
.
Let
\Psi_{j}=\max\limits_{i}\left|\Delta_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}=\max\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}^{\prime}[i,:]-H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
and
\Phi_{j}=\max\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
.
We denote the
u_{j}^{\ast}=\argmax\limits_{i}\left|\Delta_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
and
v_{j}^{\ast}=\argmax\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}
.
To simplify the derivation, we abbreviate the following statistics
\kappa=C_{\phi}B\|W_{1}\|_{2}
and
\tau=d\mathcal{C}
throughout the proof where
\mathcal{C}=C_{\phi}C_{\rho}C_{g}\|W_{2}\|_{2}
is the
percolation complexity
.

Upper Bound on the Max Node Representation.

For any step
j<l
, we can derive an upper bound on the
\ell_{2}
norm of the aggregated message of any node
i
as follows,

\displaystyle\left|\bar{M}_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]\left(g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}\right)\right)[k,:]\right|_{2}=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}[k,:]H_{j-1}\right)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]\left|g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}[k,:]H_{j-1}\right)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left|C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}[k,:]H_{j-1}\right|_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left|\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}[k,t]H_{j-1}[t,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]\left|H_{j-1}[t,:]\right|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]\Phi_{j-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq dC_{g}\Phi_{j-1}.

(47)

Then we can derive an upper bound on the maximum (w.r.t.
\ell_{2}
norm) node representation as follows,

\displaystyle\Phi_{j}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}=\left|\phi\left(XW_{1}+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\phi\left(\left(XW_{1}+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}\left|\left(XW_{1}+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{\phi}\left|\left(XW_{1}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]+\left(\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}\left|\left(XW_{1}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}+C_{\phi}\left|\left(\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{\phi}\left|X[v_{j}^{\ast},:]W_{1}\right|_{2}+C_{\phi}\left|\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]W_{2}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}\left|X[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\|W_{1}\|_{2}+C_{\phi}\left|\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\|W_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}B\|W_{1}\|_{2}+C_{\phi}\left|\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)\right|_{2}\|W_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}B\|W_{1}\|_{2}+C_{\phi}C_{\rho}\left|\bar{M}_{j}[v_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\|W_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}B\|W_{1}\|_{2}+dC_{\phi}C_{\rho}C_{g}\Phi_{j-1}\|W_{2}\|_{2}=\kappa+\tau\Phi_{j-1}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{j}\Phi_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\tau^{j-1-i}\kappa\qquad(\text{Unroll recursion})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\tau^{j-1-i}\kappa\qquad(\text{Use }\Phi_{0}=0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}j\kappa,&\text{if}\ \tau=1\\
\kappa\frac{\tau^{j}-1}{\tau-1},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(48)

Upper Bound on the Max Change of Node Representation.

For any step
j<l
, we can derive an upper bound on the maximum (w.r.t.
\ell_{2}
norm) change between the aggregated message with and without the weight perturbation for any node
i
as follows,

\displaystyle\left|\bar{M}_{j}^{\prime}[i,:]-\bar{M}_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(C_{\operatorname{in}}g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}^{\prime}\right)\right)[i,:]-\left(C_{\operatorname{in}}g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}\right)\right)[i,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]\left(g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}^{\prime}\right)-g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}\right)\right)[k,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]\left|\left(g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}^{\prime}\right)-g\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}\right)\right)[k,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]\left|g\left(\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}^{\prime}\right)[k,:]\right)-g\left(\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}\right)[k,:]\right)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left|\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}^{\prime}\right)[k,:]-\left(C_{\operatorname{out}}^{\top}H_{j-1}\right)[k,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left|\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]H_{j-1}^{\prime}[t,:]-\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]H_{j-1}[t,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left|\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]\left(H_{j-1}^{\prime}[t,:]-H_{j-1}[t,:]\right)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{c}C_{\operatorname{in}}[i,k]C_{g}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{\operatorname{out}}[t,k]\left|H_{j-1}^{\prime}[t,:]-H_{j-1}[t,:]\right|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq dC_{g}\Psi_{j-1}

(49)

Based on Eq. (
A.4
), we can derive an upper bound on the maximum (w.r.t.
\ell_{2}
norm) change between the representations with and without the weight perturbation for any node as follows,

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{i}\left|H_{j}^{\prime}[i,:]-H_{j}[i,:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(\phi\left(X(W_{1}+U_{1})+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}^{\prime}\right)(W_{2}+U_{2})\right)\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]-\left(\phi\left(XW_{1}+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\phi\left(\left(X(W_{1}+U_{1})+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}^{\prime}\right)(W_{2}+U_{2})\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)-\phi\left(\left(XW_{1}+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right)\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\phi}\left|\left(X(W_{1}+U_{1})+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}^{\prime}\right)(W_{2}+U_{2})\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]-\left(XW_{1}+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)W_{2}\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\phi}\left|X[u_{j}^{\ast},:]U_{1}+\left(\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}^{\prime}\right)\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:](W_{2}+U_{2})-\left(\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]W_{2}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\phi}\left|X[u_{j}^{\ast},:]U_{1}+\left(\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}^{\prime}\right)-\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:](W_{2}+U_{2})+\rho\left(\bar{M}_{j}\right)[u_{j}^{\ast},:]U_{2}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\phi}B\left\|U_{1}\right\|_{2}+C_{\phi}C_{\rho}\left|\bar{M}_{j}^{\prime}[u_{j}^{\ast},:]-\bar{M}_{j}[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\left\|W_{2}+U_{2}\right\|_{2}+C_{\phi}C_{\rho}\left|\bar{M}_{j}[u_{j}^{\ast},:]\right|_{2}\left\|U_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}}+d\mathcal{C}\Psi_{j-1}\frac{\|W_{2}+U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}+d\mathcal{C}\Phi_{j-1}\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}\qquad(\text{Use Eq. (\ref{eq:mpgnn_msg_bound}) and (\ref{eq:mpgnn_msg_diff_bound})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left(1+\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}}+\tau\Phi_{j-1}\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}

(50)

If
\tau=1
, then we have,

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left(1+\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}}+\tau\Phi_{j-1}\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\left\|U_{2}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{2}}\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\left(\frac{\left\|U_{1}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{2}}+\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}(j-1)\right)\qquad(\text{Use Eq. (\ref{eq:mpgnn_output_bound})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\eta\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\eta\left(1+(j-1)\right)\qquad\left(\text{Use }\eta=\max\left(\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\eta\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\eta j.

(51)

If
\tau\neq 1
, then we have,

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left(1+\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}}+\tau\Phi_{j-1}\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left(1+\frac{\left\|U_{2}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{2}}\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\left(\frac{\left\|U_{1}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{2}}+\tau\frac{\left\|U_{2}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{2}}\frac{\tau^{j-1}-1}{\tau-1}\right)\qquad(\text{Use Eq. (\ref{eq:mpgnn_output_bound})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left(1+\eta\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\eta\left(1+\frac{\tau^{j}-\tau}{\tau-1}\right)\qquad\left(\text{Use }\eta=\max\left(\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\left(1+\eta\right)\Psi_{j-1}+\kappa\eta\left(\frac{\tau^{j}-1}{\tau-1}\right).

(52)

Recall from Eq. (
A.3
), if
\Psi_{j}\leq a_{j-1}\Psi_{j-1}+b_{j-1}
and
\Psi_{0}=0
, then
\Psi_{j}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}b_{k}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}a_{i}\right)
.
If
\tau=1
, then we have
a_{j-1}=1+\eta
,
b_{j-1}=\kappa\eta j
in our case and,

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}\leq

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k=0}^{j-1}b_{k}\left(\prod\nolimits_{i=k+1}^{j-1}a_{i}\right)=\sum\nolimits_{k=0}^{j-1}\kappa\eta(k+1)\left(1+\eta\right)^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\eta\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(k+1)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-k-1}\qquad\left(\text{Use }\eta\leq\frac{1}{l}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\eta\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}\sum_{k=1}^{j}k\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\eta\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-1}\frac{1-(j+1)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-j}+j\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-j-1}}{\left(1-\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-1}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\eta\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j+1}-(j+1)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)+j}{\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{1}-1\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\eta l^{2}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j+1}-(j+1)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)+j\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\eta l^{2}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j+1}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\eta l\left(l+1\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}

(53)

If
\tau\neq 1
, then we have
a_{j-1}=\tau\left(1+\eta\right)
,
b_{j-1}=\kappa\eta\left(\frac{\tau^{j}-1}{\tau-1}\right)
in our case and,

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}b_{k}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}a_{i}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\kappa\eta\left(\frac{\tau^{k+1}-1}{\tau-1}\right)\tau^{j-k-1}\left(1+\eta\right)^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\eta\tau^{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{\tau^{k+1}-1}{\tau-1}\right)\tau^{-k-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-k-1}\qquad\left(\text{Use }\eta\leq\frac{1}{l}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\eta\tau^{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1-\tau^{-k-1}}{\tau-1}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\eta\tau^{j}}{\tau-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(1-\tau^{-k-1}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\eta\tau^{j}}{\tau-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\left(1-\tau^{-k}\right)

(54)

Final Bound with Readout Function

Now let us consider the readout function.
Since the last readout layer produces a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{1\times C}
, we have,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{l}|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}^{\prime}(W_{l}+U_{l})-\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}W_{l}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}\Delta_{l-1}(W_{l}+U_{l})+\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}U_{l}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left|\bm{1}_{n}\Delta_{l-1}(W_{l}+U_{l})\right|_{2}+\frac{1}{n}\left|\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}U_{l}\right|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\|W_{l}+U_{l}\|_{2}|\bm{1}_{n}\Delta_{l-1}|_{2}+\frac{1}{n}\|U_{l}\|_{2}|\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}+U_{l}\|_{2}\Psi_{l-1}+\|U_{l}\|_{2}\Phi_{l-1}

(55)

If
\tau=1
, we have,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{l}|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\kappa\eta l\left(l+1\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1}+(l-1)\kappa\|U_{l}\|_{2}\qquad(\text{Use Eq. (\ref{eq:mpgnn_output_bound}), (\ref{eq:psi_value_tau_is_1})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\kappa\left(\eta l\left(l+1\right)+(l-1)\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-l}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}e\kappa\eta\left(l(l+1)+(l-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}e\kappa\eta\left(l^{2}+2l-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}e\kappa\eta\left(l+1\right)^{2}

(56)

Otherwise, we have,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{l}|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\frac{\kappa\eta\tau^{l-1}}{\tau-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\left(1-\tau^{-k}\right)+\kappa\|U_{l}\|_{2}\frac{\tau^{l-1}-1}{\tau-1}\qquad(\text{Use Eq. (\ref{eq:mpgnn_output_bound}), (\ref{eq:psi_value_tau_is_not_1})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\frac{\kappa\tau^{l-1}}{\tau-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\left(\eta\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\left(1-\tau^{-k}\right)+\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}(1-\tau^{1-l})\right)

(57)

If
\tau>1
, then
\frac{1-\tau^{-k}}{\tau-1}\leq\frac{1-\tau^{1-l}}{\tau-1}
when
1\leq k\leq l-1
.
If
\tau<1
, we also have
\frac{1-\tau^{-k}}{\tau-1}\leq\frac{1-\tau^{1-l}}{\tau-1}
when
1\leq k\leq l-1
.
Therefore, Eq. (
A.4
) can be further relaxed as,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{l}|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\frac{\kappa\tau^{l-1}}{\tau-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\left(\eta\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\left(1-\tau^{-k}\right)+\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}(1-\tau^{1-l})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\kappa\tau^{l-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\left(\eta\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{1-\tau^{-k}}{\tau-1}+\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\frac{1-\tau^{1-l}}{\tau-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\kappa\tau^{l-1}e\left(\eta(l-1)\frac{\left(1-\tau^{1-l}\right)}{\tau-1}+\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\frac{\left(1-\tau^{1-l}\right)}{\tau-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{l}\|_{2}\kappa\tau^{l-1}e\eta l\frac{\left(1-\tau^{1-l}\right)}{\tau-1}\qquad\left(\text{Use }\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\leq\eta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\eta l\kappa\|W_{l}\|_{2}\frac{\tau^{l-1}-1}{\tau-1},

(58)

Therefore, combining Eq. (
A.4
) and Eq. (
A.4
), we have,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{l}|_{2}\leq\begin{cases}e\eta\kappa\left(l+1\right)^{2}\|W_{l}\|_{2},&\text{if}\ d\mathcal{C}=1\\
e\eta\kappa l\|W_{l}\|_{2}\frac{\tau^{l-1}-1}{\tau-1},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(59)

which proves the lemma.

Theorem
3.4
.

(MPGNN Generalization Bound) For any
B>0,l>1
, let
f_{w}\in\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{K}
be a
l
-step MPGNN.
Then for any
\delta,\gamma>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the choice of an i.i.d. size-
m
training set
S
according to
\mathcal{D}
, for any
w
, we have,

1.

If
d\mathcal{C}\neq 1
, then

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}\left(\max\left(\zeta^{-(l+1)},(\lambda\xi)^{(l+1)/l}\right)\right)^{2}l^{2}h\log(lh)|w|_{2}^{2}+\log\frac{m(l+1)}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right).

2.

If
d\mathcal{C}=1
, then

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}\max\left(\zeta^{-6},\lambda^{3}C_{\phi}^{3}\right)(l+1)^{4}h\log(lh)|w|_{2}^{2}+\log\frac{m}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right).

where

\zeta=\min\left(\|W_{1}\|_{2},\|W_{2}\|_{2},\|W_{l}\|_{2}\right)
,

|w|_{2}^{2}=\|W_{1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|W_{2}\|_{F}^{2}+\|W_{l}\|_{F}^{2}
,

\mathcal{C}=C_{\phi}C_{\rho}C_{g}\|W_{2}\|_{2}
,

\lambda=\|W_{1}\|_{2}\|W_{l}\|_{2}
,
and
\xi=C_{\phi}\frac{\left(d\mathcal{C}\right)^{l-1}-1}{d\mathcal{C}-1}
.

Proof.

We will derive the results conditioning on the value of
d\mathcal{C}
.

General Case
d\mathcal{C}\neq 1

We first consider the general case
d\mathcal{C}\neq 1
.
To derive the generalization bound, we construct a special statistic of the learned weights

\beta=\max\left(\frac{1}{\zeta},\left(\lambda\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{l}}\right)
.
It is clear that
\frac{1}{\zeta}\leq\beta
,
\lambda\xi\leq\beta^{l}
, and
\lambda\xi/\zeta\leq\beta^{l+1}
.
Note that
\frac{1}{\zeta}=\max\left(\frac{1}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}},\frac{1}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}},\frac{1}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\right)
.

Consider the prior
P=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
and the random perturbation
u\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
.
Note that the
\sigma
of the prior and the perturbation are the same and will be set according to
\beta
.
More precisely, we will set the
\sigma
based on some approximation
\tilde{\beta}
of
\beta
since the prior
P
can not depend on any learned weights directly.
The approximation
\tilde{\beta}
is chosen to be a cover set which covers the meaningful range of
\beta
.
For now, let us fix any
\tilde{\beta}
and consider
\beta
which satisfies
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l+1}\beta
.
This also implies,

\displaystyle|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l+1}\beta~\Rightarrow~

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{l+1}\right)\beta\leq\tilde{\beta}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l+1}\right)\beta

\displaystyle~\Rightarrow~

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{l+1}\right)^{l+1}\beta^{l+1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l+1}\right)^{l+1}\beta^{l+1}

\displaystyle~\Rightarrow~

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e}\beta^{l+1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\leq e\beta^{l+1}

(60)

From
Tropp 2012
, for
U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times h}
and
U_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\sigma^{2}I)
, we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|U_{i}\|_{2}\geq t\right)\leq 2he^{-t^{2}/2h\sigma^{2}}.

(61)

Taking a union bound, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|U_{1}\|_{2}<t~\&~\cdots~\&~\|U_{l}\|_{2}<t\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\exists i,\|U_{i}\|_{2}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathbb{P}\left(\|U_{i}\|_{2}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-2lhe^{-t^{2}/2h\sigma^{2}}.

(62)

Setting
2lhe^{-t^{2}/2h\sigma^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
, we have
t=\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}
.
This implies that the probability that the spectral norm of the perturbation of any layer is no larger than
\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}
holds with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}
.
Plugging this bound into Lemma
3.3
, we have with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle|f_{w+u}(X,A)-f_{w}(X,A)|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq e\frac{t}{\zeta}lC_{\phi}B\|W_{1}\|_{2}\|W_{l}\|_{2}\frac{(d\mathcal{C})^{l-1}-1}{d\mathcal{C}-1}

\displaystyle=etlB\frac{\lambda\xi}{\zeta}

\displaystyle=eBl\beta^{l+1}t\leq e^{2}Bl\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}\leq\frac{\gamma}{4},

(63)

where we can set
\sigma=\frac{\gamma}{42Bl\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\sqrt{h\log(4lh)}}
to get the last inequality.
Note that Lemma
3.3
also requires
\max\left(\frac{\|U_{1}\|_{2}}{\|W_{1}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{2}\|_{2}}{\|W_{2}\|_{2}},\frac{\|U_{l}\|_{2}}{\|W_{l}\|_{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{l}
.
The requirement is satisfied if
\sigma\leq\frac{\zeta}{l\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}}
which in turn can be satisfied if

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{4eBl\beta^{l+1}\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta l\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}},

(64)

since the chosen value of
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma\leq\frac{\gamma}{4eBl\beta^{l+1}\sqrt{2h\log(4lh)}}
and
\frac{1}{\beta}\leq\zeta
.
Therefore, one sufficient condition to make Eq. (
64
) hold is
\frac{\gamma}{4eB}\leq\beta^{l}
.
We will see how to satisfy this condition later.

We now compute the KL term in the PAC-Bayes bound in Lemma
2.2
.

\displaystyle\text{KL}\left(Q\|P\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|w|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{42^{2}B^{2}\tilde{\beta}^{2l+2}l^{2}h\log(4lh)}{2\gamma^{2}}\left(\|W_{1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|W_{2}\|_{F}^{2}+\|W_{l}\|_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{B^{2}\beta^{2l+2}l^{2}h\log(lh)}{\gamma^{2}}\left(\|W_{1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|W_{2}\|_{F}^{2}+\|W_{l}\|_{F}^{2}\right)\right)

(65)

From Lemma
2.2
, fixing any
\tilde{\beta}
, with probability
1-\delta
and for all
w
such that
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l+1}\beta
, we have,

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}\beta^{2l+2}l^{2}h\log(lh)|w|_{2}^{2}+\log\frac{m}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right).

(66)

Finally, we need to consider multiple choices of
\tilde{\beta}
so that for any
\beta

ReLU Networks are Special GCNs

Since regular feedforward neural networks could be viewed as a special case of GNNs by treating each sample as the node feature of a single-node graph, it is natural to investigate the connections between these two classes of models.
In particular, we consider the class of ReLU networks studied in
Neyshabur et al. 2017
,

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=X\quad

\displaystyle(\text{Input Node Feature})

\displaystyle H_{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\left(H_{k-1}W_{k}\right)\quad

\displaystyle(k\text{-th Layer})

\displaystyle H_{l}

\displaystyle=H_{l-1}W_{l}\quad

\displaystyle(\text{Readout Layer}),

(86)

where
\sigma_{k}=\text{ReLU}

It includes two commonly-seen types of deep neural networks,

Comparing Eq. (

Connections of Generalization Bounds

Let us restate the PAC-Bayes bound of ReLU networks in

as below,

\displaystyle L_{D,0}(f_{w})\leq L_{S,\gamma}(f_{w})+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}l^{2}h\log(lh)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\|W_{i}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|W_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}+\log\frac{ml}{\delta}}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right).

A.7
Experimental Details

Datasets

We create 6 synthetic datasets by generating random graphs from different random graph models.
In particular, the first 4 synthetic datasets correspond to the Erdős–Rényi models with different edge probabilities: 1) Erdős–Rényi-1 (ER-1), edge probability = 0.1; 2) Erdős–Rényi-2 (ER-2), edge probability = 0.3; 3) Erdős–Rényi-3 (ER-3), edge probability = 0.5; 4) Erdős–Rényi-4 (ER-4), edge probability = 0.7.
The remaining 2 synthetic datasets correspond to the stochastic block model with the following settings: 1) Stochastic-Block-Model-1 (SBM-1), two blocks, sizes = [40, 60], edge probability = [[0.25, 0.13], [0.13, 0.37]]; 2) Stochastic-Block-Model-2 (SBM-2), three blocks, sizes = [25, 25, 50], edge probability = [[0.25, 0.05, 0.02], [0.05, 0.35, 0.07], [0.02, 0.07, 0.40]].
Each synthetic dataset has 200 graphs where the number of nodes of individual graph is 100, the number of classes is 2, and the random train-test split ratio is
90\%/10\%
.
For each random graph of individual synthetic dataset, we generate the 16-dimension random Gaussian node feature (normalized to have unit
\ell_{2}
norm) and a binary class label following a uniform distribution.

Bound Computations

For all datasets, we compute the bound values for the learned model saved in the end of the training.
We also consider the constants of both bounds in the computation.
In particular, for our bound, we compute the following quantity

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{42^{2}B^{2}\left(\max\left(\zeta^{-(l+1)},(\lambda\xi)^{\frac{l+1}{l}}\right)\right)^{2}l^{2}h\log(4lh)|w|_{2}^{2}}{\gamma^{2}m}}.

d
(max-node-degree) and large
l
(number-of-steps)”.
This makes sense since we have a square term on the number of steps
l
and it will play a role when the term involved with
d
is comparable (
i.e
.
, when
d
is small).
Again, all quantities are in the log domain.

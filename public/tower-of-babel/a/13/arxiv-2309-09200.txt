Scaling limit of the random walk on a Galton-Watson tree with regular varying offspring distribution
Dongjian Qian, Yang Xiao

Abstract
We consider a random walk on a Galton-Watson tree whose offspring distribution has a regular varying tail of index κ ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] \kappa\in(1,2] . We prove the convergence of the renormalised height function of the walk towards the continuous-time height process of a spectrally positive strictly stable Lévy process, jointly with the convergence of the renormalised trace of the walk towards the continuum tree coded by the latter continuous-time height process.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a Galton-Watson tree with root
\rho
. For every vertex
u
of
\mathbb{T}
, denote by
\nu(u)
the number of its children, which are i.i.d random variables with the generic law
\nu
. Throughout the article, we assume that the tail distribution of
\nu
is regularly varying with index
\kappa\in(1,2]
i.e.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\nu>x){\sim}x^{-\kappa}l(x),

where
l(x)
is a slowly varying function and we say
f(x)\sim g(x)
if

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1.

We may and will assume that
l(x)
is a positive constant when
x
is small. We see that
\nu
has a finite moment of order
\alpha
for
\alpha\in(0,\kappa)
. In particular, let
m:=E[\nu]<\infty
be the expectation of reproduction. We assume
m>1
so that
\mathbb{T}
has a positive probability to be infinite by knowledge of branching processes.

For a vertex
u
in the tree, we will denote by
|u|
its generation, by
u^{*}
its parent, and by
c(u)
the set of its children. We also set
u_{0},u_{1},\dots u_{|u|-1}
as the ancestors of
u
at generations
0,1,\dots|u|-1
. For
u,v\in\mathbb{T}
, we write
u\leq v
when
u
is an ancestor of
v
and
u<v
when
u\leq v
and
u\neq v
. Let
\mathbb{T}_{u}:=\{v\in T;u\leq v\}
be the subtree of
\mathbb{T}
rooted at
u
.

The collection of all the Galton-Watson trees is equipped with the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
, and with
\mathbb{E}
as the corresponding expectation. Since
\mathbb{T}
has a positive probability to be infinite, we let
\mathbb{P}^{*}
be the measure conditioned on survival, that is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{*}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathbb{T}\text{ is infinite}).

Now we define the
\lambda
-baised random walk
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on a fixed tree
\mathbb{T}
. The walk starts from the root
\rho
, with the transition probability as follows:

\displaystyle P^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{n+1}=u^{*}|X_{n}=u)=\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda+\nu(u)};

\displaystyle P^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{n+1}=v|X_{n}=u)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda+\nu(u)},\quad\forall v\in c(u).

For the root, we add an extra parent
\rho^{*}
to it and suppose that the random walk is reflected at
\rho^{*}
. We let
P^{\mathbb{T}}
(resp.
E^{\mathbb{T}}
) denote the law (resp. expectation) of the random walk conditioned on
\mathbb{T}
, which is called the quenched law. Another measure
P
, often referred to as the annealed law, is obtained by averaging the quenched law over
\mathbb{P}
. We set
E
as the annealed expectation. Also, we denote by
P^{*}
(resp.
E^{*}
) the annealed probability (resp. expectation) conditioned on non-extinction.

On the topic of
\lambda
-baised random walk on a Galton-Watson tree, it is first proved in
[
11
]
that, under
P^{*}
,
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is transient when
\lambda>m
, positive recurrent when
0<\lambda<m
and null recurrent when
m=\lambda
. In the critical case, namely
\lambda=m
, Y.Peres and O.Zeitouni prove in
[
12
]
that a central limit theorem on the height function of the walk holds, under some moments condition on the offspring distribution. This theorem is extended by A.Dembo and N.Sun in
[
4
]
to the case of multi-type Galton-Watson tree, under some weaker hypotheses.

In
[
1
]
, L.Raphélis and E.Aïdékon consider the trace of the
\lambda
-baised walk at time
n
, that is the subtree of
\mathbb{T}
made up the vertices visited until time
n
. They consider the graph
\mathcal{R}_{n}:=(\mathbb{T}^{n},d_{\mathbb{T}_{n}})
where
\mathbb{T}^{n}:=\{u\in\mathbb{T}:\exists k\leq n,X_{k}=u\}
is regarded as a metric space, equipped with the natural graph distance
d_{\mathbb{T}_{n}}
. The trace can also be considered as an unlabelled tree, with the edges inherited from
\mathbb{T}
. It is proved in
[
1
]
that when
\lambda=m>1
, and
\nu
has finite variation, the following convergence holds both under
P^{*}
(annealed case) and under
P^{\mathbb{T}}
for
\mathbb{P}^{*}
-a,e. tree (quenched case):

(1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}n}}\left(\mathcal{R}_{n},(X_{[nt]}\right)_{t\in[0,1]})\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\left(\mathcal{T}_{(B_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}},(B_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\right),

where
\sigma^{2}:={m(m-1)}/{\mathbb{E}[\nu(\nu-1)]}
. Here
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
is a reflected Brownian motion in time interval
[0,1]
and
\mathcal{T}_{(B_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}}
is a real tree encoded by the Brownian motion. The convergence holds in law for the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff topology on metric spaces and Skorokhod topology on cádlág functions. The concept of convergence of real trees can be found, for example, in
[
8
]
.

We are interested in the case when
\nu
does not have the second moment. The
\lambda
-baised random walk can be viewed as a random walk in random environment, where in fact no randomness is introduced in the environment. More precisely, the potential of a vertex
u
in the first generation of
\mathbb{T}
, denoted by
\triangle V(u)
, is
\ln(\lambda)
with probability 1. The Laplace transform of the point process of the potential is defined for
t\geq 0
as

\displaystyle\psi(t):=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{|u|=1}e^{-tV(u)}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\nu}{\lambda^{t}}\right].

In
[
3
]
, L.Raphélis proves that under some hypothesis (the hypothesis
(\mathbf{H}_{c})
and
(\mathbf{H}_{\kappa})
in the article) on
\psi
, the height function of the walk
(|X_{n}|)_{n\geq 0}
and the trace
(\mathcal{R}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
converge jointly towards the continuous-time height process of a spectrally positive stable Lévy process of index
\kappa
and Lévy forest coded by it. The proof of the main theorem in that article relies on the theory about the convergence of height process and Levy trees constructed in
[
5
]
.

The results in
[
3
]
do not cover the
\lambda
-biased random walk. On one hand, in that article,
[
7
, Theorem B]
is applied as a main tool to get the regularly varying tail, which requires the non-lattice condition. On the other hand, the condition
(\mathbf{H}_{\kappa})
in that article is not satisfied for general
l(x)
, even when
l(x)
is a constant. Indeed, we cannot guarantee
\mathbb{P}(\nu>x){\sim}x^{-\kappa}l(x)
and
\mathbb{E}[\nu^{\kappa}]<\infty
at the same time. It is not just a technique problem, as will be shown in the Section
4
, that the regularly varying tail of a key random variable comes from a totally different place.

From now on, we assume
\lambda=m
. We also define

\displaystyle a_{n}:=\inf\{x:P(v>x)\leq n^{-1}\},

which is the proper scale as is shown in
[
6
, Theorem 3.7.2]
. When
l(x)=1
, we can take
a_{n}=n^{1/\kappa}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose the offspring distribution
\nu
is regularly varying with index
\kappa
for some
\kappa\in(1,2)
. There exists a constant
C^{*}\in(0,\infty)
such that the following convergence holds in law under
P^{*}
or
P^{T}
for
\mathbb{P}^{*}
-a.e. trees:

\displaystyle\dfrac{a(n)}{n}\bigg((|X_{[nt]}|)_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{R}_{n}\bigg)\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\Longrightarrow}C^{*}\bigg((H_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{T}_{(H_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1}}\bigg),

where
(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the continuous-time height process of a strictly stable spectrally positive Lévy process of index
\kappa
and
\mathcal{T}_{(H_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1}}
is the real tree coded by
(H_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1}
. The convergence holds in law for Skorokhod topology on cádlág functions and the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff topology on metric spaces.

In particular, when
l(x)
is a constant
C_{l}
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{1-\frac{1}{\kappa}}}\bigg((|X_{[nt]}|)_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{R}_{n}\bigg)\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\Longrightarrow}C^{**}\bigg((H_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{T}_{(H_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1}}\bigg),

where
C^{**}=C^{*}C_{l}^{-1/\kappa}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The constant
C^{*}
can be computed explicitly:

\displaystyle C^{*}=\left(C_{0}|\Gamma(1-\kappa)|\right)^{-\frac{1}{\kappa}}2^{-\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa}}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}},

where
C_{0}
is given in Proposition
4.5
.

When
\kappa=2
, we define the truncated moment function

\displaystyle\mu(x):=E_{1}\left[(L^{1}-1)^{2}1_{\{L^{1}\leq x\}}\right],

where the definitions of the measure
E_{1}
and the random variable
L^{1}
appear in Section
4
. We show in Remark
4.6
that if
E_{1}[(L^{1})^{2}]=\infty
,

\displaystyle\mu(x)\sim 2C_{0}\int_{0}^{x}y^{-1}l(y)\mathrm{d}y

which is slowly varying by
[
2
, Proposition 1.5.9 a]
. Note that the tail of
\mu(x)
is different from that of
l(x)
. In fact,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{l(x)}\int^{x}_{0}t^{-1}l(t)\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

We define
a^{\prime}(n)
as the function of
n
satisfying

\displaystyle\dfrac{n\mu(a^{\prime}(n))}{(a^{\prime}(n))^{2}}\rightarrow C_{a}

for some constant
C_{a}
. Then we can get the parallel result for
\kappa=2
. The proof is quite the same as the case
1<\kappa<2
, so we will omit it.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose the offspring distribution
\nu
is regularly varying with index
\kappa=2
. There exists a constant
C^{*}\in(0,\infty)
such that the following convergence holds in law under
P^{*}
or
P^{T}
for
\mathbb{P}^{*}
-a.e. trees:

\displaystyle\dfrac{a^{\prime}(n)}{n}\bigg((|X_{[nt]}|)_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{R}_{n}\bigg)\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\Longrightarrow}C^{*}\bigg((|B_{t}|)_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{T}_{|B_{t}|_{0\leq t\leq 1}}\bigg),

where
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion and

\displaystyle C^{*}=\left(\frac{C_{a}}{2}\right)^{-1/2}\frac{m}{m-1}.

In particular, when
l(x)
is a constant
C_{l}
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(n\ln^{-1}(n))^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg((|X_{[nt]}|)_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{R}_{n}\bigg)\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\Longrightarrow}C^{*}\bigg((|B_{t}|)_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{T}_{|B_{t}|_{0\leq t\leq 1}}\bigg),

with
C^{*}=(C_{0}C_{l})^{-1/2}m/(m-1)
.

Remark 1.4
.

When
\nu
has the second moment, which is equivalent to
x^{-1}l(x)
is integrable,
\mu(x)
is bounded. Thus
a^{\prime}(n)=\sqrt{n}
and
C_{a}=E_{1}[(L^{1}-1)^{2}]
. It can be easily checked that (
2
) degenerates to (
1
).

The article is arranged as follows. In Section 2, a global strategy is given and the details are omitted, which can be referred to in
[
3
]
. The theorem then boils down to the check of a hypothesis
(\mathbf{H}_{l})
in that section. In Section 3, two equivalent change of measure of the trace is introduced, which is used in the following proof. Then, the whole Section 4 is devoted to proving the hypothesis
(\mathbf{H}_{l})
. The Subsection 4.2 is core part and the main contribution of the paper. In that section, we apply the Laplace transform and dominated convergence theorem to obtain regular variation of the tail of a key random variable and complete the proof.

2
Overview of the proof

We first show an annealed version of the main theorem for random walks on forests. Denote
\mathbb{F}
a forest made up of a collection i.i.d Galton-Watson trees
(\mathbb{T}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
with corresponding roots
(\rho_{i})_{i\geq 1}
. Let
(X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a nearest-neighbour random walk on
\mathbb{F}
starting from
\rho_{1}
with the transition probability:

\displaystyle P^{\mathbb{F}}\left(X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n+1}=v|X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n}=u\right)=\frac{1}{m+\nu(u)},\quad

\displaystyle v\in c(u);

\displaystyle P^{\mathbb{F}}\left(X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n+1}=u^{*}|X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n}=u\right)=\frac{m}{m+\nu(u)},\quad

\displaystyle u\neq\rho_{i},\,\forall i;

\displaystyle P^{\mathbb{F}}\left(X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n+1}=\rho_{i+1}|X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n}=u\right)=\frac{m}{m+\nu(u)},\quad

\displaystyle u=\rho_{i}.

The behavior of the random walk on
\mathbb{F}
is similar to that on
\mathbb{T}
except when it is on the roots. Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}:=\{u\in\mathbb{F},\exists n\geq 0,X^{\mathbb{F}}_{n}=u\}

be the sub-forest made up with vertices once visited by the random walk. It is made up of i.i.d multi-type trees
(\mathbf{T}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
. As there should be no ambiguity on the context hereafter, we will simply denote the walk by
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and still denote by
\mathcal{R}_{n}
the set of vertices of
\mathbb{F}
visited up to time
n
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
{\nu}
is regularly varying with index
\kappa
with
\kappa\in(1,2)
. Then the following convergence holds in law under
P
:

\displaystyle\dfrac{a(n)}{n}\bigg((|X_{[nt]}|)_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{R}_{n}\bigg)\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\Longrightarrow}C^{*}\bigg((H_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{T}_{(H_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1}}\bigg)

where
(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the continuous-time height process of a strictly stable spectrally positive Lévy process of index
\kappa
. The convergence holds in law for Skorokhod topology on cádlág functions and the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff topology on metric spaces.

Theorem
1.1
then follows from Proposition
2.1
by
[
3
, Section 5]
. Note that, in
[
3
, Section 5]
, the only requirement on
\nu
used in the proof is
\psi((\kappa+1)/2)=m^{(1-\kappa)/2}<1
, which is obviously satisfied.

The outline of proof of Proposition
2.1
is the same as that in
[
3
]
. First, the trace
\mathcal{R}_{n}
can be seen as multi-type Galton-Watson forest. Then
(|X_{n}|,\mathbf{F})
is associated with two leafed Galton-Watson forests with edge lengths, denoted by
\mathbf{F}^{R}
and
\mathbf{F}^{X}
. The height process of
\mathbf{F}^{X}
is equal to
(|X_{n}|)_{n\geq 0}
and the height process of of
\mathbf{F}^{R}
is equal to that of
\mathbf{F}
. Next, we state a result on the associated height process of such forests, under certain hypotheses. At last, we conclude Proposition
2.1
provided
\mathbf{F}^{R}
and
\mathbf{F}^{X}
satisfy the hypotheses.

Reduction of trees

In this section, we briefly introduce the reduction of trees. One can see

for details. For every

, which is not a root, we denote by

\displaystyle\beta(u):=\sharp\{n\geq 0:X_{n}=u^{*},X_{n+1}=u\},

which is the number of visits of

from

to

. If

is a root, set

. Since in our case,

is recurrent, it’s given in

Lemma 2.2
.

[
1

Under the annealed law

, the marked forest

is a multi-type Galton-Watson forest with roots of initial type 1.

Leafed Galton-Watson forests with edge lengths are multi-type Galton-Watson forests that every vertex has two types, 0 and 1, and only vertices of type 1 may give progeny. We can denote it by a triplet
(\mathbf{F},e,l)
where for

,

stands for the type of

and

is the length of edge joining

We can define its associated weighted height process

: if

is the
n
-th vertex of

in the lexicographical order, then

\displaystyle H^{l}_{F}(n)=\sum\limits_{\rho\leq v\leq u(n)}l(v).

We also define the notion of the optional line of a given type. Let

be the set of vertices descending from
u
in
\mathbf{F}
having no ancestor of type
1
since

. Formally,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{1}_{u}:=\{v\in F:u<v,\,\beta(w)\neq 1,\,\forall u<w<v\}.

Also, denote by

the set of vertices of type
1
descending from

in
\mathbf{F}
and having no ancestor of type
1
since

. Formally,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{1}_{u}:=\{v\in F:u<v,\,\beta(v)=1,\,\beta(w)\neq 1,\,\forall u<w<v\}.

Denote by

(resp.

) the cardinality of
\mathcal{L}^{1}_{u}
(resp.
\mathcal{B}^{1}_{u}
).

Now, let us briefly introduce the forests
\mathbf{F}^{R}
and
\mathbf{F}^{X}
. Each component

(resp.
\mathbf{T}^{X}_{k}
) of

of

. We just introduce the notion without giving any intuition.
\mathbf{T}^{R}_{k}
is built as follows:

Initialisation

Generation 0 of

2.

Induction

Suppose that generation

Convergence of the height process associated with

3
Change of measure

For
u\in\mathbf{F}
, we can easily compute that under
P^{\mathbb{T}}
conditioned on
\beta(u)=k
, the process
(\beta(v))_{v\in c(u)}
has the negative multinomial distribution
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}(u)
, that is

\displaystyle P^{\mathbb{T}}\left((\beta(v))_{v\in c(u)}=(l_{v})_{v\in c(u)}|\beta(u)=k\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{m}{m+\nu(u)}\right)^{k}\left(\dfrac{1}{m+\nu(u)}\right)^{\sum\limits_{v\in c(u)}l_{v}}\dfrac{\left(k-1+\sum_{v\in c(u)l_{v}}\right)!}{(k-1)!\prod_{v\in c(u)}l_{v}!}.

The formula can be understood in this way. When the random walk
X
arrived at
u
, a coin is tossed with successful probability
{\nu(u)}/({m+\nu(u)})
. If succeed,
X
moves to one of the children of
u
with the same probability
{1}/({m+\nu(u)})
. Since
X
is recurrent, it must come back to

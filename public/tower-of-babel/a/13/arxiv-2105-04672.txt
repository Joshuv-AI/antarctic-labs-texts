New integral estimates in substatic Riemannian manifolds and the Alexandrov Theorem Thanks: A. Pinamonti is partially supported by the INdAM-GNAMPA 2020 project Convergenze variazionali per funzionali e operatori dipendenti da campi vettoriali.
Mattia Fogagnolo, Andrea Pinamonti

Abstract
. We derive new integral estimates on substatic manifolds with boundary of horizon type, naturally arising in General Relativity. In particular, we generalize to this setting an identity due to Magnanini-Poggesi [ MP19 ] leading to the Alexandrov Theorem in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and improve on a Heintze-Karcher type inequality due to Li-Xia [ LX19 ] . Our method relies on the introduction of a new vector field with nonnegative divergence, generalizing to this setting the P-function technique of Weinberger [ Wei71 ] .



1.
Introduction

The characterization of hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature (and also of other functions of the principal curvatures) is an issue that, since the seminal papers of Alexandrov
[
Ale62
,
Ale62a
]
generated a vast and fertile literature on related problems, both because of its intrinsic interest and also because it naturally links different topics, such as boundary value problems and Riemannian geometry.

We recall some of the progresses sprung from this problem, in order to help framing our work. In the just mentioned initial papers of Alexandrov, the author showed, by means of a brilliant technique now known as
Alexandrov moving planes
, that embedded closed hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and in space forms (the hyperbolic space and the hemisphere) were shown to be only geodesic balls. Reilly, on the other hand, proposed an alternative proof
[
Rei77
]
based on an integral identity now known as Reilly’s formula.
After that, inspired by some of the computations worked out by Weinberger
[
Wei71
]
in order to yield an alternative, way easier proof to a special case of Serrin’s Theorem about overdetermined problems
[
Ser71
]
, Reilly gave a third proof
[
Rei82
]
. This approach was indeed ruled by the sub-harmonicity of Weinberger’s function

P=\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\frac{2}{n}u,

where
u
is the solution to
\Delta u=-1
in the bounded set
\Omega
contoured by the hypersurface
\Sigma
with constant mean-curvature with vanishing boundary value. Carefully retracing this path, Magnanini and Poggesi
[
MP19
, Theorem 2.2]
discovered the following remarkable integral identity, holding true for any bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\Sigma
and mean curvature
{\rm H}

(1.1)

\int_{\Sigma}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}(\overline{{\rm H}}-{\rm H}){\,\rm d}\sigma,=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\right|^{2}{\,\rm d}\mu\,+\,\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{1}{R}\int_{\Sigma}(R-\lvert\nabla u\rvert)^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma,

where
\overline{{\rm H}}
and
R
are the values achieved by the mean curvature of
\Sigma
and
\lvert\nabla u\rvert
on
\Sigma
, respectively, on a ball of the same volume as
\Omega
. The Riemannian metric
g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the classical flat metric of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, while
{\,\rm d}\sigma
and
{\,\rm d}\mu
denote respectively the
n-1
-dimensional and the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measures. The above equation carries a quantitative information about hypersurfaces with almost constant mean curvature, since it shows that the (integral) deficit from being constantly mean-curved controls the
L^{2}
-norm of the traceless part of the Hessian of
u
, that actually vanishes if and only if
u
is rotationally symmetric. In particular, (
1.1
) represents a generalization of the Alexandrov Theorem holding true for any bounded set with smooth boundary.

Again as a consequence of the sub-harmonicity of
P
, Magnanini and Poggesi got a version of an inequality now known as Heintze-Karcher inequality, originated in
[
HK78
]
and rediscovered in the form that is usually meant in
[
Ros87
, Theorem 1]
, reading

(1.2)

\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{{\rm H}}{\,\rm d}\sigma\,\geq\,\lvert\Omega\rvert

for open subsets
\Omega
with strictly
mean-convex
(i.e. with strictly positive mean curvature) boundary
\Sigma
.
In fact, a
L^{2}
-quantity like that in (
1.1
) is found to appear as a deficit also the above inequality.

The aim of the present paper is to extend this circle of ideas to the vast context of substatic manifolds with horizon boundary.

Leaving the rigorous (and actually more general) definitions to the next section, we consider Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
of dimension
n\geq 2
endowed with a nonnegative smooth function
f
, with a compact,
minimal
(i.e. with vanishing mean curvature) connected boundary
N=\{f=0\}
that is a regular level set of
f
(i.e.
\lvert\nabla f\rvert>0
on
N
). This is substantially what we mean by
horizon boundary
. Moreover, the curvature of
g
satisfies the relation

(1.3)

f\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits-\nabla\nabla f+(\Delta f)g\geq 0,

that is what
substatic
stands for. We will refer to
f
as
substatic potential
. In order to discuss the relevance of this conditions, let us start from the
static
case, that occurs when (
1.3
) holds with equality at any point. Static Riemannian metrics naturally arise when considering solutions to the Einstein equations in the vacuum, that is, with vanishing stress-energy tensor. When the resulting Lorentzian manifold is also assumed to admit a global timelike Killing vector field with integrable orthogonal distribution, then time-slices are immediately seen to be static, with actually an additional partial differential constraint on the static potential that in turn implies the boundary to be of horizon-type. Discussing in details the deep connections with Mathematical General Relativity is far out the scope of this contribution, and so we refer the interested reader to the comprehensive thesis of Borghini
[
Bor18
]
and to the references therein. The substatic condition appear more generally in General Relativity in relation with the so called
null convergence condition

[
WWZ17
]
.

It was also observed by Brendle
[
Bre13
]
that this condition is naturally satisfied on warped product manifolds substantially fulfilling certain natural assumptions on the warping functions, that are clearly satisfied by the rotationally symmetric models of static metrics (de Sitter-Schwarzschild and anti de Sitter-Schwarzschild) as well as by the Reissner-Nordström metric. In particular, in
[
Bre13
]
the author actually provides a version of the Alexandrov Theorem and of the Heintze-Karcher inequality for such warped product metrics. A generalization of the latter in the substatic setting has been obtained in
[
LX19
]
, through the application of a suitable Reilly-type identity. In concluding their proof of the Heintze-Karcher inequality, Li-Xia introduced and considered the fundamental boundary value problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=-1+\frac{\Delta f}{f}u&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=c&\mbox{on}\,\,N\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\Sigma,\end{cases}

for a suitable nonnegative constant
c
on a bounded set
\Omega\subset M
with smooth boundary given by
\partial\Omega=N\sqcup\Sigma
. This equation constitutes the core of our generalization of (
1.1
). Namely, in our main result Theorem
3.5
we prove that, under the additional assumption to be discussed below of existence of a strictly mean-convex hypersurface
S
bounding a subset
\Omega
with boundary
\partial\Omega=N\sqcup S
, we can choose

(1.5)

c=\frac{\int_{N}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma}{\int_{N}\lvert\nabla f\rvert\left[\frac{\Delta f}{f}-\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right)\right]{\,\rm d}\sigma}

and that with this choice we have

(1.6)

\begin{split}0&\leq\int_{\Omega}f\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\right|^{2}+Q\left(\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f,\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f\right){\,\rm d}\mu\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{1}{R}\int_{\Sigma}f(R-\lvert\nabla u\rvert)^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma=\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}(\overline{{\rm H}}-{\rm H}){\,\rm d}\sigma,\end{split}

where

Q=f\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits-\nabla\nabla f+\Delta fg\geq 0

and

(1.7)

R=\frac{\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu+c\int_{N}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma}{\int_{\Sigma}f{\,\rm d}\sigma}\qquad\overline{{\rm H}}=\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{1}{R}.

It is then easy to show that if
{\rm H}\geq\overline{{\rm H}}
on the whole hypersurface
\Sigma
then it is totally umbilical. To our knowledge, the above identity is new also in the more symmetric case of the warped product metrics considered in
[
Bre13
]
. We included an Appendix where we show how Brendle’s Alexandrov Theorem
can be derived from the results contained in our paper. In fact, our notion of substatic metrics with horizon boundary (Definitions
2.2
and
2.1
) holds true also for empty horizon boundaries, in order to encompass also the hemisphere and the hyperbolic space as well as the huge and thoroughly studied class of complete manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature.

As for (
1.1
), the relation (
1.6
) naturally yields a natural deficit for
\Sigma
from being, in loose integral sense, of constant mean curvature
\overline{{\rm H}}
. Namely, by applying the Hölder inequality to (
1.6
), we get

(1.8)

\big|\big|{\rm H}-\overline{{\rm H}}\big|\big|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\geq\frac{\int_{\Omega}f\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\right|^{2}+Q\left(\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f,\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f\right){\,\rm d}\mu}{||f\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma)}},

where
p\geq 1
and
p^{\prime}
is its Hölder conjugate.
In flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the numerator on the right hand side reduces to the
L^{2}
-norm of the traceless hessian of
u
, inequalities of the above form have been exploited in order to get a more quantitative description in terms of a suitable distance between
\Sigma
and a suitable reference ball, see
[
MP19
,
MP20a
,
MP20
,
CM17
,
JN20
]
. In the hyperbolic space and in the hemisphere, this approach has been generalized in the recent
[
Sch21
, Theorem 1.5]
, where the role of the
L^{2}
-norm of the traceless Hessian of a suitable function of
u
has been largely discussed and exploited. Providing similar results using the deficit in (
1.8
) looks quite intriguing, as it would for example yield a complete quantitative description of Brendle’s Alexandrov Theorem
[
Bre13
]
in substatic warped products. Concerning other quantitative versions of the Alexandrov Theorem, this time obtained through the Alexandrov moving planes method, we mention the sharp
[
CV18
]
in flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
[
CV20
,
CRV21
]
about space forms.

As already mentioned, we prove our result by providing a suitable generalization of the
P
-function computation of Weinberger as a sort of pointwise alternative to Li-Xia’s Reilly-type integral identity
[
LX19
]
. What we discover is a new vector field
X
that in the substatic case has nonnegative divergence, that substantially coincides with the integrand in the numerator on the right hand side of (
1.8
). Such vector field coincides with
\nabla P
on manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature, and it is nontrivially linked (Remark
2.5
) with the P-function of constant curvature spaces
[
QX17
,
CV19
]
.
In static and substatic manifolds with harmonic potential, a very different vector field with nonnegative divergence have been found and utilized for different aims in
[
AM17
,
AMO20
]
.
Identity (
1.6
) follows very naturally from applying the Divergence Theorem to the vector field
X
in
\Omega
(Proposition
3.1
) and working out some basic algebraic manipulations. A contradiction argument exploiting the existence of a strictly mean-convex hypersurface
S
yields the positivity of the constant in (
1.5
), see Proposition
3.2
.
Observe that this is a purely geometric consequence obtained for the horizon boundary of a substatic manifold, that to our knowledge has only been pointed out without an explicit proof in
[
WW18
, Theorem 1.6]
, in the special static geometry. In such context the result appears more elegant, and it is discussed in Remark
3.3

We also provide a geometric condition ensuring
a priori
the existence of strictly mean-convex hypersurfaces like these, see Proposition
3.4
, relying on the resolution of the least area problem recently considered in
[
FM20
]
. Without additional effort, the Heintze-Karcher-type inequality for strictly mean-convex
\Sigma

(1.9)

\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{f}{{\rm H}}{\,\rm d}\sigma\geq\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu+c\int_{N}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma

is derived, again with an explicit deficit, see Theorem
3.6
. The constant
c
is again given by (
1.5
), and it is well defined as
\Sigma
itself provides the strictly mean-convex hypersurface required. An analogous inequality in substatic manifolds is provided in
[
LX19
, Theorem 1.3]
, as already said as an application of a Reilly-type identity. However, the constant
c
considered there is not known in general to be positive, and it can be checked that even when this is the case, it is a priori smaller then ours. On the other hand, they do coincide in the warped product models considered in
[
Bre13
]
.

We close this introduction with a brief summary. In Section
2
, after having introduced the setting and the basics about problem (
1.4
), we introduce the key vector field
X
. In Section
3
, we work out the main results of the paper, already discussed above. Moreover, in Subsection
3.1
, we derive stronger rigidity statements when (
1.9
) holds with equality under additional geometric assumptions. In the Appendix, we discuss Brendle’s Alexandrov Theorem in connection with the present work.

After the manuscript has been uploaded on ArXiv, Prof. C. Xia explained us that the fact that the vector field
X
defined in (
2.8
) has nonnegative divergence could be also deduced from the computations in
[
LX19
,
LX17
]
.

2.
A vector field with nonnegative divergence in substatic manifolds

Let us provide the rigorous definitions we are going to employ for substatic manifolds with horizon boundary. For all the duration of the paper,
we are considering Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n\geq 2
.

Definition 2.1

(Horizon boundaries)
.

We say that a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
endowed with a smooth nonnegative function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
has
horizon boundary
if
\nabla\nabla f/f
extends continuously at points
x\in M
where
f(x)=0
and one of the following alternatives occurs.

(i)

The function
f
is
proper
and the boundary
(N,g_{N})
of
(M,g)
is a
minimal,
smooth, closed
(n-1)
-dimensional Riemannian submanifold such that
N=\{f=0\}
and it is a regular level set for
f
. In this case, we denote with
N_{1},\dots,N_{l}
, with
l\in\mathbb{N}
, the connected components of
N
.

(ii)

The boundary of
(M,g)
is empty and
f
is strictly positive.

A couple of comments are in order. There is no completeness request in the definition above. This is not anecdotic at all, since it allows to consider fundamental examples like the De Sitter-Schwarzschild metric, that is a specific warped product of the type
([0,\overline{\rho}),d\rho\otimes d\rho+h^{2}(\rho)g_{N}
, where
N
is a closed
n-1
dimensional closed hypersurface that has horizon boundary
\{f=0\}=\{\rho=0\}=N
, where
f=h^{\prime}(\rho)
. This metric is incomplete as
\rho\to\overline{\rho}^{-}
.

The very same thing happens for the hemisphere, that we consider devoid of the equatorial
n-1
-dimensional hypersphere. It is again a warped product of the form above with a different warping function
h
, with
f=h^{\prime}(\rho)
. In this case, the metric closes smoothly as
\rho\to 0^{+}
. The boundary is thus empty and (ii) in the above definition is satisfied.

These metrics are also
substatic
.

Definition 2.2

(Substatic metrics)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold endowed with a nonnegative function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and with horizon boundary. We say that it is
substatic
if

(2.1)

f\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits-\nabla\nabla f+\Delta fg\geq 0

on the whole of
M
.
In this case, we say that
f
is the
substatic potential
of
(M,g)
.

In addition to the substatic warped products considered in
[
Bre13
]
(see also the Appendix), we mention that
complete
noncompact manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and without boundary fulfil the assumptions above by choosing
f
to be a positive constant.

2.1.
The torsion-like function

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold endowed with a nonnegative function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and with horizon boundary
(N,g_{N})
.
Our first concern consists in providing, under such assumption, the basic properties of solutions to

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=-1+\frac{\Delta f}{f}u&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=c_{j}&\mbox{on}\,\,N_{j}\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\Sigma,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset M
is a bounded open subset with
C^{2,\alpha}
- boundary such that
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\sqcup(\cup_{j\in J}N_{j})
for
J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, with
\Sigma\cap N=\emptyset
and
c_{j}>0
are positive constants.
In case
\Omega\Subset M\setminus N
(that is
J=\emptyset
), or
N
were empty (that is, (ii) occurs in Definition
2.1
), then we agree that the problem (
2.2
) reduces to

(2.3)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=-1+\frac{\Delta f}{f}u&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\Sigma.\end{cases}

In the following statement we observe that (
2.2
) admits a positive smooth solution in
\Omega
that is
C^{2,\alpha}
up to the boundary, and that also enjoys a Hopf-type property on
\Sigma
. The nonstandard maximum principle lying behind the nonnegativity and the Hopf-property of
u
follows from an argument that is inspired from a similar one in the proof of
[
CV19
, Lemma 2.4]
, in the special geometry of a hemisphere.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold endowed with a nonnegative smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
with horizon boundary. Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded subset with
C^{2,\alpha}-boundary
such that
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\sqcup(\cup_{j\in J}N_{j})
with
J\subset\{1,\dots,l\}
, and
\Sigma\cap N=\emptyset
. Then,
there exists an unique solution
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
to (
2.2
). Moreover,
u>0
in
\Omega
and
\Sigma=\{u=0\}
is a regular level set of
u
. In fact,
we have

(2.4)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x)<0

at any
x\in\Sigma
, and
\nu
is unit normal to
\Sigma
in
x
pointing outside of
\Omega
. In particular,
\nu=-\nabla u/\lvert\nabla u\rvert
.

Proof.

The existence of a unique classical solution
u
to (
2.2
) with the claimed regularity follows from
[
GT01
, Theorem 6.15]
, once checked that the operator
\Delta-\Delta f/f
has strictly positive first eigenvalue. This is shown in
[
LX19
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Assume that
N=\{f=0\}
is a smooth hypersurface and regular level set of
f
and
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\sqcup(\cup_{j\in J}N_{j})
, and let us prove that
u>0
on
\Omega
, as well as (
2.4
). The case
J=\emptyset
will follow too. Consider, for
\delta>0
, the level set
N_{\delta}=\{f=\delta\}
. Since
f
is smooth and
\lvert\nabla f\rvert>0
on
N
, then for
\delta>0
small enough
N_{\delta}
is a regular level set of
f
. Define, on
\Omega_{\delta}=\Omega\setminus\{f<\delta\}
, the function
w={u}/{f}
. Then, it is directly checked that
w
satisfies

(2.5)

\Delta w+2\langle\nabla w,\nabla f\rangle=-\frac{1}{f}<0.

In particular,
w_{\delta}
is subject to a strong minimum principle in
\Omega_{\delta}
. Since
u
is continuous up to
N_{j}
, where it takes the positive value
c_{j}
, the function
w_{\delta}>0
on
N_{\delta}\cap\Omega
, and thus its minimum value on
\partial\Omega
is zero. Thus,
u>0
on
\Omega_{\delta}
. Letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
, by the continuity up to the boundary of
u
we deduce that
u>0
on
\Omega.

Moreover, again because of (
2.5
) and by the Hopf Lemma for supersolutions to elliptic PDE’s, we get

(2.6)

0>\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{f}\left\langle\nabla u-\frac{1}{f}u\nabla f,\nu\right\rangle(x)=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x),

for any
x\in\Sigma
, as
u=0
on such hypersurface. This concludes also the proof of the claimed Hopf-type property.
∎

2.2.
The vector field X and its nonnegative divergence

On a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
consider for an open
U\subset M
, a
positive
function
f\in C^{\infty}(U)
. Then, for a smooth function
u\in C^{\infty}(U)
solving

(2.7)

\Delta u=-1+\frac{\Delta f}{f}u,

we define the vector field
X
by

(2.8)

X=f\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\frac{2}{n}f\nabla u-\nabla\nabla f\nabla u^{2}-\frac{2}{n}u\nabla f-2u\nabla\nabla u\nabla f+2u^{2}\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\nabla f.

Its divergence is computed in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
X
be defined as above on some open set
U\subset M
endowed with a positive smooth function
f
. Then, we have

(2.9)

{\mathrm{div}}X=2f\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\right|^{2}+2Q\left(\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f,\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f\right),

where the tensor
Q
is defined as

(2.10)

Q=f\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits-\nabla\nabla f+\Delta fg.

Proof.

Let us compute separately the divergence of the six summands forming
X
. By applying the Bochner identity, and using the relation (
2.7
) we have

(2.11)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{div}}(f\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2})&=\langle\nabla f,\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\rangle+f\Delta\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\langle\nabla f,\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\rangle+2f\left[\lvert\nabla\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\langle\nabla(\Delta u),\nabla u\rangle+\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right]\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\langle\nabla f,\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\rangle+2f\left[\lvert\nabla\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{f}\langle\nabla\Delta f,\nabla u\rangle-\frac{1}{f^{2}}\langle\Delta f,\nabla u\rangle+\frac{\Delta f}{f}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right]\end{split}

Observe now that, again since
u
solves (
2.7
), we have

(2.12)

\begin{split}\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\right|^{2}&=\left|\nabla\nabla u-u\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}+\frac{1}{n}g\right|^{2}=\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\left|\nabla\nabla u\right|^{2}+u^{2}\frac{\left|\nabla\nabla f\right|^{2}}{f^{2}}+\frac{1}{n}-2u\left\langle\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f},\nabla\nabla u\right\rangle+\frac{2}{n}\Delta u-\frac{2}{n}\frac{\Delta f}{f}u\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\left|\nabla\nabla u\right|^{2}+u^{2}\frac{\left|\nabla\nabla f\right|^{2}}{f^{2}}-2u\left\langle\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f},\nabla\nabla u\right\rangle+\frac{1}{n}\Delta u-\frac{1}{n}\frac{\Delta f}{f}u.\end{split}

Thus, rewriting
\lvert\nabla\nabla u\rvert^{2}
through (
2.12
), and plugging it into (
2.11
), we get

(2.13)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{div}}(f\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2})=\langle\nabla f,\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\rangle&+2\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\right|^{2}-2u^{2}\frac{|\nabla\nabla f|^{2}}{f}\\
&+4u\left\langle\nabla\nabla f,\nabla\nabla u\right\rangle-\frac{2}{n}f\Delta u+\frac{2}{n}u\Delta f+2\langle\nabla\Delta f,\nabla u\rangle\\
&-2\frac{\Delta f}{f}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle+2{\Delta f}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}+2f\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nabla u,\nabla u),\end{split}

that is the formula for the divergence of the first term in (
2.8
) we are going to employ in this proof.
The divergence of
f\nabla u
is just written as

(2.14)

{\mathrm{div}}{f\nabla u}=\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle+f\Delta u,

with no need for employing the equation solved by
u
. Let us recall now the classical identity for the Ricci tensor given by

(2.15)

\Delta\nabla v=\nabla\Delta v+\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits\nabla v

in force for any
C^{3}
-function.
Applying it to the smooth function
f
, we get, in taking the divergence of the third term,

(2.16)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{div}}(\nabla\nabla f\nabla u^{2})&=2u\langle\Delta\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle+2u\langle\nabla\nabla f,\nabla\nabla u\rangle+2\nabla\nabla f(\nabla u,\nabla u)\\
&=2u\langle\Delta\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle+2u\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nabla f,\nabla u)+2u\langle\nabla\nabla f,\nabla\nabla u\rangle+2\nabla\nabla f(\nabla u,\nabla u).\end{split}

The fourth divergence is kept as

(2.17)

{\mathrm{div}}(u\nabla f)=\langle\nabla u,\nabla f\rangle+u\Delta f.

In computing the divergence of the fifth term, we apply the identity (
2.15
) to
u
, and combine it with (
2.7
), to get

(2.18)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{div}}(u\nabla\nabla u\nabla f)&=u\langle\Delta\nabla u,\nabla f\rangle+u\langle\nabla\nabla u,\nabla\nabla f\rangle+\nabla\nabla u(\nabla u,\nabla f)\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!=\frac{u}{f}\langle\nabla\Delta f,\nabla f\rangle-\frac{u}{f^{2}}\Delta f\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2}+u\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nabla u,\nabla f)+u\langle\nabla\nabla u,\nabla\nabla f\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2},\nabla f\rangle.\end{split}

We compute the last term, again using (
2.15
) in relation with
f
, as

(2.19)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{div}}(u^{2}\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\nabla f)=\frac{u^{2}}{f}\langle\nabla\Delta f,\nabla f\rangle+\frac{u^{2}}{f}\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nabla f,\nabla f)&+2\frac{u}{f}\nabla\nabla f(\nabla u,\nabla f)\\
&-\frac{u^{2}}{f^{2}}\nabla\nabla f(\nabla f,\nabla f)+\frac{u^{2}}{f}\lvert\nabla\nabla f\rvert^{2}.\end{split}

The identity (
2.9
) now follows immediately by putting together (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.16
), (
2.17
), (
2.18
) and (
2.19
).
∎

In the following Remark, we clarify the link between the nonnegative divergence of
X
and the sub-harmonicity of the P-function
[
QX17
,
CV19
]
in space forms.

Remark 2.5

(Relation between
X
and the P-function of space forms.)
.

Let
\mathbb{M}^{n}_{K}
be the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{K}
or the hemisphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{K}
of negative or positive constant sectional curvature
K
, respectively, and of dimension
n
. Let
\mathbb{M}^{n}_{0}
be flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These manifolds can be realized as warped products
(I_{K}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1},dr\otimes\!dr+h_{K}^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, where

(2.20)

I_{K}=\begin{cases}[0,\infty)\qquad\text{if $K\leq 0$}\\
\Big[0,\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{K}}\Big)\qquad\text{if $K>0$}\end{cases}

and

(2.21)

h_{K}=\begin{cases}r\qquad\text{if $K=0$}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lvert K\rvert}}\sinh(r\sqrt{\lvert K\rvert})\quad\text{if $K<0$}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sin(r\sqrt{K})\quad\text{if $K>0$}.\end{cases}

Letting
f=\dot{h}(r)
, these manifolds are immediately seen to satisfy
\nabla\nabla f=n^{-1}\Delta fg=-Kfg
, to have constant Ricci curvature
\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits=(n-1)K
, and thus in particular to be static, that is
Q
vanishes. Plugging this information into (
2.9
), a routine calculation yields

(2.22)

{\mathrm{div}}(X)=f{\mathrm{div}}\big(\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\frac{2}{n}\nabla u+K\nabla u^{2}\big)=2f\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right|^{2}.

On the other hand, we have

\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\frac{2}{n}\nabla u+K\nabla u^{2}=\nabla P_{K},

where
P_{K}
is the P-function for space forms

(2.23)

P_{K}=\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}+\frac{2}{n}u+Ku^{2},

and thus we deduced

\Delta P_{K}=2\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right|^{2},

that is the sub-harmonicity stated in
[
CV19
, Lemma 2.1]
.

3.
Integral identities and geometric consequences

We specialize now
X
on a substatic Riemannian manifold with empty boundary or horizon boundary
N=\{f=0\}
.
The following straightforward application of the Divergence Theorem to
X
in the subset
\Omega
will naturally lead to the main results, Theorems
3.5
and
3.6
. Although the computation of the divergence of
X
in Proposition
2.4
is obviously carried out where
f\neq 0
, the identities that follow make sense continuously up to
N=\{f=0\}
since
f
appears at the denominator only as
\nabla\nabla f/f
and
\Delta f/f
, that by our Definition
2.1
of horizon boundary extend continuously at points where
f=0
.

Proposition 3.1

(Main integral identity)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold endowed with a nonnegative function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
possibly with horizon boundary
N=N_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup N_{l}
. Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded subset of
M
, with
C^{2,\alpha}
-boundary
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\sqcup\cup_{j\in J}N_{j}
for
J\subseteq\{1,\dots,l\}
, with
\Sigma\cap N=\emptyset
, and let
u
be a solution to (
2.2
) with
c_{j}>0
for any
j\in J
. Then, we have

(3.1)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{div}}X{\,\rm d}\mu\,+&\,2\sum_{j\in J}\left[\frac{(n-1)}{n}\int_{N_{j}}u\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma-\int_{N_{j}}u^{2}\lvert\nabla f\rvert\left(\frac{\Delta f}{f}-\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right)\right){\,\rm d}\sigma\right]=\\
&=-2\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}{\rm H}{\,\rm d}\sigma+2\frac{(n-1)}{n}\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma,\end{split}

where
{\rm H}
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
.

Proof.

The Divergence Theorem applied to the vector field
X
on
\Omega
states that

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{div}}X{\,\rm d}\mu=-\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle X,\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\right\rangle{\,\rm d}\sigma-\sum_{j\in J}\int_{N_{j}}\left\langle X,\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right\rangle{\,\rm d}\sigma.

Indeed, the vector field
\nabla u/\lvert\nabla u\rvert
is normal to
\Sigma
, as it is a regular level set of
u
by Theorem
2.3
, and points inside
\Omega
, and the same goes for
{\nabla f}/{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}
on
N_{j}
by assumption.
We thus compute the two boundary integrals, starting from that on
\Sigma
. We have

(3.3)

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle X,\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\right\rangle{\,\rm d}\sigma&=\int_{\Sigma}f\left\langle\nabla\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2},\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\right\rangle{\,\rm d}\sigma+\frac{2}{n}\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma\\
&=2\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert},\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\right){\,\rm d}\sigma+\frac{2}{n}\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma.\end{split}

Recall now the classical relation between the Laplacian
\Delta_{S}
induced on a hypersurface
S
and that of the ambient metric

(3.4)

\Delta_{S}\,v=\Delta v-\nabla\nabla v(\nu,\nu)-{\rm H}\,\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle

for a
C^{2}
-function
v
, where
\nu
is a unit normal to
S
, and
{\rm H}
the relative mean curvature. Applying it to
u
on
\Sigma
, using that
u=0
on
\Sigma
and that it solves
\Delta u=-1+u\Delta f/f
, we deduce

(3.5)

\nabla\nabla u\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert},\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\right)=-1+{\rm H}\lvert\nabla u\rvert

that, plugged into (
3.3
), yields

(3.6)

\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle X,\frac{\nabla u}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\right\rangle{\,\rm d}\sigma=2\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,{\rm H}{\,\rm d}\sigma-2\,\frac{(n-1)}{n}\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma.

We now turn our attention to the integrals on
N_{j}
for
j\in J
, in the right hand side of (
3.2
). Using that
N_{j}\subset\{f=0\}
is regular for
f
, and thus its normal
\nu_{j}
pointing inside
\Omega
is given by
\nu_{j}(x)=\nabla f/\lvert\nabla f\rvert(x)
for any
x\in N_{j}
, we get

(3.7)

\begin{split}\int_{N_{j}}\left\langle X,\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right\rangle{\,\rm d}\sigma=-\frac{2}{n}\int_{N_{j}}u\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma&-2\int_{N_{j}}u\lvert\nabla f\rvert\,\nabla\nabla u\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right){\,\rm d}\sigma\\
&+2\int_{N_{j}}u^{2}\lvert\nabla f\rvert\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right){\,\rm d}\sigma.\end{split}

Using again (
3.4
), this time applied to the function
u
on
N_{j}
, where
u
is still constant, we deduce

(3.8)

\nabla\nabla u\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right)=-1+\frac{\Delta f}{f}u,

where we exploited the minimality of
N_{j}
. Plugging it into (
3.7
), we get

(3.9)

\int_{N_{j}}\left\langle X,\nu_{j}\right\rangle{\,\rm d}\sigma=2\frac{(n-1)}{n}\int_{N_{j}}u\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma-\int_{N_{j}}u^{2}\lvert\nabla f\rvert\left(\frac{\Delta f}{f}-\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right)\right){\,\rm d}\sigma,

that, combined with (
3.3
) into (
3.2
), provides (
3.1
).
∎

The above result should be compared with
[
MP19
, Theorem 2.1]
, that yields (
3.1
) in the very special geometry of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

As a first application of Proposition
3.1
, we observe how, in the substatic case, in the mere presence of a
strictly mean-convex
hypersurface
\Sigma
homologous to
\cup_{j\in J}N_{j}Fs
, a purely geometric integral quantity computed on the horizon
N
is positive. This is what will enable us to choose the sharpest constants
c_{j}
in (
2.2
). With
S
being homologous to
\cup_{j\in J}N_{j}
, we mean that there exists a a bounded open set
E
with
\partial E=S\sqcup(\cup_{j\in J}N_{j})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a substatic Riemannian manifold with substatic potential
f
and nonempty horizon boundary
N=N_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup N_{l}
. Assume there exists a
C^{2,\alpha}
strictly mean-convex hypersurface
S
homologous to
\cup_{j\in J}N_{j}
. Then

(3.10)

\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert\left[\frac{\Delta f}{f}-\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right)\right]{\,\rm d}\sigma>0

for any
j\in J
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists
i\in J
such that (
3.10
) fails. Then, let
E
be the subset bounded by the disjoint
\Sigma
and
\cup_{j\in J}N_{j}
, and let
u
be a solution to (
2.2
) for some positive
c_{1},\dots,c_{l}
. Apply (
3.1
) in
E
to get

(3.11)

\begin{split}2\frac{(n-1)}{n}\int_{N_{i}}c_{i}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma\leq\sum_{j\in(J\setminus\{i\})}&c_{j}\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert\left[\frac{\Delta f}{f}-\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right)\right]{\,\rm d}\sigma\\
&-2\inf_{S}f\inf_{S}{\rm H}\int_{S}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma+2\frac{(n-1)}{n}\sup_{S}f\int_{S}\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma,\end{split}

where we exploited the substaticity of
(M,g)
to infer the nonnegativity of the divergence of
X
computed in Proposition
2.4
, the assumption on the mean curvature of
S
and the strict positivity of
f
outside
N
. We are going to derive a contradiction by letting
c_{i}\to+\infty
. Let then
\{c_{ik}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a diverging sequence of positive numbers, and let
u_{k}
be the corresponding solutions of (
2.2
) (the other constants
c_{j}
with
j\neq i
remain unchanged). Observe that the left hand side of (
3.11
) diverges as
k\to+\infty
, and thus the above inequality immediately yields a contradiction if the integral of
\lvert\nabla u_{k}\rvert
remains bounded as
k\to+\infty
. We are thus left to consider the case where the integral of
\lvert\nabla u_{k}\rvert
diverges as
k\to+\infty
. By the Hölder inequality, we can obviously deduce that the same happens for the integral of
\lvert\nabla u_{k}\rvert^{2}
, and estimating the right hand side of (
3.11
) with that we get

(3.12)

c_{ik}\int_{N_{i}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma\leq K_{1}\left(\int_{S}\lvert\nabla u_{k}\rvert^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma\right)^{1/2}-K_{2}\int_{S}\lvert\nabla u_{k}\rvert^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma,

where we absorbed the quantities in front of the integrals, depending only on
S
, in the positive constants
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
.
The right hand side diverging to
-\infty
as
k\to+\infty
, against the left hand side diverging at
+\infty
yields the desired contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.3

(Specialization to static metrics)
.

On a static metric with nonempty horizon boundary, that is with the tensor
Q
constantly vanishing and with
N\neq\emptyset
, the geometric inequality (
3.10
) simplifies as follows. The metric being static, and
f
vanishing on
N
, we have
\nabla\nabla f=\Delta fg
on
N
, where thus the Hessian of
f
vanishes. In particular, this implies that
N
is totally geodesic and hence minimal, and this why the request of minimality for
N
can be dropped when the metric is static. Letting
Y
be a vector field that is tangent to
N
, we get
\langle\nabla\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2},Y\rangle=0
, and so we deduce that
\lvert\nabla f\rvert
is constant on
N
. This positive number is usually referred to as
surface gravity
in the General Relativity literature. Moreover, again because of the vanishing of
Q
, the term in square brackets in (
3.10
) coincides with
-\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nu,\nu)
, where
\nu=\nabla f/\lvert\nabla f\rvert
. Consequently, in the special case of static metrics, we proved that if there exists a strictly mean-convex hypersurface homologous to
N
then

\int_{N}\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\nu,\nu){\,\rm d}\sigma<0,

as observed in
[
WW18
, Theorem 1.6]
without an explicit proof.

We do not know whether the minimality of
N
and the substaticity of
(M,g)
somehow suffice to produce a strictly mean-convex hypersurface as required above. However, this is better understood under asymptotic conditions at infinity. Going beyond the asymptotic flatness or asymptotic hyperbolicity, that naturally yield in the asymptotic region a foliation of strictly mean-convex hypersurfaces, we observe that, as a consequence of the recent work on the least area problem with obstacle
[
FM20
]
and of a nice approximation lemma via Mean Curvature Flow
[
HI01
]
, in a noncompact Riemannian manifold of dimension
2\leq n\leq 7
and with
outermost boundary
, the existence of a positive isoperimetric constant suffices to this aim. Let us recall that the boundary of a Riemannian manifold
M
is called
outermost
if there are no closed minimal hypersurfaces compactly contained in
M\setminus N
. Such result holds actually regardless of curvature conditions on the Riemannian manifold with boundary.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a noncompact Riemannian manifold of dimension
2\leq n\leq 7
with smooth, compact and outermost boundary
N
. Assume there exists a positive isoperimetric constant
C_{\mathrm{iso}}
, that is

(3.13)

\frac{{\lvert\partial E\rvert}^{n}}{\lvert E\rvert^{n-1}}\geq C_{\mathrm{iso}}

for any bounded
E\subset M
with smooth boundary, where we are denoting with
\lvert\partial E\rvert
and
\lvert E\rvert
the
(n-1)
-dimensional and the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\partial E
and
E
respectively. Then there exists a strictly mean-convex smooth hypersurface
\Sigma
homologous to
N
.

Proof.

Let
d:M\to[0,+\infty)
be the distance function from the boundary
N
. By the smoothness of such hypersurface, there exists
\delta>0
small enough such that
N_{\delta}=\{f=\delta\}
is a smooth
(n-1)
-dimensional hypersurface. Indeed, by
[
Man
, Proposition 5.17]
,
d
is smooth in neighbourhood of
N
, and thus it classically solves its defining equation
\lvert\nabla d\rvert=1
, and the regularity of
\{f=\delta\}
follows. By
[
FM20
, Theorem 1.1]
, we can solve the least area problem among sets containing
E_{\delta}=\{f\leq\delta\}
, and get a set
E_{\delta}^{*}
with boundary
N\sqcup\Sigma_{\delta}
, where
\Sigma_{\delta}
, due to our dimensional restriction, enjoys
C^{1,1}
-regularity by
[
SZW91
]
(see also the statement
[
FM20
, Theorem 2.18]
). Observe that, although all the work in
[
FM20
]
has been carried out in complete manifolds without boundary, it extends without any further effort to the present case. Since
\Sigma_{\delta}
minimizes the area among outward variations, we immediately get by considering its first variation that its weak mean curvature (classically defined almost everywhere due to the
C^{1,1}
-regularity) is nonnegative. Observe that
\Sigma_{\delta}
cannot be minimal, because of the boundary
N
being outermost. Then, we can approximate
\Sigma_{\delta}
in
C^{1}
by smooth strictly mean convex hypersurfaces, as done in
[
HI01
, Lemma 5.6]
, and let
\Sigma
be one of these approximators.
∎

We come back now to the proof of the main result.

Theorem 3.5

(Integral identity for Alexandrov’s Theorem)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a substatic Riemannian manifold with substatic potential
f
and with horizon boundary
N=N_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup N_{l}
. Assume there exists a
C^{2,\alpha}
strictly mean-convex hypersurface
S
homologous to
\cup_{j\in J}N_{j}
. Let
\Omega\subset M
be any bounded subset with
C^{2,\alpha}
-boundary
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\sqcup(\cup_{j\in J}N_{j})
, for
J\subseteq\{1,\dots,l\}
, with
\Sigma\cap N=\emptyset
, and let
u
be the solution to (
2.2
) with

(3.14)

c_{j}=\frac{\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma}{\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert\left[\frac{\Delta f}{f}-\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert},\frac{\nabla f}{\lvert\nabla f\rvert}\right)\right]{\,\rm d}\sigma}

for any
j\in J
. Then, we have

(3.15)

\begin{split}0&\leq\int_{\Omega}f\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\right|^{2}+Q\left(\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f,\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f\right){\,\rm d}\mu\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{1}{R}\int_{\Sigma}f(R-\lvert\nabla u\rvert)^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma=\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}(\overline{{\rm H}}-{\rm H}){\,\rm d}\sigma,\end{split}

where

Q=f\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits-\nabla\nabla f+\Delta fg\geq 0

and

(3.16)

R=\frac{\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu+\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma}{\int_{\Sigma}f{\,\rm d}\sigma}\qquad\overline{{\rm H}}=\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{1}{R}.

In particular, if
{\rm H}\geq\overline{{\rm H}}
on
\Sigma
, then
\Sigma
is a totally umbilical hypersurface.

Proof.

First observe that, under the above assumptions,
c_{j}
is well defined due to Proposition
3.2
. Then, plugging
u
with such boundary data into (
3.1
), we see that the integrals on
N_{j}
cancel out. Moreover, by Proposition
2.4
, the integral on
\Omega
in the central term of the chain (
3.15
) coincides with the integral of the divergence of
X
.

Let us now compute the boundary integral in such central term.
Observe that, on the one hand, we have by Divergence Theorem, the regularity of
\Sigma
as
0
-level set of
u
and
N
being a horizon,

(3.17)

\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{div}}(f\nabla u){\,\rm d}\mu=-\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma,

while on the other hand we can compute the same term as

(3.18)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{div}}(f\nabla u){\,\rm d}\mu&=\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle{\,\rm d}\mu-\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu+\int_{\Omega}\Delta fu{\,\rm d}\mu\\
&=-\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu-c_{j}\sum_{j\in J}\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma,\end{split}

where in the first identity we employed the differential relation in (
2.2
) satisfied by
u
, while the second one is an integration by parts. Comparing (
3.17
) with (
3.18
), we get

(3.19)

\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu=\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma-\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma.

The above identity immediately implies that, with the choice of
R
done in (
3.16
), we have

(3.20)

\frac{1}{R}\int_{\Sigma}f(R-\lvert\nabla u\rvert)^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma=\frac{1}{R}\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma-\int_{\Sigma}f\lvert\nabla u\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma.

The derivation of (
3.15
) from (
3.1
) is now straightforward.

For what it concerns the total umbilicality of
\Sigma
in case
{\rm H}\geq\overline{{\rm H}}
, just observe that in this case we immediately deduce that

\left|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\right|^{2}=0

on
\Omega
. In particular, since
u\in C^{2,\alpha}
up to
\Sigma
, where
u=0
, we deduce that

\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g=0

on
\Sigma
, that we recall being a regular level set for
u
. Thus, we have

(3.21)

{\rm h}_{ij}-\frac{{\rm H}}{n-1}g_{ij}=\frac{1}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\left(\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right)(e_{i},e_{j})=0,

for any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, where
{\rm h}
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
and
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}}
form an orthonormal basis for the tangent space of
\Sigma
at any of its points. This is the umbilicality of
\Sigma
.
∎

The Heintze-Karcher inequality with explicit
L^{2}
-deficit follows from Propositions
3.15
and
3.2
as well.

Theorem 3.6

(Heintze-Karcher inequality with
L^{2}
-deficit)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a substatic Riemannian manifold with substatic potential
f
and with horizon boundary
N=N_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup N_{l}
. Let
\Omega\subset M
be any bounded subset with
C^{2,\alpha}
-boundary
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\sqcup(\cup_{j\in J}N_{j})
, for
J\subseteq\{1,\dots,l\}
, with
\Sigma\cap N=\emptyset
, such that
\Sigma
is
strictly mean-convex
. Let
u
be the solution to (
2.2
) with
c_{j}

\begin{split}0\,\leq\,\,\frac{n}{n-1}&\int_{\Omega}\bigg|\nabla\nabla u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g-u\left(\frac{\nabla\nabla f}{f}-\frac{\Delta f}{nf}g\right)\bigg|^{2}+Q\left(\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f,\nabla u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla f\right){\,\rm d}\mu\\
&+\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{{\rm H}}\left(1-\frac{n}{n-1}{\rm H}\lvert\nabla u\rvert\right)^{2}{\,\rm d}\sigma=\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{f}{{\rm H}}{\,\rm d}\sigma-\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\!-\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma.\end{split}

In particular, the Heintze-Karcher inequality

(3.23)

\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{f}{{\rm H}}{\,\rm d}\sigma\geq\int_{\Omega}f{\,\rm d}\mu+\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}\int_{N_{j}}\lvert\nabla f\rvert{\,\rm d}\sigma

holds true, and equality is achieved only if

is totally umbilical.

Proof.

Observe first that
\Sigma
itself furnishes the strictly mean-convex hypersurface fulfilling the assumption of Proposition

The rigidity statement concerning the equality case in (

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

Proof.

Observe first that by the rigidity statement in Theorem
3.5

\Sigma
is an umbilical hypersurface. Then, denoting with
\nu=-\nabla u/\lvert\nabla u\rvert
the unit outer normal to
\Sigma
,
by the (traced) Codazzi-Mainardi equations we have on

\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits_{j\nu}=\nabla_{i}{\rm h}_{ij}-\nabla_{j}{\rm H}=\frac{1}{n-1}\nabla_{j}{\rm H}-\nabla_{j}{\rm H}=-\frac{n-2}{n-1}\nabla_{j}{\rm H}=0,

Proposition 3.8

(Improved rigidity under a conformal Hessian condition)

Actually the basic Minkowski identity in space forms implies that, in this setting, connected embedded hypersurfaces of constant mean curvature necessarily satisfies

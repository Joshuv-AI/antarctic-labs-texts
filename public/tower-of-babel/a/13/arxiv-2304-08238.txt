Gradient estimate for solutions of the equation Δ p ​ v + a ​ v q = 0 \Delta_{p}v+av^{q}=0 on a complete Riemannian manifold
Jie He, Youde Wang, Guodong Wei

Abstract
. In this paper, we use the Nash-Moser iteration method to study the local and global behaviors of positive solutions to the nonlinear elliptic equation Δ p ​ v + a ​ v q = 0 \Delta_{p}v+av^{q}=0 defined on a complete Riemannian manifolds ( M , g ) (M,g) where p > 1 p>1 , a a and q q are constants and Δ p ( v ) = div ( | ∇ v | p − 2 ∇ v ) \Delta_{p}(v)={\rm div}(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v) is the p p -Laplace operator. Under some assumptions on a a , p p and q q , we derive gradient estimates and Liouville type theorems for such positive solutions.

Introduction

Gradient estimate is a fundamental and powerful technique in the study of partial differential equations on Riemannian manifolds. For instance, one can use gradient estimates to derive Harnack inequalities, to deduce Liouville type theorems, to study the geometry of manifolds, etc. Many mathematicians pay attention to the study on this topic (see for example,

,

In this paper we are concerned with the following equation defined on a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v+av^{q}=0,

(1.1)

where
p>1
,
a
,
q\in\mathbb{R}
are constants and
\Delta_{p}(v)={\rm div}(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v)
is the
p
-Laplace operator.

When

and

, the constant

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v+v^{q}=0,

(1.2)

which has been widely studied in the literature (see for example,
[

,
2
,
3
,
4
,

,
14
,
20

).
In particular, Serrin-Zou in
[
20
]
showed that if
1<p<n
, then equation (
1.2
) defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
admits no positive solution if and only if

0<q<\frac{np}{n-p}-1.

Next, we pay our main attentions on the equation (
1.1
) defined on a Riemannian manifold. When
p=2
, and

, equation (
1.1
) has a deep relationship with the Yamabe problem (see
[

,

,

]

Now, we will focus on the Liouville type results for equation (
1.1
) on Riemannian manifolds. When

,
equation (
1.1
) becomes the
p
-Laplace equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v=0,\quad p>1.

(1.3)

The celebrated Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate for harmonic functions showed

[
5
]
) provided the Ricci curvature of the manifold is nonnegative.
Kotschwar-Ni (

-harmonic function on a complete Riemannian manifold with nonnegative sectional curvature is a constant. Later, Wang-Zhang (
[
25
]
) initially applied the Nash-Moser iteration technique to study the gradient estimates and proved that any positive solution to (

) is a constant provided the Ricci curvature of the manifold is nonnegative. Wang-Zhang’s results only assumed the lower bound of Ricci curvature of

, and hence generalized Cheng-Yau’s result (
[
5
]
) for harmonic function to

) and substantially improved Kotschwar-Ni’s results. Compared with Cheng-Yau’s method, Wang-Zhang’s approach only involves differentiating the distance function once and hence bypasses the Laplacian comparison theorem.

Motivated by Wang-Zhang’s method, Zhao-Yang in
[
28

studied the gradient estimates of the following weighted

\Delta_{p,f}u+au^{\sigma}=0,\ \ p>1\ \text{and}\ \sigma\leq p-1,

defined on a Riemannian manifold with

is a constant and
a=0
. Very recently, the second named author and third named author (

]

) where

and

has non-negative Ricci curvature and

Instead of assuming the lower boundedness of curvature, it is worthy to point out that Grigor’yan-Sun showed in
[
10

that any nonnegative solution to equation (

) with

Inspired by Wang-Zhang’s work (
[

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dim(

) complete manifold with

, where

is a non-negative constant. Assume

) on the geodesic ball

satisfy one of the following two conditions,

(1)

\displaystyle a\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)\geq 0;

(1.4)

(2)

\displaystyle p-1<q<\frac{n+3}{n-1}(p-1).

(1.5)

Then there holds

\sup_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v^{2}}\leq c(n,p,q)\frac{(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)^{2}}{R^{2}}.

Note that when

By carefully analyzing the conditions (

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dim(
n>2
) complete manifold with

{\rm Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
,
where
\kappa
is a non-negative constant. Assume

) on the geodesic ball

\displaystyle a>0\quad\text{ and }\quad q<\frac{n+3}{n-1}(p-1),

When the Ricci curvature of

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature. If

and

.

Here we want to give several remarks of the above Liouville type result.

(1)

There is no extra restriction on

(2)

In

(3)

When

, we deduce that for

equation(

Theorem
1.3
improved Wang-Wei’s main results in

]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with

) with the constants

We notice that just one week ago, Huang-Guo-Guo posted a preprint on arxiv (see

The rest of our paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we will give a meticulous estimate of

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we denote
(M,g)
an
n
-dim Riemannian manifold, and
\nabla
the corresponding Levi-Civita connection. For any function
\varphi\in C^{1}(M)
, we denote
\nabla\varphi\in\Gamma(T^{*}M)
by
\nabla\varphi(X)=\nabla_{X}\varphi
. We denote the volume form
{\rm{vol}}=\sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}dx_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{n}
where
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a local coordinates, and for simplicity we may omit the volume form of integral over
M
.

The
p
-Laplace operator is defined by

\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right).

The solution of
p
-Laplace equation
\Delta_{p}u=0
is the critical point of the energy functional

E(u)=\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}.

Definition 2.1
.

v
is said to be a (weak) solution of equation (
1.1
) on a region
\Omega\subset M
, if
v\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)

and for all
\psi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\bm{\langle}\nabla v,\nabla\psi\bm{\rangle}+\int_{\Omega}av^{q}\psi=0.

It is worth mentioning that any solution
v
of equation (
1.1
) satisfies
v\in W^{2,2}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
v\in C^{1,\beta}(\Omega)
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
(for example, see
[
8
,
23
,
24
]
).

Next, we recall the Saloff-Coste’s Sobolev inequalities (see
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
) which shall play an key role in our proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete manifold with
Ric\geq-(n-1)\kappa
. For
n>2
, there exists a positive constant
C_{n}
depending only on
n
, such that for all
B\subset M
of radius R and volume
V
we have for
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B)

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\leq e^{C_{n}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}R^{2}\left(\int|\nabla f|^{2}+R^{-2}f^{2}\right).

For
n=2
, the above inequality holds with
n
replaced by any fixed
n^{\prime}>2
.

By a logarithmic transformation
u=-(p-1)\log v
, equation (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u-|\nabla u|^{p}-be^{cu}=0,

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle b=a(p-1)^{p-1},\quad c=\frac{p-q-1}{p-1}.

Now we consider the linearisation operator
\mathcal{L}
of
p
-Laplace operator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\psi)={\rm div}\left(f^{p/2-1}A(\nabla\psi)\right),

(2.2)

where
f=|\nabla u|^{2}
, and

\displaystyle A(\nabla\psi)=\nabla\psi+(p-2)f^{-1}\bm{\langle}\nabla\psi,\nabla u\bm{\rangle}\nabla u.

(2.3)

We first derive an useful expression of
\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})
for any
\alpha>0
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\alpha>0
, the equality

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})=&\alpha\left(\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2\right)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3}|\nabla f|^{2}+2\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2}\left(|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right)\\
&+\alpha(p-2)(\alpha-1)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-4}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}+2\alpha f^{\alpha-1}\langle\nabla\Delta_{p}u,\nabla u\rangle\end{split}

(2.4)

holds point-wisely in
\{x:f(x)>0\}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
A
in (
2.3
), we have

A\Big(\nabla(f^{\alpha})\Big)=\alpha f^{\alpha-1}\nabla f+\alpha(p-2)f^{\alpha-2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\nabla u=\alpha f^{\alpha-1}A(\nabla f).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\alpha\text{div}\Big(f^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}A(\nabla f)\Big)=\alpha\Big\langle\nabla(f^{\alpha-1}),f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}A(\nabla f)\Big\rangle+\alpha f^{\alpha-1}\mathcal{L}(f).

Direction computation shows that

\displaystyle\alpha\Big\langle\nabla(f^{\alpha-1}),f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}A(\nabla f)\Big\rangle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\alpha(\alpha-1)f^{\alpha-2}\nabla f,f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\nabla f+(p-2)f^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\nabla u\Big\rangle,

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha f^{\alpha-1}\mathcal{L}(f)=&\alpha f^{\alpha-1}\Big(\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)f^{\frac{p}{2}-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\Delta f+(p-2)\left(\frac{p}{2}-2\right)f^{\frac{p}{2}-3}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}\\
&+(p-2)f^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\langle\nabla\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle,\nabla u\rangle+(p-2)f^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\Delta u\Big).\end{split}

(2.6)

We combine (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})=&\alpha\left(\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2\right)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3}|\nabla f|^{2}+\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2}\Delta f\\
&+\alpha(p-2)\left(\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3\right)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-4}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}\\
&+\alpha(p-2)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3}\langle\nabla\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle,\nabla u\rangle+\alpha(p-2)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\Delta u.\end{split}

(2.7)

On the other hand, by the definition of the
p
-Laplacian we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\Delta_{p}u,\nabla u\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)\left(\frac{p}{2}-2\right)f^{\frac{p}{2}-3}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}+\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)f^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\langle\nabla\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle,\nabla u\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)f^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\Delta u+f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle.

Thus, the last term of the right hand side of (
2.7
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha(p-2)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\Delta u&=2\alpha f^{\alpha-1}\langle\nabla\Delta_{p}u,\nabla u\rangle-2\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2}\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle\\
&\quad-\alpha(p-2)\left(\frac{p}{2}-2\right)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-4}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}\\
&\quad-\alpha(p-2)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3}\langle\nabla\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle,\nabla u\rangle.\end{split}

(2.8)

By (
2.8
) and the following Bochner formula

\frac{1}{2}\Delta f=|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle,

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})=

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2\right)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-3}|\nabla f|^{2}+2\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2}\left(|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha(p-2)(\alpha-1)f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-4}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}+2\alpha f^{\alpha-1}\langle\nabla\Delta_{p}u,\nabla u\rangle.

Thus, we finish the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Proof of main theorem

3.1.
Estimate for the linearisation operator of
p
-Laplace operator

We first need to prove a pointwise estimate for
\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
be a solution of equation (
2.1
) on
(M,g)
with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq-(n-1)\kappa
. Denote
f=|\nabla u|^{2}
and
a_{1}=\left|p-\frac{2(p-1)}{n-1}\right|
. The followings hold:

(1)

If

a\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)\geq 0,

then for any
\alpha\geq 1
, the inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}}}{n-1}-2(n-1)\alpha\kappa f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-1}-\alpha a_{1}f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}|\nabla f|.

holds point-wisely in
\{x:f(x)>0\}
.

(2)

If

\displaystyle p-1<q<\frac{n+3}{n-1}(p-1),

then there exists some
\alpha_{0}(p,q,n)>0
such that for any
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, the inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}}-2\alpha(n-1)\kappa f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-1}-\alpha a_{1}f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}|\nabla f|

holds point-wisely in
\{x:f(x)>0\}
, where

\displaystyle\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}=\frac{1}{n-1}-\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)^{2}\frac{(2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1}{4(2\alpha-1)}>0.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be an orthonormal frame of
TM
on a domain with
f\neq 0
such that
e_{1}=\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
. We have
u_{1}=f^{1/2}
and

\displaystyle u_{11}=\frac{1}{2}f^{-1/2}f_{1}=\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\bm{\langle}\nabla u,\nabla f\bm{\rangle}.

(3.1)

In this case,

has the following expression (see
[

,
25
]

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u=

\displaystyle f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\left((p-1)u_{11}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}u_{ii}\right).

Substituting the above equality into equation (
2.1
), we obtain:

\displaystyle(p-1)u_{11}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}u_{ii}=f+be^{cu}f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

(3.2)

Using the fact
u_{1}=f^{1/2}
again yields

\displaystyle|\nabla f|^{2}/f=4\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{1i}^{2}.

(3.3)

By Cauchy inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{1i}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}u_{ii}^{2}\geq\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{4f}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=2}u_{ii}\right)^{2}.

(3.4)

It follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\Delta_{p}u,\nabla u\rangle=pf^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{11}+bce^{cu}f.

(3.5)

Substituting (

), (
3.4
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}\left(f^{\alpha}\right)\geq&\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\frac{p-3}{2}\right)\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=2}u_{ii}\right)^{2}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\\
&+2(p-2)(\alpha-1)u_{11}^{2}+f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(pf^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{11}+bce^{cu}f\right).\end{split}

(3.6)

Furthermore, noting that
|\nabla f|^{2}/f\geq 4u_{11}^{2}
which follows from (
3.3
), and the fact
\alpha+\frac{p-3}{2}>0
since

and

, we can infer from (

) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}\left(f^{\alpha}\right)\geq&2\left(\alpha+\frac{p-3}{2}\right)u_{11}^{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=2}u_{ii}\right)^{2}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\\
&+2(p-2)(\alpha-1)u_{11}^{2}+f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(pf^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{11}+bce^{cu}f\right).\end{split}

(3.7)

By (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=2}u_{ii}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\left(f+be^{cu}f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}-(p-1)u_{11}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{2}+\left(be^{cu}f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}-(p-1)u_{11}\right)^{2}+2be^{cu}f^{2-\frac{p}{2}}-2f(p-1)u_{11}.

Substituting the above inequality into (

) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}\left(f^{\alpha}\right)\geq&(p-1)(2\alpha-1)u_{11}^{2}-(n-1)\kappa f+\left(p-\frac{2(p-1)}{n-1}\right)fu_{11}+\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}\\
&+b\left(c+\frac{2}{n-1}\right)e^{cu}f^{2-\frac{p}{2}}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(be^{cu}f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}-(p-1)u_{11}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(3.8)

If we denote
a_{1}=\left|p-\frac{2(p-1)}{n-1}\right|
, by (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle 2\left(p-\frac{2(p-1)}{n-1}\right)fu_{11}\geq-a_{1}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|.

Substituting
a_{1}
into (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq&(p-1)(2\alpha-1)u_{11}^{2}-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|+\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}\\
&+b\left(c+\frac{2}{n-1}\right)e^{cu}f^{2-\frac{p}{2}}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(be^{cu}f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}-(p-1)u_{11}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(3.9)

Case I:
the constants
a
,
p
and
q
satisfy

a\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)\geq 0.

For this case we have

be^{cu}f\left(c+\frac{2}{n-1}\right)=a(p-1)^{p-1}e^{cu}f\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)\geq 0.

Since
\alpha\geq 1
, by discarding some non-negative terms in (
3.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-2}\left(\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|\right),

which is just the inequality in the first case of Lemma
3.1
.

By expanding the last term of the right hand side of (
3.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq&(p-1)\left(2\alpha-1+\frac{p-1}{n-1}\right)u_{11}^{2}-(n-1)\kappa f\\
&+b\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)e^{cu}f^{2-\frac{p}{2}}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|+\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}\\
&+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(b^{2}e^{2cu}f^{2-p}-2(p-1)be^{cu}f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}u_{11}\right).\end{split}

(3.10)

Case II :
the constants
a
,
p
and
q
satisfy

\displaystyle p-1<q<\frac{n+3}{n-1}(p-1).

In the present situation, the condition is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)^{2}>0.

This implies

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{1}{n-1}-\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)^{2}\frac{(2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1}{4(2\alpha-1)}>0.

Thus, we can choose
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,q)
large enough such that, for any
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
there holds true

\displaystyle\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}=\frac{1}{n-1}-\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)^{2}\frac{(2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1}{4(2\alpha-1)}>0.

(3.11)

On the other hand, by using the inequality
a^{2}-2ab\geq-b^{2}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(p-1)\left(2\alpha-1+\frac{p-1}{n-1}\right)u_{11}^{2}-2\frac{(p-1)}{n-1}be^{cu}f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}u_{11}\\
\geq&-\frac{(p-1)b^{2}e^{2cu}f^{2-p}}{((2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1)(n-1)}.\end{split}

(3.12)

Combining (
3.10
) and (
3.12
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq&\frac{(2\alpha-1)b^{2}e^{2cu}f^{2-p}}{(2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1}-\kappa(n-1)f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|\\
&+b\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)e^{cu}f^{2-\frac{p}{2}}+\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Applying the inequality
a^{2}+2ab\geq-b^{2}
again, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{(2\alpha-1)b^{2}e^{2cu}f^{2-p}}{(2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1}+b\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)e^{cu}f^{2-\frac{p}{2}}\\
\geq&-\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)^{2}\frac{(2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1}{4(2\alpha-1)}f^{2}\end{split}

(3.14)

Substituting (
3.14
) into (
3.13
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n-1}-\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)^{2}\frac{(2\alpha-1)(n-1)+p-1}{4(2\alpha-1)}\right)f^{2}

\displaystyle-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle 2\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}}-2\alpha(n-1)\kappa f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-1}-a_{1}\alpha f^{\alpha+\frac{p}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}|\nabla f|,

where
\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}>0
is defined in (
3.11
). Thus, we complete the proof of this lemma.
∎

From now on, we fix

\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha_{0}(n,p,q)

and use
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
\cdots
, to denote constants depending only on
n
,
p
and
q
. It is worthy to point out that if

q\to\frac{n+3}{n-1}(p-1)\quad\text{or}\quad q\to p-1,

then
\alpha_{0}(n,p,q)\to\infty
.

Denote
\beta_{n,p,q}=\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha_{0}}
. Moreover, from the definition of
\beta_{n,p,q,\alpha}
(see (
3.11
)) we can also see easily that

\displaystyle\beta_{n,p,q}<\frac{1}{n-1}.

So, we actually have proved that there holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha_{0}})\geq 2\alpha_{0}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-2}\left(\beta_{n,p,q}f^{2}-2(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla f|\right),

(3.15)

if one of the conditions (1) and (2) in Lemma
3.1
is satisfied.

3.2.
Deducing the main integral inequality

Now, we need to establish a key integral inequality of
f=|\nabla u|^{2}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}(o)\subset M
be a geodesic ball. Under the same assumptions as in Lemma
3.1
, we have the following integral inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2},\end{split}

where
\beta_{n,p,q}
is given in the above Lemma
3.1
,
a_{3}
,
a_{4}
and
a_{5}
depend only on
n
,
p
and
q
.

Proof.

By choosing
\psi=f_{\epsilon}^{t}\eta^{2}
as the test function of (
3.15
), where
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
is non-negative,
f_{\epsilon}=(f-\epsilon)^{+}
with
\epsilon>0
, and
t>1
is to be determined later, we can deduce from (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\langle}f^{p/2-1}\nabla f^{\alpha_{0}}+(p-2)f^{p/2-2}\bm{\langle}\nabla f^{\alpha_{0}},\nabla u\bm{\rangle}\nabla u,\nabla\psi\bm{\rangle}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\beta_{n,p,q}\alpha_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}}f^{t}_{\epsilon}\eta^{2}-2(n-1)\alpha_{0}\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}f^{t}_{\epsilon}\eta^{2}-a_{1}\alpha_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p-3}{2}}f^{t}_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|\eta^{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{0}tf^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-2}f^{t-1}_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}+t\alpha_{0}(p-2)f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-3}f^{t-1}_{\epsilon}\bm{\langle}\nabla f,\nabla u\bm{\rangle}^{2}\eta^{2}\\
&-\int_{\Omega}2\eta\alpha_{0}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-2}f^{t}_{\epsilon}\bm{\langle}\nabla f,\nabla\eta\bm{\rangle}+2\alpha_{0}\eta(p-2)f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-3}f^{t}_{\epsilon}\bm{\langle}\nabla f,\nabla u\bm{\rangle}\bm{\langle}\nabla u,\nabla\eta\bm{\rangle}\\
\geq&2\beta_{n,p,q}\alpha_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}}f^{t}_{\epsilon}\eta^{2}-2(n-1)\alpha_{0}\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}f^{t}_{\epsilon}\eta^{2}-a_{1}\alpha_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p-3}{2}}f^{t}_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|\eta^{2}.\end{split}

(3.16)

Next, we need to use the following two inequalities

\displaystyle f^{t-1}_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|^{2}+(p-2)f^{t-1}_{\epsilon}f^{-1}\bm{\langle}\nabla f,\nabla u\bm{\rangle}^{2}\geq a_{2}f^{t-1}_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|^{2}

(3.17)

where
a_{2}=\min\{1,p-1\}
, and

\displaystyle f^{t}_{\epsilon}\bm{\langle}\nabla f,\nabla\eta\bm{\rangle}+(p-2)f^{t}_{\epsilon}f^{-1}\bm{\langle}\nabla f,\nabla u\bm{\rangle}\bm{\langle}\nabla u,\nabla\eta\bm{\rangle}\geq-(p+1)f^{t}_{\epsilon}|\nabla f||\nabla\eta|.

(3.18)

Now, substituting (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) into (
3.16
), dividing both sides by
\alpha_{0}
and letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&2\beta_{n,p,q}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+a_{2}t\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}\\
\leq&2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+a_{1}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p-3}{2}+t}|\nabla f|\eta^{2}\\
&+2(p+1)\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-2}|\nabla f||\nabla\eta|\eta.\end{split}

(3.19)

Since
u\in W^{2,2}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\beta}(\Omega)
, we have
f\in C^{\beta}(\Omega)
and
|\nabla f|\in L^{2}_{loc}
, and hence the integrals in the above make sense.

By Cauchy-inequality, we have

\displaystyle a_{1}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p-3}{2}+t}|\nabla f|\eta^{2}\leq\frac{a_{2}t}{4}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}t}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2},

(3.20)

and

\displaystyle 2(p+1)f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-2}|\nabla f||\nabla\eta|\eta\leq\frac{a_{2}t}{4}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}+\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{a_{2}t}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

(3.21)

Now we choose
t
large enough such that

\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}t}\leq\beta_{n,p,q}.

(3.22)

Then, it follows from (
3.19
), (
3.20
), (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{2}t}{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}\\
\leq&2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{a_{2}t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.23)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}\leq&\left|\nabla f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\right|^{2}\eta^{2}+f^{\alpha_{0}+t-1+\frac{p}{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\\
=&\frac{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}{16}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}+f^{\alpha_{0}+t-1+\frac{p}{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.24)

Substituting (
3.24
) into (
3.23
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{4a_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}\\
\leq&2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{a_{2}t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\\
&+\frac{8a_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.\end{split}

We choose then
a_{3}
and
a_{4}
depending on
n
,
p
and
q
such that

\displaystyle\frac{a_{3}}{t}\leq\frac{4a_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{8a_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}+\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{a_{2}t}\leq\frac{a_{4}}{t}.

Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}\\
\leq&2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.25)

Moreover, Saloff-Coste’s Sobolev inequality tells us

e^{-C_{n}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}+R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2}.

Substituting the above into (
3.25
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-C_{n}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}\int_{\Omega}R^{-2}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}.\end{split}

(3.26)

Now we set
t_{0}=c_{1}({n,p,q})(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)
where

c_{1}(n,p,q)=\max\left\{C_{n},\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}\beta_{n,p,q}},2\right\},

and choose
t
such that
t\geq t_{0}
. Since

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\kappa R^{2}\leq\frac{2(n-1)}{c_{1}^{2}(n,p,q)}t_{0}^{2}\quad\text{ and}\quad\frac{a_{3}}{t}\leq\frac{a_{3}}{c_{1}(n,p,q)},

there exists
a_{5}=a_{5}(n,p,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\kappa R^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}\leq a_{5}t_{0}^{2}=a_{5}c_{1}^{2}(n,p,q)\left(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R\right)^{2}.

(3.27)

It follows from (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{n,p,q}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.28)

This is the required inequality and we finish the proof of this lemma.
∎

3.3.
L^{\beta}
bound of gradient in a ball with radius
3R/4

Next, we turn to giving the following
L^{\beta}
upper bound of the gradient of positive solutions to equation (
1.1
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete manifold with
Ric\geq-(n-1)\kappa
and

\beta=\left(\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1\right)\frac{n}{n-2}.

Assume
u
is a positive solution to equation (
2.1
) on the geodesic ball
B(o,R)\subset M
and
f=|\nabla u|^{2}
. Then there exists
a_{8}=a_{8}(n,p,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\beta}(B_{3R/4}(o))}\leq a_{8}V^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}},

(3.29)

where
V
is the volume of geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)
.

Proof.

If

f\geq\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,q}R^{2}},

then we can obtain from (
3.28
)

a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}\leq\frac{\beta_{n,p,q}}{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}.

We denote
\Omega_{1}=\{f\geq\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,q}R^{2}}\}
and
\Omega_{2}=\Omega\backslash\Omega_{1}
. Then, it is not difficult to see

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}=&a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega_{1}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega_{2}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}\\
\leq&\frac{\beta_{n,p,q}}{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,q}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}V,\end{split}

(3.30)

where
V
is the volume of
B_{R}(o)
. By choosing
t=t_{0}
we can obtain from (
3.28
) and (
3.30
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\beta_{n,p,q}}{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t_{0}}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,q}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}-1}V+\frac{a_{4}}{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.31)

Now, we choose
\eta_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R}(o))
satisfying

\begin{cases}0\leq\eta_{1}\leq 1,\quad\eta_{1}\equiv 1\text{ in }B_{\frac{3R}{4}}(o);\\
|\nabla\eta_{1}|\leq\frac{C(n)}{R},\end{cases}

and let
\eta=\eta_{1}^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}
in (
3.31
). Then, we take a direct calculation to derive

\displaystyle a_{4}R^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\leq a_{4}C^{2}(n)\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha\right)^{2}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha_{0}+2t_{0}+p-2}{\alpha_{0}+p/2+t_{0}}}\leq a_{6}t^{2}_{0}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha_{0}+p+2t_{0}-2}{\alpha_{0}+p/2+t_{0}}}.

By Hölder inequality and Young inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{a_{4}}{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\frac{p}{2}+\alpha+t_{0}-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\leq&\frac{a_{6}t_{0}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}+t_{0}-1}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha_{0}+p+2t_{0}-2}{\alpha+p/2+t_{0}}}\\
\leq&\frac{a_{6}t_{0}}{R^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}}\eta^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+p/2+t_{0}-1}{\alpha+p/2+t_{0}}}V^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+p/2}}\\
\leq&\frac{\beta_{n,p,q}}{2}\left[\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}}\eta^{2}+\left(\frac{2a_{6}t_{0}}{\beta_{n,p,q}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+p/2}V\right].\end{split}

(3.32)

By combining (
3.31
) and (
3.32
) we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{\frac{n(p/2+\alpha_{0}+t_{0}-1)}{n-2}}\eta^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\\
\leq&\frac{t_{0}}{a_{3}}e^{t_{0}}V^{1-\frac{2}{n}}R^{2}\left[\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2a_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,q}R^{2}}\right)^{t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1}+\frac{a_{6}t_{0}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2a_{6}t_{0}}{\beta_{n,p,q}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}\right]\\
\leq&a_{7}^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}e^{t_{0}}V^{1-\frac{2}{n}}t_{0}^{3}\left(\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1},\end{split}

(3.33)

where
a_{7}
depending only on
n
,
p
and
q
satisfies

a_{7}^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}\geq\frac{2a_{5}}{a_{3}}\left(\frac{2a_{5}}{\beta_{n,p,q}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}+\frac{a_{6}}{a_{3}t_{0}}\left(\frac{2a_{6}}{\beta_{n,p,q}t_{0}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}.

Thus

\displaystyle\left\|f\eta^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+p/2-1}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)}\leq a_{7}e^{\frac{t_{0}}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}V^{1/\beta}t_{0}^{\frac{3}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+p/2-1}}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\leq a_{8}V^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}},

where
a_{8}
depending only on
n,p,q
satisfies

a_{8}=a_{7}\sup_{t_{0}\geq 1}t_{0}^{\frac{3}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+p/2-1}}e^{\frac{t_{0}}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}.

Since
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{3R/4}
, we obtain that

\|f\|_{L^{\beta}(B_{3R/4}(o))}\leq a_{8}V^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}.

Thus, we complete the proof of this lemma.
∎

3.4.
Moser iteration

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete manifold with
Ric\geq-(n-1)\kappa
. Assume
u
is a solution to equation (
2.1
) on the geodesic ball
B(o,R)\subset M
and
f=|\nabla u|^{2}
. Then there exists
a_{11}=a_{11}(n,p,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(o))}\leq

\displaystyle a_{11}\frac{(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)^{2}}{R^{2}}.

Proof.

We discard the first term of the left hand side of (
3.28
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\leq a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

(3.34)

Let
r_{k}=\frac{R}{2}+\frac{R}{4^{k}}
and
\Omega_{k}=B_{r_{k}}(o)
. It is easy to see that there exist cut-off functions
\eta_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{k})
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0\leq\eta_{k}\leq 1,\quad|\nabla\eta_{k}|\leq\frac{C4^{k}}{R};\\
\eta_{k}\equiv 1\text{ in }B_{r_{k+1}}(o),\end{cases}

where
k=1,\,2,\,3,\,\cdots
. Substituting
\eta_{k}
into (
3.34
) instead of
\eta
, we arrive at

\displaystyle a_{3}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta_{k}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega_{k})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle a_{5}t_{0}^{2}t\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta_{k}^{2}+a_{4}R^{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\left|\nabla\eta_{k}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(a_{5}t_{0}^{2}t+C^{2}16^{k}\right)\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}.

(3.35)

By picking
\beta_{1}=\beta
and
\beta_{k+1}=\frac{n\beta_{k}}{n-2}
, and letting
t=t_{k}
such that

t_{k}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha-1=\beta_{k},

we can deduce from (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle a_{3}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq

\displaystyle e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}\left(a_{5}t_{0}^{2}\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{n-2}\right)^{k}+C^{2}16^{k}\right)\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}},

where
k=1,\,2,\,3\,\cdots
.

On the other hand, we can choose
a_{9}=a_{9}(n,p,q)
which satisfies

a_{9}t_{0}^{3}\geq\max\left\{a_{5}t_{0}^{2}\left(\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1\right),\,C^{2}\right\},

since
\frac{n}{n-2}<16
. Then we have

\displaystyle a_{3}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq

\displaystyle 2a_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}16^{k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}}.

(3.36)

Taking
\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}
power of the both sides of (
3.36
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\beta_{k+1}}(\Omega_{k+1})}\leq

\displaystyle\left(2a_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}16^{\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}}\|f\|_{L^{\beta_{k}}(\Omega_{k})}.

Noting

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}=\frac{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}}{1-\frac{n-2}{n}}=\frac{n}{2\beta_{1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}<\infty,

we can derive

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(o))}\leq

\displaystyle a_{10}V^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{\beta}(B_{3R/4}(o))},

where
a_{10}
depending only on

satisfies

a_{10}\geq\left(2a_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2\beta_{1}}}16^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}}.

In view of (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(o))}\leq

\displaystyle a_{11}\frac{(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)^{2}}{R^{2}},

where
a_{11}=a_{10}a_{8}c_{1}(n,p,q)
.
∎

Recalling
u=-(p-1)\log v
where
v
is a positive solution to (
1.1
), actually we can conclude from Lemma
3.4
the following

Theorem 3.5

(=Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be an complete Riemannian manifold with
{\rm Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
, where
\kappa
is a non-positive constant.
Assume
a
,
q
and
p\ (p>1)
satisfy one of the following two conditions,

(1)

a\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)\geq 0,

(2)

p-1<q<\frac{n+3}{n-1}(p-1).

Then for any positive solution of
\Delta_{p}v+av^{q}=0
on a geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)\subset M
, we have

\sup_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v^{2}}\leq c(n,p,q)\frac{(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)^{2}}{R^{2}}.

Proof of Corollory
1.2
.

When
a>0
, the union of the range of
q
in condition (
1.4
) and the range of
q
in (
1.5
) is

q<\frac{n+3}{n-1}(p-1).

When
a<0
, the union of the ranges of
q
in condition (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) is

q>p-1.

Hence, Corollory
1.2
can be directly deduced from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Choosing
\kappa=0
in Theorem
1.1
implies

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq

\displaystyle c(n,p,q)\frac{1}{R}.

(3.37)

By letting
R\to\infty
in (
3.37
), we obtain

\nabla v=0.

Hence
v
is a constant and
\Delta_{p}v=0
. This contradicts to equation (
1.1
) since
v
is positive. We complete the proof.
∎

Next, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By Theorem
1.1
, for any
p\in M
we have

\displaystyle|\nabla u(p)|\leq\sup_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(p)}|\nabla u|\leq

\displaystyle c(n,p,q)\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we obtain that

|\nabla u(p)|\leq c(n,p,q)\sqrt{\kappa},\quad\forall p\in M.

Fix
x_{0}\in M
, for any
x\in M
, choose a minimizing geodesic
\gamma(t)
connecting
x_{0}
and
x
:

\gamma:[0,d]\to M,\quad\gamma(0)=x_{0},\quad\gamma(d)=x,

where
d=dist(x,x_{0})
is the distance of
x_{0}
and
x
. So we have

\displaystyle u(x)-u(x_{0})=\int_{0}^{d}\frac{d}{dt}u\circ\gamma(t)dt.

(3.38)

Since

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}u\circ\gamma(t)\right|\leq|\nabla u||\gamma^{\prime}(t)|=c(n,p,q)\sqrt{\kappa},

(3.39)

it follows from (
3.38
) and (
3.39
) that

\displaystyle u(x_{0})-c(n,p,q)\sqrt{\kappa}d\leq u(x)\leq u(x_{0})+c(n,p,q)\sqrt{\kappa}d.

As
u=-(p-1)\ln v
, we can derive the required inequality. Thus we finish the proof.
∎

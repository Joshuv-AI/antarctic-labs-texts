Frequency dependence of prestin: Intrinsic transition rates and viscoelastic relaxation
Kuni H Iwasa, NIDCD, NIH

Abstract




Abstract

Outer hair cells in the inner ear is important for the sensitivity, frequency selectivity, and dynamic range of the mammalian ear. Such a physiological role of outer hair cells is thought to be based an amplifying effect of those cells, which have mechanosensitive their hair bundles and motile cell body embedding prestin, a membrane protein with electromechanical coupling at high density. Mechanical power production by outer hair cells were previously evaluated based on an assumption that the intrinsic transition rates of this motile protein is faster than the operating frequency.
This report generalizes the previous treatment by incorporating intrinsic transition rates of the motile membrane protein. It was found that the transition rates attenuates mechanical power production of an outer hair cell such that viscous drag cannot be counteracted beyond the characteristic frequency of gating.

Introduction

Fast motile activity (
electromotility
) of outer hair cells (OHCs) is based on conformational transitions (gating) of prestin, a molecule with mechanoelectric coupling, with which motile response is elicited by the receptor potential. A charge movement results in extra membrane capacitance (nonlinear capacitance) and area changes in the axial displacement of the cell.

The speed of conformational transitions, which is critical for the function of OHCs, should depend on both “intrinsic” transition rates and mechanical load
[
1
]
.

Generation of mechanical power, which is critical to the physiological function of these cells, has been examined assuming infinitely fast intrinsic transitions
[
2
,
3
]
. What is the effect of finite intrinsic transition rates on power generation?

Specific Questions

•

How to describe both intrinsic transition rates and mechanical load?

•

How much power output is affected by finite “intrinsic” transition rates?

•

What is amplifier gain and limiting frequency?

Recall how an optimal limiting frequency was determined if gating is infinitely fast. In the presence of inertia, power generation has a peak value

\displaystyle P_{\infty}^{\mathrm{max}}/[1+(\omega_{L}/\omega_{g})^{2}]\approx\mu\omega_{L}^{2}.

(21)

\displaystyle(\omega_{L}/\omega_{g})^{2}\approx\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1+4(\omega_{\ell}/\omega_{g})^{2}}-1\right).

Discussion

Observations on the gating speed

Experimental data, which constrain the gating speed of prestin, include the frequency dependence of quasi-isometric movement of OHCs, stimulated by capacitive coupling in the microchamber configuration
[
5
]
, the frequency dependence of the membrane capacitance
[
6
,
7
]
and current noise spectrum of sealed lateral membrane
[
7
]
, and whole-cell mode with capacitance compensation
[
1
]
.

Of these experimental observations, fast gating is consistent with the force measured under quasi-isometric condition using AFM cantilever, which does not roll-off up to 60 kHz
[
5
]
. The current noise spectrum
[
7
]
suggests fast gating, above 40 kHz.

The most recent report
[
1
]
, however, indicates that the intrinsic gating of prestin is about 3 kHz, much lower to cover the auditory range.

The implication of the frequency dependence of the membrane capacitance of sealed lateral membrane of OHCs
[
6
,
7
]
depends on the interpretation as to whether the limiting factor is the intrinsic gating or mechanical relaxation time.

Compatibility with the physiological role

An upper bound of the limiting frequency estimated previously based on the fast gating model was about 10 kHz, consistent with the hearing range in the order of magnitude. If the gating frequency is
3 kHz
, however, the corresponding limiting frequency is only
3.3 kHz.

Conclusions

•

The power output of OHC can be significantly attenuated by the frequency of gating.

•

Gating frequency can dictate a limiting frequency of the local cochlear amplifier.

•

A large gap between the gating frequency and the hearing frequency range is a big puzzle.

Tradeoff between Diversity and Multiplexing Gains in Block Fading Optical Wireless Channels
Sufang Yang, Longguang Li, Haoyue Tang, Jintao Wang

Abstract
The diversity-multiplexing tradeoff (DMT) provides a fundamental performance metric for different multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) schemes in wireless communications. In this paper, we explore the block fading optical wireless communication (OWC) channels and characterize the DMT in the presence of both optical peak- and average-power constraints. Three different fading distributions are considered, which reflect different channel conditions. In each channel condition, we obtain the optimal DMT when the block length is sufficiently large, and we also derive the lower and upper bounds of the DMT curve when the block length is small. These results are dramatically different from the existing DMT results in radio-frequency (RF) channels. These differences may be due to the fact that the optical input signal is real and bounded, while its RF counterpart is usually complex and unbounded. Index terms — Peak- and average-power constraints, outage probability, average error probability, optical wireless communication, diversity-multiplexing tradeoff.

Although there has been extensive research on the DMT in RF systems, few studies tap into characterizing the optimal DMT for the OWC systems. Some recent results have shown that many derivations in RF systems are not applicable for OWC systems due to the unique IM-DD transmission scheme

\displaystyle d^{\star}_{\textnormal{JB}}(r)=\frac{1}{2}d^{\star}_{\textnormal{ZT}}(r).

Also, under a small block length, they found analogous DMT bounds as in (
2

The one-half factor in (
4
) reflects the loss of half of the degree of freedom compared with RF channels, which is due to the fact that optical inputs need to be real-valued. However, as mentioned before, inputs in OWC channels represent optical intensities, and hence their values must also be nonnegative. In fact, it is the nonnegativity of the optical inputs that significantly complicates the analysis of performance limits in OWC channels.
Hence, directly applying half of the traditional RF MIMO capacity formula to calculate diversity gain in OWC channels cannot be theoretically justified. Also, the nonnegativity of optical inputs implies that the commonly used two-sided Gaussian random codes in RF channels are no longer admissible in OWC channels. Furthermore, due to the optical intensity inputs, the power constraints imposed on the inputs need to be described differently. All the above issues indicate the existing DMT results in RF channels cannot be directly extended here. Hence, how to characterize the optimal DMT in practical OWC channels still remains an open problem.

In this paper, we first investigate the optimal DMT by fully considering practical optical input constraints in three different fading channels. We first restrict the optical inputs to be real-valued and nonnegative. Also, a peak- and an average-power constraints are imposed on the inputs. Then we use negative exponential, gamma-gamma, and log-normal distributions to model the channel with atmospheric turbulence fluctuation from strong to weak intensities. For each channel condition, we establish optimal DMT in different block length regimes. It turns out that our derived results are fundamentally different from the above existing results, which may more precisely reflect the fundamental limits of practical OWC systems.

Specifically, the main contributions in this paper are as follows.

•

Bounds on Instantaneous Capacity:
By using a truncated exponential random coding argument and further applying the generalized entropy power inequality (GEPI)
[
30

, we first derive a lower bound on instantaneous capacity. Then, an upper bound is established by some algebraic manipulations on the asymptotic capacity in
[

•

Exact Characterization of Outage Diversity Gain:

With the above new instantaneous capacity bounds, we establish lower and upper bounds on outage diversity gain in general fading channels. Applying these bounds into our considered channels, we precisely characterize the outage diversity gain.

•

Error Probability Bounds on Truncated Exponential Random Coding:
We propose a truncated exponential random coding scheme, which helps to derive a new upper bound on average error probability. Based on this bound, we derive a tight lower bound on the optimal diversity gain in different fading channels.

•

\displaystyle(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r),

DMT Analysis on Negative Exponential Channel

In this section we consider the OWC channel with atmospheric turbulence according to negative exponential distribution. This distribution is commonly used to model the channel with relatively high atmospheric turbulence intensity. We first present results on the outage diversity order, and based on this, we further characterize the optimal DMT.

d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)
,
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)
and
d_{\textnormal{NE}}^{\star}(r)
as the parameters for negative exponential channel. Similar notations will also be used in the following gamma-gamma and log-normal channels.

As shown in Theorem
5
, to estimate the outage diversity order, we first need to calculate the distribution of vector
\bm{a}
in (
27
). To do this, we first analyze the distribution of
\mathbf{H}
. Since the entries in
\mathbf{H}
are assumed i.i.d., and by (
10
) we expand the distribution of
\mathbf{H}
as

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}{f}\left({h}_{ij}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\exp\left(-e^{\nu d_{ij}}{h}_{ij}+\nu d_{ij}\right).

(41)

Now we decompose
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{UDQ}
, and then for any entry
h_{ij}=\left[\mathrm{H}\right]_{ij}
, we have

h_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}u_{ik}\times(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{a_{k}}{2}}\times q_{kj},

(42)

where
u_{ik}=\left[\mathrm{U}\right]_{ik}
and
q_{ik}=\left[\mathrm{Q}\right]_{ik}
. Since
a_{i}
s are arranged in a decreasing order, at high OSNR, the last term
u_{in_{\textnormal{R}}}\times(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}}\times q_{n_{\textnormal{R}}j}
dominates other terms. By the fact that the entries of
\mathrm{H}
are nonnegative, we have
u_{in_{\textnormal{R}}}q_{n_{\textnormal{R}}j}\geq 0
. Hence
{f}\left(\mathrm{H}\right)
can be further simplified as

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{H})

\displaystyle\doteq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\exp\left(-(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}}\right).

(43)

Substituting (
43
) into (
27
), we get

\displaystyle f(a)\doteq(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\frac{n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2i-1}{2}a_{i}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\exp\left(-(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}}\right).

(44)

Now we apply Theorem
5
to bound the outage probability. Note that when
a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}<0
, in (
44
) the exponential term
\exp{\left(-(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}}\right)}\rightarrow 0
, and hence
f(a)\rightarrow 0
. With this observation, we can further reduce the integral domain
\mathcal{A}
in (
28
) to be
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}\cap\left\{a:a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\geq 0\right\}
and
\mathcal{B}
in (
28
) to be
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}\cap\left\{a:a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\geq 0\right\}
. Furthermore, when
a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\geq 0
,
\exp{\left(-(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}}\right)}\rightarrow 1\textnormal{ or }e^{-1}
, and then we can further simplify
f(a)
in (
44
) as

\displaystyle f(a)\doteq(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\frac{n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2i-1}{2}a_{i}}.

(45)

Now by definition (
17
), substituting (
45
) into (
28
), the outage diversity gain
d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)
at multiplexing order
r
is bounded by

\displaystyle\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)\leq d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)\leq\inf_{a\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a),

(46)

where function
d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)
is defined as

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\frac{n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2i-1}{2}a_{i};

(47)

and where
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\Biggl\{a:a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\geq 0,\textnormal{ and }(2n_{\textnormal{R}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}a_{i})^{+}\leq 2r\Biggr\},

(48)

and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\Biggl\{a:a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\geq 0,\textnormal{ and }2n_{\textnormal{R}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}a_{i}\leq 2r\Biggr\}.

(49)

5.2
Optimal DMT Characterization

We now characterize the optimal DMT of negative exponential channel. In fact, our derived outage probability can serve as a lower bound on average error probability. By Fano’s inequality
[
39
]
, we can show

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{\textnormal{e}}(\mathsf{OSNR})\,\,\dot{\geqslant}\,\,(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-d_{\textnormal{out}}(r)}.

(50)

Furthermore, note that the error probability bound on truncated exponential coding established in Section
4
can serve as an upper bound on average error probability. Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{\textnormal{e}}(\mathsf{OSNR})\,\,

\displaystyle\dot{\leqslant}

\displaystyle\,\,(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-d_{\textnormal{out}}(r)}+(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-d_{\textnormal{te}}(r)}.

(51)

Comparing the bounds in (
50
) and (
51
), we have the following observations:

•

If
d_{\textnormal{te}}(r)\geq d_{\textnormal{out}}(r)
,
d_{\textnormal{out}}(r)
becomes the dominant term in the RHS of (
51
). Combined with (
50
), the optimal diversity gain
d^{\star}(r)
is equal to the outage diversity gain, i.e.,

\displaystyle d^{\star}(r)=d_{\textnormal{out}}(r).

(52)

•

If
d_{\textnormal{te}}(r)<d_{\textnormal{out}}(r)
,
d_{\textnormal{te}}(r)
turns to be the dominant term in the RHS of (
51
). In this case, we can simply bound
d^{\star}(r)
as

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}(r)\leq d^{\star}(r)\leq d_{\textnormal{out}}(r).

(53)

With the above two observations, we only need to calculate
d_{\textnormal{out}}(r)
and
d_{\textnormal{te}}(r)
. The optimal DMT of negative exponential channel is characterized in the following theorem.

Theorem 7 (DMT of Negative Exponential Channel).

Given a channel with distribution in (
41
), if
l\geq l_{\textnormal{th}}^{\textnormal{NE}}=n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1
, the optimal diversity order is given by

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{NE}}^{\star}(r)=(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r),

(54)

otherwise,

\displaystyle l(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)\leq d_{\textnormal{NE}}^{\star}(r)\leq(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r).

(55)

{IEEEproof}

We first calculate
d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)
and
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)
, and then characterize the optimal diversity order
d_{\textnormal{NE}}^{\star}(r)
.

By linear optimization, it is straightforward to get that the infimums of
d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)
over feasible regimes
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
are both achieved at
a_{\textnormal{out}}^{\star}=[2(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r),0,\ldots,0]
in (
46
), and the infimums match. Hence the outage diversity gain is given by

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)=(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r).

(56)

Now we calculate
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)
. By reducing the integral domain
\mathcal{C}^{c}
in (
34
) to
\mathcal{A}^{c}
, and enlarging the integral domain
\mathcal{C}^{c}
in (
34
) to
\mathcal{B}^{c}
, we bound
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)
as

\displaystyle\inf_{\left(\mathcal{B}^{c}\right)^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)\leq d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{\left(\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a),

(57)

where function
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)
is given by

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\frac{n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2i-1}{2}a_{i}+\frac{l}{2}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\left(2-a_{i}\right)^{+}-2r\biggr);

(58)

and where
(\mathcal{A}^{c})^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{c})^{\prime}=\Biggl\{a:a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\geq 0,\textnormal{ and }(2n_{\textnormal{R}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}a_{i})^{+}\geq 2r\Biggr\},

(59)

and
(\mathcal{B}^{c})^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}^{c})^{\prime}=\Biggl\{a:a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\geq 0,\textnormal{ and }2n_{\textnormal{R}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}a_{i}\geq 2r\Biggr\}.

(60)

Compared with (
47
), we can see
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)
in (
58
) is also linear with respect to vector
a
, but with an extra term
\frac{l}{2}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\left(2-a_{i}\right)^{+}-2r\bigr)
. Given a block length
l
, we can still analyze the infimum of
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r,a)
over
(\mathcal{A}^{c})^{\prime}
or
(\mathcal{B}^{c})^{\prime}
through linear optimization. Here, we show that the infimums in (
57
) also match the same optimal point
a_{\textnormal{te}}^{\star}
. In the following, we characterize
a_{\textnormal{te}}^{\star}
under different block length
l
:

•

when
l<n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1
, the optimal
a_{\textnormal{te}}^{\star}
is given by

\displaystyle a_{i}^{\star}=0,\textnormal{ for }i=1,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{R}};

(61)

•

when
n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2k-1\leq l<n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2(k+1)-1
,
\forall k\in\{1,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{R}}-r\}
, the optimal
a_{\textnormal{te}}^{\star}
is given by

\displaystyle{}a_{i}^{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2,\textnormal{ for }i=1,\cdots,k,

(62a)

\displaystyle a_{i}^{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\textnormal{ for }i=k+1,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{R}};

(62b)

•

when
l\geq n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2((n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)+1)-1
, the optimal
a_{\textnormal{te}}^{\star}
is given by

\displaystyle{}a_{i}^{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2,\textnormal{ for }i=1,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{R}}-r,

(63a)

\displaystyle a_{i}^{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\textnormal{ for }i=n_{\textnormal{R}}-r+1,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{R}}.

(63b)

Substituting
a_{\textnormal{te}}^{\star}
into (
57
), we obtain that if
l<n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1
, then

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r).

(64)

Otherwise if
l\geq n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2i-1)+l(n_{\textnormal{R}}-k-r),\ k\in\{1,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{R}}-r\},

(65)

where the choice of
k
depends on the channel block length
l
(see (
61
), (
62a
), and (
63a
)).

Last, we characterize
d_{\textnormal{NE}}^{\star}(r)
. If
l\geq n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1
and at any
k
in set
\{1,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{R}}-r\}
,

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2-1)+(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-k-r)

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r).

The proof in this case is concluded by combining (
52
) with (
56
) and (
66
).

If
l<n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1
, by (
61
) and (
56
), we have

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{NE}}(r).

Combing (
53
) with (
56
), (
64
) and (
67
), the proof is concluded in this case.

),

6
DMT Analysis on Gamma-Gamma Channel

In this section, we consider the OWC channel with atmospheric turbulence according to gamma-gamma distribution. This distribution is commonly used to model the channel with moderate-to-strong atmospheric turbulence intensity. The outage diversity and the optimal DMT are characterized in the following.

6.1
Outage Diversity Gain Characterization

Due to the presence of
K_{\tau}(\cdot)
in (
11
), it is difficult to analyze original gamma-gamma distribution directly. Instead, we choose an alternative expression in terms of power series, i.e.,

\displaystyle{f}({h}_{ij}^{r})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})({h}_{ij}^{r})^{n+\rho_{2}-1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(\rho_{2},\rho_{1})({h}_{ij}^{r})^{n+\rho_{1}-1},

(69)

where

\displaystyle d_{n}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=\frac{(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\rho_{2}+n}\Gamma(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})}{\Gamma(\rho_{1})\Gamma(\rho_{2})(1+\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})_{n}n!}.

(70)

Combined with (
9
) and (
40
), we obtain the distribution of gamma-gamma channel as

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{H})=\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\Biggl\{

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\left(e^{\nu d_{ij}}h_{ij}\right)^{n+\rho_{2}-1}e^{\nu d_{ij}}

(71)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(\rho_{2},\rho_{1})\left(e^{\nu d_{ij}}h_{ij}\right)^{n+\rho_{1}-1}e^{\nu d_{ij}}\Biggr\}.

After some algebraic manipulations, we can rewrite (
71
) as

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{H})

\displaystyle\doteq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\Biggl\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\left(e^{\nu d_{ij}}\right)^{n+\rho_{2}}(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{n+\rho_{2}-1}{2}a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}

(72)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(\rho_{2},\rho_{1})\left(e^{\nu d_{ij}}\right)^{n+\rho_{1}}(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{n+\rho_{1}-1}{2}a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\doteq

\displaystyle(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}(\rho-1)},

(73)

where
\rho
denotes
\min\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}
.

Substituting (
73
) into (
27
) and (
28
), the outage diversity order
d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)
at multiplexing order
r
is bounded by

\displaystyle\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)\leq d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)\leq\inf_{a\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a),

(74)

where function
d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)
is defined as

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\frac{\left(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2i-1\right)}{2}a_{i}+\frac{n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}\left(\rho-1\right);

(75)

and where sets
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
are defined as in (
48
) and (
49
), respectively.

6.2
Optimal DMT Characterization

We present the main results in the following theorem.

Theorem 10 (DMT of Gamma-Gamma Channel).

Given a channel with distribution in (
71
), when
n_{\textnormal{R}}=1
, if
l\geq l_{\textnormal{th}}^{\textnormal{GG}}=\rho n_{\textnormal{T}}
, the optimal diversity order is

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{GG}}^{\star}(r)=\rho n_{\textnormal{T}}(1-r),

(76)

otherwise,

\displaystyle l(1-r)\leq d_{\textnormal{GG}}^{\star}(r)\leq\rho n_{\textnormal{T}}(1-r);

(77)

when
n_{\textnormal{R}}>1
, if
l\geq l_{\textnormal{th}}^{\textnormal{GG}}=n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1
, then

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{GG}}^{\star}(r)=(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r),

(78)

otherwise,

\displaystyle l(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)\leq d_{\textnormal{GG}}^{\star}(r)\leq(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r).

(79)

{IEEEproof}

We follow the similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
7
. Here we mainly emphasize the differences.

Compared with the function in (
47
),
d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)
in (
75
) contains an extra term
n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}(\rho-1)a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}/2

n_{\textnormal{R}}=1

n_{\textnormal{R}}>1

When

) are both achieved at point

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1+n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}(\rho-1)\right)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho n_{\textnormal{T}}(1-r).

When
n_{\textnormal{R}}>1

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{out}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)=(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1)(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r).

Now we calculate
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)
. Modifying the optimization domain in (
34
) with
\mathcal{A}^{c}
or
\mathcal{B}^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{(\mathcal{B}^{c})^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)\leq d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)\leq\inf_{(\mathcal{A}^{c})^{\prime}}d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a),

(82)

where function
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)
is defined as

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\frac{n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+2i-1}{2}a_{i}+\frac{n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}\left(\rho-1\right)+\frac{l}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}(2-a_{i})^{+}-2r\right);

(83)

and where sets
(\mathcal{A}^{c})^{\prime}
and
(\mathcal{B}^{c})^{\prime}
are defined as in (
59
) and (
60
), respectively.

Compared with the function in (
58
),
d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r,a)
also has an extra term
n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}(\rho-1)a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}/2
. We still separately consider the cases when
n_{\textnormal{R}}=1
and
n_{\textnormal{R}}>1
.

When
n_{\textnormal{R}}=1
, if
l<n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1+n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}(\rho-1)=\rho n_{\textnormal{T}}

82
) are both achieved at

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)=l(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r),

(84)

otherwise the infimums in (

) are both achieved at
a_{\textnormal{te}}^{\star}=2(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)

\displaystyle d_{\textnormal{te}}^{\textnormal{GG}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{\textnormal{T}}-n_{\textnormal{R}}+1+n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}(\rho-1))(n_{\textnormal{R}}-r)

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho n_{\textnormal{T}}(1-r).

When
n_{\textnormal{R}}>1
, since sets
(\mathcal{A})^{\prime}
and
(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}

DMT Analysis on Log-Normal Channel

In this section, we consider the OWC channel with atmospheric turbulence according to log-normal distribution. This distribution is commonly used to model the channel with weak atmospheric turbulence intensity. The outage diversity order and the optimal DMT are characterized in the following.

7.1
Outage Diversity Gain Characterization

Substituting (
9
) and (
12
) into (

), we have

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\left\{\frac{1}{h_{ij}\sqrt{2\pi{\sigma}_{l}^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\Bigl(\log\left(\exp\left(\nu d_{ij}\right)h_{ij}\right)-\mu_{l}\Bigr)^{2}}{2{\sigma}_{l}^{2}}\right)\right\}.

(86)

Following similar arguments as in Section
5.1
, we simplify
f(\mathrm{H})
as

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{H})

\displaystyle\doteq

\displaystyle(\mathsf{OSNR})^{\frac{n_{\textnormal{R}}n_{\textnormal{T}}a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{R}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\left\{\exp\left(-\frac{\biggl(\log\Bigl(\exp\left(\nu d_{ij}\right)(\mathsf{OSNR})^{-\frac{a_{n_{\textnormal{R}}}}{2}}\Bigr)-\mu_{l}\biggr)^{2}}{2{\sigma}_{l}^{2}}\right)\right\}\quad

9
Conclusion Remarks

This paper investigates three different block fading optical wireless communication channels and derives the optimal DMT under both optical peak- and average-power input constraints. We first establish an upper bound on the optimal diversity gain by characterizing the outage diversity gain. Then by analyzing the error probability of a random coding scheme, we establish a new lower bound. It turns out that these two bounds are close, hence give good approximations on the optimal diversity gain. In fact, at a large block length regime, these bounds match, thus we can precisely characterize the optimal diversity gain. Our derived DMT results are fundamentally different from their counterparts in traditional RF channels. These differences are due to the unique input constraints in the optical wireless channels. From the perspective of a practical
n_{\textnormal{T}}\times n_{\textnormal{R}}
OWC system design, our results imply at a specific multiplexing order
r
, letting the numbers of transmit and receive antennas satisfy
\lfloor{n_{\textnormal{T}}+1+r}\rfloor=2n_{\textnormal{R}}
may achieve the best performance in terms of diversity gain.

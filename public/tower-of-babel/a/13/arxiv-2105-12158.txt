Abstract
. The paper studies the initial boundary value problem related to the dynamic evolution of an elastic beam interacting with a substrate through an elastic-breakable forcing term. This discontinuous interaction is aimed to model the phenomenon of attachement-detachement of the beam occurring in adhesion phenomena. We prove existence of solutions in energy space and exhibit various counterexamples to uniqueness. Furthermore we characterize some relavant features of the solutions, ruling the main effectes of the nonlinearity due to the elasic-breakable term on the dynamical evolution, by proving the linearization property according to [ 5 ] and an asymtotic result pertaining the long time behavior.

1.
Introduction

In a broader sense the term
adhesion
refers to a physical situation in which two material bodies, during their mechanical evolution, experience a contact interaction which fails in (possibly bounded) regions of space-time and restores after a while. The phenomenon strongly depends on the constitutive properties of the involved materials and a crucial problem relies in understanding the nature of the interaction. The manifestation of such such phenomenon occurs at every scale, ranging from DNA molecules to the structural engineering works. Mathematics has looked at these problems, at least in the stationary case, since
the seminal paper
[
1
]
and
in some recent works (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
) one of the authors contributed to the study of the static problem of adhesion of elastic structures by exploiting different constitutive assumptions to the aim of
characterizing, in a variational framework, the interplay of the of debonding with other constitutive properties.

However the dynamical problem is a different story since at the heart of the question stays the understanding of the
attachement-detachement occurrence and how this affects the whole evolution problem. This is a subtle problem since the analytical tools at disposal, such as spatio-temporal estimates in some norms, seem too rough to catch exhaustive quantitative informations, even in short time.

In
[
3
,
4
]
the simplest mechanical model consisting in elastic string was considered, whereas a discontinuous forcing term was assumed to model the adhesive interaction of the string with a rigid substrate. The resulting mathematical problem is then ruled by an initial boundary value problem for a semilinear second order wave equation and the results in
[
3
,
4
]
show some tricky peculiarities of the problem. As it is well known in continuum mechanics, the other basic model for one dimensional elastic structures
is represented by the so called Bernoulli-Navier beam governing the flexural deformations of a slender material body. In the linear elastic framework the equation expressing the balance of momentum is ruled by a fourth order spatial differential operator.
Analogously to
[
3
]
, we assume a discontinuous forcing term to model the adhesive interaction of the beam with the external environment. One can visualize as a physical situation an elastic beam connected to a rigid substrate through a
foundation made of continuous distribution of elastic-breakable springs.

We study the well posedness of the mathematical problem proving existence of global in time solutions
in the natural energy space and exploit some features of the solutions to obtain information about the role played by the attachment-detachment occurrence on the dynamical evolution. Indeed, in
[
2
]
it was proved
that the main
effects induced by the nonlinearity at the transition from attached to detached states consist in
a loss of regularity of the solution and in a migration of the total energy through the scales.
Here we deepen the analysis by using the
linearization condition
introduced by Patrick Gérard in
[
5
]
according to which one can conclude that if the semilinear evolution problem satisfies such condition, then the nonlinear forcing term
does not induce any new oscillation or energy concentrations
(
[
5
]
). Furthermore we prove an asymptotic result
for the long time behavior in the case of bounded solutions, asserting the occurrence of three mutually exclusive states: the trivial one, the totally detached state, the totally attached state.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we formulate the initial boundary value problem. In Section
3
we state the main results of the paper consisting in
Theorem
3.1
(Existence of solutions), Theorem
3.2
(Characerization of adhesive states),
Theorem
3.3
(Long time behavior), Theorem
3.4
(Linearization property).
The proofs of these theorems are given respectively in Sections
4
,
5
,

6
,
7
.
In Section
8
we provide some examples showing non-uniqueness and lack ofsmoothness of the solutions.

2.
Statement of the problem

Let us consider an elastic beam under Bernoulli-Navier constitutive assumption, occupying in the reference configuration the
interval
[0,L]\subset\mathbb{R}
, the balance of linear momentum delivers
the semilinear initial boundary value problem

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\rho\partial_{tt}^{2}u=-\mu\partial_{xxxx}^{4}u-\Phi^{\prime}\left(u\right)&\quad t>0,0<x<L,\\
\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,0)=\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,L)=0&\quad t>0,\\
\partial_{xxx}^{3}u(t,0)=\partial_{xxx}^{3}u(t,L)=0&\quad t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\quad 0<x<L,\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x)&\quad 0<x<L.\end{cases}

We shall assume that

(
H
.
1
)

\Phi\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1,-1\})
,
\Phi
is positive, constant in
(-\infty,-1]
and in
[1,\infty)
,
convex in
[-1,1]
, decreasing in
[-1,0]
, increasing in
[0,1]
, and
\Phi(u)\geq\kappa u^{2}
in
[-1,1]
for some constant
\kappa>0
;

(
H
.
2
)

u_{0}\in H^{2}(0,L)
,
u_{1}\in L^{2}(0,L)
;

(
H
.
3
)

\rho>0
is the constant material density and
\mu>0
is the elastic stiffness.

As a consequence of
(H.1)
,
\Phi^{\prime}
has a jump discontinuity in
u=\pm 1
and

\displaystyle u\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,\infty)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}(u)=0,

\displaystyle 0<u<1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 0<\Phi^{\prime}(u)\leq\lim_{u\to 1^{-}}\Phi^{\prime}(u),

\displaystyle-1<u<0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 0>\Phi^{\prime}(u)\geq\lim_{u\to-1^{+}}\Phi^{\prime}(u).

To fix ideas, a function satisfying such assumption is

(2.2)

\Phi(u)=\begin{cases}u^{2}&\qquad\text{if $|u|\leq 1$},\\
1&\qquad\text{if $|u|>1$}.\end{cases}

In particular we have for all
u\neq\pm 1

(2.3)

\Phi^{\prime}(u)=\begin{cases}2u&\qquad\text{if $|u|<1$},\\
0&\qquad\text{if $|u|>1$}.\end{cases}

The forcing term
\Phi^{\prime}(u)
is thought to model the elastic breakable interaction between the beam and the external environment. It confers to the problem a localized nonlinearity which affects the evolution in a significant way.

The natural energy associated to the Problem (
2.1
) (i.e. to any solution
u
to (
2.1
)), is given at time
t
by the quantity

(2.4)

E[u](t)=\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{\rho(\partial_{t}u(t,x))^{2}+\mu(\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,x))^{2}}{2}+\Phi(u(t,x))\right)dx.

In general, the lack of Lipschitz continuity in the nonlinear term
\Phi^{\prime}
suggests we cannot expect the existence of conservative solutions, i.e. solutions that
preserve energy.
Also the physics underlying the problem, foresees a kind of
dissipation
when the material is detaching from the substrate and this is
accompanied by hysteresis cycles (see e.g.
[
8
, Appendix B]
).

3.
Main results

We begin by studying the well-posedness of Problem (
2.1
) and the regularity of its solutions (see Definition
3.1
below).
We prove the existence of Lipshitz continuous dissipative solutions and give examples of distinct solutions to (
2.1
) which do not depend continuously on the initial conditions.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a function
u:[0,\infty)\times[0,L]\to\mathbb{R}
is a dissipative solution of (
2.1
) if

(
i
)

u\in C([0,\infty)\times[0,L])
;

(
ii
)

\partial_{t}u,\,\partial_{xx}^{2}u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(0,L))
;

(
iii
)

u
is a weak solution to (
2.1
), i.e. for every test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with compact support

(3.1)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{L}&\left(\rho u\partial_{tt}^{2}\varphi+\mu\partial_{xx}^{2}u\partial_{xx}^{2}\varphi+h_{u}\varphi\right)dtdx\\
&-\int_{0}^{L}\rho u_{1}(x)\varphi(0,x)dx+\int_{0}^{L}\rho u_{0}(x)\partial_{t}\varphi(0,x)dx=0,\end{split}

where
h_{u}\in\partial\Phi^{\prime}\left(u\right)
, that is the subdifferential of
\Phi^{\prime}(u)
;

(
iv
)

u
may dissipate energy, i.e. for almost every
t>0
:
E[u](t)\leq E[u](0)
, namely (see (
2.4
))

(3.2)

\begin{split}E[u](t)=\int_{0}^{L}&\left(\frac{\rho(\partial_{t}u(t,x))^{2}+\mu(\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,x))^{2}}{2}+\Phi(u(t,x))\right)dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{\rho(u_{1}(x))^{2}+\mu(\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{0}(x))^{2}}{2}+\Phi(u_{0}(x))\right)dx=E[u](0).\end{split}

We remind that

h\in\partial\Phi^{\prime}\left(u\right)

means that
h:[0,\infty)\times[0,L]\to\mathbb{R}
sastifies

h_{u}(t,x)\begin{cases}=\Phi^{\prime}(u(t,x)),&\text{if $|u(t,x)|<1$,}\\
=0,&\text{if $|u(t,x)|>1$,}\\
\in[0,\Phi^{\prime}(1^{-})],&\text{if $u(t,x)=1$,}\\
\in[\Phi^{\prime}(-1^{+}),0],&\text{if $u(t,x)=-1$.}\end{cases}

Let us state the following theorem asserting the existence of dissipative solutions.

Theorem 3.1

(
Existence).

If
\Phi
satisfies
(H.1)
and
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
satisfy
(H.2)
,
then problem (
2.1
) admits a dissipative solution in the sense of
Definition
3.1
.

The following result provides a sufficient condition ruling the non-detachment of dissipative solutions in dependence on the initial data.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u
be a dissipative solution of (
2.1
).
If

(3.3)

\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,L)}<1\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad E[u](0)<\frac{4\kappa\mu}{3\vee 2\kappa}(\leq 2\mu),

where
\kappa
is defined in
(H.1)
, then

(3.4)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times(0,L))}<1.

The long time behavior is a very subtle problem for evolutionary partial differential equations, so by restricting the focus on bounded dissipative solutions, we are able to prove the following statement.

Theorem 3.3

(
Long Time Behavior).

Let
u
be a dissipative solution of (
2.1
) and
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
such that
t_{n}\to\infty
.
If

(3.5)

u\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times(0,L)),

then there exist a subsequence
\{t_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and two constants
a,\,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that

u(t_{n_{k}},\cdot)\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}\quad\text{weakly in $H^{2}(0,L)$ as $k\to\infty$,}

where

(3.6)

u_{\infty}(x)=ax+b.

Moreover, only one of the following statements can occur

(3.7)

u_{\infty}\equiv 0
,

(3.8)

u_{\infty}(x)\geq 1
for every
x\in[0,L]
,

(3.9)

u_{\infty}(x)\leq-1
for every
x\in[0,L]
.

The question at the basis of the last subsequent result can be formulated as follows:

How the nonlinearity characterizing the forcing term
\Phi^{\prime}
affects the evolution problem
?
We retain that Gérard’s linearization condition provides a precise mathematical tool to answer to the previous rather vague question. Indeed the absence of further energy concentrations or oscillations due to the nonlinearity, suspected to arise in correspondence of the attachment-detacment process, constitutes an interesting property in itself, also considering the nonuniqueness
of the solutions
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
below.

Theorem 3.4

(
Linearization Property).

Let
\{u_{0,n}\}_{n}\subset H^{2}(0,L),\,\{u_{1,n}\}_{n}\subset L^{2}(0,L)
,
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of dissipative solutions of (
2.1
) in correspondence of
such initial data, and
v_{n}
be the dissipative solution of the linearized problem

(3.10)

\begin{cases}\rho\partial_{tt}^{2}v_{n}=-\mu\partial_{xxxx}^{4}v_{n}&\quad t>0,0<x<L,\\
\partial_{xx}^{2}v_{n}(t,0)=\partial_{xx}^{2}v_{n}(t,L)=0&\quad t>0,\\
\partial_{xxx}^{3}v_{n}(t,0)=\partial_{xxx}^{3}v_{n}(t,L)=0&\quad t>0,\\
v_{n}(0,x)=u_{0,n}(x)&\quad 0<x<L,\\
\partial_{t}v_{n}(0,x)=u_{1,n}(x)&\quad 0<x<L.\end{cases}

If

(3.11)

\displaystyle\text{$u_{0,n}\rightharpoonup 0$ weakly in $H^{2}(0,L)$},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\text{$u_{1,n}\rightharpoonup 0$ weakly in $L^{2}(0,L)$},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{\rho u_{1,n}^{2}+\mu(\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{0,n})^{2}}{2}\right)dx<\frac{4\kappa\mu}{3\vee 2\kappa}(\leq 2\mu),

where
k
is defined in
(H.1)
, then the following linearization condition holds true

(3.14)

\left\|\partial_{t}(u_{n}-v_{n})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}+\left\|\partial_{xx}^{2}(u_{n}-v_{n})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\to 0

for every
T\geq 0
as
n\to\infty
.

4.
Existence of Dissipative Solutions

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Our argument is based on the approximation of the Neumann problem (
2.1
)
with a sequence of Neumann problems (
4.1
) characterized by smooth source terms and smooth initial data.
More precisely,
let
\{u_{0,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\,\{u_{1,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}([0,L]),\,\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
consider the approximating problems

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\rho\partial_{tt}^{2}u_{n}=-\mu\partial_{xxxx}^{4}u_{n}-\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(u_{n})&\quad t>0,0<x<L,\\
\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{n}(t,0)=\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{n}(t,L)=0&\quad t>0,\\
\partial_{xxx}^{3}u_{n}(t,0)=\partial_{xxx}^{3}u_{n}(t,L)=0&\quad t>0,\\
u_{n}(0,x)=u_{0,n}(x)&\quad 0<x<L,\\
\partial_{t}u_{n}(0,x)=u_{1,n}(x)&\quad 0<x<L,\end{cases}

where
\{u_{0,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\,\{u_{1,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are sequences of smooth approximations of

u_{0},\,u_{1}
, and
\Phi
respectively, i.e. they satisfy the following requirements

(4.2)

\begin{split}&u_{0,n}\to u_{0}\quad\text{in $H^{1}(0,L)$},\quad u_{1,n}\to u_{1}\quad\text{in $L^{2}(0,L)$},\quad\Phi_{n}\to\Phi\quad\text{uniformly in $\mathbb{R}$},\\
&\Phi_{n}^{\prime}\to\Phi^{\prime}\quad\text{pointwise and uniformly in
$\mathbb{R}\setminus\left((-1-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon)\cup(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\right)$ for every $\varepsilon$},\\
&|u|\geq 1+\varepsilon\Rightarrow\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(u)=0,\qquad\varepsilon>0,\>n\in\mathbb{N},\\
&\left\|u_{0,n}\right\|_{H^{1}(0,L)}\leq C,\quad\left\|u_{1,n}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}\leq C,\quad 0\leq\Phi_{n},\,\Phi_{n}^{\prime}\leq C,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},\\
&u_{0,n}^{\prime\prime}(0)=u_{0,n}^{\prime\prime}(L)=u_{1,n}(0)=u_{1,n}(L)=0,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},\end{split}

where
C
is a positive constant which does not depend on
n
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, (
4.1
) admits a classical solution for short time thanks to the Cauchy-Kowaleskaya Theorem (see
[
14
]
). Furthermore,
for such a problem, solutions are indeed global in time thanks to the following results.
Let
u_{n}
be the unique classical solution to (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.1

(
Energy conservation).

Classical solution to (
4.1
) preserves energy.

Proof.

Set
E_{n}:=E[u_{n}]
the energy corresponding to
u_{n}
.
We have to prove that the function
t\mapsto E_{n}(t)
is constant (with constant value
E_{n}(0)
). Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle E_{n}^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{\rho(\partial_{t}u_{n})^{2}+\mu(\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{n})^{2}}{2}+\Phi_{n}(u_{n})\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\left(\rho\partial_{t}u_{n}\partial_{tt}^{2}u_{n}+\mu\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{n}\partial_{txx}^{3}u_{n}+\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(u_{n})\partial_{t}u_{n}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\partial_{t}u_{n}\underbrace{\left(\rho\partial_{tt}^{2}u_{n}+\mu\partial_{xxxx}^{4}u_{n}+\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(u_{n})\right)}_{=0}dx=0.

∎

As a consequence of energy conservation, since the functions
\Phi_{n}
are positive,
we have the following boundedness result.

Corollary 4.1
.

The sequences

\{\partial_{t}u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are bounded in

L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(0,L))
.

Lemma 4.2

(
L^{2}
estimate).

The sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))
, for every
T>0
.

Proof.

Using the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}u_{n}^{2}(t,x)dx=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\left(u_{0,n}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}u_{n}(s,x)ds\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{L}u_{0,n}^{2}(x)dx+2\int_{0}^{L}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{s}u_{n}(s,x)|ds\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{L}u_{0,n}^{2}(x)dx+2t\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{L}(\partial_{s}u_{n}(s,x))^{2}dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{L}u_{0,n}^{2}(x)dx+2t^{2}\sup_{s\geq 0}\int_{0}^{L}(\partial_{s}u_{n}(s,x))^{2}dx,

the claim follows from Corollary
4.1
.
∎

The following result follows from Corollary
4.1
by a straightforward application of Gagliardo-Nirenberg Interpolation Inequality (see for instance
[
12
, Theorem at page 125]
).

Lemma 4.3

(
H^{1}
estimate).

The sequence

\{\partial_{x}u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))
, for every
T>0
.

Lemma 4.4

(
L^{\infty}
estimate).

The sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times(0,L))
, for every
T>0
.

Proof.

Fix
0<t<T
.
Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
and Corollary
4.1
imply that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))
.
Since
H^{1}(0,L)\subset L^{\infty}(0,L)
we have

|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\left\|u_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,L)}\leq c\left\|u_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(0,L)}\leq c\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))},\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,L),

for some constant
c>0
depending only on
L
. Therefore

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times(0,L))}\leq c\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))},

that gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(
space Lipschitz estimate).

The sequence

\{\partial_{x}u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times(0,L))
, for every
T>0
.

Proof.

Fix
0<t<T
and
0<x<L
.
Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
, and
4.3
imply that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(0,L))
.
Since
H^{1}(0,L)\subset L^{\infty}(0,L)
we have, for every
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,L)
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}u_{n}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\partial_{x}u_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,L)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\partial_{x}u_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(0,L)}\leq c\left\|u_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{2}(0,L)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(0,L))},

for some constant
c>0
depending only on
L
. Therefore

\left\|\partial_{x}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times(0,L))}\leq c\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(0,L))},

that gives the claim.
∎

.

Therefore, Definition

5.
Adhesive states

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Since

is continuous by (
3.3
) there exists
\tau>0
such that in the short time interval

[0,\tau]
we have

|u(t,x)|<1,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,\tau]\times[0,L].

So we can define
\tau^{*}
as follows

\tau^{*}=\sup\left\{\tau>0\;|\;|u(t,x)|<1\mbox{ for all }(t,x)\in[0,\tau]\times[0,L]\right\}.

We claim that

(5.1)

\tau^{*}=\infty.

Observe that, due to
(H.1)
,

\Phi(u(t,x))\geq\kappa u^{2}(t,x),\qquad(t,x)\in[0,\tau^{*})\times[0,L].

Therefore

E[u](t)\geq\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{\rho(\partial_{t}u(t,x))^{2}+\mu(\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,x))^{2}}{2}+\kappa(u(t,x))^{2}\right)dx,\qquad t\in[0,\tau^{*}),

and, in particular,

E[u](t)\geq\frac{\mu\left\|\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}}{2}+\kappa\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2},\qquad t\in[0,\tau^{*}).

Since
u
is dissipative, using the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(0,L)\subset L^{\infty}(0,L)
(see
[
6
, Theorem 8.5]
) and Lemma
4.3
, we have from every
t\in[0,\tau^{*})

\displaystyle\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,L)}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(0,L)}^{2}}{2}=\frac{\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+\frac{\left\|\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4\kappa}\vee\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\left\|\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}}{2}+\kappa\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\frac{3}{4\kappa}\vee\frac{1}{2}\right)E(t)\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\frac{3}{4\kappa}\vee\frac{1}{2}\right)E(0)<1,

(where the last inequality holds thanks to (
3.3
))
that proves (
5.1
).
∎

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{L}\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\rho(\partial_{t}w_{n})^{2}+\mu(\partial_{xx}^{2}w_{n})^{2}}{2}dx

8.

This section is devoted to exploit some qualitative properties of (

A key mechanism ruling these phenomena relies in the transition between the two configurations induced by the discontinuity affecting the forcing term

In particular, the first two examples show the lack of uniqueness while the last one and the numerical experiments enlighten the occurrences of lack of smoothness.

Example 8.1

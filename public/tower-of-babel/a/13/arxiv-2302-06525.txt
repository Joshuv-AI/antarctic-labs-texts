On finite generation in magnitude (co)homology, and its torsion
Luigi Caputi, Carlo Collari

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to apply the framework developed by Sam and Snowden to study structural properties of graph homologies, in the spirit of Ramos, Miyata and Proudfoot. Our main results concern the magnitude homology of graphs introduced by Hepworth and Willerton, and we prove that it is a finetely generated functor (on graphs of bounded genus). More precisely, for graphs of bounded genus, we prove that magnitude cohomology, in each homological degree, has rank which grows at most polynomially in the number of vertices, and that its torsion is bounded. As a consequence, we obtain analogous results for path homology of (undirected) graphs.



Introduction

Magnitude is a numerical invariant of isometry classes of metric spaces, and it
finds its motivations in the study of species similarity
[
SP94
]
– see
[
Lei21
]
for a general overview on the topic.
From the mathematical point of view, the definition of magnitude was motivated, and naturally arose, from considerations on Euler characteristic in enriched category theory
[
Lei13
]
– see also
[
LW13
,
Lei21
]
.
Aside from category theory and metric geometry, magnitude has also many interesting connections with other areas of mathematics, such as differential geometry
[
Wil14
]
, minimal surfaces
[
GG23
]
, geometric measure theory, and potential theory
[
LM17
]
.

Graphs, endowed with the shortest path distance, are prominent examples of metric spaces.
In this context, magnitude was studied first in
[
Lei19
]
.
Leinster showed that magnitude of graphs has a wealth of interesting properties.
Among those, we have that magnitude is a rational function which can be expressed as an integral power series, that is multiplicative with respect to the Cartesian product of graphs, and that it satisfies an inclusion-exclusion formula.
Remarkably,
magnitude shares similar features with – and yet is not determined by – the Tutte polynomial, see
[
Lei19
]
.

Rather than on magnitude itself, here we focus on its categorification: magnitude (co)homology, as defined by Hepworth and Willerton
[
HW17
,
Hep22
]
, see also
[
LS21
]
.
As with magnitude, also its categorification has attracted some attention – see, for instance, the recent papers
[
Gu18
,
AHK21
,
SS21
,
Asa22
]
.
In this context, categorification means to associate to a numerical (or polynomial) invariant a whole homology theory, whose Euler characteristic recovers the original invariant. The simplest example of categorification is given by the classical Euler-Poincaré characteristic of simplicial complexes, which is categorified by simplicial homology.
The categorification of the Jones polynomial introduced by Khovanov
[
Kho00
]
has shown the advantage of the homological and categorical approaches to the study of (polynomial) invariants of graphs, knots,
etc
.
After Khovanov’s discovery, the interest in categorification of
knots and graph invariants, and their properties, skyrocketed.
Among the well-known graph invariants which have been categorified using Khovanov’s framework, we can find the chromatic polynomial
[
HGR05
]
, and the Tutte polynomial
[
JHR06
]
. Magnitude homology follows similar ideas.
A consequence of the categorification process is that it
brings more refined invariants. This is also the case for magnitude homology: there exist graphs with the same magnitude, but non-isomorphic magnitude homology groups
[
Gu18
, Appendix A]
. Furthermore, the categorical approach allows us to explain some intrinsic properties of the magnitude of graphs, in homological terms; for example, the multiplicative property with respect to Cartesian products descends from a Künneth theorem, and a Mayer-Vietoris theorem categorifies the inclusion-exclusion formula.

Statement of results

We now come to the main point of this paper. It was asked in
[
HW17
]
whether there are graphs with torsion in their magnitude homology. This question was positively answered, first by Kaneta and Yoshinaga
[
KY21
]
, and then extended by Sazdanovic and Summers
[
SS21
]
. Computations show that any finitely generated Abelian group appears as a subgroup of the magnitude homology of a graph
[
SS21
, Theorem 3.14]
. However, to get such torsion, Sazdanovic and Summers increased the combinatorial complexity of the graphs. This means that, to get new torsion, they increase the combinatorial genus of the graphs. It is not clear from their proof whether this behaviour is a structural property of magnitude homology, or if it depends on the methods developed in
[
KY21
,
SS21
]
. Instead of working with magnitude homology, here we use its cohomological version
[
Hep22
]
, and we will confine ourselves within the category of finite, connected, undirected graphs. As magnitude homology and cohomology are related by a short exact sequence
[
Hep22
, Remark 2.5]
, passing to cohomology is not a restriction.

The main goal of this work is to prove that, to get torsion of higher order in magnitude cohomology, it is indeed necessary to increase the combinatorial complexity of the graphs. Therefore, this behaviour is somehow due to a structural property of magnitude (co)homology.
To achieve such result, we borrow methods from representation stability
[
CEF15
]
of (combinatorial) categories, as recently developed by Sam and Snowden
[
SS17
]
.
The categorical viewpoint enables us to gain a deeper understanding of the behaviour of magnitude (co)homology (and of its torsion), by looking at combinatorial properties of the category of graphs considered.
Our ideas were inspired by works from Ramos, Miyata and Proudfoot
[
Ram22
,
PR22
,
MR20
]
, who proved similar statements for matching complexes and unordered configuration spaces of graphs.
Recall that the magnitude cohomology of a graph
{\tt G}
, with coefficients in a commutative ring
R
, is a bigraded
R
-module
\MH^{*}_{*}({\tt G};R)=\bigoplus_{k,l}\MH^{k}_{l}({\tt G};R)
, where
k
is the cohomological grading, and
l
is the length, see Definition
2.1
. The main result of the paper is the following –
cf.
Theorem
3.5
;

Theorem 1
.

For every pair of integers
k,g\geq 0
, there exists
m=m(g,k)\in\mathbb{Z}
which annihilates the torsion subgroup of
\MH^{k}_{*}({\tt G};\mathbb{Z})
, for each graph
{\tt G}
of genus at most
g
.

Roughly speaking, the theorem says that the order of torsion classes in integral magnitude cohomology of connected graphs of genus at most
g
, in a fixed cohomological degree
k
, is bounded.
In fact, to apply the ideas of Ramos, Miyata, and Proudfoot to our context, we consider the category
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}_{{\leq}g}
of graphs with genus at most
g
(see Remark
1.7
) and contractions. The opposite category of this category is quasi-Gröbner in the sense of
[
SS17
]
, as proved in
[
PR22
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Let
\mathcal{V}
be the
{\bf CGraph}_{\leq g}
-module which associates to a graph
{\tt G}
the free
R
-module generated by the vertices of
{\tt G}
; we call it the
vertex module
(see Definition
2.7
). Then, in the spirit of
[
Ram22
,
PR22
,
MR20
]
, the main technical result of the paper is the following – cf. Theorem
2.11
:

Theorem 2
.

For any
k\geq 1
natural number, the
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k},R)
is finitely generated.

Using Theorem
2
, we prove that magnitude cohomology is a finitely generated functor on the category
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}^{\rm op}_{{\leq}g}
, and the technology developed in
[
SS17
]
allows us to infer Theorem
1
. As a byproduct, we also obtain an estimate of the growth of the ranks of magnitude cohomology groups –
cf.
Corollary
3.1
;

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field, fix a cohomological grading
k
and genus
g\geq 0
. Then, there exists a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
of degree at most
g+k+1
, such that, for all graphs
{\tt G}
of genus at most
g
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\MH_{*}^{k}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})\leq f(\#E({\tt G}))\ ,

where
\#E({\tt G})
is the number of edges of
{\tt G}
.

This last result says that the growth of the ranks of magnitude cohomology, in a fixed cohomological degree
k
, is at most polynomial, provided we restrict to graphs of bounded genus –
cf.
Corollary
3.2
.
We remark here that the results are consistent with previous computations – see also Example
3.3
, and the text thereafter – for example,
for cycle graphs
[
Gu18
]
. An immediate question is whether the polynomial nature of these estimates also holds for the category of graphs.
Unfortunately, at the moment, it is not known if the category of graphs
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}^{\rm op}
is a quasi-Gröbner category. Therefore our result does not extend
to the whole category of graphs, and an analogue of Theorem
3
may or may not hold.
We also remark here
that there are examples of cohomology theories of (directed) graphs whose rank grows exponentially in the number of vertices – see, for example, the growth rate of multipath cohomology
[
CCDT21
]
with coefficients in an algebra, see
[
CCDT22
, Table 2]
.

It was recently shown by Asao
[
Asa22
]
that magnitude homology is related to another homology theory of directed graphs, the so-called
path homology

[
GLMY20
]
. As a corollary of Asao’s work, we can directly infer similar results to those above for path (co)homology of undirected graphs. For example, we get the following – see Corollary
3.6
.

Theorem 4
.

For each
g,k
positive integers, there exists an integer
d=d({g,k})\in\mathbb{Z}
such that, for each graph
{\tt G}
of genus
g
, the torsion part of the path cohomology
\mathrm{PH}^{k}({\tt G},\mathbb{Z})

has exponent at most
d
.

The main ingredient in the proof of the theorems is that magnitude cohomology is a finitely generated
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}_{{\leq}g}^{\rm op}
-module, in the sense of representation theory of categories –
cf.
Corollary
2.12
.
It would be interesting to see whether, also in the context of directed graphs, the usual homology theories considered in the literature are finitely generated.

Conventions

All graphs are assumed to be finite, connected and undirected, and are denoted in typewriter font, e.g.
{\tt G}
. Unless otherwise specified,
R
will denote a Noetherian commutative ring with identity.
By
\mathbb{N}
we will denote the set of non-negative integers.

1.
Basic notions

In this section, we recall some basic notions needed in the follow-up.

1.1.
Finitely generated
\mathbf{C}
-modules

We start with some categorical notions as introduced in
[
CEF15
]
in the context of FI-modules. We recall the notion of Noetherian modules and categories, following the general framework developed in
[
SS17
]
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a (essentially) small
category, and
R\neq 0
be a commutative unital ring.
A
representation
of the category
\mathbf{C}
, or a
{\bf C}
-module over
R
, is a functor
\mathcal{M}\colon\mathbf{C}\to R\text{-{\bf Mod}}
with values in the category of (left)
R
-modules. A map of
\mathbf{C}
-modules is a natural transformation of functors. Denote by
{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})
the resulting category of
\mathbf{C}
-modules over
R
. The category
\mathbf{Rep}_{R}(\mathbf{C})
is an Abelian category, and all the classical notions as submodules, kernels, cokernels, injections, surjections, can be defined pointwise.
For example, a
submodule
of a
\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{N}\colon\mathbf{C}\to R\text{-{\bf Mod}}
such that
\mathcal{N}(c)
is a submodule of
\mathcal{M}(c)
, for each object
c
of
\mathbf{C}
, and such that the inclusion maps define a natural transformation. A submodule
\mathcal{N}
is called
proper
, if
\mathcal{N}(c)
is a proper submodule of
\mathcal{M}(c)
for at least one
c
. Analogously for kernels and cokernels.

For a given
\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, by an
element
of
\mathcal{M}
we mean an element of
\mathcal{M}(c)
for some object
c
of
\mathbf{C}
. If
S
is a subset of the disjoint union
\bigoplus_{c\in\mathbf{C}}\mathcal{M}(c)
, the
span

\mathrm{span}(S)
of
S
is the minimal
\mathbf{C}
-submodule of
\mathcal{M}
containing each element of
S
. We are primarily interested in finitely generated modules, which can be defined as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

A
\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is
finitely generated
if there exists a finite set of elements

m_{1},...,m_{k}\in\bigoplus_{c\in\mathbf{C}}\mathcal{M}(c)
,
such that
\mathrm{span}(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})=\mathcal{M}
.

We can also characterise finitely generated modules in terms of simpler modules.
For each object
c
of
\mathbf{C}
, define a
principal projective

\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{P}_{c}
, as follows. The functor
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is defined on an object
c^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{C}
by setting

\mathcal{P}_{c}(c^{\prime})\coloneqq R\,\langle\Hom_{\mathbf{C}}(c,c^{\prime})\rangle\ ,

i.e.
the free
R
-module with basis
\Hom_{\mathbf{C}}(c,c^{\prime})
;
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is then extended to morphisms accordingly, by compositions. For a morphism
\gamma\colon c\to c^{\prime}
, we denote by
e_{\gamma}
the corresponding element in
\mathcal{P}_{c}(c^{\prime})
.
Observe that, for any
\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, we have a natural isomorphism

\Hom_{{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})}(\mathcal{P}_{c},\mathcal{M})\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{M}(c)\ ,

which is given by
\phi\mapsto\phi(c)(e_{{\rm id}_{c}})
. Therefore, for each short exact sequence of
\mathbf{C}
-modules

0\to\mathcal{M}_{1}\overset{f}{\to}\mathcal{M}_{2}\overset{g}{\to}\mathcal{M}_{3}\to 0

application of the functor
\Hom_{{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})}(\mathcal{P}_{c},-)\colon{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})\to R\text{-{\bf Mod}}
yields a sequence which is naturally equivalent to

0\to\mathcal{M}_{1}(c)\overset{f(c)}{\to}\mathcal{M}_{2}(c)\overset{g(c)}{\to}\mathcal{M}_{3}(c)\to 0

and hence exact. It follows that the module
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is a projective object in
{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})
– which justifies the name principal projective. Finitely generated
\mathbf{C}
-modules can be characterised in terms of principal projectives,
cf.

[
CEFN14
, Proposition 2.3]
:

Lemma 1.2
.

A
\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is finitely generated if and only if there exists a surjection

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{c_{i}}\to\mathcal{M}

for some objects
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
of
\mathbf{C}
.

For a finitely generated
\mathbf{C}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, we will also refer to the objects
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
of
\mathbf{C}
in the lemma as
generators
of
\mathcal{M}
.
We now recall the notion of Noetherianity in the context of modules over an arbitrary category, which is central for this work.

Definition 1.3
.

A
\mathbf{C}
-module is
Noetherian
if all its submodules are finitely generated. The category
{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})
is Noetherian if all its finitely generated
\mathbf{C}
-modules are Noetherian.

Observe that, in discussing properties related to finite generation, it is often possible to restrict to principal projective modules. Indeed, by
[
SS17
, Proposition 3.1.1]
, the category
{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})
is Noetherian if and only if every principal projective module
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is Noetherian.

Example 1.4
.

Denote by
\mathbf{FI}
the category of finite sets and injective functions. Then, the category
{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{FI})
is Noetherian by
[
CEFN14
, Theorem A]
for any Noetherian ring
R
.

The category
\mathbf{FI}
is an example of an
EI
-category,
i.e.
a category in which every endomorphism is an isomorphism. It was proven that
{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{C})
is Noetherian, for any finite
EI
-category
\mathbf{C}
and Noetherian ring
R

[
Lüc89
, Lemma 16.10]
. This result was further extended to infinite
EI
-categories (satisfying some mild combinatorial conditions) in
[
GL15
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
\mathbf{FA}
be the category of finite sets, and all functions. Then, the category
{\mathbf{Rep}}_{R}(\mathbf{FA})
is Noetherian
[
SS17
, Corollary 7.3.5]
for any Noetherian commutative unital ring
R
.

Following the seminal paper
[
CEF15
]
, Noetherian properties of various other categories have been extensively investigated, in particular thanks to the techniques developed by Sam and Snowden in
[
SS17
]
. One of the main results in the latter paper is that, for a given category
\mathbf{C}
(with some combinatorial assumptions), and a (possibly non-commutative) left Noetherian ring
R
, the associated category of representations is Noetherian as well.

1.2.
Graph categories

The aim of this section is to discuss the combinatorial properties of certain categories of graphs, from the viewpoint of
[
PR22
]
.

In the following, by a graph
{\tt G}
we mean a finite, connected and non-empty
1
-dimensional CW-complex; it has a set of vertices
V({\tt G})
and a set of edges
E({\tt G})
, which are unordered pairs of vertices, possibly with multiplicities.
We recall the intuitive notions of contractions, deletions and minor morphisms; for a more detailed account of these operations, we refer to
[
Ram22
,
MR20
]
.

Let
{\tt G}
be a graph and
e\in E({\tt G})
be an edge. The
contraction
of
{\tt G}
with respect to the edge
e
, is the graph
{\tt G}/e
obtained from
{\tt G}
by contracting
e
to a point.
The
deletion
of
e
is the graph
{\tt G}\setminus e
obtained from
{\tt G}
by removing
e
from the set of edges of
{\tt G}
.
Note that the operation of contracting edges does not change the homotopy type of
{\tt G}
, unless the edge contracted is a self-loop.
We are not going to consider this latter case, and only allow contractions of edges with distinct endpoints. Similarly, when dealing with deletions, we will only allow deletions of graphs for which
{\tt G}\setminus e
is connected.
A
minor
of a graph
{\tt G}^{\prime}
is a graph
{\tt G}
that is isomorphic to a graph obtained from
{\tt G}^{\prime}
by iterative contractions and deletions.
More formally, we have the following definition of minor morphism of graphs.

Definition 1.6

(
[
Ram22
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

A
minor morphism

\phi\colon{\tt G}^{\prime}\to{\tt G}
is a map of sets

\phi\colon V({\tt G}^{\prime})\sqcup E({\tt G}^{\prime})\sqcup\{\star\}\to V({\tt G})\sqcup E({\tt G})\sqcup\{\star\}\ ,

such that:

•

\phi(V({\tt G}^{\prime}))=V({\tt G})
and
\phi(\star)=\star
;

•

if an edge
e\in E({\tt G}^{\prime})
has endpoints
\{v,w\}
,
and
\phi(e)\neq\star
,
then either
\phi(e)=\phi(v)=\phi(w)
is a vertex of
{\tt G}
,
or
\phi(e)
is an edge of
{\tt G}
with endpoints
\phi(v)
and
\phi(w)
;

•

\phi
maps
\phi^{-1}(E({\tt G}))
bijectively onto
E({\tt G})
;

•

for each vertex
v\in{\tt G}
, the preimage
\phi^{-1}(v)
(as a subgraph of
{\tt G}^{\prime}
) is a tree.

The preimage of
\star
under
\phi
consists of deleted edges, whereas the edges that are mapped to vertices of
{\tt G}
represent the contracted ones.
Furthermore, the last item in the definition implies that self-loops cannot be contracted, but only deleted.

In the follow-up, we will mainly consider (subcategories of) the category
\mathbf{Graph}

of finite non-empty graphs, with minor morphisms of graphs. For example,
we consider the subcategory
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}
of
\mathbf{Graph}
consisting of finite, non-empty, connected, graphs, where the morphisms are given by contractions.

For a graph
{\tt G}
, we call the (combinatorial)
genus
of
{\tt G}
the quantity
|E({\tt G})|-|V({\tt G})|+1
. This is often called
circuit rank
but, for consistency, here we prefer to follow the terminology adopted in
[
PR22
]
.

Remark 1.7
.

Consider the full subcategory
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}_{g}
of
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}
given by graphs of genus
g
. In particular, as contractions do not change the genus of a graph,
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}
can be seen as the disjoint union of the categories
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}_{g}
, for
g\in\mathbb{N}
. We will also denote by
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}_{\leq g}
the subcategory of
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}
spanned by graphs of genus
\leq g
. The category
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}_{g}
was denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{g}
in
[
PR22
]
.

We have the following key result:

Theorem 1.8

(
[
PR22
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For any
g\geq 0
, the representation category of
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
is Noetherian over any Noetherian ring.

Thus, for any commutative unital Noetherian ring
R
, all submodules of finitely generated
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
modules
are finitely generated.

2.
Magnitude (co)homology

In this section, we prove the main result of the paper, that is, that magnitude cohomology of graphs is a finitely generated functor on the category
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}^{\rm op}_{\leq g}
of connected graphs with bounded genus, and contractions. As a consequence, in the next section, we shall recover structural results on its torsion and rank growth.

2.1.
Magnitude homology and cohomology of graphs

We start with recalling the definition of magnitude homology of graphs, and we will then focus on magnitude cohomology. We will mainly follow
[
HW17
,
Asa22
,
Hep22
]
.

First, observe that a connected graph can be seen as a metric space with the path metric – the distance between two vertices of the graph being given by the length of a shortest path in the graph connecting them.
To be more precise, the points of the metric space associated to a graph
{\tt G}
are its vertices, and edges are declared to have length
1
. Concretely, the metric
d_{{\tt G}}
on
{\tt G}
is given by

\displaystyle d_{{\tt G}}(v,w)\coloneqq\min\{d_{{\tt G}}(v,v_{1})+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}d_{{\tt G}}(v_{i},v_{i+1})+d_{{\tt G}}(v_{k-1},w)\mid

\displaystyle\{v,v_{1}\},\{v_{k-1},w\},\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\in E({\tt G}),i=1,\dots,k-2\}

for
v,w
vertices of
{\tt G}
. If the graph is not connected, we set
d_{{\tt G}}(v,w)\coloneqq\infty
for every
v,w\in{\tt G}
not connected by any path; hence, for non-connected graphs,
d_{{\tt G}}
is an extended metric. For a
(k+1)
-tuple
(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
of vertices of
{\tt G}
, with
v_{i}\neq v_{i+1}
and
d_{{\tt G}}(v_{i},v_{i+1})<\infty
for each
i
, the
length
of
(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
in
{\tt G}
is the number

\ell(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}d_{{\tt G}}(v_{i},v_{i+1})\ .

We recall the definition of magnitude chain groups. Let
R
be a commutative unital ring.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\tt G}
be a graph, and
l,k\in\mathbb{N}
natural numbers. We let

\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)\coloneqq R\langle(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})\mid v_{0}\neq\dots\neq v_{k},\ell(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})=l\rangle

be the free
R
-module on the paths of length
l
on
k+1
vertices of
{\tt G}
.
The differential

\partial\colon\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)\to\MC_{k-1,l}({\tt G};R)

is defined on
(k+1)
-uples
(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
by

\partial(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i}(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})\ ,

where
\partial_{i}(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})=(v_{0},\dots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},\dots,v_{k})
assuming that
\ell(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})=l=\ell(v_{0},\dots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},\dots,v_{k})
, and
\partial_{i}
is set to be
0
otherwise.

For a given
l\in\mathbb{N}
, the pair
(\MC_{*,l}({\tt G};R),\partial)
is a chain complex by
[
HW17
, Lemma 2.11]
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
HW17
, Definition 2.4]
)
.

The
magnitude homology

\MH_{*,*}({\tt G};R)
of
{\tt G}
is defined as the bigraded
R
-module
\bigoplus_{k,l\geq 0}\MH_{k,l}({\tt G};R)
, where

\MH_{k,l}({\tt G};R)\coloneqq\mathrm{H}_{k}(\MC_{*,l}({\tt G};R),\partial)

is given by the homology of the magnitude chain complex.

We can reinterpret the definition of magnitude homology groups as follows – see also
[
Asa22
, Section 2]
for the case of directed graphs.
Given a non negative integer
k
, define
\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)
as the
R
-module freely generated by the
(k+1)
-uples of vertices of
{\tt G}
, that is

\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)\coloneqq R\langle(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})\mid v_{i}\in V({\tt G})\rangle\ .

Consider the submodule

I_{k}({\tt G};R)\coloneqq R\langle(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})\mid v_{i}=v_{i+1}\text{ for some }i\rangle

of
\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)
, where
I_{0}({\tt G})
is set to
0
. Note that these modules can be equipped with a differential
{\rm d}
, defined analogously to
\partial
,
which makes them
chain complexes. Since
I_{k}({\tt G};R)\subseteq\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)
, we can form the quotient chain complex with modules
R_{k}({\tt G};R)\coloneqq\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)/I_{k}({\tt G};R)
. Then, confining ourselves to the setting of undirected graphs, we get that the
R
-module

\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)
can also be defined as the submodule of
R_{k}({\tt G};R)
given by
(k+1)
-uples of vertices whose length is precisely
l
;
this is compatible with the chain complex structure –
cf.

[
Asa22
, Lemma 2.14]
. In particular, we get the following:

Remark 2.3
.

The magnitude chain modules
\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)
are sub-quotients of the free
R
-module
\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)
.

It is possible to modify the differential
\delta
so to get a new differential

\delta^{\prime}\colon\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)\to\MC_{k-1,l-1}({\tt G};R)\ ,

in turn inducing an homomorphism
\partial^{\prime}\colon\MH_{k,l}({\tt G};R)\to\MH_{k-1,l-1}({\tt G};R)
between the magnitude homology groups. Equipped with this new differential, also magnitude homology can be seen as a chain complex
(\MH_{k-*,l-*}({\tt G};R),\partial^{\prime})
. The homology of the resulting chain complex was denoted by
\mathcal{MH}_{k-*}^{l-*}({\tt G})
in
[
Asa22
, Definition 2.21]
.

Remark 2.4

(
[
Asa22
, Proposition 6.11]
)
.

For
k=l
, the homology theory
\mathcal{MH}_{k-*}^{l-*}({\tt G})
, for directed graphs, recovers the (reduced) path homology of directed graphs introduced in
[
GLMY20
]
. The same proof, in the undirected setting, produces an isomorphism of
\mathcal{MH}_{k-*}^{l-*}({\tt G})
for undirected graphs, with the reduced path homology of graphs – see, e.g.
[
BGJW19
, Section 2.2]
for the definition.

Magnitude homology is an homology theory of (directed) graphs, and it is functorial with respect to contractions. Recall that a contraction of a graph
{\tt G}
with respect to an edge
e
is the graph obtained from
{\tt G}
by contracting
e
to a point. More specifically, if
{\tt G}
and
{\tt H}
are graphs, consider maps
\phi\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt H}
of vertices that preserve or contract each edge of
{\tt G}
. Observe that such maps do not increase the length of tuples of vertices of
{\tt G}
: that is we have

\ell(\phi(v_{0}),\dots,\phi(v_{k}))\leq\ell(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
.
Every contraction
\phi\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt H}
of graphs induces a chain map

\phi_{\#}\colon\MC_{*,*}({\tt G};R)\to\MC_{*,*}({\tt H};R)

which to a tuple
(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
of
{\tt G}
associates the tuple
(\phi(v_{0}),\dots,\phi(v_{k}))
if the length
\ell(\phi(v_{0}),\dots,\phi(v_{k}))
equals the length
\ell(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
, and it is set to be
0
otherwise. The map
\phi_{\#}
is a chain map, as it commutes with the differential
\delta
, and it induces a map
in magnitude homology. Recall that we denote by
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}
the category of graphs and contractions.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
HW17
, Proposition 3.3]
)
.

Magnitude homology is a functor

\MH_{*,*}\colon\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}\to\mathbf{BiGrMod}_{R}

from the contraction category of graphs to the category of bigraded
R
-modules.

By dualising the definition of magnitude homology, as customary, we get the definition of magnitude cohomology – see
[
Hep22
]
– which we now recall:

Definition 2.6
.

Magnitude cohomology

{\rm MH}^{*}_{*}
is the cohomology of the complex

{\rm MC}_{l}^{k}({\tt G};R)={\rm Hom}({\rm MC}_{l,k}({\tt G};R);R)\ ,

equipped with the dual differential.

This defines a functor with respect to the dual maps inducing functoriality in magnitude cohomology –
cf.

[
Hep22
, Definition 2.2]
.
In particular, the dualisation defines a functor

\MH_{*}^{*}\colon{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}\to\mathbf{BiGrMod}_{R}\ .

We recall that in
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}
there is a morphism
{\tt G}\to{\tt G}^{\prime}
if, and only if, the graph
{\tt G}
is obtained from the graph
{\tt G}^{\prime}
by a sequence of contractions. We conclude the section observing that magnitude homology and magnitude cohomology are related by a universal coefficients short exact sequence by
[
Hep22
, Remark 2.5]
. Thanks to this short exact sequence, results on magnitude homology of graphs can be derived from results on magnitude cohomology, as customary. In the next subsections, we will restrict ourselves to the case of magnitude cohomology.

2.2.
Magnitude cohomology is finitely generated

In order to prove that magnitude cohomology (in a fixed
k
-degree) is finitely generated, we exhibit a finitely generated module such that magnitude cohomology is a subquotient of it.

Recall that
\mathbf{Graph}
denotes the category of graphs and minor morphisms and let
R
be a commutative unital Noetherian ring.

Definition 2.7
.

The
vertex module
is the functor

\mathcal{V}\colon{\bf Graph}\to R\text{-{\bf Mod}}
,
which assigns to each graph
{\tt G}
the
R
-module

\mathcal{V}({{\tt G}})=R\left\langle v\mid v\in V({\tt G})\right\rangle

freely generated by the vertices of
{\tt G}
.
To each minor morphism
\phi\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}^{\prime}
, that is if
{\tt G}^{\prime}
is obtained from
{\tt G}
via contractions and deletions, it assigns the map
\mathcal{V}(\phi)\colon\mathcal{V}({{\tt G}}){\longrightarrow}\mathcal{V}({{\tt G}}^{\prime})
given by
{v}\mapsto{\phi(v)}
.

Consider the restriction of the module
\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}
to the category
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Graph}
of graphs and contractions. Denote by
\MH_{k,*}(-;R)
the sum
\bigoplus_{l}\MH_{k,l}(-;R)
.

Lemma 2.8
.

For each
k
, the magnitude homology
\MH_{k,*}(-;R)
is a sub-quotient of the module
\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k+1}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to notice that
\Lambda_{k}({\tt G})
can be identified with
\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k+1}({\tt G})
, by identifying
(v_{0},...,v_{k})
with the elementary tensor
v_{0}\otimes...\otimes v_{k}
.
The maps which induce the functoriality in magnitude homology are induced by the identification of the vertices of the contracted edges.
Taking the directed sum over all lengths
l
, we get the result.
∎

Magnitude cohomology
{\rm MH}^{*}_{*}
is by definition the cohomology of the dual complex of the magnitude homology of graphs. If a module
M
is a subquotient of a module
N
, then it is not generally true that
{\rm Hom}(M,R)
is a subquotient of
{\rm Hom}(N,R)
. However, this is the case for magnitude cohomology; in fact, we have the following:

Proposition 2.9
.

For each non-negative integer
k
, magnitude cohomology
\MH^{k}_{*}(-;R)=\bigoplus_{l}\MH^{k}_{l}(-;R)
is a sub-quotient of
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k+1},R)
.

Proof.

Fix
l
a natural number and consider the magnitude chain group
\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)
of a graph
{\tt G}
. This is a sub-quotient of
\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k+1}({\tt G})
by Lemma
2.8
. Observe that the modules
\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R),I_{k}({\tt G};R)
and
R_{k}({\tt G};R)
are free
R
-modules. Thus,

{\rm Hom}_{R}(R_{k}({\tt G};R),R)
can be seen as a submodule of
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k+1}({\tt G};R),R)
– since
\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)\cong\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k+1}({\tt G})
canonically. By definition,
R_{k}({\tt G};R)\coloneqq\Lambda_{k}({\tt G};R)/I_{k}({\tt G};R)
consists of tuples of subsequently distinct elements of
V({\tt G})
, and
\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)
is given by the tuples
(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
in
R_{k}({\tt G};R)
of length
l
. Using the parameter
l
, we can filter the free module
R_{k}({\tt G};R)
. Using such filtration, also the magnitude homology groups
\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)
are spanned by elements of a basis of
R_{k}({\tt G};R)
.

We have a short exact sequence

(1)

0\to\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R)\to R_{k}({\tt G};R)\to Q_{k}({\tt G};R)\to 0

where
Q_{k}({\tt G};R)
is the associated quotient, which is also a free
R
-module.
In particular, the group
\Ext^{i}_{R}(Q_{k}({\tt G};R),M)
are zero for all
i\geq 1
and
R
-module
M
. Hence, dualising the short exact sequence in Eq. (
1
), we get the short exact sequence

0\to{\Hom}_{R}(Q_{k}({\tt G};R),R)\to{\rm Hom}_{R}(R_{k}({\tt G};R),R)\to\underbrace{{\rm Hom}_{R}(\MC_{k,l}({\tt G};R),R)}_{=\MC^{k}_{l}({\tt G};R))}\to 0

from which the statement follows.
∎

Our goal is now to show that the module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k},R)
is finitely generated, for all
k>0
. As we do not know whether the full category of graphs and minor morphisms, or simply contractions, is Noetherian, we shall restrict to the subcategory of graphs with bounded genus.

For any fixed graph
{\tt G}
the
R
-module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G}),R)
is freely generated (as
R
-module) by the functions

\delta^{{\tt G}}_{v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}}\colon\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G})\longrightarrow R
,
defined by

\delta^{{\tt G}}_{v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}}(w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{k})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }w_{i}=v_{i}\text{ for all }i,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Denote by

M_{k}({\tt G})={\rm span}_{R}(\psi_{c}(\delta^{{\tt G}^{\prime}}_{w_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{k}})\mid c\in{\rm Hom}_{{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}}({\tt G}^{\prime},{\tt G}),{\tt G}^{\prime}\neq{\tt G},w_{i}\in V({\tt G}^{\prime})\ \forall i)\ ,

where
\psi_{c}\colon{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G}^{\prime}),R)\to{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G}),R)
denotes the map induced by the contraction
c\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}^{\prime}
. More precisely,
\psi_{c}
is just the pre-composition with the map
\mathcal{V}(c)\colon\mathcal{V}({\tt G})\to\mathcal{V}({\tt G}^{\prime})
.
We start by proving the following:

Proposition 2.10
.

Given a connected graph
{\tt G}
with more than
k+1
vertices, we have that

{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G}),R)=M_{k}({\tt G})
.

Proof.

Pick
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in V({\tt G})

and indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
such that the vertices
v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{n}}
are distinct and
\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{n}}\}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
.
First, we need to establish some notation. Set
I_{j}\coloneqq\{t\mid v_{t}=v_{i_{j}}\}
, for
j=1,\dots,n
, and

a_{i}=a_{i}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=|\{j\mid|I_{j}|=i\}|\ ,

that is
a_{i}
is the number of
v_{i_{j}}
s which appear exactly
i
times among
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
.
Set also

l=l(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=\min\{d_{{\tt G}}(v,w)\mid v,w\in V({\tt G})\setminus\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\}\ ,

with
d_{{\tt G}}
is the shortest path metric.
With this notation in place, we can associate to each
k
-tuple
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
an element
\chi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1}
by setting

\chi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\coloneqq(a_{k},\dots,a_{1},l)
.
The set of all possible values of
\chi
is totally ordered by the lexicographic order
<_{\rm lex}
.
We shall prove by recursion on the values of
\chi
that

(2)

\delta^{{\tt G}}_{v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}}\in M_{k}({\tt G})\ ,

for all choices of
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in V({\tt G})
. As
M_{k}({\tt G})
is by definition a submodule of the
R
-module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G}),R)
, the statement will follow.

First, we prove the claim for all
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
such that

\chi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=(0,\dots,0,k,1)
,
which is the minimum possible value of
\chi
.
In this case, there are two vertices
v,w\in V({\tt G})\setminus\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}

which are the endpoints of an edge
e
. Then, if
c
denotes the contraction of
e
, we have

\psi_{c}(\delta^{{\tt G}^{\prime}}_{\overline{v}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\overline{v}_{k}})=\delta^{{\tt G}}_{v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{k}}\ ,

where
\overline{v}_{k}\coloneqq c(v_{k})
.
This proves (
2
) in this case.

We recursively assume that (
2
) holds for all
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
such that

\chi(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})<_{\rm lex}\chi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
.
Let
v,w\in V({\tt G})\setminus\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
be such that
d_{{\tt G}}(v,w)=l(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
. We have either
d_{{\tt G}}(v,w)=1
or
d_{{\tt G}}(v,w)=l>1
. In the former case, with the same reasoning used for the minimal values of
\chi
, we get that (
2
) holds. Thus, we can assume
l>1
. In this case, any minimal path between
v
and
w
contains only
v_{i}
s. Let
v_{i_{s}}
be the first vertex after
v
on such a minimal path.
Then, if
c
denotes the contraction of an edge between
v
and
v_{i_{s}}
, by definition of
\psi_{c}
we have

(3)

\psi_{c}(\delta^{{\tt G}^{\prime}}_{\overline{v}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\overline{v}_{k}})=\sum_{S\subseteq I_{s}}\delta^{{\tt G}}_{x^{S}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x^{S}_{k}}\ ,

where
x^{S}_{j}
equals
v
if
j\in S
, and
v_{j}
otherwise.
Clearly,
x^{\emptyset}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x^{\emptyset}_{k}=v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{k}
.
Therefore we can rewrite (
3
) as:

(4)

\delta^{{\tt G}}_{v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{k}}=\psi_{c}(\delta^{{\tt G}^{\prime}}_{\overline{v}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\overline{v}_{k}})-\sum_{\tiny\begin{matrix}S\subseteq I_{s}\\
S\neq\emptyset\end{matrix}}\delta^{{\tt G}}_{x^{S}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x^{S}_{k}}\ .

We now claim that for all
S\subseteq I_{s}
non-empty

\chi(x^{S}_{1},\dots,x^{S}_{k})<_{\rm lex}\chi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
.
If
S\neq\emptyset,I_{s}
, then
a_{i}(x^{S}_{1},\dots,x^{S}_{k})=a_{i}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
for
i>|I_{s}|
, since all
v_{j}
s but
v_{i_{s}}
appear with the same multiplicity – and
v
and
v_{i_{s}}
appear with multiplicity at most
|I_{s}|
. On the other hand,
a_{|I_{s}|}(x^{S}_{1},\dots,x^{S}_{k})<a_{|I_{s}|}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
because
v_{i_{s}}
appears now
|I_{s}\setminus S|
times and
v
appears
|S|
times.
If
S=I_{s}
, then the
a_{i}
s are unchanged (since
v_{i_{s}}
does not appear anymore, and
v
appears
|I_{s}|
times). Nonetheless, we have

l(x^{I_{s}}_{1},\dots,x^{I_{s}}_{k})\leq d_{{\tt G}}(v_{i_{s}},w)=d_{{\tt G}}(v,w)-1=l(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})-1<l(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\ ,

and thus
\chi(x^{S}_{1},\dots,x^{S}_{k})<_{\rm lex}\chi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
, as claimed.
Therefore, by recursive hypothesis we get

\sum_{\tiny\begin{matrix}S\subseteq I_{s}\\
S\neq\emptyset\end{matrix}}\delta^{{\tt G}}_{x^{S}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x^{S}_{k}}\in M_{k}({\tt G})\ .

Thus, from (
4
), we obtain that (
2
) also holds for
l>1
, concluding the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this section. Fix an integer
k\geq 1
.

Theorem 2.11
.

The
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k},R)
is finitely generated. Moreover, a set of generators is given by all graphs of genus at most
g
with at most
k+1
vertices.

Proof.

Let
N_{k}
be the sub-module of the
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}(-);R)
spanned by the set
S=\{\delta^{{\tt G}}_{v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}}\}
, where
{\tt G}
ranges among all graphs of genus at most
g
with at most
k+1
vertices, and
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
range among the vertices of
{\tt G}
. These graphs are a finite number since the genus is bounded, and hence
S
is a finite set.

Clearly,
N_{k}({\tt G})={\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G});R)
for all graphs with at most
k+1
vertices.

We proceed by induction on the number of vertices to cover the remaining cases.
Suppose that, whenever
{\tt G}^{\prime}
has less than
r>k+1
vertices
N_{k}({\tt G}^{\prime})={\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G}^{\prime});R)
. Then, by Proposition
2.10
, every
\phi\in{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k}({\tt G});R)
can be written as

\phi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\psi_{c_{i}}(\delta^{{\tt G}_{i}}_{v^{i}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{i}_{k}})\ ,

for some contractions
c_{i}\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}_{i}
, and some
v^{i}_{1},\dots,v^{i}_{k}\in V({\tt G}_{i})
. Since the
c_{i}
s are contractions,
{\tt G}_{i}
has at most
r-1
vertices for each
i
. Hence,
\delta^{{\tt G}_{i}}_{v^{i}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{i}_{k}}\in N_{k}({\tt G}_{i})
by inductive hypothesis.
Since
\psi_{c_{i}}(N_{k}({\tt G}_{i}))\subseteq N_{k}({\tt G})
for each
i
, the first part of the statement follows. To conclude, note that the generators of the module
N_{k}
are all graphs of genus at most
g
with at most
k+1
vertices, in view of Lemma
1.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

The
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-module
{\rm MH}^{k}_{l}(-;R)
is finitely generated.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
1.8
, sub-quotients of finitely generated
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-modules are finitely generated.
By Theorem
2.11
, the
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes k+1},R)
is finitely generated. Therefore, the statement follows from Proposition
2.9
.
∎

3.
Applications

A first consequence of the finite generation property described in Corollary
2.12
is a bound on the magnitude (co)homology ranks that depends on the number of edges of the considered graphs.
Our first corollary is a general result for graphs of bounded genus, and the second is related to subsequent subdivisions of the edges.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field, and
g\geq 0
. Then, there exists a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
of degree at most
g+k+1

{\tt G}
of genus at most
g

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\MH_{*}^{k}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})\leq f(\#E({\tt G}))\ ,

where
\#E({\tt G})
is the number of edges of
{\tt G}
.

Proof.

The statement directly follows from
[
PR22
, Proposition 4.3]
after noticing that magnitude cohomology, in cohomological degree
k
and independently on the length degree
l
, is a subquotient of a module which is finitely generated in degree
\leq g+k+1
– since it is generated by graphs of genus
\leq g
and at most
k+2
vertices, cf. Theorem
2.11
.
∎

Let
{\tt G}
be a graph of genus
g
,
\underline{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})

{\tt G}

{\tt G}(\underline{e},\underline{m})
be the graph obtained from
{\tt G}
by subdividing each edge
e_{i}

m_{i}=0

e_{i}

\mathbf{OI}
the category of linearly ordered finite sets and ordered inclusions. Consider the product category
\mathbf{OI}^{r}

e_{i}
have been chosen in order to construct a subdivision functor

\Phi_{{\tt G},\underline{e}}\colon\mathbf{OI}^{r}\to{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}

which associates to a linearly ordered set
[\underline{m}]\in\mathbf{OI}^{r}
the graph
{\tt G}(\underline{e},\underline{m})

[\underline{m}]\to[\underline{n}]

\mathcal{M}({\tt G}(\underline{e},\underline{m}))
is bounded by a polynomial in
\underline{m}
of degree
\leq d
–
c.f.

[
PR22

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and
{\tt G}

A second application, of main interest to us, concerns the behaviour of torsion in magnitude (co)homology.
It was shown in
[
SS21
, Theorem 3.14]
that any finitely generated Abelian group may appear as a subgroup of the
magnitude homology of a graph, and that there are infinitely many
such graphs. More precisely, Sadzanovic and Summers proved the following:

Theorem 3.4

(
[
SS21
, Theorem 3.13]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime and
n,m\geq 1
integers. There exist infinitely many distinct
isomorphism classes of graphs whose magnitude homology contains
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
torsion in bigrading
(3,2n+3)
.

The proof of
[
SS21
, Theorem 3.13]
is based on Kaneta-Yoshinaga construction
[
KY21
]
. Graphs whose integral magnitude homology has
{p^{r}}
-torsion are obtained from triangulations of (generalised) lens spaces and iterated subdivisions.
However, there is no structural theorem concerning the complexity of graphs having given torsion in integral magnitude homology.
The next result suggests that, in order to find more torsion in magnitude (co)homology, one needs to increase the combinatorial complexity of the graphs.

Theorem 3.5
.

For every pair of integers
k,g\geq 0
, there exists
m=m(g,k)\in\mathbb{Z}
which annihilates the torsion subgroup of
\MH^{k}_{*}({\tt G};\mathbb{Z})
, for each graph
{\tt G}
of genus at most
g
.

Proof.

First, note that magnitude homology and magnitude cohomology are related by a universal coefficients short exact sequence by
[
Hep22
, Remark 2.5]
, hence we can restrict to magnitude cohomology (and results for magnitude homology will be derived by application of such short exact sequence).

Fix a degree
k
, and take
R=\mathbb{Z}
the integers. By Corollary
2.12
, the
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-module
\MH^{k}_{l}(-;\mathbb{Z})
is finitely generated. Let
{\tt G}
be a graph of bounded genus
\leq g
, and
\tau
a torsion class in
\MH^{k}_{l}({\tt G};\mathbb{Z})
. For
\phi\colon{\tt H}\to{\tt G}
a contraction of graphs, we get by functoriality a map in magnitude cohomology that preserves the torsion class. Therefore, we can consider the submodule
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\MH^{k}_{l}(-;\mathbb{Z})
which sends a graph
{\tt G}
to the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\MH^{k}_{l}({\tt G};\mathbb{Z})
. Then, by Corollary
1.8
, the
{\bf CGraph}^{{\rm op}}_{\leq g}
-module
\mathcal{T}
is also finitely generated. But, by definition, this means that there exist graphs

of genus bounded by
g

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m(k)}\mathcal{P}_{{\tt G}_{i}}\to\mathcal{T}
from the associated principal projectives. Now, choose

{\tt G}

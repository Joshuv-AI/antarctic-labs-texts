Projectively flat foliations
Stéphane Druel

Abstract
. We describe the structure of regular codimension 1 1 foliations with numerically projectively flat tangent bundle on complex projective manifolds of dimension at least 4 4 . Along the way, we prove that either the normal bundle of a regular codimension 1 1 foliation is pseudo-effective, or its conormal bundle is nef.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
37F75, 32Q30, 32Q26, 14E20, 14E30, 53B10

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective algebraic variety over the field of complex numbers, of dimension
n\geqslant 2
. If
T_{X}
is numerically flat, then
X
is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety as a classical consequence of Yau’s theorem on the existence of a Kähler-Einstein metric. In
[
JR13
]
, Jahnke and Radloff proved that if the normalized tangent bundle
\textup{S}^{n}T_{X}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(K_{X})
is numerically flat, then
K_{X}\equiv 0
, and hence
T_{X}
is numerically flat. Thus
X
is again a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety. Let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geqslant 1
on
X
. Recall from
[
JR13
, Theorem 1.1]
that the normalized vector bundle
\textup{S}^{n}\mathscr{E}\otimes\det(\mathscr{E}^{*})
is numerically flat if and only if
\mathscr{E}
is semistable with respect to some ample divisor
H
on
X
and equality holds in the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality,

c_{2}(\mathscr{E})\cdot H^{n-2}=\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1}(\mathscr{E})^{2}\cdot H^{n-2}.

The aim of this paper is to generalize the theorem of Jahnke and Radloff to codimension
1
regular foliations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 4
, and let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation. Suppose that the normalized vector bundle
\textup{S}^{n-1}\mathscr{F}\otimes\det(\mathscr{F}^{*})
is numerically flat. Then one of the following holds.

(1)

There exists a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle structure
\varphi\colon X\to Y
onto a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety, and
\mathscr{F}
induces a flat Ehresmann connection on
\varphi
.

(2)

There exists an abelian variety
A
as well as a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon A\to X
such that

\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is a linear foliation.

(3)

There exists an abelian scheme
f\colon B\to C
onto a smooth complete curve of genus at least
2
as well as a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon B\to X
such that
\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is induced by
f
.

(4)

The divisor
K_{X}
is ample and
\kappa(K_{\mathscr{F}})=\dim X
. Moreover, the vector bundles
\mathscr{N}^{*}
and
\Omega_{X}^{1}
are nef.

Notice that no example of foliations satisfying the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
(4) is known to the author. In addition, if we relax the condition on the dimension of
X
then the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
may be false (see
[
Bru97
]
).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
makes use of the following result, which might be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 1
, and let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation with normal bundle
\mathscr{N}
. Then, either
\mathscr{N}
is pseudo-effective or
\mathscr{N}^{*}
is nef.

In fact, a more general statement is true (see Theorem
4.4
).

Previous results

Greb, Kebekus, and Peternell generalized the theorem of Jahnke and Radloff to projective varieties with klt singularities in
[
GKP21
]
. More precisely, they proved that torus quotients are the only klt varieties with semistable tangent sheaf and extremal Chern classes. In
[
Dru23
]
, the structure of projective log smooth pairs with numerically flat normalized logarithmic tangent bundle is described. Passing to an appropriate finite cover and up to isomorphism, these are projective spaces and log smooth pairs with numerically flat logarithmic tangent bundles blown-up at finitely many points away from the boundary. In addition, it has been shown in
[
DLB21
]
that log smooth pairs with numerically flat logarithmic tangent bundle are toric fiber bundles over finite étale quotients of abelian varieties.

Structure of the paper

Section
2
gathers notation, known results and global conventions that will be used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we provide technical tools for the proof of our main results. Section
4
is mostly taken up by the proof of Theorem
1.2
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is long and therefore subdivided into numerous steps: Sections
5
to
9
prepare for it. In section
5
, we describe regular codimension
1
foliations with numerically projectively flat tangent bundle on non-minimal complex projective manifolds.
Next, we prove abundance for
K_{X}
in Section
6
following the strategy employed in
[
JR13
]
: we use a Shafarevich map construction to prove that it reduces to the special case where
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}^{\oplus n}
for some line bundle
\mathscr{L}
.
In Section
7
, we address regular codimension
1
foliations with numerically projectively flat tangent bundle and nef conormal bundle on minimal varieties.
Section
9
describes regular codimension
1
foliations with numerically projectively flat tangent bundle and pseudo-effective normal bundle still on minimal varieties. To this end, we show in Section
8
that the nef reduction map for
K_{\mathscr{F}}
is an abelian scheme up to a finite étale cover. With these preparations at hand, the proof of Theorem
1.1
which we give in Section
10
becomes short.

2.
Notation, conventions, and used facts

2.1.
Global conventions

Throughout the paper, all varieties are assumed to be defined over the field of complex numbers. We will freely switch between the algebraic and analytic context.

2.2.
Projective space bundles

If
\mathscr{E}
is a locally free sheaf of finite rank on a variety
X
,
we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathscr{E})
the variety
\textup{Proj}_{X}(\textup{S}^{\bullet}\mathscr{E})
.

2.3.
Stability

The word
stable
will always mean
slope-stable with respect to a
given ample divisor
. Ditto for
semistable
and
polystable
.
We refer to
[
HL97
, Definition 1.2.12]
for their precise definitions.

2.4.
Numerically flat vector bundles

For the reader’s convenience, we recall the definition of numerically flat vector bundles following
[
DPS94
, Definition 1.17]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
of rank
r\geqslant 1
on a smooth projective variety is called
numerically flat
if
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{*}
are nef vector bundes.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geqslant 1
on
X
with
c_{1}(\mathscr{E})\equiv 0
. Then

\mathscr{E}
is numerically flat if and only if
\mathscr{E}
is nef.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geqslant 1
on
X
. Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
is numerically flat.

(2)

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
is flat and semistable with respect to
H
.

(3)

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
admits a filtration

\{0\}=\mathscr{E}_{0}\subset\mathscr{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathscr{E}_{m}=\mathscr{E}

by vector subbundles such that the quotients
\mathscr{Q}_{k}:=\mathscr{E}_{k}/\mathscr{E}_{k-1}
are hermitian flat.

Proof.

This follows easily from
[
DPS94
, Theorem 1.18]
and
[
Sim92
]
together (see
[
Dru23
, Theorem 2.9]
).
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Setting and notation as in Theorem
2.3
. If
\mathscr{E}
is numerically flat, then it is semistable with respect to any ample divisor.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
DPS94
, Corollary 1.19]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle on a smooth projective variety. If
\mathscr{E}
is numerically flat, then
c_{i}(\mathscr{E})\equiv 0
for any integer
i\geqslant 1
.

2.5.
Projectively flat and numerically projectively flat vector bundles

Next, we recall the definition of projectively flat vector bundles.

Definition 2.6
.

A vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
of rank
r\geqslant 1
on a smooth projective variety
X
is called
projectively flat
if
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})
comes from a
representation
\pi_{1}(X)\to\textup{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
JR13
, Proof of Proposition 1.1 (2)]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle on a smooth projective variety. If
\mathscr{E}
is projectively flat, then
c_{i}(\mathscr{E})\equiv\frac{1}{r^{i}}\binom{r}{i}c_{1}(\mathscr{E})^{i}
for any integer
i\geqslant 1
.

One key notion is that of a numerically projectively flat vector bundle. We recall the definition.

Definition 2.8
.

A vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
of rank
r\geqslant 1
on a smooth projective variety
X
is called
numerically projectively flat
if
\textup{S}^{r}\mathscr{E}\otimes\det\mathscr{E}^{*}
is numerically flat.

Remark 2.9
.

Setting and notation as in Definition
2.8
. Then
\mathscr{E}
is numerically projectively flat if and only if
\textup{S}^{r}\mathscr{E}\otimes\det\mathscr{E}^{*}
is nef since

\det(\textup{S}^{r}\mathscr{E}\otimes\det\mathscr{E}^{*})\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
(see Remark
2.2
).

Remark 2.10
.

Setting and notation as in Definition
2.8
. Then
\mathscr{E}
is numerically flat if and only if
\mathscr{E}
is numerically projectively flat and
c_{1}(\mathscr{E})\equiv 0
.

We will need the following characterization of numerically projectively flat vector bundles.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\geqslant 2
and let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geqslant 1
on
X
. Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
is numerically projectively flat.

(2)

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
is projectively flat and semistable with respect to
H
.

(3)

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
admits a filtration

\{0\}=\mathscr{E}_{0}\subset\mathscr{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathscr{E}_{m}=\mathscr{E}

by vector subbundles such that the quotients
\mathscr{Q}_{k}:=\mathscr{E}_{k}/\mathscr{E}_{k-1}
are hermitian projectively flat with

\frac{1}{\textup{rank}\,\mathscr{Q}_{k}}c_{1}(\mathscr{Q}_{k})\equiv\frac{1}{r}c_{1}(\mathscr{E}).

(4)

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
is semistable with respect to
H
and equality holds in the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality,

c_{2}(\mathscr{E})\cdot H^{n-2}=\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1}(\mathscr{E})^{2}\cdot H^{n-2}.

(5)

For any morphism
C\to X
from a smooth complete curve
C
, the vector bundle
\mathscr{E}|_{C}
is semistable.

Proof.

This follows easily from
[
JR13
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
JR13
, Proposition 1.1]
together (see also
[
Dru23
, Theorem 2.13]
).
∎

Remark 2.12
.

Setting and notation as in Theorem
2.11
. If
\mathscr{E}
is numerically projectively flat, then it is semistable with respect to any ample divisor.

Example 2.13
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle on a smooth projective variety
X
. Then
\mathscr{L}^{\oplus m}
is numerically projectively flat for any integer
m\geqslant 1
.

The following is an easy consequence of Theorem
2.11
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\geqslant 1
, and let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geqslant 1
on
X
.

(1)

If
\mathscr{E}
is numerically projectively flat, then
\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{L}
is numerically projectively flat as well for any line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
X
.

(2)

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety and let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism. If
\mathscr{E}
is numerically projectively flat, then
f^{*}\mathscr{E}
is also numerically projectively flat. In addition, the converse statement is true if
f
is surjective.

(3)

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a vector bundle on
X
of rank
s\geqslant 1
. Then
\mathscr{E}\oplus\mathscr{G}
is numerically projectively flat if and only if
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{G}
are numerically projectively flat and

\frac{1}{r}c_{1}(\mathscr{E})\equiv\frac{1}{s}c_{1}(\mathscr{G})\equiv\frac{1}{r+s}c_{1}(\mathscr{E}\oplus\mathscr{G}).

We will need the following easy observation.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth projective morphism with connected fibers between smooth algebraic varieties, and let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geqslant 1
on
X
. Suppose that the restriction of
\mathscr{E}
to every fiber of
f
is numerically projectively flat.
Suppose in addition that
\mathscr{E}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus r}
, where
F
is a general fiber of
f
. Then there exists a vector bundle
\mathscr{G}
on
Y
such that
\mathscr{E}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{G}
.

Proof.

Let
F
be any fiber of
f
. Because the function

y\mapsto h^{0}(X_{y},\mathscr{E}|_{X_{y}})
are upper semicontinuous in the Zariski topology on
Y
(see
[
Har77
, Theorem 12.8]
), we have

h^{0}(F,\mathscr{E}|_{F})\geqslant r
.
By
[
DPS94
, Proposition 1.16]
, the evaluation map
H^{0}(F,\mathscr{E}|_{F})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{F}\to\mathscr{E}|_{F}
is an injective morphism of vector bundles. This implies that
\mathscr{E}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus r}
. By
[
Har77
, Corollary 12.9]
together with the base change theorem
[
Har77
, Theorem 12.11]
, the sheaf
\mathscr{G}:=f_{*}\mathscr{E}
is locally free of rank
r
and the formation of
f_{*}\mathscr{E}
commutes with arbitrary base change. This easily implies that
\mathscr{E}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{G}
.
∎

2.6.
Foliation

In this section, we have gathered a number of results and facts concerning foliations which will later be used
in the proofs.

Definition 2.16
.

A
foliation
on a smooth variety
X
is a coherent subsheaf
\mathscr{F}\subseteq T_{X}
such that

(1)

\mathscr{F}
is closed under the Lie bracket, and

(2)

\mathscr{F}
is saturated in
T_{X}
. In other words, the quotient
T_{X}/\mathscr{F}
is torsion-free.

The
rank

r
of
\mathscr{F}
is the generic rank of
\mathscr{F}
.
The
codimension
of
\mathscr{F}
is defined as
q:=\dim X-r
.

The
canonical class

K_{\mathscr{F}}
of
\mathscr{F}
is any Weil divisor on
X
such that

\mathscr{O}_{X}(-K_{\mathscr{F}})\cong\det\mathscr{F}
.

Let
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
be the open set where
\mathscr{F}
is a subbundle of
T_{X}
. The foliation is called
regular
if
X^{\circ}=X
.

A
leaf
of
\mathscr{F}
is a maximal connected and immersed holomorphic submanifold
L\subseteq X^{\circ}
such that

T_{L}=\mathscr{F}_{|L}
.

The
normal sheaf
of
\mathscr{F}
is
\mathscr{N}:=(T_{X}/\mathscr{F})^{**}
.
The
q
-th wedge product of the inclusion

\mathscr{N}^{*}\subseteq\Omega^{1}_{X}
gives rise to a non-zero global section
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{q}_{X}\otimes\det\mathscr{N})
whose zero locus has codimension at least
2
in
X
.
Moreover,
\omega
is
locally decomposable
and
integrable
.
To say that
\omega
is locally decomposable means that,
in a neighborhood of a general point of
X
,
\omega
decomposes as the wedge product of
q
local
1
-forms

\omega=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{q}
.
To say that it is integrable means that for this local decomposition one has

d\omega_{i}\wedge\omega=0
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
.
The integrability condition for
\omega
is equivalent to the condition that
\mathscr{F}

is closed under the Lie bracket. Conversely, let
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle on
X
, and let

\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{q}_{X}\otimes\mathscr{L})
be a global section
whose zero locus has codimension at least
2
in
X
.
Suppose that
\omega
is locally decomposable and integrable.
Then the kernel of the morphism
T_{X}\to\Omega^{q-1}_{X}\otimes\mathscr{L}
given by the contraction with
\omega
defines a foliation of codimension
q
on
X
.
These constructions are inverse of each other.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth complex algebraic varieties, and let
f\colon X\to Y
be a dominant morphism that restricts to a smooth morphism
f^{\circ}\colon X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
,
where
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
and
Y^{\circ}\subseteq Y
are dense open subsets.
Let
\mathscr{F}
be a foliation on
Y
.
The pull-back
f^{-1}\mathscr{F}
of
\mathscr{F}
via
f
is the foliation on
X
whose restriction to
X^{\circ}
is
(df^{\circ})^{-1}\big(\mathscr{F}_{|Y^{\circ}}\big)
.
We will need the following easy observation.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a finite étale cover of smooth projective varieties, and let
\mathscr{F}
be a regular foliation on
Y
. Then the following holds.

(1)

The foliation
f^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is regular. Moreover
f^{-1}\mathscr{F}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{F}
, and
\mathscr{N}_{f^{-1}\mathscr{F}}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{F}}
.

(2)

We have
\kappa(K_{f^{-1}\mathscr{F}})=\kappa(K_{\mathscr{F}})
, and
\nu(K_{f^{-1}\mathscr{F}})=\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement is obvious. For the second part, note that
K_{f^{-1}\mathscr{F}}=f^{*}K_{\mathscr{F}}
by (1). Then we have
\kappa(K_{f^{-1}\mathscr{F}})=\kappa(K_{\mathscr{F}})
by
[
Uen75
, Theorem 5.13]
, and
\nu(K_{f^{-1}\mathscr{F}})=\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})
by
[
Nak04
, Proposition V.2.22]
.
∎

2.7.
Ehresmann connection

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a surjective proper morphism between smooth varieties. An
Ehresmann connection
on
f
is a distribution
\mathscr{D}\subseteq T_{X}
such that the restriction of the tangent map

Tf\colon T_{X}\to f^{*}T_{Y}
to
\mathscr{D}
induces an isomorphism
\mathscr{D}\cong f^{*}T_{Y}
. The Ehresmann connection
\mathscr{D}
is said to be
flat
if
\mathscr{D}
is a foliation.

If
f
has an Ehresmann connection, then
f
is a locally trivial fibration for the analytic topology since complex flows of vector fields on analytic spaces exist.

Suppose that
f
has a flat Ehresmann connection
\mathscr{D}
. Let
F
be any fiber of
f
, and set
y:=f(F)
. Let also
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(Y,y)\to\textup{Aut}(F)
be the monodromy representation of
\mathscr{D}
. Then

X\cong(\widetilde{Y}\times F)/\pi_{1}(Y,y),

where
\widetilde{Y}
denotes the universal cover of
Y
based at
y
and
\pi_{1}(Y,y)
acts diagonally on the product.

Example 2.18
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
be an integer.
Let
A=\mathbb{C}^{n-1}/\Lambda
be a complex abelian variety, and let

\rho\colon\pi_{1}(A)\to\textup{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})
be a representation of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(A)\cong\Lambda
of
A
.
Then the group
\pi_{1}(A)
acts diagonally on
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
by

\gamma\cdot(z,p)=\big(\gamma(z),\rho(\gamma)(p)\big)
. Set
X:=(\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})/\pi_{1}(A)
,
and denote by
\psi\colon X\to A\cong\mathbb{C}^{n-1}/\pi_{1}(A)
the projection morphism, which is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle.
The foliation on
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
induced by the projection
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is invariant under the action of
\pi_{1}(A)
and gives a flat Ehresmann connection
\mathscr{F}
on
\psi
. Then
\rho
identifies with the monodromy representation of
\mathscr{F}
.

2.8.
The standard setting

Throughout the present paper, we will be working in the following setup and use the notation below.

Setup 2.19
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 3
, and let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation with normal bundle
\mathscr{N}
. Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is numerically projectively flat. Let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\textup{PGL}(n-1,\mathbb{C})
denote a representation that defines the projectively flat structure on
\mathscr{F}
.

Remark 2.20
.

Setting and notation as in Setup
2.19
. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a finite étale cover. Then the pull-back
f^{-1}\mathscr{F}
of
\mathscr{F}
on
Y
satisfies all the conditions listed in Setup
2.19
(see Lemma
2.17
).

We will need the following observation.

Lemma 2.21
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Then
\mathscr{F}^{*}
is a nef vector bundle.

Proof.

In order to prove the lemma, it suffices to show that
K_{\mathscr{F}}
is nef since
\mathscr{F}
is numerically projectively flat by assumption.
We argue by contradiction and assume that
K_{\mathscr{F}}
is not nef. Then there exists a smooth complete curve
C\to X
such that
\deg_{C}K_{\mathscr{F}}<0
. This implies that the vector bundle

\mathscr{F}|_{C}
is ample since it is semistable with positive slope by our current assumption.
By
[
BM16
, Main Theorem]
(see also
[
KSCT07
, Theorem 1]
), the leaf
F
of
\mathscr{F}
through a general point in
C
is algebraic and rationally connected. It follows that
\mathscr{F}
is induced by a morphism with connected fibers
f\colon X\to B

onto a smooth complete curve
B
. By
[
Kol96
, Theorem IV.2.10]
applied to a general fiber
F
of
f
, there exists a minimal free rational curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to F

which means that there exists an integer
0\leqslant p\leqslant n-2
such that

\mathscr{F}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong T_{F}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus p}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus n-2-p}.

Since
\mathscr{F}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is projectively flat by assumption, we must have
p=0
and
n=2
, yielding a contradiction. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Preparations for the proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we provide technical tools for the proof of our main results.

3.1.
Bott (partial) connection and applications

We briefly recall the relevant notions first. Let
X
be a complex manifold, and let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular foliation with normal bundle
\mathscr{N}
. Let
p\colon T_{X}\to\mathscr{N}
denotes the natural projection. For sections
U
of
\mathscr{N}
,
T
of
T_{X}
, and
V
of
\mathscr{F}
over some open subset of
X
with
U=p(T)
, set
D_{V}U=p([V,U])
. This expression is well defined,
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-linear in
V
, and satisfies the Leibnitz rule

D_{V}(fU)=fD_{V}U+(Vf)U
so that
D
is an
\mathscr{F}
-connection on
\mathscr{N}

(see
[
BB70
]
). We refer to it as the
Bott connection
on
\mathscr{N}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
, and let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular foliation of codimension
1\leqslant q\leqslant n-1
with normal bundle
\mathscr{N}
.

(1)

The Bott connection is flat when restricted to the leaves of
\mathscr{F}
.

(2)

The cohomology class
c_{1}(\mathscr{N})\in H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
lies in the image of the natural map

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{N}^{*})\to H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}).

In particular, we have

c_{1}(\mathscr{N})^{q+1}=0\in H^{q+1}(X,\Omega^{q+1}_{X})
.

Proof.

Item (1) follows from an easy (local) computation. The arguments of
[
BB70
, Proof of Corollary 3.4]
give a proof of Item (2).
∎

The following easy consequence of Lemma
3.1
above will prove to be crucial.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
and let
Y\subseteq X
be a compact Kähler submanifold. Let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation with normal bundle
\mathscr{N}
. Suppose that
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{n-1}
for some line bundles
\mathscr{L}_{i}

with
c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1})=\cdots=c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{n-1})\in H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
.
Then

c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{i}|_{Y})^{2}=c_{1}(\mathscr{N}|_{Y})^{2}=c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{i}|_{Y})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{N}|_{Y})\equiv 0.

Moreover, the linear subspace

L:=\langle c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1}|_{Y}),\ldots,c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{n-1}|_{Y}),c_{1}(\mathscr{N}|_{Y})\rangle\subseteq H^{1}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})

has dimension at most one.

Proof.

The second assertion follows immediately from the first using the Hodge index theorem.

Set
c:=c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1})=\cdots=c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{n-1})\in H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
. Notice that
\mathscr{L}_{i}\subset T_{X}
defines a regular foliation for any index
i\in I
. Denote by
\mathscr{N}_{i}
its normal bundle. Then Lemma
3.1
shows that

c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{i})=c_{1}(\mathscr{N})+(n-2)c\in H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
lies in the image of the natural map

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{N}^{*}_{i})\to H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}\textup{Im}\left(H^{1}(X,\mathscr{N}^{*}_{i})\to H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}\textup{Ker}\left(H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})\to H^{1}(X,\mathscr{L}^{*}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\textup{Ker}\left(H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})\to\bigoplus_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}H^{1}(X,\mathscr{L}^{*}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\textup{Ker}\left(H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})\to H^{1}(X,\mathscr{F}^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=\textup{Im}\left(H^{1}(X,\mathscr{N}^{*})\to H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})\right),

and hence
c_{1}(\mathscr{N})+(n-2)c\in H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
lies in the image of the natural map

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{N}^{*})\to H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
. By Lemma
3.1
again,
c_{1}(\mathscr{N})\in H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
lies in the image of the natural map

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{N}^{*})\to H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}).

Now, we have a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(X,\mathscr{N}^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(Y,\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{Y})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y}).}

The first assertion follows easily since
\mathscr{N}
has rank
1
by assumption.
∎

The proof of Proposition
3.4
below makes use of the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a complex projective variety, and let
\nu\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be the normalization morphism. Suppose that
\omega_{X}
is invertible. Then the following holds.

(1)

There exists an effective Weil divisor on
\widetilde{X}
such that
\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\nu^{*}\omega_{X}(-E)
.

(2)

We have
E=0
if and only if
X
is normal.

Proof.

By
[
KM98
, Proposition 5.67]
and
[
KM98
, Proposition 5.68]
together, we have
\nu_{*}\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\mathscr{C}\otimes\omega_{X}
, where
\mathscr{C}:=\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}(\nu_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}},\mathscr{O}_{X})\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}
is the conductor ideal of
\widetilde{X}
in
X
. This gives
\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}\otimes\nu^{*}\omega_{X}
, where

\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}:=\mathscr{C}\cdot\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}
. Recall that
\omega_{\widetilde{X}}
is reflexive since
\widetilde{X}
is normal. It follows that
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
is reflexive as well. Hence, there exists an effective Weil divisor on
\widetilde{X}
such that
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-E)
, proving (1).

If
E=0
, then
\mathscr{C}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
. This immediately implies that
\nu
is an isomorphism, finishing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 3
, and let
Y\subseteq X
be a compact Kähler submanifold of dimension
m\geqslant 2
. Let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation with normal bundle
\mathscr{N}
. Suppose that
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{n-1}
for some line bundles
\mathscr{L}_{i}
with
c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1})=\cdots=c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{n-1})\in H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a line bundle on
Y
such that
c_{1}(\mathscr{M})\in\langle c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1}|_{Y}),\ldots,c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{n-1}|_{Y}),c_{1}(\mathscr{N}|_{Y})\rangle\subseteq H^{1}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})
. Suppose that

\mathscr{M}
is nef and
\mathscr{M}\equiv\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}B_{i}
, where
b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and the
B_{i}
are distinct prime divisors.
Suppose in addition that
\mathscr{N}_{Y/X}^{*}|_{B_{i}}
is nef if
m<n
. Then the following holds.

(1)

For every index
i\in I
,
B_{i}
is a finite étale quotient of a complex torus.

(2)

We have
B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
.

(3)

The vector bundles
\mathscr{N}_{B_{i}/Y}
and
\mathscr{N}_{B_{i}/X}
are numerically flat. In particular
B_{i}\subset Y
is a nef divisor.

Proof.

Suppose that
m<n
. If
m=n
, the proof of the proposition is very similar and therefore omitted.

We may assume without loss of generality that
c_{1}(\mathscr{M})\not\equiv 0
. In other words, assume that
I
is not empty. By Proposition
3.2
, we have
c_{1}(\mathscr{M})^{2}\equiv 0
. It follows that
\mathscr{M}|_{B_{i}}\equiv 0
for any
i\in I
since

\mathscr{M}
is nef by assumption. By Proposition
3.2
again,

L:=\langle c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1}|_{Y}),\ldots,c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{n-1}|_{Y}),c_{1}(\mathscr{N}|_{Y})\rangle=\langle c_{1}(\mathscr{M}|_{Y})\rangle\subseteq H^{1}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y}).

Thus
\mathscr{L}_{j}|_{B_{i}}\equiv 0
and
\mathscr{N}|_{B_{i}}\equiv 0
.
As a consequence, the vector bundle
\Omega_{X}^{1}|_{B_{i}}
is numerically flat.
Consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{N}_{Y/X}^{*}|_{B_{i}}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}|_{B_{i}}\to\Omega_{Y}^{1}|_{B_{i}}\to 0.

Since
\mathscr{N}_{Y/X}^{*}|_{B_{i}}
is nef by assumption, we infer that
\mathscr{N}_{Y/X}^{*}|_{B_{i}}
and

\Omega_{Y}^{1}|_{B_{i}}
are numerically flat as well.

Next, consider the exact sequence of sheaves

0\to\mathscr{O}_{B_{i}}(-B_{i})\to\Omega_{Y}^{1}|_{B_{i}}\to\Omega_{B_{i}}^{1}\to 0.

Since
-B_{i}|_{B_{i}}\equiv\frac{1}{b_{i}}\sum_{j\neq i}b_{j}B_{j}|_{B_{i}}
, we conclude that
-B_{i}|_{B_{i}}\equiv 0
and that
\mathscr{O}_{B_{i}}(-B_{i})
is a subbundle of
\Omega_{Y}^{1}|_{B_{i}}
(see
[
DPS94
, Proposition 1.16]
). As a consequence, the sheaf

\Omega_{B_{i}}^{1}
is locally free and numerically flat. Moreover,
B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
.

Let
\nu_{i}\colon\widetilde{B}_{i}\to B_{i}
be the normalization morphism. By the adjunction formula,

\omega_{B_{i}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{Y}(K_{Y}+B_{i})|_{B_{i}}\equiv 0.

In particular,
\omega_{B_{i}}
is locally free. By Lemma
3.3
above, there exists an effective Weil divisor
E_{i}
on
\widetilde{B}_{i}
such that
\omega_{\widetilde{B}_{i}}\cong\nu_{i}^{*}\omega_{B_{i}}(-E_{i})
.

Suppose that
E_{i}\neq 0
. Then
K_{\widetilde{B}_{i}}
is not pseudo-effective, and hence
B_{i}
is uniruled by
[
BDPP13
, Corollary 0.3]
applied to a resolution of
\widetilde{B}_{i}
.
Let
C\subseteq B_{i}
be a rational curve. Then
\Omega^{1}_{Y}|_{C}
is the trivial vector bundle since
\Omega^{1}_{Y}|_{C}
is numerically flat, yielding a contradiction. This shows that
E_{i}=0
, and hence
B_{i}
is normal by Lemma
3.3
. Then
[
BDPP13
, Corollary 0.3]
applies to show that
B_{i}
has canonical singularities since
K_{B_{i}}\equiv 0
. By the solution of the Zariski-Lipman conjecture for canonical spaces (see
[
GKKP11
, Theorem 6.1]
or
[
Dru14
, Theorem 1.1]
), we conclude that
B_{i}
is smooth. In addition, we have
c_{1}(B_{i})\equiv 0
and
c_{2}(B_{i})\equiv 0
by
[
DPS94
, Corollary 1.19]
.
As a classical consequence of Yau’s theorem on the existence of a Kähler-Einstein metric,
B_{i}
is then covered by a complex torus (see
[
Kob87
, Chapter IV Corollary 4.15]
). This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 3
, and let
Y\subseteq X
be a compact Kähler submanifold of dimension
m\geqslant 2
. Let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation. Suppose that
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{n-1}
for some line bundles
\mathscr{L}_{i}

with
c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1})=\cdots=c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{n-1})\in H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
. Suppose furthermore that
c_{k}(\mathscr{N}_{Y/X})=0
for any

if

.
Then

and

for any

.

.

Suppose that
m<n
. If

, the proof of the proposition is very similar and therefore omitted.

of

satisfies

3.2.
Abelian schemes

The proof of Theorem
1.1
uses Kollár’s
characterisation of étale quotients of abelian schemes
[
Kol93
, Theorem 6.3]
. We recall the relevant notion first. Let
X
be a normal projective variety and let
Y\subseteq X
be a closed subvariety. We say that X has
generically large fundamental group on
Y
if for any very general point
y\in Y
and for every closed and positive-dimensional subvariety
y\in Z\subseteq Y
with normalization
\overline{Z}
, the image of the natural morphism
\pi_{1}(\overline{Z})\to\pi_{1}(X)
is infinite. The following observation will prove to be crucial.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism with connected fibers between smooth algebraic varieties, and let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. Suppose that every fiber of
f

(
with its reduced structure
)
is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety.
Then the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(F)\to\pi(X)
is injective. In particular,
X
has generically large fundamental group on
F
.

Proof.

By
[
Hör13
, Proof of Lemma 2.2]
and
[
Nak99
, Lemma 7.3]
,
there exists an open subset
Y^{\circ}\subseteq Y
with complement of codimension at least
2

such that the restriction
f^{\circ}
of
f
to
X^{\circ}:=f^{-1}(Y^{\circ})
is locally homotopically
Q
-smooth (we refer the reader to
[
Nak99
, Definition 7.2]
for this notion). Then

[
Nak99
, Theorem 7.8]
applies to show that
f^{\circ}
is birational to a
Q
-smooth

Q
-torus fibration
g^{\circ}\colon Z^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
over
Y^{\circ}
. In other words,
there exists a finite morphism
Y_{1}^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
such that the normalization

Z_{1}^{\circ}
of
Y_{1}^{\circ}\times_{Y^{\circ}}Z^{\circ}
is étale over
Z^{\circ}
and such that the morphism
Z_{1}^{\circ}\to Y_{1}^{\circ}
is smooth with fibers being finite étale quotients of abelian varieties. This immediately implies that any fiber of
g^{\circ}
(with its reduced structure) is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety. It then follows that
X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}\cong Z^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}
. Let
X_{1}^{\circ}\to X^{\circ}
be the induced finite étale cover and denote by
f_{1}^{\circ}\colon X_{1}^{\circ}\to Y_{1}^{\circ}
the corresponding
smooth fibration. By
[
Nak99
, Theorem 3.14]
, there exists a finite étale cover
X_{2}^{\circ}\to X_{1}^{\circ}
such that any fiber of the composition
f_{2}^{\circ}\colon X_{2}^{\circ}\to X_{1}^{\circ}\to Y_{1}^{\circ}=:Y_{2}^{\circ}
is an abelian variety.
Let
F_{2}
be a general fiber of
f_{2}^{\circ}
. By
[
Cla10
, Proof of Lemma 2.3]
,
the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(F_{2})\to\pi_{1}(X_{2}^{\circ})
is injective. Moreover, there is a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(F_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X_{2}^{\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X^{\circ}),}

where
F
denotes the image of
F_{2}
in
X^{\circ}
. By
[
Nak99
, Corollary 3.7]
,

\pi_{1}(F)
is torsion free. This easily implies that the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(F)\to\pi(X)
is also injective since
\pi_{1}(F_{2})
has finite index in
\pi(F)
. Finally, the inclusion induces an isomorphism
\pi_{1}(X^{\circ})\cong\pi_{1}(X)
of fundamental groups since
X\setminus X^{\circ}
is a closed subset of codimension at least
2
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We will also need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth projective morphism with connected fibers between smooth algebraic varieties. Suppose that there exists
y_{0}\in Y
such that the fiber
f^{-1}(y_{0})
of
f
over
y_{0}
is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety. Then every fiber of
f
is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety. Moreover, there exist a dense open set
Y^{\circ}\subseteq Y
and a finite étale cover
X_{1}^{\circ}\to X^{\circ}:=f^{-1}(Y^{\circ})
such that the fibration
X_{1}^{\circ}\to Y_{1}^{\circ}
obtained as the Stein factorization of the composition
X_{1}^{\circ}\to X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
is an abelian scheme equipped with a level three structure.

By
[
Nak99
, Theorem 3.14]
, there exists a finite étale cover
X_{1}\to X
such that the fiber
f_{1}^{-1}(y_{0})
of the composition
f_{1}\colon X_{1}\to X\to Y=:Y_{1}
is an abelian variety. Notice that
f_{1}
is a smooth morphism. By Ehresmann’s theorem, any fiber
F_{1}
of
f_{1}
is homeomorphic to an abelian variety. Then
[
Cat02
, Theorem 4.8]
applies to show that
F_{1}
is an abelian variety. Finally, shrinking
Y_{1}

The proof of Theorem
1.1
makes use of the following result, which might be of independent interest.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an abelian scheme over a projective base equipped with a level three structure, and let
\beta\colon X\to M
be a birational morphism onto a smooth projective variety. Then
M
is an abelian scheme over a smooth projective base
N
and there exists a birational morphism
Y\to N
such that
X/Y\cong Y\times_{N}M/Y
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq X
be the neutral section of
f
, and let
N
be the normalization of
\Lambda:=\beta(\Sigma)
. Let also
\mathscr{A}(3)
be the fine moduli space of polarized abelian varieties with a level three structure.

Let
C\subseteq X
be a rational curve such that
\dim\beta(C)=0
, and set
B:=f(C)
. By
[
Kol93
, Lemma 5.9.3]
,
\big(f^{-1}(B)/B,\Sigma|_{f^{-1}(B)}\big)\cong(B\times A/B,B\times\{0_{A}\})
, where
(A,0_{A})
is an abelian variety. Notice that
C=B\times\{a_{0}\}
for some point
a_{0}\in A
. In particular, either
C
is disjoint from
\Sigma
or
C
is contained in
\Sigma
. This immediately implies that
\Sigma=\beta^{-1}(\Lambda)

since every positive-dimensional fiber of
\beta
is rationally chain connected. By the rigidity lemma (see
[
Deb01
, Lemma 1.15]
), the morphism
Y\to\mathscr{A}(3)
corresponding to
f
factorizes through the map
\eta\colon Y\cong\Sigma\to N
induced by
\beta|_{\Sigma}
. In other words, the abelian scheme
f
is the pull-back of an abelian scheme
M_{1}
over
N
via
\eta
. Let
\beta_{1}\colon X\to M_{1}
be the induced morphism.

To prove the statement, it suffices to show that there exists an isomorphism
\tau\colon M\to M_{1}
such that
\beta_{1}=\tau\circ\beta
or equivalently that
\beta
(resp.
\beta_{1}
) factorizes through
\beta_{1}
(resp.
\beta
).

Let
F
be a positive-dimensional fiber of
\beta
. Recall that
F
is rationally chain connected. Set
G:=f(F)
. By
[
Kol93
, Lemma 5.9.3]
again,
\big(f^{-1}(G)/G,\Sigma|_{f^{-1}(G)}\big)\cong(G\times A_{2}/G,G\times\{0_{A_{2}}\})
, where
(A_{2},0_{A_{2}})
is an abelian variety. Moreover,
F=G\times\{a_{2}\}
for some point
a_{2}\in A_{2}
. Since
G
is a fiber of
\eta
,
F
is contracted by
\beta_{1}
. By the rigidity lemma (see
[
Deb01
, Lemma 1.15]
),
\beta_{1}
factorizes through
\beta
. Let now
F_{1}
be a fiber of
\beta_{1}
.
Set
G_{1}:=f(F_{1})
. Then
G_{1}
is a fiber of
\eta
. Moreover,
\big(f^{-1}(G_{1})/G_{1},\Sigma|_{f^{-1}(G_{1})}\big)\cong(G_{1}\times A_{1}/G_{1},G_{1}\times\{0_{A_{1}}\})
for some abelian variety
(A_{1},0_{A_{1}})
, and
F_{1}\cong G_{1}\times\{a_{1}\}
for some point
a_{1}\in A_{1}
. Since
G_{1}\times\{0_{A_{1}}\}\cong\Sigma\cap f^{-1}(G_{1})
is contracted by
\beta
,
F_{1}
is contracted by
\beta
as well. By the rigidity lemma (see
[
Deb01
, Lemma 1.15]
),
\beta
factorizes through
\beta_{1}
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.3.
Descent of line bundles

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of algebraic varieties, and let
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. We give sufficient conditions that guarantee that there exist a line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on
Y
and a positive integer
m
such that
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
. The following observation is rather standard. We include a proof here for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a flat projective morphism with integral fibers between smooth complex algebraic varieties. Let
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle on
X
, and let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. Suppose that
\mathscr{L}|_{F}
is a torsion line bundle. Then there exists a line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on
Y
and a positive integer
m
such that

\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

Let
m
be a positive integer such that the set of points
y\in Y
with

\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}|_{X_{y}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X_{y}}
is dense.
Because the functions
y\mapsto h^{0}\big(X_{y},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes\pm m}|_{X_{y}}\big)
are upper semicontinuous in the Zariski topology on
Y
(see
[
Har77
, Theorem 12.8]
), we have

h^{0}\big(X_{y},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes\pm m}|_{X_{y}}\big)=1
for every
y\in Y
. This implies that

\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}|_{X_{y}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X_{y}}
since
X_{y}
is integral by assumption. By

[
Har77
, Corollary 12.9]
together with the base change theorem
[
Har77
, Theorem 12.11]
,
\mathscr{M}:=f_{*}\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}
is a line bundle and the formation of
f_{*}\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}
commutes with arbitrary base change. This easily implies that
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Setting and notation as in Lemma
3.9
. Suppose in addition that

. Then the proof of Lemma
3.9
shows that there exists a line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on

such that
\mathscr{L}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold and let

\mathscr{L}
be a nef line bundle on

such that
\mathscr{L}|_{F}
is torsion, where

is a general fiber of

. Then there exist a line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on
B
and a positive integer
m
such that
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
. In particular,
\mathscr{L}\equiv_{B}0
.

Proof.

Let
B^{\circ}\subseteq B
be a dense open set such that
f^{\circ}:=f|_{X^{\circ}}
is a smooth morphism, where
X^{\circ}:=f^{-1}(B^{\circ})
. Let
m
be a positive integer. Because the functions
b\mapsto h^{0}\big(X_{b},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes\pm m}|_{X_{b}}\big)
are upper semicontinuous in the Zariski topology on
B
(see
[
Har77
, Theorem 12.8]
), the set of points
b
on
B^{\circ}
such that
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}|_{X_{b}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X_{b}}
is closed. As a consequence, there exists a positive integer
m_{1}
such that
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m_{1}}|_{X_{b}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X_{b}}
for all
b\in B^{\circ}
.
From
[
Har77
, Corollary 12.9]
, we see that
f^{\circ}_{*}(\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m_{1}}|_{X^{\circ}})
is a line bundle. Thus, there exists a divisor
D
on
X
with
f(\textup{Supp}\,D)\subsetneq B

such that
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m_{1}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}(D)
.

Let
S\subset X
be a
2
-dimensional complete intersection of general elements of a very ample linear system on
X
. This is a smooth surface in
X
and the restriction
f|_{S}
of
f
to
S
is a surjective morphism with connected fibers onto
B
. By Zariski’s Lemma, we have
D|_{S}^{2}\leqslant 0
. Since
D
is nef by assumption, we must have
D|_{S}^{2}=0
. By Zariski’s Lemma again, there exist a positive integer
m_{2}
and a divisor
G
on
B
such that
m_{2}D|_{S}=f|_{S}^{*}G
. The claim now follows from the Grothendieck-Lefchetz hyperplane theorem.
∎

The following result is an easy consequence of Proposition
3.11
above.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an equidimensional morphism with connected fibers between normal projective varieties, and let
\mathscr{L}
be a nef line bundle on
X
such that
\mathscr{L}|_{F}
is torsion, where
F
is a general fiber of
f
. Then
\mathscr{L}\equiv_{Y}0
.

Proof.

Notice that a general fiber of
f
is irreducible by
[
Deb01
, 5.5]
. By a theorem of Chevalley (see
[
Gro66
, Corollaire 14.4.4]
, the morphism
f
is universally open.

Let
y\in Y
be a point and let
B\to Y
be a morphism from a smooth complete curve
B
whose image contains
y
and a general point in
Y
. Observe that product
B\times_{Y}X
is irreducible since the projection
B\times_{Y}X\to B
is open with irreducible general fibers. Let
Z
be a resolution of
(B\times_{Y}X)_{\textup{red}}
. Our claim follows from Proposition
3.11
applied to the pull-back of
\mathscr{L}
to
Z
.
∎

We will also need the following elementary observation.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a surjective morphism of normal projective varieties with
n:=\dim X\geqslant 2
and
m:=\dim Y\geqslant 1
. Let
D
be a prime
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
such that
\dim f(D)=0
. Suppose in addition that
D^{2}\cdot H^{n-2}\geqslant 0
for some very ample divisor
H
on
X
. Then
\dim Y=\kappa(D)=1
, and
f
is induced by the linear system
|mD|
for
m
sufficiently large.

Proof.

Let us first show that
\dim Y=1
. We argue by contradiction and assume that
\dim Y\geqslant 2
. Let
S\subseteq X
be a
2
-dimensional complete intersection of general elements of
|H|
, and set
C:=S\cap D
. By Bertini’s theorem (see
[
Jou83
, Théorème I.6.10]
), we may assume without loss of generality that
S
and
C
are reduced and irreducible.
Then
D^{2}\cdot S=(D|_{S})^{2}<0
since
C
is contracted by the generically finite morphism

f|_{S}
, yielding a contradiction since
D^{2}\cdot S=D^{2}\cdot H^{n-2}\geqslant 0
by assumption. This shows
\dim Y=1
.

Zariski’s Lemma then applies to show that some non-zero multiple of
D|_{S}=C
is a fiber of

f|_{S}
. This easily implies that some non-zero multiple of
D
is a fiber of
f
, and hence

\kappa(D)=1
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.4.
Abundance

We will later use the following special case of the abundance
conjecture, whose proof relies on an extension theorem proved in
[
DHP13
]
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
X
be a smooth complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
, and let
B
be a non-zero divisor on
X
. Let
B:=\sum_{i\in I}B_{i}
be its decomposition into irreducible components. Suppose that
B_{i}
is smooth with
K_{B_{i}}\equiv 0
and
B_{i}^{2}\equiv 0
. Suppose moreover that
B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
. Suppose finally
\kappa(X)\geqslant 0
and
K_{X}
nef. Then there exists a morphism with connected fibers
f\colon X\to C
onto a smooth complete curve such that
B_{i}
is a fiber of
f
for every
i\in I
. In addition,
K_{X}
is semiample,
K_{X}|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
for a general fiber
F
of
f
and

\kappa(X)=\nu(K_{X})\leqslant 1
. In particular,
K_{X}+B
is semiample with
\kappa(K_{X}+B)=\nu(K_{X}+B)\leqslant 1
.

Proof.

Let
D
be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D
.
Notice that
D\cdot B_{i}=K_{X}\cdot B_{i}\equiv 0
since
B_{i}^{2}\equiv 0
and
K_{B_{i}}\equiv 0
by assumption. Thus, there is a decomposition
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
, where
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with

\textup{Supp}\,D_{1}\cap\textup{Supp}\,D_{2}=\emptyset
and
\textup{Supp}\,D_{2}\subseteq\textup{Supp}\,B
.

Let us show that
D_{1}
is nef. We argue by contradiction and assume that there exists a curve
C\subset X
such that
D_{1}\cdot C<0
. Then
C\subseteq\textup{Supp}\,D_{1}
and hence
C\cap\textup{Supp}\,D_{2}=\emptyset
. Thus
K_{X}\cdot D=D\cdot C=D_{1}\cdot C<0
, yielding a contradiction. Notice also that
B_{i}
is nef since
B_{i}^{2}\equiv 0
by assumption.

Pick
i_{0}\in I
. Then
K_{X}+B_{i_{0}}+\varepsilon\big(D_{1}+\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{i_{0}\}}B_{i}\big)
is nef as well for every
\varepsilon>0
. Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
be a rational number such that
the pair
\big(X,B_{i_{0}}+\varepsilon\big(D_{1}+\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{i_{0}\}}B_{i}\big)\big)
is plt. Then
[
DHP13
, Corollary 1.8]
applies to show that there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G_{1}
such that

K_{X}+B_{i_{0}}+\varepsilon\big(D_{1}+\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{i_{0}\}}B_{i}\big)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}G_{1}
and
B_{i_{0}}\cap G_{1}=\emptyset
since

\big(K_{X}+B_{i_{0}}+\varepsilon\big(D_{1}+\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{i_{0}\}}B_{i}\big)\big)|_{B_{i_{0}}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{B_{i_{0}}}
is torsion. Set
G_{2}:=D+B_{i_{0}}+\varepsilon\big(D_{1}+\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{i_{0}\}}B_{i}\big)
. Then
G_{2}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}G_{1}
and
\textup{mult}_{B_{i_{0}}}\,G_{2}\geqslant 1
.

Let
m
be a positive integer such that
mG_{1}
and
mG_{2}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-divisors, and let
X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the corresponding rational map. Let
p\colon Y\to X
and
q\colon Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a common resolution of singularities. We may assume without loss of generality that
p
restricts to an isomorphism over some Zariski open neighborhood of
B_{i_{0}}
since
B_{i_{0}}\cap G_{1}=\emptyset
by construction. Let
g\colon Y\to C
be the Stein factorization of
q
. By Zariski’s Lemma, some positive multiple of the strict transform of
B_{i_{0}}
in
Y
is a fiber of
g
since
B_{i_{0}}^{2}\equiv 0
. This immediately implies that the rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow C
is a morphism. By Zariski’s Lemma again, for every
i\in I
, some positive multiple of
B_{i}
is a fiber of
f
. Let now
F
be a general fiber of
f
. Then, by construction,
\big(K_{X}+B_{i_{0}}+\varepsilon\big(D_{1}+\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{i_{0}\}}B_{i}\big)\big)|_{F}=K_{X}|_{F}
is torsion. But
K_{X}|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}K_{F}
by the adjunction formula. The statement easily follows from Proposition
3.11
.
∎

3.5.
Foliations and rational curves

In order to study foliations on non-minimal varieties it is useful to understand their behavior with respect to rational curves.
The following result is a minor generalization of
[
Fig22
, Lemma 2.18]
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
, and let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation. Let
S
be a rational ruled surface, and let
\gamma\colon S\to X
be a generically finite morphism. Then there exists a rational curve
C\subset S
which is not contracted by
\gamma
such that
\gamma(C)
is tangent to
\mathscr{F}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{N}
denote the normal bundle of
\mathscr{F}
, and let
f\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
denote the natural morphism. Recall that
\textup{N}^{1}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}[C_{0}]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[F]
, where
F
is any fiber of
f
and
C_{0}
is a section of
f
with
C_{0}^{2}=-e\leqslant 0
.

Set
\mathscr{N}_{S}:=\mathscr{N}|_{S}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have
c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})^{2}=0
.
Write
c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})\equiv aC_{0}+bF
. Then

0=c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})^{2}=(aC_{0}+bF)^{2}=a(-ae+2b),

c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})\cdot F=a

and

c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})\cdot C_{0}=-ae+b.

We may obviously assume that
F
is not tangent to
\mathscr{F}
. Then the composition

T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong T_{F}\to T_{X}|_{F}\to\mathscr{N}|_{F}=\mathscr{N}_{S}|_{F}

is non-zero, and hence
a=c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})\cdot F\geqslant 2>0
. It follows that
2b=ae
. In particular, we have
b\geqslant 0
.

If
C_{0}
is contracted by
\gamma
, then
c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})\cdot C_{0}=0
. This immediately implies
b=e=0
. Then
S\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
f
identifies with the projection onto the first factor. Moreover, the image of a general fiber of the projection onto the second factor is tangent to
\mathscr{F}
.

Suppose from now on that
C_{0}
is not contracted by
\gamma
and that
\gamma(C_{0})
is not tangent to
\mathscr{F}
. Then, as above, we must have
c_{1}(\mathscr{N}_{S})\cdot C_{0}\geqslant 2
. It follows that
b\geqslant ae+2
, and hence
b+2\leqslant 0
, yielding a contradiction. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We will also need the following auxiliary results.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
, and let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{X}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation. Let
\varphi\colon X\to Y
be an elementary Fano-Mori contraction. Suppose that there exists an irreducible component
F_{1}
of a positive-dimensional fiber
F
of
\varphi
which is not tangent to
\mathscr{F}
. Then
Y
is smooth, and either
\varphi
is the blow-up of a smooth codimension
2
submanifold or
\varphi
is a conic bundle.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{N}
denote the normal bundle of
\mathscr{F}
, and let
C\subseteq F
be a rational curve. Suppose that
\mathscr{N}\cdot C=0
. Then
C
is tangent to
\mathscr{F}
and moreover
\mathscr{N}|_{F}\equiv 0
since
\varphi
is elementary by assumption. This in turn implies that any irreducible component of
F
is tangent to
\mathscr{F}
since
F
is rationally chain connected by
[
HM07
, Corollary 1.4]
, yielding a contradiction. This shows that
\mathscr{N}\cdot C\neq 0
. On the other hand,
c_{1}(\mathscr{N})^{2}\equiv 0
by Lemma
3.1
. This immediately implies that any positive-dimensional fiber of
\varphi
is
1
-dimensional. The claim now follows from
[
Wiś91
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
, and let
\mathscr{F}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation. Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is projectively flat, and let
C\subset X
be a rational curve such that
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot C\leqslant 0
. Then
T_{X}|_{C}
is nef. In particular,
X
is uniruled.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{N}
denote the normal bundle of
\mathscr{F}
, and let
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to C\subset X
be the normalization morphism. Since
\mathscr{F}
is projectively flat, there exists
a\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathscr{F}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a)^{\oplus n-1}
. Notice that
a\geqslant 0
since
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot C\leqslant 0
by assumption.

If
C
is tangent to
\mathscr{F}
, then
\mathscr{N}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is a flat line bundle by Lemma
3.1
, and hence
\mathscr{N}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. If

C
is not tangent to
\mathscr{F}
, then the composition

T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to T_{X}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to\mathscr{N}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

is non-zero, and hence
\mathscr{N}\cdot C\geqslant 2
. In either case, the exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{F}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to T_{X}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to\mathscr{N}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to 0

shows that
T_{X}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is nef. But then
X
is uniruled by
[
Kol96
, Theorem IV.1.9]
.
∎

In this subsection we treat a few easy special cases of Theorem

.

Proposition 3.18
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Suppose in addition that
K_{\mathscr{F}}\equiv 0
. Then one of the following holds.

(1)

There exists a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle structure
\varphi\colon X\to Y
onto a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety, and

.

(2)

There exists an abelian variety
A

\gamma\colon A\to X

\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}

.

(3)

There exists a smooth complete curve

\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}

This follows easily from

.

Remark 3.19
.

In the setup of Proposition
3.18
, there exists a finite étale cover
\eta\colon Z\to X
such that
\eta^{-1}\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{Z}^{\oplus n-1}
.

Proposition 3.20
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Suppose in addition that
\mathscr{F}
is algebraically integrable. Then one of the following holds.

(1)

There exists an abelian variety
A
as well as a smooth complete curve
C
of genus at most
1
and a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon A\times C\to X
such that
\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is induced by the projection
A\times C\to C
.

(2)

There exists a smooth abelian scheme
f\colon B\to C
over a smooth complete curve
C
of genus at least
2
as well as a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon B\to X
such that
\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is induced by
f
.

In particular,
K_{\mathscr{F}}
is semiample with
\kappa(\mathscr{F})=\nu(\mathscr{F})\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\mathscr{F}
is induced by a morphism with irreducible fibers
f\colon X\to C
onto a smooth complete curve
C
. By
[
JR13
, Theorem 0.1]
, any leaf of
\mathscr{F}
is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety. By Lemma
3.6
and
[
Kol93
, Theorem 6.3]
together, after replacing
X
by a finite étale cover, the fibration
f
is birational to an abelian scheme over
C
. This easily implies that
f
is an abelian scheme.
Replacing
X
by a further finite étale cover, we may also assume that
f
is polarized with a level three structure. Let
\mathscr{A}(3)
be the corresponding fine moduli space of polarized abelian varieties with a level three structure, and let

be the moduli map. By
[

∎

Remark 3.21
.

In the setup of Proposition
3.20
(2), we have

\Omega^{1}_{B/C}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{E}
with

.

Lemma 3.22
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Suppose in addition that
X
is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety. Then
K_{\mathscr{F}}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Replacing
X
by a finite étale cover, if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
X
is an abelian variety. Then
\mathscr{N}
is generated by its global section. On the other hand, we have
\mathscr{N}^{2}\equiv 0
by Lemma
3.1
. If
\mathscr{N}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
, then
K_{\mathscr{F}}\equiv 0
by the adjunction formula. Suppose that
\mathscr{N}\not\equiv 0
. Then, after a further finite étale cover, we have
X\cong A\times E
, where
A
is an abelian variety of dimension
n-1
and
E
is an elliptic curve by Poincaré’s complete reducibility theorem. Moreover,
\mathscr{N}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
for some line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on

.
Then Lemma

below applies to show that

since

by assumption. This implies
\mathscr{N}\equiv 0
, yielding a contradiction. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
C
be a smooth complete curve, and let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
n-1\geqslant 1
. Set
X:=C\times A
and
f:=\textup{pr}_{C}
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a codimension
1
foliation on
X
with normal bundle
\mathscr{N}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{L}
for some line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
C
. Then
\mathscr{F}
is regular and
one of the following conditions holds.

(1)

We have
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\oplus n-1}
.

(2)

We have
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\oplus n-2}\oplus f^{*}(T_{C}\otimes\mathscr{L}^{*})
.

(3)

We have
\mathscr{L}\cong\mathscr{O}_{C}
and there is an exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{O}^{\oplus n-2}\to\mathscr{F}\to f^{*}T_{C}\to 0.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{N})
be a twisted
1
-form defining
\mathscr{F}
.
Let
F\cong A
be a fiber of
f
. Then
T_{X}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus n}
and
\mathscr{N}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}
. This immediately implies that
\mathscr{F}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus n-1}
and that
\mathscr{F}
is regular since
the zero set of
\omega
has codimension at least
2
in
X
. Moreover, there exists a vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
on
C
such that
\mathscr{F}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{E}
.

We may obviously assume that
\mathscr{F}\neq T_{X/C}
. If
F
is a general fiber of
f
, then
\mathscr{F}\cap T_{X/C}|_{F}\subset T_{F}
is a linear codimension
1
foliation on
F\cong A
. Hence there exists a subbundle
\mathscr{K}\subset\mathscr{E}
of rank
n-2
such that
\mathscr{F}\cap T_{X/C}=f^{*}\mathscr{K}\subset\mathscr{F}
.
Set
g:=\textup{pr}_{A}
. Write
\omega=\alpha+\beta\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{N})\cong H^{0}(X,f^{*}\omega_{C}\otimes\mathscr{N})\oplus H^{0}(X,g^{*}\Omega_{A}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{N})

with

\alpha\in H^{0}(X,f^{*}\omega_{C}\otimes\mathscr{N})
and
\beta\in H^{0}(X,g^{*}\Omega_{A}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{N})\cong H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})\otimes H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L})
. By construction, there is an effective divisor
D_{1}
on
C
and an exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{K}\to H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})^{*}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C}\to\mathscr{L}(-D_{1})\to 0,

where the map
H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})^{*}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C}\to\mathscr{L}(-D_{1})
is induced by
\beta
.
Since
\mathscr{F}\neq T_{X/C}
, the natural map
\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\oplus n-1}\cong T_{X/C}\to\mathscr{N}
is non-zero, and hence
h^{0}(X,\mathscr{L})\geqslant 1
. Let
D_{2}
be any effective divisor on
C
such that
\mathscr{L}\cong\mathscr{O}_{C}(D_{2})
. We view
\omega
,
\alpha
and
\beta
as rational
1
-forms on
X
with poles along the divisor
f^{*}D_{2}
. We have
\textup{d}\alpha=0
and
\textup{d}\beta\wedge\alpha=0
.
But then
\textup{d}\beta\wedge\beta=\textup{d}\omega\wedge\omega=0
.
Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n-1}
be a basis of
H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})
and let
U\subset C\setminus\textup{Supp}\,D_{2}
be an open affine subset. Then

\beta|_{U\times A}=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}t_{i}\gamma_{i}

for some regular functions
t_{i}
on
U
. Moreover,
\textup{d}\beta\wedge\beta=0
if and only if
\frac{\textup{d}t_{i}}{t_{i}}=\frac{\textup{d}t_{j}}{t_{j}}
for all indices
i
and
j
.
Suppose that
t_{j}\neq 0
. Then
t_{i}=c_{ij}t_{j}
for some
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
.
This implies that there exists
s\in H^{0}(Y,\mathscr{L})
such that
\beta=\gamma\otimes s
, where
\gamma:=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}c_{ij}\gamma_{i}\in H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})
. Then
\textup{div}\,s=D_{1}
and
\mathscr{K}\cong\mathscr{O}_{C}^{\oplus n-2}
.

Set
\mathscr{Q}:=\mathscr{E}/\mathscr{K}
. Then
\mathscr{Q}\cong T_{C}\otimes\mathscr{L}^{*}
. If
h^{0}(C,\mathscr{L}^{*})=0
, then

h^{1}(C,\mathscr{Q}^{*})=h^{1}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes\mathscr{L})=h^{0}(C,\mathscr{L}^{*})=0
by Serre duality, and hence the exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{Q}\to 0

splits. Then
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\oplus n-2}\oplus f^{*}(T_{C}\otimes\mathscr{L}^{*})
.

Suppose finally that
h^{0}(C,\mathscr{L}^{*})\geqslant 1
. Then
\mathscr{L}\cong\mathscr{O}_{C}
since we also have

. Then
\mathscr{Q}\cong T_{C}
, finishing the proof of the lemma.

4.
Normal bundles of regular foliations

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
and give a few applications to codimension
1
foliations on varieties of general type. If
C
is a smooth complete curve, then
\pm K_{C}
is pseudo-effective. Theorem
1.2
can be seen as the analogous statement for the canonical divisor of the space of leaves of a codimension
1
regular foliation.

We will need the following Bertini-type result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n\geqslant 2
, and let
Y\subset X
be a subvariety of codimension
c\geqslant 2
. Let
1\leqslant k\leqslant c-1
be an integer, and let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
be very ample divisors on
X
. Suppose that
X
is Cohen-Macaulay. Then there exist large enough positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
and dense open subsets
V_{i}\subseteq|\mathscr{I}_{Y}(m_{i}H_{i})|
such that for any tuple

(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})\in V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{k}
, the associated complete intersection

S:=A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{k}
is reduced and irreducible. In addition,
S
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

To prove the statement, we argue by induction on
k
.

Suppose
k=1
. Let
\beta\colon Z\to X
be the blow-up of
X
along
Y
, and let
E
be the effective divisor on
Z
corresponding to the invertible sheaf
\beta^{-1}\mathscr{I}_{Y}\cdot\mathscr{O}_{Z}
. Notice that
Z
is reduced and irreducible. Let
m_{1}
be a positive integer such that
\mathscr{I}_{Y}(m_{1}H_{1})
is generated by its global sections. Then the linear system
|m_{1}\beta^{*}H_{1}-E|
induces a morphism
\varphi_{1}\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
, where
N_{1}:=\dim|\mathscr{I}_{Y}(m_{1}H_{1})|
. We may also assume without loss of generality that
\dim\varphi_{1}(Z)\geqslant 2
. By Bertini’s theorem (see
[
Jou83
, Théorème I.6.10]
), a general member of the linear system
|m_{1}\beta^{*}H_{1}-E|
is reduced and irreducible. This immediately implies that a general member
A_{1}
of
|\mathscr{I}_{Y}(m_{1}H_{1})|
is generically reduced and irreducible. Since
A_{1}
is Cohen-Macaulay (see
[
Eis95
, Proposition 18.13]
), we see that
A_{1}
is reduced by Serre’s criterion.

Suppose
k\geqslant 2
. Then the induction hypothesis applied to
A_{1}
and
H_{2}|_{A_{1}},\ldots,H_{k}|_{A_{1}}
show that there exist positive integers
m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}
and dense open subsets
V_{i}\subseteq|\mathscr{I}_{Y}(m_{i}H_{i}|_{A_{1}})|
such that for any tuple

(B_{2},\ldots,B_{k})\in V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{k}
, the associated complete intersection

S:=B_{2}\cap\cdots\cap B_{k}
is reduced and irreducible. In addition,
S
is Cohen-Macaulay.
We may also assume without loss of generality that

h^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}(m_{i}H_{i}-m_{1}H_{1}))=0
for any
i\geqslant 2
. This implies
that
|\mathscr{I}_{Y}(m_{i}H_{i})|_{A_{1}}=|\mathscr{I}_{Y}(m_{i}H_{i}|_{A_{1}})|
. The claim now follows easily.
∎

The proof of Theorem
4.4
below makes use of the following elementary results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface, and let
D
be a divisor on
S
such that
D^{2}=0
. Then
\pm D
is pseudo-effective.

Proof.

Let
H
be an ample divisor on
S
. Replacing
D
by
-D
if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
D\cdot H\geqslant 0
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be a rational number, and set

D_{\varepsilon}:=D+\epsilon H
. Then

D_{\varepsilon}\cdot H=D\cdot H+\varepsilon H^{2}>0\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\varepsilon}^{2}=\varepsilon(2D\cdot H+\varepsilon H^{2})>0.

Applying
[
Har77
, Corollary V.1.8]
, we see that
D_{\varepsilon}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective. This shows that
D
is pseudo-effective, proving the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface, and let
D
be a pseudo-effective divisor on
S
with
D^{2}=0
. Suppose in addition that
\kappa(D)\leqslant 1
. Then
D
is nef.

Proof.

Let
D=P+N
be the Zariski decomposition of
D
. Then
P
is a nef
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor while
N
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. In addition,
P\cdot N=0
and, either
N=0
or the intersection matrix
(N_{i}\cdot N_{j})_{i,j\in I}
is negative definite, where the
N_{i}
with
i\in I
are the irreducible components of
N
. Now
\kappa(P)\leqslant\kappa(D)\leqslant 1
by assumption, and hence
P^{2}=0
. It follows that
0=D^{2}=N^{2}
, and hence
N=0
, proving the lemma.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 1
, and let
D
be a divisor on
X
such that
D^{2}\equiv 0
. Suppose in addition that
D
is not pseudo-effective and that

. Then
-D
is nef.

Proof.

If
n=1
, then
\deg D\leqslant 0
since
D
is not pseudo-effective by assumption, and hence
-D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective. Suppose from now on that
n\geqslant 2
.
By
[
BDPP13
, Theorem 1.5]
, there exists a smooth projective variety
Z
, as well as a birational morphism
\beta\colon Z\to X
, and very ample divisors
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n-1}
on
Z
such that

\beta^{*}D\cdot H_{1}\cdots H_{n-1}<0.

Let
C\subset Z
be a curve. By Lemma
4.1

there exist positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-2}
and a tuple

(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n-2})\in|\mathscr{I}_{C}(m_{1}H_{1})|\times\cdots\times|\mathscr{I}_{C}(m_{n-2}H_{n-2})|

such that the surface

is reduced and irreducible.

We have
\beta^{*}D|_{S}\cdot H_{n-1}|_{S}<0
and
\beta^{*}D|^{2}_{S}=0
. Let
S_{1}\to S
be a resolution of singularities. Applying Lemma
4.2
to the pull-back

\beta^{*}D|_{S_{1}}
of
\beta^{*}D|_{S}
on

, we conclude that
-\beta^{*}D|_{S_{1}}
is pseudo-effective.

Let
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(D))\setminus\{0\}
. The twisted

-form
\omega
yields an injective map
\mathscr{O}_{X}(-D)\to\Omega^{1}_{X}
of sheaves. We may assume without loss of generality that the composition

\mathscr{O}_{X}(-D)|_{S_{1}}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}|_{S_{1}}\to\Omega^{1}_{S_{1}}
is non-zero. By the Bogomolov-Sommese vanishing theorem, we then have
\kappa(\mathscr{O}_{X}(-D)|_{S_{1}})\leqslant 1
. Applying Lemma

, we conclude that
-\beta^{*}D|_{S_{1}}
is nef. In particular,

-\beta^{*}D\cdot C\geqslant 0
. This shows that
-D
is nef, completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem

since

by Lemma

.
∎

The same argument used in the proof of Theorem

Theorem 4.5

Let

Abundance

In this section we prove a special case of the abundance conjecture (see Proposition
6.3
below). More precisely, we prove that if the pair
(X,\mathscr{F})
satisfies all the conditions listed in Setup
2.19
, then
\kappa(X)=\nu(X)
. First, we consider foliations with projectively trivial tangent bundle. The proof of Proposition
6.1
below
follows an argument that goes back at least as far as
[
Bru04
, Chapter 9]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 3
, and let
\mathscr{F}
be a regular codimension
1
foliation. Suppose that
\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}^{\oplus n-1}
for some line bundle
\mathscr{L}
. Then
\mathscr{L}^{*}
is semiample with
\nu(\mathscr{L}^{*})\leqslant 1
.

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience, the proof is subdivided into a number of steps. Notice that

\mathscr{L}^{*}
is nef by Lemma
2.21
and that
\nu(\mathscr{L}^{*})\leqslant 1
by Proposition
3.2
. Therefore, in order to prove the
proposition, it suffices to show that
\kappa(\mathscr{L}^{*})=\nu(\mathscr{L}^{*})
(see
[
Kaw85
, Proposition 2.1]
). Notice also that
\kappa(\mathscr{L}^{*})\leqslant\nu(\mathscr{L}^{*})
by
[
Kaw85
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Step 1.
We show that
\kappa(\mathscr{L}^{*})\geqslant 0
.

Suppose first that
q(X)>0
.

If
h^{0}(X,\mathscr{L}^{*})\geqslant 1
, then there is nothing to show. Suppose now that
h^{0}(X,\mathscr{L}^{*})=0
. Let
a\colon X\to A
be the Albanese morphism, and let
f\colon X\to Y
be its Stein factorization. Notice that
\dim Y\geqslant 1
since
q(X)>0
by assumption.
Now the standard pull-back map of Kähler differentials

H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\cong a^{*}\Omega_{A}^{1}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}

identifies with the evaluation map. It follows that
\mathscr{F}
is contained in the kernel of the tangent map

Ta\colon T_{X}\to a^{*}T_{A}

since
h^{0}(X,\mathscr{L}^{*})=0
by our current assumption. Thus
\dim Y=1
, and
\mathscr{F}
is induced by the fibration
f
. Let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. By
[
JR13
, Theorem 0.1]
,
F
is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety. In particular,
\mathscr{L}|_{F}
is a torsion line bundle. This easily implies that there exists a line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on
Y
and
a positive integer
m
such that
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes m}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
(see also Proposition
3.11
). Notice that
\mathscr{M}^{*}
is nef since
\mathscr{L}^{*}
is nef. But then
\kappa(\mathscr{M}^{*})\geqslant 0
and hence
\kappa(\mathscr{L}^{*})\geqslant 0
.

Suppose now that
q(X)=0
.

If
\mathscr{L}\equiv 0
, then
\mathscr{L}
is a torsion line bundle, and hence
\kappa(\mathscr{L}^{*})=0
. Suppose now that
\mathscr{L}\not\equiv 0
. From Proposition
3.5
, we get
\chi(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})=0
and hence

for some

. Using the exact sequence

we see that

for some non-negative integers

and
v
such that

. By Proposition
3.2
, there exist integers
r>0
and
s
such that
\mathscr{N}^{\otimes r}\cong\mathscr{L}^{\otimes s}
. It follows that

h^{0}\big(X,(\mathscr{L}^{*})^{\otimes su+rv}\big)>0
. Since
\mathscr{L}^{*}
is nef and
\mathscr{L}^{*}\not\equiv 0
, we must have

. This shows that
\kappa(\mathscr{L}^{*})\geqslant 0
in the case where

.

Step 2.
Write

(6.1)

-c_{1}(\mathscr{L})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}B_{i}

\mathscr{L}\equiv 0
, then
\mathscr{L}
is a torsion line bundle since

Then Proposition

is a flat line bundle as well by Equation (

Step 3.
Suppose now that the composition

is non-zero. In other words, assume that

is not a leaf of

. Then there exists a foliation by curves

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension

. Suppose that

\mathscr{L}

By
[

,
c_{1}(\mathscr{L})=0
. If

, then
\mathscr{L}
is torsion. Otherwise, we have
\mathscr{L}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
. In either case,
\mathscr{L}
is torsion, say of order
m
. Let
\gamma\colon Y\to X
be the associated cyclic cover (see
[
KM98
, Definition 2.52]
) which is étale with Galois group

. By construction,
T_{Y}\cong\gamma^{*}T_{X}\cong\mathscr{O}_{Y}^{\oplus n}
.
By a result proved by Wang (
[
Wan54
, Corollary 2]
),

is a complex torus,

Y=V/\Lambda
, where
V
is complex vector space of dimension
n
and
\Lambda\subset V
is a lattice.
Set

. There exists a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

satisfying

such that
\tau=t_{y_{0}}\circ\overline{L}
, where
\overline{L}
is the automorphism of
Y
induced by
L
. Notice that the linear map

\tau_{*}

Since

is a positive-dimensional analytic subvariety since

Proposition 6.3

denotes the radical of

\textup{sh}_{\rho_{1}}\colon X\dashrightarrow Y

be the
\rho_{1}
-Shafarevich map, where
\textup{sh}_{\rho_{1}}
is dominant and

is a smooth projective variety. The rational map
\textup{sh}_{\rho_{1}}
is almost proper with connected fibers. By
[
CCE15
, Théorème 1]
, we may assume without loss of generality that
Y
is of general type and that the representation
\rho_{1}
factorizes through
\textup{sh}_{\rho_{1}}
. If
\dim Y=\dim X
, then
X
is of general type and there is nothing to show.
Suppose from now on that
\dim Y<\dim X
.

Let
F
be a general (smooth) fiber of
\textup{sh}_{\rho_{1}}
. By
[
Dru23
, Proposition 2.23]
, in order to prove that
K_{X}
is abundant, it suffices to prove that
K_{F}
is abundant.

Suppose first
\dim Y=0
and
q(X)=0
.

Since
\rho(\pi_{1}(X))\subseteq\textup{Rad}(\textup{H})
, the restriction of
\rho
to a finite index subgroup of
\pi_{1}(X)
is the trivial representation. Thus, there exists a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon Y\to X
, as well as a line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
Y
such that
\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}^{\oplus n-1}
. Then
Proposition
6.1
applies to show that
\mathscr{L}^{*}
is semiample.
This in turn implies that
\mathscr{O}_{X}(K_{\mathscr{F}})
is semiample (
[
Uen75
, Lemma 5.12]
).

If
K_{\mathscr{F}}\equiv 0
, then our claim follows from Proposition
3.18
.

Suppose now that
K_{\mathscr{F}}\not\equiv 0
. By Proposition
3.2
and using the fact that
q(X)=0
, there exist integers
r>0
and
s
such that
\mathscr{N}^{\otimes r}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}(K_{\mathscr{F}})^{\otimes s}
. The claim now follows easily from the adjunction formula
\mathscr{O}_{X}(K_{X})\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\otimes\mathscr{N}^{*}
.

Suppose from now on that either
\dim Y>0
or
q(X)>0
.

Let
a\colon F\to\textup{A}
be the Albanese morphism. By construction, we have
\dim\textup{A}=q(F)
. Let
G
be a general (smooth) fiber of the Stein factorization of
F\to a(F)
.
By
[
Hu16
, Theorem 1.1]
, in order to prove that
K_{F}
is abundant, it suffices to prove that
K_{G}
is abundant. Thus, we may assume without loss of generality that
\dim G>0
. Repeating the process finitely many times, we may also assume that
q(G)=0
. Since
\rho(\pi_{1}(G))\subseteq\textup{Rad}(\textup{H})
, the restriction of
\rho
to a finite index subgroup of
\pi_{1}(G)
is the trivial representation.

Let
U\subseteq X
be a neighborhood of
G
in
X
for the analytic topology. Shrinking
U
, if necessary, we may assume that there exists a smooth projective morphism with connected fibers
f\colon U\to T
onto a simply connected complex manifold
T
such that
G
is a fiber of
f
. Then

\mathscr{N}_{G/U}\cong\mathscr{O}_{G}^{\oplus\dim X-\dim G}
. Moreover, we have
\dim G<\dim X
because
\dim Y>0
or
q(X)>0
by our current assumption. Since
\rho(\pi_{1}(G))
is finite, there exists a finite étale cover
g\colon U_{1}\to U
such that the inverse image
G_{1}
of
G
in
U_{1}
is connected and such that
\mathscr{F}_{U_{1}}:=g^{-1}(\mathscr{F}|_{U})\cong\mathscr{L}_{U_{1}}^{\oplus n-1}
for some line bundle
\mathscr{L}_{U_{1}}
on
U_{1}
.

If
G_{1}
is a leaf of
\mathscr{F}_{U_{1}}
, then
G_{1}
is an abelian variety by Lemma
6.2
. This in turn implies that
K_{G}
is torsion.

Suppose from now on that either
\dim G_{1}\leqslant n-2
or that
\dim G_{1}=n-1
and
G_{1}
is not a leaf of
\mathscr{F}_{U_{1}}
. Then the composition

\mathscr{F}_{U_{1}}|_{G_{1}}\cong\mathscr{L}_{U_{1}}|_{G_{1}}^{\oplus n-1}\to T_{U_{1}}|_{G_{1}}\to\mathscr{N}_{G_{1}/U_{1}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{G_{1}}^{\oplus\dim X-\dim G}

is non-zero, and hence
h^{0}(G_{1},\mathscr{L}_{G_{1}}^{*})\geqslant 1
, where

\mathscr{L}_{G_{1}}:=\mathscr{L}_{U_{1}}|_{G_{1}}
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{L}_{G_{1}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{G_{1}}
. If
T_{G_{1}}\subset\mathscr{F}_{U_{1}}|_{G_{1}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{G_{1}}^{\oplus n-1}
then
\Omega_{G_{1}}^{1}
is generated by its global sections and hence
K_{G_{1}}
is abundant. It follows from
[
Uen75
, Theorem 5.13]
that
K_{G}
is abundant as well. Suppose now that
T_{G_{1}}\not\subset\mathscr{F}_{U_{1}}|_{G_{1}}
. Then
T_{G_{1}}\cap(\mathscr{F}_{U_{1}}|_{G_{1}})\subset T_{G_{1}}
is a codimension
1
foliation with trivial tangent bundle.
Then
K_{G_{1}}
is abundant by Proposition
3.18
, using the fact that
K_{G_{1}}=K_{X}|_{G_{1}}
is pseudo-effective. Again, by
[
Uen75
, Theorem 5.13]
, we conclude that
K_{G}
is abundant.

Suppose finally that
\mathscr{L}_{G_{1}}\not\equiv 0
. By Lemma
2.21
,
\mathscr{L}_{G_{1}}^{*}
is nef. Moreover, by Proposition
3.2
, there is a number
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
K_{G_{1}}\equiv\lambda c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{G_{1}})
. Observe that
\lambda\leqslant 0
since
K_{G}=K_{X}|_{G}
is pseudo-effective. Since
h^{0}(G_{1},\mathscr{L}_{G_{1}}^{*})\geqslant 1
, we see that
\kappa(G_{1})\geqslant 0
by
[
CKP12
, Corollary 3.2]
. Notice also that

K_{G_{1}}
and hence
K_{G}
are nef. Then
[
Uen75
, Theorem 5.13]
implies that
\kappa(K_{G})\geqslant 0
.
Write
K_{G}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}B_{i}
with
b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
,
B_{i}
irreducible and

B_{i}\neq B_{j}
if
i\neq j
. By Proposition
3.4
applied to

K_{G_{1}}=(g|_{G_{1}})^{*}K_{G}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}(g|_{G_{1}})^{*}B_{i}
,

is a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety and

if

. Moreover, we have

. Then Proposition
3.14
applies to show that

is abundant. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

7.
Projectively flat foliations with nef conormal bundle

In this section we address codimension
1
regular foliations with numerically projectively flat tangent sheaf and nef conormal bundle. The proof of Theorem
7.2
below makes use of the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 7.1
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Suppose that
X
is an abelian scheme over a smooth projective base
Y
of dimension
m:=\dim Y\leqslant n-1
, and denote by
f\colon X\to Y
the natural morphism.
Suppose in addition that
\mathscr{N}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
for some line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on
Y
and that
T_{X/Y}\not\subseteq\mathscr{F}
. Then
f
is isotrivial.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{N})
be a twisted
1
-form defining
\mathscr{F}
, and let
\Sigma\subset X
be the neutral section of
f
.
By assumption, the composition
\mathscr{F}\to T_{X}\to f^{*}T_{Y}
is generically surjective.
Notice that the sheaf
f_{*}T_{X/Y}
is locally free of rank
n-m
.
Let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. The morphism
\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus n-m}\cong T_{X/Y}|_{F}\to\mathscr{N}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}
induced by
\omega

is generically surjective by assumption, and hence surjective. This implies that the saturation

\mathscr{E}
of
f_{*}(\mathscr{F}\cap T_{X/Y})
in
f_{*}T_{X/Y}
has rank
n-m-1
. Let
Y^{\circ}\subseteq Y
be an open subset with complement of codimension at least
2
such that
\mathscr{E}^{\circ}:=\mathscr{E}|_{Y^{\circ}}
is a subbundle of
f_{*}T_{X/Y}
. Set
X^{\circ}:=f^{-1}(Y^{\circ})
and
f^{\circ}:=f|_{X^{\circ}}
.
Then

(f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{E}^{\circ}=\mathscr{F}^{\circ}\cap T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}}\subset T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}}
since
\mathscr{F}^{\circ}\cap T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}}
is saturated in

T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}}
. In particular,
\mathscr{F}^{\circ}\cap T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}}

is a subbundle of
\mathscr{F}^{\circ}
. Set
\mathscr{Q}^{\circ}:=\mathscr{F}^{\circ}/(\mathscr{F}^{\circ}\cap T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}})
. Notice also that

\mathscr{Q}^{\circ}|_{F}\cong(f^{*}T_{Y})|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus m},

where
F
is a general fiber
F
of
f^{\circ}
. In particular,
K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}0
. Then Lemma
3.9
applies to show that there exists a line bundle
\mathscr{K}
on
Y
such that
\mathscr{O}_{X}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\cong f^{*}\mathscr{K}
.

Let now
F
be any fiber of
f^{\circ}
. Then the vector bundle
\mathscr{F}|_{F}
is numerically flat since

K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}0
. It follows that
\mathscr{Q}^{\circ}|_{F}
is numerically flat as well.
Then Lemma
2.15
applies to show that there exists a vector bundle
\mathscr{G}^{\circ}
on
Y^{\circ}
such that
\mathscr{Q}^{\circ}\cong(f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{G}^{\circ}
. By construction, there is an exact sequence

0\to(f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{E}^{\circ}\to\mathscr{F}^{\circ}\to(f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{G}^{\circ}\to 0.

Let again
F
be a general fiber of
f^{\circ}
. To prove the statement, it suffices to show that the exact sequence

(7.1)

0\to T_{X/Y}|_{F}\to T_{X}|_{F}\to(f^{*}T_{Y})|_{F}\to 0

splits. This is because complex flows of vector fields on analytic spaces exist.
If the short exact sequence

(7.2)

0\to(f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{F}\to\mathscr{F}^{\circ}|_{F}\to(f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{G}^{\circ}|_{F}\to 0

splits, then the exact sequence (
7.1
) splits as well, proving our claim. Suppose from now on that the exact sequence (
7.2
) does not split. Then the image of
\textup{Id}_{\mathscr{G}^{\circ}}\in H^{0}(Y^{\circ},\mathscr{E}nd(\mathscr{G}^{\circ}))
under the connecting homomorphism

H^{0}(Y^{\circ},\mathscr{E}nd(\mathscr{G}^{\circ}))\to R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\big((f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{E}^{\circ}\otimes((f^{\circ})^{*}\mathscr{G}^{\circ})^{*}\big)\cong R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{\circ}\otimes(\mathscr{G}^{\circ})^{*}

is non-zero.
Let
C
be a complete intersection curve of general elements of a very ample linear system on
Y
. By general choice of
C
, we have
C\subseteq Y^{\circ}
since
Y\setminus Y^{\circ}
has codimension at least
2
. Moreover,

\mu^{\textup{max}}\big((R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{\circ}\otimes(\mathscr{G}^{\circ})^{*})|_{C}\big)\geqslant 0.

Then

\displaystyle\mu^{\textup{max}}\big((R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{\circ}\otimes(\mathscr{G}^{\circ})^{*})|_{C}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}|_{C}\big)+\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)+\mu^{\textup{max}}\big((\mathscr{G}^{\circ})^{*}|_{C}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}|_{C}\big)+\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)-\mu^{\textup{min}}\big(\mathscr{G}^{\circ}|_{C}\big),

and hence

\mu^{\textup{min}}\big(\mathscr{G}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)\leqslant\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}|_{C}\big)+\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\big).

Since
(R^{1}f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X})^{*}\cong f_{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}
is nef (see

[
Gri70
, Theorem 5.2]
), we have

\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}|_{C}\big)\leqslant 0.

On the other hand, viewing
C
as a curve in
\Sigma
,
there is an exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\to\mathscr{F}^{\circ}|_{C}\to\mathscr{G}^{\circ}|_{C}\to 0.

It follows that

\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)\leqslant\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{C})\leqslant\mu^{\textup{min}}\big(\mathscr{G}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)

since
\mathscr{F}|_{C}
is semistable by assumption. This gives

\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{C})\leqslant\mu^{\textup{min}}\big(\mathscr{G}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)\leqslant\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}|_{C}\big)+\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)\leqslant\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)\leqslant\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{C}),

and hence

\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(R^{1}f^{\circ}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X^{\circ}}|_{C}\big)=0\quad\textup{and}\quad\mu^{\textup{max}}\big(\mathscr{E}^{\circ}|_{C}\big)=\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{C})=\mu^{\textup{min}}\big(\mathscr{G}^{\circ}|_{C}\big).

This implies that the vector bundle
f_{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}|_{C}
is numerically flat. In particular,
we have
\deg(f_{*}\mathscr{O}(K_{X/Y})|_{C})=0
.
Then
[
Uen78
, Corollary 2.3]
applies to show that
f
is isotrivial. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.2
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Suppose in addition that the conormal bundle
\mathscr{N}^{*}
of
\mathscr{F}
is nef.
Then one of the following holds.

(1)

There exists an abelian variety
A
as well as a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon A\to X
such that

\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is a linear foliation.

(2)

There exists an abelian scheme
f\colon B\to C
onto a smooth complete curve of genus at least
2
as well as a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon B\to X
such that
\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is induced by
f
.

(3)

The variety
X
is of general type, and
\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})=\dim X
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.21
,
\mathscr{F}^{*}
is a nef vector bundle. This in turn implies that
\Omega_{X}^{1}
is nef since
\mathscr{N}^{*}
is nef by assumption. In particular,
K_{X}
is nef.
On the other hand,
K_{X}
is abundant by Proposition
6.3
. Then

[
Kaw85
, Theorem 1.1]
applies to show that
K_{X}
is semiample. Moreover, by
[
Hör13
, Theorem 1.2]
, replacing
X
by a finite étale cover, if necessary, we may assume that
X
is an abelian scheme over a smooth projective variety
Y
with ample canonical divisor (see Lemma
2.17
). Let us denote by
f\colon X\to Y
the natural morphism and by
\Sigma
any section of
f
. Set
m:=\dim Y
.

Suppose that
m=0
. Then
X
is an abelian variety. This easily implies

\mathscr{N}^{*}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
. As a consequence,
\mathscr{F}
is a linear foliation on
X
, and we are in case (1) of Theorem
7.2
.

Suppose from now on that
m\geqslant 1
.

Set
\mathscr{E}:=f_{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}
so that

\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{E}
. By
[
VZ07
, Theorem 1 and Remark 2]
, we have

(7.3)

2\mu(\mathscr{E})\leqslant\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y}),

where
\mu
denotes the slope with respect to
K_{Y}
, which is ample by construction.
A celebrated theorem proved by Aubin and Yau moreover asserts that
Y
is Kähler-Einstein. As a consequence, the tangent bundle
T_{Y}
is polystable with respect to
K_{Y}
by a result proved by Kobayashi and Lübke independently.

The short exact sequences

0\to\mathscr{N}^{*}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}\to\mathscr{F}^{*}\to 0\quad\textup{and}\quad 0\to f^{*}\Omega^{1}_{Y}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}\to 0

give

(7.4)

\frac{\mu(\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma})+(n-1)\mu(\mathscr{F}^{*}|_{\Sigma})}{n}=\mu(\Omega^{1}_{X}|_{\Sigma})=\frac{m\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})+(n-m)\mu(\mathscr{E})}{n}.

Suppose first that the composition
\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}|_{\Sigma}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}|_{\Sigma}\cong\mathscr{E}
vanishes. Then

\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma}\subseteq f^{*}\Omega^{1}_{Y}|_{\Sigma}\cong\Omega^{1}_{Y}

and there exists a surjective morphism

\mathscr{F}^{*}|_{\Sigma}\twoheadrightarrow f^{*}\mathscr{E}|_{\Sigma}\cong\mathscr{E}.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.14
, the vector bundle
\mathscr{F}|_{\Sigma}
is numerically projectively flat. It follows that

\mu(\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma})\leqslant\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})\quad\textup{and}\quad\mu(\mathscr{F}^{*}|_{\Sigma})\leqslant\mu(\mathscr{E}).

Then Equation (
7.4
) yields

m\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})+(n-m)\mu(\mathscr{E})=\mu(\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma})+(n-1)\mu(\mathscr{F}^{*}|_{\Sigma})\leqslant\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})+(n-1)\mu(\mathscr{E}),

and hence, using Equation (
7.3
), we obtain

(m-1)\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})\leqslant(m-1)\mu(\mathscr{E})\leqslant\frac{m-1}{2}\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y}).

This immediately implies
m=1
since
\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})>0
. Moreover, we have
\mathscr{N}^{*}=f^{*}\Omega^{1}_{Y}\subset\Omega_{X}^{1}
. As a consequence,
\mathscr{F}
is induced by
f
, and we are in case (2) of Theorem
7.2
.

Suppose finally that the composition
\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}|_{\Sigma}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}|_{\Sigma}\cong\mathscr{E}
is non-zero. Then the composition
\mathscr{F}|_{\Sigma}\to T_{X}|_{\Sigma}\to f^{*}T_{Y}|_{\Sigma}\cong T_{Y}
is generically surjective. Since
the vector bundle
\mathscr{F}|_{\Sigma}
is numerically projectively flat, we have

(7.5)

\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})\leqslant\mu(\mathscr{F}^{*}|_{\Sigma}).

Together with Equations (
7.3
)
and (
7.4
) we obtain

2\mu(\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma})+2(n-1)\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})\leqslant 2\mu(\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma})+2(n-1)\mu(\mathscr{F}^{*}|_{\Sigma})=\\
2m\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})+2(n-m)\mu(\mathscr{E})\leqslant 2m\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})+(n-m)\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})=(n+m)\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y}),

and thus

(n-2-m)\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})+2\mu(\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma})\leqslant 0.

Notice that
\mu(\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma})\geqslant 0
since
\mathscr{N}^{*}
is nef
and that
\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})>0
since
m\geqslant 1
. In particular,
m\in\{n-2,n-1,n\}
.

Claim 7.3
.

There exists a line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on
Y
such that
\mathscr{N}^{*}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. The composition
\mathscr{N}^{*}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}
is non-zero by our current assumption and induces an injective map
\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{F}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus n-m}
. Since
\mathscr{N}^{*}
is nef, we have
\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{F}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}
.
The claim now follows from Lemma
3.9
.
∎

Suppose that
m=n
. Then
X=Y
is of general type and
\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})=\dim X
by Lemma
7.4
below. We are in case (3) of Theorem
7.2
.

Suppose that
m\in\{n-2,n-1\}
. By Lemma
7.1
, after replacing
X
by a finite étale cover, we may assume that
X\cong Y\times A
for some abelian variety
A
of dimension
n-m\in\{1,2\}
and that
f
is induced by the projection onto
Y
. Since the composition
\mathscr{N}^{*}|_{\Sigma}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}|_{\Sigma}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}|_{\Sigma}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma}^{\oplus n-m}
is non-zero and
\mathscr{N}^{*}
is nef, we have
\mathscr{N}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
.

If
m=n-1
, then
\mathscr{F}
is everywhere transverse to
f
. This implies that
T_{Y}
is numerically projectively flat. But then
K_{Y}\equiv 0
by
[
JR13
, Theorem 0.1]
, yielding a contradiction.

Suppose finally that
m=n-2
. Then Equations (
7.3
),
(
7.4
) and (
7.5
) give
\mu(\mathscr{F}^{*}|_{\Sigma})=\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})
and

2\mu(\mathscr{E})=\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})
. Now this is impossible since
\mu(\mathscr{E})=0
and
\mu(\Omega^{1}_{Y})>0
. This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Suppose in addition that
X
is of general type. Then
\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})=\dim X
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction and assume that
\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant\dim X-1
. By Corollary
4.7
,
\mathscr{N}^{*}
is nef. It follows that
\Omega_{X}^{1}
is nef since
\mathscr{F}^{*}
is also nef by Lemma
2.21
. This implies that
X
contains no rational curve, and hence
K_{X}
is an ample divisor. A celebrated theorem proved independently to Aubin and Yau then asserts that
X
is Kähler-Einstein. As a consequence, the tangent bundle
T_{X}
is polystable with respect to
K_{X}
by a result proved by Kobayashi and Lübke independently.

Set
n:=\dim X
. Then

K_{\mathscr{F}}^{n}=(K_{X}+c_{1}(\mathscr{N}))^{n}=K_{X}^{n}+nK_{X}^{n-1}\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{N})=0

since

K_{\mathscr{F}}=K_{X}+c_{1}(\mathscr{N})
by the adjunction formula and

c_{1}(\mathscr{N})^{2}=0
by Lemma
3.1
.
It follows that
\mu(\mathscr{N})=\mu(T_{X})
, where
\mu
denotes the slope with respect to
K_{X}
, and hence
T_{X}\cong\mathscr{F}\oplus\mathscr{N}
since
T_{X}
is polystable.

By Lemma
7.5
(1) below, we then have

(2nc_{2}(\mathscr{F})-(n-1)c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2})\cdot K_{X}^{n-2}\geqslant 0.

Since
c_{2}(\mathscr{F})=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}c_{1}(\mathscr{F})
(see Lemma
2.7
), we obtain

c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}\cdot K_{X}^{n-2}=0.

But this contradicts Lemma
7.5
(2) below, finishing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold with
K_{X}
ample, and let
\Omega^{1}_{X}:=\mathscr{A}\oplus\mathscr{B}
be a decomposition of
\Omega^{1}_{X}
into locally free subsheaves. Suppose that
\mathscr{A}
has rank
a\geqslant 2
, and set
n:=\dim X
.
Then

(1)

(2(a+1)c_{2}(\mathscr{A})-ac_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{2})\cdot K_{X}^{n-2}\geqslant 0
, and

(2)

c_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{2}\cdot K_{X}^{n-2}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{E}:=\Omega_{X}^{1}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{X}
equipped with the Higgs field
\vartheta
defined by

\Omega_{X}^{1}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{X}\ni\alpha+f\mapsto(0+1)\otimes\alpha\in(\Omega_{X}^{1}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{X})\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}.

Notice that
\vartheta(\mathscr{A}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{X})\subset(\mathscr{A}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{X})\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}
so that
(\mathscr{G}:=\mathscr{A}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{X},\vartheta|_{\mathscr{G}})
is a Higgs bundle as well.

Now, a theorem proved independently to Aubin and Yau then asserts that
X
is Kähler-Einstein. As a consequence, the tangent bundle
T_{X}
is polystable with respect to
K_{X}
by a result proved by Kobayashi and Lübke independently. This in turn implies that
\mathscr{A}
is polystable with respect to
K_{X}
with
\mu(\mathscr{A})=\mu(\Omega_{X}^{1})>0
, where
\mu
denotes the slope with respect to
K_{X}
.

Next we show that the Higgs bundle
(\mathscr{G},\vartheta|_{\mathscr{G}})
is stable with respect to
K_{X}
. Let
\mathscr{H}\subset\mathscr{G}
be a saturated subsheaf of rank
0<r<a+1
preserved by
\vartheta|_{\mathscr{G}}
, and let
p\colon\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}
be the composition
\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{A}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{X}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}
.
Observe that
p
is not the zero map since
\mathscr{H}
is preserved by
\vartheta|_{\mathscr{G}}
by assumption.

If
p
is injective, then
\mu(\mathscr{H})\leqslant 0<\mu(\mathscr{G})=\frac{a}{a+1}\mu(\mathscr{A})
. Suppose that
p
is not injective. Note that we must have
r\geqslant 2
and
\mu(\textup{Im}\,p)\leqslant 0
. Moreover,

\mu(\textup{Ker}\,p)\leqslant\mu(\mathscr{A})
since
\textup{Ker}\,p\subseteq\mathscr{A}
and

\mathscr{A}
is polystable with respect to
K_{X}
. Then, we have

\mu(\mathscr{H})=\frac{(r-1)\mu(\textup{Ker}\,p)+\mu(\textup{Im}\,p)}{r}\leqslant\frac{r-1}{r}\mu(\mathscr{A})=\frac{r-1}{r}\frac{a+1}{a}\mu(\mathscr{G})\leqslant\frac{a^{2}-1}{a^{2}}\mu(\mathscr{G})<\mu(\mathscr{G}),

proving our claim.

Item (1) now follows from
[
Sim88
, Theorem 1 and Proposition 3.4]
.

Let
b\geqslant 0
denote the rank of
\mathscr{B}
. By
[
Bea00
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have

c_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{a+1}\equiv 0
and
c_{1}(\mathscr{B})^{b+1}\equiv 0
. Then

c_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{2}\cdot K_{X}^{n-2}=c_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{2}\cdot(c_{1}(\mathscr{A})+c_{1}(\mathscr{B}))^{n-2}=\binom{n-2}{b}c_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{a}\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{B})^{b},

and

K_{X}^{n}=\binom{n}{b}c_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{a}\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{B})^{b}.

It follows that
c_{1}(\mathscr{A})^{2})\cdot K_{X}^{n-2}>0
since
K_{X}^{n}>0
by assumption, proving Item (2).
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

Setting and notation as in

. Suppose in addition that

By Proposition
6.3
,

is abundant. Let

[
KM98
]
and
[

]

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n\geqslant 2
with
\kappa(X)=\nu(X)\geqslant 0
. Suppose that there exists a smooth prime divisor
B\subset X
with
K_{B}\equiv 0
and
B^{2}\equiv 0
. Then
\nu(X)\leqslant 1
.

Proof.

By
[
GL13
, Theorem 4.3]
, we may run a minimal model program for
X
and end with a minimal model. Hence, there exists a birational map
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
, where
Y
is a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective variety with
K_{Y}
nef. Moreover,
Y
has terminal singularities, and
\varphi^{-1}
does not contract any divisor. Let
p\colon Z\to X
and
q\colon Z\to Y
be a common resolution of singularities. Let
C
be the strict transform of
B
in
Z
.
If
B
is contracted by
\varphi
, then it is uniruled by
[
Kaw91
, Theorem 1]
, yielding a contradiction since
K_{B}\equiv 0
by assumption. Set
D:=\varphi_{*}B
.

Let
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}
be a positive number such that
\varphi
is a MMP for
K_{X}+\varepsilon B
. By
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.38]
, there exists an effective
q
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
F
such that

p^{*}(K_{X}+\varepsilon B)=q^{*}(K_{Y}+\varepsilon D)+F.

Moreover, by the negativity lemma, there exists an effective
q
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G
such that

p^{*}B+G=q^{*}D

since
B
is nef. Notice also that
K_{X}|_{B}\equiv 0
by the adjunction formula
K_{B}=(K_{X}+B)|_{B}
.
We then have

0\equiv p^{*}(K_{X}+\varepsilon B)|_{C}=q^{*}(K_{Y}+\varepsilon D)|_{C}+F|_{C}\equiv(q^{*}K_{Y})|_{C}+\varepsilon G|_{C}+F|_{C}.

Since
G|_{C}+F|_{C}
is effective and
(q^{*}K_{Y})|_{C}

is nef, we have

K_{Y}|_{D}\equiv 0\quad\textup{and}\quad G\cap C=\emptyset.

This immediately implies
D^{2}\equiv 0
. Now, we have
\kappa(X)=\kappa(Y)
and
\nu(X)=\nu(Y)
by
[
Nak04
, Proposition V.2.7]
, and hence
\kappa(Y)=\nu(Y)
. Then

[
Kaw85
, Theorem 1.1]
applies to show that
K_{Y}
is semiample. We denote the Iitaka fibration of
Y
by

f\colon Y\to T
. Since
K_{Y}|_{D}\equiv 0
, we have
\dim f(D)=0
.
If
\dim T=0
, then
\nu(X)=0
and there is nothing to show. If
\dim T\geqslant 1
, then Lemma
3.13

applies to show
\nu(X)=1
.
This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

8.
The nef reduction map

In this section we provide a technical tool for the proof of Theorem
9.1
below. We briefly recall the relevant notion first. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
\mathscr{L}
be a nef line bundle on
X
. Then there exists an almost proper, dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
such that

(1)

\mathscr{L}|_{F}\equiv 0
for a general fiber
F
of
f
,

(2)

for every general point
x\in X
and every irreducible curve
C
passing through
x
with
\dim f(C)=1
, we have
\mathscr{L}\cdot C>0
.

The map
f
is unique up to birational equivalence of
Y
.
We refer to it as the
nef reduction map
of
\mathscr{L}
. The dimension of
Y
is an invariant of
\mathscr{L}
called the
nef dimension
. We write
\textup{nd}(\mathscr{L}):=\dim Y
. We have
\kappa(\mathscr{L})\leqslant\nu(\mathscr{L})\leqslant\textup{nd}(\mathscr{L})
.
We refer to
[
BCE+02
]
for further explanations concerning these notions. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a regular foliation on a complex projective manifold and suppose that
\mathscr{F}
satisfies all the conditions listed in Setup
2.19
. Recall from Lemma
2.21
that
K_{\mathscr{F}}
is nef. Then Proposition
8.1
below gives sufficient conditions that guarantee
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})<\dim X
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Setting and notation as in
2.19
. Suppose in addition that
K_{X}
is nef and that
\mathscr{N}
is pseudo-effective. Set
m:=\min(\nu(K_{X}),n-2)
. Then

\nu(K_{X})\leqslant n-1
and
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant m+1\leqslant n-1
.

Proof.

Let us first show
\nu(K_{X})\leqslant n-1
. Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
. By
[
GT22
, Theorem B]
, we have

\Big(c_{2}(X)-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}c_{1}(X)^{2}\Big)\cdot K_{X}^{m}\cdot H^{n-2-m}\geqslant 0.

We have

c_{2}(X)=c_{2}(\mathscr{F})+c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{N})\quad\text{and}\quad c_{1}(X)^{2}\equiv c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}+2c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{N})

since
c_{1}(\mathscr{N})^{2}\equiv 0
by Lemma
3.1
. In additon, we have

c_{2}(\mathscr{F})\equiv\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}

by
[
JR13
, Proposition 1.1]
.
We obtain

(8.1)

\Big(-\frac{1}{n^{2}-1}c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}+\frac{1}{n+1}c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{N})\Big)\cdot K_{X}^{m}\cdot H^{n-2-m}\geqslant 0.

On the other hand,
K_{\mathscr{F}}=-c_{1}(\mathscr{F})
is nef by Lemma
2.21
. This immediately implies

-c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}\cdot K_{X}^{m}\cdot H^{n-2-m}\leqslant 0

and

c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{N})\cdot K_{X}^{m}\cdot H^{n-2-m}\leqslant 0

since
\mathscr{N}
is pseudo-effective by assumption. Equation (
8.1
) then gives

(8.2)

c_{1}(\mathscr{F})^{2}\cdot K_{X}^{m}\cdot H^{n-2-m}=c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{N})\cdot K_{X}^{m}\cdot H^{n-2-m}=0.

Therefore, we have
K_{X}^{m+2}\cdot H^{n-2-m}=0
, and hence
K_{X}^{n}=0
since
m=\min(\nu(K_{X}),n-2)
. This shows
\nu(K_{X})\leqslant n-1
.

Let us now show
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant m+1
.
If
\mathscr{F}
is algebraically integrable, then
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant 1
by Proposition
3.20
. Suppose from now on that
\mathscr{F}
is not algebraically integrable.

By Proposition
6.3
and
[
Kaw85
, Theorem 1.1]
together,
K_{X}
is semiample. We denote the Iitaka fibration by

f\colon X\to Y
. Let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. Then
F
is smooth. By construction, there exists an ample divisor
A
on
Y
such that
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}A
. In particular, we have
K_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
by the adjunction formula.

Suppose first that
m=\dim Y=\nu(K_{X})\leqslant n-2
.

Then
c_{2}(T_{X}|_{F})\cdot H|_{F}^{n-2-m}=\frac{1}{A^{m}}c_{2}(X)\cdot K_{X}^{m}\cdot H^{n-2-m}=0
by Equation (
8.2
). The exact sequence

0\to T_{F}\to T_{X}|_{F}\to\mathscr{N}_{F/X}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus m}\to 0

then gives
c_{2}(T_{F})\cdot H|_{F}^{n-2-m}=0
.
As a classical consequence of Yau’s theorem on the existence of a Kähler-Einstein metric,
F
is then covered by a complex torus (see
[
Kob87
, Chapter IV Corollary 4.15]
). Thus, there exist an abelian variety
F_{1}
of dimension at least
2

and a finite étale cover
\eta\colon F_{1}\to F
. Then
[
GKP21
, Proposition 3.3]

applies to show that there exists a line bundle
\mathscr{L}_{1}
on
F_{1}
as well as a flat vector bundle
\mathscr{G}_{1}
on
F_{1}
such that
\eta^{*}(\mathscr{F}|_{F})\cong\mathscr{G}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{1}
. In addition,
\mathscr{G}_{1}
admits a filtration
0=F_{0}\mathscr{G}_{1}\subsetneq F_{1}\mathscr{G}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_{n-1}\mathscr{G}_{1}=\mathscr{G}_{1}

by flat subbundles
F_{i}\mathscr{G}_{1}
with
\textup{rank}\,F_{i}\mathscr{G}_{1}=i
. By Equation (
8.2
), we have

(8.3)

c_{1}(\eta^{*}(\mathscr{F}|_{F}))^{2}\cdot\eta^{*}(H|_{F})^{n-2-m}=0.

If
K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F}\equiv 0
, then
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant\dim\textup{Iit}(X)=m\leqslant m+1
.

Suppose that
K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F}\not\equiv 0
.

If
m=0
, then
F=X
and there exists a generically surjective morphism

\Omega^{1}_{F_{1}}\to\eta^{*}(\mathscr{F}|_{F})^{*}
. This immediately implies
h^{0}(F_{1},\mathscr{O}_{F_{1}}(\eta^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F})))\geqslant 1
. Then
[
Deb99
, Théorème VI.5.1]
applies to show that
the line bundle
\mathscr{O}_{F_{1}}(\eta^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F}))
is semiample. Notice that

\nu(\mathscr{O}_{F_{1}}(\eta^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F})))=1
by Equation (
8.3
). This easily implies
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})=1=m+1
.

Suppose that
m\geqslant 1
. Since
\mathscr{F}
is not algebraically integrable by our current assumption, the composition
\mathscr{F}|_{F}\to T_{X}|_{F}\to\mathscr{N}_{F/X}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\oplus m}
is non-zero. As a consequence, there is an index
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1

such that the line bundle
\mathscr{Q}_{i}:=\big((F_{i+1}\mathscr{G}_{1}/F_{i}\mathscr{G}_{1})\otimes\mathscr{L}_{1}\big)^{*}
has a non-zero global section. Then
[
Deb99
, Théorème VI.5.1]
again applies to show that
\mathscr{Q}_{i}
is semiample. We have
\nu(\mathscr{Q}_{i})=1
since

c_{1}(\mathscr{Q}_{i})\equiv c_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1})\equiv\frac{1}{n-1}\eta^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{F})
. This implies
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant\dim Y+1=m+1
.

Suppose finally that
\nu(K_{X})=n-1
. In particular,
m=n-2
.

Then
F
is a smooth curve of genus
1
. By Equation (
8.2
), we have

K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot F=\frac{1}{A^{n-1}}K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot K_{X}^{n-1}=\frac{1}{A^{n-1}}c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot(c_{1}(\mathscr{F})+c_{1}(\mathscr{N}))\cdot K_{X}^{n-2}=0.

As before, this implies
\textup{nd}(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant n-1=m+1
, completing the proof of Proposition
8.1
.
∎

Setting and notation as in
2.19

K_{X}

Dru14
, Theorem 1.1]
), we conclude that
B_{i}
is smooth.

By Proposition
6.3
, we have
\kappa(X)=\nu(X)\geqslant 0
. Applying
Proposition
3.14
to
(X,\sum_{i\in I}B_{i})
, we see that

K_{\mathscr{F}}
is semiample with
\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})\leqslant 1
. This proves that
m=1
.

By Lemma
3.23
and Lemma
2.21
together, the vector bundle
\mathscr{F}
is then numerically flat, and hence
K_{\mathscr{F}}=0
, yielding a contradiction. This
finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

10.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose first that
K_{X}
is not nef. Then there exists a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle structure
\varphi\colon X\to Y
onto a finite étale quotient of an abelian variety, and
\mathscr{F}
induces a flat Ehresmann connection on
\varphi
by Theorem
5.1
.

Suppose from now on that
K_{X}
is nef.

If
\mathscr{N}
is pseudo-effective, then Theorem
9.1
applies to show that
K_{\mathscr{F}}\equiv 0
. By Proposition
3.18
, there exists an abelian variety
A
and a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon A\to X
such that
\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is a linear foliation on
A
.

Suppose finally that
\mathscr{N}
is not pseudo-effective. Then
\mathscr{N}^{*}
is nef by Theorem
1.2
. Then Theorem
7.2
applies to show that either there exists a smooth complete curve
B
of genus at least
2
as well as an abelian variety
A
, and a finite étale cover
\gamma\colon B\times A\to X
such that
\gamma^{-1}\mathscr{F}
is induced by the projection morphism
B\times A\to B
, or
X
is of general type and
\nu(K_{\mathscr{F}})=\dim X
. In the latter case,
\Omega_{X}^{1}
is nef by Lemma
2.21
, and hence
K_{X}
is ample. In addition,
\kappa(K_{\mathscr{F}})=\dim X
by Lemma
7.4
. This finishes the proof of theorem.
∎

Remark 10.1
.

If we relax the integrability condition, then the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
is false. Indeed, let
E
be an elliptic curve, and let
B
be an abelian variety of dimension
\dim B=n-1\geqslant 1
. Set
A:=E\times B
. Let
\mathscr{M}
be a line bundle of degree
2
on
E
, and set
\mathscr{L}:=\textup{pr}_{E}^{*}\mathscr{M}^{*}
. Let
(s_{i},t_{i})\in H^{0}(A,\mathscr{L}^{*})^{\times 2}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Suppose that the zero sets of the
s_{i}
and the
t_{i}
are pairwise disjoint. Write
T_{A}=\oplus_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}\mathscr{O}_{A}v_{i}
. Then the sections
s_{i}
and
t_{i}
give an injective map of vector bundles
\mathscr{L}\subset\mathscr{O}_{A}v_{i}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{A}v_{i+1}
. In addition, the induced map

\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{L}^{\oplus n-1}\to T_{A}
is an injective map of vector bundles as well. The vector bundle is obviously numerically projectively flat.

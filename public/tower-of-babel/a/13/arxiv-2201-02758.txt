Self-orthogonal generalized twisted Reed-Solomon codes
Canze Zhu, Qunying Liao

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, by calculating the dual code of the Schur square for the standard twisted Reed-Solomon code, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for the generalized twisted Reed-Solomon code with
h+t\leq k-1
to be self-orthogonal, where
k
is dimension,
h
is hook and
t
is twist. And then, we show that there is no self-orthogonal generalized twisted Reed-Solomon code under some conditions. Furthermore, several classes of self-orthogonal generalized twisted Reed-Solomon codes are constructed, and some of these codes are non-GRS self-orthogonal MDS codes or NMDS codes.

Keywords.

generalized twisted Reed-Solomon code; the Schur product; non-GRS self-orthogonal MDS code; self-orthogonal NMDS code.

1
Introduction

An
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum (Hamming) distance
d
and length
n
. For
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
,
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})

\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, the Euclidean inner product is defined as
\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle_{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}
. And then
the dual code of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{c}^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}~|~\langle\mathbf{c}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{c}\rangle_{E}=0,\text{for any}~\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

We called
\mathcal{C}
is self-orthogonal if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
. Especially,
\mathcal{C}
is self-dual if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
.

Both self-orthogonal codes and MDS codes have widely practical applications. On the one hand, in quantum information theory,
self-orthogonal codes can be used to construct pure additive quantum codes
[
25
]
, quantum stabilizer codes
[
19
]
,
and so on. On the other hand, the MDS code can correct maximal number of errors for a given code rate
[
15
]
. In addition, MDS codes are closely connected to combinatorial designs and finite geometry
[
15
,
24
]
. For the above reasons, self-orthogonal MDS
codes have been attracted much attentions
[
6
,
13
]
. As a special case, the self-dual MDS code is of particular importance due to
its optimality and self-duality, a lot of these codes were constructed in various ways
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
27
,
28
,
30
]
, especially, generalized Reed-Solomon (in short, GRS) codes are a class of MDS codes and a lot of self-dual MDS codes are constructed from GRS codes
[
7
,
16
,
21
,
31
,
32
]
.

The near MDS (in short, NMDS) code is introduced in
[
5
]
. A linear code
\mathcal{C}
is called an near MDS code if both
\mathcal{C}
and its dual code have one singleton defect from being an MDS code. NMDS codes approximate maximal minimum Hamming distance for a given code rate. Futhermore, NMDS codes also have been applicated in secret sharing scheme
[
29
]
, the index coding problem
[
33
]
, the informed source coding problem
[
34
]
, and so on. From both theoretical and practical point of views, to study self-orthogonal NMDS is interesting.
In 2017, inspired by twisted Gabidulin codes
[
26
]
, Beelen, et al. firstly introduced the twisted Reed-Solomon (in short, TRS) codes, and also showed that TRS codes could be well decoded
[
1
,
2
]
. Different from GRS codes, a generalized twisted Reed-Solomon (in short, GTRS) code is not necessarily MDS
[
1
]
. The authors also showed that some of GTRS codes are not GRS codes
[
3
,
22
]
. Furthermore, basing on GTRS codes, Lavauzelle, et al. presented an efficient key-recovery attack used in the McEliece cryptosystem
[
20
]
. GTRS codes are also used to construct linear complementary dual (in short, LCD) MDS codes by their applications in cryptography
[
14
,
22
]
. In 2021, Liu et, al. gave the parity-check matrix for the GTRS code with dimension
k
, hook
k-1
and twist
1
, and then constructed several classes of self-dual MDS or NMDS codes from these GTRS codes
[
23
]
. Recently, the authors generalized Liu et, al.’s work,
presented the parity-check matrix for the GTRS code with any given dimension
k
, hook
h
and twist
t
, gave a sufficient and necessary condition for the GTRS code with
h+t=k
and
h\geq t
to be self-dual, and then also constructed some self-dual codes with small defect
[
35
]
.

In this paper, by calculating the dual code of the Schur square for the standard TRS code, a sufficient and necessary condition for the GTRS code with
h+t\leq k-1
to be self-orthogonal is given. And then, basing on this sufficient and necessary condition, we prove that there is no self-orthogonal GTRS code under some conditions. Furthermore, we construct several classes of self-orthogonal GTRS codes, especially, some of these codes are proved to be non-GRS self-orthogonal MDS codes or NMDS codes.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2, some basic notations and results about GTRS codes, GRS codes and the Shur product are given. In section 3, the dual code of the Schur square for the standard TRS code is given. In section 4, a sufficient and necessary condition for the GTRS code with
h+t\leq k-1
to be self-orthogonal is obtained, and then several classes of self-orthogonal GTRS codes are constructed. In section 5, we conclude the whole paper and give the further study.

2.1
GTRS codes

In this subsection, we give some notations and lemmas about GTRS codes and GRS codes, respectively.

Definition 2.1

For a
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, let
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}})
and
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
, then the linear code with length
n
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})=\{(v_{1}f({\alpha_{1}}),\ldots,v_{n}f(\alpha_{n}))~|~f(x)\in\mathcal{V}\}.

Remark 2.1

(1) For
i=1,\ldots,k
and
f_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, let

\mathcal{V}=\big\{a_{1}f_{1}(x)+\cdots+a_{k}f_{k}(x)~|~a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}~(i=1,\ldots,k)\big\}.

By the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})=\big\langle\big(v_{1}f_{i}({\alpha_{1}}),\ldots,v_{n}f_{i}(\alpha_{n})\big)~(i=1,\ldots,k)\big\rangle.

(2) For convinience, we denote
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}(\bm{v})=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
for
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q})
.

Definition 2.2

[
1
]

Let
h\leq k-1
and
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Define the set of
(k,t,h,\eta)
-twisted polynomials as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}=\bigg\{f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}x^{i}+\eta a_{h}x^{k-1+t}~|~a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}~(i=0,\ldots,k-1)\bigg\},

which is a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. We call
h
hook and
t
twist.

Definition 2.3

[
1
]

For
h\leq k-1
and
k+t\leq n
, let
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}})
and
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is called the GTRS code. Especially,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
is called the TRS code.

Remark 2.2

For
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q})
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
is the standard TRS code.

Remark 2.3

By the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})=\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{h}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k-1+t}(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}\backslash\{h\})\big\rangle.

Remark 2.4

For any
f(x)\in\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}
, we have
\deg f(x)\leq k-1+t<n
, thus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is with dimension
k
and minimum distance
d\geq n-k-t+1
.

Lemma 2.1
(Lemma 1,
[
14
]
)

For
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}})
and
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, let

T_{k}(\bm{\alpha})=\Big\{(-1)^{k}\prod_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}^{-1}~\bigg|~\forall I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}\text{~with~}|I|=k\Big\}.

Then we have

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,1,0,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is an MDS code if and only if
\eta\notin T_{k}(\bm{\alpha})
,

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,1,0,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is an NMDS code if and only if
\eta\in T_{k}(\bm{\alpha})
.

Lemma 2.2
(Lemma 12,
[
2
]
)

Let
q
be odd and
3\leq k\leq\frac{q-1}{2}-2
. If
n>\frac{q+1}{2}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,1,0,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is not MDS.

Lemma 2.3
(Theorem 17,
[
1
]
)

Let
\mathbb{F}_{s}
be a proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{F}_{s}^{n}
. If
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{s}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is MDS.

Definition 2.4

[
24
]

Let
\mathcal{V}_{k}=\{f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]|\deg f(x)\leq k-1\}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is called the GRS code. Especially,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
is called the RS code.

Remark 2.5

(1) By the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})=\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}\backslash\{h\})\big\rangle.

(2) For convinience, we denote
\mathcal{RS}_{k}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
for
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q})
.

Lemma 2.4
(Lemma 2.3,
[
16
]
)

\mathcal{RS}_{k}^{\perp}=\mathcal{RS}_{q-k}
.

2.2
The Schur product

The Schur product is defined as follows.

Definition 2.5

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, the Schur product of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
is
defined as

x*y:=(x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{n}y_{n}),

The Schur product of two
q
-ary codes
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
with length
n
is
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}*\mathcal{C}_{2}=\langle\mathbf{c}_{1}*\mathbf{c}_{2}~|~\mathbf{c}_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}\rangle.

Especially, for a code
\mathcal{C}
, we call
\mathcal{C}^{2}:=\mathcal{C}*\mathcal{C}
the Schur square for
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 2.6

For any two linear codes
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\langle\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{k_{1}}\rangle
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\langle\bm{w}_{1},\ldots,\bm{w}_{k_{2}}\rangle
with
\bm{v}_{i},\bm{w}_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}~(i=1,\ldots,k_{1},~j=1,\ldots,k_{2})
, then by the definition of the Schur product, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}*\mathcal{C}_{2}=\langle\bm{v}_{i}*\bm{w}_{j}~(i=1,\ldots,k_{1},~j=1,\ldots,k_{2})\rangle.

(2.1)

Remark 2.7

For any
k\leq\frac{n+1}{2}
,
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{v}
, it is easy to see that

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k}}^{2}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{2k-1}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
, and then

\dim\big(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k}}^{2}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})\big)=\dim\big(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k}}^{2}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})\big)=2k-1.

3
\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}
and Its Dual Code

\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
and
\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}
are given in Lemmas
3.1
-
3.2
, respectively.

Lemma 3.1

For
t+h\leq k-1
,
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k-1},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{4k-4}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{3k-3}\backslash\{0,1,2k-1\})\big\rangle,&\text{if~}h=0\text{~and~}t=k-1;\\
\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2+t}\backslash\{0\})\big\rangle,&\text{if~}h=0\text{~and~}t\leq k-2;\\
\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2+t}\cup\{2k+2t-2\})\big\rangle,&\text{if~}h\geq 1\text{~and~}t+h\leq k-1.\\
\end{cases}

Proof
. By Remarks
2.1
and
2.7
, one has

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})\\
=&\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{i}*\bm{\alpha}^{j},\bm{\alpha}^{l}*(\bm{\alpha}^{h}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k-1+t}),(\bm{\alpha}^{h}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k-1+t})*(\bm{\alpha}^{h}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k-1+t})(i,j,l\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}\backslash\{h\})\big\rangle\\
=&\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{i+j},\bm{\alpha}^{h+l}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k-1+t+l},\bm{\alpha}^{2h}+2\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k-1+t+h}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(i,j,l\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}\backslash\{h\})\big\rangle.\end{aligned}

By the assumption
t+h\leq k-1
, we have the following five cases.

Case 1
. If
h=0
and
t=k-1
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{l}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k-2+l},\bm{1}+2\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k-2}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{4k-4}~(s\in\{2,\ldots,2k-2\},~l\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k-1},\bm{\alpha}^{2k-2+l},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{4k-4}~(s\in\{2,\ldots,2k-2\},~l\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{l},\bm{\alpha}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k-1},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{4k-4}~(s\in\{2,\ldots,2k-2\},~l\in\{2k,\ldots,3k-3\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k-1},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{4k-4}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{3k-3}\backslash\{0,1,2k-1\})\big\rangle.

Case 2
. If
h=0
and
t\leq k-2
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{l}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1+l},\bm{1}+2\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\{2,\ldots,2k-2\},~l\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k+t},\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1+l},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\{2,\ldots,2k-2\},~l\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{l},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\{1,2,\ldots,2k-2\},~l\in\{k+1+t,\ldots,2k-2+t\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{1}+\eta^{2}\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2+t}\backslash\{0\})\big\rangle.

Case 3
. If
h=1
and
t\leq k-2
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{l+1}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1+l},~2\bm{\alpha}^{k+t}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}(s\in\{0,2,\ldots,2k-2\},l\in\{0,2,\ldots,k-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1},\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1+l},2\bm{\alpha}^{k+t}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\{0,2,\ldots,2k-2\},~l\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{l},\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2},~l\in\{k+1+t,\ldots,2k-2+t\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2+t}\cup\{2k+2t-2\})\big\rangle.

Case 4
. If
2\leq h\leq k-3
and

, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{h+l}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1+l},~2\bm{\alpha}^{k+t+h-1}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2},~l\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}\backslash\{h\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{k+t-1+l},\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2},~l\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}\backslash\{h\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{l},\bm{\alpha}^{2k+2t-2}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2},~l\in\{k+t-1,\ldots,2k-2+t\}\backslash\{k+t+h-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2+t}\cup\{2k+2t-2\})\big\rangle.

Case 5
. If
h=k-2
and
t=1
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{k-2+l}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{k+l},~2\bm{\alpha}^{2k-2}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k}~(s\in\{0,\ldots,2k-4,2k-2\},~l\in\{0,\ldots,k-3,k-1\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s},\bm{\alpha}^{k+l},\bm{\alpha}^{2k-3}+\eta\bm{\alpha}^{2k-1},\bm{\alpha}^{2k}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-2},~l\in\{0,\ldots,k-3\})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{\alpha}^{s}~(s\in\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{2k})\big\rangle.

So far, by

-

, we complete the proof.

4
Self-orthogonal GTRS Codes

In this section, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for a GTRS code to be self-orthogonal. Furthermore, we construct some GTRS codes basing on this sufficient and necessary condition.

4.1
A sufficient and necessary condition for a GTRS code to be self-orthogonal

Theorem 4.1

For
2k\leq n\leq q
and
t+h\leq k-1
, let
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}})
and
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{*}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is self-orthogonal if and only if there is a
f(x)\in\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}^{\perp}
, such that

f(\alpha_{i_{j}})=v_{j}^{2}\quad(j=1,\ldots,n)\text{~~and~~}f(\beta)=0\quad(\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}}\}).

Proof
. By the definition,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is self-orthogonal if and only if for any

(v_{1}c_{1,i_{1}},\ldots,v_{n}c_{1,i_{n}}),~(v_{1}c_{2,i_{1}},\ldots,v_{n}c_{2,i_{n}})\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v}),

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(v_{j}c_{1,i_{j}})(v_{j}c_{2,i_{j}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}^{2}c_{1,i_{j}}c_{2,i_{j}}=0.

(4.1)

Note that

v_{j}\neq 0~~(j=1,\ldots,n)\text{~~and~~}(c_{1,i_{1}},\ldots,c_{1,i_{n}}),~(c_{2,i_{1}},\ldots,c_{2,i_{n}})\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1}),

(\ref{ddd})
is equavient to that
there is a
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q}
with
c_{i}=0

(i\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\backslash\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\})
and
v_{j}^{2}=c_{i_{j}}

(j=1,\ldots,n)
such that for any
(c_{1,1},\ldots,c_{1,q}),~(c_{2,1},\ldots,c_{2,q})\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q}c_{j}(c_{1,i_{j}}c_{2,i_{j}})=0.

Namely, there is a
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q})\in\big(\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}\big)^{\perp}
with
Supp(\mathbf{c})=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\}
and
v_{j}^{2}=c_{i_{j}}

(j=1,\ldots,n)
.

Now by the assumption
t+h\leq k-1
and Lemma
3.4
, we complete the proof.
\hfill\Box

In Remark
3.2
, for
k>\frac{q+1-t}{2}
, since
\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}^{\perp}=\{0\}
, thus we have

Corollary 4.1

For
t+h\leq k-1
, there is no self-orthogonal GTRS code with dimension

k>\frac{q-t+1}{2}.

By Theorem
4.1
, it is easy to see that if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is self-orthogonal, then there exists a
f(x)\in\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}^{\perp}
with
q-n
pairwise distinct roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus we have

Corollary 4.2

For any
t+h\leq k-1
with
(h,t)\neq(0,k-1)
and
3\leq k\leq\frac{q-t+1}{2}
, there is no self-orthogonal GTRS code with length
n<2k+t
.

4.2
The existence for a self-orthogonal GTRS code

In this subsetion, we assume
t+h\leq k-1
, and then based on the sufficient and necessary condition for the GTRS code with
h+t\leq k-1
to be self-orthogonal, some self-orthogonal GTRS codes are constructed.

4.2.1
The self-orthogonal GTRS code with dimension
k

(\frac{q-2t+1}{2}<k<\frac{q-t+1}{2})

Theorem 4.2

For
\frac{q-2t+1}{2}<k<\frac{q-t+1}{2}
,
l\leq\frac{q-2k-t}{2}
,
n=q-l
, and
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. If
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}})
and
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
with
v_{j}=\prod\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}}\}}(\alpha_{i_{j}}-\beta)
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v})
is self-orthogonal.

Proof
. Let

f(x)=\Big(\prod\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}}\}}(x-\beta)\Big)^{2},

by the assumptions
\frac{q-2t+1}{2}<k<\frac{q-t+1}{2}

and then

Furthermore, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle v_{j}^{2}=f(\alpha_{i_{j}})\quad(j=1,\ldots,n),

\displaystyle f(\beta)=0\quad(\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{q-l}}\}).

So far, by Theorem

Theorem 4.4

For prime
p
, positive integers
r
and
m
with
3\leq r
and
r\mid m
, if
q=p^{m}
,
n=p^{r}
,
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{*}
,
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}=\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}}\}
and
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}})

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1})
is self-orthogonal if one of the following conditions holds.

(1)

t=1
and
2k+t+2=p^{r}
;

(2)

t
is odd with
t\geq 3
, and
2k+t=p^{r}
;

(3)

t=2
and
2k+t+3=p^{r}
;

(4)

t
is even with
t\geq 4
, and
2k+t+1=p^{r}
.

Proof
. Let

f(x)=\prod\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}}(x-\beta),

then

\deg f(x)=p^{m}-p^{r}\text{~~and~~}f(x)=\frac{x^{p^{m}}-x}{x^{p^{r}}-x}=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{p^{m}-1}{p^{r}-1}-1}x^{i(p^{r}-1)}.

If one of the conditions
(1)
-
(4)
holds, then we have
3\leq k<\frac{p^{m}-t+1}{2}
and

p^{m}-2k-2t+1\neq i(p^{r}-1)+1~\Big(i=0,\ldots,\frac{p^{m}-1}{p^{r}-1}-1\Big).

Thus the coefficient of
x^{p^{m}-2k-2t+1}
in
f(x)
is zero, and so
f(x)\in\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}^{\perp}
.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle f(\alpha_{i_{j}})=\prod_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}}(\alpha_{i_{j}}-\beta)=\prod_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\backslash\{\alpha_{i_{j}}\}}(\alpha_{i_{j}}-\beta)\Big(\prod_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\backslash\{\alpha_{i_{j}}\}}(\alpha_{i_{j}}-\beta)\Big)^{-1}=1,

and

\displaystyle f(\beta)=0\quad(\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}).

Now by Theorem
4.1
, we have the desired results.
\hfill\Box

5
Conclusions and Further Study

In this paper, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for the GTRS code with
h+t\leq k-1
to be self-orthogonal. And then, based on this sufficient and necessary condition, some of non-GRS self-orthogonal MDS codes or NMDS codes are constructed from these GTRS codes.

In Corollary
4.2
, we show that for any
t+h\leq k-1
with
(h,t)\neq(0,k-1)
and
3\leq k\leq\frac{q-t+1}{2}
, there is no self-orthogonal GTRS code with length
n<2k+t
. However, under the condition
(h,t)=(0,k-1)
, we can not obtain the similar result, or construct a self-orthogonal GTRS code with length
n<2k+t
.

Furthermore, for the case
h+t\geq k
, it is interesting to give a sufficient and necessary condition for the GTRS code to be self-orthogonal.

On the logarithmic derivative of characteristic polynomials for random unitary matrices
Fan Ge

Abstract
. Let U ∈ U ⁡ ( N ) U\in U(N) be a random unitary matrix of size N N , distributed with respect to the Haar measure on U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) . Let P ​ ( z ) = P U ​ ( z ) P(z)=P_{U}(z) be the characteristic polynomial of U U . We prove that for z z close to the unit circle, P ′ P ​ ( z ) \frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(z) can be approximated using zeros of P P very close to z z , with a typically controllable error term. 1.
Introduction

Let
\zeta(s)
be the Riemann zeta-function, and let
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
denote a generic nontrivial zero of zeta.
A beautiful result of Selberg
[
19
]
says that for
s=\frac{1}{2}+it
with
t\in[T,2T]
and
s\neq\rho
we can write

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{|t-\gamma|<\frac{1}{\log T}}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+D,

where the error term
D
is in terms of an explicit Dirichlet polynomial and satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|D|^{2K}dt\ll_{K}\log^{2K}T

for all positive integers
K
. In other words,
\zeta^{\prime}/\zeta(s)
can be approximated using zeros very close to
s
, with a typically controllable error. Radziwiłł
[
17
]
observed that Selberg’s argument with some modification also gives that for every constant
0<c\leq 1
and
s
close to the critical line (
0\leq\Re(s)-1/2\ll 1/\log T
, say),

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{|t-\gamma|<\frac{c}{\log T}}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+E,

with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|E|^{2K}dt\ll_{K}\left(\frac{\log T}{c}\right)^{2K}.

(Radziwiłł’s paper assumed the Riemann Hypothesis mainly for other purposes; for (
2
) alone one can show it holds unconditionally.) For applications of these results, see for example Selberg
[
19
]
, Radziwiłł
[
17
]
, and Ge
[
11
]
.

It is well known that characteristic polynomials of the circular unitary ensemble (CUE) models the Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
, with the matrix size
N
about the same as
\log T
. Our first result in this paper is a CUE analogue of (
1
) and (
2
). Throughout, let
U(N)
be the set of unitary matrices of size
N
, equipped with Haar measure. For
U\in U(N)
, write

\displaystyle P(z)=P_{U}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-z_{j})

for the characteristic polynomial of
U
, where

\displaystyle z_{j}=e^{i\theta_{j}}\text{ with }-\pi<\theta_{j}\leq\pi,\text{ for }j=1,2,...,N.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<c\leq 1
be a constant. For
1-\frac{1}{N}\leq z\leq 1
and
z\neq z_{j}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(z)=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|<\frac{c}{N}}\frac{1}{z-z_{j}}+\mathcal{E},

where the error term
\mathcal{E}=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|\geq\frac{c}{N}}\frac{1}{z-z_{j}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{E}|^{2K}\ll_{K}\left(\frac{N}{c}\right)^{2K}

for every positive integer
K
. Here the expection
\mathbb{E}
is over
U(N)
with respect to the Haar measure.

Remark 1
.

Since the Haar density of a configuration of eigenvalues in CUE is invariant under rotations, Theorem
1.1
holds not just for real
z
but for all
1-\frac{1}{N}\leq|z|\leq 1
with obvious modifications in the statement.

Remark 2
.

Bailey, Bettin, Blower, Conrey, Prokhorov, Rubinstein and Snaith
[
3
]
proved that for
K\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

(3)

\displaystyle\int_{U(N)}\left|\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\Big(1-\frac{a}{N}\Big)\right|^{2K}dU\sim\binom{2K-2}{K-1}\frac{N^{2K}}{(2a)^{2K-1}}

where
a\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. They also conjectured a similar asymptotic for zeta, and this was studied in
[
11
]
.
In
[
1
]
Alvarez and Snaith proved analogous results of (
3
) for orthogonal and symplectic random matrices. More recently, Alvarez, Bousseyroux and Snaith
[
2
]
extended the corresponding result for the odd orthogonal ensemble to non-integer moments (namely,
K\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
). In
[
12
]
we shall apply our Theorem
1.1
to obtain asymptotics for real moments analogues of (
3
) in unitary, even orthogonal, and symplectic ensembles.

We also investigate the value distribution of
\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(z)
in the mesoscopic range away from the unit circle, and prove that it obeys a central limit theorem (CLT). To put it in context, Selberg’s central limit theorem states that, roughly speaking, the value distribution of the vector

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\log\log T}}\left(\Re\log\zeta(1/2+it),\Im\log\zeta(1/2+it)\right),\quad t\in[T,2T]

converges to a normal random vector
(X,Y)
as
T
tends to infinity. Here
X
and
Y
are independent and both have mean
0
and variance
1
, and
t
is drawn uniformly from
[T,2T]
. The result also holds for
\sigma+it
in place of
1/2+it
if
\sigma
is close to
1/2
, but the variance changes accordingly. See Tsang’s thesis
[
22
]
for a detailed description and proof. The imaginary part of
\log\zeta(1/2+it)
is related to the counting of zeta zeros via the Riemann-von Mangoldt formula; in this direction Selberg’s CLT for
\Im\log\zeta(1/2+it)
is related to macroscopic and mesoscopic CLTs for value distributions (again
t
being drawn uniformly in
[T,2T]
) of quantities of the form
\sum_{\rho}\eta(\Delta\cdot(\rho-t))
, where the sum is over zeta zeros,
\eta
is a suitable function, and
\Delta
is a scaling factor. Here macroscopic scale refers to the case when
\Delta
is of constant size, and mesoscopic scale is the case when
\Delta\to\infty
with
T
but
\Delta=o(\log T)
. See Fujii
[
10
]
, Bourgade and Kuan
[
5
]
, Rodgers
[
18
]
, and Maples and Rodgers
[
16
]
. Another related result is a mesoscopic CLT of Lester
[
15
]
for the logarithmic derivative of zeta away from the critical line, who proved that when
t
ranges from
T
to
2T
the real part and the imaginary part of

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\psi(T)}{\log T}+it\right)

are close to independent normal with mean
0
and variance

\displaystyle V_{zeta}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)^{2}}{n^{1+\frac{2\psi(T)}{\log T}}},

provided that
\psi(T)=o(\log T)
and
\psi(T)\to\infty
with
T
.
A standard calculation shows that

(4)

\displaystyle V_{zeta}\sim\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{\log T}{\psi(T)}\right)^{2}.

Analogues of Selberg’s CLT as well as macroscopic and mesoscopic CLTs for sums over zeta zeros are also known in the CUE setting; see Keating and Snaith
[
14
]
, Hughes, Keating and O’Connell
[
13
]
, Bourgade
[
4
]
, Szegö
[
21
]
, Wieand
[
23
]
, Diaconis and Evans
[
8
]
, and Soshnikov
[
20
]
. Related to Lester’s result, in the CUE setting there is a mesoscopic CLT away from the real line proved by Chhaibi, Najnudel and Nikeghbali
[
6
]
for a limiting object of the characteristic polynomials. Our next result is a CUE analogue of Lester’s CLT.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
L=L_{N}
be a quantity such that
L_{N}=o(N)
and
L_{N}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
. Then with
U
drawn from
U(N)
with respect to the Haar measure, the random vector

\displaystyle\left(\Re\left[\frac{L}{N}\cdot\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)\right],\Im\left[\frac{L}{N}\cdot\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)\right]\right)

converges in distribution to a normal vector
(G,H)
as
N\to\infty
, where
G
and
H
are independent normals with mean
0
and variance
1/8
.

Therefore, for large
N
the real part and the imaginary part of
\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)
are close to independent normal random variables with mean
0
and variance
V
with

(5)

\displaystyle V=\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{N}{L}\right)^{2}.

This variance agrees with Lester’s variance (
4
) in the zeta case.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Define

(6)

\displaystyle z_{0}=1-\frac{1}{N}

and let

(7)

\displaystyle z_{0}\leq z\leq 1.

We write

\displaystyle\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(z)=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|<\frac{c}{N}}\frac{1}{z-z_{j}}+X_{1}+X_{2}-X_{3},

where

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(z_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{j}},

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|\geq\frac{c}{N}}\left(\frac{1}{z-z_{j}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{j}}\right),

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|<\frac{c}{N}}\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{j}}.

Theorem
1.1
follows from the next three propositions.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
K\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have
\mathbb{E}|X_{1}|^{2K}\ll_{K}N^{2K}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
K\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have
\mathbb{E}|X_{2}|^{2K}\ll_{K}\left(\frac{N}{c}\right)^{2K}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
K\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have
\mathbb{E}|X_{3}|^{2K}\ll_{K}N^{2K}
.

2.1.
Proof of Proposition
2.1

We will need the following ratios formula from Conrey and Snaith
[
7
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(Conrey and Snaith.)
If
\Re\alpha_{j}>0
and
\Re\beta_{j}>0
for
\alpha_{j}\in A
and
\beta_{j}\in B
, then
J(A;B)=J^{*}(A;B)
where

\displaystyle J(A;B)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{U(N)}\prod_{\alpha\in A}(-e^{-\alpha})\frac{P_{U}^{\prime}}{P_{U}}(e^{-\alpha})\prod_{\beta\in B}(-e^{-\beta})\frac{P_{U^{*}}^{\prime}}{P_{U^{*}}}(e^{-\beta})~dU,

\displaystyle J^{*}(A;B):=

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{S\subset A,T\subset B\atop|S|=|T|}e^{-N(\sum_{\hat{\alpha}\in S}\hat{\alpha}+\sum_{\hat{\beta}\in T}\hat{\beta})}\frac{Z(S,T)Z(S^{-},T^{-})}{Z^{\dagger}(S,S^{-})Z^{\dagger}(T,T^{-})}\sum_{{(A-S)+(B-T)\atop=U_{1}+\dots+U_{R}}\atop|U_{r}|\leq 2}\prod_{r=1}^{R}H_{S,T}(U_{r}),

and

H_{S,T}(W)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{\hat{\alpha}\in S}\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}(\alpha-\hat{\alpha})-\sum_{\hat{\beta}\in T}\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}(\alpha+\hat{\beta})&\mbox{ if $W=\{\alpha\}\subset A-S$}\\
\sum_{\hat{\beta}\in T}\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}(\beta-\hat{\beta})-\sum_{\hat{\alpha}\in S}\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}(\beta+\hat{\alpha})&\mbox{ if $W=\{\beta\}\subset B-T$}\\
\left(\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}\right)^{\prime}(\alpha+\beta)&\mbox{ if
$W=\{\alpha,\beta\}$ with ${\alpha\in A-S,\atop\beta\in B-T}$}\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Here
z(x)=(1-e^{-x})^{-1}
,
S^{-}=\{-s:s\in S\}
(similarly for
T^{-}
) and
Z(A,B)=\prod_{\alpha\in A\atop\beta\in B}z(\alpha+\beta)
,
with the dagger on
Z^{\dagger}(S,S^{-})
imposing the additional
restriction that a factor
z(x)
is omitted if its argument is
zero.

Here the
U^{*}
is the conjugate transpose of
U
, and it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{P_{U}^{\prime}}{P_{U}}(z)\right|^{2K}=\int_{U(N)}\left(\frac{P_{U}^{\prime}}{P_{U}}(z)\right)^{K}\left(\frac{P_{U^{*}}^{\prime}}{P_{U^{*}}}(\overline{z})\right)^{K}~dU.

One may try to apply the above theorem by letting all the
\alpha
’s in
A
equal and similar for
\beta
’s in
B
. However, this will cause complications because many terms in the
J^{*}
will have poles. To get around this, we will apply the ratios formula to a discretized set of points, as follows.
Let
r=\frac{1}{2N}
,
r_{k}=\frac{r}{2^{k}}
, and define

G(x_{1},...,x_{K})=\int_{U(N)}\frac{P_{U}^{\prime}}{P_{U}}(x_{1})\frac{P_{U^{*}}^{\prime}}{P_{U^{*}}}(x_{1})\cdots\frac{P_{U}^{\prime}}{P_{U}}(x_{K})\frac{P_{U^{*}}^{\prime}}{P_{U^{*}}}(x_{K})~dU.

Since
z_{0}
is real, to prove the proposition it suffices to show that
G(z_{0},...,z_{0})\ll_{K}N^{2K}.

Observe that
G
is holomorphic in each variable in a certain domain around
z_{0}
, so applying the maximum modulus principle to
G(x,z_{0},...,z_{0})
as a function of
x
, we see that there exists a
w_{1}
on the circle
C(z_{0},r_{1})
centered at
z_{0}
with radius
r_{1}
such that

|G(w_{1},z_{0},...,z_{0})|\geq|G(z_{0},z_{0},...,z_{0})|.

Next, apply the maximum modulus principle to
G(w_{1},x,z_{0},...,z_{0})
as a function of
x
, and we see that there exists a
w_{2}
on the circle
C(z_{0},r_{2})
such that

|G(w_{1},w_{2},z_{0},...,z_{0})|\geq|G(w_{1},z_{0},...,z_{0})|.

Repeat this and we conclude that there are points
w_{1},...,w_{k}
with
w_{i}
on the circle
C(z_{0},r_{i})
for each
i
, such that

|G(w_{1},...,w_{k})|\geq|G(z_{0},...,z_{0})|.

By the definition (
6
) of
z_{0}
, it is easy to see that
\min_{i,j}|w_{i}-w_{j}|\gg_{K}r
. Since the function
\log(1-x)
is close to
-x
around
x=0
, we conclude that the points
\log(w_{k})
’s are also well-spaced by a distance
\gg r\gg 1/N
.
We can now apply the ratios formula to the
w_{k}
’s. In the formula for
J^{*}
, from the above observation that these points are well-spaced, it is easy to see that all the
Z(S,T)
and
Z(S^{-},T^{-})
factors on the numerator are each bounded by
N^{|S|^{2}}
, and all the
Z^{\dagger}(S,S^{-})
and
Z^{\dagger}(T,T^{-})
factors on the denominator are each bounded from below by
N^{|S|^{2}-|S|}
. (Indeed, for
x
close to
0
the function
z(x)
is close to
1/x
, and thus, the
z
factors in the
Z
and
Z^{\dagger}
functions are
\gg
and
\ll N
by our choice of the
w_{k}
’s. The claimed bounds for
Z
and
Z^{\dagger}
follow by noticing that there are
|S|^{2}
factors in
Z(S,T)
and
|S|^{2}-|S|
factors in
Z^{\dagger}(S,S^{-})
.) Similarly, all the
H_{S,T}
factors are bounded by
N^{2K-2|S|}
. Collecting these estimates we conclude that
J^{*}\ll_{K}N^{2K}
, and this finishes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

2.2.
Proof of Proposition
2.2

Recall that

z_{0}=1-\frac{1}{N}

and

z_{0}\leq z\leq 1.

Observe that

\displaystyle|z_{0}-z_{j}|\leq|z_{0}-z|+|z-z_{j}|\ll 1/N+|z-z_{j}|

and that for
|\theta_{j}|\geq c/N

\displaystyle|z-z_{j}|\gg c/N.

It follows that

\displaystyle c|z_{0}-z_{j}|\ll c/N+c|z-z_{j}|\ll|z-z_{j}|+c|z-z_{j}|,

and thus, for
0<c\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle c|z_{0}-z_{j}|\ll|z-z_{j}|.

Using this we see that

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|\geq\frac{c}{N}}\left(\frac{1}{z-z_{j}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|\geq\frac{c}{N}}\frac{z_{0}-z}{(z-z_{j})(z_{0}-z_{j})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{cN}\sum_{|\theta_{j}|\geq\frac{c}{N}}\frac{1}{|z_{0}-z_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{cN}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{|z_{0}-z_{j}|^{2}},

We claim that

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{|z_{0}-z_{j}|^{2}}\ll N(N+|X_{1}|).

From this together with Proposition
2.1
we will obtain Proposition
2.2
.

To prove the claim (
8
), we would like to connect its left-hand side with

\displaystyle X_{1}=\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(z_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{j}},

and a natural way is to take the real or imaginary part of
X_{1}
. However, unlike in the zeta case, after taking real or imaginary parts the summands may have different signs or even be
0
, and this prevents us from controlling the size of the left-hand side of (
8
). To get around this,
we introduce a change of variable

\displaystyle P(z)=P(e^{s})=Q(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(e^{s}-e^{i\theta_{j}}).

We then consider a variant of the Weierstrass-Hadamard factorization for each
e^{s}-e^{i\theta_{j}}
and first write for purely imaginary
s
that

\displaystyle e^{s}-e^{i\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{s+i\theta_{j}}{2}}\cdot(2i)\cdot\sin\left(\frac{-is-\theta_{j}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{s+i\theta_{j}}{2}}\cdot(s-i\theta_{j})\cdot\prod_{n\neq 0}\left(1+\frac{is+\theta_{j}}{2n\pi}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{Q^{\prime}}{Q}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{(e^{s}-e^{i\theta_{j}})^{\prime}}{e^{s}-e^{i\theta_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{s-i\theta_{j}}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{1}{-i2n\pi+s-i\theta_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{-i2n\pi+s-i\theta_{j}}\right)

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{s-i(\theta_{j}+2n\pi)}.

Remark: One may view this formula as an analogue of the Hadamard fraction formula for zeta, where the
N/2
in (
2.2
) corresponds to the contribution of trivial zeta zeros which is about
-\frac{\log T}{2}
. The difference of the sign comes from the fact that
s
is to the left of the ‘critical line’ in the CUE case.

These equations extend to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
(except at poles), and thus,

\displaystyle\Re\left(\frac{Q^{\prime}}{Q}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\Re(s)}{|s-i(\theta_{j}+2n\pi)|^{2}}.

Set
s=s_{0}
in the above equation, where
e^{s_{0}}=z_{0}
, so that
s_{0}=\log(1-\frac{1}{N})
is about
-1/N
. We have

\displaystyle 0<\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{-s_{0}}{|s_{0}-i\theta_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{-s_{0}}{|s_{0}-i(\theta_{j}+2n\pi)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}-\Re\left(\frac{Q^{\prime}}{Q}(s_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{2}+\left|\frac{Q^{\prime}}{Q}(s_{0})\right|.

Now observe that

\displaystyle|z_{0}-z_{j}|\gg|s_{0}-i\theta_{j}|

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{Q^{\prime}}{Q}(s_{0})\right|=\left|\frac{P^{\prime}(e^{s_{0}})\cdot e^{s_{0}}}{P(e^{s_{0}})}\right|\ll\left|\frac{P^{\prime}(z_{0})}{P(z_{0})}\right|=|X_{1}|.

Combine these with the above and we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{|z_{0}-z_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{|s_{0}-i\theta_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{-s_{0}}{|s_{0}-i\theta_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll N+\left|\frac{Q^{\prime}}{Q}(s_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\ll N+|X_{1}|,

proving (
8
).

Proof of Proposition

We have

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\theta_{j}|<\frac{c}{N}}\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{j}}\ll\left(\sum_{|\theta_{j}|<\frac{c}{N}}1\right)\cdot N\leq\frac{1}{N}\cdot\left(\sum_{|\theta_{j}|<\frac{1}{N}}1\right)\cdot N^{2}.

). Proposition

now follows from Proposition

.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In view of Lester’s result, we expect that the variance of the complex random variable

\displaystyle\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(1-L/N)-e^{i\theta_{j}}}.

is a constant times
(N/L)^{2}
. Therefore, we introduce the following rescaling

\displaystyle f(\theta)=f_{N,L}(\theta)=\frac{L}{N}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-L/N)-e^{i\theta}},

\displaystyle g(\theta)=g_{N,L}(\theta)=\Re f(\theta),

\displaystyle h(\theta)=h_{N,L}(\theta)=\Im f(\theta),

and denote

\displaystyle S_{N}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(\theta_{j})=\frac{L}{N}\cdot\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right),

\displaystyle S_{N}(g)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}g(\theta_{j})=\Re\left[\frac{L}{N}\cdot\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)\right],

\displaystyle S_{N}(h)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}h(\theta_{j})=\Im\left[\frac{L}{N}\cdot\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)\right].

We compute the characteristic function (ch.f.)

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(u,v)=\mathbb{E}e^{i(uS_{N}(g)+vS_{N}(h))}

of the random vector
(S_{N}(g),S_{N}(h))
. We will show that

(10)

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(u,v)\to e^{-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{8}}\quad\text{ as }N\to\infty,

for every
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and this will prove Theorem
1.2
according to the convergence theorem for random vectors (see for example Theorem 3.10.5 in Durrett
[
9
]
).
To prove (
10
), it suffices to show that for every
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the real random variable

(11)

\displaystyle uS_{N}(g)+vS_{N}(h)\longrightarrow Normal\left(0,\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{8}\right)\text{ in distribution,}

for this will imply pointwise convergence of the ch.f. of
uS_{N}(g)+vS_{N}(h)
, thus in particular its ch.f. evaluated at
1
, which gives (
10
).

The main tool we use to prove (
11
) is the following result of Soshnikov
[
20
]
, which is a combination of Lemma 1 and the main combinatorial lemma in that paper.

Proposition 3.1
.

(Soshinikov.)
Let
F(\theta)
be a real-valued function on the unit circle with continuous derivative and satisfy

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2}<\infty,

where

\hat{F}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}d\theta

are the Fourier coefficients of
F
. Let
C_{\ell}(F)
be the
\ell
-th cumulant of
S_{N}(F)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}F(\theta_{j})
. Then we have

(12)

\displaystyle C_{1}(F)

\displaystyle=\hat{F}(0)\cdot N,

(13)

\displaystyle\left|C_{2}(F)-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|>N/2}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2},

and for
\ell\geq 3

(14)

\displaystyle\left|C_{\ell}(F)\right|\ll_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0\\
|k_{1}|+\cdots+|k_{\ell}|>N\end{subarray}}|k_{1}||\hat{F}(k_{1})\cdots\hat{F}(k_{\ell})|.

We shall apply Proposition
3.1
to

\displaystyle F(\theta)=F_{N,L,u,v}(\theta)=ug(\theta)+vh(\theta)

for every
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and thus,

\displaystyle S_{N}(F)=uS_{N}(g)+vS_{N}(h).

Since the normal distribution is determined by cumulants, to prove (
11
) it suffices to prove that

(15)

\displaystyle C_{1}(F)\to 0,\quad C_{2}(F)\to\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{8},\quad\text{ and }\quad C_{\ell}(F)\to 0\text{ for }\ell\geq 3

as
N\to\infty
.

We start by computing Fourier coefficients of
f
. Recall that

\displaystyle f(\theta)=\frac{L}{N}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-L/N)-e^{i\theta}}.

It follows easily that

\displaystyle\hat{f}(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{ if }k\geq 0,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{-L}{N}\cdot\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{-k-1},&\text{ if }k<0.\end{array}\right.

Note that here all
\hat{f}(k)
are real. From this we deduce the Fourier coefficents for
g
and
h
:

\displaystyle\hat{g}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{f}(k)+\overline{\hat{f}(-k)}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{ if }k=0,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{-L}{2N}\cdot\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|-1},&\text{ if }k\neq 0,\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\hat{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2i}\left(\hat{f}(k)-\overline{\hat{f}(-k)}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{ if }k=0,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{-L}{2iN}\cdot\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|-1},&\text{ if }k<0,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{L}{2iN}\cdot\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|-1},&\text{ if }k>0.\end{array}\right.

Since
F=ug+vh
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{F}(k)=u\hat{g}(k)+v\hat{h}(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{ if }k=0,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{-L}{2N}\cdot\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|-1}\cdot(u-iv),&\text{ if }k<0,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{L}{2N}\cdot\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|-1}\cdot(u+iv),&\text{ if }k>0.\end{array}\right.

We first estimate the two sums in (
13
). A staightforward computation shows

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2}=\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{-2}\sum_{k\geq 1}k\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2k}.

Denote temporarily
A(x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}kx^{2k}
and
B(x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}x^{2k}
. For
0<x<1
we have
B(x)=(1-x^{2})^{-1}-1
. Differentiating
B(x)
yields
A(x)=x^{2}(1-x^{2})^{-2}
for
0<x<1
. Therefore, letting
x=1-L/N
in the above equation we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2}=\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{\left(2-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2}},

which is finite for fixed
N,L,u
and
v
. Therefore, Proposition
3.1
applies to our function
F
.
From the above equation we also see

(19)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2}=\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{8}.

A similar treatment for the second sum in (
13
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{|k|>N/2}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{-2}\sum_{k\geq N/2}k\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{-2}\frac{\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2M}}{\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2}\left(2-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2}}\left(M+(1-M)\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2}\right),

where
M
is the least integer greater than
N/2
. Since we assume
L=o(N)
, there is no harm to assume
L/N<1/2
, say. Thus, it is not difficult to see that the above is

\displaystyle\ll_{u,v}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2M+2}+\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2M}\cdot M\cdot\frac{L}{N}

\displaystyle\ll_{u,v}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2M}\cdot L

\displaystyle\ll_{u,v}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{N}\cdot L

\displaystyle\ll_{u,v}e^{-L}L.

Since
L\to\infty
with
N
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{|k|>N/2}|k||\hat{F}(k)|^{2}=0.

Combining (
19
) and (
20
) we conclude that

(21)

\displaystyle C_{2}(F)\to\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{8}

as
N\to\infty
.

Moreover, from (
3
) and Proposition
3.1
it follows immediately that

(22)

\displaystyle C_{1}(F)=0.

In view of (
15
), (
22
), (
21
) and (
14
), it only remains to prove

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0\\
|k_{1}|+\cdots+|k_{\ell}|>N\end{subarray}}|k_{1}||\hat{F}(k_{1})\cdots\hat{F}(k_{\ell})|\to 0

as
N\to\infty
, for each
\ell\geq 3
. From (
3
) we have

\displaystyle|\hat{F}(k)|\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}=0,&\text{ if }k=0,\\[4.30554pt]
\ll_{u,v}\frac{L}{N}\cdot\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|},&\text{ if }k\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0\\
|k_{1}|+\cdots+|k_{\ell}|>N\end{subarray}}|k_{1}||\hat{F}(k_{1})\cdots\hat{F}(k_{\ell})|\ll_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0\\
|k_{1}|+\cdots+|k_{\ell}|>N\\
|k_{1}|\geq|k_{2}|,\dots,|k_{\ell}|\end{subarray}}|k_{1}||\hat{F}(k_{1})\cdots\hat{F}(k_{\ell})|,

and the conditions in the last sum imply that
|k_{1}|>N/\ell
. Thus, the above sum is

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\sum_{|k|>N/\ell}|k||\hat{F}(k)|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k_{2}|\leq|k|,...,|k_{\ell}|\leq|k|\\
k+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0\end{subarray}}|\hat{F}(k_{2})\cdots\hat{F}(k_{\ell})|.

Plug in the bounds for
|\hat{F}(k)|
, and note that the inner sum condition
k+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0
implies
|k_{2}|+\cdots+|k_{\ell}|\geq|k|
. Thus, the above is

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell,u,v}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{\ell}\cdot\sum_{|k|>N/\ell}|k|\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k_{2}|\leq|k|,...,|k_{\ell}|\leq|k|\\
k+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k_{2}|+\cdots+|k_{\ell}|}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell,u,v}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{\ell}\cdot\sum_{|k|>N/\ell}|k|\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k_{2}|\leq|k|,...,|k_{\ell-1}|\leq|k|\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{|k|}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell,u,v}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{\ell}\cdot\sum_{|k|>N/\ell}|k|\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2|k|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k_{2}|\leq|k|,...,|k_{\ell-1}|\leq|k|\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell,u,v}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{\ell}\cdot\sum_{|k|>N/\ell}|k|^{\ell-1}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2|k|}

(23)

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell,u,v}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{\ell}\cdot\sum_{k>N/\ell}k^{\ell-1}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2k}.

Let
D_{n}(y)=\sum_{k\geq n}y^{k}=y^{n}(1-y)^{-1}
for
0<y<1
. Differentiating
\ell-1
times with respect to
y
, we have, for fixed
\ell
and for
n>2\ell
, that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq n}k^{\ell-1}y^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq n}k^{\ell-1}y^{k-\ell+1}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\left(\frac{d}{dy}\right)^{\ell-1}D_{n}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{\ell-1}{j}\cdot\left(\frac{d}{dy}\right)^{j}y^{n}\cdot\left(\frac{d}{dy}\right)^{\ell-1-j}\frac{1}{1-y}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}n^{j}y^{n-j}\frac{1}{(1-y)^{\ell-j}}.

Plug in
y=(1-L/N)^{2}
and
n=
the least integer
>N/\ell
, and let
N
be sufficiently large. We obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k>N/\ell}k^{\ell-1}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2k}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{N}{\ell}\right)^{j}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2(n-j)}\frac{1}{\left(2-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{\ell-j}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{\ell-j}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}N^{j}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2n}\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{j-\ell}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}N^{\ell}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2N/\ell}L^{-1}.

From this and (
3
) it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=0\\
|k_{1}|+\cdots+|k_{\ell}|>N\end{subarray}}|k_{1}||\hat{F}(k_{1})\cdots\hat{F}(k_{\ell})|

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell,u,v}\left(1-\frac{L}{N}\right)^{2N/\ell}L^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell,u,v}e^{-2L/\ell}L^{\ell-1}

which tends to
0
as
L\to\infty
(or
N\to\infty
). This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

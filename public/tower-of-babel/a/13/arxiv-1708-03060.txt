Initial degenerations of Grassmannians
Daniel Corey

Abstract
We construct closed immersions from initial degenerations of Gr 0 ⁡ ( d , n ) \Gr_{0}(d,n) —the open cell in the Grassmannian Gr ⁡ ( d , n ) \Gr(d,n) given by the nonvanishing of all Plücker coordinates—to limits of thin Schubert cells associated to diagrams induced by the face poset of the corresponding tropical linear space. These are isomorphisms when ( d , n ) (d,n) equals ( 2 , n ) (2,n) , ( 3 , 6 ) (3,6) and ( 3 , 7 ) (3,7) . As an application we prove Gr 0 ⁡ ( 3 , 7 ) \Gr_{0}(3,7) is schön, and the Chow quotient of Gr ⁡ ( 3 , 7 ) \Gr(3,7) by the maximal torus in PGL ( 7 ) \operatorname*{PGL}(7) is the log canonical compactification of the moduli space of 7 points in ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} in linear general position, making progress on a conjecture of Hacking, Keel, and Tevelev. Keywords : Grassmannians, initial degenerations, thin Schubert cells, matroids, Chow quotient, log canonical compactification. Mathematics Subject Classification : 14T05 (primary), 14M15, 14E25 (secondary).

Preliminaries

2.1
Initial Degenerations

We recall some facts about initial degenerations and tropicalization of varieties defined over a trivially valued field from the Gröbner-theoretic perspective, see
[
28
, Chapters 2, 3]
for a comprehensive treatment, including the non-trivially valued case. Let
X
be the closed subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{a}=\Proj(\mathbf{k}[t_{0},\ldots,t_{a}])
with homogeneous ideal
I\subset\mathbf{k}[t_{0},\ldots,t_{a}]
. Assume that
X
meets the dense torus
T
. Set
X_{0}=X\cap T
and
I_{0}=I\cdot\mathbf{k}[t_{0}^{\pm},\ldots,t_{a}^{\pm}]
. We will often find it easier to work with
\Spec(\mathbf{k}[t_{0}^{\pm},\ldots,t_{a}^{\pm}]/I_{0})
. This space is
\pi^{-1}(X_{0})
, where
\pi:\mathbb{A}^{a+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}^{a}
is the natural projection. Note that
\pi^{-1}(X_{0})\cong X_{0}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

Let
N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{a+1}/{\mathbb{Z}}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{1}
denote the cocharacter lattice of
T
, where
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)
. For
z=(z_{0},\ldots,z_{a})
, we write
t^{z}=t^{z_{0}}\cdots t^{z_{a}}
. The
initial form
of
f\in\mathbf{k}[t_{0}^{\pm},\ldots,t_{a}^{\pm}]
with respect to
w\in(N_{T})_{{\mathbb{R}}}:=(N_{T})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
is

\inw_{w}f=\sum_{w:\left\langle w,z\right\rangle\text{ minimal}}a_{z}t^{z}\hskip 10.0pt\text{ where }\hskip 10.0ptf=\sum a_{z}t^{z}.

That is,
\inw_{w}f
is the sum of all monomials
a_{z}t^{z}
of
f
with minimal
w
-weight. The
initial ideals
of
I_{0}
and
I
with respect to
w
are

\inw_{w}I_{0}=\langle\inw_{w}f\,|\,f\in I_{0}\rangle\hskip 10.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.0pt\inw_{w}I=\langle\inw_{w}f\,|\,f\in I\rangle,

respectively. The
initial degeneration
of
X_{0}
with respect to
w
is

\inw_{w}X_{0}=T\cap\Proj(\mathbf{k}[t_{0},\cdots,t_{a}]/\inw_{w}I).

There is a complete polyhedral fan
\Sigma_{\Grob}(X_{0})
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, called the
Gröbner fan
, where
w
and
w^{\prime}
belong to the relative interior of the same cone in
\Sigma_{\Grob}(X_{0})
if and only if
\inw_{w}I=\inw_{w^{\prime}}I

[
37
, Theorem 1.2]
. The
tropicalization
of
X_{0}
is

\Trop X_{0}=\left\{w\in(N_{T})_{{\mathbb{R}}}\ |\ \inw_{w}I_{0}\neq\left\langle 1\right\rangle\right\}.

When
X_{0}
is irreducible,
\Trop X_{0}
is the support of a pure
\dim(X_{0})
-dimensional subfan of
\Sigma_{\Grob}(X_{0})
. Denote the restriction of
\Sigma_{\Grob}(X_{0})
to
\Trop X_{0}
by
{\pazocal G}_{X_{0}}
. While
\inw_{w}X_{0}
depends only on the cone of
{\pazocal G}_{X_{0}}
containing
w
in its relative interior, it is possible that
\inw_{w}X_{0}=\inw_{w^{\prime}}X_{0}
when
w
and
w^{\prime}
belong to distinct locally closed cones. In this case,
\inw_{w}I
and
\inw_{w^{\prime}}I
differ by primary components contained in
\langle t_{0},\ldots,t_{a}\rangle
.

2.2
Matroid polytopes

We assume familiarity with basic notions of matroids and refer the reader to
[
30
]
for a detailed treatment. For brevity, we say that a rank
d
matroid on
[n]
is a
(d,n)
-matroid. Given a matroid
M
, we write
{\pazocal B}(M)
for its set of bases and
\rho_{M}
for its rank function. The uniform
(d,n)
-matroid is denoted by
U(d,n)
. For
\eta\subset[n]
,
M/\eta
denotes the contraction of
M
by
\eta
and
M|\eta
denotes the restriction of
M
to
\eta
.

Let
e_{0},\ldots,e_{n-1}
denote the standard basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and for a subset
\lambda=\{\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\}
of
[n]
, let
e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda_{0}}+\cdots+e_{\lambda_{k}}.

The
hypersimplex

\Delta(d,n)
is the polytope in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
defined by

\Delta(d,n)=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,\left|\,x_{[n]}=d,\ 0\leq x_{i}\leq 1\right.\right\}.

(2.1)

The vertices of
\Delta(d,n)
are the points
e_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in{[n]\choose d}:=\{\sigma\subset[n]\,|\,|\sigma|=d\}
. The
matroid polytope
of
M
is

Q_{M}=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,\left|\,x_{[n]}=d,\ x_{\eta}\leq\rho_{M}(\eta),\ \eta\subset[n]\right.\right\}.

(2.2)

The vertices of
Q_{M}
are the points
e_{\beta}
for
\beta\in{\pazocal B}(M)
. Given a collection of vertices of
\Delta(d,n)
, its convex hull
Q
is a matroid polytope if and only if every edge of
Q
is parallel to some
e_{i}-e_{j}

[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
; we write
M_{Q}
for the corresponding matroid. In particular, any face of a matroid polytope is a matroid polytope.

Throughout, we will consider the face order on polytopes:
Q^{\prime}\leq Q
whenever
Q^{\prime}
is a face of
Q
, and
Q^{\prime}\lessdot Q
when
Q^{\prime}
is a facet of
Q
. This induces a partial order on the set of
(d,n)
-matroids:
M^{\prime}\leq M
whenever
Q_{M^{\prime}}\leq Q_{M}
, and
M^{\prime}\lessdot M
if
Q_{M^{\prime}}\lessdot Q_{M}
. Given
\eta\subset[n]
, let
M_{\eta}=M_{Q^{\prime}}
where
Q^{\prime}=Q_{M}\cap\{x_{\eta}=\rho_{M}(\eta)\}
. The bases of
M_{\eta}
are

{\pazocal B}(M_{\eta})=\{\beta\in{\pazocal B}(M)\,|\,|\beta\cap\eta|=\rho_{M}(\eta)\},

and the remaining
\beta\in{\pazocal B}(M)\setminus{\pazocal B}(M_{\eta})
satisfy
|\beta\cap\eta|<\rho_{M}(\eta)
. It is not hard to produce an isomorphism
M_{\eta}\cong M/\eta\oplus M|\eta
.
When
M
is connected, a nonempty subset
\eta
is
nondegenerate
if
M/{\eta}
and
M|\eta
are connected. The following proposition may be found in
[
10
, Section 2.5]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
M
be a connected matroid on
[n]
, then
\eta\mapsto Q_{M_{\eta}}
is a one-to-one correspondence between nondegenerate subsets
\eta
and the facets of
Q_{M}
.

Finally, we remark that if
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
M_{w}
is the matroid of minimal
w
-weight as in
[
1
]
, then our
M_{\eta}
is just
M_{-\chi(\eta)}
where
\chi
is the characteristic function.

2.3
Thin Schubert cells

The Grassmannian
\Gr(d,n)
of
d
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbf{k}^{n}
is a subvariety of
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{d}\mathbf{k}^{n})
via the Plücker embedding. We write
\mathbf{k}[p_{\lambda}]
for the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{d}\mathbf{k}^{n})
,
I^{d,n}\subset\mathbf{k}[p_{\lambda}]
for the Plücker ideal, and
p_{\lambda}(F)
for the
\lambda
-th Plücker coordinate of
F\in\Gr(d,n)
. As observed by
[
8
]
,
\Gr(d,n)
decomposes into locally closed subschemes
\Gr_{M}
called
thin Schubert cells
which are indexed by
\mathbf{k}
-realizable
(d,n)
-matroids.
Set-theoretically,

\Gr_{M}=\{F\in\Gr(d,n)\,|\,p_{\lambda}(F)\neq 0\text{ if and only if }\lambda\in{\pazocal B}(M)\}.

Observe that
\Gr_{0}(d,n)=\Gr_{U(d,n)}
. We realize
\Gr_{M}
as a scheme in the following way. Define

•

B_{M}=\mathbf{k}[p_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\in{\pazocal B}(M)]\subset\mathbf{k}[p_{\lambda}]
,

•

I_{M}=\left(I^{d,n}+\left\langle p_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\in{[n]\choose d}\setminus{\pazocal B}(M)\right\rangle\right)\cap B_{M}
,

•

S_{M}
the multiplicative semigroup of
B_{M}
generated by
p_{\lambda}
such that
\lambda\in{\pazocal B}(M)
, and

•

R_{M}=S_{M}^{-1}B_{M}/I_{M}
.

Then

\Gr_{M}=T(M)\cap\Proj(B_{M}/I_{M})

where
T(M)
is the dense torus of
\Proj(B_{M})
. For computations, we will often find it easier to work with
\Spec(R_{M})\cong\Gr_{M}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. The ideal
I_{M}
is generated by

P_{M}(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{i:\mu\cup i,\nu\setminus i\in{\pazocal B}(M)}\sgn(i;\mu,\nu)p_{\mu\cup i}p_{\nu\setminus i}

(2.3)

where
\mu\in{[n]\choose d-1}
is independent and
\nu\in{[n]\choose d+1}
,
\nu\not\subset\mu
has rank
d

[
28
, Equation 4.4.1]
. Here,
\sgn(i;\mu,\nu)
equals
(-1)^{\ell}
where
\ell
is the number of
j\in\nu
with
i<j
plus the number of elements
j^{\prime}\in\mu
such that

]

4
Geometry of thin Schubert cells

By Mnëv universality, there exist
(3,n)
matroids whose thin Schubert cells are singular or reducible, for sufficiently large
n
. Nevertheless,
\Gr_{M}
is smooth and irreducible when
M
is a rank 2 matroid, or a rank
3
matroid on
[6]
or
[7]
, as we demonstrate in this section. Let
M
be a
\mathbf{k}
-realizable matroid. For each rank 1 flat
\eta
of
M
, choose a non-loop
s_{\eta}\in\eta
, and set
S=\{s_{\eta}\,|\,\eta\text{ is a rank 1 flat}\}
. Then
M|S
is a simple matroid, and
\Gr_{M}
is the product of
\Gr_{M|S}
with an algebraic torus as discussed in
Lemma

C.2
. Because the only simple
(2,n)
-matroid is
U(2,n)
, this leads to a straightforward proof that
\Gr_{M}
is smooth and irreducible in the rank 2 case. Therefore, we will focus on rank
3
matroids.

Let
M
be a
\mathbf{k}
-realizable loop-free
(3,n)
-matroid for
n\geq 3
. We can represent
M
as a configuration of
n
points
p_{0},\ldots,p_{n-1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The elements
i,j
are parallel in
M
if and only if
p_{i},p_{j}
coincide. A subset
\beta\subset[n]
is a basis if and only if
|\beta|=3
and
p_{i}
are not collinear for
i\in\beta
, and
\eta\subset[n]
is a rank
2
flat if and only if there is a line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
p_{i}\in L
precisely when
i\in\eta
. When drawing these pictures, we will only draw the points (labeled
0,\ldots,n-1
) and lines through at least 3 rank 1 flats, see Figures
6.1
and
8.1
. With this in mind, we say that
\eta
is a
line
of
M
if
\eta
is a rank
2
flat and
|\eta\cap S|\geq 3
. The set of lines of
M
, denoted by
{\pazocal L}(M)
, completely determines
M|S
.

All
(3,n)
-matroids for
3\leq n\leq 7
(up to
S_{n}
-symmetry) can be found in the online
Database of Matroids

http://www-imai.is.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~ymatsu/matroid/

In showing that
\Gr_{M}
is smooth and irreducible for these matroids, we start with
n=3
and work inductively. At each step, we need only consider simple and connected matroids by Lemmas
C.1
and
C.2
. However, there are still 8, resp. 21, simple and connected
\mathbf{k}
-realizable
(3,6)
, resp.
(3,7)
-matroids. We use
Lemma

4.1
to handle the remaining cases.

In this section and the next, we will need the following definitions. A morphism of schemes is said to have
connected fibers
if all of its nonempty fibers are connected. We say that
f:X\to Y
is a
SDC-morphism
if it is smooth and dominant with connected fibers.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
M
is a loop-free
\mathbf{k}
-realizable
(3,n)
-matroid,
i\in[n]
contained in exactly
k
lines where
0\leq k\leq 2
, and
\Gr_{M|[n]\setminus i}
is integral. The composition of a dominant open immersion
\Gr_{M}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{M|[n]\setminus i}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{3-k}
, followed by the projection away from
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{3-k}
produces a SDC-morphism
\Gr_{M}\to\Gr_{M|[n]\setminus i}
.

Proof.

We use affine coordinates as in
2.2
. Assume that
\{0,1,2\}
is a basis of
M
,
i=n-1
, and the first 3 columns of
X
form the identity matrix. Suppose
n-1
is not contained in any line. This means that
\{i,j,n-1\}\in{\pazocal B}(M)
for
0\leq i<j\leq n-2
, so
I_{M}^{x}
is generated by
X_{\lambda}
for suitable
\lambda\in{[n-1]\choose 3}
. Therefore
R_{M}^{x}
is obtained from
R_{M|[n-1]}^{x}[x_{0,n-4}^{\pm},x_{1,n-4}^{\pm},x_{2,n-4}^{\pm}]
by inverting
X_{\beta}
for
\beta\in{\pazocal B}(M)
. These ring elements are nonzero divisors since they are not 0 (by
\mathbf{k}
-realizability) and
R_{M|[n-1]}
is an integral domain. This localization produces the open immersion
\Gr_{M}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{M|[n-1]}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{3}
.

Suppose
n-1
is contained in exactly one line
\eta
. By applying a suitable permutation, assume
0,1\in\eta
. Since
\lambda_{2,j-3}\notin{\pazocal B}(M)
when
j\in\eta
,
I_{M}^{x}
is generated by
X_{\lambda}
for suitable
\lambda\in{[n-1]\choose 3}
, and
R_{M}^{x}
is obtained from
R_{M|[n-1]}^{x}[x_{0,n-4}^{\pm},x_{1,n-4}^{\pm}]
by inverting the nonzero divisors
X_{\beta}
for
\beta\in{\pazocal B}(M)
, producing the open immersion
\Gr_{M}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{M|[n-1]}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2}
.

Now assume
n
is contained in exactly two distinct lines
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
. We may assume
0,1\in\eta_{1}
and
2\in\eta_{2}
. Because
\lambda_{0,j-3},\lambda_{1,j-3}\in{\pazocal B}(M)
when

, the corresponding
x_{0,j-3},x_{1,j-3}
are invertible in

. Similar to the previous case,

R_{M}^{x}=(S_{M}^{x})^{-1}R_{M|[n-1]}^{x}[x_{0,n-4}^{\pm},x_{1,n-4}^{\pm}]/\langle x_{0,j-3}x_{1,n-4}-x_{1,j-3}x_{0,n-4}\,|\,j\in\eta_{2}\setminus\{2\}\rangle.

Since
|\eta_{2}|\geq 3
, this ring is isomorphic to
(S_{M}^{x})^{-1}R_{M|[n-1]}^{x}[x_{1,n-1}^{\pm}]
, and we have an open immersion
\Gr_{M}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{M|[n-1]}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. Finally,
\Gr_{M}\to\Gr_{M|[n-1]}
is a SDC-morphism by
Proposition

A.3
and the fact that the projection away from
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{3-k}
is SDC.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For

,

is smooth and irreducible for any
\mathbf{k}
-realizable
(3,n)
-matroid

The only

,

Next, consider the

In this section, we will consider the morphisms

For arbitrary matroids

Therefore, we focus on the rank

is a SDC-morphism for pairs

of
(3,7)
-matroids where

is not a face of the hypersimplex. To do this, we will find it convenient to show that

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a simple and connected
\mathbf{k}
-realizable
(3,n)
-matroid, and

|\eta|=1
and

is connected, or

2.

|\eta|=n-1
and
M|\eta
is connected, or

3.

\eta
is a line.

Proof.

First, we claim that if
\eta
is nondegenerate, then
|\eta|=1,n-1
or
\eta
is a line. To that end, fix a subset
\eta
such that

, and
\eta
is not a line (

is clearly degenerate when

or

and

or

, then there is a line

properly containing

becomes a loop in

has at least 2 elements, having a loop implies that it is not connected. If

is a loop in

Since

is connected,

is not connected. In all cases,

is degenerate, hence the claim.

If

which is connected, so

is nondegenerate if and only if
M/\eta
is connected. Similarly, if
|\eta|=n-1
, then

which is connected, so

is nondegenerate if and only if

is connected. Finally, suppose

is a line. Then

Throughout this section, all
\mathbf{k}
-schemes are Noetherian and of finite-type over
\mathbf{k}
. In addition, all morphisms of
\mathbf{k}
-schemes are also of finite-type. Recall from the beginning of
Section

that a SDC-morphism of
\mathbf{k}
-schemes is one that is smooth and dominant with connected fibers. In this section, we will catalog properties of SDC-morphisms used throughout the paper. First, we discuss how to deduce smoothness or connectedness of a
\mathbf{k}
-scheme

and

Proposition A.1
.

Let

be
\mathbf{k}
-schemes as above.

1.

If

is a dominant morphism with connected fibers and

is irreducible, then
X
connected.

2.

If
f:X\to Y
is a SDC-morphism and
Y
is smooth and irreducible, then so is

.

Proof.

Let
V
be the image of
f
. Since
f
is dominant and
Y
is irreducible,
V
is also irreducible. Therefore,
f:X\to V
is a surjective morphism with connected fibers. We conclude that

is connected by
[
36
,
Tag 0378
]
. Finally, (2) follows easily from (1).
∎

Next, we explore how SDC-morphisms behave under base change. This proposition is crucial in the proof of

Proposition A.2

Suppose we have a pullback diagram

Remark A.7

An arbitrary finite limit over a diagram of smooth and irreducible

-schemes in which every morphism is SDC-need not be irreducible.

Appendix C
Maps between thin Schubert cells and inverse limits (written by María Angélica Cueto)

In this appendix, we discuss how to reduce the study of geometric properties of thin Schubert cells to the case of simple and connected matroids. Because the only simple rank 2 matroid is
U(2,n)

\Gr_{M}
in the rank 2 case, and simplifies the study of rank
3
matroids in Sections
4
and
5

\Gr_{M,w}
induced by a matroid subdivision
\Delta_{M,w}
depends only on the adjacency graph of
\Delta_{M,w}

Appendix

\Gr_{M,w}
as in

6
.

C.1
Reduction to simple and connected matroids

The following two Lemmas demonstrate that thin Schubert cells are compatible with decomposition into connected components and removal of loops and parallel elements.
Lemma

C.1
appears
[
21
, Proposition 9.4]
without proof, and
Lemma

C.2
will appear in an upcoming paper
[
4
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we sketch their proofs.

Lemma C.1
.

If
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
, then
\Gr_{M}\cong\Gr_{M_{1}}\times\Gr_{M_{2}}
. In particular,
\Gr_{M}\cong\Gr_{M|T}
where
T\subset[n]
is the set of non-loop elements.

Proof.

Suppose
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are matrices giving rise to the rings
R_{M_{1}}^{x}
,
R_{M_{2}}^{x}
as in
2.2
. Let
X
be the block matrix with
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
on the diagonal. Then
R_{M}^{x}\cong R_{M_{1}}^{x}\otimes R_{M_{2}}^{x}
. The second statement follow from
M\cong M|T\oplus U(0,|T|)
.
∎

Given a matroid
M
, we define a simple matroid by removing loops and parallel elements in the following way. Let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}
be the rank 1 flats of
M
, choose nonloop elements

and set

. Then

is a simple matroid. Let

be the number of loops in

.

Lemma C.2
.

We have an isomorphism
\Gr_{M}\cong\Gr_{M|S}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n-k-\ell}
.

Proof.

By

C.1
, we may assume that
M
has no loops.
Suppose
i,j
are parallel in
M
, then
\Gr_{M}\cong\Gr_{M_{[n]\setminus i}}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.
Suppose
i
and

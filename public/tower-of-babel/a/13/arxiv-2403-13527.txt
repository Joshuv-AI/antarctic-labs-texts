The global Σ n + 1 1 \Sigma^{1}_{n+1} -Uniformization Property and 𝖡𝖯𝖥𝖠 \mathsf{BPFA}
Stefan Hoffelner

Abstract
We show that given a reflecting cardinal, one can produce a model of 𝖡𝖯𝖥𝖠 \mathsf{BPFA} where the Σ n 1 \Sigma^{1}_{n} -uniformization property holds simultaneously for all n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 .

Introduction

Given

, we say that

is a uniformization (or a uniformizing function) of

such that

dom(f)=pr_{1}(A)=\{x\in 2^{\omega}\,:\,\exists y((x,y)\in A\}

and the graph of

Definition 1.1

(Uniformization Property)
.

We say that the projective pointclass
\Gamma\in\{\Sigma^{1}_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}\cup\{\Pi^{1}_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}
has the uniformization
property iff every
\Gamma
-set in the plane admits a uniformization
whose graph is in
\Gamma
, i.e. the relation
(x,y)\in f
is in
\Gamma
.

It is a classical result due to M. Kondo that lightface
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-sets do have the
uniformization property, this also yields the uniformization property for
\Sigma^{1}_{2}
-sets. This is all
\mathsf{ZFC}
can prove about the uniformization property of projective sets. In the constructible universe
L
, for every
n\geq 3
,
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
does have the uniformization property which follows from the existence of a good wellorder by an old result of Addison (see
[
1
]
). Recall that a
\Delta^{1}_{n}
-definable wellorder
<
of the reals is a
good

\Delta^{1}_{n}
-wellorder if
<
is of ordertype
\omega_{1}
and the relation
<_{I}\subset(\omega^{\omega})^{2}
defined via

x<_{I}y\Leftrightarrow\{(x)_{n}\,:\,n\in\omega\}=\{z\,:\,z<y\}

where

is some fixed recursive partition of

into
\omega
-many reals, is a
\Delta^{1}_{n}
-definable. It is easy to check that the canonical wellorder of the reals in

is a good

-wellorder so the
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
-uniformization property follows for

. The very same argument also shows that inner models of the form

for

a normal,
\kappa
-complete ultrafilter on
\kappa
share the same
L
-like pattern for uniformization.

On the other hand, large cardinal assumptions draw a very different picture. Due to the celebrated result of Moschovakis (see
[
14
]
39.9),
\mathsf{PD}
implies that
\Pi^{1}_{2n+1}
and
\Sigma^{1}_{2n+2}
-sets have the uniformization property for

. By the famous Martin-Steel result (see
[
15
]
, Theorem 13.6.), the assumption of infinitely many Woodin cardinals outright implies
\mathsf{PD}
, and hence large cardinals fully settle the behaviour of the uniformization property within the projective hierarchy.

The connection of
\mathsf{PD}
with forcing axioms is established via core model induction. Under the assumption of the proper forcing axiom, Schimmerling, Steelt and Woodin showed that
\mathsf{PD}
is true (in fact much more is true, see
[
17
]
), thus also under
\mathsf{PFA}
the

-uniformization holds for

.
As the uniformization property for one pointclass rules out the uniformization property of the dual pointclass, the behaviour of sets of reals in
L
and under
\mathsf{PFA}
contradict each other.

It is natural to investigate uniformization in the presence of weaker forcing axioms, the bounded proper forcing axiom
\mathsf{BPFA}
(introduced by Goldstern-Shelah, see
[
6
]
) being the paradigmatic example together with Martin’s Axiom.
A first step was taken in
[
10
]
, where it is shown that
\mathsf{BPFA}
and “
\omega_{1}
is not inaccessible to reals
"
outright imply that the boldface
\bf{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-uniformization property holds.
However
Addisons result from 1959 has been the only tool to obtain the
L
-like global uniformization pattern. As a consequence the known universes which satisfy the
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
uniformization property for all

are quite special, in particular they must satisfy
\mathsf{CH}
, hence
\mathsf{BPFA}
fails in such universes.

The main goal of this paper is to produce a new method which will force the
L
-like uniformization pattern. We will introduce a family of
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
-predicates whose truth value can be switched from false to true using carefully designed forcings which are

-preserving but not proper. These predicates in turn will be used to define an iterated forcing construction yielding a universe in which the
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
-uniformization property holds for each

simultaneously. The definitions of the uniformizing functions are robust under additional usage of proper forcings, which opens the possibility of forcing
\mathsf{BPFA}
simultaneously.

Theorem 1.2

Working in

and the

-uniformization property for every

The forcing method which yields the

-uniformization property seems to be quite flexible and we expect several further applications.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

be a bookkeeping function. We say that an iteration

4

Recall first that
\kappa

-elementary in the universe

A weighted Minkowski theorem for pseudo-cones
Rolf Schneider

Abstract
A nonempty closed convex set in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} , not containing the origin, is called a pseudo-cone if with every x x it also contains λ ​ x \lambda x for x ≥ 1 x\geq 1 . We consider pseudo-cones with a given recession cone C C , called C C -pseudo-cones. The family of C C -pseudo-cones can, with reasonable justification, be considered as a counterpart to the family of convex bodies containing the origin in the interior. For a C C -pseudo-cone one can naturally define a surface area measure and a covolume. Since they are in general infinite, we introduce a weighting, leading to modified versions of surface area and covolume. These are finite and still homogeneous, though of other degrees. Our main result is a Minkowski type existence theorem for C C -pseudo-cones with given weighted surface area measure. Keywords: pseudo-cone, recession cone, surface area measure, covolume, weighting, Minkowski’s existence theorem 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 52A20, Secondary 52A38



1
Introduction

A pseudo-cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a nonempty closed convex set
K
with the property that
o\notin K
(where
o
denotes the origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) and that
\lambda K\subseteq K
for all
\lambda\geq 1
. Like convex bodies containing the origin in the interior, pseudo-cones admit a kind of polarity, now called copolarity, which is defined by

K^{*}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq-1\mbox{ for all }y\in K\},

where
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
denotes the scalar product of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. If
K
is a pseudo-cone, then its copolar set
K^{*}
is again a pseudo-cone, and
K^{**}=K
. Copolarity (though not under this name) was pointed out in
[
3
]
as a special example within a systematic study of dualities and was investigated in
[
19
]
, and pseudo-cones were treated in
[
13
,
18
]
.

If
K
is a pseudo-cone and
{\rm rec}\,K
is its recession cone, then
K\subset{\rm rec}\,K
, and
{\rm rec}\,K^{*}=({\rm rec}\,K)^{\circ}
, where
C^{\circ}
denotes the
dual cone of a convex cone
C
. In the following, we will deal with pseudo-cones with given recession cone
C
, which we call
C
-pseudo-cones. Of the cone
C
, we always assume that it is pointed and
n
-dimensional. If
C
is given, the subsets

\Omega_{C}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C,\qquad\Omega_{C^{\circ}}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}

of the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
(where
{\rm int}
denotes the interior) will play an important role.

With their copolarity, pseudo-cones are an interesting counterpart to convex bodies containing the origin in the interior. Many notions familiar for convex bodies can be carried over to pseudo-cones. We mention first the surface area measure. Let
K
be a
C
-pseudo-cone. Its surface area measure
S_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
is the image measure of the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, restricted to
\partial K\cap{\rm int}\,C
, under a suitably defined spherical image map. Here
\partial K
denotes the boundary of
K
, and the spherical image map is only defined at boundary points of
K
where there exists a unique outer unit normal vector, and this is in
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. The surface area measure
S_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
is a Borel measure, but contrary to the case of convex bodies, it is only defined on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, and it may be infinite. The latter fact poses new problems, in particular if one asks for an analogue of Minkowski’s existence and uniqueness theorem.

For convex bodies, Minkowski’s problem asks for necessary and sufficient conditions on a Borel measure on the unit sphere to be the surface area measure of a convex body, and for the degree of uniqueness of such a body. The answers are well known. However, necessary and sufficient conditions on a Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
to be the surface area measure of a
C
-pseudo-cone are not known. It was shown in
[
17
]
that every nonzero finite Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is the surface area measure of a
C
-pseudo-cone. This pseudo-cone
K
has the additional property of being
C
-close, which means that
C\setminus K
has finite volume (and among
C
-close pseudo-cones,
K
is unique). For finite measures, Minkowski type theorems for pseudo-cones were also investigated in the
L_{p}
Brunn–Minkowski theory, by Yang, Ye and Zhu
[
20
]
, and for dual curvature measures by Li, Ye and Zhu
[
11
]
. Much less progress has been made for infinite measures. In
[
18
]
, a sufficient condition on a not necessarily finite Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
to be the surface area measure of a
C
-close pseudo-cone was found. It remained open whether this condition is also necessary. For a necessary condition satisfied by surface area measures of general
C
-pseudo-cones, we refer to
[
17
, Sect. 4]
.

Unlike convex bodies, pseudo-cones have the property that far away from
o
their shape is rather restricted: if the distance from
o
tends to infinity, a
C
-pseudo-cone looks more and more like its recession cone
C
. Thus, close to the origin the surface area measure of a pseudo-cone has higher influence on the shape of the pseudo-cone and should, therefore, be given higher weight. For convex bodies, weighted Minkowski problems have been considered before, see Livshyts
[
12
]
and Krysonov and Langharst
[
9
]
. For
C
-pseudo-cones, a weighting seems particularly natural, since it allows to neglect those regions where the surface area measure has decreasing influence on the shape of the pseudo-cone.

Therefore, we define the following class of functions. The simplifying assumptions are appropriate for our purposes.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Theta:C\setminus\{o\}\to(0,\infty)
be continuous and homogeneous of degree
-q
, where
q\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

An example is given by
\Theta(y):=\|y\|^{-q}
for
y\in C\setminus\{o\}
. To give another example, we recall from
[
18
, Sect. 2]
that we can choose a unit vector
{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\rm int}\,C
with
-{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
; this vector will be fixed in the following. Then
\Theta(y):=\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle^{-q}
,
y\in C\setminus\{o\}
, also satisfies the assumptions.

Let
K
be a
C
-pseudo-cone, and write
\partial_{i}K:=\partial K\cap{\rm int}\,C
. Let
\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K
be the set of boundary points
y\in\partial_{i}K
where the outer unit normal vector
\nu_{K}(y)
is unique and belongs to
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. (Possibly
\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K=\emptyset
, namely if
K=C+z
with
z\in C\setminus\{o\}
.) It is known that the set
\sigma_{K}
of boundary points of
K
where the outer unit normal vector is not unique satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma_{K})=0
(where
\mathcal{H}^{k}
denotes the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure) and that
\nu_{K}:\partial K\setminus\sigma_{K}\to{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is continuous (
{\rm cl}
denotes the closure). Therefore, it makes sense to define

S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\omega):=\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)

for Borel sets
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. This yields a Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, which we call the
\Theta
-weighted surface area measure
of
K
. For convex bodies, surface area measures with respect to a given measure were introduced in
[
12
]
. Weighted surface area measures appear also in
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
.

We shall prove the following weighted Minkowski theorem for pseudo-cones.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Theta
be according to Definition
1
, and suppose that
n-1<q<n
. Then
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)
is finite for every
C
-pseudo-cone
K
.

Let
\varphi
be a nonzero, finite Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Then there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
with

S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)=\varphi.

In general,
K
is not unique up to translation, as we will explain after the proof of Theorem
1
.

In the next section, we fix some notation and collect elementary facts about pseudo-cones. Section
3
introduces weighted surface area measures and proves a weak continuity property. Then weighted covolumes are considered. Section
5
deals with Wulff shapes, here yielding pseudo-cones. This serves as a preparation for Section
6
, where a variational lemma is proved. In principle, and for convex bodies, this goes back to Aleksandrov. However, his proof, involving Minkowski’s inequalities for mixed volumes, cannot be used, and we have to adapt to pseudo-cones an approach found by Huang, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
7
]
. Then follows the proof of Theorem
1
.

2
Preliminaries on pseudo-cones

We have already said that we work in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) and that
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is the unit sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Further,
B^{n}
is the unit ball. By
\mathcal{B}(\cdot)
we denote the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets of a given space.

We repeat that a pointed,
n
-dimensional closed convex cone
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is given. We recall that

C^{\circ}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 0\mbox{ for all }y\in C\}

is its dual cone. The recession cone of a nonempty closed convex set
A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
can be defined by

{\rm rec}\,A=\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:A+z\subseteq A\}

(cf.
[
14
, §8]
). The set of all
C
-pseudo-cones, that is, pseudo-cones with recession cone
C
, is denoted by
ps(C)
.

We use the unit vector
\mathfrak{v}\in({\rm int}\,C)\cap(-{\rm int}\,C^{\circ})
to define, for
t>0
, the sets

C(t):=\{y\in C:\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle=t\},\quad C^{-}(t):=\{y\in C:\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle\leq t\}.

These sets are compact.

Convergence of
C
-pseudo-cones is defined as in
[
18
]
:

Definition 2
.

If
K_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are
C
-pseudo-cones, we write

K_{j}\to K_{0}

and say that
(K_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to
K_{0}
if there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t_{0})\not=\emptyset
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}(K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t))=K_{0}\cap C^{-}(t)\quad\mbox{for all }t\geq t_{0},

where the latter means convergence of convex bodies with respect to the Hausdorff metric
d_{H}
.

We mention the following counterpart to the Blaschke selection theorem (see
[
18
, Lem. 1]
).

Lemma 1
.

Every sequence of
C
-pseudo-cones for which the distances from the origin are bounded and bounded away from zero has a subsequence that converges to a
C
-pseudo-cone.

In the following, we try to keep the notation close to that of
[
7
]
. There are differences, due to the non-compactness of pseudo-cones. One has also to be careful with parts of a
C
-pseudo-cone contained in the boundary of
C
. Moreover, the role of the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
in
[
7
]
is now split between its two subsets
\Omega_{C}
and
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, which are not closed. For greater clarity, we try to reserve the letter
u
for vectors in
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and the letter
v
for vectors in
\Omega_{C}
. Similarly, we often denote subsets of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
by
\omega
and subsets of
\Omega_{C}
by
\eta
.

Let
K\in ps(C)
. The
support function
of
K
is defined by

h_{K}(x):=\sup\{\langle x,y\rangle:y\in K\}\quad\mbox{for }x\in C^{\circ}.

The supremum is an attained maximum if
x\in{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
. For
x\in\partial C^{\circ}
, it may either be attained or not attained. We have restricted the domain to
C^{\circ}
to obtain a real-valued support function. The support function is continuous and sublinear. Since for pseudo-cones it is non-positive, we often write
\overline{h}_{K}:=-h_{K}
.

For
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
we denote by

H_{K}(u):=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle=h_{K}(u)\},\quad H^{-}_{K}(u):=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq h_{K}(u)\}

the
supporting hyperplane
of
K
with outer unit normal vector
u
and the halfspace containing
K
that it bounds.

The
radial function
of
K
is defined by

\varrho_{K}(y):=\min\{r\in{\mathbb{R}}:ry\in K\}\quad\mbox{ for }y\in C_{K},

where

C_{K}:=\{y\in C:ry\in K\mbox{ for some }r>0\}\supseteq{\rm int}\,C.

The latter inclusion holds since
C
is the recession cone of
K
. We point out that
\varrho_{K}(y)y\in\partial K
. Note that
C_{K}
is the maximal domain on which
\varrho_{K}
can be defined as a real number. We set also

\Omega_{C,K}:=C_{K}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}.

The radial function is continuous, positive, and homogeneous of degree
-1
.

For
u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
,

\displaystyle h_{K}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{\langle u,y\rangle:y\in K\}=\sup\{\langle u,y\rangle:y\in\partial K\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{\langle u,\varrho_{K}(v)v\rangle:v\in\Omega_{C,K}\}=\sup\{\langle u,\varrho_{K}(v)v\rangle:v\in\Omega_{C}\}

(where the last equality holds since
\Omega_{C,K}\subseteq{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C}
), hence

h_{K}(u)=\sup_{v\in\Omega_{C}}\langle u,v\rangle\varrho_{K}(v)\quad\mbox{for }u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}.

(1)

For
v\in\Omega_{C}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varrho_{K}(v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{1/r:rv\in K\}=\max\{1/r:\langle rv,u\rangle\leq h_{K}(u)\,\forall\,u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{1/r:\frac{|\langle v,u\rangle|}{\overline{h}_{K}(u)}\geq\frac{1}{r}\,\forall\,u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\lambda:\min_{u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\frac{|\langle v,u\rangle|}{\overline{h}_{K}(u)}\geq\lambda\right\},

hence

\frac{1}{\varrho_{K}(v)}=\min_{u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\frac{|\langle v,u\rangle|}{\overline{h}_{K}(u)}\quad\mbox{for }v\in\Omega_{C}.

(2)

Remark.

It follows from the derivation that the minimum is attained at
u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
if and only if
u
is an outer unit normal vector of
K
at
\varrho_{K}(v)v
.

The following three definitions can essentially be carried over from the case of convex bodies. As mentioned, the notation is kept close to that of
[
7
]
. We always assume that
K
is a given
C
-pseudo-cone.

Definition 3
.

The reverse spherical image of
\omega\subseteq\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
(with respect to
K
) is defined by

\bm{x}_{K}(\omega):=\{y\in\partial K:y\in H_{K}(u)\mbox{ for some }u\in\omega\}.

Recall that
\sigma_{K}
is the set of all
y\in\partial K
where the outer unit normal vector is not unique. Let
\omega_{K}
be the set of all
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
for which the set
\bm{x}_{K}(\{u\})
contains more than one element.

Definition 4
.

For
y\in\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K
, let
\nu_{K}(y)\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be the unique outer unit normal vector of
K
at
y
. The map
\nu_{K}:\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K\to\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is called the spherical image map of
K
.

For
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{K}
, let
x_{K}\in\partial K
be the unique element of
\bm{x}_{K}(\{u\})
. The map
x_{K}:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{K}\to\partial K
is called the reverse spherical image map of
K
.

The following assertions follow directly from the corresponding ones for convex bodies (see
[
7
, p. 339]
for references). As already mentioned, we have
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma_{K})=0
, and the spherical image map
\nu_{K}
is continuous on
\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K\setminus\sigma_{K}
. We have
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega_{K})=0
, and the reverse spherical image map
x_{K}
is continuous on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{K}
.

Definition 5
.

The radial map
r_{K}:\Omega_{C,K}\to\partial K
of
K
is defined by

r_{K}(v):=\varrho_{K}(v)v\quad\mbox{for }v\in\Omega_{C,K}.

We define
\eta_{K}:=r_{K}^{-1}(\sigma_{K})
.

The radial Gauss map
\alpha_{K}:\Omega_{C,K}\setminus\eta_{K}\to{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
of
K
is defined by

\alpha_{K}:=\nu_{K}\circ r_{K}.

We recall from
[
16
, Sect. 8]
that
K
is
C
-determined
by the nonempty, compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
if

K=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}H^{-}_{K}(u).

By
{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
we denote the set of
C
-pseudo-cones that are
C
-determined by
\omega
. If
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
, then the absolute support function
\overline{h}_{K}
, restricted to
\omega
, is positive and bounded away from zero. If
K
is
C
-determined by
\omega
, then
C\setminus K
is bounded, since
\omega
has a positive spherical distance from the boundary of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. A
C
-pseudo-cone
K
is called
C
-determined if it is
C
-determined by some nonempty, compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.

Given a pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
and a nonempty, compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, we can define a
C
-determined set by

K^{(\omega)}:=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}H^{-}_{K}(u).

(3)

For this construction, we shall need the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

If
K_{j}\in ps(C)
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, satisfy
K_{j}\to K_{0}
as
j\to\infty
and if
\emptyset\not=\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is compact, then

K_{j}^{(\omega)}\to K_{0}^{(\omega)}\quad\mbox{as }j\to\infty.

Proof.

First we note that the convergence
K_{j}\to K_{0}
implies the existence of a number
t_{0}
such that
K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t_{0})\not=\emptyset
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t)\to K\cap C^{-}(t)
for
t\geq t_{0}
, with convergence in the sense of the Hausdorff metric for convex bodies. Since
\omega
is a compact subset of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, we can choose
t_{0}
so large that
\bm{x}_{K_{j}}(\omega)\subset C^{-}(t_{0})
for all
j
. In particular,
h_{K_{j}}(u)\to h_{K_{0}}(u)
for
u\in\omega
.

We use Theorem 1.8.8 of
[
15
]
. Let
x\in K_{0}^{(\omega)}
. Let
x_{j}
be the point where the ray through
o
and
x
intersects the boundary of
K_{j}^{(\omega)}
. Then
x_{j}
is contained in the boundary of a halfspace
\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq h_{K_{j}}(u)\}
for some
u\in\omega
, and
x
is contained in the halfspace

\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq h_{K_{0}}(u)\}
. The boundaries of these halfspaces have distance
|h_{K_{j}}(u)-h_{K_{0}}(u)|\leq d_{H}(K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t_{0}),K_{0}\cap C^{-}(t_{0}))
. The angle
\alpha
between
x
and
u
satisfies
|\cos\alpha|\geq a
with some constant
a>0
, since
\omega
has a positive spherical distance from the boundary of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. It follows that
\|x_{j}-x\|\leq d_{H}(K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t_{0}),K_{0}\cap C^{-}(t_{0}))/a
. We conclude that
x=\lim_{j\to\infty}x_{j}
; thus (a) of
[
15
, Thm. 1.8.8]
is satisfied.

Suppose that
x_{j}\in K_{i_{j}}^{(\omega)}
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x_{j}\to x
. Then
x\in C
. For each
u\in\omega
we have
\langle x_{j},u\rangle\leq h_{K_{i_{j}}}(u)
for all
j
, hence also
\langle x,u\rangle\leq h_{K_{0}}(u)
. This shows that
x\in K_{0}^{(\omega)}
. We have proved that also (b) of
[
15
, Thm. 1.8.8]
is satisfied.
∎

3
Weighted surface area measures

Since in the following we deal with measures on the sphere, we confirm first the convention that the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is equipped with the usual spherical Lebesgue measure and integrals such as
\int_{\beta}\cdot\,{\rm d}u
, where
\beta\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is Lebesgue measurable, always refer to integration with respect to this measure. Also ‘measurable’ without further comment means ‘Lebesgue measurable’, and ‘almost all
u\in\beta
’ means all
u\in\beta
up to a subset of
\beta
of Lebesgue measure zero.

In the following, we assume that a function
\Theta
according to Definition
1
is given. In this section, we assume that
q>n-1
. For a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
, we denote by
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)
its
\Theta
-weighted surface area measure.

Lemma 3
.

If
q>n-1
, the measure
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)
is finite for every
C
-pseudo-cone
K
.

Proof.

First, let
y\in C\setminus\{o\}
. There exists
\lambda>0
with
\langle\lambda y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle=1
. Since
C(1)
is compact and
\Theta
is continuous on
C(1)
, there exists a number
c_{0}>0
with
\Theta(z)\leq c_{0}
for
z\in C(1)
. Hence,
\Theta(\lambda y)\leq c_{0}\langle\lambda y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle^{-q}
. Since
\Theta
is homogeneous of degree
-q
, it follows that

\Theta(y)\leq c_{0}\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle^{-q}\quad\mbox{for }y\in C\setminus\{o\}.

(4)

We choose
z\in{\rm int}\,K
, then the translated cone
C_{z}:=C+z
is contained in
K
(since
C
is the recession cone of
K
). Further, we choose
t_{0}>0
with
C(t_{0})\cap{\rm int}\,C_{z}\not=\emptyset
. Since

\int_{\partial K\cap C^{-}(t_{0})}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)<\infty,

it suffices to show that

\int_{\partial K\setminus C^{-}(t_{0})}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)<\infty.

To prove this, we want to use that the surface area of convex bodies is monotone under set inclusion. Therefore, we define
t_{j}:=t_{0}+j
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and the convex bodies

K_{j}:=\{y\in K:t_{j}\leq\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle\leq t_{j+1}\},

together with parts of their boundary,

S_{j}:=\{y\in\partial K_{j}:t_{j}<\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle<t_{j+1}\}.

We compare
K_{j}
with the cylinder

Z_{j}:=\left[K\cap C(t_{j+1})\right]+\{\lambda\mathfrak{v}:-1\leq\lambda\leq 0\}

and its boundary part

T_{j}:=\{y\in\partial Z_{j}:t_{j}<\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle<t_{j+1}\}.

We need also the
(n-2)
-dimensional sets

m_{j}:=\partial K\cap C(t_{j}).

Since
K_{j}\subset Z_{j}
we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{j})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(T_{j})+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}((T_{j}\setminus K_{j})\cap C(t_{j})).

Here we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(T_{j})=\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(m_{j+1}),

and since
C_{z}\subset K
, we can estimate

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}((T_{j}\setminus K_{j})\cap C(t_{j}))\leq c_{1}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(m_{j})\leq c_{1}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(m_{j+1})

with a constant
c_{1}
independent of
j
. Thus,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{j})\leq c_{2}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(m_{j+1})

(5)

with a constant
c_{2}
. Using (
4
) and (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K\setminus C^{-}(t_{0})}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)\leq c_{0}\int_{\partial K\setminus C^{-}(t_{0})}\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle^{-q}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle=c_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{S_{j}}\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle^{-q}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)\leq c_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{S_{j}}t_{j}^{-q}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}c_{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t_{j}^{-q}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(m_{j+1})\leq c_{0}c_{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t_{j}^{-q}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(\partial C\cap C(t_{j+1}))

\displaystyle=c_{0}c_{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t_{j}^{-q}t_{j+1}^{n-2}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(\partial C\cap C(1))<\infty,

as stated. It is now obvious why we have assumed that
q>n-1
.
∎

We need a lemma to transform integrals over
\partial_{i}K
to integrals over
\Omega_{C}
, via the radial map. For convex bodies, a corresponding lemma is proved in
[
7
, Lem. 2.9]
by using the divergence theorem for sets of finite perimeter (
[
4
, §5.8]
), and also in
[
8
, p. 171]
by employing the coarea formula for rectifiable manifolds (
[
5
, Thm. 3.2.22]
). The result appeared already as Lemma 1 in Aleksandrov
[
1
]
, but with a different interpretation of the surface area of a Borel set in the boundary of a convex body, not using the Hausdorff measure. The proof given in
[
8
]
works for pseudo-cones as well. Nevertheless, we want to indicate also an approach along more familiar lines.

Lemma 4
.

Let
K
be a
C
-pseudo-cone. Let
F:\partial_{i}K\to{\mathbb{R}}
be nonnegative and Borel measurable or
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-integrable. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\partial_{i}K}F(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)=\int_{\Omega_{C}}F(r_{K}(v))\frac{\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)}{\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))}\,{\rm d}v

(6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{C}}F(r_{K}(v))\frac{\varrho_{K}^{n-1}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K}(v)\rangle|}\,{\rm d}v.

(7)

Proof.

We recall from
[
16
, Sect. 10]
that the cone-volume measure of
K
is defined by

V_{K}(\omega):=\mathcal{H}^{n}\left(\bigcup_{y\in\bm{x}_{K}(\omega)}[o,y]\right)

for
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
, where
[o,y]
is the closed segment with endpoints
o
and
y
, and that the use of spherical coordinates gives

V_{K}(\omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)\,{\rm d}v

(8)

(where
\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega):=\{v\in\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{K}:\alpha_{K}(v)\in\omega\}
). By
[
16
, Lem. 9]
and the interpretation of
S_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
as the image measure of
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, restricted to
\partial_{i}K
, under the spherical image map we have

V_{K}(\omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\omega}\overline{h}_{K}(u)\,S_{n-1}(K,{\rm d}u)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\bm{x}_{K}(\omega)}|\langle y,\nu_{K}(y)\rangle|\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y).

(9)

Now let
\eta\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C})
. Relations (
8
) and (
9
), applied to
\omega=\alpha_{K}(\eta\setminus\eta_{K})
(so that
\bm{x}_{K}(\omega)=r_{K}(\eta)
, up to a set of
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-measure zero), show that

\int_{\partial_{i}K}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\eta}(r_{K}^{-1}(y))|\langle y,\nu_{K}(y)\rangle|\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)=\int_{\Omega_{C}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\eta}(v)\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)\,{\rm d}v.

Thus, the relation

\int_{\partial_{i}K}g(r_{K}^{-1}(y))|\langle y,\nu_{K}(y)\rangle|\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)=\int_{\Omega_{C}}g(v)\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)\,{\rm d}v

holds if
g
is the characteristic function of a Borel set in
\Omega_{C}
. By the usual extension procedure for integrals, it holds if
g
is any nonnegative measurable or integrable function on
\Omega_{C}
. With
g(v):=F(r_{K}(v))/\overline{h}_{L}(\alpha_{K}(v))
, we obtain (
6
), and the version (
7
) follows from
\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))=|\langle\varrho_{K}(v)v,\alpha_{K}(v)\rangle|
.
∎

For expressing integrals with respect to the weighted surface area measure as integrals over parts of
\Omega_{C}
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\omega\subseteq\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be a nonempty Borel set and
g:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded and measurable. Then

\int_{\omega}g(u)\,S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,{\rm d}u)=\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}g(\alpha_{K}(v))\Theta(r_{K}(v))\frac{\varrho^{n-1}_{K}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K}(v)\rangle|}\,{\rm d}v.

Proof.

With
F(y):={\mathbbm{1}}_{\omega}(\nu_{K}(y))g(\nu_{K}(y))\Theta(y)
, Lemma
4
yields

\int_{\partial_{i}K}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\omega}(\nu_{K}(y))g(\nu_{K}(y))\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)=\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}g(\alpha_{K}(v))\Theta(r_{K}(v))\frac{\varrho_{K}^{n-1}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K}(v(\rangle|}\,{\rm d}v.

By the definition of
S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
and the transformation formula for integrals, we have

\int_{\omega}g(u)\,S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,{\rm d}u)=\int_{\partial_{i}K}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\omega}(\nu_{K}(y))g(\nu_{K}(y))\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y).

These two equations together give the assertion.
∎

The following lemma shows that weighted surface area measures are weakly continuous on each set
{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
with compact
\omega
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be a nonempty, compact subset, and let
K_{j}\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then
K_{j}\to K_{0}
as
j\to\infty
implies the weak convergence
S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K_{j},\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K_{0},\cdot)
.

Proof.

Let
g:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded and continuous. By Lemma
5
we have

\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g(u)\,S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K_{j},{\rm d}u)=\int_{\Omega_{C}}g(\alpha_{K_{j}}(v))\Theta(r_{K_{j}}(v))\frac{\varrho_{K_{j}}^{n-1}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K_{j}}(v)\rangle|}\,{\rm d}v.

For almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
it holds that

g(\alpha_{K_{j}}(v))\Theta(r_{K_{j}}(v))\frac{\varrho_{K_{j}}^{n-1}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K_{j}}(v)\rangle|}\to g(\alpha_{K_{0}}(v))\Theta(r_{K_{0}}(v))\frac{\varrho_{K_{0}}^{n-1}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K_{0}}(v)\rangle|}

as
j\to\infty
. The functions on the left-hand side are uniformly bounded, since
K_{j}\to K_{0}
and
K_{j}
is
C
-determined by the compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Therefore,
\|r_{K_{j}}(v)\|
is bounded by a constant. Further,
|\langle v,\alpha_{K_{j}}(v)\rangle|
is bounded away from
0
, since the spherical distance from the boundary of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is continuous and strictly positive on
\omega
. Now the dominated convergence theorem proves the assertion.
∎

4
Weighted covolumes

Additionally to
n-1<q
, in this section we also assume that
q<n
. This assumption ensures that

\int_{B^{n}\cap C}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y)<\infty.

(10)

In fact, since for
y\in C
we have
\Theta(y)\leq c_{0}\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle^{-q}
by (
4
) and
\langle y,\mathfrak{v}\rangle\geq c_{1}\|y\|
for
y\in C
with some constant
c_{1}
(because of
-{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
), (
10
) follows from

\int_{B^{n}\cap C}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y)\leq c_{0}c_{1}^{-q}\int_{B^{n}}\|y\|^{-q}\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y)=c_{0}c_{1}^{-q}\int_{0}^{1}r^{-q}r^{n-1}\omega_{n}\,{\rm d}r<\infty,

where
\omega_{n}
is the surface area of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. By homogeneity and the positivity of
\Theta
it follows that

\int_{C^{-}(t)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y)<\infty

(11)

for all
t>0
.

For a Borel subset
A\subset C
we define

\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}(A):=\int_{A}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y),

(12)

and for a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
we call

V_{\Theta}(K):=\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}(C\setminus K)

the
\Theta
-weighted covolume
of
K
. For general
\Theta
, this can be infinite.

Lemma 7
.

If
n-1<q<n
, the
\Theta
-weighted covolume is finite and continuous (in the sense that
K_{j}\to K
implies
V_{\Theta}(K_{j})\to V_{\Theta}(K)
).

Proof.

Let
K\in ps(C)
be a
C
-pseudo-cone. We choose
t>0
so that
z:=t\mathfrak{v}\in K
and set
C_{z}:=C+z
. Since
{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\rm int}\,C
and
C
is the recession cone of
K
, the number
t
exists and we have
C_{z}\subseteq K
. Because of (
11
), it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}((C\setminus K)\setminus C^{-}(t))<\infty
. Using (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}((C\setminus K)\setminus C^{-}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{(C\setminus K)\cap C(\tau)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{0}\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{(C\setminus K)\cap C(\tau)}\tau^{-q}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{0}\int_{t}^{\infty}\tau^{-q}\left[\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(C\cap C(\tau))-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(C_{z}\cap C(\tau))\right]\,{\rm d}\tau.

Writing
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(C\cap C(1))=:c_{2}
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(C\cap C(\tau))=\tau^{n-1}c_{2}
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(C_{z}\cap C(\tau))=(\tau-t)^{n-1}c_{2}
, hence

\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}((C\setminus K)\setminus C^{-}(t))\leq c_{0}c_{2}\int_{t}^{\infty}\tau^{-q}[\tau^{n-1}-(\tau-t)^{n-1}]\,{\rm d}\tau<\infty,

showing the finiteness of the
\Theta
-weighted covolume on
C
-pseudo-cones.

To prove the continuity, let
K,K_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be
C
-pseudo-cones with
K_{j}\to K
as
j\to\infty
. We choose
t,z,C_{z}
as above, but now such that
z\in{\rm int}\,K
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. It follows from the argument above that there exists a number
s>0
such that
z\in{\rm int}\,C^{-}(s)
and

\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}((C\setminus C_{z})\setminus C^{-}(s))<\varepsilon/2.

(13)

Then we choose a number
s_{0}>0
with
K\cap C^{-}(s_{0})=\emptyset
and a constant
b
with

\Theta(y)\leq b\quad\mbox{for}\quad y\in C^{-}(s)\setminus C^{-}(s_{0}).

We remark that for convex bodies
A,B\subset C^{-}(s)
with
d_{H}(A,B)\leq\delta
(for some
\delta>0
), we have
A\setminus B\subseteq(B+\delta B^{n})\setminus B
and hence
V_{n}(A\setminus B)\leq V_{n}(B+\delta B^{n})-V_{n}(B)
. Using the Steiner formula and the monotonicity of its coefficients, we obtain
V_{n}(A\setminus B)\leq\delta a
with a constant
a
depending only on
C^{-}(s)
.

Now we choose
\delta>0
such that, for
L\in ps(C)
, the inequality

d_{H}(K\cap C^{-}(s),L\cap C^{-}(s))\leq\delta

(14)

implies that
z\in L
,
L\cap C^{-}(s_{0})=\emptyset
and
4ab\delta\leq\varepsilon
.

Let
L\in ps(C)
be such that (
14
) is satisfied. Then

V_{n}((K\setminus L)\cap C^{-}(s))\leq\delta a,\quad V_{n}((L\setminus K)\cap C^{-}(s))\leq\delta a

and therefore

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}((C\setminus K)\cap C^{-}(s))-\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}((C\setminus L)\cap C^{-}(s))|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{(K\setminus L)\cap C^{-}(s)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y)\right|+\left|\int_{(L\setminus K)\cap C^{-}(s)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2ab\delta\leq\varepsilon/2.

Together with (
13
) this shows that

|\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}(C\setminus K)-\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}(C\setminus L)|\leq\varepsilon.

There is a number
j_{0}
such that for
j\geq j_{0}
we have
d_{H}(K\cap C^{-}(s),K_{j}\cap C^{-}(s))\leq\delta
. For
j\geq j_{0}
we then have
|V_{\Theta}(K)-V_{\Theta}(K_{j})|\leq\varepsilon
.
∎

5
Wulff shapes

We use Wulff shapes in given cones, as in
[
16
]
. Let
\emptyset\not=\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be a compact set and
h:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
a positive continuous function. We define

[h]:=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq-h(u)\}

and call this set the
Wulff shape
associated with
(C,\omega,h)
. It is nonempty, since
h
is bounded. We point out that the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\omega,h)
belongs to
{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
. This follows immediately from the definition. In particular,
[h]
is a
C
-pseudo-cone.

Relation (2.24) of
[
7
]
takes a different form. For
v\in\Omega_{C}
,

\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h]}(v)}=\min_{u\in\omega}\frac{|\langle v,u\rangle|}{h(u)},

(15)

where the minimum is attained. This follows from

\displaystyle\varrho_{[h]}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{r>0:rv\in[h]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{r>0:\langle rv,u\rangle\leq-h(u)\mbox{ for all }u\in\omega\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{r>0:\langle rv,u\rangle(-h(u))^{-1}\geq 1\mbox{ for all }u\in\omega\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{r>0:r\min_{u\in\omega}|\langle v,u\rangle|h(u)^{-1}\geq 1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\min_{u\in\omega}|\langle v,u\rangle|h(u)^{-1}}.

The following is Lemma 5 in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 8
.

If
h_{i}
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are positive continuous functions on
\omega
and
h_{i}\to h_{0}
uniformly on
\omega
, as
i\to\infty
, then
[h_{i}]\to[h_{0}]
.

Let
\omega
and
h
be as above, and let
h:\omega\to(0,\infty)
and
f:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous and
\delta>0
. Define
h_{t}
by

\log h_{t}(u):=\log h(u)+tf(u)+o(t,u),\quad u\in\omega,

for
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
, where the function
o(t,\cdot):\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous and satisfies
o(0,\cdot)=0
and
\lim_{t\to 0}o(t,\cdot)/t=0
uniformly on
\omega
. The number
\delta
will later be chosen appropriately. Let
[h_{t}]
be the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\omega,h_{t})
. We call
[h_{t}]
a logarithmic family of Wulff shapes associated with
(C,\omega,h,f)
.

6
A variational lemma

Aleksandrov’s variational lemma, with its original proof, is reproduced as Lemma 7.5.3 in
[
15
]
. This is an essential part of the classical approach to Minkowski’s existence theorem. Its proof makes use of Minkowski’s inequalities for mixed volumes. It was carried over to pseudo-cones in
[
16
]
, but the method does not permit other desired generalizations. Fortunately, Huang, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
7
]
found a way to prove this lemma in a different way, and this approach also allowed to treat weighted surface area measures of convex bodies, see Kryvonos and Langharst
[
9
]
. We need a counterpart of this approach for pseudo-cones. In the following, we adapt the proofs of
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
to this case.

First we carry over Lemma 4.3 of
[
7
]
to pseudo-cones. We emphasize that the main ideas are taken from
[
7
]
, but our proof would be different even for convex bodies, since we avoid the use of convexifications and polarity. In
[
7
]
these are employed to obtain a whole series of variational lemmas.

In the following, we restrict ourselves to compact sets
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and then to
C
-pseudo-cones that are determined by
\omega
. The reason is that we need some compactness arguments. Let
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
, and recall (from Definition
5
) that
\eta_{K}
denotes the set of all
v\in\Omega_{C}
for which the outer unit normal vector of
K
at
\varrho_{K}(v)v
is not unique. If
v\in\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{K}
, then the supporting hyperplane of
K
at
\varrho_{K}(v)v
is unique, hence its outer unit normal vector belongs to
\omega
. In particular, the radial Gauss map
\alpha_{K}
of
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
maps
\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{K}
into
\omega
. This will be important.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be nonempty and compact, and let
h_{0}:\omega\to(0,\infty)
and
f:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous. If
[h_{t}]
is a logarithmic family of Wulff shapes associated with
(C,\omega,h_{0},f)
, where

\log h_{t}(u)=\log h_{0}(u)+tf(u)+o(t,u)

for
u\in\omega
, then for almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
,

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)}{t}=f(\alpha_{[h_{0}]}(v)).

(16)

Proof.

We fix a vector
v\in\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{[h_{0}]}
. The set
\eta_{[h_{0}]}
has spherical Lebesgue measure zero.

By formula (
15
) (where the minimum is attained if
v\in\Omega_{C}
), there exists
u_{t}\in\omega
such that

\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}=\frac{|\langle v,u_{t}\rangle|}{h_{t}(u_{t})}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}\leq\frac{|\langle v,u\rangle|}{h_{t}(u)}\quad\mbox{for all }u\in\omega.

(17)

By (
17
), for
t=0
,

|\langle\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)v,u_{0}\rangle|=h_{0}(u_{0}).

Since
[h_{0}]
is the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\omega,h_{0})
, we have
h_{0}(u_{0})\leq\overline{h}_{[h_{0}]}(u_{0})
, hence

|\langle\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)v,u_{0}\rangle|\leq\overline{h}_{[h_{0}]}(u_{0})=\min_{x\in\partial[h_{0}]}|\langle x,u_{0}\rangle|.

From
\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)v\in\partial[h_{0}]
it follows that the equality sign holds here, hence
u_{0}
is an (in fact the unique) outer unit normal vector of
[h_{0}]
at
\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)v
, thus

u_{0}=\alpha_{[h_{0}]}(v).

(18)

We state that

\lim_{t\to 0}u_{t}=u_{0}.

(19)

We argue as in
[
7
]
(proof of (4.8)), with the necessary changes. Since
\omega
is compact, there exists a sequence
(u_{t_{k}})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
t_{k}\to 0
that converges to some
u^{\prime}\in\omega
. It suffices to prove that every convergent sequence
(u_{t_{k}})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
t_{k}\to 0
converges to
u_{0}
. Suppose that
t_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{t_{k}}=u^{\prime}\in\omega.

Since
h_{t_{k}}\to h_{0}
uniformly on
\omega
and since
h_{0}>0
on
\omega
, we get

\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{t_{k}}]}(v)}=\frac{|\langle v,u_{t_{k}}\rangle|}{h_{t_{k}}(u_{t_{k}})}\to\frac{|\langle v,u^{\prime}\rangle|}{h_{0}(u^{\prime})}.

(20)

From Lemma
8
it follows that also

\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{t_{k}}]}(v)}\to\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{o}]}(v)},

hence

\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{o}]}(v)}=\frac{|\langle v,u^{\prime}\rangle|}{h_{0}(u^{\prime})}.

As noted above, we have
h_{0}(u)\leq\overline{h}_{[h_{0}]}(u)
, hence

\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{o}]}(v)}\geq\frac{|\langle v,u^{\prime}\rangle|}{\overline{h}_{[h_{0}]}(u^{\prime})}\geq\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{o}]}(v)},

the latter by (
2
). Since here the equality sign holds, the remark after (
2
) shows that
u^{\prime}
is an outer unit normal vector of
[h_{0}]
at
\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)v
and hence coincides with
u_{0}
. This proves (
19
).

By (
2
) we have

\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)}\leq\frac{|\langle v,u_{t}\rangle|}{\overline{h}_{[h_{0}]}(u_{t})}.

(21)

Using (
17
), (
21
), the inequality
\overline{h}_{[h_{0}]}\geq h_{0}
, and the definition of
h_{t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log h_{t}(u_{t})-\log|\langle v,u_{t}\rangle|-\log\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log h_{t}(u_{t})-\log\overline{h}_{[h_{0}]}(u_{t})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log h_{t}(u_{t})-\log h_{0}(u_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tf(u_{t})+o(t,u_{t}).

Using (
17
) (for
t=0
) and then (
15
), we get

\displaystyle\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log h_{0}(u_{0})+\log|\langle v,u_{0}\rangle|

(23)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\log h_{t}(u_{0})-\log h_{0}(u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tf(u_{0})+o(t,u_{0}).

From (
22
) and (
23
) we see that

tf(u_{0})+o(t,u_{0})\leq\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)\leq tf(u_{t})+o(t,u_{t}).

(24)

These inequalities, together with the continuity of the function
f
and the limit relation (
19
), yield

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)}{t}=f(u_{0})=f(\alpha_{[h_{0}]}(v))

and thus the assertion (
16
).
∎

We adapt Lemma 2.6 of
[
9

to
C

Lemma 10
.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be nonempty and compact, let
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
. Let
f:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous. There is a constant
\delta>0
such that the function
h_{t}
defined by

h_{t}(u):=\overline{h}_{K}(u)+tf(u),\quad u\in\omega,

is positive for
|t|\leq\delta
. Let
[h_{t}]
be the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\omega,h_{t})
, for
|t|\leq\delta
.

\rm(a)
For almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
,

\frac{{\rm d}\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}=\frac{f(\alpha_{K}(v))}{\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))}\varrho_{K}(v).

\rm(b)
There is a constant
M
with

|\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)|\leq M|t|

for all
v\in\Omega_{C}
and all
|t|\leq\delta
.

Proof.

Since
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
, the absolute support function of
K
is bounded away from
0
on
\omega
, hence we can choose
\delta_{1}>0
such that
h_{t}(u)>0
for all
u\in\omega
if
|t|\leq\delta_{1}
. From the Taylor expansion of
\log(1+x)
we get

\log h_{t}=\log\overline{h}_{K}+\frac{tf}{\overline{h}_{K}}+o(t,\cdot)\quad\mbox{on }\omega,

so that
[h_{t}]
is a logarithmic family of Wulff shapes associated with
(C,\omega,\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega},f/\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega})
. Therefore, Lemma
9
can be applied, which yields (with
h_{0}=\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}
and hence
[h_{0}]=K
)

\frac{{\rm d}\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}=\frac{f(\alpha_{[h_{0}]}(v))}{\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{[h_{0}]}(v))}=\frac{f(\alpha_{K}(v))}{\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))}

for almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
. From

\frac{{\rm d}\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}=\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{0}]}(v)}\frac{{\rm d}\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}

we get

\frac{{\rm d}\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}=\frac{f(\alpha_{K}(v))}{\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))}\varrho_{K}(v),

which is assertion (a), if
\delta\leq\delta_{1}
.

From (a) we further get, for almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
,

\Big|\frac{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}\Big|-\Big|\frac{{\rm d}\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\leq\Big|\frac{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}-\frac{{\rm d}\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\Big|\leq 1,

say, for
|t|\leq\delta_{2}
, if
\delta_{2}>0
is suitably chosen. This gives

\Big|\frac{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}\Big|\leq\Big|\frac{{\rm d}\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}+1=\varrho_{K}(v)\Big|\frac{{\rm d}\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}+1.

Here
\varrho_{K}
is bounded since
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
, and by Lemma
9
,

\Big|\frac{{\rm d}\log\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}=\frac{f}{\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}}(\alpha_{K}(v)),

where
f/\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}
is bounded. This, together with the continuity of radial functions, proves assertion (b), for
\delta\leq\delta_{2}
.
∎

Let
\Theta
be according to Definition
1
, with
n-1<q<n
. For a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
, we recall that the measure
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is defined as the image measure of the measure given by

\beta\mapsto\int_{\beta}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y),\quad\beta\in\mathcal{B}(\partial_{i}K),

under the spherical image map, and that

V_{\Theta}(K):=\int_{C\setminus K}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y).

As shown in Lemmas
3
and
7
, the weighted surface area measure
S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
and the weighted covolume
V_{\Theta}
are finite.

The following adapts Lemma 2.7 of
[
9
]
to
C
-full pseudo-cones (but we need only special densities
\Theta
as considered here).

Lemma 11
.

Let
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
, for some nonempty, compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Let
\Theta
,
S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
and
V_{\Theta}
be as above. Let
f:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous, and let
[\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}+tf]
be the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\omega,\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}+tf)
. Then

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{V_{\Theta}([\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}+tf])-V_{\Theta}(K)}{t}=\int_{\omega}f(u)\,S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,{\rm d}u).

Proof.

We write
h_{t}(u):=\overline{h}_{K}(u)+tf(u)
for
u\in\omega
and small enough
|t|
and use spherical polar coordinates to obtain

V_{\Theta}([h_{t}])=\int_{\Omega_{C}}\int_{0}^{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}\Theta(rv)r^{n-1}\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}v=\int_{\Omega_{C}}F_{t}(v)\,{\rm d}v,

where

F_{t}(v):=\int_{0}^{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}\Theta(rv)r^{n-1}\,{\rm d}r

for
v\in\Omega_{C}
. This gives

\displaystyle\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{\varrho_{K}(v)}^{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}\Theta(rv)r^{n-1}\,{\rm d}r

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}\cdot\frac{1}{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}\int_{\varrho_{K}(v)}^{\varrho_{[h_{t}]}(v)}\Theta(rv)r^{n-1}\,{\rm d}r.

As
t\to 0
, the first factor converges, by Lemma
10
, to
\varrho_{K}(v)f(\alpha_{K}(v))/\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))
, for almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
. The second factor converges to
\Theta(\varrho_{K}(v)v)\varrho^{n-1}_{K}(v)=\Theta(r_{K}(v))\varrho^{n-1}_{K}(v)
. Thus, we get

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}=\Theta(r_{K}(v))\frac{f(\alpha_{K}(v))}{\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))}\varrho^{n}_{K}(v)

for almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
.

By (
25
) and Lemma
10
we have

\left|\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}\right|\leq M^{\prime}

with some constant
M^{\prime}
independent of
t
(for sufficiently small
|t|
), hence the dominated convergence theorem allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{V_{\Theta}([h_{t}])-V_{\Theta}(K)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{C}}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{C}}\Theta(r_{K}(v))\frac{f(\nu_{K}(r_{K}(v))}{\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))}\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial_{i}K}f(\nu_{K}(y))\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}f(u)\,S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,{\rm d}u).

Here we have used Lemma
4
and the definition of
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)
, together with the transformation formula for integrals. The final integral is only over
\omega
, since

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\nu_{K}^{-1}(\Omega_{C}\setminus\omega))=0

for the set
K
determined by
\omega
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

We assume that
\Theta
is chosen according to Definition
1
, with
n-1<q<n
. Under this assumption, we have already proved in Lemmas
3
and
7
that
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)
and
V_{\Theta}(K)
are finite for every
C
-pseudo-cone
K
.

First we state two lemmas, which will later be needed.

Lemma 12
.

There is a constant
c
, depending only on
C
and
\Theta
, such that every pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
with
V_{\Theta}(K)=1
satisfies
\overline{h}_{K}\leq c
.

Proof.

Let
r
be the maximal radius of a ball with center
o
having empty intersection with
{\rm int}\,K
. Since
rB^{n}\cap K\not=\emptyset
, every supporting hyperplane of
K
with an outer normal vector
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
intersects
rB^{n}
, hence
\overline{h}_{K}(u)\leq r
. From
V_{\Theta}(K)=1
it follows that
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}(C\cap rB^{n})<1
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}
is homogeneous of degree
n-q>0
, we get
1>\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}(C\cap rB^{n})=r^{n-q}\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\Theta}(C\cap B^{n})
, which gives the desired bound.
∎

For a pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
and a nonempty compact set
\beta\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, we recall from (
3
) that the set
K^{(\beta)}\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\beta)
was defined by

K^{(\beta)}:=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\beta}H^{-}(K,u).

Lemma 13
.

Let
K\in ps(C)
and let
\omega,\beta\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be nonempty compact sets with
\omega\subset{\rm int}\,\beta
. Then
\bm{x}_{K}(\omega)=\bm{x}_{K^{(\beta)}}(\omega)
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\bm{x}_{K}(\omega)\subseteq\bm{x}_{K^{(\beta)}}(\omega)
holds trivially. Suppose that
y\in\bm{x}_{K^{(\beta)}}(\omega)
, but
y\notin\bm{x}_{K}(\omega)
. Then
y\in H(K,u)
for some
u\in\omega
, but
y\notin K
. There are unit vectors
u^{\prime}
, arbitrarily close to
u
, such that some hyperplane with normal vector
u^{\prime}
strictly separates
y
from
K
. Since
u\in\omega\subset{\rm int}\,\beta
, the vector
u^{\prime}
can be chosen in
\beta
, and then
y\notin K^{(\beta)}
and thus
y\notin\bm{x}_{K^{(\beta)}}
, a contradiction.
∎

For the proof of Theorem 1, we can use a similar argumentation as in
[
16
,
17
]
, but with modifications. First we assume that
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is nonempty and compact. Let
\varphi
be a nonzero, finite Borel measure on
\omega
. By
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\omega)
we denote the set of continuous functions
f:\omega\to(0,\infty)
, with the topology induced by the maximum norm. For
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\omega)
we recall that
[f]
denotes the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\omega,f)
. Then we define a functional
\Phi:{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\omega)\to(0,\infty)
by

\Phi(f):=V_{\Theta}([f])^{-1/(n-q)}\int_{\omega}f\,{\rm d}\varphi.

It follows from Lemmas
8
and
7
that the functional
\Phi
is continuous. We show that it attains a maximum on the set

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}:=\{\overline{h}_{L}|_{\omega}:L\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega),\,V_{\Theta}(L)=1\}.

The condition
V_{\Theta}(L)=1
can be satisfied, because
L\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
implies that
L\subset C^{-}(t)
for some
t
and then (
11
) and the homogeneity of
V_{\Theta}
can be applied.
By an extension of
[
16
, Lem. 8]
, there is a number
t>0
such that

K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)\wedge V_{\Theta}(K)=1\Rightarrow C(t)\subset K.

In fact, the proof of that Lemma extends to
V_{\Theta}
, because (
11
) holds. If now
L\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
is such that
\overline{h}_{L}|_{\omega}\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, then with
t
as above (independent of
L
) we have

\Phi(\overline{h}_{L}|_{\omega})\leq\int_{\omega}-h_{C(t)}\,{\rm d}\varphi<c

with a constant
c
independent of
L
. Thus,
\sup\{\Phi(f):f\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\}<\infty
. Precisely as in
[
16
]
(Proof of Theorem 3) one can now show that there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K_{0}
such that
\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\omega}\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and
\Phi
attains its maximum on
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
at
\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\omega}
(the condition
o\notin K_{0}
is obviously satisfied, since
V_{\Theta}(K_{0})=1
).

Since
V_{\Theta}
is homogeneous of degree
n-q
, the functional
\Phi
is homogeneous of degree zero. It follows that the maximum of
\Phi
on the set
\mathcal{L}:=\{\overline{h}_{L}|_{\omega}:L\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)\}
is also attained at
\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\omega}
.

For
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
it follows from the definition of the Wulff shape that
[\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}]=K
. Let
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\omega)
. Since
[f]\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
, we have
[\overline{h}_{[f]}|_{\omega}]=[f]
, hence
V_{\Theta}([f])=V_{\Theta}([\overline{h}_{[f]}|_{\omega}])
. Further,
f(u)\leq\overline{h}_{[f]}(u)
for

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Phi(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\Theta}([f])^{-1/(n-q)}\int_{\omega}f\,{\rm d}\varphi\leq V_{\Theta}([\overline{h}_{[f]}|_{\omega}])^{-1/(n-q)}\int_{\omega}\overline{h}_{[f]}\,{\rm d}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(\overline{h}_{[f]}|_{\omega})\leq\Phi(\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\omega}).

Thus, the maximum of
\Phi
on
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\omega)
is also attained at
\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\omega}
.

Now let
f:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous. For sufficiently small
|\tau|
, the function
\tau\mapsto\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\omega}+\tau f
belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\omega)
, hence the function
defined by
F(\tau):=\Phi(\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\omega}+\tau f)
has a maximum at
\tau=0
. By Lemma
11
(and since we have
V_{\Theta}(K_{0})=1
), we get

F^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{1}{n-q}\int_{\omega}f\,{\rm d}S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K_{0},\cdot)\cdot\int_{\omega}\overline{h}_{K_{0}}\,{\rm d}\varphi+\int_{\omega}f\,{\rm d}\varphi.

Since this is equal to
0
, setting

\lambda:=\frac{1}{n-q}\int_{\omega}\overline{h}_{K_{0}}\,{\rm d}\varphi

we obtain

\int_{\omega}f\,{\rm d}\varphi=\lambda\int_{\omega}f\,{\rm d}S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K_{0},\cdot).

Since this holds for all continuous functions
f
on
\omega
, we deduce that

\varphi=\lambda S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K_{0},\cdot)=S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)

with
K:=\lambda^{\frac{1}{n-1-q}}K_{0}
, since
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}
is homogeneous of degree
n-1-q
in its first argument.

Before we continue, we introduce some notation. For a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
, we denote by
b(K)
the distance of
K
from the origin. For
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, we denote by
\delta_{C}(u)
the spherical distance of

from the boundary of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, and for

we write

\omega(\alpha):=\{u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}:\delta_{C}(u)\geq\alpha\}.

This set is compact (possibly empty).

Now we assume, as in the formulation of Theorem
1
, that
\varphi
is a nonzero, finite Borel measure on all of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. We choose a number
\tau>0
such that
\varphi(\omega(\tau))>0
. Then we choose a sequence

of compact subsets of

,

We add a remark on the failing uniqueness, extending an observation made in
[
18
, Sect. 6]
. Suppose that the measure
\varphi
is concentrated in the one-pointed set
\omega:=\{\mathfrak{-v}\}
and assigns the value
1
to it. Since the function
\vartheta(t):=\int_{C(t)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)
,
t>0
, is continuous and homogeneous of degree
n-1-q<0
, there is a number
t_{0}
with
\vartheta(t_{0})=1
. Then the pseudo-cone
C(t_{0})+C
is in
{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
and satisfies
S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,\cdot)=\varphi
. For arbitrary
t_{1}\in(0,t_{0})
we have
\vartheta(t_{1})>\vartheta(t_{0})=1
and hence can choose an
(n-1)
-dimensional closed convex set
F\subset C(t_{1})
which satisfies
\int_{F}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)=1
and is not a translate of
C(t_{0})
. Then
L:=F+C
is a pseudo-cone for which
S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(L,\cdot)
is equal to
S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
and has support contained in
\omega
, but
K
is not a translate of
L
.

It remains open whether uniqueness holds if both,
K
and
L
, belong to
{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
.

The Hilbert function of general unions of lines and double lines in the projective space Thanks: The author was partially supported by MIUR and GNSAGA of INdAM (Italy).

Abstract
. We study the Hilbert function of a general union X ⊂ ℙ 3 X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3} of x x double lines and y y lines. In many cases (e.g. always for x = 2 x=2 and y ≥ 3 y\geq 3 or for x = 3 x=3 and y ≥ 2 y\geq 2 or for x ≥ 4 x\geq 4 and y ≥ ⌈ ( ( 3 ​ x + 4 3 ) − 27 ​ x − 12 ) / ( 3 ​ x + 2 ) ⌉ + 3 − x y\geq\lceil(\binom{3x+4}{3}-27x-12)/(3x+2)\rceil+3-x ) we prove that X X has maximal rank. We give a few examples of x x and y y for which X X has not maximal rank.

1.
Introduction

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a closed subscheme. Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that
X
is said to have
maximal rank in degree
d
or
maximal rank with respect to the line bundle
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(d)
if the
restriction map
H^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(d))\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{0}(\cal{O}_{X}(d))
has maximal rank as a linear map between finite-dimensional vector
spaces, i.e. it is either injective or surjective. Since
h^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(d))=0
,
X
has maximal rank in degree
d
if and only if either
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{X}(d))=0
or
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X}(d))=0
. The scheme
X
is said to have
maximal rank
if it has maximal rank in all degrees
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus the scheme
X
has maximal rank if and only if for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
either

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{X}(d))=0
or
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X}(d))=0
. In our case (general unions of lines and double lines), all integers

h^{0}(\cal{O}_{X}(d))
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, are known and hence knowing that
X
has maximal rank means that

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{X}(d))=\max\{0,\binom{r+d}{r}-h^{0}(\cal{O}_{X}(d))\}
for all
d
.
For any line

L\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
let

2L
denote the closed subscheme of

\mathbb{P}^{3}
with

(\cal{I}_{L})^{2}
as its ideal sheaf. We say that
2L
is a double line and that
L
is the reduction of
2L
. The Hilbert
polynomial and the Hilbert function of
2L
are well-known. Indeed,
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{2L}(d))=3d+1
for all
d
,
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2L}(t))=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2L}(t))=0
if and only if
t\leq 1
(see Remark

2.1
). Thus

2L
has maximal rank. R. Hartshorne and A. Hirschowitz proved that for all integers
r\geq 3
and
b>0
a general union

X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
of
b
lines has maximal rank. After several decades the problem was resurrected (
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
). In
[
1
]
it
was completely determined the Hilbert function of a general union
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
of one double line and a prescribed number
of lines. In this paper we only consider the case

r=3
. Any reader of
[
1
,
6
]
knows that this is from the numerical point of view the hardest and longest step and an
important step for the inductive proof of the general step. It has a key numerical simplification: we know exactly the Hilbert
polynomial of all schemes
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
we need to prove to have maximal rank. We believe that we made a very efficient
use of it.

For all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
let
Z(a,b)
denote the set of all schemes
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
with
a+b
connected
components,
a
of them being double lines and
b
of them being lines. The set
Z(a,b)
is a smooth and irreducible
quasi-projective subset of the Hilbert scheme of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Fix
X\in Z(a,b)
. Since
h^{1}(\cal{O}_{X}(t))=0
and
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{X}(t))=a(3t+1)+b(t+1)
for all

t\in\mathbb{N}
(Remark
2.1
),
X
has maximal rank if and only if
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{X}(t))=\max\{0,\binom{t+3}{3}-a(3t+1)-b(t+1)\}
for
all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
be any union of
a
disjoint double lines. The
projective space
|\cal{I}_{A}(d)|
parametrizes all degree
d
surfaces of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
singular at all points of
A_{\red}
.
Thus if a general
X\in Z(a,b)
has maximal rank with respect to the line bundle
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(d)
, then we know the
dimension of the projective space parametrizing all degree
d
surfaces containing
a+b
general lines and singular at
all points of
a
of them.

We prove the following results.

Theorem 1.1
.

A general
X_{2,b}\in Z(2,b)
has maximal rank with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(d)
, unless
b=2
and
d=4
.
Moreover,
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{X_{2,2}}(4))=1
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X_{2,2}}(4))=2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

A general
X_{3,b}\in Z(3,b)
has maximal rank with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(d)
, unless
(b,d)\in\{(0,4),(1,5)\}
. Moreover,

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{X_{3,0}}(4))=1
,

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X_{3,0}}(4))=5
,

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{X_{3,1}}(5))=4
, and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X_{3,1}}(5))=2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix integers
a\geq 4
,
b\geq 0
and
d\geq 3a+2
. A general
X\in Z(a,b)
has maximal rank with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(d)

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix integers
a\geq 4
and
b\geq\lceil(\binom{3a+4}{3}-27a-12)/(3a+2)\rceil+3-a

As far as we know the examples

,

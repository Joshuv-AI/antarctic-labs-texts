Boundedness of the p p -primary torsion of the Brauer groups of K3 surfaces
Christopher D. Lazda, Alexei N. Skorobogatov

Abstract
We prove that the transcendental Brauer group of a K3 surface X X over a finitely generated field k k is finite, unless k k has positive characteristic p p and X X is supersingular, in which case it is annihilated by p p .



Introduction

Let
k
be a field, and
X
a smooth, projective, geometrically connected variety over
k
. If
\bar{k}
is an algebraic closure of
k
, and
\overline{X}
is the base change of
X
to
\bar{k}
, we let

{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}:={\rm Im}[{\rm{Br}}(X)\to{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})]

denote the transcendental Brauer group of
X
. If
k
is a finitely generated field of characteristic
0
, and
X
is a K3 surface, it was proved in
[
SZ08
]
that this group is finite. Similarly, if
k
is finitely generated of characteristic
p>0
, and
X
is again a K3 surface, then it was proved in
[
SZ15
]
(for odd
p
) and
[
Ito18
]
(for
p=2
) that the prime-to-
p
torsion subgroup
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}(p^{\prime})\subset{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}
is finite.

In general, one cannot expect the
p
-primary torsion subgroup
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}\{p\}\subset{{\rm{Br}}}(\overline{X})^{k}
to be finite, see
[
Sko24
, §4, Example 1]
. However, one might still ask if this group has finite exponent, that is, if it is annihilated by some fixed power of
p
. Moreover, the examples where
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}\{p\}
is infinite are all supersingular, so one might ask whether
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}\{p\}
is finite whenever
X
is of finite height. Inspired by the analogous result for abelian varieties proved in
[
D’Ad24
]
, our main goal in this article is to give an affirmative answer to these questions.

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
k
is finitely generated of characteristic
p>0
, and that
X
is a K3 surface.

(1)

If
X
is of finite height
h
, then
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}\{p\}
is a finite abelian group with at most
22-2h-\rho
generators,
where
\rho
is the Picard number of
\overline{X}
.

(2)

If
X
is supersingular, then
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})\cong(\bar{k},+)
is
annihilated by
p
.

In particular,
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}\{p\}
is of finite exponent.

Note than when
k
is a finite field of characteristic
\neq 2
, a more refined version of this result can be found in
[
Mil75
]
.

In fact, modulo the Tate conjecture, (2) is well-known, and essentially goes back to Artin
[
Art74
]
. All we do here is observe that the Tate conjecture is now known for K3 surfaces, thanks to work of Charles
[
Cha13
]
, Maulik
[
Mau14
]
, Madapusi Pera
[
MP15
]
, Kim–Madapusi Pera
[
KMP16
]
and Ito–Ito–Koshikawa
[
IIK21
]
. Our proof of (1) is also based on the proof of the Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces given in
[
MP15
]
and
[
KMP16
]
, and in particular the arithmetic properties of the Kuga–Satake construction proved there. Our main observation is that the crystalline incarnation of Kuga–Satake descends from the perfection of
k
to give a map of
F
-isocrystals over
k
itself (see Theorem
3.1
below). Ultimately, this allows us to reduce Theorem
A
to the crystalline Tate conjecture for abelian varieties proved by de Jong in
[
dJ98
]
.

Combined with the above-mentioned results on the prime-to-
p
torsion, we obtain the following
corollary. Recall that the
characteristic exponent
of
k
is 1 if
{\rm char}(k)=0
,
and
p
if
{\rm char}(k)=p>0
.

Corollary B
.

Suppose that
k
is finitely generated of characteristic
exponent
p
, and that
X
is a K3 surface. Then
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}
is finite unless
p>1
and
X
is supersingular, in which case
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}
is annihilated by
p
.

Question.
Suppose that
k
is an
infinite
finitely generated field
of characteristic
p>0
, and that
X
is a
supersingular
K3 surface. Is the group
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}

infinite?

The answer to this question is positive when
X
is the Kummer surface attached to
the self-product of a supersingular elliptic curve, and
p\neq 2

[
Sko24
, §4, Example 1]
. In general,
for an infinite field
k
, the group
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}
becomes infinite
after replacing
k
by a finite extension (see Remark
1.4
below).

Let
k^{\rm s}
be the separable closure of
k
in
\bar{k}
, and let
\Gamma={\rm{Gal}}(k^{\rm s}/k)
.
Write
X^{\rm s}=X\times_{k}k^{\rm s}
.
The natural map
{\rm{Br}}(X^{\rm s})\to{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})
is injective because
the Picard scheme of a K3 surface is smooth, see
[
CTS21
, Theorem 5.2.5 (ii)]
,
[
D’Ad24
, Corollary 3.4]
.
It is known that the cokernel of the natural map
{\rm{Br}}(X)\to{\rm{Br}}(X^{\rm s})^{\Gamma}
is the direct sum of a
finite group and a
p
-torsion group of finite exponent
[
CTS21
, Theorem 5.4.12]
.
Thus we obtain the following statement.

Corollary C
.

Suppose that
k
is finitely generated of characteristic
exponent
p
, and that
X
is a K3 surface.
Then
{\rm{Br}}(X^{\rm s})^{\Gamma}
is the direct sum of a
finite group and a
p
-torsion group of finite exponent.

When the first version of this paper was written, the authors became aware of the preprint
of Z. Li and Y. Qin that gives a different proof of Corollary B. Their result applies to arbitrary smooth
projective varieties for which the Tate conjecture for divisors is known, see

[
LQ24
, Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2]
.

The authors are very grateful to the referees for their careful reading of the paper, for their
suggestions, and for spotting a gap in the first version of the paper.

The geometric Brauer group

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
, and
X
a smooth and proper variety over
k
.
We denote by
\rho={\rm{dim}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\rm NS\,}(\overline{X})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}})
the (geometric) Picard number of
X
.
Let
r
be the dimension of the
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-vector space

({\rm H}^{2}_{\rm cris}(\overline{X}/W({\bar{k}}))\otimes{\mathbb{Q}})^{F=p}
.
Using results of Illusie, it is shown in
[
Pet24
, Theorem A.1]
that there is
an isomorphism
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})\{p\}\cong({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus(r-\rho)}\oplus B
,
where
B
is annihilated by a power of
p
. For K3 surfaces, the following
precise result is known (see for example §2.G of
[
BY23
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface over
k
. If
X
has finite height
h
, then

{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})\{p\}\cong({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus(22-2h-\rho)}
. If
X
is supersingular, then
{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})\cong\bar{k}
.

Proof.

We include a proof for the reader’s convenience. We will assume that
k=\bar{k}
is algebraically closed (thus
X=\overline{X}
), and we set
W=W({\bar{k}})
.

For any smooth and proper variety
X
over
k
and any

i,j\geq 0
we have a short exact sequence
[
IR83
, Corollaire IV.3.5 (a)]

0\to{\rm H}^{j}(X,W\Omega^{i}_{X,{\rm log}})\to{\rm H}^{j}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,W\Omega^{i}_{X})\xrightarrow{1-F}{\rm H}^{j}_{{\rm Zar}}(X,W\Omega^{i}_{X})\to 0,

where
W\Omega^{i}_{X}
is the sheaf of de Rham–Witt differential forms
with its semi-linear Frobenius
F

[
Ill79
, I.2.E]
. By definition,

{\rm H}^{j}(X,W\Omega^{i}_{X,{\rm log}}):=\varprojlim{\rm H}^{j}_{\rm Zar}(X,W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,{\rm log}}),

where
W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X,{\rm log}}
is a subsheaf of ‘logarithmic’ forms in
W_{n}\Omega^{i}_{X}
.
In view of a canonical isomorphism

{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\cong{\rm H}^{i-1}(X,W\Omega^{1}_{X,{\rm log}})

[
Ill79
, §II.5]
,
we obtain an isomorphism

{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\cong{\rm H}^{i-1}(X,W\Omega^{1}_{X})^{F=1}.

We also have an exact sequence

0\to{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm fppf}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))/p^{n}\to{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm fppf}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\to{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm fppf}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{n}]\to 0

(1)

together with the exact sequence

0\to{\rm Pic}(X)/p^{n}\to{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm fppf}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\to{\rm{Br}}(X)[p^{n}]\to 0

(2)

deduced from the Kummer exact sequence in the fppf topology.

Now let
X
be a K3 surface. As explained in
[
Lie16
, Theorem 4.8]
,
X
is Shioda supersingular (that is,
\rho=22
)
if and only if it is Artin supersingular (that is, the
F
-crystal
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm cris}(X/W)
is purely of slope 1), thanks to the Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces
[
Cha13
,
Mau14
,
MP15
,
KMP16
,
IIK21
]
. (Note that while
[
Lie16
, Theorem 4.8]
is stated in odd characteristic, this restriction comes from the fact that the Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces was only known in odd characteristic when
loc. cit.
was written. Given the Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces in characteristic
2
, the proof of
[
Lie16
, Theorem 4.8]
works verbatim.)

If
X
is supersingular, then the map
{\rm Pic}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\to{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm fppf}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))
is an isomorphism by
[
Ogu79
, Corollaries 1.6 and 1.7]
(for the missing piece of the latter, see
[
Ill79
, Chapitre II, Théorème 5.14]
). We therefore deduce an isomorphism

{\rm{Br}}(X)[p^{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm fppf}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{n}]\cong\bar{k},

see
[
Ill79
, Chapitre II, §7.2(b)]
for the second isomorphism.

An entirely similar argument using the
\ell
-adic Kummer sequence shows that
{\rm{Br}}(X)\{\ell\}=0
for all
\ell\neq p
, and hence
{\rm{Br}}(X)
is
p
-torsion.

Now suppose that
X
has finite height
h
. Then
{\rm H}^{2}(X,W{\cal O}_{X})
is finitely generated
over
W

{\widehat{{\rm{Br}}}}(X)

{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm fppf}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))

are finite free over

by the argument of
[
Pet24

.
In particular, we deduce from (

\underset{n}{\rm colim}\;{\rm H}^{2}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}{\rm H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

By

Ill79

{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\cong({\rm H}^{i}_{\rm cris}(X/W)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}})^{F=p}.

Thus

is the multiplicity of the slope 1 part of

, cf.
[

The Kummer exact sequences (

\underset{n}{\rm colim}\;{\rm H}^{2}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})\twoheadrightarrow{\rm{Br}}(X)\{p\}

Thus there is an exact sequence

0\to({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus\rho}\overset{\alpha}{\to}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus(22-2h)}\to{\rm{Br}}(X)\{p\}\to 0.

The Pontryagin dual of

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\oplus(22-2h)}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\oplus\rho}
, whose kernel is therefore isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\oplus(22-2h-\rho)}

{\rm{Br}}(X)\{p\}\cong{\rm Hom}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\oplus(22-2h-\rho)},{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})=({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\oplus(22-2h-\rho)},

as required.
∎

Lemma 1.2
.

Suppose
X(k)\neq\emptyset
and
{\rm{Pic}}(X)={\rm{Pic}}(\overline{X})
. Then
the natural map
T_{p}({\rm{Br}}(\overline{X}))^{k}\to T_{p}({\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The assumption implies that
{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}(X^{\rm s}))=0
.
The map
{\rm{Br}}(X^{\rm s})\to{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})
is injective because the Picard scheme of a K3 surface is
smooth, see
[
CTS21
, Theorem 5.2.5 (ii)]
,
[
D’Ad24
, Corollary 3.4]
.
Thus, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence gives rise to an exact sequence

0\to{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}(X)\to{\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k}\to 0

which is split by the choice of a
k
-point.
Since the sequence is split, we see that
T_{p}({\rm{Br}}(X))\to T_{p}({\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k})
is surjective. Since
T_{p}({\rm{Br}}(\overline{X})^{k})\to T_{p}({\rm{Br}}(\overline{X}))

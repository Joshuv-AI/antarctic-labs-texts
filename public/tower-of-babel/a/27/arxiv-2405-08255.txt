Total Variation Distance for Product Distributions is # ​ 𝖯 \#\mathsf{P} -Complete Thanks: An extended version of this paper appeared in the proceedings of IJCAI 2023 [ BGM+23 ] .
Arnab Bhattacharyya, Sutanu Gayen, Kuldeep S. Meel, Dimitrios Myrisiotis, A. Pavan, N. V. Vinodchandran

Abstract
We show that computing the total variation distance between two product distributions is # ​ 𝖯 \#\mathsf{P} -complete. This is in stark contrast with other distance measures such as Kullback–Leibler, Chi-square, and Hellinger, which tensorize over the marginals leading to efficient algorithms.



1
Introduction

Given two distributions
P
and
Q
over a finite domain
\mathcal{D}
, their
total variation (TV) distance
or
statistical difference

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
is defined as

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\max_{S\subseteq\mathcal{D}}\!\left(P(S)-Q(S)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}}|P(x)-Q(x)|=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\max\!\left(0,P(x)-Q(x)\right).

Given two distributions
P
and
Q
over a finite domain
\mathcal{D}
, how hard is it to compute
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
?
If
P
and
Q
are explicitly specified by the probabilities of all of the points of the (discrete) domain
\mathcal{D}
, summing up the absolute values of the differences in probabilities at all points leads to a simple linear time algorithm.
However, in many applications, the distributions of interest are of a high dimension with succinct representations.
In these scenarios, since the size of the domain
\mathcal{D}
is exponentially large compared to its representation, an
O(|\mathcal{D}|)
algorithm is highly impractical.
This leads to the following fundamental computational question:

How hard is it to compute TV distance for high dimensional distributions?

The simplest model for a high-dimensional distribution is the
product distribution
, which is a product of independent Bernoulli distributions.
Let a Bernoulli distribution with parameter
p
be denoted by
\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(p\right)
.
A product distribution
P
over
\{0,1\}^{n}
can be described by
n
parameters
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
where each
p_{i}\in[0,1]
is the probability that the
i
-th coordinate equals
1
(such a
P
is usually denoted by
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Bern}(p_{i})
).
For any
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, the probability of
x
with respect to the distribution
P
is given by
P\!\left(x\right)=\prod_{i\in S}p_{i}\prod_{i\in\left[n\right]\setminus S}\left(1-p_{i}\right)\in\left[0,1\right]
, where
S\subseteq\left[n\right]
is such that
i\in S
if and only if the
i
-th coordinate of
x
is
1
, independently.

We show that the exact computation of the total variation distance between two product distributions
P
and
Q
is
\#\mathsf{P}
-complete.
This is a surprising result, given that for many other distance measures such as Hellinger, Chi-square, and Kullback–Leibler divergence, there are efficient algorithms for computing the distance between two product distributions.

Notation:

We use
\left[n\right]
to denote the
ordered
set
\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
and
\log
to denote
\log_{2}
.
Moreover, we shall assume that all probabilities are represented as rational numbers of the form
a/b
.

2
The Hardness of Computing TV Distance

Theorem 1
.

Given two product distributions
P
and
Q
, computing
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
is
\#\mathsf{P}
-complete.

We first introduce a problem called
\#\textsc{PMFEquals}
and show that it is
\#\mathsf{P}
-hard.
We have that
\#\textsc{PMFEquals}
is the following problem:
Given a probability vector
\left(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\right)
where
p_{i}\in\left[0,1\right]
and a number
v
, compute the number of
x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
such that
P\!\left(x\right)=v
(that is the number
|\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}\mid P(x)=v\}|
), where
P
is the product distribution described by
\left(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\right)
.

Lemma 2
.

\#\textsc{PMFEquals}
is
\#\mathsf{P}
-hard.

Proof.

We will show a reduction from
\#\textsc{SubsetProd}
to
\#\textsc{PMFEquals}
, and the result will follow from the fact that
\#\textsc{SubsetProd}
is
\#\mathsf{P}
-hard.

\#\textsc{SubsetProd}
is the following problem:
Given integers
a_{1},\dots,a_{n},T
, compute the number of sets
S\subseteq\left[n\right]
such that
\prod_{i\in S}a_{i}=T
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, and
T
be the numbers of an arbitrary
\#\textsc{SubsetProd}
instance
I_{S}
.
We will create a
\#\textsc{PMFEquals}
instance
I_{P}
that has the same number of solutions as
I_{S}
.
To this end, let
p_{i}:=\frac{{a_{i}}}{1+{a_{i}}}
for every
i
and
v:=T\prod_{i\in\left[n\right]}(1-p_{i})
, and observe that
a_{i}=\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}
.
For any set
S\subseteq[n]
, we have the following equivalences:

\prod_{i\in S}a_{i}=T\Leftrightarrow\prod_{i\in S}\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}=\frac{v}{\prod_{i\in\left[n\right]}(1-p_{i})}\Leftrightarrow\prod_{i\in S}p_{i}\prod_{i\notin S}\left(1-p_{i}\right)=v\Leftrightarrow P\!\left(x_{S}\right)=v,

where
x_{S}
is such that
x_{i}=1
if and only if
i\in S
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We now turn to
Theorem

1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1
.

We separately prove membership in
\#\mathsf{P}
and
\#\mathsf{P}
-hardness.

Membership in
\#\mathsf{P}
:

Let
P
and
Q
be two product distributions specified by
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
and
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
, respectively.
Without loss of generality we shall assume that these parameters are fractions (as we only have some finite precision available).
The goal is to design a nondeterministic machine
{\cal N}
that takes
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
and
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
as inputs and is such that the number of its accepting paths (normalized by an appropriate quantity) equals
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
.

Let
M
be the product of the denominators of all parameters
p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
and their complements
1-p_{1},\dots,1-p_{n},1-q_{1},\dots,1-q_{n}
.
The non-deterministic machine
{\cal N}
first guesses an element
i\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
in the sample space of
P
and
Q
, computes
|P(i)-Q(i)|
by using the parameters
p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
, then guesses an integer
0\leq z\leq M
, and finally accepts if and only if
1\leq z\leq M|P(i)-Q(i)|
.
(Note that
M|P(i)-Q(i)|=\left|M\cdot P(i)-M\cdot Q(i)\right|
is an integer.)

It follows that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\left|P\!\left(i\right)-Q\!\left(i\right)\right|
is equal to the number of accepting paths of
{\cal N}
divided by
{2M}
.

\#\mathsf{P}
-hardness:

For establishing hardness, we will show a reduction from
\#\textsc{PMFEquals}
to computing TV distance between product distributions.
The theorem will then follow from
Lemma

2
.

Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
and
v
be the numbers in an arbitrary instance of
\#\textsc{PMFEquals}
where each
p_{i}
is represented as an
m
-bit binary fraction.
With this,
P(x)
can be represented as an
nm
-bit binary fraction.
Thus without loss of generality, we can assume that
v
is also an
nm
-bit fraction.
We distinguish between two cases depending on whether
v<2^{-n}
or
v\geq 2^{-n}
.

Case A:
v<2^{-n}
.

First, we construct two distributions
\hat{P}=\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{p}_{1}\right)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{p}_{n+1}\right)
and
\hat{Q}=\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{q}_{1}\right)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{q}_{n+1}\right)
over
\{0,1\}^{n+1}
as follows:

\hat{p}_{i}:=p_{i}
for
i\in\left[n\right]
and
\hat{p}_{n+1}:=1
;
\hat{q}_{i}:=1/2
for
i\in\left[n\right]
and
\hat{q}_{n+1}:=v2^{n}
.
We have that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\hat{P},\hat{Q}\right)
is, by definition, equal to the sum
\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n+1}}\max\!\left(0,\hat{P}\!\left(x\right)-\hat{Q}\!\left(x\right)\right)
or

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}v2^{n}\right)=\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)-v\right)=\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}\left(P\!\left(x\right)-v\right).

(1)

We define two more distributions
P^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
over
\{0,1\}^{n+2}
, by making use of the following claim.

Claim 3
.

For
\beta=\frac{1}{2^{3nm}}
, the following hold for all
x
:
If
P\!\left(x\right)<v
, then
P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)<v\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)
;
if
P\!\left(x\right)>v
, then
P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)>v\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)
.

Proof.

For
Claim

3
to hold, observe that we want
\beta
to be at most
\frac{|v-P(x)|}{v+P(x)}
for every
x
, so that
P(x)\neq v
.
Since both
v
and
P(x)
have
nm
-bit representations, both
|v-P(x))|
and
v+P(x)
have
nm
-bit representations.
Thus
\frac{|v-P(x)|}{v+P(x)}
can be represented as a
2nm
-bit fraction.
Since this fraction is not zero, and the smallest
2nm
-bit fraction is
\frac{1}{2^{2nm}}
, choosing
\beta:=\frac{1}{2^{3nm}}
suffices.
∎

We now define
P^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
as follows:

p_{i}^{\prime}:=p_{i}
for
i\in[n]
,
p_{n+1}^{\prime}:=1
, and
p_{n+2}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}+\beta
;
q_{i}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}
for
i\in[n]
,
q_{n+1}^{\prime}:=v2^{n}
, and
q_{n+2}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}-\beta
where
\beta
is as in
Claim

3
.
We establish the following.

Claim 4
.

We have that
\left|\left\{x\mid P\!\left(x\right)=v\right\}\right|=\frac{1}{2\beta v}\left(d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}\right)-d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\hat{P},\hat{Q}\right)\right)
.

Proof.

We have that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}\right)
is equal to
\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n+2}}\max\!\left(0,P^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)-Q^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)\right)
or

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-\frac{v2^{n}}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)+\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-\frac{v2^{n}}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)+\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)\geq v}P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)+\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)=v}P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)+\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}P\!\left(x\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)=2\beta v\left|\left\{x\mid P\!\left(x\right)=v\right\}\right|+\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}\left(P\!\left(x\right)-v\right).

The result now follows from
Equation

1
.
The first equality above follows from the definitions of
P^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
(since
p_{n+1}^{\prime}=1
).
Note that for every
x
with
P(x)<v
, by Claim
3
,
P(x)(\frac{1}{2}+\beta)<v(\frac{1}{2}-\beta)
and if
P(x)\geq v
, then
P(x)(\frac{1}{2}+\beta)\geq v(\frac{1}{2}-\beta)
.
Also when
P(x)\leq v
, we have that
P(x)(\frac{1}{2}+\beta)
is at most
v(\frac{1}{2}-\beta)
and when
P(x)>v
, by Claim
3
, we have
P(x)(\frac{1}{2}-\beta)>v(\frac{1}{2}+\beta)
.
These imply the second equality.
The rest of the equalities hold by algebraic manipulations.
∎

Thus,
|\{x\mid P(x)=v\}|
can be computed by
computing
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
and
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\hat{P},\hat{Q})
.
Thus the proof in this case follows by
Lemma

2
.

Case B:
v\geq 2^{-n}
.

First, let us define distributions
\hat{P}=\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{p}_{1}\right)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{p}_{n}\right)
and
\hat{Q}=\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{q}_{1}\right)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\hat{q}_{n}\right)
as follows:
\hat{p}_{i}:=p_{i}
for
i\in\left[n\right]
and
\hat{p}_{n+1}:=\frac{1}{v2^{n}}
;
\hat{q}_{i}:=\frac{1}{2}
for
i\in\left[n\right]
and
\hat{q}_{n+1}:=1
.

We now have that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\hat{P},\hat{Q}\right)
is equal to
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\left|\hat{P}\!\left(x\right)-\hat{Q}\!\left(x\right)\right|
or

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,\hat{P}\!\left(x\right)-\hat{Q}\!\left(x\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)+\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)+1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}.

As earlier, we define two more distributions
P^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
, by making use of
Claim

3
.
The new distributions
P^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime}
are such that
p_{i}^{\prime}:=p_{i}
for
i\in\left[n\right]
,
p_{n+1}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{v2^{n}}
, and
p_{n+2}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}+\beta
;
q_{i}^{\prime}:=1/2
for
i\in\left[n\right]
,
q_{n+1}^{\prime}:=1
, and
q_{n+2}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}-\beta
.
We establish the following claim.

Claim 5
.

We have that
\left|\left\{x\mid P\!\left(x\right)=v\right\}\right|=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\beta}\left(d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}\right)-d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\hat{P},\hat{Q}\right)\right)
.

Proof.

We have that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}\right)
is equal to
\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)-Q^{\prime}\!\left(x\right)\right)
or

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)+\max\!\left(\!0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)\right)+\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)\right)+\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\right)\right).

Applying
Claim

3
, we have that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}\right)
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)\geq v}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)\right)+1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)=v}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{x:P\left(x\right)>v}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\right)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)\right)+1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}

\displaystyle=2\beta\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left|\left\{x\mid P\!\left(x\right)=v\right\}\right|+\sum_{x}\max\!\left(0,P\!\left(x\right)\frac{1}{v2^{n}}-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)+1-\frac{1}{v2^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{2^{n-1}}\left|\left\{x\mid P\!\left(x\right)=v\right\}\right|+d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\hat{P},\hat{Q}\right).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Thus, also in this case, the proof follows by
Lemma

2
.
∎

Bibliographical Remarks

[
SV03
]
established, in a seminal work, that additively approximating the TV distance between two distributions that are samplable by Boolean circuits is hard for
\mathsf{SZK}
.
Subsequent works captured variations of this theme
[
GSV99
,
Mal15
,
DGPV20
]
:
For example,
[
GSV99
]
showed that the problem of deciding whether a distribution samplable by a Boolean circuit is close or far from the uniform distribution is complete for
\mathsf{NISZK}
.
Finally,
[
CMR07
,
LP02
,
Kie18
]
show that it is undecidable to check whether the TV distance between two hidden Markov models is greater than a threshold or not, and that it is
\#\mathsf{P}
-hard to additively approximate it.

On an algorithmic note,
[
BGMV20
]
designed efficient algorithms to additively approximate the TV distance between distributions efficiently samplable and efficiently computable.
In a similar vein,
[
PM21
]
studied a related
property testing
variant of TV distance for distributions encoded by circuits.
Recently,
[
FGJW23
]
designed an FPRAS for relatively approximating the TV distance between two arbitrary product distributions and
[
FLL24
]
gave an FPTAS for it.

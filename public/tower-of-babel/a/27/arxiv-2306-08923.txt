Non-Hermitian skin effect enforced by nonsymmorphic symmetries
Yutaro Tanaka, Ryo Takahashi, Ryo Okugawa

Abstract
Crystal symmetries play an essential role in band structures of non-Hermitian Hamiltonian. In this paper, we propose a non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE) enforced by nonsymmorphic symmetries. We show that the NHSE inevitably occurs if a two-dimensional non-Hermitian system satisfies conditions derived from the nonsymmorphic symmetry of the doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian. This NHSE occurs in symmetry classes with and without time-reversal symmetry. The NHSE enforced by nonsymmorphic symmetries always occurs simultaneously with the closing of the point gap at zero energy. We also show that such a NHSE can occur in specific three-dimensional space groups with nonsymmorphic symmetries.

II
Nonsymmorphic-symmetry-enforced NHSE

To begin with, we explain the point-gap topology for NHSEs
Gong et al. 2018
;
Kawabata et al. 2019
;
Zhou and Lee 2019
;
Okuma et al. 2020
.
A point gap at a wavevector
\bm{k}
is open at a reference energy
E

if the Hamiltonian
H(\bm{k})
satisfies
{\rm det}[H(\bm{k})-E]\neq 0
.
When the point gap is open at
E=0
on a line along the
k_{i}~(i=x,y,z)
direction,
we can define a winding number along the
k_{i}
direction as

W_{{i}}(k_{\perp})=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{dk_{i}}{2\pi i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\log\det H(\bm{k}),

(1)

where
k_{\perp}
is the wave vector perpendicular to
k_{i}
.
If
W_{i}(k_{\perp})
is nonzero,
the NHSE occurs under the OBC in the
i
direction
Gong et al. 2018
;
Kawabata et al. 2019
;
Okuma et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2022
.
Throughout this paper, we focus on the reference energy
E=0
.

The winding number is related to Hermitian band topology.
To see a relationship, we introduce a doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}0&H(\bm{k})\\
H^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

The point-gap topology of a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H(\bm{k})
corresponds to the band topology of the doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}(\bm{k})
,
which has chiral symmetry

Schnyder et al. 2008
;
Chiu et al. 2016
;
Gong et al. 2018
;
Kawabata et al. 2019
.
The chiral symmetry is described as
\Gamma\tilde{H}(\bm{k})\Gamma^{-1}=-\tilde{H}(\bm{k})

with
\Gamma={\rm diag}(\bm{1}_{N},-\bm{1}_{N})
,
where
\bm{1}_{N}
is the identity matrix that has the same matrix size as
H(\bm{k})
.
Doubled Hermitian Hamiltonians are also characterized by a winding number equivalent to Eq.(
1
).
Therefore, we can diagnose whether the winding number
W_{i}(k_{\perp})
is nonzero from information on the topology of the doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian (see Appendix
A
for details of the connection between the topology of
\tilde{H}(\bm{k})
and
W_{i}(k_{\perp})
).

In this section, we first consider a 2D non-Hermitian Hamiltonian in
class AII
†
with time-reversal symmetry

\mathcal{T}H^{T}(\bm{k})\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H(-\bm{k})
, where
\mathcal{T}
is a unitary matrix satisfying
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{T}^{*}=-1

Kawabata et al. 2019
.
We demonstrate the conditions under which a NHSE unavoidably occurs in the presence of the time-reversal symmetry.
Initially, we assume that the matrix size of the Hamiltonian is
N=4M+2
with a nonnegative integer
M
.
Additionally, we suppose that the Hamiltonian satisfies

\displaystyle U_{g}^{\bm{k}}H(\bm{k})=\Bigl(U_{g}^{C_{2x}\bm{k}}H(C_{2x}\bm{k})\Bigr)^{\dagger},

(3)

where
C_{2x}
represents the two-fold rotation around the
x
axis, i.e.
C_{2x}\bm{k}=(k_{x},-k_{y})
.
Here
U_{g}^{\bm{k}}
is a unitary matrix defined as

\displaystyle U_{g}^{\bm{k}}:=c_{s}e^{i\frac{k_{x}}{2}}A^{\bm{k}}_{g},

(4)

where
c_{s}=i(1)
for
C_{2x}^{2}=-1(+1)
in spinful (spinless) systems,
and
A^{\bm{k}}_{g}
is an arbitrary
(4M+2)\times(4M+2)
unitary matrix that is periodic in the Brillouin zone.
In addition, in the presence of time-reversal symmetry,
U_{g}^{\bm{k}}
needs to satisfy a relationship with
\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(U_{g}^{\bm{k}})^{*}=-(U_{g}^{-C_{2x}\bm{k}})^{\dagger}\mathcal{T}.

(5)

As we discuss below, Eq. (
3
) is associated with a screw rotation
g=\{C_{2x}|\hat{\bm{x}}/2\}
symmetry,
which is a twofold rotation
C_{2x}
followed by translation by a half of a lattice vector
\hat{\bm{x}}
.
In this paper, we use Seitz notation
g=\{p_{g}|\bm{t}_{g}\}
for a spatial transformation
g
,
which transform a point
\bm{r}
as
\bm{r}\rightarrow p_{g}\bm{r}+\bm{t}_{g}
.
Here
p_{g}
is an element of the point group, and
\bm{t}_{g}
represents a translation.

Because the screw symmetry enforces NHSEs owing to the above condition,
we refer to such NHSEs as nonsymmorphic-symmetry-enforced NHSEs.
At the same time, the condition leads to the closing of the point gap at
E=0
on both
k_{y}=0
and
k_{y}=\pi
lines under the full PBC.

II.2
Conditions for the screw symmetry

Hereafter, we discuss general conditions to systematically realize NHSEs protected by screw symmetry, including the NHSE in the above 2D model.
First, we briefly review conditions of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians for doubled Hermitian Hamiltonians in Eq. (

Then, crystal symmetries can be represented by

&B^{\bm{k}}_{g}\end{pmatrix}&(c_{g}=1),\\

II.3
Mechanism of NHSEs enforced by nonsymmorphic symmetry in class AII
†

As shown in Sec.
II.2
,
if
H(\bm{k})
satisfies the condition in Eq. (
3
),

\tilde{H}(\bm{k})
has the screw rotation symmetry
g=\{C_{2x}|\hat{\bm{x}}/2\}
.
Thus, eigenstates of
\tilde{H}(\bm{k})
have the screw rotation eigenvalues
\pm c_{s}e^{-i\frac{k_{x}}{2}}
on the screw-invariant lines (
k_{y}=0,\pi
).
In order to take time-reversal symmetry into account,
we consider a case where Eq. (
5
) holds with
C_{2x}^{2}=-1
(
c_{s}=i
).

We show that the doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian has 2D Weyl points at zero energy if
c_{g}=-1
. The eigenstates on the screw-invariant lines (
k_{y}=0,\pi
) are labeled by the screw symmetry eigenvalues
\pm ie^{-i\frac{k_{x}}{2}}
. Because of the time-reversal symmetry,
while Kramers pairs at
k_{x}=0
have opposite screw rotation eigenvalues
\pm i
,
Kramers pairs at
k_{x}=\pi
have the same eigenvalues [Fig.
2
(a)].
Moreover, because the chiral operator anticommutes with the screw operator,
a pair of eigenstates with the energy
\mathcal{E}
and
-\mathcal{E}
has screw eigenvalues with the opposite signs
com
.
Since the eigenvalues must cross on the line,
the eigenstates with these symmetry eigenvalues enforce Weyl points on the screw-invariant line.
Because the number of bands of the doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian is
8M+4
by assumption, the chiral symmetry fixes the 2D Weyl points at zero energy.

The emergent Weyl points give a path with a nonzero winding number in the Brillouin zone,
depending on their chirality and positions
Matsuura et al. 2013
;
Chiu et al. 2016
;
Ahn and Yang 2017
;
Yu et al. 2022
;
Le et al. 2022
. In addition, the winding number satisfies
W_{x}(-k_{y})=-W_{x}(k_{y})
because
\mathcal{T}H^{T}(\bm{k})\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H(-\bm{k})
.
Also, as we show in Appendix
A
, when the time-reversal and the chiral operators anticommute, the 2D Weyl points at
\bm{k}_{0}
and
-\bm{k}_{0}
have the same chirality [see Eq. (
43
) in Appendix
A
].
Thus, a time-reversal pair of the Weyl points on the screw-invariant line has the same chirality.
It follows that the winding number
W_{x}(k_{y})
is nonzero at any
k_{y}
due to the positions and the chirality of the Weyl points [Fig.
2
(b)].
Furthermore, we can find anisotropy in the winding numbers
W_{x}(k_{y})
and
W_{y}(k_{x})
. Because we cannot annihilate the Weyl points on the screw-invariant lines, we can always find a path for a nonzero
W_{x}(k_{y})
in the Brillouin zone. On the other hand,
W_{y}(k_{x})
can be zero in the presence of the Weyl points with the opposite chirality at the same
k_{x}
.
As a result, the NHSE under the OBC in the
x
direction is more robust than that under the OBC in the
y
direction.

Moreover, since the 2D Weyl points appear at zero energy,

IV
Conclusion and discussion

In summary, we study NHSEs enforced by nonsymmorphic symmetry in 2D and 3D non-Hermitian systems, which we call nonsymmorphic-symmetry-enforced NHSEs.
We have clarified the conditions of symmetry and matrix size of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians for the NHSEs.
If the conditions are satisfied, these NHSEs inevitably happen.
We have also revealed that the point gap at
E=0
closes under the full PBC when the NHSEs occur.
These NHSEs can occur in 2D systems not only without time-reversal symmetry but also with time-reversal symmetry.

NHSEs have been experimentally observed in artificially designed systems
Xiao et al. 2020
;
Weidemann et al. 2020
;
Helbig et al. 2020
;
Brandenbourger et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Hofmann et al. 2020
;
Palacios et al. 2021
;
Shang et al. 2022
. We believe that our theory can be readily confirmed in artificial two-dimensional systems, such as active matter
Palacios et al. 2021
and electric circuits
Hofmann et al. 2020
;
Shang et al. 2022
.
Symmetries in these artificial platforms can be flexibly controlled.
Our theory is helpful for design of non-Hermitian band structures in terms of spatial symmetries.

A.1
Winding numbers for non-Hermitian Hamiltonians and doubled Hermitian Hamiltonians

We briefly review the relationship between the NHSEs and band topology of a doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian.
Throughout this work, we choose the convention for non-Hermitian Hamiltonians
H(\bm{k})
to satisfy
H(\bm{k}+\bm{G})=H(\bm{k})
,
as used for the Fourier transformation in the main text.

We start with a winding number for the NHSE in 2D and 3D non-Hermitian systems,
which is defined as
Zhang et al. 2022

\displaystyle W(E)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{l}d\bm{k}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}\log\det[H(\bm{k})-E],

(29)

where
l
is a loop in the Brillouin zone, and
E
is a reference energy.
If a winding number with a reference energy is nonzero in the system,
a NHSE happens under an OBC
Zhang et al. 2022
.
On the other hand, it is unclear what OBC leads to the NHSE among the various OBCs. We can determine how to take an OBC to observe a NHSE by choosing a line in the Brillouin zone as
l
.
For instance, let us introduce the following winding number

\displaystyle W_{i}(k_{\perp},E)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\log\det[H(\bm{k})-E],

(30)

where
k_{\perp}
is the wave vector perpendiclular to
k_{i}

(i=x,y,z)
.
If
W(k_{\perp},E)
is nonzero, a NHSE happens under the OBC in the
i
direction and under the PBC in the other directions
Okuma et al. 2020
.
In this paper, we investigate the winding number with
E=0

Next, we see the band topology of a doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian defined by Eq. (
2

The doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian has chiral symmetry

\Gamma={\rm diag}(\bm{1}_{N},-\bm{1}_{N})
,
where
N
is the matrix size of
{H}(\bm{k})

For the topological classification of the doubled Hermitian Hamiltonians, we introduce the

Q(\bm{k})=1-2\sum_{\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}<0}\ket{\Psi^{\alpha}(\bm{k})}\bra{\Psi^{\alpha}(\bm{k})},

(31)

where
\ket{\Psi^{\alpha}(\bm{k})}
is an eigenstate of the doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian with the energy
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
.
When chiral symmetry is diagonal,
the
Q
matrix is block off-diagonal, represented by

\displaystyle Q(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}0&q(\bm{k})\\
q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&0\end{pmatrix},

(32)

where
q(\bm{k})
is a unitary matrix.
When only chiral symmetry is present, the
Hamiltonians belong to class AIII

The Hermitian Hamiltonians can be characterized by

\displaystyle\nu_{{i}}(k_{\perp})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int^{2\pi}_{0}dk_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\log\det[q(\bm{k})],

(33)

Chiu et al. 2016
.

\nu_{{i}}(k_{\perp})
is used to characterize the topological gapless band structures

;
Le et al. 2022
.

Hereafter we show that the winding number
W_{{i}}(k_{\perp})
in the main text is equivalent to the winding number
\nu(k_{\perp})
, according to Ref.
Le et al. 2022
.
Any doubled Hermitian Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}(\bm{k})
commutes with the
Q
matrix because

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(\bm{k})Q(\bm{k})=

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(\bm{k})-2\sum_{\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}<0}\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi^{\alpha}(\bm{k})}\bra{\Psi^{\alpha}(\bm{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q(\bm{k})\tilde{H}(\bm{k}).

(34)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&H(\bm{k})\\
H^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&q(\bm{k})\\
q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&q(\bm{k})\\
q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&H(\bm{k})\\
H^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&0\end{pmatrix}=0,

(35)

we obtain

H(\bm{k})q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})=q(\bm{k})H^{\dagger}(\bm{k}).

(36)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\det[H(\bm{k})q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})]

\displaystyle=\det[q(\bm{k})H^{\dagger}(\bm{k})]

\displaystyle=\det[H(\bm{k})q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})]^{*},

(37)

which indicates that
\det[H(\bm{k})q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})]
is real. It follows that

\displaystyle\int^{2\pi}_{0}dk_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\log\det[H(\bm{k})q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})]=0,

(38)

because the integrant
\log\det[H(\bm{k})q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})]
is
real and periodic in the Brillouin zone.
Eventually, we obtain

\displaystyle\int^{2\pi}_{0}dk_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\log\det[H(\bm{k})]=-\int^{2\pi}_{0}dk_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\log\det[q^{\dagger}(\bm{k})]

\displaystyle\int^{2\pi}_{0}dk_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}}\log\det[q(\bm{k})],

(39)

where we have used

because of the unitarity of

From this equation, it is clear that

Production rates of dark photons and Z ′ Z^{\prime} in the Sun and stellar cooling bounds Preprint: August 9, 2026 Preprint: August 9, 2026
Shao-Ping Li, and Xun-Jie Xu

Abstract
Light weakly interacting particles could be copiously produced in the Sun which, as a well-understood star, could provide severe constraints on such new physics. In this work, we calculate the solar production rates of light gauge bosons (e.g. dark photon) arising from various U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) extensions of the standard model. It is known that the dark photon production rate is suppressed by the dark photon mass if it is well below the plasmon mass of the medium. We show that for more general U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge bosons, this suppression is absent if the couplings are not in alignment with those of the photon. We investigate a few frequently discussed U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) models including B − L B-L , L μ − L τ L_{\mu}-L_{\tau} , and L e − L μ ⁡ ( τ ) L_{e}-L_{\mu(\tau)} , and derive the stellar cooling bounds for these models.

2.2
The dark photon model: the photon-like and the hypercharge limits

In our work, the dark photon model is defined as a special case of
the above model: either
Q_{X}^{(\psi)}=0

is negligibly

by taking
g_{X}^{(\psi)}\equiv g_{Z^{\prime}}Q_{X}^{(\psi)}\to 0

1

Here we would like to outline two interesting limits in the
dark photon model.

•

The photon-like limit. For a very light
Z^{\prime}
, we take the limit
r_{m}\to 0

in Tab.
1

g_{X}^{(\psi)}=0

g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}=-\epsilon ec_{W}Q_{{\rm em}}^{(\psi)}\thinspace,\ \ \text{i.e.}\ g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}\propto Q_{{\rm em}}^{(\psi)}\thinspace,

(2.5)

where

and

is the electric charge
of
\psi

of the photon in the sense that
\left(g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(e)}:g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\nu)}:g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(u)}:g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(d)}\right)=\left(-1:0:2/3:-1/3\right)

•

The hypercharge limit. For a heavy

with the mass well above
the electroweak scale, we take the limit

together
with
g_{X}^{(\psi)}=0
in Tab.
1

g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}=\epsilon g\frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}}Q_{Y}^{(\psi)}\thinspace,\ \ \text{i.e.}\ g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}\propto Q_{Y}^{(\psi)}\thinspace.

(2.6)

For example, in this limit we have
\left(g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(e_{L},\nu_{L})}:g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(e_{R})}:g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(u_{L},d_{L})}\right)=\left(-1/2:-1:1/6\right)

essentially mixes only with the SM

gauge field.

2.3
The simplified dark photon model: the decoupling limit

In the literature, it is quite common that only the kinetic mixing
between
\hat{X}
and the photon (
\hat{A}
) is considered:

{\cal L}\supset-\frac{1}{4}\hat{A}_{\mu\nu}\hat{A}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\hat{X}_{\mu\nu}\hat{X}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\hat{A}_{\mu\nu}\hat{X}^{\mu\nu}+eJ_{{\rm em}}^{\mu}\hat{A}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{X}^{2}\hat{X}_{\mu}\hat{X}^{\mu}\thinspace,

(2.7)

where

and

is the electromagnetic current. This Lagrangian is not gauge invariant

In the simplified dark photon model, the transformation in Eq. (

(A,\ Z^{\prime})^{T}=\mathbb{T}(\hat{A},\ \hat{X})^{T}

\mathbb{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\varepsilon\\
0&\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\end{array}\right).

(2.8)

And the kinetic, mass, and gauge interaction terms are transformed

\displaystyle\text{kinetic matrix}:\

\displaystyle K=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon&1\end{array}\right)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\text{mass matrix}:\

\displaystyle M^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\

0&m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\end{array}\right),\ m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}=\frac{m_{X}^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\thinspace,

\displaystyle\text{current matrix}:\

\displaystyle J=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}J_{{\rm em}}\\
0\end{array}\right)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}J_{{\rm em}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
x\end{array}\right),\ x=\frac{-\varepsilon}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}}\thinspace,

where matrices

,

3.1
The vanishing production rate of a photon-like

Z^{\prime}

It is known that the dark photon production rate in a finite-temperature
environment vanishes in the limit of zero mass

;
An:2013yfc
;
Redondo:2013lna
;

This feature can be understood from the decoupling limit discussed

Here we would like to make a generalization that this is also true
for other

consisting of only protons and electrons, the
Z^{\prime}
in the
U(1)_{B-L}

model has
g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(p)}=-g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(e)}

couplings photon-like. Consequently, its production rate vanishes
in the
m_{Z^{\prime}}\to 0

Below we show explicitly how it vanishes for a photon-like
Z^{\prime}
.
In previous studies, this feature is shown for the dark photon in
the basis of

mass eigenstates. Here we adopt the physical basis (

Consider a generic Feynman diagram for

On the other hand, such a diagram is always accompanied with another

rise to the refractive index. The medium effect can be calculated

B

Z^{\prime}

Therefore, even though we have removed all mixing between gauge

5
Conclusions

Dark photons and other dark gauge bosons, generically denoted by

Z^{\prime}
in this work, could be produced in a hot and dense stellar medium,
causing additional energy loss which has been used to derive one of
the most constraining bounds on these hypothetical particles.

We show that the production rate of
Z^{\prime}
in the low-mass limit crucially
depends on how close its couplings are to the photon-like limit, as
illustrated in Fig.
3
. Consequently, the stellar
cooling bounds on
Z^{\prime}
from different models can be very different,
as shown in Fig.
4
.

For
B-L
and
L_{e}-L_{\mu(\tau)}
models with
m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 10\ \text{keV}
,
the gauge coupling
g_{Z^{\prime}}
is constrained by the solar model to
be at least below
4.1\times 10^{-10}
and
3.9\times 10^{-11}
, respectively.
The constraints would be much stronger if
m_{Z^{\prime}}
is in the resonant
production region, or if one considers RG and HB stars, albeit subject
to more astrophysical uncertainties. For dark photon and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}

models, the effective couplings generated via kinetic mixing or loop
diagrams are photon-like. Hence the lower bounds on
\epsilon
or

g_{Z^{\prime}}
for these two models are always mass-dependent.

Our model-specific bounds derived from stellar cooling might be
of importance to a variety of studies on new light particles at
the intensity frontier and in cosmology.

Appendix A
Transformation from the original basis to the physical basis

In this appendix, we present the detail of how the original basis
is transformed to the physical basis. As has been briefly described
below Eq. (
2.1
), the transformation involves two steps. First
one needs to canonicalize the kinetic terms via a linear non-unitary
transformation, denoted by
\mathbb{L}
, and then diagonalize the
mass matrix of gauge bosons by a unitary transformation, denoted by

\mathbb{O}
. The two steps are formulated as follows:

\hat{\mathbf{V}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}\mathbf{V}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{O}}\mathbf{V}_{{\rm ph}}\thinspace,\ \ \mathbf{V}=\mathbb{L}\hat{\mathbf{V}},\ \mathbf{V}_{{\rm ph}}=\mathbb{O}\mathbf{V}\thinspace,

(A.1)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\equiv(W_{1},\ W_{2},\ W_{3},\ \hat{B},\ \hat{X})^{T}\thinspace,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle\equiv(W_{1},\ W_{2},\ W_{3},\ B,\ X)^{T}\thinspace,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{{\rm ph}}

\displaystyle\equiv(W_{1},\ W_{2},\ A,\ Z,\ Z^{\prime})^{T}\thinspace.

(A.4)

The specific forms of
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{O}
in Eq. (
A.1
)
are given as follows
Lindner:2018kjo
:

\mathbb{L}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{I}_{3}\\
&1&\epsilon\\
&0&\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}\end{array}\right),\ \mathbb{O}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathbb{I}_{2}\\
&s_{W}&c_{W}&0\\
&c_{W}c_{\theta}&-s_{W}c_{\theta}&-s_{\theta}\\
&c_{W}s_{\theta}&-s_{W}s_{\theta}&c_{\theta}\end{array}\right),

(A.5)

where
\mathbb{I}_{n}
is an
n\times n
identity matrix,
(s_{W}\ c_{W})\equiv(\sin\theta_{W},\ \cos\theta_{W})

with
\theta_{W}
the Weinberg angle, and
(s_{\theta}\ c_{\theta})\equiv(\sin\theta,\ \cos\theta)
.
The angle
\theta
, to be determined later, is similar to
\theta_{W}

in the sense that it describes how much the neutral massive boson
of
SU(2)_{L}
mixes with
U(1)_{X}
instead of
U(1)_{Y}
. Hence
the orthogonal transformation
\mathbb{O}
can be regarded as a generalized
Weinberg rotation in the
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}\times U(1)_{X}

model, with two angles instead of one.

The
\mathbb{T}
matrix in Eq. (
2.3
) is given by

\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{O}\cdot\mathbb{L}\thinspace.

(A.6)

Note that the specific forms of
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{O}
are
not unique because
(\mathbb{L},\ \mathbb{O})\to(\mathbb{L}^{\prime},\ \mathbb{O}^{\prime})=(\mathbb{A}\cdot\mathbb{L},\ \mathbb{O}\cdot\mathbb{A}^{T})

with an arbitrary orthogonal matrix
\mathbb{A}
(
\mathbb{A}\cdot\mathbb{A}^{T}=1
)
can always canonicalize the kinetic terms and diagonalize the mass
matrix. Only their product
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{O}\cdot\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{O}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbb{L}^{\prime}

is unique.

The angle
\theta
depends on the mass matrix of gauge bosons. If
the
U(1)_{X}
gauge boson acquires a mass via the Stückelberg mechanism
or some dark-sector Higgses which are SM singlets, then there is no
mass mixing between
\hat{X}
and
\hat{B}
, but the
\mathbb{L}

transformation causes mass mixing between
X
and
B
. After the

\mathbb{O}
transformation, the mass matrix is re-diagonalized and

\theta
is determined by
Lindner:2018kjo

\tan\theta=\frac{\epsilon m_{Z}^{2}s_{W}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}-m_{Z}^{2}}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})\thinspace.

(A.7)

If the
U(1)_{X}
gauge boson acquires its mass via Higgs doublets,
then there would be mass mixing in the
\hat{\mathbf{V}}
basis.
After the
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{O}
transformations,
\tan\theta

receives an additional contribution almost independent of
\epsilon
,
i.e. the mass mixing depends not only on
\epsilon
but also on additional
parameters. In this case, we can treat
\theta
as a free parameter.

The gauge interactions in the
\hat{\mathbf{V}}
basis read

{\cal L}\supset\sum_{\psi}\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\mathbf{Q}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{\mu}\psi\thinspace,

(A.8)

where

\mathbf{Q}\equiv\left(g\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2},\ g\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2},\ g\frac{\sigma_{3}}{2},\ g^{\prime}Q_{Y},\ g_{Z^{\prime}}Q_{X}\right).

(A.9)

After the transformation
\hat{\mathbf{V}}\to\mathbf{V}_{{\rm ph}}=\mathbb{T}\hat{\mathbf{V}}
,
one obtains the gauge interactions in the physical (
\mathbf{V}_{{\rm ph}}
)
basis. It is straightforward to see that the charged-current interactions
in the SM are not modified. So we are only concerned with the interactions
of
A
,
Z
, and
Z^{\prime}
. The result is

{\cal L}\supset eQ_{{\rm em}}^{(\psi)}\overline{\psi}\not{A}\psi+g_{Z}^{(\psi)}\overline{\psi}\not{Z}\psi+g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}\overline{\psi}\not{Z}^{\prime}\psi\thinspace,

(A.10)

which implies that the electromagnetic interactions remain the same
as in the SM. The effective couplings
g_{Z}^{(\psi)}
and
g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}

are given by

\displaystyle g_{Z}^{(\psi)}

\displaystyle=c_{\theta}g\left[c_{W}Q_{{\rm em}}^{(\psi)}-\frac{Q_{Y}^{(\psi)}}{c_{W}}\right]-s_{\theta}\left[g_{Z^{\prime}}Q_{X}^{(\psi)}-gQ_{Y}^{(\psi)}\frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}}\epsilon\right]+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(A.11)

\displaystyle g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}

\displaystyle=s_{\theta}g\left[c_{W}Q_{{\rm em}}^{(\psi)}-\frac{Q_{Y}^{(\psi)}}{c_{W}}\right]+c_{\theta}\left[g_{Z^{\prime}}Q_{X}^{(\psi)}-gQ_{Y}^{(\psi)}\frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}}\epsilon\right]+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(A.12)

If the kinetic mixing term is the only source of mass mixing, then
we can apply Eq. (
A.7
) to obtain the expressions of
g_{Z}^{(\psi)}

and
g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(\psi)}
in Tab.
1
.

Appendix B
Medium-induced kinetic mixing rederived
from coherent scattering

As we have discussed in Sec.
3.1
, coherent scattering
of
\gamma
/
Z^{\prime}
with charged particles in a medium causes medium-induced
mixing between
\gamma
and
Z^{\prime}
. In finite-temperature/density
field theories, this can be obtained by computing the following diagram

\gamma
Z^{\prime}
f
=\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\gamma-(f)-Z^{\prime}}\ ,

(B.1)

using medium-modified fermion propagators in the loop. And the result
is essentially the well-known plasmon masses given in Eq. (
3.3
)
with
Q_{f}^{2}e^{2}\to Q_{f}eg_{Z^{\prime}}^{(f)}
.

Here we would like to rederive it from the coherent scattering of

\gamma
/
Z^{\prime}
with medium particles. By directly computing the following
diagrams

\gamma
Z^{\prime}
f
+
\gamma
Z^{\prime}
f
={\cal M}_{\mu\nu}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\mu}\epsilon_{Z^{\prime}}^{\nu}\ ,

(B.2)

and assuming that the scattering is coherent among multiple medium
particles in a local region, one can obtain the same result, but the
rederivation provides a more intuitive understanding of the medium
effect.

Let us start with Eq. (
B.2
) where
\epsilon_{\gamma}

and
\epsilon_{Z^{\prime}}
denote the polarization vectors of
\gamma

and
Z^{\prime}
respectively. The fermionic part of Eq. (
B.2
)
reads

{\cal M}^{\mu\nu}=eQ_{f}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(f)}\overline{u}\left[\gamma^{\nu}\frac{1}{\not{p}_{a}-m_{f}}\gamma^{\mu}+\gamma^{\mu}\frac{1}{\not{p}_{b}-m_{f}}\gamma^{\nu}\right]u\thinspace,

(B.3)

where
p_{a}
and
p_{b}
denote the momentum of the internal fermion
propagator of the first and second diagrams respectively. As a condition
of coherence, the momentum transfer from the photon to the medium
particle should be sufficiently small. It should be well below the
inverse of the radius of the local region maintaining coherency. For
simplicity, we take the zero limit of the momentum transfer so that
the initial
\gamma
and the final
Z^{\prime}
have exactly the same momentum.
Under this limit, we have
p_{a}^{\mu}=p^{\mu}+k^{\mu}
and
p_{b}^{\mu}=p^{\mu}-k^{\mu}

where
k^{\mu}
and
p^{\mu}
are the momenta of
\gamma
and
f

respectively. Then after applying on-shell conditions (
p^{2}=m_{f}^{2}
,

\not{p}u=m_{f}u
,
\overline{u}\not{p}=\overline{u}m_{f}
),
Eq. (
B.3
) becomes

{\cal M}^{\mu\nu}=eQ_{f}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(f)}\frac{4k.p\left(j^{\nu}k^{\mu}+j^{\mu}k^{\nu}-j\cdot k\thinspace g^{\mu\nu}\right)-2k^{2}\left(j^{\nu}p^{\mu}+j^{\mu}p^{\nu}\right)}{4(k\cdot p)^{2}-(k^{2})^{2}}\thinspace,

(B.4)

with

j^{\mu}\equiv\overline{u}\gamma^{\mu}u\thinspace.

(B.5)

Given the amplitude
{\cal M}^{\mu\nu}
, it is straightforward to
obtain the corresponding effective Lagrangian in the momentum space:

{\cal L}_{{\rm eff}}={\cal M}^{\mu\nu}|_{j\to J}A_{\mu}Z^{\prime}_{\nu}\thinspace,

(B.6)

where

J_{\mu}\equiv\overline{f}\gamma_{\mu}f\thinspace.

(B.7)

Note that
J
and
j
have different dimensions,
J\sim[E]^{3}

vs
j\sim[E]^{1}
. In a background of a large number of
f
particles,
the expectation value
\langle J_{\mu}\rangle
is exactly the classical
current of
f
particles. In particular, for a non-relativistic medium,
we have

\langle J_{\mu}\rangle\approx(n_{f},\ 0,\ 0,\ 0)\thinspace,

(B.8)

where
n_{f}
is the number density of
f
. Coherent scattering
of
\gamma
/
Z^{\prime}
with the background particles leads to a background
expectation value of
{\cal M}^{\mu\nu}|_{j\to J}
, which gives rise
to the medium-induced mixing between
\gamma
and
Z^{\prime}
:

\Pi_{\gamma-(f)-Z^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}=\left\langle{\cal M}^{\mu\nu}|_{j\to J}\right\rangle.

(B.9)

Next, we use Eqs. (
B.4
) and (
B.8
) to compute
\Pi_{\gamma-(f)-Z^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}

in Eq. (
B.9
). Since
{\cal M}^{\mu\nu}
is multiplied by

\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\mu}\epsilon_{Z^{\prime}}^{\nu}
and
k\cdot\epsilon_{\gamma}=k\cdot\epsilon_{Z^{\prime}}=0
,
the
j^{\nu}k^{\mu}+j^{\mu}k^{\nu}
term in Eq. (
B.4
) can
be ignored. In addition, we only consider a non-relativistic medium,
which implies

p^{\mu}\approx(m_{f},\ 0,\ 0,\ 0)\thinspace.

(B.10)

Since the photon is soft compared to the fermion mass (
k\ll m_{f}
),
we can neglect
(k^{2})^{2}
in the denominator of Eq. (
B.4
).
From Eqs. (
B.10
) and (
B.8
), we have
\langle J\rangle\cdot k/\left(p\cdot k\right)=n_{f}/m_{f}
.
Combining all these together, we obtain

\Pi_{\gamma-(f)-Z^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}=eQ_{f}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(f)}\left[-\frac{n_{f}}{m_{f}}g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{k^{2}}{2(k\cdot p)^{2}}\left(\langle J^{\nu}\rangle p^{\mu}+\langle J^{\mu}\rangle p^{\nu}\right)\right].

(B.11)

Without loss of generality, one can assume that
k^{\mu}
is in the

z
-axis direction,
k^{\mu}=(\omega,\ 0,\ 0,\ |\mathbf{k}|)
so
that the polarization vectors in Eq. (
3.2
) can be written
as follows:

\epsilon_{T1}^{\mu}=(0,\ 1,\ 0,\ 0)\thinspace,\ \epsilon_{T2}^{\mu}=(0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 0)\thinspace,\ \epsilon_{L}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}}}(|\mathbf{k}|,\ 0,\ 0,\ \omega)\thinspace.

(B.12)

Each of them satisfies
k\cdot\epsilon=0
and
\epsilon^{\mu}\epsilon_{\mu}=-1
.

Applying the decomposition in Eq. (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma-(f)-Z^{\prime}}^{T}

\displaystyle=eQ_{f}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(f)}\frac{n_{f}}{m_{f}}\thinspace,

(B.13)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma-(f)-Z^{\prime}}^{L}

\displaystyle=eQ_{f}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{(f)}\frac{n_{f}}{m_{f}}\left[1-\frac{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right],

(B.14)

where we have used
\epsilon_{T1}^{\mu}p_{\mu}=0
,
\epsilon_{T2}^{\mu}p_{\mu}=0
,

\epsilon_{L}^{\mu}p_{\mu}=m_{f}|\mathbf{k}|/\sqrt{k^{2}}
, and
\epsilon_{L}^{\mu}\langle J_{\mu}\rangle=n_{f}|\mathbf{k}|/\sqrt{k^{2}}
.
Therefore, the medium-induced kinetic mixing derived from the coherent
scattering theory is identical to that obtained using the finite-temperature/density
field theory.

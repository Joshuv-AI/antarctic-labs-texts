Abstract




Abstract.
Let
L_{1}=([a_{1},b_{1}],\ldots,[a_{m},b_{m}])

and
L_{2}=([c_{1},d_{1}],\ldots,[c_{n},d_{n}]
) be two sequences of intervals
consisting of nonnegative integers with
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m}
and

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
. In this paper, we first give two optimal
conditions for the sequences of intervals
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
such that
each pair
(P;Q)
with
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
,
Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
,

a_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
is
bigraphic. One of them is optimal sufficient condition and the other
one optimal necessary condition. We also present a characterization
of
(L_{1};L_{2})
that is forcibly bigraphic on sequences of intervals.
This is an extension of the well-known theorem on bigraphic
sequences due to Gale and Ryser(see [4],[9]).

Keywords.
Optimal conditions; Bigraphic sequence;
Interval; Forcibly bigraphic pair.

Mathematics Subject Classification(2000):
05C07.

1. Introduction

Throughout this paper, we consider only undirected
graphs without loops or parallel edges. Let
P_{m}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})

and
Q_{n}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})

be two non-increasing sequences
of nonnegative integers. The pair
(P_{m};Q_{n})
is said to be
bigraphic
if there is a bipartite graph
G
with partite sets

X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
and
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
such that

d_{G}(x_{i})=a_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
d_{G}(y_{i})=b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. In this case,
G
is called a
realization
of
(P_{m};Q_{n})
.
Gale [4] and Ryser [9],independently, gave a characterization for

(P_{m};Q_{n})
to be bigraphic.

Theorem 1.1.(Gale[4], Ryser[9])
.
(P_{m};Q_{n})
is bigraphic
if and only if
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
and

\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}min\{p_{i},r\}\mbox{ for each $r$ with $1\leq r\leq n$.}

[formula]

Analogous to the case of bigraphic sequences, Tripathi et al. [3]
considered two sequences of intervals of nonnegative integers and
provided a corresponding characterization, which generalized Theorem
1.1.

Let
L_{1}=([a_{1},b_{1}],\ldots,[a_{m},b_{m}]
) and

L_{2}=([c_{1},d_{1}],\ldots,[c_{n},d_{n}]
) be two sequences of intervals
consisting of nonnegative integers with
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{m}
and

c_{1}\geq\cdots\geq c_{n}
. We say that
(L_{1};L_{2})
is bigraphic if
there exists a bipartite graph
G
with partite sets

X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
and
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
such that
a_{i}\leq d_{G}(x_{i})\leq b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
c_{i}\leq d_{G}(y_{i})\leq d_{i}
for

1\leq i\leq n
. In this case,
G
is referred to as a realization of

(L_{1};L_{2})
. Tripathi et al. [3] provided a characterization of

(L_{1};L_{2})
that is bigraphic.

Theorem 1.2.(Tripathi et al.[3])
.
(L_{1};L_{2})
is
bigraphic
if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{t}c_{i}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}min\{b_{j},t\}\mbox{ for each $t$ with $1\leq t\leq n$}

and

\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}min\{d_{j},s\}\mbox{ for each $s$
with $1\leq s\leq m$}.

The main object of the paper is to investigate
optimal necessary condition and optimal sufficient condition on two
sequences of intervals and give a characterization for
(L_{1};L_{2})

to be forcibly bigraphic. The notion forcibly bigraphic pair will be
introduced later.

Theorem 1.3.
Let
L_{1}=([a_{1},b_{1}],\ldots,[a_{m},b_{m}]
) and

L_{2}=([c_{1},d_{1}],\ldots,[c_{n},d_{n}]
) be two sequences of intervals
consisting of nonnegative integers with
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m}
and

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
. If the following two inequalities hold:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\{c_{i},k\}\mbox{ for each $k$ with $1\leq k\leq m$,}

[formula]

\sum_{i=1}^{l}d_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},l\}\mbox{ for each $l$ with $1\leq l\leq n$,}

[formula]

then each pair
(P;Q)
with
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
,

Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
,
a_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
is bigraphic.

It follows easily from Gale-Ryser Theorem that the sufficient
condition for each pair
(P;Q)
being graphic is optimal.
Additionally, when it comes to the converse proposition of Theorem
1.3, we point out that the condition is sufficient but not
necessary, as can be seen by a counterexample.

Counterexample 1.
Take
L_{1}=([2,3],[1,2])
and

L_{2}=([1,2],[0,1])
, which satisfy
b_{1}\geq b_{2}
and
d_{1}\geq d_{2}
. It
is easy to check that every
(P;Q)
with
P=(p_{1},p_{2})
,

Q=(q_{1},q_{2})
,
a_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 2
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 2
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
is bigraphic. However, (2) does not
hold for
k=1
and
k=2
.

Theorem 1.4.
Let
L_{1}=([a_{1},b_{1}],\ldots,[a_{m},b_{m}]
) and

L_{2}=([c_{1},d_{1}],\ldots,[c_{n},d_{n}]
) be two sequences of intervals
consisting of nonnegative integers with
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m}
and

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
.
If each pair
(P;Q)
with

P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
,
Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
,
a_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b_{i}
for

1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
is bigraphic, then
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\{c_{i},k\}+|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}|\mbox{ for each $k$ with $1\leq k\leq m$},

[formula]

\sum_{i=1}^{l}d_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},l\}+|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}|\mbox{ for each $l$ with $1\leq l\leq n$}.

[formula]

Again, it follows from Gale-Ryser Theorem that the necessary
condition for each pair
(P;Q)
being graphic is optimal.
Note that the condition is necessary but not
sufficient, as can be seen by counterexample 2.

Counterexample 2.
Take
L_{1}=([1,3],[2,3])
and

L_{2}=([1,2],[0,2])
. It is easy to check that (4) and (5) all hold.
However,
(P;Q)
is not bigraphic with
P=(1,3)
and
Q=(2,2)
.

Moreover, the above two theorems are generalizations of the results
in [5] due to Guo and Yin. In fact, there are a lot of articles on
the subject of lists of graphs (such as Furuya and Yashima [1],
Goyal et al. [2], Lai and Hu[6],Tripathi et al. [10], Roberts [8]
and Rao [7] and so on).

Combining Theorem 1.3 with Theorem 1.4, we arrive at the
characterization of
(L_{1};L_{2})
that is forcibly bigraphic. Before
presenting it, we first give a definition.

Definition 1.5.
Let
L_{1}=([a_{1},b_{1}],\ldots,[a_{m},b_{m}]
) and

L_{2}=([c_{1},d_{1}],\ldots,[c_{n},d_{n}]
) be two sequences of intervals
consisting of nonnegative integers with
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m}
and

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
.
(L_{1};L_{2})
is said to be forcibly bigraphic
if every pair
(P;Q)
with
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
,

Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
,
a_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
is bigraphic.

Theorem 1.6.
Let
L_{1}=([a_{1},b_{1}],\ldots,[a_{m},b_{m}]
) and

L_{2}=([c_{1},d_{1}],\ldots,[c_{n},d_{n}]
) be two sequences of intervals
consisting of nonnegative integers with
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m}
,

d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}
. Then
(L_{1};L_{2})
is forcibly bigraphic if
and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\{c_{i},k\}\mbox{ for each $k$ with $1\leq k\leq m$}

and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},k\}\mbox{ for each $k$ with $1\leq k\leq n$.}

One can see that Theorem 1.6 generalizes Gale-Ryser Theorem, which
corresponds to
a_{i}=b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
c_{j}=d_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
.

2. Proof of Theorem 1.3

Choose an arbitrary pair
(P;Q)
with
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
and

Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
,

a_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
.
Suppose (2) and (3) all hold, and now it suffices to show the pair
(P;Q)
is bigraphic.

Rearrange the elements in
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
and

Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
such that they are in nonincreasing order and
then denote
P^{\prime}=(p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{m})
and
Q^{\prime}=(q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})
,
where
p^{\prime}_{1}\geq\ldots\geq p^{\prime}_{m}
and
q^{\prime}_{1}\geq\ldots\geq q^{\prime}_{n}
. On
the other hand, the intervals in
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are also rearranged
so that they are in coincidence with the above order, i.e.,

L^{\prime}_{1}=([a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1}],\ldots,[a^{\prime}_{m},b^{\prime}_{m}])
and

L^{\prime}_{2}=([c^{\prime}_{1},d^{\prime}_{1}],\ldots,[c^{\prime}_{m},d^{\prime}_{n}])
, where
a^{\prime}_{i}\leq p^{\prime}_{i}\leq b^{\prime}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
c^{\prime}_{i}\leq q^{\prime}_{i}\leq d^{\prime}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, which together with (3) yield (6) below for each
r
with
1\leq r\leq n,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q^{\prime}_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d^{\prime}_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{p^{\prime}_{i},r\}.

[formula]

Thus

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q^{\prime}_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{p^{\prime}_{i},r\}

for all
r
. From Theorem 1.1, we know
(P^{\prime};Q^{\prime})
is bigraphic, which is
equivalent to that
(P;Q)
is bigraphic and so we are done.
\Box

3. Proof of Theorem 1.4

For the proof, we first rearrange the integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}

such that they follow non-increasing order. Assume that
a^{\prime}_{1}\geq\ldots\geq a^{\prime}_{m}
and set
L^{\prime}_{1}=([a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1}],\ldots,[a^{\prime}_{m},b^{\prime}_{m}])
,
then it is easy to see that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a^{\prime}_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}
for each
r
with

1\leq r\leq n
.

In the following, we just need to prove the inequality in (5), since
the idea used to verify the other one is analogous. Without loss of
generality, we may assume that
n\geq m
,
b_{i}>a_{i}
for all
i\leq m,d_{i}>c_{i}
for all
i\leq n
,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
and set
\alpha=min\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}.

If
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i},
then let

P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})=(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m})
and

Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. Clearly,
P
and
Q
are two
non-increasing sequences satisfying
a^{\prime}_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b^{\prime}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
. By the assumption
of Theorem 1.4,
(P;Q)
is bigraphic, and then it follows from (1)
that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}
for
1\leq r\leq n
.
Thus (5) holds.

If

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}>\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i},
set

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=t,
then it is
easy to see that
1\leq t_{2}=t\leq nd_{1}-m\alpha.
Two cases now arise,
depending on whether
1\leq r<m
or
m\leq r\leq n.

Case 1.
Suppose
1\leq r<m
. To prove (5), we have to divide
the situation into three subcases. For simplicity, we call
(P;Q)
a
proper pair of
(L^{\prime}_{1};L_{2})
if
P
and
Q
satisfy the following
conditions:
a^{\prime}_{i}\leq p_{i}\leq b^{\prime}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
c_{i}\leq q_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}
. Otherwise, we call
(P;Q)
an
improper pair.

Subcase 1.1.
If
1\leq t\leq m
, then let

P=(p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{m})=(a^{\prime}_{1}+1,\ldots,a^{\prime}_{t}+1,a^{\prime}_{t+1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m})

and
Q=(q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. Note that
p^{\prime}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq p^{\prime}_{m}
,
q^{\prime}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq q^{\prime}_{n}
and
(P;Q)
is a proper
pair of
(L^{\prime}_{1};L_{2})
. So by the assumption of Theorem 1.4,
(P;Q)

is bigraphic and thus (1) follows, then we derive

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}&=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q^{\prime}_{i}\\
&\leq&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{p^{\prime}_{i},r\}\\
&=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}min\{a^{\prime}_{i}+1,r\}+\sum\limits_{i=t+1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}\\
&\leq&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}+t+\sum\limits_{i=t+1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}\\
&=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}+t\\
&=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+t_{2}.\end{array}

Hence (5) holds for all
r<m
.

Subcase 1.2.
If
m<t\leq n,
then
m\leq m+n-t<n,
which together
with
1\leq r<m
yield
r<m+n-t.
Let

P=(p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{m})=(a^{\prime}_{1}+1,\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m}+1)
and

Q=(q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r},\ldots,d_{n+m-t},d_{n+m-t+1}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1)
.
It’s easy to see that
P
and
Q
are two non-increasing sequences
and
(P;Q)
is a proper pair. Again, since
(P;Q)
is bigraphic, (1)
holds and so for each
i\leq n
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}&=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q^{\prime}_{i}\\
&\leq&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{p^{\prime}_{i},r\}\\
&=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i}+1,r\}\\
&\leq&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}+m\\
&<&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+t_{2}.\end{array}

Subcase 1.3.
If
n<t\leq nd_{1}-m\alpha

(obviously, if
nd_{1}-m\alpha\leq n
, the proof is complete.),
let
P_{1}=(p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{m})=(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m})
and

Q_{1}=(q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})=(d_{1}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1)
. If there exist
l
,

r
and
w
such that
d_{i}-1=0
for all
i>l
,
d_{i}-1>0

for all
i\leq l
and
l-w+1=t-n
, where
1\leq w<l
,
then let
Q_{2}=(q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{w-1},q^{\prime}_{w}-1,\ldots,q^{\prime}_{l}-1,q^{\prime}_{l+1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})

=(d_{1}-1,\ldots,d_{w-1}-1,d_{w}-2,\ldots,d_{l}-2,d_{l+1}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1)=(d_{1}-1,\ldots,d_{w-1}-1,d_{w}-2,\ldots,d_{l}-2,0,\ldots,0).

Otherwise repeat the same steps until the deficiency
t
is removed thoroughly. Then we may assume that

Q_{j}=(q^{j}_{1},\ldots,q^{j}_{n})=(d_{1}-(j-1),\ldots,d_{x-1}-(j-1),d_{x}-j,\ldots,d_{y}-j,0\ldots,0),

where
d_{y}-j
is the last term of nonnegative integer in
Q_{j}
.
It can be seen that
P_{1}
and
Q_{j}
are non-increasing lists and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}p^{\prime}_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}q^{j}_{i}
. If

(P_{1};Q_{j})
is improper, then the proof is complete.
Otherwise(namely,
c_{i}\leq q^{j}_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
) we
proceed with our proof as follows. Since
(P_{1};Q_{j})
is bigraphic,
(1) reduces to (7) for each
r
with
1\leq r\leq n
,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q^{j}_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{p^{\prime}_{i},r\}.

[formula]

Now we need to divide the range of

r(i.e.,1\leq r\leq n)
into three parts.

Subsubcase 1.3.1.
Suppose
1\leq r<x(x\in(1,n])
, then by
(7), we have
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}[d_{i}-(j-1)]\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}
, implying
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+t_{2}
for every
r
with

1\leq r\leq n
, where
r(j-1)\leq t_{2}.

Subsubcase 1.3.2.
Suppose
x\leq r\leq y(y\in[1,n]),
then it
follows from (7) that
[d_{1}-(j-1)]+\ldots+[d_{x-1}-(j-1)]+[d_{x}-j]+\ldots+[d_{r}-j]\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\},
that is,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+t_{2}

for each
r
with
1\leq r\leq n
, where
rj-x+1\leq t_{2}.

Subsubcase 1.3.3.
Suppose
y<r\leq n
. It is not difficult to
obtain that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q^{j}_{i}=[d_{1}-(j-1)]+\ldots+[d_{x-1}-(j-1)]+[d_{x}-j]+\ldots+[d_{y}-j]+0+\ldots+0=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{y}d_{i}-yj+x-1
. Denote
d_{i}=j_{i}
for
y+1\leq i\leq n
, then

Q_{j}=(q^{j}_{1},\ldots,q^{j}_{n})=(d_{1}-(j-1),\ldots,d_{x-1}-(j-1),d_{x}-j,\ldots,d_{y}-j,0\ldots,0)=(d_{1}-(j-1),\ldots,d_{x-1}-(j-1),d_{x}-j,\ldots,d_{y}-j,d_{y+1}-j_{y+1},\ldots,d_{n}-j_{n}).

Note that
(j-1)(x-1)+j(y-x+1)+(j_{y+1}+\ldots+j_{n})=(yj+1-x)+(j_{y+1}+\ldots+j_{n})=t_{2}
and (7) gives

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}q^{j}_{i}=[d_{1}-(j-1)]+\ldots+[d_{x-1}-(j-1)]+[d_{x}-j]+\ldots+[d_{y}-j]+[d_{y+1}-j_{y+1}]+\ldots+[d_{r}-j_{r}]\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}.
The latter is
equivalent to
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}-[(j-1)(x-1)+j(y-x+1)+(j_{y+1}+\ldots+j_{r})]\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\},
reducing to that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+(yj-x+1)+(j_{y+1}+\ldots+j_{r})\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+t_{2}.
Therefore, (5) holds for each
r
with
1\leq r<n
,
which means that (5) also holds for each
r
with
1\leq r<m
since

m\leq n
. Now we like to point out that the constructive proof, in
the case, can be implemented as an algorithm to construct a suitable

Q_{j}.

Case 2.
Suppose
m\leq r\leq n
. To prove (5), we have to
consider four subcases depending on the variation of
t
.

Subcase 2.1.
If
1\leq t<m,
then it is easy to get (5) as the
case is similar to 1.1.

Subcase 2.2.
If
m\leq t\leq m+n-r,
then
r\leq m+n-t.
So we
may take
P=(a^{\prime}_{1}+1,\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m}+1)
and

Q=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r},\ldots,d_{m+n-t},d_{m+n-t+1}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1).

Observe that
(P;Q)
is proper and bigraphic. Then it is stemmed
from (1) that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i}+1,r\}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}+m\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+t_{2}.

Subcase 2.3.
If
m+n-r<t\leq n,
then
m+n-t<r\leq n.
Let

P=(a^{\prime}_{1}+1,\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m}+1)
and

Q=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m+n-t},d_{m+n-t+1}-1,\ldots,d_{r}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1).

Evidently,
P
and
Q
are non-increasing and
(P;Q)
is proper. By
the assumption of Theorem 1.4,
(P;Q)
is bigraphic and then (1)
yields
\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m+n-t}d_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=m+n-t+1}^{r}(d_{i}-1)&\leq&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i}+1,r\},\end{array}

this is further transformed to
\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}-(r+t-m-n)&\leq&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}+m,\end{array}

so we derive that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}&\leq&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a^{\prime}_{i},r\}+r+t-n\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}min\{a_{i},r\}+t_{2}.\end{array}

Hence (5) holds for any
r
with
m\leq r\leq n
.

Subcase 2.4.
If
n<t\leq nd_{1}-m\alpha
( if
nd_{1}-m\alpha\leq n
, the proof is complete.), then set

P_{1}=(p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{m})=(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m})
and

Q_{1}=(q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})=(d_{1}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1)
. Upon applying similar
arguments as 1.3, we can derive (5) and so the proof is complete.

Exciton condensation in biased bilayer graphene
Harley D. Scammell, Oleg P. Sushkov

Abstract
We consider suspended bilayer graphene under applied perpendicular electric bias field that is known to generate a single particle gap 2 ​ Δ 2\Delta and a related electric polarization 𝒫 {\cal P} . We argue that the bias also drives a quantum phase transition from band insulator to superfluid exciton condensate. The transition occurs when the exciton binding energy exceeds the band gap 2 ​ Δ 2\Delta . We predict the critical bias (converted to band gap), Δ c ≈ 60 \Delta_{c}\approx 60 meV, below which the excitons condense. The critical temperature, T c ​ ( Δ ) T_{c}(\Delta) , is maximum at Δ ≈ 25 \Delta\approx 25 meV, T c max ≈ 115 T_{c}^{\text{max}}\approx 115 K, decreasing significantly at smaller Δ \Delta due to thermal screening. Entering the condensate phase, the superfluid transition is accompanied by a cusp in the electric polarization 𝒫 ⁡ ( Δ ) {\cal P}(\Delta) at Δ → Δ c \Delta\to\Delta_{c} , which provides a striking testable signature. Additionally, we find that the condensate prefers to form a pair density wave.

Theoretically it is convenient to classify exciton condensates (also known as exciton insulators) by the nature of the corresponding interaction-driven quantum phase transition.

Most, if not all, previous studies of exciton condensation have been aimed at class I. However, class I is necessarily a many-body problem and progress often requires uncontrolled approximations. Here, instead, we consider condensation in class II. A striking technical advantage is that we only need to consider a two-body problem. In class II the condition of exciton condensation is the equality of the exciton binding energy
\epsilon_{b}
to the single particle band gap,
2\Delta

The present work is focused on biased BLG.
Unbiased BLG is a semimetal, however, application of an electric bias (perpendicular electric field) opens a single particle band gap
2\Delta

McCann and Koshino 2013

A careful treatment of the screened attractive electron-hole Coulomb interaction is essential to make quantitative predictions of the binding energy and hence of the condensation transition. To this end, we account for three sources of screening: (i) Screening by metallic gates placed a distance
d
above and below the BLG plane;
(ii) dielectric screening due to a material between BLG and the gates; and
(iii) BLG self-screening and retardation thereof, as captured via the Random Phase Approximation (RPA). We find that all three sources of screening significantly influence, i.e. reduce, the condensation critical temperature. To this end, we propose idealised experimental setups to allow to maximise the critical temperature. A key development in this work is our treatment of the retardation of the self-screening, i.e. retardation of the screened Coulomb potential.

The key development of this work is that we are able to: use reliable two-body techniques to describe excitonic bounds states in a band insulator; account for environmental sources of screening; treat dynamically screening/retardation. This combination affords a reliable prediction of the critical temperature, and how it may be optimised with respect to system parameters.
Entering the exciton condensate phase, we lose this quantitative control over the problem, and instead resort to mean-field theory. We note that the exciton condensate is a neutral superfluid, and that experimentally distinguishing between this and the band insulator phase is subtle. Using our mean-field description, we establish two key experimental signatures of the excitonic condensate.

— Entering the condensate phase we can no longer apply two-body techniques, and therefore do not expect to make quantitative predictions. Even so, the results below provide crucial predictions for future experimental tests of the exciton condensate.

Discussion
— We presented a description for the excitonic bound states in biased BLG, and their subsequent condensation. Owing to biased BLG being a band insulator, we were able to apply two-body techniques. This is contrasted to exciton formation and condensation from a semi-metallic ground state, whereby many-body techniques are necessary, and resultantly uncontrolled approximations are often required.

We examined suspended biased BLG, with gates
d>d_{c}\approx 10-15
nm. In this regime/setup, we show that excitons condense for
\Delta<\Delta_{c}
, Fig.
3
(a). This is our primary theoretical finding. For distance to gates
d=100
nm (1000nm) the maximum critical temperature is predicted to be
100
K (
115
K), and is optimised at non-zero bias, Fig.
3
(b). Entering the BEC phase, our two-body techniques are no longer quantitatively accurate, however, we are able to establish several qualitative features of the condensate. Experimentally distinguishing between the excitonic condensate/insulator and the band insulator is a challenging task. To this end, we found that the excitons influence the macroscopic dipole moment [c.f. (
10
)], and proposed a smoking gun test for exciton condensation – quantum capacitance measurements of the dipole moment as a function of the bias field, which tunes the band insulator to exciton condensate phase transition [c.f. Fig.
4
].
We hope to entice direct experimental searches for the excitonic condensate in biased bilayer graphene. Future theoretical work may consider other candidate materials using the two-body approach applied here.

Polarization Operator

The expression for the polarization generically takes the form,

\displaystyle\Pi({\bm{q}},i\xi,T)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{\mu,\nu=\pm}\int\frac{d^{2}p}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\left(f_{\epsilon^{\mu}_{\bm{p}}}-f_{\epsilon^{\nu}_{\bm{p+q}}}\right)}{i\xi+\epsilon^{\mu}_{\bm{p}}-\epsilon^{\nu}_{\bm{p+q}}}F^{\mu\nu}_{\bm{p,p+q}}.

(S2)

Here the form factors are
F^{\mu\nu}_{\bm{p,p+q}}=|\langle\psi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{p+q},\tau}|\psi^{(\nu)}_{\bm{p},\tau}\rangle|^{2}
, with
\mu,\nu=\pm
.
Using that
-\epsilon^{-}_{\bm{p}}=\epsilon^{+}_{\bm{p}}\equiv\epsilon_{\bm{p}}
and
F^{--}_{\bm{p,p+q}}=F^{++}_{\bm{p,p+q}}
,
F^{-+}_{\bm{p,p+q}}=F^{+-}_{\bm{p,p+q}}
, we get

\displaystyle\Pi({\bm{q}},i\xi,T)

\displaystyle=8\int\frac{d^{2}p}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left[\frac{(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}-\epsilon_{\bm{p+q}})\left(f_{\epsilon_{\bm{p}}}-f_{\epsilon_{\bm{p+q}}}\right)}{\xi^{2}+(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}-\epsilon_{\bm{p+q}})^{2}}F^{++}_{\bm{p,p+q}}+\frac{(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}+\epsilon_{\bm{p+q}})\left(f_{\epsilon_{\bm{p}}}+f_{\epsilon_{\bm{p+q}}}-1\right)}{\xi^{2}+(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}+\epsilon_{\bm{p+q}})^{2}}F^{+-}_{\bm{p,p+q}}\right].

(S3)

III
Vertex Form Factors

The Coulomb interaction is taken to be

\displaystyle H_{int}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2},\bm{p}_{3}}\sum_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}V(\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{p}_{1})\Psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}_{1},\tau}\Psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}_{2},\tau^{\prime}}\Psi_{\bm{p}_{3},\tau^{\prime}}\Psi_{\bm{p}_{1}+\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{p}_{3},\tau},

(S4)

which neglects valley-exchange.
We transform to band basis,

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{p},\tau}

\displaystyle={\cal U}_{\bm{p},\tau}\begin{pmatrix}c_{\bm{p},\tau}\\
v_{\bm{p},\tau}\end{pmatrix},\ {\cal U}_{\bm{p},\tau}=(|\psi^{(+)}_{\bm{p},\tau}\rangle,|\psi^{(-)}_{\bm{p},\tau}\rangle),

with
c_{\bm{p},\tau}
and

v_{\bm{p},\tau}
the destruction operators for conduction and valence electrons.
Restricting consideration to the exciton channel, denoted
H_{int}^{X}
, the interaction becomes,

\displaystyle H_{int}^{X}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}}\sum_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}V(\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{p}_{1})c^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}_{1},\tau}c_{\bm{p}_{2},\tau}v^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}_{2},\tau^{\prime}}v_{\bm{p}_{1},\tau^{\prime}}\langle\psi^{(-)}_{\bm{p}_{2},\tau^{\prime}}|\psi^{(-)}_{\bm{p}_{1},\tau^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi^{(+)}_{\bm{p}_{1},\tau}|\psi^{(+)}_{\bm{p}_{2},\tau}\rangle.

(S5)

In the main text we denote the from factor
Z_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}}^{\tau^{\prime},\tau}=\langle\psi^{(-)}_{\bm{p}_{2},\tau^{\prime}}|\psi^{(-)}_{\bm{p}_{1},\tau^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi^{(+)}_{\bm{p}_{1},\tau}|\psi^{(+)}_{\bm{p}_{2},\tau}\rangle
.

IV
Critical temperature scaling

Setting
E=0
, the
T=0
the Lippmann-Schwinger equation gives the condition for
\Delta_{c}

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\frac{p^{4}}{4m^{2}}+\Delta_{c}^{2}}\Psi_{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}V^{0}_{\bm{p},\bm{p}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}.

(S6)

At
T>0
, the condition
E=0
requires
\Delta<\Delta_{c}
, and is given by

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\frac{p^{4}}{4m^{2}}+\Delta_{c}^{2}}\Psi_{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left[V^{0}_{\bm{p},\bm{p}^{\prime}}+\delta V_{\bm{p},\bm{p}^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{\Delta}{T_{c}}}\right]\Psi_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}.

(S7)

Using that the characteristic momentum is
p=\sqrt{2m\Delta}
, we combine these two equations as

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}

\displaystyle=\nu_{0},

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\left(\nu_{0}-\nu_{1}e^{-\frac{\Delta}{T_{c}}}\right),

\displaystyle(\Delta_{c}-\Delta)

\displaystyle=\nu_{1}e^{-\frac{\Delta}{T_{c}}},\to T_{c}=\Delta/\ln\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{\Delta_{c}-\Delta}\right).

(S8)

V
Real-space order parameter

In this appendix we derive the effective lattice model for the dominant excitonic order parameter.
Introducing the real-space creation operators for the conduction and valence bands via,

\displaystyle c_{\bm{p},\tau,s}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{r}}\phi^{(+)}_{\bm{p},\tau}(\bm{r})\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s},

\displaystyle v_{\bm{p},\tau,s}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{r}}\phi^{(-)}_{\bm{p},\tau}(\bm{r})\tilde{v}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}.

(S9)

With
\bm{r}
spanning the real-space lattice sites, and comprising two sublattices,
\sigma=A_{1},B_{2}
, and
\{a(\bm{r}\in A)=1,a(\bm{r}\in B)=0\}
and
\{b(\bm{r}\in A)=0,b(\bm{r}\in B)=1\}
.
Here,

\displaystyle\phi^{(+)}_{\bm{p},\tau}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=e^{i(\bm{p}+\tau\bm{K})}\left(\alpha^{(+)}_{\tau,\bm{k}}a(\bm{r})+\beta^{(+)}_{\tau,\bm{k}}b(\bm{r})\right),

\displaystyle\phi^{(-)}_{\bm{p},\tau}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=e^{i(\bm{p}+\tau\bm{K})}\left(\alpha^{(-)}_{\tau,\bm{k}}a(\bm{r})+\beta^{(-)}_{\tau,\bm{k}}b(\bm{r})\right),

(S10)

with definitions,

\displaystyle\alpha^{(+)}_{\tau,\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=-\cos\gamma_{\bm{p}}=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{\frac{(\varepsilon_{\bm{p}}-\Delta)^{2}}{p^{4}/(4m^{2})}+1}},

\displaystyle\beta^{(+)}_{\tau,\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=\sin\gamma_{\bm{p}}e^{2i\tau\theta_{\bm{p}}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{\bm{p}}-\Delta}{p^{2}/(2m)}\frac{e^{2i\tau\theta_{\bm{p}}}}{\sqrt{\frac{(\varepsilon_{\bm{p}}-\Delta)^{2}}{p^{4}/(4m^{2})}+1}},

\displaystyle\alpha^{(-)}_{\tau,\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=\sin\gamma_{\bm{p}}e^{-2i\tau\theta_{\bm{p}}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{\bm{p}}-\Delta}{p^{2}/(2m)}\frac{e^{-2i\tau\theta_{\bm{p}}}}{\sqrt{\frac{(\varepsilon_{\bm{p}}-\Delta)^{2}}{p^{4}/(4m^{2})}+1}},

\displaystyle\beta^{(-)}_{\tau,\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=\cos\gamma_{\bm{p}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{(\varepsilon_{\bm{p}}-\Delta)^{2}}{p^{4}/(4m^{2})}+1}}.

(S11)

The mean-field Hamiltonian becomes,

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{p},\tau,\tau^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}}c_{\bm{p},\tau,s}^{\dagger}\Phi(p)_{\tau,\tau^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}}v_{\bm{p},\tau^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\sum_{\bm{p},\tau,\tau^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}}\sum_{\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}}\phi^{(+)}_{\bm{p},\tau}(\bm{r})(\phi^{(-)}_{\bm{p},\tau^{\prime}})^{*}(\bm{r}^{\prime})\Phi(p)_{\tau,\tau^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}\tilde{v}_{\bm{r}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\ .

(S12)

We consider intervalley pairing in the
s
-wave channel and with arbitrary spin ordering (singlet or triplet). There is both a symmetric and an anti-symmetric combination of the valleys,

\displaystyle\Phi(p)_{\tau,\tau^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Phi_{0}(p)(\tau_{x})_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}\\
\Phi_{0}(p)(i\tau_{y})_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}.\end{cases}

(S13)

In fact, there is a U(1) rotational symmetry that connects these distinct valley structures – we more compactly write

\displaystyle\Phi(p)_{\tau,\tau^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0}(p)(\tau_{x})_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}e^{i\frac{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}{2}\phi}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}

(S14)

Let us consider the case that
\bm{r}\in A
and
\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B
,

\displaystyle H^{AB}_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{p},\tau,s,s^{\prime}}\sum_{\bm{r}\in A,\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B}\left(e^{i(\bm{p}+\tau\bm{K})\cdot\bm{r}}\alpha^{(+)}_{\tau,\bm{p}}a(\bm{r})\right)\left(e^{-i(\bm{p}-\tau\bm{K})\cdot\bm{r}^{\prime}}\beta^{(-)}_{-\tau,\bm{p}}b(\bm{r}^{\prime})\right)\Phi_{0}(p)e^{i\tau\phi}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}\tilde{v}_{\bm{r}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{p},\tau,s,s^{\prime}}\sum_{\bm{r}\in A,\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B}\left[e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}e^{i\tau\bm{K}\cdot(\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime})}\alpha^{(+)}_{\tau,\bm{p}}\beta^{(-)}_{-\tau,\bm{p}}\Phi_{0}(p)e^{i\tau\phi}\right]\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}\tilde{v}_{\bm{r}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\ ,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{r}\in A,\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B}\sum_{\bm{p}}\left[2e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(-\cos^{2}\gamma_{\bm{p}})\Phi_{0}(p)\right]\left[\cos(\bm{K}\cdot(\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime})+\phi)\right]\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}\tilde{v}_{\bm{r}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\ ,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{r}\in A,\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B}\Gamma^{AB}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})\left[\cos(\bm{K}\cdot(\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime})+\phi)\right]\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}\tilde{v}_{\bm{r}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\ .

(S15)

We have defined the function,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{AB}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\bm{p}}\left[2e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(-\cos^{2}\gamma_{\bm{p}})\Phi_{0}(p)\right],

(S16)

which determines the real-space amplitude and can be directly evaluated numerically.

Performing the same procedure for all combinations of
\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}
in
\{A_{1},B_{2}\}
, we arrive at the expression,

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{r},\bm{r}}\Gamma(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})\left[\cos(\bm{K}\cdot(\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime})+\phi)\right]\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}\tilde{v}_{\bm{r}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(d_{\mu}s_{\mu})_{s,s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.},

(S17)

with amplitude function,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Gamma^{AA}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})=\sum_{\bm{p}}\left[2e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(-\cos\gamma_{\bm{p}}\sin\gamma_{\bm{p}}e^{\pm 2i\tau\theta_{\bm{p}}})\Phi_{0}(p)\right],\ \text{for}\ \{\bm{r}\in A_{1},\bm{r}^{\prime}\in A_{1}\}\\
\Gamma^{BB}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})=\sum_{\bm{p}}\left[2e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(\cos\gamma_{\bm{p}}\sin\gamma_{\bm{p}}e^{\pm 2i\tau\theta_{\bm{p}}})\Phi_{0}(p)\right],\ \text{for}\ \{\bm{r}\in B_{2},\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B_{2}\}\\
\Gamma^{AB}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})=\sum_{\bm{p}}\left[2e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(-\cos^{2}\gamma_{\bm{p}})\Phi_{0}(p)\right],\ \text{for}\ \{\bm{r}\in A_{1},\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B_{2}\}\\
\Gamma^{BA}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})=\sum_{\bm{p}}\left[2e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(\sin^{2}\gamma_{\bm{p}}e^{\pm 4i\theta_{\bm{p}}})\Phi_{0}(p)\right],\ \text{for}\ \{\bm{r}\in B_{2},\bm{r}^{\prime}\in A_{1}\}.\end{cases}

(S18)

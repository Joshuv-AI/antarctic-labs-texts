New criteria for starlikeness in the unit disc
Milutin Obradović, Nikola Tuneski

Abstract
. It is well-known that the condition Re ⁡ [ 1 + z ​ f ′′ ​ ( z ) f ′ ​ ( z ) ] > 0 \operatorname{Re}\left[1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]>0 , z ∈ 𝔻 z\in{\mathbb{D}} , implies that f f is starlike function (i.e. convexity implies starlikeness). If the previous condition is not satisfied for every z ∈ 𝔻 z\in{\mathbb{D}} , then it is possible to get new criteria for starlikeness by using | arg ⁡ [ α + z ​ f ′′ ​ ( z ) f ′ ​ ( z ) ] | \left|\arg\left[\alpha+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right| , z ∈ 𝔻 z\in{\mathbb{D}} , where α > 1 . \alpha>1.

1.
Introduction and definitions

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the class of functions
f
which are analytic in the open unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|<1\}

of the form
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots
,
and let
\mathcal{S}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of functions that are univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Also, let

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}&=\left[f\in\mathcal{A}:\operatorname{Re}\left[1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]>0,\,(z\in{\mathbb{D}})\right],\\
\mathcal{S}^{\star}&=\left[f\in\mathcal{A}:\operatorname{Re}\left[\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right]>0,\,(z\in{\mathbb{D}})\right],\end{split}

denote the classes of convex and starlike functions, respectively. It is well-known that

f\in\mathcal{C}\quad\Rightarrow\quad f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star},

(see Duren
[
1
]
).
If the condition for convexity,
\operatorname{Re}\left[1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]>0,
is not satisfied for every
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then we cannot conclude that
f\in\mathcal{C}
, and consequently,
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
.

In this paper
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
in the terms of the expression

\left|\arg\left[\alpha+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right|,\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})

where
\alpha>1
.

2.
Maine results

For our consideration we need the next lemma given by Nunokawa in
[
3
]
.

Lemma A.

Let
p(z)=1+p_{1}z+p_{2}z^{2}+\ldots
be analytic in the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
and
p(z)\neq 0
for
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Also, let suppose that there exists a point
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
such that

\operatorname{Re}\,p(z)>0
for
|z|<|z_{0}|
and
\operatorname{Re}\,p(z_{0})=0
, where
p(z_{0})\neq 0
, i.e.

p(z_{0})=ia
,
a
is real and
a\neq 0
. Then we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=ik,

where
k\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)
, when
a>0
, and

k\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(|a|+\frac{1}{|a|}\right)
, when
a<0
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
\alpha>1
, and let

(1)

\left|\arg\left[\alpha+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right|<{\operatorname{arctg}}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\alpha-1}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
.

Proof.

If we put that
p(z)=\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
, then
p(0)=1
and

\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}+p(z)-1=\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}
. So, we would need to prove that the following implication holds:

(2)

\begin{split}\left|\arg\left[\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}+p(z)+\alpha-1\right]\right|&<\operatorname{arctg}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\alpha-1}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})\\[5.69054pt]
\Rightarrow\quad\operatorname{Re}p(z)&>0\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).\end{split}

First we will prove that
p(z)\neq 0,z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. On a contrary, if there exists
z_{1}\in{\mathbb{D}}
such that
z_{1}
is the zero of
order
m
of the function
p
, then

p(z)=(z-z_{1})^{m}p_{1}(z)
, where
m
is positive integer,
p_{1}
is analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
with

p_{1}(z_{1})\neq 0
, and further

\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}=\frac{mz}{z-z_{1}}+\frac{zp_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{p_{1}(z)}.

This means that the real part of the right hand side can tend to
-\infty
when
z\rightarrow z_{1}
, which is a contradiction to the assumption of the theorem regarding the argument. Thus
p(z)\neq 0
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Now, let suppose that the implication (
2
) does not hold in the unit disc. It means that there exists a point

z_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\operatorname{Re}p(z)>0
for
|z|<|z_{0}|
and
\operatorname{Re}p(z_{0})=0
, where
p(z_{0})\neq 0
.
If we put
p(z_{0})=ia
,
a
is real and
a\neq 0
, then by Lemma A
we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=ik,

where
k\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)
, when
a>0
, and

k\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(|a|+\frac{1}{|a|}\right)
, when
a<0
.

Next, if we put
\Phi(z)=\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}+p(z)+\alpha-1
, then by the previous facts:

\operatorname{Re}\Phi(z_{0})=\alpha-1,\quad\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z_{0})=k+a,

which for
a>0
implies:

\begin{split}\arg\Phi(z_{0})&=\operatorname{arctg}\frac{k+a}{\alpha-1}\geq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)+a}{\alpha-1}\\
&=\operatorname{arctg}\frac{3a+\frac{1}{a}}{2(\alpha-1)}\geq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\alpha-1},\end{split}

because the function
\varphi(a)=3a+\frac{1}{a}
has its minimum value
\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})=2\sqrt{3}.

Similarly, for
a<0
:

\begin{split}\arg\Phi(z_{0})&=\operatorname{arctg}\frac{k+a}{\alpha-1}\leq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{-\frac{1}{2}\left(|a|+\frac{1}{|a|}\right)-|a|}{\alpha-1}\\
&=-\operatorname{arctg}\frac{3|a|+\frac{1}{|a|}}{2(\alpha-1)}\leq-\operatorname{arctg}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\alpha-1}.\end{split}

Combining the cases
a>0
and
a<0
, we receive

\left|\arg\Phi(z_{0})\right|\geq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\alpha-1},

which is a contradiction to the relation (
1
).

This show that
\operatorname{Re}p(z)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}>0,
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, i.e.
that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
.
∎

Example 1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
is defined by the condition

(3)

1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=(\sqrt{3}+1)\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sqrt{3},

where we use the principal value of the square root. For real
z
close to -1 we have

\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)<0,
which means that
f
is not convex. On the other hand,
from (
3
) we have

\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+(\sqrt{3}+1)=(\sqrt{3}+1)\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and from here

\begin{split}\left|\arg\left[\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+(\sqrt{3}+1)\right]\right|&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|\arg\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right|\\
&<\frac{\pi}{4}=\operatorname{arctg}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{(\sqrt{3}+1)-1},\end{split}

which by Theorem
1
(with
\alpha=\sqrt{3}+1
) implies that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
.

Letting
\alpha
tend to 1 in Theorem
1
, we have the following well-known result.

Corollary 1
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
and

\left|\arg\left[\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+1\right]\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})

Then
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}.

Further, it is easy to verify that the disc with center
\alpha
and radius
\frac{\alpha\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3+(\alpha-1)^{2}}}
is contained in the angle

|\arg z|<\operatorname{arctg}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\alpha-1}
, and so by using the result of Theorem
1
, we have that

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|=\left|\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+\alpha\right)-\alpha\right|<\frac{\alpha\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3+(\alpha-1)^{2}}}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})

implies
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
.
Since the function
\varphi(\alpha)=:\frac{\alpha\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3+(\alpha-1)^{2}}}
,
\alpha\geq 1
, attains its maximal value 2 for

\alpha=4
, we get

Corollary 2
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
and

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|<2\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})

Then
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}.

In a similar way as in Theorem
1
, we can consider starlikeness problem in connection with the class defined by

\mathcal{G}=\left[f\in\mathcal{A}:\operatorname{Re}\left[1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]<\frac{3}{2},\,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\right].

Ozaki in
[
4
]
proved that if
f\in\mathcal{G}
, then
f
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Later, Umezava in

[
6
]
showed the functions from
\mathcal{G}
are convex in one direction. Also, it is shown in
the papers
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
that
\mathcal{G}
is subclass of
\mathcal{S}^{\star}.

If
\operatorname{Re}\left[1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]<\frac{3}{2}
is not satisfied for every
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then we can pose a question if
for some
\beta<1
, such that

(4)

\operatorname{Re}\left[\beta+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]<\frac{3}{2}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

is it possible obtain sufficient condition for starlikeness, in a similar way as in Theorem
1
.
The condition (
4
) is equivalent to

\operatorname{Re}\left[\frac{3-2\beta}{2}-\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]>0\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

and we can use similar technique. This lead so

Theorem 2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
\beta<1
, and let

\left|\arg\left[\frac{3-2\beta}{2}-\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right|<\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{5-2\beta}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
1
, we put that
p(z)=\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
, such that
p(0)=1
and

\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}+p(z)-1=\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}
. Now, our aim is to prove that for some
\beta<1

the following implication holds:

(5)

\begin{split}\left|\arg\left[\frac{5-2\beta}{2}-\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}-p(z)\right]\right|&<\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{5-2\beta}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})\\[5.69054pt]
\Rightarrow\operatorname{Re}p(z)&>0\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).\end{split}

First, as in Theorem
1
we conclude that
p(z)\neq 0,z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Further, if the implication (
5
) is not true, there exists a point

z_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
such that

\operatorname{Re}p(z)>0
for
|z|<|z_{0}|
and
\operatorname{Re}p(z_{0})=0
, where
p(z_{0})\neq 0
.
If we put
p(z_{0})=ia
,
a
is real and
a\neq 0
, then by Lemma A
we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=ik,

where
k\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)
, when
a>0
, and

k\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(|a|+\frac{1}{|a|}\right)
, when
a<0
.
Also, for

\Psi(z)=\frac{5-2\beta}{2}-\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}-p(z),

using the previous conclusions, we have

\operatorname{Re}\Psi(z_{0})=\frac{5-2\beta}{2},\quad\operatorname{Im}\Psi(z_{0})=-(k+a).

Using the same method as in the proof of Theorem
1
, we easily conclude that

\arg\Psi(z_{0})=-\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2(k+a)}{5-2\beta}\leq-\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{5-2\beta},

when
a>0,
and

\arg\Psi(z_{0})=-\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2(k+a)}{5-2\beta}\geq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{5-2\beta},

when
a<0
. These facts imply contradiction of the assumption in (
5
).

So, we have the statement of this theorem.
∎

Letting
\beta
tend to 1 in Theorem
2
, we receive

Corollary 3
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
and

\left|\arg\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right|<\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\approx 49.1^{\circ}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}.

As in the case of Theorem
1
, we can show that the disc with center
\frac{3-2\beta}{2}
and radius

\frac{(3-2\beta)\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{12+(5-2\beta)^{2}}}
is containing in the angle

|\arg z|\leq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{5-2\beta}
. So, if

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|=\left|\left(\frac{3-2\beta}{2}-\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)-\frac{3-2\beta}{2}\right|<\frac{(3-2\beta)\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{12+(5-2\beta)^{2}}},

for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then Theorem
2
brings that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}.

Since the function
\psi(\beta)=:\frac{(3-2\beta)\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{12+(5-2\beta)^{2}}}
is decreasing
on the interval
(-\infty,1]
, and
\psi(\beta)\rightarrow\sqrt{3}
, when
\beta\rightarrow-\infty,
we get

Corollary 4
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
and

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|<\sqrt{3}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}.

For a starlike function
f
it is not necessary that
Re\frac{f(z)}{z}>0,

z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. For example,
for Koebe function
k(z)=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}
we have

\left.\operatorname{Re}\frac{k(z)}{z}\right|_{z=(1+i)/\sqrt{2}}=-(\sqrt{2}+1)<0,

and this also holds for points in
{\mathbb{D}}
close enought to
\frac{1+i}{\sqrt{2}}
.

In the next theorem we give a condition which provides
\operatorname{Re}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
\gamma\geq 0
,  and let

\left|\arg\left[\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+\gamma\right]\right|<\operatorname{arctg}\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
\operatorname{Re}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Proof.

We apply the same method as in the previous theorems. In this case, we use
p(z)=\frac{f(z)}{z}
, such that
p(0)=1
and

\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}+1=\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
. So, the result we need to prove can be rewritten in the following equivalent form:

(6)

\begin{split}\left|\arg\left[\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}+1+\gamma\right]\right|&<\operatorname{arctg}\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})\\[5.69054pt]
\Rightarrow\qquad\operatorname{Re}p(z)&>0\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).\end{split}

First,
p(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Next, if the implication (
6
) does not hold in the unit disc, then there exists a point

z_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\operatorname{Re}p(z)>0
for
|z|<|z_{0}|
and
\operatorname{Re}p(z_{0})=0
, where
p(z_{0})\neq 0
.
If we put
p(z_{0})=ia
,
a
is real and
a\neq 0
, then by Lemma A
we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=ik,

where
k\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)
, when
a>0
, and

k\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(|a|+\frac{1}{|a|}\right)
, when
a<0
. Now, for
a>0
we get:

\begin{split}\arg\left[\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}+1+\gamma\right]&=\arg(ik+1+\gamma)=\operatorname{arctg}\frac{k}{1+\gamma}\\
&\geq\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)}{1+\gamma}\geq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{1}{1+\gamma},\end{split}

and for
a<0
,

\arg\left[\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}+1+\gamma\right]\leq-\operatorname{arctg}\frac{1}{1+\gamma}.

By combining the above conclusions, we receive that

\left|\arg\left(\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}+1+\gamma\right)\right|\geq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{1}{1+\gamma},

which is a contradiction to assumption in (
6
).
∎

For
\gamma=0
in previous theorem we have next result.

Corollary 5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
and

\left|\arg\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right|<\frac{\pi}{4}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
\operatorname{Re}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

In Theorem
3
the function
f
from
\mathcal{A}
need not be starlike as the next example shows.

Example 2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
is defined by

(7)

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\sqrt{3}\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}+1-\sqrt{3},

where we use the principal value for square root. For real
z
close to -1 we have that

\operatorname{Re}\left[\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right]<0,
which means that
f
is not starlike. But
from (
7
) we have

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+(\sqrt{3}-1)=\sqrt{3}\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},

and from here

\begin{split}\left|\arg\left[\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+(\sqrt{3}-1)\right]\right|&\leq\frac{1}{3}\left|\arg\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)\right|\\
&<\frac{\pi}{6}=\operatorname{arctg}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{3}-1)+1},\end{split}

which by Theorem
3
(with
\gamma=\sqrt{3}-1
) implies that
\operatorname{Re}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0,

z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

It is possible to show that the disc with center
1+\gamma
and radius

\frac{1+\gamma}{\sqrt{1+(1+\gamma)^{2}}}
lies in the angle

|\arg z|\leq\operatorname{arctg}\frac{1}{1+\gamma}
, and so, by using the result of Theorem
3
we have that

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-1\right|=\left|\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+\gamma\right)-(1+\gamma)\right|<\frac{1+\gamma}{\sqrt{1+(1+\gamma)^{2}}}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}})

implies
\operatorname{Re}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0,

z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

When
\gamma\rightarrow+\infty
we receive

Corollary 6
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
and

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-1\right|<1\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
\operatorname{Re}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0,

z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Improved upper bounds on the stabilizer rank of magic states
Hammam Qassim, Hakop Pashayan, David Gosset

Abstract
In this work we improve the runtime of recent classical algorithms for strong simulation of quantum circuits composed of Clifford and T gates. The improvement is obtained by establishing a new upper bound on the stabilizer rank of m m copies of the magic state | T ⟩ = 2 − 1 ( | 0 ⟩ + e i ​ π / 4 | 1 ⟩ ) \lvert T\rangle=\sqrt{2}^{-1}(\lvert 0\rangle+e^{i\pi/4}\lvert 1\rangle) in the limit of large m m . In particular, we show that | T ⟩ ⊗ m |T\rangle^{\otimes m} can be exactly expressed as a superposition of at most O ⁡ ( 2 α ​ m ) O(2^{\alpha m}) stabilizer states, where α ≤ 0.3963 \alpha\leq 0.3963 , improving on the best previously known bound α ≤ 0.463 \alpha\leq 0.463 . This furnishes, via known techniques, a classical algorithm which approximates output probabilities of an n n -qubit ‘Clifford + T’ circuit U U with m m uses of the T gate to within a given inverse polynomial relative error using a runtime poly ⁡ ( n , m ) ​ 2 α ​ m \mathrm{poly}(n,m)2^{\alpha m} . We also provide improved upper bounds on the stabilizer rank of symmetric product states | ψ ⟩ ⊗ m \lvert\psi\rangle^{\otimes m} more generally; as a consequence we obtain a strong simulation algorithm for circuits consisting of Clifford gates and m m instances of any (fixed) single-qubit Z Z -rotation gate with runtime poly ​ ( n , m ) ​ 2 m / 2 \text{poly}(n,m)2^{m/2} . We suggest a method to further improve the upper bounds by constructing linear codes with certain properties.

Magic code states

We can extend the method described above by replacing the magic cat state by a more general entangled state of the form

\displaystyle\lvert\hat{L}\rangle=2^{-k/2}\sum_{x\in L}\lvert\hat{x}\rangle,

(13)

where
\lvert\hat{0}\rangle=\lvert T\rangle,\lvert\hat{1}\rangle=\lvert T^{\perp}\rangle
, and

is a linear space of dimension

. Note that
\lvert\text{cat}_{m}\rangle
is recovered by taking

to be the
m

\lvert\hat{L}\rangle
has stabilizer rank upper bounded by
\chi(T^{\otimes m})
, since
\lvert\hat{L}\rangle
can be obtained from
\lvert T\rangle^{\otimes m}
using only Pauli measurements and postselection

\lvert\hat{L}\rangle\propto\prod_{i=1}^{k}(I+Z(v^{i}))\lvert T\rangle^{\otimes m},

where
Z(v):=Z^{v_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes Z^{v_{m}}
and
\{v^{i}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is any basis of
L
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose there exist polynomials
p
and
q

\gamma>0

\displaystyle\Omega(2^{\gamma n}/p(n))\leq\chi(T^{\otimes n})\leq O(q(n)2^{\gamma n}).

(14)

Then for every linear code

of block length

\displaystyle\gamma\leq\frac{\log_{2}(\chi(\hat{L}))}{m-2k}.

(15)

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k\times m}
be a generator matrix of
L
, that is, a binary matrix whose rows span
L
. Up to re-ordering the qubits we may assume that
G
is in the form

G=[I_{k\times k}\;|\;B],

for a binary matrix
B
of size
k
by
(m-k)
.
As
L
is spanned by the rows of
G
, the constraints

are uniquely satisfied by

. We infer that

\displaystyle\left(\langle T\rvert^{\otimes k}\otimes I\right)\lvert\hat{L}\rangle\propto\lvert T\rangle^{\otimes m-k},

(16)

which in turn implies
\chi(T^{\otimes m-k})\leq\chi(T^{\otimes k})\chi(\hat{L}).
Repeating the contraction in Eq. (
16
) in parallel
\ell
times gives

\chi(T^{\otimes\ell(m-k)})\leq\chi(T^{\otimes\ell k})\chi(\hat{L}^{\otimes\ell}).

Letting
\ell
get large and using Eq. (
14
) yields

2^{\gamma\ell(m-k)}\leq f(\ell)2^{\gamma\ell k}\chi(\hat{L}^{\otimes\ell})
, for some polynomial
f(\ell)
. Taking the binary log, rearranging, and using sub-multiplicativity of the stabilizer rank we get

\displaystyle\gamma\leq\frac{\log_{2}(f(\ell)\chi(\hat{L}^{\otimes\ell}))}{\ell(m-2k)}\leq\frac{\log_{2}(\chi(\hat{L}))}{m-2k}\;+\;\frac{\log_{2}(f(\ell))}{\ell(m-2k)}.

(17)

The second term vanishes for large
\ell
which completes the proof.
∎

The upper bound from Theorem
1
is recovered from Eq. (
15
) by setting
L
to be the six-bit repetition code (so that
\lvert\hat{L}\rangle=\lvert\text{cat}_{6}\rangle
). More generally, to use Eq. (
15
) we need an upper bound on
\chi(\hat{L})
which is significantly better than the trivial upper bound
\chi(\hat{L})\leq 2^{m-k}
. The latter upper bound is obtained by noting

\displaystyle\lvert\hat{L}\rangle\propto\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m-k}(I+A(u^{i}))\right)\lvert 0\rangle^{\otimes m},

(18)

where
\{u^{i}\}
is any basis of
L^{\perp}
, and we define the Clifford operators
A:=e^{-i\pi/4}SX
and
A(u)=A^{u_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes A^{u_{m}}
.
(To see this, note that
\lvert 0\rangle^{\otimes m}\propto\sum_{x}\lvert\hat{x}\rangle
,
A\lvert\hat{0}\rangle=\lvert\hat{0}\rangle
, and
A\lvert\hat{1}\rangle=-\lvert\hat{1}\rangle
).

Theorem
4
requires
k<m/2
, which is somewhat restrictive. Relaxing this requirement we can easily find magic code states with low stabilizer rank. For example, every Reed-Muller code
R_{a,b}
(of degree
a
on
b
variables) corresponds to a stabilizer state if the degree is large enough.
This is true since for any code
L
we can express
\lvert\hat{L}\rangle
in the computational basis as
\lvert\hat{L}\rangle\propto\sum_{x\in L^{\perp}}e^{i\pi|x|/4}\lvert x\rangle
, and if
\lceil b/(b-a-1)\rceil\geq 4
then
|x|=0\mod 8
for all
x\in R_{a,b}^{\perp}

[
15
]
. In this case we have
\lvert\hat{R}_{a,b}\rangle\propto\sum_{x\in R_{a,b}^{\perp}}\lvert x\rangle
, and hence it is a stabilizer state.
Another example is the magic code state associated with the 8-bit first order Reed-Muller code. Since
R_{1,3}=R_{1,3}^{\perp}
and
|x|=4
for all
x\in R_{1,3}
except the all-zero and all-one binary strings, we have the stabilizer decomposition

\displaystyle\lvert\hat{R}_{1,3}\rangle\propto 2(\lvert 0\rangle^{\otimes 8}+\lvert 1\rangle^{\otimes 8})-\sum_{x\in{R_{1,3}}}\lvert x\rangle,

(19)

hence
\chi(\hat{R}_{1,3})=2
(one can easily show that
\lvert\hat{R}_{1,3}\rangle
is not a stabilizer state).

In the preceding examples the code dimension
k
exceeds the threshold
k<m/2
required by Theorem
4
, and so one cannot obtain an upper bound on
\chi(T^{\otimes n})
that way. We leave it as an open question whether the bound in Theorem
1
can be improved using this formulation in terms of linear codes, i.e. whether there exists a linear code
L
of block length
m
and dimension
k<m/2
such that
\log_{2}(\chi(\hat{L}))/(m-2k)<\log_{2}(3)/4
.

Appendix A
Stabilizer rank of small magic cats

Here we provide further details of the claimed values of
\chi(\text{cat}_{m})
listed in Table
1
.

First let us consider the upper bounds. We have already shown
\chi(\text{cat}_{2})\leq 2
and
\chi(\text{cat}_{6})\leq 3
in the main text. Using the fact that

\langle 0|_{m}\penalty\ |\text{cat}_{m}\rangle\propto|\text{cat}_{m-1}\rangle

we see that
\chi(\text{cat}_{m})\geq\chi(\text{cat}_{m-1})
for all
m
. Therefore
\chi(\text{cat}_{5})\leq 3
as well. We have
\chi(\text{cat}_{3})\leq\chi(\text{cat}_{4})\leq 2
due to the decomposition

|\text{cat}_{4}\rangle=i|E\rangle+\left(\frac{1-i}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|0^{4}\rangle-i|1^{4}\rangle\right)\qquad\text{where}\qquad|E\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{4}:|x|even}|x\rangle.

Finally, we have
\chi(\text{cat}_{7})\leq\chi(\text{cat}_{8})\leq 6
which follows from

\langle\text{cat}_{2}|_{4,5}\penalty\ |\text{cat}_{4}\rangle|\text{cat}_{6}\rangle\propto|\text{cat}_{8}\rangle.

The left hand side is easily seen to have rank at most
6
since
\text{cat}_{2}
is a stabilizer state and
\chi(\text{cat}_{4})\leq 2
and
\chi(\text{cat}_{6})\leq 3
.

Next let us discuss the matching lower bounds for
2\leq m\leq 6
. Clearly there is nothing to show for
m=2
since all nonzero states have stabilizer rank at least
1
.

To establish the lower bounds for
m=3,\ldots,6
it will be convenient to define the
Pauli spectrum

[
16
]
of an
m
-qubit state
\psi
as the multiset

\mathrm{PS}(\psi)=\left\{\langle\psi|P|\psi\rangle:P\in\mathcal{P}_{m}\right\}

where
\mathcal{P}_{m}
is the set of
m
-qubit Pauli operators. Note that all elements of the Pauli spectrum of a stabilizer state are either
+1,-1,0
. Moreover, we have
\mathrm{PS}(C|\psi\rangle)=\mathrm{PS}(|\psi\rangle)
for any Clifford unitary
C
.

To show that
\chi(\text{cat}_{4})\geq\chi(\text{cat}_{3})\geq 2
we need only show that
\text{cat}_{3}
is not a stabilizer state. To see this we note that
PS(\text{cat}_{3})
contains elements which are not
+1,-1,0
. In particular,
\langle\text{cat}_{3}|X\otimes X\otimes I|\text{cat}_{3}\rangle=1/2
and therefore
\lvert\text{cat}_{3}\rangle
is not a stabilizer state.

In the remainder of this appendix we show that
\chi(\text{cat}_{6})\geq\chi(\text{cat}_{5})\geq 3
. We use the fact that rank-2 states can be brought into a canonical form using the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
be two
n
-qubit stabilizer states. There exists a Clifford unitary
C
such that

C|\phi_{1}\rangle=|0^{n}\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad C|\phi_{2}\rangle\propto|1^{a}0^{b}+^{n-a-b}\rangle,

(20)

for some
a\in\{0,1\}
and
0\leq b\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a Clifford such that
D|\phi_{1}\rangle=|0^{n}\rangle
and consider the stabilizer state
D|\phi_{2}\rangle
. Let
H
be the single-qubit Hadamard on qubit
j\in[n]
and write
H(y)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(H_{j})^{y_{j}}
for any binary string
y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. Since
D|\phi_{2}\rangle
is a stabilizer state it can be written in the so-called CH-form
[
5
]
as

D|\phi_{2}\rangle\propto U_{c}H(r)|s\rangle

(21)

for some
r,s\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and some Clifford
U_{c}
such that
U_{c}|0^{n}\rangle=|0^{n}\rangle
.
We can furthermore find a permutation of the qubits
P
such that

PH(r)|s\rangle=\lvert x\rangle\otimes H^{|r|}\lvert y\rangle

for some binary strings
x\in\{0,1\}^{n-|r|},y\in\{0,1\}^{|r|}
.

First consider the case in which
\langle\phi_{1}|\phi_{2}\rangle\neq 0
.
Then
x=0
, and one can directly confirm that
C=(I\otimes Z(y))P(U_{c})^{\dagger}D
satisfies Eq. (
20
) with
a=0
and
b=n-|r|
.

Next suppose
\langle\phi_{1}|\phi_{2}\rangle=0
. In this case
x\neq 0
and therefore we may choose the permutation
P
such that
x=1x^{\prime}
for some
(n-|r|-1)
-bit string
x^{\prime}
. We then choose
C=(V\otimes Z(y))P(U_{c})^{\dagger}D
where
V
is a product of
CNOT
gates controlled on the first bit of
x
such that
V|x\rangle=|10^{n-|r|-1}\rangle
. Again we can directly confirm Eq. (
20
) with
a=1
and
b=n-|r|-1
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

\chi(\mathrm{cat}_{5})>2
.

Proof.

Below we shall use the following fact about the Pauli spectrum of
\text{cat}_{5}
:

|\langle\text{cat}_{5}|P|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle|\in\{0,1/4,1/2,1\}\quad\text{for all }\quad P\in\mathcal{P}_{5}

(22)

Since there are Paulis for which
|\langle\text{cat}_{5}|P|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle|\notin\{0,\pm 1\}
, we know that
\text{cat}_{5}
is not a stabilizer state.

Suppose that
\text{cat}_{5}
can be expressed as a superposition of two stabilizer states. Then by Lemma
2
there is a Clifford
C
such that

C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle\propto|0^{n}\rangle+\gamma|1^{a}0^{b}+^{n-a-b}\rangle

for some
a\in\{0,1\}
,
0\leq b\leq n
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
. The fact that
\lvert\text{cat}_{5}\rangle
is not a stabilizer state implies
|\gamma|>0
.
We can furthermore assume that
|\gamma|\leq 1
, if necessary by applying a Clifford that permutes
\lvert 0^{n}\rangle
and
\lvert 1^{a}0^{b}+^{n-a-b}\rangle
then dividing by
|\gamma|
.

First let us suppose (to reach a contradiction) that
\text{cat}_{5}
can be written as a superposition of two orthogonal stabilizer states, i.e., the case
a=1
. Note that

2^{b}=\left|\{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}:P|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle=|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle\}\right|,

and therefore
b=1
. Thus we may find a Clifford
C
such that

C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\gamma|^{2}}}\left(|0^{5}\rangle+\gamma|10+^{3}\rangle\right).

(23)

Let us write
\gamma=re^{i\theta}
with
0<r\leq 1
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
. Now we see that

\displaystyle\left|\langle\text{cat}_{5}|C^{\dagger}Z_{1}C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1-r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\left|\langle\text{cat}_{5}|C^{\dagger}X_{1}C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{r\cos(\theta)}{\sqrt{2}(1+r^{2})}\right|

\displaystyle\left|\langle\text{cat}_{5}|C^{\dagger}X_{1}Y_{4}C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{r\sin(\theta)}{\sqrt{2}(1+r^{2})}\right|.

Recall from Eq. (
22
) that each of these three Pauli expectation values must be in the set
\{0,1/4,1/2,1\}
. Solving for
r,\theta
which satisfy this constraint we get

\gamma\in\{\frac{1+i}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1-i}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-1-i}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-1+i}{\sqrt{2}}\}

For each of the four states Eq. (
23

\gamma
we computed the Pauli spectrum using a computer and compared with that of
|\mathrm{cat}_{5}\rangle
. We found that these multisets were different (in particular, the number of Paulis with expected value equal to zero is
710
for each of the four candidate states, but
782

|\mathrm{cat}_{5}\rangle
) and therefore we conclude that
\text{cat}_{5}
cannot be written as a superposition of two orthogonal stabilizer states.

Next let us suppose (to reach a contradiction) that
\text{cat}_{5}
can be written as a superposition of two nonorthogonal stabilizer states, i.e., the case
a=0
. Again we may take
b=1
, so

C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle=\frac{1}{\left(1+|\gamma|^{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\gamma)/4\right)^{1/2}}(|0^{5}\rangle+\gamma|0+^{4})\rangle.

(24)

By a direct calculation we have

\left|\langle\text{cat}_{5}|C^{\dagger}Z_{2}X_{3}C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle\right|=\left|\frac{r\cos(\theta)}{2}\left(1+r^{2}+\frac{r\cos(\theta)}{2}\right)^{-1}\right|

\left|\langle\text{cat}_{5}|C^{\dagger}Y_{2}C|\text{cat}_{5}\rangle\right|=\left|\frac{r\sin(\theta)}{2}\left(1+r^{2}+\frac{r\cos(\theta)}{2}\right)^{-1}\right|.

Recall from Eq. (
22

\{0,1/4,1/2,1\}

0<r\leq 1

\theta\in[0,2\pi)

\gamma=re^{i\theta}
must satisfy

\gamma\in\{\pm i,-\frac{1}{2}\}.

Thus we have three candidate states Eq. (
24

\gamma

|\mathrm{cat}_{5}\rangle
. We found that these multisets were different and therefore reach a contradiction. We conclude that
|\mathrm{cat}_{5}\rangle
cannot be written as a superposition of two stabilizer states.
∎

Nonclassical properties of Hermite polynomial’s excitation on squeezed vacuum and its decoherence in phase-sensitive reservoirs
Shi-You Liu 1 , Ya-Zhou Li 1 ,Li-Yun Hu 1,2∗ , Jie-Hui Huang 1,2 , Xue-Xiang Xu 1 , and Xiang-Yang Tao 1

Abstract
We introduce Hermite polynomial excitation squeezed vacuum (SV) H n ( O ^ ) S ( r ) | 0 ⟩ H_{n}(\hat{O})S\left(r\right)\left|0\right\rangle with O ^ = μ ​ a + ν ​ a † \hat{O}=\mu a+\nu a^{\dagger} . We investigate analytically the nonclassical properties according to Mandel’s Q parameter, second correlation function, squeezing effect and the negativity of Wigner function (WF). It is found that all these nonclassicalities can be enhanced by H n ​ ( O ^ ) H_{n}(\hat{O}) operation and adjustable parameters μ \mu and ν \nu . In particular, the optimal negative volume δ o ​ p ​ t \delta_{opt} of WF can be achieved by modulating μ \mu and ν \nu for n ⩾ 2 , n\geqslant 2, while δ \delta is kept unchanged for n = 1 n=1 . Furthermore, the decoherence effect of phase-sensitive enviornment on this state is examined. It is shown that δ \delta with bigger n n diminishes more quickly than that with lower n n , which indicates that single-photon subtraction SV presents more roboustness. Parameter M M of reservoirs can be effectively used to improve the nonclassicality. 4

Wigner distribution of the HPS-SV

As a kind of quasi-probability function, the Wigner function (WF) is a
powerful tool to describe the nonclassicality of optical fields, whose partial
negativity implies the highly nonclassical properties of quantum states. In
addition, the negativity is often used to present the decoherence of quantum
states. In this section, we derive the analytical expression of WF for the
HPS-SV by using the the Weyl ordered operators’ invariance under similar
transformations
30
. For a single-mode quantum system, the WF can be
calculated as
W=
tr
\left[\rho\Delta\left(\alpha\right)\right]
,
where
\Delta\left(\alpha\right)
single-mode Wigner operator

30

31

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}}{\pi}{\displaystyle\int}\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}\left|\beta\right\rangle\left\langle-\beta\right|e^{2\left(\alpha\beta^{\ast}-\alpha^{\ast}\beta\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\colon}{\colon}\delta\left(\alpha-a\right)\delta\left(\alpha^{\ast}-a^{{\dagger}}\right)\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\colon}{\colon}.

(27)

Here
\alpha=\left(q+ip\right)/\sqrt{2}
and the symbol
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\colon}{\colon}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\colon}{\colon}
denotes Weyl ordering.

The merit of Weyl ordering lies in the Weyl ordered operators’ invariance
under similar transformations proved, which means
S\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{:}{:}\left(\circ\circ\circ\right)\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{:}{:}S^{-1}=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{:}{:}S\left(\circ\circ\circ\right)S^{-1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{:}{:}

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{:}{:}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{:}{:}
did not exist, so
S
can pass through it. Then following this invariance and
the above squeezing transform relations, we have

S^{\dagger}\Delta\left(\alpha\right)S=\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\colon}{\colon}\delta\left(\bar{\alpha}-a\right)\delta\left(\bar{\alpha}^{\ast}-a^{{\dagger}}\right)\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\colon}{\colon}=\Delta\left(\bar{\alpha}\right),

(28)

where
\bar{\alpha}=\alpha\cosh r+\alpha^{\ast}\sinh r

W\left(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast}\right)=N_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}}^{2}\left\langle 0\right|H_{n}\left(\hat{O}_{1}^{{\dagger}}\right)\Delta\left(\bar{\alpha}\right)H_{n}\left(\hat{O}_{1}\right)\left|0\right\rangle.

\displaystyle W\left(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|H_{n-l}\left(\frac{2\nu_{1}\bar{\alpha}}{i\sqrt{2\mu_{1}\nu_{1}-1}}\right)\right|^{2}.

(30)

Obviously, the WF

Decoherence of the HPS-SV in Phase-Sensitive Reservoirs

In this section, we shall examine the time evolution of the HPS-SV at the
presence of phase-sensitive reservoirs. In the interaction picture and the
Born and Markow approximation, the time evolution of the density matrix is
governed by the master equation (ME)
33
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\kappa\bar{n}L\left[a^{{\dagger}}\right]\rho+\kappa\left(\bar{n}+1\right)L\left[a\right]\rho

\displaystyle+\kappa MD\left[a\right]\rho+\kappa M^{\ast}D\left[a^{{\dagger}}\right]\rho,

(32)

and

\displaystyle L\left[O^{{\dagger}}\right]\rho

\displaystyle=2O^{{\dagger}}\rho O-OO^{{\dagger}}\rho-\rho OO^{{\dagger}},

(33)

\displaystyle D\left[a\right]\rho

\displaystyle=2a^{{\dagger}}\rho a^{{\dagger}}-a^{{\dagger}2}\rho-\rho a^{{\dagger}2}.

(34)

where
\kappa
and
\bar{n}
are the dissipative coefficient and the average
thermal photon number of the environment, respectively. Here
M
is the
complex correlation parameter between modes symmetrically displaced about
center frequency. In fact, the ME in Eq.(
32
) includes two special cases:
(1) For an uncorrelated reservoir, i.e.,
M=0,
Eq.(
32
) becomes the ME
describing the interaction between a system and a thermal environment at
finite temperature; (2) when
M=\bar{n}=0,
Eq.(
32
) reducess to the one
describing the photon-loss channel. For an (non)ideally squeezed reservoir,
the constraint condition
\left|M\right|^{2}=\bar{n}\left(\bar{n}+1\right)
(
\left|M\right|^{2}<\bar{n}\left(\bar{n}+1\right)
) is required.

In Ref.
34
, we derived the Kraus operator-sum representation of density
operator
\rho
and the time evolution of some distibution functions by using
the thermal entangled state representation
\left\langle\eta\right|
.
The evolution of Wigner function is given by

W\left(\alpha,t\right)=\frac{2\mu_{\infty}}{T}\int\frac{d^{2}\beta}{\pi}e^{-\frac{2\mu_{\infty}^{2}}{T}\Sigma\left(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}^{\ast}\right)}W\left(\beta,0\right),

(35)

where
T=1-e^{-2\allowbreak\kappa t},

\bar{\alpha}=\alpha-\beta e^{-\kappa t}
, and
\mu_{\infty}=1/\sqrt{\left(2\bar{n}+1\right)^{2}-4\left|M\right|^{2}}
,
\Sigma\left(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}^{\ast}\right)

is defined as

\displaystyle\Sigma\left(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}\bar{\alpha}&\bar{\alpha}^{\ast}\end{array}\right)\sigma_{\infty}\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}\bar{\alpha}\\
\bar{\alpha}^{\ast}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\sigma_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}M^{\ast}&\bar{n}+\frac{1}{2}\\
\bar{n}+\frac{1}{2}&M\end{array}\right).

Noting the differential expression of Wigner function Eq.(
30
), we can
finally obtain

W\left(\alpha,t\right)=W_{r}\left(\alpha,t\right)F_{n}\left(\alpha,t\right),

(41)

where
W_{r}\left(\alpha,t\right)
is the evolution of Wigner function of
squeezed vacuum in phase sensitive resevoire, and
F_{n}\left(\alpha,t\right)
is a non-Gaussian item due to the presence of Hermite
excitation,

\displaystyle W_{r}\left(\alpha,t\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{\infty}e^{-P}}{\pi\sqrt{D}}e^{\frac{1}{TD}\left(2R_{1}R_{2}R_{2}^{\ast}-R_{3}R_{2}^{\ast}{}^{2}-R_{2}^{2}R_{3}^{\ast}\right)},

(42)

\displaystyle F_{n}\left(\alpha,t\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{\left[n!\right]^{2}\left(-G_{1}\right)^{l}\left|G_{2}\right|^{n-l}}{l!\left[\left(n-l\right)!\right]^{2}}

\displaystyle\times N_{\mu,\nu}^{2}\left|H_{n-l}\left(\frac{G_{3}}{2i\sqrt{G_{2}}}\right)\right|^{2},

(43)

and
D=R_{1}^{2}-\left|R_{3}\right|^{2},

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mu_{\infty}^{2}}{T}\left[\left(2\bar{n}+1\right)\left|\alpha\right|^{2}+M\alpha^{\ast}{}^{2}+M^{\ast}\alpha^{2}\right],

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(1+2\bar{n}\right)\mu_{\infty}^{2}e^{-2(t\kappa)}+\allowbreak T\allowbreak\cosh 2r,

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{\infty}^{2}\left(\alpha^{\ast}+2\bar{n}\alpha^{\ast}+2\alpha M^{\ast}\right)e^{-\kappa t},

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=2\mu_{\infty}^{2}M^{\ast}e^{-2(t\kappa)}+T\sinh 2r,

(44)

as well as

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=4\nu_{1}^{2}\left\{1+\frac{T}{D}\left[\left(R_{3}+R_{3}^{\ast}\right)\sinh 2r-2R_{1}\cosh 2r\right]\right\},

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=2\mu_{1}\nu_{1}-1+\frac{2T\nu_{1}^{2}}{D}\left\{2R_{1}\sinh 2r\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\allowbreak\left(R_{3}-R_{3}^{\ast}+\left(R_{3}+R_{3}^{\ast}\right)\cosh 2r\right)\right\},

\displaystyle G_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\nu_{1}}{D}\allowbreak R_{1}\left(\allowbreak R_{2}^{\ast}\sinh r+R_{2}\cosh r\allowbreak\right)

(45)

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have introduced a new non-Gaussian state, which is generated
by applying Hermite-polynomial excitation on squeezed vacuum. Single-photon
addition/subtraction and Hermite polynomial’s addition/subtraction can be seen
as its three special cases. Its normalized factor is found to be a Legendre
polynomial. Then we have investigated its nonclassicality according to the
Mandel’s Q parameter, second-order correlation function, photon-number
distribution, squeezing effects and the negativity of WF in phase space. It is
shown that all these nonclassical properties can be obviously improved by the
HPS operation and can be remarkably modulated by superposition parameters

\mu
and
\nu
. The degree of squeezing of the HPS-SV increases with
r
. In
particular, comparing with the SV, an observable improvement of squeezing
effect can be achieved by Hermite photon-subtraction operation
H_{2}\left(a\right)
and the coherent superposition operation (
H_{2}\left(a+a^{{\dagger}}\right)
) in a small region (
r\lesssim 0.6
) and a large region, respectively.

In addition, the numerical calculation of negative volume
\delta
of WF
showed that
\delta
increases with the order
n
for
r\lesssim 0.45
and
decreases with
r
. It is interesting to notice that the negative volume

\delta
is given
\delta=2/\sqrt{e}-1\approx 0.2131
independent of
\mu
and

\nu
, as well as
r
. For high-order excitation (
n\geqslant 2
), the negative
volume can be optimized by modulating parameters
\nu
(
\mu=\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}
) and
n
for a given
r
. It is found that the negative volume
\delta
may
increase with
n
and decrease with
r
when
\nu
exceeds a certain
threshold. In particular, the optimal value of
\delta
can be obtained in a
bigger region of
\nu
(
1>\nu\gtrsim 0.41
) not at
\nu=0
or
\nu=1
. This
implies that the effects of the coherent operation
H_{n}(\mu a+\nu a^{\dagger})
with higher order
n\geqslant 2
are prominent than those of the
mere photon subtraction
H_{n}(\nu a^{\dagger})
and the addition
H_{n}(\nu a^{\dagger})
particularly in the larger region of parameter
\nu
, whereas
the optimal operation is not the photon subtraction or the photon addition in
this region, which is a new result.

Furthermore, we have considered the decoherence effects of the HPS-SV in
phase-sensitive reservoirs according to the analitically derived WF
distribution. It is shown that the negative area and volume diminish gradually
with the evolution of time and dissapear eventually. However, the negative
volume of the HPS-SV with higher order excitation decays more rapidly with
time, which implies that single-photon subtraction/addition SV has a more
strongger roboustness than a higher-order photon subtraction/addition although
the latter has a bigger negative volume at initial time. In addition, the
parameter
M
describing the squeezing characteristic of reservoirs can be
effectively used to enhance the nonclassicality.

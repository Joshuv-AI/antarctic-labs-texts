Thermodynamics of accelerating AdS 4 black holes from the covariant phase space
Hyojoong Kim, Nakwoo Kim, Yein Lee, and Aaron Poole

Abstract
We study the charges and first law of thermodynamics for accelerating, non-rotating black holes with dyonic charges in AdS 4 using the covariant phase space formalism. In order to apply the formalism to these solutions (which are asymptotically locally AdS and admit a non-smooth conformal boundary ℐ \mathscr{I} ) we make two key improvements: 1) We relax the requirement to impose Dirichlet boundary conditions and demand merely a well-posed variational problem. 2) We keep careful track of the codimension-2 corner term induced by the holographic counterterms, a necessary requirement due to the presence of “cosmic strings” piercing ℐ \mathscr{I} . Using these improvements we are able to match the holographic Noether charges to the Wald Hamiltonians of the covariant phase space and derive the first law of black hole thermodynamics with the correct “thermodynamic length” terms arising from the strings. We investigate the relationship between the charges imposed by supersymmetry and show that our first law can be consistently applied to various classes of non-supersymmetric solutions for which the cross-sections of the horizon are spindles.

Introduction

The understanding of black holes as thermodynamic objects is one of the key directions in uncovering the quantum nature of gravity. The origin of this field lies in the pioneering work by Bekenstein
Bekenstein:1972tm
;
Bekenstein:1973ur

S_{\text{BH}}=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{4G}.

(1)

\delta\mathcal{M}=T\delta S_{\text{BH}}+\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}\delta J+\Phi_{e}\delta Q_{e},

(2)

S_{\text{BH}}=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa_{\text{sg}}}\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\mathbf{Q},

(3)

(d-2)

;
Wald:1993nt
;

3.3
Boundary Cotton tensor

The boundary conformal class

determines (in part) the asymptotic classification of the spacetime. In particular, we will follow

Restricting consideration to the case of
m\alpha\neq 0

3

More importantly for our current analysis is the failure of the boundary to be conformally flat.
The conformal invariant we will use is the Cotton tensor of

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}C^{\phantom{(0)}t}_{(0)\phantom{t}\varphi}

\displaystyle=3K^{2}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{3/2}\left[\alpha m-2(\Xi-1)x\right]\left[x(2\alpha m-\Xi x+x)-1\right],

(39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}C^{\phantom{(0)}\varphi}_{(0)\phantom{\varphi}t}&=\frac{3\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\left\{\alpha^{2}\left[x^{2}-1\right]\left[-2\alpha mx+(\Xi-1)x^{2}+1\right]+1\right\}}{\kappa^{2}}\\
&\phantom{==}\times(2(1-\Xi)x+\alpha m).\end{split}

(40)

It will be of interest to examine the allowed variations among the parameters

\delta\left(\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}C^{\phantom{(0)}i}_{(0)\phantom{i}j}\right)=0.

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(x^{0}):\quad 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(x^{1}):\quad 0

(43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(x^{2}):\quad 0

3.4
Variational problem

We analyse the variational problem for the family of spacetimes (
6
), an issue which will be crucial in determining the class of variations which are allowed to enter into the first law of accelerating black hole thermodynamics. We begin by noting that the bulk action (
5
) must first be supplemented by a boundary action consisting of the Gibbons-Hawking-York boundary term as well as the usual holographic counterterms
Balasubramanian:1999re
;
deHaro:2000vlm
;
Skenderis:2000in
;
Skenderis:2002wp
. We begin by presenting this action at a regulated boundary
z=\epsilon>0

S_{\text{bdy}}=S_{\text{GHY}}+S_{\text{ct}}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int_{z=\epsilon}d^{3}x\,\sqrt{-h}(2\mathcal{K}-4-\mathcal{R}),

(51)

where
h_{ij}
is the induced metric on the hypersurface
z=\epsilon
,
\mathcal{K}
is its trace extrinsic curvature when embedded in the bulk spacetime and
\mathcal{R}
is the Ricci scalar of
h
. The total action is thus
4
4

4

One may conjecture that the presence of the cosmic strings should also alter the action. However, this has already been shown not to be the case
Gregory:2001dn
as the delta function arising from the extrinsic curvature precisely cancels the string contribution.

S=S_{\text{bulk}}+S_{\text{bdy}}

(52)

and we define the renormalised action as

S_{\text{ren}}=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}S.

(53)

The variational problem is well-posed when variations of the renormalised action vanish iff the equations of motion are satisfied. The general formula for the variation of the renormalised on-shell action is
Papadimitriou:2005ii

\delta S_{\text{ren}}\approx\int_{\mathscr{I}}d^{3}x\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}\,\left(\frac{1}{2}T^{ij}\delta g^{(0)}_{ij}+j^{i}\delta A^{(0)}_{i}\right)

(54)

and thus the variational problem is well-posed when the right hand term above vanishes. The typical way of ensuring this is to select Dirichlet boundary conditions
Papadimitriou:2005ii
, i.e. to demand that the variations satisfy

\delta g^{(0)}_{ij}\propto g^{(0)}_{ij},\qquad\delta A^{(0)}_{i}=0,

(55)

which clearly makes the variational problem well-posed due to the tracelessness condition (
36
). We will now show explicitly that these boundary conditions cannot be satisfied non-trivially for the solutions (
6
) when we treat the parameters
m,e,g,\alpha,K,\kappa
as changing under variation.

We start with the metric boundary condition in (
55
) which we have already built up to in the analysis of the Cotton tensor (
38
) in the previous subsection. Due to the fact that
\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}C^{\phantom{(0)}i}_{(0)\phantom{i}j}
is invariant under local conformal transformations and varies under changes in the conformal class, we have the following relationship amongst the variations:

\delta g^{(0)}_{ij}\propto g^{(0)}_{ij}\iff\delta\left(\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}C^{\phantom{(0)}i}_{(0)\phantom{i}j}\right)=0,

(56)

where we have already determined that space of variations in the right hand set above are those given in equations (
49
) and (
50
).

Moving on to the gauge field, we start by using (
25
) to write the constraint of
Papadimitriou:2005ii
as

0=\delta\mathbf{A}_{(0)}=\delta\left(-\cos\theta\left[\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}edt+gKd\varphi\right]\right),

(57)

resulting in

\delta e=\delta g=0,

(58)

which we note is a much stronger constraint than the one imposed for the solutions with
\alpha=g=0
(such as those considered in
Papadimitriou:2005ii
), where the boundary condition (
57
) is satisfied trivially.
This analysis demonstrates that if one wants to apply Dirichlet boundary conditions (
55
) then the only allowed perturbation is the trivial one

\delta m=\delta\alpha=\delta K=\delta e=\delta g=\delta\kappa=0.

(59)

At first sight this appears to be a troubling result. It means that studying the black hole first law for the class of accelerating solutions (
6
) via the approach of
Papadimitriou:2005ii
is not possible. Indeed, this tension was remarked upon in
Cassani:2021dwa
due to the variations in that work changing the boundary conformal class
[g_{(0)}]
. It is clear that if we wish to apply techniques along the line of
Papadimitriou:2005ii
then we need to consider more general boundary conditions than the Dirichlet conditions (
55

In order to resolve these tensions, one may take inspiration from
Anabalon:2018qfv
, where the requirement of a well-posed variational problem was used to fix
\kappa
in terms of the other parameters. We follow
Compere:2008us
in considering the most general solutions to the variational problem i.e. we look to solve

\delta S_{\text{ren}}\approx\int_{\mathscr{I}}d^{3}x\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}\,\left(\frac{1}{2}T^{ij}\delta g^{(0)}_{ij}+j^{i}\delta A^{(0)}_{i}\right)=0,

(60)

without applying any specific boundary conditions upon the metric and gauge field. The analysis of the
U(1)
term goes through very straightforwardly due to the gauge choice (
7

25
) and (
29
), we obtain

\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}j^{i}\delta A^{(0)}_{i}=-\frac{\sin 2\theta}{8\pi G}\frac{K}{\kappa}\left[e\kappa\delta\left(\frac{\alpha e}{\kappa}\right)+g\alpha\delta(gK)\right],

(61)

which vanishes under the integration performed in (
60

\delta S_{\text{ren}}\approx\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathscr{I}}d^{3}x\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}\,T^{ij}\delta g^{(0)}_{ij}=0.

(62)

Using (

(63)

3.5
Comment on the “master formula”

We conclude this section with some comments concerning our master formula (
63
) and a comparison to previous literature
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
;
Barrientos:2022bzm
. The first point we note about (
63
) is that it is not an integrable equation on the phase space i.e. one cannot directly solve for
\kappa
as a function of the other parameters. In order to examine the nature of the non-integrability, we find it useful to first manipulate equation (
63
) into the following form

\delta\log\kappa-\frac{1}{2}\delta\log\left(\Xi(1-\alpha^{2}\Xi)\right)=\frac{1-2\alpha^{2}\Xi}{2\alpha^{2}\Xi-2}\delta\log(\Xi K),

(64)

where we note that the terms on the left hand side are exact 1-forms on the phase space and thus the integrability is spoiled entirely by the term on the right hand side. In order to recover integrability, we immediately see that one possibility is to consider a restricted set of variations which satisfy

\delta(\Xi K)=0,

(65)

a constraint which can be interpreted as fixing the overall deficit in the spacetime (
21
). Upon making this choice, one can then solve (
64
) for
\kappa
directly in order to find

\kappa=\sqrt{\Xi(1-\alpha^{2}\Xi)},

(66)

is agreement with the
\kappa
found in previous work
Appels:2016uha
;
Appels:2017xoe
;
Gregory:2017ogk
;
Anabalon:2018ydc
;
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
. This also agrees with the motivation of
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
;
Barrientos:2022bzm
, where the requirement of a well-posed variational problem (in the setting of fixed cosmic string tensions) was used to derive
\kappa
. It is interesting to note that (
65
) is actually a smaller set of constraints than those postulated in
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
;
Barrientos:2022bzm
, where both tensions
\mu_{\pm}
were chosen to be fixed, rather than just their sum.

Returning to equation (
64
), we note that although the non-integrability may appear at first to be a disturbing feature, there is no a priori reason for (
64
) to be an integrable equation as the integrand in (
60
) is not a closed form on the phase space. Schematically speaking, we start with a generic six parameter phase space
\{m,e,g,\alpha,K,\kappa\}
and the imposition of well-posedness generically leads to a differential equation containing variations of a subset of these parameters. This has the effect of reducing the number of degrees of freedom to five, without providing an explicit relation due to the non-integrability.

In order to recover integrability one must necessarily consider a restricted set of variations such as those given in equation (
65
), resulting in a restricted phase space. Working more generally, we note that the non-integrable master formula (
64
) illustrates that
\kappa
as given in (
66
) is the correct choice when one fixes the overall deficit. However, if one chooses
\kappa
as given in (
66
) but does not impose (
65
) then the variational problem is generically
ill-posed
. The general philosophy (and technical tool) is simply that one needs to consider a space of variations amongst the parameters for which the variational problem (
60
) is well-posed. This new approach to the accelerating solutions allows us to study thermodynamics for (many) alternative choices of
\kappa
. In proceeding, we will work completely generically, i.e. we will not fix
\kappa
to the form of (
66
) but rather we will merely require that the master constraint equation (
63
) holds. We will now construct the conserved charges and derive the most general form of the first law for which the variations are well-posed and the cosmic string tensions
\mu_{\pm}
are independent thermodynamic variables. In doing this, we will regularly highlight the importance of well-posedness.

4.1
Covariant phase space

We begin by considering a variation of the Lagrangian
d
-form
\mathbf{L}[\psi]
, where
\psi
denotes the dynamical fields of the theory. A variation of
\mathbf{L}
takes the generic form

\delta\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{E}[\psi]\delta\psi+d\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\delta\psi],

(67)

where
\mathbf{E}
is the equation of motion
d
-form (
\mathbf{E}\approx 0
) and
\bm{\Theta}
is a
(d-1)
-spacetime form called the
symplectic potential
.
5
5

5

Strictly speaking, this object is a
pre
-symplectic potential as there is no guarantee that it will give rise to an invertible symplectic form on phase space. We will ignore these subtleties as they will play no role in our analysis.
We note that
\bm{\Theta}
is also a 1-form on the phase space.

Using
\bm{\Theta}
, one can construct the
symplectic current

\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta_{1}\psi,\delta_{2}\psi]=\delta_{1}\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\delta_{2}\psi]-\delta_{2}\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\delta_{1}\psi],

(68)

a
(d-1)
-form on spacetime and a 2-form on phase space. Integrating the symplectic current over a partial Cauchy slice
C
defines the
symplectic form

\Omega_{C}(\psi;\delta_{1}\psi,\delta_{2}\psi)=\int_{C}\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta_{1}\psi,\delta_{2}\psi],

(69)

a spacetime scalar and a phase space 2-form.

In order to construct the charges, we will often be interested in the case when one of the variations is generated by a Killing vector field
\xi
(
\delta_{\xi}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
) or a
U(1)
gauge transformation
f
. For these cases we are able to define the
Wald Hamiltonians
corresponding to these transformations via

\delta H_{\xi}=\Omega_{C}(\psi;\delta\psi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi)=\int_{C}\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta\psi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi]

(70)

and

\delta H_{f}=\Omega_{C}(\psi;\delta\psi,\delta_{f}\psi)=\int_{C}\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta\psi,\delta_{f}\psi].

(71)

It remains to be seen that these Wald Hamiltonians are boundary quantities. We show this first for
H_{\xi}
by defining the Noether current
(d-1)
-form

\mathbf{J}[\xi]=\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi]-i_{\xi}\mathbf{L},

(72)

an object which is locally exact on-shell i.e. when
\mathbf{E}=0
, we have

\mathbf{J}[\xi]=d\mathbf{Q}[\xi],

(73)

where
\mathbf{Q}
is the
Noether charge

(d-2)
-form. Restricting to the case when both the equations of motion
\mathbf{E}=0
and the linearised equations of motion
\delta\mathbf{E}=0
are satisfied, one is able to show that
Iyer:1994ys
;
Papadimitriou:2005ii

\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta\psi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi]=d\left(\delta\mathbf{Q}[\xi]-i_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\delta\psi]\right)

(74)

and thus

\delta H_{\xi}=\Omega_{C}(\psi;\delta\psi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi)=\int_{C}\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta\psi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi]=\int_{\partial C_{\infty}}\delta\mathbf{Q}[\xi]-i_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\delta\psi],

(75)

where
\partial C_{\infty}
is the intersection of
C
with the conformal boundary.

For the
U(1)
transformation, the analysis is even simpler in that the Noether current takes the form

\mathbf{J}[f]=\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\delta_{f}\psi]\approx d\mathbf{Q}[f].

(76)

By gauge invariance of the symplectic potential we have

\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta\psi,\delta_{f}\psi]=\delta\bm{\Theta}[\psi;\delta_{f}\psi]\approx d\delta\mathbf{Q}[f],

(77)

and thus the Wald Hamiltonian is

\delta H_{f}=\Omega_{C}(\psi;\delta\psi,\delta_{f}\psi)=\int_{C}\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta\psi,\delta_{f}\psi]=\int_{\partial C_{\infty}}\delta\mathbf{Q}[f].

(78)

This can be immediately integrated on phase space to give

H_{f}=\int_{\partial C_{\infty}}\mathbf{Q}[f].

(79)

4.2
Corner improvement

With the general terminology of the covariant phase space now introduced, we now study Einstein-Maxwell theory (

\psi=\{g_{\mu\nu},A_{\rho}\}.

We are almost at the stage of being able to compute the conserved quantities which will appear in the first law. There is one final subtlety for the accelerating solutions (

The form of this corner term can be discerned from the holographic counterterms (

\delta\mathbf{L}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij}]=\mathbf{E}_{(3)}^{kl}[h_{ij}]\delta h_{kl}+d\bm{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\delta h_{ij}],

(84)

where
\mathbf{E}_{(3)}^{ij}

The adjustments to
\bm{\Theta}

\mathbf{Q}_{\text{ren}}=\mathbf{Q}+\bm{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\mathcal{L}_{\xi}h_{ij}]

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta H^{\text{ren}}_{\xi}&=\delta H_{\xi}+\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\delta\bm{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\mathcal{L}_{\xi}h_{ij}]-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\delta h_{ij}]+di_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\delta h_{ij}]\\

(86)

\delta H^{\text{ren}}_{\xi}=\delta H_{\xi}+\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\infty}}i_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\delta h_{ij}],

(88)

4.3
Mass

The first charge we compute is the mass charge

We will be explicit in constructing the mass charge as it will provide an important illustration of the corner improvement present in our formula (

It was shown in

4.4
Electric charge

The next charge we will need to define is the electric charge, which can be done in a straightforward manner using (

in computing the

\mathbf{Q}[f]=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}f*\mathbf{F}

where we assume w.l.o.g that

as we use a volume element with the opposite sign to that work. Our result matches equation (3.24) of

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}

\displaystyle=gK\sin\theta d\theta\wedge d\varphi-\frac{e}{\kappa r^{2}}dt\wedge dr,

\displaystyle*\mathbf{F}

Looking ahead to the first law, we also note that the electric charge can be defined as an integral over a cross-section of the horizon
\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
(as opposed to a cross-section of the conformal boundary
\Sigma_{\infty}

\mathbf{E}_{\text{M}}=d*\mathbf{F}

0\approx\int_{C}d*\mathbf{F}=\int_{\partial C}*\mathbf{F}=\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}*\mathbf{F}-\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}*\mathbf{F}+\int_{\mathcal{S}_{-}}*\mathbf{F}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{+}}*\mathbf{F},

and thus the string terms do not contribute. This allows us to write the electric charge as an integral over the horizon

Q_{e}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}*\mathbf{F}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}*\mathbf{F},

(113)

a fact which will be crucial in our derivation of the first law.

4.5
Magnetic charge

Our final conserved charge is the magnetic charge
Q_{m}
, an object which seems somewhat difficult to define using the covariant phase space approach: we have already assigned conserved charges to both the time translation Killing vector
\partial_{t}
and the constant
U(1)
gauge transformation so it seems like there is nothing left to produce additional charges! (The axial Killing field
\partial_{\varphi}
is associated to angular momentum, which vanishes trivially in the case we consider.) As it turns out, one can define the magnetic charge using the covariant phase space formalism by adding a
topological term
to the action, the details of which we provide in appendix
A
. This term will play no role in the first law, so we leave the detailed discussion of this term as an aside.

The magnetic charge is given by

Q_{m}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\mathbf{F}=\frac{gK}{G},

(114)

where we used (
108
) to evaluate the charge explicitly. Following a similar line of logic to the electric charge, the magnetic charge can also be written as an integral over the bifurcation surface by using the Bianchi identity for the field strength tensor

0=\int_{C}d\mathbf{F}=\int_{\partial C}\mathbf{F}=\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\mathbf{F}-\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\mathbf{F}+\int_{\mathcal{S}_{-}}\mathbf{F}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{+}}\mathbf{F}

(115)

and in much the same manner as the electric argument, the cosmic string terms do not contribute, leaving

Q_{m}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\mathbf{F}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\mathbf{F}.

(116)

5
First law

With all of the charges defined, we are almost ready to move on to the derivation of the first law. First we have to establish the definitions of the other important quantities in the law which are not explicit conserved charges. The first is the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, given by the usual formula in terms of the horizon area
\mathcal{A}

S_{\text{BH}}=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{4G}=\frac{1}{4G}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\left.\sqrt{g_{\theta\theta}g_{\varphi\varphi}}\right|_{r=r_{+}}=\frac{K\pi r_{+}^{2}}{G(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})}.

(117)

The second quantity is the black hole temperature
T
. We recall that this is defined as
T=\beta^{-1}=\frac{\kappa_{\text{sg}}}{2\pi}
, where the surface gravity
\kappa_{\text{sg}}
is constructed from the horizon generator
\xi=\partial_{t}
via
\kappa_{\text{sg}}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}
. Utilising these definitions, we find the temperature of the black hole to be

T=\frac{Q^{\prime}(r_{+})}{4\kappa\pi r_{+}^{2}}.

(118)

We note consistency with
Wald:1993nt
;
Iyer:1994ys
in that these objects can be constructed in terms of the gravitational part of the Noether charge form (
96
)

\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{EH}}[\xi]=TS_{\text{BH}},

(119)

a fact which will reappear in our derivation of the first law.

The next quantities we need to define are the potentials dual to the electric and magnetic charges respectively
Papadimitriou:2005ii
;
Cassani:2021dwa
. In the electric case we have the electrostatic potential defined via

\Phi_{e}\equiv\Phi_{\infty}-\Phi_{H}=-\Phi_{H}=-\left.i_{\xi}\mathbf{A}\right|_{r=r_{+}},

(120)

where we used
\Phi_{\infty}=0
which is a result of the gauge choice (
25
) as there are no
\theta
-independent terms in
i_{\xi}\mathbf{A}_{(0)}

Cassani:2021dwa
. Now applying equation (
7
) allows us to read off the potential as

\Phi_{e}=\frac{e}{\kappa r_{+}}.

(121)

The magnetic potential is slightly more subtle in that in

it was simply introduced as the electric-magnetic dual of

by replacing
e\rightarrow g

Q_{m}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}*\mathbf{G},

(122)

where
\mathbf{G}=-*\mathbf{F}
. In order to compute the magnetic potential, we first compute the dual gauge field

that sources
\mathbf{G}

. Using (

\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=-\frac{g}{r\kappa}dt+eK\cos\theta d\varphi,

where we note that

\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{(0)}=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\tilde{A}_{i}|_{z=\epsilon}dx^{i}=\cos\theta\left(-\frac{g\alpha}{\kappa}dt+eKd\varphi\right)

and we have again chosen a gauge s.t.
\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\tilde{A}^{(0)}_{i}=0

\Phi_{m}=\tilde{\Phi}_{\infty}-\tilde{\Phi}_{H}=-\tilde{\Phi}_{H}=-\left.i_{\xi}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\right|_{r=r_{+}}=\frac{g}{\kappa r_{+}},

which we see can be obtained from

under replacement of

5.1
Geometric derivation

With all of the charges
(\mathcal{M},\,Q_{e},\,Q_{m})
and auxiliary quantities
(S_{\text{BH}},\,T,\,\Phi_{e},\,\Phi_{m})
defined, we are finally ready to derive the first law using the covariant phase space. In doing this, we follow
Wald:1993nt
;
Iyer:1994ys
by considering a spacelike slice of spacetime
C
which stretches from a cross-section of the black hole horizon
\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
(which can be taken without loss of generality to be the bifurcation surface
Jacobson:1993vj
) out to a cross-section of conformal infinity
\Sigma_{\infty}
. The novel aspect of this surface in case of the accelerating solution is that the boundary of
C
does not merely consist of the two aforementioned surfaces but also includes the two cosmic strings responsible for the black hole acceleration! Technically speaking, we have

\partial C=\Sigma_{\infty}-(\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}-\mathcal{S}_{-}+\mathcal{S}_{+}),

(126)

where the signs are due to the induced orientations on each surface. A similar boundary structure has already been considered in the study of accelerating black hole thermodynamics in asymptotically (locally) flat spacetime
Ball:2020vzo
;
Ball:2021xwt
and we will see that this will also play a crucial role in the thermodynamics of the AlAdS case.

The derivation of the first law follows the same logic as
Wald:1993nt
, namely in that we begin with an integral of the symplectic current over the surface
C

0=\int_{C}\bm{\omega}[\psi;\delta\psi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi]=\int_{\partial C}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\bm{k}_{\xi}-\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}_{\xi}+\int_{\mathcal{S}_{-}}\bm{k}_{\xi}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{+}}\bm{k}_{\xi},

(127)

where
\xi
is the horizon generator given in equation (
91
) and the integral vanishes by virtue of
\xi
being a Killing vector. In the series of equalities on the right hand side above we have introduced

\bm{k}_{\xi}=\delta\mathbf{Q}[\xi]-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}[\psi;\delta\psi]+di_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\delta h_{ij}],

(128)

which can be seen from equation (
74
) and we have chosen to add the additional
d
-exact term on the right hand side. We have already seen that the inclusion of this exact term gives the correct definition of the charges (
88
) and it will also be an elegant choice in explaining the role of each boundary contribution in the first law. Note however that this term is not necessary as the first law is invariant under transformations of the form
\bm{k}_{\xi}\rightarrow\bm{k}_{\xi}+d\mathbf{B}
. This can be seen directly from (
127
) where the orientations mean that all corner contributions cancel. We now will provide the analysis of each term in (
127
) in order to derive the first law.

5.1.1
Conformal boundary term

The first term we will analyse is the contribution at the conformal boundary, namely

\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\delta\mathbf{Q}[\xi]-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}[\psi;\delta\psi]+di_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}[h_{ij};\delta h_{ij}].

(129)

Upon using equations (
88
) and (
104
) we immediately see

\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\delta H_{\xi}^{\text{ren}}=\delta\mathcal{M},

(130)

so the term at the conformal boundary contributes precisely the variation of the mass charge.

5.1.2
Horizon term

The horizon term is

\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\delta\mathbf{Q}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}

(131)

and to analyse this we will use the split of the symplectic quantities into their “Einstein-Hilbert” and “Maxwell” components as in (
95
).

The gravitational piece contributes

\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}^{\text{EH}}_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\text{EH}}=\delta(TS_{\text{BH}}),

(132)

where the fact that
\xi
vanishes at the bifurcation surface removes the
i_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}_{\text{EH}}
term and then the Wald entropy formula (
119
) ensures the right equality. This term simplifies further in that we follow
Wald:1993nt
;
Papadimitriou:2005ii
in choosing perturbations such that we match the horizons of the perturbed and unperturbed solutions, as well as the unit surface gravity generators of the horizons
\tilde{\xi}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\text{sg}}}\xi
. We immediately have
\delta\kappa_{\text{sg}}=0
(as
\delta\xi=0
), so this in turn leads to
\delta T=0
. This allows us to write the final form of the horizon term as

\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}^{\text{EH}}_{\xi}=T\delta S_{\text{BH}},

(133)

precisely as one would find for AlAdS black holes without cosmic string insertion
Papadimitriou:2005ii
.

The electromagnetic piece is

\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}^{\text{M}}_{\xi}=\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\text{M}}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{M}},

(134)

where we note that the
i_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}_{\text{M}}
can no longer be ignored. We observe from (
120
) that
i_{\xi}\mathbf{A}|_{r=r_{+}}
is non-zero and thus one needs to treat the contractions of the Maxwell symplectic potential and the horizon generator more carefully. We also note that the gauge field (
7

Ferrero:2020twa
;
Cassani:2021dwa
, a statement which is generically true for spacetimes with magnetic charge. Analysing this term more carefully we find

\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\text{M}}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{M}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}\left[i_{\xi}\mathbf{A}\left(\delta*\mathbf{F}\right)+\delta\mathbf{A}\wedge i_{\xi}*\mathbf{F}\right]

(135)

and working on-shell so that
d*\mathbf{F}\approx 0
and recalling that
\delta_{\xi}*\mathbf{F}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}*\mathbf{F}=0
, we have

0=(i_{\xi}d+di_{\xi})*\mathbf{F}\approx di_{\xi}*\mathbf{F}\implies i_{\xi}*\mathbf{F}\approx d\mathbf{X}\qquad\text{(locally)}.

(136)

In order to solve for
\mathbf{X}
, we recall the dual field strength
d\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{G}=-*\mathbf{F}
and using
\delta_{\xi}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=0
we can write the equation above as

i_{\xi}*\mathbf{F}=-i_{\xi}d\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=di_{\xi}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\approx d\mathbf{X}\implies i_{\xi}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{X}.

(137)

We apply this to equation (
135
) and use “by parts” type manipulations in order to obtain

\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\text{M}}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{M}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}\left[i_{\xi}\mathbf{A}\left(\delta*\mathbf{F}\right)+i_{\xi}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\left(\delta\mathbf{F}\right)-d\left(\delta\mathbf{A}i_{\xi}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\right)\right],

(138)

which we note is consistent with previous formulae derived in
Lu:2013ura
and utilised in
Ma:2022nwq
. When integrated over the bifurcation surface (
138
) gives

\int_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}^{\text{M}}_{\xi}=\Phi_{e}\delta Q_{e}+\Phi_{m}\delta Q_{m},

(139)

where we used equations (
113
), (
116
), (
120
), (
125
) and the fact that the corner integrals over the poles of the bifurcation surface cancel one another out. We note that due to this careful analysis, we have
not
been forced to fix the electric and magnetic potentials between the perturbed and unperturbed solutions i.e. (
139
) will still hold when
\delta\Phi_{e}\neq 0
and
\delta\Phi_{m}\neq 0
. This is an advancement upon
Papadimitriou:2005ii
, where the
i_{\xi}\bm{\Theta}
term was not considered carefully enough at the horizon and they were forced to fix the value of the electric potential
\delta\Phi_{e}=0
.

5.1.3
Cosmic string terms

The final contributions to the first law are those which are special to accelerating solutions, namely the thermodynamic length and tension terms which arise from the presence of the cosmic strings. We begin by recalling that the strings are located at
\theta_{-}=0
and
\theta_{+}=\pi
respectively and thus

\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{r_{+}}^{\frac{1}{\epsilon\mp\alpha}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left.k_{r\varphi}\right|_{\theta_{\pm}}\,drd\varphi.

(140)

Using the general formula for the Maxwell contribution (
138
) we see that this term contributes nothing to the integral above except for the

-exact term which will not contribute to the first law. Thus we can treat the cosmic string terms as purely gravitational

\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\text{EH}}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{EH}}+di_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}.

(141)

Explicit computation using (
96

\mathbf{Q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}}{{=}}0
and thus we have

\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{EH}}+di_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}=-\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{EH}}+\int_{S^{1}_{\pm}}i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{ct}},

(142)

where we have used Stokes’ theorem in order to separate the integral into bare and counterterm pieces just as we did for the mass charge. We will present these results of these terms one by one, illustrating again the elegance of the corner term. The bare contribution is

\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}\bm{k}^{\text{bare}}_{\xi}=-\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{EH}}=\mp\lambda^{\text{bare}}_{\pm}\delta\mu_{\pm},

(143)

where

\lambda^{\text{bare}}_{\pm}=-\frac{1}{\kappa\epsilon}+\frac{r_{+}}{\kappa(1\pm\alpha r_{+})}

(144)

are the

and
\mu_{\pm}
are the string tensions defined in (

\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

The counterterm contribution is

\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}\bm{k}^{\text{ct}}_{\xi}=\int_{S^{1}_{\pm}}i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{ct}}=\mp\frac{1}{\kappa}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\alpha(2\alpha m\pm\Xi)\right]\delta\mu_{\pm}

(145)

and thus combining this term appropriately with (

\int_{\mathcal{S_{\pm}}}\bm{k}_{\xi}=\mp\lambda_{\pm}\delta\mu_{\pm},

(146)

where the

are defined as

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{r_{+}}{\kappa(1\pm\alpha r_{+})}-\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}(2\alpha m\pm\Xi)=\frac{r_{+}}{\kappa(1\pm\alpha r_{+})}\mp\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}P_{\pm}.

(147)

5.2
Final statement of the law

Combining equations (

), (

), (

T\delta S_{\text{BH}}=\delta\mathcal{M}-\Phi_{e}\delta Q_{e}-\Phi_{m}\delta Q_{m}+\lambda_{-}\delta\mu_{-}+\lambda_{+}\delta\mu_{+},

where, to recap, the relevant quantities are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{Km(1-\alpha^{2}\Xi)}{\kappa G},

(149)

\displaystyle S_{\text{BH}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{4G}=\frac{K\pi r_{+}^{2}}{G(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})},\qquad

\displaystyle T=\frac{Q^{\prime}(r_{+})}{4\kappa\pi r_{+}^{2}},

\displaystyle Q_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{eK}{G},\qquad

\displaystyle\Phi_{e}=\frac{e}{\kappa r_{+}},

It is important to discuss some of the key differences with the law presented in

Spindles and supersymmetry

In this section we will discuss various relations between the conserved charges

6.1
Overview

We have so far kept the choice of the deficit parameter

A crucial property for the uplift into regular solutions in supergravity is the property that the four dimensional solutions (

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle g^{2}

Charge loci

It is natural to ask what algebraic constraints are placed upon the conserved charges

6.2.1
Supersymmetric locus

The first case of interest is to apply the supersymmetry constraints (

Supersymmetric and extremal locus

An immediate application of the supersymmetry locus (

6.2.3

We note that (

6.3
Applications of the first law

We would like to apply our first law of slowly accelerating black hole thermodynamics (
148
) to the supersymmetric spindle solutions, although this is made difficult due to the global nature of such solutions. As mentioned, the supersymmetric and extremal solutions are not slowly accelerating and are thus beyond the scope of our first law. The next possible case of interest is the supersymmetric and non-extremal solutions (i.e. those where both (

6.3.1
Fixed deficit

The first case we consider is to fix the overall deficit (

Applying this relation to the first law (

T\delta S_{\text{BH}}=\delta\mathcal{M}-\Phi_{e}\delta Q_{e}-\Phi_{m}\delta Q_{m}+(\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-})\delta\mu_{+},

6.3.2
Fixed spindle

In order to write down the first law for a spindle, we start by applying equations (
159

which equivalently follows from (

6.3.3
Close-to-supersymmetric spindle

The next case is to apply some supersymmetry condition on top of (

Ferrero:2020twa
in only applying

g=\alpha m,

(166)

T\delta S_{\text{BH}}=\delta\mathcal{M}-\Phi_{e}\delta Q_{e}.

We note that this is equivalent to that written down in

6.3.4
Close-to-supersymmetric and close-to-extremal spindle

The final case of interest is the

It will be of future interest to study more carefully the effect of rotation: as well as adding an additional parameter, rotation also allows for slowly accelerating supersymmetric and extremal solutions

7
Conclusions and outlook

In this work we have extended the techniques of
Papadimitriou:2005ii
in describing the charges and thermodynamics of AlAdS solutions to encompass
accelerating
solutions in
d=4
spacetime dimensions. This effort relied on two important developments, firstly the relaxation of Dirichlet boundary conditions to the more general demand that the variational problem is well-posed (
60
). Secondly, one needs to supplement the definition of the Wald Hamiltonians by a suitable
corner improvement
due the topology of
\mathscr{I}
: a hypersurface which is not smooth due to the presence of cosmic strings piercing the poles of the constant time cross-sections. With these improvements in place, we were able to show agreement for the conserved charges between the usual holographic definitions
Papadimitriou:2005ii
;
Hollands:2005wt
and the charges constructed via the covariant phase space
Wald:1993nt
.

The main motivation in developing these techniques was to examine the first law of thermodynamics for these solutions, and in particular to make comparison and contrast with the results of
Appels:2016uha
;
Appels:2017xoe
;
Gregory:2017ogk
;
Anabalon:2018ydc
;
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
. Of crucial importance in these works was the choice of the time scaling parameter
\kappa
of which we have now shed more light on: we have shown that one can use the techniques developed in this paper to derive an entirely consistent first law of thermodynamics with
\kappa
as long as one imposes the constraint (
63
). We emphasise that the key idea in deriving this constraint is the requirement of a well-posed variational problem (
60
), an idea which was previously considered for the restricted case of fixed string tensions in
Anabalon:2018qfv
. However, such a requirement was not in place for the variations which change the overall deficit of the accelerating solution considered in
Appels:2016uha
;
Appels:2017xoe
;
Gregory:2017ogk
;
Anabalon:2018ydc
;
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
and this results in a mismatch between the holographic mass and the Wald Hamiltonian associated with the time translation. This mismatch results in an ambiguous form of the first law depending upon the “mass” that appears in the law, manifested explicitly in finding different values for the thermodynamic lengths
\lambda_{\pm}
for the holographic and Wald masses. In contrast, our first law (
149
) is valid for both the holographic and Wald masses (because they are equivalent) and thus gives the true expression for the thermodynamic lengths
\lambda_{\pm}
. We note that when one fixes the overall deficit angle in the spacetime and one chooses the value of
\kappa
as in (
66
) (as considered in
Appels:2016uha
;
Appels:2017xoe
;
Gregory:2017ogk
;
Anabalon:2018ydc
;
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
), the well-posedness constraint (
60
)
is
solved. This gives a concrete reason for the value of
\kappa
used in
Cassani:2021dwa
(the authors noted at the time that
\kappa
“gives the first law by trial and error”).

One peculiar feature that arises in our approach is that unlike
Appels:2016uha
;
Appels:2017xoe
;
Gregory:2017ogk
;
Anabalon:2018ydc
;
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Cassani:2021dwa
, the master equation (
63
) is not integrated on phase space (due to the non-integrability) and thus does not provide a closed form expression for
\kappa
in terms of the other parameters. Equation (
63
) can be understood as a constraint on variations of
\kappa
and is crucial in the derivation of the first law (
148
). As a conseqeunce of (
63
), the law effectively depends only on the variations of the (physically well-understood) parameters
m,e,g,\alpha,K
. We note however, due to their dependence on
\kappa
, one does not generically have (and cannot have due to non-integrability) closed expressions for the thermodynamic quantities
\mathcal{M},T,\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m},\lambda_{\pm}
(defined in (
149
)) in terms of
m,e,g,\alpha,K
.

This work thus provides a platform to study the thermodynamics of spindle solutions from the covariant phase space, but there are still many future directions which need to be pursued for a more complete understanding. The first issue is that of rotation, in particular in that one needs to first establish the true mass charge for accelerating, rotating solutions. The difficulty in doing this lies in the fact that the true mass charge is associated to the timelike Killing vector of the solution when the boundary is in a
non-rotating frame
, an issue which was resolved for AdS-Kerr-Newman black holes in
Gibbons:2004ai
;
Papadimitriou:2005ii
but is yet to be entirely settled for the accelerating solutions. In
Anabalon:2018qfv
;
Ferrero:2020twa
;
Cassani:2021dwa
, the mass charge is associated with the timelike Killing vector in a
rotating
frame, and only matches the true mass along the poles of the spindle (
\theta=\theta_{\pm}
). We believe that a more careful analysis of the mass charge must first be carried out, with the
\Lambda
-BMS gauge fixing of
Compere:2019bua
providing a possible algorithm in order to find the correct non-rotating frame. It would be interesting to observe how this would affect the first law, and would also open the door to apply the law to supersymmetric and extremal spindle solutions, where the rotation can be tuned so as to ensure slow acceleration
Cassani:2021dwa
.

In addition to rotation, it would also be interesting to include the other parameters of the Plebanski-Demiański family as spelled out in
Podolsky:2022xxd
. One could for example promote to thermodynamic objects the cosmological constant
\ell

Caldarelli:1999xj

Kastor:2009wy

Kubiznak:2015bya
;
Kubiznak:2016qmn
or Newton constant

Cong:2021fnf
;
Visser:2021eqk
, the former of which may be understood in terms of a braneworld interpretation as recently discussed in
Frassino:2022zaz
. Perhaps more interesting would be to include a non-zero NUT parameter
N
9

9

This must be performed together with non-zero “Kerr-like” rotation, as otherwise the accelerating, NUT-charged black holes fall outside the Plebanski-Demiański family
Podolsky:2020xkf
;

and we note that the conical singularity structure we dealt with in this work via the covariant phase space is remarkably similar to that of the Taub-NUT-AdS as discussed in

, with the difference being that the NUT charge introduces

rather than cosmic strings along the poles of the constant

As an example of this: accelerating, NUT charged black holes have recently been found in the theory of Einstein gravity conformally coupled to a scalar field (for
\Lambda=0

. If such solutions are found in the

Another important future direction would be to move away from the slowly accelerating case and apply these techniques to AlAdS solutions with an acceleration horizon. In the asymptotically (locally) flat setting, the lack of a cosmological constant forces the inclusion of an acceleration horizon and covariant phase space techniques have been used
Ball:2020vzo
;

to derive a first law using the background subtraction of the massless cosmic string spacetime. Such techniques should be readily applicable to the AlAdS case, and in fact should go further as the holographic counterterms will negate the need for background subtraction. Physically speaking, spacetimes containing multiple horizons with different surface gravities makes the assignment of a thermodynamic temperature unclear

and thus this issue would also have to be explored more deeply in the rapidly accelerating case. A direct application of this would be the

Finally, we note that the techniques pioneered here should be applicable to spacetimes whose conformal boundary cross-sections

Appendix A
Magnetic charges from the covariant phase space

In this appendix we show that the magnetic charge (
114
) can be realised as a ture conserved charge in the covariant phase space formalism by adding an additional term to the action (
52
). This term will be purely topological and thus will not contribute to the equations of motion, but will modify the symplectic structure (and thus the charges). We will show that this term does not affect either the variational problem (as studied in Section
3.4
) or the derivation of the first law (Section
5
) and thus the analysis of the main text is entirely sufficient to discuss the first law.

A.1
Topological term

As discussed in Section
4.5
, the standard discussion of Maxwell theory does not derive the magnetic charge as a conserved quantity from the covariant phase space point of view. In order to see how the magnetic charge is constructed on phase space we follow
Godazgar:2022jxm
by considering the action (
52
) supplemented by an additional term

S=S_{\text{bulk}}+S_{\text{bdy}}+S_{\text{top}},

(172)

where the new term is

S_{\text{top}}=-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int_{M}\mathbf{F}\wedge\mathbf{F}.

(173)

This term is often referred to as the “
\theta
-term”
Jackiw:1976pf
;
Callan:1976je
when added to the usual Yang-Mills Lagrangian and is responsible for the breaking of CP-symmetry in the quantum theory when included. As we will treat the theory purely classically we will not worry about such features and we use it purely to illustrate this feature of the magnetic charge.

The contribution from this new term to the equation of motion is trivial (
\mathbf{E}_{\text{top}}\equiv 0
) due to the closed field strength tensor
d\mathbf{F}=0
and thus we see that this term is entirely topological. Despite this, there will be a modification in the symplectic potential. Computing explicitly, we find

\bm{\Theta}_{\text{top}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}\delta\mathbf{A}\wedge\mathbf{F},

(174)

which shows that the effect of this topological term will be identical to that of the Maxwell term (
98
) with a replacement of
*\mathbf{F}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}
. Putting everything together, we see that the total
U(1)
Hamiltonian (
89
) is modified from the form given in (
107
) and now reads

H_{-1}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\left(*\mathbf{F}+\mathbf{F}\right)=Q_{e}+Q_{m},

(175)

i.e. the
U(1)
charge is now the sum of the electric and magnetic charges. We note that even though only the linear combination of
Q_{e}+Q_{m}
appears in the definition of the
U(1)
Hamiltonian, both can be understood as conserved charges independently. Such a result follows immediately from the equation of motion
d*\mathbf{F}\approx 0
and the Bianchi identity
d\mathbf{F}=0
.

As an aside, we note that (as expected) the electric and magnetic charges appear from the covariant phase space point of view as “dual charges”, a topic of recent interest in gravitation
Godazgar:2018qpq
;
Godazgar:2018dvh
;
Godazgar:2020gqd
;
Godazgar:2020kqd
;
Godazgar:2022jxm
. We will not need these more sophisticated notions as our solution of interest (
6
) does not contain non-trivial dual gravitational charges. It would be of interest to generalise the solutions to include spacetimes which do (for example those with non-trivial NUT parameter) although we leave these discussions to future work.

A.2
No contribution to the variational problem

As we have modified the action via the addition of the topological term (
173
), it is natural to ask whether this may affect the variational problem analysis that was performed without the term in Section
3.4
. We note that the variation of the topological term is

\delta S_{\text{top}}\approx\int d^{3}x\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}j_{m}^{i}\delta A_{i}^{(0)},

(176)

where we have introduced the magnetic current vector field
j_{m}
, defined analogously to the electric current (
29
) via

j_{m}^{i}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}n_{\mu}(*F)^{\mu i}\right],

(177)

which can also be used to define the magnetic charge

Q_{m}=\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}d^{2}x\,\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}j_{m}^{t}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\mathbf{F}.

(178)

Computing the values of the magnetic current explicitly for (
7
), we find as the only non-zero components

j_{m}^{t}=\kappa\frac{g}{4\pi G},\qquad j_{m}^{\varphi}=-\frac{\alpha e}{4K\pi G},

(179)

which clearly take constant values. Combining this with
\int_{0}^{\pi}\,d\theta\sqrt{-g_{(0)}}\delta A^{(0)}_{i}=0
immediately tells us that

\delta S_{\text{top}}\approx 0

(180)

and thus the question of well-posedness is independent of whether or not one adds (
173
) to the action. We also note that the presence of the magnetic current will not affect the Ward identities (
34
), (
35
) as these are determined by the equations of motion which are unaffected by the addition of the topological term (
173
).

A.3
No contribution to the first law

Finally, we show that this topological term gives no contribution to the first law. We start by noting that this will be the case if we can show

\bm{k}_{\xi}^{\text{top}}=\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\text{top}}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{top}}=d\mathbf{Z},

(181)

for some 1-form
\mathbf{Z}
, as then all contributions from the corners will cancel in the full statement of the law using the logic as argued in the paragraph below equation (
128
). The topological contribution to the Noether charge is

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{\text{top}}[\xi]=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}(i_{\xi}\mathbf{A})\mathbf{F},

(182)

which when combined with equation (
174
) allows us to write

\bm{k}_{\xi}^{\text{top}}=\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\text{top}}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\text{top}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}\left[(i_{\xi}\mathbf{A})\delta\mathbf{F}+\delta\mathbf{A}\wedge i_{\xi}\mathbf{F}\right].

(183)

Using
d\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{F}
together with
\delta_{\xi}\mathbf{A}=0
, we can now perform elementary manipulations to reach

\bm{k}_{\xi}^{\text{top}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}d\left[(i_{\xi}\mathbf{A})\delta\mathbf{A}\right]\implies\mathbf{Z}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G}(i_{\xi}\mathbf{A})\delta\mathbf{A},

(184)

allowing us to conclude that the topological term does not contribute to the first law.

B.1
Consistency with the “horizon polynomial” method

In equation (
148
) we wrote down the first law of accelerating black hole thermodynamics using the covariant phase space formalism, an elegant approach as this immediately allows one to identify the appearance of the conserved charges and entropy entering the law. In previous works
Appels:2016uha
;

\delta Q(r_{+})=0,

(185)

where the right hand side comes from the fact that the perturbed horizon polynomial vanishes at the perturbed horizon location.

It is natural to ask whether our law is consistent with those of
Appels:2016uha

Appels:2017xoe
;
Anabalon:2018qfv

Anabalon:2018ydc

Cassani:2021dwa

We begin by recalling the definition of the horizon polynomial from (
8
) (with
\ell=1
)

Q(r_{+})=(r_{+}^{2}-2mr_{+}+e^{2}+g^{2})(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})+r_{+}^{4}=0,

(186)

an equation we will both vary and utilise on its own. Computing the variation (
185

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\kappa T\frac{2\pi Kr_{+}}{(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})^{2}}\delta r_{+}-\frac{K}{1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2}}\delta m\\
&\phantom{==}+\frac{K}{r_{+}(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})}\left(e\delta e+g\delta g\right)-K\alpha r_{+}^{2}\frac{r_{+}-2m}{(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})^{2}}\delta\alpha\end{split}

and recalling the definition of the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy (

\delta S_{\text{BH}}=\frac{2\pi Kr_{+}}{(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})^{2}}\delta r_{+}+\frac{\pi r_{+}^{2}}{1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2}}\delta K+\frac{2K\pi r_{+}^{4}\alpha}{(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})^{2}}\delta\alpha,

allowing us to write the law in (

\displaystyle\begin{split}T\delta S_{\text{BH}}&=\frac{1}{\kappa(1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2})}\Bigg[\kappa T\pi r_{+}^{2}\delta K+\frac{Kr_{+}(2\kappa T\pi r_{+}^{3}+r_{+}^{2}-2mr_{+}+e^{2}+g^{2})}{1-\alpha^{2}r_{+}^{2}}\alpha\delta\alpha\\
&\phantom{aaaaaaaaaaaaa}+K\delta m-\frac{K}{r_{+}}\left(e\delta e+g\delta g\right)\Bigg].\end{split}

(189)

\delta\mathcal{M}-\Phi_{e}\delta Q_{e}-\Phi_{m}\delta Q_{m}+\lambda_{-}\delta\mu_{-}+\lambda_{+}\delta\mu_{+}

149

T\delta S_{\text{BH}}=\delta\mathcal{M}-\Phi_{e}\delta Q_{e}-\Phi_{m}\delta Q_{m}+\lambda_{-}\delta\mu_{-}+\lambda_{+}\delta\mu_{+}+C,

(190)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C&=\frac{Km}{\kappa^{2}}(1-\alpha^{2}\Xi)\delta\kappa\\

186

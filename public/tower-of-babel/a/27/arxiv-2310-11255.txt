Generalized Parabolic Frequency on compact manifolds
Shahroud Azami, Abimbola Abolarinwa

Abstract
. In this paper, we first prove monotonicity of a generalized parabolic frequency on weighted closed Riemannian manifolds for some linear heat equation. Secondly, a certain generalized parabolic frequency functional is defined with respect to the solutions of a nonlinear weighted p p -heat-type equation on manifolds, and its monotonicity is proved. Notably, the monotonicities are derived with no assumption on both the curvature and the potential function. Further consequences of these monotonicity formulas from which we can get backward uniqueness are discussed.

1.
Introduction

The following frequency functional was first introduced by Almgren in
[
2
]

(1.1)

\displaystyle N(r)=\frac{r\int_{B(p,r)}|\nabla v|^{2}dx}{\int_{\partial B(p,r)}v^{2}dA}

for a harmonic function
v
on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this quantity
p
is a fixed point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
B(p,r)
is a ball of radius
r>0
centred at
p
, while
\partial B(p,r)
and
dA
are the boundary of
B(p,r)
and the induced
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\partial B(p,r)
, respectively. This functional in (
1.1
) has played a crucial role in the analysis of linear and nonlinear elliptic partial differential equations, starting with its monotonicity properties proved in
[
2
]
. Properties such as local regularity of harmonic functions, unique continuation of elliptic operators, and estimates of nodal sets of solutions, to name but a few have been proved with the aid of (
1.1
). Many of these results have been extended to various settings most especially Riemannian manfold setting. For detail discussion on applications of this functional and its various extensions, and the generalization of these results interested readers can see the following literature
[
6
,
7
,
14
,
4
,
11
]
and the references cited therein.
The counterpart of
N(r)
for the solution to the heat equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called parabolic frequency functional, and was first introduced by Poon
[
17
]
who used it to study the unique continuation of solutions to parabolic equations on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Later Ni
[
16
]
proved monotonicity of such parabolic frequency functional on Riemannian (or Kähler) manifold with nonnegative sectional (or bisectional) curvature and parallel Ricci curvature for holomorphic function using Hamilton’s matrix Harnack estimate.

For a given Riemannain manifold
(M^{n},g)
with a potential function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
there is an associated second order diffusion operator called drifting or Witten Laplacian defined as follows

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}u={\rm div}_{f}(\nabla u)=e^{f}{\rm div}(e^{-f}\nabla u)=\Delta u-\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle.

If the Riemannian volume form on
M
(associated with metric
g
) is denoted by
d\nu
, then equipping
(M,g)
with the weighted volume form
d\mu=e^{-f}d\nu
makes the triple
(M,g,d\mu)
a weighted manifold which is usually called a smooth metric measure space. It is clear from (
1.2
) that drifting Laplacian,
\Delta_{f}
, is a self-adjoint operator with respect to the weighted measure,
d\mu=e^{-f}d\nu
, in the sense that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta_{f}u)wd\mu=-\int_{M}\langle\nabla u,\nabla w\rangle d\mu=\int_{M}u(\Delta_{f}w)d\mu.

Assume that
v:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is smooth and solves the weighted heat equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle v_{t}-\Delta_{f}v=0

on
M
such that
u,u_{t}\in W^{1,2}(M,d\mu)
, Colding and Minicozzi showed in
[
5
]
that the frequency functional
H(t)
defined by

(1.5)

\displaystyle H(t):=-\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}d\mu}{\int_{M}v^{2}d\mu}

is monotonically nonincreasing under (
1.4
) with no restriction on the curvature of
M
, See
[
5
, Theorem 0.6]
. For a solution of (
1.4
) Baldauf and Kim
[
9
]
defined the parabolic frequency involving some correction term as follows

U(t)=\frac{\tau(t)\langle\Delta_{f}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}^{2}}{||u||_{L^{2}(d\mu)}^{2}}e^{-\int\frac{1-k(t)}{\tau(t)}dt},

where
\tau(t)
is the backwards time,
k(t)
be the time-dependent function, and
d\mu
is the
weighted measure. They proved that parabolic frequency
U(t)
for the solution of weighted heat equation is monotone increasing along the Ricci flow with bounded Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature. See also
[
1
,
3
,
12
,
13
]
for similar results on time-dependent Riemannian metrics.

However, we will consider two generalizations of (
1.2
) in this paper. First, for a symmetric positive definite
(1,1)
-tensor
T
on
M
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
or atleast
C^{2}
, we define a generalized diffusion operator

(1.6)

\mathcal{L}u={\rm div}_{f}(T(\nabla u))={\rm div}(T(\nabla u))-\langle\nabla f,T(\nabla u)\rangle

for any
C^{2}
function
u
on
M
. Note that if
T=id
(
1.6
) reduces to (
1.2
). Throughout this paper, for any vector field
X,Y
on
M
, we denote
\langle T(X),Y\rangle
by
T(X,Y)
. From the properties of
{\rm div}_{f}
and the symmetry of
T
we have

\mathcal{L}(uv)=v\mathcal{L}(u)+u\mathcal{L}(v)+2T(\nabla u,\nabla v),

for any
u,v\in C^{2}(M)
.
Let
\Omega\subset M
be a compact bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
in
M
. Let
dm
be the volume form on the boundary induced by the outward normal vector field
\overrightarrow{n}
on
\partial\Omega
. The divergence theorem for operator
\mathcal{L}
gives

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}ud\mu=\int_{\partial\Omega}T(\nabla u,\overrightarrow{n})dm.

Therefore the integration by parts formula is

\int_{\Omega}v\mathcal{L}ud\mu=-\int_{\Omega}T(\nabla u,\nabla v)d\mu+\int_{\partial\Omega}vT(\nabla u,\overrightarrow{n})dm.

Thus in the space of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
which vanish on
\partial\Omega
the operator
\mathcal{L}
is a self-adjoint operator.
The eigenvalues of the operator
\mathcal{L}
are studied in
[
8
,
15
]
.

The second generalization is the so called weighted
p
-diffusion operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}
, defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p}:

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{f}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}T(\nabla u))=e^{f}{\rm div}(e^{-f}|\nabla u|^{p-2}T(\nabla u))

(1.7)

\displaystyle={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}T(\nabla u))-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla f,T(\nabla u)\rangle.

When
T
is the identity,
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is the weighted
p
-Laplacian, if in addition
p=2
, it is then the drifting Laplacian (
1.2
). If
T
is the identity and
f
is constant then we have the usual
p
-Laplacian,

\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
. Note that the usual
p
-Laplacian is nonlinear in general,

Now we denote
u_{t}(x,t)
as partial derivative of
u(x,t)
, and consider the following linear heat equation

(1.8)

u_{t}(x,t)-\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\phi(t)u(x,t),

where
\phi
is a smooth function with respect to time-variable
t
,
u
is a smooth function on
M\times[a,b]
. We define the parabolic frequency
U(t)
for the solution of heat equation (
1.8
) as follows

(1.9)

U(t)=\frac{D(t)}{I(t)},

where

(1.10)

D(t)=-\int_{M}T(\nabla u,\nabla u)d\mu=\int_{M}u\mathcal{L}ud\mu,

and

(1.11)

I(t)=\int_{M}u^{2}d\mu.

To conclude this introduction, we highlight the plan of the rest of the paper in order to put our results in proper perspective. In Section
2
, we prove monotonicity of
U(t)
defined in (
1.9
) under the influence of the linear heat equation (
1.8
). The result which is stated in Theorem
2.1
is a generalization of
[
5
, Theorem 0.6]
. Our method of proof follows a similar approach to
[
5
]
but it is more involved, whereas
[
5
, Theorem 0.6]
becomes a special case of ours for
T=id
on
M
and
\phi(t)\equiv 0
. An immediate consequence of this result is stated in Corollary
2.2
. Furthermore, Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.4
are presented to demonstrate that our results hold also for more general parabolic inequalities, in which case we have assumed

\displaystyle|u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u|\leq\psi(t)\left(|u|+\sqrt{T(\nabla u,\nabla u)}\right)

with
\psi(t)
being a time dependent constant function.

In the final part of this paper (Section
3
), a nonlinear
p
-heat-type equation involving weighted
p
-Laplacian,
\Delta_{f,p}
,
p>1
(where we have taken
T=id
in (
1
)), is considered

(1.12)

\displaystyle|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u=\eta(t)|u|^{p-2}u

on
M
, where
\Delta_{f,p}:={\rm div}_{f}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
,
\eta
is a smooth function with respect to time-variable
t
and
u
is a smooth function on
M\times[a,b]
. The
p
-Lpalacian heat-equation of the form

|u|^{p-2}u_{t}=\Delta_{p}u

is well studied in literature. For instance, in
[
10
,
20
]
Harnack inequalities for its positive solutions were obtained, while Wang
[
18
]
studied gradient estimates on its positive smooth solution along the Ricci flow. Recently, Xu, Shen and Wang in
[
19
]
discussed parabolic frequency monotonicity for
|u|^{p-2}u_{t}=\Delta_{p}u
on a connected finite graph. For our case a corresponding parabolic frequency functional
U_{p}(t)
is given on
M
as

(1.13)

\displaystyle U_{p}(t):=\frac{\int_{M}u\Delta_{f,p}ue^{-f}d\nu}{\int_{M}|u|^{p}e^{-f}d\nu}

with potential function
f
(see (
3.2
)). Here we establish the monotonicity of
U_{p}(t)
along the weighted
p
-heat-type equation (
1.12
) (see Theorem
3.1
), and then discuss some of its consequences, most especially to deriving backward uniqueness for the
p
-heat equation on compact manifolds. Our results in this section extends recent results obtained in
[
19
]
for graph on one hand, and that of
[
5
]
for
p=2
on the other hand.

2.
Generalized parabolic frequency

2.1.
Parabolic frequency on weighted heat-type equation

Our first result in this section is the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
solve the weighted heat-type equation (
1.8
), then
U(t)
(defined in (
1.9
) ) is a nondecreasing function. Moreover, if
U
is constant, then
u
is an eigenfunction of
f
and

(2.1)

u(x,t)=e^{tU(t)-aU(a)+\int_{a}^{t}\phi(\tau)d\tau}u(x,a).

Also, if
\phi(t)
is a nondecreasing function then
\log I
is convex.

Proof.

Direct computing and integrating by parts gives

(2.2)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}uu_{t}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}u(\mathcal{L}u+\phi u)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}T(\nabla u,\nabla u)d\mu+2\phi(t)\int_{M}u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\phi(t)I(t)+2D(t),

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}T(\nabla u,\nabla u_{t})d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}T(\nabla u,\nabla(\mathcal{L}u+\phi(t)u))d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}u)^{2}d\mu+2\phi(t)D(t).

Using (
2.2
) and the definition of
U
we obtain

(2.4)

(\log I)^{\prime}(t)=2\phi(t)+2\frac{D(t)}{I(t)}=2\phi(t)+2U(t).

Thus, applying (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and definition of
D(t)
we infer

(2.5)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(t)I(t)-D(t)I^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}u)^{2}d\mu+2\phi(t)D(t)\right)\left(\int_{M}u^{2}d\mu\right)

\displaystyle-D(t)\left(2\phi(t)I(t)+2D(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}u)^{2}d\mu\right)\left(\int_{M}u^{2}d\mu\right)-2\left(\int_{M}u\mathcal{L}u\,d\mu\right)^{2}\geq 0.

The last inequality is due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. From this we conclude that

(2.6)

U^{\prime}(t)=\frac{D^{\prime}(t)I(t)-D(t)I^{\prime}(t)}{I^{2}(t)}\geq 0.

Therefore,
U(t)
is a nondecreasing function. If
U
is constant then
U^{\prime}(t)=0
and the equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (
2.5
) implies that

(2.7)

\mathcal{L}u=c(t)u,

for some time-dependent smooth function
c
. Hence,
u
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
. By definition of
D(t)
we deduce

(2.8)

D(t)=\int_{M}u\mathcal{L}ud\mu=c(t)\int_{M}u^{2}d\mu=c(t)I(t).

It follows that
c(t)=U(t)
and
\mathcal{L}u=U(t)u
. Let
v(x,t)=e^{-Ut}u(x,t)
. Hence,

(2.9)

v_{t}(x,t)=e^{-Ut}\left(-Uu+u_{t}\right)=e^{-Ut}\left(-Uu+\mathcal{L}u+\phi(t)u\right)=\phi(t)v(x,t).

By solving the last equation we arrive at (
2.1
).

∎

An immediate consequence of the above theorem is given in the next corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
solve the heat equation (
1.8
), then

(2.10)

I(b)\geq I(a)e^{2\int_{a}^{b}\phi(t)dt+2U(a)(b-a)}.

Proof.

From Theorem (
2.1
) we infer

\displaystyle\log I(b)-\log I(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(\log I)^{\prime}(t)dt=2\int_{a}^{b}\phi(t)dt+2\int_{a}^{b}U(t)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\int_{a}^{b}\phi(t)dt+2U(a)(b-a).

∎

2.2.
Parabolic frequency on general parabolic inequality

In the next we consider a more general parabolic inequality (
2.11
) and prove the following results.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the following equation

(2.11)

|u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u|\leq\psi(t)\left(|u|+\sqrt{T(\nabla u,\nabla u)}\right)

where
\psi(t)
is a time dependent function. Then

(2.12)

\psi^{2}(t)\geq\left[\log(1-U(t))\right]^{\prime}.

Proof.

We can write
I^{\prime}(t)
as follows

(2.13)

I^{\prime}(t)=2\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u)\right)d\mu+\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)d\mu.

Since

(2.14)

D(t)=\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u)\right)d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)d\mu,

we get

(2.15)

I^{\prime}(t)D(t)=2\left(\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u)\right)d\mu\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)d\mu\right)^{2}.

Differentiating
D(t)
and using integration by parts we find

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}T(\nabla u,\nabla u_{t})d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}u_{t}\mathcal{L}ud\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}\left\{\left(u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)^{2}\right\}d\mu.

Hence,

(2.16)

D^{\prime}(t)I(t)=2I(t)\int_{M}\left(u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u)\right)^{2}d\mu-\frac{I(t)}{2}\int_{M}\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)^{2}d\mu.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
2.15
), (
2.16
), and (
2.11
) we deduce

(2.17)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(t)I(t)-D(t)I^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left\{\int_{M}u^{2}d\mu\int_{M}\left(u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u)\right)^{2}d\mu-\left(\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u)\right)d\mu\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{I(t)}{2}\int_{M}\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)^{2}d\mu+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)d\mu\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{I(t)}{2}\int_{M}\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{\psi^{2}(t)I(t)}{2}\int_{M}\left(|u|+\sqrt{T(\nabla u,\nabla u)}\right)^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\psi^{2}(t)I(t)\left(I(t)-D(t)\right).

Dividing both sides of last inequality by
I^{2}(t)
we obtain
U^{\prime}(t)\geq\psi^{2}(t)\left(U(t)-1\right)
and this completes the proof.
∎

Applying Theorem
2.3
we can prove a generalization of Corollary
2.2
as follows.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies (
2.11
), then

I(b)\geq I(a)\exp\left\{(b-a)\left((2+\mathop{\sup}\limits_{[a,b]}\psi)\exp(\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(t)dt)(U(a)-1)+2-\mathop{\sup}\limits_{[a,b]}\psi\right)\right\}.

In particular, if
u(\cdot,b)=0
then
u=0
.

Proof.

From (
2.13
) we have

\displaystyle(\log I)^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2U(t)+\frac{2}{I(t)}\int_{M}u\left(u_{t}-\mathcal{L}u\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2U(t)-\frac{2\psi(t)}{I(t)}\int_{M}|u|\left(|u|+\sqrt{T(\nabla u,\nabla u)}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2U(t)-2\psi(t)-\frac{2\sqrt{2}\psi(t)}{I(t)}\int_{M}|u|\sqrt{T(\nabla u,\nabla u)}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2U(t)-2\psi(t)(1+\sqrt{-U(t)})\geq(2+\psi(t))U(t)-3\psi(t).

Thus, by taking integration we get

(2.18)

\log I(b)-\log I(a)=\int_{a}^{b}(\log I)^{\prime}(t)dt\geq(2+\mathop{\sup}\limits_{[a,b]}\psi)\int_{a}^{b}U(t)dt-3\mathop{\sup}\limits_{[a,b]}\psi(b-a).

For
t\in[a,b]
, using (
2.12
) we obtain

\log(1-U(t))\leq(1-U(a))+\int_{a}^{t}\psi^{2}(s)ds\leq(1-U(a))+\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(s)ds.

Hence,

(2.19)

U(t))\geq(U(a)-1)\exp\left(\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(s)ds\right)+1.

Substituting (
2.19
) in (
2.18
), we deduce

\log I(b)-\log I(a)\geq(b-a)\left((2+\mathop{\sup}\limits_{[a,b]}\psi)\exp(\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(t)dt)(U(a)-1)+2-\mathop{\sup}\limits_{[a,b]}\psi\right).

This completes the proof of corollary.
∎

3.
p-parabolic frequency

In this section we consider the following nonlinear weighted
p
-heat-type equation for
1<p<\infty

(3.1)

|u(x,t)|^{p-2}u_{t}(x,t)-\Delta_{f,p}u(x,t)=\eta(t)|u(x,t)|^{p-2}u(x,t),

where
\eta
is a smooth function with respect to time-variable
t
,
u
is a smooth function on
M\times[a,b]
.
Here, the parabolic frequency
U_{p}(t)
for the solution of (
3.1
) is defined as follows

(3.2)

U_{p}(t)=\frac{D_{p}(t)}{I_{p}(t)},

where

(3.3)

D_{p}(t)=\int_{M}u\Delta_{f,p}ud\mu=-\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu,

and

(3.4)

I_{p}(t)=\int_{M}|u|^{p}d\mu

with
d\mu=e^{-f}d\nu
as defined in the introduction.
Our main results here is stated in the next theorem and we give an application in Corollary
3.2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
solves the heat equation (
3.1
), then
U_{p}(t)
is a nondecreasing function. Moreover, if
U_{p}(t)
is constant, then
u
is an eigenfunction of
f
and

(3.5)

u(x,t)|u(x,t)|^{p-2}=e^{(p-1)(U(t)t-U(a)a)}u(x,a)|u(x,a)|^{p-2}\exp\{(p-1)\int_{a}^{s}\eta(s)ds\}.

Also, if
\eta(t)
is a nondecreasing function then
\log I
is convex.

Proof.

By a straightforward computation we have

(3.6)

\partial_{t}|u|^{p}=p|u|^{p-2}uu_{t},

(3.7)

\partial_{t}|\nabla u|^{p}=p|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla u_{t}\rangle.

So we calculate the derivative of
I_{p}(t)
as follows

(3.8)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}p|u|^{p-2}uu_{t}d\mu.

Noting that by (
3.3
)

(3.9)

\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}d\mu=-\int_{M}u\Delta_{p}ud\mu=-D_{p}(t).

Multiplying both sides of (
3.1
) by
u
and integrate over
M
with respect to
d\mu
gives

(3.10)

\int_{M}(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)ud\mu=\int_{M}\eta(t)|u|^{p}d\mu=\eta(t)I_{p}(t).

Clearly combining (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{M}|u|^{p-2}u_{t}ud\mu=\eta(t)I_{p}(t)+D_{p}(t)

from where we conclude by reverting to (
3.8
) that

(3.11)

I^{\prime}_{p}(t)=p\eta(t)I_{p}(t)+pD_{p}(t).

Now, we compute the derivative of
D_{p}(t)
. Applying (
3.7
) and integration by parts, we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}p|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla u_{t}\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\eta(t)D_{p}(t)+p\int_{M}|u|^{2-p}(\Delta_{f,p}u)^{2}d\mu.

Using (
3.11
), (
3.12
), and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{p}(t)I_{p}(t)-D_{p}(t)I^{\prime}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(p\eta(t)D_{p}(t)+p\int_{M}|u|^{2-p}(\Delta_{f,p}u)^{2}d\mu\right)I_{p}(t)

\displaystyle-D_{p}(t)\left(p\eta(t)I_{p}(t)+pD_{p}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\left(\int_{M}|u|^{2-p}(\Delta_{f,p}u)^{2}d\mu\right)\left(\int_{M}|u|^{p}d\mu\right)-p\left(\int_{M}u\Delta_{f,p}ud\mu\right)^{2}\geq 0

since
p>1
.
Thus we have obtained

U^{\prime}_{p}(t)=\frac{D^{\prime}_{p}(t)I_{p}(t)-D_{p}(t)I^{\prime}_{p}(t)}{I_{p}^{2}(t)}\geq 0

which implies that
U
is hence nondecreasing. If
U
is constant then
U^{\prime}_{p}(t)=0
and equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that
\Delta_{f,p}u=\alpha(t)u|u|^{p-2}
for some time-dependent smooth function
\alpha
. Thus,
u
is an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{f,p}
. In this case, we have

D_{p}(t)=\int_{M}u\Delta_{f,p}ud\mu=\alpha(t)I_{p}(t),

and consequently,
\alpha(t)=U(t)
, that is
\Delta_{f,p}u=U(t)u
. Let

w(x,t)=e^{-(p-1)Ut}u(x,t)|u(x,t)|^{p-2}.

By taking derivative of
w(x,t)
with respect to
t
we obtain

w_{t}(x,t)=(p-1)e^{-(p-1)Ut}\left(-Uu|u|^{p-2}+u_{t}|u|^{p-2}\right)=(p-1)\eta(t)w(x,t)

Solving the last equation gives (
3.5
).

∎

The last theorem has the following as an immediate consequence.

Corollary 3.2
.

If
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies in (
3.1
) then

(3.13)

I_{p}(b)\geq I_{p}(a)e^{p\int_{a}^{b}\eta(t)dt+pU_{p}(a)(b-a)}.

Proof.

From Theorem (
3.1
) we infer

\displaystyle\log I_{p}(b)-\log I_{p}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(\log I_{p})^{\prime}(t)dt=p\int_{a}^{b}\eta(t)dt+p\int_{a}^{b}U_{p}(t)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle p\int_{a}^{b}\eta(t)dt+pU_{p}(a)(b-a).

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

3.1.
General parabolic operators

In the next we consider a more general parabolic inequality for
p>1

(3.14)

|(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)|\leq\psi(t)\left[|u|^{p-1}(1+|u|^{-\frac{p}{2}}\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{p}})\right],

where
\psi(t)
is a time dependent function, and prove the following results.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies (
3.14
). Then

(3.15)

\displaystyle U^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p}{2}\psi^{2}(t)[U_{p}(t)-1],

(3.16)

\displaystyle\psi^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{p}\left[\log(1-U_{p}(t))\right]^{\prime}

and

(3.17)

\displaystyle[\log I(t)]^{\prime}\geq p(1+\psi(t)/2)U_{p}(t)-(3p/2)\psi(t).

Proof.

Note that all the integrals in this subsection are over
M
with respect to the weighted measure
d\mu=e^{-f}d\nu
. First we write the expression for the derivative of
I_{p}(t)
as follows:

\displaystyle I_{p}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=p\int|u|^{p-2}uu_{t}

\displaystyle=p\int u\Delta_{f,p}u+p\int\left\langle u,(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\right\rangle

(3.18)

\displaystyle=p\int\left\langle u,\left[|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\right]\right\rangle+\frac{p}{2}\int\left\langle u,(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\right\rangle.

The quantity
D_{p}(t)
can also be re-written as follows

\displaystyle D_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=\int\langle u,\Delta_{f,p}u\rangle

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\int\left\langle u,[|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)]\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int\langle u,(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\rangle.

By (
3.1
) and (
3.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle D_{p}(t)I_{p}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=p\left(\int\langle u,[|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)]\rangle\right)^{2}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{p}{2}\left(\int\langle u,(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\rangle\right)^{2}.

Similarly, differentiating the quantity
D_{p}(t)
and rewrite the resulting expression gives

\displaystyle D_{p}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-p\int|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla u_{t}\rangle=p\int\langle u_{t},\Delta_{f,p}u\rangle

\displaystyle=p\int\langle u_{t},\left[|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u\right)\right]\rangle

\displaystyle=p\int\Bigg\{\Bigg||u|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left[|u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u\right)\right]\Bigg|^{2}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{1}{4}\Bigg||u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}\right)\Bigg|^{2}\Bigg\}.

Therefore we have by (
3.1
)

\displaystyle I_{p}(t)D_{p}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=pI_{p}(t)\int\Bigg||u|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left[|u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u\right)\right]\Bigg|^{2}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{pI_{p}(t)}{4}\int\Bigg||u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}\right)\Bigg|^{2}.

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle I_{p}^{2}(t)U_{p}^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\ I_{p}(t)D_{p}^{\prime}(t)-D_{p}(t)I_{p}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ p\left(\int|u|^{p}\right)\int\Bigg||u|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left[|u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u\right)\right]\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle-p\left(\int\langle u,[|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)]\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{pI_{p}(t)}{4}\int\Bigg||u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}\right)\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{p}{2}\left(\int\langle u,(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ -\frac{pI_{p}(t)}{4}\int\Bigg||u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}\right)\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{p}{2}\left(\int\langle u,(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ -\frac{pI_{p}(t)}{4}\int\Bigg||u|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}\right)\Bigg|^{2},

where we have used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that
p>1
.

Now applying the general parabolic inequality (
3.14
) leads us to

\displaystyle I_{p}^{2}(t)U_{p}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{p}{4}I_{p}(t)\psi^{2}(t)\int|u|^{2-p}\Bigg|\left(|u|^{p-1}+|u|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{p}}\right)\Bigg|^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{p}{4}I_{p}(t)\psi^{2}(t)\int\Big|\left(\sqrt{|u|^{p}}+\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{p}}\right)\Big|^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{p}{2}\psi^{2}(t)I_{p}(t)\Big(I_{p}(t)-D_{p}(t)\Big),

where we have used the elementary inequality of the form
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
. Dividing both sides by
I_{p}^{2}(t)
yields the first claim, that is, (
3.15
). Clearly (
3.16
) follows from (
3.15
).

Finally, we prove the last part of the theorem (i.e., (
3.17
)). Here we write the expression for
I_{p}^{\prime}(t)
as follows

\displaystyle I_{p}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=p\int|u|^{p-2}uu_{t}\ d\mu

\displaystyle=p\int_{M}u\Delta_{f,p}u\ d\mu+p\int_{M}\langle u,(|u|^{p-2}u_{t}-\Delta_{f,p}u)\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle\geq pD_{p}(t)-p\psi(t)\int_{M}|u|\left[|u|^{p-1}+|u|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]d\mu

(3.23)

\displaystyle=pD_{p}(t)-p\psi(t)I_{p}(t)-p\psi(t)\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{p}{2}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}\ d\mu.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we know that

\displaystyle\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{p}{2}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left(\int_{M}|u|^{p}\ d\mu\right)}\sqrt{\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}\ d\mu\right)}

(3.24)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{I_{p}(t)}\sqrt{-D_{p}(t)}.

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle I_{p}^{\prime}(t)\geq pD_{p}(t)-p\psi(t)\Big(I_{p}(t)+\sqrt{I_{p}(t)}\sqrt{-D_{p}(t)}\Big).

Hence

(3.25)

\displaystyle[\log I_{p}(t)]^{\prime}=I_{p}^{\prime}(t)/I_{p}(t)\geq pU_{p}(t)-p\psi(t)\Big(1+\sqrt{-U_{p}(t)}\Big).

Using the elementary inequality of the form
a\leq\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}+1)
applied to
a=\sqrt{-U_{p}(t)}
in (
3.25
), we have

\displaystyle[\log I_{p}(t)]^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq pU_{p}(t)-p\psi(t)\Big[1+\frac{1}{2}(-U_{p}(t)+1)\Big]

\displaystyle=p\left(1+\frac{\psi(t)}{2}\right)U_{p}(t)-\frac{3p}{2}\psi(t).

This completes the proof
∎

The next result is an immediate consequence of the above theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
u:M\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies (
3.14
). Then

(3.26)

I_{p}(b)\geq I_{p}(a)\exp\left\{\frac{p(b-a)}{2}\Lambda\right\},

where

\displaystyle\Lambda:=[U_{p}(a)-1]\exp\left\{[2+\sup_{[a,b]}\psi(t)]\frac{p}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(t)dt\right\}+1-3\sup_{[a,b]}\psi(t).

In particular, if
u(\cdot,b)=0
, then
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Starting with (
3.17
) we get

\displaystyle\log I_{p}(b)-\log I_{p}(a)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}(\log I)^{\prime}(s)ds

(3.27)

\displaystyle\geq[2+\sup_{[a,b]}\psi(t)]\frac{p}{2}\int_{a}^{b}U_{p}(s)ds-\frac{3p}{2}\sup_{[a,b]}\psi(t)(b-2).

From (
3.16
) we obtain for
r\in[a,b]

\displaystyle\log[1-U_{p}(r)]\leq\log[1-U_{p}(a)]+\frac{p}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(r)dr.

Thus

(3.28)

\displaystyle U_{p}(r)\geq\exp\left(\frac{p}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(t)dt\right)[U_{p}(a)-1]+1.

Inserting the estimate (
3.28
) into (
3.1
) and integrating the resulting expression gives

\displaystyle\log I_{p}(b)-\log I_{p}(a)\geq

\displaystyle\ \frac{p(b-a)}{2}\Bigg[[U_{p}(a)-1]\exp\left\{[2+\sup_{[a,b]}\psi(t)]\frac{p}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\psi^{2}(t)dt\right\}

\displaystyle+1-3\sup_{[a,b]}\psi(t)\Bigg]

from where the estimate (
3.26
) follows. This completes the proof.
∎

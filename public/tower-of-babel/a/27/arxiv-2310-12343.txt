New Environment Adaptation with Few Shots for OFDM Receiver and mmWave Beamforming
Ouya Wang, Shenglong Zhou, Geoffrey Ye Li

Abstract
Few-shot learning (FSL) enables adaptation to new tasks with only limited training data. In wireless communications, channel environments can vary drastically; therefore, FSL techniques can quickly adjust transceiver accordingly. In this paper, we develop two FSL frameworks that fit in wireless transceiver design. Both frameworks are base on optimization programs that can be solved by well-known algorithms like the inexact alternating direction method of multipliers (iADMM) and the inexact alternating direction method (iADM). As examples, we demonstrate how the proposed two FSL frameworks are used for the OFDM receiver and beamforming (BF) for the millimeter wave (mmWave) system. The numerical experiments confirm their desirable performance in both applications compared to other popular approaches, such as transfer learning (TL) and model-agnostic meta-learning.

These methods perform relatively well under known/trained environments but may suffer from performance degradation when deploying trained models in unseen/new environments.

DL techniques can be also used in beam prediction. In the mmWave system, DL can exploit additional contextual information, such as receiver locations and surrounding obstacles in mobile networks, to improve predictions accuracy

DL-based methods face at least three challenges when used in wireless communication systems. First, the system frequently undergoes rapid and unpredictable environmental changes. Acquiring and annotating extensive data to account for these rapid changes becomes impractical and resource-intensive, hindering real-time adaptation to new environments. Secondly, when encountering a new environment, the amount of available data is typically limited, placing significant strain on conventional DL approaches that usually demand vast labeled datasets for effective training. Finally, data distributions across various wireless scenarios exhibit distinct characteristics, which impedes the universal applicability of a single model across diverse domains.

Hence, effectively obtaining valid information from a small number of samples becomes essential to satisfy the demand for real-world wireless communications. FSL is an emerging paradigm that addresses these challenges by producing creative work on data, models, and algorithms. According to
[
8

[

Despite the similarities shared by different environments, each still has its unique characteristics. How do we extract these common and unique characteristics? Moreover, when a new environment comes with limited samples, how can we leverage prior experience from known environments to enable the wireless system to adapt to the new environment? Another challenge lies in the slow convergence during training. Given that the system needs to learn from a wide range of different environments to accumulate experience, conventional deep learning optimizers struggle to efficiently discover the optimal shared and specific parameters within limited epochs. This inevitably leads to extensive training times and high computational resource requirements. How do we develop an optimal algorithm based on conventional DL tools to accelerate the training convergence speed in this scenario?

I-B

Contribution

The main contributions of the paper are threefold. To begin with, we propose two new learning schemes, to fulfill FSL tasks in wireless communications. Both schemes provide versatile and adaptable methods that can be applied to a broad range of learning tasks, making them promising for large-scale applications. Moreover, we employ the inexact alternating direction method of multipliers (iADMM) and the inexact alternating direction method (iADM) to tackle the non-convex optimization problems involving neural networks. Our numerical results have shown that they exhibit faster convergence compared to the conventional DL optimizers, such as stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and root mean-squared propagation (RMSProp). Furthermore, we have designed a novel transformer-based OFDM receiver architecture and a DL-based mmWave BF prediction system as wireless application examples. They are augmented with a hyper-network that employs few-shot samples to generate parameters, enabling the system to adapt to various environmental conditions.

We would like to point out that a portion of our work has been previously published in
[
15
]
. In this paper, we have made two additional contributions: Firstly, we have introduced the framework of online adaptation FSL, enabling rapid adaptation to new environments. To testify its efficiency, we develop a DL-based mmWave BF prediction system. Secondly, we have incorporated second-order gradients into the iADMM algorithm, resulting in much faster convergence during offline training compared to RMSProp.

Problem Formulation

This approach comprises two phases, as shown in Fig.

EA-I: Learning from previous environments
Let

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}f^{\phi}_{i}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}_{i}):=\sum_{t=1}^{d_{i}}\ell\left(\phi(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}_{i};\mathbf{x}^{t}_{i}),\mathbf{y}^{t}_{i}\right),~~i\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{array}

Here,

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}\approx\phi({\mathbf{w}}^{*},{\mathbf{v}}_{i}^{*};\mathbf{x}_{i}),~i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

II-B

Solving problem (
II-A
)

One can discern that problems (
II-A
) and (
II-A
) involve two neural networks, i.e.,
\phi
and
\varphi
, and thus are non-convex in general. We note that the inexact ADMM (iADMM) has shown its popularity and ability in the recent several decades to solve some complex non-convex optimization. Therefore, we adopt it to address the optimization problems in Section
II-A
. We first equivalently rewrite problem (
II-A
) as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r}({\mathbf{w}}^{*},{{\mathbf{W}}}^{*},{\mathbf{V}}^{*}):={\rm argmin}_{(\mathbf{w},{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{V}})}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{\phi}_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})\\
{\rm s.t.}~\mathbf{w}_{i}=\mathbf{w},~i\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{array}

The augmented Lagrange function of the above problem is

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rrr}L(\mathbf{w},{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{V}},{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}L_{i}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i}),\end{array}

where
{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}=({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{1},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{n-1})
are the Lagrange multipliers,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}L_{i}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i})\\
:=\frac{1}{n}f^{\phi}_{i}(\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})+\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i},\mathbf{w}_{i}-\mathbf{w}\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\mathbf{w}_{i}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2},\end{array}

and
\sigma>0
is a given constant. Then the framework of iADMM can be described as follows. Given
({\mathbf{W}}^{0},{\mathbf{V}}^{0},{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{0})
, perform the following steps iteratively for
\ell=0,1,2,\cdots,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r}\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}={\rm argmin}~L(\mathbf{w},{\mathbf{W}}^{\ell},{\mathbf{V}}^{\ell},{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{\ell})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\ell}+\frac{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i}^{\ell}}{\sigma})\\
(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\ell+1})\approx~{\rm argmin}~L_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i}^{\ell}),~i\in{\mathbb{N}},\\
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i}^{\ell+1}={\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i}^{\ell}+\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\ell+1}-\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}),~i\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{array}

One can observe that the critical step is to solve the second problem in (
II-B
). To accelerate the computation, we aim to solve it inexactly by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\ell+1})={\rm argmin}~\langle({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{i}^{\ell},~{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i}^{\ell}),(\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})-(\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\ell})\rangle\\
+\frac{\rho}{2}\|(\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})-(\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\ell})\|^{2}\\
+\langle{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i}^{\ell},\mathbf{w}_{i}-\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\mathbf{w}_{i}-\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}\|^{2}\\
=(\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}-\frac{1}{\rho+\sigma}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{i}^{\ell}+{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{i}^{\ell}),~\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\ell}-\frac{1}{\rho}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i}^{\ell}),\end{array}

where
{\rho>0}
and
{({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{i}^{\ell},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i}^{\ell})\in\frac{1}{n}\partial f^{\phi}_{i}(\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\ell})}
. Here, notation
{\partial f^{\phi}_{i}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})}
, is the sub-differential of
f^{\phi}_{i}
at
{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})}
which reduces to the gradient of
f^{\phi}_{i}
if it is continuously differentiable.
One can replace
\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}
by
\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\ell}
in the above problem to update
{(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\ell+1})}
. However, this would degrade the convergence performance of the proposed algorithm as using
\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}
enables all
{\{\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\ell}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}
to converge to same value
\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}
, which makes it faster to satisfy the constraint in (
II-B
). Overall, we present these updates in Algorithm
1

({\mathbf{W}}^{0},{\mathbf{V}}^{0},{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{0})
,
\rho>0
, and
\sigma>0
. Set
\ell=0
.

for

do

Update
\mathbf{w}^{\ell+1}
by the first equation in (

for

and

II-D

System settings

We apply the framework of EA to the application in the OFDM receiver. In this scenario, we assume that pilot symbols are placed in the first block of a frame, with subsequent blocks containing data. While the channel remains constant within a frame, variations can occur between frames. The OFDM receiver processes one pilot block and one data block in each frame to recover transmitted data in an end-to-end fashion. The OFDM receiver is constructed upon the advanced T5 transformer model
[
16
]
, with the received signal from the
i
th environment with input
\mathbf{x}_{i}
and the transmitted signal as output
\mathbf{y}_{i}
. The structure of the OFDM receiver with the hyper-network is demonstrated in Fig.

[
15
]
. All transformer blocks share the same parameters across all environments, referring to shared parameter
\mathbf{w}
. Similar to the approach introduced in
[
17
]
, we learn to scale

and shift

k
th adapter layer, as shown in Fig.
3

{\{(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k},\boldsymbol{\beta}_{k}):k=0,1,2\}}
refer to particular parameters

. Unlike conventional fine-tuning methods updating all parameters of the adapter layers, our approach updates only one parameter per channel.

We consider the symbol-spaced multipath channel for the OFDM receiver case. The mean power associated with each multipath component relies on propagation delay

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}r(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\sqrt{P(\tau_{l})}\alpha_{l}(t)e^{-j\vartheta_{l}(t)}s(t-\tau_{l})+\epsilon(t),\end{array}

IV
Conclusion

We proposed two FSL schemes that enable efficient adaptation to new environments using few-shot samples based on prior knowledge from multiple known environments. Their efficiency has been evaluated by processing the issues in the context of the OFDM receiver and mmWave BF system. The proposed approaches exhibited superior performance compared to well-established alternatives, particularly in scenarios where the new environments share limited similarity to the known ones. Moreover, the developed frameworks are versatile and might be able to deal with various other FSL tasks in wireless communications.

and Hessian
\nabla^{2}g
are continuous, which together with the boundedness of
\Omega
results in the boundedness of
\delta_{\mathbf{u}}^{*}
and thus
\tau^{*}
, that is
{\delta_{\mathbf{u}}^{*}<\infty}
and
{\tau^{*}<\infty}
. Similarly, we can prove that
{\delta_{\mathbf{v}}^{*}<\infty}
and
{\kappa^{*}<\infty}
.

Now we prove the conclusion by induction. For
\ell=0
, direct calculation leads to the following chain of inequalities,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&\langle{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\rangle+c_{2}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{1}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\langle{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\rangle+\tau^{0}\|{\mathbf{u}}^{1}_{n}-{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}+c_{2}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{1}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\langle{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\rangle+c_{2}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2},\end{array}

where the second inequality is from (
III-C
). Using the fact that
2\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}/a+a\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}
for any
a>0
, the above condition suffices to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{c_{2}}{2}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{1}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{1}{c_{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{3c_{2}}{2}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{1}{c_{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}F(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}),\end{array}

resulting in

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{1}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{c_{2}^{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{c_{2}}F(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\leq\delta_{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\end{array}

due to
(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\in\Omega
, namely
\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{u}^{*},\delta_{\mathbf{u}}^{*})
. It is easy to see that
\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{u}^{*},\delta_{\mathbf{u}}^{*})
. Therefore,
\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0}(a):=a\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0}+(1-a)\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{u}^{*},\delta_{\mathbf{u}}^{*})
for any
a\in(0,1)
. Moreover, one can observe that
\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\in\mathbb{B}(0,\frac{F^{0}}{c_{4}})
owing to
c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}\leq F^{0}
. Overall, by (
Appendix: Proof of Theorem
), we derive
\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}^{2}g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0}(a),\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\|\leq\tau^{*}
. Thus by the Mean Value Theorem and denoting
\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}:=\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}-\mathbf{u}^{0}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})-g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})-\langle{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{0}_{n},\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\rangle\\
&=&\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}^{2}g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0}(a),\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\cdot\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n},\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\rangle\leq\tau^{*}\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Based on the above condition, we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})&=&g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\\
&+&c_{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}+c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}\\
&+&\langle{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{0}_{n},\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\rangle+\tau^{0}\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}+c_{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}\\
&=&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})-g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\\
&+&\langle{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{0}_{n},\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\rangle+\tau^{0}\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+(\tau^{0}-\tau^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\|^{2},\end{array}

where the first inequality is from (
III-C
).

Similarly, it follows from (
III-C
) that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&\langle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\rangle+c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&c_{1}f^{\varphi}_{n}(\mathbf{u}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})+\langle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\rangle+\kappa^{\ell}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}-\mathbf{v}^{0}_{n}\|^{2}+c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&c_{1}f^{\varphi}_{n}(\mathbf{u}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+\langle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\rangle+c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&F(\mathbf{u}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+\langle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\rangle,\end{array}

which results in

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{c_{4}}{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\|^{2}&\leq&F(\mathbf{u}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+\frac{1}{c_{4}}\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{c_{4}}{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{3}{2}F(\mathbf{u}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+\frac{1}{c_{4}}\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{3}{2}F(\mathbf{u}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+\frac{1}{c_{4}}\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n}\|^{2}.\end{array}

This condition gives rise to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{3}{c_{4}}F(\mathbf{u}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+\frac{2}{c_{4}^{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n}\|^{2}\leq\delta_{\mathbf{v}}^{*}\end{array}

due to
(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\in\Omega
, namely
\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\in\mathbb{B}(0,\delta_{\mathbf{v}}^{*})
. It is easy to see that
\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\in\mathbb{B}(0,\delta_{\mathbf{v}}^{*})
. Therefore,
\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}(a):=a\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}+(1-a)\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\in\mathbb{B}(0,\delta_{\mathbf{v}}^{*})
for any
a\in(0,1)
. Overall, by the definition of
\kappa^{*}
, we derive
\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}}^{2}f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}(a))\|\leq\kappa^{*}
. Thus by the Mean Value Theorem and denoting
\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}:=\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}-\mathbf{v}^{0}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})-f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})-\langle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n},\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\rangle\\
&=&\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}}^{2}f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}(a))\cdot\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n},\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\rangle\leq\kappa^{*}\|\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}.\end{array}

The above condition enables us to derive

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\\
&=&g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+c_{2}\|{\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}+c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+c_{2}\|{\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\|^{2}+c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0}\|^{2}\\
&+&f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})-\langle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{0}_{n},\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\rangle-\kappa^{*}\|\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})+f^{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})+c_{2}\left\|{\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\right\|^{2}\\
&+&c_{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1}\|^{2}+(\kappa^{0}-\kappa^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}\\
&=&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})+(\kappa^{0}-\kappa^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\|^{2},\end{array}

where the second inequality is from (
III-C
). Therefore, we prove

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})\\
&\geq&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})+(\tau^{0}-\tau^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})+(\tau^{0}-\tau^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}+(\kappa^{0}-\kappa^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{v}^{1}_{n}\|^{2}.\end{array}

This indicates
{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})\leq{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{0},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{0})=F^{0}
and hence
(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})\in\Omega
. Repeating the above proof, we can show that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{1})-{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{2},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{2})\\
&\geq&(\tau^{1}-\tau^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{2}_{n}\|^{2}+(\kappa^{1}-\kappa^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{v}^{2}_{n}\|^{2},\end{array}

resulting in
(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{2},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{2})\in\Omega
. Then repeating the proof enables us to prove

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&&{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\ell},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\ell})-{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\ell+1},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\ell+1})\\
&\geq&(\tau^{\ell}-\tau^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{u}^{\ell+1}_{n}\|^{2}+(\kappa^{\ell}-\kappa^{*})\|\triangle\mathbf{v}^{\ell+1}_{n}\|^{2},\end{array}

for any
{\ell\geq 0}
. Hence sequence
\{{F}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\ell},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\ell})\}
is strictly decreasing, which together with
{F(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\ell},\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\ell})\geq 0}
ensures its convergence. Finally taking the limit of both sides of the above condition leads to
{\triangle\mathbf{u}^{\ell+1}_{n}\to 0}
and
{\triangle\mathbf{v}^{\ell+1}_{n}\to 0}
when
\ell\to\infty.
The proof is finished.
∎

Dynamical large deviations for boundary driven gradient symmetric exclusion processes in mild contact with reservoirs
Angèle Bouley, Claudio Landim

Abstract
. We consider a one-dimensional gradient symmetric exclusion process in mild contact with boundary reservoirs. The hydrodynamic limit of the empirical measure is given by a non-linear second-order parabolic equation with non-linear Robin boundary conditions. We prove the dynamical large deviations principle.



1.
Introduction

A major challenge addressed in recent years consists in the physical
description of non-equilibrium states (cf.
[
18
,
3
]
and references
therein).

The analysis of interacting particle systems stationary states, as the
number of degrees of freedom diverges, has proved to be an important
step in this program. The thermodynamical functional of the empirical
measure, the observable of interest in lattice gases, corresponds to
the large deviation rate functional for the empirical measure when the
particles are distributed according to the stationary state.

In equilibrium, when there is no current of particles, the invariant
measure is given by the Gibbs distribution specified by the
Hamiltonian, and the thermodynamical functional is called the free
energy. In non-equilibrium, in the presence of currents, the
invariant measure is usually not known explicitly, and a basic problem
consists in characterizing the rate functional, known as the
non-equilibrium free energy.

In certain special cases
[
11
,
10
]
, combinatorial arguments
based on special feature of the exclusion processes provide a formula
for the non-equilibrium free energy. For general dynamics, a dynamical
approach has been proposed
[
1
]
. One fist prove a dynamical
large deviations principle for the empirical measure in a fixed time
interval
[0,T]
. The non-equilibrium free energy of a density profile

\gamma
is given by the minimal cost among all trajectories which
start from the stationary density profile and reaches
\gamma
in
finite time. This procedures provides a dynamical variational formula
for the non-equilibrium free energy.

In this article we continue the investigation started in
[
9
,
14
,
8
]
of the dynamical large deviations for interacting particle
systems whose hydrodynamic equation is described by a second-order
parabolic equation with Robin boundary conditions. We consider a
gradient exclusion process leading to a non-linear hydrodynamic
equation. This non-linearity turns the large deviations rate
functional non-convex, a property much used in the proof of the large
deviations principle lower-bound in the linear case
[
14
]
.

In possession of the dynamical large deviations, one can proceed as in

[
5
,
13
,
2
,
8
]
to prove the large deviations for the
empirical measure under the invariant measure, deriving a dynamical
variational formula for the non-equilibrium free energy. This is left
for a future work. The next step, a challenging open problem, consists
in obtaining a simple formula for the non-equilibrium free energy,
derived in
[
11
,
1
]
for symmetric exclusion processis in
strong contact with reservoirs and in
[
10
,
8
]
for these
dynamics in mild contact with reservoirs.

This article presents two novelties which can be useful in other
contexts, as for reaction-diffusion models without the concavity
assumption on the creation and destruction rates
[
17
,
6
,
16
]
.

Denote by
I_{[0,T]}(\cdot)
the large deviations rate functional.
One of the main difficulties in the proof of the lower bound consists
in showing that any trajectory
\pi(t,dx)=u(t,x)\,dx
with finite
rate function can be approximated by a sequence of smooth paths

\pi_{n}(t,dx)=u_{n}(t,x)\,dx
such that
\pi_{n}\to\pi
,

I_{[0,T]}(\pi_{n})\to I_{[0,T]}(\pi)
.

The proof of this density is divided in several steps. When the rate
functional is not convex, the standard argument to settle one of them

[
24
,
4
]
relies on a Riesz representation lemma of linear
functionals in negative Sobolev spaces (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 4.8]
). We
present here a much simpler and direct proof of this step.

The second technical novelty concerns the regularity of paths with
finite rate functionals. Fix a path
\pi(t,dx)=u(t,x)\,dx
such
that
I_{[0,T]}(\pi)<\infty
. It is known, at least since

[
4
]
, that such a path is weakly continuous in time. More
precisely, that for each space-dependent
C^{1}
function
g
, the map

t\mapsto\langle\pi_{t},g\rangle
is uniformly continuous. Proposition
4.11
below asserts that this map is absolutely continuous and its
derivative belongs to
L^{1}
. This property permits to integrate by
parts
\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}H_{t},u_{t}\rangle\,dt
, when

H(t,x)=h(t)\,g(x)
and plays a crucial role in the decomposition
of the rate functional presented in Section
5
below.

The model

We consider a one-dimensional boundary driven gradient symmetric
exclusion processes in weak contact with boundary reservoirs. Fix

N\geq 1
, and let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Lambda_{N}=\{-N+1,\dots,N-1\}
. The
state-space is represented by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}=\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{N}}
and the configurations by
the Greek letters
\eta
,
\xi
so that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\eta(x)
,

x\in\Lambda_{N}
, represents the number of particles at site
x
for
the configuration
\eta
. Here and below all notation introduced in
the text and not in displayed equations is indicated in blue.

The theorems stated in this article apply to all gradient symmetric
exclusion dynamics in mild contact with reservoirs. To avoid heavy
notation, we consider a specific example. Fix

a>-1/2
,
0<{\mathfrak{a}}
,
{\mathfrak{b}}<1
. The variables
{\mathfrak{a}}
,
{\mathfrak{b}}

represent the density of the left, right reservoirs,
respectively. Assume, without loss of generality, that

{\mathfrak{a}}\leq{\mathfrak{b}}
. Let
r_{x,x+1}\colon{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,

-N+2\leq x\leq N-3
, be the rates at which the variables
\eta(x)
,

\eta(x+1)
are exchanged, and given by

\displaystyle r_{x,x+1}(\eta)=1+a\,[\,\eta(x-1)+\eta(x+2)\,]\;.

At the boundary, replace the occupation variables which do not belong
to
\Lambda_{N}
by the density of the reservoirs:

\displaystyle r_{-N+1,-N+2}(\eta)=1+a\,[\,{\mathfrak{a}}+\eta(-N+3)\,]\;,

\displaystyle r_{N-2,N-1}(\eta)=1+a\,[\,\eta(N-3)+{{\mathfrak{b}}}\,]\;.

The generator
L_{N}
of the Markov process is given by

\displaystyle L_{N}=L_{N,0}+L_{N,b}

where
L_{N,0}
,
L_{N,b}
correspond to the bulk, boundary dynamics,
respectively. The action of the generator
L_{N,0}
on functions

f\colon{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\displaystyle(L_{N,0}f)(\eta)=N^{2}\sum_{x}r_{x,x+1}(\eta)\,[\,f(\eta^{x,x+1})-f(\eta)\,]\;,

where the sum is carried over all
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\{x,x+1\}\subset\Lambda_{N}
. Moreover,
\eta^{x,x+1}
stands for
the configuration obtained from
\eta
by exchanging the occupation
variables
\eta(x)
and
\eta(x+1)

\displaystyle\eta^{x,x+1}(z)=\begin{cases}\eta(z)&\mbox{ if }z\neq x,x+1\\
\eta(x+1)&\mbox{ if }z=x\\
\eta(x)&\mbox{ if }z=x+1\;.\end{cases}

The generator of the boundary dynamics acts on functions

f\colon{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle(L_{N,b}f)(\eta)=N\,\Big\{\,r_{L}(\eta)\,[\,f(\eta^{-N+1})-f(\eta)\,]\,+\,r_{R}(\eta)\,[\,f(\eta^{N-1})-f(\eta)\,]\,\Big\}\;.

In this formula,
\eta^{x}
represents the configuration obtained from

\eta
by flipping the occupation variable at site
x
:

\displaystyle\eta^{x}(z)=\begin{cases}\eta(z)&\mbox{ if }z\neq x\;,\\
1-\eta(x)&\mbox{ if }z=x\;,\\
\end{cases}

and the rates
r_{L}
,
r_{R}
are given by

\displaystyle r_{L}(\eta)=\eta(-N+1)\,(\,1-{{\mathfrak{a}}}\,)\,+\,[\,1-\eta(-N+1)\,]\,{{\mathfrak{a}}}\;,

\displaystyle r_{R}(\eta)=\eta(N-1)\,(\,1-{{\mathfrak{b}}}\,)\,+\,[\,1-\eta(N-1)\,]\,{{\mathfrak{b}}}\;.

Note that the bulk dynamics has been accelerated by
N^{2}
, while the
boundary one by
N
. Denote by
(\eta^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the Markov
process associated to the generator
L_{N}
. For a smooth function

\rho\colon\Omega\to]0,1[
, let
\nu_{\rho(\cdot)}^{N}
be the
Bernoulli product measure on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}
with marginals given by:

\displaystyle\nu_{\rho(\cdot)}^{N}(\eta(x)=1)=\rho(x/N)\;,\quad x\in\Lambda_{N}\;.

When the function
\rho
is constant equal to
{\mathfrak{c}}\in[0,1]
, we
denote
\nu_{\rho(\cdot)}^{N}
by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\nu_{{\mathfrak{c}}}^{N}
. An
elementary computation shows that
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{N}
is a reversible
stationary state for the Markov chain
\eta^{N}_{t}
when
{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathfrak{b}}
.

For a metric space
\mathcal{E}
and
T>0
, denote by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}D([0,T],\mathcal{E})
, the space of right-continuous
functions with left limits
{\bm{x}}\colon[0,T]\to{\mathcal{E}}
endowed with
the Skorohod topology and its Borel
\sigma
-algebra. For a
probability measure
\mu
on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}
, let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mu}
be the measure on

D([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}}_{N})
induced by the continuous-time Markov process
associated to the generator
L_{N}
starting from
\mu
. Expectation
with respect to
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mu}
is denoted by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{E}^{N}_{\mu}
.

Gradient condition

For
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\{x,x+1\}\subset\Lambda_{N}
, denote by

j_{x,x+1}
the instantaneous current over the bond
(x,x+1)
. This is
the rate at which a particle jumps from
x
to
x+1
minus the rate at
which it jumps from
x+1
to
x
:

j_{x,x+1}(\eta)=r_{x,x+1}(\eta)\,[\,\eta(x)-\eta(x+1)\,]\;.

By definition of the rates
r_{x,x+1}
, the current is given by

(2.1)

j_{x,x+1}(\eta)=[\,\eta(x)-\eta(x+1)\,]\,+\,a\,(\,\tau_{x-1}f_{1}-\tau_{x+1}f_{1}\,)\,+\,a\,(\,\tau_{x}f_{2}-\tau_{x-1}f_{2}\,)\;,

where
\{\tau_{x}:x\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
stands for the group of translations,

(\tau_{x}\eta)(y)=\eta(x+y)
,
y\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and

(\tau_{x}f)(\eta)=f(\tau_{x}\eta)
,

f_{1}(\eta)\,=\,\eta(0)\,\eta(1)\;,\quad f_{2}(\eta)\,=\,\eta(0)\,\eta(2)\;.

Note that the instantaneous current can be written as a sum of
functions minus their translations. This property is called the
gradient condition, cf.
[
19
, Chapter 7]
. We carefully defined the
jump rates
r_{x,x+1}(\eta)
in order to fullfil this condition.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Omega
the open set
]-1,1[
. Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathcal{M}
be the set of non-negative measures on

\Omega
with total mass bounded by 2 endowed with the weak topology.
For a continuous function
F\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
and a
measure
\pi\in\mathcal{M}
,
\langle\pi,F\rangle
represents the integral of

F
with respect to
\pi
:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\langle\pi,F\rangle}=\int_{\Omega}F(x)\,\pi(dx)\;.

Denote by
\pi^{N}=\pi^{N}(\eta)
,
\eta\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}
, the empirical
measure obtained by assigning mass
1/N
to each particle of a
configuration
\eta
:

(2.2)

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\pi^{N}}}\,=\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\eta(x)\,\delta_{x/N}\;\;\text{and let}\;\;{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi^{N}_{t}}=\pi^{N}(\eta^{N}_{t})\,:=\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\eta^{N}_{t}(x)\,\delta_{x/N}\;,

where
\delta_{y}
represents the Dirac mass at
y
.

For an open subset
{\mathcal{A}}
of
\mathbb{R}
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}C^{m}({\mathcal{A}})
,

1\leq m\leq+\infty
, stands for the space of real functions
defined on
{\mathcal{A}}
that are
m
times continuously differentiable. Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}C^{m}_{0}({\mathcal{A}})
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}C_{K}^{m}({\mathcal{A}})
,

1\leq m\leq+\infty
, be the subset of functions in
C^{m}({\mathcal{A}})

which vanish at the boundary of
{\mathcal{A}}
, with compact support in

{\mathcal{A}}
, respectively.

Fix a function
G
in
C^{3}(\Omega)
, which can be extended
continuously, as well as its derivatives to
\overline{\Omega}
. By
the gradient condition (
2.1
), which permits two summation by
parts and, in consequence, a Taylor expansion of
G
up to the second
order, providing in this way a factor
1/N^{2}
,

\displaystyle L_{N}\langle\pi^{N},G\rangle\;=\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x=-N+2}^{N-2}(\Delta G)(x/N)\,\big\{\,\eta(x)+2\,a\,\tau_{x}f_{1}\,-\,a\,\tau_{x}f_{2}\,\big\}\;+\;B_{N}\;+\;R_{N}\;,

where
B_{N}=B^{L}_{N}+B^{R}_{N}
is the boundary term

\displaystyle B^{L}_{N}\;

\displaystyle=\;G(-1)\,[\,{\mathfrak{a}}-\eta(-N+1)\,]\;+\;(\nabla G)(-1)\,\eta(-N+2)

\displaystyle+\,a\,(\nabla G)(-1)\,\big\{\,\tau_{-N+1}\,f_{1}\,+\,\tau_{-N+2}\,f_{1}\,-\,\tau_{-N+1}\,f_{2}\,\big\}

\displaystyle-\,(\nabla G)(-1)\,\big[\,\eta(-N+2)-\eta(-N+1)\,\big]\,\big\{\,1+a\,{\mathfrak{a}}+a\eta(-N+3)\,\big\}\;,

\displaystyle B^{R}_{N}\;

\displaystyle=\;G(1)\,[\,{\mathfrak{b}}-\eta(N-1)\,]\;-\;(\nabla G)(1)\,\eta(N-2)

\displaystyle-\,a(\nabla G)(1)\,\big\{\,\tau_{N-2}\,f_{1}\,+\,\tau_{N-3}\,f_{1}\,-\,\tau_{N-3}\,f_{2}\,\big\}

\displaystyle-\,(\nabla G)(1)\,\big[\,\eta(N-1)-\eta(N-2)\,\big]\,\big\{\,1+a\,{\mathfrak{b}}+a\eta(N-3)\,\big\}\;,

and
R_{N}
is a remainder whose absolute value is bounded by
C_{0}(G)/N

for some finite constant
C_{0}
which depends on
G
but not on

N
. All constants below are allowed to depend on the parameters
a
,

{\mathfrak{a}}
,
{\mathfrak{b}}
with any mention to that in the notation. In the
previous formula,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Delta G
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\nabla G

stand for the Laplacian, gradient of
G
, respectively.

Heuristics

Consider the martingale
M_{N}(t)
defined by

\displaystyle M_{N}(t)\;=\;\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G\rangle\,-\,\langle\pi^{N}_{0},G\rangle\,-\,\int_{0}^{t}L_{N}\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G\rangle\,ds\;.

where

is a smooth function. A computation
of its quadratic variation, similar to the one performed in

[

yields that it vanishes in

as

.

For a function

which depends only
on a finite number of coordinates, let
\widehat{f}\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}

be given by

\displaystyle\widehat{f}({\mathfrak{c}})\,=\,E_{\nu^{N}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}}[\,f\,]\;.

In this formula,
N
provided that
N
has been chosen sufficiently
large to contain the support of
f
.

If one is allowed to replace
(\tau_{x}f)(\eta^{N}_{s})
by
\widehat{f}(u(s,x/N))
for some measurable function

, which is essentially the content of the so-called
two blocks estimate, it follows from the convergence of the martingale

to

and from the computation of the previous subsection that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}G(x)\,u(t,x)\,dx\;-\;\int_{-1}^{1}G(x)\,u(0,x)\,dx\,-\,\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}(\Delta G)(x)\,P_{a}(u(s,x))\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\quad=\;\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\,G(-1)\,[\,{\mathfrak{a}}-u(s,-1)\,]\,+\,(\nabla G)(-1)\,P_{a}(u(s,-1))\,\Big\}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-\;\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\,G(1)\,[\,{\mathfrak{b}}-u(s,1)\,]\,+\,(\nabla G)(1)\,P_{a}(u(s,1))\,\Big\}\,ds\,+R_{N}\;,

where
P_{a}(z)=z+a^{2}z
. The last term in
B^{L}_{N}
,
B^{R}_{N}
does not
contribute because it has mean-zero with respect to all measures

\nu^{N}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
.

Integrating twice by parts yields that

is the solution of the
partial differential equation

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u\;=\;\nabla\big(D(u)\nabla u\,)\;,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\Omega\;,\\
&D(u)\nabla u=u-{\mathfrak{a}}\;,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\{-1\}\;,\\
&D(u)\nabla u={\mathfrak{b}}-u\;,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\{1\}\;,\end{aligned}\right.

where

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}D(\rho)=P^{\prime}_{a}(\rho)=1+2a\rho}\;.

Hydrodynamic limit

Let
\Pi_{N}\colon D([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}}_{N})\to D([0,T],\mathcal{M})
be the
map which associates to a trajectory
(\eta^{N}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T)
the
empirical measure trajectory
(\pi^{N}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T)
. For a
probability measure
\mu
in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{\mu}^{N}
stands
for the measure on
D([0,T],\mathcal{M})
given by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{Q}_{\mu}^{N}=\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{N}\,(\Pi^{N})^{-1}
.

A sequence of measures
(\mu^{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{N}
is said
to be associated to a density profile
\rho_{0}\colon\Omega\to[0,1]

if

(2.4)

\underset{N\to\infty}{\lim}\mu_{N}\Big[\,\Big|\,\langle\pi_{N}\,,\,G\rangle\,-\,\int_{\Omega}G(x)\,\rho_{0}(x)\,dx\,\Big|>\delta\,\Big]\,=\,0

for all continuous functions
G\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
and

\delta>0
. The first main result of this article establishes the
hydrodynamic limit of the empirical measure.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}C^{m,n}([0,T]\times[-1,1])
,

1\leq n,m\leq+\infty
, the space of functions

G\colon[0,T]\times[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
with
m
continuous
derivatives in time and
n
continuous derivatives in space.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathcal{H}}^{1}={\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\Omega)
be the Sobolev space of
measurable functions
G\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with generalized
derivatives
\nabla G
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The space
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
endowed
with the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1}
, defined by

(2.5)

\langle G\,,\,H\rangle_{1}\;:=\;\langle G\,,\,H\rangle\;+\;\langle\nabla G\,,\,\nabla H\rangle\;,

is a Hilbert space. The corresponding norm is denoted by

\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}
:

\|G\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}^{2}\;:=\;\int_{-1}^{1}|G(x)|^{2}\;dx\;+\;\int_{-1}^{1}|\nabla G(x)|^{2}\;dx\;.

Recall from
[
14
]
that any function
H
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
has a

Definition 2.1
.

Fix

\rho_{0}\colon[-1,1]\to[0,1]

(b)

For every function

\displaystyle\langle u_{T},H_{T}\rangle\,-\,\langle u_{0},H_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle u_{s},\partial_{s}H_{s}\rangle\,ds\,-\,\int_{0}^{T}\langle D(u_{s})\,\nabla u_{s},\nabla H_{s}\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}[\,{{\mathfrak{a}}}-\rho_{s}(-1)\,]\,H_{s}(-1)\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}[\,{{\mathfrak{b}}}-\rho_{s}(1)\,]\,H_{s}(1)\,ds\;.

The rate functional

Fix
T>0
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{0}
be the subset of
\mathcal{M}
of all
absolutely continuous measures with respect to the Lebesgue measure
with positive density bounded by 1:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathcal{M}^{0}}\,:=\,\{\pi\in\mathcal{M}\mbox{ : }\pi(dx)=\gamma(x)dx\mbox{ and }0\leq\gamma(x)\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. }\}\;.

Definition 2.3
.

Denote by
\mathcal{Q}\colon D([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{0})\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
the
energy given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\pi)\,=\,\underset{G}{\sup}\,\Big\{\,2\int_{0}^{T}\langle u_{t},\nabla G_{t}\rangle\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\chi(u(t,x))\,G(t,x)^{2}\,dx\,dt\,\Big\}\;,

where
\pi(t,dx)=u(t,x)\,dx
, the supremum is carried over all
functions
G\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times[-1,1])
and

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\chi(a)=a(1-a)
is the static compressibility of the
exclusion process.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}D_{{\mathcal{E}}}([0,T],{\mathcal{M}}^{0})
be the trajectories in

D([0,T],{\mathcal{M}}^{0})
with finite energy. Fix
0\leq r<s\leq T
, and
a trajectory
\pi\in D_{{\mathcal{E}}}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{0})
,

\pi(t,du)=u(t,x)dx
. Denote by

L_{[r,s]}\colon C^{1,1}([0,T]\times[-1,1])\to\mathbb{R}
the
linear functional given by

(2.6)

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{[r,s]}(H)}\,=\,\langle\pi_{s},H_{s}\rangle\,-\,\langle\pi_{r},H_{r}\rangle\,-\,\int_{r}^{s}\langle\pi_{t},\partial_{t}H_{t}\rangle\,dt\;.

When
r=0
,
s=T
, we represent
L_{[s,t]}
by
L_{0}
:

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{0}(H)=L_{[0,T]}(H)
.

For each
H\in C^{1,1}([r,s]\times[-1,1])
, let

\hat{J}_{H}=\hat{J}_{H,[r,s]}\colon D_{{\mathcal{E}}}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{0})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the functional given by:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{H}(\pi)\,

\displaystyle=\,L_{[r,s]}(H)\,+\,\int_{r}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}D(u(t,x))\,\nabla u(t,x)\,\nabla H(t,x)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle-\,\int_{r}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}\sigma(u(t,x))\,(\nabla H(t,x))^{2}\,dx\,dt\,-\ \int_{r}^{s}B(u_{t},H_{t})\,dt\;,

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\sigma(a)=\chi(a)\,D(a)
is the mobility, and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\displaystyle B(u_{t},H_{t})}\,:=\,u_{t}(1)\,(1-{{\mathfrak{b}}})\,\left(e^{-H(t,1)}-1\right)\,+\,{{\mathfrak{b}}}\,[1-u_{t}(1)]\,\left(e^{H(t,1)}-1\right)

\displaystyle+u_{t}(-1)\,(1-{{\mathfrak{a}}})\,\left(e^{-H(t,-1)}-1\right)\,+\,{{\mathfrak{a}}}\,[1-u_{t}(-1)]\,\left(e^{H(t,-1)}-1\right)\;.

We extend the definition of the functional
\hat{J}_{H}
to the space

D([0,T],\mathcal{M})
by setting

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}J_{H}(\pi)}\;=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{J}_{H}(\pi)&\mbox{if }\pi\in D_{{\mathcal{E}}}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{0})\;,\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise }\;.\end{array}\right.

Fix a profile
\gamma\colon\Omega\to[0,1]
and
0\leq r<s\leq T
. Denote by
I_{[r,s]}\colon D([0,T],\mathcal{M})\to[0,+\infty]

the functional given by

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{[r,s]}(\pi)}\,:=\,\underset{H\in C^{1,1}([r,s]\times[-1,1])}{\sup}\,J_{H}(\pi)\;,

and let

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{[r,s]}(\pi|\gamma)}\;=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}I_{[r,s]}(\pi)&\mbox{if }\pi_{0}(dx)\,=\,\gamma(x)\,dx\;,\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise }\;.\end{array}\right.

Theorem 2.4
.

Fix
T>0
and a measurable function
\gamma\colon[-1,1]\to[0,1]
.
The functional

I_{[0,T]}(\cdot|\gamma)\colon D([0,T],\mathcal{M})\to[0,\infty]

lower semicontinuous and has compact level sets.

For

Theorem 2.5
.

Fix

D(\gamma(-1))\,\nabla\gamma(-1)\,=\,\gamma(-1)-{\mathfrak{a}}\;,\quad D(\gamma(1))\,\nabla\gamma(1)\,=\,{\mathfrak{b}}-\gamma(1)\;,

and a sequence of configurations

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{Q}_{\eta^{N}}(C)\leq-\inf_{\pi\in C}I_{[0,T]}(\pi|\gamma)

Comments

Since the first papers on the subject
[
12
,
20
]
, robust methods
and tools have been developed to derive dynamical large deviations
principles for the empirical measures of diffusive interacting
particle systems
[
19
,
24
,
4
,
15
,
14
]
. With these tools,
the proof of the large deviations principle boils down, essentially,
to the
I_{[0,T]}(\cdot|\gamma)
-density of a set of smooth
trajectories.

The proof of the
I_{[0,T]}(\cdot|\gamma)
-density of the set

\Pi_{\gamma}
, introduced in Definition
2.8
, presents two
novelties which might be helpful for other dynamics, as
reaction-diffusion models without the concavity assumption on the
creation and destruction rates
[
17
,
6
,
16
]
.

Here, as in
[
24
,
4
,
15
]
, the rate functional

I_{[0,T]}(\cdot|\gamma)
is not convex. The lack of convexity
complicates the second step in the proof of the

I_{[0,T]}(\cdot|\gamma)
-density, handled in Lemma
6.3
. There,
we approximate a trajectory
\pi(t,dx)=u(t,x)dx
by

\pi^{\epsilon}(t,dx)=u^{\epsilon}(t,x)dx
, where

u^{\epsilon}=(1-\epsilon)u+\epsilon\rho
and
\rho
is the
solution of the hydrodynamic equation. If
I_{[0,T]}(\cdot|\gamma)

were convex, to proof that
I_{[0,T]}(\pi^{\epsilon}|\gamma)
converges
to
I_{[0,T]}(\pi|\gamma)
would be straightforward. One direction
would be obtained by the lower semi-continuity of the functional and
the other from the convexity.

The proof of this step presented in
[
24
,
4
]
(cf.
[
4
, Lemma
5.5]
) for non-convex rate functionals is based on the
representation of a linear functionals given in
[
4
, Lemma 4.8]
,
and relies on Lemmata 5.2 and 5.3 in
[
4
]
. We present here a
much simpler and direct proof which might be useful in other contexts.

The second difficulty in the proof of the

I_{[0,T]}(\cdot|\gamma)
-density arises, as in
[
14
]
, from the
presence of a boundary term in the rate
functional. Propositions
5.4
–
5.7
state that the cost

I_{[0,T]}(\pi)
of a trajectory
\pi\in D([0,T],{\mathcal{M}})
can be
expressed as the sum
I^{(1)}_{[0,T]}(\pi)+I^{(2)}_{[0,T]}(\pi)
,
where the first term accounts for the cost due to evolution in the
interior of the interval
[-1,1]
, while the second one for the
evolution at the boundary.

This decomposition is needed in Lemma
6.5
for trajectories
bounded away from
0
and
1
and presented in Propositions
5.4
,

5.5
under this assumption. In
[
14
]
(cf. equation (4.2))
the trajectory is assumed to be continuous and smooth in time. The
lack of regularity of the trajectory is responsible for the presence
of cross terms and a convoluted expression for the rate
functionals. Assuming regularity, Propositions
5.7
presents a
similar formula to the one obtained
[
14
]
.

The decomposition of the rate functional requires the following
property of trajectories. Fix a path
\pi(t,dx)=u(t,x)\,dx
in

D([0,T],{\mathcal{M}})
with finite rate function,
I_{[0,T]}(u)<\infty
.
It is known, at least since
[
4
]
, that such a path is weakly
continuous in time. More precisely, that for each

,
the map
t\mapsto\langle\pi_{t},g\rangle
is uniformly continuous (cf. Lemma

4.3
). Proposition
4.11
asserts that this map is,
actually, absolutely continuous and its derivative belongs to

. This property permits an integration by parts on the right-hand
side of (

for some

5.
Deconstructing the rate functional

The main results of this section, stated in Propositions

and

5.5
below, show that the rate function
I_{[0,T]}(\,\cdot\,)

can be decomposed as the sum of two rate functions. The first one
measures the cost of the trajectory due to its evolution in the bulk,
while the second one measures the costs due to the boundary
evolution. This decomposition of the rate function is an important
tool in the proof that any trajectory

can be approximated by a sequence of regular trajectories

(u^{n}:n\geq 1)
in such a way that

, the content of
the next section.

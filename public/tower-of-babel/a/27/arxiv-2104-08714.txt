Tensor product weight modules for the mirror-twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
Dongfang Gao, Kaiming Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we study irreducible weight modules with infinite dimensional weight spaces over the mirror-twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra 𝒟 \mathcal{D} . More precisely, the necessary and sufficient conditions for the tensor products of irreducible highest weight modules and irreducible modules of intermediates series over 𝒟 \mathcal{D} to be irreducible are determined by using “shifting technique”. This leads to a family of new irreducible weight modules over 𝒟 \mathcal{D} . Then we obtain that any two such tensor products are isomorphic if and only if the corresponding highest weight modules and modules of intermediate series are isomorphic respectively. Also we discuss submodules of the tensor product module when it is not irreducible.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

In this section we recall some notations and collect some results for the future convenience.

Definition 2.1
.

The
mirror-twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

\mathcal{D}
is a Lie algebra with basis

\{d_{m},h_{r},{\bf{c}},{\bf{l}}|m\in{\mathbb{Z}},r\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}\}
defined by the following commutation relations

\begin{split}&[d_{m},d_{n}]=(m-n)d_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}{\delta}_{m+n,0}{{\bf{c}}},\\
&[d_{m},h_{r}]=-rh_{m+r},\\
&[h_{r},h_{s}]=r{\delta}_{r+s,0}{\bf{l}},\\
&[{\bf{c}},\mathcal{D}]=[{\bf{l}},\mathcal{D}]=0,\end{split}

for
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}},r,s\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The Lie subalgebra spanned by
\{d_{m},{\bf{c}}|m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
is the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
,
and the Lie subalgebra spanned by
\{h_{r},{\bf{l}}|r\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}\}

is the twisted Heisenberg algebra
\mathcal{H}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{H}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{D}

and
\mathcal{D}
is the semi-direct product of
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{H}
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H}
are all
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebras, and their universal enveloping algebras are also
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded. The homogeneous spaces of
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D})
are actually the eigenspaces with respect to
-d_{0}
. We call the eigenvalue of a homogeneous element the
degree
, say
\deg(x)=k
if
x\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D})_{k}
for
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
.

Note that
\mathcal{D}
has the triangular decomposition:

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{-},

where

\mathcal{D}^{\pm}=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathbb{C}}d_{\pm n}\oplus\bigoplus_{r\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{\mathbb{C}}h_{\pm r},\quad\mathcal{D}^{0}={\mathbb{C}}d_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}{\bf{c}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}{\bf{l}}.

Similar,
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{H}
also have the triangular decompositions.
For convenience, we define the following notations for
r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2},z\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(r)}=\bigoplus_{i=r+1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{C}}d_{i}+{\mathbb{C}}{\bf{c}},\quad\mathcal{D}^{(r,s)}=\bigoplus_{i=r+1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{C}}d_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=s}^{\infty}{\mathbb{C}}h_{\frac{1}{2}+j}+{\mathbb{C}}{\bf{c}}+{\mathbb{C}}{\bf{l}},

\displaystyle\delta_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}=\begin{cases}&1\quad\text{ if }z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\
&0\quad\text{ if }z\notin{\mathbb{Z}},\end{cases}\ \ \,\,\,\,\delta_{z\notin{\mathbb{Z}}}=\begin{cases}&0\quad\text{ if }z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\
&1\quad\text{ if }z\notin{\mathbb{Z}}.\end{cases}

\bf{module}

Note that if

is a
\mathcal{V}
-module,

, for any

\displaystyle d_{n}\mapsto\frac{1}{2l}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{1}{2}}h_{n-k}h_{k},\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},n\neq 0,

(2.1)

\displaystyle d_{0}\mapsto\frac{1}{2l}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{1}{2}}h_{-|k|}h_{|k|}+\frac{1}{16},

(2.2)

\displaystyle h_{r}\mapsto h_{r},\quad\forall r\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}},\quad{\bf{c}}\mapsto 1,\quad{\bf{l}}\mapsto l.

2.1.
Modules of intermediate series

Definition 2.4
.

A weight
\mathcal{D}
-module
V
is called a module of
\bf{intermediate}

\bf{series}

if all its nonzero weight spaces are one-dimensional.

Recall that for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}\text{ and }\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,

A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\oplus_{k\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}v_{k}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module with the actions defined by

\begin{split}&d_{m}v_{k}=(\alpha+\beta m-k)v_{m+k},\\
&h_{r}v_{n}=v_{n+r},\\
&h_{r}v_{s}=\gamma v_{r+s},\\
&{\bf{c}}v_{k}={\bf{l}}v_{k}=0,\end{split}

(2.4)

for
{m,n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
r,s\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}
,
k\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let us recall the
\mathcal{V}
-module of the intermediate series
A(\alpha,\beta)=\oplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}v_{k}
:

d_{m}v_{k}=(\alpha+\beta m-k)v_{m+k},\,\,\,{\bf{c}}v_{k}=0,

(2.5)

for
{m,k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We denote by
A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)
the unique nontrivial irreducible subquotient module of
A(\alpha,\beta)
.
It is clear that
A(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module
if and only if
A(\alpha,\beta)
is an irreducible
\mathcal{V}
-module.
Moreover, we have the following well-known results:

Lemma 2.5
.

(1)

The
\mathcal{V}
-module
A(\alpha,\beta)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{V}
-module
A(\alpha+m,\beta)
for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(2)

The
\mathcal{V}
-module
A(\alpha,\beta)
is irreducible if and only if

\alpha\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
or
\beta\not=0,-1
.

(3)

If
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\beta=0
, then
A(\alpha,\beta)
has a unique nontrivial irreducible quotient module.

(4)

If
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\beta=-1
, then
A(\alpha,\beta)
has a unique nontrivial irreducible submodule.

(5)

A^{\prime}(\alpha,0)\cong A^{\prime}(\alpha,-1)
for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

We have the classification for all irreducible modules of intermediate series over
\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(cf.
[
17
, Theorem 3.9]
)
If
V
is an irreducible module of intermediate series over
\mathcal{D}
,
then
V\cong A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
or
V\cong A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}

for some
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

2.2.
Verma modules

Definition 2.7
.

A weight
\mathcal{D}
-module
V
is called a

highest weight module
with highest weight
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
if there exists a nonzero weight vector
v\in V_{\lambda}
such that
V
is generated by
v
as
\mathcal{D}
-module and
\mathcal{D}^{+}v=0
.

Definition 2.8
.

For
(c,h,l)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
, let
{\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}
be a
(\mathcal{D}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{+})
-module with

d_{0}{\bf 1}=h{\bf 1},\quad{\bf{c}{\bf 1}}=c{\bf 1},\quad\mathbf{l}\cdot{{\bf 1}}=l{\bf 1},\quad\mathcal{D}^{+}{\bf 1}=0.

The
Verma module
over
{\mathcal{D}}
is defined by

M(c,h,l)=U(\mathcal{D})\otimes_{U(\mathcal{D}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{+})}{\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}.

Let
J(c,h,l)
be the unique maximal submodule of
M(c,h,l)
.
Then we have the irreducible highest weight module
L(c,h,l)=M(c,h,l)/J(c,h,l)
.
We will use the
{\bf 1}
and
{\overline{\bf 1}}
to denote the highest weight vectors
of
M(c,h,l)
and
L(c,h,l)
respectively.
Similarly, we have
M_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h),L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)=M_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)/J_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)
for the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
,
and
M_{\mathcal{H}}(l),L_{\mathcal{H}}(l)=M_{\mathcal{H}}(l)/J_{\mathcal{H}}(l)
for the twisted Heisenberg algebra
\mathcal{H}
.
It is clear that they are all restricted modules.
Moreover, we have the following results about highest weight modules over
\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

(cf.
[
17
, Proposition 5.1]
)
Let
c,h\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
l\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then we have
\mathcal{D}
-module isomorphisms

\displaystyle\pi_{1}:M(c,h,l)\rightarrow M_{{\mathcal{V}}}(c-1,h-\frac{1}{16})^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes M_{\mathcal{H}}(l)^{\mathcal{D}},

\displaystyle\pi_{2}:L(c,h,l)\rightarrow L_{{\mathcal{V}}}(c-1,h-\frac{1}{16})^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes M_{\mathcal{H}}(l)^{\mathcal{D}}.

Lemma 2.10
.

(cf.
[
17
, Corollary 5.2]
)
Let
c,h\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
l\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
Then the Verma module
M(c,h,l)
is irreducible if and only if

\phi_{r,s}(c-1,h-\frac{1}{16})\neq 0
for all
r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, where

\displaystyle\phi_{r,s}(c,h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h-\frac{1}{48}(13-c)(r^{2}+s^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{48}\left(\sqrt{(c-1)(c-25)}(r^{2}-s^{2})-24rs-2+2c\right).

Lemma 2.11
.

(cf.
[
18

)
Let

be a Lie algebra over

For any

.

(cf.
[

)
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie algebra over

with a countable basis, and

be

Suppose that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

The module

is irreducible and

and all finite subsets

(1)

of

(2)

3.
Irreducibility of tensor product weight modules

From Section 2, we know that all irreducible modules of intermediate series over
\mathcal{D}

are
A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}
or
A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
where
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
In this section, we will determine the necessary and sufficient conditions
for the tensor product modules
L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}},L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
to be irreducible
for any
c,h,l,\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
V,W
are
\mathcal{V}
-modules, and
H,K\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}}
are irreducible with nonzero action of
\bf{l}
.
Then

(1)

any
\mathcal{D}
-submodule of
V^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes H^{\mathcal{D}}
is of the
form
(V^{\prime})^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes H^{\mathcal{D}}
for some
\mathcal{V}
-submodule
V^{\prime}
of

V
. In particular,
V^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes H^{\mathcal{D}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module
if and only if
V
is an irreducible
\mathcal{V}
-module;

(2)

V^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes H^{\mathcal{D}}\cong W^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes K^{\mathcal{D}}
if and
only if
V\cong W
and
H\cong K
.

Proof.

(1) Note that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq Ann_{\mathcal{D}}({V^{\mathcal{D}}})
, and
H^{\mathcal{D}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{H}
-module.
Thus the result follows from Lemma
2.12
.

(2) It is clear for the sufficiency.

Now we show the necessity. Suppose that
\varphi:V^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes H^{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow W^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes K^{\mathcal{D}}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-module isomorphism.
Let’s fix a nonzero
x_{0}\in H
.
For any
0\neq v\in\mathcal{V}
,
we write
\varphi(v\otimes x_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}w_{i}\otimes y_{i}
with minimal
t
.
We know that
y_{i}
’s are linearly independent.
Then there exists some
u_{0}\in\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}
such that
u_{0}y_{i}=\delta_{1,i}y_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,t
by Lemma
2.11
.
Therefore,

\varphi(v\otimes uu_{0}x_{0})=\varphi(uu_{0}(v\otimes x_{0}))=w_{1}\otimes uy_{1},\forall u\in\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}.

Denote
x_{1}=u_{0}x_{0}
. Then we have
\varphi(v\otimes ux_{1})=w_{1}\otimes uy_{1},\forall u\in\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}.

Notice
\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}x_{1}=H,\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}y_{1}=K
, since
H,K
are irreducible.
Define
\varphi_{v}:H\rightarrow K
by
\varphi_{v}(ux_{1})=uy_{1},
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}
.
It is easy to see that
\varphi_{v}
is a
\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}
-module isomorphism.
That shows
H\cong K
as
\mathcal{H}
-modules.

At the moment, we can assume that
H=K
.
Then
\varphi
is an isomorphism
from
V^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes H^{\mathcal{D}}
to
W^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes H^{\mathcal{D}}
,
and
\varphi_{v}
is an automorphism of
H
.
In fact,
\varphi_{v}
is a scalar denoted by
c_{v}
,
since
H
is irreducible and
\mathcal{U}{(\mathcal{H})}
is countably generated.
Then for any
v\in V,x\in H
,

\varphi(v\otimes x)=c_{v}w_{v}\otimes x,

for some
w_{v}\in H
.
Define the map
\varpi:V\rightarrow W
by
v\rightarrow c_{v}w_{v}
.
It’s clear that
\varpi
is bijective.
Furthermore, we have that
d_{n}\varpi(v)\otimes x=\varpi(d_{n}v)\otimes x
,
since
d_{n}\varphi(v\otimes x)=\varphi(d_{n}(v\otimes x)),
for all
v\in V,x\in H
.
Therefore,
d_{n}\varpi(v)=\varpi(d_{n}v)
,
that means
\varpi
is a
\mathcal{V}
-module isomorphism.
So,
V\cong W
as
\mathcal{V}
-modules.
We complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
c,h,l,\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

(1)

Suppose
l\neq 0
. Then
L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module
if and only if
L_{\mathcal{V}}(c-1,h-\frac{1}{16})\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)
is an irreducible
\mathcal{V}
-module.

(2)

Suppose
l=0
. Then
L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module
if and only if
L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)
is an irreducible
\mathcal{V}
-module.

(3)

Suppose
l=0
, then
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is irreducible.

Proof.

(1) From Lemma
2.9
we know that

L(c,h,l)\cong L_{\mathcal{V}}(c-1,h-\frac{1}{16})^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes M_{\mathcal{H}}(l)^{\mathcal{D}}.

So we have

L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}\cong M_{\mathcal{H}}(l)^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes(L_{\mathcal{V}}(c-1,h-\frac{1}{16})\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta))^{\mathcal{D}}.

It is clear that
M_{\mathcal{H}}(l)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}}
is irreducible when
l\neq 0
.
Therefore, the result follows from Lemma
3.1
.

(2) Since
l=0
, we see that
L(c,h,l)\cong L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)^{\mathcal{D}}
as
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
Thus

L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}\cong L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}\cong(L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta))^{\mathcal{D}}.

Then the result is clear.

(3) Since
l=0
, we have
L(c,h,l)\cong L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)^{\mathcal{D}}
as
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
Notice that for any finite subset
S\subseteq L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)^{\mathcal{D}}
,
there exists some
r
such that
\mathcal{V}^{(r)}+\mathcal{H}\subseteq Ann_{\mathcal{D}}(S)
.
Moreover, we see that the module
A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
defined in (
2.4
) is irreducible as
(\mathcal{V}^{(r)}+\mathcal{H})
-module by the following two observations:

(a)

Each
v_{k}
can generate
A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
as an
\mathcal{H}
-submodule;

(b)

h_{\frac{1}{2}}h_{-m-\frac{1}{2}}d_{m}v_{k}=\gamma(\alpha-k+m\beta)v_{k}
for any
m>r
.

So the result follows from Lemma
2.12
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In the above theorem, we reduce the irreducibility of
\mathcal{D}
-module
L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)^{\mathcal{D}}

to the irreducibility of the
\mathcal{V}
-module
L_{\mathcal{V}}(c,h)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)

or
L_{\mathcal{V}}(c-1,h-\frac{1}{16})\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta)
.
Thanks to
[
5
]
,
the irreducibility of the
\mathcal{V}
-module

has been completely determined for

.

Now we have only the most difficult case left:
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
with

. To determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for this tensor product module to be irreducible we will break the arguments into several lemmas.
From now on we assume that
c,h,\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},l,\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, and we will use the definition for

in (

Lemma 3.4
.

(a).
For any
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
,

{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}\notin U(\mathcal{D})({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\subseteq M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma).

(b). The
\mathcal{D}
-module
M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is not irreducible.

Proof.

(a).
Note that for any
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
,

U(\mathcal{D})({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{1}{2}})=U(\mathcal{D}^{-})U(\mathcal{D}^{+})({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\subseteq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}U(\mathcal{D}^{-})({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}}).

So, it is sufficient to show that
{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}\notin\displaystyle{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}}U(\mathcal{D}^{-})({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}}).

Assume that
{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}\in\displaystyle{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}}U(\mathcal{D}^{-})({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}})
.
Then by PBW Theorem we know that

{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{-\frac{i}{2}}({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{i}{2}}),

where
u_{-\frac{i}{2}}\in U(\mathcal{D}^{-})_{-\frac{i}{2}}
with
u_{-\frac{r}{2}}\neq 0
.
Thus we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{-\frac{i}{2}}({\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{i}{2}})-{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}=0,

i.e.,

\sum_{i=1}^{r}(u_{-\frac{i}{2}}{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{i}{2}})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}({\bf{1}}\otimes(u_{-\frac{i}{2}}v_{k+\frac{i}{2}})-{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}=0.

This is impossible since the nonzero vector

u_{-\frac{r}{2}}{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{r}{2}}
cannot be in the span of the other vectors in the above formula.

(b) follows directly from (a).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
M
be a nonzero
\mathcal{D}
-submodule of
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
.

(a). There exists
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
such that
\bar{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}\in M
.

(b). There exists
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
such that
\bar{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{i}\in M
for all
i\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
with
i\geq k
.

Proof.

(a). Since
M
is a weight
\mathcal{D}
-module, we can take a nonzero weight vector

w=\sum_{i=0}^{s}w_{-\frac{i}{2}}\otimes v_{k+\frac{i}{2}}\in M,\text{ with }w_{0}=\bar{\bf{1}},k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2},w_{-\frac{i}{2}}\in U(\mathcal{D}^{-})_{-\frac{i}{2}}\bar{\bf{1}}.

There is an
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

d_{j}w_{-\frac{i}{2}}=h_{\frac{1}{2}+j}w_{-\frac{i}{2}}=0
for all
j\geq n,i=0,1,\cdots,s
.
Since
A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is an irreducible
(\mathcal{V}^{(n)}+\mathcal{H})
-module,
by Lemma
2.11
we may take some
u\in U(\mathcal{V}^{(n)}+\mathcal{H})

such that

uv_{k+\frac{i}{2}}=\delta_{0,\frac{i}{2}}v_{0},\forall i=0,1,\ldots,s.

We can write
u=\sum_{i}u_{i}u_{i}^{\prime}
with
u_{i}\in U(\mathcal{H})
and
u_{i}^{\prime}\in U(\mathcal{V}^{(n)})
by PBW Theorem.
For any
v\in\ A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
, we have

\begin{split}\begin{cases}&h_{j}h_{i}v=h_{\frac{i+j}{2}}h_{\frac{i+j}{2}}v,\quad{\rm if}\quad i+j\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}+1,\\
&h_{j}h_{i}v=h_{\frac{i+j}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\frac{i+j}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}v,\quad{\rm if}\quad i+j\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}.\end{cases}\end{split}

Take a sufficiently large
r
. By repeatedly and properly replacing

h_{j}h_{i}
with
h_{\frac{i+j}{2}}h_{\frac{i+j}{2}}

or
h_{\frac{i+j}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\frac{i+j}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}

in the expression of
h_{r}u
we can result in an element
u^{\prime}\in U(\mathcal{D}^{(n,n)})
such that

u^{\prime}v_{k+\frac{i}{2}}=h_{r}uv_{k+\frac{i}{2}}=\delta_{0,\frac{i}{2}}v_{r},\forall i=0,1,\ldots,s.

Since
u^{\prime}w_{-\frac{i}{2}}=0
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,s
, we deduce that
\bar{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{r}=u^{\prime}w\in M
.

(b). This follows by applying
h_{i}
to
\bar{\bf{1}}\otimes v_{k}\in M
in (a) for all
i\in\frac{{\mathbb{N}}}{2}
.
∎

Now, we need to introduce the “shifting technique”.
For any highest weight
\mathcal{D}
-module
V
with highest weight vector
{\bf v}
,
it is easy to check that
V\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]

can be endowed a
\mathcal{D}
-module structure by following actions

\begin{split}&d_{n}(P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i})=(d_{n}+\alpha+\beta n+\deg(P)-i)P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i+n},\\
&h_{r}(P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i})=(h_{r}+\delta_{i-\deg(P)\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{i-\deg(P)\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i+r},\\
&{\bf{c}}(P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i})=c(P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i}),\quad{\bf{l}}(P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i})=l(P{\bf{v}}\otimes y^{i}),\end{split}

(3.1)

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}},r\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}},i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
and homogeneous element
P\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})
.
Then it is not hard to see that

V\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\cong V\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]

as
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
Specially, we have

\displaystyle M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\cong M(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}],

\displaystyle L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\cong L(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}].

Henceforth we may view
M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
as

M(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}],L(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]
respectively.
Moreover, for any
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
,

M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k}=\{v\in M(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]\mid d_{0}v=(\alpha+h-k)v\},

thus
M(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]=\oplus_{i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}}(M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{i})
.
Denote

\begin{split}&W^{(k)}=\sum_{i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{2}}\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D})({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k+i})\subseteq M(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}],\\
&W^{(k)}_{n}=W^{(k)}\cap(M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{n}),\forall n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}.\end{split}

We have following observations:

Lemma 3.6
.

(1)

W^{(k)}=\sum_{i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{2}}U(\mathcal{D}^{-})({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k+i})
.

(2)

W^{(k)}\supseteq\oplus_{k\leq i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}}M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{i}
.

(3)

M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}=W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})
.

(4)

If
P\in U(\mathcal{D}^{-})
is homogeneous such that

P{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\in W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}},

then
(U(\mathcal{D}^{-})P{\bf{1}})\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from the following computations:

\begin{split}W^{(k)}&=\sum_{i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{2}}\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D})({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k+i})=\sum_{i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{2}}\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{+})({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k+i})\\
&=\sum_{i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{2}}\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})(\sum_{j\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{2}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k+i+j}))=\sum_{i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{2}}\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k+i}).\end{split}

(2) By PBW Theorem, each nonzero element of
M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{i}
can be written as linear combinations of vectors

v_{s,t}=h_{-p_{s}}\dots h_{-p_{1}}d_{-q_{t}}\dots d_{-q_{1}}{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i},

where
q_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}},p_{j^{\prime}}\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
for
1\leq j\leq t,1\leq j^{\prime}\leq s
.

Now, we show the result by induction on
s+t
.
If
s+t=0
, then
v_{s,t}={\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i}\in W^{(k)},\forall k\leq i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
is trivial.
Suppose that the result is right when
s+t=m
.
For
s+t=m+1
, we may assume that

v_{s,t}=h_{-r}Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i}\text{ where }Q\in U(\mathcal{D}^{-}),r>0,\text{ or }

v_{s,t}=d_{-n}Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i},\text{ where }Q\in U(\mathcal{V}^{-}),n>0.

Then by equations
3.1
and inductive hypothesis on
Q
, we have

\displaystyle v_{s,t}=

\displaystyle h_{-r}Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{-r}(Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i+r})-(\delta_{i+r-\deg(Q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{i+r-\deg(Q)\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i}\in W^{(k)},\text{ or }

\displaystyle v_{s,t}=

\displaystyle d_{-n}Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{-n}(Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i+n})-(\alpha-\beta n+\deg(Q)-i-n)Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i}\in W^{(k)}.

Thus
M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{i}\in W^{(k)},\forall k\leq i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
.

(3)
W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})\subseteq M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}
is clear by definition.

W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\cap{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})=0

follows from the Lemma
3.4
.

Now we show that

M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})
.
Note that every nonzero element of
M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}

can be written as linear combinations of vectors

v_{s,t}=h_{-p_{s}}\dots h_{-p_{1}}d_{-q_{t}}\dots d_{-q_{1}}{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}},

where
q_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}},p_{j^{\prime}}\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
for
1\leq j\leq t,1\leq j^{\prime}\leq s

by PBW Theorem.
By induction on
s+t
which is similar to (2), we obtain
v_{s,t}\in W^{(k)}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})
.
Thus
M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq W^{(k)}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})
.
Then
M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})
.

(4) By PBW Theorem, every nonzero element of
(U(\mathcal{D}^{-})P{\bf{1}})\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}

can be written as linear combinations of vectors

v_{s,t}=h_{-p_{s}}\dots h_{-p_{1}}d_{-q_{t}}\dots d_{-q_{1}}P{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}},

where
q_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}},p_{j^{\prime}}\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
for
1\leq j\leq t,1\leq j^{\prime}\leq s
.
By induction on
s+t
which is similar to (2), we obtain
v_{s,t}\in W^{(k)}
.
Thus
(U(\mathcal{D}^{-})P{\bf{1}})\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\in W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

Now, similar to the definition of
\varphi_{n}
in
[
5
]
,
we want to define the linear map
\rho_{n}:\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, for any
n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
.
Let
T(\mathcal{D}^{-})
denote the tensor algebra of
\mathcal{D}^{-}
, i.e.,

T(\mathcal{D}^{-})={\mathbb{C}}\oplus(\mathcal{D}^{-})\oplus(\mathcal{D}^{-}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{-})\oplus\cdots,

which is a free associative algebra.
For any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define
\deg(d_{-k})=-k,\deg(h_{\frac{1}{2}-k})=\frac{1}{2}-k
and
\deg(1)=0
,
then
T(\mathcal{D}^{-})
is a
\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
-graded algebra.
For any
n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2},\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
we may inductively define the linear map
\overline{\rho_{n}}:T(\mathcal{D}^{-})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{\rho_{n}}(1)=1,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\overline{\rho_{n}}(h_{-r}u)=-(\delta_{n+r+k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{n+r+k\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})\overline{\rho_{n}}(u),

\displaystyle\overline{\rho_{n}}(d_{-i}u)=-(\alpha-\beta i-k-i-n)\overline{\rho_{n}}(u),

where
r,i>0
and
u
is an arbitrary homogeneous element of degree
-k
in
T(\mathcal{D}^{-})
.
Note that
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})
is the quotient algebra
T(\mathcal{D}^{-})/I

where
I
is the two-sided ideal generated by

\displaystyle d_{-i}d_{-j}-d_{-j}d_{-i}-(j-i)d_{-i-j},

\displaystyle d_{-i}h_{-r}-h_{-r}d_{-i}-rh_{-i-r},

\displaystyle h_{-r}h_{-r^{\prime}}-h_{-r^{\prime}}h_{-r},

for
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}},r,r^{\prime}\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
It is not hard to see that
I\subseteq ker(\overline{\rho_{n}})
.
So
\overline{\rho_{n}}
induces a linear map
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, denoted by
\rho_{n}
.
Now we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-}),n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
. Then

(1)

P{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\equiv\rho_{n}(P){\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\pmod{W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}};

(2)

P{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\in W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}
if and only if
\rho_{n}(P)=0
.

Proof.

(1)
It is enough to show it for homogeneous
P
by induction on
\deg(P)
. If
\deg(P)=0
, i.e.
P\in{\mathbb{C}}
, then

P{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\equiv\rho_{n}(P){\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\pmod{W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}}

is trivial.
Suppose
Q\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})
is a homogeneous element
and the result holds for this
Q
.
For
k\in{\mathbb{N}},r\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, using Lemma
3.6
we have

\displaystyle d_{-k}(Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n+k})=(d_{-k}+\alpha-\beta k+\deg(Q)-n-k)Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\in W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})},

\displaystyle h_{-r}(Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n+r})=(h_{-r}+\delta_{n+r-\deg(Q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{n+r-\deg(Q)\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\in W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}.

By equations (
3.2
), these indicate that

\displaystyle d_{-k}Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv-(\alpha-\beta k+\deg(Q)-n-k)Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv-(\alpha-\beta k+\deg(Q)-n-k)\rho_{n}(Q){\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv\rho_{n}(d_{-k}Q){\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\pmod{W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}};

\displaystyle h_{-r}Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv-(\delta_{n+r-\deg(Q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{n+r-\deg(Q)\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})Q{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv-(\delta_{n+r-\deg(Q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{n+r-\deg(Q)\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})\rho_{n}(Q){\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv\rho_{n}(h_{-r}Q){\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\pmod{W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}}.

Then the result follows from induction on
\deg(Q)
.

(2)
It follows from
(1)
and Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose that
c,h,\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},l,\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)

is irreducible if and only if

(\rho_{n}(Q_{1}),\rho_{n}(Q_{2}))\neq(0,0)

for all
n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
,
where
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}^{-})
are in Lemma
3.9

Proof.

Let
J=U(\mathcal{D}^{-})Q_{1}{\bf{1}}+U(\mathcal{D}^{-})Q_{2}{\bf{1}}
be the unique maximal proper submodule of
M(c,h,l)
,

\pi
be the canonical map from
M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
to
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
.
First, we prove the following statement.

Claim 1
.

L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is irreducible if and only if

M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{n}=W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}_{n}+J\otimes y^{n}
for all
n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}.

(
\Rightarrow
) Since
W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})},J\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
are submodules of
M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
, and
W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}\nsubseteq J\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
,
we see that

J\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\subsetneq W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}+J\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\subseteq M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma).

But
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is irreducible,
so
W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}+J\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=M(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma).

Thus we have
M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{n}=W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}_{n}+J\otimes y^{n},\forall n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume that
V_{1}
is a nonzero submodule of
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
,
then there exists
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}

such that
\bar{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{i}\in V_{1},\forall k\leq i\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
by Lemma
3.5
.
It shows that
\pi({W^{(k)}+J\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)})\subseteq V_{1}
. From Lemma
3.6
(3) we know that

W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}})=M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}=W^{(k)}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}+J\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq\pi^{-1}(V_{1}).

So
\bar{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\in V_{1}
.
Inductively,

for all

.

.
Therefore,

is irreducible.
Claim
1
is proved.

Using Claim 1 and Lemmas
3.4
,
3.6
,
3.7
we see that

\displaystyle L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\text{ is irreducible }

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}_{n}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}({\bf 1}\otimes y^{n})=M(c,h,l)\otimes y^{n}=W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}_{n}+J\otimes y^{n},\forall n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(U(\mathcal{D}^{-})Q_{1}{\bf{1}}+U(\mathcal{D}^{-})Q_{2}{\bf{1}})\otimes y^{n}\nsubseteq W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}_{n},\forall n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\{Q_{1}{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n},Q_{2}{\bf{1}}\otimes y^{n}\}\nsubseteq W^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}_{n},\forall n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\{\rho_{n}(Q_{1}),\rho_{n}(Q_{2})\}\neq\{0,0\},\forall n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}.

Now, we complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.
Isomorphism theorem

In the section, we determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for two of
the tensor product modules studied in Section 3 to be isomorphic.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then

A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\cong A(\alpha+n,\beta,\gamma)
for any
n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
.

Proof.

For any
n\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
, it is straightforward to verify that the linear map

F:A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\rightarrow A(\alpha+n,\beta,\gamma),\quad v_{k}\rightarrow(\delta_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\delta_{n\notin{\mathbb{Z}}}(\delta_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{k\notin{\mathbb{Z}}}))v_{k+n}

is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
c,h,l,\alpha,\beta,c_{1},h_{1},l_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\in{\mathbb{C}},\gamma,\gamma_{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
Then
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\cong L(c_{1},h_{1},l_{1})\otimes A(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1})

if and only if
(\beta,\gamma,c,h,l)=(\beta_{1},\gamma_{1},c_{1},h_{1},l_{1})
and
\alpha-\alpha_{1}\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.1
, we may assume that
0\leq\mathfrak{Re}\alpha,\mathfrak{Re}\alpha_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
.
Since the “if part” is trivial,
we only need to show the “only if part”.

We identify the
\mathcal{D}
-modules
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
with
V_{1}=L(c,h,l)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]
, and
L(c_{1},h_{1},l_{1})\otimes A(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1})
with
V_{2}=L(c_{1},h_{1},l_{1})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]
.
Let
{\overline{\bf 1}}
and
{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}
be the highest weight vectors of
L(c,h,l)
and
L(c_{1},h_{1},l_{1})
respectively.
It is clear that

c=c_{1},l=l_{1}.

Suppose
\varphi:V_{1}\to V_{2}

is a
\mathcal{D}
-module isomorphism.
For
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
, let
\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{s}P_{i}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{t}

for some
t\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
where
P_{i}\in U(\mathcal{D}^{-})_{-i}
is homogeneous.
Note that
\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k})
and
{\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k}

have the same weight. We deduce that

\alpha+h-k=\alpha_{1}+h_{1}-t.

(4.1)

For any
m,n>s
, we compute

\begin{split}(\delta_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}&+\gamma\delta_{k\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k+m+n+\frac{1}{2}})=\varphi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+m+n}({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k}))\\
&=h_{\frac{1}{2}+m+n}\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{s}(\delta_{t+i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma_{1}\delta_{t+i\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})P_{i}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{t+m+n+\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(4.2)

\begin{split}&(\delta_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma\delta_{k\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})(\alpha+\beta m-k-n-\frac{1}{2})\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k+m+n+\frac{1}{2}})\\
=&\varphi(d_{m}h_{\frac{1}{2}+n}({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k}))=d_{m}h_{\frac{1}{2}+n}\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{s}d_{m}h_{\frac{1}{2}+n}(P_{i}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{t})\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{s}(\delta_{t+i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}+\gamma_{1}\delta_{t+i\notin{\mathbb{Z}}})(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}m-i-t-n-\frac{1}{2})P_{i}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{t+m+n+\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(4.3)

Comparing the above two equations we obtain that

\alpha+\beta m-k-n-\frac{1}{2}=\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}m-i-t-n-\frac{1}{2},\forall m,n>s,0\leq i\leq s

with
P_{i}{\bf 1}^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Combining with (
4.1
) we deduce that

\beta=\beta_{1},\alpha=\alpha_{1},i=h-h_{1},t=k-h+h_{1}.

Since
i=-\deg(P_{i})\geq 0
, so
h-h_{1}\geq 0
. By considering
\varphi^{-1}
in the above arguments we deduce that
h_{1}-h\geq 0
.
We must have

h=h_{1},t=k,s=0.

Therefore, for any
k\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}
, we may assume that

\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k})=a_{k}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{k}
where
a_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
From

\begin{split}&\gamma a_{k+m+n+1}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{k+m+n+1}=\gamma\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k+m+n+1})=\varphi(h_{\frac{1}{2}+m}h_{\frac{1}{2}+n}({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k}))\\
=&h_{\frac{1}{2}+m}h_{\frac{1}{2}+n}\varphi({\overline{\bf 1}}\otimes y^{k})=h_{\frac{1}{2}+m}h_{\frac{1}{2}+n}(a_{k}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{k})=a_{k}\gamma_{1}{\overline{\bf 1^{\prime}}}\otimes y^{k+m+n+1},\forall m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\end{split}

We deduce that

\gamma a_{k+m+n+1}=a_{k}\gamma_{1},\forall m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

yielding that
\gamma=\gamma_{1}
. We complete the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

In Proposition
4.2
we can have similar results for any highest weight modules, not only for irreducible highest weight modules.

is generated by
d_{-1}w_{1},d_{-2}w_{1}
.
Using (
2.1
) we deduce that the maximal proper submodule of
M(1,\frac{1}{16},l)
is generated by

Q_{1}w
and
Q_{2}{\bf 1}
where
Q_{1}=d_{-1}-\frac{1}{2l}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{2},Q_{2}=d_{-2}-\frac{1}{l}h_{-\frac{3}{2}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
We see that

\rho_{n}(Q_{1})=\beta-\alpha+1+n-\frac{1}{2l}\gamma,\quad\rho_{n}(Q_{2})=2\beta-\alpha+2+n-\frac{\gamma}{l}.

Taking
\rho_{n}(Q_{1})=\rho_{n}(Q_{2})=0
, we have that

n=\alpha\text{ and }\beta+1=\frac{\gamma}{2l}.

Thus, if
n=\alpha\in\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{2},\beta+1-\frac{\gamma}{2l}=0
,
then
\overline{W^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}}

is the unique irreducible submodule of
L(1,\frac{1}{16},l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)

and
\overline{W^{(\alpha)}}/\overline{W^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}}
is a highest weight module
with highest weight
(1,\frac{1}{16},l)
.
Otherwise,
L(1,\frac{1}{16},l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is irreducible.

Remark 5.5
.

For any
c,h,\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},l,\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, it is generally hard to determine the structure of nontrivial quotient modules
\overline{W^{(k)}}/\overline{W^{(k+1)}}
for the
\mathcal{D}
-module
L(c,h,l)\otimes A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
.
We also have the similar questions for the Virasoro algebra and Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, see
[
5
,
21
]
.

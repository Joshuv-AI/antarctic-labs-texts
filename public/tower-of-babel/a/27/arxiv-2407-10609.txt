On products and partial isometry of Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbols
Srijan Sarkar

Abstract
. We solve the following problems associated with Toeplitz operators T Φ T_{\Phi} on Hilbert space-valued Hardy spaces H ℰ 2 ​ ( 𝔻 n ) H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}) over the unit polydisc 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} . ( i ) (i) Given operator-valued bounded analytic functions Γ , Ψ \Gamma,\Psi on 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} , we completely characterize when the product M Γ ​ M Ψ ∗ M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*} becomes a Toeplitz operator by identifying tractable conditions on the functions. Furthermore, these conditions can be used to explicitly write the product into a sum of simple Toeplitz operators. ( i ​ i ) (ii) We prove that partially isometric Toeplitz operators admit the following factorization: T Φ = M Γ ​ M Ψ ∗ , T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}, where, Γ , Ψ \Gamma,\Psi are operator-valued inner functions on 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} . A few of the immediate consequences are: ( a ) (a) every partially isometric Toeplitz operator has a partially isometric symbol almost everywhere on 𝕋 n \mathbb{T}^{n} (distinguished boundary of 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} ). ( b ) (b) any partially isometric analytic Toeplitz operator is of the form M Γ ​ V ∗ M_{\Gamma V^{*}} , where Γ \Gamma is an operator-valued inner function and V V is an constant isometry. In connection with the result ( i ​ i ) (ii) , we establish and use a crucial phenomenon: the range of partially isometric Toeplitz operators is always a Beurling-type invariant subspace of H ℰ 2 ​ ( 𝔻 n ) H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}) . Our results are new even in the case of Hardy spaces over the unit disc and extend the work of Brown–Douglas, Deepak–Pradhan–Sarkar on scalar-valued spaces.

1.
Introduction and main results

From its inception, Toeplitz operators have played a vital role in the interplay between operator theory and function theory. This connection has been instrumental in finding new results across many disciplines, like several complex variables, non-commutative geometry, mathematical physics, and engineering sciences. Recently, there has been an active interest in extending the results of Toeplitz operators on scalar-valued Hardy spaces to vector-valued spaces. The primary reason has always been to find a deeper understanding between the operators and their corresponding symbols. Several authors have contributed to showing that analytic Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbols have many applications in systems engineering and
H^{\infty}
control theory; for instance, see the celebrated monographs
[
1
,
14
]
. Let us now briefly discuss the setting.

For any Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, the
\mathcal{E}
-valued (or vector-valued) Hardy space on the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
in the
n
-dimensional complex plane is defined by

H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}):=\big\{\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}z^{\bm{k}}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\mathcal{E}):a_{\bm{k}}\in\mathcal{E},\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\|a_{\bm{k}}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}<\infty\big\}.

In the case of
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{C}
, the above collection contains simply the scalar-valued functions. The space of all bounded operators on any Hilbert space (say)
\mathcal{E}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
, and the collection of operator-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is denoted by
H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. It is well-known that
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is isometrically isomorphic to the Hardy space
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
on the
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
(contained inside the
n
-dimensional complex plane), and this characterization is achieved using radial limits
[
27
]
. Using this identification, a Toeplitz operator is defined in the following manner
[
13
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A bounded linear operator
T
on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is said to be Toeplitz if there exists an operator-valued function
\Phi\in L_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that
T=P_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}L_{\Phi}|_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})},
where
L_{\Phi}
is the Laurent operator on
L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
(that is, the multiplication operator) associated to
\Phi.
In this case,
T
is denoted by
T_{\Phi}
, and the function
\Phi
is called the symbol of the operator
T_{\Phi}.

It is immediate that when
\Phi
is an operator-valued bounded analytic function on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, the corresponding Toeplitz operator is simply the multiplication operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. For this reason, we make a distinction and use the following convention throughout this article.

\text{If }\Phi\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),\text{ then the Toeplitz operator is denoted by }M_{\Phi}.

It is worth mentioning that unlike in the case of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, the theory of Toeplitz operators on both
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(where
n>1
) is far from being complete, as many natural questions remain unanswered. Several challenges appear whenever we leave the realm of scalar-valued functions and delve into the world of operator-valued symbols. We refer the reader to the exceptional monograph by Douglas for results on matrix-valued Toeplitz operators
[
13
]
. Recently, Curto, Hwang, and Lee have made some spectacular progress in this direction by studying Halmos’s question of subnormal Toeplitz operators for matrix or operator-valued symbols
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

In this article, we further explore this theme: the correspondence of Toeplitz operators with their symbols under operator-theoretic conditions. From the algebraic characterization of Toeplitz operators by Brown and Halmos
[
3
]
, it can be realized that the only possibility for any
T_{\phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
to become an isometry (that is,
\|T_{\phi}f\|=\|f\|
for all
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
) is when its symbol
\phi
is an inner function (that is,
\phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and
|\phi(e^{it})|=1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
). Brown and Douglas were interested in the behaviour of Toeplitz operators under the general condition of it being a partial isometry. We recall that a bounded operator
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a partial isometry if
T
is an isometry on the orthogonal complement of its kernel. In
[
2
]
, the authors proved that the only partially isometric Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
are of the form
T_{\phi}=M_{\theta}
, or else,
T_{\phi}=M_{\theta}^{*}
, where
\theta
is an inner function on
\mathbb{D}
. Recently, Deepak–Pradhan–Sarkar extended this result for Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, where
n>1
.

Theorem
.

[
11
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
\phi
be a non-zero function in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then
T_{\phi}
is a partial isometry if and only if there exist inner functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
depends on different variables and
T_{\phi}=M_{\phi_{1}}^{*}M_{\phi_{2}}
.

In other words, the condition of partial isometry on
T_{\phi}
forces a factorization
\phi=\bar{\phi_{1}}\phi_{2}
almost everywhere (denoted by a.e.) on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. An immediate question is finding the corresponding result for Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbols, hoping that a satisfying answer will explain the reasoning behind the surprising factorization of the symbol
\phi
. If we expect a straightforward generalization, then there lies an immediate obstacle. In scalar-valued cases, both Brown–Douglas and Deepak–Pradhan–Sarkar’s results show that if we start with
\phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then the only possibility for the analytic Toeplitz operator
M_{\phi}
to be a partial isometry is when
M_{\phi}
is an isometry, in other words,
\phi
must be an inner function. Many examples involving block Toeplitz operators show that this no longer holds in the setting of operator-valued symbols. First, let us note that an operator-valued function
\Theta\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is said to be an inner function if
\Theta(\bm{z}):\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
is an isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}

[
27
]
. Let us now consider the following bounded analytic matrix-valued symbol,

\Theta(z):=\begin{bmatrix}0&z\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}),

then for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

\Theta(\lambda)^{*}\Theta(\lambda)=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\bar{\lambda}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&\lambda\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}.

This implies that
\Theta(z)
is not an inner function. However,

M_{\Theta}^{*}M_{\Theta}=T_{\Theta^{*}\Theta}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\end{bmatrix}\in H_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

shows that
M_{\Theta}
is indeed a partial isometry. Thus, a non-constant analytic Toeplitz operator
M_{\Theta}
can be a partial isometry without
\Theta(z)
being an inner function. Motivated by this observation and earlier results, we study the following intriguing question in this article:
What are the partially isometric Toeplitz operators on vector-valued Hardy spaces?

Now, let us digress a little to highlight two important directions associated with this question. The first is in the study of invariant subspaces of
M_{z}\oplus M_{z}^{*}
on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
as observed by Gu and Luo in
[
19
]
. This work itself was motivated by the characterization of invariant subspaces of
M_{z}\oplus M_{z}^{*}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
by Timotin
[
34
]
. In their work, Gu and Luo observed that the invariant subspace of
M_{z}\oplus M_{z}^{*}
is related to the range of the following operator.

V_{\Phi}=\begin{bmatrix}T_{A}&T_{B}\\
H_{C}&H_{D}\end{bmatrix}:H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

where

\Phi(z)=\begin{bmatrix}A(z)&B(z)\\
C(z)&D(z)\end{bmatrix}:\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{F}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

and
T_{A},T_{B}
, and
H_{C},H_{D}
are the corresponding Toeplitz and Hankel operators, respectively. Furthermore, the authors found that the range of
V_{\Phi}
is closed in
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
V_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry. This led to the natural question of when
V_{\Phi}
becomes a partial isometry. In this regard, they made the following statement.

“The above problem seems a difficult one since the partial isometric characterizations
of Toeplitz operator
T_{A}
(A is not necessarily analytic) and Hankel operator
H_{D}
are only known when
A
and
D
are scalar functions in
L^{\infty}

[
2
,
29
]
.” In this article, we completely resolve this issue for Toeplitz operators; see Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
5.5
.

The second connection lies in studying Beurling-type invariant subspaces of Hardy spaces over the unit polydisc. In a recent article
[
10
]
, Debnath et al. used the characterization
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
to give a complete answer to the following question: given non-constant inner functions
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, when does the orthogonal projections onto invariant subspaces
\theta_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
\theta_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
commute with each other? This resulted in a definite answer to the following question raised by R.G. Douglas: given non-constant inner functions
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, when does the product of projections onto the orthogonal subspaces
(\theta_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))^{\perp}
and
(\theta_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))^{\perp}
become a finite-rank projection?

Thus, it is evident that our main result will serve as an essential component for

(i)
the characterization of invariant subspaces of sums of shift operators on vector-valued Hardy spaces over both domains
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}^{n}
;

(ii)
answering Douglas’s question in the setting of vector-valued Hardy spaces by studying commuting projections onto Beurling-type shift-invariant subspaces of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Since the proofs in
[
3
,
11
]
rely crucially on the commutativity of the symbols, the methods used in these works are not at all applicable for answering the above questions. Thus, it is essential to find a completely new approach to the above question. Based on earlier works, we can hope that a partially isometric
T_{\Phi}
may admit a factorization into the product of Toeplitz operators corresponding to inner symbols. For this purpose, let us briefly elucidate how multiplication operators corresponding to inner functions appear in the context of Hardy spaces. Recall that a closed
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
-joint invariant subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is said to be
Beurling-type
if there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
, and an inner function
\Theta\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{S}=M_{\Theta}H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. It is well-known that given a subspace
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if we consider the restriction operators
R_{i}:=M_{z_{i}}|_{\mathcal{S}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then
\mathcal{S}
is Beurling-type invariant subspace if and only if the cross-commutators
[R_{i}^{*},R_{j}]=0
for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
(
[
25
,
32
]
). Thus, if we can show that the range of Toeplitz operators satisfies the above algebraic conditions concerning the restriction operators, then we can associate inner functions to the corresponding symbol. In section
4
, we establish this connection by proving the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
T_{\Phi}
is a non-zero partially isometric Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then
\ran T_{\Phi}
is a Beurling-type invariant subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Let us note that classically, the kernel of the adjoint of Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is related to
nearly
invariant subspaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. This observation was noted by Hayashi in
[
23
]
and further explored by Sarason in
[
31
]
. We believe that the above result should serve as an impetus to study the range of Toeplitz operators as well.

With the above characterization in mind, the author could anticipate that a partially isometric Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
may admit the following factorization.

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*},

where
\Gamma,\Psi
are operator-valued inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, but this led to another challenge:
under what conditions does
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
become a Toeplitz operator?

On the scalar-valued Hardy spaces, it is well-known when the product of two Toeplitz operators is again a Toeplitz operator. Brown and Halmos developed this result for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
in
[
3
]
, and Gu proved the corresponding result for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
in
[
16
]
. However, the answer to this question for Toeplitz operators with operator-valued symbols is still unclear. We refer the reader towards some important progress in the case of block Toeplitz operators by Gu and Zheng in
[
15
,
18
]
. This is where we make another significant contribution. Section
3
is dedicated to establishing tractable conditions that solve the above product problem for not only inner but also bounded analytic functions. Before stating the result, let us set the following notation: for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the functions
\Gamma_{k}(\bm{z}),\Psi_{k}(\bm{z})
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
denote the functions
\Gamma
and
\Psi
, respectively, with
0
in the
k
-th coordinate.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and
\Gamma(\bm{z}),\Psi(\bm{z})
be
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Then
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if and only if

\big(\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})-\Gamma_{k}(\bm{\lambda})\big)\big(\Psi(\bm{\mu})-\Psi_{k}(\bm{\mu})\big)^{*}=0,

for all
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

These conditions further arise from an equivalent set of necessary and sufficient conditions on the Fourier coefficients of the symbols
\Gamma
and
\Psi
(see Proposition
3.3
). Surprisingly, these conditions also serve as an algorithm to write the product
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
into sums of elementary Toeplitz operators, as can be seen in the following case when
n=1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and
\Gamma(z),\Psi(z)
be
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,

(ii)

\big(\Gamma(\lambda)-\Gamma(0)\big)\big(\Psi(\mu)-\Psi(0)\big)^{*}=0
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{D}
,

(iii)

M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
admits the following decomposition

M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}\Psi(0)^{*}+\Gamma(0)M_{\Psi}^{*}-\Gamma(0)\Psi(0)^{*}.

The terms on the right-hand side of the above identity are analytic, co-analytic and constant Toeplitz operators, clearly showing how
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
becomes a Toeplitz operator. An immediate consequence is the following:
when
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator,
\Gamma(0)=0
or
\Psi(0)=0
, implies that
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is analytic or co-analytic, respectively
. Also, see Remark
3.1
for a function-theoretic consequence of the above characterization.

The decomposition of
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
into the sum of elementary Toeplitz operators are challenging to write down for
n>1
cases, and the difficulty increases with increasing
n
. In this article, we have meticulously written down the decompositions in the cases of
n=2,3
(Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
, respectively) and also highlighted in Remark
3.2
the method to find the decomposition when
n>3
. In section
5
, we use Theorem
1.3
to give a complete characterization for partially isometric Toeplitz operators on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Theorem 1.5
.

A non-constant Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a partial isometry if and only if there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
, and inner functions
\Gamma(\bm{z}),\Psi(\bm{z})\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*},

where
\Gamma(\bm{z})
and
\Psi(\bm{z})
satisfy

\big(\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})-\Gamma_{k}(\bm{\lambda})\big)\big(\Psi(\bm{\mu})-\Psi_{k}(\bm{\mu})\big)^{*}=0,

for all
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

For elaborate statements in the cases of
n=1,2,3
, we refer to Theorem
5.1
, Theorem
5.2
, and Theorem
5.3
, respectively. As expected, there are numerous consequences of this result. For instance, a partially isometric
T_{\Phi}
will always have a partially isometric symbol, a.e., on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
(Corollary
5.4
). At the end of section
5
, we use the above result to characterize partially isometric Toeplitz operators that have analytic symbols (Theorem
5.5
), that are hyponormal (Theorem
5.6
), and normal (Corollary
5.7
). In section
6
, we use our main result to characterize general Toeplitz operators on abstract Hilbert spaces.

In section
7
, we give a completely new proof of the main results obtained by Deepak et al. in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
and Brown et al in
[
2
]
.
This new approach shows that, in general, the condition of partial isometry on a Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
induces a factorization of the symbol
\Phi
into the product
\Gamma\Psi^{*}
where
\Gamma
and
\Psi
are inner functions jointly satisfying certain function-theoretic conditions. In scalar cases, these conditions manifest into the factorization of
\phi
into the product of functions depending on disjoint variables (see Theorem
7.1
).

Let us now describe the plan for the rest of this article. In section
2
, we set notations, definitions, and establish a few results essential for the later part. We end this article with section
8
, where we have highlighted several interesting questions worthy of further investigation.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we set the notations, definitions, and results needed in this article. Let us begin by looking at the following equivalent way of defining
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy spaces on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}):=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\mathcal{E}):\|f\|_{2}^{2}:=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\|f(re^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,re^{i\theta_{n}})\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}d\mu<\infty\},

where
\mu
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. This space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued analytic functions has a natural collection of shift operators, namely,

M_{z_{i}}f:=z_{i}f,\quad(f\in H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})),

for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We will denote by
M_{\bm{z}}=(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
as the tuple of shift operators. For
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we set the following convention,

M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}=M_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}},

and for any
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),\bm{l}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we say
\bm{k}\leq\bm{l}
if
k_{i}\leq l_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For the tuple of shift operators
L_{e^{i\theta}}=(L_{e^{i\theta_{1}}},\ldots,L_{e^{i\theta_{n}}})
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we set

L_{e^{i\theta}}^{\bm{k}}=L_{e^{i\theta_{1}}}^{k_{1}}\cdots L_{e^{i\theta_{n}}}^{k_{n}}.

Brown and Halmos gave an algebraic characterization for Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

[
3
]
. It is well-known that there exists a natural extension of this result to Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

[
24
]
. The following result shows that such a characterization holds for
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as well. The result can be proved verbatim from
[
24
, Theorem 3.1, Theorem 5.2]
. For the sake of completeness, we give a sketch of the proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

A bounded operator
T
on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if

M_{z_{i}}^{*}TM_{z_{i}}=T,

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, consider
\bm{k}_{d}:=(k,\ldots,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. From the assumption
M_{z_{i}}^{*}TM_{z_{i}}=T
, we immediately get

M_{\bm{z}}^{*\bm{k}_{d}}TM_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}_{d}}=T.

This implies that for any
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{E}
,

\langle Te_{\bm{i}+\bm{k}_{d}}\eta,e_{\bm{i}+\bm{k}_{d}}\zeta\rangle=\langle TM_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}_{d}}e_{\bm{i}}\eta,M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}_{d}}e_{\bm{j}}\zeta\rangle=\langle Te_{\bm{i}},e_{\bm{j}}\rangle,

for all
\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, for each
\bm{l},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
there exists
\bm{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that
\bm{l}+\bm{k}_{d},\bm{m}+\bm{k}_{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
for all
\bm{k}_{d}\geq\bm{t}
. Using this observation, if we set
A_{k}:=L_{e^{i\theta}}^{*\bm{k}_{d}}TP_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}L_{e^{i\theta}}^{\bm{k}_{d}}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, then we get

\langle A_{k}e_{\bm{l}},e_{\bm{m}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\langle TP_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}e_{\bm{l}+\bm{k}_{d}},e_{\bm{m}+\bm{k}_{d}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})},

and therefore, for all
\bm{k}_{d}\geq\bm{t}

\langle A_{k}e_{\bm{l}},e_{\bm{m}}\rangle\rightarrow\langle Te_{\bm{l}+t},e_{\bm{m}+t}\rangle\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

Hence, we can define a bounded bilinear form
\textsf{b}(\cdot,\cdot)
on the linear span of
\{e_{\bm{s}}\zeta:\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\zeta\in\mathcal{E}\}
, in the following manner,

\textsf{b}(e_{\bm{l}}\eta,e_{\bm{m}}\zeta)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\langle A_{k}e_{\bm{l}}\eta,e_{\bm{m}}\zeta\rangle,

for all
\bm{l},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{E}
. Therefore, there exists an operator
A_{\infty}\in\mathcal{B}(L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
such that

\langle A_{\infty}f,g\rangle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\langle A_{k}f,g\rangle,

for
f,g\in L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Let
\epsilon_{j}=(0,\ldots,0,\underset{\text{j-th position}}{1},0,\ldots,0)
. Then for all
k
sufficiently large (depending on
\bm{l},\bm{m}
and
j
), we get

\displaystyle\langle L_{e^{i\theta}}^{*\bm{k}_{d}}TP_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}L_{e^{i\theta}}^{\bm{k}_{d}}e_{\bm{l}+\epsilon_{j}},e_{\bm{m}+\epsilon_{j}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\langle TP_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}e_{\bm{l}+\bm{k}_{d}+\epsilon_{j}},e_{\bm{m}+\bm{k}_{d}+\epsilon_{j}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{k}e_{\bm{l}},e_{\bm{m}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

This immediately gives

\displaystyle\langle A_{\infty}e_{\bm{l}+\epsilon_{j}},e_{\bm{m}+\epsilon_{j}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\langle L_{e^{i\theta}}^{*\bm{k}_{d}}TP_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}L_{e^{i\theta}}^{\bm{k}_{d}}e_{\bm{l}+\epsilon_{j}},e_{\bm{m}+\epsilon_{j}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{\infty}e_{\bm{l}},e_{\bm{m}}\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

This implies that

A_{\infty}L_{e^{i\theta_{j}}}=L_{e^{i\theta_{j}}}A_{\infty},

for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. Hence, there exists
\Phi(z)\in L_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

A_{\infty}=L_{\Phi},

which further implies that
T=P_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}L_{\Phi}|_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}
.

Conversely, if we begin with
T=P_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}L_{\Phi}|_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}
for some
\Phi(z)\in L_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then for any
f,g\in H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
j=1,\ldots,n
we get

\langle M_{z_{j}}^{*}TM_{z_{j}}f,g\rangle_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\langle\Phi e^{i\theta_{j}}f,e^{i\theta_{j}}g\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\langle\Phi f,g\rangle_{L_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

In other words,

\langle M_{z_{j}}^{*}TM_{z_{j}}f,g\rangle_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\langle P_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}L_{\Phi}|_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}f,g\rangle_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})},

which implies that
M_{z_{j}}^{*}TM_{z_{j}}=T
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. This completes the proof.
∎

This property plays an important role in developing many results on Toeplitz operators and will be used throughout this article. Before moving into some of its consequences, let us briefly recall some concepts on partial isometries.

Definition 2.2
.

T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a partial isometry if
T
is an isometry on the orthogonal complement of the subspace
\{h\in\mathcal{H}:\|Th\|=0\}
(also referred to as kernel of
T
).

It is well-known that
T
is a partial isometry on
\mathcal{H}
if and only if
T
satisfies any one of the following equivalent conditions.

(i)

TT^{*}
is a projection.

(ii)

TT^{*}T=T
.

From this description, the following useful facts can be deduced.

(i)

T
is a partial isometry if and only if
T^{*}
is also a partial isometry.

(ii)
The range of a partially isometric operator must be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
.

Most importantly, in this case, we have the following identity

(2.1)

I_{\mathcal{H}}-TT^{*}=P_{\ker T^{*}}.

where
P_{\ker T^{*}}
is the orthogonal projection onto the kernel of
T^{*}
. Throughout this article, we shall use this identity, especially in the case of shift operators. We refer the readers to some fundamental results on partial isometries by Halmos, McLaughlin, and Wallen in
[
21
,
22
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
T_{\Phi}
be a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
T_{\Phi}
is an isometry if and only if
\Phi
is an inner function in
H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Proof.

Any isometry
V
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the identity
V^{*}V=I_{\mathcal{H}}
. Using this property we observe that if
T_{\Phi}
is an isometry, then

I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}\quad(\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}).

Since
M_{z_{i}}
is an isometry for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we can use identity (
2.1
) to get the following.

M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}=I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}},

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Thus, we get that
M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}=0
, which further implies,

P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}=0.

Now,
P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*})T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}=T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}-M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}
implies that the above identity should force

M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and hence,
\Phi
must be in
H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

[
27
]
. Since
T_{\Phi}
is an isometry,
\Phi
must be an inner function in
H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. For the converse direction, it is well-known that a Toeplitz operator corresponding to an inner function is always an isometry
[
27
]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
T_{\Phi}
be a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then
T_{\Phi}
is a unitary if and only if
\Phi
is a constant unitary on
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

From the assumption, both
T_{\Phi}
and
T_{\Phi^{*}}=T_{\Phi}^{*}
are isometries. Following the above result, both
\Phi
, and
\Phi^{*}
must be inner functions in
H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Thus,
\Phi
must be a constant operator on
\mathcal{E}
. Since
T_{\Phi}
is unitary,
\Phi
must be a constant unitary on
\mathcal{E}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Similar, to the case of Toeplitz operators on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})

[
7
]
, we can consider the following decomposition on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
for symbols
F,G\in L_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,

T_{FG}=T_{F}T_{G}+H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}.

Here
H_{F}:=JP_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}L_{F}|_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}:H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the Hankel operator with symbol
\Phi
, and
J:(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))^{\perp}\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the unitary defined by
J(f)(\bm{z})=\bar{\bm{z}}I_{\mathcal{E}}f(\bar{\bm{z}})
, for
f\in(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))^{\perp}
. It is well known that Hankel operators satisfy the following property for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(2.2)

M_{z_{i}}^{*}H_{F}=H_{F}M_{z_{i}}.

We refer the reader to the monographs
[
4
,
29
]
for an elaborate discussion of the above facts. We can establish the following well-known fact using the above intertwining property.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
T_{F}T_{G}
is a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then
T_{F}T_{G}=T_{FG}
.

Proof.

Note that
T_{F}T_{G}=T_{FG}-H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}
. If
T_{F}T_{G}=T_{Y}
is a Toeplitz operators (say)
T_{Y}
, then

T_{Y}=T_{FG}-H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}.

This implies that
T_{Y-FG}=H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}
. But then we have

M_{z_{i}}^{*k}H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}M_{z_{i}}^{k}=H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}.

Using the intertwining identity for Hankel operators, we get

M_{z_{i}}^{*k}H_{F^{*}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}^{*k}H_{G}=H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G},

and therefore, for each
x\in H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, we must have

\|H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}x\|\leq\|M_{z_{i}}^{*k}H_{G}x\|\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

It is because for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the operator
M_{z_{i}}
is a pure isometry, that is,
M_{z_{i}}^{*k}\rightarrow 0
in the strong operator topology as
k\rightarrow\infty
. The above inequality implies that
H_{F^{*}}^{*}H_{G}=0
, and hence,
T_{F}T_{G}=T_{FG}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Product of Toeplitz operators

In this section, we aim to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for which a certain product of Toeplitz operators corresponding to analytic symbols is again a Toeplitz operator. Let us begin with a lemma useful for finding conditions in the general situation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and
\Gamma\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
\Psi\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
be operator-valued bounded analytic functions. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

(ii)

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}^{*}}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

Using identity (
2.1
), we know that

I_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}+P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}},

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Therefore, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we get

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}+M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}.

From Theorem
2.1
, it is clear that
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if
M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. In other words,
M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We can further strengthen the above condition in the following manner.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and
\Gamma(\bm{z})\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
\Psi(\bm{z})\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then the following are equivalent for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(i)

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}^{*}}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0

(ii)

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\mathcal{E}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0
.

Proof.

Let us begin with the direction
(i)\implies(ii)
. Note that for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}^{*}}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0,

implies that

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}\big(M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}+P_{\ker M_{z_{j}}^{*}}\big)P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0,

for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. In other words,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}+M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{j}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

If
j\neq i
, then we can use the commutator identity
[M_{z_{j}},M_{z_{i}}^{*}]=0
to get

M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}+M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{j}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

By our assumption
M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0
, and hence,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{j}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

Continuing in the same manner as above, we find that the above condition implies that for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i,j\}
, we get

\displaystyle M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{z_{k}}M_{z_{k}}^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{j}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle+M_{z_{1}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{j}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=0,

which further implies that

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{j}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

Iterating the same procedure for
n-3
many times with distinct numbers in
\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i,j,k\}
, we can conclude that,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\mathcal{E}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0,

because
P_{\mathcal{E}}=\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\Pi}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}
. To prove the opposite direction, let us observe that,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\mathcal{E}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0,

for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
will imply that

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{1}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\big(\underset{k=2;k\neq i}{\overset{n}{\Pi}}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}\big)M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

This implies that

\displaystyle M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\big(\underset{k=2;k\neq i}{\overset{n}{\Pi}}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}\big)M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{z_{1}}M_{z_{1}}^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\big(\underset{k=2;k\neq i}{\overset{n}{\Pi}}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}\big)M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{1}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\big(\underset{k=2;k\neq i}{\overset{n}{\Pi}}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}\big)M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{1}}^{*}.

and therefore, using recursion, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\displaystyle M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=2;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{1}}^{m}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=2;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{1}}^{*m}.

This will imply that for any
f\in H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\|M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=2;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}f\|

\displaystyle=\|M_{z_{1}}^{m}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=2;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{1}}^{*m}f\|

\displaystyle\leq\|M_{z_{1}}^{*m}f\|\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

Hence, we get

(3.3)

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=2;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

We will repeat the same process coordinate-wise. For instance, the above identity (
3.3
) implies that,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{2}}^{*}}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=3;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0,

which further gives,

\displaystyle M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=3;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{2}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=3;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{2}}^{*}.

Again as above, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=3;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{2}}^{m}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=3;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{2}}^{*m},

which, as in the earlier case, will give

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}\Pi_{k=3;k\neq i}^{n}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

Repeating the same process for all coordinates in
\{3,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i\}
, will give us

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to establish conditions making
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
into a Toeplitz operator.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and
\Gamma(\bm{z}),\Psi(\bm{z})
be
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. More precisely, let

\Gamma(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}A_{\bm{l}}\bm{z}^{\bm{l}}\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n});\quad\Psi(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}B_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),

where
A_{\bm{k}},B_{\bm{k}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
for all
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if and only if

(3.4)

A_{\bm{l}+e_{i}}B_{\bm{m}+e_{i}}^{*}=0\quad(\forall\bm{l},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}),

where
e_{i}:=(0,\ldots,0,\underset{\text{i-th position}}{1},0,\ldots,0)
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
, we know that
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if and only if

T_{\overline{z_{i}}\Gamma}P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}\Psi^{*}}=M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\mathcal{E}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0,

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Now, for any
\bm{j},\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{E}
, we get

\langle P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}\Psi^{*}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta,P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}\Gamma^{*}}\bm{z}^{\bm{j}}\zeta\rangle=\langle P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\Psi^{*}}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{k}_{i}}}\eta,P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}^{j_{i}+1}\Gamma^{*}}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{j}_{i}}}\zeta\rangle,

where for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{m}_{i}}}:=\underset{k=1;k\neq i}{\overset{n}{\Pi}}z_{k}^{m_{k}}.

By this notation, we have
\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}=z_{i}^{m_{i}}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{m}_{i}}}
. Now, note that from our assumption

\Gamma(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\bm{z}^{\bm{l}}A_{\bm{l}};\quad\Psi(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}B_{\bm{m}},

where
A_{\bm{l}}:=A_{(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})},B_{\bm{m}}:=B_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
for all
\bm{l}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}),
and
\bm{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Now,

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{{z_{i}}^{j_{i}+1}\Gamma^{*}}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{j}_{i}}}\zeta

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{E}}P_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}{z_{i}}^{j_{i}+1}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{j}_{i}}}\Gamma(\bm{z})^{*}\zeta

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{E}}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}{z_{i}}^{j_{i}+1}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{j}_{i}}}\bar{\bm{z}}^{\bm{l}}A_{\bm{l}}^{*}\zeta

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{E}}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}{z_{i}}^{j_{i}-l_{i}+1}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{j}_{i}}}\bar{\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}}^{\bm{\hat{l}_{i}}}A_{\bm{l}}^{*}\zeta

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{E}}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n};l_{i}=j_{i}+1}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{j}_{i}-\hat{l}_{i}}}A_{\bm{l}}^{*}\zeta

\displaystyle=A_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{i-1},j_{i}+1,j_{i+1},\ldots,j_{n})}^{*}\zeta.

Similarly,

P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}\Psi^{*}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta=P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\Psi^{*}}\bm{\hat{z}_{i}}^{\bm{\hat{k}_{i}}}\eta=B_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{n})}^{*}\eta.

Hence,

\displaystyle\langle P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}\Psi^{*}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta,P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}\Gamma^{*}}\bm{z}^{\bm{j}}\zeta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle B_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{n})}^{*}\eta,A_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{i-1},j_{i}+1,j_{i+1},\ldots,j_{n})}^{*}\zeta\rangle,

for any
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{E}
. Therefore, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get
T_{\overline{z_{i}}\Gamma}P_{\mathcal{E}}T_{z_{i}\Psi^{*}}=0
if and only if

A_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{i-1},j_{i}+1,j_{i+1},\ldots,j_{n})}B_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i+1},k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{n})}^{*}=0,

for all
\bm{j},\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Using Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we get
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if

A_{\bm{l}+e_{i}}B_{\bm{m}+e_{i}}^{*}=0.

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
\bm{l},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We need the following result for the sequel.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and
\Gamma(\bm{z}),\Psi(\bm{z})
be
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Then
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}=0
if and only if
\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})\Psi(\bm{\mu})^{*}=0
for all
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Proof.

It is well known that
\{k_{\bm{\mu}}(\cdot)\eta:\eta\in\mathcal{E},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}\}
is a total set in
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and also, the Szegö kernels
k_{\bm{\mu}}(\bm{\lambda})
are never zero for any
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Hence, the following identity

\langle M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}k_{\bm{\mu}}\zeta,k_{\bm{\lambda}}\eta\rangle_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\langle k_{\bm{\mu}}\Psi(\bm{\mu})^{*}\zeta,k_{\bm{\lambda}}\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})^{*}\eta\rangle_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=k_{\bm{\mu}}(\bm{\lambda})\langle\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})\Psi(\bm{\mu})^{*}\zeta,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{E}},

for any
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{E}
, shows that
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}=0
if and only if
\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})\Psi(\bm{\mu})^{*}=0
for all
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to establish the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From Proposition
3.3
, we know that
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if
A_{\bm{l}+e_{i}}B_{\bm{m}+e_{i}}^{*}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
\bm{l},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, where

\Gamma(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}A_{\bm{l}}\bm{z}^{\bm{l}};\quad\Psi(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}B_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}.

Let us begin by assuming
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator, equivalently, the above conditions hold for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
\bm{l},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
then for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get

\begin{split}&\big(\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})-\Gamma_{k}(\bm{\lambda})\big)\big(\Psi(\bm{\mu})-\Psi_{k}(\bm{\mu})\big)^{*}\\
&=\Big(\Gamma\big((\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\big)-\Gamma\big((\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1},0,\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\big)\Big)\\
&\cdot\Big(\Psi\big((\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\big)-\Psi\big((\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k-1},0,\mu_{k+1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\big)\Big)^{*}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n};\\
l_{k}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\bm{\lambda}^{\bm{l}}A_{\bm{l}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n};\\
m_{k}\neq 0\end{subarray}}B_{\bm{m}}^{*}\bar{\bm{\mu}}^{\bm{m}}=0.\end{split}

Now, we need to prove the converse direction. So, let us suppose

\big(\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})-\Gamma_{k}(\bm{\lambda})\big)\big(\Psi(\bm{\mu})-\Psi_{k}(\bm{\mu})\big)^{*}=0,

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Using Lemma
3.4
, we can conclude that the above conditions are equivalent to

(M_{\Gamma}-M_{\Gamma_{k}})(M_{\Psi}-M_{\Psi_{k}})^{*}=0.

Hence, for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{\Psi}^{*}+M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}-M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}.

Therefore, for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\displaystyle M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}+M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{k}}-M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}+M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{z_{k}}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}-M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{k}}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}

\displaystyle=M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{k}}+M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{k}}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}-M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{k}}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}

\displaystyle=M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{\Psi}^{*}+M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}-M_{\Gamma_{k}}M_{\Psi_{k}}^{*}

\displaystyle=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

The second and third equality follow from the fact that for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the functions
\Gamma_{k},\Psi_{k}
do not depend on the
z_{k}
-variable. Using Theorem
2.1
, it is clear that the above set of identities will imply that
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
must be a Toeplitz operator. This completes the proof.
∎

It is natural to ask what will be the symbol of the Toeplitz operator in the above case. Let us start with the decomposition of
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
in the case of
n=1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

From Theorem
1.3
it is clear that
(i)\iff(ii)
. Now suppose that condition

The above identity is again equivalent to the following decomposition.

Remark 3.1
.

The above results unearths a surprising consequence:
if
\underset{l\in\mathbb{N}}{\sum}z^{l}A_{l}
and
\underset{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\sum}z^{m}B_{m}
are operator-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, then

\big(\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N};\\
l\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\sum}{\lambda}^{l}A_{l}\big)\big(\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N};\\
m\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\sum}\bar{\mu}^{m}B_{m}^{*}\big)=0,

for all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
A_{l+1}B_{m+1}^{*}=0
, for all
l,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

In particular, the product of two series is zero if and only if certain term-wise coefficients are zero.

For stating the theorem in cases when
n>1
, we need to set the following notation:
given any
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
be a non-empty subset of distinct elements in the ascending order. Given any
\Theta\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, we denote the function which has
0
’s in the
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
-coordinates by
\Theta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}(\bm{z})
. For instance,
\Theta_{1,\ldots,n}(\bm{z})=\Theta(0,\ldots,0)
and
\Theta_{1,3,n}(\bm{z})=\Theta(0,z_{2},0,z_{4},\ldots,z_{n-1},0)
, and
\Theta_{5,7,9}(\bm{z})=\Theta(z_{1},\ldots,z_{4},0,z_{6},0,z_{8},0,\bm{z}_{10},\ldots,z_{n})
. We also need the following result in the sequel.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and
\Gamma(\bm{z}),\Psi(\bm{z})
be
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Furthermore, let
A,B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
A\cup B=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then
M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}
is always a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Proof.

Suppose
i\in A
, then
\Gamma_{A}(\bm{z})
is not dependent on the variable
i
. Hence,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}.

Now let us suppose
i\in B
. For a similar reasoning as above,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{z_{i}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}=M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}=M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}.

From our assumption
A\cup B=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, it implies that
M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Thus, by using Theorem
2.1
we can conclude that
M_{\Gamma_{A}}M_{\Psi_{B}}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Beurling-type Toeplitz ranges

In the scalar cases, the main result in
[
11
]
shows that a partially isometric Toeplitz operator
T_{\phi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
must be of the following form.

T_{\phi}=M_{\phi_{1}}^{*}M_{\phi_{2}},

where
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, but depending on disjoint set of variables. As a consequence of this factorization, one observes that

\ran T_{\phi}=M_{\phi_{1}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n});\quad\ran T_{\phi}^{*}=M_{\phi_{2}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

In other words, the range of the partially isometric Toeplitz operator must be Beurling-type. This section aims to prove that this result is true even for vector-valued Hardy spaces. Since the commutativity of the symbols is lacking in vector-valued Hardy spaces, we have to pursue a completely new and different approach based on the characterization of Beurling-type invariant subspaces of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
via restriction operators. We begin by first showing that the range of partially isometric Toeplitz operators is a shift-invariant subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If a Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
on
H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a partial isometry, then the range of
T_{\Phi}
is a
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
-joint invariant closed subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Proof.

We already know that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry if and only if
T_{\Phi}^{*}
is a partial isometry. Thus,
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry implies that both
\ran T_{\Phi}
and
\ran T_{\Phi}^{*}
are closed subspaces of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Thus, we only need to show the
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
-joint invariance of
\ran T_{\Phi}
. To prove this, let us note that for any
f\in\ran T_{\Phi}
, we have the following inequalities for any arbitrary but fixed
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

\|z_{i}f\|\geq\|T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}f\|\geq\|M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}f\|=\|T_{\Phi}^{*}f\|=\|f\|=\|z_{i}f\|,

and thus,
\|T_{\Phi}^{*}z_{i}f\|=\|z_{i}f\|
. Since
T_{\Phi}^{*}
is a partial isometry, we must have
z_{i}f\in\ran T_{\Phi}
. Since
i
was arbitrarily chosen, this must be true for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Before proving our next result, let us highlight some useful facts involving shift-invariant subspaces of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Given any
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
-joint invariant closed subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, we can always associate the following restriction operators

R_{i}:=M_{z_{i}}|_{\mathcal{S}},

for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. It is well known that these operators play a crucial role in characterizing shift-invariant subspaces of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. We define the subspace
\mathcal{S}
to be
doubly commuting
if

[R_{i}^{*},R_{j}]=0

for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. From
[
32
]
, we know that the following equivalence holds:

(i)

\mathcal{S}
is doubly commuting,

(ii)

\mathcal{S}
is a Beurling-type subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, that is, by definition, there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
, and an inner function
\Theta\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

\mathcal{S}=M_{\Theta}H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

In the following result, we prove that the range of a partially isometric Toeplitz operator always admits the above description.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Our approach is to show that if
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry, then
\ran T_{\Phi}^{*}
is a Beurling-type invariant subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. This will prove what we want because then we can use the following equivalence

T_{\Phi}\text{ is a partial isometry }\Leftrightarrow T_{\Phi}^{*}\text{ is a partial isometry},

to conclude that
\ran T_{\Phi}
is Beurling-type. It is evident from the preceding discussion that we need to prove
\ran T_{\Phi}^{*}
is doubly commuting. In other words, for any distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the following conditions should be satisfied.

R_{i}^{*}R_{j}=R_{j}R_{i}^{*}.

Since
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry, we can further deduce that

R_{i}=M_{z_{i}}|_{\ran T_{\Phi}^{*}}=M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi},

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Now, let us establish a few conditions essential for the sequel. From Proposition
4.1
, we know that
\ran T_{\Phi}
is a
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
-joint invariant subspace, and therefore,

T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}.

Acting on the left by
M_{z_{i}}^{*}
gives

M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}.

Using identity (
2.1
), we deduce that

M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}(M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}+P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}})T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi},

and therefore, using the Toeplitz criterion in Theorem
2.1
, we get

T_{\Phi}+M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}.

Hence,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=0,

which again implies that

T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=0,

and therefore,

P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}=0.

In other words, since
P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}=I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}
, we get

(4.12)

M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}.

Since
i
was arbitrarily chosen, the above identity holds for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Now, if we act on the right of both sides by
T_{\Phi}^{*}
and on the left of both sides by
T_{\Phi}
, we get

T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}.

Using the
M_{z_{i}}
-invariance of
\ran T_{\Phi}
we get

(4.13)

M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}^{*}\quad(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}).

and therefore, using identity (
4.12
), we get

\displaystyle R_{i}^{*}R_{j}=T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi},

and

R_{j}R_{i}^{*}=M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}^{*}.

The last equality follows from the fact that
\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}
is
(M_{z_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})
-joint invariant. Therefore,
\ran T_{\Phi}^{*}
is a doubly commuting shift-invariant subspace if and only if

(4.14)

T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}=M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}^{*},

for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Using the identity (
4.12
), we can again observe that

\displaystyle T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}-T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}-M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}-M_{z_{j}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}^{*}.

Therefore, condition (
4.14
) is again equivalent to
T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}=0
. We will now show that this identity holds. From the identities (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), we can get for any distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

\displaystyle M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{j}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}.

Therefore, for any distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get

(4.15)

M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}=T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}.

From this, we can further deduce that

\displaystyle M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle+M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}+T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}.

Since the last equality on the right-hand side is self-adjoint, the above set of identities implies that for any distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get

(4.16)

M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}.

Now let us compute
C_{i}^{*}C_{i}
, where
C_{i}=P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}|_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

\displaystyle C_{i}^{*}C_{i}=P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}.

The second and last equalities uses the fact that
\ran T_{\Phi}
is
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
-joint invariant. Now, let us observe that

\displaystyle I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i}

\displaystyle=I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}-I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}+M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}.

By compressing the above identity with respect to
P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}
, we get

I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i}=P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}},

Therefore, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exists isometries

Y_{i}:\overline{(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\overline{T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}\mathcal{H}}

such that

Y_{i}(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}.

Now using conditions (
4.12
), (
4.13
), and (
4.15
), we deduce that

\displaystyle(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i})(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{j}^{*}C_{j})

\displaystyle=\big(M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}\big)\big(M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}

\displaystyle-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}+T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}

\displaystyle-T_{\Phi}M_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}+T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}+T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}

\displaystyle=0.

Since both
(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i})
and
(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{j}^{*}C_{j})
are non-negative operators, therefore, we have

(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{j}^{*}C_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

This implies that

T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z_{j}}P_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}=Y_{j}(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{j}^{*}C_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}(I_{\ker T_{\Phi}^{*}}-C_{i}^{*}C_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}Y_{i}^{*}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Partially isometric Toeplitz operators

This section will prove that partially isometric Toeplitz operators always admit a factorization into Toeplitz operators corresponding to inner symbols.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

From Theorem
1.2
, we know that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry implies that both
\ran T_{\Phi}
and
\ran T_{\phi}^{*}
are Beurling-type invariant subspace of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Thus, there exist Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
and inner functions
\Gamma(\bm{z})\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
\Psi(\bm{z})\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

\ran T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),

and

\ran T_{\Phi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}H_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

From the above identities, it follows that

T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*};\quad T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

Since
T_{\Phi}
is partial isometry, it further implies that

M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}=T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi},

and therefore,
\|M_{\Psi}^{*}h\|=\|M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}h\|
for any
h\in H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Since
M_{\Psi}
is an isometry, we have
\ran M_{\Psi}^{*}=H_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and therefore, we can define an isometry
X:H_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
by

XM_{\Psi}^{*}h=M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}h,

in other words,
XM_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}
on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Using this map, we can observe that

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}XM_{\Psi}^{*}.

This further implies that

X=M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{\Psi},

and hence,

M_{z_{i}}^{*}XM_{z_{i}}=M_{z_{i}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Gamma}^{*}M_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}}M_{\Psi}=M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}=X,

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle XX^{*}=M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{\Gamma}=M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=M_{\Gamma}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}M_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=M_{\Gamma}^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*}M_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=I_{H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})},

shows that
X
is a unitary between
H_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Again, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get

I_{H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=XX^{*}=M_{z_{i}}^{*}XM_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}X^{*}M_{z_{i}}=I_{H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}XP_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}X^{*}M_{z_{i}}.

Thus,
M_{z_{i}}^{*}XP_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}X^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0
, and hence
(I_{H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*})X^{*}M_{z_{i}}=0
and therefore,

(5.17)

X^{*}M_{z_{i}}=M_{z_{i}}X^{*},

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Similarly, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have

I_{H_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=X^{*}X=M_{z_{i}}^{*}X^{*}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}XM_{z_{i}}=I_{H_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{z_{i}}^{*}X^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}XM_{z_{i}}.

This implies that
M_{z_{i}}^{*}X^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{i}}^{*}}XM_{z_{i}}=0
and thus, we get

(5.18)

XM_{z_{i}}=M_{z_{i}}X,

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. From conditions (
5.17
) and (
5.18
), it implies that
X
must be a constant unitary from
H_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
to
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Therefore, we can re-write the map
X
as
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes X
, where
X:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
is a unitary. Based on these observations, we can now write

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}(I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes X)M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{*},

where
\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{z}):=\Psi(\bm{z})X^{*}\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is an inner function. Since
T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator, using Theorem
1.3
, we get that

\big(\Gamma(\bm{\lambda})-\Gamma_{k}(\bm{\lambda})\big)\big(\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{\mu})-\tilde{\Psi}_{k}(\bm{\mu})\big)^{*}=0,

for all
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Conversely, if the Toeplitz operator admits a factorization like
T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
. Then

T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{\Psi}M_{\Gamma}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*},

shows that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Following the same method as in Theorem
2.1
, we can show that
X\in\mathcal{B}(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if
(M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}})^{*}X(M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})=X
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}
are different Hilbert spaces. Using this algebraic condition, we can follow the proof of Theorem
1.5
, to obtain a similar characterization for partiallly isometric
T_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
. Our results can be directly used when
\mbox{dim }\mathcal{E}=\mbox{dim }\mathcal{F}
. In particular, we can always construct a unitary
U:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
, which can be further extended to an unitary
I\otimes U:H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. So, if
T_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
is a partial isometry then
U^{*}T_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
is a partially isometric Toeplitz operator as well. Using Theorem
1.5
, we will get
U^{*}T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
, and hence,
T_{\Phi}=M_{U\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
.

Now, an immediate question that arises is what effect does a partially isometric Toeplitz operator have on its symbol? We have the following complete answer.

Corollary 5.4
.

If
T_{\Phi}
is a partially isometric Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then
\Phi(\bm{z})
is a partial isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
2.5
, we get

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}=T_{\Gamma\Psi^{*}}.

This implies that
\Phi(\bm{z})=\Gamma(\bm{z})\Psi(\bm{z})^{*}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Since
\Gamma(\bm{z}),\Psi(\bm{z})
are inner functions it implies that
\Gamma(\bm{z)}\Gamma(\bm{z)}^{*}
is projection-valued and
\Psi(\bm{z})^{*}\Psi(\bm{z})=I_{\mathcal{F}}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and therefore,

\Phi(\bm{z})\Phi(\bm{z})^{*}=\Gamma(\bm{z})\Psi^{*}(\bm{z})\Psi(\bm{z})\Gamma^{*}(\bm{z})=\Gamma(\bm{z})\Gamma^{*}(\bm{z})\quad(\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}^{n}).

This shows that
\Phi(\bm{z})
is a partial isometry, a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The converse direction is not true, and we present an example here. Let
\theta(z)=\frac{z}{2}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and let us consider the symbol
\Phi\in L_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
defined by

\Phi(e^{it}):=\begin{bmatrix}\theta(e^{it})&(1-|\theta(e^{it})|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\theta(e^{it})&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

It is clear that

\Phi(e^{it})^{*}\Phi(e^{it})=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}.

Thus,
\Phi(e^{it})
is a partial isometry on
\mathbb{T}
. However, the corresponding Toeplitz operator

T_{\Phi}=\begin{bmatrix}T_{\theta}&T_{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\in H_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

is not partially isometric. One can easily see that if we want
T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}
, then a necessary condition is
T_{\theta}T_{\theta}^{*}=T_{\frac{1}{4}}
. This is not possible as
\theta
is an analytic function.

We will now characterize partially isometric Toeplitz operators with analytic symbols.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Phi\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
M_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if and only if there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
, an inner function
\Gamma\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, and an isometry
V:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
such that

\Phi(\bm{z})=\Gamma(\bm{z})V^{*}\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Proof.

If
M_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry, then by Theorem
1.5
, we will get the following factorization

M_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*},

for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
, and inner functions
\Gamma(\bm{z}),\Psi(\bm{z})\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Since
\Phi
is a bounded analytic function, we know

M_{z_{i}}M_{\Phi}=M_{\Phi}M_{z_{i}},

which gives,

M_{z_{i}}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}},

and therefore,

M_{\Gamma}M_{z_{i}}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}.

Using the fact that
M_{\Gamma}
is an isometry we get

M_{z_{i}}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}.

This implies that
M_{\Psi}
must be a constant isometry from
H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
to
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus,
M_{\Psi}=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes V
for some isometry
V:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
, and using this we get

M_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes V^{*})=M_{\Gamma V^{*}}.

Conversely, if the bounded analytic function admits the factorization
\Phi=\Gamma V^{*}
for some inner function
\Gamma\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, and some isometry
V:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
, then

T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes V^{*})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes V)M_{\Gamma}^{*}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*},

shows that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry. This completes the proof.
∎

We will now characterize partially isometric Toeplitz operators, which are hyponormal. Let us first recall that a bounded operator
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be hyponormal if
T^{*}T\geq TT^{*}
. We refer to the monograph
[
26
]
for an elaborate discussion on these operators. In the context of hyponormal Toeplitz operators, there has been important development starting with the celebrated result of Cowen
[
5
]
. In recent times, several authors have contributed to its extension to the case of block Toeplitz operators, for instance, Gu et al.
[
17
]
and Curto et al.
[
6
]
. One of the criteria for a block Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
on
H_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
to be hyponormal is that the symbol
\Phi
should be normal a.e. on
\mathbb{T}

[
17
, Theorem 3.3]
. The proof shows that this feature is due to the finite dimensionality of the underlying Hilbert space in
H_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. The following result shows that this feature may not hold if we consider hyponormal Toeplitz operators on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, where
\mathcal{E}
can be infinite-dimensional. Before going into the proof, let us highlight that the following result is an extension of
[
11
, Corollary 5.1]
, and the initial part of the proof follows from their method.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
T_{\Phi}
be a partially isometric Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

T_{\Phi}
is hyponormal,

(ii)
there exists Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
and inner functions
\Psi\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
\Theta\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

Proof.

T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry will imply that
T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
. Using this identity, we get,

T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}-M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*}.

Now, hyponormality of
T_{\Phi}
implies that

M_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}^{*}\leq M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

By Douglas’s lemma
[
12
]
, there exists a contraction
Z:H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

M_{\Gamma}=M_{\Psi}Z.

This implies that for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

M_{\Psi}M_{z_{i}}Z=M_{z_{i}}M_{\Psi}Z=M_{z_{i}}M_{\Gamma}=M_{\Gamma}M_{z_{i}}=M_{\Psi}ZM_{z_{i}},

Since
M_{\Psi}
is an isometry, the above identity further implies that
ZM_{z_{i}}=M_{z_{i}}Z
. Thus, there exists
\Theta\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

Z=M_{\Theta},

Furthermore,
\Theta
is an inner function because so are
\Gamma
and
\Psi
. Hence,

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

Conversely, if
T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Psi}^{*}
, then

T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}^{*}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Theta}^{*}M_{\Psi}^{*}\leq M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}=T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi},

implies that
T_{\Phi}
is hyponormal. This completes the proof.
∎

Normal operators on Hilbert spaces, that is, operators
T
on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
[T^{*},T]=0
, are important cases of hyponormal operators. Let us end this section by characterizing partially isometric and normal Toeplitz operators.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
T_{\Phi}
be a partially isometric Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

T_{\Phi}
is normal,

(ii)
there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
, an inner function
\Psi\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, and a unitary
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})
such that

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}UM_{\Psi}^{*}.

Proof.

First let us note that if
T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}UM_{\Psi}^{*}
, then

T_{\Phi}^{*}T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}=T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}.

Hence,
T_{\Phi}
is both normal as well as a partial isometry. Next, suppose that the partially isometric
T_{\Phi}
is also normal. Since a normal operator is also hyponormal, we can use the above Theorem
5.6
to claim that there exist Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
and inner functions
\Psi\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
\Theta\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

Now,
T_{\Phi}
being normal further implies that

M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}^{*}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}^{*}M_{\Psi}^{*},

and hence,

M_{\Psi}M_{\Psi}^{*}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Theta}M_{\Theta}^{*}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

This further implies that

M_{\Psi}(I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{\Theta}M_{\Theta}^{*})M_{\Psi}^{*}=0,

and therefore, if we act on the above identity by
M_{\Psi}^{*}
on the left side and by
M_{\Psi}
on the right side, then we get

I_{H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-M_{\Theta}M_{\Theta}^{*}=0.

Since
M_{\Theta}
was an isometry to begin with, the above identity further implies that
M_{\Theta}
is a unitary operator. Using Corollary
2.4
, we can deduce that
M_{\Theta}
must be a constant unitary, in other words,
M_{\Theta}=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes U
, where
U:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
is a unitary. Hence, the Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
becomes

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Psi}UM_{\Psi}^{*}.

This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Vectorial Toeplitz operators on Hilbert spaces

An important aspect of working with Toeplitz operators on vector-valued Hardy spaces is that they give important information about general operators on abstract Hilbert spaces. In particular, we can use our main result in Section 3 to prove a characterization for vectorial Toeplitz operators. Throughout this section, we assume
\mathcal{H}
to be a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space over complex numbers. To begin with, let us recall a few definitions essential for the sequel.

Definition 6.1
.

An operator
S
on
\mathcal{H}
is a
shift
if
S
is an isometry and
S^{*n}\rightarrow 0
in the strong operator topology as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Definition 6.2
.

A tuple of commuting shift operators
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
is
doubly commuting
if
S_{i}^{*}S_{j}=S_{j}S_{i}^{*}
for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

For a tuple of doubly commuting shift operators,
S
on
\mathcal{H}
, there exists a subspace of utmost importance, namely the wandering subspace denoted by

\mathcal{W}(S):=\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\cap}}\ker S_{k}^{*}.

This subspace generates the Hilbert space in the following manner.

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}S^{\bm{k}}\mathcal{W}(S),

for instance, see
[
33
, Corollary 3.2]
and originally
[
20
]
for a single shift operator. Using this structure of
\mathcal{H}
, we can define a unitary
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{W}(S)}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
by the following action.

U(S^{\bm{k}}\eta)=M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}\eta,

for all
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{W}(S)
. The construction of the unitary further facilitates the following intertwining relations

(6.19)

M_{z_{k}}U=US_{k};\quad M_{z_{k}}^{*}U=US_{k}^{*}\quad(k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}).

All the above facts will be used in the sequel. Motivated by the definition set by Page in
[
28
]
, we define vectorial Toeplitz operators corresponding to a tuple of shifts in the following manner.

Definition 6.3
.

Given a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting shift operators
S=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
, a bounded operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is said be
S
-
Toeplitz
if
S_{k}^{*}TS_{k}=T
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

The condition
S_{k}^{*}TS_{k}=T
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, imply that

M_{z_{k}}^{*}UTU^{*}M_{z_{k}}=US_{k}^{*}TS_{k}U^{*}=UTU^{*},

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. From Theorem
2.1
, it follows that
UTU^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator on
H_{\mathcal{W}(S)}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and hence, there exists a symbol
\Phi\in L_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

(6.20)

UTU^{*}=T_{\Phi}.

Our main result in this section is also related to
S
-analytic operators as introduced by Rosenblum and Rovnyak in
[
30
, Section 1.6, Page 6]
.

Definition 6.4
.

Given a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting shift operators
S=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a bounded operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
S
-analytic if

S_{i}T=TS_{i}\quad(\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}).

We are now ready to characterize
S
-Toeplitz operators on
\mathcal{H}
that are partially isometric.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
S=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
be a
n
-tuple of doubly commuting shift operators on
\mathcal{H}
and let
T
be a
S
-Toeplitz operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

T
is a partial isometry,

(ii)
there exists partially isometric
S
-analytic operators
W_{1},W_{2}
with equal final spaces such that

T=W_{1}W_{2}^{*},

and the pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
satisfy the following equivalent conditions for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

(a)

S_{k}^{*}W_{1}P_{\ker S_{k}^{*}}W_{2}^{*}S_{k}=0
,

(b)

S_{k}^{*}W_{1}P_{\mathcal{W}(S)}W_{2}^{*}S_{k}=0
.

Proof.

Since
T
is a partial isometry, the operator
UTU^{*}
is also a partial isometry, and hence, using condition (
6.20
), we get
T_{\Phi}
is also a partial isometry. Now, from Theorem
1.5
, it follows that there must exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
and inner functions
\Gamma,\Psi\in H_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

T_{\Phi}=M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}.

Since
\Gamma
is an inner function
\Gamma(e^{it}):\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
is an isometry a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We can choose any such isometry
\Gamma(e^{it})
and extend it to an isometry
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes\Gamma(e^{it}):H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. For the sake of computation, we will denote this isometry simply as
i
. This construction implies that

(6.21)

M_{z_{k}}i=iM_{z_{k}};\quad M_{z_{k}}^{*}i=iM_{z_{k}},

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Furthermore, using this isometry, we get

T=U^{*}T_{\Phi}U=U^{*}M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}U=U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}UU^{*}iM_{\Psi}^{*}U=W_{1}W_{2}^{*},

where
W_{1}=U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}U
and
W_{2}=U^{*}M_{\Psi}i^{*}U
. Now, for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get

\displaystyle W_{1}S_{k}=U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}US_{k}=U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}M_{z_{k}}U=U^{*}M_{z_{k}}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}U

\displaystyle=S_{k}U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}U

\displaystyle=S_{k}W_{1},

and similarly,

\displaystyle W_{2}S_{k}=U^{*}M_{\Psi}i^{*}US_{k}=U^{*}M_{\Psi}i^{*}M_{z_{k}}U=U^{*}M_{z_{k}}M_{\Psi}i^{*}U

\displaystyle=S_{k}U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}U

\displaystyle=S_{k}W_{2}.

Thus, we see that both
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are
S
-analytic operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Furthermore,

W_{1}^{*}W_{1}=U^{*}ii^{*}U=W_{2}^{*}W_{2},

shows that both
W_{1}
, and
W_{2}
are partial isometries with equal final spaces. Moreover, using Lemma
3.2
, we know that
M_{\Gamma}M_{\Psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}=0;\quad M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}P_{\mathcal{E}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}=0.

Now, the above conditions are again equivalent to

(6.22)

U^{*}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}UU^{*}iP_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}U=0,

(6.23)

U^{*}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}UU^{*}iP_{\mathcal{E}}M_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}U=0,

respectively. Using the intertwining relations in (
6.21
), the first condition (
6.22
), turns into

U^{*}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}UU^{*}P_{\ker M_{z_{k}}^{*}}iM_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{k}}U=0.

Using the intertwining relations in (
6.19
), we get

S_{k}^{*}U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}UP_{\ker S_{k}^{*}}U^{*}iM_{\Psi}^{*}US_{k}=0,

that is,
S_{k}^{*}W_{1}P_{\ker S_{k}^{*}}W_{2}^{*}S_{k}=0
. Similarly, condition (
6.23
) is equivalent to

U^{*}M_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}UU^{*}P_{\mathcal{E}}iM_{\Psi}^{*}M_{z_{i}}U=0,

and therefore, using the intertwining relations in (
6.19
), we get

S_{k}^{*}U^{*}M_{\Gamma}i^{*}UP_{\mathcal{W}(S)}U^{*}iM_{\Psi}^{*}US_{k}=0,

Thus, this condition is again equivalent to

S_{k}^{*}W_{1}P_{\mathcal{W}(S)}W_{2}^{*}S_{k}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the above characterization, it is indeed surprising how the product
T=W_{1}W_{2}^{*}
can be a partial isometry without any one of the
W_{1},W_{2}
being an isometry. It is because both the partial isometries have equal final spaces. In particular, this implies that

W_{1}W_{2}^{*}W_{2}W_{1}^{*}=W_{1}W_{1}^{*}W_{1}W_{1}^{*}=W_{1}W_{1}^{*}.

Hence,
W_{1}W_{2}^{*}
must be a partial isometry.

7.
An alternative proof for Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

This section will adapt the preceding results’ ideas to characterize partially isometric Toeplitz operators on the scalar-valued Hardy space on the unit polydisc. This is an alternative approach to recently obtained results by Deepak–Pradhan–Sarkar in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\zeta,\psi
be bounded analytic functions
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.Then the following are equivalent

(i)

M_{\zeta}M_{\psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator,

(ii)

M_{\zeta}
and
M_{\psi}^{*}
depend on disjoint set of variables.

(iii)

[M_{\zeta},M_{\psi}^{*}]=0
.

Proof.

Let us denote the bounded analytic functions
\zeta,\psi
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
in the following form

\zeta(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\bm{l}}z^{\bm{l}};\quad\psi(\bm{z}):=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}b_{\bm{m}}z^{\bm{m}}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

From Theorem
3.3
, we know that
M_{\zeta}M_{\psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if,

a_{\bm{l}+e_{i}}\bar{b}_{\bm{m}+e_{i}}=0.

for all
\bm{l},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. From this identity it follows that for any given
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, either
a_{\bm{l}+e_{i}}=0
for all
\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, or
b_{\bm{m}+e_{i}}=0
for all
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
or both. If
a_{\bm{l}+e_{i}}=0
, for all
\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then it implies that
\zeta(\bm{z})
does not depend on the
z_{i}
-variable. Similarly, if
b_{\bm{m}+e_{i}}=0
, for all
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then it will imply that
\psi(\bm{z})
does not depend on the
z_{i}
-variable. Thus, from this discussion it follows that if
M_{\zeta}M_{\psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator then for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, any one of the following cases can hold:

(i)

\zeta(\bm{z})
depend on the variable
z_{i}
, but
\psi(\bm{z})
does not depend on the variable
z_{i}
,

(ii)

\psi(\bm{z})
depend on the variable
z_{i}
, but
\zeta(\bm{z})
does not depend on the variable
z_{i}
,

(iii)
both
\zeta(\bm{z})
and
\psi(\bm{z})
does not depend on the variable
z_{i}
.

This completes the
(i)\implies(ii)
proof. The other directions
(ii)\implies(iii)
, and
(iii)\implies(i)
can be easily verified.
∎

Let us end this section with a new proof for the main result in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 7.2
.

A Toeplitz operator
T_{\phi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a partial isometry if and only if there exist inner functions
\zeta,\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
depending on the disjoint set of variables such that

T_{\phi}=M_{\psi}^{*}M_{\zeta}.

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
. Since
T_{\phi}
is a partial isometry, we know from Theorem
1.2
, that both
\ran T_{\phi}
and
\ran T_{\phi}^{*}
are Beurling type invariant subspaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Hence, there must exist inner functions
\gamma,\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

\ran T_{\phi}=M_{\gamma}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n});\quad\ran T_{\phi}^{*}=M_{\psi}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),

If we follow the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we can construct an unitary
X:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

T_{\phi}=M_{\gamma}XM_{\psi}^{*}.

In other words,
T_{\phi}=M_{\zeta}M_{\psi}^{*}
, where

\zeta(\bm{z}):=\lambda\gamma(\bm{z})\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}),

for some uni-modular constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. From the above Theorem
7.1
,
M_{\zeta}M_{\psi}^{*}
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if
\zeta
and
\psi
depend on the disjoint set of variables. Thus, we can write

T_{\phi}=M_{\psi}^{*}M_{\zeta}.

Conversely, if the Toeplitz operator admits the factorization
T_{\phi}=M_{\psi}^{*}M_{\zeta}
for some inner functions
\psi
and
\zeta
depending on disjoint set of variables, then

T_{\phi}T_{\phi}^{*}=M_{\psi}^{*}M_{\zeta}M_{\zeta}^{*}M_{\psi}=M_{\psi}^{*}M_{\psi}M_{\zeta}M_{\zeta}^{*}=M_{\zeta}M_{\zeta}^{*},

shows that
T_{\phi}
is a partial isometry. This completes the proof.
∎

Our methods, as shown in the above result, can be used similarly to give an alternative proof of the following characterization of Brown and Douglas
[
2
]
.

Theorem 7.3
.

A Toeplitz operator
T_{\phi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a partial isometry if and only if there exists a inner function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
T_{\phi}=M_{\theta}\text{ or }T_{\phi}=M_{\theta}^{*}.

8.
Some related questions

Based on the results of this article, we can ask several interesting questions for Toeplitz operators on vector-valued Hardy spaces worthy of further investigation.

(
I
) Characterize Toeplitz operators with shift-invariant range spaces.

A significant contribution of this article is to show that if we start with a somewhat nice Toeplitz operator, then the range is Beurling-type. This gives rise to a fascinating problem.

(
II
) Characterize Toeplitz operators with Beurling-type range spaces.

In Corollary
5.4
, we have seen that a partially isometric Toeplitz operator has a partially isometric symbol, a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, but what can we say about the converse?

(
III
) Characterize symbols
\Phi\in L_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that
T_{\Phi}
is a partial isometry.

Unlike in the scalar cases, it is still not clear when partially isometric Toeplitz operators on
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
become power partial isometries, so we end with the following question.

(
IV
) Characterize Toeplitz operators, which are power partial isometries.

In Section

. Similarly decompose

Clearly

is the only interval on which one of the strings may properly hit it and the other not.

if

, but the difference is at most the difference between the lower bound and upper bounds on

, which is (
14

1+(1+o(1))2{\rm e}^{-1}l^{1+\delta}=(1+o(1))2{\rm e}^{-1}l^{1+\delta}.

(16)

Hence, in the following lemma, we can apply Azuma’s inequality (

) with

.

Lemma 4.3

Let

, with

sufficiently large. Then with probability

>1-2\exp(-(1-o(1))l^{\delta}/8)

the number of segments properly hit is at least

.

For

and

, Lemma
4.2
gives us an estimate on the expected value of properly hit intervals. With our assumption

, the terms

and

become negligible, so

is approximately
1/2
of the expected value. When we apply Azuma’s inequality (Theorem
2.2
) with this

and
K
estimated above (
16
), the exponent in the inequality becomes (omitting factors

)

\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2K^{2}n}=\frac{n^{2}}{2.{\rm e}^{2}l^{4}.4{\rm e}^{-2}l^{2+2\delta}n}=\frac{n}{8l^{6+2\delta}}\geq\frac{l^{\delta}}{8}.

We now consider random subsets of
S
of size
k
.
In the same way as in Lemma
3.1
, we get from Lemma
4.3
:

Lemma 4.4

Let
n\geq l^{6+3\delta}
. For
X
, a random subset of
S
size

,
X
properly hits at least
n/{\rm e}l^{2}
segments

with probability
1-2\exp(-(1-o(1))l^{\delta}/8)
.

We now define cubes similarly as in Definition
3.2
.

Definition 4.2

Let
J\subseteq[n]
be nonempty and
R\subseteq S
such that

•

for every
j\in J
,
|R\cap S_{j})|=\emptyset
and
|R\cap(S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{j-1})|=0\mod l
,

•

|R|+l\cdot|J|=k
.

Then
the cube
C^{\prime}(J,R)
is the set of
k
-element subsets
X\subseteq S
such that

•

for every

,

properly hits

,

•

for all

.

The dimension of the cube is

.

If

properly hits

, then, clearly, there is a maximal cube

of dimension

.
Thus Lemma

guarantees that
{S\choose k}
is covered by

-sets of disjoint cubes of dimensions
\geq n/{\rm e}l^{2}
except for an exponentially small fraction of the elements of

, as stated in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.1

All
k
-sets in
{S\choose k}
, except for a fraction of

are contained in disjoint cubes of dimensions
\geq n/{\rm e}l^{2}
.

Let
\kappa
be a proper coloring of the shift graph
Sh(N,l)
by
3
colors; we now prefer to use colors
0,1,2
. The coloring
\phi
of
{N\choose k}
by

colors is defined as follows:

\phi(X)=(\kappa(X_{1}),\dots,\kappa(X_{n})),

where
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
is a partition of

into
n
consecutive segments of length
l
. (Recall that
k=nl
.)

The key difference between the non-explicit and explicit constructions is that we define the second coloring in a different way—we simply add the colors in
\phi(X)
modulo
c
. Thus the coloring
\gamma^{\prime}
of
[N]\choose k
is defined by

\gamma^{\prime}(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa(X_{i})\mod c.

Since Lemma
4.4
guarantees that all
k
-element sets, except for an exponentially small fraction of them, are covered by disjoint cubes of dimensions
\geq n/{\rm e}l^{2}
, it suffices to show that
\gamma^{\prime}
has exponentially low discrepancy on every cube of dimension
\geq n/{\rm e}l^{2}
.

Let
C
be a cube of dimension
d\geq n/{\rm e}l^{2}
and let
S_{i}
be a segment properly hit by the sets of
C
. For a set
X\in C
, one of the segments
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
properly hits
S_{i}
; let this be

. As in the previous construction,

is uniquely determined by

. As before, let

denote the set of such indices

, i.e.,

B_{C}:=\{j\in[n]\ |\ \exists i\in[n]\ \forall X\in C,\ X_{i}\mbox{ properly hits }S_{j}\}.

For each
j\in B_{C}
, there are two possibilities for
X_{j}
. The two possible versions are in a shift position, so they have two different colors
a_{j},b_{j}\in\{0,1,2\}
. We consider two possibilities:

1.

|a_{j}-b_{j}|=1
,

2.

|a_{j}-b_{j}|=2
.

According to these possibilities, we split
B_{C}
into two parts
B^{1}_{C}
and
B^{2}_{C}
. Let
d_{1}:=|B^{1}_{C}|
and
d_{2}:=|B^{2}_{C}|
, so
d_{1}+d_{2}=d
. Consider two cases.

Case 1,
d_{1}\geq d/2
. Then we split cube
C
into disjoint cubes of dimension

by fixing

s on every
j\in B^{2}_{C}
. Let
C^{\prime}
be one of these

cubes. Observe that the elements of
C^{\prime}
are in 1–1 correspondence with sequences
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{d_{1}})\in\{0,1\}^{d_{1}}
with each

being assigned
\xi_{j}\in\{0,1\}
depending on the color of the

-set

. Consequently, the colors
\gamma^{\prime}(X)
for

can be represented by

A+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\xi_{j}\mod c,

where

is constant and
\xi_{j}\in\{0,1\}
. According to Lemma
4.1
, there exists a constant
\alpha_{c}:=1-\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(2\pi/c))<1
such that the frequency of every modulus is

up to an error
\alpha_{c}^{d_{1}}
. Since
d_{1}\geq n/2{\rm e}l^{2}>l
, this error is negligible when compared with our target error
\exp(-\varepsilon l^{\delta})
. So, like in the first construction, the main error comes from the fraction of
k
-sets that are not covered by large cubes, which is estimated in Corollary
4.1
.

Case 2,
d_{2}\geq d/2
. The same argument shows that the colors
\gamma^{\prime}(X)
in a cube of dimension

can be represented by

but because in

we can divide by 2, we get the same conclusion.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

5
Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Recall the lemma:

Lemma 5.1
(
2.1
restated)

Let
l\leq N\leq 4\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{l-3}(2)
. Then the chromatic number of
Sh(N,l)
is
\leq 3
and, for
N\geq 2l+1
, we get equality. Moreover, such a
3
-coloring can be explicitly defined.

We start by introducing some notation and making preliminary considerations.
A proper coloring of a directed graph
G
is a proper coloring of
G
with the orientation of the edges ignored. We will denote by
\chi(G)
the chromatic number of
G
, i.e., the least number of colors needed to color the graph. There is a natural orientation of the edges of a shift graph which makes it a directed acyclic graph. We will use the same notation for the directed shift graphs.
Although directed shift graphs are acyclic, we will also need graphs with cycles.

For a directed graph
G
, we denote by
\partial G
the graph whose vertices are edges of
G
and the edges of
\partial G
are oriented paths of length 2.

We note that for
l\geq 1
,
Sh(N,l)\cong\partial^{l-1}Sh(N,1)
. For example, the vertices of
\partial^{2}Sh(N,1)
are
((a,b),(b,c)
), where
1\leq a<b<c\leq N
, and they correspond to vertices
\{a,b,c\}
in
Sh(N,3)
.

Furthermore, we will need the tower-of-square-roots-of-two function, which is defined by

\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{1}(x):=2x\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i+1}:=2^{\frac{1}{2}\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(x)}=\sqrt{2}^{\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(x)}.

We observe that
\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(x)
is an even number for all integers
i\geq 0
and
x\geq 1
.

Below we summarize some well-known results and, for completeness, we also sketch their proofs. Clearly
\chi(Sh(N,1))=N
.
The chromatic number of shift graphs for larger
l
can be estimated using the following well-known recurrence relations
[
3
,
9
,
12
]
.

Lemma 5.2

Let
s\geq 1
be an integer. Then

1.

\chi(G)\leq 2^{s}
implies
\chi(\partial G)\leq 2s
;

2.

\chi(Sh(N,h))\leq{2s\choose s}
implies
\chi(Sh(N,h+1))\leq 2s
.

1. If
\chi(G)\leq 2^{s}
, then the edge set can be covered by
s
bipartite graphs. Each of these bipartite graphs can be further decomposed into two bipartite graphs in which each edge has the tail in one set of the bipartition and the head in the complement. Such sets are independent in
\partial G
. Hence
\chi(\partial G)\leq 2s
.

2. Let
\alpha
be a coloring of
Sh(N,h)
by
{2s\choose s}
colors. Suppose that the colors are subsets of size
s
of
[2s]
. We define a coloring
\beta
of
Sh(N,h+1)
by

\beta(\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{h},x_{h+1}\})=c,

where
c\in[2s]
is an arbitrary (say, the first) element such that

c\in\alpha(\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{h}\})\setminus\alpha(\{x_{2},\dots,x_{h},x_{h+1}\}).

We leave to the reader to verify that this defines a proper coloring of
{Sh(N,h+1)}
.

Since
\partial(Sh(N,h))\cong Sh(N,h+1)
, Lemma
5.2
.1 implies

\mbox{If }\chi(Sh(N,h))\leq\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i+1}(s)\mbox{ then }\chi(Sh(N,h+1))\leq\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(s).

(17)

(Recall that
\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i+1}(s)=2^{\frac{1}{2}\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(s)}
and
\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(s)
is an even number.)
Thus
(l-2)
-times repeated application of the above implication (
17
) yields that

N=\chi(Sh(N,1))\leq\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{l-3}(s)\mbox{ implies }\chi(Sh(N,l-3))\leq\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{1}(s)=2s.

For
s=4
, this gives us:

Fact 5.1

If
N\leq\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{l-3}(4)
, then
\chi(Sh(N,l-3))\leq 8
.

Since
\chi(Sh(N,l-3))\leq 2^{3}
,
applying Lemma
5.2
.1 again yields
\chi(Sh(N,l-2))\leq 6
. Now we apply Lemma
5.2
.2 and get
\chi(Sh(N,l-1))\leq 4
. Given a
4
-coloring of
Sh(N,l-1)
, we get a homomorphism
\phi
of
Sh(N,l-1)
into
\vec{K}_{4}
, which is the directed graph on four vertices with all twelve ordered pairs as edges. As is well-known (see
[

,

,
1
]
),

can be colored by
3

is defined by

\displaystyle\psi((a,b),(b,c)):

\displaystyle=b\mbox{ if }b\neq 4

\displaystyle=d\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{a,c\}\mbox{ if }b=4.

Thus composing

with

, which proves

Fact 5.2

If
N\leq\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{l-3}(4)
, then
\chi(Sh(N,l))\leq 3
.

If
N\geq 2l+1
, then

because
Sh(2l+1,l)\subseteq Sh(N,l)
contains an undirected cycle of length

To finish the proof of Lemma
5.1
, it remains to estimate from below the tower function

using the standard tower function

. Toward that end we observe that for

2^{2x}=\sqrt{2}^{4x}\geq 4\cdot 2^{x}=2^{x+2}.

(18)

This is equivalent to
\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{2}(2x)\geq 4\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{2}(x)

\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i+1}(2x)=\sqrt{2}^{\overline{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(2x)}\geq\sqrt{2}^{4\cdot{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits}_{i}(x)}\geq 4\cdot 2^{\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{i}(x)}=4\cdot\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{i+1}(x),

where the first inequality is the induction assumption and the second one follows from (

Hence we have, for all

In particular, for

Since the colorings in Lemma

can be explicitly defined, the 3-coloring of

can also be explicitly defined.

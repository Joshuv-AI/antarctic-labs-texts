Geometrically irreducible p p -adic local systems are de Rham up to a twist
Alexander Petrov

Abstract
. We prove that any geometrically irreducible ℚ ¯ p \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p} -local system on a smooth algebraic variety over a p p -adic field K K becomes de Rham after a twist by a character of the Galois group of K K . In particular, for any geometrically irreducible ℚ ¯ p \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p} -local system on a smooth variety over a number field the associated projective representation of the fundamental group automatically satisfies the assumptions of the relative Fontaine-Mazur conjecture. The proof uses p p -adic Simpson and Riemann-Hilbert correspondences of Diao-Lan-Liu-Zhu and the Sen operator on the decompletions of those developed by Shimizu. Along the way, we observe that a p p -adic local system on a smooth geometrically connected algebraic variety over K K is Hodge-Tate if its stalk at one closed point is a Hodge-Tate Galois representation. Moreover, we prove a version of the main theorem for local systems with arbitrary geometric monodromy, which allows us to conclude that the Galois action on the pro-algebraic completion of π 1 et \pi_{1}^{\text{et}} is de Rham.

(\mathrm{ii})

As a byproduct of the above method, we show the following rigidity statement for the Hodge-Tate property of fibers of a local system on an algebraic variety

Proposition 1.3

(Proposition
7.2
& Corollary
7.3
)
.

If
{\mathbb{L}}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system on a smooth geometrically connected algebraic variety

over
K
, then the Higgs sheaf

is locally free. In particular,

does not depend on the choice of a closed point
x\in X
and, if the representation of the Galois group
\Gal{\overline{k(x)}/k(x)}
is Hodge-Tate for some closed point
x\in X
then the local system
{\mathbb{L}}
is Hodge-Tate.

The proof rests on the observation that the Higgs bundles attached to local systems on connected algebraic varieties via
p
-adic Simpson correspondence satisfy a special property (Lemma
7.1
): if a section annihilated by the Higgs field vanishes at one point then it is identically zero. This forces the Sen operator to be semi-simple if it is known that it is semi-simple at one point. The statement of this result is similar to Liu-Zhu’s rigidity theorem for the de Rham condition
[
LZ17
, Theorem 1.3]
and Shimizu’s constancy of Hodge-Tate weights that was already used above. The proofs, however, have different flavors and are exploiting entirely different effects: the rigidity theorems of
[
LZ17
]
and
[
Shi18
]
are local statements that are proven for arbitrary smooth rigid-analytic bases while our result is global: it uses crucially the comparison between the global sections of a local system and the associated Higgs bundle. It might well be the case that the rigidity is false for local systems on non-partially proper rigid analytic varieties.

Remark 2.6

In part (iii) the condition on the existence of a

-lattice is necessary. Consider a Tate elliptic curve

3.
Decompleted Simpson and Riemann-Hilbert correspondences

The decompleted versions of

We will start with a general discussion of decompletions in relative setting that will later be specialized to the cases of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{H}}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
. For the duration of this section let
Y
be a smooth quasi-compact rigid-analytic variety over
K
which we will view as an adic space over
\Spa(K,{\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. Consider the sheaves

, and
{\mathcal{O}}_{{Y}_{K_{\infty}}}{((t))}:={\mathcal{O}}_{{Y}_{K_{\infty}}}{[[t]]}\otimes_{K_{\infty}[[t]]}K_{\infty}((t))
. Denote by
{Y}_{K_{\infty}}
,

, and
{Y}_{K_{\infty}((t))}
the ringed spaces
({Y},{\mathcal{O}}_{{Y}_{K_{\infty}}})
,
(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{K_{\infty}}}{[[t]]})
, and
({Y},{\mathcal{O}}_{{Y}_{K_{\infty}}}{((t))})
respectively. We will call locally free sheaves of finite rank on these ringed spaces simply ’vector bundles’. Recall that
{Y}_{\widehat{K}_{\infty}}
is the base change
{Y}\times_{\Spa(K,{\mathcal{O}}_{K})}\Spa(\widehat{K}_{\infty},{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{K}_{\infty}})
and
{\mathcal{Y}}^{+}
,
{\mathcal{Y}}
are the ringed spaces
({Y}_{\widehat{K}_{\infty}},{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}L^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}})

Note that it is not immediate that a vector bundle on some

gives rise to a vector bundle on

because the underlying topological spaces

and

are different. The next lemma, however, shows that one can always find a cover of

that trivializes a vector bundle on

Lemma 3.1
.

(i) For any quasi-compact open
U\subset Y_{\widehat{K}_{\infty}}
there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an open
U_{n}\subset Y_{K_{n}}
such that
U=U_{n}\times_{K_{n}}\widehat{K}_{\infty}
.

(ii) If
E
is a vector bundle on

for some

then there exists a finite cover of
Y=\bigcup\limits_{i}U_{i}
such that the cover
\{U_{i,K_{n}}\}_{i\in I}
of

trivializes

.

Proof.

(i) Since it is enough to prove the statement of each member of a finite cover
U=\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}V_{i}
we may assume that
Y
is affinoid and
U
is a rational subset. The claim is then given by
[
Ber07
, Lemma 2.1.3(ii)]

(ii) As

is assumed to be quasi-compact, we may assume that it is moreover affinoid

so

is associated to a finite projective module

over

. We will prove the statement by induction on the maximum of ranks of restrictions of

to connected components of

(we cannot assume that

is connected even if it is given that

is connected). It is enough to find a cover
Y=\bigcup\limits_{i}U_{i}
by affinoids
U_{i}=\Spa(A_{i},A_{i}^{+})
such that on each
U_{i,K_{n}}
there is a section
s:{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i,K_{n}}}\to E|_{U_{i,K_{n}}}
that does not vanish at the points of connected components of
U_{i,K_{n}}
where
E
is not identically zero. The exact sequences
0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i,K_{n}}}\xrightarrow{s}E|_{U_{i,K_{n}}}\to\coker(s)\to 0
split automatically by Tate’s acyclicity and we can find a trivializing cover for
\coker(s)
by induction assumption. For a point
x\in|Y|
consider the finite set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}=\pi_{n}^{-1}(x)\subset|Y_{K_{n}}|
where
\pi_{n}:Y_{K_{n}}\to Y
is the canonical projection. For each
x_{i}
such that

is not zero on the connected component containing

we can find a section

that does not vanish on some

and a function
f_{i}\in A
that does not vanish in the residue field

but vanishes in each

for
j\neq i
. If
E
vanishes on the connected component containing
x_{i}
we declare

. Let
\widetilde{V}\subset Y_{K_{n}}
be union of the open subset where the section
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}s_{i}:A_{K_{n}}\to M
does not vanish and all the connected components of

where
E
is zero. Take

to be the intersection of its
\Gal(K_{n}/K)
-orbit. Then

for some open

and
E|_{U_{i,K_{n}}}
admits a section with desired property.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

\Gamma
-equivariant vector bundle

\Gamma
-equivariant vector bundle

(3.2)

\gamma(s)=\exp(\phi\cdot\log\chi_{\mathrm{cycl}}(\gamma))s

for all
\gamma

of
\Gamma

Denote by

the presheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\otimes_{K}K_{\infty}
-modules defined on affinoid opens in

by (

First, let us prove that

is an open covering of an affinoid by affinoids, we need to check that a section

if all its restrictions to

lie in the corresponding submodules. We may assume that the covering has finitely many opens as

is quasi-compact. For each open

there is a finite subset

such that the finite set

over

By

The local construction of the operator

from
[

Definition 3.3

Lemma 3.6
.

(i) A local system
{\mathbb{L}}
is de Rham if and only if
\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})^{\phi=0}
is a vector bundle of rank
\rk_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}
on
\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}
.

(ii) A local system
{\mathbb{L}}
is Hodge-Tate if and only if
\phi
is a semi-simple endomorphism of
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
and Hodge-Tate weights of
{\mathbb{L}}

{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

(i) By definition, a local system is de Rham if and only if the invariants

form a vector bundle on
X
of rank
\rk_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}
. Suppose that
\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})^{\phi=0}
is a vector bundle of rank
\rk_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}
on
X_{K_{\infty}}
. Therefore there is a finite cover
\overline{X}=\bigcup\limits_{i}U_{i}
such that for each

the kernel

is spanned by
\rk_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}

with
\phi(s_{ij})=0
for all

. By (
3.3
) each

is stabilized by an open subgroup of
\Gamma
. Therefore for large enough

they all are stabilized by

so

. These ranks are hence equal and

is de Rham which implies that
{\mathbb{L}}
itself is de Rham.

Conversely, suppose that
{\mathbb{L}}
is de Rham. By
[
DLLZ19
, Theorem 3.2.7(2)]
the sheaf

is an isomorphism because the map

is. In particular,
\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})^{\phi=0}
is a vector bundle of rank
\rk_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}
on
\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}
.

(ii) The operator
\phi
on
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
is semi-simple with integer eigenvalues if and only if its restriction
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})|_{X}
is. The latter condition is a restatement of what it means for

to be Hodge-Tate by
[
Shi18
, Theorem 5.5]
.

4.

As before,
X

embedded as a Zariski open subvariety into a smooth proper rigid-analytic variety

such that

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that

-local system on

-lattice. If

The next lemma says that proving the theorem amounts to proving that the projective representation of the fundamental group corresponding to

Let us introduce some notation for working with the Sen endomorphism associated to a local system

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

Denote by
{\mathbb{L}}
the

-local system corresponding to

-local system

Next, we will construct a character

Lemma 4.3
.

(i)

For any character

\eta:G_{K}\to(E^{\prime})^{\times}

-Hodge-Tate weight of

(i) A very similar argument to the one we are going to give appears in the proof of

. We will prove that there exists a continuous homomorphism

. Indeed, the topological group

Let us recall the generalized eigenspace decomposition of vector bundles with respect to an endomorphism. Suppose that we are given a locally free finite rank sheaf
M
on a ringed space
(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})
equipped with an endomorphism
f:M\to M
such that the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{f}(T):=\det(T\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{M}-f)\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})[T]
has coefficients and all of its roots in a field

. If
\chi_{f}(T)
decomposes as
\prod(T-\lambda_{i})^{m_{i}}
with
\lambda_{1},\dots\lambda_{r}
being pairwise distinct elements of

then the algebra
K[T]/\chi_{f}(T)\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i}K[T]/(T-\lambda_{i})^{m_{i}}
acts on
M
and induces a decomposition
M=\bigoplus\limits_{i}M_{\lambda_{i}}
where each
M_{\lambda_{i}}:=\ker(f-\lambda_{i}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{M})^{m_{i}}
is a locally free sheaf on
(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z})
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
E
be a finite extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
such that
{\mathbb{L}}
is a scalar extension of a local system with coefficients in
E
, we will denote this local system by the same symbol
{\mathbb{L}}
. By Lemma
4.2
it is enough to prove that the projective representation of
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})
corresponding to
{\mathbb{L}}
is Hodge-Tate. That can be checked after the base change of
X
and
{\mathbb{L}}
to a finite extension of
K
, we may therefore assume that any embedding
E\to\overline{K}
factors through
K
, and all the eigenvalues of the Sen endomorphism belong to
K
.

We have
\End_{E}{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}=E\cdot\mathrm{Id}
. Viewing
{\mathbb{L}}
as a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system, we can form a Higgs bundle
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
on
\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}
of rank
[E:{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}]\cdot\rk_{E}{\mathbb{L}}
equipped with an action of
E
. This action commutes with the Sen endomorphism
\phi
of the bundle
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
. By Proposition
3.5
(iii) we have
(\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}}))\otimes_{K_{\infty}}\widehat{K}_{\infty}=(\End_{X_{\overline{K}}}{\mathbb{L}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}C)^{H_{K}}=E\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}C^{H_{K}}=E\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\widehat{K}_{\infty}
where the second equality follows from the fact that all endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
extend to endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{L}}
over
K
, so the Galois action on the endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
is trivial. Therefore, the map
E\to\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
induces an isomorphism
E\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}K_{\infty}\simeq\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
.

As we have discussed at the beginning of this section, we get a decomposition
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})=\bigoplus\limits_{\tau}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}
. By the description of endomorphisms, for each
\tau
we have that all endomorphisms of the Higgs bundle
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}
are scalars from
K_{\infty}
. For a given
\tau
, suppose that
\prod\limits_{i}(t-\lambda_{i})^{m_{i}}
with
\lambda_{i}\in\overline{K}
is the minimal polynomial of
\phi_{\tau}
on
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}
. Enlarging
K
, we may assume that all
\lambda_{i}
s lie in
K
. Consider the generalized eigenspace decomposition
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}=\bigoplus\limits_{i}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau,\lambda_{i}}
with respect to
\phi_{\tau}
. Since
\theta\circ\phi=(\phi-1)\circ\theta
, for a local section
s\in\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau,\lambda_{i}}(U)
we have

\phi_{\tau}(\theta(s))=\theta(\phi_{\tau}(s)+s)=\theta(\lambda_{i}s+s)=(\lambda_{i}+1)\cdot\theta(s)

In other words, the Higgs field
\theta
takes the summand
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau,\lambda}
to
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau,\lambda+1}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{\infty}}}\Omega^{1}_{X_{K_{\infty}}/K_{\infty}}
, for every
\lambda
.

If all
\lambda_{i}
s do not belong to a single coset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
in
\overline{K}
then
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}
is decomposable into a direct sum of two non-zero Higgs bundles, which contradicts the fact that all endomorphisms are scalars. It remains to show that
\phi_{\tau}
is semi-simple on
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}
. Define an endomorphism

of
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}=\bigoplus\limits_{i}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau,\lambda_{i}}
by
\phi_{\tau}-\lambda_{i}
on the summand
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau,\lambda_{i}}
. By construction, it commutes with the Higgs field and is nilpotent. Therefore, it is equal to zero so
\prod\limits_{i}(\phi_{\tau}-\lambda_{i})=0
on
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}
. In other words,
\phi_{\tau}
is semi-simple. We infer that for each
\tau
there is an element
a_{\tau}\in\overline{K}
such that
\phi_{\tau}
acts on
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\tau}
semi-simply with eigenvalues from

. This means the the projective representation corresponding to
{\mathbb{L}}
is Hodge-Tate.

We stay in the same setup:

.

Suppose that

(5.1)

{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}}):=\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})^{\phi^{d}=0}

d
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}}
-modules on
\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}
equipped with an integrable connection and Griffiths transverse filtration, induced from those on
\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
. Moreover, the Higgs bundle
(\bigoplus\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\gr^{i}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}}),\gr(\nabla))
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
(the isomorphism is not compatible with the Sen endomorphisms).

Proof.

First, note that
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})
is indeed a subsheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}}
-modules inside
\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
and it is preserved by the connection
\nabla
. Suppose that the weights of
{\mathbb{L}}
are contained in the set
\{i,i-1,\dots,i-n\}
for

. Then
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})
is contained in
F^{-i}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
because for
j<-i
we have
(\gr^{j}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}}))^{\phi^{d}=0}=(\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})(j))^{\phi^{d}=0}=0
as all eigenvalues of
\phi
on
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})(j)
are in the range
\{i+j,i+j-1,\dots i+j-n\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{<0}
. Similarly, the map
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})\to F^{[-i,-i+n]}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}}):=F^{-i}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})/F^{-i+n+1}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
is injective.

The subquotient

is a locally free sheaf on
\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}
(of rank

) equipped with an
{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}}}
-linear endomorphism

and

is its generalized eigenbundle with eigenvalue

. In particular, it is a direct summand of

and is therefore locally free.

The generalized eigenspace of eigenvalue

on the graded pieces
\gr^{j}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
for

is given by
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{-j}
. Therefore we have an isomorphism of vector bundles

\gr{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{j=-i}^{-i+n}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{-j}=\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})

The Higgs field
\gr(\nabla):\gr^{j}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})\to\gr^{j-1}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{K_{\infty}}}
is restricted from
\gr^{j}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})\to\gr^{j-1}\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{K_{\infty}}}
and is thus given by

so (

) is compatible with Higgs fields.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
{\mathbb{L}}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system admitting a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattice on
X
with Hodge-Tate weights in
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\End_{X_{\overline{K}}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}C
has no nilpotent elements. Then
{\mathbb{L}}
is de Rham.

Proof.

We are going to use the criterion for de Rhamness given by Lemma
3.6
(i) which says that
{\mathbb{L}}
is de Rham if the nilpotent endomorphism
\phi
of
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})
is in fact zero. Suppose that it is not zero. Let
i
be the maximal integer such that
\phi(F^{m}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}}))\subset F^{m+i}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})
for all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We will view
\phi
as a morphism
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})\to{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})(i)
of filtered vector bundles with connection where the twist denotes the shift of the filtration:
F^{m}({\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})(i))=F^{m+i}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})
. For at least one
m
the induced map
\phi:F^{m}/F^{m+1}\to F^{m+i}/F^{m+i+1}
is non-zero so
\phi
induces a non-zero nilpotent endomorphism of the Higgs bundle
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})=\bigoplus\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\gr^{m}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{L}})
.

By Proposition

(iii) the algebra
\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes_{K_{\infty}}C
has no non-zero nilpotents under the assumption of the theorem. The same is then true for the algebra
\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
. This gives us a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety over
K
and
{\mathbb{L}}
be an etale
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-etale local system such that

. Then there exists a character

Proof.

Using resolution of singularities, choose a smooth proper algebraic variety

containing

as a dense open such that

is a normal crossing divisor and apply the discussion of sections 2-5 to the analytifications of
X
,
\overline{X}
and
{\mathbb{L}}
. We are in a position to do so because
{\mathbb{L}}
, being a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system on an algebraic variety, admits a

-lattice. Theorem

applied to

provides the character

and Theorem
5.2
applied to a descent of

∎

6.
Assumptions in the relative Fontaine-Mazur conjecture

For the duration of this section let
F
be a number field and
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a smooth scheme over
{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S}
where
S
is a finite set of places of
F
. Denote by
X
the generic fiber
{\mathfrak{X}}_{F}
. We show here that the first condition of the relative Fontaine-Mazur conjecture is automatic for geometrically irreducible local systems. Together with Corollary
5.3
this completes the proof of Theorem
2
. I have learned about this fact from Daniel Litt. The proof, in the case of curves, appears in Step 2 of the proof of
[
Lit21
, Theorem 1.1.3]
. We include here (an essentially equivalent) proof for the sake of completeness. A very similar argument also appears in
[
LZ17
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Proposition 6.1

(D. Litt)
.

For any
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system
{\mathbb{L}}
on
X
such that
\End_{X_{\overline{F}}}{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{F}}}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cdot\mathrm{Id}
there exists a finite set
S^{\prime}\supset S
such that
{\mathbb{L}}
extends to a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system on
{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S^{\prime}}}
.

Proof.

Any
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system descends to an
E
-local system for some finite extension
E
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and we can choose an
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
-lattice in this descent. We may therefore work with an
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
-local system
{\mathbb{L}}
such that
\End_{X_{\overline{F}}}{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{F}}}[\frac{1}{p}]=E\cdot\mathrm{Id}
. Note that it is enough to prove the claim for
X
and
F
replaced with
X_{F^{\prime}}
and
F^{\prime}
for some finite field extension
F^{\prime}\supset F
. Hence, we may assume that
X
admits an
F
-point
x:\Spec F\to X
and we will pick a geometric point
\overline{x}
above
x
. Denote the representation corresponding to
{\mathbb{L}}
by
\rho:\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})\to GL_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{E})
. Restriction
x^{*}{\mathbb{L}}
can be viewed as a Galois representation
\rho_{x}:G_{F}\to GL_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{E})
. Replace again
F
by a finite extension to make the residual representation
\overline{\rho}_{x}:G_{F}\to GL_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{E}/p^{2})
trivial. Also, pick a smooth proper compactification
\overline{X}\supset X
such that
D=\overline{X}\setminus X
is a normal crossings divisor. After enlarging
S
we may assume that
\overline{X}
has a smooth proper model
\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S}
such that
D
extends to a horizontal normal crossings divisor
{\mathfrak{D}}\subset\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}
with
{\mathfrak{X}}=\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}
and, moreover,
x
extends to a point in
{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{O}}_{F,S})
.

Let now
S^{\prime}
be the union of
S
with the set of places of
F
such that (i) residue characteristic of
v
divides the order of the finite group
GL_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{E}/p^{2})
or (ii) the character
\det\rho_{x}
of the Galois group
G_{F}
is ramified at
v
. The set of places satisfying the second condition is finite by
[
Ser68
, Corollary III-2.2]
so
S^{\prime}
is finite. We will now prove that the local system
{\mathbb{L}}
extends to a local system on
{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S^{\prime}}}
.

First, we will show that the Galois representation
\rho_{x}
is unramified at all places
v
outside of
S^{\prime}
. Let
v
be any such place and
l
be its residue characteristics. The prime
l
does not divide the order of any of the groups
GL_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{E}/p^{n})
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
by assumption so the representation
\rho:\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})\to GL_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{E})
factors through the prime-to-
l
quotient of
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})
which we will denote by
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})^{(l^{\prime})}
. The action of
G_{F}
on
\pi_{1}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})^{(l^{\prime})}
is unramified at
v
by
[
LO10
, Corollary A.12]
, thanks to the fact that we assumed the existence of good compactification
{\mathfrak{X}}
for
{\mathfrak{X}}
. In other words, the image of the inertia subgroup
I_{v}\subset G_{F_{v}}
inside
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})^{(l^{\prime})}
commutes with the image of
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})^{(l^{\prime})}\to\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})^{(l^{\prime})}
. It means that
\rho_{x}(I_{F_{v}})
commutes with every element of
\rho(\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}))
. By our assumption
\End_{X_{\overline{F}}}{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{F}}}[\frac{1}{p}]=E\cdot\mathrm{Id}
every element of
GL_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{E})
commuting with
\rho(\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}))
has to be a scalar multiple of identity so for every
g\in I_{F_{v}}
we have
\rho_{x}(g)=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{Id}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{\times}
. By our preparatory assumption
\lambda\equiv 1
modulo
p^{2}
and by the definition of
S^{\prime}
the power
\lambda^{d}
is equal to
1
. This implies that
\lambda
is in fact equal to
1
so
\rho_{x}(I_{F_{v}})=\{\mathrm{Id}\}
and
\rho_{x}
is unramified at all places outside of
S^{\prime}
.

This now forces
{\mathbb{L}}
to extend to
{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S^{\prime}}}
: let
F_{n}
be the finite extension of
F
trivializing
x^{*}({\mathbb{L}}/p^{n})
, by the above it is unramified at all
v\not\in S^{\prime}
and to show that
{\mathbb{L}}/p^{n}
extends from
X
to
{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S^{\prime}}}
it is enough to do so after the base change to
{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{n},S^{\prime}}
. This extension exists by
[
ST
, Lemma 57.31.7]
.
∎

Despite the fact that it is not clear whether for a geometrically irreducible
{\mathbb{L}}
on
X
there always exists a character
\chi:G_{F}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
of the global Galois group such that
{\mathbb{L}}\otimes\chi
is de Rham at all places above
p
, Moritz Kerz observed that one can still conclude from the relative Fontaine-Mazur conjecture that the underlying geometric local system comes from geometry.

Lemma 6.2

(M. Kerz)
.

Assume that Conjecture
1
holds for local systems on the variety
X\times X
. Then for any
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system
{\mathbb{L}}
on
X
such that
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{F}}}
is irreducible the local system
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{F}}}
comes from geometry.

Proof.

Denote by
p_{1},p_{2}:X\times X\to X
the two projections and consider the local system
{\mathbb{L}}\boxtimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}:=p_{1}^{*}{\mathbb{L}}\otimes p_{2}^{*}{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}
. By Corollary
5.3
the local system
({\mathbb{L}}\boxtimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee})|_{X_{F_{v}}}
is de Rham for every place
v
above
p
and
{\mathbb{L}}\boxtimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}
extends to a local system on
({\mathfrak{X}}\times{\mathfrak{X}})_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S^{\prime}}}
for some finite set of places
S^{\prime}
by Proposition
6.1
. The assumptions of Conjecture
1
are therefore satisfied for
{\mathbb{L}}\boxtimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}
so there exists an open
U\subset X
such that
({\mathbb{L}}\boxtimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee})|_{U}
appears as a subquotient of the sheaf
R^{i}\pi_{*}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(j)
for some morphism
\pi:Y\to U
.

Choose a point
x\in X(\overline{F})
such that the intersection of
U
with
\{x\}\times X
is non-empty. Then the restriction
i_{x}^{*}({\mathbb{L}}\boxtimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee})
of the local system
({\mathbb{L}}\boxtimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee})_{(X\times X)_{\overline{F}}}
along the inclusion
i_{x}:X_{\overline{F}}=\{x\}\times X_{\overline{F}}\to(X\times X)_{\overline{F}}
comes from geometry. However, this restriction is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{F}}}\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}_{x}\simeq{\mathbb{L}}_{X_{\overline{F}}}^{\rk{\mathbb{L}}}
so the local system
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{F}}}
on
X_{\overline{F}}
comes from geometry.
∎

7.
Rigidity for Hodge-Tate local systems on algebraic varieties

For this section we go back into the setup of Section
2
:
X
is a smooth geometrically connected rigid-analytic variety over
K
with a smooth proper compactification
\overline{X}\supset X
such that
\overline{X}\setminus X
is a normal crossings divisor, the main example being provided by the analytification of a smooth algebraic variety.

Lemma 7.1
.

If
{\mathbb{L}}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system on
X
that admits a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattice then any endomorphism
f:\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})\to\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
commuting with the Higgs field that vanishes at one closed point
x\in X_{K_{\infty}}
has to be identically zero.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
(v) and Proposition
3.5
(ii) we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes_{K_{\infty}}C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End_{X_{\overline{K}}}{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End_{K_{\infty}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x}\otimes_{K_{\infty}}C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}C}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

As
{\mathbb{L}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}
is a local system the right vertical arrow is injective, so the left one is injective as well.

∎

Proposition 7.2
.

For any
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system on
X
that admits a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattice the associated coherent Higgs sheaf
D_{\mathrm{HT}}({\mathbb{L}})
is locally free.

Proof.

By
[
Shi18
, Proposition 5.3]
this statement amounts to the fact that
\bigoplus\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})^{\phi=i}|_{X_{K_{\infty}}}
is locally free. Assume, by enlarging
K
, that all Hodge-Tate weights of
{\mathbb{L}}
belong to
K
. Let
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})=\bigoplus\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\lambda}
be the generalized eigenbundle decomposition associated to the endomorphism
\phi
. Denote by
f
the endomorphism of
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})
defined by
\phi-\lambda
on the component
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{\lambda}
. It commutes with the Higgs field because of the identity
\theta\circ\phi=(\phi-1)\circ\theta
. To prove that
\bigoplus\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{H}({\mathbb{L}})^{\phi=i}|_{X_{K_{\infty}}}
is locally free, it is enough to show that the rank of

is constant across
X_{K_{\infty}}
. Equivalently, we need to prove that, if for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
the exterior power
\Lambda^{m}f\in\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}(\Lambda^{m}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}}))

, then

7.1
.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Suppose that
{\mathbb{L}}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system on
X
admiting a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattice. If for one classical point

the restriction
{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}
is a Hodge-Tate representation of

is Hodge-Tate. In particular, for any other closed point

By the assumption, all Hodge-Tate weights of
{\mathbb{L}}
belong to
{\mathbb{Z}}
. The proof of Proposition

shows that for every

is constant across

. Since the restriction of
\phi-i
to

annihilates all of the generalized eigenspace corresponding to the weight

∎

8.
Reducible local systems and Galois action on the fundamental group

In this section, we extend Theorem
1
to local systems that are not necessarily geometrically irreducible by proving the following result. I learned about this generalization and its interpretation in terms of the Galois action on
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})
from Alexander Beilinson. As before,
X
is a smooth rigid-analytic variety that admits a smooth proper compactification
\overline{X}\supset X
over a complete discretely valued field
K
of characteristic zero with a perfect residue field of characteristic
p
.

Theorem 8.1
.

For any
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system
{\mathbb{L}}
on
X
there exists a de Rham local system
{\mathbb{M}}
on
X
such that there is an inclusion
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}\subset{\mathbb{M}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
of local systems on

.

Proof.

Note that if the theorem is proven for the base change of
X
to a finite extension

then it follows for the initial

as well by considering the pushforward of
{\mathbb{M}}
along
X_{K^{\prime}}\to X
. We may therefore assume that
X
has a
K
-point

. Pick also a geometric point
\overline{x}
above

. We will view the stalk
{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}
as a representation of

.

The local system
{\mathbb{M}}
can now be defined constructively. Denote by
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})
the minimal sub-local system of
{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}
whose stalk

contains the identity endomorphism
\id_{{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}_{\overline{x}}\otimes{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}=\End_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}({\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}})
. In terms of representations of

, the stalk
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})_{\overline{x}}
is the
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-span of the image of the geometric fundamental group

. Note that

is therefore minimal among local systems on

and containing

in their stalk at

Now define
{\mathbb{M}}=({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})^{\vee})^{\oplus\rk{\mathbb{L}}}

{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes{\mathbb{L}}_{x}^{\vee}
surjects onto
{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}

∎

Proposition 8.2
.

For any
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-local system
{\mathbb{L}}
, the local system
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})
defined above is de Rham.

Proof.

Note that the subspace
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})_{\overline{x}}\subset\End_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}({\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}})
only depends on
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
so, in particular, for a finite extension

we have
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{K^{\prime}}})={\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})|_{X_{K^{\prime}}}
. We may thus freely replace
K
by a finite extension. If
{\mathbb{K}}
is a local system of
E
-vector spaces on
X
for a finite extension

we will denote by
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{K}})
the sub local system of
{\mathbb{K}}^{\vee}\otimes_{E}{\mathbb{K}}_{\overline{x}}

-local systems. If

denotes the same
{\mathbb{K}}
, considered as a

-local system of rank

, then

a

-local system to begin with.

As in the proof of Theorem

, let us start by translating the defining property of

\id_{{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}}\otimes 1
of the left hand side gets carried to the element
\id_{\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x}}\otimes 1\in\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))_{x}\otimes_{K_{\infty}}C\subset\End_{K_{\infty}}(\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x})\otimes_{K_{\infty}}C
because
\id_{{\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}}\in\End({\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}})
is stable under the Galois action. By comparison isomorphism of Proposition
3.5
(iii), this implies that any endomorphism
f\in\End_{\overline{X}_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))
satisfying
f(\id_{\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x}})=0
has to be zero.

We can now conclude that
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})
has integral Hodge-Tate weights. The direct sum
\bigoplus\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))_{i}\subset\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))
of generalized eigenbundles of
\phi
with integral eigenvalues is a direct summand of the Higgs bundle
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))
or, in other words, there is an idempotent
e\in\End_{X_{K_{\infty}},\mathrm{Higgs}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))
such that
\ker(e)=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))_{i}
. Since
\id_{\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x}}\in\End(\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x})=\End(\mathrm{H}({\mathbb{L}}_{\overline{x}}))
spans the result of applying
\mathrm{H}
to the trivial Galois representation,
\phi(\id_{\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x}})=0
and hence
e(\id_{\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x}})=0
. This forces
e
to be zero, so every eigenvalue of
\phi
on
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))
is an integer.

We can now finish the proof of the proposition analogously to the proof of Theorem
5.2
. Consider the sheaf
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))=\mathrm{RH}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))^{\phi^{\rk({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))}=0}
as in Proposition
5.1
. If
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})
is not de Rham, then the Sen endomorphism on
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))
induces, as described in the proof of Theorem
5.2
, a non-zero nilpotent endomorphism of the Higgs bundle
\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))
. This endomorphism, however, annihilates the element
\id_{\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathbb{L}})_{x}}\in\mathrm{H}^{\log}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}}))_{x}
, so it has to be zero by the discussion above. Hence the local system
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{L}})
is de Rham.
∎

It is unclear if it is always possible to choose
{\mathbb{M}}
such that
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}\simeq{\mathbb{M}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
(note that this is the case for a geometrically irreducible
{\mathbb{L}}
by Theorem
1
). In particular, I do not know the answer to the following question

Question 8.3
.

If
{\mathbb{L}}_{1},{\mathbb{L}}_{2}
are de Rham local systems on
X
and
0\to{\mathbb{L}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{L}}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{2}\to 0
is an extension of local systems on
X
, does there exists an extension
0\to{\mathbb{L}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{L}}^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{2}\to 0
such that
{\mathbb{L}}^{\prime}
is de Rham and there is an isomorphism
{\mathbb{L}}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}\simeq{\mathbb{L}}^{\prime}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
of extensions of
{\mathbb{L}}_{2}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
by
{\mathbb{L}}_{1}|_{X_{\overline{K}}}
?

From now on we assume that
x
is either a
K
-point of
X
or a tangential base point of
X
in the sense of
[
Del89
, §15]
and
\overline{x}
is a geometric base point above
x
. We can reformulate the above result in terms of the Galois action on the pro-algebraic completion of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})
. We will now recall all the necessary information about pro-algebraic completions of pro-finite groups and refer the reader to Section 3.1 of
[
Dri18
]
for a more thorough introduction.

Definition 8.4
.

For a compact Hausdorff topological group
\Gamma
the
pro-algebraic completion

\Gamma^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}
is the pro-algebraic group over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, equipped with a continuous map
\alpha:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
defined by the following universal property. For any continous homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma\to GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
there exists a unique algebraic morphism
\rho^{\mathrm{alg}}:\Gamma^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}\to GL_{n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
such that
\alpha\circ\rho^{\mathrm{alg}}=\rho
.

Here, for a pro-algebraic group
G
expressed as a limit
\lim\limits_{i\in I}G_{i}
of algebraic groups
G_{i}
we endow
G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})=\lim\limits_{i\in I}G_{i}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
with a structure of a topological group given by the limit of the topological groups
G_{i}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
, each carrying the standard
p
-adic topology. Note that, as
\Gamma
is assumed to be compact and Hausdorff, any continuous homomorphism
\Gamma\to GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
factors through
GL_{n}(E)
for some finite extension of
E
, by a Baire category theorem argument, cf.
[
BM02
, Lemma 2.2.1.1]
.

We will denote by
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\Gamma^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}]
the ring of regular functions on
\Gamma^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}
. Our main objects of interest are pro-algebraic compeltions of the profinite groups
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})
and
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})
, which we denote by
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})
and
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X,\overline{x})
, respectively. The continuous action of
G_{K}
on
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})
induces an action on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})]
.

Proposition 8.5
.

Any finite-dimensional
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-representation
V
of
G_{K}
that can be embedded into
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})]
is de Rham.

Proof.

The span of the
G_{K}
-orbit of a function
f\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})]
is finite-dimensional if and only if
f
extends to a regular function on the pro-algebraic completion of the arithmetic fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})
. Therefore
V
is contained in the image of the restriction map
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X,\overline{x})]\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})]
.

By Theorem
8.1
, for any continuous homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})\to GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
there exists another one
\rho^{\prime}:\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})\to GL_{n+m}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
such that the image of the restriction map
\rho^{*}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[GL_{n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}]\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K},\overline{x}})]
is contained in the image of the restriction map
\rho^{\prime*}
, and
\rho^{\prime}
corresponds to a de Rham local system.

Denote by
W
the Galois representation obtained by restricting
\rho^{\prime}
along
x_{*}:G_{K}=\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(\Spec K)\to\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X,\overline{x})
. If
x\in X(K)
is a classical base point then
W
is evidently de Rham. If
x
is a tangential base point, the fact that
W
is de Rham was proven in
[
DLLZ19
, Corollary 4.3.4]
as a consequence of their results on the compatibility of the functor
D_{\mathrm{dR}}
with the functor of nearby cycles. The image
\rho^{\prime*}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[GL_{n+m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}])\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})]
is a direct sum of Galois representations of the form
W^{\otimes a}\otimes W^{\vee\otimes b}\otimes(\det W)^{c}
for varying
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
W
is de Rham, the image of
\rho^{\prime*}
is thus a union of de Rham representations. We have therefore proven that the image of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X,\overline{x})]\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{K}},\overline{x})]
is a union of finite dimensional de Rham representations, because any function on the pro-algebraic completion
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X,\overline{x})
factors through some morphism of pro-algebraic groups
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X,\overline{x})\to GL_{n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
.
∎

Corollary 8.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety over a number field
F
, equipped with a base point
x
that is either a classical point
x\in X(F)
or a tangential base point. Any finite-dimensional representation of
G_{F}
contained in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})]
is geometric in the sense of Fontaine-Mazur: it is de Rham at all places
v
of
F
above
p
and it is almost everywhere unramified.

Proof.

Let
V\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})]
be a finite-dimensional subspace stable under the action of
G_{F}
. The de Rhamness has just been proven, so it remains to prove that
V
is an almost unramified representation. By definition, there exists a map of pro-algebraic groups
f:\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\to GL_{n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
such that every function from
V
factors through it. Since the image of
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
under
f
is contained in
GL_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{E})\subset GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
for some finite extension
E\supset{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, there exists a finite set
S
of rational primes such the restriction of
f
to
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
factors through the pro-
S
completion
\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})^{(S)}
of this group. Therefore,
V
lies in the image of the canonical map
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})^{(S)}]\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})]
. After enlarging
S
we may assume that
X
is the generic fiber of a smooth
{\mathcal{O}}_{F,S}
-scheme
{\mathfrak{X}}
that can be established as the complement to a horizontal normal crossings divisor
{\mathfrak{D}}\subset\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}
in a smooth proper
\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}
and, moreover,
x
extends to a point in
{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{O}}_{F,S})
. By Lemma
8.7
below the action of
G_{F}
on
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{pro}-\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})^{(S)}
is unramified at all places not lying above
S
. Therefore
V
is almost everywhere unramified.
∎

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
v
be a finite place of
F
of residue characteristics
l=\mathrm{char}\,k(v)
. Suppose that
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a smooth scheme over
{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}
that can be embedded into a smooth proper
{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}
scheme
\overline{{\mathcal{Y}}}
such that
\overline{{\mathcal{Y}}}\setminus{\mathcal{Y}}
is a relative normal crossings divisor. If
y
is a classical or tangential base point of
{\mathcal{Y}}
then there is an isomorphism

(8.1)

\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\overline{F}_{v}},\overline{y}_{F_{v}})^{(l^{\prime})}\simeq\pi_{1}^{\text{et}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\overline{k(v)}},\overline{y}_{k(v)})^{(l^{\prime})}

compatible with the action of
G_{F_{v}}
where the action on the right hand side is given by precomposing the natural action of
G_{k(v)}
with the surjection
G_{F_{v}}\to G_{k(v)}
. Here
y_{F_{v}}
(resp.
y_{k(v)}
) denotes the base point of
{\mathcal{Y}}_{F_{v}}
(resp.
{\mathcal{Y}}_{k(v)}
) induces by
y
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}^{nr}_{F_{v}}
denote the completed ring of integers of the maximal unramified extension of
F_{v}
. By
[
LO10
, Theorem A.7]
(or
[
SGA1
, Corollaire X.3.9]
if

) the categories of Galois covers of degrees prime to

,

which implies the result.
∎

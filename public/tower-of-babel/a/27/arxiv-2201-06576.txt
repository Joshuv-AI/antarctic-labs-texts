Asymptotic Gaussianity via coalescence probabilities in the Hammond-Sheffield urn
Jan Lukas Igelbrink, Anton Wakolbinger

Abstract
. For the renormalised sums of the random ± 1 \pm 1 -colouring of the connected components of ℤ \mathbb{Z} generated by the coalescing renewal processes in the “power law Pólya’s urn” of Hammond and Sheffield [ HS13 ] we prove functional convergence towards fractional Brownian motion, closing a gap in the tightness argument of their paper. In addition, in the regime of the strong renewal theorem we gain insights into the coalescing renewal processes in the Hammond-Sheffield urn (such as the asymptotic depth of most recent common ancestors) and are able to control the coalescence probabilities of two, three and four individuals that are randomly sampled from [ n ] [n] . This allows us to obtain a new, conceptual proof of the asymptotic Gaussianity (including the functional convergence) of the renormalised sums of more general colourings, which can be seen as an invariance principle beyond the main result of [ HS13 ] . In this proof, a key ingredient of independent interest is a sufficient criterion for the asymptotic Gaussianity of the renormalised sums in randomly coloured random partitions of [ n ] [n] , based on Stein’s method. Along the way we also prove a statement on the asymptotics of the coalescence probabilities in the long-range seedbank model of Blath, González Casanova, Kurt, and Spanò, see [ BGKS13 ] .

Coalescence probabilities in the Hammond-Sheffield urn

Proposition 2.1

2.1
) that the weights

) claimed in Proposition

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

where

is of the form

with

a positive constant and

, a bounded measurable function converging to
0
as

Then
(r_{n})
is ultimately decreasing, and

(4.7)

\sum_{j\geq 1}r_{j}r_{i+j}\sim K(i)^{2}i^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+x)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}x\quad\mbox{as }i\to\infty,

with

(4.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+x)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha-1}dx=B(\alpha,1-2\alpha)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-2\alpha)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}.

Proof.

a) The equality (
4.8
) is readily checked by substituting

.

b) The fact that
(r_{n})
is ultimately decreasing follows from (
4.5
) together with the Karamata representation (
4.6
) of

. To see this, we argue as follows, putting

. Since

tends to zero for

we know that there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

one has

. This implies

\displaystyle\frac{r_{n}}{r_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{-\beta}}{(n+1)^{-\beta}}\cdot\frac{K(n)}{K(n+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{-\beta}}{(n+1)^{-\beta}}\exp\left(-\int_{n}^{n+1}\frac{l(t)}{t}\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\beta\left(\ln(n+1)-\ln(n)\right)-\int_{n}^{n+1}\frac{l(t)}{t}\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\int_{n}^{n+1}\left(\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{l(t)}{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right).

Since by assumption the integrand on the right-hand side is strictly positive for
n\geq n_{0}
, we obtain that
(r_{n})_{n}
is decreasing for
n\geq n_{0}
.

c) In view of (
4.5
), the claimed asymptotics (

) is equivalent to

(4.9)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{i}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{r_{j}}{r_{i}}\frac{r_{i+j}}{r_{i}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+x)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}x.

We now set out to prove (

such that for all sufficiently large

is ultimately decreasing, (
4.10
) will follow if we can show that exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all sufficiently large

\frac{1}{i}\sum_{j\geq Ni}\left(\frac{r_{j}}{r_{i}}\right)^{2}<\varepsilon.

\displaystyle\EW\left[|A_{0}\cap A_{i}\cap\{0,\ldots,-(r+\varepsilon)i\}|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(r+\varepsilon)i}\left(f_{i}(k)\sum_{l=0}^{(r+\varepsilon)i-k}q_{l}^{2}\right)\quad\geq\quad\sum_{k=0}^{ri}\left(f_{i}(k)\sum_{l=0}^{(r+\varepsilon)i-k}q_{l}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\varepsilon i}q_{l}^{2}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{ri}f_{i}(k)\qquad=\quad\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\varepsilon i}q_{l}^{2}\right)F_{i}(ri).

Using (
5.2
), now with
r+\varepsilon
instead of
r
, we get

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\WS\left(D_{i}\leq ri\,|\,0\sim i\right)\leq B(\alpha,1-2\alpha)^{-1}\int_{0}^{r+\varepsilon}x^{\alpha-1}(1+x)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, this together with (
5.3
) gives the assertion of Proposition
2.5
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For all

and

not depending on

\WS\left(0\sim i\sim\lfloor(1+r)i\rfloor\right)\leq\left(r^{\alpha-1}+r^{2\alpha-1}\right)\tilde{L}(i)\,i^{4\alpha-2}.

Proof.

Let
(A_{k})
be the independent (non-merging) ancestral lineages defined in (
4.1
).
We set

A_{k,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}A_{\max(A_{k}\cap A_{\ell})}&\mbox{ if }A_{k}\cap A_{\ell}\neq\emptyset,\\
\emptyset&\mbox{ if }A_{k}\cap A_{\ell}=\emptyset,\end{cases}

In words,

is the union of the (non-merging) ancestral lineages starting at all the points of intersection of

and

. Distinguishing the 3 shapes of the ancestral tree of the individuals

,
i
and
(1+r)i
on the event

(and omitting the Gauss brackets in

(6.2)

\displaystyle\WS\left(0\sim i\sim(1+r)i\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\WS\left(E;\mathscr{M}(0,i)\geq\mathscr{M}(0,(1+r)i\right)+\WS\left(E;\mathscr{M}(0,(1+r)i)\geq\mathscr{M}(0,i)\right)

\displaystyle+\WS\left(E;\mathscr{M}(i,(1+r)i)\geq\mathscr{M}(0,i)\right)

\displaystyle\WS\left(A_{0,i}\cap A_{(1+r)i}\neq\emptyset\right)+\WS\left(A_{0,(1+r)i}\cap A_{i}\neq\emptyset\right)+\WS\left(A_{i,(1+r)i}\cap A_{0}\neq\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle\EW\left[|A_{0,i}\cap A_{(1+r)i}|\right]+\EW\left[|A_{0,(1+r)i}\cap A_{i}|\right]+\EW\left[|A_{i,(1+r)i}\cap A_{0}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\EW\left[|B_{0,i}\cap A_{(1+r)i}|\right]+\EW\left[|B_{0,(1+r)i}\cap A_{i}|\right]+\EW\left[|B_{i,(1+r)i}\cap A_{0}|\right].

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}q_{m}q_{m+i}\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}q_{n+(1+r)i+m}+\sum_{m\geq 0}q_{m}q_{m+(1+r)i}\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}q_{n+i+m}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m\geq 0}q_{m}q_{ri+m}\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}q_{n+i-m}.

An inspection of the third summand on the right-hand side leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}q_{m}q_{ri+m}\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}q_{n+i-m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{m}q_{ri+i}\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}q_{n}+\sum_{m\neq i}q_{m}q_{ri+m}\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}q_{n+i-m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(q_{i}q_{(1+r)i}+\sum_{m\neq i}\frac{m^{\alpha-1}}{L(m)}\frac{(m+ri)^{\alpha-1}}{L(m+ri)}\frac{|i-m|^{2\alpha-1}}{L(|i-m|)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{i^{2\alpha-2}}{L(i)^{2}}+\frac{i^{4\alpha-2}}{L(i)^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}(x+r)^{\alpha-1}|1-x|^{2\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(i)i^{4\alpha-2}r^{\alpha-1},

where

is a slowly varying function that dominates

, and where the asymptotics is justified in the same way as (

) was derived first for slowly varying functions satisfying (

∎

For

We then have

) is immediate from the following

Lemma 7.1
.

For pairwise distinct

\WS\left(i\sim k\not\sim j\sim\ell\right)\leq\WS\left(i\sim k\right)\WS\left(j\sim\ell\right)

Proof.

Let
A_{g}
,
g\in\mathbb{Z}
, be defined as in (
4.1
). For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
we define an
(A_{i_{1}},\ldots,A_{i_{m}})

which is equal in distribution to the subgraph of

that is formed by the (possibly coalescing) ancestral lineages of

Inspired by the proof of Theorem 1 in

(
[
Bil68

Let

1.3
.

We first note the following immediate consequence of (
1.7
) and (
1.8
):
There exist constants

Next we fix

and

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[c_{3}(k-j)^{2\alpha+1}L(k-j)^{-2}\right]\sigma_{n}^{-2}

is a slowly varying function. (Remember that

\displaystyle\tilde{c}^{2}(u-s)^{1+2\alpha}\,=\,\left[c_{0}u-c_{0}s\right]^{1+2\alpha}

is a quick consequence of Corollary
1.4
and the Borell-TIS inequality.

10.
Coalescence probabilities in long-range seedbanks

In this section we will assume that the weights
q_{n}
of the renewal measure defined in (
1.4
) obey the asymptotics
(
1.10
), see the discussion after equation (
1.10
).
Following
[
BGKS13
]
we extend the model described in Section
1
as follows. For fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the set of vertices of
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
is now
\mathbb{Z}\times[N]
. The set of those vertices whose first component is
i
constitutes the population of individuals living at time
i
. The parent of the individual
(i,k)
is
(i-R_{i,k},H_{i,k})
, where the random variables
R_{i,k}
are independent copies of
R
and the random variables
H_{i,k}
are i.i.d. picks from
[N]
. In words, each individual chooses its parent uniformly from a previous time with delay (or dormancy) distribution
\mu
. The corresponding urn model, which goes back to Kaj, Krone and Lascoux (
[
KKL01
]
), thus specialises to the Hammond-Sheffield urn for
N=1
.

Again we write
(i,k)\sim(j,\ell)
if the two individuals
(i,k)
and
(j,\ell)
belong to the same connected component of
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
.
Thanks to Proposition
4.1
, which also provides a proof of
[
BGKS13
, Lemma 3.1 (c)]
, we arrive at the following analogue of Proposition
2.1
(see also
[
BGKS13
, Theorem 3(c))]
:

Proposition 10.1
.

For all
k,\ell\in[N]

(10.1)

\WS\left((0,k)\sim(i,\ell)\right)\sim C_{\alpha,N}\frac{i^{2\alpha-1}}{L(i)^{2}}\quad\mbox{ as }i\to\infty,

where now

C_{\alpha,N}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{N+\sum_{m\geq 1}q_{m}^{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma(1-2\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)^{3}}.

Proof.

Denote by

\tilde{A}_{0,k}
and
\tilde{A}_{i,\ell}
the decoupled ancestral lineages of the individuals
(0,k)
and
(i,\ell)
constructed in analogy to (
4.1
). Like in (
4.2
) we observe

\EW\left[|\tilde{A}_{0,k}\cap\tilde{A}_{i,\ell}|\right]=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{N}q_{m}q_{m+i}.

Decomposing at the most recent collision time of
\tilde{A}_{0,k}
and
\tilde{A}_{i,\ell}
we get

\displaystyle\EW\left[\tilde{A}_{0,k}\cap\tilde{A}_{0,\ell}\right]=\WS\left(\tilde{A}_{0,k}\cap\tilde{A}_{i,\ell}\not=\emptyset\right)\cdot\left(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{N}q_{m}^{2}\right).

The last two equalities combine to

\displaystyle\WS\left(A_{0,k}\cap A_{i,\ell}\not=\emptyset\right)=\frac{\ \sum_{m\geq 0}q_{m}q_{m+i}}{N+\sum_{m\geq 1}q_{m}^{2}}.

The claimed asymptotics (
10.1
) is now immediate from Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Remark 10.2
.

For given natural numbers
n
and
N
let

\mathscr{I},\mathscr{J},\mathscr{K}
and
\mathscr{L}

be independent and uniformly distributed on
[n]\times[N]
. In complete analogy to Proposition
2.3
one can derive that for all
\delta>0
and for a constant
C
not depending on
n
and
N

\displaystyle\WS\left(\mathscr{I}\sim\mathscr{J}\sim\mathscr{K}\right)\qquad

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N^{2}}n^{4\alpha-2+\delta},

\displaystyle\WS\left(\mathscr{I}\sim\mathscr{J}\sim\mathscr{K}\sim\mathscr{L}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N^{3}}n^{6\alpha-3+\delta}.

Consequently, along the lines of the proof of Theorem
1.1
one obtains the convergence of an analogue of
\big(S^{(n)}\big)
towards fractional Brownian motion also in the long-range seedbank model of
[
BGKS13
]
.

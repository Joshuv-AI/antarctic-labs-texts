Systole functions and Weil-Petersson geometry
Yunhui Wu

Abstract
. A basic feature of Teichmüller theory of Riemann surfaces is the interplay of two dimensional hyperbolic geometry, the behavior of geodesic-length functions and Weil-Petersson geometry. Let 𝒯 g \mathcal{T}_{g} ( g ⩾ 2 ) (g\geqslant 2) be the Teichmüller space of closed Riemann surfaces of genus g g . Our goal in this paper is to study the gradients of geodesic-length functions along systolic curves. We show that their L p L^{p} ( 1 ⩽ p ⩽ ∞ ) (1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty) -norms at every hyperbolic surface X ∈ 𝒯 g X\in\mathcal{T}_{g} are uniformly comparable to ℓ s ​ y ​ s ​ ( X ) 1 p \ell_{sys}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}} where ℓ s ​ y ​ s ​ ( X ) \ell_{sys}(X) is the systole of X X . As an application, we show that the minimal Weil-Petersson holomorphic sectional curvature at every hyperbolic surface X ∈ 𝒯 g X\in\mathcal{T}_{g} is bounded above by a uniform negative constant independent of g g , which negatively answers a question of M. Mirzakhani. Some other applications to the geometry of 𝒯 g \mathcal{T}_{g} will also be discussed.

1.
Introduction

Let
S_{g}
be a closed surface of genus
g

(g\geqslant 2)
, and
\mathcal{T}_{g}
be the Teichmüller space of
S_{g}
. The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
of
S_{g}
is the quotient space
\mathcal{T}_{g}/\mbox{\rm Mod}(S_{g})
where
\mbox{\rm Mod}(S_{g})
is the mapping class group of
S_{g}
. For any non-trivial loop
\alpha\subset S_{g}
and
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, there exists a unique closed geodesic
[\alpha]\subset X
representing
\alpha
. Denote by
\ell_{\alpha}(X)
the length of
[\alpha]
in
X
. This quantity
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
gives a real analytic function on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. The Weil-Petersson gradient
\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)
of
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
evaluated at
X
is a harmonic Beltrami differential of
X
. Actually Gardiner in
[
Gar75
]
provided a precise formula for
\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)
, which one may see in formula (
2.5
). Recall that the
systole

\ell_{sys}(X)
of
X
is the length of shortest closed geodesics on
X
. We prove

Theorem 1.1

(Uniform
L^{p}
bounds)
.

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
and
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, then we have

(1)

for any
\alpha\subset X
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
,

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}\asymp\ell_{sys}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2)

For any simple loop
\beta\subset X
with
\ell_{\beta}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
where
L_{0}>0
is any given constant,

||\nabla\ell_{\beta}(X)||_{p}\asymp\ell_{\beta}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Where
||\nabla\ell_{*}(X)||_{p}:=(\int_{X}|\nabla\ell_{*}(X)|^{p}\cdot\dArea)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark
.

If
p=2
, in light of (
2.7
) which is due to Riera, it suffices to show the uniform upper bounds in Theorem
1.1
. For this case,

(i).

Part (1) of Theorem
1.1
was firstly obtained in
[
Wu19
, Proposition 2]
whose proof highly relies on formula (
2.6
) of Riera. In this paper, we give a different and more fundamental proof without using Riera’s formula.

(ii).

For Part (2) when
p=2
, the curve
\beta\subset X
is not required to be a systolic curve of
X
. Theorem
1.1
was firstly obtained by Wolpert
[
Wol08
, Lemma 3.16]
by using Riera’s formula; very recently Bridgeman and Bromberg in
[
BB22
]
applied Riera’s formula to obtain an upper bound by an explicit elementary function in terms of
\ell_{\alpha}
(see
[
BB22
, Theorem 1.6]
); our proof is different without using Riera’s formula.

In the subsequent subsections, we make several applications of Theorem
1.1
to the geometry of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
, especially on its Weil-Petersson geometry.

1.1.
Application to Weil-Petersson curvatures

The Weil-Petersson curvature of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
has been studied over the past several decades. One may see Section
2
for related references. In this paper we apply Theorem
1.1
to obtain certain new uniform and optimal bounds on Weil-Petersson curvatures. The main one is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, let
\alpha\subset X
be a simple closed geodesic satisfying

(1)

either
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
or

(2)

\ell_{\alpha}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
where
L_{0}>0
is any fixed constant,

then the Weil-Petersson holomorphic sectional curvature

\HolK(\nabla\ell_{\alpha})(X)\asymp\frac{-1}{\ell_{sys}(X)}.

Remark
.

(1)

For the behavior as
\ell_{sys}(X)\to 0
,
[
Wol12b
, Corollary 16]
or
[
Wol12b
, Theorem 19]
tells that
\HolK(\nabla\ell_{\alpha})(X)=\frac{-3}{\pi\ell_{sys}(X)}+O(\ell_{sys}(X))
where the dominated term
\frac{-3}{\pi\ell_{sys}(X)}
is explicit; however the error term
O(\ell_{sys}(X))
may depend on the topology
g
. The bound in Theorem
1.2
is uniform.

(2)

Very recently joint with Martin Bridgeman, we show in
[
BW21
]
that for any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\ell_{sys}(X)\leqslant 2\arcsinh^{-1}(1)
, then
for any
\mu\neq 0\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}^{n}
, the Weil-Petersson Ricci curvature
\Ric(\mu)
satisfies that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Ric(\mu)\geqslant-\frac{4}{\ell_{sys}(X)}.

Theorem
1.2
tells that the above uniform lower bound in terms of
\ell_{sys}(X)
is optimal. Actually we have

Corollary 1.3
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\ell_{sys}(X)\leqslant 2\arcsinh^{-1}(1)
, then the minimal Weil-Petersson sectional curvature
K
at
X

\min_{\emph{realplane\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\asymp\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\Ric(\mu)\asymp-\frac{1}{\ell_{sys}(X)}.

Proof.

Since the Weil-Petersson sectional curvature is negative
[
Wol86
]
or
[
Tro86
]
, by (
1.1
) we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\ell_{sys}(X)}\prec\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\Ric(\mu)<\min_{\emph{realplane$\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}$}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Thus, it suffices to show that
\min_{\emph{realplane$\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}$}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\prec-\frac{1}{\ell_{sys}(X)}
. This can be directly followed by using Theorem
1.2
because

\displaystyle\min_{\emph{realplane$\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}$}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\min_{\alpha\subset X;\ \ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)}\HolK(\nabla\ell_{\alpha})(X)

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{\ell_{sys}(X)}

which completes the proof.
∎

Combining
[
WW18
]
with Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following uniform upper bounds.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
,

\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(\mu)\prec-1<0.

Remarks
.

(i).

If
\ell_{sys}(X)
is big enough, Theorem
1.4
was firstly obtained by Wolf and the author in
[
WW18
]
that partially answered a question which is due to Maryam Mirzakhani:
for any fixed constant
\epsilon>0
, whether the Weil-Petersson curvatures restricting on any
\epsilon
-thick part of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
go to
0
as the genus
g\to\infty
?

(ii).

It was shown in
[
Wu17
, Theorem 1.4]
that the limit of the following probability holds:

\lim_{g\to\infty}\Prob\{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(\mu)\prec-1<0\}=1.

Theorem
1.4
generalizes these two results above and give a complete negative answer to Mirzakhani’s question.

Given any constant
\varepsilon>0
, restricted on the
\varepsilon
-thick part of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
Huang
[
Hua07b
]
showed that the Weil-Petersson sectional curvature is uniformly bounded from below by a constant only depending on
\varepsilon
. Later Teo
[
Teo09
]
generalized Huang’s result to Weil-Petersson Ricci curvature. The following result roughly says that the uniform lower bounds of Huang and Teo are optimal. More precisely, as an application of Theorem
1.4
we prove

Corollary 1.5
.

Given any constant
\varepsilon>0
, then for any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant\varepsilon
, the minimal Weil-Petersson sectional curvature
K
at
X

\min_{\emph{realplane\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\asymp\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\Ric(\mu)\asymp-1.

Proof.

Since
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant\varepsilon
, by
[
Teo09
, Proposition 3.3]
of Teo we have

\displaystyle-1\prec\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\Ric(\mu).

Since the Weil-Petersson sectional curvature is negative
[
Wol86
]
or
[
Tro86
]
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\Ric(\mu)<\min_{\emph{realplane$\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}$}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Thus, it suffices to show that
\min_{\emph{realplane$\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}$}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\prec-1
. This can be directly followed by using Theorem
1.4
because

\displaystyle\min_{\emph{realplane$\emph{span}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}$}}K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\leqslant\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(\mu)\prec-1.

Which completes the proof.
∎

It was shown in
[
Wol12b
, Corollary 23]
that there exists a constant
c_{g}<0
, only depending on
g
, such that any subspace
S\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}S>(3g-3)
contains a section with Weil-Petersson sectional curvature at most
c_{g}
. In light of Theorem
1.4
, it would be interesting to know that whether this result of Wolpert is still true if one replaces
c_{g}
by a uniform constant
c<0
. More precisely,

Question
.

Does there exist a uniform constant
c<0
, independent of
g
, such that any subspace
S\subset T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}S>(3g-3)
contains a section with Weil-Petersson sectional curvature at most
c
?

1.2.
Application to Weil-Petersson distance

Let
\Teich(S_{g})
be the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
endowed with the Weil-Petersson metric. In
[
Wu19
]
we studied the behavior of the systole function along Weil-Petersson geodesics and proved the following uniform Lipschitz property.

Theorem
.

[
Wu19
, Theorem 1.3]

For all
X,Y\in\Teich(S_{g})
,

|\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(X)}-\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(Y)}|\prec\dist_{wp}(X,Y)

where
\dist_{wp}
is the Weil-Petersson distance.

One essential step in the proof of the theorem above is to show Theorem
1.1
for
p=2
. We outline the proof of the theorem above as follows. One may see
[
Wu19
]
for more details.

Outline proof.

By the real analyticity of the geodesic length function, the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
and the Weil-Petersson metric, it was shown in
[
Wu19
, Lemma 4.5]
that the systole function
\ell_{sys}(\cdot):\Teich(S_{g})\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
is piecewisely real analytic in the sense that for any Weil-Petersson geodesic segment
c:[0,r]\to\Teich(S_{g})
of arc-length parameter, where
r>0
is any constant, the function
\ell_{sys}(c(t)):[0,r]\to R^{>0}
is piecewisely real analytic.

By
[
Wol87
]
one may let
c:[0,s]\to\Teich(S_{g})
be the unique Weil-Petersson geodesic joining
X
and
Y
of arc-length parameter where
s=\dist_{wp}(X,Y)>0
. Since the function
\ell_{sys}(c(t)):[0,s]\to R^{>0}
is piecewisely real analytic, one may apply Theorem
1.1
for the case
p=2
to obtain that for all
t\in(0,s)
except finite values,

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{sys}^{\frac{1}{2}}(c(t))||_{2}\asymp 1.

Then by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(X)}-\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(Y)}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\int_{0}^{s}\langle\nabla\ell_{sys}^{\frac{1}{2}}(c(t)),c^{\prime}(t)\rangle_{wp}dt|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}||\nabla\ell_{sys}^{\frac{1}{2}}(c(t))||_{2}dt

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle s=\dist_{wp}(X,Y)

where we apply (
1.2
) in the last equation. Which completes the proof.
∎

Remarks
.

(1)

Bridgeman and Bromberg in
[
BB22
]
applied their explicit bounds on
||\nabla\ell_{sys}(X)||_{wp}
to show that the function

\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(\cdot)}:\Teich(S_{g})\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}

is
\frac{1}{2}
-Lipschitz.

(2)

In
[
Wu22
]
, motivated by the work
[
RT18
]
of Rupflin and Topping, we use a purely differential geometrical method to show that
\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(\cdot)}
is
0.5492
- Lipschitz, without using any estimations on
||\nabla(\ell_{sys}(\cdot))||_{wp}
.

In
[
Wu19
]
we applied the uniform Lipschitz Theorem above to determine the growth rate of the inradius of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
or
\Teich(S_{g})
for large genus (also for large punctures). Recall that the
inradius

\InR(\mathcal{M}_{g})
of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is defined as

\InR(\mathcal{M}_{g}):=\sup_{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\dist_{wp}(X,\partial\mathcal{M}_{g})

where
\partial\mathcal{M}_{g}
is the Weil-Petersson boundary of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
which is also the same as the boundary of the Deligne-Mumford compactification of
\mathcal{M}_{g}

[
Mas76
]
. It is known by Wolpert
[
Wol08
, Section 4]
that for any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
,
\dist_{wp}(X,\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\alpha})\leqslant\sqrt{2\pi\cdot\ell_{\alpha}(X)}
where
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\alpha}
is the stratum of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
whose pinching curve is
\alpha
. If we choose
\alpha\subset X
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
, then
\dist_{wp}(X,\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\alpha})\leqslant\sqrt{2\pi\ell_{sys}(X)}
. It is well-known that
\ell_{sys}(X)\prec\ln{(g)}
. So we have
\InR(\mathcal{M}_{g})\prec\sqrt{\ln{(g)}}
. For the other direction, first by Buser-Sarnak
[
BS94
]
one may choose a surface
\mathcal{X}_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
such that
\ell_{sys}(\mathcal{X}_{g})\asymp\ln{(g)}
. Clearly the uniform Lipschitz Theorem above implies that
\dist_{wp}(\mathcal{X}_{g},\partial\mathcal{M}_{g})\succ\sqrt{\ln{(g)}}
which in particular implies that
\InR(\mathcal{M}_{g})\succ\sqrt{\ln{(g)}}
. Thus, we have

Theorem
.

[
Wu19
, Theorem 1.1]

For all
g\geqslant 2
,

\InR(\mathcal{M}_{g})\asymp\sqrt{\ln{(g)}}.

Remark
.

Bridgeman and Bromberg in
[
BB22
]
showed that

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{\InR(\mathcal{M}_{g})}{\sqrt{\max\limits_{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\ell_{sys}(X)}}=\sqrt{2\pi}.

One may also see
[
Wu22
]
for an alternative proof.

Remark
.

Cavendish-Parlier
[
CP12
]
showed that the diameter
\diam(\mathcal{M}_{g})
of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
satisfies
\sqrt{g}\prec\diam(\mathcal{M}_{g})\prec\sqrt{g}\cdot\ln{g}
where the upper bound refines Brock’s quasi-isometry of
\Teich(S_{g})
to the pants graph
[
Bro03
]
. As far as we know, it is still an open problem:
whether
\sqrt{g}\cdot\ln{g}
is the correct growth rate for
\diam(\mathcal{M}_{g})
as
g\to\infty
?

(1<p\leqslant\infty)

The Weil-Petersson metric is the

-metric on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and the Teichmüller metric is the

\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Similarly for any

\mathcal{M}_{g}

(\mathcal{M}_{g},||\cdot||_{L^{p}})

we show that

.

Let

, for

(ii).

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

In this section we set up the notations and provide some necessary background on two-dimensional hyperbolic geometry, Teichmüller theory and the Weil-Petersson metric.

2.1.
Hyperbolic upper half plane

Let
\mathbb{H}
be the upper half plane endowed with the hyperbolic metric
\rho(z)|dz|^{2}
where

\rho(z)=\frac{1}{(\im(z))^{2}}.

A geodesic line in
\mathbb{H}
is either a vertical line or an upper semi-circle centered at some point on the real axis. For
z=(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H}
given in polar coordinates where
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, the hyperbolic distance between
z
and the imaginary axis
\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}}
is

(2.1)

\displaystyle\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})=\ln|\csc{\theta}+|\cot{\theta}||.

Thus,

(2.2)

\displaystyle e^{-2\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})}\leqslant\sin^{2}{\theta}=\frac{\im^{2}(z)}{|z|^{2}}\leqslant 4e^{-2\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})}.

It is known that any eigenfunction with positive eigenvalue of the hyperbolic Laplacian of
\mathbb{H}
satisfies the mean value property
[
Fay77
, Coro.1.3]
. For
z=(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H}
given in polar coordinate, the function

u(\theta)=1-\theta\cot{\theta}

is a positive
2
-eigenfunction. Thus,
u
satisfies the mean value property. It is not hard to see that
\min\{u(\theta),u(\pi-\theta)\}
also satisfies the mean value property. Since
\min\{u(\theta),u(\pi-\theta)\}
is comparable to
\sin^{2}{\theta}
, from inequality (
2.2
) we know that the function
e^{-2\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})}
satisfies the mean value property in
\mathbb{H}
. The following lemma is the simplest version of
[
Wol08
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
r>0
and
p\in\mathbb{H}
, there exists a positive constant
c(r)
, only depending on
r
, such that

\displaystyle e^{-2\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(p,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})}\leqslant c(r)\int_{B_{\mathbb{H}}(p;r)}e^{-2\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})}\dArea

where
B_{\mathbb{H}}(p;r)=\{z\in\mathbb{H};\ \dist_{\mathbb{H}}(p,z)<r\}
is the hyperbolic geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at
p
and
\dArea
is the hyperbolic area form.

2.2.
Uniform collars

Let
X
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g

(g\geqslant 2)
, and
\alpha\subset X
be an essential simple loop. There always exists a unique closed geodesic, still denoted by
\alpha
, representing this loop. We denote by
\ell_{\alpha}(X)
the length of
\alpha
in
X
. If the
\delta
-neighborhood

\mathcal{C}_{\delta}(\alpha):=\{x\in X;\ \dist(x,\alpha)<\delta\}

is isometric to a cylinder
(\rho,t)\in(-\delta,\delta)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, endowed with the standard hyperbolic metric

ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+\ell_{\alpha}(X)^{2}\cosh^{2}\rho dt^{2},

this set
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}(\alpha)
is called a
\delta
-collar
of
\alpha
on
X
. Let
w(\alpha)
be the supremum of all such
\delta>0
. Then for all
0<\delta<w(\alpha)
, the geodesic arcs of length
\delta
emanating perpendicularly from
\alpha
are pairwisely disjoint. We call
\mathcal{C}_{w(\alpha)}(\alpha)
the
maximal collar
of
\alpha
, and
w(\alpha)
the
width
of
\alpha
.
First we recall the following classical Collar Lemma. One may refer to
[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.1.1]
or
[
Hub06
, Theorem 3.8.3]
for more details.

Lemma 2.2

(Collar lemma-1)
.

For any essential simple closed geodesic
\alpha\subset X
, then the width
w(\alpha)
of
\alpha
satisfies that

w(\alpha)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1}).

As the length
\ell_{\alpha}(X)
of the central closed geodesic
\alpha
goes to
0
, the width
w(\alpha)
tends to infinity. Set
f(\ell_{\alpha}(X))=\frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1})
. Then the quantity
f(\ell_{\alpha}(X))
tends to
0
as
\ell_{\alpha}(X)
goes to infinity. Another part of the classical Collar Lemma
[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.1.1]
or
[
Hub06
, Theorem 3.8.3]
says that
\mathcal{C}_{f(\ell_{\alpha_{1}}(X))}(\alpha_{1})\cap\mathcal{C}_{f(\ell_{\alpha_{2}}(X))}(\alpha_{2})=\emptyset
for any
\alpha_{1}\cap\alpha_{2}=\emptyset
. In this paper we will not use this disjoint property.

The
systole
of
X
is the length of a shortest closed geodesic in
X
. Curves that realize the systole are often referred to
systolic curves
. We denote by
\ell_{sys}(X)
the systole of
X
. It is known by Buser-Sarnak
[
BS94
]
that for each
g\geqslant 2
,
\ell_{sys}(X)\asymp\ln{(g)}
for certain hyperbolic surface
X
of genus
g
. Which in particular tells that
\ell_{\alpha}(X)
could be arbitrarily large for large enough
g
. If
\alpha
is a systolic curve, the following lemma may be well-known to experts. Since we can not find the obvious references, we provide a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2.3

(Collar lemma-2)
.

For any
\alpha\subset X
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
, then the width
w(\alpha)
of
\alpha
satisfies that

w(\alpha)\geqslant\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
w(\alpha)<\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}
. Then there exist two geodesic arcs
c^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime\prime}
of length
w(\alpha)
emanating perpendicularly from
\alpha
such that they have the same endpoint
p_{1}\in X
. The two starting points of
c^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime\prime}
divide
\alpha
into two arcs. We choose
\alpha_{1}
to be the shorter one. In particular, we have

\length(\alpha_{1})\leqslant\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}.

For the geodesic triangle
\Delta_{1}:=c^{\prime}\cup c^{\prime\prime}\cup\alpha_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\length(\Delta_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2w(\alpha)+\length(\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2\cdot\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}+\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X).

Which implies that
\Delta_{1}
bounds a disk. On the other hand, the geodesic triangle
\Delta_{1}=c^{\prime}\cup c^{\prime\prime}\cup\alpha_{1}
has two interior right angles, in particular, the geodesic triangle
\Delta_{1}
has total interior angles no less than
\pi
, which is a contradiction by Gauss-Bonnet.
∎

The two lemmas above imply that

Proposition 2.4

(Uniform Collar)
.

For any
\alpha\subset X
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
, then the width
w(\alpha)
of
\alpha
satisfies that

(2.3)

\displaystyle w(\alpha)\geqslant\max{\{\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1},\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}\}}.

Moreover, we have

w(\alpha)>\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

It follows by Lemma
2.2
and
2.3
that

w(\alpha)\geqslant\max{\{\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1},\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}\}}.

The minimum of the right hand side holds only if
\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1}=\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}
. If we set
t=e^{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}}
, this is equivalent to solve the equation
t^{3}-t^{2}-t-1=0.
A direct computation implies that the unique real solution is
t\sim 1.8392
. So we have

\max{\{\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1},\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{4}\}}\sim\ln{(1.8392)}\sim 0.5877>\frac{1}{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

2.3.
Teichmüller space

Let
S_{g}
be a closed surface of genus
g\ (g\geqslant 2)
. Let
\textsl{M}_{-1}
be the space of Riemannian metrics on
S_{g}
with constant curvature
-1
, and
X=(S_{g},\sigma(z)|dz|^{2})\in\textsl{M}_{-1}
. The group
\Diff_{+}(S_{g})
, which is the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of
S_{g}
, acts by pull back on
\textsl{M}_{-1}
. In particular this also holds for the normal subgroup
\Diff_{0}(S_{g})
of
\Diff_{+}(S_{g})
, the group of diffeomorphisms
isotopic to the identity. The group
\mbox{\rm Mod}(S_{g}):=\Diff_{+}(S_{g})/\Diff_{0}(S_{g})
is called the
mapping class group
of
S_{g}
.

The
Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}_{g}
of
S_{g}
is defined as

\mathcal{T}_{g}:=M_{-1}/\Diff_{0}(S_{g}).

The
moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{g}
of
S_{g}
is defined as

\mathcal{M}_{g}:=\mathcal{T}_{g}/\mbox{\rm Mod}(S_{g}).

The Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is a real analytic manifold. Let
\alpha\subset S_{g}
be an essential simple closed curve, then for any
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
, there exists a unique closed geodesic, still denoted by
\alpha
in
X
, which represents for
\alpha
in the fundamental group of
S_{g}
. The geodesic length function
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
defines a function on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. It is well-known that this function
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
is real-analytic on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. For more details on Teichmüller theory, one may refer to
[
Bus10
,
IT92
,
Hub06
]
.

Recall that the systole
\ell_{sys}(X)
of
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
is the length of shortest closed geodesics on
X
. It defines a continuous function
\ell_{sys}(\cdot):\mathcal{T}_{g}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which is called the
systole function
on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. In general, the systole function is continuous but not smooth because of corners where there may exist multiple essential simple closed geodesics realizing the systole. This function is very useful in Teichmüller theory. One may refer to
[
Akr03
,
Gen15
,
Sch93
]
for more details.

2.4.
Weil-Petersson metric

Let
X=(S_{g},\sigma(z)|dz|^{2})\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
. The tangent space
T_{X}\mathcal{T}_{g}
at
X
is identified with the space of
harmonic Beltrami differentials
on
X
, i.e. forms on
X
expressible as

\mu=\frac{\overline{\psi}}{\sigma}
where
\psi\in H^{0}(X,K^{2})
is a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
. Let
z=x+\textbf{i}y
and
\dArea=\sigma(z)dxdy
be the volume form. The
Weil-Petersson metric
is the Hermitian
metric on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
arising from the the
Petersson scalar product

\left<\varphi,\psi\right>_{wp}=\int_{X}\frac{\varphi\cdot\overline{\psi}}{\sigma^{2}}\dArea

via duality. We will concern ourselves primarily with its Riemannian part
ds^{2}_{\WP}
. Throughout this paper we denote the Teichmüller space endowed with the Weil-Petersson metric by
\Teich(S_{g})
. By definition it is easy to see that the mapping class group
\mbox{\rm Mod}(S_{g})
acts on
\Teich(S_{g})
as isometries. Thus, the Weil-Petersson metric descends into a metric, also called the Weil-Petersson metric, on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Throughout this paper we also denote by
\mathcal{M}_{g}
the moduli space endowed with the Weil-Petersson metric.

2.5.
Fenchel-Nielsen deformation

For any essential simple closed curve
\alpha\subset S_{g}
, the geodesic length function
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
is real-analytic over
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. Let
X=(S_{g},\sigma(z)|dz|^{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
be a hyperbolic surface and
\Gamma
be its associated Fuchsian group. Recall that we also let
\alpha
denote its unique closed geodesic representative in
X
. One may denote by
A:z\to e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}\cdot z
the deck transformation on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
corresponding to the simple closed geodesic
\alpha\subset X
.

Definition 2.5
.

Associated to the geodesic
\alpha
, we define the following holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
as

(2.4)

\Theta_{\alpha}(z):=\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{E^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{E(z)^{2}}dz^{2}

where
\left<A\right>
is the cyclic group generated by
A
.

Let
\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the Weil-Petersson gradient of the geodesic length function
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
at
X
. By
[
Wol82
]
it is known that
\nabla\ell_{\alpha}=-2\textbf{i}\cdot t_{\alpha}
where
t_{\alpha}
is the infinitesimal Fenchel-Nielsen right twist deformation along
\alpha
. Moreover,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)(z)=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{\overline{\Theta}_{\alpha}(z)}{\rho(z)|dz|^{2}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{\overline{E}^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{\overline{E}(z)^{2}\rho(z)}\frac{d\overline{z}}{dz}\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}

where
\rho(z)|dz|^{2}=\frac{|dz|^{2}}{(\im(z))^{2}}
is the hyperbolic metric on the upper half plane.

A special formula of Riera in
[
Rie05
]
says that

(2.6)

\langle\,\nabla\ell_{\alpha},\nabla\ell_{\alpha}\rangle_{wp}(X)=\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\ell_{\alpha}(X)+\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\{\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma/\left<A\right>-id\}}(u\ln{\frac{u+1}{u-1}}-2)\right)

where
u=\cosh{(\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(\widetilde{\alpha},E\circ\widetilde{\alpha}))}
and the double-coset of the identity element is omitted from the sum. In particular, one has

(2.7)

\langle\,\nabla\ell_{\alpha},\nabla\ell_{\alpha}\rangle_{wp}(X)>\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\ell_{\alpha}(X).

Definition 2.6
.

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
,
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
, we define the
L^{p}
-norm

||\mu||_{p}
of
\mu
to be

(2.8)

\displaystyle||\mu||_{p}:=\left(\int_{X}|\mu|^{p}\cdot\dArea\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If
p=2
, this is the standard Weil-Petersson norm. If
p=\infty
,
||\mu||_{\infty}=\max_{z\in X}|\mu(z)|
. One main goal of this paper is to study
||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}
when
\alpha\subset X
is a systolic curve.

2.6.
Weil-Petersson curvatures.

The curvature tensor of the Weil-Petersson metric on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is given as follows. Let
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}
be two elements in the tangent space
T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
at
X
, so that the metric tensor might be written in local coordinates as

\displaystyle g_{i\overline{j}}=\int_{X}\mu_{i}\cdot\overline{\mu_{j}}\dArea.

For the inverse of
(g_{i\overline{j}})
, we use the convention

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}g_{k\overline{j}}=\delta_{ik}.

Then the curvature tensor is given by

\displaystyle R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{l}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{k}\partial\overline{t^{l}}}g_{i\overline{j}}-g^{s\overline{t}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{k}}g_{i\overline{t}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{t^{l}}}g_{s\overline{j}}.

The following curvature formula was established in
[
Tro86
,
Wol86
]
. It has been applied to study various curvature properties of the Weil-Petersson metric. Wolpert
[
Wol86
]
and Tromba
[
Tro86
]
independently showed that
\mathcal{M}_{g}
has negative sectional curvature. In
[
Sch86
]
Schumacher showed that
\Teich(S_{g})
has strongly negative curvature in the sense of Siu. Liu-Sun-Yau in
[
LSY08
]
showed that
\mathcal{M}_{g}
has dual Nakano negative curvature, which says that the complex curvature operator on the dual tangent bundle is positive in some sense. It was shown in
[
Wu14
]
that the
\mathcal{M}_{g}
has non-positive definite Riemannian curvature operator. One can also see
[
Hua05
,
Hua07b
,
Hua07a
,
LSY04
,
MZ19
,
Teo09
,
Wol11
,
Wol12b
,
Wu17
,
WW18
]
for other aspects of the Weil-Petersson curvatures of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Set
D=-2(\Delta-2)^{-1}
where
\Delta
is the Beltrami-Laplace operator on
X=(S_{g},\sigma(z)|dz|^{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
. The operator
D
is positive and self-adjoint.

Theorem 2.7

(Tromba, Wolpert)
.

The curvature tensor satisfies

R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{l}}=\int_{X}D(\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}})\cdot(\mu_{k}\mu_{\overline{l}})\dArea+\int_{X}D(\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{l}})\cdot(\mu_{k}\mu_{\overline{j}})\dArea.

2.6.1.
Weil-Petersson holomorphic sectional curvatures.

Recall that a holomorphic sectional curvature is a sectional curvature along a holomorphic line. Let
\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Then Theorem
2.7
tells that the Weil-Petersson holomorphic sectional curvature
\HolK(\mu)
along the holomorphic line spanned by
\mu
is

\HolK(\mu)=\frac{-2\cdot\int_{X}D(|\mu|^{2})\cdot(|\mu|^{2})\dArea}{||\mu||_{\WP}^{4}}.

Assume that
||\mu||_{\WP}=1
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and an estimation of Wolf in
[
Wol12a
]
we know that
[
WW18
, Proposition 2.7]

(2.9)

-2\int_{X}|\mu|^{4}\dArea\leqslant\HolK(\mu)\leqslant-\frac{2}{3}\int_{X}|\mu|^{4}\dArea.

2.6.2.
Weil-Petersson sectional curvatures.

Let
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
be two orthogonal tangent vectors with
||\mu_{i}||_{\WP}=||\mu_{j}||_{\WP}=1
. Then the sectional curvature
K(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})
of the plane spanned by the real vectors corresponding to
\mu_{i}
and
\mu_{j}
is
[
Wol86
]

\displaystyle K(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\re{\int_{X}D(\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}})\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}}\dArea}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}D(\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}})\mu_{\overline{i}}\mu_{j}\dArea

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}|^{2})|\mu_{j}|^{2}\dArea.

Apply
[
Wol86
, Lemma 4.3]
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality one may have

\displaystyle\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}||\mu_{j}|)|\mu_{i}||\mu_{j}|\dArea

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}D(|\mu_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}|\mu_{i}||\mu_{j}|\dArea

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}|^{2})|\mu_{j}|^{2}\dArea)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times(\int_{X}D(|\mu_{j}|^{2})|\mu_{i}|^{2}\dArea)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}|^{2})|\mu_{j}|^{2}\dArea.

Where in the last inequality we apply the fact that the operator
D
is self-adjoint. It is clear that

|\int_{X}D(\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}})\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}}\dArea|\leqslant\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}||\mu_{j}|)|\mu_{i}||\mu_{j}|\dArea

and

|\int_{X}D(\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}})\mu_{\overline{i}}\mu_{j}\dArea|\leqslant\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}||\mu_{j}|)|\mu_{i}||\mu_{j}|\dArea.

Then one may have the following bound,

(2.10)

K(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})\geqslant-2\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}|^{2})|\mu_{j}|^{2}\dArea.

2.6.3.
Weil-Petersson Ricci curvatures.

Let
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3g-3}
be a holomorphic orthonormal basis of
T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Then the Ricci curvature
\Ric(\mu_{i})
of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
at
X
in the direction
\mu_{i}
is given by

\displaystyle\Ric(\mu_{i})=-\sum_{j=1}^{3g-3}R_{i\overline{j}j\overline{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{3g-3}(\int_{X}D(\mu_{i}\mu_{\overline{j}})\cdot(\mu_{j}\mu_{\overline{i}})\dArea+\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}|^{2})\cdot(|\mu_{j}|^{2})\dArea).

Since
\int_{X}D(f)\cdot\overline{f}\dArea\geqslant 0
for any function
f
on
X
, by applying the argument in the proof of (
2.10
) one may have

(2.11)

-2\leqslant\frac{\Ric(\mu_{i})}{\sum_{j=1}^{3g-3}\int_{X}D(|\mu_{i}|^{2})\cdot(|\mu_{j}|^{2})\dArea}\leqslant-1.

3.
A upper bound for
\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)

In this section we provide an elementary upper bound
H(z)
for
|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)(z)|
and detect the region where
H(z)
attains its maximal value.

Let
\alpha\subset X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
be an essential non-trivial loop. Up to conjugacy, one may assume that the closed geodesic, still denoted by
\alpha
, representing
\alpha
corresponds to the deck transformation
A:z\to e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}\cdot z
with axis
\widetilde{\alpha}=\textbf{i}\mathbb{R}^{+}
which is the imaginary axis, and the fundamental domain
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
with respect to this cyclic group
\left<A\right>
is

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\{z\in\mathbb{H};1\leqslant|z|\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}\}.

Recall that the Weil-Petersson gradient of
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
at
X
is
[
Gar75
,
Wol82
]

\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)(z)=\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{\overline{E}^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{\overline{E}(z)^{2}\rho(z)}\frac{d\overline{z}}{dz}\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}

where
\rho(z)=\frac{1}{\im(z)^{2}}
. Since
\rho(\gamma(z))|\gamma^{\prime}(z)|^{2}=\rho(z)
for any
\gamma\in\Aut(\mathbb{H})
, the triangle inequality gives that for all
z\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
,

(3.2)

|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)|(z)\leqslant\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{1}{|E(z)|^{2}}\times\frac{1}{\rho(E(z))}.

Set

(3.3)

\displaystyle H(z):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{1}{|E(z)|^{2}}\times\frac{1}{\rho(E(z))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\sin^{2}(\theta(E(z)))

where we write
E(z)=(r(E(z)),\theta(E(z)))
in the polar coordinate of
\mathbb{H}
. Since
H(\gamma\cdot z)=H(z)
for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, it descends into a function on
X

h:X\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}

defined as

h(\mathcal{\pi}(z)):=H(z)

where
\mathcal{\pi}:\mathbb{H}\to X
is the covering map and
z\in\mathbb{H}
is any lift point of
\mathcal{\pi}(z)\in X
.

By (
3.2
) we know that

(3.4)

|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)|(\mathcal{\pi}(z))\leqslant\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot H(z).

Now we estimate
\max\limits_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z)
or
\max\limits_{z\in X}h(z)
. First we compute the Laplacian of
H(z)
to detect the rough region where
\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z)
holds.

We rewrite
\frac{1}{|E(z)|^{2}}\times\frac{1}{\rho(E(z))}
as

f(z)=\frac{1}{4}\times\frac{(E(z)-\overline{E}(z))(\overline{E}(z)-E(z))}{E(z)\times\overline{E}(z)}.

Note that
E(z)
is analytic, i.e.,
E_{\overline{z}}(z)=0
. So we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\times(\frac{2E_{z}(z)\cdot(\overline{E}(z)-E(z))}{E(z)\times\overline{E}(z)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{(E(z)-\overline{E}(z))\cdot(\overline{E}(z)-E(z))}{E^{2}(z)\times\overline{E}(z)}\cdot E_{z}(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\frac{(E(z)+\overline{E}(z))\cdot(\overline{E}(z)-E(z))}{E^{2}(z)\times\overline{E}(z)}\cdot E_{z}(z).

Take one more derivative we get,

(3.6)

\displaystyle 4\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\overline{z}\partial z}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{E}(z)-E(z)}{E^{2}(z)\times\overline{E}(z)}\cdot E_{z}(z)\cdot\overline{E}_{\overline{z}}(z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{E(z)+\overline{E}(z)}{E^{2}(z)\times\overline{E}(z)}\cdot E_{z}(z)\cdot\overline{E}_{\overline{z}}(z)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{(E(z)+\overline{E}(z))\cdot(\overline{E}(z)-E(z))}{E^{2}(z)\times\overline{E}^{2}(z)}\cdot E_{z}(z)\cdot\overline{E}_{\overline{z}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|E_{z}(z)|^{2}}{|E(z)|^{4}}\times(E^{2}(z)+\overline{E}^{2}(z)).

Let
E(z)=\re{E(z)}+\textbf{i}\cdot\im{E(z)}
. Then

(3.7)

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\overline{z}\partial z}f(z)=\frac{|E_{z}(z)|^{2}}{2|E(z)|^{4}}\times\left((\re{E})^{2}-(\im{E})^{2}\right).

Recall that for
z=(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H}
given in the polar coordinate where
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, the hyperbolic distance between
z
and the imaginary axis
\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}}
is given in (
2.1
) saying

\displaystyle\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})=\ln|\csc{\theta}+|\cot{\theta}||.

Consider the set

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{H};\ y\geqslant|x|\}\cap\{(r,\theta);\ 1\leqslant r\leqslant\ell_{\alpha}(X)\}.

Geometrically,
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
is the set in
\mathbb{H}
which has distance to the imaginary axis with

\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})\leqslant\ln(\sqrt{2}+1).

Equation (
3.7
) tells that if
E(z)\notin\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
, then we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Delta f(z)\geqslant 0.

Recall that
\alpha
is the closed geodesic representing for
\alpha
in
X
which is lifted into
\mathbb{H}
as
\{\textbf{i}\cdot t\}
where
1\leqslant t\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}
. For any
\mathcal{\pi}(z)\in X
with
\dist(\pi(z),\alpha)>\ln(\sqrt{2}+1)
, we have that for any lift
z\in\mathbb{H}
of
\mathcal{\pi}(z)\in X
and any
E\in\Gamma
,

\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(E(z),\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})>\ln(\sqrt{2}+1).

Therefore equation (
3.7
) implies that for any
z\in\mathbb{H}
with
\dist(\mathcal{\pi}(z),\alpha)>\ln(\sqrt{2}+1)
we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\Delta H(z)=\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{|E_{z}(z)|^{2}}{4|E(z)|^{4}}\times(E^{2}(z)+\overline{E}^{2}(z))>0.

Recall that
h(\mathcal{\pi}(z))=H(z)
for any
z\in\mathbb{H}
. The following proposition tells that the maximum of
h
can only happen near the central closed geodesic. More precisely,

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

\max_{\pi(z)\in X}h(\pi(z))=\max_{\pi(z)\in X;\ \dist(\pi(z),\alpha)\leqslant\ln(\sqrt{2}+1)}h(\pi(z)).

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that there exists a point
\pi(w)\in X
with
\dist(\pi(w),\alpha)>\ln(\sqrt{2}+1)
such that

h(\pi(w))=\max_{\pi(z)\in X}h(\pi(z)).

Let
w\in\mathbb{H}
be a lift of
\pi(w)\in X
. Then we have

H(w)=\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z).

In particular, by the maximal principal we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\Delta H(w)\leqslant 0.

On the other hand, since
\dist(\pi(w),\alpha)>\ln(\sqrt{2}+1)
, for any
E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma
we have

\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(E\circ w,\alpha)>\ln(\sqrt{2}+1).

Then it follows by (
3.9
) that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Delta H(w)>0

which is a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The proposition above tells that the maximum of
H
can only happen in the
\ln(\sqrt{2}+1)-
neighborhood of
\cup_{E\in\left<A\right>/\Gamma}E\circ\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}}
, which is also the same as
\cup_{E\in\left<A\right>/\Gamma}E\circ\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
.

4.
Uniform upper bounds for
L^{\infty}
-norms

In this section we will prove the uniform upper bounds in Theorem
1.1
for
p=\infty
. Part (1) and (2) of Theorem
1.1
in this case will be proved separately. We first show

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\alpha\subset X
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
. Then

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\prec 1.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
is splitted into two parts. We first deal with the case that
X
is always contained in certain fixed thick part of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any given constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\alpha\subset X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}
, then there exists a uniform constant
C_{1}(\varepsilon_{0})>0
, only depending on
\varepsilon_{0}
, such that

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\leqslant C_{1}(\varepsilon_{0}).

Proof.

By (
3.4
) it suffices to show that

\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z)\leqslant C^{\prime}_{1}(\varepsilon_{0})

for some uniform constant
C^{\prime}_{1}(\varepsilon_{0})>0
only depending on
\varepsilon_{0}
.

Let
z_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
is the fundamental domain of
X
which contains the lift
\{\textbf{i}\cdot t\}_{1\leqslant t\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}}
of the shortest closed geodesic
\alpha
in
X
, such that
H
attains its maximum at
z_{0}
. That is,

H(z_{0})=\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z).

Since
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}
, it follows by the triangle inequality that for any
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
, the geodesic balls satisfy that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\circ B(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\cap\gamma_{2}\circ B(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})=\emptyset.

Recall that
\frac{1}{|E(z)|^{2}}\times\frac{1}{\rho(E(z))}=\sin^{2}(\theta(E(z)))
where we use polar coordinate
(r,\theta)
for
E(z)
. For any
E\notin\left<A\right>
, the point
E\circ z_{0}
must lie outside of a lift
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{w(\alpha)}(\alpha)
of the maximal collar
\mathcal{C}_{w(\alpha)}(\alpha)
where

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{w(\alpha)}(\alpha)=\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha};\ \dist_{H}(z,\textbf{i}\cdot\mathbb{R}^{+})\leqslant w(\alpha)\}.

In particular, by Lemma
2.3
we have for all
E\notin\left<A\right>
,

\displaystyle\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(E\circ z_{0},\textbf{i}\cdot\mathbb{R^{+}})\geqslant\frac{\ell_{sys}(X)}{4}.

Then it follows by the triangle inequality that for any
E\notin\left<A\right>
and
z\in E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\cdot\mathbb{R^{+}})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(E\circ z_{0},\textbf{i}\cdot\mathbb{R^{+}})-\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(E\circ z_{0},z)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{sys}(X)}{4}-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{sys}(X)}{8}.

Then by formula (
2.1
) we have that for any
E\notin\left<A\right>
and
z\in E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})
,

\displaystyle\ln{(\frac{2}{\sin(\theta(z))})}\geqslant\frac{\ell_{sys}(X)}{8}.

Recall that
e^{x}\geqslant x
for all
x\geqslant 0
. Thus, for all
z\in E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})
where
E\notin\left<A\right>
we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sin{(\theta(z))}\leqslant\frac{16}{\ell_{sys}(X)}.

Now we apply the mean value inequality. Recall that (
3.3
) says that

H(z)=\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\sin^{2}(\theta(E(z))).

Then it follows by (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
that

(4.4)

\displaystyle H(z_{0})-\sin^{2}(\theta(z_{0}))\leqslant\sum_{E\neq\left<A\right>\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}4e^{-2\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(E\circ z_{0},\textbf{i}\cdot\mathbb{R^{+}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4c(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\sum_{E\neq\left<A\right>\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\int_{E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})}e^{-2\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z,\textbf{i}\cdot\mathbb{R^{+}})}\dArea.

Then by the triangle inequality and (
4.3
) we know that for all
E\notin\left<A\right>
,

E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\subset\{(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H};\ e^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}}\leqslant r\leqslant e^{\ell_{sys}(X)+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}}\ \emph{and}\ \sin(\theta)\leqslant\frac{16}{\ell_{sys}(X)}\}.

Set

\mathcal{S}:=\{(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H};\ e^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}}\leqslant r\leqslant e^{\ell_{sys}(X)+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}}\ \emph{and}\ \sin(\theta)\leqslant\frac{16}{\ell_{sys}(X)}\}.

By (
4.1
) we know that the balls
\{E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\}
are pairwisely disjoint. Then it follows by (
2.2
) and (
4.4
) that

(4.5)

\displaystyle H(z_{0})\leqslant\sin^{2}(\theta(z_{0}))+4c(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\int_{\mathcal{S}}\sin^{2}{\theta}\dArea

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+8c(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\int_{0}^{\arcsin(\min\{\frac{16}{\ell_{sys}(X)},1\})}\int_{e^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}}}^{e^{\ell_{sys}(X)+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}}}\frac{\sin^{2}{\theta}}{r^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}}rdrd\theta

\displaystyle=1+8c(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\cdot(\ell_{sys}(X)+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4})\cdot\arcsin(\min\{\frac{16}{\ell_{sys}(X)},1\})

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+10c(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8})\cdot\ell_{sys}(X)\cdot\arcsin(\min\{\frac{16}{\ell_{sys}(X)},1\})

where we apply
\dArea=\frac{|dz|^{2}}{y^{2}}=\frac{rdrd\theta}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}
in the second inequality and
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}>0
in the last inequality. As
\ell_{sys}(X)\to\infty
, the right hand side above goes to
(1+160c(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{8}))
which is bounded. Recall that we always assume that
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Therefore there exists a uniform constant
C^{\prime}_{1}(\varepsilon_{0})>0
such that

H(z_{0})=\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z)\leqslant C^{\prime}_{1}(\varepsilon_{0}).

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The proof above does not cover Proposition
5.1
for the case that
\ell_{sys}(X)\to 0
because the constant
c(\varepsilon_{0})\to\infty
as
\varepsilon_{0}\to 0
. We will use a different way to prove it in the next lemma.

Now we deal with the case that
\ell_{sys}(X)
is short. For this case actually we will prove a more general result which does not require
\alpha
to be a systolic curve of
X
. This is also a special case of the uniform upper bounds in Part (2) of Theorem
1.1
for
p=\infty
. More precisely,

Lemma 4.4
.

For any given constant
0<L_{0}<\frac{1}{1000}
and
\alpha\subset X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
, then there exists a uniform constant
C_{2}(L_{0})>0
, only depending on
L_{0}
, such that

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\leqslant C_{2}(L_{0}).

Proof.

We follow the argument in
[
Wol92
, Lemma 2.2]
or
[
Wol12b
, Proposition 6]
. By (
3.4
) it suffices to show that

\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z)\leqslant C^{\prime}_{2}(L_{0})

for some uniform constant
C^{\prime}_{2}(L_{0})>0
only depending on
L_{0}
.

Let
z_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\{z\in\mathbb{H};\ 1\leqslant|z|\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is the fundamental domain which contains the lift
\{\textbf{i}\cdot t\}_{1\leqslant t\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}}
of the closed geodesic
\alpha
, such that

H(z_{0})=\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}}H(z).

By Proposition
3.1
we know that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\dist_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0},\textbf{i}\mathbb{R^{+}})\leqslant\ln{(1+\sqrt{2})}.

By Lemma
2.2
we know that the width
w(\alpha)
of the maximal collar of
\alpha
satisfies that

(4.7)

\displaystyle w(\alpha)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{\cosh{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln{(\frac{e^{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}+1}{e^{\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}}-1})}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\ln{(\frac{2}{\ell_{\alpha}(X)})}

where we apply
\ell_{\alpha}(X)<\frac{1}{1000}
in the last inequality. In particular,

\displaystyle w(\alpha)>\ln{(2000)}.

Let
\inj(\pi(z_{0}))
be the injectivity radius of
\pi(z_{0})\in X
. By (
4.6
) we have

\dist(\pi(z_{0}),\alpha)\leqslant\ln{(1+\sqrt{2})}.

Then it follows by the triangle inequality that the geodesic ball
B(\pi(z_{0});1)
in
X
centered at
\pi(z_{0})
of radius
1
is contained in the maximal collar of
\alpha
. That is,

(4.8)

B(\pi(z_{0});1)\subset\mathcal{C}_{w(\alpha)}(\alpha)

which together with the Collar Lemma
[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.1.6]
implies that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\inj(\pi(z_{0}))\geqslant\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}{2}.

Now we apply the mean value inequality. Recall that (
3.3
) says that

H(z)=\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{1}{|E(z)|^{2}}\times\frac{1}{\rho(E(z))}=\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\sin^{2}(\theta(E(z))).

Then it follows by (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
that

(4.10)

\displaystyle H(z_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4c(1)\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\int_{B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};1)}\sin^{2}(\theta(E(z)))\dArea

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4c(1)\int_{B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};1)}H(z)\dArea.

Similar as
[
Wol92
, Lemma 2.2]
or
[
Wol12b
, Proposition 6]
, the projection
\pi:\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};1)
onto its image in
X
has multiplicity at most
\frac{c^{\prime}}{\inj(\pi(z_{0}))}
where
c^{\prime}>0
is a uniform constant. Indeed, for any
\gamma\notin\left<A\right>
it follows by the triangle inequality that
\dist_{H}(\gamma\circ z_{0},z_{0})\geqslant\dist_{H}(\gamma\circ z_{0},\textbf{i}\mathbb{R}^{+})-\dist_{H}(z_{0},\textbf{i}\mathbb{R}^{+})\geqslant w(\alpha)-\ln{(1+\sqrt{2})}>\ln(2000)-1>2
. This tells that the multiplicity of the projection
\pi:\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};1)
onto its image only comes from the cyclic group
\left<A\right>
. Which in particular implies that the multiplicity of the projection is at most
\frac{c^{\prime}}{\inj(\pi(z_{0}))}
for some uniform constant
c^{\prime}>0
.

Recall that
H(\gamma\circ z)=H(z)
. Thus, the last integral in (
4.10
) is bounded above by

\frac{c^{\prime}}{\inj(\pi(z_{0}))}\cdot\int_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\bigcap(\bigcup_{E\in\left<A\right>/\Gamma}E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};1))}\sin^{2}(\theta(z))\dArea.

Therefore, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ H(z_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{4c^{\prime}\cdot c(1)}{\inj(\pi(z_{0}))}\cdot\int_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\bigcap(\bigcup_{E\in\left<A\right>/\Gamma}E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};1))}\sin^{2}(\theta(z))\dArea

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{4c^{\prime}\cdot c(1)}{\inj(\pi(z_{0}))}\cdot\int_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\sin^{2}(\theta(z))\dArea

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4c^{\prime}\cdot c(1)}{\inj(\pi(z_{0}))}\cdot\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{1}^{e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}}\frac{\sin^{2}{\theta}}{r^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}}rdrd\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi c^{\prime}\cdot c(1)}{\inj(\pi(z_{0}))}\cdot\ell_{\alpha}(X)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 8\pi c^{\prime}\cdot c(1)

where we apply (
4.9
) in the last inequality. Then the conclusion follows by choosing

C^{\prime}_{2}(L_{0})=8\pi c^{\prime}\cdot c(1).

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Let
\mu=\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)
in
[
Wol17
, Definition 10]
. Then the quantity
Comp(\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X))
is comparable to
\frac{1}{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{wp}}
. Thus,
[

, Lemma 11]
implies that if
\alpha
has length at most
c_{0}
, then there exists two positive constants
c^{\prime}
and

such that

The upper bound
C_{2}(L_{0})
in Lemma
4.4
is uniform. And we will use this upper bound and (

) of Riera to show that
||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\geqslant C_{2}^{\prime}(L_{0})
for some uniform constant

. The methods for obtaining these two bounds are similar.

Remark 4.6
.

The upper bound
\frac{1}{1000}
in the assumption of Lemma
4.4
is clearly not optimal. However it is already good enough for proving the uniform upper bounds of Theorem
1.1
for
p=\infty
.

Now we prove Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

If
\ell_{sys}(X)<\frac{1}{1000}
, it follows by Lemma
4.4
that for any
\alpha\subset X
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
we have

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\leqslant C_{2}(\frac{1}{1000})

where
C_{2}(\cdot)
is the constant in Lemma
4.4
.

If
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant\frac{1}{1000}
, it follows by Lemma
4.2
that

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\leqslant C_{1}(\frac{1}{1000})

where
C_{1}(\cdot)
is the constant in Lemma
4.2
.

Thus, we have that for any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\alpha\subset X
with

,

Then the conclusion follows.
∎

Next we prove the uniform upper bounds in Part (2) of Theorem
1.1
for
p=\infty
. That is to extend the constant
\frac{1}{1000}
in Lemma
4.4
to an arbitrary fixed positive constant. More precisely,

Proposition 4.7
.

For any given constant
L_{0}>0
and simple loop
\alpha\subset X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
, then there exists a uniform constant
C_{3}(L_{0})>0
, only depending on
L_{0}
, such that

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\leqslant C_{3}(L_{0}).

That is,

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\prec 1.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
one may assume that

\frac{1}{1000}\leqslant\ell_{\alpha}(X)\leqslant L_{0}

where
L_{0}\geqslant\frac{1}{1000}
. It follows by (
3.4
) and Proposition
3.1
that

(4.12)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\leqslant\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\max_{\pi(z)\in X;\ \dist(\pi(z),\alpha)\leqslant\ln(\sqrt{2}+1)}h(\pi(z))

where
h(\pi(z))=H(z)
and
H(z)=\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\sin^{2}(\theta(E(z)))
given in (
3.3
).

Claim:
there exists a uniform constant
s_{0}=s_{0}(L_{0})>0
only depending on
L_{0}
such that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in X;\ \dist(p,\alpha)\leqslant\ln{(\sqrt{2}+1)}}\inj(p)\geqslant s_{0}.

Proof of Claim.
Let
p\in X
with
\dist(p,\alpha)\leqslant\ln{(\sqrt{2}+1)}
such that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\inj(p)<\frac{1}{2000};

otherwise we are done. Since
\ell_{\alpha}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
, it follows by the triangle inequality that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\alpha\subset B(p;\ln{(\sqrt{2}+1)}+\frac{L_{0}}{2}).

Since
\ell_{\alpha}(X)\geqslant\frac{1}{1000}
, by (
4.14
) and the Collar Lemma
[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.1.6]
there exists a closed geodesic
\beta\neq\alpha
such that

p\in\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)

where
\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)
is the maximal collar of
\beta
. Let
\partial\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)
be the boundary of the maximal collar
\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)
. Since
\beta
is the unique closed geodesic in
\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)
,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\dist(p,\partial\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta))\leqslant\ln{(\sqrt{2}+1)}+\frac{L_{0}}{2};

otherwise the geodesic ball
B(p;\ln{(\sqrt{2}+1)}+\frac{L_{0}}{2})\subset\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)
that together with (
4.15
) implies that

\alpha\subset\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)

which is impossible because the collar
\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)
can not contain two different simple closed geodesics
\alpha
and
\beta
. Recall that the Collar Lemma
[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.1.6]
tells that

\sinh{(\inj(p))}=\cosh{(\frac{\ell_{\beta}(X)}{2})}\cosh(\dist(p,\partial\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)))-\sinh(\dist(p,\partial\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)))

which together with (
4.16
) implies that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \sinh{(\inj(p))}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\cosh(\dist(p,\partial\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)))-\sinh(\dist(p,\partial\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\dist(p,\partial\mathcal{C}_{w(\beta)}(\beta))}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(\sqrt{2}-1)\cdot e^{-\frac{L_{0}}{2}}.

So we have

\inj(p)\geqslant\sinh^{-1}((\sqrt{2}-1)\cdot e^{-\frac{L_{0}}{2}}).

Since
p\in X
with
\dist(p,\alpha)\leqslant\ln{(\sqrt{2}+1)}
is arbitrary, the claim follows by setting

(4.18)

\displaystyle s_{0}=s_{0}(L_{0})=\min\{\frac{1}{2000},\sinh^{-1}((\sqrt{2}-1)\cdot e^{-\frac{L_{0}}{2}})\}.

The remaining follows by a standard
unfolding
argument. More precisely we let
\pi(z_{0})\in X
such that

h(\pi(z_{0}))=\sup_{p\in X}h(p).

By Proposition
3.1
and (
4.13
) we know that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\inj(\pi(z_{0}))\geqslant s_{0}.

Then it follows by the triangle inequality that for any
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
, the geodesic balls satisfy that

\gamma_{1}\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{s_{0}}{4})\cap\gamma_{2}\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{s_{0}}{4})=\emptyset

where
z_{0}\in\{(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H};\ 1\leqslant r\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}\}
is a lift of
\pi(z_{0})
. Then it follows by (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle h(\pi(z_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(z_{0})=\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\sin^{2}(\theta(E(z_{0})))

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4c(\frac{s_{0}}{4})\cdot\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\int_{B_{\mathbb{H}}(E\circ z_{0};\frac{s_{0}}{4})}\sin^{2}(\theta(z))\dArea.

These balls
\{E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{s_{0}}{4})\}
are pairwisely disjoint and for all
E\notin\left<A\right>
,

E\circ B_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{0};\frac{s_{0}}{4})\subset\{(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H};\ e^{-\frac{s_{0}}{4}}\leqslant r\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)+\frac{s_{0}}{4}}\}.

Thus, we have

(4.20)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\leqslant\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot h(\pi(z_{0}))

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{8}{\pi}\cdot c(\frac{s_{0}}{4})\cdot\int_{z\in\mathbb{H};\ e^{-\frac{s_{0}}{4}}\leqslant r\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)+\frac{s_{0}}{4}}}\sin^{2}(\theta(z))\dArea

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{\pi}\cdot c(\frac{s_{0}}{4})\cdot\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{e^{-\frac{s_{0}}{4}}}^{e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)+\frac{s_{0}}{4}}}\frac{\sin^{2}{\theta}}{r^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}}rdrd\theta

\displaystyle=8\cdot c(\frac{s_{0}}{4})\cdot(\frac{s_{0}}{2}+\ell_{\alpha}(X))

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\cdot c(\frac{s_{0}}{4})\cdot(\frac{s_{0}}{2}+L_{0})

which completes the proof by setting
C_{3}(L_{0})=8\cdot c(\frac{s_{0}}{4})\cdot(\frac{s_{0}}{2}+L_{0})
.
∎

5.
Uniform bounds for
L^{p}(1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty)
-norm

Recall that we always use the notation:
r^{\frac{1}{\infty}}=1
for any
r>0
.

In this section we prove

Theorem 5.1

(=Theorem
1.1
)
.

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
and
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}

(g\geqslant 2)
, then we have

(1)

for any
\alpha\subset X
with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
,

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}\asymp\ell_{sys}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2)

For any simple loop
\beta\subset X
with
\ell_{\beta}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
where
L_{0}>0
is any given constant,

||\nabla\ell_{\beta}(X)||_{p}\asymp\ell_{\beta}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We will first show Theorem
5.1
for the cases that
p=1,2
and
\infty
. Then for general
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,\infty)
, it follows by a standard argument through using Hölder’s inequality for integrals.

Before proving Theorem
5.1
, we first show that
||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\prec\ell_{\alpha}(X)
where
\alpha\subset X
is not necessary to be a systolic curve. More precisely,

Lemma 5.2
.

For any non-trivial loop
\alpha\subset X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, then we have

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\leqslant 2\ell_{\alpha}(X).

Proof.

The proof is a standard
unfolding
argument. Recall that

\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)(z)=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{\overline{\Theta}_{\alpha}(z)}{\rho(z)|dz|^{2}}

where
\Theta_{\alpha}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\frac{E^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{E(z)^{2}}dz^{2}
. Let
\mathbb{F}\subset\{z\in\mathbb{H};1\leqslant|z|\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}\}
be a fundamental domain of
X
. Then

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{F}}|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)(z)|\cdot\rho(z)|dz|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{E\in\left<A\right>\backslash\Gamma}\int_{\mathbb{F}}\frac{|E^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{|E(z)|^{2}}|dz|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{z\in\mathbb{H};\ 1\leqslant|z|\leqslant e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}}\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}|dz|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{1}^{e^{\ell_{\alpha}(X)}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\cdot rdrd\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\ell_{\alpha}(X).

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We first prove Part (1). The proof is splitted into the following four cases.

Case-1:
p=1
.
First by Lemma
5.2
we clearly have

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\prec\ell_{\alpha}(X).

For the other direction, since
||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}^{2}\leqslant||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\cdot||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}
, by (
2.7
) of Riera and Proposition
4.1
we have

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\geqslant\frac{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}^{2}}{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}}\succ\ell_{\alpha}(X).

Thus, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\asymp\ell_{sys}(X).

Case-2:
p=2
.
First (
2.7
) of Riera says that

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}\succ(\ell_{\alpha}(X))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the other direction, since
||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}^{2}\leqslant||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\cdot||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}
, by Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
5.2
we have

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}\leqslant\sqrt{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\cdot||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}}\prec(\ell_{\alpha}(X))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}\asymp\ell_{sys}(X)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Case-3:
p=\infty
.
First Proposition
4.1
says that

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\prec 1.

For the other direction, since
||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}^{2}\leqslant||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\cdot||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}
, by Case-1 and Case-2 we have

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\geqslant\frac{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}^{2}}{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}}\asymp 1.

Thus, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}\asymp 1.

Case-4: general
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,\infty)
.
First since

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}^{p}\leqslant||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{1}\cdot||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{\infty}^{p-1},

by Proposition
4.1
and Case-1 we have

||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}\prec\ell_{\alpha}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For the other direction, let
q\in(1,2)\cup(2,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Similar as above we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{q}\prec\ell_{\alpha}(X)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Recall that Hölder inequality says that

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}\geqslant\frac{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{2}^{2}}{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{q}}.

By Case-2 and (
5.4
) we have

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}\succ\ell_{\alpha}(X)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}=\ell_{\alpha}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus, we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)||_{p}\asymp\ell_{sys}(X)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then the conclusion follows by (
5.1
), (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.5
).

To prove Part (2), it follows by the same argument as the proof of Part (1) by applying Proposition
4.7
instead of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

6.
Applications to Weil-Petersson geometry

In this section we make several applications of Theorem
1.1
to the Weil-Petersson geometry of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

6.1.
A uniform lower bound for Weil-Petersson distance

As discussed in the introduction, Theorem
5.1
can be applied to prove the following two results on the global Weil-Petersson geometry of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
Wu19
, Theorem 1.3]

For all
X,Y\in\Teich(S_{g})
,

|\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(X)}-\sqrt{\ell_{sys}(Y)}|\prec\dist_{wp}(X,Y)

where
\dist_{wp}
is the Weil-Petersson distance.

Theorem 6.2
.

[
Wu19
, Theorem 1.1]

For all
g\geqslant 2
,

\InR(\mathcal{M}_{g})\asymp\sqrt{\ln{(g)}}.

6.2.
Uniform bounds on Weil-Petersson curvatures

In this subsection we prove several new uniform bounds on Weil-Petersson curvatures. Recall the formula (
2.9
) says that for all
\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
||\mu||_{2}=1
,

(6.1)

\HolK(\mu)\asymp-\int_{X}|\mu|^{4}\dArea.

Our first bound on Weil-Petersson curvature is as follows.

Theorem 6.3

(=Theorem
1.2
)
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, let
\alpha\subset X
be a simple closed geodesic satisfying

(1)

either
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
or

(2)

\ell_{\alpha}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
where
L_{0}>0
is any fixed constant,

then the Weil-Petersson holomorphic sectional curvature

\HolK(\nabla\ell_{\alpha})(X)\asymp\frac{-1}{\ell_{sys}(X)}.

Proof of Theorem
6.3
.

By (
6.1
) we have

(6.2)

\displaystyle\HolK(\nabla\ell_{\alpha})(X)\asymp-\frac{\int_{X}|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)|^{4}\dArea}{(\int_{X}|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)|^{2}\dArea)^{2}}.

We apply Theorem
5.1
for the cases that
p=2,4
to obtain

(6.3)

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)|^{4}\dArea\asymp\ell_{sys}(X)

and

(6.4)

\displaystyle(\int_{X}|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)|^{2}\dArea)^{2}\asymp\ell_{sys}^{2}(X).

Then the conclusion follows by these three equations above.
∎

Buser-Sarnak in
[
BS94
]
show that
\max_{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\ell_{sys}(X)\asymp\ln(g)
for all
g\geqslant 2
. A direct consequence of Theorem
6.3
is as follows.

Corollary 6.4
.

For all
g\geqslant 2
,

\sup_{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(\nabla\ell_{\alpha})(X)\asymp\frac{-1}{\ln(g)}

where
\alpha\subset X
satisfies that
\ell_{\alpha}(X)=\ell_{sys}(X)
.

Another application of Theorem
5.1
is to show that the minimum Weil-Petersson holomorphic sectional curvature at any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
is bounded from above by a uniform negative number. More precisely,

Theorem 6.5

(=Theorem
1.4
)
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
,

\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(\mu)\prec-1<0.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
be arbitrary. We split the proof into two cases.

Case-1:
\ell_{sys}(X)=\ell_{\alpha}(X)<100
for some
\alpha\subset X
. It follows by Theorem
6.3
that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(\mu)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\HolK(\nabla\ell_{\alpha})(X)

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{\ell_{sys}(X)}

\displaystyle\prec

\displaystyle-1

where we apply
\ell_{sys}(X)<100
in the last equation.

Case-2:
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant 100
. We use the
\mu_{0}(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\overline{r^{\prime}(z)^{2}}}{\rho(z)}\frac{d\overline{z}}{dz}\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
instead of
\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)
. Since
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant 100>2\ln{(3+2\sqrt{2})}
, it follows by
[
Wu17
, Theorem 6.1]
or the proof of
[
WW18
, Theorem 1.1]
that

\displaystyle\HolK(\mu_{0})(X)\prec-1.

Thus, we have if
\ell_{sys}(X)\geqslant 100
,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(P)\leqslant\HolK(\mu_{0})(X)\prec-1.

Therefore the conclusion follows by the two cases above.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

Let
\widetilde{Q}:\wedge^{2}T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}\to\wedge^{2}T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the real Riemannian curvature operator. This is an endomorphism of a
(3g-3)(6g-7)
dimensional vector space. It was shown in
[
Wu14
]
that
\widetilde{Q}
is non-positive definite. Moreover, it was shown in
[
WW18
, Theorem 1.2]
that the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm
||\widetilde{Q}||_{\ell^{\infty}}(X)
of
\widetilde{Q}
at any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
satisfies that

||\widetilde{Q}||_{\ell^{\infty}}(X)\geqslant\frac{1}{2\pi}.

In particular, we have
||\widetilde{Q}||_{\ell^{\infty}}(X)\succ 1
. The proof of this inequality highly depends on the Weil-Petersson curvature operator formula developed in
[
Wu14
]
. By definition and the negativity of Weil-Petersson curvature one knows that

||\widetilde{Q}||_{\ell^{\infty}}(X)\geqslant\max_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}|\HolK(\mu)|=-\min_{\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}}\HolK(\mu).

Thus, by Theorem
6.5
we also get

||\widetilde{Q}||_{\ell^{\infty}}(X)\succ 1.

Although the uniform lower bound is not as explicit as
\frac{1}{2\pi}
in
[
WW18
, Theorem 1.2]
, as a direct consequence of Theorem
6.5
, we give a completely different proof on this uniform lower bound
||\widetilde{Q}||_{\ell^{\infty}}(X)\succ 1
without using the Weil-Petersson curvature operator formula.

7.
Applications to the
L^{p}
metric on
\mathcal{M}_{g}

In this section we study the
L^{p}

(1<p\leqslant\infty)
metric on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and its relation to Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(X,\sigma(z)|dz|^{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
be a hyperbolic surface and
\phi\in Q(X)
be a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
. For any
p\in[1,\infty]
, we define

(7.1)

\displaystyle||\phi||_{L^{p}}:=||\frac{\overline{\phi}}{\sigma}||_{p}=\left(\int_{X}\left(\frac{|\phi(z)|}{\sigma(z)}\right)^{p}\cdot\dArea\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The
L^{p}
metric on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is defined by (
7.1
) via duality. More precisely,

Definition 7.1
.

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
, the
L^{p}
-metric
on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is defined as follows: for any
\mu\in T_{X}\mathcal{M}_{g}
which is a harmonic Beltrami differential on
X
we define

(7.2)

\displaystyle||\mu||_{L^{p}}:=\sup_{\phi\in Q(X);\ ||\phi||_{L^{p}}=1}\re\int_{X}\left(\frac{\phi}{\sigma}\cdot\mu\right)\cdot\dArea

We call that
(\mathcal{M}_{g},||\cdot||_{L^{p}})
is
the moduli space endowed with the
L^{p}
-metric
, and denote by
\dist_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
the distance function on
(\mathcal{M}_{g},||\cdot||_{L^{p}})
.

By definition we know that
(\mathcal{M}_{g},||\cdot||_{L^{1}})
is the Teichmüller metric on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
which is a complete Finsler metric. And

(\mathcal{M}_{g},||\cdot||_{L^{2}})
is the Weil-Petersson metric on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
which is an incomplete Kähler metric.

Let
q\in[1,\infty]
be the conjugate number of
p
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
. For (
7.2
) first by the Hölder inequality we have

(7.3)

\displaystyle||\mu||_{L^{p}}\leqslant||\mu||_{q}

where
||\mu||_{q}
is defined in (
2.8
). On the other hand, by choosing
\phi=\frac{\overline{\mu}\cdot\sigma}{||\mu||_{p}}
in (
7.2
) we have

(7.4)

\displaystyle||\mu||_{L^{p}}\geqslant\frac{||\mu||_{2}^{2}}{||\mu||_{p}}=\frac{||\mu||_{\WP}^{2}}{||\mu||_{p}}.

In particular,

||\mu||_{L^{2}}=||\mu||_{2}=||\mu||_{\WP}.

Now we are ready to state our result in this section.

Theorem 7.2

(=Theorem
1.6
)
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\alpha\subset X
be a non-trivial simple loop with
\ell_{\alpha}(X)\leqslant L_{0}
where
L_{0}>0
is any given constant. Then for any
p\in(1,\infty]
,

\dist_{p}(X,\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\alpha})\prec(\ell_{\alpha}(X))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

where
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\alpha}
is the stratum of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
whose pinching curve is
\alpha
. In particular, the space
(\mathcal{M}_{g},||\cdot||_{L^{p}})

(1<p\leqslant\infty)
is incomplete.

Proof.

Let
\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(X)
be the Weil-Petersson gradient of the geodesic length function
\ell_{\alpha}(\cdot)
at
X
. Now we consider the integral curve of the vector field
-\frac{\nabla\ell_{\alpha}}{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}||_{L^{p}}}
. More precisely, let
c:[0,s)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
be a curve, where
s>0
is length of the maximal interval, satisfying

(1)

c(0)=X
and

(2)

c^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))}{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))||_{L^{p}}}
.

Direct computations show that
t
is an arc-length parameter of
c(\cdot)
in
(\mathcal{M}_{g},||\cdot||_{L^{p}})
and
\ell_{\alpha}(c(t_{1}))<\ell_{\alpha}(c(t_{2}))
for any
s>t_{1}>t_{2}\geqslant 0
. Thus, as
t\to s
,
c(t)
goes to
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\alpha}
. So we have

\lim_{t\to s}\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))=0.

Since
\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))
is decreasing,
\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))\leqslant L_{0}
for all
t\in[0,s)
. It follows by Part (2) of Theorem
1.1
that for all
t\in[0,s)
and
r\in[1,\infty]
,

(7.5)

\displaystyle||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))||_{r}\asymp(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t)))^{\frac{1}{r}}

which together with (
7.3
) and (
7.4
) implies that

(7.6)

\displaystyle|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))||_{L^{p}}\asymp(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t)))^{\frac{1}{q}}

where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
. Thus, we have for all
s>t_{1}>t_{2}\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle|(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t_{1})))^{\frac{1}{q}}-(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t_{2})))^{\frac{1}{q}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\frac{(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t)))^{\frac{1}{q}-1}}{q}\left<\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(c(t)),c^{\prime}(t)\right>_{wp}dt|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\frac{||\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))||^{2}_{\WP}}{(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t)))^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot|\nabla\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))||_{L^{p}}}dt

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\frac{\ell_{\alpha}(c(t))}{(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t)))^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot(\ell_{\alpha}(c(t)))^{\frac{1}{q}}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{1}-t_{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\dist_{p}(c(t_{1}),c(t_{2}))

where in the last inequality we apply
t
is an arc-length parameter for
c(\cdot)
. We choose
t_{2}=0
and let
t_{1}\to s
to get

(7.7)

\displaystyle\dist_{p}(X,\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\alpha})\leqslant\liminf_{t\to s}\dist_{p}(c(t),c(0))\prec(\ell_{\alpha}(X))^{\frac{1}{q}}

which completes the proof because
\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{1}{p}
.
∎

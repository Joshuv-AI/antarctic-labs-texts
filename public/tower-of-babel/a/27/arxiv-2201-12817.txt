Topology of Singularities of Optimal Semicouplings
J. H. Martel

Abstract
. This article is a summary of some results from the author’s thesis [ Mar ] . We study the topology of singularities of c c -optimal semicouplings in unequal dimension. Our main results 1 , 2 describe homotopy-reductions from a source space ( X , σ ) (X,\sigma) onto the singularities Z j Z_{j} , j ≥ 0 j\geq 0 of c c -optimal semicouplings whenever ( Y , τ ) (Y,\tau) is a Riemannian target space and c : X × Y → ℝ ∪ { + ∞ } c:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\} is a cost satisfying some general assumptions (A0)–(A5). We construct continuous strong deformation retracts X ↝ Z j X\leadsto Z_{j} whenever a condition called Uniform Halfspace (UHS) condition is satisfied along appropriate subsets.

Cost Assumptions

The topology of the locus-of-discontinuities and the singularity

-optimal semicouplings is determined by the geometry of the cost

c
-concave potentials. The cost

\ldots

(A0)

The cost

is continuous throughout

and uniformly bounded from below, e.g.

. Moreover we assume the sublevels

are compact subsets of

,
y\in Y

(A1)

The cost is twice continuously differentiable with respect to the source variable

(A2)

The function

(A3)

For every

.

(A4)

The cost satisfies (Twist) condition with respect to the source variable throughout

. So for every

(A5)

For every

is continuously differentiable; and for every

Consequences of Assumptions (A0), (A1),

3.
Optimal Semicouplings and Monge-Kantorovich Duality

Now we briefly introduce the optimal semicoupling program and Kantorovich’s dual max program.

Definition 1

(Semicoupling)
.

A
semicoupling
between source
(X,\sigma)
and target
(Y,\tau)
is a Borel-Radon measure
\pi
on the product space
X\times Y
with
proj_{Y}\#\pi=\tau
and
proj_{X}\#\pi\leq\sigma
.

The inequality
proj_{X}\#\pi\leq\sigma
holds if for every Borel subset
O
, the numerical inequality
(proj_{X}\#\pi)[O]\leq\sigma[O]
is satisfied. We remark that
\pi
is a coupling between
\sigma
and
\tau
when
proj_{X}\#\pi=\sigma
.

Definition 2

(
c
-transforms)
.

If
\psi:Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is any function on the target
Y
, then the
c
-Legendre transform
\psi^{c}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by

\psi^{c}(x):=\sup_{y\in Y}[\psi(y)-c(x,y)],

for
x\in X
.

If
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is any function on the source
X
, then the
c
-Legendre transform
\phi^{c}:Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is defined by the rule

\phi^{c}(y)=\inf_{x\in X}[c(x,y)+\phi(x)],

for
y\in Y
.

Definition 3

(
c
-concavity)
.

A function
\psi:Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
c
-concave if
\psi^{cc}=(\psi^{c})^{c}
coincides pointwise with
\psi
. Equivalently
\psi
is
c
-concave if there exists a lower semicontinuous function
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
\phi^{c}=\psi
pointwise.

The above definitions imply
\psi,\psi^{c}
satisfy the pointwise inequality

(5)

-\psi^{c}(x)+\psi(y)\leq c(x,y)

for all
x\in X
,
y\in Y
. The inequality (
5
) and especially the case of equality is very important for our applications.

Definition 4

(
c
-subdifferential)
.

Let
\psi:Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be
c
-concave potential
\psi^{cc}=\psi
. Select
y_{0}\in Y
where
\psi(y_{0})
is finite-valued. The subdifferential
\partial^{c}\psi(y_{0})\subset X
consists of those points
x^{\prime}\in X
such that

-\psi^{c}(x^{\prime})+\psi(y_{0})=c(x^{\prime},y_{0}).

Or equivalently such that for all
y\in Y
,

\psi(y)-c(x^{\prime},y)\leq\psi(y_{0})-c(x^{\prime},y_{0}).

The main technical result we need is Monge-Kantorovich duality. Let
SC(\sigma,\tau)\subset\mathscr{M}_{\geq 0}(X\times Y)
denote the set of all semicoupling measures
\pi
between source
\sigma
and target
\tau
.

[
Vil09
, Theorem 5.10, pp.57]
.

Monge-Kantorovich Duality
.

Let
c:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a cost satisfying Assumptions (A0)–(A5). Let
\sigma,\tau
be source and target measures on
X
,
Y
, respectively. Assume
\sigma,\tau
are absolutely continuous with respect to Hausdorff measures
\mathscr{H}_{X}
,
\mathscr{H}_{Y}

Then there exists unique closed domain

-optimal semicoupling

Moreover there exists

-concave potentials

-convex potentials

(6)

\max_{\psi~c\text{-concave}}[\int_{A}-\psi^{c}(x)d\sigma(x)+\int_{Y}\psi(y)d\tau(y)]=\min_{\pi\in SC(\sigma,\tau)}[\int_{X\times Y}c(x,y)d\pi(x,y)]

Moreover the

-optimal semicoupling

-subdifferentials
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)

Assumptions (A0),

-subdifferentials. The first useful property is that

-subdifferentials are nonempty wherever the potentials

4.
(Twist) Condition

Thus far we have established the existence of optimal semicouplings and uniqueness of active domains. Now we describe the (Twist) hypothesis and the uniqueness of optimal couplings when the source measure
\sigma
is absolutely continuous with respect to the reference source measure
\mathscr{H}_{X}^{d}
in

The next definition elaborates Assumption (A4) from §
2
.

Definition 5

(
(Twist)
)
.

Let

be cost function satisfying Assumptions (A0)–(A1). Then

satisfies (Twist) condition if for every

defines an injective mapping
\nabla_{x}c(x^{\prime},\cdot):dom(c_{x^{\prime}})\to T_{x^{\prime}}X

Observe that (Twist) condition is equivalent to the function

admitting no critical points on
X
, whenever
y_{0},y_{1}\in Y
are distinct. If
X
is closed manifold, then

certainly has critical points which violates (Twist). Our settings assume

is a manifold-with-corners with nontrivial boundary
\partial X\neq\emptyset
. The (Twist) condition requires

admit no critical points on the interior of

, and all maxima/minima exist on the boundary. For instance, the repulsion costs constructed in
[

have the property that
c_{\Delta}(x;y_{0},y_{1})
converges to
-\infty
when

, and converges to
+\infty
when
x\to y_{0}
, and all other level sets
c_{\Delta}(-;y_{0},y_{1})^{-1}(s)\subset X
are topologically connected and separating

into two components, for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

Proposition 1
.

Let
c:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be a cost satisfying Assumptions (A0)–(A5). Let
\psi:Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be a
c
-concave potential. Then
\psi
is locally Lipschitz on its domain
\psi:dom(\psi)\to\mathbb{R}
.

Recall the definition of semiconvexity
[
Vil09
, Definition 10.10, pp.228]
:

Definition 6

(Semiconvexity)

A function
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is semiconvex on an open subset

\phi(\gamma(t))\leq(1-t)\phi(\gamma(0))+t\phi(\gamma(1))+t(1-t)Cdist(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))^{2}

.
The function is locally semiconvex if
\phi
is semiconvex at every

, with respect to a modulus

depending uniformly on
\gamma(0)
,
\gamma(1)
varying in compact subsets

of

.

Lemma 2
.

Let
c:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be cost satisfying Assumptions (A0)–(A2). Then every

-convex potential
\psi^{c}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is
\mathscr{H}_{X}
-almost everywhere locally-Lipschitz on its domain

. Furthermore every

-convex potential is locally-semiconvex on

.

Proof.

Omitted.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let

be cost satisfying Assumptions (A0)–(A2). Then
c
-convex potentials
\psi^{c}
are
\mathscr{H}_{X}
-almost everywhere differentiable on

-measure subset of

According to Lemma

-convex potentials

are locally Lipschitz on their domains

5.
Statement of Main Results

The unique

Our first Theorem

of

For given

11.
Local Topology and Local Dimensions of

The present section examines the local differential topology and dimensions of Kantorovich’s contravariant functor

, where the cost

satisfies Assumptions (A0)–(A5). If

is a
c
-concave potential, then we first seek a description of the differential topology of the cells

The main results are

and

is essentially a local adaptation of the recent work of
[

]
, and

is a corollary to a theorem of Alberti

Let the reader recall the definitions of
c
-concavity
3
and
c
-subdifferentials

-concavity
\psi^{cc}=\psi
represents a pointwise inequality on

, namely

-\psi^{c}(x)+\psi(y)\leq c(x,y)\text{~~for all~}(x,y)\in X\times Y.

The case of equality

is most important, and occurs if and only if

, and if and only if

. We recall that

-optimal semicouplings

are supported on the graphs of

-subdifferentials, hence the equality

holds

is a

-concave maximizer to Kantorovich’s dual program.

Recall that if

is a convex lower semicontinuous function on some

-dimensional vector space, then the following are equivalent
[
ET99
, I.5.3, pp.23]
:

-
\phi
is continuous and finite at

\partial\phi(x)
a singleton;

-
\phi
is differentiable at
x
with
\partial\phi(x)=\{D\phi(x)\}
.

Thus
\phi
is continuous throughout the interior of
dom(\phi)
. So if
\phi=\psi^{c}
is a
c
-convex potential on
X
, then
\partial^{c}\phi(x)
is multivalued if and only if
\phi
is non-differentiable at
x
.

Returning to
c
-convexity: in case
\partial^{c}\phi(x_{0})
is a singleton, say
\{y_{0}\}
, then
-\psi^{c}(x_{0})+\psi(y)<c(x_{0},y)
for every

. The set of

is then characterized as the subset where

\psi(y_{0})\geq c(x,y_{0})+\phi(x)

according to (

), i.e. as the superlevel set of
x\mapsto c(x,y_{0})+\phi(x)
and Assumptions (A0)–(A3) imply these superlevel sets are closed. More concretely, we find
Z(\{y_{0}\})
is characterized by the equations

(24)

Z(\{y_{0}\}=\{x\in X|~\psi(y_{0})=c(x,y_{0})+\phi(x)\}.

Equivalently, we find
x\in\partial^{c}\psi(y_{0})
if and only if

(25)

y_{0}\in argmax[\{\psi(y)-c(x,y)+c(x,y_{0})~|~y\in Y\}].

If
x
is such that

is not a singleton, say including two distinct points

, then

satisfies the equations

\psi^{c}(x)+c(x,y_{0})=\psi(y_{0}),~~~\psi^{c}(x)+c(x,y_{1})=\psi(y_{1}).

Subtracting these two equations, we can eliminate
\phi(x)
and obtain the equation

0=\psi(y_{1})-\psi(y_{0})+c_{\Delta}(x,y_{0},y_{1}),

where we abbreviate

for the two-pointed cross difference. If
y_{0}\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)
, and

, then

0<\psi(y)-\psi(y_{0})+c_{\Delta}(x,y_{0},y).

Definition 12
.

Let
\phi
be a
c
-convex potential on

,
\phi^{cc}=\phi
. We say

is a singular point if

is not differentiable at

.

If

is not a singleton and the cell

is described by the system of equations

(26)

Z^{\prime}(x_{0})=\{x\in X~|~0=\psi(y_{0})-\psi(y_{1})+c_{\Delta}(x;y_{1},y_{0}),~y_{1}\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x_{0}),y_{1}\neq y_{0}\}

according to (
25

.

From the equations (
26

Lemma 8
.

Under Assumptions (A0)–(A3), the cell
Z^{\prime}(x)=Z(\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x))
is a closed locally DC-subvariety in
X
for every singular point

.

Proof.

The cell

is the intersection of sets of the form
\partial^{c}\psi(y)
, which are closed
1
. Assumption (A1) implies

. Therefore every

and a constant

is locally semiconcave function on

is locally-DC subvariety of

.
∎

To illustrate, consider the Euclidean quadratic cost

on

. The cross-difference

is an affine function of

Therefore the cells

are locally affine subsets of

for every singular point

.

We caution the reader that Lemma

does not the specify the dimension of

– the dimension of these cells is addressed below
3
. Again

is viewed as a local cell on the active domain

. Our next step is to describe the space of directions

, compare
[

, Def.10.4.6, pp.257]
. The definition of the vector field

involves the orthogonal projection

of

onto the space of directions.

Lemma 9
.

Let
x\in X
be a singular point where

is not a singleton. Then the space of directions

is a subset of the orthogonal complement

orthog[\{\nabla_{x}c_{\Delta}(x;y_{0},y_{1})~|~y_{1},y_{0}\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)\}]

in
T_{x}X.

Proof.

Let
\phi=\psi^{c}
. For
y_{0},y_{1}\in Y
, we abbreviate
A(x,y_{0},y_{1})=c_{\Delta}(x,y,y_{0})-\psi(y)+\psi(y_{0})
. If

is a singular point, then

is characterized by the vanishing

for

. A first-order deformation

at

and tangent to

must preserve the system of equations

. But this only if

satisfies the homogeneous linear equations

for every

.
∎

Lemma

indicates the

, namely the dimension of the orthogonal complement
\{\nabla_{x}c_{\Delta}(x,y,y^{\prime})~|~y,y^{\prime}\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)\}
.
A standard application of Clarke’s Nonsmooth Implicit Function theorem confirms this dimension estimate. Some preliminary notation is convenient. Recall

is a Riemannian manifold-with-corners, equipped with Riemannian exponential mapping

. If

is complete Cartan-Hadamard manifold, then

is diffeomorphism between

and the universal covering space

. On general Riemannian manifold-with-corners

the exponential map is a local diffeomorphism between sufficiently small open neighborhoods

of
x_{0}\in X
with open balls
U^{\prime}
in the tangent space
T_{x_{0}}X
. So we have
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms between local neighborhoods of
x_{0}\in X
with neighborhoods of
0
in euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n=\dim(X)=\dim(T_{x_{0}}X)
. Thus for every
x_{0}\in X
there exists a local diffeomorphism splitting
B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})
as a product
B_{\epsilon}(x_{0}^{\prime})\times B_{\epsilon}(x_{0}^{\prime\prime})
, viewed as subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n-j}\times R^{j}
with
x_{0}=(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0}^{\prime\prime})
for

.

Recall
c
satisfies Assumptions (A0)–(A4). Let
\psi:Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be a
c
-concave potential, with
\phi=\psi^{c}
. Let

be the active domain of the semicoupling defined by

, and let

.

Proposition 3
.

Under the above hypotheses, let
x_{0}
be a singular point. Suppose the gradients
\{\nabla_{x}c_{\Delta}(x;y_{0},y_{1})~|~y_{0},y_{1}\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x_{0})\}
span a

-dimensional subspace of

for every

. Then

is a codimension-

local DC-subvariety of the active domain

.

Proof.

Fix some
y_{0}\in\partial\phi(x_{0})
. Abbreviate
A(x,y):=c(x,y)-c(x,y_{0})
. Consider the mapping
G:B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

G(x)=(A(x,y_{1}),A(x,y_{2}),\ldots A(x,y_{j}))

for

. Assume

\{\nabla_{x}A(x_{0},y_{i},y_{0})\}_{1\leq i\leq j}

is a linearly independent subset of

. The map

is local DC-function according to Lemma

.

We use exponential mapping to obtain local

-diffeomorphism between an open neighborhood of

in

. Next we apply the

-implicit function theorem as stated in
[

, and conclude there exists

and a biLipschitz DC-mapping

from

to

such that, for all

we have

if and only if

.

The basic subdifferential inequalities (

) imply

if and only if

. Now because

can be covered by countably many sufficiently small open balls, we conclude that

is a local DC-subvariety with Hausdorff dimension
\dim_{\mathscr{H}}Z^{\prime}(x_{0})

=n-j
.
∎

The above

is a type of “constant-rank” theorem for Lipschitz maps, c.f.
[

]
.

N.B. The description of

is symmetric with respect to

. There is further symmetry from the additive relations between cross-costs

This implies the obvious estimate

under general conditions.

Recently

12.
The Descending Filtration
\{Z_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}

Following an idea of Prof. D. Bar-Natan
[
Bar02
]
we “skewer the cube”

(more accurately the support

) according to local codimensions and obtain a descending filtration. The previous section described various cells

which decompose the activated source
A
of optimal semicouplings. Assembling these cells into the Kantorovich functor

leads to a useful filtration of the source

.

Definition 13
.

For integers
j=0,1,2,\ldots
, let

Z_{j+1}:=\{x\in X|~~\dim[span\{\nabla_{x}c_{\Delta}(x;y,y^{\prime})~|~y,y^{\prime}\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)\}]\geq j\}

where
Z^{\prime}(x)=Z(\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x))
for a
c
-concave potential
\psi^{cc}=\psi
on target space
Y
.

According to the definition,
Z_{j}
is supported on the subcategory
spt(Z)
of
2^{Y}
for every
j\geq 1
. The activated support
A
coincides with the support of
Z_{1}
. The cells
Z(Y_{I})
are closed (
1
). If the support
spt(Z)
of the functor is finite, then the unions
Z_{j+1}
are closed. However if
spt(Z)
is infinite, then the union
Z_{2}
,
Z_{3}
,
\ldots
is possibly not closed and depends on the cost
c
. For example, the Euclidean quadratic cost
c=d^{2}/2
typically has

not closed when the target
Y
has
\dim(Y)>0
. This motivates our study of repulsion costs in
[
Mar
, Ch.4]
.

Now we replace the
c
-subdifferentials with an important “localized” version using (Twist) condition (A4). The
c
-subdifferential
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}
of a
c
-convex function
\psi^{c}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is non-local subset of
Y
. The subsets
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}\subset Y
depends on global data, namely the values of
c(x,y)
for all
y\in dom(c_{x})
. Hence
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x_{0})
depends on the values of
\psi(y)
and
\psi^{c}(x)
for every
y\in Y,x\in X
and not simply the local behaviour of
\psi^{c}
near
x_{0}
. It is useful to introduce a local subdifferential, namely the so-called “subgradients” of a function
\psi^{c}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

Definition 14

(Local Subgradients
\partial_{\bullet}\phi
)
.

Let
U
be open set in
X
, sufficiently small such
U
is
C^{1}
-diffeomorphic to an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
n=\dim(X)
. Let
\phi:U\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. Then
\phi
is subdifferentiable at
x
with subgradient
v^{*}\in T_{x}^{*}X
if

\phi(z)\geq\phi(x)+v^{*}(z-x)+o(|z-x|)\text{~for all~}z\text{~near~}x.

Let
\partial_{\bullet}\phi(x)\subset T_{x}^{*}X
denote the set of all subgradients to
\phi
at
x
. Here

is the “little-oh” notation.

Evidently the subgradient
\partial_{\bullet}\phi(x)
is local, and depending on the values of
\phi
near
x
. Moreover
\partial_{\bullet}\phi(x)
is a closed convex subset of
T_{x}^{*}X
for every
x\in dom(\phi)
.

The Assumptions (A0)–(A3) imply
\psi^{c}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is locally semiconvex over its domain
dom(\psi^{c})\subset X
(Lemma
2
). In otherwords, every
x\in dom(\psi^{c})
admits an open neighborhood
U
of
x
and a constant
C\geq 0
such that
D^{2}\psi^{c}\geq-CId
throughout
U
, where
D^{2}\psi^{c}=D^{2}_{xx}\psi^{c}
is the distributional Hessian in the source variable
x
(
[
Vil09
, Theorem 14.1, pp.363]
). So given a
c
-convex potential
\psi^{c}
on
X
, for every
x_{0}\in X
there exists open neighborhood
U
and
C\geq 0
such that
\psi^{c}|_{U_{x}}+C||x||^{2}/2
is strictly convex throughout
U
. This implies the local subdifferential and subgradients
\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}(x)
coincide with the convex-analytic subdifferential of the local function
\psi^{c}|_{U}+C||x||^{2}/2
, when restricted to the sufficiently small neighborhood
x
in
X
.

Having introduced the local subdifferential, there is important comparison between
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x_{0})\subset Y
and
\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}(x_{0})
, assuming (Twist) condition Assumption (A4). This relation is the inclusion

(27)

\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x_{0})\subset\{y\in Y|-\nabla_{x}c(x_{0},y)\in\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}(x_{0})\}.

Observe
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x_{0})
is nonempty whenever
x_{0}\in dom(\psi^{c})
. We abuse notation and denote
\nabla_{x}c(x,y)
for the canonical covector in
T_{x}^{*}X
, with the tangent vector in
T_{x}X
using the ambient Riemannian structure. The inclusion (
27
) allows us to replace the global
c
-subdifferential
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}
with the local convex set of subgradients
\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}
. The inclusion is generally strict. However it produces basic upper bounds on the Hausdorff dimension of
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}
. We quote the following theorem of G. Alberti:

Theorem 3

(
[
Alb94
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be proper lowersemicontinuous convex function. For every
0<k<n
, let
S^{k}(f)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the subset defined by

S^{k}(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(\partial_{\bullet}(f))\geq k\}.

Then
S^{k}(f)
can be covered by countably many
(n-k)
-dimensional DC-manifolds.

In otherwords
S^{k}(f)
is countably
(n-k)
-rectifiable and has Hausdorff dimension
\leq(n-k)
. If
f
is
+\infty
-valued on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then Alberti’s method shows
S^{k}(f)
can be covered by countably many
(n^{\prime}-k)
-dimensional manifolds where
n^{\prime}=\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(dom(f))
. The domain
dom(f)
is a closed convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
having well-defined Hausdorff dimension.

Proposition 4
.

Let
c:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be cost satisfying Assumptions (A0)–(A4). Let
\psi:Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be
c
-concave potential
\psi^{cc}=\psi
with singularity functor
Z:2^{Y}\to 2^{X}
. Define
n^{\prime}:=\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(dom(\psi^{c}))
. Then for every integer
j\geq 1
, the subvariety
Z_{j}
has Hausdorff dimension

\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(Z_{j})\leq n^{\prime}-j+1.

Proof.

Consider the inclusion (
27
). For given
x_{0}
, let
U
be the open neighborhood and
C>0
such that
\psi^{c}|_{U}+C||x||^{2}/2
is strictly convex throughout
U
. For
x\in U
the
c
-subdifferentials
\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)
are contained in the closed convex local subdifferentials
\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}|U(x)
. Now we have equality

(28)

\partial_{\bullet}(\psi^{c}|_{U}(x)+C|x|^{2}/2)=\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}|_{U}(x)+C\langle-,x\rangle.

So the local subdifferential
\partial_{\bullet}(\psi^{c}_{U}+C|x|^{2}/2)
in
T_{x}^{*}X
is an affine translate of
\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}|_{U}
by the linear functional
C\langle-,x\rangle
.

Next we apply Alberti’s theorem to the localized convex function
\psi^{c}|_{U}+C|x|^{2}/2
. Thus
S^{k}(\psi^{c}|_{U}+C|x|^{2}/2)
and
S^{k}(\psi^{c}|_{U})
can be covered by countably many
(n^{\prime}-k)
-dimensional manifolds where
n^{\prime}:=\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(dom\psi^{c}|_{U})
.

Finally we relate
\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}|_{U}(x))
to the Hausdorff dimension of
Z^{\prime}(x)=Z(\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x))

=\cap_{y\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)}\partial^{c}\psi(y)
. From the definition of subgradients, we have

conv(\{\nabla_{x}c(x,y)|y\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)\})\subset\partial_{\bullet}\psi^{c}(x).

Moreover the closed convex hull
conv(\{\nabla_{x}c(x,y)|y\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)\})
has dimension

j=\dim(span\{\nabla_{x}c_{\Delta}(x,y,y_{0})~|~y\in\partial^{c}\psi^{c}(x)\}).

For every
x\in Z_{j}
we conclude
Z_{j}\cap U\subset S^{j}(\psi^{c}|_{U}+C|x|^{2}/2),
which according to Alberti’s theorem
3
yields the upper bound
\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(Z_{j}\cap U)\leq n^{\prime}-j.
To conclude, we observe that
Z_{j}
can be covered by countably many open neighborhoods
U
of points
x\in Z_{j}
.

∎

We supplement
4
with the following application of Clarke’s Implicit Function Theorem, which gives a criterion for the singularities

to be

subsets of

.

Proposition 5
.

Let
c:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be a cost satisfying Assumptions (A0)–(A4). Let
\phi=\psi^{c}

Brief Review of Singularity in the Literature

The term “singularity” is evidently overburdened having various interpretations within the literature. We use the term “singularity” as arising in convex geometry: if
\phi
is a lower semicontinuous convex function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then the “singularity” of
\phi
corresponds to the locus of non-differentiability on
dom(\phi)
, i.e. the set of points
x\in dom(\phi)
where
\partial\phi(x)\subset(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{*}
is not single-valued. From the viewpoint of optimal transport,
“singularity” then refers to the locus-of-discontinuity of
c
-optimal semicouplings.

The Kantorovich singularity (Def.
10
) is both categorical and topological, and provides an alternative view to the so-called “regularity theory” of optimal transportation. Regularity in optimal transport usually refers to the Monge map
y=T(x)
defined by the (Twist) condition, and

T(x^{\prime}):=\nabla_{x}c(x^{\prime},-)^{-1}(-\nabla_{x}\psi^{c}(x^{\prime})).

There is large volume of research concerning the
C^{1,\alpha}
or
C^{2}
,
C^{\infty}
regularity of
T
under various hypotheses on
c,\sigma,\tau
. C.f.
[
Vil09
, Ch.12]
. This article however is strictly interested in the
discontinuities
of
T
. So we pass silently over questions of the type “How regular is the map
T
away from the singularities?”. This article and
[
Mar
]
rather studies the continuity properties of the locus-of-discontinuity of
T
.

Several results concerning singularities of optimal transports have been attained in the literature. Alberti’s paper
[
Alb94
]
is basic starting point. Figalli
[
Fig10
]
studies the singularities of optimal transports between two probability measures supported on bounded open domains in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with respect to the quadratic Euclidean cost
c(x,y)=||x-y||^{2}/2
(equivalently
c=-\langle x,y\rangle
). The main result of
[
Fig10
, §3.2]
is that the singularity (
Z_{2}
in our notation) has topological closure
\overline{Z_{2}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with zero two-dimensional Hausdorff measure,
\mathscr{H}^{2}(\overline{Z_{2}})=0.
Here we remark that the closure
\overline{Z_{2}}
is not easily topologized from the singularity
Z_{2}
. In otherwords the closure is not entirely explicit. Figalli’s work was extended in
[
FK10
]
, where the evaluation
\mathscr{H}^{n}(\overline{Z_{2}})=0
was established for singularities of optimal couplings under the hypothesis that probability measures are supported on bounded open domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and again with respect to Euclidean quadratic cost. In
[
PF
]
a similar result is established with respect to more general costs on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying basic nondegeneracy conditions, namely:

- the cost
c\in C^{2}(X\times Y)
with
||c||_{C^{2}}<+\infty
,

- the rule
x\mapsto\nabla_{y}c(x,y)
is injective map for every
y
,

- the rule
y\mapsto\nabla_{x}c(x,y)
is injective map for every
x
, and

-
det(D_{xy}^{2}c)\neq 0
for all
(x,y)
.

The investigations of Figalli suggest the following: if the source and target measures
\sigma,\tau
are absolutely continuous to
\mathscr{H}_{X},\mathscr{H}_{Y}
, and the cost
c
satisfies (A0)–(A4), then the singularity
Z_{2}
has Hausdorff dimension satisfying

\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(Z_{2})\leq\dim(Y).

We motivate this dimension formula in
4
. The recent work
[
KM18
]
confirms this estimate under some particular conditions, namely Euclidean quadratic cost, convex source, and convexity and affine-independence of the disjoint target components.

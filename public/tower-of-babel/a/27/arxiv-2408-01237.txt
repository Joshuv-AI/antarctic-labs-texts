Covariance identities and Variance bounds for Infinitely Divisible Random Variables and their Applications
Kalyan Barman, Neelesh S Upadhye, Palaniappan Vellaisamy

Abstract
. In this article, we establish a general covariance identity for infinitely divisible distributions ( I ​ D ​ D IDD ). Using this result, we derive Cacoullos type variance bounds for the I ​ D ​ D IDD . Applications to some important distributions are discussed, in addition to the computation of variance bounds for certain posterior distributions. As another application, we derive the Stein-type identity for the I ​ D ​ D IDD , which involves the Lévy measure. This result in turn is used to derive the Stein-type identity for the C ​ G ​ M ​ Y CGMY distributions and the variance-gamma distributions ( V ​ G ​ D VGD ). This approach, especially for the V ​ G ​ D VGD is new and simpler, compared to the ones available in the literature. Finally, as another nontrivial application, we apply the covariance identity in deriving known and some new formulas for the weighted premium calculation principles ( W ​ P ​ C ​ P WPCP ) and Gini coefficient for the I ​ D ​ D IDD .

1.
Introduction

In 1972, Charles Stein, while attempting a new proof of central limit theorem, invented a new identity for normal distribution and used it to study the normal approximation problems for the sums of dependent random variables (rvs) (see, Stein
[
28
]
). The invention of similar identities for other probability distributions is well studied in the literature, with applications to limit theorems
[
3
,
6
,
23
,
31
]
, runs
[
32
,
33
]
, estimation theory
[
4
,
34
]
, functional inequalities
[
2
]
, insurance
[
25
]
and various other fields.

In particular, Stein
[
28
]
proved that for a normal random variable (rv)
X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
,

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(X,g(X))=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}[g^{\prime}(X)],

(1.1)

where
g
is an absolutely continuous function with
\mathbb{E}[g^{\prime}(X)]<\infty
.

Goldstein and Reinert
[
16
]
generalized the Stein identity in (
1.1
) as follows. Let
X
be a real-valued rv with mean zero and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Then, for any differentiable function
g
with
\mathbb{E}(Xg(X))<\infty
, there exists a rv
X^{*}
having the unimodal probability density function (
pdf
)

\displaystyle f_{X^{*}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left(X\mathbf{I}(X>x)\right)

such that

\displaystyle Cov(X,g(X))=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X^{*})\right).

(1.2)

We note that, from
[
16
, Lemma 2.1(ii)]
,
X^{*}
is supported on the closed convex hull of the support of
X
. Also, they applied the covariance identity (
1.2
) to obtain the rate of convergence in the central limit theorem. Further, they presented a nice application to dependent samples.

Later, Papadatos and Papathanasiou
[
24
]
established a covariance identity, similar to (
1.2
), for an absolutely continuous rv
X
given by

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(X,g(X))=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}[g^{\prime}(X^{*})],

(1.3)

where the rv
X^{*}
has the unimodal density

\displaystyle f_{X^{*}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}[t-\mathbb{E}(X)]f_{X}(t)dt.

(1.4)

From (
1.3
), it is clear that, if
X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
, then
X^{*}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
. Observe that the Stein-type covariance identity (
1.3
) depends on the
pdf
of
X
. The derivation of the identity becomes difficult, whenever
f_{X}(x)
is not in the closed form.

Let
X
follow the infinitely divisible distribution (
IDD
), denoted by
IDD(\mu,\sigma^{2},\nu)
, with parameters
\mu,\sigma^{2}
and the associated Lévy measure
\nu
(see (
2.1
)). It is known that the
pdf
of some distributions belonging to
IDD
family may not be available in a closed form. Therefore, the derivation of Stein-type covariance identity is not possible for all the distributions belonging to
IDD
family using the approach given in
[
24
]
. In 1998, Houdré
et al.

[
19
]
established a covariance representation for
IDD
via the generator approach. They applied it to study the association problems for
IDD
and also correlation inequalities. Recently, Arras and Houdré
[
1
]
obtained a Stein-type covariance identity, like (
1.1
) for
IDD
with first finite moment using covariance representation given in
[
19
, Proposition 2]
. More recently, Upadhye and Barman
[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
established a Stein-type identity for
IDD
via the characteristic function (cf) approach.

In this article, we first obtain an extended Stein-type covariance identity in Theorem
3.1
for the
IDD
. The identity for the positive
IDD
is illustrated for the first time, in terms of cumulants, to the best of our knowledge. Although covariance identities for
IDD
are well studied (see,
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
), the covariance identity in Theorem
3.1
is useful. Using this result, we establish Cacoullos type variance bounds which are used to compute variance bounds of the parameters of the posterior distributions. Also, we obtain Stein identity for the variance-gamma distributions (
VGD
) using covariance identity and the associated Lévy measure. Our approach is new and much simpler. We finally apply the covariance identity in deriving some new formulas of weighted premium calculation principles (
WPCP
), that is, for
\mathbb{E}[Xw(X)]\big/\mathbb{E}[w(X)]
, in terms of Lévy measure. Also, the Gini coefficient for
IDD
is derived. Observe that the density of the
IDD
is not usually available in explicit form, and in such case the approach based on the Lévy measure is quite useful. Several well chosen examples illustrate our methodology, giving simpler derivation of the known results as well as some new formulas.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the Lévy-Khintchine representation of
IDD(\mu,\sigma^{2},\nu)
. Note that the class of
IDD(\mu,\sigma^{2},\nu)
is quite large and it includes many subclasses of distributions, such as compound Poisson distributions (
CPD
) and generalized tempered stable distributions (
GTSD
). Also, we discuss rather in detail some special cases of the
GTSD
, which include variance-gamma distributions (
VGD
), bilateral-gamma distributions (
BGD
) and Carr, Geman, Madan and Yor (
CGMY
) distributions. In Section
3
, we derive an interesting covariance identity for
IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
(see Theorem
3.1
), which depends on the Lévy measure
\nu
. Using this result, we obtain new covariance identities for
GTSD
and
VGD
. In Section
4
, using our covariance identity, given in Section
3
, we derive Cacoullos-type variance bounds for
g(X)
, where
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
. Applications to gamma, Laplace and two-sided exponential distributions are considered. Also, as another application to the Bayesian inference, we find variance bounds for the posterior distributions. In Section
5
, we consider two important applications of our results. The first one is concerned with obtaining Stein identities via the covariance identity. We consider the
VGD
and derive the Stein identity using the covariance identity and the Lévy measure of the
VGD
. It can be seen that our approach involves much simpler calculations, compared to the one used in
[
17
]
where the density approach is used, and it involves lengthy calculations and modified Bessel functions. Our second application is the calculation of
WPCP
. First, we obtain a result for the
WPCP
, when
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
. Using this result, we compute
WPCP
for gamma,
CPD
,
BGD
,
VGD
, inverse Gaussian and
CGMY
distributions. Finally, we compute also the Gini coefficient for
IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
and its applications to
VGD
and
CGMY
distributions are discussed.

Notations and Preliminary Results

Let
\textbf{I}_{A}(.)
denote the indicator function of the set
A
. Let
X
be an infinitely divisible rv. Then its Lévy-Khintchine representation of cf (see
[
26
]

\phi_{X}(t)=\exp\left(it\mu_{0}-\frac{\sigma^{2}t^{2}}{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{itu}-1-itu\mathbf{I}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}(u))\nu(du)\right),~~t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where
\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma\geq 0
and
\nu
, the Lévy measure on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1\wedge u^{2})\nu(du)<\infty.
Observe that if
\int_{\{|u|>1\}}u\nu(du)<\infty,
then (
2.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\phi_{X}(t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(it\mu-\frac{\sigma^{2}t^{2}}{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{itu}-1-itu\right)\nu(du)\right),~~t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.2)

where
\mu=\mu_{0}+\int_{\{|u|>1\}}u\nu(du)
. For a rv
X
with cf (
2.2
), we write
X\sim IDD(\mu,\sigma^{2},\nu)
.

If
X\sim IDD(\mu,\sigma^{2},\nu)
, then its
n
-th cumulant is

\displaystyle C_{n}(X):=(-i)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\log\phi_{X}(t)\bigg|_{t=0},~n\geq 1.

(2.3)

Let
X\sim IDD(\mu,\sigma^{2},\nu)
have moments of arbitrary order. Then

\displaystyle C_{1}(X)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(X)=\mu,

(2.4)

\displaystyle C_{2}(X)

\displaystyle=Var(X)=\sigma^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\nu(du),\text{ and }

(2.5)

\displaystyle C_{k}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{k}\nu(du),~k\geq 3.

(2.6)

The class of
IDD
is quite large, and see
[
26
]
for more properties. Next, we discuss some important subclasses of
IDD
, which are useful in later sections.

2.1.
Compound Poisson distributions

A rv
X
is said to have

if its cf (see
[
26
]
) is given by

\phi_{cp}(t)=\exp\left(\nu(\mathbb{R})\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{itu}-1)\nu_{0}(du)\right),~~t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.7)

\overset{d}{=}

3.
A generalized Stein-type covariance identity

In this section, we establish a covariance identity for infinitely divisible rvs. Before stating our result, let us define, for an integer
k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{+}(u)=\int_{u}^{\infty}y^{k}\nu(dy),~u>0,\text{ and }\eta_{k}^{-}(u)=-\int_{-\infty}^{u}y^{k}\nu(dy),~u<0,

(3.1)

where
\nu
is the Lévy measure of the
IDD
(see (
2.1
)). Let

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(u):=\eta_{k}^{+}(u)\mathbf{I}_{(0,\infty)}(u)+\eta_{k}^{-}(u)\mathbf{I}_{(-\infty,0)}(u),~u\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.2)

Let
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
with cf
\phi_{X}(t)
given in (
2.2
). Also let
(X_{s},Y_{s})
be a infinitely divisible random vector with joint cf

\displaystyle\phi_{s}(t,z)=\phi_{X}^{1-s}(t)\phi_{X}^{1-s}(z)\phi_{X}^{s}(t+z),

(3.3)

for all
t,z\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in[0,1]
. It can be seen that
\phi_{X_{s}}(t)=\phi_{Y_{s}}(t)=\phi_{X}(t)
, so that
X_{s}
and
Y_{s}
are identically, but not independently, distributed.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X\sim\text{IDD}(\mu,0,\nu)
which has
\mathbb{E}(X^{n})<\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and the random vector
(X_{s},Y_{s})
have the cf given in (
3.3
). Then

\text{Cov}(X^{n},g(X))=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\eta_{n-k}(v)dv\right)ds,

(3.4)

where
g
is an absolutely continuous function with
\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\eta_{n-k}(v)dv\right)<\infty
, for
0\leq k\leq(n-1)
.

Proof.

Recall first (see (
2.2
)) that the cf of
X
is

\displaystyle\phi_{X}(t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(it\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{itu}-1-itu\right)\nu(du)\right),~~t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.5)

To prove our result, we use the covariance representation obtained in
[
19
, Proposition 2]
for infinitely divisible rvs. Let
\mathcal{G}^{1}
be the class of real-valued differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}
such that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are bounded on any bounded interval, the set of discontinuity points of
f^{\prime}
has zero probability, and with

\mathbb{E}\bigg(f^{2}(X)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(f(X+u)-f(X))^{2}\nu(du)\bigg)<\infty.

Then we have for
f,g\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
,

\displaystyle Cov(f(X),g(X))=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(f(Y_{s}+u)-f(Y_{s})\bigg)\bigg(g(X_{s}+u)-g(X_{s})\bigg)\nu(du)ds,

(3.6)

where
(X_{s},Y_{s})
has the cf given in (
3.3
). Let now
f(x)=x^{n}
in (
3.6
), then we get

\displaystyle Cov(X^{n},g(X))=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg((Y_{s}+u)^{n}-Y_{s}^{n}\bigg)\bigg(g(X_{s}+u)-g(X_{s})\bigg)\nu(du)\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}Y_{s}^{k}u^{n-k}\right)\bigg(g(X_{s}+u)-g(X_{s})\bigg)\nu(du)\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(g(X_{s}+u)-g(X_{s})\bigg)u^{n-k}\nu(du)\right)ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(g(X_{s}+u)-g(X_{s})\bigg)u^{n-k}\nu(du)\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{u}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)dv\right)u^{n-k}\nu(du)\right)ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\int_{u}^{0}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)dv\right)(-u^{n-k})\nu(du)\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\int_{v}^{\infty}u^{n-k}\nu(du)\bigg)dv\right)ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\int_{-\infty}^{v}(-u^{n-k})\nu(du)\bigg)dv\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\bigg(\eta_{n-k}^{+}(v)\mathbf{I}_{(0,\infty)}(v)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\eta_{n-k}^{-}(v)\mathbf{I}_{(-\infty,0)}(v)\bigg)dv\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\eta_{n-k}(v)dv\right)ds,

which proves the result.

\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right)<\infty.

Proof.

From (
3.4
) and (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle Cov(X,g(X))

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\eta_{1}(v)dv\right)ds

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X+v)\eta_{1}(v)dv\right)ds\text{ (since $X_{s}\overset{d}{=}X$)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X+v)\eta_{1}(v)dv\right)

\displaystyle=Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right),

which proves the result. ∎

Remark 3.3
.

(i) If
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}),
then Stein identity is

\mathbb{E}(Xg(X))=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}(X)),

where
g
is an absolutely continuous function with
\mathbb{E}[g^{\prime}(X)]<\infty
. However, in general if
X
is such that
\mathbb{E}(X)=0,
and
Var(X)=\sigma^{2}
, then
X^{*}
is said to have
X
-zero bias distribution if

\mathbb{E}(Xg(X))=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}(X^{*})),

for all absolutely continuous
``g"
.

(ii)Let
X^{*}=X+Y_{1}
. Then (
3.8
) becomes

Cov(X,g(X))=Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X^{*})\right),

and so we may say
X^{*}
has the
X
-non-zero bias distribution. Usually, the covariance identities are for
X
-zero bias distributions, see
[
11
]
.

Note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}^{+}(u)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{u}^{\infty}y^{k}\nu(dy)du,~u>0

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k}\bigg(\int_{0}^{y}du\bigg)\nu(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k+1}\nu(dy).

(3.9)

If
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
is non-negative, then the
k
-th cumulant of
X
is
C_{k}(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k}\nu(du),
for some integer
k\geq 1,
and in that case

\displaystyle f_{k}(y)=\frac{\eta_{k}^{+}(y)}{C_{k+1}(X)},~y\in(0,\infty),

(3.10)

is a density on
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,\infty)
.

We now have the following Corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X\sim\text{IDD}(\mu,0,\nu)
be non-negative which has
\mathbb{E}(X^{n})<\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
Y_{k}
(
k\geq 1
), independent of

, has the density as defined in (
3.10
). Also let the random vector
(X_{s},Y_{s})
have the cf given in (
3.3
). Then

\text{Cov}(X^{n},g(X))=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}C_{n-k+1}(X)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+Y_{n-k})\right)ds,

(3.11)

where
g
is such that
\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+Y_{n-k})\right)<\infty,
for

Proof.

Since

is non-negative, the support of
\nu
is in
(0,\infty)
. Also, from

the assumption
\mathbb{E}(X^{n})<\infty
,

, for all

\displaystyle Cov(X^{n},g(X))=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\eta_{n-k}^{+}(v)dv\right)ds

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}C_{n-k+1}(X)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}g^{\prime}(X_{s}+Y_{n-k})\right)ds,

which proves the result.
∎

4.
Variance bounds

In this section, we discuss the use of covariance identities to establish bounds on variance of function of infinitely divisible rv.

4.1.
Cacoullos type variance bounds

Deriving upper bounds on the variance of a function of a rv has a long and rich history, starting in 1981 from the work of Chernoff
[
9
]
. Over the years, upper and lower variance bounds have received much interest in the statistics literature; see, for instance
[
10
,
11
]
and the references therein. In the following result, we obtain two-side bounds (Cacoullos type; see
[
10
]
) on the variance of
g(X)
, where
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
. Our approach is new and exploits the identity (
3.8
), which is based on non-zero-biased distribution of
X
. Recall first that an upper bound for variance of function of an infinitely divisible rv due to Chen
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
is as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X\sim\text{IDD}(\mu,0,\nu)
. Then

\displaystyle Var(g(X))\leq\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(g(X+u)-g(X)\right)^{2}\nu(du),

(4.1)

where
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
\mathbb{E}\bigg(g^{2}(X)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(g(X+u)-g(X)\right)^{2}\nu(du)\bigg)<\infty.

Next, we establish a Cacoullos type variance bounds for an infinitely divisible rv.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
and
Y_{1}
have density defined in (
3.7
). Then

\displaystyle Var(X)\mathbb{E}^{2}\left[g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right]\leq Var(g(X))\leq Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left[g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right]^{2},

(4.2)

where
g
is an absolutely continuous function with
\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right)<\infty.

Proof.

From (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle(Var(X))^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}\left[g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right]=

\displaystyle(C_{2}(X))^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}\left[g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[Cov(X,g(X))\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left((X-\mathbb{E}X)(g(X)-\mathbb{E}g(X)\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Var(X)Var(g(X)),

(4.3)

by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. That is,

\displaystyle Var(g(X))\geq Var(X)\mathbb{E}^{2}\left[g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right],

(4.4)

where
Y_{1}
has the density given in (
3.7
).

Next, observe that

\displaystyle Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left[g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right]^{2}=

\displaystyle C_{2}(X)\mathbb{E}\left[g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{2}(X)\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[g^{\prime}(X+v)\right]^{2}f_{1}(v)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[g^{\prime}(X+v)\right]^{2}\eta_{1}(v)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(g^{\prime}(X+v)\right)^{2}\int_{v}^{\infty}u\nu(du)dv\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(g^{\prime}(X+v)\right)^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{v}(-u)\nu(du)dv\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\left(g^{\prime}(X+v)\right)^{2}dv\right)u\nu(du)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\int_{u}^{0}\left(g^{\prime}(X+v)\right)^{2}dv\right)(-u)\nu(du)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\left(g^{\prime}(X+v)\right)^{2}dv\right)u\nu(du)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{u}dv\int_{0}^{u}\left(g^{\prime}(X+v)\right)^{2}dv\right)\nu(du)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{u}g^{\prime}(X+v)dv\right)^{2}\nu(du)

(by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g(X+u)-g(X))^{2}\nu(du)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\geq

\displaystyle Var(g(X))\text{ (using \eqref{Hcorep1})}.

(4.5)

Hence combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), the desired conclusion follows. ∎

4.2.
Application to posterior distributions

Here, we demonstrate, using Cacoullos type bounds, through examples to compute variance bounds of the parameters of the posterior distributions. Recently Daly
et al.

[
11
, Example 2.3]
discussed variance bounds within a Bayesian context for the Pearson family by using Stein kernels. In the Bayesian methodology, the parameters are treated as random variables. Let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a random sample of size
n
, where the joint density of
\Theta
and
X_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n,
is
\pi(\theta,x)
. Assume the prior density of
\Theta
is
\pi(\theta).
Then we update from prior to posterior density as
\pi(\theta|x)=\kappa(x)\pi(\theta,x)\pi(\theta),
where
\kappa(x)
is a normalizing constant that depends only on the data
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

Example 4.6

(Gamma data, inference on scale, gamma prior)
.

Let
(X|\theta)\sim Ga(k,\theta)
with
k,\theta>0,
and
\Theta\sim Ga(a,b)
with
a,b>0
, the prior distribution. Then the posterior distribution is
(\Theta|x)\sim Ga(nk+a,n\bar{x}+b)
, where

. Hence, from (
4.7
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{(nk+a)}{(n\bar{x}+b)^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{2}\left(g^{\prime}(Z)\right)\leq Var(g(\Theta|x))\leq\frac{(nk+a)}{(n\bar{x}+b)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(Z)\right)^{2},

(4.10)

where the rv
Z\sim Ga(nk+a+1,n\bar{x}+b)
.

For example, when
g(x)=x
, from (
4.10
), we get

\displaystyle Var(\Theta|x)=\frac{(nk+a)}{(n\bar{x}+b)^{2}},

(4.11)

as expected.

When
g(x)=x^{2}
, and using (
4.10
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{4(nk+a)(nk+a+1)^{2}}{(n\bar{x}+b)^{4}}\leq Var(g(\Theta|x))\leq\frac{4(nk+a)(nk+a+1)(nk+a+2)}{(n\bar{x}+b)^{4}}.

(4.12)

Observe also that, the upper and lower bounds in (
4.12
) coincides with the one given in
[
11
, Example B.4]
.

Example 4.7

(Poisson data, inference on scale, gamma prior)
.

Let

with
\theta>0
, and
\Theta\sim Ga(a,b)
, with
a,b>0
, the prior distribution. Then the posterior distribution is
(\Theta|x)\sim Ga(n\bar{x}+a,n+b)
. Again from (
4.7
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{(n\bar{x}+a)}{(n+b)^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{2}\left(g^{\prime}(Z)\right)\leq Var(g(\Theta|x))\leq\frac{(n\bar{x}+a)}{(n+b)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(Z)\right)^{2},

(4.13)

where the rv
Z\sim Ga(n\bar{x}+a+1,n+b)
.

For example, when
g(x)=x
, from (
4.13
), we get

\displaystyle Var(\Theta|x)=\frac{(n\bar{x}+a)}{(n+b)^{2}},

(4.14)

as expected.

When
g(x)=x^{2}
, we get from (
4.13
)

\displaystyle\frac{4(n\bar{x}+a)(n\bar{x}+a+1)^{2}}{(n+b)^{4}}\leq Var(g(\Theta|x))\leq\frac{4(n\bar{x}+a)(n\bar{x}+a+1)(n\bar{x}+a+2)}{(n+b)^{4}}.

(4.15)

The bounds given above coincide with the one given in
[
11
, Example B.6]
.

5.
Applications

In this section, we discuss applications of our results.

5.1.
Stein identities via covariance identities

Here, we demonstrate the use of covariance identities to provide Stein identities for several probability distributions. It is well known that such identities are useful to obtain explicit approximations, using Stein’s method, for sums of independent rvs, but such applications are beyond the scope of this paper. Before stating our next results, we recall the Schwartz space of functions. Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be the Schwartz space defined by

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}):\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|x^{m}f^{(n)}(x)|=0,\text{ for all }m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\},

where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
f^{(n)}
denotes the
n
-th derivative of
f
and
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is the class of infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
27
]
for more details). This function space is useful for choosing an appropriate function space for Stein identities. Our first example yields a Stein identity for
CGMY
distributions.

Example 5.1
.

(
CGMY
distributions)
Let
X\sim CGMY(\alpha,\beta,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
, whose Lévy measure
\nu_{cgmy}
is given in (
2.19
).
Recall that
CGMY(\alpha,\beta,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})\overset{d}{=}IDD(\mu,0,\nu_{cgmy})
, where
\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{cgmy}(du).

Hence by Corollary
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle Cov(X,g(X))=Var(X)\mathbb{E}\bigg(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\bigg),

where
Y_{1}
has density

f_{1}(v)=\frac{[\int_{v}^{\infty}u\nu_{cgmy}(du)]\mathbf{I}_{(0,\infty)}(v)+[\int_{-\infty}^{v}(-u)\nu_{cgmy}(du)]\mathbf{I}_{(-\infty,0)}(v)}{Var(X)},~v\in\mathbb{R}.

So,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(X-\mu\right)g(X)=

\displaystyle Cov(X,g(X))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Var(X)\mathbb{E}\bigg(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\bigg)

(5.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{\prime}(X+v)\int_{v}^{\infty}u\nu_{cgmy}(du)dv

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}g^{\prime}(X+v)\int_{-\infty}^{v}(-u)\nu_{cgmy}(du)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{u}g^{\prime}(X+v)dv\right)u\nu_{cgmy}(du)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\int_{u}^{0}g^{\prime}(X+v)dv\right)(-u)\nu_{cgmy}(du)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(g(X+u)-g(X)\right)u\nu_{cgmy}(du)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(g(X+u)-g(X)\right)u\nu_{cgmy}(du)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(g(X+u)-g(X))\nu_{cgmy}(du)\right).

(5.2)

Since
\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{cgmy}(du),
then (
5.2
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Xg(X)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}ug(X+u)\nu_{cgmy}(du)\right)=0,

(5.3)

which is a Stein identity for
CGMY(\alpha,\beta,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
distribution.

Next, we obtain a Stein identity for
VGD
, using our approach.

Example 5.2
.

(Variance-gamma distributions)
Let
X\sim VGD_{0}(\mu_{0},\alpha,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
whose Lévy measure
\nu_{vg}
is defined in (
2.15
). Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Now, using the fact
\mathbb{E}(X)=\mu_{0}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{vg}(du)
, and following steps similar to Example
5.1
, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg((X-\mu_{0})g(X)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ug(X+u)\nu_{vg}(du)

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\lambda^{+}u}g(X+u)-e^{-\lambda^{-}u}g(X-u)\right)du.

(5.4)

Applying integration by parts formula two times on the right hand side of (
5.4
) and rearranging the integrals, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X-\mu_{0})g(X)=

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)\mathbb{E}g(X)+\frac{2\alpha}{\lambda^{+}\lambda^{-}}\mathbb{E}g^{\prime}(X)

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\lambda^{+}u}g^{\prime}(X+u)-e^{-\lambda^{-}u}g^{\prime}(X-u)\right)du

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^{+}\lambda^{-}}\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\lambda^{+}u}g^{\prime\prime}(X+u)-e^{-\lambda^{-}u}g^{\prime\prime}(X-u)\right)du.

(5.5)

Next taking
g^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime\prime}
in (
5.4
)

\displaystyle(a)~~\mathbb{E}(X-\mu_{0})g^{\prime}(X)

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\lambda^{+}u}g^{\prime}(X+u)-e^{-\lambda^{-}u}g^{\prime}(X-u)\right)du,

(5.6a)

\displaystyle(b)~~\mathbb{E}(X-\mu_{0})g^{\prime\prime}(X)

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\lambda^{+}u}g^{\prime\prime}(X+u)-e^{-\lambda^{-}u}g^{\prime\prime}(X-u)\right)du,

(5.6b)

as
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Now, using (
5.6a
) and (
5.6b
) on (
5.5
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X-\mu_{0})g(X)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)g(X)\right)+\frac{2\alpha}{\lambda^{+}\lambda^{-}}\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left(X-\mu_{0}\right)g^{\prime}(X)\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}\lambda^{-}}(X-\mu_{0})g^{\prime\prime}(X)\right).

(5.7)

Setting
\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}\lambda^{-}}=\sigma^{2},\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)=2\theta,
and
\alpha=\frac{r}{2}
, we get
\mathbb{E}(X)=\mu_{0}+r\theta
and
Var(X)=r(\sigma^{2}+2\theta^{2})
. Also, for these parameters, (
5.7
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sigma^{2}(X-\mu_{0})g^{\prime\prime}(X)+\left(\sigma^{2}r+2\theta(X-\mu_{0})\right)g^{\prime}(X)+\left(r\theta-(X-\mu_{0})\right)g(X)\bigg)=0,

(5.8)

which is a Stein identity for
VGD_{1}(\mu_{0},\sigma^{2},r,\theta)
.

Observe that the Stein identity given by Gaunt
[
17
]
, and our Stein identity given in (
5.8
) match except for the choice of function space. Note that the derivation of Stein identity given in
[
17
]
is by modifying the density approach developed by Stein
et al.

[
29
]
, and the density of
VGD
is usually written in terms of modified Bessel functions.

Finally, we obtain a Stein identity for
BGD
. This identity is, in a sense a generalization of the
VGD
Stein identity, since
VGD_{0}(0,\alpha,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})\overset{d}{=}BGD(\alpha,\lambda^{+},\alpha,\lambda^{-}).

Example 5.3
.

(Bilateral-gamma distributions)
Let
X\sim BGD(\alpha^{+},\lambda^{+},\alpha^{-},\lambda^{-})
whose Lévy measure
\nu_{bg}
is defined in (
2.11
). Then
BGD(\alpha^{+},\lambda^{+},\alpha^{-},\lambda^{-})\overset{d}{=}IDD(\mu,0,

\nu_{bg})
, where
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{bg}(du)
and the rv
Y_{1}
has density

f_{1}(v)=\frac{[\int_{v}^{\infty}u\nu_{bg}(du)]\mathbf{I}_{(0,\infty)}(v)+[\int_{-\infty}^{v}(-u)\nu_{bg}(du)]\mathbf{I}_{(-\infty,0)}(v)}{Var(X)},~v\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Then by Corollary
3.2
,

Cov(X,g(X))=Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right)=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(g(X+u)-g(X))\nu_{bg}(du)\bigg),

which can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(Xg(X)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ug(X+u)\nu_{bg}(du)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\alpha^{+}e^{-\lambda^{+}u}g(X+u)-\alpha^{-}e^{-\lambda^{-}u}g(X-u)\right)du.

(5.9)

Following steps similar to the derivation of Stein identity for
VGD_{1}(\mu_{0},

\sigma^{2},r,\theta)
in Example
5.2
, we get a Stein identity for
BGD(\alpha^{+},\lambda^{+},\alpha^{-},\lambda^{-})
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\bigg(Xg^{\prime\prime}(X)+\left(\left(\alpha^{+}+\alpha^{-}\right)-\left(\lambda^{+}-\lambda^{-}\right)X\right)g^{\prime}(X)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left(\left(\alpha^{+}\lambda^{-}-\alpha^{-}\lambda^{+}\right)-\lambda^{+}\lambda^{-}X\right)g(X)\bigg)=0,~~g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

(5.10)

The identity in (
5.10
) has been recently and independently derived by Forrester
[
13
, Proposition 1]
, using the density approach, and also for a different function space.

5.2.
Application to weighted premium calculation principles

In this section, we apply Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
to premium calculation principles. First, we obtain a new formula of the
WPCP
given in (
5.12
) for
IDD
, in terms of Lévy measure via Stein-type covariance identity given in Corollary
3.2
which is novel in our opinion. Recall first that the
WPCP
due to Furman and Zitikis
[
14
]
is as follows.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
X
be a loss rv of risk and
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a function such that
0<\mathbb{E}(w(X))<\infty
. Then the
WPCP
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(Xw(X)\right)}{\mathbb{E}\left(w(X)\right)},

(5.11)

which can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\mathbb{E}(X)+\frac{\text{Cov}(X,w(X))}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}.

(5.12)

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
X\sim\text{IDD}(\mu,0,\nu)
with
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu(du)<\infty
and
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a function such that
0<\mathbb{E}(w(X))<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(X+u)u\nu(du)\big)}{\mathbb{E}(w(X)}.

(5.13)

Proof.

From Corollary
3.2
, we get

\text{Cov}(X,g(X))=Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right),

where the random variables
X
and
Y_{1}
are independent, with
pdf

f_{1}(y)
of
Y_{1}
given in (
3.7
). Now

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(X,g(X))=

\displaystyle Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Var(X)\mathbb{E}\left(g^{\prime}(X+Y_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Var(X)\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X+v)f_{1}(v)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(X+v)\left(\eta_{1}^{+}(v)\mathbf{I}_{(0,\infty)}(v)+\eta_{1}^{-}(v)\mathbf{I}_{(-\infty,0)}(v)\right)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{\prime}(X+v)\int_{v}^{\infty}u\nu(du)dv

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}g^{\prime}(X+v)\int_{-\infty}^{v}(-u)\nu(du)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g(X+u)-g(X))u\nu(du),

(5.14)

where the last equality is followed by Fubini’s theorem and adjusting the integrals. Recall that, for any
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
0<\mathbb{E}(w(X))<\infty
, the
WPCP
(see
[
14
]
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(Xw(X)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}=\mathbb{E}(X)+\frac{Cov(X,w(X))}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}.

(5.15)

Next, replacing
g
by
w
in (
5.14
) and substituting it in (
5.15
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(X)+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(w(X+u)-w(X)\right)u\nu(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(X+u)u\nu(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))},

(5.16)

which proves the result. ∎

Remark 5.6
.

Recently, Psarraakos
[
25
]
proposed an alternate formula of
WPCP
for a non-negative continuous rv using Stein-type covariance identity and its formula involves the
pdf
of
X
. Hence the derivation of
WPCP
is not straightforward, whenever the
pdf
is not in closed form (or
pdf
is, in terms of, some special functions, for example, bilateral-gamma
[
22
]
and variance-gamma distributions
[
17
]
).

Example 5.7
.

(Compound Poisson distributions).
Let
X\sim CPD(\nu(\mathbb{R}),\nu_{0})
. That is,
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
, where
\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu(du)
and
\nu(du)=\nu(\mathbb{R})\nu_{0}(du)
. Then by (
5.13
), the
WPCP
is given by

\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\nu(\mathbb{R})\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(X+u)u\nu_{0}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}.

In particular, for
w(x)=e^{\kappa x},

\kappa>0
,
\nu(\mathbb{R})=\lambda
and
\nu_{0}(du)=\delta_{1}(du)
, the
WPCP
reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\lambda\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\kappa(X+u)}u\delta_{1}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})}=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\kappa u}u\delta_{1}(du)=\lambda e^{\kappa},

which is the Esscher principle for
Poi(\lambda)
distribution, see
[
12
, p. 51]
.

Note that the covariance identity obtained in
[
25
]
does not hold for discrete distributions. Therefore the
WPCP
for Poisson distribution can not be retrieved from the covariance identity given in
[
25
]
.

The following examples are new to the literature and also establish the importance of our approach using Lévy measure.

Example 5.8

(
CGMY
distributions)
.

Let
X\sim CGMY(\alpha,\beta,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
with non-zero mean. That is,
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu_{cgmy})
, where
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{cgmy}(du)
and the Lévy measure
\nu_{cgmy}
is defined in (
2.19
). Also,
Var(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\nu_{cgmy}(du)
. Then by (
5.13
), the
WPCP
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(X+u)u\nu_{cgmy}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}.

Let
w(x)=x
(known as the modified variance principle, see
[
14
, Section 2]
), then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(X+u)u\nu_{cgmy}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}(X)\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{cgmy}(du)+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\nu_{cgmy}(du)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X)+\frac{Var(X)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta)}{(\lambda^{+})^{1-\beta}}-\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta)}{(\lambda^{-})^{1-\beta}}\right)+\frac{\frac{\Gamma(2-\beta)}{(\lambda^{+})^{2-\beta}}+\frac{\Gamma(2-\beta)}{(\lambda^{-})^{2-\beta}}}{\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta)}{(\lambda^{+})^{1-\beta}}-\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta)}{(\lambda^{-})^{1-\beta}}}.

Also, for
w(x)=e^{\kappa x},~\kappa>0
, the Esscher principle, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\kappa(X+u)}u\nu_{cgmy}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{\kappa u}\nu_{cgmy}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})}

\displaystyle=\alpha\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}ue^{\kappa u}\frac{e^{-\lambda^{+}u}}{u^{1+\beta}}du+\alpha\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}ue^{\kappa u}\frac{e^{-\lambda^{-}|u|}}{|u|^{1+\beta}}du

\displaystyle=\alpha\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(u^{-\beta}e^{-(\lambda^{+}-\kappa)u}-u^{-\beta}e^{-(\lambda^{-}+\kappa)u}\right)du

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-\beta)}{(\lambda^{+}-\kappa)^{1-\beta}}-\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-\beta)}{(\lambda^{-}+\kappa)^{1-\beta}},~0<\kappa<\lambda^{+}.

Example 5.9

(Bilateral-gamma distributions)
.

Let
X\sim BGD(\alpha^{+},\lambda^{+},\alpha^{-},\lambda^{-})
with non-zero mean. That is,
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu_{bg})
, where
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{bg}(du)
and the Lévy measure
\nu_{bg}
is defined in (
2.11
). Also,
Var(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\nu_{bg}(du)
. Then by (
5.13
), the
WPCP
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(X+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)du}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}.

For
w(x)=x
, the modified variance principle, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(X+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{bg}(du)+\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\nu_{bg}(du)}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{bg}(du)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha^{+}}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{\alpha^{-}}{\lambda^{-}}\right)+\frac{\left(\frac{\alpha^{+}}{(\lambda^{+})^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{-}}{(\lambda^{-})^{2}}\right)}{\left(\frac{\alpha^{+}}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{\alpha^{-}}{\lambda^{-}}\right)}.

(5.17)

When
\alpha^{+}=\alpha^{-}=\alpha
, from (
5.17
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)+\frac{\frac{1}{(\lambda^{+})^{2}}+\frac{1}{(\lambda^{-})^{2}}}{\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda^{+}\lambda^{-}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda^{-}-\lambda^{+}\right)^{-1}\left(\alpha(\lambda^{-}-\lambda^{+})^{2}+(\lambda^{+})^{2}+(\lambda^{-})^{2}\right),

which is the modified variance principle of
VGD_{0}(0,\alpha,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
distribution.

Also, for
w(x)=e^{\kappa x},~\kappa>0
, the Esscher principle, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\kappa(X+u)}u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{\kappa u}\nu_{bg}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{+}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda^{+}-\kappa)u}du-\alpha^{-}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda^{-}+\kappa)u}du

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{+}}{\lambda^{+}-\kappa}-\frac{\alpha^{-}}{\lambda^{-}+\kappa},~0<\kappa<\lambda^{+}.

(5.18)

Also, when
\alpha^{+}=\alpha^{-}=\alpha,
from (
5.18
), we get

\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}-\kappa}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}+\kappa}\right),~0<\kappa<\lambda^{+},

which is the Esscher principle of
VGD_{0}(0,\alpha,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
distribution.

Example 5.10

(Inverse Gaussian distribution)
.

Let
X
have an inverse Gaussian distribution with parameters
\alpha,\lambda>0
with
pdf

f_{X}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi\lambda}-\lambda x-\pi\alpha^{2}/x},~x>0.

That is,
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu_{ig})
, where
\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{ig}(du)
and the Lévy measure
\nu_{ig}
is given by
\nu_{ig}(du)=\alpha e^{-\lambda u}u^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{I}_{(0,\infty)}(u)du
(see
[
7
, Section 4.4.2]
). Then by (
5.13
), the
WPCP
is given by

\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\alpha\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda u}u^{-\frac{1}{2}}w(X+u)du}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}.

For
w(x)=x
, the modified variance principle, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(X+u)u\nu_{ig}(du)\right)}{\mathbb{E}(X)}=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{ig}(du)+\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\nu_{ig}(du)}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu_{ig}(du)}=\frac{1+2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}{2\lambda}.

Also, for
w(x)=e^{\kappa x},~\kappa>0
, the Esscher principle, we have

\mathcal{H}_{w}(X)=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}{(\lambda-\kappa)^{\frac{3}{2}}},~0<\kappa<\lambda.

5.2.1.
Generalized
WPCP

Here, we apply Theorem
3.1
to generalized
WPCP
. We first recall the following definition; see
[
14
, Section 4]
for more details.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
X
be a rv of risk and
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an increasing function. Then for an increasing non-negative function
g
, the generalized
WPCP
is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{g,w}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{E}(g(X)w(X))}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))}.

In particular, using (
3.4
) with
g(x)=x^{n},~n\geq 1
, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
X\sim IDD(\mu,0,\nu)
with
\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\nu(du)<\infty
and
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a function such that
0<\mathbb{E}(w(X))<\infty
. Suppose that for
x\in[0,\infty)
,
g(x)=x^{n}
,
n\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{x^{n},w}(X)=\mathbb{E}(X^{n})+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}w^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\eta_{n-k}(v)dv\right)ds}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))},

(5.19)

where the random vector
(X_{s},Y_{s})
have the cf given in (
3.3
) and
\eta_{k}
’s are defined in (
3.2
).

Next, we discuss an example of generalized
WPCP
using the above formula.

Example 5.13
.

(Gamma distribution)
Let
X\sim Ga(a,b)
(
a,b>0
) with
pdf

\displaystyle f_{X}(x)=\frac{b^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-bx},~x>0.

(5.20)

Let
\Gamma(s,x)=\int_{s}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{x-1}dt
(
s,x>0
) be the incomplete gamma function. Let
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a function such that
0<\mathbb{E}(w(X))<\infty
. Suppose also that for
x\in[0,\infty)
,
g(x)=x^{n}
,
n\geq 1
. Then by (
5.19
), the generalized
WPCP
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{x^{n},w}(X)=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)b^{n}}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}(X_{s}+v)\eta_{n-k}(v)dv\right)ds}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))},

(5.21)

where
\eta_{k}
’s are defined by, for any
y>0
,

\eta_{k}(y)=\frac{a}{b^{k}}\Gamma(by,k),~k\geq 1.

Note also that
(X_{s},Y_{s})
has the cf

\displaystyle\phi_{s}(t,z)=\bigg(1-\frac{it}{b}\bigg)^{-(1-s)a}\bigg(1-\frac{iz}{b}\bigg)^{-(1-s)a}\bigg(1-\frac{i(t+z)}{b}\bigg)^{-sa},~t,z\in\mathbb{R},

(5.22)

since
\phi_{X}(t)=\bigg(1-\frac{it}{b}\bigg)^{-a}
.

Also, by Corollary
3.4
, the formula in (
5.21
) can be seen as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{x^{n},w}(X)=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)b^{n}}+\frac{(n!)}{b^{n+1}}\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{ab^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{s}^{k}w^{\prime}(X_{s}+Y_{n-k})\right)ds}{\mathbb{E}(w(X))},

(5.23)

where the rv
Y_{k}
has the density, for any
y>0
,

f_{k}(y)=\frac{b}{\Gamma(k+1)}\Gamma(by,k),~k\geq 1.

For example, when
n=1
and
w(x)=e^{\kappa x},~\kappa>0
, from (
5.23
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{x,w}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b}+\frac{\kappa a}{b^{2}}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\kappa(X_{s}+Y_{1})}\right)ds}{\mathbb{E}(e^{X})},

(5.24)

where
Y_{1}
has density

f_{1}(y)=be^{-by},~y>0.

Since
X_{s}\overset{d}{=}X,
then (
5.24
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{x,w}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b}+\frac{\kappa a}{b^{2}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\kappa(X+Y_{1})}\right)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa X})}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b}+\frac{\kappa a}{b^{2}}\mathbb{E}(e^{\kappa Y_{1}})

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b}+\frac{\kappa a}{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(b-\kappa)y}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b}+\frac{\kappa a}{b(b-\kappa)}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b-\kappa},~0<\kappa<b,

(5.25)

which is the Esscher principle of gamma
Ga(a,b)
distribution.

Observe that, for
a=b=1
, the
WPCP
in (
5.25
) matches exactly with the Esscher principle of
Ga(1,1)
distribution (exponential distribution with mean
1
) (see
[
12
, Example 3.1]
), where the author uses density function to derive the premium calculation principle.

5.3.
Application to Gini coefficient

Here, we obtain an alternate formula of the Gini coefficient for
IDD
, in terms of Lévy measure via Stein-type covariance identity. Recall that the Gini coefficient is defined as follows (see
[
30
]
).

Definition 5.14
.

Let
X
have the distribution
F_{X}(x)
. The Gini coefficient of the distribution function
F_{X}(x)
is defined as

\displaystyle G=\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}(X)}\text{Cov}(X,F_{X}(X)).

(5.26)

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
X\sim\text{IDD}(\mu,0,\nu)
and
F_{X}(x)
be the distribution of
X
. Then for
\mu>0
,

\displaystyle G=\frac{2}{\mu}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(F_{X}(X+u)-F_{X}(X))\nu(du).

(5.27)

Proof.

From Corollary
3.2
, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(X,g(X))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(g(X+u)-g(X))\nu(du).

(5.28)

Replacing
g
by
F_{X}
in (
5.28
) and substituting in (
5.26
), the desired conclusion follows.
∎

Next, we discuss some examples.

Example 5.16

(
CGMY
distribution)
.

Let
X\sim CGMY(\alpha,\beta,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
. Then the mean is given by

\mathbb{E}(X)=\mu=\Gamma(1-\beta)\frac{\alpha}{(\lambda^{+})^{1-\beta}}-\Gamma(1-\beta)\frac{\alpha}{(\lambda^{-})^{1-\beta}}.

Then using (
5.27
), the Gini coefficient is given by

G_{\text{CGMY}}=\frac{2}{\mu}\left(\Gamma(2-\beta)\frac{\alpha}{(\lambda^{+})^{2-\beta}}+\Gamma(2-\beta)\frac{\alpha}{(\lambda^{-})^{2-\beta}}\right).

Example 5.17

(Bilateral gamma distribution)
.

Let
X\sim BGD(\alpha^{+},\lambda^{+},\alpha^{-},\lambda^{-})
. Then the mean is given by

\mathbb{E}(X)=\mu=\frac{\alpha^{+}}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{\alpha^{+}}{\lambda^{+}}.

Then using (
5.27
), the Gini coefficient is given by

G_{\text{BGD}}=\frac{2}{\mu}\left(\frac{\alpha^{+}}{(\lambda^{+})^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{-}}{(\lambda^{-})^{2}}\right).

Example 5.18

(Variance gamma distribution)
.

Let
X\sim VGD_{0}(\mu_{0},\alpha,\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
. Then the mean is given by

\mathbb{E}(X)=\mu=\mu_{0}+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^{+}}-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^{+}}.

Then using (
5.27
), the Gini coefficient is given by

G_{\text{VGD}}=\frac{2\alpha}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda^{+})^{2}}+\frac{1}{(\lambda^{-})^{2}}\right).

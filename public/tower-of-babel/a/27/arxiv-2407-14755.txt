Remoteness in the category of bilocales

Abstract
. In locale theory, a sublocale is said to be remote in case it misses every nowhere dense sublocale. 1.1.
Locales

A

is a complete lattice satisfying:

a\wedge\bigvee B=\bigvee\{a\wedge b:b\in B\}

for all
a\in L
,
B\subseteq L
.

1_{L}
and
0_{L}

L
. We denote by
a^{*}
the
pseudocomplement
of an element
a\in L
. An element

is said to be
dense
and
complemented

a^{*}=0
and
a\vee a^{\ast}=1
, respectively.

We denote by
\mathfrak{O}X
the locale of open subsets of a topological space
X
.

A
localic map
is an infima-preserving function
f:L\rightarrow M
between locales such that the corresponding left adjoint
f^{*}
, called its associated
frame homomorphism
, preserves binary meets. Throughout the paper, if
f
is a localic map, we will write
h
for its associated frame homomorphism.

\Frm
and
\Loc
represent the categories of locales whose morphisms are frame homomorphisms and localic maps, respectively.

1.2.
Sublocales

A

\mathcal{S}(L)

\mathsf{O}
the smallest sublocale of a locale
L
and we shall say that a sublocale

T\cap S=\mathsf{O}
. The largest sublocale of a locale
L
missing a sublocale
S
of
L
is denoted by
L\smallsetminus S
. The sublocales

{\mathfrak{c}}(a)=\{x\in L:a\leq x\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{o}(a)=\{a\rightarrow x:x\in L\},

of a locale
L
are respectively the

and

of

containing

of

and denoted by
\overline{S}
.
S\in\mathcal{S}(L)

\mathfrak{B}(L)=\{x\rightarrow 0:x\in L\}
is the least dense sublocale of

.

For each sublocale
S\subseteq L
there is an onto frame homomorphism
\nu_{S}:L\rightarrow S
defined by
\nu_{S}(a)={\bigwedge}\{s\in S:a\leq s\}.
Open sublocales and closed sublocales of a sublocale

of

\mathfrak{o}_{S}(\nu_{S}(a))=S\cap\mathfrak{o}(a)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathfrak{c}_{S}}(\nu_{S}(a))=S\cap\mathfrak{c}(a),

Each localic map

f_{-1}[T]={\bigvee}\{A\in\mathcal{S}(L):A\subseteq f^{-1}(T)\}.

For a localic map

f_{-1}[\mathfrak{c}_{M}(x)]=\mathfrak{c}_{L}(h(x))\quad\text{and}\quad f_{-1}[\mathfrak{o}_{M}(x)]=\mathfrak{o}_{L}(h(x)).

We denote by

a sublocale of
\mathfrak{O}X

induced
by a subset

of a topological space

.

A

We write

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Int_{j}(\mathfrak{c}(X\smallsetminus\cl_{\tau_{i}}(A)))=\mathsf{O}\quad\text{from Proposition \ref{int}(9)}

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Int_{j}\left(\widetilde{\cl_{\tau_{i}}(A)}\right)=\mathsf{O}\quad\text{since}\quad\cl_{\tau_{i}}(A)\quad\text{is}\quad\tau\text{-closed}

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Int_{j}\left(\cl_{i}(\widetilde{A})\right)=\mathsf{O}\quad\text{since}\quad\widetilde{\cl_{\tau_{i}}(A)}=\cl_{i}(\widetilde{A})

which proves the result.
∎

We characterize
(i,j)
-nowhere dense sublocales in Theorem

below. We shall need the following lemma whose proof is straighforward.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
be a bilocale with

. If

is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense and

, then

is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
be a bilocale and
S\in\mathcal{S}(L)
. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

S
is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense.

(2)

L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)
is
j
-dense.

(3)

\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)^{\bullet}=0
.

(4)

\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}
is
j
-dense.

(5)

\cl_{i}(S)
is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense.

(6)

\overline{S}
is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense.

Proof.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
: We have that

\displaystyle\Int_{j}(\cl_{i}(S))=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{a\in L_{j}:\mathfrak{o}(a)\subseteq\cl_{i}(S)\}\right)=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{c}\left(\bigvee\{a\in L_{j}:\mathfrak{o}(a)\subseteq\cl_{i}(S)\}\right)=L

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{c}\left(\bigvee\{a\in L_{j}:(L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S))\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(a)\}\right)=L\quad\text{since }\cl_{i}(S)\text{ is complemented}.

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\cl_{j}(L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S))=L\quad\text{by Proposition \ref{int}(4)}.

(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
: Observe that

\displaystyle\cl_{j}(L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S))=L

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\cl_{j}\left(L\smallsetminus\mathfrak{c}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)\right)=L

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\cl_{j}\left(\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)\right)=L

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{c}\left(\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)^{\bullet}\right)=L\quad\text{by Proposition \ref{int}(10)}

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)^{\bullet}=0.

(3)\Leftrightarrow(4)
: Follows from definition of
j
-density.

(4)\Leftrightarrow(5)
: We have that

\displaystyle\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\text{ is }j\text{-dense}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:\cl_{i}(S)\subseteq\cl_{i}(\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\text{ is }j\text{-dense}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:\mathfrak{o}(x)\subseteq L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)\}\text{ is }j\text{-dense}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\cl_{j}\left(\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:\mathfrak{o}(x)\subseteq L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)\}\right)\right)=L

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\cl_{j}\left(\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)\right)=L

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\cl_{j}\left(L\smallsetminus\mathfrak{c}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:S\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)\right)=L

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\cl_{j}\left(L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)\right)=L

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow L\smallsetminus\cl_{j}\left(L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)\right)=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{a\in L_{j}:L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(a)\}\right)=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{a\in L_{j}:\mathfrak{o}(a)\subseteq\cl_{i}(S)\}\right)=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Int_{j}\big(\cl_{i}(S)\big)=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Int_{j}\big(\cl_{i}(\cl_{i}(S))\big)=\mathsf{O}\quad\text{since }\cl_{i}(\cl_{i}(S))=\cl_{i}(S)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\cl_{i}(S)\ \text{is}\ (i,j)\text{-nowhere dense}.

(5)\Rightarrow(6)
: Since
\overline{S}\subseteq\cl_{i}(S)
and
\cl_{i}(S)
is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense, it follows from Lemma
2.11
that
\overline{S}
is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense.

(6)\Rightarrow(1)
: This is another application of Lemma
2.11
.
∎

An element

For any bilocale

In locales,

is the only clopen nowhere dense sublocale of a locale. However, in bilocales, this need not be the case, as shown below.

Example 2.16
.

Recall that the void sublocale is the only clopen nowhere dense sublocale of a locale. This follows since if
S
is a clopen and nowhere dense sublocale of a locale

\mathsf{O}=\inte(\overline{S})=\inte(S)=S.

In bilocales, for a given bilocale

, a sublocale

of

and at the same be
(i,j)
-nowhere dense without being void. For instance, consider the bitopological space
(X,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
, where

\tau_{1}=\{\emptyset,X,\{a\},\{b,c\}\}

In locales, nowhere dense sublocales are precisely those that miss the least dense sublocale. We have the following result in bilocales.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
be a bilocale and

. Then

is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense whenever
\cl_{i}(S)\cap\mathfrak{B}L=\mathsf{O}
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\cl_{i}(S)\cap\mathfrak{B}L=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{B}L\subseteq L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)\text{ since }\cl_{i}(S)\text{ is complemented}

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\overline{L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S)}=L

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad\cl_{j}(L\smallsetminus\cl_{i}(S))=L\quad\text{by Proposition \ref{int}(1)}

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Int_{j}(\cl_{i}(S))=\mathsf{O}

where the latter equivalence can be deduced from the proof of Proposition

(4)\Leftrightarrow(5)
.
∎

The converse of Proposition

In Proposition

.

Recall from
[
9
]
that a bilocale
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
is

balanced
if

implies

and
x\in L_{2}
implies
x^{*}\in L_{1}
.
In a balanced bilocale
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
,
a^{*}=a^{\bullet}
for all

. Indeed, it is clear that
a^{\bullet}\leq a^{*}
. Furthermore, if
y=a^{*}
, then
y\in L_{j}
and
y\wedge a=0
. Therefore
y\in\{x\in L_{j}:a\wedge x=0\}
. Thus

y=a^{*}\leq\bigvee\{x\in L_{j}:a\wedge x=0\}=a^{\bullet}.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
be a balanced bilocale and
N\in\mathcal{S}(L)
. Then
N\in\mathcal{S}(L)
is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense iff
\mathfrak{B}L\cap\cl_{i}(N)=\mathsf{O}
.

Proof.

For each
N\in\mathcal{S}(L)
, we have that

\displaystyle N\quad\text{is}\quad(i,j)\text{-nowhere dense}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:N\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)^{\bullet}=0\text{ by Theorem \ref{ijnd}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:N\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)^{*}=0\text{ since }(L,L_{1},L_{2})\text{ is balanced}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:N\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)\ \text{is dense}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{B}L\subseteq\mathfrak{o}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:N\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{B}L\cap\mathfrak{c}\left(\bigvee\{x\in L_{i}:N\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\}\right)=\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{B}L\cap\cl_{i}(N)=\mathsf{O}

which proves the result.
∎

The above result implies the folowing.

Corollary 2.19
.

In a balanced bilocale

of

-remote sublocales and study their properties.

Definition 3.1
.

be a bilocale. A sublocale

(i,j)
-remote (resp. weakly
(i,j)

for every
(i,j)
-nowhere dense (resp. clopen
(i,j)
-nowhere dense) sublocale

.

We consider some examples.

Example 3.2
.

(1) The void sublocale is both
(i,j)
-remote and weakly
(i,j)
-remote.

(2) If

(i,j)
-remote (resp. weakly
(i,j)
-remote) sublocale of

(i,j)
-remote (resp. weakly
(i,j)
-remote).

(3) In a

, which was defined in
[

]
as a biframe
(L,L_{1},L_{2})

(we shall only write

a)

(i,j)
-remoteness coincides with remoteness. As a result, the sublocale
\mathfrak{B}L
is
(i,j)
-remote.

b)

Every sublocale is weakly
(i,j)
-remote. This follows since
\mathsf{O}
is the only clopen
(i,j)
-nowhere dense sublocale of the locale

.

(4) Every
(i,j)
-remote sublocale is weakly
(i,j)
-remote, but the converse is not always true: Since, by
[
14
]

-remote sublocale is

-remote.

(5) For any symmetric bilocale

,

is the collection of all

-remote sublocales of

: Let

be an
(i,j)
-remote sublocale of

. Since

is the largest remote sublocale and remoteness coincides with

. Since

.

On the other hand, let

and consider the sublocale

of

-remote sublocale of

.

(6) In a balanced bilocale, every remote sublocale is
(i,j)
-remote (In particular, the Booleanization is
(i,j)
-remote): Let
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
be a balanced bilocale,

a remote sublocale of

(i,j)
-nowhere dense sublocale

. By Corollary
2.19
,

, making

as required.

In the following main result of this section, we give a characterization of

-remote and weakly

-remote sublocales. We only prove results about

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(L,L_{1},L_{2})
be a bilocale and
S\in\mathcal{S}(L)
. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

S
is
(i,j)
-remote (resp. weakly
(i,j)
-remote).

(2)

S\cap\cl_{i}(N)=\mathsf{O}
for every
(i,j)
-nowhere dense (resp. clopen
(i,j)
-nowhere dense)
N
.

(3)

S\cap\overline{N}=\mathsf{O}
for every
(i,j)
-nowhere dense (resp. clopen
(i,j)
-nowhere dense)
N
.

(4)

S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)=\mathsf{O}
for all
j
-dense (resp. complemented
j
-dense)

.

(5)

S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(a)
for every

-dense (resp. complemented
j

.

(6)

\nu_{S}(d)=1
for every
j
-dense (resp. complemented
j

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
: Follows since a sublocale

of

(i,j)
-nowhere dense if and only if
\cl_{i}(N)
is
(i,j)
-nowhere dense if and only if
\overline{N}

-nowhere dense, by Theorem

.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
: Let

(4)\Leftrightarrow(5)

.

∎

(1) The above result also holds for weakly

(2) Since for all sublocales

.

. Then

(\Leftarrow):

(1) For a locale

\mathfrak{J}L

\bigvee\!I
complemented in

\bigvee\!I

\mathsf{O}=({\downarrow}a)\cap({\downarrow}\bigvee\!I)={\downarrow}a\cap I

and

(\mathfrak{J}L)_{j}
-dense,
{\downarrow}a=0_{\mathfrak{J}L}

\bigvee\!I

j

1_{L}
is the only

-dense element of
L_{i}
which is complemented in

\bigvee\!I=1

\Rmt_{(i,j)}\!\mathfrak{J}L=\mathfrak{J}L

On the other hand, assume that
\Rmt_{(i,j)}\!\mathfrak{J}L=\mathfrak{J}L
and let

be a
j
-dense element of
L_{i}
which is complemented in

for each

j
-density of

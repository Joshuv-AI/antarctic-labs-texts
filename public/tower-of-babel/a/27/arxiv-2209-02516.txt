On a matrix element representation of the GKZ hypergeometric functions
A.A. Gerasimov, D.R. Lebedev, S.V. Oblezin

Abstract
. We develop a representation theory approach to the study of generalized hypergeometric functions of Gelfand, Kapranov and Zelevisnky (GKZ). We show that the GKZ hypergeometric functions may be identified with matrix elements of non-reductive Lie algebras 𝔏 N {\mathfrak{L}}_{N} of oscillator type. The Whittaker functions associated with principal series representations of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) being special cases of GKZ hypergeometric functions, thus admit along with a standard matrix element representations associated with reductive Lie algebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) , another matrix element representation in terms of 𝔏 ℓ ⁡ ( ℓ + 1 ) {\mathfrak{L}}_{\ell(\ell+1)} .



1
Introduction

One way of solving explicitly a quantum integrable system is to
realize the system as a quantum reduction of a larger quantum
integrable system for which explicit solution may be easily found.
Choice of the larger system is obviously not unique and is a matter
of convenience. To practically implement this approach one should
represent wave functions of quantum integrable system as particular
matrix elements of suitable Lie algebra representations allowing
central characters. In this representation the reduction is realized
via proper choice of the matrix element. Interesting examples arise
by considering reductions with respect to non-abelian Lie group
symmetries. Typical case (along with various kinds of the Calogero
models
[
OP
]
) is given by families of quantum open Toda chains
solved via special matrix elements of principle series
representations of non-abelian reductive Lie groups identified
with Whittaker functions
[
Ja
]
,
[
Sch
]
,
[
Ha
]
and
[
Ko1
]
,
[
Ko2
]
.

Recall that another more traditional approach to integration of
quantum integrable system employs large abelian Lie group of
symmetries generated by mutually commuting integrable flows. The
large abelian symmetry allows to find a proper set of quantum
canonical variables and to realize wave functions of the quantum
integrable systems as matrix elements of representations of the
Heisenberg Lie algebras. However, this straightforward quantum
generalization of the classical integration algorithm encounters
various difficulties. The main reason is that quite non-trivial
realization of the corresponding abelian symmetries does not allow
to fix properly operator ordering ambiguities in a simple and
explicit way.

It is natural to try to combine these two complimentary approaches
by considering a larger explicitly integrable quantum theory
completely defined in terms of its Lie symmetry given by extensions
of the Heisenberg Lie algebras. As it was demonstrated in the
previous short announcement
[
GLO5
]
the theory of generalized
hypergeometric functions developed in
[
GGZ
]
,
[
GKZ
]

supplies us with a large class of quantum integrable systems which
may be solved this way.

In this note we show that general GKZ hypergeometric function may be
identified with a matrix element in an irreducible representation of
a suitable multidimensional oscillator Lie algebra. The explicit
identification is most natural when is done in terms of
Gelfand-Graev hypergeometric functions
(referred to as GG-functions). The latter were introduced in

[
GG
]
as a more symmetric formulation of the original
GKZ hypergeometric functions.

The class of integrable systems solved in terms of GKZ hypergeometric functions
includes open
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-Toda chains so that the corresponding
solutions given by
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-Whittaker functions
allow integral representations via GKZ integrals (see

[
BCFKvS
]
). The matrix element representation of open

\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-Toda chain wave functions introduced in this note
should be contrasted with another one based on representation
theory of reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

[
Ko1
]
,
[
Ko2
]
. To stress the difference we provide a
detailed description of both formulations in the case of Toda type
models associated with maximal and minimal parabolic subalgebras of

\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}

[
BCFKvS
]
,
[
GLO4
]
. On a more fundamental
level the relation between two matrix element formulations will be
considered elsewhere.

Our interest in various realizations of the Whittaker functions
steams for the fact that the Whittaker functions play important role
in the formulation of local Archimedean Langlands correspondence
(see
[
GLO1
]
,
[
GLO3
]
,
[
G
]
and references therein). Thus
the identification of the Whittaker functions with matrix elements
of the non-reductive oscillator Lie algebra possibly leads to
interesting implications for Archimedean Langlands correspondence
related to various approaches based on abelianization/torification
and mirror symmetry. This theme will be discussed in future
publications.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we provide basics
of GKZ hypergeometric structure and associated hypergeometric
functions. Following mostly
[
GG
]
we also define GG-system as a
symmetric form of the GKZ hypergeometric structure. The
corresponding GG-functions are the main objects of interest in the
following Sections. In Section 3 matrix element realization of the
general GG hypergeometric functions via representations theory of
multidimensional oscillator algebras is presented. In Section 4 the
set of defining equations for GG hypergeometric functions is
rederived using matrix element representation introduced in Section
3. Finally in Section 5 the matrix element representation in terms
of oscillator Lie algebra and reductive Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
, for
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-Whittaker
functions associated with maximal and minimal parabolic subalgebras
are considered in detail.

The GKZ and GG hypergeometric structures

The GKZ hypergeometric functions are defined as solutions to
system of differential equations associated with the

(\mathcal{A},c)
-hypergeometric structures, first introduced and studied in

[
GGZ
]
,
[
GKZ
]
. The GKZ hypergeometric structure is defined
by the following data. We chose an
N
-element subset

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{A}\,=\,\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{m},\end{array}

such that
\mathcal{A}
generates
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
as abelian group. In addition, we
fix a complex vector,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}c\,=\,(c^{1},\ldots,c^{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\,.\end{array}

The collection
\mathcal{A}
defines the
m\times N
-matrix of maximal
possible rank:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}A\,=\,\|a^{s}_{i}\|\,\in\,{\rm Mat}_{m\times N}(\mathbb{Z})\,,\qquad\operatorname{rank}(A)=m\end{array}

where
a_{i}=(a_{i}^{1},\cdots,a_{i}^{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
are elements of
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\mathbb{L}\subset\mathcal{A}
be the relations lattice of
\mathcal{A}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{L}\,=\,\{(l_{1},\ldots,l_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\,:\,l_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+l_{N}a_{N}=0\}\,.\end{array}

Choosing a basis
\{l^{{\alpha}},\,{\alpha}\in J\}
of the lattice
\mathbb{L}

indexed by
J=\{1,\ldots,N-m\}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{L}\,=\,{\mathop{\rm span}}\bigl\{l^{{\alpha}}=(l_{1}^{{\alpha}},\,\ldots,\,l^{{\alpha}}_{N})\,:\quad{\alpha}\in J\bigr\}\,,\end{array}

we consider the corresponding relation matrix

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}M\,=\,\|l^{{\alpha}}_{i}\|\,\in\,{\rm Mat}_{(N-m)\times N}(\mathbb{Z})\,,\qquad\operatorname{rank}(M)=N-m\,.\end{array}

The matrices
A
and
M
enjoy the orthogonality property

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}AM^{\top}\,=\,0\,\in\,{\rm Mat}_{m\times(N-m)}(\mathbb{Z})\,,\end{array}

where
\top
denotes the standard matrix transposition. The GKZ
hypergeometric function
f(u)
is a solution to the GKZ-system
introduced below.

Definition 2.1

The GKZ-system of differential equations
associated with the data
(\mathcal{A},\,c)
consists of the
following set of equations in variables

u=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
:

(1)

For every
l\in\mathbb{L}
one has

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\prod_{i\in I\atop l_{i}<0}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\right)^{-l_{i}}\,f\,=\,\prod_{i\in I\atop l_{i}>0}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\right)^{l_{i}}\,f\,.\end{array}

(2)

The differential equations enumerated by
s\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{1}^{s}u_{1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u_{1}}+\cdots+a_{N}^{s}u_{N}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u_{N}}=c^{s}f.\end{array}

The equations (
(2)
) may be easily integrated and thus allow
a reduction of the solutions to the system of equations (
(1)
),
(
(2)
) to functions in

(N-m)
-variables indexed by
J=\{1,\ldots,N-m\}
. However the
resulting equations on the function
(N-m)
-variables have more
complicated form.

Now we fix a special solution to the GKZ-system (
(1)
),
(
(2)
).

Proposition 2.1

Given the GKZ data
(\mathcal{A},c)
, the GKZ-system
(
(1)
), (
(2)
) allows the following solution:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{{\gamma}}(u)\,=\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\!\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{{\gamma}_{i}}\,e^{-u_{i}t_{i}}\prod_{{\alpha}\in J}\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}t_{j}^{l^{{\alpha}}_{j}}\,-\,1\Big)\,,\qquad{\rm Re}({\gamma}_{i})>0\,.\end{array}

Here
\{l^{\alpha},\,{\alpha}\in J\}\subset\mathbb{L}
is a basis (
2
) and

{\gamma}=({\gamma}_{1},\ldots,{\gamma}_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a vector subjected to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}c^{s}+\sum_{j\in I}a^{s}_{j}{\gamma}_{j}\,=\,0\,,\qquad s\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\,.\end{array}

The function (
2.1
) is independent of the choice of the basis
as well as of the choice of
{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
satisfying (
2.1
).

Proof
. Let
{\gamma}=({\gamma}_{1},\ldots,{\gamma}_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that
{\rm Re}({\gamma}_{i})>0,\,i\in I
. For every
l\in\mathbb{L}
, let us verify the first
assertion by substituting (
2.1
) into (
(1)
) and
(
(2)
). For the first equation,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\prod_{i\in I\atop l_{i}>0}\Big(-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\Big)^{l_{i}}\cdot f_{{\gamma}}(u)\,-\prod_{i\in I\atop l_{i}<0}\Big(-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\Big)^{-l_{i}}\cdot f_{{\gamma}}(u)\\
=\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\!\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{{\gamma}_{i}}\,e^{-u_{i}t_{i}}\,\Big(\prod_{i\in I\atop l_{i}>0}t_{i}^{l_{i}}-\prod_{i\in I\atop l_{i}<0}t_{i}^{-l_{i}}\Big)\prod_{\alpha\in J}\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}t_{j}^{l^{{\alpha}}_{j}}\,-\,1\Big)\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}=\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\!\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{{\gamma}_{i}}\,e^{-u_{i}t_{i}}\,\prod_{i\in I\atop l_{i}<0}t_{i}^{-l_{i}}\,\,\,\Big(\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{l_{i}}-1\Big)\prod_{\alpha\in J}\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}t_{j}^{l^{{\alpha}}_{j}}\,-\,1\Big)=0.\end{array}

The last equality follows since for
l=\sum\limits_{{\alpha}\in J}n_{{\alpha}}l^{{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{L}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{l_{i}}\,=\,\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{\sum\limits_{{\alpha}\in J}n_{{\alpha}}l^{{\alpha}}_{i}}\,=\,\prod_{{\alpha}\in J}\Big(\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{l^{{\alpha}}_{i}}\Big)^{n_{{\alpha}}}\,,\end{array}

which equals 1 by taking into account the delta-factors in the
integrand of (
2.1
).

To prove the second equation it is useful to change the integration
variables
t_{i}\to u_{i}^{-1}t_{i}
, so the integral (
2.1
) takes
the form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{{\gamma}}(u)\,=\,\prod_{i\in I}u_{i}^{-{\gamma}_{i}}\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\!\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{{\gamma}_{i}}\,e^{-t_{i}}\prod_{{\alpha}\in J}\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}u_{j}^{-l^{{\alpha}}_{j}}t_{j}^{l^{{\alpha}}_{j}}\,-\,1\Big)\,.\end{array}

Then the equations (
(2)
)
follow from the orthogonality relation (
2
) between

A
and
M
.

For the solution (
2.1
), independence of the choice of the
basis
\{l^{\alpha},\,{\alpha}\in J\}\subset\mathbb{L}
can be verified as follows. Let

\{\tilde{l}^{{\alpha}},\,{\alpha}\in J\}\subset\mathbb{L}
be another basis and let

\|g^{{\alpha}}_{\beta}\|\in GL_{N-m}(\mathbb{Z})
be the transition matrix
between the two bases:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\tilde{l}^{{\alpha}}\,=\,\sum_{\beta\in J}g^{{\alpha}}_{\beta}l^{\beta}\,,\qquad\det\|g^{{\alpha}}_{\beta}\|\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}=\{\pm 1\}.\end{array}

By change of integration variables
T_{i}=\ln t_{i},\,i\in I
,
(
2.1
) takes the following form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{{\gamma}}(u)\,=\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!\prod_{i=1}^{N}dT_{i}\,e^{{\gamma}_{i}T_{i}\,-\,u_{i}e^{T_{i}}}\prod_{{\alpha}\in J}\delta\Big(\sum_{j\in I}l^{{\alpha}}_{j}T_{j}\Big)\,.\end{array}

For
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, consider vector
S\in\mathbb{R}^{N-m}

with the coordinates
S^{{\alpha}}=\sum\limits_{j\in I}l^{{\alpha}}_{j}T_{j}
, and
define

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta(S)=\prod_{{\alpha}\in J}\delta(S^{\alpha})=\prod_{{\alpha}\in J}\delta\Big(\sum_{j\in I}l^{{\alpha}}_{j}T_{j}\Big).\end{array}

Then applying standard rule for linear change of arguments in
delta-functions

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta(gS)=\frac{1}{|\det(g)|}\,\delta(S)=\delta(S),\qquad\det(g)\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}=\{\pm 1\}\,,\end{array}

which verifies the independence of the choice of the basis

\{l^{\alpha}\}\subset\mathbb{L}
for (
2.1
).

Finally, for
f_{{\gamma}}(u)
, independence of the choice of solution

\gamma=({\gamma}_{1},\ldots,{\gamma}_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
to the equations (
2.1
)
may be checked as follows. Given a pair of solutions,

\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
, they are related by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\gamma}_{j}={\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}+\sum_{{\alpha}\in J}l^{\alpha}_{j}\xi_{\alpha},\qquad\xi=\|\xi_{{\alpha}}\|\in\mathbb{C}^{N-m}\,.\end{array}

Then substituting this into the integral (
2.1
) results in

f_{{\gamma}}=f_{{\gamma}^{\prime}}
by taking into account the delta-factors in the
integrand.
\Box

3
Matrix element representation

In this section we provide a representation of the GG-hypergeometric
functions (
2.2
) as matrix elements in irreducible
representation of a suitable class of non-reductive Lie algebras.
Namely, for
I=\{1,\ldots,N\}
, let
{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
be the Lie algebra
generated by the central element
\mathcal{C}
and by

\{\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{i},\,i\in I\}
subjected to the following defining
relations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{j}]=\mathcal{C}\delta_{ij}\,,\qquad[\mathcal{H}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{j}]=-\delta_{ij}\mathcal{E}_{j}\,,\qquad[\mathcal{H}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\mathcal{F}_{j}\,,\qquad i,j\in I\,.\end{array}

The subalgebra in
{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
generated by
\mathcal{C}
and by

\{\mathcal{E}_{i},\,\mathcal{F}_{i},\,i\in I\}
is isomorphic to the

(2N+1)
-dimensional Heisenberg algebra. Thus the Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
may be considered as multidimensional version of the
standard oscillator Lie algebra.

For
{\gamma}=({\gamma}_{1},\ldots,{\gamma}_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that
{\rm Re}({\gamma}_{i})>0,\,i\in I
, let
\pi_{{\gamma}}
be the
{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
-representation
in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
of smooth functions in

decreasing rapidly with all its

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}=\bigl\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}):\lim_{t_{i}\to\infty}\!\bigl(t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{N}^{n_{N}}|\partial_{t_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots\partial_{t_{N}}^{m_{N}}\!f(t)|\bigr)=0,\,\forall n_{i},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\bigr\}.\end{array}

Namely, the representation
(\pi_{{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})
is defined by the
following action of the generators (
3
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{C})=1,\quad\pi_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{E}_{i})=-\partial_{t_{i}}\,,\quad\pi_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=-t_{i}\,,\quad\pi_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{H}_{i})={\gamma}_{i}+t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}\,,\quad i\in I\,.\end{array}

Let
\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{L}}_{N})
be the universal enveloping algebra of
{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
,
then
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
affords a structure of

\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{L}}_{N})
-module. Moreover, the action of generator

\mathcal{H}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
in
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
integrates to the action of the group

(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
. It will be also important in the following that the
generators
\mathcal{F}_{i},\,i\in I
are invertible in the representation

\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}
, therefore
(\pi_{{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}})
extends to
representation of a larger algebra containing negative powers of

\mathcal{F}_{i},\,i\in I
. We fix a dual module
\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}
realized
in the space of generalized functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
and denote the
corresponding contragradient representation by
\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}
.
There is a non-degenerate
{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
-invariant pairing

\langle\,\,,\,\rangle\,:\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}\times\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\phi,\,\varphi\rangle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\prod_{i\in I}dt\,\phi(t)\,\varphi(t)\,,\qquad\varphi\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}\,,\quad\phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}\,,\\
\langle\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}(X)\cdot\phi,\,\varphi\rangle\,=\,-\langle\phi,\,\pi_{{\gamma}}(X)\cdot\varphi\rangle,\qquad\forall X\in{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}\,.\end{array}

Then the
{\mathfrak{L}}_{N}
-action in the dual module
\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}
can be
computed explicitly:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{C})=-1,\qquad\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{E}_{i})=-\partial_{t_{i}}\,,\qquad\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=t_{i}\,,\\
\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{H}_{i})=1-{\gamma}_{i}+t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}\,,\qquad i\in I\,.\end{array}

Given the GKZ-datum
\mathcal{A}
, let us fix

\mathbb{L}
(

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\prod_{i\in I\atop l^{{\alpha}}_{i}<0}\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})^{-l^{{\alpha}}_{i}}\cdot\phi_{L}\,=\,\prod_{i\in I\atop l^{{\alpha}}_{i}>0}\pi^{\vee}_{{\gamma}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})^{l^{{\alpha}}_{i}}\cdot\phi_{L}\,,\quad{\alpha}\in\{1,\ldots,N-m\}\,,\\

\times\prod_{1\leq k\leq i\leq\ell}\!\!\Big\{\frac{da_{k,i}}{a_{k,i}}\frac{db_{k,i}}{b_{k,i}}\,\,a_{k,i}^{{\gamma}_{k,i}}b_{k,i}^{\nu_{k,i}}\,e^{-\,a_{k,i}e^{y_{k,i}}-b_{k,i}e^{z_{k,i}}}\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\delta\Big(e^{-y_{\ell,i}-z_{\ell,i}}a_{\ell,i}b_{\ell,i}\,-\,1\Big)\\
\times\prod_{1\leq i\leq k<\ell}\!\!\delta\Big(e^{-y_{k,i}-z_{k,i}+y_{k+1,\,i+1}+z_{k+1,\,i}}a_{k,i}b_{k,i}a_{k+1,\,i+1}^{-1}b_{k+1,\,i}^{-1}\,-\,1\Big)\\
=\,e^{{\gamma}_{*}y_{*}\,-\,e^{y_{*}}}\times\Phi^{\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}}_{{\gamma},\,\nu}(e^{y},e^{z})\,.\end{array}

Therefore, the
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-Whittaker function (
5.1
) can
be obtained as the following limit of the GG-hypergeometric function
(
5.1
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi^{\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{x})\,=\,\lim_{\epsilon\to-\infty}\Big({}^{\rm res}\widehat{\Phi}_{{\gamma}(\lambda),\nu(\lambda);\,\epsilon^{-1}{\gamma}_{*}}(e^{x},\,e^{\epsilon y_{*}})\Big)\Big|_{y_{*}=x_{\ell+1}\atop{\gamma}_{*}=\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{\ell+1}}\,,\end{array}

where
{}^{\rm res}\widehat{\Phi}
is obtained by imposing the
restrictions (
5.1
). Thus
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-Whittaker
function belongs to a partial compactification of the space of
GG-hypergeometric functions.

5.2
Maximal parabolic
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-Whittaker function

Recall from
[
GLO4
]
the definition of generalized Whittaker
function associated with maximal
parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{1,\ell+1}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}

(called the
(1;\ell+1)
-Whittaker function
in
[
GLO4
]
). To the maximal parabolic subalgebra

\mathfrak{p}_{(1,\ell+1)}
we associate the following decomposition of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}\,=\,{\mathfrak{h}}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}\,\oplus\,{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}\,,\end{array}

where
{\mathfrak{n}}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{-}\subset{\mathfrak{b}}_{-}
is the

\ell(\ell+1)/2
-dimensional subalgebra generated by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{E}_{\ell+1,\,1}\,;\qquad\mathcal{E}_{m+1,\,m}\,,\quad\mathcal{E}_{m+1,\,m+1}\,,\quad 1\leq m<\ell\,.\end{array}

and
\mathfrak{h}^{(1,\ell+1)}
is the
(\ell+1)
-dimensional
commutative subalgebra spanned by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{1}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}=\mathcal{E}_{11}\,,\qquad h_{2}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}=\mathcal{E}_{22}+\ldots+\mathcal{E}_{\ell+1,\,\ell+1}\,,\\
h_{m+1}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}=\mathcal{E}_{\ell+1,\,m}\,,\quad 1<m\leq\ell\,.\end{array}

For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell+1}
, let

(\rho_{\lambda},\,\mathcal{W}_{\lambda})
be the principal series representation of

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1})
. Then we choose the appropriately defined dual
module
(\rho^{\vee}_{\lambda},\mathcal{W}^{\vee}_{\lambda})
. Given the
decomposition (
5.2
), we introduce the pair of vectors

\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}\in\mathcal{W}^{\vee}_{\lambda}
and
\psi_{R}\in\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
to be
the generic characters of the subalgebras
{\mathfrak{n}}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{-}
and

{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
, where
{\mathfrak{n}}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{-}
is generated by (
5.2
)
(see
[
GLO4
]
,
[
O
]
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho^{\vee}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell+1,\,1})\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}\,=\,-(-1)^{\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2}}\,\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}\,,\\
\rho^{\vee}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{E}_{21})\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}=\rho^{\vee}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{E}_{m+1,\,m})\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}=0\,,\quad 1<m<\ell\,;\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho_{\lambda}(\mathcal{E}_{i,\,i+1})\psi_{R}\,=\,-\psi_{R}\,,\qquad 1\leq i\leq\ell\,.\end{array}

We imply the existence of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
-invariant non-degenerate
pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle
between the submodules

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1})\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{\vee}_{\lambda}

and
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1})\psi_{R}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
. Then the maximal
parabolic Whittaker function is defined by the following matrix
element:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi_{\lambda}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}(e^{x_{1}},e^{x_{2}},\ldots,e^{x_{\ell+1}})\,=\,e^{x_{1}-x_{\ell+1}}\langle\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)},\,\rho_{\lambda}\Big(e^{\sum_{j}\,x_{j}h^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{j}}\Big)\psi_{R}\rangle\,.\end{array}

The expression (
5.2
) implies that the action of the part
of subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}
may be integrated to the
action of the corresponding group. In the following we consider the
restricted maximal parabolic Whittaker function

[
GLO4
]
,
[
O
]
, for
{\rm Re}(\lambda_{i})>0,\,1\leq i\leq\ell+1

given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi_{\lambda}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}(e^{x})\,:=\,\Psi_{\lambda}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}(e^{x},1\,\ldots,1)\,=\,e^{x}\langle\psi_{L}^{(1;\,\ell+1)},\,\rho_{\lambda}(e^{x\,\mathcal{E}_{11}})\psi_{R}\rangle\\
=e^{\lambda_{\ell+1}x}\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell}}\!\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{\ell+1}}\,e^{-t_{i}}\,e^{-e^{x}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}t_{i}^{-1}}\\
=\,\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1}}\!\!\prod_{i=1}^{\ell+1}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}\,e^{-t_{i}}\,\delta\Big(e^{-x}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\ell+1}t_{i}\,-1\,\Big)\,.\end{array}

Therefore, we only shall assume that the action of the generator

h_{1}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}=\mathcal{E}_{11}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}
integrates to the
action of the corresponding one-parameter subgroup in

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
.

To put the restricted maximal parabolic Whittaker function
(
5.2
) into the framework of GG-hyper- geometric
functions, consider the following instance of GKZ-system with

|J|=1
,
|I|=N=\ell+1,\,m=\ell
and the following matrices

A\in{\rm Mat}_{\ell\times(\ell+1)}(\mathbb{Z})
and

M\in{\rm Mat}_{1\times(\ell+1)}(\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}M=(1\ldots 1)\,,\qquad A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1&0&\ldots&0\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\ldots&0&1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right),\\
A^{\top}=({\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\,,\qquad{\alpha}_{j}=\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{j+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1},\qquad MA^{\top}=0.\end{array}

Here
\{\epsilon_{j}:\,j\in I\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\ell+1}
is the standard basis.
Let
{\mathfrak{L}}_{\ell+1}
be the Lie algebra (
3
), and for

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell+1},\,{\rm Re}(\lambda_{i})>0
, let
\pi_{\lambda}
be the
\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{L}}_{\ell+1})
-module

\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
(
3
) modeled on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1})
. Vector

\phi_{R}\in\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
and covector
\phi_{L}\in\mathcal{V}^{\vee}_{\lambda}
read
from (
3.1
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}(t)\,=\,\prod_{i\in I}e^{-t_{i}}\,,\qquad\langle\phi_{L},\,\varphi\rangle\,=\,\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1}}\!\!\!\prod_{i\in I}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}\,\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}t_{j}\,-\,1\Big)\,\varphi(t)\,,\end{array}

for an arbitrary test function
\varphi(t)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1})
.
Then the matrix element (
3.1
) in the

{\mathfrak{L}}_{\ell+1}
-representation
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
affords the following
integral representation:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{y_{1}},\ldots,e^{y_{\ell+1}})\,=\,\bigl\langle\phi_{L},\,\pi_{\lambda}\Big(e^{\sum_{i}y_{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}}\Big)\phi_{R}\bigr\rangle\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}=\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1}}\!\!\prod_{i\in I}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}\,e^{\lambda_{i}y_{i}\,-\,t_{i}e^{y_{i}}}\,\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}t_{j}\,-\,1\Big)\\
=\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1}}\!\!\prod_{i\in I}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}\,e^{-\,t_{i}}\,\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}e^{-y_{j}}t_{j}\,-\,1\Big)\,.\end{array}

Let us introduce variables
u_{i}:=e^{y_{i}},\,i\in I
and write
(
5.2
) as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell+1})\,=\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1}}\!\!\prod_{i\in I}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}\,e^{-\,t_{i}}\,\delta\Big(\prod_{j\in I}u_{j}^{-1}t_{j}\,-\,1\Big)\,.\end{array}

By the results of Section 2, (or via a simple direct
computation), the GG-hypergeometric function

\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)
satisfies the following instances of
equations (
(1)
)-(
(4)
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\prod_{i\in I}\Big(-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{u_{i}}\Big)\cdot\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,=\,\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,;\\
\prod_{i\in I}e^{\partial_{\lambda_{i}}}\cdot\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,=\,\prod_{i\in I}u_{i}\,\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,;\\
\Big(u_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}-u_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}\Big)\cdot\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,=\,0\,,\qquad i\neq j\,;\\
\Big\{e^{\partial_{\lambda_{i}}}\,-\,e^{\partial_{\lambda_{j}}}\Big\}\cdot\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,=\,0\,,\qquad i\neq j\,,\end{array}

and the following instance of (
2.3
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Big\{-u_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\,+\,\lambda_{i}\Big\}\cdot\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,=\,e^{\partial_{\lambda_{i}}}\cdot\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,.\end{array}

The first line of equations in (
5.2
) can be written in the
following form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Big\{\prod_{i\in I}\Big(-u_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}+\lambda_{i}\Big)\,-\,\prod_{i\in I}u_{i}\Big\}\cdot\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)\,=\,0\,,\end{array}

As a consequence of the third line of equations in (
5.2
),
the function
\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(u)

reduces to a function in one variable

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{y_{1}},\ldots,e^{y_{\ell+1}})=\widetilde{\Phi}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{x})\,,\qquad x=y_{1}+\ldots+y_{\ell+1},\end{array}

satisfying the reduced form of (
5.2
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Big\{\prod_{i\in I}\Big(-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\lambda_{i}\Big)\,-\,e^{x}\Big\}\cdot\widetilde{\Phi}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda}(e^{x})\,=\,0\,.\end{array}

Indeed, the equation (
5.2
) is a simple consequence of the
first line in (
5.2
). By the third equation in
(
5.2
), the function (
5.2
) depends on a single
variable
x=y_{1}+\ldots+y_{\ell+1}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{y_{1}},\ldots,e^{y_{\ell+1}})\,=\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1}}\!\!\prod_{i\in I}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}\,e^{-\,t_{i}}\,\delta\Big(e^{-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\ell+1}y_{j}}\prod_{j\in I}t_{j}\,-\,1\Big)\,.\end{array}

Thus we may introduce the function

\widetilde{\Phi}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{x})
, which by
(
5.2
) has the following integral representation:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\widetilde{\Phi}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{x})\,:=\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ell+1}}\!\!\prod_{i\in I}\frac{dt_{i}}{t_{i}}\,t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}\,e^{-\,t_{i}}\,\delta\Big(e^{-x}\prod_{j\in I}t_{j}\,-\,1\Big)\,,\end{array}

and satisfies the equation (
5.2
) by construction.

Finally, we may identify the maximal parabolic Whittaker function
\Psi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{x})
defined by
(
5.2
) with the reduced GG-hypergeometric function
(
5.2
) as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{x})\,=\,\widetilde{\Phi}^{(1;\,\ell+1)}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell+1}}(e^{x})\,.\end{array}

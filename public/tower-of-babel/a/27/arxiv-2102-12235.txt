Cohomology, Extensions and Automorphisms of Skew Braces
Nishant, Manoj K. Yadav

Abstract
. The second cohomology group of a left skew brace with coefficients in a trivial left brace with non-trivial actions is defined, its connection with extensions of a left skew brace by a trivial braces is established and a Wells’ like exact sequence relating the second cohomology group with inducible automorphisms of an extension of left skew braces is constructed.

1.
Introduction

Classification of set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, proposed by Drinfeld
[
14
]
, is a wide open problem.
In 2007, Rump
[
26
]
introduced the notion of braces in connection with involutive non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. This concept was generalised by Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
16
]
to the concept of skew braces to take into account non-involutive non-degenerate solutions too. An algebraic structure
(E,+,\circ)
is said to be a left skew brace if
(E,+)
and
(E,\circ)
are groups and, for all
a,b,c\in E
, the following compatibility condition holds:

a\circ(b+c)=(a\circ b)-a+(a\circ c).

A right skew brace can be defined analogously. In the present article, we shall consider only left skew braces. A left skew brace is called a left brace if
(E,+)
is an abelian group. The present definition of a left brace, which is slightly different but equivalent to the one given by Rump, is due to Cedo, Jespers and Onkinski
[
9
]
.

As it is well known now (see
[
9
]
), a left brace gives rise to a non-degenerate involutive set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation (and vice-versa). There is also a bijection between left skew braces and non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation (see
[
16
]
). Not only this, braces have deep connections with many other algebraic structures; linear cycle sets, radical rings, Hopf-Galois extensions, bijective
1
-cocycles, to name some. So each new construction of a left (skew) brace contributes to the solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation and enriches the class of other related structures. Constructing new algebraic structures from the existing ones is always an important and interesting problem. So is the case for skew braces. We mention some such constructions. Notion of semidirect product of braces was introduced in
[
27
]
, which was generalised to asymmetric product of braces in
[
11
]
. Asymmetric product of braces was further studied in
[
3
]
, where the authors also studied the wreath product of braces. Matched product of braces was investigated in
[
1
]
. Iterated matched product of braces was taken up in
[
2
]
. Matched product of skew braces was studied in
[
12
,
13
]
.

Our interest in this note is the cohomology and extension theory of skew braces. Extension theory on the one hand provides new constructions of the skew braces and on the other hand is expected to play a fundamental role in the classification of braces and skew braces. Vendramin
[
19
]
studied dynamical extensions of finite cycle sets and its relationship with dynamical co-cycles. The topic was further studied by Castelli, Catino, Miccoli and Pinto
[
8
]
for quasi-linear cycle sets. Dynamical extensions give rise to non-degenerate involutive multipermutation solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. Extensions of bijective
1
-cocycles were investigated by Ben David and Ginosar
[
5
]
. Homology and cohomology theories for solution sets of the Yang-Baxter equations were developed by Carter, Elhambadi and Saito
[
7
]
. Different homology theories for various structures related to solutions of the Yang-Baxter equations were extensively investigated by Lebel and Vendramin
[
21
]
. They also defined second cohomology group for a cycle set
X
with coefficients in an abelian group
A
and established a bijective correspondence between the second cohomology group and equivalence classes of extensions of
X
by
A
.

The present article is based on several further advances in this direction: (a) A general homology and cohomology theory was developed for linear cycle sets and left braces by Lebed and Vendramin
[
20
]
with trivial actions, where the authors extensively explored close connections between the second cohomology of linear cycle sets and extension theory. (J.A. Guccione and J.J. Guccione
[
15
]
further investigated the ideas of
[
20
]
and used ‘perturbation lemma’ for computing first and second cohomology groups and extensions of cyclic linear cycles set acting trivially on an abelian group). (b) Bachiller
[
1
]
considered extensions of left braces with non-trivial actions and characterised equivalent extension in the form of certain pairs of maps and relations among them. It turns out that these pairs of maps generalise the 2-cocycles from (a).

We develop extension theory for skew braces, define the second cohomology group of a skew brace acting on a trivial brace with non-trivial actions and establish connections between these. This generalises results from (a) and (b). We then present an exact sequence connecting automorphism groups of skew braces with second cohomology group.
Let
H:=(H,+,\circ)
be a left skew brace and
I:=(I,+)
an abelian group viewed as a trivial left brace. Suppose that
(H,\circ)
acts on
(I,+)
from left by an action
\nu
and from right by actons
\mu
and
\sigma
. The images of

in
\operatorname{Aut}(I,+)
, the automorphism group of the group
(I,+)
, under
\nu
,

and

are denoted by

,

and

\displaystyle\mu_{-h_{1}+(h_{1}\circ h_{3})}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nu_{h_{1}\circ(h_{2}+h_{3})}(\sigma_{h_{2}+h_{3}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y)))+\mu_{-h_{1}+(h_{1}\circ h_{3})}(\nu_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}(\sigma_{h_{2}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y))))

\displaystyle+\;\nu_{h_{1}\circ h_{3}}(\sigma_{h_{3}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y)))

\displaystyle\mu_{-h_{1}+(h_{1}\circ h_{3})}(\nu_{h_{1}}(y))

\displaystyle=

Theorem A
.

Let

\operatorname{H}_{N}^{2}(H,I)

the set of all brace automorphisms of

which normalize

is a subgroup of

. Let

\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}:=\{(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)\mid\nu_{h}=\theta^{-1}\nu_{\phi(h)}\theta,\mu_{h}=\theta^{-1}\mu_{\phi(h)}\theta\mbox{ and }\sigma_{h}=\theta^{-1}\sigma_{\phi(h)}\theta\}.

Our next result is an analog for skew braces of an exact sequence, called the fundamental exact sequence of Wells
[
28

, relating derivations, automorphisms and cohomology group of groups. For unexplained symbols in the following result see Section 5.

Theorem B
.

Let

where
\omega(\mathcal{E})
is, in general, only a derivation.

[
17
,
18
,
22
,
25
]
. The exposition which we present here is analogous to the one in
[
18
]
. A unified treatment of the fundamental exact sequence of Wells with various applications is carried out in all fine details in
[
23
, Chapter 2]
. A similar exact sequence for cohomology, extensions and automorphisms of quandles was constructed in
[
4
]
.

We close this section with a quick layout of the article. Section 2 contains basic definitions and observations on left skew braces. In Section 3 we define the second cohomology group, develop extension theory of left skew braces and establish connection between these two, which enables us to prove Theorem A (as Theorem
3.6
). An explicit construction is carried out for left braces of order
8
. In Section 4, generalising some ideas developed in
[
20
]
, we define general cohomology theory of left skew braces in a particular case, but with non-trivial actions. The last section deals with automorphisms of skew braces, where various actions are exhibited which prepare a road map for the proof of Theorem B (as Theorem
5.7
). Finally, an application of Theorem B is exhibited, inducibility of a pair of automorphisms of skew braces is discussed in module theoretic language and a reduction argument for lifting and extension of automorphisms of nilpotent skew braces is presented.

2.
Preliminaries

An algebraic structure
(E,+,\circ)
is said to be a
left skew brace
if
(E,+)
and
(E,\circ)
are a group and, the following compatibility condition holds:

(1)

a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c.

for all
a,b,c\in E
.
Notice that the identity element
0
of
(E,+)
coincides with the identity element of
(E,\circ)
. A right skew brace can be defined analogously. In this article we’ll only consider left skew braces and may take the liberty of using ‘skew brace’ for ‘left skew brace’ at various places.

For a left skew brace
E
and
a\in E
, define a map
\lambda_{a}:E\to E
by

\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+(a\circ b)

for all
b\in E
. The automorphism group of a group
G
is denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. We have the following result which was proved by Rump
[
26
]
in linear cycle set settings.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
a\in E
, the map
\lambda_{a}
is an automorphism of
(E,+)
and the map
\lambda:(E,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(E,+)
given by
\lambda(a)=\lambda_{a}
is a group homomorphism.

A sub skew brace
I
of a left skew brace
E
is said to be a
left ideal
of
E
if
\lambda_{a}(y)\in I
for all
a\in E
and
y\in I
. A left ideal of
E
is said to be an
ideal
if
(I,\circ)
is a normal subgroup of
(E,\circ)
. An ideal
I
of
E
is said to be
central
if
y\circ a=a\circ y=a+y
for all
a\in A
and
y\in I
.

The following is an easy but important observation, which will be used several times in what follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
E
be a left skew brace. Then for all
a,b\in E
, the following hold:

(i)
a+b=a\circ\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b)
.

(ii)
a\circ b=a+\lambda_{a}(b)
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two left skew braces. A map
f:E_{1}\to E_{2}
is said to be a
brace homomorphism
if
f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b)
and
f(a\circ b)=f(a)\circ f(b)
for all
a,b\in E_{1}
. A one-to-one and onto brace homomorphism from
E_{1}
to itself is called an
automorphism
of
E_{1}
. The
kernel
of a homomorphism
f:E_{1}\to E_{2}
is defined to be the subset
\{a\in E_{1}\mid f(a)=0\}
of
E_{1}
. It turns out that
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
, the kernel of
f
, is an ideal of
E_{1}
. The set of all brace automorphisms of a left skew brace
E
, denoted by
\operatorname{Autb}(E)
, is a group.

Let

and

, we mean a left skew brace

Let

The set of all equivalence classes of extensions of

\displaystyle(\partial^{0,2}_{h}(g)-\partial^{1,1}_{v}(f))(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})

\displaystyle\nu_{h_{1}}(\mu_{h_{3}}(\theta(h_{2})))-\mu_{-h_{1}+(h_{1}\circ h_{3})}(\nu_{h_{1}}(\theta(h_{2})))

Let

\operatorname{B}_{N}^{2}(H,I):=\operatorname{Im}(\partial^{1})

Elements of

and

and

-cocycles

We remark that when

is defined by

such that

The next result follows from the definitions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(g,f)\in C_{N}^{2}
. Then, for all

, the following hold:

(i)
g(h_{1},0)=g(0,h_{2})=0
.

(ii)
f(h_{1},0)=f(0,h_{2})=0
.

Let
(\beta,\tau)\in C_{N}^{2}
. Define on

, the following operations:

(4)

\displaystyle(h_{1},y_{1})+(h_{2},y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h_{1}+h_{2},\mu_{h_{2}}(y_{1})+y_{2}+\beta(h_{1},h_{2})\big).

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h_{1}\circ h_{2},\nu_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}(\sigma_{h_{2}}(\nu_{h_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1})))+\nu_{h_{1}}(y_{2})+\tau(h_{1},h_{2})\big).

Then we have

be a good triplet of actions of

on

. Then

Proof.

It is not difficult to show that the operations

\displaystyle(h_{1},y_{1})\circ((h_{2},y_{2})+(h_{3},y_{3}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h_{1},y_{1})\circ(h_{2}+h_{3},\mu_{h_{3}}(y_{2})+y_{3}+\beta(h_{2},h_{3}))

\displaystyle(h_{1}\circ(h_{2}+h_{3}),\nu_{h_{1}\circ(h_{2}+h_{3})}(\sigma_{h_{2}+h_{3}}(\nu_{h_{1}}^{-1}(y_{1}))))

\displaystyle(s(h_{1})+y)\circ\big(s(h_{2})+s(h_{3})\big)

\displaystyle=

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>I@>{i}>{}>(H,I,\nu,\mu,\sigma,\beta_{1},\tau_{1})@>{{\pi}}>{}>H@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{{\text{Id}}}V{}V@V{{\phi}}V{}V@V{\text{Id}}V{}V\\

Notice that the linearity of
\phi

follows from the equations (

is a brace homomorphism. It is now obvious that the preceding diagram is commutative. Thus the extensions
(H,I,\nu,\mu,\sigma,\beta_{1},\tau_{1})

[\mathcal{E}]\mapsto[\mathcal{E}^{(\phi,\theta)}].

If
\phi
and

[\mathcal{E}]^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}=\big([\mathcal{E}]^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}\big)^{(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}.

We conclude that the association (
15

\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)
on the set
\operatorname{Ext}(H,I)
.

As we know that
\operatorname{Ext}(H,I)=\bigsqcup_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)
.
Let
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
be an arbitrary but fixed good triplet of actions of
H
on

Let
\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
denote the stabiliser of

in

\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}=\{(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)\mid\nu_{h}=\theta^{-1}\nu_{\phi(h)}\theta,\mu_{h}=\theta^{-1}\mu_{\phi(h)}\theta\mbox{ and }\sigma_{h}=\theta^{-1}\sigma_{\phi(h)}\theta\}.

Notice that
\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}

\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)

\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)

Next we consider an action of
\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
on
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I)

It is not difficult to see that the group
\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)

\operatorname{Fun}(H^{n},I)

C_{N}^{i,j}(H,I)

(16)

f\mapsto f^{(\phi,\theta)}.

It is also obvious that
\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}

\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}

\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}

(\beta,\tau)\mapsto\big(\beta^{(\phi,\theta)},\tau^{(\phi,\theta)}\big).

Lemma 5.1
.

For
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
, the following hold:

(i) If
(\beta,\tau)\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}_{N}(H,I)
, then
\big(\beta^{(\phi,\theta)},\tau^{(\phi,\theta)}\big)\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}_{N}(H,I)

(ii) If
(\beta,\tau)\in\operatorname{B}^{2}_{N}(H,I)

\big(\beta^{(\phi,\theta)},\tau^{(\phi,\theta)}\big)\in\operatorname{B}^{2}_{N}(H,I)
.

Hence the association (
17

\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
on
\operatorname{H}_{N}^{2}(H,I)
by automorphisms if we define

[(\beta,\tau)]^{(\phi,\theta)}=[\big(\beta^{(\phi,\theta)},\tau^{(\phi,\theta)}\big)].

Proof.

That
\partial^{0,2}_{v}\big(\beta^{(\phi,\theta)}\big)
is a zero function in
\operatorname{C}_{N}^{3}
holds trivially. Since
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
, we have

\nu_{h_{1}}(\theta^{-1}(\tau(\phi(h_{2}),\phi(h_{3}))))=\theta^{-1}\big(\nu_{\phi(h_{1})}(\tau(\phi(h_{2}),\phi(h_{3})))\big)

\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
on
\operatorname{H}_{N}^{2}(H,I)
by automorphisms, is now straighforward, which completes the proof.

Let

\mathcal{E}:0\rightarrow I\rightarrow E\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}H

\nu_{h}=\gamma_{I}^{-1}\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h)}\gamma_{I}

\mu_{h}=\gamma_{I}^{-1}\mu_{\gamma_{H}(h)}\gamma_{I}

\gamma^{-1}

\displaystyle h,

\displaystyle\circ\;\sigma_{(h_{1}\circ h_{2})^{-1}}\big(\nu^{-1}_{h_{2}}(y^{\gamma}_{h_{2}})\big)\circ s(h_{1})\circ s(h_{2}),

which, using
(y^{\gamma}_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}})^{-1}=-y^{\gamma}_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}
, implies that

\sigma_{(h_{1}\circ h_{2})^{-1}}\big(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}(y^{\gamma}_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}})\big)=\sigma_{h_{1}^{-1}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y^{\gamma}_{h_{1}}))+\sigma_{(h_{1}\circ h_{2})^{-1}}\big(\nu^{-1}_{h_{2}}(y^{\gamma}_{h_{2}})\big).

This, on further simplification, finally gives

y^{\gamma}_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}=\nu_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}\big(\sigma_{h_{2}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y^{\gamma}_{h_{1}}))\big)+\nu_{h_{1}}(y^{\gamma}_{h_{2}}).

For
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
, it easily follows that

y_{h_{1}+h_{2}}^{\gamma}=\mu_{h_{2}}(y_{h_{1}}^{\gamma})+y_{h_{2}}^{\gamma}.

We have now proved that
\zeta(\gamma)
is a derivation. That both
\eta
and
\zeta
are homomorphisms, and
\eta\zeta
and
\zeta\eta
are, respectively, the identity elements of
\operatorname{Autb}^{H,I}(E)
and
\operatorname{Z}^{1}_{N}(H,I)
is obvious. Hence
\operatorname{Autb}^{H,I}(E)\cong\operatorname{Z}^{1}_{N}(H,I)
, and the proof is complete.
\Box

∎

We finally get the following Wells’ like exact sequence for skew braces.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:0\rightarrow I\rightarrow E\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}H
be an extension of a left skew brace
H
by a trivial brace
I
such that
[\mathcal{E}]\in\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)
. Then we have the following exact sequence of groups

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Z}^{1}_{N}(H,I)\rightarrow\operatorname{Autb}_{I}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho(\mathcal{E})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega(\mathcal{E})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I),

where
\omega(\mathcal{E})
is, in general, only a derivation.

A pair of brace automorphisms
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)
is said to be
inducible
if
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Im}(\rho(\mathcal{E}))
. As a straightforward application of Theorem
5.7
, we get

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:0\rightarrow I\rightarrow E\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}H
be an extension of a left skew brace
H
by a trivial brace
I
such that
[\mathcal{E}]\in\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)
. If
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I)
is a trivial group, then every element of
\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is inducible.

Let
\mathcal{E}:0\rightarrow I\rightarrow E\rightarrow H\rightarrow 0
be an extension of a left skew brace
H
by a trivial brace
I
. Then
I
can be viewed as an
H
-module through the corresponding good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
as defined above. An automorphism
\phi
of the skew brace
H
defines a new
H
-module structure on
I
given by
(\nu\phi,\mu\phi,\sigma\phi)
, which we denote by
I_{\phi}
, where
\nu\phi(h)=\nu_{\phi(h)}
,
\mu\phi(h)(y)=-s(\phi(h))+y+s(\phi(h))
and
\sigma\phi(h)(y)=s(\phi(h))^{-1}ys(\phi(h))
for
h\in H
and
y\in I
. It is not difficult to show that the automorphism
\phi
induces an isomorphism
\phi^{*}
of cohomology groups

\phi^{*}:\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I_{\phi})

defined by

\phi^{*}([(\beta,\tau)])=[\big(\beta^{(\phi,\operatorname{Id})},\tau^{(\phi,\operatorname{Id})}\big)].

Further, any
H
-module isomorphism
\theta:I\rightarrow I_{\phi}
induces an isomorphism
\theta^{*}
of cohomology groups

\theta^{*}:\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I_{\phi})

given by

\theta^{*}([(\beta,\tau)])=[\big(\beta^{(\operatorname{Id},\theta^{-1})},\tau^{(\operatorname{Id},\theta^{-1})}\big)].

With this set-up, we have

Theorem 5.9
.

A pair of automorphisms
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)
is inducible if and only if the following conditions hold:

(i)
\theta:I\rightarrow I_{\phi}
is an isomorphism of
H
-modules.

(ii)
\theta^{*}([(\beta,\tau)])=\phi^{*}([(\beta,\tau)])
.

Proof.

Let
(\phi,\theta)
be inducible. Then there exists an automorphism
\gamma\in\operatorname{Autb}_{I}(E)
such that
(\phi,\theta)=(\gamma_{H},\gamma_{I})
. For
h\in H
and
y\in I
, we know that
\gamma(s(h))=s(\gamma_{H}(h))\circ y_{h}
for some
y_{h}\in I
. We have

(21)

\displaystyle\gamma_{I}(\nu_{h}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\big(-s(h)+s(h)\circ y\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma(s(h))+\gamma(s(h))\circ\gamma(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(s(\gamma_{H}(h))\circ y_{h})+s(\gamma_{H}(h))\circ(y_{h}+\gamma(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-s(\gamma_{H}(h))+s(\gamma_{H}(h))\circ\gamma_{I}(y)\ \ \ \mbox{(using \eqref{bcomp})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h)}(\gamma_{I}(y)).

Similarly it also follows that

(22)

\gamma_{I}(\sigma_{h}(y))=\sigma_{\gamma_{H}(h)}(\gamma_{I}(y))

and

(23)

\gamma_{I}(\mu_{h}(y))=\mu_{\gamma_{H}(h)}(\gamma_{I}(y)).

This shows that
(\operatorname{Id},\gamma_{I})
is compatible with the pairs of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
and
(\nu\gamma_{H},\mu\gamma_{H},\sigma\gamma_{H})
; hence condition (i) holds.

For each
h\in H
, there exists a unique element (say)
\lambda(h)
in
I
such that
\gamma(s(h))=s(\gamma_{H}(h))+\lambda(h)
. It turns out that
\lambda:H\rightarrow I
given by
h\mapsto\lambda(h)
lies in
C_{N}^{1}
. Given elements
x_{1},x_{2}\in E
have unique expressions of the form
x_{1}=s(h_{1})+y_{1}
and
x_{2}=s(h_{2})+y_{2}
for some
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
and
y_{1}
and
y_{2}\in I
. Now

\displaystyle\gamma(x_{1}+x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\big(s(h_{1}+h_{2})+\beta(h_{1},h_{2})+\mu_{h_{2}}(y_{1})+y_{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(s(h_{1}+h_{2}))+\gamma_{I}(\beta(h_{1},h_{2}))+\gamma_{I}(\mu_{h_{2}}(y_{1})+y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\big(\gamma_{H}(h_{1})+\gamma_{H}(h_{2})\big)+\lambda(h_{1}+h_{2})+\gamma_{I}(\beta(h_{1},h_{2}))+\gamma_{I}(\mu_{h_{2}}(y_{1})+y_{2}).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\gamma(x_{1})+\gamma(x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}))+\lambda(h_{1})+\gamma_{I}(y_{1})+s(\gamma_{H}(h_{2}))+\lambda(h_{2})+\gamma_{I}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}))+s(\gamma_{H}(h_{2}))+\mu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\lambda(h_{1}))+\mu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\gamma_{I}(y_{1}))+\lambda(h_{2})+\gamma_{I}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\gamma_{H}(h_{1})+\gamma_{H}(h_{2}))+\beta(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}),\gamma_{H}(h_{2}))+\mu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\lambda(h_{1}))

\displaystyle+\;\mu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\gamma_{I}(y_{1}))+\lambda(h_{2})+\gamma_{I}(y_{2}).

Since
\gamma(x_{1}+x_{2})=\gamma(x_{1})+\gamma(x_{2})
, preceding two equations, using (
23
), give

(24)

\gamma_{I}(\beta(h_{1},h_{2}))-\beta(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}),\gamma_{H}(h_{2}))=\mu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\lambda(h_{1}))-\lambda(h_{1}+h_{2})+\lambda(h_{2}).

Notice that
s(h)+\lambda(h)=s(h)\circ\nu^{-1}_{h}(\lambda(h))
and
s(h)\circ\lambda(h)=s(h)+\nu_{h}(\lambda(h))
. By this and using (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
) we have

\displaystyle\gamma(x_{1}\circ x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\big((s(h_{1})+y_{1})\circ((s(h_{2})+y_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\big(s(h_{1}\circ h_{2})\circ\big(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}(\tau(h_{1},h_{2}))+\sigma_{h_{2}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y_{1}))+\nu^{-1}_{h_{2}}(y_{2})\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\big(s(h_{1}\circ h_{2})+\nu_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}(\sigma_{h_{2}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y_{1})))+\nu_{h_{1}}(y_{2})+\tau(h_{1},h_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}\circ h_{2}))+\lambda(h_{1}\circ h_{2})+\gamma_{I}(\nu_{h_{1}\circ h_{2}}(\sigma_{h_{2}}(\nu^{-1}_{h_{1}}(y_{1}))))+\gamma_{I}(\nu_{h_{1}}(y_{2}))+\gamma_{I}(\tau(h_{1},h_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}\circ h_{2}))+\lambda(h_{1}\circ h_{2})+\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1}\circ h_{2})}(\sigma_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\nu^{-1}_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\gamma_{I}(y_{1}))))

\displaystyle+\;\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\gamma_{I}(y_{2}))+\gamma_{I}(\tau(h_{1},h_{2})).

By similar computations, on the other hand, we get

\displaystyle\gamma(x_{1})\circ\gamma(x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}\circ h_{2}))+\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1}\circ h_{2})}(\sigma_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\nu^{-1}_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\gamma_{I}(y_{1}))))

\displaystyle+\;\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1}\circ h_{2})}(\sigma_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\nu^{-1}_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\lambda(h_{1}))))+\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\gamma_{I}(y_{2}))

\displaystyle+\;\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\lambda(h_{2}))+\tau(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}),\gamma_{H}(h_{2})).

Preceding two equations give

\gamma_{I}(\tau(h_{1},h_{2}))-\tau(\gamma_{H}(h_{1}),\gamma_{H}(h_{2}))=\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1}\circ h_{2})}(\sigma_{\gamma_{H}(h_{2})}(\nu^{-1}_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\lambda(h_{1}))))-\lambda(h_{1}\circ h_{2})+\nu_{\gamma_{H}(h_{1})}(\lambda(h_{2})),

which, along with (
24
) proves condition (ii), that is,
\gamma_{I}^{*}([(\beta,\tau)])=\gamma_{H}^{*}([(\beta,\tau)])
.

Conversely, let
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Autb}(H)\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)
satisfy conditions (i) and (ii). Condition (ii) guarantees the existence of a map
\lambda:H\rightarrow I
in
C_{N}^{1}
such that

\big(\beta^{(\phi,\operatorname{Id})},\tau^{(\phi,\operatorname{Id})}\big)-\big(\beta^{(\operatorname{Id},\theta^{-1})},\tau^{(\operatorname{Id},\theta^{-1})}\big)=\partial^{1}(\lambda).

Define the map
\gamma:E\rightarrow E
, for all
h\in H
and
y\in I
, by

\gamma(s(h)+y)=s(\phi(h))+\lambda(h)+\theta(y).

A routine check then shows that
\gamma\in\operatorname{Autb}_{I}(E)
,
\gamma_{H}=\phi
and
\gamma_{I}=\theta
. The proof is now complete.
\Box

∎

We conclude with a reduction argument on extension and lifting problem for automorphisms of skew braces of nilpotent type, i.e.,
(H,+)
is nilpotent. We’ll only deal with brace extensions from
\operatorname{STExt}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)
, with
H
of finite order, for a given good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
of
H
on
I
. Let us start with the following fixed extension of a finite left skew brace
H
by a trivial brace
I
lying in
\operatorname{STExt}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)
:

\mathcal{E}:0\to I\rightarrow E\rightarrow H\to 0.

For this extension
\mathcal{E}
, by Theorem
5.5
, we get the exact sequence of groups

(25)

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Autb}^{H,I}(E)\rightarrow\operatorname{Autb}_{I}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho(\mathcal{E})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega(\mathcal{E})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(H,I),

where
\omega(\mathcal{E})
is a derivation.

Let
P_{i}
denote a Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup of
(H,\circ)
, where
p_{i}
is a prime divisor of the order of
H
. Notice that
P_{i}
is also the Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup of
(H,+)
; hence,
H
being nilpotent type,
P_{i}
becomes a left ideal of
H
(
[
10
, Lemma 4.10]
). Let
R_{i}
denote the pre-image of
P_{i}
in
E
. Notice that
(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})
is a good triplet of actions of
P_{i}
on
I
, where
\nu^{i}:=\nu|_{P_{i}}
,
\mu^{i}:=\mu|_{P_{i}}
and
\sigma^{i}:=\sigma|_{P_{i}}
, and the extension

\mathcal{E}_{i}:0\to I\to R_{i}\to P_{i}\to 0

lies in
\operatorname{STExt}_{(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})}(H,I)
.
Set

\operatorname{C}_{(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})}:=\{(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Autb}(P_{i})\times\operatorname{Autb}(I)\mid\nu^{i}_{h}=\theta^{-1}\nu^{i}_{\phi(h)}\theta,\mu^{i}_{h}=\theta^{-1}\mu^{i}_{\phi(h)}\theta\mbox{ and }\sigma^{i}_{h}=\theta^{-1}\sigma^{i}_{\phi(h)}\theta\}.

The extension
\mathcal{E}_{i}
now gives the exact sequence of groups

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Autb}^{P_{i},I}(R_{i})\rightarrow\operatorname{Autb}_{I}(R_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho(\mathcal{E}_{i})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{C}_{(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega(\mathcal{E}_{i})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(P_{i},I).

On the other hand, denote by
\operatorname{Autb}_{I,R_{i}}(E,I)
the subgroup of
\operatorname{Autb}_{I}(H,I)
consisting of all automorphism of
E
normalising
I
as well as
R_{i}
, and set
\operatorname{C}^{i}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}:=\{(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}\mid\phi(P_{i})=P_{i}\}
. Then for the extension

\mathcal{E}:0\to I\to E\to H\to 0,

we get the following exact sequence of groups from (
25
):

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Autb}^{H,I}(E)\rightarrow\operatorname{Autb}_{I,R_{i}}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho(\mathcal{E})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{C}^{i}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega(\mathcal{E})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(H,I).

Let
\operatorname{res}^{H}_{P_{i}}:\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(H,I)\to\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(P_{i},I)
be the restriction homomorphisms as defined in Corollary
4.5
. Define
r^{H}_{P_{i}}:\operatorname{C}^{i}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}\to\operatorname{C}_{(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})}
by

r^{H}_{P_{i}}(\phi,\theta)=(\phi|_{P_{i}},\theta).

Using the definition of
\omega(\mathcal{E})
, we now get the following commutative diagram:

(26)

\begin{CD}\operatorname{C}^{i}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}@>{\omega(\mathcal{E})}>{}>\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(H,I)\\
@V{}V{r^{H}_{P_{i}}}V@V{}V{\operatorname{res}^{H}_{P_{i}}}V\\
\operatorname{C}_{(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})}@>{\omega(\mathcal{E}_{i})}>{}>\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(P_{i},I).\end{CD}

Recall that
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((H,\circ),I)
denotes the second cohomology group of the group
(H,\circ)
with coefficients in
I
, where the right action of
H
on
I
is through
\sigma
. Similarly
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((P_{i},\circ),I)
denotes the second cohomology group of
(P_{i},\circ)
with coefficients in
I
, where the right action of
P_{i}
on
I
is through
\sigma^{i}=\sigma|_{P_{i}}
. By Proposition
4.3
there exist embeddings
\iota:\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(H,I)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((H,\circ),I)
and
\iota_{i}:\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(P_{i},I)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((P_{i},\circ),I)
. We now get the following commutative diagram:

(27)

\begin{CD}\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(H,I)@>{\iota}>{}>\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((H,\circ),I)\\
@V{}V{\operatorname{res}^{H}_{P_{i}}}V@V{}V{\operatorname{res}^{H}_{P_{i}}}V\\
\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(P_{i},I)@>{\iota_{i}}>{}>\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((P_{i},\circ),I).\end{CD}

Let
\pi(H):=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\}
be the set of all distinct prime divisors of the order of
H
. Please note the ad-hoc use of
\pi
. With this set-up, we finally have

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
be such that
(\phi|_{P_{i}},\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})}
is inducible for some Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup of
H
for each
p_{i}\in\pi(H)
. Then
(\phi,\theta)
is inducible.

Conversely, if
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is inducible, then
(\phi|_{P_{i}},\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu^{i},\mu^{i},\sigma^{i})}
is inducible whenever
\phi(P_{i})=P_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{C}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
be as in the statement and
\omega(\mathcal{E})(\phi,\theta)=[f]
. Then by the hypothesis and the commutativity of diagram (
26
), it follows that
\operatorname{res}^{H}_{P_{i}}([f])
is the identity element of
\operatorname{RH}^{2}_{N}(P_{i},I)
. Further, by the commutativity of diagram (
27
), we have
\operatorname{res}^{H}_{P_{i}}([f^{\prime}])
is the identity element of
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((P_{i},\circ),I)
. Being the cohomology of groups, we can now use corestriction-restriction homomorphism result
[
6
, (9.5) Proposition. (ii)]
to deduce that
k[f^{\prime}]
is the zero cohomology class of
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((H,\circ),I)
, where
k
is the index of
P_{i}
in
H
. Since this is true for at least one Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup for each
p_{i}\in\pi(H)
, it follows that
[f^{\prime}]
is the identity element of
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}((H,\circ),I)
, which,
\iota
being an embedding, implies that
[f]
is the zero cohomology class of
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I)
. Hence
(\phi,\theta)
is inducible.

The converse part is left as an exercise for the reader.
\Box

∎

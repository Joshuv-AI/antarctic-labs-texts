Certain Family of Some Beta Distributions Arising from Distribution of Randomly Weighted Average
Rasool Roozegar

Abstract
We give the exact distribution of the average of n n independent beta random variables weighted by the selected cuts of ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) by the order statistics of a random sample of size n − 1 n-1 from the uniform distribution U ⁡ ( 0 , 1 ) U(0,1) , for each n n . A new integral transformation that is similar to generalized Stieltjes transform is given with various properties. The result of Soltani and Roozegar [On distribution of randomly ordered uniform incremental weighted averages: Divided difference approach. Statist Probab Lett. 2012;82(5):1012–1020] with this new transform and also integral representation of the Gauss-hypergeometric function in some parts are employed to achieve the exact distribution. Several examples of the new family are investigated. Keywords and phrases: Selected order statistics; Beta distribution. Additive Stieltjes transform; Gauss-hypergeometric function; Randomly weighted average, exact distribution. 2010 AMS Subject Classification: 60E05, 46F12, 62E15.

Introduction

Let
U_{(1)}<U_{(2)}<\ldots<U_{(n-1)}
be order statistics based on a random sample of size
n-1
from the uniform distribution
U(0,1)
,
U_{(0)}=0
and
U_{(n)}=1
. Randomly weighted average (RWA) of independent and continuous random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
with respective distribution functions
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
, is defined by

S_{n}=R_{1}X_{1}+R_{2}X_{2}+\cdots+R_{n}X_{n},\ \ \ n\geq 2,

(1.1)

where the proportions
R_{i}=U_{(i)}-U_{(i-1)},

i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
R_{n}=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R_{i}
are random weights. Suppose that among the above whole order statistics, we select
k-1
order statistics
U_{(n_{1})}<U_{(n_{2})}<\ldots<U_{(n_{k-1})}
, where
k=k(n)
,
2‎\leq k‎\leq n‎‎
and
n_{0}=0<n_{1}<n_{2}<‎\cdots‎<n_{k-1}<n_{k}=n‎
. A general form to
S_{n}
will be the RWA of
k
independent and continuous random variables
X_{1},‎\ldots,X_{k}‎
, denoted by
S_{n:n_{1},‎\ldots,n_{k-1}‎}
, which is given by

S_{n:n_{1},‎\ldots,n_{k-1}‎}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}V_{j}X_{j},

(1.2)

where the random weights
V_{j}
are defined by

V_{j}=U_{(n_{j})}-U_{(n_{j-1})},\ \ \ \ j=1,2,‎\ldots,k.

In the above we have put the conventions
U_{(n_{0})}=0
,
U_{(n_{k})}=1
and
r_{j}=n_{j}-n_{j-1},\ j=1,2,‎\ldots,k‎
. Then
U_{(n_{j})}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}V_{i}
and
V_{j}=\sum_{i=n_{j-1}+1}^{n_{j}}R_{i}
.
The RWA on the form (1.2) was defined and studied by
[
7
]
. In fact the random vector
‎\textbf{V}‎=(V_{1},V_{2},‎\ldots,V_{k}‎)
has the Dirichlet distribution,
Dir(r_{1},r_{2},‎\ldots,r_{k}‎)
, with the probability density function (pdf)

f_{‎\textbf{V}‎}(v_{1},‎\ldots,v_{k}‎)=‎\dfrac{‎\Gamma‎(n)}{\prod‎_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma‎(r_{j})‎}‎\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}v_{j}^{r_{j}}(1-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}v_{j})^{r_{k}},

at any point in the canonical simplex
\{(v_{1},‎\ldots,v_{k}‎)|v_{i}‎\geq 0,\ i=1,2,‎\ldots,k,\ \sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}=1‎‎\}
in the
(k-1)
-dimensional real space
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
and zero outside.

To state assertions, we introduce beta distributions,
\tilde{b‎}eta(p,q‎‎)
‎, over
[a,b]

f_{‎p,q}(x‎)=‎\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}‎\dfrac{1}{B(p,q)(b-a)^{p+q-1}}‎(x-a)^{p-1}(b-x)^{q-1}&\mathrm{if}~a<x<b,\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

for

is beta function.

Let us denote the usual beta distribution over
[0,1]
by

with above pdf when

and

. The power semicircle distribution with parameters

The ordinary Stieltjes transform (ST) and generalized Stieltjes transform (GST) of a distribution

\mathcal{S}[H](z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-x}dH(x),\text{ \ \ }z\in\mathbb{C}\cap(\mbox{supp}\mathit{H})^{c},

\mathcal{S}[H;\rho](z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{(z-x)^{\rho}}dH(x),\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}\cap(\mbox{supp}\mathit{H})^{c},\ \rho>0,

where
\mathbb{C}

(1.3)

In particular

\mathcal{S}[F_{S_{n}};n](z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{S}[F_{i}](z),\text{ \ \ \ }z\in\mathbb{C}{\ }{\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}}(\mbox{supp}\text{ }\mathit{F_{i}})^{c}.

The Gauss-hypergeometric function

(1.4)

2
New transform and RWA distribution

First, we define a new univariate characteristic function called an additive Stieltjes transform (AST).

Definition 2.1

If
X
is a random variable with distribution
H
on a subset
S
of
A=[-a,a]
,
a>0
, its AST is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{AS}[H;d](z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{X}[‎\dfrac{1}{(1-zX)^{d}}‎]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}‎\frac{1}{(1-zx)^{d}}‎dH(x),\ \ |z|<‎\dfrac{1}{a}‎,

where
d
is a positive real number.

The assumptions that
d
is positive and
H
has a support in
S
are needed for the one to one correspondence between AST and
H
in next theorem.

We have the following relationship between the AST and the GST of a distribution
H
,

\mathcal{AS}[H;d](z)=\frac{1}{z^{d}}\mathcal{S}\left[H;d\right](\frac{1}{z}).

(2.1)

Theorem 2.1

For distributions
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
in a subset
S
of
A=[-a,a]
and any positive real number
d
, if we have

\mathcal{AS}[H_{1};d](z)=\mathcal{AS}[H_{2};d](z),

(2.2)

for all
|z|<‎\dfrac{1}{a}
, then
H_{1}=H_{2}
.

Proof

Since
d
is a real number and
|zx|<1
, we have

(1-zx)^{-d}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}‎\dfrac{‎\Gamma‎(d+m)}{‎\Gamma‎(d)m!}‎(zx)^{m}.

(2.3)

By Definition 2.1 and equations (2.2) and (2.3), we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}‎\dfrac{‎\Gamma‎(d+m)}{‎\Gamma‎(d)m!}‎z^{m}\int_{S}x^{m}dH_{1}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}‎\dfrac{‎\Gamma‎(d+m)}{‎\Gamma‎(d)m!}‎z^{m}\int_{S}x^{m}dH_{2}(x),

(2.4)

for all
|z|<‎\dfrac{1}{a}
. Given an integer
k
, treating
z
as variable and equating the corresponding coefficients of
z^{m}
(
\dfrac{‎\Gamma‎(d+m)}{‎\Gamma‎(d)}‎‎\neq 0‎
, for all
m
, as
d
is positive) for each
m
in the two sums, we obtain

\int_{S}x^{m}dH_{1}(x)=\int_{S}x^{m}dH_{2}(x).

Hence we see that

\int_{S}P(x)\ dH_{1}(x)=\int_{S}P(x)\ dH_{2}(x),

where
P(x)
is any polynomial function of
x
, and similarly for any continuous function. Thus
H_{1}=H_{2}
.

\square

The following theorem enables us to represent a relationship between the AST of the distribution of RWA
S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}
and to those of
X_{1},‎\ldots,X_{k}‎
. This theorem is the main theorem of
[
7
]
based on the AST.

Theorem 2.2

Let
S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}
be the RWA given in (1.2). Assume random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
are independent and
continuous with distribution functions
F_{1},‎\ldots,F_{k}
, respectively. Then

\mathcal{AS}[F_{S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}};\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}](z)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{AS}\left[F_{i};r_{i}\right](z),

for all
|z|<‎\dfrac{1}{a}
.

Proof

Applying Theorem 3.1 of
[
7
]
and then (2.1) to conclude the result.

\square

3
Families of RWA on some beta distributions

In this section, we provide some families of RWA
S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}
distributions.

Theorem 3.1

Let
X_{i},\ i=1,2,‎\ldots,k
be independent random variables with
\tilde{b‎}eta(r_{i}+1/2,r_{i}+1/2‎‎)
distributions. Then the RWA
S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}
has
\tilde{b‎}eta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}+1/2,\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}‎‎+1/2)
distribution.

Proof

By definition of AST of beta distribution on
[a,b]
, we have

\mathcal{AS}\left[F;r_{i}\right](z)=\int_{a}^{b}‎\dfrac{1}{(1-zx)^{r_{i}}}‎‎\frac{(x-a)^{r_{i}-1/2}(b-x)^{r_{i}-1/2}}{B(r_{i}+1/2,r_{i}+1/2)(b-a)^{2r_{i}}}‎dx.

By the change of variable
t=‎\dfrac{2x-(b+a)}{b-a}‎
, we obtain that

\mathcal{AS}\left[F;r_{i}\right](z)=‎\dfrac{1}{[1-(a+b)z/2]^{r_{i}}}‎\int_{-1}^{1}‎\dfrac{1}{(1-‎\dfrac{z(b-a)}{2-(a+b)z}‎t)^{r_{i}}}‎‎\frac{(t+1)^{r_{i}-1/2}(1-t)^{r_{i}-1/2}}{2^{2r_{i}}B(r_{i}+1/2,r_{i}+1/2)}‎dt.

By Table 3 in
[
5
]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{AS}\left[F;r_{i}\right](z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle‎\dfrac{1}{[1-(a+b)z/2]^{r_{i}}}‎‎\left[‎\dfrac{2}{1+‎\sqrt{1-‎\dfrac{z^{2}(b-a)^{2}}{(2-(a+b)z)^{2}}‎}‎}‎‎\right]^{r_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle‎\left[‎\dfrac{4}{2-(a+b)z+2‎\sqrt{1-(a+b)z+abz^{2}}‎}‎‎\right]^{r_{i}}‎‎.

Using Theorem 2.2 we obtain

\mathcal{AS}[F_{S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}};\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}](z)=\left[‎\dfrac{4}{2-(a+b)z+2‎\sqrt{1-(a+b)z+abz^{2}}‎}‎‎\right]^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}},

which is the AST of
\tilde{b‎}eta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}+1/2,\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}‎‎+1/2)
distribution. Therefore
{S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}}
has
\tilde{b‎}eta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}+1/2,\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}‎‎+1/2)
distribution.

\square

Another general family of RWA on beta distributions on
[a,b]
is presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2

Let
X_{i},\ i=1,2,‎\ldots,k
be independent random variables with
\tilde{b‎}eta(s_{i},r_{i}-s_{i}‎‎)
distributions. Then the RWA
S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}
has
\tilde{b‎}eta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}‎‎-\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i})
distribution.

Proof

By using AST of
\tilde{b‎}eta(s_{i},r_{i}-s_{i}‎‎)
distribution, we have

\mathcal{AS}\left[F;r_{i}\right](z)=‎\int_{a}^{b}‎\dfrac{1}{(1-zx)^{r_{i}}}‎‎\frac{(x-a)^{s_{i}-1}(b-x)^{r_{i}-s_{i}-1}}{B(s_{i},r_{i}-s_{i})(b-a)^{r_{i}-1}}‎dx.

Changing of variable
t=‎\dfrac{x-a}{b-a}‎
implies

\mathcal{AS}\left[F;r_{i}\right](z)=‎\dfrac{1}{(1-za)^{r_{i}}}‎\int_{0}^{1}‎\dfrac{1}{(1-‎\dfrac{z(b-a)}{1-za}‎t)^{r_{i}}}‎‎\frac{t^{s_{i}-1}(1-t)^{r_{i}-s_{i}-1}}{B(s_{i},r_{i}-s_{i})}‎dt.

Using (1.4), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{AS}\left[F;r_{i}\right](z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle‎\dfrac{1}{(1-za)^{r_{i}}}‎F_{D}^{(1)}(r_{i},s_{i};r_{i};‎\dfrac{z(b-a)}{1-za}‎)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle‎\dfrac{1}{(1-za)^{r_{i}}}‎\dfrac{1}{(1-‎\dfrac{z(b-a)}{1-za})^{s_{i}}}‎

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle‎\dfrac{1}{(1-za)^{r_{i}-s_{i}}}‎\dfrac{1}{(1-zb)^{s_{i}}}.

From Theorem 2.2, we get

\mathcal{AS}[F_{S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}};\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}](z)=\dfrac{1}{(1-za)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(r_{i}-s_{i})}}‎\dfrac{1}{(1-zb)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}}}.

which is the AST of
\tilde{b‎}eta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}‎‎-\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i})
distribution. Hence
{S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}}
has
\tilde{b‎}eta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}‎‎-\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i})
distribution.

\square

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem 3.2.

Corollary 3.1

For independent random variables
X_{i},\ i=1,2,‎\ldots,k‎
with
\tilde{b‎}eta(r_{i}/2,r_{i}/2)
distributions, the RWA
S_{n:n_{1},...,n_{k-1}}
has
\tilde{b‎}eta(\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}/2,\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}/2)
distribution.

The following theorem of
[
6
]
comes as a corollary to Corollary 3.1.

Corollary 3.2

For every integer
n‎‎\geq 2‎‎
, a power semicircle distribution with shape parameter
\theta=(n-3)/2
and any positive range parameter is a RWA
S_{n}
distribution.

Proof

Consider
k=n
,
r_{i}=1
for
i=1,2,‎\ldots,k‎
,
p=q=1/2
,
a=-\sigma
and
b=\sigma
, then use Corollary 3.1 to obtain the result.

\square

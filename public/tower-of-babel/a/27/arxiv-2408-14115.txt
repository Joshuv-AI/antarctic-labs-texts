Port-Hamiltonian Realizations of Positive Real Descriptor Systems
Chu Delin, Volker Mehrmann

Abstract
The relationship between port-Hamiltonian and positive real descriptor systems is investigated. It is well-known that port-Hamiltonian systems are positive real, but the converse implication does not always hold. In [K. Cherifi, H. Gernandt, and D. Hinsen. The difference between port-Hamiltonian, passive and positive real descriptor systems. Math. Control Signals Systems, pages 1–32, 2023] sufficient conditions for the converse are presented. We refine these conditions and present for a completely controllable, completely observable and positive real descriptor system a necessary and sufficient condition as well as an explicit method to compute a port-Hamiltonian realization of a general positive real descriptor system.



Keywords
Port-Hamiltonian descriptor system, positive real descriptor system, port-Hamiltonian realization.

Introduction

In this paper the relationship between two special classes of linear descriptor systems is studied. These are port-Hamiltonian and positive real systems. But let us first discuss general linear descriptor systems.

A linear time-invariant (LTI)
descriptor system
on a time interval
\mathbb{I}=[t_{0},t_{1}]
has the form

\displaystyle E{\dot{x}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ax(t)+Bu(t),

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cx(t)+Du(t)

(1)

with coefficient matrices
E,A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
,
C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
,
D\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times m}
. Here
x:\mathbb{I}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the state,
y:\mathbb{I}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
is the output, and
u:\mathbb{I}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is the input of the system. A specific property of descriptor system is that
E
is allowed to be singular. Although more general systems are of practical interest, see e.g.
[
15
]
, we require that the system (
1
), respectively the pair of coefficients
(E,A)
, is
regular
, i.e., that

{\rm det}(\alpha E-\beta A)\not=0{\ \ \mbox{for some}}\ \ (\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}.

(2)

The
generalized eigenvalues
of a regular pair
(E,A)
are the pairs
(\alpha_{j}\,,\beta_{j})\,\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
such that

{\rm det}(\alpha_{j}E-\beta_{j}A)=0\,,\ \ \ \ j=1,2,\ldots,n\,.

(3)

If
\,\beta_{j}\not=0
, then the eigenvalue is said to be
finite
with value
\,\lambda_{j}=\alpha_{j}/\beta_{j}
, and otherwise, if
\,\beta_{j}=0
, then the pair is the eigenvalue
infinity
.
The maximal number of finite eigenvalues that a pair
(E,A)
can have is less than or equal to the rank of
E
.

If the system (
1
) is regular, then for
consistent initial values

x(t_{0})=x_{0}
and sufficiently smooth inputs
u
, the existence and uniqueness of classical solutions is guaranteed.
The solutions are then characterized in terms of the
Weierstraß Canonical Form (WCF)
of the pair
(E,A)
, which states that there exist nonsingular matrices
X
and
Y
such that

XEY=\hbox{}\;\vbox{\kern 28.30554pt\hbox{$\kern 70.7615pt\kern-8.75002pt\left[\kern-70.7615pt\vbox{\vbox{\halign{$#$\hfil\kern 2\p@\kern\p@renwd&\thinspace\hfil$#$\hfil&&\quad\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\hfil\crcr\kern-12.0pt\cr$\hfil\kern 2.0pt\kern 8.75002pt&\mu_{1}&n-\mu_{1}\crcr\kern 2.0pt\cr\mu_{1}$\hfil\kern 2.0pt\kern 8.75002pt&I&0\cr n-\mu_{1}$\hfil\kern 2.0pt\kern 8.75002pt&0&J\cr\cr}}}\,\right]$}},\quad XAY=\hbox{}\;\vbox{\kern 28.30554pt\hbox{$\kern 72.23604pt\kern-8.75002pt\left[\kern-72.23604pt\vbox{\vbox{\halign{$#$\hfil\kern 2\p@\kern\p@renwd&\thinspace\hfil$#$\hfil&&\quad\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\hfil\crcr\kern-12.0pt\cr$\hfil\kern 2.0pt\kern 8.75002pt&\mu_{1}&n-\mu_{1}\crcr\kern 2.0pt\cr\mu_{1}$\hfil\kern 2.0pt\kern 8.75002pt&A_{1}&0\cr n-\mu_{1}$\hfil\kern 2.0pt\kern 8.75002pt&0&I\cr\cr}}}\,\right]$}},

(4)

where

J=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&J_{k}\end{array}\right],\ \quad J_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&0&1\\
&&&0\end{array}\right]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{i}\times n_{i}},\ i=1,\cdots,k.

Here
I
is the identity matrix of appropriate size.
The eigenvalues of
A_{1}
coincide with the finite eigenvalues of the pencil
(E,A)
and
J
is a nilpotent matrix
corresponding to the infinite eigenvalues. The
index
of a descriptor system, denoted by
{\rm ind}(E,A)
,
is the degree of nilpotency of the matrix
J
, i.e. the integer
i>0
, such that
J^{i}=0
,
J^{i-1}\not=0
.

It is well-known, see
[
8
,
15
]
that the system (
1
) is regular and has
index at most one
if and only if it has exactly
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E)
finite eigenvalues, or equivalently that
J=0
in (
4
).

Assuming for simplicity that
x_{0}=0
and applying a Laplace transform to (
1
), one obtains the
transfer function

{\mathcal{T}}(s)=C(sE-A)^{-1}B+D,

that maps the Laplace transform of the input to that of the output.
The transfer function is invariant under system transformations, such as those in (
4
), and so there is whole equivalence class of possible quintuples
(\hat{E},\hat{A},\hat{B},\hat{C},\hat{D})
with the same transfer function. This leads to the following concept of realizations.

Definition 1

Given a transfer function of system (
1
), then a quintuple
(\hat{E},\hat{A},\hat{B},\hat{C},\hat{D})
with
\hat{E},\hat{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}\times\hat{n}}
,
\hat{B},\hat{C}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}\times m}
,
\hat{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is called a
realization
of the transfer function
{\mathcal{T}}(s)
or the system (
1
) if

{\mathcal{T}}(s)=C(sE-A)^{-1}B+D=\hat{C}(s\hat{E}-\hat{A})^{-1}\hat{B}+\hat{D}.

Using the notation that for a real symmetric matrix
M
,
M\geq 0

(M\leq 0)
if
M
is positive (negative) semidefinite and introducing
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}=\{s~|s\in{\mathbb{C}},~{\rm Re}(s)>0\}
, we have the following definition of a
positive real system
, see e.g.
[
2
]
.

Definition 2

System (
1
) is called
positive real
if the transfer function
{\mathcal{T}}(s)
is analytic in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and

{\mathcal{T}}(s)+({\mathcal{T}}(s))^{H}\geq 0\ \mbox{\rm for all}\ s\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}.

It is not easy to check positive realness of a descriptor system, necessary and sufficient conditions for this have been derived in
[
7
]
, see also Section
2
.

For standard state-space systems, i.e. if
E=I
, it is well-known that in the representation of a transfer function, using the Kalman decomposition, the parts that are not
controllable
and
observable
can be removed, leading to
a system with minimal state-space dimension, see e.g.
[
14
]
, called
minimal realization
.
For descriptor systems the situation is more complicated, see
[
8
]
, and in particular
[
4
]
for the approach to construct minimal realizations in a numerically stable way.

Another class of systems that has recently received a lot of attention are so called port-Hamiltonian (pH) systems,

[
22
]
which have been shown to provide a natural setting for control of real world physical systems and also naturally generalize to the descriptor case, see
[
19
]
for a recent survey presenting also a large number of applications.
In the LTI case we have the following definition.

Definition 3

An LTI system of the form (
1
) is called
port-Hamiltonian (pH) descriptor system
if the coefficients can be expressed as

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(J-R)Q&G-P\\
(G+P)^{T}Q&S+N\end{array}\right],

where
S={1\over 2}(D+D^{T})
,
N={1\over 2}(D-D^{T})
,

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}J&G\\
-G^{T}&N\end{array}\right]=-\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}J&G\\
-G^{T}&N\end{array}\right]^{T},

Q^{T}E=E^{T}Q\geq 0,

(5)

and

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}Q^{T}RQ&Q^{T}P\\
P^{T}Q&S\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}Q^{T}RQ&Q^{T}P\\
P^{T}Q&S\end{array}\right]^{T}\geq 0.

(6)

The quadratic function

\mathcal{H}(x)={1\over 2}x^{T}E^{T}Qx
is called the
Hamiltonian
of the system.

One of the major properties of pH descriptor systems is the power balance equation that replaces the conservation of energy, see e.g.
[
18
,
19
]
.

Theorem 4

Consider a pH descriptor system. Then for any input
u
the
power balance equation

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}(x(t))=-\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\end{bmatrix}^{T}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}Q^{T}RQ&Q^{T}P\\
P^{T}Q&S\end{array}\right]\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\end{bmatrix}+y^{T}u

(7)

holds along any solution
x
.

Both representations of LTI descriptor systems, that in frequency domain via a positive real transfer function and that in state domain via port-Hamiltonian systems are closely related, see
[
5
]
for a detailed analysis of the relationship. It is well-known, see e.g.
[
3
,
5
]
, that for a pH descriptor system the transfer function is positive real, but a positive real descriptor
system does not necessarily have a port-Hamiltonian realization. To characterize when the two classes are equivalent, we discuss
the following natural questions.

Problem 1.
Given a positive real descriptor system of the form (
1
). Determine necessary and sufficient conditions such that the system (
1
) is port-Hamiltonian.

Problem 2.
Given a positive real descriptor system of the form (
1
). Determine a port-Hamiltonian realization

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}{\dot{x}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}x(t)+{\mathcal{B}}u(t),

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}x(t)+{\mathcal{D}}u,

(8)

of the system (
1
) in the sense
that system (
8
) is port-Hamiltonian and

{\mathcal{T}}(s)=C(sE-A)^{-1}B+D={\mathcal{C}}(s{\mathcal{E}}-{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{D}}.

In
[
5
]
a partial solution to Problem 1. is presented that relies on certain controllability and observability assumptions, see Section
2
. In Section
3
, we extend this partial solution and present a necessary and sufficient condition.
For Problem 2., in Section
4
, we develop a (numerically reliable) procedure that includes the treatment of systems that are not completely controllable or completely observable.
Concluding remarks are given in
Section
5
.

5
Concluding Remarks

The relationship between port-Hamiltonian and positive real LTI descriptor systems has been studied. A necessary and sufficient condition is presented
under which a completely controllable, completely observable, and positive real LTI descriptor system is port-Hamiltonian. A new numerically reliable
procedure is established for computing a port-Hamiltonian realization of a general positive real LTI descriptor system.
The framework is illustrated with small examples. Future work include the implementation of numerical software for the described procedures.

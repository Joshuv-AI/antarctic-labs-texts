The asymptotic expansion of the Bernoulli polynomials of the second kind
R. B. Paris

Abstract
We consider the Bernoulli polynomials of the second kind, which can be related to the generalised Bernoulli polynomials B n ( n ) ​ ( z ) B_{n}^{(n)}(z) . The asymptotic expansions of the scaled polynomials B n ( n ) ​ ( n ​ z ) B_{n}^{(n)}(nz) are obtained as n → ∞ n\to\infty when (i) z z is real and (ii) z z is complex bounded away from [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] . These results complement recent work of Štampach [ J. Approx. Theory , 262 (2021) 105517]. Numerical results are presented to illustrate the accuracy of the different expansions obtained. Mathematics subject classification (2020): 11B68, 34E05, 41A30, 41A60 Keywords: Bernoulli polynomials of the second kind, generalised Bernoulli polynomials, asymptotic expansions, Stokes phenomenon



1. Introduction

The generalised Bernoulli polynomials of order
\mu
are defined by the generating function

\biggl(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\biggr)^{\!\mu}e^{zt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(\mu)}(z)\,\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\qquad(|t|<2\pi)

(1.1)

and satisfy the well-known property

B_{n}^{(\mu)}(-z)=(-)^{n}B_{n}^{(\mu)}(\mu+z).

(1.2)

The Bernoulli polynomials of the second kind
b_{n}(z)
are defined by

\frac{t(1+t)^{z}}{\log(1+t)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(z)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\qquad(|t|<1)

and are related to the generalised Bernoulli polynomials by

b_{n}(z)=B_{n}^{(n)}(z+1).

(1.3)

Consequently, it will be sufficient in what follows to consider the polynomials
B_{n}^{(n)}(z)
instead of
b_{n}(z)
on account of the relation (
1.3
).
The first few polynomials
B_{n}^{(n)}(z)
are

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,\quad B_{1}^{(1)}(z)=\frac{1}{2}(-1+2z),\quad B_{2}^{(2)}(z)=\frac{1}{6}(5-12z+6z^{2}),

\displaystyle B_{3}^{(3)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(-9+24z-18z^{2}+4z^{3}),

\displaystyle B_{4}^{(4)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{30}(251-720z+66z^{2}-240z^{3}+30z^{4}),

\displaystyle B_{5}^{(5)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}(-475+1449z-1500z^{2}+700z^{3}-150z^{4}+12z^{5}),\ldots\ .

The asymptotic behaviour when

Štampach determined the behaviour of the zeros near the endpoints 0 and

In this article, we complement the analysis of Štampach

Unlike Štampach, who used different representations for the different cases, we use a single integral representation combined with the method of steepest descents.

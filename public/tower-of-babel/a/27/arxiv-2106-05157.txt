IV
Efficiency and
\eta_{c}
invariant-mass resolution

To compute the reconstruction and selection efficiency, MC signal events are generated using a detailed detector simulation
geant
;
BabarZ
in which the
\eta_{c}
mesons decay uniformly in phase space.
These simulated events are reconstructed and analyzed in the same manner as data.
We define the helicity angle
\theta_{H}
as the angle formed by the
h^{+}
(where
h=\pi,K

h^{+}h^{-}
rest frame, and the
\eta^{\prime}
(
\eta

h^{+}h^{-}\eta^{\prime}

For each final state, we compute the raw efficiency in 50
\times
50 intervals of the invariant-mass,
m(h^{+}h^{-})

\cos\theta_{H}

To smoothen statistical fluctuations, the efficiency maps are parameterized as follows.
We first fit the efficiency as a function of
\cos\theta_{H}
in each of the 100
{\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}
wide intervals of

\epsilon(\cos\theta_{H})=\sum_{L=0}^{12}a_{L}(m)Y^{0}_{L}(\cos\theta_{H}),

(3)

where
m
denotes the
h^{+}h^{-}
invariant-mass.
For a given value of
m(h^{+}h^{-})
, the efficiency is interpolated linearly between adjacent mass intervals.

Fig.

\eta\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}

\approx

Yields and branching fractions

In this section, we fit the invariant-mass distributions to obtain the numbers of selected
\eta_{c}
events,
N_{\eta^{\prime}K^{+}K^{-}}
,
N_{\eta^{\prime}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}
, and
N_{\eta\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}
, for each
\eta^{\prime}
or
\eta
decay mode.
We then use the
\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-}
and
\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
yields to compute the ratio of branching fractions for
\eta_{c}
to the
\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-}
and
\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
final states. This ratio is computed as

\begin{split}{\cal R}=&\frac{{\cal B}(\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-})}{{\cal B}(\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$})}\\
=&\frac{N_{\eta^{\prime}K^{+}K^{-}}}{N_{\eta^{\prime}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}}\frac{\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}}{\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}K^{+}K^{-}}}\end{split}

(4)

for each
\eta^{\prime}
decay mode,
where
\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}K^{+}K^{-}}
and
\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}
are the corresponding weighted efficiencies described in the following Sec.
V.2
.

PDG

VI
Dalitz plot analyses

We perform Dalitz plot analyses of the
\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
,
\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-}
, and
\eta\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
systems in the
\eta_{c}
mass region using unbinned maximum likelihood fits.
The likelihood function is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\bigg[

\displaystyle f_{\rm sig}\cdot\epsilon(x^{\prime}_{n},y^{\prime}_{n})\frac{\sum_{i,j}c_{i}c_{j}^{*}A_{i}(x_{n},y_{n})A_{j}^{*}(x_{n},y_{n})}{\sum_{i,j}c_{i}c_{j}^{*}I_{A_{i}A_{j}^{*}}}

(9)

\displaystyle+(1-f_{\rm sig})\frac{\sum_{i}k_{i}B_{i}(x_{n},y_{n})}{\sum_{i}k_{i}I_{B_{i}}}\bigg],

where:

•

N
is the number of events in the signal region;

•

f_{\rm sig}
is the fraction of those events attributed to
\eta_{c}
decays;

•

for the
n
-th event,
x_{n}=m^{2}(\eta/\eta^{\prime}h^{+})
,
y_{n}=m^{2}(\eta/\eta^{\prime}h^{-})
, and

•

\epsilon(x^{\prime}_{n},y^{\prime}_{n})
is the efficiency, parameterized as a function of
x^{\prime}_{n}=m(h^{+}h^{-})
and
y^{\prime}_{n}=\cos\theta_{H}
(see Sec.
IV
);

•

c_{i}
is the complex amplitude of the
i-
th signal component; the
c_{i}
are free parameters of the fit;

•

for the
n
-th event,
A_{i}(x_{n},y_{n})
describe the
i-th
complex signal-amplitude contribution;

•

k_{i}
is the magnitude of the
i-
th background component; the
k_{i}
parameters are obtained by fitting the sideband regions;

•

for the
n
-th event,
B_{i}(x_{n},y_{n})
is the probability-density function of the
i
-th background contribution; we assume that interference between signal and background amplitudes can be ignored;

•

I_{A_{i}A_{j}^{*}}=\int A_{i}(x,y)A_{j}^{*}(x,y)\epsilon(m(h^{+}h^{-}),\cos\theta_{H})\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}y
and

I_{B_{i}}~=~\int B_{i}(x,y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y
are normalization
integrals; numerical integration is performed on phase-space generated events.

Amplitudes are parameterized as described in Refs.
Asner:2003gh
and
ds
.
They include a relativistic Breit-Wigner function having a variable width modulated by the Blatt-Weisskopf
blatt
spin form factors
and the relevant spin-angular information. Note that these factors are both one for scalar resonances.

The efficiency-corrected fractional contribution
f_{i}
due to resonant or non-resonant contribution (NR) is defined as follows:

f_{i}=\frac{|c_{i}|^{2}\int|A_{i}(x_{n},y_{n})|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y}{\int|\sum_{j}c_{j}A_{j}(x,y)|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y}.

(10)

The
f_{i}
do not necessarily sum to 100% because of interference effects. The uncertainty for each
f_{i}
is evaluated by propagating the full covariance matrix obtained from the fit.

The search for the amplitudes contributing to the signal or background is performed by starting with the largest resonance observed in the mass projections, which is taken as the reference amplitude with
c_{1}=1
and phase zero. We then add, one by one, possible processes that could contribute to the decay, testing for an increase in the likelihood value.
Amplitudes are discarded if no
significant improvement in the likelihood (
\Delta(-2\log{\cal L})>2
) is obtained. Each excluded resonance is reiterated many times
in combination with other possible resonant contributions.
Where possible, resonance parameters are left free, for comparison with existing values; otherwise, they are fixed to PDG values.

X
Summary

We study the processes
\gamma\gamma\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-}
,
\gamma\gamma\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
, and
\gamma\gamma\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
using a data sample of 519
{\mathrm{fb}^{-1}}
recorded with the
B
A
B
AR
detector operating at the SLAC PEP-II asymmetric-energy
e^{+}e^{-}
collider at center-of-mass energies at and near the
\mathchar 28935\relax(nS)
(
n=2,3,4
) resonances.
We observe
\eta_{c}
decays to all the above final states and perform Dalitz plot analyses to measure intermediate resonant fractions and relative phases.
Significant interference effects of the
\eta_{c}
with the two-photon background are observed only for the decay
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
.

The decay
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-}
is observed for the first time and we measure the branching fraction relative to
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}

\frac{{\cal B}(\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-})}{{\cal B}(\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$})}=0.644\pm 0.039_{\rm stat}\pm 0.032_{\rm sys}.

Using published information from the
B
A
B
AR
and Belle experiments, and this analysis of
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-}
, we obtain measurements of the
K^{*}_{0}(1430)
resonance parameters:

\begin{split}m(K^{*}_{0}(1430))&=1449\pm 17_{\rm stat}\pm 2_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$};\\
g^{2}_{K\pi}&=0.458\pm 0.032_{\rm stat}\pm 0.044_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{GeV^{2}}}/c^{4}$};\\
\frac{g^{2}_{\eta^{\prime}K}}{g^{2}_{\pi K}}&=1.50\pm 0.24_{\rm stat}\pm 0.24_{\rm sys}.\end{split}

We also measure the ratio of couplings of the
K^{*}_{0}(1430)
resonance to
\eta^{\prime}K
and
\pi K
,

\frac{\mbox{${\cal B}$}(K^{*}_{0}(1430)^{+}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}K)}{\mbox{${\cal B}$}(K^{*}_{0}(1430)^{+}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\pi K)}=0.450\pm 0.072_{\rm stat}\pm 0.061_{\rm sys}.

The
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${K^{+}}$}K^{-}
decay contains a significant contribution from
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}f_{0}(1710)
, and we measure the
f_{0}(1710)
resonance parameters:

\begin{split}m(f_{0}(1710)&=1757\pm 24_{\rm stat}\pm 9_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$};\\
\Gamma(f_{0}(1710))&=175\pm 23_{\rm stat}\pm 4_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$}.\end{split}

Evidence is also found for the
K^{*}_{0}(1950)
, whose parameters are measured as:

\begin{split}m(K^{*}_{0}(1950))&=1942\pm 22_{\rm stat}\pm 21_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$};\\
\Gamma(K^{*}_{0}(1950))&=80\pm 32_{\rm stat}\pm 20_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$}.\end{split}

We find no evidence for the
\kappa/K^{*}_{0}(700)
in
\eta_{c}
decays.

The
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta^{\prime}\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}
decay is found to be dominated by the
f_{0}(2100)
resonance, also observed in radiative
J/\psi
decays, and we measure the resonance parameters:

\begin{split}m(f_{0}(2100))&=2116\pm 27_{\rm stat}\pm 17_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$};\\
\Gamma(f_{0}(2100))&=289\pm 34_{\rm stat}\pm 15_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$}.\end{split}

Evidence is also found for the
f_{2}(1430)
, and we measure the resonance parameters:

\begin{split}m(f_{2}(1430))&=1440\pm 11_{\rm stat}\pm 3_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$};\\
\Gamma(f_{2}(1430))&=46\pm 15_{\rm stat}\pm 5_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$}.\end{split}

The Dalitz plot analysis of the
\eta_{c}\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta\mbox{${\pi^{+}}$}\mbox{${\pi^{-}}$}

a_{0}(1700)\mbox{$\rightarrow$}\eta\pi

\Gamma(a_{0}(1700))&=110\pm 15_{\rm stat}\pm 11_{\rm sys}\ \mbox{${\mathrm{MeV}}/c^{2}$}.\end{split}

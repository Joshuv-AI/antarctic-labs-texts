A generalized buchdahl model for compact stars in f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity
J. Kumar, H. D. Singh, A. K. Prasad

Abstract
In this paper, we study the stellar structure in terms of alternative theory of gravity specially by f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity theory. Here, we consider the function f ⁡ ( R , T ) = R + 2 ​ ς ​ T f(R,T)=R+2\varsigma T where R R is the Ricci scalar, T T is the stress-energy momentum and ς \varsigma is the coupling constant. Using it we developed a stellar model that briefly explains the isotropic matter distribution within the compact object filled with perfect fluid. The stability of the model is shown by several physical and stability conditions. With the accecptibility of our theory, we were able to collect data for compact stars like PSR-B0943+10, CEN X-3, SMC X-4, Her X-1 and 4U1538-52 with great accuracy.

General mathematical formulation of
f(R,T)
gravity theory

In this section, we introduced the formation of
f(R,T)

[
41
]
gravity theory. While obtaining the Einstein’s field equations, the ricci scalar in Einstein-Hilbert action is integrated over
d^{4}x
as

\displaystyle S_{EH}=\frac{1}{16}\int{R\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x}

(1)

Now one can get
f(R,T)
field equation from the above equation if instead of Ricci scalar
R
, we select
f(R,T)
. Thus, the full action for the modified theory of gravity is given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{16}\int{f(R,T)\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x}+\int{L_{M}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x}

(2)

where in the first term
f(R,T)
is arbitrary function of Ricci scalar
R
with
g
as the determinant of the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
and
T
being the trace of the stress-energy tensor,
T_{\mu\nu}
. And in the second term
L_{M}
is the matter Lagrangian density which is related to stress-momentum tensor given as

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}L_{M})}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}

(3)

with trace
T=g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}
. Assuming that the Lagrangian density
L_{M}

[
42
]
only depends on the metric tensor components
g_{\mu\nu}
, we obtain eqn.(
3
) as

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}L_{M}-\frac{2\delta(L_{M})}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}

(4)

By varying eqn.(
2
) with respect to
g_{\mu\nu}
, we obtained the field equation

\displaystyle(R_{\mu\nu}-\bigtriangledown_{\mu}\bigtriangledown_{\nu})f_{R}(R,T)+g_{\mu\nu}\diamondsuit f_{R}(R,T)-\frac{1}{2}f(R,T)g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}-f_{T}(R,T)(T_{\mu\nu}+\varPsi_{\mu\nu})

(5)

where
f_{R}(R,T)=\frac{\delta f(R,T)}{\delta R}
and
f_{T}(R,T)=\frac{\delta f(R,T)}{\delta T}
. The covariant derivative is denoted by
\bigtriangledown_{\mu}
and the diamond operature
\diamondsuit
is defined as

\displaystyle\diamondsuit=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{\mu}}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{\nu}})

\displaystyle\varPsi_{\mu\nu}=g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}

Now we perform the covariant derivative of eqn.(
5
) by which we obtain the covariant derivative of the energy-momentum tensor as

\displaystyle\bigtriangledown^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{f_{T}(R,T)}{8\pi-f_{T}(R,T)}[(T_{\mu\nu}+\varPsi_{\mu\nu})\bigtriangledown^{\mu}lnf_{T}(R,T)+\varPsi_{\mu\nu}\bigtriangledown^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\bigtriangledown^{\mu}T]

(6)

Clearly from eqn.(
6
) we can see that the stress-energy momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
in
f(R,T)
theory does not follow the conservation law as in Einstein general relativity (GR) due to the presence of some nonminimal matter geometry coupling in its formulation. By using eqn.(
4
), the tensor
\varPsi_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle\varPsi_{\mu\nu}=-2T_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}L_{M}-2g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta^{2}L_{M}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}

(7)

To find the field equations, we consider the interior of star to be filled with a perfect fluid source along with the energy-momentum tensor of the form

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}-pg_{\mu\nu}

(8)

provided the four velocity
u_{\nu}
satifies
u_{\mu}u^{\mu}=-1
and
u_{\nu}\bigtriangledown^{\mu}u_{\nu}=0
,
\rho
and
p
is the matter density and isotropic pressure respectively . Taking the matter Lagrangian as
L_{M}=-p
, we obtain eqn.(
7
) as

\displaystyle\varPsi_{\mu\nu}=-2T_{\mu\nu}-pg_{\mu\nu}

(9)

In this paper, we assumed the function
f(R,T)
as
f(R,T)=R+2f(T)
where
f(T)
is the function of trace
(T)
of the stress-energy tensor of matter. Here, we choose
f(T)=\varsigma T
in order to determine the modified theory of gravity, where
\varsigma
is a coupling constant.

Using the expression in eqn.(
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}+\varsigma Tg_{\mu\nu}+2\varsigma(T_{\mu\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu})

(10)

When
f(R,T)\equiv R
, then eqn.(
5
) reduces to Einstein field equations. By putting
f(R,T)=R+2\varsigma T
and eqn.(
9
) in eqn.(
6
) we get

\displaystyle\bigtriangledown^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{\varsigma}{2(4\pi+\varsigma)}[g_{\mu\nu}\bigtriangledown^{\mu}T+2\bigtriangledown^{\mu}(pg_{\mu\nu})]

(11)

Thus, one can easily obtain the conservation equation in Einstein’s gravity by putting
\varsigma=0
.
For studying cosmological and astrophysical problems
f(R,T)
has been widely considered due to its advantages in explaining such space problems.

\displaystyle p_{E}=\frac{e^{-\lambda}}{8\pi}\bigg(\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\frac{\nu^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{e^{\lambda}}{r^{2}}\bigg)

(14)

\displaystyle p_{E}=\frac{e^{-\lambda}}{32\pi}\bigg(2\nu^{\prime\prime}+\nu^{\prime 2}-\lambda^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}+2\frac{\nu^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}}{r}\bigg)

where

and

\displaystyle\rho_{E}=\rho_{F}+\frac{\varsigma}{8\pi}(3\rho_{F}-p_{F})

which provides the hydrostatic equillibrium condition for general relativity when we take

p_{F}

\displaystyle p_{F}=\frac{\pi}{(8\pi^{2}+6\pi\varsigma+\varsigma^{2})}[(8\pi+3\varsigma)p_{E}+\varsigma\rho_{E}]

\displaystyle\rho_{F}=\frac{\pi}{(8\pi^{2}+6\pi\varsigma+\varsigma^{2})}[(8\pi+3\varsigma)\rho_{E}+\varsigma p_{E}]

Now, it has been assumed that all the cosmological phenomena are engulfed in the vacuum space-time, so we need to check our solution for interior space-time with the exterior Schwarzschild line element as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\bigg(1-\frac{2M}{r}\bigg)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{2M}{r}}+r^{2}\bigg(d\theta^{2}+sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\bigg)

Buchdahl ansatz in
f(R,T)
gravity theory

In order to find the solution of the modified field equation, we use Buchdahl anastz
[
44
]
which easily investigate all the physical parameters of a stellar model. The most broadly studied Buchdahl metric anastz is given by

\displaystyle e^{\lambda}=\dfrac{K(1+Cr^{2})}{K+Cr^{2}}

(21)

where
K
and
C
are constant parameters that depends on the geometry of the given star.
Now we consider few transformations for solving field equations ,

\displaystyle\chi=\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}~~~~\textrm{and}~~~~~e^{\nu}=W^{2}

(22)

Substituting eqn.(
21
) and eqn.(
22
) in eqn.(
13
) and eqn.(
14
) and doing some simple algebraic calculations we obtained the following equations which will be later used in solving exact solutions,

\displaystyle-\kappa p_{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{(K+Cr^{2})}{K(1+Cr^{2})}\bigg[-\frac{2W^{\prime}}{rW}+\frac{C(K-1)}{K+Cr^{2}}\bigg]

(23)

\displaystyle-\kappa p_{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{(K+Cr^{2})}{K(1+Cr^{2})}\bigg[-\frac{W^{\prime\prime}}{W}+\frac{W^{\prime}}{rW}-\frac{C(K-1)}{(K+Cr^{2})(1+Cr^{2})}\bigg(1+\frac{rW^{\prime}}{W}\bigg)\bigg]

(24)

\displaystyle\kappa c^{2}\rho_{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{C(K-1)(3+Cr^{2})}{K(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}

(25)

Now subtracting eqn.(
24
) from eqn.(
23
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{K+Cr^{2}}{K(1+Cr^{2})}\Bigg[\dfrac{W^{\prime\prime}}{W}-\dfrac{W^{\prime}}{rW}+\dfrac{C(K-1)r(Cr-W^{\prime}/W)}{(K+Cr^{2})(1+Cr^{2})}\Bigg]=0

(26)

Using eqn.(
22

\displaystyle(1-\chi^{2})\frac{d^{2}W}{d\chi^{2}}+\chi\frac{dW}{d\chi}+(1-K)W=0

To find the solution of the above equation we use transformation for two cases :

Case 1 for
K<0

\displaystyle W=(1-\chi^{2})^{1/4}U

Thus eqn.(
27
) reduces to

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{2}U}{dX^{2}}+\psi U=0

where

\displaystyle\psi=\frac{1-K}{1-\chi^{2}}-\frac{2+3\chi^{2}}{4(1-\chi^{2})^{2}}

In order to solve the eqn.(
29

\displaystyle\psi=\frac{A_{1}}{\chi^{2}}

(30)

where

be any arbitrary constant.

Thus eqn.(
29

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{2}U}{dX^{2}}+\frac{A_{1}}{\chi^{2}}U=0

Solving the differential equation we obtain the solution as

\displaystyle U=c_{1}\frac{\chi^{1-A_{1}}}{1-A_{1}}+c_{2}

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle W=(1-\chi^{2})^{1/4}\bigg[c_{1}\frac{\chi^{1-A_{1}}}{1-A_{1}}+c_{2}\bigg]

(32)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}

Using eqn.(

\displaystyle p_{E}

(33)

\displaystyle\rho_{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{C(K-1)(3+Cr^{2})}{8\pi K(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}

(34)

Substituting the above equations in eqns.(

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \frac{2\chi^{1-A_{1}}}{\bigg[\frac{\chi^{1-A_{1}}}{1-A_{1}}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\bigg]}\bigg]+\varsigma\frac{(K-1)(3+Cr^{2})}{8\pi K(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}\bigg]

(35)

\displaystyle\rho_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{(8\pi^{2}+6\pi\varsigma+\varsigma^{2})}\bigg[(8\pi+3\varsigma)\frac{(K-1)(3+Cr^{2})}{8\pi K(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}+\frac{\varsigma}{8\pi K(1+Cr^{2})}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \bigg[-\frac{\chi^{2}}{1-\chi^{2}}-(K-1)+\frac{2\chi^{1-A_{1}}}{\bigg[\frac{\chi^{1-A_{1}}}{1-A_{1}}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\bigg]}\bigg]

Localization of eigenvalues for non-self-adjoint Dirac and Klein-Gordon operators
∗ P. D’Ancona , † L. Fanelli , ‡ D. Krejčiřík , and ∗ N.M. Schiavone

Abstract
This note aims to give prominence to some new results on the absence and localization of eigenvalues for the Dirac and Klein-Gordon operators, starting from known resolvent estimates already established in the literature combined with the renowned Birman-Schwinger principle.

Introduction

Since around the turn of the millennium,
there has been a tremendous rise in interest
for spectral properties of non-self-adjoint operators in quantum mechanics.
The physical relevance relies, inter alia, on the new concept
of representing observables by operators which are merely
similar to self-adjoint ones,
while the mathematical community is challenged
by the absence of the spectral theorem and variational tools.

To make up for this lack of conventional methods,
the key tool usually employed in the non-selfadjoint settings
is the celebrated Birman-Schwinger principle
(see, e.g.,

[
20
,
10
,
16
,
21
,
9
,
18
,
17
,
3
,
14
,
3
]

to cite just few recent works).
Roughly speaking (see below for precise statements),
the principle states that
z
is an eigenvalue
of an operator
H:=H_{0}+B^{*}A

if and only if
-1
is an eigenvalue of the Birman-Schwinger operator

K_{z}:=A(H_{0}-z)^{-1}B^{*}
.
In typical quantum-mechanical examples,

H_{0}
is a differential operator representing
the kinetic energy of the system,
while
B^{*}A
is a factorization of an operator of multiplication
representing an electromagnetic interaction.
In this way, the spectral problem for an unbounded differential operator
is reduced to a bounded integral operator.
In particular, the eigenvalues of the perturbed operator
H

are confined in the complex region defined by

1\leq\left\lVert K_{z}\right\rVert
and the point spectrum is empty if
\left\lVert K_{z}\right\rVert<1

uniformly with respect to
z
.
We refer to
[
24
]
for an abstract
spectral-theoretic machinery based on the Birman-Schwinger idea.

It is clear from
the definition of the Birman-Schwinger operator
that this approach reduces to establishing suitable
resolvent estimates for the unperturbed operator
H_{0}
.
Indeed, once we know how to bound
(H_{0}-z)^{-1}
,
it is usually an easy matter setting
A
and
B

in a suitable normed space,
and then obtain an estimate for
K_{z}
. Of course, this naïf reasoning is well-known,
and can be synthesized claiming that each resolvent estimate corresponds, via the Birman-Schwinger principle, to a localization estimate for the eigenvalues of the perturbed operator.

Since resolvent estimates have been an object of study for a considerably longer time with respect to the eigenvalues confinement for non-selfadjoint operators, it is natural that some results for the latter problem, even if interesting per se, go unnoticed. The goal of the current note is indeed bringing to light some new spectral results for the Dirac and Klein-Gordon operators, by inserting already established resolvent estimates in the main engine of the Birman-Schwinger principle.

The assumptions we will impose on the potential are essentially pointwise smallness and decay near the origin and infinity. It would be desirable to explore other conditions, notably those involving
L^{p}
norms of the potential, as in the case of Schrödinger operators in the seminal work by Frank
[
20
]
. However, for this purpose, one needs
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for the resolvent, which are not easy to get and in some situations fail to hold. For example, Cuenin, Laptev and Tretter
[
10
]
prove in
1
-dimension their celebrated result about the eigenvalues enclosure of the perturbed Dirac operator in two (optimal) disks of the complex plane, provided that the
L^{1}
norm of the potential is small. This is obtained by straightforward computations based on an
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
resolvent estimate. However, such
L^{p}-L^{p^{\prime}}
estimates does not hold in higher dimensions, as observed in
[
8
]

(see also
[
17
]
).

Before moving to the main results of the present note,
let us observe that despite the robustness
of the Birman-Schwinger principle,
it is not the only tool for obtaining
spectral enclosures for non-self-adjoint operators.
Indeed, another powerful technique which has been recently
employed in various related problems is the method of multipliers
(see, e.g.,
[
18
,
19
,
6
,
7
,
5
]
).

The present note is organized as follows.
In the next Section
2

we informally introduce the operators
and list our main results.
The key resolvent estimates are collected in Section
3
,
while the Birman-Schwinger principle is recalled in Section
4
,
where we also properly define the perturbed operators.
Finally, the main results are established in Section
5
.

2
Main theorems

In this note we are concerned with relativistic
quantum-mechanical systems modelled by
scalar Klein-Gordon and spinorial Dirac operators
in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. They are formally defined respectively as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{m,V}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{m}+V,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{m,V}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{m}+V,

where, for fixed mass
m\geq 0
, the free Klein-Gordon and Dirac operators are

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{m}=\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{m}=-i\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\cdot\nabla+m\alpha_{0}=-i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\partial_{k}+m\alpha_{0}.

The matrices
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
,
N:=2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}
being
\lceil\cdot\rceil
the ceiling function, are elements of the Clifford algebra satisfying the anti-commutation relations

\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{k}\alpha_{j}=2\delta^{j}_{k}I_{N}\quad\text{for $j,k\in\{0,\dots,n\}$}

where
\delta^{j}_{k}
is the Kronecker symbol.
If we set for simplicity
N:=1
when we are dealing with the Klein-Gordon operator, we can say that both the operators
\mathcal{G}_{m}
and
\mathcal{D}_{m}
act on
\mathfrak{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N})
, have domain
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N})
and are self-adjoint with core
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N})
.

Concerning both perturbed operators, the potential
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
is a generic, possibly non-Hermitian, matrix-valued function (resp. scalar valued in the case of Klein-Gordon).
With the usual abuse of notation, we denote with the same symbol
V
the multiplication operator by the matrix
V
in
\mathfrak{H}
with initial domain
\dom(V)=C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N})
.

Moreover, for any matrix-valued function
M\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
and norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we write
\left\lVert M\right\rVert:=\left\lVert|M|\right\rVert
, where
|M(x)|
denotes the operator norm of the matrix
M(x)
.

For simplicity, we will say that the spectrum of
\mathcal{G}_{m,V}
or
\mathcal{D}_{m,V}
is
stable
(with respect to the corresponding free operator spectrum) if

\sigma(\mathcal{G}_{m,V})=\sigma_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{m,V})=\sigma(\mathcal{G}_{m})=[m,+\infty)

(2.1)

in the case of the Klein-Gordon operator, whereas

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{D}_{0,V})=\sigma_{c}(\mathcal{D}_{0,V})=\sigma(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\mathbb{R}\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{D}_{m,V})=\sigma_{c}(\mathcal{D}_{m,V})=\sigma(\mathcal{D}_{m})=(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,+\infty)\,,

(2.3)

in the case of the massless and massive Dirac operators respectively. In any case, note that this means in particular that the point and residual spectra of the perturbed operator are empty.

Finally, let us introduce the weights defined as

\displaystyle\tau_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle:=|x|^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}+|x|

(2.4)

\displaystyle w_{\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle:=|x|(1+|\log|x||)^{\sigma},\quad\text{for $\sigma>1$.}

(2.5)

We are ready to enunciate the claimed results.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geq 3
. There exist positive constants
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
, independent of
V
, such that if

\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\alpha

then the spectrum of

\mathcal{G}_{m,V}
is stable, viz. (
2.1
) holds true.

Theorem 2
.

Let
n\geq 3
. For
m=0
, there exists a positive constant
\alpha
, independent of
V
, such that if

\left\lVert w_{\sigma}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\alpha

then the spectrum of

\mathcal{D}_{0,V}
is stable, viz. (
2.2
) holds true.

For
m>0
, there exist positive constants
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
, independent of
V
, such that if

\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\alpha

then the spectrum of

\mathcal{D}_{m,V}
is stable, viz. (
2.3
) holds true.

For the Dirac operator we can improve the above theorem in two ways. Firstly, slightly generalizing the choice of the weights (see also Remark
2.3
below). Secondly, and above all, we can give a quantitative form for the smallness condition of the potential
(even if our expression for the constant is probably far from being optimal).
With this aim we bring into play the dyadic norms defined as

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}:=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{p}_{L^{q}(2^{j-1}\leq|x|<2^{j})}\right)^{1/p},\qquad\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{q}}:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}(2^{j-1}\leq|x|<2^{j})},

(2.6)

for
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
m\geq 0
and
\rho\in\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a positive weight. If
m>0
, assume in addition that
|x|^{1/2}\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For
m>0
, define

\begin{split}C_{1}\equiv C_{1}(n,m,\rho):=\,&576n\left[\sqrt{n}+(2m+1)\sqrt[4]{64n+324}\right]\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}\\
&+(2m+1)\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2(n-2)}}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\end{split}

whereas if
m=0
,

\displaystyle C_{1}\equiv C_{1}(n,0,\rho):=2C_{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}},\quad C_{2}\equiv C_{2}(n):=576n\max\{\sqrt{n},\sqrt[4]{64n+324}\}.

(2.7)

Supposing

C_{1}\left\lVert|x|\rho^{-2}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<1

then the spectrum of

\mathcal{D}_{m,V}
is stable, viz. (
2.3
) holds true.

In the massless case, we can ask for less stringent conditions on the potential in order to still get the spectrum stable.

Theorem 4
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
m=0
and

2C_{2}\left\lVert|x|V\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{\infty}}<1,

where
C_{2}
is defined in (
2.7
). Then the spectrum of

\mathcal{D}_{0,V}
is stable, viz. (
2.2
) holds true.

Last but not least, we prove some results on the eigenvalues confinement in two complex disks for the massive Dirac operator.
To this end one can use either the weighted dyadic norm (this gives the counterpart for
m>0
of Theorem
4
), or again the weighted-
L^{2}
norm with weaker conditions on the weight
\rho
(namely, removing in Theorem
3
the assumption
|x|^{1/2}\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
m>0
).

Theorem 5
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
m>0
and

N_{1}(V):=\left\lVert|x|V\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{\infty}}
,
N_{2}(V):=\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert|x|\rho^{-2}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

for some positive weight
\rho\in\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For fixed
j\in\{1,2\}
, if we assume

2C_{2}N_{j}(V)<1,

with
C_{2}
defined in (
2.7
), then

\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{D}_{m,V})\subset\overline{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}^{-})\cup\overline{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}^{+})

where the two closed complex disks have centres
x_{0}^{-},x_{0}^{+}
and radius
r_{0}
defined by

x_{0}^{\pm}:=\pm m\frac{\mathcal{V}_{j}^{2}+1}{\mathcal{V}_{j}^{2}-1},\quad r_{0}:=m\frac{2\mathcal{V}_{j}}{\mathcal{V}_{j}^{2}-1},\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{V}_{j}:=\left[\frac{1}{C_{2}N_{j}(V)}-1\right]^{2}>1.

Remark 2.1
.

In the above Theorem
5
, the case
j=2
is actually redundant. Indeed, one can easily observe that
N_{1}(V)\leq N_{2}(V)
simply by Hölder’s inequality. Thus, if
2C_{2}N_{2}(V)<1
, it follows that
\mathcal{V}_{2}\leq\mathcal{V}_{1}
and the disks obtained for
j=1
are enclosed in those obtained for
j=2
.
However, we explicit both the case since, as observed above, Theorem
5
is in some sense the counterpart of Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
.

Remark 2.2
.

In our results, the low dimensional cases
n=1,2
are excluded. This restriction comes from the key resolvent estimates we are going to employ, collected in Lemma
1
, Lemma
2
and Lemma
3
and proved in
[
13
]
and
[
2
]
(see Section
3
below).
Indeed, regarding the last lemma, it holds for
n\geq 3
since to prove it the multiplier method is exploited, which fails in low dimensions. In the case of the first two lemmata instead, the low dimensions are excluded essentially due to the use of Kato-Yajima’s estimates; but there is a deeper reason behind instead of a mere technical one.

In fact, tracing back the computations in
[
13
]
, a key step in the proof of Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
is equation (2.19) of
[
13
]
concerning the Schrödinger resolvent, namely

\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(-\Delta-z)^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+|z|^{2})^{-1/2}\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

with some positive constant
C
and
n\geq 3
. The above inequality is obtained by fusing together results by Barcelo, Ruiz and Vega
[
1
]
and by Kato and Yajima
[
26
]
, and it is without any doubt false for
n=1,2
. In fact, by contradiction, exploiting computations similar to the ones we will carry on in Section
5
, one should be able to prove the counterpart of Theorems
1
&
2
for the Schrödinger operator, in other words the spectrum of
-\Delta+V
would be stable if
\left\lVert\tau^{2}_{\varepsilon}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\alpha
for some positive constants
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
. This assertion is true for
n\geq 3
, but certainly impossible for
n=1,2
, due to the well-know fact that the Schrödinger operator is critical if, and only if,
n=1,2
.

The
criticality
of an operator
H_{0}
means that it is not stable against small perturbations: there exists a compactly
supported potential
V
such that
H_{0}+\epsilon V

possesses a discrete eigenvalue for all small
\epsilon>0
.
For the Schrödinger operator this is equivalent to the lack of Hardy’s inequality.
On the contrary, the existence of Hardy’s inequality in dimension
n\geq 3
is sometimes referred to as the
subcriticality
of
-\Delta
.

In the light of this argument for the Schrödinger operator, a very interesting question, deserving to be pursued, naturally arises: one can conjecture that also the Klein-Gordon and Dirac operators are critical if and only if
n=1,2
, that is Theorems
1
&
2
are false in low dimensions and their spectra are not stable if perturbed by small compactly supported potentials.

Remark 2.3
.

In Theorem
1
&
2
we used the explicit weights
\tau_{\varepsilon}
and
w_{\sigma}
, while in the subsequent statements exploiting the weighted-
L^{2}
norm they are replaced by the weight
|x|\rho^{-2}
with
\rho\in\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We compare these assumptions.

It easy to check that
\rho_{1}:=(1+|\log|x||)^{-\sigma/2}
and
\rho_{2}:=(|x|^{-\varepsilon}+|x|^{\delta})^{-1}
are weights in
\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
\sigma>1
and
\varepsilon,\delta>0
.
Consequently we can set
|x|\rho^{-2}=w_{\sigma}(x)
or
|x|\rho^{-2}=(|x|^{1/2-\varepsilon}+|x|^{1/2+\delta})^{2}
. The additional condition
|x|^{1/2}\rho\in L^{\infty}
can be obtained for
\rho_{2}
if we set
\delta=1/2
, and hence
\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}=|x|\rho^{-2}_{2}
.
In other words,
w_{\sigma}
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}
are the prototypes of the class of weights we used, since
|x|^{1/2}w_{\sigma}^{-1/2},|x|^{1/2}\tau_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\in\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
|x|\tau_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

This generalization gives only a minor improvement in the type of admissible weights, however we think it is useful since it stresses the properties and limiting behaviors required of them.

Finally, we note that the extra condition
|x|^{1/2}\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
affects the behavior of
\rho\in\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
only at infinity. Indeed, near the origin, say when
|x|\leq 1
,

|x|^{1/2}\rho\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}},

so no further requirement is added on the behavior of
\rho
near
x=0
; on the contrary

\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(|x|\geq 1)}\leq\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(|x|\geq 1)}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=\sqrt{2}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

when
|x|\geq 1
, so
L^{\infty}(|x|\geq 1)\subset\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(|x|\geq 1)
.

Remark 2.4
.

For a concrete example, let us make the constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
explicit in a special case. We set
n=3
,
m\in[0,1]
and choose
\rho=|x|^{1/2}\tau_{1/2}^{-1}=(|x|^{-1/2}+|x|^{1/2})^{-1}

and

Therefore, it follows that

,

if

and

.
Hence the smallness condition on the potential in Theorem

\left\lVert(1+|x|)^{2}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\begin{cases}9.00\cdot 10^{-6}&\text{if $m>0$,}\\
1.51\cdot 10^{-5}&\text{if $m=0$,}\end{cases}

and the one in Theorem
4
by

\left\lVert|x|V\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{\infty}}<6.06\cdot 10^{-5}.

Our conditions on the potential

are certainly not sharp.
We conjecture that the pointwise smallness conditions
of Theorem

n=3
.
There exists a positive constant
\alpha

if

(respectively,
\|V\|_{L^{3}}+\|V\|_{L^{3/2}}<\alpha

(respectively,

)
(respectively, (

3
A bundle of resolvent estimates

As anticipated in the Introduction, the main ingredients in our proofs are a collection of inequalities already published in the literature.
The first two, recalled in the next two lemmata, come from
[
13
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. There exist
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}-z)^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

where
the weight
\tau_{\varepsilon}
is defined in (
2.4
).

The massless (
m=0
) case for this Klein-Gordon resolvent estimate is obtained by equation (2.39) in
[
13
]
letting
W=0
. Instead, equation (2.43) from the same paper gives us the massive case for unitary mass
m=1
, and for all positive
m
by a change of variables.

Let us face now the Dirac operator.

Lemma 2
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. There exist
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert w_{\sigma}^{-1/2}(\mathcal{D}_{0}-zI_{N})^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert w_{\sigma}^{1/2}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\mathcal{D}_{m}-zI_{N})^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

(3.2)

in the massless and massive case respectively, where the weights
\tau_{\varepsilon}
and
w_{\sigma}
are defined in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).

These estimates correspond to equation (2.49) and (2.52) from
[
13
]
respectively, even if the estimate for the massless case was previously proved in
[
12
]
by the same authors.
It should be noted that, in the cited paper, estimates (2.49) and (2.52) are explicated only in the
3
-dimensional case, but it can be easily seen that they hold in any dimension
n\geq 3
, since their proofs mostly rely on the well-known identity
\mathcal{D}_{m}^{2}=(-\Delta+m^{2})I_{N}
.

The resolvent estimates just stated are uniform, in the sense that the constant
C
in the estimates is independent of
z
. This will imply, as we will see, the total absence of eigenvalues under suitable smallness assumptions on the potential.

For the Dirac operator the above result can be improved.
First of all, we can give a non-sharp but explicit estimate for the constant
C
. Moreover, paying with a constant dependent on
z
(obtaining then a localization for the eigenvalues instead of their absence in the massless case) we can substitute the weighted-
L^{2}
norms with dyadic ones, or relax the hypothesis on the weights in the massive case.

This step-up will be gained making use of the sharp resolvent estimate for the Schrödinger operator in dimension
n\geq 3
contained in Theorem 1.1 of
[
2
]
(the same estimate can be obtained also e.g. from Theorem 1.2 in
[
11
]
, but the latter does not provide explicit constants).
Setting
a=I_{n}
,
b=c=0
,
N=\nu=1
and
C_{a}=C_{b}=C_{c}=C_{-}=C_{+}=0
in the referred theorem, one immediately obtain the trio of estimates stated below.

Lemma 3
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
and
R_{0}(z):=(-\Delta-z)^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{\dot{X}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(288n)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}^{*}}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle|\Re z|\left\lVert R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(576\sqrt{2}\,n^{2})^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}^{*}}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle|\Im z|\left\lVert R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(864\sqrt{2}\,n)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}^{*}}^{2}\,,

where the
\dot{X}
and
\dot{Y}
norms are the Morrey-Campanato type norms defined by

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{X}}^{2}:=\sup_{R>0}\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{|x|=R}|u|^{2}dS,\qquad\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}}^{2}:=\sup_{R>0}\frac{1}{R}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u|^{2}dx,

and the
\dot{Y}^{*}
norm is predual to the
\dot{Y}
norm.

Since the Morrey-Campanato type norms above introduced are not so handy, observe that the
\dot{X}
norm can be written as a radial-angular norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{X}}=\left\lVert|x|^{-1}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{|x|}L^{2}_{\theta}}:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{R\in[2^{j-1},2^{j})}\left\lVert|x|^{-1}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(|x|=R)}

whereas the
\dot{Y}
norm is equivalent to the weighted dyadic norm
\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\,\cdot\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}
, and hence by duality the
\dot{Y}^{*}
norm is equivalent to
\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\,\cdot\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}
(being
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}
defined in (
2.6
)). More precisely, since we want to show explicit constants, we have that

\begin{split}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}|x|^{-1}|u|^{2}dx\leq 2\,\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\int_{|x|\leq 2^{j}}|u|^{2}dx\leq 2\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}}^{2},\end{split}

while from the other side, fixed
R\in[2^{j-1},2^{j})
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{R}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u|^{2}dx\leq 2^{1-j}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{j}2^{n}\int_{2^{n-1}}^{2^{n}}|x|^{-1}|u|^{2}dx\leq 4\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Summarizing

\displaystyle{2}^{-1/2}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}}\leq 2\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle{2}^{-1}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{Y}^{*}}\leq 2^{1/2}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}.

Inserting the above norm equivalence relations in Lemma
3
one can straightforwardly infer the following.

Corollary 1
.

Under the same assumptions of Lemma
3
, the estimates

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{-1}R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{|x|}L^{2}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq 576n\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}f\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle|z|^{1/2}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 576n\sqrt[4]{64n+324}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}f\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\nabla R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 576n\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}f\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}\,,

hold true.

Simply applying Hölder’s inequality, one can deduce also the weighted-
L^{2}
version of Lemma
1
. Moreover, this allows us to employ the
-\Delta
-supersmoothness of
|x|^{-1}
to obtain a homogeneous (in effect even stronger) weighted-
L^{2}
estimate for the Schrödinger resolvent. Namely, we have the following.

Corollary 2
.

Under the same assumptions of Lemma
3
, the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{-3/2}\rho R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 576n\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle|z|^{1/2}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 576n\sqrt[4]{64n+324}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho\nabla R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 576n\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

(3.3)

for any arbitrary positive weight
\rho\in\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

If in addition
|x|^{1/2}\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\langle z\rangle^{1/2}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C_{3}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

where

C_{3}\equiv C_{3}(n,\rho):=576n\sqrt[4]{64n+324}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}^{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2(n-2)}}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

and
\langle x\rangle:=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}
are the Japanese brackets.

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality we easily obtain the set of inequalities

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\rho^{-1}|x|^{1/2}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{-3/2}\rho u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{2}_{|x|}L^{\infty}_{\theta}}\left\lVert|x|^{-1}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{|x|}L^{2}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}\left\lVert|x|^{-1}u\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{|x|}L^{2}_{\theta}}\,,

which inserted in Corollary
1
give us the first three weighted-
L^{2}
estimates.

The last one is instead obtained making use of the celebrated Kato-Yajima result in
[
26
]
, that is

\left\lVert|x|^{-1}R_{0}(z)f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2(n-2)}}\left\lVert|x|f\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

with the best constant furnished by Simon
[
27
]
, combined with the trivial bounds

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert|x|^{-1}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

given again by Hölder’s inequality.
∎

We can return now to the Dirac operator. As a consequences of Corollaries
1
&
2
we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\colon|\zeta|\geq m\}
. Then

\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq C_{2}\left[1+\left\lvert\frac{z+m}{z-m}\right\rvert^{\sgn\Re z/2}\right]\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}f\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}

where
C_{2}
is defined in (
2.7
), and in particular

\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C_{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}^{2}\left[1+\left\lvert\frac{z+m}{z-m}\right\rvert^{\sgn\Re z/2}\right]\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

(3.4)

for any positive weight
\rho\in\ell^{2}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

If in addition
|x|^{1/2}\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

(3.5)

where
C_{1}
is defined in the statement of Theorem
3
.

Proof.

By Corollary
1
and the identity

(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}=(\mathcal{D}_{m}+z)(-\Delta+m^{2}-z^{2})^{-1}I_{N}

we obtain

\begin{split}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq&\,\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\partial_{k}R_{0}(z^{2}-m^{2})f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\\
&+\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho(m\alpha_{0}+zI_{N})R_{0}(z^{2}-m^{2})f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\\
\leq&\,\sqrt{n}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho\nabla R_{0}(z^{2}-m^{2})f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\\
&+\max\{|z+m|,|z-m|\}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}\rho R_{0}(z^{2}-m^{2})f\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}\\
\leq&\,C_{2}\left[1+\left|\frac{z+m}{z-m}\right|^{\sgn(\Re z)/2}\right]\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}\rho^{-1}f\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}.\end{split}

Similarly we have the other two inequalities, using Corollary
2

and the fact that

\max\{|z+m|,|z-m|\}\langle z^{2}-m^{2}\rangle^{-1/2}\leq 2m+1

for the homogenous estimate (
3.5
). Note also that, in the massless case, (
3.5
) is already contained in (
3.4
).
∎

4
The Birman-Schwinger principle

In this section, we recall the technicalities of the Birman-Schwinger principle and we properly define an operator perturbed by a factorizable potential. We rely completely on the abstract analysis carried out by Hansmann and Krejčiřík in
[
24
]
, to which we refer for more results and background.
There, in addition to the point spectrum, appropriate versions of the principle are stated even for the residual, essential and continuous spectra.

Let us start recalling some spectral definitions. The
spectrum

\sigma(H)
of a closed operator
H
in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
is the set of the complex numbers
z
for which
H-z\colon\dom(H)\to\mathfrak{H}
is not bijective. The
resolvent set
is the complement of the spectrum,
\rho(H):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(H)
. The
point spectrum

\sigma_{p}(H)
is the set of eigenvalues of
H
, namely the set of complex number such that
H-z
is not injective. The
continuous spectrum

\sigma_{c}(H)
is the set of elements of
\sigma(H)\setminus\sigma_{p}(H)
such that the closure of the range of
H-z
equals
\mathfrak{H}
; if instead such closure is a proper subset of
H
, we speak of the
residual spectrum

\sigma_{r}(H)
.

Here we collect the set of hypotheses we need.

Assumption I
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
be complex separable Hilbert spaces,
H_{0}
be a self-adjoint operator in
\mathfrak{H}
and
|H_{0}|:=(H_{0}^{2})^{1/2}
its absolute value. Also, let
A\colon\dom(A)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
and
B\colon\dom(B)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
be linear operators such that
\dom(|H_{0}|^{1/2})\subseteq\dom(A)\cap\dom(B)
.

We assume that for some (and hence for all)
b>0
the operators
A(|H_{0}|+b)^{-1/2}
and
B(|H_{0}|+b)^{-1/2}

are bounded and linear from
\mathfrak{H}
to
\mathfrak{H}
.

At this point, defining
G_{0}:=|H_{0}|+1
, we can consider, for any
z\in\rho(H_{0})
, the
Birman-Schwinger operator

K_{z}:=[AG_{0}^{-1/2}][G_{0}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}][BG_{0}^{-1/2}]^{*},

(4.1)

which is linear and bounded from
\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
to
\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
.

The second assumption we need is stated below.

Assumption II
.

There exists
z_{0}\in\rho(H_{0})
such that

-1\not\in\sigma(K_{z_{0}}).

While in general Assumption
I
is easy to check in the applications, Assumption
II
is more tricky. Thus, we can replace it with the following one, stronger but more manageable.

Assumption II
\,{}^{\prime}
.

There exists
z_{0}\in\rho(H_{0})
such that
\left\lVert K_{z_{0}}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}}<1
.

That the latter implies Assumption
II
can be easily proved by observing that the spectral radius is dominated by the operator norm, or recurring to Neumann series. Alternative conditions implying Assumption
II
are collected in Lemma 1 of
[
24
]
, but for our purposes Assumption
II

′

will be enough.

Before recalling the Birman-Schwinger principle, we properly define the formal perturbed operator
H_{0}+V
with
V=B^{*}A
.

Theorem 6
.

Under Assumptions
I
and
II
, there exists a unique closed extension
H_{V}
of
H_{0}+V
such that
\dom(H_{V})\subseteq\dom(|H_{0}|^{1/2})
and the following representation formula holds true:

(\phi,H_{V}\psi)_{\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}}=(G_{0}^{1/2}\phi,(H_{0}G_{0}^{-1}+[BG_{0}^{-1/2}]^{*}AG_{0}^{-1/2})G_{0}^{1/2}\psi)_{\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}}

for
\phi\in\dom(|H_{0}|^{1/2})
,
\psi\in\dom(H_{V})
.

This result correspond to Theorem 5 in
[
24
]
, where the operator
H_{V}
is obtained via the pseudo-Friedrichs extension. However, an alternative approach to obtain a closed (and quasi-selfadjoint) extension of
H_{0}+B^{*}A
is following Kato
[
25
]
. We refer to the paper of Hansmann and Krejčiřík for a cost-benefit comparison of the two methods, and for a list of cases when the two extensions coincide.

Finally, we can exhibit the abstract Birman-Schwinger principle, for the proof of which see Theorem
6, 7, 8
and Corollary 4 of
[
24
]
.

Theorem 7
.

Under Assumption
I
and
II
, we have:

(i)

if
z\in\rho(H_{0})
, then
z\in\sigma_{p}(H_{V})
if and only if
-1\in\sigma_{p}(K_{z})
;

(ii)

if
z\in\sigma_{c}(H_{0})\cap\sigma_{p}(H_{V})
and
H_{V}\psi=z\psi
for
0\neq\psi\in\dom(H_{V})
, then
A\psi\neq 0
and

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{\pm}}(K_{z+i\varepsilon}A\psi,\phi)_{\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}}=-(A\psi,\phi)_{\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}}

for all
\phi\in\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
.

In particular

(i)

if
z\in\sigma_{p}(H_{V})\cap\rho(H_{0})
, then
\left\lVert K_{z}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}}\geq 1
;

(ii)

if
z\in\sigma_{p}(H_{V})\cap\sigma_{c}(H_{0})
, then
\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{\pm}}\left\lVert K_{z+i\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}}\geq 1.

While from the “in particular” part of the previous theorem one could infer a localization for the eigenvalues of
H_{V}
, the principle can be employed in a “negative” way to prove their absence when the norm of the Birman-Schwinger operator is strictly less than
1
uniformly respect to
z\in\rho(H_{0})
.
This is precisely stated in the next concluding result, corresponding to Theorem 3 in
[
24
]
, which is even richer: not only gives information on the absence of the eigenvalues, but also on the invariance of the spectrum of the perturbed operator.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose Assumption
I
and that

\sup_{z\in\rho(H_{0})}\left\lVert K_{z}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}}<1.

Then we have:

(i)

\sigma(H_{0})=\sigma(H_{V})
;

(ii)

\sigma_{p}(H_{V})\cup\sigma_{r}(H_{V})\subseteq\sigma_{p}(H_{0})
and
\sigma_{c}(H_{0})\subseteq\sigma_{c}(H_{V})
.

In particular, if
\sigma(H_{0})=\sigma_{c}(H_{0})
, then
\sigma(H_{V})=\sigma_{c}(H_{V})=\sigma_{c}(H_{0})
.

4.1
A concrete case

We now specialize the situation from the abstract to a concrete setting, typical in many common applications and relevant for this note.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N\times N})
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
V
is the multiplication operator generated in
\mathfrak{H}
by a matrix-valued (scalar-valued if
N=1
) function
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
, with initial domain
\dom(V)=C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N})
.
As customary, we consider the factorization of
V
given by the polar decomposition
V=UW
, where
W=\sqrt{V^{*}V}
and the unitary matrix
U
is a partial isometry. Therefore we may set
A=\sqrt{W}
,
B=\sqrt{W}U^{*}
and consider the corresponding multiplication operators generated by
A
and
B^{*}
in
\mathfrak{H}
with initial domain
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N})
, denoted by the same symbols.
In the end, we can factorize the potential
V
in two closed operators
A
and
B^{*}
. Via the closed graph theorem, Assumption
I
is verified.

Furthermore, in general the operator
K_{z}
defined in (
4.1
) is a bounded extension of the classical Birman-Schwinger operator
A(H_{0}-z)^{-1}B^{*}
defined on
\dom(B^{*})
. Since in our case the initial domain of
B^{*}
is
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{N})
, hence dense in
\mathfrak{H}
, we get that
K_{z}
is exactly the closure of
A(H_{0}-z)^{-1}B^{*}
.

In conclusion, as suggested in the Introduction, everything reduces to the study of
\left\lVert A(H_{0}-z)^{-1}B^{*}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}}
: if there exists
z_{0}\in\rho(H_{0})
such that this norm is strictly less than
1
, then Theorem
7
holds; if this is true uniformly respect to
z\in\rho(H_{0})
, then also Theorem
8
holds true.

5
Proofs of the main theorems

Taking into account the last subsection
and recalling the uniform resolvent estimates
from Section
3
,
proving our claimed results on the Klein-Gordon and Dirac operators is now a simple matter.

For
z\in\rho(H_{0})
and
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, from the resolvent estimate in Lemma
1
, we immediately get

\displaystyle\left\lVert A(\mathcal{G}_{m}-z)^{-1}B^{*}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert A\tau_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{m}-z)^{-1}B^{*}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert A\tau_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}B^{*}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle<\alpha C\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

If
\alpha=1/C
, then Theorem
1
follows from Theorem
8
. By analogous computations one obtains Theorem
2
making use of the resolvent estimates in Lemma
2
, and the other theorems concerning the Dirac operator exploiting Lemma
4
.

Let us just make explicit the computations for Theorem
5
with
N_{1}(V)=\left\lVert|x|V\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{\infty}}
. By Lemma
4
we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert A(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}B^{*}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert A|x|^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}\left\lVert|x|^{-1/2}(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}B^{*}\phi\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left[1+\left\lvert\frac{z+m}{z-m}\right\rvert^{\sgn\Re z/2}\right]\left\lVert A|x|^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}L^{\infty}}\left\lVert|x|^{1/2}B^{*}\phi\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left[1+\left\lvert\frac{z+m}{z-m}\right\rvert^{\sgn\Re z/2}\right]\left\lVert|x|V\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Setting
\mathcal{V}_{1}:=[1/[C_{2}N_{1}(V)]-1]^{2}>1
, the condition
\left\lVert A(\mathcal{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}B^{*}\phi\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}}\geq 1
turns out to be equivalent to the expression

\left(\Re z-\sgn(\Re z)m\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{2}+1}{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{2}-1}\right)^{2}+(\Im z)^{2}\leq\left(m\frac{2\mathcal{V}_{1}}{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{2}-1}\right)^{2}

which define exactly the disks in the statement of the theorem. Just take any
z_{0}\in\rho(\mathcal{D}_{m})
outside these two disks to verify Assumption
II

′

, and finally we can prove the statement applying the “in particular” part of Theorem
7
.

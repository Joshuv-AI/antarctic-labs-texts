1
Introduction

A
weighing matrix
of order
v
and weight
k
, denoted
\text{W}(v,k)
, is a
(0,\pm 1)
-matrix
W
of order
v
such that
WW^{\top}=kI
. The special cases in which
k=n-1
and
k=n
are termed the
Conference
and
Hadamard
matrices, respectively.

A
unit-weighing matrix

W
of order
v
and weight
k
is a matrix with entries from
\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{C}\mid|x|=1\}
of order
v
such that
WW^{*}=kI_{v}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{n}=\{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{n}}:0\leq m<n\}
be the set of the complex
n
-th roots of unity, and let
W
be a matrix of order
v
over
\{0\}\cup\mathcal{R}_{n}
such that
WW^{*}=kI_{v}
. We then say that
W
is a
Butson weighing
matrix of order
v
and weight
k
over
\mathcal{R}_{n}
.

A weighing matrix is said to be
balanced
if after changing all the entries to their absolute values, it reduces to the incidence matrix of a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
balanced incomplete block design. In this case, the design is said to be
signed
and has the property of being
signable
.

The largest class of balanced weighing matrices are those with the classical parameters
\left(v=\frac{q^{m+1}-1}{q-1},k=q^{m},\lambda=q^{m}-q^{m-1}\right)
over cyclic groups, where
q
is a prime power. Recently, it was shown in
[
kps21
]
that the symmetric designs with parameters
\left(v=1+9\cdot\frac{9^{m+1}-1}{4},k=9^{m+1},\lambda=4\cdot 9^{m}\right)
are signable.

In this paper, we introduce a new class of weighing matrices which we will call
quasi-balanced
. For a quasi-balanced weighing matrix
W
, the absolute value matrix, i.e. the matrix whose entries are the absolute values of the entries in
W
, denoted
|W|
, enjoys the properties that
|W|
and
|W|^{\top}
commute, and the product
|W||W|^{\top}
has at most two off-diagonal entries. Of particular interest is the case in which the matrix
|W|
is highly structured, such as being the adjacency matrix of a strongly regular graph or the incidence matrix of a symmetric group divisible design.

A strongly regular graph with adjacency matrix
A
is signable if some of the nonzero entries of
A
can be changed to
-1
such that the resulting matrix is a quasi-balanced weighing matrix. In our study of signable strongly regular graphs, we were led to consider the larger class of Butson weighing matrices.

Finally, we study the association schemes obtained from signed strongly regular graphs or symmetric group divisible designs with certain block regularity conditions and the reverse implications.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this work,
I_{n},J_{n},O_{n}
denote the identity matrix, all-ones matrix, and zero matrix all of order
n
, respectively.
We omit the subscript when the order is clear from the context.

A
negacirculant matrix
of order
2n
is a polynomial in the signed permutation (nega-shift) matrix of order
n

N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
-1&0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

The matrix
N
forms a multiplicative cyclic group of order
2n
which we denote as
\mathcal{N}_{2,n}
. Note that
g^{-1}=g^{\top}
and
-g=g^{n}
, for every
g\in\mathcal{N}_{2,n}
.

The idea of the previous paragraph can be generalized as follows. Let
\omega
be a generator of
\mathcal{R}_{u}
. Then an
\omega
-circulant matrix is a polynomial in the monomial (
\omega
-shift) matrix of order
n

U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
\omega&0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

As before, this generates a multiplicative cyclic group of order
\absolutevalue{\omega}n
which we will denote as
\mathcal{N}_{u,n}
. Note that
g^{-1}=g^{*}
and
\omega g=g^{n}
, for every
g\in\mathcal{N}_{u,n}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an undirected graph with
|V|=v
. The
adjacency matrix
A
of
\mathcal{G}
is a
v\times v

(0,1)
-matrix with rows and columns indexed by the elements of
V
such that
A_{xy}=1
if and only if
\{x,y\}\in E
. A graph is said to be a
strongly regular graph
with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
if its adjacency matrix
A
satisfies
A^{2}=kI_{v}+\lambda A+\mu(J_{v}-I_{v}-A)
. A graph is said to be a
Deza graph
with parameters
(v,k,b,a)
,
b>a
, if its adjacency matrix
A
satisfies
A^{2}=kI_{v}+bX+a(J_{v}-I_{v}-X)
for some symmetric
(0,1)
-matrix
X
with diagonal entries
0
. Of course, a Deza graph for which
X=A
is a strongly regular graph. Strongly regular graphs are studied in
[
BVM
]
. Strongly regular graphs in the context of the more general distance regular graphs and along with their connections to the objects used in the essay are studied together in
[
BCN
,
CVL
,
Tonchev
]
.

Let
m,n
be positive integers both not smaller than two. A
(square) group divisible design
with parameters
(v,k,m,n,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is a pair
(V,\mathcal{B})
, where
V
is a finite set of
v
elements called points, and
\mathcal{B}
a collection of
k
-element subsets of
V
called blocks, where
|\mathcal{B}|=v
, and where the following are satisfied:

1.

The point set
V
can be partitioned into
m
equinumerous classes of size
n
,

2.

distinct points from the same class are incident with
\lambda_{1}
blocks, and

3.

points from distinct classes are incident with
\lambda_{2}
blocks.

A group divisible design is said to be
symmetric
(or to have the
dual property
) if its dual, that is, the incidence structure obtained by interchanging the roles of the points and blocks is again a group divisible design with the same parameters.
Throughout this paper, we always assume that
k>0
.

The
incidence matrix
of a symmetric group divisible design is a
v\times v

(0,1)
-matrix
A
with rows and columns indexed by the elements of
\mathcal{B}
and
V
, respectively, such that, for
x\in V
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle A_{B,x}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in B,\\
0&\text{ if }x\not\in B.\end{cases}

After reordering the elements of
V
and
\mathcal{B}
appropriately,

\displaystyle AA^{\top}=A^{\top}A=kI_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{J}_{m,n}-I_{v})+\lambda_{2}(J_{v}-\mathcal{J}_{m,n}),

(1)

where
\mathcal{J}_{m,n}=I_{m}\otimes J_{n}
. Then the
v\times v

(0,1)
-matrix
A
is the incidence matrix of a symmetric group divisible design if and only if the matrix
A
satisfies Equation (
1
). Thus, we also refer to the
v\times v

(0,1)
-matrix
A
satisfying (
1
) as a symmetric group divisible design. A
divisible design graph
is a graph such that its adjacency matrix is the incidence matrix of a symmetric group divisible design. The reader may consult the encyclopedic work
[
BJL
]
for greater detail of divisible designs and related configurations.

Let
G
be a multiplicatively written finite group not containing the symbol
0
. A
balanced generalized weighing matrix
with parameters
(v,k,\lambda)
over
G
, denoted
\text{BGW}(v,k,\lambda)
over
G
, is a matrix
W
of order
v
with entries from
\{0\}\cup G
such that

1.

every row of
W
contains exactly
k
nonzero entries, and

2.

for any distinct
i,h\in\{1,2,\ldots,v\}
, every element of
G
has exactly
\lambda/|G|
copies in the multiset
\{W_{ij}W^{-1}_{hj}:1\leq j\leq v,W_{ij}\neq 0,W_{hj}\neq 0\}
.

A BGW with
k=\lambda=v
is said to be a
generalized Hadamard matrix
, and a BGW with
k=v-1,\lambda=v-2
is said to be a
generalized conference matrix
. A BGW, say
W
, over
C_{2n}=\langle g\rangle
, a cyclic group of order
2n
, is skew-symmetric if
W_{ji}=g^{n}W_{ij}
whenever
i\neq j
and
W_{ij}\neq 0
, and
W_{ii}=0
, for every
i
. For an overview of the theory of balanced generalized weighing matrices, please consult
[
JK
]
and the references cited therein. The reader may also profitably consult the monograph
[
IS
]
for a study of BGWs together with their relations to the objects studied here.

The largest class of known balanced generalized weighing matrices are those with the so-called classical parameters corresponding to the prime power
q

\left(\frac{q^{m+1}-1}{q-1},q^{m},q^{m}-q^{m-1}\right)

over a cyclic group
C_{n}
in any of the following situations:

1.

For any odd
q
, positive integer
m
, and a divisor
n
of
q-1
;

2.

q
is even,
m
is odd, and
n
a divisor of
q-1
; and

3.

q
and
m
are even, and
n
a divisor of
2(q-1)
.

Moreover, these matrices can be assumed to be
\omega
-circulant . Furthermore, for odd
q
, if the ratio
\frac{q-1}{n}
is odd. the matrix is skew-symmetric; and if the ratio is even, the matrix is symmetric. See
[
arasu
,
gm2
,
IS
,
jt
,
jt-ii
]
for the constructions of these objects.

We follow
[
BI
]
as our reference for the remaining part of this section. A
symmetric association scheme
with
d
-classes
and a finite vertex set
X
, is a set of non-zero
(0,1)
-matrices
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}
with rows and columns indexed by
X
, such that

(i)

A_{0}=I_{|X|}
;

(ii)

\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}=J_{|X|}
;

(iii)

A_{i}^{\top}=A_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
; and

(iv)

for any
i
,
j
and
k
, there exist non-negative integers
p_{i,j}^{k}
such that
A_{i}A_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}p_{i,j}^{k}A_{k}
.

Note that a graph with adjacency matrix
A
is strongly regular if and only if
\{I,A,J-I-A\}
is a symmetric association scheme with
2
-classes. The
intersection matrix

B_{i}
is defined by
(B_{i})_{jk}=p_{i,j}^{k}
. Since each
A_{i}
is symmetric, it follows from condition (iv) that the
A_{i}
’s necessarily commute. The linear space spanned by the
A_{i}
’s over the complex number field is closed under standard matrix multiplication and, therefore, forms a commutative algebra. We denote this algebra by
\mathcal{A}
, and it is called the
Bose-Mesner algebra
. There exists also a basis of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of primitive idempotents, say
E_{0}=(1/|X|)J_{|X|},E_{1},\ldots,E_{d}
. Since
\{A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}\}
and
\{E_{0},E_{1},\ldots,E_{d}\}
are two bases of
\mathcal{A}
, there exist change-of-basis matrices
P
and
Q
such that

\displaystyle A_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}P_{ij}E_{i},\quad E_{j}=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{i=0}^{d}Q_{ij}A_{i}.

The matrices
P,Q
are said to be the
first and second eigenmatrices
, respectively.

3
Quasi-balanced weighing matrices

3.1
Definition

We now introduce the concept of a quasi-balanced weighing matrix for the first time.
A weighing matrix
W
is said to be
quasi-balanced
if the matrix
|W|
defined by
|W|_{ij}=|W_{ij}|
commutes with
|W|^{\top}
, and if the product
|W||W|^{\top}
has at most two off-diagonal entries.

Whenever
|W||W|^{\top}
has exactly one off-diagonal entry, the
(0,\pm 1)
-weighing matrix
W
coincides with a balanced weighing matrix.
Throughout this paper we restrict our interest to the following four possibilities for the structure of
|W|
:

(i)

The adjacency matrix of a strongly regular graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
. Then
|W|=A
satisfies
A^{\top}=A
and

A^{2}=kI_{v}+\lambda_{1}A+\mu(J_{v}-I_{v}-A);

(ii)

the adjacency matrix of a divisible design graph with parameters
(v,k,m,n,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
. Then
|W|=A
satisfies
A^{\top}=A
and

A^{2}=(k-\lambda_{1})I_{v}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\mathcal{J}_{m,n}+\lambda_{2}J_{v};

(iii)

the incidence matrix of a symmetric group divisible design with parameters
(v,k,m,n,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
. Then
|W|=A
satisfies

AA^{\top}=A^{\top}A=(k-\lambda_{1})I_{v}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\mathcal{J}_{m,n}+\lambda_{2}J_{v}\text{; and}

(iv)

the adjacency matrix of a Deza graph with parameters
(v,k,b,a)
. Then
|W|=A
satisfies
A^{\top}=A
and

A^{2}=kI_{v}+bX+a(J_{v}-I_{v}-X),

for some symmetric
(0,1)
-matrix
X
with diagonal entries
0
.

The concept of
Siamese objects
and the related graph decomposition will be touched upon in what follows. The reader is referred to
[
kt
,
krw
]
for the appropriate definitions.

3.2
Constructions for signed strongly regular graphs

It was shown in
[
kt
]
that
the complete graph
K_{1+q+q^{2}+q^{3}}
on
1+q+q^{2}+q^{3}
vertices can be decomposed into
1+q
strongly regular graphs sharing
1+q^{2}
cliques of size
1+q
. In this section it is shown that the decomposition is singable.
We give the constructions according to the three cases
q\equiv-1\text{ (mod $4$)}
,
q\equiv 1\text{ (mod $4$)}
, and finally
q\equiv 0\text{ (mod $2$)}
.

We begin with the case that
q\equiv-1\pmod{4}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power congruent to
-1\pmod{4}
. Then there is a quasi-balanced weighing matrix
\text{W}(1+q+q^{2}+q^{3},q+q^{2})
, say
W
, for which
|W|
is an

\text{SRG}(1+q+q^{2}+q^{3},q+q^{2},q-1,q+1).

Furthermore,
K_{1+q+q^{2}+q^{3}}
decomposes into
1+q
Siamese signed strongly regular graphs with the above parameters sharing
1+q^{2}
cliques of size
1+q
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the skew-symmetric
\text{BGW}(1+q^{2},q^{2},q^{2}-1)
over the cyclic group
\mathcal{N}_{2,1+q}
of order
2+2q
generated by the nega-shift matrix
N
of order
1+q
. It follows from Section 3 of
[
jt
]
that there is a negacirculant
\text{W}(q+1,q)
with zero diagonal, say
C
. Let
W
be the block matrix defined by

\displaystyle W_{ij}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}C&\text{if }i=j\text{, and}\\
H_{ij}R&\text{if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

where
R
is the back identity matrix of order
1+q
. We claim that
W
is a quasi-balanced weighing matrix
\text{W}(1+q+q^{2}+q^{3},q+q^{2})
.

To see this, let
r_{1},\dots,r_{v}
be the block rows of
W
. Then

r_{i}r_{i}^{\top}=CC^{\top}+\sum_{i=1}^{q^{2}}N^{\ell_{i}}R(N^{\ell_{i}}R)^{\top},

for some
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{q^{2}}\in\{0,\dots,2q-1\}
. But
CC^{\top}=qI
and
N^{\ell}R(N^{\ell}R)^{\top}=I
, for all
\ell
; hence,
r_{i}r_{i}^{\top}=(q+q^{2})I
.

Next, if
i<j
, then

\displaystyle r_{i}r_{j}^{\top}

\displaystyle=C(H_{ji}R)^{\top}+H_{ij}RC^{\top}+\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)\sum_{g\in\mathcal{N}_{2,1+q}}g

\displaystyle=-CH_{ij}R+CH_{ij}R+\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)\sum_{g\in\mathcal{N}_{2,1+q}}g.

Since the elements of
\mathcal{N}_{2,1+q}
sum to
O
, it follows that
r_{i}r_{j}^{\top}=O
.

Next, we show that
\absolutevalue{W}
is an SRG with the stated parameters, where it remains to show that
\lambda=q-1
and
\mu=q+1
. Note that

\absolutevalue{W}_{ij}=\begin{cases}J-I&\text{if $i=j$, and}\\
\absolutevalue{H_{ij}}R&\text{if $i\neq j$.}\end{cases}

Then
\absolutevalue{r_i}\absolutevalue{r_i}^{T}=(J-I)^{2}+q^{2}I=(q+q^{2})I+(q-1)(J-I)
. For
i<j
, we have that

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{r_i}\absolutevalue{r_j}^{T}

\displaystyle=(J-I)(\absolutevalue{H_{ji}}R)^{T}+(\absolutevalue{H_{ij}}R)(J-I)^{T}+(q-1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}\absolutevalue{N}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=(q+1)J-2\absolutevalue{H_{ij}}R,

which concludes the derivation.

Finally, for
\ell\in\{0,\dots,q\}
, we define
W_{\ell}
by

(W_{\ell})_{ij}=\begin{cases}C&\text{if }i=j\text{, and}\\
H_{ij}N^{\ell}R&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Then
W=W_{0},\dots,W_{q}
is the required signed decomposition of
K_{1+q+q^{2}+q^{3}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

be a prime power congruent to

\text{W}(1+q+q^{2}+q^{3},q+q^{2})

\text{SRG}(1+q+q^{2}+q^{3},q+q^{2},q-1,q+1).

Furthermore, the complete graph on

with the above parameters all sharing

Proof.

The construction is similar to the construction in Theorem

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

be an even prime power. Then there is a quasi-balanced weighing matrix
\text{W}(1+q+q^{2}+q^{3},1+q^{3})
of quaternions, say

, for which the off-diagonal entries of
\absolutevalue{W}\absolutevalue{W}^{\top}

Proof.

Let
H
be the symmetric

\displaystyle W_{ij}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}kI_{q}&\text{if }i=j\text{, and}\\

Let
r_{1},\dots,r_{v}

Next, noting that

3.3
Constructions for signed symmetric group divisible designs

We now present several constructions of signed divisible designs.

Theorem 3.7
.

If there exist a
\text{BGW}(m,m,m)
over the cyclic group of order
2n
and a
\text{BGW}(n,k,\lambda)
over
\{1,-1\}
, then there exists a quasi-balanced weighing matrix
\text{W}(mn,mk)
, say
W
, for which
|W|
is an

\text{SGDD}\left(mn,mk,m,n,m\lambda,\frac{m}{n}k^{2}\right)

|W|\mathcal{J}_{m,n}=\mathcal{J}_{m,n}|W|=kJ_{mn}

Proof.

Let
C_{1}
be a
\text{BGW}(m,m,m)
over the cyclic group of order

of order

, and

be a
\text{BGW}(n,k,\lambda)
over
\{1,-1\}

, write the

as

for

.

Define an
m\times m

(0,\pm 1)
block matrix

by
W_{ij}=N^{c_{ij}}C_{2}
. Then
W
is a quasi-balanced weighing matrix. Indeed, for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

\displaystyle(WW^{\top})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}(N^{c_{i\ell}}C_{2})(N^{c_{j\ell}}C_{2})^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}N^{c_{i\ell}}C_{2}C_{2}^{\top}N^{-c_{j\ell}}

\displaystyle=k\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}N^{c_{i\ell}-c_{j\ell}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}mkI_{n}&\text{if $i=j$, and}\\
O&\text{if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

which shows that
W
is a weighing matrix of order
mn
and weight
mk
.
Since
W_{ij}=N^{c_{ij}}C_{2}
,
|W|_{ij}=P^{c_{ij}}|C_{2}|
where
P
is the circulant matrix with first row
(010\cdots 0)
of order
n
, we have

\displaystyle(|W||W|^{\top})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}(P^{c_{i\ell}}|C_{2}|)(P^{c_{j\ell}}|C_{2}|)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}P^{c_{i\ell}}|C_{2}|\cdot|C_{2}|^{\top}P^{-c_{j\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}P^{c_{i\ell}}((k-\lambda)I+\lambda J)P^{-c_{j\ell}}

\displaystyle=(k-\lambda)\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}P^{c_{i\ell}-c_{j\ell}}+\lambda\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}P^{c_{i\ell}}JP^{-c_{j\ell}}

\displaystyle=(k-\lambda)\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}P^{c_{i\ell}-c_{j\ell}}+\lambda\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}J

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}m((k-\lambda)I+\lambda J)&\text{if $i=j$, and}\\
(\frac{m}{n}(k-\lambda)+m\lambda)J&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(|W|^{\top}|W|)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}(P^{c_{i\ell}}|C_{2}|)^{\top}(P^{c_{j\ell}}|C_{2}|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq m}|C_{2}|^{\top}P^{-c_{i\ell}+c_{j\ell}}|C_{2}|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}m|C_{2}|^{\top}|C_{2}|&\text{if $i=j$, and}\\
\frac{m}{n}|C_{2}|^{\top}J|C_{2}|&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}m((k-\lambda)I+\lambda J)&\text{if $i=j$, and}\\
\frac{m}{n}k^{2}J&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

By the equality
k(k-1)=\lambda(n-1)
, we have
|W||W|^{\top}=|W|^{\top}|W|
.
Therefore
|W|
is a symmetric group divisible design with parameters
(mn,mk,m,n,m\lambda,\frac{m}{n}k^{2})
.
The property that
|W|\mathcal{J}_{m,n}=\mathcal{J}_{m,n}|W|=kJ_{mn}
is easy to see.
∎

3.4
Computer searches

To make a computer search feasible the case of a quasi-balanced signing of the adjacency matrix of a strongly regular graph is considered in this section.

Let
W
be a quasi-balanced signing of an SRG
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
over
\mathcal{R}_{n}
, and consider the multisets

S_{ij}=\{W_{i\ell}\overline{W_{j\ell}}:0\leq\ell<v,W_{i\ell}\neq 0,W_{j\ell}\neq 0\},\qquad 0\leq i<j<v.

If each element of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
appears either
\lambda/n
or
\mu/n
times in every
S_{ij}
predicated upon whether or not
W_{ij}\neq 0
, then we say that the signing is
srg-balanced
. Note that this assumes
n
is a common divisor of
\lambda
and
\mu
.

Assume now that
W
corresponds to a balanced signing of an SRG
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
with adjacency matrix
A
, and continue to assume that
i\neq j
. If the
i
-th and
j
-th rows of
A
are incident in
\lambda
nonzero positions, i.e.
W_{ij}\neq 0
, then the conjugate inner product of the corresponding rows of
W
must contain each element of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
a constant number of times, namely,
\lambda/n
times. Similarly, if
W_{ij}=0
, then the product between the corresponding rows must contain each element of
\mathcal{R}_{n}

\mu/n
times. Put another way,
W
corresponds to an srg-balanced signing if and only if

WW^{*}=kI_{v}+\frac{\lambda}{n}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in R_{n}}\alpha\right)A+\frac{\mu}{n}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in R_{n}}\alpha\right)(J_{v}-I_{v}-A).

Since
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\alpha=0
, we obtain again that
WW^{*}=kI_{v}
. Evidently, then, an srg-balanced signing implies the property of being a Butson weighing matrix. The converse is not true in general, however.

We consider quasi-balanced weighing matrices

such that

\displaystyle=kA_{0}+\frac{1}{2}(k+s+t+st)A_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k+st)A_{3},

\displaystyle A_{5}A_{6}

\displaystyle=A_{6}A_{5}=kA_{1}+\frac{1}{2}(k+s+t+st)A_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k+st)A_{3},

\displaystyle A_{6}^{2}

\displaystyle=kA_{0}+\frac{1}{2}(k+s+t+st)A_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k+st)A_{3}.

From these identities, it follows that
(A_{5}-A_{6})^{2}=2k(A_{0}-A_{1})
, that is,

\displaystyle(W_{1}-W_{2})(W_{1}^{\top}-W_{2}^{\top})

\displaystyle=kI.

Hence,
W_{1}-W_{2}
is a quasi-balanced weighing matrix whose absolute matrix is a strongly regular graph with the desired parameters.
∎

4.2
Association schemes from signed symmetric group divisible designs

The constructed quasi-balanced weighing matrices

in Theorems

and

have the property that
|W|\mathcal{J}_{m,n}=\mathcal{J}_{m,n}|W|=\frac{k}{m}J_{v}
or that
|W|\mathcal{J}_{m,n}=\mathcal{J}_{m,n}|W|=\frac{k}{m-1}(J_{v}-\mathcal{J}_{m,n})

In this subsection, we provide a relation between such quasi-balanced weighing matrices and association schemes.

For a weighing matrix

, write

and

Let

\displaystyle A_{2}

.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume
mn>k
.
If there exists an association scheme with the eigenmatrices given in Theorem
4.3
, then there exists a quasi-balanced weighing matrix
W
such that
|W|
is a
\text{SGDD}(v,k,m,n,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
with the property that
|W|\mathcal{J}_{m,n}=\mathcal{J}_{m,n}|W|=\frac{k}{m}J_{v}
.

Proof.

Consider the matrix
A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}
. Its eigenvalues are
2mn-1
and
-1
with multiplicities
2
and
4mn-2
respectively. Then
A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}
is the adjacency matrix of two copies of
K_{2mn}
, the complete graph on
2mn
vertices.
Next, by the eigenvalues of
A_{1}
, it is the adjacency matrix of a graph of disjoint
2v
edges.
We may assume that
A_{1}
is of the form in (
6
).
Thirdly consider
A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2}
.
By the given eigenmatrices, we find that
A_{1}(A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2})=(A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2})A_{1}=A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2}
, which yields

A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{2}\otimes X&0\\
0&J_{2}\otimes Y\end{pmatrix},

for some matrices
X
and
Y
.
Again by the eigenmatrices, we find
(A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2})^{2}=2n(A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2})
and thus
X^{2}=nX
and
Y^{2}=nY
. This equation shows that
X=Y=\mathcal{J}_{m,n}
after suitably permuting the columns and rows.
Therefore we have the forms in (
7
) and (
8
).

By the given eigenmatrices, we find that
A_{1}A_{4}=A_{5}
, which yields

A_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&W_{1}&W_{2}\\
0&0&W_{2}&W_{1}\\
W_{1}^{\top}&W_{2}^{\top}&0&0\\
W_{2}^{\top}&W_{1}^{\top}&0&0\end{pmatrix},A_{5}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&W_{2}&W_{1}\\
0&0&W_{1}&W_{2}\\
W_{2}^{\top}&W_{1}^{\top}&0&0\\
W_{1}^{\top}&W_{2}^{\top}&0&0\end{pmatrix},

for some
(0,1)
-matrices
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
.
Again, by the eigenmatrices, we have that

\displaystyle A_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle=kA_{0}+\frac{(k-m)k}{2m(n-1)}A_{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{2mn}A_{3},

\displaystyle A_{4}A_{5}

\displaystyle=A_{5}A_{4}=kA_{1}+\frac{(k-m)k}{2m(n-1)}A_{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{2mn}A_{3},

\displaystyle A_{5}^{2}

\displaystyle=kA_{0}+\frac{(k-m)k}{2m(n-1)}A_{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{2mn}A_{3}.

From these identities, it follows that
(A_{4}-A_{5})^{2}=2k(A_{0}-A_{1}),(A_{4}+A_{5})^{2}=2k(A_{0}+A_{1})+\frac{(k-m)k}{m(n-1)}A_{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{mn}A_{3}
, that is,

\displaystyle(W_{1}-W_{2})(W_{1}^{\top}-W_{2}^{\top})

\displaystyle=kI_{mn},

\displaystyle(W_{1}+W_{2})(W_{1}^{\top}+W_{2}^{\top})=(W_{1}^{\top}+W_{2}^{\top})(W_{1}+W_{2})

\displaystyle=(k-\lambda)I_{v}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\mathcal{J}_{m,n}+\lambda_{2}J_{mn}.

Finally,

\displaystyle(A_{0}+A_{2})A_{4}=A_{4}(A_{0}+A_{2})=\frac{k}{m}(A_{4}+A_{5}+A_{6})

implies that

(W_{1}+W_{2})\mathcal{J}_{m,n}=\mathcal{J}_{m,n}(W_{1}+W_{2})=\frac{k}{m}J_{v}.

Hence,
W_{1}-W_{2}
is a quasi-balanced weighing matrix with the desired properties and parameters.
∎

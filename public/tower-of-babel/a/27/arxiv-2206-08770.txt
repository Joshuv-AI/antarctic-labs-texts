Abstract
. We investigate the blow-up behavior of sequences of sign-changing solutions for the Yamabe equation on a Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) of positive Yamabe type. For each dimension n ≥ 11 n\geq 11 , we describe the value of the minimal energy threshold at which blow-up occurs. In dimensions 11 ≤ n ≤ 24 11\leq n\leq 24 , where the set of positive solutions is known to be compact, we show that the set of sign-changing solutions is not compact and that blow-up already occurs at the lowest possible energy level. We prove this result by constructing a smooth, non-locally conformally flat metric on space forms 𝕊 n / Γ \mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma , Γ ≠ { 1 } \Gamma\neq\{1\} , whose Yamabe equation admits a family of sign-changing blowing-up solutions. As a counterpart of this result, we also prove a sharp compactness result for sign-changing solutions at the lowest energy level, in small dimensions or under strong geometric assumptions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Introduction and statements of the main results

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed (i.e. compact and without boundary) Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. In this paper, we are interested in the existence of sequences of sign-changing blowing-up solutions
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
C^{2}\left(M\right)
to the nodal (or sign-changing) Yamabe equation

\Delta_{g}u_{k}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}u_{k}=\left|u_{k}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{k}\quad\text{in }M,

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{g}:=-\text{div}_{g}\nabla
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator,
c_{n}:=\frac{n-2}{4\left(n-1\right)}
,
\Scal_{g}
is the scalar curvature of the manifold and
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
is the critical exponent for the embeddings of the Sobolev space
H^{1}\left(M\right)
into Lebesgue’s spaces. Solutions of (
1.1
) are in
C^{3,\alpha}(M)
for
0<\alpha<\min(2^{*}-2,1)
by Trudinger’s result
[
56
]
and standard elliptic theory. We recall that
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to
blow up
as
k\to\infty
if
\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

The
Yamabe invariant
of the conformal class
[g]
is defined as

\displaystyle Y(M,[g])

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\hat{g}\in[g]}\left(\Vol_{\hat{g}}\left(M\right)^{\frac{2-n}{n}}\int_{M}\Scal_{\hat{g}}dv_{\hat{g}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\cdot\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}(M)}\frac{\int_{M}\left(|\nabla u|_{g}^{2}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}u^{2}\right)dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}|u|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}},

where
\Vol_{\hat{g}}\left(M\right)
is the volume of
\left(M,\hat{g}\right)
. Letting
\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std}\right)
be the standard unit sphere of dimension
n\geq 3
, by conformal invariance, we also have

Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])=\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}},

so that
Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is the optimal constant for the Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
(M,g)
is of
positive Yamabe type
if
Y(M,[g])>0
, i.e.
\triangle_{g}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}
is coercive. In this case, when
(M,g)
is not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std})
(which we denote in what follows by
(M,g)\not\approx(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std})
), we have
Y(M,[g])<Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])
, and the existence of a positive solution
u_{0}
to the Yamabe equation

\Delta_{g}u_{0}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}u_{0}=u_{0}^{2^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }M

(1.2)

attaining the Yamabe invariant
Y(M,[g])
is known since the work of Trudinger
[
56
]
, Aubin
[
3
]
and Schoen
[
50
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in determining the value of the minimal energy level at which
sign-changing
blow-up occurs for (
1.1
). For every
u\in H^{1}(M)
, we define the energy of
u
as

E\left(u\right):=\int_{M}\left|u\right|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}.

We define
I(M,[g])\subset(0,\infty]
as the set of numbers
E\in(0,+\infty]
such that (
1.1
) admits a
blowing-up
sequence of solutions
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\limsup_{k\to\infty}E(u_{k})=E
. We then define

E(M,[g]):=\inf I(M,[g]).

Similarly, we let
I_{+}(M,[g])\subset(0,+\infty]
be the set of numbers
E\in(0,+\infty]
such that (
1.1
) admits a
blowing-up
sequence of
positive
solutions
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
\limsup_{k\to\infty}E(u_{k})=E
, and we define

E_{+}(M,[g]):=\inf I_{+}(M,[g]).

The value
+\infty
is allowed in the definitions of
E(M,[g])
and
E_{+}(M,[g])
and corresponds to sequences of solutions of (
1.1
) with diverging energies. When
I(M,[g])
(resp.
I_{+}(M,[g])
) is empty, it means that (
1.1
) does not admit any blowing-up solutions (resp. positive blowing-up solutions), and thus the set of solutions (resp. positive solutions) of (
1.1
) is compact in
C^{2}(M)
by standard elliptic theory. In this case, we let
E(M,[g]):=-\infty
(resp
E_{+}(M,[g]):=-\infty
). If
I(M,[g])\neq\emptyset
and
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of solutions of (
1.1
) satisfying
\limsup_{k\to\infty}E(u_{k})<E(M,[g])
, then by definition
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
does not blow-up, and is thus precompact in
C^{2}(M)
.

As long as
positive
solutions are considered,
E_{+}(M,[g])
is well understood: in the proof of the compactness of positive solutions of the Yamabe equation, assuming the validity of the Positive Mass Theorem when
n\geq 8
, Khuri–Marques–Schoen
[
25
]
(with previous contributions by Schoen
[
51
,
52
]
, Li–Zhu
[
29
]
, Druet
[
19
]
, Marques
[
30
]
and Li–Zhang
[
27
,
28
]
) proved that

E_{+}(M,[g])=-\infty\text{ when }\left\{\begin{aligned} &3\leq n\leq 24\text{ and }(M,g)\not\approx(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std}),\\
&(M,g)\text{ is locally conformally flat or }\\
&n\geq 6\text{ and }\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{n-6}{2}]}|\nabla^{k}\Weyl_{g}(x)|^{2}>0\;\;\forall x\in M,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

where
\Weyl_{g}
is the Weyl curvature tensor of the manifold. On the other side, Brendle
[
7
]
and Brendle–Marques
[
8
]
proved that there exists a non-locally conformally flat metric
g
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

E_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g])=Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}\quad\text{ when }n\geq 25.

(1.4)

The blow-up behavior of
sign-changing
solutions of (
1.1
) is far less understood. A simple application of Struwe’s celebrated
H^{1}
-compactness result
[
53
]
(see Proposition
5.1
below for a proof) shows that if
(M,g)
is of positive Yamabe type, then any
sign-changing
blowing-up sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of solutions of (
1.1
) satisfies

\liminf_{k\to\infty}E\left(u_{k}\right)\geq Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

(1.5)

As a consequence, if
g
is the Brendle
[
7
]
and Brendle–Marques
[
8
]
metric, then, by (
1.4
), we obtain

E(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g])=E_{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g])=Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}\quad\text{ when }n\geq 25.

In dimensions
n\geq 25
, blow-up for (
1.1
) thus occurs at the lowest energy level for positive solutions. Our aim in this paper is to investigate the situation for
sign-changing
solutions and in particular, the value of
E(M,[g])
in dimensions
n\leq 24
, where the set of
positive
solutions of (
1.1
) is compact, i.e.
E_{+}(M,[g])=-\infty
. As follows from (
1.5
), a lower bound on
E(M,[g])
is given by
Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Our main result shows that this lower bound is attained in dimensions
11\leq n\leq 24
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
11\leq n\leq 24
and let
\Gamma
be a finite subgroup of isometries of
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0})
,
\Gamma\neq\{Id\}
, acting freely and smoothly on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. There exists a smooth, non-locally conformally flat Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma
of positive Yamabe type and such that

E(M,[g])=Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

The metric
g
in Theorem
1.1
is not the quotient metric
g_{\Gamma}
but can be chosen arbitrarily close to it. Theorem
1.1
is a special case of a more general result, Theorem
2.2
below, which holds true in any dimension
n\geq 11
and for a larger class of manifolds than the spherical space forms
\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma
. We refer to Section
2
for more details on this regard.

By (
1.3
), the set of
positive
solutions of (
1.1
) on
(\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma,g)
, where
g
is given by Theorem
1.1
, is compact in
C^{2}(M)
. As Theorem
1.1
shows, however, in this case, the set of
sign-changing
solutions is not compact and blow-up already occurs at the minimal energy level. This phenomenon of loss of compactness for sign-changing solutions in situations where the set of positive solutions is compact was recently highlighted in Premoselli–Vétois
[
42
]
for critical Schrödinger-type equations in
M
. We prove Theorem
1.1
by constructing a sign-changing blowing-up sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of solutions of (
1.1
) such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}E\left(u_{k}\right)=Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

(1.6)

As a counterpart of Theorem
1.1
, we also prove the following result, which provides a lower bound for
E(M,[g])
in smaller dimensions or under strong geometric assumptions:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and positive Yamabe type which is not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])
. Assume that one of the following conditions is satisfied:

•

(M,g)
is locally conformally flat,

•

n\leq 9
,

•

n=10
and
u_{0}\neq\frac{5}{567}|\Weyl_{g}|^{2}_{g}
for all points in
M
and all solutions
u_{0}
of (
1.2
) attaining
Y(M,[g])
, or

•

n\geq 11
and
\Weyl_{g}\neq 0
for all points in
M
.

Then

E(M,[g])>Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
and by (
1.3
), the set of
positive
solutions of (
1.1
) is compact in
C^{2}(M)
, and thus blow-up for (
1.1
) can only occur for
sign-changing
solutions, but at an energy level that is strictly higher than the minimal one given by (
1.5
). In particular, if the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
are satisfied, then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the set of all solutions
u\in C^{2}\left(M\right)
of (
1.1
) satisfying

E\left(u\right)\leq Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+\varepsilon_{0}

is compact in
C^{2}\left(M\right)
. The contrapositive of Theorem
1.2
provides necessary conditions for sign-changing blowing-up solutions to exist at the minimal energy level. In dimensions
n\geq 11
, this condition is that
\Weyl_{g}
vanishes at some (but not all) points in
M
. This is consistent with Theorem
1.1
since the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that we construct to prove Theorem
1.1
blows up at a point where
\Weyl_{g}
vanishes. Hence, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are sharp in every dimension
11\leq n\leq 24
. The condition arising when
n=10
is purely analytical (see (
5.44
) below). The exact value of
E(M,[g])
when
3\leq n\leq 10
is not yet known: as Theorem
1.2
shows, at least when
n\leq 9
, it is strictly larger than
Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Computing
E(M,[g])
when
3\leq n\leq 10
will be the focus of forthcoming work.

We conclude this subsection by mentioning an important additional motivation for investigating the value of
E(M,[g])
and, more generally, (non-)compactness issues for sign-changing solutions of (
1.1
). Let
g
be a Riemannian metric in
M
of positive Yamabe type. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tilde{g}\in[g]
, we denote by
\lambda_{k}(\tilde{g})
the
k
-th eigenvalue (counted with multiplicity) of the conformal Laplacian
\triangle_{\tilde{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\tilde{g}}
in
M
. Ammann–Humbert introduced in
[
2
]
the so-called
k
-th Yamabe invariant:

\mu_{k}(M,[g])=\inf_{\tilde{g}\in[g]}\lambda_{k}(\tilde{g})\Vol_{\hat{g}}\left(M\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}.

In the case where
k=2
, test functions computations show that

\mu_{2}(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}

(1.7)

holds. For
k\geq 2
, extremal metrics attaining
\mu_{k}(M,[g])
, when they exist, are not smooth in general. When
(M,g)
is of positive Yamabe type, Amman–Humbert
[
2
]
established the existence of extremal metrics attaining
\mu_{2}(M,[g])
provided
(M,g)
is not locally conformally flat and
n\geq 11
. Moreover, Amman–Humbert
[
2
]
obtained that if
\mu_{2}(M,[g])
is attained by a generalized metric
\tilde{g}=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g
with
u\geq 0
and
\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(M)}=1
, then there exists a generalized eigenvector
\tilde{w}
associated to
\mu_{2}(M,[g])
such that
u=|\tilde{w}|
and
w:=\mu_{2}(M,[g])^{\frac{n-2}{4}}\tilde{w}
is a sign-changing solution of (
1.1
) satisfying
E(w)=\mu_{2}(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}
. With (
1.7
), this shows that extremal metrics for
\mu_{2}(M,[g])
give rise to sign-changing solutions of (
1.1
) whose energies lie below the minimal blow-up level given by (
1.5
). Theorem
1.2
thus provides a compactness result for a range of energy levels including
\mu_{2}(M,[g])
. Such a result is generally perceived as a strong indication that
\mu_{2}(M,[g])
is attained (at least for analogous problems in the two-dimensional case, see for example Matthiesen–Siffert
[
31
]
or Pétrides
[
37
]
): Theorem
1.2
can therefore also be seen as a first step in a more systematic investigation of
\mu_{k}(M,[g])
.

2.
Y
-non-degenerate metrics and a refined version of Theorem
1.1

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and positive Yamabe type. By the resolution of the Yamabe problem (see Trudinger
[
56
]
, Aubin
[
3
]
and Schoen
[
50
]
) there exists a smooth positive function
u_{0}
in
M
that attains
Y(M,[g])
and solves (
1.2
).
In particular
\int_{M}u_{0}^{2^{*}}dv_{g}=Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M),\varphi>0
. By the conformal invariance property of the conformal Laplacian,
\hat{u}_{0}=u_{0}/\varphi
solves

\triangle_{g_{0}}\hat{u}_{0}+c_{n}\Scal_{g_{0}}\hat{u}_{0}=\hat{u}_{0}^{2^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }M,

where we have let
g_{0}:=\varphi^{2^{*}-2}g
. Assume that one of the positive minimizers
u_{0}
achieving
Y(M,[g])
is non-degenerate as a solution of the Yamabe equation. This means that

\textrm{Ker}\big(\triangle_{g}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}-(2^{*}-1)u_{0}^{2^{*}-2}\big)=\{0\},

(2.1)

where this kernel is regarded as a subset of
H^{1}(M)
. A simple application of the Implicit Function Theorem shows that for any metric
g_{*}
close enough to
g
in some
C^{p}
topology,
p\geq 3
, there exists a unique positive
u_{*}\in C^{2}(M)
close to
u_{0}
in
C^{2}(M)
that solves

\triangle_{g_{*}}u_{*}+c_{n}\Scal_{g_{*}}u_{*}=u_{*}^{2^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }M

(2.2)

and that is also non-degenerate. Generically with respect to perturbations of the metric, at least in dimensions
n\leq 24
(see Theorem 10.3 of Khuri–Marques–Schoen
[
25
]
), all positive solutions of (
1.2
) are non-degenerate. In the locally conformally flat case, concrete examples of situations where
u_{0}
is non-degenerate are given by
u_{0}:=\left(\left(n-2\right)/2\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\left(r\right)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{1}\left(r\right)
is the circle of radius
r\in\left(0,\infty\right)\backslash\left\{i/\sqrt{n-2}:i\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is the unit
(n-1)
-sphere, both equipped with their standard metrics (see Proposition 3.4 of Robert–Vétois
[
46
]
).

We introduce the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and positive Yamabe type. We say that
g
is
Y
-non-degenerate if

•

one of the positive minimizers
u_{0}
achieving
Y(M,[g])
is non-degenerate

•

and there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{g}>0
such that for any metric
g_{*}
satisfying
\|g_{*}-g\|_{C^{3}(M)}\leq\varepsilon_{g}
, the unique function
u_{*}
close to
u_{0}
in
C^{2}(M)
satisfying (
2.2
) still attains
Y(M,[g_{*}])
, i.e. satisfies

\int_{M}u_{*}^{2^{*}}dv_{g_{*}}=Y(M,[g_{*}])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

By the conformal invariance of
\triangle_{g}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}
, if
g
is Y-non-degenerate in
M
then any metric in the conformal class of
g
is still Y-non-degenerate. This notion allows us to state a generalization of Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(M,\hat{g})
be a smooth, closed, locally conformally flat Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 11
which is
Y-non-degenerate
in the sense of Definition
2.1
. Then there exist a smooth, non-locally conformally flat metric
g
in
M
and a sequence of blowing-up sign-changing solutions
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
u_{k}\in C^{3,\alpha}(M)
for some
0<\alpha<1
, to the nodal Yamabe equation for
g
:

\triangle_{g}u_{k}+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\Scal_{g}u_{k}=|u_{k}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{k}\quad\text{in }M,

which satisfies

\int_{M}|u_{k}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\nearrow Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}

as
k\to\infty
.

The metric
g
in Theorem
2.2
can be chosen arbitrarily close to the original metric
\hat{g}
in
C^{p}(M)
for any
p\geq 3
. Theorem
2.2
is proven in Sections
3
and
4
. In the rest of this section, we prove that spherical space forms and their locally conformally flat perturbations are
Y
-non-degenerate and that Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
2.2
. The first result is as follows:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\Gamma
be a finite subgroup of isometries of
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std})
,
\Gamma\neq\{Id\}
acting freely and smoothly on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The quotient manifold
M_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma
endowed with the quotient metric
g_{\Gamma}
is locally conformally flat and Y-non-degenerate in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Proof.

By definition, (
M_{\Gamma}
,
g_{\Gamma}
) is locally isometric to
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std})
, hence it is locally conformally flat, has constant scalar curvature equal to
n(n-1)
and is thus of positive Yamabe type.

The constant function
u_{0}\equiv\left(n(n-2)/4\right)^{\frac{n-2}{4}}
is a solution of the Yamabe equation on
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std})
,
so
u_{0}
descends to
M_{\Gamma}
as a constant positive solution, that we still denote by
u_{0}
, of the Yamabe equation

\triangle_{g_{\Gamma}}u_{0}+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}u_{0}=u_{0}^{2^{*}-1}\quad\textrm{in }M_{\Gamma}.

(2.3)

We claim that the linearized operator at
u_{0}
in
M_{\Gamma}
, which is given by
L=\triangle_{g_{\Gamma}}-n
, has zero kernel. Indeed, if
L\varphi=0
for some
\varphi\in H^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
then
\varphi
lifts to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
as a function
\tilde{\varphi}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
which is
\Gamma
-invariant and satisfies

\triangle_{g_{std}}\tilde{\varphi}-n\tilde{\varphi}=0
. But the kernel of
\triangle_{g_{std}}-n
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
consists of the restrictions of the coordinate functions
(x_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, that are not
\Gamma
-invariant. Hence
\varphi\equiv 0
and
u_{0}
is a non-degenerate solution of the Yamabe equation in
M_{\Gamma}
. This proves the first point in Definition
2.1
.

We now prove the second point. Since
(M_{\Gamma},g_{\Gamma})
is Einstein, a celebrated theorem of Obata
[
36
]
shows that
u_{0}
is the only positive solution of (
2.3
), so in particular
u_{0}
satisfies

\int_{M_{\Gamma}}u_{0}^{2^{*}}dv_{g_{\Gamma}}=Y(M_{\Gamma},[g_{\Gamma}])^{\frac{n}{2}}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

By the Implicit Function Theorem there is
\eta_{0}>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon
small enough and any metric
g
on
M_{\Gamma}
such that
\|g-g_{\Gamma}\|_{C^{3}(M_{\Gamma})}\leq\varepsilon
, there is a unique positive function
u_{g}\in C^{2}(M_{\Gamma})\cap B_{H^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}(u_{0},\eta_{0})
satisfying

\triangle_{g}u_{g}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}u_{g}=u_{g}^{2^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }M.

(2.4)

We also have
\|u_{g}-u_{0}\|_{C^{2}(M_{\Gamma})}\leq C\varepsilon
for some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
, so
u_{g}
is still non-degenerate for
\varepsilon
small enough. We claim that for
\varepsilon
small enough, we again have

\int_{M_{\Gamma}}u_{g}^{2^{*}}dv_{g}=Y(M_{\Gamma},[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}

for any
g
with
\|g-g_{\Gamma}\|_{C^{3}(M_{\Gamma})}\leq\varepsilon
. Assume by contradiction that, for a sequence of real numbers
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
and a sequence of metrics
(g_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\|g_{j}-g_{\Gamma}\|_{C^{3}(M_{\Gamma})}\leq\varepsilon_{j}
, the latter equality does not hold. Then

\int_{M_{\Gamma}}u_{j}^{2^{*}}dv_{g_{j}}>Y(M_{\Gamma},[g_{j}])^{\frac{n}{2}}\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N},

where we have let
u_{j}:=u_{g_{j}}
. Since
g_{j}\to g_{\Gamma}
in
C^{3}(M_{\Gamma})
, we have
Y(M_{\Gamma},[g_{j}])\to Y(M_{\Gamma},[g_{\Gamma}])<Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])
and thus, by the resolution of the Yamabe problem, there exists a sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive solutions of (
2.4
) with
g=g_{j}
satisfying

\int_{M_{\Gamma}}v_{j}^{2^{*}}dv_{g_{j}}=Y(M_{\Gamma},[g_{j}])^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+\smallo\left(1\right)

(2.5)

as
j\to\infty
. In particular,
u_{j}\neq v_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Struwe’s
H^{1}
-compactness result
[
53
]
together with (
2.5
) show that the sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
strongly converges in
H^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
as
j\to\infty
towards a positive solution of (
2.3
). By the uniqueness result of Obata
[
36
]
,
v_{j}\to u_{0}
, and hence the local uniqueness shows that
v_{j}=u_{j}
for large
j
, a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Thanks to Proposition
2.3
, we can apply Theorem
2.2
to
(M,\hat{g})=(M_{\Gamma},g_{\Gamma})
for some finite subgroup
\Gamma\neq\{Id\}
of isometries of
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std})
acting freely and smoothly on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We can then let
g
be as in the statement of Theorem
2.2
. By definition of
E(M,[g])
, Theorem
2.2
then gives

E(M,[g])\leq Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

The other inequality follows from Proposition
5.1
below, which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

The arguments developed in the proof of Proposition
2.3
similarly show that if a metric
g
in
M
attains
Y(M,[g])
at a unique positive minimizer
u_{0}
, that is also non-degenerate in the sense of (
2.1
), then
(M,g)
is
Y
-non-degenerate. With this observation, we can prove the following result that provides additional
Y
-non-degenerate examples to which Theorem
2.2
applies:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
, let
\Gamma
be a finite subgroup of isometries of
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std})
,
\Gamma\neq\{Id\}
acting freely and smoothly on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and let
g
be a locally conformally flat metric in
M_{\Gamma}
,
\Gamma\neq\{Id\}
, that is close to
g_{\Gamma}
in
C^{p}(M_{\Gamma})
for some
p
large enough. Then
g
is
Y
-non-degenerate.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the uniqueness result of de Lima–Piccione–Zedda
[
14
]
(Theorem
5
). We provide some additional details here since the result of
[
14
]
is not stated in this way. The analysis in
[
14
]
leading to Theorem
5
applies as long as sequences of Yamabe metrics close to
g_{\Gamma}
can be made to converge strongly up to a subsequence. This is the case for sequences of locally conformally flat metrics
(g_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on
M_{\Gamma}
close to
g_{\Gamma}
in
C^{p}(M_{\Gamma})
for some
p
large enough. Local arguments indeed show that any metric
g_{k}
has positive Riemannian mass, with a positive uniform bound from below, at every point of
M_{\Gamma}
. This is a purely local argument that works for any dimension
n\geq 3
and does not rely on the positive mass theorem. The convergence of
(g_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
up to a subsequence then follows from the arguments of Schoen
[
52
]
and Li–Zhu
[
29
]
. Hence for any metric
g
on
M_{\Gamma}
with
|\Gamma|\geq 2
that is locally conformally flat and
C^{p}
-close to
g_{\Gamma}
for
p
large enough, the equation (
2.4
) has a unique positive solution

. This solution

remains non-degenerate for

close enough to
g_{\Gamma}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

A natural question connected with Definition
2.1
, and with the observation before Proposition
2.4
, is whether it is possible that the Yamabe equation admits multiple minimizers. This is indeed the case. Examples of such situations can be obtained by considering manifolds with a nontrivial isometry group. In particular, Schoen
[
52
]
gave a detailed study of the multiplicity of positive solutions to the Yamabe equation in the case of the product manifold
\mathbb{S}^{1}\left(r\right)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with
r>0
. In this case, if
r
is chosen large enough, then there exists a family of distinct (degenerate) minimizers parametrized by
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. This example can be extended to more general manifolds with different isometry groups (see Hebey–Vaugon
[
24
]
).

satisfying (
3.12
) follows. By standard elliptic theory
R_{k,t,z}
is smooth.

By (
3.2
),
h_{k}
is trace-free and satisfies
\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x-y_{k})_{j}(h_{k})_{ij}(x)=0
for all
1\leq j\leq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Direct computations then show that

\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}(h_{k})_{pq}\,\partial_{pq}^{2}B_{k,t,z}=-\frac{\mu_{k}^{2}}{n(n-2)}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}(h_{k})_{pq}z_{p}z_{q}\frac{\mu_{k}(t)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(\mu_{k}(t)^{2}+\frac{|\cdot-\xi_{k}(z)|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}.

It is then easily seen that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

R_{k,t,z}(x)=\varepsilon_{k}\mu_{k}^{3-\frac{n}{2}}R_{t,z}\left(\frac{x-y_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)

(3.14)

holds, where
R_{t,z}
is the unique solution in
K_{t,z}^{\perp}
of

\triangle_{\delta_{0}}R_{t,z}-(2^{*}-1)B_{t,z}^{2^{*}-2}R_{t,z}=-\frac{t^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{n(n-2)}\frac{\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}h_{pq}z_{p}z_{q}}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{|\cdot-z|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(3.15)

where we have let

B_{t,z}(x):=t^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(t^{2}+\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}

(3.16)

and

K_{t,z}:=\textrm{span}\left\{\partial_{t}B_{t,z},\partial_{z_{1}}B_{t,z},\dotsc,\partial_{z_{n}}B_{t,z}\right\}.

Since
t\in[1/A,A]
and
z\in B(0,1)
and since the
h_{ij}
are homogeneous of degree
2
, we can again write

R_{t,z}(x)=t^{1-\frac{n}{2}}S_{0,z}\left(\frac{x-z}{t}\right)

for some smooth function
S_{0,z}\in K_{1,0}^{\perp}
that satisfies

\big|\triangle_{\delta_{0}}S_{0,z}(y)-(2^{*}-1)B_{1,0}(y)^{2^{*}-2}S_{0,z}(y)\big|\leq C|z|^{2}\left(1+|y|\right)^{-n}

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for some
C>0
independent of
t
and
z
. We can now write a representation formula for
\triangle_{\delta_{0}}-(2^{*}-1)B_{1,0}^{2^{*}-2}
(see for example Lemma 3.3 of Premoselli
[
40
]
) that shows that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|S_{0,z}(x)|\leq C|z|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-y|^{2-n}(1+|y|)^{-n}dy\leq C|z|^{2}\frac{\ln(2+|x|)}{\left(1+|x|\right)^{n-2}}

for some constant
C>0
independent of
z
. Differentiating the representation formula similarly yields

|\nabla S_{0,z}(x)|\leq C|z|^{2}\frac{\ln(2+|x|)}{\left(1+|x|\right)^{n-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla S_{0,z}(x)|^{2}\leq C|z|^{2}\frac{\ln(2+|x|)}{\left(1+|x|\right)^{n}}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Going back to
R_{k,t,z}
, this proves (
3.13
).
∎

Let again
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
,
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}\backslash[0,2]
. For
t\in[1/A,A]
and
z\in B(0,1)
, we let

U_{k,t,z}(x):=\chi\left(\frac{x-y_{k}}{r_{k}}\right)\left(B_{k,t,z}(x)+R_{k,t,z}(x)\right)

(3.17)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

W_{k,t,z}(x):=U_{k,t,z}\left(\Phi(x)\right)-\check{u}_{0}(x)

(3.18)

for any
x\in M
, where
\Phi
is the chart around
x_{0}
introduced in the previous subsection, and where
\check{u}_{0}
is as in (
3.5
). We let

f(s):=|s|^{2^{*}-2}s\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.19)

We also let for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
(t,z)\in[1/A,A]\times B(0,1)
,

E_{k,t,z}:=\left(\triangle_{\check{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\check{g}}\right)W_{k,t,z}-f(W_{k,t,z}).

(3.20)

Until the end of this section,
C
will denote a positive constant independent of
k,t
and
z
, that might change from one line to the other.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
(t,z)\in[1/A,A]\times B(0,1)
, we have

\|E_{k,t,z}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(M)}\leq C\mu_{k}^{\frac{n+2}{4}}.

(3.21)

Proof.

By (
3.1
) and (
3.13
), we have
\left\|R_{k,t,z}/B_{k,t,z}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{k},2r_{k}))}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
uniformly with respect to
t,z
. As a consequence, and since
\mu_{k}^{2}=\smallo\left(r_{k}\right)
,

\big|f(W_{k,t,z})-f(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi)+f(\check{u}_{0})\big|\leq C\left\{\begin{aligned} &\big(B_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\big)^{2^{*}-2}&&\textrm{in }\Phi\big(B(\xi_{k}(z),\sqrt{\mu_{k}(t)})\big)\\
&\big(B_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\big)&&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\check{u}_{0}
satisfies (
3.5
) and
\tilde{g}=\Phi_{*}\check{g}
in
B(0,4\delta)
, we obtain

\|E_{k,t,z}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(M)}\leq\|(\triangle_{\tilde{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\tilde{g}})U_{k,t,z}-f(U_{k,t,z})\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B(y_{k},2r_{k}))}+C\mu_{k}^{\frac{n+2}{4}},

(3.22)

where
\tilde{g}
is given by (
3.3
). First, by using (
3.13
) together with straightforward computations, we obtain

(\triangle_{\tilde{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\tilde{g}})U_{k,t,z}-f(U_{k,t,z})=\bigO\left(\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}r_{k}^{-n}\right)

(3.23)

in
B(y_{k},2r_{k})\backslash B(y_{k},r_{k})
. In
B(y_{k},r_{k})
, by (
3.4
), we have

\tilde{g}=\exp\left(\varepsilon_{k}h_{k}(x)\right),

where
h_{k}
is defined in (
3.11
). Since the components of
h
are homogeneous of degree two,
|\varepsilon_{k}h_{k}(x)|\leq C\varepsilon_{k}r_{k}^{2}=\smallo\left(1\right)
holds for all
x\in B(y_{k},r_{k})
. The definition of
\tilde{g}
therefore allows to expand its inverse as

\tilde{g}^{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}-\varepsilon_{k}(h_{k})_{ij}(x)+\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{n}(h_{k})_{ip}(x)(h_{k})_{pj}(x)+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{3}|x-y_{k}|^{6}\right)

(3.24)

for
i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
. Similarly, the Christoffel symbols of
\tilde{g}
expand as

\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}(\tilde{g})(x)=\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\left(\partial_{i}(h_{k})_{j\ell}(x)+\partial_{j}(h_{k})_{i\ell}(x)-\partial_{\ell}(h_{k})_{ij}(x)\right)+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{2}|x-y_{k}|^{3}\right)

(3.25)

for
i,j,\ell\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
. Using Proposition
26
of Brendle
[
7
]
, and since
h_{k}
is trace-free and divergence-free, the scalar curvature of
\tilde{g}
expands as

\Scal_{\tilde{g}}(x)=-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}(h_{k})_{j\ell}(x))^{2}+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{3}|x-y_{k}|^{4}\right).

(3.26)

Remark finally that, by definition of
\xi_{k}(z)
in (
3.7
), there exists
C>1
such that for any
x\in B(y_{k},r_{k})
,

\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{\mu_{k}+|x-y_{k}|}{\mu_{k}+|x-\xi_{k}(z)|}\leq C

holds true. Using (
3.24
), (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), we thus have, for
x\in B(y_{k},r_{k})
,

\displaystyle(\triangle_{\tilde{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\tilde{g}})B_{k,t,z}-f(B_{k,t,z})

\displaystyle\qquad=(\triangle_{\tilde{g}}-\triangle_{\delta_{0}})B_{k,t,z}+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\mu_{k}+|x-y_{k}|)^{4-n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\varepsilon_{k}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}(h_{k})_{pq}\,\partial^{2}_{pq}B_{k,t,z}+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\mu_{k}+|x-y_{k}|)^{4-n}\right).

(3.27)

Since
U_{k,t,z}(x)=B_{k,t,z}(x)+R_{k,t,z}(x)
, by (
3.13
),

\left|f(U_{k,t,z}(x))-f(B_{k,t,z}(x))-f^{\prime}(B_{k,t,z}(x))R_{k,t,z}(x)\right|\leq CB_{k,t,z}(x)^{2^{*}-3}R_{k,t,z}(x)^{2}

holds for any
x\in B(y_{k},r_{k})
. With (
3.1
), (
3.13
), (
3.24
), (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and (
3.2
), we obtain that, in
B(y_{k},r_{k})
,

\displaystyle(\triangle_{\tilde{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\tilde{g}})U_{k,t,z}-f(U_{k,t,z})

\displaystyle\qquad=(\triangle_{\tilde{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\tilde{g}})B_{k,t,z}-f(B_{k,t,z})+\triangle_{\delta_{0}}R_{k,t,z}-f^{\prime}(B_{k,t,z}(x))R_{k,t,z}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(\triangle_{\tilde{g}}-\triangle_{\delta_{0}})R_{k,t,z}+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{2}(\mu_{k}+|x-y_{k}|)^{2}|R_{k,t,z}|\right)+\bigO\left(B_{k,t,z}^{2^{*}-3}R_{k,t,z}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\mu_{k}+|x-y_{k}|)^{4-n}\right)

holds. With (
3.1
), (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), this finally shows that

\|E_{k,t,z}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(M)}\leq C\left(\mu_{k}^{\frac{n+2}{4}}+\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}r_{k}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}+\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\mu_{k}^{4}\right)\leq C\mu_{k}^{\frac{n+2}{4}},

where the last inequality follows since
n\geq 11
and
\mu_{k}=\smallo\left(r_{k}^{p}\right)
for any
p\geq 1
.
∎

3.3.
The Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction

We endow
H^{1}(M)
with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{1}(M)}:=\sqrt{\int_{M}\left(|\nabla u|_{\check{g}}^{2}+c_{n}\Scal_{\check{g}}u^{2}\right)dv_{\check{g}}},

and for
u\in H^{1}(M)
, we let

I(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla u|_{\check{g}}^{2}+c_{n}\Scal_{\check{g}}u^{2}\right)dv_{\check{g}}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{M}|u|^{2^{*}}dv_{\check{g}}.

For
(t,z)\in[1/A,A]\times B(0,1)
,
1\leq j\leq n
and
x\in M
, we let

\hat{Z}_{0,k,t,z}(x):=\frac{2}{n-2}\,t\,\partial_{t}W_{k,t,z}(x)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\hat{Z}_{j,k,t,z}(x):=-n\,t\,\partial_{z_{j}}W_{k,t,z}(x),

(3.28)

and we let

\hat{K}_{k,t,z}:=\textrm{span}\big\{\hat{Z}_{j,k,t,z}:\,0\leq j\leq n\big\},

which is regarded as a subset of
H^{1}(M)
. We denote by
\hat{K}_{k,t,z}^{\perp}
its orthogonal complement in
H^{1}(M)
. The following proposition shows the existence of a canonical solution of (
3.6
) in
\hat{K}_{k,t,z}^{\perp}
:

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists
C>0
and, for large
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a function
\varphi_{k}:[1/A,A]\times B(0,1)\to H^{1}(M)
of class
C^{1}
which is the only solution of

\Pi_{\hat{K}_{k,t,z}^{\perp}}\left(u_{k,t,z}-\big(\triangle_{\check{g}}+c_{n}\Scal_{\check{g}}\big)^{-1}\big(f(u_{k,t,z})\big)\right)=0

in the set

\left\{\varphi\in\hat{K}_{k,t,z}^{\perp}:\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(M)}\leq C\|E_{k,t,z}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(M)}\right\},

where we have let
u_{k,t,z}:=W_{k,t,z}+\varphi_{k}(t,z)
. In particular,

\|\varphi_{k}(t,z)\|_{H^{1}(M)}\leq C\|E_{k,t,z}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(M)}

(3.29)

for some
C>0
independent of
k,t,z
. In addition, for large
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
u_{k,t,z}
is a critical point of
I
(hence a solution of (
3.6
)) if and only if
(t,z)
is a critical point of the mapping
(t,z)\mapsto I\left(u_{k,t,z}\right)
.

Proof.

By (
3.18
), we have, for
0\leq j\leq n
and
x\in B(y_{k},2r_{k})
,

\hat{Z}_{j,k,t,z}\left(\Phi^{-1}(x)\right)=\chi\left(\frac{x-y_{k}}{r_{k}}\right)\left(Z_{j,k,t,z}(x)+Q_{j,k,t,z}(x)\right),

(3.30)

where we have let
Q_{0,k,t,z}:=\frac{2}{n-2}t\,\partial_{t}R_{k,t,z}
and
Q_{j,k,t,z}:=-nt\,\partial_{z_{j}}R_{k,t,z}
. By differentiating (
3.12
) and using (
3.13
), one gets that, for
0\leq j\leq n
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in B(y_{k},2r_{k})
and any
(t,z)\in[1/A,A]\times B(0,1)
,
Q_{j,k,t,z}
satifies

\big|\triangle_{\delta_{0}}Q_{j,k,t,z}-(2^{*}-1)B_{k,t,z}^{2^{*}-2}Q_{j,k,t,z}\big|\leq C\varepsilon_{k}\mu_{k}^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\left(\mu_{k}+|\xi_{k}(z)-\cdot|\right)^{-n}.

(3.31)

As in the proof of (
3.13
), a representation formula yields once again that, for
i\in\left\{0,1,2\right\}
,

|\nabla^{i}Q_{j,k,t,z}(x)|\leq C\varepsilon_{k}\mu_{k}(t)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\frac{\ln\left(\frac{2\mu_{k}(t)+|x-\xi_{k}(z)|}{\mu_{k}(t)}\right)}{\left(\mu_{k}(t)+|x-\xi_{k}(z)|\right)^{n-2+i}}

(3.32)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. With (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.28
), we then obtain, for
0\leq j\leq n
and
x\in B(y_{k},2r_{k})
, that

\displaystyle\hat{Z}_{j,k,t,z}\left(\Phi^{-1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(\frac{x-y_{k}}{r_{k}}\right)Z_{j,k,t,z}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}\mu_{k}(t)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\frac{\ln\left(\frac{2\mu_{k}(t)+|x-\xi_{k}(z)|}{\mu_{k}(t)}\right)}{\left(\mu_{k}(t)+|x-\xi_{k}(z)|\right)^{n-2}}\right),

(3.33)

\displaystyle\nabla\big(\hat{Z}_{j,k,t,z}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\chi\left(\frac{\cdot-y_{k}}{r_{k}}\right)Z_{j,k,t,z}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigO\left(\varepsilon_{k}\mu_{k}(t)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\frac{\ln\left(\frac{2\mu_{k}(t)+|x-\xi_{k}(z)|}{\mu_{k}(t)}\right)}{\left(\mu_{k}(t)+|x-\xi_{k}(z)|\right)^{n-1}}\right).

(3.34)

With these estimates, we can easily adapt the proof of Proposition 5.1 of Robert–Vétois
[
47
]
(see also Esposito–Pistoia–Vétois
[
21
]
), and Proposition
3.3
follows.
∎

We let
K_{n}
be the optimal constant of the Sobolev embedding
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. As explained in the introduction,
K_{n}^{-2}=Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])
. Since the latter is attained by the stereographic projection of the bubbles

defined in (

The explicit value of

is known (see Aubin
[
4
]
and Talenti
[
54
]
). The next result is an expansion of

.

Proposition 3.4
.

We have, as

I\left(W_{k,t,z}\right)=\frac{1}{n}K_{n}^{-n}+I(\check{u}_{0})+\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Bigg(\Lambda(n)\check{u}_{0}(x_{0})t^{\frac{n-2}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla R_{t,z}|^{2}dx\\
+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hskip-2.0pt\sum_{i,j,p=1}^{n}\hskip-2.0pth_{ip}\,h_{pj}\,\partial_{i}B_{t,z}\,\partial_{j}B_{t,z}\,dx-\frac{n-2}{32(n-1)}\hskip-2.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hskip-2.0pt\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}h_{j\ell})^{2}B_{t,z}^{2}\,dx+\smallo\left(1\right)\Bigg),

(3.35)

where
\check{u}_{0}
is as in (
3.5
),

is as in (
3.14
) and where

is a positive dimensional constant given by (

C^{1}([1/A,A]\times B(0,1))

Proof.

First, by (
3.13
) and (
3.17

0\leq U_{k,t,z}\leq CB_{k,t,z}
in

\displaystyle\Big||W_{k,t,z}|^{2^{*}}-\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{2^{*}}-\check{u}_{0}^{2^{*}}+2^{*}\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{2^{*}-1}\check{u}_{0}+2^{*}\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)\check{u}_{0}^{2^{*}-1}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left(B_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{2^{*}-1-\theta}+\left(B_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{1+\theta}\right)

holds in

. As a consequence, and since
\check{u}_{0}
solves (
3.5

\displaystyle I\left(W_{k,t,z}\right)=I\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)+I(\check{u}_{0})+\int_{M}\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{2^{*}-1}\check{u}_{0}dv_{\check{g}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\bigO\left(\int_{M}\left(\left(B_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{2^{*}-1-\theta}+\left(B_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{1+\theta}\right)dv_{\check{g}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=I\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)+I(\check{u}_{0})+\int_{M}\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{2^{*}-1}\check{u}_{0}\,dv_{\check{g}}+\bigO\left(\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}(1+\theta)}\right).

(3.36)

Independently, by (
3.13
) and (
3.17
), and since
\check{u}_{0}
is of class
C^{2}
, we have

\int_{M}\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)^{2^{*}-1}\check{u}_{0}\,dv_{\check{g}}=\Lambda(n)\check{u}_{0}(x_{0})\mu_{k}(t)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}+\smallo\left(\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right),

(3.37)

where
\mu_{k}(t):=\mu_{k}t
is as in (
3.7
) and where we have let

\Lambda(n):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}dx.

(3.38)

We have thus proven that

I\left(W_{k,t,z}\right)=I\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)+I(\check{u}_{0})+\Lambda(n)\check{u}_{0}(x_{0})\mu_{k}(t)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}+\smallo\left(\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right).

(3.39)

It remains to expand
I\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)
. By definition, we have
\check{g}=\Phi^{*}\tilde{g}
in

, where
\tilde{g}
is given by (
3.3
), so by (

) and since
\textrm{det}\,\tilde{g}=1

I\left(U_{k,t,z}\circ\Phi\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\left|\nabla\left(B_{k,t,z}+R_{k,t,z}\right)\right|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}+c_{n}\Scal_{\tilde{g}}\left(B_{k,t,z}+R_{k,t,z}\right)^{2}\right)dx\\
-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|B_{k,t,z}+R_{k,t,z}\right|^{2^{*}}dx+\smallo\left(\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right),

(3.40)

where
R_{k,t,z}
is given by Lemma
3.1
. In the latter equality, we implicitly assumed, in accordance with (
3.3
), that
\tilde{g}
has been extended as a metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that coincides with the Euclidean metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B(0,4\delta)
. First, by (
3.13
) and (
3.24

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla\left(B_{k,t,z}+R_{k,t,z}\right)\right|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla B_{k,t,z}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nabla B_{k,t,z},\nabla R_{k,t,z}\rangle\,dx\\

F(t,0)=F_{2}(t,0)+\Lambda(n)\check{u}_{0}(x_{0})t^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
for any

. By (

), we have, for all

\partial_{i}h_{j\ell}(x)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{p=1}^{n}(W_{ji\ell p}+W_{jp\ell i})x_{p}.

Hence, by (
4

F_{2}(t,0)=-\frac{n-2}{32(n-1)}t^{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}h_{j\ell}(x))^{2}d\sigma(x)\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n+1}dr}{\big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n-2}}.

Recall that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}x_{i}x_{j}\,d\sigma(x)=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}\delta_{ij}

(4.10)

for all

. As a consequence, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}h_{j\ell}(x))^{2}d\sigma(x)=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{9n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}T_{kk},

where

4.1

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n+1}dr}{\big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n-2}}=\frac{4n^{2}(n-1)(n-2)}{(n-4)(n-6)}\frac{K_{n}^{-n}}{\omega_{n-1}}.

This shows that

F(t,0)=-\left(\frac{n(n-2)^{2}}{72(n-4)(n-6)}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}T_{kk}\right)t^{4}+\Lambda(n)\check{u}_{0}(x_{0})t^{\frac{n-2}{2}}.

(4.11)

Since
\Lambda(n)>0
,
\check{u}_{0}>0
in
M
,
\sum_{k=1}^{n}T_{kk}>0
and
n\geq 11
, Lemma
4.1
follows.
∎

The value of

only depends on

and
\check{u}_{0}
, that have been fixed from the beginning. From now on, we will let
A>0
be chosen large enough so that

. We now show that the second derivatives of

at

in

:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
n\geq 11
and let
t>0
be fixed. Then, for any
1\leq k,\ell\leq n
,

\partial^{2}_{z_{k}z_{\ell}}F(t,z)_{|z=0}=0.

Proof.

Let
t>0
and
k,\ell\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
be fixed throughout this proof.
First, by (
3.15
) and the uniqueness of
R_{t,z}
, we have
R_{t,0}=0
and
\partial_{z_{i}}\left(R_{t,z}\right)_{|z=0}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. As a consequence,

\partial^{2}_{z_{k}z_{\ell}}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}=0
. On the one hand, with (
4

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{z_{k}z_{\ell}}F_{1}(t,z)_{|z=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{p=1}^{n}h(x)_{kp}\,h(x)_{p\ell}\frac{t^{n-2}dx}{\big(t^{2}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n}}.

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2n^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{p=1}^{n}h(x)_{kp}\,h(x)_{p\ell}\,d\sigma(x)\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n+3}dr}{\big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n}}.

By using the symmetries of
W
, it is easily seen that

\sum_{p,i,j=1}^{n}W_{kpij}\left(W_{\ell pij}+W_{\ell jip}\right)=\frac{1}{2}T_{k\ell},

where
T_{k\ell}
is as in (
4.1
). Using (
4.2
) and Corollary 29 of Brendle
[
7
]
, we thus obtain

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{p=1}^{n}h(x)_{kp}\,h(x)_{p\ell}\,d\sigma(x)=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{18n(n+2)}T_{k\ell}.

Together with (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we then obtain

\partial^{2}_{z_{k}z_{\ell}}F_{1}(t,z)_{|z=0}=\frac{t^{2}}{36}\frac{n-2}{n-4}K_{n}^{-n}T_{k\ell}.

(4.12)

By using (
4.9
) together with change of variables, we obtain

F_{2}(t,z)=-\frac{n-2}{288(n-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}T_{pq}(x+z)_{p}(x+z)_{q}\frac{t^{n-2}dx}{\big(t^{2}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n-2}},

so that differentiating and using (

4.7
) and (

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{z_{k}z_{\ell}}F_{2}(t,z)_{|z=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-2}{144(n-1)}T_{k\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{t^{2}dx}{\big(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n-2}}=-\frac{t^{2}}{36}\frac{n-2}{n-4}K_{n}^{-n}T_{k\ell}.

(4.13)

Together with (
4.12

4.2
.
∎

In order to prove that
(t_{0},0)
is a non-degenerate critical point of

, we now expand, for a fixed value of

,

to the fourth order as

. A first, obvious, remark is that for

, we have

\partial_{z_{i}z_{j}z_{k}z_{l}}^{4}F_{2}(t,z)_{|z=0}=0.

(4.14)

This easily follows from the expression of

given by (
4

4.2
),
\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}h_{j\ell}(x))^{2}
is a second-order polynomial in

, hence

is a second-order polynomial in
z
by a change of variables. We now expand
F_{1}+F_{2}
:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
t>0
. We have, as
z\to 0
,

F_{1}(t,z)+F_{2}(t,z)=F_{2}(t,0)+\frac{K_{n}^{-n}}{4n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\big(h_{pq}(z)\big)^{2}+\bigO\left(|z|^{5}\right).

Proof.

By definition,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are even in
z
. The expansions to second-order thus follow from
F_{1}(t,0)=0
, (
4.12
) and (
4.13
). By (
4.14
), we only have to expand
F_{1}(t,z)
to fourth order. By (
4
), (
4
) and a change of variables, we have

F_{1}(t,z)=\frac{1}{4n^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{t^{n-2}}{\big(t^{2}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n}}\sum_{i,j,p=1}^{n}h(x+z)_{ip}\,h(x+z)_{pj}z_{i}z_{j}\,dx.

It is easily seen that, for any
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq a,b,c,d\leq n,

(x+z)_{a}(x+z)_{b}(x+z)_{c}(x+z)_{d}=x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}x_{d}+x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}z_{d}+x_{a}x_{b}x_{d}z_{c}+x_{a}x_{c}x_{d}z_{b}\\
+x_{b}x_{c}x_{d}z_{a}+x_{a}x_{b}z_{c}z_{d}+x_{a}x_{c}z_{b}z_{d}+x_{a}x_{d}z_{b}z_{c}+x_{b}x_{c}z_{a}z_{d}+x_{b}x_{d}z_{a}z_{c}+x_{c}x_{d}z_{a}z_{b}\\
+\bigO\left(|z|^{3}|x|+|z|^{4}\right).

As a consequence and by (
4.2
), we have, as
z\to 0
,

F_{1}(t,z)+F_{2}(t,z)=F_{2}(t,0)+G(z)+\bigO\left(|z|^{5}\right),

where

G(z):=\frac{1}{36n^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-n}\sum_{i,j,p,a,b,c,d=1}^{n}W_{iapb}W_{pcjd}z_{i}z_{j}(x_{a}x_{b}z_{c}z_{d}\\
+x_{a}x_{c}z_{b}z_{d}+x_{a}x_{d}z_{b}z_{c}+x_{b}x_{c}z_{a}z_{d}+x_{b}x_{d}z_{a}z_{c}+x_{c}x_{d}z_{a}z_{b})\,dx.

By (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.10
), we have, for
1\leq a,b\leq n
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{x_{a}x_{b}\,dx}{\big(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{n}}=nK_{n}^{-n}\delta_{ab}.

Since
W
is totally traceless, we thus obtain

G(z)=\frac{1}{36n}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{i,j,p,a,b,c=1}^{n}\big(W_{iapb}W_{pajc}z_{b}z_{c}z_{i}z_{j}+W_{iapb}W_{pcja}z_{b}z_{c}z_{i}z_{j}\\
+W_{iapb}W_{pbjc}z_{a}z_{c}z_{i}z_{j}+W_{iapb}W_{pcjb}z_{a}z_{c}z_{i}z_{j}\big).

(4.15)

Since
W_{pajc}=-W_{pacj}
, we have
\sum_{i,j,p,a,b,c=1}^{n}W_{iapb}W_{pajc}z_{b}z_{c}z_{i}z_{j}=0
. Similarly, we obtain that the last two terms in the right-hand side of (
4.15
) vanish. Since moreover
W_{pcja}=W_{japc}
, we then obtain

\displaystyle G(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{36n}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{i,j,p,a,b,c=1}^{n}W_{iapb}W_{japc}z_{b}z_{c}z_{i}z_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{36n}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{a,p=1}^{n}\left(-\sum_{i,b=1}^{n}W_{iabp}z_{i}z_{b}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4n}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{a,p=1}^{n}\left(h_{ap}(z)\right)^{2}

by (
4.2
), which concludes the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

We next expand

at fourth-order in

at
(t,0)
for some fixed

:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

. As

, we have

F_{3}(t,z)=-\frac{n+4}{48(n+1)}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left(h_{pq}(z)\right)^{2}+\bigO\left(|z|^{5}\right).

Proof.

By (

), for
(t,z)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
R_{t,z}=\tilde{R}_{t,z}(x-z)
, where
\tilde{R}_{t,z}
is the unique solution in

\triangle_{\delta_{0}}\tilde{R}_{t,z}(x)-(2^{*}-1)B_{t,0}(x)^{2^{*}-2}\tilde{R}_{t,z}(x)=-\frac{t^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{n(n-2)}\frac{\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}h_{pq}(x+z)z_{p}z_{q}}{\big(t^{2}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}},

(4.16)

where
h
is given by (
4.2
). Hence, for fixed
t>0
, all the derivatives of
R_{t,z}
with respect to
z
, taken at
z=0
, belong to
K_{t,0}^{\perp}
. We let in what follows, for
a,b\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
,

L_{ab}:=\partial^{2}_{z_{a}z_{b}}\left(R_{t,z}\right)_{|z=0}\in K_{t,0}^{\perp}.

Since
R_{t,0}=0
and
\partial_{z_{i}}\left(R_{t,z}\right)_{|z=0}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, it is easily seen that, for
1\leq a,b,c,d\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{a}}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\partial^{2}_{z_{a}z_{b}}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}=0,\quad\partial^{3}_{z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}=0\quad\text{ and }

(4.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}z_{d}}^{4}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\langle\nabla L_{ab},\nabla L_{cd}\rangle+\langle\nabla L_{ac},\nabla L_{bd}\rangle+\langle\nabla L_{ad},\nabla L_{bd}\rangle\right)dx.

Differentiating (
4.16
) shows that
L_{ab}
satisfies

\triangle_{\delta_{0}}L_{ab}(x)-(2^{*}-1)B_{t,0}^{2^{*}-2}L_{ab}(x)=-\frac{2t^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{n(n-2)}\frac{h_{ab}(x)}{\big(t^{2}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}.

In particular, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
L_{ab}(x)=t^{1-\frac{n}{2}}L_{ab}^{0}(x/t)
, where
L_{ab}^{0}\in K_{1,0}^{\perp}
satisfies

\triangle_{\delta_{0}}L_{ab}^{0}(x)-(2^{*}-1)B_{1,0}^{2^{*}-2}L_{ab}^{0}(x)=-\frac{2}{n(n-2)}\frac{h_{ab}(x)}{\big(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}.

(4.18)

Coming back to (
4.17
), we also have, with a change of variables,

\partial_{z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}z_{d}}^{4}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\langle\nabla L_{ab}^{0},\nabla L_{cd}^{0}\rangle+\langle\nabla L_{ac}^{0},\nabla L_{bd}^{0}\rangle+\langle\nabla L_{ad}^{0},\nabla L_{bd}^{0}\rangle\right)dx.

(4.19)

We first find an explicit expression for
L_{ab}^{0}
. Since
W
is totally traceless,
h
is a harmonic polynomial in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As a consequence, we can look for a solution of (

) under the form

L_{ab}^{0}(x)=h_{ab}(x)f(|x|^{2}).

Simple computations show that
f(t)=-\frac{1}{n}\big(1+\frac{t}{n(n-2)}\big)^{-n/2}
satisfies (
4.18
) for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By using (
4.2
), we thus obtain

L_{ab}^{0}(x)=-\frac{1}{n}\frac{h_{ab}(x)}{\big(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}}}=-\frac{1}{3n}\frac{\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}W_{apbq}x_{p}x_{q}}{\big(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}}}

(4.20)

for
1\leq a,b\leq n
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that
L_{ab}^{0}
given by (
4.20
) belongs to
K_{1,0}^{\perp}
, which follows again from the symmetries of
W
, and is thus the unique solution of (
4.18
) in
K_{1,0}^{\perp}
. Straightforward computations with (
4.20

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nabla L_{ab}^{0},\nabla L_{cd}^{0}\rangle\,dx=K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3},

(4.21)

where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{9n^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-n}\sum_{i,p,q=1}^{n}(W_{aibp}+W_{apbi})(W_{cidq}+W_{cqdi})x_{p}x_{q}\,dx,

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n^{2}(n-2)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-n-2}|x|^{2}h_{ab}(x)\,h_{cd}(x)\,dx,

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{4}{n^{2}(n-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-n-1}h_{ab}(x)h_{cd}(x)\,dx.

By (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{9n}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left(W_{apbq}+W_{aqbp}\right)\left(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{9n}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}W_{apbq}\left(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp}\right).

Using again Corollary 29 of Brendle
[
7
]
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{ab}(x)\,h_{cd}(x)\,d\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{9n(n+2)}\omega_{n-1}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}W_{apbq}\left(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp}\right).

Together with (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}(n-2)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n+5}}{(1+\frac{r^{2}}{n(n-2)})^{n+2}}dr\cdot\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{ab}(x)\,h_{cd}(x)\,d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{n+4}{36(n+1)}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}W_{apbq}\left(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp}\right).

Similarly, we get

A_{3}=-\frac{2}{9n}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}W_{apbq}\left(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp}\right).

Combining the latter equalities with (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nabla L_{ab}^{0},\nabla L_{cd}^{0}\rangle\,dx=\frac{n+4}{36(n+1)}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}W_{apbq}\left(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp}\right).

Going back to (

\partial_{z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}z_{d}}^{4}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}=-\frac{n+4}{36(n+1)}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}(W_{apbq}(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp})\\
+W_{apcq}(W_{bpdq}+W_{bqdp})+W_{apdq}(W_{bpcq}+W_{bqcp})).

(4.22)

Using the symmetries of

, it is now easily seen that

\sum_{a,b,c,d,p,q=1}^{n}W_{apbq}\left(W_{cpdq}+W_{cqdp}\right)z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}z_{d}=18\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left(h_{pq}(z)\right)^{2}.

A Taylor formula for
F_{3}
at fourth order in
(t,0)
thus shows with (

) that

\displaystyle F_{3}(t,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{a,b,c,d=1}^{n}\partial_{z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}z_{d}}^{4}F_{3}(t,z)_{|z=0}z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}z_{d}+\bigO\left(|z|^{5}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+4}{48(n+1)}K_{n}^{-n}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left(h_{pq}(z)\right)^{2}+\bigO\left(|z|^{5}\right),

which concludes the proof of Proposition
4.4
.
∎

.

k
large enough. Lemma A.1 of Thizy–Vétois
[
55
]
then applies and shows that, for
k
large enough,
F_{k}
admits a critical point
(t_{k},z_{k})
in
(t_{0}-\eta,t_{0}+\eta)\times B(0,\varepsilon)
. By Proposition
3.3
,
u_{k,t_{k},z_{k}}
is then a solution of (
3.6
). Standard arguments show that
u_{k}
blows-up as
k\to\infty
. Finally, by Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
and since
u_{k,t_{k},z_{k}}
solves (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|u_{k,t_{k},z_{k}}|^{2^{*}}dv_{\tilde{g}}=nI\left(u_{k,t_{k},z_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=K_{n}^{-n}+\int_{M}\check{u}_{0}^{2^{*}}dv_{\check{g}}+n\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(F(t_{k},z_{k})+\smallo\left(1\right)\right)

\displaystyle<K_{n}^{-n}+\int_{M}\check{u}_{0}^{2^{*}}dv_{\check{g}}

since
F(t_{k},z_{k})<0
. Since
\int_{M}\check{u}_{0}^{2^{*}}dv_{\check{g}}=Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}
by the
Y
-non-degeneracy assumption, this concludes the proof of Theorem
2.2
. ∎

Remark 4.5
.

If
\tilde{g}
is defined as in (
3.3
), it follows from an expansion at first-order in
x-y_{k}
that
\tilde{g}
satisfies

\Weyl_{\tilde{g}}(y_{k})=-\varepsilon_{k}W

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In view of (
4.11
), Proposition
3.4
then shows that

I\left(W_{k,t,0}\right)=\frac{1}{n}K_{n}^{-n}+I(\check{u}_{0})+\Lambda(n)\check{u}_{0}(x_{0})\mu_{k}(t)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}-C(n)|\Weyl_{\tilde{g}}(y_{k})|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}\mu_{k}(t)^{4}\\
+\smallo\left(\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right),

an expansion that is reminiscent of those in Esposito–Pistoia–Vétois
[
21
]
and Premoselli–Wei
[
43
]
.

5.
Asymptotic analysis at the lowest sign-changing energy level and proof of Theorem
1.2

We begin this section with a simple result showing that
Y\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{0}]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y\left(M,[g]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
is the minimal energy level for the blow-up of sign-changing solutions of (
1.1
):

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and positive Yamabe type. Let
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a blowing-up sequence of solutions to (
1.1
). If
u_{k}
changes sign for large
k
and blows-up as
k\to\infty
, then

E\left(u_{k}\right)\geq Y\left(M,[g]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}+\smallo\left(1\right)

(5.1)

as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, if equality holds true in (
5.1
) and
\left(M,g\right)
is not conformally diffeomorphic to
\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std}\right)
, then up to a subsequence and replacing
u_{k}
by
-u_{k}
if necessary,
u_{k}
is of the form

u_{k}=u_{0}-\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{k}^{2}+\frac{d_{g}\left(\cdot,\xi_{k}\right)^{2}}{n(n-2)}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}+\smallo\left(1\right)\text{ in }H^{1}\left(M\right)\text{ as }k\to\infty,

(5.2)

where
u_{0}
is a positive solution to the Yamabe equation (
1.2
) that attains
Y\left(M,[g]\right)
,
d_{g}
is the geodesic distance with respect to the metric
g
and
\left(\mu_{k}\right)_{k}
and
\left(\xi_{k}\right)_{k}
are two families of positive numbers and points in
M
, respectively, such that
\mu_{k}\to 0
and
\xi_{k}\to\xi_{0}
as
k\to\infty
for some point
\xi_{0}\in M
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

We let

E:=\liminf_{k\to\infty}E\left(u_{k}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad E_{0}:=Y\left(M,[g]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

On the one hand,
Y\left(M,[g]\right)<Y\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}]\right)
when
\left(M,g\right)
is not conformally diffeomorphic to
\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std}\right)
by the results of Trudinger
[
56
]
, Aubin
[
3
]
and Schoen
[
50
]
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that the energy of any sign-changing solutions to the Yamabe equation on
\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{std}\right)
is greater than
2Y\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Struwe’s decomposition
[
53
]
then shows that up to a subsequence and replacing
u_{k}
by
-u_{k}
if necessary,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{\eqref{IntroEq3} holds true with $u_{0}=0$ in $M$ if }E<E_{0},\\
&\text{\eqref{IntroEq3} holds true for some energy-minimizing solution $u_{0}$ of \eqref{IntroEq4} if }E=E_{0}\text{ and}\\
&\text{$\left(M,g\right)$ is not conformally diffeomorphic to $\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0}\right)$.}\end{aligned}\right.

We are left to show that (
5.2
) cannot hold true with
u_{0}=0
in
M
. Assume by contradiction that this is the case. For large
k
, by testing (
1.1
) with
u_{k}^{+}:=\max\left(u_{k},0\right)
, we obtain

\int_{M}\big(\left|\nabla u_{k}^{+}\right|^{2}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}\left(u_{k}^{+}\right)^{2}\big)dv_{g}=\int_{M}\left(u_{k}^{+}\right)^{2^{*}}dv_{g}.

(5.3)

Since
u_{k}
changes sign the right-hand side in (
5.3
) is nonzero. An easy density argument then gives

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{M}\big(\left|\nabla u_{k}^{+}\right|^{2}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}\left(u_{k}^{+}\right)^{2}\big)dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}\left(u_{k}^{+}\right)^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}\left(M\right),\,u>0}\frac{\int_{M}\big(\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}u^{2}\big)dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}u^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}}

\displaystyle=c_{n}Y\left(M,[g]\right),

(5.4)

where
c_{n}:=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}
. By using (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we then obtain

\displaystyle c_{n}Y\left(M,[g]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}\left(u_{k}^{+}\right)^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{2/n}.

(5.5)

Since
Y\left(M,[g]\right)>0
, it then follows from (
5.5
) that
u_{k}^{+}\not\to 0
in
L^{2^{*}}\left(M\right)
as
k\to\infty
. This is in contradiction with (
5.2
) when
u_{0}=0
in
M
.
∎

The rest of this subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. By Lee and Parker’s construction of conformal normal coordinates
[
26
]
there exists a smooth family of metrics
\left(g_{\xi}\right)_{\xi\in M}
,
g_{\xi}=\Lambda_{\xi}^{2^{*}-2}g
, such that for every point
\xi\in M
, the function
\Lambda_{\xi}
is smooth, positive and satisfies

\Lambda_{\xi}\left(\xi\right)=1,\quad\nabla\Lambda_{\xi}\left(\xi\right)=0\quad\text{and}\quad dv_{g_{\xi}}\left(x\right)=\big(1+\bigO\big(\left|x\right|^{N}\big)\big)dv_{\delta_{0}}\left(x\right)

(5.6)

in geodesic normal coordinates, where
dv_{g_{\xi}}
and
dv_{\delta_{0}}
are the volume elements of the metric
g_{\xi}
and the Euclidean metric
\delta_{0}
, respectively, and
N\in\mathbb{N}
can be chosen arbitrarily large. In particular (see
[
26
]
), it follows from (
5.6
) that

\Scal_{g_{\xi}}(\xi)=0,\quad\nabla\Scal_{g_{\xi}}(\xi)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{g}\Scal_{g_{\xi}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{6}|\Weyl_{g}(\xi)|_{g}^{2}.

(5.7)

Also, if
g
is locally conformally flat in a neighbourhood of
\xi\in M
, then
g_{\xi}
can be chosen flat in a neighbourhood of
\xi
. We first state some preliminary lemmas that apply to the more general case where the weak limit
u_{0}
is a possibly sign-changing solution of

\Delta_{g}u_{0}+c_{n}\Scal_{g}u_{0}=\left|u_{0}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{0}\quad\text{in }M.

(5.8)

The following lemma is essentially contained in Premoselli
[
39
]
(see also Druet–Hebey–Robert
[
20
]
in the case of positive solutions):

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and
u_{0}
be a solution to (
5.8
). Assume that there exists a sequence of solutions
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of type (
5.2
) to (
1.1
). Let
\left(g_{\xi}\right)_{\xi\in M}
,
g_{\xi}=\Lambda_{\xi}^{2^{*}-2}g
, be the Lee–Parker family of conformal metrics to
g
. Let
\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\left(\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be families of positive numbers and points in
M
, respectively, such that

u_{k}\left(\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)=\min_{M}u_{k}=-\overline{\mu}_{k}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}.

(5.9)

Then

u_{k}-u_{0}+B_{k}=\smallo\big(B_{k}+1\big)

(5.10)

as
k\to\infty
, uniformly in
M
, where
B_{k}
is defined as

B_{k}\left(x\right):=\Lambda_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}\left(x\right)\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\Big(\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}+\frac{d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)^{2}}{n(n-2)}\Big)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\quad\forall x\in M.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Since
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k}
is of the form (
5.2
), the main result in Premoselli
[
39
]
shows that

u_{k}=u_{0}-\frac{\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(\mu_{k}^{2}+\frac{d_{g}\left(x,\xi_{k}\right)^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}+\smallo\left(1+\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{k}^{2}+d_{g}\left(\cdot,\xi_{k}\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right)

(5.11)

as
k\to\infty
, uniformly in
M
, where
\left(\mu_{k}\right)_{k}
and
\left(\xi_{k}\right)_{k}
are as in (
5.2
). Let
\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}\right)_{k}
and
\left(\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)_{k}
be such that (
5.9
) holds true. It then follows from (
5.11
) that

\overline{\mu}_{k}\sim\mu_{k}\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{g}\left(\overline{\xi}_{k},\xi_{k}\right)=\smallo\left(\mu_{k}\right)

(5.12)

as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, since
\Lambda_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}\left(\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)=1
, we obtain

d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)^{2}=d_{g}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)^{2}+\bigO\left(d_{g}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)^{3}\right)

(5.13)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in M
. By using again that
\Lambda_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}\left(\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)=1
together with (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), we obtain

\frac{\mu_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(\mu_{k}^{2}+\frac{d_{g}\left(x,\xi_{k}\right)^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}=B_{k}+\smallo\big(B_{k}+1\big)

(5.14)

as
k\to\infty
, uniformly in
M
, so that (
5.10
) follows from (
5.11
) and (
5.14
).
∎

We then state the following result from Premoselli–Vétois
[
42
]
, which essentially follows from a Pohozaev-type identity together with the estimate (
5.11
):

Lemma 5.3
.

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
42
]
)
Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and
u_{0}
be a solution to (
5.8
). Assume that there exists a sequence of solutions
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to (
1.1
) satisfying (
5.2
). Let
\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}\right)_{k}
,
\left(\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)_{k}
,
\left(B_{k}\right)_{k}
and
\left(g_{\xi}\right)_{\xi}
be as in Lemma
5.2
. Then

\int_{B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)}\left(\left<\nabla\hat{u}_{k},\cdot\right>_{\delta_{0}}+\frac{n-2}{2}\hat{u}_{k}\right)\left(\left(\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}-\Delta_{\delta_{0}}\right)\hat{u}_{k}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\hat{h}_{k}\hat{u}_{k}\right)dv_{\hat{g}_{k}}\\
=\frac{1}{2}n^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(n-2)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\omega_{n-1}u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}+\smallo\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right)

(5.15)

as
k\to\infty
, where

\hat{u}_{k}\left(y\right):=\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\big(\Lambda_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}^{-1}u_{k}\big)\big(\exp_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}y\right)\big),\quad\hat{g}_{k}\left(y\right):=\exp_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}^{*}g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}y\right)

(5.16)

and

\hat{h}_{k}\left(y\right):=c_{n}\Scal_{\hat{g}_{k}}\big(\exp_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}y\right)\big)

(5.17)

for all points
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
,
\omega_{n-1}
is the volume of the standard unit sphere of dimension
n-1
,
\delta_{0}
is the Euclidean metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\exp_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}
is the exponential map at the point
\overline{\xi}_{k}
with respect to the metric
g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}
and we identify
T_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}M
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Finally, to prove Theorem
1.2
in dimensions
n\geq 7
, we need the following refinement of Lemma
5.2
:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 7
and
u_{0}
be a solution to (
5.8
). Let
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k}
,
\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}\right)_{k}
,
\left(\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)_{k}
,
\left(B_{k}\right)_{k}
and
\left(g_{\xi}\right)_{\xi}
be as in Lemma
5.2
. Then, for
i\in\left\{0,1,2\right\}
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{i}\left(u_{k}-u_{0}+B_{k}\right)\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\bigO\left(\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\right)^{i}}&&\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is l.c.f.}\\
&\frac{1}{\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\right)^{i}}+\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\right)^{n-6+i}}&&\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right\}\right)

(5.18)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in M
, where l.c.f. stands for locally conformally flat.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

We only need to prove (
5.4
) when
i=0
, since the cases
i=1
and
i=2
follow by standard interior estimates. Remark that when
x
satisfies
d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\geq\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}
, the estimate (
5.4
) when
i=0
follows from (
5.10
). Therefore, it suffices to consider the case where
d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)<\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}
as
k\to\infty
. In this case, we use an approach inspired from the work of Chen–Lin
[
10
]
in the case of positive solutions that was applied to the Yamabe equation by Marques
[
30
]
. By letting
x=\exp_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}y\right)
for
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
, it is easily seen that (
5.4
) is implied by the following estimate:

\left|\nabla^{i}\left(\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}\right)\left(y\right)\right|=\bigO\left(\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{i}}&&\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is l.c.f.}\\
&\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{i}}+\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{4}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n-6+i}}&&\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right\}\right),

(5.19)

where
\hat{u}_{k}
is as in (
5.16
) and we have let

B_{0}\left(y\right):=\left(1+\frac{\left|y\right|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.20)

As mentioned before, we only have to prove (
5.19
) for
i=0
. By conformal invariance of the conformal Laplacian, the equation (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}\hat{u}_{k}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\hat{h}_{k}\hat{u}_{k}=\left|\hat{u}_{k}\right|^{2^{*}-2}\hat{u}_{k}\quad\text{in }B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big),

(5.21)

where
\hat{h}_{k}
and
\hat{g}_{k}
are as in (
5.16
) and (
5.17
). We let
y_{k}\in\overline{B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big)}
be such that

\left|\left(\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}\right)\left(y_{k}\right)\right|=\max_{\overline{B(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}})}}\left|\left(\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}\right)\right|=:\lambda_{k},

(5.22)

and we define

\psi_{k}:=\lambda_{k}^{-1}\left(\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}\right).

By (
5.10
),
\lambda_{k}=\smallo(1)
as
k\to\infty
. By using (
5.21
) together with the equation
\Delta_{\delta_{0}}B_{0}=B_{0}^{2^{*}-1}
, we obtain

\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}\psi_{k}=\left(2^{*}-1\right)B_{0}^{2^{*}-2}\psi_{k}+\lambda_{k}^{-1}f_{k}\quad\text{in }B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big),

(5.23)

where

f_{k}:=\left(\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}-\Delta_{\delta_{0}}\right)B_{0}-\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\hat{h}_{k}\hat{u}_{k}+\left|\hat{u}_{k}\right|^{2^{*}-2}\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}^{2^{*}-1}-\left(2^{*}-1\right)\lambda_{k}B_{0}^{2^{*}-2}\psi_{k}.

(5.24)

We now estimate the terms in the right-hand side of (
5.24
). Since
B_{0}
is radially symmetric, it follows from (
5.6
) that

\left(\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}-\Delta_{\delta_{0}}\right)B_{0}\left(y\right)=\bigO\big(\overline{\mu}_{k}^{N}\left|y\right|^{N-1}\left|\nabla B_{0}\left(y\right)\right|\big)=\bigO\left(\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{N}\left|y\right|^{N}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n}}\right)

(5.25)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
. By using (
5.7
) and (
5.10
), we obtain

\hat{h}_{k}\left(y\right)\hat{u}_{k}\left(y\right)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is l.c.f.}\\
&\bigO\left(\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\left|y\right|^{2}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n-2}}\right)&&\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

(5.26)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
. Since
2^{*}-2<2
when
n\geq 7
, by using (
5.10
) together with straightforward estimates, we obtain

\left|\hat{u}_{k}\right|^{2^{*}-2}\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}^{2^{*}-1}-\left(2^{*}-1\right)\lambda_{k}B_{0}^{2^{*}-2}\psi_{k}=\smallo\left(\lambda_{k}B_{0}^{2^{*}-2}\left|\psi_{k}\right|\right)

(5.27)

as
k\to\infty
, uniformly with respect to
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
. Let
G_{k}
be the Green’s function of the operator
\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}
in
B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
with zero Dirichlet boundary condition. Then

\psi_{k}\left(y\right)=\int_{B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)}G_{k}\left(y,\cdot\right)\left(\left(2^{*}-1\right)B_{0}^{2^{*}-2}\psi_{k}+\lambda_{k}^{-1}f_{k}\right)dv_{\hat{g}_{k}}\\
-\int_{\partial B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)}\partial_{\nu}G_{k}\left(y,\cdot\right)\psi_{k}\,d\sigma_{\hat{g}_{k}}

(5.28)

for all points
x\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
.
Standard estimates on Green’s functions (see e.g. Robert
[
45
]
) give that for large
k
,

G_{k}\left(y,z\right)=\bigO\big(\left|y-z\right|^{2-n}\big)\quad\text{for }z\in B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big)

(5.29)

and

\partial_{\nu}G_{k}\left(y,z\right)=\bigO\big(\left|y-z\right|^{1-n}\big)\quad\text{for }z\in\partial B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big)

(5.30)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
. Remark also that (
5.10
) gives

\psi_{k}=\bigO\left(\lambda_{k}^{-1}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right)\,\text{as }k\to\infty\text{, uniformly in }B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big)\backslash B\big(0,1/2\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big)\ .

(5.31)

By using (
5.25
), (
5.26
), (
5.27
) and (
5.28
)–(
5.31
) together with straightforward integral estimates and the fact that
n\geq 7
, we obtain

\psi_{k}\left(y\right)=\bigO\Bigg(\int_{B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)}\frac{\left|\psi_{k}\left(z\right)\right|dz}{\left|y-z\right|^{n-2}\left(1+\left|z\right|\right)^{4}}+\lambda_{k}^{-1}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\\
\left\{+\frac{\lambda_{k}^{-1}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{4}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n-6}}\quad\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is not l.c.f.}\right\}\Bigg)

(5.32)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
. We now claim that

\lambda_{k}=\bigO\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left\{+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{4}\quad\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is not l.c.f.}\right\}\right)

(5.33)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume by contradiction that (
5.33
) does not hold true, i.e. there exists a subsequence
\left(k_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive numbers such that

k_{j}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\mu}_{k_{j}}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\big\{+\overline{\mu}_{k_{j}}^{4}\quad\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is not l.c.f.}\big\}=\smallo\left(\lambda_{k_{j}}\right)

(5.34)

as
j\to\infty
. Since
\left|\psi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq 1
in
B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k_{j}}}\big)
and
\hat{g}_{k_{j}}\to\delta_{0}
as
j\to\infty
, uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it follows from (
5.23
)–(
5.27
) together with standard elliptic estimates that up to a subsequence,
\left(\psi_{k_{j}}\right)_{j}
converges in
C^{1}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
as
j\to\infty
to a solution
\psi_{0}\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
of the equation

\Delta_{\delta_{0}}\psi_{0}=\left(2^{*}-1\right)B_{0}^{2^{*}-2}\psi_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.35)

Independently, it follows from (
5.32
) and (
5.34
) that

\psi_{k_{j}}\left(y\right)=\bigO\big(\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{-2}\big)+\smallo\left(1\right)

(5.36)

as
j\to\infty
, uniformly with respect to
y\in B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k_{j}}}\big)
. Passing to the limit into (
5.36
) as
j\to\infty
, we then obtain

\psi_{0}\left(y\right)=\bigO\big(\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{-2}\big)

(5.37)

uniformly with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By applying Lemma 2.4 of Chen–Lin
[
9
]
, it follows from (
5.35
) and (
5.37
) that

\psi_{0}\left(y\right)=c_{0}\frac{1-\frac{\left|y\right|^{2}}{n(n-2)}}{\left(1+\frac{\left|y\right|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\frac{y_{i}}{\left(1+\frac{\left|y\right|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for some numbers
c_{0},\dotsc,c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand, it follows from (
5.9
) that
\psi_{k}\left(0\right)=\left|\nabla\psi_{k}\left(0\right)\right|=0
, which gives
\psi_{0}\left(0\right)=\left|\nabla\psi_{0}\left(0\right)\right|=0
. Therefore, we obtain
c_{0}=\dotsb=c_{n}=0
, and so
\psi_{0}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
\psi_{k_{j}}\left(y_{k_{j}}\right)=1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
y_{k_{j}}
is as in (
5.22
), we then obtain that
\left|y_{k_{j}}\right|\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, which is in contradiction with (
5.36
). This proves that (
5.33
) holds true. Finally, by using (
5.33
) together with successive applications of (
5.32
), we obtain that

\psi_{k}\left(y\right)=\bigO\Bigg(\frac{\ln\left(2+\left|y\right|\right)}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n-2}}+\lambda_{k}^{-1}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left\{+\frac{\lambda_{k}^{-1}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{4}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n-6}}\quad\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is not l.c.f.}\right\}\Bigg)

(5.38)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in B\big(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\big)
. The estimate (
5.19
) with
i=0
then follows from (
5.33
) and (
5.38
).
∎

We can now use Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume by contradiction that

E(M,[g])\leq Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}])^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y(M,[g])^{\frac{n}{2}}.

By using the definition of
E(M,[g])
together with a diagonal argument, it follows that there exists a sequence of blowing-up solutions
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to (
1.1
) such that

E\left(u_{k}\right)\leq Y\left(M,[g]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}+Y\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{std}]\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}+\smallo\left(1\right)

as
k\to\infty
. In the case where the functions
u_{k}
do not change sign, we obtain a contradiction with the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
by applying the compactness results for positive solutions of the Yamabe equation (see Schoen
[
51
,
52
]
, Li–Zhu
[
29
]
, Druet
[
19
]
, Marques
[
30
]
, Li–Zhang
[
27
,
28
]
and Khuri–Marques–Schoen
[
25
]
). Therefore, in what follows, we assume that the functions
u_{k}
change sign. It then follows from Proposition
5.1
that up to a subsequence and replacing
u_{k}
by
-u_{k}
if necessary, the functions
u_{k}
are of the form (
5.2
) for some energy-minimizing, positive solution
u_{0}
to (
1.2
). In the case where
n\leq 6
, we can apply a more general compactness result that we obtained in Premoselli–Vétois
[
42
]
(Theorem 1.2 in
[
42
]
). Therefore, in what follows, we assume that
n\geq 7
. By using (
5.4
) (see also (
5.19
)) and (
5.25
), we then obtain

\displaystyle\left(\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}-\Delta_{\delta_{0}}\right)\hat{u}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bigO\Bigg(\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{N}\left|y\right|^{N}}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n}}+\left|\left(\hat{g}_{k}-\delta_{0}\right)\left(y\right)\right||\nabla^{2}\left(\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}\right)\left(y\right)|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\nabla\left(\hat{g}_{k}-\delta_{0}\right)\left(y\right)\right|\left|\nabla\left(\hat{u}_{k}+B_{0}\right)\left(y\right)\right|\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\bigO\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\{+\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{6}\left|y\right|}{\left(1+\left|y\right|\right)^{n-4}}\quad\text{ if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is not l.c.f.}\right\}\right)

(5.39)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)
. It then follows from (
5.4
), (
5.26
) and (
5
) that

\int_{B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)}\left(\left<\nabla\hat{u}_{k},\cdot\right>_{\delta_{0}}+\frac{n-2}{2}\hat{u}_{k}\right)\left(\left(\Delta_{\hat{g}_{k}}-\Delta_{\delta_{0}}\right)\hat{u}_{k}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\hat{h}_{k}\hat{u}_{k}\right)dv_{\delta_{0}}\\
-\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\int_{B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)}\left(\left<\nabla B_{0},\cdot\right>_{\delta_{0}}+\frac{n-2}{2}B_{0}\right)\hat{h}_{k}B_{0}\,dv_{\delta_{0}}\\
=\bigO\left(\left\{\begin{aligned} &\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}}&&\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is l.c.f.}\\
&\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{6}&&\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right\}\right)

(5.40)

for large
k
. On the other hand, by using (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) together with straightforward computations and symmetry arguments, we obtain

\int_{B\left(0,1/\sqrt{\overline{\mu}_{k}}\right)}\left(\left<\nabla B_{0},\cdot\right>_{\delta_{0}}+\frac{n-2}{2}B_{0}\right)\hat{h}_{k}B_{0}\,dv_{\delta_{0}}\\
=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is l.c.f.}\\
&a_{n}\left|\Weyl_{g}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}+\smallo\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\right)&&\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

(5.41)

as
k\to\infty
, where

a_{n}:=\frac{c_{n}}{24}(n-2)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(1+\frac{\left|y\right|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\bigg)^{1-n}\bigg(\frac{\left|y\right|^{2}}{n(n-2)}-1\bigg)\frac{\left|y\right|^{2}dy}{n(n-2)}.

(5.42)

The constant

\displaystyle a_{n}=\frac{c_{n}}{6}\frac{n^{\frac{n}{2}}(n-2)^{\frac{n+4}{2}}}{n-6}\omega_{n-1}I_{n-1}^{\frac{n}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(n-2)^{\frac{n+4}{2}}}{12(n-6)(n-4)}\omega_{n-1}I_{n}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-2)^{2}}{6(n-6)(n-4)}K_{n}^{-n}.

(5.43)

By putting together (
5.15

5.40

\frac{1}{2}n^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(n-2)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\omega_{n-1}u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-6}{2}}-a_{n}\left|\Weyl_{g}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}\\

(5.45)

Remark 5.5
.

The approach used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
can be extended to the case of sequences of solutions
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of type (
5.2
) to linear perturbations of the Yamabe equation of the form

\Delta_{g}u_{k}+\left(c_{n}\Scal_{g}+\varepsilon_{k}h\right)u_{k}=\left|u_{k}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{k}\quad\text{in }M,

(5.46)

where
h\in C^{0,\vartheta}\left(M\right)
,
\vartheta\in\left(0,1\right)
, and
\left(\varepsilon_{k}\right)_{k}
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. In the case of positive solutions, perturbed equations of the form (
5.2
) have been studied for example by Esposito–Pistoia–Vétois
[
21
]
, Morabito–Pistoia–Vaira
[
34
]
, Premoselli
[
40
]
and Robert–Vétois
[
49
]
. In this case, in place of (
5.4
), we obtain, for
i\in\left\{0,1,2\right\}
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{i}\left(u_{k}-u_{0}+B_{k}\right)\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\bigO\left(\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\right)^{i}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{k}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\right)^{n-4+i}}\quad\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is l.c.f.}\\
&\frac{1}{\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\right)^{i}}+\frac{\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\varepsilon_{k}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)^{2}\right)}{\left(\overline{\mu}_{k}+d_{g_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left(x,\overline{\xi}_{k}\right)\right)^{n-4+i}}\quad\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right\}\right)

uniformly with respect to
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in M
, and in place of (
5.44
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}n^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(n-2)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\omega_{n-1}u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-6}{2}}-a_{n}\left|\Weyl_{g}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}+b_{n}\varepsilon_{k}h\left(\xi_{0}\right)\\
=\smallo\left(\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon_{k}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-6}{2}}&&\text{if }\left(M,g\right)\text{ is l.c.f.}\\
&\varepsilon_{k}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{2}+\overline{\mu}_{k}^{\frac{n-6}{2}}&&\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right\}\right)

as
k\to\infty
, where
a_{n}
is as in (
5.42
) and
b_{n}
is another positive constant depending only on
n
. This yields that there does not exist any sign-changing blowing-up sequences of solutions of type (
5.2
) to (
5.46
) in each of the following situations:

•

3\leq n\leq 6
,

•

h\geq 0
and
7\leq n\leq 9
,

•

h\geq 0
,
n=10
and
u_{0}>\frac{5}{567}|\Weyl_{g}|^{2}_{g}
for all points in
M
,

•

h\leq 0
,
n=10
and
u_{0}<\frac{5}{567}|\Weyl_{g}|^{2}_{g}
for all points in
M
,

•

h\geq 0
,
n\geq 11
and
\left(M,g\right)
is locally conformally flat,

•

h\leq 0
,
n\geq 11
and
\Weyl_{g}\neq 0
for all points in
M
.

Conversely, it is not difficult to adapt the constructive proofs in Esposito–Pistoia–Vétois
[
21
]
and Robert–Vétois
[
49
]
to prove that if
u_{0}
is a nondegenerate positive solution to (
1.2
), then there exists a blowing-up sequence of solutions of type (
5.2
) to (
5.46
) in each of the following situations:

•

\displaystyle\min_{M}h<0
and
7\leq n\leq 9
,

•

\displaystyle\min_{M}h<0
,
n=10
and
u_{0}>\frac{5}{567}|\Weyl_{g}|^{2}_{g}
for all points in
M
,

•

\displaystyle\max_{M}h>0
,
n=10
and
u_{0}<\frac{5}{567}|\Weyl_{g}|^{2}_{g}
for all points in

•

\displaystyle\min_{M}h<0

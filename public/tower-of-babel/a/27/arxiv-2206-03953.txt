Tight Bounds on the Chromatic Edge Stability Index of Graphs
Saieed Akbari, John Haslegrave, Mehrbod Javadi, Nasim Nahvi, Helia Niaparast

Abstract
The chromatic edge stability index es χ ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G) of a graph G G is the minimum number of edges whose removal results in a graph with smaller chromatic index. We give best-possible upper bounds on es χ ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G) in terms of the number of vertices of degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) (if G G is Class 2), and the numbers of vertices of degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) and Δ ⁡ ( G ) − 1 {\Delta(G)-1} (if G G is Class 1). If G G is bipartite we give an exact expression for es χ ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G) involving the maximum size of a matching in the subgraph induced by the vertices of degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) . Finally, we consider whether a minimum mitigating set, that is a set of size es χ ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G) whose removal reduces the chromatic index, has the property that every edge meets a vertex of degree at least Δ ⁡ ( G ) − 1 \Delta(G)-1 ; we prove that this is true for some minimum mitigating set of G G , but not necessarily for every minimum mitigating set of G G .

Main Results

We now state our first theorem regarding the chromatic edge stability index.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a graph of Class
2
. Then,

{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leqslant{\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}}}
.
Furthermore, if
G
is connected and
t_{\Delta}
is odd, then equality holds if and only if
G
is obtained from
K_{2n+1}
by removing
s
independent edges for some integers

and
s
,
{0\leq s\leq n-1}
.

Proof.

Note that it is sufficient to remove a set of edges

such that
|F|\leq\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}
and
{G\setminus F}
is Class 1, since then we have
{\chi^{\prime}(G\setminus F)=\Delta(G\setminus F)\leq\Delta(G)<\chi^{\prime}(G)}
.

By Theorem
2
, since
G
is Class 2 it contains a cycle of vertices of degree
\Delta(G)
; removing an edge from that cycle reduces the number of vertices of degree
\Delta(G)
by
2
. Continue in this manner until either the remaining graph is Class 1 or
{\lfloor\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}}\rfloor
edges have been removed. In the latter case the number of vertices of degree
\Delta(G)
remaining is at most
2
, so there is no cycle of such vertices and Theorem
2
implies the remaining graph is Class 1. Thus
{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leqslant\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}}
.

Now, we prove the second part of the theorem. Let

A
be the set of vertices of maximum degree. First, assume that
{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}}
. Consider a maximum matching
M
in
G_{\Delta}
and denote by

the set of vertices saturated by

. Remove the edges of

from

and call the new graph
G^{\prime}
. Since

is odd,

. Note that
G^{\prime}_{\Delta}=G[A\setminus V_{M}]
has no edges and by Theorem
2

G^{\prime}
is Class 1. Hence,
{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)}\leqslant\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})
.
Notice that we have

{\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})\leq\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}}

{\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})=\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}}
. Hence,
{|A\setminus V_{M}|=1}
. Suppose that
{A\setminus V_{M}=\{v\}}
. We claim that

. By contradiction, assume
{uv\notin E(G_{\Delta})}

{u\in V_{M}}
. Suppose

except

.
Note that
{V(G^{\prime\prime}_{\Delta})=\{u,v,w\}}

{\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime\prime})=\Delta(G^{\prime\prime})=\Delta(G)<\chi^{\prime}(G)}

Similarly, for every vertex
{u\in V_{M}}

.
Therefore,

{G_{\Delta}\cong K_{t_{\Delta}}}
. Now, remove

{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)-1}

{\chi^{\prime}(H)=\chi^{\prime}(G)}
. Note that
{\Delta(H)=\Delta(G)}
. Hence, we have

{\chi^{\prime}(H)=\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1=\Delta(H)+1}

,

is obtained from

by removing

s

{s=(n-1)-(\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})-1)=n-\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})=n-\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq n-1}
.

Now, assume that

{\frac{t_{\Delta}-1}{2}=\frac{2n+1-2s-1}{2}=n-s}

{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq n-s}

Now, we prove that
{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\geq n-s}

In the next result, we provide an upper bound for the chromatic edge stability index in terms of

Theorem 5
.

For every graph
G
,
\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq t_{\Delta}+\frac{t_{\Delta-1}}{2}
. Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
t_{\Delta-1}=0
and
G_{\Delta}
has no edges.

Proof.

We prove the following slightly stronger statement by induction. Let
H
be a graph with maximum degree at most
d
.
Let
t
be the number of vertices of degree
d
, and let
s
be the number of vertices of degree
d-1
that have a neighbor of degree at least
d-1
. Then we may remove at most
k
edges from
H
to leave a graph of chromatic index at most
d-1
, where
k=t
if
s=0
and the vertices of degree
d
form an independent set, and
k=t+\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\rfloor
otherwise.

We prove this by induction on
t
. If
t=0
and
s=0
, the statement follows immediately from Theorem
1
. If
t=0
and
s\geq 1
then
H
has maximum degree
d-1
and it suffices to remove
\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\rfloor
edges to leave a graph of Class 1. We do this as in the proof of Theorem
4
: remove edges between vertices of degree
d-1
until at most one such edge remains, leaving a graph of Class 1 by Theorem
2
. All such edges lie within the set

of vertices of degree
d-1
meeting another vertex of degree
d-1
, initially
|A|=s
, and each edge removed reduces
|A|
by
2
, so after at most
\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\rfloor
edges are removed we have
|A|\leq 2
and so

has at most one edge, as required.

Now suppose
t>0
. If there is an edge
uv
between two vertices of degree
d
, remove that edge. If not, let
w
be an arbitrary vertex of degree
d
. If
w
has a neighbor
x
of degree
d-1
, remove the edge
wx
. Otherwise, choose an arbitrary neighbor
y
and remove the edge
wy
. In each case we have removed one edge to leave a graph
H^{\prime}
; let
t^{\prime}
be the number of vertices of
H^{\prime}
having degree
d
, and
s^{\prime}
be the number of vertices of degree
d-1
that are adjacent to some other vertex of degree at least
d-1
. We now apply the induction hypothesis to
H^{\prime}
.

In the first case, we have
t^{\prime}=t-2
and
s^{\prime}\leq s+2
. We may therefore remove at most
t-2+\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\rfloor
edges from
H^{\prime}
to leave a graph
H^{\prime\prime}
with
\chi^{\prime}(H^{\prime\prime})\leq d-1
; together with the edge

, we have removed at most
t+\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\rfloor
edges from

to obtain

.

In the second case, we have
t^{\prime}=t-1
and
s^{\prime}\leq s
, since the set of vertices of degree

with a neighbor of degree at least
d-1
may gain

, but must lose

. We may therefore remove at most
k^{\prime}
edges from
H^{\prime}
to leave a graph
H^{\prime\prime}
with
\chi^{\prime}(H^{\prime\prime})\leq d-1
, where
k^{\prime}=t^{\prime}=t-1
if

and
k^{\prime}=t^{\prime}+\lfloor\frac{s^{\prime}-1}{2}\rfloor
if

. It follows that
k^{\prime}\leq k-1
and so we have removed at most
k
edges from
H
to obtain
H^{\prime\prime}
.

In the final case, we have
t^{\prime}=t-1
and
s^{\prime}=s
, since

but
w
has no neighbor of degree at least
d-1
, so is not counted in

. Thus we may remove at most

to leave a graph

with
\chi^{\prime}(H^{\prime\prime})\leq d-1
, as required.

This completes the proof of the stronger statement. The required upper bounds follow by taking

and

, since
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta
. Note that we obtain the stronger bound of

and

has no edges.

It remains to show that equality holds if

and

has no edges. In this case, by Theorem

, we have
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta
.
In order to reduce the chromatic index to

\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\geq t_{\Delta}

(see Remark

). Let

be a graph with

,

Now, we provide a formula for the chromatic edge stability index of bipartite graphs in terms of

Theorem 6
.

For every bipartite graph
G
,
\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=t_{\Delta}-\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\geq t_{\Delta}-\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})}
. Assume that
S^{\prime}
is the smallest mitigating set of
G
. Note that since
G
is bipartite, it is Class 1
[
6
]
. Hence, in order to reduce
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, it is necessary and sufficient that there are no vertices of degree
\Delta(G)
left in the remaining graph.
We now state two claims:

Claim 1.

G[S^{\prime}]
is acyclic.
By contradiction, assume that
G[S^{\prime}]
contains a cycle
C
. Consider an edge
e=uv
in
C
and let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained by removing the edges of the path
C-e
from
G
. Note that
d_{G^{\prime}}(u)
and
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)
do not exceed
\Delta(G)-1
. Hence, by removing
S^{\prime}-e
from
G
,
\chi^{\prime}(G)
is reduced, a contradiction.

Claim 2.

G[S^{\prime}]
contains no path of length 3.
Similarly to the previous claim, if
G[S^{\prime}]
contains a path of length 3, by removing the middle edge of such a path from
S^{\prime}
, we get a subset of the edges of size
|S^{\prime}|-1
whose removal reduces
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, a contradiction.

By the claims above, one can conclude that each component of
G[S^{\prime}]
is the graph
K_{1,r}
for some
r\geq 1
.

Assume that the components of
G[S^{\prime}]
are
{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}}
. If
S_{i}
is a component with at least two edges and
l
is a leaf of
S_{i}
, then
{d_{G}(l)=\Delta(G)}
, because otherwise by removing the edge incident with
l
from
S^{\prime}
, we get a subset of edges of size
|S^{\prime}|-1
whose removal reduces
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, a contradiction. By a similar argument, if
S_{i}
is a single edge, at least one of its endpoints has degree
\Delta(G)
in
G
. Now, assume that in exactly
r
components of
G[S^{\prime}]
, all vertices have degree
\Delta(G)
in
G
. Hence, if we take one edge of each of these components, we get a matching of size
r
in
G_{\Delta}
. Therefore,
{\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})\geq r}
. Note that
G[S^{\prime}]
contains all vertices of degree
\Delta(G)
in
G
. Since
t_{\Delta}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{|E(S_{i})|}+r
, we have the following:

\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=|S^{\prime}|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{|E(S_{i})|}=t_{\Delta}-r\geq t_{\Delta}-\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta}).

Now, we prove that
{\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq t_{\Delta}-\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta})}
.
Consider a maximum matching
M
in
G_{\Delta}
. There are
t_{\Delta}-2|M|
vertices of degree
\Delta(G)
in
G
which are not saturated by
M
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained by removing the edges of
M
and one incident edge of each of these vertices from
G
. Notice that
{\Delta(G^{\prime})\leq\Delta(G)-1}
and since
G^{\prime}
is Class 1,
{\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G^{\prime})<\Delta(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)}
. Hence, we get the following:

\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq|M|+t_{\Delta}-2|M|=t_{\Delta}-\alpha^{\prime}(G_{\Delta}).

The proof is completed.

3
Structure of minimum mitigating sets

The mitigating sets constructed in the previous section all have the property that they only contain edges meeting vertices of degree
\Delta(G)
or
\Delta(G)-1
. It is natural to ask whether minimum-size mitigating sets (i.e. sets of
\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)
edges whose removal reduces the chromatic index) necessarily have this property. We will show that the answer is “yes” in the sense that every graph has a minimum-size mitigating set with this property, but “no” in the sense that some graphs also have minimum-size mitigating sets that do not satisfy this property.

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be any graph. Then there exists a mitigating set
S
for
G
of size
\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)
such that if
e=uv\in S
then

\max\{d(u),d(v)\}\geq\Delta(G)-1.

(1)

Proof.

Let
T
be any mitigating set of
G
of size
\mathrm{es}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)
, and suppose that
T
contains an edge
e=uv
which does not satisfy (
1
). We will adapt the proof of Vizing’s theorem to show that there is some edge
uv^{\prime}
with
d(v^{\prime})\geq\Delta(G)-1
, such that
T\cup\{uv^{\prime}\}\setminus\{uv\}
is also a mitigating set for
G
. By a sequence of such replacements, we eventually obtain a set
S
with the required properties.

Write
G^{\prime}=G\setminus T
. Then, by minimality of
T
,
\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime})=\chi^{\prime}(G)-1
but
\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime}\cup\{uv\})=\chi^{\prime}(G)
. Write
k
for
\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime})
; note that
k\geq\Delta(G)-1
. Fix a
k
-edge-coloring of
G^{\prime}
. For any two colors
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, we say that a path is a
c_{1}c_{2}

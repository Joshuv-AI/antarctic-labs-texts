The latter can be written as a

In this article we improve on both points. We perform a novel analysis of the RC to
g_{A}
based on the
dispersion relation (DR) approach. It is a powerful tool which has proved successful in the treatment of the RCs to the Fermi amplitude in the free neutron and superallowed

;
Seng et al. 2019
;
Gorchtein 2019
;
Seng et al. 2020a

and

and

and
\lambda

General framework

We start by defining the hadronic currents relevant to the
\beta
-decay of the free neutron:

\displaystyle J_{em}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\bar{u}\gamma^{\mu}u-\frac{1}{3}\bar{d}\gamma^{\mu}d

\displaystyle J_{W}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})d~.

(4)

Their single-nucleon matrix elements are given by:

\displaystyle\langle N(p_{f},s_{f})|J_{em}^{\mu}|N(p_{i},s_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{s_{f}}(p_{f})\left[F_{1}^{N}\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{i}{2M}F_{2}^{N}\sigma^{\mu\nu}(p_{f}-p_{i})_{\nu}\right]u_{s_{i}}(p_{i})

\displaystyle\langle p(p_{f},s_{f})|J_{W}^{\mu}|n(p_{i},s_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{s_{f}}(p_{f})\left[F_{1}^{W}\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{i}{2M}F_{2}^{W}\sigma^{\mu\nu}(p_{f}-p_{i})_{\nu}+G_{A}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}-\frac{G_{P}}{2M}\gamma_{5}(p_{f}-p_{i})^{\mu}\right]u_{s_{i}}(p_{i})~,

(5)

where

All the form factors above are functions of
-(p_{i}-p_{f})^{2}

F_{1,2}^{V}=F_{1,2}^{p}-F_{1,2}^{n}

F_{1}^{W}(0)=\mathring{g}_{V}
,

, which represent the Fermi and Gamow-Teller matrix element in neutron
\beta
-decay respectively. In particular,

;

\mathring{g}_{A}
is not protected by any exact symmetry. We do not include the isospin-breaking correction to
\mathring{g}_{A}

The nucleon mass difference
\Delta=M_{n}-M_{p}\approx 1.3

. Therefore, the tree-level amplitude of the decay process

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{tree}}=-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}L_{\lambda}\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}(p)\gamma^{\mu}(\mathring{g}_{V}+\mathring{g}_{A}\gamma_{5})u_{s}(p)+\mathcal{O}(\Delta^{2})~,

(6)

where
G_{F}=1.1663787(6)\times 10^{-5}

is the Fermi constant measured from the muon decay
Zyla et al. 2020

p=(p_{n}+p_{p})/2
is the average nucleon momentum, and
L_{\lambda}=\bar{u}(p_{e})\gamma_{\lambda}(1-\gamma_{5})v(p_{\nu})
is the lepton piece. The recoil corrections scale as
\Delta/M\sim 10^{-3}
, which are small but important in precision physics. They were studied in detail with both conventional methods and effective field theory (EFT)
Holstein 1974
;
Wilkinson 1982
;
Ando et al. 2004
;
Gudkov 2008
;
Ivanov et al. 2013
;
Ivanov et al. 2021

RCs of the order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{em}/\pi)
must be included for a precise extraction of the weak coupling parameters. In the usual nomenclature, they are divided into the “outer” and “inner” corrections The former is a function of
\{E_{e},m_{e}\}

but depends on details of the hadronic structure. The squared amplitude for the decay of a polarized neutron (to unpolarized final states) after the inclusion of the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{em}/\pi)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16G_{F}^{2}|V_{ud}|^{2}M_{n}M_{p}E_{e}(E_{m}-E_{e})g_{V}^{2}(1+3\lambda^{2})F(\beta)\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}\delta^{(1)}\right)\left\{1+\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}\delta^{(2)}\right)a_{0}\frac{\vec{p}_{e}\cdot\vec{p}_{\nu}}{E_{e}E_{\nu}}\right.

(7)

The
\gamma W
-box correction is of the natural size
\alpha_{em}/\pi\sim 10^{-3}
. Taking into account recoil corrections
\sim\Delta/M,\,m_{e}/M

\Delta=m_{e}=0
throughout the calculation below, as well as the proton recoil. This approximation also leads to the neglect of the pion pole due to the partially-conserved axial current (PCAC) hypothesis: the pion pole contribution, when contracted with the lepton tensor, results in lepton mass terms which, as stated are neglected. This precision level is supported by the fact that the lowest hadronic state is separated by the pion mass
\sim 140
MeV which is about hundred times larger than
\Delta
. Notice however that this approximation may not be as safe for nuclear
\beta
-decay where the available
Q

The part of the
\gamma W
-box diagram amplitude that contributes to the inner correction must involve an antisymmetric tensor that stems from the lepton spinor structure. It reads:

\mathcal{M}_{\gamma W}^{\mathrm{inner}}=-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}L_{\lambda}I_{\gamma W}^{\lambda}~,

(12)

where

I_{\gamma W}^{\lambda}=\bar{u}_{s}(p)\gamma^{\lambda}\left[\mathring{g}_{V}\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}+\mathring{g}_{A}\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}\gamma_{5}\right]u_{s}(p)=ie^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}-q^{2}}\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\lambda}q_{\alpha}}{(q^{2})^{2}}T_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W}

(13)

with
\epsilon^{0123}=-1
in our convention. The forward generalized Compton tensor describing the
W^{+}n\rightarrow\gamma p
process, is defined as:

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{\gamma W}=\int dxe^{iqx}\langle p|T[J^{\mu}_{em}(x)J^{\nu}_{W}(0)]|n\rangle.

(14)

To extract
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}
and
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
, we use following identities:

\frac{1}{2}\bar{u}_{s}(p)\gamma^{\mu}u_{s}(p)=p^{\mu}~,\>\>\frac{1}{2}\bar{u}_{s}(p)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}u_{s}(p)=S^{\mu}~,

(15)

where the spin vector
S^{\mu}
is fixed by
S^{2}=-M^{2}
and

, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ie^{2}}{2M^{2}\mathring{g}_{V}}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}-q^{2}}\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\lambda}q_{\alpha}p_{\lambda}}{(q^{2})^{2}}T_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W}

\displaystyle\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ie^{2}}{2M^{2}\mathring{g}_{A}}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}-q^{2}}\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\lambda}q_{\alpha}S_{\lambda}}{(q^{2})^{2}}T_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W}~.

(16)

In what follows, we use
\mathring{g}_{A}\approx\lambda=-1.2756

\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}\sim 10^{-3}

\displaystyle\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{2M\mathring{g}_{V}}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\frac{1}{(Q^{2})^{2}}\frac{\nu^{2}+Q^{2}}{\nu}T_{3}

\displaystyle\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{M\mathring{g}_{A}}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\frac{1}{(Q^{2})^{2}}\left\{\frac{\nu^{2}-{2Q^{2}}}{3\nu}{S_{1}}-\frac{Q^{2}}{\nu}{S_{2}}\right\}~,

(18)

where we have used the following identities:

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}q_{\alpha}F(\nu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\nu}{M}p_{\alpha}F(\nu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}q_{\alpha}q_{\beta}F(\nu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left[-\frac{\nu^{2}+Q^{2}}{3}g_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{4\nu^{2}+Q^{2}}{3}\frac{p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}}{M^{2}}\right]F(\nu,Q^{2})

(19)

To evaluate the loop integrals, we need to discuss the symmetry properties of the Compton amplitudes. We start by considering the isospin structure of amplitudes
T_{i},\,S_{i}
. Electromagnetic interaction does not conserve isospin and contains both isoscalar (
I=0
) and isovector (
I=1
) components. Therefore, each amplitude
A=T_{i},\,S_{i}
can be decomposed into components contributed by the isoscalar and isovector electromagnetic current respectively:

\displaystyle A=A^{(0)}+A^{(1)}.

(20)

The two isospin amplitudes have a different behavior under
\nu\rightarrow-\nu
:

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(I)}(-\nu,Q^{2})=\xi_{i}^{(I)}A_{i}^{(I)}(\nu,Q^{2}),

(21)

with
\xi_{i}^{(I)}=\pm 1
and
\xi_{i}^{(0)}=-\xi_{i}^{(1)}
. It is easy to show that
\xi_{i}^{(0)}=-1
for
T_{3}
and
S_{1,2}
, so only the
I=0
component of these amplitudes survives in the integrals in Eq.(

IV
Dispersion representation of the forward Compton amplitudes

Figure 2:
The singularities of the forward Compton amplitudes on the complex-
\nu

Forward Compton amplitudes have singularities along the real axis
\nu
: poles due to a single nucleon intermediate state in the
s-
and
u
-channels at
\nu=\pm\nu_{B}=\pm Q^{2}/(2M)
, respectively, and unitarity cuts at
\nu\geq\nu_{\pi}
and
\nu\leq-\nu_{\pi}
where
\nu_{\pi}=(2Mm_{\pi}+m_{\pi}^{2}+Q^{2})/(2M)
is the pion production threshold (see Fig.
2
). The discontinuity of the forward Compton tensor in the
s-
channel (i.e.
\nu\geq\nu_{B}
) is given by the generalization of the on-shell hadronic tensor to the
\gamma W
-interference:

\mathrm{Disc}T^{\gamma W}_{\mu\nu}(\nu)\equiv T^{\gamma W}_{\mu\nu}(\nu+i\varepsilon)-T^{\gamma W}_{\mu\nu}(\nu-i\varepsilon)=4\pi W_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W}~,

(22)

where

\displaystyle W_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{X}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+q-p_{X})\langle p|J_{\mu}^{em}(0)|X\rangle\langle X|J_{\nu}^{W}(0)|n\rangle

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-\frac{i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}q^{\alpha}p^{\beta}}{2(p\cdot q)}F_{3}+\frac{i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}q^{\alpha}}{(p\cdot q)}\left[S^{\beta}g_{1}+\left(S^{\beta}-\frac{(S\cdot q)}{p\cdot q}p^{\beta}\right)g_{2}\right]+...}

The structure functions
F_{3}
and
g_{1,2}
can be decomposed similarly to
I=0,1
components just like Eq.(
20
).

According to the crossing behavior established earlier and noticing that they cannot diverge faster than
\nu
when
\nu\rightarrow\infty
, the amplitudes entering Eq. (
18
) have the following dispersion representation:

\displaystyle T_{3}^{(0)}(\nu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4i\nu\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\frac{F_{3}^{(0)}(\nu^{\prime},Q^{2})}{\nu^{\prime 2}-\nu^{2}}

\displaystyle S_{1}^{(0)}(\nu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4i\nu\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\frac{g_{1}^{(0)}(\nu^{\prime},Q^{2})}{\nu^{\prime 2}-\nu^{2}}

\displaystyle S_{2}^{(0)}(\nu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4i\nu\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\frac{g_{2}^{(0)}(\nu^{\prime},Q^{2})}{\nu^{\prime 2}-\nu^{2}}=-4i\nu^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu^{\prime}\frac{g_{2}^{(0)}(\nu^{\prime},Q^{2})}{\nu^{\prime 2}(\nu^{\prime 2}-\nu^{2})}~,

(24)

where the
\nu
-integration is extended down to 0 to include the Born contribution. Notice that in the last line we have slightly modified the DR of
S_{2}^{(0)}
using the Burkhardt-Cottingham (BC) sum rule
Burkhardt and Cottingham 1970
:

\int_{0}^{1}dxg_{2}(x,Q^{2})=0~,

(25)

where
x=Q^{2}/(2M\nu^{\prime})
is the Bjorken variable. This sum rule is a superconvergence relation and is expected to hold at all
Q^{2}
. The benefit of this treatment will become apparent in the later section. Substituting Eq.(
24
) into Eq.(
18
) and using the following Wick rotation formula
Marciano and Sirlin 1975
,

\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}F(\nu,Q^{2})=\frac{i}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dQ^{2}Q^{2}\int_{-1}^{+1}du\sqrt{1-u^{2}}F(iQu,Q^{2})~,

(26)

we can integrate the variable
u
analytically to obtain our final dispersive representation of
\delta g_{V,A}^{\gamma W}
as follows:

\displaystyle\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{em}}{\pi\mathring{g}_{V}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{2}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{1+2r}{(1+r)^{2}}F_{3}^{(0)}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\alpha_{em}}{\pi\mathring{g}_{A}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{2}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{(1+r)^{2}}\left[\frac{5+4r}{3}g_{1}^{(0)}(x,Q^{2})-\frac{4M^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}g_{2}^{(0)}(x,Q^{2})\right]~,

(27)

with
r=\sqrt{1+4M^{2}x^{2}/Q^{2}}
. As a useful crosscheck, the two-photon exchange correction to the hyperfine splitting in ordinary and muonic atoms is expressed through analogous two-fold integrals over electromagnetic spin structure functions
Carlson et al. 2008
;
Carlson et al. 2011
.

The quantity
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}
, relevant for the extraction of
V_{ud}
from superallowed
\beta
-decays is well-studied within the dispersive approach
Seng et al. 2018
;
Seng et al. 2019
;
Seng et al. 2020a
;
Shiells et al. 2020
and is not addressed here. The main obstacle in those studies is the absence of direct experimental data of the structure function
F_{3}^{(0)}
. This forces one to either rely on data of
F_{3}
from a different isospin channel (whose relation to
F_{3}^{(0)}
contains a residual model-dependence), or from indirect lattice QCD data. On the other hand, despite having received much less attention, a high-precision dispersive analysis of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
is in fact much more robust because it depends on the parity-conserving, spin-dependent structure functions
g_{1,2}^{(0)}
. The isospin symmetry unambiguously relates them to
g_{1,2}^{N}
measured in ordinary DIS:

g_{1,2}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{g_{1,2}^{p}-g_{1,2}^{n}\right\}~,

(28)

the latter are defined via

\displaystyle W_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma\gamma,N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{X}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+q-p_{X})\langle N|J_{\mu}^{em}(0)|X\rangle\langle X|J_{\nu}^{em}(0)|N\rangle

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}q^{\alpha}}{(p\cdot q)}\left[S^{\beta}g_{1}^{N}+\left(S^{\beta}-\frac{(S\cdot q)}{p\cdot q}p^{\beta}\right)g_{2}^{N}\right]+...}

Therefore, it is possible to perform a fully data-driven analysis of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
without introducing further model-dependence at low
Q^{2}
. We will perform such an analysis in the sections below. Following Sirlin’s notation

, we express our result as

\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}=\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}d=\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}\left[d_{B}+d_{1}+d_{2}\right]~,

(30)

where
d_{B}
,
d_{1}
and

represent the elastic (Born) contribution, the inelastic contributions from
g_{1}^{(0)}
and the inelastic contributions from
g_{2}^{(0)}
respectively, which we will evaluate separately in the following sections.

(32)

where

.
We notice that

(33)

and

We pause here to comment on the Born contribution before moving on to the inelastic contributions. One may also try to derive it by calculating the Compton amplitudes from the first two Feynman diagrams in Fig.

\displaystyle-\frac{iM\nu Q^{2}}{Q^{4}-4M^{2}\nu^{2}-i\varepsilon}\left[F_{1}^{W}G_{M}^{S}+F_{1}^{S}G_{M}^{W}\right]+\frac{i\nu}{2M}F_{2}^{W}F_{2}^{S}

\displaystyle S_{2}^{(0),B}(\nu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle\frac{i\nu Q^{4}}{4M\left(Q^{4}-4M^{2}\nu^{2}-i\varepsilon\right)}\left[F_{2}^{W}G_{M}^{S}+G_{M}^{W}F_{2}^{S}\right]-\frac{i\nu}{4M}\left[F_{2}^{W}G_{M}^{S}+G_{M}^{W}F_{2}^{S}\right]~.

.

This discussion simply means that the definition of the “elastic” contribution is not exactly the same in the diagrammatic and the dispersive representation. Of course, if we were able to calculate the full (i.e. pole + seagull)

The total contribution of
g_{1}
reads,

d_{1}=d_{1}^{<}+d_{1}^{>}=2.14(4)_{\mathrm{data}}(1)_{\mathrm{HT}}~.

(45)

We note that the integral below
Q^{2}\leq 0.05
GeV
2
not covered by the data but making part of
d_{1}^{<}
is controlled by the isovector GDH sum rule
Gerasimov 1965
;
Drell and Hearn 1966
,
d\Gamma_{1}^{p-n}(Q^{2}=0)/dQ^{2}=(\kappa_{n}^{2}-\kappa_{p}^{2})/8M^{2}
, with
\kappa_{p,n}
denoting the proton’s (neutron’s) anomalous magnetic moment, respectively. Connecting the GDH-fixed value at
Q^{2}=0
to the lowest data point produces a negligible
d_{1}^{\mathrm{low}~Q^{2}}\lesssim 0.003
contribution which is safely accommodated within the uncertainty.

VI.2
Contribution of
g_{2}^{(0)}

In fact, we have already implemented this sum rule in the derivation of the DR of
S_{2}^{(0)}
. We emphasize the importance of this procedure for a reliable estimate of the contribution of
g_{2}^{(0)}
to
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
: since experimental data typically only cover the inelastic region, enforcing an exact vanishing of the first moment of
g_{2}^{(0)}
while operating with phenomenological parametrizations of different pieces can be a delicate matter. The explicit use of the BC sum rule thus precludes any numerically significant mistake caused by an imperfection of these parametrizations.
As a result, the dispersive representation of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
only contains higher moments of
g_{2}^{(0)}
, in which the non-perturbative physics at small
x
is suppressed. Additionally, since every extra power of
x^{2}
is accompanied by
1/Q^{2}
, the contribution of
g_{2}^{(0)}
bears no large logarithms.
Due to the smallness of the
g_{2}
contribution, we opt for an approximate treatment and include this result in the estimate of the systematic uncertainty.

The inelastic contribution coming from
g_{2}^{(0)}
reads,

\displaystyle d_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\mathring{g}_{A}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{2}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\int_{0}^{x_{\pi}}\frac{dx}{(1+r)^{2}}\frac{4M^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}\left[g_{2}^{p}(x,Q^{2})-g_{2}^{n}(x,Q^{2})\right]~,

(46)

where the isospin relation in Eq.(
28
) is used.
Rather than relying on data on
g_{2}^{N}
, we decompose
g_{2}
into twist-two and twist-three (and higher) components
Zyla et al. 2020
,
g_{2}^{N}=g_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{N}+g_{2,\mathrm{tw3}+}^{N}
, and use the Wandzura-Wilczek relation
Wandzura and Wilczek 1977
for the former,

\displaystyle g_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{N}(x,Q^{2})=-g_{1}^{N}(x,Q^{2})+\int\limits_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}g_{1}^{N}(y,Q^{2})~.

(47)

Notice that the relation above should be understood to not contain the elastic contribution at
x=1
, because otherwise one could take
x_{\pi}<x<1
at both sides, and then the left hand side and the first term at the right hand side would vanish but the second term at the right hand side would not, which is a contradiction. With this in mind, we multiply both sides by
x^{i-1}
and integrate them at
0<x<x_{\pi}
to obtain:

\int_{0}^{x_{\pi}}x^{n-1}g_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{N}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{1-n}{n}\Gamma_{n}^{N}(Q^{2})~,

(48)

where
\Gamma_{n}^{N}
is defined in Eq.(
36
). Therefore, we may use the available information of
g_{1}
to evaluate the twist-two contribution to
d_{2}
.

Again, we discuss the integral at large and small
Q^{2}
separately. For
Q^{2}>Q_{0}^{2}
, we keep the leading term,

d_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{>}\approx-\frac{4}{3\mathring{g}_{A}}\int_{Q_{0}^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{2}}\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\frac{M^{2}}{Q^{2}}\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}(Q^{2})~,

(49)

where we have used Eq.(
48
) and set
r\rightarrow 1
. The
Q^{2}
dependence of the
W
propagator can also be safely neglected as the integral converges.
There is no simple sum rule for
\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}
at large
Q^{2}
, but we may adopt a naïve valence quark picture that assumes each valence quark carries 1/3 of the nucleon’s momentum. This gives:

g_{1}^{p-n}(x)\approx\frac{|\mathring{g}_{A}|}{6}\delta\left(x-1/3\right)~,\quad\text{large $Q^{2}$}

(50)

which automatically reproduces the free polarized Bjorken sum rule. This naïve picture predicts
\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}\approx 0.11\Gamma_{1}^{p-n}
, which we may check against the experimental data: At
Q^{2}\approx 3.4
GeV
2
, Refs.
Fersch et al. 2017
;
Guler et al. 2015
give
\Gamma_{1}^{p-n}\approx 0.1558
,
\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}\approx 0.0128
, i.e.
\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}\approx 0.08\Gamma_{1}^{p-n}
. So our naïve picture overestimates the size of
\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}
by some 30%, an acceptable uncertainty given our precision goal. With the above, we obtain:

d_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{>}\approx\frac{2}{81}\int_{Q_{0}^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{2}}\frac{M^{2}}{Q^{2}}=\frac{2M^{2}}{81Q_{0}^{2}}=0.010(3)~.

(51)

Next we turn to the small-
Q^{2}
region where accounting for the leading twist may not be sufficient.
As before, the leading twist contribution

\displaystyle d_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{<}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\mathring{g}_{A}}\int_{0}^{Q_{0}^{2}}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{2}}\int_{0}^{x_{\pi}}\frac{dx}{(1+r)^{2}}\frac{4M^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}\left[g_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{p}(x,Q^{2})-g_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{n}(x,Q^{2})\right]~,

(52)

is reconstructed by using the WW relation and the
x
-integral related to measured moments of
g_{1}
by performing a two parameter fit of the kinematical function
f^{\prime}(x,Q^{2})
for fixed
Q^{2}
(since the first moment is removed by the BC sum rule, a two-parameter fit is already sufficient for our precision goal).

f^{\prime}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{1}{(1+r)^{2}}\frac{4M^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}=b^{\prime}(Q^{2})x^{2}+c^{\prime}(Q^{2})x^{4}+\dots~,\quad 0<x<x_{\pi}~.

(53)

Using Eq.(
48
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{x_{\pi}}\frac{dx}{(1+r)^{2}}\frac{4M^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}\left[g_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{p}(x,Q^{2})-g_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{n}(x,Q^{2})\right]\approx-\frac{2}{3}b^{\prime}(Q^{2})\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}(Q^{2})-\frac{4}{5}c^{\prime}(Q^{2})\Gamma_{5}^{p-n}(Q^{2})~.

(54)

With
\Gamma_{i}^{p-n}
taken from Refs.
Fersch et al. 2017
;
Guler et al. 2015
, this gives,

d_{2,\mathrm{tw}2}^{<}\approx-\frac{2}{\mathring{g}_{A}}\int_{0}^{Q_{0}^{2}}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{2}}\left[\frac{2}{3}b^{\prime}(Q^{2})\Gamma_{3}^{p-n}(Q^{2})+\frac{4}{5}c^{\prime}(Q^{2})\Gamma_{5}^{p-n}(Q^{2})\right]=0.01(1)~,

(55)

where we assigned a conservative 100% uncertainty to the entire contribution.

To quantify higher twist contributions we recall the definition of the “color polarizability”
Shuryak and Vainshtein 1982
;
Jaffe 1990

{\mathbf{d}_{2}(Q^{2})=3\int_{0}^{1}dxx^{2}[g_{2}(x,Q^{2})-g_{2,\mathrm{tw2}}(x,Q^{2})],}

(56)

of which we only consider the inelastic part
\bar{\mathbf{d}}_{2}(Q^{2})
coming from the interval
0\leq x\leq x_{\pi}

Alarcón et al. 2020
since the elastic part is already taken into account.
In terms of this polarizability and neglecting higher moments, we obtain for the contribution of twist-three and higher,

\displaystyle d_{2,\mathrm{tw}3+}^{<}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{2M^{2}}{3\mathring{g}_{A}}\int_{0}^{Q_{0}^{2}}\frac{dQ^{2}}{Q^{4}}[\bar{\mathbf{d}}_{2}^{p}(Q^{2})-\bar{\mathbf{d}}_{2}^{n}(Q^{2})]}~.

(57)

For numerical estimates, we rely on the recent analysis of generalized spin polarizabilities of the nucleon in baryon chiral effective theory
Alarcón et al. 2020
.
We obtain, assigning a conservative 100% uncertainty,

\displaystyle d_{2,\mathrm{tw}3+}^{<}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.03(3)~.

(58)

Combining the various pieces we finally arrive at

d_{2}=0.05(3)

(59)

as our estimate of the total inelastic contribution from
g_{2}^{(0)}
. We observe that it is two orders of magnitude smaller than
d_{1}
, following the same hierarchy as in
d_{B}
.

The inelastic contribution in our DR analysis then reads
d_{1}+d_{2}=2.19(4)_{\mathrm{data}}(1)_{\mathrm{HT}}(3)_{g_{2}}
. This is to be compared with
2.31(9)
from Refs.
Hayen 2021a
;
Hayen 2021b
, and we see that the two do not quite agree within error bars. While being identical in the large-
Q^{2}
treatment, their estimation of the low-
Q^{2}
contribution is largely model-based, raising questions about the reliability of the uncertainty. In contrast, in our treatment the low-
Q^{2}
contribution is completely fixed by experimental data without any further assumption, apart from the small
g_{2}
correction for which only few assumption were made, which will become testable as soon as new, higher-quality low-
Q^{2}
data for
g_{2}
will become available.

VII
Final discussions

Collecting all the results from Sec.
V
and
VI
gives:

\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}=3.96(1)_{\mathrm{FF}}(5)_{\mathrm{data}}(1)_{\mathrm{HT}}(3)_{g_{2}}\times 10^{-3}=3.96(6)\times 10^{-3}~,

(60)

where the uncertainties come from the elastic form factors, the low-
Q^{2}

g_{1}
data, the HT-correction to
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, respectively. We compare this to our recent update of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}
using indirect lattice inputs:
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}=3.83(11)\times 10^{-3}

Seng et al. 2020a
. These two numbers are very close to each other, and in fact their difference is consistent with zero:

\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}-\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}=0.13(11)_{V}(6)_{A}\times 10^{-3}~.

(61)

Using Eqs.(
11
), (
61
) and the PDG average
\lambda=-1.2756(13)

Zyla et al. 2020
, we obtain:

\mathring{g}_{A}=-1.2754(13)_{\mathrm{exp}}(2)_{\mathrm{RC}}~,

(62)

which is consistent with the result from the current best lattice QCD determination.
Our result indicates that there is no practical distinction between
\lambda
and
\mathring{g}_{A}
, unless the experimental precision of the former and the lattice precision of the latter have reached
3\times 10^{-4}
or better.

We wrap up with some discussions of the future prospects. Within the same DR framework, a much better precision is achieved for
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
than for
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}
thanks to the existence of high-quality data of the structure function
g_{1}
at
Q^{2}<2
GeV
2
. On the other hand, the precision of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}
is limited by the low-quality data of the structure function
F_{3}
from neutrino (antineutrino)-nucleus scattering experiments in the 80s
Bolognese et al. 1983
;
Allasia et al. 1985
. Better-quality data may come from the Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) in the next decade
Acciarri et al. 2016
;
Alvarez-Ruso et al. 2018
.

It was pointed out that a direct lattice QCD calculation of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}
is a promising way to proceed at the present stage
Seng and Meißner 2019
. Several exploratory calculations of mesonic
\gamma W
-box diagrams have shown great success
Feng et al. 2020
;
Ma et al. 2021
and the same technology is directly applicable to nucleon.
At present, no such direct calculation on the nucleon is available yet. A more involved comparison of the DR result for
\Box_{\gamma W}^{V}
with the lattice computation of the respective quantity on the pion, amended with further phenomenological ingredients shows a nearly perfect agreement
Seng et al. 2020a
. Even with this reassuring agreement, it is not unthinkable of that a direct lattice calculation could still disagree with the phenomenological, DR-based evaluation.
Examples of such an unexpected disagreement are the pion-nucleon sigma term
\sigma_{\pi N}
(see Ref.
Aoki et al. 2019
and references therein) and, more recently, the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution to
g_{\mu}-2

Borsanyi et al. 2020
;
Aoyama et al. 2020
. They show that even carefully-performed first-principles calculations or fully data-driven analysis may still contain unknown, previously unanticipated systematic effects which may seriously affect the implications of the corresponding precision experiments. Given these precedents, it is always useful to cross-check the lattice calculations with alternative methods.
Our new result of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
is perfectly up to this task as it is a solid phenomenological determination with the uncertainty very well under control.

Further effort from the DR side should be dedicated to RCs to the GT strength in nuclear mirror decays where a recent study
Hayen and Severijns 2019
revealed inconsistencies in the previous analyses. Removing these inconsistencies led to a better agreement for the
V_{ud}
extracted across mirror and superallowed nuclear decays, as well as neutron decay. However, Ref.
Hayen and Severijns 2019
only partially accounted for the
\gamma W
-box contribution. The dispersion formulation of the
\Box_{\gamma W}^{A}
correction developed in this work can be directly applied to mirror systems.
Following Refs.
Seng et al. 2019
;
Gorchtein 2019
, nuclear modifications of the universal free-neutron
\gamma W
-box correction can be computed, and we defer this task to future work.

Appendix A
Alternative derivation of Eq.(
35
)

In this Appendix we outline a derivation of Eq.(
35
), namely the elastic contribution in the diagrammatic representation, directly from the tensor
T^{\gamma W}_{\mu\nu}
without going through its invariant amplitudes. It involves some interesting tricks to deal with fermionic spinors and thus is worthwhile to be displayed for pedagogical purposes.

We start by computing
T^{\gamma W}_{\mu\nu}
from the direct (D) and crossed (C) pole diagrams in Fig.
3
. Using the elastic form factors as effective vertices, we obtain:

T_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W,B}=\frac{\bar{u}_{s}(p)\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{s}(p)}{2M\nu-Q^{2}}+\frac{\bar{u}_{s}(p)\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{C}}u_{s}(p)}{-2M\nu-Q^{2}}

(63)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[F_{1}^{p}\gamma_{\mu}-\frac{iF_{2}^{p}}{2M}\sigma_{\mu\alpha}q^{\alpha}\right]i\left[\not{p}+\not{q}+M\right]\left[F_{1}^{W}\gamma_{\nu}+\frac{iF_{2}^{W}}{2M}\sigma_{\nu\beta}q^{\beta}+G_{A}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{5}-\frac{G_{P}}{2M}\gamma_{5}q_{\nu}\right]

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[F_{1}^{W}\gamma_{\nu}+\frac{iF_{2}^{W}}{2M}\sigma_{\nu\beta}q^{\beta}+G_{A}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{5}-\frac{G_{P}}{2M}\gamma_{5}q_{\nu}\right]i\left[\not{p}-\not{q}+M\right]\left[F_{1}^{n}\gamma_{\mu}-\frac{iF_{2}^{n}}{2M}\sigma_{\mu\alpha}q^{\alpha}\right]

(64)

are matrices in the Dirac space.

We can get rid of the nucleon spinors
\bar{u}_{s}(p)
,
u_{s}(p)

in the expression of
T_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W,B}
using the
following trick. First, we recall that any Dirac structure
\Gamma

can be decomposed in terms of standard Dirac basis
1
,
\gamma_{5}
,

\gamma^{\alpha}
,
\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma_{5}
,
\sigma^{\alpha\beta}

using the following identity:

\Gamma=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}[\Gamma]+\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}[\gamma_{5}\Gamma]\gamma_{5}+\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}[\gamma_{\alpha}\Gamma]\gamma^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}[\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\Gamma]\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma_{5}+\frac{1}{8}\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\Gamma]\sigma^{\alpha\beta}.

(65)

Next, we have the following identities when a Dirac basis is sandwiched between
\bar{u}_{s}(p)
and
u_{s}(p)
:

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{s}(p)u_{s}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2M

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{s}(p)\gamma_{5}u_{s}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{s}(p)\gamma^{\alpha}u_{s}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{s}(p)\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma_{5}u_{s}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2S^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{s}(p)\sigma^{\alpha\beta}u_{s}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{M}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\rho\sigma}p_{\rho}S_{\sigma}~.

(66)

Combining Eqs.(
65
) and (
66
), we obtain:

\bar{u}_{s}(p)\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}u_{s}(p)=\frac{M}{2}\mathrm{Tr}[\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}]+\frac{p^{\alpha}}{2}\mathrm{Tr}[\gamma_{\alpha}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}]-\frac{S^{\alpha}}{2}\mathrm{Tr}[\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}]+\frac{1}{4M}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\rho\sigma}p_{\rho}S_{\sigma}\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}],

(67)

where
i=\mathrm{D,C}.
The right hand side is free from the
nucleon spinors. The trace of the Dirac matrices can be performed using various

Mathematica
packages, so we do not display
the explicit results here.

We then plug our expression of
T_{\mu\nu}^{\gamma W,B}
, now free from nucleon spinors, into Eq.(
16
) and use Eq.(
19
) to simplify the integrals. Finally, we perform the Wick rotation using Eq.(
26
) and integrate out the variable
u
analytically. This brings us exactly to Eq.(
35
).

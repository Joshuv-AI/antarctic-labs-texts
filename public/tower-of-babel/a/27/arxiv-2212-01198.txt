Club Stationary Reflection and other Combinatorial Principles at ℵ ω + 2 \aleph_{\omega+2} Thanks: Š. Stejskalová was supported by FWF/GAČR grant Compactness principles and combinatorics (19-29633L).
Thomas Gilton, Šárka Stejskalová

Abstract
. In this paper we continue the study in [ 11 ] of compactness and incompactness principles at double successors, focusing here on the case of double successors of singulars of countable cofinality. We obtain models which satisfy the tree property and club stationary reflection at these double successors. Moreover, we can additionally obtain either approachability or its failure. We also show how to obtain our results on ℵ ω + 2 \aleph_{\omega+2} by incorporating collapses; particularly relevant for these circumstances is a new indestructibility theorem of ours showing that posets satisfying certain linked assumptions preserve club stationary reflection.

1.
Introduction

A long-standing and fruitful line of research in contemporary set theory is concerned with the tension between two classes of combinatorial principles (see, for example,
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
2
]
). The first of these classes we refer to as compactness and reflection principles, and examples of principles in this class are the tree property and stationary reflection. In tension with this first class, we have the class of incompactness and non-reflection principles which are typified by square principles and their consequences. Since principles in the latter class often imply the failure of principles in the other, it is of interest when principles from these classes are jointly consistent.

A central character in this line of research is the so-called square principle
\Box_{\mu}
, for a cardinal
\mu
, which Jensen (
[
22
]
) first showed is consistent with
\sf ZFC
. While we won’t need the definition here,
\Box_{\mu}
intuitively asserts the existence of a coherent system of clubs which singularize all ordinals in the interval
[\mu,\mu^{+})
. A
\Box_{\mu}
sequence is frequently used to continue through limit stages of inductive constructions of length
\mu^{+}
.

A
\Box_{\mu}
sequence has a deep impact on the combinatorics of
\mu^{+}
. For instance, it implies that the approachability property holds at
\mu^{+}
, but that the tree property and the stationary reflection property both fail at
\mu^{+}
(these principles will all be defined later). In
[
6
]
, Cummings, Friedman, Magidor, Rinot, and Sinapova showed that these three consequences of
\Box_{\mu}
are mutually independent in the sense that all eight of their Boolean combinations are consistent, usually from large cardinals. They obtain their results in the case when
\mu
is itself a successor, say
\kappa^{+}
(and thus their results are on the double successor of
\kappa
), both when
\kappa
is regular and when
\kappa
is singular of cofinality
\omega
. Moreover, they showed that they can make
\kappa
become
\aleph_{\omega}
, the first singular cardinal.

Recent results in this vein (
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
) have built upon the work of
[
6
]
. In addition, in
[
11
]
, the authors (together with Levine) continued the work in
[
6
]
, showing, in particular, that one can obtain any Boolean combination of approachability and the tree property at
\kappa^{++}
together with club stationary reflection at
\kappa^{++}
. These results were obtained in the case when
\kappa
is regular. (Note that the Boolean combination of club stationary reflection, the approachability property, and the failure of the tree property already holds in Magidor’s model
[
26
]
.)

In this paper, we take the next step and show how to obtain, when
\kappa
is singular of cofinality
\omega
, a variety of combinatorics at
\kappa^{++}
simultaneously with club stationary reflection at
\kappa^{++}
. We also show how to make
\kappa
become
\aleph_{\omega}
. More precisely, we prove the following theorem (the notation will be defined later):

Theorem 1.1
.

Assuming the existence of a supercompact cardinal
\kappa
and a weakly compact cardinal
\lambda>\kappa
, there are models witnessing each of the following:

(1)

\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
,
\lambda=\kappa^{++}
, and
\mathsf{CSR}(\lambda)\wedge{\sf AP}(\lambda)\wedge\sf TP(\lambda)
;

(2)

\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
,
\lambda=\kappa^{++}
, and
\mathsf{CSR}(\lambda)\wedge\neg{\sf AP}(\lambda)\wedge\sf TP(\lambda)
.

Moreover, we can obtain (1) and (2) with
\kappa=\aleph_{\omega}
.

Our results combine a variety of techniques such as Mitchell-style forcings, club adding iterations, guessing functions from weakly compact cardinals, Woodin’s fast function forcing and Prikry-type forcings (both with and without collapses). In more detail, our paper is organized as follows:

In Section
2
, we review the preliminaries. We record the definitions of almost all of the forcings which we use throughout the paper, and we review their central features.

In Section
3
, we state a number of known indestructibility results which we will use liberally in our paper. We also include a new indestructibility result showing that a
\kappa^{+}
-c.c.,
\kappa^{+}
-linked poset (in particular, a poset which is
\kappa
-linked) preserves club stationary reflection at
\kappa^{++}
. This theorem simplifies and generalizes Theorem 4.12(iii) of
[
21
]
and shows that a variety of Prikry-type posets (including many that add collapses) preserve club stationary reflection at
\kappa^{++}
. We also review the types of guessing functions from weakly compact cardinals which we use throughout the paper. In particular, we tweak Hamkins’ construction (
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
) for obtaining weakly compact Laver diamonds in order to suit some of our later purposes. Finally, we will review the results from
[
11
]
which we will need later in the paper.

In Section
4
, we show how to obtain guiding generics which are appropriate for the collapses in the Prikry forcing. There are a variety of different cases in which we need these guiding generics, and we build off of the work of
[
16
]
and
[
31
]
to show how to tailor the preparatory forcings in each of the desired circumstances. We then show that these guiding generics are preserved by forcing with the club adding posets of interest.

Section

which assume that
\lambda

2.1.
Combinatorial Principles

In this subsection, we review the definitions of the combinatorial principles which we are studying in this paper. We begin with the varieties of stationary reflection of interest. Recall that if
S\subseteq\kappa^{+}

In this subsection, we review the two standard varieties of Prikry forcing which we use to singularize

Definition 2.22
.

The poset

consists of all pairs

so that

(1)

is a finite, increasing sequence of inaccessibles below
\kappa
;

(2)

and

.

We say

(1)

s\sqsubseteq t
, and
\operatorname{ran}(t\backslash s)\subseteq A

(2)

.

We say that

is a

, denoted
(t,B)\leq^{*}(s,A)
, if

and

.

\mathrm{Prk}(U)
is
\kappa^{+}
-Knaster since there are
\kappa
-many possible stems and any two conditions with the same stem are compatible.
\mathrm{Prk}(U)
also satisfies the following significant property, known as the

: if

is a sentence in the forcing language of

and

a condition, then there exists a direct extension

so that

decides

. Since the direct extension order

is
\kappa
-closed, the Prikry property implies that no bounded subsets of

are added. Combining all of this, we see that all cardinals are preserved and

becomes singular of cofinality

.

We next define a variation of the above which turns
\kappa
into

. Roughly, the forcing will simultaneously add an
\omega
-sequence cofinal in
\kappa
and collapse all but finitely-many cardinals between the points on the
\omega
-sequence. It is important that the two tasks (adding an

-sequence and collapsing) are performed at the same time; it is known that performing them one-by-one may lead to collapsing (see
[
19
]
for more details). Prikry forcing with collapses was introduced in
[
13
]
, and we follow their presentation.

In order to make this task work, we need a way of constraining the collapses in order to prove the Prikry lemma. We use a collapse constraining function. This function will delimit the possibilities for new collapses added for additional Prikry points, and we ensure that these constraining functions are well-behaved by requiring that their

-equivalence classes come from a

, which we now define.

Definition 2.23
.

Fix

. A

-guiding generic
is a filter

which is an element of
V
and which is

-generic over the poset

.

Next up is the definition of the Prikry-type forcing:

The direct extension ordering is defined in the natural way:

below

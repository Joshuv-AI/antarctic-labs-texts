Introduction and Main results

1.1
Introduction

In this work, we consider a
d
-dimensional measure-valued Markov process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, called super-Brownian motion (henceforth SBM). For convenience of the reader, we give a brief introduction to the SBM and some pertinent results needed in this article.

To characterize the SBM, we first introduce some notations. Let
p>d
be a constant. Define
\phi_{p}(x):=(1+|x|^{2})^{-p/2},~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Denote by

M_{p}:=\left\{\mu~\text{is a locally finite measure on}~\mathbb{R}^{d}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{p}(x)\mu(dx)<\infty\right\}

the space of
p
-tempered measures; see Etheridge
[
5
, p23]
. We equip
M_{p}
with the topology such that
\mu_{n}
converges to
\mu
in
M_{p}
if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mu_{n}(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mu(dx),~\forall f\in\{g+\alpha\phi_{p}:\alpha\in\mathbb{R},g\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\},

where
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
stands for the class of continuous functions of compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In this paper, for a measure
\mu
, we always use
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}
to denote the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
, the probability measure under which the SBM has initial value
X_{0}=\mu
. Let
\psi
be a function of the form

\psi(u)=au+bu^{2}+\int_{(0,\infty)}\left(e^{-ru}-1+ru\right)n(dr),~u\geq 0,

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\geq 0
and
n
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(0,\infty)
such that
\int_{(0,\infty)}(r\wedge r^{2})n(dr)<\infty
.

The SBM with initial value
\mu\in M_{p}
and branching mechanism
\psi
is a measure-valued process, whose transition probabilities are characterized through their Laplace transforms. For any
\mu\in M_{p}
and nonnegative continuous function
\phi
satisfying
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\phi(x)}{\phi_{p}(x)}<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\phi>}\right]=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\mu(dx)},

(1.1)

where
<X_{t},\phi>:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)X_{t}(dx)
and
u(t,x)
is the unique positive solution to the following nonlinear partial differential equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)-\psi(u(t,x)),\cr u(0,x)=\phi(x).\end{cases}

In above,
\Delta u(t,x):=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\frac{\partial^{2}u(t,x)}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
is the Laplace operator.
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is called a supercritical (critical, subcritical) SBM if
a<0~(=0,>0).
Our works only consider a typical critical branching mechanism, called binary branching, which is given by

\psi(u)=u^{2}.

In this case, the partial differential equation above is reduced to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)-u^{2}(t,x),\cr u(0,x)=\phi(x).\end{cases}

(1.2)

Let
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion. Note that the partial differential equation above is equivalent to the integral equation:

\displaystyle u(t,x)+\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u^{2}(t-s,W_{s})\right]ds=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\phi(W_{t})\right],

(1.3)

where for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{E}_{x}
stands for the expectation with respect to the probability
\mathbb{P}_{x}
, the probability measure under which
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
starts from
x
. Furthermore, one can also use the martingale problem to characterize the SBM; see Perkins
[
16
, p159]
. We refer the reader to Etheridge
[
5
]
, Perkins
[
16
]
, Le Gall

[
11
]
and Li
[
13
]
for a more detailed overview to SBM.

In our work, we consider the SBM starts from the Poisson random measure whose intensity is the Lebeguse measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
\text{PRM}(\lambda)
for short). Namely, for any Borel measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\text{PRM}(\lambda)}(X_{0}(A)=k)=\frac{\lambda^{k}(A)}{k!}e^{-\lambda(A)},~k\geq 0.

For ease of notation, we write
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{\text{PRM}(\lambda)}
.

For
u>0
, let
B(u):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<u\}
be the
d
-dimensional ball with radius
u
and center at the origin. Write

R_{t}:=\sup\{u>0:X_{t}(B(u))=0\},

with the convention
\sup\emptyset=0
. In other words,
R_{t}
is the radius of the largest ball around the origin which does not contain any mass at time
t
and
B(R(t))
is the largest empty ball.

This paper aims at showing that after suitable renormalization,
R_{t}
converges in distribution to some non-degenerate limit as
t\to\infty
. We note that the renormalization scale depends on the dimension.

The research on the empty ball was first conducted by Révész
[
19
]
for the critical branching Wiener process model started from
\text{PRM}(\lambda)
. This model, denoted by
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, is defined as follows. At time
0
, there exist infinite many particles distributed according to
\text{PRM}(\lambda)
. Then, these particles move independently according to the standard normal distribution in unit time. Afterwards, each particle produces children independently according to the Bernoulli distribution
\xi
in a instant, where
\mathbb{P}(\xi=0)=\mathbb{P}(\xi=1)=1/2
. This forms a random measure at time
1
, denoted by
Z_{1}
. Similarly, for
n\geq 2
, each particle at time
n-1
, starting from where its parent die, executes a displacement according to the standard normal distribution during time
n-1
to time
n
and afterwards executes a reproduction instantly according to
\xi
. This forms a random measure at time
n
, denoted by
Z_{n}
.

Let

\displaystyle R(n):=\sup\{u>0:Z_{n}(B(u))=0\}.

(1.4)

Révész
[
19
]
proved that
R(n)/n
converges in distribution to an exponential distribution for the case
d=1
. For
d\geq 2
, he presented following two conjectures (see Révész
[
19
, Conjecture 1]
):

(i) If
d=2
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)=e^{-F_{2}(r)}\in(0,1),

where
F_{2}(r)
satisfies

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{F_{2}(r)}{\pi r^{2}}=1;

(ii) If
d\geq 3
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R(n)\geq r)=e^{-F_{d}(r)}\in(0,1),

(1.5)

where
F_{d}(r)
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{d}(r)}{C_{d}r^{d-2}}=1,

(1.6)

and
\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d}/\big[\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}\big]^{(d-2)/d}=1
(
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function). Later, Hu
[
8
]
partially confirmed Révész’s conjecture for
d\geq 3
by showing that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R(n)\geq r)
exists in
(0,1)
. But (
1.6
) remains unproven.

Our work gives complete weak convergence results of
R_{t}
, and theses results are consistent with Révész’s conjectures. In
d=1
, we use the modulus of continuity for SBM and Markov property of historical SBM to show that
R_{t}/t
converges in law. For
d=2
, by using the scaling property of SBM, we obtain
R_{t}/\sqrt{t}
converges in law. For
d\geq 3
, we use the mild solution of the PDE (
1.2
) and Feynman-Kac formula to prove that
R_{t}
converges in law. Moreover, in our recent study, we believe that our results for the SBM model can facilitate us to solve Révész’s conjectures, especially in the case of
d=2
.

A relevant work to our problem is Zhou
[
21
]
. Let
\{K_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a
d
-dimensional
(1+\beta)
-SBM
and
\tau:=\sup\{t\geq 0:K_{t}(B(g(t)))>0\}
be the local extinction time, where
\beta\in(0,1]
and
g(t)\geq 0
is a nondecreasing and right continuous function on
[0,\infty)
. Assume that
d\beta<2
. Zhou proved that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(\tau<\infty)=\begin{cases}1,~\text{if}\int^{\infty}_{1}g^{d}(y)y^{-(1+\frac{1}{\beta})}dy<\infty;\cr 0,~\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This result implies that in our setting, if
d=1
and
X_{0}=\lambda
, then the leading order of
R_{t}
is
t
as
t\to\infty
. Conversely, by studying
R_{t}
in the case of
d\geq 2
, one can also obtain some results of the local extinction time of
(1+\beta)
-SBM in the case of
d\beta\geq 2
.

We also mention that in the last few decades, limit theory of SBMs concerning its local behaviours has been studied intensively. For example, Iscoe
[
9
]
studied the decay rate of the hitting probability
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\exists t>0,X_{t}(B(r))>0)
as
|x|\to\infty
. Dawson et al.
[
2
]
considered decay rates of the probabilities

\mathbb{P}_{m}(X_{t}(B(x,r))>0),~\mathbb{P}_{m}(\exists t\geq\delta~\text{s.t.}~X_{t}(B(x,r))>0)

and

\mathbb{P}_{m}(\exists t\geq 0~\text{s.t.}~X_{t}(B(x,r))>0)~\text{as}~r\to 0,

where
\delta,~r>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
B(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y-x|\leq r\}
and
m
is a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Namely, the probabilities above consider the SBM hits an arbitrarily small ball. Note that

\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq r)=1-\mathbb{P}(X_{t}(B(r))>0).

Their works can also give some results for
R_{t}
. However, since they assumed
X_{0}=m
is a finite measure, the SBM will die out in finite time. This implies
R_{t}=\infty
for large
t
. Therefore, to make this question meaningful, we consider the SBM starts from an infinite measure
\text{PRM}(\lambda)
. For the maximum of supercritical SBM, see Kyprianou et al.
[
10
]
and Pinsky
[
18
]
. Moreover, Ren et al.
[
17
]
and Engländer
[
4
]
considered the corresponding large deviation probabilities. In addition, Mueller et al.
[
15
]
investigated the left tail probability of the density of the SBM. For the local times of SBM, see Sugitani
[
20
]
, Hong
[
7
,
6
]
and Dawson et al.
[
3
]
and the references therein.

1.2
Main Results

We first consider the
1
-dimensional super Brownian motion.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
d=1
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{t}\geq r\right)=e^{-2r}.

Intuitively, the reason for an empty ball
B(u)
(
u>0
and may depend on
t
) to form is that an SBM starting away from
B(u)
is hard to reach
B(u)
, while the SBM starting from the set
B(u)
of finite measure will die out in finite time. Since in higher dimensions there are more "particles" in
B(u)
, the SBM starting from
B(u)
will take a longer time to die out. Thus, in higher dimensions,
R_{t}
is smaller.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
d=2
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\geq r\right)=e^{-A_{2}(r)}\in(0,1),

where
A_{2}(r):=-\log\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)
satisfying

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{A_{2}(r)}{\pi r^{2}}=1.

Remark 1.1
.

Recall that
R(n)
is defined in (
1.4
). Assume that
d=2
. In
[
19
, Theorem 2]
, Révész proved that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
0<c(\varepsilon)<C(\varepsilon)<\infty
such that

c(\varepsilon)e^{-2\pi(1+\varepsilon)r^{2}}<\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R(n)}{\sqrt{n}}>r\right)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R(n)}{\sqrt{n}}>r\right)\leq C(\varepsilon)e^{-2\pi(1-\varepsilon)r^{2}}.

From above two theorems, one can see that
R_{t}\to\infty
for
1\leq d<3
. This is because the SBM suffers local extinction in low dimensions. However, since in high dimensions (
d\geq 3
), the SBM is persistent (see
[
5
, p49]
), we don’t need any renormalization for
R_{t}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
d\geq 3
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq r)=e^{-\kappa_{d}r^{d-2}},

where
\kappa_{d}:=-\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(1))=0)dx\in(0,\infty)
.

Remark 1.2
.

For
d\geq 3
, Révész
[
19
, Theorem 3]
proved that there exist constants
0<c_{d}<C_{d}<\infty
such that

e^{-c_{d}r^{d-2}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R(n)>r)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R(n)>r)\leq e^{-C_{d}r^{d-2}}.

Later, Hu
[
8
]
proved that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R(n)>r)
exists. However, Hu did not give any explicit expression of the limit.

Remark 1.3
.

Although in all theorems above we consider
X_{0}=\text{PRM}(\lambda)
, it is still true if
X_{0}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
T>0
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(R_{t}/t\geq r\right)
is decreasing (w.r.t.
t
) when
d=1
and
t\geq T
;
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(R_{t}/\sqrt{t}\geq r\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(R_{1}\geq r\right)~\text{for}~d=2~\text{and}~t>0;

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(R_{t}\geq r\right)
is increasing when
d\geq 3
and
t\geq T
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect some properties of the total mass process
\{X_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}_{t\geq 0}
and the historical super-Brownian motion. In Section
3
, we use the modulus of continuity for historical SBM to prove that for
d=1
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))=0\right)=e^{-2r}.

Then, by (
1.3
), we argue that, under
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\text{PRM}(\lambda)}
,
R_{t}/t
have the same limit (in the sense of convergence in distribution). Theorem
1.1
is then proved. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.2
by the scaling property of the
2
-dimensional SBM. We prove Theorem
1.3
in Section
5
. The idea is to use the mild solution of the partial differential equation (
1.2
) to establish the existence of the limit. Then, using the second moment method and
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]
, we conclude that the limit is non-degenerate.

2
Preliminaries

The total mass process
\{X_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}_{t\geq 0}
with initial measure
m\in M_{p}
is the so-called continuous state branching process with initial value
m(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We refer the reader to
[
12
]
for a more detailed overview to it. The following lemma considers the Laplace transform and extinction probability of the continuous state branching process, which can be found in
[
5
, p22]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\theta>0
,
d\geq 1
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{m}\left[e^{-\theta X_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]=e^{-\frac{\theta m(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{1+\theta t}}

and

\mathbb{P}_{m}(X_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=0)=e^{-\frac{m(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{t}},

where by convention, if
m(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}_{m}\left[e^{-\theta X_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]=0.

In the remaining of this section, we always assume
d=1
. We first give a brief introduction to the historical SBM. For an explicit definition and a more elaborated discussion on it, we refer the reader to
[
16
, p187]
. Let
D(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the space of c
\grave{\text{a}}
dl
\grave{\text{a}}
g paths from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}
with the Skorokhod topology. For
y_{\cdot}\in D(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
t\geq 0
, let
y^{t}_{\cdot}=y_{\cdot\wedge t}
. Define the stopped function space

\hat{E}:=\{(t,y^{t}_{\cdot}):t\geq 0,~y_{\cdot}\in D(\mathbb{R}_{+})\}

with the subspace topology it inherits from
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times D(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then
\hat{E}
is a Polish space. Define an
\hat{E}
-valued process
\{\hat{W}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
:

\hat{W}_{t}:=(t,W_{\cdot\wedge t}).

Let
D(\mathbb{R}_{+},\hat{E})
be the space of c
\grave{\text{a}}
dl
\grave{\text{a}}
g paths from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\hat{E}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(D(\mathbb{R}_{+},\hat{E}))
, we define

\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\hat{W}_{\cdot}\in A):=\mathbb{P}_{x}((\cdot,W^{\cdot})\in A).

Then
(\{\hat{W}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}})
is an
\hat{E}
-valued Borel strong Markov process. For a space
E
, we use
M_{F}(E)
to denote the space of finite measures on
E
. Thus, for
m\in M_{F}(\mathbb{R})
, we can construct an
M_{F}(\hat{E})
-valued superprocess
\{Y_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with spatial motion
\{\hat{W}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, binary branching and initial value
m
(by identifying
\mathbb{R}=\{(0,y^{0}_{\cdot}):y_{\cdot}\in D(\mathbb{R}_{+})\}\subset\hat{E}
, so
M_{F}(\mathbb{R})\subset M_{F}(\hat{E})
). Let
\mathbb{Q}_{m}
be the corresponding probability measure. The historical SBM
\{H_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
(with respect to the original SBM
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with initial value
m
) is defined by

H_{t}(A):=Y_{t}(\Pi^{-1}(A)),A\subset D_{t}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

where
D_{t}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=\{~y_{\cdot}\in D(\mathbb{R}_{+}):y_{\cdot}=y^{t}_{{\cdot}}\}
and
\Pi((t,y^{t}_{\cdot}))=y^{t}_{\cdot}
is the projection map from
\hat{E}
to
D(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Moreover,
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
can be obtained through

X_{t}(A):=H_{t}(\{y_{\cdot}\in D_{t}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):y_{t}\in A\})~\text{for}~A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}).

Let
S(H_{t})
be the closed support of the random measure
H_{t}
and
C(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the space of continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}
. The following lemma gives a uniform modulus of continuity for all the paths in
S(H_{t}),~t\geq 0
; see
[
16
, p195]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
c>2
be a constant. There exists a random variable
\Delta
such that
\mathbb{Q}_{m}
almost surely, for all
t\geq 0
,

S(H_{t})\subset\left\{y_{\cdot}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+}):|y_{r}-y_{s}|\leq c|(r-s)\log(r-s)|^{1/2},\forall r,s>0,|r-s|\leq\Delta\right\}.

Moreover, there are constants
\rho>0
depending only on
c
and
\kappa>0
depending only on
d,~c
such that

\mathbb{Q}_{m}(\Delta\leq r)\leq\kappa m(1)r^{\rho}~\text{for}~r\in[0,1].

For
t\geq 0
, define

M^{t}_{F}(D(\mathbb{R}_{+})):=\left\{\mu\in M_{F}(D(\mathbb{R}_{+})):\mu\big(\{y_{\cdot}\in D(\mathbb{R}_{+}):y^{t}_{\cdot}\neq y_{\cdot}\}\big)=0\right\}

In fact,
\{H_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an inhomogeneous Borel strong Markov process. Furthermore,
H_{t}\in M^{t}_{F}(D(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
. For
\tau\geq 0
and
\mu\in M^{\tau}_{F}(D(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, write

\mathbb{Q}_{\tau,\mu}(H_{\tau+t}\in A):=\mathbb{Q}_{m}(H_{\tau+t}\in A|H_{\tau}=\mu)
for measurable set
A\subset M^{\tau+t}_{F}(D(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
. In other worlds,
under
\mathbb{Q}_{\tau,\mu}
, the historical SBM starts at time
\tau
with its initial value
\mu
. The following lemma is borrowed from
[
16
, p194]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
A
is a Borel subset of
D(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and
\mu\in M^{\tau}_{F}(D(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, then for any

,

\mathbb{\mathbb{Q}}_{\tau,\mu}(H_{s}(\{y_{\cdot}\in D(\mathbb{R}_{+}):y^{\tau}_{\cdot}\in A\})=0,~\forall s\geq t)=e^{-\frac{2\mu(A)}{t-\tau}}.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

This section is devoted to prove Theorem
1.1
. We first present a proposition concerning the empty ball of SBM starting from Lebesgue measure
\lambda
. The proof is mainly inspired by Zhou
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
. As usual, for a measure
\mu
on some space
E
, we write
\mu(1):=\mu(E)
for convenience.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
d=1
, then for any
r>0
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))=0\right)=e^{-2r}.

Proof.

For a Borel measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\lambda|A:=\lambda(\cdot\cap A)
the Lebesgue measure restricted to
A
. Fix
\delta\in(1/2,1)
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right)+\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B^{c}(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right).

(3.1)

For the first term on the r.h.s. of (
3.1
), by Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(1)>0\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=1-\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{t}\lambda(B(rt+t^{\delta}))\right\}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=1-\exp\left\{-\frac{2}{t}(rt+t^{\delta})\right\}.

(3.4)

Thus,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right)\leq 1-e^{-2r}.

(3.5)

Next, we use the continuity modulus of historical SBM to prove that the second term on the r.h.s. of (
3.1
) tends to
0
. For
j=0,1,...
, let

\lambda_{j}=:\lambda|\left(B(rt+t^{\delta}-1+t^{\delta(j+1)})-B(rt+t^{\delta}-1+t^{\delta j})\right).

So,
\lambda|B^{c}(rt+t^{\delta})=\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}
. Recall that under
\mathbb{\mathbb{Q}}_{m}
,
\{H_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
stands for the historical super-Brownian of
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
starting from measure
m\in\mathcal{M}_{F}(\mathbb{R})
. It is simple to see

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B^{c}(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda_{j}}\left(\exists s\in[0,t],X_{s}(B(rt))>0\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbb{\mathbb{Q}}_{\lambda_{j}}\left(\exists s\in[0,t],H_{s}(A(s,rt))>0\right),

(3.7)

where

A(u,v):=\left\{y_{\cdot}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\inf_{s\leq u}|y_{s}|\leq v\right\}~\text{for}~u,~v\geq 0.

Fix
\bar{\delta}\in(0,2\delta-1)
. For
t>1
, let
l_{j}:=\left\lfloor te^{t^{\bar{\delta}(j\vee 1)}}\right\rfloor
,
j\geq 0
. Define
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(H_{s},s\in[0,t])
. By the Markov property of
\{H_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[H_{(i+1)t/l_{j}}(A(it/l_{j},rt+1))=0\right]

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[\mathbb{Q}_{it/l_{i},H_{it/l_{j}}}\left[H_{(i+1)t/l_{j}}(A(it/l_{j},rt+1))=0|\mathcal{F}_{it/l_{j}}\right]\right]

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[\mathbb{Q}_{it/l_{i},H_{it/l_{j}}}\left[H_{(i+1)t/l_{j}}\left(\Big\{y_{\cdot}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+}):y^{it/l_{j}}_{\cdot}\in A(it/l_{j},rt+1)\Big\}\right)=0\Bigg|\mathcal{F}_{it/l_{j}}\right]\right]

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[\mathbb{Q}_{it/l_{i},H_{it/l_{j}}}\left[H_{s}\left(\Big\{y_{\cdot}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+}):y^{it/l_{j}}_{\cdot}\in A(it/l_{j},rt+1)\Big\}\right)=0,\right.\right.

(3.11)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\left.\left.~\forall s\geq(i+1)t/l_{j}\Bigg|\mathcal{F}_{it/l_{j}}\right]\right]

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[\exp\left\{-\frac{2H_{it/l_{j}}\left(A(it/l_{j},rt+1)\right)}{t/l_{j}}\right\}\right].

(3.13)

Thus, for any
1\leq i\leq l_{j}-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[H_{(i+1)t/l_{j}}(A(it/l_{j},rt+1))>0\right]

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[\exp\left\{-\frac{2}{t/l_{j}}H_{it/l_{j}}\left(A(it/l_{j},rt+1)\right)\right\}\right]

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2l_{j}}{t}\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[H_{it/l_{j}}(A(it/l_{j},rt+1))\right]

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\frac{2l_{j}}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{Q}_{\delta_{x}}\left[H_{it/l_{j}}(A(it/l_{j},rt+1))\right]\lambda_{j}(dx)

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{2l_{j}}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\inf_{s\leq it/l_{j}}|W_{s}|\leq rt+1\right)\lambda_{j}(dx)

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2l_{j}}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\inf_{s\leq it/l_{j}}(|x|-|W_{s}|)\leq rt+1\right)\lambda_{j}(dx)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2l_{j}}{t}\lambda_{j}(1)\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq it/l_{j}}|W_{s}|>t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}\right),

(3.19)

where the third equality follows from the fact that the mean measure of
H_{t}
is the Wiener measure stopped at time
t
(i.e. one moment formula of
H_{t}
; see
[
16
, p191, II.8.5]
). It is simple to see that for any
t>4
(thus
t^{\delta}>2
) and
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq it/l_{j}}|W_{s}|>t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}\right)

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\Big|\sup_{s\leq it/l_{j}}W_{s}\Big|>t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}~\text{or}~\Big|\inf_{s\leq it/l_{j}}W_{s}\Big|>t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}\right)

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\leq it/l_{j}}W_{s}>t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}\right)

(3.22)

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\big|W_{it/l_{j}}\big|>t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}\right)

(3.23)

\displaystyle=4\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\frac{W_{it/l_{j}}}{\sqrt{it/l_{j}}}>\frac{t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}}{\sqrt{it/l_{j}}}\right)

(3.24)

\displaystyle=4\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\frac{W_{it/l_{j}}}{\sqrt{it/l_{j}}}>t^{\left(\delta(j\vee 1)-\frac{1}{2}\right)}/2\right)

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{t^{-(\delta(j\vee 1)-\frac{1}{2})}}\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2\delta(j\vee 1)-1}}{8}\right\},

(3.26)

where the second equality follows from the reflection property of the Brownian motion (see
[
14
, p45]
) and the last inequality comes from the following classical estimate for standard normal random variable
X
:

\mathbb{P}(X>x)\leq\frac{1}{x\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}},~x>0.

Plugging (
3.20
) into (
3.14
) yields that there exists
C_{1}>0
depending only on
r,~\delta
and
\bar{\delta}
such that for any
t>C_{1}
and
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[H_{(i+1)t/l_{j}}(A(it/l_{j},rt+1))>0\right]

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16l_{j}}{t\sqrt{2\pi}}\lambda_{j}(1){t^{-(\delta(j\vee 1)-\frac{1}{2})}}\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2\delta(j\vee 1)-1}}{8}\right\}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16e^{t^{\bar{\delta}(j\vee 1)}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}3(r+1)t^{\delta((j\vee 1)+1)}{t^{-(\delta(j\vee 1)-\frac{1}{2})}}\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2\delta(j\vee 1)-1}}{8}\right\}

(3.29)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2\delta(j\vee 1)-1}}{9}\right\}.

(3.30)

Fix
j\geq 0
. Consider
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
starts from
\lambda_{j}
. Write

T_{j}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:X_{s}(B(rt))>0\}

with the convention that
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
. Since under the event
\{\exists s\in[0,t],H_{s}(A(s,rt))>0\}
, we have
0<T_{j}\leq t
, there exists an integer
i\in\{0,1,...,l_{j}-1\}
such that
T_{j}\in(it/l_{j},(i+1)t/l_{j}]
. For
i\geq 1
, if the SBM

\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has charged the set
B(rt+1)
at time
(i-1)t/l_{j}
then
H_{it/l_{j}}(A((i-1)t/l_{j},rt+1))>0
. Otherwise, it has not charge
B(rt+1)
, then the support process for
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has to travel a distance of at least
1
on time interval
[(i-1)t/l_{j},T_{j}]\subset[(i-1)t/l_{j},(i+1)t/l_{j}]
, which implies
\Delta<2t/l_{j}
(use the modulus continuity and the fact
2t/l_{j}\to 0
). For
i=0
, since

X_{0}=\lambda_{j}=\lambda|\left(B(rt+t^{\delta}-1+t^{\delta(j+1)})-B(rt+t^{\delta}-1+t^{\delta j})\right),

the support process for
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has to travel a distance of at least
t^{\delta}-2+t^{\delta j}
on time interval
[0,t/l_{j}]
, which also implies
\Delta<2t/l_{j}
. Putting these together,
there exists
C_{2}>0
depending only on
r,~\delta,~\bar{\delta},~\kappa
and
\rho
such that for any
t>C_{2}
and
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}(\exists s\in[0,t],H_{s}(A(s,rt))>0)

(3.31)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{l_{j}-1}_{i=1}\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}\left[H_{(i+1)t/l_{j}}(A(it/l_{j},rt+1))>0\right]+\mathbb{Q}_{\lambda_{j}}(\Delta\leq 2t/l_{j})

(3.32)

\displaystyle\leq l_{j}\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2\delta(j\vee 1)-1}}{9}\right\}+\lambda_{j}(1)\kappa 2^{\rho}\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}(j\vee 1)}\right\}

(3.33)

\displaystyle\leq te^{t^{\bar{\delta}(j\vee 1)}}\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2\delta(j\vee 1)-1}}{9}\right\}+3(r+1)t^{2\delta(j\vee 1+1)}\kappa 2^{\rho}\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}(j\vee 1)}\right\}

(3.34)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}(j\vee 1)}/2\right\},

(3.35)

where the second inequality follows from (
3.27
) and Lemma
2.2
. Plugging above into (
3.6
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B^{c}(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 2}\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}j}/2\right\}+2\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}}/2\right\}

(3.36)

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\infty}_{1}\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}x}/2\right\}dx+2\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}}/2\right\}

(3.37)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{\delta}\log t}\int^{\infty}_{\rho t^{\bar{\delta}}/2}\frac{e^{-u}}{u}du+2\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}}/2\right\}

(3.38)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{2}{\rho t^{\bar{\delta}}\bar{\delta}\log t}+2\right]\exp\left\{-\rho t^{\bar{\delta}}/2\right\}.

(3.39)

Thus,

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{\rho t^{\bar{\delta}}/3}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B^{c}(rt+t^{\delta})}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))>0\right)=0.

(3.40)

This, combined with (
3.1
) and (
3.5
), gives

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}(B(rt))=0\right)\geq e^{-2r}.

(3.41)

Next, we deal with the upper bound. By the first moment formula of SBM (see
[
11
, p38]
), we have for any
\varepsilon\in(0,r)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[\frac{1}{t}X_{t}(B^{c}(rt))\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{|x|\leq rt+t^{\delta}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(|x+W_{t}|>rt)dx

(3.42)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{|x|\leq(r-\varepsilon)t}\mathbb{P}_{0}(|W_{t}|>\varepsilon t)dx+\frac{1}{t}\int_{(r-\varepsilon)t<|x|\leq rt+t^{\delta}}1dx

(3.43)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{0}(|W_{t}|>\varepsilon t)(r-\varepsilon)+2(\varepsilon t+t^{\delta})/t.

By the law of large numbers,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[\frac{1}{t}X_{t}(B^{c}(rt))\right]\leq 2\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[\frac{1}{t}X_{t}(B^{c}(rt))\right]=0.

(3.44)

By the branching property of SBM, for any
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[e^{-\eta t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))}\right]

(3.45)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B^{c}(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[e^{-\eta t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))}\right]\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[e^{-\eta t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))}\right]

(3.46)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[e^{-\eta t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))}\right],

where the last inequality is because by (
3.40
),

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))=0,~\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B^{c}(rt+t^{\delta})}\text{-in distribution}.

On the other hand, from (
3.44
),
\frac{1}{t}X_{t}(B^{c}(rt))
converges in distribution to
0
(under
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}
). Furthermore, by Lemma
2.1
, we have
t^{-1}X_{t}(1)
converges in distribution (under
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}
). Therefore, under
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}
,
t^{-1}X_{t}(1)-t^{-1}X_{t}(B^{c}(rt)
converges in distribution. Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[e^{-\eta t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[e^{-\eta[t^{-1}X_{t}(1)-t^{-1}X_{t}(B^{c}(rt))]}\right]

(3.47)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda|B(rt+t^{\delta})}\left[e^{-\eta t^{-1}X_{t}(1)}\right]

(3.48)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{\eta t^{-1}\lambda(B(rt+t^{\delta}))}{1+\eta}\right\}

(3.49)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{2r\eta}{1+\eta}}.

(3.50)

Thus, for any
\eta>0

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{t}(B(rt))=0)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))=0)

(3.51)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}[e^{-\eta t^{-1}X_{t}(B(rt))}]

(3.52)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{2r\eta}{1+\eta}}.

(3.53)

Finally,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{t}(B(rt))=0)\leq\lim_{\eta\to\infty}e^{-\frac{2r\eta}{1+\eta}}\leq e^{-2r}.

This, combined with (
3.41
), concludes the proposition.

\Box

Now, we are ready to present the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Namely, we are going to prove

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{t}\geq r\right)=e^{-2r}~\text{for}~r>0.

The idea of the proof is to use the integral equation (
1.3
) to argue that
\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{t}\geq r\right)
and
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{t}\geq r\right)
have the same asymptotics. We then use Proposition
3.1
to conclude the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
. For a point measure
F
, write
u\in F
if
u
is an atom of
F
. Recall that
X_{0}=\sum_{u\in X_{0}}\delta_{u}
is a Poisson random measure with intensity measure
\lambda
. Let
\{X^{\delta_{u}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the SBM started from
\delta_{u}
(i.e. a single particle at position
u
). By the branching property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{t}\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}(B(tr))=0\right)

(3.54)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\forall u\in X_{0},X^{\delta_{u}}_{t}(B(tr))=0\right)

(3.55)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Pi_{u\in X_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(X_{t}(B(tr))=0)\right]

(3.56)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\sum_{u\in X_{0}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{u}}(X_{t}(B(tr))=0)}\right]

(3.57)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(tr))=0)X_{0}(dx)}\right]

(3.58)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(tr))>0)dx},

(3.59)

where the last equality follows from the Laplace functional formula of Poisson random measures (see
[
1
, p19, (2.17)]
).

For
\theta>0
, let

u_{\theta}(t,x):=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\theta{\bf 1}_{B(tr)}>}\right],~t\geq 0,~x\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
u_{\theta}(t,x)
is increasing w.r.t.
\theta
,
u(t,x):=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}u_{\theta}(t,x)
exists. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(tr))=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\theta{\bf 1}_{B(tr)}>}\right]

(3.60)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e^{-u_{\theta}(t,x)}

(3.61)

\displaystyle=e^{-u(t,x)}.

(3.62)

From (
1.3
), we have

u_{\theta}(t,x)+\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u^{2}_{\theta}(t-s,W_{s})\right]ds=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\theta{\bf 1}_{B(tr)}(W_{t})\right].

Integrating w.r.t.
x
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t,x)dx+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}_{\theta}(t-s,y)dyds=2r\theta t.

Set

G_{\theta}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t,x)dx=2r\theta t-\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}_{\theta}(s,y)dyds.

From Lemma
2.1
, for any
\theta,~t>0
,

\displaystyle u_{\theta}(t,x)<\frac{1}{t}.

(3.63)

By (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{t}(B(tr))=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\theta{\bf 1}_{B(tr)}>}\right]

(3.64)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t,x)dx}

(3.65)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx},

(3.66)

where the last inequality follows from Lévy’s monotone convergence theorem. Thus, by Proposition
3.1
, there exists some
C_{3}>1
depending only on
r
such that for
t>C_{3}
and
\theta>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t,x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx<3r.

Then, for
\theta>\frac{3}{2}
and
t>C_{3}
,

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=2r\theta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}_{\theta}(t,y)dy

(3.67)

\displaystyle\geq 2r\theta-\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t,y)dy

(3.68)

\displaystyle\geq 2r\theta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t,y)dy

(3.69)

\displaystyle\geq 2r\theta-3r

(3.70)

\displaystyle>0,

(3.71)

where the first inequality follows from (
3.63
). Thus, for any
t_{2}>t_{1}>C_{3}
and
\theta>\frac{3}{2}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t_{1},x)dx<\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t_{2},x)dx.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\theta}(t,x)dx
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t_{1},x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t_{2},x)dx.

So
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx
is increasing on
(C_{3},\infty)
. Thus, by Proposition
3.1
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{t}(B(tr))=0)\downarrow e^{-2r}.

(3.72)

Since
x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}<1-e^{-x}<x
for
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}1-e^{-u(t,x)}dx

(3.73)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)-\frac{u^{2}(t,x)}{2}dx

(3.74)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{2t}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx.

(3.75)

where the last inequality uses the fact that
u(t,x)=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}u_{\theta}(t,x)\leq\frac{1}{t}
(see
3.63
). This, combined with (
3.72
), yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1-e^{-u(t,x)}dx=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx=2r.

(3.77)

So, by (
3.54
) and (
3.60
),

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{t}\geq r\right)=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}1-e^{-u(t,x)}dx}=e^{-2r}.

We have completed the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1.2
. Namely, if
d=2
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\geq r\right)=e^{-A_{2}(r)}\in(0,1),

where
A_{2}(r)=-\log\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)
satisfying

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{A_{2}(r)}{\pi r^{2}}=1.

The idea of the proof can be divided into 4 steps:

Step 1. By the scaling property, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}\left(B(\sqrt{t}r)\right)=0\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)~\text{for}~t>0;

Step 2. Use the Feynman-Kac representation to give the desired lower bound of
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)
;

Step 3. From the observation

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda|B(r)}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)

and the extinction probability of
\{X_{t}(1)\}_{t\geq 0}
, we get the desired upper bound of
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)
;

Step 4. Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{t}/\sqrt{t}\geq r\right)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}(B(\sqrt{t}r))=0\right).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Step 1
. From
[
5
, p51]
, we have the following scaling property for 2-dimensional SBM
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
started from Lebesgue measure: for any
\eta,~t>0
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

X_{t}(A)\overset{\text{Law}}{=}\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}X_{\eta^{2}t}(\eta A).

Let
\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
and
A=B(\sqrt{t}r)
, then

X_{t}(B(\sqrt{t}r))\overset{\text{Law}}{=}\frac{1}{t}X_{1}(B(r)).

Thus,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}\left(B(\sqrt{t}r)\right)=0\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0).

Step 2
.
Similar to the case of
d=1
, let

u(t,x):=-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))=0),~t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Due to the same reason as (
3.64
), we have

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(1,x)dx}.

(4.1)

Thus, to finish Step 2, it suffices to give an upper bound of
u(1,x)
. Fix
\delta>0
, we are going to prove

M_{\delta}(r):=\sup_{|x|>(1+\delta)r,~t>0}u(t,x)<\infty.

(4.2)

Suppose that (
4.2
) is not true. Then, by the fact that
u(t,x)<1/t
, there exist some
|x_{n}|>(1+\delta)r
,
n\geq 1
and
t_{n}\rightarrow 0
such that
u(t_{n},x_{n})\rightarrow\infty
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x_{n}}}(X_{t_{n}}(B(r))>0)

\displaystyle=1-\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x_{n}}}(X_{t_{n}}(B(r))=0)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=1-\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{-u(t_{n},x_{n})}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=1.

(4.5)

On the other hand, since
B(r)\subset B^{c}(x_{n},\delta r)
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x_{n}}}(X_{t_{n}}(B(r))>0)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x_{n}}}(X_{t_{n}}(B(x_{n},\delta r)^{c})>0)

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{t_{n}}(B(0,\delta r)^{c})>0)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=0,

(4.8)

where the last equality follows from the continuity modulus of the historical SBM. Thus, (
4.6
) contradicts (
4.3
), and therefore (
4.2
) holds. Furthermore, since
\delta
and
r
are arbitrary positive constants, we have

M(r):=\sup_{|x|\geq r,~t>0}u(t,x)<\infty.

(4.9)

Let
\phi(x)={\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)
. For
\theta>0
, let

u_{\theta}(t,x):=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\theta\phi>}\right].

By the Feynman-Kac representation
[
16
, p170]
,

u_{\theta}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\theta\phi(W_{t})e^{-\int^{t}_{0}u_{\theta}(t-s,W_{s})}\right].

Let
T_{r}:=\inf\{t>0:|W_{t}|=r\}
. Then, by the strong Markov property of Brownian motion, for
|x|\geq(1+\delta)r
,

\displaystyle u_{\theta}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{T_{r}<t\}}e^{-\int^{T_{r}}_{0}u_{\theta}(t-s,W_{s})ds}\mathbb{E}_{W_{T_{r}}}\left[\theta\phi(W_{t-T_{r}})e^{-\int^{t-T_{r}}_{0}u_{\theta}(t-T_{r}-s,W_{s})ds}\right]\right]

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{T_{r}<t\}}u_{\theta}(t-T_{r},W_{T_{r}})\right]

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq M(r)\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\inf_{s\in(0,t)}|x+W_{s}|\leq r\right),

(4.12)

where the first equality follows from the fact that
\phi(W_{t})=0
for
T_{r}>t
and the last inequality follows from (
4.9
). Let
\{W^{1}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the one-dimensional standard-Brownian motion. Since
\sup_{s\in(0,t)}W^{1}_{s}\overset{\text{Law}}{=}|W^{1}_{t}|
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\inf_{s\in(0,t)}|x+W_{s}|\leq r\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|x|-r\leq\sup_{s\in(0,t)}|W_{s}|\right)

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{s\in(0,t)}W^{1}_{s}\geq(|x|-r)/\sqrt{2}\right)

(4.15)

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|W^{1}_{t}|/\sqrt{t}\geq(|x|-r)/\sqrt{2t}\right)

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{\pi}(|x|-r)}e^{-\frac{(|x|-r)^{2}}{4t}}.

(4.17)

Plugging above into (
4.10
) yields that

u_{\theta}(t,x)\leq M(r)\frac{4\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{\pi}(|x|-r)}e^{-\frac{(|x|-r)^{2}}{4t}}.

Since
M(r)
is decreasing (thus bounded) and
\lim_{\theta\to\infty}u_{\theta}(t,x)=u(t,x)
, there exists some constant
C_{4}>0
such that for any
|x|\geq(1+\delta)r
,

u(1,x)\leq M(r)\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}(|x|-r)}e^{-\frac{(|x|-r)^{2}}{4}}<\frac{C_{4}}{|x|-r}e^{-\frac{(|x|-r)^{2}}{4}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(1,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|\leq(1+\delta)r}1dx+\int_{|x|>(1+\delta)r}\frac{C_{4}}{|x|-r}e^{-\frac{(|x|-r)^{2}}{2}}dx

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq\pi(1+\delta)^{2}r^{2}+\int_{|x|>(1+\delta)r}\frac{C_{4}}{|x|-r}e^{-\frac{(|x|-r)^{2}}{2}}dx

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq\pi(1+\delta)^{2}r^{2}+\int_{w>\delta r}\frac{C_{4}}{w}e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{2}}w^{2}dw

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\pi(1+\delta)^{2}r^{2}+C_{4}e^{-\delta^{2}r^{2}/2}.

Taking limits yields that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(1,x)dx}{\pi r^{2}}\leq 1+\delta.

Let
\delta\to 0
, then plugging it into (
4.1
) yields

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)}{\pi r^{2}}\leq 1.

(4.21)

Step 3
. Fix
\bar{\delta}\in(0,1)
. It is simple to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)dx\right\}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{\int_{|x|\leq(1-\bar{\delta})r}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)dx\right\}

(4.23)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\int_{|x|\leq(1-\bar{\delta})r}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0)dx\right\}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{\int_{|x|\leq(1-\bar{\delta})r}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(B(\bar{\delta}r))=0)dx\right\}

(4.25)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\pi(1-\bar{\delta})^{2}r^{2}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(B(\bar{\delta}r))=0)\right\}.

(4.26)

By the dominated convergence theorem and Lemma
2.1
,

\lim_{r\to\infty}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(B(\bar{\delta}r))=0)=\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=0)=-1.

Therefore,

\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{1}(B(r))=0)}{\pi r^{2}}\geq(1-\bar{\delta})^{2}.

The desired lower bound follows by letting
\bar{\delta}\to 0
.

Step 4
. By similar arguments as in the proof of (
3.54
)-(
3.77
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\geq r\right)=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}1-e^{-u(t,x)}dx}=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(t,x)dx}.

In fact, the mainly changes are to replace
t
,
\mathbb{R}
, Proposition
3.1
with
\sqrt{t}
,
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,(
4.21
), respectively. So, we feel free to omit its details here. Putting all steps together, we get that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{R_{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\geq r\right)

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{1}(B(r))=0\right)

(4.28)

\displaystyle=:e^{-A_{2}(r)}.

We have completed the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

\Box

5
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1.3
. Namely, if
d\geq 3
, then for any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq r)=e^{-\kappa_{d}r^{d-2}}\in(0,1).

The proof will be divided into 4 steps:

Step 1. Using the semigroup property of
u(t,x)
and the mild form of the PDE (
1.2
) to show that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(R_{t}\geq r)~\text{exists};

Step 2. By the scaling property of
u(t,x)
, we obtain

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq r)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(R_{t}\geq r)=e^{-\kappa_{d}r^{d-2}};

Step 3. Applying
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]
, we prove
\kappa_{d}>0
.

Step 4. By the second moment method, we show
\kappa_{d}<\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Step 1.
In this step, we show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(R_{t}\geq r\right)
exists. Let
\psi(x)\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a non-negative radially symmetric function such that
\{x:\psi(x)>0\}=\{x:|x|\leq r\}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[e^{-<X_{t},\theta{\bf 1}_{B(r)}>}]

(5.1)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\theta\psi(x)>}\right]

(5.2)

\displaystyle=:\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e^{-u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t,x)}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=:e^{-u(t,x)},

(5.4)

where
u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t,x)
is the unique positive solution to the equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)-u^{2}(t,x),\cr u(0,x)=\theta\psi(x).\end{cases}

(5.5)

Fix
t_{1}\in(0,t)
. From (
1.3
), we have

u^{(u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t_{1},\cdot))}_{\theta}(t-t_{1},x)+\int^{t-t_{1}}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(u^{(u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t_{1},\cdot))}_{\theta}(t-t_{1}-s,W_{s})\right)^{2}\right]ds=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t_{1},W_{t-t_{1}})\right].

By the semigroup property
u^{(u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t_{1},\cdot))}_{\theta}(t-t_{1},x)=u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t,x)
(see
[
11
, p32]
), we get that

u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t,x)+\int^{t-t_{1}}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t-s,W_{s})\right)^{2}\right]ds=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t_{1},W_{t-t_{1}})\right].

Since
u^{(\psi)}_{\theta}(t,x)<\frac{1}{t}
, by the dominated convergence theorem,

u(t,x)+\int^{t-t_{1}}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(u(t-s,W_{s})\right)^{2}\right]ds=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u(t_{1},W_{t-t_{1}})\right].

We write above into its mild form:

u(t,x)=P_{t-t_{1}}(t_{1},x)-\int^{t}_{t_{1}}P_{t-s}u^{2}(s,x)ds.

Integrating w.r.t.
x
and making use of Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-t_{1}}(x,y)dx\right]u(t_{1},y)dy-\int^{t}_{t_{1}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x,y)dxu^{2}(s,y)dy\right]ds

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t_{1},y)dy-\int^{t}_{t_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}(s,y)dyds.

Thus,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)dx
is decreasing w.r.t.
t
. This, together with (
3.64
), implies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(R_{t}\geq r\right)=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)dx}

exists.

Step 2.
In this step, we show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{t}\geq r\right)=e^{-\kappa_{d}r^{d-2}}
. Let
\phi(x)\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a non-negative radially symmetric function such that
\{x:\phi(x)>0\}=\{x:|x|\leq 1\}
. Let
\phi_{r}(x)=\phi(x/r)
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\theta{\bf 1}_{B(r)}>}\right]

(5.7)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-<X_{t},\theta\phi_{r}(x)>}\right]

(5.8)

\displaystyle=:\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e^{-u^{r}_{\theta}(t,x)}

(5.9)

\displaystyle=e^{-u(t,x)}.

(5.10)

In this step, we write
u^{r}(t,x):=u(t,x)
to emphasize that
u(t,x)
depends on
r
. So,

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))=0)=e^{-u^{r}(t,x)}.

(5.11)

Thus, to finish this step it suffices to give an upper bound of
u^{r}(t,x)
. Note that
u^{r}_{\theta}(t,x)
is the unique solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)-u^{2}(t,x),\cr u(0,x)=\theta\phi_{r}(x).\end{cases}

(5.12)

Therefore, we have the following scaling property of
u(t,x)
. For
\theta,~\varepsilon>0
,

u^{r}_{\theta}(t,x)={\varepsilon}^{-2}u^{r/\varepsilon}_{\theta{\varepsilon}^{2}}(t{\varepsilon}^{-2},x{\varepsilon}^{-1}).

This yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{t}(B(r))=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{r}_{\theta}(t,x)dx}

(5.13)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{-2}u^{1}_{\theta r^{2}}(tr^{-2},xr^{-1})dx}

(5.14)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{-2}u^{1}(tr^{-2},xr^{-1})dx}

(5.15)

\displaystyle=e^{-r^{d-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{1}(tr^{-2},x)dx}.

(5.16)

Hence, using the monotonicity of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)dx
and (
5.1
), we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(X_{t}(B(r))=0)=e^{-\kappa_{d}r^{d-2}}.

This, combined with (
3.73
), yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{t}\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\int\left(1-e^{-u(t,x)}\right)dx}

(5.17)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\int u(t,x)dx}

(5.18)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(R_{t}\geq r\right)

(5.19)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(X_{t}(B(r))=0\right)

(5.20)

\displaystyle=e^{-\kappa_{d}r^{d-2}}.

(5.21)

Step 3.
In this step, we show that
\kappa_{d}<\infty
. From
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]
, there exists a constant
C(d)
depending only on
d
such that for all
t>1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

u^{1}(t,x)<C(d)p(t+1,x),

where
p(t,x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}}
is the density function of the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion. Hence,

\kappa_{d}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{1}(t,x)dx\leq C(d)<\infty.

Step 4.
In this step, we show that
\kappa_{d}>0
. In fact, this has been proved in
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
. Nevertheless, here we use a different method to prove it. Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq r)=e^{-\int\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))>0)dx},

(5.22)

To prove
\kappa_{d}>0
, it suffices to get a lower bound of
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))>0)
.

By the Paley-Zygmund inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))>0)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}^{2}_{\delta_{x}}[X_{t}(B(r))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[X^{2}_{t}(B(r))]}

(5.23)

\displaystyle=\frac{(P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x))^{2}}{(P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x))^{2}+2\int^{t}_{0}P_{s}\left[P_{t-s}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)\right]^{2}ds},

(5.24)

where the equality follows from the moments formula of SBM (see
[
11
, p38-39]
).

In the next, we are going to give a lower bound of
P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)
. In the following, we assume
t\geq r^{2}
. Observe that

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{2r|x|}{2t}}\geq\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{t}},&~|x|\geq r;\cr e^{-1},&~|x|<r.\end{cases}

Thus,

e^{-\frac{2r|x|}{2t}}\geq e^{-1}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{t}}.

(5.25)

Observe that if
|y|\leq r
, then

|y-x|^{2}\leq(|x|+|y|)^{2}\leq|x|^{2}+2r|x|+r^{2}.

Let
v_{d}(r)
be the volume of
d
-dimensional ball with radius
r
. Through simple calculations, we have

\displaystyle P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}(|W_{t}+x|\leq r)

(5.26)

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq r}\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t}}dy

(5.27)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|y|\leq r}\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}+2r|x|}{2t}}dy

(5.28)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|y|\leq r}\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}}e^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{2r|x|}{2t}}dy

(5.29)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|y|\leq r}\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}}e^{-3/2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{t}}dy

(5.30)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-3/2}\int_{|y|\leq r}3^{-d/2}\frac{1}{(2\pi t/3)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t/3}}dy

(5.31)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-3/2}3^{-d/2}v_{d}(1)r^{d}p(t/3,x),

(5.32)

where the third inequality follows from (
5.25
).

In the next, we give an upper bound of
\int^{t}_{0}P_{s}\left[P_{t-s}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)\right]^{2}ds
. Note that for
s\in(0,t)
,

\displaystyle P_{t-s}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|x+W_{t-s}|\leq r\right)

(5.34)

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq r}\frac{1}{(2\pi(t-s))^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}dy

(5.35)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{v_{d}(1)r^{d}}{(t-s)^{d/2}}.

Thus, by the semigroup property of
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, we have

P_{s}\left[P_{t-s}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)\right]^{2}\leq\left[\frac{v_{d}(1)r^{d}}{(t-s)^{d/2}}\wedge 1\right]P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x),

which yields

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}P_{s}\left[P_{t-s}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)\right]^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)\left[\int^{t-r^{2}}_{0}\frac{r^{d}}{(t-s)^{d/2}}ds+\int^{t}_{t-r^{2}}1ds\right]

(5.36)

\displaystyle=P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)\left[\int^{t}_{r^{2}}\frac{r^{d}}{u^{d/2}}du+r^{2}\right]

(5.37)

\displaystyle=P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)\left[\frac{2}{d-2}(r^{2}-t^{1-\frac{d}{2}}r^{d})+r^{2}\right]

(5.38)

\displaystyle\leq 3r^{2}P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x).

(5.39)

Plugging (
5.26
) and (
5.36
) into (
5.23
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(X_{t}(B(r))>0)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)}{P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)+6r^{2}}

(5.40)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e^{-3/2}3^{-d/2}v_{d}(1)r^{d}p(t/3,x)}{7r^{2}}

(5.41)

\displaystyle=c(d)r^{d-2}p(t/3,x),

(5.42)

where
c(d):=e^{-3/2}3^{-d/2}v_{d}(1)/7
and the second inequality follows from the fact that for
t
large enough,

\displaystyle P_{t}{\bf 1}_{B(r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq r}\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t}}dy

(5.43)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}v_{d}(1)r^{d}

(5.44)

\displaystyle<r^{2}.

Plugging (
5.40
) into (
5.22
) yields that for
t
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq r)\leq e^{-c(d)r^{d-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t/3,x)dx}=e^{-c(d)r^{d-2}}.

Thus,
\kappa_{d}\geq c_{d}>0
.

\Box

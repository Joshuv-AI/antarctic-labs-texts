Transport in Deformed Centrosymmetric Networks
Adway Kumar Das, Anandamohan Ghosh

Abstract
Centrosymmetry often mediates Perfect State Transfer (PST) in various complex systems ranging from quantum wires to photosynthetic networks. We introduce the Deformed Centrosymmetric Ensemble (DCE) of random matrices, H ​ ( λ ) ≡ H + + λ ​ H − H\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}\equiv H_{+}+\lambda H_{-} , where H + H_{+} is centrosymmetric while H − H_{-} is skew-centrosymmetric. The relative strength of the H ± H_{\pm} prompts the system size scaling of the control parameter as λ = N − γ 2 \lambda=N^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}} . We propose two quantities, 𝒫 \mathcal{P} and 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , quantifying centro- and skewcentro-symmetry, respectively, exhibiting second order phase transitions at γ P ≡ 1 \gamma_{\text{P}}\equiv 1 and γ C ≡ − 1 \gamma_{\text{C}}\equiv-1 . In addition, DCE posses an ergodic transition at γ E ≡ 0 \gamma_{\text{E}}\equiv 0 . Thus equipped with a precise control of the extent of centrosymmetry in DCE, we study the manifestation of γ \gamma on the transport properties of complex networks. We propose that such random networks can be constructed using the eigenvectors of H ​ ( λ ) H\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)} and establish that the maximum transfer fidelity, F T F_{T} , is equivalent to the degree of centrosymmetry, 𝒫 \mathcal{P} .

Introduction

The ideal choice for communication in quantum devices is a quantum wire, described by a network of
N
qubits. These systems are experimentally realizable in optical lattices
Mandel1
, quantum dots
Loss1
, NMR
Gershenfeld1
, trapped ions

or multiple-spin encoding
Haselgrove1
, however, at the cost of introducing decoherence
Benjamin1

;
Karbach1
;
Shi1
;
Venuti1

with wide range of potential applications from mathematical finance

Surprisingly, all the above systems exhibiting PST posses centrosymmetry (also known as exchange/ mirror-symmetry). The degree of centrosymmetry is highly correlated to the transfer fidelity in the photosynthetic structures
Scholak1
;
Zech1
and disordered networks modeled by embedded ensembles
Ortega1
. Centrosymmetry is interesting on its own right and holds a special place in diverse disciplines ranging from information theory
Gersho1
;
Magee1
to engineering problems
Datta1
;
Gaudreau1
. Schematic of a typical centrosymmetric matrix is shown in Fig.
1

;
Cantoni1
, as depicted in Fig.
1

1

Abu-Jeib1
, having the chiral symmetry, which is an important ingredient in topological superconductors
Beenakker1
, spin-liquids
Schneider1
, QCD
Verbaarschot1
and experimentally realized in microwave platforms

. Here we achieve a precise control of the extent of centrosymmetry in a Hamiltonian and study its dynamical manifestations. We demonstrate that the transport in a random network is enhanced as the extent of centrosymmetry is increased.

The organization of the current paper is as follows: in Sec.
II
, we construct the Deformed Centrosymmetric Ensemble (DCE) parametrized by
\lambda

\gamma=\pm 1,0

IV

V

VI

III
Phase Transitions in DCE

All the elements of
H_{\pm}
follow normal distribution, hence their typical fluctuations,
\Delta H_{\pm}(i,j)\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
. The coordination numbers of the diagonal elements for both the matrices are
\mathcal{O}\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
. For
\lambda=1
, the equal contribution from
H_{+}
and
\lambda H_{-}
completely breaks the centrosymmetry of
H(\lambda)
in Eq. (
6
) and produces a GOE matrix.
H(\lambda=1)
belonging to GOE is reminiscent of the emergence of GOE in the Rosenzweig-Porter ensemble
Kravtsov1
;
Das1
. Moreover, due to the random sign altering nature of the elements of DCE matrices, there exists another coordination number
\sim\mathcal{O}\mathinner{\left(\sqrt{N}\right)}
. Thus the critical behaviors can be expected for
\sqrt{N}\Delta H_{+}\sim\lambda N\Delta H_{-}\Rightarrow\lambda\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
and
N\Delta H_{+}\sim\lambda\sqrt{N}\Delta H_{-}\Rightarrow\lambda\sim\sqrt{N}
. Thus we consider the following system size scaling

\displaystyle\lambda=N^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}},\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

(8)

Such scaling suggests possible criticalities at
\gamma=\pm 1,0
, separating four distinct phases based on their symmetry/ chiral properties, which can be identified by the following measures.

Measure of centrosymmetry:

In our matrix model (Eq. (
6
)),
\lambda
controls the extent of centrosymmetry, reflected in
\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle\equiv\bra{\Psi}\mathbb{J}\ket{\Psi}
, where
\ket{\Psi}
is any wavefunction. In SC matrices,
\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle
can be 1 or -1 as corresponding energy states are symmetric (
\mathbb{J}\ket{\Psi}=\ket{\Psi}
) or antisymmetric (
\mathbb{J}\ket{\Psi}=-\ket{\Psi}

\mathinner{\left(E,\ket{\Psi}\right)}

\mathinner{\left(-E,\mathbb{J}\ket{\Psi}\right)}
, hence
\ket{\Psi}
is orthogonal to
\mathbb{J}\ket{\Psi}
and
\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle=0
. In the case of equal contributions from SC and SSC matrices, i.e. for

Berry5
, implying
\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle
follows
\mathcal{N}\mathinner{\left(0,\frac{2}{N}\right)}
, i.e. Gaussian distribution with variance
\frac{2}{N}
. In Fig.
2

\text{P}\mathinner{\left(\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle\right)}
, for various
\gamma

. For
\gamma\ll 0
,
\text{P}\mathinner{\left(\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle\right)}

\gamma\gg 0
, the distribution is bimodal with peaks at

and
\text{P}\mathinner{\left(\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle\right)}_{\gamma=0}\sim\mathcal{N}\mathinner{\left(0,\frac{2}{N}\right)}
.
Since
\text{P}\mathinner{\left(\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle\right)}
is symmetric about zero, the ensemble average of

is 0
\forall\>\gamma
. Thus to measure the presence of centrosymmetry, we look at the quantity,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\langle\mathbb{J}\rangle\right\rvert}^{2}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\bra{\Psi}\mathbb{J}\ket{\Psi}\right\rvert}^{2}

(9)

Clearly

and 0 for centrosymmetric and skew-centrosymmetric matrices, respectively, while we get the Gamma distribution

(

. Similarly

\overline{\mathcal{P}}\to 0
for
\gamma\ll-1
. Exactly at
\gamma=0

, which is the mean of the distribution

Identification of mixed states:

So far we have observed that

IV
Perfect Transport

Let,
\mathcal{G}
be a simple undirected graph with vertices labelled as
1,2,\dots,N
. Corresponding adjacency matrix,
\mathbb{A}
, with an eigendecomposition
\mathinner{\left\{E_{k},\ket{\Phi_{k}}\right\}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{A}&=\sum_{k=1}^{N}E_{k}\ket{\Phi_{k}}\bra{\Phi_{k}}\\
&\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}E_{2j-1}\ket{\Phi_{2j-1}}\bra{\Phi_{2j-1}}+E_{2j}\ket{\Phi_{2j}}\bra{\Phi_{2j}}.\end{split}

(11)

Now we demand that
\mathbb{A}
has centrosymmetry as in
H_{+}
, such that,
\ket{\Phi_{2j-1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\ket{y_{j}}\\
-\mathbb{J}\ket{y_{j}}\end{pmatrix}
and
\ket{\Phi_{2j}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\ket{u_{j}}\\
\mathbb{J}\ket{u_{j}}\end{pmatrix}
, where
\mathinner{\left\{\ket{y_{j}}\right\}}
and
\mathinner{\left\{\ket{u_{j}}\right\}}
are orthonormal bases. Given such a centrosymmetric adjacency matrix, we want to transfer a state initially localized at vertex 1 to the vertex
N
of the graph
\mathcal{G}
. The fidelity of such an excitation transfer has the time evolution,

\displaystyle F(t)=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\bra{N}e^{-i\mathbb{A}t}\ket{1}\right\rvert}^{2}

(12)

where
\ket{j}\equiv\hat{e}_{j}
. A Perfect State Transfer (PST) implies that
F_{T}\equiv F(t=T)=1
. Note that PST is not realizable in a generic random SC matrix, as an initially localized state diffuses, while the network equilibrates to a state with the initial excitation uniformly spread over all the vertices. Nevertheless, the periodicity of
F_{T}
along with the orthonormality of the eigenbasis,
\mathinner{\left\{\Phi_{k}\right\}}
, lead to the following
necessary and sufficient
conditions for PST in any centrosymmetric
\mathbb{A}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}r_{k}e^{-iE_{k}T}&=1\\
\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}r_{k}&=0,\;2T\textrm{ Periodic}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{N}r_{k}&=1,\;\textrm{Orthonormality}\end{split}

(13)

where
r_{k}\equiv\Phi_{k}(1)^{2}\in[0,1]
. Here we ignore any global phase factor in the time evolved state at
t=T
, which can be nullified by an overall shift of the energy axis. The criteria in Eq. (
13
) are equivalently known as the
spectrum parity matching conditions

Shi1
.

There exist various solutions of Eq. (
13
), where both the energy levels and the eigenstates of
\mathbb{A}
i.e. all the
E_{k}
’s and
r_{k}
’s are constrained
Albanese1
;
Christandl4
. However, Eq. (
13
) implies that out of
2N
variables, we have the freedom to constrain any
N
variables. We now illustrate two cases where either
E_{k}
’s or
r_{k}
’s are constrained.

Example 1:

One of the simplest solutions of Eq. (
13
) is to consider any random energy sequence
\mathinner{\left\{E_{k}\right\}}
, while both
\mathinner{\left\{\ket{y_{j}}\right\}}
and
\mathinner{\left\{\ket{u_{j}}\right\}}
are unit bases, leading to the eigenvectors,

\displaystyle\ket{\Phi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathinner{\left(\dots,0,\underset{\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil}{\underbrace{(-1)^{k}}},0\dots,0,\underset{N+1-\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil}{\underbrace{1}},0,\dots\right)}.

(14)

Then the adjacency matrix can be expressed as a superposition of diagonal and anti-diagonal elements,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{A}&=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}A&C\mathbb{J}\\
\mathbb{J}C&\mathbb{J}A\mathbb{J}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{matrix}A_{ij}=\delta_{ij}(E_{2j}+E_{2j-1})\\
C_{ij}=\delta_{ij}(E_{2j}-E_{2j-1})\end{matrix}.\end{split}

(15)

For spin-1/2 particles with XY interaction,
\mathbb{A}
represents an abstract spin-ladder
Song1
of length
\frac{N}{2}

(23)

VI
Discussions

In this work, our principal goal is to control the transfer fidelity between two vertices of a pre-engineered network in the absence of any environment induced decoherence. We show that the maximum transfer fidelity can be exactly controlled by tuning the extent of centrosymmetry in an adjacency matrix of a network. We construct the Deformed Centrosymmetric Ensemble (DCE) interpolating between the random SC (
H_{+}
), GOE and SSC (
H_{-}
) matrices. Various deformed ensembles have been used to study symmetry breaking in small-world networks
Carvalho2
, isospin mixing
Guhr3
, vibrations of crystal block
Carvalho1
and we show that the breaking of centrosymmetry naturally leads to the emergence of DCE. The competition of
H_{\pm}
in DCE prompts the system size scaling of the model parameter as
\lambda=N^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}
. Firstly, we propose a measure of exchange symmetry,
\mathcal{P}
, and identify the centrosymmetric regime for
\gamma>\gamma_{\text{P}}\equiv 1
. Secondly, we identify the skew-centrosymmetric regime for
\gamma<\gamma_{\text{C}}\equiv-1
based on the measure of chirality,
\mathcal{C}
. For
-1<\gamma<0
, the fraction of mixed states tends to unity in the thermodynamic limit as
\mathcal{P}\sim 0
, while
\mathcal{C}
becomes extensive. However,
\mathcal{C}
does not capture any canonical symmetry implying that
\gamma<\gamma_{\text{E}}\equiv 0
is the ergodic regime. Contrarily for
0<\gamma<1
,
\mathcal{P}
becomes extensive indicating the breaking of ergodicity with the emergence of correlations due to centrosymmetry.

Next we study the perfect transport in a centrosymmetric network and present two examples with freedom in the choice of (a) the energy levels (b) the energy states of the adjacency matrix. In both the cases, the analytically calculated time evolution of the transfer fidelity is in agreement with the numerical simulations. Finally we show that it is possible to tune the maximum transfer fidelity by breaking centrosymmetry. So from the eigenvectors of DCE we construct an adjacency matrix,
\mathbb{A}\mathinner{\left(\gamma\right)}
and we prove that the ensemble averaged maximum fidelity is equivalent to the measure of centrosymmetry,
\mathcal{P}
.

Our construction can be seen as a realization of a quantum wire allowing tunable transfer of information between the input and the output qubits. The adjacency matrix
\mathbb{A}\mathinner{\left(\gamma\right)}
is related to the graph
\mathcal{G}\mathinner{\left(\gamma\right)}
, where we place
N
spin-1/2 particles on each vertex, the
i^{th}
spin has Zeeman energy
\mathbb{A}_{ii}
and an isotropic XY interaction,
\mathbb{A}_{ij}
, with the
j^{th}
spin. In the underlying Hamiltonian,
\hat{H}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathinner{\left(\gamma\right)}
, Z-component of the total angular momentum is conserved and all spins down is an eigenstate. Hence the dynamics of any arbitrary single excitation is confined to the first excitation subspace justifying our study of the evolution of
\ket{1}
to
\ket{N}

Christandl1
. Our construction is applicable to the perfect transfer of many-particle state in a network of indistinguishable non-interacting spinless Fermions obtained by the Jordan-Wigner transformation
Albanese1
. However, it is not clear that the maximum fidelity of multi-particle excitation is same as
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
even with the loss of centrosymmetry. Importantly unlike previous studies
Christandl1
;
Albanese1
;
Christandl4
, our stochastic approach allows the construction of an infinite number of networks with identical transport properties. Our study proposes a possible mechanism to achieve a controlled transmission in quantum information processing over complex networks.

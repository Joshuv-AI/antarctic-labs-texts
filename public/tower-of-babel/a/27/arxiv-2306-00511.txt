Introduction

The recent research on fractional and nonlocal partial differential equations has extensively and actively been performed not only in pure mathematics but also in scientific areas that require its concrete applications.
This type of problems appear in various applications such as continuum mechanics, phase transition phenomena that are related to a nonlocal version of the classical Allen-Cahn equation, population dynamics, nonlocal minimal surfaces, a nonlocal version of Schrödinger equations for standing waves ( see
[
BV
,
CK1
,
CK2
]
), game theory and also constrained variational problems with fractional diffusion arising in the quasi-geostrophic flow model, anomalous diffusions and American options with jump processes ( see
[
CV
,
C
,
S
]
).

For
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1
(
p>1
,
0<s<1
), let
{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the class of all potentials
V=V_{+}-V_{-}
such that (i)
V_{-}\in L^{1}_{{\text{\rm loc}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, (ii)
V_{+}\in L^{q}_{{\text{\rm loc}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, (iii) there is an almost everywhere positive bounded function
b
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
so that

V_{+}/b\in L^{q}_{{\text{\rm loc}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
(V_{-},V_{+}^{b,i})
belongs to the
(A_{1},A_{1})
-Muckenhoupt class and
V_{+}^{b,i}
is in the
A_{1}
-Muckenhoupt class for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where

V^{b,i}_{+}:=\frac{\max\{b,1/i\}}{b}\,V_{+}.

If
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1
(
p>1
,
0<s<1
), then we say that

V
is a
A_{1}^{p}
-Muckenhoupt potential.
When
p=2
, we call it
A_{1}
-Muckenhoupt potential.

The aim of this paper is to establish an interior Hölder regularity of weak solutions of nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equations with
A_{1}^{p}
-Muckenhoupt potentials and to additionally obtain the local boundedness of weak subsolutions of the nonlocal equation.

For
p>1
, let
{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
be the collection of all positive symmetric kernels
satisfying the uniformly ellipticity assumption

(1.1)

\frac{c_{n,p,s}\,\lambda}{|y|^{n+ps}}\leq K(y)=K(-y)\leq\frac{c_{n,p,s}\,\Lambda}{|y|^{n+ps}},\,\,0<s<1,\,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

where
c_{n,p,s}>0
is the normalization constant given by

c_{n,p,s}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})\,p\,(1-s)}{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}.

For
K\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
(
\,p>1
), we consider integro-differential operators
{\text{\rm L}}^{p}_{K}
given
by

(1.2)

{\text{\rm L}}^{p}_{K}u(x,t)=\text{\rm p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))K(x-y)\,dy

where
H_{p}(t)=|t|^{p-2}t
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. If
p=2
, then we write
{\text{\rm L}}^{p}_{K}={\text{\rm L}}_{K}
.
In particular, if
K(y)=c_{n,p,s}|y|^{-n-ps}
,
then
{\text{\rm L}}^{p}_{K}=(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
is the fractional
p
-Laplacian and it
is well-known
[
IN
]
that

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=-\Delta_{p}u

for any function
u
in the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where

\Delta_{p}u={\text{\rm div}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)

is the classical
p
-Laplacian.

We are interested in the Dirichlet problem for the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation

(1.3)

\begin{cases}{\text{\rm L}}^{p}_{K}u+V|u|^{p-2}u=0&\text{ in $\Omega$ }\\
u=g&\text{ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega$ }\end{cases}

where
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1
(
\,p>1
and
0<s<1
),

g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz
boundary. The existence and uniqueness of weak solution to the above nonlocal equation was obtained in
[
K3
]
by applying standard technique of calculus of variations.
More precisely speaking about the problem, by employing the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory, we obtain an interior Hölder regularity of weak solutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equations, and also we get the local boundedness of weak subsolutions of the nonlocal equation. Here, we note that the boundary condition is imposed on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
with nonlocality. In fact, from the probabilistic point of view, it conforms to the natural phenomenon that a discontinuous Lévy process on the domain
\Omega
can exit
\Omega
for the first time jumping to any point in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

When
p=2
, the research on the above nonlocal equations was strongly motivated by the study of standing wave solutions of the form

\Psi(x,t)=e^{-i\omega t}u(x)

of the time-dependent nonlocal Schrödinger equations

i\,\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}={\text{\rm L}}_{K}\Psi+V(x)\Psi

which is a fundamental equation of fractional quantum mechanics and fractional quantum physics. This equation was used for the first time in the literature by Laskin ( see
[
L
]
).

It turns out in Section 3 that any potential
V
in
{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfies

(1.4)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy

and

(1.5)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy

for every
\varphi\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, whenever
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1
(
\,p>1
and
0<s<1
).
As a matter of fact, the inequality (1.4) is a useful tool for the proof of nonlocal Caccioppoli type inequality to be given in Theorem 1.5, and also the inequality (1.5) makes it possible to prove in Lemma 3.3 below that
{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

is a quasi-Banach space.

We now state one of our main results which is called the local boundedness of weak subsolutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation (1.3), as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1

(

p>1
,
s\in(0,1)

)
and
B^{0}_{2r}\subset\Omega
. If
u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega)^{-}
is a weak subsolution of nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation
(1.3)
, then there is a constant
C_{0}>0
depending only on
n,s,p,\lambda,\Lambda
and
\Omega
such that

\sup_{B^{0}_{r}}u\leq\delta\,{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u_{+};x_{0})+C_{0}\,\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{2r}}u_{+}^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
\delta\in(0,1]
.

Remark.
(a) If
u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega)^{-}
is a weak subsolution of the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schr
{\ddot{\rm o}}
dinger equation (1.4) and
g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
s\in(0,1)
, then we see that
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
u\leq g
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, and thus
u_{+}\leq g_{+}
there. Then it follows from Hölder’s inequality and fractional Sobolev inequalities (2.4) and (2.5) that

\begin{split}[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u_{+};x_{0})]^{p-1}&\leq\frac{ps\,r^{ps}}{|S^{n-1}|}\biggl(\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{g_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+ps}}\,dy+\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+ps}}\,dy\biggr)\\
&\lesssim\frac{ps\,r^{-n(1-\frac{1}{p})}}{(((p-1)n+p^{2}s)|S^{n-1}|)^{1/p}}\bigl(\,\|g\|^{p-1}_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\bigr)<\infty.\end{split}

(b) If
u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega)^{+}
is a weak supersolution of the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schr
{\ddot{\rm o}}
dinger equation (1.4) and
g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
s\in(0,1)
, then
-u
is its weak subsolution and
u\geq g
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, and so
u_{-}\leq g_{-}
there. Then, as in the above (a), we obtain that

[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{p-1}\lesssim\frac{ps\,r^{-n(1-\frac{1}{p})}}{(((p-1)n+p^{2}s)|S^{n-1}|)^{1/p}}\bigl(\,\|g\|^{p-1}_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\bigr)<\infty.

Then, from Theorem 1.1, we easily have that

-\inf_{B^{0}_{r}}u=\sup_{B^{0}_{r}}(-u)\leq\delta\,{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u_{-};x_{0})+C_{0}\,\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{2r}}u_{-}^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
\delta\in(0,1]
.

(c) If
u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega)^{+}
is a weak solution of the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schr
{\ddot{\rm o}}
dinger equation (1.4) and
g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
s\in(0,1)
, then it follows from (a), (b) and Theorem 1.1 that

\underset{B^{0}_{r}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u\leq 2\delta\,{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u;x_{0})+2C_{0}\,\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{2r}}|u|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
\delta\in(0,1]
.

The following
logarithmic estimate
plays a crucial role in proving the Hölder regularity of weak solutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation and in showing that the logarithm of such weak solution is a function with locally bounded mean oscillation. In a different way from
[
DKP1
]
, we obtain the logarithmic estimate. We now state it as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1

(
\,p>1
and
0<s<1
)
.
If
u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega)^{+}
is a weak supersolution of nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation
(1.3)
with
u\geq 0
in
B^{0}_{R}\subset\Omega
, then there is a constant
c_{0}>0
depending only on
n,s,p,\lambda,\Lambda
and
\Omega
such that

\begin{split}&\iint_{B^{0}_{r}\times B^{0}_{r}}\,\biggl|\ln\biggl(\frac{u(x)+b}{u(y)+b}\biggr)\biggr|^{p}\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq c_{0}\,r^{n-ps}\biggl[\frac{1}{b^{p-1}}\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{p-1}+\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)\biggr]\end{split}

for any
b\in(0,1)
and
r\in(0,R/2)
, where
d_{K}(x,y)=K(x-y)\,dx\,dy
.

Employing the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory and using Theorem 1.1 and 1.2, we obtain the following Hölder continuity of weak solutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation, and also we can easily derive Corollary 1.4 as a natural by-product of Theorem 1.3.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

for

(

If

\eta_{0}^{-}\in(0,\frac{ps}{2(p-1)})

\eta_{0}^{+}\in(\frac{ps}{2(p-1)},\frac{ps}{p-1})

is locally
\eta

\underset{B^{0}_{r}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u\lesssim\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{\eta}\,\biggl[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u;x_{0})+\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{2R}}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\biggr]

\begin{split}&\int_{B^{0}_{r}}[w(y)\zeta(y)]^{p}V(y)\,dy+\iint_{B^{0}_{r}\times B^{0}_{r}}|\zeta(x)w(x)-\zeta(y)w(y)|^{p}\,d_{K}(x,y)\\

{\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
(see
[
FSV
]
and Theorem 1.4.2.2 in
[
G
]
), we can take a sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that

\varphi_{k}\to\varphi\,\,\text{ in ${\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)$.}

Since
V\in{\mathbb{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, there is a nonnegative bounded function
b
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

(3.3)

V^{b}_{+}:=\frac{V_{+}}{b}\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),(V_{-},V^{b,i}_{+})\in(A_{1},A_{1})\text{ and }V^{b,i}_{+}\in A_{1}

for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then we claim that

(3.4)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy;

indeed, we note that
\|\varphi_{k}\|_{{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|\varphi\|_{{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}
for all sufficiently large
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and also we see that, for any
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

\begin{split}|\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}-|\varphi(y)|^{p}&=\int_{|\varphi(y)|}^{|\varphi_{k}(y)|}\frac{d}{d\tau}\tau^{p}\,d\tau\\
&\leq p\,\bigl(|\varphi_{k}(y)|-|\varphi(y)|\bigr)\,\bigl(|\varphi_{k}(y)|\vee|\varphi(y)|\bigr)^{p-1}.\end{split}

Thus it follows from the fractional Sobolev inequality and Hölder’s inequality that

(3.5)

\begin{split}&\biggl|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\biggr|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\bigl||\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}-|\varphi(y)|^{p}\bigr|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq p\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\bigl||\varphi_{k}(y)|-|\varphi(y)|\bigr|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\bigl(|\varphi_{k}(y)|\vee|\varphi(y)|\bigr)^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\|\varphi_{k}-\varphi\|_{{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\to 0\,\,\,\,\text{ as $k\to\infty$.}\end{split}

Also, we claim that

(3.6)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy;

indeed, we have that

V_{-}(y)\leq[V_{-},V_{+}^{b,1}]_{(A_{1},A_{1})}V_{+}^{b,1}(y)\,\,\text{ $\text{\rm a.e.}$ $y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$,}

because
(V_{-},V_{+}^{b,1})\in(A_{1},A_{1})
by (3.3). Since
V_{+}^{b}\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by (3.3), as in (3.5) we obtain that

\begin{split}&\biggl|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\biggr|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\bigl||\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}-|\varphi(y)|^{p}\bigr|\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\bigl(\|b\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\vee 1\bigr)[V_{-},V_{+}^{b,1}]_{(A_{1},A_{1})}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{\Omega}\bigl||\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}-|\varphi(y)|^{p}\bigr|\,V^{b}_{+}(y)\,dy\to 0\,\,\,\,\text{ as $k\to\infty$.}\end{split}

Thus, by (3.2), (3.4) and (3.6), we obtain that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\\
&\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{k}(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy.\end{split}

Hence we are done. ∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1

(
\,p>1
,
0<s<1
)
, then
{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a quasi-Banach space with the quasinorm
\|\cdot\|_{{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}
given by

\|u\|^{p}_{{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|^{p}_{{\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{p}V(y)\,dy,\,\,u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Moreover,
{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)={\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and they are quasinorm-equivalent.

Proof.
It follows from Lemma 3.1 and (2.5) that

\begin{split}\|u\|^{p}_{{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}&\leq\|u\|^{p}_{{\text{\rm Y}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|^{p}_{{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{V_{+}}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq(1+|\Omega|^{\frac{ps}{n}-\frac{1}{q}})\|u\|^{p}_{{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Since
{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a Banach space, this implies the required results. ∎

Theorem 3.3
.

If
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1
and
0<s<1
, then

(3.7)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy

for any
\varphi\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.
Take any
\varphi\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
is dense in
{\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

(see
[
G
]
and
[
FSV
]
),
{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)={\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and they are quasinorm-equivalent by Lemma 3.2, we can take a sequence
\{\varphi_{i}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi_{i}\to\varphi
in
{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. So by (2.5) we also have that
\varphi_{i}\to\varphi
in
L^{p_{*}}(\Omega)
, where
p_{*}=\frac{pn}{n-ps}
. So we can choose a subsequence
\{\varphi_{i_{k}}\}
such that

(3.8)

\varphi_{i_{k}}\to\varphi\,\,\text{ $\text{\rm a.e.}$ in $\Omega$. }

Also we have that

(3.9)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{i_{k}}(y)|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy;

indeed, it follows from the fractional Sobolev inequality and Hölder’s inequality that

\begin{split}&\biggl|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{i_{k}}(y)|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\biggr|\\
&\quad\qquad\leq\int_{\Omega}\bigl||\varphi_{i_{k}}(y)|-|\varphi(y)|\bigr|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\\
&\quad\qquad\leq\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\bigl||\varphi_{i_{k}}(y)|-|\varphi(y)|\bigr|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\biggl(\int_{\Omega}V_{+}(y)\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\\
&\quad\qquad\lesssim|\Omega|^{\frac{s}{n}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p}}\,\|\varphi_{i_{k}}-\varphi\|_{{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\,\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\to 0\,\,\text{ as $k\to\infty$ }\end{split}

where
q^{\prime}
is the dual exponent of
q
.
Hence, by Fatou’s lemma, Lemma 3.1, Lemma 3.5 in
[
K2
]
, (3.2), (3.4), (3.8) and (3.9), we conclude that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{-}(y)\,dy&=\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\\
&\leq\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{i_{k}}(y)|^{p}\,V_{-}(y)\,dy\\
&\leq\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi_{i_{k}}(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\\
&\leq\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,V_{+}(y)\,dy\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\varphi(y)|\,V_{+}(y)\,dy.\end{split}

Therefore we complete the proof.
∎

4.
Local properties of weak subsolutions

In this section, we shall obtain certain local properties for weak subsolutions of the
nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation. These results play a crucial role
in establishing a nonlocal Hölder continuity for weak solutions of the nonlocal equation (1.3).
In order to establish the result, we need several steps.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
p>1
and
M>0
, let
h(t)=t^{p-1}(t-M)_{+}-(t-M)_{+}^{p}\geq 0
. Then
h
is Lipschitz continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.
We note that
h(t)=0
for
t<M
,
h
is in
C^{1}(M,\infty)
,

\lim_{t\to M^{+}}\frac{h(t)-h(M)}{t-M}=0\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\lim_{t\to M^{-}}\frac{h(t)-h(M)}{t-M}=M^{p-1}.

Also it is easy to check that

\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)=0
, because

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{t^{p-1}(t-M)_{+}}{(t-M)_{+}^{p}}=1.

Moreover, in order to check the differentiability of
h
at the infinity, set
g(t)=h(1/t)
.
Then we have that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{g(t)-g(0)}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\biggl[\frac{1}{t^{p}}\bigl(\frac{1}{t}-M\bigr)-\frac{1}{t}\bigl(\frac{1}{t}-M\bigr)^{p}\biggr]=0,

because

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\biggl[\frac{1}{t^{p}}\biggl(\frac{1}{t}-M\bigr)\bigl/\frac{1}{t}\bigl(\frac{1}{t}-M\bigr)^{p}\biggr]=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1-tM}{(1-tM)^{p}}=1.

Thus this implies the required result. ∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If
M>0
and
u\in{\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}(\Omega)
for
p>1
and
0<s<1
, then

(a)

|u|^{p-2}u(u-M)_{+}-(u-M)_{+}^{p}\in{\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}(\Omega)
and it is nonnegative in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and

(b)

\bigl[|u|^{p-2}u(u-M)_{+}-(u-M)_{+}^{p}\bigr]\zeta^{p}\in{\text{\rm X}}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
for any nonnegative
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
.

Proof.
(a) Note that
|u|^{p-2}u(u-M)_{+}-(u-M)_{+}^{p}=h\circ u
for the function
h
given in Lemma 4.1.
Since
h
is Lipschitz continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}
by Lemma 4.1, it is obvious that
0\leq h\circ u\in{\text{\rm X}}^{s,p}(\Omega)
.

(b) By the mean value theorem, we have that

\begin{split}\bigl|\zeta^{p}(x)-\zeta^{p}(y)\bigr|&=\biggl|\int_{\zeta(y)}^{\zeta(x)}\frac{d}{d\tau}\tau^{p}\,d\tau\biggr|\leq p\bigl(\zeta^{p-1}(x)\vee\zeta^{p-1}(y)\bigr)|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|\\
&\leq 2p\,\|\zeta\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|\end{split}

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
This means that
\zeta^{p}
is Lipschitz continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. So we can easily derive the result.
Hence we complete the proof. ∎

Next we need the following
nonlocal Caccioppoli type inequality
which is Theorem 1.5. This is a very useful tool in proving an interior Hölder regularity of weak solutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation.

[Proof of Theorem 1.5.]
For simplicity of the proof, we assume that

x_{0}=0
.
Let
w=(u-M)_{+}
for
M\in[0,\infty)
and take any nonnegative
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r})
. We use
\varphi=w\zeta^{p}
as a testing function in
the weak formulation of the equation. Then we have that

(4.1)

\begin{split}\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{H_{p},K}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|u(y)|^{p-2}u(y)\varphi(y)V(y)\,dy\leq 0,\end{split}

where
d_{K}(x,y)=K(x-y)\,dx\,dy
and

\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{H_{p},K}=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\,d_{K}(x,y)\,\text{ for $p\geq 1$.}

The first term in the left-hand side of the above inequality can be decomposed into two parts as follows;

(4.2)

\begin{split}\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{H_{p},K}&=\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\quad\qquad+2\iint_{({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r})\times B_{r}}H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))\,\varphi(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&:=I_{1}+2I_{2}.\end{split}

For the estimate of
I_{1}
, without loss of generality we may assume that
u(x)\geq u(y)
. Then we first observe that
w(x)\geq w(y)
and

(4.3)

H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\geq(w(x)-w(y))^{p-1}(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))

whenever
x,y\in B_{r}
; indeed, it can easily be checked by considering three possible occasions (i)
u(x),u(y)>M
, (ii)
u(x)>M
,
u(y)\leq M
, and (iii)
u(y)\leq u(x)\leq M
.
Also we observe that

(4.4)

\begin{split}|\zeta(x)w(x)-\zeta(y)w(y)|^{p}&\leq 2^{p-1}|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}(\zeta^{p}(x)+\zeta^{p}(y))\\
&\quad+2^{p-1}(w^{p}(x)+w^{p}(y))|\zeta(y)-\zeta(x)|^{p}.\end{split}

By (b) of Lemma 2.4, (4.3) and (4.4), we have that

(4.5)

\begin{split}&H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\\
&\quad\qquad\geq\frac{1}{4}|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}(\zeta^{p}(x)+\zeta^{p}(y))\\
&\qquad\qquad-d_{p}(w^{p}(x)+w^{p}(y))|\zeta(y)-\zeta(x)|^{p}\\
&\qquad\quad\geq\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}|\zeta(x)w(x)-\zeta(y)w(y)|^{p}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\bigl(\frac{1}{4}+d_{p}\bigr)(w^{p}(x)+w^{p}(y))|\zeta(y)-\zeta(x)|^{p}.\end{split}

Thus it follows from (4.5) that

(4.6)

\begin{split}I_{1}&\geq\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}|\zeta(x)w(x)-\zeta(y)w(y)|^{p}\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\quad-2^{p}\bigl(\frac{1}{4}+d_{p}\bigr)\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}(w^{p}(x)+w^{p}(y))|\zeta(y)-\zeta(x)|^{p}\,d_{K}(x,y).\end{split}

For the estimate of
I_{2}
, we note that

\begin{split}H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))\varphi(x)&\geq-(u(y)-u(x))^{p-1}_{+}(u(x)-M)_{+}\zeta^{p}(x)\\
&\geq-(u(y)-M)^{p-1}_{+}(u(x)-M)_{+}\zeta^{p}(x)\\
&=-w^{p-1}(y)w(x)\zeta^{p}(x)\end{split}

and thus we have that

(4.7)

\begin{split}I_{2}&\geq-\iint_{({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r})\times B_{r}}w^{p-1}(y)w(x)\zeta^{p}(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\geq-\biggl(\,\sup_{x\in\text{\rm supp}(\zeta)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}w^{p-1}(y)\,K(x-y)\,dy\biggr)\int_{B_{r}}w(x)\zeta^{p}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Finally, we claim that

(4.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|u(y)|^{p-2}u(y)\varphi(y)V(y)\,dy\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}w^{p}(y)\zeta^{p}(y)V(y)\,dy.

This is equivalent to the following inequality

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\bigl[|u(y)|^{p-2}u(y)w(y)-w^{p}(y)\bigr]\zeta^{p}(y)V_{+}(y)\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\bigl[|u(y)|^{p-2}u(y)w(y)-w^{p}(y)\bigr]\zeta^{p}(y)V_{-}(y)\,dy,\end{split}

whose proof is just a direct application of Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 4.2.
Hence the required inequality can be obtained from (4.1), (4.2), (4.6), (4.7) and (4.8). ∎

Next, we shall obtain the local boundedness of such weak subsolutions which is Theorem 1.1 and a relation between the nonlocal tail terms of the positive part and the negative part of weak solutions of the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation (1.3) in the following theorems.

[Proof of Theorem 1.1.]
Take any
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B^{0}_{r})
such that
|\nabla\zeta|\leq c/r
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Let
w=(u-M)_{+}
for
M\in(0,\infty)
.
By Lemma 3.2, Theorem 1.6 and the mean value theorem, we have that

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\iint_{B^{0}_{r}\times B^{0}_{r}}|\zeta(x)w(x)-\zeta(y)w(y)|^{p}\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\,r^{p-ps}\|\nabla\zeta\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(B^{0}_{r})}\|w\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B^{0}_{r})}+{\mathcal{A}}(w,\zeta,r,s)\,\|w\|_{L^{1}(B^{0}_{r})}\end{split}

where
{\mathcal{A}}(w,\zeta,r,s)
is the value given by

{\mathcal{A}}(w,\zeta,r,s)=\sup_{x\in\text{\rm supp}(\zeta)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B^{0}_{r}}w^{p-1}(y)\,K(x-y)\,dy.

Applying Proposition 2.1 to (4.9), we obtain that

(4.10)

\begin{split}\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{r}}|w\zeta|^{p\gamma}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}&\,\,\lesssim\bigl(r^{p-ps}\|\nabla\zeta\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(B^{0}_{r})}+r^{-ps}\bigr)r^{ps}\fint_{B^{0}_{r}}|w|^{p}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+{\mathcal{A}}(w,\zeta,r,s)\,r^{ps}\fint_{B^{0}_{r}}w\,dx\end{split}

where
\gamma=\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-ps}>1
.
For
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, we set

\begin{split}&r_{k}=(1+2^{-k})r,\,\,\,\,r^{*}_{k}=\frac{r_{k}+r_{k+1}}{2},\\
&M_{k}=M+(1-2^{-k})M_{*},\,\,\,\,M^{*}_{k}=\frac{M_{k}+M_{k+1}}{2},\end{split}

w_{k}=(u-M_{k})_{+}
and
w^{*}_{k}=(u-M^{*}_{k})_{+}
for a constant
M_{*}>0
to be determined later.
In (4.9), for
k=0,1,\cdots,
we choose a function
\zeta_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B^{0}_{r^{*}_{k}})
with
\zeta_{k}|_{B^{0}_{r_{k+1}}}\equiv 1
such that
0\leq\zeta_{k}\leq 1
and

|\nabla\zeta_{k}|\leq c\,2^{k+2}/r\,\,\text{ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}$.}

For
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, we set

N_{k}=\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{r_{k}}}|w_{k}|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
w_{k}^{*}\geq w_{k+1}
and

w_{k}^{*}(x)\geq M_{k+1}-M_{k}^{*}=2^{-k-2}M_{*}

whenever
u(x)\geq M_{k+1}
, we then have that

(4.11)

\begin{split}N_{k+1}&\lesssim\biggl(\frac{1}{|B^{0}_{r_{k}}|}\int_{B^{0}_{r_{k+1}}}\frac{w^{p}_{k+1}(w_{k}^{*})^{p(\gamma-1)}}{(M_{k+1}-M_{k}^{*})^{p(\gamma-1)}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\lesssim\biggl(\frac{2^{k}}{M_{*}}\biggr)^{\gamma-1}\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{r_{k}}}|w^{*}_{k}\,\zeta_{k}|^{p\gamma}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

Since
\zeta_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B^{0}_{r_{k}})
,
w_{k}^{*}\leq w_{0}
for all
k
and

\begin{split}|y-x|&\geq|y-x_{0}|-|x-x_{0}|\geq\biggl(1-\frac{r_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\biggr)|y-x_{0}|\geq 2^{-k-2}|y-x_{0}|\end{split}

for any
x\in B^{0}_{r^{*}_{k}}
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B^{0}_{r_{k}}
, we easily obtain that

(4.12)

{\mathcal{A}}(w_{k}^{*},\zeta_{k},r_{k},s)\leq c\,2^{k(n+ps)}\,r^{-ps}\,[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(w_{0};x_{0})]^{p-1}.

Since
0\leq w_{k}^{*}\leq w_{k}
and
w_{k}(x)\geq M_{k}^{*}-M_{k}=2^{-k-2}M_{*}
if
u(x)\geq M_{k}^{*}
, it follows from (4.10), (4.11) and (4.12) that

\begin{split}\biggl(\frac{2^{k}}{M_{*}}\biggr)^{-\frac{p(\gamma-1)}{\gamma}}N^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}_{k+1}&\leq c\,2^{pk}\fint_{B^{0}_{r_{k}}}|w^{*}_{k}|^{p}\,dx\\
&\qquad+c\,2^{k(n+ps)}\biggl(\frac{r_{k}}{r}\biggr)^{ps}\,[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(w_{0};x_{0})]^{p-1}\,\fint_{B^{0}_{r_{k}}}w^{*}_{k}\,dx\\
&\leq c\,2^{pk}N_{k}^{p}+c[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(w_{0};x_{0})]^{p-1}\,2^{k(n+ps)}\fint_{B^{0}_{r_{k}}}\frac{w^{*}_{k}w_{k}^{p-1}}{(M^{*}_{k}-M_{k})^{p-1}}\,dx\\
&\leq c\,\biggl(2^{pk}+2^{k(n+ps)}\biggl(\frac{2^{k}}{M_{*}}\biggr)^{p-1}\,[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(w_{0};x_{0})]^{p-1}\biggr)\,N_{k}^{p}.\end{split}

Taking
M^{*}
in the above so that

M_{*}\geq\delta\,{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(w_{0};x_{0})\,\,\text{ for $\delta\in(0,1]$,}

we obtain that

\frac{N_{k+1}}{M_{*}}\leq d_{0}\,a^{k}\,\biggl(\frac{N_{k}}{M_{*}}\biggr)^{1+\eta}

where
d_{0}=c^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}\delta^{-\frac{p-1}{p}\gamma}>0
,
a=2^{\frac{\gamma}{p}(n+2s+p-1)+\frac{ps}{n-ps}}>1
and
\eta=\gamma-1>0
.

If
N_{0}\leq d_{0}^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}a^{-\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}}M_{*},
then we set

M_{*}=\delta\,{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(w_{0};x_{0})+c_{0}\,\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}a^{\frac{(n-ps)^{2}}{p^{2}s^{2}}}N_{0}

where
c_{0}=c^{\frac{n}{sp^{2}}}
.
By Lemma 2.3, we conclude that

\begin{split}\sup_{B^{0}_{r}}u&\leq M+M_{*}\\
&\leq M+\delta\,{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(w_{0};x_{0})+c_{0}\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}a^{\frac{(n-ps)^{2}}{p^{2}s^{2}}}\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{2r}}(u-M)_{+}^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

Hence, taking
M\downarrow 0
in the above estimate, we obtain the required result. ∎

The third one is a lemma which furnishes a relation between the nonlocal tails of the positive and negative part of weak solutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1

(
\,p>1
,
s\in(0,1)
)
.
If
u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation
(1.3)
such that
u\geq 0
in
B^{0}_{R}\subset\Omega
, then we have the estimate

{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u_{+};x_{0})\lesssim\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)\,\sup_{B^{0}_{r}}u+\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{\frac{ps}{p-1}}{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};x_{0})

for any
r\in(0,R)
.

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{0}=0
.
Let
M=\sup_{B_{r}}u
and
\varphi(x)=w(x)\zeta^{p}(x)
where
w(x)=u(x)-2M
and
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{3r/4})
is a function satisfying that
\zeta|_{B_{r/2}}\equiv 1
,
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
and
|\nabla\zeta|\leq c/r
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then we have that

(4.13)

\begin{split}0&=\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\quad+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}H_{p}(u(x)-u(y))(u(x)-2M)\zeta^{p}(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}V(x)|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x)(u(x)-2M)\zeta^{p}(x)\,dx\\
&:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.\end{split}

Since the fact that

(4.14)

-2M\leq w(x):=u(x)-2M\leq-M

for any
x\in B_{r}
, by (a) of Lemma 2.4 we have that

H_{p}(w(x)-w(y))(w(x)\zeta^{p}(x)-w(y)\zeta^{p}(y))\geq-c_{p}4^{p}M^{p}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{p}

for any
x,y\in B_{r}
,
it follows from simple calculation that

(4.15)

\begin{split}J_{1}&\geq-c_{p}4^{p}M^{p}\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{p}\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\gtrsim-M^{p}r^{-ps}|B_{r}|.\end{split}

The lower estimate on
J_{2}
can be splitted as follows;

\begin{split}J_{2}&\geq 4\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}M(u(y)-M)^{p-1}_{+}\,\zeta^{p}(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\qquad-4M\int_{E_{M}}\int_{B_{r}}(u(x)-u(y))^{p-1}_{+}\,\zeta^{p}(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&:=J_{2,1}-J_{2,2},\end{split}

where
E_{M}=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}:u(y)<M\}
.
Since
(u(y)-M)_{+}\geq u_{+}(y)-M
, it follows from (c) of Lemma 2.4 that

(u(y)-M)^{p-1}_{+}\geq b_{p}u_{+}^{p-1}(y)-M^{p-1}

where
b_{p}=\mathbbm{1}_{(1,2]}(p)+2^{-(p-1)}\mathbbm{1}_{(2,\infty)}(p)
.
Thus the lower estimate on
J_{2,1}
can be obtained as

(4.16)

\begin{split}J_{2,1}&\geq d_{2}Mr^{-ps}|B_{r}|\,\bigl[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u_{+};0)\bigr]^{p-1}-d_{3}M^{p}r^{-ps}|B_{r}|\end{split}

with universal constants
d_{2},d_{3}>0
. If
x\in B_{r}
and
y\in E_{M}
, then we observe that

\begin{split}(u(x)-u(y))^{p-1}_{+}&\leq a_{p}\bigl(|u(x)-M|^{p-1}+|M-u(y)|^{p-1}\bigr)\\
&\leq a_{p}M^{p-1}+a_{p}(M+u_{-}(y)-u_{+}(y))^{p-1}\\
&\leq a_{p}M^{p-1}+a_{p}(M+u_{-}(y))^{p-1}\\
&\leq a_{p}(1+a_{p})M^{p-1}+a_{p}^{2}\,[u_{-}(y)]^{p-1}\end{split}

where
a_{p}=\mathbbm{1}_{(1,2]}(p)+2^{p-1}\mathbbm{1}_{(2,\infty)}(p)
,
because
u_{+}(y)<M+u_{-}(y)
for any
y\in E_{M}
.
Since
u_{-}(y)=0
for all
y\in B_{R}
, the upper estimate on
J_{2,2}
can thus be achieved by

(4.17)

\begin{split}J_{2,2}&\leq 4a_{p}(1+a_{p})M^{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{p}(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&+4a_{p}M\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{B_{r}}[u_{-}(y)]^{p-1}\zeta^{2}(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\leq d_{4}M^{p}r^{-ps}|B_{r}|+d_{5}MR^{-ps}|B_{r}|\,\bigl[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};0)\bigr]^{p-1}\end{split}

with universal constants
d_{4},d_{5}>0
. Thus, by (4.16) and (4.17), we have that

(4.18)

\begin{split}J_{2}&\geq-dM^{p}r^{-ps}|B_{r}|-dMR^{-ps}|B_{r}|\,\bigl[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};0)\bigr]^{p-1}\\
&\quad+eMr^{-ps}|B_{r}|\,\bigl[{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(u_{+};0)\bigr]^{p-1}\end{split}

where
d,e>0
are some universal constants depending only on
n,s,\lambda
and
\Lambda
.

Finally, it follows from (4.14), H
{\ddot{\rm o}}
lder’s inequality and the fractional Sobolev inequality that

(4.19)

\begin{split}J_{3}&\geq-2M^{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}V_{+}(x)\zeta^{p}(x)\,dx\\
&\geq-2M^{p}\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\biggl(\,\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{pq^{\prime}}(x)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\\
&\geq-2M^{p}\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\biggl(\,\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{\frac{pn}{n-ps}}(x)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{n-ps}{n}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{n-ps}{n}}\\
&\geq-2M^{p}\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{n-ps}{n}}\bigl(r^{-ps}\|\zeta\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+[\zeta]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{r})}\bigr)\\
&\gtrsim-\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}M^{p}r^{-ps}|B_{r}|\end{split}

where
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1
and
1<q^{\prime}<\frac{n}{n-ps}
with

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.

Hence the estimates (4.13), (4.15), (4.18) and (4.19) give the required estimate. ∎

Next we shall obtain the local boundedness for nonnegative weak subsolutions of the nonlocal equation (1.3) by employing Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 4.3. It is interesting that this estimate no longer depends on the nonlocal tail term, whose proof is pretty simple.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
V\in{\mathcal{P}}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
g\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1

(

p>1
,
s\in(0,1)

)
.
If
u\in{\text{\rm Y}}^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega)
is a nonnegative weak solution of the nonlocal
p
-Laplace type Schrödinger equation
(1.3)
, then we have the estimate

\sup_{B^{0}_{r}}u\leq C\,\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{2r}}u^{p}(x)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
r>0
with
B^{0}_{2r}\subset\Omega
.

Proof.
We choose some
\delta\in(0,1]
so that
1-\delta d_{0}>0
and take any
r>0
with
B^{0}_{2r}\subset\Omega
where

d_{0}=c_{0}(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})>0

for the universal constant
c_{0}>0
given in Lemma 4.3. Then it follows from Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 4.3 that

\begin{split}\sup_{B^{0}_{r}}u&\leq\delta\,d_{0}\biggl[\,\sup_{B^{0}_{r}}u+{\mathcal{T}}_{2r}(u^{-};x_{0}))\biggr]+C_{0}\,\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{0}_{2r}}u^{p}(x)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

Since
{\mathcal{T}}_{2r}(u^{-};x_{0})=0
, we can easily derive the required result by taking

C=\frac{C_{0}\,\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}}{1-\delta d_{0}}.

Hence we complete the proof. ∎

5.
The logarithm of a weak solution is a locally bounded mean oscillation function

In this section, we prove that the logarithm of a weak supersolution to the nonlocal equation (1.3) is a function with locally bounded mean oscillation. To do this, the following tool which is called the
fractional Poincaré inequality
is very useful.

Let
n\geq 1
,
p\geq 1
,
s\in(0,1)
and
sp<n
. For a ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let
u_{B}
denote the average of
u\in W^{s,p}(B)
over
B
, i.e.

u_{B}=\fint_{\,\,\,B}u(y)\,dy.

Then it was shown in [BBM, MS] that

(5.1)

\|u-u_{B}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B)}\leq\frac{c_{n,p}(1-s)|B|^{\frac{sp}{n}}}{(n-sp)^{p-1}}\,[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B)}

with a universal constant
c_{n,p}>0
depending only on
n
and
p
, which is usually very useful in getting the logarithmic estimate of weak supersolutions.
Of course, the logarithmic estimate could be obtained as in
[
DKP1
]
, but we will not apply their approach to achieve it. Our method to realize the logarithmic estimate is easier and more simple than their method.

[Proof of Theorem 1.2.]
For simplicity, we set
x_{0}=0
. So, in what follows, we write
B_{r}:=B^{0}_{r}
for
r>0
. Take any
r>0
so that
B_{2r}\subset B_{R}
where
B_{R}\subset\Omega
. Consider a radial function
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{3r/2})
with values in
[0,1]
such that

\zeta|_{B_{r}}\equiv 1
,
\zeta|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}}\equiv 0
and

|\nabla\zeta|\lesssim\frac{1}{r}\,\,\text{ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}$. }

We use the function

\varphi(x)=\frac{\zeta^{p}(x)}{u^{p-1}_{b}(x)}

as a testing function to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation (1.3), where
u_{b}(x)=u(x)+b
.
Then we have that

(5.2)

\begin{split}0&\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}H_{p}(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\,d_{K}(x,y)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}V(x)H_{p}(u(x))\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&=\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}H_{p}(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\quad\qquad+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}}\int_{B_{2r}}H_{p}(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))\,\varphi(x)\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\quad\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}V(x)|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&:=H(u,\varphi)+I(u,\varphi)+J(u,\varphi).\end{split}

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

u_{b}(x)\geq u_{b}(y)

for the estimate
H(u,\varphi)
; for, by symmetry, the other case
u_{b}(x)<u_{b}(y)
can be treated in the exactly same way.
Then we have two possible occasions; (a)
u_{b}(x)\leq 2u_{b}(y)
, (b)
u_{b}(x)>2u_{b}(y)
.

[Case (a) :
u_{b}(y)\leq u_{b}(x)\leq 2u_{b}(y)
] By the mean value theorem, we note that

(5.3)

\begin{split}\zeta(x)\geq\zeta(y)\,\,&\Rightarrow\,\,\zeta^{p}(x)-\zeta^{p}(y)=p\int_{\zeta(y)}^{\zeta(x)}\tau^{p-1}d\tau\leq p\zeta^{p-1}(x)(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)),\\
\zeta(x)<\zeta(y)\,\,&\Rightarrow\,\,\zeta^{p}(x)-\zeta^{p}(y)=p\int_{\zeta(x)}^{\zeta(y)}(-\tau^{p-1})d\tau\leq p\zeta^{p-1}(x)(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)).\end{split}

Then it follows from (5.3) that

(5.4)

\begin{split}\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)&=\frac{\zeta^{p}(x)-\zeta^{p}(y)}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}+\zeta^{p}(x)\,\biggl(\frac{1}{u_{b}^{p-1}(x)}-\frac{1}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}\biggr)\\
&\leq\frac{p\zeta^{p-1}(x)(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}\\
&\quad+\zeta^{p}(x)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\tau}\biggl(\frac{1}{[\tau(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))+u_{b}(y)]^{p-1}}\biggr)\,d\tau\\
&\leq\frac{p\zeta^{p-1}(x)(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}\\
&\quad\qquad-(p-1)\frac{\zeta^{p}(x)(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))}{u_{b}^{p}(x)}\\
&\leq\frac{p\zeta^{p-1}(x)\,|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|\,u_{b}(y)}{u_{b}^{p}(y)}\\
&\quad\qquad-\frac{(p-1)}{2^{p}}\,\frac{\zeta^{p}(x)(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))}{u_{b}^{p}(y)}.\end{split}

Applying Young’s inequality with indices
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1},p,\varepsilon
, it follows from (5.2) that

(5.5)

\begin{split}&H(u,\varphi)\leq c_{n,p,s}\Lambda p\\
&\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\frac{\varepsilon(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))^{p}\zeta^{p}(x)+c_{\varepsilon}|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}u_{b}^{p}(y)}{u_{b}^{p}(y)}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\\
&\qquad-\frac{c_{n,p,s}\lambda(p-1)}{2^{p}}\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\frac{(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))^{p}\zeta^{p}(x)}{u_{b}^{p}(y)}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}.\end{split}

If we choose
\varepsilon=\frac{\lambda(p-1)}{2^{p+1}p\Lambda}
in (5.5), then we have that

(5.6)

\begin{split}H(u,\varphi)&\lesssim-\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\zeta^{p}(x)\frac{(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))^{p}}{u_{b}^{p}(y)}\frac{\,dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\\
&\quad\qquad+\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\frac{|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\lesssim-\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\zeta^{p}(x)\frac{(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))^{p}}{u_{b}^{p}(y)}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}+r^{n-ps}.\end{split}

because
x,y\in B_{2r}
.
Since
0\leq u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y)\leq u_{b}(y)
, we have that

(5.7)

\begin{split}\bigl|\ln u_{b}(x)-\ln u_{b}(y)\bigr|^{p}&=\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y)}{\tau(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))+u_{b}(y)}\,d\tau\biggr)^{p}\\
&\leq\frac{(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))^{p}}{u^{p}_{b}(y)}.\end{split}

Thus by (5.6) and (5.7) we have that

(5.8)

\begin{split}H(u,\varphi)&\lesssim-\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\zeta^{p}(x)\,\biggl|\ln\,\biggl(\frac{u_{b}(x)}{u_{b}(y)}\biggr)\biggr|^{p}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}+r^{n-ps}\\
&\lesssim-\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}\,\biggl|\ln\,\biggl(\frac{u_{b}(x)}{u_{b}(y)}\biggr)\biggr|^{p}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}+r^{n-ps}.\end{split}

[Case (b) :
u_{b}(x)>2u_{b}(y)
] It follows from the inequality in Lemma 2.5 with
\varepsilon=(2^{p-1}-1)/2
that

(5.9)

\begin{split}\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)&=\frac{\zeta^{p}(x)-\zeta^{p}(y)}{u_{b}^{p-1}(x)}+\zeta^{p}(y)\,\biggl(\frac{1}{u_{b}^{p-1}(x)}-\frac{1}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}\biggr)\\
&\leq\frac{\zeta^{p}(x)-\zeta^{p}(y)}{u_{b}^{p-1}(x)}+\zeta^{p}(y)\,\biggl(\frac{1}{2^{p-1}u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}-\frac{1}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}\biggr)\\
&\leq\frac{\varepsilon\zeta^{p}(y)+c_{\varepsilon}|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}}{u_{b}^{p-1}(x)}-(1-2^{-p+1})\frac{\zeta^{p}(y)}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}\\
&\leq\frac{c\,|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}}{u_{b}^{p-1}(x)}-\biggl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\biggr)\,\frac{\zeta^{p}(y)}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}.\end{split}

Since
u_{b}(x)\geq u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y)\geq u_{b}(y),
by (5.2) and (5.9) we have that

(5.10)

\begin{split}\frac{H(u,\varphi)}{c_{n,p,s}}&\leq c\Lambda\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\frac{|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\quad-c\lambda\biggl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\biggr)\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\zeta^{p}(y)\,\frac{(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))^{p-1}}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}.\end{split}

Since we know that

(\ln t)^{p}\leq c\,(t-1)^{p-1}\,\,\text{ for $t>2$,}

we have that

(5.11)

\begin{split}\bigl|\ln u_{b}(x)-\ln u_{b}(y)\bigr|^{p}&\leq c\,\biggl(\frac{u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y)}{u_{b}(y)}\biggr)^{p-1}\\
&=c\,\frac{(u_{b}(x)-u_{b}(y))^{p-1}}{u_{b}^{p-1}(y)}.\end{split}

Combining (5.10) with (5.11), we have that

(5.12)

\begin{split}H(u,\varphi)&\lesssim-\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\zeta^{p}(y)\,\biggl|\ln\,\biggl(\frac{u_{b}(x)}{u_{b}(y)}\biggr)\biggr|^{p}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\iint_{B_{2r}\times B_{2r}}\frac{|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\lesssim-\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}\,\biggl|\ln\,\biggl(\frac{u_{b}(x)}{u_{b}(y)}\biggr)\biggr|^{p}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}+r^{n-ps}.\end{split}

Hence, by (5.8) and (5.12), we conclude that

(5.13)

H(u,\varphi)\lesssim-\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}\biggl|\ln\,\biggl(\frac{u_{b}(x)}{u_{b}(y)}\biggr)\,\biggr|\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}+r^{n-ps}.

For the estimate of
I(u,\varphi)
, we note that
(i)
u(y)\geq 0
and
u(x)-u(y)\leq u(x)
for
(x,y)\in B_{2r}\times(B_{R}\setminus B_{2r})
and (ii)
(u(x)-u(y))_{+}\leq u(x)+u_{-}(y)
for
(x,y)\in B_{2r}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R})
.
Since
\zeta
is supported in
B_{3r/2}
, the above observations (i) and (ii) yield that

(5.14)

\begin{split}I(u,\varphi)&\leq 2\,c_{n,p,s}\Lambda\int_{B_{3r/2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}}\frac{1}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+ps}}\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad+2\,c_{n,p,s}\Lambda\int_{B_{3r/2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u_{-}^{p-1}(y)}{u_{b}^{p-1}(x)}\,\frac{dy\,dx}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+ps}}\\
&\lesssim r^{n-ps}+\frac{r^{n-ps}}{b^{p-1}}\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{p-1}\end{split}

because we see
|y-x|\geq|y-x_{0}|-|x-x_{0}|\geq|y-x_{0}|/4
for any
(x,y)\in B_{3r/2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{2r})
.
Also, it follows from Hölder’s inequality and Proposition 2.1 that

(5.15)

\begin{split}J(u,\varphi)&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}V_{+}(x)\zeta^{p}(x)\,dx\\
&\leq\|V_{+}\|_{L^{\tau}(\Omega)}\biggl(\,\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p\tau^{\prime}}(x)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{\tau^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\biggl(\,\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{\frac{pn}{n-ps}}(x)\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{n-ps}{n}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{n-ps}{n}}\\
&\leq\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{n-ps}{n}}\bigl(r^{-ps}\|\zeta\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+[\zeta]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{r})}\bigr)\\
&\leq\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{n-ps}{n}}r^{n-ps}\lesssim\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,r^{n-ps}\end{split}

where
q>\frac{n}{ps}>1
,
p>1
and
1<q^{\prime}<\frac{n}{n-ps}
with

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.

By (5.13), (5.14) and (5.15), we obtain that

\begin{split}\iint_{B_{r}\times B_{r}}\,\biggl|\ln\biggl(\frac{u(x)+b}{u(y)+b}\biggr)\biggr|^{p}&\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\\
&\lesssim\frac{r^{n-ps}}{b^{p-1}}\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{p-1}+r^{n-ps}\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)\end{split}

for any
b\in(0,1)
and
r\in(0,R/2)
, since
x,y\in B_{2r}
.
Hence we complete the proof by applying (1.1). ∎

We now introduce a kind of local BMO spaces on
B^{0}_{R}\subset\Omega
, i.e.
{\text{\rm BMO}}^{p}(B^{0}_{R})
for
p>0
.
We define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}^{p}(B^{0}_{R})}
by

\|f\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}^{p}(B^{0}_{R})}=\sup_{r\in(0,R/2)}\biggl(\fint_{B^{0}_{r}}\bigl|f(y)-f_{B^{0}_{r}}\bigr|^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and the space

{\text{\rm BMO}}^{p}(B^{0}_{R})=\{f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\|f\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}^{p}(B^{0}_{R})}<\infty\}.

When
p=1
, we write
{\text{\rm BMO}}^{p}(B^{0}_{R})={\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})
.
Then we easily see that

(5.16)

\|\,|f|\,\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}\leq\|f\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}

because
\bigl||f|-|f|_{B^{0}_{r}}\bigr|\leq|f-f_{B^{0}_{r}}|
, and also

(5.17)

\|f\pm g\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}\leq\|f\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}+\|g\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}.

We now observe that

a\wedge b=\frac{a+b-|a-b|}{2}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,a\vee b=\frac{a+b+|a-b|}{2}

for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. This implies that

(5.18)

\begin{split}\|f\vee g\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}&\leq\|f\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}+\|g\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})},\\
\|f\wedge g\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}&\leq\|f\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}+\|g\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}.\end{split}

In addition, we can obtain the following
John-Nirenberg inequality
(as in
[
Gr
]
) by using the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition in harmonic analysis as follows; there exists some constants
b_{1},b_{2}>0
depending only on the dimension
n
such that

\bigl|\{x\in B^{0}_{r}:|f(x)-f_{B^{0}_{r}}|>\lambda\}\bigr|\leq b_{1}\,e^{-(b_{2}/\|f\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B_{R}^{0})})\lambda}|B^{0}_{r}|

for any
f\in{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{r})
, every
r>0
with
B^{0}_{2r}\subset B^{0}_{R}
and
B^{0}_{R}\subset\Omega
, and every
\lambda>0
. By standard analysis, this inequality makes it possible to easily show the following fact;

(5.19)

\begin{split}&\text{If $f\in{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})$ for $B^{0}_{R}\subset\Omega$ and $1<p<\infty$, then $\|\cdot\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}$ is }\\
&\,\,\text{ norm-equivalent to $\|\cdot\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}^{p}(B^{0}_{r})}$.}\end{split}

Lemma 5.1
.

If we set

v(x)=\displaystyle\ln\biggl(\frac{a+b}{u(x)+b}\biggr)\,\,\text{ for $a,b\in(0,1)$ }

where the function
u
satisfies the same assumption as Theorem 1.2, then we have that

\fint_{B^{0}_{r}}|v(x)-v_{B^{0}_{r}}|^{p}\,dx\lesssim\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})

for any
r\in(0,R/2)
,
where
{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})
is given by

{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})=\frac{1}{b^{p-1}}\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{p-1}+\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr).

It follows from this that
v\in{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})
and moreover

\|v\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}\leq[{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Proof.
The first part easily follows from the fractional Poincaré inequality (5.1) and Theorem 1.2.
Also the second part can be shown by applying the Remark of Theorem 1.1 and Hölder’s inequality because
u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. ∎

Corollary 5.2
.

If we set

\bar{v}=(v\vee 0)\wedge d\,\,\text{ for $d>0$ }

with the same
v
as Lemma 5.1, then we have that

\fint_{B^{0}_{r}}|\bar{v}(x)-\bar{v}_{B^{0}_{r}}|^{p}\,dx\lesssim\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})

for any
r\in(0,R/2)
,
where
{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})
is given by

{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})=\frac{1}{b^{p-1}}\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{p-1}+\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr).

It follows from this that
\bar{v}\in{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})
and moreover

\|\bar{v}\|_{{\text{\rm BMO}}(B^{0}_{R})}\leq[{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Proof.
Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{0}=0
. By Lemma 5.1, we have that

\fint_{B_{r}}\bigl||v(x)|-|v|_{B_{r}}\bigr|^{p}\,dx\lesssim\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};0),

because we see that

\bigl||v(x)|-|v|_{B_{r}}\bigr|\leq|v(x)-v_{B_{r}}|.

Then we can easily derive from (5.17) and (5.19) that

\fint_{B_{r}}|\bar{v}(x)-\bar{v}_{B_{r}}|\,dx\lesssim\,[{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};0)]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Finally, the second part can be done as in Lemma 5.1. Hence we complete the proof. ∎

6.
Interior H
\ddot{\rm o}
lder regularity

In this section, we establish an interior Hölder regularity of weak solutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian type Schrödinger equation (1.3) by applying the previous results obtained in Section 4 and Section 5.

[Proof of Theorem 1.3]
Fix any
p>1
and
0<s<1
and take any
R>0
with
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
.
For simplicity, without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{0}=0
.
For any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
r\in(0,R/2)
, we set

r_{k}=\frac{\delta^{k}r}{2}\text{ for $\delta\in\biggl(0,\bigl(\frac{1}{4}\bigr)^{\frac{p-1}{ps}}\biggr),$ $\,B_{k}=B_{r_{k}}$ and $\,B^{*}_{k}=B_{2r_{k}}$. }

Let us set

\displaystyle\Xi(r_{0})=2\,{\mathcal{T}}_{r/2}(u;0)+2\,C_{0}\,\biggl(\,\fint_{B_{r}}|u|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where
C_{0}>1
is the constant given in Theorem 1.1. As in the proof of Theorem 1.2, without loss of generality we may assume that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\frac{1}{4C_{0}}<1.

So we see that

0\leq\Xi(r_{0})<1.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, we set

\Xi(r_{k})=\biggl(\frac{r_{k}}{r_{0}}\biggr)^{\eta}\Xi(r_{0})

where
\eta\in(0,\frac{ps}{p-1})
is some constant to be determined later. For our proof, we have only to prove that

(6.1)

\underset{B_{k}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u\leq\Xi(r_{k})

for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
.

We proceed the proof by using the mathematical induction. By the remark of Theorem 1.1, we see that

\underset{B_{0}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u\leq\Xi(r_{0}).

Assume that (6.1) holds for all
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,m\}
. Then we will show that (6.1) is still true for
m+1
. For this proof, we consider two possible cases; either

(6.2)

\frac{\bigl|B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\bigl\{u\geq\displaystyle\inf_{B_{k}}u+\Xi(r_{k})/2\bigr\}\bigr|}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}\geq\frac{1}{2}

or

(6.3)

\frac{\bigl|B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\bigl\{u\leq\displaystyle\inf_{B_{k}}u+\Xi(r_{k})/2\bigr\}\bigr|}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,.

If (6.2) is true, then we set
u_{k}=u-\displaystyle\inf_{B_{k}}u
, and if (6.3) is true, then we set

u_{k}=\Xi(r_{k})-\bigl(u-\inf_{B_{k}}u\bigr).

In these two cases, we see that
u_{k}\geq 0
in
B_{k}
and

(6.4)

\frac{\bigl|B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{u_{k}\geq\Xi(r_{k})/2\}\bigr|}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,.

Furthermore,
u_{k}
is a weak solution satisfying that

(6.5)

\sup_{B_{k}}|u_{m}|\leq 2\,\Xi(r_{k})

for all
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,m\}
.
Under the induction hypothesis, if
m\geq 1
, then we now claim that

(6.6)

[{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{k}}(u_{k};0)]^{p-1}\leq c\,\delta^{-(p-1)\eta}\,[\Xi(r_{k})]^{p-1}

for all
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,m\}
.
Indeed, by (6.5) and the fact that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{0}}\frac{|u_{m}(x)|^{p-1}}{|x|^{n+ps}}\,dx&\lesssim r_{0}^{-ps}\sup_{B_{0}}|u|^{p-1}\\
&+r_{0}^{-ps}\,[\Xi(r_{0})]^{p-1}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{0}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{|x|^{n+ps}}\,dx\\
&\lesssim r_{1}^{-ps}\,\Xi(r_{0}),\end{split}

we have the following estimate

\begin{split}&[{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{m}}(u_{m};0)]^{p-1}=c\,r_{m}^{ps}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{B_{k-1}\setminus B_{k}}\frac{|u_{m}(x)|^{p-1}}{|x|^{n+ps}}\,dx+c\,r_{m}^{ps}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{0}}\frac{|u_{m}(x)|^{p-1}}{|x|^{n+ps}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\quad\lesssim r_{m}^{ps}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bigl[\,\sup_{B_{k-1}}|u_{m}|\,\bigr]^{p-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{k}}\frac{1}{|x|^{n+ps}}\,dx+\,r_{m}^{ps}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{0}}\frac{|u_{m}(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x|^{n+ps}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\quad\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\biggl(\frac{r_{m}}{r_{k}}\biggr)^{ps}[\Xi(r_{k-1})]^{p-1}=[\Xi(r_{0})]^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\biggl(\frac{r_{m}}{r_{k}}\biggr)^{ps}\biggl(\frac{r_{k-1}}{r_{0}}\biggr)^{(p-1)\eta}\\
&\qquad\quad=[\Xi(r_{0})]^{p-1}\biggl(\frac{r_{m}}{r_{0}}\biggr)^{(p-1)\eta}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\biggl(\frac{r_{m}}{r_{k}}\biggr)^{ps-(p-1)\eta}\biggl(\frac{r_{k-1}}{r_{k}}\biggr)^{(p-1)\eta}\\
&\qquad\quad=[\Xi(r_{m})]^{p-1}\delta^{-(p-1)\eta}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\delta^{(m-k)[ps-(p-1)\eta]}\\
&\qquad\quad\leq\delta^{-(p-1)\eta}\,\frac{\delta^{(m-1)[ps-(p-1)\eta]}}{1-\delta^{-[ps-(p-1)\eta]}}\,[\Xi(r_{m})]^{p-1}\lesssim\delta^{-(p-1)\eta}\,[\Xi(r_{m})]^{p-1}.\end{split}

For
k
and
d>0
, we set

v_{k}=\biggl[\ln\biggl(\frac{\Xi(r_{k})/2+b}{u_{k}+b}\biggr)\vee 0\biggr]\wedge d.

Applying Corollary 5.2 with
a=\Xi(r_{k})/2
,
b\in(0,1)
and
d>0
, we have that

(6.7)

\begin{split}\fint_{B^{*}_{k+1}}|v_{k}(x)-(v_{k})_{B^{*}_{k+1}}|^{p}\,dx\lesssim\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r_{k+1},r_{k}}((u_{k})_{-};0)\end{split}

where
{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})
is given by

{\mathfrak{R}}_{b,r,R}(u_{-};x_{0})=\frac{1}{b^{p-1}}\biggl(\frac{r}{R}\biggr)^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{R}(u_{-};x_{0})]^{p-1}+\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr).

If we set
b=\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k})
in (6.7), then by (5.19) and (6.6) we obtain that

(6.8)

\begin{split}&\fint_{B^{*}_{k+1}}|v_{k}(x)-(v_{k})_{B^{*}_{k+1}}|\,dx\\
&\quad\qquad\leq\,\biggl(\,\fint_{B^{*}_{k+1}}|v_{k}(x)-(v_{k})_{B^{*}_{k+1}}|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\quad\qquad\leq c\,\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

where
c>0
is the constant depending only on
n,s,p,\eta,\lambda
and
\Lambda
.
From (6.4), we can derive the following estimate

(6.9)

\begin{split}d&=\frac{1}{|B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{u_{k}\geq\Xi(r_{k})/2\}|}\int_{B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{u_{k}\geq\Xi(r_{k})/2\}}d\,\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{|B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{u_{k}\geq\Xi(r_{k})/2\}|}\int_{B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{v_{k}=0\}}d\,\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{2}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}\int_{B^{*}_{k+1}}(d-v_{k})\,dx=2\bigl(d-(v_{k})_{B^{*}_{k+1}}\bigr).\end{split}

The estimates (6.8) and (6.9) make it possible to obtain the estimate

(6.10)

\begin{split}\frac{|B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{v_{k}=d\}|}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}\,d&\leq\frac{2}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}\int_{B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{v_{k}=d\}}\bigl(d-(v_{k})_{B^{*}_{k+1}}\bigr)\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{2}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}\int_{B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{v_{k}=d\}}\bigl(v_{k}-(v_{k})_{B^{*}_{k+1}}\bigr)\,dx\\
&\lesssim\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

We now set

d=d_{*}:=\ln\,\biggl(\frac{\Xi(r_{k})/2+\delta^{2s-\gamma}\,\Xi(r_{k})}{3\,\delta^{2s-\gamma}\,\Xi(r_{k})}\biggr).

Then we see that
d_{*}\sim\ln(1/\delta)
. By (6.10), we have that

(6.11)

\begin{split}\frac{|B^{*}_{k+1}\cap\{u_{k}\leq 2\,\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k})\}|}{|B^{*}_{k+1}|}&\leq\frac{c\,\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{d_{*}}\\
&\leq\frac{c_{0}\,\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\ln(1/\delta)}.\end{split}

Now we proceed the next step with a well-known iteration process as follows.
For
i\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, we set

\rho_{i}=(1+2^{-i})r_{k+1},\,\bar{\rho}_{i}=\frac{\rho_{i}+\rho_{i+1}}{2},\,B_{i}=B_{\rho_{i}}\,\text{ and }\,{\bar{B}}_{i}=B_{\bar{\rho}_{i}}.

For
i\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, we consider a function
\zeta_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\bar{\rho}_{i}})
with
\zeta_{i}|_{B_{\rho_{i+1}}}\equiv 1
such that
0\leq\zeta_{i}\leq 1
and
|\nabla\zeta_{i}|\leq c\rho_{i}^{-1}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Moreover we set

d_{i}=(1+2^{-i})\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k})
and
w_{i}=(d_{i}-u_{k})_{+}
and

N_{i}=\frac{|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|}{|B_{i}|}=\frac{|B_{i}\cap\{w_{i}\geq 0\}|}{|B_{i}|}.

From (6.11), we see that

(6.12)

N_{0}\leq\frac{c_{0}\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\ln(1/\delta)}.

By Theorem 1.5, we have that

\begin{split}[w_{i}\zeta_{i}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{\rho_{i}})}&\lesssim\iint_{B_{\rho_{i}}\times B_{\rho_{i}}}[w_{i}(x)\vee w_{i}(y)]^{p}|\zeta_{i}(x)-\zeta_{i}(y)|^{p}\,d_{K}(x,y)\\
&\qquad+\biggl(\,\sup_{x\in\text{\rm supp}(\zeta_{i})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho_{i}}}[w_{i}(y)]^{p-1}\,K(x-y)\,dy\biggr)\|w_{i}\zeta_{i}^{p}\|_{L^{1}(B_{i})}\\
&\quad:=A(\rho_{i},w_{i},\zeta_{i})+B(\rho_{i},w_{i},\zeta_{i}).\end{split}

Then we have the following estimate

(6.13)

\begin{split}A(\rho_{i},w_{i},\zeta_{i})&\lesssim\,d_{i}^{p}\int_{B_{\rho_{i}}}\int_{B_{\rho_{i}}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}}\frac{\sup_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla\zeta_{i}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps-p}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\lesssim\,d_{i}^{p}\biggl(\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}\biggr)^{p}\int_{B_{\rho_{i}}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}}\int_{B_{2\rho_{i}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{n+2s-2}}\,dy\,dx\\
&\lesssim\,\,d_{i}^{p}\,\rho_{i}^{-ps}\,|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|.\end{split}

From the fact that

|y-x|\geq|y|-|x|\geq\biggl(1-\frac{\bar{\rho}_{i}}{\rho_{i}}\biggr)|y|\geq 2^{-i-2}|y|

for any
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho_{i}}
and
x\in B_{\bar{\rho}_{i}}
, we obtain that

(6.14)

\begin{split}B(\rho_{i},w_{i},\zeta_{i})&\lesssim\,d_{i}\,2^{i(n+ps)}\,|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho_{i}}}\frac{|w_{i}(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{n+ps}}\,dy\\
&\lesssim\,2^{i(n+ps)}\,d_{i}\,\rho_{i}^{-ps}\,|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|\,[{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{k+1}}(w_{i};0)]^{p-1}.\end{split}

Thus it follows from (6.13) and (6.14) that

(6.15)

\begin{split}&[w_{i}\zeta_{i}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{\rho_{i}})}\\
&\quad\lesssim\,\bigl(\,d_{i}^{p}+2^{i(n+ps)}\,d_{i}\,[{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{k+1}}(w_{i};0)]^{p-1}\bigr)\rho_{i}^{-ps}\,|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|.\end{split}

From (6.6) and the facts that
w_{i}\leq 2\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k})
in
B_{k}
and
w_{i}\leq|u_{k}|+2\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we can derive that

(6.16)

\begin{split}&[{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{k+1}}(w_{i};0)]^{p-1}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\,r^{ps}_{k+1}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}\frac{|w_{i}(y,t)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{n+ps}}\,dy+\biggl(\frac{r_{k+1}}{r_{k}}\biggr)^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{k}}(w_{i};0)]^{p-1}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\,\delta^{ps-(p-1)\eta}\,[\Xi(r_{k})]^{p-1}+\delta^{ps}[{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{k}}(u_{k};(0,0))]^{p-1}\lesssim\,d_{i}^{p-1}.\end{split}

Thus by (6.15) and (6.16), we have that

(6.17)

\begin{split}[w_{i}\zeta_{i}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{\rho_{i}})}\lesssim\,2^{i(n+ps)}\,d^{p}_{i}\,\rho_{i}^{-ps}\,|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|.\end{split}

By applying (6.17) and the fractional Sobolev’s inequality with the exponent

\gamma=\frac{n}{n-ps},

we can deduce the following inequalities

(6.18)

\begin{split}\biggl(\int_{B_{i+1}}|w_{i}|^{p\gamma}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}&\leq\biggl(\int_{B_{i}}|w_{i}\zeta_{i}|^{\frac{pn}{n-ps}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{n-ps}{n}}\\
&\lesssim[w_{i}\zeta_{i}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{\rho_{i}})}+\rho^{-ps}_{i}\|w_{i}\zeta_{i}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{\rho_{i}})}\\
&\lesssim\,2^{i(n+ps)}\,d^{p}_{i}\,\rho_{i}^{-ps}\,|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|.\end{split}

Since
|B_{i+1}|\sim\rho_{i}^{n}\sim|B_{i}|
and

w_{i}=(d_{i}-u_{k})_{+}\geq(d_{i}-d_{i+1})\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{u_{k}\leq d_{i+1}\}}\geq 2^{-i-2}d_{i}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{u_{k}\leq d_{i+1}\}},

this estimate (6.18) yields that

\begin{split}(d_{i}-d_{i+1})^{p}\biggl(\frac{|B_{i+1}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i+1}|}{|B_{i+1}|}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}&\leq\frac{\rho_{i}^{ps}}{|B_{i}|}\biggl(\int_{B_{i+1}}|w_{i}|^{p\gamma}\,dx\,dt\biggr)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\\
&\lesssim\,2^{i(n+ps)}\,d^{p}_{i}\,\frac{|B_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\leq d_{i}\}|}{|B_{i}|},\end{split}

which gives that

N_{i+1}^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\leq c\,\frac{2^{i(n+ps)}\,d_{i}^{p}}{(d_{i}-d_{i+1})^{p}}\,N_{i}\leq c\,2^{i(n+ps+p)}N_{i}.

This leads us to obtain that

N_{i+1}\leq c_{1}\,2^{i\gamma(n+ps+p)}\,N_{i}^{1+\frac{ps}{n-ps}}.

If we could show that

(6.19)

N_{0}=\frac{|B_{0}\cap\{u_{k}\leq 2\,\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k})\}|}{|B_{0}|}\leq c_{1}^{-\frac{n-ps}{ps}}2^{-\frac{\gamma(n-ps)^{2}(n+ps+p)}{p^{2}s^{2}}}:=c_{*},

then by Lemma 2.2 we conclude that
\lim_{i\to\infty}N_{i}=0
, i.e.

\inf_{B_{k+1}}u_{k}>\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k}).

In order to guarantee (6.19), by (6.12) we observe that

c_{*}\geq\frac{c_{0}\bigl(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\ln(1/\delta)}\,\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,\,\delta\leq e^{-(c_{0}/c_{*})(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})^{1/p}},

and so we choose
\delta>0
as

\delta=e^{-(c_{0}/c_{*})(1+\|V_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})^{1/p}}\wedge\bigl(\frac{1}{4}\bigr)^{\frac{p-1}{ps}}.

If
u_{k}=u-\inf_{B_{k}}u
, then by (6.1) we have that

(6.20)

\underset{B_{k+1}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u=\underset{B_{k+1}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u_{k}\leq\underset{B_{k}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u-\inf_{B_{k+1}}u_{k}\leq(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta})\,\Xi(r_{k}).

If
u_{k}=\Xi(r_{k})-(u-\inf_{B_{k}}u)
, then we have that

(6.21)

\underset{B_{k+1}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u=\underset{B_{k+1}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u_{k}=\Xi(r_{k})-\inf_{B_{k+1}}u+\inf_{B_{k}}u-\inf_{B_{k+1}}u_{k}\leq(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta})\,\Xi(r_{k}).

From (6.20) and (6.21), we obtain that

(6.22)

\begin{split}\underset{B_{k+1}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u&\leq(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta})\,\Xi(r_{k})=(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta})\biggl(\frac{r_{k}}{r_{k+1}}\biggr)^{\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k+1})\\
&=(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta})\delta^{-\eta}\,\Xi(r_{k+1}).\end{split}

To find
\eta
so that
(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta})\delta^{-\eta}\leq 1
, we consider the function

\xi(\eta)=\delta^{\eta}+\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}.

We note that

\xi^{\prime}(\eta)=\delta^{\eta}\ln\delta\,\bigl(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-2\eta}\bigr)=0\,\,\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,\,\,\eta=\frac{ps}{2(p-1)}:=\eta_{p,s}\,,

\xi^{\prime}(\eta)<0
for
\eta<\eta_{p,s}
and
\xi^{\prime}(\eta)>0
for
\eta>\eta_{p,s}
. These facts imply that the graph of
\xi
is going down from the point
(0,1+\delta^{2\eta_{p,s}})
to the point
(\eta_{p,s},1+2\delta^{\eta_{p,s}})
and is going up the point
(2\eta_{p,s},1+\delta^{2\eta_{p,s}})
right after that. Since we see that

2\delta^{\eta_{p,s}}<1+\delta^{2\eta_{p,s}}\,\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\,2\delta^{\eta_{p,s}}<1,

we can find exactly two
\eta
’s inside
(0,\frac{ps}{p-1})
so that

(6.23)

\delta^{\eta}+\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta}=1.

If we set
Y=\delta^{\eta}
, then the above equation (6.23) will be transformed into

\frac{Y^{2}-Y+\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}}}{Y}=0

and its solutions are

\delta^{\eta}=Y=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}}}}{2}\in(0,1),

because
\delta<(1/4)^{\frac{p-1}{ps}}
. Hence it turns out that the solutions of (6.23) are

\eta_{0}^{\pm}=\frac{\ln\biggl(\,\displaystyle\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}}}}{2}\,\biggr)}{\ln\delta}.

Then we see that
\xi(\eta)\geq 1
, i.e.
(1-\delta^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\eta})\delta^{-\eta}\leq 1
for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0}^{-}]\cup[\eta_{0}^{+},2\eta_{p,s})
. Thus, if
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0}^{-}]\cup[\eta_{0}^{+},2\eta_{p,s})
, then by (6.22) we conclude that

\underset{B_{k+1}}{\text{\rm osc}}\,u\leq\Xi(r_{k+1}).

Therefore we complete the proof. ∎

Abstract
. Let E ⊂ Ω E\subset\Omega be a local almost-minimizer of the relative perimeter in the open set Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} . We prove a free-boundary monotonicity inequality for E E at a point x ∈ ∂ Ω x\in\partial\Omega , under a geometric property called “visibility”, that Ω \Omega is required to satisfy in a neighborhood of x x . Incidentally, the visibility property is satisfied by a considerably large class of Lipschitz and possibly non-smooth domains. Then, we prove the existence of the density of the relative perimeter of E E at x x , as well as the fact that any blow-up of E E at x x is necessarily a perimeter-minimizing cone within the tangent cone to Ω \Omega at x x .

1.
Introduction

Consider an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary, and fix
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. The main goal of this work is to prove a free-boundary monotonicity inequality for a local almost-minimizer
E\subset\Omega
of the relative perimeter at the boundary point
x_{0}
, under a suitable geometric property of
\partial\Omega
near
x_{0}
, which we call “visibility”.

Monotonicity inequalities are key tools in the regularity theory for minimizers and almost-minimizers of the area functional. In the prototypical setting of
E
being a local perimeter minimizer near
0\in\Omega
(or even just a critical point of the perimeter functional) and given
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
such that the ball
B_{r_{2}}
of radius
r_{2}
and center
0
is contained in
\Omega
, it is known that

(1.1)

\int_{(B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}})\cap\partial^{*}E}\frac{\left\langle x,\nu_{E}\right\rangle^{2}}{|x|^{n+1}}d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(x)\leq\frac{P(E;B_{r_{2}})}{r_{2}^{n-1}}-\frac{P(E;B_{r_{1}})}{r_{1}^{n-1}}\,,

where
P(E;B_{r})
is the perimeter of
E
in
B_{r}
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
is the Hausdorff
(n-1)
-dimensional measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\partial^{*}E
is the reduced boundary of
E
, and
\nu_{E}
is the weak interior normal defined for
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in\partial^{*}E
.

The first, fundamental consequences of an inequality like (
1.1
) are:

(i)

the monotonicity of the renormalized perimeter
r\mapsto\frac{P(E;B_{r})}{r^{n-1}}
, which turns out to be a non-decreasing function admitting a finite limit as
r\to 0
(denoted as
\theta_{E}(0)
, the perimeter density of
E
at
0
);

(ii)

the fact that, if a perimeter minimizer has a constant renormalized perimeter, then the left-hand side of (
1.1
) vanishes for all
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
and consequently
E
coincides up to null sets with a cone with vertex at the origin.

These two facts allow the application of the monotonicity formula in several steps of the proof of the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of the reduced boundary
\partial^{*}E
, as well as in the analysis of the singular set
\partial E\setminus\partial^{*}E
. After the pioneering work of De Giorgi
[
10
]
on the partial regularity of local perimeter minimizers, the internal regularity theory has been successfully extended to (rectifiable) sets of varying topological type
[
26
]
, area-minimizing integral currents
[
17
,
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, and varifolds
[
3
,
1
]
. In the codimension-
1
case, in particular, the dimension of the singular set is sharply estimated as
\leq n-8
, after the work of Bombieri-De Giorgi-Giusti
[
7
]
combined with a dimension-reduction argument due to Federer
[
29
]
. In these works, monotonicity formulas arise in specific but substantially equivalent forms, through proofs based either on comparison with cones or by testing the first variation of area with suitable radially-symmetric vector fields.

A key feature of regularity theory is its stability under perturbations that, at small scales, have a higher order of infinitesimality than area. This has led to the extension of the regularity theory to wider classes of almost-minimizers, including for instance minimizers of isoperimetric or prescribed mean curvature problems (see, e.g.,
[
25
,
28
,
8
,
19
,
30
]
).

Coming to the boundary properties of almost-minimizers, we record regularity results proved for integral currents minimizing parametric elliptic integrals and
k
-varifolds with mean curvature in
L^{p}
for
p>k
, assuming
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of their boundary
[
2
,
14
,
9
]
. In the free-boundary case, regularity results have been proved for area-minimizing currents and varifolds when the domain
\Omega
is either of class
C^{2}

[
22
,
21
,
20
,
23
]
or, more recently, a local
C^{2}
-deformation of a wedge-type polyhedral cone
[
15
]
. In these cases, monotonicity properties of the renormalized area are shown by testing with locally constructed, almost-radial vector fields that are smooth and tangent to
\partial\Omega
.

The background motivation of this paper is the study of the free-boundary properties and regularity of perimeter almost-minimizers when the boundary of the domain
\Omega
is Lipschitz but not necessarily smooth. We aim to consider
\Omega
that might not be locally of class
C^{1,\alpha}
or coincide with a smooth deformation of a polyhedral wedge (as assumed in
[
15
]
). Carrying over the full regularity program in such a general setting seems particularly challenging. For this reason, in this paper, we focus on the monotonicity property as a preliminary step towards the extension of the free-boundary regularity results mentioned so far.

1.1.
Description of the main definitions and results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set with Lipschitz boundary and
E\subset\Omega
be a measurable set. In what follows,
E
will be called a local almost-minimizer of the relative perimeter in
\Omega
if, for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
there exists
r_{x}>0
such that, for any
0<r<r_{x}
and any measurable
F\subset\Omega
with
F\Delta E\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
, one has

P(E;B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\leq P(F;B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)+|F\Delta E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)\,,

for a suitable function
\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)
such that
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)=0
. Of course, when the error term
|F\Delta E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)
vanishes, we have a local perimeter minimizer. We conveniently introduce the following, classical notation. Given a function
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
1
1

1

Here we mean that
f\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
and has bounded variation in
\Omega\cap A
, for any bounded set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
, we define the
minimality gap
of
f
in
A
as

\Psi_{\Omega}(f;A)=|Df(\Omega\cap A)|-\inf\big\{|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)\,:\,g\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega),\ \mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-f)\subset\!\subset A\big\}

and, when
f=\mathbf{1}_{E}
, we set
\Psi_{\Omega}(E;A):=\Psi_{\Omega}(\mathbf{1}_{E};A)
. If
E
is an almost-minimizer and
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, it is immediate to check that
\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}(x))\lesssim r^{n-1}\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)
. The two main applications of Theorem
5.2
(our general monotonicity inequality) are Corollary
5.3
and Theorem
6.1
. For them, we will need to assume that the minimality gap on balls centered at
0\in\partial\Omega
decays to
0
suitably fast as
r\to 0^{+}
, which is expressed through the following summability property:

(1.2)

\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n}}\,d\rho\simeq\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{\psi_{\Omega}(E;0,r)}{\rho}\,d\rho<+\infty\,.

Several notions of almost-minimality are available in the literature. Among those where the minimality error is in additive form, like ours, we mention the well-known
\Lambda
-minimality
[
5
,
24
]
, where the minimality error is estimated by
\Lambda|F\Delta E|
for some
\Lambda>0
, which is a special case of our definition. We also remark that the definition of almost-minimality given by Tamanini in
[
31
]
2
2

2

in Tamanini’s definition, the term
|F\Delta E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}
is replaced by
r^{n-1}
.
is slightly more general than ours, even though we take direct inspiration from it. The reason why we are forced to consider a slightly stronger almost-minimality is that, in the boundary case, it seems impossible to use the monotonicity inequality to prove lower-density estimates for the perimeter and the volume of
E
at
x\in\partial\Omega
, as it happens for the interior case. On the other hand, a local almost-minimizer in our sense turns out to fulfill volume and perimeter density estimates directly (see Lemma
3.8
). We point out that these estimates are crucially used in the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

The key assumption needed for the proof of our monotonicity formula is the aforementioned visibility property, that we now briefly describe. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set with Lipschitz boundary such that
0\in\partial\Omega
and in graphical form around
0
with respect to the variables
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},...,x_{n-1})
, so that
\Omega
is locally the epigraph of some Lipschitz function of
x^{\prime}
. We say that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property at
0
if there exist
R>0
and a function
v\in C^{1}([0,R))
such that:

(V1)

v(0)=v^{\prime}(0)=0
and
0\leq v^{\prime}\leq 2^{-1}
;

(V2)

The function

\gamma_{v}(r):=r^{-1}\sup_{0<s\leq r}\sqrt{\frac{v(s)}{s}+v^{\prime}(s)}

is summable on
(0,R)
;

(V3)

for all
0<r<R
, the segment joining the point
V_{r}=(0,\dots,0,-v(r))
with a point
x
belonging to
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}
does not intersect
\Omega
.

The visibility property allows us to construct a quasi-conical competitor for a local almost-minimizer of the relative perimeter in
\Omega
, which is a key step in the proof of our monotonicity inequality. It is worth observing that this property guarantees the existence of the tangent cone to
\Omega
at
0
(Proposition
4.5
). The visibility property at
0
is satisfied for instance by cones with vertex at
0
(with the trivial choice
v\equiv 0
), by
C^{1,\beta}
open sets and also by convex sets suitably approximated by their tangent cone at
0
; one can also easily construct examples of Lipschitz domains that do not satisfy the property, (see Section
4.4
for more details).
Another consequence of the visibility property is the existence of a foliation of a neighborhood of
0
by spheres with varying centers, which correspond to the level-sets of a function
\phi
of class
C^{1}
, i.e., such that
\phi^{-1}(r)=\partial B_{r}(V_{r})
. Moreover, by (V1) and (V2) one can show that
\phi
is
C^{1}
-close to the function
|x|
(see Lemma
4.9
).

In our main result, Theorem
5.2
, we establish the following monotonicity inequality for
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
, under the visibility property:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\phi^{1-n}\left|\left\langle\nu_{f},\nabla\phi\right\rangle\right|\,d|Df|\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\frac{|\nabla\phi(x)|}{\phi(x)^{n-1}}\,d|Df|\right)

(1.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\bigg[\mu_{f}(r_{2})-\mu_{f}(r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))d\rho+G(f;r_{1},r_{2})\bigg]\,.

Here,
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
are sufficiently small,
\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}=B_{r_{2}}(V_{r_{2}})\setminus B_{r_{1}}(V_{r_{1}})
,
\nu_{f}
represents the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
Df
with respect to its total variation
|Df|
,

\mu_{f}(r)=\frac{|Df|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))}{r^{n-1}}\,,

and
G(f;r_{1},r_{2})
is an error term whose precise expression is given in (
5.1
), which depends on the properties of the boundary of
\Omega
, and goes to
0
when
r_{2}\to 0
as soon as

(1.4)

\mu_{f}(r)\leq C\,,\qquad\text{for all $r>0$ small enough and for some $C>0$.}

Inequality (
1.3
) has important consequences when
f=\mathbf{1}_{E}
and
E
is a local almost-minimizer of the relative perimeter in
\Omega
satisfying the assumption (
1.2
).
Indeed, in this case, the above-mentioned density estimates guarantee that (
1.4
) holds. We then infer that the function

\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))d\rho+G(f;0,r)

is infinitesimal as
r\to 0^{+}
. Consequently, the density ratio
\mu_{f}(r)
is almost-monotone, and hence it admits a finite limit
\theta_{E}(0)
as
r\to 0^{+}
(see Corollary
5.3
).

Finally, the left-hand side of (
1.3
) can be interpreted as an approximate conical deviation term, since it is as small as
E
is close to being a cone with vertex at
0
(at least when
\phi(x)=|x|
, i.e. if the visibility property at
0
is satisfied with
v\equiv 0
). This observation is exploited in the proof of the last Theorem
6.1
, where we show that any blow-up sequence of an almost-minimizer of the relative perimeter in
\Omega
admits a subsequence that converges to a minimizing cone in the tangent cone to
\Omega
at
0
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
. For
i=1,...,n
, we denote by
e_{i}
the
i
-th vector of the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, their Euclidean product is
\langle x,y\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
, while the Euclidean norm of
x
is
|x|:=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
. When needed, we will write
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
, where
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},...,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. We let
B_{r}(x)
be the open ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we set
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
.
Similarly, we let
B^{\prime}_{r}(x^{\prime})
be the
(n-1)
-dimensional open ball with center
x^{\prime}
and radius
r
. We denote by either
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(E)
or
|E|
the Lebesgue measure of
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we set
\omega_{n}:={\mathcal{L}}^{n}(B_{1})
.
Given a measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

A^{(t)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{|E\cap B_{r}(x)|}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}=t\right\}\,,\qquad\text{for all $0\leq t\leq 1$ \, .}

Given two non-empty, bounded subsets
A
,
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}}(A,B)
the Hausdorff distance between
A
and
B
, that is

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}}(A,B)=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in A}\,\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,B)\,,\,\sup_{y\in B}\,\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(y,A)\right\}\,,

where
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,Z):=\inf_{z\in Z}|x-z|
. Note that the Hausdorff distance becomes a proper distance function when restricted to compact sets (see
[
27
]
).

Given
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
open and a vector-valued Radon measure
\mu=(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{p})
on
\Omega
, we denote by
|\mu|
its total variation. Let
u=(u_{1},...,u_{p}):\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
be summable with respect to
|\mu|
, then
u\cdot\mu
denotes the Radon measure defined by

u\cdot\mu(E):=\int_{E}u\cdot d\mu=\sum_{q=1}^{p}\int_{E}u_{q}\,d\mu_{q}\,.

It can be proved (see
[
6
, Proposition 1.23]
) that

|u\cdot\mu|=|u|\cdot\mu\,.

Given a Lipschitz map
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(g)
its Lipschitz constant, and we denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of Lipschitz real-valued functions. We record for future reference the following, elementary fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\sup_{j\geq 1}\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(f_{j})<\infty
. Assume further that
f_{j}(x)\rightarrow f(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
f_{j}\rightarrow f
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Given
f\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
, we define

|Df|(\Omega):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\phi(x)\,dx\,:\,\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,\,||\phi||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\right\}\,.

We denote by
BV(\Omega)
the space of functions
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
with the property that
|Df|(\Omega)<\infty
. In other words, a function
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
belongs to
BV(\Omega)
if and only if its distributional gradient
Df
is represented by a
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Radon measure with finite total variation.

When
E
is a measurable set, we define the perimeter of
E
in
\Omega
as

P(E;\Omega):=|D\mathbf{1}_{E}|(\Omega)\,,

where
\mathbf{1}_{E}
is the characteristic function of
E
. We say that
E
has
finite perimeter
in
\Omega
provided
P(E;\Omega)<+\infty
. In the sequel, we will often use the following notation: given another open set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

P_{\Omega}(E;A):=P(E;A\cap\Omega)\,,

with the short form
P_{\Omega}(E):=P_{\Omega}(E;A)
whenever
A
contains
\Omega
. We call
P_{\Omega}
the relative perimeter in
\Omega
, and
P_{\Omega}(E;A)
the relative perimeter of
E
in
\Omega
restricted to
A
.

For the specific purposes of this paper, it is convenient to define
BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
as the space of functions
f\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
such that
f\in BV(\Omega\cap A)
for all open, bounded sets
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We remark that
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
implies that
f
has locally bounded variation in
\Omega
, but the converse does not necessarily hold.

Let now
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. As a consequence of
[
24
, Corollary 5.11]
we can consider the density of
Df
with respect to
|Df|
defined by

\nu_{f}(x)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{Df(B_{r}(x))}{|Df|(B_{r}(x))}\,,\qquad\text{for $|Df|$-a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

and satisfying
Df=\nu_{f}\cdot|Df|
and
|\nu_{f}(x)|=1
for
|Df|
-almost every
x\in\Omega
. When
E
has locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
, we set
\nu_{E}=\nu_{\mathbf{1}_{E}}
.

Next we recall some well-known facts concerning the total variation functional,
BV
functions, and sets of locally finite perimeter. For further details, the reader can consult, e.g.,
[
24
,
16
,
18
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Lower-semicontinuity)
.

Let
f,f_{j}\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
f_{j}\to f
in
L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. Then

|Df|(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{j}|Df_{j}|(\Omega).

BV functions can be approximated by smooth functions in a suitable sense.

Theorem 2.3

(Approximation)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
f\in BV(\Omega)
. Then there exists a sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap BV(\Omega)
such that

(2.1)

||f_{j}-f||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\,,\qquad|Df_{j}|(\Omega)\rightarrow|Df|(\Omega)\,.

Moreover, provided (
2.1
) holds, we also have
Df_{j}\rightharpoonup^{\ast}Df
, i.e., for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi\cdot dDf_{j}\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi\cdot dDf\,,\qquad\text{as }j\rightarrow\infty\,.

Remark 2.4
.

If property (
2.1
) holds, we say that the sequence
f_{j}

strictly converges
to
f
(see
[
6
, Definition 3.14]
).
Thus Theorem
2.3
tells us in particular that
C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap BV(\Omega)
is dense in
BV(\Omega)
with respect to the strict convergence.
Moreover, the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
can be chosen in such a way that the following extra property is satisfied (see
[
18
, Remark 1.18]
)

(2.2)

\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0^{+}}\rho^{-N}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|f_{j}-f|dx=0\,,\qquad\text{for all $N>0$, $x_{0}\in\partial\Omega$, $j\in\mathbb{N}\,.$}

Theorem 2.5

(Compactness)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded set with Lipschitz boundary, let
C>0
be a fixed constant, and for
j\in\mathbb{N}
let
f_{j}\in BV(\Omega)
be such that

\|f_{j}\|_{BV(\Omega)}:=\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+|Df_{j}|(\Omega)\leq C\,,\qquad\text{for every $j$.}

Then there exists
f\in BV(\Omega)
and a subsequence
f_{j_{k}}
of
f_{j}
such that

\|f_{j_{k}}-f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\,.

Theorem 2.6

(Coarea for
BV
functions)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let

E_{t}=\{x\in\Omega\,:\,f(x)>t\}\,.

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

if
f\in BV(\Omega)
, then
E_{t}
has finite perimeter for almost every

,

is measurable, and

|Df|(\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}P(E_{t};\Omega)\,dt\,;

(ii)

conversely, if
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
t\mapsto P(E_{t};\Omega)
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}P(E_{t};\Omega)\,dt<+\infty\,,

then
f\in BV(\Omega)
.

Theorem 2.7

(Coarea for Lipschitz functions)
.

If
\phi\in\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{E}f|\nabla\phi|=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{E\cap\{\phi=t\}}f\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\,dt\,,

where
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
denotes the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure in

.

We will also need the following well-known result, which proof can be found in

Theorem 2.8

(Area Formula)

Let

{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
-rectifiable set and

(i)

for

, the restriction of

3.1.
Some estimates for the minimality gap

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
,
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open sets and
E\subset\Omega
be such that
\mathbf{1}_{E}\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. Then

\Psi_{\Omega}(E;A)=P_{\Omega}(E;A)-\inf\big\{P_{\Omega}(F;A)\,:\,\mathbf{1}_{F}\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)\,,\,F\Delta E\subset\!\subset A\big\}\,.

Proof.

Let us define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}=\inf\{|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)\,:\,g\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)\,,\,\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-\mathbf{1}_{E})\subset\!\subset A\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}=\inf\{P_{\Omega}(F;A)\,:\,\mathbf{1}_{F}\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)\,,\,F\Delta E\subset\!\subset A\}\,.

It suffices to show that
\mathcal{I}_{1}=\mathcal{I}_{2}
. For sure,
\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq\mathcal{I}_{2}
because we can take
g=\mathbf{1}_{F}
in the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{1}
. Fix now
\varepsilon>0
, and let
g\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
be such that
\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-\mathbf{1}_{E})\subset\subset A
and

(3.2)

|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)\leq\mathcal{I}_{1}+\varepsilon\,.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let us set

G_{t}=\{x\in\Omega\,:\,g(x)>t\}\,.

We observe that, for each
0<t<1
,
G_{t}\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-\mathbf{1}_{E})=E\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-\mathbf{1}_{E})
, and so

(3.3)

G_{t}\Delta E\subset\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-\mathbf{1}_{E})\subset\subset A\,.

We can now exploit Theorem
2.6
and (
3.3
) to infer that

(3.4)

|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\Omega}(G_{t};A)dt\geq\int_{0}^{1}P_{\Omega}(G_{t};A)dt\geq\mathcal{I}_{2}\,.

By (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we deduce that
\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq\mathcal{I}_{1}+\varepsilon
and then, since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we get
\mathcal{I}_{1}=\mathcal{I}_{2}
and complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The minimality gap of a local almost-minimizer
E
satisfies the estimate

\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}(x))\leq\omega_{n}^{1-\frac{1}{n}}r^{n-1}\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)

for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,
0<r<r_{x}
, where
\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)
is the function appearing in (
3.1
). This means that
\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}(x))=o(r^{n-1})
as
r\to 0
. However, we anticipate here that the monotonicity formula proved in Section
5
will require a slightly stronger assumption on
\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)
, namely that
r^{-1}\psi_{\Omega}(E;x,r)
is summable on
(0,r_{x})
. We notice that this kind of hypothesis is somehow well-known in the context of regularity theory (see
[
31
]
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Omega,A,A^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open sets and
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. If
A\subset\!\subset A^{\prime}
, then

\Psi_{\Omega}(f;A)\leq\Psi_{\Omega}(f;A^{\prime})\,.

Proof.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
and take
g\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
such that

\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-f)\subset\!\subset A\qquad\text{and}\qquad|Df|(\Omega\cap A)-|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)\geq\Psi_{\Omega}(f;A)-\varepsilon\,.

Of course,
g
also satisfies also
\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(g-f)\subset\!\subset A^{\prime}
, hence
we get

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega}(f;A^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq|Df|(\Omega\cap A^{\prime})-|Dg|(\Omega\cap A^{\prime})

\displaystyle=|Df|(\Omega\cap A)+|Df|(\Omega\cap(A^{\prime}\setminus A))-|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)-|Df|(\Omega\cap(A^{\prime}\setminus A))

\displaystyle=|Df|(\Omega\cap A)-|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)

\displaystyle\geq\Psi_{\Omega}(f;A)-\varepsilon\,.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude the proof.
∎

We now prove two key results. The first one is the lower semicontinuity property of the minimality gap for uniform sequences of local almost-minimizers. An extra, technical difficulty arising in the proof, is that the tangent cone to the domain may not locally contain the dilations of the domain itself, since we are in the boundary case. This requires to suitably extend a competitor from the tangent cone
\Omega_{0}
to the rescaled domains of the form
t^{-1}\Omega
,
t\to 0^{+}
via Lemma
2.15
.
The second is an upper bound for the difference between the minimality gaps of two
BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}
functions.

In what follows, we will say that
\Omega_{j}\to\Omega
locally in Hausdorff distance if there exist
r_{0},m,L>0
, with
r_{0}<m
, and
L
-Lipschitz functions
\omega_{j},\omega:B^{\prime}_{r_{0}}\to(-m,m)
providing local graphical representations of
\partial\Omega_{j},\ \partial\Omega
, respectively, as in (
2.12
), such that
\omega_{j}\to\omega
uniformly in
B^{\prime}_{r_{0}}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
we let
\Omega_{j},\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open sets with uniformly Lipschitz boundary, such that
0\in\partial\Omega
and
\Omega_{j}\to\Omega
locally in Hausdorff distance. Let
E_{j},E
be sets of locally finite perimeter, such that
E_{j}\subset\Omega_{j},\ E\subset\Omega
, and
E_{j}\to E
in
L^{1}(B_{r_{0}})
. Finally, we assume that
E_{j}
satisfies (
3.1
) for all
0<r<r_{0}
and
x=0
, and that moreover we have

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\,\sup_{j}\psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};0,r)=0\,.

Then,
E
satisfies (
3.1
) for all
0<r<r_{0}
, with
\psi_{\Omega}(E;0,r)=\sup_{j}\psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};0,r)
, and

(3.5)

\liminf_{j}\Psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})\geq\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r_{0}})\,.

Moreover, if
\Psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, then for almost all
0<r<r_{0}
we have

(3.6)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r})=P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})\,,\qquad\text{for almost all $r>0\,.$}

Proof.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
F\subset\Omega
be such that
F\Delta E\subset\subset B_{r_{0}}
and

(3.7)

\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r_{0}})\leq P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r_{0}})-P_{\Omega}(F;B_{r_{0}})+\varepsilon\,.

By Lemma
2.12
, for all
j\geq 1
, for almost every
0<r<r_{0}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in\partial B_{r}
, we have

(3.8)

\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}}(x)={\mathrm{Tr}}^{\pm}(E_{j},\partial B_{r})(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)={\mathrm{Tr}}^{\pm}(E,\partial B_{r})(x)\,,

where
{\mathrm{Tr}}^{\pm}(A,\partial B_{r}):={\mathrm{Tr}}^{\pm}(\mathbf{1}_{A},\partial B_{r})
. By the
L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}
-convergence of
E_{j}
to
E
, we can choose
r<r_{0}
with the above property and the additional

(3.9)

P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})\geq P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r_{0}})-\varepsilon\,,

then take
j_{\varepsilon}
large enough, such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle F\Delta E\subset\subset B_{r}\,,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}}-\mathbf{1}_{E}|d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}<\varepsilon\qquad\text{for $j\geq j_{\varepsilon}$}\,.

Let us fix
\delta>0
, define

U_{\delta}:=\big\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{r,m}:\ \omega(x^{\prime})-\delta<x_{n}\leq\omega(x^{\prime})\big\}\,,

and assume
\delta
so small that

(3.12)

{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}\cap U_{\delta})<\varepsilon

and

(3.13)

P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})\leq P(E;B_{r}\cap(\Omega+\delta e_{n}))+\varepsilon\,.

Owing to the uniform convergence of
\omega_{j}
to
\omega
, we can select
j_{\delta}\geq 1
such that
\|\omega_{j}-\omega\|_{\infty}<\delta
for all
j\geq j_{\delta}
, then for those
j
we define

A_{j}:=\Omega_{j}\cap\Omega\cap B_{r}\,,\qquad B_{j}:=(\Omega_{j}\setminus\Omega)\cap B_{r}\,,

and observe that
B_{j}\subset U_{\delta}
. Now we set

(3.14)

F_{j}:=(\tilde{F}\cap\Omega_{j}\cap B_{r})\cup\big(E_{j}\cap(B_{r_{0}}\setminus B_{r})\big)\,,

where
\tilde{F}=F\cup(S(F)\setminus\Omega)
and
S
is the symmetry through
\partial\Omega
defined in (
2.13
) (with
\rho=r_{0}
). Thanks to (
3.10
), we have
F_{j}\subset\Omega_{j}
and
F_{j}\Delta E_{j}\subset\subset B_{r_{0}}
, which means that
F_{j}
is a competitor for
E_{j}
in the definition of
\Psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j},B_{r_{0}})
. Moreover, by (
3.14
), (
3.11
), and (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle P_{\Omega_{j}}(F_{j};B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq P_{\Omega_{j}}(F_{j};B_{r})+P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}}\setminus\overline{B_{r}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega_{j}\cap\Omega}|\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}}-\mathbf{1}_{E}|d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}+{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}\cap U_{\delta})

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq P_{\Omega_{j}}(F_{j};B_{r})+P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}}\setminus\overline{B_{r}})+2\varepsilon\,.

Let us compute
P(F_{j};B_{r}\cap\Omega_{j})
. By Lemma
2.15
,
P(\tilde{F};\partial\Omega\cap\mathcal{C}_{r_{0},m})=0
, hence

(3.16)

\displaystyle P_{\Omega_{j}}(F_{j};B_{r})

\displaystyle=P(F;A_{j})+P(S(F);B_{j})\,.

Again by Lemma
2.15
, up to possibly taking a smaller
\delta
, we have

(3.17)

P(S(F);B_{j})=P(\tilde{F};B_{j})\leq P(\tilde{F};U_{\delta})\leq\varepsilon\,.

Putting together (
3.15
), (
3.16
), and (
3.17
), and taking into account (
3.8
), we obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})-P_{\Omega_{j}}(F_{j};B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r})-P(F;A_{j})-3\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r})-P_{\Omega}(F;B_{r_{0}})-3\varepsilon\,.

Now, since for all
j\geq j_{\varepsilon}
we have
\|\omega_{j}-\omega\|_{\infty}<\delta
, we infer that

B_{r}\cap(\Omega+\delta e_{n})\subset B_{r}\cap\Omega_{j}\,.

This inclusion combined with (
3.18
) and (
3.13
) gives

(3.19)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq P(E_{j};B_{r}\cap(\Omega+\delta e_{n}))-P(F;B_{r_{0}}\cap\Omega)-3\varepsilon\,,

so that taking the liminf as
j\to\infty
in (
3.19
), and using the lower semicontinuity of the perimeter, (
3.13
), (
3.9
), and (
3.7
), we find

\displaystyle\liminf_{j}\Psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{j}P(E_{j};B_{r}\cap(\Omega+\delta e_{n}))-P_{\Omega}(F;B_{r_{0}})-3\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq P(E;B_{r}\cap(\Omega+\delta e_{n}))-P_{\Omega}(F;B_{r_{0}})-3\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})-P_{\Omega}(F;B_{r_{0}})-4\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r_{0}})-P_{\Omega}(F;B_{r_{0}})-5\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r_{0}})-6\varepsilon\,.

Then, the arbitrary choice of
\varepsilon
implies (
3.5
). Now, the fact that
E
satisfies (
3.1
) with
\psi_{\Omega}(E;0,r)
as in the statement can be proved with the same argument used to show (
3.5
), also taking into account that
|E_{j}\Delta F_{j}|\to|E\Delta F|
as
j\to\infty
. Finally, to prove (
3.6
) we consider the sequence
F_{j}
defined as before, but now with
F=E
. Choosing
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily, for
j
large enough we obtain as before

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r_{0}})-P_{\Omega_{j}}(F_{j};B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{r})-P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})-3\varepsilon\,,

which gives the desired conclusion.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\Omega,A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open sets, with
\partial\Omega
Lipschitz and
A
bounded, of class
C^{2}
, and such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial A\cap\partial\Omega)=0
. Let
f,g\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
, then

(3.20)

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\Omega}(f,A)-\Psi_{\Omega}(g,A)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big||Df|(\Omega\cap A)-|Dg|(\Omega\cap A)\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0},\partial A)-{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(g_{0},\partial A)\|_{L^{1}(\partial A)}\,,

where
f_{0},g_{0}
denote the zero-extensions of
f
and
g
respectively on
A\setminus\Omega
.

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
h\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
with
\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(h-f)\subset\!\subset A
, such that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega}(f,A)

\displaystyle\leq|Df|(A\cap\Omega)-|Dh|(A\cap\Omega)+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\big||Df|(A\cap\Omega)-|Dg|(A\cap\Omega)\big|+\Psi_{\Omega}(g,A)+|D\tilde{h}|(A\cap\Omega)-|Dh|(A\cap\Omega)+\varepsilon\,,

where
\tilde{h}\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
will be suitably chosen, so that in particular
\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(\tilde{h}-g)\subset\!\subset A
. For the definition of
\tilde{h}
, we claim that it is possible to construct a sequence
A^{(k)}
of inner parallel sets of
A
that converge to
A
, for which
|Df_{0}|(\partial A^{(k)})=|Dg_{0}|(\partial A^{(k)})=0
and, moreover,

(3.22)

\lim_{k}\int_{\partial A^{(k)}}|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A^{(k)})|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial A}|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\,.

For the proof of (
3.22
) we argue as follows. Since
A
is of class
C^{2}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for all
0<t<\delta
, the map
\zeta_{t}(x)=x+t\nu_{A}(x)
is a diffeomorphism of class
C^{1}
between
\partial A
and the boundary
\partial A_{t}
of the inner parallel set
A_{t}=\{x\in A:\ \mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial A)>t\}
.
Now, we consider two sequences
f_{0,j},g_{0,j}
of smooth approximations of
f_{0},g_{0}
on
A
, with traces
{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0,j},\partial A)={\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0},\partial A)
and
{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(g_{0,j},\partial A)={\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(g_{0},\partial A)
, respectively (see Remark
2.10
). By inspecting the proof of Anzellotti-Giaquinta’s approximation theorem, it is not restrictive to ask that the sequences
f_{0,j},g_{0,j}
also satisfy

(3.23)

\displaystyle\int_{A\setminus A_{1/k}}(|f_{0,j}-f_{0}|+|g_{0,j}-g_{0}|)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}\,,

for all
j
and for
k>\delta^{-1}
.
We note that the tangential Jacobian of
\zeta_{t}
satisfies
J\zeta_{t}(x)=1+O(t)
, hence the area formula gives

(3.24)

\int_{\partial A_{t}}|f_{0,j}(y)-g_{0,j}(y)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(y)=(1+O(t))\int_{\partial A}|f_{0,j}(\zeta_{t}(x))-g_{0,j}(\zeta_{t}(x))|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(x)\,.

As
t\to 0^{+}
we have
\zeta_{t}(x)\to x
uniformly. Therefore, by following the same Cauchy-sequence argument as in the classical construction of the trace (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
), the compositions
f_{0,j}(\zeta_{t}(x))
and
g_{0,j}(\zeta_{t}(x))
can be shown to converge in
L^{1}(\partial A)
to some limits
\hat{f}_{0,j}
and
\hat{g}_{0,j}
, respectively. Hence (
3.24
) implies

(3.25)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\partial A_{t}}|f_{0,j}(y)-g_{0,j}(y)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(y)=\int_{\partial A}|\hat{f}_{0,j}(x)-\hat{g}_{0,j}(x)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(x)\,.

At the same time, if we choose a vector field
\xi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and set either
u_{j}=f_{0,j}
or
u_{j}=g_{0,j}
, by Gauss-Green Theorem we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{A_{t}}(u_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\xi+\nabla u_{j}\cdot\xi)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial A_{t}}u_{j}\,\xi\cdot\nu_{A_{t}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=-(1+O(t))\int_{\partial A}u_{j}(\zeta_{t}(x))\,\xi(\zeta_{t}(x))\cdot\nu_{A}(x)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(x)\,,

hence taking the limit as
t\to 0^{+}
gives

\displaystyle\int_{A}(u_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\xi+\nabla u_{j}\cdot\xi)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial A}\hat{u}_{j}\,\xi\cdot\nu_{A}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\,,

which means that
\hat{f}_{0,j}
and
\hat{g}_{0,j}
coincide, respectively, with
{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0},\partial A)
and
{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(g_{0},\partial A)
up to
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-null sets and for all
j
, by the uniqueness of the trace. We can thus rewrite (
3.25
) as

(3.26)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\partial A_{t}}|f_{0,j}(y)-g_{0,j}(y)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(y)=\int_{\partial A}|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A)(x)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(x)\,.

To get (
3.22
) from (
3.26
), we must choose
A^{(k)}
appropriately. To this aim, we apply the coarea formula to the integral in (
3.23
) and average the resulting inequality, deducing the existence of
0<t_{k}<1/k
such that for all
j

(3.27)

\int_{\partial A^{(k)}}(|f_{0,j}-{\mathrm{Tr}}(f_{0},\partial A^{(k)})|+|g_{0,j}-{\mathrm{Tr}}(g_{0},\partial A^{(k)})|)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\leq\frac{1}{k}\,,

where we have set
A^{(k)}=A_{t_{k}}
. By the triangle inequality and (
3.27
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial A^{(k)}}|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A^{(k)})|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial A^{(k)}}|f_{0,j}-g_{0,j}|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{k}\,,

which gives (
3.22
) at once from (
3.26
).

Now we observe that
|Df_{0}|(\partial A^{(k)})=|Dg_{0}|(\partial A^{(k)})=0
because the inner and outer traces of
f_{0}
and
g_{0}
on
\partial A^{(k)}
coincide, hence we can define

\tilde{h}=h\mathbf{1}_{A^{(k)}}+g\mathbf{1}_{A\setminus A^{(k)}}\,.

Note that
\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(\tilde{h}-g)\subset\!\subset A
and, if
k
is large enough,
\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(h-f)\subset\!\subset A^{(k)}
, so that

\displaystyle|D\tilde{h}|(A\cap\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq|Dh|(A^{(k)}\cap\Omega)+|Dg_{0}|(A\setminus\overline{A^{(k)}})+\int_{\partial A^{(k)}}|{\mathrm{Tr}}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A^{(k)})|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\,.

By choosing
k
large enough we obtain
|Dg_{0}|(A\setminus A^{(k)})<\varepsilon
and thus

(3.28)

|D\tilde{h}|(A\cap\Omega)-|Dh|(A\cap\Omega)\leq\int_{\partial A}|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}+\varepsilon.

By combining (
3.21
) and (
3.28
), we get

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega}(f,A)

\displaystyle\leq\big||Df|(A\cap\Omega)-|Dg|(A\cap\Omega)\big|+\Psi_{\Omega}(g,A)+\int_{\partial A}|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}+2\varepsilon\,.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega}(f,A)-\Psi_{\Omega}(g,A)

\displaystyle\leq\big||Df|(A\cap\Omega)-|Dg|(A\cap\Omega)\big|+\int_{\partial A}|{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0}-g_{0},\partial A)|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}

and, by exchanging the role of
f
and
g
in the argument above, we obtain (
3.20
).
∎

3.2.
Density estimates

In this subsection we establish perimeter and volume density estimates for almost-minimizers at a point either in
\Omega
or on
\partial\Omega
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set with a Lipschitz boundary. Let
E
be an almost-minimizer in
\Omega
, and let
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Assume that
P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}(x))>0
for all
r>0
. Then, there exist constants
C\geq 1,\ r_{0}>0
, depending on
\Omega
,
E
and
x
, such that

(3.29)

\displaystyle C^{-1}r^{n-1}\leq P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}(x))\leq Cr^{n-1}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\min\big(|E\cap B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega|,\ |(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\setminus E|\big)\geq C^{-1}r^{n}\,,

for all
0<r<r_{0}
.

Proof.

Up to a translation, we assume that
x=0
. We start proving (
3.30
). Given
r>0
we set

m(r):=|B_{r}\cap\Omega\cap E|\,,\quad\mu(r):=|B_{r}\cap\Omega\setminus E|\,.

Both
m
and
\mu
are non-decreasing, thus differentiable for almost all
r>0
. By
[
24
, Example 13.3]
, for almost all
r>0
, we have

m^{\prime}(r)={\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(E\cap\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega)\,,\quad\mu^{\prime}(r)={\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega\setminus E)\,.

Now, up to an isometry, and for
r_{0}
sufficiently small and
L\geq 1
sufficiently large, if we set
Q_{L,r_{0}}=B^{\prime}_{r_{0}}\times(-Lr_{0},Lr_{0})
and
\Omega_{L,r_{0}}:=\Omega\cap Q_{L,r_{0}}
, we have that
\Omega_{L,r_{0}}
is connected and has a Lipschitz boundary, hence it supports a relative isoperimetric inequality of the form

(3.31)

P(F;\Omega_{L,r_{0}})\geq C_{0}\min\{|F\cap\Omega_{L,r_{0}}|,|\Omega_{L,r_{0}}\setminus F|\}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\,.

From (
3.31
) and the fact that
B_{r}\subset\!\subset Q_{L,r_{0}}
for all
0<r<r_{0}
, we infer that

(3.32)

m^{\prime}(r)+P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})=P(E\cap B_{r};\Omega_{L,r_{0}})\geq C_{0}\min\{m(r),\mu(r)\}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\,.

and

(3.33)

\mu^{\prime}(r)+P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})=P(B_{r}\setminus E;\Omega_{L,r_{0}})\geq C_{0}\min\{m(r),\mu(r)\}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

for almost all
0<r<r_{0}
. Set
0<t<r
and define the competitor

F_{t}=\begin{cases}E\cup B_{t}\cap\Omega&\text{if }m(t)>\mu(t),\\
E\setminus B_{t}\cap\Omega&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We note that in the first case
F_{t}\Delta E=B_{t}\cap\Omega\setminus E
, while in the second case
F_{t}\Delta E=B_{t}\cap\Omega\cap E
. In any case, we have
F_{t}\Delta E\subset\subset B_{r}\cap\Omega
. Thus, by the almost-minimality of
E
in
\Omega
, and for almost all
0<t<r

(3.34)

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})

\displaystyle\leq P_{\Omega}(F_{t};B_{r})+\mu(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi(r)

\displaystyle\leq P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}\setminus\overline{B_{t}})+{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial B_{t}\cap\Omega\setminus E)+\mu(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi(r)\,,

or

(3.35)

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})

\displaystyle\leq P_{\Omega}(F_{t};B_{r})+m(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi(r)

\displaystyle\leq P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}\setminus\overline{B_{t}})+{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\partial B_{t}\cap\Omega\cap E)+m(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi(r)\,.

where

. Taking the limit as
t\nearrow r
in (

) and (
3.35
), and using (
3.32
), we deduce that, if

, then for almost all

2\mu^{\prime}(r)+\mu(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi(r)\geq C_{0}\mu(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\,,

while otherwise, by (
3.33
), we have

2m^{\prime}(r)+m(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi(r)\geq C_{0}m(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\,.

Therefore, calling
s(r):=\min\{m(r),\mu(r)\}
and owing to the infinitesimality of
\psi(r)
as
r\to 0
, we obtain

\dfrac{s^{\prime}(r)}{s(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}\geq C\,,

for
0<r<r_{0}
and for some explicit constant
C>0
depending on
C_{0}
. Integrating this inequality on the interval
(0,r)
we obtain (
3.30
). Then, the first inequality in (
3.29
) follows from (
3.30
) and (
3.32
). Finally, the second inequality in (
3.29
) follows from the observation that, taking the limit as

in (

) and possibly redefining

and

, we have

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{H}}(\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega\setminus E)+\mu(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\psi(r)\leq Cr^{n-1}\,,

for every
0<r<r_{0}
.
∎

4.
The visibility property

In this section, we introduce the visibility property and its main consequences. In what follows,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes an open set with Lipschitz boundary such that
0\in\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega
is a graph in a neighborhood of
0
, as in (
2.12
). For notational convenience, we will only consider the visibility property at
0
, but of course, we could equally define the property at a generic point of
\partial\Omega
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property provided there exist
T>0
and a function
u\in C^{1}([0,T))
5
5

5

By
u\in C^{1}([0,T))
we mean that
u\in C^{1}(0,T)
and there exist finite limits of
u(t)
and
u^{\prime}(t)
as
t\to 0
.
such that:

(V1)

u(0)=u^{\prime}(0)=0
and
0\leq u^{\prime}\leq 2^{-1}
;

(V2)

The function

\gamma_{u}(t):=t^{-1}\sup_{0<s\leq t}\sqrt{\frac{u(s)}{s}+u^{\prime}(s)}

is summable on
(0,T)
;

(V3)

for all
0<t<T
, the segment joining the point
U_{t}=-u(t)\,e_{n}
with a point
x
belonging to
\partial\Omega\cap B_{t}
does not intersect
\Omega
.

Remark 4.2
.

We note that (V1) and (V2) imply that
u(t)=o(t)
and

(4.1)

t\gamma_{u}(t)\to 0\,,

as
t\to 0
. Moreover, since by (V1) we have
0\leq u(t)\leq t/2
, we infer that

0\leq\frac{u(t)}{t^{2}}=t^{-1}\frac{u(t)}{t}\leq t^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{u(t)}{t}}\leq\gamma_{u}(t)\,.

Therefore the summability of
\gamma_{u}
implies that of
t^{-2}u(t)
.

In the following proposition, we rewrite the assumption (V3) in the form of a property involving the functions
\omega(x^{\prime})
and
u(t)
. This will be particularly useful when checking the visibility property for relevant classes of domains (see the examples at the end of the section).

Proposition 4.3
.

The set
\Omega
satisfies the property (V3) in Definition
4.1
if and only if, for any
\nu\in\partial B^{\prime}_{1}
and for all
0<t<T
, the slope
m_{t}(s)
of the line connecting
U_{t}
with
(s,\omega(s\nu))
, that is given by

m_{t}(s)=\dfrac{\omega(s\nu)+u(t)}{s}\,,

is non-increasing as a function of
s
, for
s>0
such that
s^{2}+\omega(s\nu)^{2}<t^{2}
.

Proof.

Let us assume that (V3) holds, and set
\omega_{\nu}(s)=\omega(s\nu)
for more simplicity. By contradiction, let
s_{1}<s_{2}
be such that
s_{i}^{2}+\omega_{\nu}(s_{i})^{2}<t^{2}
, for
i=1,

2
, and
m_{t}(s_{1})<m_{t}(s_{2})
. By definition of
m_{t}
, we have

\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s_{1})+u(t)}{s_{1}}<\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s_{2})+u(t)}{s_{2}}\,,

and so equivalently

\omega_{\nu}(s_{1})<\dfrac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\big(\omega_{\nu}(s_{2})+u(t)\big)-u(t)\,.

This implies that the point

x=\left(s_{1}\nu,\dfrac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\big(\omega_{\nu}(s_{2})+u(t)\big)-u(t)\right)

is internal to
\Omega
and lies on the segment connecting
(s_{2}\nu,\omega_{\nu}(s_{2}))
. This contradicts (V3).

Conversely, let us suppose that
m_{t}(s)
is non-increasing in
s
, for
s>0
such that
s^{2}+\omega_{\nu}(s)^{2}<t^{2}
. Set
P(s)=(s\nu,\omega_{\nu}(s))
, then, arguing by contradiction, assume that
s_{2}>0
is such that
s_{2}^{2}+\omega_{\nu}(s_{2})^{2}<t^{2}
and there exists
\lambda\in(0,1)
with the property

(1-\lambda)U_{t}+\lambda P(s_{2})=(\lambda s_{2},\lambda(u(t)+\omega_{\nu}(s_{2}))-u(t))\in\Omega\,.

Then
\omega_{\nu}(\lambda s_{2})<\lambda(u(t)+\omega_{\nu}(s_{2}))-u(t)
. By continuity, for all
\delta>0
there exists
\lambda_{\delta}\in(\lambda,1)
such that

(4.2)

\lambda_{\delta}(u(t)+\omega_{\nu}(s_{2}))-u(t)-\delta<\omega_{\nu}(\lambda_{\delta}\,s_{2})<\lambda_{\delta}(u(t)+\omega_{\nu}(s_{2}))-u(t)\,.

Since the segment
[U_{t},P(s_{2})]
is compactly contained in
B_{t}
, by (
4.2
), we can pick
\delta>0
small enough and a correspondent
\lambda_{\delta}
such that

(4.3)

P(\lambda_{\delta}s_{2})\in B_{t}\,.

Let
s_{1}=\lambda_{\delta}s_{2}
. We observe that (
4.3
) and (
4.2
) imply

m_{t}(s_{1})<m_{t}(s_{2})\,,\qquad s_{1}^{2}+\omega_{\nu}(s_{1})^{2}<t^{2}\,,

and this contradicts our hypothesis. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
\omega
satisfies

(4.4)

\langle x^{\prime},\nabla\omega(x^{\prime})\rangle\leq\omega(x^{\prime})+u(|x^{\prime}|)\qquad\text{ for a.e. }x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}_{\rho}\,,

where
u:(0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a non-decreasing function satisfying properties (V1) and (V2). Then
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property.

Proof.

Since
\omega
is Lipschitz, the function
m_{t}
defined in the statement of Proposition
4.3
is almost everywhere differentiable, thus
m_{t}
is non-increasing if and only if
m_{t}^{\prime}(s)\leq 0
at almost every
s
. We observe that

m_{t}^{\prime}(s)=\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)}{s}-\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)+u(t)}{s^{2}}\,,

thus
m_{t}^{\prime}\leq 0
if and only if

(4.5)

s\,\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)\leq\omega_{\nu}(s)+u(t)\,.

The hypothesis (
4.4
) implies that

(4.6)

s\,\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)\leq\omega_{\nu}(s)+u(s)\qquad\text{for almost all $0<s<T\,.$}

Hence if
s>0
is such that
s^{2}+\omega_{\nu}(s)^{2}<t^{2}
, then
s<t
, and by (
4.6
), since
u
is non-decreasing, we obtain

s\,\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)\leq\omega_{\nu}(s)+u(t)\,,

which is precisely (
4.5
). Consequently, (V3) is verified thanks to Proposition
4.3
.
∎

4.1.
Existence of the tangent cone

An important consequence of the visibility property is the existence of the tangent cone to
\Omega
at
0
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the visibility property. Then there exists the tangent cone to
\Omega
at
0
, denoted by
\Omega_{0}
. More precisely, if we set
\Omega_{s}:=s^{-1}\Omega
for
s>0
, we obtain

(4.7)

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathop{\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}}(\Omega_{s}\cap B_{R},\Omega_{0}\cap B_{R})=0\,,\qquad\text{for all $R>0$.}

Proof.

Let us fix
\nu\in\partial B^{\prime}_{1}
, and let

\omega_{\nu}(s)=\omega(s\nu)\,,\qquad s\geq 0\,,

where
\omega
is the function realizing (
2.12
). Let
s>0
be a point where
\omega_{\nu}
is differentiable and let
t=\sqrt{s^{2}+\omega_{\nu}(s)^{2}}
. By (V3) in Definition
4.1
, we deduce that the slope of the line connecting
(s,\omega_{\nu}(s))
with
U_{t}
needs to be bounded below by
\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)
, that is,

\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)\leq\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)+u(t)}{s}\,.

We set
L=\sqrt{1+\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(\omega)^{2}}
and observe that
t\leq Ls
. Since
u
is non-decreasing by (V1), we infer

\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)\leq\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)+u(Ls)}{s}\,,

hence we get

(4.8)

\left(\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)}{s}\right)^{\prime}=\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)}{s}-\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)}{s^{2}}\leq\dfrac{u(Ls)}{s^{2}}\leq L^{2}\dfrac{u(Ls)}{(Ls)^{2}}\,.

We integrate (
4.8
) between
s_{1}<s_{2}
thanks to (V2) (see Remark
4.2
), and obtain

\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s_{2})}{s_{2}}-\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s_{1})}{s_{1}}\leq L^{2}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\dfrac{u(Ls)}{(Ls)^{2}}\,ds=L\int_{Ls_{1}}^{Ls_{2}}\dfrac{u(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt\,.

Thus we conclude that the function

s\mapsto\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)}{s}-L\int_{0}^{Ls}\dfrac{u(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt

is monotonically non-increasing in
s
and bounded by
\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(\omega)+L\int_{0}^{Ls}t^{-2}u(t)\,dt
, for
0<s<L^{-1}T
. Therefore there exists

D_{\nu}^{+}\omega(0):=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)}{s}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)}{s}-L\int_{0}^{Ls}\dfrac{u(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Let us define

\omega_{0}(x^{\prime})=\begin{cases}|x^{\prime}|D_{\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|}}^{+}\omega(0)&\text{if }x^{\prime}\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }x^{\prime}=0\,.\end{cases}

The function
\omega_{0}
is
1
-homogeneous, therefore the set

\Omega_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,x_{n}>\omega_{0}(x^{\prime})\}

is a cone with vertex at
0
. Now, for all
s>0
, we set

\omega_{s}(x^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\omega(sx^{\prime})}{s}&\text{if }x^{\prime}\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }x^{\prime}=0\,.\end{cases}

It is immediate to observe that
\omega_{s}(0)=0
and, setting
t=s|x^{\prime}|
,

\displaystyle\omega_{s}(x^{\prime})=\dfrac{\omega\left(t(x^{\prime}/|x^{\prime}|)\right)}{t}\,|x^{\prime}|\rightarrow|x^{\prime}|\,D^{+}_{\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|}}\omega(0)=\omega_{0}(x^{\prime})\,,\qquad\text{as $s\rightarrow 0^{+}\,.$}

Since
\{\omega_{s}\}_{s>0}
is a one-parameter family of locally equi-bounded and equi-Lipschitz functions that pointwisely converge to
\omega_{0}
as
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we can apply Lemma
2.1
to conclude that this convergence is locally uniform. This easily implies the Hausdorff convergence stated in (
4.7
).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

We note that for the proof of Proposition

4.2.
An off-centric visibility property

The next lemma shows that the assumption (V3) in Definition

Lemma 4.7
.

The following properties are equivalent:

(i)

\Omega
satisfies the visibility property;

(ii)

There exist

, and

(V3’) for all

.

We prove that (i) implies (ii). Let

. We can find

\dfrac{1}{2}\,t\leq z(t)\leq t\,.

Let

(4.9)

r\leq z^{-1}(r)\leq 2\,r\,.

For all

4.3.
Foliation by off-centric spheres

Let us consider the family of off-centric balls

V_{r}=-v(r)e_{n}

\phi

is the

Lemma 4.9
.

There exists a function
\phi\in C^{1}(B_{R}(V_{R})\setminus\{0\})
such that
0\leq\phi<R
and

\partial B_{r}(V_{r})=\phi^{-1}(r)\,,\qquad\text{for any $0<r<R$.}

In particular, for any

(4.11)

\dfrac{\nabla\phi(x)}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}=\dfrac{x-V_{\phi(x)}}{|x-V_{\phi(x)}|}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla\phi(x)-\dfrac{x}{|x|}\right|\leq 4\,\sqrt{\dfrac{v(\phi(x))}{\phi(x)}+v^{\prime}(\phi(x))}\,.

Proof.

If
v(r)
is identically
0
, then there is nothing to prove because
\phi(x)=|x|
in this case. We then suppose

. Let us start by proving the existence of the function
\phi
. We observe that, for any
x\in B_{R}(V_{R})
, there exists a unique

such that

. Indeed, if
x=0
, we can take
r=0
. Otherwise, let
F:(B_{R}(V_{R})\setminus\{0\})\times(0,R)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

(4.13)

F(x,r)=|x-V_{r}|^{2}-r^{2}\,.

It is immediate to observe that
F
is continuous. Moreover,

F(x,0)=|x|^{2}>0\quad\text{and}\quad F(x,R)<0\,,\qquad\text{for all }x\in B_{R}(V_{R})\setminus\{0\}\,.

Hence we can find
r\in(0,R)
such that
F(x,r)=0
, i.e. such that
x\in\partial B_{r}(V_{r})
. Let us show the uniqueness. Indeed, if
r
,

have the property that

then we get

thus we must have

. Now we can define
\phi(x)=r_{x}
. Let us show that
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(B_{R}(R)\setminus\{0\})
. To do so, we note that
\phi
is implicitly defined by

F(x,\phi(x))=0\,,

where
F
is the function defined in (
4.13
). Easy computations give

\partial_{r}F(x,r)=-2r+2v^{\prime}(r)(x_{n}+v(r))\,.

Therefore, if we assume

(that is,

) we obtain

\partial_{r}F(x,r)=-2r+2v^{\prime}(r)(x_{n}+v(r))\leq 2r+|x_{n}+v(r)|\leq-r\,,

where the first inequality follows from the assumption

, while the second inequality from

By the Implicit Function Theorem we deduce that
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(B_{R}(V_{R})\setminus\{0\})
. The identity (
4.11
) is a consequence of the fact that, if
\phi(x)=r
, then the vector

is orthogonal to the level set
\partial B_{r}(V_{r})=\phi^{-1}(r)
at

.

Let us now prove (
4.12
). We first observe that, if
\phi(x)=r>0
, then

(4.14)

\nabla\phi(x)=-\dfrac{\partial_{x}F(x,r)}{\partial_{r}F(x,r)}=\dfrac{x+v(r)e_{n}}{r-v^{\prime}(r)(x_{n}+v(r))}

Then (
4.14
) yields

\displaystyle\left|\nabla\phi(x)-\dfrac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|x+ve_{n}|^{2}}{(r-v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v))^{2}}+1-2\,\left\langle\dfrac{x+ve_{n}}{r-v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v)},\dfrac{x}{|x|}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|x+ve_{n}|^{2}}{(r-v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v))^{2}}+1-2\,\dfrac{|x+ve_{n}|^{2}-v(x_{n}+v)}{(r-v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v))|x|}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|x|\,r^{2}+|x|(r-v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v))^{2}-2(r-v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v))[r^{2}-v(x_{n}+v)]}{|x|(r-v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\frac{|x|}{r}+\frac{|x|}{r}\left(1-\frac{v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v)}{r}\right)^{2}-2\,\left(1-\frac{v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v)}{r}\right)\left(1-\frac{v}{r}\,\frac{x_{n}+v}{r}\right)}{\frac{|x|}{r}\left(1-\frac{v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v)}{r}\right)^{2}}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1+\left(1-\frac{v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v)}{r}\right)^{2}-2\,\dfrac{r}{|x|}\left(1-\frac{v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v)}{r}\right)\left(1-\frac{v}{r}\,\frac{x_{n}+v}{r}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{v^{\prime}(x_{n}+v)}{r}\right)^{2}}\,,

where the short forms
v=v(r)
and
v^{\prime}=v^{\prime}(r)
have been used.
Next we observe that
\big||x|-r\big|=\big|x-|x-V_{r}|\big|
, hence

(4.16)

r-v(r)\leq|x|\leq r+v(r)\,.

Exploiting (
4.16
) in (
4.15
) and recalling that
v^{\prime}(r)\leq 1/2
and
|x_{n}+v(r)|\leq r
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\nabla\phi(x)-\dfrac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2-2\,\left(1-\frac{v(r)}{r+v(r)}\right)(1-v^{\prime}(r))\left(1-\frac{v(r)}{r}\right)}{(1-v^{\prime}(r))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(1-\left(1-\frac{v(r)}{r}\right)^{2}(1-v^{\prime}(r))\right)

\displaystyle\leq 16\left(v^{\prime}(r)+\frac{v(r)}{r}\right)\,,

and this concludes the proof.
∎

4.4.
Some examples

We note that there are examples of sets with Lipschitz boundary that do not satisfy the visibility property at
0
, like for instance the epigraph of the function

\omega(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2}\sin(|x|^{-1})&\text{if }x\neq 0\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Indeed, one can easily check that at
x_{k}=\frac{1}{(2k+1)\pi}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\omega(x_{k})=0
and
\omega^{\prime}(x_{k})=1
, hence any visibility function
u
for which (V3) holds must satisfy
u(x_{k})\geq x_{k}
, which contradicts both (V1) and (V2) (see Remark
4.2
).

In the following, we exhibit some examples of domains for which the visibility holds. We recall that
\omega_{\nu}(s)=\omega(s\nu)
for
s\geq 0
.

Example 4.10

(Lipschitz cones and outer star-shaped sets)
.

Let
\Omega
be either a cone with respect to
0
, or such that its complement
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is locally star-shaped with respect to
0
. It is immediate to check that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property with visibility function
u(t)\equiv 0
.

Example 4.11

(
C^{1,\beta}
-sets)
.

Let
\Omega
have
C^{1,\beta}
boundary and assume
0\in\partial\Omega
. We show that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property. Up to a rotation we can assume that
\Omega
admits a representation as in (
2.12
) with
\omega\in C^{1,\beta}(B^{\prime}_{\rho})
. By Corollary
4.4
, it is enough to show that
\omega
satisfies (
4.4
). Since
\nabla\omega
is
\beta
-Hölder, we have

(4.17)

\langle x^{\prime},\nabla\omega(x^{\prime})\rangle\leq\langle x^{\prime},\nabla\omega(0)\rangle+u(|x^{\prime}|)\,.

where
u(t)=C\,t^{1+\beta}
for some
C>0
. Set
\overline{\omega}(x^{\prime}):=\omega(x^{\prime})-\langle\nabla\omega(0),x^{\prime}\rangle
and note that
\overline{\omega}
is
C^{1,\beta}
,
\overline{\omega}(0)=0
,
\nabla\overline{\omega}(0)=0
, and

\displaystyle|\omega(x^{\prime})-\langle x^{\prime},\nabla\omega(0)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{|y^{\prime}|\leq|x^{\prime}|}|\nabla\overline{\omega}(y^{\prime})||x^{\prime}|

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq(|\nabla\overline{\omega}(0)|+C|x|^{\beta})|x^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=C\,|x^{\prime}|^{1+\beta}\,.

Putting together (
4.17
) and (
4.18
), we finally get

\langle x^{\prime},\nabla\omega(x^{\prime})\rangle\leq\omega(x^{\prime})+u(|x^{\prime}|),\,\qquad\text{ for a.e. }x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}_{\rho}\,,

which is precisely (
4.4
). Since trivially
u
is non-decreasing and satisfies (V2), by Corollary
4.4
we infer that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property.

Example 4.12

(Convex sets satisfying (V2))
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex set with
0\in\partial\Omega
. For
s>0
, let
\Omega_{s}:=s^{-1}\Omega
and
\Omega_{0}:=\bigcup_{s>0}\Omega_{s}
. The set
\Omega_{0}
is the tangent cone to
\Omega
at
0
. Let

(4.19)

u(r):=\mathop{\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}}}(\Omega\cap B_{r},\Omega_{0}\cap B_{r})\,,

and assume that
u(r)
satisfies (V2). We observe that
u
is non-decreasing in
r
. Let us prove that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility property. As before, we assume that
\Omega
admits a graphical representation as in (
2.12
) with the further property that
\omega:B^{\prime}_{\rho}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is convex. Using the notations introduced in the proof of Proposition
4.5
, by the convexity of
\omega
, we can define

\omega_{0}(x^{\prime})=\begin{cases}|x^{\prime}|D_{\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|}}^{+}\omega(0)&\text{if }x^{\prime}\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }x^{\prime}=0\,,\end{cases}

and deduce that

\Omega_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,x_{n}>\omega_{0}(x^{\prime})\}\,.

By the definition of
u
given in (
4.19
), we have

(4.20)

||\omega-\omega_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{r})}\leq Cu(r)\,,\qquad\text{for some $C>0\,.$}

Owing to Corollary
4.4
, the visibility property can be proved by showing that (
4.4
) holds. Thanks to the convexity of
\omega
, for all
\nu\in\partial B^{\prime}_{1}
, we have

(4.21)

D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)\leq\omega^{\prime}_{\nu}\left(\frac{s}{2}^{+}\right):=\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s/2+\sigma)-\omega_{\nu}(s/2)}{\sigma}\,,\qquad\text{for all $0\leq s<\rho\,.$}

Moreover, for all
0<\sigma<s/2
, by (
4.20
) and the convexity of
\omega_{\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s/2+\sigma)-\omega_{\nu}(s/2)}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)-\omega_{\nu}(s/2)}{s/2}

\displaystyle=2\,\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)}{s}-\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s/2)}{s/2}

(4.22)

\displaystyle=2\,\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s)}{s}-2D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)+D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)-\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(s/2)}{s/2}+D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{s}\,\left(\omega_{\nu}(s)-\omega_{0}(s\nu)+\omega_{0}(s\nu/2)-\omega_{\nu}(s/2)\right)+D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)

\displaystyle\leq D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)+\tilde{C}\,\dfrac{u(s)}{s}\,,\qquad\text{for some $\tilde{C}>0\,.$}

Putting together (
4.21
) and (
4.22
), we obtain

\left|D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)-\omega^{\prime}_{\nu}\left(\frac{s}{2}^{+}\right)\right|\leq\tilde{C}\,\dfrac{u(s)}{s}\,.

This suffices for us to conclude. In fact, if
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}_{\rho}\setminus\{0\}
is such that
\omega
is differentiable at
x^{\prime}
, setting
s:=|x^{\prime}|
,
\nu:=x^{\prime}/|x^{\prime}|
, for some
C>0
, we achieve

\displaystyle\langle x^{\prime},\nabla\omega(x^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle s\nu,\nabla\omega(s\nu)\rangle=s\,\omega^{\prime}_{\nu}(s^{+})

\displaystyle\leq s\,D^{+}_{\nu}\omega(0)+C\,u(s)

\displaystyle\leq\omega(s\nu)+C\,u(s)=\omega(x^{\prime})+C\,u(|x^{\prime}|)\,.

Since
\omega
is convex, it is differentiable almost everywhere in
B^{\prime}_{\rho}
, and so (
4.4
) is verified. Moreover,
u
is non-decreasing and satisfies (V2) by our assumption, thus we conclude.

Example 4.13
.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
y_{i}=2^{-i}
and
\tilde{y}_{i}=2^{-i}+4^{-i}
.
We observe that
y_{i+1}<\tilde{y}_{i+1}<y_{i}
. We define
P_{i}=(y_{i},\tilde{y}_{i})
,
Q_{i}=(\tilde{y}_{i},\tilde{y}_{i})
, and consider the polygonal curve formed by the segments
\overline{P_{i}Q_{i+1}}
,
\overline{Q_{i+1}P_{i+1}}
, for all
i\geq 0
. It is immediate to observe that this curve coincides with the graph of the function
\omega:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
\omega(0)=0
and

(4.23)

\omega(y)=\begin{cases}2^{-(i+1)}+4^{-(i+1)}&\text{if }y\in(y_{i+1},\tilde{y}_{i+1}],\\
a_{i}\,y-b_{i}&\text{if }y\in(\tilde{y}_{i+1},y_{i}]\,,\end{cases}

where

a_{i}=\dfrac{1+3\cdot 2^{-(i+1)}}{1-2^{-(i+1)}}\,,\qquad b_{i}=\dfrac{4^{-i}+2^{-(3i+1)}}{1-2^{-(i+1)}}\,.

Figure 1
.
The graph of
\omega(y)
"bounces" between the graphs of
y
and
y+y^{2}
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open set such that

\Omega\cap((-1,1)\times\mathbb{R})=\{x=(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,z>\omega(|y|)\}\,.

Let us show that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility condition at
0
. Owing to Corollary
4.4
, it suffices to show the existence of a non-decreasing function
u:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying (V1), (V2) and such that

(4.24)

y\,\omega^{\prime}(y)\leq\omega(y)+u(y)\,,\qquad\text{ for a.e. }y\in(0,1)\,.

By (
4.23
), for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

y\,\omega^{\prime}(y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }y\in(y_{i+1},\tilde{y}_{i+1}],\\
a_{i}\,y&\text{if }y\in(\tilde{y}_{i+1},y_{i}]\,.\end{cases}

Thus, in order to verify (
4.24
), it suffices to choose a function
u=u(y)
greater or equal than the function

\overline{u}(y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }y\in(y_{i+1},\tilde{y}_{i+1}],\\
b_{i}&\text{if }y\in(\tilde{y}_{i+1},y_{i}]\,.\end{cases}

We look for
\alpha>0
such that

(4.25)

u_{\alpha}(y)=\alpha y^{2}\geq\overline{u}(y)\,.

To obtain (
4.25
), it suffices to impose that

u_{\alpha}(\tilde{y}_{i+1})=\alpha\,\tilde{y}_{i+1}^{2}\geq b_{i}\,.

It is immediate to observe that

\alpha\,\tilde{y}_{n+1}^{2}\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{4}\,2^{-2i}\,,\qquad b_{i}\leq 4\cdot 2^{-2i}\,,

hence, if for instance we take
\alpha=16
, (
4.25
) holds. Since the function
u(y)=16\,y^{2}
fulfills (V1) and (V2), we conclude that
\Omega
satisfies the visibility condition at
0
.

5.
Free-boundary monotonicity

The present section is devoted to showing a free-boundary monotonicity property for local almost-minimizers of
P_{\Omega}
at a point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
satisfying the visibility property up to an isometry (hence, from now on, we will directly assume
x_{0}=0
).
Following the notation of the previous section, given
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
, we recall that
V_{r}=-v(r)e_{n}
and define

\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}:=B_{r_{2}}(V_{r_{2}})\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}(V_{r_{1}})}=\phi^{-1}(r_{1},r_{2})\,,

where
\phi(x)
is the function defined in Lemma
4.9
. We also conveniently introduce some further notation. Given
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
and
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
, we set

\mu_{f}(r)=\dfrac{|Df|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))}{r^{n-1}}

and

(5.1)

\displaystyle G(f;r_{1},r_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(V_{\rho})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|\,d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{1}{r_{2}^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{2}}(V_{r_{2}})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|-\dfrac{1}{r_{1}^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{1}}(V_{r_{1}})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|\,.

In the next proposition, we combine the visibility property with an upper bound on
\mu_{f}(r)
and obtain the finiteness of
\lim_{\rho\to 0}G(f;\rho,r)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the visibility property as in Definition
4.1
, and let
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. Assume that
\mu_{f}(r)\leq C
for some constant
C>0
and for all
r\in(0,R)
. Then for
r\in(0,R)
the limit

\displaystyle G(f;r)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\rho\to 0}G(f;\rho,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(V_{\rho})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|\,d\rho+\dfrac{1}{r^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|

exists and is finite.

Proof.

By (
4.12
), for all
x\in B_{\rho}(V_{\rho})
we have

\displaystyle\left||\nabla\phi(x)|-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\nabla\phi(x)-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{v(\phi(x))}{\phi(x)}+v^{\prime}(\phi(x))}\leq 4\sup_{0<r\leq\rho}\sqrt{\frac{v(r)}{r}+v^{\prime}(r)}=4\rho\gamma_{v}(\rho)\,,

where
\gamma_{v}
is the function defined in the visibility property (V2). Then, using the upper bound on
\mu_{f}
, for
0<\rho<r<R
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{1-n}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(V_{\rho})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|\right|\leq 4C\rho\gamma_{v}(\rho)\,.

Thanks to the summability of
\gamma_{v}
(see property (V2) of the visibility property) and to (
4.1
), from the last inequality we easily get the proof of the proposition.
∎

Our monotonicity formula will then follow from the general inequality proved in the next theorem.

Theorem 5.2

(Monotonicity inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the visibility property, and let
f\in BV_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. Then, for
R>0
small enough, for almost every
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\phi^{1-n}\left|\left\langle\nu_{f},\nabla\phi\right\rangle\right|\,d|Df|\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\frac{|\nabla\phi(x)|}{\phi(x)^{n-1}}\,d|Df|\right)

(5.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\bigg[\mu_{f}(r_{2})-\mu_{f}(r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))d\rho+G(f;r_{1},r_{2})\bigg]\,,

where
\nu_{f}
is such that
Df=\nu_{f}|Df|
.

Proof.

We start by assuming
f\in BV(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
. For all
0<r<R
and
x\in{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\cap B_{r}(V_{r})
, where
{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}
is defined in (
4.10
), we let

Y_{r}(x)=V_{r}+r\,\dfrac{x-V_{r}}{|x-V_{r}|}\,.

Standard computations yield

(5.3)

\displaystyle DY_{r}(x)

\displaystyle=r\,\left[\dfrac{1}{|x-V_{r}|}D(x-V_{r})+(x-V_{r})\otimes\nabla|x-V_{r}|^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{r}{|x-V_{r}|}\,\left[\mathrm{Id}-\dfrac{x-V_{r}}{|x-V_{r}|}\otimes\dfrac{x-V_{r}}{|x-V_{r}|}\right]\,.

We define

g_{r}(x)=f(Y_{r}(x))\qquad\text{for all $x\in{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\,,$}

then the “off-centric conical competitor” is

f_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}g_{r}(x)&\text{if $x\in{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\cap B_{r}(V_{r})$}\\
f(x)&\text{if $x\in\Omega\setminus B_{r}(V_{r})\,.$}\end{cases}

By definition,
f_{r}
coincides with
f
in
\Omega\setminus B_{r}(V_{r})
, hence we infer

(5.4)

\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{r}(V_{r}))\geq|Df|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))-|Df_{r}|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))\,.

Then, by (
5.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|Df|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))-\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{r}(V_{r}))

\displaystyle\leq|Df_{r}|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq|Df_{r}|({\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla g_{r}(x)|dx\,.

Let us now compute the gradient of
g_{r}
. By (
5.3
), setting

\nu_{r}(x)=\dfrac{x-V_{r}}{|x-V_{r}|}\,,\qquad Y_{r}=Y_{r}(x)\,,

we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla g_{r}(x)=DY_{r}\cdot\nabla f(Y_{r})=\dfrac{r}{|x-V_{r}|}\,\nabla f(Y_{r})^{\nu_{r}(x)^{\perp}}\,,

where
\nabla f(Y_{r})^{\nu_{r}(x)^{\perp}}
denotes the projection of
\nabla f(Y_{r})
onto the hyperplane

\nu_{r}(x)^{\perp}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle\nu_{r}(x),y\rangle=0\}\,.

Going on with the computations, we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla g_{r}(x)|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{r}{|x-V_{r}|}\,\sqrt{|\nabla f(Y_{r})|^{2}-\langle\nabla f(Y_{r}),\nu_{r}(x)\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{r}{|x-V_{r}|}\,\left|\nabla f(Y_{r})\right|\,\sqrt{1-\dfrac{\left\langle\nabla f(Y_{r}),\nu_{r}(x)\right\rangle^{2}}{|\nabla f(Y_{r})|^{2}}}\,.

Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla g_{r}|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\cap\partial B_{\rho}(V_{r})}\dfrac{r}{\rho}\,\left|\nabla f(Y_{r})\right|\,\sqrt{1-\dfrac{\left\langle\nabla f(Y_{r}),\nu_{r}\right\rangle^{2}}{|\nabla f(Y_{r})|^{2}}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{n-2}d\rho\int_{{\mathfrak{C}}_{r}\cap\partial B_{r}(V_{r})}\left|\nabla f\right|\,\sqrt{1-\dfrac{\left\langle\nabla f,\nu_{r}\right\rangle^{2}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{r}{n-1}\,\Bigg\{\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f|d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}(V_{r})}\dfrac{\left\langle\nabla f,\nu_{r}\right\rangle^{2}}{|\nabla f|}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\Bigg\}\,.

Combining (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we get

(5.7)

\displaystyle\dfrac{r}{2(n-1)}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}(V_{r})}\dfrac{\left\langle\nabla f,\nu_{r}\right\rangle^{2}}{|\nabla f|}d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\dfrac{r}{n-1}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f|d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}-\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f|dx+\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{r}(V_{r}))\,.

Multiplying both sides of (
5.7
) by
(n-1)r^{-n}
and observing that
r=\phi(y)
for any
y\in\partial B_{r}(V_{r})
, we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}(V_{r})}

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f,\dfrac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}\right\rangle^{2}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla f|\,\phi^{n-1}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{r^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}+\dfrac{n-1}{r^{n}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f|dx+\dfrac{n-1}{r^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{r}(V_{r}))

(5.8)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{d}{dr}\left(\dfrac{1}{r^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f||\nabla\phi|dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\dfrac{n-1}{r^{n}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f|(|\nabla\phi|-1)dx+\dfrac{n-1}{r^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{r}(V_{r}))\,.

Let us now integrate (
5.8
) between
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
. We then achieve

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\left\langle\nabla f(x),\dfrac{\nabla\phi(x)}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}\right\rangle^{2}\dfrac{|\nabla\phi(x)|}{|\nabla f(x)|\,\phi(x)^{n-1}}\,dx

(5.9)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\ \dfrac{1}{r_{2}^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{2}}(V_{r_{2}})}|\nabla f(x)||\nabla\phi(x)|dx-\dfrac{1}{r_{1}^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{1}}(V_{r_{1}})}|\nabla f(x)||\nabla\phi(x)|dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{r^{n}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r})}|\nabla f(x)|(|\nabla\phi(x)|-1)dx\,dr+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{r^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{r}(V_{r}))dr\,.

By Hölder’s Inequality, we get

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\left|\left\langle\nabla f(x),\nabla\phi(x)\right\rangle\right|\,\dfrac{dx}{\phi(x)^{n-1}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\frac{|\nabla\phi(x)|}{\phi(x)^{n-1}}|\nabla f(x)|dx\right)\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\left\langle\nabla f(x),\dfrac{\nabla\phi(x)}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}\right\rangle^{2}\,\dfrac{|\nabla\phi(x)|\,dx}{|\nabla f(x)|\phi(x)^{n-1}}\right)\,.

Putting together (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\left|\left\langle\nabla f(x),\nabla\phi(x)\right\rangle\right|\,\dfrac{dx}{\phi(x)^{n-1}}\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\frac{|\nabla\phi(x)|}{\phi(x)^{n-1}}|\nabla f(x)|dx\right)

(5.11)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{r_{2}^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{2}}(V_{r_{2}})}|\nabla f(x)|dx-\dfrac{1}{r_{1}^{n-1}}\,\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{1}}(V_{r_{1}})}|\nabla f(x)|dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))d\rho+G(f;r_{1},r_{2})\Bigg)\,,

where
G(f;r_{1},r_{2})
is as in (
5.1
). This proves (
5.2
) for all
f\in BV(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
.

Let now
f\in BV(\Omega)
. We can select a sequence
f_{j}\in BV(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(5.12)

||f_{j}-f||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\to 0\,,\qquad|Df_{j}|(\Omega)\to|Df|(\Omega)\,,\qquad Df_{j}\mathop{\rightharpoonup}^{\ast}Df\,\,\,\,\,\text{in $\Omega\,.$}

In particular, by the continuity of the trace with respect to the strict convergence, we have

(5.13)

||{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{j},\partial\Omega)-{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f,\partial\Omega)||_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega,{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1})}\to 0\,.

Let us consider the extensions

f_{0,j}(x)=\begin{cases}f_{j}(x)&\text{if $x\in\Omega$}\\
0&\text{if $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\,,$}\end{cases}\qquad f_{0}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if $x\in\Omega$}\\
0&\text{if $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\,.$}\end{cases}

We observe that, by (
5.12
), (
5.13
),

||f_{0,j}-f_{0}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0\,,\qquad|Df_{0,j}|(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to|Df_{0}|(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,.

By Proposition
2.13
, for almost all
0<r<R
,

(5.14)

|Df_{0,j}|(B_{r}(V_{r}))\to|Df_{0}|(B_{r}(V_{r}))\,,\qquad||{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0,j},\partial B_{r}(V_{r}))-{\mathrm{Tr}}^{+}(f_{0},\partial B_{r}(V_{r}))||_{L^{1}(\partial B_{r}(V_{r}))}\to 0\,,

and in particular, owing to (
5.13
),

(5.15)

|Df_{j}|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))=|Df_{0,j}|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))\to|Df_{0}|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))=|Df|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))\,.

Now (
5.14
), (
5.15
) allow us to apply Lemma
3.7
, and we deduce that

|\Psi_{\Omega}(f_{j};B_{r}(V_{r}))-\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{r}(V_{r}))|\to 0\,,\qquad\text{as $j\rightarrow\infty\,,$}

for almost all
0<r<R
. This implies that

\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f_{j};B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))d\rho\to\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\dfrac{n-1}{\rho^{n}}\,\Psi_{\Omega}(f;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))d\rho\,.

Finally, to conclude that the RHS of (
5.11
) for
f=f_{j}
, passes to the limit as
j\rightarrow\infty
, giving precisely the RHS of (
5.2
), it suffices to show that the terms

\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\phi^{1-n}\,d|Df_{j}|\,,\quad\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{1}}(V_{r_{1}})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df_{j}|\,,\quad\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{2}}(V_{r_{2}})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df_{j}|\,,

converge as
j\rightarrow\infty
respectively to

\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\phi^{1-n}\,d|Df|\,,\quad\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{1}}(V_{r_{1}})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|\,,\quad\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{2}}(V_{r_{2}})}(|\nabla\phi|-1)\,d|Df|\,.

To see this, it suffices to construct a suitable partition of each domain, for instance using portions of circular annuli whose boundaries are negligible for
|Df|
and
|Df_{j}|
for all
j\geq 1
, to uniformly approximate each integrand by simple functions (up to removing a small neighborhood of
0
in the case of the last two integrals). About the LHS of (
5.11
), we observe that (
5.12
) implies

Df_{j}\mathop{\rightharpoonup}^{\ast}Df\qquad\text{in $\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}\,.$}

Now, for
f
smooth, the LHS of (
5.2
) and the LHS of (
5.11
) coincide. Moreover, we have

\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}}\phi(x)^{1-n}\left|\left\langle\nu_{f}(x),\nabla\phi(x)\right\rangle\right|\,d|Df|(x)=\left|\phi^{1-n}\nabla\phi\cdot Df_{j}\right|(\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}})\,.

In particular, (
5.12
) implies that

\phi^{1-n}\nabla\phi\cdot Df_{j}\ \mathop{\rightharpoonup}^{\ast}\ \phi^{1-n}\nabla\phi\cdot Df\,,

and well-known properties of the weak-star convergence of Radon measures (see
[
24
]
) ensure that

\left|\phi^{1-n}\nabla\phi\cdot Df\right|(\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}})\leq\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left|\phi^{1-n}\nabla\phi\cdot Df_{j}\right|(\Omega\cap\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}})\,.

This implies (
5.2
) and concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The next corollary, a first important consequence of Theorem
5.2
, states the monotonicity of a suitable function of the radius
r
, which is defined by three terms: the renormalized perimeter
\mu_{E}(r)
, the integral of a renormalized minimality gap
\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r}(V_{r}))
, and the visibility error
G(E,r)
. In particular, when
E
is an almost-minimizer, the infinitesimality of the second and third terms implies that
\mu_{E}(r)
is “almost-increasing”, hence that it admits a finite limit as
r\to 0
. This limit represents the perimeter density of
E
at
0
, see Remark
5.4
below.

Corollary 5.3

(Boundary monotonicity for almost-minimizers)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the visibility property. Let
E\subset\Omega
be a local almost-minimizer, such that
P_{\Omega}(E,B_{r})>0
for all
r>0
and

\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{-n}\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))\,d\rho<+\infty\,.

Then, using the same notation introduced at the beginning of the section, we can find
R^{\prime}>0
such that the function

r\mapsto\mu_{E}(r)+(n-1)\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{-n}\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))\,d\rho+G(E;r)

is non-decreasing on
(0,R^{\prime})
. Moreover, the two terms

\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{-n}\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{\rho}(V_{\rho}))\,d\rho
and
G(E;r)
are infinitesimal as
r\to 0
, hence in particular
\mu_{E}(r)
is “almost-monotone” and the limit

\theta_{E}(0):=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\mu_{E}(r)

exists and is finite.

Proof of Corollary
5.3
.

By Lemma
3.8
and the fact that
B_{r}(V_{r})\subset B_{r+v(r)}
, we can find constants
C,R>0
such that

\dfrac{|Df|(\Omega\cap B_{r}(V_{r}))}{r^{n-1}}\leq\dfrac{|Df|(\Omega\cap B_{r+v(r)})}{(r+v(r))^{n-1}}\left(1+\dfrac{v(r)}{r}\right)^{n-1}\leq C\,,\qquad\text{for all $0<r<R$.}

By combining Proposition
5.1
with (
4.12
) and the previous bound, up to redefining the constants
C,R>0
, we obtain

(5.16)

|G(E;r)|\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{r}\gamma_{v}(\rho)\,d\rho+r\gamma_{v}(r)\right)\,,\qquad\text{for all $0<r<R$.}

Finally, the proof of the corollary follows directly from Theorem
5.2
and from the observation that the RHS of (
5.16
) is infinitesimal as
r\to 0^{+}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

It is easy to check that, under the assumptions of Corollary
5.3
, one has

\exists\,\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{P_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})}{r^{n-1}}=\theta_{E}(0)\,.

Indeed, this is an immediate consequence of the inclusions

B_{r-v(r)}(V_{r})\subset B_{r}\subset B_{r+v(r)}(V_{r})

combined with
v(r)=o(r)
as
r\to 0
.

6.
Blow-up limits of almost-minimizers are cones

We now apply Theorem
5.2
and prove that any blow-up limit of a local almost-minimizer
E
of
P_{\Omega}
is a perimeter-minimizing cone.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the visibility property. Let
E\subset\Omega
be a local almost-minimizer in
\Omega
such that

\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{r})}{r^{n}}dr<\infty\,.

Fix a decreasing sequence
t_{j}\to 0
and set
E_{t_{j}}=t_{j}^{-1}E
. Then, up to subsequences,
E_{t_{j}}
converges to
E_{0}\subset\Omega_{0}
in
L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
E_{0}
is a nontrivial cone minimizing the relative perimeter in
\Omega_{0}
.

Proof.

Set
E_{j}=E_{t_{j}}
and
\Omega_{j}=\Omega_{t_{j}}
for more simplicity. Then by the upper density estimate on the relative perimeter of
E
(Lemma
3.8
) coupled with analogous estimates satisfied by
\Omega
Lipschitz, we can find a constant
C>0
such that, for every fixed
R>0
,

\displaystyle P(E_{j};B_{R})

\displaystyle\leq P(\Omega_{j};B_{R})+P_{\Omega_{j}}(E_{j};B_{R})

\displaystyle=t_{j}^{1-n}\big(P(\Omega;B_{Rt_{j}})+P_{\Omega}(E;B_{Rt_{j}})\big)

\displaystyle\leq CR^{n-1}\,.

By the compactness property of sequences of sets with uniformly bounded relative perimeter the ball
B_{R}
, we conclude that there exists a not relabeled subsequence
E_{j}
and a set
E_{0}
of finite perimeter in
B_{R}
, such that
E_{j}\to E_{0}
in
L^{1}(B_{R})
as
j\to\infty
. The fact that
E_{0}\subset\Omega_{0}
up to null sets is immediate, since
E_{j}\subset\Omega_{j}
, for all
j
, and the sequence
\Omega_{j}
converges to the tangent cone
\Omega_{0}
locally in Hausdorff distance (hence, in
L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) thanks to Proposition
4.5
. Up to a standard diagonal argument we can assume that the subsequence
E_{j}
converges to
E_{0}
in
L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover by the lower-density estimates on the volume of
E
we also deduce that
E_{0}
can be neither the empty set, nor the whole
\Omega_{0}
up to null sets (that is,
E_{0}
is nontrivial).

By the scaling properties of the perimeter, for any fixed
R>0
we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega_{t_{j}}}(E_{t_{j}};B_{R})=\dfrac{1}{t_{j}^{n-1}}\Psi_{\Omega}(E;B_{t_{j}R})\leq\omega_{n}^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\,R^{n-1}\psi_{\Omega}(E;0,t_{j}R)\longrightarrow 0\,,

therefore we can apply Lemma
3.6
and deduce that

\Psi_{\Omega_{0}}(E_{0};B_{R})\leq\liminf_{j}\Psi_{\Omega_{t_{j}}}(E_{t_{j}};B_{R})=0

for all
R>0
and, also owing to Corollary
5.3
,

\displaystyle P_{\Omega_{0}}(E_{0};B_{R})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}P_{\Omega_{t_{j}}}(E_{t_{j}};B_{R})=\lim_{j}t_{j}^{1-n}P_{\Omega}(E;B_{t_{j}R}\cap\Omega)=R^{n-1}\theta_{E}(0)\,.

Thus,
E_{0}
is a minimizer for the relative perimeter in the cone
\Omega_{0}
, such that

\frac{P(E_{0};B_{R}\cap\Omega_{0})}{R^{n-1}}=\theta_{E}(0)\qquad\text{for all $R>0$.}

Now, the monotonicity inequality (
5.2
) written for
f=\mathbf{1}_{E_{0}}
and
\Omega=\Omega_{0}
takes the form

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\Omega_{0}\cap(B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}})}

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\left\langle\nu_{E_{0}}(x),x\right\rangle|}{|x|^{n}}\,d|D\mathbf{1}_{E}|(x)\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}\cap(B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}})}|x|^{1-n}\,d|D\mathbf{1}_{E_{0}}(x)|\right)\cdot\left(\dfrac{P_{\Omega_{0}}(E_{0};B_{r_{2}})}{r_{2}^{n-1}}-\dfrac{P_{\Omega_{0}}(E_{0};B_{r_{1}})}{r_{1}^{n-1}}\right)=0\,,

for almost all
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
. The only possibility is then that
\langle\nu_{E_{0}}(x),x\rangle=0
at
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in\partial^{\ast}E_{0}
. By
[
24
, Proposition 28.8]
we infer that
E_{0}
is a cone with vertex at the origin, up to negligible sets, and the proof is concluded.
∎

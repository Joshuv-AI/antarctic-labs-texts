On common energies and sumsets
Shkredov I.D

Abstract




Annotation.

We obtain a polynomial criterion for a set to have a small doubling in terms of the common energy of its subsets.

1
Introduction

Given an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}
and two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
, define
the
sumset

of
A
and
B
as

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

(1)

In a similar way we define the
difference sets
and the
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
2A-A
is
A+A-A
.
The
doubling constant
of a finite set
A
is defined by formula

\mathcal{D}[A]:=\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}

(2)

and is an important additive–combinatorial characteristic of the set
A
.
The study of the structure of sumsets is a fundamental problem in classical additive combinatorics
[
11
]
, and this relatively new branch of mathematics provides us with a number of excellent results on this object.
For example, Freiman’s theory about the structure of the family of sets having small doubling (i.e., sets for which the ratio (
2
) is “bounded” with respect to the size of
A
) seems especially complete (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
,
[
11
, Section 5]
and recent results in
[
4
]
).
Another combinatorial concept closely related to
\mathcal{D}[A]
is the
additive energy

\mathsf{E}(A,A)
or, more generally, the
common additive energy

\mathsf{E}(A,B)
of
A
and
B
, which is defined as

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A\times A\times B\times B~:~a_{1}-b_{1}=a_{2}-b_{2}\}|\,.

(3)

A trivial application of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives us the connection between the sumset of
A
and
B
and its common energy

\mathsf{E}(A,B)|A\pm B|\geqslant|A|^{2}|B|^{2}\,,

(4)

but a much deeper connection is given to us by the famous Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers theorem (see
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
).

Theorem 1

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set and
K\geqslant 1
be a real number.
Then

\mathsf{E}(A,A)\gg\frac{|A|^{3}}{K^{C_{1}}}\quad\quad\mbox{ iff }\quad\quad\exists A^{\prime}\subseteq A~:~|A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}|\ll K^{C_{2}}|A|\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|A^{\prime}|\gg\frac{|A|}{K^{C_{2}}}\,.

(5)

Here
C_{1}=O(C_{2})
and
C_{2}=O(C_{1})
.

In other words, the dependence between the quantities
K^{C_{1}}
,
K^{C_{2}}
in the criterion (
5
) is
polynomial
, and this partially explains the importance of this remarkable result. On the other hand, the Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers theorem is formulated in terms of
subsets
of
A
, and, as is known,
it is impossible to do without them.

In this paper we connect the two basic quantities (
2
) and (
3
) in another way and we give a polynomial criterion for a set to have a small doubling.
In particular, we obtain (the complete formulation can be found in our main Theorem
24
)
for any
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and an arbitrary real number
K\geqslant 1
that

|A+A|\ll K^{C_{1}}|A|\quad\quad\mbox{ iff }\quad\quad\forall X,Y\subseteq A,\,|X|\geqslant|A|/2~:~\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\geqslant\frac{|X||Y|^{2}}{K^{C_{2}}}

(6)

and therefore it is possible to express the doubling constant of
A
in terms of the common additive energy of subsets of
A
.
Here, as above,
C_{1}=O(C_{2})
and
C_{2}=O(C_{1})
.
One direction of the criterion (
6
) follows easily from (
4
), but another one is more interesting.
We also show that the constant
1/2
in formula (
6
) is optimal and cannot be replaced by
1/2+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
, see Remark
13
of Section
4
.

Another remarkable result of additive combinatorics is the following Ruzsa’s triangle inequality, valid
for any sets
A,B,C\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}

|A||B-C|\leqslant|B-A||A-C|\,.

(7)

Using the common energies and other characteristics that are polynomially equivalent to the doubling constants of our sets, we show (see the last formula (
29
) in Theorem
29
below)
that a common energy analogue of (
7
) takes place, namely, (all required definitions can be found in Section
2
)

\mathcal{E}[B;C]\leqslant\mathcal{E}[B;A]\mathcal{E}[A;C]\,.

(8)

We hope that the tools developed in this paper will add flexibility to working with the doubling constants.

2
Definitions

Below
{\mathbf{G}}
denotes an abelian group with the group operation
+
.
The sumset of two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
was defined in formula (
1
) of the introduction.
We say that the sum of
A
and
B
is direct
if
|A+B|=|A||B|
.
In this case we sometimes write
A\dotplus B
.
The important Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequality (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
or
[
11
]
) says that for any positive integers
n
and
m

the following holds

|nA-mA|\leqslant\left(\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\right)^{n+m}\cdot|A|\,.

(9)

Therefore,
bound (
9
) connects the cardinality of the original
set
A
and its higher sumsets
nA-mA
.
The common additive energy was defined in (
3
) and the generalization of the additive energy is as follows

\mathsf{T}_{k}(f)=\sum_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{k}=a^{\prime}_{1}+\dots+a^{\prime}_{k}}f(a_{1})\dots f(a_{k})f(a^{\prime}_{1})\dots f(a^{\prime}_{k})\,,

(10)

where
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is an arbitrary function.
We use the same capital letter to denote a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and its characteristic function
A:{\mathbf{G}}\to\{0,1\}
.
Thus,
\mathsf{T}_{2}(A)=\mathsf{E}(A,A)
and for brevity we will write
\mathsf{E}(A)
instead of
\mathsf{E}(A,A)
.
Also, we use representation function notations like
r_{A+B}(x)
or
r_{A-B}(x)
and so on, which counts the number of ways
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
can be expressed as a sum
a+b
or
a-b
with
a\in A
,
b\in B
, respectively.

Given finite sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and a real number
T\geqslant 1
let us define a generalization of the doubling constant (
2
)

\mathcal{D}[A;B]:=\frac{|A+B|}{|A|}\,.

In this paper we use the following new additive–combinatorial characteristics of finite sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}

\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]=\max_{X\subseteq A,\,|X|\geqslant|A|/T,\,Y\subseteq B,\,|X+Y|=|X||Y|}\frac{|X||Y|}{|A|}

\leqslant\mathcal{S}[A;B]=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}[A;B]:=\max_{X\subseteq A,\,Y\subseteq B,\,|X+Y|=|X||Y|}\frac{|X||Y|}{|A|}\leqslant|B|\,,

and

\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]=\max_{X\subseteq A,\,|X|\geqslant|A|/T,\,Y\subseteq B}\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{|A|\mathsf{E}(X,Y)}

\leqslant\mathcal{E}[A;B]=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[A;B]:=\max_{X\subseteq A,\,Y\subseteq B}\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{|A|\mathsf{E}(X,Y)}\leqslant|B|\,.

Clearly, the sequences
\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]
,
\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]
increase monotonically as
T
tends to infinity.
If
A=B
, then we write
\mathcal{D}[A]
for
\mathcal{D}[A;A]
,
\mathcal{S}_{T}[A]
for
\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;A]
and similarly for the all other quantities.

Example 2

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a Sidon set (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Section 6.2]
).
Then
\mathcal{D}[A]=(|A|+1)/2
and
\mathcal{S}[A]\sim\mathcal{E}[A]\sim|A|
, since we can take disjoint sets
X,Y\subseteq A
,
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
such that
|X|=|Y|=[|A|/2]
.

In the general case,
\mathcal{D}[A;B]\neq\mathcal{D}[B;A]
, and similarly for other all quantities.
Thus, our variation quantities given above are asymmetric.
It is easy to
see
that the quantities
\mathcal{D}[A]
and
\mathcal{S}[A]
are different,
consult, for example,
Proposition
20
below.
Finally, notice that if
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
and
R:=|A|/|A^{\prime}|\geqslant 1
, then by definition

\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]\geqslant\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B^{\prime}],\,\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad R\cdot\mathcal{S}_{RT}[A;B]\geqslant\mathcal{S}_{T}[A^{\prime};B]\,.

(11)

Recall that a set
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
is called
dissociated
if any equality of the form

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\varepsilon_{\lambda}\lambda=0\,,\quad\quad\mbox{ where }\quad\quad\varepsilon_{\lambda}\in\{0,\pm 1\}\,,\quad\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda

implies
\varepsilon_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda
.
Let
{\rm dim}(A)
be the size of the largest dissociated subset of
A
and we call
{\rm dim}(A)
the
additive dimension
of
A
.

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
If
a\ll b
and
b\ll a
, then we write
a\sim b
.
All logarithms are to base
e
.
For a prime number
p
we write
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

3
Preliminary results

We start this section discussing some preliminary results on the quantities
\mathcal{S}[A]
,
\mathcal{E}[A]
.
First of all, we need
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
which was proved by a clever random choice.

Theorem 3

Let
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be sets, and
E:=\mathsf{E}(A,B)-|A||B|
.
Suppose that
E>|A|
and for some positive integers
k,l
such that
1\leqslant k\leqslant|A|/2
,
1\leqslant l\leqslant|B|
one has

kl^{2}\leqslant\frac{|A|^{2}|B|^{2}}{2E}\,.

(12)

Then there exists sets
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
,
|A^{\prime}|=k
,
|B^{\prime}|=l
and
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|=|A^{\prime}||B^{\prime}|
.

Also, we need a simple lemma which gives another connection between the quantity
\mathcal{S}[A;B]
and the common additive energy
\mathsf{E}(A,B)
.

Lemma 4

Let
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be sets and
\kappa\in(0,1)
be a real number.
Suppose that
\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|A||B|^{2}/K
.
Then there exists
A_{*}\subseteq A
,
|A_{*}|>(1-\kappa)|A|
and
B_{*}\subseteq B
,
|B_{*}|\gg(\kappa K)^{1/4}
such that the sum

A_{*}+B_{*}
is direct.

P r o o f.

Let
1\leqslant M\leqslant K
be a parameter which we will choose later.
Put

\Omega_{M}=\{b,b^{\prime}\in B,b\neq b^{\prime}~:~|(A+b)\cap(A+b^{\prime})|\geqslant|A|/M\}\,.

We have
\mathsf{E}(A,B)=\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}|(A+b)\cap(A+b^{\prime})|
and hence the size of
\Omega_{M}
does not exceed
M|B|^{2}/K
due to

\frac{|A|}{M}\cdot|\Omega_{M}|\leqslant\mathsf{E}(A,B)-|A||B|<\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{K}\,.

Now choose a set
B_{*}\subseteq B
uniformly at random with probability
\delta=\sqrt{K/2M}\cdot|B|^{-1}
.
Then taking the expectation
\mathbb{E}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}|\Omega_{M}\cap(B_{*}\times B_{*})|=\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\Omega_{M}(\alpha,\beta)\mathbb{E}B_{*}(\alpha)B_{*}(\beta)=\delta^{2}|\Omega_{M}|\,,

and therefore with high probability there is a set
B_{*}\subseteq B
with
2\delta|B|\geqslant|B_{*}|\geqslant 2^{-1}\delta|B|\gg\sqrt{K/M}
and such that
\delta^{2}|\Omega_{M}|<1
.
Now let

A_{*}=A\setminus\left(\bigcup_{b,b^{\prime}\in B_{*},\,b\neq b^{\prime}}(A-b+b^{\prime})\right)

(13)

and by our construction we have

|A_{*}|>|A|-\frac{|B_{*}-B_{*}||A|}{M}\geqslant|A|-\frac{|B_{*}|^{2}|A|}{M}\geqslant|A|-\frac{4K}{2M^{2}}\cdot|A|\geqslant(1-\kappa)|A|\,,

(14)

where we have chosen
M=2\sqrt{K/\kappa}
.
Finally, it is easy to see that the sum
A_{*}+B_{*}
is direct.
It remains to check that
2\delta|B|=2\sqrt{K/2M}=(\kappa K)^{1/4}\leqslant|B|
but the last follows
from the fact that
K\leqslant|B|
. This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Theorem
3
and Lemma
4
(applicable in the case
E\leqslant|A|
), as well as
the definition of the quantity
\mathcal{E}_{1}[A;B]

give us,
in particular, that for any
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
one has

\mathsf{E}(A,B)\gg\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}[A;B]}\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\mathsf{E}(A,B)\geqslant\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{\mathcal{E}_{1}[A;B]}\,,

(15)

and thanks to the second formula of (
11
),
we know that
for any
A^{\prime}\subseteq A

the following holds

\mathsf{E}(A^{\prime},B)\gg\frac{|A^{\prime}|^{3}|B|^{2}}{|A|^{2}\mathcal{S}^{2}_{T}[A;B]}\,,

(16)

where
T=\frac{|A|}{|A^{\prime}|}
.

Let us derive another simple
consequence
of Lemma
4
.
Given a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
consider the quantity

\mathcal{K}(A)=\min_{s\neq 0}\frac{|A|}{|A\cap(A-s)|}\,.

Also, let
k(A)
be the length of the maximal arithmetic progression in
A
. In the next corollary we show that
\mathcal{S}[A]
controls either
k(A)
or
\mathcal{K}(A)
.

Corollary 5

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set.
Then either
k(A)\leqslant 2\mathcal{S}[A]
or

\mathcal{K}(A)\leqslant 8\mathcal{S}[A]
.
In other words,

\mathcal{S}[A]\gg\Omega(\min\{k(A),\mathcal{K}(A)\})\,.

P r o o f.

Let
k=k(A)
,
P\subseteq A
be any arithmetic progression of length
l\leqslant k

and
K=\mathcal{K}(A)
.
By definition, we have
|A\cap(A-s)|\leqslant|A|/K
for all
s\neq 0
.
Repeating the calculations in (
14
) (the set
A_{*}
is defined in (
13
)), we see that

\mathcal{S}[A]\geqslant\frac{|A_{*}||P|}{|A|}\geqslant\frac{l}{2}\,,

provided
|P-P|=2l-1\leqslant K/2
.
Hence either
l\leqslant 2\mathcal{S}[A]
or
2l-1>K/2
.
Thus either
k(A)\leqslant 2\mathcal{S}[A]
or one can take
l=2\mathcal{S}[A]+1
and then obtain
K\leqslant 8\mathcal{S}[A]
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

It is known that sets with small doubling have small additive dimension, see
[
8
]
.
We show that the same is true for sets
having
small quantities
\mathcal{S}[A]
and
\mathcal{E}[A]
.

Corollary 6

Then

{\rm dim}(A)\ll\mathcal{S}^{2}[A]\log|A|\,,\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad{\rm dim}(A)\ll\mathcal{E}[A]\log|A|

P r o o f.

Let
M_{1}=\mathcal{E}[A]
and
M_{2}=\mathcal{S}[A]
.
By definition we have for all sets
X,Y\subseteq A

\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\geqslant\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{M_{1}|A|}\,,

(17)

and for an arbitrary
Z\subseteq A

(thanks to the first formula of (
15
))
the following holds

\mathsf{E}(A,Z)\gg\frac{|A||Z|^{2}}{M^{2}_{2}}\,.

(18)

Let us consider inequality (
17
), as for (
18
) the argument is similar.
Take a dissociated set
\Lambda\subseteq A
such that
|\Lambda|={\rm dim}(A)
and apply the previous inequalities with
X=Z=\Lambda
and
Y=A
. Using the Hölder inequality, as well the Rudin bound (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Section 4]
), namely,
\mathsf{T}_{k}(\Lambda)\leqslant k^{O(k)}|\Lambda|^{k}
for any
k\geqslant 2
, we obtain (see details in
[
8
]
)

\frac{|\Lambda|^{2}|A|}{M_{1}}\leqslant\mathsf{E}(\Lambda,A)\leqslant\mathsf{T}_{k}(\Lambda)^{1/k}|A|^{1+1/k}\ll k|\Lambda||A|^{1+1/k}\ll|\Lambda||A|\log|A|

as we can choose
k\sim\log|A|
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

We conclude this section by computing the value of
\mathcal{S}[A]
for a random set.
The argument of the proof of Lemma
4
suggests that this quantity should not be as large as for Sidon sets, see Example
2
.

Proposition 7

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group,
|{\mathbf{G}}|=N
, where
N
be a sufficiently large number.
Suppose that
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a random set formed by choosing
elements for
A
from
{\mathbf{G}}
independently with probability
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\delta\gg N^{-1/2}
.
Then

\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\ll\mathcal{S}[A]\ll\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\cdot\log^{2}|A|\,.

(19)

If
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, then, in addition,

\mathcal{S}[A]\gg\min\left\{\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{\log|A|}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\}\,.

(20)

P r o o f.

Let
L=\log|A|
.
We follow the argument of the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 4.3, Theorem 1.5]
.
By the Chernoff’s inequality we have
||A|-\delta N|\leqslant 4\sqrt{|A|}
with probability at least
3/4
, say.
Further, writing
f(x)=A(x)-\delta
,
\mathbb{E}f=0
, we see that

\mathsf{E}(A)=\frac{|A|^{4}}{N}+\mathsf{E}(f)-\frac{(|A|-\delta N)^{4}}{N}=\frac{|A|^{4}}{N}+\mathsf{E}(f)+\mathcal{E}\,,

where
|\mathcal{E}|\leqslant 2^{8}|A|^{2}N^{-1}
.
Hence

\mathbb{E}\mathsf{E}(A)=\delta^{4}N^{3}+\mathbb{E}\sum_{x+y=z+w~:~|\{x,y,z,w\}|\leqslant 2}f(x)f(y)f(z)f(w)+\mathcal{E}=\delta^{4}N^{3}+\mathbb{E}Z+\mathcal{E}\,,

where
\mathbb{E}Z\leqslant 3|A|^{2}\leqslant 6\delta^{2}N^{2}
.
Similarly, as
|f(x)|^{2}\leqslant A(x)+\delta^{2}
, we have

\mathbb{E}Z^{2}\leqslant 3\mathbb{E}\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{4}}(A(x_{1})+\delta^{2})\dots(A(x_{4})+\delta^{2})\leqslant 48|A|^{4}\,.

Thus with probability at least
1/2
we have
|Z|\leqslant 10\delta^{2}N^{2}
.
In particular, one has
\mathsf{E}(A)\ll\delta^{4}N^{3}+\delta^{2}N^{2}
and by Theorem
3

(see the first estimate in (
15
))
the following holds

\mathcal{S}[A]\gg\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}
.
Now to obtain the upper bound from (
19
) we apply the argument of the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 4.3]
and derive that

\max_{x\neq 0}\,r_{A-A}(x)\leqslant 2\delta^{2}N+3\log N

with probability greater than
9/10
under the assumption
\delta\geqslant N^{-2/3}
.
Finally, repeating the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 1.5]
and using the obtained bound
|Z|\leqslant 10\delta^{2}N^{2}
, we see that with probability
1-o(1)
one has

kl^{2}\ll L^{2}N

(21)

for any
X,Y\subseteq A
,
|X|=k
,
|Y|=l
,
k\geqslant 2l
and
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
.
Now suppose that
\mathcal{S}[A]
is attained at
X,Y\subseteq A
with
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
,
|X|=k
,
|Y|=l
and such that
k\geqslant 2l
(taking a half of
X
or
Y
we can always assume that the last condition holds).
Combining the lower bound (
19
) and estimate (
21
), we get

\delta^{-1/2}\ll\mathcal{S}[A]\ll\frac{kl}{|A|}\ll\frac{L^{2}}{\delta l}

and hence
l\ll L^{2}\delta^{-1/2}
.
But then

\mathcal{S}[A]\ll\frac{kl}{|A|}\leqslant l\ll L^{2}\delta^{-1/2}

as required.

To get (
20
), simply find an arithmetic progression
P\subseteq A
of size
\Omega(L/\log(1/\delta))
(it is possible to do with high probability). Also, we can assume that
\delta\gg\sqrt{L/N}
(otherwise estimate (
20
) is trivial) and hence

\max_{x\neq 0}\,r_{A-A}(x)\leqslant 2\delta^{2}N+3\log N\ll\delta^{2}N\,.

Using the argument of the proof of Lemma
4
with
B=P
, we see that
\mathcal{S}[A]\gg\delta^{-1}
, provided
|B|\ll\delta^{-1}
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

4
The proof of the main result

Now we are ready to prove our main result which shows that all quantities
\mathcal{S}[A]
,
\mathcal{E}[A]
and
\mathcal{D}[A]
are polynomial equivalent.
Additionally, the estimate (
24
) below says that the size of the maximum direct sum in
A
and
nA
are somewhat comparable.

Theorem 8

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group, and
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be sets.
Then for any
T\geqslant 1
the following holds

1\leqslant\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]\leqslant\mathcal{D}[A;B],\quad\quad\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]\ll T\mathcal{S}^{2}_{T}[A;B]\,,

(22)

and for all
T\geqslant 2
there are positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\mathcal{D}[A]\ll\mathcal{S}^{C_{1}}[A]\,,\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\mathcal{D}[A]\ll\mathcal{E}^{C_{2}}_{T}[A]\,.

(23)

In particular, for any non–zero integers
n
and
m
one has

\mathcal{S}[nA;mA]\leqslant\mathcal{E}[nA;mA]\leqslant\mathcal{D}[nA;mA]\ll\mathcal{S}^{C_{1}(|n|+|m|)}[A]\,.

(24)

P r o o f.

Let
X=A
and let
Y\subseteq B
be any set such that
|Y|=1
. Then we see that
\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]\geqslant 1
.
Now let us show that
\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]
.
Indeed, find
X\subseteq A
,
|X|\geqslant|A|/T
and
Y\subseteq B
such that
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
and
\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]=\frac{|X||Y|}{|A|}
.
Then

\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]\geqslant\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{|A|\mathsf{E}(X,Y)}=\frac{|X||Y|}{|A|}=\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]

as required.
Further, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (
4
), one has
\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\geqslant\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{|A+B|}
and hence for any
T\geqslant 1
the following holds
\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]\leqslant\mathcal{D}[A;B]
.
Finally, by
formulae in (
11
), (
15
), we have (recall that
|X|\geqslant|A|/T
)

\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]=\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{|A|\mathsf{E}(X,Y)}\ll\frac{|X|}{|A|}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}[X;Y]\leqslant\frac{|X|}{|A|}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}[X;B]\leqslant T\mathcal{S}^{2}_{T}[A;B]\,.

In particular, for any fixed
T
the quantities
\mathcal{E}_{T}[A;B]
and
\mathcal{S}_{T}[A;B]
are polynomial equivalent.

Now let
M=\mathcal{S}[A;B]
and
\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|A||B|^{2}/K
.
Applying the first estimate of (
15
) we see that

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{K}\gg\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{M^{2}}\,,

(25)

and therefore
K\ll M^{2}
.
Hence the common additive energy
\mathsf{E}(A,B)
is large.
Recall that in our case
A=B
and we need to show that

\mathcal{D}[A]\ll\mathcal{S}^{C}[A]
.
Using a version
of the Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers Theorem
1
, we find
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
|A^{\prime}|\gg|A|/K
such that
|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\ll K^{C}|A^{\prime}|
, where
C>0
is an absolute constant (one can take
C=4
, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
).
Consider a maximal set
Y\subseteq A
such that the sum
A^{\prime}+Y
is disjoint. Then, by maximality, one has
A\subseteq A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}+Y
and therefore by the Plünnecke inequality (
9
) (also, see
[
6
]
) one has

|A+A|\leqslant|2A^{\prime}-2A^{\prime}+Y+Y|\leqslant|2A^{\prime}-2A^{\prime}||Y|^{2}\ll K^{4C}|Y|^{2}|A^{\prime}|\ll M^{8C}|Y|^{2}|A^{\prime}|\,.

(26)

It remains to estimate
|Y|
.
But using the definition of the quantity
\mathcal{S}[A]
one more time, we get

M|A|\geqslant|A^{\prime}||Y|

and thus

\mathcal{D}[A]|A|^{2}M^{-2}\ll|A+A||A^{\prime}|\ll M^{8C+2}|A|^{2}

as required.
Finally, the first estimate in (
23
) immediately implies (
24
).
Indeed, by the Plünnecke inequality (
9
), we obtain

\mathcal{S}[nA;mA]\leqslant\mathcal{D}[nA;mA]\leqslant\mathcal{D}^{|n|+|m|}[A]\ll\mathcal{S}^{C_{1}(|n|+|m|)}[A]\,.

Now let us obtain the second bound from (
23
),
and it suffices to consider only the case
T=2
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]=M_{*}
.
Then
\mathsf{E}(A)\geqslant|A|^{3}/M_{*}
and applying the Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers Theorem again, we find
A_{1}\subseteq A
,
|A_{1}|\gg|A|/M_{*}
such that
|A_{1}-A_{1}|\ll M^{C}_{*}|A_{1}|
, where
C>0
.
If
|A_{1}|\geqslant|A|/2
, then we can use the argument as above to find a maximal set
X\subseteq A
such that the sum
A_{1}+X
is direct, hence
A\subseteq A_{1}-A_{1}+X
and we obtain an analogue of (
26
) with
K=M_{*}
.
Also, by definition of the quantity
\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]
, we have

|A_{1}||X|=\mathsf{E}(A_{1},X)\geqslant\frac{|A_{1}|^{2}|X|^{2}}{M_{*}|A|}\geqslant\frac{|A_{1}||X|^{2}}{2M_{*}}\,,

and hence
|X|\leqslant 2M_{*}
. Thus

\mathcal{D}[A]\ll M^{4C+2}_{*}
.
Now if
|A_{1}|\leqslant|A|/2
, then we take
A^{c}_{1}=A\setminus A_{1}
,
|A^{c}_{1}|\geqslant|A|/2
and by definition of the quantity
\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]
, one has

\sum_{x}|A^{c}_{1}\cap(A_{1}+x)|^{2}=\mathsf{E}(A^{c}_{1},A_{1})\geqslant\frac{|A^{c}_{1}||A_{1}|^{2}}{2M_{*}}\,.

(27)

Hence there is
x_{1}\in{\mathbf{G}}
such that
|A^{c}_{1}\cap(A_{1}+x_{1})|\geqslant|A_{1}|/(2M_{*})\gg|A|/M^{2}_{*}
.
Put
A_{2}=A_{1}\bigsqcup(A^{c}_{1}\cap(A_{1}+x_{1}))
and then consider
A^{c}_{2}=A\setminus A_{2}
.
If
|A_{2}|\geqslant|A|/2
, then we stop our algorithm.
If not, then we have an analogue of (
27
), namely,

\sum_{x}|A^{c}_{2}\cap(A_{1}+x)|^{2}=\mathsf{E}(A^{c}_{2},A_{1})\geqslant\frac{|A^{c}_{2}||A_{1}|^{2}}{2M_{*}}\,,

and therefore exists
x_{2}\in{\mathbf{G}}
such that
|A^{c}_{2}\cap(A_{1}+x_{2})|\geqslant|A_{1}|/M_{*}\gg|A|/M^{2}_{*}
.
Put
A_{3}=A_{2}\bigsqcup(A^{c}_{2}\cap(A_{1}+x_{2}))
and consider
A^{c}_{3}=A\setminus A_{3}
.
And so on. Clearly, our algorithm must stop after
no more than

s=O(M^{2}_{*})

steps.
At the end we have a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A\cap(A_{1}+Z)
,
|Z|\leqslant s
,
|A^{\prime}|\geqslant|A|/2
.
Applying the argument as above, we find
W\subseteq A
,
|W|\leqslant 2M_{*}
and such that
A\subseteq A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}+W
.
It gives us

|A+A|\leqslant|2A^{\prime}-2A^{\prime}+2W|\leqslant|2A_{1}-2A_{1}+2W+2Z-2Z|\leqslant|2A_{1}-2A_{1}||Z|^{4}|W|^{2}\ll M^{4C+10}_{*}|A_{1}|\,,

and hence
\mathcal{D}[A]\ll M^{4C+10}_{*}
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

As one can see from the proof it is possible to take
C_{1}=36
and
C_{2}=26
in (
23
).
Now let us make a series of remarks concerning possible generalizations and limitations of Theorem
24
.

Remark 9

It is easy to see that
the quantity

R_{A}[A]=\min_{\emptyset\neq X\subseteq A}\frac{|B+X|}{|X|}

appeared in the modern proof of the Plünnecke inequality (
9
) (see
[
6
]
)
is polynomial equivalent to the doubling constant
\mathcal{D}[A]
and hence
\mathcal{S}[A]
.
Indeed, clearly,

R_{A}[A]\leqslant\mathcal{D}[A]

but from the Petridis lemma
[
6
]
one has

\mathcal{D}[A]|A|=|A+A|\leqslant|A+A+X|\leqslant R^{2}_{A}[A]|A|\,,

and thus
\mathcal{D}[A]\leqslant R^{2}_{A}[A]
.

Remark 10

It is easy to see that quantities

\mathcal{S}[A;B],\mathcal{E}[A;B]
and
\mathcal{D}[A;B]
are incomparable.
Indeed, let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
k
be a large integer parameter,
A=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}H_{j}
, where
H_{j}\leqslant{\mathbf{G}}
are mutually additively disjoint, having the same size
h
, and
B=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}S_{j}
, where
S_{j}\subseteq H_{j}
are some sets,
|S_{j}|\sim\sqrt{|H_{j}|}
, say.
Then
|A|=hk
,
|B|\sim k\sqrt{h}
and

\mathcal{D}[A;B]
is
\Omega(hk)
but

\mathcal{S}[A;B]\ll|B|^{2}/|A|=k
is incomparable with
hk
. A similar calculation shows
(or just see the next remark)
that
\mathcal{E}[A;B]\sim k^{2}
.

Nevertheless, it is easy to see that any two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
L:=|A|/|B|\geqslant 1
contain pairs of large subsets
(H_{j},S_{j})
,
j\in[k]
such that
\mathcal{D}[H_{j};S_{j}]
and
\mathcal{S}[H_{j};S_{j}]
are polynomial comparable. Here we give a sketch of the proof. Let
M=\mathcal{S}[A;B]
. Then as in (
25
) one derives
\mathsf{E}(A,B)\geqslant|A||B|^{2}/M^{2}
. After that apply the asymmetric Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers theorem
[
11
, Theorem 2.35]
and find a subset
B^{\prime}=B\cap H
, as well as sets
H,\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
such that
|B^{\prime}|\gg M^{-O_{\varepsilon}(1)}L^{-\varepsilon}|B|
,
|H+H|\ll M^{O_{\varepsilon}(1)}L^{\varepsilon}|H|
and
A^{\prime}=A\cap(H\dotplus\Lambda)
,
|A^{\prime}|\gg M^{-O_{\varepsilon}(1)}L^{-\varepsilon}|A|
,
as well as

|\Lambda||H|\ll M^{O_{\varepsilon}(1)}L^{\varepsilon}|A|
.
Then it is easy to see that
\mathcal{D}[A^{\prime};B^{\prime}]\sim\mathcal{S}[A^{\prime};B^{\prime}]\sim M^{O_{\varepsilon}(1)}L^{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 11

Theorem
24
says, in particular,
that
\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]
and
\mathcal{D}[A]
are polynomial dependent.
Nevertheless, one can show that
the quantities
\mathcal{E}_{1}[A]
and
\mathcal{D}[A]
are incomparable.
Indeed, let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
k
be a large integer parameter,
A=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}H_{j}
, where
H_{j}\leqslant{\mathbf{G}}
are mutually additively disjoint and having the same size
h
. Then
\mathcal{D}[A]\sim|A|

and for an arbitrary
X\subseteq A
,
X=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}(X\cap H_{j})=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}
one has

\mathsf{E}(A,X)\geqslant\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathsf{E}(A_{j},X_{j})\geqslant h\sum_{j=1}^{k}|X_{j}|^{2}\geqslant k^{-1}h|X|^{2}=k^{-2}|A||X|^{2}\,,

and hence
\mathcal{E}_{1}[A]\leqslant k^{2}
.

Once again,
the second formula of (
23
) from Theorem
24
says that
\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]
and
\mathcal{D}[A]
are equivalent.
We
now show that in some sense similar results can be obtained for
\mathcal{S}_{2+o(1)}[A]
.

Corollary 12

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group, and
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set. Suppose that
|A+A|=K|A|
and
\kappa\in(0,1/2)
is a real number.
Then there is an absolute constant
c>0
and sets
X,Y\subseteq A
such that

|X|\geqslant(1/2-\kappa)|A|,\quad|X+Y|=|X||Y|\quad\mbox{and}\quad|X||Y|\gg\kappa^{1/4}K^{c}|A|\,.

P r o o f.

By Theorem
24
we know that
K:=\mathcal{D}[A]
and
M:=\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]
are polynomial equivalent, that is
K\ll M^{C}
, where
C>0
is an absolute constant.
It means that
\mathsf{E}(X_{*},Y_{*})\gg\frac{|X_{*}||Y_{*}|^{2}}{M}
for some
X_{*},Y_{*}\subseteq A
,
|X_{*}|\geqslant|A|/2
.
Using the argument of the proof of Lemma
4
, we find some sets
X\subseteq X_{*}
,
Y\subseteq Y_{*}
such that
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
,
|X|\geqslant(1/2-\kappa)|A|
and
|Y|^{4}\sim\kappa M

as required.
Another possible way to obtain the same is to repeat the exhaustion argument of the proof of Theorem
24
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Remark 13

It is easy to see that it is impossible to replace
1/2-\kappa
to
1/2+\kappa
in Corollary
12
.
Indeed, let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
A=H_{1}\bigsqcup H_{2}
, where
H_{1},H_{2}\leqslant{\mathbf{G}}
are mutually additively disjoint and having the same size
|A|/2
.
Then
\mathcal{D}[A]\sim|A|
but if
X\subseteq A
,
|X|\geqslant(1/2+\kappa)|A|
then it is easy to see that any set
Y\subseteq A
such that
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
satisfies
|Y|\ll\kappa^{-1}
.
It implies that
\mathcal{S}_{(1/2+\kappa)^{-1}}[A]\ll\kappa^{-1}

(and, therefore, according to the formula (
22
) automatically
\mathcal{E}_{(1/2+\kappa)^{-1}}[A]\ll\kappa^{-2}
)
and thus this quantity is incomparable with
\mathcal{D}[A]
.

Let
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be sets.
Suppose that the quantity
\mathcal{S}[A;B]
is attained at
X\subseteq A
,
Y\subseteq B
, that is
\mathcal{S}[A;B]=|X||Y|/|A|
, where
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
.
Then by the maximality one has

B\subseteq X-X+Y\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad A\subseteq Y-Y+X\,.

Thus, an analogue of the covering lemma (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Section 2.4]
) takes place (recall that the sizes of
X,Y
are controllable, namely,
|X||Y|=|A|\mathcal{S}[A;B]
).
Similarly,
we show
that an analogue of the triangle inequality (
7
) holds.
Surprisingly, but our bounds (
28
), (
29
) do not depend on cardinalities of sets unlike in the classical triangle inequality.
In the proof we use an idea from the higher energies
[
10
]
.

Theorem 14

Let
A,B,C\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be sets, and
T\geqslant 1
be a real number.
Then

\mathcal{S}_{T}[B;C]\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]\mathcal{S}[A;C]\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;C]\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]\mathcal{E}[A;C]\,.

(28)

In particular, one has

\mathcal{S}[B;C]\leqslant\mathcal{E}[B;A]\mathcal{S}[A;C]\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\mathcal{E}[B;C]\leqslant\mathcal{E}[B;A]\mathcal{E}[A;C]\,.

(29)

P r o o f.

Find
X\subseteq B
,
Y\subseteq C
such that
|X|\geqslant|B|/T
,
|X+Y|=|X||Y|
and
|X||Y|=|B|\mathcal{S}_{T}[B;C]
.
We have

\sum_{z}|A\cap(X+z)|^{2}=\mathsf{E}(X,A)\geqslant\frac{|X|^{2}|A|^{2}}{\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]|B|}

(30)

and therefore there is
z
such that the set
A^{\prime}:=A\cap(X+z)
has size at least
|A||X|/(\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]|B|)
.
But then

\mathcal{S}[A;C]\geqslant\frac{|A^{\prime}||Y|}{|A|}\geqslant\frac{|X||Y|}{\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]|B|}=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{T}[B;C]}{\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]}

as required.

Similarly, take
X\subseteq B
,
Y\subseteq C
such that
|X|\geqslant|B|/T
and

|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}=|B|\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;C]\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\,.

(31)

For an arbitrary
z\in{\mathbf{G}}
put
A^{X}_{z}=A\cap(X+z)
.
Then by definition, we have

\mathsf{E}(A^{X}_{z},Y)\cdot|A|\mathcal{E}[A;C]\geqslant|A^{X}_{z}|^{2}|Y|^{2}\,.

Summing the last inequality over all
z
, we get

|A|\mathcal{E}[A;C]\cdot\sum_{z}\mathsf{E}(A^{X}_{z},Y)=|A|\mathcal{E}[A;C]\cdot\sum_{w}r_{A-A}(w)r_{X-X}(w)r_{Y-Y}(w)\geqslant|Y|^{2}\mathsf{E}(X,A)\,.

Combining this estimate, as well as formulae (
30
), (
31
), we obtain

|A|^{2}\mathcal{E}[A;C]\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\geqslant|Y|^{2}\frac{|X|^{2}|A|^{2}}{\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]|B|}\geqslant\frac{|A|^{2}\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;C]\mathsf{E}(X,Y)}{\mathcal{E}_{T}[B;A]}

as required.
Finally, taking

T=\infty
, we get
(
29
).
This completes the proof.

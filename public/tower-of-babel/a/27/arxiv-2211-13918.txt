The American Put Option with A Random Time Horizon
Zhuoshu Wu, Libo Li

Abstract
This paper is intended to provide a unique valuation formula for the American put option in a random time horizon; more precisely, such option restricts any stopping rules to be made before the last exit time of the price of the underlying assets at any fixed level. By exploiting the theory of enlargement of filtrations associated with random times, this pricing problem can be transformed into an equivalent optimal stopping problem with a semi-continuous, time-dependent gain function whose partial derivative is singular at certain point. The difficulties in establishing the monotonicity of the optimal stopping boundary and the regularity of the value function lie essentially in these somewhat unpleasant features of the gain function. However, it turns out that a successful analysis of the continuation and stopping sets with proper assumption can help us overcome these difficulties and obtain the important properties that possessed by the free-boundary, which eventually leads us to the desired free-boundary problem. After this, the nonlinear integral equations that characterise the free-boundary and the value function can be derived. The solution to these equations are examined in details. † † The authors are greatly indebted to Professor Ben Goldys for very helpful discussions. Key Words: Option pricing, last exit time, optimal stopping, free-boundary problem

Introduction

Consider a complete defaultable market, with only one stock and one bond whose processes follow the stochastic differential equations:

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=rX_{t}dt+\sigma X_{t}dW_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x>0,

\displaystyle dB_{t}

\displaystyle=rB_{t}dt,\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,B_{0}=1,

where
W=(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion started at zero under measure
P
,
r>0
is the interest rate and
\sigma>0
is the volatility coefficient. This is a risk neutral model so that the discounted stock price is a martingale under measure
P
; in other words,
P
is the risk neutral measure.

Then there is this option with expiration date
T

K

L
(whichever comes first); otherwise, it becomes worthless. How much are you willing to pay for this option?

When a contingent claim is valued, it is conventional to assume that there is no risk that the counter-party will default, that is, the counter party will always be able to make the promised payment. However, this assumption is far less defensible in the non-exchange-traded (or over-the-counter) market and the importance of considering default risk in over-counter markets is recognised by bank capital adequacy standards, see
[
17
, Page 300]
for a detailed introduction of default risk. According to
[

, the contract given above can be viewed as the defaultable American contingent claims, with its default time modelled by the
last exit time
and by assuming the default market is complete, such contract is hedgeable.

A new factor that lies at the heart of current work is the presence of the last exit time in the classic valuation of American contingent claim. For a solid introduction and background of last exit time that is not stopping time, we refer to
[
23
, Chapter 8.2]

In the Infinite-time Horizon

2.1
Reformulation and Basics

In this subsection, we reformulate our main optimal stopping problem and prove some basic facts. By smoothing lemma and the measurability of stopping time
\tau
,

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau}E_{x}\left(E_{x}\left(e^{-r\tau}(K-X_{\tau})^{+}I\{\tau<\theta\}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right)\right),

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau}E_{x}\left(e^{-r\tau}(K-X_{\tau})^{+}P\left(\tau<\theta|\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right)\right).

(2.2)

Next in line is the result of the Azéma supermartingale but first for notational convenience, we set
Z(x)=\left(\frac{L}{x}\right)^{\alpha}\wedge 1
and
\alpha=\frac{2r}{\sigma^{2}}-1<0
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
P\left(\theta>t\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)
be the Azéma supermartingales associated with the random times
\theta
. Then,

\displaystyle P\left(\theta>t\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=Z(X_{t}).

Proof.

As before, the set equality suggests that

\displaystyle\{\theta>t\}=\bigg\{\max_{u\geq t}X_{u}\geq L\bigg\},

so that

\displaystyle P\left(\theta>t\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\max_{u\geq 0}X_{t+u}\geq L\right)=P\left(\max_{u\geq 0}X_{t}e^{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)u+\sigma W_{u}}\geq L\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\max_{u\geq 0}{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)u+\sigma W_{u}}\geq\log{\frac{L}{X_{t}}}\right)=\left(\frac{L}{X_{t}}\right)^{\alpha},

where the last equality follows via the general result in
[
30
, Page 759 and 760]
.
∎

Combining the above computation, the original problems (
2.2
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau}E_{x}\left(e^{-r\tau}(K-X_{\tau})^{+}Z(X_{\tau})\right),

(2.3)

and for the sake of brevity, we denote the gain function
G(x)=(K-x)^{+}Z(x)
.

Let us end this subsection by a useful property possessed by function
Z
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The function
Z(x)
satisfies the following ordinary differential equation, for
x\in(0,L)\cup(L,\infty)
,

\displaystyle rx\frac{d}{dx}Z(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}Z(x)=0.

(2.4)

Proof.

Remember that the Doob-Meyer decomposition of the Azéma supermartingale is given by

\displaystyle Z(X_{t})=Z(X_{0})+M_{t}^{\theta}-A_{t}^{\theta}

(2.5)

and the measure
dA_{t}^{\theta}
is carried by the set
\{t:X_{t}=L\}
.

Now, by the
local time-space formular
, we have

\displaystyle dZ(X_{t})

\displaystyle=\left(rX_{t}Z_{x}(X_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}X_{t}^{2}Z_{xx}(X_{t})\right)I\{X_{t}\neq L\}dt

\displaystyle+\sigma X_{t}Z_{x}(X_{t})I\{X_{t}\neq L\}dW_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\left(Z_{x}(L+)-Z_{x}(L-)\right)dl_{t}^{L}(X),

where
l_{t}^{L}
is the local time of
X
at the level
L
given by

l_{t}^{L}(X)=P-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}I\{|X_{s}-L|<\epsilon\}d\langle X,X\rangle_{s},

which, joining with (
2.5
), shows that

\displaystyle M_{t}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma-\frac{2r}{\sigma}\right)\left(\frac{L}{X_{s}}\right)^{\alpha}I\{X_{s}\neq L\}dW_{s}

\displaystyle A_{t}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{r}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{L}dl_{s}^{L}(X),

and thereby, proving that (
2.4
) holds true.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

An alternative is to compute the first and second derivative of the function
Z
directly. Admittedly, the above proof is more clumsy than direct computation, but latter, as we shall see in the setting of finite time, the method of using Doob-Meyer decompostion is far more convenient.

where

Now, with a little bit of effort, we can compute:

\displaystyle\left(-rG+rxG_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2}G_{xx}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-rKZ-\sigma^{2}x^{2}Z_{x},&\text{for $x<L$},\\

and

\displaystyle G_{x}(L-)=-1-\alpha(K-L)L^{-1},

\displaystyle G_{x}(L+)=-1,

\displaystyle e^{-rt}G(X_{t})

\displaystyle=G(x)+M_{t}-rK\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}I\{L<X_{u}<K\}du

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}\left(rKZ+\sigma^{2}X_{u}^{2}Z_{x}\right)(X_{u})I\{X_{u}<L\}du

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}\alpha(K-L)L^{-1}dl_{u}^{L}(X)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}dl_{u}^{K}(X),

so that

\displaystyle E_{x}\left(e^{-rt}G(X_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=G(x)+E\left(-rK\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}I\{L<X_{u}<K\}du\right.

(2.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}\left(rK-\alpha\sigma^{2}X_{u}\right)Z(X_{u})I\{X_{u}<L\}du\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}\alpha(K-L)L^{-1}dl_{u}^{L}(X)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}dl_{u}^{K}(X)\right).

\displaystyle\tau_{b}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\leq b\}

The next logical task is, of course, to determine whether or not such stopping time

Corollary 2.4
.

The stopping time
\tau_{b}
is finite, i.e.
P_{x}(\tau_{b}<\infty)=1

Proof.

To verify
P_{x}(\tau_{b}<\infty)=1

P_{x}\left(\min\limits_{t\geq 0}X_{t}\leq b\right)=1

With appeal to general result from
[

\displaystyle P_{x}\left(\min\limits_{t\geq 0}xe^{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)t+\sigma W_{t}}\leq b\right)

\displaystyle=P_{x}\left(\min\limits_{t\geq 0}\left(\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)t+\sigma W_{t}\right)\leq\log{\frac{b}{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{x}\left(-\max\limits_{t\geq 0}\left(-\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)t-\sigma W_{t}\right)\leq\log{\frac{b}{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{x}\left(\max\limits_{t\geq 0}\left(-\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)t+\sigma W_{t}\right)\geq-\log{\frac{b}{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=1-P_{x}\left(\max\limits_{t\geq 0}\left(-\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)t+\sigma W_{t}\right)\leq-\log{\frac{b}{x}}\right),

where the third equality holds true as
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}(-W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Hence, for

(i.e.

\displaystyle P_{x}\left(\min\limits_{t\geq 0}X_{t}\leq b\right)=1,

which, joining with the fact that

∎

Another message of the free-boundary problem is that the optimal stopping level

3.1

In this subsection, we once again reformulate problem (

By the measurability of stopping time

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq{T}}E_{0,x}\left(e^{-r\tau}\left(K-X_{\tau}\right)^{+}I\{\theta>\tau\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq{T}}E_{0,x}\left(e^{-r\tau}\left(K-X_{\tau}\right)^{+}P(\theta>\tau|\mathcal{F}_{\tau})\right).

(3.26)

The process
X
is a strong Markov (diffusion) process with the infinitesimal generator given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{X}=rx\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}x^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}.

In the view of (
3.26
), the first thing is, of course, to calculate
P(\theta>\tau|\mathcal{F}_{\tau})
and let us introduce the notation,

\displaystyle Z(t,x)=\left(\Phi\left(\frac{-\log{\frac{L}{x}}+\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)(T-t)}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\right)+\left(\frac{L}{x}\right)^{\alpha}\Phi\left(\frac{-\log{\frac{L}{x}}-\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)(T-t)}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\right)\right)\wedge 1,

where
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}dz
and

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
P\left(\theta>t|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)
be the Azéma supermartingale associated with the random time
\theta
. Then, for
r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma>0
, for

\displaystyle P\left(\theta>t|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=Z(t,X_{t}).

Proof.

First of all, suppose that

\displaystyle{d}_{t}

\displaystyle=\inf\{u\geq t:X_{u}\geq L\}

\displaystyle=t+\inf\{u\geq 0:\bar{X}_{0}=X_{t},\bar{X}_{u}\geq L\},

where the second equality follows from the Markov property of
X
and for easy reference, let

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{L}

\displaystyle=\inf\{u\geq 0:\bar{X}_{0}=X_{t},\bar{X}_{u}\geq L\},

in plain language,
\bar{h}_{L}
is the first time when a geometric Brownian motion
\bar{X}
, starting from
X_{t}
, hits the level
L
.

Then, it follows that

\displaystyle P(\theta>t|\mathcal{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=1-P\left(\theta\leq t|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=1-P\left(d_{t}>T|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=1-P\left(\bar{h}_{L}>T-t|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right).

Upon observing that, in fact,

\displaystyle\{\bar{h}_{L}\leq T-t|\mathcal{F}_{t}\}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\max_{0\leq u\leq T-t}\bar{X}_{u}\geq L|\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigg\}=\bigg\{\max_{0\leq u\leq T-t}X_{t}e^{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)u+\sigma W_{u}}\geq L\bigg\},

(3.27)

after which, an application of general result from
[
30
, Page 759]
proves that, for
\log{\frac{L}{X_{t}}}\geq 0
,
r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma>0
,

\displaystyle P\left(\theta>t|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\frac{-\log{\frac{L}{X_{t}}}+\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)(T-t)}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\frac{L}{X_{t}}\right)^{\alpha}\Phi\left(\frac{-\log{\frac{L}{X_{t}}}-\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)(T-t)}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\right).

while for
\log{\frac{L}{X_{t}}}<0
, observe that event (
3.27
) happens almost surely. As for
t=T
, from the set equality (
3.27
), it follows that

\displaystyle P(\theta>t|\mathcal{F}_{t})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{for $\log{\frac{L}{X_{t}}}\leq 0$},\\
0,&\text{for $\log{\frac{L}{X_{t}}}>0$}.\end{cases}

∎

Having introduced the Azéma supermartingale, the following facts are just around the corner.

Proposition 3.2
.

The function
Z(t,x)
satisfies the following partial differential equation, for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\{(0,L)\cup(L,\infty)\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Z(t,x)+rx\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Z(t,x)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}Z(t,x)=0.

(3.28)

Proof.

Notice that direct computation is certainly an option, but making use of the Doob-Meyer decomposition shall be a much efficient way.

Let us apply the local time-space formula to obtain

\displaystyle dZ(t,X_{t})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Z+rX_{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Z+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}{X_{t}}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}Z\right)(t,X_{t})I\{X_{t}\neq L\}dt

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\sigma X_{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Z(t,X_{t})I\{X_{t}\neq L\}dW_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Z(t,L+)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Z(t,L-)\right)dl_{t}^{L},

(3.29)

where
l_{t}^{L}
is the local time of
X
at the level

given by

l_{t}^{L}=P-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}I\{|X_{s}-L|<\epsilon\}d\langle X,X\rangle_{s},

after which, we can lean on the Doob-Meyer decomposition
Z(t,X_{t})=Z(0,x)+M_{t}-A_{t}
to conclude that

\displaystyle M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma X_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Z(t,X_{s})I\{X_{s}\neq L\}dW_{s},\qquad A_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Z(s,L-)dl_{s}^{L},

where
M_{t}
is the c
\grave{a}
dl
\grave{a}
g martingale and

is the predictable projection whose measure

is carried by the set

, and consequently, (

∎

Lemma 3.6

The value function

Proof.

As was noticed before, the supremum of an l.s.c function defined an l.s.c function, so we only have to establish the continuity (thereby l.s.c) of

To achieve this, we begin by showing that (i)

is continuous on
[0,T)
for each

in

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq E_{t_{1},x}\left(e^{-r\tau}G(t_{1}+\tau,X_{t_{1}+\tau})\right)-E_{t_{2},x}\left(e^{-r\tau}G(t_{2}+\tau,X_{t_{2}+\tau})\right)

\displaystyle=E\left(e^{-r\tau}\left(K-X_{\tau}^{x}\right)^{+}\left(Z(t_{1}+\tau,X_{\tau}^{x})-Z(t_{2}+\tau,X_{\tau}^{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq KE\left(Z(t_{1}+\tau,X_{\tau}^{x})-Z(t_{2}+\tau,X_{\tau}^{x})\right),

where the first inequality is based on the map
t\mapsto G(t,x)
is decreasing, and first equality follows via the time-homogeneous property of GBM; after which, let

, we see that by the dominated convergence theorem (function

),

It remains to show that (ii)

(0,\infty)
for each

. We simply observe that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|E_{t,x_{1}}\left(e^{-r\tau}G(t+\tau,X_{t+\tau})\right)-E_{t,x_{2}}\left(e^{-r\tau}G(t+\tau,X_{t+\tau})\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq E\bigg|Z(t+\tau,X_{\tau}^{x_{1}})(X_{\tau}^{x_{1}}-X_{\tau}^{x_{2}})\bigg|+KE\bigg|Z(t+\tau,X_{\tau}^{x_{1}})-Z(t+\tau,X_{\tau}^{x_{2}})\bigg|

for

By combining assertions (i) and (ii), the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

All points

belongs to the stopping set
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle V^{A}(t,x)=G^{A}(x),

and notice that in this set,

.

Now, knowing that

\displaystyle{V}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T-t}E_{t,x}\left(e^{-r\tau}Z\left(t+\tau,X_{t+\tau}\right)(K-X_{t+\tau})^{+}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T-t}E_{t,x}\left(e^{-r\tau}(K-X_{t+\tau})^{+}\right)=V^{A}(t,x),

where the inequality is due to function

\displaystyle 0=V^{A}(t,x)-G^{A}(x)\geq{V}(t,x)-{G}(t,x),

on the set

and thereby, proving the desired assertion.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

The value function
V
is continuous on
[0,T)\times(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The proof involves proving two assertions:
(i) The map
t\mapsto V(t,x)
is continuous on
[0,T)
for each
x\in(0,\infty)
given and fixed.
Take any
t_{1}<t_{2}
in
[0,T)
, let
\epsilon>0
and
\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}
be the stopping time such that

\displaystyle E_{t_{1},x}\left(e^{-r\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}G\left(t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon},X_{t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}\right)\right)\geq V(t_{1},x)-\epsilon.

Then, by setting
\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}=\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}\wedge(T-t_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle E_{t_{2},x}\left(e^{-r\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}G\left(t_{2}+\tau_{2}^{\epsilon},X_{t_{2}+\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}\right)\right)\leq V(t_{2},x).

and that

\displaystyle 0\leq V(t_{1},x)-V(t_{2},x)

\displaystyle\leq E\left(e^{-r\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}G\left(t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon},X_{t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)\right)-E\left(e^{-r\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}G\left(t_{2}+\tau_{2}^{\epsilon},X_{t_{2}+\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)\right)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq E\left(e^{-r\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}\left(G\left(t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon},X^{x}_{\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}\right)-G\left(t_{2}+\tau_{2}^{\epsilon},X^{x}_{\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}\right)\right)\right)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq E\left(G\left(t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon},X^{x}_{\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}\right)-G\left(t_{2}+\tau_{2}^{\epsilon},X^{x}_{\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}\right)\right)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq E\left(\left(X^{x}_{\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}-X^{x}_{\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}\right)^{+}Z\left(t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon},X^{x}_{\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,\,\,+\left.\left(K-X^{x}_{\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}\right)^{+}\left(Z\left(t_{1}+\tau_{1}^{\epsilon},X^{x}_{\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}}\right)-Z\left(t_{2}+\tau_{2}^{\epsilon},X^{x}_{\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}}\right)\right)\right)+\epsilon,

(3.45)

where the first inequality is because of the map
t\mapsto V(t,x)
being decreasing and the last inequality holds via

\displaystyle(K-y)^{+}-(K-z)^{+}\leq(z-y)^{+}\qquad\text{for $y,z\in\mathbb{R}$}.

(3.46)

By letting
t_{1}\to t_{2}
, using
\tau_{1}^{\epsilon}\to\tau_{2}^{\epsilon}
and then
\epsilon\to 0
on (
3.45
), the dominated convergence theorem allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle V(t_{1},x)-V(t_{2},x)\to 0,

and assertion (i) follows.

(ii) The map
x\mapsto V(t,x)
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
for each
t\in[0,T)
given and fixed.

Note that by the up-down connectedness of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, for any
x_{1}<x_{2}
in
(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle G(t,x_{2})-G(t,x_{1})\leq V(t,x_{2})-V(t,x_{1}),

and let
\tau_{2}
be optimal for
V(t,x_{2})
such that

\displaystyle G(t,x_{2})-G(t,x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq V(t,x_{2})-V(t,x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq E\left(e^{-r\tau_{2}}G(t+\tau_{2},X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{2}})\right)-E\left(e^{-r\tau_{2}}G(t+\tau_{2},X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{1}})\right),

(3.47)

after which, by letting
x_{1}\to x_{2}
, the dominated convergence theorem leads us to

V(t,x_{2})-V(t,x_{1})\to 0

and thereby, proving the left-continuity of
V
.

And, needless to say, if
x_{2}\leq B(t)
, Lemma
3.12
tells us that
\tau_{2}=0
and the continuity of
V
follows directly from that of the gain function
G
.

On the other hand, if
x_{2}>B(t)
, we know there exists
\delta>0
so that
B(t)>L+\delta>L
(see figure
2
) and that for
0<x_{2}-x_{1}<\frac{x_{1}\delta}{2L}
, immediately from the monotonicity of
B
, we have

X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{2}}\geq B(t+\tau_{2})>B(t)>L+\delta,

which implies
X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{1}}>L
a.s.; more precisely, by the strong solution of GBM and the optimality of
\tau_{2}
,
X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{2}}>L+\delta
entails

e^{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\tau_{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{2}}}>\frac{L+\delta}{x_{2}},

and that for
x_{2}\in\left(0,x_{1}\left(1+{\delta}/{(2L)}\right)\right)
,

\displaystyle X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{1}}=x_{1}e^{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\tau_{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{2}}}>\frac{x_{1}(L+\delta)}{x_{2}}>\frac{x_{1}(L+\delta)}{x_{1}\left(1+\delta/(2L)\right)}=L+\frac{\delta L}{\delta+2L}>L,

after which, it follows that as
Z(t,x)=1
for
x>L
,

\displaystyle G(t,x_{2})-G(t,x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq V(t,x_{2})-V(t,x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq E\Big(e^{-r\tau_{2}}G\left(t+\tau_{2},X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{2}}\right)\Big)-E\Big(e^{-r\tau_{2}}G\left(t+\tau_{2},X_{\tau_{2}}^{x_{1}}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(x_{1}-x_{2})^{+}E\left(e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\tau_{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{2}}}\right)=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{+},

and then by letting
x_{2}\to x_{1}
, we have
V(t,x_{2})-V(t,x_{1})\to 0
, that is, the right-continuity of

. By combining the claims that

is left and right-continuous for
x\in(0,\infty)
for each
t\in[0,T)
given and fixed, statement (ii) follows.
∎

By taking advantage of the above analysis , we obtain the following result:

Proposition 3.17

(The Free-boundary Problem)
.

The stopping set and the continuation set are of the forms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\{(t,x)\in[0,T)\times(0,\infty):x>b(t)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\{(t,x)\in[0,T)\times(0,\infty):x\leq b(t)\}\cup\{(T,x):x\leq b(T)\},

where the map
t\mapsto b(t)
is increasing.

The free-boundary problem is rearranged as follows:

\displaystyle{V}_{t}+\mathbb{L}_{x}{V}-r{V}=0\qquad\qquad\text{for $(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}$},

(3.48)

\displaystyle{V}(t,x)=K-x\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for $x=b(t)$, instantaneous stopping},

(3.49)

\displaystyle{V}_{x}(t,x)=-1\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for $x=b(t)$, smooth-fit},

(3.50)

\displaystyle{V}(t,x)>{G}(t,x)\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for $(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}$},

(3.51)

\displaystyle{V}(t,x)={G}(t,x)\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for $(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}$}.

(3.52)

Just to complete the picture of the free-boundary problem, we now justify equation (
3.50
).

Proof of the Smooth-fit Condition.

Let a point
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,\infty)
lying on the boundary
b
be fixed, i.e.
x=b(t)
. Then,
L<x<K
and for all
\epsilon>0
such that
L<x+\epsilon<K
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{V(t,x+\epsilon)-V(t,x)}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{G(t,x+\epsilon)-G(t,x)}{\epsilon}=\frac{(K-x-\epsilon)-(K-x)}{\epsilon}=-1,

of which, taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
, we have
\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{V(t,x+\epsilon)-V(t,x)}{\epsilon}\geq-1
.

In order to prove the converse inequality, we fix a sufficiently small
\epsilon
so that
0<\epsilon<\frac{x-L}{2}
and that
L<x+\epsilon<K
, and consider the optimal stopping time
\tau_{\epsilon}
for
V(t,x+\epsilon)
so that the monotonicity of
b
tells us that
X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}=b(t+\tau_{\epsilon})>b(t)\geq L
implies
X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}>L
almost surely, as before, by the strong solution of GBM,

e^{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\tau_{\epsilon}+\sigma W_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}>\frac{b(t)}{x+\epsilon}=\frac{x}{x+\epsilon},

so that, for
x>L
,

\displaystyle xe^{\left(r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\tau_{\epsilon}+\sigma W_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}>\frac{x^{2}}{x+\epsilon}=\frac{x^{2}-\epsilon^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}{x+\epsilon}>x-\epsilon>x-\frac{x-L}{2}=\frac{x+L}{2}>L.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{V(t,x+\epsilon)-V(t,x)}{\epsilon}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}E\left(e^{-r\tau_{\epsilon}}G(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})\right)-E\left(e^{-r\tau_{\epsilon}}G(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\epsilon}E\left(e^{-r\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\right)^{+}-\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)^{+}\right)Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\,\,+\left.e^{-r\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)^{+}\left(Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})-Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}E\left(e^{-r\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\right)Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\,\,+\left.e^{-r\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)^{+}\left(Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})-Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-E\left(e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{\epsilon}}{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\frac{1}{\epsilon}E\left(e^{-r\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)^{+}\left(Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})-Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-E\left(e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{\epsilon}}{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\frac{1}{\epsilon}E\left(\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)^{+}\left(Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})-Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-E\left(e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{\epsilon}}{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\frac{K}{\epsilon}E\left(I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}<L\}\left(Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})-Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-E\left(e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{\epsilon}}{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\}\right)

where the second inequality follows from

\displaystyle\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\right)^{+}-\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle=\left(X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\right)I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\}-\left(K-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)^{+}I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\geq K\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}-X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\right)I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\},

(3.53)

and the second equality is from the strong solution of
X
and linearity; moreover, the fourth inequality is due to the fact that
I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\geq L\}=1
entails
I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\geq L\}=1
and that
Z\left(t+\epsilon,X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)=Z\left(t+\epsilon,X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}\right)=1
, while we used
I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}<L\}=0
for chosen
\epsilon
in the last step. Via an appeal to the dominated convergence theorem, upon letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we have
\tau_{\epsilon}\to 0
(see
A.1
for its justification) so that

\displaystyle E\left(e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}\tau_{\epsilon}}{2}+\sigma W_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}Z(t+\tau_{\epsilon},X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon})I\{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x+\epsilon}<K\}\right)\to 1.

Thus,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{V(t,x+\epsilon)-V(t,x)}{\epsilon}\leq-1,

after which, the conclusion follows via the fact that for
(t,x)
and
(t,x-\epsilon)
belong to
\mathcal{D}
,

\frac{V(t,x)-V(t,x-\epsilon)}{\epsilon}=\frac{G(t,x)-G(t,x-\epsilon)}{\epsilon}=-1.

∎

To prepare for the main theorem presented in Subsection

Lemma 3.19
.

The optimal stopping boundary
b
is continuous on
[0,T]
and
b(T-)=K
.

Proof.

Th proof proceeds in three steps, which follows essentially from
[
24
, Page 383]
.

(i) The boundary
b
is right-continuous on
[0,T]
.

Let us fix
t\in[0,T)
and take a sequence
t_{n}\downarrow t
as
n\to\infty
. Since
b
is increasing, the right-limit
b(t+)
exists.
Remember that
\mathcal{D}
is a closed set, meaning that its limit point
\lim_{n\to\infty}(t_{n},b(t_{n}))\to(t,b(t+))
is contained in
\mathcal{D}
, it then follows, together with the construction of the stopping set that
b(t+)\leq b(t)
. However, the fact that
b
is increasing suggests that
b(t+)\geq b(t)
and thereby, forcing the equation
b(t+)=b(t)
and proving statement (i).

(ii) The boundary
b
is left-continuous on
[0,T)
.

Assume that, for contradiction, at some point
t\in(0,T)
, the function makes a jump, that is,
b(t)>b(t-)
. Then, fix a point
y_{*}\in(b(t-),b(t))
. By (
3.49
) and (
3.50
), we have

\displaystyle V(s,y_{*})-G(s,y_{*})=\int_{b(s)}^{y_{*}}\int_{b(s)}^{x}\left(V_{xx}-G_{xx}\right)(s,z)dzdx,

(3.54)

for each
s\in(t-\delta,t)
, where
\delta>0
and
t-\delta>0
. Despite the fact that function
G
is not differentiable at
L
, the fact that
b(t)>L
once again comes to our rescue.

Knowing that the value function satisfies (
3.48
), we see that, for
(t,x)\in\mathcal{R}
, where
\mathcal{R}
is a curved trapezoid formed by the vertices
(s,b(s))
,
(s,y_{*})
,
(t,b(t-))
and
(t,y_{*})
,

\displaystyle V_{xx}(t,x)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}x^{2}}\left(rV-V_{t}-rxV_{x}\right)(t,x)>\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}x^{2}}rV(t,x)>\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}x^{2}}rG(t,x)\geq c>0,

where the first strict inequality follows via the fact that both the maps
t\mapsto V(t,x)
and
x\mapsto V(t,x)
are decreasing on
\mathcal{R}
, and the last one is due to the fact that
x<K
on
\mathcal{R}
.
Moreover,
G_{xx}(t,x)=0
on
\mathcal{R}
.

Then, from the continuity of the value function and the gain function, upon letting
s\uparrow t
on (
3.54
) and using dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle V(t-,y_{*})-G(t-,y_{*})=V(t,y_{*})-G(t,y)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{b(t-)}^{y_{*}}\int_{b(t-)}^{x}cdzdx

\displaystyle=\frac{c(y_{*}-b(t-))^{2}}{2}>0,

which is a contradiction, since
(t,y_{*})\in\mathcal{D}
, indicating that such point cannot exist and
b(t-)=b(t)
must hold true.
Immediate from statements (i) and (ii), the continuity of

Proposition 3.20
.

On the state space

is right-connected and the continuation set

Proof.

Remark 3.23

Knowing from derivative computation that for each fixed

Proof of Lemma
3.22
.

(i) To show that
G
is uniformly continuous on
[0,t_{*}]\times(0,L]
, is to show that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
so that for all
(t_{1},x_{1}),(t_{2},x_{2})\in[0,t_{*}]\times(0,L]
,
\lvert t_{1}-t_{2}\rvert+\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\rvert<\delta
implies
\lvert G(t_{1},x_{1})-G(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert<\epsilon
.

Notice that

\displaystyle\lvert G(t_{1},x_{1})-G(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x_{2}-x_{1}\rvert Z(t_{1},x_{1})+\lvert K-x_{2}\rvert\lvert Z(t_{1},x_{1})-Z(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle<\lvert x_{2}-x_{1}\rvert+K\lvert Z(t_{1},x_{1})-Z(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert.

Observe that on
[0,t_{*}]\times(0,L]
,

\displaystyle\lvert Z(t_{1},x_{1})-Z(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert Z(t_{1},x_{1})-Z(t_{2},x_{1})+Z(t_{2},x_{1})-Z(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert Z(t_{1},x_{1})-Z(t_{2},x_{1})\rvert+\lvert Z(t_{2},x_{1})-Z(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq M\lvert t_{1}-t_{2}\rvert+N\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\rvert,

where the second inequality holds true by the mean value theorem and the fact that the partial derivatives of
Z_{x}(t,x)
and
Z_{t}(t,x)
are bounded, say by constants
N
and
M
, on the set
[0,t_{*}]\times(0,L]
(see (
A.85
), (
A.86
)). Hence,

\displaystyle\lvert G(t_{1},x_{1})-G(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert<KM\lvert t_{1}-t_{2}\rvert+(KN+1)\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\rvert,

and we can now choose
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{\max\{KM,KN+1\}}
and verify that if
(t_{1},x_{1}),(t_{2},x_{2})\in[0,t_{*}]\times(0,L]
satisfy
\lvert t_{1}-t_{2}\rvert+\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\rvert<\delta
, then

\displaystyle\lvert G(t_{1},x_{1})-G(t_{2},x_{2})\rvert<\epsilon,

thus proving statement (i) as desired.

(ii) The uniform continuity of
G
means that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,t_{*}]
and
x\in(0,L]
,

\displaystyle\text{$\lvert t_{1}-t_{2}\rvert<\delta$ implies $\lvert G(t_{1},x)-G(t_{2},x)\rvert<\epsilon$ },

which, after rearranging, equals

\displaystyle G(t_{2},x)-\epsilon\leq G(t_{1},x)\leq G(t_{2},x)+\epsilon,

so we must have,

\displaystyle\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{1},x)

\displaystyle\geq\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{2},x)-\epsilon,

\displaystyle\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{1},x)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{2},x)+\epsilon,

after which, the conclusion follows from

\displaystyle\lvert\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{1},x)-\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{2},x)\rvert<\epsilon.

(iii) Assume that, for contradiction, statement (iii) is false.
To say that,
t\mapsto x^{*}(t)
is not continuous on
[0,t_{*}]
means that there exists a sequence
(t_{n})\subseteq[0,t_{*}]
where
(t_{n})\to t
(Since the the set is compact, the limit point

is always contained on
[0,t_{*}]
.) such that

does not converge
x^{*}(t)
.

According to statement (ii) and the definition of

,

\displaystyle\lim_{t_{n}\to t}\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{n},x)\to\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t,x)=G(t,x^{*}(t)),

but the assumption is

\displaystyle\lim_{t_{n}\to t}\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t_{n},x)\to\max_{x\in(0,L]}G(t,x)=G\left(t,\lim_{t_{n}\to t}x^{*}(t_{n})\right)\neq G(t,x^{*}(t)),

which is a contradiction, we may therefore conclude that statement (iii) holds true.
∎

Proposition 3.34

(The Free-boundary Problem)
.

The stopping set and the continuation set are of the forms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{(t,x)\in[0,T)\times(0,\infty):x>b(t)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=\{(t,x)\in[0,T)\times(0,\infty):x\leq b(t)\},

where the map
t\mapsto b(t)
is increasing. The free-boundary problem in section 2.2 is therefore reshuffled accordingly as

\displaystyle{V}_{t}+\mathbb{L}_{x}{V}-r{V}=0\qquad\qquad\text{for $(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}$},

(3.59)

\displaystyle{V}(t,x)=G(t,x)\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for $x=b(t)$ (instantaneous stopping)},

(3.60)

\displaystyle{V}_{x}(t,x)=G_{x}(t,x)\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for $x=b(t)<L$ and $x=b(t)>L$ (smooth-fit)},

(3.61)

8. Useful result for proving smooth-fit condition:

Lemma A.1
.

As
\epsilon\to 0
,
\tau_{\epsilon}\to 0
almost surely.

Proof.

By the construction of
\tau_{\epsilon}
and the strong solution of
X
,

\displaystyle\tau_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\inf\{0\leq s\leq T-t:X_{t+s}^{x+\epsilon}\leq b(t+s)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{0\leq s\leq T-t:W_{s}\leq\frac{\log{\frac{b(t+s)}{x+\epsilon}}-\beta s}{\sigma}\bigg\}=\inf\{0\leq s\leq T-t:-W_{s}\geq g(s)\},

where
\beta=r-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
and
g(s)=\frac{-\log{\frac{b(t+s)}{x+\epsilon}}+\beta s}{\sigma}
.
As
\epsilon\to 0
(recalling that
x=b(t)
),

\displaystyle g(s)\to\frac{-\log{\frac{b(t+s)}{b(t)}}+\beta s}{\sigma}\leq\frac{\beta s}{\sigma},

where the inequality is from the map
t\mapsto b(t)
being increasing and since
s\mapsto\frac{\beta s}{\sigma}
is the lower function of the Brownian motion, so is
g(s)
, implying that
P(\tau_{\epsilon}=0)=1
, which is precisely the claim.
∎

On certain DG-algebra resolutions
Tony J. Puthenpurakal

Abstract
. In this paper we give several classes of Non-Gorenstein local rings A A which satisfy the property that Ext A i ​ ( M , A ) = 0 \text{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,A)=0 for i ≫ 0 i\gg 0 then projdim A ​ M \text{projdim}_{A}M is finite. We also show that if injdim A ​ M = ∞ \text{injdim}_{A}M=\infty then over such rings the bass-numbers of M M (with respect to 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} ) are unbounded. When A A is a hypersurface ring we give an alternate proof of a result due to Takahashi regarding thick subcategories of the stable category of maximal Cohen-Macaulay A A -modules. This result of Takahashi implies some results due to Avramov, Buchweitez, Huneke and Wiegand. The technique used to prove our results is that the minimal resolution of the relevant rings have an appropriate DG-algebra structure (philosophically this technique is due to Nasseh, Ono, and Yoshino).

2.

In this section we recall some notions of differentially graded algebra and modules. Throughout
Q
is a commutative Noetherian ring. We assume the reader is acquainted with the basic notions on complexes of
Q
-modules. We index complexes homologically, i.e.,

M\colon\cdots\rightarrow M_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}}M_{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}}\cdots.

If
m\in M_{n}
then we set
|m|=n
. We say

M_{n}=0
for all

.
All complexes considered in this paper will be bounded below
.
The tensor product of two
Q

will be denoted by

and the Hom complex will be denoted as
\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M,N)
.

2.1
.

By a DG (= differential graded)
Q
-algebra we mean an associative algebra
\mathcal{R}
over
Q
equipped with a
Q
-linear map
d\colon\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{i}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{i}\mathcal{R}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{i+j}
for all
i,j
.

(2)

\mathcal{R}
has a unit element
1
with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=Q1
and
\mathcal{R}_{i}
is a finitely generated
Q
-module for all
i\geq 0
.

(3)

\mathcal{R}
is strictly skew commutative, i.e.,
xy=(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
for all
x,y
and
x^{2}=0
if
|x|
is odd.

(4)

The map
d
is a skew-derivation of degree
-1
, i.e.,
d(\mathcal{R}_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n-1}
,
d^{2}=0
and

d(xy)=d(x)y+(-1)^{|x|}xd(y).

The underlying algebra is the ring
\mathcal{R}^{\natural}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{i}
. A morphism of DG
Q
-algebras is a
chain map

f\colon\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
between DG
R
-algebras respecting
products and multiplicative identities:

f(rt)=f(r)f(t)
and
f(1)=1
.

2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a DG-
Q
algebra. By a DG
\mathcal{R}
-module we mean a
Q
-complex

such that

is an associative, unital
\mathcal{R}^{\natural}
-module and for all

and

A morphism of DG
\mathcal{R}
-modules is a

that respects scalar multiplication:
f(am)=af(m)
. We say

is a quasi-isomorphism if the induced maps
H_{i}(f)\colon H_{i}(M)\rightarrow H_{i}(N)
are isomorphism for all
i
.

2.3
.

A bounded below DG
\mathcal{R}
-module

is said to be

if

is a free
\mathcal{R}^{\natural}
-module. Suppose
M
is a bounded below complex. Then

has a

, i.e., a quasi-isomorphism

with

If

a finitely generated

-module,

2.4
.

Let

be DG
\mathcal{R}
-modules. A (homogeneous) map

of the underlying complexes is said to be

-linear if
\beta(rm)=(-1)^{|r||\beta|}r\beta(m)
for all homogeneous

A homomorphism

The action

Let

3.1

Let

-module structure. We note that

Assume

\mathcal{R}

\overline{\mathcal{R}}
-module.

(1)

Note that

-regular. Clearly

\overline{\mathcal{R}}

(2)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{R})}(F,N)=H_{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{R}}(F,N))=H_{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}(\overline{F},N))=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\overline{\mathcal{R}})}(\overline{F},N).

(3)

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{R}}^{i}(F,N)=\operatorname{Ext}_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}^{i}(\overline{F},N)

We need the following result

Lemma 3.4

\mathcal{R}
be a DG

This induces a triangle

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{R})}(F,M[-i])\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{R})}(F,M[-i])\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{R})}(F,\overline{M}[-i])\rightarrow

. So again we get a strictly decreasing sequence of positive integers, a contradiction.

Thus

.
∎

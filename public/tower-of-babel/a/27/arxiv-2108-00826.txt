Euler sums of generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers II
Rusen Li

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a new type of generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers H n ( p , r , s 1 , s 2 ) H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})} , and show that Euler sums of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers H n ( p , r , s 1 , s 2 ) H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})} can be expressed in terms of linear combinations of classical (alternating) Euler sums. Keywords: generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers, alternating Euler sums, truncated Faulhaber’s formula, combinatorial approach



1
Introduction and preliminaries

The generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers (of type I)
H_{n}^{(p,r,1)}
are introduced by the author
[
12
]
which are defined as

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(p,r,1)}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}H_{k}^{(p,r-1,1)}\quad(n,p,r\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\cdots\},H_{n}^{(p,1,1)}=H_{n}^{(p)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{p}})\,.

These numbers are alternating analogues of the classical generalized hyperharmonic numbers which are defined by (see
[
6
,
17
]
)

H_{n}^{(p,r)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}H_{j}^{(p,r-1)}\quad(n,p,r\in\mathbb{N}),

where
H_{n}^{(p,1)}=H_{n}^{(p)}
are the classical generalized harmonic numbers. It is obvious that
H_{n}^{(1,r)}=h_{n}^{(r)}
are the classical hyperharmonic numbers
[
2
,
4
]
. In particular
H_{n}^{(1,1)}=H_{n}
are the classical harmonic numbers. It is obvious that these harmonic numbers play an essential role in number theory and combinatorics. For some applications in analysis of algorithms and some other areas, please see Knuth’s book
[
10
]
.

In this paper, as a natural consideration, we introduce a new type of generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
, which are unified extension of the generalized hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r)}
and the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers (of type I)
H_{n}^{(p,r,1)}
. We also study the properties of their Euler sums of type
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}/{{n}^{q}}
. We will show that their Euler sums can be expressed in terms of linear combinations of classical (alternating) Euler sums.

In the spirit of Flajolet and Salvy
[
7
]
, we write four types of linear (alternating) Euler sums as

\displaystyle S_{p,q}^{+,+}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{(p)}}{{n}^{q}},\quad S_{p,q}^{+,-}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{H_{n}^{(p)}}{{n}^{q}},

\displaystyle S_{p,q}^{-,+}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{H}_{n}^{(p)}}{{n}^{q}},\quad S_{p,q}^{-,-}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{\overline{H}_{n}^{(p)}}{{n}^{q}}\,,

where
\overline{H}_{n}^{(p)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}/{j^{p}}
denotes the classical generalized alternating harmonic numbers. When
p\geq 0
,
H_{n}^{(-p)}
and
\overline{H}_{n}^{(-p)}
are understood to be the sum

\sum_{j=1}^{n}j^{p}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}j^{p}
, respectively.

For convenience, we recall the well-known Hurwitz zeta function defined by

\zeta(s,a)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+a)^{s}}\quad(s\in\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{R}e(s)>1,a>0).

Note that
\mathbb{C}
denotes the set of complex numbers and
\mathfrak{R}e(s)
denotes the real part of the complex number
s
. When
a=1
,
\zeta(s,1)
is the famous Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}
.

Euler sums of harmonic numbers have been studied by many famous mathematicians since the time of Euler, for the reason that the classical (alternating) Euler sums are closely related to the Riemann zeta function. A well-known result
[
7
]
that can be traced back to the time of Euler is as the following:

2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}}{n^{m}}=(m+2)\zeta(m+1)-\sum_{n=1}^{m-2}\zeta(m-n)\zeta(n+1),\quad m=2,3,\cdots.

By using numerical computations, Bailey, Borwein and Girgensohn
[
1
]
determined, whether or not a particular infinite sum involving the generalized harmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(m)}
could be expressed as a rational linear combination of several given constants.

Since hyperharmonic and generalized (alternating) hyperharmonic numbers are hyper-generalizations of harmonic numbers, their Euler sums (see
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
]
for example) may have interesting relations with the Riemann zeta function.

The most powerful method for computing Euler sums of harmonic numbers is the contour integral representation approach developed by Flajolet and Salvy
[
7
]
. Due to some parity restrictions, some Euler sums can only be computed in pairs. For hyperharmonic number case, Mező and Dil
[
16
]
considered the following infinite sum

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{n}^{(r)}}{n^{m}}\quad(m\geq r+1,m\in\mathbb{N}),

and showed that it could be reduced to infinite series involving the Hurwitz zeta function values. Later Dil and Boyadzhiev
[
5
]
extended this result to infinite series involving the multiple sums of the Hurwitz zeta function values.

The analytic continuation of the Riemann zeta function has been studied extensively, the reader can find its analytic continuation in any standard textbook of analytic number theory. On the contrary, the analytic continuation of Euler sums of hyperharmonic numbers has also attracted mathematician’s interest. If we regard
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}^{(r)}/{n^{s}}
as a complex function in variable
s
, i.e. we regard the hyperharmonic number as a weight in the infinite series, we can obtain more results in this direction. For example, Matsuoka
[
15
]
proved that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}^{(1)}/{n^{s}}
admits a meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane. Kamano
[
9
]
proved that the complex variable function
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}^{(r)}/{n^{s}}
could be meromorphically continued to the entire complex plane. In addition, the residue at each pole was also given.

As a natural consideration, Dil, Mező and Cenkci
[
5
]
considered the Euler sums of the generalized hyperharmonic numbers

\zeta_{H^{(p,r)}}(m):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}^{(p,r)}/{n^{m}}.

They proved that for positive integers
p,r
and
m
with
m>r
,
\zeta_{H^{(p,r)}}(m)
could be reduced to infinite series of multiple sums of the Hurwitz zeta function values, which extended Dil-Boyadzhiev’s result. For
r=1,2,3
,
\zeta_{H^{(p,r)}}(m)
were also written explicitly by means of (multiple) zeta values. By computing a series of special polynomials, the author
[
11
]
obtain a nice formula for the generalized hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r)}
. As a corollary, the author proved that
\zeta_{H^{(p,r)}}(m)
could be expressed as linear combinations of classical Euler sums.

Apart from analytic properties, there are some more interesting combinatorial properties about the generalized hyperharmonic numbers. For instance, Ömür and Koparal
[
17
]
introduced the generalized hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r)}
from a combinatorial point of view, defined two
n\times n
matrices
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
with
a_{i,j}=H_{i}^{(j,r)}
and
b_{i,j}=H_{i}^{(p,j)}
, respectively, and gave some interesting factorizations and determinant properties of the matrices
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
.

Motivated by Flajolet-Salvy’s paper
[
7
]
and Dil-Mező-Cenkci’s paper
[
6
]
, the author
[
12
]
introduced the notion of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
and proved that Euler sums of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers (of type I)
H_{n}^{(p,r,1)}
could be expressed in terms of linear combinations of classical (alternating) Euler sums.

Before going further, we introduce the definition of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
.

Definition 1
.

For
p,r,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with
s_{1}+s_{2}\geq 1
, define the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers of type
(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})
as

\displaystyle{\scriptsize H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}:=\begin{cases}\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}^{(p,r-1,s_{1},s_{2})},&\text{$r-1\equiv 1,\cdots,s_{1}\pmod{s_{1}+s_{2}}$};\\
\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}H_{k}^{(p,r-1,s_{1},s_{2})},&\text{$r-1\equiv s_{1}+1,\cdots,s_{1}+s_{2}\pmod{s_{1}+s_{2}}$}.\end{cases}\,}

(1)

It is easy to verify that if
H_{n}^{(p,1,s_{1},s_{2})}=H_{n}^{(p)}
, then

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}=\begin{cases}H_{n}^{(p,r)},&\text{$s_{1}=1,s_{2}=0$};\\
H_{n}^{(p,r,1)},&\text{$s_{1}=0,s_{2}=1$}.\end{cases}\,

In this paper, we firstly investigate two special cases
H_{n}^{(p,r,2,1)}
and
H_{n}^{(p,r,1,2)}
, and prove that Euler sums of these two numbers could be reduced to linear combinations of classical (alternating) Euler sums. Secondly, by using similar method, we obtain same results for the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
. In addition, we also present several conjectures on these coefficients.

To be able to reach our goal, we recall truncated Faulhaber’s formula on (alternating) sums of powers.

It is well known that the sum of powers of consecutive intergers
1^{k}+2^{k}+\cdots+n^{k}
can be explicitly expressed in terms of Bernoulli numbers or Bernoulli polynomials. Faulhaber’s formula can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ell^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{j}B_{j}^{+}n^{k+1-j}

(2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}(B_{k+1}(n+1)-B_{k+1}(1))\quad\hbox{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CFZ}{}{}]}}\,,

(3)

where Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}^{+}
are determined by the recurrence formula

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{j}B_{j}^{+}=k+1\quad(k\geq 0)

or by the generating function

\displaystyle\frac{t}{1-e^{-t}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{+}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

(4)

and Bernoulli polynomials
B_{n}(x)
are defined by the following generating function

\frac{te^{xt}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,.

We now provide explicit expressions of truncated Faulhaber’s formula on (alternating) sums of powers.

Lemma 1
.

[
12
]

For
m,n,t\in\mathbb{N}
with
t\leq n
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=t}^{n}\ell^{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}c(m,j)n^{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}d(m,j)t^{j}-e(m,0)\,,

where

\displaystyle c(m,j)=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{m+1}{m+1-j}B_{m+1-j}^{+}\quad(1\leq j\leq m+1)\,,

\displaystyle d(m,j)=\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{k=j-1}^{m}\binom{m+1}{m-k}B_{m-k}^{+}\binom{1+k}{j}(-1)^{1+k-j}\quad(1\leq j\leq m+1)\,,

\displaystyle e(m,0)=\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m+1}{m-k}B_{m-k}^{+}(-1)^{1+k}\,.

Remark 1
.

By considering
t=(1-e^{-t})\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{+}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
, using Taylor expansion and comparing coefficients on both sides, we get that
e(m,0)=-\delta_{m0}
, where
\delta_{mn}
is the Kronecker delta, that is,
\delta_{mm}=1
,
\delta_{mn}=0
for
m\neq n
.

Lemma 2
.

[
12
]

For
m,n,t\in\mathbb{N}
with
t\leq n
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=t}^{n}(-1)^{\ell-1}\ell^{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{1}(m,j)(-1)^{n-1}n^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{m}d_{1}(m,j)(-1)^{t-1}t^{j}\,,

where

\displaystyle c_{1}(m,j)=\frac{1}{2(m+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{m-j}\binom{m+1}{k}B_{k}^{+}2^{k}\binom{m+1-k}{j}(-1)^{m-k-j}\,,

\displaystyle d_{1}(m,j)=\frac{1}{2(m+1)}\sum_{k=j}^{m}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\sum_{x=0}^{m-k}\binom{m+1}{x}B_{x}^{+}2^{x}\binom{m+1-x}{k}(-1)^{m-x-k}\,.

Remark 2
.

It seems that the following identities hold:

\displaystyle c(m,j)=(-1)^{m+1-j}d(m,j)\quad(1\leq j\leq m+1)\,,

\displaystyle c_{1}(m,j)=(-1)^{m-j}d_{1}(m,j)\quad(0\leq j\leq m)\,.

Since these two identities are not essential in the proof of our main theorem, we leave it as a problem to the reader.

2
Euler sums of generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,2,1)}

In this section, we prove that Euler sums of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,2,1)}
can be expressed in terms of linear combinations of classical (alternating) Euler sums. In order to reach our goal, we introduce some notations.

Definition 2
.

For
r,n,t\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle T(r,2,1,n,t)=\begin{cases}\sum_{k=t}^{n}T(r-1,2,1,k,t),&\text{$r-1\equiv 1,2\pmod{3}$};\\
\sum_{k=t}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}T(r-1,2,1,k,t),&\text{$r-1\equiv 3\pmod{3}$}.\end{cases}

Definition 3
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle f(r,2,1)=\begin{cases}f(r-1,2,1)+1,&\text{$r-1\equiv 1,2,6\pmod{6}$};\\
f(r-1,2,1),&\text{$r-1\equiv 3,4,5\pmod{6}$}.\end{cases}

By direct computation, we have

\displaystyle T(1,2,1,n,t)=1\,,

\displaystyle T(2,2,1,n,t)=n-t+1\,,

\displaystyle T(3,2,1,n,t)=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+(-t+\frac{3}{2})n+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}-\frac{3}{2}t+1\,,

\displaystyle T(4,2,1,n,t)=\frac{1}{4}(-1)^{n-1}n^{2}+(-\frac{1}{2}t+1)(-1)^{n-1}n+\frac{1}{4}(-1)^{n-1}t^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-(-1)^{n-1}t+\frac{7}{8}(-1)^{n-1}+\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{t-1}\,,

\displaystyle T(5,2,1,n,t)=\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{n-1}n^{2}+\bigl(-\frac{1}{4}(-1)^{n-1}t+\frac{5}{8}(-1)^{n-1}+\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{t-1}\bigr)n

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{n-1}t^{2}-\bigl(\frac{5}{8}(-1)^{n-1}+\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{t-1}\bigr)t+\frac{11}{16}(-1)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{5}{16}(-1)^{t-1}\,,

\displaystyle T(6,2,1,n,t)=\frac{1}{16}\bigl((-1)^{n-1}+(-1)^{t-1}\bigr)(n^{2}+t^{2})-\frac{1}{8}\bigl((-1)^{n-1}+(-1)^{t-1}\bigr)nt

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{3}{8}\bigl((-1)^{n-1}+(-1)^{t-1}\bigr)(n-t)+\frac{1}{2}\bigl((-1)^{n-1}+(-1)^{t-1}\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle T(7,2,1,n,t)=\frac{1}{48}(n^{3}-t^{3})+\bigl(\frac{7}{32}+\frac{1}{32}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}\bigr)(n^{2}+t^{2})-\frac{1}{16}n^{2}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{16}nt^{2}-nt\bigl(\frac{7}{16}+\frac{1}{16}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\bigl(\frac{67}{96}+\frac{7}{32}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}\bigr)(n-t)+\frac{21}{32}+\frac{11}{32}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}\,,

\displaystyle T(8,2,1,n,t)=\frac{1}{192}n^{4}+n^{3}\bigl(-\frac{1}{48}t+\frac{1}{12}\bigr)+n^{2}\bigl(\frac{1}{32}t^{2}-\frac{1}{4}t+\frac{89}{192}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{64}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}n^{2}+n\bigl(-\frac{1}{48}t^{3}+\frac{1}{4}t^{2}-\frac{89}{96}t\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+n\bigl(-\frac{1}{32}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}t+\frac{25}{24}+\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{192}t^{4}-\frac{1}{12}t^{3}+\bigl(\frac{89}{192}+\frac{1}{64}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}\bigr)t^{2}-\frac{25}{24}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}t+\frac{99}{128}+\frac{29}{128}(-1)^{n-1+t-1}\,.

\displaystyle f(1,2,1)=0,\quad f(2,2,1)=1,\quad f(3,2,1)=2,\quad f(4,2,1)=2,

\displaystyle f(5,2,1)=2,\quad f(6,2,1)=2,\quad f(7,2,1)=3,\quad f(8,2,1)=4\,.

Observing the above facts, we may assume

\displaystyle T(r,2,1,n,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}\sum_{j=0}^{f(r,2,1)-m}\biggl(b(r,2,1,m,j,0)+b(r,2,1,m,j,1)(-1)^{n-1+t-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+b(r,2,1,m,j,2)(-1)^{n-1}+b(r,2,1,m,j,3)(-1)^{t-1}\biggr)t^{j}n^{m}\,,

with

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,k)=0\quad(m+j\geq f(r,2,1)+1,m\geq 0,j\geq 0,k=0,1,2,3)\,.

We now prove the above assumption by mathematical induction.

Lemma 3
.

For
r,n,t\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle T(r,2,1,n,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}\sum_{j=0}^{f(r,2,1)-m}\biggl(b(r,2,1,m,j,0)+b(r,2,1,m,j,1)(-1)^{n-1+t-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+b(r,2,1,m,j,2)(-1)^{n-1}+b(r,2,1,m,j,3)(-1)^{t-1}\biggr)t^{j}n^{m}\,,

where

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,2)=b(r,2,1,m,j,3)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 1,2,3\pmod{6}\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,0)=b(r,2,1,m,j,1)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 4,5,6\pmod{6}\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,1)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 1\pmod{6},\quad m,j\geq 0,\quad m+j\geq f(r,2,1)\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,1)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 2\pmod{6},\quad m,j\geq 0,\quad m+j\geq f(r,2,1)-1\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,1)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 3\pmod{6},\quad m,j\geq 0,\quad m+j\geq f(r,2,1)-2\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,3)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 4\pmod{6},\quad m,j\geq 0,\quad m+j\geq f(r,2,1)-1\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle b(r,2,1,m,j,3)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 5\pmod{6},\quad m,j\geq 0,\quad m+j\geq f(r,2,1)\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}b(r,2,1,m,f(r,2,1)-m,0)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 2,3\pmod{6}\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)-1}b(r,2,1,m,f(r,2,1)-1-m,0)=0\quad\bigl(r\equiv 3\pmod{6}\bigr)\,,

and for
k=0,1,2,3
,
b(r,2,1,m,j,k)
satisfy the following recurrence relations:

Case
1
, if
r\equiv 1,2\pmod{6}
, then we have
f(r+1,2,1)=f(r,2,1)+1
,

\displaystyle b(r+1,2,1,m,j,0)=\sum_{\ell=m-1}^{f(r,2,1)}b(r,2,1,\ell,j,0)c(\ell,m)\,,

\displaystyle(1\leq m\leq f(r,2,1)+1,\quad 0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)+1-m)\,;

\displaystyle\quad b(r+1,2,1,0,j,0)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ell=j\\
0\leq j_{1}\leq f(r,2,1)-m\\
1\leq\ell\leq m+1\end{subarray}}b(r,2,1,m,j_{1},0)d(m,\ell)+b(r,2,1,0,j,0)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ell=j\\
0\leq j_{1}\leq f(r,2,1)-m\\
0\leq\ell\leq m\end{subarray}}b(r,2,1,m,j_{1},1)d_{1}(m,\ell)\quad(0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)+1)\,;

\displaystyle b(r+1,2,1,m,j,1)=\sum_{\ell=m}^{f(r,2,1)}b(r,2,1,\ell,j,1)c_{1}(\ell,m)\,,

\displaystyle(0\leq m\leq f(r,2,1),\quad 0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)-m)\,.

Case
2
, if
r\equiv 3\pmod{6}
, then we have
f(r+1,2,1)=f(r,2,1)
,

\displaystyle b(r+1,2,1,m,j,2)=\sum_{\ell=m}^{f(r,2,1)}b(r,2,1,\ell,j,0)c_{1}(\ell,m)\,,

\displaystyle(0\leq m\leq f(r,2,1),\quad 0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)-m)\,;

\displaystyle b(r+1,2,1,m,j,3)=\sum_{\ell=m-1}^{f(r,2,1)}b(r,2,1,\ell,j,1)c(\ell,m)\,,

\displaystyle(1\leq m\leq f(r,2,1)+1,\quad 0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)+1-m)\,;

\displaystyle\quad b(r+1,2,1,0,j,3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ell=j\\
0\leq j_{1}\leq f(r,2,1)-m\\
0\leq\ell\leq m\end{subarray}}b(r,2,1,m,j_{1},0)d_{1}(m,\ell)+b(r,2,1,0,j,1)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ell=j\\
0\leq j_{1}\leq f(r,2,1)-m\\
1\leq\ell\leq m+1\end{subarray}}b(r,2,1,m,j_{1},1)d(m,\ell)\quad(0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)+1)\,.

Case
3
, if
r\equiv 4,5\pmod{6}
, then we have
f(r+1,2,1)=f(r,2,1)
,

\displaystyle b(r+1,2,1,m,j,2)=\sum_{\ell=m}^{f(r,2,1)}b(r,2,1,\ell,j,2)c_{1}(\ell,m)\,,

\displaystyle(0\leq m\leq f(r,2,1),\quad 0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)-m)\,;

\displaystyle b(r+1,2,1,m,j,3)=\sum_{\ell=m-1}^{f(r,2,1)}b(r,2,1,\ell,j,3)c(\ell,m)\,,

\displaystyle(1\leq m\leq f(r,2,1)+1,\quad 0\leq j\leq f(r,2,1)+1-m)\,;

\displaystyle\quad b(r+1,2,1,0,j,3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,2,1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ell=j\\
0\leq j_{1}\leq f(r,2,1)-m\\
0\leq\ell\leq m\end{subarray}}b(r,2,1,m,j_{1},2)d_{1}(m,\ell)+b(r,2,1,0,j,3)

with the help of Lemma

1\leq\ell\leq m+1\end{subarray}}b(r,s_{1},s_{2},m,j_{1},2)d(m,\ell)+b(r,s_{1},s_{2},0,j,2)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ell=j\\
0\leq j_{1}\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m\\
0\leq\ell\leq m\end{subarray}}b(r,s_{1},s_{2},m,j_{1},3)d_{1}(m,\ell)\quad(0\leq j\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})+1)\,;

\displaystyle b(r+1,s_{1},s_{2},m,j,1)=\sum_{\ell=m}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})}b(r,s_{1},s_{2},\ell,j,3)c_{1}(\ell,m)\,,

\displaystyle(0\leq m\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2}),\quad 0\leq j\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m)\,.

The initial value is given by
T(1,s_{1},s_{2},n,t)=1
.

We have the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
r,p\in\mathbb{N}
and
q
be a positive real number with
q\geq r+1
, we have,

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}}{n^{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})}\sum_{j=0}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m}\biggl(b(r,s_{1},s_{2},j,m,0)S_{p-m,q-j}^{+,+}+b(r,s_{1},s_{2},j,m,1)S_{p-m,q-j}^{-,-}

\displaystyle\qquad+b(r,s_{1},s_{2},j,m,2)S_{p-m,q-j}^{+,-}+b(r,s_{1},s_{2},j,m,3)S_{p-m,q-j}^{-,+}\biggr).

Therefore Euler sums of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
can be expressed in terms of linear combinations of classical (alternating) Euler sums.

We now present the following three conjectures on the coefficients.

Conjecture 1
.

For
r,m,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq m\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})
and
0\leq j\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m
, we conjecture that:

b(r,s_{1},s_{2},m,j,k)=(-1)^{m+j}b(r,s_{1},s_{2},j,m,k)\quad(k=0,1,2,3).

Conjecture 2
.

For
r,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we conjecture that:

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{3}b(r,s_{1},s_{2},m-j,j,k)=\delta_{m0}\quad\bigl(0\leq m\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})\bigr),

where
\delta_{mn}
is the Kronecker delta, that is,
\delta_{mm}=1
,
\delta_{mn}=0
for
m\neq n
.

Conjecture 3
.

For
r,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq m\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})
, we conjecture that:
for
0\leq m\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})
and
0\leq j\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\sum_{k=0}^{3}b(r,s_{1},s_{2},m,j,k))=(-1)^{j}\,,

where
\mathrm{sgn}(x)
is the signum function defined by

\mathrm{sgn}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{$x>0$},\\
0&\text{$x=0$},\\
-1&\text{$x<0$}.\end{cases}

In particular for
0\leq m\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})
and
0\leq j\leq f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{3}b(r,s_{1},s_{2},m,j,k)\neq 0\,.

5
Nonlinear Euler sums of generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}

In this section, we investigate nonlinear Euler sums of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k,r_{1},\cdots,r_{k},p_{1},p_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{1,1},\cdots,s_{k,1},s_{1,2},\cdots s_{k,2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},1\leq i\leq k,s_{i,1}+s_{i,2}\geq 1
and
q
be a positive real number large enough, we can obtain that
nonlinear Euler sums of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
can be expressed in terms of linear combinations of classical nonlinear (alternating) Euler sums.

Proof.

Using Lemma
3
, we can write

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{(p_{1},r_{1},s_{1,1},s_{1,2})}\cdots H_{n}^{(p_{k},r_{k},s_{k,1},s_{k,2})}}{n^{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{q}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{m_{i}=0}^{f(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2})}\sum_{j_{i}=0}^{f(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2})-m_{i}}\biggl(b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},0)n^{j_{i}}H_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},1)(-1)^{n-1}n^{j_{i}}\bar{H}_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},2)

\displaystyle\quad\times(-1)^{n-1}n^{j_{i}}H_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},3)n^{j_{i}}\bar{H}_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}\biggr)\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}=0}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m_{1}}\cdots\sum_{m_{k}=0}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})}\sum_{j_{k}=0}^{f(r,s_{1},s_{2})-m_{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{k}\biggl(b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},0)n^{j_{i}}H_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},1)(-1)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times n^{j_{i}}\bar{H}_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},2)(-1)^{n-1}n^{j_{i}}H_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},3)n^{j_{i}}\bar{H}_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}\biggr).

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{q}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\biggl(b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},0)n^{j_{i}}H_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},1)(-1)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times n^{j_{i}}\bar{H}_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},2)(-1)^{n-1}n^{j_{i}}H_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad+b(r_{i},s_{i,1},s_{i,2},j_{i},m_{i},3)n^{j_{i}}\bar{H}_{n}^{(p_{i}-m_{i})}\biggr)

is reduced to linear combinations of classical nonlinear (alternating) Euler sums, we obtain the desired result.
∎

6
Some open problems

We now provide some open problems for further considerations.

Problem 1
.

It is well known (see
[
2
]
) that the (hyper-)harmonic numbers are closely related to (
r
-)Stirling numbers:

\displaystyle H_{n}=\frac{\left[n+1\atop 2\right]}{n!}\,,h_{n}^{r}=\frac{\left[n+r\atop r+1\right]_{r}}{n!}\,,

where where
\left[n\atop k\right]
and
\left[n\atop k\right]_{r}
denote the (unsigned) Stirling number of the first kind and
r
-Stirling number, respectively. So we may ask such a question: Can we find the corresponding Stirling numbers for the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
?

Problem 2
.

Göral and Sertbaş (see
[
8
]
) studied divisibility properties of hyperharmonic numbers and extend Wolstenholme’s theorem to them. Moreover, they proved that all hyperharmonic numbers in their reduced fractional form are odd and provided
p
-adic value lower bounds for certain hyperharmonic numbers. Can we extend Wolstenholme’s theorem to the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
? Is it possible to obtain similar divisibility properties for the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
?

Problem 3
.

Kamano
[
9
]
proved the analytic continuation of
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}^{(r)}/{n^{s}}
. Is it possible to obtain similar analytic continuation for Euler sums of the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
? Similar with the infamous Riemann Hypothesis, can we study the nontrivial zeros of this complex function
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}/{n^{s}}
?

Problem 4
.

Ömür and Koparal
[
17
]
defined two
n\times n
matrices
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
with
a_{i,j}=H_{i}^{(j,r)}
and
b_{i,j}=H_{i}^{(p,j)}
, respectively, and gave some interesting factorizations and determinant properties of the matrices
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
. Is it possible to obtain similar determinant properties for the generalized alternating hyperharmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(p,r,s_{1},s_{2})}
?

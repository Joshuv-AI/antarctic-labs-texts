Work done in a decoherence process
Gianluca Francica

Abstract
We investigate the thermodynamics of a quench realized by turning on an interaction generating pure decoherence. We characterize the work probability distribution function, also with a fluctuation relation, a lower bound of the work and by considering some physical examples.



I
Introduction

The interaction of an open quantum system with is environment can lead to the phenomenon of decoherence
zurek91
;
bookBreuer06
. Specifically, the interaction creates correlations between the states of the system and the environment and destroys superposition of these states in the course of time. In the last decades the non-equilibrium thermodynamics has received a great attention
kosloff13
;
Vinjanampathy16
;
bookthermo18
and recently also the thermodynamics of decoherence has been investigated
Popovic21
.

In the same spirit of Ref.
Popovic21
, in this paper we examine the thermodynamics of a quench realized by turning on an interaction generating pure decoherence. In this case the work done does not change the internal energy of the system and is completely converted to heat. In particular, we express the work probability distribution function as a convex combination of probability distribution functions of the environment. Thus, we derive a fluctuation relation characterizing the process and a lower bound of the work done. We proceed with the investigation by considering some physical examples.

II
Thermodynamics of the process

We consider a system interacting with its environment, such that for times
t>0
the total Hamiltonian is of the form

H=H_{S}+H_{B}+H_{I}

(1)

where
H_{S}
and
H_{B}
describe the free evolution of the system and the environment, respectively. The interaction is taken to be

H_{I}=\sum_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes B_{n}

(2)

where
|n\rangle
are the eigenstates of the system Hamiltonian
H_{S}
and
B_{n}
are arbitrary environment hermitian operators. We recall that, due to this kind of interaction, an initial state
|\Psi(0)\rangle=\sum_{n}c_{n}|n\rangle\otimes|\phi\rangle
, where
|\phi\rangle
is an arbitrary environment state, evolves into the entangled system-environment state
|\Psi(t)\rangle=U_{t,0}|\Psi(0)\rangle=\sum_{n}c_{n}|n\rangle\otimes|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle
, where the time evolution operator is
U_{t,0}=e^{-iHt}
. By considering the reduced system density matrix
\rho_{S}(t)=\text{Tr}_{B}\left\{|\Psi(t)\rangle\langle\Psi(t)|\right\}
, we note that, since
\langle\phi_{n}(t)|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle=1
, the populations are
\langle n|\rho_{S}(t)|n\rangle=\left|c_{n}\right|^{2}
and are constant in time. Conversely, the coherences are
\langle n|\rho_{S}(t)|m\rangle=c_{n}c_{m}^{*}\langle\phi_{m}(t)|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle
, with
n\neq m
, and typically the overlap
\langle\phi_{m}(t)|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle
rapidly decreases in time, generating the phenomenon of pure decoherence. In particular it is useful to define the decoherence function
\Gamma_{nm}(t)
(for
n\neq m
) such that
\left|\langle\phi_{n}(t)|\phi_{m}(t)\rangle\right|=e^{\Gamma_{nm}(t)}
.

Here, we take in exam the quench
H_{0}\to H
, where initially the interaction is turned off, i.e. for times
t\leq 0
the Hamiltonian reads

H_{0}=H_{S}+H_{B}

(3)

and the total system is prepared in the initial equilibrium state

\rho(0)=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{0}}}{Z_{0}}=\rho_{S}(0)\otimes\rho_{B}(0)

(4)

where the partition function is
Z_{0}=\text{Tr}\left\{e^{-\beta H_{0}}\right\}
and the initial reduced states are
\rho_{X}(0)=e^{-\beta H_{X}}/Z_{X}
with
Z_{X}=\text{Tr}\left\{e^{-\beta H_{X}}\right\}
, for
X=S,B
.
The energy difference of the system after a time
t
is
\langle\Delta E_{S}\rangle=\text{Tr}\left\{(U_{t,0}^{\dagger}H_{S}U_{t,0}-H_{S})\rho(0)\right\}
and, by noting that
[H,H_{S}]=0
, we obtain
\langle\Delta E_{S}\rangle=0
for any time
t>0
. We note that, also for strong coupling, the internal energy change, as defined in Ref.
rivas20
, is equal to
\langle\Delta E_{S}\rangle=0
(see Appendix). From a thermodynamic point of view, this means that the work done on the system is equal to the heat exchange with its environment, i.e.
\langle w\rangle=\langle Q\rangle
. In particular, the work reads

\langle w\rangle=\text{Tr}\left\{(U_{t,0}^{\dagger}HU_{t,0}-H_{0})\rho(0)\right\}=\text{Tr}\left\{H_{I}\rho(0)\right\}\,.

(5)

It is worth noting that the work is produced suddenly at the instant in which the interaction is turned on. Thus, during the decoherence process, i.e. for
t>0
, no heat is exchanged with the environment. Anyway, we can realize a pure decoherence process generated by a time-dependent Hamiltonian, for instance by considering time-dependent operators
B_{n}(t)
(such that
B_{n}(0)=0
), such that work, and so heat (always equal to the work), are produced during the decoherence process.
By focusing on the interaction of Eq. (
2
), with constant
B_{n}
, the work reads

\langle w\rangle=\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}\text{Tr}\left\{B_{n}\rho_{B}(0)\right\}\,,

(6)

where we have defined the system populations
p^{S}_{n}=\langle n|\rho_{S}(0)|n\rangle
. Thus, the work is the weighted mean of the averages of the environment operators
B_{n}
, calculated with respect to the state
\rho_{B}(0)
, with weights
p^{S}_{n}
.

By considering a two-measurement scheme, the work can be expressed as
\langle w\rangle=\int wp(w)dw
, where
p(w)
is the work probability distribution function (see, e.g., Ref.
campisi11
for its general definition).
By taking in account the eigenvalue equation

(H_{B}+B_{n})|E^{n}_{k}\rangle=E^{n}_{k}|E^{n}_{k}\rangle\,,

(7)

the work probability distribution function can be expressed as the convex combination

p(w)=\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}p_{n}(w)

(8)

where
p_{n}(w)
is the probability distribution function

p_{n}(w)=\sum_{mk}p^{B}_{m}p^{B}_{nk|m}\delta(w-E^{n}_{k}+E_{m})

(9)

where the transition probability is
p^{B}_{nk|m}=\left|\langle E^{n}_{k}|E_{m}\rangle\right|^{2}
,
|E_{m}\rangle
is the eigenstate of
H_{B}
with eigenvalue
E_{m}
and the environment populations are
p^{B}_{m}=\langle E_{m}|\rho_{B}(0)|E_{m}\rangle
. The proof of Eq. (
8
) immediately follows from the definition of
p(w)
by noting that the eigenstates of
H
are the states
|n\rangle\otimes|E^{n}_{k}\rangle
. Physically,
p_{n}(w)
represents the probability distribution function of the environment energy change in the quench
H_{B}\to H_{B}+B_{n}
, starting from the initial state
\rho_{B}(0)
. Since
\rho_{B}(0)
is an equilibrium state, we obtain the Jarzynski equality
campisi11
;
Jarzynski97

\langle e^{-\beta w}\rangle_{n}=e^{-\beta\Delta F_{B}^{n}}

(10)

where the average
\langle\cdots\rangle_{n}
is calculated with respect to
p_{n}(w)
, and the free energy difference is
\Delta F_{B}^{n}=F^{n}_{B}-F_{B}
with
F_{B}^{n}=-\ln(Z_{B}^{n})/\beta
,
Z_{B}^{n}=\text{Tr}\left\{e^{-\beta(H_{B}+B_{n})}\right\}
and
F_{B}=-\ln(Z_{B})/\beta
.
Thus, from Eq. (
10
) we get the Jarzynski equality

\langle e^{-\beta w}\rangle=\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}e^{-\beta\Delta F_{B}^{n}}=e^{-\beta\Delta F}

(11)

where
\Delta F
is the total free energy difference. In particular, in this form, the Eq. (
11
) relates the work done to the free energy differences of the environment.
From Eq. (
11
), by using the Jensen’s inequality we obtain the second law

\langle w\rangle\geq\Delta F=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}e^{-\beta\Delta F_{B}^{n}}\right)

(12)

Since
f(x)=-\ln x
is a convex function, we have

\Delta F\leq\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}F_{B}^{n}-F_{B}\,.

(13)

Conversely, by applying the Jensen’s inequality to Eq. (
10
), we obtain

\langle w\rangle_{n}\geq F_{B}^{n}-F_{B}\,,

(14)

from which, since
\langle w\rangle=\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}\langle w\rangle_{n}
, we get the tighter lower bound of the work

\langle w\rangle\geq\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}F_{B}^{n}-F_{B}\geq\Delta F\,.

(15)

Of course, from Eq. (
15
) we also get a lower bound of the irreversible work
\langle w_{irr}\rangle=\langle w\rangle-\Delta F
.
We note that if all the operators
B_{n}
commutate with the environment Hamiltonian
H_{B}
, i.e.
[H_{B},B_{n}]=0
for each
n
, the probability distribution function
p_{n}(w)
simplifies into
p_{n}(w)=\sum_{m}p^{B}_{m}\delta(w-E^{n}_{m}+E_{m})
.
Furthermore, we observe that our results inherent in the work probability distribution function and the lower bound can be immediately generalized to the case of time-dependent operators
B_{n}(t)
by taking the transition probability
p^{B}_{nk|m}=\left|\langle E^{n}_{k}(t)|e^{-iH_{B}t}V_{n}(t)|E_{m}\rangle\right|^{2}
, where we have defined the unitary operator
V_{n}(t)=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}B^{I}_{n}(s)ds}
, the time-ordering operator
\mathcal{T}
and
B^{I}_{n}(t)=e^{iH_{B}t}B_{n}(t)e^{-iH_{B}t}
. Thus, in this case
\langle w\rangle_{n}
reads

\displaystyle\langle w\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{(V_{n}^{\dagger}(t)H_{B}V_{n}(t)-H_{B})\rho_{B}(0)\right\}

(16)

\displaystyle+\text{Tr}\left\{V_{n}^{\dagger}(t)B^{I}_{n}(t)V_{n}(t)\rho_{B}(0)\right\}\,.

We recall that by considering the initial state
\rho(0)=|\psi(0)\rangle\langle\psi(0)|\otimes\rho_{B}(0)
, the decoherence function takes the form
\Gamma_{nm}(t)=\ln\left|\text{Tr}\left\{V_{m}^{\dagger}(t)V_{n}(t)\rho_{B}(0)\right\}\right|
, then there seems to be no strong relation between the decoherence and the work done.
We note that by switching off the interaction at the final time the work will be equal to the environment energy change, such that we get as particular case the situation investigated in Ref.
Popovic21
.

III
Physical examples

We consider a system that is a qubit with Hamiltonian
H_{S}=-\omega\sigma_{z}/2
, where the matrix
\sigma_{z}
is the third Pauli matrix and the parameter
\omega
is the difference of energy levels of the system. We focus on the interaction
H_{I}=\sigma_{z}\otimes B
. By defining the eigenstates
|0\rangle
and
|1\rangle
of
\sigma_{z}
with eigenvalues
1
and
-1
, the interaction reads
H_{I}=|0\rangle\langle 0|\otimes B_{0}+|1\rangle\langle 1|\otimes B_{1}
with
B_{0}=-B_{1}=B
. We have the work

\langle w\rangle=\delta p^{S}\text{Tr}\left\{B\rho_{B}(0)\right\}

(17)

where we have defined the population unbalance
\delta p^{S}=p^{S}_{0}-p^{S}_{1}=\tanh(\beta\omega/2)
, and the second moment

\langle w^{2}\rangle=\text{Tr}\left\{B^{2}\rho_{B}(0)\right\}\,.

(18)

We start by considering a bosonic environment with Hamiltonian
H_{B}=\sum_{k}\omega_{k}a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}
and
B=\sum_{k}g_{k}(a_{k}+a_{k}^{\dagger})
. We label with
k
the modes of the environment having frequency
\omega_{k}
and creation and destruction operators
a^{\dagger}_{k}
and
a_{k}
, such that
[a_{k},a_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
and the real parameters
g_{k}
are the coupling constants.
We have that
\text{Tr}\left\{B\rho_{B}(0)\right\}=0
, then
\langle w\rangle=0
such that we need a non-linear interaction for obtaining
\langle w\rangle\neq 0
. Anyway, the higher work moments can be different from zero, for instance the second moment reads
\langle w^{2}\rangle=\sum_{k}g^{2}_{k}\coth(\beta\omega_{k}/2)
.

We proceed by considering a fermionic environment with Hamiltonians
H_{B}=-t\sum_{j}(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j})-\mu\sum_{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}
and
B=\sum_{j}g_{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}
. In particular, the parameter
t
is the hopping amplitude,
\mu
is the chemical potential and
c^{\dagger}_{j}
and
c_{j}
are creation and destruction operators, such that
\{c_{j},c_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}
. In this case the work can be non-zero.
We focus on the particular case of a homogeneous coupling
g_{j}=g
, such that we have
B=gN
where the number operator is
N=\sum_{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}
. Since
[N,H_{B}]=0
, the work moments are related to the moments of the number operator by the equations
\langle w^{2n-1}\rangle=g^{2n-1}\delta p^{S}\text{Tr}\left\{N^{2n-1}\rho_{B}(0)\right\}
and
\langle w^{2n}\rangle=g^{2n}\text{Tr}\left\{N^{2n}\rho_{B}(0)\right\}
.
Then, by performing a Fourier transformation and expressing the environment Hamiltonian in the diagonal form
H_{B}=\sum\epsilon_{k}c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{k}
with
\epsilon_{k}=-\mu-2t\cos k
, it is easy to calculate the work, which reads
\langle w\rangle=g\delta p^{S}\sum_{k}1/(e^{\beta\epsilon_{k}}+1)
. In particular, the work can be positive or negative depending on the sign of the coupling
g
.
The lower bound of the work is
\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}F^{n}_{B}-F_{B}=e^{\beta\omega/2}F_{B}(\mu-g)/Z_{S}+e^{-\beta\omega/2}F_{B}(\mu+g)/Z_{S}-F_{B}(\mu)
, where
Z_{S}=2\cosh(\beta\omega/2)
and
F_{B}(\mu)=-\sum_{k}\ln(1+e^{-\beta\epsilon_{k}})/\beta
. If
j=1,\ldots,L
and
g=g^{\prime}/L
, in the limit
L\to\infty
the bound is saturated, i.e.
\langle w\rangle=\sum_{n}p^{S}_{n}F^{n}_{B}-F_{B}=g^{\prime}\delta p^{S}/\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}dk/(e^{\beta\epsilon_{k}}+1)
. We emphasize that we get the same bound for any time-dependent operator
B(t)
such that at the final time is equal to
B=gN
.

IV
Conclusions

In summary, we have investigated the quench thermodynamics of a pure decoherence process. In this case the work done on the system is equal to the heat exchanged with the environment. We have characterized the work probability distribution function, also with a fluctuation relation, a lower bound of the work done and by considering some physical examples.

We note that our results are different from the ones of Ref.
Popovic21
because we do not switch off the interaction at the final time. In particular, in the switching off process an amount of work is suddenly produced such that the total work is always non-negative, since the protocol becomes cyclic.

Appendix A
Strong coupling

Since
H_{S}
is constant, from Ref.
rivas20
we have the internal energy at the time
t

\langle E_{S}(t)\rangle=\text{Tr}\left\{(H^{*}_{S}(\beta,t)+\beta\partial_{\beta}H^{*}_{S}(\beta,t))\rho_{S}(t)\right\}

(19)

where
\rho_{S}(t)=\text{Tr}_{B}\left\{U_{t,0}\rho(0)U^{\dagger}_{t,0}\right\}
and

H^{*}_{S}(\beta,t)=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\text{Tr}_{B}\left\{U_{t,0}e^{-\beta H_{S}}\otimes\rho_{B}(0)U^{\dagger}_{t,0}\right\}\right)

(20)

We consider the eigenvalue equation
H_{S}|n\rangle=\epsilon_{n}|n\rangle
and the time evolved state
U_{t,0}|n\rangle\otimes|E_{k}\rangle=|n\rangle\otimes|\phi^{n}_{k}(t)\rangle
, then

\displaystyle U_{t,0}e^{-\beta H_{S}}\otimes\rho_{B}(0)U^{\dagger}_{t,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}e^{-\beta\epsilon_{n}}|n\rangle\langle n|\otimes\sum_{k}\frac{e^{-\beta E_{k}}}{Z_{B}}

(21)

\displaystyle\times|\phi^{n}_{k}(t)\rangle\langle\phi^{n}_{k}(t)|

Since
\text{Tr}\left\{|\phi^{n}_{k}(t)\rangle\langle\phi^{n}_{k}(t)|\right\}=1
, by taking the partial trace we get

\text{Tr}_{B}\left\{U_{t,0}e^{-\beta H_{S}}\otimes\rho_{B}(0)U^{\dagger}_{t,0}\right\}=e^{-\beta H_{S}}

(22)

then
H^{*}_{S}(\beta,t)=H_{S}
and so the internal energy is
\langle E_{S}(t)\rangle=\text{Tr}\left\{U_{t,0}^{\dagger}H_{S}U_{t,0}\rho(0)\right\}
.

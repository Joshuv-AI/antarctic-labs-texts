On the order of magnitude of certain integer sequences In memory of our revered teacher Ernst Kunz.
M. Hellus, A. Rechenauer, R. Waldi

Abstract
Let p p be a prime number, and let S S be the numerical semigroup generated by the prime numbers not less than p p . We compare the orders of magnitude of some invariants of S S with each other, e. g., the biggest atom u u of S S with p p itself: By Harald Helfgott ( [ 5 ] ), every odd integer N N greater than five can be written as the sum of three prime numbers. There is numerical evidence suggesting that the summands of N N always can be chosen between N 6 \frac{N}{6} and N 2 \frac{N}{2} . This would imply that u u is less than 6 ​ p 6p .

5
Remark on the ternary Goldbach problem

By Harald Helfgott (
[
5
]
), every odd integer

greater than five can be written as the sum of three prime numbers.

It is known that for large
N
these prime numbers can be chosen very closely to
\frac{N}{3}
, cf. e. g.
[

, Thm. 1.1]

With regard to an upper bound for
\frac{u}{p}
, the following weak, but effective version of
[
6
, Thm. 1.1]
would be helpful.

More generally, for reals
t>3
and odd integers

we define:

H(K,t)
: Every odd integer
N\geq K
can be written as the sum
N=q(1)+q(2)+q(3)

|\frac{N}{3}-q(i)|\leq\frac{N}{t}\text{ for }i=1,2,3.

By

Every odd integer

of prime numbers

\frac{N}{6}\leq q(i)\leq\frac{N}{2}\text{ for }i=1,2,3.

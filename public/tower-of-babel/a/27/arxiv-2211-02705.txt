Moments and tails of L q L_{q} -valued chaoses of order two based on independent variables with log-concave tails Thanks: This research was partly supported by the National Science Centre, Poland grants 2018/29/N/ST1/00454 and 2021/40/C/ST1/00330
Rafał Meller

Abstract
We derive a lower bound for moments of random chaoses of order two with coefficients in arbitrary Banach space F F generated by independent symmetric random variables with logarithmically concave tails (which is probably two-sided). We also provide two upper bounds for moments of such chaoses when F = L q F=L_{q} . The first is valid under the additional subgaussanity assumption. The second one does not require additional assumptions but is not optimal in general. Both upper bounds are sufficient for obtaining two-sided moment estimates for chaoses with values in L q L_{q} generated by Weibull random variables with shape parameter greater or equal to 1 1 . Keywords: Random chaoses; Random quadratic forms; Tail and moment estimates; Logarithmically concave tails AMS MSC 2010: 60E15

Introduction

A (homogeneous) polynomial chaos of order
d
is a random variable defined as

S=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{d}},

where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent random variables and
(a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}})_{1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\leq n}
are coefficients (
d
-indexed) that belong to a Banach space
(F,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
such that
\ a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}=0
if
i_{k}=i_{l}
for some
k\neq l
. The natural question is whether it is possible to give an exact description of the moments of
S
defined as
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{p}:=({\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert S\right\rVert^{p})^{1/p}
. By exact bounds, we mean two-sided ones. So, we look, for deterministic expression
f
(which may depend on
p
, the coefficients etc.) such that

\frac{f}{C(d)}\leq\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{p}\leq C(d)f,

(1)

where
C(d)
is some constant that depends only on
d
, the order of chaos. To derive an effective formula for
f
one must assume something about the distribution of
(X_{i})_{i}
(with an exception on the real line for
d=1
). For several reasons, it is very convenient to work with symmetric random variables
X_{i}
’s that have logarithmically concave tails (LCT for short) i.e., such that for any
i
the function
t\mapsto-\ln{\mathbb{P}}(|X_{i}|\geq t)\in[0,\infty]
is convex. First, this class contains many natural distributions such as Gaussian, exponential, Rademacher, and Weibull (with a shape parameter greater than or equal to 1). Second, in this class (
1
) can be proved with a reasonable formula
f
in several cases (see examples below). Finally, in this class, moments estimates imply bounds on the tails of
S
that is on
{\mathbb{P}}(\left\lVert S\right\rVert\geq t),\ t>0
. The argument is standard regardless of the chaos order and the Banach space’s choice.

The problem of establishing (
1
) naturally falls into two parts depending on whether the coefficients
a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}
are real or vector-valued, with significant unanswered questions even on the real line.

Now we present the state of the art. We start with the real coefficients. For
d=1
, deterministic bounds on the moments of
S
are known only under the symmetry or non-negativity assumption
[
17
]
. If the variables and coefficients are non-negative, then the formula for the moments is known for any order
d
under the assumption that the moments of the r.v.’s
X_{i}
grow at most polynomially (moments of a variable with logarithmically concave tails grow at most linearly)
[
22
]
. The case of symmetric variables is much less understood. We know formulas for
d=2
when the moments of variables grow at most polynomially
[
23
]
, and for
d=3
, when the variables have logarithmic tails
[
3
]
. For any
d
, formulas are known if the variables are Gaussian
[
20
]
, exponential
[
3
]
, or have logarithmically convex tails
[
11
]
(which includes the Weibull random variable with a shape parameter less than or equal to
1
). In the Gaussian case, something even more general is known, namely the formula for the moments of any polynomial in Gaussian variables
[
4
]
. Łochowski’s result
[
24
]
is also worth mentioning. He obtained bounds for chaos of arbitrary order based on symmetric r.v.’s with logarithmically concave tails. However, his bounds involve expectations for the suprema of non-Gaussian processes, which are very difficult to estimate.

Very little is known in the case of vector-valued coefficients, except for chaos of order
1
. If
d=1
, we know formulas when the moments of
(X_{i})_{i}
grow at most polynomially
[
14
]
, i.e. in quite satisfying generality. For
d\geq 2
, nothing is known except for two cases: Gaussian chaos of arbitrary order in spaces of type
2
and exponential chaos of arbitrary order in
L_{q}
spaces
[
1
,
2
]
. The latter results could only be derived because the exponential symmetric r.v. is (almost) a product of two independent Gaussian r.v.’s. Thus, we could reduce the study of exponential chaos of order
d
to Gaussian chaos of order
2d
. There are formulas in the literature for moments of Gaussian chaos of any order in arbitrary Banach space. Unfortunately, they involve the expectation of suprema of Gaussian processes (which are very hard to estimate), see
[
21
, Chapter 3.2]
and
[
6
]
for details. It is also worth mentioning Adamczak’s result
[
5
]
. Although his bounds are not two-sided (which was not the goal of this work), they are obtained in greater generality and any Banach space. To some extent, our formulas are similar to those obtained by Adamczak.

We are interested in studying moments of
L_{q}
-valued random chaos of order 2, based on symmetric random variables with logarithmically concave tails. This can be seen as the first attempt to study the moments in the non-Gaussian case (as we mentioned, the exponential chaoses can be reduced to the Gaussian ones). In our setup

S=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}X_{i}X_{j},\ \textrm{where }a_{ij}\in L_{q},\textrm{ and }a_{ii}\equiv 0,

where
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
are independent symmetric random variables with LCT (with logarithmically concave tails). If
a_{ii}\neq 0
then standard arguments like Fact
A.31
allow us to study the diagonal and off-diagonal parts of the chaos separately. The diagonal part is a linear combination of independent random variables, and certain results can be applied, e.g.
[
14
]
. Thus, we focus on the case when
a_{ii}\equiv 0
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
and then use Theorem
A.30
to reduce the problem of estimating
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{p}
to estimating moments of decoupled chaos

S^{\prime}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j},\ a_{ii}\equiv 0,

where the random vector
(Y_{i})_{i}
is an independent copy of the random vector
(X_{i})_{i}
(
[
7
,
12
]
). The latter object has a richer structure and is easier to work with. Therefore, in this paper, we will focus only on decoupled chaos. We will also not assume that
a_{ii}\equiv 0
since this is irrelevant in the case of decoupled chaos.

First, we give a simple lower bound for
\left\lVert S^{\prime}\right\rVert_{p}
in any Banach space
(F,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
, which is probably two-sided (at least in a class of Banach spaces with non-trivial cotype). Then we restrict our attention to
L_{q}
spaces. We give two upper bounds. The first is optimal in the class of sub-Gaussian random variables with logarithmically concave tails. The second is not optimal, but it does not require additional assumptions on the random variables. These theorems imply two-sided moment estimates for
L_{q}
valued chaos based on symmetric random variables with CDF equal to
1-\exp(-x^{r})
for
r\geq 1
(Weibull random variable with shape parameter
r
). This can be considered as the main result of the paper.

In the next section, we set up a notation and present the main results. In Section
3
we show that the main difficulty in obtaining upper bounds for the moments of random chaos is to properly estimate the expectation of the supremum of a given stochastic process. In Section
4
we derive the upper bound for the expectation of the supremum of a certain Gaussian process. It is a generalization of the upper bound obtained in
[
1
]
. Section
5
deals with the case of the supremum of an exponential process. In Section
6
we use the ideas from
[
3
]
to derive the decomposition theorem for exponential processes in
L_{q}
space. In the last section, we prove the main theorems. Unfortunately, our approach is not sufficient to obtain a more general result, and new ideas are needed. In this paper, we use many results from other papers. To overcome this inconvenience, we have collected all the cited facts in the appendix. We also provide a glossary explaining the notation that precedes the bibliography.

Let
(X_{i})_{i},(Y_{j})_{j}
be independent, symmetric random variables. We define the functions

N^{X}_{i}(t)=-\ln{\mathbb{P}}(|X_{i}|\geq t)\in[0,\infty],\ N^{Y}_{j}(t)=-\ln{\mathbb{P}}(|Y_{j}|\geq t)\in[0,\infty].

Our basic assumption is that
(X_{i})_{i},(Y_{j})_{j}
have LCT (logarithmically concave tails) i.e. the functions
(N^{X}_{i})_{i},(N^{Y}_{j})_{j}
are convex. We are mainly concerned with the homogeneous inequalities of degree one, so we can normalize the random variables as follows

\displaystyle\inf\left\{t\geq 0,\ N^{X}_{i}(t)\geq 1\right\}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0,\ N^{Y}_{i}(t)\geq 1\right\}=1.

(2)

We set

\hat{N}^{X}_{i}(t)=\begin{cases}t^{2}&\textrm{for }|t|\leq 1\\
N^{X}_{i}(t)&\textrm{for }|t|>1\end{cases},\quad\hat{N}^{Y}_{j}(t)=\begin{cases}t^{2}&\textrm{for }|t|\leq 1\\
N^{Y}_{i}(t)&\textrm{for }|t|>1\end{cases}.

Note that the convexity of
N^{X}_{i},N^{Y}_{j}
and the normalization condition (
2
) imply that

\hat{N}^{X}_{i}(t)=N^{X}_{i}(t)\geq t,\quad\hat{N}^{Y}_{j}(t)=N^{Y}_{i}(t)\geq t\quad\textrm{for }t\geq 1,

(3)

1/e\leq{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}_{i},{\mathbb{E}}Y^{2}_{j}\leq 1+4/e\leq 3,\quad{\mathbb{E}}X^{4}_{i},{\mathbb{E}}Y^{4}_{j}\leq 1+64/e.

(4)

The first formula is clear, to prove the second one it is enough to observe that

1/e\leq{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{2}_{i}|\geq 1)\leq{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}_{i}\leq 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}2xe^{-x}dx=1+4/e\leq 3

(we prove the bounds for
{\mathbb{E}}X^{4}_{i}
analogously).
We define

B^{X}_{p}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\sum_{i}\hat{N}^{X}_{i}(x_{i})\leq p\},\quad B^{Y}_{p}=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\sum_{j}\hat{N}^{Y}_{j}(y_{j})\leq p\}.

(5)

Observe that (
3
) implies that

B^{X}_{p},B^{Y}_{p}\subset p^{1/2}B^{n}_{2}+pB^{n}_{1}.

(6)

Let
(a_{ij})_{ij}
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
valued matrix and
(a_{i})_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The following three norms will play a crucial role in this paper:

\displaystyle\left\lVert(a_{ij})_{ij}\right\rVert_{X,Y,p}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x_{i}y_{j}:\sum_{i}\hat{N}^{X}_{i}(x_{i})\leq p,\ \sum_{j}\hat{N}^{Y}_{j}(y_{j})\leq p\right\}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in B^{X}_{p}\\
y\in B^{Y}_{p}\end{subarray}}\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x_{i}y_{j},

\displaystyle\left\lVert(a_{i})_{i}\right\rVert_{X,p}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\sum_{i}a_{i}x_{i}:\sum_{i}\hat{N}^{X}_{i}(x_{i})\leq p\right\}=\sup_{x\in B^{X}_{p}}\sum_{i}a_{i}x_{i},

\displaystyle\left\lVert(a_{j})_{j}\right\rVert_{Y,p}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\sum_{j}a_{j}y_{j}\>\sum_{j}\hat{N}^{Y}_{j}(y_{j})\leq p\right\}=\sup_{y\in B^{Y}_{p}}\sum_{j}a_{j}y_{j}.

It can be shown that the above objects are norms, not just seminorms (this follows from our normalization (

Since
\hat{N}^{X}_{i}(t/u)\leq\hat{N}^{X}_{i}(t)/u

\left\lVert(a_{i})_{i}\right\rVert_{X,up}\leq u\left\lVert(a_{i})_{i}\right\rVert_{X,p},\ \left\lVert(a_{ij})_{ij}\right\rVert_{X,Y,up}\leq u^{2}\left\lVert(a_{ij})_{ij}\right\rVert_{X,Y,p},

(7)

and the first formula is also valid for

We start with a simple lower bound.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
are independent symmetric r.v.’s with LCT such that the normalization condition (
2
) holds. Let
(a_{ij})_{ij}
belong to a Banach space
(F,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\gtrsim{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert+\sup_{x\in B^{X}_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert+\sup_{y\in B^{Y}_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}y_{j}\right\rVert

\displaystyle+\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\left\lVert\left(\sqrt{\sum_{j}(f(a_{ij}))^{2}}\right)_{i}\right\rVert_{X,p}+\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\left\lVert\left(\sqrt{\sum_{i}(f(a_{ij}))^{2}}\right)_{j}\right\rVert_{Y,p}

\displaystyle+\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\left\lVert(f(a_{ij}))_{ij}\right\rVert_{X,Y,p}=:W^{X,Y}(p).

(8)

Moreover, for any
t\geq 1

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert\geq W^{X,Y}(t)\right)\gtrsim\exp(-Ct).

(9)

Proof.

Trivially
\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\geq{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert
. Using Theorem
A.9
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\sum_{ij}f(a_{ij})X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\geq\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}f(a_{ij})X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\left\lVert\left(\sqrt{\sum_{j}(f(a_{ij}))^{2}}\right)_{i}\right\rVert_{X,p}+\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\left\lVert\left(\sqrt{\sum_{i}(f(a_{ij}))^{2}}\right)_{j}\right\rVert_{Y,p}

\displaystyle+\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}\left\lVert\left(f(a_{ij})\right)_{ij}\right\rVert_{X,Y,p}.

Jensen’s inequality and Theorem
A.1
yield

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\geq\left({\mathbb{E}}^{Y}\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F)}{\mathbb{E}}^{X}\left|\sum_{ij}f(a_{ij})X_{i}Y_{j}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left\lVert\sup_{f\in B^{*}(F),x\in B^{X}_{p}}\sum_{ij}f(a_{ij})x_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\geq\sup_{x\in B^{X}_{p}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\geq\sup_{x\in B^{X}_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert.

Analogously
\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\gtrsim\sup_{y\in B^{Y}_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}y_{j}\right\rVert
and (
9
) follows. A standard application of the Paley-Zygmund inequality, the fact that the
p
th and
2p
th moments of
\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}
are comparable up to a numerical constant (by Fact

) and (
7

9
) (for details see the proof of
[
23
, Corollary 2.3]
).
∎

We suspect that the inequality (
8
) can be reversed when the Banach space has the non-trivial co-type. Whether it can be reversed in any Banach space would be a challenging problem. A positive premise is that (
8
) can be reversed in any Banach space with logarithmic accuracy when the variables have the normal distribution cf.

. However, there may also be a simple counterexample. In this paper, we provide some results for

First, we formulate an upper bound for the moments of chaos under an additional assumption of subgaussianity. We say that a r.v.
Y
is subgaussian with constant
\gamma>0
if
{\mathbb{E}}Y=0
and for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

{\mathbb{E}}\exp(tY)\leq\exp(\gamma t^{2})
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
are independent symmetric r.v.’s with LCT such that (
2
) holds. Assume also that
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
are subgaussian with constant
\gamma
. Let
(a_{ij})_{ij}
be an
L_{q}(V,\mu)
-valued matrix. Then for any
p\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{p}\lesssim^{q}\gamma{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{L_{q}}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in B^{X}_{p}\\
y\in B^{Y}_{p}\end{subarray}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{L_{q}}+{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}y_{j}\right\rVert_{L_{q}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sup_{f\in B^{*}(L_{q})}\left\lVert\left(\sqrt{\sum_{j}(f(a_{ij}))^{2}}\right)_{i}\right\rVert_{X,p}+\sup_{f\in B^{*}(L_{q})}\left\lVert(f(a_{ij}))_{ij}\right\rVert_{X,Y,p}=:W^{X,Y,\gamma}(p),

(10)

and

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert\geq W^{X,Y,\gamma}(t)\right)\leq C(q)\exp\left(-\frac{W^{X,Y,\gamma}(t)}{C(q)}\right).

(11)

Remark 2.3
.

A version of Lemma
A.7
(which is for

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{L_{q}}\approx^{q}\left\lVert\sqrt{\sum_{ij}a^{2}_{ij}}\right\rVert_{L_{q}},\ {\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}x_{i}Y_{j}\right\rVert_{L_{q}}\approx^{q}\left\lVert\sqrt{\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i}x_{i}a_{ij}\right)^{2}}\right\rVert_{L_{q}},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{i}y_{j}\right\rVert_{L_{q}}\approx^{q}\left\lVert\sqrt{\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{i}y_{j}a_{ij}\right)^{2}}\right\rVert_{L_{q}}.

Thus one may rewrite (
10
) without using expectations.

The formula (
11

10
). We will prove the latter in Section
7

ensures that (

Remark 2.5

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
and

4

Let us fix a tensor

. The main result of this section is Proposition

(G_{(x,t)})_{(x,t)\in V}
, where

,

and
T
is arbitrary. To estimate such a quantity, one needs to study the distance on
p^{-1/2}B^{X}_{p}\times T
given by

d_{A}((x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=({\mathbb{E}}|G_{(x,t)}-G_{(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})}|^{2})^{1/2}=\alpha_{A}(x\otimes t-x^{\prime}\otimes t^{\prime}),

where

and
\alpha_{A}
is the norm (see Remark
4.1
below) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{nm}
defined by the formula

\alpha_{A}((x)_{jk})=\sqrt{\sum_{i}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{ijk}x_{jk}\right)^{2}}.

Remark 4.1
.

We may assume that
\alpha_{A}
is a norm (a priori it is only a seminorm). We can replace the tensor
A
by
\hat{A}=(\hat{a}_{ijk})_{ijk}
where

\hat{a}_{ijk}=\begin{cases}a_{ijk}&i\leq n,\\
0&n<i\leq n+nm\textrm{ and }(j,k)\neq\sigma(i)\\
\eta&n<i\leq n+nm\textrm{ and }(j,k)=\sigma(i)\end{cases},

(20)

\sigma
is any bijection between the sets
[nm]
and
[n]\times[m]
, and
\eta>0
is a small number. Then
\alpha_{\hat{A}}
is a norm given by

\alpha_{\hat{A}}((x))_{jk})=\sqrt{\sum_{i}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{ijk}x_{jk}\right)^{2}+\eta\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}}\mathrel{{\mathop{>}\limits}}0.

It is enough to derive all the results for
\hat{A}
and take

.

We use the scheme introduced in
[
1
]
. To proceed, we need some entropy estimates for subsets of
(B_{2}+p^{1/2}B_{1})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. As usual
N(U,\rho,\varepsilon)
stands for the smallest number of closed balls with the diameter
\varepsilon
in metric
\rho
that cover the set
U
.
The crucial idea is to consider the distribution of the vector
\varepsilon(G_{n}+E_{n})
,
\varepsilon>0
which is denoted by
\mu_{n,\varepsilon}
(we recall that
E_{n}
is the symmetric exponential vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
independent of
G_{n}
).

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and define the following norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(a priori it is only a seminorm, but after applying Remark
4.1
it is a norm)

\beta_{A,S}(x):={\mathbb{E}}\sup_{s\in S}\left|\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}g_{i}x_{j}s_{k}\right|.

By the classical Sudakov minoration (Theorem
A.20
), for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
there exists a set
S_{x,\varepsilon}\subset S
of cardinality at most
\exp(C\varepsilon^{-2})
such that

\displaystyle\forall_{t\in S}\exists_{t^{\prime}\in S_{x,\varepsilon}}\ \alpha_{A}(x\otimes(t-t^{\prime}))\leq\varepsilon{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{s\in S}\left|\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}g_{i}x_{j}s_{k}\right|=\varepsilon\beta_{A,S}(x).

(21)

We define the following measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times S
:

\hat{\mu}_{\varepsilon,S}(C)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{t\in S_{x,\varepsilon}}\mathbb{1}_{C}((x,t)))d\mu_{n,\varepsilon}(x).

(22)

The following technical lemma (together with standard measure theory considerations) ensures that there is no measurability problem under the integral in (
22

Lemma 4.2
.

The sets
S_{x,\varepsilon}\subset S
(sets satisfying (
21
)) can be defined in such way that
x\mapsto S_{x,\varepsilon}
is a simple function (i.e. this function has finitely many values and the preimage of each value is a Borel set).

Proof.

W.l.o.g. we can assume that for fixed, small
\eta>0
we have

\forall_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},s\in S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\sqrt{\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{jk}a_{ijk}x_{j}s_{j}\right)^{2}}\geq\eta\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{2}

(see Remark
4.1
). In particular for any vector
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\displaystyle\beta_{A,S}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{s\in S}{\mathbb{E}}|\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}g_{i}x_{j}s_{k}|=\sup_{s\in S}\sqrt{\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{jk}a_{ijk}x_{j}s_{k}\right)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}|g_{1}|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}\sup_{s\in S}\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{2}.

(23)

Since (
21

S_{x,\varepsilon}
on the unit sphere (and then put
S_{x,\varepsilon}:=S_{x/\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2},\varepsilon}
). We decompose the unit sphere
S_{n-1}
into Borel sets such that

S_{n-1}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{U}}U,\ \sup_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\Delta_{2}(U)\leq\frac{\varepsilon\cdot\eta\sup_{s\in S}\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{2}}{(2+\varepsilon)H(S)}\textrm{ and }|\mathcal{U}|<\infty,\

where
\Delta_{2}
is the diameter of the set in the Euclidean metric, and

H(S):=\sup_{t\in S}\sqrt{\sum_{ij}\left(\sum_{k}a_{ijk}s_{k}\right)^{2}}+{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{s\in S}\sqrt{\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{ik}a_{ijk}g_{i}s_{k}\right)^{2}}\in(0,\infty).

Fix any
U\in\mathcal{U}
and take any
x\in U
. Let
S_{x,\varepsilon}\subset S
be the set such that (
21
) holds (for
x

S_{x^{\prime},\varepsilon}:=S_{x,\varepsilon}

21

replaced by
x^{\prime}

2\varepsilon

\varepsilon

\alpha_{A}(x\otimes(t-t^{\prime}))\leq\varepsilon{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{s\in S}\left|\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}g_{i}x_{j}s_{k}\right|=\varepsilon\beta_{A,S}(x).

(24)

It suffices to show that (since

\displaystyle\alpha_{A}(x^{\prime}\otimes(t-t^{\prime}))\leq 2\varepsilon\beta_{A,S}(x^{\prime}).

Using the triangle inequality, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (

\alpha_{A}(x^{\prime}\otimes(t-t^{\prime}))\leq\alpha_{A}((x^{\prime}-x)\otimes(t-t^{\prime}))+\alpha_{A}(x\otimes(t-t^{\prime}))\leq 2H(S)\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}+\varepsilon\beta_{A,S}(x).

\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}H(S)(2+\varepsilon)\leq\Delta_{2}(U)H(S)(2+\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon\eta\sup_{s\in S}\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{2}\leq\varepsilon\beta_{A,S}(x^{\prime}).

.

For any finite set

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

,

Remark 4.8
.

The formulation of Corollary

may seem unusual. The reason comes from our chaining argument. Our goal is to upper bound

, where

into smaller pieces using Corollary

. Then we decompose each of the

using this corollary, which ensures that

and

are subsets of
V_{i}-V_{i}\subset 2(B_{2}+p^{1/2}B_{1})\times(T-T)
. So after the first step, the boundary conditions stabilize. This is why one needs to consider both cases

and

. It is also the reason why the definition of the coefficients

in the proof of Proposition
4.9
is different for

and

(see below).

Proposition 4.9
.

For any

, any non-empty

\displaystyle F_{A}(V)\lesssim\frac{1}{p^{1/2}}s_{A}(T)+\sup_{(x,t)\in V}\beta_{A,T}(x)+\sup_{(x,t)\in V}{\mathbb{E}}\alpha_{A}(E_{n}\otimes t)+p^{1/2}\Delta_{A}(V).

Proof.

W.l.o.g we may assume that
V
is finite and
V\subset(1/2(B^{n}_{2}+p^{1/2}B^{n}_{1}))\times T
, so that

. We define

Let for

\displaystyle c(0,k):=\sup\{

\displaystyle F_{A}(U):U\subset V,\ |U|\leq k\},

\displaystyle c(l,k):=\sup\{

and if

and

A.19

\displaystyle\leq p^{1/2}\Delta_{A}(V)+Cp\Delta_{\infty,A}(V)+\frac{1}{4}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{(x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in V}\sum_{i}g_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}\sum_{jk}a_{ijk}(x_{j}t_{k}-x^{\prime}_{j}t^{\prime}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq p^{1/2}\Delta_{A}(V)+Cp\Delta_{\infty,A}(V)+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{(x,t)\in V}\sum_{i}g_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}\sum_{jk}a_{ijk}x_{j}t_{k}.

(39)

Applying (

)-(
39

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{(x,t)\in V}\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}g_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}x_{j}t_{k}

\displaystyle+p^{1/2}\sup_{y\in B_{2},(x,t)\in V}\alpha_{\infty,A}(y\otimes t)+p^{1/2}\Delta_{A}(V)+p\Delta_{\infty,A}(V)\Bigg)+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{(x,t)\in V}\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}g_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}x_{j}t_{k}.

The assertion follows.

6
Main Decomposition Lemma

The goal of this section is to provide decomposition

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
be any norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

(we recall that

By Lemma

By Royen’s Theorem
A.11

\displaystyle\gamma_{n,\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{n,\varepsilon}\left(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\alpha_{\infty,A}(x\otimes t)\leq\varepsilon^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\alpha_{A}(E_{n}\otimes t)\right)\gamma_{n,\varepsilon}\left(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq C\varepsilon{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert E_{n}\right\rVert\right)

stands for the distribution of
\varepsilon E_{n}

Theorem A.3
.

Let

Lemma A.23
.

Fact A.31
.

Fix

are coefficients from a Banach space
(F,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)

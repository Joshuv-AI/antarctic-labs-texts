Mid-surface scaling invariance of some bending strain measures
Amit Acharya

Abstract
The mid-surface scaling invariance of bending strain measures proposed in [ 1 ] is discussed in light of the work of [ 3 ] .



1
Introduction

This brief note discusses the mid-surface scaling invariance of three nonlinear measures of pure bending strain, as introduced in
[
1
]
and physically motivated therein more than 20 years ago, in light of the recent work of
[
3
]
where the said invariance is introduced.

It is shown that one of the strain measures introduced in
[
1
]
possesses scaling invariance, and the other two are easily modified to have the invariance as well. There has been a recent surge of interest in such matters, as can be seen from the works of
[
3
,
5
,
6
]
.

We use the notation of
[
1
]
: a shell mid-surface is thought of as a 2D surface in ambient 3D space (the qualification ‘mid-surface’ will not be used in all instances; it is hoped that the meaning will be clear from the context). Both the
reference and deformed shells are parametrized by the same coordinate system
((\xi^{\alpha}),\alpha=1,2)
(convected
coordinates). Points on the reference geometry are denoted generically by
{\mathbfit X}
and on the deformed
geometry by
{\mathbfit x}
. The reference unit normal is denoted by
{\mathbfit N}
and the unit normal on the deformed
geometry by
{\mathbfit n}
. A subscript comma refers to partial differentiation, e.g.
\frac{\partial()}{\partial\xi^{\alpha}}=()_{,\alpha}
. Summation over repeated indices will be assumed. The convected coordinate basis vectors in the reference geometry will be referred to by the symbols
({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha})
and those in the deformed geometry by
({\mathbfit e}_{\alpha})
,
\alpha=1,2
, with corresponding dual bases
({\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}),({\mathbfit e}^{\alpha})
, respectively. A suitable number of dots placed between two tensors represent the operation of contraction, while the symbol
\otimes
will represent a tensor product. The deformation gradient will be denoted by
{\mathbfit f}={\mathbfit e}_{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}
and admits the right polar decomposition
{\mathbfit f}={\mathbfit r}\cdot{\mathbfit U}
, where
{\mathbfit U}({\mathbfit X}):T_{\mathbfit X}\to T_{\mathbfit X}
and
{\mathbfit r}({\mathbfit X}):T_{\mathbfit X}\to T_{\mathbfit x}
, where
T_{\mathbfit c}
represents the tangent space of the shell at the point
{\mathbfit c}
. The curvature tensor on the deformed shell is denoted as
{\mathbfit b}={\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}\otimes{\mathbfit e}^{\beta}
and that on the undeformed shell as
{\mathbfit B}={\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta}
.

2
Some measures of pure bending and their invariance under mid-surface scaling

In
[
1
]
three measures of bending strain were proposed, given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathbfit K}}

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit r}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}-{\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}\right){\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta}={\mathbfit f}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit b}\cdot{\mathbfit f}-{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit B}

(1a)

\displaystyle\check{{\mathbfit K}}

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit r}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}+{\mathbfit E}_{\beta}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\alpha}\right)\right){\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta}

\displaystyle={\mathbfit f}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit b}\cdot{\mathbfit f}-({\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit B})_{sym}

(1b)

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathbfit K}}

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit r}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}-{\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}\right){\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta}={\mathbfit r}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit b}\cdot{\mathbfit f}-{\mathbfit B}.

(1c)

Equation (
1c
) was unnumbered in that work, as the main emphasis was to obtain a nonlinear generalization of the Koiter-Sanders-Budiansky bending strain measure
[
4
,
2
]
;
\widetilde{{\mathbfit K}}
is introduced as Equation (8) and
\check{{\mathbfit K}}
as Equation (10) in
[
1
]
.

In
[
3
]
a physically natural requirement of invariance of bending strain measure under simple scalings of the form

{\mathbfit x}\to a{\mathbfit x},\qquad 0<a\in\mathbb{R}

is introduced (for plates, but the requirement is natural for shells as well) and it is shown that the measures
\widetilde{{\mathbfit K}},\check{{\mathbfit K}}
are not invariant under such a scaling. The measure
\overline{{\mathbfit K}}
is not discussed in
[
3
]
.

It is straightforward to see that under the said scaling, the deformation gradient scales as

\frac{\partial{\mathbfit x}}{\partial{\mathbfit X}}={\mathbfit r}\cdot{\mathbfit U}={\mathbfit f}\qquad\to\qquad a{\mathbfit f}={\mathbfit r}\cdot(a{\mathbfit U})=a\frac{\partial{\mathbfit x}}{\partial{\mathbfit X}},

resulting in the bending measures scaling as

\widetilde{{\mathbfit K}}\to a\widetilde{{\mathbfit K}};\qquad\check{{\mathbfit K}}\to a\check{{\mathbfit K}};\qquad\overline{{\mathbfit K}}\to\overline{{\mathbfit K}}.

Thus, the bending strain measure
\overline{{\mathbfit K}}
from
[
1
]
, not discussed by
[
3
]
, is actually
invariant under scaling deformations of the deformed shell mid-surface
. Furthermore, the simple modifications of the measures
\widetilde{{\mathbfit K}},\check{{\mathbfit K}}
to

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathbfit K}}_{mod}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|{\mathbfit U}|}\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit r}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}-{\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}\right){\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta}

(2a)

\displaystyle=\left(tr\left({\mathbfit f}^{T}{\mathbfit f}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left({\mathbfit x}_{,\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}-{\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}\right){\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta}

\displaystyle\check{{\mathbfit K}}_{mod}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|{\mathbfit U}|}\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit r}^{T}\cdot{\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}+{\mathbfit E}_{\beta}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\alpha}\right)\right){\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta}

(2b)

\displaystyle=\left(tr\left({\mathbfit f}^{T}{\mathbfit f}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left({\mathbfit x}_{,\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit n}_{,\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathbfit E}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\beta}+{\mathbfit E}_{\beta}\cdot{\mathbfit U}\cdot{\mathbfit N}_{,\alpha}\right)\right){\mathbfit E}^{\alpha}\otimes{\mathbfit E}^{\beta},

where

|{\mathbfit U}|=\sqrt{{\mathbfit U}:{\mathbfit U}}=\sqrt{tr\left({\mathbfit f}^{T}{\mathbfit f}\right)},

make them mid-surface scaling invariant.

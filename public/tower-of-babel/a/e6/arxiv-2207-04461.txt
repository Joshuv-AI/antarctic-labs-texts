Stability of neutron stars in Horndeski theories with Gauss-Bonnet couplings Preprint: WUCG-22-05
Masato Minamitsuji 1 and Shinji Tsujikawa 2

Abstract
In Horndeski theories containing a scalar coupling with the Gauss-Bonnet (GB) curvature invariant R GB 2 R_{\rm GB}^{2} , we study the existence and linear stability of neutron star (NS) solutions on a static and spherically symmetric background. For a scalar-GB coupling of the form α ​ ξ ​ ( ϕ ) ​ R GB 2 \alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2} , where ξ \xi is a function of the scalar field ϕ \phi , the existence of linearly stable stars with a nontrivial scalar profile without instabilities puts an upper bound on the strength of the dimensionless coupling constant | α | |\alpha| . To realize maximum masses of NSs for a linear (or dilatonic) GB coupling α GB ​ ϕ ​ R GB 2 \alpha_{\rm GB}\phi R_{\rm GB}^{2} with typical nuclear equations of state, we obtain the theoretical upper limit | α GB | < 0.7 ​ km \sqrt{|\alpha_{\rm GB}|}<0.7~{\rm km} . This is tighter than those obtained by the observations of gravitational waves emitted from binaries containing NSs. We also incorporate cubic-order scalar derivative interactions, quartic derivative couplings with nonminimal couplings to a Ricci scalar besides the scalar-GB coupling and show that NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile satisfying all the linear stability conditions are present for certain ranges of the coupling constants. In regularized 4-dimensional Einstein-GB gravity obtained from a Kaluza-Klein reduction with an appropriate rescaling of the GB coupling constant, we find that NSs in this theory suffer from a strong coupling problem as well as Laplacian instability of even-parity perturbations. We also study NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile in power-law F ⁡ ( R GB 2 ) F(R_{\rm GB}^{2}) models, and show that they are pathological in the interior of stars and plagued by ghost instability together with the asymptotic strong coupling problem in the exterior of stars.

Introduction

After the dawn of gravitational waves (GW) astronomy
from a binary system of black holes (BHs)
Abbott et al. 2016
, a new observational probe
of the physics on a strong gravitational background
has begun.
In particular, the GW170817 event
Abbott et al. 2017

allowed us to put constraints on the mass-radius relation of
neutron stars (NSs) from their tidal deformation before
the coalescence
Abbott et al. 2018
.
After a merger of binaries, a compact object exhibits
a damped sinusoidal oscillation with quasi-normal
frequencies
Kokkotas and Schmidt 1999
;
Nollert 1999
.
Upcoming observational data of GWs will provide us further
detailed information for new physics in strong gravity regimes
in the vicinity of BHs and in the interior of NSs.

From cosmological observational data, we know that
about 95 % of the energy density of today’s Universe
is dominated by mysterious components dubbed dark energy and
dark matter
Riess et al. 1998
;
Perlmutter et al. 1999
;
Spergel et al. 2003
;
Tegmark et al. 2004
;
Ade et al. 2014
.
Since it is still challenging to explain their origins
within the framework of General Relativity (GR) and
Standard Model of particle physics, there is
a motivation to introduce new degrees of freedom
Copeland et al. 2006
;
Silvestri and Trodden 2009
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
;
Clifton et al. 2012
;
Joyce et al. 2015
;
Koyama 2016
;
Heisenberg 2019
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2019a
.
A scalar field is one of the simplest candidates,
and widely used to explain physical phenomena relevant to
the dark sector of the Universe.

On a spherically symmetric background,
an asymptotically-flat vacuum solution in GR is
uniquely described by the Schwarzschild metric
with the mass of a compact body.
The background Schwarzschild geometry can be modified
by introducing a new degree of freedom.
For asymptotically-flat BHs, however, the property of the
absence of a nontrivial scalar profile holds for
a wide class of scalar-tensor theories–including
a canonical scalar
field
Hawking 1972a
;
Bekenstein 1972
,
k-essence
Graham and Jha 2014
,
nonminimally coupled scalar field with Ricci
scalar
Hawking 1972b
;
Bekenstein 1995
;
Sotiriou and Faraoni 2012
;
Faraoni 2017
,
and regular derivative couplings
in shift-symmetric Horndeski theories
Hui and Nicolis 2013
.
An exceptional case is a scalar coupling with the GB curvature invariant
R_{\rm GB}^{2}
of the form

\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
, where
\alpha
is a dimensionless
coupling constant and
\xi
is a regular function of the scalar
field
\phi

Kanti et al. 1996
;
Torii et al. 1997
;
Kanti et al. 1998
;
Chen et al. 2007
;
Guo et al. 2008
;
Guo et al. 2009
;
Pani and Cardoso 2009
;
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014a
;
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014b
;
Ayzenberg and Yunes 2014
;
Maselli et al. 2015
;
Kleihaus et al. 2011
;
Kleihaus et al. 2016
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018a
;
Silva et al. 2018
;
Antoniou et al. 2018
;
Blázquez-Salcedo et al. 2018
;
Minamitsuji and Ikeda 2019
;
Silva et al. 2019
;
Macedo et al. 2019
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2022
;
Minamitsuji et al. 2022a
.
This scalar-GB coupling belongs to a subclass of Horndeski
theories
Horndeski 1974
containing nonanalytic functions like
\ln|X|

in the coupling functions
G_{2,3,4,5}

Kobayashi et al. 2011
, where
X
is
a canonical field kinetic term.

For a NS, the presence of matter inside the star can modify
the no-hair property of BHs in several subclasses of
Horndeski theories.
Nonminimal couplings with the Ricci scalar
R
of the form

G_{4}(\phi)R
allow a possibility for realizing NS solutions
endowed with a nontrivial scalar profile,
especially via the mechanism of spontaneous
scalarization
Damour and Esposito-Farese 1993
;
Harada 1998
;
Novak 1998
;
Sotani and Kokkotas 2004
;
Cooney et al. 2010
;
Arapoglu et al. 2011
;
Orellana et al. 2013
;
Aparicio Resco et al. 2016
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2019b
.
This includes Brans-Dicke theories
Brans and Dicke 1961

and
f(R)
gravity
Starobinsky 1980
, where
the latter corresponds to a particular class of the former
with a scalar potential
O’Hanlon 1972
;
Chiba 2003
.
These nonminimally coupled theories belong to a subclass
of non-shift-symmetric Horndeski theories.

In shift-symmetric subclass of Horndeski theories
where the field equations of motion are invariant under
the shift
\phi\to\phi+c
,
there is a no-hair argument of stars under several
assumptions
Lehébel et al. 2017

analogous to BHs discussed in Ref.
Hui and Nicolis 2013
.
The assumptions are as follows:

(i)

the scalar field and metrics are regular, static, and
spherically symmetric with an asymptotically-flat
spacetime geometry,

(ii)

a canonical kinetic term
X
is present in the action,

(iii)

the action is analytic with regular coupling functions
G_{2,3,4,5}
.

Under these hypotheses, we end up with a no-hair solution

\phi={\rm constant}
.

One way of breaking the assumption
(
i
) is to postulate
a scalar field of the form
\phi=qt+\psi(r)
,
where
q
is a nonvanishing constant,
t
and
r
are
time and radial coordinates, respectively.
Originally, this type of field configuration was considered to
search for BH solutions
with a nontrivial profile of the scalar field in shift-symmetric Horndeski
theories
Babichev and Charmousis 2014
.
The analysis was further extended to relativistic stars in DHOST theories
Babichev et al. 2016
;
Sakstein et al. 2017
;
Kobayashi and Hiramatsu 2018
;
Ogawa et al. 2020
;
Ikeda et al. 2021
.
In this paper, we do not consider such a time-dependent
background scalar field and focus on the case
q=0
, i.e., the static scalar field.

If we break the assumption (
ii
), i.e.,
no canonical kinetic term in the action,
it is known that the quartic nonminimal derivative
coupling
G_{4}\supset\mu_{4}X
gives rise to
a solution endowed with a nontrivial scalar profile
inside the star
Cisterna et al. 2015
.
However, it was recognized that this solution with a
nontrivial scalar profile is plagued by an angular
Laplacian instability of even-parity perturbations
for large multipoles around the surface of star
Kase and Tsujikawa 2021
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
.

Finally, one can break the above assumption
(
iii
) by introducing nonanalytic coupling functions.
In the presence of
X
, the quintic-order coupling

G_{5}=-4\alpha\ln|X|
, which is equivalent to the linear scalar-GB coupling
\alpha\phi R_{\rm GB}^{2}
, gives rise to
NS solutions endowed with a nontrivial
scalar profile
Maselli et al. 2016
.
This linear coupling can also accommodate a dilatonic coupling

\xi(\phi)\propto{\rm e}^{\mu\phi}

in the limit
|\mu\phi|\ll 1
.
For the same scalar-GB coupling, there are also asymptotically-flat hairy
BHs
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014a
;
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014b
consistent with
all the linear stability conditions against odd- and
even-parity perturbations
Minamitsuji et al. 2022b
.
We note that BH solutions present for other nonanalytic
functions in
G_{2,3,4}

Babichev et al. 2017

are either unstable around the horizon or
asymptotically
non-flat
Creminelli et al. 2020
;
Minamitsuji et al. 2022b
.
For the linear scalar-GB coupling, it is not yet clear whether
NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile satisfy
all the stability conditions against odd- and
even-parity perturbations.
For this purpose, we can exploit conditions for the
absence of ghost/Laplacian instabilities recently
derived in full Horndeski theories
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022

(see also Refs.
Kobayashi et al. 2012
;
Kobayashi et al. 2014
;
Kase et al. 2020
).
NSs can have a nontrivial scalar profile in more general GB couplings
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
with
a canonical scalar kinetic term
Pani et al. 2011
;
Kleihaus et al. 2014
;
Silva et al. 2018
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
;
Blázquez-Salcedo et al. 2016
;
Olmo et al. 2020
.
This belongs to a subclass of non-shift-symmetric
Horndeski theories. In this paper, for such general GB couplings, we will first study
the background NS solutions and their linear stability both analytically and numerically.
We show that the strength of dimensionless coupling constant

|\alpha|
has an upper bound to ensure the existence of NSs with a nontrivial scalar profile free from instabilities
around the center of star.
Besides
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
,
we also incorporate regular
coupling functions like
G_{3}\supset\mu_{3}X
,

G_{4}\supset\mu_{4}X
, and
G_{4}\supset\lambda_{4}\phi

as the representative cases,
and study their effects on the existence and stability
of solutions. Provided the coupling constants are
in certain ranges, NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar
profile consistent with all the linear stability conditions
are still present in such combined theories.

Moreover, there are also several other gravitational theories containing the GB term in the action. In so-called 4-dimensional-Einstein-GB (4DEGB)
gravity
Glavan and Lin 2020
, the contribution
of the GB term
in spacetime dimensions
D
higher than 4 can be extracted
by rescaling the GB coupling constant as
\alpha\to\alpha/(D-4)
.
If we perform a Kaluza-Klein reduction on a flat
internal space whose volume is characterized by the
scalar field
\phi
, the effective
4DEGB theory after the rescaling of
\alpha

belongs to
a subclass of shift-symmetric Horndeski theories containing the
linear GB coupling but without the canonical scalar kinetic term
Lu and Pang 2020
;
Kobayashi 2020
(see also Ref.
Fernandes et al. 2020
;
Hennigar et al. 2020

for a conformal regularization equivalent to the Kaluza-Klein reduction).
It is known that NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile
are present in the regularized 4DEGB theory
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2021
, but we will show that
they are plagued by a strong coupling problem and Laplacian
instability of even-parity perturbations.
In theories given by the Lagrangian

R+F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})

Nojiri and Odintsov 2005
;
De Felice and Hindmarsh 2007
;
Li et al. 2007
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2009a
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2009b
,
where
F
is a positive power-law function of
R_{\rm GB}^{2}
,
we will also show that a ghost instability and strong coupling
at spatial infinity arise for NS solutions with a nontrivial
scalar profile.
In these 4DEGB and
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
theories, there are no canonical kinetic terms in the action,
which implies the unhealthy propagation of scalar field perturbations, as it also happens in derivative coupling theories without the canonical kinetic term
Kase and Tsujikawa 2021
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we present the linear stability
conditions for relativistic stars on the

In Sec.
III
, we derive solutions expanded around the center of star and at spatial infinity in the presence of scalar-GB couplings

We give a new theoretical bound on
\alpha

for the linear scalar-GB coupling.
In Sec.
IV

NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile without

In Sec.
V

and Laplacian instability for
NSs with a nontrivial scalar profile arising in 4DEGB gravity.
In Sec.
VI
, we prove that a non-vanishing scalar-field
branch appearing in power-law
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
models is
plagued by ghost and strong coupling problems
at large distances.
Sec.
VII
is devoted to conclusions.

II
Background equations and linear
stability conditions

We study the existence and stability of NS solutions
in Horndeski theories
Horndeski 1974
;
Deffayet et al. 2011
;
Kobayashi et al. 2011
;
Charmousis et al. 2012
,
whose action is given by

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,{\cal L}_{H}+{\cal S}_{m}(g_{\mu\nu},\Psi_{m})\,,

(1)

where
g
is a determinant of
the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
, and

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{2}(\phi,X)-G_{3}(\phi,X)\square\phi+G_{4}(\phi,X)\,R+G_{4,X}(\phi,X)\left[(\square\phi)^{2}-(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi)\right]+G_{5}(\phi,X)G_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi

(2)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}G_{5,X}(\phi,X)\left[(\square\phi)^{3}-3(\square\phi)\,(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi)+2(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi)(\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\phi)(\nabla^{\beta}\nabla_{\mu}\phi)\right]\,,

where the coupling functions
G_{j}
(
j=2,3,4,5
) depend on
the scalar field
\phi
and its kinetic term

X=-g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi/2
,
with the covariant derivative operator
\nabla_{\mu}
.
We will use the notations

\square\phi\equiv\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi
and

G_{j,\phi}\equiv\partial G_{j}/\partial\phi
,

G_{j,X}\equiv\partial G_{j}/\partial X
,

G_{j,\phi X}\equiv\partial^{2}G_{j}/(\partial X\partial\phi)
,
and so on.
The scalar field
\phi
and its derivatives are
nonminimally coupled to the Ricci scalar
R
and
Einstein tensor
G_{\mu\nu}

through the couplings
G_{4}
and
G_{5}
, respectively.
For the matter fields
\Psi_{m}
inside NSs, we consider a perfect fluid given by the energy-momentum tensor

T_{\mu\nu}=\left(\rho+P\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+Pg_{\mu\nu}\,,

(3)

where
\rho
and
P
are the density and pressure, respectively,
and
u_{\mu}
is the four-velocity of the fluid satisfying the normalization relation

u_{\mu}u^{\mu}=-1
.
Assuming that the perfect fluid is minimally coupled to gravity,
it obeys the continuity equation

\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=0\,.

(4)

In terms of the action approach, the perfect fluid can be described by a Schutz-Sorkin action
Schutz and Sorkin 1977
;
Brown 1993
;
De Felice et al. 2010
.

\rho

.

II.2
Linear stability conditions

To study the linear stability of NS solutions, we consider metric perturbations
h_{\mu\nu}
on top of the background (
5
) besides perturbations of the scalar field
and perfect fluid. Expanding perturbations on the
background (
5
) in terms of the spherical harmonics
of the unit two-sphere
Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)
,
one can decompose them into the two different sectors
depending on the parity under the rotation
along two-dimensional
sphere
Regge and Wheeler 1957
;
Zerilli 1970
.
The odd- and even-parity perturbations have
the parities
(-1)^{l+1}
and
(-1)^{l}
, respectively.
Any scalar perturbation has the even mode alone, whereas vector and tensor perturbations contain both
odd and even modes.
The decomposition of perturbations of metrics, scalar field,
and perfect fluids into the odd- and even-parity modes was
addressed in Ref.
De Felice et al. 2011
;
Motohashi and Suyama 2011
;
Kobayashi et al. 2012
;
Kobayashi et al. 2014
;
Kase et al. 2020
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
.
In the presence of perfect fluids the stability conditions against odd- and even-parity perturbations were already derived
in Ref.
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
, so we briefly
summarize them in the following.

In the odd-parity sector, there is a dynamical
perturbation
\chi
arising from the gravity sector
besides a nondynamical perturbation
\delta j
related to
the
(\theta,\varphi)
components of fluid four
velocity
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
.
In the limit of large frequencies and multipoles
l
,
the no-ghost condition for the dynamical field
\chi

translates to

{\cal G}\equiv 2G_{4}+2h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{4,X}-h\phi^{\prime 2}\left(G_{5,\phi}+\frac{f^{\prime}h\phi^{\prime}G_{5,X}}{2f}\right)>0\,.

(15)

Under this condition, the Laplacian instability along
the radial and angular directions can be avoided for

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2G_{4}+2h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{4,X}-h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{5,\phi}-\frac{h^{2}\phi^{\prime 3}G_{5,X}}{r}>0\,,

(16)

\displaystyle{\cal F}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2G_{4}+h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{5,\phi}-h\phi^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+h\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)G_{5,X}>0\,,

(17)

under which the squared propagation speeds

c_{r}^{2}={\cal G}/{\cal F}
and

c_{\Omega}^{2}={\cal G}/{\cal H}
are positive.
The expressions (
15
)-(
17
) coincide with those
originally derived in Ref.
Kobayashi et al. 2012

in the absence of the perfect fluid.

In the even-parity sector, there are three dynamical perturbations:
matter perturbation
\delta\rho
,
gravitational perturbations
\psi
, and
scalar-field perturbation
\delta\phi
.
We focus on the linear stability conditions of high radial and angular momentum modes. With the condition (
16
),
there are no ghosts for even-parity perturbations if

\displaystyle\rho+P>0\,,

(18)

\displaystyle{\cal K}\equiv(2{\cal P}_{1}-{\cal F})h\mu^{2}-2{\cal H}^{2}r^{4}(\rho+P)>0\,,

(19)

where

{\cal P}_{1}\equiv\frac{h\mu}{2fr^{2}{\cal H}^{2}}\left(\frac{fr^{4}{\cal H}^{4}}{\mu^{2}h}\right)^{\prime}\,,\qquad\mu\equiv\frac{2(\phi^{\prime}a_{1}+r\sqrt{fh}{\cal H})}{\sqrt{fh}}\,.

(20)

The definition of
a_{1}
is given in Appendix
B
.

The radial propagation speed squared of
\psi

is given by
c_{r2}^{2}={\cal G}/{\cal F}
, which is the same as
that of
\chi
.
Thus the gravitational perturbations in the
odd- and even-parity sectors propagate in the same manner
along the radial direction.
The radial Laplacian instabilities of
\delta\rho

and
\delta\phi
can be avoided for

\displaystyle c_{m}^{2}\equiv\frac{n\rho_{,nn}}{\rho_{,n}}>0\,,

(21)

\displaystyle c_{r3}^{2}\equiv\frac{2\phi^{\prime}[4r^{2}(fh)^{3/2}{\cal H}c_{4}(2\phi^{\prime}a_{1}+r\sqrt{fh}\,{\cal H})-2a_{1}^{2}f^{3/2}\sqrt{h}\phi^{\prime}{\cal G}+(a_{1}f^{\prime}+2c_{2}f)r^{2}fh{\cal H}^{2}]}{f^{5/2}\sqrt{h}\,{\cal K}}>0\,,

(22)

where
c_{2}
and
c_{4}
are given in Appendix
B
.
The conditions (
18
) and (
21
) correspond to
those in the perfect fluid sector. We will consider the fluid
equation of state (EOS) satisfying these inequalities.
We note that
c_{r3}^{2}
corresponds to
the propagation speed squared of scalar-field
perturbation
\delta\phi
.

Along the angular direction, the perfect fluid in the
even-parity sector has the propagation speed squared same as

c_{m}^{2}=n\rho_{,nn}/\rho_{,n}
.
The angular Laplacian instabilities for
\psi
and
\delta\phi

are absent if

c_{\Omega\pm}^{2}=-B_{1}\pm\sqrt{B_{1}^{2}-B_{2}}>0\,,

(23)

where

\displaystyle B_{1}\equiv\frac{r^{3}\sqrt{fh}{\cal H}[4h\beta_{0}\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}-4\phi^{\prime}a_{1}\beta_{3}+r\sqrt{f^{3}h}{\cal G}{\cal H}(\rho+P)]-2fh{\cal G}[r\sqrt{fh}(2{\cal P}_{1}-{\cal F}){\cal H}(\beta_{0}+\phi^{\prime}a_{1})+2\phi^{\prime 2}a_{1}^{2}{\cal P}_{1}]}{4fh(2{\cal P}_{1}-{\cal F}){\cal H}\beta_{0}^{2}-2r^{4}f^{2}h{\cal H}^{3}(\rho+P)},

(24)

\displaystyle B_{2}\equiv-2r^{2}{\frac{r^{2}h\beta_{1}(2fh{\cal F}{\cal G}\beta_{0}+r^{2}\beta_{2})-{r}^{4}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}-fh{\cal F}{\cal G}(\phi^{\prime}fh{\cal F}{\cal G}a_{1}+2r^{3}\sqrt{fh}{\cal H}\beta_{3})}{2fh\phi^{\prime}a_{1}(2{\cal P}_{1}-{\cal F}){\cal F}\beta_{0}^{2}-r^{4}f^{2}h\phi^{\prime}a_{1}{\cal F}{\cal H}^{2}(\rho+P)}}\,.

(25)

The explicit forms of
\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
are
presented in Appendix
B
.
The stability conditions (
23
) are satisfied if

B_{1}^{2}\geq B_{2}>0\,,\quad{\rm and}\quad B_{1}<0\,.

(26)

In summary, we require that NS solutions with
a nontrivial scalar profile should satisfy
the inequalities (
15
), (
16
), (
17
),
(
19
), (
22
), and (
23
) besides
the fluid stability conditions (
18
) and (
21
).

III
Scalar-Gauss-Bonnet couplings

Let us first consider the Einstein-scalar-GB theory
given by the action

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+\eta X+\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}\right]\,,

(27)

where
\eta
is a constant,

M_{\rm Pl}
is the reduced Planck mass,

\alpha
is a dimensionless coupling,

\xi
is a function of
\phi
, and

R_{\rm GB}^{2}\equiv R^{2}-4R_{\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}+R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}R^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\,,

(28)

with
R_{\alpha\beta}
and
R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
being
the Ricci and Riemann tensors respectively.
The action (
27
) belongs to a subclass of
Horndeski theories with the coupling functions

\displaystyle G_{2}=\eta X+8\alpha\xi^{(4)}(\phi)X^{2}(3-\ln|X|)\,,\qquad G_{3}=4\alpha\xi^{(3)}(\phi)X(7-3\ln|X|)\,,

\displaystyle G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+4\alpha\xi^{(2)}(\phi)X(2-\ln|X|)\,,\qquad G_{5}=-4\alpha\xi^{(1)}(\phi)\ln|X|\,,

(29)

where
\xi^{(n)}(\phi)\equiv{\rm d}^{n}\xi(\phi)/{\rm d}\phi^{n}
.

The background Eqs. (
9
), (
10
),
and (
12
) reduce, respectively, to

\displaystyle h^{\prime}=\frac{2(1-h)[M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-8\alpha h(\phi^{\prime\prime}\xi_{,\phi}+\phi^{\prime 2}\xi_{,\phi\phi})]-r^{2}(2\rho+\eta h\phi^{\prime 2})}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r+8\alpha(1-3h)\phi^{\prime}\xi_{,\phi}}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}=\frac{f[2(1-h)M_{\rm Pl}^{2}+r^{2}(2P+\eta h\phi^{\prime 2})]}{h[2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r+8\alpha(1-3h)\phi^{\prime}\xi_{,\phi}]}\,,

(31)

\displaystyle\eta h\phi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\eta(f^{\prime}hr+fh^{\prime}r+4fh)}{2rf}\phi^{\prime}-\frac{2\alpha\xi_{,\phi}[h(1-h)(2ff^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime 2})+ff^{\prime}h^{\prime}(1-3h)]}{r^{2}f^{2}}=0\,.

(32)

The linear stability conditions
(
15
)-(
17
)
in the odd-parity sector translate to

\displaystyle{\cal G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-\frac{4\alpha\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime}f^{\prime}h}{f}>0\,,

(33)

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-\frac{8\alpha\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime}h}{r}>0\,,

(34)

\displaystyle{\cal F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-4\alpha\left(2h\xi_{,\phi\phi}\phi^{\prime 2}+2h\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime\prime}+h^{\prime}\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime}\right)>0\,.

(35)

In the limit of a small GB coupling
\alpha\to 0
,
all of
{\cal G}
,
{\cal H}
, and
{\cal F}

approach
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}
,
so the stability against
odd-parity perturbations is ensured.
As we will see later in
Sec.
III.1
, the leading-order term of

\phi^{\prime}
around
r=0
is proportional to
r
,
so
{\cal H}
does not diverge at the center of star.

On using Eqs. (
30
) and (
31
) to
eliminate
\rho
and
P
, the no-ghost condition (
19
) in the even-parity
sector yields

\displaystyle{\cal K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta h\phi^{\prime 2}r^{2}(M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r-8\alpha h\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime})^{2}

(36)

\displaystyle+64\alpha^{2}h(h-1)\phi^{\prime 2}\xi_{,\phi}^{2}[M_{\rm Pl}^{2}(2rh^{\prime}-h+1)-4\alpha\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime}h^{\prime}(1+3h)+8\alpha h(h-1)(\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime\prime}+\xi_{,\phi\phi}\phi^{\prime 2})]>0\,.

In the limit
\alpha\to 0
, we have

{\cal K}\to 2\eta h\phi^{\prime 2}M_{\rm Pl}^{4}r^{4}
and hence the ghost can be avoided for

\eta>0\,.

(37)

In the following, we will focus on the case in which
\eta
is positive.

The condition (
22
) for the absence of
the Laplacian instability along the radial direction reduces to

c_{r3}^{2}=\frac{2h\phi^{\prime 2}[\eta fr^{2}(M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r-8\alpha h\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime})^{2}-32\alpha^{2}(h-1)\xi_{,\phi}^{2}\{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}[f(h-1)-2rf^{\prime}h]+4\alpha\xi_{,\phi}\phi^{\prime}f^{\prime}h(1+3h)\}]}{f{\cal K}}>0\,.

(38)

Expanding
c_{r3}^{2}
around
\alpha=0
, we obtain

c_{r3}^{2}=1-\frac{64\xi_{,\phi}^{2}(1-h)(f^{\prime}h-fh^{\prime})}{\eta fM_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{3}}\alpha^{2}+{\cal O}(\alpha^{3})\,,

(39)

and hence
c_{r3}^{2}\to 1
as
\alpha\to 0
.

The squared angular propagation speeds of even-parity perturbations
are complicated, but expanding

c_{\Omega,\pm}^{2}
around
\alpha=0
leads to

\displaystyle c_{\Omega,\pm}^{2}=1+\frac{\xi_{,\phi}[2\sqrt{\eta}fh(2f-rf^{\prime})r\phi^{\prime}\pm\sqrt{2{\cal B}}]}{\sqrt{\eta}f^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{2}}|\alpha|+{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})\,,

(40)

where

{\cal B}\equiv M_{\rm Pl}^{2}[f^{\prime 2}hr^{2}+2f^{2}(rh^{\prime}-2h+2)-rf(2f^{\prime\prime}hr+rf^{\prime}h^{\prime}-2f^{\prime}h)]^{2}+2\eta f^{2}h^{2}(rf^{\prime}-2f)^{2}r^{2}\phi^{\prime 2}\,.

(41)

Under the no-ghost condition
\eta>0
,
{\cal B}

is positive and hence
c_{\Omega,\pm}^{2}
are the real values.
In the limit
\alpha\to 0
,
c_{\Omega,\pm}^{2}

approach 1.

From the above discussion, all the linear stability conditions
should be consistently satisfied for
\eta>0
and
|\alpha|\ll 1
.
We note, however, that the quantities like

{\cal H}
,
c_{r3}^{2}
,
c_{\Omega,\pm}^{2}
contain
positive power-law terms of
r
in the denominators.
To show the finiteness of these quantities at
r=0
,
we derive the solutions to
f
,
h
,
\phi

expanded around
r=0
in Sec.
III.1
.
For the existence of hairy stars free from instabilities,
we then put a limit on the coupling constant
\alpha
.

III.1
Solutions expanded around
r=0
and their stability

Around the center of star, we impose
the regular boundary conditions

f(0)=f_{c}
,
h(0)=1
,
\phi(0)=\phi_{c}
,

\rho(0)=\rho_{c}
,
P(0)=P_{c}
and

f^{\prime}(0)=h^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=\rho^{\prime}(0)=P^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Then, around
r=0
, the scalar field is
expanded as

\phi=\phi_{c}+\phi_{2}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(42)

likewise for
f
,
h
,
\rho
, and
P
.
We can also expand the coupling function

\xi(\phi)
(and its
\phi
derivatives), as

\xi(\phi)=\xi(\phi_{c})+\sum_{n\geq 1}\xi^{(n)}(\phi_{c})\frac{(\phi-\phi_{c})^{n}}{n!}\,.

(43)

On using the background
Eqs. (
30
)-(
32
) with Eq. (
8
), the quantity
\phi_{2}
in Eq. (
42
) obeys
the following algebraic equation

Y(x)\equiv\frac{9x(1-16x)^{3}}{2[1+3w_{c}(1-16x)]}=\kappa\alpha^{2}\,,

(44)

where

x\equiv\frac{\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\phi_{2}\,,\qquad\kappa\equiv\frac{\rho_{c}^{2}\xi_{,\phi}^{2}(\phi_{c})}{\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{6}}\,,\qquad w_{c}=\frac{P_{c}}{\rho_{c}}\,.

(45)

We assume that the EOS parameter
w_{c}
is
in the range
w_{c}>0
.
In the small-coupling limit
|\alpha|\ll 1
, we have

9x/[2(1+3w_{c})]\simeq\kappa\alpha^{2}
and hence

\phi_{2}=\frac{2\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})\rho_{c}^{2}(1+3w_{c})}{9\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{4}}\alpha+{\cal O}(\alpha^{3})\,.

(46)

Substituting the leading-order solution of Eq. (
46
)
into the definition of
x
in Eq. (
45
),
we have

x>0\,,

(47)

under the condition
\eta>0
.
By the end of this section,
we will
not
exploit the small
\alpha
expansion
to discuss the linear stability of NSs.

The solutions to
f
,
h
,
P
expanded around
r=0

are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{c}+\frac{f_{c}[M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\rho_{c}(1+3w_{c})-48\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})\rho_{c}w_{c}\phi_{2}]}{6[M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-16\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})\phi_{2}]^{2}}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(48)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\rho_{c}}{3[M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-16\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})\phi_{2}]}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(49)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{c}-\frac{\rho_{c}(1+w_{c})[M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\rho_{c}(1+3w_{c})-48\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})\rho_{c}w_{c}\phi_{2}]}{12[M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-16\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})\phi_{2}]^{2}}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,.

(50)

The scalar field is of the form (
42
) with
\phi_{2}
satisfying the relation (
44
).
On using these solutions, the quantities
{\cal G}
,
{\cal H}
, and
{\cal F}
at
r=0
reduce to

{\cal G}(r=0)=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\,,\qquad{\cal H}(r=0)={\cal F}(r=0)=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left(1-16x\right)\,,

(51)

where
{\cal H}
is finite at
r=0
due to the property that

\phi^{\prime}=2\phi_{2}r
at leading order.
The squared propagation speeds of odd-parity perturbations
along the radial and angular directions are given by

c_{r}^{2}(r=0)=c_{\Omega}^{2}(r=0)=\frac{1}{1-16x}\,.

(52)

Then, there are neither ghost nor Laplacian instabilities
in the odd-parity sector if

0<x<\frac{1}{16}\,,

(53)

where we have also taken into account the condition (
47
).
Both
c_{r}^{2}(r=0)
and
c_{\Omega}^{2}(r=0)

are larger than 1.
In the limit that
\alpha\to 0
, we have
x\to 0
and
hence
c_{r}^{2}(r=0)=c_{\Omega}^{2}(r=0)\to 1

as expected.
In the subject of NSs, “superluminality” is sometimes argued as
a sign of “acausality.”
We emphasize that the propagation speed
of scalar-field perturbations just fixes a causal boundary of the scalar field at each position,
and the superluminal speeds do not mean acausality.

In the even-parity sector, the leading-order term of
{\cal K}
expanded around
r=0
is proportional to
r^{6}
, i.e.,

{\cal K}=\frac{16\rho_{c}^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}x[1+48x+3w_{c}(1-16x)]}{9(1-16x)}r^{6}+{\cal O}(r^{7})\,,

(54)

where we used Eq. (
44
) to eliminate
\kappa\alpha^{2}
.
Under the condition (
53
), the coefficient of

r^{6}
in Eq. (
54
) is positive.
The squared radial and angular propagation speeds
of
\delta\phi
at
r=0
are given by

c_{r3}^{2}(r=0)=c_{\Omega-}^{2}(r=0)=\frac{(1+3w_{c})(1-64x)+2304w_{c}x^{2}}{(1-16x)[1+48x+3w_{c}(1-16x)]}\,,

(55)

whereas
c_{\Omega+}^{2}(r=0)
is equivalent to Eq. (
52
).
Under the condition (
53
), the absence of
Laplacian instability of
\delta\phi
requires a positivity
of the numerator of Eq. (
55

0<x<x_{m}\equiv\frac{2(1+3w_{c})-\sqrt{(1+3w_{c})(4+3w_{c})}}{144w_{c}}\,.

(56)

For
0<w_{c}<\infty
we have
1/64<x_{m}<1/48
, so

x_{m}
is smaller than
1/16
.
The function
Y(x)
in Eq. (
44
) has a maximum value

Y(x_{m})
at

.
Provided that
0<\kappa\alpha^{2}<Y(x_{m})
, there are solutions to Eq. (
44

This gives an upper bound on

, as

|\alpha|<\frac{\sqrt{\eta}M_{\rm Pl}^{3}}{\rho_{c}\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})}\frac{\sqrt{6}(2\sqrt{1+3w_{c}}-\sqrt{4+3w_{c}})^{1/2}[\sqrt{(1+3w_{c})(4+3w_{c})}-2+3w_{c}]^{3/2}}{216w_{c}^{2}(\sqrt{1+3w_{c}}+\sqrt{4+3w_{c}})^{1/2}}\,.

(57)

Among the two solutions of
\kappa\alpha^{2}=Y(x)
,
one of them (
x=x_{1}
) exists in
0<x_{1}<x_{m}
,
while the other (
x=x_{2}
) is in the region
x_{m}<x_{2}<1/16

The former is in the region (
56

condition of Laplacian stability.
In summary, as long as
\alpha
is in the range (
57

there is a solution
x=x_{1}
consistent will all the linear
stability conditions at
r=0
.
The criterion (
57
) is also valid for nonrelativistic stars
with
w_{c}\ll 1
. In this case, the scalar-GB coupling is constrained
to be

|\alpha|<\frac{9\sqrt{6\eta}M_{\rm Pl}^{3}}{128\rho_{c}\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})}\,,\quad{\rm for}\quad w_{c}\to 0\,,

(58)

which can be applied to nonrelativistic objects such as Sun and Earth.

|\alpha|

Let us consider the power-law

(59)

(61)

For the linear GB coupling (

\rho_{c}
and EOS parameter
w_{c}
determine the upper
limit of
|\alpha|
, without having the dependence of
\phi_{c}
.
In Fig.

as a function of

for SLy (solid) and FPS (dashed)

Haensel and Potekhin 2004

.

.

(62)

For FPS EOS, we also obtain a similar upper bound.

.

The dilatonic GB coupling

III.2
Solutions expanded at spatial infinity
and their stability

The surface of star is defined by the radius
r_{s}

at which the fluid pressure
P
vanishes.
Outside the star (
r>r_{s}
), we have
\rho=0=P

in the background Eqs. (
9
)-(

).
Imposing the asymptotic flatness at spatial infinity,
we can expand
f
,
h
, and
\phi
in the forms

,

h=1+\sum_{i=1}\hat{h}_{i}/r^{i}
, and

\phi=\phi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}\hat{\phi}_{i}/r^{i}
.
We also use the expansions of
\xi(\phi)
and
its
\phi
derivatives analogous to Eq. (
43
), with the
replacement
\phi_{c}\to\phi_{0}
.
The large-distance solutions consistent with
the background Eqs. (
30
)-(

given by

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{\eta M\hat{\phi}_{1}^{2}}{6M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{3}}+\frac{M\hat{\phi}_{1}[\eta M\hat{\phi}_{1}+24\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{0})]}{3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{4}}+{\cal O}(r^{-5})\,,

(64)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{\eta\hat{\phi}_{1}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{2}}+\frac{\eta M\hat{\phi}_{1}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{3}}+\frac{2M\hat{\phi}_{1}[\eta M\hat{\phi}_{1}+24\alpha\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{0})]}{3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{4}}+{\cal O}(r^{-5})\,,

(65)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{0}+\frac{\hat{\phi}_{1}}{r}+\frac{M\hat{\phi}_{1}}{r^{2}}+\frac{(16M^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}-\eta\hat{\phi}_{1}^{2})\hat{\phi}_{1}}{12M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{3}}+\frac{M[6\eta M^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\hat{\phi}_{1}-\eta^{2}\hat{\phi}_{1}^{3}-12\alpha MM_{\rm Pl}^{2}\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{0})]}{3\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{4}}+{\cal O}(r^{-5})\,,

(66)

where we set

.

)-(

(67)

c_{r}^{2}
and
c_{\Omega}^{2}

{\cal K}
is

{\cal K}=2\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{4}\hat{\phi}_{1}^{2}+\frac{4\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{4}M\hat{\phi}_{1}^{2}}{r}+{\cal O}(r^{-2})\,,

(68)

whose positivity is ensured for

.

c_{r3}^{2}=1-\frac{128\alpha^{2}M\xi_{,\phi}^{2}(\phi_{0})\hat{\phi}_{1}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{4}r^{7}}+{\cal O}(r^{-8})\,,

(69)

III.3
Numerical solutions and stability conditions

The discussions in Secs.
III.1
and
III.2

show that, under the bound (
57
)
with
\eta>0
, there are NS solutions with a nontrivial
scalar profile consistent with all the linear stability
conditions around
r=0
and
r\to\infty
.
However, they do not necessarily guarantee the linear stability
of solutions at intermediate distances, so we will numerically
study whether neither ghost nor Laplacian instabilities
appear at any radius
r
.

For concreteness, we study the linear scalar-GB coupling given by the

Since

To satisfy the boundary conditions of

Then, the field derivative can be expressed as

To perform the numerical integration, we introduce

s=\ln\frac{r}{r_{0}}\,,\qquad{\cal M}(r)=4\pi rM_{\rm Pl}^{2}(1-h)\,,\qquad m(r)=\frac{3{\cal M}(r)}{4\pi r_{0}^{3}\rho_{0}}\,,\qquad y=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{0}}\,,\qquad z=\frac{P}{\rho_{0}}\,.

The ADM mass of star can be computed as

where

For the NS EOS, we exploit the analytic representation

IV
Scalar-Gauss-Bonnet and regular couplings

In this section, we study the existence and the linear stability of
NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile
in the presence of several regular couplings
besides the scalar-GB coupling

\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.
We also take into account the Einstein-Hilbert term
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}R/2

and the canonical kinetic term
\eta X
(with
\eta>0
) in the action.
We exploit SLy EOS for the numerical analysis
in this section.

IV.1
Cubic Galileon and scalar-GB couplings

The cubic Galileon corresponds to the coupling function

G_{3}\supset\mu_{3}X
, where
\mu_{3}
is a constant.
Let us consider theories given by the action

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+\eta X+\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}+\mu_{3}X\square\phi\right]\,.

(79)

We first derive the solutions expanded around

r=0
and then discuss the linear stability of them.
In doing so, we use the approximation
|\alpha|\ll 1
and pick up terms up to
the order of
\alpha^{2}
.
Then, the coefficient
\phi_{2}
in the field
expansion (
42
) is

\phi_{2}=\frac{3\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{6}-32\rho_{c}^{2}(1+2w_{c})\xi_{,\phi}^{2}(\phi_{c})\alpha^{2}}{24\mu_{3}M_{\rm Pl}^{6}}\left[1-\sqrt{1-\frac{32M_{\rm Pl}^{8}\rho_{c}^{2}(1+3w_{c})\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})\mu_{3}\alpha}{[3\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{6}-32\rho_{c}^{2}(1+2w_{c})\xi_{,\phi}^{2}(\phi_{c})\alpha^{2}]^{2}}}\right]+{\cal O}(\alpha^{3})\,,

(80)

where we have chosen the branch recovering
Eq. (
46
) in the limit
\mu_{3}\to 0
.
We do not necessarily assume that the
cubic Galileon coupling is of the same
order as the scalar-GB coupling.
The consistency of the small
\alpha
expansion requires that

\xi_{,\phi}(\phi_{c})|\mu_{3}\alpha|\ll\frac{\eta^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{4}}{\rho_{c}^{2}(1+3w_{c})}\,,

(81)

where we also assumed
\alpha^{2}\rho_{c}^{2}\xi_{,\phi}^{2}(\phi_{c})\ll\eta M_{\rm Pl}^{6}
.
Up to the order of
r^{2}
, the metric
components and fluid pressure are the same
forms as Eqs. (
48
)-(
50
) with

\phi^{\prime}(r)=2\phi_{2}r
.
In the limit that
\alpha\to 0
, we have

\phi_{2}=0
even for
\mu_{3}\neq 0
.
This shows that the cubic-order coupling alone does
not give rise to NS solutions with

\phi^{\prime}(r)\neq 0
.
This is consistent with the no-hair argument of Ref.
Lehébel et al. 2017
for regular couplings in shift-symmetric
Horndeski theories.
In other words, the scalar-GB coupling is needed for the realization of NSs with a nontrivial scalar profile.

On using the above background solutions around

r=0
, we find that
{\cal G}
,
{\cal H}
,
and
{\cal F}
are
of the same form as Eq. (
51

\phi_{2}

satisfying Eq. (
80

Then, the absence of ghost/Laplacian instabilities in the odd-parity

(82)

On the other hand, even if

On using the solution (
80

IV.2

\mu_{4}X

G_{5}

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+\eta X+\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}+\mu_{4}XR+\mu_{4}\left\{(\square\phi)^{2}-(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi)\right\}\right]\,.

NS solutions

this no-hair property.

Then, the solutions expanded around
r=0

IV.3

At the end of this section, we study the existence and stability of NS

further, it is possible to realize NSs with a nontrivial scalar profile whose mass and radius
exhibit notable difference from those in GR.
If we take into account Solar System constraints, however, the coupling
\tilde{\lambda}_{4}

should be less than the order
10^{-3}
. Hence we do not consider the case in
which the product
|\tilde{\lambda}_{4}\phi_{c}|
exceeds the order of
10^{-3}
.

If the scalar-GB coupling is present besides the nonminimal coupling,
it is possible to realize NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile as well.
The solid curves in the left panel of Fig.
5
correspond to
the radial dependence of
\phi^{\prime}
and
{\cal M}

for
\alpha=2.5\times 10^{-4}
,
\tilde{\lambda}_{4}=-2.5\times 10^{-3}
,
and
\phi_{c}=M_{\rm Pl}
. Inside the NS, the field derivative is significantly
enhanced in comparison to the case
\alpha=0
.
Around the surface of star
\phi^{\prime}
starts to decrease rapidly,
but it enters the region with the radial dependence

\phi^{\prime}\simeq-\hat{\phi}_{1}/r^{2}
for
r\gtrsim r_{0}
.
As we already mentioned, this latter property is attributed to
the fact that the contribution to
\phi^{\prime}
from the nonminimal
coupling dominates over that from the scalar-GB
coupling at large distances.
For
\alpha=2.5\times 10^{-4}
the mass and radius of NS are found to be
M=1.873M_{\odot}
and
r_{s}=10.93
km, both of which are slightly
smaller than those for
\alpha=0
mentioned above.
In the right panel of Fig.
5
, we show the five
squared propagation speeds versus
r/r_{0}
for
\alpha=2.5\times 10^{-4}
,

\tilde{\lambda}_{4}=-2.5\times 10^{-3}
, and
\phi_{c}=M_{\rm Pl}
.
In comparison to the case
\alpha=0
where all the propagation
speeds are equivalent to 1, they are different from 1 deep inside the NS
and approach 1 outside the star.
For the model parameters used in Fig.
5
, there are neither
ghost nor Laplacian instabilities for
NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile.

Provided that
|\tilde{\lambda}_{4}\phi_{c}|\lesssim 10^{-3}
,
the scalar-GB coupling
|\alpha|
larger than the order
10^{-5}

gives the dominant contribution to
\phi^{\prime}
inside the star.
Then, for
|\alpha|\gtrsim 10^{-5}
, the background NS
solution and its linear stability are not much different from
those for the scalar-GB coupling alone discussed in Sec.
III
.

V
Regularized 4D-Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity

In this section, we study the linear stability of NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile
in so-called “4DEGB gravity” arising from
the reduction of higher-dimensional GB theory
to 4 dimensions.
If we consider the GB term
{\cal R}_{\rm GB}^{2}

in spacetime dimensions
D
higher than 4,
the field equations of motion following from the Lagrangian

L=\sqrt{-g}\,\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GB}{\cal R}_{\rm GB}^{2}
vanish in 4 dimensions
Lovelock 1971
.
However, rescaling the GB coupling constant as

\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GB}\to\alpha_{\rm GB}/(D-4)
allows a possibility
for extracting contributions of the higher-dimensional GB term
Glavan and Lin 2020
.
Under such a rescaling, in the limit
D\to 4
,
it is possible to construct a regularized 4-dimensional theory by adding
a counter-term to eliminate
divergent parts of the theory
Fernandes et al. 2020
;
Hennigar et al. 2020
.
The other equivalent procedure is
to perform a Kaluza-Klein reduction of
D
-dimensional Einstein-GB
gravity on a
(D-4)
-dimensional
maximally symmetric space with
a vanishing spatial curvature
Lu and Pang 2020
;
Kobayashi 2020
.
The size of such a maximally symmetric space
is characterized by a scalar field
\phi
.
The 4-dimensional action obtained from the Kaluza-Klein
reduction of
D
-dimensional Einstein-GB theory belongs to
a subclass of shift-symmetric Horndeski theories
given by the coupling functions

G_{2}=8\alpha_{\rm GB}X^{2}\,,\qquad G_{3}=8\alpha_{\rm GB}X\,,\qquad G_{4}=1+4\alpha_{\rm GB}X\,,\qquad G_{5}=4\alpha_{\rm GB}\ln|X|\,.

(123)

Notice that the standard kinetic term
X
is absent in
G_{2}
.
Throughout this section, we use the unit
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}/2=1
.

From the scalar-field Eq. (
12
), we have

r^{2}\sqrt{f/h}\,J^{r}=Q={\rm constant}
and hence

4\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\left(f^{\prime}+2\phi^{\prime}f\right)\left[1-h(1+r\phi^{\prime})^{2}\right]\alpha_{\rm GB}=Q\,.

(124)

Due to the regularity conditions
f^{\prime}(0)=0
and
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0

at the center of star, it follows that
Q=0
.
To satisfy Eq. (
124
) with
Q=0
at any radius
r
,
we require that
1-h(1+r\phi^{\prime})^{2}=0
.
The branch where
\phi(r)
decreases at spatial infinity
(satisfying the asymptotic flatness
h\to 1
as
r\to\infty
)
is given by

\phi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}-1\right)\,.

(125)

Taking the
r
derivative of this equation and substituting

\phi^{\prime\prime}
and Eq. (
125
) into Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
),
we obtain

\displaystyle h^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2(h-1)[r^{2}+(h-1)\alpha_{\rm GB}]+\rho r^{4}}{2r[r^{2}-2(h-1)\alpha_{\rm GB}]}\,,

(126)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f\frac{2(h-1)[r^{2}+(h-1)\alpha_{\rm GB}]-Pr^{4}}{2hr[r^{2}-2(h-1)\alpha_{\rm GB}]}\,.

(127)

Outside the star (
\rho=0=P
), there is the following
analytic solution
Lu and Pang 2020
;
Fernandes et al. 2021

h=f=1+\frac{r^{2}}{2\alpha_{\rm GB}}\left[1-\sqrt{1+\frac{8\alpha_{\rm GB}M}{r^{3}}}\right]\,,

(128)

where
M
is an integration constant.
At spatial infinity, this has the asymptotic behavior

h=f=1-2M/r+{\cal O}(r^{-4})
and
\phi^{\prime}=M/r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{-3})
.

Inside the NS, the integrated solutions to
h
and
f
depend
on the fluid EOS. For constant density
\rho
, we have
the following exact solution
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2021

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\zeta r^{2}\,,

(129)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha_{\rm GB}\zeta)^{2}}{4(1+2\alpha_{\rm GB}\zeta)^{2}}\left[\frac{3(1+\alpha_{\rm GB}\zeta)}{1-\alpha_{\rm GB}\zeta}\sqrt{1-\zeta r_{s}^{2}}-\sqrt{1-\zeta r^{2}}\right]^{2}\,,

(130)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\frac{(1-\alpha_{\rm GB}\zeta)[\sqrt{1-\zeta r^{2}}-\sqrt{1-\zeta r_{s}^{2}}]}{3(1+\alpha_{\rm GB}\zeta)\sqrt{1-\zeta r_{s}^{2}}-(1-\alpha_{\rm GB}\zeta)\sqrt{1-\zeta r^{2}}}=\rho\left(\sqrt{\frac{1-\zeta r_{s}^{2}}{f}}-1\right)\,,

(131)

where

\zeta\equiv\frac{1}{2\alpha_{\rm GB}}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{2}{3}\rho\alpha_{\rm GB}}-1\right)\,.

(132)

At the surface of star (
r=r_{s}
), the fluid pressure (
131
) vanishes.
Matching
h
and
f
with Eq. (
128
) at
r=r_{s}
,
there is the relation
M=\rho r_{s}^{3}/12
.
In the following, we will study the linear stability of NSs without assuming
their EOSs.

The quantities associated with the linear stability against odd-parity
perturbations are given by

\displaystyle{\cal G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{h}\,r^{4}-2r^{2}(\sqrt{h}-1)(Pr^{2}+2-2h+4\sqrt{h})\alpha_{\rm GB}-4(\sqrt{h}+1)(\sqrt{h}-1)^{4}\alpha_{\rm GB}^{2}}{r^{2}\sqrt{h}\,[r^{2}+2(1-h)\alpha_{\rm GB}]}\,,

(133)

\displaystyle{\cal F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\{r^{4}+r^{2}[\rho r^{2}+2(h-1)]\alpha_{\rm GB}-2(h-1)^{2}\alpha_{\rm GB}^{2}\}}{r^{2}[r^{2}+2(1-h)\alpha_{\rm GB}]}\,,

(134)

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2+\frac{4(1-h)}{r^{2}}\alpha_{\rm GB}\,.

(135)

In the limit that
\alpha_{\rm GB}\to 0
we have
{\cal G}={\cal F}={\cal H}=2
,
so the linear stability against odd-parity perturbations is ensured for the small GB coupling.
On using the background Eqs. (
126
) and (
127
),
it follows that

{\cal K}=0\,,

(136)

at any radius
r>0
.
Inside the star, the term
(2{\cal P}_{1}-{\cal F})h\mu^{2}

exactly cancels the contribution
-2{\cal H}^{2}r^{4}(\rho+P)
,
while, outside the star,
{\cal K}=(2{\cal P}_{1}-{\cal F})h\mu^{2}=0
.
The fact that
{\cal K}
vanishes everywhere, which is mostly
attributed to the absence of a standard kinetic term in
G_{2}
,
is the signal of a strong coupling problem.
Indeed, the squared radial propagation speed
associated with the stability of scalar-field perturbation
in the even-parity sector yields

c_{r3}^{2}\to\infty\,.

(137)

This divergent property of
c_{r3}^{2}
arises from the existence
of term
{\cal K}
in the denominator of Eq. (
22
).
We note that the product
{\cal K}c_{r3}^{2}
is finite.
Exploiting the exact solution (
128
) outside the star
and performing the expansion with respect to
1/r
at
spatial infinity, we obtain the dependence

{\cal K}c_{r3}^{2}=-\frac{256M^{3}\alpha_{\rm GB}}{r^{3}}+{\cal O}(r^{-4})\,.

(138)

For
\alpha_{\rm GB}<0
, the leading-order contribution
to
{\cal K}c_{r3}^{2}
is positive at large distances.

The quantities
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
also diverge, so
this leads to the divergence of angular propagation speeds
in the even-parity sector.
Expanding the product
{\cal K}B_{2}
at spatial infinity,
it follows that

{\cal K}B_{2}=\frac{128M^{3}\alpha_{\rm GB}}{r^{3}}+{\cal O}(r^{-4})\,.

(139)

Dividing Eq. (
138
) by Eq. (
139
), we obtain

\frac{c_{r3}^{2}}{B_{2}}=-2+{\cal O}(r^{-1})\,.

(140)

Since the signs of
c_{r3}^{2}
and
B_{2}
are different from
each other, either of the linear stability conditions
(
22
) or (
26
) is violated at large distances.
Thus, the NS solutions in 4DEGB gravity not only suffer from
the strong coupling problem but also the Laplacian
instability of even-parity perturbations.
These problems also persist for hairy BHs given by
the line element (
128
) present in 4DEGB theory.
In the case of BHs, there is also the instability of even-parity
perturbations in the vicinity of the event horizon
Tsujikawa 2022
.

VI
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity

Finally, we study a modified GB gravity in which the 4-dimensional
action contains an arbitrary function
F
of the GB term

R_{\rm GB}^{2}
besides the Einstein-Hilbert term.
This theory is given by the action

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})\right]\,,

(141)

which is equivalent to
Kobayashi et al. 2011

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}-V(\phi)\right]\,,

(142)

where

\varphi\equiv R_{\rm GB}^{2}\,,\qquad\phi\equiv M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{4}\varphi\,,\qquad\xi(\phi)\equiv F_{,\varphi}\,,\qquad V(\phi)\equiv\varphi\xi-F\,.

(143)

The action (
142
) belongs to a subclass of Horndeski
theories given by the coupling functions

\displaystyle G_{2}=-V(\phi)+8\xi^{(4)}(\phi)X^{2}(3-\ln|X|)\,,\qquad G_{3}=4\xi^{(3)}(\phi)X(7-3\ln|X|)\,,

\displaystyle G_{4}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}+4\xi^{(2)}(\phi)X(2-\ln|X|)\,,\qquad G_{5}=-4\xi^{(1)}(\phi)\ln|X|\,.

(144)

Hence there is no standard scalar kinetic term in
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity.

Let us focus on the power-law
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
models given by

F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})=\beta\left(R_{\rm GB}\right)^{n}\,,

(145)

where
\beta
and
n
are constants.
We consider the positive integers
n
in the range
n\geq 2
.
Then, the GB coupling function
\xi(\phi)
and scalar potential
V(\phi)

in the action (
142
) yield

\xi(\phi)=\alpha n\frac{r_{0}^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{\rm Pl}}\right)^{n-1}\,,\qquad V(\phi)=\alpha(n-1)\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2r_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{\rm Pl}}\right)^{n}\,,

(146)

where
\alpha\equiv 2r_{0}^{2-4n}M_{\rm Pl}^{-2}\beta
is
a dimensionless coupling.

From the scalar-field Eq. (
12
), we obtain

\alpha\phi^{n-2}\left\{r^{2}f^{2}\phi-2\left[ff^{\prime}h^{\prime}(3h-1)+f^{\prime 2}h(1-h)+2f^{\prime\prime}fh(h-1)\right]M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{4}\right\}=0\,.

(147)

For
n=2
, there is only one branch characterized by

\phi=\frac{2\left[ff^{\prime}h^{\prime}(3h-1)+f^{\prime 2}h(1-h)+2f^{\prime\prime}fh(h-1)\right]M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{4}}{r^{2}f^{2}}\,.

(148)

For
n\geq 3
, we also have the no-hair branch
\phi=0

besides (
148
).

Let us first consider the power
n=2
. Around the center of star, the solutions consistent with the
boundary conditions at
r=0
are

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{c}+\frac{f_{c}[M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{2}\rho_{c}(1+3w_{c})-M_{\rm Pl}\phi_{c}^{2}\alpha-24(2w_{c}\rho_{c}r_{0}^{2}-\phi_{c}^{2}\alpha)r_{0}^{2}\phi_{2}\alpha]}{6M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{2}(M_{\rm Pl}-16r_{0}^{2}\phi_{2}\alpha)^{2}}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(149)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2\rho_{c}r_{0}^{2}+\alpha\phi_{c}^{2}}{6M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{2}(M_{\rm Pl}-16\alpha r_{0}^{2}\phi_{2})}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(150)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{c}+\phi_{2}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(151)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{c}-\frac{\rho_{c}(1+w_{c})\{M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{2}\rho_{c}(1+3w_{c})-M_{\rm Pl}\phi_{c}^{2}\alpha-24(2w_{c}\rho_{c}r_{0}^{2}-\phi_{c}^{2}\alpha)r_{0}^{2}\phi_{2}\alpha\}}{12M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{2}(M_{\rm Pl}-16r_{0}^{2}\phi_{2}\alpha)^{2}}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,.

(153)

Since Eq. (

\phi_{2}

In the limit
\alpha\to 0

is approximately given by

\displaystyle\phi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{48M_{\rm Pl}r_{0}^{2}\alpha\phi_{c}}\left[3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\phi_{c}-2\times 6^{2/3}M_{\rm Pl}w_{c}\phi_{c}\left(\frac{r_{0}^{8}\rho_{c}^{4}}{3M_{\rm Pl}^{3}\phi_{c}^{2}+\sqrt{3M_{\rm Pl}^{3}\phi_{c}^{3}\left(16r_{0}^{4}w_{c}^{3}\rho_{c}^{2}+3M_{\rm Pl}^{3}\phi_{c}\right)}}\right)^{1/3}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+6^{1/3}r_{0}\left\{r_{0}\rho_{c}^{2}\left(3M_{\rm Pl}^{3}\phi_{c}^{2}+\sqrt{3M_{\rm Pl}^{3}\phi_{c}^{3}\left(16r_{0}^{4}w_{c}^{3}\rho_{c}^{2}+3M_{\rm Pl}^{3}\phi_{c}\right)}\right)\right\}^{1/3}\right]+{\cal O}(\alpha^{0}).

as
\alpha\to 0
. In general,
in the limit

.
This indicates a pathology of the interior solutions in the power-law

The solutions expanded far outside the star are

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{4608M^{3}r_{0}^{6}\alpha}{r^{9}}+{\cal O}(r^{-{10}})\,,

VII
Conclusions

In gravitational theories with a coupling to
GB curvature invariant
R_{\rm GB}^{2}
,
we studied the existence and stability of NS solutions with a nontrivial profile of the scalar field on a static and spherically symmetric background.
For this purpose, we exploited conditions for avoiding ghost/Laplacian instabilities of
odd- and even-parity perturbations with
high radial and angular momentum
modes
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
.
These linear stability conditions in full
Horndeski theories, which are summarized in Sec.
II
, can be applied not only to BHs, i.e., the vacuum case,
but also to NSs, i.e., the case with
matter fluids.

The scalar-GB coupling
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
gives rise to NSs endowed with a
nontrivial scalar profile both inside and outside the star.
In Sec.
III
, we derived the
approximated background solutions
by using the expansion both around
r=0
and
at spatial infinity. We then studied the linear stability of them. The no-ghost condition requires that the theory has to contain
a positive scalar kinetic term of the scalar field., i.e.,
\eta>0
.
For the existence of NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile free from instabilities at
r=0
, we derived the upper bound (
57
) on the dimensionless coupling constant
\alpha
.
For the linear scalar-GB coupling

\alpha_{\rm GB}\phi R_{\rm GB}^{2}
,
this bound translates to
\sqrt{|\alpha_{\rm GB}|}<0.7~{\rm km}
to realize maximum masses
of NSs for typical EOSs.
This theoretical limit is stronger than those observationally constrained from the GW measurements emitted from binaries
containing NSs.
Numerically, we confirmed that the bound (
57
) is sufficiently accurate for ensuring the existence of NSs with a nontrivial scalar profile consistent with all the linear stability conditions.
As we see in Fig.
2
, the squared propagation speeds of odd- and even-parity perturbations deviate from 1 deep
inside the NS, but all of them approach 1 outside the star.
Note that the propagation speeds different
from the speed of light do not imply any acausality and instead determine the
causal boundaries locally at each position.

In Sec.
IV
, we incorporated several
regular Horndeski couplings besides the scalar-GB coupling
\alpha\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.
In the presence of a canonical kinetic term
\eta X
,
the cubic-order derivative interaction of the
scalar field
G_{3}\supset\mu_{3}X
or
the quartic-order derivative coupling

G_{4}\supset\mu_{4}X
alone do not give rise to asymptotically-flat NS solutions with
a nontrivial scalar profile
Lehébel et al. 2017
.
We confirmed that the presence of the GB coupling is mandatory to obtain the NS solutions with a nontrivial profile of the scalar field.

In the case where the scalar-GB coupling is present,
we showed the existence of NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile free from ghost/Laplacian instabilities for certain ranges of the coupling constants
\mu_{3}
and
\mu_{4}
.
As we see in Figs.
3
and
4
,
these new couplings do not lead to significant
changes to the ADM mass of NSs,
but the squared propagation speeds
c_{r3}^{2}
and

c_{\Omega-}^{2}
inside the star are subject to modifications. We also showed that NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile exist for
a linear nonminimal coupling
G_{4}\supset\lambda_{4}\phi
alone. Under local gravity constraints on the coupling constant
\lambda_{4}
, however, the scalar-GB coupling gives dominant contributions to the background scalar-field profile as well as the linear stability of NSs for
|\alpha|

close to its upper limit.

In Sec.
V
, we addressed the linear stability of NS solutions with a nontrivial scalar profile
in regularized 4DEGB gravity.
After the Kaluza-Klein reduction of
D
-dimensional Einstein-GB theory on a flat internal space,
the resulting 4-dimensional action belongs to a subclass of shift-symmetric Horndeski theories.
For the solution with a nontrivial scalar
profile (
125
), we showed that
{\cal K}=0

and
c_{r3}^{2}\to\infty
at any radius
r
,
and hence it is plagued by a strong coupling
problem. Moreover the leading-order contribution
to
c_{r3}^{2}/B_{2}
is
-2
at large distances,
so there is also the Laplacian instability
for even-parity perturbations.
Along with the fact that the BH solutions in regularized 4DEGB gravity are also
unstable
Tsujikawa 2022
,
there are no stable compact objects endowed with
a nontrivial scalar profile in this theory.

In Sec.
VI
, we considered the power-law
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
models with the Einstein-Hilbert term, which are equivalent to
the action (
142
) with
\xi(\phi)
and
V(\phi)

given by Eq. (
146
).
For
n\geq 2
, there is a nonvanishing scalar-field branch
characterized by Eq. (
148
).
If NSs with a nontrivial scalar profile are present, the interior solutions should join
the large-distance exterior solutions (
155
)-(
157
).
On using the latter, we find that the
leading-order term of
{\cal K}
is negative.
For increasing
r
,
{\cal K}
rapidly decreases
toward
-0
with a large negative power-law
dependence on
r
.
Hence the hairy branch (
148
) is excluded by the problems of ghost instability and strong coupling at spatial infinity.
This is analogous to what was found for BHs
in the same theory
Minamitsuji et al. 2022a
.
In both regularized 4DEGB theory and
F(R_{\rm GB}^{2})
gravity, the instabilities of NSs with a nontrivial scalar profile arise from the unhealthy propagation
of
\delta\phi
associated with the absence of
a canonical kinetic term.

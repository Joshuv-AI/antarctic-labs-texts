Differential analysis of incompressibility in neutron-rich nuclei
Bao-An Li 1 and Wen-Jie Xie 2

Abstract
Both the incompressibility K A K_{\rm A} of a finite nucleus of mass A and that ( K ∞ K_{\infty} ) of infinite nuclear matter are fundamentally important for many critical issues in nuclear physics and astrophysics. While some consensus has been reached about the K ∞ K_{\infty} , accurate theoretical predictions and experimental extractions of K τ K_{\tau} characterizing the isospin dependence of K A K_{\rm A} have been very difficult. We propose a differential approach to extract the K τ K_{\tau} and K ∞ K_{\infty} independently from the K A K_{\rm A} data of any two nuclei in a given isotope chain. Applying this new method to the K A K_{\rm A} data from isoscalar giant monopole resonances (ISGMR) in even-even Pb, Sn, Cd and Ca isotopes taken by U. Garg et al. at the Research Center for Nuclear Physics (RCNP), Osaka University, Japan, we find that the 106 Cd- 116 Cd and 112 Sn- 124 Sn pairs having the largest differences in isospin asymmetries in their respective isotope chains measured so far provide consistently the most accurate up-to-date K τ K_{\tau} value of K τ = − 616 ± 59 K_{\tau}=-616\pm 59 MeV and K τ = − 623 ± 86 K_{\tau}=-623\pm 86 MeV, respectively, largely independent of the remaining uncertainties of the surface and Coulomb terms in expanding the K A K_{\rm A} , while the K ∞ K_{\infty} values extracted from different isotopes chains are all well within the current uncertainty range of the community consensus for K ∞ K_{\infty} . Moreover, the size and origin of the “Soft Sn Puzzle” is studied with respect to the “Stiff Pb Phenomenon”. It is found that the latter is favored due to a much larger (by ∼ 380 \sim 380 MeV) K τ K_{\tau} for Pb isotopes than for Sn isotopes, while the K ∞ K_{\infty} from analyzing the K A K_{\rm A} data of Sn isotopes is only about 5 MeV less than that from analyzing the Pb data.

While it is well known that the

In some earlier studies, see, e.g., Ref.

;

;

;

;

Thus, regardless of whatever criticisms people may have for using the Eq. (

Indeed, shell and pairing effects are not considered in the Eq. (

The well-known problems mentioned above about the reliability of the leptodermus coefficients extracted from the

In this work, we propose a differential approach to extract exactly the values of
K_{\infty}
and
K_{\tau}
independently from the
K_{\rm A}
data of two nuclei in any isotopic chain. The nucleus-nucleus pair having the largest difference in their isospin asymmetries is found to give the most accurate

values simultaneously. Effects of varying the

parameters by

K_{\infty}
are compatible with those from using the state-of-the-art microscopic theories in the “consensus” approach, while the accuracy of the extracted
K_{\tau}

II
The differential approach

Applying the Eq.(
1
) to any two isospin asymmetric (
\delta\neq 0
) nuclei of mass and charge
(A_{1},Z_{1})
and
(A_{2},Z_{2})
separately, the
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
can be expressed exactly as

\displaystyle K_{\tau}=\left[\frac{K_{\rm A_{1}}}{S_{1}}-\frac{K_{\rm A_{2}}}{S_{2}}-K_{\rm{Cou}}\left(\frac{Z_{1}^{2}A_{1}^{-4/3}}{S_{1}}-\frac{Z_{2}^{2}A_{2}^{-4/3}}{S_{2}}\right)\right]\bigg/\left(\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{S_{1}}-\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}}{S_{2}}\right),

(2)

\displaystyle K_{\infty}=\left[\frac{K_{\rm A_{1}}}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-\frac{K_{\rm A_{2}}}{\delta_{2}^{2}}-K_{\rm{Cou}}\left(\frac{Z_{1}^{2}A_{1}^{-4/3}}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-\frac{Z_{2}^{2}A_{2}^{-4/3}}{\delta_{2}^{2}}\right)\right]\bigg/\left(\frac{S_{1}}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-\frac{S_{2}}{\delta_{2}^{2}}\right)

(3)

where
S_{i}=1+cA_{i}^{-1/3}
for the nucleus-i with i=1 or 2. One can understand intuitively the physical meanings of the above expressions by using the mathematical definitions of
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
based on Eq. (1). Namely, neglecting the Coulomb correction,
K_{\tau}\equiv\left(\partial K_{\rm A}/\partial\delta^{2}\right)_{\rm S}\approx\Delta(K_{\rm A}/S)/\Delta(\delta^{2}/S)=(\frac{K_{\rm A_{1}}}{S_{1}}-\frac{K_{\rm A_{2}}}{S_{2}})/(\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{S_{1}}-\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}}{S_{2}})
gives the leading term of
K_{\tau}
in Eq. (2). It is simply the changing rate of
K_{\rm A}
with respect to
\delta^{2}
evaluated by using the ratio of their finite changes.
Similarly,
K_{\infty}\equiv\left(\partial K_{\rm A}/\partial S\right)_{\delta}\approx\Delta(K_{\rm A}/\delta^{2})/\Delta(S/\delta^{2})=(\frac{K_{\rm A_{1}}}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-\frac{K_{\rm A_{2}}}{\delta_{2}^{2}})/(\frac{S_{1}}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-\frac{S_{2}}{\delta_{2}^{2}})
gives the leading term of
K_{\infty}
in Eq. (3).

We notice that while the
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
are determined independently by the
K_{\rm A}
data themselves of any two nuclei used, they satisfy the constraint given by Eq. (
1
). Therefore, there is an intrinsic correlation between
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
when they are varied using the
K_{\rm A}
data of many different nucleus-nucleus pairs in a given isotopic chain as we shall demonstrate.

The corresponding uncertainties of
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
can be calculated
exactly according to the rules of error propagation using the experimental errors of
K_{\rm A}
data, i.e.,
\sigma_{K_{\rm A_{1}}}
and
\sigma_{K_{\rm A_{2}}}
in the nucleus-1 and nucleus-2 considered.
Nevertheless, to see analytically what nucleus-nucleus pairs may give the most accurate
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
values, we notice that for heavy nuclei in the same isotope chain,
S_{1}\approx S_{2}\approx 1
, the error bars are reduced to

\displaystyle\sigma_{K_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sigma_{K_{\rm A_{1}}}^{2}+\sigma_{K_{\rm A_{2}}}^{2}}\bigg/\bigg|\delta_{1}^{2}-\delta_{2}^{2}\bigg|,

(4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{K_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\delta_{2}^{2}\cdot\sigma_{K_{\rm A_{1}}})^{2}+(\delta_{1}^{2}\cdot\sigma_{K_{\rm A_{2}}})^{2}}\bigg/\bigg|\delta_{1}^{2}-\delta_{2}^{2}\bigg|.

(5)

They both are
inversely
proportional to
|\delta_{1}^{2}-\delta_{2}^{2}|
, thus nuclear pairs having the largest difference in their isospin asymmetries will give the most accurate
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
values simultaneously. Moreover,
because of the weighting of
\sigma_{K_{A}}
by
\delta^{2}\ll 1
in evaluating the
\sigma_{K_{\infty}}
, the
K_{\infty}
can be more precisely evaluated than the
K_{\tau}
, explaining the relatively larger errors of the extracted
K_{\tau}
values.

While in principle the above formalisms can be applied to any two nuclei, we shall restrict their applications to nuclei in the same isotopic chain. This will reduce not only effects of systematic experimental errors as what is being
used is the
difference
in
K_{\rm A}
scaled by either the surface factor S or isospin asymmetry
\delta
of the two nuclei in the same isotopic chain, but also effects of the higher-order terms neglected in expanding the
K_{\rm A}
in Eq. (
1
). This is also one of the reasons why the differential approach can more precisely extract both the
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
compared to typical integral approaches normally used in the literature.

In cases where one of the nuclei is isospin-symmetric, say
\delta_{1}=0
, its
K_{\rm A}
alone can be used to evaluate the
K_{\infty}
according to
K_{\infty}=K_{\rm A_{1}}/S_{1}-K_{\rm{Cou}}Z_{1}^{2}A_{1}^{-4/3}/S_{1}
while the
K_{\tau}
can be evaluated from the Eq. (2) by choosing the nucleus-2 as neutron-rich as possible to get the most accurate result, indicating the importance of using exotic heavy isotopes. As noticed already in the literature, see, e.g., Ref.
Shlomo
, the leptodermous expansion in Eq. (1) itself may not be a good approximation for light nuclei, the differential approach is thus expected to work better for more heavy nuclei.

\chi^{2}

K_{\rm A}
data or Bayesian analysis of all
K_{\rm A}
data available thus can not reliably extract the value of
K_{\tau}
from its small contribution relative to
K_{\infty}
to the
K_{\rm A}
. In turn, the uncertainty of extracting the
K_{\infty}
can not be better than
K_{\tau}\delta^{2}/K_{\infty}
in the integral analyses of the
K_{\rm A}
data. On the contrary, the differential approach decouples completely the extractions of
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
for each isotope pair used. Only the
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
extracted independently for different isotope pairs along an isotope chain show an expected intrinsic correlation within their respective error bars.

IV
Effects of the surface and Coulomb parameters

We have used above the known most probable values of
c=-1.2

Pat02
and
K_{\rm Cou}=-5.2
MeV
Sag07
. It is also known that the Coulomb parameter is rather model independent
Sag07
;
Cou2
while the calculations
Blaizot
;
JPB2
;
Tre
;
JPB3
;
Myer1
;
Myer2
of the surface parameter
c
show somewhat larger variations around
c\sim-1
. It is generally accepted that both the
c
and
K_{\rm Cou}
parameters have less than about
(10-20)\%
uncertainties
Garg18
;
Stone
. How do theses uncertainties affect the accuracies of extracting the
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
in the differential analyses? To answer this question, we have carried out systematic calculations by varying the two parameters independently by
\pm 20\%
around their most probable values.

As an example, shown in Fig.
3
are the variations of the
K_{\tau}
(lower windows) and
K_{\infty}
(upper windows) due to the variation of the surface parameter
c
(left windows) and Coulomb parameter
K_{\rm Cou}
(right windows) for the Sn isotopes. Qualitatively, effects of varying the
K_{\rm Cou}
and especially the parameter
c
are much smaller on
K_{\tau}
than on
K_{\infty}
. Quantitatively, for the
112
Sn-
124
Sn pair, changing the
c
parameter by 40% from
-1.2\times 0.8
to
-1.2\times 1.2
makes the
K_{\tau}
change by about 6% from
-624\pm 84
MeV to
-663\pm 87
MeV, while the
K_{\infty}
changes by about 13% from
205\pm 29
MeV to
236\pm 34
MeV, respectively.
On the other hand, by changing the
K_{\rm Cou}
by 40% from
-5.2\times 0.8
to
-5.2\times 1.2
, the
K_{\tau}
change by about 8% from
-616\pm 87
MeV to
-669\pm 87
MeV, while the
K_{\infty}
changes by about
6% from
213\pm 31
MeV to
227\pm 31
MeV. Thus, the (6-8)% uncertainty of
K_{\tau}
due to the
\pm 20\%
uncertainty of the surface parameter is much smaller than the approximately 14% uncertainty due to the experimental errors of
K_{\rm A}
. While the (8-13)% uncertainty of
K_{\infty}
due to the
\pm 20\%
uncertainty in the Coulomb parameter is compatible with that due to the experimental errors of the
K_{\rm A}
data. Thus, the remaining uncertainties of the surface and Coulomb parameters of about (10-20)% have essentially no effect on the extraction of
K_{\tau}
.

The observed dependences of
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
on the variations of the surface and Coulomb parameters can be understood analytically by further examining the expressions of
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
in Eq. (2) and Eq. (3), respectively. Firstly, we examine effects of the parameter
c
. The c-dependent part of
K_{\tau}
is

K_{\tau}\propto\frac{(1+cA_{2}^{-1/3})K_{\rm A_{1}}-(1+cA_{1}^{-1/3})K_{\rm A_{2}}}{(1+cA_{2}^{-1/3})\delta_{1}^{2}-(1+cA_{1}^{-1/3})\delta_{2}^{2}}.

(7)

Because the parameter
c
appears in all terms, its effect largely cancels out. In particular, for heavy nuclei
c/A^{1/3}\approx 0
, the
K_{\tau}
then becomes independent of
c
, i.e.,

K_{\tau}\rightarrow\frac{K_{\rm A_{1}}-K_{\rm A_{2}}}{\delta_{1}^{2}-\delta_{2}^{2}}.

(8)

While the c-dependent part of
K_{\infty}
is

K_{\infty}\propto\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}K_{\rm A_{1}}-\delta_{1}^{2}K_{\rm A_{2}}}{c\cdot(\delta_{2}^{2}A_{1}^{-1/3}-\delta_{1}^{2}A_{2}^{-1/3})+\delta_{2}^{2}-\delta_{1}^{2}}.

(9)

The parameter
c
only appears in the first term of the denominator. Since both terms in the denominator are very small, a very small change in the parameter
c
can thus lead to a large change in the value of
K_{\infty}
. This also implies that the surface properties of different nuclei may affect significantly the extraction of
K_{\infty}
from the
K_{\rm A}
data as already noticed in the
\chi^{2}
analyses in Ref.
Stone
.

Similar analyses can be done to understand effects of the Coulomb parameter
K_{\rm Cou}
. More specifically,

K_{\tau}\propto-K_{\rm Cou}Z^{2}\frac{(1+cA_{2}^{-1/3})A_{1}^{-4/3}-(1+cA_{1}^{-1/3})A_{2}^{-4/3}}{(1+cA_{2}^{-1/3})\delta_{1}^{2}-(1+cA_{1}^{-1/3})\delta_{2}^{2}}.

(10)

Again, the parameter
c
has little effect as it appears in all terms. Considering
cA^{-1/3}\approx 0
for heavy nuclei, the above expression reduces to

K_{\tau}\rightarrow-K_{\rm Cou}Z^{2}\times\frac{A_{1}^{-4/3}-A_{2}^{-4/3}}{\delta_{1}^{2}-\delta_{2}^{2}}.

(11)

As heavy nuclei are more neutron rich in a given chain of isotopes, i.e., for
A_{2}>A_{1}
,
\delta_{2}>\delta_{1}
, the fraction in the above equation is always negative. Thus, one obtains
K_{\tau}\propto K_{\rm Cou}
. While the
K_{\rm Cou}
itself is negative,
thus a larger negative
K_{\rm Cou}
decreases the value of
K_{\tau}
as seen in our numerical calculations. As the overall contribution of the Coulomb term to the
K_{\tau}
is small, its variation causes little change in the final
K_{\tau}
value. While for the Coulomb effect on
K_{\infty}
, a similar analysis leads to

K_{\infty}\propto-K_{\rm Cou}\frac{Z^{2}}{A_{1}^{4/3}A_{2}^{4/3}}\times\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}A_{2}^{4/3}-\delta_{1}^{2}A_{1}^{4/3}}{\delta_{2}^{2}-\delta_{1}^{2}}.

(12)

Since the last fraction is always positive, thus
K_{\tau}\propto-K_{\rm Cou}
. Therefore, a larger negative
K_{\rm Cou}
increases the value of
K_{\infty}
. Moreover, the above analysis clearly explains why the Coulomb parameter has opposite effects on extracting the
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
values.

V
Summary and conclusions

In summary, we emphasize the following aspects of our work

•

To our best knowledge, the differential approach we proposed here is original. The analytical expressions for
K_{\infty}
and
K_{\tau}
are derived from solving exactly two linear equations for two unknowns using the
K_{\rm A}
data as the only input. While the approach is very simple, its physics is sound. It also has no dependence on any nuclear many-body theories nor interactions. There is absolutely no fitting procedure involved, thus it does not suffer from the well-known problems in fitting the
K_{\rm A}
data using the Eq. (
1
).

•

The
K_{\infty}
and
K_{\tau}
are directly extracted from the experimental
K_{\rm A}
data of any two nuclei in a given isotope chain.
Besides showing that the new approach gives a
K_{\infty}
consistent with its fiducial value from the “consensus” approach, and a
K_{\tau}
that is much more accurate than what is available in the literature, we also ask for the first time the question which isotope pairs are most useful for extracting especially the
K_{\tau}
at rare isotope beam facilities. Our answer to this question is expected to be useful for future experiments using rare isotopes to study the equation of state of neutron-rich matter.

•

The so-called “Soft Sn puzzle” (when one uses the interactions that correctly reproduce the GMR strength in
208
Pb to calculate the GMR strengths in the Sn and Cd isotopes within the “consensus” approach, the experimental values are always overestimated) has been alive for over 10 years. However, people have not really understood the underlying cause of the puzzle, leading to the conclusion that it is not feasible to simultaneously reproduce both the
208
Pb and Sn’s GMR data by the same interaction
Jorge09
using the state-of-the-art theories within the “consensus” approach. While we did not solve this puzzle in this work, we showed for the first time that the
K_{\infty}
from analyzing the GMR data of Pb, Sn, Gd isotopes are not much different within the experimental error bars to indicate strongly a “Soft Sn Puzzle”. Quantitatively, the
K_{\infty}
from Sn isotopes is only about 5 MeV smaller than that from the Pb isotopes. On the other hand, the
K_{\tau}
from analyzing the
204,206,208
Pb data is significantly higher (by
\sim 380
MeV) than the converged asymptotic
K_{\tau}
value at large isospin separations in analyzing the Sn and Gd isotopes, indicating strongly a “Stiff Pb Phenomenon”
Ehan
. To verify this further, differential analyses of future GMR data of more Pb isotopes will be very useful. The suggestion of having more Pb data was also made for addressing the same puzzle from a different perspective in Ref.
Ehan
.

•

There are several caveats in our work. Firstly, if the leptodermous expansion of Eq. (
1
) is perfect, one expects the
K_{\infty}
and
K_{\tau}
extracted from all pairs of nuclei to be identical. In reality, this is of course not the case. As mentioned earlier, we expect the differential approach to work better for heavy nuclei along the same isotope chains. Indeed, the
K_{\tau}
from most isotopes fall approximately on the same line at large isospin separations within still relatively large error bars, while the
K_{\infty}
from different isotope chains especially the light nuclei scatter more broadly due to mostly the remaining (approximately
\pm 20\%
) uncertainty of the surface parameter
\mathrm{c}
. Secondly, to avoid introducing any model dependence in presenting their
K_{\rm A}
data, the experimentalists translated their original GMR observables to the “experimental”
K_{\rm A}
data by using the experimentally measured charge radii instead of the matter radii which are inherently model dependent. This probably introduced a systematic error in the “experimental”
K_{\rm A}
data and its effects have not been evaluated yet. We used the “experimental”
K_{\rm A}
data as in all previous analyses in the literature. Thus, all results presented here should be understood within the context and with the cautions discussed above. Nevertheless, the importance and new physics revealed in our work can be clearly seen from comparing our approach and results with the traditional ones in the literature. We also emphasize that the focus of this work is a more accurate determination of
K_{\tau}
for finite nuclei, while the
K_{\infty}
for infinite nuclear matter just came out naturally consistent with its fiducial value that has not changed much since 1980.

In conclusion, we proposed a differential approach to analyze the incompressibilities of neutron-rich nuclei and investigated which nuclear pairs give the most accurate results using both the
K_{\rm A}
data and
analytically. The nucleus-nucleus pair having the largest difference in their isospin asymmetries in a given isotope chain is found to give the most accurate values of both
K_{\tau}
and
K_{\infty}
simultaneously. Applying this new approach to the
K_{\rm A}
data from RCNP, we found that the
106
Cd-
116
Cd and
112
Sn-
124
Sn pairs give consistently the most accurate up-to-date
K_{\tau}
values of
-616\pm 59
MeV and
-623\pm 86
MeV, respectively, largely independent of the remaining uncertainties of the surface and Coulomb parameters. These results can exclude many predictions based on various microscopic and/or phenomenological nuclear many-body theories in the literature. We also studied the “Stiff Pb Phenomenon” versus the “Soft Sn Puzzle” and found that the former is favored. Thus, compared to the integral approach widely used in the literature, the differential analysis can reveal some interesting new physics underlying the incompressibilities of finite nuclei.

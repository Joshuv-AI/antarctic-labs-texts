1
Introduction

A set
{\cal A}
of nonnegative integers is called a
B_{h}
-set if every solution to

(1)

a_{1}+\dots+a_{h}=b_{1}+\dots+b_{h},\quad a_{i},b_{i}\in{\cal A}

has
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{h}\}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{h}\}
(as multisets).

These sets (with
h=2
) first arose in harmonic analysis
[
12
]
as a tool to create trigonometric polynomials with peculiar properties. Prompted by Sidon’s work, Erdős defined
B_{h}
-sets
[
14
]
and they have become a central tool and nexus of problems in combinatorial number theory, beginning with the seminal work of Erdős & Turán in 1941
[
3
]
. They were indepedently discovered by Babcock
[
1
]
as a means to avoid third order intermodulation of frequencies (
B_{2}
-sets) and fifth-order intermodulation (
B_{3}
-sets). Golomb rediscovered
B_{2}
-sets as a means to represent graphs (the vertices are a
B_{2}
-set, and an edge between
a
and
b
is uniquely coded as
|a-b|
), and some of his questions were written up by Martin Gardner in Scientific American
[
4
]
. See
[
10
]
for an extensive bibliography.

The primary problem of interest is to give a finite
B_{h}
-set with many elements compared to its diameter (for
{\cal A}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\diam({\cal A})\coloneq 1+\max{\cal A}-\min{\cal A}
). A natural method to construct a
B_{h}
-set with small diameter is to simply be greedy. We set
\gamma_{0}=0,\gamma_{1}=1
, and thereafter set
\gamma_{k+1}(h)
to be the smallest positive integer
x
with the property that
\{\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k}(h),x\}
is a
B_{h}
-set. The infinite set
\{\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}(h),\dots\}
is the
greedy

B_{h}
-set; it is the lexicographically first infinite
B_{h}
-set.

\gamma_{k}(h)
(OEIS

). The
h=1

and
k=1

and
k=3

, and a formula for

The greedy
B_{2}
-set was first considered in the literature in 1944
[
7
]
, and in the years since there has been no progress on understanding greedy
B_{h}
-sets other than the computation of more terms. The purpose of the present work is to give the first nontrivial upper bounds on
\gamma_{k}(h)
for
k\geq 5
, and a non-trivial lower bound on
\gamma_{5}(h)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha_{5}\coloneqq 0.467214,\alpha_{6}\coloneqq 0.382978,\alpha_{7}\coloneqq 0.269877
, and for
k\geq 7
set

\alpha_{k+1}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\binom{k}{j}2^{j}=\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(1-k,-k;1;2).

Then, for all
k\geq 5
,

\gamma_{k}(h)\leq\alpha_{k}h^{k-1}+O_{k}(h^{k-2}).

Theorem 1.2
.

The fifth positive element of the greedy
B_{h}
-set satisfies

\displaystyle\gamma_{5}(h)\geq\tfrac{1}{8}h^{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}h^{3}
.

A skeptical reader (or referee) might ask whether bounding, for example, the tenth positive element of the greedy
B_{100}
-set is an interesting problem. First,
B_{h}
-sets are natural objects of study, and deserve answers for aesthetic reasons. Second, non-greedy methods of constructing
B_{h}
-sets require working in large finite fields, and working in
\mathbb{F}_{11}^{101}
(to get an
11
-element
B_{100}
-set using Singer’s construction
[
13
,
11
]
) is not plausible, while finding a formula in terms of
h
for the
10
-th positive element is hard but plausible. Finally, the actual motivation for this project was a desire to better understand the greedy algorithm for
B_{2}
-sets (which have physical real-world applications), and greedy algorithms for additive problems in general.

A similar phenomenon to that discussed here is seen in Ulam sets
[
6
]
. The Ulam set
U_{h}
is the lexicographically least set of positive integers after
\{1,h\}
and with the property that each element can be written as a sum of two other elements in a unique way. While
U_{2}
is famously mysterious, polynomial patterns emerge when looking at the way
U_{h}
starts as a function of
h
.

Cilleruelo
[
2
]
considered a greedy algorithm for
B_{h}[g]
-sets (in a
B_{h}
-set, the
h
-fold sums don’t repeat, while in a
B_{h}[g]
-set the
h
-fold sums do not repeat more than
g

g
that helps the greedy process proceed more smoothly.

2
A Tour of the Greedy
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
-sets

In this section, we repeat results that are either folklore or already in the literature. Only Lemma
2.4
is used in this work, but we suspect the reader will appreciate a summary of known bounds with indications of their derivations.

We start with relatively straightforward combinatorial bounds on the size of a
B_{h}
-set. These are often referenced in the
B_{h}
-set literature, but only special cases have been made explicit. The proof of Lemma
2.1
is simply the observation that the
\binom{k+h}{k}
distinct possibilities for
h
-fold sums must all lie in the interval
[ha_{0},ha_{k}]
, and for a
B_{h}
-set they must all be distinct.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\cal A}\coloneqq\{a_{0}<a_{1}<\dots<a_{k}\}
be a
B_{h}
-set. Then

a_{k}\geq a_{0}+\frac{1}{h}\left[\binom{k+h}{k}-1\right].

Given a finite
B_{h}
-set, there are only finitely many integers you can union into it that will
not
give a
B_{h}
-set. Quantifying this for general
h,k
hasn’t appeared in the literature.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\cal A}\coloneqq\{a_{0}<a_{1}<\dots<a_{k}\}
be a
B_{h}
-set. There are at most

\sum_{r=1}^{h-1}\sum_{M=1}^{\infty}\binom{k}{M}\binom{k-M+h-r}{h-r}\left[\binom{h}{M}-\binom{r}{M}\right]

integers
f>a_{k}
for which
{\cal A}\cup\{f\}
is not a
B_{h}
-set. They are all in the interval
[ha_{0},ha_{k}]
.

Proof.

As the
B_{h}
property is translation invariant, it is without loss of generality that we assume
a_{0}=0
.
There must be some equation

x_{1}+\dots+x_{h}=y_{1}+\dots+y_{h},\quad x_{i},y_{i}\in{\cal A}

involving
f
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
X\coloneqq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{h}\}
and
Y\coloneqq\{y_{1},\dots,y_{h}\}
are disjoint, and that
f\in Y
with multiplicity
r
. Letting
m_{i},n_{i}
be the mulitplicity of
a_{i}
in
X,Y
, respectively, we have

(2)

f=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(m_{i}-n_{i})a_{i},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{k}m_{i}=h,\quad\sum_{i=0}^{k}n_{i}=h-r,\quad m_{1}n_{1}=\cdots=m_{k}n_{k}=0.

Since we are only concerned with
f>a_{k}
, we further know that
r<\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}
.
Let
M
be the number of
m_{1},\dots,m_{k}
that are nonzero, and
N
the number of
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
that are nonzero.
For a fixed
r,m_{0},M,N
, there are

\binom{k}{M}\binom{k-M}{N}\binom{h-m_{0}-1}{N}\binom{h-r}{N}

possible
m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{k},n_{0},n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
that satisfy the conditions on Line (
2
) (and
r<\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}
), by nested stars-and-bars style counting. Summing over
0\leq n\leq\min\{h-r,k-M\}
,
1\leq M\leq\min\{k,h-m_{0}\}
,
0\leq m_{0}\leq h-r-1
, and
1\leq r\leq h-1
and simplifying completes the proof of the first sentence of this lemma. That all of these “forbidden”
f
are in
[0,ha_{k}]
is immediate from Line (
2
).
∎

This proof doesn’t take full consideration of the requirement that
f>a_{k}
, nor that
f
is an integer. Consequently, there is room to improve, but we are skeptical that this bound can be improved by more than a factor of
1/2
without a substantial new idea.

We turn now to consider the greedy
B_{h}
-sets specifically. Lemma
2.1
immediately gives the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k,h
be positive integers. Then

(3)

\gamma_{k}(h)\geq\frac{1}{h}\left[\binom{k+h}{k}-1\right].

Consequently,for fixed
k
we have
\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(h)\geq\frac{1}{k!}\,h^{k-1}+O_{k}(h^{k-2})
and for fixed
h
we have
\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(h)\geq\frac{1}{h\cdot h!}k^{h}+O_{h}(k^{h-1})
.

The lower bound for fixed
h
has been improved (see
[
5
]
for improvements for general
h
) for all
B_{h}
-sets, not only the greedy sets.
The last sentence of Lemma
2.2
implies that
\gamma_{k+1}(h)\leq h\cdot\gamma_{k}(h)+1
, and from this it follows that the lower bound in Lemma
2.3
for fixed
k
has the correct order of growth.

We can set particular values for
h
in Lemma
2.2
and get explicit bounds:

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}k^{3}+\frac{1}{2}k

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{12}k^{5}+\frac{5}{12}k^{4}+\frac{3}{4}k^{3}+\frac{13}{12}k^{2}+\frac{2}{3}k

and so on. In general, one finds that

\gamma_{k}(h)\leq\frac{1}{h!(h-1)!}k^{2h-1}+O_{h}(k^{2h-2}).

This author is not aware of any improvement to this bound.

The following formulas for
\gamma_{k}(h)
for
k\leq 4
are proved in
[
9
,
8
]
. We give proofs of these in Appendix

in the spirit of the lower bound we give for
\gamma_{5}(h)
in Theorem

.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have explicit formulas for
\gamma_{k}(h)
for
k\leq 4
:

\gamma_{0}=0
,
\gamma_{1}=1
,
\gamma_{2}(h)=h+1
,
\gamma_{3}(h)=h^{2}+h+1
, and
\gamma_{4}(h)=\left\lfloor\frac{h+3}{2}\right\rfloor h^{2}+\left\lfloor\frac{3h+2}{2}\right\rfloor

3
The Greedy
{\mathbf{B}}_{\mathbf{h}}
-set

For the remainder of this work, we write
\gamma_{k}
instead of
\gamma_{k}(h)
.

We set
\gamma_{0}=0
, and inductively set
\gamma_{k+1}
to the smallest integer greater than
\gamma_{k}
such that
\{\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k},\gamma_{k+1}\}
is a
B_{h}
-set. We call
{\cal G}(h)\coloneqq\{\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots\}
be the
greedy
B_{h}
-set
. It is the lexicographically first infinite
B_{h}
-set of natural numbers.

We define

(4)

\displaystyle F_{r}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\cdot(m_{i}-m^{\prime}_{i}):m_{i},m^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}\leq h,\sum_{i=1}^{k}m^{\prime}_{i}\leq h-r\right\},

(5)

\displaystyle F^{(k)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigcup_{r=1}^{h}F_{r}.

We draw the reader’s attention to the omission of
\gamma_{0}
from the definition of
F_{r}^{(k)}
, and that the sum of multiplicities need not be exactly
h
, rather, the sum of multiplicities is at most
h
. Moreover, in the definition of
F_{r}
we will often assume (without loss of generality) that at least one of
m_{i},m^{\prime}_{i}
is
0
for each
i
.

The
\mex
of a set is the smallest nonnegative integer that is excluded from the set.

Lemma 3.1
.

The
(k+1)
-st positive element of the greedy
B_{h}
-set is
\displaystyle\gamma_{k+1}=\mex F^{(k)}
.

Proof.

If
\gamma_{k}<x<\mex F
, then
x=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\cdot(m_{i}-m^{\prime}_{i})
as in (
4
). Whence, as
\gamma_{0}=0
, we have

x\cdot r+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}m^{\prime}_{i}+\gamma_{0}\cdot(h-r-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m^{\prime}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}m_{i}+\gamma_{0}\cdot(h-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}),

proving that
\{\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{k}\}\cup\{x\}
is not a
B_{h}
-set. On the other hand, let
x=\mex F
and suppose that
\{\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{k}\}\cup\{x\}
is not a
B_{h}
-set, so that there is a solution to

xm_{k+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\gamma_{i}m_{i}=xm^{\prime}_{k+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\gamma_{i}m^{\prime}_{i}

with
\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}m_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}m^{\prime}_{i}=h
.
As (inductively)
\{\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{k}\}
is a
B_{h}
-set, it must be that at least one of
m_{k+1},m^{\prime}_{k+1}
is positive, say
m^{\prime}_{k+1}\geq m_{k+1}\geq 0
. If
m^{\prime}_{k+1}=m_{k+1}
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}m_{i}+\gamma_{0}\cdot(m_{0}+m_{k+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}m^{\prime}_{i}+\gamma_{0}\cdot(m^{\prime}_{0}+m^{\prime}_{k+1}),

contradicting that
\{\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{k}\}
is a
B_{h}
-set. Ergo, we may set
r\coloneqq m^{\prime}_{k+1}-m_{k+1}>0
. We have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}m_{i}=xr+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}m^{\prime}_{i},

which we can rearrange to

x=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\cdot(m_{i}-m^{\prime}_{i}),

with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}=h-m_{0}-m_{k+1}\leq h
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}m^{\prime}_{i}=h-(m^{\prime}_{0}+m^{\prime}_{k+1})\leq h-r
.
∎

A Lower Bound on
\mathbf{\gamma}_{\mathbf{5}}

We now proceed to prove that
\gamma_{5}(h)\geq\frac{1}{8}h^{4}+\frac{1}{2}h^{3}
. We assume that
h\geq 5
, as the inequality holds by direct computation for
1\leq h\leq 4
. The “Iverson Bracket”
\left[\!\!\left[\,P\,\right]\!\!\right]
is
1
if
P
is true, and is
0
if
P
is false.
We define functions
\ell_{i},u_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 4
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{|rl|rl|}\hline\cr\ell_{4}&\coloneqq 1&u_{4}&\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{h+3}{4}\right\rfloor\\
\ell_{3}(d_{4})&\displaystyle\coloneqq d_{4}-\left\lfloor\frac{h+1}{2}\right\rfloor&u_{3}(d_{4})&\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{h+1}{2}\right\rfloor-d_{4}\\
\ell_{2}(d_{4},d_{3})&\coloneqq d_{4}+|d_{3}|-h&u_{2}(d_{4},d_{3})&\coloneqq h-d_{4}-|d_{3}|\\
\ell_{1}(d_{4},d_{3},d_{2})&\coloneqq\displaystyle 1-h-\sum_{i=1}^{4}d_{i}\left[\!\!\left[\,d_{i}\leq 0\,\right]\!\!\right]&u_{1}(d_{4},d_{3},d_{2})&\displaystyle\coloneqq h-\sum_{i=1}^{4}d_{i}\left[\!\!\left[\,d_{i}\geq 0\,\right]\!\!\right]\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

and now set

\Delta\coloneqq\bigg\{\left\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}\right\rangle:d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{4}d_{i}\left[\!\!\left[\,d_{i}\geq 0\,\right]\!\!\right]\leq h,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{4}d_{i}\left[\!\!\left[\,d_{i}<0\,\right]\!\!\right]\geq 1-h,\\
\ell_{4}\leq d_{4}\leq u_{4},\quad\ell_{3}(d_{4})\leq d_{3}\leq u_{3}(d_{4}),\\
\ell_{2}(d_{4},d_{3})\leq d_{2}\leq u_{2}(d_{4},d_{3}),\quad\ell_{1}(d_{4},d_{3},d_{2})\leq d_{1}\leq u_{1}(d_{4},d_{3},d_{2})\bigg\}.

The author is unreasonably proud of this definition, in large part because of the unreasonable amount of time, effort, and computation he spent finding it. It is not dictated by the argument below, and may not be ideal.

For an element
\delta\in\Delta
, its
image
is the integer
\delta\cdot\left\langle\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}\right\rangle
. Clearly the images of elements of
\Delta
are in
F_{1}
(defined on line (
4
)), and so
\gamma_{5}
is at least the
\mex
of the images of
\Delta
.

We order distinct vectors of the same length by setting
\mu<\nu
if either
last(\mu)<last(\nu)
or both
last(\mu)=last(\nu)
and
drop(\mu,-1)<drop(\nu,-1)
. Otherwise,
\mu\geq\nu
. That is, we order vectors lexicographically considering first their last components. On an odometer, the units digit climbs from its minimum to its maximum, and then resets to its minimum at the same moment the tens digit increases by one. If both the ones and tens digit are at their maximum, then the hundreds digits increments when the ones and tens digits reset to their minimums. Our ordering on
\Delta
is in this spirit, with the least significant digits listed first.

We note that both

An upper bound on
{\mathbf{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{k}}

In this section we determine a nonincreasing sequence of real numbers
\alpha_{k}
with

\gamma_{k+1}(h)\leq\alpha_{k+1}h^{k}+O(h^{k-1})

and
\alpha_{k}\to 0
. We will inductively use the value of
\alpha_{k}
to give
\alpha_{k+1}
. We take
\alpha_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}=1
,
\alpha_{3}=1
,
\alpha_{4}=\frac{1}{2}
, in accordance with our formulas for
\gamma_{k}
,
0\leq k\leq 4
, and we assume henceforth that

and are working to bound
\gamma_{k+1}
.

Fix a real number
\beta_{k}
with
\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}\leq\beta_{k}\leq\alpha_{k}
. We will use

\beta_{4}\coloneqq 0.406671,\quad\beta_{5}\coloneqq 0.308672,\quad\beta_{6}\coloneqq 0.203975,\quad\beta_{k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k},\quad(k\geq 7).

The engine of our bound (recall the definitions of
F_{r}^{(k)},F_{r}
on lines (
4

5
)) is that

\displaystyle\gamma_{k+1}=\mex F^{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\beta_{k}h^{k}+\left|F^{(k)}\cap(\beta_{k}h^{k},\infty)\right|

(6)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\beta_{k}h^{k}+\sum_{r=1}^{h}\big|F_{r}^{(k)}\cap(\beta_{k}h^{k},\infty)\big|.

For
r\geq 3
, (and sufficiently large

, a hypothesis we use repeatedly in this section)

\max F_{r}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{r}\cdot h\gamma_{k}\leq\frac{1}{3}\cdot h\cdot\left(\alpha_{k}h^{k-1}+O(h^{k-2})\right)<\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}h^{k}\leq\beta_{k}h^{k}.

Thus, for

we know that
|F_{r}^{(k)}\cap(\beta_{k}h^{k},\infty)|=0
. For

, we arrive at

\max F_{2}^{(k)}\leq\beta_{k}h^{k}+O(h^{k-1}),

whence
|F_{2}^{(k)}\cap(\beta_{k}h^{k},\infty)|=O(h^{k-1})
.
The bound on line (

(7)

\gamma_{k+1}\leq\beta_{k}h^{k}+O(h^{k-1})+|F_{1}^{(k)}\cap(\beta_{k}h^{k},\infty)|.

We now stratify
F_{1}^{(k)}
as

m_{k},m^{\prime}_{k}
is
0
, and

F_{1}(m_{k},m^{\prime}_{k})\coloneqq(m_{k}-m^{\prime}_{k})\gamma_{k}+\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(m_{i}-m^{\prime}_{i})\gamma_{i}:\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}m_{i}\leq h-m_{k},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}m^{\prime}_{i}\leq h-1-m^{\prime}_{k},\quad m_{1}m^{\prime}_{1}=\cdots=m_{k-1}m^{\prime}_{k-1}=0\bigg\}.

The largest element of

\max F_{1}(0,m^{\prime}_{k})=-m^{\prime}_{k}\gamma_{k}+h\gamma_{k-1}\leq h\cdot\big(\alpha_{k-1}h^{k-2}+O(h^{k-3})\big)\\
=\alpha_{k-1}h^{k-1}+O(h^{k-2})<\beta_{k}h^{k},

(for sufficiently large

.
We may thus assume without loss that
m_{k}>0
and
m^{\prime}_{k}=0
. The largest element of

is

\max F_{1}(m_{k},0)=m_{k}\gamma_{k}+(h-m_{k})\gamma_{k-1}\leq\alpha_{k}\frac{m_{k}}{h}h^{k}+O(h^{k-1}).

If
m_{k}<h{\beta_{k}}/{\alpha_{k}}
, then
\max F_{1}(m_{k},0)
is less than
\beta_{k}h^{k}
(as always in this section, for large

). Ergo, we may assume that

(recall our assumption that

Equation (
7

(8)

\gamma_{k+1}\leq\beta_{k}h^{k}+O(h^{k-1})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{k}\\
\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}h\leq m\leq h\end{subarray}}|F_{1}(m_{k},0)|.

Notice that we have dropped the intersection with the interval
(\beta_{k}h^{k},\infty)
from our concerns. That might hurt the bound, but simplicity is its own reward.

We have

|F_{1}(m_{k},0)|\leq\bigg|\bigg\{\big((m_{1},\dots,m_{k-1}),(m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{k-1})\big):\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}m_{i}\leq h-m_{k},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}m^{\prime}_{i}\leq h-1,\quad m_{1}m^{\prime}_{1}=\cdots=m_{k-1}m^{\prime}_{k-1}=0\bigg\}\bigg|.

Suppose that exactly
j
of
m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{k-1}
are nonzero. By stars-and-bars (
h-1-j
stars and
j
bars), there are
\binom{k-1}{j}\binom{h-1}{h-1-j}
such tuples. For each such tuple, there are

stars and
(k-1-j)
bars in the count of tuples
(m_{1},\dots,m_{k-1})
; that is,
\binom{h-m_{k}+(k-1-j)}{h-m_{k}}
valid tuples
(m_{1},\dots,m_{k-1})
. Altogether, then

|F_{1}(m_{k},0)|=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\binom{h-1}{h-1-j}\binom{h-m_{k}+(k-1-j)}{h-m_{k}}.

Clearly,

\binom{h-1}{h-1-j}\leq\frac{1}{j!}h^{j}

and since

also

\displaystyle\binom{h-m_{k}+(k-1-j)}{h-m_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(h-m_{k}+k-1-j)^{k-1-j}}{(k-1-j)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1-j)!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1-j}\binom{k-1-j}{\ell}(h-m_{k})^{k-1-j-\ell}(k-1-j)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1-j)!}(h-m_{k})^{k-1-j}+O((h-m_{k})^{k-2-j})

\displaystyle=h^{k-1-j}\frac{(1-m_{k}/h)^{k-1-j}}{(k-1-j)!}+O(h^{k-2-j}).

Thus,

\displaystyle|F_{1}(m_{k},0)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\frac{h^{j}}{j!}\left(\frac{h^{k-1-j}(1-m_{k}/h)^{k-1-j}}{(k-1-j)!}+O(h^{k-2-j})\right)

\displaystyle=h^{k-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\frac{(1-m_{k}/h)^{k-1-j}}{j!(k-1-j)!}\right)+O(h^{k-2}).

Since

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{k}\\
\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}h\leq m\leq h\end{subarray}}(1-m_{k}/h)^{k-1-j}=h\int_{\beta_{k}/\alpha_{k}}^{1}(1-x)^{k-1-j}\,dx+O(1)=h\frac{\left(1-\beta_{k}/\alpha_{k}\right)^{k-j}}{k-j}+O(1),

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{k}\\
\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}h\leq m\leq h\end{subarray}}|F_{1}(m_{k},0)|

\displaystyle=h^{k}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\frac{(1-\beta_{k}/\alpha_{k})^{k-j}}{j!(k-j)!}\right)+O(h^{k-1})

Line (
8

\gamma_{k+1}\leq\alpha_{k+1}h^{k}+O(h^{k-1})
provided that

(9)

\beta_{k}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\frac{(1-\beta_{k}/\alpha_{k})^{k-j}}{j!(k-j)!}\right)\leq\alpha_{k+1}.

Recall our earlier assumption that
\beta_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}

We proceed by choosing
\beta_{k}
to minimize this expression, giving us the smallest possible value of

\beta_{4}\coloneqq 0.406671,\quad\beta_{5}\coloneqq 0.308672,\quad\beta_{6}\coloneqq 0.203975,\quad\beta_{k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k},\quad(k\geq 7).

We use
\beta_{k}
and
\alpha_{k}

\alpha_{k+1}

\begin{array}[]{rcc}k&\beta_{k}&\alpha_{k}\\
\hline\cr 1&&1\\

Now set

A.2
A Formula for
\mathbf{\gamma_{3}}

By Lemma
3.1
,

\gamma_{3}=\mex\bigg\{\frac{1}{r}\big((m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1})+(h+1)(m_{2}-m^{\prime}_{2})\big):1\leq r\leq h,\quad m_{1},m^{\prime}_{1},m_{2},m^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\\

A.3
A Formula for
\mathbf{\gamma_{4}}

The formula for
\gamma_{4}
, proved in
[

]
,is more involved, and the proof requires substantially better organization. The argument given here is not substantively different from that of

, although the exposition is quite different.

The “Iverson Bracket”
\left[\!\!\left[\,P\,\right]\!\!\right]
is
1
if
P
is true, and is
0
if
P
is false. Set

\Delta_{r}\coloneqq\left\{\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle:d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{3}d_{i}\left[\!\!\left[\,d_{i}>0\,\right]\!\!\right]\leq h,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{3}(-d_{i})\left[\!\!\left[\,d_{i}<0\,\right]\!\!\right]\leq h-r\right\}.

Then
\gamma_{4}
is smallest nonnegative integer that is not equal to

\frac{1}{r}\,\delta\cdot\left\langle\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\right\rangle

for any
\delta\in\Delta_{r}
for any

.

Define

\Delta\coloneqq\bigg\{\left\langle m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1},m_{2}-m^{\prime}_{2},m_{3}\right\rangle:m_{i},m^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}\leq h,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{2}m^{\prime}_{i}\leq h-1,\\

which is clearly a subset of
\Delta_{1}
. The cleverness in this proof, if there is any, is in the choice of the conditions
m^{\prime}_{2}+m_{3}\leq h,m_{3}\leq\frac{h+1}{2}
, which we don’t explain but are used below.

The values for
m_{3}
that appear in
\Delta
are
[0,\lfloor{(h+1)/2}\rfloor]
. For each value of
m_{3}
, the quantity
m_{2}-m^{\prime}_{2}
varies from
-(h-m_{3})
up to
h-m_{3}
. For each pair of values
m_{2}-m^{\prime}_{2},m_{3}
, the quantity
m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1}
varies through an interval (which depends on whether
m_{2}-m^{\prime}_{2}
is nonnegative or negative).

We order the elements of
\Delta
as follows. We define
\delta=\left\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\right\rangle<\delta^{\prime}=\left\langle d^{\prime}_{1},d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{3}\right\rangle
if there is a

with
d_{3-i}=d^{\prime}_{3-i}
for

and
d_{3-j}<d^{\prime}_{3-j}
. In this ordering, we define the
odometer
of
\Delta
to be the numbering
\delta_{0}\coloneqq\left\langle 0,0,0\right\rangle<\delta_{1}<\dots
of all elements of
\Delta
that are at least
\delta_{0}
.
Let
\vec{\gamma}\coloneqq\left\langle\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\right\rangle=\left\langle 1,h+1,h^{2}+h+1\right\rangle
. The sequence
\delta_{0}\cdot\vec{\gamma},\delta_{1}\cdot\vec{\gamma},\delta_{2}\cdot\vec{\gamma},\dots
tends to increase, but need not do so monotonically. We call
\delta\cdot\vec{\gamma}
the

\delta
.

In
\Delta
, the last component
d_{3}
is never negative. Consecutive elements of the odometer have either first coordinate going up by
1
, second coordinate going up by
1
, or third coordinate going up by
1
(just like a car odometer, which always has some digit going up by 1, while less significant digits drop to their minimum possible values).
Consecutive elements of the odometer have one of four shapes:

(
i
)

\left\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\right\rangle<\left\langle d_{1}+1,d_{2},d_{3}\right\rangle
;

(

)

d_{2}<0
and
\left\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\right\rangle=\left\langle h-m_{3},-m^{\prime}_{2},m_{3}\right\rangle<\left\langle-h+m^{\prime}_{2},-m^{\prime}_{2}+1,m_{3}\right\rangle
;

(
iii
)

d_{2}\geq 0
and
\left\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\right\rangle=\left\langle h-m_{2}-m_{3},m_{2},m_{3}\right\rangle<\left\langle-(h-1),m_{2}+1,m_{3}\right\rangle
;

(

)

\left\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\right\rangle=\left\langle 0,h-m_{3},m_{3}\right\rangle<\left\langle-(h-m_{3}-1),-m_{3},m_{3}+1\right\rangle
.

The restriction
m_{3}\leq(h+1)/2
in
\Delta
prevents a fifth shape.

In case
(i)
, the image goes up by
1
.

In case
(ii)
, the image increases by

\big(\left\langle-h+m^{\prime}_{2},-m^{\prime}_{2}+1,m_{3}\right\rangle-\left\langle h-m_{3},-m^{\prime}_{2},m_{3}\right\rangle\big)\cdot\left\langle 1,h+1,\gamma_{3}\right\rangle=-h+m^{\prime}_{2}+m_{3}+1\leq 1,

where we have used the condition in the definition of
\Delta

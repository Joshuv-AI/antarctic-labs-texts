Preliminaries

It is well known that, for
\nu\in M_{0}(\mathbb{C})
, in the sense of distribution,

\displaystyle\ \bar{\partial}\mathcal{C}(\nu)=-\pi\nu.

(2.1)

The following is a simple relationship of
\mathcal{L}^{2}
,
\alpha
, and
\gamma

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{L}^{2}(E)\leq 4\pi\alpha(E)^{2},~\alpha(E)\leq\gamma(E)

(2.2)

where
E
is a bounded measurable subset (see Theorem 3.2 on page 204 of
[
G69
]
).
The maximal Cauchy transform is defined by

\ \mathcal{C}_{*}(\nu)(z)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}|\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}(\nu)(z)|.

A related capacity,
\gamma_{+},
is defined for subsets
E
of
\mathbb{C}
by:

\ \gamma_{+}(E)=\sup\|\eta\|,

where the supremum is taken over
\eta\in M_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{C})
with
\text{spt}(\eta)\subset E
for which
\|\mathcal{C}(\eta)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})}\leq 1.

Since
\mathcal{C}\eta
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\mbox{\text{spt}}(\eta)
and
|(\mathcal{C}(\eta)^{\prime}(\infty)|=\|\eta\|
,
we have:

\gamma_{+}(E)\leq\gamma(E)

for all subsets
E
of
\mathbb{C}
.
The capacity
\alpha_{+}
of a bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
is defined as

\ \alpha_{+}(E)=\sup\|\eta\|,

where the supremum is taken over
\eta\in M_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{C})
with
\text{spt}(\eta)\subset E
such that
\mathcal{C}(\eta)
is continuous on
\mathbb{C}
and
|\mathcal{C}(\eta)(z)|\leq 1
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. We
clearly have

\alpha_{+}(E)\leq\alpha(E)
,
because
|\mathcal{C}(\eta)^{\prime}(\infty)|=\|\eta\|
.

X. Tolsa has established the following astounding results.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Tolsa’s Theorem)

(1)
\gamma_{+}
and
\gamma
are equivalent.
\alpha_{+}
and
\alpha
are equivalent.
That is, there is an absolute positive constant
C_{T}
such that

\displaystyle\ \gamma(E)\leq C_{T}\gamma_{+}(E),~\alpha(E)\leq C_{T}\alpha_{+}(E).

(2.3)

(2) Semiadditivity of analytic capacity:

\displaystyle\ \gamma\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\right)\leq C_{T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(E_{i}),~\alpha\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\right)\leq C_{T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha(E_{i}).

(2.4)

(3) There is an absolute positive constant
C_{T}
such that, for any
a>0
, we have:

\ \gamma(\{\mathcal{C}_{*}(\nu)\geq a\})\leq\dfrac{C_{T}}{a}\|\nu\|.

For (1) and (2), see
[
To03
]
(also see Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.3 in
[
To14
]
) and
[
To04
]
.
(3) follows from Proposition 2.1 of
[
To02
]
(also see
[
To14
]
Proposition 4.16).

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose that
\nu,\nu_{j}\in M_{0}(\mathbb{C})
for
j\geq 1
. The following statements are true.

(1) There exists
Z\subset\mathbb{C}
with
\gamma(Z)=0
such that
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}(\nu)(z)
exists for
z\in Z^{c}
.

(2) For
\epsilon>0
, there exists a Borel subset
F
such that
\gamma(F^{c})<\epsilon
and for

\ \sup_{z\in F}\mathcal{C}_{*}(\nu_{j})(z)<\infty,~j\geq 1.

Proof.

(1) follows from combination Theorem 4.14 and Theorem 8.1 in
[
To14
]
with Theorem
2.1
(1) (also see
[
To98
]
).

(2) is an application of Theorem
2.1
(2) and (3). In fact, let
A_{j}=\{\mathcal{C}_{*}(\nu_{j})(z)\leq M_{j}\}
. By Theorem
2.1
(3), we can select

so that
\gamma(A_{j}^{c})<\frac{\epsilon}{2^{j+1}C_{T}}
. Set

\ \gamma(F^{c})\leq C_{T}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(A_{j}^{c})<\epsilon.

3.

From now on, we assume
K\subset\mathbb{C}
is a compact subset and
U\subset K
is an open subset.
For
\delta>0,
set
B(\lambda,\delta)=\{z:|z-\lambda|<\delta\}
.
For a compactly supported smooth function
\varphi
, the localization operator

T_{\varphi}
is defined by

\displaystyle\ (T_{\varphi}f)(\lambda)=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int\dfrac{f(z)-f(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\bar{\partial}\varphi(z)d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z),

(3.1)

where
f\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
. It is well known that
T_{\varphi}R(K)\subset R(K)
and
T_{\varphi}A(K,U)\subset A(K,U)
. For a continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty},
let

\ \omega(f,\delta)=\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in B(\lambda,\delta),\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})|.

The following
norm estimation is well known for a continuous function
f
:

\displaystyle\ \|T_{\varphi}f\|\leq 8\omega(f,\delta)\delta\|\bar{\partial}\varphi\|

(3.2)

for a smooth function
\varphi

(4.1)

Lemma 4.1

Let
\mathcal{M}

Proof.

Let

be a smooth function with compact support.
We have the following calculation

\ \begin{aligned} \ &\int T_{\varphi}\mathcal{C}\eta(z)d\nu(z)\\
\ =&\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int\int\dfrac{\mathcal{C}\eta(z)-\mathcal{C}\eta(w)}{z-w}\bar{\partial}\varphi(w)d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)d\nu(z)\\
\ =&\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int(\mathcal{C}((\mathcal{C}\eta)\nu)(w)-\mathcal{C}\eta(w)\mathcal{C}\nu(w))\bar{\partial}\varphi(w)d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\\
\ =&0.\ \end{aligned}

The lemma is proved.
∎

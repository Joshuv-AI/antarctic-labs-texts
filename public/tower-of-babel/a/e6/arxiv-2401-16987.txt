The higher order chain rule in Sobolev spaces
Gérard Bourdaud

Abstract
We establish the Faà di Bruno formula, in the sense of distributions and almost everywhere, for derivatives of the composed function f ∘ g f\circ g , for all function f : ℝ → ℝ f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}} such that f f acts on W p m ​ ( ℝ n ) W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}) by composition, and all g ∈ W p m ​ ( ℝ n ) g\in W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}) .

Introduction

If
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is sufficiently regular and
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, we denote by
g^{(\alpha)}
the partial derivative

\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}g}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}\,,

where
|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}
. In all the paper
{\mathbb{N}}
denotes the set of natural numbers, including
0
.
If moreover
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is sufficiently regular, we set :

U_{\gamma,s}(f,g):=(f^{(s)}\circ g)\,g^{(\gamma_{1})}\ldots g^{(\gamma_{s})}\,,

for all integer
s>0
, and all

\gamma:=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{s})\,,\quad\gamma_{r}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\,,r=1,\dots,s\,.

(1)

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Let us recall the classical Faà di Bruno’s formula :

(f\circ g)^{(\alpha)}=\sum c_{\alpha,s,\gamma}U_{\gamma,s}(f,g)\,,

(2)

where the sum runs over all parameters
s,\gamma
s.t. (
1
) and

s=1,\ldots,|\alpha|\,,\quad\sum_{r=1}^{s}\gamma_{r}=\alpha\,,

and the
c_{\alpha,s,\gamma}
’s are some combinatorial constants.
This formula holds true as soon as the functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
are differentiable up to order
|\alpha|
. We are asking ourselves if it remains true for functions
g
in the Sobolev space
W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, if the function
f
satisfies minimal regularity assumptions.

In all the paper
p
denotes a real number
\geq 1
. We denote by
c
a positive constant depending only on
m,n,p
; its value may change from place to place. If
E,F
are function spaces, the notation
E\hookrightarrow F
means that
E\subseteq F
and that the natural inclusion mapping
E\rightarrow F
is continuous. All functions are assumed to be real valued. We use the following function spaces :

•

{\mathcal{M}}
the space of all the Lebesgue measurable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
;
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
the subspace of all functions
f
s.t.
f(x)=0
a.e. ;
{\mathbb{M}}
the factor space

. Let

be the quotient mapping; if
f\in{\mathcal{M}}
, then

is called the
class
of
f
; if

, any

s.t.

of

•

L_{1,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset{\mathbb{M}}
the space of all the classes of locally integrable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

•

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the space of tempered distributions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

•

C^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the set of all continuously differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, up to order
m
, and

the set of all smooth functions on

•

the set of functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
, with bounded continuous derivatives, up to order

.

•

•

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(resp.
\dot{W}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
) the inhomogeneous (resp. homogeneous) Sobolev space of distributions
f
s.t.
f^{(\alpha)}\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for all
|\alpha|\leq m
(resp.
|\alpha|=m
) endowed with its natural norm (resp. seminorm) :

\|f\|_{W^{m}_{p}}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|f^{(\alpha)}\|_{p}\quad(\mathrm{resp.}\quad\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{m}_{p}}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\|f^{(\alpha)}\|_{p}\,)\,.

Let us consider the composition operator
N_{f}(g):=f\circ g
. Thirty years ago, we have characterized the functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
s.t.

N_{f}(W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\subseteq W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, see
[
2
]
and
[

By using formula (
2

\|f\circ g\|_{W^{m}_{p}}\leq C(f)\,\left(1+\|g\|_{W^{m}_{p}}\right)^{m}\,,

(3)

Then a standard approximation argument (see

2.3
Main theorem on composition

Here is the characterization of composition operators in
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, see
[
3
, thm. 1]
and
[
4
, prop. 3 and thm. 2]
:

Theorem 1
.

Let
m
be a natural number
\geq 2
, and
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
.

•

If
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
, then the operator
N_{f}
sends
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to itself and the estimate

\|f\circ g\|_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}\leq c\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}\left(1+\|g\|_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}\right)^{m}

(4)

holds true for all
g\in\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

•

Assume moreover that
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is not embedded into
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. If
N_{f}
sends
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to itself, then
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
.

Recall that

\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is not embedded into
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, iff

m\not=n\quad\mathrm{or}\quad p>1\,,

(5)

see
[
4
, prop. 3]
.
Proposition
1
has an useful corollary :

Proposition 3
.

Let
m
be a natural number
\geq 1
. Let
f
be s.t.
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
. Then there exists a sequence
(f_{k})
of smooth functions s.t.

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|f^{\prime}_{k}-f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}=0

and
f_{k}\rightarrow f
uniformly on each bounded subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
1
, there exists a sequence
(u_{k})
of smooth functions s.t.

\varepsilon_{k}:=\|u^{\prime}_{k}-f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}

tends to
0
. By Proposition
2
, we have

\|u^{\prime}_{k}-f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq c\varepsilon_{k}
. If we define
f_{k}(x):=u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(0)+f(0)
, we obtain

|f_{k}(x)-f(x)|\leq c|x|\varepsilon_{k}
, for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.∎

3
Main result

First of all, we need an unambiguous definition of

U_{\gamma,s}(f,g)\in{\mathbb{M}}
if
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
and
g\in\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be any measurable function. If
v,w_{r}\in{\mathcal{M}}
are respective representatives of
g
and
g^{(\gamma_{r})}
,
r=1,\ldots s
, we define

\mathcal{U}_{\gamma,s}(u,v,w_{1},\ldots,w_{s})(x):=u(v(x))\,w_{1}(x)\cdots w_{s}(x)\,.

Clearly, the class of
\mathcal{U}_{\gamma,s}(u,v,w_{1},\ldots,w_{s})
does not depend on the choice of the specific representatives
v,w_{r}
,
r=1,\ldots s
; it depends only on
u
and
g
; we denote it by
\mathbb{U}_{\gamma,s}(u,g)
.

In case
s<m
, by the normalization convention and by Proposition
2
, the function
f^{(s)}
is continuous. There is no need to choose another representative than
f^{(s)}
itself.
Then we set
U_{\gamma,s}(f,g):=\mathbb{U}_{\gamma,s}(f^{(s)},g)
. That works as well for
s=m
if
f\in C^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Definition 1
.

If

\mathbb{U}_{\gamma,m}(u_{1},g)=\mathbb{U}_{\gamma,m}(u_{2},g)
for any representatives
u_{1},u_{2}
of
f^{(m)}
,
we say that
U_{\gamma,m}(f,g)
is well defined, and we set

U_{\gamma,m}(f,g):=\mathbb{U}_{\gamma,m}(u,g)
for any representative
u
of
f^{(m)}
.

Our main theorem is the following :

Theorem 2
.

Let
m
be a natural number
\geq 2
. Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a function s.t.
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
.
Then, for all function
g\in\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

•

U_{\gamma,s}(f,g)
is well defined and satisfies

\|U_{\gamma,s}(f,g)\|_{pm/\ell}\leq c\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}\left(1+\|g\|_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}\right)^{m},

(6)

for all
s=1,\ldots,m
,
\gamma
s.t. (
1
) and

\ell:=\sum_{r=1}^{s}|\gamma_{r}|\leq m\,.

(7)

•

The higher order chain rule (
2
) holds true in the sense of distributions, and almost everywhere, for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
s.t.
0<|\alpha|\leq m
.

3.1
Proof of the estimate (
6
) in case
s<m

This part of the proof is classical. For the sake of completeness, we outline the argument.

We exploit the Gagliardo-Nirenberg embedding :

\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{\ell}_{pm/\ell}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\,,\quad\ell=1,\ldots,m\,,

(8)

together with the estimate

\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{\ell}_{pm/\ell}}\leq c\,\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{1}_{pm}}^{\frac{m-\ell}{m-1}}\,\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{m}_{p}}^{\frac{\ell-1}{m-1}}\,.

(9)

According to Proposition
2
,
f^{(s)}\in C^{0}_{b}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\|f^{(s)}\|_{\infty}\leq c\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}
. Then
f^{(s)}\circ g
is a bounded measurable function s.t.

\|f^{(s)}\circ g\|_{\infty}\leq c\|f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}
.
By Hölder inequality, and by (
7
), we obtain

\|g^{(\gamma_{1})}\cdots g^{(\gamma_{s})}\|_{pm/\ell}\leq\prod_{r=1}^{s}\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{|\gamma_{r}|}_{pm/|\gamma_{r}|}}\,.

By (
9
),
the right hand side of the above inequality is estimated by

\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s}\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{1}_{pm}}^{\frac{m-|\gamma_{r}|}{m-1}}\right)\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s}\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{m}_{p}}^{\frac{|\gamma_{r}|-1}{m-1}}\right)\,.

By summing up the exponents, we obtain

\|g^{(\gamma_{1})}\cdots g^{(\gamma_{s})}\|_{pm/\ell}\leq c\,\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{1}_{pm}}^{\frac{sm-\ell}{m-1}}\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{m}_{p}}^{\frac{\ell-s}{m-1}}\leq c\,\|g\|^{s}_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}\,.

That implies the estimate (
6
).

3.2
Proof of the estimate (
6
), and the well-definiteness of
U_{\gamma,m}

In that case, there exist integers

j_{r}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
r=1,\ldots,m
, s.t.

U_{\gamma,m}(f,g)=(f^{(m)}\circ g)\,\partial_{j_{1}}g\cdots\partial_{j_{m}}g
for smooth functions
f,g
.

3.2.1
Distinguished representatives

Let us define

A({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
as the set of measurable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which are locally absolutely continuous on almost every line parallel to any coordinate axis.
The pertinency of the class
A({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in the framework of Sobolev spaces relies upon the following classical result, due to Gagliardo, Morrey and Calkin (see
[
6
]
,
[
7
, Lem. 1.5]
,
[
8
, 1.1.3, thm. 1]
):

Proposition 4
.

All
g\in W^{1}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, admit a representative
v\in A({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
s.t. the usual gradient of
v
is a representative of the distributional gradient of
g
.

In the remaining of Section
3.2
, we consider a fixed
g\in\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Since
g\in\dot{A}^{1}_{mp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then
g
belongs locally to
W^{1}_{mp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. We fix a representative
v
of
g
satisfying the conditions of
Proposition
4
, that we call
a
distinguished representative
.

3.2.2
The case of a smooth function
f

We follow the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 1]
, with
the same notation. Without loss of generality, we replace
U_{\gamma,m}(f,g)
by

S_{D}(g):=(f^{(m)}\circ g)\,(Dg)^{m}\,,

where
D
is any first order differential operator with constant coefficients.
Then

\|S_{D}(g)\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{j=1,2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(F_{j}\circ g)\,T_{j}(g)\,{\rm d}x\,,

(10)

where
F_{1},F_{2}
are bounded continuous functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
s.t.
\|F_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq c\,\|f^{\prime}\|^{p}_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}
, and

T_{1}(g):=(mp-1)\,(D^{2}g)\,|Dg|^{mp-2}\,,\quad T_{2}(g):=|Dg|^{mp}\,.

Notice that the proof of (
10
) makes use of the distinguished representative of
g
.
Then
\|T_{2}(g)\|_{1}\leq c\,\|g\|^{mp}_{\dot{W}^{1}_{mp}}

and, by Hölder and Gagliardo-Nirenberg,

\|T_{1}(g)\|_{1}\leq c\,\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{1}_{pm}}^{mp-2}\,\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{2}_{pm/2}}\leq c_{1}\,\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{1}_{pm}}^{mp-\frac{m}{m-1}}\,\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{m}_{p}}^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\leq c_{1}\|g\|^{mp-1}_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}\,.

We obtain

\|S_{D}(g)\|_{p}\leq c\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}\,\left(\|g\|^{m-(1/p)}_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}+\|g\|^{m}_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}\right)\,.

That implies the estimate (
6
).

3.2.3
The general case

By Section
3.2.2
,
the operator
f\mapsto U_{\gamma,m}(f,g)
can be uniquely extended by density to the whole set of functions
f
s.t.
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
. Let us define
V_{\gamma,m}(f,g)\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
as the limit of the sequence

(U_{\gamma,m}(f_{k},g))
, for any
sequence
(f_{k})
of smooth functions approximating
f
in the sense of Proposition
3
. It holds

\|V_{\gamma,m}(f,g)\|_{p}\leq c\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}}(1+\|g\|_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}})^{m}\,.

(11)

Let us consider any representative
u
of
f^{(m)}
. Let
h
be a representative of
V_{\gamma,m}(f,g)
.
We claim that

h(x)=\mathcal{U}_{\gamma,m}(u,v,\partial_{j_{1}}v,\ldots,\partial_{j_{m}}v)(x)\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\,.

(12)

For convenience, we assume that
j_{1}=1
. By assumption on
v
, there exists a set
N
of measure
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
s.t., for every
x^{\prime}\notin N
, the function
t\mapsto v(t,x^{\prime})
is locally absolutely continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
(f_{k})
be a sequence of smooth functions approximating
f
in the sense of Proposition
3
. By taking a subsequence, if necessary, we have

f_{k}^{(m)}(t)\rightarrow u(t)\quad\mathrm{for\,almost\,every}\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,

\mathcal{U}_{\gamma,m}(f_{k}^{(m)},v,\partial_{j_{1}}v,\ldots,\partial_{j_{m}}v)(x)\rightarrow h(x)\quad\mathrm{for\,almost\,every}\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,.

(13)

Let
M
be the set of
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
s.t.
f_{k}^{(m)}(t)
does not converges to
u(t)
. Then
M
is a set of measure
0
. Applying de la Vallée-Poussin’s lemma (
[
10
]
and
[
7
, lem. 1.1]
), we deduce that the set

B_{x^{\prime}}:=\{\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,v(t,x^{\prime})\in M\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\partial_{1}v(t,x^{\prime})\not=0\,\}

is of measure
0
, for any
x^{\prime}\notin N
. Let us define :

C:=\{(t,x^{\prime})\,|\,t\notin B_{x^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{and}\,x^{\prime}\notin N\}\,.

Then
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus C
is a set of measure
0
and
f_{k}^{(m)}(v(t,x^{\prime})))\,\partial_{1}v(t,x^{\prime})\rightarrow u(v(t,x^{\prime}))\,\partial_{1}v(t,x^{\prime})
for all
(t,x^{\prime})\in C
. By using the property (
13
), we may conclude that the property (
12
) is satisfied. We conclude that

V_{\gamma,m}(f,g)={\mathbb{U}}_{\gamma,m}(u,g)\,.

It follows that

{\mathbb{U}}_{\gamma,m}(u,g)
does not depend on the specific representative
u
.
Hence
U_{\gamma,m}(f,g)
is well defined, and
U_{\gamma,m}(f,g)=V_{\gamma,m}(f,g)
. The estimate (
6
) follows by (
11
).

3.3
Proof of the chain rule

3.3.1
A preparation

The function space
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is endowed with a seminorm s.t.
\|g\|=0
iff
g
is a constant function.
We have the following statement, of immediate proof :

Proposition 5
.

Let
g
be a function in
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfying the following property : the chain rule (
2
) holds true for all
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
and all
0<|\alpha|\leq m
. Then, for all number

, the function

satisfies the same property.

Let
E
be a subspace of
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
s.t.

\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=E\oplus\mathcal{P}_{0}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{0}
denotes the set of constant functions. Consider the associated projection mapping
R:\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{S^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
s.t.
R(g)
is the unique element of
E

s.t.
g-R(g)\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. If
R
is a continuous mapping from
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, endowed with its semi-norm,
to
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, endowed with the
\ast
-weak topology,

is called a
realization
of
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
; if endowed with the
norm

\|.\|_{\dot{A}^{m}_{p}}
,
E
turns out to be a Banach space, continuously embedded into
L_{p,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. We refer to
[
4
, 4.1]
for details. Till the end of Section

,

will denote a realization of
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proposition 6
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2
, the mapping

g\mapsto U_{\gamma,s}(f,g)

is continuous from
E
to
L_{pm/\ell}
.

Proof.

Step 1 : case
s<m
.
As observed in Section
3.1
, the function
f^{(s)}
is bounded and continuous, and the mapping
g\mapsto g^{(\gamma_{1})}\cdots g^{(\gamma_{s})}
is continuous from
E
to
L_{pm/\ell}
.
Then the wished property follows by
[
4
, prop. 9]
.

Step 2 : case
s=m
.
The continuity of
g\mapsto U_{\gamma,m}(f,g)
is proved in
[
4
, 5.1.2]
. ∎

3.3.2
The case of a smooth function
f

Let
g\in E
. Let us consider a sequence
(g_{k})
a smooth functions s.t.
g_{k}\rightarrow g
in
E
. Then

(f\circ g_{k})^{(\alpha)}=\sum c_{\alpha,s,\gamma}U_{\gamma,s}(f,g_{k})

holds true in the usual sense. A standard argument shows that

(f\circ g_{k})^{(\alpha)}\rightarrow(f\circ g)^{(\alpha)}

in the sense of distributions, see e.g.
[
3
, step 2, p. 6111]
. By Proposition
6
,

U_{\gamma,s}(f,g_{k})
tends to
U_{\gamma,s}(f,g)
in
L_{pm/|\alpha|}
, hence in the sense of distributions.
Then the chain rule holds true in the sense of distributions. By Theorem
1
, and embedding (
8
), we know that
(f\circ g)^{(\alpha)}
belongs to
L_{pm/|\alpha|}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. We conclude that the chain rule holds true a.e..

3.3.3
The general case

Let
g\in E
. We use again a sequence
(f_{k})
given by Proposition
3
. By Section
3.3.2
, we have

(f_{k}\circ g)^{(\alpha)}=\sum c_{\alpha,s,\gamma}U_{\gamma,s}(f_{k},g)\,,

a.e., for any

. By the estimate (
6
),
U_{\gamma,s}(f_{k},g)
tends to

in

, it follows easily that

in

4
Related results

Now we state two corollaries of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
m
be a natural number
\geq 2
. Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a function s.t.
N_{f}
sends
\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to itself. Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
be s.t.
0<|\alpha|\leq m
. Then the formula (
2
) holds true in the sense of distributions and almost everywhere, for all
g\in\dot{A}^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proof.

If the condition (
5
) holds, Theorem
1
says us that
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
; then the chain rule follows by Theorem
2
.

Assume now that
N_{f}
sends
\dot{A}^{n}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to itself. We know that
\dot{A}^{n}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a subspace of
C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, see
[
4
, prop. 3]
. According to
[
4
, thm. 3]
, it holds

f\in W^{n}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})_{loc}
. Let
g\in\dot{A}^{n}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. We may consider a smooth compactly supported function
\psi
s.t.
\psi(x)=1
on the range of
g
. Then
f\circ g=(f\psi)\circ g
and
(f\psi)^{\prime}\in W^{n-1}_{E_{1}}
. By applying Theorem
2
to the function

f\psi
, we obtain the formula (
2
).∎

Theorem
3
has a counterpart for usual inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces. Here we consider the inhomogeneous
Adams-Frazier space
A^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=(W^{m}_{p}\cap\dot{W}^{1}_{mp})({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, endowed with its natural norm.

Theorem 4
.

Let
m
be a natural number
\geq 2
. Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a function s.t.
N_{f}
sends
A^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(resp.
W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
) to itself. Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
be s.t.

. Then the formula (

) holds true in the sense of distributions and almost everywhere, for all

(resp.
W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

Proof.

Step 1 : the case of
A^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Substep 1.1.
If

m>n/p
, or
m=n
and

, then

A^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
According to
[
4

, it holds
f\in W^{m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}})_{loc}
. Then we conclude the proof, exactly as that of Theorem
3
.

Substep 1.2.
If

m\leq n/p
, and
m\not=n
or
p>1
, then we have
f^{\prime}\in W^{m-1}_{E_{p}}
,
see
[
4
, thm. 2]

2
.

Step 2 : the case of

.

Substep 2.1.
If
m\geq n/p
, then

, and we are reduced to Step 1.

Substep 2.2.
If

1+\frac{1}{p}<m<\frac{n}{p}\,,

it holds

for some constant
c
, see
[
5
]
, then the chain rule holds trivially.

Substep 2.3.
If
n=1,2

Substep 2.4.
Let us turn to the case

. According to
[
2
, thm. 3]
, it holds
f^{\prime\prime}\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})

Arguing so as in Section

\|(f^{\prime\prime}\circ g)\,(\partial_{j}g)(\partial_{k}g)\|_{1}\leq\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{1}\|g\|_{W^{2}_{1}}

Neutrino Oscillation Constraints on U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} Models: from Non-Standard Interactions to Long-Range Forces Preprint: IFT-UAM/CSIC-20-133, YITP-SB-2020-30
Pilar Coloma, M. C. Gonzalez-Garcia, Michele Maltoni

Abstract
We quantify the effect of gauge bosons from a weakly coupled lepton flavor dependent U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} interaction on the matter background in the evolution of solar, atmospheric, reactor and long-baseline accelerator neutrinos in the global analysis of oscillation data. The analysis is performed for interaction lengths ranging from the Sun-Earth distance to effective contact neutrino interactions. We survey ∼ 10000 \sim 10000 set of models characterized by the six relevant fermion U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} charges and find that in all cases, constraints on the coupling and mass of the Z ′ Z^{\prime} can be derived. We also find that about 5% of the U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} model charges lead to a viable LMA-D solution but this is only possible in the contact interaction limit. We explicitly quantify the constraints for a variety of models including U ​ ( 1 ) B − 3 ​ L e U(1)_{B-3L_{e}} , U ​ ( 1 ) B − 3 ​ L μ U(1)_{B-3L_{\mu}} , U ​ ( 1 ) B − 3 ​ L τ U(1)_{B-3L_{\tau}} , U ​ ( 1 ) B − 3 2 ​ ( L μ + L τ ) U(1)_{B-\frac{3}{2}(L_{\mu}+L_{\tau})} , U ​ ( 1 ) L e − L μ U(1)_{L_{e}-L_{\mu}} , U ​ ( 1 ) L e − L τ U(1)_{L_{e}-L_{\tau}} , U ​ ( 1 ) L e − 1 2 ​ ( L μ + L τ ) U(1)_{L_{e}-\frac{1}{2}(L_{\mu}+L_{\tau})} . We compare the constraints imposed by our oscillation analysis with the strongest bounds from fifth force searches, violation of equivalence principle as well as bounds from scattering experiments and white dwarf cooling. Our results show that generically, the oscillation analysis improves over the existing bounds from gravity tests for Z ′ Z^{\prime} lighter than ∼ 10 − 11 \sim 10^{-11} eV. In the contact interaction limit, we find that for most models listed above there are values of g ′ g^{\prime} and M Z ′ M_{Z^{\prime}} for which the oscillation analysis provides constraints beyond those imposed by laboratory experiments. Finally we illustrate the range of Z ′ Z^{\prime} and couplings leading to a viable LMA-D solution for two sets of models.



1
Introduction

Experiments measuring the flavor composition of neutrinos produced in
the Sun, in the Earth’s atmosphere, in nuclear reactors and in
particle accelerators have established that lepton flavor is not
conserved in neutrino propagation, but it oscillates with a wavelength
which depends on distance and energy. This demonstrates beyond doubt
that neutrinos are massive and that the mass states are non-trivial
admixtures of flavor states
Pontecorvo:1967fh
;
Gribov:1968kq
,
see Ref.
GonzalezGarcia:2007ib
for an overview.

When traveling through matter, the flavor evolution of the neutrino
ensemble is affected by the difference in the effective potential
induced by elastic forward scattering of neutrino with matter, the
so-called Mikheev-Smirnov-Wolfenstein (MSW)
mechanism
Wolfenstein:1977ue
;
Mikheev:1986gs
. Within the
context of the Standard Model (SM) of particle interactions, this
effect is fully determined and leads to a matter potential which, for
neutral matter, is proportional to the number density of electrons at
the neutrino position,
V=\sqrt{2}G_{F}N_{e}(r)
, and which only affects
electron neutrinos. New flavor dependent interactions can modify the
matter potential and consequently alter the pattern of flavor
transitions, thus leaving imprints in the oscillation data involving
neutrinos which have traveled through large regions of matter, as is
the case for solar and atmospheric neutrinos.

Forward elastic scattering takes place in the limit of zero momentum
transfer, so as long as the range of the interaction is shorter than
the scale over which the matter density extends, the effective matter
potential can be obtained in the contact interaction approximation
between the neutrinos and the matter particles. The paradigmatic
example is provided by neutral current non-standard interactions
(NSI)
Wolfenstein:1977ue
;
Valle:1987gv
;
Guzzo:1991hi
between
neutrinos and matter (for recent reviews, see
Ohlsson:2012kf
;
Miranda:2015dra
;
Farzan:2017xzy
;
Dev:2019anc
), which can be
parametrized as

\mathcal{L}_{\text{NSI}}=-2\sqrt{2}G_{F}\sum_{f,P,\alpha,\beta}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,P}(\bar{\nu}_{\alpha}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{\beta})(\bar{f}\gamma_{\mu}Pf)\,,

(1)

where
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant,
\alpha,\beta
are flavor indices,

P\equiv P_{L},P_{R}
and
f
is a SM charged fermion. These operators
are expected to arise generically from the exchange of some mediator
state heavy enough for the contact interaction approximation to hold.
In this notation,
\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,P}
parametrizes the strength
of the new interaction with respect to the Fermi constant,

\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,P}\sim\mathcal{O}(G_{X}/G_{F})
. Generically they
modify the matter potential in neutrino propagation, but – being
local interactions – the resulting potential is still proportional to
the number density of particles in the medium at the neutrino
position. Since such modifications arise from a coherent effect,
oscillation bounds apply even to NSI induced by ultra light mediators,
as long as their interaction length is shorter than the neutrino
oscillation length. For the experiments considered here, such
condition is fulfilled as long as
M_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 10^{-12}
eV
GonzalezGarcia:2006vp
.

Conversely, if the mediator is too light then the contact interaction
approximation is no longer valid, and the flavor dependent forces
between neutrino and matter particles become long-range. In this case
neutrino propagation can still be described in terms of a matter
potential, which however is no longer simply determined by the number
density of particles in the medium at the neutrino position, but it
depends instead on the average of the matter density within a radius

\sim 1/M_{Z^{\prime}}
around it
Grifols:2003gy
;
Joshipura:2003jh
;
GonzalezGarcia:2006vp
;
Davoudiasl:2011sz
;
Wise:2018rnb
.

At present, the global analysis of data from oscillation experiments
provides some of the strongest constraints on the size of the NSI
affecting neutrino propagation
GonzalezGarcia:2011my
;
Gonzalez-Garcia:2013usa
;
Esteban:2018ppq
. Analysis of early
oscillation data was also used to impose constraints on flavor
dependent long-range forces
Grifols:2003gy
;
Joshipura:2003jh
;
GonzalezGarcia:2006vp
.

Straightforward constructions leading to Eq. (
1
)
have an extended gauge sector with an additional
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry with
charge involving some of the lepton flavors and an heavy enough gauge
boson. Conversely if the gauge boson is light enough a long range
force will be generated. Thus the analysis of neutrino oscillation
data can shed light on the valid range of
Z^{\prime}
mass and coupling in
both regimes. Following this approach, the marginalized bounds on the
NSI coefficients derived from the global analysis of oscillations in
presence of NSI performed in Ref.
Esteban:2018ppq
were adapted
to place constraints on the coupling and mass of the new gauge boson
both in the NSI limit
Heeck:2018nzc
and in the long-range
regime
Wise:2018rnb
for several
U(1)^{\prime}
flavor symmetries.
However, strictly speaking, the bounds derived in
Ref.
Esteban:2018ppq
cannot be directly used to constraint the

U(1)^{\prime}
scenarios because in the latter case only flavor diagonal
interactions (and only some of them depending on the
U(1)^{\prime}
charge)
are generated, while the bounds derived in Ref.
Esteban:2018ppq

were obtained in the most general parameter space with all relevant
four-fermion interactions (flavor conserving and flavor changing)
being simultaneously non-vanishing. In order to derive statistically
consistent bounds on each
U(1)^{\prime}
scenario a dedicated analysis has to
be performed in its reduced parameter space.

With this motivation, in this work we perform such dedicated global
analysis of oscillation data in the framework of lepton flavor
dependent
U(1)^{\prime}
interactions which affect the neutrino evolution in
matter, with interaction lengths ranging from the Sun-Earth distance
to effective contact neutrino interactions. In Sec.
2
we
describe the models which will be studied and derive the matter
potential generated both in the contact interaction limit (in
Sec.
2.1
) and in the case of finite interaction range (in
Sec.
2.2
) as a function of the
U(1)^{\prime}
charges. The results
of the global analysis are presented in Sec.
3
. In
particular the bounds imposed by the analysis and how they compare
with those from other experiments are presented in
Sec.
3.1
. An additional consideration that we take into
account is that in the presence of NSI a degeneracy exists in
oscillation data, leading to the so called LMA-Dark
(LMA-D)
Miranda:2004nb
solution first observed in solar
neutrinos, where for suitable NSI the data can be explained by a
mixing angle
\theta_{12}
in the second octant. For this new solution
to appear the new interactions must be such that the matter potential
difference for electron neutrinos reverses its sign with respect to
that in the SM. It is not trivial to generate such large effects
without conflicting with bounds from other experiments, though models
with light mediators (
i.e.
, below the electroweak scale) have
been proposed as viable candidates
Farzan:2017xzy
;
Miranda:2015dra
;
Farzan:2015doa
;
Farzan:2015hkd
;
Babu:2017olk
;
Denton:2018xmq
. Section
3.2
contains our findings on
viable models for LMA-D. We summarize our conclusions in
Sec.
4
. We present some details of the translation of
the bounds from some experiments to the models studied in an appendix.

2
Formalism

We are going to focus on
U(1)^{\prime}
models which can be tested in
neutrino oscillation experiments via its contribution to the matter
potential. As a start this requires that the new gauge boson couples
to the fermions of the first generation.

An important issue when enlarging the Standard Model with a new

U(1)^{\prime}
gauge group is the possibility of mixing between the three
neutral gauge bosons of the model which can, in general, be induced in
either kinetic or mass terms. While kinetic mixing is fairly generic
as it can be generated at the loop level with the SM particle
contents, matter mixing is model dependent as it requires an extended
scalar sector with a vacuum expectation value charged both under the
SM and the
U(1)^{\prime}
. In what respects the effect of the new
U(1)^{\prime}
in
oscillation experiments an important observation is that if the new
interaction does not couple directly to fermions of the first
generation, no matter effects can be generated by kinetic
mixing
Heeck:2018nzc
. Thus neglecting mixing effects yields the
most model independent and conservative bounds from neutrino
oscillation results. So in what follows we are going to work under
the assumption that the
Z^{\prime}
mixing with SM gauge bosons can be safely
neglected.

In addition we notice that only vector interactions contribute to the
matter potential in neutrino propagation. Altogether the part of the

U(1)^{\prime}
Lagrangian relevant for propagation in ordinary matter has the
most general form

\mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}}^{\text{matter}}=-g^{\prime}\big(a_{u}\,\bar{u}\gamma^{\alpha}u+a_{d}\,\bar{d}\gamma^{\alpha}d+a_{e}\,\bar{e}\gamma^{\alpha}e\\
+b_{e}\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\gamma^{\alpha}P_{L}\nu_{e}+b_{\mu}\,\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}P_{L}\nu_{\mu}+b_{\tau}\,\bar{\nu}_{\tau}\gamma^{\alpha}P_{L}\nu_{\tau}\big)Z^{\prime}_{\alpha}

(2)

with arbitrary charges
a_{u,d,e}
and
b_{e,\mu,\tau}
.

These charges can be accommodated in generalized anomaly free
UV-complete models including only the SM particles plus right-handed
neutrinos
Allanach:2018vjg
. If, in addition, one requires all
couplings to be vector-like and the quark couplings to be generation
independent, the condition of anomaly cancellation for models with
only SM plus right handed neutrinos imposes constrains over the six
charges above and one ends with a subclass of models characterized by
three independent charges which can be chosen to be, for example,

B-L
,
(L_{\mu}-L_{\tau})
, and
(L_{\mu}-L_{e})

Heeck:2018nzc
:

c_{\textsc{bl}}(B-L)+c_{\mu\tau}(L_{\mu}-L_{\tau})+c_{\mu e}(L_{\mu}-L_{e})\,,

(3)

so
a_{u}=a_{d}=c_{\textsc{bl}}/3
,
a_{e}=b_{e}=-(c_{\textsc{bl}}+c_{\mu e})
,
b_{\mu}=-c_{\textsc{bl}}+c_{\mu e}+c_{\mu\tau}
, and

b_{\tau}=-(c_{\textsc{bl}}+c_{\mu\tau})
. In particular models with
charges as in Eq. (
3

In general, the evolution of the neutrino and antineutrino flavor
state during propagation is governed by the Hamiltonian:

H^{\nu}=H_{\text{vac}}+H_{\text{mat}}\quad\text{and}\quad H^{\bar{\nu}}=(H_{\text{vac}}-H_{\text{mat}})^{*}\,,

where

H_{\text{vac}}=U_{\text{vac}}D_{\text{vac}}U_{\text{vac}}^{\dagger}\quad\text{with}\quad D_{\text{vac}}=\frac{1}{2E_{\nu}}\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(0,\Delta m^{2}_{21},\Delta m^{2}_{31})\,.

2.1
The large (
M_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 10^{-12}
eV)
M_{Z^{\prime}}
limit: the NSI regime

In the limit of large
M_{Z^{\prime}}
, the
Z^{\prime}
field can be integrated out
from the spectrum and Eq. (
2
) generate effective
dimension-six four-fermion interactions leading to Neutral Current NSI
between neutrinos and matter which are usually parametrized in the
form of Eq. (
1
). The coefficients

\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,P}
parametrizes the strength of the new
interaction with respect to the Fermi constant, with

\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,L}=\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,R}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}G_{F}}\,\frac{g^{\prime 2}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\,a_{f}\,b_{\alpha}\equiv\delta_{\alpha\beta}\frac{1}{2}\,a_{f}\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{0}

(6)

where we have introduced the notation

\varepsilon^{0}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}G_{F}}\frac{g^{\prime 2}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}

(7)

As it is well known, only vector NSI contribute to the matter
potential in neutrino oscillations. It is therefore convenient to
define the parameters relevant for neutrino oscillation experiments
as:

\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f}\equiv\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,L}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f,R}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\,a_{f}\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{0}\,.

(8)

These interactions lead to a flavor diagonal modification of the
matter potential

H_{\text{mat}}=\sqrt{2}G_{F}N_{e}(\vec{x})\begin{pmatrix}1+\mathcal{E}_{ee}(\vec{x})&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{E}_{\mu\mu}(\vec{x})&0\\
0&0&\mathcal{E}_{\tau\tau}(\vec{x})\end{pmatrix}

(9)

where the “
+1
” term in the
ee
entry accounts for the standard
contribution, and

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\alpha}(\vec{x})=\sum_{f=e,u,d}\frac{N_{f}(\vec{x})}{N_{e}(\vec{x})}\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{f}

(10)

describes the non-standard part. Here
N_{f}(\vec{x})
is the number
density of fermion
f
at the position
\vec{x}
along the neutrino
trajectory. In Eq. (
10
) we have limited the sum to the
charged fermions present in ordinary matter,
f=e,u,d
. Taking into
account that
N_{u}(\vec{x})=2N_{p}(\vec{x})+N_{n}(\vec{x})
and

N_{d}(\vec{x})=N_{p}(\vec{x})+2N_{n}(\vec{x})
, and also that matter
neutrality implies
N_{p}(\vec{x})=N_{e}(\vec{x})
,
Eq. (
10

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\alpha}(\vec{x})=\big(\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{e}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{p}\big)+Y_{n}(\vec{x})\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{n}\quad\text{with}\quad Y_{n}(\vec{x})\equiv\frac{N_{n}(\vec{x})}{N_{e}(\vec{x})}

(11)

where

\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{p}\equiv 2\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{u}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{d}=a_{p}\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{0}\,,\qquad\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{n}\equiv 2\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{d}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{u}=a_{n}\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{0}\,.

(12)

and we have introduced the proton and neutron
Z^{\prime}
couplings

a_{p}\equiv 2a_{u}+a_{d}\,,\qquad a_{n}\equiv 2a_{d}+a_{u}\,.

(13)

As discussed in Ref.
GonzalezGarcia:2011my
, in the Earth the
neutron/proton ratio

which characterize the matter

Y_{n}^{\oplus}=1.051

10

and (

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\alpha}(\vec{x})\equiv\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{\oplus}

\begin{split}\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{\oplus}&=\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{e}+\big(2+Y_{n}^{\oplus}\big)\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{u}+\big(1+2Y_{n}^{\oplus}\big)\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{d}=\big(\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{e}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{p}\big)+Y_{n}^{\oplus}\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{n}\\
&=\big[(a_{e}+a_{p})+Y_{n}^{\oplus}a_{n}\big]\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{0}\,.\end{split}

(14)

neutrino mass ordering,
i.e.
, the sign of
\Delta m^{2}_{31}
. For
that reason it has been called “generalized mass ordering
degeneracy” in Ref.
Coloma:2016gei
. Because of the position
dependence of the NSI hamiltonian described by

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\alpha}(\vec{x})
this degeneracy is only approximate,
mostly due to the non-trivial neutron/proton ratio along the neutrino
path inside the Sun. In what follows when marginalizing over

\theta_{12}
we consider two distinct parts of the parameter space:
one with
\theta_{12}<45^{\circ}
, which we denote as
LIGHT
,
and one with
\theta_{12}>45^{\circ}
, which we denote by

DARK
.

Apart from the appearance of this degenerate solution, another feature
to consider in the global analysis of oscillation data in presence of
NSI is the possibility to further improve the quality of the fit with
respect to that of
standard

3\nu
oscillations in the

LIGHT
sector. Till recently this was indeed the case because
for the last decade the value of
\Delta m^{2}_{21}
preferred by KamLAND was
somewhat higher than the one from solar experiments. This tension
appeared due to a combination of two effects: the fact that the

\mbox{}^{8}
B
measurements performed by SNO, SK and Borexino showed no
evidence of the low energy spectrum turn-up expected in the standard
LMA-MSW
Wolfenstein:1977ue
;
Mikheev:1986gs
solution for the
value of
\Delta m^{2}_{21}
favored by KamLAND, and the observation of a
non-vanishing day-night asymmetry in SK, whose size was larger than
the one predicted for the
\Delta m^{2}_{21}
value indicated by KamLAND.
With the data included in the analysis in Ref.
Esteban:2018azc
;
Esteban:2018ppq
this resulted into a tension of
\Delta\chi^{2}\sim 7.4
for the standard
3\nu
oscillations. Such tension could be
alleviated in presence of a non-standard matter potential, thus
leading to a possible decrease in the minimum
\chi^{2}
. However, with
the latest 2970-days SK4 results presented at the Neutrino2020
conference
SK:nu2020
in the form of total energy spectrum and
updated day-night asymmetry, the tension between the best fit

\Delta m^{2}_{21}
of KamLAND and that of the solar results has
decreased. Currently they are compatible within
1.1\sigma
in the
latest global analysis
Esteban:2020cvm
.

2.2
The finite
M_{Z^{\prime}}
case: the long-range interaction regime

If
M_{Z^{\prime}}
is very light the four-fermion contact interaction
approximation in Eq. (
1
) does not hold and the
potential encountered by the neutrino in its trajectory depends on the
integral of the source density within a radius
\sim 1/M_{Z^{\prime}}
around
it. However, following Ref.
GonzalezGarcia:2006vp
the
generalized matter potential can still be written as
Eq. (
9
) provided that Eq. (
10
) is modified
as:

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\alpha}(\vec{x})=\sum_{f=e,u,d}\frac{\hat{N}_{f}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})}{N_{e}(\vec{x})}\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{f}

(26)

where

\hat{N}_{f}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})\equiv\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{4\pi}\int N_{f}(\vec{\rho})\,\frac{e^{-M_{Z^{\prime}}|\vec{\rho}-\vec{x}|}}{|\vec{\rho}-\vec{x}|}\,d^{3}\vec{\rho}\,.

(27)

Taking into account that ordinary matter is neutral and only contains

f=e,u,d
, we can rewrite Eq. (
26
) in a way that
generalizes Eq. (
11
):

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\alpha}(\vec{x})=F_{e}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})\big(\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{e}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{p}\big)+F_{n}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})Y_{n}(\vec{x})\varepsilon_{\alpha\alpha}^{n}\\
\text{with}\quad F_{i}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})\equiv\frac{\hat{N}_{i}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})}{N_{i}(\vec{x})}\quad\text{and}\quad i\in\{e,n\}\,.

(28)

For what concerns neutrinos traveling inside the Sun, the propagation
effects induced by the new interactions are completely dominated by
the solar matter distribution. Denoting by
\vec{x}_{\odot}
the center
of the Sun and accounting for the spherical symmetry of the matter
potential we can write:

F_{i}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})\simeq F_{i}^{\odot}(|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{\odot}|,M_{Z^{\prime}})\\
\text{with}\quad F_{i}^{\odot}(r,M_{Z^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{N_{i}^{\odot}(r)}\cdot\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}}{2\,r}\int_{0}^{R_{\odot}}\rho\,N_{i}^{\odot}(\rho)\big[e^{-M_{Z^{\prime}}|\rho-r|}-e^{-M_{Z^{\prime}}(\rho+r)}\big]\,d\rho\,.

(29)

A similar formula can be derived for neutrinos traveling inside the
Earth, but in this case the effective potential has an extra term
induced by the Sun matter density. Concretely, denoting by

\vec{x}_{\oplus}
the center of the Earth and by
X_{\ominus}=|\vec{x}_{\odot}-\vec{x}_{\oplus}|
the Sun-Earth distance, we have:

F_{i}(\vec{x},M_{Z^{\prime}})\simeq F_{i}^{\oplus}(|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{\oplus}|,M_{Z^{\prime}})\\
\text{with}\quad F_{i}^{\oplus}(r,M_{Z^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{N_{i}^{\oplus}(r)}M_{Z^{\prime}}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2\,r}\int_{0}^{R_{\oplus}}\rho\,N_{i}^{\oplus}(\rho)\big[e^{-M_{Z^{\prime}}|\rho-r|}-e^{-M_{Z^{\prime}}(\rho+r)}\big]\,d\rho\\
+\frac{e^{-M_{Z^{\prime}}X_{\ominus}}}{X_{\ominus}}\int_{0}^{R_{\odot}}\rho\,N_{i}^{\odot}(\rho)\sinh(\rho\,M_{Z^{\prime}})\,d\rho\Bigg\}\,.

(30)

The solar-induced contribution becomes non-negligible when the range
of the interactions,
1\big/M_{Z^{\prime}}
, is comparable or larger than the
Sun-Earth distance
X_{\ominus}
.

The factors
F_{i}^{\odot}(r,M_{Z^{\prime}})
and
F_{i}^{\oplus}(r,M_{Z^{\prime}})
represent
the modification of the matter potential due to the finite range of
the interaction mediated by the
Z^{\prime}
with respect to that obtained in
the contact interaction limit. Such limit is recovered when the range
of the new interactions become shorter than the typical size of the
matter distribution,
i.e.
,
R_{\odot(\oplus)}
. Hence:

F_{i}^{\odot(\oplus)}(r,M_{Z^{\prime}})\to 1\quad\text{for}\quad M_{Z^{\prime}}\gg 1/R_{\odot(\oplus)}\,.

(31)

For solar neutrinos further simplification follows if one takes into
account that for adiabatic transitions the dominant matter effects is
generated by the potential at the neutrino production point which is
is close to the Sun center. So to a very good approximation one can
scale the contact interaction potential with a position independent
factor
F_{i}^{\odot}(0,M_{Z^{\prime}})
.
For the Earth matter potential the position dependence of the factor

F_{i}^{\oplus}(r,M_{Z^{\prime}})
is very weak in the current experiments, so one
can also scale the contact interaction potential with an approximate

F_{i}^{\oplus}(\bar{r},M_{Z^{\prime}})
evaluated at a fix
\bar{r}
which we
take to be also
\bar{r}=0
.

In Fig.
1
we plot these scale factors

F_{e}^{\odot(\oplus)}(0,M_{Z^{\prime}})
. As seen in the figure for

M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 10^{-13}
eV the matter potential in the Earth is more
suppressed with respect to that in the Sun. In principle, this opens
the possibility of configurations for which the
U(1)^{\prime}
-induced matter
potential in the Sun is large enough without conflicting with bounds
imposed by atmospheric and long-baseline experiments. This also
implies that in the combined analysis of solar and KamLAND data, for a
given value of
M_{Z^{\prime}}
, the effective matter potential for solar
neutrinos will be suppressed by a different factor than that for
KamLAND antineutrinos. To illustrate the overall
M_{Z^{\prime}}
dependence
of the effect we show in Fig.
1
the effective
suppression factor in the combined solar+KamLAND analysis calculated
by scaling the results obtained for a specific model (concretely, for
a
Z^{\prime}
coupled to
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
, but the results are similar for models
with other couplings) for each
M_{Z^{\prime}}
to those obtained in the large

M_{Z^{\prime}}
regime. As seen in the figure for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 10^{-10}
eV both the effective potential in the Solar+KamLAND
analysis and the Earth matter potential relevant for atmospheric and
LBL neutrinos are well within the contact interaction
regime. Conversely for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 10^{-13}
eV all the matter
potentials in the analysis show deviations from the contact
interaction regime.

Concerning the phenomenology of neutrino oscillations in the presence
of the modified matter potential in this long-range interaction
regime, the main difference with the NSI contact interaction case is
the impossibility of realizing the “generalized mass ordering
degeneracy” in Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
) because
of the very different
\vec{x}
dependence of the SM matter potential
and the one generated by the
Z^{\prime}
. In other words, one cannot “flip
the sign of the matter hamiltonian” by adding to the standard

N_{e}(x)
something which has a completely different
\vec{x}

profile. Hence, there is no LMA-D solution for these models.
On the other hand, it is still possible, at least in principle, that a
long-range potential leads to an improvement on the fit to solar and
KamLAND data with respect to the pure LMA solution.

3
Results of the global oscillation analysis

We have performed a global fit to neutrino oscillation data in the
framework of
3\nu
massive neutrinos with new neutrino-matter
interactions generated by
U(1)^{\prime}
models and characterized by the
Lagrangian in Eq. (
2
). For the detailed description
of methodology and data included we refer to the comprehensive global
fit in Ref.
Esteban:2018ppq
performed in the framework of
three-flavor oscillations plus NSI. In addition in the present
analysis we account for the latest LBL data samples included in
NuFIT-5.0
Esteban:2020cvm
which includes the previously cited
solar neutrinos 2970-days SK4 results
SK:nu2020
, the updated
medium baseline reactor samples from RENO
RENO:nu2020
and
Double Chooz
DoubleC:nu2020
, and the latest long-baseline
samples from T2K
T2K:nu2020
and NO
\nu
A
NOvA:nu2020
.
Notice that in order to keep the fit manageable we proceed as in in
Ref.
Esteban:2018ppq
and restrict ourselves to the
CP-conserving case and set
\delta_{\text{CP}}\in\{0,\pi\}
.
Consequently the T2K and NO
\nu
A appearance data (which exhibit
substantial dependence on the leptonic CP phase) are not included in
the fit. With these data we construct a global
\chi^{2}
function:

\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC+Z'}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}}|\vec{\omega})

(32)

for each
Z^{\prime}
model. In general each model belongs to a family
characterized by a set of
U(1)^{\prime}
charges; for each family

\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC+Z'}}
depends on the two variables parametrizing the
new interaction,
g^{\prime}
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}
, plus the six oscillation
parameters
\vec{\omega}\equiv(\Delta m^{2}_{21},\Delta m^{2}_{31},\theta_{12},\theta_{13},\theta_{23},\delta_{\text{CP}})
.

Following the discussion in Sec.
2
, we have performed
the analysis in two physically distinctive domains of
M_{Z^{\prime}}
:

•

NSI Domain (DOM=NSI): In this case, the range of the induced
non-standard interactions in both the Sun and the Earth matter is
short enough for the four-fermion effective description to hold. As
seen in Fig.
1
this happens for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 10^{-10}
eV. In this regime a possible conflict with the
cosmological bound on
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
may appear because of
the contribution of either the
Z^{\prime}
itself (if lighter than all
active neutrinos) or by the extra contribution to the neutrino
density produced by the
Z^{\prime}
decay (if heavier than some
\nu
mass
eigenstate). These bounds can be evaded in two distinct ranges of
the
U(1)^{\prime}
interactions:

–

M_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 5
MeV for which the contribution to the
neutrino energy density due to the decay of the
Z^{\prime}
into
neutrinos is sufficiently suppressed by the Boltzmann factor
\sim\exp(-M_{Z^{\prime}}/T)

Kamada:2015era
;

–

M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(\text{eV})
but with very weak
coupling
g^{\prime}<10^{-10}
for which the
Z^{\prime}
is produced through
freeze-in. In this regime, even if
Z^{\prime}
could decay to the
lightest neutrinos this would happen after neutrino decoupling
making the contribution to
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
negligible or at
most within the present allowed range
Escudero:2019gzq
.

•

Long-Range Domain (DOM=LRI): If
M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 10^{-13}
eV
interactions in the Earth and Sun matter are long range, and for a
given value of
g^{\prime}
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}
the effects in the Earth are
suppressed with respect to those in the Sun. As mentioned above, for

g^{\prime}<10^{-10}
the contribution to
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
is
negligible.

Correspondingly we define

\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC+Z',DOM}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}}|\vec{\omega})\equiv\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC+Z'}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}}\in\text{DOM}|\vec{\omega})

(33)

In both domains we compare the results of the fit including the new

U(1)^{\prime}
interaction with those obtained in the “standard”

3\nu
-mixing scenario, which we will denote as “OSC”, and for which
the present global fit yields

\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC,min}}=718.5\,.

(34)

We will classify the models according to the quality of the fit in the
presence of the
U(1)^{\prime}
interactions compared with that of OSC by
defining

\Delta\chi^{2}_{\text{LIGHT,DOM}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}})\equiv\left.\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC+Z',DOM}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}}|\vec{\omega})\right|_{\text{marg,LIGHT}}-\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC,min}}\,,

(35)

where by
\left.\right|_{\text{marg,LIGHT}}
we imply that the
minimization over the oscillation parameters is done in the

LIGHT
sector of parameter space.
In addition the presence of a viable LMA-D solution can be quantified
in terms of

\Delta\chi^{2}_{\text{DARK,DOM}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}})\equiv\left.\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC+Z',DOM}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}}|\vec{\omega})\right|_{\text{marg, DARK}}-\chi^{2}_{\text{OSC,min}}\,,

(36)

where by
\left.\right|_{\text{marg,DARK}}
we imply that the
minimization over the oscillation parameters is done in the

DARK
sector.

We have surveyed the model space by performing the global oscillation
analysis for a grid of
U(1)^{\prime}
interactions characterized by the six
couplings
a_{u,d}\in\{-1,0,1\}
and
a_{e},\,b_{e,\mu,\tau}\in\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}
. In this way our survey covers a total of

\sim 10000
different sets of
U(1)^{\prime}
charges which can produce
effects in matter propagation in neutrino oscillation experiments.

4
Conclusions

In this work we have performed dedicated global analysis of
oscillation data in the framework of lepton flavor dependent
U(1)^{\prime}

interactions which affect the neutrino evolution in matter. The
analysis is performed for interaction lengths ranging from larger than
the Sun radius (covering what we label as LRI domain) to effective
contact neutrino interactions (NSI domain). We survey
\sim 10000
set
of models characterized by the charges of the first generation charged
fermions and the three flavor neutrinos. We find that

•

In the LIGHT sector of the oscillation parameter space the
introduction of new interactions does not lead to a significantly
better fit of the oscillation data compared to standard
oscillations, irrespective of the
U(1)^{\prime}
coupling in either NSI or
LRI domains. Thus for all cases the analysis of oscillation data in
the LIGHT sector results in excluded ranges of
g^{\prime}
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}
.

•

The excluded ranges for the
Z^{\prime}
coupling and mass for the
models with couplings listed in table
1
are shown in
Figs.
2
and
3
for
M_{Z^{\prime}}
below
1 eV and in the
\mathcal{O}(\text{MeV--GeV})
range, respectively
–
i.e.
, below and above the window strongly disfavored by
the cosmological bound on
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
.

•

In the regime of ultra-light mediators, for all models shown,
the oscillation analysis improves over the bounds from tests of
fifth forces and of violation of equivalence principle and for
Z^{\prime}

lighter than
\sim 10^{-11}
eV.

•

For mediators in the
\mathcal{O}(\text{MeV--GeV})
range we
list in table
3
the derived constraints on
g^{\prime}

versus
M_{Z^{\prime}}
. We find that for several of the considered models
there are values of
g^{\prime}
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}
for which the oscillation
analysis provides constraints extending beyond those from other
experiments.

•

In what respects to LMA-D we find that it cannot be realized in
the LRI domain. In the NSI domain we have found that 4.8% of the
set of charges studied can have a best fit in LMA-D, and 5.2% lead
to LMA-D as a valid solution within 4 units of
\Delta\chi^{2}
of
standard oscillations. None of these set of charges correspond to an
anomaly free model based on gauging SM global symmetries with SM
plus right-handed neutrinos matter content (Eq. (
3
)).
So, generically,
Z^{\prime}
models for LMA-D with gauged SM global
symmetries require additional states for anomaly cancellation.

ones for the range of masses under consideration, we refer the

Ilten:2018crw
;
Fabbrichesi:2020wbt
;
Bauer:2018onh
;
Wise:2018rnb
;
Jaeckel:2010ni
;
Essig:2013lka
;

for a selection of works which include a systematic

of the bounds shown by the colored regions in Figs.
2

and
3
, as well as the procedure used to rescale them

Bounds from searches for gravitational fifth forces

Generically gravitational fifth force experiments look for deviation
from the standard Newton potential (
\propto 1/r
) between two
objects.

We have taken the results from Ref.
Salumbides:2013dua
(which,
in turn, were recasted from Fig. 10 in Ref.
Adelberger:2009zz
)
where they present the constraints on the coupling
\alpha_{5}
versus
the interaction length
\lambda
(or, equivalently, versus
m_{5}=\frac{1}{\lambda}
) defined as the constant entering the potential of
the fifth force

V_{5}(r)=\alpha_{5}\,\mathcal{N}_{1}\,\mathcal{N}_{2}\,\frac{e^{-r/\lambda}}{r}

(43)

where
\mathcal{N}_{1,2}
is the total charge of each object, which
they take as the total number of baryons.

In particular from Fig. 3 in
Salumbides:2013dua
we read their
boundary curve

\left(\frac{\alpha^{\text{max}}_{5}}{\alpha_{em}}\,,\,m_{5}\right)\,,

(44)

where
\alpha_{em}
is the SM fine structure constant. In order to
rescale it to the different
U(1)^{\prime}
models we notice that the
corresponding potential for the
U(1)^{\prime}
interaction for the same
objects is

V^{\prime}(r)=C_{1}C_{2}\frac{{g^{\prime}}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-r/\lambda^{\prime}}}{r}\,,

(45)

where
C_{i}
is the total
Z^{\prime}
charge of object
i
and
\lambda^{\prime}
is
the
U(1)^{\prime}
interaction length

\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{1}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}\,,\quad C_{i}\equiv\frac{\mathcal{N}_{i}}{A_{i}}c_{i}\equiv\frac{\mathcal{N}_{i}}{A_{i}}\left[Z_{i}(a_{e}+2a_{u}+a_{d})+(A_{i}-Z_{i})(a_{u}+2a_{d})\right]\,,

(46)

where
Z_{i}
and
A_{i}
are the atomic number and mass number of the
material of which the object
i
is made, so
c_{i}
is the charge under

Z^{\prime}
for each atom of the material. Assuming that the number of
protons and neutrons in the material are not very different (that is,

A_{i}/Z_{i}\sim 2
), we get

V^{\prime}(r)=V_{5}(r)\times\frac{g^{\prime 2}}{4\pi\alpha_{5}}\frac{(a_{e}+3a_{u}+3a_{d})^{2}}{4}

(47)

with
m_{5}=M_{Z^{\prime}}
. So the boundary in the
g^{\prime}
vs
M_{Z^{\prime}}
plane
will be

\big(g^{\prime}_{\text{max}},M_{Z^{\prime}}\big)=\left(\frac{2}{a_{e}+3a_{u}+3a_{d}}\sqrt{4\pi\alpha_{em}}\times\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\text{max}}_{5}}{\alpha_{em}}}\,,\,m_{5}\right)\,.

(48)

Bounds from searches for violation of the equivalence principle

Similarly as in the case of fifth force searches, this bound comes
from precise measurements of the gravitational potential between two
objects. However, in this case one tests the differences of the
potential for the same total mass of two different test materials,
using a pendulum which is attracted by the same mass. The bounds are
taken from Ref.
Schlamminger:2007ht
, where they define the
potential due to the new force as:

V_{G}(r)=\alpha\,G\frac{m_{t}m_{s}}{r}\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{t}\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{s}e^{-r/\lambda}\,.

(49)

where
G
is the gravitational constant,
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
refers
to the new interaction charge per mass unit, and the subindices
t

and
s
stand for test (or pendulum) and source masses,
respectively. In Ref.
Schlamminger:2007ht
they assume that the
new interaction couples to the number of baryons. Thus, they use
beryllium and titanium as test materials, chosen to maximize the
difference in baryon number per unit mass. To be specific, we use

\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Be}}=0.99868
and

\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Ti}}=1.001077
, as in
Ref.
Schlamminger:2007ht
.

Noting that the total potential will be the sum of the standard
gravitational potential plus the contribution from the new
interaction, the authors of Ref.
Schlamminger:2007ht
put a
bound on

\eta=2\frac{V_{\text{Be}}-V_{\text{Ti}}}{V_{\text{Be}}+V_{\text{Ti}}}\sim\frac{\Delta V_{G}(r)}{\frac{Gm_{s}m_{t}}{r}}=\alpha(\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Ti}}-\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Be}})\hat{\mathcal{N}_{s}}e^{-r/\lambda}

(50)

which is used to set a constraint on
\alpha
as a function of

\lambda
,

\big(\alpha^{\text{max}}\,,\,\lambda\big)\,.

(51)

This can be used to set a bound on
\Delta V^{\prime}(r)
for a general model,
noting that

\begin{split}\Delta V^{\prime}(r)&=\frac{{g^{\prime}}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-rM_{Z^{\prime}}}}{r}\,C_{s}(C_{\text{Be}}-C_{\text{Ti}})\\
&=\Delta V_{G}(r)\times\frac{g^{\prime 2}/4\pi}{\alpha G\cdot u^{2}}\times\frac{c_{\text{Ti}}/A_{\text{Ti}}-c_{\text{Be}}/A_{\text{Be}}}{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Ti}}-\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Be}}}\times\frac{c_{s}/A_{s}}{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{s}}\end{split}

(52)

where
u
stands for the atomic mass unit in GeV (
u\simeq 0.931
GeV).
So the boundary in the
g^{\prime}
vs
M_{Z^{\prime}}
plane will be

\big(g^{\prime}_{\text{max}}\,,\,M_{Z^{\prime}}\big)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Ti}}-\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{Be}}}{c_{\text{Ti}}/A_{\text{Ti}}-c_{\text{Be}}/A_{\text{Be}}}\times\frac{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{s}}{c_{s}/A_{s}}\,4\pi\,G\,u^{2}}\times\sqrt{\alpha^{\text{max}}}\,,\,\frac{1}{\lambda}\right).

(53)

Bounds from white-dwarf cooling

We based the bounds shown in Figs.
3

and
4
on the study presented in
Ref.
Dreiner:2013tja
. There, upper bounds on new interactions
are imposed on the basis that the energy losses from plasmon decays
into particles that escape the star is not larger than the energy
losses due to neutrino emission in the SM.

In the
U(1)^{\prime}
scenarios here considered the minimum new contribution
is due to
Z^{\prime}
mediated decays into neutrinos

\Gamma_{\text{plasmon}\to\nu\bar{\nu},Z^{\prime}}^{s}\lesssim\Gamma_{\text{plasmon}\to\nu\bar{\nu},\text{SM}}^{s}=\frac{C_{e,V}^{2}G_{F}^{2}}{48\pi^{2}\alpha_{em}}\frac{Z_{s}\pi_{s}^{3}}{\omega_{s}}

(54)

where
C_{e,V}
is the vector coupling to the electron current in the
SM,
Z_{s}
is the plasmon wavefunction renormalization and
\pi_{s}
is
the effective plasmon mass which enters in the dispersion relation

\omega^{2}_{s}-k^{2}=\pi_{s}(\omega_{s},k)
. Here,
s=T,L
refers to the
plasmon polarization (transverse or longitudinal).

Under the assumption that the mass of the
Z^{\prime}
is much larger than the
frequency of the plasmon we can write its rate into neutrinos of a
given flavor
\beta
due to the new interactions as

\Gamma_{\text{plasmon}\to\nu_{\beta}\bar{\nu}_{\beta},Z^{\prime}}^{s}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{{g^{\prime}}^{4}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{4}}\frac{(a_{e}\,b_{\beta})^{2}}{48\pi^{2}\alpha_{em}}\frac{Z_{s}\pi_{s}^{3}}{\omega_{s}}

(55)

And the upper bound obtained in Ref.
Dreiner:2013tja
translates
into:

\sqrt{\sum_{\beta}\frac{(a_{e}\,b_{\beta})^{2}}{3}}\cdot\frac{{g^{\prime}}^{2}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\leq C_{e,V}\,G_{F}=1.12\times 10^{-5}~\text{GeV}^{-2}\,.

(56)

Bounds from coherent neutrino-nucleus scattering

We have performed our own reanalysis of coherent neutrino-nucleus
scattering data using the time and energy information from COHERENT
experiment
Akimov:2017ade
;
Akimov:2018vzs
on CsI based on our
recent analysis in Ref.
Coloma:2019mbs
performed for NSI with a

Fig.

correspond to the analysis performed using the

Akimov:2017ade

Ref.
Coloma:2019mbs
for details).

Model predictions are obtained exactly as in
Coloma:2019mbs
,
but replacing the cross section for coherent scattering in the
presence of NSI with that induced by the
Z^{\prime}

define

\varepsilon^{\prime}(Q^{2},g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}})\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}G_{F}}\frac{{g^{\prime}}^{2}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}+Q^{2}}

(57)

one can use the same cross section expression for NSI simply
replacing:

\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{q,V}\to\delta_{\alpha\beta}\,a_{q}\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{\prime}(Q^{2},g_{X},M_{Z^{\prime}})

(58)

where
Q^{2}=2MT
is the momentum transfer. In this case, the
differential cross section for coherent scattering of a neutrino with
flavor
\alpha
reads:

\frac{d\sigma_{\alpha}}{dT}=\frac{G_{F}^{2}}{2\pi}W_{\alpha}^{2}(Q^{2},g_{X},M_{Z^{\prime}})F^{2}(Q^{2})M\left(2-\frac{MT}{E_{\nu}^{2}}\right)

(59)

where
M
is the mass of the nucleus,
T
is the nuclear recoil
energy, and
E_{\nu}
is the incident neutrino energy. We have defined a
modified weak charge for the nucleus as:

\begin{split}W_{\alpha}(Q^{2},g_{X},M_{Z^{\prime}})&=Z\big[g_{p}^{V}+(2a_{u}+a_{d})\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{\prime}(Q^{2},g_{X},M_{Z^{\prime}})\big]+\\
&+N\big[g_{n}^{V}+(a_{u}+2a_{d})\,b_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{\prime}(Q^{2},g_{X},M_{Z^{\prime}})\big]\,.\end{split}

(60)

Thus, unlike in the case of NSI, the weak charge now depends on the
momentum transferred in the process.

The construction of
\chi^{2}_{\text{COH}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}})
is totally
analogous to that of
\chi^{2}_{\text{COH}}(\vec{\varepsilon})
in

Coloma:2019mbs

when deriving the oscillation bounds the COHERENT regions shown in

corresponds to the

coupling and mass for

\chi^{2}_{\text{COH}}(g^{\prime},M_{Z^{\prime}})-\chi^{2}_{\text{COH,SM}}>4.

(61)

Raffelt:2006cw

Chang:2016ntp

Finally additional constraints can be derived from cosmological
observations, from the energy injection of the
Z^{\prime}
onto
e^{+}e^{-}
in

1 MeV (see,
e.g.
, Fig. 6 in
Coffey:2020oir

again in this case it is uncertain how to recast these bounds to a
general
U(1)^{\prime}

not included them here.

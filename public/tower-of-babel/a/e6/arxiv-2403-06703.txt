A viscoelastic model for skin via homogenization theory.
Juan Casado-Díaz Dpto. de Ecuaciones Diferenciales y Análisis Numérico Universidad de Sevilla jcasadod@us.es

Abstract
We carry out the homogenization of a fluid-structure interaction problem consisting in the periodic inclusions of a viscous fluid in an elastic body. We get a macrostructure model where the body behaves as a viscoelastic material with a long-range memory term. Our aim is not only to get this limit problem but also to study its main properties. Using the micro-structure variables it is simple to check that it satisfies an energy conservation law assuring in particular the existence and uniqueness of solution. The difficulty is to characterize these properties using only the macroscopic system. We prove that the nonlocal term is given through a convolution kernel which exponentially decreases to zero and satisfies some positive conditions which we write in terms of the Laplace transform. These conditions can be used to directly prove the existence and uniqueness of solution. The results apply to modeling the mechanical behavior of skin as an indicator of what kind of models we should use. In a naive interpretation the fluid inclusions represent the cells and the elastic medium the extracellular matrix.



Keywords:
homogenization, fluid-structure interaction, viscoelastic material, long-range memory term, skin mechanical behavior.

Introduction.

In the modeling of the mechanical properties of skin, it is usual to considered that it behaves as a viscoelastic material, i.e. an elastic material where the stress tensor contains a memory term. The most simple case corresponds to an instantaneous memory term in such a way that the elastic deformation
u
solves a local partial differential system of the form

\rho\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}\Big(Ae_{x}(u)+Be_{x}(\partial_{t}u)\Big)=f,

with
\rho>0
the density of the material,
f
an exterior force,
e_{x}(u)
the symmetric part of the spatial derivative of
u
(strain tensor) and
A,B
symmetric positive tensors on the space of symmetric matrices. The instantaneous memory term corresponds to
-{\rm div}_{x}(Be_{x}(\partial_{t}u))
. It was noted in
[
13
]
, chapter 10, that it may be better to work with a long-range memory term. In the most simple case this corresponds to

\rho\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}\left(Ae_{x}(u)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)e_{x}(u)ds\right)=f.

Observe that integrating by parts the integral term in this equation and assuming that
S
is quickly decreasing in
s
, we get that the behavior of the solutions of this second equation approximates that of the previous one.

Our purpose in the present paper is to prove how this kind of memory terms can be obtained by using inclusions of a viscous fluid in a material governed by the usual linear elastic system. Namely, we consider a very simple and naive model for skin deformation, where the cells are represented by small connected domains filled of a viscous fluid and periodically distributed. They are surrounded by an elastic material, which represents what in Biology is known as the extra-celular matrix. The behavior of the fluid inside the cells is modeled by the Stokes system, while the deformation of the extra-celular matrix is given by the linear elasticity system. More exactly, recalling that the elasticity system is written in Lagrange coordinate, the fluid-structure interaction model that we consider consists in writing also the Navier-Stokes system in Lagrange coordinates and then linearizing the resulting equations. This provides a partial differential system in a bounded reference domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
(
N=2,3
in the applications), where the cells ocupe a family of connected open sets periodically distributed and the unknowns are the elastic deformation of the extra-celular matrix and the velocity and pressure of the fluid inside the cells. The system is accomplished with two boundary conditions on the interface corresponding to the continuity of the velocity and the equilibrium of internal forces. See (
4.6
) below. The system is hyperbolic in the solid part and parabolic in the fluid one.
We assume that the period is given by a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
and that the set of cells is given as

\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{l}:=\bigcup_{\varepsilon k+\varepsilon Y\subset\Omega\atop k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon O),

with
Y
the unitary cube in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
O
a connected open set sufficiently smooth and such that
\overline{O}\subset Y
. We also denote by
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{s}
the solid part. Our purpose is to pass to the limit when
\varepsilon
tends to zero in this partial differential system to get a macroscopic model.

Taking initial conditions bounded in the good space to have a unique solution, and extracting a convergent subsequence (see Proposition
4.4
),
we prove in Theorem
4.11

that denoting by
u_{\varepsilon}
the elastic deformation of the of the solid part and by
v_{\varepsilon}
the velocity of the fluid, we have

u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u\ \hbox{ in }W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))^{N}\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{N},\quad{1\over|O|}v_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{l}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\partial_{t}u\ \hbox{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))^{N},

where
u
solves a partial differential system of the form

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\rho^{eff}\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}\Big(A_{h}e_{x}(u)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)e_{x}(u)\,ds\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\partial O}R(t,y)\big(e_{x}(u^{0})y+u^{1}_{0}\big)\,d\sigma(y)\Big)=f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega.\end{array}

(1.1)

In this system
f
is the exterior force,
\rho^{eff}
is the mean value of the solid and fluid densities,
u^{0}
the initial condition for
u
, and
u_{1}^{0}
a function in
H^{1\over 2}(\partial O)
depending also on the initial conditions. The integral on
\partial O
must be understood as the duality product of
R(t,.)\in H^{-{1\over 2}}(\partial O)^{N\times N\times N}
and
e_{x}(u^{0})y+u^{1}_{0}\in H^{1\over 2}(\partial O)^{N}
. The proof uses the unfolding method (
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
), which is strongly related with the two-scale convergence method in periodic homogenization (
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
).

As we said above the microscopic model considered is very simple from the biological point of view, but we are not only interested in getting this homogenized system (
1.1
). Our aim is to impose the properties of this limit problem to general models describing the mechanical properties of skin.

We prove that the functions
R
and
S
in (
1.1
) have an exponential decay at infinity. This means that although the model has memory, only recent evens have influence. We also prove that the tensor
A_{h}
is symmetric and positive and
S
is symmetric. Finally, we deduce that
the limit model satisfies an energy conservation law (see (
4.51
)) depending on the microscopic structure, where
due to the viscosity of the fluid, the energy decreases with time. The difficulty is to write this condition using only the macroscopic model. We prove that this provides
a positivity condition for the sum of
A_{h}
and the memory term which we write in terms of the Laplace transform of
S
(see (
4.62
), (
4.63
)).
As a consequence of this conditions we deduce the existence of
c>0
, such that
A_{h}
and
S
satisfy the inequality

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(A_{h}E+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)E(s)ds\Big){dE\over dt}\,dt\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\geq c|E(T)|^{2}+c\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}{dE\over ds}ds\Big|^{2}dt+c\Big|\int_{0}^{T}e^{-(T-s)}{dE\over ds}ds\Big|^{2},\end{array}

for every
E\in W^{1,\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{C}^{N}_{s})
, such that
E(0)=0
. It allows us to prove the existence and uniqueness of solution for (
1.1
).

Although in our knowledge this is the first work where a memory term is rigorously obtained from the microscopic mixture of fluids and solids, the apparition of these terms in homogenization has before been obtained in other situations. In this way, it was proved in
[
16
]
, chapter 6, that the limit of the solutions of a viscoelastic problem with an instantaneous memory term where the coefficients are rapidly oscillating gives in general a viscoelastic problem with a long-range memory term with exponential decay at infinity.
In
[
3
]
, it is considered a geometry similar to that of this paper, but instead of taking an interaction fluid-structure problem, the authors consider the heat equation where the diffusion coefficients are of order
\varepsilon^{2}
in the periodic inclusions and of order one in the rest. The corresponding limit equation contains a zero-order memory term which exponentially decreases to zero. If instead of considering the heat equation we consider the linear elasticity system, then we get a viscoelastic behavior in the limit with a stress tensor containing a memory term (
[
5
]
, see also
[
4
]
for the case of the elasticity system with a fixed frequency). Since the elasticity system conserves the energy, this memory term does not converges exponentially to zero as in our case. Another kind of equations which provide a memory term corresponds to the wave equation (or the elasticity system) with a highly oscillating drift term (
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
). In this case we get a nonlocal drift term depending on a dependence cone.

3
A fluid-structure interaction model.

We present in this section the partial differential system corresponding to the simple fluid-structure interaction model that we will use in the following one. We also recall some results about the existence and uniqueness of solution for this problem.

We consider two open sets
\omega,\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\overline{\omega}\subset\Omega
and such that their respective boundaries can be locally written as the graph of a Lipschitz function. We assume that
\omega
is filled with a viscous fluid governed by the Navier-Stokes system, while
\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}
is the solid part, governed by the linear elasticity system.

We recall that the elasticity system is usually written in Lagrange coordinates, i.e. the coordinates refer to a fixed geometry in which there is no internal forces. In our case this geometry is given by
\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}
. The linear elasticity system is then given by

\rho^{s}\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}(Ae_{x}(u))=f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}),

(3.1)

where
T>0
is the final time,
\rho^{s}>0
the density of the solid part,
A\in{\cal L}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s})
, the stress tensor, is an element of
{\cal L}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s})
which we assume to be positive and symmetric, i.e. such that

\exists\,\alpha>0,\ \ A\xi:\xi\geq\alpha|\xi|^{2},\ \forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s},\qquad A\xi:\eta=A\eta:\xi,\ \ \forall\,\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s},

(3.2)

and
f
, the exterior force, is a function in
(0,T)\times\Omega
.

We recall that a particular, and important, case corresponds to the isotropic elastic materials where there exist
\lambda^{s},\mu^{s}>0
, the Lamé’s constants, such that the stress tensor
A
is given by

A\xi=\lambda^{s}{\rm trace}(\xi)I+2\mu^{s}\xi,\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s}.

(3.3)

For our purposes we will not need
A
to be isotropic.

The function
u=u(t,x)
in (
3.1
) represents the deformation. This means that the particle that was in position
x
at the initial time moved to
x+u(t,x)
at time
t
.

The movement of the fluid is usually written in Euler coordinates. Thus, these equations are not given in the fixed open set
\omega
but in an open set
\omega(t)
which variess with
t
, and are given by the Navier-Stokes system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\rho^{l}\big(\partial_{t}v+(v\cdot\nabla_{y})v\big)-2\mu\,{\rm div}_{y}e_{y}(u)+\nabla_{y}p=F\ \hbox{ in }\big\{(t,x):t\in(0,T),\ y\in\omega(t)\big\}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{y}\,v=0\ \hbox{ in }\big\{(t,x):t\in(0,T),\ y\in\omega(t)\big\},\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

where
\rho^{l}>0
is the density of the fluid,
\mu>0
the viscosity of the fluid and
F=F(t,y)
the exterior force. In these variables
v(t,y)
is the velocity of the particle that at time
t
occupies the position
y
. This position varies with time contrarily to the Lagrange coordinates. The function
p
represents the pressure of the fluid.

The Lagrange coordinates
(t,x)
and the Euler coordinates
(t,y)
are linked by

y=x+u^{l}(t,x),

with
u^{l}
the deformation of the fluid in Lagrange coordinates, i.e. the solution of

\partial_{t}u^{l}(t,x)=v(t,x+u^{l}(t,x)),\quad u^{l}(0,x)=x.

In the following, we consider a simple fluid-structure interaction model. It consists in writing the Navier-Stokes system in Lagrange coordinates and then to carry out a linearization around the null function. This means that we are assuming the deformations to be small. The boundary conditions at the interface correspond to the the equilibrium of forces and the continuity of the deformation between the fluid and the solid parts. Adding the corresponding initial conditions and assuming to simplify that the solid part is fixed at the boundary of
\Omega
, we get the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\rho^{s}\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}(Ae_{x}(u))=f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega})\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\rho^{l}\partial_{t}v-2\mu\,{\rm div}_{x}e_{x}(v)+\nabla_{x}p=f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}v=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=v,\ \ Ae_{x}(u)\nu=(2\mu e_{x}(v)-pI)\nu\quad\hbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u=0\ \hbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u_{|t=0}=u^{0},\ (\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}=u^{1}\quad\hbox{in }\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega},\qquad v_{|t=0}=v^{0}\ \hbox{ in }\omega,\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

where
\nu
denotes the outside normal unitary vector on
\partial\omega
.

Remark 3.1
.

In fluid mechanics it is usual to denote the symmetric part of the derivative of the velocity (the strain rate tensor) by
d_{x}(v)
instead of
e_{x}(v)
, but because from the Mathematical point of view this is the same differential operator that the one used to define the strain tensor
e_{x}(u)
, we prefer to use the same notation for both of them.

In order to get an existence and uniqueness result for (
3.5
), let us take
f
,
u^{0}
,
u^{1}
and
v^{0}
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))^{N},\ u^{0}\in H^{1}_{\partial\Omega}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega})^{N},\ u^{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega})^{N}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle v^{0}\in L^{2}(\omega)^{N},\ {\rm div}_{x}v^{0}=0\hbox{ in }\omega.\end{array}

(3.6)

We look for solutions
(u,v,p)
, which satisfy the problem in the distributional sense. Namely, using test functions with null divergence in the fluid part
\omega
, we say that
(u,v,p)
is a solution of (
3.6
) if and only if
(u,v)
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}))^{N}\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{\partial\Omega}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}))^{N}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))^{N}\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\omega))^{N}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}v=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\omega,\quad\partial_{t}u=v\ \hbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u_{|t=0}=u^{0},\ (\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}=u^{1}\quad\hbox{in }\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega},\qquad v_{|t=0}=v^{0}\ \hbox{ in }\omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{d\over dt}\left(\rho^{s}\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}}\hskip-4.0pt\partial_{t}u\cdot\varphi\,dx+\rho^{l}\int_{\omega}v\cdot\varphi\,dx\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}}\hskip-4.0ptAe_{x}(u):e_{x}(\varphi)\,dx+2\mu\int_{\omega}e_{x}(v):e_{x}(\varphi)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\varphi\,dx\ \hbox{ in }{\cal D}^{\prime}(0,T),\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\forall\,\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{N},\quad{\rm div}_{x}\varphi=0\ \hbox{ in }\omega.\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

Then, De Rham’s theorem proves the existence of a distribution
p\in{\cal D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\omega)
such that the second equation and the second boundary condition in the fourth line of (
3.5
) are satisfied.

The existence and uniqueness theorem for (
3.5
) is given by

Theorem 3.2
.

For every right-hand side
f
and every initial data
(u^{0},u^{1},v^{0})
satisfying (
3.6
), there exists a unique solution
(u,v,p)
of (
3.5
), such that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}))^{N}\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{\partial\Omega}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}))^{N}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))^{N}\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\omega))^{N},\quad p\in W^{-1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega)).\end{array}

(3.8)

It also satisfies the energy identity

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\left(\rho^{s}\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}}\big(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+Ae_{x}(u):e_{x}(u)\big)dx+\rho^{l}\int_{\omega}|v|^{2}dx\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\mu\int_{\omega}|e_{x}(v)|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}}\hskip-4.0ptf\cdot\partial_{t}u\,dx+\int_{\omega}f\cdot\ v\,dx\ \hbox{ in }(0,T).\end{array}

(3.9)

Proof.
Let us use the Hille-Yoshida theorem. Taking
w=\partial_{t}u
, we write (
3.5
) as the first order system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u=w\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega})\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}w={1\over\rho^{s}}\,{\rm div}_{x}Ae_{x}(u)+{1\over\rho^{s}}f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega})\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\partial_{t}v={2\mu\over\rho^{l}}\,{\rm div}_{x}e_{x}(v)-{1\over\rho^{l}}\nabla_{x}p+{1\over\rho^{l}}f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}v=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\omega\\

\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u=0\ \hbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u_{|t=0}=u^{0},\ w_{|t=0}=u^{1}\quad\hbox{in }\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega},\qquad v_{|t=0}=v^{0}\ \hbox{ in }\omega.\end{array}\right.

We take

as the Hilbert space

endowed with the scalar product

Then we define the unbounded operator

by

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle D({\cal A}):=\Big\{(u,w,v)\in{\cal H}:\ {\rm div}_{x}(Ae_{x}(u))\in L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega})^{N},\ w\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega})^{N},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\exists p\in L^{2}(\omega)\hbox{ with }2\mu\,{\rm div}_{x}e_{x}(u)-\nabla_{x}p\in L^{2}(\omega)^{N},\ w=v,\ Ae_{x}(u)\nu=(2\mu\,e_{x}(v)-pI)\nu\ \hbox{ on }\partial\omega\Big\},\end{array}

and (observe that

in the definition of

is unique)

{\cal A}(u,w,v)=\Big(w,\,{1\over\rho^{s}}{\rm div}\,Ae(u),\,{2\mu\over\rho^{l}}\,{\rm div}\,e(v)-{1\over\rho^{l}}\nabla p\Big),\quad\forall\,(u,w,v)\in D({\cal A}).

Taking into account that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\big({\cal A}(u,w,v),(u,w,v)\big)_{\cal H}=\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}}\big(Ae(w):e(u)+{\rm div}\,(Ae(u))\cdot w\big)dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle+\int_{\omega}\big(2\mu\,{\rm div}\,e(v)-\nabla p\big)\cdot v\,dx=-2\mu\int_{\omega}|e(v)|^{2}dx\leq 0,\end{array}

If

4
The viscoelastic model.

In the present section we prove that the inclusions of small liquid bubbles in an elastic medium provide a viscoelastic material.

We denote by
\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{N}

\int_{\partial O}y\,d\sigma(y)=0.

Lemma 4.9
.

For every
\zeta^{0}\in H^{1\over 2}(\partial O)^{N}
, with
{\cal P}\zeta^{0}=0
, there exits a unique solution
(\zeta,\eta,q)
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\zeta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O}))^{N},\ \ \eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);H^{1}(O))^{N},\ \ q\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(O))\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle-{\rm div}_{y}\big(Ae_{y}(\zeta)\big)=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty)\times(Y\setminus\overline{O})\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle-2\mu\,{\rm div}_{y}e_{y}(\eta)+\nabla_{y}q=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty)\times O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{y}\eta=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty)\times O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\zeta\hbox{ periodic in }Y,\ \ Ae_{y}(\zeta)\nu\hbox{ anti-periodic in }Y\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal Q}\partial_{t}\zeta=\eta,\ {\cal P}\big(Ae_{y}(\zeta)\nu\big)=0,\ Ae_{y}(\zeta)\nu=\big(2\mu e_{y}(\eta)-qI\big)\nu\ \hbox{ on }(0,\infty)\times\partial O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle({\cal Q}\zeta)_{|t=0}=\zeta^{0}\ \hbox{ on }\partial O.\end{array}\right.

(4.39)

Moreover, it satisfies the energy identity

{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}(\zeta):e_{y}(\zeta)dy+2\mu\int_{O}|e_{y}(\eta)|^{2}dy=0\ \hbox{ in }[0,\infty),

(4.40)

and there exist two constants
\lambda,\Lambda>0
such that

\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O})^{N}}+\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(O)^{N}}+\|q\|_{L^{2}(O)}\leq\Lambda e^{-\lambda t}\|\zeta^{0}\|_{H^{1\over 2}(\partial O)^{N}}\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty).

(4.41)

Proof.
We introduce the Hilbert space

{\cal H}=\big\{h\in H^{1\over 2}(\partial O)^{N}:\ {\cal P}h=0\big\}.

Then, we define
{\cal R}=({\cal R}_{1},{\cal R}_{2},{\cal R}_{3}):{\cal H}\to H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O})^{N}\times H^{1}(O)^{N}\times L^{2}(O)

in the following way:

For
h\in H
,
{\cal R}_{1}h
is the solution of the linear elasticity problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{\rm div}_{y}\big(Ae_{y}({\cal R}_{1}h)\big)=0\ \hbox{ in }Y\setminus\overline{O}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal R}_{1}h\hbox{ perodic in Y},\ Ae_{y}({\cal R}_{1}h)\nu\hbox{ anti-periodic in }Y\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal Q}{\cal R}_{1}h=h,\ {\cal P}\big(Ae_{y}({\cal R}_{1}h)\nu\big)=0\ \hbox{ on }\partial O,\quad\int_{\partial O}{\cal R}_{1}h\,d\sigma(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.42)

The existence and uniqueness of this problem follows from Lax-Milgram’s theorem writing it in the variational form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\cal R}_{1}h\in H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O}),\quad{\cal Q}{\cal R}_{1}h=h\ \hbox{ on }\partial O,\quad\int_{\partial O}{\cal R}_{1}h\,d\sigma(x)=0\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}({\cal R}_{1}h):e_{y}(\phi)dy=0,\quad\displaystyle\forall\,\phi\in H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O})^{N},\ {\cal Q}\phi=0\ \hbox{ on }\partial O.\end{array}\right.

The pair
\big({\cal R}_{2}h,{\cal R}_{3}h)
is defined as the unique solution of the Stokes problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-2\mu\,{\rm div}_{y}e_{y}\big({\cal R}_{2}h\big)+\nabla_{y}{\cal R}_{3}h=0\ \hbox{ in }O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{y}{\cal R}_{2}h=0\ \hbox{ in }O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\big(2\mu e_{y}\big({\cal R}_{2}h\big)-{\cal R}_{3}I\big)\nu=Ae_{y}\big({\cal R}_{1}h\big)\nu,\ {\cal P}{\cal R}_{2}h=0\quad\hbox{on }\partial O,\end{array}\right.

(4.43)

whose existence and uniqueness also follows from Lax-Milgram’s theorem.

Using these operators, we get that
(\zeta,\eta,q)
is a solution of (
4.39
) if and only
taking
h(t)={\cal Q}\zeta(t,.)_{|O}\in{\cal H},
we have

\zeta(t,.)={\cal R}_{1}h(t),\quad\eta(t,.)={\cal R}_{2}h(t),\quad q(t)={\cal R}_{3}h(t),\quad{dh\over dt}=({\cal R}_{2}h)_{|O},\quad h(0)=\zeta_{0}.

Therefore, defining
{\cal T}:{\cal H}\to{\cal H}
by

{\cal T}h=({\cal R}_{2}h)_{|O},

(4.44)

the problem is the existence of solution of the differential equation in
{\cal H}

{dh\over dt}={\cal T}h,\quad h(0)=\zeta_{0}.

This just follows from
{\cal T}
being a linear and continuous operator in
{\cal H}
. Thus, it defines a uniformly continuous semigroup
{\cal S}(t)
of bounded linear operators in
{\cal H}
by

{\cal S}(t)h=e^{t{\cal T}}h=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{t^{n}\over n!}{\cal T}^{n}h\ \hbox{ in }{\cal H},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

(4.45)

In order to prove (
4.40
) we just
use
\partial_{t}\zeta
as test function in the first equation in (
4.39
) and
\eta
as test function in the second one.

Let us now prove (
4.41
). For this purpose, we define
(\zeta^{l},q^{l})\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);H^{1}(O))^{N}\cap C^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(O)/\mathbb{R})^{N}
as the solution of the Stokes problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-2\mu\,{\rm div}_{y}e_{y}(\zeta^{l})+\nabla_{y}q^{l}=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty)\times O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{y}\zeta^{l}=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty)\times O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\zeta^{l}={\cal Q}\zeta\ \hbox{ on }(0,\infty)\times\partial O,\end{array}\right.

which satisfies

\int_{O}|e_{y}(\zeta^{l})|^{2}dy\leq C\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}|e_{y}(\zeta)|^{2}dy\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty).

for some
C
independent on
\zeta
. Using this inequality, taking
\zeta
as test function in the first equation in (
4.39
),
\zeta^{l}
as test function in the second one and adding the corresponding equalities we easily get

\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}(\zeta):e_{y}(\zeta)\,dy\leq C\int_{O}|e_{y}(\eta)|^{2}dy\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty).

Replaced in (
4.40
), this proves

{d\over dt}\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}(\zeta):e_{y}(\zeta)\,dy\leq-{2\mu\over C}\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}(\zeta):e_{y}(\zeta)dy\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty),

and then by Gronwall’s inequality

\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}(\zeta):e_{y}(\zeta)dy\leq e^{-{2\mu\over C}t}\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}(\zeta(0,y)):e_{y}(\zeta(0,y))dy\hbox{ in }(0,\infty).

(4.46)

Since
\zeta(0,y)={\cal R}_{1}\zeta_{0}
, we get

\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}Ae_{y}(\zeta(0,y)):e_{y}(\zeta(0,y))dy\leq C\|\zeta_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1\over 2}(\partial 0)^{N}}.

(4.47)

On the other hand, using that
(\eta,q)
satifies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-2\mu\,{\rm div}_{y}e_{y}(\eta)+\nabla q=0\ \hbox{ in }O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{y}\eta=0\ \hbox{ in }O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal P}\eta=0,\ \big(2\mu e_{y}(\eta)-qI\big)\nu=Ae_{y}(\zeta)\nu\ \hbox{ on }\partial O,\end{array}\right.\qquad\hbox{ in }(0,\infty),

we also have

\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(O)^{N}}+\|q\|_{L^{2}(O)}\leq C\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(Y\setminus\overline{O})^{N}}\ \hbox{ in }(0,\infty),

which combined with (
4.46
) and (
4.47
) proves (
4.41
).

Lemma 4.10
.

For every

,
there exits a unique solution
(\zeta,\eta,q)
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\zeta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O}))^{N},\ \ \eta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(O))^{N},\ \ q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(O))\\

\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{y}\eta=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\zeta\hbox{ periodic in }Y,\ A\big(E+e_{y}(\zeta)\big)\nu\hbox{ anti-periodic in }Y\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\cal Q}\big(Ey+\zeta\big)=\eta\ \hbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial O\\

\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle A\big(E+e_{y}(\zeta)\big)\nu=(2\mu\,e_{y}(\eta)-qI)\nu\ \hbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal Q}(Ey+\zeta)_{|t=0}=0\ \hbox{ on }\partial O.\end{array}\right.

(4.48)

Moreover, there exist a positive and symmetric tensor

with

\|S(t)\|_{{\cal L}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{s};\mathbb{R}^{N}_{s})}\leq\Lambda e^{-\lambda t},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

(4.49)

for some
\lambda,\Lambda>0
, such that

\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}A\big(E+e_{y}(\zeta)\big)dy+\int_{O}\big(2\mu e_{y}(\eta)-qI\big)dy=A_{h}E+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)E(s)\,ds\ \hbox{ in }(0,T),

(4.50)

and, assuming

in
W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s})

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\left(A_{h}E+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)E(s)\,ds\right):{dE\over dt}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle={1\over 2}{d\over dt}\int_{Y\setminus\overline{O}}A\big(E+e_{y}(\zeta)\big):\big(E+e_{y}(\zeta)\big)dy+2\mu\int_{O}|e_{y}(\eta)|^{2}dy.\end{array}

(4.51)

Proof.
Let us first assume that
E
is in
W^{1,1}(0,T)
. We define
Z:\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s}\to H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O})^{N}
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{\rm div}_{y}\big(Ae_{y}(Z\xi)\big)=0\ \hbox{ in }Y\setminus\overline{O}\\

(4.52)

Then, taking
\tilde{\zeta}=\zeta-ZE
, we get that (
4.48
) is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\tilde{\zeta}\in W^{1,1}(0,T;H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{O}))^{N},\ \ \eta\in W^{1,1}(0,T;H^{1}(O))^{N},\ \ q\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(O))\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle-{\rm div}_{y}\big(Ae_{y}(\tilde{\zeta})\big)=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times(Y\setminus\overline{O})\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle-2\mu\,{\rm div}_{y}e_{y}(\eta)+\nabla_{y}q=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\rm div}_{y}\eta=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times O\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\tilde{\zeta}\hbox{ periodic in }Y,\ Ae_{y}(\tilde{\zeta})\nu\hbox{ anti-periodic in }Y\\

\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal Q}(Ey+\tilde{\zeta}+ZE)_{|t=0}=0\ \hbox{ on }\partial O.\end{array}\right.

Using the operators

and then

just in

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2at}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}{dE_{T}\over ds}\Big|^{2}dt=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2at}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}{dE\over ds}\Big|^{2}dt+\Big|\int_{0}^{T}e^{s}{dE\over ds}\Big|^{2}\int_{T}^{\infty}e^{-(2a+1)t}dt\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2at}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}{dE\over ds}ds\Big|^{2}dt+{e^{-2aT}\over 2a+1}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}e^{-(T-s)}{dE\over ds}ds\Big|^{2}.\end{array}

Therefore (
4.76
) implies

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2at}\Big(A_{h}E+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)E(S)ds\Big){dE\over dt}\,dt\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\geq ca\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2at}|E(t)|^{2}dt+{ce^{-2aT}\over 2}|E(T)|^{2}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle+c\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2at}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}{dE\over ds}ds\Big|^{2}dt+c{e^{-2aT}\over 2a+1}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}e^{-(T-s)}{dE\over ds}ds\Big|^{2}.\end{array}

Taking
a
tending to zero in this inequality we conclude (
4.64
).
\Box

Proposition 4.15
.

Assume that
O
is a ball and that
A
is isotropic. Then problem (
4.61
) has no solution for any
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}_{s}
,
\xi\not=0
,
k\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.
Assume that
O
agrees with the ball
B_{R}
of center
0
and radius
R\in(0,1/2)
and that there exists
\lambda^{s}\geq 0,\mu^{s}>0
such that (
3.3
) holds. Reasoning by contradiction, we also assume that there exists
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}_{s}
,
\xi\not=0
,
k\in\mathbb{C}
,
w_{\xi}\in H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y\setminus\overline{B_{R}})^{N}
solution of (
4.61
). The condition
{\cal Q}(\xi y+w_{\xi})=0
on
\partial B_{R}
means that there exists
a\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
skew-symmetric, such that
\xi y+w_{\xi}=a+By
on
\partial B_{R}
. Then, the function

\phi(y)=\xi y+w_{\xi}-a-By,\quad y\in Y\setminus\overline{B_{R}},

satisfies the conditions

-{\rm div}_{y}\big(Ae_{y}(\phi)\big)=0\ \hbox{ in }Y\setminus\overline{B_{R}},\quad\phi=0,\ Ae_{y}(\phi)\nu=k\nu\ \hbox{ on }\partial B_{R}.

(4.77)

Using polar coordinates, we can construct another solution of (
4.77
) as

\psi(y)=h(|y|)y,

with
h
the unique solution of the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{d\over dr}\Big(r^{N+1}(\lambda+2\mu){dh\over dr}+r^{N}(\lambda N+2\mu)h\Big)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+r^{N}(\lambda N+2\mu){dh\over dr}+r^{N-1}N(N\lambda+2\mu)h=0\ \hbox{ in }(0,R),\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle h(R)=0,\quad(2\mu+\lambda)Rh^{\prime}(R)+(\lambda N+2\mu)h(R)=k,\end{array}\right.

Thus,
\phi-\psi
solves

-{\rm div}_{y}\big(Ae_{y}(\phi-\psi)\big)=0\ \hbox{ in }Y\setminus\overline{B_{R}},\quad\phi-\psi=0,\ Ae_{y}(\phi-\psi)\nu=r\nu\ \hbox{ on }\partial B_{R}.

By unique continuation we conclude that
\phi-\psi
vanishes on

and then

w_{\xi}=-\xi y+a+By+h(|y|)y\ \hbox{ in }Y\setminus\overline{B_{R}},

but then

is not periodic in

Proposition 4.16
.

Assume
A_{h},B
two positive symmetric tensors in
{\cal L}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}_{s})
and
\gamma>0
such that

\gamma A_{h}-B\ \hbox{ is }positive.

(4.78)

Then,
A_{h}
and
S(t):=-Be^{-\gamma t},
satisfy (
4.62
) and (
4.63
),
with
LS
the Laplace transform of
S
.

Proof.
Taking into account that

LS(a+ib)=-{1\over\gamma+a+ib}B=-{\gamma+a-ib\over(\gamma+a)^{2}+b^{2}}B,\quad\forall\,a>0,\ b\in\mathbb{R},

we have

(a-ib)(A_{h}+LS(a+ib))=aA_{h}-{a(\gamma+a)-b^{2}\over(\gamma+a)^{2}+b^{2}}B-bA_{h}i+{b\gamma\over(\gamma+a)^{2}+b^{2}}Bi

and thus

Re\big((a-ib)(A_{h}+LS(a+ib))\big)=a\Big(A_{h}-{\gamma+2a\over(a+\gamma)^{2}+b^{2}}B\Big)+{a^{2}+b^{2}\over(a+\gamma)^{2}+b^{2}}B

b\,Im\big((a-ib)(A_{h}+LS(a+ib))\big)=-b^{2}\Big(A_{h}-{\gamma\over(\gamma+a)^{2}+b^{2}}B\Big)

Since

\max_{a\geq 0,\,b\in\mathbb{R}}{\gamma+2a\over(\gamma+a)^{2}+b^{2}}={1\over\gamma},

and (
4.78
) holds, there exists
c>0
such that

\Big(A_{h}-{\gamma\over(\gamma+a)^{2}+b^{2}}B\Big)\xi\cdot\overline{\xi}\geq\Big(A_{h}-{\gamma+2a\over(\gamma+a)^{2}+b^{2}}B\Big)\xi\cdot\overline{\xi}\geq c|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}_{s},

which combined with the positivity of
B
proves (
4.62
) and (
4.63
).
\Box

Remark 4.17
.

For
A_{h},B,\gamma
In the conditions of Proposition
4.16
and
\rho>0
, we remark that problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\rho\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}\Big(A_{h}e_{x}(u)-\int_{0}^{t}Be^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,e_{x}(u)ds\Big)=f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u=0\ \hbox{ on }\partial\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u_{|t=0}=u^{0},\ (\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}=u^{1}\ \hbox{ in }\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(4.79)

satisfies an energy conservation law. For this purpose, it is better to integrate by parts the integral term in the equation to get

\rho\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}\Big(\Big(A_{h}-{1\over\gamma}B\Big)e_{x}(u)+{1\over\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}Be^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,e_{x}(\partial_{s}u)ds\Big)=f+{e^{-\gamma t}\over\gamma}{\rm div}_{x}\big(Be_{x}(u^{0})\big)\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

which taking

w=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}u\,ds,

allows us to write the differential equation as the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\rho\partial^{2}_{tt}u-{\rm div}_{x}\Big(\Big(A_{h}-{1\over\gamma}B\Big)e_{x}(u)+{1\over\gamma}Be_{x}(\partial_{t}w)\Big)=f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+\gamma w=u\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.80)

Multiplying (formally) the first equation by
\partial_{t}u
and integrating in
\Omega
we get

{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\rho|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\Big(A_{h}-{1\over\gamma}B\Big)e_{x}(u):e_{x}(u)\Big)dx+{1\over\gamma}\int_{\Omega}Be_{x}(\partial_{t}w):e_{x}(\partial_{t}u)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\partial_{t}u\,dx,

where thanks to the second equation in (
4.80
), we have

\int_{\Omega}Be_{x}(\partial_{t}w):e_{x}(\partial_{t}u)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}Be_{x}(\partial_{t}w):e_{x}(\partial^{2}_{tt}w)\,dx+\gamma\int_{\Omega}Be_{x}(\partial_{t}w):e_{x}(\partial_{t}w)\,,dx.

Therefore, we get the energy identity

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\rho|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\Big(A_{h}-{1\over\gamma}B\Big)e_{x}(u):e_{x}(u)+{1\over\gamma}Be_{x}(\partial_{t}w):e_{x}(\partial_{t}w)\Big)dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega}Be_{x}(\partial_{t}w):e_{x}(\partial_{t}w)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\partial_{t}u\,dx.\end{array}

Assuming
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))^{N},

u^{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{N}
,
u^{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}
, this allows us in particular to deduce the existence of a unique solution
u
for (
4.79
) in
W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))^{N}\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{N}
.

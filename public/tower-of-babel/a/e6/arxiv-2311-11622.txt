Functional relative error regression under left truncation and right censoring
A. Boucetta, Z. Guessoum, E. Ould-Said

Abstract
The nonparametric estimators built by minimizing the mean squared relative error are gaining in popularity for their robustness in the presence of outliers in comparison to the Nadaraya Watson estimators. In this paper we build a relative error regression function estimator in the case of a functional explanatory variable and a left truncated and right censored scalar variable. The pointwise and uniform convergence of the estimator is proved and its performance is assessed by a numerical study in particularly the robustness which is highlighted using the influence function as a measure of robustness.

1
Introduction

Functional data is a generalization of the multivariate data concept (vectors) to infinite dimension. This type of data is obtained when an observed phenomenon varies over a continuum set which is often the case in fields such as physics, chemistry (spectrometric analysis), health (ECG data for example), …

To exploit the richness of information brought by the infinite dimension structure of the data, a set of statistical tools is needed. Functional data analysis (FDA) is a statistical field that deals with the treatment and analysis of this particular type of data, it has been popularized mostly by the monographs of
Ramsay and Silverman 2005
and
Ferraty and Vieu 2006
. The work of
Ramsay and Silverman 2005
is a good introduction to the practical aspects of FDA and to parametric functional models while the work of
Ferraty and Vieu 2006
focuses on the nonparametric methods applied to functional data and is considered as a major reference on the topic of nonparametric functional regression which is the approach on which this work is based. For a recent account of the theory of FDA we refer the interested reader to
Hsing and Eubank 2015
.

In a scalar on functional regression framework the task is to model the link between a real random variable denoted by
Y
and a functional explanatory variable
\boldsymbol{\chi}
taking its values in
(\mathcal{F},d)
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a semi metric space and
d
its corresponding semi metric, such that
Y=r(\boldsymbol{\chi})+\epsilon,

where
r(\cdot)
is the regression operator mapping
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon
an error random variable. It is worth noting that in some practical cases, the variable of interest
Y
, can not be fully observed. When we deal with time-to-event data as in survival analysis, the lifetime may be left truncated and/or right censored. Right censoring occurs when for some individuals, the event of interest is observed only if it happens prior to some specified times and these times vary from an individual to another. We have left truncation when only the individuals who experience the event of interest after a certain delayed entry time are included in the study
(
Klein and Moeschberger 2003
)
.
Usually
r
is estimated by minimizing the least squares criterion. The estimations obtained then are sensitive to outliers since it assumes that all the variables have an equal weight, which makes them inadequate in some situations with large outliers. To overcome this limitation we shall use the following mean quadratic relative error as a loss function:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{Y-r(\boldsymbol{\chi})}{Y}\right)^{2}\mid\boldsymbol{\chi}\right]\text{, for }Y>0.

(1)

Park and Stefanski 1998
have shown that the minimizer of (
1
) is given by:

r(\chi)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{-1}\mid\boldsymbol{\chi}=\chi\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{-2}\mid\boldsymbol{\chi}=\chi\right]}=:\frac{r_{1}(\chi)}{r_{2}(\chi)},

(2)

where
r_{1}(\chi)
and
r_{2}(\chi)
are assumed finite almost surely. The first nonparametric estimator of equation (
2
) was proposed by
Jones et al. 2008
in the case when the explanatory variable is a real variable while in the functional case we refer to
Demongeot et al. 2016
. Research that treats nonparametric functional data analysis (or NPFDA) and the relative error regression estimator with incomplete data includes but not limited to
Altendji et al. 2018
,
Mechab et al. 2019
and
Fetitah et al. 2020
.

The aim of this paper is to bring together the areas of NPFDA, robust estimation and survival analysis by establishing the pointwise and the uniform convergence with rates of the constructed estimator that takes into account both right-censoring and left-truncation of the variable of interest and the functional character of the covariable and highlighting its asymptotic properties and robustness.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the LTRC model and its components. Section
3
discusses the construction of the proposed estimator. In Section
4
and Section
5
the main results and their assumptions are stated while the proofs are relegated to Section
7
. Section
6
is devoted to a deep simulation study of the performance and robustness of the relative error regression estimator using the influence function.

2
LTRC model

Let
(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1,\cdots,N}
be an
N
independent identically distributed (iid) sample of the pair
(\boldsymbol{\chi},Y)
with values in
{\cal F}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
where the sample size
N
is deterministic but unknown and
Y
represents a lifetime variable with continuous distribution function
F
.

In an LTRC model the variable of interest is subject to a left truncation and a right censoring. The right censoring is induced by a random variable
S
supposed strictly positive with continuous distribution function denoted by
G
, that is, we observe
(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i},Z_{i},\delta_{i})
where
Z_{i}=\min\{Y_{i},S_{i}\}
and
\delta_{i}=\mathds{1}_{\{Y_{i}\leq S_{i}\}}
the indicator of non censoring, meanwhile, the left truncation is due to the random variable
T
, that is, we observe
Z_{i}
only if
Z_{i}\geq T_{i}
. We denote by
L
the distribution function of
T
and by
\alpha=\mathbb{P}(Z_{i}\geq T_{i})
the probability of absence of truncation which is assumed strictly positive (if not then nothing is observed). So ultimately one observes the quadruple
(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i},Z_{i},T_{i},\delta_{i})

i=1,...,n
with
n\leq N
if there is no confusion in regard to the index
i
, note that conditionally on the value of
n
the observed data are still iid.

We assume that the random variables
Z,T,S
are mutually independent and it follows that the distribution function of
Z
is given by
H=1-(1-F)(1-G)
. Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the probability measure associated to the original
N
sample and
\mathbf{P}
the probability measure related to the sample observed conditionally on the event
\{Z_{i}\geq T_{i}\}
, also we denote by
\mathbb{E}
and
\mathbf{E}
their respective associated expectation operators.

Let
C(y)=\mathbf{P}(T\leq y\leq Z)=\mathbf{L}(y)-\mathbf{H}(y)
, where
\mathbf{L}(y)
,
\mathbf{H}(y)
and
C(y)
can be estimated empirically by

\mathbf{L}_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq y\}}\;\;\text{, }\;\mathbf{H}_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{Z_{i}\leq y\}}\;\text{and }\;C_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq y\leq Z_{i}\}}.

In a LTRC model the distribution function
F
is estimated by the product limit estimator given in
Tsai et al. 1987
called the TJW estimator defined as
F_{n}(y)=1-\prod_{Z_{i}\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{nC_{n}(Z_{i})}\right)^{\delta_{i}}.

Note that
C(y)
can be rewritten as

C(y)=\mathbb{P}(T\leq y\leq Z\mid Z\geq T)=\alpha^{-1}L(y)\bar{H}(y)=\alpha^{-1}L(y)\bar{F}(y)\bar{G}(y),

which implies

\alpha=\frac{L(y)\bar{F}(y)\bar{G}(y)}{C(y)}.

(3)

He and Yang 1998
showed that a consistent estimator of
\alpha
is given by

\alpha_{n}=\dfrac{L_{n}(y)\bar{F}_{n}(y)\bar{G}_{n}(y)}{C_{n}(y)}\text{ , for any $y$ such that }C_{n}(y)\neq 0,

(4)

where
L_{n}(y)
is the
Lynden-Bell 1971
product limit estimator of the distribution function L defined as
L_{n}(y)=\prod_{T_{i}>y}\left(1-\frac{1}{nC_{n}(T_{i})}\right)
and
G_{n}(y)
a TJW-type estimator of the distribution function
G
defined by
G_{n}(y)=1-\prod_{Z_{i}\leq y}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{nC_{n}(Z_{i})}\right)^{1-\delta_{i}}.

Remark 1
.

He and Yang 1998
showed that the value of
\alpha_{n}
does not depend on the value of
y
.

For any distribution function W denote by
a_{W}=\sup\{y:W(y)>0\}
and
b_{K}=\inf\{y:W(y)<1\}
its support endpoints. Following
Gijbels and Wang 1993
the distribution function
F
is identifiable, in a LTRC setting, only under some conditions on the support of
L
and
H
(
a_{L}\leq a_{H}
and
b_{L}\leq b_{H}
). We assume that
a_{L}<a_{H}
,
b_{L}\leq b_{H}
and that for some
b>0
,
b<b_{H}
. Furthermore we suppose that
(T,S)
and
(\boldsymbol{\chi},\;Y)
are independent.

3
Construction of the estimator

Under a LTRC setting, one has to account for both left truncation and right censoring effect and by combining the ideas of
Altendji et al. 2018
and
Mechab et al. 2019
, we propose the following mean relative error regression estimator:

\displaystyle\widehat{r}_{n}(\chi)=\displaystyle\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\displaystyle\delta_{i}K(\displaystyle\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})Z_{i}^{-1}}{L_{n}(Z_{i})\bar{G_{n}}(Z_{i})}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\displaystyle\delta_{i}K\displaystyle(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})Z_{i}^{-2}}{L_{n}(Z_{i})\bar{G_{n}}(Z_{i})}},

(5)

where
K
is a kernel and
h\coloneqq h_{n}
is a sequence of positive real numbers. For technical reasons, we rewrite (
5
) as

\displaystyle\widehat{r}_{n}(\chi)=\displaystyle\dfrac{\displaystyle\hat{r}_{1,n}(\chi)}{\hat{r}_{2,n}(\chi)},

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widehat{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{n\mathbb{E}\Big[K\displaystyle\Big(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h}\Big)\Big]}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\delta_{i}K\big(\displaystyle\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h}\big)Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{L_{n}(Z_{i})\bar{G_{n}}(Z_{i})}

for
\ell=1,\;2.

4
Pointwise almost sure convergence

Let
\chi
denote an element in
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\chi}
its neighborhood, for
\rho>0
we denote by
B(\chi,\rho)
the closed ball with radius
\rho
and
\chi
its center that is
B(\chi,\rho)=\{\chi^{\prime}:d(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\leq\rho\}
. For easy writing we shall denote by
C
,
C^{\prime}

c
,… any positive constant whose value can change from time to time, in absence of confusion.

In what follows, we suppose that the next assumptions hold true:

(H1)
.

\forall h>0
,
\phi_{\chi}(h)=\mathbb{P}\big(\boldsymbol{\chi}\in B(\chi,h)\big)>0
and
\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0}\phi_{\chi}(h)=0
.

(H2)
.

\forall(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\mathcal{N}_{\chi}\times\mathcal{N}_{\chi}
and for some
k_{\ell}>0
we have
\mid r_{\ell}(\chi_{1})-r_{\ell}(\chi_{2})\mid\leq Cd^{k_{\ell}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\text{, }\ \ \ell=1,2.

(H3)
.

The kernel K is a measurable function with support
\left]0,1\right[
and satisfies:

0<C\mathds{1}_{\left[0,1\right]}(.)\leq K(.)\leq C^{\prime}\mathds{1}_{\left[0,1\right]}(.)<\infty.

(H4)
.

The bandwidth
h
is such that
\lim_{n\longrightarrow+\infty}h=0\text{ and }\lim_{n\longrightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{\log(n)}{n\phi_{\chi}(h)}=0.

(H5)
.

For any
m\geq 2
, the conditional moments of
Y^{-m}
satisfy
\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{-m}\mid\boldsymbol{\chi}=\chi\right]<C<\infty.

Remark 2
.

Assumption
(H1)
is a classic hypothesis in functional data analysis it means that the probability to have
\boldsymbol{\chi}
, around the element at which the regression operator is evaluated, is non null. Assumption
(H2)
is used to study the bias term of our estimator while the assumptions
(H3)
-
(H5)
are technical conditions needed to prove our result.

The following theorem gives the pointwise almost sure convergence with rate of the proposed estimator
\hat{r}_{n}(\chi)
towards
r(\chi)
.

Theorem 1
.

Under assumptions
(H1)
-
(H5)
, we have:

\mid\hat{r}_{n}(\chi)-r(\chi)\mid=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{k_{1}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(h^{k_{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(n)}{n\phi_{\chi}(h)}}\right)\text{ a.s}.

Remark 3
.

Our stated rate of convergence is the same as the one obtained by
Demongeot et al. 2016
in the complete data case and by
(
Mechab et al. 2019
)
and
(
Altendji et al. 2018
)
respectively for the case where the data is subject to censoring or truncation.

5
Uniform almost sure convergence

In this section we establish the uniform almost sure convergence of our estimator over some subset
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
of
\mathcal{F}
. In odrer to do so, it is necessary to make some additional topological assumptions since in the functional case, the uniform consistency is not a straightforward generalization of the pointwise convergence (see
Ferraty et al. 2010
).

(H6)
.

There exists a function
\phi(.)
, such that:
\forall\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
,
\forall h>0
,
0<C\phi(h)<\mathbb{P}({\chi}\in B(\chi,h))<C^{\prime}\phi(h)<\infty
.

(H7)
.

There exists
\beta>0
such that
\forall\;\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\beta},\quad\mid r_{\ell}(\chi_{1})-r_{\ell}(\chi_{2})\mid\leq Cd^{k_{\ell}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),
where

\mathcal{S}^{\beta}_{\mathcal{F}}=\left\{\chi\in\mathcal{F},\exists\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\text{ such that }d\left(\chi,\chi^{\prime}\right)\leq\beta\right\}
.

(H8)
.

The kernel K is bounded and Lipschitzian on
\left[0,1\right]
.

(H9)
.

The functions
\phi(.)
and Kolmogorov’s
\epsilon
-entropy
\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}(.)
of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
are such that:

•

There exist
\eta_{0}
such that
\forall\eta<\eta_{0}
,
\phi^{\prime}\left(\eta_{0}\right)<C
.

•

For
n
large enough, we have
\frac{\left(\log n\right)^{2}}{n\phi\left(h\right)}<\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)<\frac{n\phi\left(h\right)}{\log n}.

•

Kolmogorov’s
\epsilon
-entropy
\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}(.)
of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
verifies that
\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(\left(1-\gamma\right)\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\right)<\infty,

for at least one
\gamma>1
.

(H10)
.

For any
m\geq 2
,
\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{-m}\mid\boldsymbol{\chi}=\chi\right]\leq C<\infty
for all
\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\inf\limits_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}r_{2}(\chi)\geq C^{\prime}>0
.

Remark 4
.

Assumptions
(H6)
,
(H7)
,
(H10)
are the same considered by
Demongeot et al. 2016
and they represent a reformulation of assumptions
(H1)
,
(H2)
,
(H5)
in the uniform consistency context, while Assumption
(H9)
controls the Kolmogorov’s
\epsilon
-entropy of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
and ensures that
\dfrac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)}{n\phi(h)}
tends to
0
when
n\longrightarrow\infty

(
Ferraty et al. 2010
)
.

Theorem 2
.

Let Assumptions
(H6)
-
(H10)
hold. Then we have:

\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\mid\hat{r}_{n}(\chi)-r(\chi)\mid=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{k_{1}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(h^{k_{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)}{n\phi\left(h\right)}}\right)\text{ a.s}.

Remark 5
.

The rate of uniform convergence obtained is equivalent to the uniform rate in the complete data framework from
Demongeot et al. 2016
and to the one stated in the censoring case in the work of
Fetitah et al. 2020
. To our knowledge, no research paper has yet dealt with the uniform convergence in a relative error functional regression context when the variable of interest is left truncated.

6.2
Influence function

In this part we shall investigate the robustness of the relative error estimator in the presence of an atypical observation in the data using the influence function (IF).
The influence function measures the effect induced by outliers on an estimate. Note that an estimator can be written as a functional of the distribution function
F
of the data for some functional
\mathcal{T}
(see
Maronna et al. 2019
and the examples within) that is
\mathcal{T}\left(F\right)
. For an extra data point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, define
F_{\epsilon}=\left(1-\epsilon\right)F+\epsilon\Delta_{z_{0}}
, which represents the distribution
F
contaminated by a small fraction
\epsilon
of outliers with
\Delta_{z_{0}}
being the Dirac mass at the added point
z_{0}
. Hence, the influence function defined by:

IF\left(z_{0},\mathcal{T}\left(F\right)\right)=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{T}\left(F_{\epsilon}\right)-\mathcal{T}\left(F\right)}{\epsilon},

represents the sensitivity of
\mathcal{T}\left(F\right)
to
z_{0}
. From now on, the functional
\mathcal{T}
is taken to be
\widehat{r}_{RER}
or
\widehat{r}_{NW}\left(\chi\right)
. The influence function can be seen as an asymptotic version of the standardized sensitivity curve (SSC) defined by

SSC_{n}\left(z_{0},\hat{r}_{n}\left(\chi_{0}\right)\right)=\left(n+1\right)\left(\widehat{r}_{n+1}\left(\chi_{0};z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{0}\right)-\widehat{r}_{n}\left(\chi_{0};z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\right)\right)

where
\chi_{0}
is a fixed curve in
\mathcal{F}
and
\widehat{r}_{n+1}\left(\chi_{0};z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{0}\right)
(resp.
\widehat{r}_{n}\left(\chi_{0};z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\right)
)is the estimator built using the (n+1)-sample
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{0}
(resp. the (n)-sample
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{0}
) evaluated at
\chi_{0}
. Indeed, if we add the extra data point
z_{0}
to the n-sample
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}
therefore the fraction of contamination
\epsilon
is equal to
\frac{1}{n+1}
. Then define the empirical influence function as
EIF_{\widehat{r}_{n}}(\chi)=SSC_{n}(z_{0},\widehat{r}_{n}\left(\chi\right))
. Note that for a robust estimator (meaning that an outlier has a very limited effect on the estimator) the empirical influence function must be close to 0.

\mid\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))-r_{\ell}(\chi)\mid=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{k_{\ell}}\right).

Proof.

We have that
\mid\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))-r_{\ell}(\chi)\mid=\left|\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{E}(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})}{h}))}\mathbf{E}\left(\dfrac{\alpha\delta_{1}K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})}{h})Z_{1}^{-\ell}}{L(Z_{1})\bar{G}(Z_{1})}\right)-r_{\ell}(\chi)\right|,
and we can show that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\dfrac{\alpha\delta_{1}K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})}{h})Z_{1}^{-\ell}}{L(Z_{1})\bar{G}(Z_{1})}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})}{h})r_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})\right).

(8)

Hence and under Assumption
(H2)
we obtain:

\displaystyle\mid\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))-r_{\ell}(\chi)\mid\leq\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{E}(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})}{h}))}\mathbb{E}\left[K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})}{h})Cd^{k_{\ell}}(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})\right]\leq Ch^{k_{\ell}}.

∎

Lemma 2
.

Under assumptions
(H1)
,
(H3)
-
(H5)
we have for
\ell=1,2
:

\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))\mid=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(n)}{n\phi_{\chi}(h)}}\right)\text{ a.s}.

Proof.

Set
\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{1})}{h})\right)}\left[\frac{\alpha\delta_{i}K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}-\mathbf{E}\left(\frac{\alpha\delta_{i}K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}\right)\right]=:\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}W_{i,\ell}
.
We use an exponential type inequality given in Corrolary A8.ii in
Ferraty and Vieu 2006
, and for that we need to evaluate
\mathbf{E}(\mid W_{i,\ell}^{m}\mid)
for
m\geq 2
. Assumptions
(H3)
,
(H5)
and the binomial expansion formula allow to show that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(\mid W_{i,\ell}^{m}\mid)

\displaystyle\leq\max\limits_{k\in\{0,1,2,...,m\}}\left\{\binom{m}{k}C^{m-k}\right\}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\mathbf{E}\left|\frac{\left(\frac{\alpha\delta_{i}K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}\right)}{\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{)}}{h})\right)}\right|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\limits_{k\in\{0,1,2,...,m\}}\phi_{\chi}^{-k+1}(h)\leq C\phi_{\chi}^{-m+1}(h).

We apply the mentioned inequality with
a=\phi_{\chi}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(h)
and
u_{n}=\frac{a^{2}\log(n)}{n}
, and we obtain by setting
\epsilon=\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{u_{n}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))\mid>\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{u_{n}}\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i,\ell}\right|>\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{u_{n}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{2}\log(n)}{2(1+\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{u_{n}})}\right)\leq 2n^{-C\epsilon_{0}}.

An appropriate choice of
\epsilon_{0}
and Borel-Cantelli’s lemma allow us to conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Under assumptions
(H1)
,
(H3)
-
(H5)
we have for
\ell=1,2
:

\mid\widehat{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)\mid=\mathcal{O}\left((n^{-1}\log\log(n))^{1/2})\right)\text{ a.s}.

Proof.

When replacing
\alpha,\alpha_{n}
by their expressions (
3
) and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\mid\widehat{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)\mid

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\Bigg|\frac{\bar{F}_{n}(t)}{C_{n}(t)}-\frac{\bar{F}(t)}{C(t)}\Bigg|\frac{1}{n\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{)}}{h})\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\Bigg|\frac{\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid F_{n}(t)-F(t)\mid}{\inf\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid C(t)\mid-\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid C_{n}(t)-C(t)\mid}+\frac{\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid C_{n}(t)-C(t)\mid}{\inf\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid C(t)\mid(\inf\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid C(t)\mid-\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid C_{n}(t)-C(t)\mid)}\Bigg|}_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}

\displaystyle\times\underbrace{\frac{1}{n\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{)}}{h})\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}}_{\mathcal{I}_{2}}=\mathcal{I}_{1}\times\mathcal{I}_{2}.

On the one hand by Corollary 1 in
Gijbels and Wang 1993
we have that

\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid F_{n}(t)-F(t)\mid=\mathcal{O}\left((n^{-1}\log\log(n))^{1/2})\right)
. Also from Lemma 3.3 in
Zhou and Yip 1999
we have
\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}\mid C_{n}(t)-C(t)\mid=\mathcal{O}\left((n^{-1}\log\log(n))^{1/2})\right)
a.s, and using the fact that
C(t)>0
so
\exists

\beta>0
such that
C(t)\geq\beta>0
we obtain:

\mathcal{I}_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left((n^{-1}\log\log(n))^{1/2})\right)\text{ a.s}.

(9)

On the other hand by stationarity we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{n\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{)}}{h})\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}-\mathbf{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}\right)\right]}_{\mathcal{I}_{2.1}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{)}}{h})\right)}\mathbf{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h})\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}\right)}_{\mathcal{I}_{2.2}}.

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
2
one can show that
\mathcal{I}_{2.1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(n)}{n\phi_{\chi}(h)}}\right)
a.s. Next,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2.2}\leq\frac{\alpha^{-1}}{\mathbb{E}\left(K(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{)}}{h})\right)}\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}L(t)\bar{G}(t)\iint K(\frac{d(\chi,u)}{h})y^{-\ell}f_{Y\mid\boldsymbol{\chi}}(y\mid u)dyd\mathbb{P}^{\boldsymbol{\chi}}(u)\leq\alpha^{-1}\sup\limits_{a_{H}\leq t\leq b}L(t)\bar{G}(t)C.

Then
\mathcal{I}_{2.2}=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
,
so
\mathcal{I}_{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\text{ a.s}
. We conclude that
\mid\widehat{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)\mid=\mathcal{O}\left((n^{-1}\log\log(n))^{1/2})\right)\text{ a.s}.

∎

Theorem
1
is proved.
\square

Lemma 6
.

Under assumptions
(H6)
,
(H8)
-
(H10)
we have for
\ell=1,2
:

\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))\mid=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right)\text{ a.s}.

Proof.

Let
\chi_{1}
,
\chi_{2}
,
\cdots
,
\chi_{N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})}
a finite set of elements in
\mathcal{F}
where
N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})
represents the minimal number of open balls in
\mathcal{F}
of radius
\epsilon
needed to cover
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
, that is

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})}B(\chi_{k},\epsilon)\text{ and we take }\epsilon=\frac{\log n}{n}.

Set
k(\chi)=arg\min\limits_{k\in\left\{1,2,\cdots,N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})\right\}}d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{k})
for all
\chi\in\mathcal{F}
and
K_{i}\left(\chi\right):=K\left(\frac{d(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i})}{h}\right)
.
We have

\displaystyle\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))\mid

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\mid+\underbrace{\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})-\mathbf{E}\left(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\right)}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\mid

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\mid\mathbf{E}\left(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\right)-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi))}_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\mid.

In order to achieve the proof we must evaluate the convergence rate of each term
\mathcal{L}_{j}
for
j=1,2,3
. So

\mathcal{L}_{1}\leq\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\dfrac{\alpha\delta_{i}K_{i}(\chi)Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{\mathbb{E}\left(K_{i}(\chi)\right)L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}-\dfrac{\alpha\delta_{i}K_{i}(\chi_{k(\chi)})Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{\mathbb{E}\left(K_{i}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\right)L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}\right|

By combining assumptions
(H6)
and
(H8)
we get
\forall\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\text{, }0<C\phi\left(h\right)<\mathbb{E}\left(K_{i}(\chi)\right)<C^{\prime}\phi\left(h\right),

then:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{c}{n\phi(h)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|K_{i}(\chi)-K_{i}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\right|\times\frac{\alpha\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}\leq\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{c}{nh\phi(h)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|d\left(\chi,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i}\right)-d\left(\chi_{i},\chi_{k(\chi)}\right)\right|\times\frac{\alpha\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{c}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\epsilon\alpha\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-\ell}}{h\phi(h)L(Z_{i})\bar{G}(Z_{i})}=:\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{c}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}.

Next, we evaluate
\mathbf{E}\left(F_{i}^{m}\right)
,
m\geq 2
, for applying an exponential type inequality for unbounded random variables (see Corollary A8 in
Ferraty and Vieu 2006
). We have under assumption
(H10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(F_{i}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{h^{m}\phi(h)^{m}}\mathbf{E}\left(\frac{\alpha^{m}\delta_{i}Z_{i}^{-m\ell}}{L(Z_{i})^{m}\bar{G}(Z_{i})^{m}}\right)=\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{h^{m}\phi(h)^{m}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\alpha^{m-1}Y^{-m\ell}}{L(Y)^{m-1}\bar{G}(Y)^{m-1}}\right)\leq\frac{C\epsilon^{m}}{h^{m}\phi(h)^{m}}.

We apply the fore-mentioned exponential inequality with

a^{2}=\dfrac{\epsilon}{h\phi(h)}=\frac{\log n}{nh\phi(h)}
and Assumption
(H9)
to get

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left((\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)}{n\phi(h)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

.

For the term
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, and for all
\eta>0
we have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{L}_{2}>\eta(\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}(\epsilon)}{n\phi(h)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})\max\limits_{k(\chi)\in\left\{1,2,\cdots,N_{\epsilon}\right\}}\mathbf{P}\left(\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)}))\mid>\eta\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right).

By using the same arguments as in Lemma
2
(taking
a^{2}=\phi(h)^{-m+1}
) we can show that:

\mathbf{P}\left(\left|\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})-\mathbf{E}\left(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\right)\right|>\eta\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\eta^{2}\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)\right).

We have
\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)=\log N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})
as defined in
Ferraty et al. 2010
then:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\left|\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})-\mathbf{E}\left(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\right)\right|>\eta\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-C\eta^{2}\log N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})\right)\leq 2N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})^{-C\eta^{2}},

it follows that:

\displaystyle N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})\max\limits_{k(\chi)\in\{1,2,\cdots,N_{\epsilon}\}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})-\mathbf{E}\left(\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\right)\mid>\eta\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right)\leq 2N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})^{1-C\eta^{2}}.

It is sufficient to take
\eta=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{C}}
which under assumption
(H9)
and by Borel-Cantelli’s lemma gives:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{L}_{2}>\eta\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}})^{1-\gamma}<\infty\Rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right)

Regarding the term
\mathcal{L}_{3}
it is obvious by Jensen’s inequality that:

\mathcal{L}_{3}\leq\mathbf{E}\left(\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\mid\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi)-\tilde{r}_{\ell,n}(\chi_{k(\chi)})\mid\right),

then the proof for the term
\mathcal{L}_{1}
remains valid for showing that

\mathcal{L}_{3}=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{\psi_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\epsilon\right)}{n\phi(h)}}\right)

since
\epsilon=\frac{\log n}{n}
, this concludes the proof of Lemma
6
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
2
is now complete.
\square

On the properties of multidimensional electrostatic oscillations of an electron plasma
Olga S. Rozanova

Abstract
. We consider the classical Cauchy problem for a system of equations describing 3D arbitrary electrostatic oscillations of the cold plasma and introduce an iteration procedure that allows estimating the blow-up time from below. This procedure is constructive provided one succeed to obtain a two-sided estimate of an additional quantity depending on the solution. We show that this is possible in the case of one and two dimensions, as well as for solutions with zero vorticity. For the particular case of two-dimensional initial data with the radial symmetry, refined sufficient conditions for destruction and preservation of smoothness in the first period of oscillations are obtained. Moreover, we give the example of estimating the blow-up time for the data for which results of numerics exist and discuss a roughness of our estimate.

3.2.
Globally smooth affine solutions

Definition 3
.

A solution
{\bf V},{\bf E}
is called an affine solution if it has the form

(17)

{\bf V}={\mathcal{Q}}(t){\bf r},\quad{\bf E}={\mathcal{R}}(t){\bf r},

with
(3\times 3)

{\mathcal{Q}}=(q_{ij})
and
{\mathcal{R}}=(r_{ij})
.

Condition

5

,

, so we get
the matrix equation

\dot{\mathcal{Q}}+{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}+{\mathcal{R}}=0,\quad\dot{\mathcal{R}}=(1-{\rm tr}{\mathcal{R}}){\mathcal{Q}}

or quadratically

For the plain oscillations
q_{3j}=q_{i3}=r_{3j}=r_{i3}=0
,

, so the number of equations is 6.

Definition 4
.

A solution
{\bf V},{\bf E}
is called radially symmetric if

{\bf V}=F(t,r){\bf r},\,{\bf E}=G(t,r){\bf r},\,{\bf r}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),\,r=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}}.

It is easy to check that for the affine solutions and radially symmetric solutions

and condition (
5
) holds.

Let us consider solutions that are both affine and radially symmetric, i.e

,
G=\beta(t)
.
Here
{\mathcal{D}}=3\alpha
,
J=3\alpha^{2}
,
\lambda=3\beta
, and the system (

) takes a closed form

\displaystyle\dot{\alpha}=-{\alpha}^{2}-\beta,\quad\dot{\beta}=\alpha(1-3\beta),

the first integral is

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}=2\beta-1+K|1-3\beta|^{\frac{2}{3}},\quad K=\frac{1-2\beta(0)+\alpha^{2}(0)}{|1-3\beta(0)|^{\frac{2}{3}}}.

Recall that the positivity of density requires
1-3\beta>0,
see
(
3
). The prevailing term of the right hand side as

is
2\beta
, it tends to
-\infty
for any

. This means
that
\alpha
and
\beta
are bounded
for any initial data
,
the density is bounded and the smooth in time solution to the Cauchy
problem exists for all radially symmetric initial data.

In the case of plane oscillations we have

{\bf V}=F(t,r){\bf r},\,{\bf E}=G(t,r){\bf r},\,{\bf r}=(x_{1},x_{2},0),\,r=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}.

For the corresponding affine solution
{\mathcal{D}}=2\alpha
,

,

, therefore system

) takes a closed form

\displaystyle\dot{\alpha}=-{\alpha}^{2}-\beta,\quad\dot{\beta}=\alpha(1-2\beta),

the first integral is

\displaystyle 2\alpha^{2}=-(1-2\beta)\ln|1-2\beta|+K(1-2\beta)+1,\quad K=\frac{1-2\alpha^{2}(0)}{1-2\beta(0)}-\ln|1-2\beta(0)|.

From the positivity of density we have

therefore the
prevailing term of the right hand side as

is the
logarithmic one, it tends to

for any

. This means that

\alpha
and
\beta
are bounded for any initial data, the density
is bounded and the smooth in time solution to the Cauchy problem
exists for all radially symmetric initial data (see also

[
2
]
,
[
11
]
).

These examples show, in particular, that there are three-dimensional initial data that do not lead a finite time blow-up.
However, as was proved in
[

]
, radially symmetric solutions of the cold plasma equation, which are not affine, in the general case blow up even if they are arbitrarily small perturbations of the zero stationary state.

7.
Discussion

In this paper, we constructed a method, that allows one to obtain a sufficient condition that guarantees the boundedness of the density component on a given time interval for the solution to the Cauchy problem of the system
of PDE describing general 3D electrostatic oscillations.
For particular cases
(plain and irrotational oscollations) we find an explicit sufficient condition for preventing a blow-up in the first period of oscillations.
Next, we compare the sufficient condition with the known criteria for the formation of singularities, if such a possibility exists. Further, we consider the case of
2D axisymmetric oscillations with special initial data, where there
is a possibility of comparison with the results of a numerical study
of the blow-up process.
For this case, we obtain a sufficient condition for the preservation of smoothness, which is less rough than in the general case, as well as a sufficient condition for a blow-up.

As shown in §
3
, in the 1D case, as well as for the affine and radially symmetric cases in many dimensions, they exist globally in
t
smooth solutions of the Cauchy problem. It is very interesting to check whether there are globally smooth solutions in the multidimensional case without these restrictive assumptions. The numerics suggest that any other solution
necessarily blows up. Recently, a finite-time blow-up for general radially symmetric initial data, even arbitrarily small, has been proved analytically
[
11
]
. The only exception is the initial data in the form of so-called simple waves, where the solution either tends to the affine one or blows up. The present paper can be regarded as an addition to this result, since it makes it possible to estimate the lifetime of a smooth solution. Now it is not known where affine solutions without radial symmetry necessarily blows up.

Another important open question is the description of the complete class of solutions corresponding to electrostatic oscillations.
The solutions considered in this article belong to this class, and the hypothesis is that in the multidimensional case, electrostatic oscillations must necessarily be radially symmetric or affine. However, this fact has not yet been proven.

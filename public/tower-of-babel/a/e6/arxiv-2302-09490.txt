Introduction

In the present paper, we analyse qualitative properties of nonnegative solutions for the aggregation-diffusion equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u^{m}-\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla c\right),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

Here the diffusion exponent is taken to be
m=\frac{2d}{d+2s}
for
2<2s<d
with
d\geq 3.

u
represents the density of cells, the chemoattractant
c
is governed by a diffusion process and can be expressed by the convolution of Riesz potential and
u(x,t)
, that is

\displaystyle c(x,t)=\frac{1}{d-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy.

(3)

The parabolic equation on
u
gives nonnegative solutions
u(x,t)\geq 0,c(x,t)\geq 0
. Another property we will use is the conservation of the total number of cells

\displaystyle M:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u_{0}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x,t)dx.

(4)

Initial data will be assumed throughout this paper

\displaystyle u_{0}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx<\infty,\quad\nabla u_{0}^{m}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

(5)

The nonlinear diffusion
m>1
models the local repulsion of cells while the attractive nonlocal term models cell movement toward chemotactic sources or attractive interaction between cells due to cell adhesion
[
7
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
. The main characteristic of equation (
1
) is the competition between the diffusion and the nonlocal aggregation. This is well presented by the free energy

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx-\frac{1}{2(d-2s)}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x,t)u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy.

(6)

The competition between these two terms leads to finite-time blow-up and infinite time existence. As a matter of fact, (
1
) can be recast as

\displaystyle u_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla\mu\right),

(7)

where
\mu
is the chemical potential

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{m}{m-1}u^{m-1}-c.

(8)

Multiplying (
7
) by
\mu
and integrating it in space one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{dF(u)}{dt}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u|\nabla\mu|^{2}dx=0.

(9)

This implies that
F[u(\cdot,t)]
is non-increasing with respect to time.

Notice that equation (
1
) possesses a scaling invariance which leaves the
L^{p}
norm invariant and produces a balance between the diffusion and the aggregation terms where

\displaystyle p=\frac{d(2-m)}{2s}=m=\frac{2d}{d+2s}.

(10)

Indeed, if
u(x,t)
is a solution to (
1
), then
u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u\left(\lambda^{\frac{2-m}{2s}}x,\lambda^{m-1+\frac{2-m}{s}}t\right)
is also a solution and this scaling preserves the
L^{p}
norm
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.
For the critical case
m_{c}=2-2s/d
, the above scaling becomes the mass invariant scaling
u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u\left(\lambda^{1/d}x,\lambda^{1+\frac{2-2s}{d}}t\right)
. For the subcritical case
m>2-2s/d
, the aggregation dominates the diffusion for low density and prevents spreading. While for high density, the diffusion dominates the aggregation thus blow-up is precluded. On the contrary, for the supercritical case
1\leq m<2-2s/d,
the aggregation dominates the diffusion for high density (large
\lambda
) and the density may have finite-time blow-up. While for low density (small
\lambda
), the diffusion dominates the aggregation and the density has infinite-time spreading. These behaviors also appear in many other physical systems such as thin film, Hele-Shaw, Stellar collapse as well as the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
[
8
,
16
,
31
,
32
]
.

Our main goal in this paper is to classify the global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions by the initial data. The main tool for the analysis of (
1

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{m}{m-1}U_{s}^{m-1}-C_{s}=\overline{C},~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega,\\
U_{s}=0\mbox{~ in ~}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega,\quad U_{s}>0\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\

Here

is any constant chemical potential and

is a connected open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Therefore, let us begin with the analysis of stationary states. We firstly stress that a lot of efforts have been put to investigate the steady states of (

Theorem 6

(Local in time existence and blow-up criteria)

\displaystyle(2s-2)C_{dsr}(r-1)\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{r+1}{1+(2s-2)/d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{r+1}\leq

.

\displaystyle\varepsilon(2s-2)\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(x)u_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\left(|x-y|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{d+2-2s}{2}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,t;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C,~~\mbox{for}~~1\leq r<\infty.

3
Steady states

As we have just seen, the existence time of the free energy solution to (
1
) heavily depends on the behavior of its
L^{m}
-norm. Moreover, the first term of the free energy involves the
L^{m}
-norm and (
24
) tells us that the second term of the free energy is related to the stationary solution
U_{s}
to (
1
). Hence, in order to prove the existence of a global weak solution by the energy functional, the information given by steady states will be of paramount importance. Therefore, in this section, we will give a detailed analysis of steady states to (
1
).

The steady equation of (
1
) is followed in the sense of distribution

\displaystyle\Delta U_{s}^{m}-\nabla\cdot\left(U_{s}(x)\nabla C_{s}(x)\right)=0,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

(69)

where
C_{s}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle C_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{d-2s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{U_{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy.

(70)

Starting from a Pohozaev type identity, we will show a full characterization of minimizers of
F(u)
to relate to the steady states.

Lemma 8
.

(A Pohozaev identity for steady solutions) Assume
U_{s}\in L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
satisfying (
69
), then the steady solution satisfies the following identity in the sense of distribution

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}dx=\frac{d-2s}{2d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}C_{s}U_{s}dx.

(71)

Proof.

We follow a similar argument as done in
[
5
,
28
]
. Consider a cut-off function
\psi_{R}(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\psi_{R}(x)=|x|^{2}
for
|x|<R,~\psi_{R}(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2R.
Then we compute

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Delta U_{s}^{m}\psi_{R}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}\Delta\psi_{R}(x)dx

(72)

and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi_{R}(x)\nabla\cdot(U_{s}\nabla C_{s})dx=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla\psi_{R}(x)\cdot U_{s}\nabla C_{s}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{[\nabla\psi_{R}(x)-\nabla\psi_{R}(y)]\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\frac{U_{s}(x)U_{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy.

(73)

Both terms in the right-hand side of (
72
) and (
3
) are bounded (because
\Delta\psi_{R}(x)
and
\frac{[\nabla\psi_{R}(x)-\nabla\psi_{R}(y)]\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}
are bounded and
U_{s}\in L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
). Therefore, as
R\to\infty,
we may pass to the limit in each term using the Lebesgue monotone convergence theorem and obtain the identity.
\Box

With this identity in hand, we are now ready to make use of the variational structure of the free energy to deduce the following results.

Proposition 9
.

(Critical point of
F(u)
)
Let
\bar{u}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=M
be a global minimizer of
F(u)
. Then
\bar{u}
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange condition

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m-1}\bar{u}^{m-1}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\bar{u}\ast\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2s}}=\overline{C},~~\forall~x\in supp(\bar{u}),

(74)

where the constant

\overline{C}=\frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\bar{u}(x)\bar{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy\right).

Furthermore, such minimizer is the steady state
U_{s}
of (
1
) in the distributional sense (
69
). Therefore, for
m=\frac{2d}{d+2s},

supp(U_{s})={\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Proof.

We use some ideas from
[
1
,
11
,
13
]
. Set
Y_{M}=\left\{u\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=M\right\}
. Let
\bar{u}\in Y_{M}
be a global minimizer of
F(u)
with
\Omega=supp(\bar{u}).
For any
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),
denoting

\displaystyle\varphi=\left(\psi-\frac{1}{M}\int_{\Omega}\psi\bar{u}dx\right)\bar{u}(x),

(75)

then
supp(\varphi)\subseteq\Omega
and
\int_{\Omega}\varphi dx=0.
Besides, there exists

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\displaystyle\min_{y\in supp\varphi}\bar{u}(y)}{\displaystyle\max_{y\in supp\varphi}\left|\varphi(y)\right|}>0

such that
\bar{u}+\varepsilon\varphi\geq 0
in
\Omega
for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.
Now
\bar{u}
is a global minimizer of
F(u)
in
\Omega
if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}F(\bar{u}+\varepsilon\varphi)=0.

(76)

The above definition implies that for any
\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)dx=0

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\bar{u}^{m-1}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\bar{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy\right)\varphi(x)dx=0.

(77)

Then by the definition (
75
) of
\varphi,
(
77
) can be read as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\bar{u}^{m-1}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\bar{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy-\overline{C}\right)\bar{u}\psi dx=0,~~\mbox{for any}~\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(78)

This gives

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m-1}\bar{u}^{m-1}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\bar{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy=\overline{C},~~a.e.~~\mbox{in}~\Omega.

(79)

Since
\bar{u}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),
we have from similar arguments of
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
that
\bar{u}
is continuous in
\Omega
. Owning the regularity we arrive at

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\nabla\bar{u}^{m}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\bar{u}\nabla\bar{u}\ast\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2s}}\right)=\nabla\cdot\left(\bar{u}\nabla\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\bar{u}^{m-1}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\bar{u}\ast\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2s}}\right)\right)=0

(80)

in the sense of distribution. Therefore, all global minimizers of the free energy are stationary solutions of (
1
) in the distributional sense characterized by (
80
). Then by employing Theorem 2.2 of
[
12
]
, the steady states must be radially decreasing up to translations.

Now we declare that the steady state
U_{s}
of (
1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m-1}U_{s}^{m-1}-\frac{1}{d-2s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{U_{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy=\overline{C},~~\forall~x\in\Omega=supp(U_{s}).

(81)

Keeping (
71
) in mind, taking inner product to (
81
) by
U_{s}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
yields

\displaystyle\overline{C}=\frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{1}{m-1}-\frac{d+2s}{d-2s}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}dx.

(82)

When
m=\frac{2d}{d+2s},\overline{C}=0,
thus from (
81
) we claim that
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
If not, choose a point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega,
as we take a sequence of point
x_{n}\to x_{0}
with
x_{n}\in\Omega,
we have that
\frac{m}{m-1}U_{s}^{m-1}(x_{n})\to 0,
whereas the sequence
\frac{1}{d-2s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{U_{s}(y)}{|x_{n}-y|^{d-2s}}dy>0
, a contradiction.
\Box

Now we are ready to assert that the steady states of (
1
) can be expressed explicitly. By Proposition
9
one has that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{m}{m-1}U_{s}^{m-1}-C_{s}=0,~~\mbox{in}~~{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
C_{s}=\frac{1}{d-2s}U_{s}\ast\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2s}}.\end{array}\right.

Hence
C_{s}
is endowed with the following integral equation

\displaystyle C_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d-2s}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{C_{s}(y)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d-2s}\left(\frac{d-2s}{2d}\right)^{\frac{d+2s}{d-2s}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{C_{s}(y)^{\frac{d+2s}{d-2s}}}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy.

(85)

It has been proved in
[
17
]
that every positive solution of (
3
) is radially symmetric and decreasing about some point
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and therefore assumes the form

\displaystyle C_{s}(x)=c\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d-2s}{2}}

(86)

with some positive constants
c
and
\lambda.
It gives our final estimates for this part.

Proposition 10
.

For
m=\frac{2d}{d+2s},
any steady state to (
1
) is radially symmetric up to translation and given by a one-parameter family for some
\lambda>0
and
c>0

\displaystyle U_{s}(x)=c\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d+2s}{2}}

(87)

with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}(x)dx=C(d).

4
Proof of Theorem
5

With the aid of the characterization of steady states and the free energy, we can proceed to distinguish the two cases
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
to show
\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
can be bounded from below or above separately, then we establish the global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions to (
1
).

4.1
Proof of the finite time blow-up (ii) of Theorem
5

In this subsection, we start with the case
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and show the finite time blow-up of the weak solution to (
1
). Firstly we prepare a priori bound.

Lemma 11
.

(A priori bound)
Under assumption (
5
), if the initial free energy
F(u_{0})<F(U_{s})
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, then there exists a constant
\mu>1
such that the corresponding free energy solution
u
satisfies that for any
t>0

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\mu\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(88)

Proof.

Firstly letting

\displaystyle f=u(x),h=u(y),\beta=d-2s,q=q_{1}=\frac{2d}{2d-\beta}

in the HLS inequality (
21
), we can infer from the expression of
F(u)
that

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}-\frac{1}{2(d-2s)}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{m-1}\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}-\frac{C(d,\beta)}{2(d-2s)}\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}.

(89)

While (
24
) also tells us that

\displaystyle F(U_{s})=\frac{1}{m-1}\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}-\frac{C(d,\beta)}{2(d-2s)}\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}.

(90)

We define an auxiliary function

g(x)=\frac{1}{m-1}x-\frac{C(d,\beta)}{2(d-2s)}x^{2/m}.

It can be seen from (
4.1
) and (
90
) that

g\left(\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}\right)=F(U_{s})\geq g\left(\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}\right)

for all
u\in L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).
Namely, the maximum point
x_{0}
of
g(x)
is attained by

\displaystyle x_{0}=\left(\frac{m(d-2s)}{(m-1)C(d,\beta)}\right)^{\frac{m}{2-m}}=\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}.

(91)

In addition, in the case that
F(u_{0})<F(U_{s}),
there is a
0<\delta<1
such that

\displaystyle F(u_{0})<\delta F(U_{s}).

(92)

Thus, by the monotonicity of
F(u)
with respect to time, it follows that

\displaystyle g\left(\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}\right)\leq F(u)\leq F(u_{0})<\delta F(U_{s})=\delta g\left(\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}\right).

(93)

Therefore, for any
x>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m},g(x)
is strictly decreasing and it has a strictly decreasing inverse function
g^{-1}.
So if
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m},
one has that for some
\mu>1
depending on
\delta,

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}>\mu\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}.

(94)

This completes this lemma.
\Box

When
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, following the lines (
4.1
)-(
93
) in the proof of Lemma
11
, we can deduce the following reverse result.

Corollary 12
.

Under assumption (
5
), if the initial free energy
F(u_{0})<F(U_{s})
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, then there exists a constant
\mu_{1}<1
such that the corresponding free energy solution
u
satisfies that for any
t>0

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\mu_{1}\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

(95)

Now we can utilize the standard argument relying on the evolution of the second moment of solutions as originally done in
[
20
]
to prove the finite time blow-up result in Theorem
5
.

Proposition 13
.

(Finite time blow-up)
Assume
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx<\infty,F(u_{0})<F(U_{s})
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
. Let
u(x,t)
be a weak free energy solution to (
1
) on
[0,T_{w}),
then it satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx=-4s\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx+2(d-2s)F(u),~0<t<T_{w}.

(96)

Here
T_{w}<\infty
and
\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
blows up in finite time.

Proof.

Here we show the formal computation, the passing to the limit from the approximated problem (
2
) can be done on account of
[
1
, Theorem 2.11]
and
[
29
, Lemma 6.2]
without any further complication. By integrating by parts in (
1
) and symmetrising the second term, the time derivative of the second moment of the weak solution is endowed with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx=2d\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx-\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x,t)u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\left(2d-\frac{2}{m-1}(d-2s)\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx+2(d-2s)F(u)

\displaystyle=-4s\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m}dx+2(d-2s)F(u).

(97)

Then applying Lemma
11
with some
\mu>1
and the decreasing of
F(u)
in time indicates

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\leq-4s\mu\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}+2(d-2s)F(u_{0})

\displaystyle<-4s\mu\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}+2(d-2s)F(U_{s})

\displaystyle=4s(1-\mu)\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}<0,

(98)

where we have used the identity
F(U_{s})=\frac{2s}{d-2s}\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}
which can be derived by

\displaystyle F(U_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}C_{s}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}dx-\frac{m}{2(m-1)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2-m}{2(m-1)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{s}^{m}dx=\frac{2s}{d-2s}\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{m}

(99)

due to
C_{s}=\frac{m}{m-1}U_{s}^{m-1}.
Thus there exists a
T>0
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}m_{2}(t)=0
.

On the other hand, it follows from the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)dx=\int_{|x|\leq R}u(x)dx+\int_{|x|>R}u(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{(m-1)d/m}\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}m_{2}(t).

(100)

Choosing
R=\left(\frac{Cm_{2}(t)}{\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a+2}}
with
a=\frac{(m-1)d}{m}
gives

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq c\|u\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{2}{a+2}}m_{2}(t)^{\frac{a}{a+2}}.

(101)

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\geq\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{(a+2)/2}}{cm_{2}(t)^{a/2}}=\infty.

(102)

Thus the proof is completed.
\Box

4.2
Proof of the global existence (i) of Theorem
5

A direct consequence of Theorem
6
and Corollary
12
is the following global existence result.

Proposition 14
.

(Global existence)
Under assumption (
5
), assume
F(u_{0})<F(U_{s})
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, then there exists a free energy solution to (
1
) on
[0,\infty).

Proof.

By Theorem
6
, there are
T_{w}
and a free energy solution to (
1
) in
[0,T_{w})
with initial data
u_{0}.
We then infer from Corollary
12
that there exists a
\mu_{1}<1
, for all
t\in[0,T_{w})
,

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\mu_{1}\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Therefore, we can deduce that
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,\min(T,T_{w});L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\right)
for every
T>0
which implies that
T_{w}=\infty
by Theorem
6
.
\Box

5
Conclusions

This paper concerns equation (
1
) for the energy critical exponent
m=\frac{2d}{d+2s}
. It is proved that any steady states
U_{s}(x)
of (
1
) is radially symmetric and decreasing about some point
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and assumes the from
U_{s}(x)=c\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d+2s}{2}}
for some positive constants
c
and
\lambda
. Our results show that the
L^{p}
norm of the stationary solutions plays a critical role on determining the global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions to (
1
). Here
p=\frac{d(2-m)}{2s}=m
and the
L^{p}
norm produces a balance between the diffusion and the aggregation terms under the invariant scaling for equation (
1
). Actually,
\|U_{s}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
is a constant only depending on
d,m
and we prove that the
L^{p}
norm of the stationary solutions
\|U_{s}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
is the sharp condition on the initial data separating infinite-time existence from finite-time blow-up. Precisely, in the case that the initial free energy
F(u_{0})
is less than
F(U_{s})
which is the free energy of the stationary solutions, there exists a global weak solution satisfying the free energy inequality when the initial data satisfies
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and the solution blows up at finite time provided by the condition
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
. For the critical case
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},
whether the solution exists globally in time or blows up in finite time is still unknown.

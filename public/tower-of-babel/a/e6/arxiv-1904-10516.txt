Abstract
. We show that the Abraham-Rubin-Shelah Open Coloring Axiom is consistent with a large continuum, in particular, consistent with 2 ℵ 0 = ℵ 3 2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{3} . This answers one of the main open questions from [ 1 ] . As in [ 1 ] , we need to construct names for so-called preassignments of colors in order to add the necessary homogeneous sets. However, the known constructions of preassignments (ours in particular) only work assuming the 𝖢𝖧 \mathsf{CH} . In order to address this difficulty, we show how to construct such names with very strong symmetry conditions. This symmetry allows us to combine them in many different ways, using a new type of poset called a partition product . Partition products may be thought of as a restricted memory iteration with stringent isomorphism and coherent-overlap conditions on the memories. We finally construct, in L L , the partition product which gives us a model of 𝖮𝖢𝖠 A ​ R ​ S \mathsf{OCA}_{ARS} in which 2 ℵ 0 = ℵ 3 2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{3} .



1.
Introduction

Ramsey’s Theorem, regarding colorings of tuples of
\omega
, is a fundamental result in combinatorics. Naturally, set theorists have studied generalizations of this theorem which concern colorings of pairs of countable ordinals, that is to say, colorings on
\omega_{1}
. The most straightforward generalization of this theorem is the assertion that any coloring of pairs of countable ordinals has an uncountable homogeneous set. However, this naive generalization is provably false, at least in
\mathsf{ZFC}
(see
[
9
]
). One way to obtain consistent generalizations of Ramsey’s Theorem to uncountable sets,
\omega_{1}
in particular, is to place various topological constraints on the colorings. This results in principles known as Open Coloring Axioms, which we discuss presently. In what follows, we will use the notation
[A]^{2}
to denote all two-element subsets of
A
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A
be a set and
\chi:[A]^{2}\longrightarrow\left\{0,1\right\}
a function.
H\subseteq A
is said to be
0-homogeneous (respectively 1-homogeneous) with respect to
\chi
if
\chi
takes the constant value 0 (respectively 1) on
[H]^{2}
.
H
is said to be
\chi
-homogeneous
if it is either 0 or 1 homogeneous with respect to
\chi
.

A function
\chi:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\longrightarrow\left\{0,1\right\}
is said to be an
open coloring
if
\chi^{-1}(\left\{0\right\})
and
\chi^{-1}(\left\{1\right\})
are both open in the product topology with respect to some second countable, Hausdorff topology on
\omega_{1}
.

The
Abraham-Rubin-Shelah Open Coloring Axiom
, abbreviated
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
, states that for any open coloring
\chi
on
\omega_{1}
, there exists a partition
\omega_{1}=\bigcup_{n<\omega}A_{n}
such that each
A_{n}
is
\chi
-homogeneous.

Abraham and Shelah (
[
2
]
) first studied the restriction of this axiom to colorings arising from injective functions
f:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, where
|A|=\aleph_{1}
and used these ideas to show that Martin’s Axiom does not imply Baumgartner’s Axiom (
[
3
]
). The full version made its debut in
[
1
]
, where the authors studied it alongside a number of other axioms about
\aleph_{1}
-sized sets of reals. In particular, they showed that
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
is consistent with
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

A little later, Todorčević isolated the following axiom (
[
11
]
):

Definition 1.2
.

The
Todorčević Open Coloring Axiom
, abbreviated
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}
, states the following: let
A
be a set of reals, and suppose that
\chi:[A]^{2}\longrightarrow\left\{0,1\right\}
is a coloring so that
\chi^{-1}(\left\{0\right\})
is open in
A\times A
. Then either there is an uncountable
A_{0}\subseteq A
so that
A_{0}
is 0-homogeneous with respect to
\chi
or there exists a partition
A=\bigcup_{n<\omega}B_{n}
so that each
B_{n}
is 1-homogeneous with respect to
\chi
.

If we restrict our attention to sets of reals
A
with size
\aleph_{1}
, we denote this axiom by
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}(\aleph_{1})
.

Both of these versions of open coloring axioms imply that the
\mathsf{CH}
is false. Indeed,
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
implies that if
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
has size
\aleph_{1}
and
f:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is injective, then
f
is a union of countably-many monotonic subfunctions. However, under the
\mathsf{CH}
, there exists a function
f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
which is not continuous on any uncountable set, and hence not monotonic on any uncountable set (see
C_{62}
of
[
10
]
). In fact, under the
\mathsf{CH}
, there is even an uncountable, injective, partial
f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
with no uncountable monotonic subfunction (see
[
4
]
), and thus even continuous colorings may fail to have large homogeneous sets under the
\mathsf{CH}
. With regards to
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}
, this axiom implies that the bounding number
\mathfrak{b}
is
\aleph_{2}
.

It is therefore of interest whether or not these axioms, individually or jointly, actually decide the value of the continuum. In the case of
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}
, Farah has shown in an unpublished note that
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}(\aleph_{1})
is consistent with an arbitrarily large value of the continuum, though it is not known whether the full
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}
is consistent with larger values of the continuum than
\aleph_{2}
. On the other hand, Moore has shown in
[
6
]
that
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}+\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
does decide that the continuum is exactly
\aleph_{2}
.

The question of whether
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
is powerful enough to decide the value of the continuum on its own, first asked in
[
1
]
, has remained open. There are a number of difficulties in obtaining a model of
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
with a “large continuum,” i.e., with
2^{\aleph_{0}}>\aleph_{2}
. Chief among these difficulties is to construct so-called
preassignments of colors
. The authors of
[
2
]
first discovered the technique of preassigning colors. As used in
[
1
]
, a preassignment of colors is a function which decides, in the ground model, whether the forcing will place a countable ordinal
\alpha
inside
some
0-homogeneous or
some
1-homogeneous set, with respect to a fixed coloring. The key to the consistency of
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
is to construct preassignments in such a way that the posets which add the requisite homogeneous sets, as guided by the preassignments, are c.c.c.

However, the known constructions of “good” preassignments (ours in particular) only work under the
\mathsf{CH}
. The construction of a preassignment involves diagonalizing out of closed sets in finite products of the space, ensuring that no such closed set can be the closure of an uncountable antichain of conditions in the poset (see the discussion preceding Remark
4.10
). Under the
\mathsf{CH}
, this task can be completed in
\omega_{1}
-many steps, since the spaces we consider are second countable (and so there are at most
\aleph_{1}
-many such closed sets). Since forcing iterations whose strict initial segments satisfy the
\mathsf{CH}
can only lead to a model where the continuum is at most
\aleph_{2}
, this creates considerable difficulties for obtaining models of
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
in which the continuum is, say,
\aleph_{3}
.

In this paper, we prove the following theorem, thereby providing an answer to this question:

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\mathsf{ZFC}
is consistent, then so is
\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}+2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{3}
.

The general theme of the paper is the following: short iterations are necessary to preserve the
\mathsf{CH}
and thereby construct effective preassignments; longer iterations, built out of these smaller ones in specific ways, can be used to obtain models with a large continuum.

One can check using cardinality arguments that executing this theme requires the construction of names for preassignments with substantial symmetry. Let us briefly explain what we mean by this. For the purposes of the introduction, if
\chi
is a coloring as in the definition of
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
and
f:\omega_{1}\longrightarrow 2
is an arbitrary function (any such is called a preassignment), then we use
\mathbb{Q}(\chi,f)
to denote the poset to decompose
\omega_{1}
into countably-many
\chi
-homogeneous sets as guided by
f
(see Section 4.2 for a precise description). In
[
1
]
, the authors show that if
\mathbb{P}
is a sufficiently nice c.c.c. poset preserving the
\mathsf{CH}
and
\dot{\chi}
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name for an open coloring, then there exists a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{f}
for a preassignment so that
\mathbb{P}
outright forces that
\mathbb{Q}(\dot{\chi},\dot{f})
is c.c.c. In our case, we are able to construct a
single

\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{f}
so that the following (and much more besides) holds: if
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
are mutually generic filters for
\mathbb{P}
, then the poset

\mathbb{Q}(\dot{\chi}[G_{L}],\dot{f}[G_{L}])\times\mathbb{Q}(\dot{\chi}[G_{R}],\dot{f}[G_{R}])

is c.c.c. in
V[G_{L}\times G_{R}]
. Constructing names for symmetric preassignments takes us beyond the techniques of
[
1
]
. Since there are only
\aleph_{2}
names for preassignments named in short iteration forcings, many of them must be reused more than once in a long iteration, and therefore this kind of symmetry proves to be necessary for executing our theme. Determining exactly how much “symmetry” the names for preassignments need to satisfy leads us to the notion of a
Partition Product
, which is a type of restricted memory iteration with various isomorphism and coherent overlap conditions on the memories.

Our method for proving Theorem
1.3
is general enough that it can be adapted to incorporate posets of size
\leq\aleph_{1}
with the Knaster property, thereby adding the forcing axiom
\mathsf{FA}(\aleph_{2},\text{Knaster}(\aleph_{1}))
to the conclusion. This forcing axiom asserts that for any poset
\mathbb{P}
of size
\leq\aleph_{1}
which has the Knaster property and any sequence
\langle D_{i}:i<\omega_{2}\rangle
of
\aleph_{2}
dense subsets of
\mathbb{P}
, there is a filter for
\mathbb{P}
which meets each of the
D_{i}
. Thus we also obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 1.4
.

If
\mathsf{ZFC}
is consistent, then so is
\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}+2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{3}+\mathsf{FA}(\aleph_{2},\emph{Knaster}(\aleph_{1}))
.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows: in Section 2, we introduce the axiomatic definition of a
partition product
and prove a number of general facts about this type of poset. In Section 3, we develop the machinery to combine partition products in a variety of ways. The main goal of Section 4 is to isolate exactly what we need our names for preassignments to satisfy. To do so, we introduce and develop the notion of a
finitely generated
partition product. We also use the machinery developed so far to count these; the counting arguments will be crucial for the diagonalization arguments involved in constructing preassignments. Section 5 includes the actual construction of our highly symmetric names for preassignments. Finally, in section 6, we show how to construct partition products in
L
. It would be helpful, though not necessary, for the reader to be familiar with the first few sections of the paper
[
1
]
, in particular, their construction of preassignments and the role which preassignments play in showing the consistency of
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
.

We would like to thank the referee for investing a considerable amount of time in providing extensive comments which have helped, we believe, to improve the exposition of this paper.

2.
Partition Products

2.1.
Notation and Conventions

We begin with some remarks about notation and conventions. First, if
f
is a function and
A\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(f)
, then we will in general use
f[A]
to denote
\left\{f(x):x\in A\right\}
, the pointwise image of
A
under
f
.

Second, given two sets
X
and
Y
, we will use
X\biguplus Y
to denote their disjoint union, and if
X
and
Y
are also topological spaces with respective topologies
\tau_{X}
and
\tau_{Y}
, we take the topology on
X\biguplus Y
to be the disjoint union of the respective topologies on
X
and
Y
, denoted
\tau_{X}\biguplus\tau_{Y}
. Similarly, if
f_{X}
and
f_{Y}
are functions with domains
X
and
Y
, we take
f_{X}\biguplus f_{Y}
to be the disjoint union of these functions defined in the natural way on
X\biguplus Y
.

Additionally, we will often be working in the context of a poset
\mathbb{R}
as well as various other posets related to it; these other posets will have notational decorations, for example,
\mathbb{R}^{*}
. If
\dot{G}
is the canonical
\mathbb{R}
-name for a generic filter, we use the corresponding decorations, such as
\dot{G}^{*}
, to denote the related names.

Finally, regarding iterations, we will want to consider forcing iterations where the domain of the iteration is not necessarily an ordinal, but possibly a non-transitive set of ordinals. This will help smooth over various technicalities later. We view elements in an iteration as partial functions where each value of the function is forced by the restriction of the function to be a condition in the appropriate name for a poset.

2.2.
Definition and Basic Facts

Our first main goal in this section is to define the notion of a
partition product
and prove a few basic lemmas. Before giving the definition, we have a few paragraphs of remarks which will help provide motivation.

Roughly speaking, the class of partition products consists of finite support iterations with restricted memories which are built in very specific ways, but which is rich enough to be closed under products, closed under products of iterations taken over a common initial segment, and closed under more general “partitioned products” of segments of the iterations taken over common earlier segments.

Iterations with restricted memory first appeared in Shelah’s work (see
[
7
]
; see also
[
8
]
for further applications to the null ideal and
[
5
]
for applications to cardinal characteristics). The following definition captures restricted memory in a convenient way for us. The memory limitation is in condition (
2
) of the definition.

Definition 2.1
.

\mathbb{R}
is a finite support
restricted memory iteration
on
X
, of iterand names
\langle\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}\mid\xi\in X\rangle
, with
memory function

\xi\mapsto b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
(
\xi\in X
), if the following conditions hold:

(1)

for each
\xi\in X
,
b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)\subseteq X\cap\xi
. If
\zeta\in b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
then
b_{\mathbb{R}}(\zeta)\subseteq b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
;

(2)

\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}
is an
\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
-name;

(3)

conditions in
\mathbb{R}
are finite partial functions
p
on
X
. For each
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
,
p(\xi)
is a canonical
\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
-name for an element of
\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}
;

(4)

q\leq p
in
\mathbb{R}
if
\operatorname{dom}(q)\supseteq\operatorname{dom}(p)
and
q\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)\Vdash_{\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)}q(\xi)\leq p(\xi)
for each
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
.

X
is the
domain
of the iteration, denoted
\operatorname{dom}(\mathbb{R})
. A function satisfying condition (
1
) is a
memory function
on
X
. The poset
\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
in conditions (
2
)–(
4
) is the restriction of
\mathbb{R}
to conditions
p
with
\operatorname{dom}(p)\subseteq b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
. Using condition (
1
) one can check that
\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\mu)
is a restricted memory iteration of
\langle\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}\mid\xi\in b_{\mathbb{R}}(\mu)\rangle
. More generally,
Y\subseteq X
is
memory closed
, also called
base closed
, if for all
\xi\in Y
,
b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)\subseteq Y
. Then
\mathbb{R}\restriction Y
is the restriction of
\mathbb{R}
to conditions
p
with
\operatorname{dom}(p)\subseteq Y
. One can check this is exactly the restricted memory iteration of
\langle\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}\mid\xi\in Y\rangle
with memory function
b_{\mathbb{R}}\restriction Y
.

Remark 2.2
.

Strictly speaking
\mathbb{R}
as in Definition
2.1
is not actually an iteration, even if
X
is transitive. This is because we take
p(\xi)
to be an
\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
-name rather than an
\mathbb{R}\restriction\xi
-name. But, because
\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}
is an
\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
-name, every
\mathbb{R}\restriction\xi
-name for an element of
\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}
can be forced in
\mathbb{R}\restriction\xi
to be equal to an
\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
-name. Using this and the finiteness of the supports one can check that
\mathbb{R}
is isomorphic to a dense subset of an iteration. Working with
\mathbb{R}
, instead of the actual iteration, simplifies our definitions.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
b
be a memory function on
X
. A bijection
\sigma\colon X\longrightarrow X^{*}
is an
acceptable rearrangement
of
X,b
if for all
\zeta,\xi\in X
,
\zeta\in b(\xi)
implies
\sigma(\zeta)<\sigma(\xi)
. We then define
\sigma(b)
, the
\sigma
-rearrangement
of
b
, to be the function on
X^{*}
given by
\sigma(b)(\sigma(\xi))=\sigma[b(\xi)]
, and we note that
\sigma(b)
is a memory function on
X^{*}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose
\mathbb{R}
is a restricted memory iteration on
X
, of
\langle\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}\mid\xi\in X\rangle
, with memory function
b_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\sigma\colon X\longrightarrow X^{*}
be an acceptable rearrangement of
X,b_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\sigma
induces an isomorphism, which we also denote
\sigma
, between
\mathbb{R}
and a restricted memory iteration
\mathbb{R}^{*}
on
X^{*}
. This isomorphism in turn extends to act on
\mathbb{R}
-names
\dot{u}
and maps them to
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-names
\sigma(\dot{u})
so that
\dot{u}[G]=\sigma(\dot{u})[\sigma[G]]
. These liftings are determined uniquely by the following conditions:

(1)

\mathbb{R}^{*}
is a restricted memory iteration of
\langle\dot{\mathbb{U}}^{*}_{\xi}\mid\xi\in X^{*}\rangle
with memory function
\sigma(b_{\mathbb{R}})
;

(2)

for

,
\operatorname{dom}(\sigma(p))=\sigma[\operatorname{dom}(p)]
and
\sigma(p)(\sigma(\xi))=\sigma(p(\xi))
;

(3)

for an
\mathbb{R}
-name
\dot{u}
,
\sigma(\dot{u})
is the
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-name
\{\langle\sigma(\dot{z}),\sigma(p)\rangle\mid\langle\dot{z},p\rangle\in\dot{u}\}

(4)

\dot{\mathbb{U}}^{*}_{\sigma(\mu)}=\sigma(\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\mu})
.

The last three conditions are recursive. Knowledge of
\dot{\mathbb{U}}^{*}_{\sigma(\xi)}
for
\xi<\mu
allows determining
\sigma(p)
for

,
\sigma(\dot{u})
for
\mathbb{R}\restriction\mu
-names
\dot{u}
, and
\dot{\mathbb{U}}^{*}_{\sigma(\mu)}

\mathbb{R}^{*}
as the
\sigma
-rearrangement
of
\mathbb{R}
, denoted
\sigma(\mathbb{R})
. We also refer to
\sigma(\dot{u})
as the
\sigma
-rearrangement
of

\mathbb{R}
.

A partition product is a restricted memory iteration with two additional structural requirements. The first requirement is that the iterand names (up to isomorphisms induced by rearrangements) are all taken from a restricted alphabet. This is phrased precisely in Definition
2.6
. The second requirement is that the memory sets
b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)
, when not disjoint, intersect in very specific ways. This is phrased precisely in Definitions
2.11
and
2.12
. The first requirement captures the sense in which we construct long iterations from a small set of short building blocks. The addition of the second requirement helps us bound the class of partition products of size
\aleph_{1}
, up to isomorphism.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
C\subseteq\omega_{2}\setminus\omega
. A
restricted memory alphabet
on
C
is a pair of sequences
\underline{\mathbb{P}}=\langle\mathbb{P}_{\delta}:\delta\in C\rangle
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}=\langle\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\delta}:\delta\in C\rangle
, where each
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
is a restricted memory iteration, and each
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\delta}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
-name for a poset.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\underline{\mathbb{P}}
and
\dot{\underline{\mathbb{Q}}}
be a restricted memory alphabet on
C
. A
simplified partition product
based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}
is a restricted memory iteration
\mathbb{R}
of iterands
\langle\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}\mid\xi\in X\rangle
, with memory function

, and additional functions
\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}
on

\pi^{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}
for each
\xi\in X
, so that:

(1)

\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)\in C

(2)

\mathbb{P}_{\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)}

(3)

\mathbb{R}\restriction b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)

-rearrangement of
\mathbb{P}_{\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)}

(4)

\dot{\mathbb{U}}_{\xi}=\pi^{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi)})

of
\mathbb{R}

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{R}

Example 2.9
.

Before proceeding to the full definition of partition products, we give a few examples of simplified partition products.

(1)

First, let

\mathbb{P}_{\alpha_{0}}

(4)

Let

with large continuum.

Definition 2.10
.

A

on a set

\underline{\mathbb{P}}=\langle\mathbb{P}_{\delta}:\delta\in C\rangle
and

(1)

\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\delta

(2)

for each
\delta\in C

A

for the basic alphabet is a sequence
\vec{\varphi}=\langle\varphi_{\delta,\mu}\mid\delta\in C,\mu<\rho_{\delta}\rangle
so that
\varphi_{\delta,\mu}\colon\delta\longrightarrow\mu

Compared with the restricted memory alphabet in Definition

from the components in the alphabet up to

.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\underline{\mathbb{P}}
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}

C

\vec{\varphi}

(2)

(3)

(Collapse)

The existence of coherently collapsing sets

3

The Hull and Closure conditions in Definition

Lemma 2.20

(Rearrangement Lemma)

This summarizes facts from the previous subsection.

, as defined in Lemma
2.20
, so that
\dot{\tau}[G]=\pi(\dot{\tau})[\pi(G)]
. We also see that
\pi(\dot{\tau})
is an
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-name, since it is a
\pi\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
-name and since, by the second part of the current lemma,
\pi\left(\mathbb{R}\right)=\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction\sigma[X]
and
\sigma[X]
is base-closed. Furthermore,
\sigma[G]
(the pointwise image) is a subset of
G^{*}
, by the elementarity of
\sigma^{*}
. However, by the first part of the current lemma,
\sigma[G]=\left\{\sigma(p):p\in G\right\}=\left\{\pi(p):p\in G\right\}=\pi\left(G\right)
, and therefore

\dot{\tau}[G]=\pi(\dot{\tau})[\pi\left(G\right)]=\pi(\dot{\tau})[G^{*}].

Finally, if
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}\in M
and satisfies the assumptions in the statement of the lemma, then
\sigma(\dot{\mathbb{Q}})=\sigma[\dot{\mathbb{Q}}]
, and
\sigma[\dot{\mathbb{Q}}]=\pi(\dot{\mathbb{Q}})
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Before we give applications of the Rearrangement Lemma, we record our definition of an embedding.

Definition 2.23
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{*}
are partition products with respective domains
X
and
X^{*}
. We say that an injection
\sigma:X\longrightarrow X^{*}

embeds

\mathbb{R}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
if
\sigma:X\longrightarrow\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{R}
, and if
\sigma\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{R}}\right)=\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
and
\sigma\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}\right)=\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
.

It is straightforward to check that if
\sigma
is an embedding as in Definition
2.23
, and if
G^{*}
is generic over
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, then the filter
\sigma^{-1}\left(G^{*}\right):=\left\{p\in\mathbb{R}:\sigma(p)\in G^{*}\right\}
is generic over
\mathbb{R}
. We also remark that, in the context of the above definition,
\sigma\left(\mathbb{R}\right)=\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
.

Lemma 2.24
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}
is a partition product with domain
X
and
B\subseteq X
is base-closed. Then
\mathbb{R}
is isomorphic to a partition product
\mathbb{R}^{*}
with a domain
X^{*}
such that
B
is an initial segment of
X^{*}
and
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction B=\mathbb{R}\restriction B
.

Proof.

We define a map
\sigma
with domain
X
which will lift to give us
\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Let
\xi\in X
. If
\xi\in B
, then set
\sigma(\xi)=\xi
. On the other hand, if
\xi\in X\backslash B
, say that
\xi
is the
\gamma
th element of
X\backslash B
, then we define
\sigma(\xi)=\sup(X)+1+\gamma
.

We show that
\sigma
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{R}
, and then we may set
\mathbb{R}^{*}:=\sigma\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
by Lemma
2.20
. So suppose that
\zeta,\xi\in X
and
\zeta\in b(\xi)
; we check that
\sigma(\zeta)<\sigma(\xi)
. There are two cases. On the one hand, if
\xi\in B
, then
b(\xi)\subseteq B
, since
B
is base-closed, and therefore
\zeta\in B
. Then
\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta<\xi=\sigma(\xi)
. On the other hand, if
\xi\notin B
, then either
\zeta\in B
or not. If
\zeta\in B
, then
\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta<\sup(X)+1\leq\sigma(\xi)
, and if
\zeta\notin B
, then
\sigma(\zeta)<\sigma(\xi)
since
\sigma
is order-preserving on
X\backslash B
.
∎

It will be helpful later on to know that we can apply Lemma
2.24

\omega
-many times, as in the following corollary.

Corollary 2.25
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}
is a partition product with domain
X
and that for each
n<\omega
,
\pi_{n}
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
\langle B_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
is a
\subseteq
-increasing sequence of base-closed subsets of
X
where
B_{0}=\emptyset
and where
X=\bigcup_{n}B_{n}
. Then there is a partition product
\mathbb{R}^{*}
which has domain an ordinal
\rho^{*}
and an acceptable rearrangement
\sigma:X\longrightarrow\rho^{*}
of
\mathbb{R}
which lifts to an isomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{*}
and which also satisfies that for each
n<\omega
,
\sigma[B_{n}]
is an ordinal and
\pi_{n}\circ\sigma^{-1}
is order-preserving on
\sigma[B_{n+1}\backslash B_{n}]
.

Proof.

We aim to recursively construct a sequence
\langle\mathbb{R}_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
of partition products, where
\mathbb{R}_{n}
has domain
X_{n}
, and a sequence
\langle\sigma_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
of bijections, where
\sigma_{n}:X\longrightarrow X_{n}
, so that

(1)

\sigma_{n}
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{R}
;

(2)

\sigma_{n}[B_{n}]
is an ordinal, and in particular, an initial segment of
X_{n}
;

(3)

for each
k<m<\omega
,
\sigma_{k}[B_{k}]=\sigma_{m}[B_{k}]
;

(4)

for each
n<\omega
,
\pi_{n}\circ\sigma_{n+1}^{-1}
is order-preserving on
\sigma_{n+1}[B_{n+1}\backslash B_{n}]
.

Suppose that we can do this. Then we define a map
\sigma
on
X
, by taking
\sigma(\xi)
to be the eventual value of the sequence
\langle\sigma_{n}(\xi):n<\omega\rangle
; we see that this sequence is eventually constant by (3) and the assumption that
\bigcup_{n}B_{n}=X
. By (2) and (3),
\sigma[B_{n}]
is an ordinal, for each
n<\omega
, and therefore the range of
\sigma
is an ordinal, which we call
\rho^{*}
. Furthermore,
\pi_{n}\circ\sigma^{-1}
is order-preserving on
\sigma[B_{n+1}\backslash B_{n}]
by (4), and since
\sigma
and
\sigma_{n+1}
agree on
B_{n+1}
. Finally, by (1) we see that
\sigma
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{R}
, and we thus take
\mathbb{R}^{*}
to be the partition product isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
via
\sigma
, by Lemma
2.20
.

We now show how to create the above objects. Suppose that
\langle\mathbb{R}_{m}:m<n\rangle
and
\langle\sigma_{m}:m<n\rangle
have been constructed. If
n=0
, we take
\mathbb{R}_{0}=\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma_{0}
to be the identity; since
B_{0}=\emptyset
, this completes the base case. So suppose
n>0
. Apply Lemma
2.24
to the partition product
\mathbb{R}_{n-1}
and the base-closed subset
\sigma_{n-1}[B_{n}]
of
X_{n-1}
to create a partition product
\mathbb{R}_{n}
on a set
X_{n}
which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}_{n-1}
via the acceptable rearrangement
\tau_{n}:X_{n-1}\longrightarrow X_{n}
and which satisfies that
\sigma_{n-1}[B_{n}]
is an initial segment of
X_{n}
. Since
\sigma_{n-1}[B_{n-1}]
is an ordinal, by (2) applied to
n-1
, and since
\sigma_{n-1}[B_{n}]
is an initial segment of
X_{n}
, we see that
\tau_{n}
is the identity on
\sigma_{n-1}[B_{n-1}]
. Also, by composing
\tau_{n}
with a further function and relabeling if necessary, we may assume that
\pi_{n-1}\circ\tau_{n}^{-1}
just shifts the ordinals in
\sigma_{n-1}[B_{n}\backslash B_{n-1}]
in an order-preserving way and that
\tau_{n}\circ\sigma_{n-1}[B_{n}]
is an ordinal. We now take
\sigma_{n}
to be
\tau_{n}\circ\sigma_{n-1}
, and we see that
\sigma_{n}
and
\mathbb{R}_{n}
satisfy the recursive hypotheses.
∎

Lemma 2.26
.

Suppose that
\beta\in C\cap\kappa
and that
\mathbb{R}
is a partition product with domain
X
based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\beta+1)
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction(\beta+1)
. Then, letting
B:=\left\{\xi\in X:\operatorname{index}(\xi)<\beta\right\}
and
I:=\left\{\xi\in X:\operatorname{index}(\xi)=\beta\right\}
,
B
is base-closed, and
\mathbb{R}
is isomorphic to

(\mathbb{R}\restriction B)\ast\prod_{\xi\in I}\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta}\left[\pi_{\xi}^{-1}\left(\dot{G}_{B}\restriction b(\xi)\right)\right],

where
\dot{G}_{B}
is the canonical
\mathbb{R}\restriction B
-name for the generic filter.

Proof.

To see that
B
is base-closed, fix
\xi\in B
. Then for all
\zeta\in b(\xi)
,
\operatorname{index}(\zeta)<\operatorname{index}(\xi)<\beta
by Lemma
2.18
, and so
\zeta\in B
. Thus by Lemma
2.24
, we may assume that
B
is an initial segment of
X
, and hence
I
is a tail segment of
X
. Now let
G_{B}
be generic for
\mathbb{R}\restriction B
, and for each
\xi\in I
, let
G_{B,\xi}
denote
\pi_{\xi}^{-1}\left(G_{B}\restriction b(\xi)\right)
, which is generic for
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
. The sequence of posets
\langle\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta}[G_{B,\xi}]:\xi\in I\rangle
is in
V[G_{B}]
, and consequently the finite support iteration of
\langle\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta}[G_{B,\xi}]:\xi\in I\rangle
in
V[G_{B}]
is isomorphic to the (finite support) product
\prod_{\xi\in I}\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta}[G_{B,\xi}]
. Therefore, in
V
,
\mathbb{R}
is isomorphic to the poset in the statement of the lemma.
∎

Remark 2.27
.

The previous lemma shows that a partition product does indeed have product-like behavior, and it is part of the justification for our term “partition product.”

Lemma 2.28
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}
is a partition product with domain
X
based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
. Suppose
\kappa
is a limit ordinal, and let
\langle\kappa_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\delta\rangle
be cofinal in
\kappa
. Finally, let
B_{\alpha}:=\left\{\xi\in X:\operatorname{index}(\xi)<\alpha\right\}
. Then each
B_{\alpha}
is base closed,
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\alpha}
is a complete subposet of
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\beta}
when
\alpha\leq\beta
, and
\mathbb{R}
is the direct limit of the posets
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\alpha}
. In particular if each
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\alpha}
is c.c.c., then so is
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Base closure follows as in Lemma
2.26
. By Lemma
2.19
,
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\alpha}
is a complete subposet of
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\beta}
when
\alpha\leq\beta
.
\mathbb{R}
is the union of the posets
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\alpha}
since
\bigcup_{\alpha<\delta}B_{\alpha}=X
. The countable chain condition is preserved under this union since the supports are finite.
∎

2.4.
Further Remarks on Coherent Overlaps

In this subsection we state and prove a few consequences of the
Hull and Closure conditions (
1
), (
2
)
of Definition
2.11
. These results will, in combination with the ability to rearrange a partition product, allow us to find isomorphism types of sufficiently simple partition products inside many countably-closed
M\prec H(\omega_{3})
, as well as their transitive collapses (see Lemma
4.16
).

Lemma 2.29
.

Let
\mathbb{R}
be a partition product, say with domain
X
, based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in X
, set
\delta_{i}=\operatorname{index}(\xi_{i})
, for
i=1,2
, and suppose that
\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}
and
\rho_{\delta_{2}}<\omega_{3}
. Finally, let
A=\pi_{\xi_{2}}^{-1}[b(\xi_{1})\cap b(\xi_{2})]
. Then
A
is definable in
H(\omega_{3})
from
\vec{\varphi}
, the ordinals
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
, and any cofinal
Z\subseteq A
.

Proof.

Let
Z\subseteq A
be cofinal. For each
\alpha\in Z
, we have from Definition
2.16
(
3
) and condition (
1
) of Definition
2.11
that
A\cap\alpha=\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}]
. Therefore
A=\bigcup_{\alpha\in Z}\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}]
, which is
definable in
H(\omega_{3})
from
Z
,
\vec{\varphi}
,
\delta_{1}
, and
\delta_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 2.30
.

Let
\mathbb{R}
,
X
,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
, and
A
be as in Lemma
2.29
. Assume that for all
\xi\in C
,
\rho_{\xi}<\omega_{2}
. Let
M\prec H(\omega_{3})
be countably-closed containing the objects
\underline{\mathbb{P}}
,
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}
,
\vec{\varphi}
, and
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
. Then
A
is a member of
M
as well as the transitive collapse of
M
.

Proof.

First observe that
A
is a subset of
\rho_{\delta_{2}}
, which is a member of
M
. Since
\rho_{\delta_{2}}<\omega_{2}
and
M
contains
\omega_{1}
as a subset,
\rho_{\delta_{2}}\subseteq M
. In particular,
\sup(A)
is an element of
M
.

Consider the case that
\sup(A)
has countable cofinality. Then by the countable closure of
M
, we can find a cofinal subset
Z
of
A
inside
M
. By Lemma
2.29
, we then conclude that
A\in M
.

Now suppose that
\sup(A)
has uncountable cofinality. Recall from condition (
2
) of Definition
2.11
that
A
is countably closed in
\sup(A)
. Moreover, since
A\cap\alpha=\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}]
for each
\alpha\in A
, we know that the sequence of sets
\langle\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}]:\alpha\in A\rangle
is
\subseteq
-increasing. By the elementarity of
M
, we may find an
\omega
-closed, cofinal subset
Z
of
\sup(A)
such that
Z\in M
for which the sequence of sets
\langle\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}]:\alpha\in Z\rangle
is
\subseteq
-increasing. Combining this with the fact that
Z\cap A
is also
\omega
-closed and cofinal in
\sup(A)
, we have that

A=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A\cap Z}\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}]=\bigcup_{\alpha\in Z}\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}],

and hence
A
is in
M
, as
\bigcup_{\alpha\in Z}\varphi_{\delta_{2},\alpha}[\delta_{1}]
is in
M
by elementarity. Finally, since
A
is bounded in the ordinal
M\cap\omega_{2}
,
A
is fixed by the transitive collapse map.
∎

3.
Combining Partition Products

In this section, we develop the machinery necessary to combine partition products in various ways. This will be essential for later arguments where, in the context of working with a partition product
\mathbb{R}
, we will want to create another partition product
\mathbb{R}^{*}
into which
\mathbb{R}
embeds in a variety of ways. Forcing with
\mathbb{R}^{*}
will then add plenty of generics for
\mathbb{R}
, with various amounts of agreement or mutual genericity.

The main result of this section is a so-called “grafting lemma” which gives conditions under which, given partition products
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{R}
, we may extend
\mathbb{R}
to another partition product
\mathbb{R}^{*}
in such a way that
\mathbb{R}^{*}
subsumes an isomorphic image of
\mathbb{P}
; in this case
\mathbb{P}
is, in some sense, “grafted onto”
\mathbb{R}
. One trivial way of doing this, we will show, is to take the partition product
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{R}
. However, the issue becomes somewhat delicate if we desire, as later on we often will, that
\mathbb{R}
and the isomorphic copy of
\mathbb{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{*}
have coordinates in common, and hence share some part of their generics. Doing so requires that we keep track of more information about the structure of a partition product, and we begin with the relevant definition in the first subsection.

3.1.
Shadow Bases

Definition 3.1
.

A triple
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
is said to be a
shadow base
if the following conditions are satisfied:
\alpha\in C
,
\pi_{x}
has domain
\gamma_{x}
for some
\gamma_{x}\leq\rho_{\alpha}
, and
\pi_{x}:\gamma_{x}\longrightarrow x
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\restriction\gamma_{x}
.

Moreover, if
\mathbb{R}
is a partition product, say with domain
X
, we say that a shadow base
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
is an
\mathbb{R}
-
shadow base
if
x\subseteq X
is base-closed and if
\pi_{x}
embeds
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\restriction\gamma_{x}
into
\mathbb{R}\restriction x
.

For example, if
\mathbb{R}
is a partition product with domain
X
, then for any
\xi\in X
the triple
\langle b(\xi),\pi_{\xi},\operatorname{index}(\xi)\rangle
is an
\mathbb{R}
- “shadow” base; this is part of the motivation for the term. In practice, a shadow base will be an initial segment, in a sense we will specify soon, of such a triple.

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose that
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
are two shadow bases. We say that they
cohere
if the following holds: suppose that
\alpha\leq\beta
and that there is some
\zeta\in x\cap y
. Define
\mu_{x}:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(\zeta)
and
\mu_{y}:=\pi_{y}^{-1}(\zeta)
. Then

(1)

\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]\subseteq\pi_{y}[\mu_{y}]
; and

(2)

\pi_{y}^{-1}[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]]
coherently collapses
\langle\beta,\mu_{y}\rangle
to
\langle\alpha,\mu_{x}\rangle
.

A collection
\mathcal{B}
of shadow bases is said to
cohere
if any two elements of
\mathcal{B}
cohere.

Note that with this definition, item (3) of Definition
2.16
could be rephrased as saying that the two shadow bases
\langle b(\xi_{1}),\pi_{\xi_{1}},\operatorname{index}(\xi_{1})\rangle
and
\langle b(\xi_{2}),\pi_{\xi_{2}},\operatorname{index}(\xi_{2})\rangle
cohere.

Remark 3.3
.

It is straightforward to check that Corollary
2.30
holds for shadow bases too, in the following sense. Suppose that
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
are two coherent shadow bases, say with
\alpha\leq\beta
. Then
\pi_{y}^{-1}[x\cap y]
is a member of any
M
as in the statement of Corollary
2.30
, provided that
\alpha
and
\beta
, as well as the additional parameters
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\beta
,
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\beta
, and
\vec{\varphi}
, are all in
M
.

Definition 3.4
.

Given a shadow base
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and some
a\subseteq x
, we say that
a
is an
initial segment
of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
if
a
is of the form
\pi_{x}[\mu]
for some
\mu\leq\operatorname{dom}(\pi_{x})
.

Given two shadow bases
\langle{x_{0}},\pi_{x_{0}},\alpha_{0}\rangle
and
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
, we say that
\langle{x_{0}},\pi_{x_{0}},\alpha_{0}\rangle
is an
initial segment
of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
if
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
,
x_{0}
is an initial segment of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
, and
\pi_{x}\restriction\operatorname{dom}(\pi_{x_{0}})=\pi_{x_{0}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

A simple but useful observation is that if
\langle x_{0},\pi_{x_{0}},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
are two coherent shadow bases,
\langle x_{0},\pi_{x_{0}},\alpha\rangle
is an initial segment of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
, and
(x\backslash x_{0})\cap y=\emptyset
, then
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
cohere.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
are coherent shadow bases and
\alpha\leq\beta
. Then
\pi_{x}^{-1}[x\cap y]
is an ordinal
\leq\operatorname{dom}(\pi_{x})
, and hence
x\cap y
is an initial segment of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
.

Proof.

Fix
\xi\in x\cap y
. By the definition of coherence and the fact that
\alpha\leq\beta
, we see that
\pi_{x}^{-1}(\xi)+1\subseteq\pi_{x}^{-1}[x\cap y]
. Thus

\pi_{x}^{-1}[x\cap y]=\bigcup_{\xi\in x\cap y}(\pi_{x}^{-1}(\xi)+1),

and therefore
\pi_{x}^{-1}[x\cap y]
is an ordinal.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
are two coherent shadow bases, where
\alpha\leq\beta
. Let
\zeta\in x\cap y
, and define
\mu_{x}:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(\zeta)
and
\mu_{y}:=\pi_{y}^{-1}(\zeta)
. Then
\pi_{y}^{-1}\circ\pi_{x}
is an order preserving map from
\mu_{x}
into
\mu_{y}
. In particular,
\mu_{x}\leq\mu_{y}
, and
\pi_{x}^{-1}\circ\pi_{y}
is the transitive collapse of
\pi_{y}^{-1}[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]]
.

Proof.

By Definition
3.2
(1), we know that
\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]
is a subset of
\pi_{y}[\mu_{y}]
, and so
\pi_{y}^{-1}\circ\pi_{x}
is indeed a map from
\mu_{x}
into
\mu_{y}
. Let us abbreviate
\pi_{y}^{-1}\circ\pi_{x}
by
j
. Suppose that
\zeta<\eta<\mu_{x}
, and we show
j(\zeta)<j(\eta)
. Set
\zeta_{y}=j(\zeta)
and
\eta_{y}=j(\eta)
. Since
\pi_{x}(\eta)=\pi_{y}(\eta_{y})\in x\cap y
, Definition
3.2
(1) implies that
\pi_{x}[\eta]\subseteq\pi_{y}[\eta_{y}]
. Next, as
\zeta<\eta
,
\pi_{x}(\zeta)\in\pi_{x}[\eta]
, and so
\pi_{y}(\zeta_{y})\in\pi_{y}[\eta_{y}]
. Finally, since
\pi_{y}
is a bijection we conclude that
\zeta_{y}\in\eta_{y}
, i.e.,
j(\zeta)<j(\eta)
.
∎

As a result of the previous lemma, if two coherent shadow bases have the same “index”, then their intersection is an initial segment of both.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\alpha\rangle
are two coherent shadow bases and that
\zeta\in x\cap y
. Then
\zeta_{0}:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(\zeta)=\pi_{y}^{-1}(\zeta)
, and in fact,
\pi_{x}\restriction(\zeta_{0}+1)=\pi_{y}\restriction(\zeta_{0}+1)
.

Proof.

Fix
\eta\in x\cap y
. Since both shadow bases have index
\alpha
, we know from Lemma
3.7
that
\pi_{x}^{-1}(\eta)=\pi_{y}^{-1}(\eta)
. Since this holds for any
\eta\in x\cap y
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

In the context of Corollary
3.8
, we note that
\pi_{x}^{-1}[x\cap y]=\pi_{y}^{-1}[x\cap y]
is an ordinal
\leq\rho_{\alpha}
, and if
x\neq y
, then this ordinal is strictly less than
\rho_{\alpha}
.

We conclude this subsection with a very useful lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
,
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
, and
\langle z,\pi_{z},\gamma\rangle
are shadow bases such that
\alpha,\beta\leq\gamma
. Suppose further that
x\cap y\subseteq z
, that
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle z,\pi_{z},\gamma\rangle
cohere, and that
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle z,\pi_{z},\gamma\rangle
cohere. Then
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
cohere.

Proof.

By relabeling if necessary, we assume that
\alpha\leq\beta
. Let
\zeta\in x\cap y
, and we will show that (1) and (2) of Definition
3.2
hold. Define
\mu_{x}:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(\zeta)
and
\mu_{y}:=\pi^{-1}_{y}(\zeta)
. As
x\cap y\subseteq z
,
\zeta\in z
, and therefore we may also define
\mu_{z}:=\pi^{-1}_{z}(\zeta)
. Applying the coherence assumptions in the statement of the lemma, we conclude that

\pi_{z}^{-1}\left[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]\right]=\varphi_{\gamma,\mu_{z}}[\alpha]\,\text{ and }\,\pi_{z}^{-1}[\pi_{y}[\mu_{y}]]=\varphi_{\gamma,\mu_{z}}[\beta].

Since
\alpha\leq\beta
, it then follows that
\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]\subseteq\pi_{y}[\mu_{y}]
.

We next show that
\pi_{y}^{-1}[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]]=\varphi_{\beta,\mu_{y}}[\alpha]
. By Lemma
3.7
applied to the shadow bases
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle z,\pi_{z},\gamma\rangle
, we conclude that
\pi^{-1}_{y}\circ\pi_{z}
, which we abbreviate as
j_{z,y}
, is the transitive collapse of
\pi_{z}^{-1}[\pi_{y}[\mu_{y}]]
. Furthermore, the definition of coherence also implies that
j_{z,y}\circ\varphi_{\gamma,\mu_{z}}\restriction\beta=\varphi_{\beta,\mu_{y}}
. Since
\alpha\leq\beta
and since
\pi_{z}^{-1}[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]]=\varphi_{\gamma,\mu_{z}}[\alpha]
, we apply
j_{z,y}
to conclude that
\pi_{y}^{-1}[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]]=\varphi_{\beta,\mu_{y}}[\alpha]
.

Now let
j_{y,x}
denote the transitive collapse of
\pi_{y}^{-1}[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]]
; we check that
j_{y,x}\circ\varphi_{\beta,\mu_{y}}\restriction\alpha=\varphi_{\alpha,\mu_{x}}
. We also let
j_{z,x}
be the transitive collapse of
\pi_{z}^{-1}[\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}]]
. From Lemma
3.7
, we know that
j_{y,x}=\pi_{x}^{-1}\circ\pi_{y}
and
j_{z,x}=\pi_{x}^{-1}\circ\pi_{z}
. Thus
j_{z,x}=j_{y,x}\circ j_{z,y}
. Since
j_{z,y}\circ\varphi_{\gamma,\mu_{z}}\restriction\beta=\varphi_{\beta,\mu_{y}}
and
\alpha\leq\beta
, we conclude that
\varphi_{\alpha,\mu_{x}}=j_{y,x}\circ\varphi_{\beta,\mu_{y}}\restriction\alpha
, completing the proof.
∎

Note that the proof of Lemma
3.10
uses the Collapse condition (
3
) of Definition
2.11
for
y
and
z
, in order to prove one of the
other
conditions, namely the Hull condition (
1
), for
x
and
y
.

3.2.
Enriched Partition Products

In this subsection, we will consider in greater detail how shadow bases interact with partition products. We begin with the following definition.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\mathbb{R}
be a partition product with domain
X
. A collection
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbb{R}
-shadow bases is said to be
\mathbb{R}
-full
if for all
\xi\in X
,
\langle b(\xi),\pi_{\xi},\operatorname{index}(\xi)\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
.
\mathcal{B}
is said to be an
\mathbb{R}
-enrichment
if
\mathcal{B}
is both coherent and
\mathbb{R}
-full.

An
enriched partition product
is a pair
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
where
\mathcal{B}
is an enrichment of
\mathbb{R}
.

The next definition is a strengthening of the notion of a base-closed subset which allows us to restrict an enrichment.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
be an enriched partition product with domain
X
. A base-closed subset
B\subseteq X
is said to
cohere
with
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
if for all triples
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
in
\mathcal{B}
and for every
\zeta\in B\cap x
, if
\zeta=\pi_{x}(\zeta_{0})
, say, then
\pi_{x}[\zeta_{0}]\subseteq B
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose that
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
is an enriched partition product with domain
X
and that
B\subseteq X
coheres with
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
. Let
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
, and define
\pi_{x\cap B}
to be the restriction of
\pi_{x}
mapping onto
x\cap B
. Then
\langle x\cap B,\pi_{x\cap B},\alpha\rangle
is a shadow base.

Additionally, if we define

\mathcal{B}\restriction B:=\left\{\langle x\cap B,\pi_{x\cap B},\alpha\rangle:\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}\right\},

then
(\mathbb{R}\restriction B,\mathcal{B}\restriction B)
is an enriched partition product.

Proof.

To see that
\langle x\cap B,\pi_{x\cap B},\alpha\rangle
is a shadow base, it suffices to show that
\pi^{-1}_{x}[x\cap B]
is an ordinal. This holds since for each
\xi\in x\cap B
, by the coherence of
B
with
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
,
\pi_{x}^{-1}(\xi)+1\subseteq\pi_{x}^{-1}[x\cap B]
.

Now we need to verify that
(\mathbb{R}\restriction B,\mathcal{B}\restriction B)
is an enriched partition product. It is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{B}\restriction B
is
(\mathbb{R}\restriction B)
-full, since
B
is base-closed and since the base and index functions for
\mathbb{R}\restriction B
are exactly the restrictions of those for
\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, we see that each shadow base in
\mathcal{B}\restriction B
is in fact an
(\mathbb{R}\restriction B)
-shadow base. Thus we need to check that any two elements of
\mathcal{B}\restriction B
cohere. Fix
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
in
\mathcal{B}
, and suppose that there exists
\zeta\in(x\cap B)\cap(y\cap B)
. Let
\mu_{x}<\rho_{\alpha}
be such that
\zeta=\pi_{x\cap B}(\mu_{x})
, and let
\mu_{y}<\rho_{\beta}
be such that
\zeta=\pi_{y\cap B}(\mu_{y})
. Then since
B
coheres with
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
,
\pi_{x}\restriction(\mu_{x}+1)=\pi_{x\cap B}\restriction(\mu_{x}+1)
, and similarly
\pi_{y}\restriction(\mu_{y}+1)=\pi_{y\cap B}\restriction(\mu_{y}+1)
. Therefore conditions (1) and (2) of Definition
3.2
at
\zeta
follow from their applications to
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
at
\zeta
.
∎

Definition 3.14
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{R}
are partition products and
\sigma
embeds
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{R}
. If
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
is a
\mathbb{P}
-shadow base, we define
\sigma(\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle)
to be the triple

\langle\sigma[x],\sigma\circ\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a collection of
\mathbb{P}
-shadow bases, we define
\sigma\left(\mathcal{B}\right):=\left\{\sigma(t):t\in\mathcal{B}\right\}
.

The proof of the following lemma is routine.

Lemma 3.15
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{R}
are partition products,
\sigma
embeds
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{B}
is a collection of
\mathbb{P}
-shadow bases. Then
\sigma\left(\mathcal{B}\right)
is a collection of
\mathbb{R}
-shadow bases.

The following technical lemma will be of some use later.

Lemma 3.16
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{*}
are partition products,
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are embeddings of
\mathbb{R}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, and
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
are two coherent
\mathbb{R}
-shadow bases, with
\alpha\leq\beta
. Let
a
be an initial segment of
x
such that
a\subseteq y
,
\sigma_{1}\restriction a=\sigma_{2}\restriction a
, and
\sigma_{1}[x\backslash a]
is disjoint from
\sigma_{2}[y\backslash a]
. Then
\sigma_{1}(\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle)
and
\sigma_{2}(\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle)
are coherent
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-shadow bases.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.15
, we see that
\sigma_{1}(\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle)
and
\sigma_{2}(\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle)
are
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-shadow bases. Furthermore, if
\zeta^{*}\in\sigma_{1}[x]\cap\sigma_{2}[y]
, then
\zeta^{*}
must be in
\sigma_{1}[a]\cap\sigma_{2}[a]
, since
\sigma_{1}[x\backslash a]\cap\sigma_{2}[y\backslash a]=\emptyset
and since
\sigma_{1}\restriction a=\sigma_{2}\restriction a
. As the injections
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are equal on
a
, we then have that
\sigma_{1}^{-1}(\zeta^{*})=\sigma_{2}^{-1}(\zeta^{*})=:\zeta
. Thus
\zeta\in x\cap y
, and the coherence of the original triples at
\zeta
implies the coherence of their images at
\zeta^{*}
.
∎

We next define a notion of embedding for enriched partition products.

Definition 3.17
.

Suppose that
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
is an enriched partition product with domain
X
,
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
is an enriched partition product with domain
Y
, and
\sigma:X\longrightarrow Y
is a function. We say that
\sigma

embeds

(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})

into

(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
if
\sigma
embeds
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{R}
, as in Definition
2.23
, and if
\sigma\left(\mathcal{B}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{D}
.

We may now state and prove the Grafting Lemma; proving this lemma is one of the main reasons we introduced shadow bases.

Lemma 3.18
.

(Grafting Lemma)
Let
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
and
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
be enriched partition products with respective domains
X
and
Y
. Suppose that
\hat{X}\subseteq X
coheres with
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
and that there is a map
\sigma:\hat{X}\longrightarrow Y
which embeds
(\mathbb{P}\restriction\hat{X},\mathcal{B}\restriction\hat{X})
into
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
.Then there is an enriched partition product
(\mathbb{R}^{*},\mathcal{D}^{*})
with domain
Y^{*}
such that
Y\subseteq Y^{*}
,
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction Y=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{*}
, and such that there is an extension
\sigma^{*}
of
\sigma
which embeds
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
into
(\mathbb{R}^{*},\mathcal{D}^{*})
and which satisfies
\sigma^{*}\left[X\backslash\hat{X}\right]=Y^{*}\backslash Y
.

Proof.

We first define the map
\sigma^{*}
extending
\sigma
: if
\xi\in\hat{X}
, then set
\sigma^{*}(\xi):=\sigma(\xi)
. If
\xi\in X\backslash\hat{X}
, say
\xi
is the
\gamma
th such element, then we set
\sigma^{*}(\xi):=\sup(Y)+1+\gamma
. Then
\sigma^{*}
is an acceptable rearrangement, since
\hat{X}
is base-closed. Let
Y^{*}:=Y\cup\operatorname{ran}(\sigma^{*})
. Recalling that
\sigma
embeds
\mathbb{P}\restriction\hat{X}
into
\mathbb{R}
, we know that
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)=\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{R}}\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
and that
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)=\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
. Thus if we define
\operatorname{base}^{*}:=\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{R}}\cup\,\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}:=\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}\cup\,\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
, then
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
are functions.

Before we check that
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
support a partition product on
Y^{*}
, we need to check that
\mathcal{D}\cup\sigma^{*}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)
consists of a coherent collection of shadow bases. To facilitate the discussion, we set
\mathcal{B}^{*}:=\sigma^{*}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}:=\mathcal{D}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}
. So fix
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
and
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
in
\mathcal{D}
, and we check that
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
cohere, where
x^{*}:=\sigma^{*}[x]
and
\pi_{x^{*}}:=\sigma^{*}\circ\pi_{x}
. By our assumption that
\sigma
embeds
(\mathbb{P}\restriction\hat{X},\mathcal{B}\restriction\hat{X})
into
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
, we know that
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle\sigma[x\cap\hat{X}],\sigma\circ\pi_{x\cap\hat{X}},\alpha\rangle
cohere. However,
\langle\sigma[x\cap\hat{X}],\sigma\circ\pi_{x\cap\hat{X}},\alpha\rangle
is an initial segment of
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
, as in Definition
3.4
. Therefore by Remark
3.5
, since
\sigma^{*}[X\backslash\hat{X}]
is disjoint from
y
, we have that
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
cohere.

We now check that
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
support a partition product on
Y^{*}
. Conditions (1) and (2) of Definition
2.16
for
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
follow because they hold for
\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}
, as well as
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
and
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
individually, and since
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
are functions. Thus we need to verify condition (3). For this it suffices to check that it holds for
\xi_{1}\in Y
and
\xi_{2}\in Y^{*}\backslash Y
. Rephrasing, we need to show that the triples
\langle b^{*}(\xi_{1}),\pi^{*}_{\xi_{1}},\operatorname{index}^{*}(\xi_{1})\rangle
and
\langle b^{*}(\xi_{2}),\pi^{*}_{\xi_{2}},\operatorname{index}^{*}(\xi_{2})\rangle
cohere. The first triple equals
\langle b_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi_{1}),\pi^{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi_{1}},\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi_{1})\rangle
and so is in
\mathcal{D}
since
\mathcal{D}
is
\mathbb{R}
-full. The second triple is in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
, since it equals
\langle\sigma^{*}[b_{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\xi}_{2})],\sigma^{*}\circ\pi^{\mathbb{P}}_{\hat{\xi}_{2}},\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\xi}_{2})\rangle
, where
\sigma^{*}(\hat{\xi}_{2})=\xi_{2}
. Consequently, both shadow bases are in
\mathcal{D}^{*}
and are therefore coherent, by the previous paragraph. Thus condition (3) of Definition
2.16
is satisfied.

Thus
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
support a partition product on
Y^{*}
, which we call
\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Since the restrictions of
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
to
Y
equal
\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively, we have that
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction Y=\mathbb{R}
. Additionally,
\sigma^{*}
embeds
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, since
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
restricted to
\operatorname{ran}(\sigma^{*})
equal
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
and
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
respectively. Thus it remains to check that
\mathcal{D}^{*}
is an enrichment of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, and for this, it only remains to check that
\mathcal{D}^{*}
is
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-full. However,
\mathcal{D}
is
\mathbb{R}
-full, and since
\mathcal{B}
is
\mathbb{P}
-full,
\mathcal{B}^{*}
is full with respect to
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction\operatorname{ran}(\sigma^{*})
. Thus
\mathcal{D}^{*}
is
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-full.
∎

Definition 3.19
.

Let
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
,
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
,
(\mathbb{R}^{*},\mathcal{D}^{*})
,
\hat{X}
,
\sigma
, and
\sigma^{*}
be as in Lemma
3.18
. We will say in this case that
(\mathbb{R}^{*},\mathcal{D}^{*})
is
the extension of

(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})

by grafting

(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})

over

\sigma
, and we will call
\sigma^{*}
the
grafting embedding
.

Note that as a corollary, we get that the product of two partition products is isomorphic to a partition product; this fact could also be proven directly from the definitions.

Corollary 3.20
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{R}
are partition products with respective domains
X
and
Y
. Then
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{R}
is isomorphic to a partition product
\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

In fact, by Lemma
2.20
we may assume that
X\cap Y=\emptyset
, that
\mathbb{R}^{*}
is a partition product on
X\cup Y
, and that
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction X=\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction Y=\mathbb{R}
. Finally, in this case, if
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{D}
are enrichments of
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{R}
respectively, then
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{D}
is an enrichment of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

The following technical lemma gives a situation under which, after creating a single grafting embedding, we may extend a number of other embeddings without further grafting; it will be used later in constructing preassignments (see Lemma
5.4
).

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
and
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
be enriched partition products with domains
X
and
Y
respectively. Suppose that
X
can be written as
X=X_{0}\cup X_{1}
, where both
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
cohere with
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite collection of maps which embed
(\mathbb{P}\restriction X_{0},\mathcal{B}\restriction X_{0})
into
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
, and suppose that for each
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
,

\sigma_{0}[X_{0}\cap X_{1}]=\sigma_{1}[X_{0}\cap X_{1}].

Finally, fix a particular
\sigma_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
, let
(\mathbb{R}^{*},\mathcal{D}^{*})
be the extension of
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
by grafting
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
over
\sigma_{0}
, and let
\sigma_{0}^{*}
be the grafting embedding. Then for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}
, the map

\sigma^{*}:=\sigma\cup(\sigma_{0}^{*}\restriction(X_{1}\backslash X_{0}))

embeds
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
into
(\mathbb{R}^{*},\mathcal{D}^{*})
.

Proof.

Fix
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}
. Before we continue, we note that
\sigma^{*}
and
\sigma^{*}_{0}
agree on all of
X_{1}
, since they agree on
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
by assumption and on
X_{1}\backslash X_{0}
by definition.

We first verify that
\sigma^{*}
provides an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{P}
. So let
\zeta,\xi\in X
so that
\zeta\in b_{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)
. If
\xi\in X_{0},
then
\zeta
is too, since
X_{0}
is base-closed. Then
\sigma^{*}(\zeta)=\sigma(\zeta)<\sigma(\xi)=\sigma^{*}(\xi)
, since
\sigma
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{P}\restriction X_{0}
. On the other hand, if
\xi\in X_{1}
, then
\zeta\in X_{1}
. Since
\sigma^{*}\restriction X_{1}=\sigma^{*}_{0}\restriction X_{1}
, and
\sigma^{*}_{0}\restriction X_{1}
is an acceptable rearrangement of
\mathbb{P}\restriction X_{1}
, we get that
\sigma^{*}(\zeta)<\sigma^{*}(\xi)
.

We may now see that
\sigma^{*}
embeds
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, as follows: let
\operatorname{base}^{*}
and
\operatorname{index}^{*}
be the functions which support
\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Then
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
and
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
agree with
\operatorname{index}^{*}
and
\operatorname{base}^{*}
on
\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
, since
\sigma
embeds
\mathbb{P}\restriction X_{0}
into
\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore,
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
and
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
agree with
\operatorname{index}^{*}
and
\operatorname{base}^{*}
on
\sigma^{*}[X_{1}]
, since they are equal, respectively, to
\sigma_{0}^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
and
\sigma^{*}_{0}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
restricted to
\sigma^{*}_{0}[X_{1}]
. Thus
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{index}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
and
\sigma^{*}\left(\operatorname{base}_{\mathbb{P}}\right)
are equal to the restriction of
\operatorname{index}^{*}
and
\operatorname{base}^{*}
to
\operatorname{ran}(\sigma^{*})
, and consequently,
\sigma^{*}
embeds
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

We finish the proof of the lemma by showing that
\sigma^{*}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{*}
. To see this, fix some
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
. We first claim that either
x\subseteq X_{0}
or
x\subseteq X_{1}
. If this is false, then there exist
\alpha\in x\backslash X_{0}
and
\beta\in x\backslash X_{1}
. Since
X_{0}\cup X_{1}=X
, we then have
\alpha\in X_{1}
and
\beta\in X_{0}
. We suppose, by relabeling if necessary, that
\alpha_{0}:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(\alpha)<\pi_{x}^{-1}(\beta)=:\beta_{0}
. By the coherence of
X_{0}
with
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{B})
, we conclude that
\pi_{x}[\beta_{0}]\subseteq X_{0}
. However,
\alpha=\pi_{x}(\alpha_{0})\in\pi_{x}[\beta_{0}]
, and therefore
\alpha\in X_{0}
, a contradiction.

We now show that the shadow base
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
is in
\mathcal{D}^{*}
, where
x^{*}:=\sigma^{*}[x]
and
\pi_{x^{*}}=\sigma^{*}\circ\pi_{x}
. On the one hand, if
x\subseteq X_{0}
, then the shadow base
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
is in
\mathcal{B}\restriction X_{0}
, and therefore
\langle\sigma[x],\sigma\circ\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
is a member of
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{*}
. Since
\sigma=\sigma^{*}\restriction X_{0}
,
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle=\langle\sigma[x],\sigma\circ\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
, completing this subcase. On the other hand, if
x\subseteq X_{1}
, then we see that
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle=\langle\sigma_{0}^{*}[x],\sigma_{0}^{*}\circ\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
, since
\sigma^{*}\restriction X_{1}=\sigma_{0}^{*}\restriction X_{1}
. It is therefore a member of
\mathcal{D}^{*}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Simplifying the Task: Finitely Generated Partition Products

In this section we begin the basic inductive step of constructing a single highly symmetric preassignment name. We make the following assumption, which we will secure through an induction later on.

Assumption 4.1(a)
The
\mathsf{CH}
holds.
\kappa<\omega_{2}
is in
C
, and for each
\xi\in C\cap\kappa
,
\rho_{\xi}
is below
\omega_{2}
. Additionally, the
\kappa
-alphabetical partition product
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
is defined, and in particular,
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
is a partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
. We also assume that the
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
-names
\dot{S}_{\kappa}
and
\dot{\chi}_{\kappa}
are defined and satisfy that
\dot{S}_{\kappa}
names a countable basis for a second countable, Hausdorff topology on
\omega_{1}
and
\dot{\chi}_{\kappa}
names a coloring, as in the definition of
\mathsf{OCA}_{ARS}
, which is open with respect to the topology generated by
\dot{S}_{\kappa}
.

Recall that we want substantial symmetry for the poset
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}
. In the introduction we mentioned, for example, that if
G_{L}\times G_{R}
is generic for
{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa}\times{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa}
, then we need
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}[G_{L}]\times\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}[G_{R}]
to be c.c.c. in
V[G_{L}\times G_{R}]
. In fact we need to handle not just interpretations of
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}
by generics arising through powers of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa}
, but any combination of generics arising through copies of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa}
in partition products based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
. We need to know that any product of such interpretations is c.c.c. In other words, we need to construct
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}
in such a way that any partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
is c.c.c.

We will inductively assume:

Assumption 4.1(b)
Any partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
is c.c.c.

We refer to the conjunction of Assumption 4.1(a) and 4.1(b) as Assumption 4.1. Our basic step goal then is to construct, under Assumption 4.1, a
{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa}
-name
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}
which secures Assumption 4.1(b) at
\kappa+1
. In this section we make a series of reductions to streamline and simplify this goal. We will complete the basic step construction in Section 5, and in Section 6 we will use this as part of an inductive construction of a sequence
\underline{\mathbb{P}}
which provides the right building blocks for our main theorem.

4.1.
\kappa
-Suitable Collections

We now consider how various copies of
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
fit into a partition product
\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathbb{R}
is based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
. Even though we have yet to construct the name
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}
, we would still like to isolate the relevant behavior of copies of
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
inside such an
\mathbb{R}
which these copies
would
have if
\mathbb{R}

were
of the form

\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction\left\{\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathbb{R}^{*}):\operatorname{index}(\xi)<\kappa\right\},

for some partition product
\mathbb{R}^{*}
based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
. Put differently, we need a mechanism to enforce that the copies of
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
behave like they would after we construct
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{R}
be a partition product with domain
X
based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
. Let
\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
be a set of pairs, where each
B_{\iota}\subseteq X
is base-closed and where
\psi_{\iota}:\rho_{\kappa}\longrightarrow B_{\iota}
is a bijection which embeds
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
into
\mathbb{R}
. We say that the collection
\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
is
\kappa
-
suitable with respect to

\mathbb{R}
if

\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota},\kappa\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}\cup\left\{\langle b(\xi),\pi_{\xi},\operatorname{index}(\xi)\rangle:\xi\in X\right\}

is a coherent set of
\mathbb{R}
-shadow bases.

Moreover, if
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
is an enriched partition product, we say that
\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
is
\kappa
-
suitable with respect to

(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
if
\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota},\kappa\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and if
\alpha\leq\kappa
for all
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
.

The remaining results in this subsection will develop in detail a number of technical properties of
\kappa
-suitable collections. The main point to keep in mind is that in a
\kappa
-suitable collection, any two copies of
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
agree on some initial segment of
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
and are disjoint afterwards. In the later construction of preassignments (see Lemma
5.4
) this allows a natural induction on the “height” of the collection, namely, the maximal such agreement.

As the next lemma shows,
\kappa
-suitable collections give us subsets which cohere with the original partition product, since the indices of the triples in the enrichment do not exceed
\kappa
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
is
\kappa
-suitable with respect to an enriched partition product
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
. Then for any
I_{0}\subseteq I
,
\bigcup_{\iota\in I_{0}}B_{\iota}
coheres with
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

Let
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
, and suppose that there exists
\zeta\in(\bigcup_{\iota\in I_{0}}B_{\iota})\cap x
. Fix some
\iota\in I_{0}
such that
\zeta\in B_{\iota}\cap x
. Then
\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota},\kappa\rangle
is in
\mathcal{B}
. Furthermore,
\alpha\leq\kappa
, by definition of
\kappa
-suitability with respect to
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is coherent, by definition of an enrichment, and since
\alpha\leq\kappa
, we have by Definition
3.2
that

\pi_{x}[\pi_{x}^{-1}(\zeta)]\subseteq\psi_{\iota}[\psi_{\iota}^{-1}(\zeta)].

Since ran
(\psi_{\iota})=B_{\iota}
, this finishes the proof.
∎

We will often be interested in the following strengthening of the notion of an embedding, one which preserves the
\kappa
-suitable structure.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{*}
be two partition products, and let
\mathcal{S}=\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}=\left\{\langle B^{*}_{\eta},\psi^{*}_{\eta}\rangle:\eta\in I^{*}\right\}
be
\kappa
-suitable collections with respect to
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{*}
respectively. An embedding
\sigma
of
\mathbb{R}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
is said to be
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{*})
-
suitable
if for each
\iota\in I
, there is some
\eta\in I^{*}
such that
\sigma\restriction B_{\iota}
isomorphs
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\iota}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction B^{*}_{\eta}
and
\psi^{*}_{\eta}=\sigma\circ\psi_{\iota}
. A collection
\mathcal{F}
of embeddings is said to be
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{*})
-
suitable
if each
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}
is
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{*})
-suitable.

If
\sigma
is
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{*})
-suitable, we let
h_{\sigma}
denote the
map from
I
into
I^{*}
such that

\eta=h_{\sigma}(\iota)
witnesses suitability at
\iota
, for each
\iota\in I
.

The following technical lemmas will be used in the next section.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
is
\kappa
-suitable with respect to an enriched partition product
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
and that the elements of
\left\{B_{\iota}:\iota\in I\right\}
are pairwise disjoint. Then for any
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
,
x\cap(\bigcup_{\iota\in I}B_{\iota})=x\cap B_{\iota_{0}}
for a unique
\iota_{0}\in I
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise, and fix
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
as well as distinct
\iota_{0},\iota_{1}\in I
such that
x\cap B_{\iota_{0}}\neq\emptyset
and
x\cap B_{\iota_{1}}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\zeta\in x\cap B_{\iota_{0}}
and
\eta\in x\cap B_{\iota_{1}}
. Then
\zeta\neq\eta
, since
B_{\iota_{0}}\cap B_{\iota_{1}}=\emptyset
. Define
\zeta_{0}:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(\zeta)
and
\eta_{0}:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(\eta)
. Since
\zeta_{0}\neq\eta_{0}
, we suppose, by relabeling if necessary, that
\zeta_{0}<\eta_{0}
. By definition of an enrichment, we know that
\langle B_{\iota_{1}},\psi_{\iota_{1}},\kappa\rangle
and
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
cohere, and since
\alpha\leq\kappa
and
\eta\in B_{\iota_{1}}\cap x
, we conclude that
\pi_{x}[\eta_{0}]\subseteq B_{\iota_{1}}
. However,
\zeta_{0}<\eta_{0}
, and so
\zeta=\pi_{x}(\zeta_{0})\in\pi_{x}[\eta_{0}]
, which implies that
\zeta\in B_{\iota_{1}}
. This contradicts the fact that
B_{\iota_{0}}\cap B_{\iota_{1}}=\emptyset
.
∎

The next lemma gives a sufficient condition for creating an enrichment. It will be used as part of the base case of the main lemma which constructs preassignments (see Lemma
5.4
), in which the
\kappa
-suitable collection is isomorphic to a product (i.e., there is no overlap between the various copies of
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
in the
\kappa
-suitable collection).

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}=\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
is
\kappa
-suitable with respect to an enriched partition product
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
and that the elements of
\left\{B_{\iota}:\iota\in I\right\}
are pairwise disjoint. Suppose further that
\mathbb{R}^{*}
is a partition product with domain
X^{*}
and that
\mathcal{S}^{*}=\left\{\langle B^{*}_{\eta},\psi^{*}_{\eta}\rangle:\eta\in I^{*}\right\}
is
\kappa
-suitable with respect to
\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Finally, set
\hat{X}:=\bigcup_{\iota\in I}B_{\iota}
, and suppose that there exists a finite collection
\mathcal{F}
of
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{*})
-suitable embeddings of
\mathbb{R}\restriction\hat{X}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
such that for any distinct
\iota_{0},\iota_{1}\in I
and any (not necessarily distinct)
\pi_{0},\pi_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
,

B^{*}_{h_{\pi_{0}}(\iota_{0})}\cap B^{*}_{h_{\pi_{1}}(\iota_{1})}=\emptyset,

where for each
\pi\in\mathcal{F}
,
h_{\pi}
is the associated
map from Definition
4.4
. Then

\mathcal{B}^{*}:=\left\{\langle b^{*}(\xi),\pi^{*}_{\xi},\operatorname{index}^{*}(\xi)\rangle:\xi\in X^{*}\right\}\cup\bigcup_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}}\pi\left(\mathcal{B}\restriction\hat{X}\right)\cup\left\{\langle B^{*}_{\eta},\psi^{*}_{\eta},\kappa\rangle:\eta\in I^{*}\right\}

is an enrichment of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}
is
\kappa
-suitable with respect to
(\mathbb{R}^{*},\mathcal{B}^{*})
.

Proof.

We will first show that
\bigcup_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}}\pi\left(\mathcal{B}\restriction\hat{X}\right)
is a coherent collection of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-shadow bases. Since each
\pi\in\mathcal{F}
is an embedding of
\mathbb{R}\restriction\hat{X}
into
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, Lemma
3.15
implies that this is a set of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-shadow bases. Thus we check coherence.

Fix
\pi_{0},\pi_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
and
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle,\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle\in\mathcal{B}\restriction\hat{X}
, and assume, by relabeling if necessary, that
\alpha\leq\beta
. We show that
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y^{*},\pi_{y^{*}},\beta\rangle
cohere, where
x^{*}:=\pi_{0}[x]
and
\pi_{x^{*}}:=\pi_{0}\circ\pi_{x}
, and where
y^{*}:=\pi_{1}[y]
,
\pi_{y^{*}}:=\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{y}
. By Lemma
4.5
, and since
x
and
y
are subsets of
\hat{X}
, we may fix
\iota_{0},\iota_{1}\in I
such that
x=x\cap\hat{X}=x\cap B_{\iota_{0}}
and
y=y\cap\hat{X}=y\cap B_{\iota_{1}}
. There are two cases.

First suppose that
\iota_{0}\neq\iota_{1}
. Then we must have that
x^{*}\cap y^{*}=\emptyset
. To see this, observe that

x^{*}=\pi_{0}[x]=\pi_{0}[x\cap B_{\iota_{0}}]\subseteq B^{*}_{h_{\pi_{0}}(\iota_{0})},

and

y^{*}=\pi_{1}[y]=\pi_{1}[y\cap B_{\iota_{1}}]\subseteq B^{*}_{h_{\pi_{1}}(\iota_{1})}.

Therefore
x^{*}\cap y^{*}=\emptyset
, as
B^{*}_{h_{\pi_{0}}(\iota_{0})}\cap B^{*}_{h_{\pi_{1}}(\iota_{1})}=\emptyset
, by assumption. We thus trivially have the coherence of
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y^{*},\pi_{y^{*}},\beta\rangle
in this case.

On the other hand, suppose that
\iota:=\iota_{0}=\iota_{1}
. Define
a\subseteq x
to be the largest initial segment (see Definition
3.4
) of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
on which
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
agree, and set
a^{*}:=\pi_{0}[a]=\pi_{1}[a]
. In order to see that
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle y^{*},\pi_{y^{*}},\beta\rangle
cohere, it suffices, in light of Lemma
3.16
, to show that
\pi_{0}[x\backslash a]
is disjoint from
\pi_{1}[y\backslash a]
. Towards this end, fix some
\zeta^{*}\in x^{*}\cap y^{*}
, and suppose for a contradiction that
\zeta^{*}\notin a^{*}
. Define
\mu_{x}:=\pi_{x^{*}}^{-1}(\zeta^{*})
, and observe that
\mu_{x}
is greater than the ordinal
\pi_{x}^{-1}[a]
, since
\zeta^{*}\notin a^{*}
. Using the abbreviation
\eta_{i}:=h_{\pi_{i}}(\iota)
, for
i\in\left\{0,1\right\}
, we see that
\zeta^{*}\in B^{*}_{\eta_{0}}\cap B^{*}_{\eta_{1}}
, as
x^{*}=\pi_{0}[x\cap B_{\iota}]\subseteq B^{*}_{\eta_{0}}
, and as
y^{*}=\pi_{1}[y\cap B_{\iota}]\subseteq B^{*}_{\eta_{1}}
. Set
\zeta_{0}:=(\psi^{*}_{\eta_{0}})^{-1}(\zeta^{*})
. Since the
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-shadow bases
\langle B^{*}_{\eta_{0}},\psi^{*}_{\eta_{0}},\kappa\rangle
and
\langle B^{*}_{\eta_{1}},\psi^{*}_{\eta_{1}},\kappa\rangle
cohere, Corollary
3.8
implies that
\psi^{*}_{\eta_{0}}\restriction(\zeta_{0}+1)=\psi^{*}_{\eta_{1}}\restriction(\zeta_{0}+1)
.

Now we observe that

\pi_{x^{*}}(\mu_{x})=\pi_{0}(\pi_{x}(\mu_{x}))=\zeta^{*}=\psi_{\eta_{0}}^{*}(\zeta_{0})=\pi_{0}(\psi_{\iota}(\zeta_{0})),

and therefore
\pi_{x}(\mu_{x})=\psi_{\iota}(\zeta_{0})
. Let us call this ordinal
\zeta
. Since
\zeta\in B_{\iota}\cap x
, the coherence of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
with
\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota},\kappa\rangle
and the fact that
\alpha\leq\kappa
imply that

\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}+1]\subseteq\psi_{\iota}[\zeta_{0}+1].

As noted above,
\psi^{*}_{\eta_{0}}\restriction(\zeta_{0}+1)=\psi^{*}_{\eta_{1}}\restriction(\zeta_{0}+1)
,
and since
\psi^{*}_{\eta_{i}}=\pi_{i}\circ\psi_{\iota}
by Definition
4.4
,
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
agree on
\psi_{\iota}[\zeta_{0}+1]
. In particular, they agree on
\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}+1]
. Thus
\pi_{x}[\mu_{x}+1]
is an initial segment of
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
on which
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
agree. Since
\zeta=\pi_{x}(\mu_{x})\notin a
, this contradicts the maximality of
a
.

At this point, we have shown that
\bigcup_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}}\pi\left(\mathcal{B}\restriction\hat{X}\right)
is a coherent collection of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-shadow bases. We introduce the abbreviation

\mathcal{B}^{*}_{0}:=\left\{\langle b^{*}(\xi),\pi^{*}_{\xi},\operatorname{index}^{*}(\xi)\rangle:\xi\in X^{*}\right\}\cup\left\{\langle B^{*}_{\eta},\psi^{*}_{\eta},\kappa\rangle:\eta\in I^{*}\right\}.

We know that
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{0}
is a coherent set of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-shadow bases, by the definition of
\kappa
-suitability. Therefore, to finish showing that
\mathcal{B}^{*}
is an enrichment of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, we now check that if
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{0}
,
\pi\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle\in\mathcal{B}\restriction\hat{X}
, then
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
cohere, where
x^{*}:=\pi[x]
and
\pi_{x^{*}}=\pi\circ\pi_{x}
. By Lemma
4.5
, let
\iota\in I
be such that
x=x\cap\hat{X}=x\cap B_{\iota}
. Then
x^{*}=\pi[x]=\pi[x\cap B_{\iota}]\subseteq B^{*}_{h_{\pi}(\iota)}
. Now
\langle x,\pi_{x},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota},\kappa\rangle
cohere, and moreover,
\pi
isomorphs
\mathbb{R}\restriction B_{\iota}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{*}\restriction B^{*}_{h_{\pi}(\iota)}
and satisfies that
\psi^{*}_{h_{\pi}(\iota)}=\pi\circ\psi_{\iota}
. It is straightforward to see from this that
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
and
\langle B^{*}_{h_{\pi}(\iota)},\psi^{*}_{h_{\pi}(\iota)},\kappa\rangle
cohere. However,
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle B^{*}_{h_{\pi}(\iota)},\psi^{*}_{h_{\pi}(\iota)},\kappa\rangle
also cohere, by definition of
\kappa
-suitability. Since
\alpha,\beta\leq\kappa
, Lemma
3.10
therefore implies that
\langle y,\pi_{y},\beta\rangle
and
\langle x^{*},\pi_{x^{*}},\alpha\rangle
cohere, which is what we wanted to show.
∎

We note that the final case in the proof of Lemma
4.6
makes use of Lemma
3.10
, and through this the first essential use of the Collapse condition (
3
) of Definition
2.11
.

4.2.
What Suffices: Finitely Generated Partition Products

Given a (possibly partial) 2-coloring
\chi
on
\omega_{1}
and a function
f
from
\omega_{1}
into
\left\{0,1\right\}
, we use
\mathbb{Q}(\chi,f)
to denote the poset to decompose
\omega_{1}
into countably-many
\chi
-homogeneous sets which respect the function
f
. More precisely, a condition is a finite partial function
q
with
\operatorname{dom}(q)\subseteq\omega
such that for each
n\in\operatorname{dom}(q)
,
q(n)
is a finite subset of
\omega_{1}
on which
f
is constant, say with value
i
, and
q(n)
is
\chi
-homogeneous with color
i
, meaning that if
x,y\in q(n)
and
\langle x,y\rangle\in\operatorname{dom}(\chi)
, then
\chi(x,y)=i
. The ordering is
q_{1}\leq q_{0}
iff
\operatorname{dom}(q_{0})\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(q_{1})
, and for each
n\in\operatorname{dom}(q_{0})
,
q_{0}(n)\subseteq q_{1}(n)
. This forcing was introduced and extensively analyzed in
[
1
]
.

Following
[
1
]
, we refer to any such
f
as a
preassignment of colors
. Our main goal in this section is to come up with a
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
-name
\dot{f}
for a particularly nice preassignment of colors for
\dot{\chi}_{\kappa}
, in the following sense:

Proposition 4.7
.

There is a
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
-name
\dot{f}
for a preassignment of colors so that for any partition product
\mathbb{R}
based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
, any generic
G
for
\mathbb{R}
, and any finite collection
\left\{\langle B_{\iota},\psi_{\iota}\rangle:\iota\in I\right\}
which is
\kappa
-suitable with respect to
\mathbb{R}
, the poset

\prod_{\iota\in I}\mathbb{Q}\left(\dot{\chi}_{\kappa}\left[\psi_{\iota}^{-1}(G\restriction B_{\iota})\right],\dot{f}\left[\psi_{\iota}^{-1}(G\restriction B_{\iota})\right]\right)

is c.c.c. in
V[G]
.

Remark 4.8
.

Observe that in the previous proposition, the same name
\dot{f}
is interpreted in a variety of ways, namely, by various generics for
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
added by forcing with
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
\dot{f}
is strong enough that the product of the induced homogeneous set posets is c.c.c. This is what we mean by referring to the name as “symmetric.”

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\dot{f}_{\kappa}
be a name witnessing Proposition
4.7
, and set
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}
to be the
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
-name
\mathbb{Q}(\dot{\chi}_{\kappa},\dot{f}_{\kappa})
. Then any partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
is c.c.c.

Proof of Corollary
4.9
.

Let
\mathbb{R}
be a partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
, and let
X
be the domain of
\mathbb{R}
. Set
\hat{X}:=\left\{\xi\in X:\operatorname{index}(\xi)<\kappa\right\}
, and let
I:=\left\{\xi\in X:\operatorname{index}(\xi)=\kappa\right\}
. By Lemma
2.26
,
\mathbb{R}
is isomorphic to

(\mathbb{R}\restriction\hat{X})\ast\prod_{\xi\in I}\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}\left[\pi_{\xi}^{-1}\left(\dot{G}\restriction b(\xi)\right)\right],

and
\mathbb{R}\restriction\hat{X}
is a partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
. By Assumption 4.1,
\mathbb{R}\restriction\hat{X}
is c.c.c. It is also straightforward to check that
\left\{\langle b(\xi),\pi_{\xi}\rangle:\xi\in I\right\}
is
\kappa
-suitable, by the definition of
\mathbb{R}
as a partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
and
\underline{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
. Finally, from Proposition
4.7
, we know each finitely-supported subproduct of

\prod_{\xi\in I}\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\kappa}\left[\pi_{\xi}^{-1}\left(G\restriction b(\xi)\right)\right]

is c.c.c. in
V[G\restriction\hat{X}]

4.3.
Counting Finitely Generated Partition Products

The main reason we prefer to work with finitely generated partition products is that we are now able, using the machinery developed up to this point, to carry out counting arguments. More specifically, we can show that there are only
\aleph_{1}
-many isomorphism types of such partition products.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
M\prec H(\omega_{3})
be countably closed with
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
,
\dot{\underline{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa\in M
.
If
\mathbb{R}
is a finitely generated partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\dot{\underline{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa

is isomorphic to a partition product which has domain an ordinal

below

Proof.

Fix such an

, and let
\mathbb{R}
be a finitely generated partition product based upon
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction\kappa
and
\dot{\underline{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa
, say with domain

. Let
\left\{\langle B_{m},\psi_{m}\rangle:m<n\right\}
be the
\kappa
-suitable collection and

the auxiliary part, where we assume that

is disjoint from the union of the

as

and set
\delta_{k}:=\operatorname{index}(\xi_{k})
for each

. Furthermore, we let
\pi_{k}
be the rearrangement of
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{k}}
associated to
\operatorname{base}(\xi_{k})
.

We intend to apply Corollary
2.25
, and so we define a sequence
\langle\tau_{m}:m<\omega\rangle
of rearrangements of
\mathbb{R}
and base-closed subsets

of

. For each
m<n
, set
\tau_{m}
to be the rearrangement which first shifts the ordinals in

past
\sup(B_{m})
and then acts as
\psi_{m}^{-1}
on

. For each

, say

, we set
\tau_{m}
to be the rearrangement which first shifts the ordinals in

past
\xi_{k}
and then acts as
\pi_{k}^{-1}
on

and sends

to

. We set

,

for

, and

for

.

By Corollary
2.25
, let
\sigma
be a rearrangement of
\mathbb{R}
so that
\operatorname{ran}(\sigma)
is an ordinal
\rho
and so that for each

is an ordinal and

, and if

, then

is no larger than

, where

\rho=\sum_{m<\omega}\text{ot}(\sigma[D_{m+1}\backslash D_{m}])\leq\rho_{\kappa}\cdot n+\sum_{k<\omega}(\rho_{\delta_{k}}+1).

By the elementarity and countable closure of

, the ordinal on the right hand side is an element of

. Thus

since

is an ordinal.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let

be countably closed containing
\underline{\mathbb{P}}\restriction(\kappa+1)
,
\dot{\underline{\mathbb{Q}}}\restriction\kappa

Lemma 6.2

Suppose that

Lemma 6.3

Suppose that

\kappa

\omega_{2}

\mathbb{A}\restriction\bar{\kappa}

\text{ot}(H\cap\delta)

\bar{\kappa}

\mathbb{A}\restriction\bar{\kappa}

Proof.

Let

\omega

in

∎

Remark 6.4

It is straightforward to check that if
\kappa

\omega_{2}
with respect to
\mathbb{A}

in

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose that
\gamma+1<\gamma(\kappa)
. Then
H(\kappa,\gamma)\cap\kappa\in\kappa

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. Then
\kappa\subseteq H(\kappa,\gamma)
. Since
\gamma+1<\gamma(\kappa)
, we know that
\hat{\delta}:=\delta_{\gamma+1}(\kappa)
exists, and in particular,
\delta_{\gamma}(\kappa)<\hat{\delta}

is a member of

and the facts that
\delta(\gamma,\kappa),{\mathbb{A}}\in L_{\hat{\delta}}

onto

in

satisfies that

is

∎

If

Lemma 6.6

Suppose that
\gamma+1<\gamma(\kappa)

and
\tau

\bar{\kappa}
and
\mathbb{A}\restriction\bar{\kappa}

Let us abbreviate

by

with respect to

\bar{\kappa}
with respect to

, and that

We force over

and is c.c.c., it forces that the continuum has size no more than
\aleph_{3}
. However,
\mathbb{P}_{\omega_{2}}
adds

-many reals, and to see this, we first recall that by Remark

,

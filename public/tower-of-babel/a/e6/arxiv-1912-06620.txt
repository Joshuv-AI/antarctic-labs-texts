Propagation of Singularities for the Stochastic Wave Equation
Cheuk Yin Lee and Yimin Xiao

Abstract
. We study the existence and propagation of singularities of the solution to a one-dimensional linear stochastic wave equation driven by an additive Gaussian noise that is white in time and colored in space. Our approach is based on a simultaneous law of the iterated logarithm and general methods for Gaussian processes.

60H15, 60G17

1.
Introduction

Consider the linear stochastic wave equation in one spatial dimension:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt\displaystyle{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}u(t,x)-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}u(t,x)=\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R},}\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(0,x)=0,}\end{cases}

where
\dot{W}
is a Gaussian noise that is white in time and colored in space with
spatial covariance given by the Riesz kernel of exponent
0<\beta<1
, i.e.

(1.2)

{\mathbb{E}}[\dot{W}(t,x)\dot{W}(s,y)]=\delta_{0}(t-s)|x-y|^{-\beta}.

The existence of the mild solution
u(t,x)
of (
1.1
) was studied by Dalang
[
4
]
,
see (
2.3
) below. The purpose of this article is to study the existence and propagation
of singularities of
\{u(t,x),(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
.

In this article, singularities are associated with the law of the iterated logarithm (LIL),
or the local modulus of continuity. They refer to the random points at which the process exhibits
local oscillations that are much larger than those given by the LIL. For Brownian motion,
this phenomenon was first studied by Orey and Taylor
[
15
]
. It is well known that at a
fixed time point, the local oscillation of a Brownian sample path satisfies the LIL almost surely.
However, it is not true that the LIL holds simultaneously for all points with probability 1. Indeed,
Orey and Taylor
[
15
]
show that, according to Lévy’s uniform modulus of continuity,
one can find random points at which the LIL fails and the oscillation is exceptionally large.
Therefore these exceptional points may be defined as singularities. Similarly, singularities can
be defined for other stochastic processes and, more generally, random fields.

The singularities of the Brownian sheet and the one-dimensional stochastic wave equation
driven by the space-time white noise were studied by Walsh
[
21
,
22
]
, and the case of
semi-fractional Brownian sheet was studied by Blath and Martin
[
2
]
.
Based on a simultaneous law of the iterated logarithm, Walsh
[
21
]
showed that the
singularities of the Brownian sheet propagate parallel to the coordinate axis. Moreover,
Walsh
[
22
]
established an interesting relation between the Brownian sheet and the
solution
u(t,x)
to (
1.1
) driven by the space-time white noise.
Indeed, it follows from Theorem 3.1 in
[
22
]

that the solution can be decomposed into three components:

(1.3)

u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\left[B\Big(\frac{t-x}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{t+x}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)+\hat{W}\Big(\frac{t-x}{\sqrt{2}},0\Big)+\hat{W}\Big(0,\frac{t+x}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\right],

where the main component
B
is a Brownian sheet and
\hat{W}
is the modified Brownian
sheet defined in Chapter 1 of Walsh
[
22
]
, and the processes
\{B(s,t),s,t\geq 0\}
,

\{\hat{W}(s,0),s\geq 0\}
and
\{\hat{W}(0,t),t\geq 0\}
are independent. This relation
implies that the singularities of
u(t,x)
propagate along the characteristic
lines
t-x=c
and
t+x=c
.

The study of singularities of the solution to (
1.1
) driven by the space-time white noise in

[
21
,
22
]
was later extended by Carmona and Nualart
[
3
]
to one-dimensional
nonlinear stochastic wave equations driven by the space-time white noise on the whole line
{\mathbb{R}}
and
on a bounded interval, respectively. Their method is based
on the general theory of semimartingales and two-parameter strong martingales. They showed that,
in the white noise case, their solution
X(t,x)
has the following important properties:

(i).

For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\big\{X(\frac{h}{\sqrt{2}},x+\frac{h}{\sqrt{2}}),h\geq 0\big\}
is
a continuous semimartingale (cf,
[
3
, p.741]
).

(ii).

The increments of
X(t,x)
over a certain class of rectangles form a two-parameter
strong martingale (cf,
[
3
, p.740]
).

In
[
3
]
, Carmona and Nualart proved the law of the iterated logarithm for a semimartingale
by the LIL of Brownian motion and a time change. They also proved that, for a class of
two-parameter strong martingales, the law of the iterated logarithm in one variable holds
simultaneously for all values of the other variable. By applying these results and properties (i) and (ii),
Carmona and Nualart proved the existence and propagation of singularities of the solution
in
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
.

The main objective of this article is to study the existence and propagation of singularities of the
solution to the linear stochastic wave equation (
1.1
) driven by a Gaussian noise that is
white in time and colored in space with spatial covariance given by (
1.2
) with
0<\beta<1
.
In this case, the solution is a mean zero Gaussian random field that shares some
similarity with the fractional Brownian sheet, but it seems to us that there is not a
natural relation like (
1.3
) between the solution and the fractional Brownian sheet.
Also, by applying the computation in the proofs of Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
below,
one can verify that, when
0<\beta<1
, the solution
u(t,x)
does not have the aforementioned
properties (i) and (ii). Consequently, the method in Carmona and Nualart
[
3
]
can not be applied. Our approach for studying the existence and propagation of singularities
of the solution
u(t,x)
is based on a simultaneous LIL that is established by applying general
methods for Gaussian processes. We believe that this approach can be applied in more general settings.
For example, one may study the singularities of the solutions of linear stochastic wave equations driven
by more general Gaussian noises or in two spatial dimensions. One may also consider non-vanishing
initial data and study the effects of both initial data and the Gaussian noise on the existence and propagation
of singularities. These extensions are non-trivial and go beyond the scope of the present paper.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. First, we establish a simultaneous LIL in Propositions
2.1 and 3.1 for the solution of the linear stochastic wave equation (
1.1
). We prove that
after a rotation, the LIL in one variable holds simultaneously for all values of the other variable.
The proof consists of two parts. The upper bound for the LIL is proved in Section 2 and the
corresponding lower bound is proved in Section 3. In Section 4, we define singularity for the solution
and apply the simultaneous LIL to study the propagation of singularities. The main result Theorem

4.3
shows that singularities propagate along the characteristic lines.

It would be interesting to study the existence and propagation of singularities of the solutions of the nonlinear
stochastic wave equations with a Gaussian noise which is white in time and colored in space (
[
4
]
)
or stochastic wave equations with a multiplicative space-time homogeneous Gaussian noise (
[
1
]
).
For solving these problems, the martingale based methods of Carmona and Nualart
[
3
]
are not
applicable. We believe that the Gaussian method in this article, together with an appropriate approximation
argument, would be useful. However, we have not been able to find a quantitative bound on the
approximation that is precise enough for our purpose. Some new argument seems to be needed.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any

(2.4)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{(\tau+\lambda)h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}\leq K_{\beta}\textup{ for all }\tau\in[0,\infty)\right)=1,

In order to prove (

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
0<\beta<1
,
a<b
and
c<d
, we have

(2.6)

C_{\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)|^{2}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}=\frac{4\pi}{(2-\beta)(1-\beta)}(b-a)^{2-\beta}

and

(2.7)

C_{\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[c,d]}(\xi)}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}=\frac{2\pi}{(2-\beta)(1-\beta)}\Big(|c-b|^{2-\beta}+|d-a|^{2-\beta}-|c-a|^{2-\beta}-|d-b|^{2-\beta}\Big).

Proof.

The Fourier transform of the function
{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}
is

\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)=\frac{e^{-i\xi a}-e^{-i\xi b}}{i\xi}.

It follows that

\displaystyle C_{\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)|^{2}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}

\displaystyle=C_{\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{|e^{i\xi(b-a)}-1|^{2}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}

\displaystyle=C_{\beta}(b-a)^{2-\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{|e^{i\xi}-1|^{2}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}.

The last equality follows by scaling.
The proof of Proposition 7.2.8 of
[
17
]
shows that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|e^{i\xi}-1|^{2}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}=\frac{2\pi}{(2-\beta)\Gamma(2-\beta)\sin(\frac{\pi\beta}{2})}.

Also, using the relations
\Gamma(2z)=2^{2z-1}\pi^{-1/2}\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+1)
,
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin(\pi z)
and
z\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(z+1)
(cf.
[
8
, p.895–896]
), we can show that

C_{\beta}=\frac{2\Gamma(2-\beta)\sin(\frac{\pi\beta}{2})}{1-\beta}.

Hence (
2.6
) follows.

For the second part,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[c,d]}(\xi)}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(e^{i\xi(c-a)}+e^{i\xi(d-b)}-e^{i\xi(c-b)}-e^{i\xi(d-a)}\big)\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}.

Note that this integral is real, so we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[c,d]}(\xi)}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(e^{i\xi(c-a)}+e^{-i\xi(c-a)}+e^{i\xi(d-b)}+e^{-i\xi(d-b)}

\displaystyle\qquad-e^{i\xi(c-b)}-e^{-i\xi(c-b)}-e^{i\xi(d-a)}-e^{-i\xi(d-a)}\big)\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}.

Since
|e^{i\xi(x-y)}-1|^{2}=2-e^{i\xi(x-y)}-e^{-i\xi(x-y)}
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[c,d]}(\xi)}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(-|e^{i\xi(c-a)}-1|^{2}-|e^{i\xi(d-b)}-1|^{2}+|e^{i\xi(c-b)}-1|^{2}+|e^{i\xi(d-a)}-1|^{2}\big)\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}.

Hence the result (
2.7
) follows from the first part of the proof.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

Consider the canonical metric

T
defined by
d_{Z}(s,t)={\mathbb{E}}[(Z(s)-Z(t))^{2}]^{1/2}
.
Assume that for some constant
M>\sigma_{T}
, some
\alpha>0
and
some
0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq\sigma_{T}
,

N(T,d_{Z},\varepsilon)\leq\left(\frac{M}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},

where
N(T,d_{Z},\varepsilon)
is the smallest number of
d_{Z}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover
T
.
Then for any
\gamma>\sigma_{T}^{2}[(1+\sqrt{\alpha})/\varepsilon_{0}]
, we have

(2.10)

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{t\in T}Z(t)\geq\gamma\bigg\}\leq\left(\frac{KM\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha}\,\sigma_{T}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}\Phi\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{T}}\right),

where
\Phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(-z^{2}/2)\,dz

and
K
is a universal constant.

The conditions of Lemma
2.5
is easier to verify and the upper bound in (
2.10
) is more precise than
that is given by (
2.9
) if
M/\sigma_{T}
is not too large. However, the upper bound in (
2.10
) may
not be useful when
M/\sigma_{T}
becomes very large [this is the case for the even
A_{n}
in (
2.12
) below.]
On the other hand, Lemma
2.6
is more efficient if the variance of
Z(t)
attains its maximum
at a unique point because the size of the set
T_{\rho}
can be very small.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{Z(t),t\in T\}
be a mean zero continuous Gaussian process as in Lemma
2.5
.
For
\rho>0
, set

T_{\rho}=\{t\in T:{\mathbb{E}}[Z(t)^{2}]\geq\sigma_{T}^{2}-\rho^{2}\}.

Assume that there exist constants
v\geq w\geq 1
such that for all
\rho>0
,
and
0<\varepsilon\leq\rho(1+\sqrt{v})/\sqrt{w}
, we have

N(T_{\rho},d_{Z},\varepsilon)\leq A\rho^{w}\varepsilon^{-v}.

Then for any
\gamma>2\sigma_{T}\sqrt{w}
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{t\in T}Z(t)\geq\gamma\bigg\}\leq\frac{Aw^{w/2}}{v^{v/2}}K^{v+w}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{T}^{2}}\right)^{v-w}\Phi\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{T}}\right).

Now, we prove Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Fix
\lambda>0
.
It suffices to show that for any
0\leq a<b<\infty
and any
0<\varepsilon<1
,

(2.11)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{(\tau+\lambda)h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)K_{\beta}\textup{ for all }\tau\in[a,b]\right)=1.

Let
c\in[a,b]
,
\delta=(c+\lambda)\varepsilon/2
and
d=c+\delta
.
Choose a real number
q
such that
1<q<(1+\varepsilon)^{1/(2-\beta)}
.
For every integer
n\geq 1
, consider the event

(2.12)

A_{n}=\bigg\{\sup_{\tau\in[c,d]}\sup_{h\in[0,q^{-n}]}\big|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)\big|>\gamma_{n}\bigg\},

where

\gamma_{n}=(1+\varepsilon)K_{\beta}\sqrt{(c+\lambda)(q^{-n-1})^{2-\beta}\log\log q^{n}}.

To estimate
{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})
, we will apply Lemma
2.6
. Define
T=[c,d]\times[0,q^{-n}]

and
Z(\tau,h)=\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)

for
(\tau,h)\in T
. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that
{\mathbb{E}}\big[Z(\tau,h)^{2}\big]

attains its unique maximum
\sigma^{2}_{T}
at
(d,q^{-n})
, where

\sigma^{2}_{T}=\frac{1}{2}K_{\beta}^{2}\big[(d+\lambda)q^{-n(2-\beta)}+(3-\beta)^{-1}q^{-n(3-\beta)}\big].

For any
(\tau,h),(\tau^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in T
, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
.
By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle d_{Z}((\tau,h),(\tau^{\prime},h^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big[(Z(\tau,h)-Z(\tau,h^{\prime}))^{2}\big]^{1/2}+{\mathbb{E}}\big[(Z(\tau^{\prime},h^{\prime})-Z(\tau,h^{\prime}))^{2}\big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\big[(\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h^{\prime}))^{2}\big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathbb{E}}\big[(\tilde{u}(\tau^{\prime},\lambda+h^{\prime})-\tilde{u}(\tau^{\prime},\lambda)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h^{\prime})+\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda))^{2}\big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=:S_{1}+S_{2}.

By Lemma
2.3
,
S_{1}\leq C|h-h^{\prime}|^{(2-\beta)/2}
.
The term
S_{2}
can be evaluated by using Lemma
2.4
.
In particular, if
|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|\geq h^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}K_{\beta}\,q^{-n(2-\beta)/2}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{1/2}.

If
|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|<h^{\prime}
, since
h^{\prime}\leq q^{-n}
and
(2-\beta)/2>1/2
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}K_{\beta}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{(2-\beta)/2}(h^{\prime})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}K_{\beta}\,q^{-n(2-\beta)/2}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{1/2}.

Hence,

(2.13)

d_{Z}((\tau,h),(\tau^{\prime},h^{\prime}))\leq C(q^{-n(2-\beta)/2}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{1/2}+|h-h^{\prime}|^{(2-\beta)/2}).

Next, we estimate
N(T_{\rho},d_{Z},\varepsilon)
, where

T_{\rho}=\{(\tau,h)\in T:\sigma^{2}_{T}-{\mathbb{E}}[Z(\tau,h)^{2}]\leq\rho^{2}\}.

We can write
\sigma^{2}_{T}-{\mathbb{E}}[Z(\tau,h)^{2}]
as the sum of three terms:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}K_{\beta}^{2}\Big[(d-\tau)q^{-n(2-\beta)}+(\tau+\lambda)(q^{-n(2-\beta)}-h^{2-\beta})+(3-\beta)^{-1}(q^{-n(3-\beta)}-h^{3-\beta})\Big].

It follows that
(\tau,h)\in T_{\rho}
implies that each of the three terms
is at most
\rho^{2}
. Then, together with the elementary inequality

x^{1/(2-\beta)}-y^{1/(2-\beta)}\leq(x-y)^{1/(2-\beta)}

for
x\geq y>0
and
0<1/(2-\beta)<1
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle T_{\rho}\subset[d-C_{1}q^{n(2-\beta)}\rho^{2},d]\times[q^{-n}-C_{2}\rho^{2/(2-\beta)},q^{-n}]

for some constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. This and (
2.13
) imply that

\displaystyle N(T_{\rho},d_{Z},\varepsilon)\leq C_{0}(\rho/\varepsilon)^{2+\frac{2}{2-\beta}}.

Hence, we apply Lemma
2.6
with
v=w=2+\frac{2}{2-\beta}

and the standard estimate
\Phi(x)\leq(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^{2}/2}
for
x\geq 1

to get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\Big(-\frac{\gamma_{n}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}_{T}}\Big)=(n\log q)^{-p_{n}},

where

\displaystyle p_{n}=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{q^{2-\beta}\big[\frac{d+\lambda}{c+\lambda}+(3-\beta)^{-1}(c+\lambda)^{-1}q^{-n}\big]}.

Recall that
d=c+\delta
and
\delta=(c+\lambda)\varepsilon/2
.
When
n
is large enough,

(3-\beta)^{-1}(c+\lambda)^{-1}q^{-n}\leq\varepsilon/2
,
which implies that

p_{n}\geq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{q^{2-\beta}}>1.

Hence
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})<\infty
and
by the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we have

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n}\textup{ i.o.})=0
.
It follows that with probability 1,

\sup_{\tau\in[c,d]}\sup_{h\in[q^{-n-1},q^{-n}]}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{(c+\lambda)(q^{-n-1})^{2-\beta}\log\log q^{n}}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)K_{\beta}

eventually for all large
n
. Hence

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{(\tau+\lambda)h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)K_{\beta}\textup{ for all }\tau\in[c,d]\right)=1.

From this, we can deduce (
2.11
) by covering the interval
[a,b]
by finitely many intervals
[c,d]
of length
\delta
.
∎

3.
Simultaneous Law of Iterated Logarithm: Lower Bound

Next, we prove the lower bound for the simultaneous LIL:

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
\lambda>0
,

(3.1)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{(\tau+\lambda)h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}\geq K_{\beta}\textup{ for all }\tau\in[0,\infty)\right)=1,

where
K_{\beta}
is the constant in (
2.5
) of Proposition
2.1
.

Recall the following version of Borel-Cantelli lemma
[
16
, p.391]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{A_{n}:n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of events. If

(i)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})=\infty
and

(ii)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}\cap A_{k})}{[\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j})]^{2}}=1
,

then
{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n}\textup{ i.o.})=1
.

We will also use the following lemma, which is essentially proved in
[
18
]
. For the sake of completeness,
we provide a proof for this result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
be jointly Gaussian random variables with
{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{i})=0
,
{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{i}^{2})=1
and
{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{1}Z_{2})=r
.
Then for any
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
, there exists a number
r^{*}
between
0
and
r
such that

{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{1}>\gamma_{1},Z_{2}>\gamma_{2})-{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{1}>\gamma_{1}){\mathbb{P}}(Z_{2}>\gamma_{2})=rg(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2};r^{*}),

where
g(x,y;r)
is the standard bivariate Gaussian density with correlation
r
, i.e.

g(x,y;r)=\frac{1}{2\pi(1-r^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}-2rxy}{2(1-r^{2})}\right).

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
and
p(r)=\int_{\gamma_{1}}^{\infty}\int_{\gamma_{2}}^{\infty}g(x,y;r)\,dx\,dy
.
Define the Fourier transform of a function
f(x,y)
as
\mathscr{F}f(\xi,\zeta)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i(x\xi+y\zeta)}f(x,y)\,dx\,dy
.
Note that

g(x,y;r)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\zeta)}[\mathscr{F}g(\ast\,;r)](\xi,\zeta)\,d\xi\,d\zeta

and

[\mathscr{F}g(\ast\,;r)](\xi,\zeta)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\xi^{2}+2r\xi\zeta+\zeta^{2})}.

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}g(x,y;r)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\zeta)}\xi\zeta[\mathscr{F}g(\ast\,;r)](\xi,\zeta)\,d\xi\,d\zeta.

Since
(i\xi)(i\zeta)\cdot\mathscr{F}f(\xi,\zeta)=[\mathscr{F}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}f](\xi,\zeta)
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}g(x,y;r)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\zeta)}[\mathscr{F}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}g(\ast;r)](\xi,\zeta)\,d\xi\,d\zeta=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}g(x,y;r).

Therefore,

\partial_{r}p=\int_{\gamma_{1}}^{\infty}\int_{\gamma_{2}}^{\infty}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}g(x,y;r)\,dx\,dy=g(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2};r).

The mean value theorem implies that
p(r)-p(0)=rg(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2};r^{*})
for some
r^{*}
between
0
and
r
,
and hence the result.
∎

Let
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
be the canonical metric on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}
for
u
and
\tilde{u}
respectively, i.e.,

{\sigma}[(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]={\mathbb{E}}[({u}(t,x)-{u}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}))^{2}]^{1/2},

\tilde{\sigma}[(\tau,\lambda),(\tau^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})]={\mathbb{E}}[(\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)-\tilde{u}(\tau^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}))^{2}]^{1/2}.

For a rectangle
I=[a,a^{\prime}]\times[-b,b]
, where
0<a<a^{\prime}<\infty
and
0<b<\infty
,
recall from
[
6
, Proposition 4.1]
that there exist positive finite constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(3.2)

C_{1}\big(|t-t^{\prime}|+|x-x^{\prime}|\big)^{(2-\beta)/2}\leq\sigma[(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\leq C_{2}\big(|t-t^{\prime}|+|x-x^{\prime}|\big)^{(2-\beta)/2}

for all
(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in I
.

The proof of the following lemma is based on the method in
[
13
,
14
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\tau>0
,
\lambda>0
and
q>1
. Then for all
0<\varepsilon<1
,

(3.3)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n-1})}{\tilde{\sigma}[(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n}),(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n-1})]}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log\log q^{n}}\textup{ infinitely often in }n\right)=1.

Proof.

For
n\geq 1
, let
A_{n}=\{Z_{n}>\gamma_{n}\}
, where

Z_{n}=\frac{\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n-1})}{\tilde{\sigma}[(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n}),(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n-1})]}

and

\gamma_{n}=(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log\log q^{n}}.

We will complete the proof by showing that (i) and (ii) of Lemma
3.2
are satisfied.
For (i), by using the standard estimate

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(Z>x)\geq(2\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}x^{-1}\exp(-x^{2}/2),\quad x>1,

for a standard Gaussian random variable
Z
, we derive that for large
n
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}>\gamma_{n})\geq\frac{C}{n^{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}\sqrt{\log n}}

and hence
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})=\infty
.

Next, we show that (ii) is satisfied. Since

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big[{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}\cap A_{k})-{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}){\mathbb{P}}(A_{k})\big]={\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big({\bf 1}_{A_{j}}-{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j})\big)\bigg)^{2}\Bigg]\geq 0

and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})=\infty
, it is enough to prove that

(3.5)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}[{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}\cap A_{k})-{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}){\mathbb{P}}(A_{k})]}{[\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j})]^{2}}\leq 0.

We are going to use Lemma
3.3
to estimate the difference in the numerator. First, we
estimate the correlation
r_{jk}
between
Z_{j}
and
Z_{k}
for
j<k
.
Let
(t_{n},x_{n})=(\frac{\tau+\lambda+q^{-n}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda+q^{-n}}{\sqrt{2}})
.
By (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[(\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-j})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-j-1}))(\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-k})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-k-1}))\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[W\big(\Delta(t_{j},x_{j})\backslash\Delta(t_{j+1},x_{j+1})\big)W\big(\Delta(t_{k},x_{k})\backslash\Delta(t_{k+1},x_{k+1})\big)\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{C_{\beta}d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}\mathscr{F}({\bf 1}_{\Delta(t_{j},x_{j})\backslash\Delta(t_{j+1},x_{j+1})}(s,\cdot))(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}({\bf 1}_{\Delta(t_{k},x_{k})\backslash\Delta(t_{k+1},x_{k+1})}(s,\cdot))(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{t_{k+1}}ds\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{C_{\beta}d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}\mathscr{F}({\bf 1}_{[x_{k+1}+t_{k+1}-s,\,x_{k}+t_{k}-s]})(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}({\bf 1}_{[x_{j+1}+t_{j+1}-s,\,x_{j}+t_{j}-s]})(\xi)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{t_{k+1}}^{t_{k}}ds\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{C_{\beta}d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}\mathscr{F}({\bf 1}_{[x_{k}-t_{k}+s,\,x_{k}+t_{k}-s]})(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}({\bf 1}_{[x_{j+1}+t_{j+1}-s,\,x_{j}+t_{j}-s]})(\xi)}.

Note that (
2.2
) also implies that this covariance is nonnegative.
Then by Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[(\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-j})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-j-1}))(\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-k})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-k-1}))\big]

\displaystyle=Ct_{k+1}\left[(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k-1})^{2-\beta}+(q^{-j}-q^{-k-1})^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{q^{-k-1}}^{q^{-k}}\left[(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-s)^{2-\beta}+(q^{-j}-s)^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}\right]ds

(3.6)

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

Let us consider the first term
J_{1}
.
By the mean value theorem, we can find some
a
and
b
such that

q^{-j-1}-q^{-k}\leq a\leq q^{-j-1}-q^{-k-1}<q^{-j}-q^{-k}\leq b\leq q^{-j}-q^{-k-1}.

and

\displaystyle(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k-1})^{2-\beta}+(q^{-j}-q^{-k-1})^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}

\displaystyle=(2-\beta)(b^{1-\beta}-a^{1-\beta})(q^{-k}-q^{-k-1})

\displaystyle\leq(2-\beta)[(q^{-j}-q^{-k-1})^{1-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{1-\beta}]q^{-k}.

Suppose
j\leq k-2
.
By the mean value theorem again, we can find some
\xi
between
q^{-j}-q^{-k-1}
and
q^{-j-1}-q^{-k}

such that

\displaystyle(q^{-j}-q^{-k-1})^{1-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta)\xi^{-\beta}[(q^{-j}-q^{-k-1})-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{-\beta}q^{-j}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)(q^{-j-1}-q^{-j-2})^{-\beta}q^{-j}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)(q^{-1}-q^{-2})(q^{-j})^{1-\beta}.

It follows that

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq C(q^{-j})^{1-\beta}q^{-k}.

Next, we consider the term
J_{2}
in (
3
). For every

, we can find
some
\tilde{a}
and
\tilde{b}
(depending on

) such that

and

\displaystyle(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-s)^{2-\beta}+(q^{-j}-s)^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}

\displaystyle=(2-\beta)(\tilde{b}^{1-\beta}-\tilde{a}^{1-\beta})(q^{-k}-s)

\displaystyle\leq(2-\beta)[(q^{-j}-s)^{1-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{1-\beta}]q^{-k}.

For
j\leq k-2
, by the mean value theorem again, there exists
\eta
between
q^{-j}-s
and
q^{-j-1}-q^{-k}
such that

\displaystyle(q^{-j}-s)^{1-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta)\eta^{-\beta}[(q^{-j}-s)-(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)(q^{-j-1}-q^{-j-2})^{-\beta}q^{j}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)(q^{-1}-q^{-2})(q^{-j})^{1-\beta}.

Then we have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=C\int_{q^{-k-1}}^{q^{-k}}\left[(q^{-j-1}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j-1}-s)^{2-\beta}+(q^{-j}-s)^{2-\beta}-(q^{-j}-q^{-k})^{2-\beta}\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq C(q^{-j})^{1-\beta}(q^{-k})^{2}\leq C(q^{-j})^{1-\beta}q^{-k}.

Therefore, by combining (
3
), the upper bounds for
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
, and recalling (
3.2
), we see that
for
j\leq k-2
, the correlation
r_{jk}
between
Z_{j}
and
Z_{k}
satisfies

(3.7)

\displaystyle 0\leq r_{jk}={\mathbb{E}}(Z_{j}Z_{k})\leq\frac{C(q^{-j})^{1-\beta}q^{-k}}{(q^{-j})^{1-\beta/2}(q^{-k})^{1-\beta/2}}=C_{0}(q^{-(k-j)})^{\beta/2}=:\xi_{jk}.

By (
3.7
), we can choose a fixed

such that
r:=\sup\{r_{jk}:j\leq k-l\}<1
.
Since
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})=\infty
, in order to prove (
3.5
), it suffices to
prove that for any
\delta>0
, there exists
m
such that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k-l}[{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}\cap A_{k})-{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}){\mathbb{P}}(A_{k})]}{[\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j})]^{2}}\leq\delta.

Let
\delta>0
be given and let
m
be a large integer that will be chosen appropriately depending on
\delta
.
Let
\rho_{k}=\frac{4}{(\beta/2)\log q}\log\gamma_{k}
, so that for

,

(3.9)

\xi_{jk}\leq C_{0}\gamma_{k}^{-4}.

Provided
m
is large,
1<k-\rho_{k}<k-l
for all
k\geq m
.
By Lemma
3.3
, we have

(3.10)

\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k-l}[{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}\cap A_{k})-{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}){\mathbb{P}}(A_{k})]\leq\left(\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}+\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}^{k-l}\right)r_{jk}g(\gamma_{j},\gamma_{k};r_{jk}^{*}),

where
r_{jk}^{*}
is a number such that
0\leq r_{jk}^{*}\leq r_{jk}
for each
j,k
.
Let us consider the two sums on the right-hand side of (
3.10
) separately.
By (
3.7
), the first sum is

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}\frac{r_{jk}}{2\pi({1-r_{jk}^{*2}})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma_{j}^{2}+\gamma_{k}^{2}-2r_{jk}^{*}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}}{2(1-r_{jk}^{*2})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}\frac{\xi_{jk}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}}{2\pi(1-\xi_{jk}^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(\frac{-r_{jk}^{*2}(\gamma_{j}^{2}+\gamma_{k}^{2})+2r_{jk}^{*}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}}{2(1-r_{jk}^{*2})}\right)\gamma_{j}^{-1}e^{-\gamma_{j}^{2}/2}\gamma_{k}^{-1}e^{-\gamma_{k}^{2}/2}.

Note that
\gamma_{j}<\gamma_{k}
for
j<k
. Then by (

), (
3.7
) and (

), the sum is

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}\frac{C_{0}\gamma_{k}^{-2}}{(1-C_{0}^{2}\gamma_{k}^{-8})^{1/2}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{0}\gamma_{k}^{-2}}{1-C_{0}^{2}\gamma_{k}^{-8}}\right){\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}){\mathbb{P}}(A_{k}).

Since
\gamma_{k}\to\infty
, we may choose
m
to be large enough such that this sum is
\leq\delta[\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j})]^{2}
.

By (
3.4
), the second sum on the right-hand side of (
3.10
) is

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}^{k-l}\frac{r_{jk}}{2\pi({1-r_{jk}^{*2}})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma_{j}^{2}+\gamma_{k}^{2}-2r_{jk}^{*}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}}{2(1-r_{jk}^{*2})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}^{k-l}\frac{r_{jk}\gamma_{j}}{2\pi(1-r_{jk}^{*2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\gamma_{k}-r_{jk}^{*}\gamma_{j})^{2}}{2(1-r_{jk}^{*2})}\right)\gamma_{j}^{-1}e^{-\gamma_{j}^{2}/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor}^{k-l}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{(1-r_{jk}^{*2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(1-r_{jk}^{*})^{2}\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2(1-r_{jk}^{*2})}\right){\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}).

Recall that
r=\sup\{r_{jk}:j\leq k-l\}<1
. Moreover, if
m
is large enough, then

\frac{\gamma_{k}\log\gamma_{k}}{(1-r^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(1-r)\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2(1+r)}\right)\leq\delta

and
k-\rho_{k}>k/2
for all
k\geq m
, so that the last sum above is

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\frac{\rho_{k}\gamma_{k}}{(1-r^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(1-r)\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2(1+r)}\right){\mathbb{P}}(A_{\lfloor k-\rho_{k}\rfloor})

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=m}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{k}\log\gamma_{k}}{(1-r^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(1-r)\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2(1+r)}\right){\mathbb{P}}(A_{\lfloor k/2\rfloor})

\displaystyle\leq 2C\delta\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{k}).

We get that

\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k-l}[{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}\cap A_{k})-{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}){\mathbb{P}}(A_{k})]\leq\delta\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j})\Bigg)^{2}+2C\delta\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j}).

Hence (
3.8
) follows and the proof of Lemma
3.4
is complete.
∎

.

.

Note that when

(1-\varepsilon/4)\bigg(\frac{q-1}{q}\bigg)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}-q^{-\frac{2-\beta}{2}}\bigg(1+\frac{q^{-n-1}}{\tau+\lambda}\bigg)^{1/2}-(1-\varepsilon)>\varepsilon/4

We also choose

Since we can cover

(

) holds for all
\tau\in[c,d]
, where

Let us define the increment of
\tilde{u}
over a rectangle

Then for all

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)&=\tilde{u}(d,\lambda+q^{-n})-\tilde{u}(d,\lambda+q^{-n-1})\\
&\quad+\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+q^{-n-1})-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)\\

By Lemma

\frac{|\tilde{u}(d,\lambda+q^{-n})-\tilde{u}(d,\lambda+q^{-n-1})|}{\tilde{\sigma}[(d,\lambda+q^{-n}),(d,\lambda+q^{-n-1})]}\geq(1-\varepsilon/4)\sqrt{2\log\log q^{n}}

infinitely often in

so we have

(3.15)

|\tilde{u}(d,\lambda+q^{-n})-\tilde{u}(d,\lambda+q^{-n-1})|\geq(1-\varepsilon/4)K_{\beta}\sqrt{(d+\lambda)(q^{-n}-q^{-n-1})^{2-\beta}\log\log q^{n}}

n
with probability 1.
Also, by Proposition
2.1

\tau\in[c,d]
simultaneously,

eventually for all large

.

Next, we derive a bound for the term
\Delta\tilde{u}((\tau,d]\times(\lambda+q^{-n-1},\lambda+q^{-n}])
.
For

, let

Then

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})\leq{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n}^{1})+{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n}^{2}),

where

\displaystyle A_{n}^{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sup_{\tau\in[c,d-(q^{-n}-q^{-n-1})]}|\Delta\tilde{u}((\tau,d]\times(\lambda+q^{-n-1},\lambda+q^{-n}])|>\gamma_{n}\right\},

\displaystyle A_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sup_{\tau\in[d-(q^{-n}-q^{-n-1}),d]}|\Delta\tilde{u}((\tau,d]\times(\lambda+q^{-n-1},\lambda+q^{-n}])|>\gamma_{n}\right\}.

We first estimate
{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n}^{1})
using Lemma
2.6
.
Let

,

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}[(Z(\tau)-Z(\tau^{\prime}))^{2}]^{1/2}

C|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{(2-\beta)/2}(q^{-n}-q^{-n-1})^{1/2}&\text{if }q^{-n}-q^{-n-1}>|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq Cq^{-n(2-\beta)/2}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{1/2}.

4.
Singularities and Their Propagation

In this section, we study the existence and propagation of singularities of the stochastic wave equation (
1.1
).
The main result is Theorem
4.3
.

Let us first discuss the interpretation of singularities and how they may arise.
Propositions
2.1
and
3.1
imply that LIL holds at any fixed point
(t,x)
:

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|u(t+\frac{h}{\sqrt{2}},x+\frac{h}{\sqrt{2}})-u(t,x)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=K_{\beta}(\sqrt{2}t)^{1/2}\quad\text{a.s.}

It describes the rate of local oscillation of
u
when
(t,x)
is fixed. On the other hand, when
(t,x)
is not fixed,
the behavior will be different. Indeed, from the uniform modulus of continuity in Theorem 3.1 of
[
11
]
,
we know that for
I=[a,a^{\prime}]\times[-b,b]
, where
0<a<a^{\prime}
and
b>0
, there exists a positive finite constant

K
such that

\lim_{h\to 0+}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in I:\\
0<\sigma[(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\leq h\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-u(t,x)|}{\sigma[(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\sqrt{\log(1+\sigma[(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]^{-1})}}=K\quad\text{a.s.}

Recalling (
3.2
), this result shows that the largest oscillation in
I
is of order

\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log(1/h)}
, which is larger than
\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}
as specified by the LIL.
It suggests that the LIL does not hold simultaneously for all

(t,x)\in I
and there may be (random) exceptional points with much larger oscillation.
Therefore, we can define singularities as such points where the LIL fails.
More precisely, we say that
(\tau,\lambda)
is a
singular point
of
\tilde{u}
in the
\lambda
-direction if

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty

and a
singular point
in the
\tau
-direction if

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau+h,\lambda)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty.

Our goal is to justify the existence of random singular points and study their propagation.

Fix
\tau_{0}>0
. Let us decompose
\tilde{u}
into
\tilde{u}_{1}+\tilde{u}_{2}
, where

\tilde{u}_{i}(\tau,\lambda)=u_{i}\Big(\frac{\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\quad i=1,2,

and

\displaystyle{u}_{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,W\Big(\Delta(t,x)\cap\Pi(\tau_{0})\Big),

\displaystyle{u}_{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,W\Big(\Delta(t,x)\cap\Pi(\tau_{0})^{c}\Big),

where
\Pi(\tau_{0})
is the vertical stripe
\Pi(\tau_{0})=\big\{(s,y):0\leq s<\tau_{0}/\sqrt{2},y\in{\mathbb{R}}\big\}

and
\Pi(\tau_{0})^{c}
is the complement of
\Pi(\tau_{0})
.

Let
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
be the
\sigma
-field generated by
\{W\big(B\cap\Pi(\tau_{0})\big):B\in\mathscr{B}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\}
and the
{\mathbb{P}}
-null sets.
Note that
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
is independent of the process
\tilde{u}_{2}
.

Following the approach of Walsh
[
21
]
and Blath and Martin
[
2
]
, we will use Meyer’s section
theorem to prove the existence of a random singularity. Let us recall Meyer’s section theorem (
[
7
]
,
Theorem 37, p.18):

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{G},{\mathbb{P}})
be a complete probability space and
S
be a

\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times\mathscr{G}
-measurable subset of

\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega
. Then there exists a
\mathscr{G}
-measurable
random variable
T
with values in
(0,\infty]
such that

(a)

the graph of T, denoted by

[T]:=\{(t,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega:T(\omega)=t\}
,
is contained in
S
;

(b)

\{T<\infty\}
is equal to the projection
\pi(S)
of
S
onto
\Omega
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\tau_{0}>0
. Then there exists a positive, finite,
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
-measurable random variable
\Lambda

such that

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda+h)-\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Proof.

Note that

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda+h)-\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda+h)-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}},

where
\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda)=\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda)-\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},0)
.
The covariance for the process
\{\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda),\lambda\geq 0\}
is

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda)\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda^{\prime})]=\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}[W(A_{\lambda})W(A_{\lambda^{\prime}})]

for
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\geq 0
, where
A_{\lambda}=\{(t,x):0\leq t<\tau_{0}/\sqrt{2},-t<x\leq\sqrt{2}\lambda-t\}
.
By (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda)\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}/\sqrt{2}}ds\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{C_{\beta}d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[-s,\,\sqrt{2}\lambda-s]}(\xi)\overline{\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[-s,\,\sqrt{2}\lambda^{\prime}-s]}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4(2-\beta)(1-\beta)}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}/\sqrt{2}}\Big(|\sqrt{2}\lambda|^{2-\beta}+|\sqrt{2}\lambda^{\prime}|^{2-\beta}-|\sqrt{2}\lambda-\sqrt{2}\lambda^{\prime}|^{2-\beta}\Big)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-(3+\beta)/2}\,\tau_{0}}{(2-\beta)(1-\beta)}\Big(|\lambda|^{2-\beta}+|\lambda^{\prime}|^{2-\beta}-|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|^{2-\beta}\Big).

It follows that
\{C_{0}\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda),\lambda\geq 0\}
is a fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter

(2-\beta)/2
for some constant
C_{0}
depending on
\tau_{0}
and
\beta
.

Let

S=\Bigg\{(\lambda,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega:\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda+h)(\omega)-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda)(\omega)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty\Bigg\}.

Then
S
is
\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})\times\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
-measurable.
Using Meyer’s section theorem, we can find a positive
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
-measurable random variable
\Lambda
such that
(a)
[\Lambda]\subset S
, and (b)
\pi(S)=\{\Lambda<\infty\}
.

We claim that
\Lambda<\infty
a.s. Indeed, by the uniform modulus of continuity for fractional Brownian motion
(cf.
[
9
]
, Theorem 1.1), for any
0\leq a<b
,

(4.1)

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\sup_{\lambda\in[a,b]}\frac{|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda+h)-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log(1/h)}}=C_{0}^{-1}\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

We now use an argument with nested intervals (cf.
[
15
]
, Theorem 1) to show the existence of a random

\lambda^{*}
such that

(4.2)

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda^{*}+h)-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda^{*})|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty

with probability 1.
First take an event
\Omega^{*}
of probability 1 such that (
4.1
) holds for all intervals
[a,b]
,
where
a
and
b
are rational numbers. Let

\varphi(h)=\frac{1}{2}C_{0}^{-1}\sqrt{2h^{2-\beta}\log(1/h)}.

Let
h_{0}>0
be small such that
\varphi
is increasing on
[0,h_{0}]
.
For an
\omega\in\Omega^{*}
, we define two sequences
(\lambda_{n}),(\lambda_{n}^{\prime})
as follows.
By (
4.1
), we can choose
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
, say in
[1,2]
, with
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{1}^{\prime}

such that
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}<h_{0}
and

|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{1}^{\prime})-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{1})|>\varphi(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}).

Suppose
n\geq 1
and
\lambda_{n}
,
\lambda_{n}^{\prime}
are chosen with
0<\lambda^{\prime}_{n}-\lambda_{n}\leq 2^{-n+1}
and

|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{n}^{\prime})-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{n})|>\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{\prime}-\lambda_{n}).

Since
\tilde{v}_{1}
is continuous in
\lambda
and
\varphi(h)
is increasing for
h
small, we can find some

\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
such that
\lambda_{n}<\tilde{\lambda}_{n}<\min\{\lambda_{n}^{\prime},\lambda_{n}+2^{-n}\}
and

(4.3)

|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{n}^{\prime})-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda)|>\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{\prime}-\lambda)\quad\text{ for all }\lambda\in[\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}].

Then we can apply (
4.1
) for a rational interval
[a,b]\subseteq[\lambda_{n},\tilde{\lambda}_{n}]
to find

\lambda_{n+1}
and
\lambda_{n+1}^{\prime}
such that
\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda_{n+1}<\lambda_{n+1}^{\prime}\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
and

|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{n+1}^{\prime})-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{n+1})|>\varphi(\lambda_{n+1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{n+1}).

We obtain a sequence of nested intervals
[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1}^{\prime}]\supset[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}^{\prime}]\supset\cdots

with lengths
\lambda_{n}^{\prime}-\lambda_{n}\leq 2^{-n+1}

Hence, for each
\omega\in\Omega^{*}
, there is at least one
\lambda^{*}>0

)

) holds.
It implies that
\Omega^{*}\subset\pi(S)

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda+h)-\tilde{v}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\tau_{0}>0
and
\lambda>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}_{2}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}_{2}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=K_{\beta}(\tau-\tau_{0}+\lambda)^{1/2}\textup{ for all }\tau\geq\tau_{0}\right)=1.

Proof.

By Propositions
2.1
and
3.1
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=K_{\beta}(\tau+\lambda)^{1/2}\textup{ for all }\tau\geq 0\right)=1.

Then the result can be obtained by the observation that
\{\tilde{u}_{2}(\tau_{0}+\tau,\lambda),\tau,\lambda\geq 0\}

has the same distribution as
\{\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda),\tau,\lambda\geq 0\}
.
Indeed, for any bounded Borel sets
A,B
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and
c=(c_{1},c_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, by
(
2.2
) and change of variables we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[W(A+c)W(B+c)\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{c_{1}}^{\infty}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy^{\prime}\,{\bf 1}_{A}(s-c_{1},y-c_{2})|y-y^{\prime}|^{-\beta}{\bf 1}_{B}(s-c_{1},y-c_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy^{\prime}\,{\bf 1}_{A}(s,y)|y-y^{\prime}|^{-\beta}{\bf 1}_{B}(s,y)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\big[W(A)W(B)\big].

Since

\Delta\Big(\frac{\tau_{0}+\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau_{0}-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\cap\Pi(\tau_{0})^{c}=\Delta\Big(\frac{\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)+c,

where
c=(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\sqrt{2}},-\frac{\tau_{0}}{\sqrt{2}})
, it follows that for any
\tau,\lambda,\tau^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\tilde{u}_{2}(\tau_{0}+\tau,\lambda)\tilde{u}_{2}(\tau_{0}+\tau^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[W\Big(\Delta\Big(\frac{\tau_{0}+\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau_{0}-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\cap\Pi(\tau_{0})^{c}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times W\Big(\Delta\Big(\frac{\tau_{0}+\tau^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau_{0}-\tau^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\cap\Pi(\tau_{0})^{c}\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[W\Big(\Delta\Big(\frac{\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\Big)W\Big(\Delta\Big(\frac{\tau^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau^{\prime}+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)\tilde{u}(\tau^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})].

The result follows immediately.
∎

We are now in a position to state and prove our main theorem below.
The first part shows that if we fix
\tau_{0}>0
, then based on the information from the
\sigma
-field

\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
, we are able to show the existence of a random singularity
(\tau_{0},\Lambda)

in the
\lambda
-direction. The second part says that if
(\tau_{0},\Lambda)
is a singular point in the

\lambda
-direction, then
(\tau,\Lambda)
is also a singular point for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
.
In other words, singularities in the
\lambda
-direction propagate orthogonally, along the line that is
parallel to the
\tau
-axis. By symmetry, it follows immediately that singularities in the
\tau
-direction
propagate along the line parallel to the
\lambda
-axis. These are the directions of the characteristic
lines
t+x=c
and
t-x=c
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\tau_{0}>0
. The following statements hold.

(i)

There exists a positive, finite,
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
-measurable random variable
\Lambda
such that

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau_{0},\Lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

(ii)

If
\Lambda
is any positive, finite,
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
-measurable random variable, then on an event of probability 1, we have

\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau_{0},\Lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty\,\Leftrightarrow\,\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\Lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\Lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}=\infty

for all
\tau>\tau_{0}
simultaneously.

Proof.

To simplify notations, let us denote

L(\tau,\lambda)=\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}

and for
i=1,2
,

L_{i}(\tau,\lambda)=\limsup_{h\to 0+}\frac{|\tilde{u}_{i}(\tau,\lambda+h)-\tilde{u}_{i}(\tau,\lambda)|}{\sqrt{h^{2-\beta}\log\log(1/h)}}.

As in Walsh
[
21
]
and Blath and Martin
[
2
]
, we are going to use the fact that for two functions
f
and
g
,
provided
\limsup_{h\to 0}|g(h)|<\infty
, we have

(4.4)

\limsup_{h\to 0}|f(h)|-\limsup_{h\to 0}|g(h)|\leq\limsup_{h\to 0}|f(h)+g(h)|\leq\limsup_{h\to 0}|f(h)|+\limsup_{h\to 0}|g(h)|.

(i). By Lemma
4.1
, we can find a positive, finite,
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
-measurable random variable
\Lambda
such that

L_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)=\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
\Lambda
and the process
\tilde{u}_{2}
are independent, Lemma
4.2
implies that

L_{2}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)=K_{\beta}\Lambda^{1/2}\quad\text{a.s.}

and this is finite a.s.
Since
\tilde{u}=\tilde{u}_{1}+\tilde{u}_{2}
, it follows from the lower bound of (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle L(\tau_{0},\Lambda)\geq L_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)-L_{2}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)=\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

This completes the proof of (i).

(ii). Suppose
\Lambda
is a positive, finite,
\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{0}}
-measurable random variable. By (
4.4
), we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle L_{1}(\tau,\Lambda)-L_{2}(\tau,\Lambda)\leq L(\tau,\Lambda)\leq L_{1}(\tau,\Lambda)+L_{2}(\tau,\Lambda)

for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, provided that
L_{2}(\tau,\Lambda)<\infty
. Observe that for
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
,

\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau,\Lambda+h)-\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau,\Lambda)=\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda+h)-\tilde{u}_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda),

hence
L_{1}(\tau,\Lambda)=L_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)
.
Also, by Lemma
4.2
and independence between
\Lambda
and
\tilde{u}_{2}
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\Big(L_{2}(\tau,\Lambda)=K_{\beta}(\tau-\tau_{0}+\Lambda)^{1/2}\text{ for all }\tau\geq\tau_{0}\Big)=1.

Since
\Lambda
is finite a.s., it follows from (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Big(L_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)-K_{\beta}(\tau-\tau_{0}+\Lambda)^{1/2}\leq L(\tau,\Lambda)\leq L_{1}(\tau_{0},\Lambda)+K_{\beta}(\tau-\tau_{0}+\Lambda)^{1/2}\text{ for all }\tau\geq\tau_{0}\Big)=1,

and it implies

{\mathbb{P}}\Big(L(\tau_{0},\Lambda)=\infty\Leftrightarrow L(\tau,\Lambda)=\infty\text{ for all }\tau\geq\tau_{0}\Big)=1.\qed

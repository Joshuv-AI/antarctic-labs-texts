On absolute continuity of inhomogeneous and contracting on average self-similar measures
Samuel Kittle, Constantin Kogler

Abstract
. We give a condition for absolute continuity of self-similar measures in arbitrary dimensions. This allows us to construct the first explicit absolutely continuous examples of inhomogeneous self-similar measures in dimension one and two. In fact, for d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 and any given rotations in O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) acting irreducibly on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} as well as any distinct translations, all having algebraic coefficients, we construct absolutely continuous self-similar measures with the given rotations and translations. We furthermore strengthen Varjú’s result for Bernoulli convolutions, treat complex Bernoulli convolutions and in dimension ≥ 3 \geq 3 improve the condition on absolute continuity by Lindenstrauss-Varjú. Moreover, self-similar measures of contracting on average measures are studied, which may include expanding similarities in their support.



1.
Introduction

In the study of self-similar measures it is fundamental to determine their dimension and to find conditions for absolute continuity. For the former problem progress was made by Hochman (
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
), relating the dimension of a self-similar measure to the entropy and Lyapunov exponent provided the generating measure satisfies a mild separation condition. While it was shown by Saglietti-Shmerkin-Solomyak
[
36
]
that, under suitable assumptions, generic one-dimensional self-similar measures are absolutely continuous, finding explicit examples remains challenging. It was shown by Varjú
[
47
]
that Bernoulli convolutions are absolutely continuous if their defining parameter is sufficiently close to
1
in terms of the Mahler measure. In dimension
d\geq 3
, assuming that the rotation part of the self-similar measure is fixed and has an
L^{2}
spectral gap on
O(d)
, Lindenstrauss-Varjú
[
33
]
showed absolute continuity if all of the contraction rates are sufficiently close to 1. In this paper we strengthen and vastly generalise these two results. Moreover, we give the first explicit examples of absolutely continuous self-similar measures in dimension one and two with non-uniform contraction rates. For instance, consider for
x\in\mathbb{R}
the similarities

(1.1)

g_{1}(x)=\frac{n}{n+1}x\quad\text{ and }\quad g_{2}(x)=\frac{n}{n+2}x+1.

We then show that the self-similar measure of
\frac{1}{2}\delta_{g_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{g_{2}}
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{R}
for any sufficiently large integer
n\geq 1
. Furthermore, our methods allow us to construct several classes of explicit absolutely continuous examples for
g_{i}(x)=\rho_{i}U_{i}x+b_{i}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
in any dimension
d\geq 1
as well as for every collection of orthogonal matrices
U_{i}
acting irreducibly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and distinct vectors
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, provided they all have algebraic entries.

Let
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the group of similarities on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
O(d)
be the group of orthogonal
d\times d
matrices. For each
g\in G
there exists a scalar
\rho(g)>0
, an orthogonal matrix
U(g)\in O(d)
and a vector
b(g)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
g(x)=\rho(g)U(g)x+b(g)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A similarity is called contracting if
\rho(g)<1
and expanding when
\rho(g)>1
.

The Lyapunov exponent of a probability measure
\mu
on
G
is defined, whenever it exists, as

\chi_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}_{g\sim\mu}[\log\rho(g)].

Throughout this paper we use the following terminology.

Definition 1.1
.

If
\chi_{\mu}<0
, we call
\mu

contracting on average
. Moreover, if every
g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
is contracting, we say that
\mu
is
contracting
. When
\chi_{\mu}<0
and there is
g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
such that
\rho(g)>1
, then we call
\mu

contracting only on average
.

It is well-known (
[
23
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
) that when
\mu
is a finitely supported contracting on average probability measure on
G
, then there exists a unique probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is
\mu
-stationary (i.e.
\nu
satisfies
\mu*\nu=\nu
) and referred to as the self-similar measure of
\mu
. Under these assumptions, it follows from the moment estimates of
[
19
]
*Proposition 5.1 that
\nu
has a polynomial tail decay in the sense that there exists some
\alpha=\alpha(\mu)>0
such that as
R\to\infty
,

(1.2)

\nu(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x|\geq R)\ll_{\mu}R^{-\alpha}

for an implied constant depending only on
\mu
. The authors have given in
[
26
]
an independent proof of (
1.2
) for contracting on average measures on arbitrary metric spaces.

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\nu
the self-similar measure associated to
\mu
. If
\mu
is (only) contracting on average, we say that
\nu
is a (only) contracting on average self-similar measure. Moreover,
\mu
or respectively
\nu
is called homogeneous if there are
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
U\in O(d)
such that
r=\rho(g)
and
U=U(g)
for all
g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
. When this is not the case, we say that
\mu
and
\nu
are inhomogeneous. A particular goal of this paper is to give explicit examples of inhomogeneous as well as contracting only on average self-similar measures which are absolutely continuous.

To state our main result, we first discuss the Hausdorff dimension of
\nu
, which is defined as

\dim\nu=\inf\{\dim E\,:\,E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ measurable and }\nu(E)>0\}

where
\dim E
is the Hausdorff dimension of
E
. In order to state the landmark results by Hochman
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
, recall that the random walk entropy of a finitely supported measure
\mu
is defined as

(1.3)

h_{\mu}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H(\mu^{*n})=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}H(\mu^{*n}),

where
H(\cdot)
is the Shannon entropy. Observe that if
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
has no exact overlaps, which means that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
generates a free semigroup, then
h_{\mu}=H(\mu)=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}.

Moreover, as in
[
22
]
, denote by
d(\cdot,\cdot)
the metric on
G
defined for
g=\rho_{1}U_{1}+b_{1}
and
h=\rho_{2}U_{2}+b_{2}
as

d(g,h)=|\log\rho_{1}-\log\rho_{2}|+||U_{1}-U_{2}||+|b_{1}-b_{2}|

for
||\cdot||
the operator norm and
|\cdot|
the euclidean norm.

To distinguish between the results for dimension and absolute continuity, denote

\Delta_{n}=\min\{d(g,h)\text{ for }g,h\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu^{*n})\text{ with }g\neq h\}

and

M_{n}=\min\left\{d(g,h)\text{ for }g,h\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\mathrm{supp}(\mu^{*i})\text{ with }g\neq h\right\}.

Furthermore we set

(1.4)

S_{n}=-\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad S_{\mu}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}S_{n},

where
S_{\mu}
is referred to as the splitting rate.

We call a subgroup
H
of
O(d)
irreducible if
H
acts irreducibly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e. the only
H
-invariant subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
\{0\}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, we say that a measure
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
on
G
or
O(d)\subset G
is irreducible if the group generated by
\{U(g_{1}),\ldots,U(g_{n})\}
is irreducible. When the elements in the support of
\mu
have a common fixed point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\delta_{x}
is the self-similar measure of
\mu
. To avoid the latter case, we say that
\mu
has no common fixed point if the similarities in
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
do not.

It follows by Hochman
[
22
]
, generalising
[
21
]
, that if
\mu
is a finitely supported, contracting and irreducible probability measure on
G
without a common fixed point such that
\Delta_{n}\geq e^{-cn}
for some
c>0
and infinitely many
n\geq 1
, then
\dim\nu=\min\{d,\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\}.

In the accompanying paper
[
24
]
we use the techniques of this paper to generalise Hochman’s result to contracting on average measures. Moreover, we show that a weaker requirement than exponential separation at all scales is sufficient (see
[
24
]
for a discussion). We work with
M_{n}
instead of
\Delta_{n}
for convenience only and in order to apply the general entropy gap results from
[
25
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
24
]
*Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3)
Let
\mu
be a finitely supported, contracting on average and irreducible probability measure on
G
without a common fixed point. Assume that either of the following two properties holds:

(i)

For some
c>0
,
M_{n}\geq e^{-cn}
for infinitely many
n\geq 1
,

(ii)

For some
\varepsilon>0
,
\log M_{n}\geq-n\exp((\log n)^{1/3-\varepsilon})
for all sufficiently large
n\geq 1
.

Then

\dim\nu=\min\left\{d,\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\right\}.

It is well-established that
\dim\nu\leq\{d,\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\}
. Therefore,
\nu
can only be absolutely continuous if
h_{\mu}\geq d\,|\chi_{\mu}|
. The following folklore conjecture is expected to hold, which to the authors knowledge has not been explicitly stated before.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported, contracting on average and irreducible probability measure on
G
without a common fixed point. Then
\nu
is absolutely continuous if

\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}>d.

The latter conjecture is completely open and not known for any class of self-similar measures. Our main result establishes a weakening of the conjecture. Indeed, we show Conjecture
1.3
with the
d
being replaced by a large constant depending on the given rotations multiplied by a term involving the logarithmic separation rate
\log S_{\mu}
.

More precisely, our result is uniform in perturbations of the orthogonal matrices. To capture this, denote for every
k\geq 2
by

\mathcal{I}_{k}=\{(U_{1},\ldots,U_{k})\in O(d)^{k}\,:\,\text{the group generated by }U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\text{ is irreducible}\}.

As shown in Lemma
3.13
, the set
\mathcal{I}_{k}
is an open subset of
O(d)^{k}
. We now state our main result, for which we are allowed to choose the rotations of
\mu
from a given compact subset of
\mathcal{I}_{k}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Given a compact subset
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{I}_{k}
there exists a constant
C\geq 1
depending on
d,\varepsilon
and
\mathcal{I}
such that the following holds. Let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
be a contracting probability measure on
G
without a common fixed point satisfying
(U(g_{1}),\ldots,U(g_{k}))\in\mathcal{I}
and
p_{i}\geq\varepsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq k
as well as
S_{\mu}<\infty
. Then the self-similar measure
\nu
is absolutely continuous if

(1.5)

\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}>C\left(\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}\right)^{2}.

Remark 1.5
.

We observe that by the assumptions on
\mu
in Theorem
1.4
it follows that
h_{\mu}\leq\log k\leq\log\varepsilon^{-1}
and therefore
|\chi_{\mu}|<C^{-1}\log\varepsilon^{-1}
. Thus, since
\mu
is contracting and
p_{i}\geq\varepsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, we can deduce that
\rho(g_{i})\in(\tilde{\rho},1)
for all
1\leq i\leq k
and
\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
a constant sufficiently close to
1
depending on
C
and
\varepsilon
.

Theorem
1.4
is a special case of the more general Theorem
2.4
, which requires a few new definitions we state in Section
2.1
. When
d=1
we note that every probability measure on
O(1)=\{\pm 1\}
is irreducible and therefore we can set
\mathcal{I}=O(1)^{k}
.

While Theorem
1.4
applies in the case when the
L^{2}(O(d))
-spectral gap of the measure
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{U(g_{i})}
is zero, the dependence of
C
can be made more uniform in the presence of a spectral gap. To introduce notation, given a closed subgroup
H\subset G
and assuming that
\mu_{U}
is a probability measure on
O(d)
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{U})\subset H
, we denote by
\mathrm{gap}_{H}(\mu_{U})
the
L^{2}
-spectral gap of
\mu_{U}
in
H
as defined in (
2.19
). Given a measure
\mu
on
G
we denote by
U(\mu)
the pushforward of
\mu
under the map
g\mapsto U(g)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 1
depending only on
d
and
\varepsilon
such that the following holds. Let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
be a contracting probability measure on
G
without a common fixed point satisfying
p_{i}\geq\varepsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq k
as well as
S_{\mu}<\infty
. Assume further that
\mathrm{gap}_{H}(U(\mu))\geq\varepsilon>0
for
H
the closure of the subgroup generated by the support of
U(\mu)
. Then the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
holds.

We point out that in Theorem
1.6
the constant
C
is independent of the subgroup
H
and the statement applies when
H
is a finite irreducible subgroup of
O(d)
as well as when
H
is a positive dimensional irreducible Lie subgroup of
O(d)
. As is shown in section
7
, this observation relies on uniform convergence of
\mu_{U}^{*n}
towards the Haar probability measure
m_{H}
and on Schur’s lemma implying that
\mathbb{E}_{h\sim m_{H}}[|x\cdot hy|^{2}]=d^{-1}
for any unit vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any irreducible subgroup
H\subset O(d)
.

To construct explicit examples of absolutely continuous self-similar measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Theorem
1.4
requires to estimate
h_{\mu},|\chi_{\mu}|
and
S_{\mu}
. It is straightforward to deal with
|\chi_{\mu}|
as it can be explicitly computed. Lower bounds on the random walk entropy follow in many cases (see Section
8.1
) by the ping-pong lemma or by Breuillard’s strong Tits alternative
[
13
]
. It also holds that
h_{U(\mu)}\leq h_{\mu}
, so when
h_{U(\mu)}>0
, we only need to control
|\chi_{\mu}|
and
S_{\mu}
. With current methods we can usually only bound
S_{\mu}
if all of the coefficients of the elements in the support of
\mu
are algebraic. In the latter case, as shown in Section
8.2
, when all of the coefficients of elements in the support of
\mu
lie in a number field
K
and have logarithmic height at most
L
(see (
1.7
)), then
S_{\mu}\ll_{d}L\cdot[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. We observe that
\log S_{\mu}
is usually very small as it is double logarithmic in the arithmetic complexity of the coefficients. All this information makes it straightforward to find explicit examples of absolutely continuous self-similar measures. The constants
C
in Theorem
1.4
can be computed from the involved terms, yet we do not make the dependence explicit in this work.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
2.4
builds on new techniques initiated by the first-named author in
[
28
]
and further developed in this paper, while being inspired by ideas from
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
47
]
and
[
27
]
. We give an outline of our proof in Section
2.2
and note that the main novelties exploited are strong product bounds for detail at scale
r
(a notion introduced in
[
27
]
) and a decomposition theory for stopped random walks to capture the amount of variance we can gain at a given scale, a technique we call the variance summation method.
[
28
]
is concerned with constructing absolutely continuous Furstenberg measures of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on 1-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\sim
and an analogue of Theorem
2.4
is shown. However, we currently can’t deduce a result similar to Theorem
1.4
for Furstenberg measures of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as the dynamics of the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
are more difficult to control than the one of the
\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
action on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Indeed, we exploit that one can rescale and translate self-similar measures without changing the Lyapunov exponent, the separation rate, the random walk entropy or the spectral gap of the generating measure. Moreover, an analogue of Theorem
2.4
as well as Theorem
1.2
for Furstenberg measures of arbitrary dimensions is presently out of reach since the current methods cannot deal with non-conformal measures.

To also treat contracting on average measures, we state the following version of Theorem
1.4
. We require some control on the scaling rate of the expanding similarities.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
k\geq 2
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{I}_{k}
be a compact subset and let
R>1
. Let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
be a contracting on average probability measure on
G
without a common fixed point satisfying
(U(g_{1}),\ldots,U(g_{k}))\in\mathcal{I}
and
p_{i}\geq\varepsilon
as well as
\rho(g_{i})\in[R^{-1},R]
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Then there is some
\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
and
C>1
depending on
d,R,\varepsilon
and
\mathcal{I}
such that the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
holds provided that for some
\hat{\rho}\in(\tilde{\rho},1)
we have

\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\gamma\sim\mu}[|\hat{\rho}-\rho(\gamma)|]}{1-\mathbb{E}_{\gamma\sim\mu}[\rho(\gamma)]}<1-\varepsilon.

In the presence of a spectral gap, the analogue of Theorem
1.6
also holds for Theorem
1.7
. Using Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
2.4
one can construct a versatile collection of explicit absolutely continuous self-similar measures. We give a few cases below and encourage the reader to find further examples. Indeed, as shown in Corollary
1.11
and Corollary
1.12
, for any given irreducible probability measure
\mu_{U}
on
O(d)
supported on matrices with algebraic entries and algebraic vectors
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
with
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
, we can find explicit contracting as well as contracting only on average measures
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
on
G
with
U(\mu)=\mu_{U}
and
b(g_{i})=b_{i}
for

and having absolutely continuous self-similar measure.

Inhomogeneous Self-Similar Measures in Dimension
1

As a first example, we present results for self-similar measures supported on two similarities in dimension one. Upon conjugating, we can assume without loss of generality that our generating measure is supported on
x\mapsto\lambda_{1}x
and
x\mapsto\lambda_{2}x+1
for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in(0,1)
.

We recall the definition of the height of algebraic numbers, which measures the arithmetic complexity. For a number field
K
and an algebraic number
\alpha\in K
one defines the absolute height as

(1.6)

\mathcal{H}(\alpha)=\left(\prod_{v\in M_{K}}\max(1,|\alpha|_{v})^{n_{v}}\right)^{1/[K:\mathbb{Q}]}

where
M_{K}
is the set of places of
K
,
n_{v}=[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]
is the local degree at
v
and
|\cdot|_{v}
is the absolute value associated with the place
v
. We refer to
[
34
]
for basic properties of heights and note that the height of
\alpha
is independent of the number field
K
. We will also work with the logarithmic height

(1.7)

h(\alpha)=\log\mathcal{H}(\alpha).

Corollary 1.8
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a small constant
c=c(\varepsilon)>0
such that the following holds. Let
K
be a number field and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K\cap(0,1)
and write
h(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\max\{h(\lambda_{1}),h(\lambda_{2})\}
. Consider the similarities given for
x\in\mathbb{R}
as

(1.8)

g_{1}(x)=\lambda_{1}x\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad g_{2}(x)=\lambda_{2}x+1.

Then the self-similar measure of
\mu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{g_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{g_{2}}
is absolutely continuous if

h(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\geq\varepsilon\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad|\chi_{\mu}|\max\{1,\log([K:\mathbb{Q}]h(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}))\}^{2}<c.

Corollary
1.8
can be viewed as an inhomogeneous version of our strengthening of Varjú’s result for Bernoulli convolutions (Corolarry
1.15
), yet with an additional dependence on the number field
K
and on the lower bound of
\max\{h(\lambda_{1}),h(\lambda_{2})\}
. We further note that Lehmer’s conjecture states the existence of an absolute
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
h(\lambda)\geq\varepsilon_{0}/[K:\mathbb{Q}]
for all
\lambda\in K
and any number field
K
. Concretely, generalising the example discussed in (
1.1
), we can make the following conclusion.

Corollary 1.9
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
\lambda_{i}=1-p_{i}/q_{i}\in(0,1)
be rational for
i\in\{1,2\}
with coprime integers
p_{i},q_{i}\geq 1
and let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be the similarities from (
1.8
). Then the self-similar measure of
\frac{1}{2}\delta_{g_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{g_{2}}
is absolutely continuous if for
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}}\leq\frac{c}{(\log\log\max\{q_{1},q_{2}\})^{2}}.

It is straightforward to adapt Corollary
1.8
and Corollary
1.9
to multiple maps and also to include contracting on average measures. In the next section we discuss such examples in arbitrary dimensions and just mention the following simple example here.

Corollary 1.10
.

For
n\geq 1
consider the similarities given for
x\in\mathbb{R}
as

g_{1}(x)=\frac{n+1}{n}x\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad g_{2}(x)=\frac{n}{n+1}x+1.

Then the self-similar measure of
\frac{1}{5}\delta_{g_{1}}+\frac{4}{5}\delta_{g_{2}}
is absolutely continuous for sufficiently large
n
.

Self-similar measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

With Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.7
numerous explicit classes of absolutely continuous self-similar measures in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be constructed. In order to apply these results we need to estimate
h_{\mu}
. In the following examples we have used the ping-pong lemma (see section
8
) in two ways in order to establish lower bounds on
h_{\mu}
. For the first class of examples we have applied
p
-adic ping-pong as in Lemma
8.4
.

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\mu_{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{U_{i}}
be an irreducible probability measure on
O(d)
with
p_{i}\geq\varepsilon
and let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
. Assume that
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
have algebraic coefficients. Let
q
be a prime number and for
1\leq i\leq k
consider

g_{i}(x)=\frac{q}{q+a_{i,q}}U_{i}x+b_{i}\quad\quad\text{ for any integer }\quad\quad a_{i,q}\in[1,q^{1-\varepsilon}].

Assume that
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
do not have a common fixed point and consider
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
. Then the self-similar measure of
\mu
is absolutely continuous for
q
a sufficiently large prime depending on
d,\varepsilon,U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
.

We point out that any choice of integers
a_{i,q}
works and that the necessary size of
q
to derive absolute continuity does not depend on this choice, leading to a vast number of examples. Moreover, we can adapt Corollary
1.11
to give contracting only on average examples. In order to satisfy the assumption from Theorem
1.7
, we require that
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
satisfies that
p_{k}\leq\frac{1}{3}
. This nonetheless leads to absolutely continuous examples with
U(\mu)=\mu_{U}
for any given irreducible probability measure
\mu_{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{U_{i}}
on
O(d)
as we do not require that the
U_{i}
are distinct.

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
d,\varepsilon
and
\mu_{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{U_{i}}
as well as
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
be as in Proposition
1.11
. Let
q
be a prime number and consider for
1\leq i\leq k-1

g_{i}(x)=\frac{q}{q+3}U_{i}x+b_{i}\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad g_{k}(x)=\frac{q}{q-1}U_{k}x+b_{k}.

Assume that
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
do not have a common fixed point and further that

p_{k}\leq\frac{1}{3}.

Then the self-similar measure of
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
is absolutely continuous for
q
a sufficiently large prime depending on
d,\varepsilon,U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
.

We give a second class of examples that rely on Galois ping-pong in as Lemma
8.4
.

Corollary 1.13
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\mu_{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{U_{i}}
an irreducible probability measure on
O(d)
with
p_{i}\geq\varepsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Assume furthermore that
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
have algebraic entries. Let
C>1
be sufficiently large in terms of
d,\varepsilon
and
\mu_{U}
.

Suppose that
g_{i}(x)=\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}\sqrt{q}}{c_{i}}U_{i}x+d_{i}
with
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
1\leq i\leq k
and a prime number
q
are contracting and do not have a common fixed point. Then the self-similar measure associated to
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
is absolutely continuous if the following properties are satisfied:

(i)

For
j=1
and for
j=2
we have

\bigg|\frac{a_{j}-b_{j}\sqrt{q}}{c_{j}}\bigg|<\frac{1}{3},

(ii)

For
L=\max(\sqrt{q},|a_{i}|,|b_{i}|,|c_{i}|,|d_{i}|_{\infty})
we have

C|\chi_{\mu}|\leq\frac{1}{(\log(\log L))^{2}}.

As a particular case of Corollary
1.13
, we can consider as shown in Lemma
8.12
the maps

g_{i}(x)=\frac{\lceil\sqrt{q}\rceil-m_{i,q}+2\sqrt{q}}{3\lceil\sqrt{q}\rceil}U_{i}x+d_{i}

for any
m_{i,q}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfying for some
\varepsilon>0
that

m_{i,q}\in[0,q^{1/2-\varepsilon}]\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad|d_{i}|_{\infty}\leq\exp(\exp(q^{\varepsilon/3})).

Then the self-similar measure of
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
is absolutely continuous for sufficiently large primes
q
depending on
d,\mu_{U}
and
\varepsilon
, provided that
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
do not have a common fixed point. We note that since we have a double exponential range for
d_{i}
, we get abundantly many examples.

Dimension
d\geq 3

The case
d\geq 3
is discussed next. We recall that Lindenstrauss-Varjú
[
33
]
proved the following. Let
d\geq 3
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\mu_{U}
a finitely supported probability measure on
SO(d)
, whose support generates a dense subgroup of
SO(d)
and with
\mathrm{gap}_{SO(d)}(\mu_{U})\geq\varepsilon
. Then there exists a constant
\widetilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
depending on
d
and
\varepsilon
such that every finitely supported contracting probability measure
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
on
G
with
U(\mu)=\mu_{U}
and

(1.9)

p_{i}\geq\varepsilon\quad\text{ as well as }\quad\rho(g_{i})\in(\tilde{\rho},1)\quad\quad\text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i\leq k

has an absolutely continuous self-similar measure
\nu
. Moreover,
[
33
]
show that
\nu
has a
C^{k}
-density if the constant
\widetilde{\rho}
is in addition sufficiently close to
1
in terms of
k
. By current methods (
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
) spectral gap of
U(\mu)
is only known when
\mathrm{supp}(U(\mu))
generates a dense subgroup and all of the entries of elements in
\mathrm{supp}(U(\mu))
are algebraic.

Instead of using the entropy and separation rate on
G
, we can use the same quantities on
O(d)
and apply similar methods to Theorem
1.4
to establish absolute continuity of self-similar measures. Denote by
h_{U(\mu)}
the random walk entropy of
U(\mu)
as defined in (
1.3
) and by
S_{U(\mu)}
the separation rate on
O(d)
as in (
1.4
) yet with the metric
||A-B||
for
A,B\in O(d)
and
||\cdot||
the operator norm.

We note that
h_{U(\mu)}\leq h_{\mu}
yet we do not have in general that
S_{U(\mu)}\geq S_{\mu}
. In the case when
S_{U(\mu)}\geq S_{\mu}
, which for example holds when the support of
U(\mu)
generates a free group, (
1.9
) follows from Theorem
1.4
. Moreover, our method can be adapted to work with
S_{U(\mu)}
and
h_{U(\mu)}
instead of
S_{\mu}
and
h_{\mu}
and we establish a generalisation of (
1.9
) (in the case when
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{U})
consists of matrices with algebraic coefficients) that we state next. In the case that
U(g_{1}),\ldots,U(g_{k})
does not generate a virtually solvable subgroup, uniform bounds on
h_{U(\mu)}
follow from the strong Tits alternative
[
13
]
, which make our results particularly strong as stated in (
1.11
).

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
d\geq 3
and let
k,\varepsilon,\mathcal{I}
and
\mu
be as in Theorem
1.4
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 1
depending on
d,\varepsilon
and
\mathcal{I}
such that
\nu
is absolutely continuous if

(1.10)

\frac{h_{U(\mu)}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\geq C\left(\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{U(\mu)}}{h_{U(\mu)}}\right\}\right)^{2}.

If in addition the group generated by
U(g_{1}),\ldots,U(g_{k})
is not virtually solvable, there exists a constant
c\leq 1
depending on
d,\varepsilon
and
\mathcal{I}
such that
\nu
is absolutely continuous if

(1.11)

|\chi_{\mu}|\leq\frac{c}{\max\{1,\log S_{U(\mu)}\}^{2}}.

The result (
1.11
) implies and generalises (
1.9
) (in the case when
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{U})
consists of matrices with algebraic coefficients) as we do not require
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{U})
to generate a dense subgroup of
O(d)
or
SO(d)
. If, moreover, all of the coefficients of the matrices in
\mathrm{supp}(U(\mu))
lie in the number field
K
and have logarithmic height at most
L\geq 1
, then as shown in Section
8.2
it holds that
S_{U(\mu)}\ll_{d}L\cdot[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, which makes (
1.11
) particularly effective. We finally mention that it is straightforward to adapt Theorem
1.14
to contracting on average measures, analogously to Theorem
1.7
.

As in (
1.9
), to apply Theorem
1.14
, we do not require that all the entries of elements in
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
are algebraic and only require the latter for
U(\mu)
, leading to parametric families of examples. The advantage of Theorem
1.14
over (
1.9
) is that not only is our result somewhat uniform when the rotations are perturbed, yet it is also particularly effective when
U(\mu)
has high entropy (for example when
\mathrm{supp}(U(\mu))
generates a free semigroup).

Real and Complex Bernoulli Convolutions

We finally discuss Bernoulli convolutions. Although Theorem
1.4
applies to arbitrary self-similar measures, it gives new results for Bernoulli convolutions. Let
\lambda\in(1/2,1)
and denote by
\nu_{\lambda}
the unbiased Bernoulli convolution of parameter
\lambda
, i.e. the law of the random variable
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{n}\lambda^{n}
with
\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots
independent Bernoulli random variables with
\mathbb{P}[\xi_{i}=1]=\mathbb{P}[\xi_{i}=-1]=1/2
. It was shown by Solomyak
[
43
]
that for almost all
\lambda\in(1/2,1)
the Bernoulli convolution
\nu_{\lambda}
has a density in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, while Erdős
[
16
]
proved that
\nu_{\lambda}
is singular whenever
\lambda^{-1}
is a Pisot number.

The Mahler measure of an algebraic number
\lambda
is defined as

M_{\lambda}=|a|\prod_{|z_{j}|>1}|z_{j}|

with
a(x-z_{1})\cdots(x-z_{\ell})
the minimal polynomial of
\lambda
over
\mathbb{Z}
. We note that as in Corollary 5.9 of
[
28
]
it holds that

(1.12)

S_{\nu_{\lambda}}\leq\log M_{\lambda}.

Garsia
[
18
, Theorem 1.8]
showed that
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous for algebraic
\lambda
with
M_{\lambda}=2
, while the first-named author
[
27
]
established that
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous if
M_{\lambda}\approx 2
. In landmark work, Varjú
[
47
]
proved for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a constant
C>1
such that that
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous if

(1.13)

\lambda>1-C^{-1}\min\{\log M_{\lambda},(\log M_{\lambda})^{-1+\varepsilon}\}.

When applying Theorem
1.4
to Bernoulli convolutions we deduce the following strengthening of (
1.13
), exploiting the comparison between the entropy and the Mahler measure for Bernoulli convolution due to
[
14
]
.

Corollary 1.15
.

There is an absolute constant
C>1
such that the following holds. Let
\lambda\in(1/2,1)
be a real algebraic number. Then the Bernoulli convolution
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{R}
if

(1.14)

\lambda>1-C^{-1}\min\{\log M_{\lambda},(\log\log M_{\lambda})^{-2}\}.

We estimate that a direct application of our method would lead to
C\approx 10^{10}
in Corollary
1.15
. It would be an interesting further direction to try to optimise
C
for Bernoulli convolutions and in particular for the case
\lambda=1-\frac{1}{n}
.

Our most general result, Theorem
2.4
, also applies to complex Bernoulli convolutions, which are defined analogously for
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|\lambda|<1\}
. When
|\lambda|\in(0,2^{-1/2})
, then
\dim\nu_{\lambda}\leq\frac{\log 2}{|\log\lambda|}<2
and
\nu_{\lambda}
is singular to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
. It was shown by Shmerkin-Solomyak
[
38
]
that the set of
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|\in(2^{-1/2},1)
and
\nu_{\lambda}
is singular has Hausdorff dimension zero, whereas Solomyak-Xu
[
42
]
showed that
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{C}
for a non-real algebraic
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
with
M_{\lambda}=2
. We extend Corollary
1.15
to complex parameters while assuming (
8.7
) in order to ensure that the rotation part of
\lambda
mixes fast enough and so that our measure is sufficiently non-degenerate (see section
2.1
).

Corollary 1.16
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that the following holds. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
be a complex algebraic number such that
|\lambda|\in(2^{-1/2},1)
and

(1.15)

|\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|\geq\varepsilon.

Then the Bernoulli convolution
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{C}
if

|\lambda|>1-C^{-1}\min\{\log M_{\lambda},(\log\log M_{\lambda})^{-2}\}.

Main Result and Outline

In this section we first state our main results and give an outline of the proof of the main theorem in section
2.2
. Then we collect for the convenience of the reader some notation used throughout this paper in section
2.3
and comment on the organisation of the paper in section
2.4
.

2.1.
Main Result

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To state our main results in full generality we introduce notions that capture how well
U(\mu)
mixes on
O(d)
and how degenerate
\nu
is.

Denote by
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots
independent samples from
\mu
, write

q_{n}:=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\dots\gamma_{n}

and given
\kappa>0
let
\tau_{\kappa}
be the stopping time defined by

\tau_{\kappa}:=\inf\{n\geq 1:\rho(q_{n})\leq\kappa\}.

We then have the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
generating a self-similar measure
\nu
.

(i)

We say that
\mu
is
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degenerate
for
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\theta,A>0
if for any proper subspace
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x-(y+W)|<\theta\text{ or }|x|\geq A\})\leq\alpha_{0}.

(ii)

We say that
\mu
is
(c,T)
-well-mixing
for
c\in(0,1)
and
T\geq 0
if for any unit vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\mathbb{E}[|x\cdot U(q_{F})y|^{2}]\geq c,

where
F
is a uniform random variable on
[0,T]
which is independent of the
\gamma_{i}
.

For
d=1
the measure
\mu
will always be
(1,1)
-well-mixing. As we show in section
7.1
, when
U(\mu)
is fixed and irreducible, there exists
(c,T)
depending only on
U(\mu)
such that
\mu
is
(c,T)
-well-mixing. This follows as
U(q_{F})\to m_{H}
in distribution as
T\to\infty
, where
H
is the closure of the subgroup generated by
\mathrm{supp}(U(\mu))
and
m_{H}
the Haar probability measure on
H
. The latter would not be true if we fix
F
to be a deterministic random variable and therefore we have introduced the above definition. Moreover, it is easy to see that
(c,T)
-well-mixing in continuous in
\mu_{U}
.

Dealing with non-degeneracy is more involved and uniform results for many classes of self-similar measures do not hold. However, instead of our given measure we can consider a conjugated measure to establish uniform non-degeneracy results. Indeed, for
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
a measure on
G
and
h\in G
we denote

\mu_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{hg_{i}h^{-1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu_{h}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{hg_{i}h^{-1}}.

Then as we show in Lemma
7.6
, absolute continuity of any of the self-similar measures of
\mu,\mu_{h}
or
\mu_{h}^{\prime}
is equivalent and all relevant quantities such as
h_{\mu}
,
S_{\mu}
and
|\chi_{\mu}|
are the same or comparable.

Towards Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.7
, as we state in Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
we have essentially uniform
(c,T)
-mixing and uniform
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degeneracy as long as we assume
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{k})\in\mathcal{I}
. We first state a uniform mixing result adapted for Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.6
in the contracting case.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{I}_{k}
be a compact subset. Then there exists
\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
,
(c,T)
and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
depending on
d,\varepsilon
and
\mathcal{I}
such that the following holds. Let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
be a contracting probability measure on
G
without a common fixed point and with
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{k})\in\mathcal{I}
and

p_{i}\geq\varepsilon\quad\text{ as well as }\quad\rho(g_{i})\in(\tilde{\rho},1)\quad\quad\text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i\leq k.

Then there is
h\in G
such that
\mu_{h}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{hg_{i}h^{-1}}
is
(c,T)
-well-mixing and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degenerate.

Moreover, if
\mathrm{gap}_{H}(U(\mu))\geq\varepsilon>0
for
H
the closure of the subgroup generated by the support of
U(\mu)
(without assuming
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{k})\in\mathcal{I}
), then there exist
\tilde{\rho}
,
(c,T)
and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
depending only on

and

such that the above conclusion holds.

For Theorem
1.7
we state a similar result for contracting on average measures.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{I}_{k}
be a compact subset. Then there exists
\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
,
(c,T)
depending on

and

and

and

. Assume that for some

-well-mixing and

-non-degenerate.

Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
are proved in section
7
. We are now in a suitable position to state our main result. Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.6
and Theorem

follow from the main result Theorem

Theorem 2.4
.

For every

and

with

and

there is a constant

-non-degenerate probability measure on

\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subset\{g\in G\,:\,\rho(g)\in[R^{-1},R]\}

\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}>C\left(\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}\right)^{2}.

For convenience of the reader, we formally deduce Theorem
1.4
, Theorem

and Theorem

from these results.

Proof.

(of Theorem

, Theorem

)

. Then by Remark

\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)

. Therefore, the assumptions of Proposition

(c,T)
and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
depending only on

and

such that a conjugate

of
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu
is
(c,T)
-well-mixing and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degenrate. As by Lemma

it holds that

,

and

, Theorem

follows from Theorem
2.4
.
∎

A similar result to Theorem

for Furstenberg measures of

Next, we state a version of our main theorem for

\mu

(c,T)
-well-mixing and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degenerate probability measure on

2.2.
Outline

We give a sketch for the proof of Theorem
2.4
. Our proof extends the strategy of
[
28
]
to self-similar measures and generalises it to higher dimensions, which in turn is inspired by ideas and techniques developed in
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
47
]
and
[
27
]
. Proposition
2.2
will be discussed and proved in section
7
. An entropy theory for random walks on general Lie groups was developed in
[
25
]
and will be used in this paper.

Let
\mu
be a measure on
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots
be independent
\mu
-distributed random variables. For a stopping time
\tau
write
q_{\tau}=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{\tau}
. Note that if
x
is a sample of
\nu
then so is
q_{\tau}x
. The basic idea of our proof is to decompose
q_{\tau}x
as a sum

(2.1)

q_{\tau}x=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}

with
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
independent random variables. We aim to show that for each scale
r>0
and a suitable stopping time
\tau
that we can find a decomposition (
2.1
) such that for all
i\in[n]
,

(2.2)

|X_{i}|\leq C^{-1}r\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}\,X_{i}\geq C(\log\log r^{-1})r^{2}I

for a sufficiently large fixed constant
C=C(d)>0
only depending on
d
, where
\mathrm{Var}\,X_{j}
is the covariance matrix of
X_{j}
and we denote by
\geq
the partial order defined in (
2.16
). The proof of Theorem
2.4
comprises to establish (
2.2
) and to deduce from (
2.2
) that
\nu
is absolutely continuous. For the former we use adequate entropy results and for the latter we work with the detail of a measure. The constant
C
is be closely related to the one from Theorem
2.4
.

From Decomposition to Absolute Continuity

The notion of detail
s_{r}(\nu)
at scale
r>0
of a measure
\nu
is a tool introduced in
[
27
]
measuring how smooth
\nu
is at scale
r
. Detail is an analogue of the entropy between scales
1-H(\nu;r|2r)
used by
[
47
]
, yet with better properties. Our goal is to deduce from (
2.2
) that our self-similar measure
\nu
satisfies for
r
sufficiently small,

(2.3)

s_{r}(\nu)\leq(\log r^{-1})^{-2},

which implies that
\nu
is absolutely continuous, as shown in
[
27
]
.

A novelty introduced in
[
28
]
is a strong product bound for detail on
\mathbb{R}
, which we prove for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in this paper. Indeed, if
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
are measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
a<b
and
r>0
with
s_{r}(\lambda_{i})\leq\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
and all
r\in[a,b]
and
1\leq i\leq k
, then, as shown in Corollary
4.6
,

(2.4)

s_{a\sqrt{k}}(\lambda_{1}*\cdots*\lambda_{k})\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k-1}(\alpha^{k}+k!ka^{2}b^{-2})

for some constant
Q^{\prime}(d)
depending only on
d
. To prove (
2.4
),
[
28
]
introduced
k
order detail, which we generalise to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We note that (
2.4
) is stronger than the product bounds
[
27
, Theorem 1.17]
and
[
47
, Theorem 3]
and is required in our proof.

To convert (
2.2
) into (
2.3
), we first partition
[n]
as
J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{k}
for
k\asymp\log\log r^{-1}
such that the random variables
Y_{j}=\sum_{i\in J_{j}}X_{i}
satisfy
\mathrm{Var}\,Y_{j}\gg_{d}C
. Then we apply a Berry-Essen type result to deduce that each
Y_{j}
is well-approximated by a Gaussian random variable and therefore that
s_{r}(Y_{j})\leq\alpha
for some constant
\alpha
depending on
C
, with
\alpha
tending to zero as
C
tends to
\infty
. Finally we conclude by (
2.4
) that we roughly get
s_{r}(\nu)\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k}\alpha^{k}=e^{k(\log Q^{\prime}(d)+\log\alpha)}
. We choose
k\asymp\log\log r^{-1}
and therefore deduce (
2.3
) provided that
\alpha
is sufficiently small in terms of
d
or equivalently
C
is sufficiently large. This proves that
\nu
is absolutely continuous.

From Decomposition on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to Decomposition on
G

It remains to explain how to establish (
2.2
), which we first translate into an analogous question on
G
. Indeed, we will make a decomposition of
q_{\tau}
into

(2.5)

q_{\tau}=g_{1}\exp(U_{1})g_{2}\exp(U_{2})\cdots g_{n}\exp(U_{n})

for random variables
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
on
G
and
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. In order to express
q_{\tau}v
as a sum of random variables using (
2.5
), we apply Taylor’s theorem in Proposition
3.4
to deduce

(2.6)

q_{\tau}v\approx g_{1}\cdots g_{n}v+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}(U_{i}),

where

\zeta_{i}=D_{u}(g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{i}\exp(u)g_{i+1}g_{i+2}\cdots g_{n}v)|_{u=0}.

For notational convenience we write in this outline of proofs

g_{i}^{\prime}=g_{1}\cdots g_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad g_{i}^{\prime\prime}=g_{i+1}\cdots g_{n}

and denote

\rho_{x}=D_{u}(\exp(u)x)|_{u=0}.

Then by the chain rule, as shown in Lemma
3.3
,

\var(\zeta_{i}(U_{i}))=\rho(g_{i}^{\prime})^{2}\,U(g_{i}^{\prime})\var(\rho_{g_{i}^{\prime\prime}x}(U_{i}))U(g_{i}^{\prime})^{T}.

We will use the
(c,T)
-well-mixing and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degeneracy condition to ensure that

(2.7)

\var(\zeta_{i}(U_{i}))\geq c_{1}\rho(g_{i}^{\prime})^{2}\mathrm{tr}(U_{i})I=c_{1}\mathrm{tr}(\rho(g_{i}^{\prime})U_{i})I

for some constant
c_{1}>0
depending on
d,c,T,\alpha_{0},\theta
and
A
and where
\mathrm{tr}(U_{i})
is the trace of the covariance matrix of
U_{i}
. This will be shown in Proposition
6.3
by ensuring that each of the
g_{i}
is a product of sufficiently many
\gamma_{j}
such that we can apply well-mixing and non-degeneracy as
g_{i}x
is close in distribution to
\nu
. In fact, we exploit suitable properties of the derivative of
\rho_{x}
and use a principal component decomposition.

So in order to achieve (
2.2
), we require that

(2.8)

|U_{i}|\leq\rho(g_{i}^{\prime})^{-1}r\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{tr}(\rho(g_{i}^{\prime})U_{i})\geq C^{3}c_{1}^{-1}(\log\log r^{-1})r^{2}

for the constant
C
from (
2.2
). Note that to arrive at (
2.2
) we replace
U_{i}
by
C^{-1}U_{i}
and use (
2.7
).

Entropy Gap and Trace Bounds for Stopped Random Walk

We prove (
2.8
) by establishing suitable entropy bounds on
G
and then translate them to the necessary trace bounds. We use the following notation. For a random variable
g
on
G
and
s>0
, we define
\mathrm{tr}(g;s)
to be the supremum of all
t\geq 0
such that we can find some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
and some
\mathscr{A}
-measurable random variable
h
taking values in
G
such that

|\log(h^{-1}g)|\leq s\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{tr}(\log(h^{-1}g)|\mathscr{A})]\geq ts^{2},

where
\log:G\to\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie group logarithm and we assume that
h^{-1}g
is supported on a small ball around the identity. The reason we need to work with the conditional trace is to use (
2.12
).

To establish (
2.8
) we therefore need to find a collection of scales
s_{i}=\rho(g_{i}^{\prime})^{-1}r
such that

(2.9)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau};s_{i})\geq Cc_{1}^{-1}\log\log r^{-1}

for
C
an absolute constant depending only on
d
.

To show (
2.9
) one converts entropy estimates for
q_{\tau}
into trace estimates, using in essence that for an absolutely continuous random variable
Z
on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
we have

(2.10)

H(Z)\leq\frac{\ell}{2}\log\left(\frac{2\pi e}{\ell}\cdot\mathrm{tr}(Z)\right),

where
H
is the differential entropy and
\mathrm{tr}(Z)
is the trace of the covariance matrix of
Z
. Equality holds in (
2.10
) if and only if
Z
is a spherical Gaussian.

We will work with entropy between scales on
G
. Precise definitions are given in section
5.1
. For the purposes of this outline consider the entropy between scales defined for a random variable
g
taking values in
G
, two scales
r_{1},r_{2}>0
and a parameter
a>0
as

H_{a}(g;r_{1}|r_{2})=(H(gs_{r_{1},a})-H(s_{r_{1},a}))-(H(gs_{r_{2},a})-H(s_{r_{2},a})),

where
H(\cdot)
is the differential entropy and
s_{r,a}
is a smoothing function supported on a ball of radius
ar
and satisfying for
\ell=\dim\mathfrak{g}
that

(2.11)

\mathrm{tr}(\log(s_{r,a}))\asymp\ell r^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad H(s_{r,a})=\frac{\ell}{2}\log 2\pi er^{2}+O_{d}(e^{-a^{2}/4})-O_{d,a}(r).

The function
s_{r,a}
is chosen such that
H(s_{r,a})
is essentially maximal while being compactly supported, which is necessary towards establishing (
2.9
). The parameter
a>0
is useful as it gives us a uniform error bound in (
2.11
). By using moreover (
2.10
), we relate in
[
25
]
*Proposition 1.5 entropy between scales and the trace by

(2.12)

\mathrm{tr}(g;2ar)\gg a^{-2}(H_{a}(g;r|2r)-O_{d}(e^{-a^{2}/4})-O_{d,a}(r)).

For
\kappa>0
denote by

\tau_{\kappa}=\inf\{n\geq 1\,:\,\rho(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{n})\leq\kappa\}.

It is then shown in Proposition
5.1
for
r_{1}<r_{2}
and with
r_{1}\leq\kappa^{\frac{S_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}}
that as
\kappa\to 0
the following entropy gap holds:

(2.13)

H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};r_{1}|r_{2})\geq\left(\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}-d\right)\log\kappa^{-1}+\ell\cdot\log r_{2}+o_{\mu,d,a}(\log\kappa^{-1}).

We will give a sketch of the proof of (
2.13
) in the beginning of section
5
and just note that the main point of (
2.13
) is that most of the elements in the support of
q_{\tau_{\kappa}}
are separated by
\kappa^{\frac{S_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}}
, which by standard properties of entropy implies that
H(q_{\tau_{\kappa}}s_{r_{1},a})\approx H(q_{\tau_{\kappa}})+H(s_{r_{1},a})
. As we have to use a stopping time in (
2.13
), we will need to work with
q_{\tau}
instead of a deterministic time throughout our proof.

By (
2.13
) it follows, assuming
h_{\mu}/|\chi_{\mu}|
is sufficiently large and
\kappa
is sufficiently small, that

(2.14)

H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};\kappa^{\frac{S_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}}|\kappa^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{2\ell|\chi_{\mu}|}})\gg_{d}\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\log\kappa^{-1}.

Using (
2.14
) and (
2.12
), we show in Proposition
5.4
with setting
S=2\max\{S_{\mu},h_{\mu}\}
that for a collection of scales

s_{i}\in(\kappa^{\frac{S}{|\chi_{\mu}|}},\kappa^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{2\ell|\chi_{\mu}|}})\quad\quad\text{ with }\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq\hat{m}

and
\hat{m}
being a fixed constant depending on
S_{\mu}
and
\chi_{\mu}
that

(2.15)

\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{m}}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};s_{i})\gg_{d}\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}^{-1}.

As we explain at the beginning of section
5
, the error term
\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}^{-1}
arises from the error
O_{d}(e^{-a^{2}/4})
in (
2.12
).

Conclusion of Proof

The trace bound (
2.15
) is not sufficient to establish (
2.9
) as we require a lower bound depending on
\log\log r^{-1}
. To achieve such a bound and to conclude the proof, we concatenate several decompositions arising from (
2.15
) and therefore develop a suitable theory of such decompositions in section
6
.

It therefore remains to find sufficiently many parameters
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{m}
such that the resulting intervals

(\kappa_{1}^{\frac{S}{|\chi_{\mu}|}},\kappa_{1}^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{2\ell|\chi_{\mu}|}}),\quad(\kappa_{2}^{\frac{S}{|\chi_{\mu}|}},\kappa_{2}^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{2\ell|\chi_{\mu}|}}),\quad\ldots\quad(\kappa_{m}^{\frac{S}{|\chi_{\mu}|}},\kappa_{m}^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{2\ell|\chi_{\mu}|}})

are disjoint. As we require that all of the scales are
\geq r
, we set
\kappa_{1}=r^{\frac{|\chi_{\mu}|}{S}}
. On the other hand, we want all scales to be sufficiently small. We, for example, therefore require that
\kappa_{m}^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{2\ell|\chi_{\mu}|}}<e^{-10}
. Thus setting
\kappa_{i+1}=\kappa_{i}^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{3\ell S}}
, thereby ensuring that the resulting intervals are disjoint (provided
h_{\mu}/\chi_{\mu}
is sufficiently large), a calculation shows that the maximal
m
we can take is

m\asymp\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}^{-1}\log\log r^{-1}.

Combining all of the above, it follows that when summing over all the scales

\sum_{i}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa_{1}}};s_{i})\gg_{d}\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}^{-2}\log\log r^{-1}.

We therefore require in order to satisfy (
2.9
) that

\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}^{-2}\geq C^{3}c_{1}^{-1},

which leads us to the condition from Theorem
2.4
and concludes our sketch of the proof.

3.1.
Derivative Bounds

3.1.1.
Basic Properties

Let
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
consider the map

w_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\quad u\mapsto\exp(u)x.

Denote by
\psi_{x}=D_{0}w_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
the differential at zero of
w_{x}
.

Note that we can embed
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
\mathrm{GL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
via the map

g\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}r(g)U(g)&b(g)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We can therefore identify
\mathfrak{g}
as a matrix Lie algebra and so can write

\mathfrak{g}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\,:\,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}I+\mathfrak{so}_{d}(\mathbb{R}),\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})

Thus for
u=(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix})
it follows that
\psi_{x}(u)=u(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
1\end{smallmatrix})=\alpha x+\beta
. With this viewpoint we also use the following convenient notation

(3.1)

ux=\psi_{x}(u)=\alpha x+\beta

We fix an inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
and denote by
|\circ|
the associated norm. Moreover, we choose an ordered orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
, endowing
\mathfrak{g}
with a coordinate system. So every element
u\in\mathfrak{g}
can be written as a sum
u=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}u_{i}
, where
u_{i}
is the projection of
u
to the
i
-th basis vector. On numerous occasions we will consider derivatives with respect to
u_{i}
.

In the following lemma, some properties about the derivatives of
w_{x},\psi_{x}
and the map
g
are collected. For notational convenience, we denote throughout this subsection by
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
the derivative
D_{x}f
of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
at a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
. We furthermore write
\ell=\dim\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

Let
g=\rho U+b\in G
. Then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}=\rho U
and all of the second derivatives of
g
are zero.

(ii)

Whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
|u|\leq 1
and
1\leq i,j\leq\ell
,

\bigg|\frac{\partial w_{x}}{\partial u_{i}}\bigg|\ll_{d}\max(|x|,1)\quad\text{ and }\quad\bigg|\frac{\partial w_{x}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}\bigg|\ll_{d}\max(|x|,1).

(iii)

For any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have that

||\psi_{x_{1}}-\psi_{x_{2}}||\ll_{d}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

(iv)

Let
u\in\mathfrak{g}\backslash\{0\}
. Then there is a proper subspace
W_{u}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a vector
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that if
\psi_{x}(u)=0
then
x\in u_{0}+W_{u}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(v)

For all
\theta,A>0
there is
\delta>0
such that the following is true. Let
v\in\mathfrak{g}
be a unit vector. Then there is a proper subspace
W_{v}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a vector
v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that if

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{\theta}(v_{0}+W_{v})\quad\text{ and }\quad|x|\leq A

for

the

Proposition 3.2

We applied here the notation (

3.2.
Large Deviation Principle

In this subsection we review various versions of the large deviation principle. Throughout this section we denote by
\mu

G

independent samples from

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mu

We generalise Lemma

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported contracting on average probability measure on
G
and let
\kappa>0
and denote

\tau_{\kappa}=\inf\{n\geq 1\,:\,\rho(\gamma_{1}\ldots\gamma_{n})\leq\kappa\}.

Then for every
\varepsilon>0

\delta>0
such that for sufficiently small

\mathbb{P}\Big[\Big|\tau_{\kappa}-\frac{\log\kappa^{-1}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\Big|>\varepsilon\log\kappa^{-1}\Big]\leq e^{-\delta\log\kappa^{-1}}

Proof.

If
\tau_{\kappa}>\frac{\log\kappa^{-1}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}+\varepsilon\log\kappa^{-1}
then

\rho(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{\lfloor\frac{\log\kappa^{-1}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}+\varepsilon\log\kappa^{-1}\rfloor})\geq\kappa,

which by Lemma
3.8
has probability at most
e^{-\delta\log\kappa^{-1}}
for some
\delta>0
and sufficiently small
\kappa
.

Write
R=\inf\{\rho(g)\,:\,g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\}\in(0,1)
, which is non-zero since
\mu
is compactly supported. Therefore when
\tau_{\kappa}<\frac{\log\kappa^{-1}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}-\varepsilon\log\kappa^{-1}
happens there must be some integer

k\in\left[\frac{\log\kappa^{-1}}{|\log R|},\frac{\log\kappa^{-1}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}-\varepsilon\log\kappa^{-1}\right]

such that

\log\rho(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{k})\leq\log\kappa.

Note that for sufficiently small
\kappa
we have
k|\chi_{\mu}|\leq\log\kappa^{-1}-\varepsilon|\chi_{\mu}|\,|\log R|
and therefore

(3.4)

\log\rho(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{k})\leq\log\kappa\leq k(\chi_{\mu}+\varepsilon|\log R|\chi_{\mu}).

By Lemma
3.8
the probability that (
3.4
) happens is
\leq e^{-\delta^{\prime}k}=e^{-\delta^{\prime}O_{\mu}(\log\kappa^{-1})}
for some
\delta^{\prime}>0
. Since there are at most
O_{\mu}(\log\kappa^{-1})
many possibilities for

, the claim follows by the union bound.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

.

Proof.

We show that the complement

k

(U_{1},\dots,U_{k})
where the generated group
\langle U_{1},\dots,U_{k}\rangle

such that

P
be the projection matrix onto a subspace

is equivalent to the matrix condition
U_{i}P=PU_{i}

The set of all subspaces, or equivalently projection matrices, with a fixed rank
\ell

\mathrm{Gr}(\ell,d)
, which is a compact space. Then consider the set
S_{\ell}
defined as

S_{\ell}=\left\{(U_{1},\dots,U_{k},P)\in O(d)^{k}\times\mathrm{Gr}(\ell,d)\mid U_{i}P=PU_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,k\right\}.

To see that

is a closed subset of

\Phi:O(d)^{k}\times\mathrm{Gr}(\ell,d)\to(\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})^{k}
given by

f(U_{1},\dots,U_{k},P)=(U_{1}P-PU_{1},\dots,U_{k}P-PU_{k})

Then

is closed.

Finally, write

S=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{d-1}S_{\ell},

As
S

To conclude the argument, observe that the set

we show how to convert the conditions from (
2.2
) into good estimates for detail. The latter requires Berry-Essen type results, the Wasserstein distance bounds from section
4.3
, (
4.1
) and a suitable partition of
\sum_{i}X_{i}
.

All of these results will be used in section
6
.

4.1.
Definitions

Denote by
\eta_{y}
the standard Gaussian density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with covariance matrix
y\cdot\mathrm{I}_{d}
, i.e.

\eta_{y}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi y)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{||x||^{2}}{2y}\right).

Moreover, we write

\eta_{y}^{(1)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\eta_{y}.

Given a probability measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
the detail of
\lambda
at scale
r>0
is defined as

s_{r}(\lambda)=r^{2}\,Q(d)\,||\lambda*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}||_{1},

where
Q(d)=||\eta_{1}^{(1)}||^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})(\frac{d}{2e})^{-d/2}
and note that by Stirling’s approximation
d^{-1/2}\leq Q(d)\leq ed^{-1/2}
for all
d\geq 1
. Moreover,
r^{2}Q(d)=||\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}||^{-1}
and therefore
s_{r}(\lambda)\leq 1
for every probability measure
\lambda
.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
27
, section 2]

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the following properties hold:

(i)

Suppose that there is
\beta>1
such that
s_{r}(\lambda)<(\log r^{-1})^{-\beta}
for sufficiently small
r
. Then
\lambda
is absolutely continuous.

(ii)

s_{r}(\lambda*\mu)\leq s_{r}(\lambda).

Definition 4.2
.

Given a probability measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some
k\geq 1
we define the
order
k
detail of
\lambda
at scale
r
as

s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda)=r^{2k}\,Q(d)^{k}\,||\lambda*\eta_{kr^{2}}^{(k)}||_{1},

where
\eta_{y}^{(k)}=\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial y^{k}}\eta_{y}
.

4.2.
Bounding Detail

We have the following properties:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

(4.3)

s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda_{1}*\lambda_{2}*\ldots*\lambda_{k})\leq s_{r}(\lambda_{1})s_{2}(\lambda_{2})\cdots s_{r}(\lambda_{k}).

In particular, for any probability measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
k\geq 1
,

(4.4)

s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda)\leq 1.

Proof.

Recall that by the Heat equation
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\eta_{y}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\eta_{y}(x)
and therefore by standard properties of convolution

\displaystyle\eta^{(k)}_{kr^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i_{1}}^{2}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i_{k}}^{2}}\eta_{kr^{2}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\eta_{r^{2}}\right)*\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\eta_{r^{2}}\right)*\cdots*\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\eta_{r^{2}}\right)}_{k\text{ times}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}*\cdots*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}}_{k\text{ times}}.

This concludes the proof of (
4.3
) as

\displaystyle||\lambda_{1}*\ldots*\lambda_{k}*\eta_{kr^{2}}^{(k)}||_{1}

\displaystyle=||\lambda_{1}*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}*\lambda_{2}*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}*\cdots*\lambda_{k}*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq||\lambda_{1}*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}||_{1}\cdot||\lambda_{2}*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}||_{1}\cdots||\lambda_{k}*\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}||_{1}.

To show (
4.4
) we set
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
and
\lambda_{2}=\ldots=\lambda_{k}=\delta_{e}
and use that
s_{r}(\lambda_{i})\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
k
be an integer greater than
1
and suppose that
\lambda
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
a,b,c>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Assume that
a<b
and that for all
r\in[a,b]
it holds that

s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda)\leq\alpha+cr^{2k}.

Then for all
r\in\left[a\sqrt{\frac{k}{k-1}},b\sqrt{\frac{k}{k-1}}\right]
we have

s_{r}^{(k-1)}(\lambda)\leq 2eQ(d)^{-1}\left(\alpha+(b^{-2(k-1)}+ckb^{2})r^{2(k-1)}\right).

Proof.

By the assumption and the definition of detail for
y\in[ka^{2},kb^{2}]
and writing
y=kr^{2}
,

||\lambda*\eta_{y}^{(k)}||_{1}\leq r^{-2k}Q(d)^{-k}(\alpha+cr^{2k})=\alpha y^{-k}k^{k}Q(d)^{-k}+cQ(d)^{-k}.

Therefore with
y\in[ka^{2},kb^{2}]
,

\displaystyle||\lambda*\eta^{(k-1)}_{y}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq||\lambda*\eta^{(k-1)}_{kb^{2}}||_{1}+\int_{y}^{kb^{2}}||\lambda*\eta^{(k)}_{u}||_{1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq||\eta^{(k-1)}_{kb^{2}}||_{1}+\int_{y}^{kb^{2}}\alpha u^{-k}k^{k}Q(d)^{-k}+cQ(d)^{-k}\,du

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{kb^{2}}{k-1})^{-(k-1)}Q(d)^{-(k-1)}+\alpha k^{k}Q(d)^{-k}\tfrac{y^{-(k-1)}}{k-1}+Q(d)^{-k}ckb^{2},

where we bounded in the last inequality
||\eta^{(k-1)}_{kb^{2}}||_{1}
by using that order
(k-1)
-detail is at most one,
\int_{y}^{kb^{2}}\alpha u^{-k}k^{k}Q(d)^{-k}\,du
by
\int_{y}^{\infty}\alpha u^{-k}k^{k}Q(d)^{-k}\,du
and
\int_{y}^{kb^{2}}cQ(d)^{-k}\,du
by
\int_{0}^{kb^{2}}cQ(d)^{-k}\,du
. Using that
(\tfrac{k}{k-1})^{-(k-1)}<1
we therefore get

||\lambda*\eta^{(k-1)}_{y}||_{1}\leq\alpha k^{k}Q(d)^{-k}\frac{y^{-(k-1)}}{k-1}+(b^{-(2k-2)}+Q(d)^{-1}ckb^{2})Q(d)^{-(k-1)}.

Substituting the definition of order
k
detail gives for
y=(k-1)r^{2}\in[ka^{2},kb^{2}]
or equivalently
r\in\left[a\sqrt{\frac{k}{k-1}},b\sqrt{\frac{k}{k-1}}\right]
,

\displaystyle s_{r}^{(k-1)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=r^{2(k-1)}Q(d)^{k-1}||\lambda*\eta^{(k-1)}_{(k-1)r^{2}}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha r^{2(k-1)}k^{k}Q(d)^{-1}\frac{((k-1)r^{2})^{-(k-1)}}{k-1}+r^{2(k-1)}(b^{-2(k-1)}+Q(d)^{-1}ckb^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha Q(d)^{-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{k-1}\right)^{k}+(b^{-2(k-1)}+Q(d)^{-1}ckb^{2})r^{2(k-1)}.

Finally using that
\left(1+\frac{1}{k-1}\right)^{k}\leq 2e
and that
2eQ(d)^{-1}\geq 1
the proof is concluded.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
k
be an integer greater than
1
and suppose that
\lambda
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
a,b>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Assume that
a<b
and that for all
r\in[a,b]
we have

s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda)\leq\alpha.

Then we have that

s_{a\sqrt{k}}(\lambda)\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k-1}(\alpha+k!\cdot ka^{2}b^{-2})

for
Q^{\prime}(d)=4eQ(d)^{-1}\geq 1
.

Proof.

We will show by induction for
j=k,k-1,\ldots,1
that for all
r\in\left[a\sqrt{\frac{k}{j}},b\sqrt{\frac{k}{j}}\right]
we have

(4.5)

s_{r}^{(j)}(\lambda)\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k-j}\left(\alpha+\frac{k!}{j!}b^{-2j}r^{2j}\right),

which implies the claim by setting
j=1
and
r=a\sqrt{k}
.
The case
j=k
follows from the conditions of the lemma. For the inductive step assume now that for all
r\in\left[a\sqrt{\frac{k}{j}},b\sqrt{\frac{k}{j}}\right]
we have that (
4.5
) holds. Then by Lemma
4.4
we have for all
r\in\left[a\sqrt{\frac{k}{j-1}},b\sqrt{\frac{k}{j-1}}\right]

\displaystyle s_{r}^{(j-1)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k-j}2eQ(d)^{-1}\left(\alpha+\left(b^{-2(j-1)}+\frac{k!}{j!}b^{-2j}jb^{2}\right)r^{2(j-1)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k-j}2eQ(d)^{-1}\left(\alpha+\left(1+\frac{k!}{(j-1)!}\right)b^{-2(j-1)}r^{2(j-1)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k-(j-1)}\left(\alpha+\frac{k!}{(j-1)!}b^{-2(j-1)}r^{2(j-1)}\right).

∎

Combining Lemma
4.3
and Proposition
4.5
, we arrive at the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
a,b>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Assume that
a<b
and that for all
r\in[a,b]
and
i\in[k]
we have

s_{r}(\lambda_{i})\leq\alpha.

Then it holds that

s_{a\sqrt{k}}(\lambda)\leq Q^{\prime}(d)^{k-1}(\alpha^{k}+k!\cdot ka^{2}b^{-2}).

4.3.
Wasserstein Distance

Recall as in (
2.17
) that the Wasserstein 1-distance on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
is defined as

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|\,d\gamma(x,y),

where
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is the set of couplings between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
. We show that detail is bounded up to a constant by the Wasserstein distance.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for
k\geq 1
and
r>0
,

|s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda_{1})-s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda_{2})|\leq edr^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),

where
e
is Euler’s number.

Proof.

Let
X
and
Y
be random variables with laws
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
respectively. Then

(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})*\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v)=\mathbb{E}\left[\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-X)-\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-Y)\right]

and therefore

\big|(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})*\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v)\big|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-X)-\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-Y)\big|\right].

Note that

\big|\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-X)-\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-Y)\big|\leq\int_{X}^{Y}\big|\nabla\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-u)\big|\,|du|,

where
\int_{x}^{y}\cdot|du|
is understood to be the integral along the shortest path between
x
and
y
and
\nabla
is the gradient. Thus

\displaystyle||(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})*\eta^{(k)}_{kr}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{X}^{Y}\big|\nabla\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-u)\big|\,|du|\right]\,dv

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{X}^{Y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\nabla\eta^{(k)}_{kr}(v-u)\big|\,dv\,|du|\right]

\displaystyle=||\nabla\eta_{kr}^{(k)}||_{1}\mathbb{E}[|X-Y|]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg|\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\eta^{(k)}_{kr}\bigg|\bigg|_{1}\right)\mathbb{E}[|X-Y|]

We next bound
||\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\eta^{(k)}_{kr}||_{1}
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, it follows that

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\eta^{(k)}_{kr^{2}}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\eta_{\frac{k}{k+1}r^{2}}\right)*\underbrace{\eta^{(1)}_{\frac{k}{k+1}r^{2}}*\ldots*\eta^{(1)}_{\frac{k}{k+1}r^{2}}}_{k\text{ times}}.

Using standard properties of Gaussian integrals,

\bigg|\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\eta_{\frac{k}{k+1}r^{2}}\bigg|\bigg|_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{2(k+1)}{k\pi}}r^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{k+1}{k}}r^{-1}

and therefore

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\eta^{(k)}_{kr}\bigg|\bigg|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\eta_{\frac{k}{k+1}r^{2}}\bigg|\bigg|_{1}\cdot\big|\big|\eta^{(1)}_{\frac{k}{k+1}r^{2}}\big|\big|_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{(k+1)/2}Q(d)^{-k}r^{-2k-1}.

Using that
\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{(k+1)/2}\leq e
, we conclude

\displaystyle|s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda_{1})-s_{r}^{(k)}(\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq r^{2k}Q(d)^{k}||(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})*\eta_{kr}^{(k)}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq der^{-1}\mathbb{E}[|X-Y|].

Choosing a coupling for
X
and
Y
which minimizes
\mathbb{E}[|X-Y|]
gives the required result.
∎

4.4.
Small Random Variables Bound in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

The aim of this subsection is to show that the sum of independent random variables in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has small detail whenever they are support close to
0
and have a sufficiently large variance. To state our result, we use the partial order (
2.16
) for positive semi-definite symmetric matrices.

Proposition 4.8
.

For every positive integer
d\geq 1
and every
\alpha>0
there exists some
C=C(\alpha,d)>0
such that the following is true for all
r>0
and positive integers
k
. Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent random variables taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that almost surely

|X_{i}|\leq C^{-1}r\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}\,X_{i}\geq Ckr^{2}I.

Then

s_{r}^{(k)}(X_{1}+\ldots+X_{n})\leq\alpha^{k}.

Proposition
4.8
relies on a higher dimensional Berry-Essen type result, which implies Proposition
4.8
for
k=1
, as deduced in Lemma
4.11
. To prove the higher dimensional Berry-Essen type result we first need the following.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
be independent random variables taking values in
\mathbb{R}
with mean
0
and for each
i\in[n]
let
\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]=\omega_{i}^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}[|X_{i}|^{3}]=\gamma_{i}^{3}<\infty
. Let
\omega^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}^{2}
and let
S=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}
. Let
N
be a normal distribution with mean
0
and variance
\omega^{2}
. Then for an absolute implied constant

\mathcal{W}_{1}(S,N)\ll\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}^{3}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}^{2}}.

Proof.

A proof of this result may be found in
[
17
]
.
∎

From this we may deduce the following higher dimensional Berry-Essen type result by using projections onto one-dimensional subspaces.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent random variables taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with mean
0
and for each
i\in[n]
write

\Sigma_{i}=\mathrm{Var}\,X_{i}.

Suppose that
\delta>0
is such that for each
i\in[n]
we have
|X_{i}|\leq\delta
almost surely. Let
\Sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{i}
and
S=X_{1}+\ldots+X_{n}
. Let
N
be a multivariate normal distribution with mean
0
and covariance matrix
\Sigma
. Then

\mathcal{W}_{1}(S,N)\ll_{d}\delta.

Proof.

First we will deduce this from Theorem
4.9
when
d=1
. In this case simply note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}^{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|X_{i}|^{3}]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\delta|X_{i}|^{2}]=\delta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}^{2},

showing the claim.

Now in the case
d\geq 1
the lemma follows by using, as shown in
[
6
]
*Theorem 2.1, that

\mathcal{W}_{1}(S,N)\ll_{d}\sup_{p}\mathcal{W}_{1}(pS,pN),

where the supremum is taken over all one dimensional projections
p
. The result is therefore deduced as in the one dimensional case by using that
\mathbb{E}[|pX_{i}|^{3}]\leq\delta\mathbb{E}[|pX_{i}|^{2}]
.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

For every positive integer
d\geq 1
and every
\alpha>0
there exists some
C=C(\alpha,d)>0
such that the following is true. Let
r>0
and let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent random variables taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

|X_{i}|\leq C^{-1}r\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}\,X_{i}\geq Cr^{2}I.

Then

s_{r}(X_{1}+\ldots+X_{n})\leq\alpha.

Proof.

Denote for
1\leq i\leq n
by
X_{i}^{\prime}=X_{i}-\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]
and let
S^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{\prime}
. Note that
s_{r}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i})=s_{r}(S^{\prime})
. Write
\Sigma_{i}=\mathrm{Var}\,X_{i}
and let
\Sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{i}
. Let
N
be a multivariate normal distribution with mean
0
and covariance matrix
\Sigma
. Note that
|X_{i}^{\prime}|\leq 2C^{-1}r
almost surely. Therefore by Lemma
4.10
,

\mathcal{W}_{1}(S^{\prime},N)\ll_{d}C^{-1}r.

Also

\displaystyle s_{r}(N)

\displaystyle\leq s_{r}(\eta_{C^{2}r^{2}})=\frac{\|\eta_{C^{2}r^{2}+r^{2}}^{(1)}\|}{\|\eta_{r^{2}}^{(1)}\|}=\frac{1}{C^{2}+1}.

Thus by Lemma
4.7
,

s_{r}(X_{1}+\ldots+X_{n})=s_{r}(S^{\prime})\ll_{d}C^{-1}+\frac{1}{1+C^{2}},

implying the claim.
∎

The proof of Proposition
4.8
in the case
k\geq 2
is more involved than the proof in the case
k=1
. In order to prove this proposition we also need the following lemma and a corollary of it.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
V
be a Euclidean vector space, let
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in V
and write
S=v_{1}+\dots+v_{n}
. Let
c_{1},c_{2}>0
be such that for all
i\in[n]
we have

|v_{i}|\leq c_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad v_{i}\cdot S\geq c_{2}|v_{i}||S|.

Let
k
be a positive integer. Then we can partition
[n]
as
J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup J_{k}
such that for each
j\in[k]
we have

|S_{j}-\tfrac{1}{k}S|<c_{2}^{-1}\sqrt{\tfrac{2c_{1}}{k}|S|}+2c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}

where
S_{j}=\sum_{i\in J_{j}}v_{i}
.

Proof.

Choose the
J_{j}
such that

(4.6)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}|S_{j}|^{2}

is minimized. For each
i\in[n]
let
j(i)
denote the unique
j\in[k]
such that
i\in J_{j}
. For each
i\in[n]
and
j^{\prime}\in[k]
we know that moving
i
from
J_{j(i)}
to
J_{j^{\prime}}
cannot decrease the sum in (
4.6
). Therefore

|S_{j(i)}-v_{i}|^{2}+|S_{j^{\prime}}+v_{i}|^{2}\geq|S_{j(i)}|^{2}+|S_{j^{\prime}}|^{2}.

Expanding this out and cancelling gives

S_{j(i)}\cdot v_{i}-|v_{i}|^{2}\leq S_{j^{\prime}}\cdot v_{i}

and summing over all
i\in J_{j}
, we get

S_{j}\cdot S_{j}\leq S_{j}\cdot S_{j^{\prime}}+\sum_{i\in J_{j}}|v_{i}|^{2}.

Let
A_{j}
denote
\sum_{i\in J_{j}}|v_{i}|^{2}
. Note that the above equation gives
|S_{j}-S_{j^{\prime}}|^{2}\leq A_{j}+A_{j^{\prime}}

and so

(4.7)

\displaystyle|S_{j}-\tfrac{1}{k}S|\leq\max_{j^{\prime}\in[k]}|S_{j}-S_{j^{\prime}}|\leq\sqrt{2\max_{j^{\prime}\in[k]}A_{j^{\prime}}}.

Now let
\Lambda^{2}=\max_{j^{\prime}\in[k]}A_{j^{\prime}}
. We compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J_{j}}|v_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}^{-2}|S|^{-2}\sum_{i\in J_{j}}(v_{i}\cdot S)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}^{-2}|S|^{-2}\sum_{i\in J_{j}}(v_{i}\cdot S)c_{1}|S|

\displaystyle=c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}|S|^{-1}S\cdot S_{j}\leq c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}|S_{j}|\leq c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}(\tfrac{1}{k}|S|+\sqrt{2}\Lambda).

Therefore
\Lambda^{2}\leq c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}(|S|/k+\sqrt{2}\Lambda)
, which gives

\left(\Lambda-c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}/\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}\leq c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}|S|/k+c_{2}^{-4}c_{1}^{2}/2

and so

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}|S|}{k}+\frac{c_{2}^{-4}c_{1}^{2}}{2}}+\frac{c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}^{-1}\sqrt{\tfrac{c_{1}}{k}|S|}+c_{2}^{-2}c_{1}\sqrt{2},

showing the required result by (
4.7
).
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
be symmetric positive semi-definite
d\times d
matrices. Suppose that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\geq CkI
and that for each
i\in[n]
we have
\|A_{i}\|\leq c
. Then we can partition
[n]
as
J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup J_{k}
such that for each
j\in[k]
we have

\sum_{i\in J_{j}}A_{i}\geq\left(C-d\sqrt{2cC}-2d^{3/2}c\right)I.

Proof.

Let
M=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}
. We know that
M
is symmetric positive semi-definite and so it may be diagonalised as
M=P^{-1}DP
for some orthogonal matrix
P
and a diagonal matrix
D
with non-zero real entries. Since
M\geq CkI
all of the diagonal entries of
D
are at least
Ck
. Let
D^{\prime}=\sqrt{CkD^{-1}}
be a diagonal matrix and for each
i\in[n]
let
A_{i}^{\prime}=QA_{i}Q
where
Q=P^{-1}D^{\prime}P
. Note that
A_{i}^{\prime}
is symmetric positive semi-definite,
\|A_{i}^{\prime}\|\leq c
as
\|Q\|\leq 1
and that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\prime}=CkI
since

QMQ=(P^{-1}D^{\prime}P)(P^{-1}DP)(P^{-1}D^{\prime}P)=P^{-1}D^{\prime}DD^{\prime}P=CkI.

We now apply Lemma
4.12
with
V
being the space of symmetric
d\times d
matrices with inner product given by
A\cdot B=\sum_{x=1}^{n}\sum_{y=1}^{n}A_{xy}B_{xy}=\mathrm{tr}\,AB
and with
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
being
A_{1}^{\prime},\dots,A_{n}^{\prime}
. We will denote the norm induced by this inner product by
|\cdot|
. Note that given a symmetric matrix
A
we have that
|A|^{2}
is equal to the sum of the squares of the eigenvalues of
A
and so in particular
\|\cdot\|\leq|\cdot|\leq\sqrt{d}\|\cdot\|
. This means that we can take
c_{1}=\sqrt{d}c
so that
|A_{1}^{\prime}|\leq c_{1}
.

All that we need to do is find some lower bound on
A_{i}^{\prime}\cdot CkI
in terms of
|A_{i}^{\prime}|\cdot|CkI|
. Note that
\mathrm{tr}\,A_{i}^{\prime}
is equal to the sum of the eigenvalues of
A_{i}^{\prime}
and that
|A_{i}^{\prime}|^{2}
is equal to the sum of the squares of these eigenvalues. In particular since the eigenvalues are non-negative
\mathrm{tr}\,A_{i}^{\prime}\geq|A_{i}^{\prime}|
and so

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\prime}\cdot CkI

\displaystyle=Ck\,\mathrm{tr}\,A_{i}^{\prime}\geq Ck|A_{i}^{\prime}|=|A_{i}^{\prime}|\cdot|CkI|/\sqrt{d}.

This means that we can take
c_{2}=1/\sqrt{d}
.

We now apply Lemma
4.12
with
S=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\prime}=CkI
to construct our partition
[n]=J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup J_{k}
such that for all
j\in[k]
,

\left\|\sum_{i\in J_{j}}A_{i}^{\prime}-CI\right\|\leq\left|\sum_{i\in J_{j}}A_{i}^{\prime}-CI\right|\leq d\sqrt{2cC}+2d^{3/2}c.

Therefore

\left\|\sum_{i\in J_{j}}A_{i}-CQ^{-2}\right\|\leq(d\sqrt{2cC}+2d^{3/2}c)||Q^{-2}||

and hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J_{j}}A_{i}

\displaystyle\geq CQ^{-2}-\left(d\sqrt{2cC}+2d^{3/2}c\right)||Q^{-2}||I

\displaystyle\geq CI-\left(d\sqrt{2cC}-2d^{3/2}c\right)||Q^{-2}||I

\displaystyle\geq\left(C-d\sqrt{2cC}-2d^{3/2}c\right)I

using in the penultimate line that
Q^{-2}=P^{-1}(D^{\prime})^{-2}P
is symmetric and all eigenvalues are
\geq 1
and in the last line that
||Q^{-1}||\geq 1
.
∎

Finally we can prove Proposition
4.8
.

Proof of Proposition
4.8
.

Note that since
|X_{i}|\leq C^{-1}r
almost surely we have
\|\var X_{i}\|\leq C^{-2}r^{2}
. By Corollary
4.13
we can partition
[n]
as
J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup J_{k}
such that for each
j\in[k]
we have

\sum_{i\in J_{j}}\var X_{i}\geq\left(C-d\sqrt{2C^{-1}}-2d^{3/2}C^{-2}\right)r^{2}I.

This means that by Lemma
4.11
, provided that
C
is sufficiently large in terms of
d
, we know that

s_{r}\left(\sum_{i\in J_{j}}X_{i}\right)\leq\alpha.

The result now follows from Proposition
4.3
.
∎

5.
Entropy Gap and Variance Growth on
\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Throughout this section we use various results from
[
25
]
and we refer the reader to the latter paper for an extensive discussion of the used results. We first give an outline of how the main results in this section are established.

For
\mu
a probability measure on
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we denote by
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots
independent
\mu
-distributed samples of
\mu
and write

q_{n}=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{n}.

For
\kappa>0
be denote by
\tau_{\kappa}
the stopping time

\tau_{\kappa}=\inf\{n\,:\,\rho(q_{n})\leq\kappa\}.

The goal of this section is to give bounds for
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},s_{i})
for suitable scales
s_{i}
, for
\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},s_{i})
as defined in section
5.1
. Towards the proof of our main theorem as discussed in section
2.2
, it would be ideal to give a bound roughly of the form

(5.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},2^{i}ar)\gg\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\log\kappa^{-1}\quad\quad\text{ with }\quad r\approx\kappa^{\frac{S_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}}\quad\text{ and }\quad 2^{N}r\approx\kappa^{\frac{h_{\mu}}{2\ell|\chi_{\mu}|}}

for sufficiently small
\kappa
. As we explain below, we can’t quite achieve (
5.1
) and the bound we arrive at will also depend on the separation rate
S_{\mu}
. To estimate the left hand side of (
5.1
) we apply
[
25
]
*Theorem 1.5 to each of the terms
\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},2^{i}ar)
which gives

(5.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},2^{i}ar)\gg a^{-2}(H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};r|2^{N}r)+O_{d}(Ne^{-a^{2}/4})+O_{d}(r))

having used that by a telescoping sum

H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};r|2^{N}r)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};2^{i-1}r|2^{i}r).

The main contribution from (
5.1
) comes from suitable estimates for
H_{a}(q_{\tau};r|2^{N}r)
. Indeed, we will show in Proposition
5.1
that, up to negligible error terms,

(5.3)

H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};r|2^{N}r)\gg\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\log\kappa^{-1}.

To show this, we recall that

H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};r|2^{N}r)=H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};r)-H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};2^{N}r)

and therefore we need to estimate the terms
H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};r)
and
H_{a}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};2^{N}r)
. To bound the first term (see
[
25
]
), we use that with high probability
\tau_{\kappa}\approx\log(\kappa^{-1})/|\chi_{\mu}|
and so the points in the support of
q_{\tau_{\kappa}}
are separated by distance
r\approx\kappa^{\frac{S_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}}\approx\exp(-S_{\mu}\tau_{\kappa})
. For the second term we exploit decay properties of smoothenings of our self-similar measure.

Combining (
5.2
) with (
5.3
) would lead to (
5.1
) would it not be for the error term
O_{d}(Ne^{-a^{2}/\ell})
. Indeed, to not cancel out the lower bound from (
5.3
) we require that

Ne^{-a^{2}/\ell}\leq c\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\log\kappa^{-1}

for a sufficiently small constant
c
. By our choice of
N
it holds that
N\approx\frac{S_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\log\kappa^{-1}
and therefore

e^{-a^{2}/\ell}\leq c\frac{h_{\mu}}{S_{\mu}}.

So we have to set

a^{2}=c\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}.

Applying then (
5.2
), since the error term
O_{d}(r)
is negligible, we conclude that

(5.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},2^{i}ar)\gg\frac{h_{\mu}}{|\chi_{\mu}|}\log\kappa^{-1}\max\left\{1,\log\frac{S_{\mu}}{h_{\mu}}\right\}^{-1}.

We will deduce a more precise result from
[
25
]
*Proposition 1.5 in Proposition
5.4
.

5.1.
Definitions of entropy and trace

For the convenience of the reader, we recall the definitions from
[
25
]
. The following smoothing functions are used: For given
r>0
and
a\geq 1
, denote by
\beta_{a,r}
a random variable on
\mathfrak{g}
with density function
f_{a,r}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

f_{a,r}(x)=\begin{cases}C_{a,r}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2r^{2}}}&\text{if }|x|\leq ar,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
C_{a,r}
is a normalizing constant to ensure that
f_{a,r}
integrates to
1
. We then set

(5.5)

s_{a,r}=\exp(\beta_{a,r})

and then define the entropy of a
G
-valued random variable
g
at scale
r>0
with respect to the parameter
a\geq 1
as

(5.6)

H_{a}(g;r)=H(g;s_{a,r})=H(gs_{a,r})-H(s_{a,r}),

where
H
is the differential entropy with respect to the Haar measure
m_{G}
on
G
. The entropy between scales
r_{1},r_{2}>0
is defined as

\displaystyle H_{a}(g;r_{1}|r_{2})

\displaystyle=H(g;s_{r_{1},a}|s_{r_{2},a})=H_{a}(g;r_{1})-H_{a}(g;r_{2})

\displaystyle=(H(gs_{r_{1},a})-H(s_{r_{1},a}))-(H(gs_{r_{2},a})-H(s_{r_{2},a})).

We define the trace of
g
(a
G
-valued random variable) at scale
r
, denoted
\mathrm{tr}(g;r)
, to be the supremum of
t\geq 0
such there exists some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
and a
\mathscr{A}
-measurable random variable
h
taking values in
G
such that

|\log(h^{-1}g)|\leq r\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{tr}_{h}(g|\mathscr{A})]\geq tr^{2}.

In this subsection we show that the entropy between scales is large for a suitable stopped random walk on

*Corollary 1.3 since

Lemma 5.3

Under the assumptions of Proposition

Proof.

For convenience write
\tau=\tau_{\kappa}

*Lemma 2.5. Indeed, note that a choice of Haar measure on

We next deal with

6.
Decomposition of Stopped Random Walk

In this section Theorem
2.4
is proved. We construct samples from
\nu
in a suitable way in order to bound the order

detail of
\nu
. Given a probability measure
\mu
on
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

As discussed in the outline of proofs, one uses Proposition

for a sufficiently large constant

and a given scale

. The definition of

requires us to work with a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
and with the conditional trace in (

). As stated in (

), we need to have (

) at

many suitable times

.

Indeed, in order to deduce (

) from Proposition

To combine the trace bounds at the various scales while ensuring that the above conditions are satisfied, a theory of decompositions of the form (

. It is important for our purposes to track the amount of variance we can gain from a given proper decomposition, which is a quantity we will call the variance sum and denote by

In section

. Proper decompositions can be concatenated in such a way that the variance sum is additive, as is shown in section

.

6.1.
Proper Decompositions

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
, let
n,K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and let
A,r>0
and
r\in(0,1)
. Then a
proper decomposition
of
(\mu,n,K,A)
at scale
r
consists of the following data

(i)

f=(f_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and
h=(h_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
random variables taking values in
G
,

(ii)

U=(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
random variables taking values in
\mathfrak{g}
,

(iii)

\mathscr{A}_{0}\subset\mathscr{A}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathscr{A}_{n}
a nested sequence of
\sigma
-algebras,

(iv)

\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be i.i.d. samples from
\mu
and let
\mathscr{F}=(\mathscr{F}_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a filtration for
\gamma
with
\gamma_{i+1}
being independent from
\mathscr{F}_{i}
for
i\geq 1
,

(v)

stopping times
S=(S_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and
T=(T_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
for the filtration
\mathscr{F}
,

(vi)

m=(m_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
non-negative real numbers,

satisfying the following properties:

A1

The stopping times satisfy

S_{1}\leq T_{1}\leq S_{2}\leq T_{2}\leq\ldots\leq S_{n}\leq T_{n},

S_{1}\geq K
as well as
S_{i}\geq T_{i-1}+K
and
T_{i}\geq S_{i}+K
for
i\in[n]
,

A2

We have
f_{1}\exp(U_{1})=\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{S_{1}}
and for
2\leq i\leq n
we have
f_{i}\exp(U_{i})=\gamma_{T_{i-1}+1}\cdots\gamma_{S_{i}}
. Furthermore for each
i
we have that
f_{i}
is
\mathscr{A}_{i}
-measurable,

A3

h_{i}=\gamma_{S_{i}+1}\cdots\gamma_{T_{i}}
and
h_{i}
is
\mathscr{A}_{i}
-measurable,

A4

\rho(f_{i})<1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

A5

Whenever
|b(h_{i})|>A
, we have
U_{i}=0
,

A6

For each
1\leq i\leq n
we have

|U_{i}|\leq\rho(f_{1}h_{1}f_{2}h_{2}\cdots h_{i-1}f_{i})^{-1}r,

A7

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, we have that
U_{i}
is conditionally independent of
\mathscr{A}_{n}
given
\mathscr{A}_{i}
,

A8

The
U_{i}
are conditionally independent given
\mathscr{A}_{n}
,

A9

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, it holds

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\var(\rho(f_{i})U(f_{i})U_{i}b(h_{i})|\mathscr{A}_{i})}{\rho(f_{1}h_{1}f_{2}h_{2}\cdots f_{i-1}h_{i-1})^{-2}r^{2}}\,|\,\mathscr{A}_{i-1}\right]\geq m_{i}I.

Note that in
A9
by
\var
we mean the covariance matrix and we are using the ordering given by positive semi-definiteness (
2.16
) and we denote, as in section
3.1
, by
U_{i}b(h_{i})=\psi_{b(h_{i})}(U_{i})
.

A proper decomposition as above gives us

(6.3)

\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{T_{n}}=f_{1}\exp(U_{1})h_{1}f_{2}\exp(U_{2})h_{2}\cdots h_{n-1}f_{n}\exp(U_{n})h_{n}

We briefly comment on the various properties of proper decompositions. We use the parameter
K
and
A1
to ensure that each of the
f_{i}x
and
h_{i}x
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are close in distribution to
\nu
. Properties
A4
,
A5
and
A6
are needed in order to apply Proposition
3.4
. We require
A7
so that we have
\mathrm{Var}(U_{i}|\mathscr{A}_{n})=\mathrm{Var}(U_{i}|\mathscr{A}_{i})
and in particular the latter is a
\mathscr{A}_{i}
-measurable random variable.
A8
is needed so that
[U_{1}|\mathscr{A}_{n}],\ldots,[U_{n}|\mathscr{A}_{n}]
are independent random variables and therefore we can apply Proposition
4.8
.

One works with two sequences of random variables
f
and
h
instead of one in order to be able to concatenate proper decompositions as in Proposition
6.4
. Indeed, if we had proper decompositions of the form

\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{T_{n}}=g_{1}\exp(U_{1})g_{2}\exp(U_{2})g_{3}\cdots g_{n}\exp(U_{n})g_{n+1}

we could show a variant of (
6.7
) and all other results on proper decompositions. However we could not prove anything like Proposition
6.4
, whose flexible choice of the parameter
M
is necessary to apply Proposition
5.4
.

Next, we define the
V
function mentioned above. The additional parameter
\kappa>0
is introduced in order to be able to concatenate the decompositions in a suitable way (Proposition
6.4
).

Definition 6.2
.

Given
(\mu,n,K,A)
and
\kappa,r>0
we denote by

V(\mu,n,K,\kappa,A;r)

the
variance sum
defined as the supremum for
k=0,1,2,\ldots,n
of all possible values of

\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}

for a proper decomposition of
(\mu,k,K,A)
at scale
r
with
\rho(f_{1}h_{1}\cdots f_{k}h_{k})\geq\kappa
almost surely.

It is clear that for any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
with
\kappa^{\prime}\leq\kappa
we have

(6.4)

V(\mu,n,K,\kappa^{\prime},A;r)\geq V(\mu,n,K,\kappa,A;r).

6.2.
Existence of Proper Decompositions

We show that for a suitable dependence of the involved parameters, we can construct proper decompositions comparing the variance sum and the trace.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
c,T,\alpha_{0},\theta,A,R>0
with
c,\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
and
T\geq 1
. Then there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(d,R,c,T,\alpha_{0},\theta,A)>0
such that the following is true. Let
\mu
be a contracting on average,
(c,T)
-well-mixing and
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degenerate probability measure on
G
such that
\rho(g)\in[R^{-1},R]
for all
g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
.

Let
\kappa,s>0
with
\kappa
and
s
sufficiently small (in terms of
\mu
and
R
). Let
K
be sufficiently large in terms of
\mu
,
R
, and
T
. Then

V(\mu,1,K,R^{-3K}\kappa,A;R^{-K}\kappa s)\geq c_{1}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};s).

Proof.

We construct a proper decomposition with
n=1
. Let
F
be uniform on
[0,T]\cap\mathbb{Z}
and independent of
\gamma
. Let
\underline{S}
be defined as

\underline{S}=\inf\{n\,:\,\rho(q_{n})\leq R^{-K-1}\}+F

and let

S_{1}:=\inf\{n\geq\underline{S}\,:\,\rho(\gamma_{\underline{S}+1}\cdots\gamma_{n})\leq\kappa\}.

Denote

\underline{f}=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{\underline{S}}\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad g=\gamma_{\underline{S}+1}\gamma_{\underline{S}+2}\cdots\gamma_{S_{1}}.

By the definition of
\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},s)
there is some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
, some random variable
V
taking values in
\mathfrak{g}
, some
\mathscr{A}
-measurable random variable
\overline{f}
taking values in
G
such that
g=\overline{f}\exp(V)
with
|V|\leq s
and

(6.5)

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{tr}(V|\mathscr{A})]\geq\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}},s).

We define
T_{1}=S_{1}+K
and set

h_{1}=\gamma_{S_{1}+1}\gamma_{S_{1}+2}\cdots\gamma_{T_{1}}.

Denote

U_{1}=\begin{cases}V&\text{if }|b(h_{1})|\leq A,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{1}=\begin{cases}\underline{f}\overline{f}&\text{if }|b(h_{1})|\leq A,\\
\underline{f}g&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Furthermore we set
\mathscr{A}_{1}=\sigma(\underline{f},f_{1},h_{1},\mathscr{A})
.

We have

\displaystyle R^{-K-3}R^{-T}\kappa\leq\rho(\underline{f}g)\leq R^{-K-1}R^{T}\kappa.

In particular, we note that
|U_{1}|\leq s
and so providing
\kappa
and
s
are sufficiently small in terms of
R
, we have
R^{-K-4}R^{-T}\kappa\leq\rho(f_{1})\leq R^{T-K}\kappa<1
for
K
sufficiently large in terms of
T
. This means that
|U_{1}|\leq s\leq\rho(f_{1})^{-1}R^{T-K}\kappa s
.

Now let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a unit vector. We wish to show that

\mathbb{E}\left[\var(x\cdot\rho(f_{1})U(f_{1})U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})\right]\geq c_{1}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};s)R^{-2K}\kappa^{2}s^{2}.

Let
f^{\prime}=\underline{f}^{-1}f_{1}
and let
P_{1},\dots,P_{d}
be orthogonal eigenvectors of the covariance matrix of
(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A})
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{d}
. We have

\displaystyle\var(x\cdot\rho(f_{1})U(f_{1})U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}\kappa^{2}\var(x\cdot U(\underline{f})U(f^{\prime})U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}\kappa^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|x\cdot U(\underline{f})U(f^{\prime})P_{i}\right|^{2}\lambda_{i}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}\kappa^{2}\left|x\cdot U(\underline{f})U(f^{\prime})P_{1}\right|^{2}\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})/d.

By Proposition
3.2
we know that when
b(h_{1})\in E_{\theta}(V)
and
|b(h_{1})|\leq A
we have

\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})\geq\delta\cdot\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}|\mathscr{A}_{1}).

By our
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degeneracy condition and since
\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}
converges to
\nu
exponentially fast (see for example
[
26
]
*Lemma 2.2) we know that providing
K
is sufficiently large this happens, conditional on
\mathscr{A}
, with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}(1-\alpha)
. Therefore by (
6.5
)

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})]\geq\frac{1}{4}(1-\alpha)\delta\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};s)s^{2}.

By our
(c,T)
-well-mixing condition we have that providing
K
is sufficiently large in terms of
\mu
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|x\cdot U(\underline{f})U(f^{\prime})P_{1}\right|^{2}|\sigma(h_{1},\mathscr{A})\right]\geq c.

Clearly
\var(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})
is
\sigma(h_{1},\mathscr{A})
-measurable. Therefore, by (
6.6
) and conditioning by
\sigma(h_{1},\mathscr{A})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\var(x\cdot\rho(f_{1})U(f_{1})U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}\kappa^{2}d^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|x\cdot U(\underline{f})U(f^{\prime})P_{1}\right|^{2}\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}\kappa^{2}d^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left|x\cdot U(\underline{f})U(f^{\prime})P_{1}\right|^{2}\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})\,\Big|\,\sigma(h_{1},\mathscr{A})\right]\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}\kappa^{2}d^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left|x\cdot U(\underline{f})U(f^{\prime})P_{1}\right|^{2}\,\Big|\,\sigma(h_{1},\mathscr{A})\right]\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}\kappa^{2}d^{-1}c\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{tr}(U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle R^{-2K-8}R^{-2T}d^{-1}c\cdot\frac{1}{4}(1-\alpha)\delta\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};s)\kappa^{2}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};s)R^{-2K}\kappa^{2}s^{2}

where
c_{1}=R^{-8}R^{-2T}d^{-1}(1-\alpha)\delta c/4
. Since this is true for any unit vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\var(\rho(f_{1})U(f_{1})U_{1}b(h_{1})|\mathscr{A}_{1})}{R^{-2K}\kappa^{2}s^{2}}\right]\geq c_{1}\mathrm{tr}(q_{\tau_{\kappa}};s)I

as required. Finally note that

\displaystyle\rho(f_{1}h_{1})\geq R^{-1}\rho(\underline{f}gh_{1})\geq R^{-1}R^{-K-3}R^{-T}\kappa\cdot R^{-K}=\kappa R^{-2K-4-T}\geq R^{-3K}\kappa

providing
K
is sufficiently large in terms of
T
and
R
.
∎

6.3.
Concatenating Decompositions

We note that it is straightforward to show that for any measure
\mu
and any admissible choice of coefficients, the variance sum is additive

\displaystyle V(\mu,n_{1}+n_{2},K,\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2},A;r)

(6.7)

\displaystyle\geq V(\mu,n_{1},K,\kappa_{1},A;r)+V(\mu,n_{2},K,\kappa_{2},A;\kappa_{1}^{-1}r).

However, in order to use Proposition
5.4
it is necessary to work with different scales
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
and therefore we show the following proposition.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
. Let
R>1
be such that
\rho(g)\in[R^{-1},R]
for every
g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
. Let
n_{1},n_{2},K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
n_{2},K>0
and let
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},r\in(0,1)
. Let
A>0
and let
M\geq R
. Then

\displaystyle V(\mu,n_{1}+n_{2},K,R^{-1}M^{-1}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2},A;r)

\displaystyle\geq V(\mu,n_{1},K,\kappa_{1},A;r)+V(\mu,n_{2},K,\kappa_{2},A;M\kappa_{1}^{-1}r).

Proof.

For
j\in\{1,2\}
let
\gamma_{1}^{(j)},\gamma_{2}^{(j)},\dots
be a sequence of i.i.d. samples from
\mu
defined on the probability space
\left(\Omega_{(j)},\mathscr{F}_{(j)},\mathbb{P}_{(j)}\right)
. Let
\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2},\dots
be a sequence of i.i.d. samples from
\mu
defined on the probability space
\left(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{F}},\hat{\mathbb{P}}\right)
. Consider the product probability space

\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}\right)=\left(\Omega_{1}\times\hat{\Omega}\times\Omega_{2},\mathscr{F}_{1}\times\hat{\mathscr{F}}\times\mathscr{F}_{2},\mathbb{P}_{1}\times\hat{\mathbb{P}}\times\mathbb{P}_{2}\right).

Let
\left(\gamma_{i}^{(1)},S_{i}^{(1)},T_{i}^{(1)},f_{i}^{(1)},U_{i}^{(1)},h_{i}^{(1)},\mathscr{A}_{i}^{(1)},m_{i}^{(1)}\right)
be a proper decomposition for
(\mu,k_{1},K,\kappa_{1},A)
at scale
r
defined on the probability space
\left(\Omega^{(1)},\mathscr{F}^{(1)},\mathbb{P}^{(1)}\right)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}m_{i}^{(1)}
approaches
V(\mu,n_{1},K,\kappa_{1},A;r)
and

\rho(f_{1}^{(1)}h_{1}^{(1)}\cdots f_{k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{k_{1}}^{(1)})\geq\kappa_{1}.

Given
\omega_{1}\in\Omega_{1}
and
\hat{\omega}\in\hat{\Omega}
, let
\tau=\tau(\omega_{1},\hat{\omega})
be given by

\tau=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:\rho(f^{(1)}_{1}h^{(1)}_{1}f^{(1)}_{2}h^{(1)}_{2}\dots f^{(1)}_{k_{1}}h^{(1)}_{k_{1}}\hat{\gamma}_{1}\hat{\gamma}_{2}\dots\hat{\gamma}_{k})<M^{-1}\kappa_{1}\}

and let
\hat{\rho}=\rho(f^{(1)}_{1}h^{(1)}_{1}f^{(1)}_{2}h^{(1)}_{2}\dots f^{(1)}_{k_{1}}h^{(1)}_{k_{1}}\hat{\gamma}_{1}\hat{\gamma}_{2}\dots\hat{\gamma}_{\tau})
such that

\hat{\rho}\in[M^{-1}R^{-1}\kappa_{1},M^{-1}\kappa_{1}].

Now given
\omega_{1}\in\Omega_{1}
and
\hat{\omega}\in\hat{\Omega}
, let
\left(\gamma_{i}^{(2)},S_{i}^{(2)},T_{i}^{(2)},f_{i}^{(2)},U_{i}^{(2)},h_{i}^{(2)},\mathscr{A}_{i}^{(2)},m_{i}^{(2)}\right)
be a proper decomposition for
(\mu,k_{2},K,\kappa_{2},A)
at scale
M\kappa_{1}^{-1}r
defined on the probability space
\left(\Omega^{(2)},\mathscr{F}^{(2)},\mathbb{P}^{(2)}\right)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}m_{i}^{(2)}
approaches
V(\mu,n_{2},K,\kappa_{2},A;M\kappa_{1}^{-1}r)
and

\rho(f_{1}^{(1)}h_{1}^{(1)}\cdots f_{k_{2}}^{(1)}h_{k_{2}}^{(1)})\geq\kappa_{2}.

We now concatenate the two decompositions as follows. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots
be the sequence of random variables on the probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
defined by

\gamma_{i}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{i}^{(1)}&\text{if }i\leq T^{(1)}_{k_{1}}\\
\hat{\gamma}_{i-T^{(1)}_{k_{1}}}&\text{if }i>T^{(1)}_{k_{1}}\text{ and }i\leq T^{(1)}_{k_{1}}+\tau\\
\gamma_{i-T^{(1)}_{k_{1}}-\tau}&\text{if }i>T^{(1)}_{k_{1}}+\tau.\end{cases}

Clearly these are i.i.d. samples from
\mu
. For
i=1,2,\dots,k_{1}+k_{2}
we define
S_{i}
by

S_{i}=\begin{cases}S_{i}^{(1)}&\text{if }i\leq k_{1}\\
S_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)}+T^{(1)}_{k_{1}}+\tau&\text{if }i>k_{1}\end{cases}

and we define
T_{i}
analogously. We define
f_{i}
by

f_{i}=\begin{cases}f_{i}^{(1)}&\text{if }i\leq k_{1}\\
\hat{\gamma}_{1}\dots\hat{\gamma}_{\tau}f_{1}^{(2)}&\text{if }i=k_{1}+1\\
f_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)}&\text{if }i>k_{1}+1.\end{cases}

We define
U_{i}
by

U_{i}=\begin{cases}U_{i}^{(1)}&\text{if }i\leq k_{1}\\
U_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)}&\text{if }i>k_{1}.\end{cases}

and define
h_{i}
and
m_{i}
analogously. Finally we define
\mathscr{A}_{i}
by

\mathscr{A}_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathscr{A}_{i}^{(1)}\times\hat{\Omega}\times\Omega^{(2)}&\text{if }i\leq k_{1}\\
\mathscr{A}_{k_{1}}^{(1)}\times\hat{\mathscr{F}}\times\mathscr{A}_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)}&\text{if }i>k_{1}.\end{cases}

It is easy to check that
\left(\gamma_{i},S_{i},T_{i},f_{i},U_{i},h_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},m_{i}\right)
is a proper decomposition for
(\mu,R,k_{1}+k_{2},K,R^{-1}M^{-1}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2},A)
at scale
r
and it holds that

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}m_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}m_{i}^{(1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}m_{i}^{(2)}.

Indeed, we note that for
i>k_{2}
we have that since
M\kappa_{1}^{-1}\leq\hat{\rho}^{-1}
,

\displaystyle|U_{i}|=|U_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)}|

\displaystyle\leq\rho(f_{1}^{(2)}h_{1}^{(2)}f_{2}^{(2)}h_{2}^{(2)}\cdots h_{i-k_{1}-1}^{(2)}f_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)})^{-1}M\kappa_{1}^{-1}r

\displaystyle\leq\hat{\rho}^{-1}\rho(f_{1}^{(2)}h_{1}^{(2)}f_{2}^{(2)}h_{2}^{(2)}\cdots h_{i-k_{1}-1}^{(2)}f_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)})^{-1}r

\displaystyle=\rho(f_{1}h_{1}f_{2}h_{2}\cdots h_{i-1}f_{i})^{-1}r.

Similarly, for
i>k_{2}+1
and using that
\hat{\rho}^{2}M^{2}\kappa_{1}^{-2}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\var(\rho(f_{i})U(f_{i})U_{i}b(h_{i})|\mathscr{A}_{i})}{\rho(f_{1}h_{1}f_{2}h_{2}\cdots f_{i-1}h_{i-1})^{-2}r^{2}}\,|\,\mathscr{A}_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\var(\rho(f^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}})U(f^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}})U^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}}b(h^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}})|\mathscr{A}_{i})}{\hat{\rho}^{-2}\rho(f_{1}^{(2)}h_{1}^{(2)}f_{2}^{(2)}h_{2}^{(2)}\cdots h_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)})^{-2}r^{2}}\,|\,\mathscr{A}_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\var(\rho(f^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}})U(f^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}})U^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}}b(h^{(2)}_{i-k_{1}})|\mathscr{A}_{i})}{\hat{\rho}^{-2}\rho(f_{1}^{(2)}h_{1}^{(2)}f_{2}^{(2)}h_{2}^{(2)}\cdots h_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)})^{-2}\hat{\rho}^{2}M^{2}\kappa_{1}^{-2}r^{2}}\,|\,\mathscr{A}_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq m_{i-k_{1}}^{(2)}I.

The remainder of the properties are straightforward to check.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
. Let
R>1
be such that
\rho(g)\in[R^{-1},R]
for every
g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
. Let
n,K\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and let
\kappa,r\in(0,1)
. Let
C,A>0
and let
M\geq R
. Then

\displaystyle V(\mu,n,K,R^{-1}M^{-1}\kappa,A;M^{-1}r)\geq V(\mu,n,K,\kappa,A;r)

Proof.

By Proposition
6.4
we have

\displaystyle V(\mu,n,K,R^{-1}M^{-1}\kappa,A;M^{-1}r)

\displaystyle\geq V(\mu,0,K,1,A;M^{-1}r)+V(\mu,n,K,\kappa,A;r).

and simply note that
V(\mu,0,K,1,A;M^{-1}r)=0
.
∎

6.4.
From Variance Sum to Bounding Detail

Proposition 6.6
.

For every
d\geq 1
and
A,\alpha>0
there is a constants
C=C(d,A,\alpha)>0
such that the following is true. Suppose that
\mu
is a contracting on average probability measure on
G
. Then there is some
c=c(\mu)>0
such that whenever
\kappa\leq 1
and
k,K,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
with
K
and
n
sufficiently large (in terms of
A,\alpha
and
\mu
) and
r>0
is sufficiently small (in terms of
A,\alpha
and
\mu
) and

V(\mu,n,K,\kappa,A;r)>Ck

we have

s_{r}^{(k)}(\nu)<\alpha^{k}+n\exp(-cK)+C^{n}\kappa^{-1}r.

Proof.

Suppose that
(f,h,U,\mathscr{A},\gamma,\mathscr{F},S,T,m)
is a proper decomposition of
(\mu,n,K,A)
at scale
r
such that

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
VB
has mean zero and covariance matrix of the form
\lambda I
for some
\lambda\geq 0
.

Proof.

For
h\in H
the random variables
hVB
and
VB
have the same law. This means that the mean of

is invariant under
H
and so since
H
is irreducible it must be zero. Moreover the covariance matrix
M
of

is invariant under conjugation by elements of
H
. Since
M
is symmetric positive definite, it has an eigenvector
v
and therefore
Mv=\lambda v
and
Mhv=hMv=\lambda hv
for some
\lambda\geq 0
and all
h\in H
. Since
H
is irreducible it therefore follows that
M=\lambda I
as claimed.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mu_{U}
be a finitely supported probability measure on
O(d)
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{U})
acts irreducibly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then there exists

only depending on

such that every finitely supported probability measure
\mu
on
G
with

is
(\frac{1}{2d},T)
-well-mixing.

Proof.

Let
H\subset O(d)
be the closure of the group generated by
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{U})
. Then
H
is compact and let

be the Haar probablility measure on
G
and denote by
V
a uniform random variable on
H
. We first claim that for all unit vectors

and

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

(7.1)

\mathbb{E}[|x\cdot Vy|^{2}]=\frac{1}{d}.

Indeed, we can view

as a random variable independent from

and therefore, by Lemma

, the random variable
Vy
has mean zero and covariance matrix
\lambda I
. Moreover, since
\mathbb{E}[|Vy|^{2}]=d\lambda=1
it follows that

and therefore (

) holds.

Let
F
be a uniform random variable on
[0,T]
. Then

is distributed as

(7.2)

\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{i=0}^{T}\mu_{U}^{*i}.

We claim that
(

)

in the weak

-topology. Indeed, we note that any weak

-limit

of (

) is

-stationary and, upon performing an ergodic decomposition, we may assume without loss of generality that

is in addition ergodic. As this is equivalent to the measure being extremal, we conclude that

and therefore also by

, implying that

.

Finally, we just choose

and

sufficiently large depending on

)
is sufficiently close in distribution to

and therefore

for all unit vectors

∎

It is straightforward to show that

-well-mixing is continuous in measure

. We state the following version with the

-Wasserstein distance in order to prove our non-degeneracy results in the next subsection.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\mu_{U}
be a probability measure on

and suppose that

is

-well-mixing for some

. Then for every

, for

, then

is

-well-mixing.

Proof.

This follows as small pertubations in the matrices, lead to small pertubations in

for all

with unit norm.
∎

For a closed subgroup
H\subset O(d)
and a probability measure
\mu_{U}
supported on
H
we denote, as defined in (
2.19
), by
\mathrm{gap}_{H}(\mu_{U})
the
L^{2}
-spectral gap of
\mu_{U}
on

. We aim to show uniform well-mixing as long as
\mathrm{gap}_{H}(\mu_{U})\geq\varepsilon
independent of the subgroup
H
. To do so, we first show that we have uniform convergence in the Wasserstein distance with a rate only depending on
\varepsilon
and

.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
d\geq 1,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
\mu_{U}
be a probability measure on
O(d)
. Assume that
\mathrm{gap}_{H}(\mu_{U})\geq\varepsilon
for
H
the subgroup generated by the support of
\mu_{U}
. Then for
n\geq 1

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu_{U}^{*n},m_{H})\ll_{d}(1-\varepsilon)^{\alpha n}

for
\alpha=(1+\frac{1}{2}\dim O(d))^{-1}
.

Proof.

We consider the metric
d(g_{1},g_{2})=||g_{1}-g_{2}||
on
O(d)
for
||\circ||
the operator norm and note that it is bi-invariant and restricts to
H
. Denote by
B^{H}_{\delta}(h)
for
h\in H
and
\delta>0
the
\delta
-ball around
h\in H
and denote

P_{\delta}=\frac{1_{B^{H}_{\delta}(e)}}{m_{H}(B^{H}_{\delta}(e))}.

For
\delta\in(0,1)
we note that
m_{H}(B^{H}_{\delta}(e))\gg_{d}\delta^{\dim O(d)}
for an implied constant depending only on
d
and therefore
||P_{\delta}||_{2}\ll_{d}\delta^{-(\dim O(d))/2}
. Also we note that for
h\in H
we have
(\mu^{*n}*P_{\delta})(h)=\frac{\mu^{*n}(B_{\delta}^{H}(h))}{m_{H}(B^{H}_{\delta}(e))}.
By the triangle inequality,

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{*n},m_{H})\leq\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{*n},\mu^{*n}*P_{\delta})+\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{*n}*P_{\delta},m_{H}).

Note
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{*n},\mu^{*n}*P_{\delta})\ll_{d}\delta
and since
H
is compact,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{*n}*P_{\delta},m_{H})

\displaystyle\ll_{d}||\mu^{*n}*P_{\delta}-1||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq||\mu^{*n}*P_{\delta}-1||_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}||P_{\delta}||_{2}\ll_{d}(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\delta^{-(\dim O(d))/2}.

To conclude, if follows

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{*n},m_{H})\ll_{d}\delta+(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\delta^{-(\dim O(d))/2}.

Therefore setting
\delta=(1-\varepsilon)^{\alpha n}
for
\alpha=(1+\frac{1}{2}\dim O(d))^{-1}
implies the claim.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
d\geq 1,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
\mu_{U}
be a probability measure on
O(d)
. Assume that
\mathrm{gap}_{H}(\mu_{U})\geq\varepsilon
for
H
the subgroup generated by the support of
\mu_{U}
. Then there exists
T=T(d,\varepsilon)
only depending on
d
and
\varepsilon
such every probability measure
\mu
on
G
with
U(\mu)=\mu_{U}
is
(\frac{1}{2d},T)
-well-mixing.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of Lemma
7.2
and recall the notation used in it. Consider a list of tuples of unit vectors
(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{m},y_{m})
such that for every two unit vectors
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
there is some
i\in[m]
such that

\sup_{U\in O(d)}\Big|\,|x\cdot Uy|^{2}-|x_{i}\cdot Uy_{i}|^{2}\Big|<\frac{1}{4d}.

Such a list of tuples exists as the action of
O(d)
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is uniformly continuous. We claim that for
T
large enough depending only on
\varepsilon
we have for all
i\in[m]
that

\mathbb{E}[|x_{i}\cdot U(q_{F})y_{i}|^{2}]\geq\frac{3}{4d}.

Indeed, we note that for

we have

\big|\,|x_{i}\cdot h_{1}y_{i}|^{2}-|x_{i}\cdot h_{2}y_{i}|^{2}\,\big|\leq\big|\,|x_{i}\cdot h_{1}y_{i}|+|x_{i}\cdot h_{2}y_{i}|\,\big|\cdot\big|\,|x_{i}\cdot h_{1}y_{i}|-|x_{i}\cdot h_{2}y_{i}|\,\big|\leq 2||h_{1}-h_{2}||.

Thus it follows that

\mathbb{E}[|x_{i}\cdot Vy_{i}|^{2}-|x_{i}\cdot U(q_{n})y_{i}|^{2}]\leq 2\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{*n},m_{H})

and the claim follows by Lemma
7.4
. This concludes the proof as for all
x
and

we have

\mathbb{E}[|x\cdot U(q_{F})y|^{2}]\geq\sup_{i\in[m]}\mathbb{E}[|x_{i}\cdot U(q_{F})y_{i}|^{2}]-\frac{1}{4d}\geq\frac{1}{2d}.

∎

7.2.
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degeneracy

In order to state our results on
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degeneracy it is useful to understand that we can translate and rescale our generating measures, without changing any of the fundamental properties. It is also beneficial to replace
\mu
by
\frac{1}{2}\delta_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\mu
and we show in the following lemma that these changes do not change our self-similar measure or any of the relevant constants in a fundamental way.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
\mu=\sum_{i}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
be a contracting on average probability measure on
G
with self-measure
\nu
. Let
h\in G
and consider the measures

\mu_{h}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\delta_{hg_{i}h^{-1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu_{h}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{h}.

Then the following properties hold:

(i)

h_{\mu}=h_{\mu_{h}}=2h_{\mu_{h}^{\prime}}
,

(ii)

\chi_{\mu}=\chi_{\mu_{h}}=2\chi_{\mu_{h}^{\prime}}
,

(iii)

S_{\mu}=S_{\mu_{h}}=S_{\mu_{h}^{\prime}}
,

(iv)

\mathrm{gap}_{H}(\mu)=\mathrm{gap}_{U(h)HU(h)^{-1}}(\mu_{h})=2\mathrm{gap}_{U(h)HU(h)^{-1}}(\mu_{h}^{\prime})
,

(v)

\mu_{h}
and
\mu_{h^{\prime}}
have
h\nu
as self-similar measure.

Proof.

As conjugation is a bijection on
G
and by using
[
20
, Lemma 6.8]
, (i) follows. Moreover, (ii) follows since
\rho(hg_{i}h^{-1})=\rho(g_{i})
and (iv) follows similarly. To show (iii) note
S_{\mu_{h}}=S_{\mu_{h}^{\prime}}
since by the triangle inequality
d(g,h)\leq d(g,e)+d(e,h)
for all
g,h\in G
. To show that
S_{\mu}=S_{\mu_{h}}
, set

A=\min_{g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu),g_{1}\neq g_{2}}d(g_{1},g_{2})

and note that there is a constant
C_{h}
depending on
h
such that
d(hg_{1}h^{-1},hg_{2}h^{-1})\leq C_{h}d(g_{1},g_{2})
for
d(g_{1},g_{2})\leq A
. Thus it holds that

\displaystyle S_{\mu_{h}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{g_{1},g_{2}\in S_{n},g_{1}\neq g_{2}}-\frac{1}{n}\log d(hg_{1}h^{-1},hg_{2}h^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{g_{1},g_{2}\in S_{n},g_{1}\neq g_{2}}-\frac{1}{n}\log C_{h}d(g_{1},g_{2})=S_{\mu}

Applying the same argument to conjugation by
h^{-1}
implies the claim. Finally, we note that
\mu_{h}
and
\mu_{h}^{\prime}
have the same self-similar measure and it holds that

h\nu=h\sum_{i}p_{i}g_{i}\nu=\sum_{i}p_{i}hg_{i}h^{-1}h\nu

and therefore
h\nu
is the self-similar measure of
\mu_{h}
and
\mu_{h}^{\prime}
.
∎

In particular, it follows that the self-similar measure of
\mu
is absolutely continuous if and only if the one of
\mu_{h}
or
\mu_{h^{\prime}}
is and all of the relevant quantities are the same up to a factor of
2
.

To give an idea of the proof of the main results in this subsection, we first discuss how to show that real Bernoulli convolutions
\nu_{\lambda}
are uniformly non-degenerate. Indeed, we distinguish between
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}
for some
\lambda_{0}
sufficiently close to
1
. Note that
\nu_{\lambda}
is supported on
[-(1-\lambda)^{-1},(1-\lambda)^{-1}]
and thus when

one easily checks uniform non-degeneracy depending only on

using for example that Bernoulli convolutions are symmetric around

. In the case

the idea is to use an

-version of the Berry-Essen Theorem as stated in Theorem

. Indeed, by rescaling

by

to

so that it has unit variance it follows from Theorem

that
\mathcal{W}_{3}(\nu_{\lambda},\mathcal{N}(0,1)))\ll\frac{(1-\lambda^{2})^{1/2}}{(1-\lambda^{3})^{1/3}}\to 0
as

. The latter then implies the claim by Lemma

using that

. It is necessary to work with

as a brief calculation show that the above calculation would not work with

or

.

Our results will be deduced from suitable results in the case when

has a uniform contraction ratio and then in the general case from comparing our given measure with a self-similar measure with uniform contraction ratio. We now state the main proposition of this section.

Proposition 7.7
.

,

and

We first show how to deduce from Proposition
7.7
the two propositions
2.2
and
2.3
from section

. To do so we first state the following lemma.

Lemma 7.8
.

Suppose
x_{1}<x_{2}
and let
X
be a real-valued random variable such that
X\leq x_{2}
almost surely and
\mathbb{P}[X\leq x_{1}]\geq 1/2+p
for some
p>0
. Then

\mathbb{E}[|X-x_{1}|]\leq\mathbb{E}[|X-x_{2}|]-2p(x_{2}-x_{1}).

Proof.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
have the same law as
X
and be coupled such that at least one of them is at most
x_{1}
almost surely. Let
A
be the event that both
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are at most
x_{1}
. Noting that
A
has probability at least
2p
we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}-x_{1}|+|X_{2}-x_{1}|]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(|X_{1}-x_{1}|+|X_{2}-x_{1}|)\mathbb{I}_{A^{C}}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[(|X_{1}-x_{1}|+|X_{2}-x_{1}|)\mathbb{I}_{A}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[(|X_{1}-x_{2}|+|X_{2}-x_{2}|)\mathbb{I}_{A^{C}}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[(|X_{1}-x_{2}|+|X_{2}-x_{2}|-2(x_{2}-x_{1}))\mathbb{I}_{A}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}-x_{2}|+|X_{2}-x_{2}|]-4p(x_{2}-x_{1}).

The result follows.
∎

We now prove Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. samples from
\mu
. Let
p_{\text{min}}
be the smallest of the
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
and let
\rho_{\text{min}}
be the smallest of the
\rho(g_{1}),\dots,\rho(g_{k})
. Clearly

\mathbb{P}[\rho(\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n})\leq\rho_{\text{min}}]\geq 1-(1-p_{\text{min}})^{n}.

In particular there is some
n
depending only on
\varepsilon
such that this is at least
3/4
. Note that by Lemma
7.8
with
x_{1}=\rho_{\min}
and
x_{2}=1
and

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[|\rho(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{n})-\rho_{\text{min}}|]}{1-\mathbb{E}[\rho(\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n})]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\mathbb{E}[\rho(\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n})]-(1-\rho_{\text{min}})/2}{1-\mathbb{E}[\rho(\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n})]}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1-\rho_{\text{min}}}{2(1-\mathbb{E}[\rho(\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n})])}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{1-\rho_{\text{min}}}{2(1-\rho_{\text{min}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{1}{2n}.

The result now follows by applying Proposition

, Lemma
7.2
and Lemma

to

along with noting that compact subsets of

are also compact with respect to

.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

This follows directly by Proposition

, Lemma
7.2
and Lemma

.
∎

Now we prove Proposition
7.7
. We use the following definition.

Definition 7.9
.

Given two measures
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define

\mathcal{PW}_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}\sup_{p\in P(d)}\int|px-py|\,d\gamma(x,y)

where
P(d)
is the set of orthogonal projections onto one dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is the set of couplings between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
.

We use this to show that if a measure is sufficiently close to a spherical normal distribution then it is
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degenerate.

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
I
be the
d\times d
identity matrix. Then given any
p\in P(d)
we have

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim N(0,I)}[|px|]=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}.

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\theta,A>0
such that if
\nu
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\mathcal{PW}_{1}(\nu,N(0,I))<\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}-\varepsilon

then
\nu
is
(\alpha_{0},\theta,A)
-non-degenerate.

Proof.

The first part follows since if
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a unit vector, then
\langle X,u\rangle
is distributed as
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. The second part follows from the first part, the fact that the
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim N(0,1)}|x-y|
is minimal is
y=0
and Markov’s inequality.

More precisely, we aim to estimate for all
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all proper subspaces
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x-(y_{0}+W)|<\theta\text{ or }|x|\geq A\}),

which is bounded by
\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x-(y_{0}+W)|<\theta\})+\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x|\geq A\})
. To deal with the second term we note that by Markov’s inequality for a coupling
\gamma
between
\nu
and
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
we have

\displaystyle\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x|\geq A\})

\displaystyle\leq A^{-1}\int|x|\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq A^{-1}\left(\int|y|\,d\mathcal{N}(0,I)(y)+\int|x-y|\,d\gamma(x,y)\right).

In order to apply our bound for
\mathcal{PW}_{1}(\nu,N(0,I))
we consider the projections
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
to the coordinate axes. Then
|x-y|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}|p_{i}x-p_{i}y|
and therefore by choosing a suitable coupling, it follows that for
A
sufficiently large only depending on
d
and
\varepsilon
we have that
\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x|\geq A\})\leq\varepsilon/16
.

To deal with the first term
\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x-(y_{0}+W)|<\theta\})
, we assume without loss of generality that
W
has dimension
d-1
and we let
p
be the orthogonal projection to the orthogonal complement of
W
. Then it holds that
|x-(y_{0}+W)|=|px-py_{0}|
and therefore

\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x-(y_{0}+W)|<\theta\})=\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|px-py_{0}|<\theta\}).

In the following we identify
p\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the real line. Let
\gamma
be any coupling between
\nu
and
\mathcal{N}(0,I)
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\int|px-py|\,d\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\int|px-py|1_{|px-py_{0}|<\theta}(x,y)\,d\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,(|px-py_{0}|<\theta\})\int|py-py_{0}|-\theta)\,d\mathcal{N}(0,I)(y)

\displaystyle\geq\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,(|px-py_{0}|<\theta\})\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}-\theta\right),

having used in the last line that
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim N(0,1)}|x-y|
is minimal is
y=0
. By choosing a suitable coupling and setting
\theta=\varepsilon/4
it therefore follows for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small that

\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,(|px-py_{0}|<\theta\})\leq\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}-\varepsilon/2}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}-\varepsilon/4}\leq 1-\varepsilon/8.

The claim follows by combining the above two estimates.
∎

To make this useful we need to show that our self-similar measures are close to spherical normal distributions. We prove this in the case where all of the

are equal with the following proposition.

In order to prove Proposition

Theorem 7.16
.

For every

such that the following holds. Suppose that

are independent random variables taking values in

with mean

\Sigma_{i}=\var X_{i}
, suppose that

This is enough to deduce the following estimate. We note that we work with
\mathcal{W}_{3}
norm in order to establish the decaying
(n^{\prime})^{-1/6}
term in (
7.4

Lemma 7.17
.

Let
(p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
be a probability vector,
U_{1},\dots,U_{k}\in O(d)
generate an irreducible subgroup,
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\rho\in(0,1)
. Let
\mu
be the probability measure on
G
given by
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
where
g_{i}:x\mapsto\rho U_{i}x+b_{i}
. Suppose that
\mu
is centred at zero and that all of the
b_{i}
have modulus at most
1
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. samples from
\mu
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

Given
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we define
S_{\ell}:=\mathbb{E}[|\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{\ell}(0)|^{2}]
and

W_{\ell}:=\mathcal{W}_{3}\left(d^{1/2}S_{\ell}^{-1/2}\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{\ell}(0),N(0,I)\right).

Suppose that there exist
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that for
V
a uniform random variable on the closure of the subgroup generated by the
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
we have

\mathcal{W}_{3}(U(\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{m}),V)<\varepsilon\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad\frac{m}{S_{n}^{1/2}}<\varepsilon.

Then for
n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

(7.3)

W_{(m+n)n^{\prime}}\ll_{d}(T^{-1/6}+T^{1/2}\varepsilon)(W_{n}+1)

where
T:=\sum_{i=0}^{n^{\prime}-1}\rho^{(m+n)i}
. In particular if
\rho^{(m+n)n^{\prime}}>1/2
then
n^{\prime}/2\leq T\leq n^{\prime}
and therefore

(7.4)

W_{(m+n)n^{\prime}}\ll_{d}((n^{\prime})^{-1/6}+(n^{\prime})^{1/2}\varepsilon)(W_{n}+1)

Proof.

For
i=1,\dots,n^{\prime}
let

X_{i}:=\gamma_{(i-1)(n+m)+1}\dots\gamma_{(i-1)(n+m)+m}

and

Y_{i}:=\gamma_{(i-1)(n+m)+m+1}\dots\gamma_{i(n+m)}

such that

Z_{i}=X_{i}Y_{i}=\gamma_{(i-1)(n+m)+1}\dots\gamma_{i(n+m)}.

Furthermore consider
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
independent random variables which are uniform on
H
(the closure of the subgroup generated by the
U_{i}
), independent of the
Y_{i}
and are such that

\mathbb{E}[\|U(X_{i})-V_{i}\|^{3}]<\varepsilon^{3}.

Note that

\displaystyle Z_{1}\dots Z_{n^{\prime}}(0)

\displaystyle=Z_{1}(0)+\rho^{(m+n)}U(Z_{1})Z_{2}(0)+

\displaystyle\dots+\rho^{(m+n)(n^{\prime}-1)}U(Z_{1}\dots Z_{n^{\prime}-1})Z_{n^{\prime}}(0).

Also note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}\left(\rho^{(m+n)(i-1)}U(Z_{1}\dots Z_{i-1})Z_{i}(0),\rho^{(m+n)(i-1)+m}V_{i}Y_{i}(0)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho^{(m+n)(i-1)}\mathcal{W}_{3}\left(U(Z_{1}\dots Z_{i-1})(\rho^{m}U(X_{i})Y_{i}(0)+X_{i}(0)),\rho^{m}V_{i}Y_{i}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{(m+n)(i-1)}(m+\varepsilon\rho^{m}(\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{i}(0)|^{3}\right])^{1/3})

\displaystyle\ll_{d}\varepsilon\rho^{(m+n)(i-1)}S_{n}^{1/2}(W_{n}+1),

having used the triangle inequality in the second line and that
|X_{i}(0)|\leq m
as
\sup_{i}|b_{i}|\leq 1
as well as that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}\left(U(Z_{1}\dots Z_{i-1})U(X_{i})Y_{i}(0),V_{i}Y_{i}(0)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{3}\left(U(Z_{1}\dots Z_{i-1})U(X_{i})Y_{i}(0),U(Z_{1}\dots Z_{i-1})V_{i}Y_{i}(0)\right)

as
V_{i}
is distributed like the Haar measure on
H
.

Note that by Lemma
7.1
the covariance matrix of
V_{i}Y_{i}(0)
is
d^{-1}S_{n}I
. Therefore by Theorem
7.16
letting
A=d^{-1/2}\left(\frac{1-\rho^{2n^{\prime}(m+n)}}{1-\rho^{2(m+n)}}\right)^{1/2}S_{n}^{1/2}
we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}\left(A^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\rho^{(m+n)(i-1)}V_{i}Y_{i}(0)\right),N(0,I)\right)

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[|A^{-1}\rho^{(m+n)(i-1)}Y_{i}(0)|^{3}\right]\right)^{1/3}

\displaystyle\ll_{d}A^{-1}\left(\frac{1-\rho^{3(m+n)n^{\prime}}}{1-\rho^{3(m+n)}}\right)^{1/3}(W_{n}+1)

\displaystyle\ll_{d}T^{-1/6}(W_{n}+1),

where we exploited that

\frac{1-\rho^{2n^{\prime}(m+n)}}{1-\rho^{2(m+n)}}=\frac{1-\rho^{n^{\prime}(m+n)}}{1-\rho^{(m+n)}}\frac{1+\rho^{n^{\prime}(m+n)}}{1+\rho^{(m+n)}}\in[T/2,T]

and a similar estimate for
\left(\frac{1-\rho^{3(m+n)n^{\prime}}}{1-\rho^{3(m+n)}}\right)^{1/3}
.

Therefore we may deduce that

\mathcal{W}_{3}\left(A^{-1}\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{(m+n)n^{\prime}}(0),N(0,I)\right)\ll_{d}T^{-1/6}(W_{n}+1)+\varepsilon T^{1/2}(W_{n}+1)

By Lemma
7.15
we have that

\frac{d^{-1/2}S_{n^{\prime}}^{-1/2}}{A}=1+O(\frac{m}{n})=1+O(\varepsilon).

We conclude

\displaystyle W_{(m+n)n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ll_{d}T^{-1/6}(W_{n}+1)+\varepsilon T^{1/2}(W_{n}+1)+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\ll_{d}T^{-1/6}(W_{n}+1)+\varepsilon T^{1/2}(W_{n}+1)

as required.
∎

\varepsilon>0
and every irreducible probability measure

on

\delta>0
,

and

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0

7.15

Throughout this section we denote as usual by

8.1.
Bounding Random Walk Entropy

The techniques from
[
20
, Section 6.3]
or
[
28
, Section 9.2]
follow through to our setting. In particular we have the following using Breuillard’s strong Tits alternative.

Proposition 8.1
.

(
[
20
, Section 6.3]
)
Let
d\geq 1
. Then for every
p_{0}>0
there exists
\rho=\rho(p_{0},d)
such that if
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}
is a finitely supported probability measure on

with

and
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
generates a non-virtually solvable subgroup, then

.

We will also use the following version of the ping-pong lemma for which we provide a full proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 8.2
.

(Ping-Pong)
Let
G
be a group acting on a set
X
and let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
. Assume there exist disjoint non-empty sets
A_{1},A_{2}\subset X
such

g_{1}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})\subset A_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad g_{2}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})\subset A_{2}.

Then
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
generate a free semigroup.

When this happens we say that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}

play ping pong
.

Proof.

Let
w_{1}=h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{\ell_{1}}
and
w_{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{\ell_{2}}
with distinct sequences
h_{i},f_{j}\in\{g_{1},g_{2}\}
. Assume without loss of generality that
\ell_{1}\leq\ell_{2}
. First assume that there is some
1\leq k\leq\ell_{1}
such that
h_{k}\neq f_{k}
. Choose the smallest such
k
and note that it suffices to show that
h_{k}\cdots h_{\ell_{1}}\neq f_{k}\cdots f_{\ell_{2}}
, which follows by applying the resulting maps to any
x\in A_{1}\cup A_{2}
and noting that
h_{k}\cdots h_{\ell_{1}}x\neq f_{k}\cdots f_{\ell_{2}}x
. On the other hand assume that
h_{i}=f_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell_{1}
, in which case we need to show that
w^{\prime}=f_{\ell_{1}+1}\cdots f_{\ell_{2}}
is not the identity. Without loss of generality assume that
f_{\ell_{1}+1}=g_{1}
. Then for
x\in A_{2}
we have that
w^{\prime}x\in A_{1}
and thus
w^{\prime}
is not the identity. We note that in particular it follows by the assumptions that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
have infinite order.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported probability measure on
G
such that
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
generate a free semigroup. Then

h_{\mu}\gg\min\{\mu(g_{1}),\mu(g_{2})\}.

Proof.

Denote
\mu^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\mu
. Then by
[
20
, Lemma 6.8]
we have
h_{\mu^{\prime}}=h_{\mu}/2
. Thus the claim follows from
[
28
, Proposition 9.7]
(generalised to
G
and applied to
K=\min\{\mu(g_{1}),\mu(g_{2})\}/2
).
∎

8.1.1.
p
-adic Ping-Pong

We first use ping-pong in a

-adic setting. For a number field
K
with ring of integers
O_{K}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset O_{K}
be a prime ideal and we denote by
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
the localization of

at

defined as

R_{\mathfrak{p}}=\left\{\frac{a}{b}:a\in O_{K},b\in O_{K}\backslash\mathfrak{p}\right\}.

Lemma 8.4
.

(
p
-adic Ping-Pong)
Let
K
be a number field and let
O_{K}
be its ring of integers. Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset O_{K}
be a prime ideal and let
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the ideal of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
defined by

M_{\mathfrak{p}}=\left\{\frac{a}{b}:a\in\mathfrak{p},b\in O_{K}\backslash\mathfrak{p}\right\}.

Let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
be such that all of the entries of
\rho(g_{1})U(g_{1})
and
\rho(g_{2})U(g_{2})
are in
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
and all components of
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are in

. Suppose that

M_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\cdots\times M_{\mathfrak{p}}+b_{1}\neq M_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\cdots\times M_{\mathfrak{p}}+b_{2}.

Then

and
g_{2}
generate a free semigroup.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma

with

and

for

.
∎

8.1.2.
Ping-Pong under a Galois transform

We can also apply the ping-pong lemma using field automorphisms. Recall that given a number field
K
, the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(K/\mathbb{Q})
consists of field automorphisms that fix
\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 8.5
.

(Galois Ping-Pong)
Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be two elements in
G
whose coefficients lie in a real number field
K
and without a common fixed point. Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(K/\mathbb{Q})
be such that for
i=1,2
we have

|\rho(\Phi(g_{i}))|<1/3.

Then
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
generate a free semigroup.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
write
h_{i}=\Phi(g_{i})
and let
p_{i}
be the fixed point of
h_{i}
, which has coefficients in
K
since it arises from a linear equation over
K
. Then
h_{1}\neq h_{2}
as
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
have no common fixed point. Consider
A_{i}=B_{d(h_{1},h_{2})/2}(h_{i})
(the open ball around
h_{i}
of radius
d(h_{1},h_{2})/2
) and note further that
h_{1}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})\subset A_{1}
and
h_{2}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})\subset A_{2}
. So the claim follows by Lemma
8.2
.
∎

8.1.3.

In dimension one we also have the following tool for bounding the random walk entropy. We use the absolute height

of an algebraic number

8.2.
Heights and Separation

In this subsection we will review some techniques for bounding
S_{\mu}
using heights as defined in (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). We wish to bound the size of polynomials of algebraic numbers. To do this we need the following way of measuring the complexity of a polynomial.

Definition 8.8
.

Given some polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}]
we define the
length
of
P
, which we denote by
\mathcal{L}(P)
, to be the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients of
P
.

We recall the following basic facts about heights.

Lemma 8.9
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

\mathcal{H}(\alpha^{-1})=\mathcal{H}(\alpha)
for any non-zero algebraic number
\alpha
.

(ii)

If
\alpha
is a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
,

\mathcal{H}(\alpha)^{-d}\leq|\alpha|\leq\mathcal{H}(\alpha)^{d}.

(iii)

Given
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}]
of degree at most
L_{1}\geq 0
in
X_{1}
,
\dots
,
L_{n}\geq 0
in
X_{n}
and algebraic numbers
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{n}
we have

\mathcal{H}(P(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{n}))\leq\mathcal{L}(P)\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1})^{L_{1}}\dots\mathcal{H}(\xi_{n})^{L_{n}}

Proof.

(i) and (ii) are well-known and (iii) is
[
34
, Proposition 14.7]
.
∎

Proposition 8.10
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a finitely supported measure on
G=\mathrm{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
S
be the set of coefficients of
\rho(g),U(g)
and
b(g)
with
g\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
supported on a finite set of points. Suppose that all of the elements of
S
are algebraic and let
K
be the number field generated by
S
. Then

S_{\mu}\ll_{d}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\max(\{h(y):y\in S\}\cup\{1\}).

Proof.

We let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and we consider an expression of the from

a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m}

for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}
elements in the support of
\mu
. We wish to show that this is either the identity or at least some distance away from the identity. Let
C:=\max\{\mathcal{H}(y):y\in S\}
. First note that

\rho(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})-1

is a polynomial in elements of
S
and their inverses with length
2
and total degree at most
n+m
. Therefore by Lemma
8.9

H(\rho(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})-1)\leq 2C^{m+n}

and so either
\rho(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})=1
or

|\rho(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})-1|\geq 2^{-[K:\mathbb{Q}]}C^{-(m+n)[K:\mathbb{Q}]}.

By a similar argument, using that the coefficients of the inverse matrix of a matrix are polynomial in the coefficients of the given matrix, we see that either

U(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})=I

or

||U(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})-I||\geq(d^{m+n}+1)^{-[K:\mathbb{Q}]}C^{-O_{d}(m+n)[K:\mathbb{Q}]}

and that either
b(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})=0
or

|b(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m})|\geq(d^{m+n}+1)^{-[K:\mathbb{Q}]}C^{-O_{d}(m+n)[K:\mathbb{Q}]}.

Overall this means that either
a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m}=\id
or

\log d(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}^{-1}\dots a_{n}^{-1}b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{m},\id)\gg_{d}-(m+n)(\log C+1)[K:\mathbb{Q}].

The result follows.
∎

C>1
such that the following holds. Let
\lambda\in(1/2,1)
be a real algebraic number. Then the Bernoulli convolution
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{R}
if

(8.2)

\lambda>1-C^{-1}\min\{\log M_{\lambda},(\log\log M_{\lambda})^{-2}\}.

Proof of Corollary
1.15
.

As in the paragraph before Proposition
7.7
, Bernoulli convolutions are uniformly non-degenerate. Since we are in
d=1
they are
(1,0)
-well-mixing and therefore Theorem
2.4
applies. For convenience write
\eta=\log M_{\lambda}
and
h_{\lambda}=h_{\nu_{\lambda}}
. We don’t keep track of possible enlargements of
C
. That Bernoulli convolutions are uniformly non-degenerate follows from Proposition
2.2
. Then Theorem
2.4
implies that if

(8.3)

(1-\lambda)^{-1}h_{\lambda}>C\left(\max\left\{1,\log\eta/h_{\lambda}\right\}\right)^{2},

then
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous. Recall that by
[
14
, Theorem 5]
(which is stated with logarithms base 2) there is an absolute
c_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
c_{0}\min(\log 2,\eta)\leq h_{\lambda}\leq\min(\log 2,\eta).

We proceed with a case distinction. First assume that
\eta\leq\log 2
. Then
c_{0}^{-1}\geq\eta/h_{\lambda}\geq 1
and therefore by (
8.3
) the condition
(1-\lambda)^{-1}c_{0}\eta>C
is sufficient for absolute continuity, which is equivalent to

(8.4)

\lambda>1-C^{-1}\eta.

Next assume that
\eta\geq\log 2
. Then
c_{0}\log 2\leq h_{\lambda}\leq\log 2
and so (
8.3
) gives

(1-\lambda)\max\{1,\log\eta+\log(c_{0}\log 2)^{-1}\}^{2}<C^{-1}.

Note that
\max\{1,\log\eta+\log(c_{0}\log 2)^{-1}\}\leq 2\log(c_{0}\log 2)^{-1}\max\{1,\log\eta\}
. Therefore we get the condition

(8.5)

\lambda>1-C^{-1}\max\{1,\log\eta\}^{-2}=1-C^{-1}\min\{1,(\log\eta)^{-2}\}.

To deduce (
8.2
), we note that there is a unique
\eta^{\prime}>0
with
\eta^{\prime}=(\log\eta^{\prime})^{-2}
and this
\eta^{\prime}
satisfies
2\leq\eta^{\prime}\leq 5/2
. Moreover
\log\eta<(\log\eta)^{-2}
for
0<\eta<\eta^{\prime}
and
\log\eta>(\log\eta)^{-2}
for
\eta>\eta^{\prime}
. Therefore (
8.2
) holds for
\eta<\log(2)
and
\eta>2\eta^{\prime}
by (
8.4
) and (
8.5
). In the range
\log(2)<\eta<2\eta^{\prime}
, we enlarge
C
to ensure that (
8.2
) holds.
∎

We note that if
\lambda
is algebraic and not the root of any non-zero polynomial with coefficients
0,\pm 1
, then
h_{\lambda}=2
and also as mentioned in Remark 5.10 of
[
27
]
,
M_{\lambda}\geq 2
. Therefore for such a
\lambda
,
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous if

(8.6)

\lambda>1-C^{-1}\min\{1,(\log\log M_{\lambda})^{-2}\}.

8.6.
Complex Bernoulli Convolutions

Corollary

(Restatement of Corollary
1.16
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that the following holds. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
be a complex algebraic number such that
|\lambda|\in(2^{-1/2},1)
and

(8.7)

|\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|\geq\varepsilon.

Then the Bernoulli convolution
\nu_{\lambda}
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{C}
if

|\lambda|>1-C^{-1}\min\{\log M_{\lambda},(\log\log M_{\lambda})^{-2}\}.

Proof of Corollary
1.16
.

We can’t directly apply Proposition
2.2
so we give a direct proof of mixing and non-degeneracy. First note that (
8.7
) ensures that there is some
c>0
and
T\geq 1
depending only on
\varepsilon
such that the
(c,T)
-well-mixing property is satisfied.

To deal with non-degeneracy, we distinguish the case when
|\lambda|\leq\lambda_{0}
and
|\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
for some
\lambda_{0}
sufficiently close to
1
. As in the case of real Bernoulli convolution, for any given
\lambda_{0}
, the family of Bernoulli convolutions with
|\lambda|\leq\lambda_{0}
are easily seen to be uniformly non-degenerate depending on
\lambda_{0}
. To deal with the case
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, we rescale our measure to the one given by the law of
B_{\lambda}=\sqrt{1-|\lambda|^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\pm\lambda^{i}
and denote the resulting measure by
\nu_{\lambda}^{\prime}
. Now let
\Sigma
be the covariance matrix of
\nu_{\lambda}^{\prime}
under the natural identification of
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Note that the trace of
\Sigma
is
1
and we claim that the smallest eigenvalue of
\Sigma
is
\gg_{\varepsilon}1
. Indeed, for a unit vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we want to estimate
x^{T}\Sigma x
, which is by identifying
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}
equal to

\mathbb{E}[|B_{\lambda}\cdot x|^{2}]=(1-|\lambda|^{2})\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|\lambda^{i}\cdot x|^{2}\gg_{\varepsilon}1,

which follows as
|\lambda^{i}\cdot x|^{2}\gg|\lambda|^{2}
unless
\lambda^{i}
and
x
are almost colinear, which is only the case for a very small proportion of
i
’s. It follows that

\inf_{p\in P(2)}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)}[|px|]\gg_{\varepsilon}1

for
p
ranging in the orthogonal projections of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as in section
7.2
. By for example Lemma
4.10
we know that
\mathcal{W}_{1}\left(\nu_{\lambda}^{\prime},N(0,\Sigma)\right)\ll\sqrt{1-|\lambda|^{2}}.
Therefore for
\lambda_{0}
sufficiently close to
1
in terms of
\varepsilon
, uniform non-degeneracy follows as in Lemma
7.10
. Having establish uniform well-mixing and non-degeneracy, Corollary
1.16
is established by the same argument as the proof of Corollary
1.15
.
∎

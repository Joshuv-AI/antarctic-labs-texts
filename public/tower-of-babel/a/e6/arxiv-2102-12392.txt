Closed Form Equations for Triangular Numbers Multiple of Other Triangular Numbers
Vladimir PLETSER

Abstract
. Triangular numbers that are multiple of other triangular numbers are investigated. It is known that for any positive non-square integer multiplier, there is an infinity of multiples of triangular numbers which are triangular numbers. If the multiplier is a squared integer, there is either one or no solution, depending on the multiplier value. Instead of recurrent relations, we develop in this paper closed form equations to calculate directly the values of triangular numbers and their indices without the need of knowing the previous solutions. We develop the theoretical equations for four cases of ranks from 1 to 4 and we give several examples for non-square multipliers 2, 3, 5 and 8.



Keywords
: Triangular Numbers, Multiple of Triangular Numbers,
Closed Form Equations

Recently, Pletser showed (

(2)

\displaystyle t_{n}

\displaystyle=2\left(\kappa+1\right)t_{n-r}-t_{n-2r}+\kappa

\displaystyle=2\left(\kappa+1\right)\xi_{n-r}-\xi_{n-2r}+\kappa

\displaystyle T_{t_{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(4\left(\kappa+1\right)^{2}-2\right)T_{t_{n-r}}-T_{t_{n-2r}}+\left(T_{\kappa}-\gamma\right)

2.1.
General equations

The associated closed form equation is the sum of the homogeneous

The homogeneous characteristic equation associated to (

reads successively

(6)

\displaystyle x^{2r}-2\left(\kappa+1\right)x^{r}+1

\displaystyle=0

(7)

\displaystyle\left(x^{r}-\alpha^{r}\right)\left(x^{r}-\beta^{r}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\kappa+1\right)+\sqrt{\kappa\left(\kappa+2\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

(9)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\kappa+1\right)-\sqrt{\kappa\left(\kappa+2\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

with the obvious relation

distinct characteristic roots,
including real and complex roots and, for odd

one has

\displaystyle x^{r}-\alpha^{r}

\displaystyle=\left(x-\alpha\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2\rho}\alpha^{j}x^{2\rho-j}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=\left(x-\alpha\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\rho}\left[x-\alpha\left(\cos\left(\frac{2j\pi}{r}\right)\pm i\sin\left(\frac{2j\pi}{r}\right)\right)\right]

and for even

r=2\rho

\displaystyle x^{r}-\alpha^{r}

\displaystyle=\left(x-\alpha\right)\left(x+\alpha\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\rho-1}\alpha^{2j}x^{2\left(\rho-j-1\right)}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle=\left(x-\alpha\right)\left(x+\alpha\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\rho-1}\left[x-\alpha\left(\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{\rho}\right)\pm i\sin\left(\frac{j\pi}{\rho}\right)\right)\right]

and similar relations for
\beta
replacing

. Recall that
complex characteristic roots introduce the element of periodicity.
The homogeneous solution is therefore respectively (
12
)
for odd
r
,
r=2\rho+1
and (

) for even
r
,

(12)

\displaystyle t_{h_{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(A+\sum_{j=1}^{\rho}\left[A_{j}^{\prime}\cos\left(\frac{2nj\pi}{r}\right)+A_{j}^{\prime\prime}\sin\left(\frac{2nj\pi}{r}\right)\right]\right)\alpha^{n}

\displaystyle+\left(B+\sum_{j=1}^{\rho}\left[B_{j}^{\prime}\cos\left(\frac{2nj\pi}{r}\right)+B_{j}^{\prime\prime}\sin\left(\frac{2nj\pi}{r}\right)\right]\right)\beta^{n}

\displaystyle t_{h_{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(A+\left(-1\right)^{n}A^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{\rho-1}\left[A_{j}^{\prime\prime}\cos\left(\frac{nj\pi}{\rho}\right)+A_{j}^{\prime\prime\prime}\sin\left(\frac{nj\pi}{\rho}\right)\right]\right)\alpha^{n}

The particular solution

3.
Conclusions

We have shown that for triangular numbers multiple of other triangular
numbers, closed form equations can be developed instead of recurrent
relations, to calculate directly the values of triangular numbers
and their indices without knowing the whole preceding sequence of
values. We developed the theoretical equations for four cases of ranks
from 1 to 4 and we applied them in several examples for the non-square
multipliers 2, 3, 5 and 8.

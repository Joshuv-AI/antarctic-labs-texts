Diagram Calculus for the Affine Temperley–Lieb Algebra of Type D D
Riccardo Biagioli, Giuliana Fatabbi, Elisa Sasso

Abstract
Let ( W , S ) (W,S) be a Coxeter system of affine type D ~ \widetilde{D} , and let TL ⁡ ( W ) \mathrm{TL}(W) the corresponding generalized Temperley-Lieb algebra. In this extended abstract we define an infinite dimensional associative algebra made of decorated diagrams which is isomorphic to TL ⁡ ( W ) \mathrm{TL}(W) . Moreover, we describe an explicit basis for such an algebra of diagrams which is in bijective correspondence with the classical monomial basis of TL ⁡ ( W ) \mathrm{TL}(W) , indexed by the fully commutative elements of W W .



1
Introduction

The Temperley-Lieb algebra is a very classical mathematical object studied in algebra, combinatorics, statistical mechanics and mathematical physics, introduced by Temperley and Lieb in 1971
[
14
]
. Thanks to Kauffman
[
12
]
and Penrose
[
13
]
, it was showed that the Temperley-Lieb algebra can be realized as a diagram algebra, that is an associative algebra with a basis given by certain diagrams on the plane. On the other hand, Jones presented the Temperley-Lieb algebra in terms of abstract generators and relations. In
[
11
]
, he also showed that this algebra occurs naturally as a quotient of the Hecke algebra of type
A
. The realization of the Temperley-Lieb algebra as a Hecke algebra quotient was generalized by Graham in
[
6
]
. He defined the so-called generalized Temperley Lieb algebra TL(
\Gamma
) for any Coxeter system of type
\Gamma
and showed that TL(
\Gamma
) has a monomial basis indexed by the fully commutative elements of the underlying Coxeter group.

\widetilde{D}_{n+2}
.

During the years, diagrammatic representations for
\mathrm{TL}(\Gamma)
have been found for each Coxeter system of finite type but only for two systems of affine type. More precisely, in
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
7
]
Green defined a diagram calculus in finite Coxeter types
B
,
D
,
E
and
H
. For affine types, in
[
5
]
Fan and Green provided a realization of TL(
\widetilde{A}
) as a diagram algebra on a cylinder and, more recently, in
[
3
,
4
]
Ernst represented TL(
\widetilde{C}
) as an algebra of decorated diagrams. In this extended abstract, we present a new diagrammatic representation for
\mathrm{TL}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
. Our method can be extended also to the affine case
\widetilde{B}
. Here we recall the presentation of TL(
\widetilde{D}_{n+2}
) given by Green in
[
9
]
that we consider as definition: TL(
\widetilde{D}_{n+2}
) is the
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-algebra generated by
\{b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n+2}\}
with defining relations:

(d1)

b_{i}^{2}=\delta b_{i}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,n+2\}
,

(d2)

b_{i}b_{j}=b_{j}b_{i}
if
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are not adjacent nodes in the Coxeter graph of type
\widetilde{D}_{n+2}
;

(d3)

b_{i}b_{j}b_{i}=b_{i}
if
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are adjacent nodes in the Coxeter graph of type
\widetilde{D}_{n+2}
.

Similarly to Ernst in
[
3
,
4
]
, we define the diagrams for our representation starting from the classical ones of type
A
and adding decorations on the edges.

Then, as usual, we define a product of decorated diagrams by concatenation. This operation turns this set into an infinite dimensional
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-algebra, of which we consider a quotient modulo some new relations. Within this quotient, we consider a specific
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-subalgebra called
algebra of admissible diagrams
and denoted by
\mathbb{D}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
. The main result of this extended abstract states that
\mathbb{D}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
and
\mathrm{TL}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
are isomorphic
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-algebras.

2
Fully commutative elements of Coxeter groups

Let
M
be a square symmetric matrix indexed by a finite set
S
, satisfying
m_{ss}=1
and, for
s\neq t
,
m_{st}=m_{ts}\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}
. The
Coxeter group

W
associated with the
Coxeter matrix

M
is defined by generators
S
and relations
(st)^{m_{st}}=1
if
m_{st}<\infty
. These relations can be rewritten more explicitly as
s^{2}=1
for all
s
, and

\underbrace{sts\cdots}_{m_{st}}=\underbrace{tst\cdots}_{m_{st}},

where
m_{st}<\infty
, the latter being called
braid relations
. When
m_{st}=2
, they are simply
commutation relations

st=ts
.
For
w\in W
, the
length
of
w
, denoted by
\ell(w)
, is the minimum length
l
of an expression
s_{1}\cdots s_{l}
of
w
with
s_{i}\in S
. The expressions of length
\ell(w)
are called
reduced
.

Definition 2.1

An element
w\in W
is fully commutative (FC) if any reduced expression of
w
can be obtained from any other reduced expression of
w
using only commutation relations.

The concept of heap helps in studying problems related to full commutativity, for more details see for instance
[
2
]
. Briefly, given a reduced expression of
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}\in W
, its
heap
is a poset on the index set
\{1,\ldots,k\}
together with a labeling map. Heaps can be represented via Hasse diagrams; moreover, if
w\in\operatorname{FC}
, its heap does not depend on its reduced expression.
Fully commutative elements heaps of type
\widetilde{D}_{n+2}
have been classified in
[
2
, §3.2]
: they can be split in five disjoint families, depending on the shapes of their associated heaps, whose elements are respectively called Alternating elements (ALT), Left-Peaks (LP), Right-Peaks (RP), Left-Right-Peaks (LRP) and Pseudo-Zigzags (PZZ), see Figure
2
.

Decorated diagrams

A
concrete pseudo k-diagram
consists of a finite number of disjoint plane curves, called
edges
, embedded in a box having
k
nodes on the top (
north
) face and
k
nodes on the bottom (
south
) face. The nodes are endpoints of edges and all other embedded edges must be closed (isotopic to circles) and disjoint from the box. We refer to a closed edge as a
loop
. It follows that there cannot exist isolated nodes and from each node a single edge starts. By
\{a,b\}
we mean an edge that joins the node
a
to the node
b
.

Figure 3:
Two equivalent concrete pseudo 7-diagrams.

We say that two concrete pseudo
k
-diagrams are
(isotopically) equivalent
if one can be obtained from the other by isotopically deforming the edges such that any intermediate diagram is also a concrete pseudo
k
-diagram (Figure
3
). We define a
pseudo k-diagram
as an equivalence class of concrete pseudo
k
-diagrams with respect to isotopically equivalence. Given two of these diagrams
D,D^{\prime}
, we define the product
D^{\prime}D
as the pseudo
k
-diagram obtained by placing
D^{\prime}
on top of
D
so that node
i
of
D
coincides with node
i^{\prime}
of
D^{\prime}
and then rescaling.

Now let
D
be a concrete pseudo
k
-diagram. Consider the set
\Omega=\{\bullet,\circ\}
and the monoid
\Omega^{*}
. Our goal is to adorn the edges of
D
with elements of
\Omega
which we call
decorations
. In particular,
\bullet
is called a
L-decoration
and
\circ
is called
R-decoration
. We call a
LR
-
decorated pseudo k-diagram
a pseudo
k
-diagram decorated with these decorations up to certain rules that we do not list here. We denote the set of LR-decorated pseudo
k
-diagrams by
T^{LR}_{k}(\Omega)
and define
\mathcal{P}^{LR}_{k}(\Omega)
to be the
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-module having the elements of
T^{LR}_{k}(\Omega)
as a basis.

As before, we define multiplication in
\mathcal{P}^{LR}_{k}(\Omega)
by concatenating two basis elements and then extend it bilinearly, see the first equality in Figure
4
.

We can show that this product gives a structure of
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-algebra to

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{LR}_{k}(\Omega)

4
Cut and paste operation

In this section we present the major combinatorial technique we used to prove the faithfulness of the diagrammatic representation we just defined.

An edge on the north face
e
of
D\in\mathtt{ad}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
joining two consecutive edges is called a
simple edge
if either (a)
e
is undecorated, or (b)
e=\{1,2\}
and it is decorated by a single
\bullet
, or (c)
e=\{n+1,n+2\}
and it is decorated by a single
\circ
.

We consider a subset of simple edges, called
suitable
edges, and to each of those, we assign a
neighbor
edge which often is a
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\circ}
or leaves one of the nodes adjacent to the suitable edge. Now we can introduce the following procedure, called
cut and paste operation
, an example is in Figure
8
, right.

Figure 8:
Cut and paste operation.

Definition 4.1
(Cut and paste operation)

Let
D
be an admissible diagram with a suitable edge
e=\{i,i+1\}
.

(cp1)

Delete the simple edge
e
.

(cp2)

Cut the neighbor of
e
and join the two free endpoints of the cut edge to the nodes
i
and
i+1
as to obtain two new non intersecting edges or the edge
\{i,i+1\}
, see Figure
8
, left.

(cp3)

If the neighbor edge of
e
was decorated, then distribute its decorations on the new edges in an admissible way.

The importance of this procedure relies in the possibility of finding a unique diagram
D^{\prime}
from another diagram
D
that factorizes as
D=D_{i}D^{\prime}
with the property that
D^{\prime}
has length equal to
\ell(D)-1
. Thanks to this inductive argument, we can prove the results stated in the next section. Moreover, this procedure provides an algorithm to factorize an admissible diagram into a product of simple diagrams.

5
Main results

Let
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
be a reduced expression of
w\in\operatorname{FC}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
and define
D_{w}:=D_{i_{1}}D_{i_{2}}\cdots D_{i_{k}}
. Note that
D_{w}
does not depend on the chosen reduced expression of
w
since
w\in\operatorname{FC}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
. Define the

\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-algebra homomorphism

\tilde{\theta}_{D}:\mathrm{TL}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})\qquad\mbox{ such that }\qquad\tilde{\theta}_{D}(b_{i})=D_{i}

for all
i=0,\ldots,n+2
.
Clearly
\tilde{\theta}_{D}
is surjective and maps the monomial basis element
b_{w}:=b_{i_{1}}b_{i_{2}}\cdots b_{i_{k}}
into the diagram
D_{w}
. Our goal is to show that this map is actually an algebra isomorphism. The proof is based on five steps that are summarized in the next theorem. Point 4) is by induction on the length of an admissible diagram and it uses the cut and paste algorithm.

Theorem 5.1

1.

Let
w
be a FC element of a certain type, then the image of the basis element
b_{w}
is a diagram of the analogous type and vice versa (for instance,
w
has a heap of type (ALT) if and only if
\tilde{\theta}_{D}(b_{w})=D_{w}
is an ALT-diagram, and so on).

2.

The lengths of
w
and
D_{w}
are equal.

3.

Every admissible diagram
D
is of the form
D_{w}
where
w\in\operatorname{FC}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
.

4.

The admissible diagrams form a
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-algebra that coincides with
\mathbb{D}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
. Moreover, the set of admissible diagrams is a basis for
\mathbb{D}(\widetilde{D}_{n+2})
.

5.

The map
\tilde{\theta}_{D}
is an algebra isomorphism.

Abstract
. A vertex transitive graph Γ \Gamma is said to be 2 2 -distance transitive if for each vertex u u , the group of automorphisms of Γ \Gamma fixing the vertex u u acts transitively on the set of vertices at distance 1 1 and 2 2 from u u , while Γ \Gamma is said to be 2 2 -arc transitive if its automorphism group is transitive on the set of 2 2 -arcs. Then 2 2 -arc transitive graphs are 2 2 -distance transitive. The classification of 2 2 -arc transitive Cayley graphs on dihedral groups was given by Du, Malnič and Marušič in [Classification of 2-arc-transitive dihedrants, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 98 (2008), 1349–1372]. In this paper, it is shown that a connected 2-distance transitive Cayley graph on the dihedral group of order 2 ​ n 2n is either 2 2 -arc transitive, or isomorphic to the complete multipartite graph 𝖪 m ⁡ [ b ] \mathsf{K}_{m[b]} for some m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 and b ≥ 2 b\geq 2 with m ​ b = 2 ​ n mb=2n .

Introduction

In this paper, all graphs are finite, simple, connected and undirected.
For a graph
\mathrm{\Gamma}
, we use
V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
,
E(\mathrm{\Gamma})
,
A(\mathrm{\Gamma})
and
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
to denote its vertex set, edge set, arc set and automorphism group, respectively.
If
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
is transitive on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
,
E(\mathrm{\Gamma})
or
A(\mathrm{\Gamma})
, then
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is said to be
vertex transitive
,
edge transitive
or
arc transitive
, respectively.
Let
u
and
v
be two distinct vertices of
\mathrm{\Gamma}
.
The
distance
between
u
and
v
in
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is the smallest length of paths between
u
and
v
, denoted by
d_{\mathrm{\Gamma}}(u,v)
, while the
diameter
of
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is the maximal value of the distance of all vertex pairs, denoted by
\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
.
For a positive integer
i
, denote by
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{i}(u)
the set of vertices at distance
i
with vertex
u
in
\mathrm{\Gamma}
.
In particular,
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{1}(u)
is simply denoted by
\mathrm{\Gamma}(u)
.
Let
\mathrm{\Gamma}
be a graph admitting a vertex transitive group
G
of automorphisms of
\mathrm{\Gamma}
.
Then
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is said to be
(G,s)
-distance transitive
, if for each vertex
u\in V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
, the stabilizer
G_{u}
is transitive on
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{i}(u)
for all
i\leq s
.
If
s=\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
, then
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is said to be
G
-distance transitive
.
When
G=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
, a
(G,s)
-distance transitive graph or
G
-distance transitive graph will be simply called an
s
-distance transitive graph
or a
distance transitive graph
, respectively.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
S
be a subset of
G\setminus\{1\}
such that
S=S^{-1}
and
G=\langle S\rangle
. The
Cayley graph
on
G
with respect to

is defined to be the graph with vertex set

and edge set
\{\{g,sg\}\mid g\in G,s\in S\}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
.
It is widely known that a graph
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is a Cayley graph on
G
if and only if
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
has a regular subgroup isomorphic to
G
, see
[
2
, Lemma 16.3]
. Given
g\in G
, the right multiplication
R(g):x\mapsto xg
,
x\in G
, is an automorphism of
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
, and
R(G):=\{R(g)\ |\ g\in G\}
is a regular group of automorphisms of
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
, that is, the image of
G
under its right regular representation. A Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is said to be
normal
if
R(G)
is a normal subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cay}(G,S))
. Furthermore, a Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is called a
dihedrant
if
G
is a dihedral group.

The investigation of distance-transitive graphs is one in which the theoretical developments in algebraic graph theory can be traced back to the 1980s. Many researchers have contributed significantly to this field and have achieved important research results. For a comprehensive overview, refer to the book
[
5
]
and the references therein.
Notably, all distance-transitive graphs are
2
-distance transitive, but the converse is not true.
In fact, an infinite family of normal Cayley graphs on the extraspecial
p
-group of order
p^{3}
and exponent
p
with
p
an odd prime was constructed in
[
18
]
. These graphs are
2
-distance transitive but are neither distance transitive nor
2
-arc transitive.
The extensive study of
2
-distance transitive graphs has gained momentum in recent years, as evidenced by works such as those cited in
[
6
,
8
,
18
,
21
,
22
]
.
This paper aims to contribute to this evolving topic.

Let
u,v
and

be three distinct vertices of a graph
\mathrm{\Gamma}
. The triple
(u,v,w)
is called a
2
-arc
if

is adjacent to both

and

, and
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is said to be
2
-arc transitive
if
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
is transitive on

and on the set of
2
-arcs of
\mathrm{\Gamma}
.
It is important to note that all
2
-arc transitive graphs are
2
-distance transitive, but the reverse is not necessarily true.
For example, the complete multipartite graphs
\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
with
m\geq 3
parts, each containing
b\geq 2
vertices, serve as instances where the graphs are
2
-distance transitive but not
2
-arc transitive.
There has been a significant body of work on the
2
-arc transitivity of graphs in the literature, see
[
1
,
14
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
for some examples.
When studying
2
-distance transitive graphs, particular interest lies in graphs that are not
2
-arc transitive.

The symmetry of Cayley graphs has attracted significant attention from researchers, with one noteworthy contribution being the completion of the classification of
2
-distance transitive Cayley graphs on cyclic groups. This work is documented in
[
1
,
6
]
.
Subsequently, a natural problem arises: the classification of
2
-distance transitive Cayley graphs on dihedral groups. There have been fascinating classification results on dihedrants under special symmetries. For instance, the classification of
2
-arc transitive dihedrants was presented across a series of papers
[
14
,
28
,
29
]
, and the classification of distance transitive or locally primitive dihedrants was detailed in
[
30
]
and
[
32
]
, respectively.
Furthermore, the classifications of arc transitive or edge transitive Cayley graphs on the dihedral group
\mathrm{D}_{2p^{n}}
where

is a prime, were conducted in
[
24
]
and
[
39
]
, respectively. The classifications of quasiprimitive or biquasiprimitive edge-transitive dihedrants were also provided in
[

Recently, Jin and Tan explored

-distance transitive dihedrants of girth
3

Conjecture
.

A connected
2
-distance transitive dihedrant either is known
2
-arc transitive dihedrant, or is isomorphic to
\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
for some
m\geq 3
and
b\geq 2
, or
\mathrm{G}(2,p,(p-1)/4)
for a prime
p

.

In this conjecture,
\mathrm{G}(2,p,(p-1)/4)
represents a family of arc transitive graphs of order

that were constructed in
[
7

. However, it can be demonstrated that
\mathrm{G}(2,p,(p-1)/4)
is not
2
-distance transitive. This fact can be verified by examining certain graphs with small values of

In the paper, the conjecture mentioned is confirmed by excluding the inclusion of

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathrm{\Gamma}
be a connected Cayley graph on the dihedral group
\mathrm{D}_{2n}
of order
2n
with
n\geq 2
. Then
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
2
-distance transitive if and only if either
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is a
2
-arc transitive graph, or
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
for some integers
m\geq 3
and
b\geq 2
with
mb=2n
.

Remark.
(1) By Theorem
1.1
, the classification of
2
-distance transitive dihedrants depends on the classification of
2
-arc transitive dihedrants. The study of
2
-arc transitive dihedrants was initiated by Marušič in
[

-arc-transitive dihedrants was established. Subsequently, the classification of

-arc transitive dihedrants was completed in

[

]

-arc transitive dihedrants that were not covered in

]
.

(2) A
2
-arc
(u,v,w)
of a graph
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is termed a
2
-geodesic
if

and
w
are not adjacent, and an arc transitive graph
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is said to be
2
-geodesic transitive

is transitive on the set of

-geodesic transitive graphs are

As a consequence of Theorem

, a classification of connected

A transitive permutation group is called

if each of its nontrivial normal subgroups has at most two orbits and at least one has exactly two orbits. A transitive permutation group

\Omega

such that

and

Proposition 2.4

Each biquasiprimitive permutation group containing a regular dihedral subgroup is biprimitive.

It is well-known that
\mathrm{GL}(d,q)
has a cyclic group of order
q^{d}-1

of
\mathrm{GL}(d,q)
, which induces an cyclic group of order

of

Lemma 2.5
.

(1)

Let
\langle x\rangle
be a Singer cycle of
\mathrm{PGL}(d,q)
with
d\geq 3
, and let
\langle x\rangle<G<\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(d,q)
with
|G:\langle x\rangle|=2
. Then
G
cannot be cyclic or dihedral.

(2)

Let
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q).o\leq\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,q)
with
q=r^{f}\equiv 3\pmod{4}
for a prime
r
, and
o\leq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{f}
does not contain the diagonal automorphism of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)
. Then every dihedral group of order
q+1
in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q).o
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)

Proof.
Note that
\langle x\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{(q^{d}-1)/(q-1)}
. Since
|G:\langle x\rangle|=2
, we have
\langle x\rangle\trianglelefteq G
, and so
G\leq N_{\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(d,q)}(\langle x\rangle)
.
By
[
19
, P. 187, 7.3 Satz]
,
N_{\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(d,q)}(\langle x\rangle)=\langle x\rangle:\langle\delta\rangle
with
\langle\delta\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
and
x^{\delta}=x^{q}
.
Assume that
G
is cyclic or dihedral.
Then there is an integer
0\leq i<d
such that
x^{\delta^{i}}=x^{q^{i}}=x^{\pm 1}
, that is,
x^{q^{i\pm 1}}=1
.
This implies that the order of
x
is a divisor of
q^{i}\pm 1
, that is,
(q^{d}-1)/(q-1)=q^{d-1}+\cdots+q+1
divides
q^{i}\pm 1
, which leads to

and

, contradicting the assumption

. This completes the proof of part (1).

To prove part (2), let
L\leq\mathrm{PSL}(2,q).o
with

.
Suppose that

.
Since
o\leq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{f}
does not contain the diagonal automorphism of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)
, we have that

is cyclic. Note that

and

. This implies
L\cap\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)\not=1
, and since
L
is dihedral and
q+1\geq 8
(

), we have
|L:L\cap\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)|=2
, forcing
2\mid|o|
.
Thus,
f
is even as
|o|\mid f
. It follows that
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
from

In the subsequent lemma, we provide a concise proof based on an elementary group result. The equivalence of

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathrm{\Gamma}=\mathsf{K}_{n,n}
,
\mathrm{B}(H_{11})
or
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}(H_{11})
, and let

. Then
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
(X,2)
-distance transitive if and only if
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
(X,2)
-arc transitive.

Proof.
To complete the proof, it is sufficient to demonstrate the necessity, which entails proving that
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
(X,2)
-arc transitive given that
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
(X,2)
-distance transitive. Let
(u,v,w)
be a
2
-arc of
\mathrm{\Gamma}
. As
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is bipartite, it follows that
v\in\mathrm{\Gamma}(u)
and

. Moreover, since
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
(X,2)
-distance transitive,

exhibits transitivity on both
\mathrm{\Gamma}(u)
and
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{2}(u)
.

Assume
\mathrm{\Gamma}=\mathsf{K}_{n,n}
. Then

.
By utilizing the orbit-stabilizer theorem
[
12
, Theorem 1.4A]
, we find that

(n,n-1)=1

|X_{u}:X_{uvw}|=|X_{u}:X_{uv}|\cdot|X_{u}:X_{uw}|

.
Furthermore, as
|X_{uv}X_{uw}|=|X_{uv}||X_{uw}|/|X_{uv}\cap X_{uw}|=|X_{uv}||X_{uw}|/|X_{uvw}|

\mathrm{\Gamma}_{2}(u)

is transitive on
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{2}(u)
. Since

is arc transitive,

2
-arc transitive.

Assume

or
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}(H_{11})
.
In these cases, we have

[

must have a subgroup of index

has no subgroup of index

.

\mathrm{\Gamma}
has girth

and valency

divides

. It follows that

Now let

\mathrm{\Gamma}

.

Let
\mathrm{\Gamma}

of

. Then

\mathsf{K}_{r+1,r+1}-(r+1)\mathsf{K}_{2}

Let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
be the bipartite sets of
\mathrm{\Gamma}
with

\Box

Now we are ready to demonstrate the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Certainly, a connected
2
-arc transitive graph is also
2
-distance transitive. Let
m\geq 3
and
b\geq 2
be integers such that
mb=2n
. It can be straightforwardly demonstrated that
\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
is a
2
-distance transitive but not
2
-arc transitive dihedrant (see
[
30
, P. 186]
). Thus, the sufficiency of Theorem
1.1
is established.

It is important to note that all
2
-arc transitive dihedrants were classified in
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
.
To establish the necessity of Theorem
1.1
, let
\mathrm{\Gamma}=\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
be a connected
2
-distance transitive but not
2
-arc transitive Cayley graph, where
G=\langle x,y\mid x^{n}=y^{2}=1,x^{y}=x^{-1}\rangle\cong\mathrm{D}_{2n}
for some integer
n\geq 2
. To complete the proof, it is sufficient to demonstrate that
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
for some integers
m\geq 3
and
b\geq 2
satisfying
mb=2n
.

Let
A=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{\Gamma})
.
If
A
is quasiprimitive on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
, then according to Proposition
2.3
,
A
must be
2
-transitive on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
. This implies
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{2n}
, which contradicts the fact that
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is not
2
-arc transitive.
If
A
is biquasiprimitive on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
, by Lemma
4.3
, it would imply that
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
2
-arc transitive, leading to a contradiction.
Therefore, we assume that
A
is neither quasiprimitive nor biquasiprimitive. This implies that
A
possesses a nontrivial maximal normal subgroup
N
with at least three orbits, and any normal subgroup of
A
containing
N
properly has either one or two orbits on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
. Consequently,
A/N
is either quasiprimitive or biquasiprimitive on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma}_{N})
.

If
A/N
is biquasiprimitive on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma}_{N})
, then Lemma
4.3
implies that
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{n,n}-n\mathsf{K}_{2}
for some
n\geq 4
, or
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
(A,2)
-arc transitive. In both scenarios,
\mathrm{\Gamma}
would be
2
-arc transitive, leading to a contradiction. Hence, we can assume that
A/N
is quasiprimitive on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma}_{N})
.
By Proposition
2.9
, either
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
with
m\geq 3
and
b\geq 2
, or
N
acts semiregularly on
V(\mathrm{\Gamma})
and
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is a cover of
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{N}
.
For the former, we are done, and so we may assume that the latter occurs, that is,
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is a cover of
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{N}
. By Proposition
2.10
,
A/N\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{\Gamma}_{N})
,
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{N}
is
(A/N,2)
-distance transitive, but not
(A/N,2)
-arc transitive.
By Lemma
4.2
, we reach the conclusion that either
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is
(A,2)
-arc transitive, or
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
with
m\geq 3
and
b\geq 2
, or
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{p,p}-p\mathsf{K}_{2}
for a prime
p\geq 5
.
It follows that
\mathrm{\Gamma}\cong\mathsf{K}_{m[b]}
since
\mathrm{\Gamma}
is not
2
-arc transitive, as desired.
\Box

Absolute Minima of Potentials of a Certain Class of Spherical Designs


Abstract
We use linear programming techniques to find points of absolute minimum over the unit sphere S d S^{d} in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} of the total potential of a point configuration ω N ⊂ S d \omega_{N}\subset S^{d} which is a spherical ( 2 ​ m − 1 ) (2m-1) -design contained in the union of some m m parallel hyperplanes. The interaction between points is described by the kernel K ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) = f ⁡ ( | 𝐱 − 𝐲 | 2 ) K({\bf x},{\bf y})=f(\left|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\right|^{2}) , where | ⋅ | \left|\ \!\cdot\ \!\right| is the Euclidean norm in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} . The potential function f f is assumed to have a convex derivative f ( 2 ​ m − 2 ) f^{(2m-2)} . Points of minimum do not depend on f f and are those and only those which form exactly m m distinct dot products with points of ω N \omega_{N} . The proof of this theorem was presented at a workshop at ESI in January 2022. Using this result, we find sets of universal minima of certain six configurations on higher-dimensional spheres.

Introduction and setting of the problem

Let
S^{d}:=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{d+1}^{2}=1\}
denote the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

The problem of finding absolute minimum points for the total potential over
S^{d}
of a point configuration (regular in some sense) was earlier studied in a number of works. In particular, papers
[
28
,
29
,
25
,
26

focused on a regular
N
-gon on

and a regular simplex, regular cross-polytope, and cube on

We use a general approach to characterizing the absolute minima of the potential by first finding the Hermite interpolating polynomial for the potential function and then applying properties of Gegenbauer polynomials. This is the classical Delsarte-Yudin (or linear programming) method, see the work by Cohn and Kumar
[
14
]
, book
[
9
]
, and references therein.

One important application of this problem is the maximal polarization problem on the sphere, which requires maximizing the absolute minimum over
S^{d}
of the total potential of
N
points lying on
S^{d}
. When the solution to the maximal polarization problem is expected to be a regular configuration, knowing the minimum value of its potential over the sphere would provide a sharp upper bound to prove. Reviews of known results on polarization can be found, for example, in book
[
9

with most recent work reviewed in
[
4

Find the quantity

We recall that the

(4)

\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}
), the set
\overline{\omega}_{2d+2}
is
2
-stiff. By Proposition
7.2
, we then have
{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,d
. Since
d+1
is even and

\sum_{i=0}^{d}{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}={\bf y}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{d}{\bf x}_{i}={\bf y}\cdot{\bf 0}=0,

exactly half of dot products
{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}
equal
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
and the other half equals
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
. Let
I\subset\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
be the
\frac{d+1}{2}
-element subset such that
{\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
,
i\in I
. Pick some index
m\in I
and some index
k\in J:=\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\setminus I
. Then for every
i\in I\setminus\{m\}
, we have
{\bf y}\cdot({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{m})=0
and, for every
i\in J\setminus\{k\}
, we have
{\bf y}\cdot({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{k})=0
. Let us show that the set

T:=\{{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{m}\}_{i\in I\setminus\{m\}}\cup\{{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{k}\}_{i\in J\setminus\{k\}}

of
d-1
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(orthogonal to
{\bf y}
) is linearly independent. Let
c_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\setminus\{m,k\}
be any numbers such that

\sum\limits_{i\in I\setminus\{m\}}c_{i}({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{m})+\sum\limits_{i\in J\setminus\{k\}}c_{i}({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{k})={\bf 0}.

Then

\sum\limits_{i=0\atop i\neq m,k}^{d}c_{i}({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{m})-\left(\sum\limits_{i\in J\setminus\{k\}}c_{i}\right)({\bf x}_{k}-{\bf x}_{m})={\bf 0}.

(20)

Since
{\bf x}_{0},\ldots,{\bf x}_{d}
form a non-degenerate simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the set of vectors
{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{m}
, where
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\setminus\{m\}
is linearly independent. Then (
20
) implies that
c_{i}=0
,
i\neq m,k
; that is,
T
is linearly independent. Thus,
{\bf y}
appears to be orthogonal to the
(d-1)
-dimensional subspace spanned by
T
. Then
{\bf y}
belongs to the intersection of the one-dimensional subspace
T^{\bot}
with
S^{d-1}
; that is there exist only two (opposite) vectors that
{\bf y}
can equal to.

Let
{\bf u}:=\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{d+1}\sum_{j\in I}{\bf x}_{j}
. Then
{\bf u}\in S^{d-1}\cap\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}
and equation (
18
) implies that, for every

, we have
{\bf u}\cdot({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{m})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}=0
. Equation (
19
) implies that, for every
i\in J\setminus\{k\}
, we have
{\bf u}\cdot({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{k})=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}=0
. Thus,
{\bf u}\in T^{\bot}\cap S^{d-1}
. This implies that
{\bf y}={\bf u}
or
{\bf y}=-{\bf u}
. Since
\sum_{i=0}^{d}{\bf x}_{i}={\bf 0}
, we have
-{\bf u}=\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{d+1}\sum_{j\in J}{\bf x}_{j}\in\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}
. Thus,
{\bf y}\in\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}
.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

For every
d\geq 3
odd, the configuration
\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}\subset S^{d-1}
is a spherical
3
-design.

Proof.

To prove the lemma, we will use Theorem
7.1
.
Since
\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}
is centrally symmetric and Gegenbauer polynomials
P_{n}^{(d-1)}
,
n=1,3
, are odd, we have

M_{n}^{(d-1)}(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}):=\sum\limits_{{\bf x}\in\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}}\sum\limits_{{\bf y}\in\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}}P_{n}^{(d-1)}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y})=0

(21)

for
n=1,3
. It remains to show equality (
21
) for
n=2
.
Let
{\bf x}_{0},{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{d}
be the vertices of the regular simplex in the definition of the configuration
\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}
in (
17
). Let
{\bf x}_{I}:=\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{d+1}\sum_{i\in I}{\bf x}_{i}
, where
I
is any
\ell:=\frac{d+1}{2}
-element subset of
\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
. Let
0\leq k\leq\ell
be any integer and
J\subset\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
be any
\ell
-element subset whose intersection with
I
has exactly
k
elements. There are
\binom{\ell}{k}\binom{\ell}{\ell-k}=\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}
such sets
J
and, for every such
J
,

\begin{split}{\bf x}_{I}\cdot{\bf x}_{J}&=\frac{4d}{(d+1)^{2}}\left(\sum_{i\in I\cap J}{\bf x}_{i}+\sum_{i\in I\setminus J}{\bf x}_{i}\right)\cdot\sum_{j\in J}{\bf x}_{j}\\
&=\frac{d}{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{i\in I\cap J}\left(1-\frac{\ell-1}{d}\right)+\sum_{i\in I\setminus J}\left(-\frac{\ell}{d}\right)\right)=\frac{2k}{\ell}-1,\end{split}

k=0,1,\ldots,\ell
. Then

\begin{split}M_{2}^{(d-1)}&(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)})=\kappa(d)\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}P_{2}^{(d-1)}\left(\frac{2k}{\ell}-1\right)\\
&=\frac{d\kappa(d)}{d-1}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}\left(\left(\frac{2k}{\ell}-1\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\\
&=\frac{d\kappa(d)}{d-1}\left(\frac{4}{\ell^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}k^{2}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}-\frac{4}{\ell}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}k\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}+\frac{d-1}{d}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(22)

Using the standard formula
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}=\binom{2\ell}{\ell}
(which can be seen by counting the total number of subsets
J
above), we obtain that

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}k^{2}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\ell}\ell^{2}\binom{\ell-1}{k-1}^{2}=\ell^{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{\ell-1}{k}^{2}=\ell^{2}\binom{2\ell-2}{\ell-1}.

Finally, from (
21
) we obtain that

\begin{split}&0=\frac{M_{1}^{(d-1)}(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)})}{\kappa(d)}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}P_{1}^{(d-1)}\left(\frac{2k}{\ell}-1\right)\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}\left(\frac{2k}{\ell}-1\right)=\frac{2}{\ell}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}k\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}-\binom{2\ell}{\ell}.\end{split}

Then
\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}k\binom{\ell}{k}^{2}=\frac{\ell}{2}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}
and from (
22
) we obtain

M_{2}^{(d-1)}(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)})=\frac{d\kappa(d)}{d-1}\left(4\binom{2\ell-2}{\ell-1}-2\binom{2\ell}{\ell}+\frac{2\ell-2}{2\ell-1}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}\right)=0.

Thus,
\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}
is a spherical
3
-design.
∎

Lemma 8.4
.

For every
d\geq 3
odd,
\mathcal{D}_{2}(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)})=\overline{\omega}_{2d+2}
.

Proof.

Relations (
18
) and (
19
) imply that
\overline{\omega}_{2d+2}\subset\mathcal{D}_{2}(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)})
.
Let
{\bf x}
be any element of
\mathcal{D}_{2}(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)})
and
{\bf x}_{0},{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{d}
be the vertices of the regular simplex in the definition of the configuration

in (

Let

(d+1)/2
-element subset of the set

. Then relations (

) and (

\begin{split}\theta:=\left(\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{d+1}\sum\limits_{i\in I}{\bf x}_{i}\right)\cdot{\bf x}&=\left(\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{d+1}\sum\limits_{i\in I}{\bf x}_{i}\right)\cdot\left(\sum\limits_{j\in I}\alpha_{j}{\bf x}_{j}+\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\alpha_{j}{\bf x}_{j}\right)\\

The set
\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}

3
-design by Lemma
8.3

\mathcal{D}_{2}(\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)})\neq\emptyset

\overline{\omega}_{2d+2}

\overline{\omega}_{\kappa(d)}

2
-stiff. Then in view of Proposition
7.2
, the above dot product is

Since

\begin{split}\left|{\bf x}\right|^{2}&=\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}{\bf x}_{i}\right)^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}=\left(1+\frac{1}{d}\right)\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}\right)^{2}\\

Choose arbitrary indices
j\neq k

(d+1)/2
-element subset such that

-1

or

. Thus, for some index

. For every index

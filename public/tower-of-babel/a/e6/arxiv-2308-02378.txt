On a Dowker-type problem for convex disks with almost constant curvature Thanks: Partially supported by the National Research, Development and Innovation Office, NKFI, K-147544 grant
Bushra Basit, Zsolt Lángi

Abstract
. A classical result of Dowker (Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 50: 120-122, 1944) states that for any plane convex body K K , the areas of the maximum (resp. minimum) area convex n n -gons inscribed (resp. circumscribed) in K K is a concave (resp. convex) sequence. It is known that this theorem remains true if we replace area by perimeter, or convex n n -gons by disk- n n -gons, obtained as the intersection of n n closed Euclidean unit disks. It has been proved recently that if C C is the unit disk of a normed plane, then the same properties hold for the area of C C - n n -gons circumscribed about a C C -convex disk K K and for the perimeters of C C - n n -gons inscribed or circumscribed about a C C -convex disk K K , but for a typical origin-symmetric convex disk C C with respect to Hausdorff distance, there is a C C -convex disk K K such that the sequence of the areas of the maximum area C C - n n -gons inscribed in K K is not concave. The aim of this paper is to investigate this question if we replace the topology induced by Hausdorff distance with a topology induced by the surface area measure of the boundary of C C .

For any

Note that by the results in

Our second result is the following.

Theorem 2

A typical element

Definition 5

Let

(1)

d_{PM}(C,D)=\\

.

We note that by its definition, within the class

Proposition 1
.

Let
\{C_{n}\}
be a sequence of convex disks in
\mathcal{K}_{o}
and let

. If

with respect to the topology induced by

with respect to the topology induced by

. Furthermore, there is a sequence

of convex disks in
\mathcal{K}_{o}
and a convex disk

such that

with respect to

, but

Proof.

Assume that

with respect to

, and let
\varepsilon>0
be a sufficiently small fixed value. Then, if

is sufficiently large, for any Borel set
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we have

|A_{C_{n}}(\sigma)-A_{C}(\sigma)|\leq\varepsilon\cdot\mathcal{H}(\sigma)\leq 2\pi\varepsilon.

In particular, we obtain that if

is sufficiently large, then
|\perim(C_{n})-\perim(C)|\leq 2\pi\varepsilon
, implying that for some
0<r<R
, each

and

contains

and is contained in

. Thus, by

, we have that if

is sufficiently small, then, for some translate

of

, we have

for all sufficiently large values of

are

with respect to Hausdorff distance.

Now, let

, and
D_{n}=\conv(K\cup\{p_{n},-p_{n}\})
, where

. Then, clearly,

with respect to Hausdorff distance. On the other hand, setting

Let

Proof of Theorem

Let

Remark 3
.

By our conditions about the curvatures at the corresponding points of
\bd(K)
and
\bd(C)
, it is easy to see that for any points

, the set

.

Let us parametrize
\bd(K)
and

as the curves
\Gamma:[0,\perim(K)]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Theta:[0,\perim(C)]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

and

in positive orientation. We choose the starting points
\Gamma(0)
and
\Theta(0)
in such a way that here
\bd(K)
and
\bd(C)
have the same outer unit normal.
For any two points
p=\Gamma(s_{1}),q=\Gamma(s_{2})
, let us denote the arc
\widehat{pq}
of
\Gamma

\Gamma|_{[s_{1},s_{2}]}
, and
set
A(s_{1},s_{2})=\area(r_{K}(\Gamma(s_{1}),\Gamma(s_{2})))
.

Our main tool will be the following. First, we compute the mixed partial derivative
(\partial_{s}\partial_{t}A)(\bar{s},\bar{t})
for any points

, where
\widehat{pq}
has turning angle strictly less than
\pi
.
We note that our formula for

will be valid for any

, not only for

.
In the second part, we show that if

, then this derivative is nonnegative, and apply Lemma
1
.

3.1.
Deriving a formula for the mixed partial derivative
(\partial_{s}\partial_{t}A)(\bar{s},\bar{t})

Without loss of generality, let
p=\Theta(\bar{S}),q=\Theta(\bar{T})
, where we assume that
0<\bar{s}<\bar{t}<\perim(K)
and
0<\bar{S}<\bar{T}<\perim(C)
.

Note that by Remark
3
, for any
0<s<t<\perim(K)
, there are unique values
0<S^{\prime}<T^{\prime}<\perim(C)
with the property that
\Theta(T^{\prime})-\Theta(S^{\prime})=\Gamma(t)-\Gamma(s)
. Thus, if we set
H=\{(s,t):0<s<t<\perim(K)\}
, we may define the functions
S,T:H\to\mathbb{R}
to identically satisfy
\Theta(T(s,t))-\Theta(S(s,t))=\Gamma(t)-\Gamma(s)
. Note that
\bar{S}=S(\bar{s},\bar{t})
and
\bar{T}=T(\bar{s},\bar{t})
. More specifically, consider the function
f(s,t,S^{\prime},T^{\prime})=\Theta(S^{\prime})-\Theta(T^{\prime})+\Gamma(s)-\Gamma(t)
. This is a twice continuously differentiable function in a neighborhood of
(\bar{s},\bar{t},\bar{S},\bar{T})
, and satisfies
f(\bar{s},\bar{t},\bar{S},\bar{T})=0
. Its Jacobian is the
2\times 4
matrix
[\Gamma^{\prime}(s)\quad-\Gamma^{\prime}(t)|-\Theta^{\prime}(S^{\prime})\quad\Theta^{\prime}(T^{\prime})]
. Since
\Theta^{\prime}(\bar{S})
and
\Theta^{\prime}(\bar{T})

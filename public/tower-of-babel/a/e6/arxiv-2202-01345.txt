Fluctuation scaling limits for positive recurrent jumping-in diffusions with large jumps


Abstract
For positive recurrent jumping-in diffusions with large jumps, we study scaling limits of the fluctuations of inverse local times and occupation times. We generalize the eigenfunctions with modified Neumann boundary condition, which have been introduced in the previous study for small jumps, to a class of diffusions of stronger singularity.

Introduction

Let us consider a strong Markov process
X
on the half line
[0,\infty)
(resp. the real line
\mathbb{R}
) which has continuous paths up to the first hitting time of
0
and, as soon as
X
hits
0
,
X
jumps into the interior
(0,\infty)
(resp.
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
) and starts afresh. We call such a process
X
a
unilateral
(resp.
bilateral
)
jumping-in diffusion
.

As we will see in Section
3
, unilateral jumping-in diffusions are specified by two Radon measures on
(0,\infty)
, the speed measure
m
which characterize the diffusive behavior on
(0,\infty)
and the jumping-in measure
j
which do the jumps from the origin.
Let
X
be a positive recurrent unilateral jumping-in diffusion with large jumps, that is,
\int_{0}^{\infty}xj(dx)=\infty
.
Positive recurrence ensures existence of the following limit of the inverse local time
\eta
at
0
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\eta(t)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{P}b\quad(b\in[0,\infty)).

(1.1)

Our main aim is to study the
fluctuation scaling limit
of
\eta
:

\displaystyle f(\gamma)\left(\frac{\eta(\gamma t)}{\gamma}-bt\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{d}B(\kappa t)\quad\mathrm{on}\ \mathbb{D}

(1.2)

for some scaling function
f
, a constant
\kappa>0
and a standard Brownian motion
B
, where
\mathbb{D}
denotes the space of càdlàg paths from
[0,\infty)
to
\mathbb{R}
equipped with Skorokhod’s
J_{1}
-topology.
When
\eta
is of finite variance, this scaling limit can be easily obtained by the classical central limit theorem with
f(\gamma)=\gamma^{1/2}
. Hence our main concern is the case of infinite variance.

In the previous paper
[
8
]
, the author has studied positive recurrent jumping-in diffusions with small jumps:
\int_{0}^{\infty}xj(dx)<\infty
.
One of the main results in
[
8
]
was to show the fluctuation scaling limit (
1.2
) when the limit process is an
\alpha
-stable process
S_{\alpha}
(
1<\alpha<2
) without negative jumps, that is,

\displaystyle f(\gamma)\left(\frac{\eta(\gamma t)}{\gamma}-bt\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{d}S_{\alpha}(t)\quad\mathrm{on}\ \mathbb{D}.

(1.3)

The main tool was the Krein-Kotani correspondence, which gives a one-to-one and bi-continuous correspondence between a class of speed measures and a class of Herglotz functions. To apply the correspondence, it was necessary to assume

\displaystyle\int_{0+}m(x,1)^{2}dx<\infty

(1.4)

for the speed measure
m
of jumping-in diffusions. Under (
1.4
), we may define the eigenfunction for
\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d}{dx}
with
modified Neumann boundary condition
at
0
and the Laplace exponent of
\eta
may be represented by the eigenfunctions and the Herglotz function corresponding to
m
. The fluctuation scaling limit (
1.3
) was proven by showing a continuity of them w.r.t.
m
and
j
.
In the present paper, we allow exit boundaries with stronger singularity, including those that do not satisfy (
1.4
), and construct eigenfunctions subject to more singular boundary conditions.

As an application of (
1.2
), we consider the fluctuation scaling limit of the occupation time of a bilateral jumping-in diffusion
X
:
A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1_{(0,\infty)}(X_{s})ds
. In the positive recurrent case where
A(t)
has a degenerate mean:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}A(t)\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{P}p\in(0,1),

(1.5)

we establish the fluctuation scaling limits of the form:

\displaystyle f(\gamma)\left(\frac{A(\gamma t)}{\gamma}-pt\right)\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{f.d.}B(\kappa t)

(1.6)

for some constant
\kappa
and a function
f(\gamma)
in (
1.2
). Here
\xrightarrow{f.d.}
denotes the convergence of finite-dimensional distributions.

1.1
Main results

According to some results of Feller
[
2
]
and Itô
[
4
]
, under the natural scale, a unilateral jumping-in diffusion can be characterized by its speed measure
m
and its jumping-in measure
j
, both of which are Radon measures on
(0,\infty)
. The jumping-in diffusion is a strong Markov process on
[0,\infty)
which behaves as a
\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}
-diffusion during staying in
(0,\infty)
and jumps from the origin to
(0,\infty)
according to
j
, where
\frac{d^{+}}{dx}
denotes the right-differentiation operator. In Section
3
, we will give the precise description. We denote the jumping-in diffusion by
X_{m,j}
and its inverse local time at
0
by
\eta_{m,j}
.

We introduce conditions on
m
and
j
for our main results.
Let
K
and
L
be functions from
(0,\infty)
to
\mathbb{R}
which vary slowly at
\infty
. Consider the following conditions for
\alpha>1
and
\beta>0
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{(M)}_{\alpha,K}

\displaystyle:\ m(x,\infty)\sim(\alpha-1)^{-1}x^{1/\alpha-1}K(x)\quad(x\to\infty),

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{(J)}_{\beta,L}

\displaystyle:\ j(x,\infty)\sim x^{-\beta}L(x)\quad(x\to\infty),

(1.8)

where
f(x)\sim g(x)\ (x\to\infty)
means
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/g(x)=1
.
Note that the case
\beta\leq 1
corresponds to the large jump case
\int_{0}^{\infty}xj(dx)=\infty
and the case
\beta>1
does to the small jump case
\int_{0}^{\infty}xj(dx)<\infty
.

In this paper, we show the fluctuation scaling limit (
1.2
) under
\mathrm{(M)}_{\alpha,K}
and
\mathrm{(J)}_{\beta,L}
with
\alpha\geq 2
and
\alpha\beta=2
.
These conditions obviously implies
\beta\leq 1
and thus the process
X_{m,j}
has large jumps:
\int_{0}^{\infty}xj(dx)=\infty
.
The reason why we only consider such
\alpha
and
\beta
is as follows: the case
1<\alpha<2
and
\beta>1
(small jump case) has already been treated in
[
8
]
(we will review in more detail in Section
1.4
) and the case
\alpha>2
and
\alpha\beta>2
is easy because the variance of
\eta_{m,j}
is finite, and we can appeal to the classical central limit theorem.

Now we explain our main results.
Let
d(m)
denote the constant of singularity of
m
near
0
, which we will introduce in (
1.39
) and let
n_{m,j}
denote an excursion measure of
X_{m,j}
away from
0
, whose precise form is in (
3.4
).
A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of
X_{m,j}
will be given in Section
3
.
The following Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
are one of our main results, which gives the fluctuation scaling limit of the inverse local time
\eta_{m,j}
under
\mathrm{(M)}_{\alpha,K}
and
\mathrm{(J)}_{\beta,L}
with
\alpha\geq 2
and
\alpha\beta=2
in the case of
\alpha
is integer or not, respectively.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in Section
4
and that of Theorem
1.4
will be given in Appendix
A
.
Examples for these theorems will be given in Section
5
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>2
be not an integer and assume
X_{m,j}
exists. Suppose the following holds

(i)

d(m)<\infty
.

(ii)

\mathrm{(M)}_{\alpha,K}
holds.

(iii)

\mathrm{(J)}_{2/\alpha,L}
holds.

Define

\displaystyle N(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{\gamma}j(dx)\int_{0}^{x}dy\int_{y}^{\gamma}dm(z)\int_{0}^{z}m(w,\infty)dw.

(1.9)

Let
u
and
v
be slowly varying functions at
\infty
such that

\displaystyle u(\gamma)^{2}v(\gamma)\sim N(\gamma)\quad(\gamma\to\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{K(\gamma)}{u(\gamma)}=\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{L(\gamma)}{v(\gamma)}=0.

(1.10)

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma^{1/2}u(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\left(\eta_{m,j}\left(\frac{\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)-b\frac{\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{d}B(2t)\quad\mathrm{on}\ \mathbb{D},

(1.11)

and

\displaystyle n_{m,j}[T_{0}>s]=o(s^{-2}U^{\sharp}(s^{2})^{-1}v(s^{\alpha}U^{\sharp}(s^{2})^{\alpha/2}))\quad(s\to\infty),

(1.12)

where
b:=\int_{0}^{\infty}j(dx)\int_{0}^{x}m(y,\infty)dy
,
U^{\sharp}
is a de Bruijn conjugate of
U(s)=u(s^{\alpha/2})
(see e.g.,
[
1
, p.29]
for the definition of de Bruijn conjugate) and
T_{0}
is the lifetime of an excursion path (see Section
3
).

Remark 1.2
.

When
\alpha
is not an integer, we always have the functions
u
and
v
satisfying (
1.10
).
As we will show in Proposition
4.1
, it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{K(\gamma)^{2}L(\gamma)}{N(\gamma)}=0.

(1.13)

Thus, for example, the following
u
and
v
satisfy the desired condition:

\displaystyle u(\gamma)=S(\gamma)^{p/2}K(\gamma),\quad v(\gamma)=S(\gamma)^{1-p}L(\gamma)

(1.14)

for
S(\gamma):=N(\gamma)/K(\gamma)^{2}L(\gamma)
and
p\in(0,1)
.

Remark 1.3
.

The limit result (
1.11
) is equivalent to the following form which is consistent with (
1.2
):

\displaystyle f(\gamma)\left(\frac{\eta_{m,j}(\gamma t)}{\gamma}-b\right)\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{d}B(2\kappa t)\quad\mathrm{on}\ \mathbb{D}

(1.15)

with

\displaystyle f(\gamma)=\frac{1}{u(\gamma^{\alpha/2}w^{\sharp}(\gamma)^{\alpha/2})}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{w^{\sharp}(\gamma)}},

(1.16)

where
w^{\sharp}
is a de Bruijn conjugate of
w(\gamma)=v(\gamma)^{-1}
.

In the case where
\alpha
is integer, we need some additional assumptions.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq 2
be an integer and assume
X_{m,j}
exists.
Suppose the conditions (i) - (iii) in Theorem
1.1
holds.
Let
u
and

be slowly varying functions at
\infty
satisfying (

)
for

in (

).
Assume the following:

(iv)

.

In the case of

.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma^{1/2}u(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\left(\eta_{m,j}\left(\frac{\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)-b\frac{\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{d}B(2t)\quad\mathrm{on}\ \mathbb{D},

and

\displaystyle n_{m,j}[T_{0}>s]=o(s^{-2}U^{\sharp}(s^{2})^{-1}v(s^{\alpha}U^{\sharp}(s^{2})^{\alpha/2}))\quad(s\to\infty),

1.2
Scaling limits of occupation times of bilateral jumping-in diffusions

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we establish the fluctuation scaling limit of the form (
1.6
) for the occupation times on the half line of bilateral jumping-in diffusions.

As we will see in Section
3
, a bilateral jumping-in diffusion is characterized by two pairs of the speed measures and jumping-in measures
(m_{\pm},j_{\pm})
.
For a bilateral jumping-in diffusion
X=X_{m_{+},j_{+};m_{-},j_{-}}
, we denote its occupation time on the positive side by

\displaystyle A(t):=\int_{0}^{t}1_{(0,\infty)}(X_{s})ds.

(1.19)

We focus on the case where the limit ratio degenerates, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}A(t)\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{P}p\in(0,1).

(1.20)

The following two theorems give the fluctuation scaling limit of
A(t)
.
We always write
\widetilde{B}
for an independent copy of
B
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\alpha>2
be not an integer and assume
X_{m_{+},j_{+};m_{-},j_{-}}
exists.
Suppose the following holds for constants
w_{\pm}>0
and
r_{\pm}>0
:

(i)

d(m_{\pm})<\infty
.

(ii)

m_{\pm}
satisfies
\mathrm{(M)}_{\alpha,w_{\pm}K}
.

(iii)

j_{\pm}
satisfies
\mathrm{(J)}_{2/\alpha,r_{\pm}^{2}L}
.

We define
N(\gamma)
as (
1.9
) for

\displaystyle m(\gamma,\infty):=w_{+}^{-1}m_{+}(\gamma,\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad j(\gamma,\infty):=r_{+}^{-2}j_{+}(\gamma,\infty).

(1.21)

Let
u
and
v
be slowly varying functions at
\infty
such that

\displaystyle u(\gamma)^{2}v(\gamma)\sim N(\gamma)\quad(\gamma\to\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{K(\gamma)}{u(\gamma)}=\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{L(\gamma)}{v(\gamma)}=0.

(1.22)

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma^{1/2}u(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\left(A\left(\frac{\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)-\frac{p\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{f.d.}(1-p)w_{+}r_{+}B(2t)-pw_{-}r_{-}\widetilde{B}(2t),

(1.23)

where

\displaystyle b_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\pm}(dx)\int_{0}^{x}m_{\pm}(y,\infty)dy\quad\text{and}\quad p=\frac{b_{+}}{b_{+}+b_{-}}.

(1.24)

Similar to Theorem
1.4
,
we need additional assumptions when
\alpha
is an integer.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\alpha\geq 2
be an integer and assume
X_{m_{+},j_{+};m_{-},j_{-}}
exists.
Suppose the conditions (i)-(iii) in Theorem
1.5
for constants
w_{\pm}>0
and
r_{\pm}>0
holds. Let
u
and
v
be slowly varying functions at
\infty
satisfying (
1.22
) for

in Theorem
1.5
. Assume the following:

(iv)

\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{K(\gamma)^{d-\alpha+1}\int_{1}^{\gamma}\frac{K(x)^{\alpha}}{x}dx}{u(\gamma)^{d+1}}=0\quad
for some
d>0
.

When
\alpha=2
, we also assume

(v)

\limsup_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{1}{v(\gamma)}\int_{1}^{\gamma}\frac{L(x)}{x}dx<\infty
.

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma^{1/2}u(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\left(A\left(\frac{\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)-\frac{p\gamma t}{v(\gamma^{\alpha/2})}\right)\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{f.d.}(1-p)w_{+}r_{+}B(2t)-pw_{-}r_{-}\widetilde{B}(2t),

(1.25)

where

\displaystyle b_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\pm}(dx)\int_{0}^{x}m_{\pm}(y,\infty)dy\quad\text{and}\quad p=\frac{b_{+}}{b_{+}+b_{-}}.

(1.26)

could be given by reducing the fluctuation of the occupation time of a bilateral jumping-in diffusion to that of the inverse local times of unilateral jumping-in diffusions via Itô’s excursion theory.

1.3
Eigenfunctions with modified Neumann boundary condition

Our method is similar to that of
[
8
]
: we reduce the fluctuation scaling limit of
\eta_{m,j}
to continuity w.r.t.
m
and
j
.
There is, however, a significant difference.
As we have already mentioned in Section
1
, in
[
8
]
the assumption (
1.4
) was indispensable.
In the present paper, in order to treat speed measures which not necessarily satisfy (
1.4
), we introduce a class of speed measures and a notion of convergence for the class in Definition
3.2
.

We explain our method more precisely.
Let
\chi_{m,j}
denote the Laplace exponent of
\eta_{m,j}
, that is,
E[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\eta_{m,j}(1)}]=\mathrm{e}^{-\chi_{m,j}(\lambda)}
. It can be represented, as we will see in Section
3
, as

\displaystyle\chi_{m,j}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-g_{m}(\lambda;x))j(dx),

(1.27)

where
u=g_{m}
is the unique solution to the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}u=\lambda u,\quad u(0)=1,\quad u\text{ is non-increasing}.

(1.28)

To show the convergence (
1.2
), it is enough to prove that of Laplace exponents of Lévy processes:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{m,j,\gamma}(t):=f(\gamma)\left(\frac{\eta_{m,j}(\gamma t)}{\gamma}-bt\right)\quad(\gamma>0)

(1.29)

as
\gamma\to\infty
. By changing variables, the Laplace exponent
\widetilde{\chi}_{m,j,\gamma}
of
\widetilde{\eta}_{m,j,\gamma}
may be represented by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}_{m,j,\gamma}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-g_{m}\left(\frac{f(\gamma)}{\gamma}\lambda;x\right)\right)j(dx)-bf(\gamma)\lambda

(1.30)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-g_{m_{\gamma}}(\lambda;x))j_{\gamma}(dx)-b_{\gamma}\lambda

(1.31)

\displaystyle=\chi_{m_{\gamma},j_{\gamma}}(\lambda)-b_{\gamma}\lambda

(1.32)

for appropriate Radon measures
m_{\gamma}
and
j_{\gamma}
and a constant
b_{\gamma}
.
Therefore our problem is reduced to the continuity of the Laplace exponent
\chi_{m,j}
with respect to
m
and
j
.

To analyze the function
g_{m}
, we use eigenfunctions of initial value problems.
When the boundary
0
for
dm
is regular, we have a unique solution
u=\psi_{m}(\lambda;\cdot)
to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}u=\lambda u,\quad u(0)=0,\quad u^{+}(0)=1

(1.33)

and a unique solution
u=\varphi_{m}(\lambda;\cdot)
to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}u=\lambda u,\quad u(0)=1,\quad u^{+}(0)=0,

(1.34)

where
u^{+}
denotes a right-derivative of
u
.
When the boundary
0
for
dm
is exit, we still have
\psi_{m}
but do not
\varphi_{m}
. We would like to introduce a counterpart for
\varphi_{m}
.

In the unilateral case, the speed measure
m
comes from a string
m
, i.e.,
m:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-decreasing, right-continuous function:
m(a,b]=m(b)-m(a)
.
In
[
8
]
, for a string
m
with

\displaystyle\int_{0+}m(x)^{2}dx<\infty,

(1.35)

we introduced the eigenfunction
u=\varphi^{1}_{m}(\lambda;\cdot)
of the differential equation
\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}u=\lambda u\ (\lambda>0)
with the
modified Neumann boundary condition
at
0
:

\displaystyle u(0)=1,\quad\lim_{x\to+0}(u^{+}(x)-\lambda(m(x)-m(1)))=0.

(1.36)

When (
1.35
) does not hold, the solution for (
1.36
) does not exist.
Hence we introduce more general boundary condition at
0
and construct the solution for it.

For a string
m
, we define

\displaystyle G^{1}_{m}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}(m(y)-m(1))dy

(1.37)

and inductively define for
k\geq 2

\displaystyle G^{k}_{m}(x)=-\int_{0}^{x}dy\int_{y}^{1}G^{k-1}_{m}(z)dm(z).

(1.38)

Note that
(-1)^{k}G^{k}
is non-negative on
[0,1]
.
We define

\displaystyle d(m)=\inf\left\{k\geq 1\;\middle|\;\int_{0}^{1}(-1)^{k}G^{k}_{m}(x)dm(x)<\infty\right\},

(1.39)

where we interpret
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
We also note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(-1)^{k}G^{k}_{m}(x)dm(x)\begin{cases}=\infty&k<d(m),\\
<\infty&k\geq d(m).\end{cases}

(1.40)

Roughly speaking, the value
d(m)
is larger when a string
m
diverges faster at
0
.
For a string
m
with
d(m)<\infty
and an integer
d\geq d(m)
, we can prove the existence of an eigenfunction
u=\varphi^{d}_{m}(\lambda;x)\ (\lambda>0)
with the
modified Neumann boundary condition
at
0
defined as follows:

Definition 1.7
.

Let
m
be a string with
d(m)<\infty
,
d\geq d(m)\vee 1
and
\lambda>0
.
We say that
u
is the
\lambda
-eigenfunction with the modified Neumann boundary condition at
0
of order
d
when it holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}u=\lambda u,\quad u(0)=1

(1.41)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+0}\left(u^{+}(x)-\left(\lambda m(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\lambda^{k}\int_{x}^{1}G^{k}_{m}(y)dm(y)\right)\right)=0.

(1.42)

We denote the function
u
by
\varphi^{d}_{m}
.

We will construct
\varphi^{d}_{m}
in Section
2
.
Then for a suitable constant
c^{d}_{m}(\lambda)
, we have the following expression

\displaystyle g_{m}(\lambda;x)=\varphi^{d}_{m}(\lambda;x)-c^{d}_{m}(\lambda)\psi_{m}(\lambda;x),

(1.43)

and we may exploit
g_{m}
through
\varphi^{d}_{m}
and
\psi_{m}
.

1.4
Previous studies

Here we recall several previous studied related to our main results.

Previous study on scaling limits for diffusions

In order to study the inverse local times and occupation times for diffusions (without any jumps at all), we may assume without loss of generality that diffusions have a natural scale; whose local generators are of the form
\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}
with a
speed measure

m
.

Kasahara and Watanabe
[
6
]
has shown the scaling limit of inverse local times for unilateral diffusions.

Theorem 1.8

(Kasahara and Watanabe
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
)
.

Let
m
be a finite Radon measure on
[0,\infty)
with full support and let
\eta
be the inverse local time at
0
of
\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}
-diffusion on
[0,\infty)
.
Define

\displaystyle K(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{\gamma}m(x,\infty)^{2}dx

(1.44)

and assume
K
varies slowly at
\infty
as
\gamma\to\infty
.
Then we have

\displaystyle f(\gamma)\left(\frac{\eta(\gamma t)}{\gamma}-bt\right)\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{d}B(2t)\quad\text{on}\ \mathbb{D},

(1.45)

where

\displaystyle f(\gamma)=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{K(\gamma)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\ b=m[0,\infty).

(1.46)

Applying this result, they showed the scaling limit of the occupation time for bilateral diffusions (without any jumps at all).

Theorem 1.9

(Kasahara and Watanabe
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
)
.

Let
m
be a finite Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}
with full support and let
X
be a
\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}
-diffusion.
Define the occupation time on the positive side:

\displaystyle A(t):=\int_{0}^{t}1_{(0,\infty)}(X_{s})ds.

(1.47)

Assume the following holds:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\gamma}m(x,\infty)^{2}dx\sim\sigma_{+}^{2}K(\gamma),\quad\int_{0}^{\gamma}m(-\infty,-x)^{2}dx\sim\sigma_{-}^{2}K(\gamma)\quad(\gamma\to\infty).

Their idea of the proof was following. For a string
m
with

(1.50)

i.e.,
\int_{0}^{\delta}m(x)^{2}dx<\infty

they constructed the inverse local time

at
0
of a unilateral
\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}
-diffusion in a generalized sense, and showed a kind of continuity of

w.r.t.
m
.
Then they reduced the scaling limit to the continuity.

[

has studied scaling limits of jumping-in diffusions, whose one of the main results is the following:

Theorem 1.10

(Yano
[

)

Let

and
0<\beta<1/\alpha
.
Assume
\mathrm{(M)}_{\alpha,K}
and
\mathrm{(J)}_{\beta,L}
holds.
Then (with some technical conditions) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}X_{m,j}(\gamma^{1/\alpha}K(\gamma)t)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma^{1/\alpha}K(\gamma)}\eta_{m,j}\left(\frac{\gamma^{\beta}t}{L(\gamma)}\right)

\alpha\beta

\alpha\beta

This result says under

3
Convergence of inverse local times

Here we briefly review the construction of sample paths of jumping-in diffusions.
Let us assume a strong Markov process
X
on
[0,\infty)
has continuous paths and natural scale on
(0,\infty)
and, as soon as
X
hits the origin, it jumps into
(0,\infty)
.
Then there exist Radon measures
m
on
(0,\infty)
with full support and
j
on
(0,\infty)
and such a process has the local generator
\mathcal{L}
of the form

\mathcal{L}u(x)=\frac{d}{dm}\frac{d^{+}}{dx}u(x)\quad\text{for}\ x\in(0,\infty)

(3.1)

subject to Feller’s boundary condition (see e.g., Feller
[
2
]
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(u(x)-u(0))j(dx)=0.

(3.2)

Conversely, we can construct such processes from
(m,j)
with

\text{(C)}\left\{\begin{aligned} &(i)\ j(1,\infty)+\int_{0}^{1}xj(dx)+\int_{0}^{1}|G_{m}(x)|j(dx)<\infty,\\
&(ii)\ j(0,1)=\infty\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

(see Itô
[
4
]
and Rogers
[
7
]
).

Sample paths of a unilateral jumping-in diffusion is constructed from
m
and
j
via Itô’s excursion theory as follows: Let
m
and
j
be Radon measures on
(0,\infty)
with the condition (C).
Define
T_{0}(e)=\inf\{s>0\mid e(s)=0\}\ (e\in\mathbb{D})
and
\mathbb{E}
as the set of all elements
e
in
\mathbb{D}
which satisfy that
e(u)=0
for every
u\geq T_{0}(e)
if
T_{0}(e)<\infty
.
Let
P^{m}_{x}\ (x\in(0,\infty))
be the law of the diffusion process with speed measure
dm
starting from
x
and killed at
0
.
Then the following measure
n_{m,j}
is the excursion measure of the process
X_{m,j}
:

\displaystyle n_{m,j}(A)=\int_{0}^{\infty}P^{m}_{x}(A)j(dx)\quad(A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{E})).

(3.4)

We construct the sample paths of
X_{m,j}
.
We define
N(dsde)
as a Poisson random measure on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{E}
having intensity measure
dx\otimes n_{m,j}
being defined on some probability space. Here
dx
is the Lebesgue measure. We define
D(p)=\{s\in(0,\infty)\mid N(\{s\}\times\mathbb{E})=1\}
and the map
p:D(p)\to\mathbb{E}

such that
p[s]\ (s\in D(p))
is the only one element of the support of the measure
N(\{s\}\times de)
.
We define the process
\eta_{m,j}
as follows:

\displaystyle\eta_{m,j}(u)=\int_{(0,u]\times\mathbb{E}}T_{0}(e)N(dsde).

(3.5)

Then we construct
X_{m,j}
as follows:

X_{m,j}(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &p[u](t-\eta_{m,j}(u-))&&\text{if}\ u\in D(p)\ \text{and}\ \eta_{m,j}(u-)\leq t<\eta_{m,j}(u),\\
&0&&\text{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

Then
\eta_{m,j}
plays the role of the inverse local time at
0
of
X_{m,j}
.
This process
X_{m,j}
thus constructed is the unilateral jumping-in diffusion associated to
m
and
j
and started from
0
. We denote the law of
X_{m,j}
by
P
.

We explain a construction of a bilateral jumping-in diffusion.
Let
m_{+},m_{-}
and
j_{+},j_{-}
be Radon measures on
(0,\infty)
and suppose
(m_{+},j_{+})
and
(m_{-},j_{-})
satisfy (C). Then we construct the bilateral jumping-in diffusion processes
X_{m_{+},j_{+};m_{-},j_{-}}
i.e., a Markov process on
\mathbb{R}
which behaves like
X_{m_{+},j_{+}}
while
X
is positive and like
-X_{m_{-},j_{-}}
while
X
is negative and as soon as the process hits the origin it jumps into
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
according to
j_{+}
and
j_{-}
.
The precise definition is as follows.
Take two independent Poisson random measures
N_{+}
and
\widetilde{N}_{-}
defined on a common probability space whose intensity measures are
n_{m_{+},j_{+}}
and
n_{m_{-},j_{-}}
, respectively.
Define
N_{m_{-},j_{-}}(dsde)=\widetilde{N}_{m_{-},j_{-}}(dsd(-e))
. Then we can define
X_{m_{+},j_{+};m_{-},j_{-}}
from the excursion point process

Proof.

We may assume

\displaystyle N(\gamma)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\gamma}j(x,\gamma)dy\int_{y}^{\gamma}G_{m}(z)dm(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

Let us consider a unilateral jumping-in diffusion
X
whose local generator
L
on
(0,\infty)
is

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+b(x)\frac{d}{dx}\right).

(5.1)

We assume
b(x)
can be represented as

\displaystyle b(x)=\frac{\delta-1+\epsilon(x)}{x}+\eta(x)\quad(x>0)

(5.2)

for some
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and some measurable functions
\epsilon
and
\eta
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\epsilon(x)=0,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_{1}^{x}\eta(y)dy=\overline{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.3)

When
b(x)=(\delta-1)/x
, that is, when
\epsilon(x)=\eta(x)=0\ (x>0)
, the local generator
L
is equal to that of Bessel process of dimension
\delta
. Thus, we call jumping-in diffusions with a drift of the form (
5.2
)
jumping-in diffusions with a Bessel-like drift
. In Kasahara and Kotani
[
5
]
, they have studied a one-dimensional diffusion (without jumps) with the same form of drift and have shown the relation between the asymptotic behavior of the drift coefficient and that of the speed measure.

Define for an arbitrary fixed
x_{0}>0
,

\displaystyle W(x):=\exp\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{x}b(y)dy\right)=x^{\delta-1}c(x)\exp\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{\epsilon(y)}{y}dy\right)

(5.4)

with
c(x)=\exp\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\eta(y)dy
.
Let us assume
\delta<0
.
For the generator
L
in (
5.1
), we define a corresponding string
\widetilde{m}
and its scale function
\widetilde{s}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}(x)=2\int_{1}^{x}W(y)dy,\quad\widetilde{s}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dy}{W(y)},

(5.5)

and then it follows that

\displaystyle A=\frac{d}{d\widetilde{m}}\frac{d}{d\widetilde{s}}.

(5.6)

Under the natural scale, the speed measure
m
is given by
m(x):=\widetilde{m}(\widetilde{s}^{-1}(x))
.

To apply our main results, we need to check when the tail of
m
varies regularly at
\infty
.
Since the function
W
of the form (
5.4
) varies regularly at
\infty
with exponent
\delta-1
(see e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 1.3.1]
),
we set

\displaystyle\ell(x):=c(x)\exp\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{\epsilon(y)}{y}dy\right).

(5.7)

Then
W(x)=x^{\delta-1}\ell(x)
and
\ell
is a slowly varying function at
\infty
.
Note that since it holds that
\widetilde{m}^{\prime}(x)=2W(x)
and
\widetilde{s}^{\prime}(x)=1/W(x)
, we have

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(x)=\widetilde{m}^{\prime}(\widetilde{s}^{-1}(x))(\widetilde{s}^{-1}(x))^{\prime}=2W(\widetilde{s}^{-1}(x))^{2}.

(5.8)

From Karamata’s theorem, the function
\widetilde{s}(x)
varies regularly at
\infty
with exponent
2-\delta
and therefore
\widetilde{s}^{-1}(x)
varies regularly at
\infty
with exponent
1/(2-\delta)
(see e.g.,
[
1
, Proposition 1.5.15]
).
Then from
[
1
, Proposition 1.5.14]
and some calculation, we have

\displaystyle m(x,\infty)\sim(\alpha-1)^{-1}x^{1/\alpha-1}K(x)\quad(x\to\infty)

(5.9)

for
\alpha:=1-2/\delta
and a slowly varying function

at
\infty

.
Here

Hence

satisfies
\mathrm{(M)}_{\alpha,K}

To apply Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
, we need to take appropriate slowly varying functions

and
v
satisfying (

To obtain the asymptotic behavior of

, we consider a specific example.

(5.11)

Let

\displaystyle j(dx):=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{1}{x^{a+1}}\wedge\frac{(\log x)^{t-1}}{x^{2/\alpha+1}}\right)dx

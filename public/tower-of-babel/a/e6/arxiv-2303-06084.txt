Some Rigorous Results on the Lévy Spin Glass Model
Wei-Kuo Chen, Heejune Kim, Arnab Sen

Abstract
We study the Lévy spin glass model, a fully connected model on N N vertices with heavy-tailed interactions governed by a power law distribution of order 0 < α < 2 . 0<\alpha<2. Our investigation is divided into three cases 0 < α < 1 0<\alpha<1 , α = 1 \alpha=1 , and 1 < α < 2 . 1<\alpha<2. When 1 < α < 2 , 1<\alpha<2, we identify a high temperature regime, in which the limit and fluctuation of the free energy are explicitly obtained and the site and bond overlaps are shown to exhibit concentration, interestingly, while the former is concentrated around zero, the latter obeys a positivity behavior. At any temperature, we further establish the existence of the limiting free energy and derive a variational formula analogous to Panchenko’s framework in the setting of the Poissonian Viana-Bray model. For α = 1 \alpha=1 , the free energy scales super-linearly and converges to a constant proportional to β \beta in probability at any temperature. In the case of 0 < α < 1 0<\alpha<1 , the scaling for the free energy is again super-linear, however, it converges weakly to the sum of a Poisson Point Process at any temperature. Additionally, we show that the Gibbs measure puts most of its mass on the configurations that align with signs of the polynomially many heaviest edge weights.

Introduction

The study of spin glasses has drawn attention from both physicists and mathematicians for decades, in particular, a lot of efforts have been made to understand the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick mean-field model
[
46
]
as well as the Edwards-Anderson short-range model
[
24
]
. Whereas the investigation on the former has achieved tremendous success (see, e.g.,
[
37
,
40
,
48
,
49
]
), the latter was scarcely understood due to its rigid geometry until recent years, see
[
15
]
and the references therein. In the hope of bridging the gap between the mean-field model and realistic models of short-range interactions, diluted variants of the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model have been introduced, such as the spin glass model on a Bethe lattice, the Viana-Bray model, and the diluted
p
-spin models (see
[
32
,
36
,
51
]
) that possess a bounded average number of spin interactions at any site as opposed to the diverging connectivity in the fully-connected models. For recent progress on the diluted and related models, see
[
1
,
5
,
11
,
18
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
36
,
43
,
47
]
.

In the present paper, we aim to consider the Lévy spin glass proposed by Cizeau-Bouchaud
[
16
]
, which is a Sherrington-Kirkpatrick-type model, however, the spin interactions are formulated through a heavy-tailed distribution with a power law density
2^{-1}\alpha{|x|}^{-(1+\alpha)}
for
|x|\geq 1
with stable exponent
0<\alpha<2.
A major motivation for considering the heavy-tailed interactions stems from its relevance in the study of the Ruderman–Kittel–Kasuya–Yosida interaction in the experimental metallic spin glass
[
33
]
, see the elaboration in
[
16
]
. On the other hand, it is also an intermediate model that bridges the mean-field and the diluted models, since even though the Lévy spin glass is a fully connected model, the strong bonds have a diluted structure.
In physics literature, the investigation of the Lévy model started from
[
16
]
by means of the cavity method, and
subsequently, several papers (
[
30
,
31
,
38
]
) revisited this model with different tools, such as the replica method. Notably,
[
30
]
introduced one way to understand the Lévy model through the Viana-Bray model by adopting a cutoff to divide the interactions into two groups of strong bonds and weak bonds and then sending this cutoff to zero to restore the original problem.

Besides spin glass, symmetric matrices with heavy-tailed distribution have also been studied extensively both in physics and mathematics, see
[
7
,
9
,
17
,
50
]
and references therein. After a series of impressive works
[
2
,
3
,
13
,
14
]
, it is now known that for
1<\alpha<2
, the eigenvectors are completely delocalized. For
0<\alpha<1
, the model exhibits a phase transition similar to Anderson localization, i.e., the eigenvector
transitions from being completely delocalized to being localized as its associated eigenvalue crosses some threshold (mobility edge).

In contrast, the mathematical investigation of the Lévy model remains very scarce. The key difficulty is attributed to the fact that the power-law distribution is not additive.
As a result, many well-known techniques and tools such as the smart path method and integration by parts are no longer available in this model. To the best of our knowledge, the only rigorous result in the Lévy model is the existence of the limiting free energy established recently in
[
29
]
for
1<\alpha<2
and a special choice of the heavy-tailed distribution.

Our study for the Lévy model considers the entire domain
0<\alpha<2
and general distributions.
In the case of
1<\alpha<2,
we identify a high temperature regime through a critical temperature
\beta_{\alpha}
by the second moment method and show that in this regime, the free energy and its fluctuation can be explicitly computed. More importantly, we show that the site and bond overlaps are concentrated for different structural reasons - while the individual spins do not have favored directions, the heavy weights on the edges do influence the behavior of the spin interactions as a whole. As a result, the site overlap is shown to be concentrated around zero; on the contrary, the bond overlap is concentrated around a positive quantity. Another main theme of this paper carries out the cutoff framework proposed in
[
30
]
, based on which we obtain quantitative controls between the Lévy model and the Viana-Bray model as well as its Poissonian variant. Within this framework, we establish the superadditivity for the free energy and conclude the existence of the limiting free energy as in
[
29
]
. Furthermore, we show that the limiting free energy admits a variational representation analogous to the formulation in Panchenko’s treatment for the Poissonian Viana-Bray model, see
[
41
]
.

Interestingly, the behavior of the Lévy model is significantly different when
\alpha=1
and
0<\alpha<1.
In both cases, the free energy scales super-linearly, instead of
N
as in the case of
1<\alpha<2
. For
\alpha=1
, the scaling is slower than
N^{1+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
, and at any inverse temperature
\beta
, the normalized free energy concentrates around
\beta
in probability. For
0<\alpha<1
, the normalization in the free energy is roughly like
N^{1/\alpha}
. We show that at any temperature, the normalized free energy always converges weakly to the sum of a Poisson Point Process parametrized by the heavy tailed distribution. Furthermore, we prove that the Gibbs measure carries most of its weights on the spin configurations, for which the signs of the spin interactions agree with those of
N^{\min(1/2,1-\alpha)-}
many leading heavy weights.

We close this section by adding that there are many fundamentally important questions left unaddressed in the present work (see Section
2.3
) - our article can be viewed just as a beginning step towards the rigorous understanding of the Lévy model.

2
Main Results and Open Problems

For
0<\alpha<2,
let
X
be a symmetric random variable with tail probability

\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)=\frac{L(x)}{x^{\alpha}},\ \forall x>0,

where
L:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a slowly varying function at
\infty
, i.e., for any
t>0
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{L(tx)}{L(x)}=1.

For each
N\geq 1,
define

J=a_{N}^{-1}X,

(2.1)

where
a_{N}:=\inf\bigl\{x>0:N\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)\leq 1\bigr\}.

An important case of
X
is the (symmetric) Pareto distribution that has the density

\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{-(\alpha+1)}\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\geq 1}

so that
a_{N}=N^{1/\alpha}
and
the density of
J=N^{-1/\alpha}X
is equal to

\frac{\alpha}{2N}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\geq N^{-1/\alpha}\}}}{|x|^{\alpha+1}}dx.

(2.2)

This is the disorder for the original Lévy model considered by Cizeau-Bouchaud
[
16
]
.

For a given (inverse) temperature
\beta>0
, the Hamiltonian of the Lévy model is defined as

-H_{N}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}J_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

for
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N},
where
(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
are i.i.d. copies of
J.
Set the partition function as

Z_{N}=Z_{N}(\beta)=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{-H_{N}(\sigma)}.

Define the free energy by
\ln Z_{N}
and the Gibbs measure by
G_{N}(\sigma)=e^{-H_{N}(\sigma)}Z_{N}^{-1}
for
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}.

We denote by
\sigma,\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2},\ldots
the i.i.d. samples (replicas) from
G_{N}
and by
\langle\cdot\rangle
the Gibbs expectation with respect to these random variables. There are two important parameters naturally associated with the Lévy model. The first is the site (multiple) overlap defined as

R_{k}=R(\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{k}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma^{1}_{i}\sigma^{2}_{i}\dots\sigma^{k}_{i}

for
k\geq 1,
which measures the average similarities of the replicas over each spin site. Another parameter is the bond overlap that is concerned with the average similarities of two replicas on the edges, whose absolute disorder strengths are at least
K
, namely, for
K\geq 0,

Q_{K}=Q_{K}(\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2}):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i<j}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{ij}|\geq K\}}\sigma^{1}_{i}\sigma^{1}_{j}\sigma^{2}_{i}\sigma^{2}_{j},&\text{if $M\geq 1$},\\
0,&\text{if $M=0$}.\end{cases}

Here,
M:=\sum_{i<j}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{ij}|\geq K\}}
is
Binomial
(N(N-1)/2,\min(1,\mathbb{P}(|J|\geq K)))

, so
M
is of order
N
with high probability if
K>0
and of order
N^{2}
if
K=0
. We now state our main results in two major cases:
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
1<\alpha<2
.

2.1
Case
1<\alpha<2

2.1.1
High Temperature Behavior

We proceed to state our main results on the high-temperature behavior of the Lévy model. For
0<\alpha<2,
define

\beta_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Bigl(\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh^{2}(x)}{x^{\alpha+1}}dx\Bigr)^{-1/\alpha},

(2.3)

where this integral is finite since
|\tanh(x)|\leq\min(|x|,1).
We say that the model is in the high-temperature regime if
0<\beta<\beta_{\alpha}.
For
1<\alpha<2,
the following theorem establishes the limiting free energy inside this regime.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
1<\alpha<2
and
0<\beta<\beta_{\alpha}
. For every
0<p<\alpha,
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\ln Z_{N}-\Bigl(\ln 2+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln\cosh(\beta x)}{x^{\alpha+1}}dx\Bigr)\Bigr|^{p}=0.

Following the convergence of the free energy, we establish the fluctuation of the free energy, which holds for all
0<\alpha<2
and
0<\beta<\beta_{\alpha}.
For notational brevity, define

b_{N}=a_{N}^{-1}a_{{N\choose 2}}.

(2.4)

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
0<\alpha<2
and
0<\beta<\beta_{\alpha}.
We have that

\frac{1}{b_{N}}\Bigl(\ln Z_{N}-N\ln 2-{N\choose 2}\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\ln\cosh(\beta J)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ln\cosh(\beta J)\leq\beta b_{N}\}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\beta Y_{\alpha},

where the characteristic function of
Y_{\alpha}
is given by

t\mapsto\exp\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{itx}-1-itx\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\leq 1\}})\alpha x^{-(\alpha+1)}dx\Bigr).

For any
0<\alpha<2
, the normalizing factor
b_{N}
scales like
N^{1/\alpha}
times some slowly varying function. When
1<\alpha<2
, the centering term is linear in
N
and hence, is of order

\gg b_{N}
. For
\alpha=1
, the centering term is again of order
\gg b_{N}
. However, when
0<\alpha<1
, the order of the centering term is also
b_{N}
, so it can be absorbed in the limiting distribution and this allows us to get a distributional convergence of
b_{N}^{-1}\ln Z_{N}
.
However, as we see in Theorem
2.11
, the same convergence continues to hold, with a more illuminating description, for any temperature.

Remark 2.3
.

Y_{\alpha}
is a stable distribution with stable index
\alpha
and skewness parameter
\kappa=1
(see Equations (3.8.10) and (3.8.11) in
[
23
]
). As a result, its right tail decays polynomially as
\mathbb{P}(Y_{\alpha}>x)\sim cx^{-\alpha}
as
x\to\infty
for some positive constant
c=c(\alpha)
. When
0<\alpha<1
, the infimum of the support is finite, while if
1\leq\alpha<2
, the support of
Y_{\alpha}
is the entire
\mathbb{R}
, in which case, the left tail decays faster than a power law. See, e.g.,
[
39
, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 3.1]
.

Next, we study the behavior of the site and bond overlaps.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume
1<\alpha<2
. For any
k\geq 1,
the site overlap satisfies

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{2k}^{2}\bigr\rangle=0

(2.5)

for any
0<\beta<\beta_{\alpha}

and

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{2k}^{2}\rangle=1.

(2.6)

Furthermore, for any
0<\beta<\beta_{\alpha}
and
K>0,
the bond overlap is concentrated at a positive number,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(Q_{K}-\alpha K^{\alpha}\int_{K}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh^{2}(\beta x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}dx\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr\rangle=0.

(2.7)

We comment that the behavior of the overlap in (
2.5
) essentially says that the spins do not have favored directions on each site, whereas the positivity of the bond overlap in (
2.7
) indicates that the spins on each edge tend to align in certain directions so that
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\mbox{sign}(J_{ij})
as long as the absolute disorder exceeds the level
K.

If we do not restrict ourselves on the heavy edges, i.e., set
K=0
instead, then
Q_{K}=N(N-1)^{-1}R_{2}^{2}-(N-1)^{-1}
and from (
2.5
), this quantity vanishes under
\mathbb{E}\langle\cdot\rangle
as
N\to\infty.

From (
2.6
), it seems reasonable to expect that the site overlap should exhibit a phase transition in temperature in the sense that there exists a critical temperature that distinguishes when the site overlap is a Dirac measure at zero or has a nontrivial (symmetric) distribution. See the second open problem in Section
2.3
.

2.1.2
Superadditivity of the Free Energy

Our next result establishes the superadditivity and convergence of the free energy in the Lévy model with Pareto distributions and
1<\alpha<2.
Note that the convergence in high temperature is already apparent as seen in Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
1<\alpha<2
and consider the Pareto distribution. For any
\beta>0,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
M,N\geq 1
with
N/2\leq M\leq 2N,

\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{M+N}\geq\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{M}+\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{N}-C\phi(M+N)

for
\phi(x)=x^{1-(2-\alpha)/(1+3\alpha)}.
Furthermore,
\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{N}
exists and is the limit of
N^{-1}\ln Z_{N}
in any
L^{p}
norm for
1\leq p<\alpha
.

Our approach to this superadditivity relies on making a connection to a special case of the so-called Viana-Bray as well as its Poissonian generalization, which are diluted 2-spin models defined on the sparse Erdős-Rényi and Poisson (multi)graph, respectively. For convenience, they are called the VB and PVB models for the rest of the paper. First of all, note that for any given
\varepsilon>0,
by splitting the disorder in the Hamiltonian
H_{N}
as
J\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|<\varepsilon\}}+J\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|\geq\varepsilon\}}
for each pair and dropping the first components, as we shall prove, the resulting free energy will not deviate away from the original one too much. Now, on the one hand, since in distribution
J\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|\geq\varepsilon\}}=Bg_{\varepsilon}
for some independent
B\sim
Ber
(\varepsilon^{-\alpha}/N)
and
g_{\varepsilon}\sim 2^{-1}\alpha\varepsilon^{\alpha}|x|^{-(1+\alpha)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\geq\varepsilon\}}
and
g_{\varepsilon}
has a finite first moment, the free energy corresponding to the truncated Hamiltonian indeed equals that of the VB model, whose Hamiltonian is defined as

-H_{N,\varepsilon}^{\mbox{\tiny VB}}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}B_{ij}g_{\varepsilon,ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j},

where
(B_{ij},g_{\varepsilon,ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
are i.i.d. copies of
(B,g_{\varepsilon}).
On the other hand, since each
B_{ij}
is asymptotically equal to the Poisson random variable with mean
\varepsilon^{-\alpha}/N
, we can further argue that the free energy of the VB model is essentially the same as that of the PVB model, whose Hamiltonian is defined as

-H_{N,\varepsilon}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{k=1}^{\pi(\varepsilon^{-\alpha}N)}g_{\varepsilon,k}\sigma_{I(1,k)}\sigma_{I(2,k)},

(2.8)

where
\pi(\varepsilon^{-\alpha}N)
is Poisson with mean
\varepsilon^{-\alpha}N
,
(g_{\varepsilon,i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. copies of
g_{\varepsilon},

(I(1,k),I(2,k))_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. sampled uniformly from
\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq N\},
and these are all independent of each other. These three approximations are obtained quantitatively in terms of
\varepsilon
and
N
, based on which we prove Theorem
2.5
by using a key fact, the free energy of the PVB model is superadditive, that will be established in this paper by an adoption of the Franz-Leone type interpolation in
[
25
]
.
The existence of the limiting free energy in the Lévy model was also established in a recent work
[
29
]
relying again on the superadditivity argument, where the authors adopted the combinatorial interpolation strategy in
[
8
]
, different from our present approach.
While no characterization of the limiting free energy was given in
[
29
]
, one of the main contributions of our paper is to provide a variational formula for this limit in Theorem
2.7
below.

2.1.3
Invariance Principle and Variational Formula

In this subsection, we will establish an invariance principle and a variational representation for the limiting free energy in the case of the Pareto distribution.

Our formulation is closely connected to the framework in the PVB model established by Panchenko
[
41
]
. To begin with, denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of all measurable functions
\sigma:[-1,1]^{4}\to\{-1,1\}
. Recall the PVB model from (
2.8
). Consider the perturbed PVB Hamiltonian defined in (
7.1
) associated with the choice of the parameter (
6.3
) and denote by
(\sigma^{l})_{l\geq 1}
the replicas from the corresponding Gibbs measure. The Gibbs expectation with respect to these replicas is denoted by
\langle\cdot\rangle^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}_{\varepsilon}
. Observe that for any
N\geq 1,
the replicas
(\sigma^{l})_{l\geq 1}
enjoy two symmetries, in the sense that under
\mathbb{E}\langle\cdot\rangle^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}_{\varepsilon}
, their joint distribution is invariant under any finite permutations of the spin indices
i
and the replica indices
l.
If, along a subsequence as
N\to\infty
, their joint distribution converges to a certain infinite array
(s_{i}^{l})_{i,l\geq 1}
, then the same symmetries will still be preserved, namely,
(s_{i}^{l})_{i,l\geq 1}
is invariant with respect to any finite row or column permutations. By the Aldous-Hoover representation
[
6
,
28
]
, we can then express

s_{i}^{l}=\sigma(w,u_{l},v_{i},x_{i,l})

for all
i,l\geq 1
for some
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
the collection of all
\sigma
that arise from the Aldous-Hoover representation for any possible weak limits of the replicas. In
[
41
]
, it was shown that for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
, the corresponding spin distributions
(s_{i}^{l})_{i,l\geq 1}
satisfy an invariant principle and the free energy can be expressed as a variational formula in terms of
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
, see the detailed review in Section
7
.

Our main results below validate the analogous framework in the setting of the Lévy model, where the key ingredient in our formulation is played by the set
\mathcal{N}
consisting of all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
that can be obtained as the limit of some sequence,
(\sigma_{m})_{m\geq 1}
, in the finite-dimensional distribution sense (see Definition
8.3
and Remark
8.4
), where
\sigma_{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon_{m}}
for all
m\geq 1
and
\varepsilon_{m}\downarrow 0
. Before stating our results, we introduce the following notation.
Consider the measure
\mu
defined on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
with density

\mu(dx)=\frac{\alpha dx}{2|x|^{1+\alpha}}.

(2.9)

Let
\lambda>0.
Denote by
(\xi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(\xi_{k}^{\prime})_{k\geq 1}
Poisson Point Processes (PPP) with mean measures
\mu,
and
\lambda\mu/2,
respectively. Assume that their absolute values are ordered in the decreasing manner. Denote by
(\xi_{i,k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(\xi_{i,k}^{\prime})_{k\geq 1}

(\xi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(\xi_{k}^{\prime})_{k\geq 1}

\delta

be i.i.d. Rademacher random variables. Let

Denote by

For

For each

Note that this functional is always finite as also guaranteed by Theorem

below. Our next result shows that the limiting free energy can be expressed as a variational formula.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume
1<\alpha<2.
Let
\beta>0
and
1\leq p<\alpha.
For the Pareto distribution, the following convergence holds with respect to the
L^{p}
norm,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln Z_{N}=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{N}}Q(\sigma)=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{inv}}}Q(\sigma).

Later on, we will show that the limiting free energy does not change if we replace the Pareto distribution with the general Lévy distribution, see (
D.1
). The proposition below carries out the structure of
\sigma\in\mathcal{N}
at high temperature.

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume
1<\alpha<2
and the Pareto distribution. Let
0<\beta<\beta_{\alpha}.
For any
\sigma\in\mathcal{N}
, we have that
\mathbb{E}_{x}\sigma(w,u,v,x)=0
.

As a consequence of this proposition, it can be checked directly that when
\beta<\beta_{\alpha},

\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{N}}Q(\sigma)
matches the limiting free energy obtained in Theorem
2.1
.
We comment that while the definition of
\mathcal{N}
involves
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
, it seems more natural that both Theorems
2.6
and
2.7
should still hold with the replacement of
\mathcal{N}
by the set
\mathcal{N}^{\circ}
that gathers all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
arising from the Aldous-Hoover representation for all possible weak limits of the replicas sampled from the Lévy model with an added perturbative Hamiltonian term to the original system. Although this is exactly the case in the PVB model in
[
41
]
, the Poissonian perturbation approach therein does not seem to work in the Lévy model since our heavy-tailed disorder is not additive. For this reason, we approach our theorems by analyzing the
\varepsilon
-limit of Panchenko’s invariance principle and the variational formula in the PVB model with truncation level
\varepsilon
; handling such limit involves a heavy analysis, especially, in obtaining the uniform convergences and continuities of the related functionals.

Remark 2.9
.

In Theorem
D.1
below, we will show that the expectation of the free energy for the general Lévy model is asymptotically the same as that associated to the Pareto distribution. Thus, the existence of the limiting free energy in Theorem
2.5
and the same expressions in Theorem
2.7
hold for the general Lévy model.

2.2
Case
0<\alpha\leq 1

In this section, we investigate the behavior of the limiting free energy and the Gibbs measure in the Lévy model assuming that
0<\alpha\leq 1.

In the case
\alpha=1
, upon suitable normalization, the limiting free energy is given by a simple constant proportional to
\beta
and the formula holds for any temperature. Recall
b_{N}
from (
2.4
). Let

h_{N}=\int_{0}^{b_{N}}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy.

Theorem 2.10
.

Assume
\alpha=1
.
We have that
1\ll h_{N}\ll N^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, for any
\beta>0
,

\frac{1}{Nh_{N}}\ln Z_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\frac{\beta}{2}.

Recall from Theorem
2.1
that when
1<\alpha<2,
the normalization for
\ln Z_{N}
is
N
and the free energy converges to a deterministic constant in any
L^{p}
norm for
1\leq p<\alpha
. In sharp contrast, we show that when
0<\alpha<1
, the correct normalization for
\ln Z_{N}
becomes
b_{N}
which is roughly like
N^{1/\alpha}
and the normalized free energy converges to the sum of a PPP in distribution. More precisely, let
E_{1},E_{2},\ldots
be i.i.d. exponential random variables with rate one and let their cumulative sums be given by
\gamma_{j}=E_{1}+E_{2}+\cdots+E_{j}
for
j\geq 1
. Note that
(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
forms a PPP with unit intensity. By the mapping theorem, the points

\gamma_{1}^{-1/\alpha}>\gamma_{2}^{-1/\alpha}>\cdots

(2.12)

form a PPP with intensity
\nu(x)=\alpha x^{-(1+\alpha)}
on
(0,\infty)
, ordered descendingly. The following is our main result.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
0<\alpha<1
and
\beta>0
, we have that as
N\to\infty,

\frac{1}{b_{N}}\ln Z_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\beta\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{-1/\alpha},

where the infinite sum on the right-hand side is finite a.s..

Next, we prove that a spin configuration drawn from the Gibbs measure at any temperature aligns itself with the signs of the interactions on the heaviest edges with high probability. In fact, the number of such heaviest edges can be taken to be growing polynomially with
N
. Denote
n=N(N-1)/2
and let
1\leq U(k,1)<U(k,2)\leq N
for
1\leq k\leq n
be the indices such that

|J_{U(1,1),U(1,2)}|>|J_{U(2,1),U(2,2)}|>\cdots>|J_{U(n,1),U(n,2)}|.

(2.13)

For
1\leq k\leq n
, define the indicator variable

\chi_{k}=\chi_{k}(\sigma)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathrm{sgn}(J_{U(k,1),U(k,2)}\sigma_{U(k,1)}\sigma_{U(k,2)})=1\}}.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\beta>0
. Set
\kappa=\min(1/2,1-\alpha)-\delta
for some arbitrarily small constant
\delta>0
and take
R=N^{\kappa}
.
Then, as
N\to\infty
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\{\chi_{k}=1,\ 1\leq k\leq R\}}\bigr\rangle\to 1.

The exponent
\kappa
in Theorem
2.12
is not likely to be optimal.
We add that from our analysis for Theorem
2.11
, one can obtain the limit of the ground state energy, i.e.,
b_{N}^{-1}\min_{\sigma}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}
, as well as construct near ground states (near-minimizers). We omit the details in this work.

2.3
Open Problems

There are still many open problems in the Lévy model which have not been explored in this paper. Below, we highlight three major important directions.

1.

Following Theorem
2.6
, one of the main remaining questions in the Lévy model is to understand the so-called Mézard-Parisi ansatz for the limiting free energy for
1<\alpha<2
. In the setting of the PVB model, if one assumes that the overlap is supported only on a finite number of atoms, then this ansatz was understood in the work
[
42
]
and led to the Mézard-Parisi formula for the limiting free energy. However, since the overlap distribution is expected to be supported on an interval in the low temperature regime, the general scenario remains open and as a result, we cannot take this formula to understand the limiting free energy of the Lévy model directly.

2.

Understanding the behavior of the site and bond overlaps are important subjects in the Lévy model. In particular, in the case
1<\alpha<2
, it is predicted in
[
16
]
that there exists another critical temperature
\beta_{c}>\beta_{\alpha}
such that for
\beta_{\alpha}<\beta<\beta_{c},
the site overlap is concentrated at a positive number, while for
\beta>\beta_{c}
, this quantity possesses a nontrivial distribution. For
0<\alpha\leq 1,
we do not have any results on the overlaps. It would be particularly interesting to understand the phase transition of the overlaps with respect to the temperature. We anticipate that the bond overlap should be strictly positive at any temperature.

Establishing the Limiting Free Energy at High Temperature

3.1
Concentration of the Free Energy

Note for any
a=\pm 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R},
we have the identity,

e^{ax}=\cosh(x)\bigl(1+\tanh(x)a\bigr).

(3.1)

Using this, we can split the partition function into the scalar and effective parts as

Z_{N}=\bar{Z}_{N}\cdot\widehat{Z}_{N},

(3.2)

where

\bar{Z}_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{i<j}\cosh(\beta J_{ij})\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\widehat{Z}_{N}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}\prod_{i<j}\big(1+\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\tanh(\beta J_{ij})\big).

We need the concentration of the free energy in the Lévy model, which will be used to establish the limiting free energy at high temperature. Incidentally, the same concentration was recently established in
[
29
, Proposition 5.1]
. For the sake of completeness, we present a proof below.

Theorem 3.1

(Concentration)
.

Assume
1<\alpha<2
and
\beta>0.

For any
0<\varepsilon<\alpha-1
and
(2+\varepsilon)(1+\alpha)^{-1}\alpha<p<\alpha
,
we have for large enough
N
,

\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\frac{\ln Z_{N}}{N}-\mathbb{E}\frac{\ln Z_{N}}{N}\Bigr|^{p}\leq\frac{K}{N^{p+p/\alpha-2-\varepsilon}}

(3.3)

and the same inequality also holds for
\widehat{Z}_{N}
and
\bar{Z}_{N}
,
where
K>0
is a constant depending only on
\alpha,p,\beta
.

Remark 3.2
.

Since
\mathbb{E}|J|^{p}<\infty
for every
p<\alpha
and
\mathbb{E}|J|^{\alpha}=\infty
, (
3.3
) cannot be extended to any
p\geq\alpha
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We revisit the argument in
[
29
]
and point out that the same steps lead to the concentration of
\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}
.
Write
X_{ij}=N^{1/\alpha}J_{ij}
so that
X_{ij}
are i.i.d. with density
2^{-1}\alpha\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\geq 1\}}x^{-1-\alpha}
. Let
n=N(N-1)/2
.
Let
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
be an arbitrary enumeration of the elements from
\mathcal{I}=\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq N\}
.
For
e=(i,j)
, let
X_{e}
and
\sigma_{e}
denote
X_{ij}
and
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}
respectively.
Define
\mathcal{F}_{k}
to be the sigma-field generated by the random variables
\{X_{e_{\ell}}:1\leq\ell\leq k\}
.
Consider the martingale difference sequence

D_{k}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln Z_{N}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigr]-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln Z_{N}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\bigr],\ \ 1\leq k\leq n,

where the expectation is well-defined since
\alpha>1
.
Writing
\langle\cdot\rangle_{k}
for the Gibbs expectation for the same Hamiltonian as
H_{N}
with
X_{e_{k}}=0
and
Z_{N}^{(k)}
for the corresponding partition function, we can express
Z_{N}=Z_{N}^{(k)}\langle\exp(\beta N^{-1/\alpha}X_{e_{k}}\sigma_{e_{k}})\rangle_{k}
. Since
\mathbb{E}[\ln Z^{(k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=\mathbb{E}[\ln Z^{(k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}]
,
we have

ND_{k}=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\langle\exp(\beta N^{-1/\alpha}X_{e_{k}}\sigma_{e_{k}})\rangle_{k}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\langle\exp(\beta N^{-1/\alpha}X_{e_{k}}\sigma_{e_{k}})\rangle_{k}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\bigr],

(3.4)

which leads to the bound

|D_{k}|\leq\beta N^{-1-1/\alpha}\bigl(|X_{e_{k}}|+\mathbb{E}|X_{e_{k}}|\bigr).

(3.5)

Let
0<p<\alpha
.
By Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, there exists a constant
C_{p}
such that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\ln Z_{N}-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{N}\Bigr|^{p}\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\sum_{k}D_{k}^{2}\Bigr|^{p/2}\leq C_{p}\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}|D_{k}|^{p}

(3.6)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
p/2<1
. Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), and
using the fact that
X_{k}
is
L^{p}
-integrable, we derive that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\ln Z_{N}-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{N}\Bigr|^{p}\leq\frac{C\mathbb{E}|X|^{p}}{N^{p-2+p/\alpha}}

(3.7)

for some constant
C=C(p,\alpha,\beta)>0
.
The proof is complete by noticing that
p-2+p/\alpha>0
is equivalent to
p>2\alpha/(1+\alpha)
.

The proof for
\ln\bar{Z}_{N}
is similar. In this case, we take our martingale difference to be

D_{k}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\bar{Z}_{N}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\bar{Z}_{N}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\bigr]=\frac{1}{N}\ln\cosh(\beta N^{-1/\alpha}X_{e_{k}})

and note that
|D_{k}|\leq\beta N^{-1-1/\alpha}|X_{e_{k}}|.

The rest of the proof remains the same. Finally, the concentration of
\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}
follows from that of
\ln Z_{N}
and
\ln\bar{Z}_{N}
by using the identity
\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}=\ln Z_{N}-\ln\bar{Z}_{N}
and the triangle inequality.

∎

3.2
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Note that as
N\to\infty
,

\mathbb{E}\frac{\ln\bar{Z}_{N}}{N}=\frac{N-1}{2}\mathbb{E}\ln\cosh(\beta J)\to\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln\cosh(\beta x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}dx,

where the inequality
|\ln\cosh(x)|\leq\min(|x|,x^{2}/2)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
assures that the integral is finite in the range
1<\alpha<2
. From this and Theorem
3.1
,
it suffices to show that
\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\mathbb{E}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}=\ln 2.

We argue by using the second moment method. First of all, by independence and symmetry of the weights
(J_{ij})
,

\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N}=\sum_{\sigma}\prod_{i<j}(1+\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathbb{E}\tanh(\beta J_{ij}))=2^{N},

Then Jensen’s inequality implies that

\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}\leq\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N}=\ln 2.

Thus, it remains to show
\liminf_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\mathbb{E}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}=\ln 2.

For any
N\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\prod_{i<j,\ k<l}\mathbb{E}\big(1+\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\tanh(\beta J_{ij})\bigr)\bigl(1+\tau_{k}\tau_{l}\tanh(\beta J_{kl})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\prod_{i<j}\bigl(1+\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\tau_{i}\tau_{j}\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)\bigr).

Using the inequality
1+x\leq e^{x}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\tau_{i}\tau_{j}\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{i\leq N}\sigma_{i}\tau_{i}\Big)^{2}\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)\Big)\leq 4^{N}\big(1-N\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)\big)^{-1/2}.

The last inequality above follows from the fact that
(\sigma_{i}\tau_{i})_{i\leq N}
can be regarded as independent Rademacher random variables on which we can apply the standard exponential moment estimate
\mathbb{E}\exp(2^{-1}(\sum_{i\leq N}a_{i}\eta_{i})^{2})\leq(1-\sum_{i\leq N}a_{i}^{2})^{-1/2}
for i.i.d. Rademacher
(\eta_{i})_{i\leq n}
and
\sum_{i\leq N}a_{i}^{2}<1
. Additionally, we used the fact that, as
N\to\infty
,

N\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)=\alpha\int_{N^{-1/\alpha}}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh^{2}(\beta x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}dx\uparrow(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha}<1.

(3.8)

Now, let
A
be the event
\{\widehat{Z}_{N}>2^{-1}\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N}\}
.
The Paley-Zygmund inequality implies

\mathbb{P}(A)\geq\frac{1}{4}\frac{(\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N})^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{1-(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha}}.

On the event
A
, it holds that

\frac{\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}}{N}>-\frac{\ln 2}{N}+\frac{\ln\mathbb{E}\widehat{Z}_{N}}{N}=-\frac{\ln 2}{N}+\ln 2.

From the concentration of
\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}
in Theorem
3.1
, we have
N^{-1}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}\to N^{-1}\mathbb{E}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}
in probability. Fix
\epsilon>0
.
For sufficiently large
N
, on an event of constant probability, we have

\frac{\mathbb{E}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}}{N}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}}{N}-\frac{\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}}{N}+\frac{\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}}{N}\geq-\epsilon-\frac{\ln 2}{N}+\ln 2.

Since the previous inequality is not random, we have
\liminf_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\mathbb{E}\ln\widehat{Z}_{N}=\ln 2,
as desired.

\forall\epsilon>0,\ \frac{1}{s_{N}^{2}}\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}X_{ij}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|X_{ij}|\geq\epsilon s_{N}\}}\to 0

holds trivially.
Hence, by the Lindeberg-Feller central limit theorem, we have, as

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigl(S_{1}-\frac{\alpha(N-1)}{2}\int_{N^{-1/\alpha}}^{1}\frac{\ln\cosh(\beta x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}dx\Bigr)=\frac{\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}}{\sqrt{N}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N\Bigl(0,\ \frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bigl(\ln\cosh(\beta x)\bigr)^{2}}{x^{1+\alpha}}dx\Bigr).

(4.3)

Next, we handle
S_{2}.
Note that
J\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|>1\}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Bg
, where
B
is Bernoulli with parameter

,

(\alpha/2)|x|^{-(1+\alpha)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|>1\}}

where
(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}

random variable. In order to find out the asymptotic distribution of

we introduce

for any
m\geq 1.
Note that
\ln\cosh(\beta g)
is a positive random variable with tail probability

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\ln\cosh(\beta g)>x\bigr)=\bigl(\beta^{-1}\mathrm{arccosh}(e^{x})\bigr)^{-\alpha}\sim\beta^{\alpha}x^{-\alpha}\,\,\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Set

It is easy to verify that

By the standard result on the sum of i.i.d. heavy-tailed random variables, see, e.g.,
[
23

\frac{S_{2,m}-d_{m}}{c_{m}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Y_{\alpha}\,\,\mbox{as $m\to\infty$,}

(4.4)

where the limiting distribution

is as in (
4.1
). We claim that

\frac{S_{2}-S_{2,m}}{c_{m}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}0\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,m=\frac{N-1}{2}\to\infty.

(4.5)

. To that end, fix

\delta>0

, which implies that the second term of (

On the other hand, from (

\lim_{K\to\infty}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(S_{2,m}\leq Kc_{m}+d_{m}\big)=1.

Now, a straightforward computation yields that

is bounded above by

and

Here, if we take

for some constant

. Consequently,

\lim_{K\to\infty}\liminf_{m=\lceil\sqrt{N}\log N\rceil\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(N^{-1/\alpha}S_{2,m}\leq KN^{-\max(\alpha^{-1}-2^{-1},(2\alpha)^{-1})}(\log N)^{2+1/\alpha}\big)=1.

Since

this limit implies that the first term of (

Finally, Theorem

follows from combining (

), (

), and (

) and using the estimate

\int_{1}^{N^{1/\alpha}}\frac{\ln\cosh(\beta x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}dx=o(N^{1/\alpha}),\,\,\forall 0<\alpha<2.

(4.7)

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
0<\alpha<2
. For
\beta<\beta_{\alpha}
,
there exists a constant

such that

\sup_{N\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sum_{\partial\Gamma=\emptyset,|\Gamma|\geq m}w(\Gamma)\Bigr)^{2}\leq Ce^{\sqrt{2m}}(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha m},\,\,m\geq 1.

Proof.

Since the weights attached to distinct edges are independent,
\mathbb{E}w(\Gamma)w(\Gamma^{\prime})=0
if
\Gamma\neq\Gamma^{\prime}
. Therefore,
we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sum_{\partial\Gamma=\emptyset,|\Gamma|\geq m}w(\Gamma)\Bigr)^{2}=\sum_{\partial\Gamma=\emptyset,|\Gamma|\geq m}\mathbb{E}w(\Gamma)^{2}.

For sufficiently large

e^{\delta}(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha}=1-1/(2\sqrt{m})

We claim that for any
N\geq m

\sum_{\gamma:|\gamma|=m}\mathbb{E}w(\gamma)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2m}(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha m},\quad\forall m\geq 1.

(4.8)

Indeed, there are

\frac{N!}{(N-m)!(2m)}

many cycles of size
m

3.8

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma:|\gamma|=m}\mathbb{E}w(\gamma)^{2}=\frac{N(N-1)\cdots(N-m+1)}{2m}\bigl(\mathbb{E}(\tanh\beta J)^{2}\bigr)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{i}{N}\Bigr)\cdot\bigl(N\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)\bigr)^{m}\leq\frac{1}{2m}(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha m}.

From the claim (
4.8
), we derive that

\displaystyle\sum_{\partial\Gamma=\emptyset,|\Gamma|\geq m}\mathbb{E}w(\Gamma)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\partial\Gamma=\emptyset,|\Gamma|\geq m}e^{\delta|\Gamma|-\delta m}\mathbb{E}w(\Gamma)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\delta m}\prod_{\gamma}\bigl(1+e^{\delta|\gamma|}\mathbb{E}w(\gamma)^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=e^{-\delta m}\prod_{k\leq N}\prod_{|\gamma|=k}\bigl(1+e^{\delta|\gamma|}\mathbb{E}w(\gamma)^{2}\bigr)\leq e^{-\delta m}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq 3}\frac{(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha k}e^{\delta k}}{2k}\Bigr),

where the last inequality used

for all

Now, we conclude that

\displaystyle e^{-\delta m}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq 3}\frac{(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha k}e^{\delta k}}{2k}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha m}}{(1-1/(2\sqrt{m}))^{m}}\exp\Bigl(\frac{1}{6(1-(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha}e^{\delta})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha m}\exp\Bigl(-m\ln(1-1/(2\sqrt{m}))+\frac{\sqrt{m}}{3}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq(\beta/\beta_{\alpha})^{\alpha m}e^{\sqrt{2m}},

where the first inequality used
\sum_{k\geq 3}x^{k}/(2k)\leq 6^{-1}(1-x)^{-1}

for
0\leq x\leq 1/2
.
This completes our proof.
∎

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{N}\mathbb{E}(\widehat{X}_{N}-\widehat{X}_{N,m})^{2}=0.

J_{ij}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl|\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}-\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}\bigr|\leq 2\beta N^{-1/\alpha}\mathbb{E}|X|.

(5.6)

From (
5.2
) and (
5.4
), we have that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{ij}(x):=\bigl|f\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij},\beta x\bigr)-f\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime},\beta x\bigr)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\min\Bigl(\frac{2\bigl|\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}-\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}\bigr|}{1-|\tanh(\beta x)|},2\Bigr).

Consequently, for any
K>N^{-1/\alpha},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[|J_{ij}|\Psi_{ij}(J_{ij});|J_{ij}|\leq K\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{|J_{ij}|}{1-|\tanh(\beta J_{ij})|};|J_{ij}|\leq K\Bigr]\mathbb{E}\bigl|\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}-\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}\bigr|,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[|J_{ij}|\Psi_{ij}(J_{ij});|J_{ij}|\geq K\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\bigl[|J_{ij}|;|J_{ij}|\geq K\bigr].

A simple computation yields

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{|J|}{1-|\tanh(\beta J)|};|J|\leq K\Bigr]\leq e^{2\beta K}\frac{\mathbb{E}|X|}{N^{1/\alpha}},\quad\mathbb{E}[|J|;|J|\geq K]\leq\frac{\alpha K^{-\alpha+1}}{(\alpha-1)N}.

These bounds, coupled with the estimate (
5.6
), imply that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|J_{ij}|\Psi_{ij}(J_{ij})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}e^{2\beta K}}{N^{-1+2/\alpha}}+C_{0}K^{-\alpha+1}.

Letting
K=(4\beta)^{-1}(2/\alpha-1)\ln N
yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}f\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime},\beta J_{ij}\bigr)\Bigr|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(\ln N)^{\alpha-1}}.

(5.7)

Next, we compute

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}\bigl[J_{ij}f\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime},\beta J_{ij}\bigr)\bigr]

with the help of the fact that
\langle\cdot\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}
is independent of
J_{ij}
.
Using the geometric series, we write

f(a,x)=a-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}(a^{\ell-1}-a^{\ell+1})\tanh(x)^{\ell}.

Therefore,

J_{ij}f\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime},\beta J_{ij}\bigr)=J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\bigl({\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}}^{\ell-1}-{\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}\bigr)J_{ij}\tanh(\beta J_{ij})^{\ell}.

Note that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\bigl|(a^{\ell-1}-a^{\ell+1})x\tanh(x)^{\ell}\bigr|\leq|x|(1-a^{2})\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}|a|^{\ell-1}=|x|(1+|a|)\leq 2|x|,

so

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl|\bigl({\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}}^{\ell-1}-{\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}\bigr)J_{ij}\tanh(\beta J_{ij})^{\ell}\bigr|\leq 2\mathbb{E}|J|<\infty.

Consequently, using the Fubini theorem and noting that
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle
and
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime}
are identically distributed yield that

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}\bigl[J_{ij}f\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime},\beta J_{ij}\bigr)\bigr]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\ell\,\,\mbox{\rm\footnotesize odd}}c_{\ell}\Bigl(\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}{\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle}^{\ell-1}-\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}{\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle}^{\ell+1}\Bigr).

To handle the right-hand side, note that we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\bigl\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\bigr\rangle^{\ell}=\sum_{i<j}\bigl\langle\sigma_{i}^{1}\cdots\sigma_{i}^{\ell}\sigma_{j}^{1}\cdots\sigma_{j}^{\ell}\bigr\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl\langle N^{2}R_{\ell}^{2}-N\bigr\rangle

(5.8)

such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}\bigl[J_{ij}f\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}^{\prime},\beta J_{ij}\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\,\,\mbox{\rm\footnotesize odd}}c_{\ell}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{\ell-1}^{2}\bigr\rangle-\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{\ell+1}^{2}\bigr\rangle\bigr).

(5.9)

Consequently, from (
5.7
)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\,\,\mbox{\rm\footnotesize odd}}c_{\ell}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{\ell-1}^{2}\bigr\rangle-\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{\ell+1}^{2}\bigr\rangle\bigr)\Bigr|=0.

Finally, note that for any sequences,
a_{0}\geq a_{2}\geq a_{4}\geq\cdots\geq 0,
we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell:\mbox{\rm\footnotesize odd}}c_{\ell}(a_{\ell-1}-a_{\ell+1})

\displaystyle=c_{1}\Bigl(a_{0}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2k-1}-c_{2k+1}}{c_{1}}a_{2k}\Bigr),

(5.10)

where
c_{1}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(c_{2k-1}-c_{2k+1})=1
. Also, note that from (
5.8
),
\mathbb{E}\langle R_{\ell-1}^{2}\rangle
is nonincreasing in odd
\ell\geq 1.

It follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\bigl(1-\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{L}^{2}\bigr\rangle\bigr)\Bigr|=0,

where
L
is a random variable, independent of
\langle\cdot\rangle
, with the probability mass function
\mathbb{P}(L=2k)=(c_{2k-1}-c_{2k+1})/c_{1}
for all
k\geq 1
.
∎

For each
\ell\geq 1
, we define

\gamma_{\ell}=\lim_{N\to\infty}c_{\ell}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\alpha\int_{N^{-1/\alpha}}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh^{\ell}(\beta x)}{x^{\alpha}}dx=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh^{\ell}(\beta x)}{x^{\alpha}}dx.

Proof of Equation (
2.5
).

Using the convexity of
0<\beta\mapsto\ln Z_{N}(\beta)
and applying Griffiths’ lemma (see, e.g.,
[
45
, Theorem 25.7]
), we obtain from Theorem
2.1
that for any
\beta<\beta_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{d}{d\beta}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{N}(\beta)=\frac{d}{d\beta}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln\cosh(\beta x)}{x^{\alpha+1}}dx\Big)=\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2},

where the derivative is handled by using the fact that
for any
\beta,\beta^{\prime}>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R},

\bigl|\ln\cosh(\beta x)-\ln\cosh(\beta^{\prime}x)\bigr|\leq\min\bigl(\max(\beta,\beta^{\prime})|x|^{2},|x|\bigr)|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|

and the dominated convergence theorem.
On the other hand, by Proposition
5.1
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{d}{d\beta}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln Z_{N}(\beta)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle=\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\big(1-\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{L}^{2}\bigr\rangle\big).

As a result, we conclude that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{L}^{2}\rangle=0
and this validates (
2.5
) since for any
\ell\geq 1,

\frac{\gamma_{\ell-1}-\gamma_{\ell+1}}{\gamma_{1}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{2\ell}^{2}\rangle\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl\langle R_{L}^{2}\bigr\rangle=0.

∎

5.2
Proof of Equation (
2.6
) in Theorem
2.4

Since
\gamma_{1}=\lim_{N\to\infty}c_{1}
, Proposition
5.1
implies

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\langle R_{L}^{2}\rangle-\Bigl(1-\frac{2}{\gamma_{1}}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle\Bigr)\Bigr|=0.

Then,

1\geq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{L}^{2}\rangle=1-\frac{2}{\gamma_{1}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle.

(5.11)

On the other hand, the average Hamiltonian at inverse temperature
\beta
is bounded above by the free energy at any inverse temperature
\beta^{\prime}>0
, as can be seen from the inequalities

\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\max_{\sigma}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\leq\frac{1}{N\beta^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\ln\sum_{\sigma}\exp\Bigl(\beta^{\prime}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\Bigr).

(5.12)

Since the limiting free energy exists for any
\beta^{\prime}>0
(see, e.g., Theorem
2.5
or Theorem
2.7
), (
5.12
) implies

\sup_{\beta>0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle\leq C_{\alpha}

(5.13)

for some constant
C_{\alpha}>0
.
A simple computation shows
\gamma_{1}=\alpha\beta^{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tanh(x)x^{-\alpha}dx
and
|R_{L}|\leq 1
. In view of (
5.13
), for any
k\geq 1
, sending
\beta\to\infty
in (
5.11
) yields

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{L}^{2}\rangle=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{L}^{2}\rangle=1.

(5.14)

Fix
k\geq 1.

Since
\lim_{N\to\infty}c_{k}=\gamma_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, it is elementary to check using integration by parts that

p_{2k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(L=2k)=\frac{\gamma_{2k-1}-\gamma_{2k+1}}{\gamma_{1}}=\frac{\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}\tanh^{2k}(x)x^{-(\alpha+1)}dx}{2k\int_{0}^{\infty}\tanh(x)x^{-\alpha}dx}>0,\,\,\forall k\geq 1.

Using the fact that
|R_{2\ell}|\leq 1
for all
\ell\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle R_{L}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(L=2k)\mathbb{E}\langle R_{2k}^{2}\rangle+\sum_{\ell\neq k}\mathbb{P}(L=2\ell)\mathbb{E}\langle R_{2\ell}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(L=2k)\mathbb{E}\langle R_{2k}^{2}\rangle+(1-\mathbb{P}(L=2k)).

Taking
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{L}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq p_{2k}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{2k}^{2}\rangle+1-p_{2k}=1-p_{2k}\bigl(1-\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{2k}^{2}\rangle\bigr)\leq 1.

(5.15)

Note that
p_{2k}
does not depend on
\beta
. Then, in view of (
5.14
), sending
\beta\to\infty
in (
5.15
) yields the desired result.

5.3
Moment Computations

This subsection is devoted to computing the first and second moments of a modified bond overlap that will be used later when we turn to the proof of the concentration of the bond overlap
Q_{K}
in the next subsection.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume
1<\alpha<2
and
\beta<\beta_{\alpha}
.
Then, for any
K>0
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbbm{1}_{|J_{ij}|\geq K}\sigma^{1}_{i}\sigma^{1}_{j}\sigma^{2}_{i}\sigma^{2}_{j}\Bigr\rangle=C_{K}

(5.16)

and

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{ij}|\geq K\}}\sigma^{1}_{i}\sigma^{1}_{j}\sigma^{2}_{i}\sigma^{2}_{j}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr\rangle=C_{K}^{2}

(5.17)

for

C_{K}:=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{K}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh^{2}(\beta x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}dx.

For the rest of this subsection, we establish this proposition. We begin with the first assertion.

Proof of Equation (
5.16
) in Proposition
5.2
.

Note that (
2.5
) of Theorem
2.4
implies that for
\beta<\beta_{\alpha}
and for any
\ell\geq 1
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2\ell}=0,

so that from (
5.5
),
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle_{12}^{2\ell}=0.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{12}|>K\}}\bigl|\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2}-\tanh^{2}(\beta J_{12})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{12}|>K\}}\Bigl|\frac{\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle_{12}(1-\tanh^{2}(\beta J_{12}))}{1+\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle_{12}\tanh(\beta J_{12})}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{12}|>K\}}\bigl|\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle_{12}\bigr|\bigl(1+|\tanh(\beta J_{12})|\bigr).

Taking expectation and using (
C.6
), we have

\mathbb{E}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{12}|>K\}}\bigl|\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2}-\tanh^{2}(\beta J_{12})\bigr|\leq 4\mathbb{E}\bigl|\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle_{12}\bigr|\mathbb{P}(|J|>K)=o(1/N).

Now, from symmetry among sites, we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{ij}|\geq K\}}\sigma^{1}_{i}\sigma^{1}_{j}\sigma^{2}_{i}\sigma^{2}_{j}\Bigr\rangle=\frac{N-1}{2}\mathbb{E}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{12}|\geq K\}}\langle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2}=\frac{N}{2}\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|>K\}}+o(1).

The proof is complete since

\lim_{N\to\infty}N\mathbb{E}\mathbbm{1}_{|J|\geq K}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)=2C_{K}
.
∎

The second moment computation requires more effort, which is based on three lemmas.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
U_{N},V_{N},A_{N},B_{N}
are
[-1,1]
-valued random variables satisfying

\displaystyle U_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{V_{N}+A_{N}\tanh(\beta J)}{1+B_{N}\tanh(\beta J)}.

Then
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|U_{N}-V_{N}|^{2}=0.

Proof.

For notational clarity, we simply write
(U,V,A,B)
for
(U_{N},V_{N},A_{N},B_{N}).

From a direct computation, we have

|U-V|=\Bigl|\frac{(A-VB)\tanh(\beta J)}{1+B\tanh(\beta J)}\Bigr|\leq\frac{2|\tanh(\beta J)|}{1-|\tanh(\beta J)|}.

Now, fix an
M>0
and write

|U-V|^{2}\leq\frac{4|\tanh(\beta J)|^{2}}{(1-|\tanh(\beta M)|)^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|\leq M\}}+4\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|\geq M\}}.

It holds that

\mathbb{E}\tanh^{2}(\beta J)1_{\{|J|\leq M\}}\leq\frac{\alpha\beta^{2}}{N(2-\alpha)}M^{2-\alpha},\quad\mathbb{P}(|J|\geq M)=\frac{1}{NM^{\alpha}}.

These imply that for
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}|U-V|^{2}\leq\frac{4\alpha\beta^{2}M^{2-\alpha}}{N(2-\alpha)(1-\tanh(\beta M))^{2}}+\frac{4}{NM^{\alpha}}.

Sending
N\to\infty
completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
U_{N},V_{N},W_{N},A_{N},B_{N},C_{N},D_{N}
be
[-1,1]
-valued random variables and
J^{\prime}
is an independent copy of
J
.
Assume that
V_{N},A_{N},J^{\prime}
are independent of
J
and that
W_{N},C_{N},D_{N}
are independent of
J,J^{\prime}
. Also suppose that
U_{N}
and
B_{N}
can be written as

U_{N}=\frac{V_{N}+A_{N}\tanh(\beta J)}{1+B_{N}\tanh(\beta J)},\qquad B_{N}=\frac{W_{N}+C_{N}\tanh(\beta J^{\prime})}{1+D_{N}\tanh(\beta J^{\prime})}.

(i)

If
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|W_{N}|=0
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|D_{N}|=0,
then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl(B_{N}^{2}-C_{N}^{2}\tanh^{2}(\beta J^{\prime})\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}\bigr|=0.

(5.18)

(ii)

If
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|W_{N}|=0
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|C_{N}|=0,
then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl(U_{N}^{2}-(V_{N}^{2}+A_{N}^{2}\tanh^{2}(\beta J))\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}\bigr|=0.

(5.19)

Proof.

For notational clarity, we again ignore the dependence of our random variables on
N.

We also let
c\geq 1
be some large absolute constants which may change line by line.
From a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|U-(V+A\tanh(\beta J))\bigr|\leq\frac{2|B|}{1-|\tanh(\beta J)|},\\
\bigl|B-(W+C\tanh(\beta J^{\prime}))\bigr|\leq\frac{2|D|}{1-|\tanh(\beta J^{\prime})|}.\end{split}

(5.20)

We first prove (
5.18
).
For
M>K
, set
\mathcal{H}=\{\min(|J|,|J^{\prime}|)\geq K,\ \max(|J|,|J^{\prime}|)\geq M\}
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|J|\geq M,|J^{\prime}|\geq K)+\mathbb{P}(|J^{\prime}|\geq M,|J|\geq K)=\frac{2}{N^{2}M^{\alpha}K^{\alpha}}.

Using
|x^{2}-y^{2}|\leq(|x|+|y|)|x-y|
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and (
5.20
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl|B^{2}-(W+C\tanh(\beta J^{\prime}))^{2}\bigr|\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\bigl|B-(W+C\tanh(\beta J^{\prime}))\bigr|\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6\mathbb{E}|D|}{1-\tanh(\beta M)}\mathbb{P}(K\leq|J|,|J^{\prime}|\leq M)+9\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6\mathbb{E}|D|}{N^{2}(1-\tanh(\beta M))K^{2\alpha}}+\frac{18}{N^{2}M^{\alpha}K^{\alpha}}.

Since
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|D|=0,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl(B^{2}-(W+C\tanh(\beta J^{\prime}))^{2}\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}\bigr|\leq 18(MK)^{-\alpha}.

Since this holds for any
M>K,

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl(B^{2}-(W+C\tanh(\beta J^{\prime}))^{2}\bigr)|\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}\bigr|=0.

Now using the symmetry of
J^{\prime}
and the independence between
J^{\prime}
and
W,C,J,
this limit becomes

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl(B^{2}-(W^{2}+C^{2}\tanh^{2}(\beta J^{\prime})\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}\bigr|=0.

From this, (
5.18
) follows since, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle N^{2}\mathbb{E}W^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}

\displaystyle=N^{2}\mathbb{E}W^{2}\cdot\mathbb{P}(|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K)=K^{-2\alpha}\mathbb{E}W^{2}\leq K^{-2\alpha}\mathbb{E}W\to 0.

To show (
5.19
),
from the independence of
V,A,J^{\prime}
with
J
and the symmetry of
J
, it suffices to prove

\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl(U^{2}-(V+A\tanh(\beta J))^{2}\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}\bigr|=0.

Using (
5.20
), for
M>K
, we similarly control

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl|U^{2}-(V+A\tanh(\beta J))^{2}\bigr|\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\bigl|U-(V+A\tanh(\beta J))\bigr|\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6\mathbb{P}(K\leq|J|\leq M)}{1-\tanh(\beta M)}\mathbb{E}|B|\mathbbm{1}_{\{K\leq|J^{\prime}|\leq M\}}+9\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6\mathbb{E}(|W|+|C|)}{N^{2}(1-\tanh(\beta M))^{2}K^{2\alpha}}+\frac{18}{N^{2}M^{\alpha}K^{\alpha}},

where we used the definition of
B
to bound it by
(|W|+|C|)/(1-\tanh(\beta|J^{\prime}|))
.
From the assumption
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(|W|+|C|)=0,
we see that for any
M>K
,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl(U^{2}-(V+A\tanh(\beta J))^{2}\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J|,|J^{\prime}|\geq K\}}\bigr|\leq\frac{18}{M^{\alpha}K^{\alpha}}.

Since this holds for any
M>K
, we obtain (
5.19
).
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
1<\alpha<2
and
\beta<\beta_{\alpha}.
Let
i,j,k,l
be distinct positive integers with
i<j
and
k<l.
For any
K>0,
we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}\rangle^{2}-\tanh^{2}(\beta J_{ij})\tanh^{2}(\beta J_{kl})\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{ij}|\geq K,|J_{kl}|\geq K\}}=0.

Proof.

Recall the notation
\langle\cdot\rangle_{ij}
from the proof of Proposition
5.1
. Denote by
\langle\cdot\rangle_{ijkl}
the Gibbs expectation associated to the original system for which
J_{ij}
and
J_{kl}
are set to zero. To ease our notation, for a sequence of random variables
(X_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, we say that
X_{N}\approx 0
if
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|X_{N}|=0.

First of all, we claim that

\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ijkl}\approx 0,\langle\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}\rangle_{ijkl}\approx 0,\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}\rangle_{ijkl}\approx 0.

(5.21)

To see this, recall that (
2.5
) and (
5.5
) imply

\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}\approx 0.

Since

\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ij}=\frac{\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ijkl}+\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}\rangle_{ijkl}\tanh(\beta J_{kl})}{1+\langle\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}\rangle_{ijkl}\tanh(\beta J_{kl})},

(5.22)

we see that from Lemma
5.3
,
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{ijkl}\approx 0
and by symmetry,
\langle\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}\rangle_{ijkl}\approx 0.
On the other hand, we can write

6
Establishing the Superadditivity of the Free Energy

We establish the proof of Theorem
2.5
in this section. First of all, we will introduce the VB and PVB models and show that their free energies are asymptotically the same. Next, we show that the free energy in the Lévy model can be approximated by that of the PVB model with a properly chosen disorder and edge connectivity. Based on these, the proof of Theorem
2.5
is presented in Subsection
6.3
.

6.1
Viana-Bray Model and its Poissonian Generalization

Let
\beta>0.
Let
\gamma>0
be a fixed parameter and let
y
be a random variable with finite first moment. Let
(B_{ij})
be i.i.d. Bernoulli
(\gamma/N)
and
(y_{ij})
and
(y_{k})
be i.i.d. sampled from
y
. For any
r\geq 0,
let
\pi(r)
be a Poisson random variable with mean
r
. Each time when we see
\pi(r),\pi(r^{\prime}),\pi(r^{\prime\prime}),\ldots
, we shall assume that they are independent of each other even when
r=r^{\prime}.
Let
(I(1,k),I(2,k))
be i.i.d. uniformly sampled from the set
\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq N\}
. We assume that these random variables are all independent of each other. The Hamiltonians of the VB and PVB are defined as

-H_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny VB}}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}B_{ij}y_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

and

-H_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{k=1}^{\pi(\gamma N)}y_{k}\sigma_{I(1,k)}\sigma_{I(2,k)}.

Define the free energy of the above two models by

F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny VB}}=\frac{1}{N}\ln\sum_{\sigma}e^{-H_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny VB}}(\sigma)}

and

F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}=\frac{1}{N}\ln\sum_{\sigma}e^{-H_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}(\sigma)}.

Note that in comparison, the PVB model has been studied a bit more owing to the fact, as observed in
[
25
]
, that many technicalities in the Bernoulli model disappear in the PVB model. The following theorem nevertheless shows that the free energies in these two models are asymptotically the same.

Theorem 6.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
y
and
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathbb{E}F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny VB}}-\mathbb{E}F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}\bigr|\leq\frac{C}{N}\Bigl(\gamma^{3}+\gamma\Bigl(1+\gamma+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{N}+\gamma^{3}\Bigr)\mathbb{E}|y|\Bigr),\,\,\forall N\geq 1.

For the rest of this subsection, we establish this theorem. We need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
|f(1)-f(0)|\leq d
and
|f(2)-f(0)|\leq d
for some
d
.
Assume that
X\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\Delta)
and
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(\Delta)
for some
\Delta>0
.
We have that

|\mathbb{E}f(X)-\mathbb{E}f(Y)|\leq\frac{3}{2}\bigl(\Delta^{3}|f(0)|+d\Delta^{2}+\Delta^{3}\mathbb{E}|f(X+3)|\bigr).

Proof.

The idea is that the distributions of
X
and
Y
are similar except when their values are small.
Write

\mathbb{E}f(X)-\mathbb{E}f(Y)=f(0)(e^{-\Delta}-(1-\Delta))+f(1)(e^{-\Delta}\Delta-\Delta)+f(2)e^{-\Delta}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2!}+\sum_{\ell\geq 3}f(\ell)e^{-\Delta}\frac{\Delta^{\ell}}{\ell!}.

The first three terms can be rearranged as

\displaystyle f(0)\bigl(e^{-\Delta}-(1-\Delta)+\Delta(e^{-\Delta}-1)+2^{-1}e^{-\Delta}\Delta^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+(f(1)-f(0))\Delta(e^{-\Delta}-1)+2^{-1}(f(2)-f(0))e^{-\Delta}\Delta^{2}

\displaystyle=f(0)\bigl(e^{-\Delta}(1+\Delta+2^{-1}\Delta^{2})-1\bigr)+(f(1)-f(0))\Delta(e^{-\Delta}-1)+2^{-1}(f(2)-f(0))e^{-\Delta}\Delta^{2}

\displaystyle=-f(0)e^{-\Delta}\sum_{\ell\geq 3}\frac{\Delta^{\ell}}{\ell!}+(f(1)-f(0))\Delta(e^{-\Delta}-1)+2^{-1}(f(2)-f(0))e^{-\Delta}\Delta^{2}.

Therefore, noting that

e^{-\Delta}\sum_{\ell\geq 3}\frac{\Delta^{\ell}}{\ell!}\leq\frac{\Delta^{3}}{6},

the absolute value of the first three terms is bounded above by

\displaystyle\frac{|f(0)|\Delta^{3}}{6}+\frac{3d\Delta^{2}}{2}.

As for the last term, it can be bounded by

\biggl|\sum_{\ell\geq 3}f(\ell)e^{-\Delta}\frac{\Delta^{\ell}}{\ell!}\biggr|\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 0}|f(\ell+3)|e^{-\Delta}\frac{\Delta^{\ell}}{\ell!}\frac{\ell!}{(\ell+3)!}\Delta^{3}\leq\frac{\Delta^{3}}{6}\mathbb{E}|f(X+3)|.

Combining everything, we have

|\mathbb{E}f(X)-\mathbb{E}f(Y)|\leq\frac{3}{2}(\Delta^{3}|f(0)|+d\Delta^{2}+\Delta^{3}\mathbb{E}|f(X+3)|).

∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
(P_{ij})
be i.i.d. Poisson
(2\gamma/N)
and let
(B_{ij})
be i.i.d. Bernoulli
(2\gamma/N)
. These are independent of each other and other randomness. Denote by
\hat{F}_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}
the same as
F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}
, but the parameter
\gamma
in
F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}
is replaced by
\gamma(N-1)/N.
Note that we can write
\pi(\gamma N){=}\pi(\gamma)+\pi(\gamma(N-1))
in distribution so that

\mathbb{E}F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}-\mathbb{E}\hat{F}_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln\Bigl\langle\exp\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{\pi(\gamma)}y_{k}^{\prime}\sigma_{I^{\prime}(1,k)}\sigma_{I^{\prime}(2,k)}\Bigr)\Bigr\rangle^{\prime},

where
(y_{k}^{\prime})
are i.i.d. copies of
y
,
(I^{\prime}(1,k),I^{\prime}(2,k))
are i.i.d. copies of
(I(1,k),I(2,k))
, and they are independent of each other and other randomness. Also,
\langle\cdot\rangle^{\prime}
is the Gibbs expectation associated with the free energy
\mathbb{E}\hat{F}_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}.
Thus,

\bigl|\mathbb{E}F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}-\mathbb{E}\hat{F}_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}\bigr|\leq\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=1}^{\pi(\gamma)}|y_{k}^{\prime}|=\frac{\gamma\mathbb{E}|y|}{N}.

(6.1)

Next, by using the thinning property of the Poisson random variable, we can write

\hat{F}_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln\sum_{\sigma}\exp\Bigl(\beta\sum_{i<j}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{P_{ij}}y_{ij}^{k}\Bigr)\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\Bigr),

where
(y_{ij}^{k}:i,j,k\geq 1)
are i.i.d. copies of
y
and independent of all other randomness.
Let
(i_{t},j_{t})
for
1\leq t\leq M:=N(N-1)/2
be an enumeration of the pairs
\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq N\}.
For any
0\leq s\leq M
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

F_{s}(n)=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln\sum_{\sigma}\exp\Bigl(\beta\sum_{1\leq t\leq s-1}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{P_{i_{t}j_{t}}}y_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{k}\Bigr)\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}+\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{i_{s}j_{s}}^{k}\Bigr)\sigma_{i_{s}}\sigma_{j_{s}}+\sum_{s+1\leq t\leq M}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{B_{i_{t}j_{t}}}y_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{k}\Bigr)\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}\Bigr).

From the assumption that
y
is integrable, we can adapt the same argument as that of (
6.1
) to show that
|F_{s}(0)-F_{s}(1)|
and
|F_{s}(0)-F_{s}(2)|
are uniformly
O(\mathbb{E}|y|/N)
over all
1\leq s\leq M
. Now that we also have

\displaystyle|F_{s}(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\ln 2+\frac{\beta}{N}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{1\leq t\leq s-1}\sum_{k=1}^{P_{i_{t}j_{t}}}|y_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{k}|+\sum_{s+1\leq t\leq M}\sum_{k=1}^{B_{i_{t}j_{t}}}|y_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{k}|\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\ln 2+\frac{\beta(M-1)}{N^{2}}2\gamma\mathbb{E}|y|

\displaystyle\leq\ln 2+\beta\gamma\mathbb{E}|y|

and for
N\geq 2,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|F_{s}(P_{i_{s}j_{s}}+3)|

\displaystyle\leq\ln 2+\frac{\beta}{N}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{1\leq t\leq s-1}\sum_{k=1}^{P_{i_{t}j_{t}}}|y_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{k}|+\sum_{k=1}^{P_{i_{s}j_{s}}+3}|y_{i_{s}j_{s}}^{k}|+\sum_{s+1\leq t\leq M}\sum_{k=1}^{B_{i_{t}j_{t}}}|y_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{k}|\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\ln 2+\frac{\beta}{N}\Bigl(\frac{2\gamma M}{N}+3\Bigr)\mathbb{E}|y|

\displaystyle\leq\ln 2+\beta\Bigl(\gamma+\frac{3}{N}\Bigr)\mathbb{E}|y|.

As a result, from Lemma
6.2
, we readily have that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
N
such that

\bigl|\mathbb{E}F_{s}(P_{i_{s}j_{s}})-\mathbb{E}F_{s}(B_{i_{s}j_{s}})\bigr|\leq\frac{C}{N^{3}}\Bigl(\gamma^{3}+\gamma^{2}\Bigl(1+\frac{\gamma}{N}+\gamma^{2}\Bigr)\mathbb{E}|y|\Bigr).

Thus, noting that

\mathbb{E}F_{s}(P_{i_{s}j_{s}})=\mathbb{E}F_{s+1}(B_{i_{s+1}j_{s+1}})\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\sum_{k=1}^{B_{i_{t}j_{t}}}y_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}B_{i_{t}j_{t}}y_{i_{t}j_{t}},

we have

This together with (
6.1
) completes our proof.
∎

6.2
Free Energy Connection Between the Lévy and Diluted Models

For any
\varepsilon>N^{-1/\alpha}
, denote by
g_{\varepsilon}
the distribution of
J
conditionally on
|J|\geq\varepsilon
, i.e.,

g_{\varepsilon}\sim\frac{\alpha\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{2}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\geq\varepsilon\}}}{|x|^{1+\alpha}}.

(6.2)

Let

y=g_{\varepsilon}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\gamma=\varepsilon^{-\alpha}.

(6.3)

Denote by the corresponding free energy of the PVB model by
F_{N,\varepsilon}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}.
Also, denote
F_{N}=N^{-1}\ln Z_{N}.
The following theorem shows that the free energy of the Lévy model is asymptotically the same as that in the PVB model whenever
1<\alpha<2
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume
1<\alpha<2
.
We have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigl|\mathbb{E}F_{N}-\mathbb{E}F_{N,\varepsilon}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}\bigr|=0.

To establish this theorem, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

For any
N^{-1/\alpha}<\varepsilon
, we have that

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}F_{N}-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln\sum_{\sigma}\exp\Bigl(\beta\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|J_{ij}|\geq\varepsilon\}}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\Bigr)\Bigr|\leq\frac{\alpha\beta^{2}\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}}{(2-\alpha)}.

(6.4)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Set
A_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x|<\varepsilon\}
. For any
t\in[0,1],
set

-H_{N,t}(\sigma)=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\bigl(t\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\notin A_{\varepsilon}\}}\bigr)\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}.

Consider the free energy associated to
H_{N,t},

F_{N,t}=\frac{1}{N}\ln\sum_{\sigma}e^{-\beta H_{N,t}(\sigma)}

and set
\langle\cdot\rangle_{t}
the Gibbs expectation associated with the Hamiltonian above.
Then

\frac{d}{dt}F_{N,t}=\beta\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\}}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t}.

Note that when
J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}
,

J_{ij}\bigl(t\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\notin A_{\varepsilon}\}}\bigr)=tJ_{ij}.

Similar to the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we can write

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t,ij}+\tanh(\beta tJ_{ij})}{1+\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t,ij}\tanh(\beta tJ_{ij})},

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{t,ij}
is the Gibbs measure associated to
H_{N,t}
with the replacement that
J_{ij}=0.
Now, using (
5.3
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|J_{ij}|\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\}}\bigl|\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t}-\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t,ij}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\bigl[|J_{ij}\tanh(\beta tJ_{ij})|;J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta\mathbb{E}\bigl[|J_{ij}|^{2};J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\alpha\beta}{N}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha-1}}dx=\frac{2\alpha\beta\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}}{N(2-\alpha)}.

On the other hand, from the independence between
J_{ij}
and
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t,ij}
and note that

has a finite mean, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}J_{ij}\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\}}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t,ij}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}J_{ij}\mathbbm{1}_{\{J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t,ij}=0.

Combining these two displays together yields that

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[J_{ij}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle_{t};J_{ij}\in A_{\varepsilon}\bigr]\bigr|\leq\frac{2\alpha\beta\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}}{N(2-\alpha)}.

Consequently,

\Bigl|\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}F_{N,t}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\alpha\beta^{2}\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}}{(2-\alpha)}

and our proof is completed by noting that
|\mathbb{E}F_{N,1}-\mathbb{E}F_{N,0}|
equals the left-hand side of (
6.4
).

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 6.5

.

(M+N)\mathbb{E}F_{M+N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}\geq M\mathbb{E}F_{M}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}+N\mathbb{E}F_{N}^{\mbox{\tiny PVB}}-6\beta\gamma\mathbb{E}|y|.

\sum_{j:r(i,j)\leq R}|J_{ij}|-\sum_{j:r(i,j)>R}|J_{ij}|\geq 10^{-1/\alpha}(N/R)^{1/\alpha}.

Proof.

(a) Let
D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{N-1}

\prod_{i=0}^{N-2}\Bigl(1-\frac{i}{n}\Bigr)\geq\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}i\Bigr)\geq e^{-2},

where we used the inequality that
1-x\geq e^{-2x}
for
0<x\leq 1/2
. Observe that for any row

(r(i,j))_{j\neq i}
has the same distribution as
(D_{1},\ldots,D_{N-1})
conditioned on them being all distinct. Therefore, for each
i
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl((r(i,j))_{j\neq i}\in\cdot\bigr)\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}((D_{1},\ldots,D_{N-1})\in\cdot)}{\mathbb{P}(\text{$D_{1},\ldots,D_{N-1}$ are all distinct})}.

(9.6)

Now since the probability of the event that there exist
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
such that
D_{j_{1}}\leq R
and
D_{j_{2}}\leq T
is bounded above by
(N-1)^{2}(R/n)(T/n)=4RTN^{-2},
from the display above, the probability that there exist
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
such that

r(i,j_{1})\leq R
and
r(i,j_{2})\leq T
is bounded above by
4e^{2}RTN^{-2}.
By a union bound, the probability of the event that there is at least one row of
A
having two distinct entries with ranks at most
R
and
T

4e^{2}RTN^{-1},
which goes to
0

(b) First, we claim that with probability going to one, no row of
A

entries with ranks at most
N

9.6

e^{2}{N-1\choose\lceil\ln N\rceil}\Big(\frac{N}{n}\Big)^{\lceil\ln N\rceil}\leq e^{2}\Big(\frac{e(N-1)}{\lceil\ln N\rceil}\Big)^{\lceil\ln N\rceil}\Big(\frac{N}{n}\Big)^{\lceil\ln N\rceil}=e^{2}\Big(\frac{2e}{\lceil\ln N\rceil}\Big)^{\lceil\ln N\rceil},

where we have used the bound

{m\choose k}\leq\Bigl(\frac{em}{k}\Bigr)^{k}

for
0<k\leq m.
The claim now follows from taking a union bound over
N
rows.

Next, by Lemma
9.1
(a), we can write

|J_{ij}|=(n/N)^{1/\alpha}(\gamma_{n+1}/n)^{1/\alpha}\gamma_{r(i,j)}^{-1/\alpha},\,\,1\leq i\neq j\leq N,

where

is independent of

0.9j^{-1/\alpha}\leq\gamma_{j}^{-1/\alpha}\leq 1.1j^{-1/\alpha},\,\,\forall j\geq j_{0}(N).

Now suppose that
i

A
has an entry with rank at most
R

T+1
and (iii) except at most
\lceil\ln N\rceil
entries, all other entries in the row has rank at least
N+1
. On the intersection of these events,

\displaystyle\sum_{j:r(i,j)\leq R}|J_{ij}|-\sum_{j:r(i,j)>R}|J_{ij}|

\displaystyle\geq(\gamma_{n+1}/N)^{1/\alpha}\big(0.9R^{-1/\alpha}-\ln N\cdot 1.1T^{-1/\alpha}-N\cdot 1.1N^{-1/\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\geq(0.9)2^{-1/\alpha}(N-1)^{1/\alpha}\big(0.9R^{-1/\alpha}-\ln N\cdot 1.1T^{-1/\alpha}-N\cdot 1.1N^{-1/\alpha}\big).

(9.7)

From the choice of
\kappa

\frac{T^{-1/\alpha}\ln N}{R^{-1/\alpha}}\leq\frac{(N^{1/2+\delta/2})^{-1/\alpha}\ln N}{(N^{1/2-\delta})^{-1/\alpha}}\to 0\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\frac{N^{1-1/\alpha}}{R^{-1/\alpha}}\leq\frac{N^{-(1-\alpha)/\alpha}}{N^{-((1-\alpha)-\delta)/\alpha}}\to 0.

Thus, for large

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle-\bigl(H_{N}(f_{B}^{-1}(\sigma_{\mathcal{V}}),\sigma_{{\mathcal{V}}^{c}})-H_{N}(\sigma)\bigr)

Lemma A.5
.

For any
m\geq 1
, we have
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)-\mathcal{A}(\sigma)|^{m}=0.

Proof.

We only consider the case that
n=1
as the general case can be treated similarly. Let
m\geq 1
be fixed.
Set

Y(\sigma)=\prod_{k\geq 1}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1}),\,\,Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)=\prod_{k:|\xi_{1,k}|>\varepsilon}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1}).

Recall from the end of the proof of Proposition

that the infinite product in

converges a.s.. Thus,

a.s. as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

From Fatou’s lemma,

\mathbb{E}|Y(\sigma)-Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)|^{m}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon^{\prime}\downarrow 0}\mathbb{E}|Y_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)-Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)|^{m}.

For
\varepsilon>0,
, we bound

\displaystyle|Y_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)-Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)|^{m}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{m}\bigl(|Y_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)|^{m}+|Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)|^{m}\bigr)|Y_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)-Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)|

\displaystyle=2^{m}\bigl(|Y_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)|^{m}+|Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)|^{m}\bigr)|Y_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)|\Bigl(1-\prod_{k:\varepsilon^{\prime}<|\xi_{1,k}|\leq\varepsilon}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})\Bigr).

From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma

, it suffices to show that

\sup_{\sigma}\sup_{0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\Bigl(1-\prod_{k:\varepsilon^{\prime}<|\xi_{k}|\leq\varepsilon}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})\Bigr)^{2}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\downarrow 0]{}0.

To see this, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl(1-\prod_{k:\varepsilon^{\prime}<|\xi_{1,k}|<\varepsilon}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=1-2\mathbb{E}\prod_{k:\varepsilon^{\prime}<|\xi_{1,k}|\leq\varepsilon}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})+\mathbb{E}\prod_{k:\varepsilon^{\prime}<|\xi_{1,k}|\leq\varepsilon}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})^{2}.

Note that for

, the random set

is in distribution the same as

Therefore, using the symmetry of
g_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},k}
, the first two terms together equals
-1
. As for the third term, write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\prod_{k:\varepsilon^{\prime}<|\xi_{1,k}|\leq\varepsilon}(1+\tanh(\beta\xi_{1,k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\prod_{k\leq\pi(\varepsilon^{-\alpha}-\varepsilon^{\prime-\alpha})}(1+\tanh(\beta g_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{w,u,\delta}\exp\Bigl((\varepsilon^{-\alpha}-\varepsilon^{\prime-\alpha})\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{g,v}(1+\tanh(\beta g_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},k})\bar{s}_{1,k}\delta_{1})^{2}-1\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{w,u}\exp\Bigl((\varepsilon^{-\alpha}-\varepsilon^{\prime-\alpha})\mathbb{E}_{g,v}\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},k})\bar{s}_{1,k}^{2})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{w,u}\exp\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{v}\bar{s}_{1,k}^{2}\int_{\varepsilon^{\prime}<|x|\leq\varepsilon}\tanh^{2}(\beta x)\mu(dx)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigl(\int_{|x|\leq\varepsilon}\tanh^{2}(\beta x)\mu(dx)\Bigr)\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\downarrow 0]{}1,

which completes our proof.

Lemma A.6
.

For any

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{\sigma}\mathbb{P}(|A_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)-A(\sigma)|>\eta)=0.

Inequality (

On the interval

(C.9)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{x_{0}/a_{N}<x<1}|(\bar{f})^{\prime}(x)|N\mathbb{P}(|X|>xa_{N})

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary enumeration
\{(i_{t},j_{t}):1\leq t\leq N(N-1)/2\}
of
\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq N\}
. For any

F_{N,t}=\frac{1}{N}\ln\sum_{\sigma}\exp\Bigl(\beta\sum_{r<t}J_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{P}\sigma_{i_{r}}\sigma_{j_{r}}+\beta\sum_{r\geq t}J_{i_{r}j_{r}}\sigma_{i_{r}}\sigma_{j_{r}}\Bigr).

Write

\displaystyle F_{N}^{P}-F_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{N(N-1)/2}\bigl(F_{N,t+1}-F_{N,t}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N(N-1)/2}\bigl(\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{P}\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}-\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}\bigr),

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{t}
is the Gibbs expectation associated to the Hamiltonian

H_{N,t}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{r<t}J_{i_{r}j_{r}}^{P}\sigma_{i_{r}}\sigma_{j_{r}}+\beta\sum_{r>t}J_{i_{r}j_{r}}\sigma_{i_{r}}\sigma_{j_{r}}.

Now, similar to (
6.5
), we can write

\mathbb{E}\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}=\mathbb{E}\ln\cosh\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}-\mathbb{E}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2r}\tanh^{2r}(\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}})\cdot\langle\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}\rangle_{t}^{2r}.

Let
K>0
and
k\geq 1.
From this and using the independence between
J_{i_{t}j_{t}}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{t},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t};|J|>K\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\cosh\beta J;|J|\leq K\bigr]

\displaystyle-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2r}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tanh^{2r}(\beta J);|J|\leq K\bigr]\cdot\mathbb{E}\langle\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}\rangle_{t}^{2r},

where
J
is independent of
\langle\cdot\rangle_{t}.

Note that
|\ln\langle e^{\beta J\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\rangle_{t}|\leq\beta|J|

and

\displaystyle\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2r}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tanh^{2r}(\beta J);|J|\leq K\bigr]\cdot\mathbb{E}\langle\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}\rangle_{t}^{2r}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tanh^{2}(\beta J);|J|\leq K\bigr]\sum_{r=k}^{\infty}\tanh^{2r}(\beta K)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tanh^{2}(\beta J);|J|\leq K\bigr]\frac{\tanh^{2k}(\beta K)}{1-\tanh^{2}(\beta K)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\cosh\beta J;|J|\leq K\bigr]+\sum_{r=1}^{k}\frac{1}{2r}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tanh^{2r}(\beta J);|J|\leq K\bigr]\cdot\mathbb{E}\langle\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}\rangle_{t}^{2r}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\beta\mathbb{E}\bigl[|J|;|J|>K\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tanh^{2}(\beta J);|J|\leq K\bigr]\frac{\tanh^{2k}(\beta K)}{1-\tanh^{2}(\beta K)}.

Using Lemma
C.1
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N(N-1)/2}\mathbb{E}\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{K}\frac{\ln\cosh(\beta x)}{x^{\alpha+1}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{r=1}^{k}\frac{\alpha}{2r}\int_{0}^{K}\frac{\tanh^{2r}(\beta x)}{x^{\alpha+1}}dx\cdot\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{N(N-1)/2}\mathbb{E}\langle\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}\rangle_{t}^{2r}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}\int_{K}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{K}\frac{\tanh^{2}(\beta x)}{x^{\alpha+1}}dx\cdot\frac{\tanh^{2k}(\beta K)}{1-\tanh^{2}(\beta K)}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}\int_{K}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}dx=\frac{\alpha\beta}{2(\alpha-1)}K^{1-\alpha}.

Note that by substituting
J
with

, the same computation still carries through and yields the same inequality. As a result,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N(N-1)/2}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}-\mathbb{E}\ln\bigl\langle e^{\beta J_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{P}\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{j_{t}}}\bigr\rangle_{t}\bigr)\Bigr|\leq\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha-1}K^{1-\alpha}.

Since this holds for any
K>0,
sending
K\to\infty
completes our proof.

D.5
Theorem
2.10

Let us write
c_{N}\sim d_{N}
if
\lim_{N\to\infty}c_{N}/d_{N}=1
for any positive real sequences
(c_{N})_{N\geq 1}
and
(d_{N})_{N\geq 1}
.
Let us write
g=g(x)=\mathrm{arccosh}(e^{x})
for the inverse function of the strictly increasing function
x\mapsto\ln\cosh(x)
on
[0,\infty)
throughout this section.
Recall the notations
a_{N}
,
b_{N}=a_{N}^{-1}a_{{N\choose 2}}
, and

h_{N}=\int_{0}^{b_{N}}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy,\quad N\geq 1.

Lemma D.3
.

For
0<\alpha<2
and
\beta>0
, it holds that

N\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\ln\cosh(\beta J)\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{N}\ln\cosh(\beta J)\leq a_{N\choose 2}\}}\Bigr)\sim\beta^{\alpha}h_{N}.

Furthermore, if
0<\alpha\leq 1
, then
1\ll h_{N}\ll N^{1-\alpha+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
If
1\leq\alpha<2
, then
Nh_{N}/b_{N}\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let us first show the last two assertions.
Suppose
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
By Fatou’s lemma and (
C.3
),

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}h_{N}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh(y)}{y^{\alpha}}dy=\infty.

On the other hand, using Potter’s bound (
C.4
), we have that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

h_{N}\ll\int_{0}^{b_{N}}\tanh(y)(1+o(1))y^{-\alpha+\varepsilon}dy\ll b_{N}^{1-\alpha+\varepsilon}\ll N^{1-\alpha+2\varepsilon}.

Note that the integral for
h_{N}
is finite on any finite interval
[0,K)
for
K>0
by (
C.9
) and (
C.10
).

Suppose
1\leq\alpha<2
.
By a change of variable and Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{Nh_{N}}{b_{N}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\tanh(b_{N}y)N^{2}\mathbb{P}\bigl(|X|>ya_{{N\choose 2}}\bigr)dy

\displaystyle\quad\geq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{y^{\alpha}}dy=\infty.

Now, let us assume
0<\alpha<2.

Since the derivative of
\ln\cosh(x)
is
\tanh(x)
, Fubini’s theorem shows

N\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\ln\cosh(\beta J)\mathbbm{1}_{\{a_{N}\ln\cosh(\beta J)\leq a_{N\choose 2}\}}\Bigr)=\int_{0}^{\beta^{-1}g(b_{N})}\beta\tanh(\beta y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy.

By a change of variable, the previous display is equal to

\int_{0}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N}/\beta)dy.

(D.9)

We first analyze (
D.9
) and show that

\int_{0}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N}/\beta)dy\sim\beta^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy.

(D.10)

The proof of Equations (
C.9
) and (
C.10
) applies to our case since the function
x\mapsto\ln\cosh(x)
is well-behaved near zero, so the integral on the interval
[0,\beta)
of (
D.9
) converges to a finite value:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\beta}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N}/\beta)dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\beta\tanh(\beta y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\beta\tanh(\beta y)}{y^{\alpha}}dy<\infty.

(D.11)

Similarly, taking the integral only on the interval
[0,\beta)
in the RHS of (
D.10
), we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\beta^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\beta}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy=\beta^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\beta}\frac{\tanh(y)}{y^{\alpha}}dy=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\beta\tanh(\beta y)}{y^{\alpha}}dy,

(D.12)

which matches with (
D.5
).
Notice that the integral on the interval
[\beta,g(b_{N}))
of the (
D.9
) can be rewritten

\displaystyle\int_{\beta}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N}/\beta)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\beta}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})\frac{\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N}/\beta)}{\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})}dy.

(D.13)

Combining (
D.5
), (
D.12
), and (
D.5
), in order to demonstrate (
D.10
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle\int_{\beta}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})\Bigl(\frac{\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N}/\beta)}{\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})}-\beta^{\alpha}\Bigr)dy

\displaystyle=o\Bigl(\int_{0}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy\Bigr).

(D.14)

From (
C.3
),

\sup_{y\in[\beta,\infty)}\Bigl|\frac{\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N}/\beta)}{\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})}-\beta^{\alpha}\Bigr|=o(1),

which readily affirms (
D.14
).

Next, we show that

\Bigl|\int_{0}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy-\int_{0}^{b_{N}}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy\Bigr|\to 0.

(D.15)

It is elementary to see that
x\mapsto g(x)-x
is an increasing non-negative function converging to
\ln 2
.
Using (
C.1
) and (
C.3
), the modulus of the difference in (
D.15
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{b_{N}}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)N\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})dy

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in[b_{N},\infty)}\Bigl|\frac{\mathbb{P}(|X|>ya_{N})}{\mathbb{P}(|X|>a_{N})}-\frac{1}{y^{\alpha}}\Bigr|\int_{b_{N}}^{g(b_{N})}\tanh(y)dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{b_{N}}^{g(b_{N})}\frac{\tanh(y)}{y^{\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq o(1)+\frac{\ln 2}{b_{N}^{\alpha}},

which vanishes as
N\to\infty
.

Finally, we can combine (
D.10
) and (
D.15
) to conclude the desired asymptotic behavior.
∎

Lemma D.4

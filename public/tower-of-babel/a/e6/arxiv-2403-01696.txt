Time reversal invariant topological 1D and 2D superconductors: doubling the Sau-Luchtin-Tewari-Sarma [ 1 ] and Oreg-Refael-von Oppen [ 2 , 3 ] proposals
Garry Goldstein 1

Abstract
In this work we present doubled versions of the Sau-Luchtin-Tewari-Sarma [ 1 ] and Oreg-Refael-von Oppen [ 2 , 3 ] proposals thereby obtaining time reversal invariant p-wave superconductivity in both 1D and 2D. This construction is much like the Kane-Mele spin Hall model [ 4 ] , which is a time reversal invariant doubling of the Haldane model [ 5 ] . We show that the low energy effective action for these doubled versions of the Sau-Luchtin-Tewari-Sarma [ 1 ] and Oreg-Refael-von Oppen [ 2 , 3 ] models correspond to single band p-wave time reversal invariant superconductors with pseudospin degree of freedom instead of spin degree of freedom. There are Majorana fermions at the ends of wires or in vortex cores of these superconductors. Furthermore these Majorana fermions are shown to be stable to small perturbations. In the supplement we present a physical realization of the system with cold atoms [ 6 ] and show that a related “no-go” theorem given in Ref. [ 7 , 8 ] has too restrictive assumptions to apply to this proposal.

II.1
Physical setup

We now present explicit orbitals with time reversal properties given
by Eq. (
5

we will consider the case of
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
orbitals which
are a linear combination of
\left|Y_{1}^{1}\right\rangle,\,\left|Y_{-1}^{1}\right\rangle

\left|+\right\rangle=\left|Y_{1}^{1}\right\rangle\sim\exp\left(i\theta\right),\;\left|-\right\rangle=\left|Y_{-1}^{1}\right\rangle\sim-\exp\left(-i\theta\right).

(1)

Here

is the azimuthal angle. For each lattice site for the
systems we will consider there will be four relevant basis states:

\left|+,\uparrow\right\rangle,\>\left|+,\downarrow\right\rangle,\>\left|-,\uparrow\right\rangle,\>\left|-,\downarrow\right\rangle

We will use
\tau
as the Pauli matrices within the orbital space
and
\sigma
to be the Pauli matrices within the spin space, below
we shall also introduce
\mu
which are Pauli matrices for particle-hole

\left|Y_{1}^{2}\right\rangle,\;\left|Y_{-1}^{2}\right\rangle

II.2
Time reversal symmetry

Under time reversal symmetry we have that
\vec{\sigma}\rightarrow-\vec{\sigma}
.
Furthermore under time reversal
Y_{m}^{l}\rightarrow\left(-1\right)^{m}Y_{-m}^{l}

(as
i\rightarrow-i
, see Eq. (
1
)) which means that
we have that in the model we propose below the time reversal operator
is given by

T:-\tau_{x}i\sigma_{y}K

(3)

Where
K
is complex conjugation. This means that we have

T:\tau_{z}\rightarrow-\tau_{z},\,\tau_{x}\rightarrow\tau_{x},\,\tau_{y}\rightarrow\tau_{y}

(4)

Furthermore under time reversal
\mathbf{k}\rightarrow-\mathbf{k}

(here
\mathbf{k}
is the pseudo-momentum in the first Brillouin
zone). As such we have that:

\displaystyle T:

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{k}+\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rightarrow-c_{-\mathbf{k}-\downarrow}^{\dagger},\>c_{\mathbf{k}+\downarrow}^{\dagger}\rightarrow+c_{-\mathbf{k}-\uparrow}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{k}-\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rightarrow-c_{-\mathbf{k}+\downarrow}^{\dagger},\>c_{\mathbf{k}-\downarrow}^{\dagger}\rightarrow+c_{-\mathbf{k}+\uparrow}^{\dagger}

(5)

III
Doubling the
Sau-Luchtin-Tewari-Sarma proposal (p-wave time reversal preserving
Hamiltonian)

III.1
Global analysis

We consider a two band spinful BDG Hamiltonian given by:

\displaystyle H\left(\mathbf{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{z}\left[\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)+\lambda_{SO}\sigma_{z}\tau_{z}+\lambda_{R}\left[\mathbf{k}_{y}\sigma_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{x}\sigma_{y}\right]\right]

\displaystyle+\Delta_{s}\mu_{x}

(6)

We note that
\left[H\left(\mathbf{k}\right),\tau_{z}\right]=0
so
the two orbitals decouple and the Hamiltonian is doubled and
\lambda_{SO}\sigma_{z}\tau_{z}

is similar to the spin orbit coupling used by Kane and Mele
4 [4]

except
\tau_{z}
is not in the valley space for graphene
4 [4]
.
We now use the basis:

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\tau_{z}}=

\displaystyle\left(c_{\mathbf{k}+\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}+\downarrow},c_{-\mathbf{k}+\downarrow}^{\dagger},-c_{-\mathbf{k}+\uparrow}^{\dagger}\right.

\displaystyle\left.c_{\mathbf{k}-\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}-\downarrow},c_{-\mathbf{k}-\downarrow}^{\dagger},-c_{-\mathbf{k}-\uparrow}^{\dagger}\right)^{T}

(7)

As such:

H\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}H_{+}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)&0\\
0&H_{-}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\end{array}\right)

(8)

with

\displaystyle H_{\pm}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\mu_{z}\left[\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)\pm\lambda_{SO}\sigma_{z}+\lambda_{R}\left[\mathbf{k}_{y}\sigma_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{x}\sigma_{y}\right]\right]

\displaystyle+\Delta_{s}\mu_{x}

(9)

being two time reversed copies of the Sau-Luchtin-Tewari-Sarma Hamiltonians
(the total Hamiltonian is TR invariant see Eq. (
5
)).
Furthermore since this is an exact doubling the phase boundaries (where
the Hamiltonian become gapless) are identical to those in the proposal
by Sau et. al.
[
1
]
with the critical boundary given
by
[
1
]
:

\mu=\sqrt{\lambda_{SO}^{2}-\Delta_{s}^{2}}

(10)

III.2
Effective low energy
theory

III.2.1
Hamiltonian ignoring
the effects of pairing

We now introduce the spinor
\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}=\left(c_{\mathbf{k}+\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}+\downarrow},c_{\mathbf{k}-\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}-\downarrow}\right)^{T}

then we consider the Hamiltonian given by:

H\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}

(11)

with:

\mathcal{H}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)+\lambda_{SO}\tau_{z}\sigma_{z}+\lambda_{R}\left(\mathbf{k}_{x}\sigma_{y}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\sigma_{x}\right)

(12)

It is straightforward to check that
\left[\mathcal{H}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),\tau_{z}\right]=0

and that the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
12
) is TR invariant
and corresponds to the non-pairing piece of the Hamiltonian in Eq.
(
6
). Now we will assume that
\lambda_{SO}\gg\lambda_{R}

so that the low energy subspace of the Hamiltonian has a basis given
by
[
21
]
:

\displaystyle\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|+,\downarrow\right\rangle-\frac{\lambda_{R}}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)\left|+,\uparrow\right\rangle+...

\displaystyle\left|\Downarrow\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|-,\uparrow\right\rangle-\frac{\lambda_{R}}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(-i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)\left|-,\downarrow\right\rangle+...

(13)

this being a Kramers doublet, with the Hamiltonian is this basis being
given by
[
21
]
:

H\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\left[\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)-\lambda_{SO}\right]\left[c_{\mathbf{k},\Uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\Uparrow}+c_{\mathbf{k},\Downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\Downarrow}\right]+...

(14)

III.2.2
Adding a small pairing

We consider adding

H_{\Delta}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\Delta c_{\mathbf{k}+\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k}+\downarrow}^{\dagger}+\Delta^{*}c_{\mathbf{k}-\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k}-\downarrow}^{\dagger}+h.c.

(15)

to the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
11
) which corresponds
to the pairing piece of the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
)..
Then we have that:

\displaystyle T\sum_{\mathbf{k}}H_{\Delta}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)T=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[-\Delta^{*}c_{-\mathbf{k}-\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}-\uparrow}^{\dagger}-\Delta c_{-\mathbf{k}+\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}-\uparrow}+h.c.\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}H_{\Delta}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),

(16)

which means the pairing term in Eq. (
15
)
is time reversal invariant. Now we consider the case where
\Delta

is the smallest energy scale to the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
11
).
As such it is sufficient to do zeroth order perturbation theory in

\Delta
. Now we write
[
21
]
:

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{k}+\uparrow}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{R}}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(-i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\Uparrow}^{\dagger}+...

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{k}+\downarrow}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=c_{\mathbf{k}\Uparrow}^{\dagger}+...

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{k}-\uparrow}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=c_{\mathbf{k}\Downarrow}^{\dagger}+...

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{k}-\downarrow}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{R}}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(+i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\Downarrow}^{\dagger}+...

(17)

As such computing matrix elements of the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
15
)
we see that within the low energy subspace:

\displaystyle H_{\Delta}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{R}\Delta}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\Uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k}\Uparrow}^{\dagger}+h.c.

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{R}\Delta^{*}}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(-i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\Downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k}\Downarrow}^{\dagger}+h.c.

(18)

As such the total low energy effective Hamiltonian is given by:

\displaystyle H\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)-\lambda_{SO}\right]\left[c_{\mathbf{k},\Uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\Uparrow}+c_{\mathbf{k},\Downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\Downarrow}\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{\lambda_{R}\Delta}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(-i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\Uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k}\Uparrow}^{\dagger}\right.

\displaystyle\;\left.-\frac{\lambda_{R}\Delta^{*}}{2\lambda_{SO}}\left(i\mathbf{k}_{x}-\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\Downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k}\Downarrow}^{\dagger}+h.c.\right]

(19)

Which is a 2D TR invariant Hamiltonian with pseudospin degree of freedom:

\Uparrow,\,\Downarrow
.

III.3
Stability analysis

It is known that superconductors whose effective theory is given by
Eq. (
19
) have a pair of Majorana Fermions
inside vortex cores
[
17
,
19
]
, denoted by
\gamma_{1}

and
\gamma_{2}
. We now show that these modes are stable until a
gap closing transition. Indeed from Brillouin-Wigner perturbation
theory we know that the zero energy manifold for a vortex has an effective
Hamiltonian which converges until there is a gap closing phase transition

[
18
]
. By requiring the Hamiltonian be Hermitian and
using fermion parity conservation we must have that the effective
Hamiltonian for the zero energy subspace is given by:

H_{eff}=i\Gamma\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}

(20)

For some
\Gamma
. If we further assume the perturbation preserves
time reversal
T
we must have that:

Ti\Gamma\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}T^{-1}=-i\Gamma\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}=i\Gamma\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\Rightarrow H_{eff}=0

(21)

so the Majorana modes are stable (cannot open a gap) and hence the
band topology is stable under small TR preserving perturbations as
well.

IV.1
Global analysis

We consider a total (doubled) Hamiltonian given by:

\displaystyle H\left(k\right)

\displaystyle\left.c_{k-\uparrow},c_{k-\downarrow},c_{-k-\downarrow}^{\dagger},-c_{-k-\uparrow}^{\dagger}\right)^{T}

So that the Hamiltonian is explicitly doubled:

with

H_{\pm}\left(k\right)=\mu_{z}\left[\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)\pm\lambda_{SO}\sigma_{z}+\lambda_{R}k\sigma_{x}\right]+\Delta_{s}\mu_{x}

(25)

being two time reversed copies of the Oreg-Refael-von Oppen Hamiltonians

[

,
3

IV.2.1
Hamiltonian no pairing

We now introduce the spinor
\Psi_{k}=\left(c_{k+\uparrow},c_{k+\downarrow},c_{k-\uparrow},c_{k-\downarrow}\right)^{T}

H\left(k\right)=\Psi_{k}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}\left(k\right)\Psi_{k}

\mathcal{H}\left(k\right)=\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)+\lambda_{SO}\tau_{z}\sigma_{z}+\lambda_{R}k\sigma_{x}

A.1.2
Time reversal symmetry
invariant p-wave 2D Hamiltonians

The simplest time reversal symmetry preserving p-wave 2D superconductors
have the following Hamiltonian
[
7
]

\displaystyle H\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2m}-\mu\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}

\displaystyle+\Delta\left(\mathbf{k}_{x}\pm i\mathbf{k}_{y}\right)c_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}+h.c.

A.2.2
Change of basis

Suppose you have an orthonormal basis
\left\{\left|U_{i}\right\rangle_{i=1,..N}\right\}

and a second orthonormal basis
\left\{\left|V_{i}\right\rangle_{i=1,...N}\right\}

then we have that:

\left|U_{j}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|V_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle V_{i}\mid U_{j}\right\rangle

(40)

Now suppose there is some relevant Hamiltonian such that
\left|V_{i}\right\rangle_{i=1,..M}

is much lower in energy then
\left|V_{i}\right\rangle_{i=M+1,...N}

then in many cases we may write that:

\left|U_{j}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|V_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle V_{i}\mid U_{j}\right\rangle+....

(41)

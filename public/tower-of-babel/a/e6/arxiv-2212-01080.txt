Some restrictions on the weight enumerators of near-extremal ternary self-dual codes and quaternary Hermitian self-dual codes
Makoto Araya, Masaaki Harada

Abstract
We give restrictions on the weight enumerators of ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length divisible by 12 12 and quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual codes of length divisible by 6 6 . We consider the weight enumerators for which there is a ternary near-extremal self-dual code of length 12 ​ m 12m for m = 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 m=3,4,5,6 . Also we consider the weight enumerators for which there is a quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual code of length 6 ​ m 6m for m = 4 , 5 , 6 m=4,5,6 .



1
Introduction

Self-dual codes are one of the most interesting classes of codes.
This interest is justified by many combinatorial objects
and algebraic objects related to self-dual codes
(see e.g.,
[
6
]
and
[
25
]
).

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field of order
q
, where
q
is a prime power.
A code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
n
is said to be
self-dual
if

C=C^{\perp}
, where
the dual code
C^{\perp}
of
C
is defined as

C^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mid\langle x,y\rangle_{E}=0\text{ for all }y\in C\}

under the standard inner product
\langle x,y\rangle_{E}
.
A code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of length
n
is said to be
Hermitian self-dual
if

C=C^{\perp_{H}}
, where
the Hermitian dual code
C^{\perp_{H}}
of
C
is defined as

C^{\perp_{H}}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}\mid\langle x,y\rangle_{H}=0\text{ for all }y\in C\}

under the Hermitian inner product
\langle x,y\rangle_{H}
.
By the Gleason–Pierce theorem, there are
nontrivial divisible self-dual
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
q=2
and
3
only, and
there are nontrivial divisible Hermitian self-dual
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for
q=2
only.
This is one of the reasons why
much work has been done concerning these self-dual codes.
Codes over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
and
\mathbb{F}_{4}
are called
ternary
and

quaternary
, respectively.
In this paper, we concentrate on ternary self-dual codes and
quaternary Hermitian self-dual codes.

The minimum weight
d
of a ternary (resp. quaternary Hermitian) self-dual
code of length
n
is bounded by

d\leq 3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3

[
21
]

(resp.
d\leq 2\lfloor n/6\rfloor+2

[
19
]
).
A ternary (resp. quaternary Hermitian) self-dual code
of length
n
and minimum weight

3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3
(resp.
2\lfloor n/6\rfloor+2
)
is called
extremal
.
By the Assmus–Mattson theorem
[
2
]
,
the supports of codewords of
weight
i
in a ternary extremal (resp. quaternary extremal Hermitian) self-dual
code of length
12m
(resp.
6m
)
form a
5
-design for
i\leq 6m+3

[
21
]

(resp.
i\leq 2\lfloor(3m+2)/2\rfloor

[
19
, Theorem 18]
).

A ternary (resp. quaternary Hermitian) self-dual code
of length
n
and minimum weight

3\lfloor n/12\rfloor
(resp.
2\lfloor n/6\rfloor
) is called
near-extremal
.
Recently, Miezaki, Munemasa and Nakasora
[
22
]
gave
a new Assmus–Mattson type theorem for
ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
12m
and
quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual codes of length
6m
.
The supports of codewords of weight
i
in
a ternary near-extremal
(resp. quaternary near-extremal Hermitian) self-dual code
of length
12m
(resp.
6m
)
form a
1
-design for
i\leq 6m-3
(resp.
i\leq 3m-1
).
This motivates our study of
ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
12m

and quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual codes of length
6m
.
In this paper, we give restrictions on the weight enumerators of these codes.
Furthermore, we give a divisibility property of the coefficients of the weight enumerators of
these codes. This divisibility property is the main result of this paper.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we give some definitions, notations
and basic results.
In Section
3
,
we show that
if there is a ternary near-extremal self-dual code
C
of length
12m
then

A_{3i}(C)\equiv 0\pmod{8}
for
i=m,m+1,\ldots,4m
,
where
A_{i}(C)
denotes the number of codewords of weight
i
in
C

(Theorem
3.2
).
Furthermore, we show that
if there is a quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual code
C
of length
6m

then
{A_{2i}(C)\equiv 0\pmod{9}}
for
{i=m,m+1,\ldots,3m}

(Theorem
3.5
).
Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
are the main results of this paper.
As a consequence, it is shown that
if there is a ternary extremal (resp. quaternary extremal Hermitian)
self-dual code
C
of length
12m
(resp.
6m
) then

A_{3i}(C)\equiv 0\pmod{8}
(resp.
A_{2i}(C)\equiv 0\pmod{9}
)
for
i=m+1,m+2,\ldots,4m
(resp.
3m
).
In Section
4
,
we consider the weight enumerators for which there is a
ternary near-extremal self-dual code of length
12m
for
m=3,4,5,6
.
In Section
5
,
we consider the weight enumerators for which there is a
quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual code of length
6m
for
m=4,5,6
.

All computer calculations in this paper were done using programs in

Magma

[
3
]
and
Mathematica

[
29
]
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we prepare some definitions, notations
and basic results.

2.1
Self-dual codes

From now on, we suppose that
q=3
or
4
.
We denote the finite fields of orders
3
and
4

by
\mathbb{F}_{3}=\{0,1,2\}
and
\mathbb{F}_{4}=\{0,1,\omega,\omega^{2}\}
, respectively,
where
\omega^{2}=\omega+1
.
An
[n,k]

code

C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

is a
k
-dimensional vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
A code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
(resp.
\mathbb{F}_{4}
) is called

ternary
(resp.
quaternary
).
The parameter
n
is called the
length
of
C
.
A generator matrix of an
[n,k]
code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k\times n

matrix such that the rows of the matrix generate
C
.
Two codes
C
and
C^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are
equivalent
if there is a
monomial matrix
P
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
C^{\prime}=C\cdot P
,
where
C\cdot P=\{xP\mid x\in C\}
.

The
support

\supp(x)
of a vector
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is
a subset
\{i\mid x_{i}\neq 0\}
of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
The
weight

\wt(x)
of a vector
x
is defined as
|\supp(x)|
.
A vector of a code
C
is called a
codeword
of
C
.
The minimum non-zero weight of all codewords in
C
is called
the
minimum weight
of
C
. An
[n,k,d]
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

is an
[n,k]
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with minimum weight
d
.
The
weight enumerator
of
C
is given by
\sum_{c\in C}y^{\wt(c)}
.

The
dual
code
C^{\perp}
of a ternary code
C
of length
n

is defined as

C^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}\mid\langle x,y\rangle_{E}=0\text{ for all }y\in C\},

where
\langle x,y\rangle_{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}{y_{i}}

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.
A ternary code
C
is said to be
self-dual
if
C=C^{\perp}
.
Every codeword of a ternary self-dual code has weight a multiple of
3
.
A ternary self-dual code of length
n
exists
if and only if
n>0
with
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}

[
21
]
.
All ternary self-dual codes were classified in
[
5
]
,

[
14
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
24
]
for lengths up to
24
.

The
Hermitian dual
code
C^{\perp_{H}}
of a quaternary
code
C
of length
n
is defined as

C^{\perp_{H}}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}\mid\langle x,y\rangle_{H}=0\text{ for all }y\in C\},

where
\langle x,y\rangle_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}^{2}

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
.
A quaternary code
C
is said to be
Hermitian self-dual
if

C=C^{\perp_{H}}
.
All codewords of a quaternary Hermitian self-dual code have even weights.
A quaternary Hermitian self-dual code of length
n
exists
if and only if
n>0
with
n\equiv 0\pmod{2}

[
19
]
.
All quaternary Hermitian self-dual codes were classified
in
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
19
]

for lengths up to
20
.

2.2
Near-extremal self-dual codes

The minimum weight
d
of a ternary (resp. quaternary Hermitian) self-dual
code of length
n
is bounded by

d\leq 3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3

[
21
]

(resp.
d\leq 2\lfloor n/6\rfloor+2

[
19
]
).
A ternary (resp. quaternary Hermitian) self-dual code of length
n
and minimum weight

3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3
(resp.
2\lfloor n/6\rfloor+2
) is called
extremal
.
A ternary (resp. quaternary Hermitian) self-dual code of length
n
and minimum weight

3\lfloor n/12\rfloor
(resp.
2\lfloor n/6\rfloor
) is called
near-extremal
.

In this paper, we are interested in ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
12m

and quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual codes of length
6m
.

Theorem 2.1

(Han and Kim
[
11
]
)
.

(i)

There is no ternary near-extremal self-dual code of length
12m

for
m\geq 147
.

(ii)

There is no quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual code of length
6m

for
m\geq 38
.

The above theorem is used in the proofs of
Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
.

2.3
Designs in self-dual codes

A
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design

\mathcal{D}
is a pair
(X,\mathcal{B})
,
where
X
is a set of
v

points
and a collection
\mathcal{B}
of

k
-subsets of
X
called
blocks

such that every
t
-subset of
X
is contained in exactly
\lambda
blocks.
A
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design is simply called a
t
-design.
The number of blocks that contain a given
point is traditionally denoted by
r
, and the total number of
blocks is
b
.
If
\mathcal{D}
is a
1
-design, then it holds that
bk=vr
.

The Assmus–Mattson theorem shows that the supports of codewords of a fixed weight
in a given code are the blocks of a
t
-design under certain conditions
[
2
]
.
We often identify a
t
-design with its set of blocks.
By the Assmus–Mattson theorem,
the supports of codewords of
weight
i
in a ternary extremal self-dual code of length
12m

form a
5
-design for
i=3m+3,3m+6,\ldots,6m+3
(see
[
21
]
).
By the Assmus–Mattson theorem,
the supports of codewords of
weight
i
in a quaternary extremal Hermitian self-dual code of length
6m

form a
5
-design for
i=2m+2,2m+4,\ldots,2\lfloor(3m+2)/2\rfloor
(see
[
19
, Theorem 18]
).

Recently, Miezaki, Munemasa and Nakasora
[
22
]
gave
an analogue of the result for
ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
12m
and
quaternary near-extremal Hermitian
self-dual codes of length
6m
.

Theorem 2.2

(Miezaki, Munemasa and Nakasora
[
22
]
)
.

(i)

The supports of codewords of weight
i
in
a ternary near-extremal self-dual code of length
12m

form a
1
-design for
i=3m,3m+3,\ldots,6m-3
.

(ii)

The supports of codewords of weight
i
in
a quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual code of length
6m

form a
1
-design for
i=2m,2m+2,\ldots,3m-1
.

The above theorem motivates our study of
ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
12m

and quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual codes of length
6m
.
The theorem is also used in the proofs of
Lemmas
3.1
and
3.4
.

2.4
Gleason type theorems

It is well known that the possible weight enumerators of ternary self-dual codes
can be determined by the Gleason type theorem (see
[
21
]
).
The weight enumerator
W
of a ternary self-dual code of length
n

is written as

W=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{12}\rfloor}a_{j}(1+8y^{3})^{\frac{n}{4}-3j}(y^{3}(1-y^{3})^{3})^{j},

(1)

using some integers
a_{j}
.
As an example, we give the weight enumerator
W_{3,12m}
of
a ternary near-extremal self-dual code of length
12m
for
m=1,2,3
:

\begin{split}W_{3,12}=&1+\alpha y^{3}+(264-3\alpha)y^{6}+(440+3\alpha)y^{9}+(24-\alpha)y^{12},\\
W_{3,24}=&1+\alpha y^{6}+(4048-6\alpha)y^{9}+(61824+15\alpha)y^{12}\\
&+(242880-20\alpha)y^{15}+(198352+15\alpha)y^{18}\\
&+(24288-6\alpha)y^{21}+(48+\alpha)y^{24},\\
W_{3,36}=&1+\alpha y^{9}+(42840-9\alpha)y^{12}+(1400256+36\alpha)y^{15}\\
&+(18452280-84\alpha)y^{18}+(90370368+126\alpha)y^{21}\\
&+(162663480-126\alpha)y^{24}+(97808480+84\alpha)y^{27}\\
&+(16210656-36\alpha)y^{30}+(471240+9\alpha)y^{33}+(888-\alpha)y^{36},\end{split}

(2)

where
\alpha
is a positive integer.
This calculation was done by
Mathematica

[
29
]
.

Also, it is well known that the possible weight enumerators of quaternary Hermitian self-dual codes
can be determined by the Gleason type theorem (see
[
18
]
).
The weight enumerator
W
of a quaternary Hermitian self-dual code of length
n

is written as

{W=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{6}\rfloor}a_{j}(1+3y^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}-3j}(y^{2}(1-y^{2})^{2})^{j},}

(3)

using some integers
a_{j}
.
As an example, we give the weight enumerator
W_{4,6m}
of
a quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual code of length
6m

for
m=1,2,3
:

\begin{split}W_{4,6}=&1+\alpha y^{2}+(45-2\alpha)y^{4}+(18+\alpha)y^{6},\\
W_{4,12}=&1+\alpha y^{4}+(396-4\alpha)y^{6}+(1485+6\alpha)y^{8}\\
&+(1980-4\alpha)y^{10}+(234+\alpha)y^{12},\\
W_{4,18}=&1+\alpha y^{6}+(2754-6\alpha y^{8}+(18360+15\alpha)y^{10}\\
&+(77112-20\alpha)y^{12}+(110160+15\alpha)y^{14}\\
&+(50949-6\alpha)y^{16}+(2808+\alpha)y^{18},\end{split}

(4)

where
\alpha
is a positive integer.
This calculation was done by
Mathematica
.

2.5
Methods for constructing self-dual codes

We give methods for constructing self-dual codes used in this paper.

Throughout this paper,
let
I_{n}
denote the identity matrix of order
n

and let

A^{T}
denote the transpose of a matrix
A
.
An
n\times n

\mu
-circulant
matrix has the following form:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}r_{0}&r_{1}&r_{2}&\cdots&r_{n-2}&r_{n-1}\\
\mu r_{n-1}&r_{0}&r_{1}&\cdots&r_{n-3}&r_{n-2}\\
\mu r_{n-2}&\mu r_{n-1}&r_{0}&\cdots&r_{n-4}&r_{n-3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
\mu r_{1}&\mu r_{2}&\mu r_{3}&\cdots&\mu r_{n-1}&r_{0}\end{array}\right),

where
\mu\neq 0
.
If
\mu=1
and
-1
,
then the matrix is called
circulant
and
negacirculant
, respectively.

If
A
and
B
are
n\times n
circulant (resp. negacirculant) matrices, then
a
[4n,2n]
code over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
having the following generator matrix:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc@{}c}&{\Large I_{2n}}&&\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
-B^{T}&A^{T}\end{array}\end{array}\right)

(5)

is called a
four-circulant
(resp.
four-negacirculant
) code.
If
A
is an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
circulant matrix,
then a
[2n,n]
code over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
having the following generator matrix:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}&&&&&0&1&\cdots&1\\
&&&&&1&&&\\
&&I_{n}&&&\vdots&&A&\\
&&&&&1&&&\\
\end{array}\right),

(6)

is called a
bordered double circulant
code.
If
A
is an
n\times n

\mu
-circulant matrix, then
a
[2n,n]
code over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
having the following generator matrix:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A\\
\end{array}\right)

(7)

is called a
\mu
-circulant
code or a quasi-twisted code.
Many
four-circulant self-dual codes,
four-negacirculant self-dual codes,
bordered double circulant codes and
\mu
-circulant codes
having large minimum weights are known
(see e.g.,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
).

For a code
C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and a vector
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
,
we denote by
\langle x\rangle
the code generated by
x
, and
we denote by
\langle C,x\rangle
the code generated by
C
and
x
.
Let
C
be a ternary self-dual code of length
n
and let
x
be a vector of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.
If
\langle x,x\rangle_{E}=0
and
x\not\in C
, then

\Nei_{E}(C,x)=\langle C\cap\langle x\rangle^{\perp},x\rangle

(8)

is a ternary self-dual code.
Let
D
be a quaternary Hermitian self-dual code of length
n
and
let
y
be a vector of
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
.
If
\langle y,y\rangle_{H}=0
and
y\not\in D
, then

\Nei_{H}(D,y)=\langle D\cap\langle y\rangle^{\perp_{H}},y\rangle

(9)

is a quaternary Hermitian self-dual code.
These codes are often called
neighbors
of
C
and
D
, respectively.
For all ternary self-dual codes
\Nei_{E}(C,x)
and quaternary Hermitian self-dual codes

\Nei_{H}(D,y)
constructed in this paper, the vectors
x
and
y
have forms

(0,0,\ldots,0,\hat{x})
and
(0,0,\ldots,0,\hat{y})
, respectively,
where
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n/2}
and
\hat{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n/2}
.
Throughout this paper, we simply write
\Nei_{E}(C,x)
and
\Nei_{H}(D,y)
by

\Nei_{E}(C,\hat{x})
and
\Nei_{H}(D,\hat{y})
, respectively.

3
Restrictions on weight enumerators of self-dual codes

In this section, we give restrictions on the weight enumerators of
ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
12m

and quaternary near-extremal Hermitian self-dual codes of length
6m
.
Furthermore, we give a divisibility property of the coefficients of the weight enumerators of
ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
12m
(Theorem

3.1
Ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
C
be a ternary near-extremal self-dual code of length
12m
.
Let
A_{3m}
denote the number of codewords of weight
3m
in
C
.
Then
A_{3m}\equiv 0\pmod{8}
.

Proof.

Let
C(3m)
be the set of codewords of weight
3m
in
C
.
There is a set
X
such that

C(3m)=X\cup Y\text{ and }X\cap Y=\emptyset,

where

Y=\{2x\mid x\in X\}
.
Let
x
and
y
be codewords of
C(3m)
such that
\supp(x)=\supp(y)
.
If
x\not\in\{y,2y\}
, then
0<\min\{\wt(x+y),\wt(x+2y)\}<3m
.
This implies that

|X|=|\{\supp(x)\mid x\in X\}|=\frac{A_{3m}}{2}.

By Theorem
2.2
,
the supports of codewords of weight
3m
in
C

form a
1
-design.
Thus, the supports of codewords in
X
form a
1
-design.
Hence, it holds that
\frac{A_{3m}}{2}3m=12mr
, where
r
is a positive integer.
Therefore,
A_{3m}\equiv 0\pmod{8}
.
∎

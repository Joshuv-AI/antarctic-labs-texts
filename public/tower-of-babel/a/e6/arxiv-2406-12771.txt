First-Order Methods for Linearly Constrained Bilevel Optimization Thanks: GK, SP, KW, and ZZ contributed equally; authors listed in alphabetical order.
Guy Kornowski, Swati Padmanabhan, Kai Wang, Zhe Zhang, Suvrit Sra

Abstract
Algorithms for bilevel optimization often encounter Hessian computations, which are prohibitive in high dimensions. While recent works offer first-order methods for unconstrained bilevel problems, the constrained setting remains relatively underexplored. We present first-order linearly constrained optimization methods with finite-time hypergradient stationarity guarantees. For linear equality constraints, we attain ϵ \epsilon -stationarity in O ~ ​ ( ϵ − 2 ) \widetilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2}) gradient oracle calls, which is nearly-optimal. For linear inequality constraints, we attain ( δ , ϵ ) (\delta,\epsilon) -Goldstein stationarity in O ~ ​ ( d ​ δ − 1 ​ ϵ − 3 ) \widetilde{O}(d{\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-3}}) gradient oracle calls, where d d is the upper-level dimension. Finally, we obtain for the linear inequality setting dimension-free rates of O ~ ​ ( δ − 1 ​ ϵ − 4 ) \widetilde{O}({\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-4}}) oracle complexity under the additional assumption of oracle access to the optimal dual variable. Along the way, we develop new nonsmooth nonconvex optimization methods with inexact oracles. We verify these guarantees with preliminary numerical experiments.

1.1
Our contributions

We provide first-order algorithms (with associated finite-time convergence guarantees) for linearly constrained bilevel programs (
Eq.

1.1

f
and

(1)

Linear equality constraints.
As our first contribution, we design first-order algorithms for solving
Eq.

1.1
where the lower-level constraint set
S(x):=\left\{y:Ax-By-b=0\right\}
comprises linear equality constraints, and
\mathcal{X}
a convex compact set.
With appropriate regularity assumptions on

Next, we provide first-order algorithms for solving

We use

A function

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|

A differentiable function

it is

if

it is
\beta

\nabla f

Definition 2.1
.

Consider a locally Lipschitz function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, a point

, and a parameter
\delta>0
. The Goldstein subdifferential
[

\partial_{\delta}f(x):=\mathrm{conv}(\cup_{y\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(x)}\partial f(y)),
where
\partial f(x)=\mathrm{conv}\left\{\lim_{n\to\infty}\nabla f(x_{n}):x_{n}\to x,~x_{n}\in\mathrm{dom}(\nabla f)\right\}
is the Clarke subdifferential
[
84
]
of

and
\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(x)
denotes the Euclidean ball of radius
\delta
around
x
.
A point

is called
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary if
\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial_{\delta}f(x))\leq\epsilon
, where
\mathrm{dist}(x,S):=\inf_{y\in S}\|x-y\|
.

Thus, for a Lipschitz function

, a point

(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary if within a
\delta
-ball around

, there exists a convex combination of subgradients of
f
with norm at most
\epsilon
.
For a differentiable function

, we say that

is
\epsilon

\|\nabla f(x)\|\leq\epsilon
.

We first obtain improved results for the setting of bilevel programs with linear

\displaystyle\mbox{minimize}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}~F(x)\coloneqq f(x,y^{*}(x))~\text{ subject to }~y^{*}(x)\in\arg\min\nolimits_{y:h(x,y)=0}g(x,y),

(3.1)

where

,

,

, and

satisfy

Assumptions

2.2
and
2.3
.
The previous best result on
Eq.

3.1
providing finite-time
\epsilon
-stationarity guarantees, by
[

]
, required certain regularity assumptions on
F
as well as Hessian computations. In contrast, our finite-time guarantees
require assumptions only on
f
and
g
, not on
F
;
indeed, in our work, these desirable properties of

are naturally implied by our analysis.
Specifically, our key insight is that the hypergradient
\nabla F(x):=\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{*})+\left(\tfrac{dy^{*}(x)}{dx}\right)^{\top}\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})

Specifically, for any fixed

v_{x}:={\frac{\nabla_{x}[g(x,y_{\delta}^{*})+\langle\lambda_{\delta}^{*},h(x,y^{*})\rangle]-\nabla_{x}[g(x,y^{*})+\langle\lambda^{*},h(x,y^{*})\rangle]}{\delta},}

where
(y_{\delta}^{*},\lambda_{\delta}^{*})
are the primal and dual solutions to the
perturbed lower-level problem:

\displaystyle y_{\delta}^{*}\coloneqq\arg\min\nolimits_{y:h(x,y)=0}~g(x,y)+\delta f(x,y).

(3.3)

We show in

in

3.1
Main technical ideas

We briefly outline the two key technical building blocks alluded to above, that together give us

Lower-level problem with linear inequality constraint: constructing the inexact gradient oracle

In this section, we present a method to approximate the hypergradient
\nabla F(x)
at any given point
x
, where:

\displaystyle\nabla F(x)=\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{*})+\left(\tfrac{dy^{*}(x)}{dx}\right)^{\top}\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*}).

(5.1)

The key computational challenge here lies in computing
\tfrac{dy^{*}(x)}{dx}
since it requires differentiating through an argmin operator,
which typically requires second-order derivatives.
Instead, here we
differentiate (using the implicit function theorem) through the KKT conditions describing
y^{*}(x)

and get:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\nabla^{2}_{yy}g+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}\nabla_{yy}^{2}h&\nabla_{y}h_{\mathcal{I}}^{\top}\\
\text{diag}(\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{I}})\nabla_{y}h_{\mathcal{I}}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{dy^{*}(x)}{dx}\\
\frac{d\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{I}}(x)}{dx}\end{bmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}\nabla^{2}_{yx}g+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}\nabla_{yx}^{2}h\\
\text{diag}(\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{I}})\nabla_{x}h_{\mathcal{I}}\end{bmatrix}

(5.2)

where given
x
, we assume efficient access to the optimal dual solution
\lambda^{*}(x)
of the LL problem in
Eq.

4.1
. In
Eq.

5.2
, we use
\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\{i\in[d_{h}]:h_{i}(x,y)=0,\lambda^{*}_{i}>0\}
to denote the set of active constraints with non-zero dual solution, with

h_{\mathcal{I}}\coloneqq[h_{i}]_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
and
\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{I}}\coloneqq[\lambda^{*}_{i}]_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
being the constraints and dual variables, respectively, corresponding to
\mathcal{I}
.

Observe that as currently stated,
Eq.

5.2
leads to a second-order computation of
{dy^{*}(x)}/{dx}
.
In the rest of the section, we provide a fully first-order approximate hypergradient oracle by constructing an equivalent reformulation of
Eq.

4.1
using a penalty function.

5.1
Reformulation via the penalty method

We begin by reformulating
Eq.

4.1
into a single level constrained optimization problem:

\displaystyle\text{minimize}_{x,y}~f(x,y)\text{ subject to}~\begin{cases}g(x,y)+(\lambda^{*}(x))^{\top}h(x,y)\leq g^{*}(x)\\
h(x,y)\leq 0\end{cases},

(5.3)

where
g^{*}(x)\coloneqq\min_{y:h(x,y)\leq 0}g(x,y)=g(x,y^{*}(x))
and
\lambda^{*}(x)
is the optimal dual solution. The equivalence of this reformulation to
Eq.

4.1
is spelled out in
Section

C.1
.
From
Eq.

5.3
, we define the following penalty function, crucial to our analysis:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda^{*},\bm{\alpha}}(x,y)=f(x,y)+\alpha_{1}\left(g(x,y)+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}h(x,y)-g^{*}(x)\right)+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y)\right\rVert^{2},

(5.4)

where
\bm{\alpha}=[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}]\geq 0
are the penalty parameters.
Notably, we can compute its derivative with respect to
x
of
Eq.

5.4
in a fully first-order fashion by the following expression:

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda^{*},\bm{\alpha}}(x,y)=\nabla_{x}f(x,y)\!+\!\alpha_{1}(\nabla_{x}g(x,y)\!+\!\nabla_{x}h(x,y)^{\top}\lambda^{*}\!-\!\nabla_{x}g^{*}(x))\!+\!\alpha_{2}\nabla_{x}h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y)^{\top}h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y).

To give some intuition for our choice of penalties in
Eq.

5.4
, we note that the two constraints in
Eq.

5.3
behave quite differently.
The first constraint
g(x,y)+\lambda^{*}(x)^{\top}h(x,y)\leq g^{*}(x)
is one-sided, i.e., can only be violated or met, and hence just needs a penalty parameter
\alpha_{1}
to weight the “violation”.
As to the second constraint
h(x,y)\leq 0
, it can be arbitrary. To allow for such a “two-sided” constraint, we penalize only the active constraints
\mathcal{I}
, i.e., we use
\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y)\right\rVert^{2}
to penalize deviation.

Next, we define the optimal solutions to the penalty function optimization by:

\displaystyle y_{\lambda^{*},\bm{\alpha}}^{*}(x):=\arg\min\nolimits_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda^{*},\bm{\alpha}}(x,y).

(5.5)

We now show that this minimizer is close to the optimal solution of the LL problem, while suffering only a small constraint violation.

Lemma 5.1
.

Given any
x
, the corresponding dual solution
\lambda^{*}(x)
, primal solution
y^{*}(x)
of the lower optimization problem in
Eq.

4.1
, and
y_{\lambda^{*},\bm{\alpha}}^{*}(x)
as in
Eq.

5.5
, satisfy:

\displaystyle\left\lVert y_{\lambda^{*},\bm{\alpha}}^{*}(x)-y^{*}(x)\right\rVert\leq O(\alpha_{1}^{-1})\text{~~and~~}\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y_{\lambda^{*},\bm{\alpha}}^{*}(x))\right\rVert\leq O(\alpha_{1}^{-1/2}\alpha_{2}^{-1/2}).

(5.6)

The proof of
Lemma

5.1
is based on the Lipschitzness of
f
and strong convexity of
g
for sufficiently large
\alpha_{1}
.
The aforementioned constraint violation bound on
h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y)
is later used in
Lemma

5.2
to bound the inexactness of our proposed gradient oracle.

7
Limitations and future directions

One limitation to our approach is that the inexact gradient oracle we constructed in
Section

5
requires access to the exact dual multiplier
\lambda^{*}
.
For a first-order algorithm, the closest proxy one could get to this would be a highly accurate approximation (which could be computed up to
\epsilon
error within
O(\log(1/\epsilon))
gradient oracle evaluations). Quantifying the effect of using such an approximate
\lambda^{*}
would be an interesting direction of future research.

Another important direction for future work would be to extend the hypergradient stationarity guarantee of
Theorem

1.4
to bilevel programs with
general convex
constraints.

To this end, we conjecture that the use of a
primal-only gradient approximation oracle could be potentially effective.
Finally, our current rate of
\widetilde{O}(\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-4})
oracle calls for reaching
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationarity is not necessarily inherent to the problem; indeed, it might be the case that an alternate approach could improve it to the best known rate of
{O}(\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-3})
for nonsmooth nonconvex optimization.

Since

is both sufficient and necessary for optimality.
Hence, consider the

\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}g(x,y^{*})+\nabla_{y}\langle\lambda^{*},h(x,y^{*})\rangle=0,\text{ and }h(x,y^{*})=0.

Differentiating
the system of equations in
Eq.

A.1
with respect to

and rearranging terms yields:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\nabla^{2}_{yy}g(x,y^{*})+\nabla^{2}_{yy}\langle\lambda^{*},h(x,y^{*})\rangle&\nabla_{y}h(x,y^{*})^{\top}\\
\nabla_{y}h(x,y^{*})&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{dy^{*}}{dx}\\
\frac{d\lambda^{*}}{dx}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\nabla^{2}_{yx}g(x,y^{*})-\nabla^{2}_{yx}\langle\lambda^{*},h(x,y^{*})\rangle\\
-\nabla_{x}h(x,y^{*})\end{bmatrix}

(A.2)

Finally, noting that

as claimed.
∎

(A.12)

where

.

A.3
The cost of inexact projected gradient descent method

In this subsection, we state the number of iterations required by projected gradient descent method to find an
\epsilon
-stationary point using inexact gradient oracles. Specifically, we consider the following non-convex smooth problem where the objective
F
is assumed to be
C_{F}
-Lipschitz smooth:

\mbox{minimize}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}F(x).

(A.13)

Since the feasible region
\mathcal{X}
is compact, we use the norm of the following gradient mapping
\mathcal{G}_{F}(x)
as the stationarity criterion

\mathcal{G}_{F}(x):={C_{F}}(x-x^{+})\text{ where }x^{+}=\arg\min_{z\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|z-\left(x-\frac{1}{C_{F}}\nabla F(x)\right)\right\|^{2}.

(A.14)

Initialized to some
x_{0}
and the inexact gradient oracle
\widetilde{\nabla}F
, the updates of the inexact projected gradient descent method is given by

\begin{split}\text{{For}}&\text{ t=1,2,..., N {do}:}\\
&\text{Set }x_{t}\leftarrow\arg\min_{z\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|z-\left(x_{t-1}-\frac{1}{C_{F}}\widetilde{\nabla}F(x_{t-1})\right)\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

(A.15)

The next proposition calculates the complexity result.

Proposition A.6
.

Consider the constrained optimization problem in (
A.13
) with
F
being
C_{F}
-Lipschitz smooth and
\mathcal{X}
having a radius of
R
. When supplied with a
\delta=\epsilon^{2}/4C_{F}R
-inexact gradient oracle
\widetilde{\nabla}F
, that is,
\|\nabla F(x)-\widetilde{\nabla}F(x)\|\leq\delta
, the solution generated by the projected gradient descent method (
A.15
) satisfies

\min_{t\in[N]}\|\mathcal{G}_{F}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{F}(F(x_{0})-F^{*})}{N}+\delta C_{F}R,

that is, it takes at most
O(\frac{C_{F}(F(x^{0})-F^{*})}{\epsilon^{2}})
iterations to generate some
\bar{x}
with
\|\mathcal{G}_{F}(x)\|\leq\epsilon
.

Proof.

By
C_{F}
-smoothness of
F
, we have

\displaystyle f(x_{t+1})=f(x_{t}-\frac{1}{C_{F}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t}))

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{t})-\frac{1}{C_{F}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})^{\top}\nabla f(x_{t})+\frac{1}{2C_{F}}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x_{t})-\frac{1}{2C_{F}}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})(x_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{C_{F}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})^{\top}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})-\nabla f(x_{t})).

(A.16)

We now show that
\frac{1}{\beta}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})^{\top}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})-\nabla f(x_{t}))\leq 0.
Let
\widetilde{y}_{t}=x_{t}-\frac{1}{C_{F}}\widetilde{\nabla}F(x_{t})
, and let
y_{t}=x_{t}-\frac{1}{C_{F}}{\nabla}f(x_{t})
.
Then have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{F}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})^{\top}(\frac{1}{C_{F}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})-\nabla f(x_{t}))

\displaystyle=C_{F}(x_{t}-\textrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(\widetilde{y}_{t}))^{\top}(y_{t}-\textrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(\widetilde{y}_{t}))

\displaystyle=C_{F}(x_{t}-\textrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(\widetilde{y}_{t}))^{\top}(\widetilde{y}_{t}-\textrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(\widetilde{y}_{t}))

\displaystyle\quad+C_{F}(x_{t}-\textrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(\widetilde{y}_{t}))^{\top}(y_{t}-\widetilde{y}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}(x_{t}-\textrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(\widetilde{y}_{t}))^{\top}(y_{t}-\widetilde{y}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\delta C_{F}R,

where the penultimate inequality uses the fact that
\mathcal{X}
is a convex set, and
R
is the diameter of the set
X
. Combining this with
Eq.

A.16
, we have that the function decrease per iteration is

F(x_{t+1})\leq F(x_{t})-\frac{1}{2C_{F}}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})\|^{2}+\delta C_{F}R.

Summing over
N
iterations telescopes the terms, we get

\min_{t\in[N]}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{N}C_{F}(F(x^{0})-F^{*})+\delta C_{F}R.

Substituting in
N=\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}C_{F}(F(x^{0})-F^{*})
and the choice of
\delta=\epsilon^{2}/4C_{F}R
, we get

\min_{t\in[N]}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}.

Taking into account the fact that
\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}_{F}}(x_{t})-\mathcal{G}_{F}(x_{t})\|\leq\|\nabla F(x^{t})-\widetilde{\nabla}F(x^{t})\|\leq\delta
, we obtain the desired result.

∎

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert=1}\left[\tfrac{d}{2\rho}(F(x+\rho w)-F(x-\rho w))w\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert=1}\left\lVert\nabla F_{\rho}(x)-\tfrac{d}{2\rho}(F(x+\rho w)-F(x-\rho w))w\right\rVert^{2}

(B.1)

We first show that replacing the gradient estimator with the inexact evaluations

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose

\displaystyle g_{x}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d}{2\rho}(F(x+\rho w)-F(x-\rho w))w~,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{g}}_{x}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d}{2\rho}(\widetilde{F}(x+\rho w)-\widetilde{F}(x-\rho w))w~,

\mathbb{E}_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert=1}\left\lVert g_{x}-\widetilde{{g}}_{x}\right\rVert\leq\frac{d}{2\rho}(2\alpha)\mathbb{E}_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert=1}\left\lVert w\right\rVert=\frac{\alpha d}{\rho}~,

To that end, we start by proving a seemingly unrelated result, asserting that online gradient descent minimizes the regret with respect to inexact evaluations. Recalling standard definitions from online learning, given a sequence of linear losses
\ell_{m}(\cdot)=\left\langle g_{m},\cdot\right\rangle
, if an algorithm chooses
{\Delta}_{1},\dots,{\Delta}_{M}
we denote the regret with respect to
u
as

\mathrm{Reg}_{M}(u):=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\langle g_{m},{\Delta}_{m}-u\right\rangle.

Consider an update rule according to online projected
inexact
gradient descent:

\Delta_{m+1}:=\mathrm{clip}_{D}(\Delta_{m}-\eta_{m}\widetilde{{g}}_{m}).

Lemma B.2

(Inexact Online Gradient Descent)
.

In the setting above, suppose that
(\widetilde{{g}}_{m})_{m=1}^{M}
are possibly randomized vectors, such that

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widetilde{{g}}_{m}-g_{m}\right\rVert\leq\alpha
and
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widetilde{{g}}_{m}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\widetilde{G}^{2}
for all
m\in[M]
.
Then for any
\left\lVert u\right\rVert\leq D
it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Reg}_{M}(u)\right]\leq\frac{D^{2}}{\eta_{M}}+\widetilde{G}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\eta_{m}+\alpha DM~,

where the expectation is with respect to the (possible) randomness of
(\widetilde{{g}}_{m})_{m=1}^{M}
.
In particular, setting
\eta_{m}\equiv\frac{D}{\widetilde{G}\sqrt{M}}
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Reg}_{M}(u)\right]\lesssim D\widetilde{G}\sqrt{M}+\alpha DM~.

Proof.

For any
m\in[M]:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Delta_{m+1}-u\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathrm{clip}_{D}(\Delta_{m}-\eta_{m}\widetilde{{g}}_{m})-u\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\Delta_{m}-\eta_{m}\widetilde{{g}}_{m}-u\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\Delta_{m}-u\right\rVert^{2}+\eta_{m}^{2}\left\lVert\widetilde{{g}}_{m}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{m}\left\langle\Delta_{m}-u,\widetilde{{g}}_{m}\right\rangle~,

thus

\left\langle\widetilde{{g}}_{m},\Delta_{m}-u\right\rangle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\Delta_{m}-u\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\Delta_{m+1}-u\right\rVert^{2}}{2\eta_{m}}+\frac{\eta_{m}}{2}\left\lVert\widetilde{{g}}_{m}\right\rVert^{2}~,

from which we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle g_{m},\Delta_{m}-u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\widetilde{{g}}_{m},\Delta_{m}-u\right\rangle+\mathbb{E}\left\langle g_{m}-\widetilde{{g}}_{m},\Delta_{m}-u\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\Delta_{m}-u\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\Delta_{m+1}-u\right\rVert^{2}}{2\eta_{m}}+\frac{\eta_{m}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widetilde{{g}}_{m}\right\rVert^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left\lVert g_{m}-\widetilde{{g}}_{m}\right\rVert\cdot\left\lVert\Delta_{m}-u\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\Delta_{m}-u\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\Delta_{m+1}-u\right\rVert^{2}}{2\eta_{m}}+\frac{\eta_{m}}{2}\widetilde{G}^{2}+\alpha D~.

Summing over
m\in[M]
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Reg}_{M}(u)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\lVert\Delta_{m}-u\right\rVert^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{m}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{m-1}}\right)+\frac{\widetilde{G}^{2}}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\eta_{m}+M\alpha D

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D^{2}}{\eta_{M}}+\widetilde{G}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\eta_{m}+\alpha DM~.

The simplification for
\eta_{m}\equiv\frac{D}{\widetilde{G}\sqrt{M}}
readily follows.
∎

We are now ready to analyze
Algorithm

3
in the inexact gradient setting.

Proof of

.

Since

, we have

\displaystyle F(x_{t})-F(x_{t-1})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t-1}+s\Delta_{t}),\Delta_{t}\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{s_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]}\left[\langle\nabla F(x_{t-1}+s_{t}{\Delta}_{t}),\Delta_{t}\rangle\right]

By summing over

C.1
Reformulation equivalence

Theorem C.1

(Reformulation equivalence)
.

When
\lambda^{*}
matches to an optimal dual solution to the lower level problem
y^{*}=\arg\min_{y}g(x,y)~\text{s.t.}~h(x,y)\leq 0
, we show that for each

y
.

Proof.

We first show that lower-level feasibility implies feasibility of the reformulated problem.
Let
y^{*},\lambda^{*}=\min\limits_{y}\max\limits_{\beta\geq 0}g(x,y)+\beta^{\top}h(x,y)
be the primal and the dual solution to the lower level problem with parameter
x
.
We can verify that
y^{*}
satisfies all the constraints in the reformulation problem. The feasibility condition
h(x,y^{*})
is automatically satisfied.
We just need to check:

\displaystyle g^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min\limits_{\theta}g(x,\theta)+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}h(x,\theta)

\displaystyle=g(x,y^{*})+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}h(x,y^{*}).

(C.1)

Therefore,
x,y^{*}
is a feasible point to the reformulation problem.

We now show the other direction, i.e., that feasibility of the reformulaed problem implies that of the lower-level problem.
Given
\lambda^{*}
, let us assume
y
satisfies
g(x,y)\leq g^{*}_{\lambda^{*}}(x)
and
h(x,y)\leq 0
.
On the other hand, assume
y^{*},\lambda^{*}=\min\limits_{y}\max\limits_{\beta\geq 0}g(x,y)+\beta^{\top}h(x,y)
be the primal and the dual solution.
We can show that:

\displaystyle g(x,y)+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}h(x,y)\leq g^{*}(x)\coloneqq\min_{\theta}g(x,\theta)+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}h(x,\theta).

(C.2)

By the strong convexity of
g+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}h
, we know that
y
matches to the unique minimum
y^{*}
, which implies that
y=y^{*}
is also a feasible point to the original bilevel problem.
∎

Proof of the main result (

See
5.3

Proof.

First, given the bound in

, we choose

Now we estimate the computation cost of the inexact gradient oracle:

Lower-level problem.

Given the oracle access to the optimal dual solution

, we can recover the primal solution
y^{*}(x)
efficiently (e.g, by
[
45
]
). Therefore, we can use the primal and dual solutions to construct the penalty function

in

5.4
.

Penalty function minimization problem.

The second main optimization problem is the penalty minimization problem in
4
of
Algorithm

5
.
Recall from
Eq.

5.4
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,\bm{\alpha}}(x,y)=f(x,y)+\alpha_{1}\left(g(x,y)+\lambda^{\top}h(x,y)-g^{*}(x)\right)+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y)\right\rVert^{2},

(C.22)

where we use the approximate dual solution
\lambda
as opposed to the optimal dual solution
\lambda^{*}
.
Given
Eq.

C.22
, we solve the penalty minimization problem:

\displaystyle y^{\prime}_{\lambda,\alpha}(x)\coloneqq\arg\min_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,\bm{\alpha}}(x,y).

The penalty minimization is a unconstrained strongly convex optimization problem, which is known to have linear convergence rate. We further analyze its convexity and smoothness below to precisely estimate the computation cost:

•

The strong convexity of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,\bm{\alpha}}(x,y)
is lower bounded by
\frac{\alpha_{1}\mu_{g}}{2}=O(\alpha_{1})
.

•

The smoothness of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,\bm{\alpha}}(x,y)
is dominated by the smoothness of
\alpha_{2}\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{I}}(x,y)\right\rVert^{2}
since
\alpha_{2}\gg\alpha_{1}
. By
Lemma

5.2
, we know that the optimal solution must lie in an open ball
B(y^{*},O(1/\alpha_{1}))
with center
y^{*}
(inner optimization primal solution) and a radius of the order of
O(\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}})
. This implies that we just need to search over a bounded feasible set of
y
, which we can bound
\left\lVert\nabla_{y}h(x,y)\right\rVert\leq L_{h}
and
h(x,y)\leq H
within the bounded region
y\in B(y^{*},O(1/\alpha_{1}))
. We can show that
h^{2}
is smooth (gradient Lipschitz) within the bounded region by the following:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla^{2}_{yy}h^{2}\right\rVert=\left\lVert h\nabla^{2}_{yy}h+\nabla_{y}h^{\top}\nabla_{y}h\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert h\nabla^{2}_{yy}h\right\rVert+\left\lVert\nabla_{y}h^{\top}\nabla_{y}h\right\rVert\leq HC_{h}+L_{h}^{2}

which also implies
h^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}
is also smooth (gradient Lipschitz).
Therefore,
\alpha h^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}
is
(HC_{h}+L_{h}^{2})\alpha_{2}=O(\alpha_{2})

Choosing
\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}

\alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}}

Therefore, by the linear convergence of gradient descent in strongly convex smooth optimization, the number of iterations needed to get to
\alpha

Therefore, we can get a near optimal solution

Overall, for the inner optimization, we can invoke the efficient optimal dual solution oracle to get the optimal dual solution

For the penalty minimization, we need

C.6
Practical oracle to optimal (approximate) dual solution

Here we discuss how practical the assumption on the oracle access to the optimal dual solution is.

For linear inequality constraint
h(x,y)=Ax-By-b
, the LL problem is a constrained strongly convex smooth optimization problem.

To show that we can compute an approximate solution to the optimal dual solution for linear inequality constraints, we apply the result from
[
93
]
:

Corollary C.3

(Application of Corollary 3.1 in
[
93
]
)
.

When
h(x,y)=Ax-By+b
is linear in
y
, the primal and dual solutions can be written as:

\displaystyle y^{*},\lambda^{*}=\arg\min_{y}\max_{\lambda}g(x,y)+(\lambda^{*})^{\top}h(x,y)=g(x,y)-(\lambda^{*})^{\top}By+R(x)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle y^{*},\lambda^{*}=\arg\min_{y}\max_{\lambda}g(x,y)-(\lambda^{*})^{\top}By

(C.26)

where
g
is strongly convex in
y
and
B
is of full rank by
Assumption

2.2
. According to Corollary 3.1 from
[
93
]
, the primal-dual gradient method guarantees a linear convergence. More precisely, in
t=O(\log\frac{1}{\alpha})
iterations, we get:

\displaystyle\left\lVert y^{t}-y^{*}\right\rVert\leq\alpha\text{ and }\left\lVert\lambda^{t}-\lambda^{*}\right\rVert\leq\alpha.

(C.27)

Given
Corollary

C.3
, we can efficiently approximate the primal and dual solutions up to high accuracy with
O(\log\frac{1}{\alpha})
oracle calls when the inequality constraints are linear. This gives us an efficient approximate oracle access to the dual solution.

Remark C.4
.

Under the assumption of an optimal dual solution oracle, all the analyses mentioned in
Section

5
hold for the general convex inequality constraints. However, the main technical challenge is that the dual solution oracle for general convex inequality cannot be guaranteed in practice.
In fact, to the best of our knowledge, there is no iterate convergence in the dual solution
\lambda
for general convex inequality constraints. Most of the literature in strongly-convex-concave saddle point convergence only guarantees dual solution convergence in terms of its duality gap or some other merit functions. We are not aware of any successful bound on the dual solution iterate convergence, which is an important research question to answer by itself.
This is the main technical bottleneck for general convex inequality constraints as well.

Remark C.5
.

On the other hand, we need the dual solution iterate convergence with rate
O(1/\alpha)
to ensure the error to be bounded. But this is not a necessary condition. To ensure a bound on the error propagation, we just need to bound some forms of merit functions (
Section

C.5
and
Eq.

C.25
) of the dual solutions, which we believe that this is much more tractable than the actual iterate dual solution convergence. We leave this as a future direction and this will generalize the analysis from linear inequality constraints to general convex inequality constraints.

C.7
The role of
\lambda^{*}(x)
in the derivative of Equation
Eq.

5.4

Notice that Equation
Eq.

5.4
, we treat the dual solution
\lambda^{*}(x)
as a constant to define the penalty function derivative. Yet, the dual solution
\lambda^{*}(x)
is in fact also a function of
x
. Therefore, in theory, we should also compute its derivative with respect to
x
.

However, notice that the following:

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}(\lambda^{*}(x))^{\top}h(x,y)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}h(x,y)^{\top}\lambda^{*}+\frac{d\lambda(x)}{dx}^{\top}h(x,y)

(C.28)

The later term in Equation
Eq.

C.28
can be divided into two cases:

•

For active constraint
i\in\mathcal{I}
with
h(x,y^{*})=0
, we know that
y^{*}_{\lambda,\alpha}
is close to
y^{*}
by
Lemma

5.1
. Therefore, the derivative
\left\lVert\frac{d\lambda(x)}{dx}^{\top}h(x,y^{*}_{\lambda,\alpha})\right\rVert\leq L_{h}L_{\lambda}\alpha_{1}=O(\alpha_{1})=O(\alpha^{2})
by the local smoothness of
h
near
y^{*}
and the Lipschitzness assumption of
\lambda^{*}
in
Assumption

2.5
.

•

For inactive constraint
i\in\bar{\mathcal{I}}
and
\lambda^{*}_{i}>0
, we can solve the KKT conditions and get
\frac{d\lambda(x)}{dx}=0
. Therefore, the second term becomes
0
.

•

For inactive constraint
i\in\bar{\mathcal{I}}
and
\lambda^{*}_{i}=0
, the KKT system degenerates and we need to use subgradient. By solving the KKT system, we find that
\frac{d\lambda(x)}{dx}=0
is a valid subgradient. Therefore, by choosing this subgradient, the second term also vanishes.

Therefore, we do not need to compute the derivative of
\lambda^{*}
as the terms involved its derivative is negligible compared to other major terms.

Appendix D
Experimental setup

All experiments were run on a computing cluster with Dual Intel Xeon Gold 6226 CPUs @ 2.7 GHz and DDR4-2933 MHz DRAM. No GPU was used, and we used 1 core with 8GB RAM per instance of the experiment. The cutoff time for running the algorithms is set to be 6 hours.
All experiments were run and averaged over 10 different random seeds.
All parameters in the constrained bilevel optimization in

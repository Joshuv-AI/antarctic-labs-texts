Equivariant perverse sheaves on Coxeter arrangements and buildings
Martin H. Weissman

Abstract
When W W is a finite Coxeter group acting by its reflection representation on E E , we describe the category 𝖯𝖾𝗋𝗏 W ​ ( E ​ C , ℋ ​ C ) {\mathsf{Perv}}_{W}(EC,\mathcal{H}C) of W W -equivariant perverse sheaves on E ​ C EC , smooth with respect to the stratification by reflection hyperplanes. By using Kapranov and Schechtman’s recent analysis of perverse sheaves on hyperplane arrangements, we find an equivalence of categories from 𝖯𝖾𝗋𝗏 W ​ ( E ​ C , ℋ ​ C ) {\mathsf{Perv}}_{W}(EC,\mathcal{H}C) to a category of finite-dimensional modules over an algebra given by explicit generators and relations. We also define categories of equivariant perverse sheaves on affine buildings, e.g., G G -equivariant perverse sheaves on the Bruhat–Tits building of a p p -adic group G G . In this setting, we find that a construction of Schneider and Stuhler gives equivariant perverse sheaves associated to depth zero representations.

Introduction

In their recent paper
[
KS16
]
, Kapranov and Schechtman give a new description of the category of perverse sheaves on a complex affine space, smooth with respect to a hyperplane arrangement, when the hyperplane arrangement arises as the complexification of a real hyperplane arrangement. Their description is explicit, in terms of the facets arising from the real hyperplane arrangement.

An important example is given by the arrangement of reflection hyperplanes, for a finite (or affine) Coxeter group
W
. In this setting, we have not only a hyperplane arrangement, but a
W
-equivariant hyperplane arrangement; thus we may study the category of
W
-equivariant perverse sheaves. Theorem
4.3.0
of this paper applies the results of
[
KS16
]
to give an explicit description of this category, as a category of modules over a finitely-presented algebra. This is closely related to remarks of
[
KS14
, §4.B]
, but while they discuss perverse sheaves on a geometric quotient
{\mathfrak{h}}/W
, we consider
W
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
{\mathfrak{h}}
; effectively we work on the stacky quotient
[{\mathfrak{h}}/W]
, which carries a bit more information. Moreover, in loc. cit., Kapranov and Schechtman state that “A complete quiver description of
\mathrm{Perv}({\mathfrak{h}}/W)
is not yet available.” So in this way, the current paper addresses a gap identified by Kapranov and Schechtman.

Our pursuit of this topic began as an effort to revisit the work of Schneider and Stuhler
[
SS97
]
, who study equivariant sheaves and cosheaves (coefficient systems) on the Bruhat–Tits building of a
p
-adic group. In their introduction, they write

the latter objects [certain coefficient systems] constitute something which one might call perverse sheaves on the building
X
. From this point of view our constructions bear a certain resemblance to the Beilinson-Bernstein localization theory from Lie algebra representations to perverse sheaves on the flag manifold.”

In this way, the vision of Schneider and Stuhler is to study something like perverse sheaves on the Bruhat–Tits building.

After Kapranov and Schechtman
[
KS16
]
, there seems to be a natural way to advance Schneider and Stuhler’s vision. For the Bruhat–Tits building is a union of (affine) hyperplane arrangements, glued along facets. While the Bruhat–Tits building itself does not seem to admit a complexification, a combinatorial notion of perverse sheaf from Kapranov and Schechtman (a monotonic, transitive, invertible bisheaf) generalizes easily enough from a single hyperplane arrangement to a complex thereof (such as an affine building).

We conclude this paper with Theorem
5.5.0
, giving an exact functor from the category of depth-zero smooth representations of a
p
-adic group to the category of
G
-equivariant perverse sheaves on the Bruhat–Tits building. We hope this is the first step in a program of studying
G
-equivariant perverse sheaves on the building. Constructing a faithful functor from higher-depth representations to perverse sheaves seems difficult, and closely related to theories of minimal K-types and Hecke algebras for
p
-adic groups.

1.
Bisheaves

We begin with an abstract treatment of bisheaves and equivariant bisheaves on posets. The results of this section are somewhat tedious exercises in diagram chasing, but seem necessary for what comes later.

1.1.
Bisheaves on a poset

Let
(\mathcal{F},\leq)
be a partially ordered set (poset). Elements of
\mathcal{F}
will be called facets, to be consistent with what comes later. Let
\Omega
be a category – we call it the
category of coefficients
. A common category of coefficients is the category
{\mathsf{Vec}}C^{\fd}
of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces and
\mathbb C
-linear maps.

A
\Omega
-valued
bisheaf

({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
on
\mathcal{F}
consists of the following data:

•

For each facet
F\in\mathcal{F}
, an object
{\mathscr{V}}_{F}\in\Omega
;

•

For each
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
, a morphism (called an
upper
)
\gamma_{F_{1},F_{2}}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{1}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}
;

•

For each
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
, a morphism (called a
downer
)
\delta_{F_{2},F_{1}}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{1}}
.

To be a bisheaf, we require the following axioms: if
F_{1}\leq F_{2}\leq F_{3}
, then

\gamma_{F_{2},F_{3}}\circ\gamma_{F_{1},F_{2}}=\gamma_{F_{1},F_{3}}\text{ and }\delta_{F_{2},F_{1}}\circ\delta_{F_{3},F_{2}}=\delta_{F_{3},F_{1}}.

Furthermore,
\delta_{F,F}=\gamma_{F,F}=\Id_{{\mathscr{V}}_{F}}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Define
{\mathsf{BiSh}}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
to be the category whose objects are bisheaves, and whose morphisms are those families of morphisms that intertwine uppers with uppers and downers with downers accordingly.

Bisheaves on
\mathcal{F}
can be viewed as functors in the following way: begin with the directed graph with vertex-set
\mathcal{F}
and two arrows
F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}
,
F_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1}
for every relation of the form
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
. When
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
, we call the arrow
F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}
an
up-arrow
, and
F_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1}
a
down-arrow
(so there is both an up-arrow and a down-arrow from every facet
F
to itself).

Let
{\mathsf{Fac}}
be the category with object set
\mathcal{F}
, with morphisms generated by this directed graph, modulo the relations:

(A)

F\xrightarrow{\gamma}F=F\xrightarrow{\delta}F=\Id_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

(B)

(F_{2}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{3})\circ(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2})=(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{3})
, for all
F_{1}\leq F_{2}\leq F_{3}\in\mathcal{F}
.

(C)

(F_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1})\circ(F_{3}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{2})=(F_{3}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1})
, for all
F_{1}\leq F_{2}\leq F_{3}\in\mathcal{F}
.

We refer to MacLane’s text
[
Mac71
, Chapter II.8]
for more detail on the construction of categories by generators and relations.

By construction, the category
{\mathsf{BiSh}}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
of
\Omega
-valued bisheaves on
\mathcal{F}
is equivalent (isomorphic, in fact) to the category of functors
{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{Fac}},\Omega)
from
{\mathsf{Fac}}
to
\Omega
. Thus we view bisheaves as functors when convenient. From this perspective, it follows that if
\Omega
happens to be an abelian category, then
{\mathsf{BiSh}}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
inherits the structure of an abelian category as well.

1.2.
Equivariant structures

Now we consider equivariant bisheaves. Suppose that a group
G
acts on the poset
(\mathcal{F},\leq)
. Thus if
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
and
g\in G
, then
gF_{1}\leq gF_{2}
.

If
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
is a
\Omega
-valued bisheaf on
(\mathcal{F},\leq)
, then a
G
-equivariant structure
consists of a family
\eta
of isomorphisms

\eta_{g,F}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{gF}\text{ for all }g\in G,F\in\mathcal{F},

such that

(Eq1)

\eta_{1,F}=\Id_{{\mathscr{V}}_{F}}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
;

(Eq2)

For all
g,h\in G
and all
F\in\mathcal{F}
,
\eta_{g,hF}\circ\eta_{h,F}=\eta_{gh,F}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{ghF}
;

(Eq3)

For all
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
, and all
g\in G
,

\eta_{g,F_{2}}\circ\gamma_{F_{1},F_{2}}=\gamma_{gF_{1},gF_{2}}\circ\eta_{g,F_{1}};

\eta_{g,F_{1}}\circ\delta_{F_{2},F_{1}}=\delta_{gF_{2},gF_{1}}\circ\eta_{g,F_{2}}.

Let
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
be the resulting category of
G
-equivariant
\Omega
-valued bisheaves
on
(\mathcal{F},\leq)
and
G
-intertwining morphisms of bisheaves.

The category of
G
-equivariant bisheaves can also be realized as a functor category. Indeed, consider the directed graph with vertex set
\mathcal{F}
, and three types of arrows: the up-arrows
\xrightarrow{\gamma}
and down-arrows
\xrightarrow{\delta}
from before, and a new set of arrows called “
G
-arrows”
F\xrightarrow{g}gF
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and every
g\in G
. Let
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
be the category with object set
\mathcal{F}
, with morphisms generated by this directed graph, modulo the previous relations (A-C) for up- and down-arrows, together with:

(D)

(F\xrightarrow{1}F)=\Id_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
;

(E)

(hF\xrightarrow{g}ghF)\circ(F\xrightarrow{h}hF)=(F\xrightarrow{gh}ghF)
for all
g,h\in G
,
F\in\mathcal{F}
;

(F)

(F_{2}\xrightarrow{g}gF_{2})\circ(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2})=(gF_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}gF_{2})\circ(F_{1}\xrightarrow{g}gF_{1})
for all
g\in G
,
F_{1}\leq F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
;

(G)

(F_{1}\xrightarrow{g}gF_{1})\circ(F_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1})=(gF_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}gF_{1})\circ(F_{2}\xrightarrow{g}gF_{2})
for all
g\in G
,
F_{1}\leq F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
.

By construction, the category
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
is isomorphic to the the category of functors
{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{Fac}}_{G},\Omega)
. Thus, if
\Omega
is an abelian category, then
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
inherits the structure of an abelian category.

1.3.
Restriction of equivariant bisheaves

Suppose that
G
acts on
(\mathcal{F},\leq)
as before. Let
\mathcal{F}^{+}
be a subposet of
\mathcal{F}
, i.e., a subset with the inherited partial order.

A
\Omega
-valued
G
-equivariant bisheaf on
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is a bisheaf
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
on the partially ordered set
\mathcal{F}^{+}
, together with a family of maps
\eta_{g,F}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{gF}
indexed by pairs
g\in G,F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
for which
gF\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
, satisfying the equivariance axioms (Eq1), (Eq2), (Eq3) in
all cases where the maps are defined (within
\mathcal{F}^{+}
)
.

Write
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{+},\Omega)
for the resulting category of
G
-equivariant bisheaves on
\mathcal{F}^{+}
.

If
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)
is a
G
-equivariant bisheaf on
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is a subposet, restriction yields a
G
-equivariant bisheaf on
\mathcal{F}^{+}
. This gives a functor,

\Res\colon{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)\rightarrow{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{+},\Omega).

One may give a functorial interpretation of
G
-equivariant bisheaves on
\mathcal{F}^{+}
, in the setting above. For this, consider the graph whose vertex set is
\mathcal{F}^{+}
, with up-arrows
F\xrightarrow{\gamma}F^{\prime}
and down-arrows
F^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\delta}F
for all
F\leq F^{\prime}
(when
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
), and with “
G
-arrows”
F\xrightarrow{\eta_{g}}gF
for all
g\in G
,
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
(when
F,gF\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
). Let
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
to be the category whose object set is
\mathcal{F}^{+}
, generated by this directed graph modulo the relations (A) – (G) whenever the facets are elements of
\mathcal{F}^{+}
.

By construction, there is an isomorphism of categories,

{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{+},\Omega)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+},\Omega).

There is an obvious functor from
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
. The objects of
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
form a subset of the objects of
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
. The generating set of morphisms of
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
is a subset of the generating set of morphisms of
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
, and the relations are a subset of the relations. But since the relations of
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
are, a priori, a subset of those arising from
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
, it is not clear whether
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
is a subcategory of
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
. I.e., faithfulness is not a consequence of the construction.

But through the functor
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
, restriction of
G
-equivariant bisheaves from
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{F}^{+}
corresponds to pullback of functors,

\Res\colon{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{Fac}}_{G},\Omega)\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+},\Omega).

In general, this restriction is not very well-behaved. But when
\mathcal{F}^{+}
carries enough information from
\mathcal{F}
, restriction does not lose much information.

A subset
\mathcal{F}^{+}\subset\mathcal{F}
is called a
G
-full subposet
if it satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

(closedness): if
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
,
F\leq F^{\prime}
, and
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
, then
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
.

(2)

(
G
-fullness) If
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then there exists
g\in G
such that
gF\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
.

Suppose that
G
acts on
(\mathcal{F},\leq)
, and
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is a
G
-full subposet of
\mathcal{F}
. Then restriction gives an equivalence of categories,

{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{+},\Omega).

It is easier to prove this theorem by viewing both categories as functor categories. From this perspective, restriction is given by pullback of functors,

\Res\colon{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{Fac}}_{G},\Omega)\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+},\Omega).

To show that
\Res
is an equivalence, we demonstrate that the inclusion functor
\Inc\colon{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
is an equivalence. And for this, we construct an inverse functor,

S\colon{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}.

The construction depends on the choice, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, of an element
n_{F}\in G
such that
n_{F}F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
. Such an element
n_{F}
is not uniquely determined by
F
, but we write
F^{+}=n_{F}F
in what follows. When
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
already, we choose
n_{F}=1
.

The functor
S
is defined on objects by
S(F)=F^{+}
. For morphisms, we must be more careful.

(1)

If
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
, write
n_{1}=n_{F_{1}}
and
n_{2}=n_{F_{2}}
for the moment. Then
n_{2}F_{1}\leq n_{2}F_{2}
, and thus
n_{2}F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
(by closedness). Therefore
F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{2}n_{1}^{-1}}n_{2}F_{1}
is a
G
-morphism in
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
. Define the functor
S
on up- and down-arrows by

S(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2})=(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}^{+})\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{2}n_{1}^{-1}}n_{2}F_{1}).

S(F_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1})=(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{n_{1}n_{2}^{-1}}F_{1}^{+})\circ(F_{2}^{+}\xrightarrow{\delta}n_{2}F_{1}).

(2)

If
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
g\in G
, then
F^{+}=n_{F}F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
(gF)^{+}=n_{(gF)}gF\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
. Define the functor
S
on
G
-arrows by

S(F\xrightarrow{g}gF)=(F^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{gF}\cdot g\cdot n_{F}^{-1}}(gF)^{+}).

In this way,
S
defines a functor from the free category on the directed graph with vertex set
\mathcal{F}
, up-arrows, down-arrows, and
G
-arrows, to the category
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
. To see that this defines a functor from
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
to
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
, we must check that relations (A)-(G) are respected.

To begin, if
F_{1}\leq F_{2}\leq F_{3}
, and the notation is as above,

\displaystyle S(F_{2}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{3})\circ S(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2})

\displaystyle=(n_{3}F_{2}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{3}^{+})\circ(F_{2}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{3}n_{2}^{-1}}n_{3}F_{2})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\circ(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}^{+})\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{2}n_{1}^{-1}}n_{2}F_{1}),

\displaystyle=(n_{3}F_{2}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{3}^{+})\circ(n_{3}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}n_{3}F_{2})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\circ(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{n_{3}n_{2}^{-1}}n_{3}F_{1})\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{2}n_{1}^{-1}}n_{2}F_{1}),\quad\text{(by F)}

\displaystyle=(n_{3}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{3}^{+})\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{3}n_{1}^{-1}}n_{3}F_{1}),\quad\text{ (by B, E)}

\displaystyle=S(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{3}).

Also, we have

\displaystyle S(\Id_{F})

\displaystyle=S(F\xrightarrow{\gamma}F)=(F^{+}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F^{+})\circ(F^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{F}n_{F}^{-1}}F^{+}),

\displaystyle=\Id_{F^{+}}\circ\Id_{F^{+}},\quad\text{(by A, D)}

\displaystyle=\Id_{F^{+}}

For down-arrows, the computations are very similar. This verifies that relations (A), (B), (C) are respected by
S
. Relation (D) is trivial to check. For relation (E), we have

\displaystyle S(hF\xrightarrow{g}ghF)\circ S(F\xrightarrow{h}hF)

\displaystyle=((hF)^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{ghF}\cdot g\cdot n_{hF}^{-1}}(ghF)^{+})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\circ(F^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{hF}\cdot h\cdot n_{F}^{-1}}(hF)^{+}),

\displaystyle=(F^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{ghF}\cdot gh\cdot n_{F}^{-1}}(ghF)^{+}),

\displaystyle=S(F\xrightarrow{gh}ghF).

For relation (F), the intertwining of
G
-arrows and up-arrows, we have

\displaystyle S(F_{2}\xrightarrow{g}gF_{2})\circ S(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2})

\displaystyle=(F_{2}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{gF_{2}}\cdot g\cdot n_{F_{2}}^{-1}}(gF_{2})^{+})\circ(n_{F_{2}}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}^{+})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{F_{2}}\cdot n_{F_{1}}^{-1}}n_{F_{2}}F_{1}),

\displaystyle=(n_{gF_{2}}gF_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}(gF_{2})^{+})\circ(n_{F_{2}}F_{1}\xrightarrow{n_{gF_{2}}\cdot g\cdot n_{F_{2}}^{-1}}n_{gF_{2}}gF_{1})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{F_{2}}\cdot n_{F_{1}}^{-1}}n_{F_{2}}F_{1}),

\displaystyle=(n_{gF_{2}}gF_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}(gF_{2})^{+})\circ((gF_{1})^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{gF_{2}}n_{gF_{1}}^{-1}}n_{gF_{2}}gF_{1})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{gF_{1}}\cdot g\cdot n_{F_{1}}^{-1}}(gF_{1})^{+}),\quad\text{ (by E)}

\displaystyle=S(gF_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}gF_{2})\circ S(F_{1}\xrightarrow{g}gF_{1}).

For the intertwining of
G
-arrows and down-arrows, the computations are similar. This verifies that relations (F) and (G) are respected by
S
, and thus we have defined a functor as claimed,

S\colon{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}.

By construction, we have
S\circ\Inc=\Id
, as functors from
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
to itself. It remains to see that
\Inc\circ S
is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor. Note that
[\Inc\circ S](F)=F^{+}
for all objects
F
of
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
. Define a natural isomorphism
N\colon\Id\Rightarrow\Inc\circ S
by putting

N(F)=(F\xrightarrow{n_{F}}F^{+}).

To see that this defines a natural isomorphism, we check the commutativity of diagrams in the category
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}
.

1.4.
Monotonic equivariant bisheaves

A bisheaf
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
on any poset
\mathcal{F}
is called
monotonic
if for all facets
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
,

\Id_{{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}}=\gamma_{F_{1},F_{2}}\circ\delta_{F_{2},F_{1}}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{1}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}.

Within the category of bisheaves, write
{\mathsf{BiSh}}^{\mon}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
for the full subcategory of
{\mathsf{BiSh}}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
whose objects are the monotonic bisheaves. Similarly, write
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}^{\mon}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
for the category of
G
-equivariant monotonic bisheaves.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is a
G
-full subposet of a poset
\mathcal{F}
with
G
-action. Then the restriction functor,

\Res\colon{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{+},\Omega),

restricts to an equivalence of categories,

\Res\colon{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}^{\mon}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}^{\mon}(\mathcal{F}^{+},\Omega).

The restriction functor sends monotonic bisheaves to monotonic bisheaves. The only thing to verify is that the inverse functor sends monotonic bisheaves to monotonic bisheaves. For this, we recall the functor
S\colon{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
(see the proof of Theorem
1.3.0
) and compute,

\displaystyle S(F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2})\circ S(F_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1})

\displaystyle=(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}^{+})\circ(F_{1}^{+}\xrightarrow{n_{2}n_{1}^{-1}}n_{2}F_{1})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\circ(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{n_{1}n_{2}^{-1}}F_{1}^{+})\circ(F_{2}^{+}\xrightarrow{\delta}n_{2}F_{1}),

\displaystyle=(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}^{+})\circ(F_{2}^{+}\xrightarrow{\delta}n_{2}F_{1}).\quad\text{(by (E))}

If
{\mathscr{V}}
is a monotonic bisheaf on
\mathcal{F}^{+}
, viewed as a functor
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}\rightarrow\Omega
, then
{\mathscr{V}}(\gamma_{F_{1},F_{2}}\circ\delta_{F_{2},F_{1}})=\Id_{{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}}
. The above computation shows that

[{\mathscr{V}}\circ S](F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta}F_{1})={\mathscr{V}}(n_{2}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\gamma}F_{2}^{+}\xrightarrow{\delta}n_{2}F_{1})=\Id.

Hence
{\mathscr{V}}\circ S=S^{\ast}{\mathscr{V}}
is a monotonic bisheaf as well.

\square

1.5.
Fundamental domains

We may strengthen the “
G
-fullness” condition, to define a notion of fundamental domain.

A subset
\mathcal{F}^{+}\subset\mathcal{F}
is called a
closed fundamental domain
if it satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

(closedness): if
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
,
F\leq F^{\prime}
, and
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
, then
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
.

(2)

If
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\{gF:g\in G\}\cap\mathcal{F}^{+}
has cardinality one.

Every closed fundamental domain in
\mathcal{F}
is also a
G
-full poset. When
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is a closed fundamental domain, the resulting category
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
is particularly nice. It is the category generated by up-arrows and down-arrows, as before, together with the following
G
-arrows: for every
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
, let
G_{F}
be the stabilizer of
F
in
G
. Then the
G
-arrows generating
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
are precisely the arrows
F\xrightarrow{g}F
, for
F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
g\in G_{F}
. In this way, the category
{\mathsf{Fac}}_{G}^{+}
can be thought of as a “stacky quotient”
[{\mathsf{Fac}}/G]
. From this perspective, it may not be surprising that
G
-equivariant bisheaves on
\mathcal{F}
can be interpreted as bisheaves on this stacky quotient
[{\mathsf{Fac}}/G]
.

In what follows, we apply this to the case of Coxeter arrangements, reducing bisheaves on the reflection representation of a Coxeter group
W
to objects on a closed chamber.

2.
Coxeter arrangements

Our treatment of Coxeter arrangements, for finite or affine Coxeter groups, follows Bourbaki
[
Bou02
, Chapter 5, §3]
. Let
E
be a real affine space of dimension
d
, whose space of translations is endowed with a Euclidean metric. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of hyperplanes in
E
, and
W
the group of affine-linear automorphisms of
E
generated by reflections across these hyperplanes. Assume the two conditions, called (D1), (D2) in Bourbaki,

(D1)

For every
w\in W
and
H\in\mathcal{H}
, the hyperplane
w(H)
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
.

(D2)

If
K
and
K^{\prime}
are compact subsets of
E
, then
\{w\in W:w(K)\cap K^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}
is finite.

It follows that the set of hyperplanes is locally finite in
E
. The set of hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}
gives an equivalence relation on
E
, by declaring
x\sim y
if for all
H\in\mathcal{H}

x,y\in H
or
x,y
lie on the same side of
H
. The equivalence classes for this relation are called
facets
, and we write
\mathcal{F}
for the set of facets. We have
E=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F
. A
chamber
is a facet which is contained in no hyperplane from
\mathcal{H}
.

If
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
, we write
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
if
F_{1}
is contained in the closure of
F_{2}
. Every facet is contained in the closure of some chamber. Every facet
F
has a well-defined dimension – the dimension of the minimal affine subspace of
E
containing
F
. The chambers are the facets of dimension
d=\dim(E)
.

Let
C
be a chamber. A facet
F\leq C
is called a
face
of
C
if
\dim(F)=d-1
. A hyperplane
H\in\mathcal{H}
is called a

of
C
if there exists a face
F\leq C
such that

. Let
S
be the set of reflections with respect to the walls of
C
. Then in
[
Bou02
, Chapter 5, §3.2]
, it is verified that
(W,S)
is a Coxeter system. This means

(1)

S
is a finite set which generates
W
.

(2)

For all
s\in S
, we have
s^{2}=1
.

(3)

For any pair
s,s^{\prime}\in S
, write
m_{s,s^{\prime}}
for the order of
ss^{\prime}
in
W
. Then a presentation of
W
is given by the set of generators
S
and the set of relations
\{(ss^{\prime})^{m_{s,s^{\prime}}}:m_{s,s^{\prime}}<\infty\}
.

For this reason, we call
(E,\mathcal{H})
a
Coxeter arrangement
. When
(E,\mathcal{H})
is a Coxeter arrangement, and

is a chamber, we have constructed a Coxeter system
(W,S)
and a poset
\mathcal{F}
endowed with a
W
-action.

2.1.

Fix a Coxeter arrangement

to

.

. For surjectivity, consider a facet

contains at least one 0-dimensional facet

. It follows that

is contained in the set

of walls containing

. But the reflections through walls containing

is finitary. Hence

is finitary.

2.2.
Modules and algebras

Let
R
be a (commutative, unital) ring, and
\Omega
the category of
R
-modules. Suppose that
(W,S)
is a
finite
Coxeter system, so that
\Lambda
is the power set of
S
. In this case, the category
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{W}^{\mon}(\Lambda,\Omega)
has a concrete interpretation.

Let
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)
be a monotonic,
W
-equivariant
\Omega
-valued bisheaf on
\Lambda
. Thus, for every
I\in\Lambda
, we have an
R
-module
{\mathscr{V}}_{I}
. The condition of monotonicity states that

\gamma_{I,J}\circ\delta_{J,I}=\Id\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{J}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{J}\text{ for all }I\supset J.

It follows that
\delta_{J,I}
is injective and
\gamma_{I,J}
is surjective.

Define the following data from
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)
:

•

Let
V={\mathscr{V}}_{S}
, the
R
-module associated to the unique vertex in
\bar{C}
.

•

For all
I\subset S
, define
e_{I}=\delta_{I,S}\circ\gamma_{S,I}\in\End(V)
. Monotonicity implies that
e_{I}
is idempotent.

•

For
w\in W
, write
w
for the automorphism
\eta(w)\in\Aut(V)=\Aut({\mathscr{V}}_{S})
arising from the equivariant structure on
{\mathscr{V}}
.

The equivariant bisheaf axioms imply the following properties of the idempotents
e_{I}
and automorphisms
\eta(w)
.

(1)

If
I\supset J
then
e_{I}e_{J}=e_{J}
.

(2)

If
s\in I
, then
se_{I}=e_{I}s
.

Motivated by this, let
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}
be the
R
-algebra
R\langle e_{I}:I\in\Lambda,\ s:s\in S\rangle
modulo the relations

(1)

If
I\supset J
then
e_{I}e_{J}=e_{J}
.

(2)

If
s\in I
then
se_{I}=e_{I}s
.

(3)

s^{2}=1
for all
s\in S
.

(4)

(ss^{\prime})^{m_{s,s^{\prime}}}=1
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S
with
m_{s,s^{\prime}}<\infty
.

The construction above gives an equivalence of categories, from
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{W}^{\mon}(\Lambda,\Omega)
to the category of modules over
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}

The construction described above gives a functor
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)\mapsto V
from
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{W}^{\mon}(\Lambda,\Omega)
to
{\mathsf{Mod}}(\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon})
.

From an
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}
-module
V
, one may reconstruct an equivariant bisheaf
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)
according to the following recipe. Write
{\mathscr{V}}_{I}=e_{I}V
. Define
\delta_{J,I}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{J}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{I}
by
\delta(v)=v
, whenever
I\supset J
, i.e.,
\delta_{J,I}
is the inclusion map. Define
\gamma_{I,J}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{I}\rightarrow{\mathscr{V}}_{J}
by
\gamma_{I,J}(v)=e_{J}v
. The bisheaf conditions are clear, as they follow from the conditions
e_{I}e_{J}=e_{J}
for
I\supset J
. We have constructed a bisheaf

on the poset
\Lambda
.

The relations (3) and (4) in

are precisely the Coxeter relations in

, and so every
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}
-module becomes a
W
-module. In this way,

inherits “

-module. The commutativity

-module. This is precisely the data of a

.

This construction gives an inverse functor
{\mathsf{Mod}}(\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon})\rightarrow{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{W}^{\mon}(\Lambda,\Omega)
.

\square

3.3.
Perverse sheaves

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a locally finite real hyperplane arrangement in the real affine space
E
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the resulting facet decomposition. Let
EC
be the complexification of
E
, and let
\mathcal{H}C
be the set of complexifications of the real hyperplanes from
\mathcal{H}
. Thus
\mathcal{H}C
is a locally finite complex hyperplane arrangement in the complex affine space
EC
.

Let
{\mathsf{Perv}}(EC,\mathcal{H}C)
be the abelian category of perverse sheaves on
EC
, smooth with respect to the stratification induced by
\mathcal{H}C
. We consider sheaves with coefficients in finite-dimensional complex vector spaces, for convenience. The main theorem of Kapranov and Schechtman
[
KS16
, Theorem 8.1, §9.B]
is the following.

The category
{\mathsf{Perv}}(EC,\mathcal{H}C)
is equivalent to the category of monotonic, transitive, invertible bisheaves of finite-dimensional
\mathbb C
-vector spaces on
\mathcal{F}
.

Note that the transitive and invertible properties depend not just on the poset
\mathcal{F}
, but on a bit more geometric structure (a notion of collinearity, at least). In
[
KS16
]
, it is noted that the notions of transitivity and invertibility can be defined if one considers not just the poset
\mathcal{F}
but also the oriented matroid structure arising from
\mathcal{H}
.

From this theorem, we believe it makes sense to
define
the category
{\mathsf{Perv}}(E,\mathcal{H})
(without complexification) as the full subcategory of bisheaves
{\mathsf{BiSh}}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
whose objects are monotonic, transitive, and invertible. Here
\Omega
denotes the category of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces. With this notation, the main theorem of
[
KS16
]
is the construction of an equivalence of abelian categories,

{\mathsf{Perv}}(EC,\mathcal{H}C)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathsf{Perv}}(E,\mathcal{H}).

The category on the left is of considerable interest to those who study the topology of algebraic varieties (and related representation theory). The category on the right is amenable to study with linear algebra and combinatorics.

If a group
G
acts on
E
, and stabilizes (i.e., permutes) the set of hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}
, one may consider
G
-equivariant perverse sheaves. In the real, combinatorial setting, we define
{\mathsf{Perv}}_{G}(E,\mathcal{H})
to be the full subcategory of
{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}(\mathcal{F},\Omega)
consisting of monotonic, transitive, invertible bisheaves. In the complex setting, a
G
-equivariant perverse sheaf is a perverse sheaf
{\mathscr{S}}\in{\mathsf{Perv}}(EC,\mathcal{H}C)
endowed with isomorphisms
\iota_{g}\colon{\mathscr{S}}\rightarrow g^{\ast}{\mathscr{S}}
, where
g^{\ast}
denotes the pullback via
g\colon EC\rightarrow EC
. This family of isomorphisms is required to satisfy compatibility conditions with the group structure on
G
, of course. Note here that we consider
G
as a discrete group, without any structure as a variety. Since the
G
-action respects complexification, the equivalence of Kapranov and Schechtman gives an equivalence of abelian categories,

{\mathsf{Perv}}_{G}(EC,\mathcal{H}C)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathsf{Perv}}_{G}(E,\mathcal{H}).

4.

-equivariant perverse sheaves on the Coxeter arrangement

Let us return to the case of a
finite
Coxeter system
(W,S)
, with

the (real) reflection representation of

and
\mathcal{H}
the set of reflection hyperplanes, and

the chamber associated to the set

. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the resulting facet decomposition of

The case of an

4.1.
The condition of invertibility

The following result translates the condition of invertibility into a condition on modules over
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}
. We consider invertibility before transitivity, since the corresponding condition on modules is simpler.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}
is the
\mathbb C
-algebra
\mathbb C\langle e_{I}:I\in\Lambda,\ s:s\in S\rangle
modulo the relations

(1)

If
I\supset J
then
e_{I}e_{J}=e_{J}
.

(2)

If
s\in I
then
se_{I}=e_{I}s

(3)

s^{2}=1

s\in S

(4)

(ss^{\prime})^{m_{s,s^{\prime}}}=1
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S

If
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)

W
-equivariant bisheaf on

the vector space

Relations (3) and (4) are those in the Coxeter group

and we view every element

\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}

Proposition
2.2.0
demonstrates that this gives an equivalence of categories, from

to

4.2.
Transitive bisheaves

The condition of transitivity is more complicated to translate into a condition on modules. This complication is already recognized, and connected to word-lengths in
W
, by Kapranov and Schechtman in
[
KS16
, §0.5]
. Part of the challenge is that the transitivity condition is
non-local
– collinear triples can be somewhat far apart, and so
W
-equivariance can only be used to move one facet of a triple
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})
into the closed Weyl chamber. But from there, collinearity has a well-known relation to minimal galleries in the Coxeter arrangement; see, for example
[
Bro89
, §VI.6, Remark]
. Minimal galleries then relate to reduced words in the Weyl group, e.g., by
[
Tit74
, Proposition 2.12, Corollary 2.23]
. We apply this combinatorial geometry below.

Define
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\trans}
as the quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\mon}
by the set of relations below.

(5)

Let
S=I\sqcup J
be a partition of
S
, and let
A
and
B
be subsets of
I
. Write
w_{A},w_{B}
for the longest elements in the Coxeter groups
(W_{A},A)
and
(W_{B},B)
, respectively. Then if
w,w_{1},w_{2}\in W_{I}
, and
w=w_{2}w_{1}
, and

\ell(ww_{B}w_{A})=\ell(ww_{B}w^{-1})+\ell(w_{2})+\ell(w_{1})+\ell(w_{A}),

the relation (associated to the data
(I,J,A,B,w_{1},w_{2})
) is

e_{A\cup J}\cdot w_{1}\cdot e_{J}\cdot w_{2}\cdot e_{B\cup J}=e_{A\cup J}\cdot w\cdot e_{B\cup J}.

Before proceeding, we note two consequences of (5). First, suppose
w=w_{1}=w_{2}=1
, and
A
and
B
are disjoint subsets of
I
. Note that
\ell(w_{A}w_{B})=\ell(w_{A})+\ell(w_{B})
in this case (it follows from
[
BB05
, Proposition 2.4.4]
, for example), so relation (5) implies

e_{A\cup J}e_{J}e_{B\cup J}=e_{A\cup J}e_{B\cup J}.

But the left side is
e_{J}
by (1). Note that
J=(A\cup J)\cap(B\cup J)
since
A
and
B

(1’)

If
I\cap J=K
then
e_{I}e_{J}=e_{K}
.

Instead of taking

(

For

This follows from the relation (
5_{\emptyset}

satisfy the braid relations.

\square

\ell(ww_{B}w_{A})=\ell(ww_{B}w^{-1})+\ell(w_{2})+\ell(w_{1})+\ell(w_{A}).

The map in one direction has been unambiguously defined, since the triple

determines

, indepently of the choice of line

In the other direction, given

We have now proven that
{\mathscr{V}}
is transitive if and only if, for all data

as above, there is an equality of maps,

\phi_{w_{1}F_{J},w_{2}w_{1}F_{B\cup J}}\circ\phi_{F_{A\cup J},w_{1}F_{J}}=\phi_{F_{A\cup J},wF_{B\cup J}}.

(4.2.1)

We translate this into conditions on the

-module

in a few steps. First, note that we have

. Thus the right side of (

), applied to a general element

\phi_{F_{A\cup J},wF_{B\cup J}}(e_{A\cup J}v)=\gamma_{\circ,wF_{B\cup J}}(e_{A\cup J}v)=e_{B\cup J}w\cdot e_{A\cup J}v.

For the left side of (

), we compute

\displaystyle\phi_{w_{1}F_{J},w_{2}w_{1}F_{B\cup J}}\circ\phi_{F_{A\cup J},w_{1}F_{J}}(e_{A\cup J}v)

\displaystyle=\phi_{w_{1}F_{J},w_{2}w_{1}F_{B\cup J}}\left(e_{J}w_{1}\cdot e_{A\cup J}v\right),

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\circ,w_{2}w_{1}F_{B\cup J}}\delta_{w_{1}F_{J},\circ}\left(e_{J}w_{1}\cdot e_{A\cup J}v\right),

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\circ,w_{2}w_{1}F_{B\cup J}}\left(w_{1}^{-1}\cdot e_{J}w_{1}\cdot e_{A\cup J}v\right),

\displaystyle=e_{B\cup J}w_{2}w_{1}\cdot w_{1}^{-1}\cdot e_{J}w_{1}\cdot e_{A\cup J}v,

\displaystyle=e_{B\cup J}\cdot w_{2}\cdot e_{J}\cdot w_{1}\cdot e_{A\cup J}v.

Therefore,

is a transitive bisheaf if and only if the

-module

satisfies relation (5) for all appropriate

\square

4.3.
Perverse sheaves

Together with Kapranov and Schechtman’s theorem (Theorem

The category

(5)

Let

At least a few words are in order about the algebra described above. We are localizing the finitely-generated
\mathbb C
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\trans}
generated by
\{e_{I}:I\in\Lambda,s:s\in S\}

\mathcal{A}_{W}^{\trans}

. The localization which we call

is the universal

-algebra, endowed with a homomorphism

•

If

is invertible.

•

If

is another

-algebra homomorphism such that

making the obvious diagram commute.

Such a universal

exists, for purely abstract reasons (the Cohn localization). In general, noncommutative localization can behave badly (e.g., it could yield the zero ring). In this article, we do not attempt to prove that this localization behaves well, e.g., we do not attempt to prove the Ore conditions. But the localization does not behave
too
badly since

contains some interesting nontrivial subrings.

By construction (see relations (3), (4) above), there is a canonical injective ring homomorphism

\mathbb C[W]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{W},

sending each generator

\mathcal{A}_{W}

\mathbb C[W]\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{W}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{W}/(e_{I}:I\neq S)

There is also an injective ring homomorphism

\frac{\mathbb C[e_{I}:I\in\Lambda]}{e_{I}e_{J}=e_{I\cap J}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{W}.

The algebra on the left is commutative, generated by the idempotents
e_{S-\{s\}}
for all

-module

can be viewed as a
W

-module structure and gradation partially commute: the graded piece

becomes a module for the parabolic subgroup
W_{I}
.

We have assumed that

is a finite Coxeter group in this theorem. In the affine setting, we expect a similar result, with the power set
\Lambda

which generate a finite subgroup of

4.4.
Representations of
W
and the braid group

The simplest
W
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
EC
are those supported at
\{0\}
. Such a perverse sheaf corresponds to a
\mathbb C[W]
-module
V
. The corresponding module over
\mathcal{A}_{W}
is the one with the same underlying
\mathbb C[W]
-module
V
and on which every non-identity idempotent
e_{I}
(i.e., those with
I\neq S
) acts as the zero endomorphism.

More interesting are the perverse sheaves arising from local systems on the open stratum. Let us denote
EC^{\circ}=\left(EC-\bigcupop\displaylimits_{H\in\mathcal{H}}HC\right)
the open stratum in
EC
. Write
j\colon EC^{\circ}\hookrightarrow EC
for the inclusion. Choose a point
c\in C
(the open chamber in
E
) to serve as a base point in
EC^{\circ}
.

If
{\mathscr{V}}
is a perverse sheaf on
EC
, smooth with respect to
\mathcal{H}C
, then the pullback
j^{\ast}{\mathscr{V}}
is a local system (up to shifting by dimension) on
EC^{\circ}
. Write
{\mathscr{L}}=j^{\ast}{\mathscr{V}}[d]
for this local system. When
{\mathscr{V}}
is
W
-equivariant,
{\mathscr{L}}
is a
W
-equivariant local system. In the other direction, if
{\mathscr{L}}
is a
W
-equivariant local system on
EC^{\circ}
, then the intermediate extension functor defines a
W
-equivariant perverse sheaf
j_{!\ast}{\mathscr{L}}[d]
on
EC
.

Since

acts freely on

, the category of
W
-equivariant local systems on

is equivalent to the category of local systems on the quotient

. This, in turn, is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional complex representations of the

\Gamma_{W}=\pi_{1}(X_{W},x),

where

is the image of

in
X_{W}
. The topology of

and structure of the braid group

is by now a classical story. The space

is the “space of regular orbits” for the (complex) reflection group

, and its fundamental group

was studied by Brieskorn
[
Bri71
]

\Gamma_{W}\twoheadrightarrow W

[
Bri71
, Satz]
proves that
\Gamma_{W}
is generated by elements
\{\gamma_{s}:s\in S\}
, with
\gamma_{s}\in\Gamma_{W}
lying over

, subject to the braid relations:

\underbrace{\gamma_{s}\gamma_{t}\gamma_{s}\cdots}_{m_{s,t}\text{ terms}}=\underbrace{\gamma_{t}\gamma_{s}\gamma_{t}\cdots}_{m_{s,t}\text{ terms}}.

In this way, a
W
-equivariant local system on
EC^{\circ}
is given by a finite-dimensional representation
M
of
\Gamma_{W}
, which is the same as a space
M
endowed with invertible monodromy operators

for all
s\in S
, satisfying the braid relations above.

Let
{\mathscr{V}}
be a
W
-equivariant perverse sheaf on
EC
, smooth with respect to
\mathcal{H}C
. Write
V
for the resulting
\mathcal{A}_{W}
-module. Write
(j^{\ast}{\mathscr{V}})_{W}
for the local system on

obtained from the
W
-equivariant local system

on
EC^{\circ}
. Then

corresponds to the vector space

with monodromy operators

\mu_{s}=e_{\emptyset}se_{\emptyset}.

Write
{\mathscr{V}}
also for the
W
-equivariant bisheaf on
\mathcal{F}
associated to
{\mathscr{V}}
. Then, following the comment of
[
KS16
, §0.3 (d)]
, the stalk of

at the base point

(an open Weyl chamber) coincides with the space
M:={\mathscr{V}}_{C}=e_{\emptyset}V
.

The monodromy operators

are determined by the “half-monodromy” operators on the bisheaf

, according to a recipe found in
[
KS16

. Namely, if

sF_{\emptyset}\mid_{F_{\{s\}}}F_{\emptyset}.

Following loc. cit., define
E_{+}={\mathscr{V}}_{F_{\emptyset}}

E_{-}={\mathscr{V}}_{sF_{\emptyset}}

\gamma_{\pm}\colon E_{0}\rightarrow E_{\pm}

\delta_{\pm}\colon E_{\pm}\rightarrow E_{0}

.

In terms of

-modules,

\gamma_{-}\delta_{+}\colon E_{+}\rightarrow E_{-}

The half-monodromy operator is an element of the fundamental groupoid of

The half-monodromy in the fundamental groupoid of

\square

Note, from Theorem

5.
Perverse sheaves on buildings

If
\mathcal{H}
is a hyperplane arrangement in a real affine space
E
, with resulting facet decomposition
\mathcal{F}
, recall that
{\mathsf{Perv}}(E,\mathcal{H})
denotes the category of monotonic, transitive, invertible bisheaves on
\mathcal{F}
. This is the category which Kapranov and Schechtman prove equivalent to the category
{\mathsf{Perv}}(EC,\mathcal{H}C)
of perverse sheaves on the complex space
EC
, smooth with respect to the hyperplane stratification given by
\mathcal{H}C
.

As affine buildings are built from real hyperplane arrangements (apartments), we extend the category
{\mathsf{Perv}}(E,\mathcal{H})
to give a category of “perverse sheaves on a building”. Buildings themselves do not seem to have a natural complexification, glued from complex hyperplane arrangements. But
categories
of perverse sheaves on each apartment can be glued nonetheless.

5.1.
Perverse sheaves on hyperplane complexes

Before specializing to buildings, we study perverse sheaves on more general spaces glued from hyperplane arrangements. We define a
hyperplane complex
to be a structure
(\mathcal{B},\{E_{i},\mathcal{H}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a topological space endowed with a topological decomposition
\mathcal{B}=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i\in I}E_{i}
into closed subspaces
E_{i}
called
apartments
, and each
E_{i}
is endowed with the structure of a real affine space together with a locally finite set of affine hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Write
\mathcal{F}_{i}
for the set of facets for the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{i}
in
E_{i}
. For this triple to be a hyperplane complex, we require the following axioms.

(1)

For all
F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
and
F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
,
F_{i}\cap F_{j}
is empty or
F_{i}=F_{j}
(they are equal as subspaces of
\mathcal{B}
). In this way, it makes sense to write
\mathcal{F}=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\mathcal{F}_{i}
for the poset of facets of
\mathcal{B}
. We write
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
in
\mathcal{B}
if there exists an apartment
E
such that
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
in
E
.

(2)

For any two facets
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists an apartment
E
such that
F_{1},F_{2}\subset E
.

(3)

Let
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
be two apartments in
\mathcal{B}
, and let
F_{1},F_{2}
be two facets in
\mathcal{B}
. If
F_{1},F_{2}\subset E_{i}
and
F_{1},F_{2}\subset E_{j}
, then there exists an isomorphism of real affine spaces
\phi\colon E_{i}\rightarrow E_{j}
such that
\phi(\mathcal{H}_{i})=\mathcal{H}_{j}
and
\phi
fixes
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
pointwise.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a hyperplane complex, and
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
two points. Then
p_{1},p_{2}
belong to unique facets
F_{1},F_{2}
of
\mathcal{B}
, and by (2) there exists an apartment
E
containing both
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
. Write
\overline{p_{1}p_{2}}
for the geodesic (line segment) in the affine space
E
joining
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
. Axiom (3) implies that the geodesic
\overline{p_{1}p_{2}}
depends only on
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, and not on the choice of apartment.

Every hyperplane complex
\mathcal{B}
yields a poset
\mathcal{F}
of facets. Thus we may consider the abelian category of (monotonic) bisheaves on
\mathcal{B}
, with coefficients in finite-dimensional complex vector spaces as usual. A monotonic bisheaf
{\mathscr{V}}
on
\mathcal{B}
is called
transitive
if its restriction to every apartment is transitive. Similarly, it is called
invertible
if its restriction to every apartment is invertible. In this way, we define the category
{\mathsf{Perv}}(\mathcal{B})
of
perverse sheaves
on the hyperplane complex
\mathcal{B}
, as the category of monotonic, transitive, and invertible bisheaves on
\mathcal{B}
.

5.2.
Bisheaves from depth zero representations

In
[
SS97
]
, Schneider and Stuhler begin with a smooth representation
\pi
of a
p
-adic group
G
, and construct an equivariant sheaf and an equivariant cosheaf on the building. An irreducible smooth representation
\pi
can be recovered in the cohomology or homology thereof. Using their sheaves, Schneider and Stuhler give a beautiful recipe to understand the character of an irreducible representation
\pi
(on elliptic elements) and shed light on a mysterious duality of Bernstein, Zelevinski, and Aubert.

Here we demonstrate that Schneider and Stuhler’s construction gives not just a sheaf and cosheaf on the building, but when
\pi
has

, their construction gives a perverse sheaf on the building.

We fix a bit of notation for what follows. Let
k
be a nonarchimedean local field. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers in
k
,
\mathfrak{m}
the maximal ideal in
\mathcal{O}
, and
\mathbb F_{q}
the residue field
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
(of cardinality
q
). Let
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}
be a connected reductive group over
k
, and
G=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}(k)
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the enlarged (accounting for the center) Bruhat–Tits building of
G
. If
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}}
is a maximal
k
-split torus in
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}
, we write
\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}})
for the associated apartment,
\Phi(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}})
for the set of roots of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}
with respect to
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}}
(over
k
), and
\Psi(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}})
for the set of affine roots.

Each apartment
\mathcal{A}
in the building
\mathcal{B}
is (after a base point is chosen) a real vector space, endowed with an affine hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}
from the vanishing loci of affine roots. Let
(\mathcal{F},\leq)
be the poset of facets in the building
\mathcal{B}
. The building is an example of a hyperplane complex, in the terminology of the previous section.

For every facet
F\in\mathcal{F}
, write
G_{F}
for the corresponding parahoric subgroup, following the convention that
G_{F}=\underline{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}}_{F}(\mathcal{O})
for the Bruhat–Tits group scheme
\underline{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}}_{F}
over
\mathcal{O}
with
connected
special fibre
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{G}}}_{F}
. Write
G_{F}^{+}
for the pro-unipotent radical of
G_{F}
. If
F\leq F^{\prime}
then

G_{F}^{+}\subset G_{F^{\prime}}^{+}\subset G_{F^{\prime}}\subset G_{F}.

Now let
(\pi,V)
be a smooth representation of
G=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}(k)
. For every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, define
{\mathscr{V}}_{F}=V^{G_{F}^{+}}
, the space of
G_{F}^{+}
-fixed vectors. For
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
, inclusion gives a map,

\delta_{F_{2},F_{1}}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}=V^{G_{F_{2}}^{+}}\hookrightarrow V^{G_{F_{1}}^{+}}={\mathscr{V}}_{F_{1}}.

On the other hand, projection gives a map,

\gamma_{F_{1},F_{2}}\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F_{1}}=V^{G_{F_{1}}^{+}}\twoheadrightarrow V^{G_{F_{2}}^{+}}={\mathscr{V}}_{F_{2}}.

Explicitly,

\gamma_{F_{1},F_{2}}(v)=\frac{1}{\Vol(G_{F_{2}}^{+})}\intop\nolimits_{G_{F_{2}}^{+}}\pi(g)v\penalty\ dg.

The axioms for a bisheaf are easily checked in this case. In fact,
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
is a monotonic bisheaf. For if
K_{1}\subset K_{2}
are compact open subgroups of
G
, then inclusion followed by projection – from
K_{2}
-fixed vectors to
K_{1}
-fixed vectors to
K_{2}
-fixed vectors – is the identity map.

This can be extended to an exact functor,

{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}(G)\rightarrow{\mathsf{BiSh}}^{\mon}(\mathcal{B}).

Here
{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}(G)
denotes the category of smooth representations of
G
and
G
-intertwining maps, and
{\mathsf{BiSh}}^{\mon}(\mathcal{F})
denotes the category of monotonic bisheaves of complex vector spaces. The functor is exact, as the operation of taking fixed vectors under a profinite group is exact.

After Moy and Prasad,
[
MP94
]
, an
irreducible
smooth representation
(\pi,V)
has
depth zero
if
V^{G_{F}^{+}}\neq 0
for some
F\in\mathcal{F}
; otherwise, we say it has positive depth. More generally, any smooth representation
(\pi,V)
is said to have depth zero if all irreducible subquotients of
(\pi,V)
have depth zero. The abelian category
{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}(G)
splits as a direct product
{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}(G)={\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}_{0}(G)\times{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}_{>0}(G)
, where the first factor denotes the full subcategory of depth zero representations. An object of the second factor
{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}_{>0}(G)
is a smooth representation, all of whose irreducible subquotients have positive depth. See
[
BKV16
, §1.2(c), 6.2]
for more details on splitting the category. The
depth zero projector
sends every smooth representation to its maximal summand of depth zero.

The functor
{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}(G)\rightarrow{\mathsf{BiSh}}^{\mon}(\mathcal{B})
sends irreducible depth zero representations to nonzero bisheaves. If an irreducible representation
(\pi,V)
has positive depth, then it has no
G_{F}^{+}
-fixed vectors at any facet
F
, and hence the functor sends
(\pi,V)
to zero. Hence the functor
{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}(G)\rightarrow{\mathsf{BiSh}}^{\mon}(\mathcal{B})
factors through the depth zero projector (cf. the recent work of Barbasch, Ciubotaru, and Moy
[
BCM16
]
).

The bisheaf
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
associated to a smooth representation
(\pi,V)
has a natural
G
-equivariant structure. Indeed, if
g\in G
, then
\pi(g)
induces an isomorphism,

\eta_{g,F}:=\pi(g)\colon{\mathscr{V}}_{F}=V^{G_{F}^{+}}\rightarrow V^{gG_{F}^{+}g^{-1}}=V^{G_{g(F)}^{+}}={\mathscr{V}}_{g(F)}.

The axioms for a
G
-equivariant structure are straightforward to check, and we find that
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)
is a
G
-equivariant monotonic bisheaf on
\mathcal{F}
. This gives a functor,

{\mathsf{Rep}}^{\sm}(G)\rightarrow{\mathsf{BiSh}}_{G}^{\mon}(\mathcal{B}).

This entire construction is the same as the construction of Schneider and Stuhler
[
SS97
]
, in a slightly different language. The primary difference is that we consider the sheaf and cosheaf structure simultaneously, and we verify further axioms on the bisheaf below.

5.3.
Invertibility

The proof of invertibility is a bit easier than the proof of transitivity, so this is where we begin. On the other hand, the proof given here of invertibiliy relies on deeper results in the representation theory of finite groups of Lie type. In particular, it is not clear how the condition of invertibility, and thus the construction of perverse sheaves, might be adapted beyond depth zero.

If
(\pi,V)
is a smooth representation of
G
, then the associated bisheaf
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
on
\mathcal{B}
is invertible.

The proof is the same as
[
MP96
, Proposition 6.1 (2)]
, and we review the structural details following their treatment. Let
F_{1},F_{2}
be facets of the same dimension
r
, lying in a common
r
-dimensional flat in some apartment, on opposite sides of an
(r-1)
-dimensional face
F_{0}
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{G}}}_{0}
be the reductive group over
\mathbb F_{q}
which occurs as the reductive quotient of the special fibre for the parahoric at
F_{0}
. Then there exist
associate
parabolic subgroups
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}}}_{1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{M}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}}}_{2}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{M}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{2}
in the reductive group
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{G}}}_{0}
, with identifications

G_{F_{1}}/G_{F_{0}}^{+}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}}}_{1}(\mathbb F_{q}),\quad G_{F_{2}}/G_{F_{0}}^{+}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}}}_{2}(\mathbb F_{q}),\text{ and }G_{F_{1}}/G_{F_{1}}^{+}=G_{F_{2}}/G_{F_{2}}^{+}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{M}}}(\mathbb F_{q}).

Define
V_{i}={\mathscr{V}}_{F_{i}}=V^{G_{F_{i}}^{+}}
, for
i=0,1,2
. Then
V_{0}
is a representation of
G_{F_{0}}
, and
V_{1},V_{2}
are the fixed vectors in
V_{0}
for the actions of
G_{F_{1}}^{+},G_{F_{2}}^{+}
respectively. These representations are trivial on
G_{F_{0}}^{+}
however, so we may instead say that
V_{0}
is a representation of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{G}}}_{0}(\mathbb F_{q})
, and
V_{1},V_{2}
are the fixed vectors for
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{1}(\mathbb F_{q})=G_{F_{1}}^{+}/G_{F_{0}}^{+}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{2}(\mathbb F_{q})=G_{F_{2}}^{+}/G_{F_{0}}^{+}
, respectively.

The map
\gamma_{F_{0},F_{2}}\delta_{F_{1},F_{0}}\colon V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
can now be identified with the map

v\mapsto\frac{1}{\#\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{2}(\mathbb F_{q})}\cdot\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{2}(\mathbb F_{q})}\pi(n)\cdot v.

By
[
MP96
, Proposition 6.1 (2)]
, following Howlett and Lehrer
[
HL94
]
, this map is an isomorphism from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
.

\square

5.4.
The transitive property

The following proposition was proven in collaboration with Jessica Fintzen.

If
(\pi,V)
is a smooth representation of
G
, then the associated monotonic bisheaf
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta)
on
\mathcal{B}
is transitive.

In order to prove that
{\mathscr{V}}
is transitive, we must consider a collinear triple
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})
of facets, all contained in a star neighborhood
\Star(F_{0})
in the building
\mathcal{B}
. Thus
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
correspond to a triple of parabolic subgroups

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}_{1}}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{M}}}_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{1},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}_{2}}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{M}}}_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{2},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}_{3}}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{M}}}_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{3}\subset\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{G}}}_{0}.

In what follows, we write
\bar{N}_{j}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{N}}}_{j}(\mathbb F_{q})
.

Write
V_{0}={\mathscr{V}}_{F_{0}}=V^{G_{F_{0}}^{+}}
, and similarly for
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
. As in the previous section,
V_{0}
is naturally a representation of
\bar{G}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{G}}}_{0}(\mathbb F_{q})
. All computations will take place within subspaces of
V_{0}
, since indeed,

V_{j}={\mathscr{V}}_{F_{j}}=V_{0}^{\bar{N}_{j}^{+}}\text{ for }j=1,2,3.

For
1\leq i<j\leq 3
, the map
\phi_{i,j}:=\phi_{F_{i},F_{j}}
is simply averaging over
\bar{N}_{j}
.

\phi_{i,j}(v)=\frac{1}{\#\bar{N}_{j}}\cdot\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\bar{N}_{j}}\pi(n)\cdot v\text{ for all }v\in V_{i}.

Choose an apartment containing
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
. This is possible, since any apartment containing
F_{1}
and
F_{3}
will contain their closure, hence
F_{0}
, and any apartment containing
F_{1}
and
F_{3}
will also contain facets along geodesics joining points of
F_{1}
to
F_{3}
, and hence will contain
F_{2}
along the way.

The choice of apartment determines a maximal
k
-split torus
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}}\subset\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}
, and a corresponding torus
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{S}}}\subset\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{G}}}_{j}
, so that the parabolics
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\bar{P}}}_{j}
are standard.

For any affine root
\psi
, let
U_{\psi}
be the affine root subgroup as defined in
[
MP94
, §2.4]
. In particular, note that
U_{\psi}\supset U_{2\psi}
when the gradient of
\psi
is multipliable. Following
[
MP94
, §2.6]
, each group
G_{F_{i}}^{+}
is generated by
\{U_{\psi}:\psi(F_{i})>0\}
together with a subset of
Z(S)
, the centralizer of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}}(k)
. Write
\bar{U}_{\psi}
for the image of
U_{\psi}
modulo
G_{F_{0}}^{+}
.

To prove transitivity,
\phi_{1,3}=\phi_{2,3}\circ\phi_{1,2}
, it suffices to prove the following:

If
\bar{U}_{\psi}\subset\bar{N}_{2}
then
\bar{U}_{\psi}\subset\bar{N}_{1}\text{ or }\bar{U}_{\psi}\subset\bar{N}_{3}
.

From the structure theory above, this is equivalent to proving

If
\psi|_{F_{2}}>0
then
\psi|_{F_{1}}>0
or
\psi|_{F_{3}}>0
.

This is clear, since
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})
is a collinear triple and
\psi
is an affine-linear function.

\square

5.5.
Perversity and conclusion

As we have proven monotonicity, invertibility, and transitivity, we have proven the following result.

If
(\pi,V)
is an admissible representation of
G
, then
({\mathscr{V}},\gamma,\delta,\eta)
is a
G
-equivariant perverse sheaf on the Bruhat–Tits building
\mathcal{B}
.

Note that we placed the hypothesis of admissibility, to guarantee that the spaces
{\mathscr{V}}_{F}
are finite-dimensional. This gives an exact functor, factoring through the depth zero projector, from the category of admissible representations of
G
to the category of
G
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, a depth zero admissible representation
(\pi,V)
is naturally identified (as a smooth representation of
G
) with the cosheaf homology
H_{0}(\mathcal{B},{\mathscr{V}})
according to
[
SS97
, Theorem II.3.1]
(with a minor restriction removed by
[
MS10
, Theorem 2.4, Lemma 2.6]
). In particular,
(\pi,V)
can be recovered from the
G
-equivariant perverse sheaf.

The restriction of such a perverse sheaf to an apartment becomes a perverse sheaf on an affine hyperplane arrangement, equivariant for an affine Weyl group. We have given an explicit description of the category of such perverse sheaves, at least for finite
W
, in terms of modules over an algebra
\mathcal{A}_{W}
. It would be interesting to connect such algebras to the Hecke algebras of a
p
-adic group, e.g., a full depth zero Hecke algebra
C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{F}^{+}\backslash G/G_{F}^{+})
for
F
a vertex in the building.

The ingredients used to demonstrate perversity (especially the invertibility condition) are not so different from the ingredients used by Moy and Prasad
[
MP96
, §1, §6]
in order to demonstrate that unrefined minimal K-types are, in fact “refined”. Therefore it seems important to determine whether one can functorially associate
G
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
\mathcal{B}
to higher-depth representations of
G
. But a simple approach of taking fixed vectors at higher-depth (e.g., for Moy-Prasad subgroups) cannot by itself lead to a perverse sheaf – the invertibility condition is not satisfied, nor are K-types refined by such a process.

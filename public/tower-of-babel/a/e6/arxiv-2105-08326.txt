Actively Learning Concepts and Conjunctive Queries under ℰ ​ ℒ r {\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r} -Ontologies
Maurice Funk, Jean Christoph Jung, Carsten Lutz

Abstract
We consider the problem to learn a concept or a query in the presence of an ontology formulated in the description logic ℰ ​ ℒ r {\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r} , in Angluin’s framework of active learning that allows the learning algorithm to interactively query an oracle (such as a domain expert). We show that the following can be learned in polynomial time: (1) ℰ ​ ℒ {\cal E\!\!\>L} -concepts, (2) symmetry-free ℰ ​ ℒℐ {\cal E\!\!\>LI} -concepts, and (3) conjunctive queries (CQs) that are chordal, symmetry-free, and of bounded arity. In all cases, the learner can pose to the oracle membership queries based on ABoxes and equivalence queries that ask whether a given concept/query from the considered class is equivalent to the target. The restriction to bounded arity in (3) can be removed when we admit unrestricted CQs in equivalence queries. We also show that ℰ ​ ℒ {\cal E\!\!\>L} -concepts are not polynomial query learnable in the presence of ℰ ​ ℒℐ {\cal E\!\!\>LI} -ontologies.



1
Introduction

In logic based knowledge representation, a significant bottleneck is
the construction of logical formulas such as description logic
(DL) concepts, queries, and ontologies, as it is laborious and
expensive. This is particularly true if the construction
involves multiple parties because logic expertise and domain knowledge
are not in the same hands. Angluin’s model of exact learning, a form
of active learning, is able to support the construction of logical formulas in terms of a game-like
collaboration between a learner and an oracle

Angluin 1987b
;
Angluin 1987a
.
Applied in knowledge representation, the learner can be a logic expert
and the oracle a domain expert that is interactively queried by the
learner. Alternatively, the oracle can take other forms such as a set
of labeled data examples that in some way represents the formula to be
learned. The aim is to find an algorithm that, when executed by the
learner, constructs the desired formula in polynomial time even when
the oracle is not able to provide most informative answers. Landmark
results from active learning state that such algorithms exist for
learning propositional Horn formulas and finite automata

Angluin et al. 1992
;
Angluin 1987a
.

The aim of this paper is to study active learning of
DL
concepts
and of
conjunctive queries (CQs)
in the presence of
an ontology. Concepts are the main building block of ontologies
Baader et al. 2017

and learning them is important for ontology engineering. CQs are
very prominent in ontology-mediated querying where data stored
in an ABox is enriched with an ontology

Bienvenu et al. 2014
. We
concentrate on the
{\cal E\!\!\>L}
family of DLs which underlies the OWL EL
profile of the OWL 2 ontology language

Krötzsch 2012
and is frequently used in biomedical
ontologies such as
Snomed CT
. We consider ontologies
formulated in the DLs
{\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r}
and
{\cal E\!\!\>LI}
where
{\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r}
extends
{\cal E\!\!\>L}
with
range restrictions and
{\cal E\!\!\>LI}
extends
{\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r}
with inverse roles. In both
DLs, concepts can be viewed as a tree-shaped
conjunctive query, and from now on we shall treat them as such. In
fact, it is not uncommon to use concepts as queries in
ontology-mediated querying, which provides an additional motivation for
learning them.

We now describe the learning protocol in detail. It is an
instance of Angluin’s model, which we do not repeat here in full
generality. The aim is to learn a target CQ
q_{T}(\bar{x})
in the
presence of an ontology
\mathcal{O}
. The learner and the oracle both know and
agree on the ontology
\mathcal{O}
, the arity of
q_{T}
, and the concept
and role names that are available for constructing
q_{T}
; we assume
that all concept and role names in
\mathcal{O}
can be used also in
q_{T}
.
The learner can ask two types of queries to the oracle. In a

membership query
, the learner provides an ABox
\mathcal{A}
and a candidate answer
\bar{a}
and asks whether

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{a})
; the oracle faithfully answers “yes” or “no”.
In an
equivalence query
, the learner provides a hypothesis CQ

q_{H}
and asks whether
q_{H}
is
equivalent to
q_{T}
under
\mathcal{O}
; the oracle answers “yes” or
provides a counterexample, that is, an ABox
\mathcal{A}
and tuple
\bar{a}

such that
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{a})
and

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}\not\models q_{H}(\bar{a})
(
positive counterexample
) or
vice versa (
negative counterexample
).
When we learn a restricted class of CQs such as
{\cal E\!\!\>L}
-concepts,
we assume that only CQs from that class are admitted in equivalence
queries. We are then interested in whether there is a learning
algorithm that constructs
q_{T}(\bar{x})
, up to equivalence under
\mathcal{O}
,
such that at any given time, the running time of the algorithm is
bounded by a polynomial in the sizes of
q_{T}
, of
\mathcal{O}
, and of the
largest counterexample given by the oracle so far. This is called

polynomial time learnability
. A weaker requirement is

polynomial query learnability
where only the sum of the
sizes of the
queries posed to the oracle up to the current time point has to be
bounded
by such a polynomial.

Our main results are that the following can be learned in polynomial
time under
{\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r}
-ontologies: (1)
{\cal E\!\!\>L}
-concepts, (2)
{\cal E\!\!\>LI}
-concepts that
are symmetry-free, and (3) CQs that are chordal, symmetry-free, and of
bounded arity. In Point (2), symmetry-freeness means that there is no
subconcept of the form
\exists r.(C\sqcap\exists r^{-}.D)
with

r
a role name, a condition that has recently been introduced in

Jung et al. 2020
, in a slightly less general form where
r
can also be
an inverse role. In Point (3), chordal means that every cycle of
length at least four that contains at least one quantified variable
has a chord and symmetry-free means that the CQ contains no atoms

r(x_{1},y),r(x_{2},y)
such that
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
,
y
is a quantified
variable, neither
r(x_{1},y)
nor
r(x_{2},y)
occur on a cycle, and
there is no atom
s(z,z)
for any
z\in\{x_{1},x_{2},y\}
. An analysis
of well-known benchmarks for ontology-mediated querying suggests that
the resulting class CQ
{}^{\text{csf}}
of CQs is sufficiently general
to include many relevant CQs that occur in practical applications.
Our proofs crucially rely on the use of a finite version of the
universal model that is specifically tailored to the class
CQ
{}^{\text{csf}}
. We also show that the restriction to bounded arity
can be removed from Point (3) when we admit unrestricted CQs as the
argument to equivalence queries. Proving this requires very
substantial changes to the learning algorithm.

In addition, we prove several negative results. First, we show that none of
the classes of CQs in Points (1) to (3) can be learned under

{\cal E\!\!\>L}
-ontologies using only membership queries or only equivalence
queries (unless
\textnormal{\sc P}=\textnormal{\sc NP}
in the latter case). Note that
polynomial time learning
with only membership queries is important because it is related to
whether CQs can be characterized up to equivalence using only polynomially many data
examples
ten Cate and
Dalmau 2020
.
We also show
the much more involved result that none of the classes of CQs in
Points (1) to (3) is polynomial query learnable under
{\cal E\!\!\>LI}
-ontologies.
Note that while polynomial time learnability cannot be expected
because subsumption in
{\cal E\!\!\>LI}
is
ExpTime
-complete, there could well
have been a polynomial time learning algorithm with access to an
oracle (in the classical sense) for subsumption/query containment
under
{\cal E\!\!\>LI}
-ontologies that attains polynomial query learnability.
Our result rules out this possibility.

Proof details are in the appendix.

Related work.

Learning
{\cal E\!\!\>L}
-ontologies, rather than
concepts or queries, was studied in

Konev et al. 2018
;
Konev et al. 2016
. It turns out
that
{\cal E\!\!\>L}
-ontologies are not polynomial time learnable while certain
fragments thereof are. In contrast, we attain polynomial time
learnability also under unrestricted
{\cal E\!\!\>L}
-ontologies. See also the surveys
Lehmann and
Völker 2014
;
Ozaki 2020
and

Ozaki et al. 2020
for a variation less related to
the current work.
It has been
shown in
ten Cate et al. 2013
;
ten Cate et al. 2018

that unions of CQs (UCQs) are polynomial time learnable, and the
presented algorithm can be adapted to CQs. Active learning of CQs
with only membership queries is considered in

ten Cate and
Dalmau 2020
where among other results
it is shown that

{\cal E\!\!\>LI}
-concepts can be learned in polynomial time with only membership
queries when the ontology is empty. PAC learnability of
concepts formulated in the DL CLASSIC, without ontologies, was studied
in
Cohen and
Hirsh 1994b
;
Cohen and
Hirsh 1994a
;
Frazier and
Pitt 1996
.

{\cal E\!\!\>LI}

A

Universal Models.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an ABox and
\mathcal{O}
an
{\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r}
-ontology. The

model of

and

{}^{\text{sf}}

3.1
Reduction to Normal Form

We show that the ontology under which we learn can w.l.o.g. be

{\cal E\!\!\>L}^{r}
-ontology

-ontologies. Care has to be exercised as the fresh concept

Proposition 1

Let

\mathcal{Q}\in\{\text{ELQ},\text{ELIQ}^{\text{sf}},\text{CQ}^{\text{csf}}_{w}\mid w\geq 0\}

-ontologies.

3.4
Unbounded Arity

To prove the remaining Point 3 of Theorem

\mathsf{refine}

We thus introduce a second version of
\mathsf{refine}
that
works rather differently from the previous one. We give an
informal description, full details are in the appendix.

Recall that refinement starts with the product

P=\mathcal{C}^{3}_{\mathcal{A}_{q_{H}},\mathcal{O}}\times\mathcal{C}^{3}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}}
. In
Section
3.3
, we blow up cycles in
P
, not
distinguishing the ABox part and the existentially generated part of
the 3-compact models involved. The second version of

\mathsf{refine}
instead unravels the existentially generated part of the two
3-compact models inside the product
P
. A full such unraveling
would eventually result in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{A}_{q_{H}},\mathcal{O}}\times\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}}
, but we interleave with a Minimize step as in
Section
3.3
and thus obtain a finite initial
piece thereof. Unlike in the previous version of
\mathsf{refine}
, we do
not have to redefine the answer variables at all (but note that they
may still change outside of
\mathsf{refine}
when we take the product).

The above suffices for target CQs from CQ
{}^{\text{csf}}
in which
every variable is reachable from an answer variable. In the general
case, disconnected Boolean components might be present (or emerge
during unraveling and minimization) that are never unraveled. To
address this, we subsequently apply the original version of

\mathsf{refine}
to such components, avoiding the Splitting step and
leaving the already unraveled parts untouched. Note that the exponential blowup
in the arity is avoided because the original
\mathsf{refine}
is only applied to
Boolean subqueries. However, the resulting queries are not guaranteed
to be in
\text{CQ}^{\text{csf}}
. We can thus
not rely on Lemma
1
as before
which is why we need CQ-equivalence queries.

\mathsf{refine}
subroutine. Recall
that we first construct a sequence

(\mathcal{B}_{1},\bar{b}_{1}),(\mathcal{B}_{2},\bar{b}_{2}),\dots
using the Expand and
Minimize steps. With
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}
,
i\geq 1
, we denote the result of
only applying the Expand step to
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, but not the Minimize
step. Also recall that the fresh individuals introduced in
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}

are denoted with
a^{\prime}
in case that the original individual was

a
. The following can easily be shown.

Lemma 12
.

Let
i\geq 1
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},a\preceq\mathcal{B}_{i},a
and
\mathcal{B}_{i},a\preceq\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},a
, for all
a\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
, and
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},a^{\prime}\preceq\mathcal{B}_{i},a
and
\mathcal{B}_{i},a\preceq\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},a^{\prime}
, for
all fresh individuals
a^{\prime}
.

We start with proving properties of the Expand/Minimize phase.

Lemma 13
.

For all
i\geq 1
, the following properties hold:

1.

\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{b}_{i})
;

2.

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{b})
for some

\bar{b}\in\tau_{i}
;

Proof.

We prove both points simultaneously by induction on
i
. For
Point 1, the case
i=1
is immediate since

(\mathcal{B}_{1},\bar{b}_{1})=\mathsf{minimize}(\mathcal{A},\bar{a})
and

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{a})
, and the case
i>1
is an immediate
consequence of the inductive hypothesis (Point 2), the choice of

\bar{b}_{i}
, and the definition of the Minimize step.

For Point 2, the induction start and step are identical.
Thus let
i\geq 1
. Assume that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
was obtained from
\mathcal{B}_{i}
by expanding cycle

R_{0}(a_{0},a_{1}),\ldots,R_{n-1}(a_{n-1},a_{n})
. By Point 1, there is a homomorphism
h
from
q_{T}
to

\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
with
h(\bar{x})=\bar{b}_{i}
. We construct a
homomorphism
g
from
q_{T}
to
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
with

g(\bar{x})=\bar{b}
for some
\bar{b}\in\tau_{i}
, which yields

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{b})
as desired. Let us partition

\mathsf{var}(q_{T})
into sets
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2}
such that:

•

x\in M_{0}
if
h(x)\in\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\}
, that is,

h(x)
lies on the expanded cycle;

•

x\in M_{1}
if
h(x)\notin\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
, that is,
h(x)
is in the part of

\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
generated by existential quantification;

•

all other variables are in
M_{2}
.

We start with setting

g(x)=h(x)\text{\quad for all }x\in M_{2}.

To define
g(x)
for the variables in
x\in M_{0}
, we first
construct an auxiliary query
q^{\prime}_{T}
of treewidth 1. If

\mathcal{Q}\in\{\text{ELQ},\text{ELIQ}^{\text{sf}}\}
, then
q^{\prime}_{T}
is
simply
q_{T}
. Now assume that
\mathcal{Q}=\text{CQ}^{\text{csf}}
.
Then
q^{\prime}_{T}
is obtained by
starting with the restriction of
q_{T}
to the variables in
M_{0}
and
then exhaustively choosing and identifying variables
x_{1},x_{2}
such that

1.

there is a cycle
R_{0}(y_{0},y_{1}),R_{1}(y_{1},y_{2}),R_{3}(y_{2},y_{0})
with
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\subseteq\{y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}\}\subseteq M_{0}
and

2.

h(x_{1})=h(x_{2})

The result of identifying an answer variable and a quantified
variable is an answer variable. Note that we may also identify two
answer variables.

We next observe that since
q_{T}
is chordal, all CQs

q_{T}=p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{k}=q^{\prime}_{T}
encountered during the construction of

q^{\prime}_{T}
are chordal as well. This can be shown by induction on the
index
i
to the CQs
p_{i}
. The induction start is clear since

q_{T}
is chordal. For the induction step, assume that
p_{i}
contains a cycle

C=S_{0}(z_{0},z_{1}),\ldots,S_{n-1}(z_{n-1},z_{n})
of length at least four
with at least one quantified variable, where
i>0
. Then
p_{i-1}
contains
C
or a cycle
C^{\prime}
that can be obtained
from
C
by replacing some edge
S_{i}(z_{i},z_{i+1})
with two edges

S_{i,1}(z_{i},u),S_{i,2}(u,z_{i+1})
(because
u
and
z_{i+1}
were
identified when constructing
p_{i}
). In the first case,
C
has a
chord in
p_{i-1}
and thus also in
p_{i}
. In the second case,
C^{\prime}

contains at least one quantified variable since
C
does and
consequently has a chord in
p_{i-1}
. If this chord is not between

z_{i}
and
z_{i+1}
, then
C
contains a chord in
p
. If the
chord is between
z_{i}
and
z_{i+1}
, then we are in the first case.

We now show that
q^{\prime}_{T}
has treewidth 1, that is, it takes the form
of a disjoint union of (not necessarily directed) trees with
multi-edges and self loops. Assume to the contrary that
q^{\prime}_{T}

contains a cycle
C
of length exceeding 2. If there is a quantified
variable
x
on
C
, then
q^{\prime}_{T}
being chordal implies that
x

occurs on a cycle of length 3, in contradiction to the construction
of
q^{\prime}_{T}
. Now assume that there is no quantified variable on

C
. As the image of
C
under
h
is a cycle in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
and the
cycle chosen by the Expansion step is chordless, the image
of
C
under
h
must contain
all
individuals

\{a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}\}
. Since all variables on
C
are answer
variables, this means that all individual in
M_{0}
are from

\bar{b}
, in contradiction to

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\not\subseteq\bar{b}_{i}
.

This finishes the construction of
q^{\prime}_{T}
. For defining
g(x)
for
the variables
x\in\mathsf{var}(q^{\prime}_{T})
, we can now start at some
arbitrary variable in each tree in
q^{\prime}_{T}
and then follow the tree
structure, switching between the individuals
a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}
and
their copies
a^{\prime}_{0},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n-1}
as necessary. We next make this
precise. For each connected component of
q^{\prime}_{T}
, choose an arbitrary
variable
z
from that component and set

g(z)=h(z).

Then exhaustively apply the following rule:
if
q^{\prime}_{T}
contains an atom
R(x,y)
with
g(y)
defined and

g(x)
undefined, set

•

g(x)=h(x)
if
g(y)=a_{i}
and either
h(x)=a_{i+1}

and
i<n-2
or
h(x)=a_{i-1}
and
i>0
;

•

g(x)=h(x)^{\prime}
if
g(y)=a_{i}^{\prime}
and either
h(x)=a_{i+1}

and
i<n-2
or
h(x)=a_{i-1}
and
i>0
;

•

g(x)=a_{0}^{\prime}
if
g(y)=a_{n-2}
and

h(x)=a_{n-1}=a_{0}
;

•

g(x)=a_{0}
if
g(y)=a_{n-2}^{\prime}
and

h(x)=a_{n-1}=a_{0}
;

•

g(x)=a_{n-2}^{\prime}
if
g(y)=a_{0}
and

h(x)=a_{n-2}
;

•

g(x)=a_{n-2}
if
g(y)=a_{0}^{\prime}
and

h(x)=a_{n-2}
.

It can be verified that in all cases,
R(g(x),g(y))\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}

by construction of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
in the unravelling step.

We can next extend
g
to all variables in
M_{0}
by setting

g(y)=g(x)
if
y
was identified with
x\in\mathsf{var}(q^{\prime}_{T})

during the construction of
q^{\prime}_{T}
(note that this implies

h(y)=h(x)
).

By definition,
g(x)\in\{h(x),h(x)^{\prime}\}
for all
x\in M_{0}
. Thus,
g(\bar{x})\in\tau_{i}
as announced.

It remains to define
g(x)
for the variables
x\in M_{1}
. By definition of
M_{1}
,
h(x)
is a trace
cw
with

c\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
and
w\neq\varepsilon
, that is,

x
is mapped into the subtree below
c
in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
.
Now do the following:

•

if there is a path in
q_{T}
from some variable
z\in M_{0}
to

x
, then choose such a
z
such that the path is shortest (thus,

h(z)=c
and
g(z)
has already been defined) and set
g(x)=g(z)w
;

•

otherwise, set
g(x)=h(x)
.

This is well-defined since, due to
Lemma
12
, the following holds:

1.

for each
c\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
, the subtrees below
c

in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
and in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
are
identical;

2.

for
0\leq j<n
, the subtree below
a_{j}
in

\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}

and the subtree below
a^{\prime}_{j}
in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
are
identical.

Set
\bar{b}=g(\bar{x})
. To prove that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{b})
, it remains to
show the following.

Claim.

g
is a homomorphism from
q_{T}
to

\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof of the claim.
Let
A(x)
be a
concept atom in
q_{T}
. Then
A(h(x))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
. If

h(x)\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
, then
g(x)
was defined such that, by
Lemma
12
,
\mathcal{B}_{i},h(x)\preceq\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},g(x)
.
By Lemma
7
,
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}},h(x)\preceq\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}},g(x)
and thus by Lemma
6

A(g(x))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
. If
h(x)\notin\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
then the
remark before the claim and the definition of
g(x)
ensures that

A(g(x))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}}
.

Now let
R(x_{1},x_{2})
be a role atom in
q_{T}
. We distinguish cases
according to
x_{1},x_{2}
belonging to
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2}
:

•

If
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{0}
, then
q^{\prime}_{T}
contains an atom

R(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2})
such that each
x_{i}
was identified with
x^{\prime}_{i}

during the construction of
q^{\prime}_{T}
. If
x^{\prime}_{1}\neq x^{\prime}_{2}
, then

R(g(x^{\prime}_{1}),g(x^{\prime}_{2}))\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
, as argued in the definition of

g
for variables from
q^{\prime}_{T}
. By that definition and the construction
of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
, the same is true when
x_{1}=x_{2}
. We have

g(x_{i})=g(x^{\prime}_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Thus
R(g(x^{\prime}_{1}),g(x^{\prime}_{2}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
as required.

•

If
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{1}
, then
h(x_{1})=bv
and
h(x_{2})=bw
for some

b\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
and some non-empty
v,w
, and
R(h(x_{1}),h(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
. By definition of

g
, we have
g(x_{1})=\widehat{b}v
and
g(x_{2})=\widehat{b}w
for some

\widehat{b}\in\{b,b^{\prime}\}
. By Lemma
12
, the
subtree below
\widehat{b}
in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}}
is identical
to the
subtree below
b
in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
.
This implies

R(g(x_{1}),g(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}}
.

•

If
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{2}
, then
g(x_{1})=h(x_{1})
,

g(x_{2})=h(x_{2})
, and
R(h(x_{1}),h(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
because
h

is a homomorphism from
q_{T}
to
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}

and
h(x_{1}),h(x_{2})\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
. Since additionally

h(x_{1}),h(x_{2})\notin\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\}
,

R(h(x_{1}),h(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
and thus
R(g(x_{1}),g(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
.

•

If
x_{1}\in M_{0}
and
x_{2}\in M_{1}
, then

h(x_{1})\in\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\}
and
h(x_{2})
takes the form

h(x_{1})rC
. Moreover,
g(x_{1})\in\{h(x_{1}),h(x_{1})^{\prime}\}
and

g(x_{2})=g(x_{1})rC
. It thus follows from
Lemma
12
,

R(h(x_{1}),h(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
, and the construction
of universal models that

R(g(x_{1}),g(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
.

•

If
x_{1}\in M_{0}
and
x_{2}\in M_{2}
, then

h(x_{1})\in\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\}
and

h(x_{2})\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})\setminus\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\}
. Moreover,

g(x_{1})\in\{h(x_{1}),h(x_{1})^{\prime}\}
and
g(x_{2})=h(x_{2})
.
It follows from
R(h(x_{1}),h(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}

that
R(h(x_{1}),h(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
. By construction of

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
, we thus have
R(g(x_{1}),g(x_{2}))\in\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
.

•

if
x_{1}\in M_{1}
,
x_{2}\in M_{2}
, then

h(x_{2})\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})\setminus\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\}

and
h(x_{1})
takes the form
h(x_{2})rC
with
R=r^{-}
. Moreover

g(x_{i})=h(x_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
and it remains to use
Lemma
12
as in previous cases.

∎

Lemma 14
.

For all
i\geq 1
,

1.

\mathcal{B}_{i}
is
\mathcal{O}
-saturated;

2.

if
h
is a homomorphism from
q_{T}
to
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
with
h(\bar{x})=\bar{b}_{i}
, then
\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})\subseteq\mathsf{img}(h^{*})
;

3.

\mathcal{B}_{i+1},\bar{b}_{i+1}\to\mathcal{B}_{i},\bar{b}_{i}
;

4.

|\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i+1})|>|\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})|
.

Proof.

We prove Point 1 by induction on
i
. For
i=1
, recall that the
initial ABox
\mathcal{A}
is of the form

\mathcal{C}^{3}_{\mathcal{A}_{q_{H}},\mathcal{O}}\times\mathcal{C}^{3}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}}

or
\mathcal{A}_{q^{\bot}}
for all uses of the subrouting

\mathsf{refine}
and that
\mathcal{B}_{1},\bar{b}_{1}=\mathsf{minimize}(\mathcal{A},\bar{a})
. In the first case,
both
\mathcal{C}^{3}_{\mathcal{A}_{q_{H}},\mathcal{O}}
and

\mathcal{C}^{3}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}}
are
\mathcal{O}
-saturated and thus their product is also

\mathcal{O}
-saturated by Lemma
5

In the second case
\mathcal{A}_{q^{\bot}}
is
\mathcal{O}
-saturated since it
contains
A(x_{0})
for all concept names
A\in\Sigma
. Moreover, the
Minimize step does not remove any concept assertions.
For the induction step, suppose

\mathcal{B}_{i+1},\mathcal{O}\models A(\widehat{a})
for some concept name
A

and some
\widehat{a}\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i+1})
with
\widehat{a}

either
a\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
or
a^{\prime}
for some
a\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i})
. By monotonicity,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}\models A(\widehat{a})
where
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
is the result of applying the Expand step
to
\mathcal{B}_{i}
before the Minimize step. By Lemma
12
,

\mathcal{B}_{i}^{\prime},\widehat{a}\preceq\mathcal{B}_{i},a
and thus
\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}\models A(a)
by Lemma
8
. By induction, we know that
A(a)\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
, and the
application of the rules ensures that
A(\widehat{a})\in\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
.

For Point 2, let
h
be a homomorphism from
q_{T}
to

\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
with
h(\bar{x})=\bar{b}_{i}
, and suppose that
there is an

that is not in
\mathsf{img}(h^{*})
.
Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be the result of removing from
\mathcal{B}_{i}
all assertions
that involve
a
. We show that

(
*
)

h
is a homomorphism from
q_{T}
to
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}}

which witnesses that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}\models q_{T}(\bar{b}_{i})
. Hence,

is dropped during the Minimize step, in contradiction to

. To see that (
*
) holds, first note that for
all

, the following
holds by Point 1 and construction of universal models:

(a)

A(b)\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{O}}
iff
A(b)\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}}
;

(b)

r(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}}
.

From (a), in turn, it follows that the subtree in

below each

is identical to the subtree
in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}}
below

. Now (
*
) is an easy consequence.

For Points 3 and 4, define a mapping

from

\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{B}_{i+1})

Claim 3.
If
g

r(a,b)\in\mathcal{B}_{i}

Claim 4.

g

Proof of Claim 4.
Let

R_{0}(a_{0},a_{1}),\ldots R_{n-1}(a_{n-1},a_{n})\in\mathcal{B}_{i}

\mathcal{B}_{i}

is a role name, but

We prove later that the Unravel step can only be applied polynomially

For example, consider the

To deal with this issue, we apply the original

subroutine
from Section

to

(with

1.

the individuals in

are not touched, that is, no cycle that

2.

\bar{b}_{n}=\bar{b}_{n+1}=\cdots

3.

the Splitting step is not applied.

We can use the claim to show the following invariant:

(
*
)

at any point in time, the sum
m
of the sizes of the
inputs to membership and equivalence queries made so far is not
smaller than the number of candidates that were removed from
\mathcal{H}
.

Note in fact that only Cases 2 and 3 of membership queries and Cases 3
and 6 of equivalence queries may remove candidates from
\mathcal{H}
and
that they all remove only one candidate for each query posed with the
exception of Case 3 of membership queries which can remove multiple
candidate. However, the claim implies that the number of removed
candidates in Case 3 of membership queries is bounded from above by
the size of the query posed.

It is clear that there is a polynomial
q_{3}
such that the size of all
counterexamples returned by the oracle is at most
q_{3}(n)
. The
overall sum of the sizes of posed membership and equivalence queries
is bounded by
p(q_{1}(n),q_{2}(n),q_{3}(n))
. It thus follows from (
*
)
that at most

candidate concepts have been
removed from
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. By the choice of
n
, at least two candidate
concepts remain in
\mathcal{H}
after the algorithm finished. Thus, the
learner cannot distinguish between them, and we have derived a
contradiction.

Measurement of alpha induced reaction cross-sections on nat Mo with detailed covariance analysis
Mahesh Choudhary, A. Gandhi, Aman Sharma, Namrata Singh, A. Kumar, S. Dasgupta, J. Datta

Abstract
In the present study we have measured the excitation functions for the nuclear reactions 100 Mo( α \alpha ,n) 103 Ru, nat Mo( α \alpha ,x) 97 Ru, nat Mo( α \alpha ,x) 95 Ru, nat Mo( α \alpha ,x) 96g Tc, nat Mo( α \alpha ,x) 95g Tc and nat Mo( α \alpha ,x) 94g Tc in the energy range 11-32 MeV. We have used the stacked foil activation technique followed by off-line gamma ray spectroscopy technique to measure the excitation functions. In this study we have also documented detailed uncertainty analysis for these nuclear reactions and their corresponding covariance matrix are also presented. The excitation functions are compared with the available experimental data from EXFOR data library and the theoretical prediction from TALYS nuclear reaction code. The present measurements are found to be consistent with the available experimental data.

\sigma=\frac{C_{\gamma}\lambda}{\varepsilon(E_{\gamma}){I_{\gamma}}D_{t}{\phi}N_{t}e^{-\lambda t_{c}}(1-e^{-\lambda t_{irr}})(1-e^{-\lambda t_{m}})}

In the above formula

C_{\gamma}
of a particular

s^{-1}

N_{t}
((
cm^{-2}

\phi

s^{-1}

In equation 3, the cooling time of the irradiated target is
t_{c}
(s) with the irradiation time

In the present study, the uncertainty in the factors contributing to the cross-section such as the efficiency of the detector

B. Covariance Analysis

A covariance analysis is a mathematical tool that can help to describe the detailed uncertainty with the cross-correlation between different measured quantities. The principle of covariance emphasizes the formulation
of physical laws using only measurements of physical quantities that can be correlated.
The covariance matrix of cross-sections I
σ
can be written as
30
;
31
;

I_{\sigma}=H_{x}C_{x}H_{x}^{T}

(4)

Where I
σ
is the covariance matrix of measured reaction cross-sections with order m
\times
m,
C_{x}
is the covariance matrix with order n
\times
n of different attributes in activation formula (equation 2) like as
\gamma
-ray intensity (
I_{\gamma}
), detector efficiency
\varepsilon(E_{\gamma})
, incident flux (
\phi

C_{\gamma}
), decay constant (
\lambda

In equation (4),
H_{x}
is the sensitivity matrix with element;

H_{xij}=\frac{\partial\sigma_{i}}{\partial x_{j}};(i=1,2,3,...m;~j=1,2,3,...n)

(5)

Here m has value equal to total number of measured cross-sections for a particular nuclear reaction and has value equal to total number of attributes in the activation formula.
Let us consider
x_{j}
(j = 1,2,3….n), various variable parameters (which are mentioned above) required in the calculation of the cross-sections, then the covariance matrix (c
x
) of these variables can be obtained from the following equation

C_{x}(x_{j},x_{k})=Cor(x_{j},x_{k})(\Delta x_{j}\Delta x_{k})

(6)

Where

x_{k}
and it has value
0\leq Cor(x_{j},x_{k})\leq 1

Cor(x_{j},x_{k})

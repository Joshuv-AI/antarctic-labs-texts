Gromov–-Hausdorff distance between vertex sets of regular polygons inscribed in a given circle
Talant Talipov

Abstract
We calculate the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between vertex sets of regular polygons endowed with the round metric. We give a full answer for the case of n n - and m m -gons with m m divisible by n n . Also, we calculate all distances to 2 2 -gons and 3 3 -gons



1
Introduction

In the present paper we study the class of all metric spaces considered up to isometry, endowed with the Gromov–Hausdorff distance. Note that the exact values of the Gromov–Hausdorff distances between specific metric spaces are known only for a small number of cases. For example, in
[
1
]
, the Gromov–-Hausdorff distances from a broad class of metric spaces to 1-spaces, i.e. metric spaces with one non-zero distance, were calculated. In
[
2
]
, the Gromov–-Hausdorff distance between a segment and a circle with intrinsic metric was obtained. In
[
3
]
, the Gromov–-Hausdorff distances between spheres of different dimensions were calculated in some cases and estimated in other ones. Also, the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between the vertex set of regular polygons and circle were calculated, as well as between different regular
m
- and
(m+1)
-gons inscribed in the same circle. In the present paper we extend the results of
[
3
]
for the case of
m
- and
n
-gons, provided that
m
is divisible by
n
. Also we have calculated all distances to
2
-gons and
3
-gons. The author expresses his gratitude to his supervisor, Prof. Alexey A. Tuzhilin, and to Prof. Alexander O. Ivanov for posing the problem and help in the work.

Preliminaries

Let
X
be an arbitrary metric space. The distance between points
x,y
we denote by
d(x,y)
or
|xy|
. For any non-empty
A
,
B
the
Hausdorff distance
between
A
and
B
is defined as follows
\/\colon

d_{H}(A,B)=\max\bigl\{\sup\limits_{a\in A}\inf\limits_{b\in B}d(a,b),\sup\limits_{b\in B}\inf\limits_{a\in A}d(a,b)\bigr\}.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces. If
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
are subsets of a metric space
Z^{\prime}
, provided
X^{\prime}
is isometric to
X
, and
Y^{\prime}
is isometric to
Y
, then
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
is called
realization
of the pair
(X,Y)
.
The Gromov–Hausdorff distance
between
X
and
Y
is the value

d_{GH}(X,Y)=\inf\bigl\{r:\exists(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime}),d_{H}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\leqslant r\bigr\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Given two sets
X
and
Y
, a
correspondence
between
X
and
Y
is a subset
R\subset X\times Y
such that for any
x\in X
there exists
y\in Y
with
(x,y)\in R
and, vise versa, for any
y\in Y
there exists
x\in X
with
(x,y)\in R
. If
X
,
Y
are metric spaces, then we define
the correspondence distortion

R
as follows
\/\colon

\operatorname{dis}R=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl||x_{1}x_{2}|-|y_{1}y_{2}|\bigr|:(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in R\Bigr\}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{R}(X,Y)
the set of all correspondences between
X
and
Y
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces. Then

d_{GH}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\inf\bigl\{\operatorname{dis}R:R\in\mathcal{R}(X,Y)\bigr\}.

In
[
3
]
the following construction was considered. For a metric space
(X,d_{X})
, we define the pseudo-ultrametric space
(X,u_{X})
where
u_{X}\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

u_{X}\colon(x,y)\mapsto\inf\bigl\{\max\limits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}d_{X}(x_{i},x_{i+1}):x_{0}=x,...,x_{n}=y\bigr\}.

Now, define
\mathbf{U}(X)
to be the quotient metric space of
(X,u_{X})
over the equivalence
x\sim y
if and only if
u_{X}(x,y)=0
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

For all bounded metric spaces
X
and
Y
, it holds

d_{GH}(X,Y)\geqslant d_{GH}\bigl(\mathbf{U}(X),\mathbf{U}(Y)\bigr).

Definition 2.2
.

By
simplex
we call a metric space, all whose non-zero distances equal to each other. If
m
is an arbitrary cardinal number, then by
\lambda\Delta_{m}
we denote a simplex containing
m
points and such that all its non-zero distances equal to
\lambda
.

Let
X
be an arbitrary set consisting of more than one point,
2\leqslant m\leqslant\#X
a cardinal number,
and
\lambda>0
. By
\mathcal{D}_{m}(X)
we denote the family of all possible partitions of the set X into m
non-empty subsets. For any non-empty
A,B\subset X
, we put
|AB|=\inf\bigl\{|ab|:a\in A,b\in B\bigr\}
. Now let
X
be a metric space. Then for each
D=\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(X)
we put

\operatorname{diam}D=\sup\limits_{i\in I}\operatorname{diam}X_{i}\/;

\alpha(D)=\inf\bigl\{|X_{i}X_{j}|:i\neq j\bigr\}.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
X\neq\Delta_{1}
be an arbitrary metric space,
2\leqslant m\leqslant\#X
a cardinal
number, and
\lambda>0
. Then

2d_{GH}(\lambda\Delta,X)=\inf\limits_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{m}(X)}\max\bigl\{\operatorname{diam}D,\lambda-\alpha(D),\operatorname{diam}X-\lambda\bigr\}.

3
Gromov–Hausdorff distance between vertex sets of regular polygons inscribed in a given circle

For each integer
n\geqslant 2
, let
P_{n}
be the set of vertices of a regular
n
-gon inscribed in the unit circle
S^{1}
. Notice that
P_{2}
is the pair of diametrically opposed points. Furthermore, we endow
P_{n}
with the restriction of the geodesic distance on
S^{1}
. For
m,n\geqslant 2
, we define the values
p_{m,n}=d_{GH}(P_{m},P_{n})
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

For all
m\geqslant 2
, we have

d_{GH}(S^{1},P_{m})=\frac{\pi}{m};

d_{GH}(P_{m},P_{m+1})=\frac{\pi}{m+1}.

The next technical result will be used in what follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
i,j=1,2,...,n
and
k,l=1,2,...,p-1
,

\Bigl|\min\bigl(|i-j|,n-|i-j|\bigr)-\min\bigl(\bigl|i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}\bigr|,n-\bigl|i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}\bigr|\bigr)\Bigr|\leqslant\frac{|k-l|}{p}.

Proof.

Put
S=\Bigl|\min\bigl(|i-j|,n-|i-j|\bigr)-\min\bigl(\bigl|i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}\bigr|,n-\bigl|i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}\bigr|\bigr)\Bigr|
. Since
\frac{|k-l|}{p}<1
, we have

|i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}|=\begin{cases}i-j+\frac{k-l}{p},&\text{if}\ i-j>0;\\
\frac{|k-l|}{p},&\text{if}\ i-j=0;\\
j-i+\frac{l-k}{p},&\text{if}\ i-j<0.\end{cases}

If
i-j=0
then

S=\Bigl|\min\bigl(0,n\bigr)-\min\bigl(\frac{|k-l|}{p},n-\frac{|k-l|}{p}\bigr)\Bigr|=\frac{|k-l|}{p}.

Without loss of generality, we will assume that
i-j>0
. Let us consider a few cases.

1) Assume that
i-j<\frac{n}{2}
. If
i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}>\frac{n}{2}
then
i-j=\frac{n-1}{2}
and
\frac{k-l}{p}>\frac{1}{2}
. In this case,

S=\Bigl|\frac{n-1}{2}-\bigl(n-(\frac{n-1}{2}+\frac{k-l}{p})\bigr)\Bigr|=\Bigl|\frac{k-l}{p}-1\Bigr|<\frac{|k-l|}{p},

where the last inequality holds because
\frac{k-l}{p}>\frac{1}{2}
. If
i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}\leqslant\frac{n}{2}
then

S=\Bigl|i-j-\bigr(i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}\bigr)\Bigr|=\frac{|k-l|}{p}.

2) Assume that
i-j=\frac{n}{2}
. Then

S=\Bigl|\frac{n}{2}-\min\bigl(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{k-l}{p},\frac{n}{2}+\frac{l-k}{p}\bigr)\Bigr|=\frac{|k-l|}{p}.

3) Assume that
i-j>\frac{n}{2}
. If
i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}<\frac{n}{2}
then
i-j=\frac{n+1}{2}
and
\frac{l-k}{p}>\frac{1}{2}
. In this case,

S=\Bigl|\frac{n-1}{2}-\bigl(\frac{n+1}{2}+\frac{k-l}{p}\bigr)\Bigr|=\Bigl|\frac{l-k}{p}-1\Bigr|<\frac{|k-l|}{p},

where the last inequality holds because
\frac{l-k}{p}>\frac{1}{2}
. If
i-j+\frac{k-l}{p}\geqslant\frac{n}{2}
then

S=\Bigl|n-i+j-\bigr(n-i+j-\frac{k-l}{p}\bigr)\Bigr|=\frac{|k-l|}{p}.

The proof is completed.
∎

Now let us formulate the main results of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant m
and
m
is divisible by
n
. Then

p_{n,m}=\frac{\pi}{n}-\frac{\pi}{m}.

Proof.

Let
u_{1},...,u_{n}
be the vertices of
P_{n}
and
v_{1},...,v_{m}
be the vertices of
P_{m}
. Let us prove that
p_{n,m}\geqslant\frac{\pi}{n}-\frac{\pi}{m}
. By Theorem
2.2
,

p_{n,m}\geqslant d_{GH}\bigl(\mathbf{U}(P_{m}),\mathbf{U}(P_{n})\bigr).

Notice that
\mathbf{U}(P_{m}),\mathbf{U}(P_{n})
are simpexes with distances
\frac{2\pi}{m}
и
\frac{2\pi}{n}
containing
m
and
n
points respectively. By Theorem
2.3
,

p_{n,m}\geqslant d_{GH}\bigl(\mathbf{U}(P_{m}),\mathbf{U}(P_{n})\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\max\Bigl\{\frac{2\pi}{m},\frac{2\pi}{n}-\frac{2\pi}{m}\Bigr\}\geqslant\frac{\pi}{n}-\frac{\pi}{m}.

Let us prove the upper bound. Let
m=pn
, where
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Then
\frac{\pi}{n}-\frac{\pi}{m}=\frac{(p-1)\pi}{pn}
. Now, we construct the following correspondence
R\in\mathcal{R}(P_{n},P_{m})
with
\operatorname{dis}R\leqslant\frac{2(p-1)\pi}{pn}
, what completes the proof. Namely, let us put

R=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bigl\{(u_{i},v_{pi-k}):k=0,1,...,p-1)\bigr\}.

Then, in accordance with Lemma
3.1
, for any
i,j=1,2,...,n
and
k,l=0,1,...,p-1
,

\displaystyle\bigl|d(u_{i},u_{j})-d(v_{pi-l},v_{pj-k})\bigr|=\Bigl|\frac{2\pi}{n}\min\bigl(|i-j|,n-|i-j|\bigr)-\frac{2\pi}{pn}\min\bigl(|pi-pj+k-l|,pn-

\displaystyle|pi-pj+k-l|\bigr)\Bigr|\leqslant\frac{2\pi|k-l|}{pn}\leqslant\frac{2(p-1)\pi}{pn}.

Hence,
\operatorname{dis}R\leqslant\frac{2(p-1)\pi}{pn}
, which is what was required.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
. Then

p_{2,m}=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{2m},&\text{if}\ m\text{ is odd;}\\
\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{m},&\text{if}\ m\text{ is even.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The case of even
m
immediately follows from Theorem
3.1
. Let
m
be an odd number. Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be the vertices of
P_{2}
and
v_{1},...,v_{m}
be the vertices of
P_{m}
. Notice that
P_{2}=\pi\Delta_{2}
. By Theorem
2.3
:

d_{GH}(P_{2},P_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{2}}\max\bigl\{\operatorname{diam}D,\/\pi-\alpha(D),\/\operatorname{diam}P_{m}-\pi\bigr\},

where
\mathcal{D}_{2}
is the family of all possible partitions of
P_{m}
into
2
non-empty subsets. In this case we have
\alpha(D)=\frac{2\pi}{m}
and
\operatorname{diam}P_{m}=\pi-\frac{\pi}{2m}
. Then

d_{GH}(P_{2},P_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{2}}\max\{\operatorname{diam}D,\/\pi-\frac{\pi}{2m}\}.

Let’s show that for any
D\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
it holds

\operatorname{diam}D\geqslant\pi-\frac{\pi}{2m}.

Assume that the reverse is true for the partition
D=\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
, i.e.
d=\operatorname{diam}D<\pi-\frac{\pi}{2m}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\operatorname{diam}X_{1}=d
. Then there are vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in P_{m}
such that
v_{i},v_{j}\in X_{1}
and
d(v_{i},v_{j})=d
. Consider the vertices
v_{k},v_{l}\in P_{m}
adjacent to
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, respectively, and not lying inside the smaller circular arc of
S^{1}
connecting
v_{i}
with
v_{j}
. The vertex
v_{k}
cannot belong to
X_{1}
, because otherwise

d=\operatorname{diam}X_{1}\geqslant d(v_{k},v_{j})=d(v_{i},v_{j})+\frac{2\pi}{m}=d+\frac{2\pi}{m}>d.

Similarly,
v_{l}\in X_{2}
. Then

d=\operatorname{diam}D\geqslant\operatorname{diam}X_{2}\geqslant d(v_{k},v_{l})\geqslant d(v_{i},v_{j})+\frac{2\pi}{m}=d+\frac{2\pi}{m}>d.

Thus, the theorem is proved.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
m\geqslant 3
and
r
is the remainder of dividing
m
by
3
. Then

p_{3,m}=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\pi}{m},&\text{if}\ r=0;\\
\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{r\pi}{3m},&\text{if}\ r\neq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.

The case of
r=0
immediately follows from Theorem
3.1
. Let
r>0
. Notice that
P_{3}=\frac{2\pi}{3}\Delta_{3}
. By Theorem
2.3
,

d_{GH}(P_{3},P_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{3}}\max\bigl\{\operatorname{diam}D,\/\frac{2\pi}{3}-\alpha(D),\/\operatorname{diam}P_{m}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\bigr\},

where
\mathcal{D}_{3}
— the family of all possible partitions of
P_{m}
into
3
non-empty subsets.
In this case we have
\alpha(D)=\frac{2\pi}{m}
and
\operatorname{diam}P_{m}\leqslant\pi
. According to Proposition
3.1
, we have
p_{3,4}=\frac{\pi}{4}
. Now let
m\geqslant 5
. In this case we have

d_{GH}(P_{3},P_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{3}}\max\{\operatorname{diam}D,\/\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{m}\}.

Put
q=[\frac{m}{3}]
. Assume that
r=1
. Consider the following partition
D=\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\}\/\colon

X_{1}=\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{q}\};

X_{2}=\{v_{q+1},v_{q+2},...,v_{2q}\};

X_{3}=\{v_{2q+1},v_{2q+2},...,v_{m}\}.

Then,
\operatorname{diam}D=(\frac{m-1}{3})\frac{2\pi}{m}=\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3m}
. Let us show that for any
D\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
we have

\operatorname{diam}D\geqslant\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3m}.

Assume that the reverse is true for the partition
D=\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\}
, i.e.
d=\operatorname{diam}D<\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3m}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that the set
X_{1}
contains more than one point. Then there are vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in P_{m}
such that
v_{i},v_{j}\in X_{1}
and
d(v_{i},v_{j})=\operatorname{diam}X_{1}
. Let us show that every vertex
v_{k}\in X_{1}
must lie inside the smaller arc of the circle
S^{1}
connecting
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
. Suppose that
v_{k}\in X_{1}
lies outside this arc. Then the circle is divided into 3 arcs
v_{i}v_{j},v_{j}v_{k},v_{k}v_{i}
. The length of each of them must not be greater than
\operatorname{diam}X_{1}\leqslant d
. Then

2\pi=d(v_{i},v_{j})+d(v_{j},v_{k})+d(v_{k},v_{i})\leqslant 3d<2\pi-\frac{2\pi}{m}<2\pi.

Thus, each of the sets
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
and
X_{3}
is a set of consecutive vertices of
P_{m}
, and diameter of each
X_{i}
is at most
\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3m}-\frac{2\pi}{m}
. Then

2\pi-\frac{6\pi}{m}=\operatorname{diam}X_{1}+\operatorname{diam}X_{2}+\operatorname{diam}X_{3}\leqslant 2\pi-\frac{6\pi}{m}-\frac{2\pi}{m}<2\pi-\frac{6\pi}{m}.

Thus,
d_{GH}(P_{3},P_{m})=\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\pi}{3m}
. Assume that
r=2
. Consider the following partition
D=\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\}\/\colon

X_{1}=\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{q}\};

X_{2}=\{v_{q+1},v_{q+2},...,v_{2q+1}\};

X_{3}=\{v_{2q+2},v_{2[\frac{m}{3}]+2},...,v_{m}\}.

Then
\operatorname{diam}D=(\frac{m-2}{3})\frac{2\pi}{m}=\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{4\pi}{3m}
. Repeating the arguments for the case
r=1
, we get that for any
D\in\mathcal{D}_{3}
we have

\operatorname{diam}D\geqslant\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{4\pi}{3m}.

Thus,
d_{GH}(P_{3},P_{m})=\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3m}
. It completes the proof.
∎

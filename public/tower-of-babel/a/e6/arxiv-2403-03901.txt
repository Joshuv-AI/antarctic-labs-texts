A notion of s s -fractional mass for 1 1 -currents in higher codimension
M. Cicalese, T. Heilmann, A. kubin, F. Onoue, M. Ponsiglione

Abstract
. In this paper we propose a notion of s s -fractional mass for 1 1 -currents in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Such a notion generalizes the notion of s s -fractional perimeters for sets in the plane to higher codimension one-dimensional singularities. Remarkably, the limit as s → 1 s\to 1 of the s s -fractional mass gives back the classical notion of length for regular enough curves in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We prove a lower semi-continuity and compactness result for sequences of 1 1 -currents with uniformly bounded fractional mass and support. Moreover, we prove the density of weighted polygonal, closed and compact oriented curves in the class of divergence-free 1-currents with compact support and finite fractional mass. Finally, we discuss some possible applications of our notion of fractional mass to build up purely geometrical approaches to the variational modeling of dislocation lines in crystals and to vortex filaments in superconductivity. Keywords: Geometric Measure Theory, Rectifiable Curves, Variational Methods, Topological Singularities AMS subject classifications: 49Q15, 49Q20, 28A75, 49J45, 58K45.



Introduction

Nonlocal energy functionals describing long-range interactions consistently garner great interest within the mathematical community.
When concentrated on interfaces or on more general singular sets, nonlocal energy functionals have a geometrical flavor. Nonlocal versions of the classical perimeter were introduced by Visintin in
[
26
]
to model long range dissipation phenomena. These became much more popular when Caffarelli, Roquejoffre, and Savin introduced in
[
5
]
the notion of
s
-fractional perimeters

P_{s}
, defined on any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the squared fractional Sobolev semi-norm of its characteristic function, i.e., by

(0.1)

P_{s}(E)=\int_{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\,dx\,dy\,.

Our aim is to introduce and analyze a class of nonlocal energy functionals defined on oriented curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(namely on
1
-currents), generalizing the notion of fractional perimeters of sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to higher codimension and to not necessarily integer rectifiable
1
-currents. The proposed notion of fractional mass (length) takes into account the cancellation effects hidden in the definition of fractional perimeters. It is well known that the fractional perimeter
P_{s}
does not control the local perimeter, and in fact there are sets with infinite perimeter but finite fractional perimeter; for these sets with finely oscillating boundaries, what makes the fractional perimeter finite are cancellations that occur for pieces of the boundary near to each other but with opposite orientations: Their contributions cancel each other rather than summing up their mass. This suggests to introduce a notion of fractional mass where the nonlocal interactions between pairs of points
x,\,y
are ruled by a kernel depending on the distance
|x-y|
with a (changing sign) pre-factor given by the scalar product between the tangent vectors to the curve at the points
x
and
y
, respectively.
Precisely, the
s
-fractional mass of a smooth curve
\gamma
is defined by

(0.2)

\mathrm{M}_{s}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\tau(x)\cdot\tau(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\

where
\tau(z)
is the tangent unit vector of
\gamma
at
z\in\gamma
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
denotes the standard one dimensional Hausdorff measure.

This notion of fractional mass not only mimics fractional perimeters, but it really extends them; in fact, on the one hand, integrating by parts (
0.1
) one can check that the
s
-fractional perimeter of a set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
coincides (up to a prefactor)
with the fractional mass
\mathrm{M}_{s}
of its (oriented) boundary; on the other hand,
\mathrm{M}_{s}
makes sense also for (not necessarily integer rectifiable)
1
-currents in higher codimension. To understand this and identify the natural domain of
\mathrm{M}_{s}
it is convenient to think in Fourier space.
Given a
1
-form
\gamma
with compact support and finite mass, we consider the corresponding vector valued measure
\mu_{\gamma}
and set (according with (
0.2
))

(0.3)

\mathrm{M}_{s}(\gamma):={c_{d,s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\mathscr{F}[\mu_{\gamma}]|^{2}\,\frac{dx}{|x|^{d-s}}

where
c_{d,s}
is a positive constant depending only on
d
and
s
and
\mathscr{F}[\mu_{\gamma}]
denotes the Fourier transform of
\mu_{\gamma}
.
As a consequence of this representation,
1
-forms with bounded
s
-fractional mass correspond to measurable vector fields in Fourier
space with a suitable (depending on
s
) weighted
L^{2}
norm finite. This observation leads to natural lower semicontinuity (see Proposition
1.13
) and compactness properties in the dual norm of regular enough
1
-forms with compact support (Theorem
3.2
).

In Theorem
5.1
we prove the density of weighted polygonal, closed and compact oriented curves in the class of divergence-free 1-currents with compact support and finite fractional mass. This result can be regarded as a nonlocal extension of the result by Marchese and Wirth in
[
15
]
and Fortuna and Garroni in
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
, related to the so-called Smirnov’s decomposition theorem
[
25
]
concerning the decomposition of divergence-free vector fields through elemental solenoids.

Remarkably, as
s\to 1
, the
s
-fractional mass, suitably scaled, converges to the classical local length (see Proposition
2.1
). This is consistent with the asymptotic analysis developed for
s
-fractional perimeters
[
7
,
1
,
23
]
and Gagliardo seminorms
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
as
s\to 1
. Clearly, replacing
s
with
1
in the definition of
\mathrm{M}_{s}
(
0.2
) would give back an infinite quantity for all non trivial given
\gamma
. On the other hand, such a formally infinite energy functional seems to be relevant in many physical systems; for instance, suitable anisotropic and material dependent variants of
\mathrm{M}_{1}
give back the self energy of dislocation lines in metals (
[
12
, formula 4.44]
). To cut off the infinite core energy, a classical approach, referred to as the core radius approach, consists in introducing a length scale
\varepsilon>0
representing the lattice spacing and in removing the elastic energy in a
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the dislocation line. The resulting energy blows up logarithmically as
\varepsilon
vanishes. This resembles the behavior of the Ginzburg Landau energy for vortex filaments in superconductivity
[
2
]
. In both models, the energy is diffused in a neighborhood of the line singularity, and concentrates on it as
\varepsilon\to 0
. It seems then interesting to introduce a purely geometric counterpart of the
\varepsilon
-regularized dislocations and Ginzburg Landau energy functionals, defined directly on the singular line
\gamma
as

(0.4)

\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}\tau(x)\cdot\tau(y)K_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\ \,,

where
K_{\varepsilon}
takes the form
K_{\varepsilon}(z):=\frac{1}{\max\{|z|,\varepsilon\}}
(or better, suitable variants of it that are strictly monotone also around zero).
In fact, the identification and proposal of this functional is one of the motivations behind this work. Before analyzing such a functional, it seemed appropriate to consider its counterparts
\mathrm{M}_{s}
with finite energy, that is, with

s\in(0,1)
. The analysis of the regularized critical limit case
\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
goes beyond the scopes of this paper, but in our opinion deserves further investigation.

Another natural extension of our theory consists in generalizing the
s
-fractional mass for
1
-currents in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the general case of
k
-currents for any
1\leq k\leq d
.
Recently, several authors have tried to extend the notion of the fractional length or area to more general (smooth) manifolds even with boundary. For instance, Paroni, Podio-Guidugli, and Seguin in
[
21
]
introduced the notion of fractional area for any smooth hypersurface with boundary and computed the Euler-Lagrange equation associated with such a functional (see also
[
22
]
for the notion of fractional curvature tensor). Moreover, Seguin in
[
24
]
introduced a notion of fractional length for (not necessarily closed) smooth curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
.
We also remark that O’Hara introduced an energy functional for smooth curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which is known as the O’Hara energy, in a series of papers
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
. Such an energy functional represents a sort of “electrostatic potential energy” for curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and it takes its minimum when a curve is a circle and it blows up when a curve has a self-intersection. In some respects our proposed
s
-fractional mass represents a natural counterpart of the O’Hara energy taking into account cancellation effects for pieces of curves with opposite orientations, that attract rather than repel each other. While a thin and elongated curve, driven by the O’Hara energy, would luckily converge to a flat disc, the corresponding evolution driven by
\mathrm{M}_{s}
should attract such lines with opposite orientations, leading to their annihilation. This dynamics mirrors exactly the dynamics observed in dislocation and vortex filaments. Our proposed fractional length represents a first attempt to establish a sound variational model for these phenomena within a purely geometric framework.

Proposition 1.1

with

open.

Now we prove that any 1-current with compact support can be uniquely extended to a bounded linear functional on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Here we consider the space of 1-forms
\mathcal{D}^{1}
as the
\mathbb{R}
-subspace of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
with compact support, then there exists a unique extension
\overline{\gamma}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}\,(\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*})
.
Moreover
\overline{\gamma}*\rho_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\overline{\gamma}*\rho_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\gamma}
in the weak star topology of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
satisfy the first axiom of countability (because they are Fréchet spaces), then we can define the extension only by means of converging sequences.
Let
K
be a compact set that contains the support of
\gamma
.
Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
such that
\varphi(x)=1
for all
x\in K
. For all
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
we consider a sequence
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}^{1}
such that
\omega_{n}\rightarrow\omega
in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. We observe that the sequence
\{\varphi\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge to
\varphi\omega
in
\mathcal{D}^{1}
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Therefore, we have that, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\overline{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
n,m>\overline{n}
,

(1.17)

|\gamma(\omega_{n})-\gamma(\omega_{m})|=|\gamma(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})|=|\gamma(\varphi\omega_{n}-\varphi\omega_{m})|\leq\varepsilon

where we have used the continuity of
\gamma
. Hence, we can define
\overline{\gamma}(\omega):=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\gamma(\omega_{n})=\gamma(\varphi\omega)
. Moreover, it is easy to check that the definition of
\overline{\gamma}
does not depend on the chosen sequence and of the specific choice of
\varphi
;
this also shows the uniqueness of the extension and the continuity of the extension and completes the proof of the first assertion. We have that
\overline{\gamma}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
observing that if
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}^{1}
is such that
\omega_{n}\rightarrow\omega
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
then
\omega_{n}\rightarrow\omega
in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

From (
1.14
) and (
1.15
), we have that, for all
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
,

(1.18)

\overline{\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}}(\omega)=\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}(\omega)=\gamma(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\omega)=\overline{\gamma}(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\omega)=\overline{\gamma}*\rho_{\varepsilon}(\omega)

and by density the above identities hold true for all
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Finally, given
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})

we have

(1.19)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\overline{\gamma}*\rho_{\varepsilon}(\omega)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\overline{\gamma}(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\omega)=\overline{\gamma}(\omega)

where we have used that
\rho_{\varepsilon}*\omega\rightarrow\omega
, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
∎

Next we define a function space which represents the natural domain for relaxing the definition of
s-
fractional mass from the class
\Gamma
to the class of 1-currents. To this purpose, we define the weighted
L^{2}
space associated with the kernel
K_{d-s}
as follows:

(1.20)

L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d}):=\left\{f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d}\mid\text{$f$ is measurable and $\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}K_{d-s}(|x|)dx<\infty$}\right\}.

Note that the space
L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
can be endowed with the following norm

\|f\|_{L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\coloneqq\left(c(s,d)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{d-s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(c(s,d)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}K_{d-s}(|x|)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We then define the Fourier transform of
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, which we denote by
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
, as a
\mathbb{C}
-valued functional defined on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Precisely, we define
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
by

(1.21)

\mathscr{F}[\gamma](\omega):=\overline{\gamma}(\mathscr{F}[\omega])

for any
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Here
\overline{\gamma}
is the unique extension of
\gamma
as in Lemma
1.4
. Notice that
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
is
\mathbb{R}
-linear and continuous on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
because
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
is the composition of
\mathbb{R}
-linear, continuous functions
\overline{\gamma}
and
\mathscr{F}
.
Moreover, we can observe that the restriction
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}
is also a bounded linear functional, i.e., a 1-current. In addition, the following Plancherel-type identity for 1-currents holds for any
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
:

(1.22)

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\mathscr{F}[\gamma]\left(\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}\right)=\gamma(\overline{\omega}^{*}).

Indeed, from the Fourier inversion theorem, we have that
\mathscr{F}\left[\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}\right]=(2\pi)^{d}\,\overline{\omega}^{*}
for any
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
. Then we obtain

(1.23)

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\mathscr{F}[\gamma]\left(\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}\right)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\overline{\gamma}\left(\mathscr{F}\left[\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}\right]\right)=\gamma(\overline{\omega}^{*})

for any
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
. Note that the Plancherel-type identity above holds true also when
\overline{\gamma}
is the extension of
\gamma
as in Lemma
1.4
.

We are now in a position to introduce the admissible class
\mathcal{C}_{s}
of 1-currents with compact support suited to define the
s
-fractional mass.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
if
\gamma
is a 1-current with compact support contained in
U

and there exists a (unique)
measurable function
\widehat{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that

(1.24)

\mathscr{F}[\gamma](\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left<\omega(x),\widehat{\gamma}(x)\right>\,dx\quad\text{with $\widehat{\gamma}\in L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})$}

for any
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Moreover, we define the norm of
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
by

\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\coloneqq\|\widehat{\gamma}\|_{L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})}.

In what follows we simply set
\mathcal{C}_{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 1.6
.

We observe that the quantity
\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
for
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
is actually a norm in
\mathcal{C}_{s}
. Indeed, assuming that
\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}=0
, we have, by definition, that
\widehat{\gamma}=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and thus
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]=0
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then, from the Plancherel-type identity, we obtain

\gamma(\overline{\omega}^{*})=\mathscr{F}[\gamma]\left(\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}\right)=0

for any
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
, which implies that
\gamma=0
on
\mathcal{D}^{1}
. The triangle inequality and absolute homogeneity hold true from the definition of
\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
.

Remark 1.7
.

We observe that
\left(\mathcal{C}_{s},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\right)
is separable.
Since
L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
satisfies the second axiom of countability, its subspaces are separable. Moreover, the map

T:\left(\mathcal{C}_{s},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\right)\rightarrow\big(L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\big),\quad T(\gamma):=\hat{\gamma}

is an isometry, therefore
\left(\mathcal{C}_{s},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\right)
is isometric to a topological separable space.

Lemma 1.8
.

If
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
, then
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
can be extended into a linear and continuous functional on
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any bounded open set. By definition, we first observe that
\hat{\gamma}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(U,\mathbb{C}^{d})
. Let
\omega\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then, from the integral representation of
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
, we have that

(1.25)

|\mathscr{F}[\gamma](\omega)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left<\omega(x),\widehat{\gamma}(x)\right>\,dx\right|\leq\|\widehat{\gamma}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(U,\mathbb{C}^{d})}\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,\,1)
. Thus, since
C_{c}^{\infty}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
is dense in
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
, in the same way as in the proof of Lemma
1.4
, we can continuously extend the domain of
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
into
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. This completes the proof.
∎

In the above setting, we define the
s
-fractional mass of 1-currents in
\mathcal{C}_{s}
as follows.

Definition 1.9

(
s
-fractional mass for 1-currents)
.

For any
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
, we define the
s
-fractional mass
M_{s}
of
\gamma
by

(1.26)

M_{s}(\gamma)\coloneqq(2\pi)^{-d}\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}^{2}.

Notice that, by definition, the
s
-fractional mass of
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
can be also written in the following way:

M_{s}(\gamma)=(2\pi)^{-d}\|\widehat{\gamma}\|_{L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})}^{2}=(2\pi)^{-d}c(s,d)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-s}}|\widehat{\gamma}(x)|^{2}\,dx

where
\widehat{\gamma}
is the function in (
1.24
) associated with
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
.

We observe that the
\mathcal{C}_{s}
-norm of smooth curves (namely, the
s
-fractional mass in Definition
1.9
) coincides with the
s
-fractional mass defined in (
1
).
To see this, we first recall the following classical result, see
[
16
, Lemma 12.12]
.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support. Then for all
0<s<d

(1.27)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)=(2\pi)^{-d}c(s,d)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-s}}|\mathscr{F}[\mu](x)|^{2}\,dx,

where
c(s,d)
is the constant of (
1.3
) for
\alpha=s
.

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
; then
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
and
\mathrm{M}_{s}(\gamma)=M_{s}(\gamma)
where
\mathrm{M}_{s}
is as in (
1
) and
M_{s}
is as in Definition
1.9
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
as in (
1.4
). With a slight abuse of notation we identify
\gamma
with its support, define for all
k=1,\dots,d
the Radon measure
\mu_{k}(A):=\int_{A\cap\gamma}\tau\cdot e_{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}
and set
\mu\coloneqq(\mu_{1},\,\cdots,\mu_{d})
.
By Lemma
1.10

we can write

(1.28)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{s}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{\gamma_{i}}\int_{\gamma_{j}}\frac{\tau_{i}(x)\cdot\tau_{j}(y)}{|y-x|^{s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y)

(1.29)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}\frac{(e_{k}\cdot\tau(x))(e_{k}\cdot\tau(y))}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mu_{k}(x)d\mu_{k}(y)

(1.30)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}(2\pi)^{-d}c(s,d)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-s}}|\mathscr{F}[\mu_{k}](x)|^{2}\,dx

(1.31)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}c(s,d)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-s}}|\mathscr{F}[\mu](x)|^{2}\,dx.

Moreover, since
\gamma\in\Gamma
can be identified with the 1-current
\mu
, we may observe that

\mathscr{F}[\gamma](\omega)\coloneqq\overline{\gamma}(\mathscr{F}[\omega])=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\mathscr{F}[\omega],\mu\rangle\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\omega,\mathscr{F}[\mu]\rangle\,dx

for any
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
and the representative
\widehat{\gamma}
of
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
is equal to
\mathscr{F}[\mu]
.
Finally, from (
1.31
), we conclude that
\widehat{\gamma}\in L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
and thus the claim holds true.
∎

The next lemma shows that the convolution of a 1-current
\gamma
in
\mathcal{C}_{s}
with the standard mollifiers converge to
\gamma
in the weak star topology of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and that the
s
-fractional mass is continuous under this convergence. Before proving the lemma we recall that
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{1}
converges to
\gamma
in the weak star topology of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
if
\gamma_{n}(\omega)\to\gamma(\omega)
as
n\to\infty
for any
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
. Then
\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\gamma
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in the weak star topology of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\|\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\rightarrow\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

The proof that
\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\gamma
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in the weak star topology of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a standard result in the theory of currents. Hence we only prove that
\|\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\rightarrow\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

We first check that
\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
. We set
k(x)\coloneqq|x|^{-\frac{d-s}{2}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
, we can choose the unique representative
\widehat{\gamma}\in L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
as in Definition
1.5
. Noticing that
k\widehat{\gamma}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
, then we may compute the Fourier transform of
\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}](\omega)=\overline{\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}}(\mathscr{F}[\omega])=\overline{\gamma}(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\mathscr{F}[\omega])

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma}\left(\mathscr{F}\left[\mathscr{F}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\,\omega\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\mathscr{F}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\,k^{-1}\omega(x),k\widehat{\gamma}(x)\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\mathscr{F}\left[\rho_{\varepsilon}*\mathscr{F}^{-1}[k^{-1}\omega]\right],k\widehat{\gamma}\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\mathscr{F}^{-1}[k^{-1}\omega],\rho_{\varepsilon}*\mathscr{F}\left[k\widehat{\gamma}\right]\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\omega,\mathscr{F}^{-1}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\widehat{\gamma}\rangle\,dx

for any
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Therefore, the (unique) representative of
\mathscr{F}[\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}]
is
\mathscr{F}^{-1}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\widehat{\gamma}
. Moreover, since
\widehat{\gamma}\in L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
it follows that
\mathscr{F}^{-1}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\,\widehat{\gamma}\in L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
, i.e.,
\gamma*\rho_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
. Finally, since for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds true that
\mathscr{F}^{-1}[\rho_{\varepsilon}](\xi)\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we conclude

\|\mathscr{F}^{-1}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\,\widehat{\gamma}\|_{L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\to\|\widehat{\gamma}\|_{L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\eqqcolon\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\qquad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

∎

Proposition 1.13

(Lower semi-continuity)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded. Let
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
be a sequence converging to some
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
in the weak star topology of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
. Then,
M_{s}(\gamma)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{s}(\gamma_{n})
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{s}(\gamma_{n})<\infty
; otherwise the claim is obvious and, up to passing to
a further subsequence, that the liminf is actually a limit.
We set
k(x)=(\mathscr{F}[K_{s}](x))^{1/2}=|x|^{-\frac{d-s}{2}}
. Then it follows that
k\,\widehat{\gamma}_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
have uniformly bounded norm and hence, up to a subsequence,
they weakly converge to some
\sigma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
. We set
\widetilde{\sigma}:=k^{-1}\sigma\in L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
; now the claim follows
from the lower semicontinuity of the norm, once we check that
\widetilde{\sigma}
is the representative of
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
. To this end, let
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. From the Fourier inversion theorem, there exists a function
\eta\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\omega=\overline{\mathscr{F}[\eta]}^{*}
. Then, recalling the notion of the extension of 1-currents as in Lemma
1.4
, we can compute
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[\gamma](\omega)=\mathscr{F}[\gamma](\overline{\mathscr{F}[\eta]}^{*})=(2\pi)^{d}\,\overline{\gamma}(\overline{\eta}^{*})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}(2\pi)^{d}\,\overline{\gamma}_{n}(\overline{\eta}^{*})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathscr{F}[\gamma_{n}](\overline{\mathscr{F}[\eta]}^{*})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k^{-1}\,\overline{\mathscr{F}[\eta]}^{*},k\,\widehat{\gamma}_{n}\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\overline{\mathscr{F}[\eta]}^{*},\widetilde{\sigma}\rangle\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\omega,\widetilde{\sigma}\rangle\,dx.

This concludes the proof that
\widetilde{\sigma}
is the representative of
\mathscr{F}[\gamma]
.

∎

2.
Asymptotic behavior of the
s
-fractional mass and

s
-fractional curvature for smooth curves as
s\uparrow 1

Now we show that our definition of
s
-fractional mass is consistent with the notion of classical length of a smooth curve as the parameter
s
goes to 1.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\ell>0
and let
\gamma:[0,\,\ell]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arc-length parametrization of a simple
C^{1,1}
-curve (not necessarily closed).
Then,

\lim_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)\,M_{s}(\gamma)=2\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma)(=2\ell).

Proof.

Since
\gamma
is of class
C^{1,1}
(and hence
\ddot{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}
), by the foundamental theorem of calculus we have

\gamma(v)-\gamma(u)=(v-u)\,\dot{\gamma}(u)+(v-u)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\ddot{\gamma}(u+t(v-u))\,dt

and

(2.1)

\dot{\gamma}(v)-\dot{\gamma}(u)=(v-u)\,\int_{0}^{1}\ddot{\gamma}(u+t(v-u))\,dt

for any
u,\,v\in[0,\,\ell]
. Thus, recalling that
|\dot{\gamma}(u)|^{2}=1
we obtain

\displaystyle|\gamma(v)-\gamma(u)|^{2}

\displaystyle=(v-u)^{2}+2(v-u)^{3}\,\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)(\ddot{\gamma}(u+t(v-u))\cdot\dot{\gamma}(u))\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad+(v-u)^{4}\,\left|\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\ddot{\gamma}(u+t(v-u))\,dt\right|^{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=(v-u)^{2}\left(1+2(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+(v-u)^{2}\,I_{2}(u,v,\gamma)\right)

for any
u,\,v\in[0,\,\ell]
where we defined
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
as

I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)(\ddot{\gamma}(u+t(v-u))\cdot\dot{\gamma}(u))\,dt\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2}(u,v,\gamma)\coloneqq\left|\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\ddot{\gamma}(u+t(v-u))\,dt\right|^{2}

for
u,\,v\in[0,\,\ell]
.
Recalling that
\gamma\in C^{1,1}
is simple, there exists
c>0
such that for any
u,\,v\in[0,\,\ell]
it holds

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{c}\leq 2(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+(v-u)^{2}\,I_{2}(u,v,\gamma)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle|2(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+(v-u)^{2}\,I_{2}(u,v,\gamma)|<c|u-v|.

Now, using (
2.2
) and the expansion
(1+x)^{-s}=1-sx+O(|x|^{2})
for
x\geq-\frac{1}{c}
where
|O(r)|\leq C|r|
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
and some constant
C>0
independent of
u
and
v
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\gamma(v)-\gamma(u)|^{-s}

\displaystyle=(v-u)^{-s}\left(1+2(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+(v-u)^{2}\,I_{2}(u,v,\gamma)\right)^{-\frac{s}{2}}

\displaystyle=|v-u|^{-s}\left(1-s(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)-\frac{s}{2}(v-u)^{2}\,I_{2}(u,v,\gamma)+O(|v-u|^{2})\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle=|v-u|^{-s}\left(1-s(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+O(|v-u|^{2})\right)

for any
u,\,v\in[0,\,\ell]
with
u\neq v
. Notice that since
\gamma
is a compact curve with bounded curvature
|\gamma(u)-\gamma(v)|
is uniformly bounded away from zero. Thus, by recalling the definition of the
s
-fractional mass for
\gamma
and from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we obtain that

\displaystyle M_{s}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\gamma\times\gamma}\frac{\tau_{\gamma}(x)\cdot\tau_{\gamma}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\iint_{\{|u-v|\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{\dot{\gamma}(u)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)}{|\gamma(u)-\gamma(v)|^{s}}\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\iint_{\{|u-v|\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{1-\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\gamma}(u)-\dot{\gamma}(v)|^{2}}{|v-u|^{s}}\left\{1-s(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+o(|v-u|)\right\}\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\iint_{\{|u-v|\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{1}{|v-u|^{s}}\,du^{,}dv

\displaystyle\qquad+\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\iint_{\{|u-v|\geq\varepsilon\}}\left\{-s\,\mathrm{sgn}\;(v-u)|v-u|^{1-s}\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+o(|v-u|^{1-s})\right\}\,du\,dv

(2.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\iint_{\{|u-v|\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{|\dot{\gamma}(u)-\dot{\gamma}(v)|^{2}}{2|v-u|^{s}}\left\{1-s(v-u)\,I_{1}(u,v,\gamma)+o(|u-v|)\right\}\,du\,dv

Notice that, from (
2.1
) and the definitions of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, the second and third terms in the right-hand side of (
2.4
) converge, and are uniformly bounded in
s\in(0,\,1)
.
Therefore, taking all of the above arguments into account, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)M_{s}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\uparrow 1}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\iint_{\{|v-u|\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{1}{|v-u|^{s}}\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)\left\{\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{v}^{\ell}\frac{du}{(u-v)^{s}}\,dv+\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{0}^{v}\frac{du}{(v-u)^{s}}\,dv\right\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)\,\frac{2\ell^{2-s}}{(1-s)(2-s)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\uparrow 1}2\ell^{2-s}=2\ell.

∎

Let us remark that Seguin in
[
24
]
introduced a notion of
s
-fractional length for smooth curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and computed the corresponding limit as
s\uparrow 1
, obtaining that the
s
-fractional length (according with his definition) of any compact,
C^{1}
-curve (not necessarily closed) converges to its classical length up to proper constants (see
[
24
, Theorem 3.2]
for the detail).

In the next proposition we compute the limit as
s\uparrow 1
of the
s
-fractional curvature for smooth curves.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\gamma:[-\frac{\ell}{2},\frac{\ell}{2}]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arc-length parametrization of a simple
C^{2}
-curve (not necessarily closed). Then, it holds that, for all
u\in(-\frac{\ell}{2},\frac{\ell}{2})
,

(2.5)

\lim_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)k_{s}(u,\gamma)=-\ddot{\gamma}(u).

where
k_{s}(u,\gamma)
is the
s
-fractional curvature of
\gamma
at
u
as in Definition
1.3
.

Proof.

We recall that
k_{s}
is defined as

(2.6)

k_{s}(u,\gamma)=s\int_{-\frac{\ell}{2}}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\frac{\dot{\gamma}(v)(\gamma(u)-\gamma(v))\cdot\dot{\gamma}(u)-\dot{\gamma}(u)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)(\gamma(u)-\gamma(v))}{|\gamma(u)-\gamma(v)|^{2+s}}dv.

We prove the formula (
2.5
) for
u=0
.
Up to a translation we have
\gamma(0)=0
. Let
\eta>0
be fixed, since
\gamma\in C^{2}
then for
\delta
small enough we have for all
v\in(-\delta,\delta)

(2.7)

\gamma(v)=\dot{\gamma}(0)v+\ddot{\gamma}(0)\frac{v^{2}}{2}+R_{1}(\gamma,v)v^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\dot{\gamma}(v)=\dot{\gamma}(0)+\ddot{\gamma}(0)v+R_{2}(\gamma,v)v

where
|R_{1}(\gamma,v)|\leq\eta
and
|R_{2}(\gamma,v)|\leq\eta
for all
v\in(-\delta,\delta)
.
We have that

(2.8)

\begin{split}&-\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0)+\dot{\gamma}(0)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)\\
&=-(v+v^{2}R_{1}(\gamma,v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0))[\dot{\gamma}(0)+\ddot{\gamma}(0)v+R_{2}(\gamma,v)v]\\
&+(1+vR_{2}(\gamma,v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0))[\dot{\gamma}(0)v+\ddot{\gamma}(0)\frac{v^{2}}{2}+R_{1}(\gamma,v)v^{2}]\\
&=-\ddot{\gamma}(0)\frac{v^{2}}{2}+R_{3}(\gamma,v)v^{2}\end{split}

where
|R_{3}(\gamma,v)|\leq\eta
up to taking
\delta>0
sufficiently small. Moreover, again for
\delta>0
sufficiently small, we have that, for all
v\in(-\delta,\delta)
,

(2.9)

\gamma(v)=\gamma(0)+(\dot{\gamma}(0)+R_{4}(\gamma,v))v=(\dot{\gamma}(0)+R_{4}(\gamma,v))v

where
|R_{4}(\gamma,v)|\leq\eta
.
We observe that

(2.10)

\lim_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\int_{[-\frac{\ell}{2},\,\frac{\ell}{2}]\setminus(-\delta,\delta)}\frac{-\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0)+\dot{\gamma}(0)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)}{|\gamma(v)|^{2+s}}dv=0.

Now we fix a co-vector
\lambda\in\Lambda^{1}
and assume, without loss of generality, that
\lambda(\ddot{\gamma}(0))\leq 0
. Note that for
\eta>0
small enough (
2.9
) gives
|v|^{2+s}(1-4\eta)\leq|\gamma(v)|^{2+s}\leq|v|^{2+s}(1+4\eta)
. Hence, using (
2.8
), (
2.9
), and (
2.10
), we have that

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\liminf_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{v^{2}(\lambda(-\frac{\ddot{\gamma}(0)}{2}+R_{3}(\gamma,v)))}{|v|^{2+s}(1+4\eta)}dv\\
&\leq\liminf_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\lambda\bigg(\int_{-\frac{\ell}{2}}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\frac{-\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0)+\dot{\gamma}(0)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)}{|\gamma(v)|^{2+s}}dv\bigg)\\
&\leq\limsup_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\lambda\bigg(\int_{-\frac{\ell}{2}}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\frac{-\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0)+\dot{\gamma}(0)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)}{|\gamma(v)|^{2+s}}dv\bigg)\\
&\leq\limsup_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{v^{2}(\lambda(-\frac{\ddot{\gamma}(0)}{2}+R_{3}(\gamma,v)))}{|v|^{2+s}(1-4\eta)}dv.\end{split}

Moreover, we have that

(2.12)

\begin{split}\frac{-\lambda(\ddot{\gamma}(0))+f_{1}(\eta)}{1+4\eta}&=\liminf_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{v^{2}(\lambda(-\frac{\ddot{\gamma}(0)}{2})-|\lambda|\eta)}{|v|^{2+s}(1+4\eta)}dv\\
&\leq\liminf_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{v^{2}(\lambda(-\frac{\ddot{\gamma}(0)}{2}+R_{3}(\gamma,v)))}{|v|^{2+s}(1+4\eta)}dv;\\
&\leq\limsup_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{v^{2}(\lambda(-\frac{\ddot{\gamma}(0)}{2})+R_{3}(\gamma,v))}{|v|^{2+s}(1-4\eta)}dv\\
&\leq\limsup_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{v^{2}(\lambda(-\frac{\ddot{\gamma}(0)}{2}+|\lambda|\eta))}{|v|^{2+s}(1-4\eta)}dv\\
&=\frac{-\lambda(\ddot{\gamma}(0))+f_{2}(\eta)}{1-4\eta}\end{split}

where
f_{1}(\eta),\,f_{2}(\eta)\rightarrow 0
as
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
Therefore by (
2.10
), (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and sending
\eta
to
0
we have that

(2.13)

\begin{split}-\lambda(\ddot{\gamma}(0))&\leq\liminf_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\lambda\bigg(\int_{-\frac{\ell}{2}}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\frac{-\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0)+\dot{\gamma}(0)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)}{|\gamma(v)|^{2+s}}dv\bigg)\\
&\leq\limsup_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)s\lambda\bigg(\int_{-\frac{\ell}{2}}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\frac{-\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(0)+\dot{\gamma}(0)\cdot\dot{\gamma}(v)\gamma(v)}{|\gamma(v)|^{2+s}}dv\bigg)=-\lambda(\ddot{\gamma}(0)).\end{split}

Hence (
2.5
) follows by the arbitrariness
of
\lambda\in\Lambda^{1}
.
∎

3.
Compactness

In this section we provide a compactness result for 1-currents with finite
s
-fractional mass.
To this end we first show that any
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
supported in
U
can be continuously extended to a linear functional defined on
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set,
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k>d-\frac{s}{2}
and
\alpha\in(0,\,1]
. Then, for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
, there exists a unique extension
\overline{\gamma}:C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}
of
\gamma
such that
\|\overline{\gamma}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}}\leq C\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
for some
C>0
, where

\|\overline{\gamma}\|_{(C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}))^{*}}\coloneqq\sup\left\{\overline{\gamma}(\omega)\mid\,\omega\in C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}),\,\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\leq 1\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Since, for all
|\beta|\leq k
,

\|D^{\beta}\omega\|_{L^{1}}\leq|U|\|D^{\beta}\omega\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\omega\|_{C^{k}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}|U|<+\infty,

then we have

|\mathscr{F}[\omega](\xi)|\leq\|\omega\|_{C^{k}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}|U||\xi|^{-k}

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, recalling (
1.23
), we have

\displaystyle|\gamma(\omega)|

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}|\mathscr{F}[\gamma](\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*})|

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\langle\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}(\xi),\widehat{\gamma}(\xi)\rangle|\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|\langle\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}(\xi),\widehat{\gamma}(\xi)\rangle|\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\,|U|\,\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}\,\left\|\widehat{\gamma}|\xi|^{\frac{s-d}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left(|B_{R}(0)|+\left\||\xi|^{\frac{d-s}{2}-k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(0))}\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}<+\infty

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\omega
. Thus, by the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}
in
C^{k,\alpha}_{0}
, we obtain that
\gamma
can be continuously extended into a functional on
C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
with a finite dual norm.
∎

For all
\lambda>0
, let
\Phi_{\lambda}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the probability density function of a Gaussian distribution with variance equal to
\lambda
, i.e.,

(3.3)

\Phi_{\lambda}(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi\lambda^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}}}\exp(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}}).

Theorem 3.2

(Compactness)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set and let
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
be such that
\|\gamma_{n}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}\leq C
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k>d-\frac{s}{2}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we have
\|\overline{\gamma}_{n}\|_{C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}}\leq C
. As a consequence, there exists a subsequence
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}\to\gamma
in
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
for some
\gamma\in C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
with
\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1
and the assumption that
\sup_{n}\|\gamma_{n}\|_{C_{s}}\leq C
, we obtain that the first part of Theorem
3.2
holds true.

To prove the second part of the theorem we show that there exists a subsequence
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which is a Cauchy sequence in
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
; if this holds true, then, by the completeness of
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
,
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some element in
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
for all
k>d-\frac{s}{2}
.
Let
\omega\in C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
with
\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\leq 1
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Recalling the definition of
\Phi_{\lambda}
in (
3.3
), we have that there exists
\lambda=\lambda_{\varepsilon}\in(0,\,1)
such that
\Phi_{\lambda}*|\cdot|^{\alpha}(0)<\varepsilon
. Then we have
that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|(\overline{\gamma}_{n}-\overline{\gamma}_{n}*\Phi_{\lambda})(\omega)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|\overline{\gamma}_{n}(\omega-\Phi_{\lambda}*\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\overline{\gamma}_{n}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}}\|\omega-\Phi_{\lambda}*\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\omega-\Phi_{\lambda}*\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi_{\lambda}(x)|x|^{\alpha}\,dx

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\Phi_{\lambda}*|\cdot|^{\alpha}(0)<\varepsilon.

We now show that the sequence
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinous in
\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda}
where we define
\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda}
by

(3.5)

\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda}:=\left\{\omega*\Phi_{\lambda}\mid\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U)}\leq 1\right\}.

Note that
\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda}
is a compact subset of
C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{U},\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
thanks to the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem. Given any two functions
\omega,\widetilde{\omega}\in C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
with
\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\leq 1
and
\|\widetilde{\omega}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\leq 1
, we have that

\displaystyle|\overline{\gamma}_{n}(\omega*\Phi_{\lambda})-\overline{\gamma}_{n}(\widetilde{\omega}*\Phi_{\lambda})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\overline{\gamma}_{n}\|_{C_{0}^{k}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}}\|\omega*\Phi_{\lambda}-\widetilde{\omega}*\Phi_{\lambda}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\omega*\Phi_{\lambda}-\widetilde{\omega}*\Phi_{\lambda}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})},

which implies the equicontinuity of
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the topological space
C\left((\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda},\|\cdot\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}),\mathbb{R}\right)
.

Therefore, from the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem, we obtain that there exists a subsequence
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, which is a Cauchy sequence in
C((\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda},\|\cdot\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}),\mathbb{R})
. In other words, we can choose
I\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
i,\,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
i,\,j\geq I
,

\|\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}-\overline{\gamma}_{n_{j}}\|_{C\left((\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda},\|\cdot\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}),\mathbb{R}\right)}<\varepsilon

where we set

(3.6)

\|f\|_{C\left((\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda},\|\cdot\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}),\mathbb{R}\right)}\coloneqq\sup\left\{|f(u*\Phi_{\lambda})|\mid u\in C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}),\,u*\Phi_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda}\right\}

for any
f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
.

Finally, by recalling (
3
), we have that, for any
i,\,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
i,\,j\geq I
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\leq 1}|(\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}-\overline{\gamma}_{n_{j}})(\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\leq 1}\bigg(|(\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}-\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}*\Phi_{\lambda})(\omega)|+|(\overline{\gamma}_{n_{j}}-\overline{\gamma}_{n_{j}}*\Phi_{\lambda})(\omega)|\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{\omega*\Phi_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda}}|\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}(\omega*\Phi_{\lambda})-\overline{\gamma}_{n_{j}}(\widetilde{\omega}*\Phi_{\lambda})|

\displaystyle<2\varepsilon+\|\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}-\overline{\gamma}_{n_{j}}\|_{C\left((\mathfrak{C}_{\lambda(\varepsilon)},\|\cdot\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}),\mathbb{R}\right)}

(3.8)

\displaystyle<3\varepsilon.

Therefore,
\overline{\gamma}_{n_{i}}\to\gamma
in
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
for some
\gamma\in C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
.

We finally prove that the restriction
\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}
to
\mathcal{D}^{1}(U)\subset C^{k,\alpha}_{0}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
. Indeed, in the same way as in Proposition
1.13
, we can choose a unique function
\widetilde{\sigma}\in L_{\mathscr{F}[K_{s}]}^{2}(U,\mathbb{C}^{d})
such that

\mathscr{F}[\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}](\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\eta,\widetilde{\sigma}\rangle\,dx

for any
\eta\in\mathcal{S}(U,\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Since
\mathrm{supp}\;\gamma
is compact in
U
, so is
\mathrm{supp}\;\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}
and thus
\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{s}(U)
.

∎

4.
Characterization of the boundary of the sets of finite
s
-fractional perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a measurable set;
we can define a 2-current
[E]\in\mathcal{D}_{2}

associated to
E
by
[E]:=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\mathcal{L}^{2}\lfloor_{E}
.
In the next theorem we prove that the
s
-fractional mass of the boundary of
[E]
, in the sense of currents, coincides, up to a constant prefactor,
to the
s
-fractional perimeter of
E
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded set such that
P_{s}(E)<+\infty
. Then
\partial[E]\in\mathcal{C}_{s}
and
\frac{1}{s^{2}}M_{s}(\partial[E])=P_{s}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
R>0
be such that
E\subset\subset B_{R}
and let
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of sets such that
E_{n}\subset B_{R}(0)
,
\partial E_{n}
is of class
C^{\infty}
,
\mathcal{L}^{2}(E_{n}\Delta E)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, and
P_{s}(E_{n})\rightarrow P_{s}(E)<+\infty
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
; we can choose such a sequence thanks to
[
14
]
. We set
\gamma_{n}:=\partial[E_{n}]
. By Proposition
1.1
, we have that
P_{s}(E_{n})=\frac{1}{s^{2}}M_{s}(\gamma_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and, thus, we have that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\gamma_{n}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{s}}<+\infty
. Hence, by Theorem
3.2
, we obtain that
\{\overline{\gamma}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a precompact set in
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(B_{R}(0),\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))^{*}
for all
k>2-\frac{s}{2}
where
\overline{\gamma}_{n}
is the extension of
\gamma_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
as in Proposition
3.1
. Hence, up to a subsequence,
\overline{\gamma}_{n}\rightarrow\gamma
in
C_{0}^{k,\alpha}(B_{R}(0),\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))^{*}
with
\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}\in\mathcal{C}_{s}(\overline{B_{R}(0)})
for all
k>2-\frac{s}{2}
. Therefore, in particular,
\partial[E_{n}]=\gamma_{n}\to\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}
in the weak star topology of
\mathcal{D}_{1}(\overline{B_{R}(0)})
and hence
\partial[E]=\gamma\lfloor_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}
.

We now claim that
\frac{1}{s^{2}}M_{s}(\partial[E])=P_{s}(E)
. We first observe that, for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\partial[E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}=\partial\left([E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}\right)
where
\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is the family of the Friedrich mollifiers. We may also observe that the representative of the Fourier transform of the boundary of the
2
-current
[E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}
is
\mathscr{F}[\nabla^{\perp}(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{E})]
where
\nabla^{\perp}u(x)\coloneqq(-\partial_{2}u(x),\,\partial_{1}u(x))
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Indeed, letting
\eta\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
, we can choose
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
such that
\eta=\overline{\mathscr{F}[\omega]}^{*}
. Then, from the Plancherel-type theorem and the divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\left[\partial([E]*\rho_{\varepsilon})\right]\left(\eta\right)=(2\pi)^{2}\,\overline{\partial\left([E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}\right)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle d\omega,(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{E})\,e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle\omega,\nabla^{\perp}\left(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{E}\right)\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle\mathscr{F}\left[\eta\right],\nabla^{\perp}\left(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{E}\right)\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle\eta,\mathscr{F}[\nabla^{\perp}\left(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{E}\right)]\rangle\,dx.

Here
\overline{\partial\left([E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}\right)}
is the extension of
\partial\left([E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}\right)
as in Lemma
1.4
. This implies that the representative of the Fourier transform of the boundary of
[E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}
is
\mathscr{F}[\nabla^{\perp}(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{E})]
.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle M_{s}(\partial[E]*\rho_{\varepsilon})=M_{s}(\partial([E]*\rho_{\varepsilon}))

5.
Approximation theorem

Our last result in this paper is the approximation of a divergence-free vector field with finite
s
-fractional mass by closed piecewise-linear curves. Precisely, we prove that, for any divergence-free vector field with compact support and finite
s
-fractional mass, there exists a sequence of closed piecewise-linear curves such that the corresponding Radon measures associated with the curves converge to the vector field and its
s
-fractional mass also converge to that of the vector field.

The idea of the proof is inspired by the one by Fortuna and Garroni in
[
10
]
and it goes as follows: we first decompose the support of a divergence-free vector field into finite number of small cubes. Then, in each cube, we construct a finite number of curves (lines) whose direction is almost parallel to the average of the vector field in the cube. To construct these lines, we consider the flux of the vector field on each face of each cube and, according to each flux, we choose a lattice whose size is much smaller than that of the cube. By “properly” choosing points on the lattice on each face, we could define a closed piecewise-constant “curve” (strictly speaking, some Radon measure supported on a piecewise-constant curves) such that it approximates the vector field in the sense of measure and such that its
s
-fractional mass converges to that of the vector field. Note that the approximating curves lie in a “small” neighborhood of the support of the vector field and, at this point, our strategy is different from the one by Fortuna and Garroni in
[
10
]
, where the approximating curves can stretch to infinity.

Conclusions

In this paper we have proposed a notion of
s
-fractional mass for
1
-currents in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
s
belongs to
(0,1)
. It is natural to ask what happens for the limit cases corresponding to
s=0
and
s=1
. We have done a partial progress in this program, showing that, for regular enough curves, the limit as
s\to 1
gives back the classical notion of length. This pointwise result could be investigated in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence, as done for
s
-fractional perimeters in
[
7
,
1
]
.

Moreover, in the Introduction we have suggested the following notion of
\varepsilon
-regularized mass for
s
equal to
1
:

(5.14)

\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\tau(x)\cdot\tau(y)}{\max\{|x-y|,\varepsilon\}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\ \,.

We expect that such an energy functional (in its material dependent declinations) could represent a purely geometric counterpart to Ginzburg Landau energy functionals for vortex filaments, as well as to the self energy of dislocation lines in crystals. In this respect, it seems interesting to study the
\Gamma
-convergence of these functionals, after scaling them by
\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}
, to line tension models, as
\varepsilon\to 0

[
11
,
8
]
. We also remark that, for technical reasons, it could be convenient to replace the denominator
{\max\{|x-y|,\varepsilon\}}
with a function which is strictly increasing also around zero.
The study of the limit as
s\to 0
of the
s
-fractional mass could also deserve some attention.

As explained in the Introduction, the proposed notion of
s
-fractional mass generalizes that of
s
-fractional perimeters of sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to higher codimention and not necessarily integer rectifiable currents.
In fact, as for sets with finite fractional perimeter, there is no reason to expects that the
1
-currents with bounded fractional mass are rectifiable; now, a natural question arises: what are integer (not necessarily rectifiable) currents in this context? One possibility is to exploit our Smirnov type approximation result, defining the integer currents with finite mass as the closure of polyhedral
1
-currents with respect to the notion of convergence in Theorem
5.1
. Now, take an integer (i.e., limit of smooth curves)
1
-current
\gamma
without boundary and with finite
s
-fractional mass; is there a
2
-current, with bounded area (i.e., bounded local mass) whose boundary is
\gamma
? Furthermore, is the mass controlled by the
s
-fractional mass of its boundary? In the terminology of geometric measure theory, we ask if the fractional mass controls the flat norm. In the flat case of 1-currents in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
this is true, being nothing but the fractional isoperimetric inequality.
A positive answer to the questions posed above would suggest that integer
1
-currents with bounded fractional mass are a good class of boundary data for setting up the Plateau problems for minimal surfaces.

Let us also mention that our definition of higher codimensional fractional mass could be generalized to the case of
k
-forms in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
1\leq k\leq d
. Finally, also the case
k=0
corresponding to charged masses could be investigated.

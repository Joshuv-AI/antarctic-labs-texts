One can obtain all continuous t-norms by ordinal sums of the product t-norm and Łukasiewicz t-norm.

[0,1]^{2}
. For each
(p,q)\in S
, without loss of generality, let

. Then one has that both
(p,p)=(p,q)\wedge(q,p)\in S
and
(q,q)=(p,q)\vee(q,p)\in S
. Therefore, the subset

K=\{p\in[0,1]\mid(p,p)\in S\}

is a complete sublattice of
[0,1]
and
(K,\&,1)
is a complete subquantale of
([0,1],\&,1)
. Furthermore, it holds that

K_{\Delta}\subseteq S\subseteq K^{2}.

Since
[0,1]\text{-}{\mathbf{Cat}}_{K_{\Delta}}
is a stable subconstruct of
[0,1]\text{-}{\mathbf{Cat}}
and it is also contained in
[0,1]\text{-}{\mathbf{Cat}}_{S}
, it is a stable subcategory of
[0,1]\text{-}{\mathbf{Cat}}_{S}
. Hence, it is cartesian closed by Lemma
4.12
. By Theorem
4.16
, it follows that
a\&a
is idempotent for all

. That means,
S\subseteq M^{2}
as desired.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

We describe the complete subquantale

(1)

Let

(2)

(3)

Let

(4)

Let

5

\mathbf{YCCat}

In this section, we show that, for each complete subquantale
(K,\&,1)
of
(M,\&,1)

K
-categories and Yoneda continuous
[0,1]
-functors form a cartesian closed category.

Definition 5.19
.

A net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
in a
[0,1]
-category
(X,r)
is
forward Cauchy

[
26
,
27
]
if

\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu\leq\gamma}r(x_{\mu},x_{\gamma})=1.

A forward Cauchy net converges to
a\in X
if

r(a,x)=\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}r(x_{\mu},x)

for all
x\in X
, and
a
is called a
Yoneda limit
of
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
, denoted as
a=\mathrm{lim}x_{\lambda}.

A
[0,1]
-category
(X,r)
is
Yoneda complete
if every forward Cauchy net in
(X,r)
converges. For a
[0,1]
-functor
f:(X,r)\longrightarrow(Y,s)
and a forward Cauchy net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
in
(X,r)
,
\{f(x_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in D}
is clearly a forward Cauchy net in
(Y,s)
, the
[0,1]
-functor
f
is
Yoneda continuous
if it preserves the Yoneda limit of all forward Cauchy nets in
(X,r)
, that is,
f(\mathrm{lim}x_{\lambda})=\mathrm{lim}f(x_{\lambda})
for all forward Cauchy nets
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
in
(X,r)
.
All Yoneda complete
[0,1]
-categories and Yoneda continuous
[0,1]
-functors constitute a category

\mathbf{YCCat}.

Proposition 5.24

Definition 5.25

Let

Lemma 5.26
.

Let
(X,r)
be a
[0,1]
-category and
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
be an eventually
\alpha
-monotone net in

for some idempotent element
\alpha
of
\&
. Then there is some
\lambda_{0}\in D
such that for all
\mu\geq\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,

\alpha\wedge r(x_{\mu},x)\leq\alpha\wedge r(x_{\lambda},x)

for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

Since
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
is eventually
\alpha
-monotone, there is some
\lambda_{0}
such that
\alpha\leq r(x_{\lambda},x_{\mu})
for all
\mu\geq\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. Thus, one has that

\alpha\wedge r(x_{\mu},x)=\alpha\wedge\alpha\wedge r(x_{\mu},x)=\alpha\wedge(\alpha\&r(x_{\mu},x))\leq\alpha\wedge\big(r(x_{\lambda},x_{\mu})\&r(x_{\mu},x)\big)\leq\alpha\wedge r(x_{\lambda},x)

for all
\mu\geq\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and all
x\in X
.
∎

Recall from
[
13
]
that
x
is
way below

y
(written as
x\ll y
) in a directed complete poset
(L,\leq)
if for all directed subset

,

always implies the existence of a
d\in D
with
x\leq d
. An element

is

if
x\ll x
.

Proposition 5.27
.

In the complete subquantale
(M,\&,1)
of
([0,1],\&,1)
, denote the set of all idempotent elements of
\&
by
\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}
, it holds that

1=\bigvee\{\alpha\in\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}\mid\alpha\ll 1\text{ in }M\}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
and Remark
4.18
, we divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1
: There is some Archimedean block
[a,b]
of the t-norm
\&
satisfying that
b=1
. One can see that the top element
1\in M
is an isolated point in
M
. Thus, one has that
1\ll 1
in
M
and
1=\bigvee\{\alpha\in\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}\mid\alpha\ll 1\text{ in }M\}
.

Case 2
: Every Archimedean block
[a,b]
of the t-norm
\&
satisfies that
b<1
. One has that the top element
1\in M
is not isolated in
\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}
, hence,
1=\bigvee\{\alpha\in\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}\mid\alpha<1\}
. Furthermore, for all idempotent
\alpha
with
\alpha<1
, it holds that
\alpha\ll 1
in

. Thus, one obtains that
1=\bigvee\{\alpha\in\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}\mid\alpha\ll 1\text{ in }M\}
as desired.

∎

Lemma 5.28
.

Each forward Cauchy net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
in an
M
-category
(X,r)
is eventually
\alpha
-monotone for all elements
\alpha\ll 1
in the complete sublattice
M
.

Proof.

Notice that

\{\alpha_{\lambda}=\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu\leq\gamma}r(x_{\mu},x_{\gamma})\mid\lambda\in D\}

is a directed subset in
M
and
\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\alpha_{\lambda}=1
. If
\alpha\ll 1
, then there is some
\lambda_{0}\in D
such that

\alpha\leq\alpha_{\lambda_{0}}=\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu\leq\gamma}r(x_{\mu},x_{\gamma}).

Thus, for all
\gamma\geq\mu\geq\lambda_{0}
,
\alpha\leq r(x_{\mu},x_{\gamma})
, which means that
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
is eventually
\alpha
-monotone.
∎

Theorem 5.29
.

Suppose
(X,r)
and
(Y,s)
are Yoneda complete
M
-categories, then the
M
-category
([(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)],{d_{{}_{\Pi}}})
is also Yoneda complete.

Proof.

Suppose
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
is a forward Cauchy net in
([(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)],{d_{{}_{\Pi}}})
. We claim that the Yoneda continuous
[0,1]
-functor
f:(X,r)\longrightarrow{(Y,s)}
given by

f(x)=\lim f_{\lambda}(x)

is a Yoneda limit of
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
in
([(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)],{d_{{}_{\Pi}}})
. That is, we need to check that, for all
g\in[(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)]
,

{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f,g)=\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g).

Let
\alpha
be an idempotent element such that
\alpha\ll 1
in
M
. By Lemma
5.28
, there is a
\lambda_{0}\in D
such that for all
\mu\geq\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,

\alpha\leq{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\lambda},f_{\mu})\leq s(f_{\lambda}(x),f_{\mu}(x))

for all
x\in X
.
Furthermore, by Lemma
5.26
, it holds that, for all
\mu\geq\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,

\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g)\leq\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\lambda},g)

and

\alpha\wedge s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))\leq\alpha\wedge s(f_{\lambda}(x),g(y))

for all
g\in[(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)]
and
x,y\in X
.

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g)

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g),

and similarly,

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y)).

for all
g\in[(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)]
and
x,y\in X
.

Therefore, for all
g\in[(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)]
, one can calculate as below:

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow s(f(x),g(y))\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow a)\right)\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow s(f(x),g(y))\right)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}\Big(\big(r(x,y)\rightarrow a\big)\wedge\big(r(x,y)\rightarrow s(f(x),g(y))\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow(\alpha\wedge s(f(x),g(y)))\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad(\text{Proposition \ref{resd prop} }(3))

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow\Big(\alpha\wedge\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))\Big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow\Big(\alpha\wedge\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))\Big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}\Big(r(x,y)\rightarrow\alpha\Big)\wedge\Big(r(x,y)\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))\Big)\quad\quad(\text{Proposition \ref{resd prop} }(3))

\displaystyle=\Big(\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow\alpha\Big)\wedge\Big(\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))\Big)

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}\bigwedge_{x,y\in X}r(x,y)\rightarrow s(f_{\mu}(x),g(y))\quad\quad(\text{Proposition \ref{resd prop} }(3))

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge\bigwedge_{\lambda_{0}\leq\mu}{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g)

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g).

Since the complete subquantale
(M,\&,1)
of
([0,1],\&,1)
satisfies the property

1=\bigvee\{\alpha\in\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}\mid a\ll 1\text{ in }M\},

it follows that

{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f,g)=\bigvee_{{\alpha\in\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}\atop\alpha\ll 1}}\Big(\alpha\wedge{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f,g)\Big)=\bigvee_{{\alpha\in\mathbf{Idm_{\&}}\atop\alpha\ll 1}}\left(\alpha\wedge\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g)\right)=\bigvee_{\lambda\in D}\bigwedge_{\lambda\leq\mu}{d_{{}_{\Pi}}}(f_{\mu},g).

for all
g\in[(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)]
.
Hence,
f
is a Yoneda limit of
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in D}
as desired.
∎

.

Proposition 5.31
.

Suppose
(X,r)
and
(Y,s)

\mathrm{ev}:(X,r)\times{([(X,r)\rightarrow(Y,s)],{d_{{}_{\Pi}}})}\longrightarrow{(Y,s)}

It is clear that
\mathrm{ev}

\mathrm{ev}(-,f):(X,r)\longrightarrow{(Y,s)}

Proposition 5.32
.

Suppose
(X,r)
,

6
Conclusion

Let
\&
be a continuous triangular norm with Archimedean blocks, that is,
\&
is not idempotent. Neither the category
[0,1]\text{-}{\mathbf{Cat}}
nor the category
\mathbf{YCCat}
is cartesian closed. Some cartesian closed subcategories of them are found out. Firstly, all cartesian closed and stable subconstructs of
[0,1]\text{-}{\mathbf{Cat}}
is characterized by suitable subsets of
[0,1]^{2}
. It is shown that a stable subconstructs of
[0,1]\text{-}{\mathbf{Cat}}
is cartesian closed if and only if the associated suitable subset
S\subseteq M^{2}
, and then
{M}\text{-}\mathbf{Cat}
is the largest one among all cartesian and stable subconstructs. Secondly, it is shown that
K\text{-}\mathbf{YCCat}
is cartesian closed if
(K,\&,1)
is a complete subquantale of
(M,\&,1)
. It is remarkable that the quantale
(M,\&,1)
need not be a frame but both the category
{M}\text{-}\mathbf{Cat}
and
{M}\text{-}\mathbf{YCCat}
are cartesian closed.

An improved upper bound for the domination number of a graph
Subramanian Arumugam, Suresh Manjanath Hegde, Shashanka Kulamarva

Abstract
Let G G be a graph of order n n . A classical upper bound for the domination number of a graph G G having no isolated vertices is ⌊ n 2 ⌋ \lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor . However, for several families of graphs, we have γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ ⌊ n ⌋ \gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor which gives a substantially improved upper bound. In this paper, we give a condition necessary for a graph G G to have γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ ⌊ n ⌋ \gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor , and some conditions sufficient for a graph G G to have γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ ⌊ n ⌋ \gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor . We also present a characterization of all connected graphs G G of order n n with γ ⁡ ( G ) = ⌊ n ⌋ \gamma(G)=\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor . Further, we prove that for a graph G G not satisfying r ​ a ​ d ​ ( G ) = d ​ i ​ a ​ m ​ ( G ) = r ​ a ​ d ​ ( G ¯ ) = d ​ i ​ a ​ m ​ ( G ¯ ) = 2 rad(G)=diam(G)=rad(\overline{G})=diam(\overline{G})=2 , deciding whether γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ ⌊ n ⌋ \gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor or γ ⁡ ( G ¯ ) ≤ ⌊ n ⌋ \gamma(\overline{G})\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor can be done in polynomial time. We conjecture that this decision problem can be solved in polynomial time for any graph G G . Introduction

By a graph
G=(V,E)
, we mean a finite undirected graph with neither loops nor multiple edges. The order
|V|
and size
|E|
of
G
are represented by
n
and
m
respectively. For basic graph theoretic terminologies, we refer to the book
[
5
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. A subset
D
of
V
is said to be a dominating set of
G
if every vertex
v\in V\setminus D
is adjacent to a vertex in
D
. The domination number of
G
denoted by
\gamma(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set of
G
. A dominating set
D
of
G
with
|D|=\gamma
is called a
\gamma
-set of
G
.

A dominating set
D
of
G
is said to be a total dominating set if the induced subgraph
G[D]
has no isolated vertices. Further, a dominating set
D
of
G
is said to be a connected dominating set if the induced subgraph
G[D]
is a connected subgraph of
G
. The total domination number
\gamma_{t}
and the connected domination number
\gamma_{c}
of
G
are defined as follows.

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}(G)

\displaystyle=\text{min }\{|D|:\text{$D$ is a total dominating set of $G$}\}

\displaystyle\gamma_{c}(G)

\displaystyle=\text{min }\{|D|:\text{$D$ is a connected dominating set of $G$}\}

A total dominating set
D
of
G
with
|D|=\gamma_{t}
is called a
\gamma_{t}
-set of
G
. Further, a connected dominating set
D
of
G
with
|D|=\gamma_{c}
is called a
\gamma_{c}
-set of
G
. We observe that
\gamma_{t}
is defined only for graphs without isolated vertices and
\gamma_{c}
is defined only for connected graphs. For domination related concepts, we refer to the book
[
7
]
.

The concept of domination has significant applications in Network Science.
Brewster et al. 2019
have investigated the concept of broadcast domination in the context of communication systems. For specific applications of dominating sets in social networks and biological networks, we refer to
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
.
Cheng et al. 2003
demonstrated that the problem of constructing a connected dominating set in a unit disc graph is the same as constructing a virtual backbone in Ad Hoc wireless networks. In all such applications, the number of vertices in the network is large and the crucial problem is to find a dominating set of small order. In this paper, we prove that for most of the graphs, there exists a dominating set of order at most
\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
, which is quite significant in the context of the above mentioned applications.

Trivially,
1\leq\gamma\leq n
and
\gamma=n
if and only if
G=\overline{K_{n}}
. For graphs without isolated vertices, a classical result of
Ore 1962
gives an improved upper bound for
\gamma
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
12
]

If a graph
G
has no isolated vertices, then
\gamma(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

For a graph theoretic parameter, Nordhaus-Gaddum type result gives an upper bound and a lower bound for the sum and product of the values of the parameter for a graph
G
and its complement
\overline{G}
. An excellent survey of Nordhaus-Gaddum type results for various parameters is given in
[
1
]
. In the classical paper
[
11
]
, Nordhaus and Gaddum established the first such result for the chromatic number.
Jaeger and Payan 1972
obtained similar results for the domination number. We state only the multiplicative version of the theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
8
]

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
. Then
\gamma(G)\gamma(\overline{G})\leq n
.

Theorem
1.2
leads to the following observation giving a much improved upper bound for the domination number.

Observation 1.3
.

If
G
is any graph of order
n
, then either
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
or
\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
.

In this paper, we investigate the structure of graphs satisfying
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
and
\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
. The following definitions and theorems are necessary for our discussions.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
2
,
13
]

For any graph
G
with maximum degree
\Delta
, we have

\left\lceil\frac{n}{1+\Delta}\right\rceil\leq\gamma(G)\leq n-\Delta

For any vertex
v\in V
, the set
N(v)=\{u\in V:uv\in E\}
is called the open neighborhood of
v
. Also,
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
is called the closed neighborhood of

.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph of order
n
. Let
S\subseteq V
and let

. A vertex

. If

Let

and

denote the set of all private neighbors of

and the set of all external private neighbors of

, respectively. One can see that

is a private neighbor of itself if and only if

is an isolated vertex in the induced subgraph
G[S]
. The following theorem mentioned in
[
7
]
is a consequence of a result proved by
Ore 1962
.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
12
]

A dominating set

of the graph

is a minimal dominating set if and only if

. If

.

The following theorem is proved by

Theorem 1.7
.

[

If

We present several structural results on graphs satisfying
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
. The following theorem gives a necessary condition for a graph
G

\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
and let
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
. Then
\Delta\geq\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil-1
and the bound is sharp.

Proof.

From Theorem
1.4
, we have
\gamma(G)\geq\left\lceil\frac{n}{1+\Delta}\right\rceil
. Also
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
and hence
\left\lceil\frac{n}{1+\Delta}\right\rceil\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
.

Therefore,
\frac{n}{1+\Delta}\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
, which implies
\Delta\geq\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil-1
.

For the cycle
C_{5}
,
\gamma=2=\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
and
\Delta=2=\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil-1
. Hence the bound is sharp.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
with

. Then
\gamma(G)=\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
if and only if

.

Proof.

Let

. Then,
G
is a graph obtained from some

and
H_{2}
as in Definition
2.2
. Therefore, we have
n=|V(G)|=k+|V(H_{2})|
and
k(k-1)\leq|V(H_{2})|\leq k^{2}+k
. Hence,
k^{2}\leq n<(k+1)^{2}
, and so
k=\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
. Clearly,
V(H_{1})
is a dominating set of
G
and hence,
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
. Now, let
D
be any
\gamma

Case 1

.

In this case,

. Let

. We claim that

Subcase 2.2

We now proceed to find sufficient conditions for a graph
G
to have
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
. If any one of the following conditions holds, then
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
.

(
i
)
.

\overline{G}
is a disconnected graph.

(
ii
)
.

diam(\overline{G})\geq 3
.

Proof.

(i)
. Since
\overline{G}
is a disconnected graph, it follows that
G=K_{2}
if
n=2
, and
G=P_{3}
or
K_{3}
if
n=3
. In both these cases,
\gamma(G)=1\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
. Now, let
n\geq 4
. Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two components of
\overline{G}
. Let
x\in V(C_{1})
and
y\in V(C_{2})
. It can be easily verified that
\{x,y\}
is a dominating set of
G
and hence,
\gamma(G)\leq 2\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
.

(ii)
. Let
diam(\overline{G})\geq 3
. Then we have
n\geq 4
. Also if
x,y\in V
and
d_{\overline{G}}(x,y)\geq 3
, then
\{x,y\}
is a dominating set of
G
. Alternatively, since
diam(\overline{G})\geq 3
, by Theorem
1.7
, we have
\gamma(G)\leq 2
. Thus
\gamma(G)\leq 2\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
.
∎

Now, we proceed towards the problem of deciding for a given graph
G
whether
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
or
\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
. For this purpose, we need the following definitions.

Definition 2.5
.

A graph
G
is said to be a
t
-complementary self-centered graph, denoted by
SCC(t)
if both
G
and
\overline{G}
are self-centered graphs satisfying the conditions:

rad(G)=diam(G)=rad(\overline{G})=diam(\overline{G})=t

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
. Then
G
is said to be in domination type-I if
\gamma(G)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
and is said to be in domination type-II if
\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
.

Observe that every graph
G
is in domination type-I or domination type-II or both.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
and let
G\notin SCC(2)
. Then whether
G
is in domination type-I or in domination type-II can be determined in polynomial time.

Proof.

If
G
is a disconnected graph, then it follows from Theorem
2.4
that
G
is in domination type-II. Similarly, if
\overline{G}
is a disconnected graph, then
G
is in domination type-I.

Suppose both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected. It follows from Theorem
2.4
that if
diam(G)\geq 3
, then
G
is in domination type-II. Similarly, if
diam(\overline{G})\geq 3
, then
G
is in domination type-I.

If
diam(G)=1
or
rad(G)=1
, then
\gamma(G)=1
, and hence,
G
is in domination type-I. Similarly, if
diam(\overline{G})=1
or
rad(\overline{G})=1
, then
\gamma(\overline{G})=1
and hence,
G
is in domination type-II.

In all other cases,
G\in SCC(2)
. Also, given a graph
G
, it can be decided in polynomial time whether
G
is connected or not. If
G
is connected, then
diam(G)
and
rad(G)
can be computed in polynomial time. Hence, the result follows.
∎

For graphs in
SCC(2)
, we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2.8
.

If
G\in SCC(2)
, then
\gamma(G)\leq\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil
and
\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil
.

Note that
C_{5}
is an example of a graph in
SCC(2)
which satisfies the conjecture.

3
Conclusion

Using the multiplicative version of the Nordhaus-Gaddum type result for the domination number
\gamma
of a graph
G
, we have obtained a substantially improved upper bound for
\gamma
and we have proved that for most of the graphs, this bound holds. An exhaustive survey of Nordhaus-Gaddum type results for various graph theoretic parameters is given in
[
1
]
. This leads to two potential directions for further research, which are given below.

Problem 1:
Determine graph theoretic parameters for which an improved upper or lower bound can be obtained using Nordhaus-Gaddum type results.

Problem 2:
For each of the parameters identified in Problem 1, investigate the structural properties of graphs satisfying the improved bound.

II
Belle Detector

The Belle detector
Bel
is a large-solid-angle magnetic spectrometer that consists of a silicon vertex detector (SVD), a 50-layer central drift chamber (CDC), an array of aerogel threshold Cherenkov counters (ACC), a barrel-like arrangement of time-of-flight scintillation counters (TOF), and an electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL) consisting of CsI(Tl) crystals.
All these detector components are located inside a superconducting solenoid coil that provides a 1.5 T magnetic field.
An iron flux-return located outside of the coil is instrumented with resistive plate chambers to detect
\displaystyle K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}
mesons and to identify muons.
Two inner detector configurations were used: a 2.0 cm beam-pipe
and a 3-layer SVD (SVD1) were used for the first sample of
\displaystyle 152\times 10^{6}

\displaystyle B\kern 1.79993pt\bar{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs, while a 1.5 cm beam-pipe, a 4-layer SVD (SVD2), and small cells in the inner layers of the CDC were used to record the remaining
\displaystyle 620\times 10^{6}

\displaystyle B\kern 1.79993pt\bar{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs
Natkaniec et al. 2006
.

We reconstruct
\displaystyle B^{0}\to\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0}
candidates from the subsequent decays of the
\displaystyle\bar{D}^{0}
and the
\displaystyle\pi^{0}
mesons. We employ two reconstruction modes:
\displaystyle B^{0}\to\bar{D}^{0}(\to K^{+}\pi^{-})\pi^{0}
(
\displaystyle B_{2b}
) and
\displaystyle B^{0}\to\bar{D}^{0}(\to K^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\pi^{0}
(
\displaystyle B_{3b}
). The
\displaystyle\pi^{0}
mesons are reconstructed from their decay to two photons.

The flavor of the neutral
\displaystyle B
-meson (
\displaystyle B^{0}
or
\displaystyle\bar{B}^{0}
) is determined by the charge of the reconstructed kaon (
\displaystyle K^{+}
or
\displaystyle K^{-}
). This method of flavor tagging is not perfect, as the wrong-sign doubly Cabibbo-suppressed decays (DCS)
\displaystyle\bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0}
(Fig.
1
c) will result in wrongly tagged flavor. The same effect occurs with charm DCS decays
\displaystyle D^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\displaystyle D^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, and charm mixing, although the charm mixing effect is negligibly smaller. These effects can be calculated using the ratio of wrong-sign (Cabibbo-suppressed) to right-sign (Cabibbo-favored) decay rates:

R=\frac{\cal{B}_{WS}}{\cal{B}_{RS}},

(2)

where

\displaystyle R(\bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0})
has not been measured and thus is approximated with
\displaystyle R({B}^{0}\to{D}^{+}\pi^{-})=(2.92\pm 0.38\pm 0.31)\times 10^{-4}
Das et al. 2010
, while
\displaystyle R(D^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-})=(3.79\pm 0.18)\times 10^{-3}
and
\displaystyle R(D^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})=(2.12\pm 0.07)\times 10^{-3}
are taken from the PDG
Zyla et al. 2020
. These effects lead to the true value of
\displaystyle\cal{B}
being
\displaystyle(0.314\pm 0.008)\%
lower than the measured value, and the true
\displaystyle{\cal A}_{CP}
being
\displaystyle(3.05\pm 0.08)\times 10^{-5}
higher than the measured value. In the case of
\displaystyle\cal{B}
this is corrected for; however, for
\displaystyle{\cal A}_{CP}
the correction is significantly smaller than our uncertainty and it is neglected.

Photon candidates are mainly taken from clusters in the ECL but additionally are reconstructed from
\displaystyle e^{+}e^{-}
pairs resulting from photon conversion in the inner detector. Photons from
\displaystyle\pi^{0}
decay must have an energy greater than
\displaystyle 50

\displaystyle(100){\rm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}}
in the barrel (endcap) region of the ECL.
The invariant mass of the two-photon combination must lie in the range
\displaystyle 104{{{\rm\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}}c^{2}}<M_{\gamma\gamma}<165{{{\rm\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}}c^{2}}
, corresponding to
\displaystyle\pm 3\sigma
around the nominal
\displaystyle\pi^{0}
mass
Zyla et al. 2020

\displaystyle\chi^{2}<50

Charged tracks originating from a
\displaystyle B
decay are required to have a distance-of-closest-approach with respect to the interaction point of less than 4.0 cm along the
\displaystyle z
-axis (the direction opposite the positron beam), and of less than 0.3 cm in the plane transverse to the
\displaystyle z
-axis.
Charged kaons and pions are identified using information from the CDC, ACC, and TOF detectors. This information is combined to form a
\displaystyle K-\pi
likelihood ratio
\displaystyle R_{K/\pi}=L_{K}/(L_{K}+L_{\pi})
, where
\displaystyle L_{K}(L_{\pi})
is the likelihood of the track being a kaon (pion). Track candidates with
\displaystyle R_{K/\pi}>0.6

\displaystyle(<0.4)
are classified as kaons (pions). The typical kaon (pion) identification efficiency is
83% (88%), with a pion (kaon) misidentification probability of 7% (11%).

Two kinematic variables are used to distinguish signal from background: the beam-energy-constrained mass,
\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}\equiv\sqrt{E_{\text{beam}}^{2}-|\vec{p}_{B}|^{2}c^{2}}/c^{2}
, and the energy difference
\displaystyle\Delta E\equiv E_{B}-E_{\text{beam}}
.
Here,
\displaystyle\vec{p}_{B}
and
\displaystyle E_{B}
are the momentum and energy, respectively, of the
\displaystyle B
-meson candidate evaluated in the center-of-mass (CM) frame, and
\displaystyle E_{\text{beam}}
is the beam energy in the CM frame.

Due to energy leakage in the ECL, the reconstructed
\displaystyle\pi^{0}
energy is typically lower than its true value. To compensate for this, we rescale the reconstructed
\displaystyle\pi^{0}
momentum to give
\displaystyle E_{\pi^{0}}\equiv E_{\rm beam}-E_{D}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\vec{p}_{\pi,\text{corr}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\vec{p}_{\pi}\times\frac{\sqrt{(E_{\text{beam}}-E_{D^{0}})^{2}-M_{\pi^{0}}^{2}c^{4}}}{|p_{\pi}|c}.

(3)

Using this we calculate a new
\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{B,\text{corr}}

\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

M_{\rm bc}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{E_{\text{beam}}^{2}-|\vec{p}_{B,\text{corr}}|^{2}c^{2}}}{c^{2}}.

(4)

\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

\displaystyle\Delta E

\displaystyle\pi^{0}

\displaystyle B^{0}

\displaystyle M_{\text{bc}}>5.25{{{\rm\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}}c^{2}}

III
Belle detector and signal selection

Backgrounds to our signal are studied using MC simulation. These simulations use EvtGen
Lange et al. 2001
and PYTHIA
Sjöstrand et al. 2006
to generate the physics interactions at the quark level, and employ GEANT3
Brun et al. 1987
to simulate the detector response.

The largest background arises from
\displaystyle e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}\;(q\in\{u,d,s,c\})
continuum events. A neural network
Feindt and Kerzel 2006
is
used to distinguish the spherical
\displaystyle B\bar{B}
signal from the
jet-like continuum background. It combines the following five observables based on the event topology: a Fisher
discriminant formed from 17 modified Fox-Wolfram moments
SFW
; the cosine of the angle between the
\displaystyle B
-meson
candidate direction and the beam axis; the cosine of the
angle between the thrust axis
Brandt et al. 1964
of the
\displaystyle B
-meson candidate and that of the rest of the event (all of these quantities
being calculated in the CM frame); the separation along
the
\displaystyle z
-axis between the vertex of the
\displaystyle B
-meson candidate
and that of the remaining tracks in the event; and the tagging quality variable from a
\displaystyle B
-meson flavor-tagging algorithm
Kakuno et al. 2004
.
The training and optimization of the neural network are performed with signal and continuum MC samples. These are divided into five training samples and one verification sample.
The output of the neural net (
\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}
) has a range of
\displaystyle(-1,1)
, with
\displaystyle 1
being the most signal-like and
\displaystyle-1
being the most background-like.

In order to maximally use
\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}
information, we impose only a loose requirement on
\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}
and use
\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}
as a variable in the fit. We require
\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}>-0.05
for both the
\displaystyle B_{2b}
and
\displaystyle B_{3b}
modes. This results in 86% background reduction and 87% signal efficiency.

\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}

\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}^{\prime}

C_{\rm NN}^{\prime}=\text{log}\left(\frac{C_{\rm NN}-C_{\rm NN}^{\text{min}}}{C_{\rm NN}^{\text{max}}-C_{\rm NN}}\right),

\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}^{\text{min}}

\displaystyle-0.05

\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}^{\text{max}}

\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}

IV
Fitting strategy

The signal yield and
\displaystyle{\cal A}_{C\!P}
are extracted via an unbinned extended maximum-likelihood fit to the variables
\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}
,
\displaystyle\Delta{}E
, and
\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}^{\prime}
. There are four categories of events fitted:
\displaystyle B^{0}\to\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0}
or
\displaystyle B^{+}\to\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{+}
signal events (
\displaystyle s
), continuum events (
\displaystyle c
), generic
\displaystyle B\bar{B}
events (
\displaystyle b
), and rare
\displaystyle B
-decay backgrounds (
\displaystyle r
). These events are described by probability density functions (PDFs) denoted as
\displaystyle P^{s}
,
\displaystyle P^{c}
,
\displaystyle P^{b}
, and
\displaystyle P^{r}
, respectively.
Separate PDFs are constructed for the
\displaystyle B_{2b}
and
\displaystyle B_{3b}
reconstruction modes, which are fitted as two separate data sets. The data are further divided into events tagged as
\displaystyle B^{0}
and
\displaystyle\bar{B}^{0}
, defined as having flavor
\displaystyle q=+1
and
\displaystyle q=-1
, respectively, based on the charge of the kaon.

The physics parameters are determined via a simultaneous fit to the four data sets. The total likelihood is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\sum_{j}N^{j}}}{\prod_{q,d}N_{q,d}!}\times\prod_{q,d}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N_{q,d}}\left(\sum_{j}f^{j}_{d}N^{j}P^{j}_{q,d}\left(M_{\text{bc}}^{i},\Delta E^{i},C_{\rm NN}^{\prime i},q\right)\right)\right].

(6)

where
\displaystyle N_{q,d}
is the number of events with flavor tag
\displaystyle q
for the data set
\displaystyle d
(
\displaystyle d\in{B_{2b},B_{3b}}
), and
\displaystyle N^{j}
is the number of events in the
\displaystyle j^{\rm th}
category (
\displaystyle j\in{s,c,b,r}
) contributing to the total yield.
The fraction of events in the data set
\displaystyle d
for category
\displaystyle j
is
\displaystyle f^{j}_{d}
, with
\displaystyle f^{j}_{3b}=1-f^{j}_{2b}
.
The PDF
\displaystyle P^{j}_{q,d}
corresponds to the
\displaystyle j^{\rm th}
category in the
\displaystyle d
data set for flavor
\displaystyle q
, measured at
\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}^{i}
,
\displaystyle\Delta E^{i}
, and
\displaystyle C_{\rm NN}^{\prime i}
for the
\displaystyle i^{\rm th}
event.

The PDF for each component is given by:

\displaystyle\displaystyle P^{j}_{q,d}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta E,C_{\rm NN}^{\prime},q)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-q\times{\cal A}^{j}_{C\!P}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times P^{j}_{d}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta E,C_{\rm NN}^{\prime}),

(7)

The model accounts for a possible direct
\displaystyle C\!P
asymmetry,
\displaystyle{\cal A}^{j}_{C\!P}
, and the fractions of signal and backgrounds expected in each reconstruction mode
\displaystyle B_{2b}
(
\displaystyle B_{3b}
).
In Eq. (
IV
), the fraction
\displaystyle f^{s}_{d}
is determined via MC studies of the
\displaystyle B_{2b}
and
\displaystyle B_{3b}
modes and is fixed in the fit to data, and
\displaystyle{\cal A}^{j}_{C\!P}(j\in{c,b,r})
is fixed based on studies of detector bias using sideband data (see Section
V
).

The 20 free parameters in the fit are: the number of signal events
\displaystyle N^{s}
, signal asymmetry
\displaystyle A^{s}_{C\!P}
, the number of continuum events (
\displaystyle N^{c}
) and generic
\displaystyle B
-decay events (
\displaystyle N^{b}
), fractions of backgrounds expected in each reconstruction mode
\displaystyle f^{j}_{d}
(
\displaystyle j\in{c,b,r}
), shape parameters of the continuum
\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}
and
\displaystyle\Delta E
PDFs, and the mean and width of the
\displaystyle B_{2b}
signal
\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}
and
\displaystyle\Delta E
PDFs. The number of rare background events (
\displaystyle N^{r}
) is fixed to that expected from MC studies. The effects of these assumptions are included in the systematic uncertainties.

The PDFs used for the
\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}
and
\displaystyle\Delta E
distributions for the various event types are as follows.

•

Signal: for the
\displaystyle B_{2b}

\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

Cry

\displaystyle B_{3b}

\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

\displaystyle\Delta E

•

Generic
\displaystyle B

\displaystyle B_{3b}

\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

\displaystyle\Delta E

Continuum background:
\displaystyle M_{\rm bc}

\displaystyle\Delta E

\displaystyle B_{3b}

\displaystyle\Delta E

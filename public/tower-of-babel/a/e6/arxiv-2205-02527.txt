Pfaffian Interaction and B ​ C ​ D BCD -quiver Matrix Models
Nicolas Babinet and Taro Kimura

Abstract
We study matrix models involving Pfaffian interactions as generalizations of the standard β = 1 \beta=1 and β = 4 \beta=4 matrix models. We present the Pfaffian formulas for the partition function and the characteristic polynomial averages. We also explore the matrix chain with the Pfaffian interaction, which realizes the B ​ C ​ D BCD -type quiver matrix models.

Introduction

Pfaffian is an analog of the determinant, which is one of the central concepts in the linear algebra.
In the context of statistical physics and quantum field theory, Pfaffian is frequently utilized to describe real (Majorana) fermions, while the determinant is used for complex (Dirac) fermions.
It has been also known that the Pfaffian structure is often discussed for certain classes of random matrices, called the orthogonal and symplectic ensembles, which are classified into the
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
models due to the Wigner–Dyson classification.
Similarly to the
\beta=2
model (unitary ensemble), which exhibits the determinantal structure, the correlation functions (including the partition function) are in general described using the Pfaffian.

In this paper, we explore formal eigenvalue integrals, involving various Pfaffian interactions, that we call the generalized
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
models.
We obtain the Pfaffian formulas for the corresponding partition functions and also the average of characteristic polynomials.
As an application of the Pfaffian interaction, we then construct the matrix chain model, involving several sets of eigenvalues.
In order to classify such a matrix chain, we use the graphical description based on the Dynkin-quiver diagram.
Such a multi-matrix model is often called the quiver matrix model, which has been originally introduced to study its connection with conformal field theory
[
MMM91
,
KMM+93
]

2
Preliminary

In this Section, we present the notations and the preliminary results that we will use in the following parts.

2.1
Notations

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a set of integers,

\displaystyle[n]:=(1,\ldots,n)\,.

(2.1)

For a subset
I\subset[n]
, we denote

\displaystyle\Sigma(I)=\sum_{i\in I}i\,.

(2.2)

Let
X
be an
n\times m
matrix.
For
I\subset[n]
,
J\subset[m]
, we denote by
X(I;J)
the submatrix of
X
obtained by picking up rows indexed by
I
and columns indexed by
J
.
If
X
is a skew-symmetric matrix, we write
X(I)=X(I;I)
for simplicity.

Operator formalism

Let
\differential{\mu(x)}
be an integral measure for the variable
x
.
We denote a function (wave function) associated with a formal state
\ket{f}
in the Hilbert space by

\displaystyle f(x)=\langle\,x\mid f\,\rangle\,.

(2.3)

For the integral operator
K
associated with the corresponding kernel
K(x,y)
, we write

\displaystyle\langle\,x\mid K\mid f\,\rangle=\int\differential{\mu(y)}K(x,y)f(y)\,.

(2.4)

In this convention, the kernel of the integral operator
K
is given by

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\langle\,x\mid K\mid y\,\rangle\,.

(2.5)

Hence, the identity operator is given by the integral over the complete set,

\displaystyle\int\differential{\mu(x)}\ket{x}\bra{x}=1\,.

(2.6)

We denote the transposition of the kernel,

\displaystyle K^{\text{T}}(y,x)=\langle\,y\mid K^{\text{T}}\mid x\,\rangle\,.

(2.7)

We also write the inner product as follows,

\displaystyle\langle\,f\mid g\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(x)}f(x)g(x)\,,

(2.8a)

\displaystyle\langle\,f\mid K\mid g\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(y)}f(x)K(x,y)g(y)\,.

(2.8b)

2.2
Determinantal formulas

2.2.1
Vandermonde determinant

We first show several determinantal formulas regarding the Vandermonde determinant, which is a main building block of the matrix model.
See also the monographs on this subject
[
Meh04
,
For10
,
EKR15
]
for more details.

Proposition 2.1

(Vandermonde determinant)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a set of variables
X=(x_{i})_{i\in[N]}
, the
N
-variable Vandermonde determinant is given by a rank
N
determinant,

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(X)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\det_{i,j\in[N]}p_{N-j}(x_{i})\,,

(2.9)

where
p_{k}(x)=x^{k}+\cdots
is a degree-
k
monic polynomial.

Using this expression, we have the following Lemma, which is a crucial identity to discuss the
\beta=4
model.

Lemma 2.2
.

For the
N
-variable Vandermonde determinant, the following determinantal formula holds,

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(X)^{4}=(-1)^{N(N+1)/2}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ p_{2N-j}'(x_i) ]\,.

(2.10)

Proof.

The
2N
-variable Vandermonde determinant of
X=(x_{i})_{i\in[N]}
and
Y=(y_{i})_{i\in[N]}
may be written as follows,

\displaystyle\Delta_{2N}(X;Y)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\\
i<j\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})(x_{i}-y_{j})(x_{j}-y_{i})\prod_{i\in[N]}(x_{i}-y_{i})\,.

(2.11)

On the other hand, the
2N
-variable Vandermonde determinant is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{2N}(X;Y)=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ p_{2N-j}(y_i) ]=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ p_{2N-j}(y_i) - p_{2N-j}(x_i)]\,.

(2.12)

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(X)^{4}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N(N+1)/2}\lim_{y_{i}\to x_{i}}\Delta_{2N}(X;Y)\prod_{i\in[N]}(y_{i}-x_{i})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N(N+1)/2}\lim_{y_{i}\to x_{i}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ \frac{p_{2N-j}(y_i) - p_{2N-j}(x_i)}{y_i - x_i} ]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N(N+1)/2}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ p_{2N-j}'(x_i) ]\,.

(2.13)

This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.2
Schur polynomials

In this paper, we shall use the Schur polynomial as the basis of the symmetric polynomial of the formal eigenvalues.
See, e.g.
[
Mac15
]
for the details on this subject.

Definition 2.3

(Schur polynomial)
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition, a non-increasing sequence of non-negative integers,

\displaystyle\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}>\lambda_{\ell+1}=\cdots=0)\,,

(2.14)

where
\ell=\ell(\lambda)
is called the length of the partition.
Denoting the transposed partition by
\lambda^{\text{T}}
, we have
\ell(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}^{\text{T}}
.
We define the size of the partition by

\displaystyle|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}^{\text{T}}=|\lambda^{\text{T}}|\,.

(2.15)

Then, the Schur polynomial of
N
variables,
X=(x_{i})_{i\in[N]}
, is defined as follows,

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{1}{\Delta_{N}(X)}\det_{i,j\in[N]}x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+N-j}\,.

(2.16)

If
\ell(\lambda)>N
, it always vanishes
s_{\lambda}(X)=0
.
We also remark
s_{\emptyset}(X)=1
.

For the latter convenience, we also define a modified version of the Schur polynomial as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and we denote
X=(x_{i})_{i\in[N]}
,
Y=(y_{i})_{i\in[N]}
.
For a partition
\lambda
, we define

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(X;Y)=\det_{i\in[N],j\in[2N]}\begin{bmatrix}x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+2N-j}&(y_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+2N-j})^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(2.17)

Then, we define a modified Schur polynomial as follows,

\displaystyle\mathsf{s}_{\lambda}(X;Y)=\frac{a_{\lambda}(X;Y)}{a_{\emptyset}(X;Y)}\,.

(2.18)

Regarding the Schur polynomials, we will use the following decomposition in the study of the characteristic polynomials.

Lemma 2.5

(Schur polynomial expansion)
.

Let
X=(x_{i})_{i\in[N]}
and
Y=(y_{i})_{i\in[M]}
.
We have the following expansions with the Schur polynomial,

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[M]\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}s_{\lambda^{\vee}}(X)s_{\lambda}(Y)\,,

(2.19a)

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[M]\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-y_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\det_{N}X^{-M}\sum_{\lambda|\ell(\lambda)\leq\operatorname{min}(M,N)}s_{\lambda}(X^{-1})s_{\lambda}(Y)\,,

\displaystyle=\det_{M}Y^{-N}\sum_{\lambda|\ell(\lambda)\leq\operatorname{min}(M,N)}s_{\lambda}(X)s_{\lambda}(Y^{-1})\,,

(2.19b)

where we define the dual partition

\displaystyle\lambda^{\vee}=(\lambda_{1}^{\vee},\ldots,\lambda_{M}^{\vee})=(N-\lambda_{M}^{\text{T}},\ldots,N-\lambda_{1}^{\text{T}})\,,

(2.20)

and the length of the partition denoted by
\ell(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}
.

Proof.

This follows from the Cauchy sum formula.
See, e.g.,
[
Mac15
]
for details.
∎

Lemma 2.6

(Cauchy determinant)
.

We define the Cauchy determinant for
X=(x_{i})_{i\in[N]}
and
Y=(y_{i})_{i\in[M]}
as follows,

\displaystyle\Delta_{N|M}(X|Y):=\frac{\Delta_{N}(X)\Delta_{M}(Y)}{\prod^{i\in[N]}_{j\in[M]}(x_{i}-y_{j})}\,.

(2.21)

Then, the following formulas hold.

\displaystyle N\geq M:

\displaystyle\Delta_{N|M}(X|Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda|\ell(\lambda)\leq M}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[M]\\
k\in[N-M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}x_{i}^{-\lambda_{j}-M+j-1}\\
x_{i}^{k-1}\end{bmatrix}\det_{i,j\in[M]}y_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+M-j}=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[M]\\
k\in[N-M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{i}-y_{j}}\\[10.00002pt]
x_{i}^{k-1}\end{bmatrix}\,

(2.22a)

\displaystyle N\leq M:

\displaystyle\Delta_{N|M}(X|Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda|\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\det_{i,j\in[N]}x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+N-j}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[M],j\in[N]\\
k\in[M-N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}y_{i}^{-\lambda_{j}-N+j-1}\\
y_{i}^{k-1}\end{bmatrix}=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[M]\\
k\in[M-N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{i}-y_{j}}\\[10.00002pt]
y_{j}^{k-1}\end{bmatrix}

(2.22b)

Proof.

The expressions as the summation over the partitions are obtained from the Schur polynomial expansion (Lemma
2.5
).
In addition, for the case
N\geq M
, the summation over the partition is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}=\sum_{0\leq\lambda_{M}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\infty}=\sum_{0\leq r_{M}<\cdots<r_{1}\leq\infty}=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{\alpha}=0,\ldots,\infty\\
r_{\alpha}\neq r_{\beta}\end{subarray}}

(2.23)

where we write
r_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}+M-\alpha
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{N|M}(X|Y)

\displaystyle=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[M]\\
k\in[N-M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}x_{i}^{-r-1}y_{j}^{r}\\
x_{i}^{k-1}\end{bmatrix}=\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[M]\\
k\in[N-M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{i}-y_{j}}\\
x_{i}^{k-1}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(2.24)

The other case
N\leq M
is similarly analyzed by exchanging the
x
and
y
variables.
∎

2.3
Pfaffian formulas

We present several formulas involving Pfaffians used in this paper.

Proposition 2.7
.

For
n,m\in 2\mathbb{N}
, let
A
and
\widetilde{A}
be invertible skew-symmetric matrices of size
n
and
m
, respectively.
Let
B
be an
n\times m
generic matrix.
Then, the following factorization formula holds for the Pfaffian of the block matrix,

\displaystyle\Pf\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
-B^{\text{T}}&\widetilde{A}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\Pf A\Pf\quantity[\widetilde{A} + B^\text{T} A^{-1} B]=\Pf\widetilde{A}\Pf\quantity[A + B \widetilde{A}^{-1} B^\text{T}]\,.

(2.25)

For
A=\widetilde{A}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pf\begin{bmatrix}0&B\\
-B^{\text{T}}&0\end{bmatrix}=\det B\,.

(2.26)

Remark 2.8
.

This formula is a Pfaffian analog of the block matrix determinant formula,

\displaystyle\det\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}=\det A\det(D - C A^{-1} B)=\det D\det(A - B D^{-1} C)\,.

(2.27)

Lemma 2.9

(Laplace-type expansion
[
Oka19
]
)
.

For an
m\times m
skew-symmetric matrix
Z
, and an
m\times n
matrix
W
with
m>n
, we have the following expansion of the Pfaffian,

\displaystyle\Pf\begin{bmatrix}Z&W\\
-W^{\text{T}}&0\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=m-n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\Sigma(I)+{m\choose 2}}\Pf Z(I)\det W([m]\backslash I;[n])

(2.28)

where
I
runs over all
(m-n)
-element subsets of
[n]
.

Lemma 2.10

(Cauchy–Binet-type formula
[
IW95
,
Oka19
]
)
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
m-l\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and
m-l\leq n
.
For an
n\times n
skew-symmetric matrix
Z
, an
m\times n
matrix
W
, and an
m\times l
matrix
X
, we have the following expansion of the Pfaffian,

\displaystyle\Pf\begin{bmatrix}WZW^{\text{T}}&X\\
-X^{\text{T}}&0\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=m-l\end{subarray}}\Pf Z(I)\det[W([m];I)\ X([m];[l])]

(2.29)

where
I
runs over all
(m-l)
-element subsets of
[n]
.
If
l=0
, there is no contribution of the matrix
X
,

\displaystyle\Pf\quantity[WZW^\text{T}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=m\end{subarray}}\Pf Z(I)\det W([m];I)\,.

(2.30)

If
m=n
(hence
l=0
), the only possibility is
I=\emptyset
, so that we have

\displaystyle\Pf\quantity[WZW^\text{T}]=\Pf Z\det W\,.

(2.31)

2.4
Integration formulas

We then introduce the integration formulas that will be crucial to perform the eigenvalue integrals discussed in this paper.

Proposition 2.11

(Andréief’s formula)
.

Let
(f_{i}(x))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(g_{i}(x))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences of integrable functions.
Denoting
\differential{\mu(X)}=\prod_{i\in[N]}\differential{\mu(x_i)}
, the following identity holds,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}f_{i}(x_{j})\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}g_{i}(x_{j})

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\langle\,f_{i}\mid g_{j}\,\rangle\,.

(2.32)

Proposition 2.12

(de Bruijn’s formula)
.

Let
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
N+M\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
Let
A(x,y)
,
B(x,y)
be skew-symmetric functions, and
S(x,y)
generic integrable two-variable function.
Let
(f_{i}(x))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(g_{i}(x))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences of integrable functions as before.
Denoting
\differential{\mu(X)}=\prod_{i\in[N]}\differential{\mu(x_i)}
, etc, the following identities hold,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2N)!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\det_{i,j\in[2N]}f_{j}(x_{i})\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}A(x_{i},x_{j})=\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}\langle\,f_{i}\mid A\mid f_{j}\,\rangle\,,

(2.33a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!M!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\differential{\mu(Y)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],~j\in[M]\\
k\in[N+M]\end{subarray}}\quantity[f_k(x_i) \ g_k(y_j) ]\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\\
k,l\in[M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}A(x_{i},{x}_{j})&S(x_{i},y_{l})\\
-S^{\text{T}}(y_{k},x_{j})&B(y_{k},y_{l})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\Pf_{i,j\in[N+M]}\quantity[
\rvev{f_i \mid A \mid f_j} + \rvev{f_i \mid S \mid g_j} - \rvev{g_i \mid S^\text{T} \mid f_j} + \rvev{g_i \mid B \mid g_j} ]\,.

(2.33b)

Remark 2.13
.

These formulas are due to C. Andréief
[
And86
]
and N. G. de Bruijn
[
dB55
]
.
See
[
For19
]
for a historical survey on these formulas.

3
Matrix model with Pfaffian interaction

In this Section, we introduce the generalized
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
matrix models.
We show that the corresponding partition functions are concisely described by the Pfaffian formula.

3.1
\beta=1
model

We start to define the following model that we call the generalized
\beta=1
matrix model.

Definition 3.1

(Generalized
\beta=1
matrix model)
.

Let
N\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and
A(x,y)
be a skew-symmetric function,
A(y,x)=-A(x,y)
.
Let
(f_{i-1}(x))_{i\in[N]}
be a sequence of the integrable functions.
We denote the integral measure by
\differential{\mu(X)}=\prod_{i\in[N]}\differential{\mu(x_i)}
.
Then, we define the generalized
\beta=1
matrix model partition function as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\det_{i,j\in[N]}f_{j-1}(x_{i})\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}A(x_{i},x_{j})\,.

(3.1)

In fact, this generalized
\beta=1
model is reduced to the standard
\beta=1
model as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Putting
A(x,y)=\operatorname{sgn}(x-y)
and
f_{i-1}(x)=p_{N-i}(x)
, the generalized
\beta=1
model reduces to the standard
\beta=1
matrix model (real symmetric matrix model; orthogonal ensemble),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}\xrightarrow[f_{i-1}\ \to\ p_{N-i}]{A\ \to\ \operatorname{sgn}}\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}|\Delta_{N}(X)|\,.

(3.2)

Proof.

Putting
A(x,y)=\operatorname{sgn}(x-y)
, we have

\displaystyle\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\operatorname{sgn}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\operatorname{sgn}(x_{i}-x_{j})\,.

(3.3)

Putting
f_{i-1}(x)=p_{N-i}(x)
, the determinant factor becomes the Vandermonde determinant (
2.9
).
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\Delta_{N}(X)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\operatorname{sgn}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}|\Delta_{N}(X)|\,.

(3.4)

This completes the proof.
∎

Then, the following Pfaffian formula holds for the generalized
\beta=1
model partition function.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
N\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
The following Pfaffian formula holds for the generalized
\beta=1
model partition function,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}=\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\mathsf{N}^{(1)}_{i-1,j-1}\,,

(3.5)

where we define a skew-symmetric matrix,

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{(1)}_{i,j}=\langle\,f_{i}\mid A\mid f_{j}\,\rangle\,.

(3.6)

Proof.

We can directly obtain this expression from de Bruijn’s integration formula (
2.33a
).
∎

Remark 3.4

(Skew-orthogonal functions)
.

Defining the skew-orthogonal functions
(F_{i})_{i\in[N]}
obeying the relation for
i,j\in[N/2]
,

\displaystyle\langle\,F_{2i-2}\mid A\mid F_{2j-1}\,\rangle=-\langle\,F_{2j-1}\mid A\mid F_{2i-2}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=h^{(1)}_{i}\delta_{i,j}\,,

(3.7a)

\displaystyle\langle\,F_{2i-2}\mid A\mid F_{2j-2}\,\rangle=\langle\,F_{2i-1}\mid A\mid F_{2i-1}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.7b)

the partition function is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}=\prod_{i\in[N/2]}h_{i-1}^{(1)}\,.

(3.8)

Similarly to the
\beta=2
matrix model, we may introduce the Christoffel–Darboux kernel for the
\beta=1
model as well, which will be a building block of the probability distribution function of the formal eigenvalues.

Definition 3.5

(Christoffel–Darboux kernel)
.

Let
N\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
We denote the inverse matrix of
\mathsf{N}^{(1)}
by
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}
.
Then, we define the Christoffel–Darboux (CD) kernel for the
\beta=1
model,

\displaystyle K_{N}^{(1)}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}f_{i}(x)\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}_{i,j}f_{j}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{F_{2i-1}(x)F_{2i-2}(y)-F_{2i-2}(x)F_{2i-1}(y)}{h_{i}^{(1)}}\,,

(3.9)

where
(F_{i})_{i\in[N]}
is a set of the skew-orthogonal functions defined in (
3.7
).
The corresponding integral operator is given by

\displaystyle K_{N}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}\ket{f_i}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}_{i,j}\bra{f_j}\,.

(3.10)

Remark 3.6
.

The CD kernel introduced here is slightly different from the quaternion kernel used in the standard analysis of the
\beta=1
model.
See, e.g.,
[
Meh04
,
For10
]
for details.

In fact, the CD kernel shows the property, called the self-reproducing property, which will be an important property to compute the correlation function.

Proposition 3.7
.

The CD kernel
K_{N}^{(1)}
is self-reproducing,

\displaystyle K_{N}^{(1)}\cdot A\cdot K_{N}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=K_{N}^{(1)}\,,

(3.11a)

\displaystyle\Tr\quantity[ K_N^{(1)} \cdot A ]

\displaystyle=N\,.

(3.11b)

Proof.

This property can be checked by direct calculation,

\displaystyle K_{N}^{(1)}\cdot A\cdot K_{N}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{N-1}\ket{f_i}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}_{i,j}\underbrace{\langle\,f_{j}\mid A\mid f_{k}\,\rangle}_{\mathsf{N}^{(1)}_{j,k}}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}_{k,l}\bra{f_l}=\sum_{i,l=0}^{N-1}\ket{f_i}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}_{i,l}\bra{f_l}=K_{N}^{(1)}\,.

(3.12)

The trace condition is shown as follows,

\displaystyle\Tr\quantity[ K_N^{(1)} \cdot A ]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}\int\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(y)}f_{i}(x)\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}_{i,j}f_{j}(y)A(y,x)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(1)}_{i,j}\mathsf{N}^{(1)}_{j,i}=N\,.

(3.13)

∎

Remark 3.8
.

From the completeness condition, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{\infty}^{(1)}(x,y)=\lim_{N\to\infty}K_{N}^{(1)}(x,y)=\widetilde{A}(x,y)\,,

(3.14)

where
\widetilde{A}(x,y)
is the kernel of the inverse operator
A^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\int\differential{\mu(z)}A(x,z)\widetilde{A}(z,y)=\int\differential{\mu(z)}\widetilde{A}(x,z)A(z,y)=\delta(x-y)\,.

(3.15)

Using this CD kernel, we may concisely express the probability distribution function as follows.

Corollary 3.9
.

The probability distribution function of the formal eigenvalues associated with the
\beta=1
matrix model (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{N}^{(1)}(X)\differential{\mu(X)}=\frac{1}{N!\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}\det_{i,j\in[N]}f_{j-1}(x_{i})\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}A(x_{i},x_{j})\differential{\mu(X)}\,,

(3.16)

is written in terms of the CD kernel

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{N}^{(1)}(X)\differential{\mu(X)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}K_{N}^{(1)}(x_{i},x_{j})\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}A(x_{i},x_{j})\differential{\mu(X)}\,.

(3.17)

Remark 3.10
.

This structure of the probability distribution function (involving both the CD kernel and the interaction kernel) is similarly discussed in the context of the coupled matrix model
[
EM98
,
BK22
]
.

3.2
\beta=4
model

We then consider the generalized version of the
\beta=4
matrix model.

Definition 3.11

(Generalized
\beta=4
matrix model)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(f_{i-1}(x))_{i\in[2N]}
,
(g_{i-1}(x))_{i\in[2N]}
be sequences of the integrable functions.
We define
2N
variables
(z_{i},z_{N+j})=(x_{i},y_{j})
for
i,j\in[N]
, and the corresponding integral measure by
\differential{\mu(Z)}=\prod_{i\in[2N]}\differential{\mu(z_i)}
.
For skew-symmetric functions,
A(x,y)
,
B(x,y)
, and a generic integrable function
S(x,y)
, we define the generalized
\beta=4
model partition function as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}=\frac{1}{N!^{2}}\int\differential{\mu(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ f_{j-1}(x_i) \ g_{j-1}(y_i) ]\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\mathsf{A}(x_{i},x_{j};y_{i},y_{j})\,,

(3.18)

where we denote

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(x,\tilde{x};y,\tilde{y})=\begin{bmatrix}A(x,\tilde{x})&S(x,\tilde{y})\\
-S^{\text{T}}(y,\tilde{x})&B(y,\tilde{y})\end{bmatrix}\,.

(3.19)

As is the case of the
\beta=1
model, the generalized
\beta=4
model is reduced to the standard
\beta=4
model via the specialization as follows.

Lemma 3.12
.

Putting

\displaystyle f_{i-1}(x)=p_{2N-i}(x)\,,\quad g_{i-1}(x)=p_{2N-i}^{\prime}(x)\,,\quad A(x,y)=B(x,y)=0\,,\quad S(x,y)=\delta(x-y)\,,

(3.20)

the generalized
\beta=4
model reduces to the standard
\beta=4
matrix model (quaternion self-dual; symplectic ensemble),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N,N}^{(4)}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\Delta_{N}(X)^{4}\,.

(3.21)

Proof.

Applying the formula (
2.26
) and Andréief’s formula (
2.32
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N,N}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}}\int\differential{\mu(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ p_{2N-j}'(y_i) ]\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}0&\delta(x_{i}-y_{j})\\
-\delta(y_{i}-x_{j})&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}}\int\differential{\mu(Z)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ p_{2N-j}'(y_i) ]\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\delta(x_{i}-y_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ p_{2N-j}(x_i) \ p_{2N-j}'(x_i) ]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\Delta_{N}(X)^{4}\,.

(3.22)

This completes the proof.
∎

Then, the following Pfaffian formula holds for the generalized
\beta=4
model partition function.

Proposition 3.13
.

The following Pfaffian formula holds for the generalized
\beta=4
model partition function,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}=\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}\mathsf{N}^{(4)}_{i-1,j-1}\,,

(3.23)

where we define a skew-symmetric matrix,

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{(4)}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\langle\,f_{i}\mid A\mid f_{j}\,\rangle+\langle\,f_{i}\mid S\mid g_{j}\,\rangle-\langle\,g_{i}\mid S^{\text{T}}\mid f_{j}\,\rangle+\langle\,g_{i}\mid B\mid g_{j}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bra{f_i}&\bra{g_i}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A&S\\
-S^{\text{T}}&B\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\ket{f_j}\\
\ket{g_j}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=:\langle\,f_{i},g_{i}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid f_{j},g_{j}\,\rangle\,.

(3.24)

Proof.

We obtain this formula by using de Bruijn’s integration formula (
2.33b
).
∎

Remark 3.14

(Skew-biorthogonal functions)
.

Defining the skew-biorthogonal functions
(F_{i},G_{i})_{i\in[N]}
obeying the relation for
i,j\in[N]
,

\displaystyle\langle\,F_{2i-2},G_{2i-2}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid F_{2j-1},G_{2j-1}\,\rangle=-\langle\,F_{2j-1},G_{2j-1}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid F_{2i-2},G_{2i-2}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=h^{(4)}_{i}\delta_{i,j}\,,

(3.25a)

\displaystyle\langle\,F_{2i-2},G_{2i-2}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid F_{2j-2},G_{2j-2}\,\rangle=\langle\,F_{2i-1},G_{2i-1}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid F_{2j-1},G_{2j-1}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.25b)

the partition function is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}=\prod_{i\in[N]}h_{i-1}^{(4)}\,.

(3.26)

Similarly to the
\beta=1
model, we consider the CD kernel for the
\beta=4
model, which is a matrix analog of the ordinary kernel.

Definition 3.15

(Matrix Christoffel–Darboux kernel)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
We denote the inverse matrix of
\mathsf{N}^{(4)}
by
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}
.
Then, we define the matrix Christoffel–Darboux (CD) kernel for the
\beta=4
model,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}(x,\tilde{x};y,\tilde{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2N-1}\begin{bmatrix}f_{i}(x)\\
g_{i}(y)\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\begin{bmatrix}f_{j}(\tilde{x})&g_{j}(\tilde{y})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{h_{i}^{(4)}}\begin{bmatrix}F_{2i+1}(x)F_{2i}(\tilde{x})-F_{2i}(x)F_{2i+1}(\tilde{x})&F_{2i+1}(x)G_{2i}(\tilde{y})-F_{2i}(x)G_{2i+1}(\tilde{y})\\
G_{2i+1}(y)F_{2i}(\tilde{x})-G_{2i}(y)F_{2i+1}(\tilde{x})&G_{2i+1}(y)G_{2i}(\tilde{y})-G_{2i}(y)G_{2i+1}(\tilde{y})\end{bmatrix}\,,

(3.27)

where
(F_{i-1},G_{i-1})_{i\in[2N]}
is a set of the skew-biorthogonal functions defined in (
3.25
).
In the operator formalism, it is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2N-1}\begin{bmatrix}\ket{f_i}\\
\ket{g_i}\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\begin{bmatrix}\bra{f_j}&\bra{g_j}\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2N-1}\begin{bmatrix}\ket{f_i}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\bra{f_j}&\ket{f_i}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\bra{g_j}\\
\ket{g_i}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\bra{f_j}&\ket{g_i}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\bra{g_j}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(3.28)

This matrix CD kernel similarly exhibits the self-reproducing property.

Proposition 3.16
.

The CD kernel
K_{N}^{(4)}
is self-reproducing,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}\cdot\mathsf{A}\cdot\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}\,,

(3.29a)

\displaystyle\Tr\quantity[ \mathsf{K}_N^{(4)} \cdot\mathsf{A} ]

\displaystyle=2N\,.

(3.29b)

Proof.

This property can be checked by direct calculation as follows,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}\cdot\mathsf{A}\cdot\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{2N-1}\begin{bmatrix}\ket{f_i}\\
\ket{g_i}\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\begin{bmatrix}\bra{f_j}&\bra{g_j}\end{bmatrix}\mathsf{A}\begin{bmatrix}\ket{f_k}\\
\ket{g_k}\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{k,l}\begin{bmatrix}\bra{f_l}&\bra{g_l}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{2N-1}\begin{bmatrix}\ket{f_i}\\
\ket{g_i}\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\mathsf{N}^{(4)}_{j,k}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{k,l}\begin{bmatrix}\bra{f_l}&\bra{g_l}\end{bmatrix}=\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}\,.

(3.30)

The trace condition is shown as follows,

\displaystyle\Tr\quantity[ \mathsf{K}_N^{(4)} \cdot\mathsf{A} ]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2N-1}\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(\tilde{x})}\differential{\mu(y)}\differential{\mu(\tilde{y})}\Tr\quantity(
\begin{bmatrix}
f_i(x) \\ g_i(y)
\end{bmatrix}
\widetilde{N}^{(4)}_{i,j}
\begin{bmatrix}
f_j(\tilde{x}) & g_j(\tilde{y})
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
A(x,\tilde{x}) & S(x,\tilde{y}) \\ - S(y,\tilde{x}) & B(y,\tilde{y})
\end{bmatrix}
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2N-1}\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}\mathsf{N}^{(4)}_{j,i}=2N\,.

(3.31)

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

From the completeness condition, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{\infty}^{(4)}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}=\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}\,,

(3.32)

which is the inverse of the integral operator
\mathsf{A}
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}\cdot\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}=\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}\cdot\mathsf{A}=1\,.

(3.33)

Using the matrix CD kernel, the probability distribution function of the formal eigenvalues may be written as follows.

Corollary 3.18
.

The probability distribution function of the formal eigenvalues associated with
the
\beta=4
matrix model (
3.18
),

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{N}^{(4)}(X;Y)\differential{\mu(X;Y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N],\ j\in[2N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ f_{j-1}(x_i) \ g_{j-1}(y_i) ]\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}A(x_{i},x_{j})&S(x_{i},y_{j})\\
-S^{\text{T}}(y_{i},x_{j})&B(y_{i},y_{j})\end{bmatrix}\differential{\mu(X;Y)}

(3.34)

is written in terms of the CD kernel,

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{N}^{(4)}(X;Y)\differential{\mu(X;Y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}}\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}(x_{i},x_{j};y_{i},y_{j})\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}A(x_{i},x_{j})&S(x_{i},y_{j})\\
-S^{\text{T}}(y_{i},x_{j})&B(y_{i},y_{j})\end{bmatrix}\differential{\mu(X;Y)}\,.

(3.35)

4
Characteristic polynomials

In this Section, we consider the characteristic polynomial averages of the
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
matrix models.
We first define the expectation value with respect to the corresponding probability distribution functions.

Definition 4.1

(Expectation value)
.

We define the expectation value with respect to the
probability distribution function
\mathsf{P}_{N}^{(\beta)}(X)
for
\beta=1,4
as follows,

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}(X)\,\rangle_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(X)}\mathsf{P}_{N}^{(\beta)}(X)\,\mathcal{O}(X)\,.

(4.1)

We remark that the number of variables are
N
and
2N
for
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
, respectively.

4.1
\beta=1
model

We consider the following special case of the
\beta=1
model (
3.1
) for
N\in 2\mathbb{N}
with a skew-symmetric function
A(x,y)=-A(y,x)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\Delta_{N}(X)\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}A(x_{i},x_{j})\,.

(4.2)

Then, we study the Schur polynomial average with respect to the
\beta=1
model, which would be a building block of the correlation functions discussed below.

Lemma 4.2

(Schur polynomial average)
.

Let
N\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
The Schur polynomial average of the
\beta=1
model is given by a rank
N
Pfaffian as follows,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{1}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\quantity[ \rvev{ x^{\lambda_i + N - i} \mid A \mid x^{\lambda_j + N - j} } ]\,.

(4.3)

Proof.

From the definition of the Schur polynomial (
2.16
), we have

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{1}=\frac{1}{N!\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}\int\differential{\mu(X)}\det_{i,j\in[N]}x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+N-j}\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}A(x_{i},x_{j})\,.

(4.4)

Then, applying the de Bruijn’s formula (
2.33a
), we arrive at the result.
∎

Applying this Schur polynomial average together with the Schur polynomial expansion of the characteristic polynomial, we obtain the Pfaffian formula for the characteristic polynomial average.

Proposition 4.3

(Characteristic polynomial)
.

Let
N,M\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
For
Z=(z_{\alpha})_{i\in[M]}
, the characteristic polynomial average of the
\beta=1
model is given by the rank
M
Pfaffian of the degree-
(N+M)
CD kernel,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N+M}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}K_{N+M}^{(1)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\,.

(4.5)

Proof.

We apply the Schur polynomial expansion formula (Lemma
2.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}s_{\lambda^{\vee}}(Z)\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}\Delta_{M}(Z)}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{\lambda^{\vee}_{\beta}+M-\beta}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ \rvev{ x^{\lambda_i + N - i} \mid A \mid x^{\lambda_j + N - j} } ]\,.

(4.6)

Applying the Laplace-type expansion (Lemma
2.9
) and the Pfaffian formula for the block matrix (
2.25
), we have

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N+M]\\
\alpha,\beta\in[M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,p_{N+M-i}\mid A\mid p_{N+M-j}\,\rangle&p_{N+M-i}(z_{\alpha})\\
-p_{N+M-i}(z_{\beta})&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N+M}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\quantity[ \sum_{i,j=0}^{N+M-1} p_i(z_\alpha) \widetilde{N}^{(1)}_{i,j} p_j(z_\beta) ]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N+M}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}K_{N+M}^{(1)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\,.

(4.7)

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we consider the average of the characteristic polynomial inverse.
For this purpose, we define the dual CD kernel as follows.

Definition 4.4

(Dual Christoffel–Darboux kernel)
.

Let
N\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
We define the integral operator as follows,

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{N}=A-\sum_{k,l=0}^{N-1}A\ket{p_k}\widetilde{N}^{(1)}_{k,l}\bra{p_l}A=A\quantity( \widetilde{A} - K_{N}^{(1)} )A\,.

(4.8)

Recalling the relation (
3.14
), we may write

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{N}=\sum_{i=N/2}^{\infty}A\quantity( \ket{\psi_{2i-1}} \bra{\psi_{2i-2}} - \ket{\psi_{2i-2}} \bra{\psi_{2i-1}} )A\,,

(4.9)

where
(\psi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a set of degree-
k
skew-orthonormal polynomials,
\langle\,\psi_{2i-2}\mid A\mid\psi_{2j-1}\,\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
,
\langle\,\psi_{2i}\mid A\mid\psi_{2j}\,\rangle=\langle\,\psi_{2i-1}\mid A\mid\psi_{2j-1}\,\rangle=0
.
Then, we define the dual Christoffel–Darboux kernel through the double Hilbert transform,

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{(1)}_{N}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(\tilde{x})}\frac{\bar{A}_{N}(x,\tilde{x})}{(z-x)(w-\tilde{x})}

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(\tilde{x})}\sum_{i=N/2}^{\infty}\frac{\bra{x \mid A }\quantity( \ket{\psi_{2i-1}} \bra{\psi_{2i-2}} - \ket{\psi_{2i-2}} \bra{\psi_{2i-1}} )\ket{ A \mid\tilde{x} }}{(z-x)(w-\tilde{x})}\,.

(4.10)

Using this dual CD kernel, we obtain the following Pfaffian formula for the characteristic polynomial inverse.

Proposition 4.5

(Characteristic polynomial inverse)
.

Let
N,M\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
For
Z=(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in[M]}
, the characteristic polynomial inverse average of the
\beta=1
model is given by a Pfaffian of the dual CD kernel,

\displaystyle N\geq M:

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)^{-1}}_{1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{NM}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-M}^{(1)}/\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\widetilde{K}_{N-M}^{(1)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\,,

(4.11a)

\displaystyle N\leq M:

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)^{-1}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1/\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\widetilde{K}_{0}^{(1)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})

\displaystyle\quad\times\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\in[M-N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ \sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^M
\rvev{p_{M-N-k} \mid z_\alpha} \rvev{z_\alpha\mid\frac{1}{\widetilde{K}^{(1)}_0} \mid z_\beta} \rvev{z_\beta\mid p_{M-N-l}} ]\,.

(4.11b)

Proof.

We first consider the case
N\geq M
.
In this case, we apply the Schur polynomial expansion formula (Lemma
2.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)^{-1}}_{1}=(-1)^{NM}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}s_{\lambda}(Z)\expectationvalue{\frac{s_\lambda(X^{-1})}{\det X^{M}}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{NM}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}\Delta_{M}(Z)}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{\lambda_{\beta}+M-\beta}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\times\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in[M]\\
k,l\in[N-M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,x^{-\lambda_{\alpha}-M+\alpha-1}\mid A\mid x^{-\lambda_{\beta}-M+\beta-1}\,\rangle&\langle\,x^{-\lambda_{\alpha}-M+\alpha-1}\mid A\mid x^{N-M-l}\,\rangle\\
\langle\,x^{N-M-k}\mid A\mid x^{-\lambda_{\beta}-M+\beta-1}\,\rangle&\langle\,x^{N-M-k}\mid A\mid x^{N-M-l}\,\rangle\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{NM}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-M}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{\lambda_{\beta}+M-\beta}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in[M]\end{subarray}}\quantity[
\rvev{ x^{- \lambda_\alpha- M + \alpha- 1} \mid\bar{A}_{N-M} \mid x^{- \lambda_\beta- M + \beta- 1} }
]\,.

(4.12)

Applying the Cauchy–Binet-type expansion (Lemma
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{\lambda_{\beta}+M-\beta}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in[M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,x^{-\lambda_{\alpha}-M+\alpha-1}\mid\bar{A}_{N-M}\mid x^{-\lambda_{\beta}-M+\beta-1}\,\rangle\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{\alpha}=0,\ldots,\infty\\
r_{\alpha}\neq r_{\beta}\end{subarray}}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{r_{\beta}}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in[M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,x^{-r_{\alpha}-1}\mid\bar{A}_{N-M}\mid x^{-r_{\beta}-1}\,\rangle\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\quantity[ \sum_{r,s=0}^\infty z_\alpha^{r} z_\beta^{s} \rvev{ x^{-r-1} \mid\bar{A}_{N-M} \mid x^{-s-1} } ]=\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\quantity[ \widetilde{K}_{N-M}^{(1)} (z_\alpha,z_\beta) ]\,.

(4.13)

The last expression is obtained as follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{r,s=0}^{\infty}z^{r}w^{s}\langle\,x^{-r-1}\mid\bar{A}_{N-M}\mid x^{-s-1}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(\tilde{x})}\frac{\bar{A}_{N-M}(x,\tilde{x})}{(z-x)(w-\tilde{x})}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{K}^{(1)}_{N-M}(z,w)\,.

(4.14)

This completes the derivation for the case
N\geq M
.
For the case
N\leq M
, on the other hand, the Schur polynomial expansion yields

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)^{-1}}_{1}=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\frac{s_{\lambda}(Z^{-1})}{\det Z^{N}}\expectationvalue{s_\lambda(X)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}\Delta_{M}(Z)}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in[M],\beta\in[N]\\
k\in[M-N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{\beta}-N+\beta-1}\\
p_{M-N-k}(z_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[N]}\langle\,x^{\lambda_{\alpha}+N-\alpha}\mid A\mid x^{\lambda_{\beta}+N-\beta}\,\rangle\,.

(4.15)

Applying the Cauchy–Binet-type expansion (
2.29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)^{-1}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1/\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in[M]\\
k,l\in[M-N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{K}_{0}^{(1)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})&p_{M-N-l}(z_{\alpha})\\
-p_{M-N-k}(z_{\beta})&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1/\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(1)}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\widetilde{K}_{0}^{(1)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\in[M-N]\end{subarray}}\quantity[ \sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^M
\rvev{p_{M-N-k} \mid z_\alpha} \rvev{z_\alpha\mid\frac{1}{\widetilde{K}^{(1)}_0} \mid z_\beta} \rvev{z_\beta\mid p_{M-N-l}} ]\,.

(4.16)

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2
\beta=4
model

We consider the following special case of the
\beta=4
model (
3.18
) for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and skew-symmetric functions
A(x,y)=-A(y,x)
and
B(x,y)=-B(y,x)
, and a generic integrable function
S(x,y)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}}\int\differential{\mu{(X;Y)}}\det_{i\in[N],j\in[2N]}\begin{bmatrix}p_{2N-j}(x_{i})&p_{2N-j}^{\prime}(y_{i})\end{bmatrix}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}A(x_{i},x_{j})&S(x_{i},y_{j})\\
-S^{\text{T}}(y_{i},x_{j})&B(y_{i},y_{j})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}}\int\differential{\mu{(X;Y)}}\det_{i\in[N],j\in[2N]}\begin{bmatrix}p_{2N-j}(x_{i})&p_{2N-j}^{\prime}(y_{i})\end{bmatrix}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\mathsf{A}(x_{i},x_{j};y_{i},y_{j})\,.

(4.18)

In order to consider the characteristic polynomial average for the
\beta=4
model, we compute the average of the modified Schur polynomial as follows.

Lemma 4.7

(Modified Schur polynomial average)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, the modified Schur polynomial average of the
\beta=4
model is given by a rank
2N
Pfaffian as follows,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}\langle\,x^{\lambda_{i}+2N-i}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid y^{\lambda_{j}+2N-j}\,\rangle\,,

(4.19)

where we here denote the norm by

\displaystyle\langle\,f\mid\mathsf{A}\mid g\,\rangle:=\langle\,f,g^{\prime}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid f,g^{\prime}\,\rangle\,.

(4.20)

Proof.

From the definition of the modified Schur polynomial (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\int\differential{\mu(X; Y)}\det_{i\in[N],j\in[2N]}\begin{bmatrix}x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+2N-j}&(y_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+2N-j})^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}A(x_{i},x_{j})&S(x_{i},y_{j})\\
-S^{\text{T}}(y_{i},x_{j})&B(y_{i},y_{j})\end{bmatrix}\,.

(4.21)

Then, applying the de Bruijn’s formula (
2.33b
), we arrive at the formula.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Putting

\displaystyle A(x,y)=B(x,y)=0\,,\qquad S(x,y)=\delta(x-y)\,,

(4.22)

as in Lemma
3.12
, the average of the modified Schur polynomial is reduced to that for the ordinary
2N
-variable Schur polynomial with respect to the symplectic ensemble,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}\ \xrightarrow{\eqref{eq:beta4specialization}}\ \expectationvalue{{s}_\lambda(X;X)}_{4}\,.

(4.23)

In order to describe the characteristic polynomial average, we define the auxiliary kernel, which is the
(1,1)
-component of the matrix kernel
\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}
, by

\displaystyle K_{N}^{(4)}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2N-1}p_{i}(x)\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(4)}_{i,j}p_{j}(y)\,.

(4.24)

Then, we may write the matrix kernel in terms of the auxiliary kernel as follows,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)}(x,\tilde{x};y,\tilde{y})=\begin{bmatrix}K_{N}^{(4)}(x,\tilde{x})&\partial_{\tilde{y}}K_{N}^{(4)}(x,\tilde{y})\\
\partial_{y}K_{N}^{(4)}({y},\tilde{x})&\partial^{2}_{y,\tilde{y}}K_{N}^{(4)}(y,\tilde{y})\end{bmatrix}\,.

(4.25)

With this auxiliary kernel, we obtain the following Pfaffian formula for the characteristic polynomial average.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
M\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and we denote
Z=(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in[M]}
.
Then, the following Pfaffian formula holds,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{2N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}s_{\lambda}(Z)\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N+M/2}^{(4)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}K_{N+M/2}^{(4)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\,.

(4.26)

Proof.

Applying the Laplace-type expansion (Lemma
2.9
) and the formula for the block matrix (
2.25
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{2N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}s_{\lambda}(Z)\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(M^{2N})}(-1)^{|\lambda|}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{\lambda_{\beta}^{\vee}+M-\beta}\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}\langle\,x^{\lambda_{i}+2N-i}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid y^{\lambda_{j}+2N-j}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[2N+M]\\
\alpha,\beta\in[M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,x^{2N+M-i}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid y^{2N+M-j}\,\rangle&z_{\alpha}^{2N+M-i}\\
-z_{\beta}^{2N+M-j}&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[2N+M]\\
\alpha,\beta\in[M]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,p_{2N+M-i}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid p_{2N+M-j}\,\rangle&p_{2N+M-i}(z_{\alpha})\\
-p_{2N+M-j}(z_{\beta})&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N+M/2}^{(4)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\quantity[ \sum_{i,j=0}^{2N+M-1} p_{i}(z_\alpha) \widetilde{N}^{(4)}_{i,j} p_j(z_\beta) ]=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N+M/2}^{(4)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}{K}_{N+M/2}^{(4)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\,.

(4.27)

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.10

(Characteristic polynomial)
.

The characteristic polynomial average with respect to the symplectic ensemble is given by a Pfaffian,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\prod_{\alpha\in[M]} \det(z_\alpha- X)^2}_{4}=\left.\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N+M/2}^{(4)}/\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}{K}_{N+M/2}^{(4)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\right|_{\eqref{eq:beta4specialization}}

(4.28)

We then consider the characteristic polynomial inverse for the
\beta=4
model.
For this purpose, we introduce the dual CD kernel as follows.

Definition 4.11

(Dual Christoffel–Darboux kernel)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
We define the integral operator as follows,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{N}=\mathsf{A}\quantity( \widetilde{\mathsf{A}} - \mathsf{K}_{N}^{(4)} )\mathsf{A}\,.

(4.29)

Recalling the relation (
3.32
), we may write

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{N}=\sum_{i=N}^{\infty}\mathsf{A}\begin{bmatrix}\ket{\phi_{2i-1}}\bra{\phi_{2i-2}}-\ket{\phi_{2i-2}}\bra{\phi_{2i-1}}&\ket{\phi_{2i-1}}\bra{\phi'_{2i-2}}-\ket{\phi_{2i-2}}\bra{\phi_{2i-1}'}\\[5.0pt]
\ket{\phi_{2i-1}'}\bra{\phi_{2i-2}}-\ket{\phi_{2i-2}'}\bra{\phi_{2i-1}}&\ket{\phi_{2i-1}'}\bra{\phi_{2i-2}'}-\ket{\phi_{2i-2}'}\bra{\phi_{2i-1}'}\end{bmatrix}\mathsf{A}\,,

(4.30)

where
(\phi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a set of degree-
k
skew-orthonormal polynomials,
\langle\,\phi_{2i-2}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid\phi_{2j-1}\,\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
,
\langle\,\phi_{2i}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid\phi_{2j}\,\rangle=\langle\,\phi_{2i-1}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid\phi_{2j-1}\,\rangle=0
.
Then, we define the dual matrix Christoffel–Darboux kernel through the double Hilbert transform,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}^{(4)}_{N}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(\tilde{x})}\frac{\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{N}(x,\tilde{x})}{(z-x)(w-\tilde{x})}\,.

(4.31)

We may also define an auxiliary kernel (the
(1,1)
-component of the matrix kernel
\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}^{(4)}_{N}
),

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{(4)}_{N}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\int\differential{\mu(x)}\differential{\mu(\tilde{x})}\sum_{i=N}^{\infty}\frac{\bra{x \mid\mathsf{A} }\quantity( \ket{\phi_{2i-1}} \bra{\phi_{2i-2}} - \ket{\phi_{2i-2}} \bra{\phi_{2i-1}} )\ket{ \mathsf{A} \mid\tilde{x} }}{(z-x)(w-\tilde{x})}\,.

(4.32)

Using this dual CD kernel, we obtain the following Pfaffian formula.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
M\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and we denote
Z=(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in[M]}
.
Then, the following Pfaffian formulas hold.

\displaystyle 2N\geq M:\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}\frac{s_{\lambda}(Z^{-1})}{\det Z^{2N}}\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-M/2}^{(4)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{N-M/2}^{(4)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\,,

(4.33a)

\displaystyle 2N\leq M:\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq 2N}\frac{s_{\lambda}(Z^{-1})}{\det Z^{2N}}\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\mathsf{\widetilde{K}}_{0}^{(4)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\Pf_{k,l\in[M-2N]}\quantity[\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^M \rvev{p_{M-2N-k} \mid z_\alpha}\rvev{z_\alpha\mid\frac{1}{\mathsf{\widetilde{K}}_0^{(4)}} \mid z_\beta} \rvev{z_\beta\mid p_{M-2N-l}}]\,.

(4.33b)

Proof.

We first consider the case

The summation over the partition is given as follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}\frac{s_{\lambda}(Z^{-1})}{\det Z^{2N}}\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq M}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{\beta}-2N+\beta-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\times\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in[M]\\

r_{\alpha}\neq r_{\beta}\end{subarray}}\det_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}z_{\alpha}^{-r_{\beta}+M-2N-1}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\langle\,x^{r_{\alpha}-M+2N}\mid\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{N-M/2}\mid y^{r_{\beta}-M+2N}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-M/2}^{(4)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\quantity[ \sum_{r,s=0}^\infty z_\alpha^{-r+M-2N-1} z_\beta^{-s+M-2N-1} \rvev{ x^{r - M + 2N} \mid\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{N-M/2} \mid y^{s - M + 2N} } ]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{M}(Z)}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{N-M/2}^{(4)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[M]}\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{N-M/2}^{(4)}(z_{\alpha},z_{\beta})\,.

(4.34)

In order to obtain the last expression, we may apply the same argument to the
\beta=1

4.14

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq 2N}\frac{s_{\lambda}(Z^{-1})}{\det Z^{2N}}\expectationvalue{\mathsf{s}_\lambda(X;Y)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}\Delta_{M}(Z)}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq 2N}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in[M],\beta\in[2N]\\
k\in[M-2N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}z_{\alpha}^{-\lambda_{\beta}-2N+\beta-1}\\
p_{M-2N-k}(z_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix}\Pf_{\alpha,\beta\in[2N]}\langle\,x^{\lambda_{\alpha}+2N-\alpha}\mid\mathsf{A}\mid y^{\lambda_{\beta}+2N-\beta}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}\Delta_{M}(Z)}\Pf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in[M]\\

5.1
D

We first consider the matrix model that we call the
D

Definition 5.1
.

Let
L,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
(X_{k},\widetilde{X}_{k})_{k\in[L]}=(x_{k,i},\tilde{x}_{k,i})_{i\in[N]}^{k\in[L]}

Denoting
Z_{k}=(z_{k,i},z_{k,N+i})_{i\in[N]}=(x_{k,i},\tilde{x}_{k,i})_{i\in[N]}
and
\underline{X}=(X_{k},\widetilde{X}_{k})_{k\in[L]}
, we define the partition function of the
D_{L+1}
-type quiver matrix model,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[D_{L+1}]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential{\mu(\underline{X}})}{(2N)!^{L-1}N!^{2}}\frac{\Delta_{2N}(Z_{1})^{2}\cdots\Delta_{2N}(Z_{L-1})^{2}\Delta_{N}(X_{L})^{2}\Delta_{N}(\widetilde{X}_{L})^{2}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i,j\in[2N]}(z_{1,i}-z_{2,j})\cdots(z_{L-2,i}-z_{L-1,j})\prod_{i\in[2N],j\in[N]}(z_{L-1,i}-x_{L,j})(z_{L-1,i}-\tilde{x}_{L,j})}\,.

(5.1)

This model is graphically described using the quiver diagram of type
D_{L+1}
as shown in (
1.2
).
For this
D
-type model, the following Pfaffian formula holds.

Proposition 5.2
.

We denote
\displaystyle\omega(x,y)=\frac{1}{x-y}
.
Then, the following Pfaffian formula holds for the partition function of the
D
-type matrix model,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[D_{L+1}]

\displaystyle=\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}\Bigg[\int\prod_{k=1}^{L}\differential{\mu(x_k;\tilde{x}_k)}\Bigg(\frac{p_{2N-i}(x_{1})}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\cdots\frac{1}{x_{L-1}-x_{L}}\frac{1}{x_{L}-\tilde{x}_{L}}\frac{1}{\tilde{x}_{L}-\tilde{x}_{L-1}}\cdots\frac{p_{2N-j}(\tilde{x}_{1})}{\tilde{x}_{2}-\tilde{x}_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt-\frac{p_{2N-j}(\tilde{x}_{1})}{\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}}\cdots\frac{1}{\tilde{x}_{L-1}-\tilde{x}_{L}}\frac{1}{\tilde{x}_{L}-{x}_{L}}\frac{1}{{x}_{L}-{x}_{L-1}}\cdots\frac{p_{2N-i}({x}_{1})}{{x}_{2}-{x}_{1}}\Bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=:\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}\quantity[ \rvev{p_{2N-i} \mid\omega^{2L-1} \mid p_{2N-j}} - \rvev{p_{2N-j} \mid\omega^{2L-1} \mid p_{2N-i}} ]\,.

(5.2)

Proof.

Recalling the relation

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(X_{L})\Delta_{N}(\widetilde{X}_{L})=\frac{\Delta_{2N}(Z_{L})}{\prod_{i,j\in[N]}(x_{L,i}-\tilde{x}_{L,j})}\,,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[D_{L+1}]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential{\mu(\underline{X}})}{(2N)!^{L-1}N!^{2}}\frac{\Delta_{2N}(Z_{1})^{2}\cdots\Delta_{2N}(Z_{L-1})^{2}\Delta_{2N}(Z_{L})^{2}}{\displaystyle\prod_{i,j\in[2N]}(z_{1,i}-z_{2,j})\cdots(z_{L-1,i}-z_{L,j})\prod_{i,j\in[N]}(x_{L,i}-\tilde{x}_{L,j})}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential{\mu(\underline{X}})}{(2N)!^{L-1}N!^{2}}\det_{i,j\in[2N]}p_{2N-i}(z_{1,j})\prod_{k=1}^{L-1}\quantity[ \det_{i,j \in[2N]} \qty(\frac{1}{z_{k,i} - z_{k+1,j}}) ]\det_{i,j\in[N]}\quantity( \frac{1}{x_{L,i} - \tilde{x}_{L,j}} )\,.

(5.4)

Applying Andréief formula (
2.32
) and de Bruijn’s formula (
2.33
) recursively, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[D_{L+1}]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential{\mu(Z_L)}}{N!^{2}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[2N]\\
j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,p_{2N-i}\mid\omega^{L-1}\mid x_{L,j}\,\rangle\\
\langle\,p_{2N-i}\mid\omega^{L-1}\mid\tilde{x}_{L,j}\,\rangle\end{bmatrix}\det_{i,j\in[N]}\langle\,x_{L,i}\mid\omega\mid\tilde{x}_{L,j}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential{\mu(Z_L)}}{N!^{2}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[2N]\\
j\in[N]\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}\langle\,p_{2N-i}\mid\omega^{L-1}\mid x_{L,j}\,\rangle\\
\langle\,p_{2N-i}\mid\omega^{L-1}\mid\tilde{x}_{L,j}\,\rangle\end{bmatrix}\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\begin{bmatrix}0&\langle\,x_{L,i}\mid\omega\mid\tilde{x}_{L,j}\,\rangle\\
-\langle\,\tilde{x}_{L,i}\mid\omega\mid{x}_{L,j}\,\rangle&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\Pf_{i,j\in[2N]}\quantity[ \rvev{p_{2N-i} \mid\omega^{2L-1} \mid p_{2N-j}} - \rvev{p_{2N-j} \mid\omega^{2L-1} \mid p_{2N-i}} ]\,.

(5.5)

This completes the proof.
∎

5.2

We then consider the matrix chain generalization of the

Definition 5.4
.

Let
N\in 2\mathbb{N}\ (\beta=1),\mathbb{N}\ (\beta=4)
,
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
\underline{X}=(X_{k})_{k\in[L]}=(x_{k,i})_{i\in[N]}^{k\in[L]}
a set of formal eigenvalues.
Let
A(x,y)
,
B(x,y)
and
S(x,y)
be skew-symmetric and generic integrable functions as before.
We denote by
(\omega_{k}(x,y))_{k=1,\ldots,L-1}
generic integrable two-variable functions, and by
(f_{i}(x),g_{i}(x))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
a set of integrable functions.
Then, we define the partition function of the
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
matrix chain models as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N^{L}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{L}}\int\differential{\mu(\underline{X})}\det_{i,j\in[N]}f_{j-1}(x_{1,i})\prod_{k=1}^{L-1}\quantity[ \det_{i,j \in[N]} \omega_{k}(x_{k,i},x_{k+1,j}) ]\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}A(x_{L,i},x_{L,j})\,,

(5.6a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N^{L}}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2L}}\int\differential{\mu(\underline{X})}\det_{i\in[N],j\in[2N]}\quantity[ f_{j-1}(x_{1,i}) \ g_{j-1}(\tilde{x}_{1,i}) ]

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\times\prod_{k=1}^{L-1}\quantity[ \det_{i,j \in[N]} {\Omega}_{k}(x_{k,i},\tilde{x}_{k,i};x_{k+1,j},\tilde{x}_{k+1,j}) ]\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\mathsf{A}(x_{L,i},\tilde{x}_{L,i};x_{L,j},\tilde{x}_{L,j})

(5.6b)

where we write

\displaystyle{\Omega}_{k}(x,\tilde{x};y,\tilde{y})=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{k}^{11}(x,y)&\omega_{k}^{12}(x,\tilde{y})\\
\omega_{k}^{21}(\tilde{x},y)&\omega_{k}^{22}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\end{bmatrix}\,,\qquad\mathsf{A}(x,\tilde{x};y,\tilde{y})=\begin{bmatrix}A(x,y)&S(x,\tilde{y})\\
-S^{\text{T}}(\tilde{x},y)&B(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\end{bmatrix}\,.

(5.6c)

For these partition functions, we obtain the Pfaffian formulas as follows.

Proposition 5.5
.

We define the dressed integral operators,

\displaystyle A_{L-1}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{L-1}\cdot A\cdot\omega_{L-1}^{\text{T}}\cdots\omega_{1}^{\text{T}}\,,

(5.7a)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{L-1}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{1}\cdots\Omega_{L-1}\cdot\mathsf{A}\cdot\Omega_{L-1}^{\text{T}}\cdots\Omega_{1}^{\text{T}}\,.

(5.7b)

Then, the partition functions of the coupled
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
models are given by Pfaffians,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N^{L}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\langle\,f_{i-1}\mid A_{L-1}\mid f_{j-1}\,\rangle\,,

(5.8a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N^{L}}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\Pf_{i,j\in[N]}\langle\,f_{i-1},g_{i-1}\mid\mathsf{A}_{L-1}\mid f_{j-1},g_{j-1}\,\rangle\,.

(5.8b)

Proof.

We first consider the case
\beta=1
.
Applying Andréief’s formula (
2.32
), we integrate the eigenvalues
(x_{k,i})_{i\in[N]}^{k=1,\ldots,L-1}
as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!^{L}}\int\differential{\mu(\underline{X})}\det_{i,j\in[N]}f_{j-1}(x_{1,i})\prod_{k=1}^{L-1}\quantity[ \det_{i,j \in[N]} \omega_{k}(x_{k,i},x_{k+1,j}) ]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int\differential{\mu(X_L)}\det_{i,j\in[N]}\langle\,f_{j-1}\mid\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{L-1}\mid x_{L,i}\,\rangle\,.

(5.9)

We have a similar relation for the case
\beta=4
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!^{2L}}\int\differential{\mu(\underline{X})}\det_{i\in[N],j\in[2N]}\quantity[ f_{j-1}(x_{1,i}) \ g_{j-1}(\tilde{x}_{1,i}) ]\prod_{k=1}^{L-1}\quantity[ \det_{i,j \in[N]} {\Omega}_{k}(x_{k,i},\tilde{x}_{k,i};x_{k+1,j},\tilde{x}_{k+1,j}) ]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!^{2}}\int\differential{\mu(X_L)}\det_{i\in[N],j\in[2N]}\quantity[
\begin{bmatrix}
\bra{f_{j-1}} \ \bra{g_{j-1}}
\end{bmatrix}
\Omega_1 \cdots\Omega_{L-1}
\begin{bmatrix}
\ket{x_{L,i}} \\ \ket{\tilde{x}_{L,i}}
\end{bmatrix}
]\,.

(5.10)

Then, we apply de Bruijn’s formula (
2.33
) to integrate the remaining variables
(x_{L,i})_{i\in[N]}
, and obtain the result.
∎

A Wong-Zakai theorem for SDEs with singular drift
Chengcheng Ling, Sebastian Riedel and Michael Scheutzow

Abstract
. We study stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with multiplicative Stratonovich-type noise of the form (0.1) d ​ X t = b ⁡ ( X t ) ​ d ​ t + σ ⁡ ( X t ) ∘ d ​ W t , X 0 = x 0 ∈ ℝ d , t ⩾ 0 , \displaystyle dX_{t}=b(X_{t})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(X_{t})\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t\geqslant 0, with a possibly singular drift b ∈ L p ​ ( ℝ d ) b\in L^{{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , p > d p>d and p ⩾ 2 p\geqslant 2 , and show that such SDEs can be approximated by random ordinary differential equations by smoothing the noise and the singular drift at the same time. We further prove a support theorem for this class of SDEs in a rather simple way using the Girsanov theorem. AMS 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 60H10, 60F15, 60J60. Keywords: singular stochastic differential equations; stability with respect to singular drifts; Wong-Zakai approximation; Zvonkin’s transformation; support theorem.



1.
Introduction

Consider the following stochastic differential equation (abbreviated as SDE) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t\geqslant 0,

where
d\geqslant 1
,
b=(b^{(i)})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\sigma=(\sigma_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow L(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(:=d\times d
real valued matrices
)
are Borel measurable, and
(W_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion defined on some filtered probability space
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},{\mathbb{P}})
. Using Picard iteration, it is easy to see that the corresponding ordinary differential equation (abbreviated as ODE)

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\quad X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t\geqslant 0,

has a unique solution provided that
b
is Lipschitz continuous. With a similar argument, under suitable growth and regularity assumptions on
\sigma
, it can be shown that there exists a unique strong solution to the SDE (
1.1
), assuming again that
b
is Lipschitz continuous. On the other hand, when
b
fails to be Lipschitz continuous, e.g. only
\alpha
-Hölder with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it is well known that the ODE (
1.2
) is generally ill-posed. Interestingly, due to a ’regularization by noise’ effect, the SDE (
1.1
) defined with a very singular
b
, maybe even discontinuous, might still be well-posed and possesses a unique solution even in the strong sense. There are many works on this aspect. In the following, we mention a few of them. A remarkable result due to Zvonkin
[
30
]
shows that if
d=1
,
\sigma
is uniformly elliptic,
b
and
\sigma
are bounded, and
\sigma
is continuous with respect to space and time variables, then the SDE (
1.1
) admits a unique strong solution
(X_{t}(x_{0}))_{t\geqslant 0}
for each
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The key idea is to use a certain transformation of the SDE, nowadays called
Zvonkin’s transformation
in the literature. Zvonkin’s result was then extended to the multidimensional case by Veretennikov
[
22
]
.
A further generalization was obtained by Krylov and Röckner
[
13
]
where existence and uniqueness of a strong solution for the SDE (
1.1
) was shown when
\sigma
is identity matrix in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(i.e. the SDE is driven by additive noise) and

(1.3)

\displaystyle b\in L^{q}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\quad\text{with}\quad q,p\in[2,\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad d/p+2/q<1.

Besides, Fedrizzi and Flandoli
[
3
]
introduced a new method to prove existence and uniqueness of a global strong solution to the SDE (
1.1
) by using regularizing properties of solutions to the Kolmogorov equation corresponding to (
1.1
), assuming that (
1.3
) holds globally. For a well-posedness result for (
1.1
) driven by multiplicative noise (i.e.
\sigma
is not a constant matrix only), we refer to Zhang
[
27
]
who applied Zvonkin’s transformation and Von der Lühe
[
17
]
where Fedrizzi and Flandoli’s method in
[
3
]
was generalized to the multiplicative noise case. More recently, Zhang and Zhao
[
29
]
obtained a well-posedness result for the martingale problem induced by the SDE (
1.1
) when the drift is a Schwartz distribution using Zvonkin’s transformation. Another way to investigate singular SDEs is rough paths theory: Harang and Perkowski in
[
9
]
, Catellier and Gubinelli in
[
2
]
, and Galeati and Gubinelli in
[
4
]
showed the pathwise uniqueness for a large class of singular SDEs.

A Wong-Zakai approximation to an SDE is given by a series of random ODEs which converge to the original equation. For classical SDEs, the approximating random ODEs can be obtained by just smoothing the noise. The first results for classical SDEs are contained in the works by Wong and Zakai
[
23
,
24
]
. In
[
23
, Theorem 2]
, it was proved that in dimension one, under the assumptions that
b\in\mathcal{C}_{b}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}_{b}^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
, if
(w^{n}_{t})_{n\geqslant 1}
is a continuous and piecewise smooth sequence of approximations to the Brownian motion
(W_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, then the solutions
X_{t}^{w^{n}}
to the following equation

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}^{w^{n}}=b(t,X_{t}^{w^{n}})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t}^{w^{n}})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}w^{n}_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\quad t\geqslant 0,

converge to
X_{t}
almost surely, where
X_{t}
solves

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t})\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\quad t\geqslant 0.

Here
\sigma(t,X_{t})\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}:=\sigma(t,X_{t})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma(t,X_{t})\frac{\partial\sigma(t,X_{t})}{\partial x}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t
, i.e. the
\circ
integral denotes the usual Stratonovich integral, and
\frac{1}{2}\sigma(t,X_{t})\frac{\partial\sigma(t,X_{t})}{\partial x}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t
is the Itô correction term. The fact that the limiting SDE has to be understood in Stratonovich sense is not surprising: if the approximating equations satisfy the usual chain rule, the same should be true for the limiting equation.
In the multidimensional case, one needs to be careful about how approximating the noise. For instance, P. Lévy showed that if
(w^{n,(1)},w^{n,(2)})
denotes the piecewise linear approximation to a two-dimensional Brownian motion
(W^{(1)},W^{(2)})
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}w^{n,(1)}_{s}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}w^{n,(2)}_{s}-w^{n,(2)}_{s}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}w^{n,(1)}_{s}\to\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}W^{(1)}_{s}\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{(2)}_{s}-W^{(2)}_{s}\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{(1)}_{s}.

The integral on the right hand side is called
Lévy area
. From this convergence, one can deduce that approximating the Brownian motion piecewise linearly in a multidimensional SDE converges, again, to the Stratonovich version of the SDE. However, E. J. McShane
[
15
]
gave an example of an approximation to a two-dimensional Brownian motion for which the limit is
not
the usual Lévy area, but the area plus another correction term (see Example
3.8
below). This implies that a multidimensional SDE with this approximation will not converge to the usual Stratonovich SDE. More results and surveys on this topic can be found in
[
11
]
,
[
10
, Chapter 7]
,
[
5
]
and the references therein. The study of Wong-Zakai approximations was further extended to stochastic partial differential equations (abbreviated as SPDEs), too, cf. e.g.
[
21
]
,
[
18
]
, and
[
8
]
. In
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, Gyöngy, Shmatkov and Stinga showed that the convergence rate of the Wong-Zakai approximation for SPDEs is
\frac{1}{2}
, which is the same as for SDEs.

Wong-Zakai approximations bridge the gap between the ODE and the SDE world. They are important for many purposes, e.g. in numerics or in subjects concerning the limiting behavior of SDEs. However, all known results exclusively treat SDEs with regular coefficients, i.e.
\sigma
and
b
were at least assumed to be Lipschitz continuous. In this article, we are interested in studying the case when the coefficients in the SDE might not be continuous at all but only belong to some
L^{p}
-space with
p>d
. Then immediately we are in the problematic situation that the approximating equation may not be well-defined for singular coefficients if we simply smooth out the noise. In fact, the roughness of the noise is essential to render the equation well-defined, most visible in the above cited works where a pathwise rough path approach is used. In order to handle this issue, we approximate the singular drift by a smooth one at the same time. Using a stability result that we obtain for singular SDEs (see Theorem
3.3
below), we can show that the solution of the approximated equation converges to the original singular SDE in the mean square sense. In the following, we loosely formulate our main result, the precise formulation can be found in Theorem
3.12
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
b
and
\sigma
be coefficients defining a singular SDE. In particular,
|b|\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p\in(d,\infty)
and
p\geqslant 2
. Let
(W_{t}^{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
resp.
(b_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be suitable approximations for a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
W_{t}
resp. for the coefficient
b
. Assume that
W^{n}
converges sufficiently fast to
W
compared to the convergence of
b_{n}
to
b
. Then the solution
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
to the singular equation

(1.5)

\displaystyle X_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}+\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{ij}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{il}\cdot\nabla_{l}\sigma_{jk}(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big)_{k=1,\cdots,d}

is the mean square limit of the solution to the equation

(1.6)

\displaystyle X_{t}^{n}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{n}(X_{s}^{n}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s}^{n}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}^{n}.

Above,
(c_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d}
are coefficients depending on the approximating sequence
(W_{t}^{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
.

The above result shows that certain random, regular ODEs (
1.6
) converge to the singular SDE (
1.5
). The condition that the speed of convergence of the noise approximation has to be faster than for the vector field approximation seems plausible, but it is open for us whether this condition is really necessary.

A typical application for the Wong-Zakai theorem is the proof of a support theorem which describes the topological support of the law of an SDE solution
[
20
,
5
,
19
,
1
]
. To our knowledge, there was no support theorem for singular SDEs in the literature until now. In principle, it is possible to mimic the proof of the classical support theorem for regular SDEs using Theorem
1.1
. However, it turns out that there is a much simpler proof, using a change of measure method to eliminate the drift which allows to apply a well-established support theorem for non-degenerate diffusions
[
20
]
. Here is a short form of our support theorem, cf. Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

It holds that

\textup{supp }\mu=\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\textup{supp }\mu
is the topological support of the distribution of the solution
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
to the singular SDE

\displaystyle X_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}.

The structure of this paper is as follows: We introduce some notation and preliminaries in Section 2. In Section 3, we first obtain the stability of the solution to a singular SDE with multiplicative noise with respect to the singular drift by using Zvonkin’s transformation. In the second part, we prove the Wong-Zakai Theorem for singular SDEs. The support theorem is shown in Section 4. The appendix collects some necessary results in PDE theory which are used when establishing the stability result in Section 2.

3.
Wong-Zakai Approximation for SDEs with singular drift driven by multiplicative noise

The proof of our main result consists of two steps. First, we prove a stability result for singular SDEs that shows that singular SDEs can be approximated by “classical” SDEs. Then, we formulate a quantitative version of the Wong-Zakai theorem. Both results together yield our main theorem. We start with step 1.

3.1.
Stability of SDEs with singular drift driven by multiplicative noise

We present a stability result under the following condition:

Assumption 3.1
.

For

(1), (2), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Psi^{1}(t,y)-\Psi^{2}(t,y)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y-\Psi^{2}(t,\cdot)\circ\Phi^{1}(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Phi^{2}(t,y)-\Phi^{1}(t,y)|

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}\|b^{1}-b^{2}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{p}^{q}(T)},

where
C_{3}=C(K,d,p,q,\|b^{1}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{p}^{q}([s_{0},t_{0}])},\|{b^{2}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{p}^{q}([s_{0},t_{0}])})
.
Then, combining (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{1}(x_{1})-X_{t}^{2}(x_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Psi^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{1}(y_{1}))-\Psi^{2}(t,Y_{t}^{2}(y_{2}))|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Psi^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{1}(y_{1}))-\Psi^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{2}(y_{2}))|^{2}+2{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Psi^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{2}(y_{2}))-\Psi^{2}(t,Y_{t}^{2}(y_{2}))|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|\nabla\Psi^{1}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}(T)}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}^{1}(y_{1})-Y_{t}^{2}(y_{2})|^{2}+C_{4}\|b^{1}-b^{2}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{p}^{q}(T)}^{2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{5}\|b^{1}-b^{2}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}_{p}^{q}(T)}^{2}+C_{7}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2},

where all constants appearing are positive.
Therefore, we obtain (

) and show that (

∎

3.2.
Wong-Zakai approximations

Next, we prove a quantitative version of the classical Wong-Zakai theorem. We follow the exposition in
[

]
.

Let
(\mathbb{W},{\mathbb{P}},\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
be a Wiener space, i.e.
\mathbb{W}:=\{\omega\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d}):\omega(0)=0\}
,
{\mathbb{P}}
is the Wiener measure defined on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathbb{W}
,
W_{t}(\omega):=\omega(t),\,t\geqslant 0
is a Wiener process and
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is the filtration generated by
(W_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
. We shall consider the following class of approximations:

Definition 3.5
.

[
10
, VI. Definition 7.1]

By an approximation of the Wiener process
W_{t}(\omega):=\omega(t),\,t\geqslant 0
, we mean a family
\{W^{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
of
d
-dimensional continuous processes defined on the Wiener space
(\mathbb{W},{\mathbb{P}},\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
such that

(1)

for every
\omega\in\mathbb{W},t\mapsto W^{n}_{t}(\omega)
is continuous and piecewise continuously differentiable,

(2)

W^{n}_{0}(\omega)
is
\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{n}}
-measurable, and
{\mathbb{E}}W^{n}_{0}=0

(3)

W^{n}_{t+\frac{k}{n}}(\omega)=W^{n}_{t}(\theta_{\frac{k}{n}}\omega)+\omega(\frac{k}{n})

and
\omega
, where
\theta_{t},t\geqslant 0
is the shift operator defined by
(\theta_{t}\omega)(s)=\omega(t+s)-\omega(t)
for
\omega\in\mathbb{W}
,

(4)

there exists a positive constant
C
such that

{\mathbb{E}}[|W^{n}_{\frac{1}{n}}|^{6}]\leqslant C\frac{1}{n^{3}},\quad{\mathbb{E}}{\Big(}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}|\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s}W^{n}_{s}|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s{\Big)}^{6}\leqslant C\frac{1}{n^{3}}.

From
[
10
, VI. Section 7]
we know that for such a sequence

we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|W_{t}-W_{t}^{n}|^{2}=0

for every

.
We further introduce the following notation for

(3.9)

\displaystyle S_{ij}(t):=\frac{{\Big(}\int_{0}^{t}W_{s}^{i}\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{j}_{s}-W_{s}^{j}\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{i}_{s}{\Big)}}{{{2}}t},\quad i,j=1,\cdots,d,\quad,t>0.

Further,

(3.10)

\displaystyle s_{ij}^{n}(t):=s_{ij}(t,n):={\mathbb{E}}S_{ij}(t,n)=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}{\Big(}\int_{0}^{t}W^{n,i}_{s}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s}W^{n,j}_{s}-W^{n,j}_{s}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s}W^{n,i}_{s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s{\Big)}}{2t},\quad i,j=1,\cdots,d,

and

(3.11)

\displaystyle c_{ij}^{n}(t):=c_{ij}(t,n):=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s}W^{n,i}_{s}{\Big(}W^{n,j}_{t}-W^{n,j}_{s}{\Big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big]}{t},\quad i,j=1,\cdots,d.

Further,
S_{ij}(0):=0
,
s_{ij}^{n}(0):=s_{ij}(0):=0
and
c_{ij}^{n}(0):=c_{ij}(0):=0
.

Observe that
(s_{ij}(t,n)):=(s_{ij}(t,n))_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d}
is a skew-symmetric
d\times d
-matrix for each

and

s_{ij}(t,n)=-s_{ji}(t,n)

The process

is known as the

in the literature and plays a fundamental role in rough paths theory. As explained in
[

, for such a general approximation

of

[

There exists a skew-symmetric

\displaystyle\big|s_{ij}^{n}(\frac{1}{n})-s_{ij}\big|\leqslant f_{n},\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}=0,\quad i,j=1,\ldots,d.

Proposition 3.7

Define

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Big|c_{ij}\Big(\frac{1}{n}Z\big(\frac{1}{n}\big),n\Big)-c_{ij}\Big|

In particular,

We provide three classical examples for such approximations. For this purpose, we introduce the following notation. Denote

In the rest of this section, we only consider time homogeneous coefficients,

To prove our main result, we need a quantitative version of the classical Wong-Zakai theorem, i.e. an upper bound for the convergence rate. It turns out that a careful inspection of the proof of
[

even leads to the known optimal convergence rate. We state this result in the next theorem.

Theorem 3.9

Suppose that for
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d
,
\sigma_{ij}\in\mathcal{C}_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(W^{n}_{t})_{n\geqslant 1}

(3.14)

\displaystyle X_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}+\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{ij}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{il}\cdot\nabla_{l}\sigma_{jk}(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big)_{k=1,\cdots,d},

and

\displaystyle X_{t}^{n}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s}^{n})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s}^{n})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}^{n},\quad t\geqslant 0.

Then, for each
T>0
and

(3.15)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\Big(}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}-X_{t}^{n}|^{2}{\Big)}\leqslant C_{1}e^{C_{2}\|b\|_{\mathcal{C}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}(f^{2}_{n}+(1+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|b(x)|^{2})\frac{1}{n^{1-\delta}}),

,

and

and

Proof.

We closely follow the argument in the proof of
[

but we keep track of the convergence rate of the approximating sequence

to

in the mean square sense on

For
\delta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle Z_{\delta}:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Z_{\delta}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{4}n^{-1}\leqslant\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1-\delta}}=0,\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}Z_{\delta}(s)=\infty.

[

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle{\Big(}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}-X_{t}^{n}|^{2}{\Big)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime}\|b\|_{\mathcal{C}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbb{E}}{\Big(}|X_{t}-X_{t}^{n}|^{2}{\Big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C^{\prime\prime}[(1+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|b(x)|^{2})Z_{\delta}(n^{-1})^{4}n^{-1}+c_{n}^{2}].

Finally, Gronwall’s inequality together with (

Remark 3.10
.

If
f_{n}=0
for every

3.9
shows that the Wong-Zakai approximation converges with rate arbitrarily close to

uniformly in

As we already mentioned earlier, to formulate a Wong-Zakai theorem for singular SDEs, we cannot just simply replace the noise by a smooth approximation since this will render the equation ill-posed. To solve this problem, we will approximate the singular coefficient
b
at the same time. We will now introduce a class of approximations for this
b
.

Definition 3.11

(
\mathcal{A}(b,p,h)
: approximation of
|b|\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
)
.

Let
(b_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of functions

. For a function

, we write
(b_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}\in\mathcal{A}(b,p,h)
if

(i)

b_{n}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{C}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i=1,\ldots,d
,

(ii)

\|b-b_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

(iii)

\|b_{n}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant h(n)\|b\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Next, we state our main theorem.

Theorem 3.12
.

Assume that

for some

and

. Let
\sigma

(W^{n}_{t})
is sequence of approximations for

, satisfying all the conditions in Definition
3.5
and Assumption
3.6

(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

(3.17)

\displaystyle X_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}+\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{ij}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{il}\cdot\nabla_{l}\sigma_{jk}(X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big)_{k=1,\cdots,d}

Remark 3.13
.

(1)

Theorem
3.12
states that an approximation to a singular SDE converges to this SDE provided that the approximation of the noise converges sufficiently fast compared to the approximation of the singular coefficient
b
, cf. condition (
3.18
). Although this condition seems plausible and is true in the “extreme” case (one can not first let
b_{n}\to b
and then
W^{n}\to W
), we do not know at the moment whether such a condition is really necessary. We leave this point as an open problem.

(2)

It is easy to deduce a convergence rate of the Wong-Zakai approximation. However, this can arbitrarily slow, depending on the convergence rate of
b_{n}\to b
. For a smooth
b
, we rediscover the known convergence rate of (almost)
\frac{1}{2}
in the classical Wong-Zakai theorem.

(3)

The approximation class
\mathcal{A}(b,p,h)
turns out to be of own interest and is discussed in more detail in
[
12
]
.

In the end we give some examples to show how this theorem works in different cases.

(2)

Support theorem for Singular SDEs

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},{\mathbb{P}})
be a probability spaces and
(W_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
a Wiener process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Consider the SDE

(4.1)

\displaystyle X_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},\quad t\in[0,T].

We are interested in describing the topological support of the distribution of the solution
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
equipped with the topology of uniform convergence when
b
is a singular drift.
The classical strategy to derive a description of the support uses the Wong-Zakai theorem, see e.g.
[
10
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
18
]
. Here, we use a different approach which turns out to be much easier in our context. Namely, we use Girsanov theory to show that actually the support of the distribution of the solution to (
4.1
) is the full canonical space
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which essentially is because of the non degenerated noise.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the conditions in Assumption
3.1
are satisfied. Let

\Omega_{x_{0}}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{\omega\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}):w_{0}=x_{0}\}.

Then

\textup{supp }\mu=\Omega_{x_{0}}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\textup{supp }\mu
is the topological support of the distribution of the solution
(X_{t}(x_{0}))_{t\in[0,T]}
to the SDE (
4.1
) in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

First we consider the following equation

\displaystyle Y_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,Y_{s})\circ{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},\quad t\geqslant 0.

By Theorem
3.2
, there is an unique and strong solution
(Y_{t}(x_{0}))_{t\geqslant 0}
to the above SDE.

Let
\textup{supp }\mu_{Y}
be the topological support of the distribution of
(Y_{t}(x_{0}))_{t\geqslant 0}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Under the condition
(H_{\sigma})
, by
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
we know that

\textup{supp }\mu_{Y}=\Omega_{x_{0}}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Besides, because of
[
25
, Lemma 4.1 (ii)]
, for any constant
\kappa>0
, for any
0\leqslant T<\infty
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\bigg\{\kappa\int_{0}^{T}|b(s,Y_{s})|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\bigg\}\leqslant C(T,\kappa,d,{p},q,\|b\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}^{q}(T)})<\infty,

i.e. Novikov’s condition is fulfilled. As a result, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\rho_{T}:={\mathbb{E}}\exp\bigg\{{\int_{0}^{T}\big[b^{*}\sigma^{-1}\big](s,Y_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\big[b^{*}(\sigma\sigma^{*})^{-1}b\big](s,Y_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\bigg\}=1.

Then by the Girsanov theorem, for any bounded functional
f
defined on
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

{\mathbb{E}}[f(X_{\cdot})]={\mathbb{E}}[\rho_{T}f(Y_{\cdot})],\quad 0\leqslant T<\infty.

This shows that the distribution of the solution
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
to SDE (
4.1
) in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
is absolutely continuous with respect to the distribution of
(Y_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and vice versa, therefore they share the same support, which completes our proof.

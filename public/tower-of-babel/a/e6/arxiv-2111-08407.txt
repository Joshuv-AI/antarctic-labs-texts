Finite Action Principle and wormholes.
Jan Chojnacki 1, Jan Kwapisz 1,2

Abstract
In this work, we elaborate on the finite action for wormholes in higher derivative theories. Both non-traversable and traversable wormholes in theories with higher curvature invariants posses finite action.



keywords
Horava gravity; Wormholes; finite action

\bodymatter

1
Introduction

The Finite Action Principle (FAP) proposing that the physical spacetimes are those for which the action is finite. This comes reasoning comes from the fact that in Euclidian path integral the weighting factor is
e^{-S[\Phi]}
. Hence lesser the action the more it contributes to the path integral. On the other hand in the Lorentztian signature the more varying actions contribute less. Hence for the actions neighbouring the infinite one the variation is significant and the neighbouring ones do cancel each other, see
Borissova and Eichhorn 2021
.

This notion has recently received significant attention in the literature, see
Barrow and Tipler 1988
;
Jonas et al. 2021
;
Chojnacki and Kwapisz 2021
for the cosmological considerations and
Borissova and Eichhorn 2021
;
Chojnacki and Kwapisz 2021
for the black hole ones. Since it is expected that the quantum gravity should resolve the black-hole singularity problem, one may ask which of the microscopic actions remain finite for non-singular black holes and conversely interfere destructively for the singular ones. This we shall call the finite action selection principle. Only after the inclusion of higher-curvature operators, beyond the Einstein-Hilbert term, such selection principle can be satisfied
Borissova and Eichhorn 2021
. Furthermore in asymptotic safety, the quantum corrections to the Newtonian potential eliminate the classical-singularity
Bosma et al. 2019
.

These findings suggest that by taking into account the higher curvatures one can resolve the singularities in black holes. Yet, an issue with the higher-curvature theory of quantum gravity is the existence of the particles with the negative mass-squared spectrum, known as
ghosts
, which makes the theory non-unitary.
In this article, we explore possible resolution, namely, we investigate Horava-Lifshitz (H-L) gravity
Hořava 2009
, where the Lorentz Invariance (LI) is broken at the fundamental level (see
Wang 2017
for a comprehensive progress report on this subject). Kinetic terms are first order in the time derivatives, while higher spatial curvature scalars regulate the UV behavior of the gravity. In our investigation we follow Horava
Hořava 2009
and assume that Wick rotation is well defined. This can be motivated by the lack of the higher time-like curvature invariants. In the usual higher derivative constructions the existence of massive poles in the propagator makes the Wick rotation troublesome
Donoghue and Menezes 2019
. Conversely to the latter, the new poles are massless and non-tachyonic for suitable choice of parameters
Bemfica and Gomes 2011
. Furthermore, the existence of the foliation supports that claim. For the discussion of Minkowski path integral and FAP, see
Borissova and Eichhorn 2021
.

In the previous article, we discussed the possibility that the Finite Action arguments applied to the projectable H-L gravity result in a flat, homogeneous, UV-complete, and ghost-free beginning of the universe
Chojnacki and Kwapisz 2021
. However there is no known regular black hole solutions in HL gravity. Here we discuss the wormholes solutions in context of the Finite Action Principle in HL gravity as well as in the
f(R)
theories. Interestingly the stable, traversable wormholes solutions are known only in the higher derivative gravities
Duplessis and Easson 2015
(without exotic matter), so there seems to be a wormhole/non-singular BH trade-off after taking into account the Finite Action Principle.

2
Horava-Lifshitz gravity

In the Horava-Lifshitz gravity, space and time are scaled in a non-equivalent way. Diffeomorphism invariance is broken by the foliation of the 4-dimensional spacetime into 3-dimensional hypersurfaces of constant time, called leaves, making the theory power-counting renormalizable (see also the renormalization group studies of the subject
D’Odorico et al. 2014
;
D’Odorico et al. 2015
;
Barvinsky et al. 2017
). The remaining symmetry respects transformations:

\displaystyle t\xrightarrow{}\xi_{0}(t),\quad x^{i}\xrightarrow{}\xi^{i}(t,x^{k}),

(1)

and is often referred to as the foliation-preserving diffeomorphism, denoted by Diff
(M,\mathcal{F})
. The diffeomorphism invariance is still present on the leaves. The four-dimensional metric may be expressed in the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM)
Arnowitt et al. 2008
variables:

\displaystyle(N,N^{i},\,^{(3)}g_{ij}),

(2)

where
N,\,N^{i},\,\,^{(3)}g_{ij}
denote respectively the lapse function, shift vector, and 3-dimensional induced metric on the leaves. The theory is constructed from the following quantities:

\,{}^{(3)}R_{ij},\quad K_{ij},\quad a_{i},\quad\,^{(3)}\nabla_{i},

(3)

where
\,{}^{(3)}R_{ij}
is the 3-dimensional Ricci curvature tensor,
\,{}^{(3)}\nabla_{i}
is the covariant derivative constructed from the 3-dimensional metric
\,{}^{(3)}g_{ij}
, and
a_{i}:=\frac{N,_{i}}{N}
. Extrinsic curvature
K_{ij}
is the only object, invariant under general spatial diffeomorphisms containing exactly one time derivative of the metric tensor
\,{}^{(3)}g_{ij}
:

\displaystyle K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2N}\left(\frac{\partial\,^{(3)}g_{ij}}{\partial t}-\,^{(3)}\nabla_{i}N_{j}-\,^{(3)}\nabla_{j}N_{i}\right).

(4)

Quantities (
2
) are tensor/vectors with respect to Diff(
M,\mathcal{F}
) possessing the following mass dimensions:

\displaystyle[\,^{(3)}R_{ij}]=2,\quad[K_{ij}]=3,\quad[a_{i}]=1,\quad[\,^{(3)}\nabla_{i}]=1.

(5)

One may use (
2
) to construct, order by order, scalar terms appearing in the Lagrangian of the theory. Following
Wang 2017
;
Maier and Soares 2017
the action of the Horava gravity takes the form:

\displaystyle S_{g}=\zeta^{2}\int dtdx^{3}N\sqrt{{}^{(3)}g}\left(\mathcal{K}-V\right),

(6)

where
\mathcal{K}=K_{ij}K^{ij}-\lambda K^{2}
with
K=K_{ij}\,^{(3)}g^{ij}
,
\,{}^{(3)}g
denotes the determinant of the 3-dimensional metric and
\zeta^{2}=1/16\pi G
.
It may be expressed as the difference of the kinetic and potential part

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{K}-V
with

\mathcal{K}=\left(K_{ij}K^{ij}-\lambda K^{2}\right)
. At the 6th order, the potential part of the lagrangian contains over 100 terms
Wang 2017
. The immense number of invariants is limited by imposing further symmetries. One possible restriction for the potential comes from the projectability condition
N=N(t)
, then terms proportional to
a_{i}\equiv 0
vanish. Up to the sixth order (compatible with power counting renormalizability), the potential
V
restricted by the projectability condition is given by:

\displaystyle V=2\Lambda\zeta^{2}-\,^{(3)}R+\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}\left(g_{2}\,^{(3)}R^{2}+g_{3}\,^{(3)}R^{ij}\,{}^{(3)}R_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\zeta^{4}}\left(g_{4}{}^{(3)}R^{3}+g_{5}\,^{(3)}R\,^{(3)}R^{ij}\,{}^{(3)}R_{ij}+g_{6}\,^{(3)}R^{i}_{j}\,{}^{(3)}R^{j}_{k}\,{}^{(3)}R^{k}_{i}\right),

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\zeta^{4}}\left(g_{7}{}^{(3)}R\nabla^{2}{}^{(3)}R+g_{8}(\nabla_{i}{}^{(3)}R_{jk})(\nabla^{i}{}^{(3)}R^{jk})\right),

(7)

where
\Lambda
is the cosmological constant and
\alpha_{ij}
are the coupling constants. For our purposes, we drop terms containing covariant derivatives
\,{}^{(3)}\nabla_{i}
. One should also mention that this
minimal theory

Horava 2011
suffers from the existence of spin 0 graviton, which is unstable in the IR. Various solutions to this problem have been proposed. One can add the additional local
U(1)
symmetry
Wang 2017
;
Horava and Melby-Thompson 2010
. Then by the introduction of new fields prevents the zero-mode from propagating. On the other hand, one can drop the projectability condition
a_{i}=0
and include the terms containing
a_{i}
in the potential term:

\displaystyle V=2\Lambda\zeta^{2}-\,^{(3)}R-\beta_{0}a_{i}a^{i}+\sum_{n=3}^{6}\mathcal{L}_{V}^{(n)},

(8)

then for the spin-0 mode to be stable one requires
0<\beta_{0}<2

Blas et al. 2011
;
Carloni et al. 2011
.

3
Black holes and wormholes

In this section, we show that H-L gravity satisfies the Finite Action selection principle for the microscopic action of quantum gravity
Borissova and Eichhorn 2021
. We study both the solutions of H-L gravity and the known, off-shell BH spacetimes, due to the fact that there are no known regular BH solutions in the HL gravity. Keep in mind that a metric does not need to be a solution to the equations of motion to enter the path integral. We require singular black-hole metrics to interfere destructively, while the regular ones with finite action contribute to the probability amplitudes. We broaden this analysis by studying the wormhole solutions.

4
Wormholes and finite action principle

Here, we take the first step in the direction of the investigations of the consequences of the Finite Action Principle in the context of wormholes (WH). The wormholes may be characterized in two classes: traversable and non-traversable. The traversable WH, colloquially speaking, are such that one can go through it to the other side, see
Morris and Thorne 1988
for specific conditions.
The pioneering Einstein-Rosen bridge has been found originally as a non-static, non-traversable solution to GR. The traversable solutions are unstable, however, they might be stabilized by an exotic matter or inclusion of the higher curvature scalar gravity
Duplessis and Easson 2015
. This is important in the context of finite action since usually the divergences of black holes do appear in the curvature squared terms. Hence, due to the inclusion of the higher-order terms in the actions, the traversable wormholes are solutions to the equations of motions without the exotic matter. The exemplary wormhole spacetimes investigated here are the Einstein-Rosen bridge proposed in
Einstein and Rosen 1935
, the Morris-Thorne (MT) wormhole
Morris and Thorne 1988
, the traversable exponential metric wormhole
Boonserm et al. 2018
and the wormhole solution discussed in the H-L gravity
Botta Cantcheff et al. 2010
. All of them have a finite action. Here, we shall discuss the exponential metric WH. The conclusions for the other possible wormholes are similar and we discuss them in the Appendix. For the exponential metric WH, the line element is given by:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{-\frac{2M}{r}}dt^{2}+e^{\frac{2M}{r}}\left(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\right).

(9)

This spacetime consists two regions: “our universe” with
r>M
and the “other universe” with
r<M
.
r=M
corresponds to the wormhole’s throat. The spacial volume of the “other universe” is infinite when
r\xrightarrow{}0
. Such volume divergence is irrelevant to our discussion since it describes large distances in the ”other universe”. Hence, we further consider only
r\geq M
.
The resulting Ricci and Kretschmann scalars calculated in
Boonserm et al. 2018
and the measure are non-singular everywhere:

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=-\frac{2M^{2}}{r^{4}}e^{\frac{-2M}{r}},

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}R^{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{4M^{2}(12r^{2}-16Mr+7M^{2})}{r^{8}}e^{-4\frac{M}{r}},

(10)

resulting in the finite action for the Stelle gravity. Similarly for the H-L gravity:

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R=R,\quad K^{2}=K_{ij}K^{ij}=0,

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R_{ij}{}^{(3)}R^{ij}=\frac{2M^{2}(M^{2}-2Mr+3r^{2})}{r^{8}}e^{-\frac{4M}{r}}.

(11)

Einstein-Rosen bridge

The Einstein-Rosen (E-R) bridge smoothly glues together two copies of the Schwarzschild spacetime: black-hole and the white-hole solutions corresponding to the
positive and negative coordinate
u
.
Metric tensor of the Einstein-Rosen wormhole proposed in
Einstein and Rosen 1935
and discussed in e.g.
Katanaev 2014
is given by:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{-u^{2}}{u^{2}+4M}dt^{2}+(u^{2}+4M)du^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(u^{2}+4M)d\Omega^{2}.

(12)

The E-R bridge is non-traversable and geodesically incomplete in
u=0
. This fact, however, does not impact the regularity of the curvature scalars.
The 4-dimensional Ricci scalar is:

\displaystyle R=\frac{2\left(64M^{2}+32Mu^{2}+4u^{4}+u^{2}\right)}{\left(4M+u^{2}\right)^{3}}.

(13)

The second order curvature scalar
R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}
is:

\displaystyle\frac{4\left(48M^{2}+8\left(4M+u^{2}\right)^{4}+(32M-1)\left(4M+u^{2}\right)^{2}\right)}{\left(4M+u^{2}\right)^{6}}.

(14)

Both of which integrated with the measure are non-singular:

\displaystyle\sqrt{g}=\frac{1}{4}u(4M+u^{2}).

(15)

The wormhole solutions analyzed in this paper generally yield the finite action in both GR and H-L. The finite Action Principle suggests, that in the quantum UV regime, singular black-hole spacetimes may be replaced with the regular wormhole solutions.

Morris-Thorne wormhole

The MT wormhole is defined in the spherically symmetric, Lorentzian spacetime by the line element:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{b(r)}{r}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}

(16)

where
\Phi(r)
is known as the redshift and there are no horizons if it is finite. Function
b(r)
determines the wormhole’s shape. We choose
\Phi(r),\,b(r)
to be:

\displaystyle\Phi(r)

\displaystyle=0,\quad b(r)=2M\left(1-e^{r_{0}-r}\right)+r_{0}e^{r_{0}-r},

(17)

where
r_{0}
is the radius of the throat of the wormhole, such that
b(r_{0})=r_{0}
. 4-dimensional curvature scalars for this spacetime have been calculated in
Mattingly et al. 2020
. The Ricci curvature scalar is singular at
r=0
, however, the radial coordinate
r
varies between
r_{0}>0
and infinity:

\displaystyle R=-2\left(2M-r_{0}\right)\frac{e^{r_{0}-r}}{r^{2}}.

(18)

The resulting
S_{s}=\int_{r_{UV}=r_{0}}^{r_{IR}}\sqrt{g}R
function is divergent as
r_{UV}\xrightarrow{}r_{0}
and cannot be expressed in terms of simple functions:

\displaystyle 2(2M-r_{0})\int_{r_{UV}=r_{0}}^{r_{IR}}\sqrt{\frac{r}{r-2M(1-e^{r_{0}-r})+r_{0}e^{r_{0}-r}}}e^{r_{0}-r}dr.

(19)

However, this is only a coordinate singularity and one may get rid of it with a proper transformation.

Higher-order curvature scalars for Morris-Thorne wormhole are:

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R

\displaystyle=\frac{2b^{\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R_{ij}{}^{(3)}R^{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{3r^{2}b^{\prime}(r)^{2}-2rb(r)b^{\prime}(r)+3b(r)^{2}}{2r^{6}},

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R^{i}_{j}{}^{(3)}R^{j}_{k}{}^{(3)}R^{k}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{-9r^{2}b(r)b^{\prime}(r)^{2}+5r^{3}b^{\prime}(r)^{3}+15rb(r)^{2}b^{\prime}(r)-3b(r)^{3}}{4r^{9}},

(20)

and integrated give action that is finite.

H-L wormhole

Static spherically traversable symmetric wormholes have been constructed in
Botta Cantcheff et al. 2010
in the H-L theory through the modification of the Rosen-Einstein spacetime:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-N^{2}(\rho)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{f(\rho)}d\rho^{2}+(r_{0}+\rho^{2})^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(21)

with additional
\mathbf{Z}_{2}
symmetry with respect to the wormhole’s throat. There are solutions with
\lambda=1
asymptotically corresponding to the Minkowski vacuum. Explicitly we have:

\displaystyle f=N^{2}

\displaystyle=1+\omega(r_{0}+\rho^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{(r_{0}+\rho^{2})\left(\omega^{2}(r_{0}+\rho^{2})^{3}+4\omega M\right)}.

(22)

Radius of the wormhole’s throat is given by
r_{0}
. The parameters
\omega,
and
M
are connected to the coupling constants in H-L action. See
Botta Cantcheff et al. 2010
for their explicit form.
Ricci scalar of the H-L wormhole invariants are given by

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{{(\rho^{2}+r_{0}){}^{2}}}\Big[2(-10\rho^{2}\sqrt{\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0})(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}

\displaystyle-4r_{0}\sqrt{\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0})(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}+16\rho^{6}\omega+8\rho^{2}

\displaystyle+36\rho^{4}r_{0}\omega+24\rho^{2}r_{0}^{2}\omega+4r_{0}^{3}\omega+4r_{0}-1)\Big],

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R_{ij}{}^{(3)}R^{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\rho^{2}+r_{0})^{4}}\left\{2(-7\rho^{2}\sqrt{\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0})(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}-2r_{0}\sqrt{\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0})(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}\right.

\displaystyle+10\rho^{6}\omega+6\rho^{2}+22\rho^{4}r_{0}\omega+14\rho^{2}r_{0}^{2}\omega+2r_{0}^{3}\omega+2r_{0}-1){}^{2}+4M

\displaystyle+\frac{4(\rho^{2}+r_{0})}{\omega(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}\left[\rho^{2}\omega(-4(\rho^{2}+r_{0})\sqrt{\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0})(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})\right]

\displaystyle\left.-2\sqrt{\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0})(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}\left[-\sqrt{\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0})(4M+\omega(r_{0}+\omega^{2}){}^{3})}+\omega(\rho^{2}+r_{0}){}^{2}+1)\right]{}^{2}\right\}

(23)

The kinetic terms with
K_{ij}=0
are vanishing, while the spacial Ricci scalar and higher curvature terms are finite.

From the point of view of the Finite Action Principle, all of the investigated wormhole spacetimes are included in the gravitational path integral.

5
Conclusions and discussion

The Finite Action Principle is a powerful tool to study quantum gravity theories and also QFTs in general. From the point of view of finite action selection principle
Borissova and Eichhorn 2021
;
Chojnacki and Kwapisz 2021
they are equally good theories, resolving the black holes singularities, assuming that ghost issue is resolved in the latter case. Yet none of the regular B-H solutions have been found in the context of H-L gravity
Wang
and the ones found for
f(R)
suffer from mass inflation issue
Carballo-Rubio et al. 2018
;
Bonanno et al. 2021
;
Bertipagani et al. 2021
. Hence it is a strong suggestion that the wormholes may appear in the UV regime of H-L gravity and can serve as a “cure” for singularities
Lobo 2016
;
Olmo et al. 2017
;
Bambi et al. 2016
.

In the case of wormholes, both traversable and non-traversable wormholes are on equal footing in the case of the Finite Action Principle, yet maybe the finite amplitude principle could distinguish between those
Jonas et al. 2021
, since the amplitude to cross non-traversable wormhole should be different than the traversable one. However, this principle suggests that there is a trade-off between the resolution of black-hole singularities and the appearance of wormhole spacetimes due to higher curvature invariants. The wormhole solutions will remain in both the LI and H-L path integrals. The higher-order curvature scalars, generically present in the quantum gravity, stabilise the wormhole solutions without the need for an exotic matter.

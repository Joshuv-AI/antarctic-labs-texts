Fourier characterizations of Pilipović spaces
Joachim Toft, Anupam Gumber

Abstract
. Let f f be a function or distribution on 𝐑 d \mathbf{R}^{d} . We show that f f belongs to a certain Pilipović space, if and only if f f and suitable partial fractional Fourier transforms of f f satisfy certain types of estimates.

Introduction

In the paper we find characterizations of
Pilipović spaces in terms of estimates
of suitable fractional Fourier transforms
of the involved functions.

Pilipović spaces is a family of function
spaces
which contains any standard Fourier invariant
Gelfand-shilov space.
The Pilipović spaces
\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
for

s\geq 0
and
\mathcal{H}_{0,s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
for
s>0
,
contain all finite linear combinations of
Hermite functions and
are dense in the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(See
[
17
]
or Section
1
for notations.)
One has

\mathcal{H}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{0,s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\qquad 0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}.

The Pilipović spaces increase with the parameter

s
above and are strongly related to the
Gelfand-Shilov spaces
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and

\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

of Roumieu and Beurling types, respectively,
which consist of all
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}}\left(\frac{|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta}f(x)|}{h^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{s}}\right)<\infty

(0.1)

holds true for some
h>0
respectively
for every
h>0
.
In fact, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{H}_{0,s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle s_{2}

\displaystyle>s_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}

(0.2)

but

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\neq\mathcal{S}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\{0\},

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{H}_{0,s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\neq\Sigma_{s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\{0\},

\displaystyle\quad s_{1}

\displaystyle<s_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(0.3)

In particular, it follows that the functions
in Pilipović spaces obey strong
ultra-differentiability conditions and
strong exponential type decay bounds at infinity,
because similar facts hold true for Gelfand-Shilov
spaces.

There are several characterizations of
Gelfand-Shilov spaces. One of the most important
is described in the following proposition,
which characterize such spaces in terms of estimates
of the involved elements and their Fourier transforms,
established by Eijndhoven in
[
10
]

and Chung, Chung and Kim in
[
8
]

.

Let

and

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

Let
0<s\in\mathbf{R}
be fixed. Then the (Fourier invariant)
Gelfand-Shilov space
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) of
Roumieu type (Beurling type) consists of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\sigma}}\equiv\sup\frac{|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}f(x)|}{h^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\,\beta!)^{s}}

(1.1)

is finite for some
h>0
(for every
h>0
). Here the supremum should be taken
over all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
. The semi-norms

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\sigma}}
induce an inductive limit topology for the
space
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and projective limit topology for
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and
the latter space becomes a Fréchet space under this topology.

The space
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}
(
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}
), if and only if

s\geq\frac{1}{2}
(
s>\frac{1}{2}
).

The
Gelfand-Shilov distribution spaces

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
are the dual spaces of
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, respectively.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{t}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(1.2)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\quad\frac{1}{2}<s<t.

Here and
in what follows we use the notation
A\hookrightarrow B
when the topological
spaces
A
and
B
satisfy
A\subseteq B
with continuous embeddings.

A convenient family of functions concerns the Hermite functions

h_{\alpha}(x)=\pi^{-\frac{d}{4}}(-1)^{|\alpha|}(2^{|\alpha|}\alpha!)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}(\partial^{\alpha}e^{-|x|^{2}}),\quad\alpha\in\mathbf{N}^{d}.

The set of Hermite functions on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is an orthonormal basis for

L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. It is also a basis for the Schwartz space and its distribution space,
and for any
\Sigma_{s}
when
s>\frac{1}{2}
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}
when
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
and their distribution
spaces. They are also eigenfunctions to the Harmonic
oscillator
H=H_{d}\equiv|x|^{2}-\Delta
and to the Fourier transform

\mathscr{F}
, given by

(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

when
f\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Here
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the usual
scalar product on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. In fact, we have

H_{d}h_{\alpha}=(2|\alpha|+d)h_{\alpha}.

The Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}
extends
uniquely to homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Furthermore,

\mathscr{F}
restricts to
homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
and to a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Similar facts hold true
when the Fourier transform is replaced by a partial
Fourier transform.

Gelfand-Shilov spaces and their distribution spaces can also
be characterized by estimates of short-time Fourier
transform, (see e. g.
[
16
,
23
,
28
]
).
More precisely, let
\phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be
fixed.
Then the
short-time
Fourier transform

V_{\phi}f
of
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with respect to the
window function

\phi
is
the Gelfand-Shilov distribution on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
, defined by

V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)=\mathscr{F}(f\,\overline{\phi(\,\cdot\,-x)})(\xi),\quad x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

If
f,\phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, then it follows that

V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(y)\overline{\phi(y-x)}e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\,dy,\quad x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

By
[
27
, Theorem 2.3]
it follows that the definition of the map

(f,\phi)\mapsto V_{\phi}f
from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
is uniquely extendable to a continuous map from

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

to
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, and restricts to a continuous map
from
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

to
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
The same conclusion holds with
\Sigma_{s}
in place of

\mathcal{S}_{s}
, at each place.

Appendix A
Estimates of Phragmén-Lindelöf types

First we recall the following result of Phragmén-Lindelöf. For completeness
we give a proof.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\rho\in\mathbf{R}
be such that

\beta>\alpha,\quad\rho>0\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(\beta-\alpha)<\pi.

Also let
F
be analytic in

\Omega=\{\,z\in\mathbf{C}\,;\,\alpha<\operatorname{Arg}(z)<\beta\,\}

and continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
such that

\displaystyle|F(z)|

\displaystyle\leq M,

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{Arg}(z)

\displaystyle\in\{\alpha,\beta\}

and

\displaystyle|F(z)|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{r|z|^{\rho}},

\displaystyle\quad z

\displaystyle\in\Omega,

for some constants
C,M,r\geq 0
. Then

|F(z)|\leq M,\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}.

(A.1)

Proof.

By considering
F_{0}(z)=F(e^{it}z)
for suitable
t\in\mathbf{R}
, we reduce
ourself to the case when
\alpha=-\beta<0
. Let
\rho_{0}>\rho

be chosen such that

\rho_{0}\beta=\frac{\rho_{0}(\beta-\alpha)}{2}<\frac{\pi}{2},

and let
\theta=\cos(\rho_{0}\beta)>0
and

H_{\varepsilon}(z)=F(z)e^{-\varepsilon z^{\rho_{0}}},\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Then
|\operatorname{Arg}(z^{\rho_{0}})|<\frac{\pi}{2}
which gives

|H_{\varepsilon}(z)|=|F(z)||e^{-\varepsilon z^{\rho_{0}}}|\leq|F(z)||e^{-\varepsilon\theta|z|^{\rho_{0}}}|.

This gives

|H_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq Ce^{r|z|^{\rho}-\varepsilon\theta|z|^{\rho_{0}}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}\ \text{and}\ |z|\to\infty,

because
\rho_{0}>\rho
. Hence for some
R_{0}>0
we have

|H_{\varepsilon}(z)|<M\quad\text{as}\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}\ \text{and}\ |z|\geq R_{0}.

(A.2)

If
\Omega_{0}
is the set of all
z\in\Omega
such that
|z|\leq R_{0}
,
then
H_{\varepsilon}
is analytic in
\Omega_{0}
, continuous on

\overline{\Omega_{0}}
, and
|H_{\varepsilon}(z)|<M
on the boundary

\partial\Omega_{0}
of
\Omega_{0}
. By the maximum modulus
principle we obtain

|H_{\varepsilon}(z)|<M\quad\text{as}\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}\ \text{and}\ |z|\leq R_{0},

(A.3)

and a combination of (
A.2
) and (
A.3
)
shows that

|H_{\varepsilon}(z)|<M\quad\text{as}\quad z\in\overline{\Omega}.

By letting
\varepsilon
tending to
0
we finally
get (
A.1
).
∎

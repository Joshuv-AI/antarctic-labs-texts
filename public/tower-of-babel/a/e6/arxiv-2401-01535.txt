Formal manifolds: foundations
Fulin Chen, Binyong Sun, Chuyun Wang

Abstract
. This is the first paper in a series that studies smooth relative Lie algebra homologies and cohomologies based on the theory of formal manifolds and formal Lie groups. In this paper, we lay the foundations for this study by introducing the notion of formal manifolds in the context of differential geometry, inspired by the notion of formal schemes in algebraic geometry. We develop the basic theory for formal manifolds, and establish a fully faithful contravariant functor from the category of formal manifolds to the category of topological ℂ \mathbb{C} -algebras. We also prove the existence of finite products in the category of formal manifolds by studying vector-valued formal functions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
58A05, 58A12

1.
Introduction and the main results

In the setting of algebraic geometry,
formal schemes are intensively studied in the literature.
In this article, we study similar objects called formal manifolds in the setting of differential geometry, motivated by their applications to the representation theory of Lie groups.

1.1.
The motivation

The theory of smooth cohomologies and smooth homologies for Lie group representations are respectively introduced in
[
HM
]
and
[
BW
]
. On the other hand,
there is a vast literature for the
algebraic theory of relative Lie algebra (co)homologies, which is concerned with the (co)homologies of representations
of certain Lie pairs
(\mathfrak{q},L)
. Here
\mathfrak{q}
is a finite-dimensional complex Lie
algebra and
L
is a compact Lie group, with additional structures that satisfy specific compatibility conditions (see
[
KV
, (1.64)]
for more details). Motivated by the recent study in representation theory and automorphic forms, there is a growing interest in establishing a smooth
(co)homology theory for representations of
general Lie pairs
(\mathfrak{q},L)
, with
L
not necessarily compact. We refer to this theory as smooth relative Lie algebra (co)homology theory, which unifies the smooth (co)homology theory (as introduced in
[
HM
]
and
[
BW
]
) and the algebraic theory of relative Lie algebra (co)homologies.

We expect that the theory of smooth relative (co)homologies will be useful in the study of
representation theory of
real reductive groups.
For example, by generalizing the work of Wong (see
[
W1
,
W2
]
), the theory should yield interesting smooth representations of real reductive groups through
cohomological induction.
This is the analytic analog of Zuckerman’s functor that
produces interesting Harish-Chandra modules (see
[
KV
]
).
However, in Zuckerman’s construction, no topology is considered, whereas the topologies that we put on the representations should play an important role in some problems in representation theory.

Starting with this paper, we will develop a new theory of smooth relative (co)homologies in several papers.
In this paper, we introduce and study formal manifolds,
which are analogs of formal schemes in the setting of differential geometry.
The definition of formal manifolds naturally comes out of the two objects in Lie pairs.
Recall that the formal Lie theory theorem asserts an equivalence of categories between
the category of finite-dimensional complex Lie algebras and the category
of complex formal group laws (see
[
H
, (14.2.3)]
for example). Underlying a formal group law is a formal power series algebra.
On the other hand, a smooth manifold (and in particular a Lie group) is naturally a locally ringed space with the sheaf of

\mathbb{C}
-valued smooth functions as its structure sheaf.
Roughly speaking, formal manifolds are locally ringed spaces over
{\mathrm{Spec}}(\mathbb{C})
that unify the notions of formal power series algebras and smooth manifolds
(see Definition
1.4
for details).

This paper primarily focuses on the foundational framework of formal manifolds. In another paper of this series, we will study formal Lie groups, which are defined as group objects in the category of formal manifolds.
We will prove a generalization of the formal Lie theory theorem, which asserts an equivalence of categories between the category of formal Lie groups
and that of Lie pairs.
Using the language of formal manifolds and formal Lie groups, we will finally establish the smooth relative
(co)homology theory.

We will define formal manifolds and related notions in what follows, and provide an outline of the main results of this paper.

1.2.
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces

As mentioned before, we will work in the framework of locally ringed spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

A
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space is a topological space
M
, together with a sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
of commutative unital
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras on it such that all the stalks are local rings. Equivalently, a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space is a locally ringed space over the locally ringed space
{\mathrm{Spec}}({\mathbb{C}})
.

All
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces form a category as usual. The morphisms in this category are morphisms of locally ringed spaces over
{\mathrm{Spec}}({\mathbb{C}})
. More precisely, a morphism from a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
to another
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})
is a pair
\varphi=({\overline{\varphi}},\varphi^{*})
, where
{\overline{\varphi}}:M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
is a continuous map and

(1.1)

\varphi^{*}:{\overline{\varphi}}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}

is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra sheaf homomorphism that induces local homomorphisms on the stalks.
For open subsets
U^{\prime}
of
M^{\prime}
and
U
of
M
such that
\overline{\varphi}(U)\subset U^{\prime}
,
we write

\varphi^{*}_{U^{\prime},U}:{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(U^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(U)

for the homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras induced by (
1.1
).
If there is no confusion, we will denote
\varphi^{*}_{U^{\prime},U}
by
\varphi^{*}_{U^{\prime}}
or
\varphi^{*}
for simplicity.

For every
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
, define a subsheaf
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}
of
{\mathcal{O}}
by

(1.2)

\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}(U):=\{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(U)\,:\,f_{a}\in\mathfrak{m}_{a}\textrm{ for all $a\in U$}\},

where
U
is an open subset of
M
,
f_{a}
is the germ of
f
at
a
, and
\mathfrak{m}_{a}
is the maximal ideal of the stalk
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
.
Note that
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}
is an ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}
. Form the quotient sheaf

(1.3)

\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}:={\mathcal{O}}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}.

Then
(M,\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
is also a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space.

Definition 1.2
.

The
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
(M,\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
defined above is called the reduction of
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
.
The
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
is said to be reduced if
\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}={\mathcal{O}}
, or equivalently,
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}
is the zero sheaf.

By abuse of notation, we will often not distinguish a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
with its underlying topological space
M
, and call
{\mathcal{O}}
the structure sheaf of
M
.
We write
\underline{M}:=M
as a topological space, to be viewed as a reduced
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space with the structure sheaf
\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}
.

All reduced
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces form a full subcategory of the category of

{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces, and thus they also form a category.
By using (
1.3
), we have an obvious morphism

(1.4)

\underline{M}\rightarrow M

of
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces. For every morphism
\varphi:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
of
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces, there is a unique morphism

(1.5)

\underline{\varphi}:\underline{M_{1}}\rightarrow\underline{M_{2}}

such that the diagram

(1.6)

\begin{CD}M_{1}@>{\varphi}>{}>M_{2}\\
@A{}A{}A@A{}A{}A\\
\underline{M_{1}}@>{\underline{\varphi}}>{}>\underline{M_{2}}\\
\end{CD}

commutes. We call
\underline{\varphi}
the reduction of
\varphi
.
The reduction assignments
M\mapsto\underline{M}
and
\varphi\mapsto\underline{\varphi}
form a functor from the category of
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces to the category of reduced
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces.

For a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
M
with structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
, the formal stalk at a point
a\in M
is defined to be the local
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

(1.7)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}:=\varprojlim_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{k}\qquad({\mathbb{N}}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}).

1.3.
Formal manifolds

We consider a smooth manifold as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space with the sheaf of complex-valued smooth functions as the structure sheaf.

Throughout this paper, we assume that all smooth manifolds are Hausdorff and paracompact. They may or may not be equi-dimensional,
and may or may not have countably many connected components. We also view the empty set as a smooth manifold. For a smooth manifold
N
, we use
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N)
to denote the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra of complex-valued smooth functions on
N
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
N
be a smooth manifold. For every open subset
U\subset N
, write

{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}(U):={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]\quad(\textrm{the algebra of formal power series}).

With the obvious restriction maps,
(N,{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)})
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space. For simplicity, we also write
N^{(k)}
for this
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space.

The
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
N^{(k)}
defined as above provides an example of formal manifolds. In general, we make the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

A formal manifold is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
such that

•

the topological space
M
is paracompact and Hausdorff; and

•

for every
a\in M
, there is an open neighborhood
U
of
a
in
M
and
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{(k)}
as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed space.

Let
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
be a formal manifold.
For every
a\in M
, the uniquely determined natural numbers
n
and
k
in Definition
1.4
are respectively
called the dimension and the degree of
M
at
a
, denoted by
\dim_{a}M
and
\deg_{a}M
.
We say that
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
has equi-dimension
n
if
\dim_{a}M=n
for all
a\in M
,
and has equi-degree
k
if
\deg_{a}M=k
for all
a\in M
.
An element in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is called a formal function on
M
.

All formal manifolds form a full subcategory of the category of
{\mathbb{C}}
-locally ringed spaces, and thus they also form a category.

Example 1.5
.

A smooth manifold is naturally a formal manifold of equi-degree
0
.
On the other hand, the reduction of a formal manifold is naturally a smooth manifold.
Moreover, for every formal manifold, the following conditions are equivalent to each other:

•

it has equi-degree
0
;

•

it is reduced in the sense of Definition
1.2
;

•

it is a smooth manifold.

Definition 1.6
.

A formal manifold is said to be infinitesimal if its underlying topological space has precisely one element.

Example 1.7
.

A formal power series algebra
A:=\mathbb{C}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]
(
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
) induces naturally an infinitesimal formal manifold
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
such that
{\mathcal{O}}(M)=A
as
\mathbb{C}
-algebras.
Conversely, up to isomorphism, every infinitesimal formal manifold has such a form.

1.4.
Structure of the paper

Here we give an outline of the main results in this paper.
Let
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
be a formal manifold.
In Section

, we establish some basic properties of
(M,{\mathcal{O}})

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
(

In Section

{\mathcal{O}}

When

In Section

With the smooth topology,

-algebra. In other words, the addition map, the multiplication map, and the scalar multiplication map, are all jointly continuous.
As a topological vector space, it is a product of nuclear Fréchet spaces.
Specifically, the formal stalk

We prove the following theorem in Section

.

For every morphism

\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})

of formal manifolds, the homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(M)

between topological

-algebras is continuous. Moreover,

(1.8)

(M,{\mathcal{O}})\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}(M),\quad(\varphi:(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}))\mapsto(\varphi^{*}:{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(M))

is a fully faithful contravariant functor from the category of formal manifolds to the category of topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras.

Throughout this paper, by an LCS, we mean a locally convex topological vector space over
{\mathbb{C}}
which may or may not be Hausdorff. For two LCS

and

and

We know that finite products exist in the category of smooth manifolds. In Section

Finite products exist in the category of formal manifolds. Moreover, for all formal manifolds

Preliminaries on formal manifolds

2.2.
Partition of unity

In this subsection, we prove that partitions of unity exist for formal manifolds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be a formal function on
M
. Then the following conditions are
equivalent to each other:

•

f
is invertible;

•

the reduction
\underline{f}
of
f
is invertible;

•

the value of
f
at
a
is nonzero for every
a\in M
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Example
2.1
and the sheaf property of
{\mathcal{O}}
and
\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be an open cover
of
M
. A family
\{f_{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
of formal functions is said to be a
partition of unity on
M
subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
if the following conditions hold:

•

\mathrm{supp}\,f_{\gamma}
is contained in
U_{\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
;

•

the family
\{\mathrm{supp}\,f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is locally finite; and

•

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f_{\gamma}=1
, that is, for every relatively compact open subset
U
of
M
,

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma;(\mathrm{supp}\,f_{\gamma})\cap U\neq\emptyset}(f_{\gamma})|_{U}=1.

Here and henceforth, “
\mathrm{supp}
” indicates the support of a section of a sheaf.

When no confusion is possible,
we write
\overline{U}
for the closure of a subset
U
in a topological space.
Similar to the smooth manifold case, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every open cover
of
M
, there is a partition of unity on
M
subordinate to it.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of the smooth manifold case (see
[
B1
, Theorem 10.1]
for example). We sketch a proof for completeness.
Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be an open cover
of
M
. Replacing
\mathcal{U}
by a refinement of it if necessary, we assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{U}
is locally finite and

U_{\gamma}
is relatively compact for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Take an open cover
\{V_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
of
M
such that the closure
\overline{V_{\gamma}}
of

is contained in

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Then for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, there is a formal function

such that

•

the support of
f^{\prime}_{\gamma}
is contained in

; and

•

is a non-negative real number for all

It is obvious that

is a well-defined formal function on

for all
a\in M
.
Lemma

implies that the formal function

The lemma then follows as

, where
f_{\gamma}:=f_{\gamma}^{\prime}\cdot f_{\Gamma}^{-1}

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
M=(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{(k)}

for every
a\in M

, the set

(2.4)

\{((x-a)^{I}y^{J})_{a}+\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}\,:\,I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}\ \text{with}\ |I|+|J|<r\}

forms a basis of
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}
, where for
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{n})
and
J=(j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{k})
,

(x-a)^{I}y^{J}:=(x_{1}-x_{1}(a))^{i_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{2}(a))^{i_{2}}\cdots(x_{n}-x_{n}(a))^{i_{n}}y_{1}^{j_{1}}y_{2}^{j_{2}}\cdots y_{k}^{j_{k}}.

Proof.

Let
U
be an open neighborhood of
a
in
M
, and let

f=\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}f_{J}y^{J}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]].

For every
J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}
with
|J|<r
, write

f_{J}=\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n};|I|<r-|J|}b_{I,J}\cdot(x-a)^{I}+\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n};|I|=r-|J|}g_{I,J}\cdot(x-a)^{I}

for the
(r-|J|-1)
-th order Taylor expansion of
f_{J}
around the point
a
, where
b_{I,J}\in\mathbb{C}
and
g_{I,J}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)
.
It is straightforward to check that

f_{a}=\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k};|I|+|J|<r}b_{I,J}\cdot((x-a)^{I}y^{J})_{a}\mod\ \mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}.

This implies that the set (
2.4
) spans
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}
.
On the other hand, by applying the
operators

\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{y}^{J}\quad(I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}\ \text{with}\ |I|+|J|<r)

to the elements in (
2.4
), we see that these elements are linearly independent.
This completes the proof.
∎

For every
a\in M
and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, Lemma
2.5
implies that the quotient space
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}
is finite-dimensional. We equip the space
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}
with the Euclidean topology, which is the unique topology that makes it a Hausdorff LCS.
Now, we endow the formal stalk

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{a}=\varprojlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}

with the projective limit topology. Then it becomes a topological

\mathbb{C}
-algebra.
On the other hand, we view
\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]
(
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
) as a topological

\mathbb{C}
-algebra with the usual term-wise convergence topology.

Proposition 2.6
.

For every
a\in M
, we have that

(2.5)

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{a}\cong\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]

as topological
\mathbb{C}
-algebras, where
n:=\dim_{a}M
and
k:=\deg_{a}M
. Furthermore, the natural map

(2.6)

{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\cap_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{a}

is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
M=(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{(k)}
and
a=0\in M
. Taylor expansion around the point
a
yields the following
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism

(2.7)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{O}}_{a}&\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}]]:=\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]],\\
f_{a}&\mapsto\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}\left(\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}}\frac{1}{I!}\partial_{x}^{I}(f_{J})(a)\cdot x^{I}\right)y^{J}\end{split}

where
f=\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}f_{J}y^{J}
is a formal function on an open neighborhood of
a

and

\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}}\frac{1}{I!}\partial_{x}^{I}(f_{J})(a)\cdot x^{I}

is the Taylor series of
f_{J}
at
a
.
Note that Borel’s lemma implies that the map (
2.7
) is surjective.

Write
\mathfrak{m}
for the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{C}[[{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}]]
. By Lemma
2.5
, we know that the algebra homomorphism

{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[[{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}]]/\mathfrak{m}^{r}\quad(r\in{\mathbb{N}})

induced by (
2.7
) is an isomorphism.
This produces a topological algebra isomorphism

(2.8)

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{a}=\varprojlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[[{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}]]=\varprojlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{C}[[{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}]]/\mathfrak{m}^{r},

which proves the first assertion in this proposition.
For the second one, one only needs to note that
the surjective map (
2.7
) is the composition of the maps

{\mathcal{O}}_{a}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}\xrightarrow{\eqref{eq:formalstalkisoring}}\mathbb{C}[[{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}]].

∎

We also record the following straightforward result as a lemma for later use.

Lemma 2.7
.

The canonical map

(2.9)

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow\prod_{a\in M}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}

is injective.

Let
\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})
be a morphism of formal manifolds.
For every
a\in M
, we let

\varphi^{*}_{a}:\ {\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{\overline{\varphi}(a)}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{a}

denote the induced local homomorphism of the stalks.
Then there is a unique local homomorphism

(2.10)

\varphi^{*}_{a}:\ \widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{\overline{\varphi}(a)}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}

between the formal stalks such that the diagram

(2.11)

\begin{CD}{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{\overline{\varphi}(a)}@>{\varphi^{*}_{a}}>{}>{\mathcal{O}}_{a}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{\overline{\varphi}(a)}@>{\varphi^{*}_{a}}>{}>\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}\\
\end{CD}

commutes. Furthermore,
we have the following result.

Lemma 2.8
.

Under the projective limit topologies, the map (
2.10
) is continuous.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that every linear map between finite-dimensional Hausdorff LCS is automatically continuous.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}),\,\psi=(\overline{\psi},\psi^{*}):\ (M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})

be two morphisms of formal manifolds. If
\overline{\varphi}=\overline{\psi}
, and for all
a\in M
,
the maps

\varphi^{*}_{a},\,\psi^{*}_{a}:\ \widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{b}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}\qquad(b:=\overline{\varphi}(a)=\overline{\psi}(a))

are equal, then
\varphi=\psi
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.7
.

∎

For every sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules and
a\in M
, we define the formal stalk of
{\mathcal{F}}
at
a
by

\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{a}:=\varprojlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{r}{\mathcal{F}}_{a}.

Example 2.10
.

Suppose that
N
is a smooth manifold and
k,l\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then there is a homomorphism
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(l)}
of sheaves of algebras given by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}(U)&\rightarrow&{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(l)}(U),\\
\sum\limits_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}f_{J}y^{J}&\mapsto&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum\limits_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}={\mathbb{N}}^{k}\times\{0\}^{l-k}\subset{\mathbb{N}}^{l}}f_{J}y^{J},&\textrm{if $k\leq l$;}\\
\sum\limits_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{l}={\mathbb{N}}^{l}\times\{0\}^{k-l}\subset{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}f_{J}y^{J},&\textrm{if $k>l$},\end{array}\right.\end{array}

where
U
is an open subset of
N
and
f_{J}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)
. By using this homomorphism, every sheaf of

{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(l)}
-modules is also a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}
-modules.
Suppose that
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(l)}
, to be viewed as a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}
-modules as above. Then,
similar to (
2.5
), for every
a\in N
, we have that

(2.12)

\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{a}\cong{\mathbb{C}}[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{l}]],\quad\textrm{where }\ n:=\dim_{a}N.

2.4.
Softness

We discuss here the softness of structure sheaves of formal manifolds. Recall that a sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
of abelian groups on a topological space
X
is said to be soft if
for every closed subset
Z
of
X
, the canonical map
{\mathcal{F}}(X)\rightarrow({\mathcal{F}}|_{Z})(Z)
is surjective.

Proposition 2.11
.

The sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
is soft.

Proof.

By
[
B2
, Chapter II, Lemma 9.14]
, it suffices to show that every
a\in M
admits
an open neighborhood
U
such that the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}|_{U}
is soft.
Thus it is enough to show that the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(k)}
(
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
) is soft.
We know that the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(0)}
of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is soft (see
[
B2
, Chapter II, Example 9.4]
).
Recall from Example
2.10
that
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(k)}
is a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(0)}
-modules.
Thus
[
B2
, Chapter II, Theorem 9.16]
implies that the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(k)}
is soft,
as required.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

For every
a\in M
, the canonical maps

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{a}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{a}

are both surjective.

Proof.

This follows from Propositions
2.11
and
2.6
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

All sheaves of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules are soft.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
2.11
and
[
B2
, Chapter II, Theorem 9.16]
.
∎

Recall the reduction
\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}
of
{\mathcal{O}}
from (
1.3
).

Proposition 2.14
.

The canonical map

(2.13)

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}(M)

is surjective.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}\rightarrow 0.

By Corollary
2.13
, the sheaf
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}
is soft. Then the proposition follows from
[
B2
, Chapter II, Theorem 9.9]
.

∎

3.
Differential operators on formal manifolds

In this section, we study differential operators between two sheaves of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules.

3.1.
Differential operators of modules

We first review the notion of differential operators of modules of a commutative unital
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra.

Let
A
be a commutative unital
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra, and let
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
be two
A
-modules.
For
D\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(F_{1},F_{2})
and
a\in A
, define an element
[D,a]\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(F_{1},F_{2})
by

[D,a](u)=D(au)-aD(u)\quad\textrm{ for all }u\in F_{1}.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
throughout this section.

Definition 3.1
.

A map
D\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(F_{1},F_{2})
is called a differential
operator of order
\leq r
if

[\cdots[[D,a_{0}],a_{1}],\cdots,a_{r}]=0\ \ \textrm{ for all $a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in A$}.

Write
\mathrm{Diff}_{r}(F_{1},F_{2})
for the space of all differential operators of order
\leq r
in
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(F_{1},F_{2})
.
Note that
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(F_{1},F_{2})={\mathrm{Hom}}_{A}(F_{1},F_{2})
, and

\mathrm{Diff}_{r_{1}}(F_{2},F_{3})\circ\mathrm{Diff}_{r_{2}}(F_{1},F_{2})\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(F_{1},F_{3})\quad\textrm{for all }r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
F_{3}
is another
A
-module.

Definition 3.2
.

An element
D\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A,F_{2})
is called a derivation if

D(a_{1}a_{2})=a_{2}D(a_{1})+a_{1}D(a_{2})\ \ \textrm{ for all $a_{1},a_{2}\in A$}.

Write
\mathrm{Der}(A,F_{2})
for the space of all derivations in
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A,F_{2})
. Obviously,
\mathrm{Der}(A,F_{2})\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{1}(A,F_{2})
.
We identify
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(A,F_{2})={\mathrm{Hom}}_{A}(A,F_{2})
with
F_{2}
via the isomorphism

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{A}(A,F_{2})\rightarrow F_{2},\quad D\mapsto D(1).

One checks that
D-D(1)
is a derivation for every
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{1}(A,F_{2})
, which implies

(3.1)

\mathrm{Diff}_{1}(A,F_{2})=\mathrm{Der}(A,F_{2})\oplus F_{2}.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{r}(F_{1},F_{2})
and every
ideal
I
of
A
, we have that

(3.2)

D(I^{i+r}F_{1})\subset I^{i}F_{2}\qquad\textrm{for all }i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Proof.

We will prove the lemma by induction on
i+r
. The lemma is obvious if
i+r=0
.
Now assume that
i+r\geq 1
and the lemma holds when
i+r
is smaller.
If
i=0
or
r=0
, then (
3.2
) clearly holds. So we assume that
i>0
and
r>0
. Let
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{i+r}\in I
.
From the fact
[D,f_{1}]\in\mathrm{Diff}_{r-1}(F_{1},F_{2})
, it follows that

\displaystyle D(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{i+r}F_{1})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle D(f_{1}I^{i+r-1}F_{1})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle[D,f_{1}](I^{i+r-1}F_{1})+f_{1}D(I^{i+r-1}F_{1})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle I^{i}F_{2}+f_{1}I^{i-1}F_{2}=I^{i}F_{2}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Assume now that
A={\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
For every
J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}
with
|J|\leq r
, we have that

F_{2}\circ\partial_{y}^{J}\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{r}(A,F_{2}),

where the identification
F_{2}=\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(A,F_{2})
is used. Conversely, we have the following result (
cf
.
[
Bj
, Theorem 1.1.8]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
A={\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
F_{2}
is an
A
-module such that

\cap_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathfrak{m}^{i}F_{2}=0
, where
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the maximal ideal in
A
.
Then

\mathrm{Diff}_{r}(A,F_{2})=\bigoplus_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k};\lvert J\rvert\leq r}F_{2}\circ\partial_{y}^{J}.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{y}]_{\leq r}\subset\mathbb{C}[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]
the subspace of polynomials of degree
\leq r
in
A
. We claim that

(3.3)

\text{the restriction map}\ \Psi_{r}:\ \mathrm{Diff}_{r}(A,F_{2})\rightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{y}]_{\leq r},F_{2})\ \text{is injective}.

Note that the lemma is implied by this claim, as

\Psi_{r}\left(\bigoplus_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k};\lvert J\rvert\leq r}F_{2}\circ\partial_{y}^{J}\right)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{y}]_{\leq r},F_{2}).

We now prove the claim (
3.3
) by induction on
r
.
The claim obviously holds when
r=0
, and so we assume that
r>0
.
Pick an element
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{r}(A,F_{2})
such that
D(f)=0
for all
f\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{y}]_{\leq r}
.
Then for every
i=1,2,\dots,k
, we have that

[D,y_{i}]\in\mathrm{Diff}_{r-1}(A,F_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad[D,y_{i}](g)=0\ \text{for all}\ g\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{y}]_{\leq r-1}.

These together with the induction hypothesis imply that
[D,y_{i}]=0
on
A
for all
i
, and then one checks that

(3.4)

D(h)=0\quad\text{for all}\ h\in{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}].

Let
f=\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}c_{J}y^{J}
be an arbitrary formal power series in
A
.
By (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.3
, for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have that

D(f)=D(f)-D\left(\sum_{|J|<j+r}c_{J}y^{J}\right)=D\left(\sum_{|J|\geq j+r}c_{J}y^{J}\right)\in D(\mathfrak{m}^{j+r})\subset\mathfrak{m}^{j}F_{2}.

Thus, the condition
\cap_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathfrak{m}^{j}F_{2}=0
in the lemma forces that
D(f)=0
.
This proves (
3.3
) and hence the lemma.
∎

3.2.
Differential operators on formal manifolds

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
be two sheaves of
\mathcal{O}
-modules. In this subsection, we introduce the
notion of differential operators between
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
.

Recall that
\mathbb{C}_{M}
denotes the sheaf of locally constant
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued functions on
M
.
Write

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
for the space of all
{\mathbb{C}}_{M}
-homomorphisms between
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
.
Then the assignment

U\mapsto{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{U}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1}|_{U},{\mathcal{F}}_{2}|_{U})\quad(\text{$U$ is an open subset of $M$})

forms a sheaf of
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector spaces over
M
, to be denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}\textrm{om}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
.

Recall that an element
D
of
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
is defined to be a family

\{(D_{U}:{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(U)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U))\}_{U\textrm{ is an open subset of }M}

of linear maps that commute with the restriction maps.

Definition 3.5
.

An element
D\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
is called a differential operator of order
\leq r
if

D_{U}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{r}({\mathcal{F}}_{1}(U),{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U))

for all open subsets
U
of
M
, where
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(U)
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U)
are viewed as
{\mathcal{O}}(U)
-modules, and
{\mathcal{O}}(U)
is viewed as a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

Write
\mathrm{Diff}_{r}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})\subset{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
for the space of differential operators of order
\leq r
.
Then we have that

\mathrm{Diff}_{0}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})=\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2}),

and

\mathrm{Diff}_{r_{1}}({\mathcal{F}}_{2},{\mathcal{F}}_{3})\circ\mathrm{Diff}_{r_{2}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{r_{1}+r_{2}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})\qquad\textrm{for all }\,r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
is another sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules.

Definition 3.6
.

An element
D\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
is called a finite order differential operator if it belongs to
\mathrm{Diff}_{r}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
for some
r\geq 0
.
It is called a differential operator if for every
a\in M
, there is an open neighborhood
U
of
a
in
M
such that
D|_{U}\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{U}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1}|_{U},{\mathcal{F}}_{2}|_{U})
is a finite order differential operator.

Respectively write
\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{fin}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
and
\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
for the space of finite order differential operators and the space of differential operators in
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
.

Definition 3.7
.

An element
D\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
is called a derivation if

(D_{U}:{\mathcal{O}}(U)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U))\in\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{O}}(U),{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U))

for every open subset
U
of
M
.

The space of all derivations in
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
is denoted by
\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
.

Write
\mathcal{D}\mathrm{iff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
for the subsheaf of
{\mathcal{H}}\textrm{om}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
given by

U\mapsto\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1}|_{U},{\mathcal{F}}_{2}|_{U})\quad(U\ \text{is an open subset of}\ M).

We also define the subpresheaf
\mathcal{D}\mathrm{iff}_{\mathrm{fin}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
of
{\mathcal{H}}\textrm{om}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
,
and the subsheaf
\mathcal{D}\textrm{er}({\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
of
{\mathcal{H}}\textrm{om}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{M}}({\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
similarly.

For every
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
and
D\in\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
, we define two differential operators
f\circ D
and
D\circ f
in
\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
by setting

(3.5)

(f\circ D)_{U}:\ u\mapsto f|_{U}(D_{U}(u))\quad\text{and}\quad(D\circ f)_{U}:\ u\mapsto D_{U}(f|_{U}u),

where
u\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(U)
and
U
is an open subset of
M
.
Then we have two natural
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
-module structures on
\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
with

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(M)\times\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2}),\quad(f,D)\mapsto f\circ D,

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(M)\times\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Diff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2}),\quad(f,D)\mapsto D\circ f.

Each of the two actions mentioned above makes

\mathcal{D}\mathrm{iff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules.
Unless otherwise mentioned,
\mathcal{D}\mathrm{iff}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
is
viewed as a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules with the action (
3.6
).
Under this action,

Proposition 3.8
.

The linear map (

. In particular, the canonical map

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{O}}(M)}({\mathcal{F}}_{1}(M),{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(M))

Proof.

Let
U
be an open subset of
M
.
We choose a family

of triples, where

•

U_{a}

are open neighborhoods of

\overline{U_{a}}\subset U^{\prime}_{a}
and
\overline{U^{\prime}_{a}}\subset{U}

•

f^{a}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is a formal function such that

and
f^{a}|_{M\setminus U^{\prime}_{a}}=0

We first prove the injectivity of (
3.12

Let

and
u\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(U)

Since
D
commutes with the restriction maps in
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and

D_{U}(u)|_{U_{a}}=D_{U_{a}}(u|_{U_{a}})=D_{U_{a}}((f^{a}u)|_{U_{a}})=D_{M}(f^{a}u)|_{U_{a}}.

This implies that
D=0
provided that
D_{M}=0
, as desired.

Now we turn to prove that the map (
3.12
) is surjective. Fix a differential operator

P\in\textrm{Diff}_{r}({\mathcal{F}}_{1}(M),{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(M))
.
We claim that

(3.13)

for every
u^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(M)
satisfying
u^{\prime}|_{U}=0
, one has that
P(u^{\prime})|_{U}=0
.

Indeed, let

be a formal function such that

Then we have that
gu^{\prime}=u^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(M)
.
Recall that
[P,g]=0
if
r=0
, and
[P,g]\in\mathrm{Diff}_{r-1}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
otherwise. Since

P(u^{\prime})|_{U_{a}}=P(gu^{\prime})|_{U_{a}}=gP(u^{\prime})|_{U_{a}}+[P,g](u^{\prime})|_{U_{a}}=[P,g](u^{\prime})|_{U_{a}},

it follows that

by induction on

.
This proves the claim (
3.13
) as
\{U_{a}\}_{a\in U}
forms an open cover of
U
.

Let
u\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(U)
. Define

(3.14)

\displaystyle P_{U_{a}}(u|_{U_{a}}):=P(f^{a}u)|_{U_{a}}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U_{a}).

For all

(f^{a}u)|_{U_{a}\cap U_{b}}=u|_{U_{a}\cap U_{b}}=(f^{b}u)|_{U_{a}\cap U_{b}}.

Thus, by applying
the claim (
3.13
) (replace
U
by

(3.15)

P_{U_{a}}(u|_{U_{a}})|_{U_{a}\cap U_{b}}=P(f^{a}u)|_{U_{a}\cap U_{b}}=P(f^{b}u)|_{U_{a}\cap U_{b}}=P_{U_{b}}(u|_{U_{b}})|_{U_{a}\cap U_{b}}.

This yields a map

P_{U}:{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(U)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U),\quad u\mapsto P_{U}(u),

where

is the section in
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(U)
obtained by gluing the family

We remark that the definition of

Indeed, let

associated to this

as desired.

and

Let

(3.16)

\displaystyle[\cdots[[P_{U},h_{1}],h_{2}],\cdots,h_{i}](u)|_{U_{a}}=[\cdots[[P,f^{a}h_{1}],f^{a}h_{2}],\cdots,f^{a}h_{i}](f^{a}u)|_{U_{a}}

for all

and

.
When

3.16
) follows from the definition of

. Assume that

and (
3.16
) holds when

is smaller.
Set

P_{U}^{\prime}:=[\cdots[[P_{U},h_{1}],h_{2}],\cdots,h_{i-1}]\ \text{and}\ P^{\prime}:=[\cdots[[P,f^{a}h_{1}],f^{a}h_{2}],\cdots,f^{a}h_{i-1}].

Then we have that

\displaystyle[\cdots[[P_{U},h_{1}],h_{2}],\cdots,h_{i}](u)|_{U_{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{U}^{\prime}(h_{i}u)|_{U_{a}}-(h_{i}P_{U}^{\prime}(u))|_{U_{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(f^{a}(h_{i}u))|_{U_{a}}-(h_{i}|_{U_{a}})(P^{\prime}(f^{a}u)|_{U_{a}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P^{\prime}(f^{a}h_{i}f^{a}u))|_{U_{a}}-((f^{a}h_{i})P^{\prime}(f^{a}u))|_{U_{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([P^{\prime},f^{a}h_{i}](f^{a}u))|_{U_{a}},

where we have used the induction hypothesis in the second equality. This finishes the proof of (

Since

it follows from the equality (

) (with

Then
D\in\textrm{Diff}_{r}({\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2})

and hence the map (

In conclusion, we have proved the first assertion of the proposition. The second assertion follows by taking

4.
The topology on the space of formal functions

In this section, we introduce a canonical topology on the space of formal functions on
M
.

4.1.
Smooth topology on
{\mathcal{F}}(M)

Recall the reduction
\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}
of
\mathcal{O}
defined in (
1.3
), to be viewed as a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules by using the reduction map.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules and let
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
. For every
u\in{\mathcal{F}}(M)
, define

\lvert u\rvert_{D}:=\sup_{a\in M}\lvert(D(u))(a)\rvert.

Then
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{D}
is a seminorm on
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
.

Definition 4.1
.

The locally convex topology on
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
defined by the family

\{\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{D}\}_{D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})}

of
seminorms is called the smooth topology of
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
.

Unless otherwise mentioned,
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is equipped with the smooth topology.

Example 4.2
.

Assume that
M=N^{(k)}
, where
N
is a smooth manifold and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then it follows from Example
3.11
that the smooth topology on
{\mathcal{O}}(M)={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]

coincides with the term-wise convergence topology, where
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)
is equipped with the usual smooth topology.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
,
the linear map

{\mathcal{F}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}(M),\quad u\mapsto{fu}

is continuous.

Proof.

For every
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
, we have that

\lvert fu\rvert_{D}=\lvert u\rvert_{D\circ f}

for all
u\in{\mathcal{F}}(M)
, where
D\circ f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
is as in (
3.5
). This implies the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The smooth topology on
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is Hausdorff if and only if for every
0\neq u\in{\mathcal{F}}(M)
, there exists some
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})

such that
D(u)\neq 0
.
Furthermore, if
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is Hausdorff, then so is
{\mathcal{F}}(U)
for every open subset
U
of
M
.

Proof.

The first assertion is obvious. For the second one, let
0\neq u\in{\mathcal{F}}(U)
.
Take open subsets
V
and
W
of
U
such that

V\subset\overline{V}\subset W\subset\overline{W}\subset U\quad\text{and}\quad u|_{V}\neq 0.

And take a formal function
f
on
M

such that
\mathrm{supp}\,f\subset W
and
f|_{\overline{V}}=1
.
Since

(fu)|_{V}=u|_{V}\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{supp}(fu)\subset W,

together with the condition that
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is Hausdorff, there is a differential operator
D^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
such that

D^{\prime}(fu)|_{W}\neq 0
. Set

D:=(D^{\prime}\circ f)|_{U}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}}|_{U},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}|_{U}).

Then
D(u)\neq 0
as

D(u)|_{W}=((D^{\prime}\circ f)|_{U}(u))|_{W}=D^{\prime}(fu)|_{W}\neq 0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
M
. Then the restriction map

(4.1)

{\mathcal{F}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}(U)

is continuous.

Proof.

For every
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}}|_{U},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}|_{U})
, we have that

\lvert u\rvert_{\mathrm{ext}_{M,U}(D)}=\left|(u|_{U})\right|_{D}\quad\textrm{for all $u\in{\mathcal{F}}(M)$}.

This implies that the restriction map (
4.1
) is continuous.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be an open cover of

. Then the linear map

(4.2)

{\mathcal{F}}(M)
is Hausdorff,
then the image of (

Proof.

The sheaf property of
{\mathcal{F}}
implies that the map (
4.2
) is injective.
Lemma
4.5
implies that the map (
4.2
) is continuous.
Let
D\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{F}},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})
. Choose
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{s}\in\Gamma
(

Let

be a partition of unity on

\{V:=M\setminus\mathrm{supp}\,D,U_{\gamma_{1}},U_{\gamma_{2}},\dots,U_{\gamma_{s}}\}

Note that for each

Proposition 4.8

-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is a topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra. As a topological vector space, it is a product of nuclear Fréchet spaces. Especially, the LCS
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is complete and nuclear.

4.2.
Morphisms to
\mathbb{R}^{m}

Define an
\mathbb{R}
-subalgebra

{\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R}):=\{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)\,:\,f(a)\in\mathbb{R}\textrm{ for all }a\in M\}

of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
.
Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let

\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(0)})

be a morphism of formal manifolds. Recall that
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(0)}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
are standard coordinate functions of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Then
\varphi
yields an
m
-tuple

c_{\varphi}:=(\varphi^{*}(x_{1}),\varphi^{*}(x_{2}),\dots,\varphi^{*}(x_{m}))\in({\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R}))^{m}.

The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.9
.

The map

(4.4)

\{\text{morphism from $(M,{\mathcal{O}})$ to $(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(0)})$}\}\rightarrow({\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R}))^{m},\quad\varphi\mapsto c_{\varphi}

is bijective.

We will prove the theorem through a sequence of lemmas.
Firstly, the continuous map
\overline{\varphi}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is determined by
c_{\varphi}
by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
a\in M
. Then

\overline{\varphi}(a)=((\varphi^{*}(x_{1}))(a),(\varphi^{*}(x_{2}))(a),\dots,(\varphi^{*}(x_{m}))(a)).

Proof.

Write
b:=\overline{\varphi}(a)
. For every
i=1,2,\dots,m
, we have that

(\varphi^{*}(x_{i}))(a)=x_{i}(b).

This implies that
b=((\varphi^{*}(x_{1}))(a),(\varphi^{*}(x_{2}))(a),\dots,(\varphi^{*}(x_{m}))(a))
.

∎

Lemma 4.11
.

The map (
4.4
) is injective.

Proof.

For simplicity, let us set
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(0)}
.
Let

\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}),\psi=(\overline{\psi},\psi^{*}):(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})

be two morphisms such that
c_{\varphi}=c_{\psi}
.
From Lemma
4.10
, it follows that
\overline{\varphi}=\overline{\psi}
.
Let
a\in M
and write
b:=\overline{\varphi}(a)=\overline{\psi}(a)
. Then the maps

\varphi^{*}_{a},\psi^{*}_{a}:\ \widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{b}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}

are equal, since they are both continuous (see Lemma
2.8
) and agree on the dense subalgebra generated by the images of the coordinate functions
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}
under the canonical map

{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{b}.

Then the lemma follows from Lemma
2.9
.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

If
M=N^{(k)}
with
N
an open submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then the map
(
4.4
) is surjective.

Proof.

Let
(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})
be an
m
-tuple in
({\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R}))^{m}
.
It yields a smooth map

\overline{\varphi}:N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m},\quad a\mapsto(f_{1}(a),f_{2}(a),\dots,f_{m}(a)).

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and set
V:=\overline{\varphi}^{-1}(U)\subset N
.
For every smooth function
g\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)
, define a formal function

(4.5)

\varphi_{U}^{*}(g):=\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}}\left(\frac{\partial_{x}^{I}(g)}{I!}\circ\overline{\varphi}\right)\cdot f^{I}|_{V}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(V)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]],

where

f^{I}:=(f_{1}-\underline{f_{1}})^{i_{1}}(f_{2}-\underline{f_{2}})^{i_{2}}\cdots(f_{m}-\underline{f_{m}})^{i_{m}}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]],

for
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
.
Here
\underline{f_{j}}\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\subset{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]
(
j=1,2,\dots,m
) is the reduction of
f_{j}
as defined in (
2.2
).

By using Leibniz’s rule, one easily checks that the map

\varphi^{*}_{U}:{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(V)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]],\quad g\mapsto\varphi^{*}_{U}(g)

is an algebra homomorphism. Then we obtain a morphism

\varphi:=(\overline{\varphi},\{\varphi^{*}_{U}\}_{U\textrm{ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^{m}$}}):(N,{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(0)})

of formal manifolds. Note that for every
i=1,2,\dots,m
,

\varphi^{*}(x_{i})=x_{i}\circ\overline{\varphi}+(f_{i}-\underline{f_{i}})=f_{i}.

This implies that
c_{\varphi}=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})
, and the proof is finished.

∎

By Lemma
4.11
, we only need to show the following lemma to finish the proof of Theorem
4.9
.

Lemma 4.13
.

The map
(
4.4
) is surjective.

Proof.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R})
, and take an atlas
\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
of
M
.
Then for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, it follows from Lemma
4.12
that there is a morphism

\varphi_{\gamma}:(U_{\gamma},{\mathcal{O}}|_{U_{\gamma}})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(0)})

such that

c_{\varphi_{\gamma}}=((f_{1})|_{U_{\gamma}},(f_{2})|_{U_{\gamma}},\dots,(f_{m})|_{U_{\gamma}}).

Moreover, Lemma
4.11
implies that
\varphi_{\gamma}
and
\varphi_{\gamma^{\prime}}

agree on
(U_{\gamma}\cap U_{\gamma^{\prime}},{\mathcal{O}}|_{U_{\gamma}\cap U_{\gamma^{\prime}}})
for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
.
Hence the family
\{\varphi_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
patches together to
a morphism

\varphi:(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(0)}).

It is clear that
c_{\varphi}=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})
. This proves the lemma.

∎

4.3.
Automatic continuity

In this subsection, we prove that for every morphism of formal manifolds, the induced homomorphism on the topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras of formal functions
is continuous.
We start with the following special case.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
n,k,m,l\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
For every morphism

\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(k)})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(l)})
, the homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{l}]]\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]

is continuous.

Proof.

When
l=0
, this follows from Theorem
4.9
and the explicit expression of
\varphi^{*}
given in (
4.5
). In general, we have an obvious morphism

\psi=(\overline{\psi},\psi^{*}):(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(l)})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{m+l},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m+l}}^{(0)})

so that the map

(4.6)

\psi^{*}:{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m+l})\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{l}]]

is given by the Taylor series expansion along
\mathbb{R}^{l}
.
It follows from Borel’s lemma that the map
\psi^{*}
is surjective.
Thus by the open mapping theorem, it is also open.

Consider the maps

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m+l})\xrightarrow{\psi^{*}}{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{l}]]\xrightarrow{\varphi^{*}}{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]].

We have shown that the composition of these two arrows is continuous, and the first arrow is open and surjective. Thus the second arrow is also continuous.

∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})
be a morphism of formal manifolds. Then the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(M)

is continuous.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
, we only need to show that for every
a\in M
, there is an open neighborhood
U
of it in
M
such that the homomorphism

(4.7)

\varphi^{*}_{M^{\prime},U}:{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(U)

is continuous.
Choose an open neighborhood
U
of
a
in
M
, and an open neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
\overline{\varphi}(a)
in
M^{\prime}
such that

•

(U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})\cong(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(k)})
(
n:=\dim_{a}(M)
,
k:=\deg_{a}(M)
);

•

(U^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}|_{U^{\prime}})\cong(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{(l)})
(
m:=\dim_{\overline{\varphi}(a)}(M^{\prime})
,
l:=\deg_{\overline{\varphi}(a)}(M^{\prime})
); and

•

\overline{\varphi}(U)\subset U^{\prime}
.

View
\varphi^{*}_{M^{\prime},U}
as the composition of the following two maps:

{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\textrm{restriction}}{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(U^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\varphi^{*}_{U^{\prime},U}}{\mathcal{O}}(U).

Note that the first arrow is continuous by Lemma
4.5
, and the second one is continuous by Lemma
4.14
. Hence the map

\varphi^{*}_{M^{\prime},U}
is also continuous and the theorem is proved.

∎

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})
be a morphism of formal manifolds. If the homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(M)

is surjective, then it is open.

Proof.

In view of (
4.3
), it suffices to prove the case that
M^{\prime}
is connected. Note that
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})
is a Fréchet space, and
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is a product of Fréchet spaces (see Corollary
4.7
).
Together with the facts that

•

all Fréchet spaces are barreled (see
[
K1
, § 21.5 (3)]
); and

•

the product of arbitrary barreled spaces is barreled (see
[
K1
, § 27.1 (5)]
),

it implies that
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is a barreled space. The assertion then follows from Theorem
4.15
and the fact that every continuous surjective linear map from a Fréchet space to a barreled space is open (see
[
K2
, § 34.2 (3)]
).
∎

5.
The algebra of formal functions

In this section, we show that a formal manifold is determined by the topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra of formal functions.

5.1.
Characters of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)

For every
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
A
, we call a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra homomorphism
A\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
a character of
A
.
For every
a\in M
, the map

(5.1)

\mathrm{Ev}_{a}:{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\quad f\mapsto f(a)

of evaluating at
a
is a continuous character of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
.
The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following theorem, which is known as Milnor’s exercise when
M
is a smooth
manifold (see
[
MS
, Problem 1-C]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that the cardinality of
\pi_{0}(M)
does not exceed the cardinality of
\mathbb{R}
.
Then every character of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is of the form
\mathrm{Ev}_{a}
for some
a\in M
.

Before proving the theorem, we establish some technical lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\pi_{0}(M)
is countable, then
there exists a formal function
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R})
such that the set
\{a\in M\,:\,f(a)\leq C\}
is compact for all
C\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Recall that the reduction
\underline{M}
of
M
is a smooth manifold.
It is well-known that there is a bounded below proper smooth map
\underline{f}:\underline{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(see the proof of
[
B1
, Chapter 2, Theorem 10.8]
for example). Let
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
be an element in the preimage of
\underline{f}
under the surjective map (
2.13
). Then
f
fulfills the requirement of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\chi:{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a character, and let
a\in M
. If
\chi\neq\mathrm{Ev}_{a}
, then there is an element
f_{\chi}^{a}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
such that

•

f_{\chi}^{a}(b)
is a non-negative real number for all
b\in M
;

•

f_{\chi}^{a}(a)=1
; and

•

\chi(f_{\chi}^{a})=0
.

Proof.

The condition
\chi\neq\mathrm{Ev}_{a}
implies that there is an element
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
such that
f(a)\neq 0
and
\chi(f)=0
.
Let
U
be an open neighborhood of
a
in
M
such that
(U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})\cong(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{(k)})
for some
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Multiplying
f
by a suitable formal function if necessary, we assume without loss of generality that
\mathrm{supp}\,f
is contained in
U
. Then we have a decomposition
f=f_{1}+\sqrt{-1}f_{2}
such that
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R})
, and the lemma follows by defining

f_{\chi}^{a}:=(f_{1}(a)^{2}+f_{2}(a)^{2})^{-1}\cdot(f_{1}+\sqrt{-1}f_{2})\cdot(f_{1}-\sqrt{-1}f_{2}).

∎

For every
Z\in\pi_{0}(M)
, write
1_{Z}
for the identity element of
{\mathcal{O}}(N)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that the cardinality of
\pi_{0}(M)
does not exceed the cardinality of
\mathbb{R}
.
Then for every character
\chi
of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
, there is a formal function

f_{\chi}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
such that

•

f_{\chi}(a)
is a non-negative real number for all
a\in M
;

•

the set

Z_{0}:=\{b\in M\,:\,f_{\chi}(b)=0\}

is a connected component of
M
; and

•

\chi(f_{\chi})=0
.

Proof.

Fix an injective map

\tau:\ \pi_{0}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

Let
g
be the formal function on
M
defined by setting
g|_{Z}=\tau(Z)1_{Z}
for all
Z\in\pi_{0}(M)
,
and set

f_{\chi}:=(g-\chi(g))\cdot(g-\overline{\chi(g)})\qquad(\ \overline{\phantom{a}}\ \textrm{ indicates the complex conjugation}).

Then

\chi(f_{\chi})=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad f_{\chi}(a)\geq 0\ \textrm{ for all }a\in M.

Moreover,

Z_{0}:=\{b\in M\,:\,f_{\chi}(b)=0\}

is either the empty set or a connected component of
M
. If
Z_{0}
is empty, then
f_{\chi}
is invertible in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
by Lemma
2.2
,
which contradicts
\chi(f_{\chi})=0
. Thus
f_{\chi}
is a desired formal function.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
:

Let
\chi:{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a character of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
.
Let
f_{\chi}
be as in Lemma
5.4
so that the set

Z_{0}:=\{b\in M\,:\,f_{\chi}(b)=0\}

is a connected component of
M
.

Applying Lemma
5.2
to the formal manifold
Z_{0}
, we have a formal function
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(Z_{0};\mathbb{R})

such that
\{b\in Z_{0}\,:\,f(b)\leq C\}
is compact for all
C\in\mathbb{R}
. By extension by zero, we also view
f
as an element of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
. Put

f^{\prime}:=(f-\chi(f))\cdot(f-\overline{\chi(f)}).

Then
f^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M;\mathbb{R})
,
\chi(f^{\prime})=0
,
f^{\prime}(b)\geq 0
for all
b\in M
, and the set

\{a\in Z_{0}\,:\,f^{\prime}(a)=0\}

is compact.

Assume by contradiction that
\chi\neq\mathrm{Ev}_{a}
for all
a\in M
. Then for every
a\in M
, there is an element
f_{\chi}^{a}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
that satisfies the three conditions in Lemma
5.3
. Since
f_{\chi}^{a}(a)=1
for all
a\in M
, there exist finitely many elements
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s}\in Z_{0}
such that

\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\{b\in Z_{0}\,:\,f_{\chi}^{a_{i}}(b)>0\}\supset\{a\in Z_{0}\,:\,f^{\prime}(a)=0\}.

Now we define

f^{\prime\prime}:=f_{\chi}+f^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}f_{\chi}^{a_{i}}.

Then
\chi(f^{\prime\prime})=0
and
f^{\prime\prime}(b)>0
for all
b\in M
.
Lemma
2.2
implies that
f^{\prime\prime}
is invertible in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
, which contradicts
\chi(f^{\prime\prime})=0
. ∎

5.2.
Continuous characters of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)

For every connected component
Z
of
M
, we view the identity element
1_{Z}\in{\mathcal{O}}(Z)
as an element of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
via extension by zero. Then
1_{Z}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is an indecomposable idempotent in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
. Namely, it is an idempotent in the sense that
1_{Z}^{2}=1_{Z}
, and it is indecomposable in the sense that it is nonzero, and is not the sum of two nonzero idempotents. Moreover,
it is obvious that every indecomposable idempotent in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is of the form
1_{Z}
for a unique connected component
Z
of
M
.

By Theorem
5.1
, we know that if the cardinality of
\pi_{0}(M)
does not exceed the cardinality of
\mathbb{R}
, then every character of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is continuous. In general, we have the following characterization of the continuity of a character of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\chi:{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a character. Then the following three conditions are equivalent to each other.

(a)

The character
\chi
is continuous.

(b)

The character
\chi
vanishes on
(1-e)\cdot{\mathcal{O}}(M)
for some indecomposable idempotent
e
in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
.

(c)

The equality
\chi=\mathrm{Ev}_{a}
holds for some
a\in M
.

When these conditions are satisfied, the elements
e\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
and
a\in M
as above are unique.

Proof.

We first prove that (a) implies (c). Suppose that
\chi
is continuous.
Since

{\mathcal{O}}(M)=\prod_{Z\in\pi_{0}(M)}{\mathcal{O}}(Z)

as LCS,
the continuity of
\chi
implies that there is an open closed subset
Z^{\prime}
of
M
, consisting of
finitely many connected components of
M
, such that
\chi
factors through the restriction map

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(Z^{\prime}).

Write
\chi^{\prime}:{\mathcal{O}}(Z^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
for the induced character. By Theorem
5.1
,
\chi^{\prime}
equals the evaluation map at some
a\in Z^{\prime}
. Thus
\chi=\mathrm{Ev}_{a}
and we have proved that (a) implies (c).

By using Theorem
5.1
, it is easy to see that (b) implies (c). The uniqueness assertion as well as the implications (c)
\Rightarrow
(a) and (c)
\Rightarrow
(b) are obvious.
∎

5.3.
The formal spectra

In this subsection, we reconstruct the formal manifold
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
from the topological
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
.
We start with the following definition.

Definition 5.6
.

A formal
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra is a topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra that is topologically isomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
for some formal manifold
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
.

All topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras together with all continuous homomorphisms between them form a category.
All formal
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras form a full subcategory of the category of topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras, and thus they also form a category.

Let
A
be a formal
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra.
Define the formal spectrum of
A
to be the set

(5.2)

\mathrm{Specf}(A):=\{\textrm{continuous character of $A$}\}.

Equip
\mathrm{Specf}(A)
with the coarsest topology such that the map

\mathrm{Specf}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\quad\chi\mapsto\chi(f)

is continuous for all
f\in A
. For every
\chi\in\mathrm{Specf}(A)
, write
A_{\chi}
for the localization of the ring
A
at the maximal ideal
\ker(\chi)
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathrm{Specf}(A)
. We say that an element

f=\{f_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in U}\in\prod_{\chi\in U}A_{\chi}

is locally represented by
A
if for every
\chi_{0}\in U
,
there is an open neighborhood
U_{0}
of
\chi_{0}
in
U
,
and an element
f_{0}\in A
such that
\{f_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in U_{0}}
equals the image of
f_{0}
under the canonical map

A\rightarrow\prod_{\chi\in U_{0}}A_{\chi}.

Define

(5.3)

{\mathcal{O}}_{A}(U):=\left\{f\in\prod_{\chi\in U}A_{\chi}\,:\,f\textrm{ is locally represented by $A$ }\right\}.

Together with the restriction maps,
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}
is a sheaf of
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras over
\mathrm{Specf}(A)
.

The rest of this subsection is devoted to a proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 5.7
.

The pair
(\mathrm{Specf}(A),{\mathcal{O}}_{A})
is a formal manifold.
Furthermore, if
A={\mathcal{O}}(M)
, then there is an identification
(\mathrm{Specf}(A),{\mathcal{O}}_{A})=(M,{\mathcal{O}})
of formal manifolds.

We begin with the following lemma, which asserts that the topology on
M
is determined by the space
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The map

(5.4)

\iota_{M}:\ M\rightarrow\prod_{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)}{\mathbb{C}},\quad a\mapsto\{f(a)\}_{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)}

is a topological embedding.

Proof.

The map (
5.4
) is clearly continuous and injective.
In what follows we prove that the inverse map

\iota_{M}^{-1}:\ \iota_{M}(M)\rightarrow M

is also continuous, which implies the lemma.

Let
a\in M
and let
U
be an open neighborhood of
a
in
M
. Take a formal function
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
such that its support is contained in
U
and
f_{0}(a)=1
.
Then

U^{\prime}:=\left\{\{c_{f}\}_{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)}\in\prod_{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)}{\mathbb{C}}\,:\,(c_{f_{0}}-1)\cdot(\overline{c_{f_{0}}}-1)<1\right\}

is an open neighborhood of
\iota_{M}(a)
in
\prod_{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)}{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\iota_{M}^{-1}(U^{\prime})\subset U
, as required.

∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
a\in M
. Then as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra, the stalk
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
is canonically isomorphic to the localization of
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
at the maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{\prime}:=\{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)\,:\,f(a)=0\}.

Proof.

Write

\rho_{a}:{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(M)_{\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{\prime}}

for the localization map. For every
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
, we have that

\displaystyle f\in\ker(\rho_{a})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle f_{0}f=0\textrm{ for some $f_{0}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{\prime}$}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle f|_{U}=0\textrm{ for some open neighborhood $U$ of $a$ in $M$}.

Moreover, for every
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{\prime}
, there is an element
f_{1}\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
such that
\rho_{a}(f_{0}f_{1}-1)=0
. This implies that the map
\rho_{a}
is surjective.

The lemma then follows by noting that the canonical map

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{a}

is also surjective (see Corollary
2.12
) and its kernel equals that of
\rho_{a}
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.7
:

Assume that
A={\mathcal{O}}(M)
. By Proposition
5.5
, we have a bijection

\mathrm{Ev}:M\rightarrow\mathrm{Specf}(A),\quad a\mapsto\mathrm{Ev}_{a}.

This is in fact a homeomorphism by Lemma
5.8
. In the notation of Lemma
5.9
, we have that
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}={\mathcal{O}}(M)_{\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{\prime}}
for every
a\in M
.
Also, note that for every open subset
U
of
M
,

{\mathcal{O}}(U)=\left\{f\in\prod_{a\in U}{\mathcal{O}}_{a}\,:\,f\textrm{ is locally represented by ${\mathcal{O}}(M)$ }\right\}.

Thus with respect to the homeomorphism
\mathrm{Ev}
, the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
on
M
coincides with the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}
on
\mathrm{Specf}(A)
. The proof of Theorem
5.7
is now finished. ∎

5.4.
An equivalence of categories

In this subsection, we prove that the category of formal manifolds is equivalent to the opposite category of that of formal
\mathbb{C}
-algebras.

Let
\psi:A^{\prime}\rightarrow A
be a continuous homomorphism of formal
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras.
Then it induces a map

(5.5)

\overline{\psi}:\,\mathrm{Specf}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{Specf}(A^{\prime}),\quad\chi\mapsto\chi\circ\psi.

It is easily seen that this map is continuous. For every
\chi\in\mathrm{Specf}(A)
,
\psi
also induces a homomorphism

\psi_{\chi}^{*}:\,A^{\prime}_{\psi(\chi)}\rightarrow A_{\chi}.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
U^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{Specf}(A^{\prime})
be an open subset and put
U:=\overline{\psi}^{-1}(U^{\prime})\subset\mathrm{Specf}(A)
. Then there exists a unique
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra homomorphism

(5.6)

\psi^{*}_{U^{\prime}}:{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{\prime}}(U^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{A}(U)

such that the diagram

\begin{CD}{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{\prime}}(U^{\prime})@>{\psi^{*}_{U^{\prime}}}>{}>{\mathcal{O}}_{A}(U)\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
A^{\prime}_{\psi(\chi)}@>{\psi_{\chi}^{*}}>{}>A_{\chi}\\
\end{CD}

commutes for all
\chi\in U
.

Proof.

The uniqueness follows from the fact that the canonical map
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}(U)\rightarrow\prod_{\chi\in U}A_{\chi}
is injective. Note that if an element

f^{\prime}=\{f^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}\}_{\chi^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}}\in\prod_{\chi^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}}A^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}

is locally represented by
A^{\prime}
, then

f:=\{\psi_{\chi}^{*}(f^{\prime}_{\overline{\psi}(\chi)})\}_{\chi\in U}\in\prod_{\chi\in U}A_{\chi}

is locally represented by
A
. Furthermore, the map

\psi^{*}_{U^{\prime}}:{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{\prime}}(U^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{A}(U),\quad f^{\prime}\mapsto f

is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism. Thus the existence assertion of the lemma also holds.

∎

The family

\{\psi^{*}_{U^{\prime}}:{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{\prime}}(U^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{A}(U)\}_{\,U^{\prime}\textrm{ is an open subset of }M^{\prime}}

as in (
5.6
) is compatible with respect to the restrictions. Thus we get a sheaf homomorphism

\psi^{*}:\overline{\psi}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{A}.

This sheaf homomorphism induces local homomorphisms of the stalks. Then we obtain in this way
a morphism

(5.7)

\mathrm{Specf}(\psi)=(\overline{\psi},\psi^{*}):\ (\mathrm{Specf}(A),{\mathcal{O}}_{A})\rightarrow(\mathrm{Specf}(A^{\prime}),{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{\prime}})

of formal manifolds. The assignment

\psi\mapsto\mathrm{Specf}(\psi)
is compatible with the compositions, and if
\psi
is an identity homomorphism,
then
\mathrm{Specf}(\psi)
is also an identity morphism. In conclusion, we get a functor

(5.8)

A\mapsto(\mathrm{Specf}(A),{\mathcal{O}}_{A})

from the opposite category of the category of formal
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras to the category of formal manifolds.

On the other hand, by Theorem
4.15
, we have a functor

(5.9)

(M,{\mathcal{O}})\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}(M)

from the category of formal manifolds to the opposite category of the category of formal
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras.
Then we have the following result, which implies Theorem
1.8
.

Theorem 5.11
.

The functors (
5.8
) and (
5.9
)
are quasi-inverse of each other.

Proof.

This is implied by
Theorem
5.7
.

∎

Remark 5.12
.

Assume that

(5.10)

\text{the cardinality of $\pi_{0}(M)$ does not exceed the cardinality of $\mathbb{R}$}.

By Theorem
5.1
, it is not difficult to show that the formal spectrum
\mathrm{Specf({\mathcal{O}}(M))}

coincides with the usual spectrum

\mathrm{Spec}({\mathcal{O}}(M)):=\{\text{character of ${\mathcal{O}}(M)$}\}

of the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
(see
[
N
, Section 8.1]
) as topological spaces.
Similar to the case of smooth manifolds (see
[
N
, Section 7]
for example), in such case,
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
can be reconstructed from the spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathcal{O}}(M))
by Theorem
5.7
.
Furthermore, for any formal manifold
(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})
(whether or not it satisfies the condition (
5.10
)),
it is easy to verify that every
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism from
O(M)
to

O^{\prime}(M^{\prime})
is automatically continuous (see
[
N
, Page 88, Proposition (a)]
).

6.
Vector-valued formal functions and products of formal manifolds

In this section, we introduce the notion of vector-valued formal functions.
Using this, we prove the existence of finite products in the category of formal manifolds.

6.1.
Vector-valued smooth functions

In this subsection, we review some basics of topological tensor products and vector-valued smooth functions.
Throughout this subsection, let
E
be an LCS which may or may not be Hausdorff.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
A
be a subset of
E
.

(a) The subset
A
is called quasi-closed if it contains all limit points of the bounded subsets in it.

(b) The quasi-closure of
A
is defined as the intersection of all the quasi-closed subsets of
E
that contain
A
.

(c) The subset
A
is called strictly dense in
E
if its quasi-closure is
E
.

The intersection of arbitrarily many quasi-closed subsets is also quasi-closed. In particular, the quasi-closure of every subset in
E
is quasi-closed.

Definition 6.2
.

An LCS is said to be complete (resp. quasi-complete) if it is Hausdorff, and every
Cauchy net (resp. bounded Cauchy net) in it has a limit point.

Definition 6.3
.

A completion of
E
is a complete LCS
\widehat{E}
together with a continuous linear map
\hat{\iota}:E\rightarrow\widehat{E}
with the following property: for every complete LCS
F
and every continuous linear map
f:E\rightarrow F
, there is a unique continuous linear map
f^{\prime}:\widehat{E}\rightarrow F
such that the diagram

\textstyle{\widehat{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hat{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{F}

commutes.

It is known that the LCS
E
has a unique completion, and the above map
\hat{\iota}
is a topological embedding with dense image when
E
is Hausdorff. See
[
T
, Theorem 5.2]
for example. When
E
is not Hausdorff, the completion

\widehat{E}
is precisely the completion of
E/\overline{\{0\}}
. Here
\overline{\{0\}}
is the closure of the zero space in
E
.

Definition 6.4
.

A quasi-completion of
E
is a quasi-complete LCS
\widetilde{E}
together with a continuous linear map
\tilde{\iota}:E\rightarrow\widetilde{E}
with the following property: for every quasi-complete LCS
F
and every continuous linear map
f:E\rightarrow F
, there is a unique continuous linear map
f^{\prime}:\widetilde{E}\rightarrow F
such that the diagram

\textstyle{\widetilde{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{F}

commutes.

The LCS
E
has a unique quasi-completion, and when
E
is Hausdorff the quasi-completion
\widetilde{E}
agrees with the quasi-closure of
\hat{\iota}(E)
in
\widehat{E}
.
See
[
S
, Page 91]
for details.
When
E
is not Hausdorff, the quasi-completion
\widetilde{E}
agrees with the quasi-completion of
E/\overline{\{0\}}
.

The following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
E_{0}
be a complete (resp. quasi-complete) LCS, and let
f:\,E\rightarrow E_{0}
be a linear topological embedding such that
f(E)
is dense (resp. strictly dense) in
F
. Then
E_{0}
is the completion (resp. quasi-completion) of
E
.

We denote by
E\otimes_{\pi}F
the projective tensor product of
E
and
F
, where
F
is another LCS.
As mentioned in the Introduction, the quasi-completion and completion of
E\otimes_{\pi}F
are denoted as
E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F
and
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F
, respectively.

The following result is about projective tensor products of products of LCS.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
E=\prod_{i\in I}E_{i}
and
F=\prod_{j\in J}F_{j}
be two products of Hausdorff LCS.
Then the canonical linear map

(6.1)

E\otimes_{\pi}F\rightarrow\prod_{(i,j)\in I\times J}E_{i}\otimes_{\pi}F_{j}

is a topological embedding, and
induces the following LCS identifications:

(6.2)

E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F=\prod_{(i,j)\in I\times J}E_{i}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F=\prod_{(i,j)\in I\times J}E_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F_{j}.

Proof.

The first assertion of the lemma and the second identification in (
6.2
) are well-known (see
[
J
, §15.4, Theorem 1]
).
Using this identification, the linear map (
6.1
) restricts to a topological linear isomorphism

\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_{i}\right)\otimes_{\pi}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in I}F_{j}\right)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\otimes_{\pi}F_{j}).

Here the direct sums are equipped with the subspace topologies of the direct products. Meanwhile, since a product of quasi-complete LCS is still quasi-complete (see
[
J
, §3.2, Proposition 6]
),
there is a linear topological embedding

E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F\rightarrow\prod_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F_{j})

induced by (
6.1
). As
\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\otimes_{\pi}F_{j})

is strictly dense in
\prod_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(E_{i}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F_{j})
,
the first identification in (
6.2
) then follows.
∎

We also denote by
E\otimes_{\varepsilon}F
the epsilon (injective) tensor product of
E
and
F
(see
[
G
]
).
Similarly, the quasi-completion and completion of
E\otimes_{\varepsilon}F
are denoted as
E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F
and
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F
, respectively.
When
E
or
F
is nuclear, it is a classical result of Grothendieck that their projective tensor product coincides with the epsilon tensor product (see
[
T
, Theorem 50.1]
).
In this case, we
will simply write

E\widetilde{\otimes}F:=E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F=E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F\quad\text{and}\quad E\widehat{\otimes}F:=E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F=E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F.

In the rest part of this subsection, assume that
E
is a quasi-complete LCS, and let

N
be a smooth manifold.
Write
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
for the space of all
E
-valued smooth functions on
N
.
Endow
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
with
the topology defined by the seminorms

\lvert f\rvert_{D,\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\mu}}:=\sup_{a\in N}\lvert(Df)(a)\rvert_{\mu}\qquad(f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)),

where
D
is a compactly supported differential operator on
N
, and
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\mu}
is a continuous seminorm on
E
.
Then
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
is a quasi-complete LCS.

Identify
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\otimes E
as a linear subspace of
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)
, which consists of the smooth functions
whose image is contained in a finite-dimensional subspace of
E
.
The following result is proved in

[
S
, Thémrème 1]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

We have that

(6.3)

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)

as LCS,
and if
E
is complete, then

(6.4)

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widehat{\otimes}E

as LCS.

Example 6.8
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and write

E[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]:=\left\{\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}v_{J}y^{J}\,:\,v_{J}\in E\textrm{ for all }J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}\right\}

for the space of the formal power series.
Equip
E[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]
with the term-wise convergence topology, which is defined by the seminorms

(6.5)

\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu,J}:\quad\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}f_{I}y^{I}\mapsto\lvert f_{J}\rvert_{\nu},

where
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\nu}
is a continuous seminorm on
E
and
J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}
.
When
N={\mathbb{N}}^{k}
, we have LCS identifications

\displaystyle E[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;E)={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}E=E\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]],

and

E[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]=E\widehat{\otimes}{\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]

is complete provided that
E
is complete.

Example 6.9
.

Let
L
be another smooth manifold. Then we have the following LCS identifications (see
[
S
, Proposition 14]
):

(6.6)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(L)={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N;{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(L))={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N)\widehat{\otimes}{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(L)={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(N\times L).

6.2.
Vector-valued formal functions

Throughout this subsection, let
E
be a quasi-complete LCS.
For every open subset
U
of
M
, write

{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U):={\mathcal{O}}(U)\widetilde{\otimes}E.

Motivated by Schwartz’s characterization of
E
-valued smooth functions (see (
6.3
)), we make the following definition.

Definition 6.10
.

An element in
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(M)
is called an
E
-valued formal function on
M
.

With the obvious restriction maps, the assignment

{\mathcal{O}}_{E}:\ U\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)\quad(\text{$U$ is an open subset of $M$})

forms a presheaf of complex vector spaces on
M
.
The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6.11
.

The presheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
is a sheaf. Moreover, if
E
is complete, then for every open subset
U
of
M
,

{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)={\mathcal{O}}(U)\widehat{\otimes}E

as LCS.

We start with the following special case.

Lemma 6.12
.

Theorem
6.11
holds when
M=N^{(k)}
, where
N
is a smooth manifold and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Let
U
be an open subset of
M
. By applying (
6.3
) and Example
6.8
, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(U)\widetilde{\otimes}E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]\widetilde{\otimes}E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U;E[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]).

Thus
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
is a sheaf. Moreover, if
E
is complete, then
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U;E[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]])

is complete by (
6.4
), and so
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)={\mathcal{O}}(U)\widehat{\otimes}E
.
∎

Write
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}
for the sheaf associated to the presheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
. Lemma
6.12
implies that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)={\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)
whenever the open set
U
is a
chart. In general, the sheaf property implies that

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)=\varprojlim_{V}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(V),

where
V
runs over all charts of
U
. Using the above equality, we view
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)
as an LCS with the projective limit topology. It is quasi-complete by
[
K1
, § 19.10 (2)]
.
If the open set
U
is a chart, then

(6.7)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)={\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)

as LCS.

The following lemma generalizes Lemma
4.5
.

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
M
. Then the restriction map

(6.8)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)

is continuous.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every chart
V
of
U
, the canonical map

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(V)

is continuous. But this is self-evidence since
V
is also a chart of
M
.
∎

Similar to Lemma
4.6
, we have the following result.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be an open cover of
M
. Then the linear map

(6.9)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)\rightarrow\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U_{\gamma}),\quad f\mapsto\{f|_{U_{\gamma}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

is a closed topological embedding.

Proof.

The sheaf property of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}
and Lemma
6.13
imply that the map (
6.9
) is continuous, injective and its image is closed in the codomain.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a net in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)
whose image under the map (
6.9
) converges to zero. Then

(6.10)

\lim_{i\in I}(f_{i}|_{U_{\gamma}})=0\quad\textrm{for all }\gamma\in\Gamma.

To finish the proof, we only need to show that

(6.11)

\lim_{i\in I}(f_{i}|_{U})=0

for all charts
U
of
M
.

By Lemma
4.6
, the linear map

{\mathcal{O}}(U)\rightarrow\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{O}}(U\cap U_{\gamma})

is a topological embedding. Since the epsilon tensor product of two linear topological embeddings of Hausdorff LCS is a linear topological embedding (see the proof of
[
T
, Proposition 43.7]
), Lemma
6.6
and (
6.7
) imply that
the linear map

(6.12)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)\rightarrow\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U\cap U_{\gamma})\ \ \textrm{ is also a topological embedding.}

Now, Lemma
6.13
and (
6.10
) imply that

(6.13)

\lim_{i\in I}(f_{i}|_{U\cap U_{\gamma}})=0\quad\textrm{for all }\gamma\in\Gamma.

Hence (
6.11
) holds by (
6.12
).

∎

Note that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}
is naturally a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules, and the module structure map

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\times\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M),\quad(g,f)\mapsto gf

is continuous and bilinear.

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
M
, and let
g\in{\mathcal{O}}(M)
be a formal function whose support is contained in
U
. Then the linear map (see (
3.11
))

(6.14)

m_{g}:\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M),\quad f\mapsto gf

is continuous.

Proof.

The lemma follows by applying Lemma
6.14
to the open cover
\{U,M\setminus\mathrm{supp}\,g\}
of
M
.

∎

Lemma 6.16
.

For every open subset
U
of
M
, the canonical linear map

{\mathcal{O}}(U)\otimes_{\pi}E\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)

is a topological embedding.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}{\mathcal{O}}(U)\otimes_{\pi}E@>{}>{}>\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)=\varprojlim_{V}{\mathcal{O}}(V)\widetilde{\otimes}E\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\left(\prod_{V}{\mathcal{O}}(V)\right)\widetilde{\otimes}E@ >>>\prod_{V}\left({\mathcal{O}}(V)\widetilde{\otimes}E\right),\\
\end{CD}

where
V
runs over all charts of
M
that are contained in
U
.
By Lemma
4.6
, Proposition
4.8
and the fact that the epsilon tensor product of two linear topological embeddings of Hausdorff LCS is a linear topological embedding (see the proof of
[
T
, Proposition 43.7]
), we see that the linear map

{\mathcal{O}}(U)\otimes_{\pi}E={\mathcal{O}}(U)\otimes_{\varepsilon}E\rightarrow\left(\prod_{V}{\mathcal{O}}(V)\right)\otimes_{\pi}E=\left(\prod_{V}{\mathcal{O}}(V)\right)\otimes_{\varepsilon}E

is a topological embedding.
This implies that
the left vertical arrow
is a linear topological embedding. By Lemma
6.6
, the bottom horizontal arrow is a topological linear isomorphism.
Since
the right vertical arrow is a linear topological embedding by Lemma
6.14
, the top horizontal arrow is also
a linear topological embedding.
∎

By Lemma
6.16
, we identify the LCS
{\mathcal{O}}(U)\otimes_{\pi}E
with a subspace of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)
.

Proposition 6.17
.

The subspace
{\mathcal{O}}(M)\otimes_{\pi}E
is strictly dense (see Definition
6.1
) in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)
.

Proof.

Take an atlas
\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
of
M
, and let
\{g_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a partition of unity on
M
subordinate to it.
Let
A
be a quasi-closed subset (see Definition
6.1
) of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)
containing
{\mathcal{O}}(M)\otimes E
.
Since the inverse image of a quasi-closed set under a continuous linear map is still quasi-closed (see
[
S
, Page 92,
2^{\circ}
)]
),
it follows from Lemma
6.15
that
m_{g_{\gamma}}^{-1}(A)
(see (
6.14
))
is quasi-closed.
This, together with (
6.7
), shows that
m_{g_{\gamma}}^{-1}(A)=\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U_{\gamma})
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
In particular, for every
f\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)
, we have that
f_{\gamma}:=g_{\gamma}\cdot f|_{U_{\gamma}}\in A
.

By Lemma
6.14
, the canonical linear map

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(M)\rightarrow\prod_{U\textrm{ is a relatively compact open subset of $M$}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)

is a topological embedding.
This implies that

B:=\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}}f_{\gamma}\,:\,\Gamma_{0}\ \text{is a finite subset of}\ \Gamma\right\}

is a bounded subset in
A
. Thus
A
contains the closure of
B
. Then

f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}g_{\gamma}\,f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f_{\gamma}

belongs to
A
since it belongs to the closure of
B
.
This completes the proof.

∎

Proof of Theorem
6.11
:

Let
U
be an open subset of
M
.
Applying Proposition
6.17
to the formal manifold
(U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})
, we know that

{\mathcal{O}}(U)\otimes_{\pi}E
is strictly dense in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)
. Therefore, by Lemma
6.5
, we have that

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{E}(U)={\mathcal{O}}(U)\widetilde{\otimes}E={\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)

as LCS.
This proves that
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
is a sheaf.
When
E
is complete, Lemmas
6.12
and
6.14
imply that
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(U)
is complete.
This finishes the proof of Theorem
6.11
.
∎

6.3.
Products of formal manifolds

In this subsection, let
(M_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{1})
and
(M_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{2})
be two formal manifolds.
For open subsets
V_{1}\subset U_{1}
of
M_{1}
, and
V_{2}\subset U_{2}
of
M_{2}
, we have the restriction map

{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(V_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(V_{2}).

Using these restriction maps, for each open subset
U_{3}
of

M_{3}:=M_{1}\times M_{2}\quad\text{(as topological spaces),}

we define

{\mathcal{O}}_{3}(U_{3}):=\varprojlim_{(U_{1},U_{2})}{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2}),

where
(U_{1},U_{2})
runs over all pairs of open subsets
U_{1}
of
M_{1}
and
U_{2}
of
M_{2}
such that
U_{1}\times U_{2}\subset U_{3}
. Then
{\mathcal{O}}_{3}
is obviously a presheaf of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras on
M_{3}
.

This subsection is devoted to a proof of the following theorem, which together with the fact
that (see Theorem
6.11
)

{\mathcal{O}}(M_{3})={\mathcal{O}}_{1}(M_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(M_{2})={\mathcal{O}}_{1}(M_{1})\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(M_{2})

implies Theorem
1.9
.

Theorem 6.18
.

The presheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{3}
on
M_{3}
is a sheaf, and
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})
is a formal manifold which is a product of
(M_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{1})
and
(M_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{2})
in the category of formal manifolds. Moreover,

(M_{3},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{3}})=(M_{1},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{1}})\times(M_{2},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{2}})

as smooth manifolds, and for
a_{1}\in M_{1},a_{2}\in M_{2}
,

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3,a_{3}}=\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}=\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\widetilde{\otimes}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}\qquad(a_{3}:=(a_{1},a_{2}))

as topological
\mathbb{C}
-algebras.

We will prove the theorem through a sequence of lemmas.

Lemma 6.19
.

Assume that
M_{1}=N^{(k)}
and
M_{2}=L^{(l)}
, where
N
and
L
are smooth manifolds and
k,l\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then
{\mathcal{O}}_{3}
is a sheaf,
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})
is a formal manifold, and
M_{3}\cong(N\times L)^{(k+l)}
as formal manifolds.

Proof.

Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be open subsets of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, respectively.
It follows from Example
6.8
and (
6.6
) that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U_{1})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U_{2})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{l}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U_{2})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{l}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U_{1}\times U_{2})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k},y_{k+1},y_{k+2},\dots,y_{k+l}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U_{1}\times U_{2})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k},y_{k+1},y_{k+2},\dots,y_{k+l}]]

as topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras. Hence by the sheaf property, we conclude that
{\mathcal{O}}_{3}={\mathcal{O}}_{N\times L}^{(k+l)}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Write
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}
for the sheafication of
{\mathcal{O}}_{3}
. We will show that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}={\mathcal{O}}_{3}
.

Lemma 6.20
.

If
M_{1}=N^{(k)}
with
N
a smooth manifold and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
{\mathcal{O}}_{3}
is a sheaf, and
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})
is a formal manifold.

Proof.

Let
U_{1}
be an open subset of
M_{1}
.
For every open subset
U_{2}
of
M_{2}
, we have a natural homomorphism

(6.15)

{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})={\mathcal{O}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2})\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2}).

This yields a sheaf homomorphism

(6.16)

{\mathcal{O}}_{2,{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})}\rightarrow(p_{2})_{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}|_{U_{1}\times M_{2}})

over
M_{2}
,
where
{\mathcal{O}}_{2,{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})}
is the sheaf

U_{2}\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})

on
M_{2}
(see Theorem
6.11
), and

p_{2}:U_{1}\times M_{2}\rightarrow M_{2}
is the projection map. By Lemma
6.19
, the map
(
6.15
) is an isomorphism when
U_{2}
is a chart. This implies that (
6.16
) is a sheaf isomorphism. In other words,

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2})={\mathcal{O}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2})

for all open subsets
U_{2}
of
M_{2}
. Then the sheaf property implies that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}={\mathcal{O}}_{3}
, and Lemma
6.19
implies that
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})
is a formal manifold.

∎

Lemma 6.21
.

The presheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{3}
is a sheaf, and
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})
is a formal manifold.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
6.20
. Let
U_{2}
be an open subset of
M_{2}
.
For every open subset
U_{1}
of
M_{1}
, we have a natural homomorphism

(6.17)

{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})={\mathcal{O}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2})\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2}).

This yields a sheaf homomorphism

(6.18)

{\mathcal{O}}_{1,{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})}\rightarrow(p_{1})_{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}|_{M_{1}\times U_{2}})

over
M_{1}
,
where
{\mathcal{O}}_{1,{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})}
is the sheaf

U_{1}\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(U_{1})\widetilde{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(U_{2})

on
M_{1}
(see Theorem
6.11
), and

p_{1}:M_{1}\times U_{2}\rightarrow M_{1}
is the projection map. By Lemma
6.20
, the map
(
6.17
) is an isomorphism when
U_{1}
is a chart. This implies that (
6.18
) is a sheaf isomorphism. In other words,

\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2})={\mathcal{O}}_{3}(U_{1}\times U_{2})

for all open subsets
U_{1}
of
M_{1}
. Thus
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3}={\mathcal{O}}_{3}
, and
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})
is a formal manifold by Lemma
6.19
.

∎

Lemma 6.22
.

The formal manifold
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})
is a product of
(M_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{1})
and
(M_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{2})
in the category of formal manifolds.

Proof.

By
Theorem
5.11
,
for every formal manifold
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mor}((M,{\mathcal{O}}),(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathcal{O}}_{3}(M_{3}),{\mathcal{O}}(M))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathcal{O}}_{1}(M_{1})\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(M_{2}),{\mathcal{O}}(M))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathcal{O}}_{1}(M_{1}),{\mathcal{O}}(M))\times{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathcal{O}}_{2}(M_{2}),{\mathcal{O}}(M))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mor}((M,{\mathcal{O}}),(M_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{1}))\times\mathrm{Mor}((M,{\mathcal{O}}),(M_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{2})).

Here
\mathrm{Mor}
indicates the set of morphisms between two formal manifolds, and
{\mathrm{Hom}}
indicates the set of continuous
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra homomorphisms.
This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.23
.

In the category of smooth manifolds, we have that

(M_{3},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{3}})=(M_{1},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{1}})\times(M_{2},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{2}}).

Proof.

Let
U_{1}\subset M_{1}
and
U_{2}\subset M_{2}
be open subsets, and set
U_{3}:=U_{1}\times U_{2}
.
Recall from Example
1.5
that the reduction assignment

(M,{\mathcal{O}})\mapsto(M,\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})

is a functor from the category of formal manifolds to the category of smooth manifolds.
Thus the projection morphisms

(U_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3}|_{U_{3}})\rightarrow(U_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{1}|_{U_{1}})
and

(U_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3}|_{U_{3}})\rightarrow(U_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{2}|_{U_{2}})
induce respectively the smooth maps

(U_{3},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{3}|_{U_{3}}})=(U_{3},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{3}}|_{U_{3}})\rightarrow(U_{1},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{1}|_{U_{1}}})=(U_{1},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{1}}|_{U_{1}})

and

(U_{3},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{3}|_{U_{3}}})=(U_{3},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{3}}|_{U_{3}})\rightarrow(U_{2},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{2}|_{U_{2}}})=(U_{2},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{2}}|_{U_{2}}).

These two smooth maps produce a smooth map

(6.19)

(U_{3},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{3}}|_{U_{3}})\rightarrow(U_{1},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{1}}|_{U_{1}})\times(U_{2},\underline{{\mathcal{O}}_{2}}|_{U_{2}}).

When
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are charts, it follows from Lemma
6.19
that the smooth map (
6.19
) is a
diffeomorphism.
This implies that the map (
6.19
) is a
diffeomorphism when
U_{1}=M_{1}
and
U_{2}=M_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 6.24
.

For every
a_{1}\in M_{1}
and
a_{2}\in M_{2}
, we have topological algebra identifications

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3,a_{3}}=\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}=\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\widetilde{\otimes}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}\qquad(a_{3}:=(a_{1},a_{2})).

Proof.

The projection morphisms
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})\rightarrow(M_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{1})
and
(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})\rightarrow(M_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{2})
induce
respectively the continuous homomorphisms (see Lemma
2.8
)

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3,a_{3}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3,a_{3}}.

Since
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3,a_{3}}
is complete, the above two maps yield a continuous homomorphism

(6.20)

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{3,a_{3}}.

Note that we may (and do) assume that
(M_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{1})=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}^{(k_{1})})
and
(M_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{2})=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}^{(k_{2})})

for some
n_{1},n_{2},k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then by Lemma
6.19
we see that

(M_{3},{\mathcal{O}}_{3})=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{3}},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{3}}}^{(k_{3})})\quad(n_{3}:=n_{1}+n_{2},\ k_{3}:=k_{1}+k_{2}).

For
i=1,2,3
, Taylor expansion at
a_{i}
yields a topological algebra isomorphism (see Proposition
2.6
and its proof)

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{i,a_{i}}\cong\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n_{i}},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k_{i}}]].

This, together with Example
6.8
, implies that
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}

=\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,a_{1}}\widetilde{\otimes}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{2,a_{2}}
, and that
the map (
6.20
) is a topological algebra isomorphism.

∎

Theorem
6.18
is now proved by combining Lemmas
6.21
,
6.22
,
6.23
and
6.24
.

Duality properties for induced and coinduced representations in positive characteristic
Sophie Chemla

Abstract
. Let k k be a field of positive characteristic p > 2 p>2 . We prove a duality property concerning the kernel of coinduced representations of Lie superalgebras. This property was already proved by M. Duflo ( [ 8 ] ) for Lie algebras in any characteristic under more restrictive finiteness conditions. It was then generalized to Lie superalgebras in characteristic 0 in previous works ( [ 1 ] , [ 5 ] ). In a second part of the article, we study the links between coinduced representations and induced representations in the case of restricted Lie superalgebras.



1.
Introduction

Assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\overline{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\overline{1}}
is a Lie superalgebra over a field
k

of characteristic
p>2
and
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Lie subsuperalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. From a
representation
(\pi,V)
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
, one can construct a representation of
{\mathfrak{g}}
in two ways :

•

Induction :
\rm Ind_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(V)=U({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U({\mathfrak{h}})}V
with left
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-module structure given by left multiplication;

•

Coinduction :
\rm Coind_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\pi)=Hom_{U({\mathfrak{h}})}(U({\mathfrak{g}}),V)
with left
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-module structure given by the transpose of right multiplication.

It is easy to see that the contragredient representation of
\rm Ind_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(V)
is isomorphic to the coinduced representation from the contragredient representation
\pi^{*}
of
\pi
(
[
7
]
).

M. Duflo (
[
8
]
) proved that, in any characteristic, for a finite dimensional Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, the kernel
I_{\pi}\subset U({\mathfrak{g}})
of

\rm Coind_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\pi)
satisfies a the duality property

\check{I}_{\pi}=I_{\pi^{*}\otimes k_{trad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}}}

where
\check{(-)}
is the antipode of
U({\mathfrak{g}})
and
trad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}
is the character
tr\circ ad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
In characteristic
0
, this duality property was extended to a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
such that only
{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}
is finite dimensional. In this article, we treat the case of a Lie superalgebra with

{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}
finite dimensional when
k
is of positive characteristic case
p>2
. We make use of the
following ascending filtration of
U({\mathfrak{g}})

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathcal{F}}_{-1}U({\mathfrak{g}})=k\\
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}U({\mathfrak{g}})=U({\mathfrak{h}})\\
{\mathcal{F}}_{r}U({\mathfrak{g}})=U({\mathfrak{h}})Vect\left(X^{p^{j}},\quad X\in{\mathfrak{g}},\;j\in[0,r-1]\right)\;{\rm if}\;r>0.\end{array}

From now on, we assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}
is finite dimensional and
k
of positive characteristic
p>2
.
In the case where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a restricted superalgebra with restricted enveloping superalgebra

U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a restricted subsuperalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, we get stronger results.
For a restricted
representation
(\pi,V)
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
, we introduce the restricted induced representation from
\pi
and the restricted
coinduced representation from
\pi
:

•

Restricted induction :
\rm Ind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(V)=U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}V

with left
U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module structure given by left multiplication;

•

Restricted coinduction :
\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)=Hom_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}(U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}}),V)
with left

U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module structure given by the transpose of right multiplication.

Generalizing a result of Borho-Brylinski (
[
6
]
), it was proved in
[
5
]
that, in characteristic 0, the induced representation of a Lie superalgebra could be realized in terms of Grothendieck local cohomology. We prove an analog result for restricted Lie superalgebras :

Theorem

6.12

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. Assume that the
k
-superspace

{{\mathfrak{g}}}/{{\mathfrak{h}}}
is finite dimensional. Set
m=dim\left({\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}\right)_{\overline{1}}
. Let
(\pi,V)
be a representation of

.
The restricted induced representation
\rm Ind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi\otimes\Pi^{m}k_{strad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}})
is isomorphic to
\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)
.

In
[
1
]
, it was noticed that (for any characteristic) Berezin integral provides a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant duality between
\rm Coind_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\pi^{*})
and

\rm Coind_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\pi\otimes\Pi^{m}k_{-strad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}})
in case the Lie superalgebra

is totally odd. We extend this result to any restricted Lie superalgebra

Theorem

6.11

Let
k
be a field of characteristic

. Assume that the
k
-superspace

{{\mathfrak{g}}}/{{\mathfrak{h}}}
is finite dimensional with odd dimension

. There exists a

-invariant duality

between

\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)
and

\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi^{*}\otimes\Pi^{m}k_{-strad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}})

Theorem
6.12
and Theorem

are linked by the

-module isomorphism

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Theta:Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi^{*})&\to&{\mathcal{I}}nd_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)^{*}\\
\lambda&\mapsto&\left[u\otimes v\mapsto<\lambda(\check{u}),v>\right].\\
\end{array}

\left(e^{\underline{a}}\epsilon^{\underline{\alpha}}\right)_{(\underline{a},\underline{\alpha})\in\mathbb{N}^{n,m}}

The one dimensional space
k\Lambda
is endowed with the following
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module structure
(see Lemma
5.3
)

\forall H\in{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\delta_{H}(\Lambda)=strad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}(H)\Lambda.

(
ii
)

Let
(\pi,V)
be a restricted representation of
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
The map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Phi:Ind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\Pi^{m}k_{stad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}}\otimes\pi)&\to&\rm Coind^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}(\pi)\\
u\otimes_{U({\mathfrak{h}})}(\Lambda\otimes v)&\mapsto&\delta_{u}(\Lambda\hat{v}_{\pi})\end{array}

is a
U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})
-isomorphism.

Proof :

The map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Phi:Ind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\Pi^{m}k_{strad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}}\otimes\pi)&\to&\rm Coind^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}(\pi)\\
u\otimes_{U({\mathfrak{h}})}(\Lambda\otimes v)&\mapsto&\delta_{u}(\Lambda\hat{v}_{\pi})\end{array}

is well defined (see Lemma
5.4
). Proceeding as in the proof of lemma
5.6
, one shows that
\Phi
is injective.
To show that
\Phi
is onto, we will make use of the following lemma :

Lemma 6.13
.

The derivation
\delta^{\pi}_{e_{i}}-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{i}}
can be written

\delta^{\pi}_{e_{i}}-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{j}}+\sum_{s=1}^{m}g_{s}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{s}}+F^{\pi}(e_{i})

with
f_{j}
,
g_{s}
belonging to
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
F^{\pi}(e_{i})(\widehat{v})\in{\mathfrak{a}}\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(V)
. A similar statement holds for

\delta_{\epsilon_{s}}
.

Proof of the lemma :

We know that

\delta_{e_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{e_{i}}(\eta_{j})\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{j}}+\sum_{s=1}^{m}\delta_{e_{i}}(\zeta_{s})\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{s}}.

But

\begin{array}[]{l}<\delta_{e_{i}}(\eta_{j}),1>=<\eta_{j},e_{i}>=\delta_{i,j},\\
<\delta_{e_{i}}(\zeta_{s}),1>=<\zeta_{s},e_{i}>=0,\\
<\delta_{\epsilon_{s}}(\zeta_{t}),1>=<\zeta_{t},\epsilon_{s}>=\delta_{s,t},\\
<\delta_{\epsilon_{s}}(\eta_{j}),1>=<\eta_{j},\epsilon_{s}>=0,\\
<F^{\pi}(e_{i})(\widehat{v}),1>=<\delta^{\pi}_{e_{i}}\widehat{v},1>=<\widehat{v},e_{i}>=0.\Box\end{array}

Let
f=\eta_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots\eta_{n}^{a_{n}}\zeta_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\zeta_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
be a monomial. Let us show by decreasing induction on
\sum a_{i}+\sum\alpha_{s}
that, for any
v\in V
,

f\widehat{v}
belongs to
Im\Phi
. The maximal degree of
f
is
n(p-1)+m
and

\eta_{1}^{p-1}\dots\eta_{n}^{p-1}\zeta_{1}\dots\zeta_{n}\widehat{v}=\Phi(1\otimes(\Lambda\otimes v))
.

Assume that
f
is of degree
d-1
and that the property has been shown for any degree
\geq d
. Two cases can happen:

First case : There exists
i\in[1,n]
such that
\eta_{i}fv
is of degree
d
. Then
\delta_{e_{i}}(\eta_{i}f)v-(a_{i}+1)f\hat{v}
has a degree superior or equal to
d
. Thus, par induction hypothesis,

\delta_{e_{i}}(\eta_{i}f\hat{v})-(a_{i}+1)f\hat{v}\in Im\Phi
. But
\eta_{i}f\hat{v}\in Im\Phi
, so does

\delta_{e_{i}}(\eta_{i}f\hat{v})
because
\Phi
is a
U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module morphism. Eventually
f\hat{v}
is in
Im\Phi
.

Second case : There exists
s\in[1,m]
such that
\zeta_{s}f\hat{v}
is of degree
d
. The argument is analog to the previous case.
\Box

The following proposition establishes a link between Propositions
6.11
and
6.12
.
It is well known that the map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Theta:Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi^{*})&\to&{\mathcal{I}}nd_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)^{*}\\
\lambda&\mapsto&\left[u\otimes v\mapsto<\lambda(\check{u}),v>\right]\\
\end{array}

is an isomorphism of
U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules.

Proposition 6.14
.

Let
\Phi:Ind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi\otimes\Pi^{m}k_{strad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}})\to\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)

the isomorphism constructed in Theorem
6.12
and

{}^{t}\Phi:\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)^{*}\to Ind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi\otimes\Pi^{m}k_{strad_{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{h}}}})^{*}
its transpose.
Denote by
\Psi^{\natural}:\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi^{*}\otimes\Pi^{m}k_{-strad})\to\left[\rm Coind_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}^{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\pi)\right]^{*}

the monomorphism determined by
\Psi
. We will show that the equality holds

{}^{t}\Phi\circ\Psi^{\natural}=\Theta.

Proof of Proposition
6.14
:

Let
u\in U({\mathfrak{g}})
,
v\in V
,
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left[{}^{t}\Phi\circ\Psi^{\natural}(f\widehat{v^{*}}\widehat{\omega_{\underline{e}}})\right]\left(u\otimes_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}(\Lambda\otimes v)\right)&=&\Psi\left[f\widehat{v^{*}}\widehat{\omega_{\underline{e}}},\delta_{u}(\Lambda\widehat{v_{\pi}})\right]\\
&=&\Psi\left[\delta_{\check{u}}(f\widehat{v^{*}}\widehat{\omega_{\underline{e}}}),\Lambda\widehat{v}_{\pi}\right](-1)^{\mid u\mid(\mid f\mid+\mid v^{*}\mid+m)}\\
&=&<<1,\delta_{\check{u}}(f\widehat{v^{*}}\widehat{\omega_{\underline{e}}})>,v>(-1)^{\mid u\mid(\mid f\mid+\mid v^{*}\mid+m)}\\
&=&<(f\widehat{v^{*}}\widehat{\omega_{\underline{e}}})(\check{u}),v>\\
&=&\Theta(f\widehat{v^{*}}\widehat{\omega_{\underline{e}}})\left(u\otimes_{U^{\prime}({\mathfrak{h}})}(v\otimes\Lambda)\right).\Box\end{array}

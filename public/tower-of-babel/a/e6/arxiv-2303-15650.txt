•

•

5.
Knot Resultant Combinatorics

Note 5.1
.

We may have a binomial coefficient
{n\choose k}
where it is possible, in application, for
k>n
. In these cases, the binomial coefficient should be understood as equal to zero.

Theorem 5.2
.

A rational link
L=N[\pm a_{1}\;\pm a_{2}\;...\;\pm a_{n}]
has at most
{n\choose 1}+{n-1\choose 2}
unique resultants
R
with
c(R)=c(L)-2
and at most
{n\choose 1}{n-1\choose 1}+{n-1\choose 3}
unique resultants with
c(R)=c(L)-3
.

Corollary 5.3
.

L
has at most
\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n+2)
unique resultants
R
with
c(R)=c(L)-2
and
\frac{1}{6}(n^{3}+5n-6)
unique resultants with
c(R)=c(L)-3
.

Theorem 5.4
.

A rational shadow
L=N[a_{1}^{\prime}\;a_{2}^{\prime}\;...\;a_{n}^{\prime}]
has at most
\lceil\frac{a_{1}+1}{2}\rceil\prod_{i=2}^{n}(a_{i}+1)
unique resultants.

Proof.

The integer tangle
x^{\prime}
has resolutions with
x-2j
crossings,
0\leq j\leq x\implies
there are
x+1
resolutions of each integer tangle. Our possible resolutions are nearly halved for the first tangle, as it sets the sign convention for the link, i.e.
N[\pm c_{1}\;a_{2}^{\prime}\;...\;a_{n}^{\prime}]
is indistinguishable from
N[\mp c_{1}\;a_{2}^{\prime}\;...\;a_{n}^{\prime}]
as we can freely redefine crossing parity here before assigning any other crossings.
∎

For any link shadow
L
, all we know it has at most
2^{c(L)-1}
unique resultants when we count a knot and its mirror image the same. This theorem gives a significantly sharper upper bound for rational links, yet there is a long way to go. The knot
8_{12}=N[2\;2\;2\;2]
has 11 unique resultants, far fewer than
2\times 3\times 3\times 3=54
, yet still much sharper than
2^{7}=128
. Theorem
2.7
is likely useful for improvements.

We will now explicitly enumerate the number of each link type
L^{\prime}
produced by rational links
L
of lengths 1 and 2. This can be converted to a probability distribution by dividing by the total number of resultants
2^{c(L)}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

D[a_{1}^{\prime}\;a_{2}^{\prime}\;...\;a_{n}^{\prime}]\rightarrow 2^{a_{n}}N[a_{1}^{\prime}\;a_{2}^{\prime}\;...\;a_{n-1}^{\prime}]
,
n\geq 2
.
D[a_{1}^{\prime}]\rightarrow 2^{a_{1}}
unknots.

This notation means we get
2^{a_{n}}
diagrams described by
N[a_{1}^{\prime}\;a_{2}^{\prime}\;...\;a_{n-1}^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

A shadow RI move is an invariant on knot shadows by creating two copies (one for each resolution) of the shadow prior to its application to a straight strand. Applying the denominator to a rational tangle connects the right ends of the
a_{n}
tangle, allowing it to be untwisted
a_{n}
times. For
n\geq 2
, what remains is the rotated version of the numerator closure. For
n=1
, the complicating twists must have been done on an unknot.
∎

Interestingly, links lack many unlink resultants. Unknots are the most likely resultant from

found experimentally all knot shadows with twelve or fewer crossings had a higher unknot probability than any nontrivial resultant probability (they did show the 14-crossing torus knot T(7,3) was a counterexample). Furthermore, we are unaware of any knot shadow where the resultant probability of any nontrivial knot is greater than or equal to 50%.

N[2^{\prime}]

Lemma 5.8
.

For all
k
, there is some
n
such that

2{{n}\choose{\frac{n-k}{2}}}\geq{{n}\choose{\frac{n}{2}}}.

n,k
are even integers,
n\geq k
.

Proof.

Converting to the factorial form of the binomial coefficient, the above inequality holds iff

\displaystyle 2\left[\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)!\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{k}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{k}{2}\right)!\iff

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)...\left(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{k}{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{k}{2}\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{k}{2}-1\right)...\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)

The left polynomial has a leading coefficient two times larger than that of the right and will eventually surpass the right polynomial for large
n
.
∎

Marcinkiewicz multipliers associated with the Kohn Laplacian on the Shilov boundary of the product domain in ℂ 2 ​ n \mathbb{C}^{2n}
Peng Chen, Michael G. Cowling, Guorong Hu, Ji Li

Abstract
. Let M ( k ) M^{(k)} , k = 1 , 2 , … , n k=1,2,\ldots,n , be the boundary of an unbounded polynomial domain Ω ( k ) \Omega^{(k)} of finite type in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} , and let □ b ( k ) \Box_{b}^{(k)} be the Kohn Laplacian on M ( k ) M^{(k)} . In this paper, we study multivariable spectral multipliers m ⁡ ( □ b ( 1 ) , … , □ b ( n ) ) m(\Box_{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\Box_{b}^{(n)}) acting on the Shilov boundary M ~ = M ( 1 ) × ⋯ × M ( n ) \widetilde{M}=M^{(1)}\times\cdots\times M^{(n)} of the product domain Ω ( 1 ) × ⋯ × Ω ( n ) \Omega^{(1)}\times\cdots\times\Omega^{(n)} . We show that if a function F ⁡ ( λ 1 , … , λ n ) F(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}) satisfies a Marcinkiewicz-type differential condition, then the spectral multiplier operator m ⁡ ( □ b ( 1 ) , … , □ b ( n ) ) m(\Box_{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\Box_{b}^{(n)}) is a product Calderón–Zygmund operator of Journé type.

The Shilov boundary

This work leads immediately to optimal estimates for the behaviour of the solution of the Kohn–Laplace equation on the decoupled boundary in

One of the important types of singular integrals on the Shilov boundary

To ease the burden of notational complexity, in what follows we consider only the two parameter case, that is,

We will need the definition of single-parameter Calderón–Zygmund operators on

A continuous function

\displaystyle|K(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq CV(x,y)^{-1};

\displaystyle|K(x,y)-K(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq C\biggl(\frac{\rho(y,y^{\prime})}{\rho(x,y)}\biggr)^{\varepsilon}V(x,y)^{-1}\quad\text{if}\quad\rho(y,y^{\prime})\leq\frac{\rho(x,y)}{2};

\displaystyle|K(x,y)-K(x^{\prime},y)|

with the following properties:

(i)

\displaystyle\langle g\otimes k,Tf\otimes h\rangle=\iint g(x_{1})\langle k,K_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})h\rangle f(y_{1})\,d\mu^{(1)}(x_{1})\,d\mu^{(1)}(y_{1})

for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M^{(1)})
and
h,k\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M^{(2)})
with
\operatorname{supp}f\cap\operatorname{supp}g=\varnothing
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\langle k\otimes g,Th\otimes f\rangle=\iint g(x_{2})\langle k,K_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})h\rangle f(y_{2})\,d\mu^{(2)}(x_{2})\,d\mu^{(2)}(y_{2})

for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M^{(2)})
and
h,k\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M^{(1)})
, with
\operatorname{supp}f\cap\operatorname{supp}g=\varnothing
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\hskip 65.44142pt\|K_{k}(x_{k},y_{k})\|_{CZ}\leq CV^{(k)}(x_{k},y_{k})^{-1};

(iv)

\displaystyle\|K_{k}(x_{k},y_{k})-K_{k}(x_{k},y^{\prime}_{k})\|_{CZ}\leq C\biggl(\frac{\rho^{(k)}(y_{k},y^{\prime}_{k})}{\rho^{(k)}(x_{k},y_{k})}\biggr)^{\varepsilon_{k}}V^{(k)}(x_{k},y_{k})^{-1}

if
\rho^{(k)}(y_{k},y^{\prime}_{k})\leq\dfrac{\rho^{(k)}(x_{k},y_{k})}{2}
;

(v)

\displaystyle\|K_{k}(x_{k},y_{k})-K_{k}(y^{\prime}_{k},y_{k})\|_{CZ}\leq C\biggl(\frac{\rho^{(k)}(x_{k},y^{\prime}_{k})}{\rho^{(k)}(x_{k},y_{k})}\biggr)^{\varepsilon_{k}}V^{(k)}(x_{k},y_{k})^{-1}

if
\rho^{(k)}(x_{k},y^{\prime}_{k})\leq\dfrac{\rho^{(k)}(x_{k},y_{k})}{2}\,.

A product singular integral operator
T
is said to be a
product Calderón–Zygmund operator
if
T
is bounded on
L^{2}(\widetilde{M})
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
5
]
)
.

Suppose that
T
is a product Calderón–Zygmund operator.
Then
T
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\widetilde{M})
when
1<p<\infty
.

For convenience in our application, we also provide the following version, where the difference is replaced by derivative in the regularity condition of the kernel.
This is a special case of the above proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
T
is bounded on
L^{2}(\widetilde{M})
, where
\widetilde{M}=M^{(1)}\times M^{(2)}
.
Suppose further that the distribution kernel
K(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})
of
T
is
C^{\infty}
away from the “cross”

\{(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}):(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{2}-y_{2})=0\}

and satisfies the following additional properties:

(i)
\langle T(\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2}),\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2}\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle\iiiint K(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})\varphi_{1}(y_{1})\varphi_{2}(y_{2})\psi_{1}(x_{1})\psi_{2}(x_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 130.88284pt\,d\mu^{(1)}(x_{1})\,d\mu^{(2)}(x_{2})\,d\mu^{(1)}(y_{1})\,d\mu^{(2)}(y_{2})

whenever
\varphi_{1},\psi_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M^{(1)})
have disjoint supports and
\varphi_{2},\psi_{2}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M^{(2)})
have disjoint supports.

(ii) For all functions
f_{2},g_{2}\in L^{2}(M^{(2)})
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
f_{2},g_{2}
such that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M^{(2)}}\int_{M^{(2)}}(X_{\alpha}^{(1)})_{x_{1}}(X_{\beta}^{(1)})_{y_{1}}K(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})f_{2}(x_{2})g_{2}(y_{2})\,d\mu^{(2)}(x_{2})\,d\mu^{(2)}(y_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 145.10922pt\leq\frac{C\rho^{(1)}(x_{1},y_{1})^{-|\alpha|-|\beta|}}{V^{(1)}(x_{1},y_{1})}\|f_{2}\|_{2}\|g_{2}\|_{2}.

(iii) The kernel
K(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})
satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle|(X_{\alpha}^{(1)})_{x_{1}}(X_{\beta}^{(1)})_{y_{1}}(X_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{(2)})_{x_{2}}(Y^{(2)}_{\beta^{\prime}})_{y_{2}}K(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})|

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\lesssim\frac{\rho^{(1)}(x_{1},y_{1})^{-|\alpha|-|\beta|}\rho^{(2)}(x_{2},y_{2})^{-|\alpha^{\prime}|-|\beta^{\prime}|}}{V^{(1)}(x_{1},y_{1})V^{(2)}(x_{2},y_{2})}.

(iv) Conditions (2) and (3) hold when the indices
1
and
2
are interchanged, that is, if the roles of
M^{(1)}
and
M^{(2)}
are interchanged.

(v) Conditions (2) to (4) hold for the three transposes of
T
, that is, the operators that arise by interchanging
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
, or interchanging
x_{2}
and
y_{2}
, or interchanging both pairs of variables.

Then
T
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\widetilde{M})
when
1<p<\infty
.

Remark 2.4
.

If
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are (single-factor) singular integral operators on
M^{(1)}
and
M^{(2)}
, then
T=T_{1}\otimes T_{2}
satisfies the above assumptions.
Here
T^{\varphi_{2},x_{2}}
is equal to
T_{1}
multiplied by the factor
T_{2}(\varphi_{2})(x_{2})
.

3.
Functional calculus

In this section, our aim is to prove two pointwise kernel estimates for the multipliers where the multiplier function is smooth enough and with compact support.

We begin with two lemmas which state some useful elementary estimates for functions.
For one-parameter version of these estimates, see Street
[
15
,
16
]
.
We write
\mathcal{F}
to indicate the Fourier transformation: subscripts
1
and
2
indicate Fourier transforms with respect to the first or second variables only.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
m:[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
is a function with
\operatorname{supp}m\subset[1/4,4]\times[1/4,4]
, and let
\phi
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\phi(\xi)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $|\xi|\leq 1/4$}\\
1&\text{if $|\xi|\geq 1/2$}.\end{cases}

Set
\psi(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=m(\lambda_{1}^{2},\lambda_{2}^{2})
.

(i) Define
F_{r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\psi(r_{1}\lambda_{1},r_{2}\lambda_{2})
.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon,N_{1},N_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle|F_{r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})|\leq C_{\varepsilon,N_{1},N_{2}}\|m\|_{W^{(\varepsilon+1/2,\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}(1+r_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}(1+r_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}

for all
r_{1},r_{2},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
.

(ii) Define
F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
by the formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\xi_{1},\lambda_{2})=\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{s_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right).

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, any
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon,\sigma_{1},N_{1},N_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle|F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\sigma_{1},N_{1},N_{2}}\|m\|_{W^{(\sigma_{1},\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[(1+s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{(N_{1}+\varepsilon+{1/2})-\sigma_{1}}\right](1+r_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}

for all
r_{1},r_{2},s_{1},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
.

(iii) Define
F_{s_{2},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
by the formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}{F_{s_{2},r_{1},r_{2}}}(\lambda_{1},\xi_{2})=\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{2}}{s_{2}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{2}{\psi}\left(r_{1}\lambda_{1},\frac{\xi_{2}}{r_{2}}\right).

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, any
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, and any
\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon,\sigma_{2},N_{1},N_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle|F_{s_{2},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\sigma_{2},N_{1},N_{2}}\|m\|_{W^{(\varepsilon+1/2,\sigma_{2}),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times(1+r_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\left[(1+s_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}\left(1+\frac{s_{2}}{r_{2}}\right)^{(N_{2}+\varepsilon+{1/2})-\sigma_{2}}\right]

for all
r_{1},r_{2},s_{2},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
.

(iv) Define
F_{s_{1},s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
by the formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{F_{s_{1},s_{2},r_{1},r_{2}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{s_{1}}\right)\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{2}}{s_{2}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{1}r_{2}}\mathcal{F}{\psi}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},\frac{\xi_{2}}{r_{2}}\right).

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, any
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, and any
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},N_{1},N_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle|F_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},s_{1},s_{2},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},N_{1},N_{2}}\|m\|_{W^{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[(1+s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{(N_{1}+\varepsilon+{1/2})-\sigma_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[(1+s_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}\left(1+\frac{s_{2}}{r_{2}}\right)^{(N_{2}+\varepsilon+{1/2})-\sigma_{2}}\right]

for all
r_{1},r_{2},s_{1},s_{2},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
.

Proof.

We first prove (i). Using the fact that
W^{(\varepsilon+1/2,\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
and

\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[1/2,2]\times[1/2,2]
, we see that

\displaystyle|F_{r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq 3^{N_{1}}3^{N_{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}(1+|\lambda_{1}|)^{-N_{1}}(1+|\lambda_{2}|)^{-N_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,N_{1},N_{2}}\|\psi\|_{W^{(\varepsilon+1/2,\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}(1+|\lambda_{1}|)^{-N_{1}}(1+|\lambda_{2}|)^{-N_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,N_{1},N_{2}}\|m\|_{W^{(\varepsilon+1/2,\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}(1+|\lambda_{1}|)^{-N_{1}}(1+|\lambda_{2}|)^{-N_{2}}.

Next we prove (ii).
Fix a function
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\eta(\lambda_{2})=1
for all
\lambda_{2}\in[1/2,2]
.
We consider four cases.

Case 1.

s_{1}\lambda_{1}\leq 1
and
s_{1}\leq r_{1}
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{s_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)e^{i\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\phi(\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|d\xi_{1}.

Now
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[1/2,2]\times[1/2,2]
, so
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\bigl(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},\cdot\bigr)\subseteq[1/2,2]

for all
s_{1},r_{1}>0
and
\xi_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence
\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\bigl(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},\lambda_{2}r_{2}\bigr)=\eta(\lambda_{2}r_{2})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\bigl(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},\lambda_{2}r_{2}\bigr)
, and it follows that

\displaystyle\left|F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left(1+\left|\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right|\right)^{\sigma_{1}-(\varepsilon+{1/2})}\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|\left(\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left|\left(1+\left|\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right|\right)^{\sigma_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|^{2}d\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\left(\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left|1+\frac{s\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right|^{-(1+2\varepsilon)}d\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+|\xi|)^{\sigma_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\xi_{1},r_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

Using the continuous inclusion
W^{\varepsilon+1/2,2}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we see that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\xi_{1},r_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}(\xi_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|(1+|\xi_{2}|)^{\varepsilon+{1/2}}\mathcal{F}{\psi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\big|^{2}d\xi_{2}.

Hence by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle|F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(1+\left|\xi_{1}\right|\right)^{\sigma_{1}}(1+|\xi_{2}|)^{\varepsilon+{1/2}}\mathcal{F}{\psi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\right|^{2}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(1+r_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}\|\psi\|_{W^{(\sigma_{1},\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|m\|_{W^{(\sigma_{1},\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}\left[(1+s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{(N_{1}+\varepsilon+{1/2})-\sigma_{1}}\right](1+r_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}},

where for the last inequality we used the estimate
s_{1}\lambda_{1}\sim 1+s_{1}\lambda_{1}
and
1+{s_{1}}/{r_{1}}\sim 1
.

Case 2.

s_{1}\lambda_{1}\geq 1
and
{s_{1}}\leq{r_{1}}
.
In this case, by elementary calculus,

\displaystyle\left|F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{s_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}^{2}\lambda_{2}\right)e^{i\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\phi(\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\phi(\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{N_{1}}[e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}]d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{N_{1}}\left[\phi(\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right]e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=N_{1}}\left(\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{1+k_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left[\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{N_{1}}\phi(\xi_{1})\right]\left[(\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{k_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi})\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right]e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=N_{1}}\left(\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{1+k_{2}}|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left[\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{k_{1}}\phi(\xi_{1})\right]\mathcal{F}_{1}{\varphi_{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|,

where
\varphi_{k_{2}}
is the function on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
given by

\varphi_{k_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=(-i\lambda_{1})^{k_{2}}\psi(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
.
Much as in Case 1,
\mathcal{F}_{1}{\varphi_{k_{2}}}({s_{1}\xi_{1}}/{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2})=\eta(\lambda_{2}r_{2})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\varphi_{k_{2}}}({s_{1}\xi_{1}}/{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2})
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\left|F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=N_{1}}\left(\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{1+k_{2}}|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}{\varphi_{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=N_{1}}\left(\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{1+k_{2}}|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\qquad\hskip 56.9055pt\times\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left(1+\left|\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right|\right)^{\sigma_{1}-(\varepsilon+{1/2})}\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}{\varphi_{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=N_{1}}\left(\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{k_{2}}|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\qquad\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left[\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\left|\left(1+\left|\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right|\right)^{\sigma_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\varphi_{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\right|^{2}d\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1+\left|\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right|\right)^{-1-2\varepsilon}d\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=N_{1}}\left(\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{k_{2}}|\eta(r_{2}\lambda_{2})|

\displaystyle\qquad\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(1+\left|\xi_{1}\right|\right)^{\sigma_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\varphi_{k_{2}}}(\xi_{1},r_{2}\lambda_{2})\right|^{2}d\xi_{1}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}(1+r_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}\|\varphi_{k_{2}}\|_{W^{(\sigma_{1},\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}(1+r_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}\|\psi\|_{W^{(\sigma_{1},\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|m\|_{W^{(\sigma_{1},\varepsilon+1/2),2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}\left[(1+s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\right)^{(N_{1}+\varepsilon+{1/2})-\sigma_{1}}\right](1+r_{2}\lambda_{2})^{-N_{2}}

where for the last inequality we used the estimate
1+s_{1}\lambda_{1}\sim 1
and
1+{s_{1}}/{r_{1}}\sim 1
.

Case 3.

s_{1}\lambda_{1}\leq 1
and
s_{1}\geq r_{1}
.
This case is similar to Case 2, and we skip the details.

Case 4.

s_{1}\lambda_{1}\geq 1
and
{s_{1}}\geq{r_{1}}
.
Taking
\varphi_{k_{2}}
as in Case 2, we see that

\displaystyle\left|F_{s_{1},r_{1},r_{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{s_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)e^{i\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\phi(\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}d\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=(s_{1}\lambda_{1})^{-N_{1}}\frac{s_{1}}{r_{1}}\left|\int_{|\xi_{1}|\geq{1/4}}\phi(\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{1}{\psi}\left(\frac{s_{1}\xi_{1}}{r_{1}},r_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{N_{1}}[e^{is_{1}\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}}]d\xi_{1}\right|

m^{2}(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})
satisfy
(i) and (ii) of Definition
2.1
.
This, together with the fact that

, implies that both
\pi^{(1)}\otimes m^{2}(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})
and

.
Thus, it remains to show that

satisfies the conditions in Proposition
2.3
.

Let
\omega
be a nonnegative smooth function on
[0,\infty)
supported in

[0,2]
such that
\omega(\lambda)=1
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
Let
\theta(\lambda)=\omega(\lambda)-\omega(2\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
.
Then
\operatorname{supp}\theta\subset[1/2,2]
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\theta(2^{-j}\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\big[\omega(2^{-j}\lambda)-\omega(2^{-(j-1)}\lambda)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-1}\big[\omega(2^{-j}\lambda)-\omega(2^{-(j-1)}\lambda)\big]+\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\big[\omega(2^{-j}\lambda)-\omega(2^{-(j-1)}\lambda)\big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to-\infty}\big[\omega(2\lambda)-\omega(2^{-j}\lambda)\big]+\lim_{j\to+\infty}\big[\omega(2^{-j}\lambda)-\omega(2\lambda)\big]

\displaystyle=-\lim_{j\to-\infty}\omega(2^{-j}\lambda)+\lim_{j\to+\infty}\omega(2^{-j}\lambda)

\displaystyle=0+1=1.

Set
m_{j_{1},j_{2}}({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},{\Box}_{b}^{(2)})=\theta(2^{-j_{1}}{\Box}_{b}^{(1)})\theta(2^{-j_{2}}{\Box}_{b}^{(2)})m({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},{\Box}_{b}^{(2)})
.
Now
m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(0,0)=0
, and it follows that
m_{j_{1},j_{2}}({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},{\Box}_{b}^{(2)})=m_{j_{1},j_{2}}(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})
.
This implies that

m(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})=\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}}m_{j_{1},j_{2}}({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},{\Box}_{b}^{(2)}).

Similarly, define

m_{j_{1}}({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})=\theta(2^{-j_{1}}{\Box}_{b}^{(1)})m({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})

and

m_{j_{2}}({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})=\theta(2^{-j_{2}}{\Box}_{b}^{(2)})m(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(1)},{\Box}_{b}^{(2)}).

Then

m(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})=\sum_{j_{1}}m_{j_{1}}({\Box}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})

and

m(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(1)},\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(2)})=\sum_{j_{2}}m_{j_{2}}(\widehat{\square}_{b}^{(1)},{\Box}_{b}^{(2)}).

Next we need the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\delta=\rho(x,y)
and
N>a\geq 0
.

(4.2)

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{\delta}{2^{-j}}\right)^{-N}\frac{2^{ja}}{V(x,\delta+2^{-j})}\leq C\frac{\delta^{-a}}{V(x,\delta)}.

Proof.

The volume of the ball satisfies the reverse doubling condition (
2.3
) and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{2^{-j}\geq\delta}\left(1+\frac{\delta}{2^{-j}}\right)^{-N}\frac{2^{ja}}{V(x,\delta+2^{-j})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{N}\sum_{2^{-j}\geq\delta}\left(\frac{\delta}{2^{-j}}\right)^{4}\frac{2^{ja}}{V(x,\delta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{N}\frac{\delta^{-a}}{V(x,\delta)}.

On the other hand, since
N>a
,

\displaystyle\sum_{2^{-j}<\delta}\left(1+\frac{\delta}{2^{-j}}\right)^{-N}\frac{2^{ja}}{V(x,\delta+2^{-j})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{N}\sum_{2^{-j}<\delta}\left(\frac{\delta}{2^{-j}}\right)^{-N}\frac{2^{ja}}{V(x,\delta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{N}\frac{\delta^{-a}}{V(x,\delta)}.

The proof of Lemma
4.1
is complete.
∎

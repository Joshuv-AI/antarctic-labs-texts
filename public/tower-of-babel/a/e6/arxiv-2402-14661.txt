State sum invariants of knots from idempotents in quandle rings
Mohamed Elhamdadi, Dipali Swain

Abstract
. We use idempotents in quandle rings in combination with the state sum invariants of knots to distinguish all of the 12965 12965 prime oriented knots up to 13 13 crossings using only 21 21 connected quandles and three quandles made of idempotents in quandle rings. We also distinguish all knots up to 13 13 crossings from their mirror images using the same 24 24 quandles. Furthermore, we distinguish all of the 2977 prime oriented knots up to 12 crossings using only 10 connected quandles and three quandles made of idempotents in quandle rings. This improves a result in [Quandle colorings of knots and applications, J. Knot Theory Ramifications 23 (2014), no. 6, 1450035]. Our computations are achieved with the help of Python and Maple softwares.

Introduction

Quandles are sets with self-distributive binary operations that axiomatize the three Reidemeister moves in classical knot theory
[
EN
,
Joyce
,
Matveev
]
. They have been used extensively to construct invariants of knots in the
3
-space and knotted surfaces in
4

[

,
CEGS
]
. A cohomology theory for quandles was introduced in
[

]
as a variation of rack homology defined by Fenn, Rourke and Sanderson in
[

]
. Two-dimensional cocycles were used to construct extensions of quandles
[
CENS
]
and also allowed to define the quandle cocycle invariant of knots
[
CJKLS
]
. This homology was extended to a homology theory of set-theoretic Yang-Baxter equations in
[

]
.

Quandle rings were introduced in
[

]
as a “linearization” of quandles. Their construction is similar to group rings
[

]
. Quandle rings have been investigated in
[

]
where various properties of quandle rings were established such as decomposition of quandle rings into simple left or right ideals, the study of isomorphisms of quandle rings and a proof that quandle rings are not power-associative rings. In
[

]
the authors investigated the existence of nontrivial zero divisors in quandle rings in parallel to Kaplansky’s study of zero divisors in group rings. It was proved in
[

that integral quandle rings of quandles of finite type that are non-trivial coverings over nice base quandles admit infinitely many non-trivial idempotents, and gave their complete description. In
[

it was shown that quandle rings and their idempotents lead to proper enhancements of the well-known quandle coloring invariant of links. The authors also determined, for quandles of small cardinality, those for which the set of all idempotents is itself a quandle. In
[

26

Review of Quandles, Colorings and State Sum Invariants of Knots

Definition 2.1
.

A quandle is a non empty set

(1)

For every
x\in X,\penalty\ x\ast x=x

(2)

For every

z\ast x=y

(3)

For every
x,y,z\in X,\penalty\ (x\ast y)\ast z=(x\ast z)\ast(y\ast z)

A quandle homomorphism is a map between two quandles

f(x\ast y)=f(x)\star f(y)

S_{y}^{-1}(x)
will be denoted by
x\overline{\ast}y=z\iff z\overline{\ast}y=x

A quandle is completely determined by the set of its right multiplications.

3.
Review of idempotents in quandle rings

Given a
non-trivial
quandle
(X,\ast)
and an associative ring
\mathbf{k}
with unity, we consider a
nonassociative
ring
\mathbf{k}[X]
called the quandle ring. Precisely, Let
\mathbf{k}[X]
be the set of elements that are uniquely expressible in the form
\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}e_{x}
, where
x\in X
and
a_{x}=0
for almost all
x

\mathbf{k}[X]
becomes a ring with the natural addition and the multiplication given by the following rule, where
x,y\in X
and
a_{x},b_{y}\in\mathbf{k}

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}e_{x})\cdot(\sum_{y\in X}b_{y}e_{y})=\sum_{x,y\in X}a_{x}b_{y}e_{x\ast y}.

For more on quandle rings, see for example
[
BPS1
,
EFT
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a quandle and
\mathbf{k}
an integral domain with unity. A non-zero element

is called an
idempotent
if
u^{2}=u
. We denote the set of all idempotents of
\mathbf{k}[X]
by
\mathcal{I}\big(\mathbf{k}[X]\big)
, that is,

\mathcal{I}\big(\mathbf{k}[X]\big)=\big\{v\in\mathbf{k}[X],\;\;v^{2}=v\big\}.

From the equation (
3.1
) it follows that the basis elements
\{e_{x},x\in X\}
are idempotents in
\mathbf{k}[X]
. We call them the
trivial idempotents

A non-trivial idempotent is an element of
\mathbf{k}[X]
that is not of the form
e_{x}
for any
x\in X
. Furthermore, for any quandle
X
and
Y
such that

, we have
\mathcal{I}\big(\mathbf{k}[X])\subset\mathcal{I}\big(\mathbf{k}[Y])

In

]

has a non-trivial idempotent. Based on this, we have the following proposition whose proof is straightforward.

Proposition 3.1

Let

is an idempotent in

A Brief Description of the Algorithm

In this section, we give a brief description of the algorithm. The algorithm was inspired by
http://shell.cas.usf.edu/~saito/Maple/
. The algorithm has three steps

•

Step 1.
Given a quandle X, we check when the set of idempotents
\mathcal{I}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[X]\big)
is a quandle.

•

Step 2.
Using a quandle
X
and its idempotent
\mathcal{I}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[X]\big)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we calculate the colorings of a given knot
K
using its braid representation.

•

Step 3.
After obtaining the coloring, we calculate the State Sum Invariant of the knot
K
using both
X
and
\mathcal{I}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[X]\big)
.

Below we give a quick description of how to find the coloring of a knot
K
and the State Sum Invariant of
K
.

•

Finding the colorings of a knot
K
:

Since every knot is the closure of a braid, to obtain a coloring of the knot, we first color the braid as follows.

(1)

Let
m
be the braid index of the knot
K
.

(2)

Let
\vec{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},...,x_{i},x_{i+1},...,x_{m})\in X^{m}
be the top color of the braid.

(3)

At any
i^{th}
crossing, if it is positive, then
\vec{x}
becomes
(x_{1},...,x_{i+1},x_{i}\ast x_{i+1},...,x_{m})
. if the
i^{th}
crossing is negative, then
\vec{x}
becomes
(x_{1},...,x_{i+1}\bar{\ast}x_{i},x_{i},...,x_{m})
. Let
(y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{m})
be the bottom vector of the braid.

(4)

A solution of the system of equations
y_{1}=x_{1},...,\penalty\ y_{m}=x_{m}
is a coloring of knot
K
by the quandle
X
. We abuse the notation and use
\vec{x}
to denote this coloring.

•

Calculating the State Sum Invariant of
K

(1)

Let
\vec{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},...,x_{i},x_{i+1},...,x_{m})\in X^{m}
be the top color of the braid, thus giving a color of
K

(2)

At a positive crossing with input colors
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
, we assign the Boltzmann weight
\phi(x_{i},x_{i+1})
(refer to the left picture of Figure
2
). Similarly at a negative crossing with output colour
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
we assign the Boltzmann weight
-\phi(x_{i},x_{i+1})
(refer to the right picture of Figure
2
).

(3)

For each fixed coloring, we compute the product
\prod_{\tau}\phi(x,y)^{\epsilon}
over all crossings. The result is an element of group
A
. To obtain the State Sum Invariant of
K
, we sum over all possible coloring obtaining the element
\Phi(K)=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\prod_{\tau}\phi(x,y)^{\epsilon}
of the integral group ring
A
.

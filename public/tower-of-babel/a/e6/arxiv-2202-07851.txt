Mean Convex Smoothing of Mean Convex Cones
Zhihan Wang

Abstract
. We show that any minimizing hypercone can be perturbed into one side to a properly embedded smooth minimizing hypersurface in the Euclidean space, and every viscosity mean convex cone admits a properly embedded smooth mean convex self-expander asymptotic to it near infinity. These two together confirm a conjecture of Lawson [ Bro86 , Problem 5.7] .



1.
Introduction

Regularity theory for minimal hypersurfaces has long been studied in the history. By the landmark work of
[
DG61
,
FF60
,
Sim68
,
Fed70
]
, an area-minimizing
hypersurface
in the Euclidean space or in a Riemannian manifold is always smooth away from some closed singular set of Hausdorff codimension
\geq 7
; Near each singularity, minimizing hypersurfaces are modeled on minimizing hypercones, i.e. minimizing hypersurfaces in the Euclidean space invariant under rescalings.
This regularity result was generalized to stable minimal hypersurfaces without codimension
1
edge-type singularities by
[
SS81
,
Wic14
]
. Later,
[
Sim93
,
CN13
,
NV20
]
carefully studied the structure of singular set, proving that they are in fact codimension
7
-rectifiable. On the other hand,
[
Sim21b
]
constructed examples of stable minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{m+7+1}
(
m\geq 1
) under
C^{\infty}
Riemannian metrics arbitrarily close to the Euclidean metric, whose singular set is any prescribed closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\{\mathbf{0}\}
. This suggests that the singular set can be bad in general.

On the other hand, those singularities of minimizing hypersurfaces seem to be
unstable
under perturbation. In
[
HS85
]
, for a regular minimizing hypercone
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(regular means
C
has only isolated singularity at the origin), it was shown that there exists a unique (up to rescaling) minimizing hypersurface
S
lying on one side of
C
which has
no singularity
. Moreover, such
S
is a radial graph over certain domain on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Based on this,
[
Sma93
]
shown that for a closed oriented
8
-manifold
M
with nontrivial
7
-th homology group and a
C^{\infty}
-generic Riemannian metric
g
, every (homologically) minimizing hypersurface in
(M,g)
has no singularity. Later, this was generalized by
[
CLS20
,
LW20
]
to generic metric on an arbitrary closed
8
-manifold to construct at least one entirely smooth minimal hypersurface (not necessarily area-minimizing).

In this paper, we generalize the existence result by Hardt and Simon to arbitrary minimizing hypercones, not necessarily with only isolated singularity.

Theorem 1.1

(c.f. Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a closed subset such that
C:=\partial E
is a minimizing hypercone in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then there exists
S\subset\text{Int}(E)
a properly embedded smooth minimizing hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with distant
1
to the origin and
C
to be the tangent cone at infinity.
Moreover,
S
is a radial graph over
\text{Int}(E)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

We might expect this theorem to be a first step towards the full generic regularity problem for minimizing hypersurfaces in general dimension.
However, we assert that the uniqueness (up to rescaling) of such one-sided smooth perturbation is still open, even in the splitting case, i.e. when
C=C_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
for some regular minimizing hypercone
C_{0}
. In
[
Sim21a
]
, uniqueness in the splitting case is proved under further strict stability assumption. We also mention that
[
Loh18
]
claimed the existence and uniqueness of such one-sided minimizing perturbation, with a seemingly incomplete proof.
On the other hand, even if the uniqueness (up to rescaling) is established, it’s still unclear how to use it to smoothing a general minimizing hypersurface in a Riemannian manifold.

When
C
is not minimizing in
E
, by a barrier argument, such minimal hypersurface lying in the interior of
E
should not exist.
[
Lin87
]
shown that for
n+1\leq 8
, for a regular minimal hypercone
C=\partial E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, there exists a smooth mean convex hypersurface asymptotic to
C
near infinity.
[
Din20
]
shown that more generally, if the cone
C
is
C^{3,\alpha}
-regular and mean convex of arbitrary dimension, but not minimizing in
E
, then there exists a unique smooth strictly mean convex self-expander sitting inside
\text{Int}(E)
and properly embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Here a self-expander is a hypersurface satisfying the equation,

\vec{H}-\frac{X^{\perp}}{2}=0,

where
\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector and
X^{\perp}
is the projection of position vector
X
onto the normal direction. Self-expander was introduced in
[
EH89
]
as model of long time behavior for mean curvature flow, and also studied by
[
Ilm98
]
as model for mean curvature flow coming out of a singularity. See Section
2.2
for more on geometry of self-expanders.

We also generalize Ding’s result to arbitrary mean convex hypercone, not necessarily regular.

Theorem 1.2

(c.f. Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a closed subset such that
C:=\partial E
is a mean convex hypercone in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and not minimizing in
E
. Then there exists
S\subset\text{Int}(E)
a smooth strictly mean convex self-expander properly embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
C
to be the tangent cone at infinity. In particular,
S
is a radial graph over
\text{Int}(E)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Here, mean convexity assumption for
E
is in viscosity sense, see Definition
2.2
. In particular, Theorem
1.2
applies when
\partial E
is a stationary minimal hypercone, possibly with singularities, which is not minimizing in
E
. On the other hand, uniqueness is again unclear in this non-smooth case.

Combining Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
, we confirm a conjecture of Lawson
[
Bro86
, Problem 5.7]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Given a stable minimal hypercone
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a properly embedded smooth hypersurface with positive mean curvature in
\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n+1}(\mathbf{0})
within Hausdorff distant
\leq\epsilon
from
C\cap\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n+1}(\mathbf{0})
.

A key ingredient of the proof of both Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
is the following divergence of positive super-solution to Jacobi field equation near singularities.

Theorem 1.4

(c.f. Corollary
4.3
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold (not necessarily complete),
\Lambda>0
. Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a two-sided stable minimal hypersurface with singular set of codimension
\geq 7
. Let
u\in C^{2}_{loc}(\Sigma)
be a positive function such that

\Delta_{\Sigma}u+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}u-\Lambda u\leq 0,

on
\Sigma
. Then for every
x\in\mathrm{Sing}(\Sigma)
,

\liminf_{y\to x}u(y)=+\infty.

Such behavior of
u
has been established by
[
Sim08
]
for minimizing hypersurfaces, or more generally, for submanifolds belonging to a
regular multiplicity 1 class
. A phenomenon of similar spirit was also exploited in
[
SY17
]
for minimal slicings and by
[
Wan20
, Lemma 2.14]
to study stable minimal hypersurfaces lying on one side of a regular cone near infinity.
In this paper, we prove it for general stable minimal hypersurfaces, following the strategy of
[
Sim08
]
but more directly by first proving a Harnack inequality for stable minimal hypersurfaces.

Sketch of the Proof of Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
.

Let
C=\partial E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a minimizing hypercone. First one can approximate
E\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}
from interior by mean convex domains
\mathcal{E}_{j}
with optimal regularity. Then consider in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
the Plateau problem of minimizing area among integral currents with boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}_{j}
. Using a similar argument as
[
HS85
]
, one can show that such area-minimizer
S_{j}
is a smooth radial graph over
\mathcal{E}_{j}
and (after subtracting the boundary) lies in the interior of the cone over
\mathcal{E}_{j}
. Moreover, when
j\to\infty
,
S_{j}
converges to the truncated cone
C\cap\mathbb{B}_{1}
. Hence if one rescale
S_{j}
by their distant to the origin, then the rescaled minimizing hypersurfaces will subconverge to some hypersurface
S
minimizing in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and lying in
E
, with distant
1
to the origin. To see
S
is smooth, consider the Jacobi field
\phi=X\cdot\nu_{S}
on regular part of
S
induced by rescaling, here
X
is the position vector and
\nu_{S}
is the unit normal field of
S
pointing away from the cone.
S
being limit of rescaling of radial graphs
S_{j}
guarantees that
\phi\geq 0
; And
\phi
can’t be identically zero since otherwise
S
must be a cone and can’t have distant
1
to the origin. Thus by strong maximum principle,
\phi
is everywhere positive; Also by definition,
\phi
is bounded on each ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Therefore, Theorem
1.4
implies that the singular set of
S
is empty.

When
E
is viscosity mean convex and not perimeter minimizing in
E
, one seeks to minimizing
\mathbf{E}
-functional introduced by
[
Ilm98
, Lecture 2 C]
(where it’s called
\mathbf{K}
-functional) to find a self-expander
S^{\prime}
asymptotic to
\partial E
near infinity. However, since the rescaling of a self-expander is usually not a self-expander, one can not use maximum principle to conclude that
S^{\prime}
is disjoint from its rescalings. Instead, we consider the level set flow
\bigsqcup_{t\geq 0}D_{t}\times\{t\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
starting from
(\text{Int}(E))^{c}
. We still first perturb
E\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}
into a sequence of optimally regular mean convex domain
\mathcal{E}_{j}
in the interior of
E\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and then solve Plateau problem to find self-expanders
S^{\prime}_{j}
lying on one side of the cone
C_{j}
over
\partial\mathcal{E}_{j}
, and asymptotic to
C_{j}
near infinity. Then using avoidance principle for weak mean curvature flow, we argue that the rescalings
\lambda\cdot S^{\prime}_{j}
are disjoint from
\text{Int}(D_{1})
for every
\lambda\geq 1
. Then by taking
j\to\infty
, we obtain an
\mathbf{E}
-minimizing self-expander
S^{\prime}\subset E
asymptotic to
\partial E
near infinity, and disjoint from
\text{Int}(D_{1})
. Using definition of level set flow, we conclude that
S^{\prime}
must coincide with
\partial D_{1}
, and the rescalings
\lambda\cdot S^{\prime}
are still all disjoint from
\text{Int}(D_{1})
,
\forall\lambda\geq 1
. Moreover,
S^{\prime}\neq\partial E
since
\partial E
is not area-minimizing in
E
. These altogether imply that the eigenfunction
\phi:=X\cdot\nu_{S^{\prime}}=2H_{S^{\prime}}
of Jacobi operator of
\mathbf{E}
-functional on
S^{\prime}
induced by rescaling is non-negative and not vanishing identically. Repeat the process above, we get
\phi>0
everywhere and
S^{\prime}
has no singularity.

Organization of the Paper.

Section
2

1.2
and finish the proof of Theorem

in Section

For a hypersurface

-functional of

Such

For a bounded open subset

\delta\mathbf{E}[V;W](X):=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mathbf{E}[e^{tX}{}_{\sharp}V;W]=0;

Let

T:=\inf\{s\in[a,b]:\mathbf{d}(s)=0\}\in(a,b],

Then since
\mathbf{d}_{\partial}(T)>0
and
\text{Int}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(t))
is smooth, we know that
\mathcal{R}:=\mathcal{M}(T)\cap\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(T)\neq\emptyset
is a closed subset contained in
\mathrm{Reg}(\mathcal{M}(T))\setminus\Gamma(T)=\eta_{\sqrt{T}}(\text{Int}(W^{\prime})\cap\Sigma)
. Take a smooth closed neighborhood
\mathcal{N}\supset\eta_{1/\sqrt{T}}(\mathcal{R})
in
\Sigma\cap\text{Int}(W^{\prime})
, we know that
\sqcup_{t\in[a,T]}\eta_{\sqrt{t}}(\mathcal{N})\times\{t\}
and restriction of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
on
[a,T]
are two classical flows over
[a,T]
which intersects along
t=T
but not intersects along the heat boundary. This violates the maximum principle for classical flows
[
Whi95
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Note that a more general result can be proved for integral Brakke motions with codimension
1
restricted to a spacetime closed subset, using the same argument as
[
Ilm94
, 10.5]
. But we don’t need it in this paper.

3.
Harnack Inequality for Minimal Hypersurfaces

By
[
SS81
]
, the convergence of stable minimal hypersurfaces might result in multiplicity in the limit. The goal of this section is to show that if a stable minimal hypersurface is away from higher multiplicity at scale
1
, then so is it in every smaller scales.
n\geq 3
.

Denote
\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2}
to be the space of hypersurfaces with at least one piece of higher multiplicity , more precisely,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2}:=\Big\{V\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{V}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{4}^{n+1}):

\displaystyle\ V=\sum_{i\geq 1}m_{i}|\Sigma_{i}|\text{ for some disjoint minimal hypersurfaces }

\displaystyle\ \Sigma_{i}\subset(\mathbb{B}_{4},g)\text{ and }m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }\|g-g_{Euc}\|_{C^{3,1/2}}\leq 1/10,

\displaystyle\ m_{1}\geq 2\text{ and }\Sigma_{1}\cap\text{Clos}(\mathbb{B}_{2})\neq\emptyset\ \Big\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda>0
,
\epsilon>0
. There exists
\delta_{1}(n,\Lambda)>0
and
\Psi(\epsilon|n,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
with the following property.
If
\Sigma\subset(\mathbb{B}_{4}^{n+1},g)
is a stable minimal hypersurface with

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|g-g_{Euc}\|_{C^{4}}\leq\delta_{1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}(\Sigma)\leq\Lambda,

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(|\Sigma|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq\epsilon.

Then for every
r\in(0,1/4)
and
x\in\text{spt}(\Sigma)\cap\mathbb{B}_{1}
, we have

\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{x,r^{-1}}(\Sigma)|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq\Psi(\epsilon|n,\Lambda).

Here,
\Psi(\epsilon|n,\Lambda)
denotes a function in
\epsilon,n,\Lambda
, which tends to
0
when
n,\Lambda
are fixed and
\epsilon\to 0
.

To prove Proposition
3.1
, we need the following preparations.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
\Lambda>0
, there exists
\delta_{2}(n,\Lambda)>0
such that for every stable minimal hypercone
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\|C\|(\mathbb{B}_{1})\leq\Lambda
, we have

\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|C|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq 10\delta_{2}.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise, there exist stable minimal hypercones
C_{j}
with uniformly bounded density at origin such that
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|C_{j}|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\to 0
. Then by
[
SS81
]
and definition of
\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2}
we have,
|C_{j}|\to m|C_{\infty}|
for some stable minimal hypercone
C_{\infty}
and integer
m\geq 2
. Also by the same argument as
[
Sha17
, Section 4, Claim 5 & 6]
we see that
C_{j}
induces a positive homogeneous degree
1
Jacobi field
u\in C_{loc}^{\infty}(C_{\infty})
.

The goal next is to show that such Jacobi field do not exist.
Let
\Gamma:=C_{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and since
u
is homogeneous degree
1
, we can write
u(r\omega)=r\cdot v(\omega)
,
r>0
,
\omega\in\Gamma
. By rewriting the Jacobi field equation in polar coordinates we derive

\displaystyle 0=(\Delta_{C_{\infty}}+|A_{C_{\infty}}|^{2})u

\displaystyle=[\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{n-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}(\Delta_{\Gamma}+|A_{\Gamma}|^{2})](r\cdot v)

\displaystyle=r^{-1}\cdot[\Delta_{\Gamma}v+(|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}+n-1)v],

where
\Delta_{\Gamma}v+|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}+n-1
is the Jacobi operator of
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Since
v>0
, this implies
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a stable minimal hypersurface, which is impossible.
∎

The following Almost Cone Rigidity Lemma follows directly from Schoen-Simon’s Compactness Theorem
[
SS81
, Theorem 2]
and monotonicity of area.

Lemma 3.3

(Almost Cone Rigidity)
.

For every
\epsilon>0
and
\Lambda>0
, there exists
\delta_{3}(\epsilon,\Lambda,n)\in(0,1)
such that if

\Sigma\cap\mathbb{B}_{1}\neq\emptyset
and

\displaystyle\|g-g_{Euc}\|_{C^{4}}\leq\delta_{3},

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|(\mathbb{B}_{4})\leq\Lambda,

\displaystyle\theta_{g}(\mathbf{0},4;\|\Sigma\|)-\theta_{g}(\mathbf{0},1;\|\Sigma\|)\leq\delta_{3}.

Then there exists a stable minimal hypercone
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}(|\Sigma|,m|C|)\leq\epsilon
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Take
\delta_{1}=\delta_{3}(\delta_{2},n,\Lambda)/10
, given by Lemma
3.2
and
3.3

Suppose for contradiction, there exists some
\epsilon>0
and stable minimal hypersurfaces

x_{j}\in\text{spt}(\Sigma_{j})\cap\mathbb{B}_{1}
and

,
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\Sigma_{j}|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq\epsilon
but
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{x_{j},r_{j}^{-1}}(\Sigma_{j})|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\to 0
when
j\to\infty
. By unique continuation for stable minimal hypersurfaces,
r_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. By possibly replace
\epsilon
by a smaller
\epsilon^{\prime}
and translate-rescale
\Sigma
, WLOG

.

For each

Since

\Sigma_{j}

\sharp I_{j}\leq C(\Lambda,n)

\displaystyle I_{j}=:\{0=\alpha_{j}^{(0)}<\alpha_{j}^{(1)}<...<\alpha_{j}^{(\sharp I_{j}-1)}=+\infty\};

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}^{k}:=+\infty,\ \text{ for }k\geq\sharp I_{j}.

Consider

\displaystyle i_{0}:=\sup\Big\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:\text{ for every sequence }

\displaystyle\{0<s_{j}\in[4^{-\alpha_{j}^{(i)}-1},1/2)\}_{j\geq 1},

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{\mathbf{0},s_{j}^{-1}}(\Sigma_{j})|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})>0\Big\}.

By our contradiction assumption,
0\leq i_{0}<C(\Lambda,n)
and for infinitely many

,
\alpha_{j}^{i_{0}}<+\infty
. Also by passing to a subsequence in
j
, suppose
0<s_{j}\in[4^{-\alpha_{j}^{(i_{0}+1)}-1},4^{-\alpha_{j}^{(i_{0})}-1})
such that
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{s_{j}^{-1}}(\Sigma_{j})|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\to 0
when
j\to\infty
.

Denote for simplicity
\hat{\Sigma}_{j}:=\eta_{4^{\alpha_{j}^{(i_{0})}+1}}(\Sigma_{j})
, by definition of
i_{0}
and Schoen-Simon’s Compactness Theorem
[
SS81
]
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\hat{\Sigma}_{j}|\to|\hat{\Sigma}_{\infty}|\ \ \ \text{ in }\mathbb{B}_{2},

for some multiplicity
1
stable minimal hypersurface
\hat{\Sigma}_{\infty}
in
\mathbb{B}_{2}
; Also,
\hat{s}_{j}:=s_{j}\cdot 4^{\alpha_{j}^{(i_{0})}+1}\in(0,1)
satisfies

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(|\eta_{\hat{s}_{j}^{-1}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{j})|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\to 0\ \ \ \text{ as }j\to\infty.

And by definition of
I_{j}
, for every integer
l\in[0,-\log_{4}(\hat{s}_{j})-2)
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\theta_{g_{j}}(\mathbf{0},4^{-l};\|\hat{\Sigma}_{j}\|)-\theta_{g_{j}}(\mathbf{0},4^{-l-1};\|\hat{\Sigma}_{j}\|)\leq\delta_{1}.

By (
3.2
) and (
3.3
),
\hat{s}_{j}\searrow 0
; By (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.3
, for every
j\geq 1
and
l\in[1,-\log_{4}(\hat{s}_{j})-1)
, there exists some stable minimal hypercone
C_{j}^{(l)}
and integer
m_{j}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{4^{l}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{j})|,m^{(l)}_{j}|C_{j}^{(l)}|)\leq\delta_{2}.

On the other hand, recall by
[
Ilm96
]
, each tangent cone of a stable minimal hypersurface is a multiplicity
1
stable hypercone. Hence, by Lemma
3.2
, there exists some integer
l_{0}=l_{0}(\hat{\Sigma}_{\infty})>>1

\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{4^{l_{0}}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{\infty})|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq 9\delta_{2}.

And since
|\hat{\Sigma}_{j}|\to|\hat{\Sigma}_{\infty}|
, we can choose
j_{0}>>1
such that for every

s_{j}<4^{-l_{0}-3}
and

\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{4^{l_{0}}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{j})|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq 8\delta_{2}

Combine this with (

\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}(m^{(l_{0})}_{j}|C^{(l_{0})}_{j}|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq 7\delta_{2}
, and hence

.
Moreover, recall for every two integral varifold

\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}((\eta_{\lambda})_{\sharp}V_{1},(\eta_{\lambda})_{\sharp}V_{2})\leq\lambda\cdot\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}(V_{1},V_{2}).

Thus combine with (
3.5
) we see,

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}(m^{(l)}_{j}|C^{(l)}_{j}|,m^{(l+1)}_{j}|C^{(l+1)}_{j}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}(m^{(l)}_{j}|C^{(l)}_{j}|,|\eta_{4^{l+1}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{j})|)+\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}(|\eta_{4^{l+1}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{j})|,m^{(l+1)}_{j}|C^{(l+1)}_{j}|)\leq 4\delta_{2}+\delta_{2}=5\delta_{2}.

Therefore, using Lemma
3.2
and starting at
l_{0}
, we can prove inductively that
m^{(l)}_{j}=1
for integer

and again by Lemma

and (

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\eta_{\hat{s}_{j}^{-1}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{j})|,\mathscr{M}^{\geq 2})\geq\delta_{2}>0

).
∎

Corollary 3.4

(Neumann-Sobolev Inequality)
.

Let
\epsilon,\Lambda>0
;
\delta_{1}
be in Proposition
3.1
. Then there exists
\beta(n,\epsilon,\Lambda)\in(0,1/2)
,
C(n,\epsilon,\Lambda)>0
with the following property.
If
\Sigma
is a stable minimal hypersurface in
(\mathbb{B}_{4}^{n+1},g)
satisfying (
3.1
). Then for every
f\in W^{1,1}(\Sigma)
, we have

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-k\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(\mathbb{B}_{\beta};\|\Sigma\|)}\leq C\cdot\int_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}|\nabla_{\Sigma}f|\ d\|\Sigma\|.

Proof.

We shall first show the following,

Claim.
There exists
\beta(n,\epsilon,\Lambda)\in(0,1/2)
,
\tilde{C}(n,\epsilon,\Lambda)>0
with the following property.
If
\Sigma
is a stable minimal hypersurface in
(\mathbb{B}_{4}^{n+1},g)
satisfying (
3.1
);
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathbf{I}^{n}(\mathbb{B}_{4})
are integral currents such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle[\Sigma]=T_{1}+T_{2};

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|=\|T_{1}\|+\|T_{2}\|.

Then we have for every
x\in\text{spt}(\Sigma)\cap\mathbb{B}_{1}
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(\partial T_{1})=\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(\partial T_{2})\geq\tilde{C}^{-1}\cdot min\{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{\beta}(x)}(T_{1}),\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{\beta}(x)}(T_{2})\}^{1-1/n}.

Proof of Claim.
By Lemma
3.2
, by a translation and rescaling of
\Sigma
and considering a smaller
\epsilon
, WLOG
x=\mathbf{0}
. Suppose the Claim fails, then there are stable minimal hypersurfaces
\Sigma^{j}
satisfying (
3.1
) and integral currents
T^{j}_{1},T^{j}_{2}\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{4})
such that
[\Sigma^{j}]=T^{j}_{1}+T^{j}_{2}
,
\|\Sigma^{j}\|=\|T^{j}_{1}\|+\|T^{j}_{2}\|
and that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(\partial T^{j}_{1})=\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(\partial T^{j}_{2})<\frac{1}{j}\cdot min\{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{j^{-1}}}(T^{j}_{1}),\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{j^{-1}}}(T^{j}_{2})\}^{1-1/n}.

Also by slicing Theorem
[
Sim83
]
, for each
T=T^{j}_{i}
(

,

) and a.e.

\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(\partial(T\llcorner\mathbb{B}_{s}))\leq\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{s}}(\partial T)+\frac{d}{ds}\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{s}}(T).

Combined with (
3.8
) and Micheal-Simon inequality
[
Sim83
, Theorem 18.6]
, this implies

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{s}}(T)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\frac{S(n)}{j}\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{s}}(T)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+\frac{d}{ds}\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{s}}(T),\ \ \ \text{ for a.e. }t\in(j^{-1},1).

Take
j\geq 10S(n)
in (
3.9
) and integrate over
(j^{-1},t)
, we derive

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{t}}(T^{j}_{i})\geq\delta(n)\cdot(t-j^{-1})^{n},\ \ \ \forall t\in(j^{-1},1).

By Proposition
3.1
and Schoen-Simon compactness, when
j\to\infty
,
|\eta_{\mathbf{0},j}(\Sigma^{j})|\to|\Sigma^{\infty}|
in
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{V}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some multiplicity
1
stable minimal hypersurface
\Sigma^{\infty}
. Moreover, since the tangent cone

of
\Sigma^{\infty}
at infinity is also some rescaled limit of
\Sigma^{j}
, we must have by Proposition
3.1
that
C
is of multiplicity
1
and hence
\Sigma^{\infty}
is connected.

By (
3.8
), Fleming-Federer Compactness Theorem and area monotonicity formula for minimal hypersurfaces,

\mathbf{F}((\eta_{\mathbf{0},j})_{\sharp}T^{j}_{i},T^{\infty}_{i})\to 0,

for some
T^{\infty}_{i}\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
(
i=1,2
) such that

\displaystyle\partial T^{\infty}_{1}=\partial T^{\infty}_{2}=0;

\displaystyle T^{\infty}_{1}+T^{\infty}_{2}=[\Sigma^{\infty}],

\displaystyle\|T^{\infty}_{1}\|+\|T^{\infty}_{2}\|=\|\Sigma^{\infty}\|.

And by (
3.10
),
\text{spt}(T^{\infty}_{1})\cap\mathbb{B}_{2}\neq\emptyset
,
\text{spt}(T^{\infty}_{2})\cap\mathbb{B}_{2}\neq\emptyset
. But by constancy Theorem
[
Sim83
, Theorem 26.27]
, this is impossible for a connected stable minimal hypersurface

. Hence the Claim is proved.

Now to prove Corollary
3.4
, suppose WLOG
f\in C^{1}(\Sigma)
, and denote for simplicity
p:=n/(n-1)
. Choose
k\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{\beta}}(\{f>k\}),\ \mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{\beta}}(\{f<k\})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{\beta}}(\Sigma).

Let

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{4}\cap\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}f^{+}|\ d\|\Sigma\|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(\partial A^{+}(t))\ dt

.

By a slight modification, a similar Neumann-Sobolev Inequality and Harnack inequality still holds for minimal hypersurfaces with bounded index, where the constant will also depend on the index upper bound.

on the other end.

4.
Growth Rate Lower Bound for Positive Jacobi Fields

The goal of this section is to prove the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
\Lambda>0
,
n\geq 7
;

\gamma_{n}:=-\frac{n-2}{2}+\sqrt{(\frac{n-2}{2})^{2}-n+1}

For every
\gamma>\gamma_{n}
, there exists
C=C(\epsilon,\Lambda,n,\gamma)>0
and
\tau_{0}(\epsilon,\Lambda,n,\gamma)\in(0,1/2)
such that the following hold.

Let
\Sigma\subset(\mathbb{B}_{4}^{n+1},g)
be a two-sided stable minimal hypersurface satisfying (
3.1
);

u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\Sigma)
be a positive super-solution of
(\Delta_{\Sigma}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-\Lambda)u=0
, i.e.

\int_{\Sigma}\nabla_{\Sigma}u\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\xi+(\Lambda-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2})u\xi\ \geq 0,\ \ \ \forall\xi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}).

Then for every
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(\Sigma)\cap\mathbb{B}_{1}
and every
r\in(0,\tau_{0})
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\inf_{\Sigma\cap\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u\geq C\cdot\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma\cap\mathbb{B}_{\tau_{0}}(p))}\cdot r^{\gamma}.

Note that the exponent
\gamma_{n}
is optimal and can be realized by a homogeneous positive Jacobi field on Simons cone.

The key for the proof is the following Growth Lemma. Let

\mathscr{C}_{n}^{=1}(\Lambda):=\{|C|:C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\text{ stable minimal hypercone with }\|C\|(\mathbb{B}_{4})\leq\Lambda\}\setminus\{\text{hyperplanes}\},

be the space of multiplicity
1
varifolds associated to nontrivial stable minimal hypercones. For a function
\phi
over
\Sigma
,
p>0
and a domain
\Omega\subset\Sigma
, let

\|\phi\|_{L^{p}_{*}(\Omega;\|\Sigma\|)}:=\big(\|\Sigma\|(\Omega)^{-1}\cdot\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{p}\ d\|\Sigma\|\big)^{1/p}.

Lemma 4.2

(Growth Lemma)
.

Let
\Lambda>0
,
\gamma>\gamma_{n}
be fixed as in Prop
4.1
. There exists
\delta(n,\Lambda,\gamma)\in(0,1)
and
r_{0}(n,\Lambda,\gamma)\in(0,1/2)
with the following property.

Let
\Sigma\subset(\mathbb{B}_{4r_{0}^{-1}},g)
be a two-sided stable minimal hypersurface with normal field
\nu
, satisfying

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sing}(\Sigma)\ni\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle\|g-g_{Euc}\|_{C^{4}}\leq\delta,

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mathbb{B}_{4}}(|\Sigma|,\mathscr{C}_{n}^{=1}(\Lambda))\leq\delta.

Let
0<u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\Sigma)
be a positive supersolution of
\Delta_{\Sigma}u+(|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-\delta)u=0
in the distribution sense on
\Sigma
, in other words,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int\nabla_{\Sigma}u\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\xi+(\delta-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2})u\xi\ d\|\Sigma\|\geq 0,\ \ \ \forall\xi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}).

Then
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{1};\|\Sigma\|)
and

\|u\|_{L^{1}_{*}(\mathbb{B}_{r};\|\Sigma\|)}\geq\|u\|_{L^{1}_{*}(\mathbb{B}_{1};\|\Sigma\|)}\cdot r^{\gamma},\ \ \ \forall r\in[r_{0}/2,r_{0}].

In
[
Sim08
, Theorem 1]
, a general Growth Theorem is established for multiplicity
1
class of minimal submanifolds. It follows directly from Proposition
3.1

B.2
) and the following Lemma
B.2
, the mean curvature
H_{\partial R}(y^{\prime})
of
\partial R
at
y^{\prime}
satisfies,

H_{\partial R}(y^{\prime})\geq\tilde{H}_{j}(y)-\bar{C}(n,g)\cdot d_{0}\geq\epsilon-\bar{C}(n,g)\cdot j_{\epsilon}^{-1},

where recall the mean curvature of
\partial[\tilde{Q}_{j}]
at
y
is
\tilde{H}_{j}(y)=\epsilon
. Choosing
j_{\epsilon}>\bar{C}(n,g)\cdot\epsilon^{-1}
, we have
H_{\partial R}(y^{\prime})>0
, violates the viscosity mean convexity of
E
and hence is a contradiction. This finish the proof of Claim 2.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\{g^{t}\}_{t\in(-1,1)}
be a smooth family of Riemannian metric on
L:=\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n}(\mathbf{0})
(not necessarily complete); Let
\{A^{t}:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(g^{t})\}_{t\in(-1,1)}
be the family of symmetric 2-tensor of derivative of
g^{t}
. Assume that
A^{0}(\mathbf{0})=0
.

Consider on the cylinder
L\times(-1,1)
, the metric
g:=g^{t}\oplus dt^{2}
. Assume that the Riemannian curvature tensor of
g
satisfies
|Rm_{g}|\leq 1/2n
on
L\times(-1,1)
.

Let
u
be a
C^{2}
-function on some small ball
\mathbb{B}_{r}(\mathbf{0})\subset L
taking value in
(-1,1)
such that
u(\mathbf{0})=:a
and
du(\mathbf{0})=0
. Let
\Gamma_{u}:=\{(z,u(z)):z\in\mathbb{B}_{r}\}\subset L\times(-1,1)
be a
C^{2}
hypersurface. Let
\{z^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
be a normal coordinates of
g^{0}
defined near
\mathbf{0}
on
L
.
Then the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{u}
at
(\mathbf{0},a)
with respect to the upper-pointed normal field is given by

H_{\Gamma_{u}}(\mathbf{0},a)=(\nabla^{2}_{g_{0}}u(\mathbf{0})-A^{a}(\mathbf{0}))_{ij}\cdot g_{a}(\mathbf{0})^{ij},

In particular, the difference between mean curvature of
\Gamma_{u}
at
(\mathbf{0},a)
and
\Gamma_{u-a}
at
(\mathbf{0},0)
has estimate

|H_{\Gamma_{u}}(\mathbf{0},a)-H_{\Gamma_{u-a}}(\mathbf{0},0)|\leq C(n)(a^{2}|A_{\Gamma_{u-a}}|(\mathbf{0},0)+a).

Proof.

First note that
\{z^{1},...,z^{n},t\}
is a local coordinates of
L\times(-1,1)
with the coordinate vector fields denoted by
\{\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n},\partial_{t}\}
. By
[
Pet06
]
, under this,

\displaystyle 2A^{t}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}g^{t}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})=\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{t}}(g(\partial_{i},\partial_{j}))=2g(\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{i}}\partial_{t},\partial_{j});

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}A^{t}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g(\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{t}}\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{i}}\partial_{t},\partial_{j})+\frac{1}{2}g(\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{t}}\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{j}}\partial_{t},\partial_{i})+g(\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{i}}\partial_{t},\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{j}}\partial_{t})

\displaystyle=Rm_{g}(\partial_{t},\partial_{i},\partial_{t},\partial_{j})+A^{t}(\partial_{i},\partial_{k})A^{t}(\partial_{j},\partial_{l})g_{t}^{kl},

where
[g_{t}^{kl}]
is the inverse of matrix
[g^{t}_{ij}]
. In particular, by a bootstrap argument, we have

(B.3)

\displaystyle|A^{t}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})|\leq\frac{t}{2},

\displaystyle|g^{t}_{ij}-\delta_{ij}|\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}.

Let
\Phi_{u}:z\mapsto(z,u(z))
be a parametrization of
\Gamma_{u}
. Then the upward pointed normal field of
\Gamma_{u}
at
(\mathbf{0},a)
is

\nu^{u}(\mathbf{0},a)=\frac{-\partial_{i}ug_{u}^{ij}\partial_{j}+\partial_{t}}{\sqrt{1+\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}ug_{u}^{ij}}}=\partial_{t};

The induced metric of
\Gamma_{u}
under this parametrization is

g(\partial_{i}\Phi_{u},\partial_{j}\Phi_{u})=g(\partial_{i}+\partial_{i}u\cdot\partial_{t},\partial_{j}+\partial_{j}u\cdot\partial_{t});

And the second fundamental form of
\Gamma_{u}
at
(\mathbf{0},a)
is

A_{\Gamma_{u}}(\partial_{i}\Phi_{u},\partial_{j}\Phi_{u})|_{(\mathbf{0},a)}=\langle\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{i}+\partial_{i}u\cdot\partial_{t}}(\partial_{j}+\partial_{j}u\cdot\partial_{t}),\nu^{u}\rangle|_{(\mathbf{0},a)}=-A^{a}(\mathbf{0})+\nabla^{2}_{g^{0}}u(\mathbf{0})(\partial_{i},\partial_{j}).

Hence the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{u}
at
(\mathbf{0},a)
is

H_{\Gamma_{u}}(\mathbf{0},a)=(\nabla_{g^{0}}^{2}u(\mathbf{0})-A^{a}(\mathbf{0}))_{ij}\cdot g_{a}(\mathbf{0})^{ij};

The estimate on the difference of mean curvature follows from the estimate (
B.3
).
∎

Corollary B.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\subset(\mathbb{S}^{n+1},g_{\text{round}})
be a closed viscosity mean convex subset with connected interior. Then there exists an increasing sequence of closed connected
C^{1,1}
optimally regular mean convex subsets
\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\subset\text{Int}(\mathcal{E})\}_{j\geq 1}
such that when
j\to\infty
,
\mathcal{E}_{j}\to\mathcal{E}
,
\partial\mathcal{E}_{j}\to\partial\mathcal{E}
both in Hausdorff distant sense.
Furthermore, if the topological boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}
is
n
-rectifiable (In particular
\mathcal{E}
is a Caccioppoli set), then we can choose so that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E}_{j})=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E}).

Proof.

Let
d_{\mathcal{E}}:=dist_{\mathbb{S}^{n+1}}(\cdot,\mathcal{E}^{c})
be the distant function to the boundary defined in
\mathcal{E}
.
\tilde{d}_{\mathcal{E}}
be a smooth function on
\text{Int}(\mathcal{E})
such that
d_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\tilde{d}_{\mathcal{E}}\leq d_{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
be a sequence of regular value of
\tilde{d}_{\mathcal{E}}
, and
Z_{j}:=\{\tilde{d}_{\mathcal{E}}\geq\epsilon_{j}\}\subset\text{Int}(\mathcal{E})
are compact smooth domains.

By Theorem
B.1
, there exists
C^{1,1}
-optimally regular closed mean convex subset
Z_{j}\subset P_{j}\subset\text{Int}(\mathcal{E})
. Clearly by definition,
P_{j}\to\mathcal{E}
and
\partial P_{j}\to\partial\mathcal{E}
both in Hausdorff distant sense. Also, let
j_{0}>>1
such that
Z_{j_{0}}\neq\emptyset
and fix
\omega_{0}\in Z_{j_{0}}
; Take
\mathcal{E}_{j}
to be the connect component of
P_{j}
containing
\omega_{0}
, we know that since
\text{Int}(\mathcal{E})
is connected,
\mathcal{E}_{j}\to\mathcal{E}
,
\partial\mathcal{E}_{j}\to\partial\mathcal{E}
also in Hausdorff distant sense.

If further
\partial\mathcal{E}
is
n
-rectifiable, by
[
Fed69
, Theorem 3.2.39]
and co-area formula, there exists
R_{j}:=\{d_{\mathcal{E}}\geq\delta_{j}\}\subset\text{Int}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{P}(R_{j})=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})
. Then by Theorem
B.1
(iii) and upper semi-continuity of perimeter under convergence, we have
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E}_{j})=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})
.
∎

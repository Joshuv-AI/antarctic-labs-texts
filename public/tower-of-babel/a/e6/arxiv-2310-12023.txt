Gravitational Waves from Particle Decays during Reheating Preprint: OU–HET–1206
Shinya Kanemura, Kunio Kaneta

Abstract
Gravitational waves have become an irreplaceable tool for exploring the post-inflationary universe. Their cosmological and astrophysical origins have been attracting numerous attention. In this Letter, we point out a novel source of ultra-high frequency gravitational waves: the decay of particles produced during the reheating era. We highlight the decay of the Higgs boson as a representative case, showing how it yields a testable gravitational wave spectrum by future observations.

II
Inflationary sector

As a viable example of inflation, we consider a Starobinsky-type model
Starobinsky 1980
, whose potential for the inflaton
\phi
is given by

\displaystyle V(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}m_{\phi}^{2}M_{P}^{2}\left(1-e^{-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{\phi}{M_{P}}}\right)^{2},

(1)

where
M_{P}\simeq 2.4\times 10^{18}
GeV is the reduced Planck mass, and
m_{\phi}
is the inflaton mass, which is determined by the amplitude of the curvature power spectrum
A_{S}
as

\displaystyle m_{\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{24\pi^{2}A_{S}}{N_{*}^{2}}M_{P}^{2},

(2)

where
N_{*}
is the number of e-folds when the pivot scale
k_{*}=0.05~{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
exits the horizon during inflation.
From the Planck data
Akrami et al. 2020
, the amplitude of the scalar perturbation is given by
A_{S}=2.099\times 10^{-9}
.
By taking
N_{*}=50
for definiteness, we obtain
m_{\phi}\simeq 3\times 10^{13}
GeV.

After inflation is over at the scale factor
a=a_{e}
, the inflaton starts oscillating about the potential minimum where approximately
V(\phi)\simeq m_{\phi}^{2}\phi^{2}/2
.
The inflaton energy density,
\rho_{\phi}
, follows the Boltzmann equation,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\phi}+3H\rho_{\phi}=-\Gamma^{\rm(E)}_{\phi}\rho_{\phi},

(3)

where
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter, and the energy transfer rate
\Gamma^{\rm(E)}_{\phi}
is defined by
Garcia et al. 2020a
;
Garcia et al. 2020b

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm(E)}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho_{\phi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int E_{n}|{\cal M}_{n}|^{2}d{\rm LIPS},

(4)

with
n
being a label of the
n
-th Fourier mode of the inflaton oscillation.
The decay amplitude of the
n
-th mode is denoted by
{\cal M}_{n}
with the transferred energy
E_{n}=nm_{\phi}
.
The Lorentz-invariant phase-space element
d{\rm LIPS}
should be taken according to the final state kinematics.

We suppose that the inflaton predominantly decays into a pair of the SM fermions (
f
), whose interaction is given by

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\phi,{\rm int}}

\displaystyle=y_{\phi}\phi\overline{f}f.

(5)

We note that the energy transfer rate in the present case coincides with the normal decay width of the inflaton,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\to\overline{f}f}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\phi}^{2}}{8\pi}m_{\phi}.

(6)

The reheating completes when
\rho_{\phi}(a_{\rm reh})=\rho_{\rm rad}(a_{\rm reh})
, where
\rho_{\rm rad}
represents the radiation energy density.
The reheating temperature is estimated as
Garcia et al. 2020a

\displaystyle T_{\rm reh}

\displaystyle\simeq 7.7\times 10^{4}~{\rm GeV}\times\left(\frac{427/4}{g_{\rm reh}}\right)^{1/4}\left(\frac{y_{\phi}}{10^{-10}}\right),

(7)

where
g_{\rm reh}=g_{*}(T_{\rm reh})=427/4
if all the SM particles are in the thermal bath at
T=T_{\rm reh}
.

III
GW spectrum from inflaton decay

Associated with the decay into fermions, a single graviton is emitted via the bremsstrahlung process
Nakayama and Tang 2019
;
Barman et al. 2023a
;
Barman et al. 2023b
.
The relevant diagrams for the three-body decay are shown in Fig.
1
.
The total decay width can then be divided into two pieces,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\phi}^{(0)}+\Gamma_{\phi}^{(1)},

(8)

where
\Gamma^{(0)}_{\phi}\simeq\Gamma_{\phi\to\overline{f}f}
does not involve gravitons in the final state, while
\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi}
has a single graviton in addition to the fermions.
It is convenient to further decompose
\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi}
into two pieces
Barman et al. 2023a
,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm rad}}+\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm GW}},

(9)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm rad}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho_{\phi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int(E_{n}-E_{\rm GW})|{\cal M}^{(1)}_{n}|^{2}d{\rm LIPS},

(10)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho_{\phi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int E_{\rm GW}|{\cal M}^{(1)}_{n}|^{2}d{\rm LIPS},

(11)

with
{\cal M}^{(1)}_{n}
being the corresponding amplitude, where
E_{\rm GW}
is the energy of graviton.

Figure 1:
Graviton bremsstrahlung with the initial state being either the inflaton or the Higgs boson.

The evolution of the graviton energy density,
\rho_{\rm GW}
, follows the Boltzmann equation,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\rm GW}+4H\rho_{\rm GW}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm GW}}\rho_{\phi},

(12)

from which we obtain

\displaystyle a_{\rm reh}^{4}\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}(a_{\rm reh})}{dE_{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle=\int_{a_{e}}^{a_{\rm reh}}da\frac{a^{3}}{H}\left[\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm GW}}}{dE_{\rm GW}}\rho_{\phi}\right].

(13)

We note that
d\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm GW}}/dE_{\rm GW}
is defined as the differential decay width of the inflaton,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm GW}}}{dE_{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{E_{\rm GW}}{\rho_{\phi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int|{\cal M}^{(1)}_{n}|^{2}\frac{d{\rm LIPS}_{3}}{dE_{\rm GW}},

(14)

where
d{\rm LIPS}_{3}/dE_{\rm GW}
indicates the integration for the three-body phase space, without integrating for
E_{\rm GW}
.
The gravitational wave spectrum can be obtained by

\displaystyle h^{2}\Omega_{\rm GW}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho_{\rm cr,0}h^{-2}}\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}(a_{\rm reh})}{d\ln E_{\rm GW}}\left(\frac{a_{\rm reh}}{a_{0}}\right)^{4},

(15)

with
\rho_{\rm cr,0}h^{-2}=8.0992\times 10^{-47}~{\rm GeV}^{4}
being the present critical density.

Based on the above argument, we can readily evaluate the GW spectrum associated with the inflaton decay into fermions.
The graviton
h_{\mu\nu}
is defined by
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2h_{\mu\nu}/M_{P}
with
\eta_{\mu\nu}={\rm diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)
, and couples to the energy-momentum tensor as

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{h_{\mu\nu},{\rm int}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{h_{\mu\nu}}{M_{P}}T^{\mu\nu}.

(16)

For a spin-1/2 particle we have

\displaystyle T^{1/2}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{4}\overline{f}(\gamma_{\mu}\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial}_{\nu}+\gamma_{\nu}\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial}_{\mu})f,

(17)

where
\overline{f}\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial}_{\mu}f\equiv\overline{f}\partial_{\mu}f-(\partial_{\mu}\overline{f})f
.
We have here neglected the terms proportional to
\eta_{\mu\nu}
as it vanishes due to the traceless condition of the graviton.
After summing over all the spin and the polarization states,
we obtain
the differential energy-transfer rate as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}_{\phi\to{\rm GW}}}{dE_{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\phi}^{2}m_{\phi}^{2}}{64\pi^{3}M_{P}^{2}}F(E_{\rm GW}/m_{\phi}),

(18)

where
F(x)=(1-2x)(1-2x+2x^{2})
under the approximation of
m_{f}\ll m_{\phi}
.
Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm GW}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{y_{\phi}^{2}m_{\phi}^{2}\rho_{e}E_{\rm GW}}{160\pi^{3}H_{e}M_{P}^{2}\rho_{\rm cr,0}}\left(\frac{a_{\rm reh}}{a_{e}}\right)^{-3/2}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{\rm reh}}\right)^{-4},

(19)

for
E_{\rm GW}\ll m_{\phi}
, where
\rho_{\phi}(a_{e})\equiv\rho_{e}\simeq 0.175m_{\phi}^{2}M_{P}^{2}
in the Starobinsky model
Ellis et al. 2015
, and
H(a_{e})\equiv H_{e}=\sqrt{\rho_{e}/3}/M_{P}\simeq 7.2\times 10^{12}~{\rm GeV}\times(\rho_{e}/0.175m_{\phi}^{2}M_{P}^{2})^{1/2}
.
Using
E_{\rm GW}=2\pi f(a_{0}/a_{\rm reh})
with
f
and
a_{0}
being the frequency of the GWs in the present universe and the today’s scale factor, respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle h^{2}\Omega_{\rm GW}

\displaystyle\simeq 1.4\times 10^{-36}(f/{\rm Hz})\left(\frac{y_{\phi}}{10^{-10}}\right),

(20)

with the peak frequency being

\displaystyle f_{\rm peak}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\phi}}{4\pi}\left(\frac{a_{\rm reh}}{a_{0}}\right)\simeq 3.7\times 10^{18}\left(\frac{y_{\phi}}{10^{-10}}\right)^{-1}~{\rm Hz}.

(21)

Our estimation is consistent with the results in Ref.
Barman et al. 2023a
.

IV
GW spectrum from the SM Higgs boson decay

We now turn to a discussion of the GWs generated by the Higgs boson decay after the end of inflation.
We suppose that the Higgs doublet
\Phi
is produced from the inflaton decay through the interaction term,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\phi,{\rm int}}

\displaystyle=\mu\phi\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi,

(22)

where
\mu
is a dimensionful coupling.
By denoting
\chi
as a real scalar degree of freedom, the decay rate of
\phi\to\chi\chi
is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\to\chi\chi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu^{2}}{32\pi m_{\phi}}.

(23)

Thus, the branching fraction is obtained as

\displaystyle{\rm Br}_{\chi}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{4\Gamma_{\phi\to\chi\chi}}{\Gamma_{\phi\to\overline{f}f}}=y_{\phi}^{-2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{2}},

(24)

where a factor of 4 counts the number of real scalar degrees of freedom in
\Phi
.
In the following, we take
{\rm Br}_{\chi}
as a free parameter.
We note that the coupling (
22
) induces a temporal contribution to the squared mass of the Higgs boson given by
\mu\sqrt{2\rho_{\phi}/m_{\phi}^{2}}
. In the parameter space of our interest, the temporal mass contribution is sufficiently smaller than
m_{\phi}
, otherwise, the nonperturbative effect becomes important
Garcia et al. 2020b
.

The produced Higgs boson decays into gauge bosons and fermions.
To illustrate how the Higgs boson decay generates GWs, for the sake of brevity, we assume that the Higgs boson predominantly decays into a pair of fermions through the Yukawa interactions.
By writing the relevant Yukawa coupling as
y_{f}
, the decay width of
\chi
into a pair of fermions is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\chi\to\overline{f}f}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{f}^{2}}{16\pi}m_{\chi},

(25)

where
m_{\chi}
is the Higgs boson mass during reheating.
Both
m_{\chi}
and
y_{f}
are to be renormalized values at the relevant scale of the decay at early times, while we take them as free parameters based on the agnostic viewpoint, as explained below.

For simplicity, we consider the case where the squared Higgs boson mass is positive so that the electroweak (EW) symmetry is not broken spontaneously during reheating; namely, the masses of the four real scalar degrees of freedom in
\Phi
are
m_{\chi}
.
The following argument, however, does not depend on whether the Higgs field acquires a vacuum expectation value or not, as long as the nonthermal condition is satisfied, as described in the following paragraph.
Although the Higgs boson mass today is
m_{\chi}\simeq 125
GeV, it can be much larger during reheating because of the unbroken EW symmetry.

When the Higgs mass during reheating is in excess of the temperature, the produced Higgs boson maintains a nonthermal spectrum
Garcia and Amin 2018
;
Ballesteros et al. 2021
;
Garcia et al. 2022
, which is given by

\displaystyle f_{\chi}(p,t)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{24\pi^{2}n_{\chi}(t)}{m_{\phi}^{3}}\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{2p}\right)^{3/2}\theta(m_{\phi}/2-p),

(26)

where
p
denotes the absolute value of the Higgs momentum, and
\theta(x)
is the Heaviside step function.
The number density of the Higgs doublet,
n_{\chi}(t)
, follows the Boltzmann equation given by

\displaystyle\dot{n}_{\chi}+3Hn_{\chi}={\rm Br}_{\chi}\Gamma_{\phi}n_{\phi}(t),

(27)

where the inflaton number density is obtained by solving Eq. (
3
), yielding
n_{\phi}=(\rho_{e}/m_{\phi})(a/a_{e})^{-3}
for
a\ll a_{\rm reh}
.
Integrating Eq. (
27
) from
a_{e}
to
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle n_{\chi}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{2{\rm Br}_{\chi}\Gamma_{\phi}\rho_{e}}{3m_{\phi}H_{e}}\left(\frac{a}{a_{e}}\right)^{-3}\left[\left(\frac{a}{a_{e}}\right)^{3/2}-1\right].

(28)

Note that when the decay rate of
\chi
becomes non-negligible,
n_{\chi}
starts decreasing as
a^{-3}
, resulting in a suppression in the GW production.
The time scale of when this happens can be estimated from
{\rm Br}_{\chi}\Gamma_{\phi}n_{\phi}(a)\simeq\Gamma_{\chi}(m_{\chi}/m_{\phi})n_{\chi}(a)

a=a_{d}
, yielding

\displaystyle\frac{a_{d}}{a_{e}}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\frac{2\Gamma_{\chi}m_{\chi}}{3H_{e}m_{\phi}}\right)^{-2/3}.

(29)

In the following, we require
a_{d}>a_{\rm reh}
, corresponding to
\Gamma_{\phi}m_{\phi}\gtrsim\Gamma_{\chi}m_{\chi}
.

In a similar manner to the inflaton decay, a single graviton is emitted via the bremsstrahlung process in the Higgs boson decay, as shown in Fig.

.
By taking the nonthermal Higgs distribution into account, the Boltzmann equation for
\rho_{\rm GW}
is written as

\displaystyle a^{-3}H\frac{d}{da}\left[a^{4}\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{dE_{\rm GW}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}_{\chi\to{\rm GW}}}{dE_{\rm GW}}\gamma,

(30)

where
d\Gamma^{(1)}_{\chi\to{\rm GW}}/dE_{\rm GW}
is the differential decay rate of the Higgs boson at rest, and

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\equiv E_{\rm GW}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{m_{\chi}}{p^{0}}f_{\chi}(p,t)\simeq 6\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{\phi}}E_{\rm GW}n_{\chi}(t),

(31)

for
m_{\chi}\ll m_{\phi}

The differential decay rate is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}_{\chi\to{\rm GW}}}{dE_{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{f}^{2}m_{\chi}^{3}}{128\pi^{3}M_{P}^{2}E_{\rm GW}}F(E_{\rm GW}/m_{\chi}),

(32)

where the kinetic factor
F(x)
is the same as the inflaton decay width.
Integrating Eq. (
30
) from
a=a_{e}
to
a=a_{\rm reh}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}(a_{\rm reh})}{dE_{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{f}^{2}{\rm Br}_{\chi}\Gamma_{\phi}m_{\chi}^{4}\rho_{e}}{640\pi^{3}H_{e}^{2}m_{\phi}^{2}M_{P}^{2}}F(E_{\rm GW}/m_{\chi})\left(\frac{a_{\rm reh}}{a_{e}}\right)^{-4}

(33)

E_{\rm GW}\ll m_{\chi}

\displaystyle h^{2}\Omega_{\rm GW}\simeq\frac{1}{\rho_{\rm cr,0}h^{-2}}\frac{y_{f}^{2}{\rm Br}_{\chi}\Gamma_{\phi}m_{\chi}^{4}\rho_{e}E_{\rm GW}}{128\pi^{3}H_{e}^{2}m_{\phi}^{2}M_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{\rm reh}}\right)^{-4}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 5.5\times 10^{-22}\left(\frac{f}{\rm Hz}\right)y_{f}^{2}{\rm Br}_{\chi}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{10^{12}~{\rm GeV}}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{y_{\phi}}{10^{-10}}\right)^{-1},

(34)

with the peak frequency given by

\displaystyle f_{\rm peak}

\displaystyle\simeq 1.2\times 10^{17}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{10^{12}~{\rm GeV}}\right)\left(\frac{y_{\phi}}{10^{-10}}\right)^{-1}~{\rm Hz}.

(35)

It is important to notice that in Eq. (
33

d\rho_{\rm GW}(a_{\rm reh})/dE_{\rm GW}
is independent from
a_{\rm reh}/a_{e}

a_{\rm reh}\gg a_{e}

d\rho_{\rm GW}(a_{\rm reh})/dE_{\rm GW}\propto(a_{\rm reh}/a_{e})^{-3/2}

The difference comes from the fact that, in the case of the Higgs boson decay, the inflaton
keeps producing
the Higgs boson during reheating, enhancing the GW production from the Higgs boson decay, and hence the dilution effect for
\rho_{\rm GW}
is mitigated.
Such an enhancement is absent in the case of inflaton decay as the inflaton is the primary source of producing GWs.

On the other hand, by noticing that the maximal GW amplitude is obtained when
a_{d}\simeq a_{\rm reh}
, corresponding to
\Gamma_{\phi}m_{\phi}\simeq\Gamma_{\chi}m_{\chi}
.
In this case, we obtain
h^{2}\Omega_{\rm GW}(f_{\rm peak})\simeq 1.2\times 10^{-19}{\rm Br}_{\chi}

This result implies that the ratio of the energy densities of radiation and GW is crucial, suggesting the following new venue for new physics to be tested by GWs.

V
GW spectrum from the B-L Higgs boson decay

As an example of new physics models, we consider the gauged
B-L
model where U(1)
B-L
is gauged by adding three right-handed neutrinos.
An additional Higgs field
S
, charged +2 under U(1)
B-L
, is introduced to spontaneously break U(1)
B-L
by acquiring a non-zero VEV,
\langle S\rangle=v_{S}
.
In the broken phase where
S=v_{S}+s/\sqrt{2}
, the real part
s
obtains a mass
m_{s}
, while the right-handed neutrinos and the U(1)
B-L
gauge boson acquire masses
m_{N}
and
m_{Z^{\prime}}
, respectively.
For simplicity, we focus on the case where the spectrum of these new particles are
m_{s}\lesssim m_{\phi}\ll m_{Z^{\prime}},m_{N}
and where the inflaton decays only into a pair of
s
.

By integrating out
N
and
Z^{\prime}
,
s
obtains an effective coupling to the Riemann tensor
Ema et al. 2022
,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{s,2h}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\alpha_{3}}\frac{s}{v_{S}}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},

(36)

with
\beta_{\alpha_{3}}=31/11520\pi^{2}
.
Thus, the decay width of
s
into a pair of gravitons is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s\to{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{\alpha_{3}}}{4\pi}\frac{m_{s}^{7}}{v_{S}^{2}M_{P}^{4}}.

(37)

The differential decay width becomes

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{s\to{\rm GW}}}{dE_{\rm GW}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{s\to{\rm GW}}\left(\frac{2E_{\rm GW}}{m_{s}}\right)^{2}\delta(E_{\rm GW}-m_{s}/2).

(38)

On the other hand,
s
may also decay into four SM fermions through off-shell
Z^{\prime}
,
s\to Z^{\prime*}Z^{\prime*}\to f\bar{f}f^{\prime}\bar{f}^{\prime}
, which is the leading decay channel for the given mass spectrum.
Taking the massless limit for the final state fermions, we obtain the decay width as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s\to{\rm rad}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{529}{11796480\pi^{4}}\frac{m_{s}^{7}}{v_{S}^{6}},

(39)

where we have used
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gg m_{s}
.

In a similar manner to the SM Higgs case, we estimate the GW spectrum as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm GW}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{5}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{s\to{\rm GW}}}{\Gamma_{s\to{\rm rad}}}\left(\frac{4\pi f}{m_{s}}\right)^{5/2}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{\rm reh}}\right)^{5/2}\left(\frac{a_{\rm eq}}{a_{0}}\right),

(40)

where
a_{\rm eq}
is the matter-radiation equality time, namely,
a_{\rm eq}/a_{0}\simeq 1/3388

Aghanim et al. 2020
.
At the peak frequency

\displaystyle f_{\rm peak}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{s}}{4\pi}\frac{a_{\rm reh}}{a_{0}}

\displaystyle\simeq 7.3\times 10^{26}~{\rm Hz}\left(\frac{m_{s}}{10^{13}~{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-5/2}\left(\frac{v_{S}}{M_{P}}\right)^{3},

(41)

we obtain

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm GW}(f_{\rm peak})

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{s\to{\rm GW}}}{\Gamma_{s\to{\rm rad}}}\left(\frac{a_{\rm eq}}{a_{0}}\right)\simeq 9.5\times 10^{-6}\left(\frac{v_{S}}{M_{P}}\right)^{4}.

(42)

VII
Discussions and conclusions

We briefly mention possible models that fit our argument.
As we have seen, to maximize the GWs from the Higgs decay,
m_{\chi}
needs to be large enough, i.e., a large hierarchy between the Higgs boson mass during reheating and the EW scale.
This type of hierarchy requires fine-tuning in a context of, for instance, the radiative breaking of EW symmetry in supersymmetric models
Ibanez and Ross 1982
;
Inoue et al. 1982a
;
Inoue et al. 1982b
;
Alvarez-Gaume et al. 1983
.
One of such models is discussed in, e.g., Ref.
Ellis et al. 2018
, where the Higgs boson mass at the EW scale is obtained by the radiative EW symmetry breaking together with cancellations among mass parameters of the order of the grand unification scale.

Finally, we emphasize that the GW spectrum discussed in this Letter is not limited to the decay of the Higgs boson.
As one such example, we have considered the
B-L
breaking Higgs decay, in which the produced GWs may be tested in future observations.
It is also applicable to heavy particles produced during reheating, including those that emerged from supersymmetry and extended Higgs sectors.
The resultant GWs are notably characterized by ultra-high frequencies, which have recently gained increased attention, spurring numerous discussions and proposals for forthcoming experiments (see, e.g., Refs.
Carney et al. 2024
;
Ito et al. 2024
).
Taking particle decay during reheating as a new source of GWs will open up avenues for extensive future research.

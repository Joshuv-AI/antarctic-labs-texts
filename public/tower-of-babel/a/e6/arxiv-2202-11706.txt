Traveling waves of a generalized Rotation-Camassa-Holm equation with the Coriolis effect
N’Gbo N’Gbo Yonghui Xia Tonghua Zhang 1. Department of Mathematics, Shanghai University, Shanghai, 200444, P.R. China 2. Department of Mathematics, Zhejiang Normal University, Jinhua, Zhejiang, 321004, P. R. China 3. Department of Mathematics, Swinburne University of Technology, Hawthorn, Victoria, 3122, Australia

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze the dynamics of a generalized Rotation-Camassa-Holm equation, which is the θ \theta -equation augmented with the Coriolis effect, induced by the earth rotation. The generalized Rotation-Camassa-Holm equation (named as Rotation- θ \theta equation) is a generalization of a family of models (including the Rotation-Camassa-Holm equation for θ = 1 3 \theta=\frac{1}{3} , asymptotic Rotation-Camassa-Holm equation for θ = 1 \theta=1 and the Rotation-Degasperis-Procesi (DP) equation for θ = 1 4 \theta=\frac{1}{4} ). Our study is conducted via the bifurcation method and qualitative theory of dynamical systems. The existence of not only smooth solitary wave solutions, periodic wave solutions, but also peakons and periodic peakon solutions is shown. The chosen values θ \theta , allow us to assess the difference in behavior between the classical θ \theta -equation and the Rotation- θ \theta equation. We conclude that the Coriolis effect does affect the traveling wave solutions. We summarize the bifurcations and explicit expressions of waves solutions in three theorems in Section 4. A conclusion ends the paper.



Keywords:
Rotation-Camassa-Holm equation; solitary wave solution; periodic wave solution; peakon solution; periodic peakon solution.

Introduction

The Coriolis force is an inertial force acting on objects that are in motion within a frame of reference. The deflection of an object due to the Coriolis force is called the Coriolis effect. The Coriolis effect is caused by the rotation of the Earth and is known to perturb wave propagation.
In 1993, Camassa and Holm
[
Camassa & Holm, 1993
]
, derived the famous classical Camassa-Holm (CH) equation, as a model for shallow-water waves. The model is formulated as follow:

u_{t}+2\lambda u_{x}-u_{xxt}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},

(1.1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R},\textit{t}>0
, and
u
, the velocity field, is a function of the position
x
at time
t
(see
[
Lui, 2008
]
). We write
u(x,t)
, depicting the water’s free surface over a flat bottom.
u_{x}
and
u_{t}
are the partial derivatives of
u
, with respect of
x
and
t
.

The extensive literature on the CH-equation contains investigations of every-other aspects of the models via countless methods. For examples, Constantin and Bressan
[
Bressan & Constantin, 2006
]
utilized a transformation method to obtain global conservative solutions for the CH equation. The significance of the CH and DP equations as shallow water waves models was proven in
[
Constantin & Lannes, 2008
]
. The scattering problem of the CH equation was considered in
[
Constantin, 2008
]
. Wei et al
[
Wei et al., 2018
]
studied the travelling wave solutions for a generalized CH equation using the bifurcation method of dynamical system. Further, Du et al
[
Du et al., 2018
]
proved the existence of solitary wave solutions for a delayed CH equation by geometrical approach. Furthermre, Ge and De
[
Ge and Du, 2018
]
studied the shallow water wave equation by singular perturbation approach. Most recently, Chu et al
[
Chu et al., 2020
]
considered the spectral problem for a modified CH equation. Sun et al
[
Sun et al., 2020
]
proved coexistence of the solitary and periodic waves in convecting shallow water fluid.

The importance of the CH equation in fluid dynamics is due to its remarkable properties. Among which, conservation laws, integrability, peakons, soliton solutions
[
Camassa & Holm, 1993
,
Bressan & Constantin, 2006
]
. Gui et al. and Luo et al. in
[
Gui et al., 2018
,
Gui et al., 2019
,
Chen et al., 2018
,
Luo et al., 2019
]
derived a series of shallow-water waves equations with the Coriolis effect, namely, the Rotation-Green-Naghdi (R-GN), the Rotation-Korteweg-De Vries (R-KdV) and Rotation-Camassa-Holm (R-CH) equations. The R-CH equation reads,

\begin{cases}m_{t}+um_{x}+2u_{x}m+ku_{x}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta}u_{xxx}+\frac{\omega_{1}}{\alpha^{2}}u^{2}u_{x}+\frac{\omega_{2}}{\alpha^{3}}u^{3}u_{x}=0,~~~x\in\mathbb{R},\textit{t}>0,\\
m=u-u_{xx},\\
\end{cases}

(1.2)

where the constants appearing in the equation are defined as:

k=\sqrt{1+\Omega^{2}}-\Omega,\alpha=\frac{k}{1+k^{2}},

\beta_{0}=\frac{k(k^{4}+6k^{2}-1)}{6(k^{2}+1)},\beta=\frac{3k^{4}+8k^{2}-1}{6(k^{2}+1)},

\omega_{1}=\frac{-3k(k^{2}-1)(k^{2}-2)}{2(1+k^{2})^{3}},\omega_{2}=\frac{(k^{2}-2)(k^{2}-1)^{2}(8k^{2}-1)}{2(1+k^{2})^{5}}
,
and
\Omega
is the Coriolis frequency caused by the Earth rotation (
[
Ni et al., 2011
]
[
Chu et al., 2020
]
). System (
1.2
) is analogous to the CH-equation, but describing the motion of the fluid with the Coriolis effect from the incompressible and irrotational two-dimensional shallow-water
in the equatorial region. For more details on the equatorial waves modeling and dynamics, one can refer to
[
Chu & Wang, 2020
,
Constantin, 2012
,
Constantin & Johnson, 2008
,
Kirbi et al., 2013
,
Constantin, 2012
,
Constantin & Germain, 2013
,
Hsu, 2015
]
.
We notice that, without the Coriolis effect ( i.e.
\Omega=0
), we have the classical Camassa-Holm with
\lambda
equal to one. In
[
Tu et al., 2019
]
, the authors proved the global existence and uniqueness of the energy
conservative weak solutions in the energy space
H^{1}
of system (
1.2
). Moreover, Fan et al
[
Fan & Yuen, 2019
]
obtained peakon weak solutions for the two component R-CH-equation formulated in
[
Fan, Gao, & Liu,2016
]
. Recently, Liang et al
[
Liang et al., 2020
]
analyzed the bifurcations and exact solutions of an asymptotic R-CH equation considered in
[
Constantin & Lannes, 2009
]
.

The study of the CH-equation brought forward many generalizations of the model. Following these ideas, Liu
[
Lui, 2008
]
derived a type of nonlocal dispersive models, namely, the
\theta
-equation:

\begin{cases}m_{t}+\theta um_{x}+(1-\theta)u_{x}m=0,\\
m=u-u_{xx}.\\
\end{cases}

(1.3)

It is easy to see that for
\theta=\frac{1}{3}
and
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
, the
\theta
-equation, respectively, becomes the CH and DP
[
Degasperis & Procesi, 1999
]
equations. Wen
[
Wen, 2014
]
will later study the bifurcations of traveling wave solutions of Eq. (
1.3
).

Motivated by the aforementioned researches, we aim to investigate the behavior of the
\theta
-equation, at the equator neighborhood by mean of the transformation technique of dynamical
system (
[
Li, 2013
,
Li, 2019
,
Zhang, et al., 2021
,
Zhang & Li, 2017
,
Du & Qiao, 2020
,
Song & Tang, 2017
,
Sun & Yu, 2019
]
).
In this paper, we study the Rotation-
\theta
equation (
\theta
-equation augmented with the Coriolis effect) taking the form of:

\begin{cases}m_{t}+\theta um_{x}+(1-\theta)u_{x}m+ku_{x}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta}u_{xxx}+\frac{\omega_{1}}{\alpha^{2}}u^{2}u_{x}+\frac{\omega_{2}}{\alpha^{3}}u^{3}u_{x}=0,~~~x\in\mathbb{R},\textit{t}>0,\\
m=u-u_{xx},~~~x\in\mathbb{R}.\\
\end{cases}

(1.4)

Notice that, for
\theta=\frac{1}{3}
, system (
1.4
) is exactly the Rotation-CH considered in
[
Gui et al., 2018
]
. For
\theta=1
, system (
1.4
) reduces to the asymptotic Rotation-CH considered in
[
Liang et al., 2020
]
. For
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
, system (
1.4
) reduces to the Rotation-Degasperis-Procesi equation. To study the travelling wave of (
1.4
),
we transform the partial differential equation (PDE) to an ordinary differential equation (ODE). We introduce the new variable
\xi
such that
\xi=x-ct
with
c
denoting the wave speed.
Next, we let
u(x,t)=\phi(\xi)
. Substituting
u(x,t)
in system (
1.4
), we obtain

-c\phi_{\xi}+c\phi_{\xi\xi\xi}+\phi\phi_{\xi}+k\phi_{\xi}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta}\phi_{\xi\xi\xi}+\frac{\omega_{1}}{\alpha^{2}}\phi^{2}\phi_{\xi}+\frac{\omega_{2}}{\alpha^{3}}\phi^{3}\phi_{\xi}=(1-\theta)\phi_{\xi}\phi_{\xi\xi}+\theta\phi\phi_{\xi\xi\xi}.

(1.5)

Integrating Eq. (
1.5
) once, taking the integration constant null, leads to

-c\phi+c\phi_{\xi\xi}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+k\phi-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta}\phi_{\xi\xi}+\frac{\omega_{1}}{3\alpha^{2}}\phi^{3}+\frac{\omega_{2}}{4\alpha^{3}}\phi^{4}=\frac{1}{2}(1-2\theta)(\phi_{\xi})^{2}+\theta\phi\phi_{\xi\xi}.

(1.6)

For convenience, we set
C_{1}=c-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta}
,
C_{2}=\frac{\omega_{1}}{3\alpha^{2}}
,
C_{3}=\frac{\omega_{2}}{4\alpha^{3}}
,and
K=-c+k
.

Then, we pose
\frac{d\phi}{d\xi}=y
and obtain the planar system

\begin{cases}\frac{d\phi}{d\xi}=y,\\
\frac{dy}{d\xi}=\frac{(\theta-\frac{1}{2})y^{2}+C_{3}\phi^{4}+C_{2}\phi^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+K\phi}{\theta\phi-C_{1}}.\\
\end{cases}

(1.7)

Subsequently, we derive the first integral of system (
1.7
). From system (
1.7
), we write

[(\theta-\frac{1}{2})y^{2}+C_{3}\phi^{4}+C_{2}\phi^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+K\phi]d\phi-(\theta\phi-C_{1})ydy=0.

(1.8)

Multiplying Eq.(
1.8
) by the integrating factor
\mu(\phi)=(\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{\big(\frac{1-3\theta}{\theta}\big)}
, we obtain the exact ordinary differential equation

[(\theta-\frac{1}{2})y^{2}+C_{3}\phi^{4}+C_{2}\phi^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+K\phi](\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{(\frac{1-3\theta}{\theta})}d\phi-\theta(\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{(\frac{1-2\theta}{\theta})}ydy=0.

(1.9)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle H(\phi,y)

\displaystyle=\int-\theta(\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{(\frac{1-2\theta}{\theta})}ydy

(1.10)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\theta(\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{(\frac{1-2\theta}{\theta})}y^{2}+p(\phi).

(1.11)

We easily get,
p^{\prime}(\phi)=(C_{3}\phi^{4}+C_{2}\phi^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+K\phi)(\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{(\frac{1-3\theta}{\theta})}
. It follows,

p(\phi)=\int(C_{3}\phi^{4}+C_{2}\phi^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+K\phi)(\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{(\frac{1-3\theta}{\theta})}d\phi.

(1.12)

In the above equation, set
m=\frac{1-3\theta}{\theta}
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Finally, we arrive at the general first integral of system (
1.7
):

\displaystyle H(\phi,y)=-\frac{1}{2}\theta y^{2}(\phi-\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{m+1}+C_{3}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\frac{\binom{m}{n}\phi^{m-n+5}(\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{n}}{m-n+5}

(1.13)

\displaystyle+C_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\frac{\binom{m}{n}\phi^{m-n+4}(\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{n}}{m-n+4}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\frac{\binom{m}{n}\phi^{m-n+3}(\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{n}}{m-n+3}

\displaystyle+K\sum_{n=0}^{m}\frac{\binom{m}{n}\phi^{m-n+2}(\frac{C_{1}}{\theta})^{n}}{m-n+2},

where
\binom{m}{n}=\frac{m!}{(m-n)!}
is the binomial coefficient. One can verify that for
m=0
, i.e.
\theta=\frac{1}{3}
. Eq.(
1.13
) only differs from the first integral in
[
Liang et al., 2020
]
by the value of certain parameters. Thereafter, we consider the cases of
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
,
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
,
\theta=1
in this paper.

The rest of our work is conducted as follows. In section 2, we present the bifurcations of phase portraits of system (
1.7
) and classification of equilibria based on the theory of dynamical systems. In section 3, dynamics of traveling wave solutions are discussed. The main results are given in Section 4. Lastly, a conclusion ends the paper.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the Rotation-
\theta
-equation (the
\theta
-equation with the Coriolis force effect), by mean of the bifurcation method and qualitative theory of dynamical system. Using traveling wave solution transformation, we obtained the first integral for the model we built, which to our knowledge has not been brought to investigation. After qualitative analysis, we were able to obtain phase portraits for certain values
\theta
and the parameters. Smooth periodic and solitary waves, as well as, peakons and periodic peakons solutions were observed. Interestingly, we noticed that the proximity of the singular line alters the nature of an equilibrium point.
One would notice that for
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
, the Rotation-
\theta
equation is reduced to the Rotation Degasperis-Procesi (R-DP) equation. Furthermore, for
\theta=1
, the Rotation-
\theta
equation is similar to the asymptotic R-CH equation in (
[
Liang et al., 2020
]
), only differing by the values of certain parameters.

The chosen values of
\theta
, allow us to assess the difference in behavior between the classical
\theta
-equation and the Rotation-
\theta
equation.
We can conclude that the coriolis effect does affect the traveling wave solutions.
Due to the form of the model and the high power of the hamiltonian, we were unable to compute some of the explicit expressions of the solutions. To the best of our knowledge, there are no mathematical tools to solve the elliptic integrals whose the power is higher than 5 (
[
Byrd & Friedman, 1971
]
). It is an interesting question and remains open.

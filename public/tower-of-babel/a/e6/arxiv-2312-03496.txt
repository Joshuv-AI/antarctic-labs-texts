Variational Formulations of the Strong Formulation - Forward and Inverse Modeling using Isogeometric Analysis and Physics-Informed Networks
Kent-Andre Mardal, Jarle Sogn, Marius Zeinhofer

Abstract




1
Introduction

The recently introduced Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)
[
5
,
13
,
8
]

have popularized least squares formulations of both forward and inverse problems
involving partial differential equations (PDEs)
in strong form
. In this brief contribution we discuss the well-posedness of both cases with PDEs of elliptic type. We address weighting parameters, solution regularity and partial input data. Specifically, we discuss the following forward problem: Find
u
such that

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\argmin_{v}\ L_{1}(v;f,g)\quad\mbox{with }

(1)

\displaystyle L_{1}(v;f,g)

\displaystyle=\|-\Delta v-f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|v-g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

(2)

where
u
is the unknown minimizer,
f,g
are the body and surface forces, respectively and
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
R^{n}
.
Here, it has been observed that for some problems
\alpha
needs
to be chosen carefully
[
11
,
15
,
16
]
.

The second problem we will consider is the following
inverse problem:

\displaystyle u,f

\displaystyle=\argmin_{v,g}\ L_{2}(v,g;u_{d},f_{p})\mbox{ with }

(3)

\displaystyle\ L_{2}(v,g;u_{d},f_{p})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\|-\Delta v-g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\beta^{2}\|g-f_{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(4)

Here
u
and
f
are the unknown state and control,
u_{d}
embodies state observations while
f_{p}
encodes prior knowledge of the control variable. We will in particular consider
partial observations
, meaning that
\Gamma
is a proper subdomain of
\Omega
, and discuss the role of
\beta
and
\gamma
. For this problem, we will assume that the Dirichlet boundary conditions for
v
are exactly satisfied by the discrete ansatz.

The weighted Sobolev spaces are defined as follows. Let
X
be a bounded domain
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
L^{2}(X)
is the space of square integrable functions on
X
and
H^{m}(X)
consists of functions with
m
derivatives in
L^{2}(X)
. Finally, the intersection space

\mu H^{m}(X)\cap\lambda H^{n}(Y)
for
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mu H^{m}(X)\cap\lambda H^{n}(Y)=\{u\ |\ \mu^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m}(X)}+\lambda^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{n}(Y)}<\infty\}

We let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{X}
denote the
L^{2}
inner product on
L^{2}(X)
and set
L^{2}(X)=H^{0}(X)
. For the intersection spaces below we will, with a slight abuse of notation, use the semi-norm of the Sobolev space with the highest regularity. For instance, the inner product of

\alpha L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
is here defined as

(u,v)_{\alpha L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)}=\alpha^{2}(u,v)_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+(\Delta u,\Delta v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We remark that
\alpha L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
is not norm equivalent to the standard
H^{2}
space.
However, in
[
17
]
it is shown that it holds
\|u\|_{\alpha L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)}\geq C\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}
which implies that
\alpha L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space. We also remark that approximation properties are typically stated in semi-norms rather than full norms
[
2
]
and as such the error rates are related to the highest order derivatives.

Below we will also use
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}
to denote
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla
.
Finally,
H^{2}_{0}
denotes the subspace of
H^{2}
with trace zero.
We will in the following assume the following regularity for the problem
-\Delta u=f
in
H^{2}_{0}

[
6
]
:

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(5)

2
Forward problem

Let
V_{\alpha}=\alpha L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
.
A straightforward calculation shows that the minimizer of
L_{1}

is the solution of the following variational problem:
Find
u\in V_{\alpha}
such that

(\Delta u,\Delta v)_{\Omega}+\alpha^{2}(u,v)_{\partial\Omega}=(f,-\Delta v)_{\Omega}+\alpha^{2}(g,v)_{\partial\Omega}\quad\forall v\in V_{\alpha}.

(6)

The corresponding strong problem is a bi-harmonic problem that reads:
Find
u
such that

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}u

\displaystyle=-\Delta f,\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\ \Omega,

(7)

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}u-\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\Delta u

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}g+\partial_{\mathbf{n}}f,\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega,

(8)

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f,\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega.

(9)

Theorem 1

The problem
eq.

1
or equivalently
eq.

6
, corresponds to the bi-harmonic problem

eq.

7
-
eq.

9
and is well-posed
in
V_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

The result is a direct consequence of Green´s lemma.

\displaystyle(-\Delta u-f,-\Delta v)_{\Omega}+\alpha^{2}(u-g,v)_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=-(-\Delta^{2}u-\Delta f,v)_{\Omega}-(-\Delta u-f,\partial_{\mathbf{n}}v)_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle-(\partial_{\mathbf{n}}(-\Delta u-f)+\alpha^{2}(u-g),v)_{\partial\Omega}.

The coercivity of
eq.

6
follows as the bilinear form is an inner product and the right-hand side clearly lies in the dual space of
V_{\alpha}
.

\square

Hence, we remark that in
eq.

8
a weighted average of the
boundary condition and normal derivative of the PDE at the boundary is enforced.

3
Inverse Problem

We discussed the handling of boundary conditions in the previous
section so let us here simplify the situation and only
consider homogeneous Dirichlet conditions, which we assume to be satisfied exactly by the ansatz class.
We recall that
\Gamma
is a subdomain of
\Omega
.
Further, let

V_{\beta}=L^{2}(\Gamma)\cap\beta H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
,
G_{\beta}=\beta L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
M_{\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta}L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then, the minimizers
u,f
of
L_{2}
in
eq.

3
satisfy the following variational problem:
Find
u\in V_{\beta},f\in G_{\beta}\in
such that

\displaystyle(u,v)_{\Gamma}+\gamma^{2}(-\Delta u-f,-\Delta v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(u_{d},v)_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle\forall v\in V_{\beta}

(10)

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}(-\Delta u,-g)_{\Omega}+(\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2})(f,g)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}(f_{p},g)_{\Omega},

\displaystyle\forall g\in G_{\beta}

(11)

The minimizers
u\in V_{\beta},f\in G_{\beta}
of
eq.

10
-
eq.

11
satisfy the following penalized Lagrangian problem problem:
Find
u,f,\lambda\in
such that

\displaystyle(u,v)_{\Gamma}+(\lambda,-\Delta v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(u_{d},v)_{\Gamma},\quad

\displaystyle\forall v\in V_{\beta}

(12)

\displaystyle\beta^{2}(f,g)_{\Omega}+(\lambda,-g)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}(f_{p},g)_{\Omega},\quad

\displaystyle\forall g\in G_{\beta}

(13)

\displaystyle(-\Delta u-f,\mu)_{\Omega}-\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}(\lambda,\mu)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\forall\mu\in M_{\beta}

(14)

It is straightforward to obtained
eq.

10
-
eq.

11
by eliminating
\lambda

from
eq.

12
-
eq.

14
.

Theorem 2

The problem
eq.

3
,
eq.

10
-
eq.

11
, or
eq.

12
-
eq.

14
is well-posed if
1/\gamma<\beta

and if the solution
u
admits
H^{2}
regularity.

Proof.

We first consider the case
\gamma=\infty
. The saddle-point problem must then satisfy the
four Brezzi conditions
[
3
]
. The two conditions of continuity follow from a straightforward calculation. Hence, we will only
consider the coercivity and inf-sup conditions.
The coercivity follows from
H^{2}
regularity
eq.

5
:

(u,u)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\beta^{2}(f,f)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq(u,u)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}(f,f)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2C^{2}}\beta^{2}(u,u)_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

The inf-sup condition can be shown with
\hat{u}=0
and
\hat{f}=\mu

\displaystyle\sup_{u,f}\frac{(-\Delta u-f,\mu)}{(\|u\|^{2}_{V_{\beta}}+\|f\|^{2}_{G_{\beta}})^{1/2}}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(-\Delta\hat{u}-\hat{f},\mu)}{(\|\hat{u}\|^{2}_{V_{\beta}}+\|\hat{f}\|^{2}_{G_{\beta}})^{1/2}}=\frac{(\mu,\mu)}{(\|\mu\|^{2}_{G_{\beta}})^{1/2}}=\|\mu\|_{M_{\beta}}

For
\gamma<\infty
the theory of Braess
[
1
]
states that a saddle point problem with a penalty term is well posed as long as the penalty is bounded by the norm of the Lagrange multiplier, which is the case when
1/\gamma<\beta
.

\square

Remark 1

The above proof follows
[
9
,
10
]
and employs
[
1
]
for
the stability estimates involving the penalty term. However, using the more general theory
[
7
,
14
]
, the requirement
that
1/\gamma<\beta
is removed if the norms
V_{\beta}=L^{2}(\Gamma)\cap(\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}})^{-1/2}H^{2}(\Omega)
and

M_{\beta}=(\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}})^{1/2}L^{2}(\Omega)

are used.

Let us consider conforming discretizations of the problem
eq.

12
-
eq.

14

such that

V_{\beta,h}\subset V_{\beta}
,

G_{\beta,h}\subset G_{\beta}
, and

M_{\beta,h}\subset M_{\beta}
. Furthermore, let

Z=\left\{u\in V_{\beta},f\in G_{\beta}\ |\ (-\Delta u-f,\mu)=0,\forall\mu\in M_{\beta}\right\}

and

Z_{h}=\left\{u\in V_{\beta,h},f\in G_{\beta,h}\ |\ (-\Delta u-f,\mu)=0,\forall\mu\in M_{\beta,h}\right\}

For conforming discretizations in which
Z_{h}\subset Z
or in our case
\Delta V_{\beta,h}\subset M_{\beta,h}

the
Theorem

2
applies, i.e., the same proof can be carried out in in the discrete case.

Finally, we remark that there are some subtleties of our approach of proving the well-posedness
of discretizations of
eq.

3
via the Lagrange formulation
eq.

12
-
eq.

14
.
A discrete version of
eq.

12
-
eq.

14
reads:

\begin{pmatrix}M_{\Gamma}&&A^{T}\\
&\beta^{2}M&M\\
A&M&-\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}M\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
f\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{d}\\
f_{p}\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Here,
A
is the stiffness matrix,
M
is the mass matrix and
M_{\Gamma}
is the mass matrix
related to the partial observation. Note that
A
is not symmetric as the trial and test functions differs. However,
M
is symmetric.
Eliminating
\lambda
yields

\begin{pmatrix}M_{\Gamma}+\gamma^{2}A^{T}M^{-1}A&\gamma^{2}A^{T}\\
\gamma^{2}A&(\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2})M\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
f\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{d}\\
f_{p}\end{pmatrix}.

(15)

Clearly, a discretization of
eq.

10
-
eq.

11
would appear similar to
eq.

15
,
except for the inverted mass matrix.
In most cases, e.g., continuous finite elements, isogeometric analysis or neural networks, the mass matrices are far from diagonal and simply replacing the inverted mass matrix with, for instance, a lumped variant results in loss of approximation. An alternative and more practical condition is that
-\Delta V_{\beta,h}\subset G_{\beta,h}
. Then
AM^{-1}A=\Delta^{2}
on
V_{\beta,h}
.
This can be shown as follows: The upper bound

(AM^{-1}Au,u)=\sup_{\lambda\in G_{h}}\frac{(Au,\lambda)^{2}}{(M\lambda,\lambda)}=\sup_{\lambda\in G_{h}}\frac{(-\Delta u,\lambda)^{2}}{\|\lambda\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Furthermore, since
-\Delta u\in G_{h}
, we can choose
\lambda=-\Delta u
and obtain a lower bound

(AM^{-1}Au,u)=\sup_{\lambda\in G_{h}}\frac{(-\Delta u,\lambda)^{2}}{\|\lambda\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\geq\frac{(-\Delta u,-\Delta u)^{2}}{\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}})=\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

H^{2}

Some properties defined by relative versions of star-covering properties II
Maddalena Bonanzinga, Davide Giacopello, Fortunato Maesano

Abstract
In this paper we consider some recent relative versions of Menger property called set strongly star Menger and set star Menger properties and the corresponding Hurewicz-type properties. In particular, using [ 2 ] , we ”easily” prove that the set strong star Menger and set strong star Hurewicz properties are between countable compactness and the property of having countable extent. Also we show that the extent of a regular set star Menger or a set star Hurewicz space cannot exceed 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} . Moreover, we construct (1) a consistent example of a set star Menger (set star Hurewicz) space which is not set strongly star Menger (set strongly star Hurewicz) and show that (2) the product of a set star Menger (set star Hurewicz) space with a compact space need not be set star Menger (set star Hurewicz). In particular, (1) and (2) answer to some questions posed by Kočinac, Konca and Singh in [ 16 ] and [ 19 ] .



Keywords:
Star compact, strongly star compact, star Lindelöf, strongly star Lindelöf, star Menger, strongly star Menger, star Hurewicz, strongly star Hurewicz, set properties.

In the following we give a partial answer to Question

and a negative answer to Question
3.2

The following fact can be easly checked (we give the proof for sake of completeness). Recall that a map is perfect if it is continuous, closed, onto and each fiber is compact.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
f:X\to Y
is a perfect map
and
A
is an uncountable closed and discrete subspace of
X
, then
f(A)
is an uncountable closed and discrete subspace of
Y
.

Proof.

Let
f
and
A
as in the hypothesis. Clearly
f(A)
is closed in
Y
. Note that, for every
y\in f(A)
,
f^{-1}(y)\cap A
is a closed subset of the compact subspace
f^{-1}(y)
and then, since
A
is discrete, it is finite. Then
A
is countable, otherwise

f(A)
is countable. Now, fix
y\in f(A)
and say
f^{-1}(y)\cap A=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
. For every
i=1,...,n
fix an open subset
U_{i}
of
X
such that
A\cap U_{i}=\{x_{i}\}
and put
U=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
. Since
A\setminus U
is a closed subset of
X
, we have that
f(A\setminus U)=f(A)\setminus\{y\}
is a closed subset of Y, and then
\{y\}
is open in
f(A)
with the topology inherited from
Y
.
∎

By the previous proposition, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.1
.

The product of a space having countable extent with a compact space has countable extent.

Proof.

Let
X
be a space with countable extent and
Y
be a compact space. The projection from
X\times Y
onto
X
is a perfect map. Then, by Proposition
3.1
,
e(X\times Y)=\omega
. ∎

1.3
, the previous result can be restated as follows.

The

The product of a set SSM space with a compact space has countable extent.

Corollary 3.3
.

The

\mathfrak{d}

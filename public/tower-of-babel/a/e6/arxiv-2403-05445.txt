1.
Introduction

Defined by Rentería, Simis and Villarreal in
[
14
]
, and by González and Rentería in
[
2
]
,
parameterized linear codes are evaluation codes over a projective toric subset
parameterized by a set of Laurent monomials, over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
.
From the start, the case when the exponent vectors of the Laurent monomials
form the incidence matrix of a graph is a case of major interest. Let us explain this in detail.
Let
G
be a simple graph, with vertex set
V_{G}=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
and
E_{G}=\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\right\}
.
Fix a finite field,
\mathbb{F}
, of order
q
and,
for any
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}^{*})^{n}
and any edge
e_{k}=\left\{i,j\right\}\in E_{G}
,
denote
e_{k}(\mathbf{x})=x_{i}x_{j}
. Denote the subset of
\mathbb{P}^{s-1}
consisting
of the points with nonzero coordinates by
\mathbb{T}^{s-1}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The
projective toric subset
parameterized by
G
is

X=\left\{(e_{1}(\mathbf{x}):\dots:e_{s}(\mathbf{x}))\in\mathbb{P}^{s-1}:\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{F}^{*})^{n}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{s-1}.

To avoid a the trivial case, we assume throughout this article that

|\mathbb{F}|=q>2
.
Denoting by
S=\mathbb{F}[t_{1},\dots,t_{s}]
the polynomial ring with variables in bijection with

E_{G}
and coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
and choosing an ordering of the points of

X=\left\{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\right\}
, define, for each
d\geq 0
, a linear
map
\operatorname{ev}_{d}\colon S_{d}\to\mathbb{F}^{m}
given by

\textstyle f\mapsto\left(\frac{f}{t_{1}^{d}}(P_{1}),\dots,\frac{f}{t_{1}^{d}}(P_{m})\right).

Definition 1.2
.

The image of
\operatorname{ev}_{d}
is called the parameterized code of order
d
over
G
and
\mathbb{F}
. We denote
these linear subspaces by
C_{X}(d)\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{m}
.

Up to linear equivalence, the codes
C_{X}(d)
do not depend on the choice
of ordering of the edges of graph nor on the ordering of the points of
X
.

Denote the vanishing ideal of
X
by
I(X)\subseteq S=\mathbb{F}[t_{1},\dots,t_{s}]
.
It is by now well-known that the properties of the family of evaluation codes
given by
C_{X}(d)
, as
d\geq 0
, tie into the algebraic properties of the quotient
S/I(X)
.
The three basic parameters of a code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{m}
are the length (
m
, the
dimension of their ambient space), the dimension and the minimum distance,
i.e.
,

\min\left\{\|\mathbf{w}\|:0\not=\mathbf{w}\in C\right\}.

The length of
C_{X}(d)
,
m=|X|
, which is constant as
d\geq 0
, can be given by the multiplicity degree of
S/I(X)
.
An explicit formula for this parameter is known. If
b_{0}(G)
denotes the number of connected components of
G

and
\gamma
is the number of those that are non-bipartite, then

(1)

m=|X|=\deg S/I(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2^{\gamma-1}}(q-1)^{n-m+\gamma-1}&\text{if $\gamma\geq 1$ and $q$ is odd,}\\
(q-1)^{n-m+\gamma-1}&\text{if $\gamma\geq 1$ and $q$ is even,}\\
(q-1)^{n-m-1},&\text{if $\gamma=0$}\\
\end{array}\right.

(
cf
.
[
14
, Corollary 3.8]
and
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
).
Concerning the parameter dimension, from Definition
1.2
, it follows that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}C_{X}(d)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\left(S/I(X)\right)_{d}.

In other words the dimension function of
C_{X}(d)

coincides with the Hilbert function of
S/I(X)
. Unlike the case of the length,
no formula holding for a general
G
is known for the dimension function.
We know of formulas for the dimension function
only in the following cases:
i
) when
X
coincides with the torus
\mathbb{T}^{s-1}
,
i.e.
,
when
G
is either a forest or a non-bipartite unicyclic graph

[
1
, Proposition 2.2]
;
ii
) when
G
is a complete bipartite graph

[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
;
iii
) when
G
is an even cycle and
\mathbb{F}
is a field
with
q=3
elements —
cf
.
[
11
, Theorem 2.8]
.
On the other hand, the behaviour of the Hilbert function of
S/I(X)
also yields another
important piece of information about the family of linear codes
C_{X}(d)
.
Since
S/I(X)
is a Cohen–Macaulay, dimension
1
graded ring, we know that
the Hilbert function eventually becomes constant and equal to
m
. This means
that for a certain value of
d
onward the codes
C_{d}(X)
are trivial, as
they coincide with their ambient space. This value of
d
is the regularity index
of
S/I(X)
or, in this case equivalently, the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
S/I(X)
. For the past years, several authors have focused on the computation of the
regularity of
S/I(X)
—

cf.

[
3
,
5
,
10
,
12
,
8
,
7
]
.
When
X
is equal to
\mathbb{T}^{s-1}
,
I(X)
is equal to the complete intersection

(t_{1}^{q-1}-t_{s}^{q-1},\dots,t_{s-1}^{q-1}-t_{s}^{q-1})

and, accordingly,
\operatorname{reg}S/I(X)=(s-1)(q-2)
—
cf
.
[
4
]
.
If
G
is bipartite,
2
-connected and affords a nested ear decomposition, then the
regularity is equal to

\textstyle\frac{n+\epsilon-3}{2}(q-2),

where
\epsilon
denotes the number of even ears —
cf
.
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
.
If
q=3
, the regularity is equal to the maximum cardinality of a subset of
edges that contains no more than half the edges of any Eulerian,
even cardinality, subgraph of
G
—
cf
.
[
9
, Theorem 4.13]
.
The regularity is known for a few more special families of graphs such as
complete graphs and complete bipartite graphs —

cf
.
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
, respectively — however, so far,
we know of no formula for the regularity of
S/I(X)
that holds
for a general graph and
q>3
.

The minimum distance of
C_{X}(d)
has remained unknown, for most cases.
When
X
coincides with the torus and
d<\operatorname{reg}S/I(X)=(q-1)(s-1)
, we know that the minimum distance of
C_{X}(d)
is equal to

(q-1)^{s-(k+2)}(q-1-\ell)

where
k
and
\ell
are such that
d=k(q-2)+\ell
,
k\geq 0
and
1\leq\ell\leq q-2
—
cf
.
[
15
, Theorem 3.5]
.
This formula can be used to express the minimum distance for the case when
G

is a complete bipartite graph,
cf
.
[
3
]
. For no other classes of graphs do we know a formula for the minimum distance.
A complete answer to this question seems, at present, a very difficult problem.

The purpose of this article is to give a formula for the minimum distance of
C_{X}(1)
when
G
is an even cycle.
This result is significant not only because it provides a closed formula for an unknown parameter,
but also because it lays the groundwork for exploring minimum distances across a wider
range of graph structures. In the next section we describe the corresponding set
X
and linear code
C
,
together with the length and dimension of the latter. In Section
3
, we state and prove
our main result, Theorem
3.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The set of points

Let
G=\mathcal{C}_{2k}
be an even cycle of length
2k\geq 4
.
The toric subset parameterized by

\mathcal{C}_{2k}
is given by:

(2)

X=\left\{(x_{1}x_{2}:x_{2}x_{3}:\dots:x_{2k-1}x_{2k}:x_{2k}x_{1})\in\mathbb{P}^{2k-1}:x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}^{*},1\leq i\leq 2k\right\}.

Using (
1
) we deduce that
X
has cardinality equal to
m=(q-1)^{2k-2}
.

2.2.
The code

Recalling that
S=\mathbb{F}[t_{1},\dots,t_{2k}]
, the code
C_{X}(1)

is the image of the map
\operatorname{ev}_{1}\colon S_{1}\to\mathbb{F}^{m}
given by

\textstyle t_{i}\mapsto\left(\frac{t_{i}}{t_{1}}(P_{1}),\dots,\frac{t_{i}}{t_{1}}(P_{m})\right).

for
i=1,\dots,2k
. For the remainder of this article we will denote this code by
C
.
Since by
[
12
, Theorem 5.9]
, the vanishing ideal of
X
is generated
by binomials of degree
\geq 2
, we deduce that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}C=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(S/I(X))_{1}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}S_{1}=2k.

Hence
C
is a code of length

m=(q-1)^{2k-2}
and dimension
2k
.

2.3.
Number of zeros of forms

The set
X
is the image of a map

\mathbb{T}^{2k-1}\to\mathbb{T}^{2k-1}
that sends
(x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2k})
to

(x_{1}x_{2}:\cdots:x_{2k-1}x_{2k}:x_{2k}x_{1})
. This is a group homomorphism
which, according to
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
,
has kernel of cardinality
q-1
. Hence, given a homogeneous polynomial
F\in\mathbb{F}[t_{1},\dots,t_{2k}]
,
the computation of the cardinality of
Z(F)\cap X
can be achieved by counting
the number of zeros of

f=F(x_{1}x_{2},\dots,x_{2k-1}x_{2k},x_{2k}x_{1})\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

on
\mathbb{T}^{2k-1}
and dividing this number by
(q-1)
. Similarly, the cardinality
of
Z(f)\cap\mathbb{T}^{2k-1}
is equal to the number of
(a_{1},\dots,a_{2k})\in(\mathbb{F}^{*})^{2k}

such that
f(a_{1},\dots,a_{2k})=0
, divided by
q-1
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, denote by
\mathbf{z}(f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell})
the number of

(a_{1},\dots,a_{2k})\in(\mathbb{F}^{*})^{2k}
such that

f_{1}(a_{1},\dots,a_{2k})=\cdots=f_{\ell}(a_{1},\dots,a_{2k})=0.

By the previous discussion we get following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
F\in S
, if
f=F(x_{1}x_{2},\dots,x_{2k-1}x_{2k},x_{2k}x_{1})\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
the cardinality of
Z(F)\cap X
is
\mathbf{z}(f)/(q-1)^{2}
.

3.
The result

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2k-1}
be the toric subset parameterized by a cycle of length
2k\geq 4
,
let
m=|X|=(q-1)^{2k-2}
, and let
C=C_{X}(1)\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{m}
be the corresponding evaluation code of order
1
.
Then the minimum distance of
C
is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(q-1)^{2k-3}(q-2),&\text{if $k=4$ and $q>3$, or $k\geq 5$,}\\
\frac{(q-1)^{2k}-q^{k}(q-2)-1}{q(q-1)},&\text{if $k=2$, or $k=3$, or $k=4$ and $q=3$}.\end{array}\right.

Since
C
is the subspace of
\mathbb{F}^{m}
obtained by evaluating forms of degree one at the points of

X
, to prove Theorem
3.1
, we need to show that

\max_{F\in S_{1}}|Z(F)\cap X|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(q-1)^{2k-3},&\text{if $k=4$ and $q>3$, or $k\geq 5$,}\\
\frac{(q-1)^{2k-1}+q^{k}(q-2)+1}{q(q-1)},&\text{if $k=2$, or $k=3$, or $k=4$ and $q=3$}.\end{array}\right.

To estimate
|Z(F)\cap X|
, with
F\in S_{1}
, we will distinguish between
two cases, corresponding to Propositions
3.2
and

3.4
, below.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
F=\alpha_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{2k}t_{2k}\in\mathbb{F}[t_{1},\dots,t_{s}]
is a nonzero linear form
such that
\alpha_{i}=0
, for some
i
, then

|Z(F)\cap X|\leq(q-1)^{2k-3}

and equality is attained, for example, for
F=t_{1}-t_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
f=F(x_{1}x_{2},\dots,x_{2k-1}x_{2k},x_{2k}x_{1})
. By our assumptions there exists
i
such that

f=h+\beta x_{i}x_{j}
with
\beta\not=0
and
h\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{2k}]
.
This implies that if an element of
(\mathbb{F}^{*})^{2k}
is a zero of
f
then
h
does not vanish and
the
i
th coordinate is determined by the value of the remaining coordinates. In other words,

\mathbf{z}(f)\leq(q-1)^{2k-1},

which, by Lemma
2.2
, says that
|Z(F)\cap X|\leq(q-1)^{2k-3}
.
When
F=t_{1}-t_{2}
we get
f=x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{3}
and, since
x_{1}x_{2}
does not vanish on any element of

(\mathbb{F}^{*})^{2k}
,

\mathbf{z}(f)=(q-1)^{2k-1}\implies|Z(F)\cap X|=(q-1)^{2k-3}.\qed

To deal with the case when all
\alpha_{i}
are nonzero, we will need the next lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
2\leq r\leq 2k
. If
g=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\beta_{i}x_{i}x_{i+1}\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{2k}]
is such that

\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r-1}
are nonzero, then

(3)

\mathbf{z}(g)=q^{-1}[(q-1)^{r}+(-1)^{r-1}(q-1)^{2}](q-1)^{2k-r}.

Proof.

Let us use induction on
r
. If
r=2
then
g=\beta_{1}x_{1}x_{2}
, with
\beta_{1}\not=0
, and
hence
\mathbf{z}_{2}(g)=0
, which agrees with the formula given. Fix
r<2k
and assume that the statement
holds for
r
. Given

\textstyle g=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta_{i}x_{i}x_{i+1}\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{2k}]

with
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}\not=0
, let us write
g=h+\beta_{r}x_{r}x_{r+1}
, where

\textstyle h=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\beta_{i}x_{i}x_{i+1}.

Now, an element of
(\mathbb{F}^{*})^{2k}
is a zero of

g
if and only if it not a zero of
h
and in this case its
r+1
coordinate is determined by
0=h+\beta_{r}x_{r}x_{r+1}
.
Hence

\mathbf{z}(g)=(q-1)^{2k-1}-\mathbf{z}(h)(q-1)^{-1},

which, by induction is equal to

\begin{array}[]{l}(q-1)^{2k-1}-q^{-1}[(q-1)^{r}+(-1)^{r-1}(q-1)^{2}](q-1)^{2k-r-1}\\
=q^{-1}[q(q-1)^{r}-(q-1)^{r}-(-1)^{r-1}(q-1)^{2}](q-1)^{2k-r-1}\\
=q^{-1}[(q-1)^{r+1}+(-1)^{r}(q-1)^{2}](q-1)^{2k-r-1}.\qed\end{array}

Proposition 3.4
.

If
F=\alpha_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{2k}t_{2k}\in\mathbb{F}[t_{1},\dots,t_{s}]
is
such that all
\alpha_{i}
are nonzero, then

|Z(F)\cap X|\leq\frac{(q-1)^{2k-1}+q^{k}(q-2)+1}{q(q-1)},

with equality if and only if
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\cdots\alpha_{2k-1}=(-1)^{k}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}\cdots\alpha_{2k}
.

Proof.

Let
f=F(x_{1}x_{2},\dots,x_{2k-1}x_{2k},x_{2k}x_{1})
. By Lemma
2.2
, we need
to show

(4)

\mathbf{z}(f)\leq q^{-1}\bigl[(q-1)^{2k}+q^{k}(q-2)(q-1)+q-1\bigr]

with equality if and only if

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\cdots\alpha_{2k-1}=(-1)^{k}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}\cdots\alpha_{2k}
. We will argue
by induction on
k\geq 2
. Assume
k=2
, so that

\begin{array}[]{l}f=\alpha_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}x_{3}+\alpha_{3}x_{3}x_{4}+\alpha_{4}x_{4}x_{1}\\
\phantom{f}=\alpha_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}x_{3}+(\alpha_{3}x_{3}+\alpha_{4}x_{1})x_{4}\\
\phantom{f}=g+hx_{4}\end{array}

where we are denoting
g=\alpha_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}x_{3}
and
h=\alpha_{3}x_{3}+\alpha_{4}x_{1}
.
If we let
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
vary in
\mathbb{F}^{*}
, a zero of
f
has
x_{4}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
determined by the values
of
g
and
h
, unless both quantities are zero. Thus, using Lemma
3.3
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{z}(f)=\mathbf{z}(g,h)+(q-1)^{3}-\mathbf{z}(g)(q-1)^{-1}-\mathbf{z}(h)(q-1)^{-1}+\mathbf{z}(g,h)(q-1)^{-1}\\
\phantom{\mathbf{z}(f)}=(q-1)^{-1}q\mathbf{z}(g,h)+(q-1)^{3}-\mathbf{z}(g)(q-1)^{-1}-\mathbf{z}(h)(q-1)^{-1}\\
\phantom{\mathbf{z}(f)}=(q-1)^{-1}q\mathbf{z}(g,h)+(q-1)^{3}-q^{-1}[(q-1)^{3}+(q-1)^{2}]-(q-1)^{2}\\
\phantom{\mathbf{z}(f)}=(q-1)^{-1}q\mathbf{z}(g,h)+(q-1)^{3}-2(q-1)^{2}\\
\phantom{\mathbf{z}(f)}=(q-1)^{-1}q\mathbf{z}((\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}\alpha_{3}^{-1})x_{1}x_{2},\alpha_{3}x_{3}+\alpha_{4}x_{4})+(q-1)^{3}-2(q-1)^{2}\\
\phantom{\mathbf{z}(f)}\leq q(q-1)^{2}+(q-1)^{3}-2(q-1)^{2}\\
\phantom{\mathbf{z}(f)}=(q-1)^{2}[2q-3]\end{array}

which coincides with (
4
) for
k=2
. Moreover, equality above holds if and only if

\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}\alpha_{3}^{-1}=0\iff\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}=(-1)^{2}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}.

Consider the induction step. Let
f\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{2k+2}]
be

\begin{array}[]{l}f=\alpha_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{2k}x_{2k}x_{2k+1}+\alpha_{2k+1}x_{2k+1}x_{2k+2}+\alpha_{2k+2}x_{2k+2}x_{1}\\
\phantom{f}=g+hx_{2k+2},\end{array}

where we
g=\alpha_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{2k}x_{2k}x_{2k+1}
and
h=\alpha_{2k+1}x_{2k+1}+\alpha_{2k+2}x_{1}
.
Arguing as in the case of
k=2
,

(5)

\mathbf{z}(f)=(q-1)^{-1}q\mathbf{z}(g,h)+(q-1)^{2k+1}-\mathbf{z}(g)(q-1)^{-1}-\mathbf{z}(h)(q-1)^{-1}.

Since

\mathbf{z}(g,h)=\mathbf{z}(f^{\prime},h)
, where

f^{\prime}=\alpha_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{2k-1}x_{2k-2}x_{2k-1}-\alpha_{2k}\alpha_{2k+2}\alpha_{2k+1}^{-1}x_{2k}x_{1},

by induction,

\mathbf{z}(g,h)\leq q^{-1}[(q-1)^{2k}+q^{k}(q-2)(q-1)+(q-1)](q-1)

with equality if and only if

(6)

\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\cdots\alpha_{2k-1}=(-1)^{k}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}\cdots\alpha_{2k-2}[-\alpha_{2k}\alpha_{2k+2}\alpha_{2k+1}^{-1}]\\
\iff\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\cdots\alpha_{2k-1}\alpha_{2k+1}=(-1)^{k+1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{4}\cdots\alpha_{2k-2}\alpha_{2k}\alpha_{2k+2}.\end{array}

Using this, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{l}(q-1)^{-1}\bigl[q\mathbf{z}(g,h)-\mathbf{z}(h)\bigr]\leq(q-1)^{2k}+q^{k}(q-2)(q-1)+(q-1)-(q-1)^{2k}\\
\phantom{(q-1)^{-1}\bigl[q\mathbf{z}(g,h)-\mathbf{z}(h)\bigr]}=q^{k}(q-2)(q-1)+(q-1)\end{array}

Together with Lemma
3.3
, this yields from (
5
)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{z}(f)\leq q^{k}(q-2)(q-1)+(q-1)+(q-1)^{2k+1}-q^{-1}[(q-1)^{2k+1}+(q-1)^{2}]\\
\phantom{\mathbf{z}(f)}=q^{-1}[(q-1)^{2k+2}+q^{k+1}(q-2)(q-1)+(q-1)],\end{array}

with equality if and only if (
6
) holds.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Propositions
3.2
and
3.4

\textstyle\max\limits_{F\in S_{1}}|Z(F)\cap X|=\max\left\{(q-1)^{2k-3},\frac{(q-1)^{2k-1}+q^{k}(q-2)+1}{q(q-1)}\right\}.

Observe that

\textstyle(q-1)^{2k-3}-\frac{(q-1)^{2k-1}+q^{k}(q-2)+1}{q(q-1)}=\frac{(q-1)^{2k-2}-q^{k}(q-2)-1}{q(q-1)}

and denote the expression
(q-1)^{2k-2}-q^{k}(q-2)
in the
numerator of this fraction by
\Delta(k,q)
.
We want to show that

\Delta(k,q)>0\iff\bigl(k>5\text{ and }q>2\bigr)\text{ or }\bigl(k=4\text{ and }q>3\bigr).

Since

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta(2,q)=(q-1)^{2}-q^{2}(q-2)<q^{2}-q^{2}(q-2)<0,\\
\Delta(3,q)=(q-1)^{4}-q^{3}(q-2)<q[(q-1)^{3}-q^{2}(q-2)]=q[-q^{2}+3q-1]<0,\end{array}

we may reduce to
k\geq 4
. By direct computation,
\Delta(4,3)=2^{6}-3^{4}<0
. Hence, if
k=4
we may reduce to
q>3
.
In this situation,
q-1\geq\frac{3}{4}q
and this yields:

\textstyle\Delta(4,q)=(q-1)^{6}-q^{4}(q-2)\geq\frac{3^{5}}{4^{5}}q\cdot q^{4}(q-2)-q^{4}(q-2),

the right side of which is positive if
3^{5}q>4^{5}
which is equivalent to
q\geq 5
. Since, by direct computation,

\Delta(4,4)=3^{6}-4^{4}\cdot 2>0
we conclude that
\Delta(4,q)>0
if and only if
q>3
.
Finally, assume that
k\geq 5
. Since

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta(k,q)=(q-1)^{2k-2}-q^{k}(q-2)\\
\phantom{\Delta(k,q)}=(q-1)^{2}\Delta(k-1,q)+(q-1)^{2}q^{k-1}(q-2)-q^{k}(q-2)\\
\phantom{\Delta(k,q)}=(q-1)^{2}\Delta(k-1,q)+[q^{2}-3q+1]q^{k-1}(q-2)\\
\end{array}

and
q^{2}-3q+1>0
for
q>2
, from the positivity of
\Delta(4,q)
, for
q>3
we get

\Delta(k,q)>0
, for
q>3
. Finally, if
q=3

\textstyle\Delta(k,3)=2^{2k-2}-3^{k}>0\iff k>\frac{\ln(4)}{\ln(4)-\ln(3)}\iff k\geq 5.\qed

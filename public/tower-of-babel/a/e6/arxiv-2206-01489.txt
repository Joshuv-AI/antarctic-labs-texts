Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(M,f,g)
be a nonzero multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule over
(R,h,k)
such that
R
has the only one maximal hyperideal. Then
M
is cyclic.

Proof.

Let
(M,f,g)
be a nonzero multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule over
(R,h,k)
. Assume that
I
is the only maximal hyperideal of
R
. The Lemma
3.2
shows that
g(I,1^{(n-2)},M)\neq M
. Then there exists some
m\in M
such that
m\notin g(I,1^{(n-2)},M)
. Thus we have
g(R,1^{(n-2)},m)=g(J,1^{(n-2)},M)
for some hyperideal
J
of
R
. Let
J\neq R
. It follows that
J\subseteq I
. Hence we get
g(R,1^{(n-2)},m)=g(J,1^{(n-2)},M)\subseteq g(I,1^{(n-2)},M)
. Since
m\in g(1^{(n-1)},m)\subseteq g(R,1^{(n-2)},m)
, then we conclude that
m\in g(I,1^{(n-2)},M)
which is contradiction. Thus
J=R
which implies
g(R,1^{(n-2)},m)=g(J,1^{(n-2)},M)=g(R,1^{(n-2)},M)=M
and the proof is completed.
∎

. Let
C=\{r\in R\ |\ g(r,1^{(n-2)},N)\subseteq L\}
. Then
A_{i}\subseteq C
. Therefore we have
h(B_{i},C,0^{(m-2)})\nsubseteq P
for each
1\leq i\leq t
. Let
B=\{r\in R\ |\ g(r,1^{(n-2)},L)\subseteq N\}
. Then we get

h(B,C,0^{(m-2)})=h(\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}B_{i},C,0^{(m-2)})\nsubseteq P
which means
B\nsubseteq P
or
C\nsubseteq P
. Hence there exists
p\in P
such that
h(1,-p,0^{(m-2)})\subseteq B\cup C=B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
with
B^{\prime}=\{r\in R\ |\ g(r,1^{(n-2)},f(L,N,0^{(m-2)}))\subseteq N\}
and
C^{\prime}=\{r\in R\ |\ g(r,1^{(n-2)},f(L,N,0^{(m-2)}))\subseteq L\}
. Then we have

\hskip 14.22636ptg(h(1,-p,0^{(m-2)}),1^{(n-2)},f(L,N,0^{(m-2)}))\subseteq L

or

\hskip 14.22636ptg(h(1,-p,0^{(m-2)}),1^{(n-2)},f(L,N,0^{(m-2)}))\subseteq N
.

Since
L
and
N
are multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodules, then by Lemma
5.5
, we conclude that
f(L,\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}N_{i},0^{(m-2)})=f(L,N,0^{(m-2)})
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(M,f,g)
be an
(m,n)
-hypermodule over
(R,h,k)
. Let
N_{1},...,N_{n}
be subhypermodules of

M
such that for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
f(N_{i},N_{j},0^{(m-2)})
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule. Then
N_{1},...,N_{n}
are multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodules if and only if
N=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}N_{i}
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule.

Proof.

\Longrightarrow
Let
N_{1},...,N_{n}
are multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodules. By
induction on
n
,
N_{2}\cap...\cap N_{n}
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule. Now, by using Theorem
5.6
and Theorem
5.4
, the proof is completed.

\Longleftarrow
Let
N=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}N_{i}
be a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule. Assume that for some maximal hyperideal
P
of
R
,
\mathcal{X}_{P}(N_{1})\neq N_{1}
. This implies that for all
2\leq i\leq n
,
\mathcal{X}_{P}(f(N_{1},N_{i},0^{(m-2)}))\neq f(N_{1},N_{i},0^{(m-2)})
. Then there exist
x_{i}\in N_{1}\cup N_{i}
and
p_{i}\in P
such that

g(h(1,-p_{i},1^{(m-2)}),1^{(n-2)},f(N_{1},N_{i},0^{(m-2)}))\subseteq g(R,1^{(n-2)},x_{i})
, by Theorem
5.1
. If
x_{i}\in N_{1}
then
N_{1}
is an n-ary
P
-cyclic. If
x_{i}\in N_{i}

(2\leq i\leq n)
, then

g(h(1,-{p_{2}},0^{(m-2)}),...,h(1,-p_{n},0^{(m-2)}),N_{1})\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}N_{i}
.

By Theorem 3.8 in
[
2
]
and using an argument similar to that in the proof of Theorem
5.1

((3)\Longrightarrow(4))
, we conclude that
N_{1}
is multiplication. Similarly, we can show that for all
2\leq i\leq n
,
N_{i}
is multiplication.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
(M,f,g)
be an
(m,n)
-hypermodule over
(R,h,k)
such that subhypermodules
N_{1},...,N_{n},N_{n+1},...,N_{k}
of
M
are multiplication. If for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
,
f(N_{i},N_{j},0^{(m-2)})
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule. Then
f(N,L,0^{(m-2)})
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule with
N=N_{1}\cap...\cap N_{n}
and
L=N_{n+1}\cap...\cap N_{k}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.7
,
N
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule. Theorem
5.6
shows that for each
n+1\leq i\leq k
,
f(N,N_{i},0^{(m-2)})
is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule. Thus
f(N,N_{n+1}\cap...\cap N_{k},0^{(m-2)})

is a multiplication
(m,n)
-hypermodule, by Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Abstract
.
Exploiting the graph product structure and results concerning amalgamated free products of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras we provide an explicit computation of the K-theoretic invariants of right-angled Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, including concrete algebraic representants of a basis in
\mathrm{K}
-theory. On the way, we show that these Hecke algebras are KK-equivalent with their undeformed counterparts and satisfy the UCT. Our results are applied to study the isomorphism problem for Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, highlighting the limits of K-theoretic classification, both for varying Coxeter type as well as for fixed Coxeter type.

1
Introduction

The notion of Coxeter groups originates in the study of reflection groups and Weyl groups. One of the most important tools when understanding combinatorial and representation theoretic questions associated with these groups are Hecke algebras as studied for example by Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
34
]
. While traditionally focused on affine Coxeter types, by the time, important results on Hecke algebras could also be obtained for arbitrary Coxeter types
[
23
]
. Operator algebraic versions of Hecke algebras have been for the first time studied in
[
17
]
. These have recently experienced a sharp rise in attention. Originally introduced as a mean to study cohomology of buildings
[
16
, Section 11]
, Garncarek established them as an interesting class of operator algebras in their own right, by proving a clear parameter dependence of factoriality for single parameter Hecke von Neumann algebras of right-angled type. Since then, Hecke operator algebras have been studied from various angles, informed by typical questions asked about group operator algebras. This comes as no surprise, since first of all Hecke operator algebras can be considered deformations of the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and the group von Neumann algebra, and second for certain rational deformation parameters, Hecke algebras arise as corners of group algebras associated with groups acting on buildings, see for example
[
45
, Section 2]
. On the other hand a constant source of inspiration for operator algebraists is the comparison of results known for reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of free groups with possible results on Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

In
[
11
]
, Caspers, Skalski and Wasilewski studied MASAs of Hecke von Neumann algebras of free product type in analogy with questions about the free group factors
[
2
]
. In
[
8
]
, Caspers obtained results on the absence of Cartan subalgebras and strong solidity for Hecke von Neumann algebras of right-angled type, which is analogue to key questions about the structure of group von Neumann algebras addressed by Ozawa
[
40
]
and Chifan and Sinclair
[
12
]
. The structure of Hecke von Neumann algebras also motivated work of Klisse
[
35
]
, which offered and exploited a new perspective on certain boundaries of Coxeter complexes, earlier introduced in
[
6
]
and
[
37
]
.

Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras have been put in the spotlight by the work of Caspers, Klisse and Larsen
[
10
]
, which obtains a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-simplicity result for right-angled Hecke algebras with certain deformation parameter (a complete characterization of the relevant parameter range was recently obtained in
[
36
]
). The investigation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-simplicity of group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras has developed a long tradition since Powers showed that reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of free groups are simple
[
42
]
. Recently it has experienced a breakthrough thanks to the work of Kalantar and Kennedy
[
33
]
and Breuillard, Kalantar, Kennedy and Ozawa
[
4
]
. See also
[
44
]
for a survey of more recent developments of the field.

It is no surprise that in the study of Hecke operator algebras, the right-angled case has received the most attention. This is because a natural description as graph products directly relates them to amalgamated free products via a dévissage procedure. Free products and amalgamated free products are of major importance in the world of operator algebras since the advent of Voiculescu’s free probability theory
[
52
]
. Operator algebraic graph products have recently been introduced by Caspers and Fima
[
9
]
, laying the basis for applications of work on amalgamated free products to right-angled Hecke operator algebras. Moreover, the particular case of Hecke operator algebras of free product type lies directly in the scope of work of Dykema
[
18
,
19
]
and Dykema and Rørdam
[
20
]
on the structure and K-theory of free product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and von Neumann algebras, guiding the way to potential future results on Hecke operator algebras of general right-angled type and beyond.

The present article investigates the classification of right-angled Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras using K-theoretic tools. K-theory of free product type algebras has been an object of interest for a long time. In
[
41
]
, Pimsner and Voiculescu provided the first calculation of
\mathrm{K}
-theory of the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of free groups, and could infer absence of non-trivial projections as well as non-isomorphism for different finite numbers of generators. Shortly after, Cuntz established a foundational perspective on
\mathrm{K}
-theory of free product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras in general, introducing the notion of
\mathrm{K}
-amenability
[
15
]
together with a conceptually simple calculation of the
\mathrm{K}
-theory of universal free products
[
13
,
14
]
. Germain was first to establish unconditional results on the K-theory of free product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras in
[
27
,
28
]
. An extension of this work to amalgamated free products was only recently obtained by Fima and Germain in
[
25
]
.

The K-theory of Hecke algebras has been mainly considered in the affine case so far, where Solleveld proved a longstanding conjecture of Higson and Plymen that describes parameter independence of
K-theory

[
49
]
. For deformed Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of non-affine type, no results are stated in the literature, although the special case of Coxeter groups of free product type is covered by known results on K-theory for free product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. In the undeformed case the K-theory groups in the even case (including the right-angled one) have been computed by Sánchez-Garcia in
[
46
]
. This was achieved via computing the Bredon K-homology of the underlying Coxeter group via the Davis complex, and then appealing to the Baum-Connes conjecture, valid for the class of groups in question via a combination of the main results of
[
3
]
and
[
31
]
. It should be noted that as often when appealing to the Baum-Connes isomorphism the arguments of
[
46
]
do not lead to the explicit identification of generators of K-theory.

The main results of this paper provide an explicit computation of K-theory in both deformed and undeformed case, including concrete algebraic representants of K-theory classes, described in Notation
4.1
. These are projections
p_{C}\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(W)
associated with a clique
C
in the commutation graph of a right-angled Coxeter group
W
.

Theorem A
(Corollary
4.5
).

Let
(W,S)
be a right-angled Coxeter system. Denote by
\Gamma
the associated commutation graph and by
\mathcal{C}
the set of all, possibly empty, cliques of
\Gamma
. Then the following statements hold true.

•

The map
C\mapsto[p_{C}]
induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{C}}\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W))
.

•

\mathrm{K}_{1}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W))=0
.

In particular,
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(W))
is a free abelian group whose rank is the number of cliques in the commutation graph of
W
. It is generated by the projections from the copies
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathrm{C}^{*}(W)
associated with the Coxeter generators
s\in S
.

Our proof of this result reduces the problem of K-theory computations to the full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of the Coxeter group in question, by means of a KK-equivalence, analogue to K-amenability results. Our key ingredients to establish this KK-equivalence are Fima’s and Germain’s work on amalgamated free products
[
25
]
, combined with the dévissage procedure for graph product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras obtained by Caspers and Fima
[
9
]
. The K-theory computations for full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of right-angled Coxeter groups then make use of six-term exact sequences established in
[
25
]
; this in particular gives an alternative approach to
[
46
, Corollary 7.3]
.

Classifying
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras by their K-theory invariants has been one of central topics of the theory of operator algebras for almost fifty years. To wit, Elliott’s classification programme for nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

[
24
]
saw recent structural breakthroughs, see e.g.
[
29
,
50
,
7
]
. But even in the early days
K-theory
proved to be a useful invariant beyond the nuclear world too. In particular the aforementioned result of Pimsner and Voiculescu
[
41
]
distinguished the reduced
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of different free groups by computing their
\mathrm{K}
-theory. Generally speaking, non-isomorphism problems regarding group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
, even in the amenable realm, are highly non-trivial, and only recent years brought the first rigidity results regarding algebras of this type; see
[
21
]
and references therein. Note that the group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of a right-angled Coxeter group depends only on the associated Coxeter graph, and the fact that the correspondence between (isomorphism classes of) Coxeter graphs and (isomorphism classes of) Coxeter groups is bijective in the right-angled case was established only recently (see
[
43
]
and references therein), whereas for general Coxeter groups the bijectivity is known to fail.

Theorem
A
implies that there is no hope to use K-theory groups in themselves to distinguish Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras associated with different right-angled Coxeter groups, as is shown in Example
4.4
. Adding further data, the explicit nature of our K-theory calculations allow us to identify the trace pairing in K-theory in Proposition
4.6
, but also this additional information does not suffices to identify the Coxeter type, as Example
4.8
shows. We point out that classification results for formally similar structures such as
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of right-angled Artin monoids achieved by Eilers, Li and Ruiz
[
22
]
are based on amenability of groupoids naturally arising when studying semigroup operator algebras. Similar groupoid models cannot be expected for operator algebras arising from right-angled Coxeter or Artin groups.

Fixing a Coxeter type one might hope that K-theoretic information, and in particular the trace pairing suffices to recover the deformation parameter of a Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. The discussion of
K-theoretic
invariants for solving this classification problem is the second main contribution of this article. For the purposes of our paper it is convenient to consider the following version of the Elliott invariant of a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ell_{unord}}(A)=(\mathrm{K}_{0}(A),\mathrm{K}_{1}(A),[1]_{\mathrm{K}_{0}(A)},\mathrm{T}(A),\rho_{A})\,\text{,}

where
\mathrm{T}(A)
denotes the trace simplex of
A
and
\rho_{A}
is the trace pairing of
\mathrm{K}_{0}(A)
and
\mathrm{T}(A)
. Combining our K-theory calculations with Dykema’s work on the structure of free product
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, which classified in particular their traces, allows us to determine this unordered Elliott invariant for Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of free product type. Focusing on the single parameter case, we obtain the following result, which illustrates both the power and limitations of K-theoretic invariants when it comes to classifying the algebras in our class.

Theorem B
(Theorem
5.4
).

Suppose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and let
q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,1]
. Then the following statements hold true.

•

If
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and one of
q_{1}
or
q_{2}
lies in either of the sets
(0,\frac{1}{n-1})
,
\{\frac{1}{n-1}\}
,
(\frac{1}{n-1}\,1]
, then so does the other.

•

Assume
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in(\frac{1}{n-1},1]
.

–

If
q_{1}
or
q_{2}
is irrational, then
q_{1}=q_{2}
.

–

If
q_{1}
or
q_{2}
is rational, then so is the other. Further,
\frac{1}{1+q_{1}}
and
\frac{1}{1+q_{2}}
have the same order in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
.

–

If
q_{1},q_{2}
are rational and
\frac{1}{1+q_{1}}
,
\frac{1}{1+q_{2}}
have the same order in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, then the unordered Elliott invariants of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
are isomorphic.

•

If
q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{n-1})
, then the unordered Elliott invariants of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
are isomorphic.

It is natural to wonder why the version of the Elliott invariant used above does not involve the positive part of
\mathrm{K}_{0}
. One can expect that the order in
\mathrm{K}_{0}
is determined by the pairing with traces. This indeed is known to hold in the undeformed case of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{red}}((\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{*n})
,
n\geq 3
, as follows from the main result of
[
20
]
. That theorem, which as far as we know is currently the best available statement regarding the relationship between the order of
\mathrm{K}_{0}
and traces for free products, assumes the so-called
Avitzour conditions
. These are known to be satisfied only in the undeformed case and we have not been able to determine the order structure of
\mathrm{K}_{0}
in the general case. The calculation of ordered K-theory for free products not satisfying the Avitzour conditions remains an interesting open problem.

Finally we should note that it is natural to expect that the deformed algebras mentioned in Theorem
B
are non-isomorphic whenever
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
. The opposite statement would solve the free group factor conjecture by showing that all the free group factors must be isomorphic.

Organisation of the article

After this introduction, in Section
2
, we recall basic facts concerning the graph products of groups and algebras, right-angled Coxeter groups, Hecke algebras and K-theory of amalgamated free products. In Section

we establish the KK-equivalence result which implies that the K-theory groups of Hecke

A
. We compute the K-theoretic invariants of the Hecke-

, we study the scope and limitations of K-theoretic classification of Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of free product type in detail and prove Theorem
B
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we recall basic facts concerning the graph products of groups and algebras, right-angled Coxeter groups, Hecke algebras and K-theory of amalgamated free products. We also show how in the case of algebras with free K-theory one can deduce from six-term exact sequences stability of the UCT under the amalgamated free products.

Notation 2.1
.

Multi-indices will be denoted by a bold font throughout this article. We assume throughout that the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras we consider are separable.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

and

2.2
Graph products of groups and algebras

Throughout the paper, by a graph
\Gamma=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})
we will understand a finite, and possibly empty, simplicial graph. Formally,
\mathrm{V}
denotes a finite set and
\mathrm{E}\subset\mathrm{V}\times\mathrm{V}
is a symmetric subset that does not intersect the diagonal. We will sometimes write
\mathrm{V}(\Gamma)
for
V
and
\mathrm{E}(\Gamma)
for
E
.
We denote the
induced subgraph
on a subset
V^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{V}(\Gamma)
by
\Gamma|_{V^{\prime}}
, so that
\mathrm{E}(\Gamma|_{V^{\prime}}):=\mathrm{E}\cap(\mathrm{V}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{V}^{\prime})
. Given a vertex
v\in\mathrm{V}
, we denote by
\Gamma\setminus v
the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
with vertex set
\mathrm{V}\setminus\{v\}
. A
clique
in a graph
\Gamma
is a complete subgraph, i.e. a subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
such that
\mathrm{E}(\Gamma^{\prime})=\Gamma^{\prime}\times\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus\{(\gamma,\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}\}
. For an element
w\in\mathrm{V}
we consider the graphs
\mathrm{Link}(w)=\Gamma|_{\{v\in\mathrm{V}:(v,w)\in\mathrm{E}\}}
and
\mathrm{Star}(w)=\Gamma|_{\{v\in\mathrm{V}:(v,w)\in\mathrm{E}\}\cup\{w\}}
.

We will need in Section
4
a simple lemma counting cliques in a given graph. Recall that the empty set is a clique in every graph.

Lemma 2.3
.

Denote by
N(\Gamma)
the number of cliques of arbitrary size in a graph
\Gamma
. Then for all
v\in\mathrm{V}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle N(\Gamma)=N(\mathrm{Link}(v))+N(\Gamma\setminus v)\,\text{.}

Proof.

For a clique
C
in
\Gamma
, there are precisely two options. Either
v\in\mathrm{V}(C)
and
C
is contained in
\mathrm{Star}(v)
or
v\notin\mathrm{V}(C)
and
C
is contained in
\Gamma\setminus v
. In the former case,
C\setminus v
is a clique in
\mathrm{Link}(v)
, and every clique in
\mathrm{Link}(v)
is of this form.

Suppose that
\Gamma=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})
is a graph and that for every
v\in\mathrm{V}
we have an associated group
G_{v}
. Based on this data, Green introduced in
[
30
]
a construction of the graph product of groups, leading to a group
G_{\Gamma}
, generated by copies of all
G_{v}
,
v\in\mathrm{V}
, such that
G_{v}
commutes with
G_{w}
if and only if
(v,w)\in\mathrm{E}
. In
[
9
]
Caspers and Fima transferred this construction to the operator algebra world, taking into account various possible variants. For us the key notions will be the
universal
graph product of unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, and the
reduced
graph product of unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras equipped with faithful states. Thus if for every
v\in\mathrm{V}
we are given a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A_{v}
(with a faithful state
\varphi_{v}
), we have the corresponding universal graph product
\prod_{\Gamma}A_{v}
(and the reduced graph product
\prod_{\Gamma}(A_{v},\varphi_{v})
). The constructions are closely related to the amalgamated free product of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, and in particular
[
9
, Section 2]
contains the following identifications, inspired by Serre’s dévissage procedure for groups acting on trees.

Theorem 2.4
(See Theorem 2.15 and Remark 2.16 of
[
9
]
).

Let
\Gamma=(\mathrm{V},E)
be a graph and
(A_{v})_{v\in\mathrm{V}}
a family of unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. Let
w\in\mathrm{V}
.

•

There is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
\prod_{\Gamma}A_{v}\to\left(\prod_{\mathrm{Star}(w)}A_{v}\right)*_{\prod_{\mathrm{Link}(w)}A_{v}}\left(\prod_{\Gamma\setminus w}A_{v}\right)
, commuting with the natural inclusions of
(A_{v})_{v\in\mathrm{V}}
in each side.

•

If
\varphi_{v}
is a faithful state on
A_{v}
for all
v\in\mathrm{V}
, then there is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
\prod_{\Gamma}(A_{v},\varphi_{v})\to\left(\prod_{\mathrm{Star}(w)}(A_{v},\varphi_{v})\right)*_{\prod_{\mathrm{Link}(w)}(A_{v},\varphi_{v})}\left(\prod_{\Gamma\setminus w}(A_{v},\varphi_{v})\right)
, commuting with the natural inclusions of
(A_{v})_{v\in\mathrm{V}}
in each side.

2.3
Coxeter systems and Hecke algebras

A good introduction to Coxeter groups can be found for example in
[
32
]
. Here we will consider only the finitely generated right-angled case. A
Coxeter matrix of right-angled type
over a non-empty finite set
S
is a symmetric
S\times S
matrix whose diagonal entries equal
1
and whose off-diagonal entries lie in the set
\{2,\infty\}
. Let
M=(m_{st})_{s,t\in S}
be a Coxeter matrix. The
Coxeter system

(W,S)
associated with
M
is given by the group

\displaystyle W=\langle S\mid\forall s,t\in S:\,s^{2}=e\textup{ and }st=ts\textup{ whenever }m_{st}=2\rangle

together with its generating set
S
. Given
w\in W
we denote by
|w|
its
word length
with respect to the generating set
S
. The data contained in the Coxeter matrix can be equivalently encoded in the
commutation graph

\Gamma_{(W,S)}
, with the vertex set
S
and the edge set
\{(s,t)\in S\times S:m_{st}=2\}
. Note that the commutation graph is the complement of the standard (unlabelled)
Coxeter graph
of
(W,S)
. In
[
10
]
the commutation graph was called Coxeter diagram, which is unfortunately in conflict with the standard terminology employed in the theory of Coxeter groups. It is easy to see, and was in fact the original inspiration to study graph products of groups, that
W
is isomorphic to the graph product of groups of order
2
; the relevant isomorphism
W\cong\prod_{\Gamma_{(W,S)}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
preserves generating sets.

The Coxeter matrix and its Coxeter system are called
irreducible
if the Coxeter matrix is not the Kronecker tensor
product of two non-trivial Coxeter matrices. Equivalently, there is no partition
S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
such that
W=\langle S_{1}\rangle\oplus\langle S_{2}\rangle
.

Given
W
as above we will write
\mathrm{C}^{*}(W)
for the universal group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of
W
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(W)
for its reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. The central object of study in this paper is the family of unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras arising as deformations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(W)
, the so-called Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of right-angled Coxeter groups. For the origins of the construction and its connections to geometry we refer to
[
16
]
and
[
45
, Section 2.4]
; many properties of the algebras in the class were recently established in
[
10
]
. Here we will take a direct way of introducing the Hecke operator algebras as generated by certain unitary representations of
W
.

Fix
(W,S)
as above and a multiparameter
\mathbf{q}\in(0,1]^{S}
. Consider
\lambda_{\mathbf{q}}:W\to B(\ell^{2}(W))
, the unitary representation given by the formulas (
s\in S
,
w\in W
)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathbf{q}}(s)\delta_{w}=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}\frac{1-q_{s}}{1+q_{s}}\delta_{w}+\frac{2q^{1/2}}{1+q_{s}}\delta_{sw}&\text{if }|sw|>|w|,\\
-\frac{1-q_{s}}{1+q_{s}}\delta_{w}+\frac{2q^{1/2}}{1+q_{s}}\delta_{sw}&\text{if }|sw|<|w|\,\text{.}\end{cases}

We then denote by
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W)
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by
\lambda_{\mathbf{q}}(W)
. Note that for
\mathbf{q}={\bf{1}}
the representation
\lambda_{\mathbf{q}}
is the left regular representation, so that we have
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textbf{1}}(W)=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(W)
. The representations

can be also viewed as graph product representations, which was the point of view exploited in
[
8
,
10
]
and systematically investigated in
[
45
, Section 4]
. Each of them is equipped with a canonical trace
\tau:\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W)\to\mathbb{C}
, given as the vector state associated with

. Finally if

we will write simply
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(W)
for
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W)
with
\mathbf{q}=(q,\ldots,q)\in(0,1]^{S}
.

The group isomorphism mentioned earlier and the general properties of the graph products of groups and operator algebras,
[
9
, Example 2.6]
yield the natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{*}(W)\cong\prod_{\Gamma_{(W,S)}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}).

Further by
[
8
, Corollary 3.4]
, see also
[
10
, Section 1.10]
, if we view
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{C}\chi_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}\chi_{-1}
via the Fourier transform
and for each
s\in S
we denote by
\varphi_{s}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}))
the state given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}(\chi_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle(1+\lambda_{\mathbf{q}}(s))\delta_{e},\delta_{e}\rangle=\frac{1}{1+q_{s}},

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}(\chi_{-1})

\displaystyle=1-\varphi_{s}(\chi_{1})=\frac{q_{s}}{1+q_{s}},

then we also have the trace preserving isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W)\cong\prod_{\Gamma_{(W,S)}}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}),\varphi_{s}),

extending the isomorphism of groups
W\to\prod_{\Gamma(W,S)}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

3
The reduction map is a KK-equivalence

In this section we will prove that the unitary representations
\lambda_{\mathbf{q}}:W\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W)
described in Section
2.3
induce a KK-equivalence of the Hecke-
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with
\mathrm{C}^{*}(W)
, in analogy with K-amenability results
[
15
]
. This implies that the K-theory of Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras associated to right-angled Coxeter groups does not depend on the deformation parameter. In particular, it coincides with the K-theory of the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of the underlying group. We will derive this result from a KK-equivalence between universal graph products and reduced graph products of unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Once again we refer to
[
25

for information on amalgamated free products of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. In the next proposition we will use the approach to
\mathbf{KK}

[

[

Applying the construction mentioned in the beginning of the proof to the *-homomorphism

-algebras described in Theorem

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

For the formulation of the next corollary, recall the representation

\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(W))\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(\prod_{\Gamma}\mathbb{C}^{2})
is free abelian and generated by the projections coming from the inclusions
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\langle s\rangle)\subset\mathrm{C}^{*}(W)
for
s\in S
. In order to calculate the rank of
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(W))
, let us write
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)=\mathrm{K}_{0}(\prod_{\Lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2})
for a finite graph
\Lambda
. Then
\Lambda\mapsto\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)
is a functor from the category of finite graphs, with morphisms given by induced inclusions, to the category of abelian torsion free groups. It maps the empty graph to
\mathbb{Z}
and satisfies the following rules:

•

if
v\in\mathrm{V}(\Lambda)
is such that
\mathrm{Star}(v)=\Lambda
then the following diagram commutes.

\textstyle{\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda\setminus v)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a\mapsto(1,-1)\otimes a}
\textstyle{\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}^{2}\otimes\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda\setminus v)}

•

if
v\in\mathrm{V}(\Lambda)

\mathrm{Star}(v)\neq\Lambda
then we have a short exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{Link}(v))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

It follows that the rank
N(\Lambda)
of
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)
evaluated on the graph with one vertex is
N(K_{1})=2
. Further,

\displaystyle N(\Lambda)=N(\mathrm{Link}(v))+N(\Lambda\setminus v)

for all
v\in\mathrm{V}(\Lambda)
. By Lemma
2.3
and a straightforward induction we thus find that
N(\Lambda)
is the number of all cliques in
\Lambda
, including the empty clique. We next show that
(p_{C})_{C\in\mathcal{C}(\Lambda)}
is a basis for
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)
, where
\mathcal{C}(\Lambda)
denotes the set of cliques of
\Lambda
. Since
|\mathcal{C}(\Lambda)|
equals the rank of
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)
, it suffices to show that
(p_{C})_{C\in\mathcal{C}(\Lambda)}
is generating. This follows by induction, because choosing
v\in\Lambda
such that
\mathrm{Star}(v)\neq\Lambda
, we saw that
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda)
is generated by
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{Star}(v))\oplus\mathrm{K}_{0}(\Lambda\setminus v)
. Since every clique of
\Lambda
is either contained in
\mathrm{Star}(v)
or in
\Lambda\setminus v
, we can conclude the proof.

Remark 4.3
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the abstract form of
\mathrm{K}
-theory groups for (reduced)
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of even, so in particular also right-angled Coxeter groups has been computed in
[
46
]
. The proof there is based on first computing the Bredon homology of the Coxeter group in question, noting that it coincides with the equivariant
\mathrm{K}
-homology, and then appealing to the main result of
[
31
]
which says that the Baum-Connes assembly map is an isomorphism for the groups which have the Haagerup property. The latter property holds for Coxeter groups due to
[
3
]
. The form of the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group given by Sánchez-Garcia in the right-angled case is expressed in terms of the number of spherical subsets of
S
, which is easily seen to be equal to the number of cliques of the commutaion graph of
(W,S)
.
It should be noted that the techniques of
[
46
, Section 9]
in principle make it possible to compute, with the computer assistence, the
\mathrm{K}
-theory groups for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of arbitrary Coxeter groups. It is however highly non-clear how these could be applied to the associated Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra beyond the right-angled case. The methods used by Solleveld in
[
49
]
in the affine case are of a completely different nature to these of our work, and use rather the explicit representation theory of the deformed algebras, as investigated in
[
48
]
.

Example 4.4
.

It is natural to ask whether
\mathrm{K}
-theory of group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras distinguishes the Coxeter type of a right-angled Coxeter group. This is not the case, as demonstrated by the two examples associated with the following commutation graphs.

The first group can be describes as the free product
((\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}*\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})*\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, while the second group is isomorphic with
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 2}*(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 2}
. They have the same K-theory by Theorem
4.2
. In Example
4.8
, we investigate the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of these groups, showing that adding other information from the Elliott invariant does not help to recover the Coxeter type.

The next result transfers the K-theory calculations obtained for universal group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra in Theorem
4.2
to the setting of Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. Recall Notation
4.1
for projections associated with cliques.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(W,S)
be a right-angled Coxeter system and
\mathbf{q}\in(0,1]^{S}
.

•

The map
C\mapsto[p_{C}]
induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{C}}\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W))
.

•

\mathrm{K}_{1}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W))=0
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
4.2
.

Now that we have found an explicit basis of
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W))
, we can calculate the trace pairing with the natural trace.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(W,S)
be a right-angled Coxeter system and
\mathbf{q}\in(0,1]^{S}
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}
the set of cliques of the commutation graph of
(W,S)
. The trace pairing of
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathbf{q}}(W))
with the natural trace is given by

\displaystyle\tau_{*}([p_{C}])=\prod_{s\in C}\frac{1}{1+q_{s}}\,\text{.}

Proof.

This follows directly from the explicit description of the projection
p_{C}
as a tensor product, and the computation in the last part of Section
2.3
.

Example 4.7
.

Let
W_{1},W_{2}
be right-angled Coxeter groups with commutation graphs
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
, respectively. Then Proposition
4.6
shows that
(\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(W_{1})),(\tau_{1})_{*})\cong(\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(W_{2})),(\tau_{2})_{*})
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the number of
n
-cliques in
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
agree.

Example 4.8
.

Having pointed out two right-angled Coxeter groups with isomorphic K-groups in Example
4.4
, one could speculate whether adding other information from the Elliott invariant helps to remedy the situation. That this is not the case, can be shown thanks to Proposition
4.6
. Considering the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of
W_{1}=((\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}*\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})*\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
W_{2}=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 2}*(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 2}
, we find identifications of
(\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(W_{i}),\tau_{*})
with
(\mathbb{Z}^{6},(1,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/4,1/4))
. Since the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
fall in the scope of the work of Dykema’s result
[
19
, Theorem 1]
, they have a unique trace. Further, Dykema and Rørdam’s work
[
20
, Theorem 2]
) shows that the order in
\mathrm{K}_{0}
is determined by this trace. So the Elliott invariants of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{red}}(W_{1})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{red}}(W_{2})
are isomorphic, whereas the groups themselves are not isomorphic, because their commutation graphs are non-isomorphic.

Specialising Proposition
4.6
to the case
\mathbf{q}={\bf 1}
, we obtain the following result which recovers the size of a maximal clique in the commutation graph.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
(W,S)
be a right-angled Coxeter group and
c
the size of a maximal clique in the commutation graph of
(W,S)
. Then
\tau_{*}(\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{r}(W)))=\frac{1}{2^{c}}\mathbb{Z}
.

5
Classification of Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras by
\mathrm{K}
-theory

In this section we will focus on the case
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n}
and understand by means of this group to which extent K-theoretic invariants can be used to classify Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. The next example collects the necessary K-theoretic calculations.

Example 5.1
.

It follows from Dykema’s work on simplicity of free products of finite dimensional abelian
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and on traces on such free products
[
19
, Theorem 4.8]
that for the single parameter case
\mathbf{q}=(q,\dotsc,q)\in(0,1]^{S}
, the Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is

•

simple with a unique trace if and only if
q>\frac{1}{n-1}
,

•

has a character whose kernel is a simple, non-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with a unique trace if
q=\frac{1}{n-1}
, and

•

a direct sum of a one dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with a simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra having a unique trace if
q<\frac{1}{n-1}
.

Let us consider the first and last case and calculate the (unordered) Elliott invariant of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
. Applying Corollary
4.5
, we find that a basis of
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
is given by
[1],[p_{1}],\dotsc,[p_{n}]
, where
p_{i}\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
denotes the spectral projection of the i-th copy of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. By Proposition
4.6
, the trace pairing with the natural trace is then given under this identification by the row vector
(1,\frac{1}{1+q},\dotsc,\frac{1}{1+q})
. If
q>\frac{1}{n-1}
, this describes the complete unordered Elliott invariant. We thus assume from now on
q<\frac{1}{n-1}
and will explicitly identify the two extremal traces on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and their pairing with
\mathrm{K}
-theory.

It follows from Dykema’s
[
19
, Theorem 4.8]
that the one dimensional direct summand of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
is generated by the projection
p=\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}p_{i}
. One extremal trace on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
is thus characterised by the formula

\displaystyle\epsilon(x)p=xp\,\text{.}

Under the identification with
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
its pairing with
\mathrm{K}
-theory is hence given by the constant row vector
(1,\dotsc,1)
.

We will next identify the second extremal trace
\varphi
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
. It satisfies
\varphi(p)=0
, so that the ansatz
\tau=t\epsilon+(1-t)\varphi
yields
t=\tau(p)
and

\displaystyle\varphi=\frac{\tau-t\epsilon}{1-t}\,\text{.}

The calculation of
\tau(p)
follows from the explicit identification of Hecke eigenvectors made for example in
[
26
, Proof of Theorem 5.3]
and
[
45
, Proposition 2.2]
. The vector

\displaystyle\eta=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n}}q^{\frac{|w|}{2}}\delta_{w}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})

lies in the image of
p
and we have

\displaystyle p\delta_{e}=\langle\delta_{e},\frac{\eta}{\|\eta\|}\rangle\frac{\eta}{\|\eta\|}=\frac{\eta_{e}}{\|\eta\|^{2}}\eta\,\text{.}

Hence

\displaystyle t=\tau(p)=\frac{\eta_{e}^{2}}{\|\eta\|^{2}}=\left(\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n}}q^{|w|}\right)^{-1}=\frac{1-(n-1)q}{1+q}\,\text{.}

Here we used the calcuation of the growth series for
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n}
, which is based on the fact that the number
s_{k}
of elements of length
k\in\mathbb{N}
is

\displaystyle s_{k}=\begin{cases}1&k=0\\
n(n-1)^{k-1}&k\neq 0\,\text{.}\end{cases}

Indeed, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\eta\|^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}s_{k}q^{k}=1+n\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(n-1)^{k}q^{k+1}=1+\frac{nq}{1-(n-1)q}=\frac{1+q}{1-(n-1)q}\,\text{.}

For the spectral projection
p_{i}
associated to the Coxeter generator, we find that

\displaystyle\varphi(p_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau(p_{i})-t\epsilon(p_{i})}{1-t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{1+q}-\frac{1-(n-1)q}{1+q}}{1-\frac{1-(n-1)q}{1+q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)q}{1+q}\cdot\frac{1+q}{1+q-1+(n-1)q}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)q}{1-(n-1)q}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\,\text{.}

It follows that the K-theory pairing of
\varphi
under the identification
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{*n})
is given by the row vector

\displaystyle\left(1,\frac{n-1}{n},\dotsc,\frac{n-1}{n}\right)\,\text{.}

In order to exploit the explicit calculations made in Example
5.1
, we need two auxiliary lemmas. The following elementary lemma is well-known.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
x,y\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
. Then
\mathbb{Z}+x\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}+y\mathbb{Z}
if and only if either

(i)

x=y
, or

(ii)

x,y
are rational and have the same order in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

If
\mathbb{Z}+x\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}+y\mathbb{Z}
holds, then there exist integers
k,l,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
x=k+ly
and
y=m+nx
. Hence
x=k+l(m+nx)
, so that
x(1-ln)\in\mathbb{Z}
holds. On the one hand, if
x
is irrational, we must have
ln=1
. Since
x,y\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, substituting
l=\pm 1
in the equality
x=k+ly
implies that
x=y
. On the other hand, if
x
is rational and
d\in\mathbb{N}
is the smallest non-zero positive integer such that
dx\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have
\frac{1}{d}\in\mathbb{Z}+x\mathbb{Z}
, and obviously also
x\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{Z}
. So
\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}+x\mathbb{Z}
. It then remains to note that if
c,d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
then
\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{c}\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{Z}
implies
c=d
.

The next result is a consequence of the lemma above and the classification of orbits of the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
action on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as studied for example in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and write
\mathbf{x}=(x,\dotsc,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbf{y}=(y,\dotsc,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

there exist
B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
C\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\mathbf{x}=C+B\mathbf{y}
;

(ii)

either
x=y
or both
x
and
y
are rational and have the same order in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Following
[
5
]
we define additive subgroups of
\mathbb{R}
by
G_{x}=\mathbb{Z}+x\mathbb{Z}
and
G_{y}=\mathbb{Z}+y\mathbb{Z}
. Note that the rank of
G_{x}
is one, if
x\in\mathbb{Q}
and two if
x\notin\mathbb{Q}
. Since
n\geq 3
, we may apply
[
5
, Corollary 16]
and infer that
(i)
holds if and only if
G_{x}=G_{y}
. The claim follows then from Lemma
5.2
.

The next theorem demonstrates the extend to which K-theoretic invariants are able to classify Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. We comment on the use of the unordered Elliott invariant in contrast to the full Elliott invariant in Remark
5.5
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and let
q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,1]
. Then the following statements hold true.

•

If
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and one of
q_{1}
or
q_{2}
lies in either of the sets
(0,\frac{1}{n-1})
,
\{\frac{1}{n-1}\}
,
(\frac{1}{n-1}\,1]
, then so does the other.

•

Assume
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in(\frac{1}{n-1},1]
.

–

If
q_{1}
or
q_{2}
is irrational, then
q_{1}=q_{2}
.

–

If
q_{1}
or
q_{2}
is rational, then so is the other. Further,
\frac{1}{1+q_{1}}
and
\frac{1}{1+q_{2}}
have the same order in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
.

–

If
q_{1},q_{2}
are rational and
\frac{1}{1+q_{1}}
,
\frac{1}{1+q_{2}}
have the same order in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, then the unordered Elliott invariants of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
are isomorphic.

•

If
q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{n-1})
, then the unordered Elliott invariants of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

The division into the three different sets follows from Dykema’s results established in
[
19
]
. We first observe that if
q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{n-1})
then Example
5.1
shows that the unordered Elliott invariant of both Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras is up to isomorphism described by

•

\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
with order unit
(1,0,\dotsc,0)^{\mathrm{t}}
,

•

\mathrm{K}_{1}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong 0
,

•

\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n}))\cong[0,1]
,

•

the pairing of the trace
\epsilon
, corresponding to
0\in[0,1]
, is given by the row vector
(1,\dotsc,1)
, and

•

the pairing of the trace
\varphi
, corresponding to
1\in[0,1]
, is given by the row vector
(1,\frac{n-1}{n},\dotsc,\frac{n-1}{n})
.

So it remains to investigate the case
q_{1},q_{2}\in(\frac{1}{n-1},1]
. The unordered Elliott invariant of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
is calculated in Example
5.1
.

•

\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
with order unit
(1,0,\dotsc,0)^{\mathrm{t}}
,

•

\mathrm{K}_{1}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})\cong 0
,

•

\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n}))\cong\{0\}
, and

•

the pairing of the unique trace
\tau
is given by the row vector
(1,\frac{1}{1+q_{i}},\dotsc,\frac{1}{1+q_{i}})
.

Automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
are given by left multiplication with
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}
. Such automorphism preserves the unit of
\mathrm{K}
-theory
(1,0,\dotsc,0)^{\mathrm{t}}
if and only if

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}1&C\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}

with
B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
C\in\mathbb{Z}^{1\times n}
. Further, the pairing with the unique trace is preserved if and only if

\displaystyle(1,y)=\left(1,\frac{1}{1+q_{2}},\dotsc,\frac{1}{1+q_{2}}\right)=\left(1,\frac{1}{1+q_{1}},\dotsc,\frac{1}{1+q_{1}}\right)A=\left(1,xB+C\right)\,\text{,}

where
y=(\frac{1}{1+q_{2}},\dotsc,\frac{1}{1+q_{2}})
and
x=(\frac{1}{1+q_{1}},\dotsc,\frac{1}{1+q_{1}})
. So Lemma
5.3
applies and says that the unordered Elliott invariants of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
are isomorphic if and only if one of the following two statements hold: either
\frac{1}{1+q_{1}}
and
\frac{1}{1+q_{2}}
are irrational and equal to each other; or
\frac{1}{1+q_{1}}
and
\frac{1}{1+q_{2}}
are rational and have the same order in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. The first case accounts exactly for
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
being irrational. The second case accounts exactly for
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
being rational.

Remark 5.5
.

In Theorem
5.4
, we only make use of the unordered Elliott invariant. In contrast, the classification programme for amenable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras considers the order structure on the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group as part of their invariant. In practice in all situations where the order structure could be calculated, it turned out to be actually described by the trace pairing of the Elliott invariant. Calculating the order structure of
\mathrm{K}
-theory for free products, extending work of Dykema-Rørdam
[
20
]
beyond the so called Avitzour condition, seems to be a challenging task that deserves separate attention.

Remark 5.6
.

Despite the limits of classification results for Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras with rational parameters described by Theorem
5.4
, one can make the following observation. Given
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
, the Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n})
with parameter
q=\frac{1}{d}
does recognise
d
as long as
d\leq n-1
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+q}=\frac{1}{\frac{1+d}{d}}=\frac{d}{d+1}

has order
d+1
in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. This observation applies to the Iwahori-Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra associated with groups acting strongly transitively on regular buildings of type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}^{*n}
and thickness
d\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}
. See
[
45
, Section 2]
for details on the relation between Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and Iwahori-Hecke
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Regression discontinuity design with right-censored survival data
Emil Aas Stoltenberg emilstoltenberg@gmail.com

Abstract
In this paper the regression discontinuity design is adapted to the survival analysis setting with right-censored data, studied in an intensity based counting process framework. In particular, a local polynomial regression version of the Aalen additive hazards estimator is introduced as an estimator of the difference between two covariate dependent cumulative hazard rate functions. Large-sample theory for this estimator is developed, including confidence intervals that take into account the uncertainty associated with bias correction. As is standard in the causality literature, the models and the theory are embedded in the potential outcomes framework. Two general results concerning potential outcomes and the multiplicative hazards model for survival data are presented.



Keywords:
Asymptotic bias; bias correction; causality; censoring; confounding; counting processes; hazard rate functions; intensity processes; martingales; multiplicative hazard model; treatment effects.

Introduction

The regression discontinuity design is a widely used technique for causal inference in economics, political science, and sociology (see
Van der Klaauw, 2008
,
Imbens and Lemieux, 2008
,
Lee and Lemieux, 2010
, and
Cattaneo and Titiunik, 2022
for recent reviews). In the regression discontinuity design, possible confounders are ‘controlled for’ by exploiting that units are assigned to treatment based on whether their value of an observed covariate is above or below some known cut-off, the idea being that units with values of this observed covariate just above the known cut-off are similar to the subjects with values just below the cut-off.

For situations where the observed outcomes are noncensored random variables, the inference theory, building on that of local polynomial regression, is well developed (see e.g.,
Fan and Gijbels, 1996
,
Hahn et al., 2001
, and
Calonico et al., 2014a
;
Calonico et al., 2014b
, and the reviews above). In this paper we extend the regression discontinuity design to the survival analysis setting. In particular, we study the regression discontinuity design applied to right-censored survival data in an intensity based counting process framework (see
Andersen et al., 1993
, and
Aalen et al., 2008
;
Aalen et al., 2010
).
The survival analysis models we study are all instances of the broad class of multiplicative intensity models, meaning that the modelling and inference revolves around the hazard rate function.

This ends our description of our basic modelling assumptions. The reader familiar with the counting process approach to, and martingale methods in, survival analysis will see that the above is nothing more than what one gets the when taking the potential outcomes framework seriously and applying it to the standard intensity based counting process approach to survival analysis. In fact, the presentation so far is just a slight notational and semantic reformulation of the theory presented in Chapter III.2 of
Andersen et al., 1993

1.4

N(t)=XN^{1}(t)+(1-X)N^{0}(t),

(1.5)

Y(t)=XY^{1}(t)+(1-X)Y^{0}(t).

(1.6)

\mathcal{E}_{t}=\sigma(\{N(s),Y(s)\}_{s\leq t})

Lemma 1.1
.

Assume that (
1.2
) and (

\mathcal{G}_{t}
-intensity of the observed counting process

is

I_{X=g}M^{g}(t)=I_{X=g}\{N^{g}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}Y^{g}(s)\alpha_{g}(s,Z,U)\,{\rm d}s\},

(1.8)

is a locally square integrable martingale with respect to

Proof.

Define
\mathscr{G}_{t}=\mathscr{G}_{t}^{0}\vee\mathscr{G}_{t}^{1}
. It follows directly from the theory on independent right-censoring

Andersen et al., 1993

-intensity of

as a constant process in

\mathcal{G}_{t}\subset\mathscr{G}_{t}

-intensity process,

Since

, in terms of the observed at-risk process in (

This entails that

Remark 3.6
.

There is an interesting affinity between Theorem
3.5
of the present paper and Theorem 2.1 in
Hjort, 1992
. Hjort’s central limit theorem pertains to parametric hazard rate functions under model misspecification. In that theorem, an extra term corresponding to the
L_{g,p,n}
appears. This term is due to the difference
\alpha(t,\theta_{\rm lf})-\alpha_{0}(t)
between the parametric model
\alpha(t,\theta)
, evaluated in the least false parameter value
\theta_{\rm lf}
, and the true hazard
\alpha_{0}(t)
, that is the hazard under which
N(t)-\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)\alpha(s)\,{\rm d}s
is a martingale. In our case it is the difference
\alpha_{g}(t,Z_{i},U_{i})-\bar{\alpha}_{g}(t,Z_{i})
that cause the extra term to appear, but this difference is a close nonparametric relative of the difference studied by Hjort. The misspecification in Theorem
3.5
occurs because the
\mathcal{F}
-hazard
\bar{\alpha}_{g}(t,Z_{i})
is a form of model misspecifiation when the analysis takes place with respect to the filtration
\mathcal{G}_{t}
.

For the results in the remainder of the paper, we use the
\mathcal{F}
-central limit theorem in Theorem
3.3
. There are two reasons for this. First, estimating the limiting variance of the martingale is more straightforward than estimating the variances and covariances appearing in Theorem
3.5
. Second, the common approach in the regression discontinuity literature is to condition on the forcing variable, and average out all other covariates (see the discussion in the Section
1
).

This means that a consistent estimator of the limiting variance of the sequence

3.4
Bias correction

In this section we follow the conventional bias correction approach in local polynomial regression (see, e.g.,
Fan and Gijbels, 1996
) and study the estimators given by

\widehat{\Theta}_{p,q}^{(\nu),{\rm bc}}(t,h,b)=\widehat{\Theta}_{1,p,q}^{(\nu),{\rm bc}}(t,h,b)-\widehat{\Theta}_{0,p,q}^{(\nu),{\rm bc}}(t,h,b),\quad\text{$q\geq p+1$}

where

\widehat{\Theta}_{g,p,q}^{(\nu),{\rm bc}}(t,h,b)=\widehat{\Theta}_{g,p}^{(\nu)}(t,h)-h^{p+1-\nu}e_{p,\nu}^{{\rm t}}\nu!\frac{\Gamma_{p}^{-1}\vartheta_{p,p+1}}{(p+1)!}\widehat{\Theta}_{g,q}^{(p+1)}(t,b),

(3.16)

Here
b
is a so-called pilot bandwidth sequence, typically larger than

(because it is harder to estimate the
(p+1)
th derivative than the
\nu
th derivative when

, as it is here). In (

) the second term on the right is an estimator of the bias term appearing in the limiting distribution of Corollary
3.4
. Under certain conditions on the bandwidth sequences

b
made precise below, subtracting off a bias estimate removes the asymptotic bias term from the limiting distribution in Corollary
3.4
, even when

tends to a positive constant. In particular, the mean squared error optimal bandwidth
h\propto n^{-1/(2p+3)}
may be employed, and the limiting martingale processes are the same as those given in said corollary.

As pointed out by
Calonico et al., 2014a
, this large-sample approximation relies on the condition

as

, which makes the variability of the bias correction estimate disappear. That is, provided

, the estimator
\widehat{\Theta}_{g,q}^{(p+1)}(t,b)

is never zero in finite samples, the idea of

as

, and thereby get limiting distributions of the estimator sequence where the variability of the bias estimator is accounted for. These ideas are formalised in Corollary

below.

By Lemma
3.1
and using the decomposition in (

) twice, we obtain

\begin{split}&(nh^{2\nu+1})^{1/2}\{\widehat{\Theta}_{g,p,q}^{(\nu),{\rm bc}}(t,h,b)-\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\alpha}_{g}^{(\nu)}(s,z_{0})\,{\rm d}s\}\\

From Lemma

(iii) in the appendix, this approximation holds with probability one when

4
Concluding remarks

The ideas underlying designs such as the regression discontinuity design, the difference in difference design, and the intstrumental variable design are not bound to any particular estimand, model, or type of data. The statistical theory for these designs, however, are much more developed for the type of estimands, models, and data often encountered in economics, than for estimands, models, and data typically encountered in other fields of application. This paper is an attempt at taking one of these designs from the estimation of a conditional average treatment effect (
cate
) based on uncensored data, to the estimation of a
cate
-like object, namely the difference of two cumulative hazards, based on right-censored survival data.

A few directions the results of the present paper can be extended in are: First, the estimator developed in this paper can be used as a building block in the estimation of
\theta(t,z_{0})
using kernel smoothing techniques similar to those introduced by
Ramlau-Hansen, 1983
(research in this direction is underway). Second, the results of this paper is limited to the sharp regression discontinuity design, and ought to be extended to the fuzzy regression discontinuity design. Third, the estimator of this paper can be used to test whether the parameter of interest in a Cox regression model with unobserved confounders equals zero or not, but it can not provide asymptotically unbiased estimates of this parameter. Whether the regression discontinuity design can be used to identify the parameter of interest in proportional hazards models under confounding, can be studied.

\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{E}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{t-}
, and so
\sigma(\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}})\subset\mathcal{F}_{t-}
. We conclude that
\mathcal{F}_{t-}=\sigma(\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}})
.

The key to the modifications argument is the following: For any event
C
in
\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}}
, meaning that
C=A\cap B
for
A\in\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{E}}
and
B\in\sigma(Z)
,

\{T\geq t\}\cap C=\{T\geq t\}\cap B\in\mathcal{Z}_{t},

(A.1)

with
B\in\sigma(Z)
. This is because

\{T\geq t\}\subset\{T\geq s\},\quad\text{for all $s\leq t$},

and

\{T\geq t\}\subset\{N(s)=0\},\quad\text{for all $s<t$},

which means that there is no set in
\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{E}}
contained in
\{T\geq t\}=\{Y(t)=1\}
, and therefore
\{T\geq t\}\cap A=\{T\geq t\}
for all
A\in\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{E}}
, which implies (
A.1
).

Define
\zeta_{t}
by

\zeta_{t}=I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})+I_{T<t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-}),

(A.2)

by which we mean that
\zeta_{t}
is a modification of the process on the right. Let
C
be any event in
\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}}
. Then
\{T\geq t\}\cap C\in\mathcal{Z}_{t}
by (
A.1
). Since
\{T<t\}\in\mathcal{F}_{t-}
and
C\in\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}}\subset\mathcal{F}_{t-}
, it is the case that
\{T<t\}\cap C\in\mathcal{F}_{t-}
. Therefore, by the definition of conditional expectation, used twice, we get that for any
C\in\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}}

\begin{split}{\rm E}\,I_{C}\zeta_{t}&={\rm E}\,I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap C}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})+{\rm E}\,I_{\{T<t\}\cap C}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})\\
&={\rm E}\,I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap C}\xi_{t}+{\rm E}\,I_{\{T<t\}\cap C}\xi_{t}={\rm E}\,I_{C}\xi_{t}.\end{split}

(A.3)

This shows that for fixed
t
, the set functions
C\mapsto{\rm E}\,I_{C}\zeta_{t}
and
C\mapsto{\rm E}\,I_{C}\xi_{t}
agree on the
\pi
-system
\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}}
.
By assumption
{\rm E}\,\sup_{t\leq\tau}\xi_{t}<\infty
(recall also that
\xi_{t}\geq 0
). Therefore
{\rm E}\,I_{\Omega}\xi_{t}\leq{\rm E}\,\xi_{t}\leq{\rm E}\,\sup_{t\leq\tau}\xi_{t}<\infty
, and since
\zeta_{t}\leq{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})+{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})
a.s., we have
{\rm E}\,I_{\Omega}\zeta_{t}={\rm E}\,\zeta_{t}\leq 2{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t})\leq 2\,{\rm E}\,\sup_{t\leq\tau}\xi_{t}<\infty
. This shows that both
C\mapsto{\rm E}\,I_{C}\zeta_{t}
and
C\mapsto{\rm E}\,I_{C}\xi_{t}
are finite measures on
\mathcal{F}_{t-}
. By Dynkin’s lemma, two finite measures that agree on a
\pi
-system agree on the
\sigma
-algebra generated by that
\pi
-system (see, e.g.,
Williams, 1991
). Since
\Pi_{t-}^{\mathcal{F}}
generates
\mathcal{F}_{t-}
, this means that (
A.3
) holds for any
C\in\mathcal{F}_{t-}
, and we can conclude that,

\zeta_{t}={\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-}),\;\text{a.s., for all $t$},

(A.4)

which is to say that
\zeta_{t}
is a modification of
{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})
.

Indistinguishable
: We now show that
\zeta_{t}
and
{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})
are both a.s. left-continuous for
t\in[0,\tau]
, as this implies that they are indistinguishable (see, e.g.,
Jacod and Shiryaev, 2003
or
Cohen and Elliott, 2015
).

By the triangle inequality, using linearity of conditional expectation and Jensen’s inequality
(
Cohen and Elliott, 2015
, Lemma 2.4.11, p. 58)
, we have

\lvert{\rm E}\,(\xi_{s}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})-{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})\lvert\,\leq{\rm E}\,(\lvert\xi_{s}-\xi_{t}\lvert\,\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})+\lvert{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})-{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})\lvert,

(A.5)

almost surely. The process
\xi_{t}
is a.s. left-continuous. Given
\varepsilon>0
, we can find
\delta>0
so that
\lvert\xi_{s}-\xi_{t}\lvert\,\leq\varepsilon/2
a.s. whenever
s\in(t-\delta,t)
, and therefore, by monotonicity of conditional expectation,
{\rm E}\,(\lvert\xi_{s}-\xi_{t}\lvert\,\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})<\varepsilon/2
a.s. whenever
s\in(t-\delta,t)
. This show that the first term on the right in (
A.5
) is a.s. left-continuous. For the second term, keep
t
fixed and let
\eta_{s}
be a modification of
{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s})
, thus

\eta_{s}={\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}),\;\text{a.s.\penalty\ for all $s$}.

Without loss of generality, we can take
\eta_{s}
to be an uniformly integrable martingale
(
Jacod and Shiryaev, 2003
, Theorem I.1.42, p. 11)
and càdlàg (by right-continuity of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, see, e.g. Corollary 5.1.9 in
Cohen and Elliott, 2015
). The process
\eta_{s-}=\lim_{u\uparrow s}\eta_{u}
is left-continuous and predictable
(
Jacod and Shiryaev, 2003
, Proposition 2.5, p. 17)
, and from Theorem 6.2.18 in
Cohen and Elliott, 2015
, we have that
\eta_{S-}={\rm E}\,(\eta_{S}\mid\mathcal{F}_{S-})
for any predictable stopping time
S
. But then

\eta_{S-}={\rm E}\,(\eta_{S}\mid\mathcal{F}_{S-})={\rm E}\,({\rm E}\,\{\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{S}\}\mid\mathcal{F}_{S-})={\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{S-}),\quad\text{a.s.},

(A.6)

for any predictable stopping time
S
. The processes
\eta_{s-}
and
{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})
are both predictable. Combining this fact with (
A.6
) gives that
\eta_{s-}
and
{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})
are indistinguishable
(
Jacod and Shiryaev, 2003
, Proposition I.2.18, p. 20)
. Hence,
s\mapsto{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})
must be a.s. left-continuous since
\eta_{s-}
is a.s. left-continuous. This means that given
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\delta>0
such that
\lvert{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})-{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})\lvert\,<\varepsilon/2
a.s. whenever
s\in(t-\delta,t)
. In summary, given
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\delta>0
such that

\lvert{\rm E}\,(\xi_{s}\mid\mathcal{F}_{s-})-{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})\lvert\,<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon,\quad\text{a.s.},

whenever
s\in(t-\delta,t)
, which is the desired result.

Next, we show that
\zeta_{t}
is a.s. left-continuous. Recall that
\zeta_{t}
is a modification of
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})+I_{T<t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})
. The process
I_{T<t}
is left-continuous, and we have just shown that
{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-})
is left-continuous. It therefore suffices to show that
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})
is a.s. left-continuous. Assume without loss of generality that
Z\geq 0
. By assumption,
Z
has density
f_{Z}(z)
with support
[z_{1},z_{2}]
, we can therefore, without loss of generality, take
[z_{1},z_{2}]=[0,1]
. For
n=1,2,\ldots
and
k=0,1,\ldots,2^{n}
, let
G_{n,k}=Z^{-1}[k/2^{n},(k+1)/2^{n})
, define the sequence of simple functions given by

Z_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}}\frac{k}{2^{n}}I_{G_{n,k}},

and define
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n}=\sigma(Y(t))\vee\sigma(Z_{n})
. Let
S
be a predictable stopping time w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. The
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n}
evaluated in
S
is

\mathcal{Z}_{S}^{n}=\sigma(Y(S))\vee\sigma(Z_{n})=\{\{T\geq S\},\{T<S\},\emptyset,\Omega\}\vee\sigma(Z_{n}).

For each
n
and
k
, the set
\{T\geq S\}\cap G_{n,k}
has no nonempty proper subset in
\mathcal{Z}_{S}^{n}
(this is because the
G_{n,0},\ldots,G_{n,2^{n}}
are disjoint). From Lemma
A.2
in the appendix we therefore get that

I_{T\geq S}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{S}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{S}^{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}}I_{\{T\geq S\}\cap G_{n,k}}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{S}\mid\{T\geq S\}\cap G_{n,k}),\quad\text{a.s.},

(A.7)

which entails that the processes (in
t
)
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})
and
\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}}I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})
are indistinguishable
(
Jacod and Shiryaev, 2003
, Proposition I.2.18, p. 20)
. We now show that the process
\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}}I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})
is a.s. left-continuous. Almost all the sample paths
t\mapsto I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}}
are left-continuous, and, by the definition of conditional expectation,

{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})=\frac{{\rm E}\,\xi_{t}I_{T\geq t}I_{G_{n,k}}}{{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})}={\rm Pr}(G_{n,k})\frac{{\rm E}\,\xi_{t}I_{T\geq t}I_{G_{n,k}}}{{\rm E}\,I_{T\geq t}I_{G_{n,k}}}.

Since
\xi_{t}
and
I_{T\geq t}
are a.s. left-continuous,
\lim_{s\uparrow t}\xi_{s}I_{\{T\geq s\}}I_{G_{n,k}}=\xi_{t}I_{\{T\geq t\}}I_{G_{n,k}}
a.s. Clearly
\lvert\xi_{t}I_{\{T\geq t\}}I_{G_{n,k}}\lvert\,\leq\xi_{t}
, and by assumption
{\rm E}\,\lvert\xi_{t}\lvert\,<\infty
, so by the dominated convergence theorem,
\lim_{s\uparrow t}{\rm E}\,\xi_{s}I_{\{T\geq s\}}I_{G_{n,k}}={\rm E}\,\xi_{t}I_{\{T\geq t\}}I_{G_{n,k}}
. Essentially the same argument shows that
\lim_{s\uparrow t}{\rm E}\,I_{T\geq s}I_{G_{n,k}}={\rm E}\,I_{T\geq t}I_{G_{n,k}}
. We then have that
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})
is indistinguishable from an a.s. left-continuous process, hence it is itself a.s. left-continuous.

The sequence of sets
\{G_{n,0},\ldots,G_{n,2^{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
is nested in the sense that for any
n\geq 1
, a set
G_{n,k}
can be written as a union,
G_{n+1,j}\cap G_{n+1,j+1}
say. This nestedness property entails that
\sigma(Z_{n})\subset\sigma(Z_{n+1})
for all
n
, and therefore
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n+1}
for all
n
. This means that
(\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a filtration, and, since for each
t
the random variable
\xi_{t}
is integrable, Theorem 7.23 in
Kallenberg, 2002
gives that as
n\to\infty
,

{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})\to{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\infty}),\;\text{a.s.}

where
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\infty}=\vee_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n}
. The dyadic rationals are dense in
[0,1]
, which means that the sets
G_{n,k}
for
k=0,1,\ldots,2^{n},n=1,2,\ldots
generate
\sigma(Z)
. Since
\sigma(Z)
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra that contains all the
G_{n,k}
,
\sigma(Z)\subset\vee_{n\geq 1}\sigma(Z_{n})
. For each
n
,
\sigma(Z_{n})\subset\sigma(Z)
and
\sigma(Z_{n})\subset\vee_{n\geq 1}\sigma(Z_{n})
, but
\vee_{n\geq 1}\sigma(Z_{n})
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra that contains all the
\sigma(Z_{n})
, thus
\vee_{n\geq 1}\sigma(Z_{n})\subset\sigma(Z)
, consequently
\vee_{n\geq 1}\sigma(Z_{n})=\sigma(Z)
, and we conclude that
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\infty}=\mathcal{Z}_{t}=\sigma(Y(t))\vee\sigma(Z)
.

We now have that
\{{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of a.s. left-continuous processes, and that
{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})\to{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})
a.s. for each
t
as
n\to\infty
. In order to conclude that
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})
is a.s. left-continuous, we must show that the convergence of
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})
to
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})
is uniform in
t
. Write

\mu_{n,k}(t)={\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}),\;\text{for $k=0,\ldots,2^{n}$ and $n\geq 1$},

so that
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})=I_{T\geq t}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}}I_{G_{n,k}}\mu_{n,k}(t)
a.s. Suppose that
G_{n+1,j}
and
G_{n+1,j+1}
are such that
G_{n,k}=G_{n+1,j}\cup G_{n+1,j+1}
. Then, on the event
G_{n+1,j}
,

\begin{split}&\lvert I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})-I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n+1})\lvert\\
&\qquad=I_{T\geq t}\lvert I_{G_{n,k}}\mu_{n,k}(t)-I_{G_{n+1,j}}\mu_{n+1,j}(t)-I_{G_{n+1,j+1}}\mu_{n+1,j+1}(t)\lvert\\
&\qquad=I_{T\geq t}\lvert I_{G_{n+1,j}}\{\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j}(t)\}+I_{G_{n+1,j+1}}\{\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j+1}(t)\}\lvert\\
&\qquad\leq I_{T\geq t}I_{G_{n+1,j}}\lvert\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j}(t)\lvert\,+I_{T\geq t}I_{G_{n+1,j+1}}\lvert\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j+1}(t)\lvert\\
&\qquad=I_{T\geq t}I_{G_{n+1,j}}\lvert\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j}(t)\lvert.\end{split}

The difference
\lvert\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j}(t)\lvert
is

\begin{split}&\lvert\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j}(t)\lvert=\lvert{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})-{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})\lvert\\
&\quad=\lvert\frac{{\rm E}\,\xi_{t}I_{\{T\geq t\}}I_{G_{n,k}}}{{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})}-\frac{{\rm E}\,\xi_{t}I_{\{T\geq t\}}I_{G_{n+1,j}}}{{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})}\lvert\\
&\quad\leq\frac{{\rm E}\,\lvert\xi_{t}\big(I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}}{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})-I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}}{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})\big)\lvert}{{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}){\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}\,\lVert I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}}{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})-I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}}{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})\rVert_{2}}{{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}){\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})},\end{split}

using Hölder’s inequality, and writing
\lVert X\rVert_{2}\,=({\rm E}\,X^{2})^{1/2}
. Here, by expanding the square on the last line above and taking expectations,

\begin{split}&\lVert I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}}{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})-I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}}{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
&\quad={\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k}){\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}){\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j+1}).\end{split}

Inserting the square root of this product in the expression for
\lvert\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j}(t)\lvert
above, and using that
{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j+1})\leq{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n,k})
, we get

I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}}\lvert\mu_{n,k}(t)-\mu_{n+1,j}(t)\lvert\,\leq\frac{\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}}}{{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})^{1/2}}.

(A.8)

By assumption,
z\mapsto{\rm Pr}(T\geq\tau\mid Z=z)
is bounded below on
[0,1]
, say
{\rm Pr}(T\geq\tau\mid Z=z)\geq c>0
, and, also by assumption, the density
f_{Z}(z)
of
Z
is bounded below on
[0,1]
. Let
f_{Z,\min}>0
be the lower bound of
f_{Z}(z)
, and denote
F_{Z}(z)
the cumulative distribution function of
Z
. Using these bounds and the mean value theorem, we have

\begin{split}{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{m,j})&\geq{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq\tau\}\cap G_{m,j})=\int_{j/2^{m}}^{(j+1)/2^{m}}{\rm Pr}(T\geq\tau\mid Z=z)f_{Z}(z)\,{\rm d}z\\
&\geq c\{F((j+1)/2^{m})-F(j/2^{m})\}=cf_{Z}(d_{j})\frac{1}{2^{m}}\geq cf_{Z,\min}\,\frac{1}{2^{m}},\end{split}

where
d_{j}
is some point in
G_{m,j}
. Inserting this lower bound in place of the denominator on the right hand side in (
A.8
), we get

\frac{\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}}}{{\rm Pr}(\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j})^{1/2}}\leq\frac{\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}I_{\{T\geq t\}\cap G_{n+1,j}}}{cf_{Z,\min}\,2^{-(n+1)/2}}\leq\frac{\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}I_{G_{n+1,j}}}{cf_{Z,\min}\,2^{-(n+1)/2}}.

This gives that

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\,\lvert I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})-I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n+1})\lvert\,\leq\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}\,\frac{I_{G_{n+1,j}}}{cf_{Z,\min}\,2^{-(n+1)/2}},\quad\text{a.s.},

where, we recall,
\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}=({\rm E}\,\xi_{t}^{2})^{1/2}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, by Markov’s inequality

\begin{split}&{\rm Pr}\big(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\,\lvert I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})-I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n+1})\lvert\,>\varepsilon\big)\\
&\qquad\leq{\rm Pr}\big(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}\,\frac{I_{G_{n+1,j}}}{cf_{Z,\min}\,2^{-(n+1)/2}}\geq\varepsilon\big)\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lVert\xi_{t}\rVert_{2}\frac{{\rm Pr}(G_{n+1,j})}{cf_{Z,\min}\,2^{-(n+1)/2}}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{f_{Z,\max}\,2^{-(n+1)/2}}{cf_{Z,\min}},\end{split}

where
f_{Z,\max}=\sup_{z\in[0,1]}f_{Z}(z)<\infty
, since
f_{Z}(z)
is continuous. Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Pr}\big(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\,\lvert I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n})-I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{n+1})\lvert\,>\varepsilon\big)\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n/2}}=1+\sqrt{2},

so by the Borel–Cantelli lemma, the Cauchy criterion (see e.g.,
Rudin, 1976
) holds with probability one. It then follows from Lemma 5.5.6 in
Cohen and Elliott, 2015
that the limit
I_{T\geq t}{\rm E}\,(\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{Z}_{t})
is a.s. left-continuous.
∎

Remark A.3
.

A theorem in statistics that also uses the martingale convergence theorem (see
Kallenberg, 2002
) that is used in the proof of Lemma
1.2
, is Doob’s consistency theorem for posterior distributions in Bayesian nonparametric statistics. See
Miller, 2018
for a detailed treatment.

A.3
Sequences involving the at-risk processes

When working with versions of the Aalen additive hazards model one often encounters averages involving integrals of the form
\int_{0}^{t}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q(Z_{i})Y_{i}(s)\,{\rm d}s
, for covariates
Z_{i}
, at-risk processes
Y_{i}(t)=I\{T_{i}\geq t\}
, and a function
Q
that may be real-valued, a vector, or a matrix, and one needs to ascertain whether such integrals converge in probability uniformly in
t
. If the pairs
(T_{i},Z_{i}),\,i=1,\ldots,n
are i.i.d., then the law of large numbers yields
n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q(Z_{i})Y_{i}(t)\to_{p}{\rm E}\,Q(Z_{1})Y_{1}(t)
for each
t
. In order to conclude that

\int_{0}^{t}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q(Z_{i})Y_{i}(s)\,{\rm d}s\overset{p}{\to}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm E}\,\{Q(Z_{1})Y_{1}(s)\}\,{\rm d}s,

we need something stronger than pointwise convergence in probability of the integrand on the left to the integrand on the right. A sufficient condition is that
\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\lVert n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q(Z_{i})Y_{i}(s)-{\rm E}\,\{Q(Z_{1})Y_{1}(s)\}\rVert\to_{p}0
. Other conditions than convergence in probability of the integrands uniformly in
t
may be imposed, see for example
Andersen et al., 1993
and
Hjort and Pollard, 1993
. In the next lemma, used repeatedly in the present paper, we establish conditions under which the convergence in probability of
n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q(Z_{i})Y_{i}(t)
to
{\rm E}\,\{Q(Z_{1})Y_{1}(t)\}
is uniform in
t
. Not surprisingly, parts of the proofs is rather similar to the proof Glivenko–Cantelli theorem, see for example
van der Vaart, 1998
or
Ferguson, 1996
. The full force of the almost sure convergence in the lemma below, rather than mere convergence in probability, is, in the present paper, only of importance as regards
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert J_{n,h}(t)-1\lvert\,\to 0
a.s. See for example the proof of Corollary
3.4
of the main text.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
(T_{i},\delta_{i},Z_{i},C_{i}),\,i=1,\ldots,n
be independent replicates of
(T,\delta,Z,C)
, for
T=\widetilde{T}\wedge C
on
[0,\tau]
, with
\widetilde{T}
the uncensored lifetime; indicators for non-censoring
\delta=I\{\widetilde{T}\leq C\}
, censoring times
C
, and a covariate
Z
. Let
Y_{i}(t)=I\{T_{i}\geq t\}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
Y(t)I\{T\geq t\}
be the at-risk indicators. Assume that
C
stems from an absolutely continuous distribution with distribution function
H
such that
H(\tau)<1
; and that
\widetilde{T}\perp\!\!\!\perp C\mid Z
.

(a)

Set
F(t\mid z)={\rm Pr}(\widetilde{T}\leq t\mid Z=z)
, suppose that
F(\tau\mid z)<1
for all
z\in(-\kappa_{0},-\kappa_{0})
, that
F(t\mid z)
is continuous in
t\in[0,\tau]
for all
z
, and that
F(t\mid z)
continuous for
z\in(-\kappa_{0},\kappa_{0})
for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.

(b)

For some
\kappa>0
, let
q(u)
be a nonnegative function that
(i)
is zero outside of
[0,\kappa]
;
(ii)
is continuous on
(0,\kappa)
, and
(iii)
is such that
\int_{0}^{\kappa}q(u)^{2}\,{\rm d}u<\infty
.

(c)

Let
\xi(t,z)
be a non-negative function that is continuous in
t\in[0,\tau]
for all
z
, and continuous for
z\in(-\kappa_{0},\kappa_{0})
for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.

(d)

The covariate
Z
has a density
f_{Z}(z)
that is continuous on
(-\kappa_{0},\kappa_{0})
.

Define
q_{h}(z)=q(z/h)/h
for
h>0
, and denote
G(t)=\lim_{h\to 0}{\rm E}\,q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)Y(t)
. Existence of this limit is part of the claim of the lemma. Then

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{h}(Z_{i})\xi(t,Z_{i})Y_{i}(t)-G(t)\lvert\to 0,

(A.9)

almost surely, as
nh\to\infty
and
h\to 0
.

Proof.

Denote
G_{n,h}(t)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{h}(Z_{i})Y_{i}(t)
,
G_{h}(t)={\rm E}\,G_{n,h}(t)
, and
G(t)=\lim_{h\to 0}G_{h}(t)
. Let
\bar{h}>0
be a number such that
\bar{h}\kappa<\kappa_{0}
. If we restrict
f_{Z}(z)
and
\xi(t,z)
to
z\in[0,\bar{h}\kappa]\subset[0,\kappa_{0})
, then since
[0,\bar{h}\kappa]
and
[0,\tau]\times[0,\bar{h}\kappa]
are compact sets, assumptions (c) and (d) entail that the restrictions of
f_{Z}(z)
and
\xi(t,z)
are bounded above. We denote these bounds by
f_{Z,\max}
and
\xi_{\max}
, respectively.

By the i.i.d. assumption and the conditional independence
\widetilde{T}\perp\!\!\!\perp C\mid Z
, the function
G_{h}(t)
is

\begin{split}G_{h}(t)&={\rm E}\,G_{n,h}(t)={\rm E}\,q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)Y(t)={\rm E}\,[q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z){\rm E}\,(Y(t)\mid Z)]\\
&=\{1-H(t)\}\,{\rm E}\,[q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)\{1-F(t\mid Z)\}].\end{split}

By a change of variable,

\begin{split}{\rm E}\,[q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)\{1-F(t\mid Z)]&=\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h\kappa}q(z/h)\xi(t,z)\{1-F(t\mid z)\}f_{Z}(z)\,{\rm d}z\\
&=\int_{0}^{\kappa}q(u)\xi(t,hu)\{1-F(t\mid hu)\}f_{Z}(hu)\,{\rm d}u.\end{split}

When
h
is so
h\kappa<\bar{h}\kappa
, then

\lvert q(u)\xi(t,hu)\{1-F(t\mid hu)\}f_{Z}(hu)\lvert\,\leq\lvert q(u)\lvert\,\xi_{\max}f_{Z,\max},

and by Hölder’s inequality

\int_{0}^{\kappa}\lvert q(u)\lvert\,{\rm d}u\leq\kappa^{1/2}(\int_{0}^{\kappa}q(u)^{2}\,{\rm d}u)^{1/2}<\infty,

(A.10)

by Assumption (b)(iii). Thus, by dominated convergence,

G_{h}(t)=G(t)+o(1),\;\text{for each $t$ as $h\to\infty$},

with

G(t)=\{1-H(t)\}\xi(t,0)\{1-F(t\mid z_{0})\}f_{Z}(0)\int_{0}^{\kappa}q(u){\rm d}u,

Next, we show that the convergence
G_{h}(t)\to G(t)
as
h\to 0
is uniform over
[0,\tau]
. Let
h_{n}
be a nonincreasing sequence of positive numbers such that
h_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Assume that
n<m
, so that
h_{m}/h_{n}<1
, and that
h_{m}\kappa\leq\bar{h}\kappa<\kappa_{0}
, then

\begin{split}\lvert G_{h_{n}}(t)-G_{h_{m}}(t)\lvert&\leq\{1-H(t)\}\,{\rm E}\,[\lvert q_{h_{n}}(Z)-q_{h_{m}}(Z)\lvert\,\xi(t,Z)\{1-F(t\mid Z)\}]\\
&\leq\xi_{\max}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert h_{n}^{-1}q(z/h_{n})-h_{m}^{-1}q(z/h_{m})\lvert\,f_{Z}(z)\,{\rm d}z\\
&\leq\xi_{\max}f_{Z,\max}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert(h_{m}/h_{n})q(uh_{m}/h_{n})-q(u)\lvert\,{\rm d}u.\end{split}

(A.11)

Since
h_{m}/h_{n}\to 1
as
n,m\to\infty
and
q(u)
is continuous on
(0,\kappa)
,

\lvert(h_{m}/h_{n})q(uh_{m}/h_{n})-q(u)\lvert\,\to 0,\;\text{as $n,m\to\infty$}.

Moreover,
\lvert(h_{m}/h_{n})q(uh_{m}/h_{n})-q(u)\lvert\,\leq\lvert q(uh_{m}/h_{n})\lvert\,+\lvert q(u)\lvert
and by a change of variable,
\int_{0}^{\infty}(\lvert q(uh_{m}/h_{n})\lvert\,+\lvert q(u)\lvert)\,{\rm d}u=\{(h_{n}/h_{m})+1\}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\lvert q(u)\lvert\,{\rm d}u<\infty
. Then
\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert(h_{m}/h_{n})q(uh_{m}/h_{n})-q(u)\lvert\,{\rm d}u\to 0
as
n,m\to\infty
by dominated convergence. Since the right hand side of (
A.11
) is independent of
t
and converges to zero, this means that for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find an
n_{0}\geq 1
such that
\lvert G_{h_{n}}(t)-G_{h_{m}}(t)\lvert\,<\varepsilon
whenever
n,m\geq n_{0}
. This shows that the sequence
h\to G_{h}(t)
satisfies the Cauchy criterion for uniform convergence (see, e.g.,
Rudin, 1976
), which entails that

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert G_{h}(t)-G(t)\lvert\,\to 0,\;\text{as $h\to 0$}.

(A.12)

We now turn to the convergence of
G_{n,h}(t)
to
G_{h}(t)
as
n\to\infty
. When
h\kappa\leq\bar{h}\kappa<\kappa_{0}
, and using the inequality in (
A.10
)

\begin{split}{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\lvert\,&=h^{-1}\int_{h\kappa_{a}}^{h\kappa_{b}}\lvert q(z/h)\lvert\,f_{Z}(z)\,{\rm d}z\\
&=\int_{\kappa_{a}}^{\kappa_{b}}\lvert q(u)\lvert\,f_{Z}(uh)\,{\rm d}u\leq f_{Z,\max}\int_{\kappa_{a}}^{\kappa_{b}}\lvert q(u)\lvert\,{\rm d}u<\infty,\end{split}

therefore
{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)Y(t)\lvert\,\leq\xi_{\max}f_{Z,\max}\,{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\lvert\,<\infty
, and it follows from the strong law of large numbers that, for
0<h\leq\bar{h}
fixed,
G_{n,h}(t)\to G_{h}(t)
almost surely as
n\to\infty
for every
t\in[0,\tau]
. It remains to show that this convergence is uniform in
t
. Write
S(t\mid z)=1-F(t\mid z)
. Assume that
h\kappa\leq\bar{h}\kappa<\kappa_{0}
, so that

q_{h}(Z)=q_{h}(Z)I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z).

Then

\begin{split}&\lvert G_{h}(t)-G_{h}(s)\lvert\,=\lvert\{1-H(t)\}\,{\rm E}\,q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)S(t\mid Z)\\
&\qquad\qquad-\{1-H(s)\}\,{\rm E}\,q_{h}(Z)\xi(s,Z)S(s\mid Z)\lvert\\
&\quad=\lvert\{H(s)-H(t)\}{\rm E}\,q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)S(t\mid Z)\\
&\qquad+\{1-H(s)\}\,{\rm E}\,\{q_{h}(Z)I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)[\xi(t,Z)S(t\mid Z)-\xi(s,Z)S(s\mid Z)]\}\lvert\\
&\quad\leq\lvert H(s)-H(t)\lvert\,{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)\lvert\\
&\qquad\qquad+{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\lvert\,\lvert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}[\xi(t,Z)S(t\mid Z)-\xi(s,Z)S(s\mid Z)]\lvert\\
&\quad\leq\lvert H(s)-H(t)\lvert\,{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)\lvert\\
&\qquad\qquad+\lVert q_{h}(Z)\rVert_{2}\,\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}\{\xi(t,Z)S(t\mid Z)-\xi(s,Z)S(s\mid Z)\}\rVert_{2}\\
\end{split}

We now look at each of the two terms on the right of this expression. By the triangle inequality and since
S(t\mid Z)\,<1
,

\begin{split}&\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)\{\xi(t,Z)S(t\mid Z)-\xi(s,Z)S(s\mid Z)\}\rVert_{2}\\
&\quad=\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)\{[\xi(t,Z)-\xi(s,Z)]S(t\mid Z)+\xi(s,Z)[S(t\mid Z)-S(s\mid Z)]\}\rVert_{2}\\
&\quad\leq\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)\{\xi(t,Z)-\xi(s,Z)\}\rVert_{2}+\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)\xi(s,Z)\{F(s\mid Z)-F(t\mid Z)\}\rVert_{2}.\end{split}

By assumption (c)
\xi(t,z)
is continuous on the compact set
[0,\tau]\times[0,\bar{h}\kappa]
, which implies that
\xi(s,z)
is uniformly continuous on this set, and so, for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\delta>0
such that

\begin{split}\lVert I_{[\kappa_{a},\kappa_{b}]}(Z)\{\xi(t,Z)-\xi(s,Z)\}\rVert_{2}^{2}&={\rm E}\,I_{[\kappa_{a},\kappa_{b}]}(Z)[\xi(t,Z)-\xi(s,Z)]^{2}<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{9},\end{split}

whenever
\lvert t-s\lvert\,<\delta
. By the same argument, using that by assumption (a),
F(t\mid z)
is uniformly continuous on
[0,\tau]\times[0,\bar{h}\kappa]
, we can find
\delta>0
such that
\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)\{F(s\mid Z)-F(t\mid Z)\}\rVert_{2}<\varepsilon/(3\xi_{\max})
whenever
\lvert t-s\lvert\,<\delta
, and so, since
\lvert\xi(s,z)\lvert\,\leq\xi_{\max}
for
(s,t)\in[0,\tau]\times[0,\bar{h}\kappa]
,

\begin{split}&\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)\xi(s,Z)\{F(s\mid Z)-F(t\mid Z)\}\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\xi_{\max}^{2}\,\lVert I_{[0,\bar{h}\kappa]}(Z)\{F(s\mid Z)-F(t\mid Z)\}\rVert_{2}^{2}<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{9},\end{split}

whenever
\lvert t-s\lvert\,<\delta
. Finally, when
h\kappa\leq\bar{h}\kappa
,
{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\xi(t,Z)\lvert\,\leq\xi_{\max}{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\lvert\,<\infty
by the inequality in (
A.10
). The distribution function
H(t)
is continuous on
[0,\tau]
, meaning that for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\delta>0
such that
\lvert H(s)-H(t)\lvert\,<\varepsilon/(3\xi_{\max}{\rm E}\,\lvert q_{h}(Z)\lvert)
whenever
\lvert t-s\lvert\,<\delta
. In summary, provided
h\kappa\leq\bar{h}\kappa<\kappa_{0}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\delta>0
such that

\lvert G_{h}(t)-G_{h}(s)\lvert\,<\varepsilon/3+\varepsilon/3+\varepsilon/3=\varepsilon,

whenever
\lvert t-s\lvert\,<\delta
. This shows that
G_{h}(t)
is a continuous function, and since
G_{h}(t)
is continuous on the closed and bounded interval
[0,\tau]
, it is uniformly continuous on this interval. The uniform continuity of
G_{h}(t)
entails that given
\varepsilon>0
, we can find a grid
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=\tau
, so that
\lvert G_{h}(t_{i})-G_{h}(t_{i-1})\lvert\,<\varepsilon
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Using that
G_{n,h}(t)
and
G_{h}(t)
are both nonincreasing in
t
(since
q(u)\geq 0
by assumption (b)), this entails that for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i})
,

\begin{split}G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t)&=G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t_{i-1})+G_{h}(t_{i-1})-G_{h}(t)\\
&<G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t_{i-1})+\varepsilon<G_{n,h}(t_{i-1})-G_{h}(t_{i-1})+\varepsilon,\end{split}

and, similarly,

\begin{split}G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t)&=G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t_{i})+G_{h}(t_{i})-G_{h}(t)\\
&>G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t_{i})-\varepsilon\geq G_{n,h}(t_{i})-G_{h}(t_{i})-\varepsilon,\end{split}

Thus, for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i})
and our chosen grid,

G_{n,h}(t_{i})-G_{h}(t_{i})-\varepsilon\leq G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t)\leq G_{n,h}(t_{i-1})-G_{h}(t_{i-1})+\varepsilon.

Because this is true for every
i=1,\ldots,k
, we must have that

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t)\lvert\,\leq\max_{0\leq i\leq k}\lvert G_{n,h}(t_{i})-G_{h}(t_{i})\lvert\,+\,\varepsilon.

Since the convergence
G_{n,h}(t)\to G_{h}(t)
a.s., is uniform over
\{t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\}
, the right hand side of the above display tends almost surely to
\varepsilon
, and so
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t)\lvert\to\varepsilon
almost surely as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, by the triangle inequality

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert G_{n,h}(t)-G(t)\lvert\,\leq\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert G_{n,h}(t)-G_{h}(t)\lvert\,+\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert G_{h}(t)-G(t)\lvert\,\to\varepsilon\,

(A.13)

almost surely, as
n\to\infty
and
h\to 0
, using that the second term on the right converges to zero as
h\to 0
by (
A.12
).
Since (
A.13
) is true for every
\varepsilon>0
, the claim in (
A.9
) follows.
∎

A.4
Sequences of random matrices

Versions of the results in this section can be found in
McKeague, 1988a
or
McKeague, 1988b
. They are included here for completeness.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
A_{n}(t),\,t\in[0,\tau]
be a sequence of symmetric matrix valued functions converging in probability (resp. almost surely) to a matrix valued function
A(t)
that is positive definite for all
t\in[0,\tau]
. Let

B_{n}=\{\text{$A_{n}(t)$ is positive definite for all $t\in[0,\tau]$}\}.

Then
{\rm Pr}(B_{n})\to 1
as
n\to\infty
(resp.
\exists\,n_{0}\geq 0
such that
{\rm Pr}(B_{n},\,\forall\,n\geq n_{0})=1
).

Proof.

We only prove the convergence in probability part. Write
A_{n}(t)=\{a_{i,j}^{n}(t)\}
and
A(t)=\{a_{i,j}(t)\}
. For each
i
and
j
,
a_{i,j}^{n}(t)=a_{i,j}(t)+r_{i,j}^{n}(t)
, where
\sup_{t}\lvert r_{i,j}(t)\lvert\to_{p}0
as
n\to\infty
. Let
z
be an arbitrary nonzero real column vector, then
z^{{\rm t}}A_{n}(t)z=z^{{\rm t}}A(t)z+\sum_{i}\sum_{j}r_{i,j}^{n}(t)z_{i}z_{j}
, where
z^{{\rm t}}A(t)z>0
for all
t
, by assumption. Since
z^{{\rm t}}A(t)z>0
there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that
z^{{\rm t}}A(t)z>\varepsilon>0
. For such an
\varepsilon>0
, we can for any
\delta>0
find an
n_{0}
such that

{\rm Pr}(\sup_{t}\,\lvert\sum_{i}\sum_{j}r_{i,j}^{n}(t)z_{i}z_{j}\lvert\,<\varepsilon)>1-\delta,

for all
n\geq n_{0}
, but then
{\rm Pr}\{\text{$z^{{\rm t}}A_{n}(t)z>0$ for all $t\in[0,\tau]$}\}>1-\delta
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
A_{n}(t)
and
A(t)
be as in Lemma
A.6
. Let
\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)=A_{n}(t)
if
A_{n}(t)
is positive definite, and
\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)
be equal to some invertible matrix otherwise. Then
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert\widetilde{A}_{n}^{-1}(t)-A^{-1}(t)\lvert\,=o(1)
almost surely.

Proof.

The following matrix inequality can be found in
McKeague, 1988b
: If
C
and
D
are nonsingular matrices such that
\lVert C-D\rVert<\lVert D\rVert
, then

\lVert C^{-1}-D^{-1}\rVert\,\leq\frac{\lVert D^{-1}\rVert^{2}\lVert C-D\rVert}{1-\lVert D^{-1}\rVert\lVert C-D\rVert}.

(A.14)

Since
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\lvert\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)-A(t)\lvert\to 0
almost surely, there is an
n_{0}\geq 1
such that the inequality above, with
C=\widetilde{A}_{n}(t)
and
D=A(t)
for all
t\in[0,\tau]
holds almost surely for all
n\geq n_{0}
. But the right hand side converges in almost surely to zero.
∎

Here is a useful lemma that does not necessarily involve neither sequences nor randomness.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})^{{\rm t}}
be a
p
-dimensional column vector,
A=\{a_{i,j}\}
a
p\times p
matrix, and
e_{\nu}
the
p
-dimensional column vector with the
\nu
th entry equal to
1
, and all other equal to zero. Then
e_{\nu}^{{\rm t}}Axx^{{\rm t}}A^{{\rm t}}e_{\nu}\leq\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert x\rVert^{2}
.

Proof.

First,
e_{\nu}^{{\rm t}}Axx^{{\rm t}}A^{{\rm t}}e_{\nu}=(e_{\nu}^{{\rm t}}Ax)^{2}
, and

\lvert e_{\nu}^{{\rm t}}Ax\lvert^{2}\,\leq\max_{1\leq\nu\leq p}\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{\nu,j}x_{j}\lvert^{2}\,\leq\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\,\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{\nu,j}x_{j}\lvert^{2}\,=\lVert Ax\rVert^{2}.

Provided that
\lVert x\rVert
is positive,
\lVert Ax\rVert=(\lVert Ax\rVert/\lVert x\rVert)\lVert x\rVert\leq\sup_{\lVert y\rVert\neq 0}(\lVert Ay\rVert/\lVert y\rVert)\lVert x\rVert=\lVert A\rVert\lVert x\rVert
.
∎

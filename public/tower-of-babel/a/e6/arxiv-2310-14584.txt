Extremal subsets and atom-positivity
Sam Armon

Abstract
Demazure crystals give a combinatorial framework in which to study Demazure modules. They are extremal , in that they satisfy Kashiwara’s string property, and they are Demazure atom-positive , in that they decompose naturally into subsets called crystal Demazure atoms. The properties of extremality and atom-positivity are further linked by a conjecture of Polo, which concerns the structure of the tensor product of two Demazure modules. We study these two properties in isolation — first providing a recursive formula for generating crystal Demazure atoms which generalizes a result of Lascoux-Schützenberger, and then examining the structure of extremal subsets and their characters — before commenting on the connection (or lack thereof) between them.



1
Introduction

The finite-dimensional simple modules
V(\lambda)
of a (connected, simply connected) complex semisimple Lie group
G
are parameterized by dominant weights
\lambda\in P^{+}
. The representation theory of
G
is fairly well-understood; in particular, for
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
, the tensor product
V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)
admits a
good filtration
; that is, a filtration in which each successive quotient is isomorphic to
V(\nu)
for some
\nu\in P^{+}
. The theory of crystal bases, developed independently by Kashiwara
[
Kas90
]
and Lusztig
[
Lus90
]
, provides a combinatorial model for the representation theory of
G
. In particular, there exists for each
\lambda\in P^{+}
a
highest weight crystal

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
— which one may regard as a directed, weighted graph — whose character agrees with
V(\lambda)
, and the tensor product of highest weight crystals
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
decomposes as a direct sum of highest weight crystals, witnessing the good filtration of
V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)
.

For
B\subseteq G
a Borel subgroup, the
Demazure module

V_{w}(\lambda)\subseteq V(\lambda)
is a cyclic
B
-submodule indexed by an element
w\in W
of the Weyl group, generated by the (unique, up to a scalar) extremal weight vector of weight
w\cdot\lambda
in
V(\lambda)

[
Dem74
]
. Generalizing the Borel-Weil-Bott theorem,
V_{w}(\lambda)
may be identified with
H^{0}(X_{w},\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda})
, the space of sections of a line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda}
associated to the antidominant weight
-\lambda
over a Schubert variety
X_{w}\subseteq G/B
. On the level of crystals, Kashiwara
[
Kas93
]
and Littelmann
[
Lit95
]
construct a subset
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
whose character agrees with the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda)
, and this character is computed via the refined Demazure character formula
[
Jos85
]
.

A
B
-module is said to have an
excellent filtration
if each successive quotient in the filtration is isomorphic to
V_{w}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
. The tensor product
V_{v}(\lambda)\otimes V_{w}(\mu)
does not, in general, admit an excellent filtration, and correspondingly,
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
does not always decompose as a direct sum of Demazure crystals. A conjecture of Polo
[
Pol89
]
states, however, that this tensor product should always admit a more general
B
-module filtration called a
Schubert filtration
, whose successive quotients are isomorphic to a
B
-module of the form
H^{0}(S,\mathcal{L}_{\lambda})
, where
S
is a
union
of Schubert varieties. We study two crystal-theoretic properties — atom-positivity, and extremality — related to Polo’s conjecture, which we will now briefly outline.

In type
A
, Demazure characters form a basis for the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
which lifts the basis of Schur polynomials for the ring of symmetric polynomials. Polynomial Demazure atoms — originally studied by Lascoux-Schützenberger
[
LS90
]
— decompose the Demazure characters, in that Demazure characters are positive sums of polynomial Demazure atoms. These polynomials were shown by Mason
[
Mas09
]
to be certain specializations of nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials in type
A
, and
[
BBBG21
]
construct a solvable lattice model whose partition function equals the Demazure atom. The product of Demazure characters cannot, in general, be written as a positive sum of Demazure characters, but Polo’s conjecture suggests that this product should expand positively in the basis of Demazure atoms; this more general conjecture was also directly proposed by Pun
[
Pun16
]
, who solved it in the special case that the indexing compositions each have length
\leq 3
.

For each
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
, Kashiwara
[
Kas02
]
defines a subset
\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
called a crystal Demazure atom which lifts the above decomposition to the level of crystals; that is, Demazure crystals naturally decompose into disjoint unions of crystal Demazure atoms, and (at least in type
A
) the character of a crystal Demazure atom is a polynomial Demazure atom. Particular crystal subsets which play a vital role in Polo’s conjecture have the
atom-positivity
property — that is, they decompose into a disjoint union of crystal Demazure atoms. To this end, we define a new family of operators
\{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i}:i\in I\}
, and prove that they may be used to recursively generate entire crystal Demazure atoms in Theorem
3.1.1
. This theorem generalizes a result of Lascoux-Schützenberger in type
A
to arbitrary finite type, and provides a global alternative to the right key map of Lascoux-Schützenberger, which was later generalized by Jacon-Lecouvey
[
JL20
]
and Santos
[
San20
]
.

The second property we consider is
extremality
; this is one of the three defining properties of Demazure crystals found by Kashiwara, and in particular is the only property which does not directly follow from by necessity (see Theorem
2.3.2
(3)). While
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
does not, in general, decompose as a direct sum of Demazure crystals, Kouno
[
Kou20
]
found a global condition which is necessary and sufficient for this decomposition to hold. A recent theorem of
[
ADG23
]
replaces Kouno’s global condition with the local condition of extremality; that is,
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals if and only if the tensor product is extremal. We prove that extremal subsets are generated by their lowest weight elements, and furthermore that an extremal subset generated by a single lowest weight element is uniquely determined by its character in Proposition
4.0.2
. We also examine the relationship between extremality and atom-positivity due to their shared relationship with Polo’s conjecture, but find that the relationship is not so direct; in particular, we construct an extremal subset whose character is not a positive sum of polynomial Demazure atoms, and an atom-positive subset which is not extremal.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we review the necessary facts about semisimple Lie groups, and then introduce highest weight crystals and Demazure crystals. In Section
3
we define crystal Demazure atoms and introduce a new family of operators which allows one to recursively construct these subsets in the crystal, before discussing the notion of (strong and weak) atom-positivity. Finally, in Section
4
we define extremal subsets and study their characters.

2
Crystals

Let
G
be a connected, simply connected, complex semisimple Lie group, with
B\subseteq G
a Borel subgroup and
T\subseteq B
a maximal torus. The adjoint action of
T
on the associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
determines a root system
\Phi
; we let
I
denote the index set of the corresponding Dynkin diagram, and
P\supseteq\Phi
the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\Phi
denote the set of simple roots,
\{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\}_{i\in I}\subset\Phi^{\vee}
the set of simple coroots, and
\{s_{i}\}_{i\in I}
the corresponding set of simple reflections.

The Weyl group
W
is generated by
\{s_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. If
w=s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
for some
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in I
, where
k
is minimal among all such expressions for
w\in W
, then we say that
s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
is a
reduced word
for
w
, and that the
length
of
w
is
\ell(w)=k
. The
Bruhat order
on
W
is the partial order
\leq
determined by the
subword property
: for
v,w\in W
, we have
v\leq w
if and only if, for any reduced word for
w
, there exists a subword which is a reduced word for
v
.

The Weyl group
W
acts on the weight lattice
P
. For any
\lambda\in P^{+}
, let
W_{\lambda}\leq W
denote the stabilizer subgroup of
\lambda
. Many of the constructions in this paper depending on
w\in W
will depend only on the left coset of
w
in
W/W_{\lambda}
for a given
\lambda\in P^{+}
. To this end, we let
W^{\lambda}
denote the set of minimal-length coset representatives in
W/W_{\lambda}
.

The cone
P^{+}\subset P
of dominant weights is given by

P^{+}=\{\lambda\in P:\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }i\in I\},

and the simple
G
-modules
V(\lambda)
are parameterized by
\lambda\in P^{+}
. Let
\mathcal{O}
denote the free ableian group generated by
\{t^{\beta}:\beta\in P\}
. Each
G
-module
V
decomposes into weight spaces
V\cong\bigoplus_{\beta\in P}V_{\beta}
, and the formal character of
V
is given by:

\mathrm{char}(V)=\sum_{\beta\in P}\dim(V_{\beta})t^{\beta}\in\mathcal{O}.

2.1
Highest weight crystals

The theory of crystal bases provides a combinatorial model for the representation theory of the quantized universal enveloping algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
in the limit
q\to 0
. A
(normal) crystal

\mathcal{B}
is a finite set equipped with the data of: a weight map
\mathrm{wt}:\mathcal{B}\to P
, raising and lowering operators
e_{i},f_{i}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\sqcup\{0\}
for each
i\in I
such that, for each
x,y\in\mathcal{B}
,

(A1)

f_{i}(x)=y
if and only if
e_{i}(y)=x
, in which case
\mathrm{wt}(y)=\mathrm{wt}(x)-\alpha_{i}
,

along with string lengths
\varepsilon_{i},\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying:

(A2)

\varphi_{i}(x)=\mathrm{max}(k:f_{i}^{k}(x)\neq 0\}
,
\varepsilon_{i}(x)=\mathrm{max}(k:e_{i}^{k}(x)\neq 0\}
, and

(A3)

\varphi_{i}(x)=\langle\mathrm{wt}(x),\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle+\varepsilon(x)
.

We depict a normal crystal as a directed, weighted graph with directed edges given by the lowering operators
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. For any
i\in I
and
x\in\mathcal{B}
, let
f_{i}^{*}(x)=f_{i}^{\varphi_{i}(x)}
(resp.
e_{i}^{*}(x)=e_{i}^{\varepsilon_{i}(x)}
), and define an equivalence relation
\sim_{i}
on
\mathcal{B}
by
x\sim_{i}y
if and only if
x=e_{i}^{k}(y)
, or vice-versa, for some
k\geq 0
. The equivalence classes are called
i
-strings
, and each equivalence class
S
contains a unique highest weight element
u_{S}
given by
u_{S}=e_{i}^{*}(x)
for any
x\in S
.

For each
\lambda\in P^{+}
the
G
-module
V(\lambda)
admits a
highest weight crystal

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
with a highest weight element
b_{\lambda}
satisfying
\mathrm{wt}(b_{\lambda})=\lambda
and
e_{i}(b_{\lambda})=0
for all
i\in I
. From now on we will only consider normal crystals whose connected components are isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in P^{+}
. The
character
of a crystal
\mathcal{B}
is:

\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{B})=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}t^{\mathrm{wt}(x)}\in\mathcal{O},

and
\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{B}(\lambda))=\mathrm{char}(V(\lambda))
recovers the formal character of the simple
G
-module
V(\lambda)
.

Furthermore, Kashiwara
[
Kas90
]
defines the tensor product
\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{C}
of two normal crystals; the vertex set is the Cartesian product
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{C}
, the weight map is given by
\mathrm{wt}(x\otimes y)=\mathrm{wt}(x)+\mathrm{wt}(y)
, and the lowering operators
f_{i}
are determined by the rule:

f_{i}(x\otimes y)=\begin{cases}f_{i}(x)\otimes y&\text{if }\varepsilon_{i}(x)\geq\varphi_{i}(y),\\
x\otimes f_{i}(y)&\text{if }\varepsilon_{i}(x)<\varphi_{i}(y).\end{cases}

The tensor product
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
of highest weight crystals decomposes as a direct sum of highest weight crystals, and this replicates the decomposition on the module level; that is,

V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)\cong\bigoplus_{\nu}V(\nu)^{\oplus c_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}}\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)\cong\bigoplus_{\nu}\mathcal{B}(\nu)^{\oplus c_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}}.

2.3
Demazure crystals

Demazure
[
Dem74
]
studied certain cyclic
B
-submodules
V_{w}(\lambda)
of
V(\lambda)
. To define them, recall that
V(\lambda)
decomposes into weight spaces as
V(\lambda)\cong\bigoplus_{\beta\in P}V(\lambda)_{\beta}
. The
extremal weight spaces
are
\{V(\lambda)_{w\cdot\lambda}:w\in W\}
, and are each one-dimensional. For any
w\in W
, the
Demazure module

V_{w}(\lambda)
is the
B
-module generated by the extremal weight space
V(\lambda)_{w\cdot\lambda}
, and for
v,w\in W^{\lambda}
, we have the containment
V_{v}(\lambda)\subseteq V_{w}(\lambda)
if and only if
v\leq w
in Bruhat order.

The character of
V_{w}(\lambda)
can be obtained as follows. For any
\beta\in P
and
i\in I
, the
Demazure operator

D_{i}
acts on
\mathcal{O}
via:

D_{i}(t^{\beta})=\frac{t^{\beta+\rho}-t^{s_{i}\cdot(\beta+\rho)}}{1-t^{-\alpha_{i}}}t^{-\rho},

where
\rho
is the Weyl vector. The Demazure operators are idempotent and satisfy the same braid relations as the underlying Weyl group, so we may define
D_{w}:=D_{i_{k}}\cdots D_{i_{1}}
for any
w\in W
with reduced word
w=s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
. The
refined Demazure character formula
is:

Theorem 2.3.1

(
[
Jos85
]
,
[
Kas93
]
,
[
Lit95
]
)
.

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
,

\mathrm{char}(V_{w}(\lambda))=D_{w}(t^{\lambda})\in\mathcal{O}.

Littelmann
[
Lit95
]
proved the existence of a subset
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
whose character agrees with
V_{w}(\lambda)
; Kashiwara
[
Kas93
]
constructed it and derived many of its beautiful properties. By analogy,
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is called a
Demazure crystal
. To understand its structure we require the following operators, defined for any
i\in I
and any
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}(X)

\displaystyle:=\{f_{i}^{d}(x):x\in X,d\geq 0\}-\{0\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}(X)

\displaystyle:=\{e_{i}^{d}(x):x\in X,d\geq 0\}-\{0\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda).

Definition 2.3.1

(
[
Kas93
]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
with
w=s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
a reduced word. The
Demazure crystal

\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is:

\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{i_{k}}\cdots\mathcal{F}_{i_{1}}(\{b_{\lambda}\}),

where
b_{\lambda}
is the highest weight element of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

Although it is not obvious from the definition, the set
\mathcal{F}_{i_{k}}\cdots\mathcal{F}_{i_{1}}(\{b_{\lambda}\})
is independent of the choice of reduced word for
w
, so
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is well-defined (see, for instance,
[
Kas93
]
or
[
BS17
]
Theorem 13.5). Strictly speaking,
\mathcal{F}_{i_{k}}\cdots\mathcal{F}_{i_{1}}(\{b_{\lambda}\})
determines the underlying vertex set, and
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is the corresponding induced subgraph of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

The element
b_{w\cdot\lambda}:=f_{i_{k}}^{*}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{*}(b_{\lambda})\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
has
\mathrm{wt}(b_{w\cdot\lambda})=w\cdot\lambda
, and
b_{w\cdot\lambda}
is the
unique
element of its weight in
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. As for Demazure modules, such elements are called
extremal weight elements
. Note that, if
w\cdot\lambda=w^{\prime}\cdot\lambda
, then
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{w^{\prime}}(\lambda)
, so the Demazure crystal
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is determined solely by the left coset of
W/W_{\lambda}
containing
w
. Thus we will often assume for simplicity that
w\in W^{\lambda}
is a minimal-length coset representative.

Theorem 2.3.2

(
[
Kas93
]
)
.

Demazure crystals satisfy the following properties:

1.

\mathcal{E}_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda))\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
for all
i\in I
,

2.

if
s_{i}w<w
, then

\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\{f_{i}^{k}(x):x\in\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(b_{\lambda}),k\geq 0,e_{i}(x)=0\}-\{0\},

so that in particular
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda))=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
, and

3.

for any
i
-string
S
in
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
,

S\cap\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\in\{\emptyset,\{u_{S}\},S\},

where
u_{S}
is the highest weight element of
S
.

Note that we only have
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda))\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
when
s_{i}w<w
; this may not be the case when
s_{i}w>w
(in fact it
never
is, so long as
w,s_{i}w
lie in different cosets of
W/W_{\lambda}
). However,
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is closed under
\mathcal{E}_{i}
for all

. The third property of Theorem
2.3.2
is noticeably different from the first two — we call a subset which satisfies this condition
extremal
— and it will receive a closer look in Section
4

One may realize a Demazure crystal of classical type combinatorially as the truncation of a tableau highest weight crystal, but there is also a dedicated tableau model for type
A
Demazure crystals which uses objects called key tableaux, due to
[
AS18
]
.

3
Demazure atoms

Just as Demazure modules are naturally filtered by Bruhat order, there is a well-known analogous result for Demazure crystals:

Proposition 3.0.1
.

For any
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
v,w\in W^{\lambda}
, we have the containment
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
of Demazure crystals if and only if
v\leq w
in Bruhat order.

Because of this containment, it is natural to study the minimal nonintersecting pieces of Demazure crystals, which was initiated by Lascoux-Schützenberger on the level of characters in type
A
. After Mason
[
Mas09
]
, these pieces are referred to as
Demazure atoms
.

Definition 3.0.1

(
[
Kas02
]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W^{\lambda}
. The
(crystal) Demazure atom

\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)
is

\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\setminus\bigcup_{v<w}\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda).

Intuitively, the crystal Demazure atom
\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)
consists of those elements of
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
which were added at the “last step”, i.e. those elements which are not contained in any strictly smaller Demazure crystal. Much of the literature concerning Demazure atoms is on the level of characters; in type
A
, the characters of Demazure crystals and Demazure atoms form combinatorially interesting bases for the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and they were studied extensively in
[
LS90
]
and
[
Mas09
]
. Following
[
BBBG21
]
, we will refer to the character of a crystal Demazure atom as a
polynomial Demazure atom
when there may be confusion.

We now briefly review this theory in type
A_{n-1}
, where
W=S_{n}
,
P=\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\langle(1,\ldots,1)\rangle
, and
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
. For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the Demazure operator
D_{i}
specializes to the
divided difference operator

\pi_{i}
, which acts on
f\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
via:

\pi_{i}(f)=\frac{t_{i}f-s_{i}\cdot(t_{i}f)}{t_{i}-t_{i+1}}\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}],

where
s_{i}\cdot f
is obtained by swapping
t_{i}
and
t_{i+1}
in
f
. Lascoux-Schützenberger define a related operator to obtain polynomial Demazure atoms:

Theorem 3.0.1

(
[
LS90
]
)
.

For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, let
\theta_{i}
denote the operator on
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
given by

\theta_{i}(f)=\pi_{i}(f)-f.

Then for
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W^{\lambda}
with
w=s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
a reduced word, the (polynomial) Demazure atom is:

\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda))=\theta_{i_{k}}\cdots\theta_{i_{1}}(t_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}).

The Demazure character is often denoted by
\kappa_{\beta}=\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda))
, where
\beta=w\cdot\lambda
is a weak composition, and called a
key polynomial
. We can similarly index Demazure atoms by weak compositions in this case, and they are denoted by
\mathfrak{A}_{\beta}=\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda))
. In fact, in type
A_{n-1}
, key polynomials
\{\kappa_{\beta}\}_{\beta}
and polynomial Demazure atoms
\{\mathfrak{A}_{\beta}\}_{\beta}
both form bases for
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
as
\beta
ranges over the weak compositions of length
n
.

Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
LS90
]
define a map
K_{+}:SSYT_{n}(\lambda)\to S_{n}
called the
(right) key map
which associates to any
SSYT
an element
K_{+}(T)
of the Weyl group, and Jacon-Lecouvey
[
JL20
]
generalize the key map to
K_{+}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to W
for
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
the highest weight crystal associated to any symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra. Santos
[
San20
]
also defines the type
C
key map with a distinct approach using the symplectic plactic monoid. The shared property of these maps is that they determine membership in a given Demazure atom:

Theorem 3.0.2

(
[
LS90
]
,
[
JL20
]
,
[
San20
]
)
.

For any
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W^{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda):K_{+}(b)\leq w\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda):K_{+}(b)=w\}.

In particular,

\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\bigsqcup_{v\leq w,v\in W^{\lambda}}\mathcal{A}_{v}(\lambda).

Taking
w=w_{0}
, the preceding theorem shows that the set of crystal Demazure atoms
\{\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda):w\in W^{\lambda}\}
are pairwise disjoint for any
\lambda\in P^{+}
.

The key map is profoundly useful in that it gives a uniform way to determine, for any given crystal element, in which Demazure atom it resides. In the following section, we approach the problem from the other direction, and provide a method for recursively generating the entire Demazure atom.

3.1
Atomic operators

Fix
\lambda\in P^{+}
for the remainder of the section. The results of this section hold in arbitrary finite type, unless otherwise indicated.

Definition 3.1.1
.

For any
i\in I
and
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, define

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i}(X)=\mathcal{F}_{i}(X)\setminus X.

We call
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
an
atomic operator
.

In type
A
, these operators are equivalent to the map
\overline{\pi}_{i}
defined on the plactic monoid in
[
LS90
]
, but we define them for any highest weight crystal of finite type. The
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
’s do not, in general, satisfy the braid relations. However, they do in the following special case.

Theorem 3.1.1
.

For
w\in W^{\lambda}
with
w=s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
a reduced word, we have

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{k}}\cdots\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}}(b_{\lambda})=\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda).

In particular, if
w=s_{j_{k}}\cdots s_{j_{1}}
is another reduced word, then

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{k}}\cdots\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}}(b_{\lambda})=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{j_{k}}\cdots\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{j_{1}}(b_{\lambda}).

Proof.

Proceed by induction on
k=\ell(w)
. When
k=1
, we have
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i}(b_{\lambda})=\mathcal{F}_{i}(b_{\lambda})\setminus\{b_{\lambda}\}=\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}}(\lambda)-\mathcal{B}_{id}(\lambda)
, and the result follows.

Now let
k>1
, and select
i\in I
such that
s_{i}w<w
. Then
s_{i}w\in W^{\lambda}
since, if it were not, then
s_{i}w\cdot\lambda=w\cdot\lambda
, which would imply that
\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
, contradicting the assumption that
w
is a minimal-length coset representative in
W/W_{\lambda}
. Since
s_{i}w<w
, we have a reduced word for
w
of the form
s_{i}s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
, so that
s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
is a reduced word for
s_{i}w
. By induction,

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{k-1}}\cdots\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}}(b_{\lambda})=\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)\setminus\bigcup_{v<s_{i}w}\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda).

We then have

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{k-1}}\cdots\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}}(b_{\lambda})=\mathcal{F}_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)\right)\setminus\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda).

Denote this set by
L
, and first notice that
L\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda))=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
. We aim to show that
L=\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)
. Let
x\in L
, so that
x\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
, but
e_{i}^{*}(x)
is. Assume to the contrary that
x\not\in\mathcal{A}_{w}(b_{\lambda})
, and pick
v<w
such that
x\in\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)
and
\ell(v)
is minimized. Then either:

i.

v=s_{i}w
, in which case
x\in\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)\setminus\bigcup_{v<s_{i}w}\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)=\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
, which is a contradiction,

ii.

v<s_{i}w
, in which case
e_{i}^{*}(x)\in\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)
by Theorem
2.3.2
(1). But we assumed
e_{i}^{*}(x)\in\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)\cap\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)=\emptyset
, so this is a contradiction,

iii.

v\not<s_{i}w
. We have
v<w
since
L\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(b_{\lambda})
, so this forces
s_{i}v<v
for the following reason: every reduced word for
v
is a subword of
s_{i}s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
, and by assumption, there is no reduced word for
v
which is also a subword of
s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
. Thus
v
must have a reduced word ending in
s_{i}
, i.e.
s_{i}v<v
. For this reason, we also have
s_{i}v<s_{i}w
. But this forces
e_{i}^{*}(x)\in\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}v}(\lambda)
by Theorem
2.3.2
.2, which is again a contradiction since, by assumption,
e_{i}^{*}(x)\in\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)\cap\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}v}(\lambda)=\emptyset
.

Thus
x\in\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)
, so that
L\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)
.

Now assume
y\in\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)
. Then
y\not\in\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
since
s_{i}w<w
, so
y\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
. Since
y\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
and
s_{i}w<w
, we have by Theorem
2.3.2
(2) that
e_{i}^{*}(y)\in\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
. Now, it suffices to show that
e_{i}^{*}(y)\in\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
, i.e.
e_{i}^{*}(y)\not\in\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)
for any
v<s_{i}w
. If this were the case for some
v<s_{i}w
, then either
s_{i}v<v
and
y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)
by Theorem
2.3.2
(2), or
v<s_{i}v
and
y\in\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}v}(\lambda)
. We have
v,s_{i}v<w
in either case, so this is a contradiction since
y\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\setminus\bigcup_{v<w}\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)
.

Thus
y\in L
and
\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)\subseteq L
; we conclude finally that the two sets are equal.
∎

Proposition 3.1.1
.

For

\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda))=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)&\text{if }s_{i}w<w\text{ or }s_{i}w\not\in W^{\lambda},\\
\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)&\text{if }w<s_{i}w\text{ and }w\in W^{\lambda}.\end{cases}

Proof.

(2). Then
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda))\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
, and if there exists

and

.

. Then by definition we have

\mathcal{A}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda))=\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda))\setminus\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda),

3.2
Atom-positivity

As seen above, Demazure crystals decompose as a disjoint union of Demazure atoms, and in particular their characters are positive sums of polynomial Demazure atoms. We generalize this notion as follows:

Definition 3.2.1
.

A subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is called
strongly atom-positive
if there exists
L_{X}\subseteq W^{\lambda}
such that

X=\bigsqcup_{w\in L_{X}}\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda),

and
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is
weakly atom-positive
if there exist
\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{k}\in P^{+}
and
w^{1},\ldots,w^{k}\in W
with each
w^{i}\in W^{\lambda^{i}}
such that

\mathrm{char}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{A}_{w^{i}}(\lambda^{i})).

Theorem 3.2.1
.

For
w^{1},\ldots,w^{k}\in W^{\lambda}
, we have:

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{w^{i}}(\lambda)=\bigsqcup_{w\leq w^{i}\text{ for some }i\text{ and }w\in W^{\lambda}}\mathcal{A}_{w}(b_{\lambda}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{w^{i}}(\lambda)
. For any
w\in W^{\lambda}
, we will verify that

\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)&\text{if }w\leq w^{i}\text{ for some }i,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

First, if

for some

, then by Theorem

we have
\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w^{i}}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{D}
, so
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)
.

If
w\not\leq w^{i}
, then
\mathcal{B}_{w^{i}}(\lambda)\cap\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)=\emptyset
, so that if
w\not\leq w^{i}
for each

, we have
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)=\emptyset
. Therefore,

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{B}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}\cap\bigsqcup_{w\in W^{\lambda}}\mathcal{A}_{w}(\lambda)=\bigsqcup_{w\leq w^{(i)}\text{ for some }i\text{ and }w\in W^{\lambda}}\mathcal{A}_{w}(b_{\lambda}).

∎

The indexing set in the above theorem forms a
lower order ideal
in Bruhat order; that is, a set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq W
such that
w\in\mathcal{I},v\leq w\Rightarrow v\in\mathcal{I}
. Any lower order ideal in Bruhat order is thus determined by its maximal elements. If
w^{1},\ldots,w^{k}\in\mathcal{I}
are the maximal elements, then we will write
\mathcal{I}=\langle w^{1},\ldots,w^{k}\rangle
. With this notation, we have the following:

Definition 3.2.2
.

For
w^{1},\ldots,w^{k}\in W^{\lambda}
, let
\mathcal{I}=\langle w^{1},\ldots,w^{k}\rangle
. The
Schubert crystal

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{w^{i}}(\lambda),

and the
Schubert character

\kappa_{\lambda,\mathcal{I}}\in\mathcal{O}
is

\kappa_{\lambda,\mathcal{I}}=\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(\lambda)).

Schubert crystals and characters play a significant role in Polo’s conjecture, as discussed below.

4
Extremal subsets

Polo’s conjecture
[
Pol89
]
concerns the structure of the tensor product of Demazure modules
V_{v}(\lambda)\otimes V_{w}(\mu)
. To introduce it, we first briefly discuss filtrations of
B
-modules. The Borel-Weil-Bott theorem realizes the simple
G
-module
V(\lambda)
as the dual of the space of sections
H^{0}(G/B,\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda})
of a line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda}
over the flag variety
G/B
. Generalizing this, the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda)
may be identified with
H^{0}(X_{w},\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda})
, where
X_{w}=\overline{BwB}/B
is a
Schubert variety
. As a further generalization, Polo defines the
B
-module
H^{0}(S,\mathcal{L}_{-\lambda})
, where
S
is a
union
of Schubert varieties, and calls it a
Schubert module
.

We say that a
B
-module
V
admits an
excellent filtration
if each successive quotient in the filtration is isomorphic to a Demazure module. The tensor product
V_{v}(\lambda)\otimes V_{w}(\lambda)
does not always admit an excellent filtration; however, Polo conjectured that there should always exist a
Schubert filtration
:

Conjecture

(
[
Pol89
]
)
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
and
v,w\in W
, the tensor product
V_{v}(\lambda)\otimes V_{w}(\lambda)
admits a filtration in which each successive quotient is isomorphic to a Schubert module. In particular, there exist
\nu^{1},\ldots,\nu^{k}\in P^{+}
and lower order ideals
\mathcal{I}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{I}^{k}\subseteq W
such that

\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda))\cdot\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\kappa_{\nu^{i},\mathcal{I}^{i}}

for some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Polo’s conjecture suggests that the tensor product
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
should admit a decomposition such that each connected component is a Schubert crystal. However, while Kashiwara’s tensor product rule witnesses good filtrations in the category of highest weight crystals — that is,
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
decomposes as a direct sum of highest weight crystals, replicating the decomposition on the module level — this rule is not well-equipped to deal with tensor products of Demazure crystals. That is, when one restricts the tensor product to the subset
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
, the connected components are not always isomorphic to Schubert crystals. However, they are in the following case:

Theorem 4.0.1

(
[
ADG23
]
)
.

The Kashiwara tensor product of Demazure crystals decomposes as a direct sum of Demazure crystals if and only if the tensor product satisfies Theorem
2.3.2
(3).

This is in fact a special case of Polo’s conjecture, as the connected components of the tensor product are single Demazure crystals, as opposed to Schubert crystals. Kouno first
[
Kou20
]
found a global criterion that gives necessary and sufficient conditions for this decomposition to hold, but we opt to work with the local criterion of
[
ADG23
]
.

Definition 4.0.1

(
[
AG21
]
)
.

Any subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
satisfying Theorem
2.3.2
(3) is called an
extremal subset
.

Note that any extremal subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
also necessarily satisfies Theorem
2.3.2
(1), and thus contains the highest weight element
b_{\lambda}
. In particular, every extremal subset of a highest weight crystal is connected. The class of extremal subsets is also closed under union and intersection:

Lemma 4.0.1
.

If
X,Y\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
are extremal subsets, then so are
X\cup Y
and
X\cap Y
.

Proof.

For a given
i
-string
S
contained in
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, we have
(X\cup Y)\cap S=(X\cap S)\cup(Y\cap S)
, so since
X\cap S=\emptyset,S
, or
u_{S}
(resp.
Y\cap S
), it follows that the same is true of
(X\cup Y)\cap S
. A similar argument shows the same result for
(X\cap Y)\cap S
.
∎

Corollary 4.0.1
.

Schubert crystals are extremal.

Thus extremal subsets of highest weight crystals take on particular importance in the context of Polo’s conjecture. As such, the original impetus for this work was to study the relationship between extremality and atom-positivity. The relationship, it turns out, is not so direct, as the class of extremal subsets is much broader than the special cases considered above. In particular, there exist extremal subsets which are not weakly atom-positive.

Example 4.0.1

\mathcal{E}(X)=\{e_{i_{k}}^{d_{k}}\cdots e_{i_{1}}^{d_{1}}(x):x\in X,i_{j}\in I,d_{j}\geq 0\}-\{0\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda).

An extremal subset is generated by its lowest weight elements in the following sense:

Proposition 4.0.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
be an extremal subset and
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\}
its set of lowest weight elements. Then
X=\mathcal{E}(\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. We must show that
x\in\mathcal{E}(z_{j})
for some
1\leq j\leq k
, or equivalently, that
z_{j}\in\mathcal{F}(x)
. If for each
i\in I
we have
f_{i}(x)=0
or
f_{i}(x)\not\in X
, then
x
is a lowest weight element of
X
, and we are done. Otherwise, select
i_{1}\in I
such that
f_{i_{1}}(x)\in X
. Then
x^{\prime}:=f_{i_{1}}^{*}(x)\in X
, since
X
is extremal. Repeating the above recipe, we find
i_{2}\in I
such that
f_{i_{2}}(x^{\prime})\in X
. If this is not possible, then
x^{\prime}\in\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\}
. Otherwise, repeat with
x^{\prime\prime}:=f_{i_{2}}^{*}(x^{\prime})
. Since
X
is finite, we can continue in this way to obtain
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in I
such that
f_{i_{k}}^{*}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{*}(x)\in\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\}
, i.e.
x\in\mathcal{E}(\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\})
.
∎

As a shorthand, we will say that a subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is
\mathcal{E}
-generated
by
L\subseteq X
if
X=\mathcal{E}(L)
. While a lowest weight element need not, in general, have extremal weight, it must in the following special case:

Proposition 4.0.2
.

If
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is extremal and
X=\mathcal{E}(\{x\})
for some
x\in X
, then
x=b_{w\cdot\lambda}
for some
w\in W^{\lambda}
.

Proof.

We will show that there exist
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in I
such that
x=f_{i_{k}}^{*}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{*}(b_{\lambda})
. Assume this is not the case, so that for every expression
x=f_{i_{k}}^{d_{k}}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{d_{1}}(b_{\lambda})
, there exists an index
j
such that
d_{j}<\varphi_{i_{j}}(f_{i_{j-1}}^{d_{j-1}}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{d_{1}}(b_{\lambda}))
. Assume
j
is the minimal such index, so that

x=f_{i_{k}}^{d_{k}}\cdots f_{i_{j}}^{d_{j}}f_{i_{j-1}}^{*}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{*}(b_{\lambda}),

and let
x^{\prime}=f_{i_{j-1}}^{*}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{*}(b_{\lambda})
. We claim that
f_{i_{j}}^{*}(x^{\prime})\not\in\mathcal{E}(\{x\})
. If it were, then
f_{i_{j}}^{*}(x^{\prime})=e_{a_{1}}^{c_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{\ell}}^{c_{\ell}}(x)
for some
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\in I
and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}>0
, so that

x=f_{a_{\ell}}^{c_{\ell}}\cdots f_{a_{1}}^{c_{1}}(f_{i_{j}}^{*}(x^{\prime}))=f_{a_{\ell}}^{c_{\ell}}\cdots f_{a_{1}}^{c_{1}}f_{i_{j}}^{*}f_{i_{j-1}}^{*}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{*}(b_{\lambda}).

By the minimality of
j
, though, this is impossible, so
f_{i_{j}}^{*}(x^{\prime})\not\in\mathcal{E}(x)
. But this now contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{E}(\{x\})
is extremal, since for
S
the
i_{j}
-string through
x^{\prime}
,

S\cap\mathcal{E}(x)\not\in\{\emptyset,\{u_{S}\},S\}.

Thus there exist
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
such that
x=f_{i_{k}}^{*}\cdots f_{i_{1}}^{*}(b_{\lambda})
, i.e.
x=b_{w\cdot\lambda}
for
w=s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
.
∎

Corollary 4.0.2
.

Assume
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is an extremal subset such that
X=\mathcal{E}(\{x\})
for some
x\in X
. If
Y\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is another extremal subset such that
\mathrm{char}(X)=\mathrm{char}(Y)
, then
X=Y
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.0.2
, we have
x=b_{w\cdot\lambda}
for some
w\in W^{\lambda}
. In particular, since
b_{w\cdot\lambda}
is the unique element of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
of weight
w\cdot\lambda
, we must have
x\in Y
since
\mathrm{char}(X)=\mathrm{char}(Y)
. Thus
X=\mathcal{E}(\{x\})\subseteq Y
since
Y
satisfies Theorem
2.3.2
(1), and since their characters coincide, we must have equality:
X=Y
.
∎

Thus extremal subsets which are
\mathcal{E}
-generated by a single element are uniquely determined by their character. This result is not true in general for extremal subsets, however:

Proposition 4.0.3
.

Extremal subsets are not uniquely determined by their character. That is, there exist
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
X,Y\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
extremal subsets such that
\mathrm{char}(X)=\mathrm{char}(Y)
.

A witness to this proposition is given in Example
4.0.4
, and the statement more or less follows from the fact that there may exist lowest weight elements in an extremal subset which do not have extremal weight.

To construct two such subsets, it is useful to recall the notion of
Levi branching
of normal crystals (see, for instance,
[
BS17
]
). For any
J\subseteq I
, we obtain a normal crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)_{J}
for the root system
\Phi_{J}
given by discarding the data of
f_{i},e_{i},\varphi_{i},\varepsilon_{i}
for
i\not\in J
. It is fairly straightforward to see that extremal subsets are preserved under Levi branching: that is, if
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is an extremal subset and
J\subseteq I
, then letting
X_{J}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)_{J}
denote the image of
X
under Levi branching to
J
, we have that each connected component of
X_{J}
is extremal.

In the special case that
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is a type
A_{n-1}
crystal and
J=[n-2]
, the branched crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)_{J}
decomposes as

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)_{J}\cong\bigoplus_{\mu:\lambda/\mu\text{ a horizontal strip}}\mathcal{B}(\mu)\boxtimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda/\mu),

where
\mathcal{B}(\mu)\boxtimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda/\mu)
consists of those
x\in SSYT_{n}(\lambda)
such that
x|_{\leq n-1}\in SSYT_{n-1}(\mu)
, and
\lambda/\mu
is a horizontal strip if it does not contain multiple cells in the same column. Thus
x,y\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
are in the same connected component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)_{J}
if and only if their entries of
n
are in the same positions. As a crystal,
\mathcal{B}(\mu)\boxtimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda/\mu)
is related to
\mathcal{B}(\mu)
via the natural restriction map on the weight lattice
P_{GL_{n}}\to P_{GL_{n-1}}
.

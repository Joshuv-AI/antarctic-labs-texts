p p -adic sheaves on classifying stacks, and the p p -adic Jacquet-Langlands correspondence
David Hansen and Lucas Mann

Abstract
. We establish several new properties of the p p -adic Jacquet-Langlands functor defined by Scholze in terms of the cohomology of the Lubin-Tate tower. In particular, we reprove Scholze’s basic finiteness theorems, prove a duality theorem, and show a kind of partial Künneth formula. Using these results, we deduce bounds on Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, together with some new vanishing and nonvanishing results. Our key new tool is the six functor formalism with solid almost 𝒪 + / p \mathcal{O}^{+}/p -coefficients developed recently by the second author [ Man22 ] . One major point of this paper is to extend the domain of validity of the ! ! -functor formalism developed in [ Man22 ] to allow certain “stacky” maps. In the language of this extended formalism, we show that if G G is a p p -adic Lie group, the structure map of the classifying small v-stack B ​ G ¯ B\underline{G} is p p -cohomologically smooth.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Prologue

This paper has two goals. Our immediate goal is to prove some new properties of the
p
-adic Jacquet-Langlands functor defined by Scholze. Unlike the classical Jacquet-Langlands correspondence, which is defined by a simple character identity, Scholze’s functor is defined inexplicitly, and has turned out to be rather difficult to study. Scholze himself proved a basic finiteness theorem, and established a local-global compatibility result in some cases. We reprove Scholze’s finiteness theorem, and prove a duality theorem and a kind of “partial” Künneth formula. As consequences, we deduce bounds on Gelfand-Kirillov dimension and prove some new vanishing and nonvanishing results for Scholze’s functor.

To explain our second goal, let us briefly recall some recent history. The study of the classical local Langlands correspondence has recently been given new life through the geometrization program proposed by Fargues
[
Far16
]
. This program has already seen spectacular success, most notably in the work of Fargues-Scholze
[
FS21
]
. A key technical ingredient in this program is a sufficiently flexible six-functor formalism for
\ell
-adic étale cohomology of diamonds and v-stacks. This formalism was developed by Scholze
[
Sch18
]
, and then extended further in
[
GHW22
]
.

Our second goal is to lay some technical foundations for the geometrization of the
p
-adic Langlands correspondence. In particular, we extend the
p
-adic six functor formalism developed in
[
Man22
]
to allow certain stacky maps, in parallel with the extension provided in
[
GHW22
]
, and show that classifying stacks of
p
-adic Lie groups fit perfectly into this formalism. This stacky six-functor machinery allows for an elegant formulation of the proof of our main results. With it in hand, one can also begin a meaningful study of
p
-adic sheaves on
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}
. We will pursue this in a subsequent paper.

1.2.
Context and duality

Let us begin by recalling the classical Jacquet-Langlands correspondence. Fix a finite extension
F/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
and an integer
n>1
. Set
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)

D/F
be the central division algebra of invariant
1/n

Theorem 1.1

(Jacquet-Langlands, Deligne-Kazhdan-Vignéras, Rogawski)
.

There is a natural bijection

\mathrm{JL}:\mathrm{Irr}_{L^{2}}(G)\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathrm{Irr}(D^{\times})

from the irreducible essentially square-integrable complex representations of
G

D^{\times}

\chi_{\mathrm{JL}(\pi)}(g^{\prime})=(-1)^{n-1}\chi_{\pi}(g)

and
g^{\prime}\in D^{\times}
.

This bijection is compatible with twisting and passing to contragredient representations.

A very similar statement holds with
\overline{\mathbf{F}_{\ell}}
-coefficients instead of complex coefficients, cf. work of Dat and Vignéras.

In recent years, a
p
-adic version of the Langlands program has emerged, many aspects of which are still conjectural. In the context of this program, a candidate for a

-adic Jacquet-Langlands functor has been proposed by Scholze
[

]

be the infinite level Lubin-Tate
space over
\mathrm{Spa}\,C

with commuting continuous actions of

\pi_{\mathrm{GH}}:\mathcal{M}_{\infty}\to\mathbf{P}_{C}^{n-1}

The period map

-equivariant for a suitable action of

Using this geometry, Scholze defines an exact functor, denoted

from the category

-sheaves on
\mathbf{P}_{C}^{n-1}

This functor is defined by a simple and explicit recipe: given any étale map

-equivariant, the sheaf

-equivariant, and the étale cohomology groups

One of the main results of

Consideration of simple examples shows that in general,

Our first main result can now be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

There is a natural equivalence
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}\circ\mathcal{J}\cong(\mathcal{J}\circ\mathcal{S}_{G})[2n-2](n-1)

1.3.
The stacky 6-functor formalism

In the process of proving our main results we also extend the 6-functor formalism from
[
Man22
]

Now, if

1.4.
Applications to representation theory

One of our main motivations for proving the duality theorem is that Kohlhaase’s duality functors are intimately related to the
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension

\dim_{G}\pi
of an admissible representation
\pi
. Recall that this is a nonnegative integer measuring the asymptotic size of
\pi
; for instance,
\dim_{G}\pi=0
exactly when
\dim_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\pi<\infty
.

Computing the dimension of admissible representations arising in nature is generally quite difficult. However, our duality theorem gives a powerful tool for understanding how the functor
\mathcal{J}
interacts with Gelfand-Kirillov dimension. Our first application in this direction is the following result, which gives a quantitative form of Scholze’s finiteness theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\pi
be an admissible smooth
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
-representation,
and let
N_{\pi}\geq 0
be the least integer such that
\mathcal{J}^{i}(\pi)=0

for all
i>N_{\pi}
. Then

\dim_{D^{\times}}\mathcal{J}^{i}(\pi)\leq\dim_{G}(\pi)+N_{\pi}

for all
i
. In particular, since
N_{\pi}\leq 2n-2
, we have

\dim_{D^{\times}}\mathcal{J}^{i}(\pi)\leq\dim_{G}(\pi)+2n-2

for all
\pi
and all
i
.

Note that the possible increase of dimension by
2n-2
is quite small compared to the maximal Gelfand-Kirillov dimension in this setting, which is
\dim G=\dim D^{\times}=n^{2}[F:\mathbf{Q}_{p}]
. Nevertheless, we conjecture that Theorem
1.5
is not optimal, and that in fact one always has an inequality

\dim_{D^{\times}}\mathcal{J}^{i}(\pi)\leq\dim_{G}(\pi),

cf. Conjecture
4.7
below. This conjecture in turn implies that
\mathcal{J}^{2n-2}(\pi)
is always a finite-dimensional vector space, which can be computed explicitly (Proposition
4.9
).

Under a stronger assumption on
\pi
, we can also prove a bound in the other direction, namely that for some
i
the association
\pi\rightsquigarrow\mathcal{J}^{i}(\pi)
cannot decrease the dimension by very much. Here is a precise statement.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\pi
be an admissible smooth
G
-representation which is Cohen-Macaulay in the sense that
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(\pi)
is nonzero for a single value of
i
. Then either
\mathcal{J}(\pi)=0
or

\max_{i}\dim_{D^{\times}}\mathcal{J}^{i}(\pi)\geq\dim_{G}\pi-(2n-2).

We note that the Cohen-Macaulay condition is often satisfied in practice, and is preserved by parabolic induction.

The duality theorem is even more useful when combined with nontrivial vanishing theorems for
\mathcal{J}

the diagonal maximal torus. For any smooth characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}:F^{\times}\to\mathbf{F}_{p}^{\times}
, we have the usual parabolic induction
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}(\omega\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})
,
where
\omega
is the mod-
p
cyclotomic character. We assume that
\chi_{1}/\chi_{2}\neq\omega^{\pm 1}
. Then
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
\pi(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})
are irreducible, and are exchanged by the functor
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{d}(-)\otimes(\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\omega\circ\det)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(-)
applied to either representation vanishes for all
i\neq d
.

By irreducibility, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{J}^{0}(\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))=0
, and likewise with the characters exchanged. Much less obviously, the main theorem of
[
JLH21
]
shows also that
\mathcal{J}^{2}(\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))=0
. With these vanishing results in mind, we define

\tau(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=\mathcal{J}^{1}(\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})).

Our duality theorem then implies the following dichotomy.

Theorem 1.7
.

Notation and assumptions as above, exactly one of the following is true.

i.
\tau(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=\tau(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})=0.

ii. Both
\tau(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
\tau(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})
are nonzero, and are exchanged by the functor
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}^{d}(-)\otimes(\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\omega\circ\mathrm{Nm})
. In particular, they are both of dimension
d
. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}^{i}(-)
applied to either representation vanishes for all
i\neq d
.

We conjecture that in fact scenario i. in this theorem
never
occurs. When
F=\mathbf{Q}_{p}
and
p>3
, this follows from work of Paškūnas
[
Paš22
]
, who showed by global methods that
\tau(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
\tau(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})
cannot
both
vanish. Combining his results with the previous theorem yields the following corollary.

Corollary 1.8
.

Maintain the notation and assumptions above, and assume moreover that
F=\mathbf{Q}_{p}
and
p>3
. Then
\tau(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
\tau(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})
are nonzero, of dimension one, and are exchanged by the functor
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}^{1}(-)\otimes(\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\omega\circ\mathrm{Nm})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}^{0}(-)
applied to either representation vanishes.

We note that the final vanishing statement here has a more concrete interpretation: it simply means that
\tau(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
does not admit any nonzero
H
-stable quotient
\sigma
with
\dim_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\sigma<\infty
, for any open subgroup
H\subset D^{\times}
(and similarly for
\tau(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})
). Intuitively, this means that
\tau(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is “maximally non-semisimple”. It would be very interesting to find an explicit construction of one-dimensional admissible representations of
D^{\times}
with this property. We also note that a similar but more complicated theorem holds in the setting of
reducible
principal series representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
; see §
4.3.1
for details.

Our last result is an intriguing alternative formula for
\mathcal{J}(\pi)
in the case where
\pi
is parabolically induced. To state this result, we momentarily change our notation slightly, to emphasize the dependence of the Jacquet-Langlands functor on the chosen integer
n
. More precisely, we write
\mathcal{J}_{n}
for the Jacquet-Langlands functor from
\D(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
towards
\D(D_{1/n}^{\times})
. Choose an integer
0<d<n
, and let
P_{n-d,d}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
be the usual upper-triangular parabolic with Levi
\mathrm{GL}_{n-d}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F)
. If
\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}
is any smooth representation of the Levi, we write
\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n-d,d}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2})
for the usual parabolic induction to a smooth representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. In §
5
below, we define a certain auxiliary spatial diamond
W_{n,d}
over
\Spd C
, which comes equipped with a natural
D_{1/n}^{\times}
-action as well as natural
D_{1/n}^{\times}
-equivariant maps
h_{1}:W_{n,d}\to B\underline{\mathrm{GL}_{n-d}(F)}
and
h_{2}:W_{n,d}\to B\underline{D_{1/d}^{\times}}
. In the special case
d=1
,
W_{n,1}
is exactly the quotient
\mathcal{M}_{P}=\mathcal{M}_{\infty}/\underline{P_{n-1,1}}
studied in
[
JLH21
]
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Notation as above, there is a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{J}_{n}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n-d,d}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}))\cong R\Gamma(W_{n,d},h_{1,{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}^{\ast}\sigma_{1}\otimes h_{2,{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}^{\ast}\mathcal{J}_{d}(\sigma_{2})).

We like to think of this as a “partial” Künneth formula. Although this theorem is logically independent of our other main results, the key point in the proof is still the new machinery provided by
[
Man22
]
.

Just as in
[
JLH21
]
, this alternative formula leads to new vanishing results. Here we state the simplest such corollary.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible admissible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(F)
which is not supersingular, and whose underlying vector space is infinite-dimensional. Then
\mathcal{J}^{4}(\pi)=0
.

Geometric preparations

In this section we recall the
p

[
Man22
]
and extend it to a context where it can be applied to classifying stacks of

2.1.
Recollections on the solid six functors

We recall the
p
-adic 6-functor formalism on small v-stacks defined in
[
Man22
]
. Let us first introduce the correct category of geometric objects to work with. In order to make sense of the sheaf
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X}/\pi
on a small v-stack, we need to choose an “untilt”
X^{\sharp}
of
X
(this is automatically chosen if
X
comes from an adic space over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
) and a pseudouiformizer
\pi
on
X^{\sharp}
. To make things more precise, we define an
untilted small v-stack

X^{\sharp}
to be a small v-stack
X
together with a chosen map
X\to\Spd\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. For every map
Y\to X
from a perfectoid space
Y
to
X
, the untilt
X^{\sharp}
induces an untilt
Y^{\sharp}
(in the sense of perfectoid spaces) of
Y
. The untilted small v-stack
X^{\sharp}
comes equipped with a structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\sharp}}
and an integral structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\sharp}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X^{\sharp}}
on
X_{\mathrm{v}}
such that on every affinoid perfectoid
Y\in X_{\mathrm{v}}
we have
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\sharp}}(Y)=\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\sharp}}(Y^{\sharp})
(and similarly for
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\sharp}}
). A
pseudouniformizer
on
X^{\sharp}
is a sheaf of ideals
\pi\subset\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\sharp}}
such that for every affinoid perfectoid
Y=\Spa(B,B^{+})\in X_{\mathrm{v}}
the restriction of
\pi
to
Y
is a principal ideal and generated by a pseudouniformizer in
B^{\sharp+}
. We denote by
\vStackspi
the category of pairs
(X^{\sharp},\pi)
, where
X^{\sharp}
is an untilted small v-stack and
\pi
is a pseudouniformizer on
X^{\sharp}
. A map
(Y^{\sharp},\pi_{Y})\to(X^{\sharp},\pi_{X})
in
\vStackspi
is a map of small v-stacks
Y\to X
over
\Spd\mathbf{Z}_{p}
such that
\pi_{X}|_{Y}=\pi_{Y}
.

The first main result of
[
Man22
]
is the following:

Theorem 2.1

(see
[
Man22
, Theorem 3.1.27]
)
.

There is a unique hypercomplete v-sheaf

\displaystyle(\vStackspi)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}at_{\infty}^{\otimes},\qquad(X^{\sharp},\pi)\mapsto\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\sharp}}/\pi)

such that for every untilted affinoid perfectoid space
X^{\sharp}=\Spa(A^{\sharp},A^{\sharp+})
with pseudouniformizer
\pi\in A^{\sharp+}
, where
X
is weakly of perfectly finite type over a totally disconnected space, we have

\displaystyle\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\sharp}}/\pi)=\Dqcohri(A^{\sharp+}/\pi).

Here
\mathcal{C}at_{\infty}^{\otimes}
denotes the
\infty
-category of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories and
\Dqcohri(A^{\sharp+}/\pi)
denotes the derived
\infty
-category of
solid almost
A^{\sharp+}/\pi
-modules
. The term “almost” refers to the fact that we care about everything only up to
\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\sharp}}
-torsion. The term “solid” was introduced by Clausen-Scholze in
[
Sch19
]
and roughly means “complete topological”. See also
[
Man22
, §2.2, §2.9]
for the precise definition of
\Dqcohri(A^{\sharp+}/\pi)
.

In the following we abstract a little further in order to simplify notation: We denote
\vStacksCoeff
the category of pairs
(X,\Lambda)
, where
X
is a small v-stack and
\Lambda
is a system of integral torsion coefficients on
X
(see
[
Man22
, Definition 3.2.10]
). The main example for
\Lambda
is
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\sharp}}/\pi
for an untilt
X^{\sharp}
of
X

\pi
on

\infty
-category
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
in the same way as in
2.1
.

The second main result of
[
Man22
]
is the construction of a 6-functor formalism for the
\infty
-categories
\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X}/\pi)
. In order to formulate it, let us recall the definition of an abstract 6-functor formalism from
[
Man22
, §A.5]
: Suppose we are given an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
of geometric objects and a collection of edges
E
in
\mathcal{C}
which contains all isomorphisms and is stable under composition and pullback. To the pair
(\mathcal{C},E)
one can then associate an
\infty
-operad
\Corr(\mathcal{C})_{E,all}^{\otimes}
which roughly has the following shape. The underlying
\infty
-category
\Corr(\mathcal{C})_{E,all}
has as objects the same objects as
\mathcal{C}
and as morphisms the diagrams of the form
Y\mathbin{\leftarrow}Y^{\prime}\to X
such that
[Y^{\prime}\to X]\in E
, where the composition of morphisms is given by the obvious pullback diagrams. The operadic structure on
\Corr(\mathcal{C})_{E,all}
is roughly given by
X\otimes Y=X\times Y
(if
\mathcal{C}
admits products then this is literally true and
\Corr(\mathcal{C})_{E,all}
is a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category). With this notation at hand, a
pre-6-functor formalism
on
(\mathcal{C},E)
is defined to be a map of
\infty
-operads
\D\colon\Corr(\mathcal{C})_{E,all}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{C}at_{\infty}^{\times}
, where
\mathcal{C}at_{\infty}^{\times}
denotes the cartesian structure on
\mathcal{C}at_{\infty}
. Such a functor
\D
encodes the following data: For every
X\in\mathcal{C}
we get a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\D(X)
, for every morphism
f\colon Y\to X
in
\mathcal{C}
we get a symmetric monoidal functor
f^{*}\colon\D(X)\to\D(Y)
and for every morphism
[f\colon Y\to X]\in E
we get a functor
f_{!}\colon\D(Y)\to\D(X)
such that the functors
\otimes
,
f^{*}
and
f_{!}
satisfy the usual compatibilities. We say that
\D
is a
6-functor formalism
if the functors
\otimes
,
f^{*}
and
f_{!}
admit right adjoints.

In the setting of the desired
p
-adic 6-functor formalism, we take
\mathcal{C}=\vStacksCoeff
and
E=bdcs
, the class of those maps
f\colon Y\to X
of small v-stacks which are
p
-bounded (this roughly means that the map has finite
p
-cohomological dimension, see
[
Man22
, §3.5]
), locally compactifiable and representable in locally spatial diamonds. The main result of
[
Man22
]
now reads as follows:

Theorem 2.2

(see
[
Man22
, Theorem 3.6.12]
)
.

There is a 6-functor formalism

\displaystyle\Dqcohri\colon\Corr(\vStacksCoeff)^{\otimes}_{bdcs,all}\to\mathcal{C}at_{\infty}^{\times},\qquad(X,\Lambda)\mapsto\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)

with the following properties:

(i)

The symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
and pullback functors
f^{*}
coincide with the version in
2.1
.

(ii)

For every bdcs morphism
f\colon Y\to X
in
\vStacksCoeff
the functor
f_{!}\colon\Dqcohri(Y,\Lambda)\to\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
has the following properties: If
f
is proper then
f_{!}=f_{*}
is right adjoint to
f^{*}
. If
f
is étale then
f_{!}
is left adjoint to
f^{*}
.

2.2.
Six functors for stacky morphisms

In order to efficiently apply the above 6-functor formalism to representation theory, i.e. sheaves on classifying stacks, it is very useful to generalize the definition of the shriek functors
f_{!}
and
f^{!}
to a larger class of maps
f
. Namely, we need to allow certain “stacky” maps
f
as well, similar to what is done in
[
GHW22
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that a bdcs map
f\colon Y\to X
of small v-stacks admits
universal
p
-codescent
if is satisfies the following property: Given any
(X^{\prime},\Lambda)\in\vStacksCoeff
which admits a map (in
\vStacksCoeff
) to some strictly totally disconnected space and given any map
X^{\prime}\to X
with base-change
f^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
and Čech nerve
Y^{\prime}_{\bullet}\to X^{\prime}
the natural functor

\displaystyle\Dqcohrishriek(X^{\prime},\Lambda)\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(Y^{\prime}_{n},\Lambda)

is an equivalence. Here
\Dqcohrishriek
denotes the functor
(Z,\Lambda)\mapsto\Dqcohrishriek(Z,\Lambda)
,
f\mapsto f^{!}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A map
f\colon Y\to X
of small v-stacks is called
p
-fine
if there is a map
g\colon Z\to Y
such that
f\mathbin{\circ}g
is bdcs and
g
is bdcs and admits universal
p
-codescent.

Lemma 2.5
.

(i)

The condition of being
p
-fine is étale local on source and target.

(ii)

The collection of
p
-fine maps is stable under composition and base-change.

(iii)

Every bdcs map is
p
-fine. In particular every étale map and every map of rigid varieties over a fixed base field is
p
-fine.

(iv)

Let
f\colon Y\to X
and
g\colon Z\to Y
be maps of small v-stacks. If
f
and
f\mathbin{\circ}g
are
p
-fine, then so is
g
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
LZ17
, Lemma 3.1.2.(4)]
that bdcs maps admitting universal
p
-codescent are stable under composition. Part (ii) of the claim follows immediately and part (iii) is obvious (see also
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.6.10, Example 3.6.11]
). Part (i) follows from the observation that étale maps admit universal
p
-codescent.

It remains to prove (iv), so let
f
and
g
be given as in the claim. Choose a bdcs map
Y^{\prime}\to Y
admitting universal
p
-codescent such that the composed map
Y^{\prime}\to X
is bdcs. Denote
g^{\prime}\colon Z^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
the base-change of
g
along
Y^{\prime}\to Y
. Since the map
Z^{\prime}\to Z
is bdcs, it follows from (ii) that the composed map
Z^{\prime}\to X
is
p
-fine. We can therefore choose a bdcs map
Z^{\prime\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
admitting universal
p
-codescent such that
Z^{\prime\prime}\to X
is bdcs. Note that the map
Z^{\prime\prime}\to X
factors as
Z^{\prime\prime}\to Y^{\prime}\to X
and since
Y^{\prime}\to X
is bdcs it follows from
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.6.10.(iv)]
that
Z^{\prime\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
is bdcs. In particular
Z^{\prime\prime}\to Y
is bdcs, so the map
Z^{\prime\prime}\to Z
shows that
g
is
p
-fine.
∎

It is completely formal that the
p
-adic 6-functor formalism extends to all
p
-fine maps (in fact a similar extension can be performed in any 6-functor formalism):

Proposition 2.6
.

The 6-functor formalism in

extends uniquely to a 6-functor formalism

\displaystyle\Dqcohri\colon\Corr(\vStacksCoeff)_{pfine,all}\to\mathcal{C}at_{\infty},\qquad(X,\Lambda)\mapsto\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda),

where

denotes the class of
p
-fine maps in
\vStacksCoeff
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\vStacksCoeff
be the full subcategory spanned by those
(X,\Lambda)
which admit a map (in
\vStacksCoeff
) to some strictly totally disconnected space. Then
\mathcal{C}
is a basis of
\vStacksCoeff
, hence by
[
Man22
, Proposition A.5.16]
and
[
Man22
, Proposition A.3.11.(i)]
it is enough to construct the desired extension of the 6-functor formalism on
\mathcal{C}
, i.e. we need to construct a 6-functor formalism
\Dqcohri\colon\Corr(\mathcal{C})_{pfine,all}\to\mathcal{C}at_{\infty}
extending the one from
2.2
. We now apply
[
Man22
, Proposition A.5.14]
with
E=bdcs
,

and
S\subset E
being the subset of those maps which admit universal
p
-codescent. Condition (a) requires
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
to be presentable, which can be achieved by temporarily restricting to

-condensed objects (as in the proof of
[

, Theorem 3.6.12]

-codescent, condition (c) follows from the definition of
p
-fine maps and condition (d) follows from
[
Man22

(see
[
Man22
, Remark A.5.15.(ii)]
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

More concretely, the shriek functor
f_{!}\colon\Dqcohri(Y,\Lambda)\to\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
along a
p
-fine map
f\colon Y\to X
in
\vStacksCoeff
is computed as follows. Via v-descent we can reduce to the case that
(X,\Lambda)
admits a map to some strictly totally disconnected space. Now choose a bdcs map
g\colon Z\to Y
such that
g
admits universal
p
-codescent and
f\mathbin{\circ}g
is bdcs. Let
g_{\bullet}\colon Z_{\bullet}\to Y
denote the Čech nerve of
g
. It follows from the
p
-codescent property of
g
that for every
\mathcal{M}\in\Dqcohri(Y,\Lambda)
the natural map
\varinjlim_{n\in\Delta}g_{n!}g_{n}^{!}\mathcal{M}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{M}
is an isomorphism. Consequently we have

\displaystyle f_{!}\mathcal{M}=f_{!}\varinjlim_{n\in\Delta}g_{n!}g_{n}^{!}\mathcal{M}=\varinjlim_{n\in\Delta}(f\mathbin{\circ}g_{n})_{!}g_{n}^{!}\mathcal{M}.

Note that the maps
f\mathbin{\circ}g_{n}\colon Z_{n}\to X
are bdcs, hence
(f\mathbin{\circ}g_{n})_{!}
was already defined in
2.2
.

In order to make use of
2.6
we need a good amount of examples of bdcs maps admitting universal
p
-codescent. Arguably the most important class of such maps is formed by the
p
-cohomologically smooth maps (see
[
Man22
, Definition 3.8.1]
). The following is an analog of
[
GHW22
, Proposition 3.16]
:

Lemma 2.8
.

Every bdcs and
p
-cohomologically smooth map of small v-stacks admits universal
p
-codescent.

Proof.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a bdcs and
p
-cohomologically smooth map in
\vStacksCoeff
with Čech nerve
f_{\bullet}\colon Y_{\bullet}\to X
; we need to show that the natural functor
f_{\bullet}^{!}\colon\Dqcohrishriek(X,\Lambda)\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(Y_{n},\Lambda)
. This functor admits a left adjoint

\displaystyle f_{\bullet!}\colon\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(Y_{n},\Lambda)\to\Dqcohrishriek(X,\Lambda),\qquad\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}\mapsto\varinjlim_{n\in\Delta}f_{n!}\mathcal{M}_{n}.

Note that
f_{\bullet}^{!}
is conservative: This follows from the fact that
f_{0}^{!}
is conservative, which in turn is implied by the fact that
f_{0}^{!}
is just a twist of
f_{0}^{*}
by definition of the
p
-cohomological smoothness of
f_{0}
. Thus in order to show that
f_{\bullet}^{!}
is an equivalence, it is enough to show that
f_{\bullet!}
is fully faithful, i.e. for given
\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}\in\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(Y_{n},\Lambda)
we need to see that the natural map
f_{\bullet}^{!}f_{\bullet!}\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}
is an isomorphism. This boils down to showing that the map
f_{0}^{!}f_{\bullet!}\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}\to\mathcal{M}_{0}
is an isomorphism. Since
f_{0}
is
p
-cohomologically smooth,
f_{0}^{!}
preserves small colimits, so we end up with the claim that the natural map

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{n\in\Delta}f_{0}^{!}f_{n!}\mathcal{M}_{n}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{M}_{0}

is an isomorphism. Let us denote by
f^{\prime}_{\bullet}\colon Y^{\prime}_{\bullet}\to Y_{0}
the base-change of
Y_{\bullet}\to X
along
f_{0}\colon Y_{0}\to X
. Denote
g_{\bullet}\colon Y^{\prime}_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
the natural projections. Then for every
n\geq 0
the natural morphism
f^{\prime}_{n!}g_{n}^{!}\mathcal{M}_{n}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}f_{0}^{!}f_{n!}\mathcal{M}_{n}
is an isomorphism (by
p
-cohomological smoothness of
f_{0}
and
g_{n}
this reduces to proper base-change). On the other hand
g_{\bullet}^{!}\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}
defines an object of
\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(Y^{\prime}_{n},\Lambda)
so the above claimed isomorphism reduces to showing that the natural functor
\Dqcohrishriek(Y_{0},\Lambda)\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(Y^{\prime}_{n},\Lambda)
is an equivalence. This means that we can replace
Y_{\bullet}\to X

Let

.

The 6-functor formalism from

\displaystyle\Dqcohri(-)^{\varphi}\colon\Corr(\vStacksCoeff)_{pfine,all}\to\mathcal{C}at_{\infty},\qquad(X,\Lambda)\mapsto\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)^{\varphi}.

\IHom
,

Cohomologically smooth maps

A

The basic results on bdcs and

p

to be any locally profinite group with locally finite
p
-cohomological dimension.

First of all, we will need the
\infty
-category of solid

\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})=\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}

[

. This can be identified with the derived
\infty
-category of the category of continuous

-vector spaces (in the obvious sense). We also have the derived category
\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
of smooth

-representations on solid
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector spaces
[
Man22

\D^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
of the abelian category of smooth
G
-representations on discrete
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector spaces. There is a natural identification
\D^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})=\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})_{\omega}
, identifying

as the full subcategory of

consisting of those smooth representations whose underlying

-vector space is discrete (in the topological, i.e. condensed, sense), which we will use without comment. We also note that the composite functor
\D^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\to\D_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
is fully faithful.

Now fix an algebraically closed nonarchimedean field
C
of residue characteristic
p
, and a pseudouniformizer
\pi|p
. Let

denote the classifying stack of

. We then have the

, which (by

Man22
, Lemma 3.4.26.(ii)]
) can be identified with the

-category of smooth
G
-representations on solid almost

-modules over

. This category can be directly accessed by the 6-functor formalism, as we will see. On the other hand, Proposition

gives a natural identification
\D^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})=\D_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}(B\underline{G},\mathbf{F}_{p})
. The Riemann-Hilbert functor then gives a natural fully faithful symmetric monoidal embedding

\D^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\to\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{B\underline{G}}/\pi)^{\varphi},

which factors through the full subcategory
\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{B\underline{G}}/\pi)^{\varphi}_{\omega}
of discrete objects.

3.1.
Smooth representations

We continue to fix a locally profinite group
G
of locally bounded
p
-cohomological dimension.
In this section, we recall some results on the derived
\infty
-category
\D^{{\mathrm{sm}}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
. However, to place our results in context, we momentarily allow our coefficients to be any field
k
(still assuming that
G
has locally bounded
\mathrm{char}k
-cohomological dimension).
Thus, let
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)

denote the category of smooth
G
-representations on
k
-vector
spaces, and let
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,k)
denote its derived
\infty
-category. When

is clear from context, we just write
\D(G)=\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,k)
. We sometimes write
\mathbf{1}\in\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)

for
k
with the trivial
G

The assertions in the following proposition are well-known.

Proposition 3.1
.

1.
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
is a Grothendieck abelian
category. In particular, unbounded complexes have
K
-injective resolutions.

2. The derived category
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,k)
is left-complete and
has a natural symmetric monoidal structure given by
k
-linear tensor
product of cochain complexes.

3. If
H\subset G
is an open subgroup, the restriction functor
r=r_{G}^{H}:\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,k)\to\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(H,k)

is
t
-exact and symmetric monoidal.

Let
\IHom_{G}(A,B)
be the internal
hom in
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,k),
defined as a formal adjoint satisfying
\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(G)}(C\otimes_{k}A,B)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(G)}(C,\IHom_{G}(A,B))
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
H\subset G
is an open subgroup, then there is a natural isomorphism

r\IHom_{G}(A,B)\cong\IHom_{H}(rA,rB)

for any
A,B\in\D(G)
. Here
r:\D(G)\to\D(H)
denotes the restriction
functor.

Proof.

The functor
r
has an left adjoint, given by the (exact) functor

\mathrm{ind}_{H}^{G}(-)=-\otimes_{k[H]}k[G]
of compact induction.
We then compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(C,r\IHom_{G}(A,B))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(G)}(\mathrm{ind}_{H}^{G}C,\IHom_{G}(A,B))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(G)}(A\otimes\mathrm{ind}_{H}^{G}C,B)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(G)}(\mathrm{ind}_{H}^{G}(rA\otimes C),B)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(rA\otimes C,rB)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(C,\IHom_{H}(rA,rB))

naturally in
C\in\D(H)
, so the result follows from Yoneda.
∎

If
A\in\D(G)
is any object and
H\subset G
is any open subgroup,
we write

R\Gamma_{{\mathrm{sm}}}(H,A)=R\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(\mathbf{1},rA)=R\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(G)}(\mathrm{ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1},A).

To actually compute this, let
A\overset{\sim}{\to}I^{\bullet}
be
a
K
-injective resolution by injectives in
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
;
then
R\Gamma_{{\mathrm{sm}}}(H,A)\cong(I^{\bullet})^{H}
. When
H
is a
p
-adic Lie group,

R\Gamma_{{\mathrm{sm}}}(H,-)
coincides with continuous group cohomology, by the main result in
[
Fus21
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
A\in\D(G)
, we have
A\cong\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G\,\mathrm{open}}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,A)
.

Proof.

If
A\overset{\sim}{\to}I^{\bullet}
is a
K
-injective resolution
by injectives, then
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,A)\cong(I^{\bullet})^{H}
for any

H
; here we use that restriction along
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)\to\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(H)

preserves
K
-injective complexes of injectives. But then

\displaystyle\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G\,\mathrm{open}}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,A)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G\,\mathrm{open}}(I^{\bullet})^{H}\cong I^{\bullet}\cong A,

using the fact that each

is a smooth representation.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
A,B\in\D(G)
, we have
\IHom_{G}(A,B)\cong\mathrm{colim}_{H}R\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(A,B).

Here (and in what follows) we drop the restriction functors

from the notation.

Proof.

There are natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{colim}_{H}R\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(A,B)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{colim}_{H}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,\IHom_{H}(A,B))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{colim}_{H}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,\IHom_{G}(A,B))

\displaystyle\cong\IHom_{G}(A,B),

where the second line follows from Lemma
3.2
, and the third line follows
from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

When
G
is locally pro-
p
and
\mathrm{char}k\neq p
,
\IHom_{G}(-,\mathbf{1})

is the usual exact smooth duality functor, i.e. it sends any
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)

to the smooth vectors in

.

Proof.

Easy, using the previous lemma together with the fact that
\mathbf{1}

is an injective object of
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(H)
for
any pro-
p
open compact
H\subset G
. (Use that any injective map

V\to W
in
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(H)
admits an
H

retraction, by averaging any
k
-linear retraction against a
k
-valued

∎

When
G
is a
p
-adic Lie group and

, the naive smooth duality functor on
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)

is rather useless, because the functor of passage to smooth vectors
is no longer exact. To remedy this, Kohlhaase has introduced a family
of derived smooth duality functors
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(-)
on
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
,
which are essentially the right-derived functors of the functor

.
More precisely, Kohlhaase defines

\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(V)=\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G\,\mathrm{open}\,\mathrm{compact}}\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]}^{i}(k,V^{\ast}).

Here
V^{\ast}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(V,\mathbf{F}_{p})
denotes the Pontryagin dual
of

with its natural pseudocompact
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-module structure; this
is naturally a pseudocompact
\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]
-module for any open compact

H\subset G
. We prefer to package the

a single functor on the derived category. In other words, we consider
the endofunctor of

defined by

V\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{G}(V)=\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G\,\mathrm{open}\,\mathrm{compact}}R\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]}(\mathbf{F}_{p},V^{\ast}),

where
V^{\ast}\in\D(\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]-\mathrm{mod})
is computed termwise.
Here

denotes
R\mathrm{Hom}
in the derived
category of
\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]
-modules. Of course,
H^{i}(\mathcal{S}(V))=\mathcal{S}^{i}(V)
when

is concentrated in degree zero.

The following proposition was noted independently by Schneider-Sorensen
[

]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

As endofunctors of
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
, the functor
\IHom_{G}(-,\mathbf{1})

coincides with Kohlhaase’s derived smooth duality functor
\mathcal{S}_{G}(-)
.

Proof.

As noted above,
\mathcal{S}_{G}(V)
is defined by the formula

\mathcal{S}_{G}(V)=\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G\,\mathrm{open}\,\mathrm{compact}}R\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]}(\mathbf{F}_{p},V^{\ast}).

To actually compute this, choose a uniformly powerful open pro-
p
subgroup

G_{0}\subset G
. By work of Lazard
[
Laz65
]
, we can choose a finite length
resolution
P_{\bullet}\to\mathbf{F}_{p}\to 0
of
\mathbf{F}_{p}
by finite free
\mathbf{F}_{p}[[G_{0}]]
-modules.
Then we can express
\mathcal{S}_{G}(-)
more concretely as

\mathcal{S}_{G}(V)=\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G_{0}\,\mathrm{open}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]}(P_{\bullet},V^{\ast}).

Set
I^{n}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cts}}(P_{n},\mathbf{F}_{p})
, so

is a finite resolution of
\mathbf{1}
by injective objects in
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G_{0})

Since Pontryagin duality gives an exact anti-equivalence between smooth

\mathbf{F}_{p}
and pseudocompact
\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]
-modules, we have
natural identifications

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}[[H]]}(P_{\bullet},V^{\ast})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(H)}(V,I^{\bullet})\cong R\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(V,\mathbf{1}).

Putting things together, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{G}(V)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G_{0}\,\mathrm{open}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(H)}(V,I^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{colim}_{H\subset G_{0}\,\mathrm{open}}R\mathrm{Hom}_{\D(H)}(V,\mathbf{1})

\displaystyle\cong\IHom_{G}(V,\mathbf{1}),

where the last line follows by Lemma
3.4
.
∎

In the remainder of this subsection, we continue to assume that
G
is a
p
-adic Lie group.
Recall that the

of an admissible smooth representation

is the quantity

\mathrm{dim}_{G}V=\mathrm{dim}\,G\cdot\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\dim_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}V^{K_{n}}}{\log\,[K_{n}:K_{0}]}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\dim_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}V^{K_{n}}}{n\log p}.

Here
K_{0}\subset G
is a fixed choice of a uniformly powerful open

p
subgroup, and

is the closed
normal subgroup generated by the

K
. (One automatically has that
[K_{n}:K_{0}]=p^{n\cdot\dim G}
,
which explains the second equality above.) By
[

, this limit exists independently
of the choice of

, and defines an
integer in the interval
[0,\dim G]
. Moreover,
\mathrm{dim}_{G}V=0

iff
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}V<\infty
. Additionally, if
0\to V^{\prime}\to V\to V^{\prime\prime}\to 0

is an exact sequence of admissible smooth representations, then
\mathrm{dim}_{G}V=\max(\mathrm{dim}_{G}V^{\prime},\dim_{G}V^{\prime\prime})
.
In particular, any subquotient
W
of a given
V
satisfies
\mathrm{dim}_{G}W\leq\mathrm{dim}_{G}V
.

A key point for us is that
\dim_{G}
is closely related to the behavior
of the functors
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

(i)

If
V
is an admissible smooth
G
-representation, then each
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(V)
is admissible smooth, with
\mathrm{dim}_{G}\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(V)\leq i
for all
i
. Moreover, if
d=\mathrm{dim}_{G}V
then
\mathrm{dim}_{G}\mathcal{S}_{G}^{d}(V)=d
and
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(V)=0
for all
i>d
.

(ii)

For any
A\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
whose cohomologies
H^{i}(A)
are all admissible, the complex
\mathcal{S}_{G}(A)
has admissible cohomologies, and the biduality map
A\to(\mathcal{S}_{G}\circ\mathcal{S}_{G})(A)
is an isomorphism.

(iii)

If
V
is an admissible smooth representation with
\dim_{G}V=0
, so
\dim_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}V<\infty
, then
\mathcal{S}(V)\cong\mathcal{S}^{0}(V)
is the naive linear dual of
V
.

Proof.

The first part is essentially a summary of results from
[
Koh17
]
. The second part is Pontryagin-dual to the biduality discussed
in the proof of
[
Koh17
, Theorem 3.5]
; see also
[
SS22
, Proposition 3.4]
. The third part is immediate from the first part.
∎

Note that part (i) here implies that
\dim_{G}V=\sup\left\{i\mid\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(V)\neq 0\right\}
. We also observe that
\mathcal{S}^{0}(-)
can be completely understood.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
V
be any admissible smooth
G
-representation. Then
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{0}(V)\neq 0

if and only if
V
admits a nonzero
G
-stable quotient
V\twoheadrightarrow W

with
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}W<\infty
.

Proof.

“If”: Given such a
V\to W
, we get a nonzero map
\mathcal{S}(W)\to\mathcal{S}(V)
.
But the previous proposition shows that
\mathcal{S}(W)=\tau^{\leq 0}\mathcal{S}(W)=\mathcal{S}^{0}(W)
,
so we get a nonzero map
\mathcal{S}^{0}(W)\to\mathcal{S}(V)
which
necessarily factors over a nonzero map
\mathcal{S}^{0}(W)\to\mathcal{S}^{0}(V)
.

“Only if”: Suppose
\mathcal{S}^{0}(V)\neq 0
. By the previous
proposition, this is zero-dimensional as a
G
-representation, so

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{S}^{0}(V))\cong\mathcal{S}^{0}(\mathcal{S}^{0}(V))
.
In particular, applying
\mathcal{S}
to the map
\mathcal{S}^{0}(V)=\tau^{\leq 0}\mathcal{S}(V)\to\mathcal{S}(V)

and using biduality, we get a nonzero map
V\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{S}(V))\to\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{S}^{0}(V))\cong\mathcal{S}^{0}(\mathcal{S}^{0}(V))
,
and
\mathcal{S}^{0}(\mathcal{S}^{0}(V))
is finite-dimensional over

\mathbf{F}_{p}
.
∎

We conclude this section by giving a geometric interpretation of
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
. Let
C
be any algebraically closed nonarchimedean field of residue characteristic
p
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Notation as above, there are natural symmetric monoidal equivalences

\D_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}([\Spd(C)/G],\mathbf{F}_{p})\cong\D([\Spd(C)/G]_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}},\mathbf{F}_{p})\cong\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p}).

In particular, the first two categories are left-complete and canonically independent of
C
.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of Theorem V.1.1 in
[
FS21
]
, noting that that
[\Spd(C)/G]_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}
locally has uniformly bounded
p
-cohomological dimension by Lazard-Serre.
∎

3.2.
Sheaves on classifying stacks

We now study the stacky 6-functor formalism introduced above in the context of the classifying stack
*/G
associated to a locally profinite group
G
. Recall that if
G
has locally finite

-cohomological dimension then for every totally disconnected space
X=\Spa(A,A^{+})
with pseudouniformizer
\pi
the
\infty
-category
\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X/G}/\pi)
can be identified with the
\infty
-category of smooth
G
-representations on solid almost
A^{+}/\pi
-modules (see
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.4.26.(ii)]
). In the following we will show that if
G
has locally finite
p
-cohomological dimension then
*/G\to*
is
p
-fine, and we will deduce a precise criterion for when the map
*/G\to*
is
p
-cohomologically smooth. In particular, this will hold in all practical situations, see Theorem
3.18
below.

To start things off, we first translate the finite
p
-cohomological dimension condition into a vanishing of Ext-groups. Also recall the definition of the
p
-cohomological dimension
\cd_{p}G
from
[
Man22
, Definition 3.4.20]
(see
[
Man22
, Proposition 3.4.22]
to relate this to more classical versions).

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
G
be a profinite group and let
M,N\in\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
be static continuous
G
-representations on solid
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector spaces. If
M
is discrete then

\displaystyle\Ext^{i}_{G}(M,N)=0\qquad\text{for $i>\cd_{p}G$}.

Proof.

The
\infty
-category
\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
comes naturally equipped with a symmetric monoidal structure such that the forgetful functor
q^{*}\colon\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}\to\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
is symmetric monoidal (e.g. one can construct
\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
as certain sheaves on the classifying stack
*/G
in a suitable geometric setup). The forgetful functor
q^{*}
has a left adjoint
q_{\natural}\colon\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})\to\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
which is given by
q_{\natural}(M)=\mathbf{F}_{p{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}[G]\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{p{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}}M
. It is straightforward to check that
q_{\natural}
satisfies the projection formula, i.e. that for all
M\in\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
and
N\in\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
the natural morphism
q_{\natural}(N\otimes q^{*}M)\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}q_{\natural}N\otimes M
is an isomorphism. The symmetric monoidal structure on
\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
is closed, i.e. admits internal hom’s
\IHom
(this is clear if we restrict to
\kappa
-condensed objects for some cardinal
\kappa
and then follows for unrestricted condensed objects by the following computation). It follows formally from the projection formula for
q_{\natural}
that for all
M,N\in\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
the natural morphism

\displaystyle q^{*}\IHom(M,N)\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\IHom(q^{*}M,q^{*}N)

is an isomorphism – in other words, the internal hom in
\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
is computed on the underlying solid
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector spaces. In particular, if
M
and
N
are static and
M
is discrete (and thus
q^{*}M\cong\bigoplus_{I}\mathbf{F}_{p}
for some set
I
) then
\IHom(M,N)
is static. Therefore, from

\displaystyle\Ext^{i}_{G}(M,N)=\pi_{0}R\Hom(M,N[i])=\pi_{0}\Hom(\mathbf{F}_{p},\IHom(M,N[i]))=H^{i}(G,\IHom(M,N))

we immediately deduce the claim.
∎

We will now show that
*/G\to*
is
p
-fine. This reduces to showing that the canonical cover
*\to*/G
admits universal
p
-codescent, which will be established by adapting Mathew’s ideas on descendable algebras
[
Mat16
]
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
G
be a profinite group with
\cd_{p}G<\infty
. Then the map
*\to*/G
of small v-stacks is bdcs and admits universal
p
-codescent.

Proof.

It is clear that the map
*\to*/G
is bdcs (e.g. the pullback along itself is
G\to*
, which is pro-étale). Now pick any
(X,\Lambda)\in\vStacksCoeff
admitting a map to a strictly totally disconnected space, pick any map
X\to*/G
and denote
q\colon Y\to X
the base-change of
*\to*/G
. We need to show that
q
admits
p
-codescent, i.e. if
q_{\bullet}\colon Y_{\bullet}\to X
denotes the Čech nerve of
q
then the natural functor

\displaystyle\Dqcohrishriek(X,\Lambda)\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(Y_{n},\Lambda)

is an equivalence. To see this we employ Lurie’s Beck-Chevalley condition, see
[
Lur
, Corollary 4.7.5.3]
. Part (2) of this condition is easily satisfied: Since all the maps in the diagram
Y_{\bullet}\to X
are proper, the required left adjointability for the upper shriek functors reduces to proper base-change (by passing to left adjoints). Thus in order to apply Lurie’s result it remains to verify the following:

(1)

The functor
q^{!}\colon\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)\to\Dqcohri(Y,\Lambda)
is conservative.

(2)

The functor
q^{!}
preserves geometric realizations of
q^{!}
-split simplicial objects in
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
.

To prove (a) and (b) we apply ideas by Mathew
[
Mat16
]
(which were also used extensively in the setup of the
p
-adic 6-functor formalism, cf.
[
Man22
, §2.6]
). More concretely, we will prove the following claim:

(
\ast
)

Let
\langle q_{*}q^{!}\rangle\subset\Fun(\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda),\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda))
be the full subcategory generated by the endofunctor
q_{*}q^{!}
under compositions, retracts and finite (co)limits. Then
\langle q_{*}q^{!}\rangle
contains the identity functor.

Let us first see how (
\ast
) implies (a) and (b), so assume that (
\ast
) holds for the moment. To prove (a), let
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be a given map in
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
such that
q^{!}\mathcal{M}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}q^{!}\mathcal{N}
is an isomorphism. Then also
q_{*}q^{!}\mathcal{M}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}q_{*}q^{!}\mathcal{N}
is an isomorphism and one deduces easily that
F(\mathcal{M})\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}F(\mathcal{N})
is an isomorphism for all
F\in\langle q_{*}q^{!}\rangle
; taking
F=\id
shows that
\mathcal{M}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{N}
is an isomorphism, as desired. To prove (b), let
\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}
be a
q^{!}
-split simplicial object in
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
. Then
q_{*}q^{!}\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}
is a constant
\Ind
-object (i.e. lies in the essential image of
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)\mathrel{\hookrightarrow}\Ind(\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda))
) and it follows immediately that the same is true for
F(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet})
for all
F\in\langle q_{*}q^{!}\rangle
. Taking
F=\id
shows that
\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}
is a constant
\Ind
-object, so that its colimit commutes with any exact functor; in particular it commutes with
q^{!}
.

It now remains to verify (
\ast
). We compute (using
[
Man22
, Corollary 3.6.17.(i)]
)

\displaystyle q_{*}q^{!}=q_{*}\IHom(\Lambda^{a},q^{!})=\IHom(q_{!}\Lambda^{a},-)=\IHom(q_{*}\Lambda^{a},-).

Therefore (
\ast
) can be reduced to showing the following: Let
\langle q_{*}\Lambda^{a}\rangle\subset\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
be the full subcategory generated by
q_{*}\Lambda^{a}
under tensor product, retracts and finite (co)limits; then
\langle q_{*}\Lambda^{a}\rangle
contains the tensor unit
\Lambda^{a}
. By
[
Mat16
, Proposition 3.20]
this is equivalent to the following claim:

(
\ast^{\prime}
)

The algebra object
q_{*}\Lambda^{a}\in\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
admits descent.

It remains to prove that (
\ast^{\prime}
) is true. We first perform some reductions. By assumption on
(X,\Lambda)
there is a map
(X,\Lambda)\to(Z,\Lambda)
with
Z=\Spa(A,A^{+})
being strictly totally disconnected. We thus get an induced map
X\to Z\times*/G=Z/G
such that
q
is the pullback of the projection
q_{Z}\colon Z\to Z/G
. The induced pullback functor
\Dqcohri(Z/G,\Lambda)\to\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
is exact, symmetric monoidal and sends
q_{Z*}\Lambda^{a}
to
q_{*}\Lambda^{a}
(by proper base-change). It is therefore enough to prove that (
\ast
’) holds for
q_{Z}
in place of
q
, so from now on we assume
X=Z/G
and hence
Y=Z
.

Given a morphism
\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda
of integral torsion coefficients on
Z
, the induced base-change functor
\Dqcohri(Z,\Lambda^{\prime})\to\Dqcohri(Z,\Lambda)
preserves tensor products, retracts and finite (co)limits and sends
q_{*}\Lambda^{\prime a}
to
q_{*}\Lambda^{a}
(by
[
Man22
, Proposition 3.6.16]
), hence if (
\ast^{\prime}
) holds for
\Lambda^{\prime}
then it also holds for
\Lambda
. By definition of integral torsion coefficients we can thus reduce to the case that
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{Z^{\sharp}}/\pi
for some untilt
Z^{\sharp}
of
Z
and some pseudouniformizer
\pi
on
Z^{\sharp}
. Pick some pseudouniformizer
\varpi
on
Z^{\sharp}
such that
\varpi|p
and some integer
n>0
such that
\pi|\varpi^{n}
. By
[
Mat16
, Proposition 3.35]
the map of discrete rings
A^{\sharp+}/\pi\to A^{\sharp+}/\varpi
admits descent, which after pullback along
Z/G\to Z
implies that the map
\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\sharp}/G}^{+a}/\pi\to\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\sharp}/G}^{+a}/\varpi
in
\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{Z^{\sharp}/G}/\pi)
admits descent. Therefore, if we can show that (
\ast^{\prime}
) holds for
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}^{+}_{Z^{\sharp}/G}/\varpi
then by
[
Mat16
, Proposition 3.24]
and
[
BS17
, Lemma 11.10]
it also follows for
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}^{+}_{Z^{\sharp}/G}/\pi
. Thus from now on we can assume that
\pi=\varpi
, i.e. we now have
\pi|p
.

Now recall the Riemann-Hilbert functor
RH\colon\D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z/G,\mathbf{F}_{p})^{\mathrm{oc}}\to\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{Z^{\sharp}/G}/\pi)
from
[
Man22
, Definition 3.9.21.(a)]
(where we ignore the
\varphi
-module structure). Then
RH
is symmetric monoidal and exact and satisfies
RH(q_{*}\mathbf{F}_{p})=q_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\sharp}}^{+a}/\pi)
. To verify the latter, note that this identity can be checked after applying
q^{*}
, where we get (using base-change)

\displaystyle q^{*}RH(q_{*}\mathbf{F}_{p})=RH(q^{*}q_{*}\mathbf{F}_{p})=RH(\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p}))=\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}A^{\sharp+,a}/\pi=\cts(G,A^{\sharp+,a}/\pi)

\displaystyle\qquad=q^{*}q_{*}(A^{\sharp+,a}/\pi)

(here we implicitly use the identity
\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{Z^{\sharp}}/\pi)=\Dqcohri(A^{\sharp+}/\pi)
). All in all this means that it is enough to show that
q_{*}\mathbf{F}_{p}\in\D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z/G,\mathbf{F}_{p})^{\mathrm{oc}}
is descendable. It follows easily from
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.9.18.(iii)]
that
\D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z/G,\mathbf{F}_{p})^{\mathrm{oc}}=\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p}(Z))_{\omega}
is the derived
\infty
-category of discrete smooth
G
-representations on
\mathbf{F}_{p}(Z)
-modules (use the same argument as in the proof of
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.4.26.(iii)]
). Our goal is to show that the
G
-representation
\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p}(Z))
admits descent in this
\infty
-category. By considering base-change along
\mathbf{F}_{p}\to\mathbf{F}_{p}(Z)
we reduce the claim to the following:

(
\ast^{\prime\prime}
)

The algebra object
\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})_{\omega}
admits descent.

Note that
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})_{\omega}
is equivalent to the full subcategory of
\D_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})^{BG}
spanned by the discrete
G
-representations (e.g. both
\infty
-categories can be constructed using the same descent diagram), hence by
3.10
it follows that for all static
M,N\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})_{\omega}
we have
\pi_{0}\Hom(M,N[i])=0
for
i>d:=\cd_{p}G
. Now consider the triangle

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{p}\to\Tot_{d}(\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})^{\otimes\bullet+1})\to X

in
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})_{\omega}
, where
\Tot_{d}
denotes the
d
-truncated totalization of a cosimplicial object (i.e. the limit over
\Delta_{\leq d}
). We know that
\Tot(\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})^{\otimes\bullet+1})=\mathbf{F}_{p}
(by descent) and by the dual version of
[
Lur
, Proposition 1.2.4.5]
we have
\pi_{i}\Tot_{d}=\pi_{i}\Tot
for
i>-d
. It follows that
X
is concentrated in homological degrees

, so that by the above vanishing of hom’s the connecting map
X\to\mathbf{F}_{p}[1]
must be zero. It follows that
\Tot_{d}(\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})^{\otimes\bullet+1})\cong\mathbf{F}_{p}\oplus X
, which immediately implies that
\cts(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
admits descent.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
G
be a profinite group with
\cd_{p}G<\infty
. Then:

(i)

The map
*/G\to*
is
p
-fine.

(ii)

For every
(X,\Lambda)\in\vStacksCoeff
with associated map
f\colon X/G\to X
there is a natural isomorphism
f_{!}=f_{*}
of functors
\Dqcohri(X/G,\Lambda)\to\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from
3.11
by using the factorization
\id=[*\to*/G\to*]
. To prove (ii) let
(X,\Lambda)
be given and assume first that
(X,\Lambda)
admits a map to some strictly totally disconnected space. Let
q\colon X\to X/G
denote the projection and let
q_{\bullet}\colon X_{\bullet}\to X/G
be the Čech nerve of
q
. By
3.11
we have
\Dqcohrishriek(X/G,\Lambda)=\varprojlim_{n\in\Delta}\Dqcohrishriek(X_{n},\Lambda)
, which means that for all
\mathcal{M}\in\Dqcohri(X/G,\Lambda)
the natural map
\varinjlim_{n\in\Delta}q_{n!}q_{n}^{!}\mathcal{M}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{M}
is an isomorphism. Since both
f_{*}
and
f_{!}
preserve small colimits (for
f_{*}
this follows from
\cd_{p}G<\infty
) and all
q_{n}
and
f\mathbin{\circ}q_{n}
are proper we deduce

\displaystyle f_{*}=f_{*}\varinjlim_{n}q_{n!}q_{n}^{!}=\varinjlim_{n}(f\mathbin{\circ}q_{n})_{*}q_{n}^{!}=\varinjlim_{n}(f\mathbin{\circ}q_{n})_{!}q_{n}^{!}=f_{!}.

The case of general
(X,\Lambda)
can be deduced via descent (more precisely, do the above computation on sections of the cocartesian fibrations over
\Delta
).
∎

Having established that
*/G\to*
is
p
-fine in many cases, we now determine criteria for when this map is
p
-cohomologically smooth. When
G
has finite
p
-cohomological dimension, the precise answer is captured by the following definition.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
G
be a profinite group with
\cd_{p}G<\infty
. We say that
G
is
p
-Poincaré of dimension
d
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

H^{i}(G,M)
is finite for all
i\geq 0
and all
G
-representations
M
on finite
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector spaces.

(2)

The solid
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector space
R\Gamma(G,\mathbf{F}_{p{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}[G])
is invertible and concentrated in cohomological degree
d
(i.e. it is isomorphic to
\mathbf{F}_{p}[-d]
).

Note that condition (i) is equivalent to the finiteness of
H^{i}(H,\mathbf{F}_{p})
for all
i\geq 0
and all open subgroups
H\subseteq G
. Note also that if
G
is
p
-Poincaré of dimension
d
, then so is every open subgroup of
G
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
G
be a profinite group with
\cd_{p}G<\infty
.

(i)

G
satisfies condition (i) of
3.13
if and only if for all strictly totally disconnected spaces
(X,\Lambda)\in\vStacksCoeff
with associated map
f\colon X/G\to X
the morphism
f^{*}\otimes f^{!}\Lambda^{a}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}f^{!}
is an equivalence of functors
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)\to\Dqcohri(X/G,\Lambda)
.

(ii)

G
is a
p
-Poincaré group (of dimension
d
) if and only if the map
*/G\to*
is
p
-cohomologically smooth (of pure dimension
d/2
).

Proof.

Fix a strictly totally disconnected space
(X,\Lambda)\in\vStacksCoeff
with associated map
f\colon X/G\to X
. Then
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)=\Dqcohri(\Lambda(X))
is the
\infty
-category of solid almost
\Lambda(X)
-modules, while
\Dqcohri(X/G,\Lambda)
is the
\infty
-category of smooth
G
-representations on such modules (see
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.4.26]
). Moreover,
f_{*}
computes smooth
G
-cohomology and
f^{*}
associates to every
M\in\Dqcohri(\Lambda(X))
the trivial
G
-representation on
M
.

We first prove (i). By
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.4.19]
the collection of functors
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,-)\colon\Dqcohri(X/G,\Lambda)\to\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
for open compact subgroups
H
is conservative. Therefore the natural map
f^{*}\otimes f^{!}\Lambda^{a}\to f^{!}
is an equivalence if and only if for all compact open subgroups
H\subset G
the map
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,f^{*}\otimes f^{!}\Lambda^{a})\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,f^{!})
is an equivalence of endofunctors of
\Dqcohri(X,\Lambda)
. Let us denote
f_{H}\colon*/H\to*
the projection, which factors over the finite étale projection
*/H\to*/G
. Then we see that
f^{*}\otimes f^{!}\Lambda^{a}\to f^{!}
is an equivalence if and only if for all compact open subgroups
H\subset G
the map

\displaystyle f_{H*}(f_{H}^{*}\otimes f_{H}^{!}\Lambda^{a})\to f_{H*}f_{H}^{!}

is an equivalence. By
3.12
we have
f_{H*}=f_{H!}
, so in particular
f_{H*}
satisfies the projection formula. We compute

\displaystyle f_{H*}(f_{H}^{*}\otimes f_{H}^{!}\Lambda^{a})

\displaystyle=-\otimes f_{H*}f_{H}^{!}\Lambda^{a}=-\otimes\IHom(f_{H*}\Lambda^{a},\Lambda^{a}),

\displaystyle f_{H*}f_{H}^{!}

\displaystyle=f_{H*}\IHom(\Lambda^{a},f_{H}^{!})=\IHom(f_{H!}\Lambda^{a},-)=\IHom(f_{H*}\Lambda^{a},-).

It follows that

. Conversely, assume that

We now prove (ii), so from now on we can assume that

\displaystyle q^{*}f^{!}\Lambda^{a}=\varinjlim_{H}\IHom(f_{H*}\Lambda^{a},\Lambda^{a})=(\varinjlim_{H}\IHom(\Gamma(H,\mathbf{F}_{p}),\mathbf{F}_{p}))\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\Lambda(X)^{a}

Theorem 3.19
.

Any torsion-free compact

-adic analytic group is

-Poincaré.

The proof relies on deep results of Lazard
[
Laz65
]
, which are in some sense the only “non-formal” input we need. To apply Lazard’s results, we need some general comparison between condensed group cohomology and classical continuous group cohomology, which takes into account a natural topological structure on the latter. To state the relevant result, fix a locally profinite group
G
. Let
V
be a continuous representation of
G
on a Hausdorff locally compact
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-module. Then
\underline{V}
is a solid
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector space with continuous
G
-action, and in particular is an
\mathbf{F}_{p{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}[G]
-module. Thus we may form the condensed group cohomology

R\Gamma(G,\underline{V})=\IHom_{\mathbf{F}_{p{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}[G]}(\mathbf{F}_{p},\underline{V})\in\mathcal{D}_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})

as considered above.

On the other hand, the classical continuous group cohomology
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,V)
is by definition computed by the usual complex

0\to V\to\mathcal{C}(G,V)\to\mathcal{C}(G^{2},V)\to\cdots

of inhomogeneous cochains. Giving each
\mathcal{C}(G^{n},V)
the compact-open topology, this is naturally a complex of Hausdorff locally compact
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-modules. In particular, passing termwise to the condensation, we get a complex

0\to\underline{V}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}(G,V)}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}(G^{2},V)}\to\cdots

of solid
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector spaces. Let
\underline{R\Gamma_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,V)}
denote the image of this complex in
\mathcal{D}_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
.

Lemma 3.20
.

There is a natural isomorphism

R\Gamma(G,\underline{V})\simeq\underline{R\Gamma_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,V)}

in
\mathcal{D}_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
.

Proof of Theorem
3.19
.

Let
G
be as in the statement of the theorem. Then condition (i) in Definition
3.13
is classical, see e.g.
[
SW00
, §5]
. For condition (ii), recall that
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p}[[G]])\simeq\mathbf{F}_{p}[-\dim G]
by
[
Laz65
, Ch. V]
and
[
SW00
, §4-5]
. Since
\underline{\mathbf{F}_{p}[[G]]}=\mathbf{F}_{p{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}[G]
, the conclusion now follows from Lemma
3.20
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.20
.

Arguing as in the proof of
[
Man22
, Proposition 3.4.6]
, one sees that
R\Gamma(G,\underline{V})
is computed as the cohomology of the complex

0\to\underline{V}\to\underline{\Hom}(\underline{G},\underline{V})\to\underline{\Hom}(\underline{G}^{2},\underline{V})\to\cdots

where the internal homs are taken in condensed sets. By the definition of
\underline{R\Gamma_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,V)}
, it now suffices to show that
\underline{\Hom}(\underline{S},\underline{V})
identifies with
\underline{\cts(S,V)}
functorially in locally profinite
S
. This is a special case of the next lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
X
be any locally compact Hausdorff space, and let
Y
be any compactly generated T1 topological space. Equip the space of continuous maps
\cts(X,Y)
with the compact-open topology. Then
\cts(X,Y)
is compactly generated T1, and
\underline{\cts(X,Y)}=\underline{\Hom}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
, where
\underline{\Hom}
denotes the internal hom in condensed sets.

Proof.

The claim regarding the topology on
\cts(X,Y)
is easy. For the remaining claim, let
T
be any profinite space. We then compute that

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathrm{Cond}}(\underline{T},\underline{\Hom}(\underline{X},\underline{Y}))

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\mathrm{Cond}}(\underline{T}\times\underline{X},\underline{Y})

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\mathrm{Top}}(T\times X,Y)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\mathrm{Top}}(T,\cts(X,Y))

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\mathrm{Cond}}(\underline{T},\underline{\cts(X,Y)})

naturally in
T,X,Y
. The first equality is immediate from the universal property of
\underline{\Hom}
. The second equality follows from the fact that on compactly generated T1 spaces,
S\mapsto\underline{S}
is fully faithful and commutes with finite products. The fourth equality follows similarly, using the first claim in the lemma. The third equality follows from the assumption that
X
is locally compact Hausdorff, which is exactly the condition needed to ensure that
\cts(X,-)
with its compact-open topology is right-adjoint to
-\times X
. Since
T
is arbitrary, the result now follows from Yoneda. ∎

3.3.
Admissible representations

Given a locally profinite group

-representations are the admissible ones. Classically, a smooth

-representation on an

-vector space

the space

of
H
-invariants is finite. In the condensed derived world the correct analogue of this definition seems to be the following:

Definition 3.22
.

Let
G
be a locally profinite group which has locally finite
p
-cohomological dimension. A smooth
G
-representation
V\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
is called

if for a basis of compact open subgroups

Remarks 3.23
.

(i)

An admissible

is automatically discrete because all

(cf.
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.4.19]
). Thus

admits a representation as a complex of classical smooth
G
-representations.

(ii)

Suppose that
G
is a
p
-adic Lie group. Then by
[
SS22
, Corollary 4.12]
a smooth representation
V\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
is admissible if and only if it is bounded and discrete and all
\pi_{n}V
are admissible smooth representations in the classical sense.

Now suppose we are additionally given an algebraically closed perfectoid field
C
with pseudouniformizer
\pi|p
and we consider the classifying stack
B\underline{G}=\Spd(C)/\underline{G}
. Then
\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{B\underline{G}}/\pi)^{\varphi}
is the
\infty
-category of smooth

-representations on solid almost

-modules over
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{C}/\pi
and by the

-category contains the
\infty
-category
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
of smooth

-representations as a full subcategory. We make the following definition:

Definition 3.24
.

Let
G
be a locally profinite group which has locally finite
p
-cohomological dimension. Let
C
be an algebraically closed perfectoid field with pseudouniformizer

such that

. A smooth

if it comes from an admissible

-representation via the embedding

\displaystyle-\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{C}/\pi\colon\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\mathrel{\hookrightarrow}\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathcal{O}_{C}/\pi)^{\varphi}

obtained from the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence on the classifying stack

.

One might also consider defining

to be admissible if the smooth group cohomology groups

are perfect for a basis of open subgroups

. In fact, this easily turns out to be equivalent to
3.24
, as we will see below. If
G
is
p
-Poincaré then there is also a characterization of admissible representations in terms of their behavior under duality. To show this, we need the following characterization of perfect (equivalently, dualizable) objects in terms of their duals:

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
K
be a perfectoid field with pseudouniformizer
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Then a
\varphi
-module
M\in\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\pi)^{\varphi}
is dualizable if and only if
M
is bounded, both
M
and
M^{\vee}
are discrete and the natural map
M\to M^{\vee\vee}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

First assume that
M
is dualizable. Then by
[
Man22
, Proposition 3.7.5]
both
M
and
M^{\vee}
are discrete. It follows from
[
Man22
, Lemmas 3.9.2, 3.9.3]
that
M
is bounded. The isomorphism
M\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}M^{\vee\vee}
is clear. This proves the “only if” part of the claim.

We now prove the “if” part, so assume that
M
is bounded,
M
and
M^{\vee}
are discrete and
M\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}M^{\vee\vee}
is an isomorphism. We want to show that
M
is dualizable, for which we can assume that
M
is concentrated in homological degree
\geq 0
. Now consider
M_{0}:=\pi_{0}M
, which is a static discrete
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}
-module. From the fact that
M^{\vee}
is discrete it follows that
\pi_{0}M_{0}^{\vee}=\pi_{0}M^{\vee}
is discrete. After choosing a surjection
\bigoplus_{I}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi\mathrel{\twoheadrightarrow}M_{0}
we get an injection
\pi_{0}M_{0}^{\vee}\mathrel{\hookrightarrow}\prod_{I}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi
. Now apply
\pi_{0}(-)_{*}
to get a submodule

\displaystyle M_{0}^{\prime}:=\pi_{0}(\pi_{0}M_{0}^{\vee})_{*}\subseteq\prod_{I}A,

where we abbreviate

. Note that
M_{0}^{\prime}
is equipped with the subspace topology since it is a kernel of a map
\prod_{I}A\to\prod_{J}A
. Moreover, the fact that
\pi_{0}M_{0}^{\vee}
is discrete implies that for every
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}_{K}
the submodule
T_{\varepsilon}\subset M_{0}^{\prime}
of
\varepsilon
-torsion in
M_{0}^{\prime}
is open. In particular there is an open subset
U_{\varepsilon}\subset\prod_{I}A
containing

such that
U_{\varepsilon}\mathbin{\cap}M_{0}^{\prime}\subseteq T_{\varepsilon}
. In fact we can choose
U_{\varepsilon}
to be an element of the standard basis for the product topology, i.e.
U_{\varepsilon}=U_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\times\prod_{I\setminus I_{\varepsilon}}A
for some finite subset
I_{\varepsilon}\subset I
and some open subset
U_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\subset\prod_{I_{\varepsilon}}A
. This means that if

and

for

then
\varepsilon(a_{i})_{i}=\varepsilon(b_{i})_{i}
. Now let

under the projection
\prod_{I}A\mathrel{\twoheadrightarrow}\prod_{I_{\varepsilon}}A
(equipped with the subspace topology). Then there is a natural projection map
f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\colon M_{0}^{\prime}\to M_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}
. We can also construct a map
g^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\colon M_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}\to M_{0}^{\prime}
as follows: Given

we pick any

such that
f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(a^{\prime})=a
. Then we set
g^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(a):=\varepsilon a^{\prime}
, which by the above observation is independent of the choice of
a^{\prime}
. Note that
f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}g^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon
and
g^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon
, hence we have
M^{\prime}_{0}\approx_{\varepsilon}M^{\prime}_{0,\varepsilon}
. Going back to the almost world we now see that

\displaystyle\pi_{0}M_{0}^{\vee}\approx_{\varepsilon}M^{\prime a}_{0,\varepsilon},\qquad\text{with $M^{\prime a}_{0,\varepsilon}\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{n_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi$ a discrete submodule},

where we took the liberty of naming

. By
[
Man22

the discrete dualization functor

is

-exact on discrete

that also

is an isomorphism. Letting
M_{0,\varepsilon}:=((M^{\prime a}_{0,\varepsilon})^{\vee})_{\omega}
we deduce that
M_{0}\approx_{\varepsilon}M_{0,\varepsilon}
and there is a surjection
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi\mathrel{\twoheadrightarrow}M_{0,\varepsilon}
. As this works for all

is almost finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi
-module. Then it is also almost finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}
-module (where we equip
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}
with the discrete topology in this proof), hence by
[
Sch13

we have

\displaystyle M_{0}\approx(\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi)^{r}\oplus\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/I_{\gamma_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/I_{\gamma_{2}}\oplus\dots

for a series of real numbers

. Here for a real number

for all

\displaystyle\pi_{0}\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/I_{\gamma})=\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi,\qquad&\text{if $\gamma\geq 1/p$},\\

as

\gamma_{i}=0
, i.e. we have
M_{0}\approx(\mathcal{O}^{a}_{K}/\pi)^{r}
. It follows that
M_{0}
is dualizable: We need to check that the natural map
\alpha\colon M_{0}\otimes M_{0}^{\vee}\to\IHom(M_{0},M_{0})
is an isomorphism but since the same statement holds for

in place of

one deduces easily that both kernel and cokernel of

are killed by

and
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}_{K}
(cf. the proof of

Man22
, Lemma 3.7.17]
). In particular

is flat (which can also be seen directly), so that

and therefore the

-module structure on

restricts to a
\varphi
-module structure on
M_{0}
. Since the conditions on

are preserved under finite limits, we reduce the claimed dualizability of

to that of

, which satisfies the same assumptions as

is bounded, we can conclude by induction.
∎

As promised, here come the three equivalent ways of characterizing admissible representations on a classifying stack. The third characterization is very useful for showing that admissible representations are stable under various of the 6 operations, and plays a key role in our analysis of the Jacquet-Langlands functor.

Proposition 3.26
.

Let
C
be an algebraically closed perfectoid field with pseudouniformizer
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
such that

and let

be a locally profinite

(i)

M
is bounded and admissible.

(ii)

M
is bounded and for a basis of compact open subgroups

,

is dualizable in
\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}_{C}/\pi)
.

(iii)

Both
M
and
M^{\vee}
are bounded and discrete and the natural morphism

is an isomorphism.

If this is the case then
M^{\vee}
is bounded and admissible and this dual can unambiguously be computed in any of the following
\infty
-categories:
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
,
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
,
\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathcal{O}_{C}/\pi)^{\varphi}_{\omega}
,
\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathcal{O}_{C}/\pi)^{\varphi}

Proof.

Note first that (i) implies (ii) because

[
Man22
, Corollary 3.4.17]
). Conversely, assume that (ii) is satisfied. Then
M
is discrete because

, where on the right-hand side

ranges through all compact open subgroups of
G
(see
[
Man22
, Lemma 3.4.19]
). We claim that

lies in

, i.e. that the natural morphism

is an isomorphism. By the colimit property we just used the collection of functors

, where
H
ranges through a basis of compact open subgroups of
G
, is conservative, hence it is enough to show that
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,M^{\varphi}\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{C})=R\Gamma_{\mathrm{sm}}(H,M)
for all

in the basis. Noting that that

(which is formal) and with

. But by assumption

, Theorem 3.9.23]
). From the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence we also deduce that

is dualizable, hence

is admissible, as desired.

We have now finished the proof that (i) and (ii) are equivalent. To prove that they are also equivalent to (iii) we make the following computation. Let

,

,
\omega_{G}:=f^{!}\Lambda^{a}
and for every compact open subgroup

be the natural map. Then from

\displaystyle f_{H*}M^{\vee}

\displaystyle=f_{H*}\IHom(M,\Lambda^{a})=f_{H*}\IHom(M\otimes\omega_{G},\omega_{G})=\IHom(f_{H*}(M\otimes\omega_{G}),\Lambda^{a})

\displaystyle=(f_{H*}(M\otimes\omega_{G}))^{\vee}.

Note also that for small enough

preserves duals up to shift. Since the functors

are also conservative (for

varying in a basis of compact open subgroups of

) it follows that (iii) is equivalent to the claim that

and
M^{\vee}
are bounded and for a basis
H
of compact open subgroups of

we have that both

and

are discrete and the natural morphism
M_{H}\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}M_{H}^{\vee\vee}
is an isomorphism. By

this implies (ii). Moreover, if (ii) is satisfied then all the conditions in (iii) follow immediately except possibly that

is also bounded. To see that

(see
[

is

∎

4.
Duality and applications

4.1.
The main result

Fix a finite extension

, let
G:=\GL_{n}(F)
, let

be the central division algebra over

of invariant
1/n

be the completed algebraic closure of

\Spa C

\Spa C

for any locally profinite group

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\underline{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\underline{D^{\times}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

Here

is obtained from the infinite level Lubin-Tate tower by modding out the commuting actions of
G
and
D^{\times}
, so that in particular we have

denotes Drinfeld space. It follows that
f
and
g
are smooth and that
g
is proper. Recall also that for every locally profinite group
H
which has locally finite
p
-cohomological dimension (e.g.
H=G
or

) we have

\displaystyle\D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(B\underline{H},\mathbf{F}_{p})

\displaystyle=\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(H,\mathbf{F}_{p}),

\displaystyle\Dqcohri(\mathcal{O}^{+}_{B\underline{H}}/p)^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(H,\mathcal{O}_{C}/p)^{\varphi},

and the upper
\infty
-category embedds fully faithfully into the lower one via the Riemann-Hilbert functor. As explained in the introduction, we can now define the Jacquet-Langlands functor as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

In the above setup, we define the
p
-adic Jacquet-Langlands functors

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle\colon\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\to\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(D^{\times},\mathbf{F}_{p}),

\displaystyle A\mapsto g_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},*}f_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{*}A,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}

\displaystyle\colon\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathcal{O}_{C}/p)^{\varphi}\to\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(D^{\times},\mathcal{O}_{C}/p)^{\varphi},

\displaystyle A\mapsto g_{*}f^{*}A,

where we implicitly use the identifications of sheaves on the classifying stack with smooth representations discussed above.

The functor
\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}
is defined in the setting of a full 6-functor formalism and should thus be seen as the “actual” Jacquet-Langlands functor, whereas
\mathcal{J}
is a more naively defined shadow of
\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}
. In fact, it is completely formal that for all

we have

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(A)=(\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(A\otimes\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{B\underline{G}}/p))^{\varphi}.

In other words,
\mathcal{J}(A)
remembers the
\varphi
-invariants of the Jacquet-Langlands correspondent

of

. It turns out that if
A
is admissible then in fact these
\varphi
-invariants capture the whole Jacquet-Langlands correspondent. In fact, the Jacquet-Langlands correspondent of an admissible representation is itself admissible and hence lies in the image of the Riemann-Hilbert functor, as is shown in the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

In the above setup, let
A\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
be admissible. Then:

(i)

\mathcal{J}(A)\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(D^{\times},\mathbf{F}_{p})
is admissible.

(ii)

The natural primitive comparison map

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(A)\otimes\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{B\underline{D^{\times}}}/p\mathbin{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(A\otimes\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{B\underline{G}}/p)

induced by

is an isomorphism.

(iii)

There is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}\circ\mathcal{J})(A)=(\mathcal{J}\circ\mathcal{S}_{G})(A)[2n-2](n-1)

in
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(D^{\times},\mathbf{F}_{p})

Recall that Scholze already proved (i) and (ii). We will prove (i)–(iii) simultaneously, independently of Scholze’s results. In fact, we will see that all (i)–(iii) follow formally from the following unconditional duality for
\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}
:

Lemma 4.3
.

In the above setup, there is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle(-)^{\vee}\circ\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}=(\mathcal{J}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}\circ(-)^{\vee})[2n-2](n-1)

of functors
\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(G,\mathcal{O}_{C}/p)^{\varphi}\to\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(D^{\times},\mathcal{O}_{C}/p)^{\varphi}
.

Proof.

For every

we get the following chain of natural equivalences (using that

is proper and smooth and that

is smooth):

\displaystyle\IHom(g_{*}f^{*}A,\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{B\underline{D^{\times}}}/p)

\displaystyle=\IHom(g_{!}f^{*}A,\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{B\underline{D^{\times}}}/p)

\displaystyle=g_{*}\IHom(f^{*}A,g^{!}(\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{B\underline{D^{\times}}}/p))

\displaystyle=g_{*}\IHom(f^{*}A,\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{X}/p)[2n-2](n-1)

\displaystyle=g_{*}f^{*}\IHom(A,\mathcal{O}^{+a}_{B\underline{G}}/p)[2n-2](n-1).

In the last step we used that by the smoothness of

,

commutes with internal hom (see
[
Man22
, Proposition 3.8.7]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Fix an admissible

as in the claim and let us denote

the corresponding object in

Remark 4.4
.

In general, both sides of the duality isomorphism are somewhat intractible. However, in certain favorable situations it reduces to a more concrete statement. We record one useful instance of this in the following proposition. Recall from Proposition
3.7
that for any admissible smooth
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
, the derived dual
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{\dim_{G}\pi}(\pi)
is always nonzero. Following
[
Koh17
]
, we say
\pi
is Cohen-Macaulay if
\mathcal{S}_{G}^{i}(\pi)=0
for all
i\neq\dim_{G}\pi
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
be an admissible representation which is Cohen-Macaulay, and such that there is an integer
c
with
\mathcal{J}^{i}(\pi)=0
for all
i\neq c
. Then there are isomorphisms

\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}^{i}(\mathcal{J}^{c}(\pi))\simeq\mathcal{J}^{i+2n-2-c-\dim_{G}\pi}(\check{\pi})

for all
i
, where
\check{\pi}=\mathcal{S}_{G}^{\dim_{G}\pi}(\pi)
is the unique nonzero derived dual of
\pi
.

Proof.

Evaluate both sides of the duality isomorphism
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}\circ\mathcal{J})\cong(\mathcal{J}\circ\mathcal{S}_{G})[2n-2](n-1)
on
\pi
. Since
\mathcal{J}(\pi)=\mathcal{J}^{c}(\pi)[-c]
by assumption, the left-hand side evaluates to
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}(\mathcal{J}^{c}(\pi)[-c])\cong\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}(\mathcal{J}^{c}(\pi))[c]
. Next, since
\mathcal{S}_{G}(\pi)=\check{\pi}[-\dim_{G}\pi]
by assumption, the right-hand side simplifies to
\mathcal{J}(\check{\pi}[-\dim_{G}\pi])[2n-2](n-1)\cong\mathcal{J}(\check{\pi})[2n-2-\dim_{G}\pi](n-1)
. Equating both sides and rearranging the shifts, we get
\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}(\mathcal{J}^{c}(\pi))=\mathcal{J}(\check{\pi})[2n-2-\dim_{G}\pi-c](n-1)
. Passing to cohomology on both sides (and ignoring the Tate twists), we get the result.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

We explain how to show a variant of our duality isomorphism in the setting of
\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}
or
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-coefficients. We explain the first case; the second follows from a limit argument. First some preliminary observations. Let
H
be any
p
-adic Lie group. One easily checks that Proposition
3.9
extends to the setting of
\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}
-coefficients, yielding a symmetric monoidal equivalence
\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(H,\mathbf{Z}/p^{n})\cong\D_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}(B\underline{H},\mathbf{Z}/p^{n})
. On these categories we again have a duality functor
\mathcal{S}_{H}(-)
, given by internal hom towards the monoidal unit. We say
A\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(H,\mathbf{Z}/p^{n})
is admissible if
R\Gamma_{{\mathrm{sm}}}(H,A)
is a perfect complex of
\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}
-modules for a basis of open subgroups
H
. Admissible complexes are automatically bounded, and are preserved by duality and
-\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}}^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}
, and the latter functor is conservative on admissible complexes. Conversely, if
A
is given and
A\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}}^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}\in\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(H,\mathbf{F}_{p})
is admissible, then
A
is necessarily admissible.

With this in mind, we
define

\mathcal{J}:\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(G,\mathbf{Z}/p^{n})\to\D^{\mathrm{sm}}(D^{\times},\mathbf{Z}/p^{n})
as
\mathcal{J}=g_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\ast}f^{\ast}_{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}
. This is compatible with the
\mathbf{F}_{p}
version of
\mathcal{J}
via
-\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}}^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}
. Using this observation and the final statement in the previous paragraph together with the finiteness properties of
\mathcal{J}
with
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-coefficients, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{J}
preserves admissibility in this setting. Now, the key observation is that there is a natural map
\mathcal{J}(A)\otimes\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}_{G}(A))\to\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}[2-2n](1-n)
, defined as the composition

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(A)\otimes\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}_{G}(A))

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{J}(A\otimes\mathcal{S}_{G}(A))

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{Z}/p^{n})

\displaystyle\to\tau^{\geq 2n-2}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{Z}/p^{n})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}[2-2n](1-n).

Here the first arrow is obtained from the lax monoidal structure of
\mathcal{J}
, the second arrow is induced by the evaluation map
A\otimes\mathcal{S}_{G}(A)\to\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}
, and the final isomorphism is induced by the usual computation of the étale cohomology of
\mathbf{P}^{n-1}
. By adjunction (and rearranging the shifts and twists), this map induces a map
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}_{G}(A))[2n-2](n-1)\to\mathcal{S}_{D^{\times}}(\mathcal{J}(A))
. When
A
is admissible, the source and target of this map are also admissible, so to see it is an isomorphism, it suffices to show this after applying
-\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}/p^{n}}^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}
. But all operations here are compatible with reduction mod
p
, so we now conclude by invoking the duality isomorphism with
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-coefficients.

\displaystyle=R\Gamma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(X_{n,d},r^{*}s^{*}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})),

where the
R\Gamma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}
on the last line denotes the
D_{1/n}^{\times}
-equivariant cohomology. To finish the proof it remains to identify the
D_{1/n}^{\times}
-equivariant sheaf
r^{*}s^{*}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})
on
X_{n,d}
. After forgetting the
D_{1/n}^{\times}
-equivariance of the sheaf, i.e. after applying
\tilde{\alpha}^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}^{*}r^{*}s^{*}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})

\displaystyle=(s\circ r\circ\tilde{\alpha})^{*}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})=((h_{1}\circ\tilde{g}_{d})\times(f_{d}\circ\tilde{h}_{2}))^{*}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}_{d}^{\ast}h_{1}^{\ast}M_{1}\otimes\tilde{h}_{2}^{\ast}f_{d}^{\ast}M_{2},

using that
s\circ r\circ\tilde{\alpha}=(h_{1}\circ\tilde{g}_{d})\times(f_{d}\circ\tilde{h}_{2})
. This gives the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

By some straightforward manipulations with the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, we easily reduce to proving the solid version of the theorem. In other words, we need to show that for any
M_{1}\in\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n-d}(F),\mathcal{O}_{C}/p)^{\varphi}
and
M_{2}\in\D^{{\mathrm{sm}},a}_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F),\mathcal{O}_{C}/p)^{\varphi}
, there is a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{J}_{n,{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n-d,d}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2}))\cong R\Gamma_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(W_{n,d},h_{1}^{\ast}M_{1}\otimes h_{2}^{\ast}\mathcal{J}_{d,{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(M_{2})).

By the previous lemma, we already have a canonical isomorphism

\mathcal{J}_{n,{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n-d,d}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2}))\cong R\Gamma_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(X_{n,d},\tilde{g}_{d}^{\ast}h_{1}^{\ast}M_{1}\otimes\tilde{h}_{2}^{\ast}f_{d}^{\ast}M_{2}).

We then compute that

\displaystyle R\Gamma_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(X_{n,d},\tilde{g}_{d}^{\ast}h_{1}^{\ast}M_{1}\otimes\tilde{h}_{2}^{\ast}f_{d}^{\ast}M_{2})

\displaystyle\cong R\Gamma_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(W_{n,d},\tilde{g}_{d\ast}(\tilde{g}_{d}^{\ast}h_{1}^{\ast}M_{1}\otimes\tilde{h}_{2}^{\ast}f_{d}^{\ast}M_{2}))

\displaystyle\cong R\Gamma_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(W_{n,d},h_{1}^{\ast}M_{1}\otimes\tilde{g}_{d\ast}\tilde{h}_{2}^{\ast}f_{d}^{\ast}M_{2}))

\displaystyle\cong R\Gamma_{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(W_{n,d},h_{1}^{\ast}M_{1}\otimes h_{2}^{\ast}g_{d\ast}f_{d}^{\ast}M_{2})).

Here the first isomorphism is trivial, the second follows from the projection formula, and the third follows from proper base change. Since
g_{d\ast}f_{d}^{\ast}M_{2}=\mathcal{J}_{d,{\scalebox{0.5}{$\square$}}}(M_{2})
by definition, this gives the desired result.
∎

By work of Johansson-Ludwig
[
JLH21
, Theorem A]
,
W_{n,1}
is a perfectoid space for all
n
. The proof of
[
JLH21
, Theorem A]
is a highly nontrivial argument using the
p
-adic geometry of global Shimura varieties. In the special case
n=2
, the first author reproved this result by purely local methods
[
JLH21
, §A.4]
. However, the arguments in
[
JLH21
, §A.4]
easily generalize to show that
W_{n,n-1}
is also a perfectoid space for all
n
. It thus seems natural to make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.4
.

For all integers
0<d<n
,
W_{n,d}
is a perfectoid space.

This conjecture implies a vanishing result.

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose
W_{n,d}
is a perfectoid space. Then for any admissible smooth representations
\sigma_{1}\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n-d}(F))
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
,
\mathcal{J}_{n}^{i}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n-d,d}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}))=0
for all
i>d+n-2
.

Note that
\mathcal{J}_{n}^{i}
trivially vanishes in degrees
>2n-2
, while this result gives vanishing in degrees
>d+n-2=2n-2-(n-d)
, so we improve on the trivial bound by an interval of length
n-d
. When
d=1
, this specializes to the main vanishing theorem in
[
JLH21
]
(using of course the perfectoidness of
W_{n,1}
proved in loc. cit.).

Proof.

By the primitive comparison theorem plus some small additional arguments, we get an almost isomorphism

\mathcal{J}_{n}^{i}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n-d,d}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}/p\cong H^{i}(W_{n,d},h_{1}^{\ast}\sigma_{1}\otimes h_{2}^{\ast}\mathcal{J}_{d}(\sigma_{2})\otimes\mathcal{O}^{+}/p).

It thus suffices to show that
H^{i}(W_{n,d},h_{1}^{\ast}\sigma_{1}\otimes h_{2}^{\ast}\mathcal{J}_{d}(\sigma_{2})\otimes\mathcal{O}^{+}/p)
is almost zero in the claimed range of degrees. Recall that
\mathcal{J}_{d}(\sigma_{2})
is concentrated in degrees
\leq 2d-2
. Note also that
W_{n,d}
is perfectoid by assumption, so the functor
H^{i}(W_{n,d},-\otimes\mathcal{O}^{+}/p)
on overconvergent étale
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-sheaves is almost zero for all
i>\dim W_{n,d}=n-d
, by the usual almost equality of étale and analytic cohomology. The evident spectral sequence then gives almost vanishing in degrees
>2d-2+n-d=d+n-2
, whence the claim.
∎

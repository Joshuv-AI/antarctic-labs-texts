Simple functional equations for generalized Selberg zeta functions with Tate motives
Shin-ya Koyama & Nobushige Kurokawa

Abstract
We prove that for a compact locally symmetric Riemannian space M M of rank 1 there exist infinitely many automorphic Tate motives f f such that the generalized Selberg zeta function Z M ⁡ ( f ) ​ ( s ) Z_{M(f)}(s) satisfies a simple functional equation in the sense that it has no gamma factors.



Key Words: Selberg zeta functions, functional equations, Tate motives

AMS Subject Classifications: 11M06, 11M41, 11F72

Introduction

Let

Z_{M}(s)=\prod_{P\in\mathrm{Prim}(M)}\prod_{\lambda}\left(1-N(P)^{-s-\lambda}\right),

be the Selberg zeta function of a compact locally symmetric Riemannian space
M
of rank 1,
where
\mathrm{Prim}(M)
denotes the set of primitive closed geodesics on
M
with

N(P)=\exp(\mathrm{length}(P)).

It is known that
Z_{M}(s)
has analytic continuation to all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
with a functional equation

Z_{M}(2\rho_{M}-s)=Z_{M}(s)S_{M}(s),

where

S_{M}(s)=\exp\left(\mathrm{vol}(M)\int_{0}^{s-\rho_{M}}\mu_{M}(it)dt\right).

Here
\mu_{M}(t)
is the Plancherel measure and
2\rho_{M},\ \mathrm{vol}(M)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

Actually, the “gamma factor”
S_{M}(s)
can be described explicitly in terms of the multiple sine function
(or the multiple gamma function) as in
[
1
]
; see §1 of the text.
In this paper we tacitly assume that the dimension
\mathrm{dim}(M)
is even
since otherwise (that is the case of
M=\Gamma\backslash SO(1,2n+1)/K
)
the factor
S_{M}(s)
is a simple exponential function.

Now, we introduce the action of
f(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]
on
Z_{M}(s)
as the
“(absolute) Tate motif” (see Manin
[
5
]
).
More concretely, for a Laurent polynomial

f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)x^{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}],

we define

Z_{M(f)}(s)=\prod_{k}Z_{M}(s-k)^{a(k)},

which is considered as a generalized Selberg zeta function.

The purpose of this paper is to prove the following result.

Theorem

Let
M
be a compact locally symmetric Riemannian space of rank 1 as above.
Then
Z_{M(f)}(s)
has simple functional equations

Z_{M(f)}(D+2\rho_{M}-s)^{C}=Z_{M(f)}(s)

for infinitely many automorphic
f
.
Here the automorphy of
f
means that

f(x^{-1})=Cx^{-D}f(x)

with
C=\pm 1
and
D\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Our proof shows that the functional equation of
Z_{M(f)}(s)
for an automorphic

f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)x^{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}],

is given as

Z_{M(f)}(D+2\rho_{M}-s)^{C}=Z_{M(f)}(s)S_{M(f)}(s)

where

S_{M(f)}(s)=\prod_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}S_{M}(s-k)^{a(k)}

in general: see Theorem 2 in §2.
Hence the crucial point is to show that

S_{M(f)}(s)=1

for infinitely many
f
. Thus we do not need any “gamma factors” in such cases.
For more explicit construction of such
f
see Theorem 3 in §3.

We notice that the original Selberg zeta function
Z_{M}(s)
was studied by Selberg
[
6
,
7
]

for a compact Riemann surface
M
of genus
g\geq 2
.
It is written as

Z_{M}(s)=\prod_{P\in\mathrm{Prim}(M)}\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1-N(P)^{-s-n}\right).

The functional equation is

Z_{M}(1-s)=Z_{M}(s)S_{M}(s)

with

\displaystyle S_{M}(s)

\displaystyle=\exp\left((4-4g)\int_{0}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\pi t\tan(\pi t)dt\right)

\displaystyle=(S_{2}(s)S_{2}(s+1))^{2-2g},

where

S_{r}(s)=S_{r}(s,(1,\cdots,1))

is the multiple sine function in
[
1
]
.
We refer to the paper
[
2
]
concerning this case.

The gamma factor

We recall the explicit calculation of
S_{M}(s)
by multiple sine functions and multiple gamma functions in
[

according to the classification of
M=\Gamma\backslash G/K
.

Theorem 1

(
[
1

)
.

The factors
S_{M}(s)
are written explicitly as follows.

(1)

S_{M}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(S_{2n}(s)S_{2n}(s+1)\right)^{\mathrm{vol}(M)(-1)^{\frac{\dim(M)}{2}}}&G=SO(1,2n)\\
\left(\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n}S_{2n}(s+k)^{\binom{n}{k}^{2}}\right)^{\mathrm{vol}(M)(-1)^{\frac{\dim(M)}{2}}}&G=SU(1,n)\\
\left(\prod\limits_{k=0}^{2n-1}S_{4n}(s+k)^{\frac{1}{2n}\binom{2n}{k}\binom{2n}{k+1}}\right)^{\mathrm{vol}(M)}&G=Sp(1,n)\\
(S_{16}(s)S_{16}(s+1)^{10}S_{16}(s+2)^{28}&\\
\quad\times S_{16}(s+3)^{28}S_{16}(s+4)^{10}S_{16}(s+5))^{\mathrm{vol}(M)}&G=F_{4}.\end{array}\right.

(2)

(\Gamma_{16}(s)\Gamma_{16}(s+1)^{10}\Gamma_{16}(s+2)^{28}&\\
\quad\times\Gamma_{16}(s+3)^{28}\Gamma_{16}(s+4)^{10}\Gamma_{16}(s+5))^{-\mathrm{vol}(M)}&G=F_{4}.\end{array}\right.

M=\Gamma\backslash SO(1,2)/K

2
Generalized functional equations

We show the functional equation for
Z_{M(f)}(s)
with automorphic Tate motif
f(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]

satisfying

f(x^{-1})=Cx^{-D}f(x).

Theorem 2
.

For each automorphic
f
we have the functional equation

Z_{M(f)}(D+2\rho_{M}-s)^{C}=Z_{M(f)}(s)S_{M(f)}(s).

Proof.

First we remark that the following equivalence is easily seen:

f(x^{-1})=Cx^{-D}f(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a(D-k)=Ca(k)\quad(\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

Then we see that

\displaystyle Z_{M(f)}(D+2\rho_{M}-s)^{C}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k}Z_{M}(D+2\rho_{M}-s-k)^{Ca(k)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k}Z_{M}(2\rho_{M}-s+(D-k))^{a(D-k)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k}Z_{M}(2\rho_{M}-(s-k))^{a(k)},

where we replaced
k
by
D-k
. Thus the functional equation for
Z_{M}(s)
gives

\displaystyle Z_{M(f)}(D+2\rho_{M}-s)^{C}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k}(Z_{M}(s-k)S_{M}(s-k))^{a(k)}

\displaystyle=Z_{M(f)}(s)S_{M(f)}(s).

∎

3
Vanishment of gamma factors

Now we prove the main result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f(x)\in(x-1)^{\dim(M)}{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]
be automorphic. Then it holds that

S_{M(f)}(s)=1.

Namely, we have

Z_{M(f)}(D+2\rho_{M}-s)^{C}=Z_{M(f)}(s)

for such
f
.

Proof.

We denote by
S_{r}^{f}(s)
the action of
f(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]
on
S_{r}(x)
.
We remark that

S_{r}^{fg}=(S_{r}^{f})^{g}.

In fact for

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}a(k)x^{k},

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}b(l)x^{l}

it holds that

f(x)g(x)=\sum_{k,l}a(k)b(l)x^{k+l}

and

S_{r}^{fg}(s)=\prod_{k,l}S_{r}(s-(k+l))^{a(k)b(l)}.

On the other hand it follows that

S_{r}^{f}(s)=\prod_{k}S_{r}(s-k)^{a(k)}

and that

\displaystyle(S_{r}^{f})^{g}(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{l}\left(\prod_{k}S_{r}((s-l)-k)^{a(k)}\right)^{b(l)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k,l}S_{r}(s-(k+l))^{a(k)b(l)}.

Thus
S_{r}^{fg}=(S_{r}^{f})^{g}
.

Let
r=\dim(M)
and put

f_{r}(x)=(1-x^{-1})^{r}.

We show that

S_{r}^{f_{r}}(s)=-1.

The case
r=1
is easy:

S_{1}^{f_{1}}(s)=\frac{S_{1}(s)}{S_{1}(s+1)}=\frac{2\sin(\pi s)}{2\sin(\pi(s+1))}=-1.

In general, the relation (see
[
1
]
)

S_{r}^{(1-x^{-1})}(s)=\frac{S_{r}(s)}{S_{r}(s+1)}=S_{r-1}(s)

gives

S_{r}^{f_{r}}(s)=S_{r-1}^{f_{r-1}}(s)=\cdots=S_{1}^{f_{1}}(s)=-1

Let

f(x)\in(x-1)^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]

and

f(x)=f_{r}(x)g(x).

Then we have

S_{r}^{f}(s)=\pm 1.

Actually,

S_{r}^{f}(s)=S_{r}^{f_{r}g}(s)=(S_{r}^{f_{r}})^{g}(s)=(-1)^{g(1)}=\pm 1.

Thus by the explicit formula for
S_{M}(s)
in §1, we see that

S_{M(f)}(s)=1.

∎

Remark.

The above arguments give the implication

\displaystyle f(1)=f^{\prime}(1)=\cdots=f^{(r-1)}(1)=0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle S_{r}^{f}(s)=\pm 1.

We refer to
[
4
,
3
]
concerning the converse and generalizations.

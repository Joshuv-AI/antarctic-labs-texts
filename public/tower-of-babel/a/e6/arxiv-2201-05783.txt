Abstract
A strict bramble of a graph G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G is a collection of pairwise-intersecting connected subgraphs of G . \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G. The order of a strict bramble ℬ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal B} is the minimum size of a set of vertices intersecting all sets of ℬ . \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal B}. The strict bramble number of G , \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G, denoted by 𝗌𝖻𝗇 ⁡ ( G ) , \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(G), is the maximum order of a strict bramble in G . \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G. The strict bramble number of G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G can be seen as a way to extend the notion of acyclicity, departing from the fact that (non-empty) acyclic graphs are exactly the graphs where every strict bramble has order one. We initiate the study of this graph parameter by providing three alternative definitions, each revealing different structural characteristics. The first is a min-max theorem asserting that 𝗌𝖻𝗇 ⁡ ( G ) \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(G) is equal to the minimum k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k for which G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G is a minor of the lexicographic product of a tree and a clique on k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k vertices (also known as the lexicographic tree product number ). The second characterization is in terms of a new variant of a tree decomposition called lenient tree decomposition . We prove that 𝗌𝖻𝗇 ⁡ ( G ) \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(G) is equal to the minimum k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k for which there exists a lenient tree decomposition of G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G of width at most k . \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k. The third characterization is in terms of extremal graphs. For this, we define, for each k , \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k, the concept of a k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k -domino-tree and we prove that every edge - maximal graph of strict bramble number at most k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k is a k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k -domino-tree. We also identify three graphs that constitute the minor-obstruction set of the class of graphs with strict bramble number at most two. We complete our results by proving that, given some G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G and k , \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k, deciding whether 𝗌𝖻𝗇 ⁡ ( G ) ≤ k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(G)\leq k is an NP -complete problem.



Keywords:
Strict bramble, Bramble, Treewidth, Lexicographic tree product number, Obstruction set, Tree decomposition, Lenient tree decomposition.

Lexicographic tree product.

Let

in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G\cdot H

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}w

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u=w
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}v
is adjacent with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}z

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H.

Lenient tree decompositions.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
a tree and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi
be a function mapping vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
to vertex sets of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
We say that two vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
are
close
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
if either they
are identical or they are adjacent.
The pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is a
lenient tree decomposition
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
if it satisfies the following three conditions:

(C1)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\bigcup_{t\in V(T)}\textchi(t)
is the vertex set of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
;

(C2)

for every edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}e
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
there are two close vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
such that,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}e\subseteq\textchi(t)\cup\textchi(t^{\prime})
;

(C3)

for every vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{t\mid x\in\textchi(t)\}
is connected in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.

We define the
width
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi),
as the maximum size of a set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.

Given a non-negative integer

a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K_{r},

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G

Integers, sets, and tuples.

We denote by

For a set

Graphs.

All graphs in this paper are simple, i.e. they are finite and they do not have multiple edges or loops. Given a graph

Given an

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G[S]=\big(S,{S\choose 2}\cap E(G)\big).

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}z\in V(P),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}z

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf deg}_{P}(z)=2,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}z

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P.
We call two paths
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(P)\cap V(Q)=\emptyset

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(P)\cap V(Q)
contains only terminal vertices. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}X,Y\subseteq V(G).

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}X\text{-}Y

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\text{-}y

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in Y.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}X,Y\subseteq V(G).

Lemma 1
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal B}\subseteq 2^{V(G)}
be a strict bramble of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Also let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}X,Y\subseteq V(G)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal B}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(X,Y)
-separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
also covers
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal B}.

Proof.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B\in{\cal B}.
Recall that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B
is connected and by definition, intersects both
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}X,Y.
Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(X,Y)
-separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S.

∎

Chordal graphs.

A graph is
chordal
if all its induced cycles are triangles. The next proposition contains some
folklore observations regarding separators in chordal graphs.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a chordal graph and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in V(G).
Any minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
induces a clique. Given a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
there exist vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x^{\prime}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y^{\prime}
in the same connected components of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G-S
as
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y
respectively, such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x^{\prime}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y^{\prime}
are adjacent to every vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S.
If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,S^{\prime}
are minimal separators of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}
is contained in some augmented connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G-S
and vice versa.

Minors.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H,G
be graphs.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and we write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H\leq_{\sf m}G
if we can obtain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
through a sequence of vertex deletions, edge deletions and edge contractions. A
minor-model of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textmu:V(H)\to 2^{V(G)}
that satisfies the following properties:

1.

for all
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u\in V(H),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textmu(u)
is connected;

2.

for any pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u,v\in V(H),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textmu(u)\cap\textmu(v)=\emptyset
;

3.

if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{u,v\}\in E(H)
then there exists an edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{w,z\}\in E(G)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}w\in\textmu(u)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}z\in\textmu(v).

It is well-known that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
if and only if there exists a minor model of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.

Tree decompositions.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
a tree and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi:V(T)\to 2^{V(G)}
be a function mapping vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
to subsets of vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
The pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is a
tree decomposition
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
if it satisfies the following three conditions:

(C1)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\bigcup_{t\in V(T)}\textchi(t)=V(G)
;

(C2)

for every edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}e\in E(G),
there is a vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T),
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}e\subseteq\textchi(t)
;

(C3)

for every vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in V(G),
the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{t\mid x\in\textchi(t)\}
is connected in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.

We refer to the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
as
the nodes
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
and to their images as
the bags
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi).
Given a vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in V(G),
we define its
trace
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
as the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Trace}_{(T,\textchi)}(x)=\{t\in V(G)\mid x\in\textchi(t)\}.
When
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is clear from the context, we simply write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Trace}(x).
Also for any leaf node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)
with (the unique) incident edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{t,t^{\prime}\}\in E(T),
we define the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
-
petal
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi),
as
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Petal}_{(T,\textchi)}(t)=\textchi(t)\setminus\textchi(t^{\prime}),
i.e. the private vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t.
When
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is clear from the context, we simply write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Petal}(t).
We define the
width
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
as the maximum size of a bag of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
minus one. The
treewidth
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf tw}(G),
is the minimum width over all the tree decompositions of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.

3
Lenient tree decompositions

In this section we provide some new concepts and we prove a series of preliminary results on lenient tree decompositions that will be useful for the proof of our main min-max equivalence Theorem in
Section 5
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
The definitions of nodes, bags and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
-petals of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi),
as well as the trace of a vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
are identical to those we gave in the case of tree decompositions. We start with the following easy observation that follows directly from the definition of a lenient tree decomposition.

Observation 3
.

If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
are two graphs where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H\leq_{\sf m}G
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
has a lenient tree decomposition of width at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k,
then so does
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Consider an internal node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
such that there exist different vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in V(G),
such that,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Trace}(x)
is a subtree of a subtree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T-t
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Trace}(y)
is a subtree of a different subtree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T-t.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.

Proof.

Fix a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\mbox{-}y
path
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and consider the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}U=\bigcup_{u\in P}{\sf Trace}(u).
Conditions
(C2)
and
(C3)
imply that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}U
is connected in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
So
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in U,
since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
is in the unique path of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
connecting the two subtrees. Thus there exists a vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P
intersecting
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)
which implies that it is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K\subseteq G
be an induced clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Then there exists an adjacent pair of nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime}\in V(T)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K)\subseteq\textchi(t)\cup\textchi(t^{\prime}).

Proof.

If there is a bag of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
that contains
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K)
we are done. Assume otherwise. The proof proceeds by orienting every node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)
towards the subtree containing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K)\setminus\textchi(t).
By our assumption every leaf of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
points towards the inner part of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
Consider an internal node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T).
By condition
(C3)
, for every vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in V(K)\setminus\textchi(t),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Trace}(x)
is exclusively contained in some subtree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T-t.
Then, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K
is a clique, because of
Section 3
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\bigcup_{x\in V(K)\setminus\textchi(t)}{\sf Trace}(x)
is exclusively contained in the same subtree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T-t.
This implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
can only point in a single direction and also that adjacent nodes of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
cannot both point outward. Consider a maximal directed path that respects the directions given to the nodes of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi),
which terminates at node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T).
Then there is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime}\in V(T),
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{t,t^{\prime}\}\in E(T),
pointing towards
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K)\subseteq\textchi(t)\cup\textchi(t^{\prime}).

∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(T)|\geq 3.
Also let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G)
be connected. Then for any three nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in V(T)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime}
is an internal node of the unique
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\text{-}t^{\prime\prime}
path
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T,
if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\cap S\neq\emptyset
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime\prime})\cap S\neq\emptyset
then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime})\cap S\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\textchi(t)\cap S,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in\textchi(t^{\prime\prime})\cap S.
If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x=y
then by condition
(C3)
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\textchi(t^{\prime}).
Else if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y
are adjacent then by condition
(C2)
, there exists a pair of close nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u,v\in V(T)
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{x,y\}\subseteq\textchi(u)\cup\textchi(v).
By condition
(C3)
, we can assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u,v\in V(P)
and thus
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P\subseteq{\sf Trace}(x)\cup{\sf Trace}(y),
which implies that either
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\textchi(t^{\prime})
or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in\textchi(t^{\prime}).
Else if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y
are not adjacent, then, by
Section 3
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime})
is an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator, and since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is connected it must intersect
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime}).

∎

Extreme lenient tree decompositions.

We call a lenient tree decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
of width
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k

extreme
, if it satisfies the following properties.

•

All bags of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
are of equal size.

•

No bag of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is a subset of another one.

•

For any node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T),
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf deg}_{T}(t)=2,
and for any two nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in V(T)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(P),
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P
is the unique
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime}\text{-}t^{\prime\prime}
path in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T,
it holds that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\nsubseteq\textchi(t^{\prime})\cup\textchi(t^{\prime\prime}).

•

For every pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in V(T)
of different leaf neighbours of a node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|{\sf Petal}(t^{\prime})\cup{\sf Petal}(t^{\prime\prime})|>k.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph. If there exists a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
of width
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k,
then there exists an extreme lenient tree decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
of the same width.

Proof.

Define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
as the lenient tree decomposition of width
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k,
that minimizes:

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle k|V(T)|-\sum_{t\in V(T)}|\textchi(t)|

(2)

and subject to (
2
), minimizes:

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle|V(T)|.

(3)

We prove that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is extreme. If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(T)|\leq 1,
then our claim trivially holds. Now, assume it is not. We distinguish cases.

Suppose that there exists a pair of bags of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
with unequal size. Then there is also a pair of adjacent nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime}\in V(T)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi(t)|<|\textchi(t^{\prime})|.
Then we can add vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime})\setminus\textchi(t)
to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t).
This contradicts the minimality of (
2
).

Now, suppose that there exists a bag that is a subset of another bag of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi).
Then, because of condition
(C3)
, there is also a pair of adjacent nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime}\in V(T)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\subseteq\textchi(t^{\prime}).
Then we can remove node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
and connect its neighbours with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime}.
This contradicts the minimality of (
2
).

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf deg}_{T}(t)=2
and a pair of nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in V(T),
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(P),
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P
is the unique
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime}\text{-}t^{\prime\prime}
path in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
Suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\subseteq\textchi(t^{\prime})\cup\textchi(t^{\prime\prime}).
Then we can remove
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,
and make
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}
adjacent. This contradicts the minimality of (
3
).

Finally, suppose that there is a node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T),
such that there exists a pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in V(T)
of different leaf neighbours of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|{\sf Petal}(t^{\prime})\cup{\sf Petal}(t^{\prime\prime})|\leq k.
Then we can identify the two leaves into a single leaf, whose bag will contain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Petal}(t^{\prime})\cup{\sf Petal}(t^{\prime\prime}),
plus some additional vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\setminus({\sf Petal}(t^{\prime})\cup{\sf Petal}(t^{\prime\prime})),
to ensure that the bag corresponding to the new leaf has size
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi(t)|.
This contradicts the minimality of (
3
).
∎

Given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and a lenient tree decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
we define the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
-completion
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
as the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G^{+}=(V(G),E^{+})
where,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle E^{+}=\bigcup_{{t,t^{\prime}\in V(T):}\atop{\text{$\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime}$ are close in $\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T$}}}{\textchi(t)\cup\textchi(t^{\prime})\choose 2}.

That is, we add edges (if they do not already exist) between all vertices of each bag or of each two “neighboring” bags.
Clearly,

Lemma 8
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Also let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G^{+}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
-completion of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G^{+}
is chordal.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in V(G)
of non-adjacent vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G^{+}
that belong to an induced cycle of size at least four. Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y
are not adjacent and the union of the bags of any adjacent pair of nodes of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
induces a clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G^{+},
the closest nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,t^{\prime}\in V(T),
whose bags contain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y
respectively are at distance at least two in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t^{\prime\prime}\in V(T)
be an internal vertex of a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\text{-}t^{\prime}
path in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
Because of
Section 3
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime\prime})
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G^{+}.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P,P^{\prime}\subseteq G^{+}
be the two internally vertex disjoint
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\text{-}y
paths that define this cycle. These paths intersect
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime\prime})
and since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t^{\prime\prime})
is a clique, the cycle has two non-consecutive vertices that are adjacent, which contradicts our assumption. Thus
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G^{+}
is chordal.
∎

.

Lemma 10
.

Amalgamations of lenient tree decompositions.

Our min-max theorem (
Section 5
) is using the technique of Bellenbaum and Diestel
[
5
]
for proving the equivalence between the bramble number (that is a max-min parameter) and treewidth (that is a min-max parameter). An important ingredient of the proof of Bellenbaum and Diestel
[
5
]
is the concept of
amalgamating tree decompositions
. We next adapt it to lenient tree decompositions.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C\in{\sf Acc}(G,S)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{-}=C-S.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s\in V(T)
be a node of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
and for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in S,
let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z_{x}\subseteq T
be an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s\text{-}{\sf Trace}(x)
path in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
Then we define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi^{\prime})
so that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)
we set,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle\textchi^{\prime}(t)=(\textchi(t)\cap V(C^{-}))\cup\{x\in S\mid t\in V(Z_{x})\}.

It is easy to observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi^{\prime})
is a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C,
where we force
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq\textchi^{\prime}(s)
while we fix condition
(C3)
of the definition with the necessary addition of vertices to every bag of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi).
We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi^{\prime})
is an
amalgamated restriction of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
with respect to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G).
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal T}=\{(T_{C},\textchi_{C})\mid C\in{\sf Acc}(G,S))\}
be a family, where for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C\in{\sf Acc}(G,S),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T_{C},\textchi_{C})
is a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
with a node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s_{C}\in V(T_{C})
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq\textchi_{C}(s_{C}).
We build from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal T},
a lenient tree decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
as follows.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
is obtained by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\bigcup_{C\in{\sf Acc}(G,C)}T_{C}
after adding a new node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t_{\rm new}
and for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T_{C},\textchi_{C})\in{\cal T},
making
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t_{\rm new}
adjacent with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s_{C}.
We finally define,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle\textchi^{\prime}=\{(t_{\rm new},S)\}\cup\bigcup_{C\in{\sf Acc}(G,C)}\textchi_{C}.

Observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
We call it the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
-
amalgamation
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal T}.
By the above construction we observe the following.

Observation 12
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\in{\mathbb{N}}.
If every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T_{C},\textchi_{C})\in{\cal T}
has width at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k,
then the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
-amalgamation of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal T}
also has width at most

]
, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G)
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C\in{\sf Acc}(G,S).
Also let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s\in V(T),
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T^{\prime},\textchi^{\prime})
be the amalgamated restriction of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
with respect to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s.
Also, suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G-(V(C)\setminus S)
contains a set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{P_{x}\mid x\in S\}
of disjoint
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\text{-}\textchi(s)
paths where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
is a terminal vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P_{x}.
Then for every node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi^{\prime}(t)|\leq|\textchi(t)|.

Proof.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)
be a node such that there is an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\textchi^{\prime}(t)\setminus\textchi(t).
By definition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi^{\prime}(t),
we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in S.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z_{x}\subseteq T
be an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s\text{-}{\sf Trace}(x)
path in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
By definition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi^{\prime}(t),
we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(Z_{x}).
Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\notin\textchi(t),
then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\notin{\sf Trace}(x),
which implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z_{x}
has length at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}1.
Moreover, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t=s,
then trivially
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(s)
contains the terminal of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P_{x}
that belongs in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(s).
Thus, we can assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
is an internal node of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y
be the terminal vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P_{x}
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(s).
Note that, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P_{x}
has length
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}1,
then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y
are adjacent and by condition
(C2)
, there exists a pair of close nodes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u,v\in V(T)
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\{x,y\}\subseteq\textchi(u)\cup\textchi(v).
By condition
(C3)
, we can assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u,v\in V(Z_{x})
and thus
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z_{x}\subseteq{\sf Trace}(x)\cup{\sf Trace}(y),
which, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\notin\textchi(t),
implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in\textchi(t).
Now, assume that,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P_{x}
has length at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2
and that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y
are not adjacent. Then, because of
Section 3
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)
is an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator, and hence contains some other distinct vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P_{x}.
Note that under the assumption that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\textchi^{\prime}(t)\setminus\textchi(t),
any such vertex is not contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi^{\prime}(t),
since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi^{\prime}(t)\subseteq V(C)
while
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(P_{x})\setminus\{x\}\subseteq V(G)\setminus V(C).
Thus
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi^{\prime}(t)|\leq|\textchi(t)|.

4
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-trees

Recall that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-trees serve as the edge-extremal graphs of graphs of bounded bramble number (via the equivalence with treewidth). In this section we define the concept of a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree
that
is the corresponding extremal structure for the strict brambles. As we will see, this notion is more entangled than
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-trees.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a chordal graph. We call a maximal clique of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,

external
(respectively
internal
), if its vertex set contains at most one (respectively at least two) minimal separator(s) of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
We say that all external maximal cliques containing the same minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,
form an
external family of

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,
and we denote it by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal K}_{G}(S).
For each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K\in{\cal K}_{G}(S),
we define its
valiancy
to be
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf val}(K)=|V(K)\setminus S|,
i.e. the number of private vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K.
We call a minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,

external
(respectively
internal
), if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal K}_{G}(S)\neq\emptyset
(respectively
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal K}_{G}(S)=\emptyset
).

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\in\mathbb{N}.
A graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-
domino-tree
if it is either
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K_{r}
for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}r\leq k,
or it satisfies the following properties:

i
.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is chordal;

ii
.

Every minimal separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
has size
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
;

iii
.

Every maximal clique of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
has size in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}[k+1,2k]
;

iv
.

The vertex set of every maximal clique of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
contains at most two minimal separators;

v
.

The vertex set of every maximal clique of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
that contains exactly two minimal separators
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,S^{\prime}
is equal to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\cup S^{\prime}
;

vi
.

Every internal minimal separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
of connectivity-degree two, is not contained in the union of two other minimal separators;

vii
.

For every external minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
of connectivity-degree two, the union of the vertex sets of the maximal cliques that contain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,
has size greater than
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2k
;

viii
.

For every external minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|{\cal K}_{G}(S)|>1,
for any different pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K,K^{\prime}\in{\cal K}_{S},

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf val}(K)+{\sf val}(K^{\prime})>k.

A graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a
partial
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree
if it is a spanning subgraph of a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree.

, in a

-domino-tree, every minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is contained in the vertex set of a maximal clique. Moreover, it is easy to observe that, each augmented connected component in

Moreover, for different pairs

so

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is external, the connectivity-degree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|{\cal K}_{G}(S)|.

The following Lemma will be very useful in our proof of the min-max theorem in
Section 5
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G)
be a minimal separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
such that there exists
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C\in{\sf cc}(G-S)
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(C)|>k.
Then there exists a minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
with the following properties:

1.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}
is properly contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{+},
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{+}=G[V(C)\cup S],
i.e.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{+}
is the augmented connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G-S,
corresponding to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C.

2.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}
are not a subset of one another.

3.

The vertices in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\cup S^{\prime}
induce a maximal clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.

4.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C-S^{\prime}
is a connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G-S^{\prime}.

Proof.

Since every maximal clique of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
has size at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2k
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(C)|>k,
there exists a pair of non adjacent vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in S,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in V(C),
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
be a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator maximizing the size of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime}_{y}\in{\sf Acc}(G,S^{\prime}),
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime}_{y}
is the augmented connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G-S^{\prime},
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in V(C^{\prime}_{y}).

Property
1.
holds, since there exists a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\text{-}y
path with internal vertices in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C,
which implies that there is a vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime},
which, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is chordal (property
i.
), implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}\subseteq V(C^{+}).

Property
2.
also holds since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in S,
while
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\notin S^{\prime}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}
contains a vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
which clearly cannot be in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S.

For property
3.
assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\cup S^{\prime}
does not induce a clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Then there exists a pair of non adjacent vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x^{\prime}\in S,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
which in turn implies the existence of a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
- separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V(G)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
which is also properly contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{+}.
Observe that since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x^{\prime}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y^{\prime}
are not adjacent,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x^{\prime}\notin S^{\prime},
which since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is a clique, implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}
is also an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x^{\prime},y)
-separator. Now let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}\in{\sf Acc}(G,S^{\prime}),
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x^{\prime}\in V(C^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}})
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime\prime}_{y}\in{\sf Acc}(G,S^{\prime\prime}),
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in V(C^{\prime\prime}_{y}).
Notice that every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x^{\prime}\text{-}y^{\prime}
path has an internal vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}
and it intersects
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime\prime}.
This implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime\prime}
is completely contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}
and in turn that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\notin S^{\prime\prime}.
Finally observe that there exists a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\mbox{-}y^{\prime}
path with internal vertices only in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime}_{y}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime\prime}
cannot intersect any such path which implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y^{\prime}\in V(C^{\prime\prime}_{y}).
This contradicts the assumption on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}.
Also since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\cup S^{\prime}
induces a clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
it is contained in a maximal clique of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
whose vertex set contains exactly two minimal separators,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime}.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\cup S^{\prime}

5
The min-max equivalence Theorem

In this section we prove the main result of this paper. We prove equivalent min-max formalizations for the strict bramble number in terms of the lexicographic tree product number, the minimum width of lenient tree decompositions and subgraph containment in extremal structures. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
be a graph and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\in\mathbb{N}.
The following statements are equivalent.

1.

There is a tree
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T\cdot K_{k}.

2.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
has a lenient tree decomposition of width at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k.

3.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
has no strict bramble of order greater than
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k.

4.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a partial
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi(t)|\geq|\textchi^{\prime}(t)|>k.
Then since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal B^{\prime}}
-admissible and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\cap V(C)\neq\emptyset,
there must be some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B\in{\cal B}
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\cap\textBeta=\emptyset.
We show that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi^{\prime}(t)\cap B=\emptyset
as well. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\textchi^{\prime}(t)\cap B.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t_{x}\in{\sf Trace}_{(T^{\prime},\textchi^{\prime})}(x)
be closest to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z_{x}\subseteq T^{\prime}
be the unique
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}s\text{-}t_{x}
shortest path in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T^{\prime}.
Suppose to the contrary that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi^{\prime}(t)\cap B\neq\emptyset.
By definition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi^{\prime}(t),
it must be that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in S
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\notin\textchi(t).
Then this implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(Z_{x}).
Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi^{\prime}(s)|\leq k,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\neq s.
Also, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\notin\textchi(t),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\neq t_{x}.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
is an internal node of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T^{\prime}.
Moreover, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(s)
covers
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal B},
we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(s)\cap B\neq\emptyset.
Also by assumption,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t_{x})\cap B\neq\emptyset.
Then, because of
Section 3
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\cap B\neq\emptyset
which contradicts the fact that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\cap B=\emptyset.

∎

6
Edge-maximal graphs

In this section we prove a bound on the number of edges of a graph with strict bramble number at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k.
Recall that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}=\{G\mid{\sf sbn}(G)\leq k\}.
We achieve this by identifying the exact structure of all edge-maximal graphs of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}.
The striking difference with treewidth (where all
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-trees on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}n
vertices have the same number of edges), is that, edge-maximal graphs of some specific size may have a varying number of edges. More formally, we do this by proving that the edge-maximal graphs of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}
are exactly the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-trees.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G\in{\cal G}_{k}.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is an edge-maximal graph if and only if,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree.

Proof.

Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G\in{\cal G}_{k},
by
Section 5
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a partial
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree. Assume that it is edge-maximal but not a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree. This is an immediate contradiction, since we can still add edges to make it a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree and remain in the class. For the converse assume that it is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree but not edge-maximal. Then, by
Section 5
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a proper spanning subgraph of a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
-domino-tree, say
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D.
We prove that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is isomorphic to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D,
thus contradicting our assumption. If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(G)\leq 2k,
by definition and properties
vii.
,
viii.
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D
are isomorphic to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K_{|V(G)|}.
Assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(G)|>2k.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D
has at least one minimal separator.

First observe that any minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D
is also a minimal separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Thus, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a proper spanning subgraph of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D,
for any connected component
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C\in{\sf cc}(G-S),
that contains some vertex of a connected component
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C^{\prime}\in{\sf cc}(D-S),
it holds that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(C)|\leq|V(C^{\prime})|.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K
be a maximal clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D.
If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
also contains
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K
as a maximal clique we are done. Assume otherwise.

First, assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K
is an external maximal clique whose vertex set contains the external minimal separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(D)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C\in{\sf Acc}(G,S),
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(C)
contains a vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K)\setminus S.
From the previous observation
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(C)|\leq 2k.
We distinguish two cases. Assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is external in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf cdeg}_{G}(S)=2,
then the union of the vertex sets of the two maximal cliques that contain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,
has size at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2k,
which contradicts property
vii.
. Otherwise, in the context of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal K}_{G}(S)
as usual and assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|{\cal K}_{G}(S)|>1.
Then for any pair of different maximal cliques, say
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K^{\prime},K^{\prime\prime}\in{\cal K}_{G}(S),
since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(C)|\leq 2k,
we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf val}(K^{\prime})+{\sf val}(K^{\prime\prime})\leq k,
which contradicts property
viii.
. Now, assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
is internal in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
contains some other external minimal separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
and we can reapply the two previous arguments.

Now, assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K
is an internal maximal clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S,S^{\prime}\subseteq V(D)
be the two minimal separators covering
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K).
Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K
is not a maximal clique in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
there exists a pair of vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in S,y\in S^{\prime},
that are not adjacent in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
Then there exists a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V(G)
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K).
Observe that,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S^{\prime\prime}
cannot be an internal minimal separator with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf cdeg}_{G}(S^{\prime\prime})=2,
as that would contradict property
vi.
. Also, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf cdeg}_{G}(S^{\prime\prime})>2,
then there exists a connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G-S^{\prime\prime},
contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K),
which implies that there exists at least one vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(K)
that is not covered by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\cup S^{\prime}
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D.
This contradicts property
v.
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}D.

∎

Bounds.

We continue by presenting a tight upper bound for the number of edges an edge-maximal graph in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}
can have. Consider
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
to be an extreme lenient tree decomposition of width
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
for some graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
We proceed to count the maximum number of edges that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
can have. Recall, that in an extreme lenient tree decomposition all bags have size
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
and no pair of bags is a subset of one another.

Root
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
from some arbitrary internal node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textrho\in V(T).
For every node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)
different than
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textrho,
let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}p\in V(T)
be the parent of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t,
i.e. the neighbor closest to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textrho.
Then, define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k_{t}=|\textchi(t)\setminus\textchi(p)|,
i.e. the number of new vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
that this node introduces. Notice that,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k_{t}\in[k].
Also for notational simplicity, let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}X=V(T)\setminus\{\textrho\}.
In this way, it is clear that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle|V(G)|=k+\sum_{t\in X}k_{t},

and for the number of edges,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle|E(G)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle=\binom{k}{2}+\sum_{t\in X}\Bigg[\binom{k_{t}}{2}+k\cdot k_{t}\Bigg]

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle=\binom{k}{2}+\bigg(k-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\bigg(|V(G)|-k\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t\in X}k_{t}^{2}.

To maximize the above quantity, we have to maximize the sum of squares. We have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\sum_{t\in X}k_{t}=|V(G)|-k
and we want to partition this quantity into
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|X|
variables,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k_{t}\in[k],
so as to maximize
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\sum_{t\in X}k_{t}^{2}.
The optimal solution is given by having as many variables equal to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
as possible. So, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(G)|=c\cdot k+r,
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}r<k,
the optimal is given when
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
vertices
are introduced for the root (this introduces
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\choose 2
edges),
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}c-1
nodes introduce
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
new vertices each (this introduces
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k^{2}+{k\choose 2}
edges at a time) and we have a single node that introduces the remainder
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}r
(this introduces
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}rk+\binom{r}{2}
edges). We have that:

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle|E(G)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle\leq

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle\binom{k}{2}+\frac{c-1}{2}\Big(3k^{2}-k\Big)+rk+\binom{r}{2}

(4)

Replacing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}c
by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(|V(G)|-r)/k
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}r
by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|V(G)|\penalty\ {\sf mod}\penalty\ k,
in (
4
) we have that:

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle|E(G)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle\leq

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle\frac{3k-1}{2}|V(G)|-k^{2}-\frac{k}{2}\Big(|V(G)|\penalty\ {\sf mod}\penalty\ k\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(|V(G)|\penalty\ {\sf mod}\penalty\ k\Big)^{2}

(5)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G\in{\cal G}_{k}.
Then there is a function

such that

where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}f(|V(G)|,k),
is defined as the right-hand upper bound in (
5

where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(G)\leq k.

Also this bound is tight. Consider a

7
Minor obstructions for strict bramble number at most two

We use the term
graph collection
for finite sets of graphs, while for infinite sets of graphs we use the term
graph class
. Given a collection of graphs
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H},
we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf exc}({\cal H})
the class of all graphs that do not contain any of the graphs in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}
as a minor.

A graph class
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
is
minor-closed
if every minor of a graph in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
belongs to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}.
We define the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}({\cal G})
of all minor-minimal graphs not belonging to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}.
A direct consequence of the celebrated Robertson and Seymour Theorem
[
19
]
, is that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}({\cal G})
is a finite set. This implies that every minor-closed graph class
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
can be finitely characterised by this
obstruction set
, as a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
belongs to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G},
if and only if, none of the (finitely many) graphs in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}({\cal G})
is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H.
Therefore
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}={\sf exc}({\sf Obs}({\cal G})).
Although the identification of the obstruction set of a minor-closed graph class can be a very difficult task (as the size of this set more often than not is enormous), there is an ever-growing list of characterizations (partial or complete) of the obstruction sets for diverse minor-closed graph classes (see e.g.,
[

,
25
,
20

8

4

,

).

Recall that we have defined
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}
to be the class of graphs with strict bramble number at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k.
The goal of this section is to give an alternative characterization of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{2}
in terms of
forbidden minors
. First, as we have already noticed,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}({\cal G}_{1})=\{K_{3}\},
as

Lemma 20

Let

•

every node of

for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a triconnected decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
which subject to
Section 7
minimizes,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\displaystyle\textmu(\textTau,\textchi):=\sum_{t\in V(T)}({\sf adv}(t)-2).

Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2
-connected we can assume that every edge of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
has adhesion exactly two. Observe that, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}W_{4}
is not a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,

Section 7

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}3
-connected
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K_{4}.
Also, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K_{3}.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi(t)|\in\{3,4\}.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t\in V(T),
let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal A}_{t}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|{\cal A}_{t}|={\sf adv}(t).

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t)\not\subseteq V(C_{i}).

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G[\textchi(t)]

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K_{3}.
Assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|{\cal A}_{t}|\geq 3.

Subcase 1a:
For some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\in[3],

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C_{i}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in A_{i}.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal Z}\subseteq{\sf Obs}({\cal G}_{2}).
Now assume that there exists an obstruction
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z\in{\sf Obs}({\cal G}_{2})\setminus{\cal Z}.
As
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z\in{\sf Obs}({\cal G}_{2}),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z
excludes all the graphs in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal Z}
as a minor. Additionally, by
Section 7
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2
-connected. Then, by
Section 7
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z\in{\cal G}_{2},
which contradicts the choice of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z.
Thus
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}({\cal G}_{2})={\cal Z}.

∎

Notice that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}({\cal G}_{2})={\cal Z},
indicates that the class
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{2}
is “orthogonal” to the class, say
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal T}_{2},
of the graphs of treewidth at most two, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}({\cal T}_{2})=\{K_{4}\}.
Indeed
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(K_{4})=2
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf tw}(K_{4})=3,
while
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(H_{1})=3
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf tw}(H_{1})=2.

8
NP
-completeness

In this section, we prove that given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and an integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\in{\mathbb{N}},
deciding whether
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(G)\leq k
or, equivalently,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf ltp}(G)\leq k
is
NP
-complete. Membership in
NP
follows from the definition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}

via lenient tree decompositions, due to the min-max equivalence of
Section 5
. For the
NP
-hardness, we reduce the problem of deciding whether
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf tw}(G)\leq k
(that is
NP
-complete
[
3
]
) to our problem. We first prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 24
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
be a graph obtained from a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
after replacing every edge of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2k-1
paths of length two. If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
admits a lenient tree decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
of width at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
then, for every pair of vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in V(G)
that are adjacent in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
there is a bag of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
that contains them both.

Proof.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in V(G)
be an adjacent pair of vertices in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G.
We can easily observe that, by the definition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H,
there are
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2k-1
disjoint
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\text{-}y
paths in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H.
Then, by Menger’s Theorem, any
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
has size at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2k-1.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t_{x},t_{y}\in V(T)
be the closest nodes in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
whose bags contain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y
respectively. We want to show that the distance between them is zero. Assume that it is at least two. Then there exists a node of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T,
in between
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t_{x}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}t_{y}
whose bag, by
Section 3
, is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(x,y)
-separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
of size at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
which is a contradiction. Now assume that the distance is one. Then since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|\textchi(t_{x})\cup\textchi(t_{y})|\leq 2k,
there is a neighbour of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
which is not contained in either
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t_{x})
or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textchi(t_{y}),
which violates condition
(C2)
.
∎

We continue with the proof of the reduction.

Theorem 25
.

There exists a polynomially computable function that, given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and an integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\in{\mathbb{N}},
outputs a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
such that,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf tw}(G)\leq k-1
if and only if,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf ltp}(H)\leq k.

Proof.

We obtain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
by replacing every edge of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2k-1
paths of length two. Observe that the forward direction easily holds. Indeed, having parallel edges and subdividing them does not increase the tree width of a graph. Then the claim follows since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf tw}(G)\leq{\sf ltp}(G).
For the backwards direction, by
Section 5
, it is equivalent to argue in terms of lenient tree decompositions. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
be a lenient tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
of width at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k-1.
Then, by
Section 8
, for every pair of vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in V(H)
adjacent in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
there is a bag of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
that contains them both. Now simply observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(T,\textchi)
is a tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G,
of width at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k-1.
Thus
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf tw}(G)\leq k-1.

∎

9
Open problems

In this paper we initiated the study of the strict bramble number. As computing this parameter is an
NP
-complete problem, it is meaningful to design a parameterized algorithm, i.e. one that can answer whether
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf sbn}(G)\leq k
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}f(k)\cdot|V(G)|^{O(1)}
steps, for some computable function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}f.
Such an algorithm actually exists because this question is equivalent to checking whether none of the graphs in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}(G_{k})
is contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
as a minor,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}(G_{k})
is finite for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
because of the Robertson and Seymour theorem
[
19
]
, and minor checking for graphs of bounded treewidth can be done in linear (on the size of the host graph) time. However, this argument is not constructive as we do not know
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}(G_{k}).
Therefore it is an open problem to actually design such an algorithm. A possible step for this would to identify
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf Obs}(G_{k})
for higher values of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k.
However, this seems to be a hard problem, even for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k=3.

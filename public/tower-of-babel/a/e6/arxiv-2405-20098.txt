Rational cohomology of ℳ 4 , 1 \mathcal{M}_{4,1}
Yiu Man Wong, Angelina Zheng

Abstract
. We compute the rational cohomology of the moduli space ℳ 4 , 1 \mathcal{M}_{4,1} of non-singular genus 4 4 curves with 1 1 marked point, using Gorinov-Vassiliev’s method.

Definition 1.1

We define the Hodge-Grothendieck polynomials of a graded
\mathbf{Q}
-vector space

which carries a mixed Hodge structure as

P_{T_{\bullet}}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}\left[T^{i}\right]t^{i}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS}_{\mathbf{Q}})\left[t\right].

If

is a quasi-projective variety, then its rational cohomology and its Borel-Moore homology carry a mixed Hodge structure and we will denote by

{P}_{X}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}\left[H^{i}(X;\mathbf{Q})\right]t^{i}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS}_{\mathbf{Q}})\left[t\right],

\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.07639pt\hbox{\kern-1.49994pt$P$\kern-0.50003pt}}_{X}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}\left[\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.07639pt\hbox{\kern-1.49994pt$H$\kern-0.50003pt}}_{i}(X;\mathbf{Q})\right]t^{i}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS}_{\mathbf{Q}})\left[t\right],

their Hodge-Grothendieck polynomials, respectively.

We will work with mixed Hodge structures which are sums of Tate-Hodge structures. Therefore, the Hodge-Grothendieck polynomials appearing in this paper are equivalent to the cohomology or the Borel-Moore-homology with rational coefficients.

.

The Hodge-Grothendieck polynomial of

\displaystyle C_{2,1}\subset\overline{C}_{1,1}\subset\overline{C}_{0,1}=\mathcal{M}_{4,1},

Theorem 1.3

For any

Since the base space has the rational cohomology of a point, the rational cohomology of

∎

The space

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will briefly introduce the techniques and prove some results which we will use frequently in the following sections.

2.1.
Gorinov-Vassiliev’s method

We briefly introduce Gorinov-Vassiliev’s method, for the sake of completeness and to set the notation. For the details of the method, we refer the readers to
[
Tom05
, Section 2.1]
.

Let
Z
be a projective variety and
V
a vector space of global sections of some vector bundle on
Z
.
The cohomology of the open space
X\subset V
of non-singular sections is equivalent to the Borel-Moore homology of the
discriminant

\Sigma=V\setminus X
via Alexander duality:

\widetilde{H}^{j}(X;\mathbf{Q})\cong\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.07639pt\hbox{\kern-1.49994pt$H$\kern-0.50003pt}}_{2\operatorname{dim}V-j-1}(\Sigma;\mathbf{Q})\otimes\mathbf{Q}(-\operatorname{dim}V).

Here
\widetilde{H}^{\bullet}
refers to the reduced cohomology and
\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.07639pt\hbox{\kern-1.49994pt$H$\kern-0.50003pt}}_{\bullet}
denote Borel-Moore homology,
[
Ful98
, Chapter 19]
.

Gorinov-Vassiliev’s method allows us to compute the Borel-Moore homology of a discriminant, by constructing a simplicial
resolution for
\Sigma
, starting from a collection
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
of families of configurations in
Z
, satisfying the axioms in
[
Tom05
, List 2.3]
.
This collection defines a space
|\mathcal{X}|,
together with a
geometric realization

|\mathcal{X}|\rightarrow\Sigma
that is a homotopy equivalence and induces an isomorphism on Borel-Moore homology, which respects mixed Hodge structures.
The method provides a stratification by locally closed spaces
\{F_{j}\}_{j=1,\dots,n}
of
|\mathcal{X}|.

This gives a spectral sequence converging to
\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.07639pt\hbox{\kern-1.49994pt$H$\kern-0.50003pt}}_{\bullet}(\Sigma;\mathbf{Q})
whose columns are such that

E^{1}_{p,q}=\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.07639pt\hbox{\kern-1.49994pt$H$\kern-0.50003pt}}_{p+q}(F_{p};\mathbf{Q}),

and can be computed using the following description of the strata, this spectral sequence will be called
Vassiliev’s spectral sequence
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Gor05
]
)
.

(1)

For any
i=1,\dots,N
the stratum
F_{i}
is a complex
vector bundle of rank
d_{i}
over
\Phi_{i}
, which is a locally trivial fibration over
X_{i}
.

(2)

If
X_{i}
consists of configurations of
m
points, the fiber of
\Phi_{i}
over any
K\in X_{i}
is an

(m-1)
-dimensional open simplex, which changes orientation under the homotopy
class of a loop in
X_{i}
interchanging a pair of points in
K
.

(3)

If
X_{N}=\{Z\}
,
F_{N}
is the open cone with vertex a point (corresponding to the
configuration
Z
), over
\cup_{j=1}^{N-1}\Phi_{j}.

2.2.
Generalized Leray-Hirsch Theorem

Theorem 2.2

(
[
PS03
]
)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety and
G
be a reductive group acting on
X
with finite stabilizers.
Consider the orbit map

\displaystyle\rho:G

\displaystyle\rightarrow X

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto g\cdot x_{0},

with
x_{0}\in X
fixed.

If the map induced by the orbit map

\rho^{*}:H^{\bullet}(X;\mathbf{Q})\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(G;\mathbf{Q})

is surjective, then there is an isomorphism of graded linear spaces

\displaystyle H^{\bullet}(X/G;\mathbf{Q})\otimes H^{\bullet}(G;\mathbf{Q})\cong H^{\bullet}(X;\mathbf{Q}).

The hypotheses of Theorem
2.2
are known to hold for moduli spaces of non-singular
(3,3)
-curves on
\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
,
[
Tom05
, 3.1]
, and for moduli of non-singular curves on a quadric cone,
[
Tom05
, 4.1]
. Moreover, by
[
BT07
, Theorem 10]
this also extends to
\mathcal{I}_{i},

i=0,1.

By considering the action of
G_{i}=\operatorname{Aut}Z_{i}
on
\mathcal{I}_{i}
, we then expect that

(2.1)

H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}_{i};\mathbf{Q})\cong H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}_{i}/G_{i};\mathbf{Q})\otimes H^{\bullet}(G_{i};\mathbf{Q}).

We will make use of the divisibility of the cohomology of
\mathcal{I}_{i}
to determine the ranks of the differential maps in the spectral sequence.

3.
Curves on a non-singular quadric

A non-singular quadric surface
Q
in
\mathbf{P}^{3}
is isomorphic to
Z_{0}=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
via the Segre embedding.
Let
[x_{0}:x_{1}]
and
[y_{0}:y_{1}]
denote the coordinates on
\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
.
A curve on
\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
is defined by a bihomogeneous polynomial on the variables
x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1}
.
We call the curves defined by a polynomial of bidegree
(m,n)
an
(m,n)
-curve
.
The lines on
\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
are the
(0,1)
-curves, respectively
(1,0)
-curves, which we will call lines of the first, respectively second ruling.

The curves which are intersections of a fixed non-singular quadric surface with a cubic surface in
\mathbf{P}^{3}
are exactly the
(3,3)
-curves on
\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
. The space of
(3,3)
-curves is a complex vector space
V=\mathbf{C}[x_{0},x_{1};y_{0},y_{1}]_{(3,3)}\cong\mathbf{C}^{16}
while the space of
(3,3)
-curves through a fixed point
p
is a vector subspace
V_{0}\subset V
of dimension
d=15

As in

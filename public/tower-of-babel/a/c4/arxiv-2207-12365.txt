Abstract
We consider the fractional Burgers equation Δ α / 2 ​ u + b ⋅ ∇ ( u ​ | u | ( α − 1 ) / β ) \Delta^{\alpha/2}u+b\cdot\nabla(u|u|^{(\alpha-1)/\beta}) on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , with α ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) \alpha\in(1,2) and β > 1 \beta>1 and prove the existence of a solution for a large class of initial conditions, which contains functions that do not belong to any L p ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty . Next, we apply the general results to the initial condition u 0 ​ ( x ) = M ​ | x | − β u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta} , 1 < β < d 1<\beta<d , and show the existence of a selfsimilar solution and derive its properties such as smoothness, two-sided estimates, asymptotics and gradient estimates.



1
Introduction

Let
d\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(1,2)
. Consider the fractional Burgers equation

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta^{\alpha/2}u+b\cdot\nabla(u|u|^{q}),&\qquad t>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\end{cases}

(1.1)

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\beta>1
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
q=\frac{\alpha-1}{\beta}
are fixed. For technical reasons, we assume (without loss of generality)
b=(|b|,0,0,...,0)
. Here,
\Delta^{\alpha/2}
is the fractional Laplacian

\displaystyle\Delta^{\alpha/2}f(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}c_{d,\alpha}\int_{|y|>\varepsilon}\frac{f(x+y)-f(x)}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}dy,\hskip 25.0ptf\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
c_{d,\alpha}
is some constant. It may be also defined by the Fourier transform

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta^{\alpha/2}f}(\xi)=-|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi).

We put

\displaystyle p(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}},\qquad t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

and denote
p(t,x,y)=p(t,y-x)
,
t>0,\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The function
p
is the density of the semigroup

P_{t}=e^{t\Delta^{\alpha/2}}

generated by the fractional Laplacian,

P_{t}f(x)=e^{t\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)f(y)dy.

By a solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) we mean a mild solution, i.e. a function
u\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the Duhamel formula

\displaystyle u(t,x)=P_{t}u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-s,x,z)u(s,z)|u(s,z)|^{q}\,dz\,ds.

(1.2)

The fractional Burgers equation was intensely studied in recent years, see e.g.
[
MR2659155
,
MR3789847
,
MR1637513
,
MR1849690
,
MR1881259
,
MR2407204
,
KMX
,
AIK
,
KNS
,
WW
,
MR3382709
]
. It is a generalization of the classical Burgers equation

\displaystyle u_{t}=\nu u_{xx}-\tfrac{1}{2}(u^{2})_{x},

which was introduced as a simplest model describing the turbulence phenomena (see e.g.
[
Burgers
]
). The fractional counterpart (
1.1
) of this equation was studied for the first time by Biler and Funaki in
[
MR1637513
]
. Later on, in
[
MR1881259
]
Biler, Karch and Woyczyński considered some further generalizations, where the fractional Laplacian was replaced by some Lévy operator and nonlinearity
f(u)
was given by a smooth function
f
. In particular, they proved that for
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists a unique solution to (
1.1
) such that (see
[
MR1881259
, Theorem 3.1]
)

\displaystyle u\in C\big((0,\infty);W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)\cap C^{1}\big((0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big),

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\gamma}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\gamma},\ \ \ \ \ t>0,\ \ \gamma\in[1,\infty].

(1.4)

We point out that in both papers the standing assumptions was
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In
[
MR1849690
]
the authors studied the equation (
1.1
) with the critical exponent
q=\frac{(\alpha-1)}{d}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta^{\alpha/2}u+b\cdot\nabla u|u|^{(\alpha-1)/d},&\qquad t>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

They showed that for
u_{0}=M\delta_{0}
,
M>0
, there is a unique selfsimilar solution (called source solution) to (
1.5
) satisfying the scaling property
U(t,x)=t^{-d/\alpha}U(1,t^{-1/\alpha}x)
(the same as the density
p
). It turns out that this self-similar solution determines the long time behavior of solutions to a large class of Cauchy problems (
1.5
) with
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\|u_{0}\|_{1}=M
(see
[
MR1849690
, Theorem 2.2]
)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{d}{\alpha}(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\|u(t,\cdot)-U(t,\cdot))\|_{\gamma}=0,\qquad\gamma\in[1,\infty].

(1.6)

If
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the existence of the solution to the problem (
1.5
) can by proved by using the Banach fixed point theorem (see
[
MR1881259
]
). The case
u_{0}=M\delta_{0}
is in some sense similar to
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
since such
u_{0}
is still integrable and the solution may be constructed by using the approximation by the solutions with initial conditions from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Nevertheless, if
u_{0}
does not belong to any
L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\gamma\in[1,\infty]
, the construction by fixed point theorems fails.

In this paper we assume that the initial condition
u_{0}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}t^{(\beta-1)/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{0}(y)|p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})dy\leq\mu,

(
A
)

where
\mu\in(0,\infty)
is some constant. Here,
p^{(d-1)}
is the density of
P_{t}
in dimension
d-1
and
\tilde{x}={(x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
(see (
2.2
) and Notation in Section 2).
The main feature of the class of functions satisfying (
A
) is that it contains some functions not belonging to any
L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\gamma\in[1,\infty]
. This allows us to drop the common assumption
u_{0}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, or even
u_{0}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and consider some singular initial conditions.
Our first result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
,
\beta>1
and
q=(\alpha-1)/\beta
. If
u_{0}
satisfies (
A
), then there exists a solution
u(t,x)
to the problem (
1.1
). Furthermore, there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
d,\alpha,\beta,b,\mu
such that

\displaystyle|u(t,x)|\leq C\,t^{-1/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{0}(y)|p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})dy\leq C\mu t^{-\beta/\alpha},\qquad t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.7)

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
we proceed similarly as in the case of source solutions and approximate the initial condition by the monotone sequence of functions from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. However, the convergence of the corresponding sequence of solutions is a major problem here. We resolve it by showing that a certain functional acting on these solutions is uniformly bounded, which ensures the point-wise convergence of the sequence almost everywhere (see Lemma
3.3
). This approach, however, requires the technical assumptions
\beta>1
and
d\geq 2
.

The first bound in Theorem
1.1
has one weakness - it does not depend on
x_{1}
, and therefore it is not optimal. Nevertheless, in many cases this inconvenience could be removed. We present it on an example, which turns out to be quite special. Namely, in the second part of the paper we focus on the solution to the problem (
1.1
) with
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta}
,
M>0
,
1<\beta<d
, constructed in Theorem
1.1
. Its first interesting property is selfsimilarity, in which one can see an analogy to the source solution in the case
\beta=d
and
u_{0}=M\delta_{0}
. Next, we turn our attention to the two-sided pointwise estimates, which not only improve (
1.7
) in this case, but complement it with the lower bound of the same form. Such estimates were obtained for the source solution of (
1.5
). In
[
MR2407204
]
Karch and Brandolese showed that for
q=\frac{\alpha-1}{d}
and
u_{0}=M\delta_{0}
with
M>0
sufficiently small, the solution
u
to (
1.5
) admits the estimates
0\leq{u(t,x)}\leq cp(t,x)
. In
[
JS1
]
we generalized this result to any
M>0
and obtained two-sided estimates
c^{-1}p(t,x)\leq{u(t,x)}\leq cp(t,x)
.
Furthermore, in
[
JS2
, Theorem 1.1]
we showed that for nonnegative
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there is a constant
C>0
such that the solution to (
1.1
) with
q\geq\frac{\alpha-1}{d}
satisfies

\displaystyle C^{-1}\,P_{t}u_{0}(x)\leq u(t,x)\leq CP_{t}u_{0}(x),\ \ \ \ \ \ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.8)

Note that the pointwise estimates of solutions are rather rare in the literature, as deriving them is more challenging than e.g. the
L^{p}
bounds. Nevertheless, they give a better insight into the behaviour of the solution. For instance the estimates (
1.8
) permitted the authors to improve the asymptotics given in (
1.6
).

In Theorem
1.2
below we prove the existence of the selfsimilar solution to the problem (
1.1
) for
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta}
with
M>0
,
1<\beta<d
and provide its properties.
Note that the assumption
\beta<d
is required in order to get local integrability of
u_{0}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
,
1<\beta<d
,
q=(\alpha-1)/\beta
,
M>0
and
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta}
. Then, there exists a function
U(x)
such that

1.

u(t,x)=t^{-\beta/\alpha}U(t^{-1/\alpha}x)
is the solution to the problem (
1.1
).

2.

There exists a constant
C_{1}>1
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-1}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}\leq U(x)\leq C_{1}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.9)

3.

There is a constant
C_{2}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\left|U(x)-P_{1}u_{0}(x)\right|\leq C_{2}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta+(\alpha-1)}},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\left|U(x)-P_{1}u_{0}(x)-\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}\nabla_{x}p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)[P_{1}u_{0}(w)]^{1+q}dwdr\right|

(1.11)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{C_{2}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta+2(\alpha-1)}}.

4.

U\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and there is a constant
C_{3}
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla U(x)|\leq C_{3}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.12)

All the constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
depend only on
d,\alpha,\beta,M,b
and might be calculated explicitly.

Remark 1.3
.

By Lemma
2.3
, the bounds in (
1.9
) may be equivalently written in the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)\approx\frac{1}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\beta}}\approx P_{t}u_{0}(x),\qquad t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

which resembles both: (
1.8
) by representation involving the semi-group, and the estimates of the source solution in the critical case derived in
[
JS2
]
by the exact form.
Similarly, since
\nabla_{x}u(t,x)=\lambda^{\beta+1}\nabla_{x}(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x)
, by (
1.12
) we have

|\nabla_{x}u(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1/\alpha}(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\beta}},\qquad t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We will use the following notation. We write ”:=” to indicate definitions, e.g.
a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\}
and
a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\}.
All the considered functions are tacitly assumed to be Borel measurable. We write
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
if
f,g\geq 0
and there is a number
c>0
not depending on other parameters than
d
,
\alpha
,
\beta
,
M
and
b
such that
f(x)\leq cg(x)
for all arguments
x
. If
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
, we write
f(x)\approx g(x)
. If the comparability constant depends additionally on some other parameters (e.g. on
\gamma
), we indicate the parameter over the comparison sign (e.g.
f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\approx}}g
).

By
\nabla=(\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{d}})
we denote the standard gradient operator, while
\nabla^{\mathbf{k}}=\partial^{k_{1}}_{x_{1}}\ldots\partial^{k_{d}}_{x_{d}}
, where
{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
. The norm of the multi-index
{\mathbf{k}}
is given by
|{\mathbf{k}}|=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}
. By writing
\nabla_{x}
and
\nabla^{\mathbf{k}}_{x}
we indicate variable with respect to which the derivatives are taken.

For any
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote
\tilde{x}:=(x_{2},x_{3},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. For any function
f\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define its rescaled version by

\displaystyle f^{*}(t,x):=t^{\beta/\alpha}f(t,t^{1/\alpha}x).

(2.1)

Similarly, we put

\displaystyle P_{t}^{*}f(x):=t^{\beta/\alpha}P_{t}f(t^{1/\alpha}x)=t^{\beta/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,t^{1/\alpha}x,y)f(y)dy.

For any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma>1
we define the french power
a^{\left\langle\gamma\right\rangle}=a|a|^{\gamma-1}
. In particular,
|u(s,z)|u(s,z)^{q}=u(s,z)^{\left\langle q+1\right\rangle}
.

2.2
Fractional Laplacian semigroup

Although in the paper we generally assume
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
d\geq 2
, the results of this section are valid for
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
. The methodology we propose in the next sections requires consideration of the density of the semigroup
P_{t}
in various dimensions at the same time. Therefore, for
m\in\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
we put

\displaystyle p^{(m)}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}},\qquad t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(2.2)

and denote
p^{(m)}(t,x,y)=p^{(m)}(t,y-x)
,
t>0,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
In the case
m=d
we simply write
p^{(d)}(t,x,y)=p(t,x,y)=p(t,y-x)
.
Below, we collect some basic properties of the density
p
.
The following scaling property holds

\displaystyle p(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x,\lambda y)=\lambda^{-d}p(t,x,y),\qquad\lambda>0.

(2.3)

Furthermore, the function
p^{(m)}
admits the two-sided estimates

\displaystyle p^{{(m)}}(t,x,y)\approx\frac{t}{\left(t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|\right)^{{m}+\alpha}},\qquad t>0,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(2.4)

where the constants in the bounds depend on
m
and
\alpha
. In particular, this implies

\displaystyle\|p(t,\cdot)\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}(1-1/\gamma)}\|p(1,\cdot)\|_{\gamma},\qquad t>0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle p(t,x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1/\alpha}p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y}),\hskip 29.0ptt>0,\,x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.6)

Lemma 2.1
.

For
t>0
,
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,x,y)dx_{1}=p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y}).

(2.7)

Proof.

By the subordination formula (see
[
MK
]
) there is a nonnegative function
\eta_{t}(s)
such that

\displaystyle p^{(m)}(t,x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(4\pi s)^{-m/2}e^{-|x-y|^{2}/4s}\eta_{t}(s)ds,\qquad m\in\mathbb{N},t>0,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(2.8)

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,x,y)dx_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(4\pi s)^{-d/2}e^{-|x-y|^{2}/4s}\eta_{t}(s)dx_{1}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(4\pi s)^{-(d-1)/2}e^{-|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|^{2}/4s}\eta_{t}(s)ds=p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y}).

∎

The subordination formula (
2.8
) allows us also to derive the following
estimates of
\nabla^{\mathbf{k}}p
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla^{{\mathbf{k}}}_{x}p(t,x)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbf{k}}}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{p(t,x)}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{|{\mathbf{k}}|}}.

(2.9)

Proof.

Let

{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and

g(s,x)=(4\pi s)^{-d/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/(4s)}
be the Gaussian kernel. Then,

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\mathbf{k}}}g(s,x)=(4s)^{-|{\mathbf{k}}|/2}g(s,x)\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{k_{i}}(x_{i}/2\sqrt{s}),

where
H_{n}
are the Hermite polynomials. By subordination formula (
2.8
) and the Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\mathbf{k}}}p(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(4s)^{-|{\mathbf{k}}|/2}g(s,x)\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{k_{i}}(x_{i}/2\sqrt{s})\eta_{t}(s)\,ds.

Since every
H_{k_{i}}
is a polynomial of degree
k_{i}
, we bound

\left|\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{k_{i}}(x_{i}/2\sqrt{s})\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbf{k}}}}{{\lesssim}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}[1+|x_{i}/2\sqrt{s}|^{k_{1}}]\lesssim 1+|x/2\sqrt{s}|^{|{\mathbf{k}}|}.

Consequently

\displaystyle|\nabla^{{\mathbf{k}}}p(t,x)|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbf{k}}}}{{\lesssim}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(4s)^{-|{\mathbf{k}}|/2}g(s,x){\eta_{t}(s)}\,ds+|x|^{|{\mathbf{k}}|}\int_{0}^{\infty}(4s)^{-|{\mathbf{k}}|}g(s,x){\eta_{t}(s)}\,ds

\displaystyle=p^{(d+|{\mathbf{k}}|)}(t,x_{+})+|x|^{|{\mathbf{k}}|}p^{(d+2|{\mathbf{k}}|)}(t,x_{++}),

where
{x_{+}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+|{\mathbf{k}}|}
and
{x_{++}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+2|{\mathbf{k}}|}
. Eventually, by (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla^{{\mathbf{k}}}p(t,x)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbf{k}}}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{t}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{d+\alpha+|{\mathbf{k}}|}}+\frac{|x|^{|{\mathbf{k}}|}t}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{d+\alpha+2|{\mathbf{k}}|}}\approx\frac{1}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{|{\mathbf{k}}|}}p(t,x),

as required.
∎

The next lemma describes the behaviour of
P_{t}
acting on the function
(r^{1/\alpha}+|y|)^{-\gamma}
, which, as we will show, is comparable with a solution to (
1.1
) for
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\gamma}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<\gamma<d
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t>0
and
r\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)(r^{1/\alpha}+|y|)^{-\gamma}dy\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\approx}}\frac{1}{(r^{1/\alpha}+t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\gamma}}.

(2.10)

Proof.

By
[
MR3933622
, Lemma 2.3]
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)|y|^{-\gamma}dy\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\approx}}\frac{1}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\gamma}},

(2.11)

which proves (
2.10
) for
r=0
. Now, let
r>0
. By (
2.11
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)(r^{1/\alpha}+|y|)^{-\gamma}dy

\displaystyle\approx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)p(r,y,z)|z|^{-\gamma}dzdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t+r,x,z)|z|^{-\gamma}dz\approx\frac{1}{((r+t)^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\gamma}}.

Since
(r+t)^{1/\alpha}\approx r^{1/\alpha}+t^{1/\alpha}
, we obtain (
2.10
).
∎

2.3
Basic properties of solutions to (
1.1
)

Let us observe that
if
u
and
v
are the solutions to
1.1
with initials conditions
u_{0}
and
-u_{0}
, respectively, then
u(t,x)=-v(t,x)
for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

As mentioned in Introduction, if
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then there exists a unique solution
u(t,x)
to the problem (
1.1
). Furthermore,
[
MR1881259
, Corollary 3.1]
implies the monotonicity property of solutions. Namely, if
u
and
v
are the solutions of (1.1) with initials conditions

u_{0},v_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{0}\geq v_{0}
, then

\displaystyle u(t,x)\geq v(t,x),\qquad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.12)

Let us now denote by
\overline{u}
and
\underline{u}
the solutions to
1.1
with initials conditions
|u_{0}|
and
-|u_{0}|
, respectively. Since
\overline{u}=-\underline{u}
, we get by (
2.12
)

\displaystyle|u|\leq|\overline{u}|\vee|\underline{u}|=\overline{u}.

(2.13)

3.1
An integral conservation law

Up till the end of the paper we assume that
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
d\geq 2
.
Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be any function.
For a function

we denote

h_{F}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(x)F(\tilde{x})dx,

whenever the integral is convergent.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then, for any
u
satisfying (
1.2
) with
|(P_{t}u_{0})_{F}|<\infty
for every
t>0
and such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}p(t-s,x,z)||u(s,z)|^{q+1}|F(\tilde{x})|\,dx\,dz\,ds<\infty,

(3.1)

we have

\displaystyle u(t,\cdot)_{F}=(P_{t}u_{0})_{F},\qquad t>0.

Proof.

Since
b=(|b|,0,\ldots,0)
, by (
1.2
) and Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle u(t,\cdot)_{F}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(P_{t}u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-s,x,z)u(s,z)^{\left\langle q+1\right\rangle}\,dz\,ds\right)F(\tilde{x})dx

\displaystyle=(P_{t}u_{0})_{F}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|b|\partial_{x_{1}}p(t-s,x,z)F(\tilde{x})dx\right)u(s,z)^{\left\langle q+1\right\rangle}\,dz\,ds

\displaystyle=(P_{t}u_{0})_{F},

where we used (
2.4
) and the fact that
F(\tilde{x})
does not depend on
x_{1}
.
∎

In particular, we show that the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
are satisfied if
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F
is bounded.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let

be bounded. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(\tilde{w})u(t,w)\,dw=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(\tilde{w})P_{t}u_{0}(w)\,dw,\qquad t>0.

Proof.

Let
K=\sup_{\tilde{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}|F(\tilde{w})|
. Since
|P_{t}u_{0}|_{F}\leq K\|u_{0}\|_{1}<\infty
, by Lemma
3.1
, we only need to show that the condition (
3.1
) holds. By (
1.4
), (
2.9
) and (

) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla p(t-s,w,z)||u(s,z)|^{1+q}|F(\tilde{w})|dw\,dz\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim K\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p(t-s,w,z)}{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}|u(s,z)|dw\,dz\,ds

\displaystyle=K\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(s,z)|dz\,ds=K\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{1}\frac{\alpha t^{1-1/\alpha}}{\alpha-1}<\infty,

which ends the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p^{(d-1)}(t_{0},\tilde{x},\tilde{w})u(t,w)\,dw=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p^{(d-1)}(t_{0},\tilde{x},\tilde{w})P_{t}u_{0}(w)\,dw,\qquad t>0.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.4
.

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{(\alpha-1+\beta)/\beta}\int_{1-1/2\kappa^{\alpha}}^{1}r^{d-\beta}(1-r)^{-1/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(1-r,x,rw)\,dw\,dr

Concerning the integral

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\kappa^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\beta}}\int_{0}^{1-1/2\kappa^{\alpha}}r^{d-\beta}(1-r)^{-1/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(1-r,x,rw)|u^{*}(r^{\alpha}t,w)|\,dw\,dr.

(3.4)

3.3
) to (

. Hence, by (
2.9
), (
2.3
), substituting
z={s^{1/\alpha}}w
and then using Lemma
3.3
and the assumption (
A
),
for
s\in(0,t/2)
we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-s,x,z){[u^{(k,n)}(s,z)]^{\left\langle 1+q\right\rangle}}dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|b|(t-s)^{-1/\alpha}s^{-(\beta+\alpha-1)/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,{x},{z})\big(\overline{u}^{(k,n)}\big)^{*}(s,s^{-1/\alpha}z)\,dz

\displaystyle=|b|(t-s)^{-1/\alpha}s^{(d+1-\beta-\alpha)/\alpha}t^{-d/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(1-(s/t),t^{-1/\alpha}{x},(s/t)^{1/\alpha}{w})\big(\overline{u}^{(k,n)}\big)^{*}(s,w)\,dw

\displaystyle\lesssim|b|t^{-1/\alpha}s^{(d+1-\beta-\alpha)/\alpha}t^{-d/\alpha}\frac{(s/t)^{(\beta-d)/\alpha}}{(1-(s/t)^{1/\alpha})^{1/\alpha}}t^{(\beta-1)/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})|u_{0}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|b|t^{-(\beta+1)/\alpha}\mu}{(1-1/2^{1/\alpha})^{1/\alpha}}s^{-(\alpha-1)/\alpha}.

On the other hand, for
s\in[t/2,t)
, by Theorem
3.4
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-s,x,z){[u^{(k,n)}(s,z)]^{\left\langle 1+q\right\rangle}}dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|b|(t-s)^{-1/\alpha}s^{-(\beta+\alpha-1)/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x,z)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim|b|t^{-(\beta+\alpha-1)/\alpha}(t-s)^{-1/\alpha}.

Since
\int_{0}^{t}(s^{-(\alpha-1)/\alpha}\vee(t-s)^{-1/\alpha})ds<\infty
, we apply the dominated convergence theorem once again and get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-s,x,z){[u^{(k,n)}(s,z)]^{\left\langle 1+q\right\rangle}}dzds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-s,x,z){u(s,z)^{\left\langle 1+q\right\rangle}}dzds,

which ends the proof.
∎

4
The self-similar solution

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, where the initial condition
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta}
,
1<\beta<d
and
M>0
is considered.

4.1
Existence and selfsimilarity

Let us start with the following observation on how scaling of the initial condition is transferred to scaling of the solution.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) with initial condition
u_{0}
,
then
v(t,x)=\lambda^{\beta/\alpha}u(\lambda t,\lambda^{1/\alpha}x)
is a solution with the initial condition
v_{0}(x)=\lambda^{\beta/\alpha}u_{0}(\lambda^{1/\alpha}x).

Proof.

By scaling property (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{\beta/\alpha}P_{\lambda t}u_{0}(\lambda^{1/\alpha}x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\beta/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(\lambda t,\lambda^{1/\alpha}x,y)u_{0}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda^{d/\alpha}p(\lambda t,\lambda^{1/\alpha}x,\lambda^{1/\alpha}w)\lambda^{\beta/\alpha}u_{0}(\lambda^{1/\alpha}w)\,dw=P_{t}v_{0}(x)\,.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\beta/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(\lambda t-s,\lambda^{1/\alpha}x,y)u^{q+1}(s,y)\,dy\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda^{d/\alpha+1}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(\lambda t-\lambda r,\lambda^{1/\alpha}x,\lambda^{1/\alpha}w)\lambda^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}\lambda^{\beta(q+1)/\alpha}u^{q+1}(\lambda r,\lambda^{1/\alpha}w)\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-r,x,w)v(r,w)\,dw\,dr.

Hence,

\displaystyle v(t,x)=\lambda^{\beta/\alpha}u(\lambda t,\lambda^{1/\alpha}x)=P_{t}v_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{x}p(t-r,x,w)v(r,w)\,dw\,dr,

as required.
∎

Next we verify the condition (
A
) in the case
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The function
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta}
,
M>0
, satisfies the condition (
A
).

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|y|^{-\beta}dy_{1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\tfrac{1}{2}(|\tilde{y}|+|y_{1}|)]^{-\beta}dy_{1}=\frac{2^{\beta+1}}{\beta-1}|\tilde{y}|^{1-\beta},

(4.1)

Lemma
2.3
gives us

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(y)p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}|\tilde{y}|^{1-\beta}p^{(d-1)}(t,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}\lesssim(t^{1/\alpha}+|\tilde{x}|)^{1-\beta},

(4.2)

which yields (
A
).
∎

Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
there exists a solution
u(t,x)
to the initial problem (
1.1
) with
u_{0}(x)=M|x|^{-\beta}
,
M>0
.
From Lemma
4.1
we know that its rescaled version
\lambda^{\beta}u(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x)
,
\lambda>0
, is also a solution to the same problem. Nevertheless, these may be two different solutions. In Lemma
4.3
we show that the solution from Theorem
1.1
is self-similar i.e. coincides with its rescaled versions.

Throughout the whole section we exploit an analogous notation to the one from the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Namely,
u^{(n)}
,
n\geq 1
, is defined as the solution to the problem (
1.1
) with

\displaystyle u_{0}^{(n)}(x)=\left(n\wedge\frac{M}{|x|^{\beta}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{B(0,n)}(x),

(4.3)

and
u(t,x):=\lim_{n\nearrow\infty}u^{(n)}(t,x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The initial condition is positive in this case, so there is no need to consider the double sequence
u_{0}^{(k,n)}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\lambda>0
we have

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\lambda^{\beta}u(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x),\ \ \ \ \ \ t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.4)

Proof.

It suffices to consider
\lambda>1
. Indeed, if we substitute
t=s/\lambda^{\alpha}
and
x=y/\lambda
in (
4.4
), we get the result for
\lambda<1
. We note that

\lambda^{\beta}u(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda^{\beta}u^{(n)}(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x),

where, by Lemma
4.1
,
\lambda^{\beta}u^{(n)}(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x)
is the solution to the problem (
1.1
) with the rescaled initial condition
\lambda^{\beta}u^{(n)}_{0}(\lambda x)=\left(\lambda^{\beta}n\wedge\frac{M}{|x|^{\beta}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{B(0,n/\lambda)}
.
Observe that for
i_{n}=\lfloor n/\lambda\rfloor
and
j_{n}=\lceil\lambda^{\beta}n\rceil
,

u^{(i_{n})}_{0}(x)\leq\lambda^{\beta}u^{(n)}_{0}(\lambda x)\leq u^{(j_{n})}_{0}(x),\ \ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
and
\lceil\cdot\rceil
are the standard floor and ceiling functions, respectively. Hence, by the monotonicity property (
2.12
), we get

u^{(i_{n})}(t,x)\leq\lambda^{\beta}u^{(n)}(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x)\leq u^{(j_{n})}(t,x),\qquad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

which yields
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda^{\beta}u^{(n)}(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x)=u(t,x)
, as required.
∎

In order to study the self-similar solution from Lemma
4.3
, it is clearly enough to study the case
t=1
. Namely, denoting
U(x):=u(1,x)
we get

\displaystyle u(t,x)=t^{-\beta/\alpha}U(t^{-1/\alpha}x).

(4.5)

In particular, this implies
u^{*}(t,x)=U(x)
, and the Duhamel formula (
2.14
) takes the form

U(x)=M\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p(1,x,y)}{|y|^{\beta}}dy+\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}\nabla_{x}p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)U(w)^{q+1}\,dw\,dr.

(4.6)

4.2
Pointwise estimates and asymptotics

From Proposition
3.5
and the estimate (
4.2
) we have the following upper bound

\displaystyle U(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|\tilde{x}|)^{\beta-1}}.

(4.7)

However, it is not optimal. In particular, the right-hand side does not depend on
x_{1}
. In the sequel, we provide precise two-sided estimates. We start with
L^{p}
bounds.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\gamma\in(d/\beta,\infty]
,
U\in L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

For
\gamma=\infty
the result follows by (
4.7
). Furthermore, by monotonicity of
u^{(n)}
, (
4.7
), Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
2.3
, for
\gamma\in\big((d-1)/(\beta-1),\infty\big)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U^{\gamma}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[u^{(n)}(1,x)]^{\gamma}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|\tilde{x}|)^{(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)}}u^{(n)}(1,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|\tilde{x}|)^{(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)}}P_{1}(u_{0}^{(n)})(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|\tilde{x}|)^{(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)}}\frac{1}{(1+|\tilde{x}|+|x_{1}|)^{\beta}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\beta-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(1+|\tilde{x}|)^{(\beta-1)\gamma}}\,d\tilde{x}<\infty.

(4.8)

Now, let
\gamma\in(\frac{d}{\beta},\frac{d-1}{\beta-1})
. By the integral Minkowski inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{1}u_{0}\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{r^{d-\beta}}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}p(1-r,rw)[U(w+\frac{x}{r})]^{1+q}\,dw\,dr\right)^{\gamma}dx\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}+\|U^{1+q}\|_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{r^{d-\beta+d/\gamma}}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}p(1-r,rw)\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle=c_{\gamma}+\|U\|_{\gamma(1+q)}^{1+q}\int_{0}^{1}r^{-\beta+d/\gamma}(1-r)^{-1/\alpha}\,dr\leq c_{0}\left(1+\|U\|_{\gamma(1+q)}^{1+q}\right).

(4.9)

Thus, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gamma(1+q)^{m}>(d-1)/(\beta-1)
, by iterating (
4.9
) we get

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq c_{m}\left(1+\|U\|_{\gamma(1+q)^{m+1}}^{(1+q)^{m+1}}\right).

(4.10)

We take the smallest
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that that
\gamma(1+q)^{m+1}>(d-1)/(\beta-1)
. Then, by (
4.10
) and (
4.8
),
U\in L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

∎

The next step is to show that
U
vanishes at infinity.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|x|>R}U(x)=0.

Proof.

Estimates (
2.4
) and Lemma
2.3
applied to (
4.6
) give us

\displaystyle U(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{M}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{r^{d-\beta}}\frac{p(1-r,x,rw)}{{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}+|x-rw|}U(w)^{q+1}\,dw\,dr.

(4.11)

Let us split the integral above into

I_{1}+I_{2}:=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\ldots)dr+\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}(\ldots)dr,

for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
. First, note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|y|^{-\beta}dy_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y_{1}^{2}+|\tilde{y}|^{2})^{-\beta/2}dy=c|\tilde{y}|^{1-\beta}.

Hence, by (
3.3
), (
4.1
) and Lemma
2.3
we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(1-r)^{-2/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p^{(d-1)}(1,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})|y|^{-\beta}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}p^{(d-1)}(1,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})|\tilde{y}|^{1-\beta}d\tilde{y}\lesssim\varepsilon\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}.

Next, using Hölder inequality and the bound (
2.5
) we get

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}{r^{d-\beta}}\int_{B(0,|x|/2)}\frac{p(1-r,x,rw)}{|x|}\|U\|_{\infty}^{q+1}\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{{r^{d-\beta}}}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}\int_{B(0,|x|/2)^{c}}p(1-r,x,rw)U(w)\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|U\|_{\infty}^{q+1}}{|x|}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}{r^{-\beta}}dr

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{{r^{d-\beta}}}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}\|p(1-r,x,r(\cdot))\|_{2d/(2d-\alpha+1)}\|\mathbf{1}_{\{B(0,|x|/2)^{c}\}}U\|_{2d/(\alpha-1)}\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|U\|_{\infty}^{q+1}}{\varepsilon^{\beta}|x|}+\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\|\mathbf{1}_{\{B(0,|x|/2)^{c}\}}U\|_{2d/(\alpha-1)}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{{r^{(\alpha-1)/2-\beta}}}{(1-r)^{1-(\alpha-1)/2\alpha}}\,dr,

which tends to zero as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
by virtue of Lemma
4.4
applied with
\gamma=2d/(\alpha-1)>d/\beta
. Thus, the integral in (
4.11
) is arbitrarly small for large
|x|
, which ends the proof.
∎

We are now ready to derive the upper bound of
U
.

Proposition 4.6
.

There is a constant
C=C(d,\alpha,b,M,\beta)
such that

\displaystyle U(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}},\ \ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Recall that
u^{(n)}
is a solution to the problem (
1.1
) with initial condition given by (
4.3
) and
\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(t,x)
is defined by (
2.1
). Note also that
\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(t,x)\nearrow u^{*}(t,x)=U(x)
. From (
1.8
) and Lemma
2.3
we have

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t>0\\
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{subarray}}{\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(t,x)}(1+|x|)^{\beta}<\infty.

It is enough to show that
A_{n}
’s are uniformly bounded.
Duhamel formula (
2.14
) combined with (
2.9
) give us

\displaystyle\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(t,x)\lesssim

\displaystyle\,C_{0}\left(\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}+I_{n}(t,x)\right),

where
C_{0}=C_{0}(d,\alpha,b,M)>0
is some constant and

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}+|x-rw|}\left[\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(r^{\alpha}t,w)\right]^{(\alpha-1+\beta)/\beta}\,dw\,dr.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1/2
. By Lemma
4.5
, we may and do choose
R>0
such that
|U(z)|<\varepsilon^{\beta/(\alpha-1)}
for
|z|\geq R
and such that
{\|U\|^{(\alpha-1)/\beta}_{\infty}}/{|x|}<\varepsilon
for
|x|>2R
.

Now let
|x|>2R
. Since
|x-rw|\geq|x|/2
for
|w|<R
and
0<r<1
, we get

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B(0,R)}r^{d-\beta}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{|x|/2}\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(r^{\alpha}t,w)\|U\|_{\infty}^{(\alpha-1)/\beta}dw\,dr

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B(0,R)^{c}}r^{d-\beta}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}}\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(r^{\alpha}t,w)\left(\sup_{|z|\geq R}U(z)\right)^{(\alpha-1)/\beta}dw\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon A_{n}\left(2\|U\|_{\infty}^{(\alpha-1)/\beta}+1\right)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}(1-r^{\alpha})^{-1/\alpha}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{(1+|w|)^{\beta}}\,dw\,dr.

By (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{(1+|w|)^{\beta}}\,dw=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{-d}\frac{p(r^{-\alpha}-1,r^{-1}x,w)}{(1+|w|)^{\beta}}\,dw\approx\frac{r^{\beta-d}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}.

(4.12)

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon A_{n}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{\alpha})^{-1/\alpha}dr\approx\frac{\varepsilon A_{n}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}.

On the other hand, for
|x|\leq 2R
,

\displaystyle\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(t,x)\leq U(x)\leq\|U\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|U\|_{\infty}(1+2R)^{\beta}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}.

Finally, there is a constant
c=c(d,\alpha,b,M,\beta)
such that for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\left(u^{(n)}\right)^{*}(t,x)\leq\frac{c}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}\left(1+\varepsilon A_{n}+\|U\|_{\infty}(1+2R)^{\beta}\right),

and consequently

A_{n}\leq c(1+\varepsilon A_{n}+\|U\|_{\infty}(1+2R)^{\beta}).

Taking
\varepsilon=1/(2c)
, we obtain

A_{n}\leq{2(1+\|U\|_{\infty}(1+2R)^{\beta})},

as required.

∎

Next we provide an asymptotic behaviour of
U
, which is driven by the functions
h_{n}
below. First, let us define the kernel

k(x,w)=\alpha\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-\beta}\nabla_{x}p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)dr,

and then, for a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the operator

\displaystyle(Kf)(x)

\displaystyle=P_{1}u_{0}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k(x,w)f(w)|f(w)|^{q}\,dw.

In view of (
4.6
), we clearly have
KU=U
. Furthermore, we put
h_{0}\equiv 0
and

\displaystyle h_{n}(x)={K}^{n}h_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},n\geq 1.

In particular,

\displaystyle h_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{1}u_{0}(x),

\displaystyle h_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{1}u_{0}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k(x,w)[P_{1}u_{0}(w)]^{q+1}dw.

One can show inductively that for any
n\geq 0
there is a constant
c_{n}>0
such that

\displaystyle|h_{n}(x)|\leq\frac{c_{n}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}},\ \ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.13)

Indeed, by (
4.12
) we get

\displaystyle|h_{n+1}(x)|

\displaystyle=|Kh_{n}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{1}u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}|\nabla_{x}p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)||h_{n}(w)|^{q+1}\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}+c_{n}^{q+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{d-\beta}}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p(1-r,x,rw)}{(1+|w|)^{\beta}}dw\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{c_{n}^{q+1}+1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}.

Note that the functions
h_{n}
might be considered as Picard approximations of
U
.
The next result supplements Proposition
4.6
precisely describing behaviour of
U(x)
for large
|x|
. Although asymptotics of the first two orders only are mentioned in Theorem
1.2
, we provide below more general result, as the arguments are similar.

Proposition 4.7
.

For every
n\geq 0
we have

|U(x)-h_{n}(x)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\lesssim}}{(1+|x|)^{-[\beta+n(\alpha-1)]\wedge[d+\alpha+1]}}.

Proof.

We will use induction. For
n=0
the assertion follows from Theorem
4.6
. Consider some
n\geq 0
. Since
U
and every
h_{n}
are bounded, we may focus only on large
|x|
.

Recall that for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma>1
we denote
a^{\left\langle\gamma\right\rangle}=a|a|^{\gamma-1}
. Note that
\frac{d}{dx}x^{\left\langle\gamma\right\rangle}=\gamma|x|^{\gamma-1}
. Hence, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma>0
we have

\displaystyle\left|b^{\left\langle\gamma+1\right\rangle}-a^{\left\langle\gamma+1\right\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{a}^{b}\frac{d}{dx}x^{\left\langle\gamma+1\right\rangle}dx\right|=(\gamma+1)\left|\int_{a}^{b}|x|^{\gamma}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma+1)|b-a|(|a|+|b|)^{\gamma}.

(4.14)

Thus, by Theorem
4.6
, (
4.13
) and (
4.14
)

\displaystyle|U(x)-h_{n+1}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|k(x,w)|\left|U(w)-h_{n}(w)\right|\left(|U(w)|+|h_{{n}}(w)|\right)^{q}\,dw

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\lesssim}}C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|k(x,w)|\frac{1}{(1+|w|)^{[\beta+n(\alpha-1)]\wedge[d+\alpha+1]}}\frac{1}{(1+|w|)^{\beta q}}dw\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}+|x-rw|}\;\frac{1}{(1+|w|)^{[\beta+(n+1)(\alpha-1)]\wedge[d+2\alpha}]}dw\,dr.

Let
a_{n}=\beta+(n+1)(\alpha-1)
. In the case
{a_{n}}<d
, we bound
|x-rw|
by zero and take advantage of (
4.12
). Assume therefore
{a_{n}}\geq d
. Since
|x-rw|\geq|x|/2
for
w\in B(0,|x|/2r)
and
0<r<1
, we split the inner integral above and estimate (applying (
2.4
)) it as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\ldots)dw

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\lesssim}}\int_{B(0,|x|/2r)}\frac{1-r^{\alpha}}{((1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{d+\alpha+1}(1+|w|)^{{a_{n}\wedge[d+2\alpha]}}}dw

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\int_{B(0,|x|/2r)^{c}}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}(1+|x|/r)^{{a_{n}\wedge[d+2\alpha]}}}dw

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d+\alpha+1}}\int_{B(0,|x|/2{r})}\frac{dw}{(1+|w|)^{{a_{n}\wedge[d+2\alpha]}}}+\frac{r^{-d+\left({a_{n}}\wedge[d+2\alpha]\right)}}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}|x|^{{a_{n}\wedge[d+2\alpha]}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{1+\mathbf{1}_{{a_{n}}=d}\ln|x/2r|}{|x|^{d+\alpha+1}}+\frac{{1}}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}|x|^{{a_{n}\wedge[d+\alpha+1]}}}

\displaystyle{\lesssim}\frac{1+{\ln(1/r)}}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}|x|^{{a_{n}}\wedge[d+\alpha+1]}}.

Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle|U(x)-h_{n+1}(x)|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{1}{|x|^{[\beta+(n+1)(\alpha-1)]\wedge[d+\alpha+1]}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{d-\beta}{(1+\ln(1/r))}}{(1-r^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}}dr

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\approx}}\frac{1}{|x|^{[\beta+(n+1)(\alpha-1)]\wedge[d+\alpha+1]}},

which ends the proof.
∎

Proposition
4.7
directly imples the lower bound for
U(x)
.

Corollary 4.8
.

We have
U(x)\gtrsim\dfrac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.7
with
n=1
and Lemma
2.3
applied to Proposition
4.6
imply the lower bound for large
|x|
. For any fixed
R>0
and
|x|<R
we employ (
1.8
) and bound

\displaystyle U(x)

\displaystyle\geq u^{(1)}(1,x)\gtrsim(M\wedge 1)(P_{1}\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)})(x)\approx\int_{|w|<1}\frac{dw}{(1+|w-x|)^{d+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{(2+R)^{d+\alpha}}\geq\frac{1}{(2+R)^{d+\alpha}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}},

which proves the assertion.
∎

4.3
Gradient of
U

Eventually, we turn our attention to the gradient of
U
. Note that the estimates in (
4.15
), that we are going to prove, coincides with the ones of
U
from Proposition
4.6
. Nevertheless, it is not true for the whole range of arguments of the solution
u(t,x)
, as the scaling property of the gradient
\nabla u(t,x)
is slightly different, i.e. we have

\displaystyle(\nabla_{x}u)(t,x)=\nabla_{x}\left(\lambda^{\beta}u(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x)\right)=\lambda^{\beta+1}(\nabla_{x}u)(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x),

and consequently, for
\lambda=t^{-1/\alpha}
, we have (cf. (
4.5
))

\displaystyle(\nabla_{x}u)(t,x)=t^{-(\beta+1)/\alpha}(\nabla_{x}u)(1,x/t^{1/\alpha})=t^{-(\beta+1)/\alpha}(\nabla U)(x/t^{1/\alpha}).

Proposition 4.9
.

We have
U\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and

\displaystyle|\nabla U(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}.

(4.15)

Proof.

By
[
MR2407204
, Theorem 3.5]
, since

|\nabla u_{0}^{(n)}(x)|\leq\frac{M\beta}{|x|^{\beta+1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{1/n<|x|<n\}}
,
we have

|\nabla u^{(n)}(t,x)|\leq c(1+t)(1+|x|)^{-d-\alpha-1}
,
where
c
may depend on
n
. Hence, for every
n
we have

A_{n}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
0<t\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|t^{1/\alpha}(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\beta}\nabla u^{(n)}(t,x)\right|<\infty.

We will show that

\displaystyle A:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}<\infty.

(4.16)

From (
1.2
) and by applying integration by parts, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
we get

\displaystyle\nabla u^{(n)}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\nabla P_{t}u^{(n)}_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{(1-\varepsilon)t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\left(b\cdot\nabla p(t-s,x,z)\right)[u^{(n)}(s,z)]^{q+1}\,dz\,ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +(q+1)\int_{(1-\varepsilon)t}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla_{z}p(t-s,x,z)u^{(n)}(s,z)^{q}\nabla u^{(n)}(s,z)\,dz\,ds.

(4.17)

Let us note that the first integrand is not absolutely integrable on
\big((1-\varepsilon)t,t\big)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which explains the above decomposition.
We estimate the term
\nabla P_{t}u^{(n)}_{0}(x)
as follows

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{t}u^{(n)}_{0}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)u^{(n)}_{0}(y)dy\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}p(t,x,y)u^{(n)}_{0}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)|y|^{-\beta}dy\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1/\alpha}\left(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|\right)^{\beta}},

where we used (
2.9
) and Lemma
2.3
.
Next, applying this, (
2.9
), (
4.5
), and Proposition
4.6
to (
4.17
), we obtain for
0<t\leq 1

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(n)}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1/\alpha}(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{0}^{(1-\varepsilon)t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{2/\alpha}}\left(\frac{\|U\|_{\infty}}{s^{\beta/\alpha}}\right)^{q}P_{t-s}((s^{1/\alpha}+|\cdot|)^{-\beta})(x)ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +A_{n}\int_{(1-\varepsilon)t}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}\left(\frac{\|U\|_{\infty}}{s^{\beta/\alpha}}\right)^{q}s^{-1/\alpha}P_{t-s}((s^{1/\alpha}+|\cdot|)^{-\beta})(x)\,ds.

By Lemma
2.3
,
P_{t-s}(1/(s^{1/\alpha}+|\cdot|)^{\beta})(x)\approx 1/(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\beta}
, and therefore

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(n)}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1/\alpha}(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\left[1+\int_{0}^{1-\varepsilon}\frac{\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}}{(1-r)^{2/\alpha}r^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}}dr+A_{n}\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}r}dr\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1/\alpha}(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{\beta}}\left[1+\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\left(\varepsilon^{-2/\alpha}+\varepsilon^{1-1/\alpha}A_{n}\right)\right].

Consequently there is a constant
c
not depending on
n
and
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle A_{n}\leq c\left(1+\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\left(\varepsilon^{-2/\alpha}+\varepsilon^{1-1/\alpha}A_{n}\right)\right).

Thus, taking
\varepsilon
small enough, we get
A_{n}\leq c\left(1+\varepsilon^{-2/\alpha}\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\right)/\left(1-c\|U\|_{\infty}^{q}\varepsilon^{1-1/\alpha}\right)
, which proves (
4.16
).
In particular, this implies

\displaystyle\left|U(x+z)-U(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\nearrow\infty}|u^{(n)}(1,x+z)-u^{(n)}(1,x)|

\displaystyle\leq|z|\sup_{0\leq v\leq 1}|\nabla u^{(n)}(1,x+vz)|\lesssim\frac{A|z|}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}},

(4.18)

whenever
|z|<1
. Let
e_{1},\ldots e_{n}
be the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Now, rewriting (
4.6
) in a similar manner as it was done in (
4.17
), for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
1\leq k\leq d
and
h\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{U(x+he_{k})-U(x)}{|h|}=M\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{p(1,x+he_{k}-y)-p(1,x-y)}{|h||y|^{\beta}}dy

(4.19)

\displaystyle+\alpha\int_{0}^{1-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}b\cdot\frac{\nabla_{w}p(1-r^{\alpha},x+he_{k},rw)-\nabla_{w}p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)}{|h|}[U(w)]^{q+1}\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle+\alpha\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}b\cdot\nabla_{w}p(1-r^{\alpha},0,rw)\frac{[U(w+(x+he_{k})/r)]^{q+1}-[U(w+x/r)]^{q+1}}{|h|}\,dw\,dr.

By (
2.9
), we may pass with
|h|
to zero under the first two integrals. Furthermore, (
4.18
) together with (
2.3
) and (
2.10
) let us bound the third integral for
|h|<1-\varepsilon
by

\displaystyle C\|U\|^{q}\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}\frac{p(1-r^{\alpha},0,rw)}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}\frac{A}{(1+|w+x/r|)^{\beta}}\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle=AC\|U\|^{q}\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{r^{-\beta}}{(1-r)^{1/\alpha}}P_{r^{-\alpha}-1}\left((1+|\cdot|)^{-\beta}\right)(xr^{-1})\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{A}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}(1-r)^{-1/\alpha}\,dr\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{1-1/\alpha}}{1-1/\alpha}A.

This gives us

\displaystyle\limsup_{|h|\rightarrow 0}\frac{U(x+he_{k})-U(x)}{|h|}-\liminf_{|h|\rightarrow 0}\frac{U(x+he_{k})-U(x)}{|h|}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-1/\alpha}A,

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Therefore the limit exists, and is bounded by (
4.15
) due to (
4.18
). Furthermore, by (
4.19
) for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we may express the gradient by

\displaystyle\nabla U(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla P_{1}u_{0}(x)+\alpha\int_{0}^{1-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}\nabla\left(b\cdot\nabla_{w}p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)\right)U^{q+1}(w)\,dw\,dr

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\alpha\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-\beta}b\cdot\nabla_{w}p(1-r^{\alpha},x,rw)U^{q}(w)\nabla U(w)\,dw\,dr,

which ensures its continuity by the estimates of
U
and
\nabla U
.
∎

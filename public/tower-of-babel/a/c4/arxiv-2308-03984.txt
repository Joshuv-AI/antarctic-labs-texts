Global Diffusive expansion of Boltzmann Equation in exterior Domain Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Junhwa Jung

Abstract
The study of flows over an obstacle is one of the fundamental problems in fluids. In this paper we establish the global validity of the diffusive expansion for the Boltzmann equations to the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system in an exterior domain. To overcome the well-known difficulty of the lack of Poincare’s inequality in the unbounded domain, we develop a new L 2 − L 3 − L 6 L^{2}-L^{3}-L^{6} splitting to extend the L 2 − L ∞ L^{2}-L^{\infty} framework into the unbounded domain.

Introduction

1.1
Background

The purpose of this paper is to establish the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier expansion from the unsteady Boltzmann equation with diffusive boundary conditions in the exterior domain. The rescaled Boltzmann equation with small Knudsen and Mach numbers is given by the following equation:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Q(F,F),\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega^{c}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

(1)

\displaystyle F|_{\gamma_{-}}=\mathscr{P}^{w}_{\gamma}(F)

(2)

\displaystyle F(t,x,v)|_{t=0}=F_{0}(x,v)\geq 0,\quad\text{on }\Omega^{c}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(3)

where
F(t,x,v)
is the distribution of particles at time
t\geq 0
, position
x
and velocity
v
. The Boltzmann collision operator
Q
can be written as

\displaystyle Q(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{+}(f,g)-Q^{-}(f,g)

(4)

\displaystyle Q^{+}(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbf{S}^{2}}B(\omega,{v-u})f(v^{\prime})g(u^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}u

(5)

\displaystyle Q^{-}(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(v)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbf{S}^{2}}B(\omega,{v-u})g(u)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}u,

(6)

where
v^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime}
are defined as the velocities of particles after collision

\displaystyle v^{\prime}=v-\omega(v-u)\cdot\omega,\quad u^{\prime}=u+\omega(v-u)\cdot\omega,

(7)

and
B(\omega,{v-u})
is the cross section for hard potentials with Grad’s angular cut off, so that
\int_{\{\left\lvert\omega\right\rvert=1\}}B(\omega,V)\mathrm{d}\omega\lesssim\left\lvert V\right\rvert^{\theta}
for
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, depending on the interaction potential.

Throughout this paper,
\Omega=\{x:\xi(x)<0\}
is a general bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Denote its boundary as
\partial\Omega=\{x:\xi(x)=0\}
, where
\xi(x)
is a

\partial\Omega

\partial\Omega

\displaystyle n(x)=-\frac{\nabla\xi(x)}{\left\lvert\nabla\xi(x)\right\rvert},

\partial\Omega

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}=\left\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v\gtrless 0\right\},

(8)

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=\left\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v=0\right\}.

(9)

The interaction of the gas with the boundary
\partial\Omega
is given by the diffusive boundary condition, defined as follows

M_{\rho,u,T}:=\frac{\rho}{(2\pi T)^{\frac{3}{2}}}exp\left[-\frac{\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert^{2}}{2T}\right],

be the local Maxwellian with density

\mu=M_{1,0,1}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}exp\left[-\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}}{2}\right].

On the boundary,

satisfies the diffusive reflection condition, defined as

where

\mathscr{P}^{w}_{\gamma}(F)(t,x,v):=M^{w}(x,v)\int_{\gamma_{+}}F(t,x,w)(n(x)\cdot w)\mathrm{d}w,

with wall Maxwellian defined as

M^{w}=\sqrt{2\pi}\mu,\quad\int_{\{v\cdot n\gtrless 0\}}M^{w}(v)\left\lvert n\cdot v\right\rvert\mathrm{d}v=1.

Since the domain is a complement of compact domain, we need to specify the condition at infinity. In this paper, we assume in a suitable sense

\lim_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to\infty}F(x,v)=\mu(v).

In the study of hydrodynamic limit, Hilbert proposed Hilbert expansion as follows

\displaystyle F=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}f_{n}.

1.5
Mathematical difficulty

One of the major difficulties in the boundary value problem of the Boltzmann equation is controlling the grazing sets, denoted as
\gamma_{0}
from (
8

L^{2}-L^{\infty}

Based on the energy estimate and the positivity of

\displaystyle\left\lVert R(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P})R\right\rVert_{\nu}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\Gamma(R,\partial_{t}R)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+1.

(37)

The main nonlinear term is

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\Gamma(\mathbf{P}R,\partial_{t}\mathbf{P}R)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}.

(38)

One of the major difficulties in the

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}f(\tau)\right\rVert_{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\lesssim

\displaystyle G(t)-G(s)+\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\frac{g(\tau)}{\sqrt{\nu}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\left\lvert r(\tau)\right\rvert_{2,-}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P})f(\tau)\right\rVert_{\nu}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\left\lvert(1-P_{\gamma})f\right\rvert_{2,-}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

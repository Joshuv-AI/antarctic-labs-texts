1.
Introduction

In the seminal paper
[
13
]
, Kechris, Solecki and Todorcevic initiated the study of descriptive combinatorics of locally finite Borel graphs. In particular, they considered general bounds for Borel chromatic numbers of locally finite Borel graphs. The Borel edge chromatic number was also considered, primarily as the Borel chromatic number of the dual graph, and they showed that any locally finite Borel graph
G
has countable Borel edge chromatic number
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(G)
, and if
G
is of bounded degree
\leq k
, then
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(G)\leq 2k-1
. Subsequently, researchers computed the Borel chromatic numbers and Borel edge chromatic numbers of various Schreier graphs of countable group actions. For example, in
[
14
]
, Marks studied combinatorics of free products of two marked groups, and showed that the Borel chromatic number
\chi_{B}(F(2^{\mathbb{F}_{n}}))=2n+1
, where
F(2^{\mathbb{F}_{n}})
denotes the Schreier graph on the free part of the Bernoulli shift action of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
, the free group with
n
generators, on
2^{\mathbb{F}_{n}}
. For an overview of the entire field of descriptive combinatorics we refer the reader to the survey
[
12
]
by Kechris and Marks.

The continuous chromatic number was first studied by the first author of the present paper and Jackson. In
[
7
]
, it was shown that there is a continuous proper
4
-coloring of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
for each
n>1
. Later in
[
8
]
, it was shown that there is no continuous proper
3
-coloring of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
for each
n>1
. Thus the continuous chromatic number
\chi_{c}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))=4
for each
n>1
. In contrast, it was shown in
[
4
]
and
[
9
]
by different methods that the Borel chromatic number
\chi_{B}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))=3
for each
n>1
. This was the first time that the Borel and continuous chromatic numbers of a locally finite Borel graph were observed to be different.

A similar phenomenon occurs when considering the Borel and continuous edge chromatic numbers of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. The Borel edge chromatic numbers were studied independently in
[
1
]
(for
n=2
),
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
. The conclusion is that for the Schreier graph of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
with the standard generating set, the Borel edge chromatic number
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))
equals the usual edge chromatic number
\chi^{\prime}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))
, which is
2n
. It is worth noting that Weilacher
[
15
]
showed that for any symmetric generating set
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
where
S
does not contain the identity, letting
F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
be the corresponding Schreier graph, we have
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))=\chi^{\prime}(F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))=|S|
. Thus the computation of the Borel edge chromatic numbers of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
was complete. As for the continuous edge chromatic number, we only had, from
[
8
]
, the result for
n=2
that
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}))=5
for the standard generating set. This already showed that the Borel and continuous edge chromatic numbers could differ, but left the computation of a lot of cases open.

In this paper, we complete this line of research by showing that, for any symmetric generating set
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
not containing the identity, the continuous edge chromatic number
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))=\chi^{\prime}(F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))+1=|S|+1
. In particular, we have
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))=2n+1
. This answers Question 10.3 of
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
and any symmetric generating set
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
not containing the identity, let
G=F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. Then
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)+1=|S|+1
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we fix the notation to be used throughout the paper. In Section
3
we prove the lower bound for the continuous edge chromatic number. Then in Section
4
we construct a proper edge coloring to witness Theorem
1.1
, first by assuming
S
to be the standard generating set and then generalizing to the general case.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we define our basic notions and fix notation. We use standard concepts and terminology from graph theory, which can be found in
[
2
]
.

As usual, a
graph

G
is a pair
(V(G),E(G))
, where
V(G)
is a set and
E(G)
is a set of unordered pairs of distinct elements of
V(G)
. Thus our graphs are
undirected
. Here
V(G)
is the set of
vertices
of
G
, and
E(G)
is the set of
edges
of
G
. Throughout the paper, when there is no danger of confusion, the vertex operator
V
of
V(G)
is sometimes omitted. If
x,y\in V(G)
are distinct and
e=\{x,y\}\in E(G)
, we also write
e=(x,y)=(y,x)
and say that
x
and
y
are
incident
with the edge
e
. For any
x\in V(G)
, the
degree
of
x
is defined as the number of distinct edges incident with
x
. A graph
G
is
locally finite
if for all
x\in V(G)
, the degree of
x
is finite. If
x,y\in V(G)
are distinct, then a
path

p
from
x
to
y
is a sequence
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
of distinct vertices of
G
such that
x_{0}=x
,
x_{k}=y
, and for all
0\leq i<k
,
(x_{i},x_{i+1})\in E(G)
; here
k
is the
length
of the path
p
.

For a graph
G
and a set
\kappa
of colors, a
proper edge
\kappa
-coloring
of
G
is a map
c:E(G)\to\kappa
such that
c((x,y))\neq c((z,w))
if the distinct edges
(x,y),(z,w)
have a vertex in common. In the sequel, we write
c(x,y)
for
c((x,y))
for simplicity. The
edge chromatic number
of
G
, denoted by
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, is the least cardinality of a set
\kappa
such that there exists a proper edge
\kappa
-coloring for
G
. When the graph
G
is a topological graph, i.e., when
V(G)
is a topological space, we may consider
continuous
(or
Borel
) proper edge colorings of
G
, and define its
continuous edge chromatic number
, denoted as
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(G)
, and its
Borel edge chromatic number
, denoted as
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(G)
. Similarly, we define proper (vertex) colorings and the continuous and Borel chromatic numbers, denoted by
\chi_{c}(G)
and
\chi_{B}(G)
respectively.

On any locally finite graph
G
, we define the
path distance

\rho
on
G
as follows. If
x,y\in V(G)
are in the same connected component (i.e. there is a path from
x
to
y
), then let
\rho(x,y)
be the length of a shortest path from
x
to
y
; otherwise let
\rho(x,y)=\infty
. Recall that a locally finite graph
G
is
regular
if the degrees of all vertices of
G
are the same.

An important class of regular topological graphs consists of the graphs induced by actions of finitely generated groups. A
marked group
is a pair
(\Gamma,S)
, where
\Gamma
is a group and
S
is a finite generating set of
\Gamma
. Usually we also require
S=S^{-1}
and
1_{\Gamma}\not\in S
. In this paper this is assumed tacitly. When the generating set is standard or otherwise understood, we omit specifying the set
S
and say that
\Gamma
is a marked group. For example, for any
n\geq 1
,
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has a standard generating set
\{\pm e_{i}\colon 1\leq i\leq n\}
where each
e_{i}
has the
i
-th coordinate
1
and
j
-th coordinate
0
for
j\neq i
. The
Cayley graph

C(\Gamma,S)
of a marked group
(\Gamma,S)
is defined by

V(C(\Gamma,S))=\Gamma

and

E(C(\Gamma,S))=\{(g,h)\in\Gamma^{2}\,:\,\exists s\in S\ h=sg\}.

When there is an action of a marked group
\Gamma
on a set
X
, the
Schreier graph
of the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
on
X
, denoted
G(\Gamma,S,X)
, is defined by

V(G(\Gamma,S,X))=X

and

E(G(\Gamma,S,X))=\{(x,y)\in X^{2}\,:\,\exists s\in S\ y=s\cdot x\}.

The Schreier graph will be particularly nice when the action is free; in this case the Schreier graph on each orbit of the action will be a copy of the Cayley graph
C(\Gamma,S)
. Let
F(\Gamma,X)=\{x\in X\colon\forall g\neq 1_{\Gamma}\ (g\cdot x\neq x)\}
be the
free part
of the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
.
F(\Gamma,X)
is an invariant set and the induced action of
\Gamma
on
F(\Gamma,X)
is free. We denote by
F(\Gamma,S,X)
the Schreier graph on
F(\Gamma,X)
. We write
F(X)
for
F(\Gamma,X)
and
F(S,X)
for
F(\Gamma,S,X)
if the group
\Gamma
and its action on
X
are understood. Furthermore, when the generating set
S
is standard or otherwise understood, we will abuse notation and simply write
F(X)
for the Schreier graph
F(S,X)
.

In this paper, we will work with the setup where
X
is a Polish space, i.e., a separable and completely metrizable space,
\Gamma
is a finitely generated group with the discrete topology, and the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is continuous.

The Bernoulli shift action induces a natural Schreier graph. Let
A
be a finite set with the discrete topology. Assume
|A|\geq 2
. For a countable discrete group
\Gamma
, the space
A^{\Gamma}
is equipped with the usual product topology and is homeomorphic to the Cantor space
2^{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, which is compact Polish.

The
Bernoulli shift action

\Gamma\curvearrowright A^{\Gamma}
is defined by

(g\cdot x)(h)=x(hg)

for
g,h\in\Gamma
and
x\in A^{\Gamma}
.
This action is continuous. The free part
F(\Gamma,A^{\Gamma})
is an invariant dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
A^{\Gamma}
, hence is a Polish space.

We are now ready to compute the continuous edge chromatic number
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))
for a generating set
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

3.
The Lower Bound

In this section we prove that for any generating set
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))>\chi^{\prime}(F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))
. We will need some results of
[
8
]
. To state the results we need to review some related concepts.

Let
(\Gamma,S)
be a marked group. We will consider the Bernoulli shift action of
\Gamma
on
A^{\Gamma}
for some finite set
A
. A
\Gamma
-subshift

Y
is a closed invariant subset of
A^{\Gamma}
. A
\Gamma
-pattern
is a map
p:F\to A
for some finite
F\subseteq\Gamma
. If
p
is a
\Gamma
-pattern and
x\in A^{\Gamma}
, we say that
p

occurs
in
x
if
p\subseteq g\cdot x
for some
g\in\Gamma
. Note that this is an invariant notion, that is, if
p
occurs in
x
then
p
occurs in any
h\cdot x
for
h\in\Gamma
. A
\Gamma
-subshift of finite type
is a
\Gamma
-subshift
Y\subseteq A^{\Gamma}
for which there is a finite set
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}
of
\Gamma
-patterns such that for any
x\in A^{\Gamma}
,
x\in Y
if and only if none of the patterns
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
occur in
x
. In this case we say that
Y
is
described
by
(A;p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
.

A graph
H
is a
(\Gamma,S)
-graph
if there is an action of
\Gamma
on
V(H)
such that
H=G(\Gamma,S,V(H))
. The Schreier graphs
G(\Gamma,S,X)
and
F(\Gamma,S,X)
are examples of
(\Gamma,S)
-graphs, but we will also consider finite
(\Gamma,S)
-graphs. When the generating set
S
is standard,
(\Gamma,S)
-graphs are also simply called
\Gamma
-graphs
.

We will work with the following example of a finite
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graph. Given positive integers
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
, the vertex set of the graph
T_{q_{1},\dots,q_{n}}
is the quotient of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by the
relation
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\sim(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
if and only if
a_{1}\equiv b_{1}\!\mod q_{1}
,
\dots,a_{n}\equiv b_{n}\!\mod q_{n}
. The group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
acts naturally on
T_{q_{1},\dots,q_{n}}
, and this action induces a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graph on
T_{q_{1},\dots,q_{n}}
with the standard generating set of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Note that
T_{q_{1},\dots,q_{n}}
has cardinality
q_{1}\cdots q_{n}
.

If
Y
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-subshift of finite type described by
(A;p_{1},\dots p_{k})
, then we say that a map

\varphi\colon T_{q_{1},\dots,q_{n}}\to A

respects

Y
if none of
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
occur in
\varphi\circ\pi\in A^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
, where
\pi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to T_{q_{1},\dots,q_{n}}
is the quotient map.

If a group
\Gamma
acts on both
X
and
Y
, we say a map
\theta\colon X\to Y
is

equivariant
if
\theta(g\cdot x)=g\cdot\theta(x)
for all
g\in\Gamma
and
x\in X
.

Theorem 3.1
.

(
[
8
, Theorem 2.7.1]
)
Let
A
be a finite set and let
Y\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-subshift of finite type. If there is a continuous equivariant map

\theta\colon F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})\to Y
, then for all
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\geq 2
and all sufficiently large
k
there is

\varphi\colon T_{q^{k}_{1},\dots,q^{k}_{n}}\to A
which respects
Y
.

Recall that a
matching
of a graph
G
is a subset
M\subseteq E(G)
such that every vertex of
G
is incident with at most one of the edges of
M
. A matching
M
of
G
is a
perfect matching
if every vertex of
G
is incident with exactly one edge in
M
. We may view a perfect matching of a graph
G
as a function
\mu:V(G)\to V(G)
with the property that for any
x\in V(G)
,
(x,\mu(x))\in E(G)
and
\mu^{2}(x)=x
. In this sense we may speak of continuous perfect matchings for topological graphs
G
. The following theorem is a generalization of
[
8
, Theorem 3.1.3]
with a similar proof.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
S
be a generating set of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then there is no continuous perfect matching of
F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
.

Proof.

Suppose
S=\{\pm s_{1},\pm s_{2},\cdots,\pm s_{m}\}
, where none of the
s_{i}
are the identity

\bar{0}
. Rewrite
S=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{2m}\}
. Assume a continuous perfect matching
\mu
of
F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
exists. Note that the vertex set of
F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
is the same as that of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. Define

\eta:F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})\rightarrow S
by letting
\eta(x)
to be the unique element
g\in S
such that
\mu(x)=g\cdot x
. Then
\eta
is continuous.

Now define

\theta:F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})\rightarrow S^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

by
\theta(x)(g)=\eta(g\cdot x)
for
x\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then
\theta
is a continuous equivariant map.

Let
Y\subseteq S^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
be the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-subshift of finite type described by
2m(2m-1)
many
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-patterns
\{p_{i,j}\colon 1\leq i,j\leq 2m,i\neq j\}
, where

p_{i,j}:\{\bar{0},u_{i}\}\rightarrow S
is defined by
p_{i,j}(\bar{0})=u_{i}
and
p_{i,j}(u_{i})=-u_{j}
. Then we in fact have
\theta:F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})\rightarrow Y
.

Let
q_{1},q_{2},\cdots,q_{n}
be odd. By Theorem
3.1
, for some large enough

there exists
\varphi\colon T_{q_{1}^{k},q_{2}^{k},\cdots,q_{n}^{k}}\rightarrow S
which respects
Y
. Consider the

-graph with vertex set
T_{q_{1}^{k},q_{2}^{k},\cdots,q_{n}^{k}}
, and denote it as

. Here since the group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
naturally acts on

, the action of each element

on an element

is well defined and hence gives rise to the Schreier graph

. As
\varphi
respects
Y
,
\varphi
induces a perfect matching of

x\in T
and
\varphi(x)=s
, then we must have
\varphi(s\cdot x)=-s
by the definition of

, and hence the edge set
\{(x,s\cdot x)\colon x\in T,s=\varphi(x)\}
is a perfect matching of
T
. This is a contradiction as the cardinality of
T
is odd.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For any generating set
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, letting

\chi^{\prime}_{c}(G)>\chi^{\prime}(G)

It is easy to see that

, contradicting Theorem

∎

C_{y}
. Then
C_{x}=C_{y}
and both have cardinality

. Thus there exists a color

such that

.

Now the uncolored edges can be decomposed into
(a_{1}+1)\cdots(a_{n-1}+1)
many paths parallel to

, i.e., each path consists of only edges parallel to

, and so each path is of the form

for some

and postive integer

. For each such path

such that
c^{*}\not\in C_{x}
for all vertices

in

. We can then use the two colors
c^{*}
and
n+1
in alternation to color all the edges of

, so that the resulting edge coloring is proper.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle of size
a_{1}\times\cdots\times a_{n}
. If

is odd for some

, then there is a proper edge

-coloring satisfying the boundary condition for

.

Proof.

We will use only the colors

. If

to color the two edges adjacent to

and

Suppose

, we divide

into

many layers perpendicular to

many paths parallel to

Let

,

of

.

Next we prove a generalization of Lemma

, where

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer and let
d=4k+2
. For any
n
-dimensional rectangle

of size
d\times\cdots\times d
and for any
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in[-2k+2,2k-2]^{n}
where

is an even number for each

, there is a proper edge
(2n+1)
-coloring satisfying both the boundary condition and the
t
-shifted core condition for

.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of Lemma
4.3
, except that at each stage of the construction

will have size

and

will have size

and

are both odd, Lemma

can be applied to complete the proof.
∎

In our proof of the main theorem below, we view an application of Lemma

as a two-step process. In the first step, we apply Lemma
4.3
to obtain a proper edge
(2n+1)
-coloring of

satisfying the core condition. Then, in the second step, we may shift the core to any other position within

as long as each coordinate of the shift vector used is even. This is not quite what happens in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, but it is a useful point of view to describe the algorithm in the proof of our main theorem.

4.2.
Continuous proper edge colorings

In this final subsection we prove the main theorem of the paper. We will use the following lemmas from
[
7
]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

(Basic clopen marker lemma
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
) Let
\rho
be the path distance on the Schreier graph
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. Then for any positive integer
d
, there is a relatively clopen set
M_{d}\subseteq F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
such that

(1)

if
x,y\in M_{d}
are distinct then
\rho(x,y)>d
;

(2)

for any
x\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
there is
y\in M_{d}
such that
\rho(x,y)\leq d
.

The integer
d
in the above lemma is usually called the
marker distance
, the set
M_{d}
is called a
marker set
, and its elements are called
marker points
. The careful reader might note that the path distance
\rho
we use in this paper is the
\ell_{\infty}
distance on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, while in
[
7
]
the distance
\rho
was the
\ell_{1}
distance. Since the proof for Lemma
4.5
only depends on the finiteness of the balls in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
under these distances, the lemma continues to hold for the path distance we use here. However, note that the balls under the
\ell_{\infty}
distance are
n
-dimensional rectangles, and hence are more convenient to use in our discussions below.

Lemma 4.6
.

(Marker regions lemma
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
) Let
d>0
be an integer. Then there is a relatively clopen subequivalence relation
R^{n}_{d}
on
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
such that each of the
R^{n}_{d}
-equivalence classes is of the form
R\cdot x
, where
R
is an
n
-dimensional rectangle with side lengths either
d
or
d+1
. Here
R^{n}_{d}
is relatively clopen means that
\left\{(x,g)\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon(x,g\cdot x)\in R^{n}_{d}\right\}
is a clopen subset of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

The
R^{n}_{d}
-equivalence classes in the above lemma are called
marker regions
.

All the terminology we defined in the preceding subsection about an
n
-dimensional rectangle
R
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be similarly defined for a marker region of the form
R\cdot x
for
x\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. Thus we may refer to a marker region as an
n
-dimensional rectangle in the Schreier graph
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. In particular, for a continuous edge coloring of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
, we may consider whether it satisfies the boundary condition and the (shifted) core condition for a marker region, and when we write the marker region as
R\cdot x
, the boundary condition and the (shifted) core condition are independent from the choice of
R
and
x
. Note that the (shifted) core condition is only valid when the side lengths of the marker region are even.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer and let

. Then there is a relatively clopen subequivalence relation

on

and a continuous proper edge
(2n+1)
-coloring of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
such that

(i)

each of the
R^{n}_{d}
-equivalence classes is of the form
R\cdot x
, where
R
is an
n
-dimensional rectangle with side lengths either
d
or
d+1
;

(ii)

the edge coloring satisfies both the boundary condition and the core condition for each of the
R^{n}_{d}
-equivalence classes.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
4.6
to obtain a relatively clopen subequivalence relation
R^{n}_{d}
on
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
so that each of the

-equivalence classes is of the form

, where

is an
n
-dimensional rectangle with side lengths either
d
or

. Consider a fixed such

and assume it is of size

, where

for each

. If any

is

, we obtain and fix a proper edge
2n
-coloring

satisfying the boundary condition for

by Lemma

. Otherwise,

is of size

Now we define a proper edge
(2n+1)
-coloring

of

as follows. For a standard generator

and

, let

Then

-dimensional rectangle

-class containing

.
∎

In particular we obtain a continuous proper edge
(2n+1)
-coloring of

. This generalizes
[

, Theorem 3.1.4]
and answers
[

, which was also stated implicitly in

Corollary 4.8

For any

Theorem 4.12
.

For any
n\geq 1
and any generating set
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
G=F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. Then
\chi^{\prime}_{c}(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)+1=|S|+1
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\chi^{\prime}(G)=|S|
. The lower bound has been established by Corollary
3.3
. We only need to construct a continuous proper edge
(|S|+1)
-coloring of
F(S,2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
.

Inductively define
S_{i}
so that

(i)

S_{0}
is a maximal linearly semi-independent subset of
S
.

(ii)

If
S_{i-1}
is defined for
i\geq 1
, let
S_{i}
be a maximal linearly semi-independent subset of
S\setminus\bigcup_{j=0}^{i-1}S_{j}
.

Then there is some
m\geq 0
such that
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}S_{i}
, where each
S_{i}
is symmetric, and in particular
|S_{0}|=2n
. Assume for
1\leq i\leq m
,
|S_{i}|=2n_{i}
. By the maximal linear semi-independence, for each
1\leq i\leq m
, let
k_{i}>0
be the smallest natural number such that the scalar multiplication
k_{i}\langle S_{i}\rangle
is contained in
\langle S_{i-1}\rangle
.

Let
\alpha=3^{n}m
and
\beta=2\cdot 3\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}
. Fix an arbitrary non-identity
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in S_{m}
and let
|s|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}|s_{k}|
. For each
1\leq i\leq m
, since
\beta
is a multiple of
\prod_{j=i+1}^{m}k_{j}
, we have
\beta s\in\bigcap_{j=i}^{m}\langle S_{j}\rangle
; in particular
\beta s\in\langle S_{i}\rangle
. Thus there exist
a_{i,1},\dots,a_{i,n_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\beta s=a_{i,1}e_{i,1}+\cdots+a_{i,n_{i}}e_{i,n_{i}}
, where
S_{i}=\{\pm e_{i,1},\dots,\pm e_{i,n_{i}}\}
. Let
\gamma=\sup\{|a_{i,j}|\colon 1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n_{i}\}
. Let
d=4\cdot 2(\gamma+1)(\alpha+1)(\beta+1)|s|+2
.

Consider the Schreier graph
G_{i}=G(\langle S_{i}\rangle,S_{i},F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))
. Let
\rho_{i}=\rho_{S_{i}}
be the path distance on
G_{i}
. We have
E(G)=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}E(G_{i})
. For each
0\leq i\leq m
, let
H_{i}
be the Schreier graph on
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
where

, and let

be a set of
2n_{i}
many fresh colors, i.e., for
i\neq j
,
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\varnothing
. Note that
H_{i}
is generated by the actions of
\bigcup_{j=i}^{m}S_{j}
. Assume
0
is a color distinct from any color in
\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}C_{i}
. Let
N_{i}=1+\sum_{j=i}^{m}2n_{j}
. To define a continuous edge
(|S|+1)
-coloring on
G
, we use reverse induction on

to define a continuous edge
N_{i}
-coloring of
H_{i}
with the colors in
\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{j=i}^{m}C_{j}
. When

the resulting edge coloring is as required.

When
i=m
we apply Proposition
4.11
to

to obtain a relatively clopen subequivalence relation

on

and a continuous proper edge

-coloring of

. Let
\kappa_{m}
denote this coloring. Then
\kappa_{m}
uses colors in
\{0\}\cup C_{m}
. For each

-marker region, which is an
n_{m}
-dimensional rectangle with side lengths either
d
or
d+1
,
\kappa_{m}
satisfies both the boundary condition and the core condition. In particular, the boundary condition guarantees that
\kappa_{m}
is a proper edge coloring of

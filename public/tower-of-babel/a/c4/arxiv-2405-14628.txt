Online robust estimation and bootstrap inference for function-on-scalar regression
Guanghui Cheng, Wenjuan Hu, Ruitao Lin, Chen Wang

Abstract
We propose a novel and robust online function-on-scalar regression technique via geometric median to learn associations between functional responses and scalar covariates based on massive or streaming datasets. The online estimation procedure, developed using the average stochastic gradient descent algorithm, offers an efficient and cost-effective method for analyzing sequentially augmented datasets, eliminating the need to store large volumes of data in memory. We establish the almost sure consistency, L p L_{p} convergence, and asymptotic normality of the online estimator. To enable efficient and fast inference of the parameters of interest, including the derivation of confidence intervals, we also develop an innovative two-step online bootstrap procedure to approximate the limiting error distribution of the robust online estimator. Numerical studies under a variety of scenarios demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed online learning method. A real application analyzing PM 2.5 air-quality data is also included to exemplify the proposed online approach.



keywords
Bootstrap approximation, functional regression, geometric median, online learning, stochastic gradient descent

1
Introduction

1.1
Function-on-scalar regression

Functional data analysis (FDA) is an effective way for modeling high-dimensional or potentially infinite-dimensional datasets and can find applications in may real-life situations.
One of the most widely studied approaches in the field of FDA, as seen in the literature, is the scalar-on-function regression, where the response is a scalar and the covariates are functional data
(
Ramsay and Silverman 2005
;
Cai and Hall 2006
;
Morris 2015
;
Wang et al. 2017
)
. In the analysis of gene expression and imaging data, the function-on-scalar regression, which involves regressing a functional response on scalar predictors, has been gaining popularity
(
Reiss et al. 2010
;
Goldsmith et al. 2015
;
Goldsmith and Kitago 2016
;
Fan and Reimherr 2017
;
Bauer et al. 2018
;
Ghosal and Maity 2023
)
.
Formally, the function-on-scalar regression model (FSRM) can be expressed as

\displaystyle Y(t)={\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)+U(t)

(1)

where
t
is the functional index,
Y(t)
is a functional response on a compact support
{\mathcal{T}}
, and
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})^{T}
are scalar covariates in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)=(\beta_{1}(t),\ldots,\beta_{d}(t))^{T}
is the slope function that of primary interest,
and
U(t)
is an residual process on
{\mathcal{T}}
that is independent of the predictors
{\bf X}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
{\mathcal{T}}=[0,1]
. If the support
{\mathcal{T}}=\{1:p\}
, model (
1
) reduces to the conventional multivariate linear regression with a
p
-dimensional response, and
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is thus a
d\times p
matrix. Throughout the paper, with a slight abuse of notation, we will omit “
(t)
” when referring to functional processes. For instance, we will use
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
to denote the entire slope function
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)
over
t\in\mathcal{T}
.

As a typical motivating application,
Zhang et al. 2017
established an air pollution monitoring network in Beijing as part of the national monitoring effort, beginning in January 2013. This network can collect more than 4 million hourly observations from various air-quality monitoring sites across the city.
In this particular example, the functional responses
Y(t)
can be a particular measurement of interest quantifying the air quality at different time points
t
(say hourly), and the scalar covariates can be some daily environmental indicators such as temperature and wind speed. Hence, by performing the function-on-scalar regression model using model (
1
), one can study the functional association between the hourly air-quality measurement and the daily environmental indicators.

In the era of big data, massive datasets and streaming datasets, where data is continually updated, are commonly encountered, presenting challenges in terms of data storage, computation, and analysis. Our paper contributes to addressing two major practical challenges involved in analyzing massive functional datasets in real-world applications:

First, the real-world data distributions are usually irregular and may have heavy tails. Using methods developed under Gaussian assumptions may be suboptimal. In the literature, the majority of existing efforts have predominantly focused on functional mean regression, where the residual term
U(t)
is assumed to be a zero-mean stochastic process on
\mathcal{T}
. Under such an assumption, the least squares approach can be applied to estimate the slope function
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
. However, such a method may not be efficient for heavy-tailed or irregular data. To address the irregularities in the distributions of high-dimensional or functional data, function-on-scalar quantile regression has been developed
(
Yang et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2022
)
. The quantile-based methods estimate conditional quantiles and enable statistical inference on the entire conditional distribution of the response.

Second, as with many real-world applications, the amount of data is enormous. For instance, in the motivating example, the dataset contains 4 million observations. Loading such a large dataset into memory all at once might be infeasible, especially when the data is collected from a large number of wearable devices. Traditional approaches typically require halting data collection at a specific time point to analyze a static dataset, which is highly inefficient. Furthermore, analyzing the entire dataset simultaneously using a standard computer is often impossible due to limited storage and computing capacity.

1.2
Our contributions

We address the aforementioned challenges by (1) employing the
geometric median

(
Cardot et al. 2013
)
, for the first time in the literature, to estimate the unknown function
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
for the FSRM given by Equation (
1
), and (2) developing an online estimation and inference procedure to eliminate the need for storing the entire dataset.
Furthermore, for an unobserved location
t\in{\mathcal{T}}
that is not included in the observed sampling points, we introduce an interpolation approach based on the discrete location observations, and derive the convergence rate. Under scenarios with dense observations, it can be demonstrated that the sampling frequency of the functional data has minimal impact on the convergence rate.

The geometric median is widely recognized as a robust central location parameter in the analysis of high-dimensional data
(
Minsker 2015
;
Godichon-Baggioni 2016
;
Godichon-Baggioni 2019
;
Li et al. 2022
)
.
Vardi and Zhang 2000
developed an iterative algorithm to compute the geometric median. For a random element in Hilbert space,
Cardot et al. 2013

developed a faster online gradient algorithm to estimate the geometric median, both demonstrating its almost sure consistency as well as asymptotic normality. However, they did not provide an online inference framework for the geometric median. As an application,

Roberts, Mueller and Mclntyre 2017
employed the geometric median on high-quality, large-scale Earth imaging data to produce Earth observation composites.
This approach can effectively reduce spatial noises while preserving spectral relationships. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is currently no existing research on applying the geometric median to the FSRM.

Instead of assuming
U(t)
has a zero mean, we assume
U(t)
on
\mathcal{T}
follows a stochastic process with a zero
geometric median
. Specifically, let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{d}
be the product space of
{\mathcal{H}}
. A commonly encountered space for
{\mathcal{H}}
is
L^{2}[0,1]
, i.e. the outcome
Y(t)
represents a function of time.
The conditional geometric median of
Y(t)
given scalar covariates
{\bf X}
for a fixed location
t
is assumed to be
m(t)={\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)
, where
m(t)
is defined as

m(t):=\argmin_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{Y(t)|{\bf X}}\{\|Y(t)-\alpha\|-\|Y(t)\|\}\,,

(2)

with
\|\cdot\|
and
\langle\cdot\rangle
denoting the associated norm and inner product in
{\mathcal{H}}
, respectively. As shown in the subsequent sections, our definition of
m(t)
via geometric median makes use of the
spatial correlation between different locations, and
enhances the efficiency and robustness of estimation and inference for
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
in the FSRM, especially when dealing with massive or streaming datasets.
Moreover, if
m^{v}(t)={\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)
is the geometric quantile which minimizes
{\mathbb{E}}_{Y|{\bf X}}\{\|Y-m^{v}(t)\|-\langle Y-m^{v}(t),v\rangle\}
, then
m^{v}(t)
generalizes (
1
) to the geometric quantile regression. This approach was studied in
Padilla et al. 2022
as a special case of ReLU Networks for multivariate responses.

To deal with massive functional data, we propose an online procedure for estimating the slope function
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
based on the functional average stochastic gradient descent (ASGD) algorithm. We establish the
L_{p}
and almost sure convergence for the proposed online estimator.
We also investigate the asymptotic behavior of the online estimator, which essentially resembles a Gaussian process. This asymptotic result is general and includes the asymptotic normality of the estimator at a given location
t
as a special case. Subsequently, we also propose a novel online bootstrap resampling procedure to approximate the limiting error distribution of our proposed estimator, enabling online inference about the slope function
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
.

Unlike classical batch learning methods, online learning dynamically updates model estimates using only the newly added data, enabling real-time fast decision-making
(
Chen et al. 2020
;
Zhu and Dong 2021
;
Lee et al. 2022
;
Li et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2022
)
. Consequently, online learning presents a crucial and efficient approach for handling and making inferences from sequentially augmented datasets. In a related work,
Xie et al. 2023
generalized the perturbed SGD in
Feng et al. 2018
to functional linear regression in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, and also established the asymptotic distribution of the proposed point-wise estimator. In fact, our online bootstrap approach possesses unique features that distinguish it from the perturbed stochastic gradient descent (SGD) algorithm for the finite-dimensional setting
in
Feng et al. 2018
as well as the infinite-dimensional setting in
Xie et al. 2023
. The resulting
inferential procedure maintains the computational efficiency of the functional ASGD
method.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section
2
introduces the online estimation algorithm for the slope function
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and establishes its almost sure and
L_{p}
convergence.
Section
3
proposes a novel online bootstrap algorithm for approximating the limiting error distribution, enabling online inference.
Section
4
extends the online estimation algorithm to an interpolation-based estimator for the entire slope function and demonstrates its convergence properties.
Section
5
presents a numerical study to evaluate the empirical performance of our proposed procedure.
Section
6
illustrates the feasibility and usefulness of our method through a real-world application.
All technical details are provided in the Appendix.

2
Online estimation of the FSRM

Suppose that for each sample
i
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, we observe
d
covariates
{\bf X}_{i}=(X_{i1},\ldots,X_{id})^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and a set of functional responses
\{Y_{i}(t),t\in\mathcal{T}\}\in{\mathcal{H}}

{\bf X}_{i}

1

Based on the definition (
2
), the geometric median-based estimator of
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
can be derived by minimizing the following empirical convex loss function

\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}=\argmin_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|Y_{i}-{\bf X}_{i}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|.

By applying the functional SGD approach, which can be written as
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{n+1}=\boldsymbol{\beta}_{n}-\gamma_{n}\nabla||Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}\beta_{n}||

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}+\gamma_{n}\frac{{\bf X}_{n+1}(Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})}{\|Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}\|}\,,

(3)

where
\gamma_{n}
is the descent step size. Therefore,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}
is an estimate based on the Robbins–Monro algorithm. We then average all the sequential estimates to obtain our final ASGD estimate, that is,
{\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{i}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n+1}={\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}+\frac{1}{n+1}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n})\,.

(4)

To derive the consistency and asymptotic results for
\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}
, we make the following assumptions throughout the paper,

Assumption 1
.

There exist constant
C>0
such that

1/C\leq\lambda_{d}(\Sigma)\leq\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)\leq C
almost surely, where
\Sigma={\mathbb{E}}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{T})
.

Assumption 2
.

The error process
U(t)
in the FSRM (
1
) has a unique geometric median at zero.

Assumption 3
.

The error process
U(t)
is not strongly concentrated around a single point; that is,
There is a constant
C>0
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\{\|U(t)-h\|^{-2}\}\leq C\,

for all
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

Assumption
1
is a mild condition on the covariates in linear regression, and has been imposed in
Zhang et al. 2022
and
Liu et al. 2022
. Assumption
2
implies that the error process
U(t)
is not concentrated on a straight line
(
Cardot et al. 2013
)
; that is, for all
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
, there is a

such that
\langle h,h^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle=0
and
{\rm Var}(\langle U(t),h^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle)>0
. Condition
3

implicitly forces no atoms exist in the distribution of
U(t)
, and it is naturally satisfied in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
whenever

, see
Cardot et al. 2017
. Note that
{\mathbb{E}}\{\|U(t)-h\|^{-2}\}\leq C
implies
{\mathbb{E}}\{\|U(t)-h\|^{-1}\}\leq\sqrt{C}
by Hölder’s inequality.

In addition, by taking
\gamma_{n}=\gamma n^{-\alpha}
, where
\gamma
is a positive constant, we impose the following assumption for the step size function
\gamma_{n}
:

Assumption 4
.

The sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the standard conditions required for the Robbins–Monro algorithm; that is,
\sum{\gamma_{n}}=\infty

and
\sum{\gamma^{2}_{n}}<\infty
.

3
Online bootstrap inference for
\beta(t)

To apply Theorem
2
for inference, it is necessary to estimate the asymptotic variance from the data. However, its computation invariably demands substantial computational resources and may become unstable or even infeasible when handling massive or streaming data. Therefore, we introduce an online bootstrap inference procedure by resampling the residuals to approximate the limiting distribution of
\sqrt{n}(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
given in Theorem
2
.

Specifically, we consider the wild-type bootstrap based on the Rademacher weight
(
Canay et al. 2021
)
. Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
be independent and identically distributed random samples of the Rademacher random variable
W
with probability mass
{\mathbb{P}}(W=1)={\mathbb{P}}(W=-1)=1/2
. Define the function

G_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(h)={\mathbb{E}}\{\|W(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}})-{\bf X}^{T}h\|-\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\}\,,

and the gradient of
G_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(h)
can be calculated as

\nabla G_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(h)=:-{\mathbb{E}}\frac{{\bf X}\{W(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}})-{\bf X}^{T}h\}}{\|W(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}})-{\bf X}^{T}h\|}\,.

Obviously,
\nabla G_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(h)
has a unique minimizer at
h=0
due to
-{\mathbb{E}}_{X}\frac{X{W(Y-X^{T}\boldsymbol{\beta})}}{||W(Y-X^{T}\boldsymbol{\beta})||}=0.
In practice, the true slope function
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is typically unknown

\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}
as a plug-in estimator.
It is feasible to simultaneously estimate
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and

As a result,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n+1}^{b}=

\displaystyle W_{n+1}^{b}(Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}),

(5)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n+1}^{b}=

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n}^{b}+\gamma_{n}\frac{{\bf X}_{n+1}(\varepsilon_{n+1}^{b}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}\Upsilon_{n}^{b})}{\|\varepsilon_{n+1}^{b}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}\Upsilon_{n}^{b}\|}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\Upsilon}_{n}^{b}+\frac{1}{n+1}(\Upsilon_{n+1}^{b}-\bar{\Upsilon}_{n}^{b})\,.

In practice, the sample functional responses {
Y_{i}(t)\}_{\char 16\relax=1}^{n}
are observed on a common discrete grid
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
in
[0,1]
, where
{\bf t}
can be a collection of different locations from all individuals.
As a result, no data are observed for
t\in\mathcal{T}/{\bf t}
. To estimate the entire slope function, we first consider estimating
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
at locations

\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\}
by minimizing the following
L_{2}
norm-based loss function using discrete data points,

\displaystyle{\tilde{L}}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigg\{Y_{j}(t_{j})-{\bf X}_{i}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t_{j})\bigg\}^{2}}\,.

(10)

Let
(\hat{\beta}(t_{1}),\ldots,\hat{\beta}(t_{m}))
denote the minimizer to (
10
).
One can then impose smoothness on
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}
by regressing
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(t_{j})
against
t_{j}
using a nonparametric kernel or spline smoothing approach
(
Rice and Silverman 1991
;
Hall et al. 2006
)
.
We adopt the
r
-th order spline interpolation approach by solving
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{r}
from

\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{r}=\argmin_{g\in W_{2}^{r}}\int\{g^{(r)}(t)\}^{2}dt,

subject to

g(t_{l})=\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(t_{l}),\quad l=1,\ldots,m.

Here,
W_{2}^{r}
denotes the
r
-th order Sobolev space, which is also a Hilbert space. Under the common design,
Cai and Yuan 2011
showed that the minimax rate is of order
m^{-2r}+n^{-1}
, which is jointly determined by the sampling frequency
m
and the number of curves
n
. Then we give the convergence rate for
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{r}
as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Under Assumptions (
1
) – (
4
), suppose that
\max\|t_{l}-t_{l-1}\|\leq Cm^{-1}
holds, it follows that

\lim_{D\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(\|\hat{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{r}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq D(m^{-2r}+n^{-1}))\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the
L_{2}
norm.

Theorem
4
indicates that, when the functional data are observed on a relatively dense grid, such that
m\gg n^{1/(2r)}
, the sampling frequency
m
does not affect the rate of convergence. This rate is of order
\mathcal{O}(1/n)
, which is solely determined by the sample size
n
.

7
Conclusion

We have introduced a novel function-on-scalar regression methodology using the geometric median, complemented by online estimation and bootstrap inference techniques suited for massive or streaming datasets. Our theoretical and numerical analyses validate the effectiveness of these proposed online methods. Notably, our geometric median-based regression demonstrates superior performance with heavy-tailed distributions compared to traditional mean-based approaches. Moreover, even when dealing with data from a Gaussian distribution, our method maintains efficiency comparable to that of mean-based techniques. Supporting theoretical evidence for these claims is detailed in
Cheng et al. 2023
.

One interesting direction for future work involves extending our method to accommodate high-dimensional covariates
{\bf X}
, as suggested by
Barber et al. 2017
and
Fan and Reimherr 2017
. Employing a geometric median-based approach in such settings could potentially enhance robustness in feature selection and inference. Additionally, refining the current methodology by developing an optimal interpolation technique for discretely sampled functional data represents another valuable direction for improvement.

Some useful Propositions and Lemmas

In the following, we provide some propositions and lemmas that are necessary for the proofs of Theorem
1
and
2
.
Denote the gradient function and the corresponding hessian function as

\Phi(h)=-{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\frac{{\bf X}(Y-{\bf X}^{T}h)}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}h\|}\right\}\,,~\Gamma_{h}={\mathbb{E}}\bigg({\bf X}{\bf X}^{T}\otimes A_{h}\bigg)\,,

where

A_{h}=\frac{1}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}h\|}\bigg({\mathbb{I}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}-\frac{(Y-{\bf X}^{T}h)\otimes(Y-{\bf X}^{T}h)}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}h\|^{2}}\bigg)\,,

and note that when
h={\boldsymbol{\beta}}
,
\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}=\Sigma\otimes A_{0}
with
A_{0}={\mathbb{E}}(A_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
, and
{\mathbb{I}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
is the identity operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

The following proposition gives an important result for
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\{\lambda_{i,\Sigma}\}_{i=1}^{d}
be the eigenvalues of
\Sigma={\mathbb{E}}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{T})
, we have
1/C\leq\lambda_{i,\Sigma}\leq C
. Moreover, for any

\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{d}
, there is a positive constant
C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}
such that,

C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\|\phi\|^{2}\leq\langle\phi,\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\phi\rangle\leq C\|\phi\|^{2}\,,

which indicates
C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\leq\lambda_{i,{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\leq C
, with
\lambda_{i,{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}
being the eigenvalues of
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda_{i,\Sigma})
be the eigenvalues
\Sigma={\mathbb{E}}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{T})=\{\sigma_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{d}
, and
\Gamma_{\beta}
can be viewed as a
d\times d
matrix
with elements being covariance operators. For any
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{d})^{T}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{d}
, it holds that

\langle\phi,\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\phi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{ij}\langle\phi_{i},A_{0}\phi_{j}\rangle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k,A_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{ij}\langle e_{k},\phi_{i}\rangle\langle e_{k},\phi_{j}\rangle\,,

where
e_{k}
is the eigenfunction corresponding to the
k
th eigenvalue
\lambda_{k,A_{0}}
of
A_{0}={\mathbb{E}}(A_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})
, and the last equality is implied by the eigenvalue decomposition
for
A_{0}
.
Together with Assumption
1
,
\Sigma
is positive definite and
1/C\leq\lambda_{i,\Sigma}\leq C
, then it holds that

\sum_{k}\lambda_{k,A_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{ij}\langle e_{k},\phi_{i}\rangle\langle e_{k},\phi_{j}\rangle=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k,A_{0}}\langle{\bf a}_{k},\Sigma{\bf a}_{k}\rangle\,,

where
{\bf a}_{k}=(a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,d})
and
a_{k,j}=\langle e_{k},h_{j}\rangle
. Hence, we have

1/C\sum_{k}\lambda_{k,A_{0}}\|\langle e_{k},h\rangle\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k}\lambda_{k,A_{0}}\langle{\bf a}_{k},\Sigma{\bf a}_{k}\rangle\leq C\sum_{k}\lambda_{k,A_{0}}\|\langle e_{k},h\rangle\|^{2}\,.

By the assumption that
{\mathbb{E}}\|U-h\|^{-2}\leq C
and Proposition 2.1 in
Cardot et al. 2017
, there exist two positive constants
c_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}
and
C
, such that for all
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

c_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\|\psi\|^{2}\leq\langle\psi,A_{0}\psi\rangle\leq C\|\psi\|^{2}\,.

By taking
C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}=c_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}/C
, it holds that

C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\|h\|^{2}\leq\langle h,\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}h\rangle\leq C\|h\|^{2}\,,

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
3
, there exists a constant
C
, such that

\|\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k})-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\|\leq C\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\,,

for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}})=0
, applying Taylor’s expansion, we have

\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k})=\int_{0}^{1}\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+t({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}){\rm d}t\,.

Let
r_{k}=\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k})-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})=\int_{0}^{1}(\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+t({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})}-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}){\rm d}t
. Following
Cardot et al. 2017
, we define the function
\varphi_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(\cdot)
from
[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{d}
as

\varphi_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t):=\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+th}(h^{{}^{\prime}})\,,

for all
h,h^{{}^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{d}
. Then,
we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}+h)-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(h)\|=

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{1}(\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+th}-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})(h){\rm d}t\|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|(\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+th}(h)-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(h))\|{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|\varphi_{h,h}(t)-\varphi_{h,h}(0)\|{\rm d}t\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}_{h,h}(t)\|\,.

Next we give an upper bound for
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}_{h,h}(t)\|
. Let

W_{h}(t)=\frac{1}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h\|},~V_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)=h^{{}^{\prime}}-\frac{\langle Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h,h^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h)}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h\|^{2}}\,,

then we have
\varphi_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)={\mathbb{E}}\{{\bf X}{\bf X}^{T}\otimes(W^{{}^{\prime}}_{h}(t)V_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)+W_{h}(t)V^{{}^{\prime}}_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t))\}
. By algebra, it holds that

\|V_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)\|\leq 2\|h^{{}^{\prime}}\|,~~~\|W^{{}^{\prime}}_{h}(t)\|\leq\frac{\|{\bf X}^{T}h\|}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h\|}\,,

and

\|V^{{}^{\prime}}_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)\|\leq\frac{4\|{\bf X}^{T}h\|\|h^{{}^{\prime}}\|}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h\|}\,.

Since
{\bf X}
is bounded almost surely, it holds that
\|\varphi_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\|\leq 6\|h\|\|h^{{}^{\prime}}\|{\mathbb{E}}\{\|U-t{\bf X}^{T}h\|^{-2}\}\leq C\|h\|\|h^{{}^{\prime}}\|
. Thus, we complete the proof by choosing
h={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
.
∎

Lemma 1
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
4
, there exist some constants
C
and
C^{{}^{\prime}}
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\}\leq Ce^{-C^{{}^{\prime}}n^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{C}{n^{\alpha}}+C\sup_{n/2-1\leq k\leq n}{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}\}\,.

Proof.

Denote that
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}={\mathbb{E}}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{T}\otimes A_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})
, then we decompose the online SGD estimator as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\gamma_{n}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})+\gamma_{n}\xi_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}r_{n}\,,

(11)

where

\xi_{n+1}=\frac{{\bf X}_{n+1}(Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})}{\|Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}\|}+\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\,.

Denote
H={\mathcal{H}}^{d}
and define

\kappa_{k}={\mathbb{I}}_{H}-\gamma_{k}\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}},~~\nu_{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\kappa_{k}
. Note that
\nu_{0}={\mathbb{I}}_{H}\,,

we then rewrite equation (
11
) as

{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\nu_{n}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})+\nu_{n}M_{n+1}-\nu_{n}R_{n+1}\,,

where

R_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\nu_{k}^{-1}r_{k}
, and
M_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\nu_{k}^{-1}\xi_{k+1}
.
Applying the inequality of arithmetic means, we have

{\mathbb{E}}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\leq 3{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\|^{2})+3{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}M_{n}\|^{2})+3{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}R_{n}\|^{2})\,.

In the following steps, we subsequently bound
{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\|^{2})
,
{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}M_{n}\|^{2})
and
{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}R_{n}\|^{2})
. Using the results of Proposition
2
, it holds that
C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\leq\lambda_{i,{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\leq C
. Next, due to fact

\|\nu_{n-1}\|\leq C_{1}\exp\{-C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}\}

for some constant
C_{1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\|^{2})\leq C_{1}^{2}\bigg\{\exp\bigg(-C_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}\bigg)\bigg\}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\leq C\exp(-C^{{}^{\prime}}n^{1-\alpha})\,.

To bound
{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\nu_{n-1}M_{n}\|^{2}\}
, we define
U_{n+1}:=-\frac{{\bf X}_{n+1}(Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})}{\|Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}\|}
. Then
\xi_{n}
can be represented as
\xi_{n}=\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})-U_{n+1}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a
\sigma-
algebra generated by
\sigma({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})=\sigma(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
, where
Z_{i}=({\bf X}_{i},Y_{i})
. Apparently, for all integers
n\geq 1
,

{\mathbb{E}}(U_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n})=\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}).

This leads to
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}
being a sequence of martingale differences. Due to the orthogonality of the martingale differences, we have
{\mathbb{E}}\langle\xi_{k},\xi_{k^{{}^{\prime}}}\rangle=0
for any
k\neq k^{{}^{\prime}}
. Hence it follows that

{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\nu_{n-1}M_{n}\|^{2}\}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\xi_{k+1}\|^{2}\}\,.

Since
\|\xi_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq 4\|{\bf X}_{n+1}\|^{2}\leq C
almost surely, we have
{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\nu_{n-1}M_{n}\|^{2}\}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}^{2}\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\|^{2}
. Using similar calculations in
Cardot et al. 2017
, we have
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}^{2}\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\|^{2}\leq Cn^{-\alpha}
. Then it holds that

{\mathbb{E}}\|\nu_{n-1}M_{n}\|^{2}\leq Cn^{-\alpha}
for all
n\geq 1
.

To bound
{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}R_{n}\|^{2})
, we write
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\gamma_{n}\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})+\gamma_{n}\xi_{n+1}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n})=

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}+\gamma_{n}^{2}\{\|\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\|^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}(\|\xi_{n+1}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n})\}

\displaystyle~~~~~~-2\gamma_{n}\langle{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\rangle\,.

As
\|\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\|^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}(\|\xi_{n+1}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n})\leq 2\|{\bf X}_{n+1}\|^{2}
and
\langle{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\rangle\geq 0
, by taking the expectation of the two sides, we have
{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2})\leq{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2})+C\gamma_{n}^{2}
, which leads to

{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2})\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}^{2}\lesssim C
. Similarly, since
\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}+C\gamma_{n}^{2}+2\gamma_{n}\langle\xi_{n+1},{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\rangle
, it holds that

{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n})\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}+C\gamma_{n}^{4}+4\gamma_{n}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}(\|\langle\xi_{n+1},{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\rangle\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n})+C\gamma_{n}^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\,.

Together with
\|\xi_{n+1}\|\leq 2\|{\bf X}_{n+1}\|^{2}
,
{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n+1}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4})\leq{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4})+C\gamma_{n}^{2}
, we can show that
{\mathbb{E}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4})\leq C
for any
n\geq 1
. Because
\|r_{n}\|\leq C\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}
by Proposition
3
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}R_{n}\|^{2})\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}\|r_{k}\|\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\right)^{2}\right\}\,.

By Lemma 5.5 in
Cardot et al. 2017
, it thus holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\|\nu_{n-1}R_{n}\|^{2})\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}\}^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{E(n/2)}\gamma_{k}\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~+C\sup_{E(n/2)-1\leq k\leq n}{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}\}\left(\sum_{k=E(n/2)+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\|\nu_{n-1}\nu_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}\right)^{2}\,,

where
E(\cdot)
denotes the integer function. It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\nu_{n-1}R_{n}\|^{2}\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{E(n/2)}\gamma_{k}e^{-C^{{}^{\prime}}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}}\right)^{2}+C\sup_{E(n/2)-1\leq k\leq n-1}{\mathbb{E}}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}\,.

Therefore, we can obtain

{\mathbb{E}}\|\nu_{n-1}R_{n}\|^{2}\leq Ce^{-C^{{}^{\prime}}n^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{C}{n^{\alpha}}+C\sup_{E(n/2)-1\leq k\leq n}{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}\}\,,

which completes the proof
∎

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\gamma_{n}\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\|^{4}\}
. Define the set of the sequence of events
for some
q\geq 1
as,

S_{n,q}:=\bigg\{\omega:\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}(\omega)-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\leq Mn^{\frac{1-\alpha}{q}}\bigg\}\,.

\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\leq 1

\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}\|\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|+1

n_{0}
,

\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\geq 1

\displaystyle\langle\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}),{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\langle\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+t({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}),{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\rangle{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1/\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|}\langle\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+t({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}),{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\rangle{\rm d}t

for all

for all

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\gamma_{n}\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\|^{4}\}=

\displaystyle~{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\gamma_{n}\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\|^{4}{\mathbb{I}}_{S_{n,q}}\}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\gamma_{n}\Phi({\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n})\|^{4}\}\leq\bigg(1-n^{-\frac{1+(q-1)\alpha}{q}}\bigg)^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{4}\}+C\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha}}\,,

which completes the proof.

\|\nabla G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(\Upsilon_{n})-{\hat{\Gamma}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}({\Upsilon}_{n})\|\leq C\|\Upsilon_{n}\|^{2},

where
{\hat{\Gamma}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}=\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(0)
.

Proof.

Note that
\nabla G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(0)=0
, applying Taylor’s expansion with integral remainders, we have

\displaystyle\nabla G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(\Upsilon_{n})=

\displaystyle\nabla G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(t(\Upsilon_{n}))\Upsilon_{n}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(t\Upsilon_{n})\Upsilon_{n}{\rm d}t\,.

It then holds that

\displaystyle\|\nabla G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(\Upsilon_{n})-{\hat{\Gamma}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}({\Upsilon}_{n})\|=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\{\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(0)-\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(t\Upsilon_{n})\}\Upsilon_{n}{\rm d}t\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\{\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(0)-\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(t\Upsilon_{n})\}\Upsilon_{n}\right\|{\rm d}t\,.

According to the proof of Proposition
2
, we have

\left\|\{\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(0)-\nabla^{2}G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(t\Upsilon_{n})\}\Upsilon_{n}\right\|\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\{\|W(Y-{\bf X}^{T}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n})-t{\bf X}^{T}\Upsilon_{n}\|^{-2}\|\Upsilon_{n}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}\leq C\|\Upsilon_{n}\|^{2}\,.

the last inequality is implied by Assumption
3
with
\|W(Y-{\bf X}^{T}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n})-t\Upsilon_{n}\|^{-2}\leq\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-t\Upsilon_{n}\|^{-2}+\|Y-{\bf X}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}+t\Upsilon_{n}\|^{-2}
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
–
3
are satisfied, we have

\|({\hat{\Gamma}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}){\Upsilon}_{n}\|\leq C\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\|\Upsilon_{n}\|,

for any
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}
is defined as

\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}=\Sigma\otimes{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|}\bigg({\mathbb{I}}_{H}-\frac{(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\otimes(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}})}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}}\bigg)\bigg\}\,.

We express
\varphi_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}
, a function
[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{d}
, as

\displaystyle\varphi_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+th}(h^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma\otimes{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h\|}\bigg({\mathbb{I}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}-\frac{(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h)\otimes(Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h)}{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}h\|^{2}}\bigg)\bigg\}(h^{{}^{\prime}})\,.

Then we can write
({\hat{\Gamma}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}){\Upsilon}_{n}={\mathbb{E}}\{\varphi_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Upsilon_{n}}(1)-\varphi_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Upsilon_{n}}(0)|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}
, and it thus follows that

{\mathbb{E}}\{\varphi_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Upsilon_{n}}(1)-\varphi_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Upsilon_{n}}(0)|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}=\int_{0}^{1}{\mathbb{E}}\{\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}(t)|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}{\rm d}t\,.

It also holds that
\|\int_{0}^{1}{\mathbb{E}}\{\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Upsilon_{n}}(t)|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}{\rm d}t\|\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Upsilon_{n}}(t)\||{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}
. Based on the proof of Proposition
3
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}_{h,h^{{}^{\prime}}}(t)\|\}\leq 6C\|h\|\|h^{{}^{\prime}}\|\}\,.

Hence, it follows that

{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\Upsilon_{n}}(t)\||{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\{\|Y-{\bf X}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-t{\bf X}^{T}(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\|^{-2}\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\|\Upsilon_{n}\||{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}\leq C\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\|\Upsilon_{n}\|\,,

which completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma gives the convergence rates of
\Upsilon_{n}
in
L_{2}
and

Lemma 5
.

Under Assumptions
1
–

, for all

and

, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\Upsilon_{n}\|^{2}\}={\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\bigg),~~~~{\mathbb{E}}\{\|\Upsilon_{n}\|^{4}\}={\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\frac{1}{n^{\theta}}\bigg)\,.

Next, we give the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

By rearranging the decomposing (
6
) and (
7
), it holds that

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n+1}=({\mathbb{I}}_{H}-\gamma_{n}\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})\Upsilon_{n}+\gamma_{n}\eta_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}r_{n1}-\gamma_{n}r_{n2}\,,

where
\eta_{n}=\nabla G_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(\Upsilon_{n})+{\bf X}_{n+1}(\Lambda_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}\Upsilon_{n})\|\Lambda_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}^{T}\Upsilon_{n}\|^{-1}
,
r_{n1}=\Phi_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}(\Upsilon_{n})-{\hat{\Gamma}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}{\Upsilon}_{n}
and
r_{n2}=({\hat{\Gamma}}_{\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}}-\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}){\Upsilon}_{n}
.
To bound the remainder term
r_{n1}
and
r_{n2}
, using the results from Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
, we have
\|r_{n1}\|\leq C\|\Upsilon_{n}\|^{2}

and
\|r_{n2}\|\leq C\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\|\Upsilon_{n}\|
. By some simple algebra with
r_{k}=r_{k1}+r_{k2}
, we can obtain that for any
k\geq 1

\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\Upsilon_{k}=\eta_{k+1}-r_{k}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(\Upsilon_{k}-\Upsilon_{k+1})\,.

Summing up these equalities, we have

n\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\bar{\Upsilon}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(\Upsilon_{k}-\Upsilon_{k+1})-\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k+1}\,.

Then it holds that with
S_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k+1}
,

\sqrt{n}\Gamma_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\Upsilon_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\{\frac{\Upsilon_{1}}{\gamma_{1}}-\frac{\Upsilon_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\Upsilon_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}}\right)\right\}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n+1}\,.

To use the martingale CLT for
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n+1}
, we denote

\eta_{n}^{*}={\bf X}_{n+1}\frac{{\Lambda}_{n+1}}{\|{\Lambda}_{n+1}\|}=W_{n+1}\frac{{\bf X}_{n+1}(Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n})}{\|Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n+1}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}\|}\,,

and write
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S^{*}_{n+1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k+1}^{*}
. According to Corollary 6 in
Lavrentyev and Nazarov 2016
, conditional on observations, we have
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S^{*}_{n+1}\xrightarrow{{\mathcal{L}}}N(0,\Omega_{n})
,
where
\Omega_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{\bf X}_{k+1}{\bf X}_{k+1}^{T}\otimes\{(Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{k})\otimes(Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{k})\}}{\|Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{k}\|^{2}}\,.

Let
\Omega_{n}^{*}
denote
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{{\bf X}_{k+1}{\bf X}_{k+1}^{T}\otimes\{(Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})\otimes(Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})\}}{\|Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\|\Omega_{n}-\Omega_{n}^{*}\|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{C}{\|Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|}\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\|Y_{k+1}-{\bf X}_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}}\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\right\}^{1/2}\,,

where the second inequality is implied by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Due to
{\mathbb{E}}\{\|U-h\|^{-2}\}\leq C
imposed in Assumption
3
and
\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|\rightarrow 0
almost surely, it thus holds that
\|\Omega_{n}-\Omega_{n}^{*}\|\xrightarrow{P}0
.
For any
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{d}
, we have

\langle u,\Omega_{n}^{*}u\rangle\xrightarrow{P}\langle u,{\bf D}\otimes B_{0}u\rangle
by law of large numbers. Conditional on observations, it holds that

\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\bigg|{\mathbb{P}}(\langle u,G\rangle\leq t)-{\mathbb{P}}^{*}(\langle u,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{*}\rangle\leq t)\bigg|\xrightarrow{p}0

where
G\xrightarrow{{\mathcal{L}}}N(0,{\bf D}\otimes B_{0})
.
Let
r_{k}^{*}=\eta_{k}-\eta_{k}^{*}
, and note that

\{r_{n}^{*}\}_{n\geq 1}
is also a sequence of martingale differences, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbb{E}}\left(r_{k}^{*}\otimes r_{k}^{*}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\right)\right\|\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\{\|Y_{n+1}-{\bf X}_{n}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}\|^{-1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\}\|\Upsilon_{n}\|\,,

Since
\|\Upsilon_{n}\|\rightarrow 0
almost surely,
it follows that
\left\|{\mathbb{E}}\left(r_{k}^{*}\otimes r_{k}^{*}|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\right)\right\|\rightarrow 0
almost
surely. We then have
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k+1}^{*}\xrightarrow{P}0
.

In the following steps, we deal with the remainder terms.
As
\Upsilon_{1}
is bounded almost surely, it holds that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|\frac{\Upsilon_{1}}{\gamma_{1}}\|\xrightarrow{p}0
, and

{\mathbb{E}}\{\|n^{-1/2}\Upsilon_{n+1}\gamma_{n}^{-1}\|^{2}\}\leq Cn^{-1}n^{\alpha}\rightarrow 0
, with Chebyshev’s inequality

n^{-1/2}\Upsilon_{n+1}\gamma_{n}^{-1}\xrightarrow{p}0
. Moreover,
|\gamma_{k}^{-1}-\gamma_{k-1}^{-1}|\leq 2\alpha\gamma k^{\alpha-1}
. By Lemma 5.5 of
Cardot et al. 2017
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\sum_{k=2}^{n}\Upsilon_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}}\right)\right\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=2}^{n}|\gamma_{k}^{-1}-\gamma_{k-1}^{-1}|\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|\Upsilon_{k}\|^{2}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~\lesssim\frac{1}{n}4\alpha^{2}\bigg(\sum_{k=2}^{n}k^{\alpha/2-1}\bigg)^{2}={\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha}}\right)\,.

We also have
n^{-1/2}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\Upsilon_{k}(\gamma_{k}^{-1}-\gamma_{k-1}^{-1})\xrightarrow{p}0
. Finally, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}\right\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|r_{k}\|^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|r_{k1}\|^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}\|r_{k2}\|^{2}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~\leq\frac{1}{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|\Upsilon_{k}\|^{4}+{\mathbb{E}}\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}\|\Upsilon_{k}\|^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{\theta/2}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~={\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\frac{1}{n^{\theta-1}}\bigg)\,.

Note that the third inequality relies on Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
, and the last inequality is indicated by Lemma
5
. As a result, we have
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}\xrightarrow{p}0
, which completes the proof.

∎

9.5
Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
\delta_{l}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}^{r}(t_{l})-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t_{l})=\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}(t_{l})-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t_{l}),l=1,\ldots,m
, and let
h
be the linear interpolation of
\{t_{l},\eta_{l}\}
, defined as

h(t)=\begin{cases}\delta_{1},\quad&0\leq t\leq t_{1},\\
\frac{t_{l+1}-t}{t_{l+1}-t_{l}}\delta_{l}+\frac{t-t_{l}}{t_{l+1}-t_{l}}\delta_{l+1},\quad&t_{l}\leq t\leq t_{l+1},\\
\delta_{m},\quad&t_{l}\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Write
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}^{r}(t)=Q_{r}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)+h(t))
, where
Q_{r}
is the operator associated with the
r
th order spline
interpolation. For a general function
f
, it holds that
Q_{r}(f)
is the solution to

Q_{r}(f)=\argmin_{g\in W_{2}^{r}}\int\{g^{(r)}(t)\}^{2}dt~~~{\rm subject~to~}g(t_{l})=f(t_{l})(l=1,\ldots,m)\,.

Since
Q_{r}
is a linear operator, we have
Q_{r}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)+h(t))=Q_{r}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})+Q_{r}(h)
, and

\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{n}^{r}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{2}\leq\|Q_{r}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{2}+\|Q_{r}(h)\|_{2}\,.

The approximation error led by the
r
th spline interpolation for
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
can be bounded by
Devore and Lorentz 1993
, that is,

\|Q_{r}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim m^{-2r}\,.

Based on the proof of Theorem
2
, it holds that
\|\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|^{2}={\mathcal{O}}_{p}(1/n)
. Moreover, by choosing
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}[0,1]
, we have
\int_{0}^{1}(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}(t)-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t)){\rm d}t=O_{p}(1/n)
. Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\|Q_{r}(h)\|^{2}\lesssim\|h\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle m^{-1}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\delta^{2}_{l}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}(t_{l})-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t_{l}))^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{1}(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}(t)-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(t))^{2}{\rm d}t\{1+o_{p}(1)\}

\displaystyle=\|{\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{2}^{2}\{1+o_{p}(1)\}={\mathcal{O}}_{p}\bigg(\frac{1}{n}\bigg)\,.

∎

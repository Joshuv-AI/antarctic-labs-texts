Rationality for arbitrary closure operations and the test ideal of full extended plus closure
Zhan Jiang, Rebecca R.G

Abstract
. We extend the notion of F-rationality to other closure operations, inspired by the work of Smith, Epstein and Schwede, and Ma and Schwede, which describe F-rationality in terms of the canonical module and top local cohomology module. We give conditions for a closure operation cl on a Cohen-Macaulay complete local ring under which cl-rationality is equivalent to parameter ideals being cl-closed. We also demonstrate that full extended plus closure as defined by Heitmann and weak full extended plus closure as defined by the first named author have no big test elements.

1.
Introduction

Rational or F-rational singularities have long been a useful descriptor for rings of equal characteristic
[
FW89
,
HH94
]
. Given the proliferation of possible closure operations in the mixed characteristic case
[
MS21
,
HM18
,
Jia21a
,
PRG21
]
, it is useful to know for which closures cl we can define cl-rational singularities, and what that definition looks like. In this paper we build on two definitions originally used for F-rationality over Cohen-Macaulay local rings: first, that a ring is F-rational if one or all parameter ideals are tightly closed
[
Hoc07
]
, and second that a ring is F-rational if the annihilator of the tight closure of 0 in the injective hull of the residue field is the whole ring
[
Smi93
]
. These ideas were used more recently in
[
MS21
]
to define BCM-rationality in terms of the annihilator in the canonical module of their BCM-closure of
(0)
in
\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)
.

Using these ideas, in Section
4
we define cl-rationality for closure operations on Cohen-Macaulay local rings in terms of the canonical module and
\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)
, and show that under conditions comparable to those used for tight closure, this is equivalent to parameter ideals being cl-closed. We expect these results to be useful for researchers defining new closure operations in any characteristic, who want to check if cl-rationality coincides with rationality in equal characteristic 0, F-rationality in equal characteristic
p
, and/or BCM-rationality in any characteristic. To demonstrate the importance of choosing the right closure, in Section
5
we show that even F-rational rings may not be
\cl_{B}
-rational for the module closures coming from some finitely-generated Cohen-Macaulay modules
B
.

The other key result of this paper is that full extended plus closure (epf) as defined by Heitmann
[
Hei01
,
Hei02
]
does not have big test elements in general (see Section
3
). This gives a more explicit way to understand how epf closure is “too big” to be the right mixed characteristic closure to define singularity types. We further prove that the epf test ideals (big and finitistic) agree with those of wepf as defined by the first named author
[
Jia21a
]
, implying that the latter also lacks big test elements.

3.
Full extended plus closure

In this section, we prove that full extended plus closure has no big test elements and that its test ideals agree with those of weak full extended plus closure.

Definition 3.1

(
[
Hei02
,
R.G16
]
)
.

Let
R
be a domain. We define the
absolute integral closure

R^{+}
of
R
to be the integral closure of
R
in an algebraic closure of its fraction field.

Let
R
be a mixed characteristic local domain whose residue field has characteristic
p
. Let
N\subseteq M
be modules over
R
. We define the
full extended plus closure
of
N
in
M
to be the set of
u\in M
such that there exists some nonzero
c\in R
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

c^{1/n}\otimes u\in\text{im}\left(R^{+}\otimes_{R}N+R^{+}\otimes_{R}p^{n}M\to R^{+}\otimes_{R}M\right).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(R,m,k)
be a complete local domain of mixed characteristic
p
with
F
-finite residue field
k
. Then
\tau_{\epf}(R)=0
.

Proof.

Since
R
is a domain, the map from
R
to itself given by multiplication by
p^{n}
is injective. Taking its Matlis dual, we see that the map from
E=E_{R}(k)
to itself given by multiplication by
p^{n}
is surjective. This is preserved by tensoring over
R
with
R^{+}
. So for any element
u\in E
,
d^{\epsilon}\otimes u\in R^{+}\otimes p^{n}E
for
\epsilon>0
rational and
d\in R^{+}
. Hence
0_{E}^{\epf}=E
, which implies that
\Ann_{R}(0_{E}^{\epf})=\Ann_{R}E=0
. ∎

Definition 3.3

(
[
Jia21a
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring whose residue field has characteristic
p>0
. We define the
weak full extended plus closure
of
N
in
M
to be

N_{M}^{\wepf}:=\bigcap_{n\geqslant 0}(N+p^{n}M)_{M}^{\epf}.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain of mixed characteristic
p
with
F
-finite residue field. Then
\tau_{\wepf}(R)=0
.

Proof.

Since
0_{E}^{\wepf}=\bigcap_{n}(p^{n}E)^{\epf}_{E}
and
E=0_{E}^{\epf}\subseteq(p^{n}E)^{\epf}_{E}\subseteq E
, we have

0_{E}^{\wepf}=\bigcap_{n}(p^{n}E)_{E}^{\wepf}=\bigcap_{n}E=E.\qed

However, as a corollary of
[
MST+22
, Corollary 4.2]
, we see that full extended plus closure does have finitistic test elements:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete normal local domain
of residue characteristic
p>0
and of dimension
d
. Let
J
be the defining ideal of the singular
locus of
R
. Then there exists an integer
N
such that
J^{N}I^{\epf}\subseteq I
for all
I\subseteq R
.

Proof.

From the proof of
[
MST+22
, Corollary 4.2]
, we have

•

I^{\epf}\subseteq(I,p^{n})B\cap R
for some fixed perfectoid big Cohen-Macaulay
R^{+}
-algebra
B
and every
n
;

•

There exists some
N
such that
J^{N}\subseteq\Ima(\Hom_{R}(B,R)\to R)
.

Then the last paragraph of the proof of
[
MST+22
, Corollary 4.2]
works with
\overline{I^{h}}
replaced by
I^{\epf}
. Explicitly, for every
r\in J^{N}
, there exists
\phi\in\Hom_{R}(B,R)
such that
\phi(1)=r
. Applying
\phi
to
I^{\epf}\subseteq(I,p^{n})B\cap R
we get
rI^{\epf}\subseteq(I,p^{n})R
for every
n
. Hence
J^{N}I^{\epf}\subseteq\bigcap_{n}(I,p^{n})R=I
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain of mixed characteristic
p>0
. Then the finitistic test ideals for
\epf
and
\wepf
are the same.

Proof.

Since
\epf\leqslant\wepf

[
Jia21a
, Remark 4.2]
, any element in
\tau_{\wepf}^{fg}(R)
will also be in
\tau_{\epf}^{fg}(R)
. For the reverse containment, if
c\in\tau_{\epf}^{fg}(R)
, then
c(I,p^{n})^{\epf}\subseteq(I,p^{n})
for all
n>0
. For any
u\in I^{\wepf}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(I,p^{n})^{\epf}
, we have
cu\in(I,p^{n})
for all
n
, which in particular implies that
cu\in I
. Hence,
c\in\tau_{\wepf}^{fg}(R)
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

We do not know if or where these two closure operations agree, despite the above result. If
\epf=\wepf
in general, then this implies that
\epf
is a Dietz closure
[
Die10
]
. If they do not always agree, then they provide an interesting example of distinct closures with the same finitistic and big test ideals.

Remark 3.8
.

The results above indicate that the finitistic and big test ideals do not coincide for either epf or wepf. This gives a partial answer to
[
PRG21
, Question 3.7]
, which asks which closure operations have the property that the big and finitistic test ideals coincide. For finitistic closure operations like Frobenius and plus closure, the big and finitistic test ideals are known to coincide, and it is conjectured that they coincide for tight closure.

4.
Canonical modules and cl-rationality

In this section we define cl-rational singularities for residual, functorial closure operations cl over Cohen-Macaulay local rings. Under hypotheses comparable to those used for F-rationality, we show that cl-rational rings are those rings whose parameter ideals are cl-closed.

We begin by setting up the necessary results from closure-interior duality.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a complete local ring. Write
\vee
for the Matlis duality operator
\Hom_{R}(-,E)
, where
E
is the injective hull of
k
. If
M
is a finitely-generated or Artinian
R
-module and
N\subseteq M^{\vee}
, we define

\Ann_{M}N:=\{f\in\Hom_{R}(M^{\vee},E)\mid f(N)=0\}.

Note that by the isomorphism
M^{\vee\vee}\cong M
, we may view this as a submodule of
M
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a complete local ring and
M
a finitely-generated or Artinian
R
-module. Then for any module
M
that is finitely-generated or Artinian and any submodule
N\subseteq M^{\vee}
we have

\left(\frac{M^{\vee}}{N}\right)^{\vee}=\Ann_{M}N

Proof.

\displaystyle f\in\left(\frac{M^{\vee}}{N}\right)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\iff f\in\Hom_{R}\left(\frac{M^{\vee}}{N},E\right)

\displaystyle\iff f\in\Hom_{R}\left(M^{\vee},E\right)\text{ and $f$ kills }N

\displaystyle\iff f\in(M^{\vee})^{\vee}=M\text{ and $f$ kills }N

\displaystyle\iff f\in\Ann_{M}(N).\qed

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring and
\cl
a closure operation on
R
-modules. Then for
M
a finitely-generated or Artinian
R
-module,

\left(\frac{M^{\vee}}{0_{M^{\vee}}^{\cl}}\right)^{\vee}=\Ann_{M}(0_{M^{\vee}}^{\cl}).

In particular,

\left(\frac{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}{0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl}}\right)^{\vee}=\Ann_{\omega}(0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl}),

where
\omega
is the canonical module of
R
and
R
has Krull dimension
d
.

Proof.

The first part follows by setting
N=0_{M^{\vee}}^{\cl}
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring and
M
a finitely-generated or Artinian
R
-module. Let
N\subseteq M^{\vee}
. Then
N=0
if and only if
\Ann_{M}N=M
.

Proof.

The forward direction is immediate. For the reverse direction, note that since
E
is injective, any map
N\to E
extends to a map
M^{\vee}\to E
, i.e., an element of
M^{\vee\vee}\cong M
. By hypothesis, this map kills
N
. Hence,
\Hom_{R}(N,E)=0
, which implies that
N=0
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring of Krull dimension
d
with canonical module
\omega
and
\cl
a residual closure operation on
R
-modules. Then

\displaystyle\cl^{\smallsmile}(\omega)=\Ann_{\omega}0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl}.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
4.3
and Definition
2.8
.
∎

As a result, we see that
\cl^{\smallsmile}(\omega)
gives us a test submodule generalizing Definition
2.14
in the same way that the dual of
\cl^{\smallsmile}(R)
gives us the traditional test ideal as in
[
ES14
,
ERG21
]
.

Definition 4.6
.

We define
\tau_{\cl}(\omega):=\Ann_{\omega}0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl}
.

We apply this to define cl-rationality:

Definition 4.7
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay complete local ring with canonical module
\omega
, and
\cl
a closure operation on
R
-modules. We say that
R
is
\cl
-rational if
\tau_{\cl}(\omega)=\omega
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay complete local ring of Krull dimension
d
with canonical module
\omega
and
\cl
a residual closure operation on
R
-modules. Then
R
is
\cl
-rational if and only if
0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl}=0
.

Proof.

This follows from Definition
4.7
and Corollary
4.4
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay complete local ring of Krull dimension
d
with canonical module
\omega
. Let
\cl\leqslant\cl^{\prime}
be closure operations on
R
-modules. If
R
is
\cl^{\prime}
-rational, then

is
\cl
-rational.

Proof.

If
\cl\leqslant\cl^{\prime}
, then
\tau_{\cl^{\prime}}(\omega)\subseteq\tau_{\cl}(\omega)
. So if
\tau_{\cl^{\prime}}(\omega)=\omega
, then
\tau_{\cl}(\omega)=\omega
.
∎

Axiom 4.10
.

Let
(R,m)
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of Krull dimension
d
with canonical module
\omega
, and
\cl
a residual closure operation on
R
-modules. We say that
\cl
satisfies the
injective finiteness condition
if
0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl}=0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl_{fg}}
. In particular, finitistic closure operations satisfy this condition.

The following result indicates that our definition of
\cl
-rationality often coincides with the more ideal-theoretic definition, as in the case of F-rationality
[
Smi94
,
Hoc07
]
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay complete local ring with canonical module
\omega
, and
\cl
a residual, functorial closure operation on
R
-modules. If
R
is
\cl
-rational, then every ideal generated by a system of parameters is
\cl
-closed. If in addition
\cl
satisfies Axiom
4.10
, then the converse holds.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.8
,
R
is
\cl
-rational if and only if
0_{\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)}^{\cl}=0
.

First we prove the converse. Since
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, for any system of parameters
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
,

\mathrm{H}_{m}^{d}(R)=\varinjlim_{t}R/(x_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{d}^{t})R.

By our hypotheses,

0_{\mathrm{H}_{d}^{m}(R)}^{\cl}=0_{\mathrm{H}_{d}^{m}(R)}^{\cl_{fg}}=\sum_{G}0_{G}^{\cl},

where
G
ranges over the finitely-generated submodules of
\mathrm{H}_{d}^{m}(R)
. These include the
R/(x_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{d}^{t})
, so

\sum_{t}0_{R/(x_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{d}^{t})}^{\cl}\subseteq 0_{\mathrm{H}_{d}^{m}(R)}^{\cl_{fg}}.

Since each
G
must be contained in
R/(x_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{d}^{t})
for some
t
and
\cl
is functorial,

0_{G}^{\cl}\subseteq 0_{R/(x_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{d}^{t})}^{\cl}

for some
t
, so

0_{\mathrm{H}_{d}^{m}(R)}^{\cl_{fg}}\subseteq\sum_{t}0_{R/(x_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{d}^{t})}^{\cl}.

Hence

0_{\mathrm{H}_{d}^{m}(R)}^{\cl}=\sum_{t}0_{R/(x_{1}^{t},\ldots,x_{d}^{t})}^{\cl}.

If every ideal generated by a system of parameters is
\cl

Remark 4.22
.

To imitate the proof that an
F
-rational ring is a normal domain from
[
Hoc07
]
, one needs the following hypotheses:

(1)

The closure operation agrees with integral closure on the
0
ideal and ideals generated by a single nonzerodivisor.

(2)

If the ring is not a domain, we need
u\in(f)^{\cl}_{R}
if and only if
u\in(\bar{f})_{R/P}^{\cl}
for every minimal prime
P
.

(3)

For
R
-modules

and
T
, and
s\in S
,
(Rs)^{\cl}_{S}\times T=(Rs\times T)^{\cl}_{S\times T}
and the same in the other coordinate.

The next part of our discussion will primarily focus on finding ways to ensure the first condition holds. The second condition can be circumvented by requiring the ring to be a domain (but continuing to use these conditions to prove the ring is normal), and the third is likely to be more straightforward to prove for many common closure operations.

The part of the first condition involving
(0)
is to get the ring to be reduced if and only if
(0)^{\cl}_{R}=(0)
, which can be circumvented in the case of a domain. The other part of the first condition is trickier.

Paralleling the original result for tight closure, one axiom that would be sufficient to guarantee the rest of the first condition is Axiom 3.7 of
[

]
, which is a Briançon-Skoda type condition. This would imply in particular for a principal ideal

that
\overline{I}\subseteq I^{\cl}
. As Murayama points out in
[

, Table 1]
, most known Dietz closures satisfy this axiom.

Another way to achieve the first condition is by a result of the first named author on plus closure.
We begin by noting a useful fact about plus closure, including a proof for completeness:

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
R
be a 2-dimensional ADE singularity, so that
R
is a ring of the form

where
g(x,y)\in k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
. Let
M
be a MCM
R
-module, that is, a cokernel of a matrix of the form

is an
n\times n
square matrix with entries in

is the

R
is not
\cl_{M}
-rational.

See
[
LW12
]
for a discussion of MCM modules over these 2-dimensional ADE singularities.

Proof.

Let
J=(x,y)
, then

implies that
\sqrt{J}=\mathfrak{m}
. Note that
z\nin J
as

where
\cl=\cl_{M}
.

By definition, we need to show that for any
u\in M
,

. Write
M
as the cokernel of

, let

. Then

.
∎

Hilbert-Schmidt composition-differentiation operators on the unit ball
Ali Abkar

Abstract




Abstract.

We use the notion of radial derivative to introduce
composition-differentiation operators on the Hardy and Bergman spaces of the unit ball and the polydisk. We seek for necessary and sufficient conditions on the inducing functions to ensure that the composition-differentiation operator is Hilbert-Schmidt.

Keywords
: Composition operator; composition-differentiation operator; radial derivative; Hilbert-Schmidt operator; Bergman space; Hardy space

MSC
: 47B33, 47B32, 32A25

1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disk in the complex plane.
For
\alpha>-1
, we define the weighted Bergman space
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})

as the space of analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
for which

\|f\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(z)<+\infty,

where
dA_{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)
, here

dA(z)=\pi^{-1}dx\,dy
is the
normalized area measure in the complex plane.
It is well-known that
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
equipped with the inner product

\left<f,g\right>=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}dA_{\alpha}(z),

is a functional Hilbert.
It follows from boundedness of the point evaluation functional together with the Riesz’ representation theorem that
each
f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
can be written as

f(w)=\langle f,K_{w}^{\alpha}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{K_{w}^{\alpha}(z)}dA_{\alpha}(z),\quad w\in\mathbb{D},

where

K_{w}^{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{w})^{\alpha+2}}

is the
reproducing kernel for
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
.
More information on the theory of Bergman spaces can be found in
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
.

We mean by the polydisk the subset

\mathbb{D}^{n}=\mathbb{D}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{D}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
and by the unit ball the
set

\mathbb{B}_{n}=\{z=(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z|^{2}=|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}<1\}.

Let
\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n})
denote the space of all holomorphic functions on the unit ball.
The weighted Bergman space on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is defined as

A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n})=\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},dv_{\alpha})

where
dv_{\alpha}(z)=c_{\alpha}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dv(z)
, and

dv(z)
is the normalized volume measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
c_{\alpha}
is a positive constant so that
v_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n})=1
.
Let

J=(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n})
be a multi-index; this means that
J
is an
n
-tuple of
nonnegative integers. In this case we write
J\geq 0
. We define
|J|=j_{1}+\cdots+j_{n}
.
It can be verified that the vectors

e_{J}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(n+|J|+\alpha+1)}{J!\,\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}}\,z^{J},\quad J=(j_{1},...,j_{n}).

(1)

form an orthonormal basis for
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. Moreover, the
reproducing kernel associated to the points
(z_{1},...,z_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},...,w_{n})
of the unit ball is given by (see
[
13
]
or
[
18
]
):

K^{\alpha}(z,w)=\frac{1}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{n+1+\alpha}},

where
\langle z,w\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}\overline{w}_{k}
is the usual inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
denote the distinguished boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and let
\mathbb{S}_{n}
denote the (topological) boundary of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Let
d\sigma
denote the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
or
\mathbb{S}_{n}
. The Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
consists of holomorphic functions on the polydisk such that

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|f(r\zeta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(\zeta)<\infty.

Similarly, the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
consists of holomorphic functions in the unit ball such that

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}|f(r\zeta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(\zeta)<\infty.

It is known that for every function
f
in the Hardy space (of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
or
\mathbb{B}_{n}
), the boundary function

f(\zeta)=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}f(r\zeta)

is well-defined almost everywhere on (
\mathbb{T}^{n}
or
\mathbb{S}_{n}
). Therefore,
H^{2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with the following inner product:

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(\zeta)\overline{g(\zeta)}\mathrm{d}\sigma(\zeta),\qquad\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n},\,\,\textrm{or}\,\,\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{n}.

We should recall that for a multi-index
J
we have (see
[
13
, Prop. 1.4.9]
, or
[
18
, Lemma 1.11]
):

\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}|z^{J}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)=\frac{(n-1)!\,J!}{(n-1+|J|)!},

from which it follows that the vectors

\sqrt{\frac{(n-1+|J|)!}{(n-1)!\,J!}}\,z^{J},\quad J=(j_{1},...,j_{n}),

(2)

form an orthonormal basis for
H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
.
Now, let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}^{m}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
be a holomorphic function;

\varphi(z_{1},...,z_{m})=\Big(\varphi_{1}(z_{1},...,z_{m}),...,\varphi_{n}(z_{1},...,z_{m})\Big).

In the operator theory of one complex variables, the composition operator is defined by
C_{\varphi}(f)=f\circ\varphi
where
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of the unit disk, and
f
is a function in the Hardy or Bergman space. This notion can be easily generalized to several variables.
For a holomorphic function
\psi:\mathbb{D}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
, the weighted composition operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
is defined by

C_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=\psi\cdot\left(f\circ\varphi\right).

This operator is a rather classical one, and was studied by several authors. It was proved by M. Stessin and Kehe Zhu that only in some instances the composition operator is bounded (see
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
).

Now, the question arises as how to generalize the one-dimensional composition-differentiation operator
D_{\varphi}(f)=f^{\prime}\circ\varphi
to several variable case. To do this, we
let
f\in\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n})
be a holomorphic function of several variables. For
t>0
, we consider the radial differential operator

R^{t}f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{t}f_{k}(z),

where
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}(z)
is the homogenous expansion of
f
.
We now introduce the weighted composition-differentiation operator

{E}_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=\psi\cdot\left((Rf)\circ\varphi\right),\quad f\in\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n}),

and the higher order weighted composition-differentiation operator

{E}_{\psi,\varphi}^{t}(f)=\psi\cdot\left((R^{t}f)\circ\varphi\right),\quad t\geq 0,\,\,f\in\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n}).

The main purpose of this paper is to study conditions on the symbol
\varphi
and the function
\psi
to ensure that the operator
{E}_{\psi,\varphi}^{t}
is Hilbert-Schmidt. This will be done in section §3. We emphasize that
{E}_{\psi,\varphi}
generalizes the one-variable composition-differentiation operator

D_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=\psi\cdot(f^{\prime}\circ\varphi)
. This operator in one-dimensional case was studied by several people;
see for instance
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.

2
Composition operators

This section is devoted to the study of Hilbert-Schmidt weighted composition operators. Our approach is based on direct computations using the standard
orthonormal basis of the space in question.

Theorem 2.1

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
be holomorphic, and let
\psi
be an analytic function on the unit disk.
The operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n(\alpha+2)-2
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2/n}}{(1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
First assume that
\psi(z)\equiv 1
.
Consider the orthonormal basis

\{e_{J}(z)\}_{J\geq 0}
, where

e_{J}(z)=\frac{z_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{j_{n}}}{\gamma_{j_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{j_{n}}}.

We then have

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\frac{\varphi_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{n}^{j_{n}}}{\gamma_{j_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{j_{n}}}\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\varphi_{1}|^{2j_{1}}}{\gamma^{2}_{j_{1}}}\cdots\frac{|\varphi_{n}|^{2j_{n}}}{\gamma^{2}_{j_{n}}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\varphi_{1}(z)|^{2j_{1}}}{\gamma^{2}_{j_{1}}}\cdots\frac{|\varphi_{n}(z)|^{2j_{n}}}{\gamma^{2}_{j_{n}}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\varphi_{1}(z)|^{2j_{1}}}{\gamma^{2}_{j_{1}}}\cdots\sum_{j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\varphi_{n}(z)|^{2j_{n}}}{\gamma^{2}_{j_{n}}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{(1-|\varphi_{1}(z)|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\cdots\frac{1}{(1-|\varphi_{n}(z)|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

In general, assume that
\psi
is a holomorphic function.
The above computations reveal that

\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

is finite if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2/n}}{(1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

\square

Remark.
The same argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
shows that if

\varphi:\mathbb{D}^{m}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
is holomorphic, and if
\psi:\mathbb{D}^{m}\to\mathbb{D}
is holomorphic, then

C_{\psi,\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
, where
\beta=n(\alpha+2)-2
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2/n}}{(1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\mathrm{d}V_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Corollary 2.2

[
16
, Theorem 33]

The operator
C_{\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2}}\Big)^{\alpha+2}\mathrm{d}\lambda(z)<\infty,

where

\mathrm{d}\lambda(z)=\frac{\mathrm{d}A(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}

is the Möbius invariant measure on the unit disk.

Proof
.
For
\beta=n(\alpha+2)-2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=(\beta+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{n(\alpha+2)-2}\mathrm{d}A(z)

\displaystyle=(\beta+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{n(\alpha+2)}\mathrm{d}\lambda(z),

from which the result follows.

\square

Theorem 2.3

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
be holomorphic, and let
\psi
be an analytic function on the unit disk.
Then operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n-2
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2/n}}{1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2}}\Big)\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
First assume that
\psi(z)\equiv 1
.
Consider the following orthonormal basis

e_{J}(z)=z_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{j_{n}},\quad J=(j_{1},...,j_{n}),

for the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. We then have

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{n}^{j_{n}}\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi_{1}|^{2j_{1}}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2j_{n}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z),

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{\infty}|\varphi_{1}(z)|^{2j_{1}}\cdots\sum_{j_{n}=0}^{\infty}|\varphi_{n}(z)|^{2j_{n}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{1-|\varphi_{k}(z)|^{2}}\Big)\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

In general, assume that
\psi
is a holomorphic function on the unit disk.
The above computations reveal that

\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

is finite if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2/n}}{1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

\square

Remark.
The same argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.3
shows that if

\varphi:\mathbb{D}^{m}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\psi:\mathbb{D}^{m}\to\mathbb{D}
are holomorphic, then

C_{\psi,\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
, where
\beta=n-2
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2/n}}{1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2}}\Big)\mathrm{d}V_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Corollary 2.4

[
16
, Theorem 32]

The operator
C_{\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-\left|\varphi_{k}(z)\right|^{2}}\Big)\mathrm{d}\lambda(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
Since
\beta=n-2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=(n-1)(1-|z|^{2})^{n-2}\mathrm{d}A(z)

\displaystyle=(n-1)(1-|z|^{2})^{n}\mathrm{d}\lambda(z),

which in turn leads to the desired conclusion.

\square

As for the Hardy space of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, we should recall that for a multi-index
J
we have (see
[
13
, Prop. 1.4.9]
, or
[
18
, Lemma 1.11]
):

\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}|z^{J}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)=\frac{(n-1)!\,J!}{(n-1+|J|)!}.

This implies that the vectors

\sqrt{\frac{(n-1+|J|)!}{(n-1)!\,J!}}\,z^{J},\quad J=(j_{1},...,j_{n}),

(3)

form an orthonormal basis for
H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
.

Theorem 2.5

Let
n>1
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n-2
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
We consider the orthonormal functions defined by
(\ref{on-hardy-ball})
.
For
\psi
identically
1
we have

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1+|J|)!}{(n-1)!\,J!}\|\varphi_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{n}^{j_{n}}\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1+|J|)!}{(n-1)!\,J!}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi_{1}|^{2j_{1}}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2j_{n}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{J\geq 0}\frac{(n-1+|J|)!}{(n-1)!\,J!}\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|J|=k}\frac{(k+n-1)!}{(n-1)!}\frac{\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}}{J!}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

On the other hand, it follows from the multi-nomial formula that

\sum_{|J|=k}\frac{\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}}{J!}=\frac{\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{k}}{k!}=\frac{|\varphi|^{2k}}{k!}.

(4)

This, in turn, leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+n-1)!}{(n-1)!\,k!}|\varphi(z)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)(k+2)\cdots(k+n-1)}{(n-1)!}|\varphi(z)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{n}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

In general, we obtain

\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z),

from which the result follows.

\square

Corollary 2.6

[
16
, Theorem 30]

C_{\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}}\right)^{n}\mathrm{d}\lambda(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
As in the preceding case, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)=(n-1)(1-|z|^{2})^{n}\mathrm{d}\lambda(z),

from which the result follows.

\square

Theorem 2.7

Let
\alpha>-1
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n-1+\alpha
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha+1}}\,\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
It is easy to see that the vectors

e_{J}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(n+|J|+\alpha+1)}{J!\,\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}}\,z^{J},\quad J=(j_{1},...,j_{n}).

(5)

form an orthonormal basis for
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
.
As in the preceding theorem we have

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(n+|J|+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\,J!}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi_{1}|^{2j_{1}}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2j_{n}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|J|=k}\frac{\Gamma(k+n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}\frac{\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}}{J!}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k+n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\,k!}|\varphi|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{\big(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha+1}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

This implies that

\sum_{J\geq 0}\|C_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}
is finite if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{\big(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha+1}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

\square

Corollary 2.8

[
16
, Theorem 31]
.
Let
\alpha>-1
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the operator
C_{\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{n-1+\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}}\bigg)^{n+\alpha+1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(z)<\infty.

.
Note that in this case

\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)=(n+\alpha)(1-|z|^{2})^{n+\alpha+1}\mathrm{d}\lambda(z).

\square

3
Composition-differentiation operators

When dealing with the theory of composition operators in the unit disk, one usually considers the composition-differentiation operator defined by

D_{\varphi}(f)=f^{\prime}\circ\varphi.

Some authors consider
D_{\varphi}
as the composition of two successive operators
C_{\varphi}
and
D
where
D(f)=f^{\prime}
is the differentiation operator. For this reason, they use the notation
C_{\varphi}D
for what we denoted by
D_{\varphi}
. This operator was studied in detail by several authors; see for instance
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.

To generalize this definition to several complex variables, one encounters with the following two notions
of differentiation. The first one is to use the gradient of
f
, that is,

\nabla f=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{n}}\right).

Using this concept, one may define

G_{\varphi}(f)=(\nabla f)\circ\varphi=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\circ\varphi,\cdots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{n}}\circ\varphi\right).

It is also possible to use the invariant gradient
\widetilde{\nabla}f
instead of the gradient of
f
.
Another approach is to use the notion of radial derivative of a holomorphic function. It turns out that the second approach is more suitable for our purposes.
For a function
f
holomorphic in the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, we write

Rf(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{k}}(z).

It is easy to verify that if

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}(z)

is the homogenous expansion of
f
, then

Rf(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kf_{k}(z).

Note that if

f(z)=e_{J}(z)=z_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{j_{n}}
is a multi-nomial,
then

f(z)=f_{k}(z)
where
k=|J|
. This implies that

Rf(\varphi(z))=k\,e_{J}(\varphi(z))=|J|\,\varphi_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{n}^{j_{n}}.

(6)

This identity reveals that the radial derivative is a good candidate to play the role of
f^{\prime}
when
f
is a
holomorphic function of several complex variables.

As in the case of usual differentiation, one can define higher order radial derivatives. Let
t
be a real number.
We define the radial differential operator
R^{t}
as follows:

R^{t}f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{t}f_{k}(z),\quad f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}(z).

We now define the
n
-variable weighted composition-differentiation operator

{E}_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=\psi\cdot\left((Rf)\circ\varphi\right),\quad f\in\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n}),

(7)

and the higher order
n
-variable weighted composition-differentiation operator

{E}_{\psi,\varphi}^{t}(f)=\psi\cdot\left((R^{t}f)\circ\varphi\right),\quad t\geq 0,\,\,f\in\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n}).

(8)

It is easy to see that if

f(z)=e_{J}(z)=z_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{j_{n}}
,
then

R^{t}f(\varphi(z))=k^{t}\,\varphi_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{n}^{j_{n}},\quad k=|J|.

(9)

For more information on the concept of fractional radial derivatives and their applications in function theory of several complex variables, we refer the reader to the book authored by Kehe Zhu
[
18
]
; who first introduced this notion in
[
14
]
.

In the following, we aim to study the conditions that are equivalent to Hilbert-Schmidtness of the operators
{E}_{\psi,\varphi}^{t}
for
t\geq 0
.
Before we shift to our main discussion, it is instructive to see how the weighted composition-differentiation operator

D_{\psi,\varphi}(f):=\psi(z)f^{\prime}(\varphi(z))

behaves in one-dimensional case. We recall that an operator
T
defined from a Hilbert space
H_{1}
to another Hilbert space
H_{2}
is called Hilbert-Schmidt if for some orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}
in
H_{1}
we have
\sum_{n}\|Te_{n}\|^{2}<\infty
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\varphi
be an analytic self-map of the unit disk, and
\psi
be an analytic function
\mathbb{D}
. Then
D_{\psi,\varphi}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{4+\alpha}}\mathrm{d}A_{\alpha}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
We consider the orthonormal basis that consists of the unit vectors

e_{k}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(k+\alpha+2)}{k!\,\Gamma(\alpha+2)}}\,z^{k},\quad k=0,1,2,\cdots.

We now have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|D_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{k})\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg|\psi(z)k\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(k+\alpha+2)}{k!\,\Gamma(\alpha+2)}}\,\varphi(z)^{k-1}\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}A_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k\,\Gamma(k+\alpha+2)}{(k-1)!\,\Gamma(\alpha+2)}\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2(k-1)}\mathrm{d}A_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)\,\Gamma(k+\alpha+3)}{k!\,\Gamma(\alpha+2)}\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\alpha}(z)

We now use the fact that (for
x>0
)

\Gamma(k+x)\asymp k^{x}\,\Gamma(x),\quad\,k\to\infty,

to conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|D_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{k})\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)\,\Gamma(k+\alpha+3)}{k!\,\Gamma(\alpha+2)}\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k+\alpha+4)}{k!\,\Gamma(\alpha+4)}\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{\alpha+4}}\mathrm{d}A_{\alpha}(z).

This completes the proof.

\square

We now return to the
n
-variable definition of weighted composition-differentiation operator

E_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=\psi\cdot\left((Rf)\circ\varphi\right),\quad f\in\hol(\mathbb{B}_{n}).

Theorem 3.2

Let
\alpha>-1
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the operator
E_{\psi,\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n-1+\alpha
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha+3}}\,\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
We start by the orthonormal vectors
(\ref{on-bergman-ball})

for
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. It follows that

\displaystyle\|E_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}=\frac{|J|^{2}\,\Gamma(n+|J|+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\,J!}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2j_{1}}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2j_{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

Using the multi-nomial formula

\sum_{|J|=k}\frac{\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}}{J!}=\frac{\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{k}}{k!}=\frac{|\varphi|^{2k}}{k!},

(10)

and
(\ref{rfphi})
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|E_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|J|=k}\frac{|J|^{2}\,\Gamma(|J|+n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}\frac{\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}}{J!}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}\,\Gamma(k+n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\,k!}|\varphi|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k+n+\alpha+3)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+3)\,k!}|\varphi|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha+3}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z),

from which the result follows.

\square

Note that if
n=1
, then
\beta=\alpha
, so that the above result reduces to the one obtained in Theorem
3.1
. This phenomenon confirms that the new notion of radial differentiation is a good generalization of the usual concept of differentiation.

Theorem 3.3

Let
n>1
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the operator
E_{\psi,\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n-2
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{n+2}}\,\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
For the orthonormal functions
(\ref{on-hardy-ball})

in
H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
we have

\displaystyle\|E_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|J|^{2}\,(n-1+|J|)!}{(n-1)!\,J!}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|E_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}\,(k+n-1)!}{(n-1)!\,k!}|\varphi(z)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+n+1)!}{(n+1)!\,k!}|\varphi(z)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{n+2}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

\square

We now take up the composition-differentiation operators
(\ref{higher-order})
defined by higher order radial derivatives.

Theorem 3.4

Let
\alpha>-1
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the operator
E^{t}_{\psi,\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n-1+\alpha
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha+2t+1}}\,\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
We start by the orthonormal vectors
(\ref{on-bergman-ball})
.
It follows from
(\ref{rtfphi})
that

\displaystyle\|E_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}=\frac{|J|^{2t}\,\Gamma(n+|J|+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\,J!}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2j_{1}}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2j_{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

Therefore from
(\ref{multi})
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|E_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2t}\,\Gamma(k+n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\,k!}|\varphi|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k+n+\alpha+2t+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+2t+1)\,k!}|\varphi|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha+2t+1}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z),

from which the result follows.

\square

Theorem 3.5

Let
n>1
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
be a holomorphic function.
Then the operator
E^{t}_{\psi,\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\to A_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\beta=n-2
, is Hilbert-Schmidt if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{\big(1-\left|\varphi(z)\right|^{2}\big)^{n+2t}}\,\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Proof
.
Choosing
e_{J}(z)
as in
(\ref{on-hardy-ball})
,
we have

\displaystyle\|E^{t}_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|_{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|J|^{2t}\,(n-1+|J|)!}{(n-1)!\,J!}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\left(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}\cdots|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{J}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{J\geq 0}\|E^{t}_{\psi,\varphi}(e_{J})\|^{2}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2t}\,(k+n-1)!}{(n-1)!\,k!}|\varphi(z)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+n+2t-1)!}{(n+2t-1)!\,k!}|\varphi(z)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\psi(z)|^{2}}{(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{n+2t}}\mathrm{d}A_{\beta}(z).

\square

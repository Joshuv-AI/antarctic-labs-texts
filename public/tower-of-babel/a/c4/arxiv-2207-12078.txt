MONOIDS RELATED TO SELF HOMOTOPY EQUIVALENCES OF FIBRED PRODUCT
Gopal Chandra Dutta, Debasis Sen

Abstract
. We introduce a nested sequence of monoids related to self homotopy equivalences of fibrewise pointed spaces, such that the limit is the group of homotopy classes of fibrewise pointed self-equivalences. We explore this monoid for fibred product in terms of individual spaces. Further we study two related invariants associated to these monoids: self closeness number and self length.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 55R70, 55P10 ; Secondary: 55R05, 55Q05.

1.
Introduction

The group of homotopy classes of self homotopy equivalences
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
of a connected based space
X
has been studied extensively for a long time (cf.
[
1
,
18
]
). Various subgroups of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
have also been studied: e.g. the subgroup consists of the self-homotopy equivalences that induces the identity homomorphism on homotopy groups upto certain range (cf.
[
2
,
16
,
20
]
). Motivated by this, Choi and Lee introduced a sequence of monoids related to
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
(see
[
4
]
). They defined
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\{f\in[X,X]~~:~~\pi_{i}(f)\text{ is an isomorphism for }0\leq i\leq k\}
. Clearly
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X).
Related to this chain of inclusions, they also studied two invariants: self-closeness number and self length (see
[
5
,
9
]
).

In this paper we introduce similar monoids for parametrized homotopy and study them for a fibred product in terms of the individual spaces. Let
B
be a connected pointed CW-complex. A
fibrewise pointed space
over
B
consists of a space
X
over
B
with a section:

B\xrightarrow{s}X\xrightarrow{p}B,\text{ }p\circ s=\operatorname{Id}_{B}.

We denote it by the triple
(X,p,s)
(
[
6
]
). Sometimes it is also called an
ex-space
(cf.
[
10
]
). A
fibrewise pointed map

f\colon X\to Y
is a map such that
q\circ f=p
and
f\circ s=t
. The collection of all such maps from
X
to
Y
is denoted by
\operatorname{map}^{B}_{B}(X,Y)
. Two fibrewise pointed maps
f,g\colon X\to Y
are called
fibrewise pointed homotopic
if there exists a homotopy
h_{\bar{t}}\colon X\to Y
such that
h_{0}=f,~~h_{1}=g
and
q\circ h_{\bar{t}}=p,~~h_{\bar{t}}\circ s=t,\forall~~\bar{t}\in I=[0,1]
and written as
f\simeq^{B}_{B}g
. This is an equivalence relation on the set
\operatorname{map}^{B}_{B}(X,Y)
and the set of equivalence classes is denoted by
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}
. For notational convenience, we do not distinguish between a map
f\colon X\to Y
and that of its homotopy class in
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}
.
The group of homotopy classes of fibrewise pointed self-equivalences of
X
is denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. This group has been studied for fibred product of spaces by several authors (cf.
[
3
,
15
]
). The group of homotopy classes of self equivalences for cartesian product (
B=\ast
) was also studied before (cf.
[
8
,
17
,
19
]
)

Let
\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
denote the full subcategory of fibrewise pointed spaces
(X,p,s)
with
p\colon X\to B
being a fibration, the total space
X
and fibre of
p
have the homotopy type of connected CW-complexes.

1.1 Definition
.

For a space
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
and a non-negative integer
k
, we define

\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X):=\{f\in[X,X]^{B}_{B}:~~~\pi_{i}(f)\colon\pi_{i}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{i}(X),~~0\leq i\leq k\}.

If
B=\ast
then
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X).
Under the composition of maps the set
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
becomes a submonoid of
[X,X]^{B}_{B}
. If
m>n
, then
\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)
. Therefore we have the following chain of inclusion,

(1.2)

{}\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{n+1}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{1}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{0}_{\#}(X)=[X,X]^{B}_{B}.

In the first part of this paper we study the monoid
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}
for fibred product. This is an extension of the results of Jun and Lee on factorisation of the monoid
\mathcal{A}^{k}(X\times Y)
of product spaces (cf.
[
9
]
). Recall that the fibred product of
(X,p,s)
and
(Y,q,t)\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
is denoted by

and is defined by the pull-back of the diagram

The section maps
s,t
induce maps

\iota_{X}\colon X\to X\times_{B}Y,~~\iota_{Y}\colon Y\to X\times_{B}Y,

which are defined as

\iota_{X}(x):=(x,tp(x)),~~\iota_{Y}(y):=(sq(y),y).

Clearly
\iota_{X},~\iota_{Y}
are sections of
p_{X},~q_{Y}
respectively. A self-map
f\colon X\times_{B}Y\to X\times_{B}Y
is equivalent to a pair of maps
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})
where
f_{X}:X\times_{B}Y\to X
and
f_{Y}:X\times_{B}Y\to Y
are maps in
\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
. Given
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{map}^{B}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y,X\times_{B}Y)
, then
(p_{X},f_{Y}),(f_{X},p_{Y})
are self-maps of
X\times_{B}Y
.

As in
[
9
]
, we need the following notion of reducibility to state our results.

1.3 Definition
.

A map
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is said to be
k
-reducible
if
(p_{X},f_{Y}),~~(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
.

Recall that, in (cf.
[
15
, Section 1]
), a map
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
was defined to be reducible if
(f_{X},p_{Y})
and
(p_{X},f_{Y})
both are in
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Therefore a map
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
is reducible if it is
k
-reducible for any non-negative integer
k
.
We have two natural sub-monoids of
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y
):

(1.4)

{}\begin{split}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)&:=\{f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y):~p_{X}\circ f=p_{X}\},\\
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)&:=\{f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y):~p_{Y}\circ f=p_{Y}\}.\end{split}

We show that the product of this two sub-monoids is the whole of
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
. This can be thought as a monoid analogue of the result of Pavešić
[
15
, Theorem 2.5]
and a parametrized generalization of
[
9
, Theorem 1]
. We define a further submonoid,

\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y):=\{(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y):~~(f_{X},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{X}=\iota_{X}\}.

Similarly
\mathfrak{A}^{k,Y}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)
is defined. By a split exact sequence of monoids, we mean usual definition in the case of groups forgetting the existence of inverse.

Theorem A
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}.
Then we have split exact sequences of monoids,

0\to\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)\xrightarrow{}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\to 0,

0\to\mathfrak{A}^{k,Y}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\xrightarrow{}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\to 0.

Moreover if each map
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is
k
-reducible and satisfying the condition
f_{Y}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y}
, then

\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\cdot\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y).

Similar result also holds for higher fibre product.

(See Theorem
2.11
, Theorem
5.6
, Proposition
5.7
for details.)

Booth and Heath obtained an exact sequence relating
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
and
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
(
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
). We obtain a similar result for the monoid
\mathfrak{A}_{\#}.

Theorem B
.

Assume that
X
is a fibrewise group-like space such that
[X\wedge_{B}Y,X]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
and
[Y,X]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
. Then we have the following short exact sequence of monoids,

0\to\mathfrak{A}^{k,Y}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)\xrightarrow{}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\times\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\to 0.

Similar result also holds for higher fibred product.

(See Theorem
2.15
, Theorem
5.8
for details.)

Observe that for finite dimensional
X
, we have
\mathfrak{A}^{\operatorname{dim}X}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. In general,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
, by Whitehead’s theorem and the fact that a homotopy equivalence between ex-spaces with the projection maps fibrations, is actually a fibre homotopy equivalence (cf.
[
7
]
). This motivates us to define parametrized
self-closeness number
of
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
as the minimum non-negative integer
k
such that
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. We denote it by
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
. This is a generalisation of self-closeness number introduced by Choi, Lee as
N\mathcal{A}(X):=\min\big\{k:\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X),~~k\geq 0\big\}
(cf.
[
4
]
). They also introduced a related invariant
self-length
of a space
X
(
[
5
]
). We define similar self-length in our parametrized setting as the number of strict inclusions in the chain of Equation
1.2
. We denote it by
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
. We obtain several properties of
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
and
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X),
and prove the following result.

Theorem C
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
.

(i)

Then the self-closeness number satisfies

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)\geq\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)\big\}.

In addition, the equality hold if for any non-negative integer
k
, each map in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}(X\times_{B}Y)
is
k
-reducible.

(ii)

For the self-length, we have

L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)+L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)-\#\big(I_{(X)}\cap I_{(Y)}\big)\leq L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)

Moreover the equality holds if
Y
is fibrewise group-like satisfying
[X\wedge_{B}Y,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
and
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
.

(See Theorems
3.6
,
3.7
,
4.6
for details.)

1.5
.

Organisation.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we consider the monoid
\mathfrak{A}_{\#}^{k}
for the fibred product of two spaces. We obtain several results on
k
-reducibility to prove Theorem A and Theorem B. In Section
3

2.
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)

In this section we study the monoid
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}
(cf. Definition

) for the fibred product
X\times_{B}Y
in terms of the individual spaces
X
and
Y
. First we recall some basic definitions (see
[

]
). Let
(X,p,s)
and
(Y,q,t)
be fibrewise pointed spaces over
B
.
A fibrewise pointed map
f\colon X\to Y
is called
fibrewise constant
if
f=t\circ p
. A fibrewise pointed map fibrewise homotopic to the fibrewise constant is called a
fibrewise pointed null-homotopic

We now obtain some general results regarding the monoid
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)

{}X_{b}:=p^{-1}(b),~~~~f_{b}:=f|_{X_{b}}:~X_{b}\to X_{b}.

f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)

Converse can be proved similarly.

We now obtain an equivalent criterion of
k
-reducibility.

2.6 Proposition
.

Let
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{map}^{B}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y,X\times_{B}Y)
. Then
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
if and only if
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
and
(p_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
if and only if
f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
.

In particular,
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
if and only if
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
and
(p_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
if and only if
f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.2
we say that
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
if and only if
(f_{X},p_{Y})_{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})
. Observe that
(f_{X},p_{Y})_{b}=(f_{X},p_{Y})|_{X_{b}\times Y_{b}}=(f_{X_{b}},p_{Y_{b}})
. Moreover using
[
9
, Lemma 2]
we have
(f_{X_{b}},p_{Y_{b}})\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})
if and only if
f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b})
, where
\iota_{X_{b}}\colon X_{b}\to X_{b}\times Y_{b}
be a map defined as
x\mapsto(x,t(b)),~~~\forall x\in X_{b}
. Observe that
(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X})_{b}=(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X})|_{X_{b}}=f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}}
,

X\xrightarrow{\iota_{X}}X\times_{B}Y\xrightarrow{f_{X}}X.

Therefore
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{K}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
if and only if
(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X})_{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b})
. By using the Proposition
2.2
we get the desired result.
The proof of the other part is similar.

∎

2.7 Corollary
.

A map
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is
k
-reducible if
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
and
f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
.

In particular, a map
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
is reducible if
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
and
f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
.

The following result gives some sufficient conditions for reducibility.

2.8 Proposition
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
and
k
be a non-negative integer.

(a)

If
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
or
[Y,X]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
, then each map
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is
k
-reducible.

(b)

Assume that, for some
b\in B
, any one of the sets
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{i}(X_{b}),\pi_{i}(Y_{b}))
and
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{i}(Y_{b}),\pi_{i}(X_{b}))
is trivial for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then each map
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is
k
-reducible.

Proof.

(a)

Let
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Then
(f_{X_{b}},f_{Y_{b}})=(f_{X},f_{Y})_{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})
from Proposition
2.2
. So the induced homomorphism
\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}},f_{Y_{b}})
is isomorphism for all
0\leq i\leq k
. Moreover using
[
17
, Section 2]
, we can say that
\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}},f_{Y_{b}})
is isomorphism if and only if the following matrix is invertible:

(2.9)

{}M_{i}(f_{X_{b}},f_{Y_{b}}):=\begin{bmatrix}\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})&\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})\\
\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})&\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})\par\end{bmatrix}

If
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
, then
f_{Y}\circ\iota_{X}\simeq^{B}_{B}t\circ p
. Thus
(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})=(f_{Y}\circ\iota_{X})_{b}
is homotopic to a constant map, i.e.
\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})=0
. Hence
\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})~~\text{and}~~\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})
are isomorphisms. Therefore
\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})~~\text{and}~~\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})
are isomorphisms for all

. So that,

(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X})_{b}=(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b}),~~(f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y})_{b}=(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y_{b}).

Moreover, using Proposition

we have
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X),~~f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
. We will get similar result if

.

(b)

Assume that
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{i}(X_{b}),\pi_{i}(Y_{b}))=\{\ast\}
for some

. Then for any map
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)

\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})\colon\pi_{i}(X_{b})\to\pi_{i}(Y_{b})
is trivial. Therefore using the matrix

of Equation
2.9
, we have
\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})~~\text{and}~~\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})
are isomorphisms. We will get similar result if we assume
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{i}(Y_{b}),\pi_{i}(X_{b}))=\{\ast\}
. Therefore
\pi_{i}(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})~~\text{and}~~\pi_{i}(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})
are isomorphisms for all
0\leq i\leq k
. So that,

(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X})_{b}=(f_{X_{b}}\circ\iota_{X_{b}})\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b}),~~(f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y})_{b}=(f_{Y_{b}}\circ\iota_{Y_{b}})\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y_{b}).

Hence
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X),~~f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
from Proposition
2.2
.

∎

2.11 Theorem
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}.
Then we have split exact sequences of monoids,

0\to\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)\xrightarrow{\phi_{X}}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\to 0,

0\to\mathfrak{A}^{k,Y}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\xrightarrow{\phi_{Y}}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\to 0.

Moreover if each map
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is
k
-reducible and
f_{Y}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y}
, then

\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\cdot\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y).

Proof.

From Proposition
2.6
, the map
\phi_{X}
is well-defined. Let us observe that
for a self-map
g\colon X\to X
, we have
p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X}\circ g=p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X}.
This is because,

p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X}\circ g(x)=p_{Y}(g(x),tpg(x))=tpg(x)=tp(x)=p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X}(x),~~\forall x\in X.

Taking
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)

and
g=f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}
, we have

\iota_{X}\circ f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}=(p_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ f_{X}\circ\iota_{X},p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X}\circ f_{X}\circ\iota_{X})=(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X},p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X})=(f_{X},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{X}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\phi_{X}\big((f_{X},p_{Y})\circ(h_{X},p_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle=\phi_{X}\big(f_{X}\circ(h_{X},p_{Y}),p_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle=f_{X}\circ(h_{X},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{X}

\displaystyle=f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ h_{X}\circ\iota_{X}

\displaystyle=\phi_{X}(f_{X},p_{Y})\circ\phi_{X}(h_{X},p_{Y}).

Hence
\phi_{X}
is a homomorphism of monoids. We now identify
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{X})
with
\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Take
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{X})
then
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}=\operatorname{Id}_{X}
. Therefore,

(f_{X},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{X}=(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X},p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X})=(\operatorname{Id}_{X},p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X})=(p_{X}\circ\iota_{X},p_{Y}\circ\iota_{X})=(p_{X},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{X}=\iota_{X}.

Thus
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{X})\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)
. To show the other inclusion,
take
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Then
(f_{X},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{X}=\iota_{X}
. Pre-composing both sides with
p_{X}
we obtain
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}=\operatorname{Id}_{X}.
Hence
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{X})=\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y).

It remains to show that there exists a splitting map of
\phi_{X}
. Define

\psi_{X}\colon\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y),~~~h\mapsto(h\circ p_{X},p_{Y}).

From Proposition
2.6
, the map
\psi_{X}
is well-defined. It is easy to verify that
\psi_{X}
is a homomorphism such that
\phi_{X}\circ\psi_{X}=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)}
.
Therefore we get the split exact sequence. Similarly we will obtain the other split exact sequence.

For the remaining part of the Theorem, observe that

\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\cap A^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)=\{\ast\}.

Therefore if the factorization of an element in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
exists, it must be unique. Let
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Then by
k
-reducibility we have
(p_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)
and
(f_{X},p_{Y})\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Then,

\displaystyle(p_{X},f_{Y})\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})

\displaystyle=(f_{X},f_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y}))

\displaystyle\simeq^{B}_{B}(f_{X},f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y}))

\displaystyle=(f_{X},f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y})

\displaystyle\simeq^{B}_{B}(f_{X},f_{Y}).

∎

2.11
to be satisfied. Recall that the

Therefore

X\wedge_{B}Y:=(X\times_{B}Y)/_{B}(X\vee_{B}Y)

2.12 Lemma
.

With notations as above, assume that
\iota^{\#}\colon[X\times_{B}Y,Y]^{B}_{B}\to[X\vee_{B}Y,Y]^{B}_{B}
is injective and
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}

f_{Y}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
\iota^{\#}(f_{Y})=\iota^{\#}(f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y})
, i.e.
f_{Y}\circ\iota=f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y}\circ\iota

and
\iota_{2}.
If we precompose with
\iota_{1}
, then both side becomes element of

Moreover for precomposing with
\iota_{2}
, observe that,

f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y}\circ\iota\circ\iota_{2}=f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ p_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}=f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}=f_{Y}\circ\iota\circ\iota_{2}.

∎

2.13 Corollary
.

Assume

and

and

We get the reducibility of maps from Lemma

and

2.15 Theorem
.

Assume that
X
is a fibrewise group-like space such that
[X\wedge_{B}Y,X]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
and
[Y,X]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
. Then we have the following split exact sequence of monoids,

0\to\mathfrak{A}^{k,Y}_{\#,X}(X\times_{B}Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\times\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\to 0,

where
\phi(f_{X},f_{Y})=(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}).

Proof.

Since
[Y,X]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
, then each map
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is

-reducible from Proposition
2.8
(a). So the map
\phi
is well-defined. Moreover, from Corollary
2.13
we have
f_{X}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X}
, for each self-map

. As in the proof of the first part of Theorem
2.11
we have
\iota_{Y}\circ f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}=(p_{X},f_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\phi\big((f_{X},f_{Y})\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle=\phi\big(f_{X}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y}),f_{Y}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(f_{X}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle\simeq^{B}_{B}\big(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ(g_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(f_{X}\circ\iota_{x}\circ g_{X}\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ(g_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X}\circ\iota_{Y},g_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ g_{X}\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ(p_{X}\circ\iota_{Y},g_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ g_{X}\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ(p_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ g_{X}\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ g_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle=\phi(f_{X},f_{Y})\circ\phi(g_{X},g_{Y}).

This shows that
\phi
is a monoid homomorphism.

Any element of
\mathfrak{A}^{k,X}_{\#,Y}(X\times_{B}Y)
is of the form
(f_{X},p_{Y})
satisfying

. Precomposing with

p_{X}
we have
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X}=p_{X}

.

. So

can be identified with

Note that,

(f\circ p_{X},g\circ p_{Y})=(f\circ p_{X},p_{Y})\circ(p_{X},g\circ p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y),

since
(f\circ p_{X},p_{Y}),~(p_{X},g\circ p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
from Proposition
2.6
. Therefore

is well-defined.
Clearly

is a monoid homomorphism such that

. This gives a splitting map of

.

3.

The self-closeness number of a space was introduced by Choi and Lee in

4
]
. Some computations of this invariant were further done in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. We define a parametrized version of self-closeness number.
Recall that we have the following chain:

\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{n+1}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{1}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{0}_{\#}(X)=[X,X]^{B}_{B}.

3.1 Definition
.

The
self-closeness number
of
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
is defined as

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X):=\min\big\{k:\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X),~~k\geq 0\big\}.

If no such
k
exists, then
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
is defined to be infinite.

3.2 Lemma
.

Let
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
is
m
-connected and of dimension
n
where
m<n
. Then
m+1\leq N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq n
, provided that
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)\neq[X,X]^{B}_{B}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is
m
connected, we have
\pi_{i}(X)=0,~~~\forall~i\leq m
. Therefore

\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}(X)=\cdots=\mathfrak{A}^{1}_{\#}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{0}_{\#}(X)=[X,X]^{B}_{B}.

Since
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)\neq[X,X]^{B}_{B}
, thus
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\geq m+1
. To complete the proof, it is sufficient to show that
\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. Let
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)
, then the map
\pi_{i}(f)\colon\pi_{i}(X)\to\pi_{i}(X)
is isomorphism for all
\forall~i\leq n
. From
[
6
, Proposition 17.6]
, we have
f_{*}\colon[X,X]^{B}_{B}\to[X,X]^{B}_{B}
is a surjective. Therefore there is a map
h\in[X,X]^{B}_{B}
such that
f_{*}(h)=\operatorname{Id}_{X},~~\text{i.e.}~~f\circ h\simeq^{B}_{B}\operatorname{Id}_{X}
. So
\pi_{i}(f)\circ\pi_{i}(h)=\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{i}(X)}.
Thus
\pi_{i}(h)
is an isomorphism for all
i\leq n
. Therefore
h_{*}\colon[X,X]^{B}_{B}\to[X,X]^{B}_{B}
is surjective, so there is a map
g\in[X,X]^{B}_{B}
such that
h_{*}(g)=\operatorname{Id}_{X},~\text{i.e.}~h\circ g\simeq^{B}_{B}\operatorname{Id}_{X}
. Thus
f\circ h\circ g\simeq^{B}_{B}f\Rightarrow g\simeq^{B}_{B}f
. Therefore
f\circ h\simeq^{B}_{B}h\circ f\simeq^{B}_{B}\operatorname{Id}_{X}
, consequently
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
.

∎

The following Corollary give us some condition when
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
is equal to an integer
n
.

3.3 Corollary
.

Let
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
is
n-1
connected and of dimension
n
. If
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)\neq[X,X]^{B}_{B}
, then
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=n
.

We now show that
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
is homotopy invariant.

3.4 Proposition
.

If
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
are fibrewise pointed homotopy equivalent, then
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that given any non-negative integer
k,~\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
if and only if
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
. Let
\phi\colon X\to Y
be a fibrewise pointed homotopy equivalence with homotopy inverse
\psi\colon Y\to X
.
Then
\phi_{*}\colon\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
defined as
f\mapsto\phi\circ f\circ\psi
, is a monoid isomorphism with inverse
\psi_{*}\colon\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
.
Therefore the restriction map
\phi_{*}\colon\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
is an isomorphism of group.
Let us assume that
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
. If
h\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
then
\psi_{*}(h)\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. Therefore
\phi_{*}\big(\psi_{*}(h)\big)\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
, i.e.
h\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
. Thus
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
, i.e.
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
. Similarly we can prove that if
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
then
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
.

∎

3.5 Lemma
.

Let
(X,p,s)\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, where
X_{b}\xrightarrow{\iota}X\xrightarrow{p}B
. Then

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}(X_{b}).

If
p
is a trivial fibration then the equality holds.

Proof.

We have to consider only the case
N\mathcal{A}(X_{b})<\infty
. Assume that
N\mathcal{A}(X_{b})=m
. Let
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}(X)
. From Proposition
2.2
we have
f_{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{m}_{\#}(X_{b})=\operatorname{Aut}(X_{b})
. Therefore using the Proposition
2.2
again we have
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. So
\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. Hence by the definition of self-closeness number we have
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq m=N\mathcal{A}(X)
.

If
p
is a trivial fibration, then
X=X_{b}\times B
with
p
is a second coordinate projection. If possible let
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
where
k<m
. Then for any element
g\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b})
we have
g\times\operatorname{Id}_{B}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
.
Using Proposition
2.2
we have
(g\times\operatorname{Id}_{B})_{b}=g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X_{b})
. Thus
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{b})=\operatorname{Aut}(X_{b})
, which contradict the fact that
N\mathcal{A}(X_{b})=m
. Hence from the definition of self-closeness number we get
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=m=N\mathcal{A}(X_{b})
.

∎

Having established some basic properties of parametrized self-closeness number, we now find this number of fibred product in terms of the individual spaces.

3.6 Theorem
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, then we have

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)\geq\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)\big\}.

Proof.

Consider the case
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)<\infty
. Let us assume that
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)=n
. It is sufficient to show that for any
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X),~~g\in\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(Y)
implies
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
and
g\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
. From Proposition
2.2
we have
f_{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\#}(X_{b})
and
g_{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\#}(Y_{b})
for some
b\in B
. Therefore
\pi_{i}(f_{b})
and
\pi_{i}(g_{b})
are isomorphisms for all
i=0,\cdots,n
. So,
\pi_{i}(f_{b}\times g_{b})
are isomorphisms for
i=0,\cdots,n
i.e.
f_{b}\times g_{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\#}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})
.
Now,
f\times_{B}g\colon X\times_{B}Y\to X\times_{B}Y
is a fibrewise pointed map defined by
(f\times_{B}g)(x,y):=\big(f(x),g(y)\big)
. Moreover
(f\times_{B}g)_{b}=(f\times_{B}g)|_{X_{b}\times Y_{b}}=(f_{b}\times g_{b})
. Therefore from Proposition
2.2
we have
f\times_{B}g\in\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y).
Again using Proposition
2.2
we have
(f_{b}\times g_{b})\in\operatorname{Aut}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})
.
Thus
\pi_{i}(f_{b}\times g_{b})
is an isomorphisms for all nonnegative integers
i
. So the maps
\pi_{i}(f_{b})\times\pi_{i}(g_{b})\colon\pi_{i}(X_{b})\times\pi_{i}(Y_{b})\to\pi_{i}(X_{b})\times\pi_{i}(Y_{b})
are isomorphisms for all
i\geq 0
. Therefore
\pi_{i}(f_{b})
and
\pi_{i}(g_{b})
are isomorphisms for all
i\geq 0
. So
f_{b}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X_{b})
and
g_{b}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y_{b})
. Hence
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X),~~g\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
from Proposition
2.2
. This implies that
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq n
and
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)\leq n
. Hence we have the desired result.

∎

The following result shows that if we assume the condition of reducibility, then we have equality in the above Theorem
3.6
.

3.7 Theorem
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
. For any
k\geq 0
, assume that each map of
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
is
k
-reducible. Then

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)=\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)\big\}.

Proof.

If
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
or
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)
is infinite then
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
is infinite from Theorem
3.6
. So we only need to consider the case
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)<\infty
and
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)<\infty
. Let
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=m,~N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)=n
and
k=\max\{m,n\}
. Take
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Then
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
and
f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
, using Corollary
2.7
. Since
k=\max\{m,n\}
, therefore
f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
and
f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(Y)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
. Hence by Proposition
2.6
, we have

(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y),~(p_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y).

As in the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 2.5]
we have,

f=(f_{X},f_{Y})=\big(p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1}\big)\circ(f_{X},p_{Y}).

Note that,
(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1}=(\bar{f}_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
(cf.
[
14
, Proposition 2.3(a)]
).
Observe that

(p_{X},f_{Y})\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1}=(\bar{f}_{X},f_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y).

By reducibility assumption we get
f_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(Y)
. Therefore
(p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
from Proposition
2.6
. Consequently
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
.
Therefore
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times_{B}Y)
, so
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)\leq k
. Using Theorem
3.6
we get

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)=\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)\}.

∎

Combining Proposition
2.8
(a) and Theorem
3.7
we obtain the following Corollary.

3.8 Corollary
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
such that
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}~~(\text{ or }[Y,X]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\})
, then

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)=\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)\big\}.

3.9 Example
.

Let
(X,p,s)
and
(B\times K(G,n),q,t)\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
are two ex-spaces over
B
, where
q\colon B\times K(G,n)\to B
is first coordinate projection. Observe that
X\times_{B}(B\times K(G,n))\cong X\times K(G,n)
. Therefore
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(X\times_{B}(B\times K(G,n))\big)=N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(X\times K(G,n)\big)
. Moreover, if
\pi_{i}(X)=0
for all
i>n
then
\pi_{i}\big(X\times K(G,n)\big)=0
for all
i>n
.
Therefore
\mathfrak{A}_{\#}^{n}(X\times K(G,n))=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X\times K(G,n)
. Hence,

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(X\times_{B}(B\times K(G,n))\big)=N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(X\times K(G,n)\big)\leq n.

3.10 Example
.

Consider two ex-spaces
\big(K(G,m)\times B,p,s\big)
and
\big(M(G,n)\times B,q,t\big)
over
B
, where
G
is an abelian group with
n\geq 3
and
m<n
. Observe that self-maps of
K(G,m)\times M(G,n)
are reducible (see
[
14
, Corollary 2.2]
). Therefore each self-map of
\big(K(G,m)\times B\big)\times_{B}\big(M(G,n)\times B\big)
is reducible using Corollary
2.5
. Moreover
\big(K(G,m)\times B\big)\times_{B}\big(M(G,n)\times B\big)\cong K(G,m)\times M(G,n)\times B
. From Theorem
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(K(G,m)\times M(G,n)\times B\big)=

\displaystyle~N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(\big(K(G,m)\times B\big)\times_{B}\big(M(G,n)\times B\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(K(G,m)\times B),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(M(G,n)\times B)\big\}.

From Lemma
3.5
we have
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(K(G,m)\times B\big)=N\mathcal{A}\big(K(G,m)\big)=m
. Similarly

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(M(G,n)\times B\big)=N\mathcal{A}\big(M(G,n)\big)=n
, (see
[
4
, Corollary 3]
). Consequently

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(K(G,m)\times M(G,n)\times B\big)=n.

4.
Self-length

In the previous section we studied self-closeness number, which is the minimum value of
n
such that
\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)
. Here we calculate the number of strict inclusions in the chain of Equation
1.2
, which was studied by Choi and Lee in
[
5
]
.

4.1 Definition
.

The number of strict inclusions in the chain of Equation
1.2
is called the
self-length
of
X
and is denoted by
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
.

We define a set
I_{(X)}
of non-negative integers by,

I_{(X)}:=\big\{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}~~:~~\mathfrak{A}^{n+1}_{\#}(X)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)\big\}.

Clearly the cardinality of the set

\#I_{(X)}=L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X).

We now explore the relation between the self closeness number and the self length.

4.2 Proposition
.

For any
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, we have
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)
. In particular, self-length of
X
is finite if and only if
X
has finite self-closeness number.

Proof.

Consider the case
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)<\infty
. Let
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=m
. Then we must have

\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)=\cdots=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k-1}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{0}_{\#}(X)=[X,X]^{B}_{B},

for some non-negative integer
k
. Clearly
k\geq m
, otherwise
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)<m
. By the definition of self-closeness number, we have
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=k
. Thus we get the desired result.

∎

4.3 Corollary
.

(a)

For any finite dimensional
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, we have
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)<\infty
.

(b)

For any
X\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, if
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=n
and
X
is
m
-connected with
m<n
, then
1\leq L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq n-m
.

Proof.

(a) If
\operatorname{dim}(X)=n
, then
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq n
. Therefore
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq n
.

(b) If
X
is
m
-connected, then
\pi_{i}(X)=0
for all
i\leq m
. Thus observed that
\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{m-1}_{\#}(X)=\cdots=\mathfrak{A}^{0}_{\#}(X)=[X,X]^{B}_{B}
. Since
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=n
, therefore we have the chain

\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{n}_{\#}(X)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{n-1}_{\#}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}(X)=[X,X]^{B}_{B}.

Therefore there are at most
n-m
strict inclusions. Hence
1\leq L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)\leq n-m
.

∎

As for self closeness number, the self-length is also homotopy invariant.

4.4 Theorem
.

Assume that
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
are fibrewise pointed homotopy equivalent. Then
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)=L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that for any non-negative integer
k,~~\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
if and only if
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
which is equivalent to show that
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
if and only if
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
. As in the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we have monoid isomorphism
\phi_{*}\colon\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
with inverse
\psi_{*}\colon\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
. Consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{*}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)}

From the commutativity we say that the above horizontal map is identity if and only if the lower horizontal map is identity. Therefore
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
if and only if
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
.

∎

Under certain conditions, the following proposition gives an equivalent criterion for a strict inequality of the chain (see Equation
1.2
) for the case of fibred product.

4.5 Proposition
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
. For any non-negative integer
k
, we have

(a)

If
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
(or
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
) then
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
.

(b)

The converse holds if
Y
is a fibrewise group-like space that satisfies the conditions
[X\wedge_{B}Y,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
and
[X,Y]^{B}_{B}=\{\ast\}
.

Proof.

(a)
Let
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
. If possible, assume that
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
. Choose
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)\setminus\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)
. Then for any
g\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
we have

f\times_{B}g\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y).

Then
f_{b}\times g_{b}=(f\times_{B}g)_{b}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})
for some
b\in B
, from Proposition
2.2
. Thus
\pi_{i}(f_{b}\times g_{b})\colon\pi_{i}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})\to\pi_{i}(X_{b}\times Y_{b})
is an isomorphism for all
i\leq k+1
. So
\pi_{i}(f_{b})\colon\pi_{i}(X_{b})\to\pi_{i}(X_{b})
is an isomorphism for all
i\leq k+1
. Hence
f_{b}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X_{b})
, so that
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)
from Proposition
2.2
. This is a contradiction. We will get the similar result if we take
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
.

(b)
For the converse implication, it is given that
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
. If possible assume that
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
and
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
. Since
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
, let
h=(h_{X},h_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)\setminus\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
. From Corollary
2.7
and Proposition
2.8
(a) we have
h_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
and
h_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
. Thus

h_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X),~~h_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)=\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y).

Therefore
(p_{X},h_{Y})
and
(h_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
from Proposition
2.6
. Combining Theorem
2.11
and Corollary
2.13
we have
h=(h_{X},h_{Y})=(p_{X},h_{Y})\circ(h_{X},p_{Y})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X\times_{B}Y)
, which is a contradiction. Consequently at least one of
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(X)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X)
and
\mathfrak{A}^{k+1}_{\#}(Y)\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(Y)
holds.

∎

We use the above proposition to deduce a relation of self-length of fibred product in terms of the individual spaces.

4.6 Theorem
.

Let
X,Y\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
. Then

L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X)+L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(Y)-\#\big(I_{(X)}\cap I_{(Y)}\big)\leq L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X\times_{B}Y)

Moreover the equality holds, if
Y
is a fibrewise group-like space that satisfies the conditions

and

.

Proof.

is infinite. So we have to only consider the case

. Observe that

implies

from Proposition

∎

Higher fibred products

In previous sections, we studied several results of the monoid

, self-closeness number and self length for fibred product of two ex-spaces. In this section we generalised the results for
n

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}

\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, with fibrations

For the rest of the section, we will use

Clearly

is an object in

where

defined as
(p_{1}\times_{B}\ldots\times_{B}p_{n})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=p_{i}(x_{i})
and
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})(b)=(s_{1}(b),\ldots,s_{n}(b))

5.1 Definition
.

A map

(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})

The following is a generalised version of Proposition

5.2 Proposition
.

Let

. Then, for
1\leq i\leq n
, we have

(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})\text{ if and only if }f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{i}).

In particular,

(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}({\bf X})\text{ if and only if }f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X_{i}).

Proof.

Let

\bar{{\bf X}}=X_{1}\times_{B}\ldots\times_{B}X_{i-1}\times_{B}X_{i+1}\ldots\times_{B}X_{n}\times_{B}X_{i}.

Consider permutation map

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{i}).

is a homeomorphism.
Therefore
(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})

where

(5.3)

\textstyle{{\bf X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

is
k

for all

, we say that
\phi_{X_{i}}
is well-defined. Let us observe that for any self-map

, for

.

,

\displaystyle\iota_{X_{i}}\circ f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}=

\displaystyle~\big(p_{X_{1}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\big(p_{X_{1}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\big(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\big)\circ\iota_{X_{i}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\phi_{X_{i}}\big((p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\circ(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\phi_{X_{i}}\big((p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}}\circ(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}),\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle f_{X_{i}}\circ(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{i}}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ g_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\phi_{X_{i}}\big(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\big)\circ\phi_{X_{i}}\big(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\big).

Hence
\phi_{X_{i}}
is a monoid homomorphism. As in the first part of Theorem
2.11
, we can show that
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{X_{i}})=\mathfrak{A}^{k,X_{i}}_{\#,\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})
. Consider the map
\psi_{X_{i}}\colon\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{i})\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#,\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})
defined as

h\mapsto\big(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,h\circ p_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\big).

This map is well-defined by Proposition
5.2
. Clearly
\psi_{X_{i}}
is a monoid homomorphism such that
\phi_{X_{i}}\circ\psi_{X_{i}}=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{i})}.

∎

The following theorem is a generalization of Theorem
2.15
for
n
-fibred product.

5.8 Theorem
.

Let us assume that each map
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
is
k
-reducible and satisfying the conditions
f_{X_{i}}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ p_{X_{i}},~~\forall~~2\leq i\leq n
. Then there exits a split short exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{A}^{k,X_{1}}_{\#,\prod_{\hat{1}}}({\bf X})\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{n})\to 0,

where
\phi(f)=(f_{X_{1}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}})
for each
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
.

Proof.

Since each map
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
is
k
-reducible, therefore the map
\phi
is well-defined by Corollary
5.4
. For
1\leq j(\neq i)\leq n
, let us observe that
h\circ p_{X_{j}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}=p_{X_{j}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}
where
h\in\operatorname{map}^{B}_{B}(X_{j})
. Moreover as in the proof of Proposition
5.7
we have
\iota_{X_{i}}\circ f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}=\big(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\big)\circ\iota_{X_{i}}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Therefore

\displaystyle\phi\big((f_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{n}})\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\phi\big(f_{X_{1}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}}),\ldots,f_{X_{n}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(f_{X_{1}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{1}},f_{X_{2}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{2}},\ldots,

\displaystyle~f_{X_{n}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\big)

\displaystyle\simeq^{B}_{B}

\displaystyle\big(f_{X_{1}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},g_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}}\circ p_{X_{2}}\ldots,g_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\circ p_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{1}},

\displaystyle~f_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}}\circ p_{X_{2}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{2}},\ldots,f_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\circ p_{X_{n}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(f_{X_{1}}\circ(g_{X_{1}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}},g_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}}\circ p_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}}\ldots,g_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\circ p_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}}),

\displaystyle~f_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}}\circ g_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}},\ldots,f_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\circ g_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(f_{X_{1}}\circ(g_{X_{1}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}},p_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}}),f_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}}\circ g_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}},

\displaystyle~\ldots,f_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\circ g_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(f_{X_{1}}\circ(g_{X_{1}},p_{X_{2}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{1}},f_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}}\circ g_{X_{2}}\circ\iota_{X_{2}},\ldots,f_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\circ g_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(f_{X_{1}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}}\circ g_{X_{1}}\circ\iota_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\circ g_{X_{n}}\circ\iota_{X_{n}}\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\phi\big((f_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{n}})\big)\circ\phi\big((g_{X_{1}},\ldots,g_{X_{n}})\big)

This shows that
\phi
is a monoid homomorphism.

Any element of
\mathfrak{A}^{k,X_{i}}_{\#,\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})
is of the form
(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})
satisfying the condition
(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\circ\iota_{X_{i}}=\iota_{X_{i}}.

First pre-composing with
p_{X_{i}}
and then post-composing with
p_{X_{i}}
we have
f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ p_{X_{i}}=p_{X_{i}}
. By assumption,
f_{X_{i}}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ p_{X_{i}}=p_{X_{i}}
, for all
2\leq i\leq n
. Therefore
\mathfrak{A}^{k,X_{i}}_{\#,\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})=\{\ast\}
for all
2\leq i\leq n
. Hence
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
can be identified with
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{X_{1}})=\mathfrak{A}^{k,X_{1}}_{\#,\prod_{\hat{1}}}({\bf X})
. Further we consider a map
\psi\colon\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{n})\to\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
defined as

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\mapsto(f_{1}\circ p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{n}\circ p_{X_{n}}).

Observe that,

(f_{1}\circ p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{n}\circ p_{X_{n}})=(f_{1}\circ p_{X_{1}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\circ\cdots\circ(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{n}\circ p_{X_{n}})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X}),

since
(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{i}\circ p_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
for
1\leq i\leq n
, from Proposition
5.2
. Therefore
\psi
is well-defined. Clearly
\psi
is a monoid homomorphism such that
\phi\circ\psi=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{n})}
.

∎

We have the following generalisation of Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
.

5.9 Theorem
.

For
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, we have

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})\geq\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}),\ldots,N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\big\}.

Further, for any
k\geq 0
if each map in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
is
k
-reducible, then

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})=\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}),\ldots,N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\big\}.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.6
we have,

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})\geq\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}\times_{B}\cdots\times_{B}X_{n-1}),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\big\}.

Similarly,

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}\times_{B}\cdots\times_{B}X_{n-1})\geq\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}\times_{B}\cdots\times_{B}X_{n-2}),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n-1})\big\}.

Combing this two inequalities we get,

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})\geq\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}\times_{B}\cdots\times_{B}X_{n-2}),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n-1}),N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\big\}.

Continuing further, we obtain the desired inequality.

For the second part, consider the case
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{i})<\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
k=\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}),\ldots,N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\}
and
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
. By
k
-reducibility we have
f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}(X_{i})=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}(X_{i})
. Therefore
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}({\bf X})
, using Proposition
5.5
. Thus
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})=\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}({\bf X})
. So
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})\leq k.
Hence by the first part, we obtain

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})=\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}),\ldots,N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\big\}.

∎

5.10 Proposition
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
. For any non-negative integer
l
, we have

(a)

If
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(X_{i})\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(X_{i})
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
then
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})
.

(b)

The converse holds if given any
k\geq 0
, each map in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
is
k
-reducible and for any
2\leq i\leq n
satisfies the conditions
f_{X_{i}}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ p_{X_{i}}
, for all
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
.

Proof.

(a)
For some fixed
i
, let
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(X_{i})\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(X_{i})
. If possible assume that
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})=\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})
. Let
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(X_{i})\setminus\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(X_{i})
. Then for some
g\in\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(\prod_{\hat{i}})
we have
g\times_{B}f\in\mathfrak{A}^{l}(\prod_{\hat{i}}\times_{B}X_{i})
. As in the proof of‘Proposition
5.2
, we have
\phi
such that
\phi^{-1}\circ g\times_{B}f\circ\phi\in\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})
(see Diagram
5.3
). Thus
\phi^{-1}\circ g\times_{B}f\circ\phi\in\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})=\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})
. So
\pi_{i}(\phi^{-1}\circ g\times_{B}f\circ\phi)
is an isomorphism for all
i\leq l+1
. Therefore using the diagram
5.3
we have
\pi_{i}(g\times_{B}f)
is isomorphism for all
i\leq l+1
, i.e.
g\times_{B}f\in\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(\prod_{\hat{i}}\times_{B}X_{i})
. As in the proof of Proposition
4.5
(a), we have
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(X_{i})
. This is a contradiction.

(b)
For the converse implication it is given that
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})
. If possible assume that
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(X_{i})=\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(X_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})\setminus\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})
. Thus by
k
-reducibility we have
f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(X_{i})
, so that
f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(X_{i})=\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(X_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. From Proposition
5.2
we have
(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{i}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\in\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Further, as in the proof of Theorem
5.6
,

f=(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,p_{X_{n-1}},f_{X_{n}})\circ(p_{X_{1}},\ldots,f_{X_{n-1}},p_{X_{n}})\circ\cdots\circ(f_{X_{1}},p_{X_{2}},\ldots,p_{X_{n}})\in\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X}).

This contradicts the fact that
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})\setminus\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})
. Hence
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}(X_{i})\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}(X_{i})
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

∎

We now obtain a generalisation of Theorem
4.6
for
n
-fibred product.

5.11 Theorem
.

For
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{i})-\mathcal{C}\leq L\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X}),

\text{ where }\mathcal{C}=\sum_{j=2}^{n}(-1)^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq n}\#(I_{(X_{i_{1}})}\cap\cdots\cap I_{(X_{i_{j}})}).

Moreover the equality holds if given any
k\geq 0
, each map in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
is
k
-reducible and for any
2\leq i\leq n
satisfies the conditions
f_{X_{i}}\simeq^{B}_{B}f_{X_{i}}\circ\iota_{X_{i}}\circ p_{X_{i}}
, for all
f\in\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
.

Proof.

Note that if any one of
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{i})
is infinite then
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})
is infinite. So we only consider the case
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{i})<\infty
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. From Proposition
5.10
(a) we have,
l\in\cup_{i=1}^{n}I_{(X_{i})}
implies
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})
. Therefore the number of strict inclusions of the chain
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}({\bf X})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}({\bf X})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{0}_{\#}({\bf X})=[{\bf X},{\bf X}]_{B}^{B}
is at least cardinality of
\cup_{i=1}^{n}I_{(X_{i})}
. Observe that,

\displaystyle\#(\cup_{i=1}^{n}I_{(X_{i})})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq n}\#(I_{(X_{i_{1}})}\cap\cdots\cap I_{(X_{i_{j}})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\#(I_{(X_{i})})-\sum_{j=2}^{n}(-1)^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq n}\#(I_{(X_{i_{1}})}\cap\cdots\cap I_{(X_{i_{j}})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{i})-\mathcal{C}

Therefore we get the desired result.

For the second part of the proof, note that
l\in\cup_{i=1}^{n}I_{(X_{i})}
if and only if
\mathfrak{A}^{l+1}_{\#}({\bf X})\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}^{l}_{\#}({\bf X})
, by Proposition
5.10
(b). Therefore the number of strict inclusions of the chain
\operatorname{Aut}_{B}^{B}({\bf X})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{m}_{\#}({\bf X})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{0}_{\#}({\bf X})=[{\bf X},{\bf X}]_{B}^{B}
is exactly the cardinality of
\cup_{i=1}^{n}I_{(X_{i})}
.

∎

5.12 Corollary
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\operatorname{CW}_{B}^{B}
. For any
k\geq 0
, if each map in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
is
k
-reducible, then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}L\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{i})-\mathcal{C}\leq L\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})\leq\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}),\ldots,N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\big\}.

Proof.

The left hand side inequality immediately follows from above Theorem
5.11
. From Proposition
4.2
we have
L\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})\leq N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})
. Moreover for any
k\geq 0
, each map in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}({\bf X})
is
k
-reducible, therefore
N\mathfrak{A}_{B}({\bf X})=\max\big\{N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{1}),\ldots,N\mathfrak{A}_{B}(X_{n})\big\}
from Theorem
5.9
. Hence the desired result.

∎

5.13 Example
.

Consider the ex-spaces
\big(B\times K(G,m_{i}),p_{i},s_{i}\big)
over
B
with
p_{i}
first coordinate projection. Take
B=M(G,n)
with the assumptions
m_{i}\leq n
and
m_{i}\neq m_{j}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Observe that,

\big(B\times K(G,m_{1})\big)\times_{B}\cdots\times_{B}\big(B\times K(G,m_{n})\big)\cong B\times K(G,m_{1})\cdots\times K(G,m_{n}),

where the right hand side is an ex-space over
B
with first factor projection. For any given
k\geq 0
,
each map in
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\#}\big(K(G,m_{1})\times\cdots\times K(G,m_{n})\big)
is
k
-reducible, so using Corollary
2.5
we say that each map in
\mathfrak{A}^{k}_{\#}\big(B\times K(G,m_{1})\times\cdots\times K(G,m_{n})\big)
is
k
-reducible. From Lemma
3.5
we have,

N\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(M(G,n)\times K(G,m_{i})\big)=N\mathcal{A}\big(K(G,m_{i})\big)=m_{i},~~1\leq i\leq n.

Moreover from Equation
4.8
we have
I_{\big(M(G,n)\times K(G,m_{i})\big)}=\{m_{i}-1\}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Therefore
\mathcal{C}=0
and so from Corollary
5.12
we have

n\leq L\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(M(G,n)\times K(G,m_{1})\times\cdots\times K(G,m_{n})\big)\leq\max\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}.

Hence

L\mathfrak{A}_{B}\big(M(G,n)\times K(G,m_{1})\times\cdots\times K(G,m_{n})\big)=n.

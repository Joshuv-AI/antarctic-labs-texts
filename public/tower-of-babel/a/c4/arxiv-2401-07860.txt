Galton-Watson theta-processes in a varying environment
Serik Sagitov, Yerakhmet Zhumayev

Abstract
We consider a special class of Galton-Watson theta-processes in a varying environment fully defined by four parameters, with two of them ( θ , r ) (\theta,r) being fixed over time n n , and the other two ( a n , c n ) (a_{n},c_{n}) characterizing the altering reproduction laws. We establish a sequence of transparent limit theorems for the theta-processes with possibly defective reproduction laws. These results may serve as a stepping stone towards incisive general results for the Galton-Watson processes in a varying environment.



1
Introduction

The basic version of the Galton-Watson process (GW-process) was conceived as a stochastic model of the population growth or extinction of a single species of individuals
[
3
,
7
]
. The GW-process
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
unfolds in the discrete time setting, with
Z_{n}
standing for the population size at the generation
n
under the assumption that each individual is replaced by a random number of offspring. It is assumed that the offspring numbers are independent random variables having the same distribution
\{p(j)\}_{j\geq 0}
.

By allowing the offspring number distribution
\{p_{n}(j)\}_{j\geq 0}
to depend on the generation number
n
, we arrive at the GW-process in a varying environment
[
4
]
.
This more flexible model is fully described by a sequence of probability generating functions

f_{n}(s)=\sum_{j\geq 0}p_{n}(j)s^{j},\quad 0\leq s\leq 1,\quad n\geq 1.

Introduce the composition of generating functions

F_{n}(s)=f_{1}\circ\ldots\circ f_{n}(s),\quad 0\leq s\leq 1,\quad n\geq 1.

Given that the GW-process starts at time zero with a single individual,
we get

\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}})=F_{n}(s),\quad\mathrm{P}(Z_{n}=0)=F_{n}(0).

The state 0 of the GW-process is absorbing and the extinction probability for the modeled population is determined by

q=\lim F_{n}(0)

(here and throughout, all limits are taken as
n\to\infty
, unless otherwise specified).
In the case of
proper
reproduction laws with
f_{n}(1)=1
for all
n\geq 1
, we get

\mathrm{E}(Z_{n})=F^{\prime}_{n}(1)=f^{\prime}_{1}(1)\cdots f^{\prime}_{n}(1),\quad\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|Z_{n}>0)=\frac{F^{\prime}_{n}(1)}{1-F_{n}(0)}.

In
[
5
]
, the usual ternary classification of the GW-processes into supercritical, critical, and subcritical processes
[
1
]
, was adapted to the framework of the varying environment. Given
0<f^{\prime}_{n}(1)<\infty
for all
n
, it was shown that under a regularity condition (A) in
[
5
]
, it makes sense to
distinguish among four classes of the GW-processes in a varying environment: supercritical, asymptotically degenerate, critical, and subcritical processes. In a more recent paper
[
10
]
devoted to the Markov theta-branching processes in a varying environment, the quaternary classification of
[
5
]
was further refined into a quinary classification, which can be adapted to the discrete time setting as follows:

supercritical case
:
q<1
and
\lim\mathrm{E}(Z_{n})=\infty
,

asymptotically degenerate case
:
q<1
and
\liminf\mathrm{E}(Z_{n})<\infty
,

critical case
:
q=1
and
\lim\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|Z_{n}>0)=\infty
,

strictly subcritical case
:
q=1
and a finite
\lim\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|Z_{n}>0)
exists,

loosely subcritical case
:
q=1
and
\lim\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|Z_{n}>0)
does not exist.

Our paper is build upon the properties of a special parametric family of generating functions
[
9
]
leading to what will be called here the Galton-Watson theta-processes or GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-processes. The remarkable property of the GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-processes in a varying environment is that the generating functions
F_{n}(s)
have explicit expressions presented in Section
2
. An important feature of the GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-processes is that they allow for defective reproduction laws. If the generating function
f_{i}(s)
is
defective
, in that

f_{i}(1)<1
, then
F_{n}(1)<1
for all
n\geq i
. In the defective case
[
6
,
11
]
, a single individual, with probability

1-f_{i}(1)
may force the entire GW-process to visit to an ancillary absorbing state
\Delta
by the observation time
n
with probability

\mathrm{P}(Z_{n}=\Delta)=1-F_{n}(1).

In Sections
3
and
4
, we state ten limit theorems for the GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-processes in a varying environment. These results are illuminated in Section
5
by ten examples describing different growth and extinction patterns under environmental variation.
The proofs are collected in Section
6
.

(c)

\theta\in(-1,0)

r=1
,

n\geq 1
,

0<a_{n}<1
,

0<c_{n}\leq 1-a_{n}

(d)

\theta\in(-1,0)

,

n\geq 1
,

0<a_{n}<1
,

(1-a_{n})(r-1)^{-\theta}\leq c_{n}\leq(1-a_{n})r^{-\theta}

(e)

\theta=0

,

n\geq 1
,

(f)

\theta=0

,

A GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-process with parameters
(\theta,r,a_{n},c_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a GW-process in a varying environment characterized by a sequence of probability generating functions
(f_{n}(s))_{n\geq 1}
defined by

f_{n}(s)=r-(a_{n}(r-s)^{-\theta}+c_{n})^{-1/\theta},\quad 0\leq s<r,\quad f_{n}(r)=r,

(1)

for
\theta\neq 0
, and for
\theta=0
, defined by

f_{n}(s)=r-(r-c_{n})^{1-a_{n}}(r-s)^{a_{n}},\quad 0\leq s\leq r.

(2)

Definition

is motivated by the Definitions 14.1 and 14.2 in
[

]

\theta\in(-1,1]

, stay constant over time, while the parameters
(a_{n},c_{n})
may vary. The case
\theta=r=1
is the well studied case of the linear-fractional reproduction law.

,

,

.

Lemma 1
.

Consider a GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-process with parameters
(\theta,r,a_{n},c_{n})
.
If
\theta\neq 0
, then

F_{n}(s)=r-(A_{n}(r-s)^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta},\quad 0\leq s<r,\quad F_{n}(r)=r,\quad n\geq 1,

Here,

(a) for
\theta\in(0,1]
,

,

0<A_{n}<\infty,\quad C_{n}>0,\quad C_{n}\geq 1-A_{n},\quad F_{n}(1)=1,\quad F^{\prime}_{n}(1)=A_{n}^{-1/\theta},\quad n\geq 1,

(b) for
\theta\in(0,1]

(c) for
\theta\in(-1,0)
,

(d) for
\theta\in(-1,0)
,
r>1
,

0<A_{n}<1,\quad(1-A_{n})(r-1)^{-\theta}\leq C_{n}\leq(1-A_{n})r^{-\theta},\quad F_{n}(1)\leq 1,\quad n\geq 1,

with
F_{n}(1)=1
if and only if
c_{k}=(1-a_{k})(r-1)^{-\theta}
,
1\leq k\leq n

(e) for
\theta=0

(f) for

with
F_{n}(1)=1
if and only if

,

Limit theorems for the defective GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-process

In the defective case, there are two kinds of absorption times:

\tau_{0}
the absorption time of the GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-process at 0,

\tau_{\Delta}
the absorption time of the GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-process at the state
\Delta
.

Let
\tau=\min(\tau_{0},\tau_{\Delta})
be the absorption time of the GW
{}^{\,\theta}
-process either at 0 or at the state
\Delta
.
Recall that
q=\mathrm{P}(\tau_{0}<\infty)
and denote

q_{\Delta}=\mathrm{P}(\tau_{\Delta}<\infty),\quad Q=\mathrm{P}(\tau<\infty)=q+q_{\Delta}.

Clearly,

\mathrm{P}(\tau\leq n)=\mathrm{P}(\tau_{0}\leq n)+\mathrm{P}(\tau_{\Delta}\leq n)=F_{n}(0)+1-F_{n}(1),

implying

\mathrm{P}(\tau>n)=F_{n}(1)-F_{n}(0).

Furthermore,

\mathrm{E}(Z_{n};\tau_{\Delta}>n)=F^{\prime}_{n}(1),\quad\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}};\tau_{\Delta}>n)=F_{n}(s),\quad 0\leq s\leq 1,

so that

\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|\tau>n)=\frac{F^{\prime}_{n}(1)}{F_{n}(1)-F_{n}(0)},\quad\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}}|\tau>n)=\frac{F_{n}(s)-F_{n}(0)}{F_{n}(1)-F_{n}(0)},\quad 0\leq s\leq 1.

{}^{\,\theta}
-process in the four defective cases.

can be upgraded to the almost surely convergence
Z_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\to}}Z_{\infty}
.

Proof of Theorems
7
and
8

In this section we prove only Theorem
7
. Theorem
8
is proven similarly.

By Lemma
1
,

F_{n}(0)=r-(A_{n}r^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta},\quad F_{n}(1)=r-(A_{n}(r-1)^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}.

It follows,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(\tau>n)

\displaystyle=(A_{n}r^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}-(A_{n}(r-1)^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta},

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|\tau>n)

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{\prime}_{n}(1)}{F_{n}(1)-F_{n}(0)}=\frac{\theta^{-1}A_{n}(A_{n}+C_{n}(r-1)^{\theta})^{-1/\theta-1}}{(A_{n}r^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}-(A_{n}(r-1)^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}}|\tau>n)

\displaystyle=\frac{F_{n}(s)-F_{n}(0)}{F_{n}(1)-F_{n}(0)}=\frac{(A_{n}r^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}-(A_{n}(r-s)^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}}{(A_{n}r^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}-(A_{n}(r-1)^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}}.

(i) Assume that
A=0
. Then the sequence of positive numbers

V_{n}=A_{n}^{-1}(C-C_{n})=c_{n+1}+c_{n+2}a_{n+1}+c_{n+3}a_{n+2}a_{n+1}+\ldots

satisfies

r^{-\theta}\leq\liminf V_{n}\leq\limsup V_{n}\leq(r-1)^{-\theta}.

For a given
x\in(0,\infty)
, put

W_{n}(x)=A_{n}^{-1}(C^{-1/\theta}-(A_{n}x+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}).

Since

W_{n}(x)=A_{n}^{-1}(C^{-1/\theta}-(A_{n}(x-V_{n})+C)^{-1/\theta})=\theta^{-1}C^{-1/\theta-1}(x-V_{n}+o(1)),

the representation

A_{n}^{-1}\mathrm{P}(\tau>n)=W_{n}((r-1)^{-\theta})-W_{n}(r^{-\theta})

yields the first asymptotic result stated in the part (i) of Theorem
7
. The other two asymptotic results follow from the representations

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|\tau>n)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta^{-1}(A_{n}+C_{n}(r-1)^{\theta})^{-1/\theta-1}}{W_{n}((r-1)^{-\theta})-W_{n}(r^{-\theta})},

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}}|\tau>n)

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{n}((r-s)^{-\theta})-W_{n}(r^{-\theta})}{W_{n}((r-1)^{-\theta})-W_{n}(r^{-\theta})}.

(ii) The second claim follows from the equality

\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}};\tau_{\Delta}>n)=r-(A_{n}(r-s)^{-\theta}+C_{n})^{-1/\theta}.

Proof of Theorem
9

If
\theta=0
and
r>1
, then by Lemma
1

\mathrm{P}(Z_{n}=0)=r-r^{A_{n}}D_{n},\quad\mathrm{P}(Z_{n}\neq\Delta)=r-(r-1)^{A_{n}}D_{n}.

It follows,

q=r-r^{A}D,\quad q_{\Delta}=1-r+(r-1)^{A}D,\quad Q=1-(r^{A}-(r-1)^{A})D.

(i) If
A=0
, then clearly

q=r-D,\quad q_{\Delta}=1-r+D,\quad Q=1,

and

\mathrm{P}(\tau>n)=(r^{A_{n}}-(r-1)^{A_{n}})D_{n}\sim(\ln r-\ln(r-1))A_{n}D_{n}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(Z_{n}|\tau>n)

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{\prime}_{n}(1)}{F_{n}(1)-F_{n}(0)}=\frac{A_{n}(r-1)^{A_{n}-1}}{r^{A_{n}}-(r-1)^{A_{n}}}\to\frac{(r-1)^{-1}}{\ln r-\ln(r-1)},

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(s^{Z_{n}}|\tau>n)

\displaystyle=\frac{F_{n}(s)-F_{n}(0)}{F_{n}(1)-F_{n}(0)}=\frac{r^{A_{n}}-(r-s)^{A_{n}}}{r^{A_{n}}-(r-1)^{A_{n}}}\to\frac{\ln r-\ln(r-s)}{\ln r-\ln(r-1)}.

(ii) In the case
A>0
, the main claim is obtained as

\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}};\tau_{\Delta}>n)=r-(r-s)^{A_{n}}D_{n}\to r-(r-s)^{A}D.

Proof of Theorem
10

If
\theta\in(-1,0)
and
r=1
, then by Lemmas
1
,
2
,

F_{n}(0)=1-(A_{n}+C_{n})^{\alpha},\quad F_{n}(1)=1-C_{n}^{\alpha},

q=1-(A+C)^{\alpha},\quad q_{\Delta}=C^{\alpha},

where
\alpha=-1/\theta
and
0<C\leq 1-A
.

(i) Suppose
A=0
. Then the sequence of positive numbers

V_{n}=A_{n}^{-1}(C-C_{n})

satisfies

0\leq\liminf V_{n}\leq\limsup V_{n}\leq 1.

For a given
x\in(0,\infty)
, put

W_{n}(x)=A_{n}^{-1}((A_{n}x+C_{n})^{\alpha}-C^{\alpha}).

Since

W_{n}(x)=\alpha C^{\alpha-1}(x-V_{n}+o(1)),

the representation

A_{n}^{-1}\mathrm{P}(\tau>n)=W_{n}(1)-W_{n}(0)

yields the first asymptotic result stated in the part (i) of Theorem
10
. The other asymptotic result follows from the representation

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(s^{Z_{n}}|\tau>n)

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{n}(1)-W_{n}((1-s)^{1/\alpha})}{W_{n}(1)-W_{n}(0)}.

(ii)
The claim (ii) is derived as

\mathrm{P}(s^{Z_{n}};\tau>n)=1-(A_{n}(1-s)^{1/\alpha}+C_{n})^{\alpha}\to 1-(A(1-s)^{1/\alpha}+C)^{\alpha}.

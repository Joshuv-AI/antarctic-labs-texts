Weyl triplons in SrCu 2 ​ ( BO 3 ​ ) 2 \text{SrCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
Dhiman Bhowmick, Pinaki Sengupta

Abstract
We propose that Weyl triplons are expected to appear in the low energy magnetic excitations in the canonical Shastry-Sutherland compound, SrCu 2 ​ ( BO 3 ​ ) 2 \text{SrCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , a quasi-2D quantum magnet. Our results show that when a minimal, realistic inter-layer coupling is added to the well-established microscopic model describing the excitation spectrum of the individual layers, the Dirac points that appear in the zero-field triplon spectrum of the 2D model split into two pairs of Weyl points along the k z k_{z} direction. Varying the strength of the inter-layer DM-interaction and applying a small longitudinal magnetic field results in a range of band-topological transitions accompanied by changing numbers of Weyl points. We propose inelastic neutron scattering along with thermal Hall effect as the experimental techniques to detect the presence of Weyl node in the triplon spectrum of this material. We show that the logarithmic divergence in the second derivative in thermal Hall conductance near phase transition from regime Weyl points to a regime with topologically gapped bands as well as a finite slope in the thermal Hall conductance as a function of magnetic field at zero magnetic field are promising evidence for the presence of Weyl triplons.

IV
Conclusion

In conclusion we have demonstrated that
\text{SrCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a possible host of Weyl triplons. Our study shows that interlayer perpendicular DMI (even if very small in magnitude) naturally give rise to the Weyl triplons. Furthermore the nature of triplon bands at low temperature depends neither on the interlayer Heisenberg interation (because of orthogonal Dimer arrangement) nor on the interlayer in-plane DMIs, which makes the appearance of Weyl nodes robust against small deviations from the idealized model. Finally, We have shown that a finite slope of thermal Hall conductance as a function of magnetic field at
h_{z}\rightarrow 0
region as well as the divergence in double derivative of thermal Hall conductivity near the phase transition from Weyl point region to topologically gapped band region, which are possible experimental signatures to detect the presence of interlayer DMI as well as Weyl nodes.

Inelastic neutron scattering provides an alternative way to probe Weyl nodes in triplon bands. Recent neutron scattering results for

\text{SrCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
show that exchange anisotropy and triplon bound
states, neglected in this study, play
an important role in determining the nature of the triplon bands
McClarty et al. 2017
.
Hybridization with the bound states lift the 3-fold degeneracy of the spin-1 Dirac point and consequently suppresses the appearance of a spin-1 Weyl point.
However, band-topological transitions still exist as a function of magnetic field and the band crossings are
expected to translate into a Weyl point in the 3D model in presence of interlayer DMI.
For the future, further theoretical and experimental study is required to investigate the presence and nature of Weyl triplons in real material.

Appendix B
Divergence of double derivative of thermal Hall conductivity at phase transition point

Figure 6:
The plot of thermal Hall conductance as function of magnetic field within the rectangular box in figure Fig.
5
(a). The red dots denote the numerical data points and the black line represents fitting of that data using the expression in equation Eq.
26
. The fitting parameters are
A=-25.78,\,B=-1.2181,\,C=-0.0086

We consider the following simplistic two band model which describes the band-topological phase transition from the region of Weyl points to a region of gapped topological bands,

\displaystyle\pazocal{H}

\displaystyle=k_{x}\sigma_{x}+k_{y}\sigma_{y}+\left|k_{z}-c\right|\sigma_{z},\,\,\,k_{z}\geq 0

\displaystyle=k_{x}\sigma_{x}+k_{y}\sigma_{y}+\left|k_{z}+c\right|\sigma_{z},\,\,\,k_{z}<0.

(21)

If
c>0
, a pair of Weyl points exist at
k_{z}=\pm c
and if
c<0
, the system consists of topologically gapped bands.
Thus
c=0
is the band-topological phase transition point.
For simplicity, we use with the formula for thermal Hall conductance at high temperature (see Eq.
13
),

\displaystyle\kappa_{xy}

\displaystyle\propto\int\int d^{2}kdk_{z}\sum_{n}\Omega_{n}^{z}(\mathbb{k})E_{n}(\mathbb{k})

\displaystyle\propto\int\int d^{2}kdk_{z}\Omega^{z}_{1}(\mathbb{k})E_{1}(\mathbb{k}),\quad\left[\because\Omega_{1}(\mathbb{k})=-\Omega_{2}(\mathbb{k})\text{ and }E_{1}(\mathbb{k})=-E_{2}(\mathbb{k})\right]

(22)

where
k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}
. The energy and the Berry curvature expressions for the lower band are given by,

E_{1}(\mathbb{k})=-\sqrt{k^{2}+(k_{z}\mp c)^{2}}\,\,,\quad\Omega_{1}^{z}(\mathbb{k})=-\frac{2\left|k_{z}\mp c\right|}{\left((k_{z}\mp c)^{2}+k^{2}\right)^{3/2}},

(23)

where the negative and positive sign in front of
c
is for
k_{z}\geq 0
and
k_{z}<0
region respectively.
The thermal Hall conductivity is given by,

\displaystyle\kappa_{xy}

\displaystyle\propto\int_{k=0}^{k=k_{c}}\int_{k_{z}=-\pi}^{k_{z}=+\pi}d^{2}kdk_{z}\frac{\left|k_{z}\mp c\right|}{(k_{z}\mp c)^{2}+k^{2}}

\displaystyle\propto\int_{k=0}^{k=k_{c}}\int_{k_{z}=0}^{k_{z}=+\pi}d^{2}kdk_{z}\frac{\left|k_{z}-c\right|}{(k_{z}-c)^{2}+k^{2}}\quad\left[\because\text{The integrand is an even function of }k_{z}\right]

\displaystyle\propto\left[\left(k_{c}^{2}+(\pi-c)^{2}\right)\log(k_c^2+(\pi-c)^2)-\left(k_{c}^{2}+(\pi-c)^{2}\right)\right]-\left[(\pi-c)^{2}\log(\pi-c)^{2}-(\pi-c)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\left[\left(k_{c}^{2}+c^{2}\right)\log\left(k_{c}^{2}+c^{2}\right)-\left(k_{c}^{2}+c^{2}\right)\right]+\left[c^{2}\log c^{2}-c^{2}\right],

(24)

where we assume the Berry curvature is only important in the region
k\leq k_{c}
.
Near phase transition point
c\rightarrow 0
we have
k_{c}\gg c
and
k_{c}\ll\pi-c
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{xy}

\displaystyle\propto c^{2}\log\left|c\right|

\displaystyle\therefore\frac{d^{2}\kappa_{xy}}{dc^{2}}

\displaystyle\propto\log\left|c\right|

(25)

Thus double derivative of thermal Hall conductivity with respect to
c
is logarithmically divergent near phase transition.

5

\kappa_{xy}=A\left(h_{z}-\frac{h_{c}}{4}\right)^{2}\log\left|h_{z}-\frac{h_{c}}{4}\right|+B\left(h_{z}-\frac{h_{c}}{4}\right)+C,

(26)

and shown in figure Fig.
6
.

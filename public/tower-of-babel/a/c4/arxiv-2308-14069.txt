On the distribution of quadratic residues
I.D. Shkredov

Abstract




Annotation.

In our paper, we apply additive–combinatorial methods to study the distribution of the set of squares
\mathcal{R}
in the prime field. We obtain the best
upper bound on the number of gaps in
\mathcal{R}
at the moment and generalize this result for sets with small doubling.

1
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number.
Consider the simplest non–trivial multiplicative subgroup in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
, namely, the set of quadratic residues

\mathcal{R}=\{x^{2}~:~x\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}\}\,.

The set
\mathcal{R}
(and its complement
\mathcal{N}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{R}
, i.e. the set of quadratic non–residues) is a good constructive model for a randomly chosen subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
(each element of the set is taken with probability
1/2
).
On the other hand,
the set of quadratic residues is
strongly
related to
bounds on
L
–functions, see

[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]

and
[
15
]
.
The question
regarding
the distribution of the elements of
\mathcal{R}
attracts the attention of many researchers see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,

[
3
]
—
[
7
]
,

[
12
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
,

[
22
]
—
[
27
]

and so on.
For example, Vinogradov
[
26
]
considered the maximal distance
d(p)
between quadratic residues and conjectured that
d(p)\ll_{\varepsilon}p^{\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon>0
is any number.
The first non–trivial results in this direction were obtained in
[
19
]
and
[
25
]
.
The best result at the moment is due to Burgess (see
[
3
]
)

d(p)\ll p^{1/4}\log p\,.

(1)

Another question concerning the distribution of the set
\mathcal{R}
is the following.
Take any integer
1\leqslant h\leqslant d(p)
and define

S(h)=S^{\mathcal{R}}(h)=\max\{|S|~:~S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p},\,S\dotplus\{0,1,\dots,h-1\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\}\,.

(2)

What can be said about the quantity
S(h)
? In other words, how many segments of length
h
are contained in
\mathcal{R}
(equivalently, how many gaps of
h
consecutive elements exist in
\mathcal{N}
)?
The mentioned
problem was considered in
[
12
, Theorem 2’]
,
[
14
, Theorem 1]
and the best result in this direction can be found in paper
[
22
]
, where the author proves, in particular, that

S(h)\ll_{\varepsilon,r}\frac{p^{1/2+1/2r+\varepsilon}}{h^{2}}\,,

(3)

where
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary number,
r
is a positive integer and
h\geqslant p^{1/2r}
.
As for applications of the upper bounds for
S(h)
,
refer to, say,

[
1
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
.

The aim of this work is to show that using combinatorial methods one can improve bound (
3
).
Also, we interpret the appearance of the multiplicative energy in Burgess’s proof
quite transparently.
Our method is more flexible and slightly different from the classical approach and its variations, see
[
6
]
and

[
15
]
.
In
the
proof, we need the only consequence of the Weil bound on the multiplicative character, namely, that for any different non–zero shifts
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
one has

|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}|:=|\mathcal{R}\cap(\mathcal{R}-s_{1})\cap\dots\cap(\mathcal{R}-s_{k})|\leqslant\frac{p}{2^{k+1}}+k\sqrt{p}

(4)

and similar for
\mathcal{N}
.
Let us formulate our main result.
For
a\neq b
denote by
\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
the set

\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}~:~\frac{x+b}{x+a}\in\mathcal{R}\right\}=\frac{a-b}{\mathcal{R}-1}-b\,,

and

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{a,b}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}~:~\frac{x+b}{x+a}\in\mathcal{N}\right\}=\frac{a-b}{\mathcal{N}-1}-b\,.

Theorem 1

Let
p
be a prime number,
C>0
be a real number, and
h
be any positive integer such that

h\geqslant 2\log p-4\log(C\log p)+4\,.

(5)

Then

S^{\mathcal{R}}(h),\,S^{\mathcal{N}}(h),\,S^{\mathcal{Q}_{a,a+1}}(h),\,S^{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{a,a+1}}(2h)\ll\frac{C_{*}\sqrt{p}\cdot\log^{2}p\cdot\log^{2}\left(\frac{4h}{\min\{h,2\log p\}}\right)\cdot\delta_{p}(h)}{h^{2}}\,,

(6)

where
C_{*}=C
if
h\leqslant 2\log p
and
C_{*}=1
otherwise, and

\delta_{p}(h)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1\,,\quad\quad\quad\,\,h\leqslant 2\log p\,,\\
\frac{h}{\log p}\,,\quad\quad\,2\log p<h\leqslant\log^{2}p\,,\\
\log p\,,\quad\quad h>\log^{2}p\,.\end{array}\right.

It is widely believed that the Burgess bound (
1
) can be improved.
On the contrary,
it can be shown
that condition (
5
) and estimate (
6
) are close to optimal, at least for small
h
, see Remark
8
below.
Also, it is easy to see that bound (
6
), in a sense, allows us to break the square–root barrier for
h\geqslant\log^{3+\varepsilon}p
,
\varepsilon>0
, see Corollary
23
.
We combine the obtained results concerning the square–root barrier in the next theorem.

Theorem 2

Let
p
be a prime number and
h\geqslant 2\log p
be an integer.
Then in the notation of Theorem
1
one has

|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{1,\dots,h}|\ll\frac{\sqrt{p}\cdot\log^{2}p\cdot\log^{2}\left(\frac{4h}{\log p}\right)\cdot\delta_{p}(h)}{h}\,.

(7)

Now let
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set and
H\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be an arithmetic progression.
Suppose that
|X|\geqslant\log p
and
H+X\subseteq\mathcal{R}
.
Then

|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{(H+X)\cup(H+X)^{-1}}|\ll\frac{\sqrt{p}\log p}{|H|^{c}}\,,

(8)

where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

In our previous paper
[
23
, Section 5]
we obtained similar bounds to (
7
), (
8
) starting from an arbitrary family of shifts
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}
in the set
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}
.
Here our
shift sets
are more
specific
(which is both an advantage and a disadvantage).

The paper is organized as follows.
We obtain Theorem
1
and the first part of Theorem
2
in Section
3
. Also, we consider the question about maximal possible shifts of so–called generalized arithmetic progressions, see the required definitions in Section
2
.
In Section
4
we study additive–combinatorial generalizations
of

S(h)
, which are interesting in its own right and obtain the second part of Theorem
2
.

We thank Jozsef Solymosi
for useful
discussions.

2
Definitions

Given two sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
, define
the
sumset

of
A
and
B
as

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

In a similar way we define the
difference sets
, the
product set
and the
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
2A-A
is
A+A-A
.
If the sum of
A
and
B
is direct, that is
|A+B|=|A||B|
, then we write
A\dotplus B
.
For an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}

the Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequality (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
) holds stating

|nA-mA|\leqslant\left(\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\right)^{n+m}\cdot|A|\,,

(9)

where
n,m
are any positive integers.
Further if
|A+B|\leqslant K|A|
for some sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
, then for any
n
one has

|nB|\leqslant K^{n}|A|\,.

(10)

If
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
\lambda\neq 0
, then we write
\lambda\cdot A:=\{\lambda a~:~a\in A\}
.
We use representation function notations like
r_{A+B}(x)
or
r_{A-B}(x)
and so on, which counts the number of ways
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
can be expressed as a sum
a+b
or
a-b
with
a\in A
,
b\in B
, respectively.
For example,
|A|=r_{A-A}(0)
.
For any two sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
the
additive energy
of
A
and
B
is defined by

\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A\times A\times B\times B~:~a_{1}-b_{1}=a_{2}-b_{2}\}|=\sum_{\lambda}r^{2}_{A-B}(\lambda)\,.

If
A=B
, then we simply write
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A)
for
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,A)
.
In a similar way, define the
multiplicative energy

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A,B)
of sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Clearly,
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,B)=\mathsf{E}^{+}(B,A)
and by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,B)|A\pm B|\geqslant|A|^{2}|B|^{2}\,.

(11)

Given a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
and elements
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we write

A^{+}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}=A^{+}_{\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}}:=A\cap(A-s_{1})\cap\dots\cap(A-s_{k})\,,

and similar for
A^{\times}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}
, where
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\neq 0
, namely,

A^{\times}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}=A^{\times}_{\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}}:=A\cap(As^{-1}_{1})\cap\dots\cap(As^{-1}_{k})\,.

For any set
A
the following holds

A^{+}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}+\{0,s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}\subseteq A\,,\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad A^{\times}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}\cdot\{1,s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}\subseteq A\,.

(12)

If
P_{1},\dots,P_{d}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
are arithmetic progressions, then
Q:=P_{1}+\dots+P_{d}
is a
generalized arithmetic progression
(GAP) of rank
\mathrm{rk}(Q)=d
. A generalized arithmetic progression,
Q,
is called to be
proper
if
|Q|=\prod_{j=1}^{d}|P_{j}|
. For properties of generalized arithmetic progressions consult, e.g.,
[
24
]
.

For any function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\rho\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
define
the Fourier transform of
f
at
\rho
by the formula

\widehat{f}(\rho)=\sum_{g\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}f(g)e(-g\rho)\,,

(13)

where
e(x):=e^{2\pi ix/p}
. We use the same capital letter to denote set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
and its characteristic function
A:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\{0,1\}
.

Let us generalize the notions of
S(h)
and
d(p)
. In this paper we have deal with the set of quadratic residues
but our arguments equally work for the set
\mathcal{N}
.
More concretely,
given a set
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
and a family of sets
\mathcal{F}
write

d_{\mathcal{F}}(p)=\max\{|A|~:~A\subseteq\mathcal{R},\,A\in\mathcal{F}\}\,,

and

S(A)=\max\{|S|~:~S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p},\,S\dotplus A\subseteq\mathcal{R}\}\,.

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
When the constants in the signs depend on a parameter
M
, we write
\ll_{M}
and
\gg_{M}
.
Let us denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
All logarithms are to base
2
.
For a prime number
p
we write
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
.

3
The proof of the main result

We need an auxiliary result in the spirit of paper
[
22
]
.
Our proof, basically,
repeats the argument of
[
2
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 3

Let
h_{*}\leqslant h
be positive integers,
8h_{*}h<p
and
Q
be the set of the primes in
[h_{*}/2,h_{*}]
. Also, let
A:=[h]\dotplus S
.
Then

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(Q,A)\ll\frac{|Q|^{2}|A|^{2}}{p}+|Q||A|\cdot\min\left\{\frac{h_{*}}{\log h_{*}},\frac{\log p}{\log h_{*}}\right\}\,.

(14)

P r o o f.

Writing
\overline{H}=[-h,h]
and
\overline{A}=S+\overline{H}
, we see that

\sigma:=\mathsf{E}^{\times}(Q,A)\ll h^{-2}\sum_{\lambda}r^{2}_{Q^{-1}(\overline{H}+\overline{A})}(\lambda)

=h^{-2}\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{q,q^{\prime}\in Q}|\{(x,x^{\prime})\in\overline{H}\times\overline{H}~:~q^{\prime}x-qx^{\prime}\equiv\lambda\}|\cdot|\{(a,a^{\prime})\in\overline{A}\times\overline{A}~:~qa^{\prime}-q^{\prime}a\equiv\lambda\}|\,.

Below we can consider the case
q\neq q^{\prime}
only because the contribution of
q=q^{\prime}
corresponds to the second term in (
14
).
Fix
\lambda
and suppose that equation
q^{\prime}x-qx^{\prime}\equiv\lambda
has one more solution
(x_{*},x^{\prime}_{*})
. Then
q^{\prime}(x-x_{*})\equiv q(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{*})\pmod{p}
and since
4h_{*}h<p
, we see that the later equation is, actually, an equation in
\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
x\equiv x_{*}\pmod{q}
and
x^{\prime}\equiv x^{\prime}_{*}\pmod{q^{\prime}}
.
Therefore we have at most
O(h/h_{*})
solutions.
Thus

h_{*}h\sigma\ll\sum_{|\lambda|\leqslant 2h_{*}h}r_{Q\overline{A}-Q\overline{A}}(\lambda)\,.

Using the Erdős–Turán inequality for the sequence of fractional parts
\{\frac{qa^{\prime}-q^{\prime}a}{p}\}
, where
(a,a^{\prime},q,q^{\prime})\in\overline{A}\times\overline{A}\times Q\times Q
(see
[
2
, Lemma 1]
) and writing
T=[\lceil p/(8hh_{*})\rceil]
, we obtain by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\sigma\ll\frac{|Q|^{2}|\overline{A}|^{2}}{p}+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{t\in T}\left|\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{a\in\overline{A}}e(tqa)\right|^{2}\ll\frac{|Q|^{2}|A|^{2}}{p}+\frac{|Q|}{p}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{q\in Q}|\widehat{\overline{A}}(tq)|^{2}

=\frac{|Q|^{2}|A|^{2}}{p}+\frac{|Q|}{p}\sum_{\lambda}|\widehat{\overline{A}}(\lambda)|^{2}r_{QT}(\lambda)\,.

Let us estimate the function
r_{QT}(\lambda)
.
If
\lambda\equiv qt\equiv q^{\prime}t^{\prime}\pmod{p}
, then
\lambda=qt=q^{\prime}t^{\prime}

thanks to
p/(2h_{*}h)\cdot h_{*}<p
.
It follows that the quantity
r_{QT}(\lambda)
does not exceed
the number of possible primes
q\in[h_{*}/2,h_{*}]
which divide
\lambda
.
In other words,

r_{QT}(\lambda)=O(\min\{h_{*}/\log h_{*},\log_{h_{*}}p\})
.
Applying the Parseval identity, we derive

\sigma\ll\frac{|Q|^{2}|A|^{2}}{p}+|Q||A|\cdot\min\left\{\frac{h_{*}}{\log h_{*}},\frac{\log p}{\log h_{*}}\right\}\,.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1
.

P r o o f.
First of all, let us recall how to obtain the bound
d(p)\ll p^{1/4}\log p
. To do this we repeat the argument of
[
23
, Appendix]
and our main bound (
6
) will be obtained similarly.

Let
l
be an integer parameter which we will choose later.
Write
P_{s}=s+\{0,1,\dots,l-1\}=s+P_{0}
for an arbitrary arithmetic progression with step one.
For any positive integer
m\leqslant l-1
we use the notation
P^{1/m}_{0}=\{0,1,\dots,[(l-1)/m]\}
and, similarly,
P^{1/m}_{s}=P^{1/m}_{0}+s
.
Also, let
L=\log p
.
At the beginning choose

l=d(p)
and take
P_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.
We have

\frac{P^{1/2}_{0}}{P^{1/2}_{s}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}-1

(15)

due to

\frac{P^{1/2}_{0}}{P^{1/2}_{s}}+1\subseteq\frac{P^{1/2}_{0}+P^{1/2}_{s}}{P^{1/2}_{s}}\subseteq\frac{P^{1}_{s}}{P^{1/2}_{s}}\subseteq\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{N}}=\mathcal{R}\,.

Let
k
be an integer parameter which we will choose in a moment.
Since for any
j\in[k]
one has
j\cdot P^{1/2k}_{0}\subseteq P^{1/2}_{0}
, it follows from (
15
) that

\frac{P^{1/2k}_{0}}{P^{1/2}_{s}}\subseteq(\mathcal{R}-1)\cap(2^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{R}-2^{-1})\cap\dots\cap(k^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{R}-k^{-1})\,.

(16)

One can show (see
[
23
, Lemma 26]
)
that
\left|\frac{P^{1/2k}_{0}}{P^{1/2}_{s}}\right|\gg|P^{1/2k}_{0}||P^{1/2}_{s}|

and using formula (
4
) (for a mixed combination of residues/non–residues), we get

d^{2}(p)k^{-1}\ll|P^{1/2k}_{0}||P^{1/2}_{a}|\leqslant\frac{p}{2^{k}}+k\sqrt{p}\ll k\sqrt{p}\,,

(17)

provided
k\gg L
.
Choosing
k\sim L
,
we
obtain

d(p)\ll p^{1/4}\log p
as required.

Now we are ready to obtain (
6
) and we consider the quantity
S^{\mathcal{R}}(h)
only because for
S^{\mathcal{N}}(h)
the argument is the same.
Choose a set
S
such that
|S|=S(h)
and
A:=S\dotplus\{0,1,\dots,h-1\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
.
Take
k=2^{-1}\min\{h/2,\log p\}-1
.
From (
5
) it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\log p-\log(C\log p)\leqslant k\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\log p-1
and
l:=h\geqslant 4k+1
.
As above we see that our main inclusion (
16
) takes place for any
s\in S
and hence arguing as in (
17
), we have

\left|\frac{P^{1/2k}_{0}}{P^{1/2}_{0}\dotplus S}\right|\ll C_{*}L\sqrt{p}\,.

(18)

Thus it is enough to obtain a good lower bound for the ratio set from (
18
) and to do this we calculate the multiplicative energy of a subset of this set.
Take the set of primes
Q
belonging the second part of
P^{1/2k}_{0}
and
apply
Lemma
14
to the sets
Q
and
P^{1/2}_{0}\dotplus S
with the parameters
h_{*}=h/2k
and
h=h/2
(it is easy to check that the first term in (
14
) is negligible and that the condition of the lemma takes place, since
h\leqslant d(p)\ll p^{1/4}L
).
It remains to combine this result with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (
11
) and estimate (
18
).
It gives us

\frac{|S|h^{2}}{k\log^{2}\left(\frac{4h}{\min\{h/2,\log p\}}\right)\cdot\delta_{p}(h)}\ll\frac{|Q|^{2}|S|^{2}h^{2}}{h|S||Q|}\cdot\left(\min\left\{\frac{h_{*}}{\log h_{*}},\frac{\log p}{\log h_{*}}\right\}\right)^{-1}

\ll\frac{|Q|^{2}|S|^{2}h^{2}}{\mathsf{E}^{\times}(Q,A)}\ll C_{*}L\sqrt{p}\,,

(19)

where

\delta_{p}(h)=\min\left\{\frac{h}{\min\{h/2,\log p\}},\log p\right\}

as required.

It remains to obtain the same bound for the quantities
S^{\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}}(h)
and
S^{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{a,b}}(h)
.
Let us start with the first one.
Shifting (that is writing
x\to x(b-a)-a=x-a
) we can assume that
a=0
and
b=1
and we see that if
x,x+1,\dots,x+h-1\in\mathcal{Q}_{0,1}
, then
\frac{x+1}{x},\frac{x+2}{x+1},\dots,\frac{x+h}{x+h-1}\in\mathcal{R}
.
Multiplying we obtain that all nonzero numbers among
x,x+1,\dots,x+h
either belong to
\mathcal{R}
or
\mathcal{N}
. In other words, we have found a gap in
\mathcal{R}
or
\mathcal{N}
and hence we can apply the previous arguments.
As for
S^{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{a,a+1}}(h)
we see that in this case a half of
x,x+1,\dots,x+h
belongs to
\mathcal{N}
or
\mathcal{R}
. This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Since

xy\in\mathcal{R}
iff
x/y\in\mathcal{R}
for any nonzero
y
, we obtain

Corollary 4

For any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
one has

\max_{h,s}|\{h~:~(x+a)(x+a+1)\in\mathcal{R}\,,x\in[h]+s\}|\ll p^{1/4}\log p\,.

The essence of Corollary
4
is uniformity in
a
and
s
. Without this
uniformity
much more general results are known, see
[
4
]
.

Now let us formulate a simple consequence of the argument above to sums of multiplicative characters with some quadratic polynomials.
By
d_{*}(p)
denote the maximum of the maximal distance in
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{N}
.
Also, let
\chi
be the Legendre symbol.

Corollary 5

For any
a,s\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and a positive integer
h
, we have

\left|\sum_{x=s+1}^{s+h}\chi((x+a)(x+a+1))\right|\leqslant h(1-2^{-1}d^{-1}_{*}(p))+4\,.

(20)

P r o o f.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
s=a=0
.
First of all, let us obtain the upper bound for the sum
\sum_{x\in[h]}\chi(x(x+1))
.
We have

[h]=\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n_{R}}R_{j}\right)\bigsqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n_{N}}N_{j}\right)\,,

(21)

where
R_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
and
N_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
are intervals.
Clearly,
|n_{R}-n_{N}|\leqslant 1
and

\sum_{x\in[h]}\chi(x(x+1))=h-2(n_{R}+n_{N})+1\,.

We have
|R_{j}|,|N_{j}|\leqslant d_{*}(p)
and thus from (
21
), we obtain
n_{R}+n_{N}\geqslant h/d_{*}(p)
as required. As for the lower bound, we need a splitting other than (
21
).
Write

[h]=\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}_{R}}R_{j}\right)\bigsqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}_{N}}N_{j}\right)\bigsqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{s}I_{j}\right)\,,

(22)

where
R_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
and
N_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
are intervals of length at least two, and intervals
I_{j}
of quadratic residues/non–residues form the complement of
R_{j},N_{j}
in
[h]
.
Again
|n^{\prime}_{R}+n^{\prime}_{N}-s|\leqslant 1
and now we have in view of
|R_{j}|,|N_{j}|\geqslant 2
that

\sum_{x\in[h]}\chi(x(x+1))=\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}_{R}}|R_{j}|+\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}_{N}}|N_{j}|-\sum_{j=1}^{s}|I_{j}|-n^{\prime}_{R}-n^{\prime}_{N}-s

=-h+2\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}_{R}}|R_{j}|+2\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}_{N}}|N_{j}|-n^{\prime}_{R}-n^{\prime}_{N}-s\geqslant-h+3n^{\prime}_{R}+3n^{\prime}_{N}-s\geqslant-h+2s-3\,.

It is easy to see that

(alternatively, we can use the quantity
2d(p)+1
here) and thus

Combining the last two inequalities, we obtain the required result.

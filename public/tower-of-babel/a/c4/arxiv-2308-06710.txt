Therefore the techniques of the proof in the present article are not a straightforward adaptation from Euclidean space. There are some intrinsic features of the hyperbolic space which appear in the proofs.

Like the Euclidean case, the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality is invariant under the action of the conformal group of the ball model
\mathbb{B}^{N}.
In the Euclidean space, the translations, dilations and inversions constitute the conformal group of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and Sobolev inequality is invariant under their actions. In the case of the hyperbolic space, the conformal group coincides with the isometry group and its elements are given by the
hyperbolic translations

\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u\circ\tau_{b}),\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u\circ\tau_{b})\rangle_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\langle(\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u)\circ\tau_{b},(\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u)\circ\tau_{b}\rangle_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

(iii)

For every open subset

of

\displaystyle\int_{U}|u\circ\tau_{b}|^{p}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\int_{\tau_{b}(U)}|u|^{p}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}},\ \mbox{for all}\ 1\leq p<\infty.

(iv)

For every
\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\phi(x)(\psi\circ\tau_{b})(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(\phi\circ\tau_{-b})(x)\psi(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

The above formulas hold by density as long as the integrals involved are finite.

Conformal change of metric.
Now we briefly discuss some properties of conformal change of metric (for more details, refer to
[
10
, Section 1.1]
). Consider the following problem

(2.3)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u-\lambda u=a(x)\left|u\right|^{2^{*}-2}u.

Let us denote the energy function corresponding to the problem (
2.3
) by

I_{h}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left(|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u|^{2}-\lambda u^{2}\right)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\Big]-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}a(x)|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x).

Observe that
u
solves the problem (
2.3
) iff
v:=\Big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u
solves the Euclidean equation

(2.4)

-\Delta v-\tilde{\lambda}\Big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\Big)^{2}v=a(x)\left|v\right|^{2^{*}-2}v,\quad v\in H^{1}_{0}(B(0,1)),

where
\tilde{\lambda}=\big(\lambda-\frac{N(N-2)}{4}\big)
, and
B(0,1)
is the open unit ball in the Euclidean space with the centre at the origin. Let us denote the energy function corresponding to (
2.4
) by

I_{e}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\int_{B(0,1)}\left(|\nabla v|^{2}-\tilde{\lambda}\Big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\Big)^{2}v^{2}\right)\mathrm{~d}x\Big]-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{B(0,1)}a(x)|v|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}x.

Therefore
I_{h}(u)=I_{e}(v)
. Then for any
u,w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, we have
\langle I^{\prime}_{h}(u),w\rangle{{}_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}=\langle I^{\prime}_{e}(\bar{u}),\bar{w}\rangle
, where
\bar{u}=\big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u
and
\bar{w}=\big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}w
.

a

Corresponding to (

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}a(x)\left\{\left(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a,f}(x)+t\mathcal{U}(\tau_{-y}(x))\right)^{2^{*}}-\left(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a,f}(x)\right)^{2^{*}}\right.

Using the continuity, we easily get

Thus, we must show

0, then

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}a(x)\left\{\left(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a,f}(x)+t\mathcal{U}(\tau_{-y}(x))\right)^{2^{*}}-\left(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a,f}(x)\right)^{2^{*}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-2^{*}t\left(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a,f}(x)\right)^{2^{*}-1}\mathcal{U}(\tau_{-y}(x))-t^{2^{*}}\mathcal{U}(\tau_{-y}(x))^{2^{*}}\right\}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{d(x,0)\leq 1}a(x)A(r)t^{2}\mathcal{U}^{2}(\tau_{-y}(x))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m^{2}\underline{\mathrm{a}}A(r)}{2^{*}}\underbrace{\int_{d(x,0)\leq 1}\mathcal{U}^{2}(\tau_{-y}(x))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)}_{E_{1}}.

\displaystyle:=\int_{d(x,0)\leq 1}\mathcal{U}^{2}(\tau_{-y}(x))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\geq C\int_{d(x,0)\leq 1}e^{-2(c(N,\lambda))d(x,y)}\;\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq C\;e^{-2(c(N,\lambda))(1+\varepsilon_{R})d(y,0)}\underbrace{\int_{d(x,0)\leq 1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)}_{:=C}

\displaystyle=\overline{C}\;e^{-2(c(N,\lambda))(1+\varepsilon_{R})d(y,0)}.

Estimate of

For

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(1-a(x))\mathcal{U}(\tau_{-y}(x))^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

.

For this, as we know from the above points that
z^{j}_{n}\circ T^{j}_{n}\rightharpoonup v^{j}\not\equiv 0
and
z^{j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
, then for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)
, we have

(7.1)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\neq\left\langle v^{j},\phi\right\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle z^{j}_{n}\circ T^{j}_{n},\phi\right\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle z^{j}_{n},\phi\circ T^{-j}_{n}\right\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle z^{j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n},\phi\circ\left(T^{-j}_{n}T^{i}_{n}\right)\right\rangle_{\lambda}.

Now if
T^{-j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n}(0)\nrightarrow\infty
, then there exists
R>0
such that
Supp\left(\phi\circ\left(T^{-j}_{n}T^{i}_{n}\right)\right)\subseteq B_{R}(0)
. Then using Vitali’s convergence Theorem and the fact that
z^{j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in the last step of the above calculations, we get a contradiction.

Induction assumption:
Let the following hold true for
j=i+(k-1)\;\forall\;(k-1)>0
:

(1)

z^{j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
.

(2)

T^{-j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n}(0)\rightarrow\infty
.

Final step:
In this step, we prove that
T^{-j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n}(0)\rightarrow\infty
for
j=i+k\;\forall\;k>0
.

Firstly, observe that
(j-1)-i=k-1
, then consider

\displaystyle z^{j}_{n}

\displaystyle=z^{j-1}_{n}-v^{j-1}(T_{n}^{-(j-1)}),

\displaystyle z^{j}_{n}(T_{n}^{i})

\displaystyle=z^{j-1}_{n}(T_{n}^{i})-v^{j-1}(T_{n}^{-(j-1)}T_{n}^{i}).

Then using the induction assumptions, it follows that
z^{j}_{n}\circ T^{i}_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
for
j=i+k.
Now the same steps as in (
7.1
), we get the desired result.

Proof of (2):
The complete proof can be found in
[
30
]
. For the sake of completeness, we sketch the proof here.

Let
W
and
v
be any Schwartz class functions in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(7.2)

D_{\epsilon_{n}^{k},y^{k}_{n}}W:=(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x-y^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\rightharpoonup 0\equiv\langle D_{\epsilon_{n}^{k},y^{k}_{n}}W,v\rangle=\langle(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x-y^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right),v\rangle\to 0.

Consider

\displaystyle\langle(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x-y^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right),v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\left((\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x-y^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\right)\nabla v\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x-y^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x.

For any
\delta>0
, we split the above integral into two components as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,\delta)}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{B(0,\delta)^{c}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x,

First, at infinity, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\delta)^{c}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,\delta)^{c})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta v(x)|\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle=\left\|(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x-y^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(y^{k}_{n},\delta)^{c})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta v(x)|\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle=o(1).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\delta)}|(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})|\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Delta v(x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{B(0,\delta)}|(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)|\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle=\left\|\Delta v(x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left(\int_{B(0,\delta)}|(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}|B(0,\delta)|^{\frac{2^{*}-1}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Delta v(x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,\delta)}|W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\delta^{\frac{N+2}{2N}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Delta v(x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left\|W\right\|_{2^{*}}\delta^{\frac{N+2}{2N}}.

Finally, for any
\delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,\delta)}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x+\int_{B(0,\delta)^{c}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left\||\Delta v(x)|\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left\|W\right\|_{2^{*}}\delta^{\frac{N+2}{2N}}.

Thus
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right)\Delta v(x+y^{k}_{n})\mathrm{~d}x=o(1)
.

Hence
\langle(\varepsilon_{n}^{k})^{\frac{2-N}{2}}W\left(\frac{x-y^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{k}}\right),v\rangle\to 0
. The result follows from the standard density arguments as the Schwartz class functions are dense in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

The following statements are equivalent to
n\to\infty
.

(7.3)

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\langle D_{\epsilon_{n}^{k},y^{k}_{n}}W\,,\,D_{\epsilon_{n}^{l},y^{l}_{n}}V\rangle=\langle D_{\epsilon_{n}^{k}\epsilon_{n}^{-l}\,,\,\,\epsilon_{n}^{-l}(y^{k}_{n}-y^{l}_{n})}W\,,\,V\rangle\to 0

\displaystyle(b)\,\,\Big|\log\big(\frac{\varepsilon^{k}_{n}}{\varepsilon^{l}_{n}}\big)\Big|+\Big|\frac{y_{n}^{k}-y^{l}_{n}}{\varepsilon^{l}_{n}}\Big|\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty\text{ for }k\neq l.

Hence to prove
(2)
, it is enough to prove
(a)
. To show that, we use the properties of Dislocation space as in
[
30
]
.

Indeed, assuming in Theorem 8 of
[
30
]
,
g_{n}^{(j)}=D_{y_{n}^{j},\lambda_{n}^{j}}\in D
and
D
is defined as the space of Unitary operators as follows

D:=\left\{D_{y,\lambda}:D_{y,\lambda}u(x):=\lambda^{\frac{2-N}{2}}u\left(\frac{x-y}{\lambda}\right)\;\;\forall u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N});\;y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\;\lambda>0\right\}.

To prove that
(D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),D)
is a dislocation space, it is enough to show using proposition 3.1 of
[
29
]
that

D_{y_{n},\lambda_{n}}\in D,D_{y_{n},\lambda_{n}}\not\rightharpoonup 0\implies D_{y_{n},\lambda_{n}}\text{has a strongly convergent subsequence}.

D_{y_{n},\lambda_{n}}\rightharpoonup 0
, i.e., there exist
v\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
D_{y_{n},\lambda_{n}}v\not\rightharpoonup 0
. Then using lemma 3 of
[
30
]
, we get
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda
and
y_{n}\rightarrow y
for some
\lambda>0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.
Then using equation (54) of
[
30
]
, we conclude
D_{y_{n},\lambda_{n}}\rightarrow D_{y,\lambda}
.
Thus using Theorem 8 of
[
30
]
, we get
D_{y_{n}^{j},\lambda_{n}^{j}}^{-1}D_{y_{n}^{k},\lambda_{n}^{k}}\rightharpoonup 0\text{ for }k\neq j.
Hence
(a)
follows.

Artin groups of type ( 2 , 3 , n ) (2,3,n)
Derek F. Holt, Sarah Rees

Abstract
Our main theorem is that the word problem in the Artin group G = ⟨ a , b , c ∣ a b a = b a b , a c = c a , ( b , c ) n = ( c , b ) n ⟩ , G=\langle a,b,c\mid aba=bab,ac=ca,{}_{n}(b,c)={}_{n}(c,b)\rangle, for n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 can be solved using a system ℛ \mathcal{R} of length preserving rewrite rules that, together with free reduction, can be used to reduce any word over { a , b , c } \{a,b,c\} to a geodesic word in G G , in quadratic time. This result builds on work of Holt and Rees, and of Blasco, Cumplido and Morris-Wright, which proves the same result for all Artin groups that are either sufficiently large or 3-free. Since every rank 3 Artin group is either spherical or in one of the categories covered by the previous results on which we build, it follows that any rank 3 Artin group has quadratic Dehn function. However we note that this and much more is a consequence of very recent work of Haettel and Huang; our contribution is to provide a particular kind or rewriting solution to the word problem for the non-spherical rank 3 Artin groups (and more).

then no such transformation is possible, and we return

So we can assume that

If

The case when

So suppose that

is unsigned. Then

is critical by

3.7
. We describe a linear-time process that will transform

to a word of the form
b^{i}a^{j}b^{k}
and return

if possible, and
return

otherwise. We shall not discuss how this process could be
implemented in linear time, except to note that such an implementation might
involve storing the word

and the transformed word as doubly linked lists.

We read the word
v
from both ends simultaneously, i.e. leftwards from
the beginning of
v
and rightwards from its end, and we shall denote the
central part of
v
that has not yet been read by
v^{\prime}
. We shall maintain
a variable
flip
that is
true
or
false
, and initially
false
. When

is true the letters

and
a^{-1}
of

are regarded as being
replaced
b
and
b^{-1}
respectively, and vice versa. We shall call the
letters of the word
v^{\prime}
after making this replacement if necessary the

interpreted
letters of
v^{\prime}
(which are the same as the letters of
v^{\prime}

itself when
flip
is
false
).

At the beginning of the process we are reading the first and last letters
of
v
which, since
v
is critical, must be
a
and
a^{-1}
, not
necessarily respectively. Suppose, for example, that
v
has the prefix

ab
and the suffix
a^{-1}
; all other cases are similar. Then we replace
the prefix
ab
by
b^{-1}
and the suffix
a^{-1}
by
ab
. We then set
the flag

to
true
. Note that, if we were to replace the letters of
the unread part
v^{\prime}
of

by their interpreted letters at this stage,
then we would have executed the
\tau
-move on
v
, but we do not do that
explicitly.

As we continue to read
v^{\prime}
from both ends, if an interpreted letter has name

, then we replace the original letter (if necessary) by the interpreted

have

. If this never happens (i.e. if

becomes empty without this

. Otherwise, if the two interpreted letters
are equal, then we check whether the remainder of the interpreted word

v^{\prime}
is just a power of

, in which case, after replacing

by its
interpreted word if necessary, the resulting transformed word has the
required from, so we return

. If it is not a power of

then we return

false
.

On the other hand, if the interpreted letters at the beginning and end of

are

and

then, by Lemma

, if the required
transformation is possible, then the interpreted word

must be critical,
which we can test for by looking at the second letters from the beginning
and end of

. If it is not critical then we return

. Otherwise we
restart the process at this point, by making the necessary changes to the

, and then changing the value of

.
∎

Definition 4.6

(Optimal RRS)
.

We say that an RRS
U=u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+1}
for

, with associated
decomposition
w=\mu w_{1}\cdots w_{m+1}\gamma
, is

if

(i)

the first letter of
w_{1}
is at least as far to the right as in
any other RRS for
w
, i.e.
\mu
is as long as we found possible,

(ii)

{\rm f}[\gamma]
does not appear in
w_{m+1}
,

(iii)

if, for some
i>1
with
1<i\leq m
,
u_{i-1}
is
a P2G word of type
\{x,y\}
and
\alpha_{i}=\epsilon
, then
u_{i}
is

either

a P2G word of type
\{z,t\}

with
|\{x,y\}\cap\{z,t\}\}|=1

or

a
critical word of type
\{a,b,c\}
with
|\{x,y\}\cap\{b,c\}\}|=1
.

Note that
our condition (iii) is slightly different from the third condition for optimality of
[
1
]
; that is because we have no generator in

that commutes with two other generators.
We prefer to state our condition (iii) in this form for greater readability.
Note also that it is possible to have
two successive
\tau
-moves of type
\{a,b,c\}
in an optimal RRS.

We shall use the following properties of an optimal RRS.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let U be an optimal RRS of length
m
and
1\leq i\leq m
.

(i)

The word
u_{i}
has no proper critical suffix of the same type as

u_{i}
that is contained within
w_{i}
.

(ii)

Let

be the first letter in
u_{i}
after
{\rm f}[u_{i}]
that is
not part of
\alpha_{i}
. Then

does not have the same name as
{\rm f}[u_{i}]
.

Proof.

(i) If

had such a suffix

with

,
then we could define a new RRS

for

with
decomposition
\mu^{\prime}w_{i2}w_{i+1}\cdots w_{m+1}\gamma
where

\mu^{\prime}=\mu w_{1}\cdots w_{i-1}w_{i1}
, contradicting the optimality of

.

(ii) If

and either

has type
\{a,b,c\}
or
\beta_{i-1}\neq\epsilon
, then

is the second letter of

and is part of

, and we see directly from the definition of

that

. Otherwise

.
We cannot have

would not

then

would have a critical suffix of the same type as

starting at

contradicting (i). So

does not have the same name as
{\rm f}[u_{i}]
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

If a word

in a
(2,3,n)
-group admits an RRS then it admits an
optimal RRS.

Proof.

This proof is essentially the same as the existence part of the proof of

[

for

-free groups.
We can clearly restrict attention to those RRS in which the first letter of

is as far right as possible.

Then, given such an RRS associated to a decomposition

of

for some

And given an RRS associated with a decomposition for which

So we can find an RRS satisfying both conditions (i) and (ii).

We prove by induction on

For suppose we have an RRS satisfying conditions (i) and (ii), but not (iii),

Then, as we observed above, the hypothesis of this condition could apply only

would be critical of the same type as

, and we could
combine

and

in the decomposition to contain an RRS

of length

for

.
∎

Definition 4.9

(The set

Proof of the main theorem

We recall Definition

of

as the set of words admitting no RRS.
Our main theorem will be an immediate consequence of the following result,
which specifies the rewrite system

.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

be the Artin group with presentation

for

be as defined above.
Then the words in

are precisely the words that are geodesics in

There is a process that, given an input word

runs in quadratic time to find geodesic representatives

in

\tau

for

.

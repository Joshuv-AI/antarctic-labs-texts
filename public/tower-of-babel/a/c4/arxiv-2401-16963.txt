Sub-Optimal Fast Fourier Series Approximation for Initial Trajectory Design
Caleb Gunsaulus, William Brown, Carl De Vries, Youngro Lee, Madhusudan Vijayakumar, Ossama Abdelkhalik

Abstract
The Finite Fourier Series (FFS) Shape-Based (SB) trajectory approximation method has been used to rapidly generate initial trajectories that satisfy the dynamics, trajectory boundary conditions, and limitation on maximum thrust acceleration. The FFS SB approach solves a nonlinear programming problem (NLP) in searching for feasible trajectories. This paper extends the development of the FFS SB approach to generate sub-optimal solutions. Specifically, the objective of the NLP is modified to also include measures for the cost and/or the time of flight. Numerical results presented in this paper show several solutions that differ from those of the original FFS SB ones. The obtained solutions are used as initial guesses in a direct optimal control solver, and the results are presented.

1
Background

Current trajectory optimization methods minimize objectives such as time of flight or fuel consumption. Two common methods to solve these problems include direct and indirect methods
[
Betts1998
]
. Direct methods are very useful, but can be computationally expensive, and also require an initial guess to start the solution process. Sub-optimal methods, which approximate trajectories much more quickly, can be used to initialize a direct solver. However, many sub-optimal methods are useful only in limited scenarios, are subject to geometric requirements, or are not able to satisfy spacecraft thrust constraints. One sub-optimal method is the Finite Fourier Series Shape-based method
[
FFSSB2012
,
FFSSB3Body
,
FFSSB3Dim
]
. The FFS SB method defines a spacecraft trajectory by solving an NLP problem for a set of Fourier coefficients which are used to approximate the spacecraft states as a function of time. To find these trajectories, the NLP problem minimizes the least squares error of the equations of motion evaluated at multiple points along the trajectory. The FFS SB method has been developed for two-body and three-body systems as well as two dimensional and three dimensional spaces. The method has been applied to interplanetary transfers, spacecraft rendezvous, orbit raising, and phasing maneuvers. While the FFS SB method doesn’t produce the optimal solution, it produces cost values (fuel mass) that are near the optimal solutions obtained using GPOPS in some test cases
[
FFSSB3Dim
]
.

Currently, the FFS SB method minimizes the equations of motion error, but does not attempt to penalize traditional objectives such as time of flight and
\Delta V
. In this paper, the FFS SB method is further developed in order to handle the addition of time of flight or
\Delta V
to the objective function. The goal is to solve this multi-objective problem given that the time of flight and
\Delta V
objectives may be contradictory and compete with the objective to satisfy equations of motion, in this formulation.

2
Finite Fourier Series Shape-Based Method

In theory, any given periodic function can be represented as an infinite sum of sine and cosine terms. The FFS-SB method is taps into this mathematical property to approximate the shape of a trajectory. This trajectory is then used to compute the thrust magnitude and hence the trajectory solution of the design problem. The The FFS-SB method approximates the states of the spacecraft, namely the radius
r
and polar angle
\theta
, as follows:

r(t)=\frac{a_{0}}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{n_{r}}[a_{n}cos(\frac{n\pi t}{T})+b_{n}sin(\frac{n\pi t}{T})]

(1)

\theta(t)=\frac{c_{0}}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\theta}}[c_{n}cos(\frac{n\pi t}{T})+d_{n}sin(\frac{n\pi t}{T})]

(2)

where,
n_{r}
and
n_{\theta}
are the number of Fourier coefficients used to represent the radius
r
and polar angle
\theta
of the spacecraft states respectively. The biggest advantage of the FFS method is that for every different selection of the Fourier coefficients, a different trajectory shape is obtained. Additionally, the states of the trajectory are discretized at
n
points and are only approximated using the FFS at these discrete points. If the initial and final Boundary Conditions (BCs) of the spacecraft states are available, they can be used to solve for some of the Fourier coefficients in terms of the other.

Although there is no specific upper limit on the number of included Fourier terms, the general rule is to have both
n_{r}
and
n_{\theta}
be greater than 2. The number of Fourier terms to be used is usually problem dependant and is obtained on a trial and error basis. Once the number of FFS terms and the number of discretization points are determined, the sine and cosine terms becomes fixed and can be calculated once and used repeatedly to save on computational cost. The Fourier approximated function can thus be reduced to the following form:

r(t)=[A]*X+B

(3)

where A is a square matrix of order
(2*n_{r}+1)\times(2*n_{r}+1)
consisting of the sine and cosine terms, X is a column matrix having (
2*n_{r}+1
) rows representing the unknown Fourier coefficients that have to be solved and B is the column matrix having (
2*n_{r}+1
) rows that represents the coefficients that were solved for using the BCs.

3
Proposed Method of Solution

The approach implemented to solve the multi-objective problem is to use a weighted summation of the objectives to construct a single objective function. The additions of the time of flight objective and the
\Delta V
objective are considered separately, and a slightly different weighting approach is taken for each case. The equations describing
the objective functions for the time of flight and
\Delta V
objective are described as shown in Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
5
) respectively.

J=(1-\omega)F^{T}F+\omega\left(\frac{ToF}{ToF_{0}}\right)

(4)

J=(1-\omega)F^{T}F+\omega\Delta V

(5)

Where, J is the objective function, F is the error in the Fourier series approximation of the equations of motion evaluated at multiple points on the trajectory, ToF is the time of flight, and
\omega
is the weight applied to the ToF or mass cost.

6
Expected Results

The results for the orbit raising scenario above highlight the usefulness of weighting the objective function terms, specifically penalizing the ToF. In addition, an approach to penalize
\Delta V
using a weighting factor has also been developed, and results show it is effective for rendezvous problems. Initial FFS SB results for orbital rendezvous maneuvers which penalize
\Delta V
have been tested in numerical simulations of the full nonlinear equations of motion using the FFS SB thrust acceleration as an open loop input. When the FFS SB equation of motion error is low enough, the open loop simulations produce trajectories which are comparable to the FFS SB trajectories. Finally, previous work has demonstrated that 3-D FFS SB methods can generate trajectories with fuel mass cost near those obtained from the optimal control software GPOPS, in some cases
[
FFSSB3Dim
]
. Work is currently being done to compare trajectories generated using the the weighted objective function FFS SB approach to the optimal solutions.

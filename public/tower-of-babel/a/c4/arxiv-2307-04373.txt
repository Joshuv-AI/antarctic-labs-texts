A determinant approach for generalized q q -Bernoulli polynomials and asymptotic results
S.Z.H. Eweis a and Z.S.I. Mansour b

Abstract
In earlier work, we introduced three families of polynomials where the generating function of each set includes one of the three Jackson q q -analogs of the Bessel function. This paper gives determinant representation for each family, their large n n asymptotics, and two expansion theorems for specific classes of entire functions. We include two examples.



keywords

q
-Bernoulli polynomials and numbers, determinants, entire function, asymptotic expansions.

A determinant approach for the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials

This section introduces a determinant representation for the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials defined in (
1.7
)-(
1.9
). We begin by recalling some essential definitions and results used in the sequel.

Appell sequences are defined for the first time in (1880) by Appell
[
4
]
, as
any polynomial sequence
(p_{n}(\cdot))_{n}
satisfying the identity

\frac{d}{dz}p_{n}(z)=np_{n-1}(z),\,\,p_{0}(z)\neq 0.

AL-Salam,
[
2
]
, defined a
q
-analog of Appell sequence to be
any set of polynomials
(p_{n}(\cdot))_{n}
satisfying

D_{q}p_{n}(z)=[n]_{q}p_{n-1}(z)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),

(2.1)

where
[n]_{q}:=\dfrac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}
is the
q
-natural number. Keleshteri and Mahmoudov, see
[
18
]
, proved that the generating function of the polynomials
(p_{n}(\cdot))_{n}
defined in (
2.1
) is given by

A_{q}(z;t)=A_{q}(t)e_{q}(zt)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(z)\frac{t^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},

(2.2)

where

A_{q}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,q}\frac{t^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},\,\,A_{q}(t)\neq 0,

(2.3)

is an analytic function at
t=0
and
A_{n,q}=P_{n}(0)
.

In
[
24
]
, Njionou called the sequence of polynomials that satisfy

D_{q}P_{n}(z)=[n]_{q}P_{n-1}(qz),

or equivalently

D_{q^{-1}}P_{n}(z)=[n]_{q}P_{n-1}(z)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

(2.4)

the
q
-Appell polynomials of type II. He also proved that the polynomials defined by (
2.4
) have the generating function

A_{q}(z;t)=A_{q}(t)E_{q}(zt)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(z)\frac{t^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},

(2.5)

where

A_{q}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,q}\frac{t^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},\,\,A_{q}(t)\neq 0,

(2.6)

is an analytic function at
t=0
and
A_{n,q}=P_{n}(0)
.

The following theorem is proved in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\{P_{n}(z)\}
are the sequence of

-Appell polynomials with

, defined in the relations (
2.2
) and (

\dfrac{1}{A_{q}(t)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k,q}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},

then

P_{0}(z)=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{0,q}}
and for

\begin{split}P_{n}(z)&=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(\mu_{0,q})^{n+1}}\\
&\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&z&z^{2}&\ldots&\ldots&z^{n-1}&z^{n}\\
\mu_{0,q}&\mu_{1,q}&\mu_{2,q}&\ldots&\ldots&\mu_{n-1,q}&\mu_{n,q}\\
0&\mu_{0,q}&\left[\begin{array}[]{c}2\\
1\end{array}\right]_{q}\mu_{1,q}&\ldots&\ldots&\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-1\\

0&0&\mu_{0,q}&\ldots&\ldots&\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-1\\
2\end{array}\right]_{q}\mu_{n-3,q}&\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
2\end{array}\right]_{q}\mu_{n-2,q}\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&0&\mu_{0,q}&\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
n-1\end{array}\right]_{q}\mu_{1,q}\\
\end{array}\right|.\end{split}

(2.7)

Under the conventions that
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
\end{array}\right]_{q}=1,\,\,\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\\
\end{array}\right]_{q}=0,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,k>n\geq 0
,
the polynomials

defined in (

\begin{split}P_{n}(z)&=\dfrac{(-1)^{n}}{(\mu_{0,q})^{n+1}}\,\text{det}\,A_{n}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}),\end{split}

where

is the
(n+1)\times(n+1)

i-1\\
\end{array}\right]_{q}\mu_{j-i+1,q},&\hbox{$1\leq i\leq n$.}\end{array}\right.

Theorem 2.2
.

The generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials

\begin{split}B^{(1)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)=(-1)^{n}\,\text{det}\,A_{n}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),\end{split}

where

is the
(n+1)\times(n+1)

\\
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
i-1\\
\end{array}\right]_{q}\dfrac{1}{2^{j-i+1}}\dfrac{(q^{2\alpha+1};q^{2})_{j-i+1}}{(q^{2\alpha+1};q)_{j-i+1}},&\hbox{$1\leq i\leq n$.}\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

Proof.

Since the polynomials
(B^{(1)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q))_{n}
satisfy the
q
-difference equation (

), then, from (
2.2
), the generating function of

where

is the matrix defined in (

3
Asymptotic behavior of the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials for sufficiently large
n

In this section, we use the Darboux Method to determine the asymptotic behavior of the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials for sufficiently large
n
. The Darboux method is described as follows, see
[
22
]
.
Let
r
be the distance from the origin of the nearest singularity of
f(t)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\,t^{n}
and suppose that we can find a comparison function g(t) having the following properties

(i)
g(t)
is analytic in
0<|t|<r
,

(ii)
f(t)-g(t)
is continuous in
0<|t|\leq r
,

(iii) the coefficients
(b_{n})_{n}
in the Laurent expansion

g(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}b_{n}\,t^{n},\quad 0<|t|<r,

have known asymptotic behavior. Then

a_{n}=b_{n}+o(r^{-n})\quad(n\rightarrow\infty).

Theorem 3.1
.

The large
n
asymptotic of the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials
(B^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q))_{n}
are

\begin{split}B^{(2)}_{2n,\alpha}(z;q)&=\dfrac{2}{1-q}\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}[2n]_{q}!\left(Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})+Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{2n+1}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&+o(r^{-2n}),\end{split}

\begin{split}B^{(2)}_{2n+1,\alpha}(z;q)&=\dfrac{2}{1-q}\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}[2n+1]_{q}!\left(Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})-Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{2n+2}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&+o(r^{-2n}),\end{split}

as
n\,\rightarrow\,\infty
, where the functions
Sin_{q}(\cdot)
and
Cos_{q}(\cdot)
are defined in (
1.6
),
0<r\leq 2\zeta_{\alpha,1},
and
\zeta_{\alpha,1}:=\dfrac{j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{2(1-q)}
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by the Darboux method. Let
f(z,t)
denote the left hand side of (
1.8
). Then

f(z,t):=\frac{E_{q}(zt)E_{q}(\frac{-t}{2})}{g^{(2)}_{\alpha}(it;q)},\quad|t|<\frac{j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q}.

Set

\begin{split}g(z,t)&:=Res(f,\frac{ij^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})\frac{1}{(t-\frac{ij^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})}+Res(f,\frac{-ij^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})\frac{1}{(t+\frac{ij^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})},\end{split}

f(z,t)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\,t^{n},\quad\text{and }\quad g(z,t)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\,t^{n}.

(3.1)

Then

\begin{split}g(z,t)=Res(f,2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}+Res(f,-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t+2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}.\end{split}

Clearly
f(z,t)-g(z,t)
is continuous within and on the the circle
|t|=2\zeta_{\alpha,1}
. Now, we calculate the coefficient of
t^{n}

in the expansion of
g(z,t)
. Indeed,

\begin{split}g(z,t)&=Res(f,2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}+Res(f,-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t+2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}\\
&=Res(f,2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})\frac{-1}{2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}})^{n}+Res(f,-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{-t}{2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}})^{n}.\end{split}

(3.2)

Since

\begin{split}&Res(f,2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})=\frac{E_{q}(2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(-i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iz(1-q);q^{2})|_{z=2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}},\\
&Res(f,-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1})=\frac{E_{q}(-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iz(1-q);q^{2})|_{z=-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}},\end{split}

then Equation (
3.2
) can be written as

\begin{split}g(z,t)=&-\frac{E_{q}(2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(-i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iz(1-q);q^{2})|_{z=2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{-n-1}}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\\
&-\frac{E_{q}(-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iz(1-q);q^{2})|_{z=-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^{-n-1}}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}.\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iz(1-q);q^{2})|_{z=2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}&=i(1-q)\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iz(1-q);q^{2})|_{z=2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}}\\
&=-i(1-q)\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}},\end{split}

then

\begin{split}g(z,t)=&-\frac{E_{q}(2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(-i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{(1-q)\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{-n}}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\\
&\,-\frac{E_{q}(-2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{(1-q)\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^{-n}}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}.\end{split}

(3.3)

Substituting with
\pm i=e^{\pm\frac{i\pi}{2}}
into (
3.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}g(z,t)=-\frac{2}{1-q}\,Re\left(\frac{E_{q}(2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(-i\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{-in\pi}{2}}}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\right).\end{split}

(3.4)

But

\begin{split}Re\,\left(e^{\frac{-in\pi}{2}}E_{q}(2i\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)E_{q}(-i\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)&=\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}\left(Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})+Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)\\
&+\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\left(Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})-Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right).\end{split}

(3.5)

Therefore, substituting from (
3.5
) into (
3.4
), we get for
|t|=2\zeta_{\alpha,1}

\begin{split}g(z,t)=&-\frac{2}{1-q}\frac{Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})+Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\\
&-\frac{2}{1-q}\frac{Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})-Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}.\end{split}

Consequently,

\begin{split}b_{n}=&-\frac{2}{1-q}\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})+Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&-\frac{2}{1-q}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})-Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}.\end{split}

By applying the Darboux method, we obtain

a_{n}=b_{n}+o(r^{-n})\quad(n\rightarrow\infty).

Thus

\begin{split}\frac{B^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)}{[n]_{q}!}=&-\frac{2}{1-q}\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})+Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&-\frac{2}{1-q}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(Sin_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Cos_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})-Cos_{q}(2\zeta_{\alpha,1}z)Sin_{q}(\zeta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\zeta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}}+o(r^{-n}).\end{split}

Hence

∎

\begin{split}B^{(3)}_{2n,\alpha}(z;q)&=\dfrac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}[2n]_{q}!\left(C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})+S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{2n+1}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&+o(r^{-2n}),\end{split}

\begin{split}\quad B^{(3)}_{2n+1,\alpha}(z;q)&=\dfrac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}[2n+1]_{q}!\left(S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})-C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{2n+2}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&+o(r^{-2n}),\end{split}

as
n\,\rightarrow\,\infty,
where
S_{q}(\cdot)
and
C_{q}(\cdot)
are the functions defined in (
1.6
),
0<r\leq 2\eta_{\alpha,1},
and
\eta_{\alpha,1}:=\dfrac{q^{\frac{1}{4}}j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q}
.

Proof.

Since the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials
B^{(3)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)
are defined by the generating function

f(z,t):=\frac{\exp_{q}(zt)\exp_{q}(\frac{-t}{2})}{g^{(3)}_{\alpha}(it;q)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B^{(3)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)\frac{t^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},

where
f(z,t)
is analytic in the disk
|t|<\frac{2q^{\frac{1}{4}}j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q}
. We take

\begin{split}g(z,t)&:=Res(f,\frac{2iq^{\frac{1}{4}}j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})\frac{1}{(t-\frac{2iq^{\frac{1}{4}}j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})}+Res(f,\frac{-2iq^{\frac{1}{4}}j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})\frac{1}{(t+\frac{2iq^{\frac{1}{4}}j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q})}.\end{split}

Set

f(z,t)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\,t^{n},\quad\text{and}\quad g(z,t)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\,t^{n}.

(3.6)

Then

\begin{split}g(z,t)=Res(f,2i\eta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t-2i\eta_{\alpha,1})}+Res(f,-2i\eta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t+2i\eta_{\alpha,1})}.\end{split}

The function
f(z,t)-g(z,t)
is continuous within and on the the circle
|t|=2\eta_{\alpha,1}
. Now, we calculate the coefficient of
t^{n}
in the expansion of
g(z,t)
. Indeed,

\begin{split}g(z,t)&=Res(f,2i\eta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t-2i\eta_{\alpha,1})}+Res(f,-2i\eta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{(t+2i\eta_{\alpha,1})}\\
&=Res(f,2i\eta_{\alpha,1})\frac{-1}{2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{2i\eta_{\alpha,1}})^{n}+Res(f,-2i\eta_{\alpha,1})\frac{1}{2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{-t}{2i\eta_{\alpha,1}})^{n}.\end{split}

(3.7)

Since

\begin{split}&Res(f,2i\eta_{\alpha,1})=\frac{\exp_{q}(2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(-i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(\frac{iz}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}};q^{2})|_{z=2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}},\\
&Res(f,-2i\eta_{\alpha,1})=\frac{\exp_{q}(-2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(\frac{iz}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}};q^{2})|_{z=-2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}},\end{split}

then Equation (
3.7
) can be written as

\begin{split}g(z,t)=&-\frac{\exp_{q}(2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(-i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(\frac{iz}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}};q^{2})|_{z=2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{-n-1}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\\
&-\frac{\exp_{q}(-2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(\frac{iz}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}};q^{2})|_{z=-2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^{-n-1}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}.\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(\frac{iz}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}};q^{2})|_{z=2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}&=\frac{i}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(\frac{iz}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}};q^{2})|_{z=2i\eta_{\alpha,1}}\\
&=-\frac{i}{2}(1-q)q^{\frac{-1}{4}}\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{\alpha,1}},\end{split}

then

\begin{split}g(z,t)=&-\frac{2q^{\frac{-1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{\exp_{q}(2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(-i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{\alpha,1}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{-n}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\\
&-\frac{2q^{\frac{-1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{\exp_{q}(-2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{\alpha,1}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^{-n}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}.\end{split}

(3.8)

Substituting with
\pm i=e^{\pm\frac{i\pi}{2}}
into (
3.8
), we get

\begin{split}g(z,t)=&-\frac{2q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{\exp_{q}(2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(-i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{-in\pi}{2}}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\\
&-\frac{2q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{\exp_{q}(-2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{in\pi}{2}}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}.\end{split}

This implies

\begin{split}g(z,t)=\frac{-4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\,Re\left(\frac{\exp_{q}(2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(-i\eta_{\alpha,1})}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{-in\pi}{2}}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\right).\end{split}

(3.9)

But

\begin{split}Re\,\left(e^{\frac{-in\pi}{2}}\exp_{q}(2i\eta_{\alpha,1}z)\exp_{q}(-i\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)&=\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}\left(C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})+S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)\\
&+\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\left(S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})-C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right).\end{split}

(3.10)

Therefore, substituting from (
3.10

3.9
), we get for
|t|=2\eta_{\alpha,1}

\begin{split}g(z,t)=&-\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{\left(C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})+S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}\\
&-\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{\left(S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})-C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}}t^{n}.\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}b_{n}=&-\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})+S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&-\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})-C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}.\end{split}

By applying the Darboux method, we get

a_{n}=b_{n}+o(r^{-n})\quad(n\rightarrow\infty).

Thus

\begin{split}\frac{B^{(3)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)}{[n]_{q}!}=&-\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})+S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&-\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\frac{\left(S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})-C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{n+1}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}+o(r^{-n}).\end{split}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}B^{(3)}_{2n,\alpha}(z;q)&=\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}[2n]_{q}!\left(C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})+S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{2n+1}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\
&+o(r^{-2n}),\\
B^{(3)}_{2n+1,\alpha}(z;q)&=\frac{4q^{\frac{1}{4}}}{1-q}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}[2n+1]_{q}!\left(S_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)C_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})-C_{q}(2\eta_{\alpha,1}z)S_{q}(\eta_{\alpha,1})\right)}{(2\eta_{\alpha,1})^{2n+2}\,\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{J}^{(3)}_{\alpha}(z;q^{2})|_{z=j^{(3)}_{1,\alpha}}}\\

which are the desired result.

∎

4
Expansion of the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials

This section introduces an expansion for a class of entire function
\emph{f}
in terms of the generalized
q
-Bernoulli polynomials defined in (
1.7
)-(
1.9
). We need the following definitions and results.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
f
be an entire function and
M(r;f):=\max\{|f(z)|:|z|=r\}
. Then the order
\rho
of
f
is defined by

\rho=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log(\log M(r;f))}{\log r}.

If
0<\rho<\infty
, the type
\sigma
is defined as

\sigma=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log M(r;f)}{r^{\rho}},

see
[
5
]
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
k
be a non zero real number, and let
p
be a real
number with
|p|>1
. An entire function
f
has a
p
-exponential growth
of order
k
and a finite type
\gamma
, if there exists real numbers
K>0
and
\gamma
, such that

|f(z)|\leq Kp^{\frac{k}{2}(\frac{log|z|}{logp})^{2}}|z|^{\gamma},

or equivalently,

|f(z)|\leq Ke^{\frac{k}{2logp}(log|z|)^{2}+\gamma log|z|},

see
[
23
]
.

In
[
23
, Lemma 2.2]
, Ramis proved that an entire function
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
has a
p
-exponential growth of order
k
and a finite type
\gamma
if and only if there exists a constant
K>0
such that

|a_{n}|<K\,p^{\frac{-(n-\gamma)^{2}}{2k}}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

(4.1)

Definition 4.3
.

The function
f
is of
\psi
-type
\tau
if there exists constants
M
and
\tau
such that

f(re^{i\theta})\leq M\Psi(\tau r)\quad(r\rightarrow\infty).

(4.2)

In the following, we assume that
\Psi
is a comparison function, i.e.

\Psi(t)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\psi_{n}t^{n},\,\,\psi_{n}\neq 0,\;\mbox{for all}\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Psi_{n+1}}{\Psi_{n}}=0,

see
[
6
]
. Nachbin in
[
20
]
proved that a function
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
of
\psi
-type
\tau
if and only if

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{|\frac{f_{n}}{\psi_{n}}|}=\tau.

(4.3)

The Borel transform of a function
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}
is defined by

F(w)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{n}}{\Psi_{n}}\frac{1}{w^{n+1}}.

(4.4)

Hence
F(w)
is analytic if
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{|\frac{f_{n}}{\Psi_{n}}|}\frac{1}{|w|}<1
, i.e
F(w)
is analytic for
|w|>\tau
.

In
[
6
]
, a set of polynomials
\{P_{n}(z)\}_{n}
has a generalized Appell representation if it is generated by the formal relation

B(\xi)\Psi(z\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(z)(W(\xi))^{n}.

(4.5)

Here
B(\xi)
and
W(\xi)
are analytic at zero. We choose
\Omega
to be a region in which
B(\xi)
and
W(\xi)
are analytic. If
\rho_{0}
is the distance from the origin to the nearest of the boundary of
\Omega
, the series in (
4.5
) is uniformly convergent in compact subset of
\Delta
, where
\Delta
is the open disk
\{\xi\in\Omega:|\xi|<\rho_{0}\}
.

In the following, let
C
be a compact subset of
\Delta
which include
D(f)
, where
D(f)
is the union of the set of all singular points of
F
and the set of all points exterior to the domain of
F
. Let
\Re_{\Psi}[C]
be the class of entire functions of finite
\Psi
-type less than or equal
\tau
.

The following theorem from
[
6
, Theorem 7.2, P. 22]
is essential in our investigation.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(P_{n}(z))_{n}
be the generalized Appell polynomials defined in (
4.5
). If
f\in\Re_{\Psi}[C]
, then

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}(f)P_{n}(z),

where

L_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{w^{n}}{B(w)}F(w)dw,

and
F(w)
is the Borel transform of
f(z)
defined in (
4.4
) and
\Gamma
is a circle
|w|=\rho
with
\tau<\rho<\rho_{0}
.

We derive the following theorem by applying Theorem
4.4
on the polynomials
(B^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}(\cdot;q))_{n}
defined in (
1.8
).

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}
be a function satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{|\frac{f_{n}}{\Psi_{n}}}|=\tau,\quad\Psi_{n}=\frac{q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}{[n]_{q}!},\quad 0\leq\tau<\min\{\frac{2}{1-q},\frac{j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q}\},

(4.6)

where
j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}
is the smallest positive zero of
J_{\alpha}^{(2)}(\cdot;q^{2})
. Then

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}(f)\frac{B^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)}{[n]_{q}!},

where

\begin{split}L_{n}(f)=(1-q)^{-n}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}f_{k}\,\frac{q^{\frac{-k(k-1)}{2}}(q;q)_{k}(q^{2\alpha+1};q^{2})_{k-n}(\frac{1}{2})^{k-n}}{(q;q)_{k-n}(q^{2\alpha+1};q)_{k-n}}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).\end{split}

(4.7)

Proof.

The generating function of
(B^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q))_{n=0}^{\infty}
can be written as

B(\xi)E_{q}(z\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)\frac{\xi^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},\quad|\xi|<\frac{j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q},

where

B(\xi)=\frac{E_{q}(\frac{-\xi}{2})}{g^{(2)}_{\alpha}(i\xi;q)}.

Hence
\Omega=\Delta=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:|\xi|<\frac{j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q}\}
. Therefore, from Theorem
4.4
,

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}(f)\frac{B^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}(z;q)}{[n]_{q}!},

where

L_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{w^{n}}{B(w)}F(w)dw,

(4.8)

\Gamma
is a circle
|w|=\rho
with
\tau<\rho<\min\{\frac{2}{1-q},\frac{j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q}\}
and
F(w)
is defined in (
4.4
). From (
4.4
) and (
4.6
),

L_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}}{\Psi_{k}w^{k+1}}\right)\frac{w^{n}}{B(w)}dw.

(4.9)

Since
\dfrac{F(w)}{B(w)}
is analytic for
|w|=\rho
,
\tau<\rho<\min\{\frac{2}{1-q},\frac{j^{(2)}_{1,\alpha}}{1-q}\}
, then we can interchange the summation with the integral in (
4.9
) to obtain

\begin{split}L_{n}(f)&=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}}{\Psi_{k}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{(1/B(w))}{w^{k-n+1}}dw\\
&=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}}{\Psi_{k}}\frac{1}{(k-n)!}\frac{d^{k-n}}{dw^{k-n}}\left(\frac{1}{B(w)}\right)|_{w=0}\\
&=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}}{\Psi_{k}}\frac{1}{(k-n)!}\frac{d^{k-n}}{dw^{k-n}}\left(g^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iw;q)e_{q}(w/2)\right)|_{w=0},\end{split}

where we used the Cauchy integral formula, see
[
1
]
. Since
\dfrac{f^{(r)}(0)}{r!}=\dfrac{D^{r}_{q}f(0)}{[r]_{q}!}
, then

\begin{split}L_{n}(f)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}}{\Psi_{k}}\frac{1}{[k-n]_{q}!}D_{q}^{k-n}\,\left(g^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iw;q)e_{q}(w/2)\right)|_{w=0}.\end{split}

(4.10)

From (
1.16
), we conclude that

\begin{split}\frac{D_{q}^{k-n}\left(g^{(2)}_{\alpha}(iw;q)e_{q}(w/2)\right)|_{w=0}}{[k-n]_{q}!}=\frac{(1-q)^{k-n}(1/2)^{k-n}(q^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}};q)_{k-n}(-q^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}};q)_{k-n}}{(q;q)_{k-n}(q^{2\alpha+1};q)_{k-n}}.\end{split}

(4.11)

Substituting from (
4.11
) into (
4.10
), we obtain

\begin{split}L_{n}(f)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k}}{\Psi_{k}}\frac{(1-q)^{k-n}(1/2)^{k-n}(q^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}};q)_{k-n}(-q^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}};q)_{k-n}}{(q;q)_{k-n}(q^{2\alpha+1};q)_{k-n}}.\end{split}

(4.12)

Now, substituting with
\Psi_{k}=\dfrac{q^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}}{[k]_{q}!}
from (
4.6
) into (
4.12
), we obtain (
4.7
) and complete the proof of this theorem.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

. Assume that the condition of Theorem
4.5

\tau=0
.

(ii) If

then

(4.13)

∎

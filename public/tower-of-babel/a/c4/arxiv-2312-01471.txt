Nonstandard finite difference methods preserving general quadratic Lyapunov functions
Manh Tuan Hoang

Abstract
. In this work, we consider a class of dynamical systems described by ordinary differential equations under the assumption that the global asymptotic stability (GAS) of equilibrium points is established based on the Lyapunov stability theory with the help of quadratic Lyapunov functions. We employ the Micken’s methodology to construct a family of explicit nonstandard finite difference (NSFD) methods preserving any given quadratic Lyapunov function V V , i.e. they admit V V as a discrete Lyapunov function. Here, the proposed NSFD methods are derived from a novel non-local approximation for the zero vector function. Through rigorous mathematical analysis, we show that the constructed NSFD methods have the ability to preserve any given quadratic Lyapunov functions regardless of the values of the step size. As an important consequence, they are dynamically consistent with respect to the GAS of continuous-time dynamical systems. On the other hand, the positivity of the proposed NSFD methods is investigated. It is proved that they can also preserve the positivity of solutions of continuous-time dynamical systems. Finally, the theoretical findings are supported by a series of illustrative numerical experiments, in which advantages of the NSFD methods are demonstrated.

1.
Introduction

We start by considering a general finite-dimensional dynamical system described by ordinary differential equations (ODEs) associated with initial values

(1.1)

\dot{y}(t)=f(y(t)),\quad y(0)=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
y=[y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}]^{T}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f=[f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}]^{T}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\dot{y}
stands for the first derivative of
y
. Throughout this paper, we always assume that the right-hand side function
f
satisfies all needed smoothness assumptions such that solutions of (
1.1
) exist and are unique (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
24
,
42
]
). It should be emphasized that the dynamical system (
1.1
) is often utilized to mathematically model important phenomena and processes in science and engineering
[
2
,
24
,
42
]
.

We recall that a point
y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called an
equilibrium point
of (
1.1
) if
f(y^{*})=0
and an equilibrium point is said to be
globally asymptotically stable
if it is
stable
and
globally attractive

[
24
,
42
]
. Without the loss of generality, we can always assume that
y^{*}=0
. It is well-known that the global asymptotic stability (GAS) analysis of equilibrium points is a very important problem with various useful applications in fields of theory and practice, for example in mathematical physics, economics, control theory, biology, ecology and so on (see, for instance,
[
24
,
29
,
30
,
32
,
42
]
). The Lyapunov stability theory with the help of suitable Lyapunov functions has been recognized as one of the most successful approaches to this problem (see, for example,
[
5
,
13
,
25
,
26
,

,

,

,
43
,

,

]
). For the sake of convenience, below we recall the Lyapunov stability theorem, which is also known as Barbashin-Krasovskii theorem, for the GAS of continuous-time dynamical systems
[
24
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
24
, Theorem 4.2]

Let
y^{*}=0
be an equilibrium point for (
1.1
). Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuously differentiable function such that:

(1.2)

\begin{split}&V(0)=0,\\
&V(y)>0,\quad\forall y\neq 0,\\
&\|y\|\to\infty\Rightarrow\|V(t)\|\to\infty,\\
&\dot{V(y)}<0,\quad\forall y\neq 0,\end{split}

then
y^{*}=0
is globally asymptotically stable.

Assume that the GAS of the dynamical system model (
1.1
) has been established through a known Lyapunov function
V
and we are using the following general one-step numerical method to approximate the solutions of (
1.1
)

(1.3)

y^{k+1}=\Phi(\Delta t,y^{k}),

where
\Delta t>0
stands for the step size and
y^{k}
is the intended approximation for
y(t_{k})
with
t_{k}=k\Delta t
(
k\geq 1
). An essential requirement for the general numerical method (
1.3
) is that it should preserve the Lyapunov function
V
for all the step sizes
\Delta t
, i.e. (
1.3
) admits the function
V
as a discrete Lyapunov function. Consequently, we deduce from the Lyapunov stability theory for discrete-time dynamical systems
[

,
42
]
that the difference equation model (
1.3
) preserves the GAS of (
1.1
) regardless of the value of the step size. In this case, according to the Mickens’ methodology
[

,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
, the numerical method is said to be dynamically consistent with respect to (w.r.t) the GAS of the counterpart differential equation model.

The study of numerical methods preserving Lyapunov functions is a very important problem, which has attracted the attention of many researchers (see, e.g.,

is a globally asymptotically stable equilibrium point of (

V_{QLF}
defined in (
1.4
) for all the finite step sizes
\Delta t

V_{QLF}
satisfies

(1.5)

\Delta V_{QLF}(y^{k}):=V_{QLF}(y^{k+1})-V_{QLF}(y^{k})<0

for all

1.2

Along with the GAS with Lyapunov functions, we also consider dynamical systems of the form (

2.
NSFD methods preserving quadratic Lyapunov functions

This section is devoted to constructing a family of explicit NSFD methods that preserve the general quadratic Lyapunov function
V_{QLF}
defined in (
1.4
). Note that the derivative of
V_{QLF}
along the solutions of (
1.1
) satisfies

(2.1)

\dot{V}_{QLF}(y)=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(y_{i}-y_{i}^{*})f_{i}(y)<0,\quad\forall y\neq y^{*}.

So, numerical methods should provide numerical approximations that satisfy (
1.5
).

First, we replace the first derivatives
\dot{y}_{i}(t)
(
1\leq i\leq n
) by

(2.2)

\dot{y}_{i}(t)|_{t=t_{k}}\approx\dfrac{y_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{k}}{\phi(\Delta t)},

where
\phi(\Delta t)
is called a denominator function, which is positive and
\phi(\Delta t)=\Delta t+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})
as
\Delta t\to 0

[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
. Next, by applying the rules of construction of NSFD
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
, we approximate the right-hand side function
f_{i}(y(t))

(1\leq i\leq n)
by

(2.3)

\begin{split}f_{i}(y(t_{k}))&=f_{i}(y(t_{k}))+\big(y_{i}(t_{k})\tau(y(t_{k}))-y_{i}(t_{k})\tau(y(t_{k}))\big)\\
&\approx f_{i}(y^{k})+\big(y_{i}^{k}\tau(y^{k})-y_{i}^{k+1}\tau(y^{k})\big),\end{split}

where
\tau(y):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function satisfying
\tau(y)\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which plays a role as a weight. The term
y_{i}^{k}\tau(y^{k})-y_{i}^{k+1}\tau(y^{k})
can be considered as a non-local approximation of the zero function. The approximations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) lead to the following family of NSFD methods

(2.4)

\dfrac{y_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{k}}{\phi(\Delta t)}=f_{i}(y^{k})+y_{i}^{k}\tau(y^{k})-y_{i}^{k+1}\tau(y^{k}).

The explicit form of (
2.4
) is given by

(2.5)

y_{i}^{k+1}=\dfrac{y_{i}^{k}+\phi(\Delta t)\big(f_{i}(y^{k})+y_{i}^{k}\tau(y^{k})\big)}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}=y_{i}^{k}+\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)f_{i}(y^{k})}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}.

We now determine conditions for the function
\tau(y)
such that (
2.4
) or also (
2.5
) preserves the general quadratic Lyapunov function (
1.4
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\tau(y)
be a function with the property that for
y\neq y^{*}

(2.6)

\tau(y)\geq\tau_{L}(y):=-\dfrac{{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(f_{i}(y))^{2}}{\dot{V}_{QLF}(y)},

where
\dot{V}_{QLF}(y)
is given in (
2.1
). Then, the NSFD model (
2.4
) admits the quadratic Lyapunov function
V_{QLF}
defined in (
1.4
) as a discrete Lyapunov function for all the values of the step size
\Delta t
.

Proof.

We only need to verify that (
1.5
) is satisfied. Indeed, by using (
2.5
) we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}\Delta V_{QLF}(y^{k})&:=V_{QLF}(y^{k+1})-V_{QLF}(y^{k})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(y_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{*})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\big(y_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{*})^{2}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\big[(y_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{*})^{2}-\big(y_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{*})^{2}\big]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\big[(y_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{k})(y_{i}^{k+1}+y_{i}^{k}-2y_{i}^{*})\big]\\
&={{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)f_{i}(y^{k})}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\bigg(2y_{i}^{k}-2y_{i}^{*}+\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)f_{i}(y^{k})}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\bigg)\\
&={{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)f_{i}(y^{k})}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\big(2y_{i}^{k}-2y_{i}^{*})+{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\bigg(\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)f_{i}(y^{k})}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\bigg)^{2}\\
&=\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}2\alpha_{i}f_{i}(y^{k})(y_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{*})+{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\bigg(\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)f_{i}(y^{k})}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\bigg)^{2}\\
&=\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\dot{V}_{QLF}(y^{k})+{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\bigg(\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)f_{i}(y^{k})}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\bigg)^{2}\\
&=\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\bigg[\dot{V}_{QLF}(y^{k})+{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}\big(f_{i}(y^{k})\big)^{2}\bigg].\end{split}

It follows from (
2.7
) that
\Delta V_{QLF}(y^{k})<0
if and only if

(2.8)

\mathcal{L}:=\dot{V}_{QLF}(y^{k})+\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)}{1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})}{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\big(f_{i}(y^{k})\big)^{2}<0,

which is equivalent to (since
1+\phi(\Delta t)\tau(y^{k})>0
)

\dot{V}_{QLF}(y^{k})+\phi(\Delta t)\bigg[\tau(y^{k})\dot{V}_{QLF}(y^{k})+{{\sum}}_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\big(f_{i}(y^{k})\big)^{2}\bigg]<0.

It is easy to check that if (
2.6
) then
\dot{V}_{QLF}(y^{k})\tau(y^{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(f_{i}(y^{k}))^{2}\leq 0
. Consequently,
\mathcal{L}
in (
2.8
) is negative. Therefore, we conclude that
\Delta V_{QLF}(y^{k})
in (
2.7
) is negative provided that (
2.6
) holds. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The function
\tau_{L}(y)
in (
2.6
) satisfies
\tau_{L}(y)\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
. So,
\tau(y)\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
.
For each function
\tau(y)
, we obtain an NSFD method preserving the quadratic Lyapunov functions. In general, we can take

\tau(y)=\tau_{L}(y)+g(y),

where
g(y)
satisfies
g(y)\geq 0
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 2.3
.

The non-local approximation (
2.3
) and the positivity of
\tau(y)
ensure that the NSFD model (
2.4
) is defined and consistent; hence, it is convergent of order one (see, for e.g.,
[
2
,
6
]
).

\begin{split}&f_{1}(y_{1},y_{2})=-Ay_{1}^{3}+By_{2},\\

4.
On the positivity of NSFD methods

In this section, we investigate the positivity of the NSFD methods constructed in Section
2
. Assume that (
1.1
) admits the set

(4.1)

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\big\{(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}\geq 0\big\}

as a positively invariant set, that is
y(t)\geq 0
whenever
y(0)\geq 0
. Here, the notation ”
\geq
” are meant entry-wise for vectors. A necessary and sufficient condition for the set
\mathbb{R}^{n}

to be positively invariant for (
1.1
) is (see
[
23
,
41
]
)

(4.2)

f_{i}(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{i-1},0,y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{n})\geq 0,\quad\forall(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{i-1},y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n-1}.

We now give conditions for the NSFD model (
2.4
) such that
y^{0}=y(0)\geq 0
implies
y^{k}\geq 0
(
k>0
) for all
\Delta t>0
. Note that (
4.2
) implies that if
f_{i}(y)<0
, then
y_{i}^{k}>0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\tau_{i}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
(
i=1,2,\ldots,n
) be functions satisfying

\tau_{i}(y)\geq\tau_{i}^{*}(y):=\begin{cases}&0\quad\mbox{if}\quad f_{i}(y)\quad\geq 0,\\
&\\
&-\dfrac{f_{i}(y)}{y_{i}}\quad\mbox{if}\quad f_{i}(y)<0\end{cases}

and
\tau_{P}(y)
be a function with the property that

\tau_{P}(y)\geq\max\big\{\tau_{1}^{*}(y),\tau_{2}^{*}(y),\ldots,\tau_{n}^{*}(y)\big\}.

Assume that
\tau(y):\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function satisfying

(4.3)

\tau(y)\geq\tau_{P}(y).

Then, the NSFD model (
2.4
) admits the set
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
defined in (
4.1
) as a positively invariant set for all the values of the step size
\Delta t
, that is
y^{k}\geq 0
for
k\geq 1
whenever
y(0)\geq 0
.

Proof.

This theorem is proved by mathematical induction. Assume that
y^{k}\geq 0
, we need to show that
y^{k+1}\geq 0
. Indeed, we deduce from (
2.5
) that
y_{i}^{k+1}\geq 0
if

(4.4)

f_{i}(y^{k})+y_{i}^{k}\tau(y^{k})\geq 0.

It is clear that (
4.4
) is automatically satisfied if
f_{i}(y^{k})\geq 0
. If
f_{i}(y^{k})<0
, then it follows from (
4.2
) that
y_{i}^{k}>0
. Hence, (
4.4
) is satisfied whenever

\tau(y^{k})\geq-\dfrac{f_{i}(y^{k})}{y_{i}^{k}}=\tau_{i}^{*}(y^{k}).

Therefore, (
4.3
) implies the preservation of positivity of the NSFD model (
2.4
). The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Combining the results constructed in Sections
2
and
4
, we obtain the NSFD methods that preserve the quadratic Lyapunov functions and the positivity of solutions of the continuous-time dynamical systems model (
1.1
). More clearly, the NSFD model (
2.4
) preserves the quadratic Lyapunov functions and the positivity of solutions of (
1.1
) if the weight
\tau(y)
is a function taking enough large values.

5.
Concluding remarks and open problems

As the main conclusion of this work, we have introduced a simple approach to the construction of NSFD methods preserving the general quadratic Lyapunov functions. The proposed NSFD methods are derived from a novel approximation for the zero vector function. We have showed that the constructed NSFD methods have the ability to preserve any given quadratic Lyapunov function regardless of the values of the step size, i.e. they admit
V
as a discrete Lyapunov function As an important consequence, they are dynamically consistent w.r.t the GAS of the continuous-time dynamical systems. On the other hand, we study the positivity of the proposed NSFD methods. It is proved that they can also preserve the positivity of the solutions of the continuous-time dynamical systems. Finally, the theoretical findings are supported by a series of illustrative numerical experiments, in which advantages of the NSFD methods are shown.

Before ending this section, we discuss some open problems that studies in NSFD methods can take.

(1)

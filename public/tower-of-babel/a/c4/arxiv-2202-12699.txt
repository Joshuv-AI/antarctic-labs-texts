Turnpike Properties for Stochastic Linear-Quadratic Optimal Control Problems
Jingrui Sun Hanxiao Wang Jiongmin Yong

Abstract




(
In the Memory of Professor Chaohao Gu
)

Abstract.

This paper analyzes the limiting behavior of stochastic linear-quadratic optimal control problems in finite time horizon
[0,T]
as
T\to\infty
. The so-called turnpike properties are established for such problems, under stabilizability condition which is weaker than the controllability, normally imposed in the similar problem for ordinary differential systems. In dealing with the turnpike problem, a crucial issue is to determine the corresponding static optimization problem.
Intuitively mimicking deterministic situations, it seems to be natural to include both the drift and the diffusion as constraints in the static optimization problem. However, this would lead us to a wrong direction. It is found that the correct static problem should contain the diffusion as a part of the objective function, which reveals a deep feature of the stochastic turnpike problem.

Keywords.

Turnpike property, stochastic optimal control, static optimization, linear-quadratic,
stabilizability, Riccati equation.

49N10, 93D23, 93E15, 93E20.

Introduction

Let
(\varOmega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which a standard one-dimensional Brownian
motion
W=\{W(t)\bigm|t\geqslant 0\}
is defined.
Denote by
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
the usual augmentation of the natural filtration generated by
W
.
For a random variable
\xi
, we write
\xi\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
if
\xi
is
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable;
and for a stochastic process
X
, we write
X\in\mathbb{F}
if it is progressively measurable with respect
to the filtration
\mathbb{F}
.

Consider the following controlled linear stochastic differential equation (SDE, for short)

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX(t)=[AX(t)+Bu(t)+b]dt+[CX(t)+Du(t)+\sigma]dW(t),\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(0)=x,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

and the following general quadratic cost functional

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;u(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\langle QX(t),X(t)\rangle+2\langle SX(t),u(t)\rangle+\langle Ru(t),u(t)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\langle q,X(t)\rangle+2\langle r,u(t)\rangle\Big]dt,\end{array}

(1.2)

where
A,C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
B,D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
,
Q\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
S\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
R\in\mathbb{S}^{m}
,
b,\sigma,q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are constant matrices or vectors with
\mathbb{S}^{k}
being the set of all
(k\times k)
symmetric matrices. The classical
stochastic linear-quadratic
(LQ, for short)
optimal control problem
over the finite time-horizon
[0,T]
is to find a control
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
from the space

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}[0,T]=\left\{u:[0,T]\times\varOmega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\bigm|u\in\mathbb{F}~\text{and}~\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}dt<\infty\right\}

(1.3)

such that the cost functional (
1.2
) is minimized over
\mathscr{U}[0,T]
, for a given initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More precisely, it can be stated as follows.

Problem (SLQ)
T
.

For any given initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, find a control
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]
such that

\displaystyle J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]}J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;u(\cdot))\equiv V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x).

(1.4)

The process
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
in (
1.4
) (if exists) is called an
open-loop optimal control

of Problem (SLQ)
T
for the initial state
x
,
the corresponding state process
\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
is called an
open-loop optimal state process
,

(\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot),\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot))
is called an
open-loop optimal pair
, and
V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
is called the
value function
of Problem (SLQ)
T
.

In this paper, we are concerned with the limiting behavior of the optimal pair
(\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
,
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot))
of Problem (SLQ)
T
as the time-horizon
T
tends to infinity. More precisely, we want to seek conditions under which there exist positive constants
K,\mu>0
, independent of
T
, such that for some
(x^{*},u^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, it holds

\big|\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-x^{*}]\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}[\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-u^{*}]\big|\leqslant K\big[e^{-\mu t}+e^{-\mu(T-t)}\big],\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(1.5)

This is referred to as the
exponential turnpike property
of Problem (SLQ)
T
. Such a property implies that for any small
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, the following is true:

\big|\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-x^{*}]\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}[\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-u^{*}]\big|\leqslant 2Ke^{-\mu\delta T},\qquad\forall t\in[\delta T,(1-\delta)T].

(1.6)

Namely, in a big portion
[\delta T,(1-\delta)T]
of
[0,T]
, the optimal pair
(\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot),\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot))
is exponentially close to the point
(x^{*},u^{*})
. This will give us the essential picture of the optimal pair without having to solve it analytically, which is very useful in applications.

The study of turnpike phenomena for deterministic problems can be traced back to the work of von Neumann
[
15
]
on problems in economics. In 1958, Dorfman, Samuelson, and Solow (
[
5
]
) coined the name “turnpike” which was used in the highway system of the United States. Since then the turnpike phenomena have attracted considerable attentions, not only in mathematical economy
[
14
]
, but also in many other fields such as mathematical biology
[
9
]
and chemical processes
[
19
]
. It is well-known by now that the turnpike property is a general phenomenon which holds for a large class of variational and optimal control problems. Numerous relevant results have been established for finite and infinite dimensional problems in the context of deterministic discrete-time and continuous-time systems (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
27
,
28
,
24
,
30
,
23
,
13
,
29
,
2
,
7
]
and the references therein). In particular, we mention the papers
[
4
,
6
]
for discrete-time LQ problems and the papers
[
17
,
18
]
for continuous-time LQ problems of ordinary differential equations.

The study of turnpike phenomena for stochastic optimal control problems is quite lacking in literature. In this paper, we shall carry out a thorough investigation on the turnpike property for the stochastic LQ optimal control problem introduced earlier.
Note that when
C=0
and
D=0
, Problem (SLQ)
{}_{{}_{T}}
reduces to a deterministic LQ problem, for which the exponential turnpike property has been established in
[
17
]

and
[
24
]
under controllability and observability assumptions. For the deterministic LQ problem (i.e., the case of
C=0,D=0,\sigma=0
), the associated static optimization problem, which is used to determine the point
(x^{*},u^{*})
, reads

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\text{Minimize}~F_{0}(x,u)\negthinspace\equiv\negthinspace\langle Qx,x\rangle\negthinspace+\negthinspace 2\langle Sx,u\rangle\negthinspace+\negthinspace\langle Ru,u\rangle+2\langle q,x\rangle+2\langle r,u\rangle,\\
\displaystyle\text{subject to}\quad Ax+Bu+b=0.\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

To establish the turnpike property for the stochastic LQ problem, suggested by the deterministic situation, one might naively introduce the following static optimization problem:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\text{Minimize}\quad F_{0}(x,u),\\
\displaystyle\text{subject to}\quad Ax+Bu+b=0,\quad Cx+Du+\sigma=0.\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

Assume the above admits an optima solution
(x^{*},u^{*})
. Then one tries to show that the optimal pair
(\bar{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot),\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot))
of Problem (SLQ)
T
satisfies (
1.5
). However, a little careful observation of the above, one immediately realize it is not natural because the condition that ensuring such an optimization problem to be feasible is already very restrictive: The two equality constraints might be contracting each other. It turns out that (
1.8
) is not the correct one, which will be shown later in this paper.
As a main contribution of this paper, we found that the correct formulation of the static optimization problem is as follows:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\hbox{Minimize}\quad F(x,u)\equiv F_{0}(x,u)+\langle P(Cx+Du+\sigma),Cx+Du+\sigma\rangle,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hbox{subject to }\quad Ax+Bu+b=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

where
P
is a positive definite solution to the following algebraic Riccati equation (ARE, for short):

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+Q\\
\displaystyle\quad-(PB+C^{\top}PD+S^{\top})(R+D^{\top}PD)^{-1}(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC+S)=0.\end{array}

(1.10)

By assuming the controlled homogenous state equation (denoted by
[A,C;B,D]
) to be
stabilizable
(see the next section for a precise definition) and the following
strong standard condition
:

R>0,\quad Q-S^{\top}R^{-1}S>0,

(1.11)

one will have a unique suitable positive definite solution
P
to the above ARE (
1.10
), and problem (
1.9
) is not only feasible, but also admits a unique solution
(x^{*},u^{*})
. We will show that there exist positive constants
K,\mu>0
, independent of
T
, such that (
1.5
) holds, and the adjoint process
\bar{Y}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
will also have the same turnpike property. Note that by a (classical) standard condition, we mean that
Q-S^{\top}R^{-1}S
is merely positive semi-definite, which could even be 0. In such cases,
P
might not be positive definite, and
(x,u)\mapsto F(x,u)
might not be coercive. Therefore, it is unclear if the optimal solution
(x^{*},u^{*})
exists, or it might not be unique. This might bring some additional issues into the study and we will try to address that in our future publications. Also, we note that for the state equation, stabilizability is strictly weaker than the (null) controllability which was assumed in
[
17
,
24
,
13
]
for deterministic problems. For the study of controllability of linear SDEs, see
[
25
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In
Section 2
, we give the preliminaries and collect some relevant results on stochastic LQ optimal control problems.
In
Section 3
we recall the notion of stabilizability and formulate the correct static optimization problem. The convergence of the solution to a related differential Riccati equation as the time-horizon tends to infinity will be presented in
Section 4
. In
Section 5
, we study the static optimization problem associated to Problem (SLQ)
T
and establish the turnpike property of Problem (SLQ)
T
as well as of the adjoint process. Some concluding remarks are collected in
Section 6
.

, and that

For the purpose of later presentation, we recall some results of time-variant stochastic LQ problem in finite horizon. Consider the state equation

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d{\bf X}(t)=[{\bf A}(t){\bf X}(t)+{\bf B}(t){\bf u}(t)+{\bf b}(t)]dt+[{\bf C}(t){\bf X}(t)+{\bf D}(t){\bf u}(t)+\boldsymbol{\sigma}(t)]dW(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\bf X}(0)=x,\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

with the cost functional

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\bf J}(x;{\bf u}(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\Big\{\langle{\bf G}{\bf X}(T),{\bf X}(T)\rangle+2\langle{\bf g},{\bf X}(T)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\Big\langle\!\!\begin{pmatrix}{\bf Q}(t)&{\bf S}(t)^{\top}\\
{\bf S}(t)&{\bf R}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\begin{pmatrix}{\bf X}(t)\\
{\bf u}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!,\begin{pmatrix}{\bf X}(t)\\
{\bf u}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\!\Big\rangle+2\Big\langle\!\!\begin{pmatrix}{\bf q}(t)\\
{\bf r}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!,\begin{pmatrix}{\bf X}(t)\\
{\bf u}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\!\Big\rangle\Big]dt\Big\},\end{array}

(2.2)

where in (
2.1
), the coefficients satisfy

{\bf A}(\cdot),{\bf C}(\cdot)\negthinspace\in\negthinspace L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),\quad{\bf B}(\cdot),{\bf D}(\cdot)\negthinspace\in\negthinspace L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}),\quad{\bf b}(\cdot),\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\cdot)\negthinspace\in\negthinspace L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and in (
2.2
), the weighting coefficients satisfy

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\bf G}\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\quad{\bf Q}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{n}),\quad{\bf S}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}),\quad{\bf R}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{m}),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\bf g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad{\bf q}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad{\bf r}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}).\end{array}

The standard stochastic LQ optimal control problem on
[0,T]
can be stated as follows.

Problem (SLQ
[0,T]
).

For a given initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, find a control
\bar{\bf u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]
such that

{\bf J}(x;\bar{\bf u}(\cdot))=\inf_{{\bf u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]}{\bf J}(x;{\bf u}(\cdot))\equiv{\bf V}(x).

(2.3)

The process
\bar{\bf u}(\cdot)
(if exists) in (
2.3
) is called an (
open-loop
)
optimal control
for the initial state
x
, and
{\bf V}(x)
is called the
value
of Problem (SLQ
[0,T]
) at
x
.

The following lemma summarizes a few results for Problem (SLQ).
For proofs, the reader is referred to the book
[
22
]
by Sun and Yong;
see also
[
20
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that for some constant
\delta>0
,

{\bf R}(t)\geqslant\delta I,\qquad{\bf Q}(t)-{\bf S}(t)^{\top}{\bf R}(t)^{-1}{\bf S}(t)\geqslant 0.

(2.4)

Then, the following hold:

(i)
For every initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Problem
(SLQ)
[0,T]
has a unique open-loop optimal control.

(ii)
A pair
(\bar{\bf X}(\cdot),\bar{\bf u}(\cdot))
is an open-loop optimal pair of Problem
(SLQ)
{}_{{}_{T}}
the initial state
x
if and only if there exists a pair
(\bar{\bf Y}(\cdot),\bar{\bf Z}(\cdot))
of adapted processes such that

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\bar{\bf X}=\big[{\bf A}(t)\bar{\bf X}(t)+{\bf B}(t)\bar{\bf u}(t)+{\bf b}(t)\big]dt+\big[{\bf C}(t)\bar{\bf X}(t)+{\bf D}(t)\bar{\bf u}(t)+\boldsymbol{\sigma}(t)\big]dW,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\bar{\bf Y}(t)=-\big[{\bf A}(t)^{\top}\bar{\bf Y}(t)+{\bf C}(t)^{\top}\bar{\bf Z}(t)+{\bf Q}(t)\bar{\bf X}(t)+{\bf S}(t)^{\top}\bar{\bf u}(t)+{\bf q}(t)\big]dt+\bar{\bf Z}(t)dW(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\bar{\bf X}(0)=x,\qquad\bar{\bf Y}(T)={\bf G}\bar{\bf X}(T)+{\bf g},\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

and the following condition holds:

{\bf B}(t)^{\top}\bar{\bf Y}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}\bar{\bf Z}(t)+{\bf S}(t)\bar{\bf X}(t)+{\bf R}(t)\bar{\bf u}(t)+{\bf r}(t)=0,\quad\text{a.e.}\;t\in[0,T],~\text{a.s.}

(2.6)

(iii)
The Riccati differential equation

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\dot{\bf P}(t)+{\bf P}(t){\bf A}(t)+{\bf A}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t)+{\bf C}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf C}(t)+{\bf Q}(t)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad-\big[{\bf P}(t){\bf B}(t)+{\bf C}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf D}(t)+{\bf S}(t)^{\top}\big]\big[{\bf R}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf D}(t)\big]^{-1}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\big[{\bf B}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf C}(t)+{\bf S}(t)\big]=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\bf P}(T)={\bf G}\end{array}\right.

(2.7)

admits a unique positive semidefinite solution
{\bf P}(\cdot)\in C([0,T];\mathbb{S}^{n})
. In particular, if

{\bf Q}(t)-{\bf S}(t)^{\top}{\bf R}(t)^{-1}{\bf S}(t)>0,

then
{\bf P}(t)>0
for all
t\in[0,T)
.

(iv)
The unique open-loop optimal control
\bar{\bf u}(\cdot)
for the initial state
x
is given by

\bar{\bf u}(t)=\boldsymbol{\Theta}(t)\bar{\bf X}(t)-\big[{\bf R}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf D}(t)\big]^{-1}\big[{\bf B}(t)^{\top}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t)\boldsymbol{\sigma}(t)+{\bf r}(t)\big],

where

\boldsymbol{\Theta}(t)=-\big[{\bf R}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf D}(t)\big]^{-1}\big[{\bf B}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf C}(t)+{\bf S}(t)\big],

and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\cdot)
is the solution to the terminal value problem of the ordinary differential equation (ODE, for short)

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}(t)+\big[{\bf A}(t)+{\bf B}(t)\boldsymbol{\Theta}(t)\big]^{\top}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t)+\big[{\bf C}(t)+{\bf D}(t)\boldsymbol{\Theta}(t)\big]^{\top}{\bf P}(t)\boldsymbol{\sigma}(t)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\boldsymbol{\Theta}(t)^{\top}{\bf r}(t)+{\bf P}(t){\bf b}(t)+{\bf q}(t)=0,\qquad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}(T)={\bf g}.\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

(v)
The value function is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\bf V}(x)={1\over 2}\langle{\bf P}(0)x,x\rangle+\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}(0),x\rangle+{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\langle{\bf P}(t)\boldsymbol{\sigma}(t),\boldsymbol{\sigma}(t)\rangle+2\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t),{\bf b}(t)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\big|\big[{\bf R}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t){\bf D}(t)\big]^{-{1\over 2}}\big[{\bf B}(t)^{\top}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t)+{\bf D}(t)^{\top}{\bf P}(t)\boldsymbol{\sigma}(t)+{\bf r}(t)\big]\big|^{2}\Big)dt.\end{array}

Now, we return to our Problem (SLQ)
{}_{{}_{T}}
. Let us make a reduction under the strong standard conditions (
1.11
). Set

u(t)=v(t)-R^{-1}SX(t),\qquad\in[0,T],

(2.9)

with

. We observe the following (

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\langle QX,X\rangle+2\langle SX,u\rangle+\langle Ru,u\rangle+2\langle q,X\rangle+2\langle r,u\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\negthinspace\langle QX,X\rangle\negthinspace+\negthinspace 2\langle SX,v\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}SX\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}r\rangle\negthinspace+\negthinspace\langle R(v\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}SX\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}r),v\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}SX\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}r\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+2\langle q,X\rangle+2\langle r,v-R^{-1}SX-R^{-1}r\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\negthinspace\langle(Q\negthinspace-\negthinspace S^{\top}R^{-1}S)X,X\rangle\negthinspace+\negthinspace\langle R(v\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}r),v\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}r\rangle\negthinspace+\negthinspace 2\langle q\negthinspace-\negthinspace S^{\top}R^{-1}r,X\rangle\negthinspace+\negthinspace 2\langle r,v\negthinspace-\negthinspace R^{-1}r\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{Q}X,X\rangle+\langle Rv,v\rangle+2\langle q-S^{\top}R^{-1}r,X\rangle-\langle R^{-1}r,r\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\equiv\langle\widehat{Q}X,X\rangle+\langle Rv,v\rangle+2\langle\widehat{q},X\rangle-\varphi_{0},\end{array}

where

\widehat{Q}=Q-S^{\top}R^{-1}S,\qquad\widehat{q}=q-S^{\top}R^{-1}r,\qquad\varphi_{0}=r^{\top}R^{-1}r.

Also, under (
2.9
), one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle AX+Bu+b=(A-R^{-1}S)X+Bv+b-BR^{-1}r\equiv\widehat{A}X+Bv+\widehat{b},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle CX+Du+\sigma=(C-R^{-1}S)X+Dv+\sigma-DR^{-1}r\equiv\widehat{C}X+Dv+\widehat{\sigma},\end{array}

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{A}=A-R^{-1}S,\qquad\widehat{b}=b-BR^{-1}r,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{C}=C-R^{-1}S,\qquad\widehat{\sigma}=\sigma-DR^{-1}r.\end{array}

From the above reduction, we end up with the following state
equation:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX(t)=\Big(\widehat{A}X(t)+Bv(t)+\widehat{b}\Big)dt+\Big(\widehat{C}X(t)+Dv(t)+\widehat{\sigma}\Big)dW(t),\\
\displaystyle X(0)=x,\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

with the cost functional

J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;v(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\big[\langle\widehat{Q}X(t),X(t)\rangle+\langle Rv(t),v(t)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{q},X(t)\rangle-\varphi_{0}\big]dt.

(2.11)

It is clear that the (open-loop) optimal pair of the LQ problem
associated with (
2.10
)–(
2.11
) is the same as that of
the LQ problem associated with the state equation (
2.10
) and the cost functional

\widehat{J}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;v(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[\langle\widehat{Q}X(t),X(t)\rangle+\langle Rv(t),v(t)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{q},X(t)\rangle\big]dt\equiv J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;v(\cdot))+{\varphi_{0}T\over 2}.

Because of the above reduction, one sees that it suffices to consider Problem (SLQ)
{}_{{}_{T}}
for the state equation (
1.1
) with the following cost functional

J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(x;u(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\big[\langle QX(t),X(t)\rangle+\langle Ru(t),u(t)\rangle+2\langle q,X(t)\rangle\big]dt.

(2.12)

In the case that
b=\sigma=q=0
, we denote the corresponding by Problem (SLQ)
{}^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}
, and call it a homogenous LQ problem on
[0,T]
. The value function of Problem (SLQ)
{}^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}
is denoted by

.

3
Stabilizability and the Static Optimization Problem

In this section, we are going to make some further preparations.

Let us denote by
[A,C]
the following linear homogeneous uncontrolled SDE:

dX(t)=AX(t)dt+CX(t)dW(t),\quad t\geqslant 0.

(3.1)

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a unique solution
X(\cdot)\equiv X(\cdot\,;x)
of the above satisfying
X(0;x)=x
.
We recall the following classical notion.

Definition 3.1
.

System
[A,C]
is said to be

(i)
L^{2}
-stable
if

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|X(t;x)|^{2}dt<\infty,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.2)

(ii)
mean-square exponentially stable
if there exists a
\beta>0
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}e^{\beta t}|X(t;x)|<\infty,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.3)

To characterize the above notions, we let
\varPhi(\cdot)
be the solution to the matrix SDE

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle d\varPhi(t)=A\varPhi(t)dt+C\varPhi(t)dW(t),\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
\displaystyle\varPhi(0)=I_{n}.\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

We have the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)
The system
[A,C]
is mean-square exponentially stable.

(ii)
There exist constants
\alpha,\beta>0
such that

\mathbb{E}|\varPhi(t)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha e^{-\beta t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

(3.5)

(iii)
It holds that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varPhi(t)|^{2}dt<\infty.

(3.6)

(iv)
The system
[A,C]
is
L^{2}
-stable.

(v)
There exists a
P\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
such that

PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC<0.

(3.7)

The proof is straightforward (see
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
, or the book
[
22
]
).

Corollary 3.3
.

If the system
[A,C]
is
L^{2}
-stable, then

|e^{At}|\leqslant\sqrt{\alpha}e^{-(\beta t/2)},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are as in (
3.5
).

Proof.

It is easy to prove by noting that
e^{At}=\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(t)]
.
∎

Now we denote by
[A,C;B,D]
the following controlled (homogeneous) linear system:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX(t)=[AX(t)+Bu(t)]dt+[CX(t)+Du(t)]dW(t),\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(0)=x,\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

where
u(\cdot)
is taken from the following set of
admissible controls

\mathscr{U}]0,\infty)\negthinspace=\negthinspace\Big\{u\negthinspace:\negthinspace[0,\infty)\negthinspace\times\negthinspace\varOmega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\bigm|u(\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressivey measurable, }\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|u(t)|^{2}dt\negthinspace<\negthinspace\infty\Big\}.

We recall the following notion (see
[
8
]
and
[
22
]
).

Definition 3.4
.

The system
[A,C;B,D]
is said to be
L^{2}
-stabilizable
if there exists a matrix
\varTheta\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

such that the (closed-loop) system
[A+B\varTheta,C+D\varTheta]
is
L^{2}
-stable.
In this case,
\varTheta
is called a
stabilizer
of
[A,C;B,D]
.

We point out that the
L^{2}
-stabilizability is a weaker condition than the
null controllability
, meaning that for any initial state
x
there exists a control to steer the system state from
x
to
0
in a finite time interval. In fact, according to
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
, the system
[A,C;B,D]
is
L^{2}
-stabilizable
if and only if for any initial state
x
, there exists an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued process

such that the solution
X(\cdot\,;x,u(\cdot))
of (

) is also square-integrable over
[0,\infty)
. Whereas, the system
[A,C;B,D]
is null controllable, then, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can find a
v(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]
for some
T>0
, such that
X(T;x,v(\cdot))=0
. Thus, with

u(t)\triangleq\left\{\begin{aligned} &v(t),&&t\in[0,T],\\
&0,&&t>T,\end{aligned}\right.

the corresponding solution
X(\cdot)\equiv X(\cdot\,;x,u(\cdot))
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|X(t)|^{2}dt<\infty
. This shows that the null controllability is stronger than the
L^{2}
-stabilizability. Controllability for linear ODEs is very standard in control theory, see
[
10
]
. For linear SDEs, the situation is much more complicated, see
[
16
,
12
,
25
]
for some known results.

We now introduce the following hypotheses.

(H1)

The system
[A,C;B,D]
is
L^{2}
-stabilizable.

(H2)

The weighting matrices
Q\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
and
R\in\mathbb{S}^{m}_{+}
.

Under the above
(H1)
–
(H2)
, we may consider the state equation (
3.8
) with the cost functional

J^{0}_{\infty}(x;u(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\langle QX(t),X(t)\rangle+\langle Ru(t),u(t)\rangle\Big)dt.

(3.9)

We could formulate the following homogeneous LQ problem in the infinite horizon
[0,\infty)
.

Problem (SLQ)
{}_{\infty}^{0}
.
For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, find
\bar{u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,\infty)
such that

J^{0}_{\infty}(x;\bar{u}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,\infty)}J^{0}_{\infty}(x;u(\cdot))\equiv V^{0}_{\infty}(x).

(3.10)

The following collects the relevant results of Problem (SLQ)
{}_{\infty}^{0}
. See
[
22
]
for a proof.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(H1)
–
(H2)
hold. Then for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Problem (LQ)
{}_{\infty}^{0}
admits a unique open-loop optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
. Moreover, the following ARE

PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+Q-(PB+C^{\top}PD)(R+D^{\top}PD)^{-1}(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC)=0,

(3.11)

admits a unique solution
P\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
such that the open-loop optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
admits the following

:

\bar{u}(t)=-\varTheta(t)\bar{X}(t),\qquad t\in[0,\infty),

(3.12)

where

\varTheta=-(R+D^{\top}PD)^{-1}(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC)

(3.13)

is a stabilizer of
[A,C;B,D]
, and the value function has the following quadratic form:

V^{0}_{\infty}(x)=\langle Px,x\rangle,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.14)

In the above case,

is referred to as a

of the ARE with respect to the system

(3.15)

Now, we define

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathscr{V}=\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\bigm|Ax+Bu+b=0\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle F(x,u)=\langle Qx,x\rangle+\langle Ru,u\rangle+2\langle q,x\rangle+\langle P(Cx+Du+\sigma),Cx+Du+\sigma\rangle,\end{array}

(3.16)

and introduce the following static optimization problem.

Problem (O).
Find
(x^{*},u^{*})\in\mathscr{V}
such that

F(x^{*},u^{*})=\min_{(x,u)\in\mathscr{V}}F(x,u).

(3.17)

For the above static optimization problem, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(H1)
–
(H2)
hold. Then Problem
(O)
admits a unique solution
(x^{*},u^{*})\in\mathscr{V}
which, together with a
Lagrange multiplier

\lambda^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is characterized by the following system of linear equations:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle Qx^{*}+A^{\top}\lambda^{*}+C^{\top}P(Cx^{*}+Du^{*}+\sigma)+q=0,\\
\displaystyle Ru^{*}+B^{\top}\lambda^{*}+D^{\top}P(Cx^{*}+Du^{*}+\sigma)=0.\end{array}\right.

(3.18)

Proof.

Since
[A,C;B,C]
is stabilizable, so must be
[A;B]\equiv[A,0;B,0]
. In fact, if
\varTheta
is a stabilizer of
[A,C;B,D]
, then there exists a
P\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
such that

P(A+B\varTheta)+(A+B\varTheta)^{\top}P+(C+D\varTheta)^{\top}P(C+D\varTheta)<0,

which leads

P(A+B\varTheta)+(A+B\varTheta)^{\top}P<0.

Thus,
[A;B]
is stabilizable which is equivalent to that the matrix
(A-\lambda I,B)
is of full rank for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\hbox{Re}\,\lambda\geqslant 0
(
[
11
]
). Consequently, by taking
\lambda=0
, one sees that the matrix
(A,B)
has rank
n
. Then the feasible set
\mathscr{V}
of Problem (O) is a non-empty closed convex set. Further, since
P>0
,

F(x,u)\geqslant\langle Qx,x\rangle+\langle Ru,u\rangle+2\langle q,x\rangle

is coercive on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
due to
(H2)
. Therefore, Problem (O) admits a unique solution.

Next, let

G(x,u)=Ax+Bu+b=(A,B)\begin{pmatrix}x\\
u\end{pmatrix}.

Then

G_{(x,u)}(x,u)=(A,B),

which is of full rank. Hence, the equality constraint is regular. Consequently, the optimal solution
(x^{*},u^{*})
can be obtained by the Lagrange multiplier method (
[
26
]
). Now, we form Lagrange function:

L(x,u,\lambda)=F(x,u)+2\lambda^{\top}(Ax+Bu+b).

Suppose
(x^{*},u^{*})
is the unique optimal solution of the above problem. Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0={1\over 2}L_{x}(x^{*},u^{*},\lambda^{*})^{\top}=(Q+C^{\top}PC)x^{*}+C^{\top}PDu^{*}+q+C^{\top}P\sigma+A^{\top}\lambda^{*},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0={1\over 2}L_{u}(x^{*},u^{*},\lambda^{*})^{\top}=(R+D^{\top}PD)u^{*}+D^{\top}PCx^{*}+D^{\top}P\sigma+B^{\top}\lambda^{*}.\end{array}

(3.19)

This leads to (
3.18
). Further, we may write the above as follows:

\begin{pmatrix}Q+C^{\top}PC&C^{\top}PD\\
D^{\top}PC&R+D^{\top}PD\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x^{*}\\
u^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}C^{\top}\\
D^{\top}\end{pmatrix}P\sigma+\begin{pmatrix}A^{\top}\\
B^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\lambda^{*}.

Since the coefficient matrix is invertible, there exists a unique solution
(x^{*},u^{*})
and

\begin{pmatrix}x^{*}\\
u^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Q+C^{\top}PC&C^{\top}PD\\
D^{\top}PC&R+D^{\top}PD\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\Big[\begin{pmatrix}C^{\top}\\
D^{\top}\end{pmatrix}P\sigma+\begin{pmatrix}A^{\top}\\
B^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\lambda^{*}\Big].

By the equality constraint, one has

-b=Ax^{*}+Bu^{*}=(A,B)\begin{pmatrix}Q+C^{\top}PC&C^{\top}PD\\
D^{\top}PC&R+D^{\top}PD\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\Big[\begin{pmatrix}C^{\top}\\
D^{\top}\end{pmatrix}P\sigma+\begin{pmatrix}A^{\top}\\
B^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\lambda^{*}\Big].

Since
(A,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n+m)}
has rank
n
, we see that

\lambda^{*}
is uniquely determined by the following:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\lambda^{*}=-\Big[(A,B)\begin{pmatrix}Q+C^{\top}PC&C^{\top}PD\\
D^{\top}PC&R+D^{\top}PD\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}A^{\top}\\
B^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\Big]^{-1}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\Big[b+(A,B)\begin{pmatrix}Q+C^{\top}PC&C^{\top}PD\\
D^{\top}PC&R+D^{\top}PD\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}C^{\top}\\
D^{\top}\end{pmatrix}P\sigma\Big].\end{array}

Hence,
(x^{*},u^{*})
is uniquely determined by (
3.18
). ∎

Now, we make some simple observation on problems (
1.8
) and Problem (O) (or (
1.9
)). Let

\mathscr{V}_{0}=\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\bigm|Ax+Bu+b=0,~Cx+Du+\sigma=0\}.

Then, it is clear that
\mathscr{V}_{0}
is a subset of
\mathscr{V}
, and
\mathscr{V}_{0}
could even be empty. Moreover, if
(x^{*},u^{*})\in\mathscr{V}
is an optimal solution of Problem (O) and
(x^{*},u^{*})\in\mathscr{V}_{0}
, then it is an optimal solution to problem (
1.8
). However, if Problem (O) has a unique solution
(x^{*},u^{*})\notin\mathscr{V}_{0}
(in particular, if
\mathscr{V}_{0}=\varnothing
), then problems (
1.8
) and Problem (O) are totally different. We present two illustrative examples below.

Example 3.7
.

Let
n=m=1
and consider the following state equation:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX(t)=u(t)dt+X(t)dW(t),\qquad t\geqslant 0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(0)=x,\end{array}\right.

with cost functional

J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(u(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(2|X(t)|^{2}+|u(t)|^{2}+4X(t)\Big)dt.

We see that in this case,

A=D=0,\quad B=C=1,\quad b=\sigma=0,\quad Q=2,\quad R=1,\quad q=2.

Then the ARE reads:

0=P+1-P^{2}=-(P-2)(P+1).

Hence the positive definite solution is
P=2
. Thus, Problem (O) is equivalent to

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hbox{Minimize}\quad 4x^{2}+u^{2}+4x,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hbox{Subject to}\quad u=0.\end{array}\right.

It is straightforward that the solution is given by

x^{*}=-{1\over 2},~u^{*}=0.

Whereas, (
1.8
) reads

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hbox{Minimize}\quad 2x^{2}+u^{2}+4x,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hbox{Subject to }~x=0,\quad u=0,\end{array}\right.

whose solution is trivially given by

\bar{x}^{*}=0,~\bar{u}^{*}=0.

Hence, the solutions to these two problems are different.

Example 3.8
.

Let
n=m=1
and consider the following state equation:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX(t)=\big[X(t)+u(t)+1\big]dt+\big[X(t)+u(t)\big]dW(t),\qquad t\geqslant 0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(0)=x,\end{array}\right.

with cost functional

J_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(u(\cdot))={1\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|X(t)|^{2}+|u(t)|^{2}\Big)dt.

We see that in this case,

A=B=C=D=Q=R=b=1,\quad\sigma=q=0.

Then

\mathscr{V}_{0}=\{(x,u)\bigm|x+u+1=0,x+u=0\}=\varnothing.

On the other hand, the ARE reads:

3P+1-{4P^{2}\over 1+P}=0,

which is equivalent to

P^{2}-4P-1=0.

Thus, the positive solution is
P=2+\sqrt{5}
. Hence,

F(x,u)=x^{2}+u^{2}+(2+\sqrt{5})|x+u|^{2}.

Consequently, Problem (O) is well-formulated and it admits a unique optimal solution.

From the above examples, together with our main result on the turnpike property of the Problem (SLQ)
{}_{{}_{T}}
which will be presented a little later, we see that (
1.8
) is not a suitable problem to be considered and the correct on is Problem (O).

An interesting further issue is how fast

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that the system
[A,C]
is
L^{2}
-stable and let
\beta
be the constant in (
3.5
).
Let
f:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a continuous function satisfying
f(t,0)=0
and

|f(t,M)-f(t,N)|\leqslant\rho|M-N|(|M|+|N|),\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,~\forall M,N\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},

for some constant
\rho>0
. Then for small initial state
\varPi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, the ODE

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{\varPi}(t)&=\varPi(t)A+A^{\top}\varPi(t)+C^{\top}\varPi(t)C+f(t,\varPi(t)),\quad t\in[0,\infty),\\
\varPi(0)&=\varPi_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

has a unique exponentially stable solution with decay rate
\beta
, i.e., for some constant
\delta>0
,

|\varPi(t)|\leqslant\delta e^{-\beta t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
be an undetermined constant, and consider the complete metric space (with respect to the uniform metric)

\mathscr{M}=\big\{M(\cdot)\in C([0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{n\times n});|M(t)|\leqslant\delta e^{-\beta t},~\forall t\geqslant 0\big\}.

Clearly, for each
M(\cdot)\in\mathscr{M}
and each initial state
\varPi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, the ODE

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\dot{\varPi}(t)=\varPi(t)A+A^{\top}\varPi(t)+C^{\top}\varPi(t)C+f(t,M(t)),\quad t\in[0,\infty),\\
\displaystyle\varPi(0)=\varPi_{0}\end{array}\right.

has a unique solution
\varPi(\cdot)\equiv\mathscr{T}[M(\cdot)]
.
Let
\varPhi(\cdot)
be the solution of (
3.4
).
By Itô’s rule, we have for
0\leqslant s\leqslant t<\infty
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle d\big[\varPhi(s)^{\top}\varPi(t-s)\varPhi(s)\big]=-\,\varPhi(s)^{\top}f(t-s,M(t-s))\varPhi(s)ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,\varPhi(s)^{\top}\big[C^{\top}\varPi(t-s)+\varPi(t-s)C\big]\varPhi(s)dW(s).\end{array}

Consequently,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\varPi(t-s)=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big[\varPhi(t)\varPhi(s)^{-1}\Big]^{\top}\varPi(0)\Big[\varPhi(t)\varPhi(s)^{-1}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{s}^{t}\Big[\varPhi(r)\varPhi(s)^{-1}\Big]^{\top}f(t-r,M(t-r))\Big[\varPhi(r)\varPhi(s)^{-1}\Big]dr\Big\}.\end{array}

Taking
s=0
yields

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\varPi(t)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\varPhi(t)^{\top}\varPi_{0}\varPhi(t)\Big]+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\varPhi(r)^{\top}f(t-r,M(t-r))\varPhi(r)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\varPhi(t)^{\top}\varPi_{0}\varPhi(t)\Big]+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\varPhi(t-s)^{\top}f(s,M(s))\varPhi(t-s)ds.\end{array}

(4.11)

It follows from (
3.5
) and the assumption on
f
that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|\varPi(t)|\leqslant|\varPi_{0}|\alpha e^{-\beta t}+\int_{0}^{t}\rho\alpha e^{-\beta(t-s)}|M(s)|^{2}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leqslant|\varPi_{0}|\alpha e^{-\beta t}+\int_{0}^{t}\rho\alpha\delta^{2}e^{-\beta(t+s)}ds\leqslant\alpha\left(|\varPi_{0}|+{\rho\delta^{2}\over\beta}\right)e^{-\beta t}.\end{array}

Take
\delta\in(0,{\beta\over 2\alpha\rho}]
. Then

\alpha\left({\delta\over 2\alpha}+{\rho\delta^{2}\over\beta}\right)\leqslant\delta.

Thus, for
|\varPi_{0}|\leqslant{\delta\over 2\alpha}
,
\mathscr{T}
maps
\mathscr{M}
into itself. Further, for any
M(\cdot),N(\cdot)\in\mathscr{M}
, we have from (
4.11
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|\mathscr{T}[M(\cdot)](t)-\mathscr{T}[N(\cdot)](t)|\leqslant\alpha\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-s)}|f(s,M(s))-f(s,N(s))|ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad~\leqslant\alpha\rho\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\beta(t-s)}|M(s)-N(s)|\Big(|M(s)|+|N(s)|\Big)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad~\leqslant 2\alpha\rho\delta\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\beta t}|M(s)-N(s)|ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad~\leqslant\Big(2\alpha\rho\delta te^{-\beta t}\Big)\sup_{s\geqslant 0}|M(s)-N(s)|.\end{array}

If we take
\delta>0
small enough so that also
2\alpha\rho\delta te^{-\beta t}\leqslant 1/2
for all
t\geqslant 0
, then
\mathscr{T}
is a contraction mapping on
\mathscr{M}
. The desired result therefore follows.
∎

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)=-{\cal A}^{\top}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-[B(\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\varTheta)]^{\top}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-[C+D\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]^{\top}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-P)\sigma^{*},\\
\displaystyle\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(T)=-\lambda^{*}.\end{array}\right.

(5.11)

By the variation of constants formula,

\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)=e^{{\cal A}^{\top}(T-t)}\Big[-\lambda^{*}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{{\cal A}^{\top}(s-T)}\rho(s)ds\Big],

(5.12)

where

\rho(s)=[B(\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-\varTheta)]^{\top}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)+[C+D\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]^{\top}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-P)\sigma^{*}.

Recall from the proof of
Theorem 4.4
that there exist constants
K,\lambda>0
,
independent of
T
, such that (
4.12
) and (
4.15
) hold,
and note that
\varSigma(T-t)=P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)
(

). We have

\displaystyle\big|e^{{\cal A}^{\top}t}\big|\leqslant Ke^{-\lambda t},\quad|P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-P|\leqslant Ke^{-{2\lambda}(T-t)},\quad\forall\,0\leqslant t\leqslant T<\infty,

(5.13)

where and hereafter,
K
represents a generic constant (independent of
T
)
which can be different from line to line,
but
\lambda
is the fixed constant in (
4.12
) and (
4.15
). Observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-\varTheta={\cal R}(P)^{-1}{\cal S}(P)-{\cal R}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s))^{-1}{\cal S}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad={\cal R}(P)^{-1}{\cal S}(P-P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s))+\Big[{\cal R}(P)^{-1}-{\cal R}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s))^{-1}\Big]{\cal S}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad={\cal R}(P)^{-1}{\cal S}(P-P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s))+{\cal R}(P)^{-1}D^{\top}[P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-P]D{\cal R}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s))^{-1}{\cal S}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)).\end{array}

Since
P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
, and hence
\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\cdot)
, is bounded uniformly in
T
, we have

|\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-\varTheta|\leqslant Ke^{-2\lambda(T-s)},\quad\forall\,0\leqslant s\leqslant T<\infty.

(5.14)

It follows that

|\rho(s)|\leqslant Ke^{-2\lambda(T-s)}\big[|\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)|+1\big].

If we let
h(t)=e^{\lambda(T-t)}|\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)|
, then by (

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle h(t)\leqslant K\Big[|\lambda^{*}|+\int_{t}^{T}Ke^{-\lambda(s-T)}|\rho(s)|ds\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K+K\int_{t}^{T}\big[e^{-\lambda(T-s)}|\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)|+e^{-\lambda(T-s)}\big]ds\\
\displaystyle=K+K\int_{t}^{T}\big[e^{-2\lambda(T-s)}h(s)+e^{-\lambda(T-s)}\big]ds.\end{array}

Applying Gronwall’s inequality we obtain that for some constant
K>0
, independent of
T>0
,

h(t)\leqslant K,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

The desired result then follows.
∎

Proof of
Theorem 5.1
.

For notational simplicity, we let

\widehat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)\triangleq-{\cal R}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t))^{-1}[B^{\top}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+D^{\top}(P_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-P)\sigma^{*}].

Substituting (

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\big\{[C+D\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+D\widehat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+\sigma^{*}\big\}dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(0)=x-x^{*}.\end{array}\right.

Using the notation (

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle d\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)=[{\cal A}\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+\xi(t)]dt+[{\cal C}\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+\eta(t)]dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(0)=x-x^{*}\equiv\widehat{x},\end{array}\right.

(5.15)

where

\xi(t)=B[\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\varTheta]\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+B\widehat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t),\quad\eta(t)=D[\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\varTheta]\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+D\widehat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)+\sigma^{*}.

Taking expectations in (

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]=\big\{{\cal A}\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]+B[\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)-\varTheta]\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]+B\widehat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)\big\}dt,\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(0)]=\widehat{x}.\end{array}\right.

(5.16)

Noting

P{\cal A}+{\cal A}^{\top}P+{\cal C}^{\top}P{\cal C}+\varTheta^{\top}R\varTheta+Q=0,

we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\langle P\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)],\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]\rangle-\langle P\widehat{x},\widehat{x}\rangle\negthinspace=\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{t}\negthinspace\negthinspace\big\{\negthinspace-\langle({\cal C}^{\top}P{\cal C}+\varTheta^{\top}R\varTheta+Q)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)],\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\langle P\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)],B[\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-\varTheta]\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]+B\widehat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)\rangle\big\}ds.\end{array}

Further, since
P>0
and
{\cal C}^{\top}P{\cal C}+\varTheta^{\top}R\varTheta+Q>0
, we have from the above that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}\leqslant\alpha_{1}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\Big[-\alpha_{2}|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]|^{2}+\alpha_{1}|\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-\varTheta|\,|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]|^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\alpha_{1}|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]|\cdot|\widehat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)|\Big]ds,\end{array}

for some constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
independent of
T
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we can obtain that with two possibly different constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0

|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}\negthinspace\leqslant\negthinspace\alpha_{1}\negthinspace+\negthinspace\mathbb{E}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{t}\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[-\alpha_{2}|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]|^{2}+\alpha_{1}|\varTheta_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)-\varTheta|\,|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]|^{2}+\alpha_{1}|\hat{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)|^{2}\Big]ds.

5.9

|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}\leqslant\alpha_{1}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\big(\alpha_{1}e^{-2\lambda(T-s)}-\alpha_{2}\big)|\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(s)]|^{2}+\alpha_{1}e^{-2\lambda(T-s)}\Big]ds.

For any

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\phi(s,t)\triangleq\exp\Big\{\int_{s}^{t}\big(\alpha_{1}e^{-2\lambda(T-r)}-\alpha_{2}\big)dr\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\exp\left\{{\alpha_{1}\over 2\lambda}\big[e^{-2\lambda(T-t)}-e^{-2\lambda(T-s)}\big]-\alpha_{2}(t-s)\right\}\leqslant e^{\alpha_{1}\over 2\lambda}e^{-\alpha_{2}(t-s)},\end{array}

and thus

\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s,t)e^{-2\lambda(T-s)}ds\leqslant e^{\alpha_{1}\over 2\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\lambda(T-s)}ds\leqslant{1\over 2\lambda}e^{\alpha_{1}\over 2\lambda}e^{-2\lambda(T-t)}.

Hence, by Gronwall’s inequality,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(t)]|^{2}\leqslant\alpha_{1}\phi(0,t)+\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s,t)e^{-2\lambda(T-s)}ds\\

6
Concluding Remarks

For linear quadratic stochastic optimal control problems in finite horizons, we have established the turnpike property under natural conditions of stabiizability of the controlled linear SDE, and the strong standard condition of the quadratic cost functional. The crucial contribution of the current paper is to find the correct form of the corresponding static optimization problem in which the diffusion part of the state equation should be getting into the cost functional, rather than taking it to be as an additional equality constraint. Such an idea should have big impact on the study of turnpike type problems for general stochastic optimal control problems. We will report some further results along this line in our future publications.

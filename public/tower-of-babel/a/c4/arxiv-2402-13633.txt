Rings whose subrings are all Noetherian or Artinian
Nathan Blacher

Abstract
We study noncommutative rings whose proper subrings all satisfy the same chain condition. We show that if every proper subring of a ring R R is right Noetherian, then R R is either right Noetherian or the trivial extension of ℤ \mathbb{Z} by the Prüfer p p -group for a prime p p . We also prove that if every proper subring of R R is right Artinian, then R R is either right Artinian or ℤ \mathbb{Z} . For commutative rings, both results were proved by Gilmer and Heinzer in 1992. Our result for right Artinian subrings only generalises the absolute case of their commutative result. We generalise the full result (when only certain subrings are right Artinian) in the context of PI rings.



1
Introduction and Notation

Subrings naturally inherit some ring-theoretic properties, such as being a domain, the ring’s characteristic and satisfying a given polynomial identity. Chain conditions however can differ widely between a ring and its subrings. For instance a field can contain a subring which is not Noetherian. In
[
1
]
, Gilmer and Heinzer characterised commutative Noetherian rings whose subrings are all Noetherian. In
[
2
]
, they showed that a commutative ring whose proper subrings are all Noetherian is either Noetherian or isomorphic to the trivial extension of
\mathbb{Z}
by the Prüfer
p
-group for a prime
p
. In the same paper they prove that a commutative ring whose proper subrings are all Artinian is either Artinian or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. We generalise both latter results to all rings, without the commutative hypothesis.

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be a ring which is not right Noetherian. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
is right Noetherian. Then
R
is the trivial extension of
\mathbb{Z}
by the Prüfer
p
-group
\mathbb{Z}\left(p^{\infty}\right)
for some prime
p
.

The proof of this theorem relies on the commutative version and follows the strategy of
[
2
]
, with the necessary changes to adapt it to the noncommutative setting.

Theorem 2
.

Let
R
be a ring which is not right Artinian. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
is right Artinian. Then
R
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.

In the commutative case, the authors of
[
2
]
proved the stronger result that if
C
is a proper subring of
R
such that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is Artinian, then
R
is Artinian. They did this by using classical Krull dimension. In particular they showed that the dimension of a commutative ring cannot strictly exceed that of all its subrings. This approach does not work in the noncommutative setting as classical Krull dimension is not as well behaved there. However in the context of rings with polynomial identity, we recover the zero-dimensional case of this result, in the form of Proposition
4.2
. This allows us to recover the stronger result for PI rings.

Theorem 3
.

Let
R
be a PI ring and
C
a central proper subring of
R
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Artinian. Then
R
is right Artinian.

In
[
3
]
, Gilmer and O’Malley considered (possibly noncommutative) rings without 1 whose subrings are all right Noetherian. They showed that such a ring is either right Noetherian or the zero ring on a Prüfer
p
-group. Of course in this context, the condition on subrings is much stronger than in ours, as for instance every one-sided ideal is a subring in a ring without 1.

All rings considered in this paper are associative with
1
. Subrings of a given ring
R
must contain the identity element of
R
. We denote inclusion by
\subset
and strict inclusion by
\subsetneq
.

For a prime number
p
, the
Prüfer
p
-group
is
\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}
with usual addition, denoted by
\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})
. It is an infinite group whose proper subgroups are all finite. More precisely, every proper subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})
is cyclic and generated by
1/p^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.

For a commutative ring
C
and a
C
-module
M
, the
trivial extension
of
C
by
M
is the ring whose additive group is the direct sum
C\oplus M
, equipped with the multiplication
(c_{1},m_{1})(c_{2},m_{2})=(c_{1}c_{2},c_{1}m_{2}+c_{2}m_{1})
. This construction can be found in
[
4
, Example 2.22(A)]
. It is sometimes called Nagata’s idealisation in the literature.

Following
[
5
, Chapter 6]
, we call
classical Krull dimension
of a ring
R
the maximal length of a chain of prime ideals in
R
. We denote it by
\Cl.K.dim(R)
. For a right
R
-module
M
, the
Krull dimension
of
M
is the deviation of the lattice of submodules of
M
. We denote it by
\K(M)
.

2
Rings whose proper Subrings are right Noetherian

The question we are considering was solved in the commutative case by Gilmer and Heinzer with the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
2
, Corollary 4]

Let
R
be a commutative ring which is not Noetherian. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
is Noetherian. Then
R
is the trivial extension of
\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})
for some prime
p
.

We generalise this result to noncommutative rings. Our proof relies on Theorem
2.1
and essentially follows the strategy of
[
2
]
, though some changes are needed to adapt it to the noncommutative world. We first need a result which is essentially a reformulation of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
R
be a ring and
I\lhd_{r}R
a right ideal of
R
that is not finitely generated. If every proper submodule of
I_{R}
is finitely generated, then
I^{2}=0
.

Proof.

Take any element
x
in
I
. If
xI=I
then
I=xR
is finitely generated, contradicting our hypothesis. So the right ideal
xI
is properly contained in
I
. Thus
xI
is finitely generated and there are
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}
in
I
such that
xI=xi_{1}R+\ldots+xi_{n}R
. Taking any
r
in
I
, we have
xr=xi_{1}r_{1}+\ldots+xi_{n}r_{n}
for some
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
in
R
. Thus
x(r-i_{1}r_{1}-\ldots-i_{n}r_{n})=0
i.e.
r-i_{1}r_{1}-\ldots-i_{n}r_{n}
belongs to the right annihilator of
x
in
I
, which we call
A
. In particular,
r
belongs to
A+i_{1}R+\ldots+i_{n}R
, and since
r
was arbitrary in
I
, we have
I=A+i_{1}R+\ldots+i_{n}R
. Since
I_{R}
is not finitely generated,
A
cannot be finitely generated either and it follows that
A=I
, that is
xI=0
. As
x
was arbitrary in
I
, this implies that
I^{2}=0
.
∎

We start by studying pairs
C\subsetneq R
, where
C
is a central proper subring of a ring
R
, and every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Noetherian. Theorem
1
will then follow by taking
C
to be the minimal subring of
R
. In the commutative case, Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
and Propositions
2.6
and
2.7
are statements of
[
2
, Theorem 3]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
R
be a ring and
C
a central proper subring of
R
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Noetherian. If a right ideal
I
of
R
is not finitely generated, then
I^{2}
is contained in
(I\cap C)R
. If moreover
I^{2}=0
then
R
is commutative.

Proof.

Since
C
is Noetherian, its ideal
I\cap C
is finitely generated, and thus so is the ideal
I^{\prime}:=(I\cap C)R
of
R
. As
I_{R}
is not finitely generated then neither is
I/I^{\prime}
as a right
R
-module, nor as a right ideal of
R/I^{\prime}
. Therefore the ring
R/I^{\prime}
is not right Noetherian, but each of its proper subrings containing
(C+I^{\prime})/I^{\prime}
is right Noetherian.

Now, since
I_{R}
is not finitely generated, neither is
I
as a right ideal of
C+I
. Thus the ring
C+I
is not right Noetherian and it follows that
C+I=R
. Therefore as Abelian groups we have

R/I^{\prime}=(C+I^{\prime})/I^{\prime}\oplus I/I^{\prime}.

Moreover, for any right ideal
A
of
R/I^{\prime}
properly contained in
I/I^{\prime}
, we have that
(C+I^{\prime})/I^{\prime}\oplus A
is a proper subring of
R/I^{\prime}
. Thus
(C+I^{\prime})/I^{\prime}\oplus A
is right Noetherian and it follows that
A
is finitely generated as a right
R/I^{\prime}
-module. Thus the right ideal
I/I^{\prime}
satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
2.2
and we have
\left(I/I^{\prime}\right)^{2}=0
, i.e.
I^{2}\subset I^{\prime}
as required.

Now since
R=C+I
, its commutator satisfies
[R,R]=[C+I,C+I]=[I,I]\subset I^{2}
using that
C
is central. Thus if
I^{2}=0
, then
R
is commutative as required.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a ring and
C
a central proper subring of
R
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Noetherian. Then
R
has ACC on semiprime ideals. In particular, for any ideal
A\lhd R
, there are only finitely many prime ideals minimal with respect to containing
A
.

Proof.

Let
I_{1}\subsetneq I_{2}\subsetneq\ldots
be a chain of semiprime ideals and let
I
denote the union
\bigcup_{j\geq 1}I_{j}
. Since
C
is Noetherian,
(I\cap C)R
is finitely generated as a right ideal of
R
and thus there exists a
k\geq 1
such that
I_{k}
contains
(I\cap C)R
. If the chain does not terminate, then
I_{R}
is not finitely generated and by Lemma
2.3
,
I^{2}
is contained in
(I\cap C)R
. Thus
I^{2}
is contained in
I_{k}
, which is only possible when
I_{k}=I
, as
I_{k}\subset I
and
I_{k}
is semiprime. For the last statement, see for instance
[
6
, Proposition 4.4.13]
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
R
be a ring and
C
a central proper subring of
R
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Noetherian. If
C
is Artinian then
R
is right Noetherian.

Proof.

We assume that
R
is not right Noetherian and we seek a contradiction. In the commutative case, this result is a consequence of
[
2
, Corollary 3]
so it is enough to show that
R
is commutative. Let
I\lhd_{r}R
be a right ideal of
R
that is not finitely generated. Take a strictly descending chain of right ideals
I=I_{0}\supsetneq I_{1}\supsetneq\ldots
such that no
I_{j}
is finitely generated. We aim to show the chain must terminate.

First, for any
j\geq 0
such that
I_{j+1}
exists, since the right ideal
I_{j+1}
is not finitely generated, the ring
C+I_{j+1}
is not right Noetherian and hence
C+I_{j+1}=R
. Thus we have
I_{j+1}\cap C\subsetneq I_{j}\cap C
. Therefore the chain
I_{0}\supsetneq I_{1}\supsetneq\ldots
yields a chain
I_{0}\cap C\supsetneq I_{1}\cap C\supsetneq\ldots
of ideals of
C
, which must terminate as
C
is Artinian. So the original chain terminates too and there is
k\geq 0
such that the right ideal
I_{k}
is not finitely generated, but all its proper submodules are. By Proposition
2.2
, it follows that
(I_{k})^{2}=0
. Therefore
R
is commutative by Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
R
be a ring and
C
a central proper subring of
R
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Noetherian. If
R
is prime then it is right Noetherian.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a right ideal
I\lhd_{r}R
that is not finitely generated. We seek a contradiction. By Lemma
2.3
we have
I^{2}\subset(I\cap C)R
. In particular, if
I^{2}\neq 0
then
I
intersects
C
nontrivially. On the other hand if
I^{2}=0
, we know that
R
is commutative. In any case we can take a nonzero central element
x
in
I
.

If the right ideal
xI
is not finitely generated then
R=C+xI
. So for any
y
in
I
, we have
y=c+xz
for some
c
in
C
,
z
in
I
. In particular
c=y-xz
belongs to
I
and since
y
was arbitrary in
I
it follows that

I\subset I\cap C+I^{2}\subset(I\cap C)R\subset I,\text{ so }I=(I\cap C)R.

As
C
is Noetherian,
(I\cap C)R=I
is finitely generated, contradicting our choice of
I
.

So
xI
is finitely generated, and we write
xI=xi_{1}R+\ldots+xi_{n}R
for some
n\geq 1
and
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}
in
I
. Then for any
r
in
I
we have elements
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
of
R
such that
xr=xi_{1}r_{1}+\ldots+xi_{n}r_{n}
. It follows that

0=Rx(r-i_{1}r_{1}-\ldots-i_{n}r_{n})=xR(r-i_{1}r_{1}-\ldots-i_{n}r_{n})

using that
x
is central in
R
. Since
R
is prime and
x
nonzero, this implies that
r=i_{1}r_{1}+\ldots+i_{n}r_{n}
. As
r
was arbitrary in
I
, we have
I=i_{1}R+\ldots+i_{n}R
, contradicting our choice of
I
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
R
be a ring and
C
a central proper subring of
R
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Noetherian. If
R
is not right Noetherian then the prime radical
N
of
R
is not Noetherian as a right
R
-module and
R=C+N
. If moreover
C
is reduced then
R
is the trivial extension of
C
by
N
.

Proof.

Let
N
be the prime radical of
R
, that is
N=\bigcap_{P\in\Spec(R)}P
. First we assume that
N_{R}
is Noetherian and seek a contradiction. Since
N_{R}
is Noetherian and
R
is not right Noetherian, it follows that the ring
R/N
is also not right Noetherian. Now, by Lemma
2.4
, there are only finitely many minimal primes in
R
, that is
N=P_{1}\cap\ldots\cap P_{m}
. This yields a natural inclusion of
R
-modules
R/N\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}R/P_{i}
, and since
R/N
is not right Noetherian, then there is at least one index
1\leq j\leq m
such that
R/P_{j}
is not right Noetherian.

So
R/P_{j}
is a prime ring which is not right Noetherian. Its subring
(C+P_{j})/P_{j}
is Noetherian and hence proper. Moreover, every proper subring of
R/P_{j}
containing
(C+P_{j})/P_{j}
is right Noetherian. This is impossible by Proposition
2.6
, and it follows that
N_{R}
is not right Noetherian. In particular, the ring
C+N
is not right Noetherian and therefore
C+N=R
.

Now in the case where
C
is reduced, we obtain
C\cap N=0
as
N
is nil (see for instance
[
5
, Theorem 0.2.6]
). Thus
R=C\oplus N
and it remains to show that
N^{2}=0
. If
M
is a proper submodule of
N_{R}
, then the proper subring
C\oplus M
of
R
is right Noetherian, and
M_{R}
is finitely generated. Thus, by Proposition
2.2
,
N^{2}=0
as required.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
1
, that is we generalise Theorem
2.1
to the noncommutative setting.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Since
R
is not right Noetherian, it must have characteristic zero by Proposition
2.5
, taking
C
to be the minimal subring of
R
. But
\mathbb{Z}
is reduced so
R
is commutative by Proposition
2.7
. We are done by Theorem
2.1
.
∎

3
Rings whose proper Subrings are right Artinian

The question was also answered for the Artinian property by Gilmer and Heinzer, with the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
2
, Theorem 2]

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
C
a proper subring of
R
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is Artinian. Then
R
is Artinian.

Taking
C
to be the minimal subring of
R
immediately yields the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

[
2
, Corollary 2]

Let
R
be a commutative ring which is not Artinian. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
is Artinian. Then
R
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.

In the noncommutative context, we start by noting that the Noetherian case yields the following immediate consequence.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that every proper subring of a ring
R
is right Artinian. Then
R
is right Noetherian.

Proof.

Every proper subring of
R
is right Artinian, and hence right Noetherian. By Theorem
1
,
R
is either right Noetherian or the trivial extension of
\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathbb{Z}\left(p^{\infty}\right)
for some prime
p
. But this trivial extension strictly contains
\mathbb{Z}
as a subring, which is not Artinian. It follows that
R
is right Noetherian.
∎

We now prove Theorem
2
, that is we generalise Corollary
3.2
to noncommutative rings.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We assume that
R
is not
\mathbb{Z}
and seek a contradiction. If the characteristic of
R
is zero, then
R
contains a copy of
\mathbb{Z}
as a proper subring, which contradicts our hypothesis. Therefore
R
has finite characteristic. By Lemma
3.3
,
R
is right Noetherian. Take an ideal
I
of
R
maximal with respect to
R/I
not being right Artinian. Then
R/I
satisfies the hypothesis of the theorem. So without loss of generality we can take
R
to be
R/I
. Furthermore, every proper homomorphic image of
R/I
is right Artinian. We will show this yields a contradiction.

We first show that
R
has prime characteristic. Write
p_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots p_{n}^{l_{n}}
for the prime factorisation of the characteristic of
R
. We have a ring isomorphism between the minimal subring
\mathbb{Z}/(p_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots p_{n}^{l_{n}})\mathbb{Z}
and
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{l_{i}}\mathbb{Z}
. The preimage of
(1,0,\ldots,0)
under this isomorphism gives us a nonzero central idempotent
e
in
R
. Thus
R=Re\oplus R(1-e)
as a direct sum of ideals. If
e\neq 1
, then both
R/Re\cong R(1-e)
and
R/R(1-e)\cong Re
are proper homomorphic images of
R
, and hence Artinian as right
R
-modules. In this case,
R_{R}
is a direct sum of Artinian modules and is hence right Artinian as a ring, contradicting our hypothesis. Therefore
e=1
, that is the characteristic of
R
is a prime power
p^{l}
.

Now consider the minimal subring
\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}
of
R
. If
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}+pR
then
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}+p(\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}+pR)=\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}+p^{2}R
and repeating the substitution yields
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}+p^{l}R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}
, which is impossible as
R
is not right Artinian. Thus
\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}+pR
is a proper subring of
R
. In particular it is right Artinian. Therefore the right
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}+pR)
-module
pR
is Artinian, and it follows that
pR
is Artinian as a right
R
-module. The short exact sequence

0\to pR\to R\to R/pR\to 0

then implies that
R/pR
is not right Artinian. This is only possible if
R/pR
is not a proper homomorphic image of
R
, i.e. if
pR=0
. Therefore the characteristic of
R
is
p
.

Now,
R
is an
F_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-algebra which is not right Artinian and such
that every proper subring is right Artinian. Take an infinite descending chain
R\supsetneq A_{1}\supsetneq A_{2}\supsetneq\ldots
of right ideals of
R
. The right
R
-module
A_{2}
is not Artinian, so the ring
F_{p}+A_{2}

is not right Artinian, hence
F_{p}+A_{2}=R
. Take
x
in
A_{1}\setminus A_{2}
. Then
x=u+a
for some
a
in

A_{2}
and nonzero
u
in
F_{p}
. But
u=x-a
belongs to
A_{1}
, which is impossible as
F_{p}
is a field
and
A_{1}
is a proper ideal. This is the required contradiction, and it follows that
R
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

The proof of
[
2
, Theorem 2]
uses classical Krull dimension. It is not as well behaved in the general noncommutative setting, and our proof of Theorem
2
followed a different approach. In the next section, we recover some of the results of
[
2
]
in the context of rings satisfying a polynomial identity (PI).

4
Classical Krull Dimension 0 in PI rings

Definition 4.1
.

[
6
, Definition 1.7.27]

Let
R
be a ring, and
W
a subring of
R
. Call
R
an
extension
of
W
if
R=W\C_{R}(W)
, where
\C_{R}(W)
is the centraliser of
W
in
R
, i.e. elements of
R
that commute with
W
.

Of course, every ring is an extension of any of its central subrings. We will use the following fact repeatedly.

Remark
.

[
6
, Remark 1.9.5]

Let
R
be an extension of a subring
W
. If
P
is a prime ideal of
R
, then
W\cap P
is a prime ideal of
W
.

The following is a generalisation to PI rings of the case
n=0
of
[
2
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
R
be a PI ring that is an extension of a proper subring
W
. Suppose that every proper subring of
R
affine over
W
has classical Krull dimension zero. Then the classical Krull dimension of
R
is also zero.

Proof.

Let us assume that there are prime ideals
P_{0}\subsetneq P_{1}
in
R
. We seek a contradiction. Since
R/P_{0}
is a prime PI ring, the nontrivial ideal
P_{1}/P_{0}
contains a nonzero central element,
\bar{c}
say (see for instance
[
6
, Theorem 1.6.27]
). Now,
R
is an extension of
W
so
R/P_{0}
is an extension of
W^{\prime}:=(W+P_{0})/P_{0}
. Moreover since
\bar{c}
is central,
R/P_{0}
also is an extension of
W^{\prime}[\bar{c}]
. The ring
W^{\prime}[\bar{c}]
is prime and its prime ideal
P_{1}^{\prime}:=P_{1}\cap W^{\prime}[\bar{c}]
is nonzero as it contains
\bar{c}\neq 0
. Thus the classical Krull dimension of
W^{\prime}[\bar{c}]
is at least one.

Taking
c
in the preimage of
\bar{c}
under the natural projection
R\twoheadrightarrow R/P_{0}
, we have
\Cl.K.dimW[c]\geq\Cl.K.dimW^{\prime}[\bar{c}]\geq 1
. It follows by hypothesis that
W[c]=R
. We can show similarly that
W[c^{2}]=R
. Therefore
c
is algebraic over
W
and in particular
\bar{c}
is algebraic over
W^{\prime}
. Let
l\geq 1
be minimal such that there exist
w_{0},\ldots,w_{l}\in W^{\prime}
not all zero and satisfying
\sum_{i=0}^{l}w_{i}\bar{c}^{i}=0
. Then
-w_{0}=\bar{c}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}w_{i+1}\bar{c}^{i}\right)
belongs to
P_{1}/P_{0}
, as
\bar{c}
does. Moreover, since
\bar{c}
is a central element in the prime ring
R/P_{0}
, it must be regular. Then, by minimality of
l
, it follows that
w_{0}
is not zero and thus the prime ideal
W^{\prime}\cap(P_{1}/P_{0})
is nontrivial. Therefore

1\leq\Cl.K.dimW^{\prime}=\Cl.K.dimW/(W\cap P_{0})\leq\Cl.K.dimW=0

which is absurd, showing that the classical Krull dimension of
R
is zero.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
R
be a PI ring with classical Krull dimension zero. The following are equivalent:

(i)

R
is right Artinian;

(ii)

the right Krull dimension of
R
exists;

(iii)

every ideal of
R
is finitely generated as a right
R
-module.

Proof.

That (i) implies (ii) and (iii) is trivial. Now if
R
has right Krull dimension
\K(R_{R})
, then
\K(R_{R})=\sup\{\K(R/P):P\in\Spec(R)\}
(see for instance
[
5
, Corollary 6.3.8]
). This is zero as every prime ideal of
R
is maximal, and the simple PI rings
R/P
are Artinian by Kaplansky’s Theorem. Thus (ii) implies (i).

Finally, we prove that (iii) implies (i). We assume that
R
is not right Artinian, and seek a contradiction. Since
R
has ACC on ideals, we can take an ideal
P
of
R
maximal with respect to
R/P
not being right Artinian. Let
A,B
be ideals strictly containing
P
. Since
A_{R}
is finitely generated, it follows that
A/AB
is finitely generated as a right module over
R/B
. As
R/B
is right Artinian, this implies that
A/AB
is Artinian as a right
R
-module. Thus, the short exact sequence of right
R
-modules
A/AB\hookrightarrow R/AB\twoheadrightarrow R/A
implies that the ring
R/AB
is right Artinian. Therefore
AB
is not contained in
P
, and we have shown that
P
is prime. The classical Krull dimension of
R
is zero so
P
is maximal, and
R/P
is a simple PI ring, which is Artinian by Kaplansky’s Theorem, contradicting our choice of
P
.
∎

Lastly, we prove Theorem
3
, i.e. we generalise Theorem
3.1
to PI rings.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Every proper subring of
R
containing
C
is right Artinian and hence has classical Krull dimension zero. Since
R
is an extension of
C
, this implies that the classical Krull dimension of
R
is also zero by Proposition
4.2
. By Proposition
2.5
,
R
is right Noetherian. Thus
R
satisfies condition (iii) of Proposition
4.3
and it follows that
R
is right Artinian.
∎

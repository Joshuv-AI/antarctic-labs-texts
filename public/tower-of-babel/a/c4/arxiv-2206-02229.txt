Abstract
In present work we study the production of ground and excited charmonium states in e − ​ e + → γ ​ χ c ​ J ​ ( n ​ P ) ​ ( J = 0 , 1 ) e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow\gamma\chi_{cJ}(nP)(J=0,1) , and e − ​ e + → γ ​ η c ​ ( n ​ S ) e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow\gamma\eta_{c}(nS) , through leading order (LO) diagrams, which proceed through exchange of a virtual photon that couples to γ \gamma and η c / χ c ​ J \eta_{c}/\chi_{cJ} through the triangular quark loop diagram, in the framework of 4 × 4 4\times 4 Bethe-Salpeter equation (BSE), at center of mass energies s = 10.6 \sqrt{s}=10.6 GeV (Belle energy), and 4.6 4.6 GeV (BESIII energy). The amplitude simplifies to a general form required by Lorentz-covariance, in terms of its form factors. The cross sections for these processes with leading order diagrams alone at s = 10.6 \sqrt{s}=10.6 GeV provide a sizable contribution, which might be mainly due to the BSE being a fully relativistic approach that incorporates the relativistic effect of quark spins and can also describe internal motion of constituent quarks within the hadron in a relativistically covariant manner. Our results are compared with recent Belle data at 10.6GeV, and BESIII data at 4.6GeV, as well as other models. Plots of cross sections versus the center-of mass energy, s \sqrt{s} reveal mild fluctuations in their behaviour for all the three processes in the low energy ( s \sqrt{s} = 4 - 6 GeV) region, and are analyzed in terms of the form factors.

2.
Bethe-Salpeter equation for
Q\bar{Q}
bound state

The Bethe-Salpeter equation (BSE) for a meson (a
q\bar{q}
bound state) can be written as,

S_{F}^{-1}(p_{1})\Psi(P,q)S_{F}^{-1}(-p_{2})=i\int\frac{d^{4}q^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{4}}K(q,q^{\prime})\Psi(P,q^{\prime}),

(1)

with
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
being the momenta of the two particles of massses
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
respectively.
P
is the external momentum of the hadron of mass,
M
, while
q
is its internal momentum. In Eq.(1),
K(q,q^{\prime})
is the interaction kernel, and
S_{F}^{-1}(\pm p_{1,2})=\pm i{\not}p_{1,2}+m_{1,2}
are the inverse propagators for the quark and antiquark.

To reduce the above equation to 3D form, we make use of the Covariant Instantaneous Ansatz
[
24
]
on the BS kernel,
K(q,q^{\prime})
, where we can write

K(q,q^{\prime})=K(\widehat{q},\widehat{q}^{\prime})
, where the BS kernel depends entirely on the component of
internal momentum of the hadron,
\widehat{q}_{\mu}=q_{\mu}-\frac{q.P}{P^{2}}P_{\mu}
, which is a 3D variable, and is orthogonal to the total
hadron momentum, i.e.
\widehat{q}.P=0
, while
\sigma P_{\mu}=\frac{q.P}{P^{2}}P_{\mu}
is the component of
q
that is longitudinal
to
P
. And the 4-dimensional volume element is,

d^{4}q=d^{3}\widehat{q}Md\sigma
.

The 3D BS wave function,
\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime})
is obtained by integrating the 4D BS wave function,
\Psi(P,q^{\prime})
over the longitudinal component,
Md\sigma^{\prime}
of the four dimensional volume element,
d^{4}q^{\prime}
as,

\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int Md\sigma^{\prime}\Psi(P,q^{\prime}),

(2)

Now, it is to be observed that the longitudinal component
M\sigma^{\prime}
of
q^{\prime}
does not appear in
K(\hat{q},\hat{q}^{\prime})
. We thus carry out integration over the longitudinal component
Md\sigma
of the four-dimensional volume element
d^{4}q
on right hand side of Eq.(1), and making use of the previous equation, we get,

S_{F}^{-1}(p_{1})\Psi(P,q)S_{F}^{-1}(p_{2})=\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}K(\widehat{q},\widehat{q}^{\prime})\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime})=\Gamma(\hat{q}).

(3)

Thus, we can identify the 4D hadron-quark vertex function,
\Gamma(\hat{q})
, which is the amputated 3-point function, and is directly related to the 4D BS wave function,
\Psi(P,q)
, which is a 3-point function.
\Psi(P,q)
is obtained by sandwiching the hadron-quark vertex function,
\Gamma(\hat{q})
between the two quark propagators as (see
[
25
]
and references therein),

\displaystyle\Psi(P,q)=S_{1}(p_{1})\Gamma(\hat{q})S_{2}(-p_{2}),

\displaystyle\Gamma(\hat{q})=\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{q}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}K(\hat{q},\hat{q}^{\prime})\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime}).

(4)

We wish to point out that the 4D BS wave function,
\Psi(P,q)
is analogous to the quark bilinear 3-point correlation function used in a recent lattice calculation
[
29
]
of nonperturbative structure of vector and axial vector vertices. Here
\Gamma(\hat{q})
is used for calculation of transition amplitudes of various processes.

A series of steps then leads to four Salpeter equations, which are the effective 3D forms of BSE
[
25
]
:

\displaystyle(M-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\psi^{++}(\hat{q})=\Lambda_{1}^{+}(\hat{q})\Gamma(\hat{q})\Lambda_{2}^{+}(\hat{q})

\displaystyle(M+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\psi^{--}(\hat{q})=-\Lambda_{1}^{-}(\hat{q})\Gamma(\hat{q})\Lambda_{2}^{-}(\hat{q})

\displaystyle\psi^{+-}(\hat{q})=0.

\displaystyle\psi^{-+}(\hat{q})=0

(5)

where,
\omega_{1,2}^{2}=m_{1,2}^{2}+\hat{q}^{2}
, and
\Lambda_{i}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2\omega_{i}}[\frac{{\not}P}{M}\omega_{i}^{\prime}\pm I(i)(im_{i}+\hat{q})]
(where i=1,2, and
I(i)=(-1)^{i+1}
) are the projection operators

]
corresponding to constituents
\#
1 and

M
, and external momentum,
P

\pm\pm
components from the hadronic wave function,

as,
\psi^{\pm\pm}(\hat{q})=\Lambda_{1}^{\pm}(\hat{q})\frac{{\not}P}{M}\psi(\hat{q})\frac{{\not}P}{M}\Lambda_{2}^{\pm}(\hat{q})
. The sum of projection operators satisfies the relation,
\Lambda_{i}^{+}+\Lambda_{i}^{-}=\frac{{\not}P}{M}
. And the relation
\frac{{\not}P}{M}\frac{{\not}P}{M}=-1

\frac{{\not}P}{M}(\Lambda_{i}^{+}+\Lambda_{i}^{-})=-1

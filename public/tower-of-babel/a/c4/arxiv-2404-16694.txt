A non-separable progressive multivariate WENO- 2 ​ r 2r point value
Pep Mulet, Juan Ruiz-Álvarez, Chi-Wang Shu, Dionisio F. Yáñez

Abstract
The weighted essentially non-oscillatory technique using a stencil of 2 ​ r 2r points (WENO- 2 ​ r 2r ) is an interpolatory method that consists in obtaining a higher approximation order from the non-linear combination of interpolants of r + 1 r+1 nodes. The result is an interpolant of order 2 ​ r 2r at the smooth parts and order r + 1 r+1 when an isolated discontinuity falls at any grid interval of the large stencil except at the central one. Recently, a new WENO method based on Aitken-Neville’s algorithm has been designed for interpolation of equally spaced data at the mid-points and presents progressive order of accuracy close to discontinuities. This paper is devoted to constructing a general progressive WENO method for non-necessarily uniformly spaced data and several variables interpolating in any point of the central interval. Also, we provide explicit formulas for linear and non-linear weights and prove the order obtained. Finally, some numerical experiments are presented to check the theoretical results.

Introduction and review

In the last years, WENO methods have been developed and used in several
applications, mainly to obtain numerical solutions of partial
differential equations (PDEs) but also in other fields, such as image processing or
computer-aided design (see e.g.
[
6
,
14
,
15
]
). The idea is to
compute a non-linear combination of interpolations through polynomials of
degree
r
, aiming to obtain the maximum possible order
2r
when the data is
free of discontinuities, and order
r+1
at the non-smooth parts. In what follows, we
briefly review the method.
Let us denote as
X
a uniform partition of the interval

[a,b]
in
J
subintervals:

X=\{x_{i}\}^{J}_{i=0},\quad x_{i}=a+i\cdot h,\quad h=\frac{b-a}{J},

and consider the point-value discretization of a piecewise smooth function
f
at the nodes
x_{i}
,

f_{i}=f(x_{i}),\,i=0,\ldots,J,\quad f=\left\{f_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{J}.

In this setting, the WENO method with
2r
nodes interpolates at the mid-point of the interval
(x_{i-1},x_{i})
, denoted by
x_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
, using the stencil
\mathcal{S}^{2r}_{0}=\{x_{i-r},\cdots,x_{i+r-1}\}
. We construct this interpolation by means of the following convex combination:

\mathcal{I}^{2r-1}(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}};f)=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\omega_{k}^{r}p_{k}^{r}(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}}),

(1)

where
\omega_{k}^{r}\geq 0,\,k=0,\cdots,r-1
are non-linear
(data-dependent) positive weights such that

\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\omega_{k}^{r}=1
, and
p_{k}^{r}
are the Lagrange interpolants with nodes
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{k}=\{x_{i-r+k},\cdots,x_{i+k}\}
.

The values of the weights
\omega_{k}^{r}
are designed to obtain an order of
accuracy
2r
at
x_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
when the function is smooth in
the large stencil, as follows: There are optimal weights
C_{k}^{r}\geq 0
, with
k=0,\ldots,r-1
that satisfy the following equality:

p_{0}^{2r-1}\left(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}{C}_{k}^{r}p_{k}^{r}\left(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

A formula for these values is obtained in
[
6
]
:

{C}_{k}^{r}=\frac{1}{2^{2r-1}}\binom{2r}{2k+1},\quad k=0,\cdots,r-1.

(2)

As stated in
[
15
]
, the weights
\omega_{k}^{r}
satisfy

\omega_{k}^{r}={C}_{k}^{r}+O(h^{{\kappa}}),\quad k=0,\cdots,r-1,

(3)

at smooth zones, with
\kappa\geq r-1
, thus assuring that the interpolation in (
1
) attains order of accuracy
2r

f(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}})-\mathcal{I}^{2r-1}\left(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}};f\right)=O(h^{2r}).

In
[
12
,
15
]
, (
3
) is achieved via the following expressions:

\omega_{k}^{r}=\frac{\alpha_{k}^{r}}{\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\alpha_{j}^{r}},\,\,\textrm{ where }\alpha_{k}^{r}=\frac{{C}_{k}^{r}}{(\epsilon+I_{k}^{r})^{t}},\quad k=0,\cdots,r-1.

(4)

In the previous expression, the parameter
t
is an integer that assures maximum order of accuracy close to the discontinuities. The parameter
\epsilon>0
is introduced to avoid divisions by zero, and is usually forced to take the size of the smoothness indicators at smooth zones. In our numerical tests, we will set it to
\epsilon=h^{2}
. The values
I_{k}^{r}
are called
smoothness indicators
for
f(x)
on each sub-stencil of
r
points.

Since it was first introduced in
[
15
]
, several successive improvements have been proposed for WENO algorithms. They have been focused on enhancing their accuracy, efficiency, and robustness in approximating solutions to hyperbolic conservation laws, but also on extending their applicability to other contexts such as approximation and interpolation of data. Initially introduced by Liu, Osher, and Chan in 1994
[
15
]
, WENO schemes have gone through several advancements. The WENO schemes proposed by Jiang and Shu in 1996
[
12
]
, improved the original idea in
[
15
]
, by proposing new smoothness indicators inspired by the measure of the total variation. These smoothness indicators were more capable of detecting discontinuities and allowed to extended the idea of WENO to higher orders of accuracy. However, the classical WENO scheme might experience a loss of accuracy when encountering critical points in the solution: for instance, a fifth-order WENO scheme may only attain third-order accuracy near smooth extrema
[
16
]
. In fact, in
[
16
]
, the authors proposed the WENO-M scheme, that not only addressed the accuracy issue but also marked the first significant improvement in the solution quality near shocks and high gradients. However, the introduced mapping method proved to be computationally expensive. This study led to the publication of the article
[
9
]
where the authors proposed the WENO-Z scheme, that uses a new set of weights, derived from previously unused information within the classical WENO scheme: a higher-order global smoothness indicator constructed through a linear combination of the original smoothness indicators. This scheme achieved superior results with almost the same computational effort as the classical WENO method. After that, many variants of WENO schemes were proposed, starting from these two methodologies (see, e.g.
[
10
,
13
,
17
]
). In the context of data approximation, we proposed a first improvement of WENO algorithm in 2018
[
2
]
, where the algorithm attained a progressive order of accuracy close to the discontinuities using a recursive formulation of the WENO weights.
Using WENO interpolation, maximum order of accuracy is obtained in smooth parts of the data but the accuracy is reduced to order
r+1
when, at least, a discontinuity crosses a stencil. In
[
3
]
, Amat, Ruiz, Shu and Yáñez present a new WENO method using the Aitken-Neville algorithm to obtain progressive order of approximation. This method is introduced for the point-value discretization in a uniform grid, and to approximate at the mid-points of the intervals. In
[
4
]
, the algorithm is extended to calculate the derivative value of a function knowing its evaluation in a non-uniform grid and for any point of the considered interval.

In this paper, we extend this method to
n
dimensions and prove its
theoretical properties. We start with dimension 1 in Section

2
, following the ideas presented in

[
3
,
4
]
. We provide a new recursive algorithm to obtain a non-linear
scalar approximation for any point in the central interval in Section
2
.
Then, in Section
3
, we review the multidimensional Lagrange interpolation
using tensor product, and the WENO version designed by Aràndiga et al. in
[
7
]
. Subsequently, we introduce, in Section
4
,
our general method for any dimension
n
starting with
n=2
,
and give an explicit expression for the optimal weights. Afterwards, we
generalize the method to any number of dimension
n
, and prove that the
approximation attains the maximum order of accuracy when the nodes are in a region
where
f
is smooth, and has an increasing order of accuracy depending on where the
isolated discontinuity is. In
Section
6
, we present the smoothness indicators. The theoretical results are confirmed by
the numerical examples, that are presented in Section
7
. Finally, some conclusions are drawn in the last section.

2.1
On smoothness indicators and the accuracy of the new univariate progressive WENO method

The choice of the smoothness indicators is crucial. To design the new non-linear algorithm, we only calculate the smoothness indicators at level
l=r
, and
we use them in all the levels, as we can see in this subsection. In
[
6
]
it is proved that if the smoothness indicators satisfy the following conditions:

P1

The order of a smoothness indicator that is free of discontinuities is
h^{2}
, i.e.

I^{r}_{k}=O(h^{2})\,\,\text{if}\,\,f\,\,\text{is smooth in }\,\,\mathcal{S}^{r}_{k}.

P2

The distance between two smoothness indicators
free of discontinuities is
h^{r+1}
, i.e. let

k,k^{\prime}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
be such that
there does not exist any discontinuity in
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{k}
and
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{k^{\prime}}
, then

I^{r}_{k}-I^{r}_{k^{\prime}}=O(h^{r+1}).

P3

When a discontinuity crosses the stencil
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{k}
then

I^{r}_{k}\nrightarrow 0\,\,\text{as}\,\,h\to 0.

Then, the optimal weights satisfy Eq. (
3
) for determined parameters
t
and

(Proposition 2 in
[
6
]
). We exploit this result to construct the smoothness indicators in each step of the Aitken-Neville’s algorithm. We will use the following definition (Definition 4.1 in
[

]

Definition 1

Let

\begin{split}&I_{k,k}^{l}=I_{k}^{r},\quad k=0,\dots,(2r-2)-l,\\
&I_{k,k+1}^{l}=I_{l-(r-1)+k}^{r},\quad k=0,\dots,(2r-2)-l.\end{split}

(13)

Therefore, in each step, we will remove the part which is “contaminated” by a discontinuity. In particular, we can prove the following proposition adapting the proof of Proposition 4.4 in

]
to any point

Proposition 2.3

Let

,

3.1
Multivariate linear interpolation

Let us start supposing

j=1,\ldots,n
, and the centered stencil:

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{0}}^{2r}&=\{x^{(i_{1}-r)}_{1},\ldots,x^{(i_{1}+r-1)}_{1}\}\times\{x^{(i_{2}-r)}_{2},\ldots,x^{(i_{2}+r-1)}_{2}\}\times\ldots\times\{x^{(i_{n}-r)}_{n},\ldots,x^{(i_{n}+r-1)}_{n}\}\\
&=:(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{2r})_{1}\times\ldots\times(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{2r})_{n}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{2r})_{j},\end{split}

(17)

then, the problem consists in calculating a polynomial of degree
2r-1
such that:

p(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x}),\quad\,\forall\,\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{S}_{(0,\ldots,0)}^{2r}.

To do so, we will use the Lagrange base of polynomials:

L^{j}_{p}(x_{j})=\prod_{k=i_{j}-r,k\neq p}^{i_{j}+r-1}\left(\frac{x_{j}-x^{(k)}_{j}}{x^{(p)}_{j}-x^{(k)}_{j}}\right),\quad p=i_{j}-r,\ldots,i_{j}+r-1,\quad j=1,\ldots,n,

then, the unique polynomial is

p_{\mathbf{0}}^{2r-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{l_{1}=i_{1}-r}^{i_{1}+r-1}\sum_{l_{2}=i_{2}-r}^{i_{2}+r-1}\ldots\sum_{l_{n}=i_{n}-r}^{i_{n}+r-1}f(x^{(l_{1})}_{1},x^{(l_{2})}_{2},\ldots,x^{(l_{n})}_{n})L^{1}_{l_{1}}(x_{1})\ldots L^{n}_{l_{n}}(x_{n}).

It can be checked (see
[
7
]
) that if
\mathbf{x}^{*}=(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{n}^{*})\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}[x^{(i_{j}-1)}_{j},x^{(i_{j})}_{j}]
, and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2nr}(\mathbb{R})
then the error satisfies:

E(\mathbf{x}^{*})=f(\mathbf{x}^{*})-p_{\mathbf{0}}^{2r-1}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=O(h^{2r}),

(18)

and if
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
m_{j}\leq 2r-1
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, we get

E^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}}{(\mathbf{m})}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=E^{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=O(h^{2r-\max\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}}),

(19)

where

E^{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\partial^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}E}{\partial^{m_{1}}x_{1}\ldots\partial^{m_{n}}x_{n}}(\mathbf{x}).

3.2
Multivariate WENO interpolation in Cartesian grids

Using the same notation as in the previous section, the goal is to construct a non-linear interpolant in multi-dimensions in the same way as we reviewed in
1d
, i.e., an interpolant with maximum order
2r
in the smooth parts and with order
r+1
when there exists a discontinuity which crosses, at least, one small stencil.

In this case, we introduce the method supposing that we want to obtain
an approximation of the function
f
evaluated at any point of the
hypercube
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}[x^{(i_{j}-1)}_{j},x^{(i_{j})}_{j}]
. The construction is similar to the

1d
case:
Firstly, we make the linear combination of interpolants of lower degree,
r
, if
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
then

p_{0}^{2r-1}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}^{n}}C^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})p_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}(\mathbf{x}^{*}),

where
C^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})
are the optimal weights, and it is not difficult to prove that they have the explicit form:

C^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}C^{r}_{k_{j}}(x^{*}_{j}),\quad\mathbf{k}\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}^{n},

being
C^{r}_{k_{j}}(x^{*}_{j})
,
k_{j}=0,\ldots,r-1
,
j=1,\ldots,n
the
optimal weights in
1d
. In the case that we want to approximate at
the mid-point of the hypercube, i.e.
\mathbf{x}^{*}=\mathbf{x}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{*}
, then they are defined in Eq. (
2
). The polynomials
p_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}
interpolate at the nodes

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}&=\{x^{(i_{1}+k_{1}-r)}_{1},\ldots,x^{(i_{1}+k_{1})}_{1}\}\times\{x^{(i_{2}+k_{2}-r)}_{2},\ldots,x^{(i_{2}+k_{2})}_{2}\}\times\ldots\times\{x^{(i_{n}+k_{n}-r)}_{n},\ldots,x^{(i_{n}+k_{n})}_{n}\}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{S}_{k_{j}}^{r}.\end{split}

Then, we replace the optimal weights by non-linear ones using the following formula

\omega^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\frac{\alpha^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})}{\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}^{n}}\alpha^{r}_{\mathbf{l}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})},\,\,\text{with}\,\,\alpha^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\frac{C^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})}{(\epsilon+I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}})^{t}},

(20)

being
I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
the smoothness indicators with the same requirements pointed out in Section
2
, i.e.:

P1

The order of the smoothness indicator free of discontinuities is
h^{2}
, i.e.

I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}=O(h^{2})\,\,\text{if}\,\,f\,\,\text{is smooth in }\,\,\mathcal{S}^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}.

P2

The distance between two smoothness indicators free
of discontinuities is
h^{r+1}
, i.e. let

\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=(k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{n})

be such that
there does not exist any discontinuity in
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
and
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
then

I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}-I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}=O(h^{r+1}).

P3

When a discontinuity crosses the stencil
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
then:

I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}\nrightarrow 0\,\,\text{as}\,\,h\to 0.

An example of smoothness indicators will be introduced in Section

6
. The parameters
\epsilon
and
t
are chosen to
obtain maximum order. In our case, following
[
6
]
and

[
7
]
we will take
\epsilon=h^{2}
and

t=\frac{1}{2}(r+1)
. Therefore, we state the next result, which is
similar to Proposition 2 in
[
6
]
and Theorem 1 in

[
7
]
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}[x^{(i_{j}-1)}_{j},x^{(i_{j})}_{j}]
, the multivariate WENO interpolant

\mathcal{I}^{2r-1}(\mathbf{x}^{*};f)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}^{n}}\omega^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})p_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}(\mathbf{x}^{*})

(21)

with
\omega^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
,
\mathbf{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}^{n}

defined in Eq. (
20
), with smoothness indicator

I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
,
\mathbf{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}^{n}
fulfilling

P1
,
P2
,
P3
;
\epsilon=h^{2}
and

t=\frac{1}{2}(r+1)
satisfies:

f(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\mathcal{I}^{2r-1}(\mathbf{x}^{*};f)=\begin{cases}O(h^{2r}),&\text{at smooth regions,}\\
O(h^{r+1}),&\text{if, at least, one stencil lies in a smooth region.}\end{cases}

4
A new progressive bivariate,
n=2
, WENO method

The idea of this new method is to reach the maximum possible order of
accuracy when one discontinuity crosses the largest stencil. For this
purpose, we use the Aitken-Neville-based algorithm developed in
multi-dimensions,
[
11
]
. We start with a
polynomial of degree
2r-1
and decompose it in
2^{2}
polynomials of
degree
2r-2
obtaining as weights polynomials of degree 1 that will
be replaced by non-linear weights, depending on the location of the
discontinuity. Afterwards, we continue decomposing the
2^{2}

polynomials of degree
2r-2
in polynomials of degree
2r-3
, and so
on. In each step, the non-linear weights determine the stencils free
of discontinuity, which will be used to approximate the value. The
procedure is similar to the method expounded in Section

2
. For
ease of reading, we start with
n=2
in this section, and then we extend our
results to any dimension
n
in Section
5
.

4.1
A first example: A progressive bivariate WENO method with
r=2

Let us start by designing a bivariate WENO method with
r=2
. We suppose a non
necessarily uniform grid in
[a_{1},b_{1}]\times[a_{2},b_{2}]

defined by
\{(x^{(l_{1})}_{1},x^{(l_{2})}_{2})\}_{(l_{1},l_{2})=(0,0)}^{(J_{1},J_{2})}
, with
a_{1}=x^{(0)}_{1}<x^{(1)}_{1}<\ldots<x^{(J_{1})}_{1}=b_{1}
, and
a_{2}=x^{(0)}_{2}<x^{(1)}_{2}<\ldots<x^{(J_{2})}_{2}=b_{2}
, and the data
f_{l_{1},l_{2}}=f(x^{(l_{1})}_{1},x^{(l_{2})}_{2})
,
l_{j}=0,\ldots,J_{j}
, with
j=1,2
. Let
(i_{1},i_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
0\leq i_{j}-2
and
i_{j}+1\leq J_{j}
, with
j=1,2
. We determine the largest stencil

\mathcal{S}^{4}_{(0,0)}=\{x^{(i_{1}-2)}_{1},x^{(i_{1}-1)}_{1},x^{(i_{1})}_{1},x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}\}\times\{x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2},x^{(i_{2}-1)}_{2},x^{(i_{2})}_{2},x^{(i_{2}+1)}_{2}\}=(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{4})_{1}\times(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{4})_{2},

and we want to interpolate at any point

\mathbf{x}^{*}=(x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2})\in[x^{(i_{1}-1)}_{1},x^{(i_{1})}_{1}]\times[x^{(i_{2}-1)}_{2},x^{(i_{2})}_{2}]

p^{3}_{(0,0)}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}=0}^{3}a_{l_{1},l_{2}}x_{1}^{l_{1}}x_{2}^{l_{2}},

such that
p^{3}_{(0,0)}(x^{(l_{1})}_{1},x^{(l_{2})}_{2})=f_{l_{1},l_{2}}
if
(x^{(l_{1})}_{1},x^{(l_{2})}_{2})\in\mathcal{S}^{4}_{(0,0)}

2^{2}
polynomials that interpolate in the stencils:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{S}^{3}_{(0,0)}=\{x^{(i_{1}-2)}_{1},x^{(i_{1}-1)}_{1},x^{(i_{1})}_{1}\}\times\{x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2},x^{(i_{2}-1)}_{2},x^{(i_{2})}_{2}\}=(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{3})_{1}\times(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{3})_{2},\\
&\mathcal{S}^{3}_{(1,0)}=\{x^{(i_{1}-1)}_{1},x^{(i_{1})}_{1},x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}\}\times\{x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2},x^{(i_{2}-1)}_{2},x^{(i_{2})}_{2}\}=(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{3})_{1}\times(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{3})_{2},\\
&\mathcal{S}^{3}_{(0,1)}=\{x^{(i_{1}-2)}_{1},x^{(i_{1}-1)}_{1},x^{(i_{1})}_{1}\}\times\{x^{(i_{2}-1)}_{2},x^{(i_{2})}_{2},x^{(i_{2}+1)}_{2}\}=(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{3})_{1}\times(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{3})_{2},\\
&\mathcal{S}^{3}_{(1,1)}=\{x^{(i_{1}-1)}_{1},x^{(i_{1})}_{1},x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}\}\times\{x^{(i_{2}-1)}_{2},x^{(i_{2})}_{2},x^{(i_{2}+1)}_{2}\}=(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{3})_{1}\times(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{3})_{2},\end{split}

called
p^{2}_{(0,0)},p^{2}_{(1,0)},p^{2}_{(0,1)},p^{2}_{(1,1)}
using, as we mentioned before, the Aitken-Neville-type formula given in

11
]

p^{3}_{(0,0)}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{2}}C_{(0,0),\mathbf{j}_{0}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}^{*})p^{2}_{\mathbf{j}_{0}}(\mathbf{x}^{*}),

(22)

with

\begin{split}C^{2}_{(0,0),(0,0)}(\mathbf{x}^{*})&=\left(\frac{x^{*}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}}{x^{(i_{1}-2)}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{x^{*}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}+1)}_{2}}{x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}+1)}_{2}}\right)=C^{2}_{0,0}(x_{1}^{*})C^{2}_{0,0}(x_{2}^{*}),\\

C^{2}_{(0,0),(0,1)}(\mathbf{x}^{*})&=\left(\frac{x^{*}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}}{x^{(i_{1}-2)}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{x^{*}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2}}{x^{(i_{2}+1)}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2}}\right)=C^{2}_{0,0}(x_{1}^{*})C^{2}_{0,1}(x_{2}^{*}),\\
C^{2}_{(0,0),(1,1)}(\mathbf{x}^{*})&=\left(\frac{x^{*}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}-2)}_{1}}{x^{(i_{1}+1)}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}-2)}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{x^{*}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2}}{x^{(i_{2}+1)}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-2)}_{2}}\right)=C^{2}_{0,1}(x_{1}^{*})C^{2}_{0,1}(x_{2}^{*}).\end{split}

Finally, we define the non-linear weights as:

\tilde{\omega}_{(0,0),\mathbf{k}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{(0,0),\mathbf{k}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}^{*})}{\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\{0,1\}^{2}}\tilde{\alpha}_{(0,0),\mathbf{l}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}^{*})},\,\,\textrm{ where }\,\,\tilde{\alpha}_{(0,0),\mathbf{k}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\frac{C_{(0,0),\mathbf{k}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}^{*})}{(\epsilon+I_{(0,0),\mathbf{k}}^{2})^{t}},\quad\mathbf{k}\in\{0,1\}^{2},

with

defined as:

where

P1

. The parameters

\begin{split}p^{2r-1}_{(0,0)}=&\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{2}}C^{2r-2}_{(0,0),\mathbf{j}_{0}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{1}\in\Omega_{0}}C_{\mathbf{j}_{0},\mathbf{j}_{1}}^{2r-3}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\in\Omega_{1}}C_{\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{j}_{2}}^{2r-4}\left(\ldots\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{r-3}\in\Omega_{r-4}}C^{r+1}_{\mathbf{j}_{r-4},\mathbf{j}_{r-3}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{r-2}\in\Omega_{r-3}}C^{r}_{\mathbf{j}_{r-3},\mathbf{j}_{r-2}}p^{r}_{\mathbf{j}_{r-2}}\right)\right)\dots\right)\right)\right),\end{split}

(27)

being

with

with

\mathbf{j}_{1}\in\mathbf{j}+\{0,1\}^{2}

Lemma 4.1

Let
r\leq l\leq 2r-2
;
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in[x^{(i_{1}-1)}_{1},x^{(i_{1})}_{1}]\times[x^{(i_{2}-1)}_{2},x^{(i_{2})}_{2}]

\begin{split}&C^{l}_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{j}+(0,0)}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\left(\frac{x^{*}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}-r+j_{1}+l+1)}_{1}}{x^{(i_{1}-r+j_{1})}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}-r+j_{1}+l+1)}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{x^{*}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-r+j_{2}+l+1)}_{2}}{x^{(i_{2}-r+j_{2})}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-r+j_{2}+l+1)}_{2}}\right)=C^{l}_{j_{1},j_{1}}(x^{*}_{1})C^{l}_{j_{2},j_{2}}(x^{*}_{2}),\\
&C^{l}_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{j}+(1,0)}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\left(\frac{x^{(i_{1}-r+j_{1})}_{1}-x^{*}_{1}}{x^{(i_{1}-r+j_{1})}_{1}-x^{(i_{1}-r+j_{1}+l+1)}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{x^{*}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-r+j_{2}+l+1)}_{2}}{x^{(i_{2}-r+j_{2})}_{2}-x^{(i_{2}-r+j_{2}+l+1)}_{2}}\right)=C^{l}_{j_{1},j_{1}+1}(x^{*}_{1})C^{l}_{j_{2},j_{2}}(x^{*}_{2}),\\

Proof.
The proof is direct, considering that the stencils used to obtain each interpolant are

5
A new progressive multivariate WENO method

In this section we generalize the method designed in Section
2
for
1d
, and in Section
4.3
for
2d
. We follow the same steps: First of all, we consider the data as in Section
3.1
, i.e., if
\prod_{j=1}^{n}[a_{j},b_{j}]
is an hypercube and

a_{j}=x^{(0)}_{j}<x^{(1)}_{j}<x^{(2)}_{j}<\ldots<x^{(J_{j})}_{j}=b_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,n,

are the points of a non-regular grid, we suppose our data as the evaluation of a unknown function at these points

f_{(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})}=f(x^{(l_{1})}_{1},x^{(l_{2})}_{2},\ldots,x^{(l_{n})}_{n}),\quad 0\leq l_{j}\leq J_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

Let
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
0\leq i_{j}-r\leq i_{j}+r-1\leq J_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, a point
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}[x^{(i_{j}-1)}_{j},x^{(i_{j})}_{j}]
, and the centered stencil:

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{0}}^{2r}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{S}_{0}^{2r})_{j},

(32)

with
\mathbf{0}=(0,\ldots,0)
. We compute the interpolatory polynomial of degree
2r-1
with nodes
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{0}}^{2r}
,
p^{2r-1}_{\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})
, and we express it as the combination of
2^{n}
polynomials of degree
2r-2
. Thus, we get

p^{2r-1}_{\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}C^{2r-2}_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{j}_{0}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})p^{2r-2}_{\mathbf{j}_{0}}(\mathbf{x}^{*}).

(33)

Now, we repeat this process up to polynomials of degree
r
(for simplicity remove the dependence of
\mathbf{x}^{*}
), we denote as
\Omega_{l}=\mathbf{j}_{l}+\{0,1\}^{n}
:

\begin{split}p^{2r-1}_{\mathbf{0}}&=\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}C^{2r-2}_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{j}_{0}}p^{2r-2}_{\mathbf{j}_{0}}\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}C^{2r-2}_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{j}_{0}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{1}\in\Omega_{0}}C^{2r-3}_{\mathbf{j}_{0},\mathbf{j}_{1}}p^{2r-3}_{\mathbf{j}_{1}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}C^{2r-2}_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{j}_{0}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{1}\in\Omega_{0}}C^{2r-3}_{\mathbf{j}_{0},\mathbf{j}_{1}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\in\Omega_{1}}C^{2r-4}_{\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{j}_{2}}p^{2r-4}_{\mathbf{j}_{2}}\right)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}C^{2r-2}_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{j}_{0}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{1}\in\Omega_{0}}C^{2r-3}_{\mathbf{j}_{0},\mathbf{j}_{1}}\left(\ldots\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{r-2}\in\Omega_{r-3}}C^{r}_{\mathbf{j}_{r-3},\mathbf{j}_{r-2}}p^{r}_{\mathbf{j}_{r-2}}\right)\ldots\right)\right),\\
\end{split}

(34)

with

C^{l}_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{j}+\{0,1\}^{n}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}C^{r}_{j_{i},j_{i}+\{0,1\}},

being
C^{l}_{j_{i},j_{i}}
, and
C^{l}_{j_{i},j_{i}+1}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
the weights defined in Eq. (
9
).

We establish the recursive process as Eq. (
10
):

\begin{split}&\tilde{p}^{r}_{\mathbf{j}_{r-2}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=p^{r}_{\mathbf{j}_{r-2}}(\mathbf{x}^{*}),\quad\mathbf{j}_{r-2}=\{0,\ldots,r-1\}^{n},\\
&\tilde{p}^{l+1}_{\mathbf{j}_{2r-l-3}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\sum_{\mathbf{j}_{2r-l-2}\in\mathbf{j}_{2r-l-3}+\{0,1\}^{n}}\tilde{\omega}^{l}_{\mathbf{j}_{2r-l-3},\mathbf{j}_{2r-l-2}}(x^{*})\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{j}_{2r-l-2}}^{l}(x^{*}),\quad l=r,\dots,2r-2,\,\,\mathbf{j}_{2r-l-3}\in\{0,\dots,2r-2-l\}^{n},\\
\end{split}

being for
l=r,\dots,2r-2
, and
\mathbf{k}\in\{0,\ldots,2r-2-l\}
,
\mathbf{k}_{1}\in\mathbf{k}+\{0,1\}^{n}
:

\begin{split}\tilde{\omega}^{l}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{1}}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{1}}^{l}(\mathbf{x}^{*})}{\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{l}}^{l}(\mathbf{x}^{*})},\quad\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{1}}^{l}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\frac{{C}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{1}}^{l}(\mathbf{x}^{*})}{(\epsilon+I^{l}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{1}})^{t}},\quad\mathbf{k}_{1}\in\mathbf{k}+\{0,1\}^{n},\end{split}

(35)

where
I^{l}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{1}}
are the smoothness indicators determined by the following formula:

{I}^{l}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{1}}=I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}+(l-(r-1))\mathbf{v}},\quad\text{if }\quad\mathbf{k}_{1}=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{v},\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{v}\in\{0,1\}^{n},

(36)

being
I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
, with
0\leq k_{j}\leq r-1
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, smoothness indicators satisfying the properties
P1
,
P2
, and
P3
.

Therefore, the approximation will be:

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{2r-1}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{0}}^{2r-1}(\mathbf{x}^{*}).

(37)

Finally, we calculate the order of accuracy stating the next theorem.

Theorem 5.1

Let
\mathbf{l}_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}^{n}
,
l_{0}=\min_{j=1,\ldots,n}(\mathbf{l}_{0})_{j}

\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}[x^{(i_{j}-1)}_{j},x^{(i_{j})}_{j}]

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{2r-1}(\mathbf{x}^{*})
the approximation defined in Eq. (

6
Smoothness indicators

In this section, we present some smoothness indicators which satisfy the above mentioned properties. We generalize the smoothness indicators introduced by Aràndiga et al. in
[
7
]
which are an adaptation of the ones presented in
[
5
]
. The idea is to design some functionals that fulfill
P1
,
P2
, and
P3
.

Given
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}>0
,
\Phi(\mathbf{s})=\mathbf{x}^{*}+(s_{1}h_{1},\dots,s_{n}h_{n})
,
f
sufficiently smooth

\displaystyle\int_{\Phi([0,1]^{n})}(f\circ\Phi^{-1})^{(\boldsymbol{l})}(\mathbf{x})^{2}d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Phi([0,1]^{n})}\big(f^{(\boldsymbol{l})}(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))h_{1}^{-l_{1}}\dots h_{n}^{-l_{n}}\big)^{2}d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=h_{1}^{-2l_{1}}\dots h_{n}^{-2l_{n}}\int_{\Phi([0,1]^{n})}f^{(\boldsymbol{l})}(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))d\mathbf{x}=h_{1}^{-2l_{1}}\dots h_{n}^{-2l_{n}}\int_{[0,1]^{n}}f^{(\boldsymbol{l})}(\mathbf{s})h_{1}\dots h_{n}d\mathbf{s}

\displaystyle=h_{1}^{-2l_{1}+1}\dots h_{n}^{-2l_{n}+1}\int_{[0,1]^{n}}f^{(\boldsymbol{l})}(\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}.

Hence, we define

I_{\Gamma}(f)=\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{J}}{h_{1}^{2l_{1}-1}\dots h_{n}^{2l_{n}-1}}\int_{\Gamma}(f^{\mathbf{l})}(\mathbf{x}))^{2}d\mathbf{x},

(39)

where
\Gamma=\prod_{j=1}^{n}[x^{(i_{j}-1)}_{j},x^{(i_{j})}_{j}]
,
\mathcal{J}=\{0,1,\ldots,r\}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
smoothness indicators

\displaystyle I_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}=I_{\Gamma}(p^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}),\quad\mathbf{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}^{n},

(40)

to get an expression which is scale independent.

Theorem 6.1

The smoothness indicators defined in Eq. (
39
-
40
)
satisfy properties
P1
,
P2
, and
P3
.

Proof.
Since
|\Gamma|=h_{1}\ldots h_{n}
,
it is clear that if
f
is smooth in
\Gamma
, then

I_{\Gamma}(f)=O(h^{2}).

We suppose that
p
is a polynomial interpolant at a stencil of
(r+1)^{n}
points, then

\displaystyle I_{\Gamma}(f)-I_{\Gamma}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{J}}{h_{1}^{2l_{1}-1}\dots h_{n}^{2l_{n}-1}}\int_{\Gamma}((f^{\mathbf{l})}(\mathbf{x}))^{2}-(p^{\mathbf{l})}(\mathbf{x}))^{2})d\mathbf{x}

(41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{J}}{h_{1}^{2l_{1}-1}\dots h_{n}^{2l_{n}-1}}\int_{\Gamma}E^{\mathbf{l})}(\mathbf{x})(2f^{\mathbf{l})}(\mathbf{x})-E^{\mathbf{l})}(\mathbf{x}))d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{J}}{h_{1}^{2l_{1}}\dots h_{n}^{2l_{n}}}O(h^{r+1-||\mathbf{l}||_{\infty}})

\displaystyle=O(h^{r+1}).

where
E
is defined in Eq. (
18
), and
||\mathbf{l}||_{\infty}=\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}|l_{j}|
.
Then, if
\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}^{n}
, we get:

I_{\Gamma}(p_{\mathbf{k}}^{r})-I_{\Gamma}(p_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{r})=I_{\Gamma}(p_{\mathbf{k}}^{r})-I_{\Gamma}(f)+I_{\Gamma}(f)-I_{\Gamma}(p_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{r})=O(h^{r+1}).

Finally, if a discontinuity crosses the stencil
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
then:

I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}\nrightarrow 0\,\,\text{as}\,\,h\to 0,

since some of the quadratic terms of
I^{r}_{\mathbf{k}}
will not converge to
0
.

\blacksquare

Conclusions and future work

In this work, we have presented a new WENO method with a progressive order of accuracy close to the discontinuities. This new algorithm
is simpler computationally than the one presented in
[
3
]
and more general. In particular, our main contributions can be summarised in the following: we design a new recursive method to compute the interpolation; we construct non-linear explicit formulas to calculate the weights to interpolate
any point in the central interval; we propose an algorithm for non-uniform grids, and finally, we extend the method to
n
dimensions. This strategy presents a drawback, the stencils
used to interpolate are square and the number of points is larger than necessary to get the optimal order of accuracy. To improve this fact and
to apply this technique in partial differential equations contexts are the principal lines of our future work.

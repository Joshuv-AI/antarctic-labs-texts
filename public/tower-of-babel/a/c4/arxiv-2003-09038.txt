Byzantine-Resilient Distributed Optimization of Multi-Dimensional Functions
Kananart Kuwaranancharoen, Lei Xin, Shreyas Sundaram

Abstract
The problem of distributed optimization requires a group of agents to reach agreement on a parameter that minimizes the average of their local cost functions using information received from their neighbors. While there are a variety of distributed optimization algorithms that can solve this problem, they are typically vulnerable to malicious (or “Byzantine”) agents that do not follow the algorithm. Recent attempts to address this issue focus on single dimensional functions, or provide analysis under certain assumptions on the statistical properties of the functions at the agents. In this paper, we propose a resilient distributed optimization algorithm for multi-dimensional convex functions. Our scheme involves two filtering steps at each iteration of the algorithm: (1) distance-based and (2) component-wise removal of extreme states. We show that this algorithm can mitigate the impact of up to F F Byzantine agents in the neighborhood of each regular node, without knowing the identities of the Byzantine agents in advance. In particular, we show that if the network topology satisfies certain conditions, all of the regular states are guaranteed to asymptotically converge to a bounded region that contains the global minimizer.



I
Introduction

The problem of distributed optimization requires a network of agents to reach agreement on a parameter that minimizes the average of their objective functions using local information received from their neighbors. This framework is motivated by various applications including machine learning, power systems, and robotic networks, and there are a variety of approaches to solve this problem
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. However, these existing works typically make the assumption that all agents follow the prescribed protocol; indeed, such protocols fail if even a single agent behaves in a malicious or incorrect manner
[
5
]
.

A handful of recent papers have considered this problem for the case where agent misbehavior follows prescribed patterns
[
6
,
7
]
. A more general (and serious) form of misbehavior is captured by the
Byzantine
adversary model from computer science, where misbehaving agents can send arbitrary (and conflicting) values to their neighbors at each iteration of the algorithm. Under such Byzantine behavior, it has been shown that it is impossible to guarantee computation of the true optimal point
[
5
,
8
]
. Thus, some papers have formulated distributed optimization algorithms that allow the non-adversarial nodes to converge to a certain region surrounding the the true minimizer, regardless of the adversaries’ actions
[
5
,
9
,
10
]
. However, the above works focus on single dimensional functions. The extension to general multi-dimensional functions remains largely open, however, since even the region containing the true minimizer of the functions is challenging to characterize in such cases
[
11
]
. The recent papers
[
12
,
13
]
consider a vector version of the resilient decentralized machine learning problem by utilizing block coordinate descent. Those papers show that the states of regular nodes will converge to the statistical minimizer with high probability, but the analysis is restricted to i.i.d training data across the network.

1

1

We note that there is also a branch of literature pertaining to optimization in a client-server architecture
[
14
]

[
15
]
; this differs from our setting where we consider a fully distributed network of agents.

In this paper, we propose an algorithm that extends the “local-filtering” dynamics proposed in
[
5
,
9
]
(for single-dimensional convex functions) to the multi-dimensional case. However, this extension is non-trivial, as simply applying the filtering operations proposed in those papers to each coordinate of the parameter vector does not appear to yield clear guarantees. Instead, we show that by having each node apply an additional filtering step on the parameter vectors that it receives from its neighbors at each step (based on the distance of those vectors from a commonly chosen reference point), one can recover certain performance guarantees in the face of Byzantine adversaries.

II-C

Adversarial Behavior

Definition 4

A node

The set of Byzantine nodes is denoted by

The identities of the Byzantine agents are unknown to regular agents in advance. Furthermore, we allow the Byzantine agents know the entire topology of the network and functions equipped by the regular nodes (such worst case behavior is typical in the study of such adversarial models

,

,

(

For

Thus, the

III
Problem formulation

Consider a group of
N
agents
\mathcal{V}
interconnected over a graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
. Each agent

has a local convex cost function

. The objective is to collaboratively solve the following minimization problem:

\displaystyle\mathop{\min}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}}f_{i}(x)

(1)

where

is the common decision variable.
We assume that nodes can only communicate with their immediate neighbors to solve the above problem. However, since Byzantine nodes are allowed to send arbitrary values to their neighbors at each iteration of any algorithm, it is not possible to solve Problem (
1
) under such misbehavior (since one is not guaranteed to infer any information about the true functions of the Byzantine agents)
[
5
,
8
]
. Thus, the optimization problem is recast into the following form:

\displaystyle\mathop{\min}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}f_{i}(x).

(2)

We will now propose an algorithm that allows the regular nodes to approximately solve the above problem (as characterized later in the paper). We will make the following assumption throughout.

Assumption 1

For all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
, the functions
f_{i}(x)
are convex, and the sets
\argmin f_{i}(x)
are non-empty and bounded.

Remark 1

The role of the auxiliary point

In other words, since adversarial nodes can try to pull the regular nodes away from the true minimizer, the auxiliary point provides a common reference point for each regular node with which to evaluate the states in its neighborhood. This motivates the distance-based filtering step of Algorithm
1
(in line 8), which removes the

The regular agents will converge to consensus exponentially fast under that algorithm, however, and thus we expect that running the resilient consensus algorithm to compute the auxiliary point simultaneously with the other update steps (given by lines 6-11) will also lead to the same guarantees. In the interest of space, we do not provide that analysis here.

\|g(x)\|_{2}\leq L
, we have

\displaystyle\cos\hat{\theta}(x)\geq\frac{f(x)-f(x^{*})}{\|g(x)\|_{2}\;\|x^{*}-x\|_{2}}\geq\frac{f(x)-f(x^{*})}{L\;\|x^{*}-x\|_{2}}.

(9)

Let
\tilde{x}
be the point on the line connecting
x^{*}
and
x
such that
f(\tilde{x})=f(x^{*})+\epsilon
. We can rewrite the point
x
as

\displaystyle x=x^{*}+t(\tilde{x}-x^{*})\quad\text{where}\quad t=\frac{\|x-x^{*}\|_{2}}{\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|_{2}}\geq 1.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}(\epsilon)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(x^{*},\delta(\epsilon))
. Consider the term on the RHS of (
9
). We have

\displaystyle\frac{f(x)-f(x^{*})}{\|x-x^{*}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(x^{*}+t(\tilde{x}-x^{*}))-f(x^{*})}{t\|\tilde{x}-x^{*}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{f(x^{*}+t(\tilde{x}-x^{*}))-f(x^{*})}{t\max_{x\in\mathcal{C}(\epsilon)}\|x-x^{*}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{f(x^{*}+t(\tilde{x}-x^{*}))-f(x^{*})}{t\delta(\epsilon)}.

(10)

Since the quantity
\frac{f(x^{*}+t(\tilde{x}-x^{*}))-f(x^{*})}{t}

is non-decreasing in
t\in(0,\infty)

[
17
, Lemma 2.80]
, the inequality (
10
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{f(x)-f(x^{*})}{\|x-x^{*}\|_{2}}\geq\frac{f(\tilde{x})-f(x^{*})}{\delta(\epsilon)}=\frac{\epsilon}{\delta(\epsilon)}.

(11)

Therefore, combining (
9
) and (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\cos\hat{\theta}(x)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{L\delta(\epsilon)}.

(12)

However, from the definition of convex functions, we have

\displaystyle f(x^{*})\geq f(\tilde{x})+\langle g(\tilde{x}),\;x^{*}-\tilde{x}\rangle.

Since
\|g(\tilde{x})\|_{2}\leq L
and
\|x^{*}-\tilde{x}\|_{2}\leq\delta(\epsilon)
, we get

\displaystyle\epsilon=f(\tilde{x})-f(x^{*})\leq-\langle g(\tilde{x}),\;x^{*}-\tilde{x}\rangle\leq L\delta(\epsilon).

Since
\epsilon>0
and
\frac{\epsilon}{L\delta(\epsilon)}\leq 1
from the above inequality, the inequality (
12
) becomes

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(x)\leq\arccos\Big(\frac{\epsilon}{L\delta(\epsilon)}\Big)\triangleq\theta(\epsilon)<\frac{\pi}{2}.

∎

From Proposition
2
, if
f_{i}
satisfies Assumptions
1
and
2
for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
, then for all
\epsilon>0
, we have
\angle(-g_{i}(x),\;x_{i}^{*}-x)\leq\theta_{i}(\epsilon)<\frac{\pi}{2}
for all
x\notin\mathcal{C}_{i}(\epsilon)
and
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
.

Define
\tilde{R}_{i}\triangleq\|x_{i}^{*}-\hat{x}\|_{2}
and

\displaystyle R^{*}\triangleq\inf_{\epsilon>0}\Big\{\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\big\{\max\{\tilde{R}_{i}\sec\theta_{i}(\epsilon),\;\tilde{R}_{i}+\delta_{i}(\epsilon)\}\big\}\Big\}.

(13)

We now come to the main result of this paper, showing that the states of all the regular nodes will asymptotically converge to a ball of radius
R^{*}
around the auxiliary point
\hat{x}
under Algorithm
1
.

Theorem 2
(Convergence)

If the states are updated using Algorithm
1
, and Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
hold, then for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
,
\limsup_{k}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq R^{*}
, regardless of the actions of any
F
-local set of Byzantine adversaries.

The full proof of the theorem is somewhat long, and is thus provided in the Appendix. We present here a sketch of the proof. We work towards the proof of Theorem
2
in several steps.
For a fixed
\epsilon>0
and for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, define the
convergence radius

\displaystyle s^{*}(\xi,\epsilon)\triangleq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\big\{\max\{\tilde{R}_{i}\sec\theta_{i}(\epsilon),\tilde{R}_{i}+\delta_{i}(\epsilon)\}\big\}+\xi.

Throughout the paper, we will omit the dependence of
\epsilon
in
s^{*}(\xi,\epsilon)
for notational simplicity.

The key idea is that for a fixed
\xi>0
, we partition the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into
3
regions:

1.

\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\;\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{i}+\delta_{i}\})
,

2.

\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\;s^{*}(\xi))\setminus\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\;\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{i}+\delta_{i}\})
, and

3.

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\;s^{*}(\xi))
.

We refer to
\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\;s^{*}(\xi))
as the
convergence region
and note that
R^{*}=\inf_{\epsilon>0}s^{*}(0)
. In Steps 1, 2 and 3, we analyze the gradient update (
5
) for each regular agent
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
, and in Step 4, we consider the sequence of distance updates of the regular agent furthest from the auxiliary point. Finally, we will take
\xi
to go to zero and take the infimum of the distance bound over
\epsilon>0
.

Step 1:
First, we show that if
k
is sufficiently large and the state
z_{i}[k]
is in Region 1 i.e.,

\displaystyle z_{i}[k]\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\})\subset\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},s^{*}(\xi)),

then by applying the gradient update (
5
), the state
x_{i}[k+1]
is still in the convergence region i.e.,
x_{i}[k+1]\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},s^{*}(\xi))
. To do this, we leverage the fact that the magnitude of the gradient step satisfies
\eta[k]\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\leq\eta[k]L
by Assumption
2
and
\eta[k]L
decreases as
k
increases by Assumption
3
.

Step 2:
We find the relationship between the terms
\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}
and
\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}
which will be used in the subsequent step. Specifically, for
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
, if the state
z_{i}[k]
is in region 2 or 3, i.e.,
\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}>\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\}
, then we show

\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\\
-\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})

(14)

where we define
\Delta_{i}:[\tilde{R}_{i},\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
to be the function

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(p,l)\triangleq 2l\Big(\sqrt{p^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}}\cos\theta_{i}-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\Big)-l^{2}.

Step 3:
We show that if
k
is sufficiently large and the state
z_{i}[k]
is in Region 2 i.e.,
\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\in\big(\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\},s^{*}(\xi)\big]
then by applying the gradient update (
5
), we have that the state
x_{i}[k+1]
is still in the convergence region i.e.,
x_{i}[k+1]\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},s^{*}(\xi))
. This is done by showing that small
\eta[k]L
makes the RHS of (
14
) bounded above by
\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}
.

From Step 1 and 3, and Proposition
1
, we can conclude that for sufficiently large
k
, for each regular node
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
, if the state
x_{i}[k]
is in the convergence region, then the state
x_{i}[k+1]
is still in the convergence region, i.e., the regular states cannot leave the convergence region.

Step 4:
We show that the states of all regular nodes eventually enter the convergence region. Specifically, from Proposition
1
, we have that

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}.

(15)

On the other hand, for the agents whose state
z_{i}[k]
is in Region 3, we show that the term
\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})
in (
14
) is bounded below as

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})>c_{i}\eta[k]

(16)

for sufficiently large
k
,
where
c_{i}
is a positive constant. Incorporating (
15
) and (
16
) into (
14
), we can conclude that the quantity
\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}
strictly decreases if it is greater than
s^{*}(\xi)
. Eventually, every state will enter the convergence region, which completes the proof.

To gain insight into the convergence region, we provide the following result.

Proposition 3

Let
x^{*}
be a solution of Problem (
2
). If Assumptions
1
and
2
hold, then
x^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},R^{*})
where
R^{*}
is defined in (
13
).

Proof:

We will show that the summation of any subgradients of the regular nodes’ functions at any point outside the region
\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},R^{*})
cannot be zero.

Let
x_{0}
be a point outside
\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},R^{*})
. Since
\|x_{0}-\hat{x}\|_{2}>\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{i}+\delta_{i}(\epsilon)\}
for some
\epsilon>0
, we have that
x_{0}\notin\mathcal{C}_{i}(\epsilon)
for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{i}(\epsilon)
in (
7
), we have

f_{i}(x_{0})>f_{i}(x_{i}^{*})+\epsilon
for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
. Since the functions
f_{i}
are convex, we obtain
g_{i}(x_{0})\neq\mathbf{0}
for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
where
g_{i}(x_{0})\in\partial f_{i}(x_{0})
.

Consider the angle between the vectors
x_{0}-x_{i}^{*}
and
x_{0}-\hat{x}
. Suppose
\tilde{R}_{i}>0
; otherwise, we have
\angle(x_{0}-x_{i}^{*},\;x_{0}-\hat{x})=0
. Using Lemma
2
in the Appendix, we can bound the angle as follows:

\displaystyle\angle(x_{0}-x_{i}^{*},\;x_{0}-\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{y\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\tilde{R}_{i})}\angle(x_{0}-y,\;x_{0}-\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\arcsin\Big(\frac{\tilde{R}_{i}}{\|x_{0}-\hat{x}\|_{2}}\Big).

Since
\|x_{0}-\hat{x}\|_{2}>\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{i}\sec\theta_{i}\}
and
\arcsin(x)
is an increasing function in
x\in[-1,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\angle(x_{0}-x_{i}^{*},\;x_{0}-\hat{x})

\displaystyle<\arcsin\Big(\frac{\tilde{R}_{i}}{\tilde{R}_{i}\sec\theta_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\arcsin(\cos\theta_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{i}.

Using Proposition
2
and the inequality above, we can bound the angle between the vectors
g_{i}(x_{0})
and
x_{0}-\hat{x}
as follows:

\displaystyle\quad\angle(g_{i}(x_{0}),\;x_{0}-\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq\angle(g_{i}(x_{0}),\;x_{0}-x_{i}^{*})+\angle(x_{0}-x_{i}^{*},\;x_{0}-\hat{x})

\displaystyle<\theta_{i}+\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{i}\Big)=\frac{\pi}{2}.

Note that the first inequality is obtained from
[
18
, Corollary 12]
. Let
u=\frac{x_{0}-\hat{x}}{\|x_{0}-\hat{x}\|_{2}}
. Compute the inner product

\displaystyle\Big\langle\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}g_{i}(x_{0}),\;u\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\langle g_{i}(x_{0}),\;u\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|g_{i}(x_{0})\|_{2}\cos\angle(g_{i}(x_{0}),\;x_{0}-\hat{x})

\displaystyle>0

since
\|g_{i}(x_{0})\|_{2}>0
and
\cos\angle(g_{i}(x_{0}),\;x_{0}-\hat{x})>0
for any
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
. This implies that
\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}g_{i}(x_{0})\neq\mathbf{0}
. Since we can arbitrarily choose
g_{i}(x_{0})
from the set
\partial f_{i}(x_{0})
, we have
\mathbf{0}\notin\partial f(x_{0})
where
f(x)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}f_{i}(x)
.
∎

Thus, Theorem
2
and Proposition
3
show that Algorithm
1
causes all regular nodes to converge to a region that also contains the true solution, regardless of the actions of any
F
-local set of Byzantine adversaries. The size of this region scales with the quantity
R^{*}
. Loosely speaking, this quantity becomes smaller as the minimizers of the local functions of the regular agents get closer together. More specifically, consider a fixed
\epsilon>0
. If the functions
f_{i}(x)
are translated so that the minimizers
x_{i}^{*}
get closer together (i.e.,
\tilde{R}_{i}
is smaller and
\theta(\epsilon)
is fixed), and the auxiliary point
\hat{x}
is in the hyperrectangle containing the minimizers (which is the case when we run a resilient consensus algorithm such as the one in
[
16
]
) then
R^{*}
also decreases, and the state
x_{i}[k]
is guaranteed to become closer to the true minimizer as
k
goes to infinity.

VII
Conclusion and Future work

In this paper, we developed a resilient distributed optimization algorithm for multi-dimensional functions. Our results guarantee that the regular states asymptotically reach consensus and enter a bounded region that contains the global minimizer, irrespective of the actions of Byzantine agents, if the network topology satisfies certain conditions. We characterized the size of this region. A promising avenue for future research would be to further refine the size of the convergence region, and to relax the conditions on the network topology.

Lemma 2

If

\displaystyle\max_{y\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},R)}\angle(x-y,\;x-\hat{x})=\arcsin\Big(\frac{R}{\|x-\hat{x}\|_{2}}\Big).

We can calculate the square of the distance by using the law of cosines:

\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}=\;\|x_{i}[k+1]-z_{i}[k]\|^{2}_{2}+\|\hat{x}-z_{i}[k]\|^{2}_{2}\\
-2\|x_{i}[k+1]-z_{i}[k]\|_{2}\cdot\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\\
\times\cos\angle(x_{i}[k+1]-z_{i}[k],\;\hat{x}-z_{i}[k]).

Using the update (
5
) and
\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}=s_{i}[k]
, we get

\displaystyle\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta[k]^{2}\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}^{2}+s_{i}[k]^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2s_{i}[k]\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\cos\psi_{i}[k]

\displaystyle\leq\eta[k]^{2}\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}^{2}+s_{i}[k]^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2s_{i}[k]\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\cos\tilde{\psi}_{i}(s_{i}[k]).

(21)

The inequality above is obtained by using the inequality (
20
). In addition, we can simplify the term
s_{i}[k]\cos\tilde{\psi}_{i}(s_{i}[k])
as follows:

\displaystyle s_{i}[k]\cos\tilde{\psi}_{i}(s_{i}[k])

\displaystyle=s_{i}[k]\cos\Big(\arcsin\frac{\tilde{R}_{i}}{s_{i}[k]}+\theta_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=s_{i}[k]\cos\Big(\arcsin\frac{\tilde{R}_{i}}{s_{i}[k]}\Big)\cos\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\quad-s_{i}[k]\sin\Big(\arcsin\frac{\tilde{R}_{i}}{s_{i}[k]}\Big)\sin\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{s_{i}[k]^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}}\cos\theta_{i}-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}.

Therefore, we can rewrite (
21
) as

\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\eta[k]^{2}\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}^{2}+s_{i}[k]^{2}\\
-2\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\;(\sqrt{s_{i}[k]^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}}\cos\theta_{i}-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}).

∎

Step 3:
We want to show that if
z_{i}[k]\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},s^{*}(\xi))
,
z_{i}[k]\notin\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\})
and
k
is large enough, then by applying the gradient update (
5
), the state
x_{i}[k+1]\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},s^{*}(\xi))
i.e.,
x_{i}[k+1]
is still in the convergence region.

Let

\displaystyle a_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle\triangleq-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}+\sqrt{\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{i}}\;,

\displaystyle a_{i}^{-}

\displaystyle\triangleq-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}-\sqrt{\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{i}}\;,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq 2\big(\sqrt{\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}}\cos\theta_{i}-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\big).

Let
k_{2}^{*}
be such that

\displaystyle\eta[k_{2}^{*}]\leq\frac{1}{L}\min_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\big\{\min\{a_{i}^{+},\;b_{i}\}\big\}.

Lemma 5

For all
i\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq k_{2}^{*}
, if
\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\in\big(\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\},s^{*}(\xi)\big]
then
\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi)
.

Proof:

Consider the function
\Gamma_{i}(p,l)
defined in (
18
). Compute the second derivative with respect to
p
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Gamma_{i}}{\partial p}=2p-2lp(p^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cos\theta_{i},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\Gamma_{i}}{\partial p^{2}}=2+2l\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}(p^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\cos\theta_{i}.

Note that
\frac{\partial^{2}\Gamma_{i}}{\partial p^{2}}>0
for all
p\in(\tilde{R}_{i},\infty)
. This implies that

\sup_{p\in(\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\},\;s^{*}(\xi)]}\Gamma_{i}(p,l)\leq\max_{p\in[\tilde{R}_{i},\;s^{*}(\xi)]}\Gamma_{i}(p,l)\\
=\max\big\{\Gamma_{i}(\tilde{R}_{i},l),\;\Gamma_{i}(s^{*}(\xi),l)\big\}.

(22)

First, let consider
\Gamma_{i}(\tilde{R}_{i},\;l)
.
From the definition of
\Gamma_{i}
in (
18
), we have that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}(\tilde{R}_{i},l)\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad l\in[a_{i}^{-},\;a_{i}^{+}].

(23)

Note that
a_{i}^{+}>0
and
a_{i}^{-}<0
since
s^{*}(\xi)\geq\tilde{R}_{i}+\xi
.
Using Assumption
2
, Assumption
3
, and the definition of
k_{2}^{*}
, we have that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq k_{2}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\leq\eta[k]L\leq\min_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\big\{\min\{a_{j}^{+},\;b_{j}\}\big\}\leq a_{i}^{+}.

By (
23
), we obtain that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq k_{2}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}(\tilde{R}_{i},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}.

(24)

Now, let consider
\Gamma_{i}(s^{*}(\xi),\;l)
.
From the definition of
\Gamma_{i}
in (
18
), we have that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}(s^{*}(\xi),l)\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad l\in[0,\;b_{i}].

(25)

Note that
b_{i}>0
since
s^{*}(\xi)\geq\tilde{R}_{i}\sec\theta_{i}+\xi
.
Using Assumption
2
, Assumption
3
, and the definition of
k_{2}^{*}
, we have that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq k_{2}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\leq\eta[k]L\leq\min_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\big\{\min\{a_{j}^{+},\;b_{j}\}\big\}\leq b_{i}.

By (
25
), we obtain that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq k_{2}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}(s^{*}(\xi),\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}.

(26)

Combine (
24
) and (
26
) to get that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq k_{2}^{*}
,

\max\big\{\Gamma_{i}(\tilde{R}_{i},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}),\;\Gamma_{i}(s^{*}(\xi),\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})\big\}\\
\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}.

(27)

Using Lemma
4
, (
22
) and (
27
), respectively, we obtain that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq k_{2}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\quad\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{p\in(\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\{\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\},\;s^{*}(\xi)]}\Gamma_{i}(p,\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\max\big\{\Gamma_{i}(\tilde{R}_{i},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}),\;\Gamma_{i}(s^{*}(\xi),\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}.

∎

Step 4:
We now show that the states of all regular nodes eventually enter the convergence region.

Lemma 6

There exists
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
such that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
,
\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi)
.

Proof:

Assume for the purpose of contradiction that for all
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
there exists
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
such that
\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}>s^{*}(\xi)
.
Define the sets of nodes

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{x}[k]\triangleq\{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}:\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}>s^{*}(\xi)\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]\triangleq\{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}:\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}>s^{*}(\xi)\}.

Note that
\mathcal{I}_{x}[k]\neq\emptyset
for all
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
by the assumption. In addition, from Lemma
3
and Lemma
5
, we have that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
and
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
, if
\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi)
then
\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi)
. This implies that for all
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
,
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{I}_{x}[k+1]\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]
.

From Lemma
4
, for
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
and
v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]
,

\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\\
-\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}).

For any
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}\Big(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\min_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}).

(28)

Consider the first term on the RHS of (
28
). By the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}=\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}.

Consider the term on the LHS of (
28
). Since
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{I}_{x}[k+1]\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]
and the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{x}[k+1]
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{x}[k+1]}\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore, the inequality (
28
) becomes

\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\\
-\min_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}).

(29)

Consider the definition of the function
\Delta_{i}
in (
17
). It is clear that if
p_{1}>p_{2}\geq\tilde{R}_{i}
then
\Delta_{i}(p_{1},l)>\Delta_{i}(p_{2},l)
.
Then, for all
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
and
v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]
, we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})>\Delta_{i}(s^{*}(\xi),\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}).

(30)

Furthermore, the function
\Delta_{i}
satisfies

\Delta_{i}(p,l)\geq\Big(\sqrt{p^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}}\cos\theta_{i}-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\Big)l\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad l\in\Big[0,\;\sqrt{p^{2}-\tilde{R}_{i}^{2}}\cos\theta_{i}-\tilde{R}_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\Big].

(31)

For
x\notin\mathcal{C}_{i}(\epsilon)
, from the definition of convex functions, we have

-\langle g_{i}(x),\;x_{i}^{*}-x\rangle\geq f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(x_{i}^{*})
.
Using the inequality (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|g_{i}(x)\|_{2}\geq\frac{f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(x_{i}^{*})}{\|x-x_{i}^{*}\|_{2}}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{\delta_{i}(\epsilon)}\triangleq\kappa_{i}(\epsilon)>0,

(32)

where
\mathcal{C}_{i}(\epsilon)
is defined in (
7
). Let
k_{3}^{*}
be such that

\displaystyle\eta[k_{3}^{*}]\leq\frac{1}{2L}\min_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}b_{i}.

Using (
32
), Assumption
2
, and the definition of
k_{3}^{*}
, we have that for all
k\geq k_{3}^{*}
and
v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]
,

\displaystyle\eta[k]\kappa_{i}\leq\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\leq\eta[k]L\leq\frac{b_{i}}{2}.

(33)

Since
\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\in[0,\;\frac{b_{i}}{2}]
, we can apply (
31
) to get

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\big(s^{*}(\xi),\;\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}\big)\geq\frac{b_{i}}{2}\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2}.

(34)

Let
k^{*}=\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*},k_{3}^{*}\}
.
Combine (
30
), (
34
), and the first inequality of (
33
) to get that for all
k\geq k^{*}
and
v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},\eta[k]\;\|g_{i}[k]\|_{2})>\frac{1}{2}b_{i}\kappa_{i}\eta[k].

(35)

On the other hand, from Proposition
1
, we have that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
,

\displaystyle\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v_{j}\in(\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}\cap\mathcal{R})\cup\{v_{i}\}}\|x_{j}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{j}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2},

which implies that

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}.

Apply the above inequality and the inequality (
35
) to (
29
) to get that for all
k\geq k^{*}
,

\displaystyle\quad\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle<\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\min_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{z}[k]}b_{i}\kappa_{i}\eta[k]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\min_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}b_{i}\kappa_{i}\eta[k].

(36)

Apply the inequality (
36
) recursively to get that for
K\geq 1
,

\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k^{*}+K]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}<\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k^{*}]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\min_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}b_{i}\kappa_{i}\sum_{k=k^{*}}^{k^{*}+K-1}\eta[k].

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta[k]=\infty
from Assumption
3
, we have that there exists
K^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k^{*}+K^{*}]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}.

This implies that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{i}[k^{*}+K^{*}]-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\big(s^{*}(\xi)\big)^{2}

which contradicts our assumption.
∎

We are now in place to prove Theorem
2
by combining the results from Lemma
3
,
5
, and
6
. Intuitively, Lemma
6
states that there is a time-step
k
that
x_{i}[k]\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},s^{*}(\xi))
for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
. Then, by applying Lemma
3
and
5
, and Proposition
1
, we can conclude that the state
x_{i}[k]
will remain inside
\mathcal{B}(\hat{x},s^{*}(\xi))
for all subsequent time-steps.

Proof:

Let
k^{*}\geq\max\big\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\big\}
be the time index that satisfies Lemma
6
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k^{*}]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi).

Applying Proposition
1
, we get

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|z_{i}[k^{*}]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k^{*}]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi).

(37)

On the other hand, from Lemma
3
and
5
, we have that for
k\geq\max\{k_{1}^{*},k_{2}^{*}\}
, if
\|z_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi)
then
\|x_{i}[k+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi)
. Therefore, the inequality (
37
) implies that

\displaystyle\max_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}\|x_{i}[k^{*}+1]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi).

Applying the above procedure recursively, we get

\displaystyle\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\leq s^{*}(\xi)\quad\text{for all}\;\;k\geq k^{*}\quad\text{and}\;\;v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}.

However, since we can choose
\xi
to be arbitrary small, we have that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{k}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq s^{*}(0)

\displaystyle=\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\big\{\max\{\tilde{R}_{j}\sec\theta_{j},\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}\}\big\}.

Furthermore, the variables
\theta_{i}(\epsilon)
and
\delta_{i}(\epsilon)
depend on
\epsilon
which is defined in (
7
) and the above inequality holds for any
\epsilon>0
. Hence, for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
,

\limsup_{k}\|x_{i}[k]-\hat{x}\|_{2}\\
\leq\inf_{\epsilon>0}\Big\{\max_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\big\{\max\{\tilde{R}_{j}\sec\theta_{j}(\epsilon),\;\tilde{R}_{j}+\delta_{j}(\epsilon)\}\big\}\Big\}.

∎

Abstract
The minimum energy path (MEP) is the most probable transition path that connects two equilibrium states of a potential energy landscape. It has been widely used to study transition mechanisms as well as transition rates in the fields of chemistry, physics, and materials science. In this paper, we derive a novel result establishing the stability of MEPs under perturbations of the energy landscape. The result also represents a crucial step towards studying the convergence of various numerical approximations of MEPs, such as the nudged elastic band and string methods.



1
Introduction

The long term evolution of physical systems is often characterised by rare transitions between energy minima on a potential energy landscape.
Within transition state theory, the leading-order contribution to the transition rate is the energy barrier between the minima.
One of the most popular methods for finding the energy barrier is to search for the minimum energy path (MEP) of the transition (see the review article
[
10
]
). This path is also interesting in its own right in that it provides modellers with insights into transition mechanisms.
The MEP can be viewed as the most probable path of the transition between the minima.
The energy barrier (at the saddle) along the MEP path can then be used to calculate the transition rate by using approximations such as harmonic transition state theory
[
2
,
9
,
21
]
.
The most widely used techniques for finding the MEP are the nudged elastic band (NEB) method
[
11
,
12
]
and the string method
[
5
,
6
,
20
]
.
They both iteratively evolve a discretised path of images in projected steepest descent directions, while keeping a smooth distribution of the images along the path
(see e.g.
[
3
,
7
,
8
,
22
]
).

To understand the rationale of modeling, the efficiency and the accuracy of numerical algorithms from a theoretical point of view, the stability of MEPs plays an important role.
In particular, it is usually required that a small perturbation (or a good approximation) of the energy landscape does not lead to a large change in the MEP.
In spite of the importance of the stability result, there was very limited work on this aspect.
In
[
4
]
, the authors investigated the MEP from a dynamical systems point of view and studied the evolution of the path by the string method, establishing that the limiting curve is indeed MEP under certain conditions.
In
[
14
]
, the authors showed that the MEP is uniformly and asymptotically stable is the sense that any curve near the MEP can be arbitrarily close to it in the Hausdorff distance under the gradient decent dynamics with long enough evolution time.
Based on this stability, they also proved the convergence of the simplified and improved string method with respect to the time step and number of images on the curve.
Neither of these stability results are strong enough to derive convergence
rates
.

The purpose of this paper is to derive a “strong” stability result for the MEP.
The novelty of our approach lies in that, we reformulate the MEP as the the root of an abstract operator, which is carefully constructed such that its linearisation becomes an isomorphism between appropriate function spaces.
With this approach, we can derive the stability that enables us to utilize powerful generic perturbation results such as the implicit function theorem to study the MEP.
In particular, it implies that a small perturbation of the energy landscape will lead to only a small deviation of the MEP, which shed lights on the understanding of both theoretical and numerical aspects for the MEP. Our analysis also lies the foundation for our approximation error analysis in
[
16
]
.

2
Main results

2.1
The Minimum energy path and its stability

Let
E:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a potential energy functional, where the configuration space
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
and the norm
|\cdot|:=\sqrt{(\cdot,\cdot)}
.
A configuration
y\in\mathcal{H}
could encode an atomic configuration, a crystalline structure, a phase field, and many other examples.
Throughout this paper, we will assume that
E\in C^{3}(\mathcal{H})
.
This regularity assumption is required because we need the Hessian
\nabla^{2}E
to be
C^{1}
in our analysis.

Given an energy functional
E
, we call
y\in\mathcal{H}
a critical point if
\nabla E(y)=0
.
We call a critical point
y
a strong local minimizer if the Hessian
\nabla^{2}E(y)\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})
is positive definite, which means
there is a positive constant
C>0
such that

\big(\nabla^{2}E(y)u,u\big)\geq C|u|^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall~u\in\mathcal{H}.

(2.1)

We call a critical point
y
an index-1 saddle point if
\nabla^{2}E(y)
has exactly one negative eigenvalue while the rest spectrum are positive.
That is to say, there exist
\lambda_{1}<0
and
v_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla^{2}E(y)v_{1}=\lambda_{1}v_{1},\quad{\rm and}\\[4.30554pt]
&\big(\nabla^{2}E(y)u,u\big)\geq C|u|^{2}\qquad\forall\quad u\in\mathcal{H},\quad(u,v_{1})=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

For the sake of brevity, we will omit the qualifiers “strong” and “index-1” and simply say “local minimizer” and “saddle point”.
We assume throughout that
E
has at least two local minimizers on the energy landscape, denoted by
y^{A}_{M}\in\mathcal{H}
and
y^{B}_{M}\in\mathcal{H}
respectively.

A minimum energy path (MEP) is a curve
\varphi\in C^{1}\big([0,1];\mathcal{H}\big)
connecting
y^{A}_{M}
and
y^{B}_{M}
whose tangent is everywhere parallel to the gradient except at the critical points.
To give a rigorous definition of the MEP, we first introduce the projection operators
P_{v}~,P_{v}^{\perp}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, given a
v\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}
,

P_{v}y:=\Big(y,\frac{v}{|v|}\Big)\frac{v}{|v|}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm and}\hskip 20.00003ptP^{\perp}_{v}:=I-P_{v},

where
I
is the identity operator.
We first define the admissible class for
“regular” curves connecting the minimizers
y^{A}_{M}
and
y^{B}_{M}
as

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}:=\Big\{\varphi\in C^{1}\big([0,1];\mathcal{H}\big):~\varphi(0)=y^{A}_{M},~\varphi(1)=y^{B}_{M},~\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\neq 0~~\forall~\alpha\in[0,1]\Big\}.

Then, a MEP connecting the minimizers
y^{A}_{M}
and
y^{B}_{M}
is a solution of the following problem: Find
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
such that

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0ptP^{\perp}_{\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)}\nabla E\big(\varphi(\alpha)\big)={0}\hskip 25.83325pt~\forall~\alpha\in[0,1],

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\Gamma(\varphi)=0,

(2.3b)

where the operator
\Gamma:C^{1}([0,1];\mathcal{H})\rightarrow C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
is given by

\Gamma(\varphi)(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{\alpha}|\varphi^{\prime}(s)|~{\rm d}s-\alpha\int_{0}^{1}|\varphi^{\prime}(s)|~{\rm d}s\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}~\alpha\in[0,1].

Note that (
2.3a
) indicates that the gradient
\nabla E\big(\varphi(\alpha)\big)
vanishes in the subspace perpendicular to the tangent
\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)
, which is well-defined since
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
.
Further, (
2.3b
) enforces the curve to be parameterized by normalized arc length, which removes the redundancy due to re-parameterization.

If
\bar{\varphi}
is a solution of (
2.3
), since
y^{A}_{M}
and
y^{B}_{M}
are local minimizers, there exists an
\bar{\it s}\in(0,1)
with
y_{S}=\bar{\varphi}(\bar{\it s})\in\mathcal{H}
such that the energy
E(y_{S})
reaches the maximum along the MEP.
This implies that
\nabla E(y_{S})
vanishes in the tangent direction
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})
and thus it is a critical point.
Since the energy
E(y_{S})
is a maximum along the tangent
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})
, we generically expect that the Hessian
\nabla^{2}E(y_{S})
has at least one negative eigenvalue.
For the sake of simplicity of the analysis, we will assume throughout this paper that

(A)

y_{M}^{A},~y_{S},~y_{M}^{B}
are the
only
critical points along the MEP
\bar{\varphi}
. Moreover,
y^{A}_{M}
and
y^{B}_{M}
are strong minimizers, while
y_{S}=\bar{\varphi}(\bar{\it s})
is an
index-1 saddle
.

Although
(A)
is natural and will be satisfied by
many
(if not most) MEPs one encounters in practice, there are also cases where this assumption fails.
For example, in
[
15
]
examples are given where there is more than one
index-1 saddle
along an MEP.
Our theory can be generalized to these cases by adjusting the formulations, provided that all critical points along the MEP satisfy certain stability conditions.
On the other hand, the strong stability assumption on the critical points cannot be readily weakened.

We observe by a direct calculation (see Lemma
3.1
) that, if
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{2}\big([0,1];\mathcal{H}\big)
solves (
2.3
), then
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(0)
,
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})
and
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(1)
are eigenvectors of the Hessians
\nabla^{2}E(y^{A}_{M})
,
\nabla^{2}E(y_{S})
and
\nabla^{2}E(y^{B}_{M})
, respectively.
This implies that the MEP has to go through the critical points in the direction of some eigenvector of the corresponding Hessian.
The following assumption formalizes the requirement that
there is a unique optimal path to exit the energy minimizer.

(B)
Let
\sigma_{A},\sigma_{B}
be the eigenvalues associated, respectively, with the eigenvectors
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(0)
and
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(1)
.
We assume that
(i) they are lower bounds of the spectrum of
\nabla^{2}E(y^{A}_{M})
and
\nabla^{2}E(y^{B}_{M})
, respectively; and
(ii) they are simple and isolated eigenvalues.

Next, we rewrite the MEP equation (
2.3
) in a form more convenient for our analysis.
Let

\displaystyle Y:=\Big\{f\in C\left([0,1];\mathcal{H}\right):~f(0)=f(1)={0},~f~\text{is differentiable at}~\alpha=0,\bar{\it s},1\Big\}

(2.4)

be the image space equipped with the following norm:

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{Y}:=\left\lVert\frac{f(\alpha)}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}\right\rVert_{C((0,1);\mathcal{H})}+\left\lVert\frac{f(\alpha)-f(\bar{\it s})}{\alpha-\bar{\it s}}\right\rVert_{C([0,\bar{\it s})\cup(\bar{\it s},1];\mathcal{H})}.

(2.5)

Then, we define

\mathscr{F}:\mathscr{A}\rightarrow Y
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(\varphi)(\alpha):=P^{\perp}_{\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)}\nabla E\big(\varphi(\alpha)\big)-\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)|-L(\varphi)}{L(\varphi)}P_{\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)}\nabla E\big(\varphi(\alpha)\big)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)|\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)|}{\bar{\it s}(\bar{\it s}-1)L(\varphi)}P_{\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)}\nabla E\big(\varphi(\bar{\it s})\big)+(\sigma_{A}+\sigma_{B})\left(\Gamma(\varphi)(\alpha)-\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\bar{\it s}(\bar{\it s}-1)}\Gamma(\varphi)(\bar{\it s})\right)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)}{\lvert\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\rvert},

for
\alpha\in[0,1]
, where
L:C^{1}([0,1];\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the length function,

L(\varphi):=\int_{0}^{1}\lvert\varphi^{\prime}(s)\rvert~{\rm d}s.

The weights
\alpha^{-1}(\alpha-1)^{-1},~(\alpha-\bar{\it s})^{-1}
in the
Y
-norm (
2.5
) and in the construction of
\mathscr{F}
are crucial for the stability analysis.
We will provide an intuitive explanation in Section
2.2
, why
\mathscr{F}
is formulated like this and how it affects the stability of MEP.
The following lemma shows that the range of
\mathscr{F}
is in
Y
, the proof of which is given in Section
3
.

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathscr{F}(\varphi)\in Y
for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
.

Now we can rewrite the MEP equation as follows:
Find
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
such that

\mathscr{F}(\varphi)=0.

(2.7)

The first term in (
2.6
) is perpendicular to the tangent direction
\varphi^{\prime}
, which is exactly the same as the left-hand side of (
2.3a
).
The three remaining terms in (
2.6
) are parallel to the tangent
\varphi^{\prime}
and are designed to enforce the curve to be parameterized by normalized arc length.
We can show some equivalence of (
2.3
) and (
2.7
) in the following lemma, whose proof is given in Section
3
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathscr{A}
solves (
2.3
) and
(A)
is satisfied. Then
\bar{\varphi}
is a solution of (
2.7
).
Moreover, let
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
be a solution of (
2.7
) and let

\tilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\alpha}|\varphi^{\prime}(s)|~{\rm d}s}{L(\varphi)}\right):=\varphi(\alpha)\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}~\alpha\in[0,1],

(2.8)

then
\tilde{\varphi}\in\mathscr{A}
and solves (
2.3
).

Since
E\in C^{3}
, we have that
\mathscr{F}
is
C^{1}
in a neighbourhood of
\mathscr{A}
.
We will denote the first variation of
\mathscr{F}
by
\delta\mathscr{F}:X\rightarrow Y
with

X:=\Big\{\psi\in C^{1}([0,1];\mathcal{H}):~\psi(0)=\psi(1)=0\Big\},

equipped with the norm
\|\psi\|_{X}:=\|\psi\|_{C^{1}([0,1];\mathcal{H})}
.
The detailed expression for
\delta\mathscr{F}
will be given in Section
3
.
The following theorem is the main result of this paper, stating the continuity and stability of
\delta\mathscr{F}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{2}\big([0,1];\mathcal{H}\big)
solves (
2.7
) and
(A)
is satisfied.

(i)

Then there exists a
\delta_{0}>0
such that for
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(\bar{\varphi})\subset\mathscr{A}
,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathscr{F}(\varphi_{1})-\mathscr{F}(\varphi_{2})\rVert_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\lVert\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\rVert_{X}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}

\displaystyle\lVert\delta\mathscr{F}(\varphi_{1})-\delta\mathscr{F}(\varphi_{2})\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\lVert\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\rVert_{X}.

where
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
are positive constants that depend only on
\delta_{0},~E
and
\bar{\varphi}
.

(ii)

If
(B)
is also satisfied, then
\delta\mathscr{F}(\bar{\varphi})
is an isomorphism.
In particular, there exists a constant
\gamma>0
depending only on
E
and
\bar{\varphi}
such that

\lVert\delta\mathscr{F}(\bar{\varphi})^{-1}\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(Y,X)}\leq\gamma.

(2.9)

Remark 2.1
.

The condition
(B)
is necessary for the stability of MEP since it enforces a unique direction along which the MEP can leave the minimizers, i.e. along the lowest-lying eigenvector
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}
.
If the corresponding eigenvalue at the minimizer is degenerate, then any perturbation within the eigenspace of this degenerated eigenvalue (which could be relatively large) may give rise to very small force.
In Section
3.4
, we provide an example to demonstrate the loss of stability due to this degeneracy.
Moreover, if
\sigma_{A}
or
\sigma_{B}
are not the lowest eigenvalues, then a curve may favor leaving the minimizers along the lowest-lying eigenvector (which is not consistent with
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}
).
We provide in Section
3.4
an example when
\delta\mathscr{F}(\bar{\varphi})
is not an isomorphism in such a situation.

We also remark on the, maybe surprising, fact that we did not require any assumption of stability of the potential energy orthogonal to the path (e.g., positivity of the hessian in directions normal to the MEP). While such a condition would be physically natural it is not required in our stability analysis since the stability is obtained via integrating a carefully formulated ODE along the path.

2.2
Motivation of the stability analysis

In this subsection, we provide a motivation behind the construction of
\mathscr{F}
and the weighted
Y
-norm.
Moreover, we will give an intuitive explanation on why the stability result in Theorem
2.1
(ii) can hold.
Our stability result means that for a curve near the MEP, its deviation to the MEP can be controlled by its force under appropriate norms.
We equip the curve with the
C^{1}
-norm and the force with the
Y
-norm (
2.5
), and formulate the stability of MEP as

\|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}\|_{X}\leq C\|\mathscr{F}(\varphi)\|_{Y}

for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
in some neighborhood of
\bar{\varphi}
.

The deviation in
C^{1}
-norm of a curve is determined by its oscillation and parameterization.
A key observation is that for a regular path
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
, even if the force on the path has a small
C
-norm, it is still possible that the curve has strong oscillations near the critical points,
y_{M}^{A},~y_{S},~y_{M}^{B}
(see Figure
2.1
for a schematic plot).
However, replacing the
C
-norm with a suitably weighted variant, namely the
Y-
norm (
2.5
), prevents such oscillations.

Figure 2.1:
MEP (red line) and a curve with oscillations near the critical points (solid black curve). The force in the
C
-norm,
\|\mathscr{F}(\varphi)\|_{C([0,1];\mathcal{H})}
, is small despite of large deviation in
C^{1}
-norm,
\|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}\|_{C^{1}([0,1];\mathcal{H})}
.

To see this, we first consider the deviation at the minimizers at
\alpha=0,1
. We obtain by a direct computation that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathscr{F}(\varphi)(\alpha)}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}=P_{\varphi^{\prime}(0)}^{\perp}\nabla^{2}E(y^{A}_{M})\varphi^{\prime}(0)-\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}(0)|-L(\varphi)}{L(\varphi)}P_{\varphi^{\prime}(0)}\nabla^{2}E(y^{A}_{M})\varphi^{\prime}(0).

(2.10)

Recall that the MEP goes through the minimizer
y^{A}_{M}
in the direction of an eigenvector of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}E(y^{A}_{M})
.
The first term of the right hand side in (
2.10
) is perpendicular to tangent
\varphi^{\prime}(0)
and it can prevent the oscillation at the minimizer
y^{A}_{M}
.
The second term of the right hand side in (
2.10
) is parallel to the tangent and it can control the parameterization by the coefficient
|\varphi^{\prime}(0)|-L(\varphi)
.

We turn to the deviation at the saddle at
\alpha=\bar{\it s}
. We observe from (
2.6
) that

\frac{\mathscr{F}(\varphi)(\bar{\it s})}{\bar{\it s}(\bar{\it s}-1)}=\frac{\nabla E\big(\varphi(\bar{\it s})\big)}{\bar{\it s}(\bar{\it s}-1)},

which restricts the distance between
\varphi(\bar{\it s})
and the saddle
y_{S}
.
Then, we obtain from a direct computation that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\bar{\it s}}\frac{\mathscr{F}(\varphi)(\alpha)-\mathscr{F}(\varphi)(\bar{\it s})}{\alpha-\bar{\it s}}=P_{\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})}^{\perp}\nabla^{2}E\big(\varphi(\bar{\it s})\big)\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})\\[4.30554pt]
-\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})|-L(\varphi)}{L(\varphi)}P_{\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})}\nabla^{2}E\big(\varphi(\bar{\it s})\big)\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})+(\sigma_{A}+\sigma_{B})\big(|\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})|-L(\varphi)\big)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})}{|\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})|}.\quad

(2.11)

With the fact that
\varphi(\bar{\it s})
is near the saddle
y_{S}
, the first term of the right hand side in (
2.11
) could prevent the oscillation at the saddle.
Besides, since the corresponding eigenvalue of
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})
is
\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}(\bar{\it s})<0
(see Lemma
3.1
(ii)) while
\sigma_{A},\sigma_{B}>0
, the second and third terms of the right hand side in (
2.11
) could control the parameterization at
\bar{\it s}
.

For the deviation between the critical points, since the gradient never vanishes, the component of the force perpendicular to the tangent
P_{\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}}^{\perp}\nabla E(\bar{\varphi})
could control the angle between the tangent and the gradient.
Thus it can prevent the oscillation. Moreover, the tangential force will naturally bound the parameterization.

2.3
Applications

The stability result in Theorem
2.1
implies in particular that a small perturbation of the potential energy model gives rise to a small perturbation in the resulting MEP. In this section we state a concrete corollary explaining this statement in more detail. But one can expect even more general consequences for the stability of the MEP under perturbations of the entire MEP equations which in particular leads to novel analyses of the nudged elastic band and string methods that are explored in full detail in
[
16
]
.

Fix an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}:=\bigcup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}B_{\epsilon}(\bar{\varphi}(\alpha))
where
B_{\epsilon}(y):=\{\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{H}:~|\tilde{y}-y|\leq\epsilon\}
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{\delta}
be a sequence of subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
and let
E_{\delta}\in C^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\delta};\mathbb{R}
) be an energy functional approximating
E|_{\mathcal{H}_{\delta}}
. We assume
consistency
of this perturbed model, that is,

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}}\inf_{y_{\delta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\delta}}|y_{\delta}-y|+\|E_{\delta}-E\|_{C^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\delta})}\rightarrow 0\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm as}~~\delta\rightarrow 0.

(2.12)

From assumption
(A)
, if
\delta
is sufficiently small (to ensure stability of the perturbed problem), then there exist two minimizers
y^{A}_{M,\delta},~y^{B}_{M,\delta}
of
E_{\delta}
satisfying

\displaystyle|y^{A}_{M,\delta}-y^{A}_{M}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|E_{\delta}-E\|_{C^{1}(\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\delta})}+C\inf_{y_{\delta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\delta}}|y_{\delta}-y^{A}_{M}|,

(2.13)

\displaystyle|y^{B}_{M,\delta}-y^{B}_{M}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|E_{\delta}-E\|_{C^{1}(\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\delta})}+C\inf_{y_{\delta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\delta}}|y_{\delta}-y^{B}_{M}|.

Define

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\delta}:=\Big\{\varphi\in C^{1}\big([0,1];\mathcal{H}_{\delta}\big):~\varphi(0)=y^{A}_{M,\delta},~\varphi(1)=y^{B}_{M,\delta},~\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\neq 0~~\forall~\alpha\in[0,1]\Big\}.

Then, a MEP in the perturbed model, connecting
y^{A}_{M,\delta}
and
y^{B}_{M,\delta}
, is the solution of the following problem: Find
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}_{\delta}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{\perp}_{\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)}\nabla E_{\delta}\big(\varphi(\alpha)\big)={0}\hskip 25.83325pt~\forall~\alpha\in[0,1],\\[4.30554pt]
&\Gamma(\varphi)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.14)

Corollary 2.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+(\sigma_{A}+\sigma_{B})\left(\delta\rho(\varphi)\psi\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{|\varphi^{\prime}|}+\rho(\varphi)\frac{P_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{\perp}\psi}{|\varphi^{\prime}|}\right),

3.3

In order to show that the operator

Given

Finally, we derive the stability (

As the linearized MEP operator (

\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}

\bar{\varphi}^{\prime\perp}

Let
\boldsymbol{t}:=\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}/|\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\perp}(\alpha):=\big\{y\in\mathcal{H}:~\big(y,\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\alpha)\big)=0\big\}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}~\alpha\in[0,1].

Since
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{2}\big([0,1];\mathcal{H}\big)

For completeness of the presentation, we briefly review the semigroup theory, starting with the definition of a sectorial operator.

Definition 3.1
.

[
17
, Definition 2.0.1]

A linear operator
A
on a Banach space
\mathcal{B}
is a sectorial operator if there are constants
\omega\in\mathbb{R},~\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi),~M>0
such that

\displaystyle\rho(A)\supset S_{\omega,\theta}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda\neq\omega,~|{\rm arg}(\lambda-\omega)|<\theta\}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm and}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|(\lambda I-A)^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{B})}\leq\frac{M}{|\lambda-\omega|}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall~\lambda\in S_{\omega,\theta}.

For
t>0
, (
3.19
) allows us to define a linear bounded operator
e^{tA}
on
\mathcal{B}

e^{tA}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\omega+\gamma_{r,\theta^{\prime}}}e^{t\lambda}(\lambda I-A)^{-1}~{\rm d}\lambda,

(3.20)

where
r>0
,
\theta^{\prime}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)
and
\gamma_{r,\theta^{\prime}}
is a contour
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:~|\text{arg}\lambda|=\theta^{\prime},~|\lambda|\geq r\}\cup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:~|\text{arg}\lambda|\leq\theta^{\prime},~|\lambda|=r\}
that oriented counterclockwise.
We also set

e^{0A}x=x\hskip 20.00003pt\forall~x\in\mathcal{B}.

(3.21)

Definition 3.2
.

[
17
, Definition 2.0.2]

Let
A\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{B})
be a sectorial operator.
The family
\{e^{tA}:~t\geq 0\}
defined by (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) is said to be the analytic semigroup generated by

in
\mathcal{B}
.

The following lemma presents some basic properties of the analytic semigroup.

Lemma 3.3

[

\displaystyle\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{B})}\leq Ce^{\omega t}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm and}\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{~{\rm d}}{~{\rm d}t}e^{tA}=Ae^{tA}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall~t>0.

Now we come back to analyze (

Define

\displaystyle\omega_{S}:=\inf\{\lambda:~\lambda\in\sigma\big(\nabla^{2}E(y_{S})\big),~\lambda>0\}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm and}

\displaystyle\omega_{B}:=\inf\{\lambda:~\lambda\in\sigma\big(\nabla^{2}E(y^{B}_{M})\big),~\lambda\neq\sigma_{B}\}.

3.4
Remarks on assumption
(B)

In Theorem
2.1
(ii), the condition that
\sigma_{A}
and
\sigma_{B}
are simple and the lowest eigenvalues is necessary for the stability result.
We provide two examples in the following, showing that if
\sigma_{A}
is a degenerated eigenvalue or not the lowest eigenvalue, then the stability result in Theorem
2.1
(ii) does not hold.

For simplicity of the presentations, we consider a finite dimensional configuration space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We first give the isometry
Q(\alpha)
.
Consider the eigenvalue problem

\Big(P^{\perp}_{\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\alpha)}\nabla^{2}E\big(\bar{\varphi}(\alpha)\big)P^{\perp}_{\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\alpha)}\Big)\xi_{i}(\alpha)=z_{i}(\alpha)\xi_{i}(\alpha)\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}~i=0,1,\cdots,N-1

(3.57)

with
\{z_{i}(\alpha)\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
the eigenvalues and
\{\xi_{i}(\alpha)\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
the corresponding eigenfunctions.
We order the functions
z_{i}(\alpha)
according to their values at
\alpha=\bar{\it s}
and their regularity with respect to
\alpha
, such that
z_{0}(\bar{\it s})\leq z_{1}(\bar{\it s})\leq\cdots\leq z_{N-1}(\bar{\it s})
and
z_{i}\in C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
for
i=0,1,\cdots,N-1
.
This can be done since
E\in C^{3}
and
P^{\perp}_{\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\alpha)}\nabla^{2}E\big(\bar{\varphi}(\alpha)\big)P^{\perp}_{\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(\alpha)}\in C^{1}\left([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}\right)
, which together with the perturbation theory for eigenvalue problems
[
13
, II-Theorem 6.8]
can lead to
z_{i}\in C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
.
Similarly, we have
\xi_{i}\in C^{1}\left([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
for
i=0,1,\cdots,N-1
.
Define
Q(\alpha):\mathcal{H}^{\perp}(\alpha)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
by

Q(\alpha)y:=\Big(\big(y,\xi_{1}(\alpha)\big),~\big(y,\xi_{2}(\alpha)\big),\cdots,\big(y,\xi_{N-1}(\alpha)\big)\Big)^{T}.

We see immediately that
Q(\alpha)
is an isometry for
\alpha\in[0,1]
and is differentiable at
0,\bar{\it s},1
.
We will now show that the assumption
(B)
is necessary for the stability result.

Assume that there exists
z_{j}(0)=\sigma_{A}=\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}(0)
with
1\leq j\leq N-1
(which is contradicted to the assumption that
\sigma_{A}
is simple).
Then we have from
E\in C^{3}
that there exists
\eta_{0}\in(0,\frac{\bar{\it s}}{2})
such that
\lVert z_{j}-\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}\rVert_{C([0,\eta_{0}];\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
.
Let

\beta_{n}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\eta_{0}-\alpha}{\eta_{0}}\right)^{n}\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)&{\rm in}~~[0,\eta_{0}]\\[4.30554pt]
0&{\rm in}~~(\eta_{0},1]\end{array},\right.\hskip 10.00002pt\psi_{n}(\alpha)=\beta_{n}(\alpha)\xi_{j}(\alpha)\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm and}\hskip 10.00002ptf_{n}=\delta\mathscr{F}(\bar{\varphi})\psi_{n}.

(3.58)

We have that
f_{n}(\alpha)=0
for
\alpha\in[\eta_{0},1]
.
For
\alpha\in[0,\eta_{0}]
we obtain, by using (
3.14
), (
3.17
) and (
3.57
), that

\displaystyle\xi_{j}(\alpha)^{T}f_{n}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=z_{j}(\alpha)\beta_{n}(\alpha)-\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\beta^{\prime}_{n}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\big(z_{j}(\alpha)-\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}(\alpha)\big)\Big(\frac{\eta_{0}-\alpha}{\eta_{0}}\Big)^{n}-\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)^{2}\frac{n(\eta_{0}-\alpha)^{n-1}}{\eta_{0}^{n}},

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(\alpha)^{T}f_{n}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\beta_{n}(\alpha)\xi_{i}(\alpha)^{T}\xi^{\prime}_{j}(\alpha)\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm for}~i=1,\cdots,N-1,~i\neq j,\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm and}

\displaystyle\xi_{0}(\alpha)^{T}f_{n}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{A}+\sigma_{B})\left(\hat{G}_{n}(\alpha)-\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\bar{\it s}(\bar{\it s}-1)}\hat{G}_{n}(\bar{\it s})\right)-\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\hat{G}^{\prime}_{n}(\alpha),

(3.59)

where
\hat{G}_{n}(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\beta_{n}(s)\xi_{0}(s)^{T}\xi^{\prime}_{j}(s)~{\rm d}s-\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\beta_{n}(s)\xi_{0}(s)^{T}\xi^{\prime}_{j}(s)~{\rm d}s
.
Using (
3.58
) and the facts that
0<\frac{\eta_{0}-\alpha}{\eta_{0}}<1
for
\alpha\in(0,\eta_{0})
,
\bar{\lambda}(0)=0
and
\|\hat{G}_{n}\|_{C^{1}([0,1];R)}\leq C\|\beta_{n}\|_{C([0,1];\mathbb{R})}
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|\hat{G}_{n}\|_{C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R})}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|\beta_{n}\|_{C([0,1];\mathbb{R})}=0.

Together with (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.1
(iii), this implies
\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{Y}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
.
Meanwhile, we have

\lVert\psi_{n}\rVert_{X}\geq\lVert\psi^{\prime}_{n}(0)\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}=\lVert\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}(0)\xi_{j}(0)\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}.

Therefore
\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{Y}
can not be uniformly bounded by
\lVert\psi_{n}\rVert_{X}
as
n
increases, and the stability result does not hold.

Assume that there exists
1\leq j\leq N-1
such that
z_{j}(0)<\sigma_{A}=\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}(0)
(which is contradicted to the assumption that
\sigma_{A}
is the lowest eigenvalue).
Let

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}:=\frac{z_{j}(0)}{\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}(0)}<1.

(3.60)

Let
\eta_{1}:=\frac{\bar{\it s}}{2}
and

\beta(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha^{\sigma_{j}}(\alpha-\eta_{1})^{2}&{\rm in}~~[0,\eta_{1}]\\[4.30554pt]
0&{\rm in}~~(\eta_{1},1]\end{array},\right.\hskip 10.00002pt\psi(\alpha)=\beta(\alpha)\xi_{j}(\alpha)\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm and}\hskip 10.00002ptf=\delta\mathscr{F}(\bar{\varphi})\psi.

From (
3.14
), (
3.17
) and (
3.57
), we obtain from a direct computation that

\displaystyle\xi_{j}(\alpha)^{T}f(\alpha)

\displaystyle=z_{j}(\alpha)\beta(\alpha)-\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\beta^{\prime}(\alpha)=\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\alpha^{\sigma_{j}}(\alpha-\eta_{1})\left(\left(\frac{z_{j}(\alpha)}{\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)}-\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\alpha}\right)(\alpha-\eta_{1})-2\right),

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(\alpha)^{T}f(\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\beta(\alpha)\xi_{i}(\alpha)^{T}\xi^{\prime}_{j}(\alpha)\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm for}~i=1,\cdots,N-1,~i\neq j\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm and}

\displaystyle\xi_{0}(\alpha)^{T}f(\alpha)

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{A}+\sigma_{B})\left(\hat{G}(\alpha)-\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\bar{\it s}(\bar{\it s}-1)}\hat{G}(\bar{\it s})\right)-\bar{\lambda}(\alpha)\hat{G}^{\prime}(\alpha),

where
\hat{G}(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\beta(s)\xi_{0}(s)^{T}\xi^{\prime}_{j}(s)~{\rm d}s-\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\beta(s)\xi_{0}(s)^{T}\xi^{\prime}_{j}(s)~{\rm d}s
.
Then applying Lemma
3.1
(iii) yields
\|f\|_{Y}<\infty
. However, using
\sigma_{j}<1
leads to

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\psi^{\prime}(\alpha)=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\beta^{\prime}(\alpha)\xi_{j}(0)=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma_{j}(1-\eta_{1})^{2}}{\alpha^{1-\sigma_{j}}}\xi_{j}(0)=\infty.

This implies that
f\in Y
has no preimage in
X
.
Therefore
\delta\mathscr{F}(\bar{\varphi}):X\rightarrow Y
is not a surjection, and thus not an isomorphism.

5
Conclusions

This paper provides a stability of the MEP, showing that the perturbation of a curve from the MEP can be controlled by the corresponding force under appropriate norms.
The stability result has many important consequences on both theoretical and numerical aspects for the MEP.
For example, we show that the MEP stays close to the original one within a small perturbation of the energy landscape or the discretization of the configuration space.
Our results also provide a crucial foundation for a convergence analysis of the string and NEB methods
[
16
]
.

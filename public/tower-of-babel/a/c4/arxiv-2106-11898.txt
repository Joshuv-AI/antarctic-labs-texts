Geometric Batyrev-Manin-Peyre for equivariant compactifications of additive groups
Loïs Faisant

Abstract
. Building on previous works by Bilu, Chambert-Loir and Loeser, we study the asymptotic behaviour of the moduli space of sections of a given family over a smooth projective curve, assuming that the generic fiber is an equivariant compactification of a finite dimensional vector space. Working in a suitable Grothendieck ring of varieties, we show that the class of these moduli spaces converges, modulo an adequate normalisation, to a non-zero effective element, when the class of the sections goes arbitrary far from the boundary of the dual of the effective cone. The limit can be interpreted as a motivic Euler product in the sense of Bilu’s thesis. This result provides a positive answer to a motivic version of the Batyrev-Manin-Peyre conjectures in this particular setting.

14H10, 14G10

Introduction

The link between the structure of higher dimensional algebraic varieties and the rational curves on such varieties has been fruitfully exploited during the last decades
[
17
,
12
]
.
In particular, this approach is relevant when one studies varieties admitting many rational curves, such as Fano varieties, by the work of Mori
[
18
]
.
Covering families of curves are mobile and it is known that the cone of mobile curves is exactly the dual of the effective cone, by the theorem of Boucksom, Demailly, Pǎun and Peternell
[
4
]
.
Starting from this remark,
a new direction consists in studying the asymptotic behaviour of the moduli space of curves on a variety,
when the class of the curves goes arbitrary far from the boundary of the dual of the effective cone.
This approach finds another source of inspiration in
the Batyrev-Manin-Peyre conjectures
[
1
,
19
]
which originally concern the estimation of the number of rational points of bounded height on an algebraic variety defined over a number field, when the bound tends to infinity.
It is then natural to consider analogous questions over function fields, which amounts to studying the morphisms from a given projective curve
C
to a given
sufficiently nice
variety, or more generally the sections of a family over
C
. Roughly speaking, in this context the notion of height translates into the notion of degree of a curve,
and the height zeta function one may introduce in the arithmetic setting becomes a motivic height zeta function; its coefficients are the classes, in a Grothendieck ring of varieties,
of the moduli spaces of sections of a given degree. The properties of such a generating series have been studied by Bourqui
[
5
]
in the case of toric varieties
and by Chambert-Loir-Loeser
[
7
]
and Bilu
[
2
]
in the case of compactifications of vector spaces.

In this paper we refine the study of the case of compactifications of vector spaces. More precisely, we study the asymptotic behaviour of the moduli space of sections of a family over a projective curve whose generic fibre is a compactification of a power of the additive group.
One can attach to such a section a class in the dual of the Picard group of the generic fibre, called the
multidegree
of the section, and consider the class of the moduli space of sections of a given multidegree in the Grothendieck ring of varieties.
The main result of this article is the following: if we normalise this last class by the class of the affine space of dimension the anticanonical degree of the curves, then it converges, when the multidegree tends to infinity
in a proper way
, to an effective element of the Grothendieck ring of varieties.
In comparison, previous results only provided information concerning the moduli space of sections with a given degree relatively to the generic log-anticanonical line bundle of the family.
By writing
in a proper way
, we mean that the multidegree goes arbitrary far from the boundary of the dual of the effective cone.
Furthermore, the limit of the normalised classes of moduli spaces can be written as a motivic Euler product in the sense of Bilu’s thesis
[
2
]
.
It has the expected form in comparison with the predictions of the Batyrev-Manin-Peyre conjectures in the arithmetic context
[
19
]
as well as in the geometric (or motivic) context
[
5
,
6
]
.
Our result also deals with the case of sections whose restriction to a fixed open subset of the curve has its image contained in a partial compactification of the vector space,
a condition which requires the introduction of additional finer numerical invariants in order to distinguish the relevant components of the moduli space.

Since we will use properties of a certain motivic height zeta function mentioned above, we adopt the framework of the last chapter of
[
2
]
which generalises the situation studied by Chambert-Loir and Loeser in
[
7
]
.

Theorem
.

Let
\pi:\mathscr{X}\to C
be a family as above, with
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
. Then

[M_{{\mathbf{n}}}]\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle}

converges in the completion of

for the weight topology, when
\min(n_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\to\infty
. Furthermore,
the limit is a non-zero effective element
which can be interpreted as a motivic Euler product in the sense of Bilu’s thesis
[
2
]
.

The condition
\min(n_{\alpha})\to\infty
actually means that the class of the sections in the dual of the effective cone goes arbitrary far from its boundaries in
\Pic(\mathscr{X})^{\vee}

(this class of curves is the
multidegree
mentioned before). However, note that this condition is not strong enough to fully distinguish components of the moduli space of sections when
\mathscr{U}
is not necessarily equal to
\mathscr{X}
: one has to introduce additional invariants, corresponding to local intersection degrees above

,
and take them to be arbitrarily large as well.
We refer to Notation
1.1
for the precise definition of such a motivic Euler product and to Proposition
4.12
and Theorem
4.15
for our most general result.
For example, still assuming in this introduction

and

, then the convergence can be understood as

[M_{\mathbf{n}}]\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbf{L}^{(1-g)\dim(X)}}{(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1})^{\rg(\Pic(X))}}\prod_{v\in C}\mathfrak{c}_{v}\qquad\text{when}\min_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(n_{\alpha})\to\infty

where for all but a finite number of places
v

\mathfrak{c}_{v}=(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1})^{\rg(\Pic(X))}\frac{[\mathscr{X}_{v}]}{\mathbf{L}^{\dim(X)}}.

We give in Remarks
3.6
and
4.13
a complete interpretation of those local factors in terms of motivic volume.

This work is based on the study of the multivariate motivic height zeta function

Z(\mathbf{T})=\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}}[M_{\mathbf{n}}]\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}}

where

\mathbf{T}
is the family of indeterminates
(T_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}
and thus
\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
stands for the product
\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}T_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}
. It is proven in
[
2
, Chapter 6]
that this function can be written as a sum over a space of characters.
From this decomposition, Bilu deduces a description of the first pole of the restriction of the motivic height zeta function to the line generated by the canonical line bundle.
Here, we push her method one step further to be able to distinguish the components given by all possible multidegrees.
In order to separate components arising from the integrality conditions, introducing new variables and refining several tools due to Bilu is found to be necessary.

In the first section of this paper we start by collecting some definitions and classical results about Grothendieck rings of varieties. We shortly recall what are symmetric products of classes in the Grothendieck ring of varieties, leading to Bilu’s definition of the motivic Euler product
[
2
]
. For completeness, we also add a motivic Fourier transform together with a motivic Poisson formula to this list of reminders.
The second section is devoted to a study of the convergence of such Euler products.
We briefly recall the main properties of the weight on the Grothendieck ring of varieties, include a useful convergence criterion for Euler products and quickly study sums of families in this context.
In the third part of this work we present a bunch of convergence results in the particular setting of compactifications of vector spaces.
The fourth and last section is the heart of the proof of our result. For clarity’s sake, we start with treating the case of the projective line and
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
, then generalise our argument to any smooth projective curve, and finally treat the general case
\mathscr{U}\subset\mathscr{X}
.

\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{R}
previously defined.

Bilu introduced in the third chapter of her thesis

the following notation.

Notation 1.1

(Motivic Euler product)
.

Consider a
R
-variety

and
\mathscr{X}=(X_{i})_{i\in I}
a family of elements of
\mathrm{A}_{X}
. Then the product

\prod_{x\in X/R}\left(1+\sum_{i\in I}X_{i,x}T_{i}\right)

is defined as a notation for the formal series

\sum_{\pi\in\mathbf{N}^{(I)}}[S^{\pi}(\mathscr{X}/R)]\mathbf{T}^{\pi}\in\mathrm{A}_{R}[[\mathbf{T}]]

where
\mathbf{T}=(T_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of indeterminates indexed by the set

and
\mathbf{T}^{\pi}
stands for
\prod_{i\in I}T_{i}^{n_{i}}
for every partition
\pi\in\mathbf{N}^{(I)}
.

The following proposition shows that this object behaves, in a sense,
like a product
.

Proposition 1.2

(§3.8.1 of
[
2
]
)
.

Let
R
be a variety,
X
be a
R
-variety and
\mathscr{X}=(X_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of elements of
\mathrm{A}_{X}
.

•

The Euler product notation is compatible with the
X=R
case:

\prod_{x\in R/R}\left(1+\sum_{i\in I}X_{i,x}T_{i}\right)=1+\sum_{i\in I}X_{i}T_{i}.

•

The Euler product notation is associative: if
X=U\cup Y
with
Y
a closed subscheme of

and

its complement, then

\prod_{x\in X/R}\left(1+\sum_{i\in I}X_{i,x}T_{i}\right)=\left(\prod_{u\in U/R}\left(1+\sum_{i\in I}X_{i,u}T_{i}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{y\in Y/R}\left(1+\sum_{i\in I}X_{i,y}T_{i}\right)\right)

when considering the Euler products of the restrictions
\mathscr{Y}=(X_{i}\times_{X}Y)_{i\in I}
and
\mathscr{U}={(X_{i}\times_{X}U)_{i\in I}}
.

•

As a consequence of the previous identity, the Euler product notation is compatible with finite products: if
X
is a finite disjoint union of varieties

, … ,
Y_{m}
isomorphic to
R
, and if

is the restriction of
\mathscr{X}
to

for
j=1,....,m
, then

\prod_{x\in X/R}\left(1+\sum_{i\in I}X_{i,x}T_{i}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(1+\sum_{i\in I}Y_{i,j}T_{i}\right).

Another illustration of this behaviour is the following non-trivial property of multiplicativity, which is a particular case of
[
2
, Proposition 3.9.2.4]
.
Note that in the remainder of the article, we will mostly restrict ourselves to the case
I=\mathbf{N}^{r}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
T_{i}=\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{m}}
, where
T_{1}
, …,
T_{r}
is a finite family of indeterminates and
\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{m}}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}T_{j}^{m_{j}}
for every
r
-tuple
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{j})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}
of non-negative integers.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
R
be a variety,
X
a variety over
R
,

\mathscr{A}=(A_{{\mathbf{m}}})_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}}
a family of effective elements of
\mathrm{A}_{R}
,
and
\mathscr{B}=(B_{{\mathbf{m}}})_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}}
a family of elements of
\mathrm{A}_{R}
such that
A_{\mathbf{0}}=B_{\mathbf{0}}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\prod_{x\in X/R}\left(\left(\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}}A_{{\mathbf{m}},x}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}\right)\left(\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}}B_{{\mathbf{m}},x}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{x\in X/R}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}}A_{{\mathbf{m}},x}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{x\in X/R}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}}B_{{\mathbf{m}},x}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}\right)\right)

in

1.4.
Motivic Fourier transform and Poisson formula in families

In this subsection we recall the motivic analogue of a bunch of Fourier analysis tools used in the sixth chapter of
[
2
]
, where an easy-to-handle expression of the motivic height zeta function is obtained by using a motivic Poisson formula. We will explicitly need this construction at the end of our proof (§
4.3
), where we will have to check an uniform convergence.
We follow
[
7
]
and the fifth chapter of
[
2
]
, which extends the scope of Hrushovski and Kazhdan’s motivic Poisson formula
[
15
]
.

1.4.1.
Local motivic Schwartz-Bruhat functions

The first building block of a
motivic
Poisson formula is a motivic analogue of classical Scwhart-Bruhat functions on the non-archimedian local field
K=k((t))
. One should keep in mind that this field can be thought about as the completion of the field of rational functions of a curve, for a valuation given by a closed point. The letter
t
denotes an uniformiser of this completion; then the ring of integers of
K
is
\mathcal{O}_{K}=k[[t]]
.

Recall that a classical Schwartz-Bruhat function
\varphi
on a locally compact, non-archimedian local field
L
is a locally constant and compactly supported function on
L
.
If
\varpi
is an uniformiser for
L
and
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is the ring of integers of
L
, then there exist integers
M\leqslant N
such that
\varphi
is zero outside
\varpi^{M}\mathcal{O}_{L}
and invariant modulo
\varpi^{N}\mathcal{O}_{L}
. The pair
(M,N)
is called the level of
\varphi
and the function
\varphi
on
L
can be seen as a function on the quotient
\varpi^{M}\mathcal{O}_{L}/\varpi^{N}\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

The motivic analogue of such a function, as it has been introduced by Hrushovski and Kazhdan in
[
15
]
, takes values in the Grothendieck ring
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{k}
. In order to deal with the fact that
k((t))
is not locally compact for the topology induced by the valuation, one takes as a first definition the previous properties concerning invariance and compact support: a motivic Schwartz-Bruhat function of level
(M,N)
is a function
t^{M}\mathcal{O}_{K}/t^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K}\to\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{k}
.
Through the identification

\begin{array}[]{rll}t^{M}\mathcal{O}_{K}/t^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K}&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{A}_{k}^{M-N}\\
a_{M}t^{M}+a_{M+1}t^{M}+...+a_{N-1}t^{N-1}&\longmapsto&(a_{M},...,a_{N-1})\end{array}

which endows
t^{M}\mathcal{O}_{K}/t^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K}
with the structure of a
k
-variety,
such a motivic Schwartz-Bruhat function on
K
is basically seen as an element of
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{A}_{k}^{N-M}}
. In what follows we will freely use the notation

\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}=\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(N-M)}.

for any
M\leqslant N
and non-negative integer
n
. The previous identification naturally extends to
K^{n}
, leading to the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
M\leqslant N
and
n\geqslant 1
be integers.
A (local) motivic Schwartz-Bruhat function of level
(M,N)
on
K^{n}
is an element of
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}}
.

The
extension-by-zero
morphism
t^{M}\mathcal{O}_{K}/t^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K}\to t^{M-1}\mathcal{O}_{K}/t^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K}
and the
cutting
morphism
t^{M}\mathcal{O}_{K}/t^{N+1}\mathcal{O}_{K}\to t^{M}\mathcal{O}_{K}/t^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K}
induce respectively a closed immersion
\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M,N)}\to\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M-1,N)}
and a trivial fibration
\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M,N+1)}\to\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M,N)}
(with fibre
\mathbf{A}_{k}^{1}
). Such morphisms in turn provide homomorphisms at the level of Grothendieck rings with exponentials, turning
(\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{A}^{n(M,N)}})_{M\leqslant N}
into a direct system, the direct limit of which is by definition the set of all motivic Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
K^{n}
.

Fourier kernel and transform.

Recall that we see
K=k((t))
as the completion
F_{v}
at a closed point
v
of the field of fractions
F=k(C)
of a smooth projective curve over
k
, together with the choice of an uniformiser
t
.
If one fixes a non-zero rational differential form
\omega\in\Omega_{K/k}
, then one obtains a non-zero
k
-linear map
r_{v}:K\to k
defined by sending any element
a\in K
to the residue at
v
of the rational form
a\omega
:

r_{v}(a)=\res_{v}(a\omega).

There exists a smallest integer
\nu
such that
r_{v}
vanishes on
t^{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{K}
, given by the order of the pole of
\omega
at
v
(the
conductor
of
r_{v}
). In particular,
r_{v}
is invariant modulo
t^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K}
for all
N\geqslant\nu
and can be seen as a linear function
r^{(M,N)}:\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M,N)}\to\mathbf{A}_{k}^{1}
for every
M\leqslant N
such that
N\geqslant\nu
.

The product
K\times K\to K
restricts to a morphism
\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M,N)}\times\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})}\to\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M+M^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime})}
for every
M\leqslant N
,
M^{\prime}\leqslant N^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime\prime}=\min(M^{\prime}+N,M+N^{\prime})
. If
N,N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}\geqslant\nu
it can be composed with
r_{v}^{(M+M^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime})}
and one obtains an element

r_{v}:\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M,N)}\times\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})}\overset{\cdot\,\times\,\cdot}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M+M^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime})}\overset{r_{v}^{(M+M^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime})}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{1}

of
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M,N)}\times_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})}}
, called the
Fourier kernel on
K
and written
\mathrm{e}(xy)
. This notation is the analogue of the exponential factor
e^{2i\pi xy}
of the integrand in classical Fourier analysis.

More generally, the pairing
\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
on
K^{n}
provides a morphism

\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}\times\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})}\overset{\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M+M^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime})}

which can be composed with
r_{v}^{(M+M^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime})}
to give the
Fourier kernel
\mathrm{e}(\langle x,y\rangle)
on
K^{n}
.

The Fourier transform of a motivic Schwart-Bruhat function
\varphi\in\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}}
of level
(M,N)
on
K^{n}
is defined as

(1.4.3)

\mathscr{F}\varphi(y)=\int_{K^{n}}\varphi(x)\mathrm{e}(\langle x,y\rangle)\mathrm{d}x

which is a notation for the class of
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)}}
given by

\mathscr{F}\varphi=\mathbf{L}^{-Nn}\varphi\times_{\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}}[\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}\times_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)},r]

where
r=r^{(M+\nu-N,N+\nu-M)}\circ\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
.
The formal variable
y
is thus
living in

\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)}
.
We can be more explicit when
\varphi=[U,f]
with
g:U\to\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}
and
f:U\to\mathbf{A}^{1}
; in this case
\mathscr{F}\varphi
is the class

\mathscr{F}\varphi=\mathbf{L}^{-Nn}[U\times_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)},f\circ\pr_{1}+r^{(M+\nu-N,N+\nu-M)}(\langle g\circ\pr_{1},\pr_{2}\rangle)]

in
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)}}
.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\times_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\times_{g}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}\times_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}\times_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(M,N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}_{k}^{n(\nu-N,\nu-M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(k)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}

Notation
1.4.3
can be seen as a variant of the exponential sum notation of §
1.1
.

From local functions to global ones and summation over rational points.

Such a construction easily extends to finite products
\prod_{s\in S}F_{s}
of completions of
F=k(C)
at a finite number of closed points
s\in S
of the curve
C
.
The motivic Schwartz-Bruhat functions of level
(M_{s},N_{s})_{s\in S}
are the elements of the ring

\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\prod_{s\in S}\Res_{\kappa(s)/k}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa(s)}^{n(M_{s},N_{s})}}

where
\Res_{\kappa(s)/k}
denotes the Weil restriction functor. The set of global motivic Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
K^{n}
is defined as a direct limit over the set
S
of closed points of
C
and one can easily extend the previous Fourier kernel and transform to this setting; we will not be more explicit about this construction for now, since it will be generalised in the next paragraph.

Nevertheless it might be enlightening for the reader to define the
sum over rational points
of a global motivic Schwartz-Bruhat is this simple setting. Let
\varphi
be a global Schwartz-Bruhat function of level
(M_{s},N_{s})_{s\in S}
where
S\subset C
is a finite set of closed points of
C
.
We choose an uniformiser
t_{s}
of
F_{s}
for every
s\in S
.
Consider the divisor
D=-\sum_{s\in S}M_{s}[s]
on
C
and remark that the embeddings
F\hookrightarrow F_{s}
map the Riemann-Roch space

L(D)=\Gamma(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(D))

(the space of global sections of the invertible sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{C}(D)
)
to
t_{s}^{M_{s}}\mathcal{O}_{s}
for every
s\in S
. This mapping provides a morphism of varieties
\theta:\mathcal{L}(D)^{n}\to\Res_{\kappa(s)/k}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa(s)}^{n(M_{s},N_{s})}
.
The sum over rational points of
\mathbf{A}^{n}

\sum_{x\in k(C)^{n}}\varphi(x)=\sum_{x\in L(D)^{n}}\theta^{*}\varphi

is by definition the image in
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{k}
of the pull-back
\theta^{*}\varphi_{S}
, that is, the image of
\varphi
through the map

\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\prod_{s\in S}\Res_{\kappa(s)/k}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa(s)}^{n(M_{s},N_{s})}}\overset{\theta^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathcal{L}(D)^{n}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{k}

which can be seen as another variant of the exponential sum notation of §
1.1
.
In this context, one gets a motivic Poisson formula

\sum_{x\in k(C)^{n}}\varphi(x)=\mathbf{L}^{(1-g)n}\sum_{y\in k(C)^{n}}\mathscr{F}\varphi(y)

(see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 1.3.10]
for a proof).

1.4.2.
Constructible families of Schwartz-Bruhat functions

Until the end of this subsection we follow
[
2
, Chapter 5]
.
We will say that a function
X\overset{f}{\to}\mathbf{Z}
on a
k
-variety is
constructible
if the inverse image of any integer by
f
is a constructible subset of
X
.

Again
C
is a smooth projective connected curve over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. Let
M,N:C\to\mathbf{Z}
be constructible functions such that
M\leqslant N
. Such functions are constant respectively equal to
M_{0}
and
N_{0}
on a dense open subset
U
of the curve
C
.
Then
\mathbf{A}_{C}^{(M,N)}
stands for the
C
-variety isomorphic to
U\times\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M_{0},N_{0})}
over
U
and with fibre over
u\notin U
equal to
\mathbf{A}_{k}^{(M_{u},N_{u})}
. Let
n
be a positive integer.
One can as well define the
n
-th product over
C

\mathbf{A}_{C}^{n(M,N)}=\underbrace{\mathbf{A}_{C}^{(M,N)}\times_{C}...\times_{C}\mathbf{A}_{C}^{(M,N)}}_{n\text{ times}}.

Until the end of this section we fix two constructible functions

a,b:C\to\mathbf{Z}
such that
a\leqslant 0\leqslant b
and we assume that there exists a dense open subset
U
of
C
such that
a_{|U}=b_{|U}=0
. Let
\Sigma
be the complement of
U
in
C
, it is a finite set of points.
Let

M=(M_{\iota})
and
N=(N_{\iota})
be families of non-negatives integers indexed by
\mathbf{N}^{p}
, such that
M_{\mathbf{0}}=N_{\mathbf{0}}=0
. This data provides a family

\mathscr{A}_{{\mathbf{n}}}(a,b,M,N)=S^{{\mathbf{n}}}\left(\left(\mathbf{A}_{C}^{n(a-M_{\iota},b+N_{\iota})}\right)_{\iota\in\mathbf{N}^{p}}\right)

for any

-tuple
{\mathbf{n}}
of non-negative integers, which is a variety over
S^{\mathbf{n}}C
.
Let us quickly describe the fibre of
\mathscr{A}_{{\mathbf{n}}}(a,b,M,N)\to S^{\mathbf{n}}C
over a schematic point

. The latter can be decomposed formally as

D=\sum_{v\in C}\iota_{v}v=\sum_{v\in U}\iota_{v}v+\sum_{v\in\Sigma}\iota_{v}v=D_{U}+D_{\Sigma}

where the
\iota_{v}
are
p
-tubles of non-negative integers (almost all equal to
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbf{N}^{p}
),

D_{U}=\sum_{v\in U}\iota_{v}v\in S^{\pi}U

for some partition

of some
{\mathbf{n}}^{U}\leqslant{\mathbf{n}}
and
{D_{\Sigma}=\sum_{v\in\Sigma}\iota_{v}v}
. Then, the fibre over
D
can be seen as the domain of definition of a Schwartz-Bruhat function
\Phi_{D}
, up to a finite extension of the residue field
\kappa(D)
of
D
(seen as a point of
S^{\mathbf{n}}C
)
[
2
, §5.3.2]
: it is of the form

(1.4.4)

\prod_{\iota\in\mathbf{N}^{p}}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa(D)}^{n_{\iota}^{U}\times n(M_{\iota}+N_{\iota})}\times_{\kappa(D)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\Sigma\\
\mathbf{i}_{v}\neq\mathbf{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa(D)}^{n(a_{v}-M_{\mathbf{i}_{v}},b_{v}+N_{\mathbf{i}_{v}})}.

Note that the first product is actually finite, since
(n_{\iota}^{U})_{\iota\in\mathbf{N}^{p}}
has finite support as a partition.

For this reason, elements of
\mathscr{E}\mathit{xp}\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{A}_{{\mathbf{n}}}(a,b,M,N)}
are called
constructible families of Schwartz-Bruhat functions of level

{\mathbf{n}}

[
2
, §5.3.4.1]
.
Two special cases arise. On one hand, if all the integers
N_{\iota}
are zero, the family is said to be
uniformly smooth
. On the other hand, if all the integers

are zero, the family is said to be
uniformly compactly supported

[
2
, §5.3.5.1]
.

1.4.3.
Motivic Fourier transform in families

It is possible to define the Fourier transform of a family

and

This allows one to define a constructible Fourier kernel morphism

.
It generalises the local and global Fourier transforms of a Schwartz-Bruhat function we presented in the first paragraph of this subsection.

1.4.4.
Local Poisson formula

Let

. A different choice of

Let

The divisor

w_{X}(\mathfrak{c}_{{\mathbf{m}}})<-\delta\langle{\mathbf{m}},\rho\rangle

{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}

On one hand, if

whenever

and
\omega^{\prime}=\max(w_{X}(\mathfrak{c}_{{\mathbf{m}}}\mid\langle{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime},\rho\rangle<i_{0}))

On the other hand, if
\langle{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime},\rho\rangle\geqslant i_{0}
then

Finally by Proposition
2.1

w_{X}\left(\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}\leqslant{\mathbf{m}}}\mathfrak{c}_{{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}\rangle}\right)<-\min\left(\frac{1}{2}\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle,\delta\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle\right)\leqslant-\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\delta\right)\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle

for all
{\mathbf{m}}
such that

Proposition 2.6
.

Fix a complex variety

Let

By definition of the motivic Euler product notation, for every

Since

So we fix

,
that is such that

Then

\displaystyle w(S^{\pi}\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle\leqslant w_{S^{\pi}X}(S^{\pi}\mathscr{X})+\dim(S^{\pi}X)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}n_{\mathbf{i}}w_{X}(X_{\mathbf{i}})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbf{N}^{r}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}n_{\mathbf{i}}w(X)

Proof of Proposition
3.8
.

Once again it is enough to check convergence on the dense subset
C_{1}\subset C
. In order to apply Proposition
2.6
, one has to bound the weight of the coefficients of
F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
. We start by doing this for
Z_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
.

First, the divisor of
f_{\xi}
can be written

\ddiv(f_{\xi})=\Xi_{\xi}-\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}d_{\alpha}(\xi)D_{\alpha}

where
\Xi_{\xi}
is the Zariski-closure of
\{\langle x,\xi\rangle=0\}
in
X
.
Over a place
v\in C_{1}
, one has
\mathscr{B}^{\mathscr{U}}_{v}=\{\beta_{v}\}
and the local factor
Z_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
can be written
Z_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\sum_{A\subset\mathscr{A}_{U}}Z_{v,A}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
with

Z_{v,A}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\int_{\Omega_{v}(A,\beta_{v})}\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{\ord x_{\alpha}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(x))\mathrm{d}x

as we already pointed out in Remark
3.3
.
It is possible to compute or at least bound the weight of this integral. We refer to
[
2
, §6.3.5.2]
for the details of such computations; in this proof we will restrict ourselves to giving a list of results we need to apply Proposition
2.6
. In particular we obtain weight-linear convergence of the Euler product
\prod_{v\in C_{1}}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
. In what follows
\mathscr{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}
is written
\mathscr{X}
for conciseness and the index
v
may be dropped.

If
A=\varnothing
then the integral above equals one.
If
A=\{\alpha\}
the intermediate step is to cut the integral into two pieces: one part corresponding to arcs with origin in the zero divisor
\Xi_{\xi}
of
f_{\xi}
and another part corresponding to arcs with origin outside
\Xi_{\xi}
.

\displaystyle Z_{v,\{\alpha\}}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{X},D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\setminus\Xi_{\xi})}\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{\ord x_{\alpha}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(x))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{X},D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi})}\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{\ord x_{\alpha}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(x))\mathrm{d}x

Then there are several cases to distinguish, according to the order of the pole of
f_{\xi}
at
D_{\alpha}
. On one hand, concerning arcs with origins outside
\Xi_{\xi}
, one has the following results.

•

If
d_{\alpha}(\xi)=0
then

\int_{\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{X},D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\setminus\Xi_{\xi})}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{\ord x_{\alpha}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(x))\mathrm{d}x=(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1})\frac{\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}}{1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}}[D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{-n+1}.

•

If
d_{\alpha}(\xi)=1
then

\int_{\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{X},D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\setminus\Xi_{\xi})}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{\ord x_{\alpha}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(x))\mathrm{d}x=-\mathbf{L}^{-2}[D^{\circ}_{\alpha}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{1-n}\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}.

Since
\dim(D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\setminus\Xi_{\xi})=n-1
, one has the following upper bound on the weight

w_{C_{1}}\left(-\mathbf{L}^{2}[D^{\circ}_{\alpha}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{1-n}\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}\right)\leqslant 2(\rho_{\alpha}-2)+1\leqslant 2\rho_{\alpha}-1.

•

If
d_{\alpha}(\xi)>1
then this integral equals zero.

On the other hand, concerning arcs with origin in
\Xi_{\xi}
, the corresponding integral can be rewritten

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{X},D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi})}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{\ord x_{\alpha}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(x))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{m}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi}\right)\times\mathscr{L}(\mathbf{A}^{1})\times\mathscr{L}(\mathbf{A}^{1},0)^{n-1}\\
\ord x=m\end{subarray}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(t^{m}x,\mathbf{y}))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}.

By (
1.5.3
), the weight of the coefficient of order
m
is smaller than the weight of the motivic volume

\displaystyle\vol\left(\{(w,x,\mathbf{y})\in\left(D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi}\right)\times\mathscr{L}(\mathbf{A}^{1})\times\mathscr{L}(\mathbf{A}^{1},0)^{n-1}\mid\ord x=m\}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi}\right]\mathbf{L}^{-m}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{L}^{-n+1}.

One has the upper bound on dimensions

\dim\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}m}[D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{-m}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{L}^{-n+1}\right)\leqslant m\rho_{\alpha}+(n-2)-m-(n-1)=m(\rho_{\alpha}-1)-1

and thus on weights

w_{C_{1}}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}m}[D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{-m}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{L}^{-n+1}\right)\leqslant 2(m(\rho_{\alpha}-1)-1)+\dim(C_{1}).

Setting
c=\underset{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}}{\max}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}}\right)
as in
[
2
]
, one gets
2\rho_{\alpha}-1\leqslant 2\rho_{\alpha}c
for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}
and

w_{C_{1}}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}m}[D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\cap\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{-m}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{L}^{-n+1}\right)\leqslant 2cm\rho_{\alpha}-1.

Now if
|A|\geqslant 2
the idea is similar. The corresponding local term is

Z_{v,A}=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{N}^{A}_{>0}}\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{m_{\alpha}}\int_{\ord x_{\alpha}=m_{\alpha}}\mathrm{e}(f_{\xi}(x,y))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

The volume of the constructible subsets of
\Omega_{v}(A)
over which integration is done is actually

\displaystyle\vol\left(\left\{(w,(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A},y)\in D_{A}^{\circ}\times\mathscr{L}(\mathbf{A}^{1})^{A}\times\mathscr{L}(\mathbf{A}^{1},0)^{n-|A|}\mid\ord x_{\alpha}=m_{\alpha}\text{ for every }\alpha\in A\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=[D_{A}^{\circ}]\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left(\mathbf{L}^{-m_{\alpha}}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)\right)\mathbf{L}^{-n+|A|}.

By definition of
D_{A}^{\circ}
, it has dimension at most
n-|A|
. Thus the dimension of the integral is at most
-\sum_{\alpha\in A}m_{\alpha}

and, using again the inequality of §
1.5.3
, the weight of the
{\mathbf{m}}
-th coefficient of
Z_{v,A}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
is bounded by

2\sum_{\alpha\in A}m_{\alpha}(\rho_{\alpha}-1)+\dim C_{1}\leqslant 2c\sum_{\alpha\in A}m_{\alpha}\rho_{\alpha}-1.

To summarize, we obtained

\displaystyle Z_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}\\
d_{\alpha}(\xi)\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}Z_{v,\alpha}(\mathbf{T},\xi)+\sum_{|A|\geqslant 2}Z_{v,A}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle Z_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle=1+\mathbf{L}^{-n}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}[D^{\circ}_{\alpha}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]\frac{\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}}{1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}}-\mathbf{L}^{-n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}(\xi)\\
d_{\alpha}(\xi)=1\end{subarray}}[D^{\circ}_{\alpha}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\text{ terms of weights bounded as in Proposition \ref{proposition:linear-conv-criterion}, locally uniformly in $\xi$}

The last step of the proof consists in multiplying by
\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}\right)
. This operation does not affect the bounds we need in order to apply Proposition
2.6
, since
\rho_{\alpha}-1\leqslant c\rho_{\alpha}
,
and allows one to control the diverging term

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}[D^{\circ}_{\alpha}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]\frac{\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}}{1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}}

in the expression of
Z_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
.
One gets

\displaystyle F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}+\mathbf{L}^{1-n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}[D^{\circ}_{\alpha}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}+P_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\left([D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]-\mathbf{L}^{n-1}\right)\mathbf{L}^{1-n}\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}+P_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

where

is a Laurent series consisting of terms satisfying the bounds of Proposition
2.6
for

c=\underset{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}}{\max}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}}\right).

Finally, by Proposition
2.1
one has for every

w_{C_{1}}\left(\left([D_{\alpha}^{\circ}\setminus\Xi_{\xi}]-\mathbf{L}^{n-1}\right)\mathbf{L}^{1-n}\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}\right)\leqslant 2(n-1)-2(n-1)+2(\rho_{\alpha}-1)=2(\rho_{\alpha}-1).

Our analysis shows that we can apply Proposition
2.6
with

,

,

arbitrary and

4.1.1.

\mathbf{m}=(m_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}

of integers,
one gets the finite sum

\overline{\mathfrak{a}}_{\mathbf{m}}=\frac{1}{\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)^{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{m}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\times\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle}.

After normalisation by
\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}
,
this is actually the
{\mathbf{m}}
-th partial sum of the Euler motivic product

\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}F_{v}\left(\left(\mathbf{L}^{-\rho_{\alpha}}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}},0\right)
.
Therefore by Proposition
3.5

\overline{\mathfrak{a}}_{\mathbf{m}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}

converges in
\widehat{\mathscr{M}_{k}}
to

\frac{1}{\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1}\right)^{r}}\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}F_{v}\left(\left(\mathbf{L}^{-\rho_{\alpha}}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}},0\right)

when
\min_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(m_{\alpha})
tends to infinity.

This heuristic argument given, now we have to justify that this is indeed the limit of

\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}[M_{U,{\mathbf{m}}}]
(up to a factor
\mathbf{L}^{n}
)
in
\widehat{\mathscr{M}_{k}}

when
\min_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(m_{\alpha})

becomes infinitely large.
First we evaluate the error term we introduced,
then we check that terms corresponding to non-trivial characters do not constribute to the limit.
We postpone the summation over all characters to the last section of this paper,
where it will be performed in full generality.

4.1.2.
Contribution of the error term

In order to control the error term we implicitly introduced in the previous paragraph, we develop the denominator of
Z(\mathbf{T},0)
as follows. We still assume
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
. Then

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{T},0)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}Z_{\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{L}}\left(\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}}-\frac{\mathbf{L}}{1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\mathbf{L})^{r}}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}}}\frac{(-\mathbf{L})^{|\mathscr{A}|-|\varepsilon|}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)}.

where
|\varepsilon|=\sum_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}
.
Let us introduce a few convenient notations.
For any
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}}
we define

G_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T})=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha})^{-1}=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}}\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}

so that
G(\mathbf{T},0)
admits a decomposition of the form

G(\mathbf{T},0)=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}}}\frac{(-\mathbf{L})^{r-|\varepsilon|}}{(1-\mathbf{L})^{r}}G_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T}).

Now one easily sees that the term of multidegree
{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}
of

G_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T})\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},0)

is the finite sum

\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{m}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle}

from which we deduce

\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{m}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle}.

The case
\varepsilon=\mathbf{0}
has been studied in the previous paragraph and it suffices to apply Lemma
2.5
to
\sum\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle}
in case
\varepsilon\neq\mathbf{0}
.
We obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1

(
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
,
C=C_{0}=\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
,
\xi=0
)
.

There exists a decomposition

Z(\mathbf{T},0)=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}}\mathfrak{a}_{{\mathbf{m}}}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}=\ \sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}}}\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}}\mathfrak{a}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}

and
a real number
\delta>0

such that for all
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

w\left(\mathfrak{a}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\right)<-\delta\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle

while

\mathfrak{a}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\mathbf{0}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\underset{\rho\text{-weight-lin.}}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{(1-\mathbf{L}^{-1})^{\rg(\Pic(X))}}\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}F_{v}\left((\mathbf{L}^{-\rho_{\alpha}})_{\alpha_{\in}\mathscr{A}},0\right)

when
\min_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(m_{\alpha})
tends to infinity.

As an immediate corollary of this proposition,
the terms
\mathfrak{a}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}
, for
\varepsilon\neq\mathbf{0}
, are negligible when
\min_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(m_{\alpha})
becomes arbitrarily large,
in comparison with
\mathfrak{a}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\mathbf{0}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}
.

4.1.3.
Non-trivial characters

We now study the asymptotic contribution of the coefficients of
Z(\mathbf{T},\xi)
for
\xi\in G(F)^{\vee}
non-trivial. We still assume
C=\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
.

Recall that given a place
v
of
C
,
the linear form
x\mapsto\langle x,\xi\rangle
on
G_{F}

can be seen as a rational function
f_{\xi}
on
X
with poles contained in
\cup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}D_{\alpha}
. If
d_{\alpha}(\xi)
denotes the order of the pole of
f_{\xi}
with respect to
D_{\alpha}
, one can define a subset of
\mathscr{A}_{U}
by setting

\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)=\{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}\mid d_{\alpha}(\xi)=0\}.

The character
\xi
being non-trivial, the cardinality of this set is strictly smaller than
|\mathscr{A}|
.
Using Notation
3.7
,
the term of the motivic height Zeta function corresponding to the character
\xi
is

Z(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}\left(F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi_{v})\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}\right)^{-1}\right).

Again we restrict our analysis to the case
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
so that
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}_{U}
. The general case will be treated together with the case of a general curve.
For any
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}

we introduce

G_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{-1}.

Adapting the computation done in the previous paragraph, one gets

G(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha})^{-1}\right)=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\frac{(-\mathbf{L})^{|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)|-|\varepsilon|}}{(1-\mathbf{L})^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\right|}}G_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

and the coefficient of multidegree
{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}
of the product

G_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi_{v})

is

\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}}^{\xi}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{m}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}^{\xi}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}

where
\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}^{\xi}
is the coefficient of multidegree
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathscr{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}
of
\prod_{v\in\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
.
Then we consider the following normalisation:

\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}}^{\xi}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{m}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}^{\xi}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}-\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}.

Applying Lemma
2.5
to this sum for every
\varepsilon
, we get the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2

(
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
,
C=C_{0}=\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
,
\xi\neq 0
)
.

There exists a decomposition

Z(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}}\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\xi,\varepsilon}\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{m}}

and a positive real number
\delta_{\xi}>0
(which only depends on the
d_{\alpha}(\xi)
) such that for every
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{0}(\xi)}

w\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\xi,\varepsilon}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\right)<-\delta_{\xi}\left(\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}+\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\right)

when
\min(m_{\alpha})\to\infty
.

Since
\mathscr{A}_{U}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)
is non-empty for every
\xi\neq 0
, the inequality of this last proposition means that the normalised terms
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\xi,\varepsilon}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}
coming form
Z(\mathbf{T},\xi)
are negligible in comparison with the main term
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathbf{0}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}
coming from
Z(\mathbf{T},0)
.
Furthermore we have a linear bound on the weight which is uniform on a finite partition of the space of characters
by Remark
3.9
.

4.2.
The case of a general curve

From now on we assume that
C
is a projective smooth irreducible curve of genus
g
over
k
having a
k
-rational point.
In this case, for
m\geqslant 2g-1
the class of
\Sym^{m}(C)
in
\mathrm{KVar}_{k}
is
[\Pic^{0}(C)][\mathbf{P}^{m-g}_{k}]

(it follows from Riemann-Roch and Serre’s duality, see
[
8
, Chapter 7, Example 1.1.10]
)
and the Kapranov zeta function of
C
is still rational (see
[
16
, Theorem 1.1.9]
or
[
8
, Chapter 7, Theorem 1.3.1]
). We have

Z_{C}^{\text{Kap}}(T)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{N}}[\Sym^{m}(C)]T^{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{2(g-1)}[\Sym^{m}(C)]T^{m}+[\Pic^{0}(C)]\sum_{m\geqslant 2g-1}\frac{\mathbf{L}^{m-g+1}-1}{\mathbf{L}-1}T^{m}

(where the first sum is empty if
g=0
).
Consider for any
\xi\in G(F)^{\vee}

G(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{v\in C}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha})^{-1}

(remark that if
\xi=0
then
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)=\mathscr{A}_{U}
).
By Proposition
1.3
one has

G(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}Z_{C}^{\text{Kap}}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}\right)

In order to easily adapt the computations done before, we may assume
g\geqslant 1
, replace
Z_{C}^{\text{Kap}}
by

\widetilde{Z^{\text{Kap}}_{C}}(T)=\sum_{m\geqslant 0}[\Pic^{0}(C)]\frac{\mathbf{L}^{m-g+1}-1}{\mathbf{L}-1}T^{m}

in the expression of
G(\mathbf{T},\xi)
and then control the error term coming from this slight modification. If we do so, then we obtain

Z(\mathbf{T},\xi)=G(\mathbf{T},\xi)\prod_{v\in C}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\widetilde{G}(\mathbf{T},\xi)\prod_{v\in C}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)+H(\mathbf{T},\xi)\prod_{v\in C}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

where

(4.2.10)

\widetilde{G}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\widetilde{Z_{C}^{\text{Kap}}}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}\right)

and

(4.2.11)

H(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}Z_{C}^{\text{Kap}}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}\right)-\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\widetilde{Z_{C}^{\text{Kap}}}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}\right).

We then put

(4.2.12)

\widetilde{Z}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\widetilde{G}(\mathbf{T},\xi)\prod_{v\in C}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi).

4.2.1.
Main term

Basically, the contribution of
\widetilde{Z}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
has already been treated in §
4.1.2
for the case
\xi=0
and in §
4.1.3
for non-trivial characters. The only difference with the particular case of the projective line is a factor
[\Pic^{0}(C)]\mathbf{L}^{(1-g)}
. Indeed, starting from (
4.2.10
) one gets

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\frac{[\Pic^{0}(C)]}{1-\mathbf{L}}\left(\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha}}-\frac{\mathbf{L}^{1-g}}{1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{[\Pic^{0}(C)]^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\right|}}{(1-\mathbf{L})^{|\mathscr{A}|}}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\frac{\left(-\mathbf{L}^{1-g}\right)^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\right|-|\varepsilon|}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{[\Pic^{0}(C)]\mathbf{L}^{1-g}}{\mathbf{L}-1}\right)^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\right|}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}(-1)^{|\varepsilon|}\widetilde{G_{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{T},\xi)\mathbf{L}^{|\varepsilon|(g-1)}

where

\widetilde{G_{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\left(1-\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha}\right)^{-1}=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}.

The following proposition summarizes what we obtain if we replace
G_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
by
\widetilde{G_{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
in the previous paragraph. Again, it ensures the negligibility of the terms corresponding to non-trivial characters (up to the error term coming from
H(\mathbf{T},\xi)
, which is treated in the next paragraph).

Proposition 4.3

(
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
,
C=C_{0}
,
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}_{U}
)
.

For any character
\xi
, there exists a decomposition

\widetilde{Z}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}}\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}^{\xi}_{{\mathbf{m}}}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}}\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{\xi,\varepsilon}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}

and a real number
\delta_{\xi}>0
(depending only on the
d_{\alpha}(\xi)
)
such that

•

if
\xi=0

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{0,\mathbf{0}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{[\Pic^{0}(C)]\mathbf{L}^{1-g}}{\mathbf{L}-1}\right)^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}\right|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}\leqslant{\mathbf{m}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}\rangle}

\displaystyle\underset{\rho\text{-weight-lin.}}{\longrightarrow}\left(\frac{[\Pic^{0}(C)]\mathbf{L}^{1-g}}{\mathbf{L}-1}\right)^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}\right|}\prod_{v\in C_{0}}F_{v}((\mathbf{L}^{-\rho_{\alpha}})_{\alpha_{\in}\mathscr{A}},0)

where
\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}}
is the
{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}
-th coefficient of
\prod_{v\in C_{0}}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},0)

and

w\left(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}^{0,\varepsilon}_{{\mathbf{m}}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\right)<-\delta_{0}\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle\text{ for all $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}$}

when
\min_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(m_{\alpha})\to\infty
;

•

if
\xi\neq 0

w\left(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\xi}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\right)<-\delta_{\xi}\left(\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}+\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\right)\text{ for all $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}$}

when
\min(m_{\alpha})\to\infty
.

4.2.2.
Error term

Now we study the contribution of
H(\mathbf{T},\xi)
, still assuming
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
. We rewrite this error term as follows, using the convenient notations
\mathbf{g}=(g,...,g)
and
\mathbf{1}=(1,...,1)
. In this paragraph
\xi
is any character, including the trivial one; in this case
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(0)=\mathscr{A}_{U}
.

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{T},\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varnothing\neq A\subset\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\setminus A}\widetilde{Z^{\text{Kap}}_{C}}(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha})\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left(Z^{\text{Kap}}_{C}(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha})-\widetilde{Z^{\text{Kap}}_{C}}(\mathbf{L}^{\rho_{\alpha}-1}T_{\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq A\subset\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\\
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\setminus A}\times\{1\}^{A}\end{subarray}}[\Pic^{0}(C)]^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\setminus A\right|}\frac{\left(-\mathbf{L}^{1-g}\right)^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\right|-|\varepsilon|}}{(1-\mathbf{L})^{\left|\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\setminus A\right|}}H_{A,\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

with for any
A\subset\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)
non-empty and
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\setminus A}\times\{1\}^{A}

H_{A,\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\mathbf{T}^{{\mathbf{m}}}\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left([\Sym^{m_{\alpha}}C]-[\Pic^{0}(C)]\frac{\mathbf{L}^{m_{\alpha}-g-1}-1}{\mathbf{L}-1}\right).

Thus studying the contribution of
H(\mathbf{T},\xi)
amounts to studying the
{\mathbf{m}}
-th coefficient of the product

H_{A,\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)\prod_{v\in C}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)

for every
A\subset\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)
non-empty and
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\setminus A}\times\{1\}^{A}
.
In what follows we fix such an
A
.
We know that the term of multi-degree
{\mathbf{m}}
of
H_{A,\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T})

is zero whenever there is
\alpha\in A
such that
m_{\alpha}\geqslant 2g-1
.
So the
{\mathbf{m}}
-th term of the product
H_{A,\varepsilon}(\mathbf{T},\xi)\prod_{v\in C}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
is equal to

(4.2.13)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{A,\varepsilon}=\mathbf{L}^{\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}({\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime})\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\times\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{m}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}_{A}^{\prime}\leqslant 2(\mathbf{g}_{A}-\mathbf{1})\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\prod_{\alpha\in A}\left([\Sym^{n_{\alpha}^{\prime}}C]-[\Pic^{0}(C)]\frac{\mathbf{L}^{n_{\alpha}^{\prime}-g-1}-1}{\mathbf{L}-1}\right)

where
\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}
is the
{\mathbf{n}}
-th coefficient of
\prod_{v\in C}F_{v}(\mathbf{T},\xi)
and
{\mathbf{n}}_{A}^{\prime}
denotes the restriction to
A
of
{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}
.
Since
A\neq\varnothing
, by Proposition
3.5
(for the trivial character), Proposition
3.8
(for non-trivial characters) and Lemma
2.5
we get the existence of a
\delta>0
(which only depends on the
d_{\alpha}(\xi)
) such that for any
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)\setminus A}\times\{1\}^{A}

\displaystyle w\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}({\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime})\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}\times\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{m}}\\
{\mathbf{n}}_{A}^{\prime}\leqslant 2(\mathbf{g}_{A}-\mathbf{1})\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle}\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}}\right)

(4.2.14)

\displaystyle<-\delta\left(\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}+\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}\right)

since for any
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathscr{A}}
one has the decomposition

\langle\rho-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}=\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle-\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}.

Remark that the product over
A

can only take a finite number of values, its weight is therefore bounded.
Combining (
4.2.13
) and (
4.2.14
), we conclude that there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that

w\left(\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle}\mathfrak{e}_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{A,\varepsilon}\right)<-\delta^{\prime}\left(\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}+\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle\right)

whenever
\langle\varepsilon,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}+\langle\rho,{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{U}^{0}(\xi)}
is large enough.

This analysis proves that the slight modification we performed on the Kapranov Zeta function of
C
is painless in the case
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
. It allows us to extend the result to any smooth projective irreducible curve of genus
g
. The control of the error terms coming from
H(\mathbf{T},\xi)
is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4

(
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{X}
,
C=C_{0}
)
.

Let
\xi
be any character.

A geometric interpretation of Proposition
4.12
is as follows.

For any
{\mathsf{M}\in\mathrm{Cl}_{S}^{\mathrm{max}}(X,D)}
, let
M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta,\mathsf{M}}\subset M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta}
be the constructible moduli subspace of sections
\sigma\in M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta}
such that

\forall s\in S\quad\forall\alpha\in\mathsf{M}_{s}\quad(\sigma(\eta_{C}),\mathscr{D}_{\alpha})_{s}>0.

Remark that by definition of the Clemens complexes,
M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta,\mathsf{M}}
is non-empty if and only if the support of
\mathsf{m}_{s}=\left((\sigma(\eta_{C}),\mathscr{D}_{\alpha})_{s}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{D}}\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}_{D}}
is
exactly

\mathsf{M}_{s}
for all
s\in S
. The maximal number of
\mathscr{D}_{\alpha}
a section can intersect above each point of
S
is encoded in
\mathrm{Cl}_{S}^{\mathrm{max}}(X,D)
.
This also means that when the multidegree of a section tends to infinity, there is an unique way to associate a maximal face to the section and

M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta,\mathsf{M}}
is actually the
right
moduli space to consider. We showed that
[M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta,\mathsf{M}}]\mathbf{L}^{-\langle{\mathbf{n}}_{U}^{\beta}+\mathsf{m}_{S},\rho^{\prime}\rangle}
converges to the constant of Proposition
4.12
. Since
[M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta,\mathsf{M}}]
coincides with the coefficient of sufficiently high degree of the refined height zeta function
\mathscr{Z}^{\beta}_{\mathsf{M}}(\mathbf{U})
, this is the following statement and main result.

Theorem 4.15
.

When the multidegree
({\mathbf{n}}_{U}^{\beta},\mathsf{m}_{S})\in\mathbf{N}^{\mathscr{A}_{U}}\times\mathbf{N}^{\mathsf{M}}
goes arbitrary far from the boundaries of the corresponding cone, the normalised class

[M_{{\mathbf{n}},\mathsf{m}_{S}}^{\beta,\mathsf{M}}]\mathbf{L}^{-\langle\rho^{\prime},{\mathbf{n}}\rangle}

\mathscr{M}_{k}
for the weight topology, to the motivic Euler product

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{(1-g)n}\left(\frac{[\Pic^{0}(C)]\mathbf{L}^{1-g}}{\mathbf{L}-1}\right)^{\rg(\Pic(U))}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{v\in C_{1}}\left((1-\mathbf{L}^{-1})^{\rg(\Pic(U))}\frac{[\mathscr{U}_{v}]}{\mathbf{L}^{n}}\right)\times\prod_{v\in C_{0}\setminus C_{1}}\left((1-\mathbf{L}^{-1})^{\rg(\Pic(U))}\mathbf{L}^{\rho^{\beta_{v}}-\langle\rho^{\prime},\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{v}}\rangle}\frac{[E_{\beta_{v}}^{\circ}]}{\mathbf{L}^{n}}\right)

Non-perturbative correction on the black hole geometry
Behnam Pourhassan 1, Hoda Farahani 1,2, Farideh Kazemian 1, İzzet Sakallı 3, Sudhaker Upadhyay 4,1, Dharm Veer Singh 5

Abstract
In this paper, we use the holographic principle to obtain a modified metric of black holes that reproduces the exponentially corrected entropy. The exponential correction of the black hole entropy comes from non-perturbative corrections. It interprets as a quantum effect which affects black hole thermodynamics especially in the infinitesimal scales. Hence, it may affect black hole stability at the final stage. Then, we study modified thermodynamics due to the non-perturbative corrections and calculate thermodynamics quantities of several non-rotating black holes.

II
General Formalism

In this section, we would like to introduce our method to produce the exponentially corrected entropy of black holes. Here, we consider a general black hole metric with the Minkowski geometry, which is given by,

ds^{2}=-g(r)\left(f(r)dt^{2}+dX^{2}\right)+\frac{dr^{2}}{h(r)},

(1)

where
g(r)
,
f(r)
and
h(r)
are the metric functions. For the
D
dimensional space-time,
dX^{2}
is a
D-2
dimensional metric, which may be a flat, open or closed space-time. Therefore, the black hole temperature is given by
1310.4319
,

\frac{1}{T}=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{g(r_{0})f^{\prime}(r_{0})h^{\prime}(r_{0})}},

(2)

where
r_{0}
is the event horizon radius. We would like to use the first law of black hole thermodynamics to obtain the black hole entropy. The first law of black hole thermodynamics in general form is given by
mann
,

dM=TdS+\Omega dJ+\Phi dQ+VdP,

(3)

where
M
,

,
S
,

,

,

and

are the black hole mass, temperature, entropy, angular momentum, electric charge, volume and pressure, respectively. Also, the rotational parameter
\Omega
and potential

are conjugate variables of angular momentum and electric charge, respectively. Hence, one can write the following thermodynamics relations:

\frac{1}{T}=\left(\frac{dS}{dM}\right)_{J,Q,P},

(4)

\frac{1}{\Omega}=\left(\frac{dJ}{dM}\right)_{S,Q,P},

(5)

\frac{1}{\Phi}=\left(\frac{dQ}{dM}\right)_{S,J,P},

(6)

\frac{1}{V}=\left(\frac{dP}{dM}\right)_{S,J,Q}.

Combining the equations (

\left(\frac{dS}{dM}\right)_{J,Q,P}=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{g(r_{0})f^{\prime}(r_{0})h^{\prime}(r_{0})}}.

(8)

Hence, the black hole entropy is related to the metric functions. In most spherically symmetric black holes, we have
g(r)=1
, and
f(r)=h(r)
. In that case, using the equation (
8
) one can write,

f^{\prime}(r_{0})=\frac{4\pi}{\left(\frac{dS}{dM}\right)_{J,Q,P}}.

(9)

This paper aims to find the appropriate metric function

1310.4319
mentioned, the obtained black hole entropy using this method is exactly equal to the area entropy formula.

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega_{D-2}^{2},

where

According (

\frac{1}{T_{H}}=\frac{dS(M)}{dM},

where

The specific heat sign analysis is one of the main tools to obtain information about the black hole’s stability.
The specific heat may be given by:

C=\frac{-[{S}^{\prime}(M)]^{2}}{{{S}^{\prime\prime}}(M)}

(15)

where prime is derivative concerning
M

V=\frac{\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}}{(\frac{D}{2})!}r_{0}^{D},

(16)

one can obtain the thermodynamics pressure.

Having pressure helps us to investigate the critical points using the two below conditions,

\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial V}\right)_{T}=0,

(17)

and

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial V^{2}}\right)_{T}=0.

(18)

We can also calculate the isothermal compressibility

\kappa_{T}=-\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}\right)_{T}.

(19)

The change of
E

\triangle E=\int_{V_{1}}^{V_{2}}{\left[T\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_{V}-P\right]dV}.

(20)

At zero specific heat, the internal energy remains constant:

C=\frac{\partial E}{\partial T}.

(21)

Hence

\mu_{J}=\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial V}\right)_{E},

(22)

is useful to find the situation of black hole temperature in a Joule expansion. If

In the case of cooling or warming, one can define the Joule-Kelvin coefficient
J1
,

\mu_{JK}=\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_{H},

(23)

where
H

Finally, it is valuable to mention that the entropy obtained in this method is related to the entropy in the dual field theory

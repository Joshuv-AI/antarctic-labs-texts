Abstract
. Chemotaxis phenomena govern the directed movement of micro-organisms in response to chemical stimuli. In this paper, we investigate two Keller–Segel systems of reaction-advection-diffusion equations modeling chemotaxis on thin networks. The distinction between two systems is driven by the rate of diffusion of the chemo-attractant. The intermediate rate of diffusion is modeled by a coupled pair of parabolic equations, while the rapid rate is described by a parabolic equation coupled with an elliptic one. Assuming the polynomial rate of growth of the chemotaxis sensitivity coefficient, we prove local well-posedness of both systems on compact metric graphs, and, in particular, prove existence of unique classical solutions. This is achieved by constructing sufficiently regular mild solutions via analytic semigroup methods and combinatorial description of the heat kernel on metric graphs. The regularity of mild solutions is shown by applying abstract semigroup results to semi-linear parabolic equations on compact graphs. In addition, for logistic type Keller–Segel systems we prove global well-posedness and, in some special cases, global uniform boundedness of solutions.

The two quantities central to the mathematical modeling of such processes are the density of bio-species

and the concentration of attracting (repelling) substance

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}J_{u,v}-\varphi(u,v)=0,\\
\tau\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}J_{v}-\psi(u,v)=0,\\
\end{cases}

where

•

•

\partial_{xx}^{2}v-\psi(u,v)=0
,

•

\varphi(u,v)
,
\psi(u,v)
describe the rates at which the biospecies, respectively, the chemo-attractant are produced.

In this paper, we provide a systematic treatment of general chemotaxis systems with moderate and fast rates of diffusion of the attractant on arbitrary compact metric graphs. Concretely, we focus on the following two systems of reaction–advection–diffusion equations of parabolic-parabolic type

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}u-\chi(u,v)\partial_{x}v)+\varphi(u,v),\\

We first recall from

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Delta:\operatorname{dom}(\Delta)\subset L^{p}(\Gamma)\rightarrow L^{p}(\Gamma),\ \Delta u:=\partial_{x}^{2}u,\ u\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta),\\
&\operatorname{dom}(\Delta):=\left\{u\in\widehat{W}^{2,p}(\Gamma):\sum\limits_{\vartheta\sim e}\partial_{\nu}u_{e}(\vartheta)=0,\ u_{e}(\vartheta)=u_{e^{\prime}}(\vartheta),\vartheta\sim e,e^{\prime}\right\}.\end{split}

For a given edge

t(\pm e)=i(\mp e)
. The graph consisting of all edges

\widetilde{\Gamma}
. We say that edges

. The collection of all paths connecting
e,e^{\prime}
along

{\mathcal{P}}_{e,e^{\prime}}(m)
. The scattering coefficient matrix of size
2|{\mathcal{E}}|\times 2|{\mathcal{E}}|
(here

denotes the number of edges of
\Gamma
) between edges

is defined via

(2.2)

S_{e,e^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{{\deg(t(e))}},&e\not=-e^{\prime},t(e)=i(e^{\prime}),\\
\frac{2}{{\deg(t(e))}}-1,&e=-e^{\prime},t(e)=i(e^{\prime}),\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

where

denotes the number of edges incident to

.

The scattering coefficient along the path

is given by

. With this notation in hand let us recall the definition of a convolution operator on a metric graph with kernel

for

,

satisfying

(2.3)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}3^{m}\|f\|_{L^{1}(m,\infty)}<\infty.

Following
[
3
, Definition 3.4]
we define kernel
K_{f}
by the formulas

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&K_{f}:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\ K_{f}=K_{f}^{(++)}+K_{f}^{(+-)}+K_{f}^{(-+)}+K_{f}^{(--)},\end{split}

where

(2.5)

\displaystyle K_{f}^{(++)}((e,\xi),(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})):=\delta_{e,e^{\prime}}f(\xi^{\prime}-\xi)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{e,e^{\prime}}(m)\end{subarray}}S_{P}f(\xi^{\prime}+|P|-\xi),

with

if the edges

and
e^{\prime}
are different, and
\delta_{e,e^{\prime}}=1
otherwise,

(2.6)

\displaystyle K_{f}^{(+-)}((e,\xi),(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{e,-e^{\prime}}(m)\end{subarray}}S_{P}f(|e^{\prime}|-\xi^{\prime}+|P|-\xi),

(2.7)

\displaystyle K_{f}^{(-+)}((e,\xi),(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{-e,e^{\prime}}(m)\end{subarray}}S_{P}f(\xi^{\prime}+|P|-(|e|-\xi)),

(2.8)

\displaystyle K_{f}^{(--)}((e,\xi),(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{-e,-e^{\prime}}(m)\end{subarray}}S_{P}f(|e^{\prime}|-\xi^{\prime}+|P|-(|e|-\xi)).

The above four terms represent sums along all possible finite paths on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
connecting points
(e,\xi)\in\Gamma
and
(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\in\Gamma
, see Figure

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&K^{(++)}_{f}\text{ terms correspond to paths }(e,\xi)\rightarrow(e,|e|)\rightarrow...(e^{\prime},0)\rightarrow(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}),\\
&K^{(+-)}_{f}\text{ terms correspond to paths }(e,\xi)\rightarrow(e,|e|)\rightarrow...(e^{\prime},|e^{\prime}|)\rightarrow(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}),\\
&K^{(-+)}_{f}\text{ terms correspond to paths }(e,\xi)\rightarrow(e,0)\rightarrow...(e^{\prime},0)\rightarrow(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}),\\
&K^{(--)}_{f}\text{ terms correspond to paths }(e,\xi)\rightarrow(e,0)\rightarrow...(e^{\prime},|e^{\prime}|)\rightarrow(e^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}).\end{split}

Then the convolution operator

or simply

(2.11)

(f*u)(\xi):=\int_{\Gamma}K_{f}(\xi,\xi^{\prime})u(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}.

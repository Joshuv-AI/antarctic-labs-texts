Degenerate polyexponential functions and degenerate Bell polynomials
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. In recent years, studying degenerate versions of some special polynomials, which was initiated by Carlitz in an investigation of the degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials, regained lively interest of many mathematicians. In this paper, as a degenerate version of polyexponential functions introduced by Hardy, we study degenerate polyexponential functions and derive various properties of them. Also, we introduce new type degenerate Bell polynomials, which are different from the previously studied partially degenerate Bell polynomials and arise naturally in the recent study of degenerate zero-truncated Poisson random variables, and deduce some of their properties. Furthermore, we derive some identities connecting the polyexponential functions and the new type degenerate Bell polynomials.

11B73; 11B83; 05A19

1.
Introduction

It is well known that the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(1)

x^{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)(x)_{l},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,6,8,12,22]),

where
(x)_{0}=1
,
(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1)
,
(n\geq 1)
.

From (
1
), we note that

(2)

\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),(\mathrm{see}\ [4,5,7,16]).

The Bell polynomials are given by the generating function

(3)

e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{l}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{k!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,13]).

When
x=1
,
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(1)
is called the
n
-th Bell number.

From (
3
), we note that

(4)

\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}S_{2}(n,k),\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,13]).

For
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the polylogarithms are defined as

(5)

\mathrm{Li}_{k}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n^{k}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,12]).

Note that
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(x)=-\log(1-x)
.

The polyexponential function was first considered by Hardy and is given by

(6)

e(x,\ a|s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{(n+a)^{s}n!},\quad(\mathrm{Re}(a)>0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,10,11]).

From (
6
), we note that

(7)

e(x,\ 1|1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{(n+1)!}=\frac{1}{x}\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}-1\bigg)=\frac{1}{x}(e^{x}-1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,9,10,11]).

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential function is defined by

(8)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14,15,19]).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e^{x}_{\lambda}(t)=e^{xt}
.

In [3], Carlitz considered the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials which are given by

(9)

\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(0)
is called the
n
-th degenerate Bernoulli number.
Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=B_{n}(x)\displaystyle
, where
B_{n}(x)
are the ordinary Bernoulli polynomials given by

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12-22]).

Recently, Kim introduced the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind which are defined by

(10)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]),

where
(x)_{0,\lambda}=1
,
(x)_{b,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda)
,
(n\geq 1)
.

In [13], the partially degenerate Bell polynomial are defined as

(11)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
10
) and (
11
), we note that

(12)

\mathrm{bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13,20]).

It is easy to show that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\mathrm{bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0)\displaystyle
.

By (
11
), we easily get

(13)

\mathrm{bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k)_{n,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

In this paper, as a degenerate version of polyexponential functions introduced by Hardy, we study degenerate polyexponential functions and derive various properties of them. Also, we introduce new type degenerate Bell polynomials, which are different from the previously studied partially degenerate Bell polynomials and arise naturally in the recent study of degenerate zero-truncated Poisson random variables, and deduce some of their properties. Furthermore, we derive some identities connecting the polyexponential functions and the new type degenerate Bell polynomials.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 1, we recall some necessary ingredients, namely polyexponential functions, degenerate Bernoulli polynomials, degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and partially degenerate Bell polynomials. In Section 2, we introduce the degenerate polyexponential functions and derive several properties of them. Especially, we find integral representations for the degenerate polyexponential functions. Then we introduce the new type degenerate Bell polynomials, deduce some of their properties and get their connections with degenerate polyexponential functions. Finally, in Section 3, we recall the degenerate Lerch zeta functions which are closely related to the degenerate polyexponential functions. Also, we note the degenerate Hurwitz zeta functions and the degenerate Riemann zeta functions, as special cases of the degenerate Lerch zeta functions.

2.
Degenerate polyexponential functions and degenerate Bell polynomials

In view of (
8
), we consider the degenerate polyexponential functions which are given by

(14)

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!(n+\delta)^{k}}x^{n},

where
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0
.

By (
14
), we easily get

(15)

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|0)=e_{\lambda}(x),\quad e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|1)=\frac{1}{x}\frac{1}{1+\lambda}(e_{\lambda}(x)-1)+\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}e_{\lambda}(x).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|1)=\frac{1}{x}(e^{x}-1)=e(x,\ 1|1).

It is known that the degenerate gamma function is defined by

(16)

\Gamma_{\lambda}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(t)dt,\quad(\mathrm{Re}(s)>0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15,19]).

From (
16
), we consider the incomplete degenerate gamma function given by

(17)

d_{\lambda}(\delta,x)=\int_{0}^{x}t^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(-t)dt=x^{\delta}e_{\lambda}(-x,\ \delta|1),

where
\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0
and
x\geq 0
.
Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}d_{\lambda}(\delta,\ x)=\Gamma_{-\lambda}(\delta)\displaystyle
.

Theorem 1
.

For
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0
, and
x\geq 0
, we have

x^{\delta}e_{\lambda}(-x,\ \delta|1)=d_{\lambda}(\delta,x).

From (
17
), we note that

(18)

\frac{d}{dx}[x^{\delta}e_{\lambda}(-x,\ \delta|1)]=x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(-x).

In particular,

e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|2)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!(n+1)^{2}}x^{n},

and

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}t^{n}\bigg)dt=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n+1,\lambda}}{(n+1)!}\int_{0}^{x}t^{n}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{(n+1)!(n+1)}(1-n\lambda)x^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!(n+1)^{2}}x^{n}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}(n+1-1)\lambda}{n!(n+1)^{2}}x^{n}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|2)-\lambda\big(e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|1)-e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|2)\big)

\displaystyle=(1+\lambda)e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|2)-\lambda e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|1).

By (
15
) and (
19
), we get

(20)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)dt+\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)dt+\frac{1}{x}\frac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}(e_{\lambda}(x)-1)+\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\bigg)^{2}e_{\lambda}(x).

Lemma 2
.

For all
x
, we have

e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|2)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)dt+\frac{1}{x}\frac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}(e_{\lambda}(x)-1)+\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\bigg)^{2}e_{\lambda}(x).

Let
\mathrm{Ein}_{\lambda}(z)
be the special degenerate entire function which is defined by

(21)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ein}_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)dt=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}\bigg(1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}(-1)^{k}t^{k}\bigg)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}t^{k}dt=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}(-1)^{k-1}\int_{0}^{x}t^{k-1}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k!\cdot k}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}.

On the other hand,

(22)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{t}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)dt+\frac{1}{x}\frac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}(e_{\lambda}(x)-1)+\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\bigg)^{2}e_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{-x}\frac{1}{t}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(-t)\big)dt+\frac{1}{x}\frac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}(e_{\lambda}(x)-1)+\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\bigg)^{2}e_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\frac{1}{x}\mathrm{Ein}_{\lambda}(-x)+\frac{1}{x}\frac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}(e_{\lambda}(x)-1)+\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\bigg)^{2}e_{\lambda}(x).

Thus, we have

(23)

x(1+\lambda)e_{\lambda}(-x,\ 1|2)=\mathrm{Ein}_{\lambda}(x)-\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\big(e_{\lambda}(-x)-1\big)+x\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda}e_{\lambda}(-x).

From (
23
), we note that

\mathrm{Ein}_{\lambda}(x)=x(1+\lambda)e_{\lambda}(-x,\ 1|2)+\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\big(e_{\lambda}(-x)-1\big)-x\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda}e_{\lambda}(-x).

It is easy to show that

\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|p+1)=\frac{d}{dx}x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n}}{n!(n+1)^{p+1}}=e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|p).

Thus, we have

e_{\lambda}(x,\ 1|p+1)=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}e_{\lambda}(t,\ 1|p)dt.

In general,

\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)\big[x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p+1)\big]=\frac{d}{dx}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n+\delta}}{n!(n+\delta)^{p+1}}=x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p).

Thus, we have

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p+1)=\frac{1}{x^{\delta}}\int_{0}^{x}t^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p)dt.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)^{p}\big[x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p)\big]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)\times\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)\times\cdots\times\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)}_{p-\mathrm{times}}\big[x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p)\big]

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)^{p-1}\big[x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p-1)\big]

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=x^{\delta-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}x^{n}=x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x).

Let
|z|<|\delta|
. Then we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p)z^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n}}{n!(n+\delta)^{p}}z^{p}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}x^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{z}{n+\delta}\bigg)^{p}

(25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}x^{n}\frac{n+\delta}{n+\delta-z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}x^{n}\bigg(1+\frac{z}{n+\delta-z}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}x^{n}+z\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!(n+\delta-z)}x^{n}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}(x)+z\,e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|1).

Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
|z|<|\delta|
, we have

\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}x\bigg)^{p}\big[x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|p)\big]=x^{\delta-1}e_{\lambda}(x),

and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|k)z^{k}=e_{\lambda}(x)+z\,e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|1).

Now, we observe that

(26)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{d}{dz}\bigg)^{m}\big(e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|1)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{d}{dz}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n}}{n!(n+\delta-z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n}m!}{n!(n+\delta-z)^{m+1}}

\displaystyle=m!e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|m+1).

By using the Taylor expansion of
e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|1)
with respect to
z
, we get

(27)

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|1)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dz}\bigg)^{m}\big(e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|1\big)\bigg|_{z=0}\cdot\frac{z^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|m+1)z^{m}.

Moreover,

(28)

\begin{split}\bigg(\frac{d}{dz}\bigg)^{m}\big(e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|s)\big)&=\bigg(\frac{d}{dz}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n}}{n!(n+\delta-z)^{s}}.\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n}}{n!}\cdot\frac{s(s+1)\cdots(s+m-1)}{(n+\delta-z)^{m+s}}\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(s+m)}{\Gamma(s)}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|m+s).\end{split}

By using Taylor expansion of
e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|s)
with respect to
z
, we get

(29)

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|s)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(\frac{d}{dz}\big)^{m}\big(e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|s)\big)\big|_{z=0}}{m!}z^{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+m)}{\Gamma(s)m!}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|m+s)z^{m}.

Therefore, from (
29
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
\delta,s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0,\ \mathrm{Re}(s)>0
, we have

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|s)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+m)}{\Gamma(s)m!}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|m+s)z^{m}.

In addition, for
s=k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta-z|k)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+m-1}{k-1}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|m+k)z^{m}.

As is traditional, let
\int_{\infty}^{(0+)}
(respectively,
\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)})
denote the integration of path that starts at infinity (respectively, at negative infinity), encircles the origin counter-clockwise direction and returns to the starting point.
Then we have the following well-known integral representation of
-2i\sin\pi s\,\Gamma(s)
as an entire function of
s
(see
[
21
]
):

(30)

\int_{\infty}^{(0+)}(-t)^{s-1}e^{-t}dt=-2i\sin\pi s\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-t}=-2i\sin\pi s\,\Gamma(s)=-2\pi i\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}.

By (
30
), we get

(31)

\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\infty}^{(0+)}(-t)^{s-1}e^{-t}dt=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}t^{s-1}e^{t}dt.

From (
31
), we easily note that

\begin{split}\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}t^{s-1}e^{\delta t}e^{nt}dt&=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{(n+\delta)^{s}}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}e^{t}t^{s-1}dt\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{(n+\delta)^{s}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(1-s)}=\frac{1}{(n+\delta)^{s}}.\end{split}

Thus, by (
14
) and (
2
), we get

(32)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}\frac{x^{n}}{(n+\delta)^{s}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}x^{n}\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}t^{s-1}e^{\delta t}e^{nt}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}e^{\delta t}t^{s-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}e^{nt}x^{n}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}e^{\delta t}t^{s-1}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})dt.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(33)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|m)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\rightarrow m}e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|s)=\lim_{s\rightarrow m}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\pi}{\Gamma(s)\sin\pi s}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}e^{\delta t}t^{s-1}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\Gamma(m)}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}e^{\delta t}t^{m-1}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\mathrm{Log}\ tdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{(m-1)!}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}e^{\delta t}t^{m-1}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\mathrm{Log}\ tdt.

Thefore, by (
32
) and (
33
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
s,\delta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0
,
s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}
, and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we have the integral representation

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|s)=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}t^{s-1}e^{\delta t}e_{\lambda}e(xe^{t})\,dt.

In addition, for
s=m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the integral representation

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|m)=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2\pi i(m-1)!}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}t^{m-1}e^{\delta t}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\mathrm{Log}\ t\,dt.

Now, we consider new type degenerate Bell polynomials which are given by

(34)

e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\cdot e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
3
) and (
34
), we note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0)\displaystyle
.

Now, we observe that

(35)

\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}e_{\lambda}(x)=\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}k^{n}x^{k}.

On the other hand,

(36)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}k^{n}x^{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=1
,
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the new type degenerate Bell numbers.

Therefore, by (
35
) and (
36
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6

(Dobinski’s formula)
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}e_{\lambda}(x)=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}k^{n}x^{k}.

From (
13
), we note that

(37)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}}{k!(k+\delta)^{s}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}}{k!}\frac{1}{\delta^{s}}\bigg(1+\frac{k}{\delta}\bigg)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}\delta^{-s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{n}\bigg(\frac{k}{\delta}\bigg)^{n}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}(x)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{n}\frac{1}{\delta^{s+n}}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{n}}{k!}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}(x)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{n}\frac{1}{\delta^{s+n}}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x).

Therefore, by (
37
), we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 7
.

For
n\geq 0
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0
,
\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0
, we have

e_{\lambda}(x,\ \delta|s)=e_{\lambda}(x)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{n}\frac{1}{\delta^{s+n}}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x).

From (
34
), we note that

(38)

\frac{d}{dt}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)=xe^{t}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\cdot(1+\lambda xe^{t})^{-1}.

Thus we have

(39)

(1+\lambda xe^{t})\frac{d}{dt}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)=xe^{t}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)=xe^{t}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

=x\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}=x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\qquad

On the other hand,

(40)

\displaystyle(1+\lambda xe^{t})\frac{d}{dt}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=(1+\lambda xe^{t})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}+\lambda x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\mathrm{Bel}_{l+1,\lambda}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
39
) and (
40
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)=x\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\big(\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)-\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{l+1,\lambda}(x)\big).

We observe that

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\cdot e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)=e^{t}e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)(1+\lambda xe^{t}\big)^{-1}-e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\lambda x}.

Thus, by (
38
) and (
41
), we get

(42)

\frac{d}{dt}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\cdot e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)=x\bigg\{\frac{d}{dx}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)+e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\frac{1}{1+\lambda x}\bigg\}.

From (
34
) and (
42
), we can derive the following identity

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\cdot e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=x\bigg\{\frac{d}{dx}\big(e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\cdot e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\big)+e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})\cdot e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\frac{1}{1+\lambda x}\bigg\}

(43)

\displaystyle=x\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)+\frac{1}{1+\lambda x}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\bigg\}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)+\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)+\lambda x\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\displaystyle
.

Comparing the coefficients on the both sides of (
43
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)=\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\big(\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)+\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)+\lambda x\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)\big),

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\displaystyle
.

By Theorem 9, we get

(44)

\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)=x\big(\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)+\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)+\lambda x\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)-\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)\big).

Thus, by Theorem 8 and (
44
), we have

(45)

\displaystyle x\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)-x\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)-\lambda x^{2}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)+\lambda x\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\big(\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)-\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{l+1,\lambda}(x)\big)-x\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)-\lambda x^{2}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)+\lambda x\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{l}\big(\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)-\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{l+1,\lambda}(x)\big)-\lambda x^{2}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x).

From (
45
), we note that

(46)

(1+\lambda x)\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{l}\big(\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)-\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{l+1,\lambda}(x)\big).

Therefore, by (
46
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda x}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{l}\big(\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x)-\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{l+1,\lambda}(x)\big).

From (
8
), we note that

(47)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)=\bigg(\frac{1+\lambda xe^{t}}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}

=\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda x}{1+\lambda x}(e^{t}-1)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}

=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}S_{2}(n,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
47
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}S_{2}(n,k).

In particular, for
x=1
, we have

\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\bigg)^{k}S_{2}(n,k).

From (
11

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2x}\mathrm{bel}_{n,\lambda}(-x)dx\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2x}e^{-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x(e_{\lambda}(t)+1)}dx\\
&=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}-\frac{2}{e_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(2t)-1}=\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}-\frac{2t}{e_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(2t)-1}\bigg)\\
&=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(\beta_{n,\lambda}-2^{n}\beta_{n,\frac{\lambda}{2}}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\beta_{n+1,\lambda}-2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{n+1}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, comparing the coefficients on both sides (
2
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 12
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2x}\mathrm{bel}_{n,\lambda}(-x)dx=\frac{\beta_{n+1,\lambda}-2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{n+1}.

By (
10

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{n+1,\lambda}-2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{n+1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2x}e^{-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}dt\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2x}x^{k}dx\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2x}x^{k}dx\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{k!}{2^{k+1}}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (

Theorem 13
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{-k-1}k!S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{n+1}\big(\beta_{n+1,\lambda}-2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\frac{\lambda}{2}}\big).

For

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}x^{n}}{n!(n+\delta)^{-p}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}(1)_{n,\lambda}n^{k}\delta^{p-k}\bigg]\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

3.
Further Remarks

For
\delta,s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
, we observe that

(51)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}(tx,\delta|s)e^{-t}dt=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!(n+\delta)^{s}}x^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}e^{-t}dt=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{(n+\delta)^{s}}x^{n}.

From (
51
), we may consider the degenerate Lerch zeta function which is given by

(52)

\Phi_{\lambda}(x,s,\delta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{(n+\delta)^{s}}x^{n},\quad(\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\Phi_{\lambda}(x,s,\delta)=\Phi(x,s,\delta)\displaystyle
, where
\Phi(x,s,\delta)
is Lerch zeta function defined by

\Phi(x,s,\delta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{(n+\delta)^{s}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,18]).

In particular, for
x=1
, we have

(53)

\Phi_{\lambda}(1,s,\delta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{(n+\delta)^{s}},\quad(\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0).

In addition, Hurwitz zeta function is defined as

(54)

\zeta(s,\delta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+\delta)^{s}},\quad(\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0).

In light of (
54
), we denote
\Phi_{\lambda}(1,s,\delta)
in (
53
) by
\zeta_{\lambda}(s,\delta)
and call it the degenerate Hurwitz zeta function as follows:

(55)

\zeta_{\lambda}(s,\delta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{(n+\delta)^{s}},\quad(\mathrm{Re}(\delta)>0).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\zeta_{\lambda}(s,\delta)=\zeta(s,\delta)\displaystyle
.

Let us take
\delta=1
. Then, by (
55
), we get

(56)

\zeta_{\lambda}(s,1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{(n+1)^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n-1,\lambda}}{n^{s}}.

In view of (
56
), we may consider the degenerate Riemann zeta function given by

(57)

\zeta_{\lambda}(s)=\zeta_{\lambda}(s,1)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n-1,\lambda}}{n^{s}},\quad(\mathrm{Re}(s)>1).

By (
3
), we get

(58)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\big(e_{\lambda}^{n}(t)-1\big)=\frac{1}{t}\bigg\{\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{n}(t)-\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\big(\beta_{p,\lambda}(n)-\beta_{p,\lambda}\big)\frac{t^{p}}{p!}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\beta_{p+1,\lambda}(n)-\beta_{p+1,\lambda}}{p+1}\bigg)\frac{t^{p}}{p!}.

On the other hand,

(59)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k)_{p,\lambda}}{p!}t^{p}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(k)_{p,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{p}}{p!}.

From (
58
) and (
59
), we have

(60)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(k)_{p,\lambda}=\frac{1}{p+1}\big\{\beta_{p+1,\lambda}(n)-\beta_{p+1,\lambda}\big\},

where
n
is a positive integer and
p
is non-negative integer. By (
60
), we easily get

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(k)_{p,\lambda}=\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{p+1}{l}(n)_{l,\lambda}\beta_{p+1-l,\lambda}.

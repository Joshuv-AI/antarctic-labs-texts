Hyperbola method on toric varieties
Marta Pieropan, Damaris Schindler

Abstract
. We develop a very general version of the hyperbola method which extends the known method by Blomer and Brüdern for products of projective spaces to complete smooth split toric varieties. We use it to count Campana points of bounded log-anticanonical height on complete smooth split toric ℚ \mathbb{Q} -varieties with torus invariant boundary. We apply the strong duality principle in linear programming to show the compatibility of our results with the conjectured asymptotic.

1.
Introduction

This paper stems from an investigation of the universal torsor method
[
Sal98
,
FP16
]
in relation to the problem of counting Campana points of bounded height on log Fano varieties in the framework of
[
PSTVA21
, Conjecture 1.1]
.
Campana points are a notion of points that interpolate between rational points and integral points on certain log smooth pairs, or orbifolds, introduced and first studied by Campana
[
Cam04
,
Cam11
,
Cam15
]
. The study of the distribution of Campana points over number fields was initiated only quite recently and the literature on this topic is still sparse
[
BVV12
,
VV12
,
BY21
,
Shu21
,
PSTVA21
,
Shu22
,
Str22
,
Xia22
,
BBK+23
]
.
In this paper we deal with toric varieties, which constitute a fundamental family of examples for the study of the distribution of rational points
[
BT95b
,
BT95a
,
BT96
,
BT98
,
Sal98
,
dlB01a
]
, via a combination of the universal torsor method with a very general version of the hyperbola method, which we develop.

We use the universal torsor method, instead of exploiting the toric group structure, because we hope to extend our approach to a larger class of log Fano varieties in the future. Indeed,
the hyperbola method is well suited to deal with subvarieties
[
Sch14
,
Sch16
,
BB17
,
BB18
,
Mig15
,
Mig16
,
Mig18
,
BH19
]
, and all log Fano varieties admit neat embeddings in toric varieties
[
ADHL15
,
GOST15
]
which can be exploited for the universal torsor method.

One of the key technical innovations in this article is the development of a very general form of the hyperbola method, which is motivated by work of Blomer and Brüdern in the case of products of projective spaces
[
BB18
]
. Mignot
[
Mig18
]
has adjusted these ideas to complete smooth split toric varieties with simplicial effective cone.
With our approach we extend the work of Blomer and Brüdern to complete smooth split toric varieties with additional flexibility to change the height function.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be an arithmetic function for which one has asymptotics for summing the function
f
over boxes, see Property I in Section
4
. Let
B
be a large real parameter,
\mathcal{K}
a finite index set and
\alpha_{i,k}\geq 0
for
1\leq i\leq s
and
k\in\mathcal{K}
. The goal is then to use this information from sums over boxes to deduce an asymptotic formula for sums of the form

S^{f}(B):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,k}}\leq B,\ \forall k\in\mathcal{K}\\
y_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq s\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y}).

We define the polyhedron
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
given by

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\leq 1,\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

and

t_{i}\geq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

Here the parameters
\varpi_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq s
are defined in Property I for the function
f
. The linear function
\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}
takes its maximal value on a face of
\mathcal{P}
which we call
F
. We write
a
for its maximal value.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a function that satisfies Property I from Section

4
.
Assume that
\mathcal{P}
is bounded and non-degenerate, and that
F
is not contained in a coordinate hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{s}
. Let
k=\dim F
. Then we have

\displaystyle S^{f}(B)=(s-1-k)!C_{f,M}c_{\mathcal{P}}(\log B)^{k}B^{a}+O\left(C_{f,E}(\log\log B)^{s}(\log B)^{k-1}B^{a}\right),

where
C_{f,M}
and
C_{f,E}
are the constants in Property I and
c_{\mathcal{P}}
is the constant in equation (
4.3
).

In comparison to earlier versions of the hyperbola method in work of Blomer and Brüdern
[
BB18
]
or Mignot
[
Mig18
]
, we only obtain a saving of a power of a
\log B
, but we can work with the weaker assumption of using only Property I. In contrast to
[
BB18
]
and
[
Mig18
]
we no longer need to assume that we can evaluate the function
f
on lower-dimensional boxes after fixing a number of variables, called Property II in their work.

The case treated in
[
BB18
]
would in our notation correspond to an index set
\mathcal{K}
with one element where all the
\alpha_{i,k}=\alpha
for all
1\leq i\leq s
and some
\alpha>0
, and
k=s-1
. Our attack to evaluate the sum
S^{f}
starts in a similar way as in
[
BB18
]
. We cover the region given by the conditions
\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,k}}\leq B
,
k\in\mathcal{K}
with boxes of different side lengths on which we can evaluate the function
f
. One important ingredient in the hyperbola method in
[
BB18
]
is a combinatorial identity for the generating series

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ldots+j_{s}\leq J\\
j_{i}\geq 0,\ 1\leq i\leq s\end{subarray}}t^{j_{1}+\ldots+j_{s}},

which needs to be evaluated for
J
going to infinity. By induction the authors give a closed expression. For us this part of the argument breaks down, as we have in general more complicated polytopes that arise in the summation condition for
S^{f}
and are not aware of comparable combinatorial identities for the tuples
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{s})
lying in general convex polytopes. Instead, we approximate the number of integer points in certain intersections of hyperplanes with a convex polytope by lattice point counting arguments and then use asymptotic evaluations for sums of the form

\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}m^{\ell}\theta^{m}

for
0<\theta<1
and
\ell,M\in\mathbb{N}
.

In comparison to Mignot’s work
[
Mig18
]
, we can deal with polytopes for which
|\mathcal{K}|>1
, and where
k
is no longer restricted to the case
k=s-1
.

Our main application of Theorem
1.1
is a proof of
[
PSTVA21
, Conjecture 1.1]
for smooth split toric varieties over
\mathbb{Q}
with the log-anticanonical height.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be the fan of a complete smooth split toric variety
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\{\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{s}\}
be the set of rays of
\Sigma
.
For each
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
fix a positive integer
m_{i}

and denote by
D_{i}
the torus invariant divisor corresponding to
\rho_{i}
. Assume that
L:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{m_{i}}D_{i}
is ample.
Let
H_{L}
be the height defined by
L
as in Section
6.3
.
Let
\mathscr{X}
be the toric scheme defined by
\Sigma
over
\mathbb{Z}
, and for each
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, let
\mathscr{D}_{i}
be the closure of
D_{i}
in
\mathscr{X}
.
For every
B>0
, let
N(B)
be the number of Campana
\mathbb{Z}
-points on the Campana orbifold
(\mathscr{X},\sum_{i=1}^{s}(1-1/m_{i})\mathscr{D}_{i})
(in the sense of
[
PSTVA21
, Definition 3.4]
) of height
H_{L}
at most
B
.
Then for sufficiently large
B>0
,

N(B)=cB(\log B)^{r-1}+O(B(\log B)^{r-2}(\log\log B)^{s}),

(1.1)

where
r
is the rank of the Picard group of
X
, and
c
is a positive constant compatible with the prediction in
[
PSTVA21
, §3.3]
.

Our application of the hyperbola method recovers Salberger’s result
[
Sal98
]
and improves on the error term by saving a factor
(\log B)^{1-\varepsilon}
where
[
Sal98
]
saves only a factor
(\log B)^{1-1/f-\varepsilon}
, where
f\geq 2
is an integer that depends on the toric variety.

Theorem
1.2
could also be deduced from work of de la Bretèche
[
dlB01b
]
,
[
dlB01a
]
, who developed a multi-dimensional Dirichlet series approach to count rational points of bounded height on toric varieties, or from work of Santens
[
San23
]
. Another approach could be via harmonic analysis of the height zeta function, even though such a proof would probably be more involved than the case of compactifications of vector groups
[
PSTVA21
]
.
Our proof proceeds via the universal torsor method introduced by Salberger in
[
Sal98
]
in combination with Theorem
1.1
.
One of our main motivations for this approach is that it opens a path to counting Campana points on subvarieties of toric varieties.

When we apply Theorem
1.1
to prove Theorem
1.2
, we need to verify that both the exponent of
B
as well as the power of
\log B
match the prediction in
[
PSTVA21
]
. The exponent
a
in Theorem
1.1
is the result of a linear optimization problem. Similarly, the construction of the height function leading to the exponent one of
B
in Theorem
1.2
involves another linear optimization problem. We use the strong duality property in linear programming
[
Dan98
, Chapter 6]
to recognize that the exponents
are indeed compatible, and that this holds heuristically also in the more general setting where the height is not necessarily log-anticanonical. For the compatibility of the exponents of
\log B
we exploit a different duality setup, which involves the Picard group of
X
.

In upcoming work, we show how Theorem
1.1
can be used to count rational points and Campana points of bounded height on certain subvarieties of toric varieties.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we provide some auxiliary estimates on variants of geometric sums which are used later in Section
4
.
In Section
3
we study volumes of slices of polytopes under small deformations.
In Section
4
we develop the hyperbola method and give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Sections
5
and
6
are dedicated to the application of the hyperbola method to prove Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
5
we study estimates for
m
-full numbers of bounded size subject to certain divisibility conditions, and we produce the estimates in boxes for the function

associated to the counting problem in Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
6
we describe the heights associated to semiample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on toric varieties over number fields, we study some combinatorial properties of the polytopes that play a prominent role in the application of the hyperbola method, and we show that the heuristic expectations coming from the hyperbola method agree with the prediction in
[
PSTVA21
, Conjecture 1.1]
on split toric varieties for Campana points of bounded height, where the height does not need to be anticanonical. We conclude the section with the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

This article subsumes our previous work on the hyperbola method, which had appeared on arxiv.org under arXiv:2001.09815v2, and which proved Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
under additional technical assumptions on the corresponding polytopes.

2.
Preliminaries

In the hyperbola method in the next section we need good approximations for finite sums of the form

g_{\ell}(M,\theta):=\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}m^{\ell}\theta^{m},

for some
0<\theta<1
and natural numbers
\ell,M\geq 0
(here and in the following we understand
0^{0}:=1
).
In this subsection we also write
g_{\ell}(M)
for
g_{\ell}(M,\theta)
.

We will use the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

For an integer
\ell\geq 0
and
0<\theta<1
and a real number
M>\ell
we have

(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=(-1)^{\ell+1}\ell!+O_{\ell}(1-\theta)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right).

Lemma
2.1
can be deduced from the following statement.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
M>\ell\geq 0
and
\theta>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=\sum_{0\leq m<\ell+1}\theta^{m}\sum_{h=0}^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}

\displaystyle+\theta^{M}\sum_{0<m\leq\ell+1}\theta^{m}\sum_{h=m}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}M^{k}(m-h)^{\ell-k}.

For the proof of Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.1
we need the following identity. For an integer
0\leq\alpha\leq\ell
we have

\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}h^{\alpha}=0.

(2.1)

To see this consider the identity

(t-1)^{\ell+1}=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}t^{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}.

Now take derivatives with respect to
t
and then set
t=1
.

Lemma
2.2
implies Lemma
2.1
.

We start in observing that

\displaystyle(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=\sum_{0\leq m<\ell+1}\theta^{m}\sum_{h=0}^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq m\leq\ell}\sum_{h=0}^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}

\displaystyle+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{l}\left(\theta^{M}M^{l}\right).

We further compute

\displaystyle(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=\sum_{0\leq m\leq\ell+1}\sum_{h=0}^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}

\displaystyle-\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(\ell+1-h)^{\ell}

\displaystyle+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right).

Note that the term in the second line is equal to zero by equation (
2.1
). Hence we have

\displaystyle(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=\sum_{0\leq m\leq\ell+1}\sum_{h=0}^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}

\displaystyle+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right).

We now switch the summation of
m
and
h
to obtain

\begin{split}(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)&=\sum_{0\leq h\leq\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{h\leq m\leq\ell+1}(m-h)^{\ell}\\
&+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right)\\
&=\sum_{0\leq h\leq\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{0\leq t\leq\ell+1-h}t^{\ell}\\
&+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right).\end{split}

By the Faulhaber formulas
\sum_{0\leq t\leq\ell+1-h}t^{\ell}
is a polynomial in
h
with leading term

\frac{(\ell+1-h)^{\ell+1}}{\ell+1}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}}{\ell+1}h^{\ell+1}+\mbox{ lower order terms in }h.

Using equation (
2.1
) we hence obtain

\displaystyle(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=\sum_{0\leq h\leq\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}}{\ell+1}h^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell+1}\sum_{0\leq h\leq\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{h}h^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right).

By equation (1.13) in
[
Gou72
]
we have

\sum_{0\leq h\leq\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{h}h^{\ell+1}=(-1)^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!

Hence we get

\displaystyle(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=(-1)^{\ell+1}\ell!+O_{\ell}(|\theta-1|)+O_{\ell}\left(\theta^{M}M^{\ell}\right).\qed

We finish this section with a proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

We compute

\begin{split}(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)&=(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}m^{\ell}\theta^{m}\\
&=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}\theta^{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}m^{\ell}\theta^{m}\\
&=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}m^{\ell}\theta^{m+h}\\
&=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{h\leq m\leq M+h}(m-h)^{\ell}\theta^{m}.\end{split}

We split the last summation into three ranges depending on the size of
m
and get

\begin{split}(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)&=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{\ell+1\leq m\leq M}(m-h)^{\ell}\theta^{m}\\
&+\sum_{h=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{h\leq m<\ell+1}(m-h)^{\ell}\theta^{m}\\
&+\sum_{h=1}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}\sum_{M<m\leq M+h}(m-h)^{\ell}\theta^{m}\\
&=\sum_{\ell+1\leq m\leq M}\theta^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}m^{k}\sum_{h=0}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(-h)^{\ell-k}\\
&+\sum_{0\leq m<\ell+1}\theta^{m}\sum_{h=0}^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}\\
&+\sum_{M<m\leq M+\ell+1}\theta^{m}\sum_{h=m-M}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}\end{split}

We now use the identity (
2.1
) for the third last line and deduce that

\displaystyle(\theta-1)^{\ell+1}g_{\ell}(M)=\sum_{0\leq m<\ell+1}\theta^{m}\sum_{h=0}^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(m-h)^{\ell}

(2.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<m\leq\ell+1}\theta^{M+m}\sum_{h=m}^{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{h}(-1)^{\ell+1-h}(M+m-h)^{\ell}

(2.3)

Now the lemma follows in expanding each of the terms
(M+m-h)^{\ell}
.
∎

3.
Volumes of certain sections of polytopes

In this section we provide some estimates on the volumes of intersections
of convex polytopes with certain hyperplanes.
These will be used in the next section in the development of our generalized form of the hyperbola method. The vector space
\mathbb{R}^{s}
is endowed with a fixed inner product that is used to define orthogonality and the Lebesgue measures.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
be an
s
-dimensional convex polytope with
s\geq 1
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a proper face of
\mathcal{P}
.
Let
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
be a hyperplane such that
H\cap\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{F}
.
Let
w\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
such that
\mathcal{P}\subseteq H+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}w
.
For
\delta>0
, let
H_{\delta}:=H+\delta w
. Let
k:=\dim\mathcal{F}
.
We denote by
\meas_{j}
the
j
-dimensional measure
induced by the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{s}
. Then

(i)

for
\delta>0
sufficiently small,

\meas_{s-1}(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P})=c\delta^{s-1-k}+O(\delta^{s-k}),

where
c
is a positive constant that depends on
\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{F}
and
H
.

(ii)

If
s\geq 2
, for
\delta>0
sufficiently small we have

\meas_{s-2}(\partial(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}))=\begin{cases}c^{\prime}\delta^{s-2-k}+O(\delta^{s-1-k}),&\text{ if }k\leq s-2,\\
c^{\prime}+O(\delta),&\text{ if }k=s-1,\end{cases}

where
c^{\prime}
is a positive constant that depends on
\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{F}
and
H
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
be a hyperplane such that
T\cap\mathcal{P}
is a face of
\mathcal{P}
. Let
d:=\dim\mathcal{P}\cap T
.
Let
u\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
be a vector such that
\mathcal{P}\subseteq T+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u
.
For
\kappa>0
, let
T_{\kappa}=T+{\kappa}u
and
T_{\leq\kappa}=T+[0,\kappa]u
. Then

(iii)

for
\delta
and
\kappa
sufficiently small and positive,

\meas_{s-1}(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}\cap T_{\leq\kappa})\ll\begin{cases}\kappa\delta^{s-1-k}&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}\not\subseteq T,\\
\min\{\kappa,\delta\}\delta^{s-2-k}&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}\subseteq T,\end{cases}

where the implicit constant is independent of
\kappa
and
\delta
.

(iv)

For
\delta
and
\kappa
sufficiently small and positive,

\meas_{s-2}(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}\cap T_{\kappa})\ll\begin{cases}\delta^{s-1-k}&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}\not\subseteq T,\\
\delta^{d-k-1}\min\{\delta,\kappa\}^{s-d-1}&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}\subseteq T\text{ and }k\leq s-2,\\
1&\text{ if }k=s-1,\end{cases}

where the implicit constant is independent of
\kappa
and
\delta
.

(v)

If
s\geq 2
, for
\delta
and
\kappa
sufficiently small and positive,

\displaystyle\meas_{s-2}\partial(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}\cap T_{\leq\kappa})\ll\begin{cases}\kappa\delta^{\max\{s-2-k,0\}}+\delta^{s-1-k}&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}\not\subseteq T,\\
\delta^{\max\{s-2-k,0\}}&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}\subseteq T,\end{cases}

where the implicit constant is independent of
\kappa
and
\delta
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
be an affine space of dimension
m
with
k\leq m\leq s-1
. We denote by
p_{V}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V
the orthogonal projection onto
V
. Then

(vi)

for
\delta
and

\min\{\kappa,\delta\}\delta^{s-3-\tilde{k}}&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq T\text{ and }\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\nsubseteq T,\\
\delta^{s-2-\widetilde{k}}&\text{ if }\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq T.\end{cases}

If
k\leq s-2
, there is an
(s-1)
-dimensional face
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
of
\mathcal{P}
that contains
\mathcal{F}
, hence
\tilde{k}=k
and we get

\meas_{s-2}\partial(H_{\delta}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\cap T_{\leq\kappa})\ll\kappa\delta^{s-2-k}

whenever
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\cap\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq T
, which is the case if
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq T
.
Then part
(v)
follows by part
(iv)
. ∎

In the next section we apply the proposition above to the polytope
\mathcal{P}
in Theorem
1.1
with
H=\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=a\}
,
H_{\delta}=\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=a-\delta\}
,
T=\{t_{i_{0}}=0\}
,
T_{\leq\kappa}=\{0\leq t_{i_{0}}\leq\kappa\}
for given
i_{0}\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{i_{0},\kappa}=\mathcal{P}\cap T_{\leq\kappa}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is a full dimensional polytope,

\meas_{s-1}(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P})=c_{\mathcal{P}}\delta^{s-1-k}+O(\delta^{s-k})

for
\delta
sufficiently small, where
c_{\mathcal{P}}
is a positive constant depending only on
\mathcal{P}
and
H
.
Since the face
F=H\cap\mathcal{P}
is not contained in any coordinate hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{s}
, we have
F\nsubseteq T
and hence

\meas_{s-1}(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}\cap T_{\leq\kappa})\ll\kappa\delta^{s-1-k},

and for
V
any coordinate subspace of dimension
m\geq k
,

\meas_{m}p_{V}(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}\cap T_{\leq\kappa})\ll\kappa\delta^{m-k},

Notice that if
m=k
, then the volume of the projection is independent of the choice of
\delta
, hence holds also for
\delta=0
.

4.
Hyperbola method

We consider a function
f:\mathbb{N}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with the following property.

Property I
: Assume that there are non-negative real constants
C_{f,M}\leq C_{f,E}
and
\varDelta>0
and
\varpi_{i}>0
,
1\leq i\leq s
such that for all
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
we have

\sum_{1\leq y_{i}\leq B_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq s}f(\mathbf{y})=C_{f,M}\prod_{i=1}^{s}B_{i}^{\varpi_{i}}+O\left(C_{f,E}\prod_{i=1}^{s}B_{i}^{\varpi_{i}}\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq s}B_{i}\right)^{-\varDelta}\right)

where the implied constant is independent of
f
.

Let
B
be a large real parameter,
\mathcal{K}
a finite index set and
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\alpha_{i,k}\geq 0
for
1\leq i\leq s
and
k\in\mathcal{K}
.

Our goal is to evaluate the sum (if finite)

S^{f}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,k}}\leq B,\ \forall k\in\mathcal{K}\\
y_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq s\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y}).

We start with a heuristic for the expected growth of the sum
S^{f}(B)
. Let
B
be a large real parameter. Consider the contribution to the sum
S^{f}(B)
from a dyadic box where each
y_{i}\sim B^{t_{i}\varpi_{i}^{-1}}
say for real parameters
t_{i}\geq 0
(for example we could think of
\tfrac{1}{2}B^{t_{i}\varpi_{i}^{-1}}\leq y_{i}\leq B^{t_{i}\varpi_{i}^{-1}}
,
1\leq i\leq s
). Such a box is expected to contribute about

\sum_{y_{i}\sim B^{t_{i}\varpi_{i}^{-1}},1\leq i\leq s}f(\mathbf{y})\sim B^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}}

to the sum
S^{f}(B)
.
In order for such a box to lie in the summation range we roughly speaking need

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\leq 1,\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

(4.1)

and

t_{i}\geq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

(4.2)

The system of equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) defines a polyhedron
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
. We make the following two assumptions on
\mathcal{P}
.

Assumption 4.1
.

We assume that
\mathcal{P}
is bounded and non-degenerate in the sense that it is not contained in a
s-1
dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{s}
.

Assumption 4.2
.

The face
F
on which the function
\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}
takes its maximum on
\mathcal{P}
is not contained in a coordinate hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{s}
(with coordinates
t_{i}
).

The linear function

\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}

takes its maximum on a face of
\mathcal{P}
. We call the maximal value
a
and assume that this maximum is obtained on a
k
-dimensional face of
\mathcal{P}
.

Let
H_{\delta}
be the hypersurface given by

\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=a-\delta.

It comes equipped with an
s-1
dimensional measure which is obtained from the pull-back of the standard Lebesgue measure to any of its coordinate plane projections. In the following we write
\meas
for this measure.
Note that Proposition
3.1
(i)
shows that Assumption
4.1
implies that the following holds.

There is a constant
c_{\mathcal{P}}
such that for
\delta>0
sufficiently small (in terms of
\mathcal{P}
) we have

|\meas_{s-1}(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P})-c_{\mathcal{P}}\delta^{s-1-k}|\leq C\delta^{s-k},

(4.3)

for a sufficiently large constant
C
, depending only on
\mathcal{P}
.

Let
\mathbf{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}
and
1<\theta\leq 2
a parameter to be chosen later. We define box counting functions

B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{i}\in[\theta_{i}^{l_{i}},\theta_{i}^{l_{i}+1}),1\leq i\leq s\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y}),

where

\theta_{i}=\theta^{\varpi_{i}^{-1}},\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

Assume that
f
satisfies Property I. Then by inclusion-exclusion we evaluate

\begin{split}B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)&=C_{f,M}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(\theta_{i}^{\varpi_{i}(l_{i}+1)}-\theta_{i}^{\varpi_{i}l_{i}}\right)+O\left(C_{f,E}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\theta_{i}^{l_{i}\varpi_{i}}\left(\min_{i}\theta_{i}^{l_{i}}\right)^{-\varDelta}\right)\\
&=C_{f,M}(\theta-1)^{s}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\theta^{l_{i}}+O\left(C_{f,E}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\theta^{l_{i}}\left(\min_{i}\theta_{i}^{l_{i}}\right)^{-\varDelta}\right).\end{split}

We deduce that

\displaystyle B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)=C_{f,M}(\theta-1)^{s}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}+O\left(C_{f,E}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}\left(\min_{i}\theta^{\varpi_{i}^{-1}l_{i}}\right)^{-\varDelta}\right).

Recall that we assumed in Property I that

C_{f,M}\leq C_{f,E}.

Hence we have

B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)\ll C_{f,E}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}.

(4.4)

We note that the sum

E_{1}^{f}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,k}}\leq B,\ k\in\mathcal{K}\\
y_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leq i\leq s\\
\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\varpi_{i}}>B^{a}\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y})

is empty.

Let
\tilde{A}
be a large natural number, which we view as a parameter to be specified later.

We set

S_{1,f}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,k}}\leq B,\ k\in\mathcal{K}\\
y_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leq i\leq s\\
y_{i}\geq(\log B)^{\tilde{A}}\forall 1\leq i\leq s\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y}),

and note that

S_{1,f}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,k}}\leq B,\ k\in\mathcal{K}\\
y_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leq i\leq s\\
\prod_{i}y_{i}^{\varpi_{i}}\leq B^{a}\\
y_{i}\geq(\log B)^{\tilde{A}}\forall 1\leq i\leq s\end{subarray}}f(\mathbf{y}).

We now cover the sum
S_{1,f}
with boxes of the form
B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{+}
be the set of
\mathbf{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}
such that the following inequalities hold

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}l_{i}\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\theta},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle l_{i}+1\geq\frac{\varpi_{i}\tilde{A}\log\log B}{\log\theta},\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

Similarly, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-}
be the set of
\mathbf{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}
such that the following inequalities hold

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}(l_{i}+1)\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\theta},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle l_{i}\geq\frac{\varpi_{i}\tilde{A}\log\log B}{\log\theta},\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

Let
C_{5}
be a positive constant such that

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}\leq C_{5},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

We define
\mathcal{L}^{-}
to be the set of
\mathbf{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}
such that the following inequalities hold

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}l_{i}\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle l_{i}\geq\frac{\varpi_{i}\tilde{A}\log\log B}{\log\theta},\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

Then we have

\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}}B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)=S_{1,f}^{-}\leq S_{1,f}\leq S_{1,f}^{+}=\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{+}}B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta),

where we read the last line as a definition for
S_{1,f}^{-}
and
S_{1,f}^{+}
.
Note that the coverings into boxes do not depend on the function
f
but only on the summation conditions on the variables
y_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq s
.

Let
r^{+}(l)
(resp.
r^{-}(l)
) be the set of
\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{+}
(resp.
\mathcal{L}^{-}
) such that

\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}=l.

We recall that

\displaystyle B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)=C_{f,M}(\theta-1)^{s}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}+O\left(C_{f,E}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}\left(\min_{i}\theta^{\varpi_{i}^{-1}l_{i}}\right)^{-\varDelta}\right).

This leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}}B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)=C_{f,M}\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}(\theta-1)^{s}+O\left(C_{f,E}\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}(\log B)^{-\varDelta\tilde{A}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{l\leq a\log B/\log\theta}r^{-}(l)\theta^{l}+O\left(C_{f,E}\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}(\log B)^{-\varDelta\tilde{A}}\right)

Every vector
\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}
satisfies the bound

\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}\leq a\frac{\log B}{\log\theta},

and hence

l_{i}\leq a\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

This leads to the bound

\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}}\theta^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}}\ll\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s}B^{a}.

We deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}}B_{f}(\mathbf{l},\theta)=(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{l\leq a\log B/\log\theta}r^{-}(l)\theta^{l}

\displaystyle+O\left(C_{f,E}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s}B^{a}(\log B)^{-\varDelta\tilde{A}}\right).

Let
\tilde{r}(l)
be the number of
\mathbf{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}=l
and the following inequalities hold

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}l_{i}\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

Note that
\tilde{r}(l)
is the number of lattice points in the polytope given by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=l,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle t_{i}\geq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

Finally, for
1\leq i_{0}\leq s
let
r_{\tilde{A},i_{0}}(l)
be the number of
\mathbf{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}=l
and

l_{i_{0}}\leq\frac{\varpi_{i}\tilde{A}\log\log B}{\log\theta},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}l_{i}\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\theta},\quad k\in\mathcal{K}.

Note that we have

r^{-}(l)=\tilde{r}(l)+O\left(\sum_{1\leq i_{0}\leq s}r_{\tilde{A},i_{0}}(l)\right).

We now stop a moment to introduce some more auxiliary polytopes. We recall that
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
is the polytope given by the system of equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).

For
1\leq i_{0}\leq s
and
\kappa>0
we introduce the polytope
\mathcal{P}_{i_{0},\kappa}
given by the system of equations

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\leq 1,\quad k\in\mathcal{K}

and

t_{i}\geq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq s,

and

t_{i_{0}}\leq\kappa.

I.e.
\mathcal{P}_{i_{0},\kappa}
is obtained from intersecting
\mathcal{P}
with the halfspace
t_{i_{0}}\leq\kappa
. Let
H_{\delta}
be defined as before, i.e. the hyperplane given by

\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=a-\delta.

By Proposition
3.1
(iii)
and under the Assumption of
4.1
and
4.2
we have the following property.

Let
0\leq k\leq s-1
. Assume that
\kappa>0
and
\delta>0
are sufficiently small in terms of the data describing
\mathcal{P}
. Then we have

\meas(H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}_{i_{0},\kappa})\ll\kappa\delta^{s-1-k}.

(4.5)

Remark 4.3
.

Note that in the case
k=0
and where the maximal face
F
is not contained in a coordinate hyperplane, the intersection
H_{\delta}\cap\mathcal{P}_{i_{0},\kappa}
is empty for
\epsilon
and
\delta
sufficiently small.

In our applications it will take
\kappa
of size
\kappa\ll\frac{\log\log B}{\log B}
. We next evaluate the function
r^{-}(l)
asymptotically.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
0\leq k\leq s-1
. Assume that Assumption
4.1
and Assumption
4.2
hold.
Let
l
be an integer with

(a-\delta)\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)\leq l\leq a\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right),

for
\delta
sufficiently small, depending only on
\mathcal{P}
, as in Proposition
3.1
(i)
,
(iii)
.

Then we have

\displaystyle r^{-}(l)=c_{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-1-k}

\displaystyle+O\left(\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-2}+\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-k}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{\log\log B}{\log B}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-1-k}\right).

Here we read
0^{0}=1
.

If

l>a\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right),

then we have
r^{-}(l)=0
.

Remark 4.5
.

Note that exactly the same asymptotic also holds for
r^{+}(l)
, but then in the range

(a-\delta)\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\leq l\leq a\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}.

Proof.

We recall that
\tilde{r}(l)
counts lattice points in the polytope
P(l,B,\theta)
given by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=l,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,k}\varpi_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\leq\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5},\quad k\in\mathcal{K},

\displaystyle t_{i}\geq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

We observe that
P(l,B,\theta)
is equal to the polytope
\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)\mathcal{P}
, i.e. the polytope
\mathcal{P}
blown up by a factor of
\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}
, intersected with the hyperplane
\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)H_{\delta^{\prime}}
given by

\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}=\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)(a-\delta^{\prime}),

where
\delta^{\prime}\geq 0
is chosen such that

l=\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)(a-\delta^{\prime}).

I.e. our task is to count lattice points in the polytope
\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)(\mathcal{P}\cap H_{\delta^{\prime}})
, which is the same as counting integer lattice points in the projection of this polytope to one of the coordinate hyperplanes. Here we can apply Davenport’s lemma
[
Dav51
]
.

By equation (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle\meas P(l,B,\theta)=c_{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)^{-1}l\right)^{s-1-k}

\displaystyle+O\left(\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)^{-1}l\right)^{s-k}\right).

We can rewrite this as

\displaystyle\meas P(l,B,\theta)=c_{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-1-k}

\displaystyle+O\left(\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-k}+\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-2}\right).

The measure of the projection of
\mathcal{P}\cap H_{\delta^{\prime}}
to various coordinate spaces is bounded. Hence the measure of the projections of dimension at most
s-2
of the blown-up polytope
\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)(\mathcal{P}\cap H_{\delta^{\prime}})
is bounded by

\ll\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-2}.

By Davenport’s lemma
[
Dav51
]
and for
\delta
sufficiently small we find that

\displaystyle\tilde{r}(l)=c_{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-1-k}

\displaystyle+O\left(\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-2}+\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-k}\right).

Finally, by the same arguments as before and equation (
4.5
) we have for any
1\leq i_{0}\leq s
that

\displaystyle r_{\tilde{A},i_{0}}(l)\ll_{\tilde{A}}\frac{\log\log B}{\log B}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-1-k}+\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-2}.

Here we use again that all volumes of projections of the corresponding polytope are bounded.

∎

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be a parameter. We write

S_{1,f}^{-}=M_{1,f}^{-}+E_{2,f}^{-}+E_{3,f}^{-}

with

M_{1,f}^{-}=(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{(a-\delta_{0})(\log B/\log-C_{5})\leq l\leq a(\log B/\log\theta-C_{5})}r^{-}(l)\theta^{l}

and

E_{2,f}^{-}\ll C_{f,E}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s}B^{a}(\log B)^{-\varDelta\tilde{A}}

and

E_{3,f}^{-}\ll(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{l\leq(a-\delta_{0})(\log B/\log\theta-C_{5})}r^{-}(l)\theta^{l}.

First we bound the error term
E_{3,f}^{-}
. For this we observe that if
l\leq(a-\delta_{0})\log B/\log\theta
, then

\theta^{l}\ll B^{a-\delta_{0}}.

Moreover, as each of the
l_{i}
in the counting function
r^{-}(l)
is bounded by
\ll\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}
we have

\sum_{l\leq a\log B/\log\theta}r^{-}(l)\ll\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s}.

This gives the estimate

E_{3,f}^{-}\ll(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s}B^{a-\delta_{0}}.

We conclude that

S_{1,f}^{-}=M_{1,f}^{-}+O\left(C_{f,E}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s}B^{a}(\log B)^{-\varDelta\tilde{A}}\right),

as long as

\Delta\tilde{A}\log\log B\leq\delta_{0}\log B.

(4.6)

We now use Lemma
4.4
to first evaluate the main term
M_{1,f}^{-}
. For
B
sufficiently large and
\delta_{0}
sufficiently small, we have

\begin{split}&M_{1,f}^{-}=(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{(a-\delta_{0})\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)\leq l\leq a\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)}r^{-}(l)\theta^{l}\\
&=(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{(a-\delta_{0})\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)\leq l\leq a\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)}c_{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-1-k}\theta^{l}\\
&+O\left(C_{f,M}(\theta-1)^{s}\sum_{l\leq a\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-2}\theta^{l}\right)\\
&+O\left(C_{f,M}(\theta-1)^{s}\sum_{l\leq a\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-k}\theta^{l}\right)\\
&+O\left(\frac{\log\log B}{\log B}(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{l\leq a\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{s-1}\left(a-\frac{\log\theta}{\log B}l\right)^{s-1-k}\theta^{l}\right).\end{split}

We can also write
M_{1,f}^{-}
as

\begin{split}&(\theta-1)^{s}C_{f,M}\sum_{(a-\delta_{0})\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)\leq l\leq a\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-C_{5}\right)}c_{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}\right)^{k}\left(a\frac{\log B}{\log\theta}-l\right)^{s-1-k}\theta^{l}\\

5.
m
-full numbers

Let

be a natural number. We recall that an integer

We introduce the function that counts the number of

\begin{split}F_{m}(B):&=\sharp\left\{1\leq y\leq B,v_{p}(y)\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{\geq m}\forall p\right\}\\
&=\sharp\left\{1\leq y\leq B,y\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}.\end{split}

Lemma 5.1

(
[
ES34
,
BG58
]
)

For each

(5.2)

For a square-free positive integer

F_{m}(B,d):=\sharp\left\{1\leq y\leq B,d\mid y,\ y\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}.

(5.3)

In this section we prove an asymptotic formula for the function

.
We will first do it for the case that
d
is a prime. We will then inductively on the number of prime factors of

provide a general asymptotic formula.
First we provide a form of inclusion-exclusion lemma, which expresses

for a prime number
p

. Before we state the lemma, we introduce a convenient piece of notation. For

\rho_{m}(k,r):=\sharp\left\{1\leq k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\leq(m-1):\ k=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{r}\right\}.

Note that

is zero, unless

Lemma 5.2
.

For
m\geq 2
one has

\begin{split}F_{m}(B,p)=&F_{m}(Bp^{-m})+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)F_{m}\left(Bp^{-(2r+1)m-k}\right)\\
&+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)F_{m}\left(Bp^{-(2r-1)m-k}\right)\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)F_{m}\left(Bp^{-2rm-k}\right)\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)F_{m}\left(Bp^{-2rm-k}\right).\end{split}

Note that the summations in Lemma
5.2
are in fact finite, as
F_{m}(P)=0

Proof.

We start the proof in reinterpreting terms of the shape
F_{m}\left(Bp^{-K}\right)
for some
K>0
as

F_{m}\left(Bp^{-K}\right)=\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{K}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}.

Then the right hand side in the identity in Lemma
5.2
becomes

\begin{split}RHS=&\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{m}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}\\
&+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{(2r+1)m+k}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}\\
&+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{(2r-1)m+k}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{2rm+k}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{2rm+k}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}.\end{split}

(5.4)

For any
K\geq 0
we use the identity

\begin{split}\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{K}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}&=\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{K}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full},p\nmid l\right\}\\
&+\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{K+m}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{K+m+k}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full},p\nmid l\right\}\end{split}

(5.5)

We use this identity for the terms in the third line in (
5.4
). We observe that the terms counting

with

m
-full identically cancel with the fourth line in (
5.4
). Hence we obtain

\begin{split}RHS=&\sharp\left\{1\leq p^{m}l\leq B,l\mbox{ is $m$-full}\right\}\\

Lemma

with coefficients

\begin{split}a_{m}(\mu)=&\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)\id_{[\mu=(2r+1)m+k]}\\
&+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)\id_{[\mu=(2r-1)m+k]}\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)\id_{[\mu=2rm+k]}\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)\id_{[\mu=2rm+k]}.\end{split}

Note that all the appearing sums are in fact finite and hence we can reorder them freely. Our next goal is to get more understanding on the coefficients

(in particular their size) and hence we group them in a generating series. Define

G_{m}(x):=\sum_{\mu=m+1}^{\infty}a_{m}(\mu)x^{\mu}.

A first rough bound on the coefficients
a_{m}(\mu)
can be obtained via the estimate

a_{m}(\mu)\leq 2\sum_{1\leq t\leq\frac{\mu}{m}}\sum_{k=t}^{t(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,t)=2\sum_{1\leq t\leq\frac{\mu}{m}}(m-1)^{t}.

For

we obtain

whereas for
m=2
we have the estimate

a_{m}(\mu)\leq 2\frac{\mu}{m}.

In particular we deduce that there is some constant
R_{m}
only depending on
m
such that the power series
G_{m}(x)
is absolutely convergent for
|x|<R_{m}
. Moreover, in choosing
R_{m}
sufficiently small we can also assume that the sum

\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=t}^{t(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,t)x^{tm+k}

is absolutely convergent. Our next goal is to write the generating series
G_{m}(x)
as a fractional function and in this way realise that it has a larger radius of absolute convergence than the bound that is obtained from the very rough estimate on
a_{m}(\mu)
. For this we observe that for
|x|<R_{m}
we can express

as

\begin{split}G_{m}(x)=&\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)x^{(2r+1)m+k}\\
&+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)x^{(2r-1)m+k}\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r-1}^{(2r-1)(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r-1)x^{2rm+k}\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2r}^{2r(m-1)}\rho_{m}(k,2r)x^{2rm+k}.\end{split}

We can now compute the generating function
G_{m}(x)
as

\begin{split}G_{m}(x)=&\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}x^{(2r+1)m}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)^{2r}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}x^{(2r-1)m}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)^{2r-1}\\
&-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}x^{2rm}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)^{2r-1}-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}x^{2rm}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)^{2r}.\end{split}

In the area of absolute convergence one may reorder the sums as

\begin{split}G_{m}(x)=&\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{t}x^{(t+1)m}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)^{t}+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{t+1}x^{tm}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)^{t}\\
=&(x^{m}-1)(-1)x^{m}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)\left(1-(-1)x^{m}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k}\right)\right)^{-1}\\
=&(1-x^{m})x^{m}x\frac{x^{m-1}-1}{x-1}\left(1+x^{m+1}\frac{x^{m-1}-1}{x-1}\right)^{-1}\\
=&(1-x^{m})x^{m+1}\frac{x^{m-1}-1}{x-1}(x-1)\left(x-1+x^{m+1}(x^{m-1}-1)\right)^{-1}\\
=&(1-x^{m})x^{m+1}(x^{m-1}-1)\left(x-1+x^{m+1}(x^{m-1}-1)\right)^{-1}\\
=&(1-x^{m})x^{m+1}(x^{m-1}-1)\left(x-1+x^{2m}-x^{m+1}\right)^{-1}\end{split}

We observe that

x^{2m}-x^{m+1}+x-1=(x^{m}-1)(x^{m}+1)-x(x^{m}-1)=(x^{m}-1)(x^{m}-x+1).

Hence we obtain

G_{m}(x)=-x^{m+1}(x^{m-1}-1)\left(x^{m}-x+1\right)^{-1}.

In the interval

the function
x^{m}-x
takes its minimum at

, and at this point
x^{m}-x=m^{-\frac{1}{m-1}}(m^{-1}-1)
. In particular we observe that the Taylor series for
G_{m}(x)
is absolutely convergent in the interval

.

\pi^{\mathfrak{c}}|_{\mathscr{Y}^{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}})}
are all isomorphic to

(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{r}
, and

\mathscr{Y}^{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}})\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{s}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}})
is the subset of
points
\mathbf{y}\in\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{c}^{D_{i}}
that satisfy

\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\mathbf{y}^{D_{\sigma}}\mathfrak{c}^{-D_{\sigma}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}.

(6.1)

Let
N
be the lattice of cocharacters of
X
. Then
\Sigma\subseteq N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
For every
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, let
\nu_{i}
be the unique generator of
\rho_{i}\cap N
.
For every torus invariant divisor
D=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}D_{i}
of
X
and for every

\sigma\in\Sigma_{max}
, let
u_{\sigma,D}
be the character of
N
determined by

u_{\sigma,D}(\nu_{j})=a_{j}
for all
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}
, and define

D(\sigma):=D-\sum_{i=1}^{s}u_{\sigma,D}(\nu_{i})D_{i}
.
Then
D
and
D(\sigma)
are linearly equivalent.
For every
i,j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, let
\beta_{\sigma,i,j}:=-u_{\sigma,D_{j}}(\nu_{i})
.
Then, for every
i,j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, we have
\beta_{\sigma,i,j}=0
whenever

j\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
, and whenever
i\neq j
are both in
\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}
. Hence,

D_{j}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}D_{j}&\text{if }j\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},\\
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}D_{i}&\text{if }j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}.\end{cases}

(6.2)

Lemma 6.1
.

For every
i,j\in\{i,\dots,s\}
and
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{max}
we have

\beta_{\sigma,i,j}=-\sum_{l\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma^{\prime}}}\beta_{\sigma^{\prime},i,l}\beta_{\sigma,l,j}.

Proof.

From the equality
D_{j}(\sigma^{\prime})=(D_{j}(\sigma))(\sigma^{\prime})

we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{s}u_{\sigma,D_{j}}(\nu_{l})D_{l}(\sigma^{\prime})=\sum_{l=1}^{s}u_{\sigma,D_{j}}\left(\nu_{l})(D_{l}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}u_{\sigma^{\prime},D_{l}}(\nu_{i})D_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left(u_{\sigma,D_{j}}(\nu_{i})-\sum_{l=1}^{s}u_{\sigma,D_{j}(\nu_{l})}u_{\sigma^{\prime},D_{l}}(\nu_{i})D_{i}\right)D_{i}.\qed

6.2.
Polytopes

In this section, we fix a semiample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
L=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}D_{i}
,
and we study a number of polytopes associated to
L
.
The content of this section is purely combinatorial,
in particular, it does not depend on the base field
\mathbb{K}
where
X
is defined.

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
, we write
L(\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,\sigma}D_{i}
.
Then
\alpha_{i,\sigma}=0
for all
i\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}
by construction.

Remark 6.2
.

Since
L
is semiample,
L(\sigma)
is effective for all
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}

by
[
CLS11
, Proposition 6.1.1]
; that is,
\alpha_{i,\sigma}\geq 0
for all

i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and all
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
If, moreover,
L
is ample, then
\alpha_{i,\sigma}>0
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
and
all
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
by
[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.1.14]
.

Assumption 6.3
.

We assume that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
there exists
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}

such that
\alpha_{i,\sigma}>0
.

We observe that Assumption
6.3
is satisfied
if
L
is ample by Remark
6.2
,
or if
L
is linearly equivalent to an effective divisor

\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}D_{i}
with
b_{i}>0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}

by Lemma
6.4
below.

6.2.1.
The polytope
P_{L}

We describe a classical polytope associated to
L
that we use in
Section
6.3
to study the height function defined by
L
.

We denote by
M
the lattice of characters of
T
, dual to
N
,
and by
M_{\mathbb{R}}
the vector space
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
Similarly, we set
\pic(X)_{\mathbb{R}}:=\pic(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
We recall that there is an exact sequence (e.g.
[
CLS11
, Theorem 4.2.1]
)

0\to M_{\mathbb{R}}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{R}D_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}\pic(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\to 0,

such that the effective cone
\eff(X)
of
X
is the image under
\varphi
of the cone generated by the
effective torus invariant divisors

C:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}D_{i}:a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\geq 0\right\}\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{R}D_{i}.

Since
L
is semiample,

P_{L}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}:m(\nu_{i})+a_{i}\geq 0\ \forall i\in\{1,\dots,s\}\}.

is a polytope with vertices

\{-u_{\sigma,L}:\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}\}

by
[
CLS11
, Proposition 4.3.8, Theorem 6.1.7]
. In particular,

P_{L}=\left\{\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}-\lambda_{\sigma}u_{\sigma,L}:(\lambda_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\Sigma_{\max}},\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}=1\right\}.

(6.3)

Lemma 6.4
.

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,

(tL+M_{\mathbb{R}})\cap C=\left\{\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}L(\sigma):(\lambda_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{\Sigma_{\max}},\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}=t\right\}.

Proof.

By (
6.3
)
L+P_{L}
is the polytope with vertices
\{L(\sigma):\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}\}
.
Since
(tL+M_{\mathbb{R}})\cap C=tL+P_{tL}=t(L+P_{L})

by
[
CLS11
, Exercise 4.3.2]
, the statement follows.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

If
L
is ample, then there exists a positive integer
t

such that
[t^{-1}L]=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{m_{i}}D_{i}\right]
with
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Indeed,
[L]
has at least one representative of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}D_{i}

with
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
by Lemma
6.4

and Remark
6.2
.
Hence, it suffices to choose any positive integer
t
such that

tb_{i}^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.

6.2.2.
The polytope
\widetilde{P}

We investigate some polytopes associated to
[L]
that we use for
the application of the hyperbola method
in Sections
6.5
–
6.6
.

We identify
\mathbb{R}^{s}
with the space of linear functions on

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{R}D_{i}
by defining

\mathbf{t}(D_{i})=t_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and all
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
.
Under this identification, the dual of
\pic(X)
is
the linear subspace
\widetilde{H}
of
\mathbb{R}^{s}
defined by

t_{j}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}\quad\forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},

(6.4)

for one, or equivalently all,
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}

(cf. Lemma
6.1
).

Let
\widetilde{P}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
be the polyhedron
defined by

t_{i}\geq 0\ \forall i\in\{1,\dots,s\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,\sigma}t_{i}\leq 1\ \forall\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}.

(6.5)

Then
\widetilde{P}
is a full dimensional convex polytope by
Remark
6.2
and Assumption
6.3
.
Moreover,
\cone(\widetilde{P})
is dual to the cone

C
defined above.
For every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
, let

\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}:=\widetilde{P}\cap\cone\left(\widetilde{P}\cap\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i,\sigma}t_{i}=1\right\}\right),

so that

\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}=\widetilde{P}.

(6.6)

We observe that the polytopes
\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}
depend only
on the class of
L
in
\pic(X)
and not on the chosen representative
\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}D_{i}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

(i)

For every
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\max}
,

\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}\cap\widetilde{P}_{\sigma^{\prime}}=\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}\cap\left\{t_{j}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}\quad\forall j\in\{l\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}:\alpha_{l,\sigma^{\prime}}\neq 0\}.\right\}.

(ii)

Under Assumption
6.3
we have

\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}=\widetilde{P}\cap\widetilde{H}
.

(iii)

If
L
is ample, then

\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}=\widetilde{P}\cap\left\{t_{j}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}\quad\forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}\right\}

for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
In particular,
\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}

is the polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
defined by

t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\geq 0,\quad t_{j}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}\quad\forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},\quad\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}t_{i}\leq 1.

Proof.

By definition,
\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}
is the set of elements
\mathbf{t}\in\widetilde{P}

such that
\mathbf{t}(L(\sigma^{\prime}))\leq\mathbf{t}(L(\sigma))

for all
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\max}
. By (
6.2
) we have

\mathbf{t}(L(\sigma)-L(\sigma^{\prime}))=\mathbf{t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\alpha_{j,\sigma^{\prime}}(D_{j}(\sigma)-D_{j})\right)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\alpha_{j,\sigma^{\prime}}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}\right)-{t_{j}}\right)

Hence,
(i)
and
the inclusion
\supseteq
in
(iii)
follow.
For the reverse inclusion in
(iii)
we fix
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}
.
By
[
Sal98
, Lemma 8.9]

there is

\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\max}
such that

\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}\cap\mathcal{J}_{\sigma^{\prime}}=\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}\smallsetminus\{j\}
.
Then

\alpha_{j,\sigma^{\prime}}\left({t_{j}}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}\right)=\mathbf{t}(L(\sigma^{\prime})-L(\sigma))\leq 0

for all
\mathbf{t}\in\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}
and
\alpha_{j,\sigma^{\prime}}>0

by Remark
6.2
.
Part
(ii)
follows
from
(i)
,
as
\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}=\widetilde{P}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\cap\widetilde{H}

for every
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\max}
by
(i)

together with Assumption
6.3
, and we conclude by (
6.6
).
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Assume that
L
is ample. Let
\varpi=(\varpi_{1},\dots,\varpi_{s})\in\mathbb{R}^{s}_{>0}
.
Let
\widetilde{F}
be the face of
\widetilde{P}
where the maximum value

a(L,\varpi)
of
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}
is attained.
Then

(i)

a(L,\varpi)>0
and
\widetilde{F}\subseteq\widetilde{H}
.

(ii)

If, additionally,
[L]=[\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}D_{i}]
in
\pic(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
a(L,\varpi)=1

and
\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{H}\cap\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}=1\}
.
In particular,
\widetilde{F}\cap\{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}>0\}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Since
s\geq 1
and
\widetilde{P}
is full dimensional, we have
a(L,\varpi)>0
.
For every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
, let

\widetilde{F}_{\sigma}:=\widetilde{F}\cap\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}
. Fix
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
Let
\mathbf{t}\in\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}\smallsetminus\widetilde{H}
.
By (
6.4
) and Lemma
6.6

there exists
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}
such that

t_{j}<\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}
.
Let
t^{\prime}_{j}:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}
.
For each
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}\smallsetminus\{j\}
let
t^{\prime}_{i}:=t_{i}
.
Then
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s})\in\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}
, and

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t^{\prime}_{i}>\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}
by construction.
Hence
\mathbf{t}\notin\widetilde{F}_{\sigma}
.
Thus
\widetilde{F}_{\sigma}\subseteq\widetilde{H}
, which implies

\widetilde{F}_{\sigma}\subseteq\widetilde{F}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
for all
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
Since this proof works for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
, we conclude that

\widetilde{F}_{\sigma}=\widetilde{F}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
for all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
Now
(i)
follows, because

\widetilde{F}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\widetilde{F}_{\sigma}
.

For
(ii)
we recall that

\alpha_{i,\sigma}=\varpi_{i}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\varpi_{j}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}

for all
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
. Hence, we have

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}t_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}

for all
\mathbf{t}\in\widetilde{H}
and for all
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
Since
\widetilde{H}
is the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{s}
dual to
\pic(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
,
a torus invariant divisor
D
satisfies
\mathbf{t}(D)=0
for all
\mathbf{t}\in\widetilde{H}

if and only if
D
is a principal divisor. Since
D_{1},\dots,D_{s}
are not principal divisors, then

\widetilde{H}\cap\{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}>0\}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\mathbf{t}\in\widetilde{H}
with
t_{1},\dots,t_{s}>0
,
up to rescaling
\mathbf{t}
by a positive real number we can assume that

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}=1
, and hence
\mathbf{t}\in\widetilde{F}
.
∎

6.2.3.
The geometric constant

We compute certain volumes of polytopes that appear in the leading constant
of the asymptotic formula
1.1
.

Fix
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
Since
X
is smooth, we know that
\pic(X)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\mathbb{Z}[D_{i}]
.
We identify
\mathbb{R}^{r}
with the space of linear functions on

\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\mathbb{R}[D_{i}]
by defining

\mathbf{z}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}a_{i}[D_{i}]\right):=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}a_{i}z_{i}

for all
\mathbf{z}=(z_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
.
Let
\lambda_{[L]}:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
be the evaluation at
[L]
;
that is,
\lambda_{[L]}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}z_{i}
.
Fix
\tilde{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
such that
\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}\neq 0
.
The change of variables
x=\lambda_{[L]}(\mathbf{z})
,

\mathrm{d}x=\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}\mathrm{d}z_{\tilde{i}}
, gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}g\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\mathrm{d}z_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{z_{\tilde{i}}=\left(x-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},i\neq\tilde{i}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}z_{i}\right)/\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}}g\ \alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}^{-1}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},i\neq\tilde{i}}\mathrm{d}z_{i}\right)\mathrm{d}x

for all integrable functions
g:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 6.8
.

The volume

\alpha(L):=\int_{\eff(X)^{*}\cap\lambda_{[L]}^{-1}(1)}\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}^{-1}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},i\neq\tilde{i}}\mathrm{d}z_{i}

is positive and independent of the choice of
\tilde{i}
and of the choice of
\sigma
.

Proof.

The transversal intersection
\eff(X)^{*}\cap\lambda_{[L]}^{-1}(1)

is an
(r-1)
-dimensional polytope, hence the volume is positive.
The independence of the choice of
\tilde{i}
is clear.
The independence of the choice of
\sigma
is a consequence of
Lemma
6.1
.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Assume that
L
is ample and
[L]=[\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}D_{i}]
with
\varpi_{1},\dots,\varpi_{s}>0
.
For
\delta\geq 0
, let
H_{\delta}
be the hyperplane defined by
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}=1-\delta
.
Then for
\delta>0
small enough,

\meas_{s-1}\left(H_{\delta}\cap\widetilde{P}\right)=c\delta^{s-r}+O(\delta^{s-r+1})
, where

\meas_{s-1}
is the
(s-1)
-dimensional measure on
H_{\delta}
given by

\prod_{1\leq i\leq s,i\neq\tilde{i}}(\varpi_{i}\mathrm{d}t_{i})

for any choice of
\tilde{i}\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, and

c=\frac{\alpha(L)}{(s-r)!}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\varpi_{i}.

Proof.

Since
L
is ample, the decomposition (
6.6
)
and Lemma
6.6
give

\meas_{s-1}\left(H_{\delta}\cap\widetilde{P}\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\meas_{s-1}\left(H_{\delta}\cap\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}\right).

Fix
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
.
Let
V_{\delta,\sigma}:=\meas_{s-1}(H_{\delta}\cap\widetilde{P}_{\sigma})
.
By the choice of
L
we have

\alpha_{i,\sigma}=\varpi_{i}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\varpi_{j}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
,
and hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}t_{i}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\varpi_{j}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}-t_{j}\right)

for every
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
.
Then
H_{0}\cap\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}\subseteq\widetilde{H}

by Lemma
6.6
(iii)
.
Fix
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s})\in H_{0}\cap\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}
, and fix
\tilde{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
.
Then
V_{\delta,\sigma}=\meas_{s-1}\left(\left(H_{\delta}\cap\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}\right)+\delta\xi\right)

is the volume of the polytope given by

t_{i}\geq\delta\xi_{i}\ \forall i\in\{1,\dots,s\},\ t_{j}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}\ \forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},\ \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}t_{i}\leq 1+\delta,\ \sum_{i=1}^{s}\varpi_{i}t_{i}=1,

with respect to the measure
\prod_{1\leq i\leq s,i\neq\tilde{i}}(\varpi_{i}\mathrm{d}t_{i})
.

For all
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
, let
u_{i}=t_{i}
.
For all
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}
, let
u_{j}=\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}-t_{j}\right)/\delta
, i.e.,

u_{j}=\delta^{-1}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},i\neq\tilde{i}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}t_{i}+\frac{\beta_{\sigma,\tilde{i},j}}{\varpi_{\tilde{i}}}\left(1-\sum_{1\leq i\leq s,i\neq\tilde{i}}\varpi_{i}t_{i}\right)-t_{j}\right).

Let
g(\mathbf{u}):=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\varpi_{j}u_{j}

and
h(\mathbf{u}):=1-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},i\neq\tilde{i}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}u_{i}
.
Then
\delta^{r-s}V_{\delta,\sigma}
is the volume of the polytope given by

\displaystyle u_{j}\geq 0\ \forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},\quad g(\mathbf{u})\leq 1,

\displaystyle u_{i}\geq\delta\xi_{i}\ \forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}\smallsetminus\{\tilde{i}\},\quad\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},i\neq\tilde{i}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}u_{i}\leq 1+\delta g(\mathbf{u}),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},i\neq\tilde{i}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}u_{i}+\frac{\beta_{\sigma,\tilde{i},j}}{\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}}h(\mathbf{u})\geq\delta\left(u_{j}+\xi_{j}-\frac{\beta_{\sigma,\tilde{i},j}}{\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}}g(\mathbf{u})\right)\ \forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},

with respect to the measure

\varpi_{\tilde{i}}\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}^{-1}\prod_{1\leq i\leq s,i\neq\tilde{i}}(\varpi_{i}\mathrm{d}u_{i})
, as

\displaystyle\left|\det\left((\partial u_{j}/\partial t_{l})_{1\leq j,l\leq s,j\neq\tilde{i},l\neq\tilde{i}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\delta^{r-s}\det\left(\left(\beta_{\sigma,\tilde{i},l}\varpi_{l}/\varpi_{\tilde{i}}+\delta_{l,j}\right)_{j,l\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta^{r-s}\left(1+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,\tilde{i},j}\varpi_{j}/\varpi_{\tilde{i}}\right)=\delta^{r-s}\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}/\varpi_{\tilde{i}},

where
\delta_{l,j}=0
if
l\neq j
and
\delta_{j,j}=1
.
By dominated convergence
we can compute
c=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{r-s}V_{\delta,\sigma}

as the volume of the polytope given by

\displaystyle u_{j}\geq 0\ \forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},\quad g(\mathbf{u})\leq 1,

\displaystyle u_{i}\geq 0\ \forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma},\quad\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}u_{i}\geq 0\ \forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},\quad\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\alpha_{i,\sigma}u_{i}=1,

with respect to the measure
\varpi_{\tilde{i}}\alpha_{\tilde{i},\sigma}^{-1}\prod_{1\leq i\leq s,i\neq\tilde{i}}(\varpi_{i}\mathrm{d}u_{i})
.
We conclude as

In this subsection we give a heuristic argument based on the hyperbola method
in support of

PSTVA21
, Conjecture 1.1]
for split toric varieties.

We assume that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is ample and that

is a big and semiample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor,
not necessarily equal to
-[K_{X}+\Delta]
in
\pic(X)_{\mathbb{R}}

We recall that
[
PSTVA21
, Conjecture 1.1]
for
(\mathscr{X},\Delta)
predicts the asymptotic formula

N_{\mathbf{m},L}(B)\sim cB^{a(L)}(\log B)^{b(L)-1},\quad B\to+\infty,

(6.8)

where

6.5.1.
Combinatorial description of

Recall the notation introduced in Section
6.2
.
We now give a characterization of

as the solution of certain linear programming problems.

Proposition 6.12
.

(i)

The number
a(L)
is the minimal value of the function

\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}
subject to the conditions

\displaystyle\lambda_{\sigma}\geq 0,\quad\forall\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max},

(6.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}\alpha_{i,\sigma}\geq\frac{1}{m_{i}},\quad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,s\}.

(6.11)

(ii)

Assume, additionally, that
L
is ample. Fix
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
and
define
\gamma_{i}:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\frac{\beta_{\sigma,i,j}}{m_{j}}
.
Then
a(L)
is the minimal value of the function
\lambda_{0}
subject to the conditions

\displaystyle\lambda_{0},\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\quad\forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma},

(6.12)

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\alpha_{i,\sigma}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}\lambda_{j}\geq\frac{1}{m_{i}}+\gamma_{i},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}.

(6.13)

Proof.

To prove part
(i)
we observe that for
t\in\mathbb{R}
the condition

t[L]+[K_{X}+\Delta]\in\eff(X)

(6.14)

is equivalent to
\varphi^{-1}(t[L]+[K_{X}+\Delta])\cap C\neq\emptyset
.
Now,
\varphi^{-1}(t[L]+[K_{X}+\Delta])=tL+K_{X}+\Delta+M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
If
D\in(tL+K_{X}+\Delta+M_{\mathbb{R}})\cap C
, then
D-(K_{X}+\Delta)\in C
as

-(K_{X}+\Delta)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{m_{i}}D_{i}\in C
and
C
is a cone.
Then (
6.14
) holds if and only if there exists a divisor

D^{\prime}\in(tL+M_{\mathbb{R}})\cap C
such that
D^{\prime}+K_{X}+\Delta\in C
.
By Lemma
6.4
this is equivalent to the existence of

\lambda_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
such that

\sum_{\sigma_{\in}\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}=t
and

\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}L(\sigma)+K_{X}+\Delta\in C
.

Now we prove part
(ii)
. Condition (
6.14
) is
equivalent to the existence of
D\in C
such that
t[L]+[K_{X}+\Delta]=\varphi(D)
in

\pic(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
X
is proper and smooth, the last equality is
equivalent to
tL(\sigma)+(K_{X}+\Delta)(\sigma)=D(\sigma)
.
Write
D=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}D_{i}
.
Then
D\in C
if and only if
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}\geq 0
.
We have

tL(\sigma)+(K_{X}+\Delta)(\sigma)-D(\sigma)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}\left(t\alpha_{i,\sigma}-\frac{1}{m_{i}}-\lambda_{i}-\gamma_{i}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\beta_{\sigma,i,j}\lambda_{j}\right)D_{i}.

Using the fact that
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
if
D\in C
,
we see that condition (
6.14
) is equivalent to the existence of

\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for all
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}
that satisfy the conditions in
the statement for
\lambda_{0}=t
.
∎

6.5.2.
Heuristic argument for
a(L)

Next we give a heuristic argument in support of
[
PSTVA21
, Conjecture 1.1]

(and
[
BM90
, §3.3]
)
regarding the expected exponent
a(L)
of
B
in the asymptotic
formula (
6.8
) for split toric varieties over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Up to a positive constant,
N_{\mathbf{m},L}(B)
is the cardinality
S
of the set of
m_{i}
-full positive integers
y_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
that satisfy the conditions
\mathbf{y}^{L(\sigma)}\leq B
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}
. We recall that
\mathbf{y}^{L(\sigma)}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,\sigma}}
.

One of the ideas of the hyperbola method is to dissect the region of summation for the variables
y_{1},\dots,y_{s}
, into different boxes. Assume that we consider a box where say
y_{i}\sim B_{i}
(here we mean that for example
B_{i}\leq y_{i}\leq 2B_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
), and let
B_{i}=B^{t_{i}}
. What contribution do such vectors
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{s})
give to computing the cardinality
S
? First we note that the contribution from this box is

\text{box contribution}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}B_{i}^{\frac{1}{m_{i}}}=B^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}\frac{1}{m_{i}}}.

In order for this to be a box that we count by
S
, the parameters
(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})
need to satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}\alpha_{i,\sigma}\leq 1,\quad\forall\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}

(6.15)

and

t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\geq 0.

(6.16)

In order to find the size of
S
we hence have the following linear programming problem
\mathcal{P}
:
Maximize the function

\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}\frac{1}{m_{i}}

(6.17)

under the conditions (
6.15
) and (
6.16
).
The conditions (
6.15
) and (
6.16
) define a polytope
\widetilde{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
and by the theory of linear programming we know that the maximum of the function
\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}\frac{1}{m_{i}}
is obtained on at least one of its vertices.

The dual linear programming problem
\mathcal{D}
is given by the following problem:
Minimize the function

\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\lambda_{\sigma}

under the conditions (
6.10
) and (
6.11
).
By the strong duality property in linear programming
[
Dan98
, Chapter 6]
, both problems have a finite optimal solution and these values are equal. Since
a(L)
is positive, by Proposition
6.12
(i)
it is the solution of the dual linear programming problem
\mathcal{D}
and also of
\mathcal{P}
.

6.5.3.
Heuristic argument for

Now we give a heuristic argument in support of
[
PSTVA21
, Conjecture 1.1]

(and
[
BM90
, §3.3]

We recall from Lemma

With this identification, the cone generated by

Fix

Then

Let

6.6.3.
The leading constant

Here we prove that the leading constant (
6.21
) can be written as

\alpha(X,\Delta)\tau(X,\Delta)\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{m_{i}},

(6.22)

where
\alpha(X,\Delta):=\alpha(-(K_{X}+\Delta))
has been introduced in
Lemma
6.8
, and

\tau(X,\Delta)=\int_{\overline{\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z})}}H_{\Delta}(x)\delta_{\Delta}(x)\tau_{X},

where
\tau_{X}
is the Tamagawa measure on the set of adelic points
X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})

defined in
[
CLT10
, Definition 2.8]
,

H_{\Delta}
is the height function defined by the divisor
\Delta
as in
[
BT95a
, §2.1]
,

\overline{\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z})}
is the closure of the set of rational points
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z})

inside the space of adelic points on
X
,
and
\delta_{\Delta}=\prod_{p\in\Omega_{f}}\delta_{\Delta,p}
,
where
\delta_{\Delta,p}
is the characteristic function of the set of
Campana points in
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for each finite place
p
.

We observe that by
[
BT95a
, Proposition 2.4.4]
,
the product (
6.22
) agrees with the expectation formulated
in
[
PSTVA21
, §3.3]
provided that the domains of integration in the definitions of

\tau(X,\Delta)
(i.e.,
\{x\in\overline{\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z})}:\delta_{\Delta}(x)=1\}
) coincide.

Proposition 6.16
.

We have

\tau(X,\Delta)=\left(2^{s-r}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\prod_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}m_{j}\right)\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})^{s}}\left(\mu(\mathbf{d})\prod_{i=1}^{s}c_{m_{i},d_{i}}\right)>0.

Proof.

For every prime number
p
, we denote by
\Fr_{p}
the geometric Frobenius acting
on
\pic(\mathscr{X}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
, and for
t\in\mathbb{C}

we define
L_{p}(t,\pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})):=\det(1-p^{-t}\Fr_{p})^{-1}
.
Since
X
is split, and hence
\mathscr{X}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
is split,
we have
L_{p}(t,\pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))=(1-p^{-t})^{-r}
for every prime
p
.
Let
\lambda_{p}:=L_{p}(1,\pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))=(1-p^{-1})^{-r}
,
and define
\lambda:=\lim_{t\to 1}(t-1)^{r}\prod_{p\in\Omega_{f}}L_{p}(t,\pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))
.
Then
\lambda=\lim_{t\to 1}(t-1)^{r}\zeta(t)^{r}=1
by properties of the residue at
1

of the Riemann zeta function. Let
\lambda_{\infty}:=1
.
By
[
CLT10
, Remark 2.9(b)]
, we have

\tau_{X}=\lambda\prod_{v\in\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}}}\lambda_{v}^{-1}\tau_{X,p}
, where
\tau_{X,v}

denotes the local measure on
X(\mathbb{Q}_{v})
defined in
[
CLT10
, §2.1.7]
.

Since split toric varieties satisfy weak approximation (e.g.,
[
Har04
, §2]
)
and
\mathscr{X}
is projective, we get

\tau(X,\Delta)=\int_{X(\mathbb{R})}H_{\Delta,\infty}(x)\tau_{X,\infty}\prod_{p\in\Omega_{f}}\lambda_{p}^{-1}\int_{{\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}}H_{\Delta,p}(x)\delta_{\Delta,p}(x)\tau_{X,p},

where

H_{\Delta}=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}}}H_{\Delta,v}
as in
[
BT95a
, §2.1]
.
We recall that the global metrization used to define
H_{\delta}
in
[
BT95a
, §2.1]

is equivalent to the one determined by
[
Sal98
, Proposition and definition 9.2]
.

Since
\tau_{X,\infty}
is the measure used in
[
Sal98
, Proposition 9.16]
for the real place,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{X(\mathbb{R})}H_{\Delta,\infty}(x)\tau_{X,\infty}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\left(2^{s-r}\prod_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}\int_{0\leq x_{j}\leq 1}x_{j}^{\frac{1}{m_{j}}-1}\mathrm{d}x_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{s-r}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\prod_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}m_{j}.

Moreover, if we use the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
-torsor structure on

\pi:\mathscr{Y}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
together
with
[
Sal98
, Corollary 2.23, Propositions 9.7, 9.13 and 9.14]
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}H_{\Delta,p}(x)\delta_{\Delta,p}(x)\tau_{X,p}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{-1}\int_{\mathscr{Y}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}H_{\Delta,p}(\pi(y))\delta_{\Delta,p}(\pi(y))\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{d}y_{i},

where
dz
and
\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{d}y_{i}
are the Haar measures on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{r}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{s}
,
respectively, induced by the Haar measure on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
normalized such that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
has volume
1
,
and
\delta_{\Delta,p}\circ\pi
is the characteristic function of the property

v_{p}(y_{i})>0\quad\Rightarrow\quad v_{p}(y_{i})\geq m_{i},\qquad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,s\}.

We have
\int_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{d}z=(1-p^{-1})^{r}
.
Let
\chi
be the characteristic function of
\mathscr{Y}
. By
[
Sal98
, Lemma 11.15]

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{Y}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}H_{\Delta,p}(\pi(\mathbf{y}))\delta_{\Delta,p}(\pi(\mathbf{y}))\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{d}y_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{N}}\chi(\mathbf{y})\delta_{\Delta,p}(\pi(\mathbf{y}))\prod_{i=1}^{s}|y_{i}|_{p}^{\frac{1}{m_{i}}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{d}y_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\mathbf{e})\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mu((p^{e_{1}},\dots,p^{e_{s}}))\int_{p^{e_{i}}\mid y_{i},1\leq i\leq s}\delta_{\Delta,p}(\pi(\mathbf{y}))\prod_{i=1}^{s}|y_{i}|_{p}^{\frac{1}{m_{i}}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{d}y_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\mathbf{e})\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mu((p^{e_{1}},\dots,p^{e_{s}}))\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left((1-e_{i})\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\mathrm{d}y_{i}+\sum_{j=m_{i}}^{\infty}\int_{p^{j}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}|y_{i}|_{p}^{\frac{1}{m_{i}}-1}\mathrm{d}y_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\mathbf{e})\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mu((p^{e_{1}},\dots,p^{e_{s}}))\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(\left(1-p^{-1}\right)\left(1-e_{i}+\sum_{j=m_{i}}^{\infty}p^{-\frac{j}{m_{i}}}\right)\right).

We observe that the product

\left(1-p^{-1}\right)\left(1-e_{i}+\sum_{j=m_{i}}^{\infty}p^{-\frac{j}{m_{i}}}\right)
equals

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1+\sum_{j=m_{i}+1}^{2m_{i}-1}p^{-\frac{j}{m_{i}}}&\text{ if }e_{i}=0\\
\left(1+\sum_{j=m_{i}+1}^{2m_{i}-1}p^{-\frac{j}{m_{i}}}\right)\left(1+p-p^{\frac{m_{i}-1}{m_{i}}}\right)^{-1}&\text{ if }e_{i}=1.\end{cases}

So, remembering (
5.2
) and (
5.10
), we get

\tau({X,\Delta})\left(2^{s-r}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\prod_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}}m_{j}\right)^{-1}\\
=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{s}C_{m_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{p\in\Omega_{f}}\sum_{\mathbf{e}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mu((p^{e_{1}},\dots,p^{e_{s}}))\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(p^{-1}+G_{m_{i}}\left(p^{-\frac{1}{m_{i}}}\right)\right)^{e_{i}}\right).

We conclude by
[
Sal98
, Lemma 11.15(e)]
as

\prod_{p\mid d_{i}}\left(p^{-1}+G_{m_{i}}\left(p^{-\frac{1}{m_{i}}}\right)\right)\ll_{\epsilon}d_{i}^{-1+\epsilon}

by (
5.14
) for all
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.

To show that
\tau({X,\Delta})
is positive it suffices to observe that

\int_{\mathscr{Y}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}H_{\Delta,p}(\pi(y))\delta_{\Delta,p}(\pi(y))\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{d}y_{i}\geq\int_{\mathscr{(}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{s}}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{d}y_{i}=(1-p^{-1})^{s}>0,

as the integral on the right is the restriction of the integral on the left to the subset

(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{s}\subseteq\mathscr{Y}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
∎

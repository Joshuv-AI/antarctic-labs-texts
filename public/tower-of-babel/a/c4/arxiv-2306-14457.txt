Fauno: The Italian Large Language Model that will leave you senza parole!
Andrea Bacciu, Giovanni Trappolini, Andrea Santilli, Emanuele Rodolà, Fabrizio Silvestri

Abstract
This paper presents Fauno, the first and largest open-source Italian conversational Large Language Model (LLM). Our goal with Fauno is to democratize the study of LLMs in Italian, demonstrating that obtaining a fine-tuned conversational bot with a single GPU is possible. In addition, we release a collection of datasets for conversational AI in Italian. The datasets on which we fine-tuned Fauno include various topics such as general question answering, computer science, and medical questions. We release our code and datasets on https://github.com/RSTLess-research/Fauno-Italian-LLM

Introduction

Recently, there has been significant progress in enhancing the capabilities of natural language processing (NLP) methods. Notably, ChatGPT
(
OpenAI 2023a
)
and GPT-4
OpenAI 2023b
have emerged as highly proficient chat models. These models exhibit an impressive aptitude for comprehending and producing human-like responses across various domains. Consequently, they have gained popularity in customer support, virtual assistants, and social media moderation applications. However, these models are often only accessible through restricted APIs, which hampers new research and development. Additionally, the limited availability of chat models creates challenges for researchers and practitioners, impeding the growth of the NLP community. This problem is further aggravated by the scarcity of publicly accessible and high-quality chat datasets for analyzing and improving these models.
Recently, Baize
Xu et al. 2023
, whose depiction can be seen in Figure
3
, has been introduced to tackle these challenges effectively, utilizing the capabilities of ChatGPT to generate a top-notch chat dataset comprising multiple conversational turns automatically. Their approach involves employing ChatGPT to engage in a simulated dialogue with itself, mimicking both user and AI responses. This generated dataset is a valuable resource for training and evaluating chat models in the context of multi-turn conversations. Additionally, by specifying an initial dataset, they extract samples from specific domains and fine-tune chat models to specialize in technology or healthcare. To fine-tune Large Language Models (LLM) with limited resources, they adopt an efficient tuning technique that maximizes the utilization of available computational capabilities. This strategy allows state-of-the-art language models to adapt and perform well in resource-constrained scenarios. Their primary objective is to enhance an open-source LLM, LLaMA
Touvron et al. 2023
, which they believe has the potential to serve as an accessible alternative to proprietary chat models. By fine-tuning LLaMA with their generated chat dataset, they introduce a new model called Baize (Bái zé), inspired by a legendary creature from Chinese folklore known for its linguistic prowess and vast knowledge.
Baize reaches incredible performance; however, as with many other models in this category, its performance heavily depends on the language considered
Ducel et al. 2022
.
In particular, these models tend to prefer the English language since this is the language they have seen the most during their training.
Their performance in other languages, such as Italian, is vastly inferior, as it can be easily checked empirically by merely using these tools.
For this reason, inspired by Latin mythology, in this paper, we introduce Fauno, the first and the largest open-source Italian conversational LLM.
To build Fauno, we translated several datasets originally used by Baize in Italian. We use them to fine-tune and train our model, reaching good generation scores, as highlighted in later sections of this paper.
Our goal is twofold.
First, we want to provide the Italian (and the international) research community with an LLM that is highly proficient in Italian. We hope this will enhance and enable new research on this particular language by surpassing the current state-of-the-art capabilities of Italian.
Second, we want to provide practitioners with a valid conversational AI able to deal with the Italian language.
We provide an example for this latter case to hint at this tool’s potential.
Italy has one of the largest populations of elderly people.
In the last twenty years, the number of people older than eighty has doubled, according to the Italian statistical entity (ISTAT).
Furthermore, roughly 1.2 million people are older than 65 and live alone.
These people are known in the medical literature to suffer from loneliness, which aggravates their health issues.
With tools like Fauno, however, one could offer a conversational AI as a telehealth service.
While not being a substitute for a human, this could improve the quality of life of millions of people and reduce the weight of the national health system.
This is just one example of applications, and we hope our model can spark many such projects and research.
Fauno can be operational on accessible hardware, thus expanding accessibility to a broader range of practitioners and researchers.

2.3
Parameter-Efficient Tuning

Typical fine-tuning involves training all parameters of an LLM, which can be inefficient as the number of parameters increases. Various techniques have been proposed to address this issue under the umbrella term of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT). These techniques include Adapters
Houlsby et al. 2019
, which adds a trainable layer inside the Transformer while freezing the others; BitFit
Zaken et al. 2022
, which only tunes bias terms in the linear layers; Prompting approaches like Prefix Tuning
Li and Liang 2021
, P-Tuning
Liu et al. 2022
or Prompt Tuning
Lester et al. 2021
which fine-tune the optimal prompt parameters for a task; or LoRA
Hu et al. 2022
which trains low-rank matrices while keeping all the remaining parameters frozen. This is a very active research area, and we refer the reader to
Zhuang et al. 2023
for an up-to-date overview.
Recently the community leveraged these techniques to train low-cost versions of Stanford Alpaca
(
Taori et al. 2023
)
under the name of Alpaca-LoRA

3.2
Translation

To translate Fauno’s data, we use two different translation systems to maximize translation accuracy and quality.
Jiao et al. (2023)
Jiao et al. 2023
have demonstrated that Google Translate is state-of-the-art among commercial translation systems. Nevertheless, Google Translate encounters significant issues when handling text incorporating natural language utterances and programming code. For instance, when translating a code snippet like ”for i in range(10)” into Italian, Google Translate would incorrectly render it as ”per i in intervallo(10)”, which disrupts the syntax and functionality of the original code. This is due to Google Translate’s inability to discern between natural language and programming code. To address this limitation, we opted to utilize ChatGPT 3.5 to translate datasets containing programming code, as it is better suited for preserving the integrity of the code’s syntax and structure.
Hence, we translate Alpaca and StackOverflow with ChatGPT 3.5 and the remaining datasets with Google Translate.

4
Train setup

We use the same hyper-parameters of
Xu et al. 2023
with a single epoch and batch size of 128 examples.
We perform our experiments on a workstation equipped with an Intel Core i9-10940X (14-core CPU running at 3.3GHz) and 256GB of RAM, and a single Nvidia RTX A6000 with 48GB of VRAM.
The training time for the 7B and 13B models are 19 and 35 hours, respectively.

Come posso copiare un file da un server tramite ssh?

Per copiare un file da un server tramite SSH, puoi utilizzare il comando ”scp” (Secure Copy). Ecco come puoi farlo:
Apri una finestra di terminale sul tuo computer locale.

Conclusion

This work shows that it is possible to democratize the research for LLMs in multiple languages with limited resources.
Furthermore, we believe that the release of the Italian datasets for conversational AI will help researchers to develop further Large Language Models.

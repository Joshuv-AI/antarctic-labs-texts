Vertically invariant minimal surfaces in unimodular semidirect products
David Moya

Abstract
A surface in a three-dimensional metric Lie group G G is said invariant if it is invariant with respect to a one-dimensional subgroup Γ \Gamma of the isometry group of G G . Is this work we focus on unimodular metric Lie groups G G that can be written as a semidirect product of the form ℝ 2 ⋊ A ℝ \mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{A}\mathbb{R} for certain matrix A ∈ ℳ 2 ​ ( ℝ ) A\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) and study the minimal surfaces which are invariant under the group Γ \Gamma generated by left translations by elements in the vertical axis { 0 } ⋊ ℝ \{0\}\rtimes\mathbb{R} . We will call these surfaces vertically invariant. In particular, we describe new examples of minimal surfaces in E ~ ​ ( 2 ) \widetilde{E}(2) which are vertically invariant.

Introduction

Constant mean curvature (CMC) surfaces (including the minimal case) in homogeneous spaces is a field of interest in recent years, specially after the generalization of the holomorphic Hopf differential by Abresch and Rosenberg
[

Among the family of CMC surfaces in a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold, it is natural to study and describe the surfaces that satisfy a further geometric extrinsic condition. For instance, if the ambient space

[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
. It is also natural to impose these geometric conditions together with constant Gaussian curvature, see
[
14
]
. Imposing these conditions usually leads to specific examples of surfaces with the desired properties.

The background spaces of this work are metric Lie groups. We will call metric Lie group to a simply connected 3-dimensional Lie group endowed with a left invariant metric. On a Lie group
G
there is a measure invariant under left translations which is called the Haar measure. When the Haar measure is also invariant under right translations, the Lie group
G
is called unimodular. See
[
10
]
for a complete description of unimodular and non-unimodular metric Lie groups. A particularly interesting algebraic condition on metric Lie groups is the property of being written as a semidirect product. Semidirect products comprise all the non-unimodular metric Lie groups and among the unimodular family there are only two cases that can not be written as semidirect products: the special unitary group
SU(2)
and the universal cover
\widetilde{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
of the special linear group.

We organise this work as follows. In Section
2
we give some preliminaries and introduce notation involving metric Lie groups, focusing on the properties we need to develop the remaining sections. In Section
3
we get an ODE for the horizontal curve which generates, through vertical translations, an invariant CMC surface. In Section

6

{\rm Nil}_{3}

\widetilde{E}(2)
of the group of orientation-preserving rigid motions of the Euclidean plane. When the ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{A}\mathbb{R}
with
A=0\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it is well-known that vertical planes are the only vertically invariant minimal surfaces. When

[

X^{\prime}=F(t,X,c)
for all
c\in[1,\infty)
, since for each
c\in[1,\infty)
the maximal interval of definition of the solution
\gamma_{c}
of (
38
) (once we have fixed the initial conditions) is
\mathbb{R}
, and the map
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
(t,X,c)\mapsto F(t,X,c)
is locally Lipschitz (in fact is differentiable), we can use the continuous dependence on the parameter to conclude that, once we fix the initial conditions, for every
\varepsilon>0
and for every interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
1\leq c<1+\delta
then
|\gamma(t)-\gamma_{c}(t)|<\varepsilon
for every
t\in I
, where
\gamma
is the solution of (
38
) with
c=1
which have been described in Subsections
6.1
and
6.2
.

Next result describes further properties of the generating curve
\gamma
in
\widetilde{E}(2)
with a non-flat metric.

Proposition 8
.

1.

If
c>1
then the x-axis and the y-axis are the only solution of (
38
) with constant
\theta
.

2.

The curvature
\kappa=\theta^{\prime}
goes to
0
when
t\to\pm\infty
and
|\kappa(t)|\leq c/2
,
\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

To prove item 1, notice that solving the equation
1+c^{2}+(1-c^{2})C=0
gives
C=\frac{c^{2}+1}{c^{2}-1}
which is strictly greater than 1. Therefore the factor
1+c^{2}+(1-c^{2})\cos(2\theta)
can not be 0. Thus, a solution of (
38
) with constant
\theta=\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
must verify

x\sin\theta_{0}-c^{2}y\cos\theta_{0}\equiv 0,

which means that the generating curve
\gamma
is contained in a straight line passing through the origin. Write
\gamma
as
\gamma(t)=tv
, where
v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and therefore we have
tv_{1}\sin\theta_{0}-c^{2}tv_{2}\cos\theta_{0}=t(-1+c^{2})v_{1}v_{2}=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. As we are assuming
c>1
, then
v_{1}v_{2}=0
.

To prove item 2, we know that
|\kappa(t)|\leq c/2
,
\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
because of the proof of Proposition
7
. To see that
\kappa=\theta^{\prime}
goes to
0
when
t\to\pm\infty
, the same proof of Theorem
7
does not work here. However, vertical planes are still minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{A}\mathbb{R}
, for any
A\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see Remark 2.10 in
[
10
]
). Suppose that
\gamma
is bounded, then the surface
\Sigma\subset\widetilde{E}(2)
generated by
\gamma
from (
6
) would be inside a vertical cylinder
\mathbb{D}(R)\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
R>0
. We can take a vertical plane disjoint from
\Sigma
and left translate it until we find a first contact point. If the first contact point is at infinity (see Remark
9
) then there is also a first finite contact point. This comes from the fact that the vertical translation of a point
(x,y,z)\in\widetilde{E}(2)
describes a helix
s\mapsto\left(x\cos s-cy\sin s,\frac{x\sin s}{c}+y\cos s,z+s\right)
. Therefore the surface
\Sigma
is periodic along the vertical axis, which implies that there are infinitely many finite contact points when we find a first contact point. Thus, we can apply the classical maximum principle to reach a contradiction and therefore
|\gamma(t)|\to\infty
when
t\to\pm\infty
. Using (
39
) we have

\kappa\leq c^{2}F\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+c^{4}y^{2}}\right),

where
F(b)=\frac{b}{c^{2}+b^{2}}
. Since
\sqrt{x^{2}+c^{4}y^{2}}\to\infty
if
|\gamma|\to\infty
and
F(b)
goes to
0
when
b\to\infty
we have that
k(t)\to 0
if
|t|\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 9
.

It is still not proved a maximum principle at infinity result or a half-space theorem for vertical planes in
\widetilde{E}(2)
. However a half-space theorem for horizontal planes was proved in
[
15
]
.

7
Surfaces with zero Gaussian curvature

Since in Section
3
we have computed the coefficients of the first and second fundamental forms, it is natural to compute the Gaussian curvature of a surface generated by (
6
) and study the vertically invariant surfaces on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{A}\mathbb{R}
with constant or zero Gaussian curvature.

Again, we assume that our metric Lie group
G=\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{A}\mathbb{R}
,
A\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is unimodular. In the literature, invariant surfaces of zero Gaussian curvature in metric Lie groups that can be written as a semidirect product has been studied in the following ambient spaces:

1.

In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, Hartman and Nirenberg proved that (Theorem III in
[
4
]
) all the complete, zero Gaussian curvature surfaces are given by right cylinders over possibly non compact curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\}
, which describes all the possible examples.

2.

The invariant surfaces with constant extrinsic Gaussian curvature in
\text{Nil}_{3}
were studied by Belarbi in
[
2
]
, where he considered all possible 1-parameter subgroups of left translations.

3.

Invariant surfaces with respect to any 1-parameter subgroup of constant intrinsic or extrinsic Gaussian curvature in
\text{Sol}_{3}
with its standard metric,
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
, were studied in López
[
7
]
and López-Munteanu
[
8
]
.

When we consider
\widetilde{E}(2)
with its standard (flat) metric, we know, by Theorem III in
[
4
]
that except for cylinders over a circumference centered at the origin, there are no complete zero Gaussian curvature invariant surfaces because vertical translations in
\widetilde{E}(2)
do not produce right cylinders except for these vertical cylinders over circumferences. Even though, we can still give a description of the vertically invariant surfaces in
\widetilde{E}(2)
with zero Gaussian curvature (since the metric is flat we are dealing with both, intrinsic and extrinsic, cases). None of these surfaces can be complete except for the vertical cylinders over circumferences centered at the origin.

Using the expressions for the first and second fundamental forms of Section
3
, and the same notation as in previous sections, since the Gaussian curvature
K
can be computed as

K=\frac{eg-f^{2}}{EG-F^{2}},

we get the following system of ODEs for a curve
\gamma=(x,y,0)
that generates, through vertical translations, a constant Gaussian curvature surface in the standard
\widetilde{E}(2)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x^{\prime}=\cos\theta\\
y^{\prime}=\sin\theta\\
K=-\frac{1+(y\cos\theta-x\sin\theta)\theta^{\prime}}{(1+(x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta))^{2}}\end{array}.\right.

(40)

If we impose
K=0
this system becomes:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x^{\prime}=\cos\theta\\
y^{\prime}=\sin\theta\\
(y\cos\theta-x\sin\theta)\theta^{\prime}=-1\end{array}.\right.

(41)

As in Section
5
, we can give a first integral for the system (
41
):

J(t)=x(t)\cos\theta(t)+y(t)\sin\theta(t)=:J\in\mathbb{R}.

J
can be written as

J(t)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(x(t)^{2}+y(t)^{2}\right).

Integrating and assuming
x(0)=x_{0}
,
y(0)=y_{0}
,
\theta(0)=\theta_{0}
,
x_{0},y_{0},\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
gives that for any
t
in the maximal interval of definition of
\gamma
we have

|\gamma(t)|^{2}=x(t)^{2}+y(t)^{2}=at+x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2},

(42)

where
a:=2J=2(x_{0}\cos\theta_{0}+y_{0}\sin\theta_{0})
. We know that if
a\not=0
there are no solutions of (
41
) defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. (
42
) implies that the maximal interval of definition of
\gamma
is contained in
\left[-(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2})/a,\infty\right)
if
a>0
or in
\left(-\infty,-(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2})/a\right]
if
a<0
. Now write
\gamma
in polar coordinates:

x(t)=r(t)\cos\alpha(t),\quad y(t)=r(t)\sin\alpha(t).

Due to (
42
) we know that

r(t)=\sqrt{at+x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}}.

(43)

Since

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=r^{\prime}\cos\alpha-r\alpha^{\prime}\sin\alpha,

\displaystyle y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=r^{\prime}\sin\alpha+r\alpha^{\prime}\cos\alpha,

equation
1=(x^{\prime})^{2}+(y^{\prime})^{2}
becomes

1=(r^{\prime})^{2}+r^{2}(\alpha^{\prime})^{2},

which implies

\alpha^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{-a^{2}+4at+4\left(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}\right)}}{\left|at+x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}\right|}.

The maximal interval of definition of
\alpha^{\prime}
(and therefore the maximal interval of definition of
\alpha
) is
I_{1}:=\left[a/4-(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2})/a,\infty\right)
if
a>0
,
I_{2}:=\left(-\infty,a/4-(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2})/a\right]
if
a<0
and
\mathbb{R}
if
a=0
. Since
I_{1}\subset\left[-(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2})/a,\infty\right)
when
a>0
(resp.
I_{2}\subset\left(-\infty,-(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2})/a\right]
) then the maximal interval of definition of a solution of (
41
) is given by
I_{1}
if
a>0
,
I_{2}
if
a<0
, or
\mathbb{R}
if
a=0
.

Figure 5
:
Left: solution
\gamma
of (
41
) with initial conditions
x_{0}=2
,
y_{0}=1
,
\theta_{0}=\frac{\pi}{4}
(
a>0
). Right: zero Gaussian curvature surface generated through (
6
) with this
\gamma
.

Set

g(t)=\sqrt{-a^{2}+4at+4(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2})},

then, integrating, we finally describe the function
\alpha
and, therefore, all the invariant surfaces in
\widetilde{E}(2)
with zero Gaussian curvature:

\begin{split}\alpha(t)=&\arctan\left(\frac{y_{0}}{x_{0}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{-a^{2}+4a\tau+4\left(x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}\right)}}{\left|a\tau+x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}\right|}d\tau\\
=&\arctan\left(\frac{y_{0}}{x_{0}}\right)+\left.\frac{\text{sgn}\left(a\tau+x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}\right)}{2a}\left(g(\tau)-a\;\text{arccot}\left(\frac{a}{g(\tau)}\right)\right)\right|_{\tau=0}^{\tau=t}.\end{split}

(44)

In summary, we have proved the following result:

Proposition 10
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\widetilde{E}(2)
be a vertically invariant surface with zero Gaussian curvature, then either
\Sigma
is a vertical cylinder over a circumference in the plane
\left\{z=0\right\}
centered at the origin (and this is the only complete example) or the generating curve
\gamma
of
\Sigma
is given in polar coordinates by (
43
) and (
44
).

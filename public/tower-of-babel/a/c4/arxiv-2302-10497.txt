Homotopy solution to non-linear evolution for heavy nuclei
Carlos Contreras, Eugene Levin, Rodrigo Meneses

Abstract
In the paper we suggest the homotopy method for solving of the non linear evolution equation. This method consists of two steps. First is the analytical solution for the linearized version of the non-linear evolution deep in the saturation region. Second, the perturbative procedure is suggested to take into account the remaining parts of the non-linear corrections. It turns out that these corrections are rather small and can be estimated in the regular iterative procedure.

m_{0}(\gamma)\Bigg(\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y};b\right)}{\partial\tilde{Y}}\,\,-\,\,\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y};b\right)}{\partial\gamma}\Bigg)\,-\,a^{II}_{0}(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y})\,\,=\,\,0;~~~\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}\left(\gamma,\tilde{Y};b\right)}{\partial\tilde{Y}}\,\,-\,\,\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y};b\right)}{\partial\gamma}=\,\,\,\dfrac{a^{II}_{0}(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y})}{m_{0}(\gamma)}

The solution to Eq. (

\tilde{z}
- representation, introducing

\tilde{\mu}_{1}\left(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y}\right)\,\,=\,\,\int\limits^{\epsilon+i\infty}_{\epsilon-i\infty}\frac{d\gamma}{2\pi i}e^{\gamma\,\tilde{z}}\frac{\tilde{m}_{1}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)}{m_{0}\left(\gamma\right)};~~~~~~~\tilde{A}\left(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y}\right)\,\,=\,\,\,\int\limits^{\epsilon+i\infty}_{\epsilon-i\infty}\frac{d\gamma}{2\pi i}e^{\gamma\,\tilde{z}}\frac{a^{II}_{0}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)}{m_{0}\left(\gamma\right)};

\frac{\partial\tilde{\mu}_{1}\left(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y}\right)}{\partial\,\delta\tilde{Y}}\,\,+\,\,\tilde{z}\,\tilde{\mu}_{1}\left(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y}\right)=\tilde{A}\left(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y}\right)

\tilde{\mu}_{1}\left(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y}\right)=e^{-\tilde{z}\,\delta\tilde{Y}}\,\int\limits_{0}^{\delta\tilde{Y}}dte^{\tilde{z}t}\tilde{A}\left(\tilde{z},\,t\right)

For the Mellin image of

\mu_{1}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)\,\,=\,\,\int\limits^{\delta\tilde{Y}}_{0}dt\frac{a^{II}_{0}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t,t\right)}{m_{0}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t\right)}

Hence, the general solution to Eq. (

m^{(II)}_{1}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)\,\,=\,\,\,-\,2\,\int\limits^{\delta\tilde{Y}}_{0}\!\!dt\tilde{\Phi}^{II}_{1}\left(t\right)m_{0}(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y})\,\,+\,\,m_{0}(\gamma)\int\limits^{\delta\tilde{Y}}_{0}dt\frac{a^{II}_{0}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t,t\right)}{m_{0}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t\right)}

The general solution for
N_{1}^{II}\left(r,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)

\displaystyle N^{II}_{1}\left(z,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)\,\,\,=\,\,\,\,2\,m(0)\int\limits^{\epsilon+i\infty}_{\epsilon-i\infty}\!\!\!\!\dfrac{d\,\gamma}{2\,\pi\,i}\,e^{(z+\zeta-z_{0})\,\gamma}e^{\frac{1}{2}\kappa\gamma^{2}}\,\int\limits^{\delta\tilde{Y}}_{0}dt\frac{a^{II}_{0}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t,t\right)}{m_{0}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t\right)}+I\left(z,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)

III.3.1
M^{I}_{n}\left(\tilde{z}\right)
and
N^{I}_{n}\left(z\right)

Assuming
\Phi^{I}_{j}(\delta\tilde{Y})=0
for
j\geq 1
, the contribution is obtained solving:

\kappa\,\dfrac{dM_{n}^{I}}{d\tilde{z}}\,=\,\chi_{0}\left(\dfrac{d}{d\tilde{z}}\right)M_{n}^{I}-(\tilde{z}-\zeta)\,M_{n}^{I}-\dfrac{dA_{n-1}^{I}}{d\tilde{z}},\quad\textrm{with}\,\dfrac{dA_{n-1}^{I}}{d\tilde{z}}\,=\,2\,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}M^{I}_{j}(\tilde{z})\,\int_{0}^{\tilde{z}}M^{I}_{n-1-j}(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}

M_{n}^{I}(\tilde{z})\,=\,\int_{\epsilon-i\infty}^{\epsilon+i\infty}\dfrac{d\gamma}{2\pi i}\,e^{(\tilde{z}+\zeta)\gamma}\,m_{n}^{I}(\gamma),\quad\dfrac{dA_{n-1}}{d\tilde{z}}\,=\,\int_{\epsilon-i\infty}^{\epsilon+i\infty}\dfrac{d\gamma}{2\pi i}\,e^{(\tilde{z}+\zeta)\gamma}\,a_{n-1}^{I}(\gamma)

we obtain the ordinary differential equation for

, which depends recursively on the previous solutions.

Using a similar procedure as for

(see Appendix A), we obtain

\kappa\dfrac{dN_{n}^{I}}{d\tilde{z}}\,+\,(z+\zeta-z_{0})\,N_{n}^{I}\,=\,-\,\underbrace{\int_{\epsilon-i\infty}^{\epsilon+i\infty}\dfrac{d\gamma}{2\pi i}\,u^{-\gamma}e^{2\psi(1)\gamma}\,a_{n-1}^{I}(\gamma)\dfrac{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)}}_{H^{I}_{n}(z)}

III.3.2
M^{II}_{n}\left(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y}\right)
and

The nonhomogeneous term takes the form:

\dfrac{\partial A^{II}_{n-1}}{\partial\tilde{z}}\,=\,2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\,M^{II}_{j}(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y})\,\int_{0}^{\tilde{z}}d\tilde{z}^{\prime}\,M^{II}_{n-1-j}(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\delta\tilde{Y})

The differences in the expressions with respect to the regions are treated considering

\int_{0}^{\tilde{z}}d\tilde{z}^{\prime}\,M_{l}(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\delta\tilde{Y})\,=\,\lambda_{l}(\delta\tilde{Y})\,+\,\int_{\tilde{z}}^{\infty}d\tilde{z}^{\prime}M^{II}_{l}(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\delta\tilde{Y}),

with
\lambda_{l}(\delta\tilde{Y})\,=\,\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tilde{z}^{\prime}\,M_{l}(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\delta\tilde{Y})
. Writing
\Phi_{l+1}^{II}\,=\,-\,\lambda_{l}(\delta\tilde{Y})\,+\,\tilde{\Phi}_{l+1}^{II}(\delta\tilde{Y})
, we have

-2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Phi_{n-j}^{II}(\delta\tilde{Y})\,M_{j}^{II}(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y})\,-\,\dfrac{\partial A_{n-1}}{\partial\tilde{z}}\,=\,-2\,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\tilde{\Phi}_{n-j}^{II}(\delta\tilde{Y})\,M^{II}_{j}(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y})\,+\,\underbrace{2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}M_{j}^{II}(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y})\,\int_{0}^{\tilde{z}}d\tilde{z}^{\prime}M^{II}_{n-1-j}(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\delta\tilde{Y})}_{\tilde{A}_{n-1}(\tilde{z},\delta\tilde{Y})}

Eq. (
33
) for the Mellin images takes the form:

\dfrac{\partial m_{n}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)}{\partial\delta\tilde{Y}}+(\kappa\gamma-\chi(\gamma)+1/\gamma)m_{n}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)-\dfrac{\partial m_{n}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)}{\partial\gamma}\,+\,2\,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\tilde{\Phi}^{II}_{n-j}\left(\delta\tilde{Y}\right)\,m_{j}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)=\,\,\,\,a_{n-1}^{II}(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y})

(108)

where
\tilde{A}_{n-1}=\int_{\epsilon-i\infty}^{\epsilon+i\infty}\frac{d\gamma}{2\pi i}e^{(\tilde{z}+\zeta)\gamma}a_{n-1}^{II}(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y})
.

Note, that for

we need to consider extra contributions in comparison with Eq. (
III.2.2
),
since the solution for each

satisfies a non-homogeneous equation. Thus, from the linearity of the equation, we can solve Eq. (

\dfrac{\partial\mu_{n}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)}{\partial\delta\tilde{Y}}\,\,-\,\,\dfrac{\partial\mu_{n}\left(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)}{\partial\gamma}=\,\,\,\dfrac{b_{n}(\gamma,\delta\tilde{Y})}{m_{0}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle N^{II}_{n}\left(z,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)\,\,\,=\,\,\,\,2\,m(0)\int\limits^{\epsilon+i\infty}_{\epsilon-i\infty}\!\!\!\!\dfrac{d\,\gamma}{2\,\pi\,i}\,e^{(z+\zeta-z_{0})\,\gamma}e^{\frac{1}{2}\kappa\gamma^{2}}\,\int\limits^{\delta\tilde{Y}}_{0}dt\frac{b_{n}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t,t\right)}{m_{0}\left(\gamma+\delta\tilde{Y}-t\right)}

into Eq. (

IV
Conclusions

In this paper we developed the homotopy approach for solving the non-linear evolution Balitsky-Kovchegov equation
BK
. The solution consists of two steps. First, we solved the linearized version of the BK equation
LT
in the momentum space deep in the saturation region. We found that this solution has the geometric scaling behaviour for
\xi\leq\xi^{A}_{0}
(see Fig.
1
). For
\xi\,>\,\xi^{A}_{0}
, we observe the strong violation of the geometric scaling behaviour in the saturation region. This solution satisfies the boundary and initial conditions which are given perturbative QCD approach for
r\,Q_{s}<1
(see Eq. (
7
)) and by McLerran-Venugopalan formula of Eq. (
14
) for
Y=Y_{A}
.

In the second step of our approach we are taking into account the remaining part of the non-linear correction that have not been included in the linearized form of BK equation. It turns out that these corrections are rather small indicating that our procedure gives a self consistent way to account them.

We believe that we suggest the method of finding solution, which allow us to treat the most essential part of the scattering amplitude analytically. The numerical part of the calculations is expressed through well converged integrals and can be easily estimated. However, in this paper we have not investigated the corrections
N^{I}\left(z\right)
and
N^{II}\left(z,\delta\tilde{Y}\right)
in full using that dependence of
z
of
{A}_{0}(z,\delta\tilde{Y})
is determined by the contribution of
M^{I}_{0}
and
M^{II}_{0}
. These study would make the paper long and unreadable. Hence, we decided to put this in a separate paper with more mathematical content, which will be prepared shortly.

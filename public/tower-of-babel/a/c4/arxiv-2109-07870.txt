Schwarz lemma: the case of equality and an extension
Haojie Chen, Xiaolan Nie

Abstract
. We prove two results related to the Schwarz lemma in complex geometry. First, we show that if the inequality in the Schwarz lemmata of Yau, Royden and Tosatti becomes equality at one point, then the equality holds on the whole manifold. In particular, the holomorphic map is totally geodesic and has constant rank. In the second part, we study the holomorphic sectional curvature on an almost Hermitian manifold and establish a Schwarz lemma in terms of holomorphic sectional curvatures in almost Hermitian setting.

Introduction

The Ahlfors-Schwarz lemma
[
1
]
is one of the most important results in complex analysis and differential geometry. It says that if
f
is a holomorphic map from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
into a Riemann surface with a conformal metric
ds^{2}
with Gaussian curvature at most
-1

is distance decreasing in the sense that

(1.1)

\displaystyle f^{*}ds^{2}\leq ds_{\mathbb{D}}^{2},

where

is the Poincaré metric on
\mathbb{D}
. In
[
8
]
, Heins proved the strong form of the Ahlfors-Schwarz lemma: if the equality of
(\ref{1.1})
holds at one point in
\mathbb{D}
, then it holds on
\mathbb{D}
and

is an isometric immersion. This generalizes the equality case of the classical Schwarz lemma. Later, the result was also proved by Royden
[
19
]
and Minda
[
15
]
using different methods (see also e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
2
]
for the boundary rigidity of the Schwarz lemma). Among other applications, the strong form of the Ahlfors-Schwarz lemma has been applied to study the Bloch constant problem
[
8
]
, which is one of the most intriguing open problems in complex analysis of one variable.

Preliminaries

In this section, we give some background materials, including almost Hermitian manifolds, the Chern connection and Kähler metrics, etc. We refer to

Let
(M,J)
be a
2m
-dimensional almost complex manifold and
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
.
(M,J,g)
is called an almost Hermitian manifold if
g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y)
for any
X,Y\in TM
.
It is known that there is a unique connection
\nabla
on
(M,J,g)
(see e.g.
[
21
]
) satisfying

\nabla J=0,\ \ \nabla g=0

and
T(X,JX)=0
for all
X\in TM
, where

T(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y]

is the torsion tensor. It is called the Chern connection or canonical connection. The last requirement is equivalent to that the (1,1) part of the torsion vanishes. The Nijenhuis tensor
N_{J}
of
J
is defined by

N_{J}(X,Y)=[X,Y]+J[JX,Y]+J[X,JY]-[JX,JY]

for
X,Y\in TM
. The Kähler form
\omega
is defined by
\omega(X,Y)=g(JX,Y)
. An almost Hermitian manifold is a Kähler manifold if
N_{J}=0
and
d\omega=0
. It is known that
(M,J,g)
is Kähler if and only if
T=0

[
29
]
.

On an almost Hermitian manifold
(M,J,g)
, denote
T^{1,0}M
and
T^{0,1}M
the
\sqrt{-1}
and
-\sqrt{-1}
eigenbundles of
J
in
TM\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
. Then
TM\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}=T^{1,0}M\oplus T^{0,1}M
. For any
\xi\in T^{1,0}M
, we can write
\xi=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X-\sqrt{-1}JX)
for some
X\in TM
. This gives an isomorphism between
T^{1,0}M
and
TM
. Given a smooth function
\phi
on
M
, the complex Hessian of
\phi
is defined to be
(\nabla^{2}\phi)^{(1,1)}
, i.e., the
(1,1)
part of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\phi
. For instance, for
\xi,\eta\in T^{1,0}M
,

(\nabla^{2}\phi)^{(1,1)}(\xi,\bar{\eta})=\nabla^{2}\phi(\xi,\bar{\eta})=\xi(\bar{\eta}\phi)-(\nabla_{\xi}\bar{\eta})\phi.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{m}\}
be a local unitary (1,0) frame on
M
and
\{\theta^{1},\theta^{2},...,\theta^{m}\}
be the dual frame so that
g=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta^{i}\otimes\bar{\theta}^{i}+\bar{\theta}^{i}\otimes\theta^{i}.

Let

\phi_{i\bar{j}}=\nabla^{2}\phi(e_{i},\overline{e_{j}})=e_{i}\overline{e_{j}}\phi-(\nabla_{e_{i}}\overline{e_{j}})\phi.

As the
(1,1)
part of the torsion of
\nabla
vanishes,
\nabla_{e_{i}}\overline{e_{j}}-\nabla_{\overline{e_{j}}}e_{i}=[e_{i},\overline{e_{j}}].
Then
\phi_{i\bar{j}}=\phi_{\bar{j}i}
. Using Einstein summation convention, locally write

(\nabla^{2}\phi)^{(1,1)}=\phi_{i\bar{j}}(\theta^{i}\otimes\bar{\theta}^{j}+\bar{\theta}^{j}\otimes\theta^{i}).

The complex Laplacian of
\phi
is defined to be

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=tr_{g}(\nabla^{2}\phi)^{(1,1)}=2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi_{i\bar{i}}.

It is known that
\Delta
is an elliptic operator which differs from the Laplacian of the Levi-Civita connection by some first order differential operators (Lemma 3.2 in
[
20
]
). Use
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
for the inner product
g
and denote

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,

\displaystyle R(X,Y,Z,W)=\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle,

to be the curvature tensors of
\nabla
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given two nonzero
(1,0)
vectors
\xi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X-\sqrt{-1}JX),\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(Y-\sqrt{-1}JY)
,
the holomorphic bisectional curvature of
\nabla
in directions
\xi,\eta
is defined to be

B(\xi,\eta)=\dfrac{R(\xi,\bar{\xi},\eta,\bar{\eta})}{|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}}=\dfrac{R(X,JX,JY,Y)}{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}},

where
|\xi|^{2}=|X|^{2}=\langle X,X\rangle
and
|\eta|^{2}=|Y|^{2}=\langle Y,Y\rangle
. The holomorphic sectional curvature in the direction
\xi
or
X
is defined to be

H(\xi)=B(\xi,\xi)=R(X,JX,JX,X)/|X|^{4}.

We also use
H(X)
for
H(\xi)
in the context. The first and second Ricci curvatures of
(J,g)
are defined by

Ric^{(1)}(\xi,\bar{\eta})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}R(\xi,\bar{\eta},e_{i},\overline{e_{i}}),\ \ Ric^{(2)}(\xi,\bar{\eta})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}R(e_{i},\overline{e_{i}},\xi,\bar{\eta}).

The Chern scalar curvature is defined to be
R=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}R(e_{i},\overline{e_{i}},e_{j},\overline{e_{j}})
.

We remark that if
(M,J,g)
is Kähler, then
Ric^{(1)}=Ric^{(2)}
by the Kähler symmetry. It is called the Ricci curvature in short. We have the following notions of boundedness on an almost Hermitian manifold (cf.
[
20
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

(i)

The first (resp. second) Ricci curvature is said to be bounded from below if there is a constant
C
such that
Ric^{(1)}(\xi,\bar{\xi})\geq C|\xi|^{2}
(resp.
Ric^{(2)}(\xi,\bar{\xi})\geq C|\xi|^{2}
) for any
\xi\in T^{1,0}M
.

(ii)

The holomorphic bisectional curvature of

is said to be bounded from below if there is a constant
C_{1}
such that
B(\xi,\eta)\geq C_{1}
for all
\xi,\eta\in T^{1,0}M
.

(iii)

The torsion tensor
T
is said to be bounded if there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that
|T(\xi,\eta)|\leq C_{2}|\xi||\eta|
for any
\xi,\eta\in T^{1,0}M
.

(iv)

The
(2,0)
part of the curvature

is said to be bounded if there is a constant
C_{3}>0
such that
|R(\xi,\eta,\xi,\bar{\eta})|\leq C_{3}|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}
for any
\xi,\eta\in T^{1,0}M
.

Note that the
(2,0)
part of the curvature tensor vanishes when

is Hermitian, i.e.
N_{J}=0
. If
M
is compact, then it clearly satisfies all the boundedness conditions. For a fixed point

, denote

the distance from

to

. It is smooth away from

and its cut locus. The following complex Hessian comparison theorem on almost Hermitian manifolds due to Tosatti

]
will be used in the proof of Theorem

[

]
).

Let

, torsion bounded by

and

Here

.

A smooth map

is called a

The equality case of Schwarz lemmata

and

for some function
f
with
f(C)\geq 0
and
f^{\prime}(x)\leq M,\forall\ x\leq C
for some
M>0
. Then
u
can not equal
C
in the interior of
\Omega
unless it is constant.

In
[
26
]
, Yau also obtained the volume form version of the Schwarz lemma for holomorphic maps under weaker curvature conditions. It was extended by Tosatti
[
20
]
to almost Hermitian manifolds as follows.

Theorem 3.3

(Tosatti)
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a
2m
-dimensional complete almost Hermitian manifold with Chern scalar curvature bounded from below by
mK_{1}<0
. Let
(N,J_{N},h)
be another
2m
-dimensional almost Hermitian manifold with the first Ricci curvature bounded from above by
K_{2}<0
. If the second Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is bounded from below and with torsion and
(2,0)
part of the curvature bounded, then for any non-degenerate holomorphic map
f:M\rightarrow N
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle f^{*}dV_{h}\leq(\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}})^{m}dV_{g}.

Here
dV_{g}
and
dV_{h}
are the volume forms on
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
respectively and the map
f
is non-degenerate if
f^{*}dV_{h}
is nowhere vanishing. Applying Remark
3.2
, we prove the following result. We would like to thank Professor Kefeng Liu for pointing it out to us.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that the equality holds at one point in
(\ref{to4})
, then it holds on the whole manifold
M
.

Proof.

Let
v=\dfrac{\det f^{*}h}{\det g}
. Then
v\leq(\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}})^{m}
by Theorem
3.3
. By the Chern-Lu formula for the volume form (see Page 1081 in
[
20
]
), we have

\Delta v\geq-2mK_{2}v^{1+\frac{1}{m}}+2mK_{1}v.

Let
f(v)=-2mK_{2}v^{1+\frac{1}{m}}+2mK_{1}v
. Then
f^{\prime}(v)\leq-2K_{1}
as
v\leq(\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}})^{m}
. The result then follows from Remark
3.2
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

By Theorem
1.4
,
tr_{g}f^{*}h\equiv\frac{k}{K}
and
f
is totally geodesic. Then the image
f(S)
is a totally geodesic submanifold of
N
. As
f
is non-constant and
m=1
, then
f
is an immersion and
f^{*}h=\frac{k}{K}ds^{2}
. By the proof of Proposition 4 in
[
18
]
, the equality forces that the Gaussian curvature of

equals
k
. Therefore,
ds^{2}=-\dfrac{1}{k}ds^{2}_{H}
by the uniqueness of the hyperbolic metric on
S
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Recently in

3.2
and the analogous equality result follows (c.f. Remark 1.10 in

). We would like to thank him for the communications.

4.
Holomorphic sectional curvature and
J
-holomorphic disc

In this section, we compare the holomorphic sectional curvatures of an almost Hermitian manifold and its almost complex submanifolds. Then we discuss the existence of some special
J
-holomorphic discs through a point in any direction on an almost Hermitian manifold, based on the work of
[
9
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(S,i)
be an immersed submanifold of
(M,J)
with
i:S\longrightarrow M
being the immersion. For any
p\in S
, identify
T_{p}S
with
i_{*}(T_{p}S)
in
T_{i(p)}M
. If
J(T_{p}S)\subset T_{p}S
, then we say
(S,i)
is an almost complex submanifold of
(M,J)
.

The pull back of
(J,g)
through
i
induces an almost Hermitian structure on
S
which we still denote by
(J,g)
. As an immersion is locally embedding, to focus on local properties, from now on we assume that
S
is an embedded almost complex submanifold and identify
S
with
i(S)
.

Denote
\nabla
and
\hat{\nabla}
the Chern connections on
M
and
S
respectively. Let
NS
be the normal bundle of
S
and
\pi^{\top}
and
\pi^{\bot}
be the tangential and normal projections:

\displaystyle\pi^{\top}:\ TM|_{S}\rightarrow TS,\ \ \ \pi^{\bot}:\ TM|_{S}\rightarrow NS.

Then
\nabla
and
\hat{\nabla}
are related as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X,Y
be in
\Gamma(TS)
which are extended to vector fields on
M
. Then we have

\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\top}.

Proof.

Define
\nabla^{\top}_{X}Y:=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\top}
. It is clear that
\nabla^{\top}
is a connection on
S
. By the uniqueness of Chern connection, to show that
\nabla^{\top}=\hat{\nabla}
, it suffices to show that
\nabla^{\top}g=0,\ \nabla^{\top}J=0
and its torsion has vanishing
(1,1)
part. Let
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TS)
which are extended to vector fields on
M
. At any
p\in S
, we have

\displaystyle X\langle Y,Z\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle+\langle Y,\nabla_{X}Z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\top},Z\rangle+\langle Y,(\nabla_{X}Z)^{\top}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{\top}_{X}Y,Z\rangle+\langle Y,\nabla^{\top}_{X}Z\rangle

Thus
\nabla^{\top}g=0
. Also, as
JX\in T_{p}S
, for any
W\in\Gamma(NS)
,

\langle X,JW\rangle=-\langle JX,W\rangle=0.
So
JW\in\Gamma(NM)
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}^{\top}(JY)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}(JY))^{\top}=(J\nabla_{X}Y)^{\top}

\displaystyle=(J\nabla^{\top}_{X}Y+J(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\bot})^{\top}

\displaystyle=J\nabla^{\top}_{X}Y.

So
\nabla^{\top}J=0
.
Finally, let
X,Y
be (1,0) and (0,1) vector fields of
S
respectively. Then

\displaystyle\nabla^{\top}_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}^{\top}X

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X)^{\top}

\displaystyle=[X,Y]^{\top}=[X,Y].

So the torsion of
\nabla^{\top}
has vanishing (1,1) part. Therefore,
\nabla^{\top}=\hat{\nabla}
.
∎

For any
X,Y\in TS
, the second fundamental form of
S
is given by

\textit{II}\ (X,Y):=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\bot}.

By Proposition
4.2
and the metric compatibility of
\nabla
, we have

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X,Y\in\Gamma(TS)
and
W\in\Gamma(NS)
which are extended to vector fields on
M
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y+\mathit{II}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}W,Y\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle W,\mathit{II}(X,Y)\rangle.

Denote
R
and
\hat{R}
the curvature for
\nabla
and
\hat{\nabla}
. With Proposition
4.3
, we get the Gauss type equation by direct calculation.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
X,Y,Z,W\in T_{p}S
,

R(X,Y,Z,W)=\hat{R}(X,Y,Z,W)-\langle\mathit{II}(X,W),\mathit{II}(Y,Z)\rangle+\langle\mathit{II}(X,Z),\textit{II}(Y,W)\rangle.

We are then able to compare the holomorphic sectional curvature of
\nabla
and
\hat{\nabla}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

H(X)=\hat{H}(X)+2|\mathit{II}(X,X)|^{2}/|X|^{4},

for
X\in TS
. Hence,
\hat{H}(X)\leq H(X)
with equality holding if and only if
\mathit{II}(X,X)=0
.

The above inequality was obtained previously in
[
11
]
by using local frames and structure equations.

Proof.

By
\nabla J=0
, we get
\nabla_{X}(JY)=J(\nabla_{X}Y)
. So
\mathit{II}(X,JY)=J\mathit{II}(X,Y)
.
As
T(X,JX)=0
gives

\nabla_{JX}X-\nabla_{X}(JX)=[JX,X],

and
JX\in TS
, then

\displaystyle\textit{II}(JX,X)=(\nabla_{JX}X)^{\bot}=(\nabla_{X}(JX)+[JX,X])^{\bot}=\textit{II}(X,JX).

By Proposition
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle H(X)

\displaystyle=R(X,JX,JX,X)/|X|^{4}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}(X)+\dfrac{1}{|X|^{4}}(\langle\mathit{II}(X,X),\mathit{II}(X,X)\rangle+\langle\mathit{II}(X,JX),\textit{II}(JX,X)\rangle)

\displaystyle=\hat{H}(X)+2|\mathit{II}(X,X)|^{2}/|X|^{4}

∎

Next, we apply Corollary
4.5
to study the curvature of the pull back metric on a
J
-holomorphic disc on
M
. Denote
\mathbb{D}=\{z=x+\sqrt{-1}y\in\mathbb{C}||z|<1\}
the unit disc associated with the standard complex structure
J_{st}
.

Definition 4.6
.

A
J
-holomorphic disc on
M
is a smooth map
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow M
such that
J\circ d\varphi=d\varphi\circ J_{st}
.

By the work of Nijenhuis-Woolf, given any point
p\in M
and
(1,0)
vector
\xi
in
T^{1,0}_{p}M
with
|\xi|
small enough, there exists a
J
-holomorphic disc on
M
such that
\varphi(0)=p
and
\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z})(p)=\xi
. Equivalently, for a sufficiently small real vector
v\in T_{p}M
, there exists a
J
-holomorphic disc with
\varphi(0)=p
and
\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(0)=\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x})(p)=v
. The following more general existence result is proved in
[
9
]
(Proposition 1.1) using the implicit function theorem.

Theorem 4.7

(Ivashkovich-Rosay)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 1
. Let
J
be a smooth almost complex structure near
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. For any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
close enough to 0, and every
\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\}\in(\mathbb{R}^{2n})^{k}
small enough, there exist a smooth
J
-holomorphic disc
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
such that
\varphi(0)=p
and
\dfrac{\partial^{l}\varphi}{\partial x^{l}}(0)=v_{l}
, for any
1\leq l\leq k
.

Then we can prove

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold. For any
p\in M
and
v\in T_{p}M
with
|v|
small enough, there exists a
J
-holomorphic disc
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow M
such that
\varphi(0)=p,\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(0)=v
and the Gaussian curvature of
\varphi^{*}g
at
0
identifies with the holomorphic sectional curvature
H(v)
.

Proof.

Let
t^{1},t^{2},...,t^{2m}
be local coordinates of
M
at
p
. Let
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
be the real Christoffel symbols of the Chern connection
\nabla
. Write
v=a^{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t^{i}}
. For
|v|
small enough, by Theorem
4.7
, there exists a
J
-holomorphic disc
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow M
such that
\varphi(0)=p
,
\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(0)=v

and

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi^{k}}{\partial x^{2}}(0)+a^{i}a^{j}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(p)=0,

where
\varphi=(\varphi^{1},\cdots,\varphi^{2n}).
If
v
is nonzero, then
\varphi
is an embedding in a neighborhood
U
of
0
. Denote
X=\varphi_{*}(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x})
the vector field near
p
, which extends to a local vector field on
M
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}X(p)=\nabla_{\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x})}(\dfrac{\partial\varphi^{i}}{\partial x}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t^{i}})(p)=(\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi^{k}}{\partial x^{2}}(0)+a^{i}a^{j}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(p))\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t^{k}}=0

Therefore,
\mathit{II}(X,X)(p)=(\nabla_{X}X)^{\bot}(p)=0
. Denote
K(z)
the Gaussian curvature of
\varphi^{*}g
in
U\subset\mathbb{D}
, which is equal to the holomorphic sectional curvature of
(\varphi(U)
. Therefore, by Proposition
4.5
,
K(0)=H(v)
.
∎

5.
A Schwarz lemma on almost Hermitian manifolds

In this section, we prove Theorem
1
. Let
(M,J,g)
and
(N,J_{N},h)
be two almost Hermitian manifolds and
f:M\longrightarrow N
a
(J,J_{N})
holomorphic map. Denote
H_{M}
and
H_{N}
the holomorphic sectional curvatures of
M
and
N
and assume
H_{M}\geq K_{1},H_{N}\leq K_{2}
for some constants
K_{1}\leq 0,K_{2}<0
. Define the supreme norm of
df
as
\|df\|_{0}
by

\displaystyle\|df\|_{0}(p)=\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\dfrac{|df(\xi)|_{h}}{|\xi|_{g}},\ \ \ p\in M,\ \xi\in T_{p}^{1,0}M.

Our main object is to estimate
||df||_{0}
under the conditions in Theorem
1
, since
||df||^{2}_{0}\leq\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}
implies that
f^{*}h\leq\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}g
.
First by definition, for any
p\in M
and any
\xi\in T_{p}^{1,0}M
, we have
|df(\xi)|_{h}\leq\|df\|_{0}(p)|\xi|_{g}
. By change of unitary frames, we may assume that there are local unitary (1,0) frames
\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{m}\}
near
p\in M
and
\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2},...,\eta_{n}\}
near
q=f(p)\in N
such that
df
is given by
df(e_{i})=f_{i}^{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}
with
f_{i}^{\alpha}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{i}^{\alpha}
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq...\geq\lambda_{r}>\lambda_{r+1}=...=0
, and

r
being the rank of
(f_{i}^{\alpha})
. Then
\|df\|_{0}=\lambda_{1}
and
|df(e_{1})|_{h}=\|df\|_{0}|e_{1}|_{g}
. We estimate
\lambda_{1}
in the following.

Let
\xi_{1}=te_{1}\in T_{p}^{1,0}M
with
t>0
small enough. By Proposition
4.8
, there is a
J
-holomorphic disc
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M
with
\varphi(0)=p,\ \dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(0)=Re(\xi_{1})
and the Gaussian curvature of
\varphi^{*}g
at
0
is
H_{M}(\xi_{1})
. Denote

\varphi^{*}g=u^{2}dzd\bar{z}

for some positive function
u
near
0
.
As
H_{M}\geq K_{1}
, and the Gaussian curvature for
\varphi^{*}g
is
-\dfrac{4}{u^{2}}\dfrac{{\partial}^{2}\log u}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}
, we have

\displaystyle-\dfrac{4}{u^{2}}\dfrac{{\partial}^{2}\log u}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}(0)\geq K_{1}.

Since both
\varphi
and
f
are holomorphic, if
\lambda_{1}(p)\neq 0
, there is a neighbourhood
U
of
0
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
(U,f\circ\varphi)
is an almost complex submanifold of
(N,J_{N})
. Then we pull back
h
to
U
to get

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(f^{*}h)=v^{2}dzd\bar{z}.

We have

(5.1)

\displaystyle v(z)=|f_{*}\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z})|_{h}\leq\|df\|_{0}|\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z})|_{g}=\lambda_{1}(\varphi(z))u(z).

As
H_{N}\leq K_{2}
, by Corollary
4.5
, we get

-\dfrac{4}{v^{2}}\dfrac{{\partial}^{2}\log v}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}(0)\leq K_{2}.

Now let
\phi
be an arbitrary smooth function on
M
. Recall that the complex Hessian of
\phi
is

(\nabla^{2}\phi)^{(1,1)}=\phi_{i\bar{j}}\theta^{i}\otimes\bar{\theta}^{j}+\phi_{\bar{i}j}\bar{\theta}^{i}\otimes\theta^{j},

where

\phi_{i\bar{j}}=\nabla^{2}\phi(e_{i},\bar{e}_{j})=e_{i}\bar{e}_{j}\phi-\nabla_{e_{i}}\bar{e}_{j}\phi=\phi_{\bar{j}i}.

We pull back
\phi
to
\mathbb{D}
by
\varphi
and compute its differentials.
As
\varphi
is holomorphic, we may write
\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z})=\varphi^{i}_{z}e_{i}
and then
\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}})=\overline{\varphi}^{i}_{z}\bar{e}_{i}
, where
\overline{\varphi}^{i}_{z}
is the complex conjugate of
\varphi^{i}_{z}
.
Then

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial\bar{z}}=

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}})\phi=\overline{\varphi}^{j}_{z}\bar{e}_{j}\phi

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}=

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z})(\bar{\varphi}^{j}_{z}\overline{e}_{j}\phi)

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi^{i}_{z}\bar{\varphi}^{j}_{z}e_{i}e_{\bar{j}}\phi+\varphi^{i}_{z}(e_{i}\bar{\varphi}^{j}_{z})\overline{e}_{j}\phi.

Since
[\varphi_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\varphi_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}]=\varphi_{*}[\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}]=0,

we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{i}_{z}(e_{i}\overline{\varphi}^{j}_{z})\overline{e}_{j}+\varphi^{i}_{z}\overline{\varphi}^{j}_{z}[e_{i},\overline{e}_{j}]-\overline{\varphi}^{j}_{z}(\overline{e}_{j}\varphi^{i}_{z})e_{i}=0.

As
\nabla_{e_{i}}\bar{e}_{j}-\nabla_{\bar{e}_{j}}e_{i}=[e_{i},\bar{e}_{j}]
, then
\nabla_{e_{i}}\bar{e}_{j}=[e_{i},\bar{e}_{j}]^{(0,1)}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\varphi^{i}_{z}\overline{\varphi}^{j}_{z}\nabla_{e_{i}}\bar{e}_{j}=

\displaystyle\varphi^{i}_{z}\overline{\varphi}^{j}_{z}[e_{i},\overline{e}_{j}]^{(0,1)}=-\varphi^{i}_{z}(e_{i}\overline{\varphi}^{j}_{z})\overline{e}_{j}

Putting it into (
5.2
), we get

(5.3)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}=\varphi^{i}_{z}\bar{\varphi}^{j}_{z}\phi_{i\bar{j}}.

Now we finish the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Fix a point
o\in M
. Let
r(q)=d(o,q)
be the distance function on
M
. For
a>0
, denote

D_{a}=\{q\in M\ |\ r(q)<a\},\ \ \ \ \overline{D}_{a}=\{q\in M\ |\ r(q)\leq a\}.

Define a function
\rho
on
D_{a}
as

\rho=(1-\dfrac{r^{2}}{a^{2}})\|df\|_{0}.

Then
\rho\geq 0
on
\overline{D}_{a}
and achieves its maximum at some
p\in D_{a}
. If
p
is not a cut point of
o
, then
r
is smooth at
p
. Otherwise, we can use the Calabi’s trick to deal with it. Indeed, let
\gamma
be a minimal unit speed geodesic from
o
to
p
. Take a point
q_{0}
on
\gamma
which is not a cut point of
p
and denote

. For any

,
\widetilde{r}(q)=b+d(q_{0},q)
is smooth near
p
. Note that
\widetilde{r}\geq r
and
\widetilde{r}(p)=r(p)
. Choose
\delta
small enough such that the open geodesic ball
B_{p}(\delta)\subset D_{a}
and
\widetilde{r}(q)<a
is smooth in
B_{p}(\delta)
. It follows that
\widetilde{\rho}=(1-\dfrac{\widetilde{r}^{2}}{a^{2}})||df||_{0}\leq\rho
and achieves the same maximum at
p
. Then we may use
\widetilde{\rho}
to replace
\rho
and apply the maximum principle to
\widetilde{\rho}
. So we just assume that
r
is smooth at
p
.

Assume that
\|df\|_{0}(p)=\lambda_{1}(p)>0
(otherwise, there is nothing to prove). Here
\lambda_{1}
is the greatest eigenvalue of
df
introduced previously. Let
\xi_{1}
be an eigenvector of
\lambda_{1}
at
p
with
|\xi_{1}|_{g}
small enough. By the previous argument, there is a
J
-holomorphic disc
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M
such that
\varphi(0)=p,\ \dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(0)=Re(\xi_{1})
and the Gaussian curvature of
\varphi^{*}g
at
0
is
H_{M}(\xi_{1})
. Consider the function

(5.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(z)=\left(1-\dfrac{r^{2}(\varphi(z))}{a^{2}}\right)\dfrac{v(z)}{u(z)}

on
\mathbb{D}
. By (

),
v(z)\leq\lambda_{1}(\varphi(z))u(z)
and
v(0)=\lambda_{1}(p)u(0)
. So
\hat{\rho}(z)\leq\rho(\varphi(z))
and
\hat{\rho}(0)=\rho(\varphi(0))
. Therefore,
\hat{\rho}

achieves its maximum at

. We have at

,

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}\log\hat{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{a^{2}+r^{2}}{(a^{2}-r^{2})^{2}}\left|\frac{\partial r}{\partial\bar{z}}\right|^{2}-\frac{2r}{a^{2}-r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}r}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}\log\frac{v(z)}{u(z)}.

Denote
r_{i}=e_{i}r,r_{\bar{i}}=\bar{e}_{i}r
. As
1=|\nabla r|^{2}_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|r_{i}|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial r}{\partial\bar{z}}\right|^{2}=|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{\varphi}^{i}_{z}r_{i}|^{2}\leq(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\varphi^{i}_{z}|^{2})(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|r_{i}|^{2})=u^{2}(z).

By (
5.3
) and Theorem
2.3
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}r}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}=\varphi^{i}_{z}\bar{\varphi}^{j}_{z}r_{i\bar{j}}\leq(\frac{1}{r}+C)g_{i\bar{j}}\varphi^{i}_{z}\bar{\varphi}^{j}_{z}=(\frac{1}{r}+C)u^{2}(z).

Therefore, at

,

\displaystyle 0\geq-2\frac{a^{2}+r^{2}}{(a^{2}-r^{2})^{2}}u^{2}(0)-(\frac{1}{r}+C)\frac{2r}{a^{2}-r^{2}}u^{2}(0)-\frac{K_{2}}{4}v^{2}(0)+\frac{K_{1}}{4}u^{2}(0).

As
K_{2}<0
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}(p)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{v^{2}(0)}{u^{2}(0)}\leq\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}-\frac{8(a^{2}+r^{2})}{K_{2}(a^{2}-r^{2})^{2}}-\frac{8(1+Cr)}{K_{2}(a^{2}-r^{2})}.

Thus for any
q\in D_{a}
,

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(q)\leq

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{a^{2}-r^{2}(p)}{a^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}-\frac{16a^{2}}{K_{2}(a^{2}-r(p)^{2})^{2}}-\frac{8(1+Ca)}{K_{2}(a^{2}-r(p)^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}-\frac{16}{K_{2}a^{2}}-\frac{8(1+Ca)}{K_{2}a^{2}}.

So we obtain the estimate for
\rho
. Then for any
q\in D_{a}
, we get

\displaystyle\|df\|_{0}^{2}(q)\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\dfrac{r^{2}(q)}{a^{2}}\right)^{-2}\left(\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}-\frac{16}{K_{2}a^{2}}-\frac{8(1+Ca)}{K_{2}a^{2}}\right)

Making
a\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain
\|df\|_{0}^{2}(q)\leq\dfrac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}
, for any

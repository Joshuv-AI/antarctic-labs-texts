Structure of Green’s function of elliptic equations and helical vortex patches for 3D incompressible Euler equations
Daomin Cao and Jie Wan

Abstract
. We develop a new structure of the Green’s function of a second-order elliptic operator in divergence form in a 2D bounded domain. Based on this structure and the theory of rearrangement of functions, we construct concentrated traveling-rotating helical vortex patches to 3D incompressible Euler equations in an infinite pipe. By solving an equation for vorticity w = 1 ε 2 ​ f ε ​ ( 𝒢 K H ​ w − α 2 ​ | x | 2 ​ | ln ⁡ ε | ) in ​ Ω w=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}f_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}w-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x|^{2}|\ln\varepsilon|\right)\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega for small ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 and considering a certain maximization problem for the vorticity, where 𝒢 K H \mathcal{G}_{K_{H}} is the inverse of an elliptic operator ℒ K H \mathcal{L}_{K_{H}} in divergence form, we get the existence of a family of concentrated helical vortex patches, which tend asymptotically to a singular helical vortex filament evolved by the binormal curvature flow. We also get nonlinear orbital stability of the maximizers in the variational problem under L p L^{p} perturbation when p ≥ 2 . p\geq 2. Keywords: Green’s function of second-order elliptic equations; Incompressible Euler equation; Helical symmetry; Maximizing of energy functional; Orbital stability.



1.
Introduction and main results

The incompressible Euler equation confined in a 3D domain
D
is governed by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}=-\nabla P,\ \ &D\times(0,T),\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0,\ \ &D\times(0,T),\\
\mathbf{v}(\cdot,0)=\mathbf{v}_{0}(\cdot),\ \ &D,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
is the velocity field,
P
is the scalar pressure and
\mathbf{v}_{0}
is the initial velocity field. If
D
has a boundary, the following impermeable boundary condition
is usually assumed

\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\ \ \partial D\times(0,T),

where
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal to
\partial D
.

Define the associated vorticity field
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})=curl\mathbf{v}=\nabla\times\mathbf{v}
, which describes the rotation of the fluid. Then
\mathbf{w}
satisfies the vorticity equations (see
[
34
,
35
]
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}=(\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v},\ \ &D\times(0,T),\\
\mathbf{w}(\cdot,0)=\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}(\cdot),\ \ &D.\end{cases}

(1.2)

1.1.
Binormal curvature flow

The study of Euler equation traced back to Helmholtz
[
23
]
in 1858, who first considered the vorticity equations (
1.2
) of the flow and found that the vortex rings, which are toroidal regions (vorticity region) in which the vorticity has small cross-section, translate with a constant speed alone the axis of symmetry. The translating speed of vortex rings was then studied by Kelvin and Hick
[
28
]
in 1899. To be more precise, let
l
be any oriented closed curve with tangent vector field
\mathbf{t}
that encircles the afore mentioned vorticity region once and
\sigma
is any surface with
l
as its boundary. Then the circulation of a vortex is defined by

d=\oint_{l}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{t}dl=\iint_{\sigma}\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}d\sigma.

(1.3)

It was shown in
[
28
]
that if the vortex ring has radius
r^{*}
, circulation
d
and its cross-section
\varepsilon
is small, then the vortex ring moves with the speed

\frac{d}{4\pi r^{*}}\left(\ln\frac{8r^{*}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{4}\right).

(1.4)

As for the motion of general vortex filament, Da Rios
[
12
]
in 1906, and Levi-Civita
[
29
]
in 1908, formally found the general law of motion of a vortex filament with a small cross-section of radius
\varepsilon
and a fixed circulation, uniformly distributed around an evolving curve
\Gamma(t)
, which is well-known as the binormal curvature flow.
More precisely, if
\Gamma(t)
is parameterized as
\gamma(s,t)
, where
s
is the parameter of arclength, then
\gamma(s,t)
asymptotically obeys a law of the form (see
[
30
,
38
]
)

\partial_{t}\gamma=\frac{d}{4\pi}|\ln\varepsilon|(\partial_{s}\gamma\times\partial_{ss}\gamma)=\frac{d\bar{K}}{4\pi}|\ln\varepsilon|\mathbf{b}_{\gamma(t)},

(1.5)

where
d
is the circulation of the velocity field on the boundary of sections
to the filament, which is assumed to be a constant independent of
\varepsilon
,
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma(t)}
is the unit binormal and
\bar{K}
is its local curvature. If we rescale
t=|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau
, then

\partial_{\tau}\gamma=\frac{d\bar{K}}{4\pi}\mathbf{b}_{\gamma(\tau)}.

(1.6)

Hence, under the binormal curvature flow (
1.6
) vortex filaments
move simply in the binormal direction with speed proportional to the local curvature and the circulation, see the survey papers by Ricca
[
37
,
38
]
for more detail. It is worthwhile to note that, when
\Gamma
is a circular filament, the leading term of (
1.4
) coincides with the coefficient of right hand side of (
1.5
) since the local curvature
\bar{K}=\frac{1}{r^{*}}.

From mathematical justification, Jerrad and Seis
[
24
]
first gave a precise form to Da Rios’ computation under some mild conditions on a solution to (
1.2
) which remains suitably concentrated
around an evolving vortex filament. Their result shows that under some assumptions on a solution
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
of (
1.2
), there holds in the sense of distribution,

\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,|\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)\to d\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)},\ \ \text{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0,

(1.7)

where
\gamma(\tau)
satisfies (
1.6
),
\mathbf{t}_{\gamma(\tau)}
is the tangent unit vector of
\gamma
and
\delta_{\gamma(\tau)}
is the uniform Dirac measure on the curve. For more results of this problem, see
[
25
]
.

The existence of a family of solutions to (
1.2
) satisfying (
1.7
), where
\gamma(\tau)
is a given curve evolved by the binormal flow (
1.6
), is well-known as the vortex filament conjecture, which is unsolved except for the filament being several kinds of special curves: the straight lines, the traveling circles and the traveling-rotating helices.
For the problem of vortex concentrating near straight lines, it corresponds to the planar Euler equations concentrating near a collection of given points governed by the 2D point vortex model, see
[
10
,
11
,
13
,
32
,
39
]
for example. When the filament is a traveling circle with radius
r^{*}
, by (
1.6
) the curve is

\gamma(s,\tau)=\left(r^{*}\cos\left(\frac{s}{r^{*}}\right),r^{*}\sin\left(\frac{s}{r^{*}}\right),\frac{d}{4\pi r^{*}}\tau\right)^{t},

(1.8)

where
\mathbf{v}^{t}
is the transposition of a vector
\mathbf{v}
. Fraenkel
[
19
]
first gave a construction of vortex rings with small cross-section without change of form concentrating near a traveling circle (
1.8
), see
[
2
,
7
,
15
,
16
,
20
]
for more results.

For vortex filament being a helix satisfying (
1.6
), the curve is parameterized as

\gamma(s,\tau)=\left(r_{*}\cos\left(\frac{-s-a_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\right),r_{*}\sin\left(\frac{-s-a_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\right),\frac{ks-b_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\right)^{t},

(1.9)

where
r_{*}>0,k\neq 0
are constants characterizing the distance between a point in
\gamma(\tau)
and the
x_{3}
-axis and the pitch of the helix, and

a_{1}=\frac{dk}{4\pi(k^{2}+r_{*}^{2})},\ b_{1}=\frac{dr_{*}^{2}}{4\pi(k^{2}+r_{*}^{2})}.

Observe that the local curvature and torsion of the helix are
\frac{r_{*}}{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}
and
\frac{k}{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}
respectively, and the parametrization (
1.9
) satisfies (
1.6
). It should be noted that the curve parameterized by (
1.9
) is a traveling-rotating helix. Let us define for any
\theta\in[0,2\pi]

\bar{R}_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\ \ \bar{Q}_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{R}_{\theta}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

By direct calculations it is easy to see that

\gamma(s,\tau)=\bar{Q}_{\frac{a_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}}\gamma(s,0)+\left(0,0,-\frac{b_{1}\tau}{\sqrt{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}\right)^{t}.

(
1.9
) with
k>0
and
k<0
correspond to the left-handed helix and the right-handed helix respectively. We always consider the case of
k>0
in this paper.

1.2.
2D vorticity equation

Our purpose in this paper is to study the vortex filament conjecture in a special case that the filament is a helix. We want to construct a family of 3D Euler flow
such that the associated vorticity field
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
concentrates near a traveling-rotating helical vortex filament (
1.9
).
Let us first define solutions of Euler equation with helical symmetry and deduce it to a 2D vorticity equation, see for example
[
17
,
18
]
. For
k>0
, define a group of one-parameter helical transformations
\mathcal{H}_{k}=\{H_{\bar{\rho}}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid\bar{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where

H_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{t}=(x_{1}\cos\bar{\rho}+x_{2}\sin\bar{\rho},-x_{1}\sin\bar{\rho}+x_{2}\cos\bar{\rho},x_{3}+k\bar{\rho})^{t}.

Define the field of tangents of symmetry lines of
\mathcal{H}_{k}

\overrightarrow{\zeta}=(x_{2},-x_{1},k)^{t}.

Suppose that the domain
D
is helical, that is,
H_{\bar{\rho}}(D)=D
for any
\bar{\rho}
.
Let
\Omega=D\cap\{x\mid x_{3}=0\}
be the section of
D
over
x_{1}Ox_{2}
plane. Then
D
can be generated by
\Omega
by letting
D=\cup_{\bar{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}}H_{\bar{\rho}}(\Omega)
. Hereafter we suppose that
\Omega
is simply-connected bounded domain with
C^{\infty}
boundary.

Now one can define helical functions and vector fields in a helical domain
D
. A scalar function
h
is
helical
, if
h(H_{\bar{\rho}}(x))=h(x)
for any
\bar{\rho}\in\mathbb{R},x\in D.

A vector field
\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})
is
helical
, if
\mathbf{h}(H_{\bar{\rho}}(x))=\bar{Q}_{\bar{\rho}}\mathbf{h}(x)
for any
\bar{\rho}\in\mathbb{R},x\in D.

Helical solutions of (
1.1
) are then defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

A function pair (
\mathbf{v},P
) is called a
helical
solution of (
1.1
) in
D
, if (
\mathbf{v},P
) satisfies (
1.1
) and both vector field
\mathbf{v}
and scalar function
P
are helical.

Similar to
[
18
]
, we assume that helical solutions satisfy the
orthogonality condition
:

\mathbf{v}\cdot\overrightarrow{\zeta}=0.

(1.10)

(
1.10
) is also called the no-swirl condition. From
[
18
]
we know that, under the assumption (
1.10
), the vorticity field
\mathbf{w}
satisfies

\mathbf{w}=\frac{w_{3}}{k}\overrightarrow{\zeta},

where
w_{3}=\partial_{x_{1}}v_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}v_{1}
is the third component of vorticity field
\mathbf{w}
, which is a helical function.
Moreover, if define
w(x_{1},x_{2},t)=w_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},0,t)
, then
w
satisfies the 2D vorticity-stream equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w+\nabla^{\perp}\varphi\cdot\nabla w=0,\ \ &\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
w=\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}\varphi,\ \ &\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
\varphi=0,\ \ &\ \partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
w(\cdot,0)=w_{0}(\cdot),\ \ &\ \Omega,\end{cases}

(1.11)

where
\varphi
is called the stream function,
\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}\varphi:=-\text{div}(K_{H}(x_{1},x_{2})\nabla\varphi)
is a second order elliptic operator of divergence type with the coefficient matrix

K_{H}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{k^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}k^{2}+x_{2}^{2}&-x_{1}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}x_{2}&k^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(1.12)

and
\perp
denotes the clockwise rotation through
\pi/2
, i.e.,
(a,b)^{\perp}=(b,-a)
. Note that
K_{H}
is strictly positive-definite, by the elliptic regularity theory, for any
p\in(1,+\infty)
one can define a continuous linear operator
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}
from
L^{p}(\Omega)
to
W^{2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi=\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}w
for any
w\in L^{p}(\Omega)
(see also Proposition
2.1
in section 2). Thus (
1.11
) can be rewritten as the following vorticity equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w+\nabla^{\perp}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}w\cdot\nabla w=0,\ \ &\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
w(\cdot,0)=w_{0}(\cdot),\ \ &\ \Omega.\end{cases}

(1.13)

If
p>4/3
, then
\nabla^{\perp}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\subset L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of p. Thus we can give the definition of weak solutions to the vorticity equation (
1.13
) since
w\cdot\nabla^{\perp}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}w
is integrable.

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose
p\in(4/3,+\infty]
. We call
w(x,t)\in L^{\infty}((0,+\infty);L^{p}(\Omega))
a weak solution to (
1.13
) if for all
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,+\infty))

\int_{\Omega}w_{0}(x)\xi(x,0)dx+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\Omega}w(\partial_{t}\xi+\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla^{\perp}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}w)dxdt=0.

(1.14)

when
p=+\infty
, the existence and uniqueness result for weak solution to vorticity equation (
1.13
) was firstly proved by
[
18
]
. For general
p>4/3
, by using an approximation procedure and the Diperna-Lions theory of linear transport equations,
[
4
]
proved the following existence result.

Theorem A
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply-connected bounded domain with
C^{\infty}
boundary. Suppose
4/3<p<+\infty
and
w_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a weak solution
w(x,t)\in L^{\infty}((0,+\infty);L^{p}(\Omega))
to the vorticity equation (
1.13
). Moreover, if
p\geq 2
, then

(i)

all
L^{\infty}((0,+\infty);L^{p}(\Omega))
solutions belong to
C([0,+\infty);L^{p}(\Omega))
;

(ii)

for any weak solution
w(x,t)\in L^{\infty}((0,+\infty);L^{p}(\Omega))
, we have
w(x,t)\in\textbf{R}_{w_{0}}
for all
t\geq 0
, where
\textbf{R}_{w_{0}}
denotes the rearrangement class of
w_{0}
,

\textbf{R}_{w_{0}}:=\{v\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\mid|\{v>a\}|=|\{w_{0}>a\}|,\forall a\in\mathbb{R}\}.

A typical weak solution for characterizing an isolated region of vorticity with jump discontinuity is the vortex patch solution, that is, the initial vorticity has the form

w_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{A_{0}},

(1.15)

where
\textbf{1}_{A_{0}}
the characteristic function of
{A_{0}}
, namely
\textbf{1}_{A_{0}}=1
in
{A_{0}}
and
\textbf{1}_{A_{0}}=0
in
{A_{0}}^{c}
, and
\varepsilon
is the vorticity strength parameter. Clearly from Theorem
A
, there exists the unique solution
w(x,t)
of (
1.13
) being of the form
w(\cdot,t)=\textbf{1}_{A_{t}}
, where
|A_{0}|=|A_{t}|
for any
t>0
. More results of global well-posedness of solutions to Euler equation (
1.1
) with helical symmetry can be seen in
[
1
,
5
,
17
,
26
]
for instance.

Observe that the motion of the intersection point of the helix (
1.9
) and the
x_{1}Ox_{2}
plane is a clockwise rotation about the origin with constant angular velocity, so it suffices to construct a family of rotation-invariant solutions to (
1.13
), such that the associated helical vorticity field
\mathbf{w}_{\varepsilon}
concentrates near the curve (
1.9
).

Thereafter we assume that
D
is an infinite pipe: for any
R^{*}>0
, define
D=B_{R^{*}}(0)\times\mathbb{R}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\mid(x_{1},x_{2})\in B_{R^{*}}(0),x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
. So
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}(0)
. Let
\alpha
be a constant. We look for solutions of (
1.13
) being of the form

w(x^{\prime},t)=W(\bar{R}_{-\alpha|\ln\varepsilon|t}(x^{\prime})),

(1.16)

where
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in B_{R^{*}}(0)
. Then one computes directly that
W
satisfies

\begin{split}\nabla W\cdot\nabla^{\perp}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}W-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}|\ln\varepsilon|\right)=0.\end{split}

(1.17)

So formally if

\begin{split}W=f_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}W-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}|\ln\varepsilon|\right),\ \ \ B_{R^{*}}(0),\end{split}

(1.18)

for some function
f_{\varepsilon}
, then (
1.17
) automatically holds.
For a solution
W
of (
1.18
), one can get a rotating-invariant solution
w
of (
1.13
) with angular velocity
\alpha|\ln\varepsilon|
by simply using (
1.16
). Observe that if we define
\varPhi=\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}W
, then it is equivalent to solve a semilinear elliptic problem in divergence form

\begin{cases}-\text{div}(K_{H}(x)\nabla\varPhi)=f_{\varepsilon}\left(\varPhi-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}|\ln\varepsilon|\right),\ \ &\ B_{R^{*}}(0),\\
\varPhi=0,\ &\ \partial B_{R^{*}}(0),\end{cases}

For a helix (
1.9
), there are a few results of existence of true solutions of (
1.2
) concentrating on this curve. D
\acute{\text{a}}
vila et al.
[

]
considered smooth helical Euler flows concentrating near a single helix in the whole space

by considering a Liouville type equation

\begin{split}-\text{div}(K_{H}(x)\nabla u)=f_{\varepsilon}\left(u-\alpha|\ln\varepsilon|\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\right)\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2},\end{split}

where
f_{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon^{2}e^{t}
and

is chosen properly. Note that by the choice of

1.3.
Main results

Our main results are divided into two parts: the expansion of Green’s function of a second-order elliptic operator with Dirichlet condition, and the existence and orbital stability of concentrated helical vortex patches to 3D Euler equations in infinite pipes.

It follows from (
1.18
) that the Green’s function of
\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}
appearing
in the construction of the stream function plays an important role in constructing concentrated helical vorticity field to Euler equations (
1.1
). Thus we shall need to understand the structure of Green’s function of the elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}
. To this end, we first study Green’s function of a linear problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{K}u:=-\text{div}(K(x)\nabla u)=f,\ \ &x\in U,\\
u=0,\ \ &x\in\partial U,\end{cases}

(1.19)

where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a simply-connected bounded domain with smooth boundary,
K=(K_{i,j})_{2\times 2}
is a positive-definite matrix satisfying

(
\mathcal{K}
1).

K_{i,j}(x)\in C^{\infty}(\overline{U})
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2.

(
\mathcal{K}
2).

-\text{div}(K(x)\nabla\cdot)
is uniformly elliptic, that is, there exist
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}>0
such that

\Lambda_{1}|\zeta|^{2}\leq(K(x)\zeta|\zeta)\leq\Lambda_{2}|\zeta|^{2},\ \ \ \ \forall\ x\in U,\ \zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Since
K
satisfies
(\mathcal{K}1)
–
(\mathcal{K}2)
, one can define a Green’s operator
\mathcal{G}_{K}
such that for every
f\in L^{p}(U)
with
p>1
, the function
u=\mathcal{G}_{K}f
is a weak solution to the problem (
1.19
), see Proposition
2.1
.

Let
\Gamma(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|
be the fundamental solution of the Laplacian
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
T_{x}
be a
C^{\infty}
positive-definite matrix-valued function determined by
K
satisfying

T_{x}^{-1}(T_{x}^{-1})^{t}=K(x)\ \ \ \ \forall\ x\in U.

(1.20)

Clearly by the Cholsky decomposition and the properties of positive-definite matrix, such
T_{x}
exists and is unique.

Our first result shows a structure of the Green’s function of (
1.19
) as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
q>2.
There exists a function
S_{K}\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(U\times U)
for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that for every
f\in L^{q}(U)
and every
x\in U
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{K}f(x)=\int_{U}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)+S_{K}(x,y)\right)f(y)dy.\end{split}

(1.21)

Moreover,

S_{K}(x,y)=S_{K}(y,x)\ \ \ \ \forall\ x,y\in U,

(1.22)

and there is a positive constant
C
such that

S_{K}(x,y)\leq C\ \ \ \ \forall\ x,y\in U.

(1.23)

Theorem
1.3
implies that the Dirichlet problem in divergence form (
1.19
) has a
Green’s function
G_{K}:U\times U\to\mathbb{R}
defined for each
x,y\in U
with
x\neq y
by

G_{K}(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{\det K(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)+S_{K}(x,y),

(1.24)

which is not known in the existing literature. It should be noted that when choosing the matrix
K(x)=Id
and
\frac{1}{b(x)}Id
, which correspond to 2D Euler flows and lake equations respectively, (
1.24
) coincides with the classical results, see
[
11
,
15
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Below we give examples of the matrix
K
in (
1.19
) to explain the expansion of Green’s function in Theorem
1.3
.

Example
1. If
K(x)=Id
, then (
1.19
) is the standard Laplacian problem, which corresponds to the vorticity-stream formulation to 2D Euler equations, see
[

,
11
,

,

]

So in this case,
S_{1}(x,y)=-H(x,y)
, where
H(x,y)
is the regular part of Green’s function of
-\Delta
in
U
with zero-Dirichlet data.

Example
2. If
K(x)=\frac{1}{b(x)}Id
, where

and
\inf_{U}b>0
, then (

which coincides with results in
[
15
]
.

Remark 1.7

The condition

Indeed, the existence of solutions of general elliptic equations in divergence form has been studied by

&+\int_{\partial B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})\left(\tilde{K}(x^{\prime})\nabla_{x^{\prime}}v(x^{\prime})|\frac{p^{\prime}-x^{\prime}}{{\bar{\epsilon}}}\right)dS_{x^{\prime}}\\
=&O({\bar{\epsilon}}|\ln{\bar{\epsilon}}|+{\bar{\epsilon}}).\end{split}

(2.10)

For
I_{1}
, notice that

\begin{split}I_{1}=&\int_{\partial B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}v(x^{\prime})\left(\tilde{K}(x^{\prime})\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\tilde{G}_{0}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|\frac{p^{\prime}-x^{\prime}}{{\bar{\epsilon}}}\right)dS_{x^{\prime}}\\
&\int_{\partial B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}v(x^{\prime})\left(\tilde{K}(x^{\prime})\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|\frac{p^{\prime}-x^{\prime}}{{\bar{\epsilon}}}\right)dS_{x^{\prime}}\\
=:&J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

(2.11)

On the one hand, by the trace theorem

\begin{split}|J_{2}|\leq&\int_{\partial B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}|v(x^{\prime})||\tilde{K}(x^{\prime})\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|dS_{x^{\prime}}\\
\leq&C\int_{\partial B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}|\partial_{x_{1}^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{x_{2}^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|dS_{x^{\prime}}\\
=&C\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}|\partial_{z_{1}}\tilde{S}_{K}({\bar{\epsilon}}z+p,p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{z_{2}}\tilde{S}_{K}({\bar{\epsilon}}z+p,p^{\prime})|dS_{z}\\
\leq&C\bigg(\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\partial_{z_{1}}\tilde{S}_{K}({\bar{\epsilon}}z+p,p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{z_{2}}\tilde{S}_{K}({\bar{\epsilon}}z+p,p^{\prime})|dz\\
&+\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\partial_{z_{1}z_{1}}\tilde{S}_{K}({\bar{\epsilon}}z+p,p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{z_{1}z_{2}}\tilde{S}_{K}({\bar{\epsilon}}z+p,p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{z_{2}z_{2}}\tilde{S}_{K}({\bar{\epsilon}}z+p,p^{\prime})|dz\bigg)\\
=&C\bigg(\frac{1}{{\bar{\epsilon}}}\int_{B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}|\partial_{x_{1}^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{x_{2}^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|dx^{\prime}\\
&+\int_{B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}|\partial_{x_{1}^{\prime}x_{1}^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{x_{1}^{\prime}x_{2}^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|+|\partial_{x_{2}^{\prime}x_{2}^{\prime}}\tilde{S}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})|dx^{\prime}\bigg)\\
\to&0\ \ \ \ \text{as}\ \ {\bar{\epsilon}}\to 0,\end{split}

(2.12)

where we have used
\tilde{S}_{K}(\cdot,p^{\prime})\in W^{2,q}(U_{p})
for
q\in(1,2).

On the other hand,

\begin{split}J_{1}&=\int_{\partial B_{\bar{\epsilon}}(p^{\prime})}v(x^{\prime})\frac{\sqrt{\det K(T_{p}^{-1}x^{\prime})}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(T_{p}^{-1}p^{\prime})}^{-1}}{2}\left(\tilde{K}(x^{\prime})\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\Gamma(x^{\prime}-p^{\prime})|\frac{p^{\prime}-x^{\prime}}{{\bar{\epsilon}}}\right)dS_{x^{\prime}}+o(1)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi{\bar{\epsilon}}}\int_{\partial B_{{\bar{\epsilon}}}(p^{\prime})}\tilde{K}_{11}(x^{\prime})\frac{\sqrt{\det K(T_{p}^{-1}x^{\prime})}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K(T_{p}^{-1}p^{\prime})}^{-1}}{2}v(x^{\prime})dS_{x^{\prime}}+o(1)\\
&=\sqrt{\det K(p)}^{-1}v(p^{\prime})+o(1)\\
&=\sqrt{\det K(p)}^{-1}u(p)+o(1).\end{split}

(2.13)

Applying (
2.10
), (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) to (
2.9
) and letting
{\bar{\epsilon}}\to 0
, we get

\begin{split}\sqrt{\det K(p)}^{-1}u(p)=&\int_{U_{p}}\tilde{G}_{K}(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})\tilde{L}v(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\\
=&\det(T_{p})\int_{U}{G}_{K}(x,p)\mathcal{L}_{K}u(x)dx\\
=&\sqrt{\det K(p)}^{-1}\int_{U}{G}_{K}(x,p)\mathcal{L}_{K}u(x)dx.\end{split}

Then (
2.7
) holds.

Finally, we observe that if
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{q}(U)
, then there holds

\begin{split}&\int_{U}f_{1}\mathcal{G}_{K}f_{2}-\iint_{U\times U}G_{0}(y,x)f_{1}(x)f_{2}(y)dxdy\\
&\ \ =\int_{U}f_{2}\mathcal{G}_{K}f_{1}-\iint_{U\times U}G_{0}(y,x)f_{2}(x)f_{1}(y)dxdy,\end{split}

and thus

\iint_{U\times U}S_{K}(y,x)f_{1}(x)f_{2}(y)dxdy=\iint_{U\times U}S_{K}(y,x)f_{2}(x)f_{1}(y)dxdy.

It follows that for every
x,y\in U
,
S_{K}(x,y)=S_{K}(y,x)
, and thus the function
S_{K}\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(U\times U).
By (
1.22
), (
2.7
) and Proposition
2.1
, we get (
1.21
). The proof of Theorem
1.3
is therefore finished.

∎

Remark 2.2
.

One can not improve the regularity of
S_{K}
to
C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{U\times U})
. Since from (
2.6
), for any
x\in\partial U
,
S_{K}(x,y)=-G_{0}(x,y)\to-\infty
as
y\to x.
This implies that
||S_{K}||_{L^{\infty}(U\times U)}
is unbounded.

3.
Variational settings: Existence and profile of maximizers

As an application, Theorem
1.3
can be used to construct concentrated helical vorticity field to 3D Euler equations in infinite pipes, which is shown in Theorem
1.6
.
From subsection 1.2, it suffices to construct a family of concentrated solutions to (
1.18
) with
f_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{\{t>\mu^{\varepsilon}\}}
for some
\mu^{\varepsilon}
.
To this end, our first step is to introduce a certain variational problem and show the existence and profile of maximizers.

Let
k>0,d>0,R^{*}>0
be three fixed numbers. For any
r_{*}\in(0,R^{*})
, from now on we will always choose
\alpha>0
such that

\alpha=\frac{d}{4\pi k\sqrt{k^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}}.

(3.1)

Denote
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}(0)
the cross-section of the infinite cylinder
D
. Let
0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,\sqrt{|\Omega|/d}\}
be a parameter. Define

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{\omega\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)~|~\int_{\Omega}\omega dx=d,\ \text{and}~0\leq\omega\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\}.

For any
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
, define the kinetic energy

\mathcal{E}(\omega):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega dx,

(3.2)

and the moment of inertia

\mathcal{I}(\omega):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega dx.

(3.3)

Consider the maximization problem of the following functional over
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega):=&\mathcal{E}(\omega)-\alpha\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{I}(\omega)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega dx-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega dx.\end{split}

(3.4)

For any
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
, by the classical elliptic estimate, we have
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
for any
1<p<+\infty
. Thus
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
is a well-defined functional on
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists
\omega=\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})=\max_{\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\omega})<+\infty.

Moreover,

\omega_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi^{\varepsilon}>0\}}\ \ a.e.\ \text{in}\ \Omega,

(3.5)

where

\psi^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\mu^{\varepsilon},

(3.6)

and the Lagrange multiplier
\mu^{\varepsilon}\geq-\frac{\alpha|R^{*}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
is determined by
\omega_{\varepsilon}
. Consequently,
\omega_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution to (
1.17
) with
f_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{\{t>\mu^{\varepsilon}\}}
.

Proof.

Clearly, it follows from the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
that

\sup_{\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\omega})<+\infty.

We may take a sequence
\{\omega_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
such that as
j\to+\infty

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{j})\to\sup\{\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\omega})\mid\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\}.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
is closed in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
weak star topology and
L^{2}(\Omega)
weak topology, there exists
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\omega_{j}\to\omega\ \ \ \ \ \ ~~\text{weakly~in}\ L^{2}(\Omega)\ \text{and~weakly~star~in}\ L^{\infty}(\Omega).

It is easily checked that
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
W^{2,2}(\Omega)
, we first have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}{\omega_{j}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{j}}dx=\int_{\Omega}{\omega\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega}dx.

On the other hand, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{j}dx=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega dx.\end{split}

Consequently, we may conclude that
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{j})=\sup\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
, with
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
.

We now turn to prove
(
3.5
)
. Consider the family of variations of
\omega

\omega_{(s)}=\omega+s(\tilde{\omega}-\omega),\ \ \ s\in[0,1],

defined for arbitrary
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
. Since
\omega
is a maximizer, we have
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)\geq\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{(s)})
, which implies that

\begin{split}0&\geq\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{(s)})|_{s=0^{+}}\\
&=\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{\omega}-\omega)\left(\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right)dx.\end{split}

This shows that for any
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
, there holds

\int_{\Omega}\omega\left(\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right)dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\omega}\left(\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right)dx.

By an adaptation of the bathtub principle (see
[
31
]
, §1.14), we obtain

\begin{cases}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu^{\varepsilon}\geq 0,&\ \ \ \omega=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}},\\
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu^{\varepsilon}=0,&\ \ \ 0<\omega<\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}},\\
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu^{\varepsilon}\leq 0,&\ \ \ \omega=0,\end{cases}

(3.7)

where
\mu^{\varepsilon}
arises as a Lagrange multiplier satisfying

\mu^{\varepsilon}=\inf\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}\,\,\,\mid\,\,\,\bigg|\left\{x\in\Omega\,\,\mid\,\,\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}>s\right\}\bigg|\leq d\varepsilon^{2}\right\}.

(3.8)

This implies that
\omega=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}
a.e. in
\{x\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu^{\varepsilon}>0\}
and
\omega=0
a.e. in
\{x\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu^{\varepsilon}<0\}
.

On the set
\{x\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu^{\varepsilon}=0\}
, by properties of Sobolev space, we have

\omega=\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}(\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega)=\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}\left(\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right)=-\frac{2k^{4}\alpha}{(k^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}<0\ \ \text{a.e.}

However, by the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
, we have
w\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
. So the Lebesgue measure of the set
\{x\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\mu^{\varepsilon}=0\}
is zero. Let
\omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega
.
Now the stated form
(
3.5
)
follows immediately.

We prove the lower bound of
\mu^{\varepsilon}
. We shall argue by contradiction. Suppose that
\mu^{\varepsilon}<-\frac{\alpha|R^{*}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
, then by (
3.8
), we get

\bigg|\left\{x\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}>-\frac{\alpha|R^{*}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right\}\bigg|\leq d\varepsilon^{2}.

(3.9)

Since
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}
is a positive elliptic operator, we have
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega>0
in
\Omega
. Note also that for any
x\in\Omega
,
|x|\leq R^{*}
. Thus we get
\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}>-\frac{\alpha|R^{*}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
in
\Omega
. This implies that
\bigg|\left\{x\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega-\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}>-\frac{\alpha|R^{*}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right\}\bigg|=|\Omega|
, which is clearly a contradiction to (
3.9
) since
\varepsilon<\sqrt{|\Omega|/d}
. By (
3.5
) , it is not hard to check that
\omega_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution to (
1.17
). The proof is thus completed.
∎

4.
Asymptotic behavior of maximizers
\omega_{\varepsilon}

Now we are to obtain the limiting behavior of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon
tends to 0. The key is to use the structure of Green’s function of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}
shown in Theorem
1.3
. For convenience we will use
C

to denote generic positive constants independent of
\varepsilon
that may change from line to line.

Let us define a function
Y(x):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
which will be frequently used in this section. For any
x\in\Omega
, define

Y(x):=\frac{d}{2\pi\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x)}}-\alpha|x|^{2}=\frac{d\sqrt{k^{2}+|x|^{2}}}{2\pi k}-\alpha|x|^{2},

(4.1)

where
\alpha
is chosen by (
3.1
). Clearly,
Y
is radially symmetric in
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}(0)
. Then one computes directly that

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the choice of
\alpha
in (
3.1
), the maximizers set of
Y
in
\Omega
is
\{x\mid|x|=r_{*}\}
. That is,
Y|_{\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)}=\max_{\Omega}Y
. Moreover, up to a rotation the maximizer is unique.

Let
G_{K_{H}}(x,y)
be the Green’s function of
\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}
in
\Omega
, with respect to zero Dirichlet data and the Lebesgue measure
dx
. That is, the solution of the second-order elliptic equation in divergence form

\begin{cases}-\text{div}(K_{H}(x)\nabla u)=f,\ &\ \Omega,\\
u=0,\ &\ \partial\Omega\end{cases}

can be expressed by

u(\cdot)=\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}f(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}G_{K_{H}}(\cdot,y)f(y)dy.

By Theorem
1.3
, we have the following properties of
G_{K_{H}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p>2.
There exists a function
S_{K_{H}}\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\Omega\times\Omega)
for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that for every
\omega\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and every
x\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega(x)=&\int_{\Omega}G_{K_{H}}(x,y)\omega(y)dy\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K_{H}(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)+S_{K_{H}}(x,y)\right)\omega(y)dy.\end{split}

Here
\Gamma(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|
is the fundamental solution of the Laplacian
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
T_{x}
is a
C^{\infty}
positive-definite matrix-valued function determined by
K_{H}
satisfying

T_{x}^{-1}(T_{x}^{-1})^{t}=K_{H}(x)\ \ \ \ \forall\ x\in\Omega.

(4.2)

Moreover,
S_{K_{H}}(x,y)=S_{K_{H}}(y,x)
for any
x,y\in\Omega
and

S_{K_{H}}(x,y)\leq C\ \ \ \ \forall\ x,y\in\Omega.

4.1.
Lower bound of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})

We begin by giving a lower bound of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
z\in\Omega
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{d}{2}Y(z)\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-C.

As a consequence, there holds

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{d}{2}Y((r_{*},0))\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-C.

(4.3)

Proof.

For any
z\in\Omega
, we choose a test function
\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
defined by

\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z},

where the matrix
T_{z}
satisfies (
4.2
), and
r_{\varepsilon}>0
satisfies

\pi r_{\varepsilon}^{2}\sqrt{\det K_{H}(z)}=\varepsilon^{2}d.

(4.4)

Thus
\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Since
\omega_{\varepsilon}
is a maximizer, we have
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon})
. By Proposition
4.2
, we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon})=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x)G_{K_{H}}(x,y)\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(y)dxdy-{\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}dx\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K_{H}(y)}^{-1}}{2\varepsilon^{4}}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)dxdy\\
&-{\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}dx-C_{1}.\end{split}

Since
r_{\varepsilon}=O(\varepsilon),
by the positive-definiteness and regularity of
K_{H}
, we have

\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x)}^{-1}=\sqrt{\det K_{H}(z)}^{-1}+O(\varepsilon),\ \ \ \ \forall x\in T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z,

\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}(x-y)\right)=\Gamma\left(T_{z}(x-y)\right)+O(\varepsilon),\ \ \ \ \forall x,y\in T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z,\ x\neq y.

Thus one computes directly that

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon})\geq&\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\left(\sqrt{\det K_{H}(z)}^{-1}+O(\varepsilon)\right)\left(\Gamma\left(T_{z}(x-y)\right)+O(\varepsilon)\right)dxdy\\
&-{\frac{d\alpha(|z|+O(\varepsilon))^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{2}\\
\geq&\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(z)}^{-1}}{4\pi\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\int_{T_{z}^{-1}B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)+z}\ln{\frac{1}{|T_{z}(x-y)|}}dxdy-{\frac{d\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{3}\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(z)}^{-1}}{4\pi\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)}\int_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)}\ln{\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}}\cdot\det K_{H}(z)dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}-{\frac{d\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{3}\\
\geq&\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(z)}}{4\pi\varepsilon^{4}}(\pi r_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-{\frac{d\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}-C_{4}\\
=&\frac{d}{2}Y(z)\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-C_{4},\end{split}

where we have used (
4.2
) and (
4.4
). By choosing
z=(r_{*},0)
and using Lemma
4.1
, we get (
4.3
). The proof is thus finished.
∎

We now turn to estimate the Lagrange multiplier
\mu^{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There holds for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,

\mu^{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{2}{d}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-C.

As a consequence,

\mu^{\varepsilon}\geq Y((r_{*},0))\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-C.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
and (
3.6
) that

\begin{split}2\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})=&\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-\alpha\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}dx-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx+d\mu^{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(4.5)

By (
3.6
) and
\mu^{\varepsilon}\geq-\frac{\alpha|R^{*}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
, we have
\psi^{\varepsilon}\leq 0
on
\partial\Omega.
So
(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).
Denote
\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega\mid\psi^{\varepsilon}>0\}
the vortex core of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}.
By Proposition
3.1
, we know that
supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})=\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}\psi^{\varepsilon}=\omega_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}|x|^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, multiplying
(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}
to both sides of this equation and integrating by parts, we get

\int_{\Omega}(K_{H}(x)\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}|\nabla(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+})dx=\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}dx-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}|x|^{2}(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}dx.

(4.6)

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}|x|^{2}=-\frac{4k^{4}}{(k^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}&(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}dx-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}_{K_{H}}|x|^{2}(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}dx\\
\leq&\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}}(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}dx\\
\leq&\frac{C|\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\frac{C|\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}|dx\\
\leq&\frac{C|\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}|}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Cd\left(\int_{\Omega}(K_{H}(x)\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}|\nabla(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(4.7)

where we have used the Sobolev imbedding
W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
, the positive-definiteness of
K_{H}
and the fact that
\frac{|\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}|}{\varepsilon^{2}}=\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx=d.

Combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we conclude that
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}(\psi^{\varepsilon})_{+}dx
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. The desired result clearly follows from (
4.5
) and Lemma
4.3
.
∎

4.2.
Asymptotic behavior of support set of
\omega_{\varepsilon}

Now we analyze the limiting behavior of the maximizer
\omega_{\varepsilon}
. We will prove that, to maximize the energy
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
, the support set of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
must shrink to a single point which is a maximizer of
Y
in
\Omega
as
\varepsilon
tends to 0. Thus from Lemma
4.1
,
supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})
will concentrate near
\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)
.

Let

\bar{P}_{\varepsilon}=\inf\{|x|\mid x\in{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\},\ \ \text{and}\ \ \bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}=\sup\{|x|\mid x\in{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\}.

(4.8)

\bar{P}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}
describe the lower bound and upper bound of the distance between the origin and
{supp}(\omega_{{\varepsilon}})
, respectively. Let
P_{\varepsilon},Q_{\varepsilon}
be two points in
{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
such that

|P_{\varepsilon}|=\bar{P}_{\varepsilon},\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ |Q_{\varepsilon}|=\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}.

We now prove that the support set of the maximizer
\omega_{\varepsilon}
constructed above
must be concentrated as
\varepsilon
tends to zero. We reach our goal by several steps as follows.

Lemma 4.5
.

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\bar{P}_{\varepsilon}=r_{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
. By Proposition
3.1
, for any
x_{\varepsilon}\in{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
, we have

\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})-\frac{\alpha|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\geq\mu^{{\varepsilon}}.

Note that

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})&=\int_{\Omega}G_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\\
&=\left(\int_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}+\int_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\right)G_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy.\end{split}

On the one hand, it follows from Proposition
4.2
that
\max_{x,y\in\Omega}S_{K_{H}}(x,y)\leq C
, which implies that
\int_{\Omega}S_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\leq C
. Direct computation shows that

\sqrt{\det K_{H}(y)}^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ y\in\Omega,

\bigg|\frac{T_{x_{\varepsilon}}+T_{y}}{2}(y-x_{\varepsilon})\bigg|\geq C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ |T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}.

Thus by the decomposition of
G_{K_{H}}
in Proposition
4.2
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\\
&\ \ \leq\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K_{H}(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x_{\varepsilon}}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x_{\varepsilon}-y\right)\right)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+C.\end{split}

(4.9)

On the other hand, by the smoothness and positive-definiteness of
K_{H}
, it is not hard to get that

\sqrt{\det K_{H}(y)}^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma},\ \ \ \ \forall\ y\in\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}.

Hence we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\\
&\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x_{\varepsilon}}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x_{\varepsilon}-y\right)\right)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy+C\\
&\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\Gamma\left(T_{x_{\varepsilon}}\left(x_{\varepsilon}-y\right)\right)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy+C\\
&=\ \ \left(\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\int_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\Gamma(z)\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}z+x_{\varepsilon})\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}dz+C.\end{split}

Denote
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\textbf{1}_{B_{t_{\varepsilon}}(0)}
the non-negative decreasing rearrangement function of
\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}z+x_{\varepsilon})\textbf{1}_{\{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}
. Here we choose
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\int\limits_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}z+x_{\varepsilon})dz=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega_{\varepsilon}^{*}dz
. By the rearrangement inequality, we have

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\Gamma(z)\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}z+x_{\varepsilon})dz\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Gamma(z)\omega_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)dz\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}z+x_{\varepsilon})dz+C,\end{split}

from which we deduce that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\\
&\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{|z|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}z+x_{\varepsilon})\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}dz+C\\
&\ \ \leq\left(\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy+C.\end{split}

(4.10)

Therefore by Lemma
4.4
, (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we conclude that for any
x_{\varepsilon}\in supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there holds

\begin{split}&\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy+\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\\
&\ \ \geq Y((r_{*},0))\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-C.\end{split}

(4.11)

Dividing both sides of the above inequality by
\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\begin{split}Y(x_{\varepsilon})\geq&\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy-\alpha|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\\
\geq&Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-d|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right\}-\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d\gamma}{2\pi}-\frac{C}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

(4.12)

Note that

\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx\geq d(\bar{P}_{\varepsilon})^{2}.

Taking
x_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon}
and letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0^{+}
, we deduce from
(
4.12
)
that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}Y(P_{\varepsilon})\geq Y((r_{*},0))-\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d\gamma/(2\pi).

(4.13)

Hence we get the desired result by letting
\gamma\to 0
and using Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Next, we estimate the moment of inertia
\mathcal{I}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
.

Lemma 4.6
.

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}(\omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx=\frac{d}{2}r_{*}^{2}.

(4.14)

As a consequence, for any
\eta>0
, there holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega\cap\{x\mid|x|\geq r_{*}+\eta\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx=0.

(4.15)

Proof.

Taking
x_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon}
in
(
4.12
)
, we know that for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

0\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left[\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-d(\bar{P}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left[\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-d(\bar{P}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d^{2}\gamma}{\alpha\pi}.

Combining this with Lemma
4.5
we get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}d(\bar{P}_{\varepsilon})^{2}=dr_{*}^{2}.

(
4.15
) follows immediately from
(
4.14
)
and Lemma
4.5
.
∎

The above lemma shows that the moment of inertia of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
will tend to
dr_{*}^{2}/2
and on the set
\Omega\cap\{x\mid|x|\geq r_{*}+\eta\}
for any
\eta>0
, the circulation of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
tends to 0 as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Based on this estimate, we can then get limits of
\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}.

Lemma 4.7
.

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}=r_{*}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}\geq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\bar{P}_{\varepsilon}=r_{*}
. Note that

\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}<+\infty.

Taking
x_{\varepsilon}=Q_{\varepsilon}
in (
4.12
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}}{2\pi}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{Q_{\varepsilon}}(y-Q_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\\
&\ \ \geq Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}(\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon})^{2}+\frac{\alpha r_{*}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d\gamma}{2\pi}\\
&\ \ \geq\frac{d\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}^{-1}}{2\pi}-\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d\gamma}{2\pi}.\end{split}

Hence

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{Q_{\varepsilon}}(y-Q_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\geq\frac{d\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}^{-1}}{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}}-d\gamma.

So
\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{Q_{\varepsilon}}(y-Q_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\geq C_{0}>0
if we choose
\gamma
such that
0<\gamma<\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}^{-1}}{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}}.

We claim that
\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}\leq r_{*}.
If not, there exists a sequence
\{\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon,n}\}
(still denoted by
\{\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}\}
for simplicity) such that
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}>r_{*}
. Note that
T_{Q_{\varepsilon}}
is a positive-definite matrix whose eigenvalues have positive upper and lower bounds uniformly about
\varepsilon.
Then we find
\eta_{0}>0
, such that
\{|T_{Q_{\varepsilon}}(y-Q_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}\subset\{|y|\geq r_{*}+\eta_{0}\}
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Thus
\int\limits_{\{|y|\geq r_{*}+\eta_{0}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\geq C_{0},
which contradicts to (
4.15
). To conclude, we get
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\bar{Q}_{\varepsilon}=r_{*}
.
∎

From Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.7
, we know that
supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})
locates in a narrow annular domain whose centerline is
\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)
. That is, for any
x\in supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}|x|=r_{*}.

The following lemma shows that the diameter of the support set of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
is the order of
\varepsilon^{\gamma}
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Lemma 4.8
.

\mathrm{[Diameter~of}

\text{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
\mathrm{]}

For any number
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there holds for
\varepsilon
small enough

diam\big({supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\big)\leq 2\varepsilon^{\gamma}.

Proof.

Since
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx=d,
it suffices to prove that

\int\limits_{B_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x_{\varepsilon})}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx>d/2,\ \ \forall\,x_{\varepsilon}\in{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon}).

(4.16)

For any
x_{\varepsilon}\in{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
, by
(
4.12
)
we get

\begin{split}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\geq&2\pi\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}\bigg[Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx+d|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right\}\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}^{-1}d\gamma}{2\pi}-\frac{C}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\bigg].\end{split}

(4.17)

From Lemmas
4.5
and
4.7
, we know that
|x_{\varepsilon}|\to r_{*}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Taking this into (
4.17
), we obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\geq d-\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}d\gamma}{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}}.

(4.18)

From the definition of
T_{x_{\varepsilon}}
, one computes directly that

\{y\mid T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}\subset B_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x_{\varepsilon}).

Thus we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{B_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x_{\varepsilon})}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\geq d-\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}d\gamma}{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}},

which implies (
4.16
) for all small
\gamma
such that
d-\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}d\gamma}{\sqrt{\det K_{H}((R^{*},0))}}>\frac{d}{2}
. Thus we conclude that
diam\big({supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\big)\leq 2\varepsilon^{\gamma}
for small
\gamma
, from which we deduce that

diam\big({supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})\big)\leq\frac{C}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}

(4.19)

provided
\varepsilon
is small enough.

Now we go back to
(
4.9
)
and improve estimates of
\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy
. From (
4.19
), we get for any
\gamma\in(0,1)

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}G_{K_{H}}(x_{\varepsilon},y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\\
&\ \ \leq\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K_{H}(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x_{\varepsilon}}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x_{\varepsilon}-y\right)\right)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dy+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy+C.\end{split}improve

Repeating the proof of (
4.12
), we can improve
(
4.12
)
as follows

\begin{split}&\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy+\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}\gamma}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|>\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\\
&\ \ \geq Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx+d|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right\}-\frac{C}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}},\end{split}

which implies that

\begin{split}&(1-\gamma)\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\\
&\ \ \geq 2\pi\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x_{\varepsilon})}\left[Y((r_{*},0))+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx+d|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right\}-\frac{C}{\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right]-d\gamma.\end{split}

(4.20)

Taking the limit inferior to (
4.20
), we get

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{|T_{x_{\varepsilon}}(y-x_{\varepsilon})|\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dy\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left[2\pi\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}(Y((r_{*},0))+\alpha r_{*}^{2})-d\gamma\right]=d,

which implies (
4.16
) for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

A direct consequence of Lemma
4.8
is the following estimates of
diam(supp(\omega_{\varepsilon}))
.

Corollary 4.9
.

There holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln diam\big(supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})\big)}{\ln{\varepsilon}}=1.

(4.21)

Proof.

Since
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx=d
and
0\leq\omega_{\varepsilon}\leq 1/{\varepsilon^{2}}
, from the isoperimetric inequality we know that there exists
r_{1}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

diam(supp(\omega_{\varepsilon}))\geq r_{1}\varepsilon.

Thus we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln diam({supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon}))}{\ln\varepsilon}\leq 1.

(4.22)

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.8
, we obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln diam(supp(\omega_{\varepsilon}))}{\ln\varepsilon}\geq\gamma\ \ \ \ \forall\,\gamma\in(0,1).

(4.23)

Combining (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), we get the result.
∎

To sum up, we have obtained the asymptotic behavior of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
as follows.

Proposition 4.10
.

\mathrm{[Diameter~and~location~of}

\text{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
\mathrm{]}

For any
\gamma\in(0,1),
there holds

diam[\text{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon})]\leq 2\varepsilon^{\gamma}

provided
\varepsilon
is small enough. Moreover,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}dist\left(\text{supp}(\omega_{\varepsilon}),\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)\right)=0,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln diam\big(supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})\big)}{\ln{\varepsilon}}=1.

Furthermore, we can improve Proposition
4.10
by showing that the diameter of
supp(\omega_{\varepsilon})
is of the order of
\varepsilon
, rather than
\varepsilon^{\gamma}
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
First, we get the following optimal asymptotic expansions of the energy
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
and Lagrange multiplier
\mu^{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

As
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d^{2}}{4\pi\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}}-\frac{d\alpha r_{*}^{2}}{2}\right)\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+O(1),

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mu^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d}{2\pi\sqrt{\det K_{H}((r_{*},0))}}-\frac{\alpha r_{*}^{2}}{2}\right)\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+O(1).

(4.25)

Proof.

We first prove
(
4.24
)
. According to Lemma
4.8
, there holds

supp\,(\omega_{\varepsilon})\subseteq B_{2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(P_{\varepsilon})

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Hence from Proposition
4.2
we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx\\
&\ \ \leq\iint\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(x)}^{-1}+\sqrt{\det K_{H}(y)}^{-1}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{T_{x}+T_{y}}{2}\left(x-y\right)\right)\omega_{\varepsilon}(x)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dxdy+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(P_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2\pi}\iint\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|T_{P_{\varepsilon}}(x-y)|}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\omega_{\varepsilon}(x)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dxdy+C\\
&\ \ \leq\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(P_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{2\pi}\iint\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}\ln\frac{1}{|T_{P_{\varepsilon}}(x-y)|}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x)\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)dxdy+C\\
&\ \ =\frac{\sqrt{\det K_{H}(P_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{2\pi}\iint\ln\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{P_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}x^{\prime})\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{P_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}y^{\prime})\det K_{H}(P_{\varepsilon})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}+C.\end{split}

By the Riesz’s rearrangement inequality,

\begin{split}\iint\ln\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{P_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}x^{\prime})\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{P_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}y^{\prime})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\leq&\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\{\int\omega_{\varepsilon}(T_{P_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right\}^{2}+C\\
=&d^{2}\det K_{H}(P_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+C.\end{split}

Thus we get

\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx\leq\frac{d^{2}\sqrt{\det K_{H}(P_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+C,

from which we conclude that

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{G}_{K_{H}}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx-\frac{\alpha}{2}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\omega_{\varepsilon}dx\\
\leq&\left(\frac{d^{2}\sqrt{\det K_{H}(P_{\varepsilon})}^{-1}}{4\pi}-\frac{d\alpha|P_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}\right)\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+C\\

Lemma 4.13

There holds

(6.3)

w^{n}(x,0)=w^{n}_{0}(x).\end{cases}

By Lemma
6.2
, it is clear that
w^{n}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
for any
t>0
, and as
n\rightarrow+\infty

\|w^{n}(\cdot,t_{n})-v^{n}_{t_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(D)}=\|w^{n}_{0}-v^{n}_{0}\|_{L^{p}(D)}\rightarrow 0.

(6.7)

Combining (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(w^{n}(\cdot,t_{n}))=\sup_{\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}.

Then by Lemma
6.1
it follows that there exists
w^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\|w^{n}(\cdot,t_{n})-w^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
, which gives

dist_{p}(w^{n}(\cdot,t_{n}),\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow 0.

(6.8)

Now (
6.5
), (
6.7
) and (
6.8
) together lead to a contradiction. Thus Theorem
1.10
is proved.

Extrinsic Derivative Formula for Distribution Dependent SDEs The author is supported by NNSFC (12301180) and Research Centre for Nonlinear Analysis at Hong Kong PolyU.
Panpan Ren

Abstract
A Bismut type formula is established for the extrinsic derivative of distribution dependent SDEs (DDSDEs). The main result is illustrated by non-degenerate DDSDEs with space-time singular drift, as well as degenerate DDSDEs with weakly monotone coefficients.



AMS subject Classification: 60B05, 60B10.

Keywords: Extrinsic formula, Bismut formula, Distribution dependent SDEs, Stochastic Hamiltonian system.

1
Introduction

Let
k\in[0,\infty)
and
\mathscr{P}_{k}
be the set of all probability measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
having finite
k^{th}
-moment; i.e.

\mu(|\cdot|^{k}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{k}\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)<\infty.

In particular,
\mathscr{P}_{0}=\mathscr{P}
is the set of all probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It is well known that
\mathscr{P}_{k}
is a complete metric space under the weighted total variance distance

\|\mu-\nu\|_{k,var}:=\sup_{|f|\leq 1+|\cdot|^{k}}|\mu(f)-\nu(f)|,

see for instance
[
11
]
.

Consider the following distribution dependent SDE (DDSDE) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(1.1)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b_{t}(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\ \ t\in[0,T],

where
T>0
is a fixed time,
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
is the distribution of
X_{t}
,
W_{t}
is the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on a probability base
(\Omega,\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
, and

b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ \sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}

are measurable. We call (
1.1
) well-posed for distributions in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, if for any
\mathscr{F}_{0}
-measurable initial value
X_{0}
with
\mathbb{E}|X_{0}|^{k}<\infty
(resp. any initial distribution
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}
),
the SDE has a unique strong (resp. weak) solution with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{\cdot}}\in C([0,T];\mathscr{P}_{k}).

In this case, to emphasize the initial distribution we denote the solution by
X_{t}^{\mu}
if
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}}=\mu,

and denote
P_{t}^{*}\mu=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{\mu}}.

For any
f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let

P_{t}f(\mu)=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t}^{\mu})],\ \ \mu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}.

To characterize the regularity of non-linear Fokker-Planck equations, following type Bismut formula have been established in
[
8
,
2
,
4
,
12
]
for the intrinsic/Lions derivative of
\mu\mapsto P_{t}f(\mu)
:

DP_{t}f(\mu)=\mathbb{E}\big[f(X_{t}^{\mu})M_{t}],\ \ f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
M_{t}
is a martingale explicitly given by the solution of (
1.1
). The crucial point of this type formula is that the derivative of
P_{t}f
is represented by
f
rather than the derivative of
f
, so that it implies regularity estimates for the distribution of solutions. In the study of particle systems, the intrinsic derivative describes the motion while the extrinsic derivative describes the birth-death of particles, see for instance
[
5
,
9
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we aim to establish the Bismut type derivative formula the same type formula for the extrinsic derivative of
P_{t}f
, which is not yet available so far.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f
be a continuous function on
\mathscr{P}_{k}
.

(1)

We call
f
extrinsically differentiable, if for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}
, the convex extrinsic derivative

\tilde{D}^{E}_{x}f(\mu):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{f((1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon\delta_{x})-f(\mu)}{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

exists, where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at point
x
.

(2)

We denote
f\in C^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}_{k})
if
f
is extrinsically differentiable, and

(x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{k}\mapsto\tilde{D}^{E}f(\mu)(x):=\tilde{D}^{E}_{x}f(\mu)

is continuous.

(3)

We write
f\in C_{K}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}_{k})
, if
f\in C^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}_{k})
and for any compact set

\mathscr{K}\subset\mathscr{P}_{k}
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\sup_{\mu\in\mathscr{K}}|\tilde{D}^{E}f(\mu)(x)|\leq c(1+|x|^{k}),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

As shown in
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
, when
f\in C_{K}^{E,1}
the function
x\mapsto\tilde{D}^{E}_{x}f(\mu)

becomes the linear functional derivative, i.e. for any
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}
,

(1.2)

f(\mu)-f(\nu)=\int_{0}^{1}(\mu-\nu)\big(\tilde{D}^{E}f(r\mu+(1-r)\nu)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}r.

To calculate
\tilde{D}^{E}P_{t}f
, we will use the semigroup of the decoupled SDE associated with (
1.1
):

(1.3)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\mu,x}=b_{t}(X_{t}^{\mu,x},P_{t}^{*}\mu)\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t}^{\mu,x})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\ \ X_{0}^{\mu,x}=x,t\in[0,T],

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}.
Let
P_{t}^{\mu}
be the associated semigroup, i.e.

P_{t}^{\mu}f(x):=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t}^{\mu,x})],\ \ t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In general, for a probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let

P_{t}^{\mu}f(\nu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{t}^{\mu}f(x)\nu(\text{\rm{d}}x),\ \ (P_{t}^{\mu})^{*}\nu:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{\mu,x}}\nu(\text{\rm{d}}x).

We make the following assumption.

(H)

Let
k\in[0,\infty)
. The following two conditions hold.

(H_{1})

For any
\gamma_{\cdot}\in C([0,T];\mathscr{P}_{k})
, the SDE

(1.4)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot},x}=b_{t}(X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot},x},\gamma_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot},x})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\ \ X_{0}^{\gamma_{\cdot},x}=x,t\in[0,T],

is well-posed, and there exist constants
c>0
and
p\geq k
independent of
\gamma_{\cdot}
such that

(1.5)

\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot},x}|^{p}\big]\leq c\big(1+|x|^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}(|\cdot|^{k})^{\frac{p}{k}}\text{\rm{d}}s\big),\ \ t\in[0,T],\\
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

\gamma_{t}(|\cdot|^{k})^{\frac{p}{k}}:=1
when
k=0.

(H_{2})

The drift
b_{t}(x,\mu)
is formulated as

b_{t}(x,\mu)=b_{t}^{(0)}(x)+\sigma_{t}(x)b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\mu),

where

b^{(0)}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ b^{(1)}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

are measurable,
b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\cdot)\in C^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}_{k}),
there exists
K\in L^{2}([0,T];(0,\infty))
such that

(1.6)

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\mu)(y)-c|\leq K_{t}(1+|y|^{k}),\ \ t\in[0,T],\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \mu\in\mathscr{P}_{k},

and there exists an increasing function
\alpha:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
with
\alpha(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
such that

(1.7)

\begin{split}&|\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\mu)(y)-\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\nu)(y)|\\
&\leq K_{t}\alpha(\|\mu-\nu\|_{k,var})\big(1+|y|^{k}+\mu(|\cdot|^{k})+\nu(|\cdot|^{k})\big),\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}.\end{split}

The condition
(H_{1})
can be checked easily by existing results concerning the well-posedness and moment estimates. So, in applications the key point is to verify
(H_{2})
for the distribution dependence of
b
. A simple example satisfying
(H_{2})
is

b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\mu)=F_{t}(x,\mu(h)),

where for some
n\geq 1
,

F:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

are measurable such that for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\nabla F_{t}(x,\cdot)\|\lor\|\nabla^{2}F_{t}(x,\cdot)\|\leq C,\ \ \ |h(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{k})

hold for some constant
C>1
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}|\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\mu)(y)-c|\leq\big|\nabla F_{t}(x,\cdot)(\mu(h))h(y)\big|\leq C(1+|y|^{k}),

\displaystyle|\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\mu)(y)-\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\nu)(y)|

\displaystyle=\big|[\nabla F_{t}(x,\cdot)(\mu(h))]\big(h(y)-\mu(h)\big)-[\nabla F_{t}(x,\cdot)(\nu(h))]\big(h(y)-\nu(h)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\nabla^{2}F_{t}(x,\cdot)\big\|_{\infty}\big|\big(\mu(h)-\nu(h)\big)\big|\cdot\big|\big(h(y)-\mu(h)\big)\big|+\big|[\nabla F_{t}(x,\cdot)(\nu(h))](\mu(h)-\nu(h))\big|

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\big(2+\mu(|\cdot|^{k})+|y|^{k}\big)\|\mu-\nu\|_{k,var}+C\|\mu-\nu\|_{k,var}.

So,
(H_{2})
holds.

Let
\mathscr{D}_{k}
be the class of measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

|f|\leq c(1+|\cdot|^{k})
for some constant
c>0
.
The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(H)
. Then the DDSDE
(
1.1
)
is well-posed for distributions in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, and the following assertions hold.

(1)

For any
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}
, the equation

(1.8)

\begin{split}&\eta_{t}^{\mu,\nu}=P_{t}^{\mu}\big(\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}(X_{t}^{\mu},P_{t}^{*}\mu)\big)(\nu)-P_{t}\big(\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}(X_{t}^{\mu},P_{t}^{*}\mu)\big)(\mu)\\
&\quad\qquad+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}(z,P_{t}^{*}\mu)(X_{t}^{\mu})\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\eta_{s}^{\mu,\nu},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\big\rangle\bigg]_{z=X_{t}^{\mu}},\ \ t\in[0,T]\end{split}

has a unique solution satisfying

(1.9)

\|\eta_{t}^{\mu,\nu}\|_{\infty}\leq cK_{t}\big(1+(\mu+\nu)(|\cdot|^{\theta})\big)\text{\rm{e}}^{c\mu(|\cdot|^{k})^{2}},\ \ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k},\ t\in[0,T]

for some constant
c>0.

(2)

For any
t\in(0,T]
and
f\in\mathscr{D}_{k},

(1.10)

\tilde{D}^{E}_{\nu}P_{t}f(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(P_{t}^{\mu}f\big)\text{\rm{d}}(\nu-\mu)+\mathbb{E}\bigg[f(X_{t}^{\mu})\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\eta_{s}^{\mu,\nu},\ \text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\big\rangle\bigg].

Consequently, there exist a constant
c>0
such that

(1.11)

\begin{split}&\sup_{|f|\leq 1+|\cdot|^{k}}|\tilde{D}^{E}_{\nu}P_{t}f(\mu)|\leq c\|\mu-\nu\|_{k,var}\\
&\quad+(1+\mu(|\cdot|^{k}))\big(1+(\mu+\nu)(|\cdot|^{k})\big)\text{\rm{e}}^{c\mu(|\cdot|^{k})^{2}}\sqrt{t},\ \ t\in[0,T].\end{split}

Remark 1.1.

For
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}
, let

\tilde{D}_{\nu}^{E}P_{t}^{*}\mu:={\mathscr{D}_{k}}\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{P_{t}^{*}((1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon\nu)-P_{t}^{*}\nu}{\varepsilon},

where the limit is defined with respect to
\mathscr{D}_{k}
: a sequence of signed measures
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\phi
with respect to
\mathscr{D}_{k}
, if
(|\phi_{n}|+|\phi|)(|\cdot|^{k})<\infty
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{n}(f)=\phi(f),\ \ f\in\mathscr{D}_{k}.

By (
1.10
),
\tilde{D}_{\nu}^{E}P_{t}^{*}\mu
exists and

\tilde{D}_{\nu}^{E}P_{t}^{*}\mu=(P_{t}^{\mu})^{*}(\nu-\mu)+Z_{t}(y)(P_{t}^{*}\mu)(\text{\rm{d}}y),

where
Z:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is measurable such that

Z_{t}(X_{t}^{\mu})=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\eta_{s}^{\mu,\nu},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\big\rangle\bigg|X_{t}^{\mu}\bigg].

In Section 2, we present some lemmas, which will be used in Section 3 to prove Theorem
1.1
.
In Sections 4, we apply the main result to non-degenerate singular DDSDEs and degenerate
models.

2
Lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
(H)
. Then the SDE
(
1.1
)
is well-posed for distributions in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, and there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}
,

(2.1)

\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{t}^{\mu}|^{p}\big|\mathscr{F}_{0}\big]\leq C\big(1+|X_{0}^{\mu}|^{p}+\mu(|\cdot|^{k})^{\frac{p}{k}}\big).

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
using the fixed point theorem.
By
(H_{1})
, for any

\gamma_{\cdot}\in\mathscr{C}^{\mu}:=\big\{\gamma_{\cdot}\in C([0,T];\mathscr{P}_{k}):\ \gamma_{0}=\mu\big\},

the SDE

(2.2)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}=b_{t}(X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}},\gamma_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\ \ t\in[0,T],X_{0}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}=X_{0}^{\mu}

has a unique solution, such that

(2.3)

\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}|^{p}|\mathscr{F}_{0}]\leq c(1+|X_{0}^{\mu}|^{p})+c\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}(|\cdot|^{k})^{\frac{p}{k}}\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ t\in[0,T].

So, the map
\Phi:\mathscr{C}^{\mu}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\mu}
defined by

\Phi_{t}(\gamma):=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}},\ \ t\in[0,T]

satisfies

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}|^{k}]\leq c^{\frac{k}{p}}(1+\mu(|\cdot|^{k}))+\bigg(c\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}(|\cdot|^{k})^{\frac{p}{k}}\text{\rm{d}}s\bigg)^{\frac{k}{p}},\ \ t\in[0,T].

Then there exists a constant
N_{0}\geq 1
, such that for any
N\geq N_{0}
, the set

\mathscr{C}^{\mu}_{N}:=\Big\{\gamma_{\cdot}\in C([0,T];\mathscr{P}_{k}):\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Nt}\gamma_{t}(|\cdot|^{k})\leq N(1+\mu(|\cdot|^{k})\Big\}

is invariant under the map
\Phi
.
Next,
(H_{2})
implies that
b_{t}(x,\cdot)\in C_{K}^{E,1}(\mathscr{P}_{k})
. By (
1.2
) we obtain

(2.5)

\begin{split}&\big|b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\mu)-b_{t}^{(1)}(x,\nu)\big|=\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}r}b_{t}\big(x,r\mu+(1-r)\nu\big)\Big)\text{\rm{d}}r\bigg|\\
&=\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\text{\rm{d}}r\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{D}^{E}b_{t}^{(1)}\big(x,r\mu+(1-r)\nu\big)(z)(\mu-\nu)(\text{\rm{d}}z)\bigg|\\
&\leq K_{t}\|\mu-\nu\|_{k,var},\ \ t\in[0,T],\ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}.\end{split}

Then for any
\gamma_{\cdot},\tilde{\gamma}_{\cdot}\in\mathscr{C}_{N}^{\mu}
, we reformulate the SDE (
2.2
) as

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}=b_{t}(X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}},\tilde{\gamma}_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}})\text{\rm{d}}\tilde{W}_{t},\ \ t\in[0,T],X_{0}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}=X_{0}^{\mu},

where by Girsanov’s theorem,

\tilde{W}_{t}:=W_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\eta_{s}\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ t\in[0,T]

is a Brownian motion under the weighted probability measure
R_{T}\text{\rm{d}}\mathbb{P}
, for
R_{T}
defined as

\displaystyle\eta_{s}:=\big[b_{s}(X_{s}^{\gamma_{\cdot}},\tilde{\gamma}_{s})-b_{s}(X_{s}^{\gamma_{\cdot}},\gamma_{s})\big],

\displaystyle R_{t}:=\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{0}^{t}\langle\eta_{s},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\eta_{s}|^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s},\ \ s,t\in[0,T].

By the weak uniqueness, and Hölder’s inequality, we find a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t}(\gamma)-\Phi_{t}(\tilde{\gamma})\|_{k,var}=\sup_{|f|\leq 1+|\cdot|^{k}}\big|\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}})(R_{t}-1)]\big|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[(1+|X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}|^{k})|R_{t}-1|]\leq c_{1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\mathbb{E}\big((1+|X_{t}^{\gamma_{\cdot}}|^{p})\big|\mathscr{F}_{0}\big)\Big)^{\frac{k}{p}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big(|R_{t}-1|^{\frac{p}{p-k}}\big|\mathscr{F}_{0}\big)\Big)^{\frac{p-k}{p}}\Big].

By (
2.5
) and
\gamma_{\cdot},\tilde{\gamma}_{\cdot}\in\mathscr{C}_{N}^{\mu}
, and noting that

|\text{\rm{e}}^{r}-1|\leq(\text{\rm{e}}^{r}+1)|r|,\ \ r\in\mathbb{R},

we find a constant
c_{2}(N)>0
such that

\Big(\mathbb{E}\big(|R_{t}-1|^{\frac{p}{p-k}}\big|\mathscr{F}_{0}\big)\Big)^{\frac{p-k}{p}}\leq c_{2}(N)\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}K_{s}^{2}\|\gamma_{s}-\tilde{\gamma}_{s}\|_{k,var}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining this with (
2.3
) we arrive at

\|\Phi_{t}(\gamma)-\Phi_{t}(\tilde{\gamma})\|_{k,var}\leq c_{3}(N)\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}K_{s}^{2}\|\gamma_{s}-\tilde{\gamma}_{s}\|_{k,var}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, when
\lambda>0
is large enough the map

\Phi
is contractive on
\mathscr{C}_{\mu}^{N}
under the complete metric

\rho_{\lambda}(\gamma_{\cdot},\tilde{\gamma}_{\cdot}):=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda t}\|\gamma_{t}-\tilde{\gamma}_{t}\|_{k,var},

and hence the SDE (
1.1
) is well-posed for distributions in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
.

Finally, by (
2.3
) for the fixed point
\gamma_{t}:=P_{t}^{*}\mu
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\mu}|^{p}|\mathscr{F}_{0}]\leq c(1+|X_{0}^{\mu}|^{p})+c\int_{0}^{t}\big(\mathbb{E}[|X_{s}^{\mu}|^{k}]\big)^{\frac{p}{k}}\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ t\in[0,T].

This implies (
1.5
) for some constant
C>0
.

∎

Noting that
X_{t}^{\mu}
solves (
1.3
)
for initial value
X_{0}^{\mu}
replacing
x
, we have

(2.6)

P_{t}^{*}\mu:=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{\mu}}=(P_{t}^{\mu})^{*}\mu,

and (
1.5
) implies

(2.7)

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{\mu}|^{p}\Big|\mathscr{F}_{0}\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{\mu,x}|^{p}\Big|\mathscr{F}_{0}\bigg]_{x=X_{0}^{\mu}}\leq c_{p}(1+|X_{0}^{\mu}|^{p}),\ \ \ \ p\in[1,\infty),\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{k},

(2.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\cdot|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}(P_{t}^{\mu})^{*}\nu\leq c_{p}(1+\nu(|\cdot|^{p})),\ \ p\in[1,k],\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
(H)
. Simply denote
\pi_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon\nu,\varepsilon\in(0,1).
Then there exists a constant
c(\mu,\nu)>0
increasing in
(\mu+\nu)(|\cdot|^{k})
such that

\|(P_{t}^{\pi_{\varepsilon}})^{*}\gamma-(P_{t}^{\mu})^{*}\gamma\|_{k,var}\leq c(\mu,\nu)(1+\gamma(|\cdot|^{k}))\varepsilon,\ \ \varepsilon\in(0,1),\gamma\in\mathscr{P}_{k}.

Proof.

Let
X_{0}
be
\mathscr{F}_{0}
-measurable with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}}=\gamma
. Consider the SDE

(2.9)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=\big\{b_{t}^{(0)}(X_{t})+\sigma_{t}(X_{t})b_{t}^{(1)}(X_{t},P_{t}^{*}\mu)\big\}\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\ \ t\in[0,T].

Then
(P_{t}^{\mu})^{*}\gamma=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}.
Let

\xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}:=b_{s}^{(1)}(X_{s},P_{s}^{*}\pi_{\varepsilon})-b_{s}^{(1)}(X_{s},P_{s}^{*}\mu),\ \ \ s\in[0,T].

By combining (
2.5
) with (
2.6
) and (
2.8
), we find a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(2.10)

|\xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\leq K_{s}\|P_{s}^{*}\pi_{\varepsilon}-P_{s}^{*}\mu\|_{k,var}\leq c_{1}K_{s}(\mu+\nu)(|\cdot|^{k}).

Then by Girsanov’s theorem,

(2.11)

W_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=W_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ t\in[0,T]

is a Brownian motion under the weighted probability
R_{T}^{\varepsilon}\text{\rm{d}}\mathbb{P}
, where

(2.12)

R_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{0}^{t}\langle\xi_{s}^{\varepsilon},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s},~~t\in[0,T].

Reformulating the SDE (
2.9
) as

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=\big\{b_{t}^{(0)}(X_{t})+\sigma_{t}(X_{t})b_{t}^{(1)}(X_{t},P_{t}^{*}\pi_{\varepsilon})\big\}\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}^{\varepsilon},\ \ t\in[0,T],

by the weak uniqueness and (
2.7
), we find some constant
c_{2}>0
such that

(2.13)

\begin{split}&\|(P_{t}^{\pi_{\varepsilon}})^{*}\gamma-(P_{t}^{\mu})^{*}\gamma\|_{k,var}=\sup_{|f|\leq 1+|\cdot|^{k}}\big|\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t})(R_{t}^{\varepsilon}-1)]\big|\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}[(1+|X_{t}|^{k})|R_{t}^{\varepsilon}-1|]\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\sqrt{\mathbb{E}((1+|X_{t}|^{k})^{2}|\mathscr{F}_{0})\mathbb{E}(|R_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-1|\mathscr{F}_{0})}\Big]\\
&\leq c_{2}(1+\mathbb{E}|X_{0}|^{k})\Big\|\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(|R_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-1|\mathscr{F}_{0})}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\\
&=c_{2}(1+\gamma(|\cdot|^{k}))\Big\|\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(|R_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-1|\mathscr{F}_{0})}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}.\end{split}

(A)

Let

(1)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{|x-y|\leq\varepsilon,t\in[0,T]}\|a_{t}(x)-a_{t}(y)\|=0.

(3)

for any
\mu\in C([0,T];\mathscr{P}_{k})

is locally bounded in

. Moreover, there exist constants

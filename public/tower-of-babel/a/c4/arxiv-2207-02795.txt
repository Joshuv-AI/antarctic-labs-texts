Positive Semidefinite Initial Cost Product Throttling
Esther Conrad

Abstract
Product throttling answers the question of minimizing the product of the resources needed to accomplish a task, and the time in which it takes to accomplish the task. In product throttling for positive semidefinite zero forcing, task that we wish to accomplish is positive semidefinite zero forcing. Positive semidefinite zero forcing is a game played on a graph G G that starts with a coloring of the vertices as white and blue. At each step any vertex colored blue with a unique white neighbor in a component of the graph formed by deleting the blue vertices from G G forces the color of the white neighbor to become blue. We give various results and bounds on the initial cost product throttling number, including a lower bound of 1 + rad ⁡ ( G ) 1+\operatorname{rad}(G) and the initial cost product throttling number of a cycle. We also include a table with results on the initial cost and no initial cost product throttling number for various graph families.



Keywords
positive semidefinite, zero forcing, throttling, propagation time

1
Introduction and Preliminaries

Positive semidefinite zero forcing is a process on a graph where vertices have two possible colors - blue and white. The goal is to find an initial set of blue vertices so that at the end of the process, which is to repeat a PSD color change rule, all the vertices in the graph are blue. In 2013, Butler and Young began the study of
(sum) throttling
in zero forcing
[
6
]
, which is the problem of minimizing the sum of the number of blue vertices in a zero forcing set and the time it takes the set to color the entire graph blue. In 2019, Carlson et al. extended (sum) throttling to the PSD color change rule
[
8
]
.

In
[
4
]
, Bonato et al. extended throttling to initial cost product throttling for Cops and Robbers, and in
[
2
]
Anderson et al. introduced the the terminology
initial cost product throttling
and
no initial cost product throttling
and extended these definitions to various other graph parameters including positive semidefinite zero forcing. Initial cost and no initial cost product throttling measure the relationship between the cost of initial blue vertices and the time it takes to color the entire graph via minimizing their product. The idea behind initial cost product throttling is to include the cost of placing the blue vertices as an extra time step. For example, the cost of placing a cop at an intersection. So, initial cost product throttling is the problem of minimizing the product of the size of the initial set of blue vertices and one plus the propagation time of this initial set. No initial cost product throttling on the other hand, does not take into account the cost of placement. So, the no initial cost product throttling problem is to minimize the product of the initial set and the propagation time of the initial set, but the solution that all vertices be blue initially is excluded.

This paper extends the results for PSD product throttling in
[
2
]
, focusing primarily on initial cost product throttling.
In this section, we define PSD zero forcing, PSD propagation time, and PSD product and sum throttling, and present various basic graph definitions. In Section
2
we present some general bounds for initial cost and no initial cost PSD product throttling including
\operatorname{rad}(G)+1
as a lower bound for the initial cost PSD product throttling for any graph
G
. In Section
3
, we present some bounds for
G\Box H
and edge operations. In Section
4
, we establish the value of the product throttling number for a cycle and present a table with results for various families of graphs.

\displaystyle S^{(0)}=S^{[0]}=S.

\displaystyle\text{For }i\geq 1,

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636ptS^{(i)}=\left\{w\in V(G)\setminus S^{[i-1]}:w\text{ can be forced by some $v$ given $S^{[i-1]}$}\right\}.

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636ptS^{[i]}=S^{[i-1]}\cup S^{(i)}.

Let

Positive semidefinite throttling was introduced in

Cops and Robbers
is a two player game on a graph. The first player places and moves a collection of cops and the second player places and moves a single robber. The players exchange turns when moving the cops and robber. The goal of the first player is for a cop to capture the robber by occupying the same vertex and the goal of the second player is for the robber to evade capture. At each round, each cop is allowed to move to an adjacent vertex or stay in the same place, followed by the robber who is allowed to move to an adjacent vertex or stay in the same place. The
cop number
of the a graph
G
is the minimum number of cops needed to catch a robber and is denoted
c(G)
. The
capture time
of
G
of an initial set
S
is the minimum number of rounds it takes cops placed on the vertices in
S
to capture a robber assuming the players are using optimal strategies and is denoted
{\mathrm{capt}}(G;S)
. If the set
S
cannot capture the robber then
{\mathrm{capt}}(G;S)=\infty

[
5
]
. The
k
-capture time,

{\mathrm{capt}}_{k}(G)=\min\{{\mathrm{capt}}(G;S):|S|=k\}
. Let
G
be a graph. The initial cost product throttling for cops and robbers of an initial set
S
is
\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}\left(G;S\right)=|S|(1+{\mathrm{capt}}(G;S))
. The initial cost product throttling number of
G
for cops and robbers is
\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}\left(G\right)=\min\{k(1+{\mathrm{capt}}_{k}(G)):k\geq c(G)\}
.

The next two results are used for examples throughout this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
2
]
Let

\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}\left(G;S\right)=\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}\left(G;S\right)
and
\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}\left(G;S\right)=\operatorname{th}_{c}^{*}\left(G;S\right)

\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}\left(G\right)=\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}\left(G\right)
and
\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}\left(G\right)=\operatorname{th}_{c}^{*}\left(G\right)

[

Let

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
G
be a connected graph,
S^{*},S^{\times}\subseteq V(G)
such that
\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}(G)<|V(G)|
,
\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}(G)=\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)
, and
\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}(G)=\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)
. Then

1.

\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)=\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{*}|)
and
\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)=\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{\times}|)
,

2.

|S^{*}|\geq|S^{\times}|
,

3.

\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)\leq\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)
,

4.

If
|S^{*}|=|S^{\times}|,
then
\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}(G)=\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)
and
\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}(G)=\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)
.

Proof.

We first prove (1). Since

\displaystyle\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}(G)\leq\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}(G,|S^{\times}|)=|S^{\times}|(1+\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{\times}|))\leq|S^{\times}|(1+\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right))=\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}(G),

it must be that
\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{\times}|))=\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)
. Similarly,
\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{*}|))=\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)
.

We next prove (2). Observe the following:

\displaystyle|S^{\times}|+|S^{\times}|\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=|S^{*}|+|S^{*}|\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=|S^{*}|+\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|S^{*}|+\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)

\displaystyle=|S^{*}|+|S^{\times}|\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right).

Thus,
|S^{\times}|\leq|S^{*}|
.

We next prove (3). Since
|S^{*}|\geq|S^{\times}|
, and by (1),

\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{*}\right)=\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{*}|)\leq\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G|S^{\times}|)=\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right).

To prove (4), we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}(G)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)

\displaystyle=|S^{\times}|(1+\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right))

\displaystyle=|S^{\times}|(1+\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{\times}|))

\displaystyle=|S^{*}|(1+\operatorname{pt_{+}}(G,|S^{*}|))

\displaystyle=|S^{*}|(1+\operatorname{pt_{+}}\left(G;S^{*}\right))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{th_{+}^{\times}}\left(G;S^{*}\right).

Similarly
\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}(G)=\operatorname{th_{+}^{*}}\left(G;S^{\times}\right)
.
∎

On the invariants of L L -functions of degree 2 2 , I: twisted degree and internal shift
J.Kaczorowski and A.Perelli

Abstract




Abstract.
This is the first part of a series of papers where the behaviour of the invariants under twist by Dirichlet characters is studied for
L
-functions of degree 2. Here we show, under suitable conditions, that degree and internal shift remain unchanged under twist. The ultimate goal of the series is to prove a general version of Weil converse theorem with minimal assumptions on the shape of the functional equation of the twists.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):
11M41

Keywords:
Twists by Dirichlet characters; Selberg class; invariants of
L
-functions; converse theorems.

1.
Introduction

Assume that

F(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)n^{-s}

(1.1)

belongs to the Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}
and let
\chi(\bmod\,q)
be a Dirichlet character. In many particular cases, the twist

F^{\chi}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}

belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}
as well. This is true for automorphic
L
-functions modulo some natural restrictions. However, there are no general theorems on
F^{\chi}
related to classes of
L
-functions like
{\mathcal{S}}
or the extended Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
. Several natural questions can be asked on this topic. A general one is the following: Given two
L
-functions
F
and
F^{\chi}
belonging
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
, what can be said about their invariants like degree, conductor, internal shift, or root number? Can the invariants of the twisted
L
-function be computed in terms of the invariants of the original
L
-function and the data of the character
\chi
? Based on the known examples, one can risk some conjectures. For instance, one can predict that twisting by a Dirichlet character cannot change the degree. This paper confirms this expectation for a large class of Dirichlet characters and degree two
L
-functions.

Questions like these are attractive, and are essential for proving converse theorems. For instance, the well-known converse theorem of Weil requires that the twists satisfy very precisely described functional equations, closely related to the functional equation of the untwisted function; cf.
[
2
, Theorem 7.8]
. In particular, the invariants of
F^{\chi}
can attain determined values only. The present paper is intended as the first part of a series with the aim of proving a general version of the Weil converse theorem with possibly minimal assumptions on the shape of the functional equation of twists. This was the main reason for undertaking the present research.

Before formulating our main result we introduce the following notation. For a Dirichlet character
\chi
, we denote by
\chi^{*}
(mod
f_{\chi}
) the primitive character inducing
\chi
. Let
D
be a fixed positive integer. We say that
F
is
saturated
(\bmod\,D)
if for every integer
M
and every reduced residue class
a
(mod
D
) there exists an integer
\nu
such that
\nu\equiv a
(mod
D
),
(\nu,M)=1
and
a(\nu)\neq 0
. Moreover, we say that
F

splits polynomially
at a prime
p
if

F(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{\partial_{p}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{j}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\sum_{p\nmid n}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}.

for some
\partial_{p}\geq 1
and
\alpha_{j}(p)\in\mathbb{C}
. We further recall that the extended Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
consists of the non identically vanishing Dirichlet series
F(s)
as in (
1.1
), absolutely convergent for
\sigma>1
and satisfying a functional equation of type

\gamma(s)F(s)=\omega\overline{\gamma}(1-s)\overline{F}(1-s),

where
|\omega|=1
and the
\gamma
-factor

\gamma_{F}(s)=Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})

has
Q>0
,
\lambda_{j}>0
and
\Re(\mu_{j})\geq 0
for
j=1,\ldots,r
. The Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}
is the subclass of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
of the functions with a general Euler product and satisfying the Ramanujan conjecture. Degree, conductor and internal shift of
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
are defined respectively as

d_{F}:=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j},\qquad q_{F}:=(2\pi)^{d_{F}}Q^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{2\lambda_{j}},\qquad\theta_{F}:=\frac{2}{d_{F}}\Im\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}

and are invariants. We refer to our survey
[
5
]
and to the papers by Selberg
[
12
]
, Conrey-Ghosh
[
1
]
and Murty
[
11
]
for further information on the Selberg classes
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
.

Theorem.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
have degree
2
, integer conductor
q_{F}
and internal shift
\theta_{F}
. Let
D
be a positive square-free integer with
D\equiv 1

(mod
q_{F}
)
such that
F
splits polynomially at every prime
p|D
. Moreover, suppose that
F
is saturated
(\bmod\,D)
and for every Dirichlet character
\chi

(mod
D
)
, the twist
F^{\chi^{*}}
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
and has degree
d_{\chi^{*}}
and internal shift
\theta_{\chi^{*}}
. Then
d_{\chi^{*}}=2
and
\theta_{\chi^{*}}=\theta_{F}
.

We remark that the assumption that
F
is saturated (mod
D
) can most probably be relaxed, at least in the case of
L
-functions in the Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}
. Using the linear independence of Dirichlet characters, one quickly shows that
F
is saturated (mod
D
) if and only if the twists
F_{|M}^{\chi}
, where

F_{|M}(s)=\sum_{(n,M)=1}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}

and
\chi
(mod
D
), are linearly independent for every positive integer
M
. Thus, if
F
and all the twists
F^{\chi^{*}}
,
\chi
(mod
D
), belong to
{\mathcal{S}}
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)
F
is saturated
(\bmod\,D)
;

(ii) the map
\chi
(mod
D
)
\mapsto F^{\chi^{*}}
is injective, i.e., for
\chi
(mod
D
) all
F^{\chi^{*}}
are different.

This follows from a general fact, namely that distinct functions from the Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}
are linearly independent over the ring of
p
-finite Dirichlet series; cf.
[
3
, Theorem 1]
,. Actually, we expect all functions in
\mathcal{S}
to be saturated
(\bmod\,D)
for every positive integer
D
.

2.
Notation.

For real numbers
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\lambda
and
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
we consider the nonlinear twist

F(s,\beta,\alpha,\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}e(-\beta n-\alpha n^{\lambda}).

(2.1)

If
d_{F}>0
and
\alpha>0
, then
F(s,0,\alpha,1/d_{F})
becomes the standard twist of
F
, is denoted by
F(s,\alpha)
and its properties are described in Theorems 1 and 2 in
[
6
]
. Indeed,
F(s,\alpha)
admits meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane, and in the half-plane
\sigma>1/2-1/(2d_{F})
, it has at most a simple pole at
s=s_{0}:=1/2+1/(2d_{F})-i\theta_{F}
with residue

{{\rm res}}_{s=s_{0}}F(s,\alpha)=c(F)\frac{\overline{a(n_{\alpha})}}{n_{\alpha}^{1-s_{0}}},

(2.2)

where
c(F)\neq 0
is a constant depending on
F
and

n_{\alpha}=q_{F}d_{F}^{-d_{F}}\alpha^{d_{F}}.

(2.3)

We use the convention that
a(\xi)=0
if
\xi\not\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
In particular,
F(s,\alpha)
is holomorphic at
s_{0}
whenever
n_{\alpha}
is not a positive integer. We also write
\overline{F}(s)=\overline{F(\overline{s})}
.

For a prime
p
we denote by
F_{p}(s)
the corresponding local factor of
F
, i.e.
F_{p}(s)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}a(p^{\ell})p^{-\ell s}
.
So
F(s)
splits polynomially at a prime
p
if
F(s)=F_{|p}(s)F_{p}(s)
and there exist an integer
\partial_{p}\geq 0
and coefficients

A_{\ell}(p)=A_{\ell}(p,F),\qquad\ell=0,\ldots,\partial_{p}\ \text{and}\ A_{\partial_{p}}(p)\neq 0

(2.4)

such that

F_{p}^{-1}(s)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\partial_{p}}A_{\ell}(p)p^{-\ell s}.

If
F
splits polynomially at all prime factors of a positive integer
D
we write

D^{*}=D^{*}(F)=\prod_{p|D}p^{\partial_{p}}

and

\prod_{p|D}F_{p}^{-1}(s)=\sum_{m|D^{*}}B_{m}(F)m^{-s},

(2.5)

where

B_{m}(F)=\prod_{p^{\ell}||m}A_{\ell}(p).

(2.6)

Clearly,
B_{m}(F)
is multiplicative in
m
.

Finally,
\tau(\chi^{*})
denotes the Gauss sum of
\chi^{*}
, and the radical
r(m)
of an integer
m=p_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{a_{k}}
is defined as

r(m)=p_{1}\cdots p_{k}.

3.
Lemmas

Lemma 1
.

Let
D
be a positive integer,
\chi_{0}
be the principal character
(mod
D
)
and let
(a,D)=1
. Then for every positive integer
n
we have

\chi_{0}(n)e(-an/D)=\frac{1}{\varphi(D)}\sum_{\chi(\bmod\,D)}c(\chi,a/D)\chi(n),

(3.1)

where

c(\chi,a/D)=\mu(D/f_{\chi})\chi^{*}(a)\overline{\chi^{*}(D/f_{\chi})}\,\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}.

(3.2)

Proof.
By elementary Fourier analysis (
3.1
) holds with

c(\chi,a/D)=\sum_{n(\bmod\,D)}\overline{\chi(n)}e(-an/D).

But with the notation in Chapter 9 of Montgomery-Vaughan
[
10
]
we have that
c(\chi,a/D)=\overline{c_{\chi}(a)}
, hence (
3.2
) follows at once from Theorem 9.12 in
[
10
]
. ∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
D
be a positive squarefree integer, suppose that
F\in\mathcal{S}^{\sharp}
splits polynomially at all primes
p|D
and let
\chi_{0}
be the principal character
(mod
D
)
. Then for every positive integer
n
we have

a(n)\chi_{0}(n)=\sum_{m|(n,D^{*})}B_{m}(F)a(n/m).

Proof.
We have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\chi_{0}(n)}{n^{s}}=\left(\prod_{p|D}F^{-1}_{p}(s)\right)F(s),

and the lemma follows at once recalling the expression (
2.5
) for the function inside brackets. ∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
D
be a positive squarefree integer and
(a,D)=1
, and suppose that
F\in\mathcal{S}^{\sharp}
splits polynomially at all primes
p|D
. Then for
\sigma>1
we have

F(s,a/D,\alpha,\lambda)=\frac{1}{\varphi(D)}\sum_{\chi(\bmod\,D)}\sum_{m|\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)^{*}}\frac{B_{m}(F)f(\chi,m,a)}{m^{s}}F^{\chi^{*}}(s,0,m^{\lambda}\alpha,\lambda),

(3.3)

where

f(\chi,m,a)=\mu\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}\overline{\chi^{*}\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)}\chi^{*}(am)r(m)\neq 0

(3.4)

and
\chi^{*}\,(
mod

f_{\chi})
is the primitive Dirichlet character inducing the character
\chi
.

Proof.
We proceed by induction over
D
. If
D=1
the assertion is obvious since both sides of (
3.3
) equal
F(s,0,\alpha,\lambda)
. Indeed, in this case we have
B_{1}(F)=1
,
f(\chi,1,1)=1
and
\chi^{*}(n)=1
for every
n
.

Let now
D>1
and assume the assertion for all proper divisors of
D
. By Lemma
2
we have

\displaystyle F_{|D}(s,a/D,\alpha,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\chi_{0}(n)}{n^{s}}e(-{an}/{D}-\alpha n^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}\sum_{k|(n,D^{*})}B_{k}(F)a({n}/{k})e(-{an}/{D}-\alpha n^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k|D^{*}}\frac{B_{k}(F)}{k^{s}}F(s,{ak}/{D},k^{\lambda}\alpha,\lambda),

Hence

\begin{split}F(s,a/D,\alpha,\lambda)&=F_{|D}(s,a/D,\alpha,\lambda)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k|D^{*}\\
k>1\end{subarray}}\frac{B_{k}(F)}{k^{s}}F(s,{ak}/{D},k^{\lambda}\alpha,\lambda)=A-B.\end{split}

(3.5)

We consider
A
first. By Lemma
1
we have

A=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\chi_{0}(n)}{n^{s}}e(-an/D)e(-\alpha n^{\lambda})=\frac{1}{\varphi(D)}\sum_{\chi(\bmod D)}c(\chi,a/D)F^{\chi}(s,0,\alpha,\lambda).

Observing that
F^{\chi}(s,0,\alpha,\lambda)=F_{|D}^{\chi^{*}}(s,0,\alpha,\lambda)
, applying Lemma
2
and (
3.2
), we obtain

A=\frac{1}{\varphi(D)}\sum_{\chi(\bmod D)}\sum_{m|\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)^{*}}\frac{B_{m}(F)\mu(D/f_{\chi})\overline{\chi^{*}(D/f_{\chi})}\,\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}\chi^{*}(am)}{m^{s}}F^{\chi^{*}}(s,0,m^{\lambda}\alpha,\lambda),

(3.6)

which is of the form (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) with 1 in place of
r(m)
. Next we consider
B
. Since

F(s,ak/D,k^{\lambda}\alpha,\lambda)=F\left(s,\frac{ak/(k,D)}{D/(k,D)},k^{\lambda}\alpha,\lambda\right)

(3.7)

and
k>1
,
k|D^{*}
, we have that
D/(k,D)
is a proper divisor of
D
and
(\frac{ak}{(k,D)},\frac{D}{(k,D)})=1
, so we may apply the inductive assumption to the right hand side of (
3.7
). We obtain

\begin{split}B=\frac{1}{\varphi(D)}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k|D^{*}\\
k>1\end{subarray}}\varphi((k,D))\sum_{\chi(\bmod\frac{D}{(k,D)})}\mu(D/f_{\chi})\overline{\chi^{*}(D/f_{\chi})}\,\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}\chi^{*}(a)\mu((k,D))\\
&\times\sum_{\nu|\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}(k,D)}\right)^{*}}\frac{B_{k\nu}(F)\chi^{*}(k\nu)r(\nu)}{(k\nu)^{s}}F^{\chi^{*}}(s,0,(k\nu)^{\lambda}\alpha,\lambda),\end{split}

(3.8)

since
B_{m}(F)
is multiplicative and
(k,\nu)=1
. But
D
is squarefree, so if
k|D^{*}
,
\nu|(D/(f_{\chi}(k,D))^{*}
and
\chi^{*}(k)\neq 0
then

k\nu\ \text{divides}\ \left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)^{*}.

We also observe that the sets of primitive characters
\chi^{*}
obtained from the characters
\chi
(mod
D
), and from the characters
\chi
(mod
D/d
), as
d
ranges over the divisors of
D
, are the same. Thus, writing
m=k\nu
, (
3.5
),(
3.6
) and (
3.8
) imply (
3.3
) with certain coefficients
f(\chi,m,a)
. It remains to show that they satisfy (
3.4
).

If
f_{\chi}=D
then
B
does not contribute any
\chi^{*}
to (
3.3
) since
D/(k,D)<D
in (
3.8
). Moreover, the inner sum in
A
has only single term corresponding to
m=1
. So, in this case

f(\chi,1,a)=\mu\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)\overline{\chi^{*}\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)}\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}\chi^{*}(a),

as required. Assume now
f_{\chi}<D
. In this case both
A
and
B
contribute to
f(\chi,m,a)
. From (
3.6
) we see that
A
contributes

\mu\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)\overline{\chi^{*}\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)}\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}\chi^{*}(am),

(3.9)

while the contribution of
B
is

\mu\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)\overline{\chi^{*}\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)}\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}\chi^{*}(am)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k|m\\
k>1\\
(k,m/k)=1\end{subarray}}\varphi((k,D))\mu((k,D))r\left(\frac{m}{k}\right).

(3.10)

But

\begin{split}r(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k|m\\
(k,m/k)=1\end{subarray}}\varphi((k,D))\mu((k,D))r\left(\frac{m}{k}\right)/r(m)&=r(m)\prod_{p^{a}\|m}\left(1+\frac{\varphi((p^{a},D))\mu((p^{a},D))}{p}\right)\\
&=r(m)\prod_{p^{a}\|m}\left(1-\frac{p-1}{p}\right)=1,\end{split}

hence (
3.10
) becomes

\mu\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)\overline{\chi^{*}\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)}\overline{\tau(\chi^{*})}\chi^{*}(am)(1-r(m)),

and the lemma follows from (
3.5
),(
3.9
) and (
3.10
). ∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
have degree
2
and integer conductor
q_{F}
. Moreover, let
D\equiv 1(\bmod\,q_{F})
be a positive integer. Then the nonlinear twist
F(s,1/D,\alpha,\lambda)
is entire if
0<\lambda<1/2
, and is holomorphic for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,3/4-i\theta_{F}]
if
\lambda=1/2
.

Proof.
We use the notation in our paper
[
9
]
. Writing

f(n)=n/D+\alpha n^{\lambda},

(3.11)

by
[
9
, Theorem 1]
, we know that the term
F(s;f)=F(s,1/D,\alpha,\lambda)
can be expressed in terms of the dual twist

\overline{F}(s;f^{*})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{a(n)}}{n^{s}}e(f^{*}(n))

in the following way. For every
K>0
there exists an integer
J=J(K)>0
, functions
W_{0}(s),\dots,W_{J}(s)
and
G_{J}(s;f)
holomorphic for
\sigma>-K
, and real numbers
0=\eta_{0}<\eta_{1}<\dots<\eta_{J}
such that

F(s,1/D,\alpha,\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}W_{j}(s)\overline{F}(s+\eta_{j}+2i\theta_{F};f^{*})+G_{J}(s;f).

(3.12)

Here
\theta_{F}
is the internal shift defined in Section 2 and
f^{*}
is the dual of
f
. The precise shape of
f^{*}
is important in this lemma, but only a general description of
f^{*}
is given in
[
9
]
; see Section 1.3 there. So we first proceed with the explicit computation of
f^{*}
.

We consider, more generally, the function

f(n)=\beta n+\alpha n^{\lambda}.

(3.13)

The first step requires the computation of the
z
-critical point
x_{0}=x_{0}(\xi)
of the function
\Phi(z,\xi)
defined by equation (1.5) of
[
9
]
, which in our case becomes

\Phi(z,\xi)=z^{1/2}-2\pi\frac{q\beta z}{\xi}-2\pi\alpha\frac{q^{\lambda}z^{\lambda}}{\xi^{\lambda}},\hskip 28.45274ptq=\frac{q_{F}}{(4\pi)^{2}}.

(3.14)

Hence
x_{0}
satisfies

x_{0}^{1/2}=4\pi q\beta\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}+4\pi\alpha\lambda q^{\lambda}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\right)^{\lambda}=4\pi q\beta\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\psi\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\right),\hskip 28.45274pt\psi(u)=1+\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\beta}q^{\lambda-1}u^{\lambda-1}.

(3.15)

Consequently, squaring both sides and solving for
(x_{0}/\xi)^{2}
, we get

\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}=(4\pi q\beta)^{-2}\xi\,\psi(x_{0}/\xi)^{-2}.

(3.16)

Next we recall from equation (1.8) of
[
9
]
that

f^{*}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Phi^{\flat}(x_{0},\xi),

(3.17)

where in our case
\Phi^{\flat}
means that we drop all terms with
\omega>1
of the asymptotic expansion

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Phi(x_{0},\xi)=\xi\sum_{\omega\in{\mathcal{D}}_{f}}A_{\omega}\xi^{-\omega},\hskip 28.45274pt{\mathcal{D}}_{f}=\{\omega=m(1-\lambda):m\in\mathbb{Z},m\geq 0\};

see Section 1.3 of
[
9
, p.6742-6743]
. Hence, if
0<\lambda<1/2
we have

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Phi^{\flat}(x_{0},\xi)=A_{0}\xi+A_{1-\lambda}\xi^{\lambda},

(3.18)

while if
\lambda=1/2
there is an additional constant term, which we omit since it is clearly irrelevant in (
3.12
). Now we compute the coefficients
A_{0}
and
A_{1-\lambda}
.

From (
3.14
),(
3.15
) and (
3.16
) we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi}\Phi(x_{0},\xi)&=2q\beta\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\psi\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\right)-q\beta\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}q^{\lambda-1}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\right)^{\lambda-1}\right)\\
&=q\beta\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(1-2\lambda)q^{\lambda-1}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\right)^{\lambda-1}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{q_{F}\beta}\xi\psi^{-2}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\right)\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(1-2\lambda)q^{\lambda-1}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}\right)^{\lambda-1}\right).\end{split}

(3.19)

Moreover, recalling the definition of
\psi(u)
in (
3.15
), as
u\to\infty
we have

\psi^{-2}(u)=1-\frac{2\alpha\lambda}{\beta}q^{\lambda-1}u^{\lambda-1}+O(u^{2(\lambda-1)}),

hence

\psi^{-2}(u)\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(1-2\lambda)q^{\lambda-1}u^{\lambda-1}\right)=1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}q^{\lambda-1}u^{\lambda-1}+O(u^{2(\lambda-1)}).

(3.20)

But from
[
7
, Lemma 2.3]
we know that in our case

\frac{x_{0}}{\xi}=\left(\frac{4\pi}{\beta q_{F}}\right)^{2}\xi+O(\xi^{1-\eta}),\quad\text{with some $\eta>0$}.

(3.21)

Thus, recalling the definition of
q
in (
3.14
), from (
3.19
),(
3.20
) and (
3.21
) we obtain that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Phi(x_{0},\xi)=\frac{1}{q_{F}\beta}\xi-\frac{\alpha}{\beta^{2\lambda}q_{F}^{\lambda}}\xi^{\lambda}+o(\xi^{\lambda}).

(3.22)

Therefore, from (
3.17
),(
3.18
) and (
3.22
) we finally get that

f^{*}(n)=\frac{1}{q_{F}\beta}n-\frac{\alpha}{\beta^{2\lambda}q_{F}^{\lambda}}n^{\lambda}.

(3.23)

We are now ready to conclude the proof of the lemma. Recalling definition (
2.1
), thanks to (
3.11
),(
3.12
),(
3.13
) and (
3.23
) we have that

\begin{split}F(s,1/D,\alpha,\lambda)&=\sum_{j=0}^{J}W_{j}(s)\overline{F}(s+\eta_{j}+2i\theta_{F},D/q_{F},-\alpha D^{2\lambda}q_{F}^{-\lambda},\lambda)+G_{J}(s;f)\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{J}W_{j}(s)\overline{F}(s+\eta_{j}+2i\theta_{F},1/q_{F},-\alpha D^{2\lambda}q_{F}^{-\lambda},\lambda)+G_{J}(s;f)\end{split}

since
D\equiv 1
(mod
q_{F}
).
Then, applying once again transformation formula (
3.12
) to the twists in the last equation, we finally obtain an expression of type

F(s,1/D,\alpha,\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{\widetilde{J}}\widetilde{W}_{j}(s)F(s+\widetilde{\eta}_{j},0,c,\lambda)+\widetilde{G}_{\widetilde{J}}(s;f)

(3.24)

with the same properties as (
3.12
), where
c>0
is a certain constant. Now, we suppose that
0<\lambda<1/2
. Then, using the notation of
[
8
, p.1352-1353]
, thanks to Remark 5 there, we have that
F
belongs to the class
M(2,\tau)
for every
\tau>0
. Thus, by
[
8
, Theorem 5]
, all twists on the right hand side of (
3.24
) are entire. Therefore
F(s,1/D,\alpha,\lambda)
is entire as well since
K
is arbitrary, and the first assertion of the lemma follows. The second assertion follows from well known properties of the standard twist, see
[
8
, Theorems 1 and 2]
. ∎

4.
Proof of the theorem

Recall that for
F
as in the Theorem and a Dirichlet character
\chi(\bmod\,D)
,
d_{\chi^{*}}
and
\theta_{\chi^{*}}
denote degree and internal shift of the twist
F^{\chi^{*}}(s)
, respectively.

We first observe that
d_{\chi^{*}}\geq 2
for all
\chi(\bmod\,D)
. Indeed, otherwise we would have
d_{\chi^{*}}=0
or
d_{\chi^{*}}=1
, as there are no
L
-functions in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
of degrees
0<d<1
and
1<d<2
, see
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
. Both possibilities lead to a contradiction. In the first case,
F^{\chi^{*}}(s)
is a Dirichlet polynomial (see
[
4
, Theorem 1]
)

F^{\chi^{*}}(s)=D(s),

and in the second a linear combination of shifted Dirichlet
L
-functions corresponding to the characters (mod
q(\chi^{*})
)

F^{\chi^{*}}(s)=\sum_{\psi(\bmod q(\chi^{*}))}P(s,\psi)L(s+i\theta,\psi^{*}),

q(\chi^{*})
being the conductor of
F^{\chi^{*}}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 2]
). Here
P(s,\psi)
are certain Dirichlet polynomials and
\theta
is a certain real shift. Thus, twisting by
\overline{\chi^{*}}
we have

F^{\chi_{0}}(s)=D^{\overline{\chi^{*}}}(s)

(4.1)

and

F^{\chi_{0}}(s)=\sum_{\psi(\bmod q(\chi^{*}))}P^{\overline{\chi^{*}}}(s,\psi)L(s+i\theta,\psi\overline{\chi^{*}}),

(4.2)

respectively, where
\chi_{0}
denotes the principal character
(\bmod\,f_{\chi})
. Obviously,
f_{\chi}|D
and hence

F^{\chi_{0}}(s)=F(s)\prod_{p|f_{\chi}}F_{p}^{-1}(s).

Recalling that the Lindelöf
\mu
-function of a given function
f(s)
is

\mu(\sigma,f)=\inf\{\xi\geq 0:f(\sigma+it)\ll(|t|+1)^{\xi}\},

we therefore see that the Lindelöf
\mu
-functions of
F^{\chi_{0}}(s)
and
F(s)
coincide. In particular, for
\sigma<0
we have

\mu(\sigma,F^{\chi_{0}})=2\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right).

But computing the same function using (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), for
\sigma<0
we obtain that

\mu(\sigma,F^{\chi_{0}})\leq\frac{1}{2}-\sigma,

a contradiction. Thus
d_{\chi^{*}}\geq 2
for all
\chi(\bmod\,D)
.

The rest of the proof will be conducted by contradiction as well. Suppose that

d_{0}:=\max_{\chi(\bmod D)}d_{\chi^{*}}>2

and write

A_{0}=\{\chi(\bmod D):d_{\chi^{*}}=d_{0}\},\quad\theta_{0}=\min_{\chi\in A_{0}}\theta_{\chi^{*}},\quad B_{0}=\{\chi\in A_{0}:\theta_{\chi^{*}}=\theta_{0}\},\quad\lambda_{0}=1/d_{0}<1/2.

By Lemma
4
, we know that the function
F(s,1/D,\alpha,\lambda_{0})
is entire. On the other hand, according to Lemma
3
, it is a combination of twists

F^{\chi^{*}}(s,0,m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha,\lambda_{0})

with
\chi(\bmod\,D)
and
m|(D/f_{\chi})^{*}
. If
\chi\not\in A_{0}
then
\lambda_{0}<1/d_{\chi^{*}}
, thus the corresponding term is entire thanks to
[
8
, Theorem 5]
. The other terms are standard twists of
F^{\chi^{*}}
, so may have a pole at
s_{0}(\chi^{*})=1/2+1/(2d_{0})-i\theta_{\chi^{*}}
, but from (
3.3
) we have

\sum_{\chi\in B_{0}}\sum_{m|\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)^{*}}\frac{B_{m}(F)f(\chi,m,1/D)}{m^{s_{0}}}{\rm Res}_{s=s_{0}}F^{\chi^{*}}(s,0,m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha,\lambda_{0})=0

(4.3)

where
s_{0}=1/2+1/(2d_{0})-i\theta_{0}
. Recalling (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we have

{\rm Res}_{s=s_{0}}F^{\chi^{*}}(s,0,m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha,\lambda_{0})=c(F^{\chi^{*}})\frac{\overline{a(n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha})}\,\overline{\chi^{*}(n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha})}}{n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha}^{1-s_{0}}},

(4.4)

where
c(F^{\chi^{*}})\neq 0
and

n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha}=n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha}(\chi^{*})=q({\chi^{*})}d_{0}^{-d_{0}}\alpha^{d_{0}}m.

Let now

q_{0}=\max_{\chi\in B_{0}}\,(D/f_{\chi})^{*}q({\chi^{*}}),\ \ \text{and for positive integers $\nu$ let $\alpha_{\nu}=d_{0}(\nu/q_{0})^{1/d_{0}}$.}

If
\chi\in B_{0}
then

n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha_{\nu}}(\chi^{*})=\frac{q(\chi^{*})m}{q_{0}}\nu,

hence if
\chi\in B_{0}
is such that
q(\chi^{*})/q_{0}\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, then
a(n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha_{\nu}})=0
and the residue in (
4.4
) vanishes. Let further

C_{0}=\{\chi\in B_{0}:\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)^{*}q(\chi^{*})=q_{0}\},

\chi\in B_{0}\backslash C_{0}
and

\frac{q(\chi^{*})}{q_{0}}=\frac{l_{\chi^{*}}}{m_{\chi^{*}}},\qquad l_{\chi^{*}},m_{\chi^{*}}\in{\mathbb{N}},\,(l_{\chi^{*}},m_{\chi^{*}})=1,\,m_{\chi^{*}}>1.

(4.5)

Then

n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha_{\nu}}(\chi^{*})=\frac{l_{\chi^{*}}m}{m_{\chi}^{*}}\nu\not\in{\mathbb{N}}

for all
(\nu,m_{\chi^{*}})=1
. Denoting by
M
the least common multiple of all numbers
m_{\chi^{*}}
in (
4.5
) we have
a(n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha_{\nu}})=0
for all
(\nu,M)=1
, and the corresponding residues in (
4.4
) vanish in all these cases. Suppose now that
\chi\in C_{0}
and
m
is a proper divisor of
(D/f_{\chi})^{*}
. Then for
(\nu,D)=1
we have

n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha_{\nu}}(\chi^{*})=\frac{m\nu}{\left(\frac{D}{f_{\chi}}\right)^{*}}\not\in{\mathbb{N}},

and the corresponding residue vanishes in these cases as well.

Finally, for
\chi\in C_{0}
and
m=(D/f_{\chi})^{*}
we have

n_{m^{\lambda_{0}}\alpha_{\nu}}(\chi^{*})=\nu.

Hence, recalling (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we conclude that for all integers
(\nu,MD)=1
, we have

a(\nu)\sum_{\chi\in C_{0}}\overline{\ell(\chi)}\chi^{*}(\nu)=0,

(4.6)

where in view of (
2.6
)

\begin{split}\ell(\chi)&=c(F^{\chi^{*}})f(\chi,(D/f_{\chi})^{*},1/D)\frac{B_{(D/f_{\chi})^{*}}(F)}{((D/f_{\chi})^{*})^{s_{0}}}\\
&=c(F^{\chi^{*}})f(\chi,(D/f_{\chi})^{*},1/D)\prod_{p|(D/f_{\chi})}\frac{A_{\partial_{p}}(p)}{p^{\partial_{p}s_{0}}}.\end{split}

But (
2.4
) and Lemma
3
imply that
\ell(\chi)\neq 0
for all
\chi\in C_{0}
, and since
F
is saturated
(\bmod\,D)
, (
4.6
) implies that the characters
\chi^{*}
with
\chi\in C_{0}
are linearly dependent, a contradiction. This shows that
d_{\chi^{*}}=2
for all
\chi(\bmod\,D)
.

The proof that
\theta_{\chi^{*}}=\theta_{F}
for all
\chi(\bmod\,D)
can be carried out analogously. As before, we proceed by contradiction, keeping the notation introduced in the first part of the proof. Now
d_{0}=2
,
\lambda_{0}=1/2
, and
A_{0}
contains all characters
\chi(\bmod\,D)
. We redefine
\theta_{0}
to be any
\theta_{\chi^{*}}\neq\theta_{F}
, so that
F(s,1/D,\alpha,1/2)
is holomorphic at
s=s_{0}
by Lemma 4.
Hence we have that (
4.3
) still holds with
\lambda_{0}
replaced by
1/2
. Then, repeating the subsequent calculations with
\lambda_{0}=1/2
, we arrive at (
4.6
), which again gives a contradiction. The theorem now follows. ∎

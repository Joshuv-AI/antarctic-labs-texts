Abstract
. We study relativized Lascar groups, which are formed by relativizing Lascar groups to the solution set of a partial type Σ \Sigma . We introduce the notion of a Lascar tuple for Σ \Sigma and by considering the space of types over a Lascar tuple for Σ \Sigma , the topology for a relativized Lascar group is (re-)defined and some fundamental facts about the Galois groups of first-order theories are generalized to the relativized context. In particular, we prove that any closed subgroup of a relativized Lascar group corresponds to a stabilizer of a bounded hyperimaginary having at least one representative in the solution set of the given partial type Σ \Sigma . Using this, we find the correspondence between subgroups of the relativized Lascar group and the relativized strong types.

D. Lascar introduced the notion of Lascar (Galois) group of a first-order theory in his paper
[
L82
]
, which is proven to have Galois theoretic correspondence between the subgroups of the automorphism group and the notions of model theoretic strong types (cf.
[
CLPZ01
,
LP01
]
).
That is, the Lascar, KP, and Shelah strong types, which play crucial roles in the study of first-order theories, are exactly the the same as the orbit equivalence relations defined by the corresponding subgroups of the Lascar group.

In
[
DKL17
]
, the relativized Lascar group, which are formed by restricting the domain of the automorphisms that fix the Lascar strong types in a parital type, was introduced to study the first homology groups in model theory.
And then the relativized Lascar group was studied further in
[
DKKL21
]
, proving that under some conditions, the relativized Lascar groups of Shelah strong types of some finite tuples are all isomorphic.
In
[
KL23
]
, the Galois theoretic correspondence for the Lascar group and the results concerning the relativized Lascar group in the previous papers were extended and generalized to the context of hyperimaginaries (=
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence classes).
There are also studies connecting the Lascar group and topological dynamics (such as
[
KPR18
]
), but this paper does not delve into that topic.

Our contribution is centered around the extension of the correspondence between subgroups of the Lascar group and the strong types over hyperimaginaries in
[
KL23
]
to the relativized context.
Because we prove every statement in this paper over a fixed hyperimaginary
\boldsymbol{e}
, we often encounter more technical difficulties;
we tried our best to prove and explain every such detail as much as we could, and sometimes separated them as remarks and claims to emphasize that hyperimaginaries can cause subtleties.

There are two natural ways to relativize a subgroup of automorphisms to a partial type
\Sigma
:
One is simply by restricting the domains of the automorphisms in the subgroup directly (Definition
3.1
, Definition
3.3
(2)), and the other one is collecting restricted automorphisms that fixes the corresponding orbits in the partial type (Definition
1.20
, Definition
3.3
(1)).
We will compare them in Section
3
, but until then, we will focus on the latter definition, re-define the topology of the relativized Lascar group, and find the correspondence between the subgroups and the strong types in the given partial type.

Given a hyperimaginary
\boldsymbol{e}
, by assuming that a type
\Sigma
is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant and there is a small tuple

\Sigma

can be, in some sense, localized;
for the definitions of
\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma
,

and

, see preliminaries, Remark

, Remark

, and Definition

.

Corollary

.

Let
\Sigma
be an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type and suppose

Let

(1)

Section
1
is dedicated to presenting preliminaries.
In Subsection
1.1
, we recall definitions and facts about hyperimaginaries and Lascar groups.
In particular, the characterizations of Lascar, KP, and Shelah strong types over a hyperimagianry are given.
In the follwing Subsection
1.2
, we recall the definition of the relativized Galois groups of first-order theories over a hyperimaginary.

In Section
2
, we develop most of the main results. We first introduce the notion of a Lascar tuple over a hyperimaginary
\boldsymbol{e}
for an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant partial type
\Sigma
and show its existence (Definition
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
). Under the assumption that there is a tuple
c
of realizations of
\Sigma
with
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(c)
, we define a topology
\mathfrak{t}
on a relativized Lascar group
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
of
\Sigma
, which is induced by the surjective map from the space of types over a Lascar tuple. The topology
\mathfrak{t}
turns out to coincide with the quotient topology given from the ordinary Lascar group of the theory, and so
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
becomes a quasi-compact group with the topology
\mathfrak{t}
.

The key result in Section
2
is the Galois theoretic correspondence between a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
(which can be characterized by its orbit equivalence relation) and a bounded hyperimaginary, whose set of representatives intersects with the set of tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
(Proposition
2.7
and Lemma
2.10
). The Lascar tuple plays an essential role in the proof of this correspondence, Proposition
2.7
. As a corollary, we find a correspondence between the relativized KP [Shelah] strong type and the closure of the trivial subgroup [the connected component containing the identity] of the relativized Lascar group (Theorem
2.19
and Corollary
2.20
).

In Section
3
, we recall another method of considering automorphisms only in the solution set of a partial type by restricting the domain, and compare with the relativized one.
We prove that if the orbit equivalence relation induced from a given subgroup is weaker than having the same Lascar strong type, then the relativized subgroup of the automorphism group is the same as the smallest group containing the restricted one and the relativized Lascar group.

coarser than

has finitely many conjugates over

holds.

If

is just =, so that

are just real tuples, then we can omit “coarser than

The following observation is necessary when we consider an equivalence class of a type-definable equivalence relation over certain parameters as a hyperimaginary.

Fact 1.19
.

Let
F
be an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type-definable equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{C}^{\alpha}
and assume that
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(b)
for some tuple
b
of elements in
\mathfrak{C}
(note that we always have at least one such
b
by letting
b=a
).
Then there is an
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation
F^{\prime}
such that for any
c\in\mathfrak{C}^{\alpha}
,
c_{F}
and
(cb)_{F^{\prime}}
are interdefinable over
\boldsymbol{e}
so that we can ‘replace’ an equivalence class of an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type-definable equivalence relation with a hyperimaginary.

Proof.

Since
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(b)
,
\boldsymbol{e}=E(\mathfrak{C},a)
is
b
-invariant, so there is a
b
-type-definable equivalence relation
E^{*}
such that
b/E^{*}=E^{*}(\mathfrak{C},b)=E(\mathfrak{C},a)=\boldsymbol{e}
.
Furthermore, because
F
is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant and
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(b)
,
F
is type-definable over
b
, say by
F(x,y;b)
.
Then for
p(x)=\operatorname{tp}(b)
, put

F^{\prime}(xz,yw):=(F(x,y;z)\wedge E^{*}(z,w)\wedge p(z)\wedge p(w))\vee xz=yw.

It is not difficult to verify that
F^{\prime}
is indeed an equivalence relation; we claim that
F^{\prime}
is the desired one.
For any

,
\models F^{\prime}(cb,f(cb))
if and only if

\models(F(c,f(c);b)\wedge E^{*}(b,f(b))\wedge p(b)\wedge p(f(b)))\vee cb=f(cb),

which is just equivalent to
\models F(c,f(c);b)\vee cb=f(cb)
.
Now it is immediate that

if and only if
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{(cb)_{F^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
.
∎

Fact 1.24
.

(1)

\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf}_{\operatorname{L}}^{\omega}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})

(2)

\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf}_{\operatorname{KP}}^{\omega}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})

(3)

\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{S}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf}_{\operatorname{S}}^{\omega}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})

Proof.

(1): The proof is the same as
[
DKL17
, Remark 3.3]
and
[

, Proposition 6.3]
, but we prove it again here because their statements are (seemingly) slightly weaker and the proof requires some facts not contained in this paper.
Let
\alpha\geq\omega
be a small ordinal and

.
We will prove that if their corresponding countable subtuples have the same Lascar strong type over
\boldsymbol{e}
, then

We will use the following facts over a hyperimaginary (

being “

”):

•

For any tuples

and

between

and

.
Also,

and

of

and

Assume that the assertion holds for all infinite ordinals less than
\alpha
, and the corresponding countable subtuples of

and

have the same Lascar strong type over
\boldsymbol{e}
, so that for each

Suppose that there is a sequence of ordinals

For any subset

If it is true that for any finite subset

(2):
\sigma\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})

.

∎

Fact 1.25
.

The relativized Lascar group
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, relativized KP group
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{KP}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, and relativized Shelah group
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{S}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
do not depend on the choice of
\mathfrak{C}
.

Proof.

Basically, the proof of
[
DKL17
, Proposition 3.6]
or
[
Lee22
, Proposition 6.5]
can be used, but the situations are not exactly the same as here.
Here we prove for
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{KP}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
in the same way with full details.
The proof for
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{S}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
can be obtained by replacing KP with L and S in the following proof, respectively.

Let
\mathfrak{C}\prec\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
be monster models.
Define
\eta:\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{KP}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}),\boldsymbol{e})\rightarrow\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{KP}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}),\boldsymbol{e})
by

f\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})\cdot\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}),\boldsymbol{e})\mapsto f^{\prime}\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})\cdot\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}),\boldsymbol{e}),

\text{or, in short, }[f]\mapsto[f^{\prime}]

where
f^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})
is any extension of
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
.

We first check that it is well-defined.
It suffices to show the following:
For any
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, if
f\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}),\boldsymbol{e})
, then
f^{\prime}\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}),\boldsymbol{e})
.
First, consider
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
such that
f\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}),\boldsymbol{e})
and any extension
f^{\prime}
to
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
.
Let
(b^{\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda}
be a tuple of realizations of
\Sigma
in
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
, and
(b_{i})_{i<\lambda}
in
\mathfrak{C}
realize
\operatorname{tp}((b^{\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda}/M)
where
M
is a small model containing
a
(so that
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(M))
.
Then
(b^{\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda}\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(b_{i})_{i<\lambda}
, and thus

f^{\prime}((b^{\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda})\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}f^{\prime}((b_{i})_{i<\lambda})=f((b_{i})_{i<\lambda})\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(b_{i})_{i<\lambda}\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(b^{\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda}.

Thus
f^{\prime}\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}),\boldsymbol{e})
.

It is straightforward to see that
\eta
is a homomorphism:
For any
f,g\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
and extensions

,

is an extension of

, hence

in
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{KP}}(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{e})
by well-definedness.

To show that it is injective, let

,
f^{\prime}_{1},f^{\prime}_{2}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})
be any extensions of them and assume

in
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{KP}}(\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}),\boldsymbol{e})

Then

, so for any tuple

of realizations of

in
\mathfrak{C}
,

.

For surjectivity, consider any

and small model

.
Choose any

such that

.
Then by Fact

of

in

2.
Topology on relativized model theoretic Galois groups

In this section, we will generalize Fact
1.15
and Fact
1.18
to the relativized Lascar group. To that end, we will find a topology
\mathfrak{t}
on the relativized Lascar group
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, so that

•

the group
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is a quasi-compact group with respect to
\mathfrak{t}
, and

•

\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\pi^{\prime-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]
and
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{S}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\pi^{\prime-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{0}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]
,

where
\pi^{\prime}:\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is the natural surjective map, and
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{0}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
are the closure of the trivial subgroup and the connected component containing the identity of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
with respect to the topology
\mathfrak{t}
, respectively.

For this purpose, we introduce a notion that will replace the role of a small model when we consider Lascar groups.

Definition 2.1
.

A small tuple
b
of realizations of
\Sigma
is called a
Lascar tuple
(in
\Sigma
over
\boldsymbol{e}
) if
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma):b\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}\sigma(b)\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant partial type
\Sigma(x)
, there is a Lascar tuple.

Proof.

By Fact
1.11
(2), the set of all Lascar strong types

C=\{c_{\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}}:c\text{ is a countable tuple of realizations of }\Sigma\}

is small, say its cardinality is
\kappa
.

Let
b=(b_{i}:i<\kappa)
be a small tuple that collects representatives of Lascar strong types in
C
, only one for each equivalence class.
Then
b
is the desired one; by Fact
1.24
, it is enough to show that for an automorphism
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, if
b\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}f(b)
, then for any countable tuple
d
of realizations of
\Sigma
,
d\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}f(d)
.
Note that there is
i<\kappa
such that
d\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}b_{i}
.
Then we have

d\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}b_{i}\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}f(b_{i})\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}f(d)

where the last equivalence follows from the invariance of
\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}
.
∎

given by the natural projection map

is also the same as the above topology, thus the topology of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is independent of the choice
b
(Fact
1.15
) and it is a topological group since a quotient group of a topological group with quotient topology is a topological group (it is the same reasoning as
[
DKL17
, Remark 3.4]
).
∎

The following proposition is a relativized analogue of
[
KL23
, Proposition 2.2]
, which have extended
[
LP01
, Lemma 1.9]
over a hyperimaginary.
The purpose of Proposition
2.7
is to interpret closed subgroups of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
using bounded hyperimaginaries, and it will play a critical role in many arguments until the end of this section.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
.
The following are equivalent.

(1)

\pi_{\Sigma}[H]
is closed in
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and
H=\pi_{\Sigma}^{-1}[\pi_{\Sigma}[H]]
.

(2)

H=\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
for some hyperimaginary
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
, and one of the representatives of
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}
is a tuple of realizations of
\Sigma
.
Caution: Recall that
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
does not mean that
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}
is a countable hyperimaginary (Definition/Remark
1.7
).

Proof.

The method of proof is the same as the proof of
[
KL23
, Proposition 2.3]
, but we use a Lascar tuple
b
instead of a model
M
. Note that the kernel of
\pi_{\Sigma}
is
\xi^{-1}[\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
We have

\operatorname{Aut}_{b}(\mathfrak{C})=\operatorname{Aut}_{b\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})\leq\xi^{-1}[\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]\leq H

and since
\pi_{\Sigma}[H]
is closed,
\nu_{b}^{-1}[\pi_{\Sigma}[H]]
is closed and thus
\{h(b):h\in H\}
is type-definable over
b
.
Hence by
[
KL23
, Proposition 2.2]
,
H=\operatorname{Aut}_{b_{F}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
for some
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation
F
.

We have
b_{F}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
:

[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C}):H]=\kappa
is small since
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\mathfrak{C},\boldsymbol{e})\leq H
, and so there is
\{f_{i}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C}):i<\kappa\}
such that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})=\bigsqcup_{i<\kappa}f_{i}\cdot H
.
Then for all
g,h\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, if
g\cdot H=h\cdot H
, then
h^{-1}g\in H
and hence
g(b_{F})=h(b_{F})
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
:
Say
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}=c_{F}
where
c
is a tuple of realizations of
\Sigma
and
F
is an
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation.
By Remark
2.3
and Remark
2.6
, we may assume that the Lascar tuple
b
contains
c
.
For
q(x)=\operatorname{tp}(c/\boldsymbol{e})
, because
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
,

F^{\prime}(z_{1},z_{2}):=(q(z_{1})\wedge q(z_{2})\wedge F(z_{1},z_{2}))\vee(\neg q(z_{1})\wedge\neg q(z_{2}))

is an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant bounded equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{C}^{|c|}
.
Since
c
is a tuple in
\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})
, for any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
,
\sigma(c)\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}c
and thus
F^{\prime}(\sigma(c),c)
by Fact
1.18
(1).
Note that
c_{F}=c_{F^{\prime}}
, so we have
\xi^{-1}[\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]\leq H=\operatorname{Aut}_{c_{F}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, hence
\pi_{\Sigma}^{-1}[\pi_{\Sigma}[H]]=H
.
Notice that
H=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C}):f(c)\models F(z,c)\}
.
Then
\nu_{b}^{-1}[\pi_{\Sigma}[H]]=\{p(z^{\prime})\in S_{b}(b):F(z,c)\subseteq p(z^{\prime})\}
where
z\subseteq z^{\prime}
and
|z^{\prime}|=|b|
.
Thus
\pi_{\Sigma}[H]
is closed.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

(1)

For
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, the relation
\equiv^{H}
is the orbit equivalence relation such that for tuples
b,c
in
\mathfrak{C}
,
b\equiv^{H}c
if and only if there is
h\in H
such that
h(b)=c
.

(2)

For
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
, we will use the same notation
\equiv^{H}
but it is confined to the tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
and
c,d
be tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.

(1)

If
H=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, then
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
.

(2)

If
H=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, then
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
.

(3)

If
H=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{S}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, then
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{S}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
.

In other words, in
\Sigma
, having the same strong type is the same as the orbit equivalence relation induced from the corresponding relativized automorphism group.
From Definition
1.20
(3) and the definition of the orbit equivalence, this remark can be proved in a direct way.

Now we prove that closed subgroups of the relativized Lascar groups are completely determined by the orbit equivalence relations they define.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
containing
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
such that
\pi_{b}[H]
is a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
Then

(1)

For any tuples
c
and
d
of realizations of
\Sigma
,
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if for all corresponding subtuples
c^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime}
of
c
and
d
, which are finite tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
,
c^{\prime}\equiv^{H}d^{\prime}
.

(2)

Given
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
, the following are equivalent.

(a)

\sigma\in H
.

(b)

\sigma
fixes all the
\equiv^{H}
-classes of any tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.

(c)

\sigma
fixes all the
\equiv^{H}
-classes of any finite tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.

Also, (2)(a) and (2)(b) are equivalent even when
\pi_{b}[H]
is not closed.

Proof.

(1): Suppose that for all corresponding subtuples
c^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime}
of
c
and
d
, which are finite tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
,
c^{\prime}\equiv^{H}d^{\prime}
.
Note that, by Proposition
2.7
,
H^{\prime}:=\pi_{\Sigma}^{-1}[\pi_{b}[H]]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
for some hyperimaginary
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
such that one of its representatives is a tuple of realizations of
\Sigma
.
Then by commutativity of the diagram in Remark
2.5
,
H=\xi[H^{\prime}]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
and so
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
\operatorname{tp}(c/\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{tp}(d/\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e})
.
Since
\operatorname{tp}(c^{\prime}/\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{tp}(d^{\prime}/\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e})
for all corresponding subtuples
c^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime}
of
c
and
d
, which are finite tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
, by compactness, we have that
\operatorname{tp}(c/\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{tp}(d/\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e})
and so
c\equiv^{H}d
.

(2): It is enough to show that
(c)
implies
(a)
. Take arbitrary
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
and suppose
\sigma
fixes all the
\equiv^{H}
-classes of any finite tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
. For any subtuple
b^{\prime}
of
b
, which is a finite tuple of realizations of
\Sigma
,
b^{\prime}\equiv^{H}\sigma(b^{\prime})
, and so by (1),
b\equiv^{H}\sigma(b)
.
Thus there is
\tau\in H
such that
\tau(b)=\sigma(b)
and so
\tau^{-1}\sigma(b)=b
.
Then we have

\tau^{-1}\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{b}(\Sigma)\leq\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\leq H,

and conclude that
\sigma\in\tau H=H
.

The last line of Lemma
2.10
can be proved in a similar way.
∎

Definition 2.11
.

(1)

\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is the topological closure of the trivial subgroup of

.

(2)

\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{0}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})

Note that
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})

Lemma 2.12
.

\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{S}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
are closed in
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\nu_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]

\displaystyle=\{\operatorname{tp}(\sigma(b)/b):\sigma\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma)\}

\displaystyle=\{\operatorname{tp}(c/b):c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}b\}.

By Fact
1.18
(2) and Remark
1.9
,
\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}
is the same as
\equiv_{\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})}
, which is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant and type-definable.
By the assumption that
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(b)
, there is
\Gamma(x,b)
over
b
, whose solution set is the same as the solution set of the type
\operatorname{tp}(b/\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e}))
, and thus
\{\operatorname{tp}(c/b):c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}b\}=\{p(x)\in S_{b}(b):\models\Gamma(x,b)\}
.
Hence
\{\operatorname{tp}(c/b):c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}b\}
is closed in
S_{b}(b)
, and this implies that
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is closed.
The proof for
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{S}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is exactly the same;
replace KP by S and
\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
by
\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})
.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

Using Fact
1.5
, we will relativize Definition
1.6
and Definition/Remark
1.7
to
\Sigma
.

(1)

If
\boldsymbol{c}

\boldsymbol{e}

.

2.7
(2) is the same as
H=\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})

\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma

Remark 2.14
.

Let
\{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}:i<\lambda\}
be a small set of arbitrary hyperimaginaries.
According to Definition
1.20
(1),
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(\Sigma)=\xi[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(\mathfrak{C})]
does
not
mean that
\sigma
fixes
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
(because
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\subseteq\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})
is not assumed), and it is not clear whether

\bigcap_{i<\lambda}\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}:i<\lambda\}}(\Sigma)\ \ (\dagger)

holds or not (note that
\bigcap_{i<\lambda}\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(\Sigma)=\bigcap_{i<\lambda}\xi[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(\mathfrak{C})]\supseteq\xi[\bigcap_{i<\lambda}\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(\mathfrak{C})]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}:i<\lambda\}}(\Sigma)
, thus one direction of the containment is trivial).

But in the case where
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
is a hyperimaginary such that one of the representatives of
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
is a tuple of realizations of
\Sigma
for each
i<\lambda
,
(\dagger)
holds.

Proof.

Say
a_{i}
is one representative of
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
in
\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})
.
If
\sigma\in\bigcap_{i<\lambda}\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(\Sigma)
, then for each
i<\lambda
,
\sigma(a_{i})
is still a representative of
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
in
\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})
, thus any extension of
\sigma
should fix
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
.
That is, any extension
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
of
\sigma
fixes every hyperimaginary in
\{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}:i<\lambda\}
, hence
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}:i<\lambda\}}(\Sigma)
.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}^{c}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}(\Sigma)\text{, and}

\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}^{0}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}(\Sigma).

Proof.

By the definition,
\operatorname{Gal}^{c}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is the intersection of all closed subgroups of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
But by Proposition
2.7
, the collection of all closed subgroups of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is the same as
\{\pi_{\Sigma}(\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})):\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma\}
.
Thus we have

\operatorname{Gal}^{c}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})].

Claim 2.16
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma
.
Then for any extension
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
of
\sigma
, we have
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, which implies that
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
.
In particular, it follows that
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
.

Proof of the claim.

Let
a^{\prime}\in\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})
be a representative of
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}
and
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
be any extension of
\sigma
.
Then
f(a^{\prime})=\sigma(a^{\prime})\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}a^{\prime}
, thus
f(\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime})\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}
and so
f(\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime})\equiv_{\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})}\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}
by Fact
1.18
.
But
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
, hence
f(\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}
.
∎

Therefore

\displaystyle\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}^{c}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\pi_{b}^{-1}[\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\xi[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}(\Sigma),

where the second equality follows because

\pi_{b}^{-1}[\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]]=\pi_{b}^{-1}[\pi_{b}\circ\xi[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]]=(\pi_{b}^{-1}\circ\pi_{b})[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)]

and we have Claim
2.16
;
the last equality follows by Remark
2.13
and
2.14
.
We have proved the first statement of this lemma.

For the second statement of this lemma, we prove a claim first:

Claim 2.17
.

\operatorname{Gal}^{0}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})].

Proof of the claim.

The proof of this claim is essentially the same as the first part of the proof of
[
KL23
, Proposition 3.12(1)]
.
Recall the definition of
\operatorname{Gal}^{0}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and the classical fact that the connected component containing the identity is the intersection of all closed (normal) subgroups of finite indices in a topological group.
Note that for any
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma
,
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
has a finite index in
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, and hence
\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]
has a finite index in
\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]=\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
;

\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]
is also closed due to Proposition
2.7
.
This proves that

\operatorname{Gal}^{0}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\subseteq\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})].

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.7
, every closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
should be
\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]
for some
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma
.
If this
\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]
has a finite index in
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
,
then
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
has a finite index in
\pi_{\Sigma}^{-1}(\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e}))=\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, thus
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma
.
This proves that

\operatorname{Gal}^{0}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\supseteq\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})].

∎

By Claim
2.17
,

\displaystyle\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}^{0}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\pi_{b}^{-1}[\pi_{\Sigma}[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\xi[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma}\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}(\Sigma),

where the second equality follows from Claim
2.16
(as
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma
implies
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma
) with the same argument as before;
the last equality follows by Remark
2.13
and
2.14
.

∎

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
c
and
d
be tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.

(1)

Let
H=\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
.
Then
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
.

(2)

Let
H=\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{0}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
.
Then
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{S}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
.

Proof.

(1): By Proposition
2.7
,
we know that
\pi_{\Sigma}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
for some
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
.
Thus by commutativity of the diagram in Remark
2.5
,
H=\xi[\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
and so
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
c\equiv_{\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{e}}d
.
By Fact
1.18
(2),
c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
if and only if
c\equiv_{\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})}d
, and
\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})
, thus
c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
implies
c\equiv^{H}d
.

By Lemma
2.12
, we have
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\leq\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and hence
H\leq\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
We already have proved that
c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
implies
c\equiv^{H}d
, and by Remark
2.9
,
c\equiv^{\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})}d
if and only if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
, so it follows that
c\equiv^{H}d
if and only if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
.

(2): By Fact
1.18
(3), if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{S}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
, then
c\equiv_{\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})}d
, so by Lemma
2.15
,
c\equiv^{H}d
.
Conversely, assume
c\equiv^{H}d
.
By Fact
1.18
(3), it suffices to show that if
F
is an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type-definable equivalence relation and
c_{F}
has finitely many automorphic images over
\boldsymbol{e}
, then
F(c,d)
holds.

Since
F
is
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant and
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(b)
, by Fact
1.19
, there is an
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation
F^{\prime}
such that
c_{F}
and
(cb)_{F^{\prime}}
are interdefinable over
\boldsymbol{e}
.
But then
(cb)_{F^{\prime}}
is a hyperimaginary in
\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})
and
cb
is a tuple of realizations of
\Sigma
, thus
(cb)_{F^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\restriction\Sigma
.
By Lemma
2.15
, we know that there is
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}(\mathfrak{C})
such that
f(c)=d
.
Then
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{(cb)_{F^{\prime}}}(\mathfrak{C})
by Remark
2.13
, and
\operatorname{Aut}_{(cb)_{F^{\prime}}}(\mathfrak{C})=\operatorname{Aut}_{c_{F}}(\mathfrak{C})
and so
F(c,d)
.
∎

Now we can give an answer to one of the main questions of this section, which says that the quotient groups of relativized KP and Shelah automorphisms are exactly the canonical normal subgroups of the relativized Lascar group.
Recall that
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(\Sigma)
means that there is a small tuple
c
of realizations of
\Sigma
such that
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(c)
.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
\Sigma
be an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type and suppose
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(\Sigma)
.
Then

(1)

\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

(2)

\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{0}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{S}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})/\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

Proof.

(1): Let
H_{1}=\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]
and
H_{2}=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{KP}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
By Remark
2.9
and Proposition
2.18
, we have that
\equiv^{H_{1}}
and
\equiv^{H_{2}}
are the same equivalence relations on the tuples of realizations of
\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})
.
So, by Lemma
2.10
,
H_{1}=H_{2}
.

(2): By exactly the same proof as (1), letting
H_{1}=\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{0}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]
and
H_{2}=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{S}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
∎

We summarize the results obtained to prove Theorem
2.19
in this section, and state the characterization of strong types in
\Sigma
, which is analogous to Fact
1.18
, in the following corollary.
It says that having the same strong type can be, in some sense, localized by finding a partial type that contains the real tuples of interest.

Corollary 2.20
.

Let
\Sigma
be an
\boldsymbol{e}
-invariant type and suppose
\boldsymbol{e}\in\operatorname{dcl}(\Sigma)
.
Let
c
and
d
be tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.

(1)

The following are equivalent.

(a)

c\equiv^{\operatorname{KP}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
.

(b)

c\equiv_{\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}d
.

(c)

There is
\sigma\in\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]
such that
\sigma(c)=d
.

(2)

The following are equivalent.

(a)

c\equiv^{\operatorname{S}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d

(b)

c\equiv_{\operatorname{acl}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}d
.

(c)

There is
\sigma\in\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{0}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]
such that
\sigma(c)=d
.

Proof.

(1):
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
is clear by Fact
1.18
(2).
By Lemma
2.15
,
\pi_{b}^{-1}[\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}^{c}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})]=\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{bdd}(\boldsymbol{e})\cap\Sigma}(\Sigma)
, so
(b)
and
(c)
are equivalent.

(c)\Rightarrow(a)
follows from Proposition
2.18
.
The proof for (2) is similar.
∎

3.
Restricted model theoretic Galois groups

There is another possible approach to consider the subgroups of the automorphism group in
\Sigma
, by restricting the domains of the automorphisms in the subgroup, which was considered in
[
DKL17
, Section 3]
.

Definition 3.1
.

For
X\in\{\operatorname{L},\operatorname{KP},\operatorname{S}\}
,

\operatorname{Autf}_{X}^{\operatorname{res}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\{f\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}):f\in\operatorname{Autf}_{X}(\mathfrak{C},\boldsymbol{e})\}\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma).

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

For
X\in\{\operatorname{L},\operatorname{KP},\operatorname{S}\}
,
\operatorname{Autf}_{X}^{\operatorname{res}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\leq\operatorname{Autf}_{X}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

(2)

Let
H_{1}=\operatorname{Autf}_{X}^{\operatorname{res}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and
H_{2}=\operatorname{Autf}_{X}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
Then
\equiv^{H_{1}}
and
\equiv^{H_{2}}
are the same.

Proof.

(1) is clear, and for (2), we only need to show that
c\equiv^{H_{2}}d
implies
c\equiv^{H_{1}}d
where
c,d
are tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.
By Remark
2.9
,
\equiv^{H_{2}}
is the same as
\equiv^{X}_{\boldsymbol{e}}
, thus
c\equiv^{H_{2}}d
implies
c\equiv^{X}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
and hence there is
f\in\operatorname{Autf}_{X}(\mathfrak{C},\boldsymbol{e})
such that
f(c)=d
.
So,
c\equiv^{H_{1}}d
.
∎

Of course, we can relativize or take a restriction of any subgroup of the automorphism group.
We will use the following notation for general subgroups.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a subgroup
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
, let
\equiv^{H}
be the orbit equivalence relation.

(1)

\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma):
for any tuple
c
of realizations of
\Sigma
,
c\equiv^{H}\sigma(c)\}
.

(2)

H^{\operatorname{res}}=\xi[H]=\{f\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C}):f\in H\}
.

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

For any
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
,
H^{\operatorname{res}}\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

(2)

For
X\in\{\operatorname{L},\operatorname{KP},\operatorname{S}\}
,

(a)

\operatorname{Autf}_{X}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{X}_{\boldsymbol{e}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, and

(b)

\operatorname{Autf}_{X}^{\operatorname{res}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf}_{X}(\mathfrak{C},\boldsymbol{e})^{\operatorname{res}}
.

The following proposition implies that if
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\mathfrak{C},\boldsymbol{e})\leq H
and
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\leq H^{\operatorname{res}}
, then
H^{\operatorname{res}}=\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})
such that
\equiv^{H}
is weaker than
\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}
(that is, if
c\equiv^{\operatorname{L}}_{\boldsymbol{e}}d
, then
c\equiv^{H}d
).
Then

H^{\operatorname{res}}\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})

where
H^{\operatorname{res}}\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\{g_{1}g_{2}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma):g_{1}\in H^{\operatorname{res}}
,
g_{2}\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\}
.
Especially, for
X\in\{\operatorname{L},\operatorname{KP},\operatorname{S}\}
,
\operatorname{Autf}_{X}^{\operatorname{res}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf}_{X}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

Proof.

(\subseteq)
: Let
\sigma\in H^{\operatorname{res}}\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, so that
\sigma=\tau\tau^{\prime}
for some
\tau\in H^{\operatorname{res}}
and
\tau^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
It is enough to show that for a Lascar tuple
b
,
\sigma(b)\equiv^{H}b
, and

\sigma(b)=\tau\tau^{\prime}(b)\equiv^{H}\tau^{\prime}(b)\equiv^{H}b.

(\supseteq)
: Take
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.
For a Lascar tuple
b
in
\Sigma
,
b\equiv^{H}\sigma(b)
, thus there is
\tau\in H
such that
\tau(b)=\sigma(b)
.
Then letting
\tau_{\Sigma}=\tau\upharpoonright\Sigma(\mathfrak{C})
, we have

\tau_{\Sigma}^{-1}\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{b}(\Sigma)\leq\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e}),

and so

\sigma\in\tau_{\Sigma}\cdot\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})\subseteq H^{\operatorname{res}}\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e}).

∎

Consideration of the small box topology in Remark
3.6
, and Example
3.7
are suggested by the referee.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C)}
and consider the small box topology on
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
, that is, let basic open sets be of the form
U_{c,d}:=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma):\sigma(c)=d\}
, where
c
and
d
are small tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
.

•

\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is a closed subset of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
:

Given
\sigma\notin\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, there is a small tuple
c
of realizations of
\Sigma
such that
c\not\equiv^{H}\sigma(c)
.
Then the open neighborhood
U_{c,\sigma(c)}
of
\sigma
is disjoint from
\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

•

H^{\operatorname{res}}
is dense in
\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
:

If
U_{c,d}
intersects
\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, then
c\equiv^{H}d
and so
U_{c,d}
intersects
H^{\operatorname{res}}
.

Thus we can conclude that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is the closure of
H^{\operatorname{res}}
in the small box topology.

If
\xi[H](=H^{\operatorname{res}})
contains
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
and
\pi_{\Sigma}[H]
is a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, then the pointwise convergence topology on
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma)
with basic open sets
U_{c,d}=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\Sigma):\sigma(c)=d\}
, where
c
and
d
are finite tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
gives the same result:

In the first bullet of the argument for the small box topology, by Lemma
2.10
(1),
\sigma\notin\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
implies that there is a finite tuple
c
of realizations of
\Sigma
such that
c\not\equiv^{H}\sigma(c)
(note that
\equiv^{H}
is the same as
\equiv^{H^{\operatorname{res}}}
for tuples of realizations of
\Sigma
).
Thus
\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is also the closure of
H^{\operatorname{res}}
in the pointwise convergence topology.

According to the observations in Remark
3.6
, we can easily find an example such that
H^{\operatorname{res}}\neq\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
:

Example 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a saturated pure set in the empty language,
\boldsymbol{e}=\emptyset
, and
\Sigma=\{x=x\}
.
Note that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{C})=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\mathfrak{C})
is the group of all permutations of
\mathfrak{C}
.

Now, let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{C})
be the group of all permutations of small support.

Then
\equiv^{H}
holds between every two small tuples of
\mathfrak{C}
, and
H^{\operatorname{res}}=H\neq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{C})=\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
.

Note that in Example
3.7
,
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}^{res}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{C})=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
, thus this example does not answer the second question of
[
DKL17
, Question 3.8]
, which asked if there is an example such that
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}^{res}}(\Sigma,\emptyset)\neq\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\emptyset)
.
Also, in Example
3.7
,
\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(T,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Gal}_{\operatorname{L}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
is trivial (so that
\pi[H]
and
\operatorname{Aut}_{\equiv^{H}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
are trivially closed), but
H=H^{\operatorname{res}}
does not contain
\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\mathfrak{C},\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{C})
. Based on Remark
3.6
and Example
3.7
, we ask the following question, which is a variant of the second question of
[
DKL17

.

Question 3.8
.

(1)

Do we have that
\operatorname{Autf}_{\operatorname{L}}^{\operatorname{res}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})=\operatorname{Autf_{\operatorname{L}}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{e})
? By Proposition
3.5

of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\boldsymbol{e}}(\mathfrak{C})

(2)

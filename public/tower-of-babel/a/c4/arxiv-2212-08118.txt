A representation for the reproducing kernel of a weighted Bergman space
Erwin Miña-Díaz

Abstract
For a weight function in the unit disk which is the modulus of a finite product of powers of Blaschke factors, we give a canonical representation for the reproducing kernel of the corresponding weighted Bergman space in terms of the values of the kernel and its derivatives at the origin. This yields a formula for the contractive zero divisor of a Bergman space corresponding to a finite zero set.



Anal. Math. Phys. 13, 58 (2023).

Keywords:
Bergman spaces, reproducing kernel, zero divisor.

1
Statement of results

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the unit disk and let
\sigma
denote the normalized area measure
d\sigma=\pi^{-1}dA
, so that
\sigma(\mathbb{D})=1
. Let
h\geq 0
be an integrable function in
\mathbb{D}
, and let
A^{2}_{h}
denote the space of functions
f
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{2}h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)<\infty.

We write
A^{2}
for
h\equiv 1
.

If
h
is such that point evaluation functionals
z\mapsto f(z)
acting on
A^{2}_{h}
are bounded (for instance, if for some
\rho<1
,
\inf_{\rho<|z|<1}h>0
), then there exists a unique function
K(z,\zeta)
in
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
, analytic in
z
and in
\overline{\zeta}
, such that

\displaystyle f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(\zeta)K(z,\zeta)h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta),\quad f\in A^{2}_{h},\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.1)

The function
K(z,\zeta)
is called the reproducing kernel of the weighted Bergman space
A^{2}_{h}
, and has the symmetry property

\displaystyle K(z,\zeta)=\overline{K(\zeta,z)}\,.

(1.2)

In this paper, we give a canonical formula for the reproducing kernel corresponding to a weight
h
of the form

\displaystyle h(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{s}\left|\frac{z-a_{k}}{1-\overline{a}_{k}z}\right|^{p_{k}}\,,

(1.3)

where
s\geq 1
,
p_{k}>-2
, and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
for all
1\leq k\leq s
, the points
a_{k}
being pairwise distinct. The formula is explicit in terms of the values of
K(z,\zeta)
and its derivatives at the origin, which can be numerically computed by integration.

The subclass of (
1.3
) consisting of weights of the form

\displaystyle h(z)=\left|\prod_{k=1}^{s}\left(\frac{z-a_{k}}{1-\overline{a}_{k}z}\right)^{m_{k}}\right|^{p},\qquad p>0,\ m_{k}\in\mathbb{N},

(1.4)

is of particular importance because for these weights there is a connection between the reproducing kernel and the contractive zero divisors of the Bergman space
A^{p}
(see Section
1.1
below).

The following notation will be used throughout the paper. For all non-negative integers
k,j
, we set

\displaystyle K^{(k,j)}(z,\zeta):=\frac{\partial^{k+j}}{\partial z^{k}\partial\overline{\zeta}^{j}}K(z,\zeta).

Note that the equality

K^{(k,j)}(z,\zeta)=\overline{K^{(j,k)}(\zeta,z)}

follows from the symmetry property (
1.2
), and that differentiating (
1.1
) yields

\displaystyle f^{(j)}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(\zeta)K^{(j,0)}(z,\zeta)h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta),\quad f\in A^{2}_{h},\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.5)

We write

\displaystyle q(z):=\prod_{k=1}^{s}(z-a_{k}),\quad q^{*}(z):=z^{s}\overline{q(1/\overline{z})}=\prod_{k=1}^{s}(1-\overline{a}_{k}z),

(1.6)

and for each
1\leq k\leq s
, set

q_{k}(z):=\underset{j\not=k}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq s}}(z-a_{j}),\quad q^{*}_{k}(z):=\underset{j\not=k}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq s}}(1-\overline{a}_{j}z)\,.

Let
c_{0},\ldots,c_{s}
denote the coefficients of
q
, and for each
1\leq k\leq s
, let
c_{k,0},\ldots,c_{k,s-1}
denote the coefficients of
q_{k}
, that is,

\displaystyle q(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}c_{\ell}z^{\ell},\qquad q_{k}(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}c_{k,\ell}z^{\ell},\qquad c_{s}=c_{k,s-1}=1.

From these coefficients, we construct the polynomials

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{k}(z):={}&z^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2-k}\overline{c_{s-\ell}}z^{\ell},\qquad 0\leq k\leq s-2,\end{split}

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle L_{k}(z,\zeta):={}

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{s-2}\left(z^{r}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2-r}\overline{c_{k,s-1-\ell}}z^{\ell}\right)\left(\overline{\zeta}^{r}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2-r}c_{k,s-1-\ell}\overline{\zeta}^{\ell}\right),

(1.8)

1\leq k\leq s
. When
s=1
, the right-hand side of (
1.8
) acquires the form
\sum_{r=0}^{-1}\cdots
. This and other empty sums that later occur are to be interpreted as zero.

Our main result is the following formula.

Theorem 1.1
.

The kernel
K(z,\zeta)
corresponding to the weight (
1.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle K(z,\zeta)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(1-z\overline{a}_{k})(1-a_{k}\overline{\zeta})}+\frac{J(z,\zeta)}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}q^{*}(z)},

(1.9)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}J(z,\zeta)={}&\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{K^{(k,j)}(0,0)}{k!\,j!}T_{k}(z)\overline{T_{j}(\zeta)}-\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}(k+1)T_{k}(z)\overline{T_{k}(\zeta)}\\
&-\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})L_{k}(z,\zeta).\end{split}

(1.10)

For
s=1
,
J(z,\zeta)\equiv 0
and (
1.9
) reduces to

\displaystyle K(z,\zeta)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{\frac{p_{1}}{2}(1-|a_{1}|^{2})}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(1-z\overline{a}_{1})(1-a_{1}\overline{\zeta})}.

For
s=2
, (
1.9
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}K(z,\zeta)={}&\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(1-z\overline{a}_{k})(1-a_{k}\overline{\zeta})}\\
&+\frac{K(0,0)-1-\frac{p_{1}}{2}(1-|a_{1}|^{2})-\frac{p_{2}}{2}(1-|a_{2}|^{2})}{(1-\overline{a}_{1}z)(1-\overline{a}_{2}z)(1-a_{1}\overline{\zeta})(1-a_{2}\overline{\zeta})}.\end{split}

(1.11)

Formula (
1.11
) was obtained by Hansbo in
[
2
, Thm. 2.7]
by means of a two-step process that initially uses an iterative procedure to prove the formula for even integer values of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, and then transitions from integer to arbitrary values of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
by way of a function theoretical argument involving Carlson’s Theorem. Hansbo’s method seems to become too intricate when higher values of
s
are considered, but for
s=2
it yields the following elegant representation for
K(0,0)
:

K(0,0)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})+|a_{1}-a_{2}|^{2}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\binom{p_{1}/2}{j}\binom{p_{2}/2}{j}\lambda^{j}}{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{p_{1}/2}{j}\binom{p_{2}/2}{j}\lambda^{j}},

with
\lambda=(1-|a_{1}|^{2})(1-|a_{2}|^{2})/|1-a_{1}\overline{a}_{2}|^{2}
. We do not know whether this representation generalizes in some satisfactory way to all the derivatives of the kernel in (
1.10
), but at least in a structural sense, formula (
1.9
) extends Hansbo’s (
1.11
) to an arbitrary
s
.

Besides Hansbo’s work, the paper by MacGregor and Stessin
[
3
]
established a couple of structural formulas for the kernel in the context of Bergman spaces
A^{p}
, for weights
h
of the form (
1.4
) with simple zeros (i.e.,
m_{k}=1
,
1\leq k\leq s
). These formulas equally extend to the more general weight (
1.3
) with little change in their statements. First, the canonical representation (13) of
[
3
]
can now be obtained as a corollary of Theorem
1.1
as follows. Since the polynomials
q^{*}_{1},\ldots,q^{*}_{s}
are linearly independent,
J(z,\zeta)
can be expanded as a sum

J(z,\zeta)=\sum_{1\leq k,j\leq s}d_{k,j}q^{*}_{k}(z)\overline{q^{*}_{j}(\zeta)}\,,

where the coefficients
d_{k,j}
are given by

d_{k,j}=\frac{J(1/\overline{a}_{k},1/\overline{a}_{j})}{q^{*}_{k}(1/\overline{a}_{k})\overline{q^{*}_{j}(1/\overline{a}_{j})}}

(if some
a_{k}
equals zero, the formula for
d_{k,j}
has to be adjusted). This gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}K(z,\zeta)={}&\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(1-z\overline{a}_{k})(1-a_{k}\overline{\zeta})}\\
&+\sum_{1\leq k,j\leq s}\frac{d_{k,j}}{(1-z\overline{a}_{k})(1-a_{j}\overline{\zeta})}\,.\end{split}

(1.12)

Taking
p_{k}=p
in (
1.12
) yields an equivalent form of
[
3
, Eq. (13)]
.

The second representation
[
3
, Eq. (7)]
that MacGregor and Stessin provide is in terms of the Blaschke products

\displaystyle B(z):=\frac{q(z)}{q^{*}(z)},\quad B_{k}(z):=\frac{q_{k}(z)}{q^{*}_{k}(z)},

(1.13)

and the values of the kernel at the points
a_{k}
. It results from taking
p_{k}=p
in the following formula, more generally valid for a weight
h
as in (
1.3
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}K(z,\zeta)={}&\frac{B(z)\overline{B(\zeta)}}{(1-\overline{\zeta}z)^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{(\frac{p_{k}}{2}+1)B_{k}(z)\overline{B_{k}(\zeta)}(\overline{\zeta}z-|a_{k}|^{2})}{(1-\overline{\zeta}z)(1-\overline{a}_{k}z)(1-a_{k}\overline{\zeta})}\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})(1-|a_{j}|^{2})B_{k}(z)\overline{B_{j}(\zeta)}}{(1-\overline{a}_{k}z)(1-a_{j}\overline{\zeta})B_{k}(a_{k})\overline{B_{j}(a_{j})}}K(a_{k},a_{j})\,.\end{split}

(1.14)

We prove (
1.14
) by following the original argument in
[
3
]
, just noting that that argument works equally well for a weight
h
as in (
1.3
). The first part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i.e., up to (
2.24
)) is also based on the same idea, but applied in a way in which values at the origin are involved instead of values at the points
a_{k}
. The second part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is more demanding, and consists of performing the algebraic manipulations needed to get the kernel written in the reduced and symmetrical form (
1.9
).

1.1
Contractive zero divisors

For a fixed
0<p<\infty
, let
A^{p}
denote the Bergman space of functions
f
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and such that

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}d\sigma(\zeta)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Any zero set of a function in
A^{p}
(each zero counted as many times as its multiplicity) is called an
A^{p}
zero set. For each
A^{p}
zero set
Z
, there exists a unique (up to a unimodular constant factor) function
G\in A^{p}
of norm
\|G\|_{p}=1
such that for every
f\in A^{p}
that vanishes on
Z
, the function
f/G\in A^{p}
and
\|f/G\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
. This function
G
is called the contractive zero divisor (or canonical divisor) of the zero set
Z
. We refer the reader to
[
1
, Ch. 5]
for the relevant theory and history.

For a finite zero set, say consisting of the points
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}
, each
a_{k}
of multiplicity
m_{k}
, the canonical divisor is given by
[
1
, Ch. 5, Thm. 4]

\displaystyle G(z)=K(0,0)^{-1/p}K(z,0)^{2/p}\prod_{k=1}^{s}\left(\frac{z-a_{k}}{1-\overline{a}_{k}z}\right)^{m_{k}}\,,

(1.15)

where
K(z,\zeta)
is the reproducing kernel for the weight (
1.4
). If we therefore evaluate (
1.9
) at
\zeta=0
, we get a structural formula for the canonical divisor, the evaluation resulting in

\displaystyle\begin{split}K(z,0)={}&\frac{\overline{q(0)}z^{s}}{q^{*}(z)}+\frac{z^{s-1}}{q^{*}(z)}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\overline{q_{k}(0)}\left(1+\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{q^{*}(z)}\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\frac{K^{(k,0)}(0,0)}{k!}T_{k}(z),\end{split}

(1.16)

where (in this context)
p_{k}=m_{k}p
,
1\leq k\leq s
.

We remark that, indeed, to get (
1.16
) one does not need the reduced symmetric representation (
1.9
). Evaluating at
\zeta=0
in the less refined version (
2.23
) provides a shorter proof.

Since the representation (
1.15
) already involves values of the kernel at
z=0
and
\zeta=0
, it is particularly fitting that (
1.16
) is equally expressed in terms of derivatives of the kernel at the origin.

1.2
Computing the values
K^{(k,j)}(0,0)

Let us now see how the values
K^{(k,j)}(0,0)
that occur in (
1.10
) can be determined by solving certain linear systems of equations that arise from integration.

Note that the polynomial
T_{k}
defined in (
1.7
) can be characterized as the unique polynomial of degree
\leq s-2
such that

\displaystyle\left[\frac{T_{k}}{q^{*}}\right]^{(\ell)}(0)=\delta_{k,\ell}k!,\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq s-2.

From (
2.24
) and (
2.22
) we find

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{K^{(k,j)}(0,0)}{k!j!}\frac{\overline{T_{j}(\zeta)}}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}}={}\frac{K^{(k,0)}(0,\zeta)}{k!}-\frac{\overline{P_{k}(\zeta)}}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}},\qquad 0\leq k\leq s-2,

(1.17)

where

\displaystyle\overline{P_{n}(\zeta)}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\zeta}^{s-1}}{n!}\left.\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial z^{n}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{q(z)}{1-z\overline{\zeta}}\right)+\frac{1}{1-z\overline{\zeta}}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})q_{k}(z)}{1-\overline{a}_{k}z}\right)\right|_{z=0}\,.

After computing the derivatives, we can alternatively write

\overline{P_{n}(\zeta)}=(n+1)q(0)\overline{\zeta}^{s+n}+\overline{\zeta}^{s-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}b_{n,j}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\,,

with

\displaystyle b_{n,j}={}

\displaystyle(n+1)c_{n+1-j}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})\sum_{r=0}^{n-j}(\overline{a}_{k})^{r}c_{k,n-j-r}\,.

By (
1.5
), we have that for all
0\leq n\leq s-2
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{T_{n}(\zeta)}{q^{*}(\zeta)}K^{(k,0)}(0,\zeta)h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{T_{n}}{q^{*}}\right]^{(k)}(0)=\delta_{n,k}n!\,,

so that after multiplying (
1.17
) by
T_{n}(\zeta)/q^{*}(\zeta)
and integrating with respect to
hd\sigma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{K^{(k,j)}(0,0)}{k!j!}\langle T_{n},T_{j}\rangle=\delta_{n,k}-\langle T_{n},P_{k}\rangle,\qquad 0\leq n\leq s-2,

with
\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f\overline{g}|q^{*}|^{-2}hd\sigma
. Since the polynomials
T_{k}
are linearly independent, the Gram matrix
\left(\langle T_{n},T_{j}\rangle\right)_{n,j=0}^{s-2}
has a non-zero determinant, and so for each
0\leq k\leq s-2
, the vector
(K^{(k,j)}(0,0))_{j=0}^{s-2}
is the solution of a non-singular linear system of equations whose coefficients and constants can be numerically computed by integration.

2
Proofs

Both Theorem
1.1
and Formula (
1.14
) will be proved with the help of Green’s Formula

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\zeta)d\zeta=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\overline{\zeta}}\,d\sigma(\zeta)\,,

valid for
\Omega
a finitely connected domain bounded by analytic Jordan curves and
g\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on many heavy computations that at times require the use of several identities. To make it easier to read, we first gather in a lemma a few of these identities that involve the polynomials
q
and
q^{*}
previously defined in (
1.6
), as well as the polynomial

\displaystyle{q^{\prime}}^{*}(t):=t^{s-1}\overline{q^{\prime}(1/\overline{t})}.

Note that since
q(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}c_{j}z^{j}
and
q^{*}(z)=z^{s}\overline{q(1/\overline{z})}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\overline{c_{s-j}}z^{j}
, we have

\displaystyle c_{j}=\frac{q^{(j)}(0)}{j!},\quad\overline{c_{s-j}}=\frac{{q^{*}}^{(j)}(0)}{j!},\quad 0\leq j\leq s.

(2.1)

Lemma 2.1
.

The following identities hold true:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\overline{\zeta}(1-z\overline{\zeta})(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\\
&={}\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right)+\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(z-t)}+\frac{\overline{\zeta}({q^{*}}^{\prime}q)(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})},\end{split}

(2.2)

\displaystyle t{q^{*}}^{\prime}(t)=sq^{*}(t)-{q^{\prime}}^{*}(t),

(2.3)

\displaystyle z^{s}\overline{(q^{*}q)(\zeta)}={}

\displaystyle z^{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2s}\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}\overline{\zeta}^{2s-\ell},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}=\frac{\overline{(q^{*}q)^{(2s-\ell)}(0)}}{(2s-\ell)!},\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq 2s,

(2.5)

\displaystyle z^{s-1}\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(\zeta)}={}z^{s-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2s-1}\frac{\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})^{(\ell)}(0)}}{\ell!}\overline{\zeta}^{\ell},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{(q{q^{\prime}}^{*})^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}=\frac{\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})^{(2s-1-\ell)}(0)}}{(2s-1-\ell)!},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq 2s-1,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}c_{k}\overline{c_{s-\ell+k}},\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq s,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}={}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}jc_{j}\overline{c_{s-1-\ell+j}}\,,\quad 0\leq\ell\leq s-1.

(2.9)

Proof.

Using that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right)=\frac{\overline{\zeta}^{2}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{\overline{\zeta}({q^{*}}^{\prime}q)(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})}+\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})},

one easily verifies the relation

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\zeta}(1-z\overline{\zeta})(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})^{2}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}+\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})}+\frac{\overline{\zeta}({q^{*}}^{\prime}q)(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right).

On the other hand, we clearly have

\displaystyle\frac{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(z-t)}-\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(1-t\overline{\zeta})},

and we get (
2.2
) by adding these two latter equalities.

Since
q^{*}(t)=t^{s}\overline{q(1/\overline{t})}
, we have

\displaystyle t{q^{*}}^{\prime}(t)={}

\displaystyle t\left(t^{s}\overline{q(1/\overline{t})}\right)^{\prime}=t\left(st^{s-1}\overline{q(1/\overline{t})}+t^{s}\overline{q^{\prime}(1/\overline{t})}(-t^{-2})\right),

which is equal to the right-hand side of (
2.3
).

We next make use of the Maclaurin expansions

\displaystyle(q^{*}q)(\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{2s}\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}\zeta^{\ell}\,,

(2.10)

\displaystyle(q^{*}q^{\prime})(\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{2s-1}\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}\zeta^{\ell},

(2.11)

\displaystyle(qq^{\prime*})(\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{2s-1}\frac{(qq^{\prime*})^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}\zeta^{\ell}\,.

(2.12)

Using (
2.10
) we get

\displaystyle(q^{*}q)(\zeta)=\zeta^{2s}\overline{(q^{*}q)(1/\overline{\zeta})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2s}\frac{\overline{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}}{\ell!}\zeta^{2s-\ell}.

(2.13)

The identity (
2.4
) arises by taking the conjugate in (
2.13
), while (
2.5
) is derived by comparing the Taylor coefficients of (
2.10
) with those of (
2.13
).

Taking conjugates in (
2.11
) yields (
2.6
). But we can also use (
2.12
) to get

\displaystyle\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(\zeta)}=\overline{\zeta}^{2s-1}(q{q^{\prime}}^{*})(1/\overline{\zeta})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2s-1}\frac{(qq^{\prime*})^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}\overline{\zeta}^{2s-1-\ell},

(2.14)

so that (
2.7
) follows by comparing the Taylor coefficients of (
2.6
) and (
2.14
).

To prove (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we first use Leibnitz’s product rule to expand the derivatives
(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)
and
(q^{*}q^{\prime})^{(\ell)}(0)
, and then rewrite the resulting sums in terms of the coefficients
c_{j}
by means of (
2.1
).

2.1
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
f
be a function analytic in the closed unit disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. By an application of the residue theorem, we get that for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(z)={}&\frac{z^{s-1}}{q^{*}(z)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{f(\zeta)q^{*}(\zeta)}{\zeta^{s-1}(\zeta-z)}d\zeta+\frac{1}{q^{*}(z)}\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{f^{(j)}(0)}{j!}T_{j}(z)\,,\end{split}

(2.15)

where

\displaystyle T_{k}(z)=\frac{k!\,z^{s-1}}{(s-2)!}\binom{s-2}{k}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2-k}}{\partial t^{s-2-k}}\left(\frac{q^{*}(t)}{z-t}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,.

(2.16)

Making use of (
2.1
), this can written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{k}(z)={}&\frac{k!z^{s-1}}{(s-2)!}\binom{s-2}{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2-k}\binom{s-2-k}{\ell}{q^{*}}^{(\ell)}(0)\frac{(s-2-k-\ell)!}{z^{s-1-k-\ell}}\\
={}&z^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2-k}\overline{c_{s-\ell}}z^{\ell}.\end{split}

Note that this is the same
T_{k}
that was previously defined in (
1.7
).

We now seek to express the integral in (
2.15
) as an integral over
\mathbb{D}
with respect to the weight
h
given by (
1.3
). On the unit circle
|\zeta|=1
,
h
is constant
1
, and also
\overline{\zeta}=1/\zeta
. Then, by an application of Green’s Formula we obtain that for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{f(\zeta)q^{*}(\zeta)}{\zeta^{s-1}(\zeta-z)}d\zeta={}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{f(\zeta)\prod_{k=1}^{s}(1-\overline{a}_{k}\zeta)}{\zeta^{s-1}(\zeta-z)}d\zeta\\
={}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})}\overline{q(\zeta)}h(\zeta)f(\zeta)d\zeta\\
={}&\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}}\left(\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})}\overline{q(\zeta)}h(\zeta)\right)f(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)\,.\end{split}

(2.17)

We clarify that to get (
2.17
) we apply Green’s Formula in a subregion of
\mathbb{D}
that lies exterior to small circles around each
a_{k}
for which
p_{k}<0
, and then take limits as the radii of these circles go to
0
.

The function

\displaystyle w(z):=\prod_{k=1}^{s}\left(\frac{z-a_{k}}{1-\overline{a}_{k}z}\right)^{p_{k}/2}

(2.18)

admits a single-valued branch around any point of
\mathbb{D}
that is not an
a_{k}
. Since
h(z)=|w(z)|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\overline{q(\zeta)}h(\zeta)}{1-z\overline{\zeta}}\right)={}&\frac{z}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}}\overline{q(\zeta)}h(\zeta)+\frac{w(\zeta)}{1-z\overline{\zeta}}\overline{\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\left(q(\zeta)w(\zeta)\right)}\,.\end{split}

(2.19)

By direct computation of the derivative using the product rule, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\left(q(\zeta)w(\zeta)\right)={}&w(\zeta)\sum_{k=1}^{s}q_{k}(\zeta)\left(1+\frac{p_{k}}{2}\frac{1-|a_{k}|^{2}}{1-\overline{a}_{k}\zeta}\right).\end{split}

(2.20)

From this point onward we will use the abbreviation

A_{k}:=\frac{p_{k}}{2}(1-|a_{k}|^{2}),\qquad 1\leq k\leq s.

The actual values of these constants play no role in what remains of the proof.

The equalities (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) then combine into

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\overline{q(\zeta)}h(\zeta)}{1-z\overline{\zeta}}\right)={}&h(\zeta)Q(z,\zeta),\end{split}

(2.21)

with

\displaystyle Q(z,\zeta):=\frac{z\overline{q(\zeta)}}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\overline{q_{k}(\zeta)}\left(1+\frac{A_{k}}{1-a_{k}\overline{\zeta}}\right).

(2.22)

If we now write the derivatives in (
2.15
) in the integral form

f^{(j)}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(\zeta)K^{(j,0)}(0,\zeta)h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta),

then (
2.15
) can be written with the help of (
2.17
) and (
2.21
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(z)={}&\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(\zeta)}{q^{*}(z)}\left(z^{s-1}Q(z,\zeta)+\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{T_{j}(z)}{j!}K^{(j,0)}(0,\zeta)\right)h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)\,.\end{split}

By a density argument, this reproducing property also holds for every
f\in A^{2}_{h}
, and so we must have

\displaystyle K(z,\zeta)=\frac{z^{s-1}Q(z,\zeta)}{q^{*}(z)}+\frac{1}{q^{*}(z)}\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\frac{T_{k}(z)K^{(k,0)}(0,\zeta)}{k!}\,.

(2.23)

By the symmetry of the kernel,

K(z,\zeta)=\frac{\overline{\zeta^{s-1}Q(\zeta,z)}}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}}+\frac{1}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}}\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{\overline{T_{j}(\zeta)}K^{(0,j)}(z,0)}{j!}\,.

Taking derivatives with respect to
z
and evaluating at
z=0
yields

\displaystyle K^{(k,0)}(0,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\zeta}^{s-1}}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial z^{k}}\overline{Q(\zeta,z)}\right]_{z=0}+\frac{1}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}}\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{\overline{T_{j}(\zeta)}K^{(k,j)}(0,0)}{j!}\,.

(2.24)

Substituting (
2.24
) into (
2.23
) we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}K(z,\zeta)={}&\frac{z^{s-1}Q(z,\zeta)}{q^{*}(z)}+\frac{\overline{\zeta}^{s-1}}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}q^{*}(z)}\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\frac{T_{k}(z)}{k!}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial z^{k}}\overline{Q(\zeta,z)}\right]_{z=0}\\
&+\frac{1}{\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}q^{*}(z)}\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{K^{(k,j)}(0,0)}{k!j!}T_{k}(z)\overline{T_{j}(\zeta)}\,.\end{split}

(2.25)

This concludes the first part of the proof. The second part amounts to performing the algebraic manipulations needed to reduce (
2.25
) to (
1.9
).
We begin by writing (
2.25
) as

\displaystyle K(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\frac{N(z,\zeta)}{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}q^{*}(z)\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}}+\frac{1}{q^{*}(z)\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}}\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\frac{K^{(k,j)}(0,0)}{k!j!}T_{k}(z)\overline{T_{j}(\zeta)}\,,

(2.26)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}N(z,\zeta):={}&(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}z^{s-1}\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}Q(z,\zeta)\\
&+(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}\overline{\zeta}^{s-1}\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\frac{T_{k}(z)}{k!}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial z^{k}}\overline{Q(\zeta,z)}\right]_{z=0}\,.\end{split}

(2.27)

By (
2.22
), and since

q^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{s}q_{k}(t)\,,

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}z^{s-1}\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}Q(z,\zeta)={}&z^{s}\overline{(q^{*}q)(\zeta)}+(1-z\overline{\zeta})z^{s-1}\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(\zeta)}\\
&+(1-z\overline{\zeta})z^{s-1}\sum_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}\overline{(q_{k}^{*}q_{k})(\zeta)}\,.\end{split}

(2.28)

In view of the representation (
2.16
) for
T_{k}
, we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\frac{T_{k}(z)}{k!}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial z^{k}}\overline{Q(\zeta,z)}\right]_{z=0}={}

\displaystyle\frac{z^{s-1}}{(s-2)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2}}{\partial t^{s-2}}\left(\frac{q^{*}(t)}{z-t}\overline{Q(\zeta,t)}\right)\right]_{t=0},

(2.29)

and this latter derivative can be expanded by differentiating the identity

\displaystyle\frac{q^{*}(t)}{z-t}\overline{Q(\zeta,t)}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}+\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}(q^{*}_{k}q_{k})(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})},

(2.30)

which is just a consequence of (
2.22
).

The relations (
2.30
), (
2.29
), (
2.28
) and (
2.27
) then allow us to write
N(z,\zeta)
in the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}N(z,\zeta)={}&z^{s}\overline{(q^{*}q)(\zeta)}+(1-z\overline{\zeta})z^{s-1}\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(\zeta)}+(1-z\overline{\zeta})(z\overline{\zeta})^{s-1}D(z,\zeta)\\
&+(1-z\overline{\zeta})z^{s-1}\sum_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}\overline{(q_{k}^{*}q_{k})(\zeta)}\\
&+\frac{(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}z^{s-1}\overline{\zeta}^{s-1}}{(s-2)!}\sum_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2}}{\partial t^{s-2}}\left(\frac{(q^{*}_{k}q_{k})(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,,\end{split}

(2.31)

where

D(z,\zeta):=\frac{1}{(s-2)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2}}{\partial t^{s-2}}\left(\frac{(1-z\overline{\zeta})\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,.

We now utilize the identity (
2.2
) and the fact that derivatives at the origin of any function
f
satisfy

f^{(m)}(0)=\frac{1}{m+1}\left.\left(tf(t)\right)^{(m+1)}\right|_{t=0}\,,

to get
D(z,\zeta)
written in the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{(s-1)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-1}}{\partial t^{s-1}}\left(\frac{t\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}-(s-1)\frac{\overline{\zeta}(q^{*}q)(t)}{1-t\overline{\zeta}}+\frac{t\overline{\zeta}({q^{*}}^{\prime}q)(t)}{1-t\overline{\zeta}}\right)\right]_{t=0}\\
&+\frac{1}{(s-2)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2}}{\partial t^{s-2}}\left(\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{z-t}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,.\end{split}

(2.32)

By rearranging terms in (
2.32
) and making use of (
2.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle D(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\frac{z\overline{\zeta}}{(s-1)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-1}}{\partial t^{s-1}}\left(\frac{(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right)\right]_{t=0}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(s-1)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-1}}{\partial t^{s-1}}\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}({q^{\prime}}^{*}q)(t)}{1-t\overline{\zeta}}\right)\right]_{t=0}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(s-2)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2}}{\partial t^{s-2}}\left(\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{z-t}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,,

and after plugging this expression for
D(z,\zeta)
in (
2.31
), we see that we can represent
N(z,\zeta)
as a sum

\displaystyle N(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle N_{1}(z,\zeta)+(1-z\overline{\zeta})N_{2}(z,\zeta)+(1-z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}M_{k}(z,\zeta)\,,

(2.33)

with

\displaystyle N_{1}(z,\zeta):={}

\displaystyle z^{s}\overline{(q^{*}q)(\zeta)}+\frac{(z\overline{\zeta})^{s}}{(s-1)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-1}}{\partial t^{s-1}}\left(\frac{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(q^{*}q)(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,,

(2.34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{2}(z,\zeta):={}&z^{s-1}\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(\zeta)}+\frac{(z\overline{\zeta})^{s-1}}{(s-2)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2}}{\partial t^{s-2}}\left(\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})(t)}{z-t}\right)\right]_{t=0}\\
&-\frac{(z\overline{\zeta})^{s-1}}{(s-1)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-1}}{\partial t^{s-1}}\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}({q^{\prime}}^{*}q)(t)}{1-t\overline{\zeta}}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,,\end{split}

(2.35)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{k}(z,\zeta):={}&z^{s-1}\overline{(q_{k}^{*}q_{k})(\zeta)}+\frac{(z\overline{\zeta})^{s-1}}{(s-2)!}\left[\frac{\partial^{s-2}}{\partial t^{s-2}}\left(\frac{(1-z\overline{\zeta})(q^{*}_{k}q_{k})(t)}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}\right)\right]_{t=0}\,.\end{split}

We next show that

\displaystyle N_{1}(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle q^{*}(z)\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}-(1-z\overline{\zeta})L(z,\zeta)\,,

(2.36)

with

\displaystyle L(z,\zeta):={}

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}(z\overline{\zeta})^{r}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1-r}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1-r}\overline{c_{s-\ell}}z^{\ell}\right)\,.

(2.37)

Since
N_{1}(z,\zeta)
and
M_{k}(z,\zeta)
have the same structure, this will simultaneously show that

\displaystyle M_{k}(z,\zeta)=q_{k}^{*}(z)\overline{q_{k}^{*}(\zeta)}-(1-z\overline{\zeta})L_{k}(z,\zeta),\quad 1\leq k\leq s,

(2.38)

with
L_{k}
given by (
1.8
).

With the help of the identity

\frac{1-z\overline{\zeta}}{(z-t)(1-t\overline{\zeta})}=\frac{1}{z-t}-\frac{\overline{\zeta}}{1-t\overline{\zeta}}\,,

the second term in the right-hand side of (
2.34
) can be written as

\displaystyle\overline{\zeta}^{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}z^{\ell}-z^{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}\overline{\zeta}^{2s-\ell}.

(2.39)

To simplify the upcoming presentation, we write

C_{\ell}:=\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq 2s\,,

so that by (
2.5
),

\overline{C_{\ell}}=\frac{(q^{*}q)^{(2s-\ell)}(0)}{(2s-\ell)!},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq 2s,

and by (
2.8
),

C_{\ell}=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}c_{k}\overline{c_{s-\ell+k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\overline{c_{s-k}}c_{\ell-k}\,,\quad 0\leq\ell\leq s.

Note that
C_{s}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}|c_{j}|^{2}\geq 1
.

Since the right-hand side of (
2.34
) is equal to the sum of (
2.4
) and (
2.39
), we get

\displaystyle N_{1}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle={}C_{s}(z\overline{\zeta})^{s}+\overline{\zeta}^{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}C_{\ell}z^{\ell}+z^{s}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\overline{C_{\ell}}\,\overline{\zeta}^{\ell}\,.

(2.40)

Let us now define, for every integer
n\geq 0
,

V_{n}(t):=\frac{t^{n+1}-1}{t-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}t^{j},

and let us also agree that
V_{-1}\equiv 0
. With the help of these polynomials, we can then write (
2.40
) as

\displaystyle N_{1}(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\left(C_{s}V_{s-1}(z\overline{\zeta})+\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}C_{\ell}\overline{\zeta}^{s-\ell}V_{\ell-1}(z\overline{\zeta})+\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\overline{C_{\ell}}z^{s-\ell}V_{\ell-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\right)

\displaystyle+C_{s}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}C_{\ell}\overline{\zeta}^{s-\ell}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\overline{C_{\ell}}z^{s-\ell}\,.

(2.41)

Letting
\ell=j-k-1
be the new counter, one verifies the equalities

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}C_{\ell}\overline{\zeta}^{s-\ell}=\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s}\overline{c_{s-k}}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j-k}\,,

(2.42)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}C_{\ell}\overline{\zeta}^{s-\ell}V_{\ell-1}(z\overline{\zeta})=\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s}\overline{c_{s-k}}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j-k}V_{s-1-(j-k)}(z\overline{\zeta})\,.

(2.43)

After expanding the product

q^{*}(z)\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}=\left(\sum_{u=0}^{s}\overline{c_{s-u}}z^{u}\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\right),

we can express it by means of the polynomials
V_{n}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}q^{*}(z)\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}={}&(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{j=0}^{s}|c_{s-j}|^{2}V_{j-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s}\left(c_{s-k}\overline{c_{s-j}}z^{j-k}+\overline{c_{s-k}}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j-k}\right)V_{k-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\\
&+C_{s}+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s}\overline{c_{s-k}}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j-k}+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s}c_{s-k}\overline{c_{s-j}}z^{j-k}\,.\end{split}

(2.44)

Four terms in (
2.1
) can be rewritten using (
2.42
) and (
2.43
), so that subtracting (
2.44
) from (
2.1
) yields

\displaystyle N_{1}(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle q^{*}(z)\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}|c_{s-j}|^{2}(z\overline{\zeta})^{j}V_{s-1-j}(z\overline{\zeta})

\displaystyle+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\overline{c_{s-k}}c_{s-j}z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}V_{s-1-j}(z\overline{\zeta})

\displaystyle+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}c_{s-k}\overline{c_{s-j}}z^{j}\overline{\zeta}^{k}V_{s-1-j}(z\overline{\zeta})\,.

If we now write these sums in the form

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}|c_{s-j}|^{2}(z\overline{\zeta})^{j}V_{s-1-j}(z\overline{\zeta})={}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=s-j}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2}\right)(z\overline{\zeta})^{j}\,,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\overline{c_{s-k}}c_{s-j}z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}V_{s-1-j}(z\overline{\zeta})={}

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\,,

then we see that
N_{1}(z,\zeta)
can be written as in (
2.36
) with

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(z,\zeta)={}&\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=s-j}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2}\right)(z\overline{\zeta})^{j}+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\\
&+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}c_{s-k+\ell}\overline{c_{s-j+\ell}}\right)z^{j}\overline{\zeta}^{k}\,.\end{split}

(2.45)

To verify that (
2.45
) coincides with (
2.37
), we group the terms corresponding to the lowest values of
\ell
to get

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(z,\zeta)={}&\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\overline{c_{s-\ell}}z^{\ell}\right)+(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\left(\sum_{\ell=s-j}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2}\right)(z\overline{\zeta})^{j}\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-2}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-2}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}c_{s-k+\ell}\overline{c_{s-j+\ell}}\right)z^{j}\overline{\zeta}^{k}\,.\end{split}

(2.46)

The sum of the last three terms equals the product of
(z\overline{\zeta})
and a second factor, which is just the sum in (
2.45
) with the terms corresponding to
j=s-1
removed. This decomposition can then be iterated to produce (
2.37
).

We will also need a further decomposition of
L(z,\zeta)
, namely, writing (
2.45
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(z,\zeta)={}&(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=s-j}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2}\right)V_{j-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)\overline{\zeta}^{j-k}V_{k-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}c_{s-k+\ell}\overline{c_{s-j+\ell}}\right)z^{j-k}V_{k-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\\
&+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=s-j}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2}\right)+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)\overline{\zeta}^{j-k}\\
&+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}c_{s-k+\ell}\overline{c_{s-j+\ell}}\right)z^{j-k}\,.\end{split}

(2.47)

We now focus on the quantity
N_{2}(z,\zeta)
defined in (
2.35
). With the aid of the relations (
2.7
) and (
2.6
), we can expand the derivatives in (
2.35
) to get

\displaystyle N_{2}(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle\overline{\zeta}^{s-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2}\frac{(q^{*}q^{\prime})^{(\ell)}(0)}{\ell!}z^{\ell}+z^{s-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\frac{\overline{(q^{*}q^{\prime})^{(\ell)}(0)}}{\ell!}\overline{\zeta}^{\ell}\,,

(2.48)

and by (
2.9
), we can then write (
2.48
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{2}(z,\zeta)={}&(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}\ell|c_{\ell}|^{2}V_{s-2}(z\overline{\zeta})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}\ell|c_{\ell}|^{2}\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2}z^{s-1-\ell}V_{\ell-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}j\overline{c_{j}}c_{s-1-\ell+j}\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2}\overline{\zeta}^{s-1-\ell}V_{\ell-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}jc_{j}\overline{c_{s-1-\ell+j}}\\
&+\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2}\overline{\zeta}^{s-1-\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}jc_{j}\overline{c_{s-1-\ell+j}}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-2}z^{s-1-\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}j\overline{c_{j}}c_{s-1-\ell+j}\,.\end{split}

(2.49)

If we now rewrite the last five summands of (
2.49
) in the form

\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}\ell|c_{\ell}|^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\sum_{\ell=s-k}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{s-2}\overline{\zeta}^{s-1-u}\sum_{j=1}^{u+1}jc_{j}\overline{c_{s-1-u+j}}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)\overline{\zeta}^{j-k},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{s-2}\overline{\zeta}^{s-1-u}V_{u-1}(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{j=1}^{u+1}jc_{j}\overline{c_{s-1-u+j}}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)V_{s-2-j+k}(z\overline{\zeta})\overline{\zeta}^{j-k},

and then subtract (
2.47
) from (
2.49
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{N_{2}(z,\zeta)-L(z,\zeta)}{(z\overline{\zeta}-1)}={}&\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}(z\overline{\zeta})^{k}V_{s-2-k}(z\overline{\zeta})\left(\sum_{\ell=s-k}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2}\right)\\
&+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-2}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)V_{s-2-j}(z\overline{\zeta})z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\\
&+\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-2}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}c_{s-k+\ell}\overline{c_{s-j+\ell}}\right)V_{s-2-j}(z\overline{\zeta})z^{j}\overline{\zeta}^{k}\,.\end{split}

(2.50)

We then replace the terms in the right-hand side of (
2.50
) with the equivalent expressions

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}(z\overline{\zeta})^{k}V_{s-2-k}(z\overline{\zeta})\left(\sum_{\ell=s-k}^{s}|c_{\ell}|^{2}\right)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}(z\overline{\zeta})^{j}\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}(\ell+1)|c_{s-j+\ell}|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-2}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\right)V_{s-2-j}(z\overline{\zeta})z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}

\displaystyle\quad={}\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-2}z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(\ell+1)\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell},

and after doing that, we group the terms corresponding to
\ell=0
to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{N_{2}(z,\zeta)-L(z,\zeta)}{(z\overline{\zeta}-1)}={}&\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\overline{c_{s-k}}z^{k}\right)\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{j=0}^{s-3}(z\overline{\zeta})^{j}\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}(\ell+2)|c_{s-j+\ell}|^{2}\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-3}z^{k}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(\ell+2)\overline{c_{s-k+\ell}}c_{s-j+\ell}\\
&+(z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{0\leq k<j\leq s-3}z^{j-1}\overline{\zeta}^{k-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(\ell+2)c_{s-k+\ell}\overline{c_{s-j+\ell}}\,.\end{split}

(2.51)

By the same argument we used to derive (
2.37
) from (
2.45
), we can iterate (
2.51
) to finally get

\displaystyle N_{2}(z,\zeta)-L(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle(z\overline{\zeta}-1)\sum_{r=0}^{s-2}(r+1)(z\overline{\zeta})^{r}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-2-r}c_{s-j}\overline{\zeta}^{j}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s-2-r}\overline{c_{s-k}}z^{k}\right).

Inserting (
2.36
), (
2.38
), and the latter equality into (
2.33
) (recall (
1.7
)) yields

\displaystyle N(z,\zeta)={}

\displaystyle q^{*}(z)\overline{q^{*}(\zeta)}-(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{s-2}(r+1)T_{r}(z)\overline{T_{r}(\zeta)}

\displaystyle+(1-z\overline{\zeta})\sum_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}q_{k}^{*}(z)\overline{q_{k}^{*}(\zeta)}-(1-z\overline{\zeta})^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}L_{k}(z,\zeta).

In view of (
2.26
), this establishes Theorem
1.1
.

2.2
Proof of Formula (
1.14
)

The proof is basically the same given in
[
3
]
, the argument simply works equally well for a product of different powers of Blaschke factors. For every
f
in the disk algebra, a residue calculation gives (this is (2) in
[
3
]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{B(z)}{B(\zeta)}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta={}&f(z)-B(z)\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{1-|a_{k}|^{2}}{B_{k}(a_{k})}\frac{f(a_{k})}{z-a_{k}}.\end{split}

(2.52)

Letting
w
be as in (
2.18
), and since
h\equiv 1
on the unit circle, we can apply Green’s Formula to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{B(z)}{B(\zeta)}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta=\frac{B(z)}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{\overline{\zeta}}{1-\overline{\zeta}z}\overline{B(\zeta)w(\zeta)}f(\zeta)w(\zeta)d\zeta\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{B(z)\overline{B(\zeta)}}{(1-\overline{\zeta}z)^{2}}+\frac{B(z)\overline{\zeta}}{1-\overline{\zeta}z}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{(\frac{p_{k}}{2}+1)(1-|a_{k}|^{2})\overline{B_{k}(\zeta)}}{(1-a_{k}\overline{\zeta})^{2}}\right)f(\zeta)h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)\end{split}

(2.53)

Writing each
f(a_{k})
in (
2.52
) as the integral of
f(\zeta)
against
K(a_{k},\zeta)
and replacing the left-hand side of (
2.52
) by the last integral in (
2.53
), we obtain a representation for
f(z)
as an integral over
\mathbb{D}
with respect to
h(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)
, whose integrand is the product of
f(\zeta)
and the function

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{B(z)\overline{B(\zeta)}}{(1-\overline{\zeta}z)^{2}}+\frac{B(z)\overline{\zeta}}{1-\overline{\zeta}z}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{(\frac{p_{k}}{2}+1)(1-|a_{k}|^{2})\overline{B_{k}(\zeta)}}{(1-a_{k}\overline{\zeta})^{2}}\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})B_{k}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{k}z)B_{k}(a_{k})}K(a_{k},\zeta).\end{split}

Since this function is analytic in
z
and anti-analytic in
\zeta
, it must coincide with
K(z,\zeta)
, and from this expression for the kernel we can continue as in
[
3
]
to derive (
1.14
).

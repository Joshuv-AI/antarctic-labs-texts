Norm principle for even K K -groups of number fields
Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
We investigate the norm maps of algebraic even K K -groups of finite extensions of number fields. Namely, we show that they are surjective in most situations. In the event that they are not surjective, we give a criterion in determining when an element in the even K K -group of the base field comes from a norm of an element from the even K K -groups of the extension field. This latter criterion is only reliant on the real primes of the base field. Keywords and Phrases : Norm maps, even K K -groups. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020 : 11R70, 19D50, 19E20.



1
Introduction

The goal of the paper is study the norm map (also called the transfer map) of
K_{2n}
-groups of finite extension of number fields. The origin of this problem stems from the classical Hasse norm theorem which says that for a cyclic extension of number fields
L/F
, an element in
F^{\times}
is a norm of an element of
L^{\times}
if and only if it is a norm everywhere locally. This can be recast to a statement on
K_{1}
-groups of number fields, namely, a criterion on when an element in
K_{1}(F)
can be realized as a norm of an element of
K_{1}(L)
. Motivated by this interpretation, Bak and Rehmann formulated and proved a
K_{2}
-analog of this statement in
[
1
]
(also see
[
4
,
8
]
). One striking phenomenon was that the
K_{2}
-analog is valid for all finite extensions unlike its classical counterpart. Their results have then led them to the question of higher
K_{m}
-analogs. Although we will not address this in our paper, we should mention that had one replaced the algebraic
K
-groups by Milnor
K
-groups, such norm principles have been established in the works of Orlov-Vishik-Voevodsky
[
11
]
and
Merkurjev-Suslin
[
8
]
. In
[
12
]
, Østvær considered the case of
K_{2n}
for
n\geq 1
, where he succeeded in establishing these higher analog for the
2
-primary part of the
K_{2n}
-group. The goal of the present paper is to complete this study building on ideas of Østvær’s in utilizing tools developed from recent progress on algebraic
K
-theory
[
18
]
.

We begin introducing some preliminary notation to state the main results of this paper. For a ring
R
with identity, write
K_{m}(R)
for the algebraic
K
-groups of
R
in the
sense of Quillen (see
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
). If
F
is a number field, and
v
is a (possibly archimedean) prime of
F
, we let
F_{v}
denote the completion of
F
at
v
. For a finite extension
L
of
F
, denote by
N_{L/F}
the norm map (also called the transfer map)
K_{2n}(L)\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)
on the
K
-groups (cf.
[
19
, Chap. IV, Definition 6.3.2]
).

Let
v
be a real prime of
F
. Denote by
l_{v}:F\longrightarrow F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
the embedding at
v
. This in turn induces a homomorphism
K_{2n}(F)\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F_{v})
which by abuse of notation is also denoted as
l_{v}
. Since
K_{2n}(F)
is a torsion group (for instance, see Lemma
2.1
), the image of
l_{v}
lies in
K_{2n}(F_{v})_{tor}
, where we write
M_{tor}
for the torsion subgroup of an abelian group
M
.

Our main result is as follows (compare with
[
1
, Theorem 2]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
F
. Then we have the following exact sequence

K_{2n}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{L/F}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{2n}(F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{v\in S_{r}}K_{2n}(F_{v})_{tor}\longrightarrow 0,

where
S_{r}
is the set of real primes of
F
ramified in
L
and the map
l
is defined by
l(x)=(l_{v}(x))_{v\in S_{r}}
.

Note that in the event that
n\neq 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
or no real prime of
F
is ramified in
L
, then Theorem
1.1
is saying that
N_{L/F}
is surjective. In other words, every element of
K_{2n}(F)
is automatically a norm of an element in
K_{2n}(L)
in these situations. For the remaining case, we have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
n=1~(mod~4)
and that at least one real prime of
F
is ramified in
L
. Let
x\in K_{2n}(F)
. Then
x
is a norm of an element in
K_{2n}(L)
if and only if
l_{v}(x)=0
for every real prime of
F
which is ramified in
L
.

In particular, Theorem
1.2
is saying that the property of
x
being a norm of an element in
K_{2n}(L)
is more or less reliant only on
F
. To illustrate this point, we record the following. Define
\mathcal{L}
to be the collection of all finite extensions
L
of
F
such that
L
has no real primes. Then the following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose that
n=1~(mod~4)
and that the number field
F
has at least one real prime. Let
x\in K_{2n}(F)
. If there exists an
L_{0}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
x
is a norm of an element in
K_{2n}(L_{0})
, then
x
can be realized as a norm of an element in
K_{2n}(L)
for every
L\in\mathcal{L}
. Vice versa, if there exists an
L_{0}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
x
is not a norm of an element in
K_{2n}(L_{0})
, then
x
cannot be realized as a norm element from
K_{2n}(L)
for any
L\in\mathcal{L}
.

Finally, in view of Theorem
1.2
, it is natural to ask if one has an “explicit” way in determining when
l_{v}(x)=0
for a real prime of
F
that is ramified in
L
. For this, we provide such a possible criterion in the form of Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
for the case
n=1
.

2
Proof of the main results

We shall prove our main results in this section. As a start, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
n\geq 1
, the group
K_{2n}(F)
is a torsion group and has no nonzero
p
-divisible subgroups for all primes
p
.

Proof.

This is certainly well-known. For the convenience of the readers, we sketch an argument. By
[
13
, Theorem 3]
, one has a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K_{2n}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v}K_{2n-1}(k_{v})\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is the ring of integers of
F
and the direct sum runs through all the finite primes of
F
with
k_{v}
being the residue field of
F_{v}
. The calculations of Quillen
[
17
]
and Borel
[
2
]
tell us that
K_{2n}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
is finite, where we note that the finiteness of
K_{2}
was previously established by Garland
[
5
]
. Quillen
[
15
]
has also established the finiteness of
K_{2n-1}(k_{v})
. In view of these deep facts, we see that
K_{2n}(F)
is an extension of a finite group and a sum of finite groups. The assertion of the lemma is now immediate from this.
∎

We now prove the following which is essentially the
p
-primary part of our main results for odd
p
. For a given abelian group
N
, denote by
N[p^{j}]
the subgroup of
N
consisting of elements annihilated by
p^{j}
. One then writes
N[p^{\infty}]=\cup_{j\geq 1}N[p^{j}]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
L/F
is a finite extension of number fields. Then the
norm map

K_{2n}(L)[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)[p^{\infty}]

is surjective for every odd prime
p
. Furthermore, if
L
is a finite Galois extension of
F
, then the norm map induces an isomorphism

\big(K_{2n}(L)[p^{\infty}]\big)_{\Gal(L/F)}\cong K_{2n}(F)[p^{\infty}].

Proof.

Observe that if
M
is a finite Galois extension of
F
containing
L
, it then follows from the transitivity of the norm maps that the surjectivity of
N_{L/F}
is a consequence of the surjectivity of
N_{M/F}
. Hence we might as well assume that
L
is a finite Galois extension of
F
at the start, and to lighten notation, we shall write
G=\Gal(L/F)
. By the Universal Coefficient Theorem
[
19
, Chap. IV Theorem 2.5]
, there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K_{2n+1}(F)/p^{i}\longrightarrow K_{2n+1}(F,\mathbb{Z}/p^{i})\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)[p^{i}]\longrightarrow 0,

where
K_{2n}(F,\mathbb{Z}/p^{i})
is the
K
-group with finite coefficients (for instance, see
[
3
]
or
[
19
, Chap. IV Section 2]
). Taking direct limit, we obtain a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K_{2n+1}(F)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow K_{2n+1}(F,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0.

One has a similar exact sequence for
L
, and together, they fit into the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\big(K_{2n+1}(L)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{2n+1}(L,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\big(K_{2n}(L)[p^{\infty}]\big)_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{2n+1}(F)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{2n+1}(F,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{2n}(F)[p^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

with exact rows and vertical maps induced by the norm maps. We claim that both assertions of the proposition will follow once we can show that the middle vertical map is an isomorphism. Indeed, supposing that for now the middle vertical map is an isomorphism. Then by a snake lemma argument, the rightmost vertical map is surjective with kernel being isomorphic to the cokernel of the leftmost vertical map. On the one hand, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that this kernel is a torsion group with no nonzero
p
-divisible subgroups. On the other hand, being isomorphic to the cokernel of the leftmost vertical map, it has to be
p
-divisible. In conclusion, this forces the kernel to be trivial, and so the rightmost vertical map is an isomorphism yielding the conclusions of the proposition.

Therefore, it remains to verify that the middle vertical map is an isomorphism. For this, we need to recall some more terminologies. Denoting by
\mu_{p^{n}}
the cyclic group generated by a primitive
p^{n}
-root of unity, we then write
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
for the direct limit of the groups
\mu_{p^{n}}
. These have natural
\Gal(\bar{F}/F)
-module structures. Furthermore, for an integer
k\geq 2
, the
k
-fold tensor products
\mu_{p^{n}}^{\otimes k}
and
\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes k}
can be endowed with
\Gal(\bar{F}/F)
-module structure via the diagonal action. For convenience, we shall sometimes write
\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes 1}=\mu_{p^{\infty}}
and
\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes 0}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is understood to have a trivial
\Gal(\bar{F}/F)
-action. Write
H^{i}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{n}}^{\otimes k}\big)=H^{i}_{\acute{e}t}\big(\mathrm{Spec}(F),\mu_{p^{n}}^{\otimes k}\big)
for the étale cohomology groups, where
\mu_{p^{n}}^{\otimes k}
is viewed as an étale sheaf over the scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(F)
in the sense of
[
9
, Chap. II]
. The direct limit of these groups is then denoted by

H^{i}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes k}\big):=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{n}H^{i}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{n}}^{\otimes k}\big).

We can now call upon the (Bloch-Lichtenbaum) motivic spectral
sequence

E^{rs}_{2}=H^{r-s}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{i}}^{\otimes(-s)}\big)\Longrightarrow K_{-r-s}(F,\mathbb{Z}/p^{i})

(cf.
[
19
, Chap VI, Theorem 4.2]
), where
s\leq r\leq 0
. (Strictly speaking, the
E^{rs}_{2}
-terms should be motivic cohomology groups, which is why the spectral sequence is also coined the “motivic-to-
K
-theory spectral sequence”. Of course, we now know that these motivic cohomology groups are isomorphic to the étale cohomology group in this field settings thanks to the work of Rost-Voevodsky
[
18
]
. Interested readers are referred to
[
6
]
and
[
19
, Chap. VI, Historical Remark 4.4]
, and the references therein for the history of this monumental spectral sequence.)
Taking direct limit of the spectral sequence with respect to
i
, we obtain

H^{r-s}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(-s)}\big)\Longrightarrow K_{-r-s}(F,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

It is a standard fact that
H^{k}_{\acute{e}t}\left(F,-\right)=0
for
k\geq 3
(cf.
[
10
, Proposition 8.3.18]
). For
s\leq-2
, we have
H^{2}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(-s)}\big)=0
by an observation of Lichtenbaum
[
7
, Lemma 9.5]
. Hence the spectral sequence degenerates to two diagonal lines
r-s=0
and
r-s=1
. This degenerating supplies an identification

H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(n+1)}\big)\cong K_{2n+1}(F,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

One has a similar identification for
L
. These groups are in turn linked via the Tate spectral sequence
[
10
, Theorem 2.5.3]

H_{r}\left(G,H^{-s}_{\acute{e}t}\big(L,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(n+1)}\big)\right)\Longrightarrow H^{-r-s}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(n+1)}\big).

As seen above,
H^{k}_{\acute{e}t}\big(L,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(n+1)}\big)=0
for
k\geq 2
. Therefore, the initial term of the spectral sequence is at the coordinate
(0,-1)
and reading off this term, we have

H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}\big(L,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(n+1)}\big)_{G}\cong H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}\big(F,\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{\otimes(n+1)}\big),

which gives the required isomorphism. The proof of the proposition is now complete.
∎

It follows immediately from Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
that

\coker\Big(N_{L/F}:K_{2n}(L)\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)\Big)=\coker\Big(N_{L/F}:K_{2n}(L)[2^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)[2^{\infty}]\Big).

It therefore remains to analyse the latter which has been accomplished by Østvær (see
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
), and we shall state his result here.

Theorem 2.3

(Østvær)
.

Suppose that either
n\neq 1~(mod~4)
or no real primes of
F
is ramified in
L
. Then the map
N_{L/F}:K_{2n}(L)[2^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F)[2^{\infty}]
is surjective.

Suppose that
n=1~(mod~4)
and some real primes of
F
is ramified in
L
. Then there
is an exact sequence

K_{2n}(L)[2^{\infty}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{L/F}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{2n}(F)[2^{\infty}]\longrightarrow\prod_{v\in S_{r}}\mathbb{Z}/2\longrightarrow 0,

where
S_{r}
is the set of real primes of
F
ramified in
L
.

We are now in position to prove our main results.

Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

If
F
has no real primes ramifying in
L
, then the direct sum is empty. In the event that
n\neq 1
(mod 4), Suslin’s result
[
14
, Theorem 4.9]
tells us that
K_{2n}(F_{v})_{tor}=0
for every real prime
v
. Therefore, in either of these situations, the exact sequence in question is saying that
N_{L/F}
is surjective. But this follows immediately from a combination of Proposition
2.2
with Theorem
2.3
.

For the remainder of the proof, we may assume that
n=1~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
and some real primes of
F
are ramified in
L
. We now set to establish Theorem
1.2
first.

Suppose that
x\in K_{2n}(F)
is a norm from
K_{2n}(L)
.
Let
v
be any real prime of
F
that is ramified in
L
and let
w
be a complex prime of
L
which lies above
v
. Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{K_{2n}(L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{N_{L/F}}
\textstyle{K_{2n}(L_{w})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{N_{L_{w}/F_{v}}}
\textstyle{K_{2n}(F)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{l_{v}}
\textstyle{K_{2n}(F_{v})}

By Suslin’s result
[
14
, Theorem 4.9]
, the group
K_{2n}(L_{w})\cong K_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
is uniquely divisible and so has no torsion. But as seen in Lemma
2.1
,
K_{2n}(L)
is a torsion group, and so the top horizontal map has to be the trivial homomorphism. Hence it follows that
l_{v}(x)=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
l_{v}(x)=0
for every real prime
v
of
F
is ramified in
L
. Write
x=x_{2}+x^{\prime}
, where
x_{2}\in K_{2n}(F)[2^{\infty}]
and
x^{\prime}
an element of odd order. By Proposition
2.2
, there exists
y^{\prime}\in K_{2n}(L)
such that
N_{L/F}(y^{\prime})=x^{\prime}
. It therefore remains to show that
x_{2}
has a preimage under
N_{L/F}
. For this, we shall make use of Østvær’s criterion (see
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
). In other words, we need to show that the image of
x_{2}
in
K_{2n}(F_{u})[2^{\infty}]
is in the image of the norm map

\bigoplus_{w|u}K_{2n}(L_{w})[2^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F_{u})[2^{\infty}]

for every prime
u
of
F
. However, as seen in
[
12
, Claim (2) in Page 492]
, this norm map is surjective for all primes except at the real primes which are ramified in
L
. But for these remaining primes, since we are assuming that their image in
K_{2n}(F_{v})[2^{\infty}]
is trivial, they are automatically in the image of the norm map. Thus, we may apply Østvær’s criterion to conclude that
x_{2}
comes from the norm of an element in
K_{2}(L)[2^{\infty}]
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We finally come to proving Theorem
1.1
for the remaining case. Observe that Theorem
1.2
is saying that the sequence in question is exact at
K_{2n}(F)
. This in turn implies that the homomorphism
l
induces an injection

\coker(N_{L/F})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S_{r}}K_{2n}(F_{v})_{tor}.

Now, by virtue of Proposition
2.2
, Theorem
2.3
and Suslin’s result
[
14
, Theorem 4.9]
, these two groups have the same order, and so the injection has to be an isomorphism. Consequently, the homomorphism
l
is surjective, as required. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is therefore complete.
∎

3
Further Remarks

In view of Theorem
1.2
, it would be of interest to examine the homomorphism
l_{v}:K_{2n}(F)\longrightarrow K_{2n}(F_{v})
for a real prime
v
of
F
in greater depth. In particular, we would like to understand when an element
x\in K_{2n}(F)
is mapped to zero in
K_{2n}(F_{v})
. We describe this for the case of
n=1
. At this point of writing, we do not know how to approach this problem for
n>1
with
n=1
(mod 4).

As before, let
F
be a number field and
v
a real prime of
F
. For
f,g\in F^{\times}
, consider the element
\{f,g\}\in K_{2}(F)
, where
\{-,-\}
is the Steinberg symbol in
F
. Under the localization map
K_{2}(F)\longrightarrow K_{2}(F_{v})
, the symbol
\{f,g\}
is mapped to
\{l_{v}(f),l_{v}(g)\}_{v}
, where
\{-,-\}_{v}
is the Steinberg symbol in
F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
. As seen previously, the element
\{l_{v}(f),l_{v}(g)\}_{v}
must lie in
K_{2}(F_{v})_{tor}=\mathbb{Z}/2
. Furthermore, it follows from
[
19
, Chap. III, Example 6.2.1]
that
\{l_{v}(f),l_{v}(g)\}_{v}\neq 0
if and only if
l_{v}(f),l_{v}(g)\in F_{v}=\mathbb{R}

are both negative. We can now record these findings in the form of the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
L/F
is a finite extension of number fields. Let
f,g\in F^{\times}
. Then
\{f,g\}\in K_{2}(F)
is a norm of an element in
K_{2}(L)
if and only if for every real prime
v
of
F
that is ramified in
L
, at least one of
l_{v}(f)
and
l_{v}(g)
is non-negative.

The preceding proposition gives a criterion in determining when a symbol can be realized as a norm. In principle, we can build on these ideas to determine whether an arbitrary element
x
of
K_{2}(F)
is a norm element. Indeed, by Matsumoto’s theorem
[
19
, Chap. III, Theorem 6.1]
, the element
x
can be expressed as a finite product of Steinberg symbols
\{f,g\}
. For each real prime
v
, we have seen above that
l_{v}(\{f,g\})\neq 0
if and only if
l_{v}(f)
and
l_{v}(g)
are negative. But as long as the number of symbols with both
l_{v}(f)
and
l_{v}(g)
being negative is even, we still have
l_{v}(x)=0
. We therefore record our observation as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
L/F
be a finite extension of number fields. Suppose that
x\in K_{2}(F)
has the following representation

x=\prod_{i\in I}\{f_{i},g_{i}\},

where
I
is a finite indexing set. Then
x
is a norm of an element in
K_{2}(L)
if and only if for each real prime
v
of
F
that ramifies in
L
, the number of symbols with both
l_{v}(f_{i})
and
l_{v}(g_{i})
being negative is even.

We end with some examples to illustrate these propositions. We should mention that the propositions and examples in this section could have been
deduced from the work of Bak-Rehmann (we thank the referees for pointing this out).

(1)

Let
L
be a number field with at least one pair of complex primes. Then it follows from Proposition
3.1
that
\{-1,-1\}\in K_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
cannot be realized as a norm of an element in
K_{2}(L)
.

(2)

The symbol
\{3,-1\}\in K_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
can be realized as a norm of an element in
K_{2}\big(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})\big)
. It is interesting to note that the elements
3
and
-1
themselves are not norm elements of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
.

(3)

Let
m
be a squarefree integer
>1
. A classical result of Bass-Tate (for instance, see
[
19
, Chap. III, Lemma 6.1.4]
) asserts that
K_{2}\big(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})\big)
is generated by symbols of the form
\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\}
and
\{c,d\}
with
a,c,d\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and
b\in\mathbb{Q}
. In the remaining discussion, we shall concentrate on symbols of the form
\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\}
.

If
a>0
, then
l_{v}(\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\})=0
for every real prime
v
of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
.

Now suppose that
a<0
. We shall write
l_{v}
and
l_{v^{\prime}}
for the embedding of
F
to
\mathbb{R}
given by
\sqrt{m}\mapsto\sqrt{m}
and
\sqrt{m}\mapsto-\sqrt{m}
respectively. We then consider three cases: (i)
\sqrt{m}>b
, (ii)
-\sqrt{m}<b<\sqrt{m}
and (iii)
b<-\sqrt{m}
. In case (i), one easily checks that
l_{v}(\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\})=0
and
l_{v^{\prime}}(\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\})=0
. In case (ii), we see that
l_{v}(\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\})=0
but
l_{v^{\prime}}(\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\})\neq 0
. Finally, for case (iii), we have
l_{v}(\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\})\neq 0
and
l_{v^{\prime}}(\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\})\neq 0
.

As a further illustration, we let
L
be an extension of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
at which both real primes of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
are ramified (for instance, one may take
L
to be
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m},\sqrt{-1})
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m},\zeta_{5})
etc.). Then the above discussion shows that
\{a,\sqrt{m}-b\}
is a norm element from
K_{2}(L)
if and only if either
a>0
or
\sqrt{m}>b
.

(4)

Let
m
be a cubefree integer
>1
. Then the field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{m})
has only one real prime which is the inclusion
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{m})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Let
L
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{m})
at which this unique real prime is ramified. Then the symbol
\{f,g\}\in K_{2}\big(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{m})\big)
can be realized as a norm of an element in
K_{2}(L)
if and only if either
f>0
or
g>0
.

Abstract
. In this note we introduce the concept of reflective projective varieties. These are stratified projective varieties with certain dimension constraints on their dual varieties. We prove that for such varieties, the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes of the strata completely determine the local Euler obstructions and the polar degrees. We also propose an algorithm to compute the local Euler obstructions when such varieties are formed by group orbits. As examples we compute the local Euler obstructions of quadratic hypersurfaces and ordinary determinantal varieties to illustrate our method.

2.1.
Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson Classes

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a projective variety. The group of constructible function is defined as the abelian group generated by indicator functions
\mathds{1}_{V}
for all irreducible subvarieties
V\subset X
. We define the pushforward for a proper morphism
f\colon X\to Y
as follows. For any closed subvariety
V\subset X
, the pushforward
F(f)(\mathds{1}_{V})(y)
evaluates
\chi(f^{-1}(y)\cap V)
for any
y\in Y
.
This makes
F
a functor from projective complex varieties to the Abelian group category. The group
F(X)
has
\{\mathds{1}_{V}\}
as a natural base.

When the base field is
\mathbb{C}
,
in 1974 MacPherson defined a local measurement for singularities and
names it the local Euler obstruction. He
proved that the local Euler obstruction functions
\{Eu_{V}|\text{V is a subvariety of X}\}
also form a base for
F(X)
. He also constructed a natural transformation
c_{*}
by sending the local Euler obstruction function
Eu_{V}
to the Chern-Mather class
c_{M}(V)
. The precise definition is left to the next subsection.
The following theorem answered a conjecture by Deligne and Grothendieck:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

The map
c_{*}
defined above is the unique natural transform from
F
to the homology functor
H_{*}
satisfying the following normalization property:
c_{*}(\mathds{1}_{X})=c(TX)\cap[X]
when
X
is smooth.

In this subsection we briefly review the definition of local Euler obstruction and Chern-Mather class, which play central role in the construction of

[

Let
\iota\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{N}

\hat{X}:=\text{closure of }\{(x,T_{x}X)|x\text{ is a smooth point}\}\subset G_{d}(T\mathbb{P}^{N})

The projection map
p\colon\hat{X}\to X

Definition
.

The local Euler obstruction of

Here

[

is defined as

c_{M}^{X}:=\iota_{*}p_{*}(c(\mathcal{T}_{X})\cap[\hat{X}])\/.

Recall that the Chow group(ring) of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
is
\mathbb{Z}[H]/H^{N+1}
, where

here is the hyperplane class
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))\cap[\mathbb{P}^{N}]
.

Definition
.

The Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class of
X
, denoted by

is defined as the pushforward
\iota_{*}c_{*}(\mathds{1}_{X})
in
A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{N})
.

Notice that when
X
is smooth, the Chern-Mather class and the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class all equal to the total Chern class
i_{*}(c(TX)\cap[X])
.

Proposition 2.3
.

We have the following properties.

(1)

Eu_{X}(x)
is a local invariant, thus only depends on an open neighborhood of

in
X
.

(2)

If

(3)

(4)

The Euler obstruction has the product property, i.e.,
Eu_{X\times Y}(x\times y)=Eu_{X}(x)\times Eu_{Y}(y)

Proposition 2.4

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
be a projective variety, and let

This is because that locally around

3.
Local Euler Obstructions of Reflective Projective Varieties

In this section we consider a special type of projective varieties: the reflective varieties.

3.1.
Main Result

Definition
.

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
N
.
We say a projective variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
is
reflective
if
X
admits a finite stratification
X=\cup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, such that

(1)

X_{n}=\bar{X}_{n}\subset\bar{X}_{n-1}\subset\cdots\bar{X}_{2}\subset\bar{X}_{1}=X
;

(2)

The dimension of the dual varieties
\bar{X}_{i}^{\vee}
satisfy the following inequalities:

\dim X^{\vee}=\dim\bar{X}^{\vee}_{0}<\dim\bar{X}^{\vee}_{1}<\cdots<\dim\bar{X}^{\vee}_{n}\/.

(3)

Y=X^{\vee}=\cup_{j=1}^{m}Y_{j}
form a finite stratification.

Example 1
.

Let
X_{A}\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
be a quadratic hypersurface defined by
x^{t}Ax=0
for some symmetric
n\times n
matrix
A
. The hypersurface
X_{A}
is smooth if and only if
|A|\neq 0
, and the dual variety
X_{A}^{\vee}
is also a smooth hypersurface. When
A
is singular of rank
k<n
, as shown in
[
11
]
, the dual variety
X_{A}^{\vee}
is a smooth quadratic hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
. The singularity locus
S_{A}
of
X_{A}
is given by the solution of the linear system
AX=0
, thus is a dimension
n-r-1
linear space. The dual space of
S_{A}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. This shows that the singular quadratic hypersurfaces are reflective.

Theorem 3.1
.

We denote
I=I_{N-1}
to be Aluffi’s projective duality involution.
Assume that there are not-all-zero integers
\{\alpha_{i}|i=1,\cdots,n\}
and
\{\beta_{j}|j=0,\cdots,m\}
such that the following involution equality holds.

I\left(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\alpha_{r}\cdot c_{sm}^{X_{r}}\right)=\sum_{s=1}^{m}\beta_{s}\cdot c_{sm}^{Y_{s}};\quad\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}=1\/.

Then we have

\alpha_{i}=Eu_{X}(X_{i});\quad\beta_{i}=Eu_{Y}(Y_{i})\/.

Proof.

We define
\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}_{i}}(H):=(-1)^{\dim X_{i}}c_{M}^{\bar{X}_{i}}(H)
to be the sighed Chern-Mather polynomials.
Since the local Euler obstructions form a basis for the constructible function group,
there are unique integers
\{E_{r,i}\}
and
\{D_{r,j}\}
such that

E_{r,r}=(-1)^{\dim X_{r}}
,
D_{s,s}=(-1)^{\dim Y_{s}}
and

c_{sm}^{X_{r}}(H)=\sum_{i=r}^{n}E_{r,i}\cdot\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}_{i}}(H);\quad c_{sm}^{Y_{s}}(H)=\sum_{j=s}^{m}D_{s,j}\cdot\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{j}}(H)\/.

The involution equality can then be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{j}\beta_{s}D_{s,j}\right)\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{m}\sum_{j=s}^{m}\beta_{s}D_{s,j}\cdot\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{j}}=\sum_{s\geq m}\beta_{s}\cdot c_{sm}^{Y_{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\left(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\alpha_{r}\cdot c_{sm}^{X_{r}}\right)=I\left(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\sum_{i=r}^{n}\alpha_{r}E_{r,i}\cdot\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n}\sum_{i=r}^{n}\alpha_{r}E_{r,i}\cdot I(\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}_{i}})=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\sum_{i=r}^{n}\alpha_{r}E_{r,i}\cdot\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}^{\vee}_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{i}\alpha_{r}E_{r,i}\right)\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}^{\vee}_{i}}

Thus we obtain the following equality

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{j}\beta_{s}D_{s,j}\right)\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{i}\alpha_{r}E_{r,i}\right)\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}^{\vee}_{i}}\/.

Recall that we have an increasing chain of dimensions from the definition of reflective projective variety:

\dim\bar{Y}_{m}<\dim\bar{Y}_{m-1}<\cdots<\dim\bar{Y}_{1}=\dim\bar{X}^{\vee}_{1}<\dim\bar{X}^{\vee}_{2}<\cdots<\dim\bar{X}^{\vee}_{n}

First we look at the highest dimension term
\bar{Y}_{m}
. Among all the polynomials
\{\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{X}^{\vee}_{i}};\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{j}}\}
, the polynomial
\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{m}}
is the only polynomial that contains
[\mathbb{P}^{\dim Y_{m}}]
. Thus we have
\sum_{s=1}^{m}\beta_{s}D_{s,m}=0
.
Since the coefficient of
\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{m}}
is
0
, then the polynomial
\hat{c}_{M}^{\bar{Y}_{m-1}}
is the only polynomial that contains
[\mathbb{P}^{\dim Y_{m-1}}]
. This forces
\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}\beta_{s}D_{s,m-1}=0
.

Continue this process until we meet
\bar{Y}_{1}=\bar{X}^{\vee}_{1}
. At this level we obtain

\beta_{1}D_{1,1}=\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1}E_{1,1}=\alpha_{1}
. Then we repeat the above procedure on the dimension of
X_{r}
’s, eventually we obtain the following linear system:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\beta_{j}D_{j,r}=0&s=2,3,\cdots,m\\
\beta_{1}D_{1,1}=\alpha_{1}E_{1,1}=1&\\
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}E_{i,r}=0&r=2,3,\cdots,m\end{cases}

Written into matrix form we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{1,m}&D_{2,m}&\cdots&D_{m-1,m}&D_{m,m}\\
D_{1,m-1}&D_{2,2}&\cdots&D_{m-1,m-1}&0\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
D_{1,1}&0&\cdots&0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{1}\\
\beta_{2}\\
\cdots\\
\beta_{m-1}\\
\beta_{m}\end{bmatrix}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
\cdots\\
0\\
1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}E_{1,n}&E_{2,n}&\cdots&E_{n-1,n}&E_{n,n}\\
E_{1,n-1}&E_{2,2}&\cdots&D_{n-1,n-1}&0\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
E_{1,1}&0&\cdots&0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\\
\cdots\\
\alpha_{m-1}\\
\alpha_{m}\end{bmatrix}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
\cdots\\
0\\
1\end{bmatrix}

The two matrices are both of full rank, since that have diagonal entries
D_{s,s}=E_{r,r}=1
for any
r,s
, thus the solution of
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m}\}
is unique. Meanwhile, notice that
\{\alpha_{i}=Eu_{X}(X_{i});\beta_{j}=Eu_{Y}(Y_{j})\}
is indeed a solution: the involution does interchanges the signed Chern-Mather classes between dual varieties. Thus by the uniqueness of the solution we completes the proof.
∎

This theorem shows that for reflective projective varieties, the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes of the strata completely determine the local Euler obstructions. Since the Chern-Mather classes are linear sums of Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes with weights being local Euler obstructions, they also determine the Chern-Mather classes. Moreover, as shown in
[
21
]
, the coefficients of the Chern-Mather classes are equivalent to the polar degrees, i.e., degrees of the polar varieties. This gives us the following.

Corollary 1
.

For a reflective projective variety
X=\sqcup X_{i}
with dual variety
X^{\vee}=Y=\sqcup Y_{j}
, the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes of the strata
\{X_{i}\}
and
\{Y_{j}\}
completely determine the local Euler obstructions
Eu_{X}(X_{i})
and
Eu_{Y}(Y_{j})
. Moreover, they completely determine the Chern-Mather classes
c_{M}^{X}
and
c_{M}^{Y}
, and thus the polar degrees
P_{k}(X)
and
P_{k}(Y)
.

3.2.
On Reflective Group Orbits

A common example of reflective projective varieties comes from group actions.
Let
G
be a connected algebraic group and
V
be a linear
G
representation of dimension
N
with finite orbits
\mathcal{O}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{s}
. From Proposition
2.7
we can label the dual orbits by the duality correspondence:
\mathbb{P}(\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{k})
is dual to
\mathbb{P}(\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{s-k})
.

Definition
.

We call such representation
V

reflective
if the following assumption is satisfied.

(1)

The
G
action contains the scalar multiplication: the orbits are
k^{*}
cones.

(2)

For any
i
we have
\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{i}=\cup_{j\geq i}\mathcal{O}_{j}
and
\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{i}=\cup_{j\geq i}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{j}
.

(3)

All the orbits
\mathcal{O}_{i}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{i}
are purely dimensional.

The assumptions forces
\mathcal{O}_{s}=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{s}=\{0\}
.
We call such group orbits
Reflective Orbits
.

Corollary 2
.

For any
i
we denote
S_{i}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
and
S^{\prime}_{i}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{i})
to be the projectivized orbits in
\mathbb{P}(V)
and
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
. Let
I=I_{N-1}
be Aluffi’s projective duiality involution. Let
c_{sm}^{S_{i}}=c_{sm}^{S_{i}}(H)
and
c_{sm}^{S^{\prime}_{i}}=c_{sm}^{S^{\prime}_{i}}(H)
be the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson polynomials in
H
.
For any
r
, assume that there are not-all-zeros integers
\{\alpha^{r}_{i}|i=r,\cdots,s-1\}
and
\{\beta^{r}_{j}|j=s-r,\cdots,s-1\}
such that

I_{N-1}\left(\sum_{i\geq r}\alpha^{r}_{i}\cdot c_{sm}^{S_{i}}\right)=\sum_{k\geq s-r}\beta^{r}_{i}\cdot c_{sm}^{S^{\prime}_{k}};\quad\alpha^{r}_{r}=\beta^{r}_{s-r}=1\/.

Then we have

\alpha^{r}_{i}=Eu_{\bar{S}_{r}}(S_{i});\quad\beta^{r}_{i}=Eu_{\bar{S}^{\prime}_{s-r}}(S^{\prime}_{i})\/.

Proof.

Denote
m_{i}
and
m^{\prime}_{j}
to be the sighed Chern-Mather class polynomials:
m_{i}:=(-1)^{\dim S_{i}}c_{M}^{\bar{S}_{i}}(H)
and
m^{\prime}_{j}:=(-1)^{\dim S^{\prime}_{j}}c_{M}^{\bar{S}^{\prime}_{j}}(H)
. For any
r
there are unique integers
E_{r,i}
and
E^{\prime}_{s-r,j}
such that
E_{r,r}=(-1)^{\dim S_{r}}
,
E^{\prime}_{s-r,s-r}=(-1)^{\dim S^{\prime}_{s-r}}
and

\displaystyle c_{sm}^{S_{r}}(H)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r}^{s-1}E_{r,i}\cdot(-1)^{\dim S_{i}}c_{M}^{\bar{S}_{i}}(H)=\sum_{i=r}^{s-1}E_{r,i}m_{i};

\displaystyle c_{sm}^{S^{\prime}_{s-r}}(H)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=s-r}^{s-1}E^{\prime}_{s-r,j}\cdot(-1)^{\dim S^{\prime}_{i}}c_{M}^{\bar{S}^{\prime}_{i}}(H)=\sum_{j=s-r}^{s-1}E^{\prime}_{r,i}m^{\prime}_{j}\/.

Since
\bar{S}_{k}
is dual to
\bar{S}^{\prime}_{s-k}
, recall from Proposition
2.3
that the involution
I_{N-1}
then takes
m_{k}
to
m^{\prime}_{s-k}
.
Thus the involution equality can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq s-r}\beta^{r}_{k}\cdot c_{sm}^{S_{k}}(H)=\sum_{k=s-r}^{s-1}\sum_{j=s-r}^{k}\beta^{r}_{i}E^{\prime}_{j,k}m^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{N-1}\left(\sum_{k\geq r}\alpha^{r}_{k}\cdot c_{sm}^{S_{k}}(H)\right)=I_{N-1}\left(\sum_{k=r}^{s-1}\alpha^{r}_{k}\cdot\sum_{i=k}^{s-1}E_{k,i}m_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=r}^{s-1}\alpha^{r}_{k}\cdot\sum_{i=k}^{s-1}E_{k,i}m^{\prime}_{s-i}=\sum_{k=1}^{s-r}\sum_{i=r}^{s-k}\alpha^{r}_{i}E_{i,s-k}m^{\prime}_{k}

Since
\alpha^{r}_{r}=\beta^{r}_{s-r}=1
, we have

m^{\prime}_{s-r}+\sum_{k=s-r+1}^{s}\sum_{i=s-r}^{k}\beta^{r}_{j}E^{\prime}_{j,k}m^{\prime}_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{s-r-1}\sum_{i=r}^{s-k}\alpha^{r}_{i}E_{i,s-k}m^{\prime}_{k}+m^{\prime}_{s-r}\/.

The corollary then follows from the same argument used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

An advantage of reflective group orbits is that,
as pointed out in
[
8
]
[
22
]
, besides the classical approach from intersection theory the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes of such group orbits can also be obtained from representation theory. This provides more choices in practice.
In this case we have the following algorithm to compute the local Euler obstructions:

Algorithm 1

(Algorithm for Reflective Group Orbits)
.

The following algorithm computed the local Euler obstructions for Reflective Group Orbits.

Step 0

For each pairs of orbits
\mathcal{O}_{i}
and
\mathcal{O}_{j}
, notice that

Eu_{\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{i}}(\mathcal{O}_{j})=Eu_{\bar{S}_{i}}(S_{j})
passes to the projective setting for
j\leq s
.

Step 1

For each projectivized orbit
S_{i}
we compute its Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class
c_{sm}^{S_{i}}(H)
.

Step 2

For each projectivized orbit
S^{\prime}_{i}
we compute its Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class
c_{sm}^{S^{\prime}_{i}}(H)
.

Step 3

For each
r
we set up the following linear system

\displaystyle\sum_{k=r}^{s-1}x^{r}_{k}c_{sm}^{S_{k}}(H)-\sum_{k=s-r}^{s-1}y^{r}_{k}c_{sm}^{S^{\prime}_{k}}(H)=0;x^{r}_{r}=y^{r}_{s-r}=1\/.

This is a linear system since
c_{sm}^{S_{k}}(H)

and
c_{sm}^{S^{\prime}_{k}}(H)

are polynomials in
H
, and the equality gives at least
s+1
linear equations concerning the coefficients of powers of
H
. As proved in the previous Proposition, the solution
\{x^{r}_{k},y^{r}_{k}\}
are the local Euler obstructions of
\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{r}
and
\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{s-r}
at each stratum.

Step 4

The local Euler obstruction of
\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{r}
at
\mathcal{O}_{s}=\{0\}
come from the algebraic Brasselet-Lê-Seade type formula proved in
[
30
]
.

Eu_{\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{r}}(0)=\sum_{k\geq r}(-1)^{\dim S_{k}}c_{sm}^{S_{k}}(-1)\cdot Eu_{\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{r}}(\mathcal{O}_{k})\/.

Same argument applies to
Eu_{\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{s-r}}(0)
.

Remark 3
.

More precisely, in
[
8
]
[
22
]
they proved that the equivariant Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes are characterized by certain Axioms. The equivariant classes are expressed as polynomials in the weights of the
G
action,
the axioms then provide constraint equations the Chern polynomials have to satisfy by studying localizations and specializations/restrictions. The solutions are certain weight functions from representation theory.
In fact, in
[
8
]
they proved that for such orbits the solutions correspond to the
K
-theoretic stable envelops studied in
[
18
]
. Thus this algorithm provides a way to study the singularities of orbits closures directly from representation theory.

Example 2
.

We use the following example to illustrate the algorithm.
We consider the symmetric rank stratification
M^{S}_{3}=\cup_{i=0}^{3}\Sigma^{S\circ}_{3,i}
. From the computation in
[
29
]
we have

\displaystyle c_{sm}^{\tau^{S\circ}_{3,0}}=

\displaystyle 3H^{4}+6H^{3}+6H^{2}+3H+1

\displaystyle c_{sm}^{\tau^{S\circ}_{3,1}}=

\displaystyle 3H^{5}+6H^{4}+10H^{3}+9H^{2}+3H;\quad c_{sm}^{\tau^{S\circ}_{3,1}}(-1)=-1

\displaystyle c_{sm}^{\tau^{S}_{3,2}}=

\displaystyle 3H^{5}+6H^{4}+4H^{3};\quad c_{sm}^{\tau^{S}_{3,2}}(-1)=-1

The orbits
\tau^{S}_{3,1}
is dual to
\tau^{S}_{3,2}
, with dimension
7
and
4
respectively.
Then we have

c_{sm}^{\tau^{S\circ}_{3,1}}(-1-H)+((1+H)^{6}-H^{6})+x\cdot c_{sm}^{\tau^{S}_{3,2}}(-1-H)+x\cdot((1+H)^{6}-H^{6})=c_{sm}^{\tau^{S}_{3,2}}(H)\/.

Expand the polynomials we get

3H^{5}+6H^{4}+4H^{3}+x\cdot(3H^{5}+6H^{4}+10H^{3}+9H^{2}+3H)=3H^{5}+6H^{4}+4H^{3}\/.

There is a unique solution
x=0
. This shows that
Eu_{\Sigma^{S}_{3,1}}(\Sigma^{S\circ}_{3,2})=0
. The Brasselet-Lê-Seade formula then gives

Eu_{\Sigma^{S}_{3,1}}(0)=Eu_{\Sigma^{S}_{3,1}}(\Sigma^{S\circ}_{3,1})\cdot c_{sm}^{\tau^{S\circ}_{3,1}}(-1)+Eu_{\Sigma^{S}_{3,1}}(\Sigma^{S\circ}_{3,2})\cdot c_{sm}^{\tau^{S}_{3,2}}(-1)=(-1)\cdot(-1)+(-1)\cdot 0=1\/.

4.
Application I: Quadratic Hypersurfaces

Let
X_{A}\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
be a quadratic hypersurface defined by
x^{t}Ax=0
for some symmetric
n+1\times n+1
matrix
A
. Here
\dim V=n+1
. The hypersurface
X_{A}\subset\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
is smooth if and only if
|A|\neq 0
, and the dual variety
X_{A}^{\vee}
is also a smooth hypersurface. When
A
is singular of rank
r\leq n
, the singular locus
S_{A}
is the projective subspace
\mathbb{P}(K)=\mathbb{P}^{n-r}
, where
K=\ker A\subset V
.

When
r=1
, the defining equation of
X_{A}
is given by
l^{2}
for some linear form
l
. Thus the reduced structure of
X_{A}
is smooth: it is a hyperplane. When
r=2
, the defining equation of
X_{A}
is given by
l_{1}\cdot l_{2}
for two distinct linear forms
l_{1},l_{2}
. Thus the hypersurface
X_{A}
is the union of two hyperplanes intersecting transversely at
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
. After taking normal slice this case is the nothing but the line arrangement
V(xy=0)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
So we will assume
r\geq 3
in this section.

Let
K^{\perp}\subset V^{*}
be the space of linear forms that vanish on
K
.
As shown in
[
11
]
, the dual variety
X_{A}^{\vee}
is a smooth
quadratic hypersurface in the linear subspace
\mathbb{P}(K^{\perp})=\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
. The dual variety
S_{A}^{\vee}
is isomorphic to the linear subspace
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
.

Remark 4
.

In general, let
A
be a
m\times n
matrix with
m\geq n
, we may consider the hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{m+n-1}
of the form
X^{t}AY=0
. This hypersurface is exactly the hypersurface
X_{B}
, where
B
is the
(m+n)\times(m+n)
symmetric matrix

\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A^{t}&0\end{bmatrix}\/.

Corollary 3
.

Let
r=r(A)
be the rank of
A
. We assume that
r\geq 3
.

(1)

Let
\Sigma_{A}\subset V
be the affine cone of
X_{A}
.
The Euler characteristic of the complex link space of
\Sigma_{A}
at
0
is
-2
.

(2)

The Milnor number of
X_{A}
at any point of
S_{A}
equals
(-1)^{n+r}
.

(3)

The Milnor class of
X_{A}
is given by

\mathcal{M}(X_{A})=(-1)^{n+r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{n-r+1}{k-j}(-2)^{j}\right)\cdot H^{k+r}

(4)

We denote
X_{A}^{\circ}:=X_{A}\setminus S_{A}
. The local Euler obstruction of
X_{A}
then equals:

Eu_{X_{A}}=\mathds{1}_{X_{A}^{\circ}}+((-1)^{r}+1)\mathds{1}_{S_{A}}\/.

Proof.

The Milnor class of
X_{A}
can be uniquely written as

(1)

\mathcal{M}(X_{A})=(-1)^{n-1}\left(\frac{2H((1+H)^{n+1}-H^{n+1})}{1+2H}-c_{sm}^{X_{A}}(H)\right)=\frac{\mu\cdot c_{sm}^{S_{A}}}{1+2H}\/.

Let
e=Eu_{X_{A}}(S_{A})
be the local Euler obstruction of
X_{A}
at
S_{A}
, and we can uniquely write

c_{sm}^{X_{A}}=c_{M}^{X_{A}}-(e-1)c_{M}^{S_{A}}=c_{M}^{X_{A}}-(e-1)c_{sm}^{S_{A}}\/.

We denote
\alpha:=e-1
,
\lambda:=c_{sm}^{X_{A}}(-1)
and
B_{n}:=(1+H)^{n+1}-H^{n+1}
.
Then we consider the following polynomial:

P:=(-1)^{n-1}\left(2HB_{n}-(1+2H)(c_{M}^{X_{A}}-\alpha\cdot c_{M}^{S_{A}})\right)-\mu c_{M}^{S_{A}}\/.

From (
1
) we know that
P=0
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{n}
be the duality involution, then we have
\mathcal{I}_{n}(P)=0
.

On the other hand, we can directly compute
I_{n}(P)
. From Proposition
2.5
we have:

\mathcal{I}_{n}\left(2H(c_{M}^{X_{A}}-\alpha\cdot c_{M}^{S_{A}})\right)=(-2-2H)\mathcal{I}_{n}(c_{M}^{X_{A}}-\alpha\cdot c_{M}^{S_{A}})-\lambda HB_{n}\/.

Meanwhile, by a direct computation we have

\mathcal{I}_{n}(HB_{n})=(-1)^{n+1}HB_{n}\/.

Thus we have the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n}(P)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\mathcal{I}_{n}(2HB_{n})-\mathcal{I}_{n}(c_{M}^{X_{A}}-\alpha c_{M}^{S_{A}})-\mathcal{I}_{n}(2H(c_{M}^{X_{A}}-\alpha c_{M}^{S_{A}}))-\mathcal{I}_{n}(\mu c_{M}^{S_{A}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2HB_{n}-(-1)^{n-1}\left((-1)^{n+r-1}c_{M}^{X^{\vee}_{A}}-(-1)^{n-1}\alpha c_{M}^{S^{\vee}_{A}}\right)+(-1)^{n-1}\lambda HB_{n}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}(2+2H)\left((-1)^{n+r-1}c_{M}^{X^{\vee}_{A}}-(-1)^{n-1}\alpha c_{M}^{S^{\vee}_{A}}\right)-(-1)^{n-1}\mu c_{M}^{S^{\vee}_{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2+(-1)^{n-1}\lambda)HB_{n}+(-1)^{r}(1+2H)c_{M}^{X^{\vee}_{A}}-(1+2H)\alpha c_{M}^{S^{\vee}_{A}}-(-1)^{n-1}\mu c_{M}^{S^{\vee}_{A}}\ =

\displaystyle 0

Notice that
X_{A}^{\vee}
is a smooth
quadratic hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
, and
S_{A}^{\vee}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
. Thus we have

c_{M}(X_{A}^{\vee})=c_{sm}^{X_{A}^{\vee}}=\frac{2H^{n-r+2}(1+H)^{r}}{1+2H};\quad c_{M}(S_{A}^{\vee})=c_{sm}^{S_{A}^{\vee}}=H^{n-r+1}(1+H)^{r}\/.

Thus we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle(2+(-1)^{n-1}\lambda)H\left((1+H)^{n+1}-H^{n+1}\right)-(-1)^{n-1}\mu H^{n-r+1}(1+H)^{r}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{r}2H^{n-r+2}(1+H)^{r}-(1+2H)\alpha H^{n-r+1}(1+H)^{r}\/.

This gives the solution

\lambda=(-1)^{n}\cdot 2;\quad e=\alpha+1=(-1)^{r}+1;\quad\mu=(-1)^{n+r}\/.

∎

5.
Application II: Generic Determinantal Varieties

The (generic) determinantal varieties are fundamental examples of non-isolated singularities, and are of great interest in singularity theory. For ordinary determinantal varieties the local Euler obtructions were computed in
[
10
]
using topological method, and in
[
28
]
for arbitrary algebraically closed field using intersection theory. In this section we use Corollary
2
to give a very short proof, from an observation on the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class formula.

5.0.1.
q
-Polynomial of Determinantal Varieties

Let
K
be a characteristic
0
algebraically closed field.
Let
M_{m,n}=Hom(K^{n},K^{n})
be the space of
n\times n
ordinary matrices .
The group
GL_{n}(K)\times GL_{n}(K)
acts on
M_{n,n}
by
(P,X,Q)\mapsto PXQ^{-1}
. The orbits consist of matrices of fixed rank. For
0\leq k\leq n-1
we denote them by

\Sigma^{\circ}_{n,k}:=\{X|\dim\ker X=k\};\quad\Sigma_{n,k}:=\{X|\dim\ker X\geq k\}\/.

They are algebraic varieties of dimension
n^{2}-k^{2}
.
We denote the projections
\mathbb{P}(\Sigma^{\circ}_{n,k})
and
\mathbb{P}(\Sigma_{n,k})
by
\tau^{\circ}_{n,k}
and
\tau_{n,k}
respectively.

As shown in
[
29
]
, we define the
Q
classes in the Grassmannian
G(r,n)
:

q_{n,r}(d):=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n(n-r)}(1+d)^{n(n-r)-k}c_{k}(Q^{\vee n})\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{nr}d^{nr-k}c_{k}(S^{\vee n})\right)\/;

and the
q
polynomial in variable
q_{n,r}(H)
by setting

q_{n,r}(d):=\int_{G(r,n)}c(S^{\vee}\otimes Q)\cdot q_{n,r}(d)\cap[G(r,n)]-d^{n^{2}}\binom{n}{r}

for integers
d
.
The Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes
c_{sm}^{\tau^{\circ}_{n,k}}
are then given by

c_{sm}^{\tau_{n,k}^{\circ}}(H)=\sum_{r=k}^{n-1}(-1)^{r-k}\binom{r}{k}\cdot q_{n,r}(H)\/.

5.0.2.
Local Euler Obstruction of Determinantal Varieties

Despite the complicated form, as we will show later, the
q_{n,r}(d)
, enjoys very nice symmetric property: it is symmetric with the
d\mapsto-1-d
substitution. This induces the following interesting application:

Corollary 4
.

The local Euler obstruction function of determinantal varieties are given by

Eu_{\Sigma_{n,k}}(\Sigma^{\circ}_{n,r})=\binom{r}{k}\/.

Thus the polynomials
q_{n,r}(H)
equal the Chern-Mather classes:

q_{n,r}(H)=c_{M}^{\tau_{n,k}}(H)\/.

Proof.

Substituting
d
by
-1-d
in the expression of
q_{n,r}(d)
we have the following:

\displaystyle q_{n,r}(-1-d)+(-1-d)^{n^{2}}\binom{n}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G(r,n)}c(S^{\vee}\otimes Q)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n(n-r)}(-d)^{n(n-r)-k}c_{k}(Q^{\vee n})\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{nr}(-1-d)^{nr-k}c_{k}(S^{\vee n})\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G(n-r,n)}c(S^{\vee}\otimes Q)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n(n-r)}(-d)^{n(n-r)-k}c_{k}(S^{n})\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{nr}(-1-d)^{nr-k}c_{k}(Q^{n})\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n^{2}}\int_{G(n-r,n)}c(S^{\vee}\otimes Q)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n(n-r)}d^{n(n-r)-k}c_{k}(S^{\vee n})\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{nr}(1+d)^{nr-k}c_{k}(Q^{\vee n})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n^{2}}q_{n,n-r}(d)+(-1)^{n^{2}}d^{n^{2}}\binom{n}{n-r}

Recall that the dimension of
\tau_{n,r}
is
n^{2}-r^{2}-1
. Since above computation holds for any integer
d
, thus as polynomial we have

\hat{q}_{n,r}(-1-H)-\hat{q}_{n,r}(-1)\left((1+H)^{n^{2}}-H^{n^{2}}\right)=\hat{q}_{n,n-r}(H)\/.

Here
\hat{q}_{n,r}(H)=(-1)^{\dim\tau_{n,r}}\cdot q_{n,r}(H)
is the signed polynomial.
Recall from §
2.3
that the projective duality
operation
I:=I_{n^{2}-1}
takes the signed Chern-Mather class of
\tau_{n,r}
to the signed Chern-Mather class of
\tau_{n,n-r}
: the dual variety of
\tau_{n,k}
is exactly
\tau_{n,n-k}
.
Let
c_{k}:=(-1)^{\dim\tau_{n,k}}\cdot c_{M}^{\tau_{n,k}}
be the Chern-Mather polynomials,
this says that

I(c_{i})=c_{n-i}
for
i=1,\cdots,n-1
.

Proved in
[
29
]
, the
q
polynomial
q_{n,r}(H)
equals
c_{*}(\phi_{n,r})
for some constructible function
\phi_{n,r}\in F(\tau_{n,r})
, defined by
\phi_{n,r}(p)=\binom{r}{l}
for
p\in\tau^{\circ}_{n,l}
.
(
In fact,
\phi_{n,r}
is the pushforward
p_{*}(\mathds{1}_{\hat{\tau}_{n,r}})
, where
p\colon\hat{\tau}_{n,r}\to\tau_{n,r}
is the Tjurina transform.
)

Since the local Euler obstructions is a basis for
F(\tau_{n,r})
, there are unique integers
\beta^{r}_{k}
such that

q_{n,r}(H)=\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\beta^{r}_{k}c_{M}^{\tau_{n,k}}(H)
,
\beta_{r}^{r}=1
. Then the involution equality shows that

I(\hat{q}_{n,r}(H))=I(\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\beta^{r}_{k}c_{k})=\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\beta^{r}_{k}c_{n-k}=\hat{q}_{n,n-r}(H)=\sum_{k=n-r}^{n-1}\beta^{r}_{k}c_{k}\/.

Since the lowest degree terms all the polynomials
c_{i}
have different degrees,
expand the above equalities for
r=1,2,\cdots,n-1
one can see that there is a unique solution

\displaystyle\beta_{B}^{A}=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1&A=B\\
0&A\neq B\end{cases}

This shows that
\phi_{n,r}=Eu_{\tau_{n,r}}
, and thus

q_{n,r}(H)=c_{M}^{\tau_{n,r}}
is the Chern-Mather polynomial.
∎

Remark 5
.

However we fail to obtain such an easy proof for the Chern-Mather classes of symmetric and skew-symmetric determinantal varieties. This is due to the term

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{e}d^{e-k}c_{k}(S^{\vee}\otimes Q^{\vee})\right)
, when substituting
d
by
-1-d
we lose the symmetry. The fact that the involution still interchanges the signed Chern-Mather classes indicates that there should be more symmetry and vanishing patterns hide behind the integrations.
It would be interesting to use Schubert calculus to give a similar proof to the symmetric and skew-symmetric determinantal varieties.

On the Accessibility and Controllability of Statistical Linearization for Stochastic Control: Algebraic Rank Conditions and their Genericity
R. Bonalli, C. Leparoux, B. Hérissé, F. Jean

Abstract
Statistical linearization has recently seen a particular surge of interest as a numerically cheap method for robust control of stochastic differential equations. Although it has already been successfully applied to control complex stochastic systems, accessibility and controllability properties of statistical linearization, which are key to make the robust control problem well-posed, have not been investigated yet. In this paper, we bridge this gap by providing sufficient conditions for the accessibility and controllability of statistical linearization. Specifically, we establish simple sufficient algebraic conditions for the accessibility and controllability of statistical linearization, which involve the rank of the Lie algebra generated by the drift only. In addition, we show these latter algebraic conditions are essentially sharp, by means of a counterexample, and that they are generic with respect to the drift and the initial condition.



keywords

Robust stochastic control, statistical linearization, accessibility and controllability, algebraic rank conditions, generic results.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space, with complete filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and let
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
d
-dimensional Wiener process which is adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. In this work, we focus on stochastic control systems which are modeled by Itô-type stochastic differential equations of the form

\mathrm{d}x(t)=f(x(t),u(t))\;\mathrm{d}t+g(x(t))\;\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

(1)

where
f
and
g
denote drift and diffusion, respectively, whereas
x
and
u
denote the finite-dimensional state of the system and the control applied to it, respectively.

Equations such as (
1
) are frequently leveraged to accurately model control systems which are subject to exogenous random perturbations, finding beneficial use in many applications which include aerospace
[
19
]
, biology
[
2
]
, and finance
[
6
]
, to name a few. In particular, one may often mitigate harmful random outcomes which perturb control systems by appropriately controlling (
1
) with the objective of minimizing (or, at least constraining) the variance of the state
x
throughout the control time interval: we call this latter
minimal variance control
. Successful application of this technique has been originally obtained in the context of linear systems, enabling numerical approaches ranging from exact covariance control
[
12
]
and variance minimization through linear-quadratic regulators
[
21
,
7
]
, to multi-objective variance minimization via sensitive robustness
[
18
]
. For what concerns non-linear systems, we find extension of this latter paradigm
[
20
]
, followed by open-loop-based sensitive robustness
[
9
]
, and more recently, by improved perturbation-based methods which penalize an appropriate sensitivity matrix within the cost
[
22
]
.

Unfortunately, the aforementioned procedures suffer from expensive numerical computations, which has recently fostered the investigation of methods for minimal variance control which rely on numerically cheaper approximations of the original formulation. Among such methods we find
statistical linearization

[
1
,
2
]
, which basically hinges upon the linearization of both the dynamics of the mean and the dynamics of the covariance of the state
x
around its expected value. More specifically, if only deterministic open-loop controls are leveraged, (
1
) may be replaced by the following system of deterministic differential equations

\begin{cases}\dot{m}(t)=f(m(t),u(t)),\\
\dot{P}(t)=D_{x}f(m(t),u(t))P(t)+P(t)D_{x}f(m(t),u(t))^{\top}+g(m(t))g(m(t))^{\top},\end{cases}

(2)

where
m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
P\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
denote first-order approximations of the mean and the covariance matrix of the state
x
, respectively. If one is capable of controlling (
2
) such that additionally
\|P\|
takes small enough values throughout the control time interval, the quantities
m
and
P
will closely resemble the exact mean and covariance matrix of the state
x
, respectively, thus endowing statistical linearization with theoretical guarantees of accurate approximation (indeed, a work in which we carefully analyze the accuracy of this method should appear soon,
see
[
14
]
). The main benefit which statistical linearization offers is to reduce minimal variance control, which is a stochastic problem, into an essentially easier problem, which consists of controlling a deterministic system subject to state constraints (on the variable
P
), yielding a numerically cheaper and theoretically guaranteed alternative.

Although this method has already been successfully applied to complex systems, e.g.,
[
22
,
19
]
, statistical linearization might suffer from controllability issues. Indeed, since the variance can be minimized only if the variable
P
can be controlled directly, at the very least the system (
2
) must be
accessible
, i.e., the reachable set of (
2
) must have non-empty interior. It is worth noting that this latter property is fundamental when leveraging statistical linearization for robust optimal control, in that it allows one to locally steer (
2
) along every directions, a key requirement without which one might not be capable to reduce the value of the cost
[
24
,
4
]
. Unfortunately, accessibility might not hold even when considering very simple settings. Indeed, in the case where (
1
) is linear, i.e., the mapping
f
is linear in
(x,u)
, and the mapping
g
is constant, the dynamics of
P
in (
2
) is independent from
m
and
u
, which makes it not accessible: in turn, it is impossible to control the values of
P
in linear settings.

Although accessibility is difficult to verify and no general exact characterizations exist, one may often establish accessibility through sufficient conditions on the rank of a certain Lie algebra (which, in special cases, may additionally yield complete controllability). This remark leads us to investigating the following first crucial question for the well-posedness of statistical linearization: are there conditions on the mappings
f
,
g
which yield accessibility of (
2
) by means of Lie algebra-based sufficient conditions? Note that in practice, uncertainty is often difficult to model. Therefore, the aforementioned sought conditions must depend on the values of the variable
m
only (that is, they must not depend on the values of
P
), and in addition, they should be preferably stated in terms of the mapping
f
uniquely, (that is, they should yield the same properties for different mappings
g
). Once such sufficient conditions are obtained, it is then paramount to understand how restrictive they are. This remark leads us to investigating the following second crucial question for the well-posedness of statistical linearization: do generic systems (
1
) satisfy the aforementioned conditions? The aim of this paper is to answer these two questions.

Before answering the aforementioned questions, let us briefly discuss connections between controllability properties of the original stochastic system and of its statistical linearization, although rigorously establishing such connections is out of the scope of the present work. A priori, the controllability properties of the original stochastic system are not directly related to the controllability properties of its statistical linearization. That said, perhaps under additional conditions one may prove a stochastic control system is “approximately controllable” in expectation and in covariance in the case its statistical linearization is controllable. Note that such result would necessarily require to derive estimates of the error between the evolution of the mean and the covariance of the original stochastic system (
1
) and the evolution of the variables
(m,P)
of its statistical linearization (
2
). Importantly, we derive some of such estimates in our upcoming work
[
14
]
, partially addressing such matter for some classes of stochastic control systems. In particular,
[
14
, Corollary 3]
and
[
14
, Proposition 4]
yield approximate controllability of the mean of the original stochastic system, although controllability of its covariance still remains an open question.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first summarize existing Lie algebra-based sufficient conditions for accessibility and controllability. Then, we express the Lie brackets generated by the system (
2
) as functions of the Lie brackets generated by the mapping
f
, thus obtaining sufficient conditions for the accessibility of (
2
) in terms of the mapping
f
uniquely. In the particular case of control-affine systems, we additionally obtain conditions for complete controllability. We also study the class of biaffine systems to show that the sufficient conditions we obtain are not necessary in general. Finally, in Section
3
we show that the aforementioned sufficient conditions for accessibility and controllability are generic for stochastic differential equations (
1
) which are control-affine. Specifically, we show that, for some given diffusion
g
, system (
2
) is accessible, generically with respect to the drift
f
and the initial condition
(m_{0},P_{0})
.

2
Conditions for accessibility and controllability

As we mentioned in the introduction, we consider stochastic control systems of the form (
1
), that is

\mathrm{d}x(t)=f(x(t),u(t))\;\mathrm{d}t+g(x(t))\;\mathrm{d}W_{t},

where the state
x
takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the control
u
takes values in a subset
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
, and the mappings
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are assumed to be smooth.
The
statistical linearization
of (
1
) is the deterministic control system

\begin{cases}\dot{m}(t)=f(m(t),u(t)),\\
\dot{P}(t)=D_{x}f(m(t),u(t))P(t)+P(t)D_{x}f(m(t),u(t))^{\top}+g(m(t))g(m(t))^{\top},\end{cases}

where
D_{x}f
denotes the Jacobian matrix of
f
with respect to
x
. Here,
m(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u(t)\in\mathcal{U}
, and
P(t)\in\textnormal{Sym}(n)
, the vector space of
(n\times n)
symmetric matrices.

Actually, one can show more specific properties of
P(t)
. Indeed, the latter satisfies the differential Lyapunov equation

\dot{P}=D_{x}f(m,u)P+PD_{x}f(m,u)^{\top}+g(m)g(m)^{\top},

for which it holds

P(t)=\Phi(t,0)P(0)\Phi(t,0)^{\top}+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(t,s)g(m(s))g(m(s))^{\top}\Phi(t,s)^{\top}\;\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0,

(3)

where the fundamental matrix
\Phi(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is defined as the unique solution to

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi(t,s)=D_{x}f\left(m(t),u(t)\right)\Phi(t,s),\\[10.0pt]
\Phi(s,s)=I.\end{cases}

From (
3
), we infer that
P(t)
is positive definite for every
t\geq 0
for which
P(t)
is well-defined, as soon as
P(0)
is itself positive definite. Motivated by this remark and the fact that the initial value of the covariance of the state
x
of (
1
) is selected as initial condition
P(0)
, from now on we assume
P(t)
is positive definite.

Denote by
\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
the set of positive definite
(n\times n)
symmetric matrices, and consider an open subset
V
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(specifically, in some settings it can be convenient to work with
m
in
V
instead of
m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
). We introduce statistical linearization as the control system on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
which is given by

\dot{X}=F(X,u),\qquad X=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
P\end{array}\right)\in V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n),\quad u\in\mathcal{U},

(4)

where the smooth mapping
F:V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textnormal{Sym}(n)
is defined by

F(X,u)=\begin{pmatrix}f(m,u)\\
D_{x}f(m,u)P+PD_{x}f(m,u)^{\top}+g(m)g(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

Our main goal is to study accessibility and controllability properties for this system.

Remark 1
.

As a consequence of (
3
), the trajectories of
F(X,u)
are defined on the same time-intervals in which the trajectories of
f(m,u)
are. In particular, if for a given
u\in\mathcal{U}
the vector field
f(\cdot,u)
is complete on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the vector field
F(\cdot,u)
is complete on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
.

2.1
General conditions for accessibility and controllability

Let us first recall the main existing accessibility and controllability conditions. We refer to
[
13
, Chapter 3]
for further details. Let

\dot{x}=f(x,u),\qquad x\in V,\ u\in\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},

(6)

be any (smooth) control system on an open subset
V
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Specifically, this control system is characterized by the family of (smooth) vector fields

\mathfrak{f}=\{f_{u}=f(\cdot,u)\ :\ u\in\mathcal{U}\}.

Two Lie algebras of vector fields are naturally associated with
\mathfrak{f}
:

•

the Lie algebra generated by
\mathfrak{f}
,

\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})=\mathrm{span}\left\{\left[f_{1},...,[f_{k-1},f_{k}]\right]:k\geq 1,f_{i}\in\mathfrak{f}\right\};

•

the so called
zero-time ideal
1
1

1

The terminology
zero-time ideal
comes from the fact that
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
is associated with the set of chained flows of the form
e^{t_{1}f_{1}}\circ\dots\circ e^{t_{k}f_{k}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{i}\in\mathfrak{f}
, where the total time
t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k}
equals zero. On the contrary,
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})
is associated with the set of such elements but for which the total time
t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k}
is let free.
, defined as

\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})=\mathrm{span}\left\{f_{1}-f_{2},f_{3}:f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathfrak{f},f_{3}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{f})\right\}\subset\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f}),

where
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{f})=\{\left[f_{1},...,[f_{k-1},f_{k}]\right]:k\geq 2,f_{i}\in\mathfrak{f}\}
.

From now on, for every
x\in V
, we denote by
\mathrm{Lie}_{x}(\mathfrak{f})
(resp.
\mathcal{I}_{x}(\mathfrak{f})
) the set of vectors
f(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f\in\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})
(resp.
f\in\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
).

Definition 2
.

We say that
\mathfrak{f}
(or the control system (
6
)) satisfies the
accessibility rank condition
at
x\in V
if
\dim\mathrm{Lie}_{x}(\mathfrak{f})=n
, and that it satisfies the
accessibility rank condition in fixed time
at
x\in V
if
\dim\mathcal{I}_{x}(\mathfrak{f})=n
.

Remark 3
.

Note that the accessibility rank condition in fixed time implies the accessibility rank condition since
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
is contained in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})
.

For every
x\in V
, let us denote by
\mathcal{A}(x,T)
the
reachable set
from
x
in time
T\geq 0
of (
6
), and by
\mathcal{A}(x)=\bigcup_{T\geq 0}\mathcal{A}(x,T)
the
reachable set
from
x
. The accessibility rank conditions give sufficient conditions such that reachable sets have nonempty interiors (see also
[
23
, Sect. 4.5]
for the necessity of these conditions).

Lemma 4
(Accessibility properties, Th. 2 p. 68, and Th. 3 p. 71 of
[
13
]
).

1.

If
\mathfrak{f}
satisfies the accessibility rank condition at every point of
V
, then
\forall x\in V
,
\mathcal{A}(x)
has nonempty interior and the set of interior points is dense in
\mathcal{A}(x)
.

2.

If
\mathfrak{f}
satisfies the accessibility rank condition in fixed time at every point in
V
, then
\forall x\in V
,
\mathcal{A}(x,T)
has nonempty interior for
T>0
small enough and the set of interior points is dense in
\mathcal{A}(x,T)
. If moreover
\mathfrak{f}
contains at least one complete vector field on
V
, then the preceding properties of
\mathcal{A}(x,T)
hold for every
T>0
.

The property for a control system of having reachable sets with nonempty interiors is often called
accessibility
, which is a crucial property especially in the context of optimal control. Indeed, it guarantees that one can generate infinitesimal perturbations in any direction around the endpoint of a trajectory by varying its generating control (either in fixed time if
\mathrm{int}\mathcal{A}(x,T)
is nonempty, or in free time if
\mathrm{int}\mathcal{A}(x)
is nonempty), opening up the possibility of performing calculus of variations.

Consider now the particular case of control-affine systems, i.e.,

f(x,u)=f_{0}(x)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{u}}u_{i}f_{i}(x),\quad u\in\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}.

(7)

The Lie algebras associated with such a system respectively take the forms

\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})=\mathrm{Lie}\{f_{i}:\ i=0,\dots,n_{u}\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})=\mathrm{Lie}\{\textnormal{ad}^{s}f_{0}\cdot f_{i}:\ s\geq 0,\ i=1,\dots,n_{u}\},

where we denote
\textnormal{ad}^{1}f_{0}\cdot f_{i}=[f_{0},f_{i}]
and
\textnormal{ad}^{s}f_{0}\cdot f_{i}=\textnormal{ad}^{1}f_{0}\cdot(\textnormal{ad}^{s-1}f_{0}\cdot f_{i})
for
s\geq 2
. The main benefit offered by these relations is that they allow for expressing the accessibility rank conditions in terms of iterated brackets of the
f_{i}
’s uniquely. Moreover, a somewhat stronger version of Lemma
4
may be established, enabling the following ease-of-use sufficient conditions for the controllability of control-affine systems.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
V
is connected. If for every
x\in V
it holds that

\dim\mathrm{Lie}_{x}\left(f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{u}}\right)=n,

(8)

then the control-affine system (
7
) is
controllable
on
V
in free and fixed time, i.e., for every
x\in V
,
\mathcal{A}(x)=V
and
\mathcal{A}(x,T)=V
for
T>0
small enough.

Remark 6
.

Note that the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Lie}\left(f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{u}}\right)
is included in
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
, therefore (
8
) implies that the accessibility rank condition in fixed time at
x
holds.

2.2
Form of the Lie brackets for statistical linearization

The accessibility rank conditions require the computation of Lie brackets. We establish here a general expression of the Lie brackets between the vector fields which define the statistical linearization. For this, given a controlled drift
f
and a diffusion
g
defining the statistical linearization (
4
), we denote by
\mathfrak{f}=\{f(\cdot,u)\ :\ u\in\mathcal{U}\}
the family of vector fields on
V
which is defined by the drift, and by
\mathfrak{F}_{g}=\{F(\cdot,u)\ :\ u\in\mathcal{U}\}
the family of vector fields (
5
) on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
which is defined by the statistical linearization. Clearly by definition, for every
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{g}
there exists
f\in\mathfrak{f}
such that

F(X)=\begin{pmatrix}f(m)\\
Df(m)P+PDf(m)^{\top}+g(m)g(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix},\quad X=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
P\end{array}\right)\in V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n).

Lemma 7
.

Consider two vector fields
F_{1},F_{2}
on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
of the form

F_{i}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{i}(m)\\
Df_{i}(m)P+PDf_{i}(m)^{\top}+B_{i}(m)\end{pmatrix},\quad i=1,2,

where
f_{1},f_{2}
are vector fields on
V
and
B_{1},B_{2}:V\to\textnormal{Sym}(n)
are smooth mappings. Then,

[F_{1},F_{2}](X)=\begin{pmatrix}[f_{1},f_{2}](m)\\
D[f_{1},f_{2}](m)P+PD[f_{1},f_{2}](m)^{\top}+B_{12}(m)\end{pmatrix},

where, for every
m\in V
,

B_{12}(m)=dB_{2}(m)\cdot f_{1}(m)-dB_{1}(m)\cdot f_{2}(m)+Df_{2}(m)B_{1}(m)-Df_{1}(m)B_{2}(m)\\
+B_{1}(m)Df_{2}(m)^{\top}-B_{2}(m)Df_{1}(m)^{\top}

(9)

is a symmetric matrix (note that we denote by
Df(m)
the Jacobian matrix of a vector field
f
, whereas we denote by
dB(m)
the differential of a map
B
at
m
).

Proof.

By definition, the Lie brackets of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
is given by

[F_{1},F_{2}](X)=dF_{2}(X)\cdot F_{1}(X)-dF_{1}(X)\cdot F_{2}(X).

Let us denote
Y=(Y_{m},Y_{P})
any element
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textnormal{Sym}(n)
. On the one hand, the first
n
components of
dF_{2}(X)\cdot F_{1}(X)
satisfy

(dF_{2}(X)\cdot F_{1}(X))_{m}=d(F_{2})_{m}(X)\cdot F_{1}(X)\\
=\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial m}(m)\cdot(F_{1}(X))_{m}+\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial P}(m)\cdot(F_{1}(X))_{P}=Df_{2}(m)f_{1}(m),

and therefore we readily obtain that

[F_{1},F_{2}](X)_{m}=[f_{1},f_{2}](m).

On the other hand, the remaining
n^{2}
components of
dF_{2}(X)\cdot F_{1}(X)
satisfy

(dF_{2}(X)\cdot F_{1}(X))_{P}=\frac{\partial\left(Df_{2}(m)P+PDf_{2}(m)^{\top}+B_{2}(m)\right)}{\partial m}\cdot f_{1}(m)\\
+\frac{\partial\left(Df_{2}(m)P+PDf_{2}(m)^{\top}+B_{2}(m)\right)}{\partial P}\cdot\left(Df_{1}(m)P+PDf_{1}(m)^{\top}+B_{1}(m)\right).

(10)

Let us develop these two terms separately. For the first term, we may compute

\frac{\partial\left(Df_{2}(m)P+PDf_{2}(m)^{\top}+B_{2}(m)\right)}{\partial m}\cdot f_{1}(m)=\\
D^{2}f_{2}(m)\cdot(f_{1}(m),.)P+P[D^{2}f_{2}(m)\cdot(f_{1}(m),.)]^{\top}+dB_{2}(m)\cdot f_{1}(m),

(11)

where, with an abuse of notation, we implicitly identify the linear mapping

D^{2}f_{2}(m)\cdot(f_{1}(m),.):h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto D^{2}f_{2}(m)\cdot(f_{1}(m),h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

to an
(n\times n)
matrix. For the second term, we may compute

\frac{\partial\left(Df_{2}(m)P+PDf_{2}(m)^{\top}+B_{2}(m)\right)}{\partial P}\cdot\left(Df_{1}(m)P+PDf_{1}(m)^{\top}+B_{1}(m)\right)=\\
Df_{2}(m)Df_{1}(m)P+Df_{2}(m)PDf_{1}(m)^{\top}+Df_{1}(m)PDf_{2}(m)^{\top}+P\left[Df_{2}(m)Df_{1}(m)\right]^{\top}\\
+Df_{2}(m)B_{1}(m)+B_{1}(m)Df_{2}(m)^{\top}.

(12)

Similar formulas hold for
(dF_{1}(X)\cdot F_{2}(X))_{P}
, which can be readily checked by index exchange. At this step, by leveraging the relation

D[f_{1},f_{2}](m)=D^{2}f_{2}(m)\cdot(f_{1}(m),.)-D^{2}f_{1}(m)\cdot(f_{2}(m),.)\\
+Df_{2}(m)Df_{1}(m)-Df_{1}(m)Df_{2}(m),

(13)

we finally obtain that

[F_{1},F_{2}]_{P}(X)=D[f_{1},f_{2}](m)P+PD[f_{1},f_{2}](m)^{\top}+B_{12}(m),

where
B_{12}
is as stated in the lemma, and the conclusion follows.

An elementary induction argument on this lemma yields a simple closed form for every element in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
and
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
, respectively.

Corollary 8
.

Every vector field
F
in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
(resp. in
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
) writes as

F(X)=\begin{pmatrix}f(m)\\
Df(m)P+PDf(m)^{\top}+B(m)\end{pmatrix},

(14)

where
f
belongs to
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})
(resp. to
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
) and
B:V\to\textnormal{Sym}(n)
is a smooth mapping. Reciprocally, if
f
belongs to
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})
(resp. to
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
), then there exists
F
in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
(resp. in
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
) and a smooth mapping
B:V\to\textnormal{Sym}(n)
such that (
14
) holds.

Finally, if
F\in\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})
(i.e. if
g=0
), then
B=0
.

Remark 9
.

The family
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
plays an important role in the forthcoming algebraic conditions. It is important to note that this family is not directly associated with the statistical linearization of a stochastic differential equation (
1
), in that the latter is not defined for
g=0
. Nevertheless, this family
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
can be interpreted as the statistical linearization of a deterministic control system subject to imperfect information on the initial condition, which is modelled through a Borel measure (see for instance
[
16
]
).

2.3
Algebraic conditions for accessibility

To simplify the expression of the accessibility rank conditions, we first provide an algebraic result which allows us to express these sought conditions independently from
g
and
P
. In what follows, we denote
E=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textnormal{Sym}(n)
, a vector space of dimension
N=n+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
denotes the family of the vector fields (
5
) associated with
g=0
. Also, we denote by
{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
the space of
(n\times n)
matrices and by
I\in\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
the identity matrix.

Lemma 10
.

For every
m\in V
, the following properties hold:

1.

\dim\mathcal{I}_{(m,I)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=N
if and only if
\dim\mathcal{I}_{(m,P)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=N

\forall P\in\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
;

2.

\dim\mathrm{Lie}_{(m,I)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=N
if and only if
\dim\mathrm{Lie}_{(m,P)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=N

\forall P\in\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
.

Proof.

For any given
P\in\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, we introduce the map
\phi_{P}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\to E
, which is defined by

\phi_{P}(v,A)=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
AP+PA^{\top}\end{pmatrix}.

We claim that

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times M_{n}(\mathbb{R})=E\oplus\ker{\phi_{P}}.

(15)

For this, note first that
\ker{\phi_{P}}=\left\{0\right\}\times\textnormal{Skew}(n)P^{-1}
, where
\textnormal{Skew}(n)
denotes the set of
(n\times n)
skew-symmetric matrices. Hence, we are left to prove that

M_{n}(\mathbb{R})=\textnormal{Sym}(n)\oplus\textnormal{Skew}(n)P^{-1},

or equivalently that

\textnormal{Sym}(n)\cap\textnormal{Skew}(n)P^{-1}=\{0\}.

For this, if we assume
S
is an element of this intersection, that is
S
is symmetric and
S=\Lambda P^{-1}
with
\Lambda\in\textnormal{Skew}(n)
, it must hold that

\Lambda P^{-1}=S=S^{\top}=(\Lambda P^{-1})^{\top}=-P^{-1}\Lambda,

implying that
P\Lambda+\Lambda P=0
. This latter Lyapunov equation has no trivial solutions if and only if
Sp\left\{P\right\}\cap Sp\left\{-P\right\}\neq\emptyset
, which is not the case since
P
is positive definite. Therefore,
\Lambda=0
and thus
S=0
. We conclude that (
15
) holds true.

At this point, we proceed by first showing the first point of the lemma. For this, fix
m\in V
and denote

\widetilde{E}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}f(m)\\
Df(m)\end{pmatrix}\ :\ f\in\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
is a Lie algebra,
\widetilde{E}
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, for every
P\in\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, thanks to Corollary
8
it holds that

\mathcal{I}_{(m,P)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=\phi_{P}(\widetilde{E})\subset E,

and hence, thanks to (
15
) we have that
\dim\mathcal{I}_{(m,P)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=N
if and only if
E\subset\widetilde{E}
. Since this last condition holds true independently from
P
, Point 1. of the lemma follows. Point 2. can be proved along the same line.

Let us now study the dimensions of the subspaces
\mathcal{I}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
and
\mathrm{Lie}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
, for
g\neq 0
. Specifically, the following key density result holds true.

Lemma 11
.

1.

If
\dim\mathcal{I}_{(m,I)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=N
for every
m\in V
, then there exists an open and dense subset
\Omega
of
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, such that
\dim\mathcal{I}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})=N
for every
X\in\Omega
.

2.

If
\dim\mathrm{Lie}_{(m,I)}(\mathfrak{F}_{0})=N
for every
m\in V
, then there exists an open and dense subset
\widetilde{\Omega}
of
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, such that
\dim\mathrm{Lie}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})=N
for every
X\in\widetilde{\Omega}
.

Proof.

We only prove Point 1., the proof of Point 2. being identical. Fix
m_{0}\in V
. By assumption, there exist
N
vector fields
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
in
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})
such that

\det\nolimits_{E}\left(\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(m_{0})\\
Df_{1}(m_{0})+Df_{1}(m_{0})^{\top}\end{pmatrix},...,\begin{pmatrix}f_{N}(m_{0})\\
Df_{N}(m_{0})+Df_{N}(m_{0})^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\right)\neq 0,

where
\det\nolimits_{E}
denotes the determinant in the vector space
E
.
By continuity, for every
m
in a neighbourhood
\mathcal{V}_{m_{0}}\subset V
of
m_{0}
it holds that

\det\nolimits_{E}\left(\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(m)\\
Df_{1}(m)+Df_{1}(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix},...,\begin{pmatrix}f_{N}(m)\\
Df_{N}(m)+Df_{N}(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\right)\neq 0.

(16)

At this step, from Corollary
8
, there exist
F_{1},\dots,F_{N}
in
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
such that

F_{i}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{i}(m)\\
Df_{i}(m)P+PDf_{i}(m)^{\top}+B_{i}(m)\end{pmatrix},\quad i=1,\dots,N.

Let us introduce the scalar function on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, which is defined by

p(X)=\det\nolimits_{E}\left(F_{1}(X),...,F_{N}(X)\right).

Clearly,
\dim\mathcal{I}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})=N
as soon as
p(X)\neq 0
. But for any fixed
m\in\mathcal{V}_{m_{0}}
, the mapping
p(X)
is a polynomial function of
P
, whose homogeneous term of highest degree is

p^{0}(X)=\det\nolimits_{E}\left(\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(m)\\
Df_{1}(m)P+PDf_{1}(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix},...,\begin{pmatrix}f_{N}(m)\\
Df_{N}(m)P+PDf_{N}(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\right).

Therefore, (
16
) yields
p^{0}(m,I)\neq 0
, and thus both
p^{0}
and
p
are not the zero
P
–polynomial. As a consequence, the subset

{\Omega}_{m_{0}}=\left\{X\in\mathcal{V}_{m_{0}}\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n):\ p(X)\neq 0\right\}

is open and dense in
\mathcal{V}_{m_{0}}\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
. By taking the union of the sets
{\Omega}_{m_{0}}
for all
m_{0}\in V
, we obtain a dense and open subset
\Omega
of
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, on which
\dim\mathcal{I}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})=N
.

2.4
Accessibility criterion for statistical linearization

We are now in position to establish sufficient conditions for the accessibility of the statistical linearization. Specifically, the following result comes from a direct application of Lemma
11
.

Proposition 12
.

Consider drift
f
and diffusion
g
mappings, and denote by
\mathfrak{f}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{g}
the families of vector fields associated with
f
and the corresponding statistical linearization (
4
), respectively. The following conditions hold true:

1.

if for every
m\in V
there holds

\dim\left\{\begin{pmatrix}f(m)\\
Df(m)+Df(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\ :\ f\in\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{f})\right\}=N,

(17)

then
\mathfrak{F}_{g}
satisfies the accessibility rank condition on an open and dense subset of
V\times\textnormal{Sym}(n)^{+}
;

2.

if for every
m\in V
there holds

\dim\left\{\begin{pmatrix}f(m)\\
Df(m)+Df(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\ :\ f\in\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})\right\}=N,

(18)

then
\mathfrak{F}_{g}
satisfies the accessibility rank condition in fixed time on an open and dense subset of
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
.

Remark 13
.

In the case
g=0
, by Lemma
10
the open and dense subsets which result from Proposition
12
are the whole space
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, and the requirements of Point 1. and 2. of the proposition are equivalent to the accessibility rank conditions in free and fixed time, respectively. This equivalence does not hold in general for nonzero
g
, see Section
2.5
for a counterexample.

Importantly, these conditions involve properties of the family of controlled drift mappings
\mathfrak{f}
uniquely, and in particular neither the diffusion
g
nor the covariance variable
P
explicitly affect them. Through these accessibility conditions, one can deduce the structure of the reachable sets in free and fixed time, and thus generic accessibility properties for (
2
) by means of Lemma
4
. Note that, thanks to Remark
1
, the assumption concerning the completeness of the vector fields which is required in Lemma
4
can be merely tested on the family
\mathfrak{f}
.

Proposition
12
may be refined in the case of control-affine drift, that is, mappings
f
of the form

f(x,u)=f_{0}(x)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{u}}u_{i}f_{i}(x),\quad u\in\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}.

In such case, the statistical linearization takes a control-affine form as well, specifically

\dot{X}=F_{f_{0},g}(X)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{u}}u_{i}F_{f_{i}}(X),\quad u\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},

(19)

where we introduced the following vector fields on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
:

\begin{array}[]{cc}F_{f_{0},g}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{0}(m)\\
Df_{0}(m)P+PDf_{0}(m)^{\top}+g(m)^{\top}g(m)\end{pmatrix},\\[14.22636pt]
F_{f_{i}}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{i}(m)\\
Df_{i}(m)P+PDf_{i}(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix},\qquad X=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
P\end{array}\right),\quad i=1,\dots,{n_{u}}.\end{array}

In addition to the accessibility stated through Proposition
12
, from a direct application of Lemma
5
we obtain stronger controllability conditions for (
19
).

Proposition 14
.

Assume
V
is connected. If for every
m\in V
there holds

\dim\left\{\begin{pmatrix}f(m)\\
Df(m)+Df(m)^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\ :\ f\in\mathrm{Lie}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{u}})\right\}=N,

then system (
19
) is controllable on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
in free and fixed time.

Proof.

It suffices to notice that, by applying Lemma
10
-Point 2. to the family
\mathfrak{F}_{0}=\{F_{f_{1}},\dots,F_{f_{n_{u}}}\}
, the assumption above implies that (
19
) satisfies the assumption of Lemma
5
.

2.5
Counterexample: the biaffine case

The aforementioned results give sufficient but not necessary conditions for accessibility. To illustrate that, below we provide an example of a particular class of systems which is generally accessible even though it does not verify the latter sufficient accessibility conditions.

The class we consider is the one associated with constant diffusions
g
and control-affine drifts
f
which depend linearly on
x
, i.e.,
f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x
with
A_{i}\in{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
for
i=0,...,m_{u}
. We say that such an
f
is a
biaffine system
, the simplest class of nonlinear systems. The statistical linearization takes the control-affine form (
19
), where

F_{f_{0},g}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{0}m\\
A_{0}P+PA_{0}^{\top}+gg^{\top}\end{pmatrix},\quad F_{f_{i}}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{i}m\\
A_{i}P+PA_{i}^{\top}\end{pmatrix},\ i=1,\dots,n_{u}.

For simplicity we set
F_{0}=F_{f_{0},g}
and
F_{i}=F_{f_{i}}
. Lemma
7
yields the following forms of the Lie brackets,

[F_{i},F_{j}](X)=\begin{pmatrix}\left[A_{i},A_{j}\right]m\\
\left[A_{i},A_{j}\right]P+P\left[A_{i},A_{j}\right]^{\top}+B_{ij}\end{pmatrix},\quad i,j=0,\dots,n_{u},

(20)

where the symmetric matrices
B_{ij}\in\textnormal{Sym}(n)
are given by

B_{0j}=A_{j}gg^{\top}+gg^{\top}A_{j}^{\top}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad B_{ij}=0\qquad\hbox{for }i,j=1,\dots,n_{u}.

(21)

We first show that, in such specific case, the accessibility conditions given by Proposition
12
do no longer hold true.

Proposition 15
.

Assume that the drift
f
is a biaffine system. Then, at every
m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the sufficient conditions (
17
) and (
18
) in Proposition
12
are not satisfied. In particular, if in addition
g=0
, then the statistical linearization satisfy none of the accessibility rank conditions.

Proof.

As a consequence of (
20
), condition (
17
) at
m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
writes as

\dim\left\{\begin{pmatrix}Am\\
A+A^{\top}\end{pmatrix}:\ A\in\mathrm{Lie}(A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n_{u}})\right\}=N.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
P\in\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
, and introduce the linear map
\psi_{(m,P)}:{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textnormal{Sym}(n)
defined by

\psi_{(m,P)}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}Am\\
AP+PA^{\top}\end{pmatrix}.

If condition (
17
) holds at
m
, then necessarily
\mathrm{rank}\psi_{(m,I)}=N
. Let us prove that this latter condition is not actually true.

For this, obviously we have that

\ker\psi_{(m,I)}=\left\{A\in\textnormal{Skew}(n):\ Am=0\right\}.

If
m=0
, then
\ker\psi_{(m,I)}=\textnormal{Skew}(n)
is of dimension
n(n-1)/2
, and thus, by the rank-nullity theorem,
\mathrm{rank}\psi_{(m,I)}=n^{2}-n(n-1)/2=n(n+1)/2
is smaller than
N
.
If
m\neq 0
, then we can find an orthogonal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
e_{1}=m
. In that basis, the element
A_{i,j}
in the
i{th}
row and the
j{th}
column of
A
satisfies

A_{i,j}=e_{i}^{\top}Ae_{j}=-e_{j}^{\top}Ae_{i}=-A_{j,i},

yielding
A_{1,j}=A_{i,1}=0
. As a consequence, in these coordinates, we have

\ker\psi_{(m,I)}=\left\{A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\bar{A}\end{pmatrix}\in{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R}):\ \bar{A}\in\textnormal{Skew}(n-1)\right\}.

Thus
\ker\psi_{(m,I)}
has the same dimension as
\textnormal{Skew}(n-1)
. Hence
\mathrm{rank}\psi_{(m,I)}=n^{2}-(n-2)(n-1)/2
is equal to
N-1
and we deduce that the condition (
17
) is never satisfied. This proof can be easily replicated to show condition (
18
).

Proposition
15
shows that our sufficient conditions for accessibility can not be leveraged to prove that biaffine systems are generally accessible. Nevertheless, it is still possible to formulate other sufficient general conditions for the accessibility of biaffine systems. In particular, the following result holds true.

Proposition 16
.

Assume that the biaffine system satisfies the followings:

(i)

\dim\mathrm{Lie}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n_{u}})=n^{2}
;

(ii)

there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{u}\}
such that
B_{0i}=A_{i}gg^{\top}+gg^{\top}A_{i}^{\top}
is nonzero.

Then
\mathfrak{F}_{g}
satisfies the accessibility rank condition in fixed time on an open and dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
.

Remark 17
.

Conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
are satisfied by generic matrices
A_{1},\dots,A_{n_{u}}
when
n_{u}>1
and
g\neq 0
, respectively. Hence the statistical linearizations of a “very large” class of biaffine systems satisfy the accessibility rank conditions, though they do not satisfy the sufficient conditions which are provided in Proposition
12
.

Proof.

Note first that, as a consequence of (
20
), every vector field in
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
writes as

F(X)=\begin{pmatrix}Am\\
AP+PA^{\top}+B\end{pmatrix},\qquad A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\quad B\in\textnormal{Sym}(n),

and thus
F(X)
depends affinely on
X=(m,P)
. As a result, the accessibility rank condition in fixed time
\dim\mathcal{I}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})=N
holds either nowhere, or for points
X
which belong to an open and dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
(to see this, use the argument we provide at the end of the proof of Lemma
11
). Therefore, it is enough to prove that
\dim\mathcal{I}_{X}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})=N
holds for any specific point
X
.

For this, by Hypothesis
(ii)
the symmetric matrix
B_{0i}
is nonzero, thus we can choose
\bar{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{P}\in\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
such that
\bar{m}^{\top}\bar{P}^{-1}B_{0i}\bar{P}^{-1}\bar{m}\neq 0
. Set
\bar{X}=(\bar{m},\bar{P})
.

Now, note that, on the one hand the set
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{X}}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
contains the vector

[F_{0},F_{i}](\bar{X})=\begin{pmatrix}A_{0i}\bar{m}\\
A_{0i}\bar{P}+\bar{P}A_{0i}^{\top}+B_{0i}\end{pmatrix},\quad\hbox{where }A_{0i}=\left[A_{0},A_{i}\right],

whereas, on the other hand, it contains the linear subset

W=\left\{F(\bar{X})\ :\ F\in\mathrm{Lie}(F_{1},\dots,F_{n_{u}})\right\}.

Thanks to (
20
) and (
21
), every
F(\bar{X})\in W
writes as

F(\bar{X})=\begin{pmatrix}A\bar{m}\\
A\bar{P}+\bar{P}A^{\top}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\hbox{with }A\in\mathrm{Lie}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n_{u}}).

It then results from Hypothesis
(i)
that
W
is the image of the map
\psi_{\bar{X}}
which is defined in the proof of Proposition
15
. Moreover, a straightforward adaptation of the latter proof implies that
\mathrm{rank}\psi_{\bar{X}}=N-1
, and therefore that
\dim W=N-1
.

Let us prove that
[F_{0},F_{i}](\bar{X})
does not belong to
W
. Indeed, if an element
(v,Q)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textnormal{Sym}(n)
belongs to
W
, then there exists a matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
such that
v=A\bar{m}
and
Q=A\bar{P}+\bar{P}A^{\top}
. Equivalently, there exists
\Omega\in\textnormal{Skew}(n)
such that
v=(\frac{1}{2}Q+\Omega)\bar{P}^{-1}\bar{m}
. By multiplying the latter expression by
\bar{m}^{\top}\bar{P}^{-1}
, we obtain that

\alpha(v,Q):=\bar{m}^{\top}\bar{P}^{-1}(v-\frac{1}{2}Q\bar{P}^{-1}\bar{m})=0.

Let us compute this expression in the case
(v,Q)=[F_{0},F_{i}](\bar{X})
. We have that

\alpha([F_{0},F_{i}](\bar{X}))=\bar{m}^{\top}\bar{P}^{-1}\left(A_{0i}\bar{m}-\frac{1}{2}(A_{0i}\bar{P}+\bar{P}A_{0i}^{\top}+B_{0i})\bar{P}^{-1}\bar{m}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{2}\bar{m}^{\top}\bar{P}^{-1}\left((A_{0i}\bar{P}-\bar{P}A_{0i}^{\top})-B_{0i}\right)\bar{P}^{-1}\bar{m}=-\frac{1}{2}\bar{m}^{\top}\bar{P}^{-1}B_{0i}\bar{P}^{-1}\bar{m}.

But the choice of
\bar{X}
guarantees
\alpha([F_{0},F_{i}](\bar{X}))\neq 0
, which implies
[F_{0},F_{i}](\bar{X})\not\in W
.

In summary, we have shown that
W\oplus\mathbb{R}[F_{0},F_{i}](\bar{X})
is a vector space of dimension
N
included in
\mathcal{I}_{\bar{X}}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})
. We deduce that
\dim\mathcal{I}_{\bar{X}}(\mathfrak{F}_{g})=N
, concluding the proof.

3
Generic accessibility and controllability

In this section, we conclude our study by establishing generic accessibility and controllability properties for (
2
). Specifically, we show that the various sufficient rank conditions which we established in Section
2.4
hold
generically
(in the sense of Thom’s transversality) with respect to the drift
f
, and eventually with respect to the initial conditions
m(0)
and
P(0)
.

From now on, we consider control-affine drift mappings uniquely, i.e.,

f(x,u)=f_{0}(x)+\sum^{n_{u}}_{i=1}u_{i}f_{i}(x),\quad u\in\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},

and we thus adopt the notation we introduced through (
19
). For an open subset
V
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\ell,r\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the following quantities (see, e.g.,
[
11
]
):

•

\pi^{\ell}_{r}:J^{\ell}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})\rightarrow V
is the vector bundle of
\ell
-jets of maps in
C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})
;

•

for every
h\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})
,
j^{\ell}_{r}(h):V\rightarrow J^{\ell}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})
is the jet map associated with
\pi^{\ell}_{r}
; in particular, when clear from the context, for the sake of conciseness we denote
j^{\ell}(h)_{y}=j^{\ell}(h)(y)\in J^{\ell}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})
, for every
h\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})
and every
y\in V
.

We recall that the family of sets

\Big\{h\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r}):\ j^{\ell}(h)(V)\subset\mathcal{V}\Big\},\quad\textnormal{$\mathcal{V}$ open subset of $J^{\ell}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})$},

generates the (weak) Whitney topology of
C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{r})
. Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 18
(Accessibility with Respect to Generic Drift).

There exists an open and dense (w.r.t. the Whitney topology) subset
\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Acc}}\subset C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n\times({n_{u}}+1)})
, such that for every
(f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{u}})\in\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Acc}}
, the family of vector fields

\mathfrak{f}=\left\{f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{u}}u_{i}f_{i}\ :\ u\in\mathcal{U}\right\}

satisfies condition (
17
) for every
m\in V
. In addition, there exists an open and dense (w.r.t. the Whitney topology) subset
\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Acc}_{+}}\subset C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n\times({n_{u}}+1)})
, such that for every
(f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{u}})\in\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Acc}_{+}}
, the aforedefined family of vector fields
\mathfrak{f}
satisfies condition (
18
) for every
m\in V
.

Together with Proposition
12
, Theorem
18
readily yields the following corollary.

Corollary 19
(Accessibility with Respect to Generic Drift and Initial Datum).

There exists an open and dense (w.r.t. the Whitney topology) subset
\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Acc}_{+}}\subset C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n\times({n_{u}}+1)})
, such that for every
(f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{u}})\in\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Acc}_{+}}
and every diffusion
g\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, the family of vector fields

\mathfrak{F}=\left\{F_{f_{0},g}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{u}}u_{i}F_{f_{i}}\ :\ u\in\mathcal{U}\right\}

satisfies the accessibility rank condition in fixed time on an open and dense subset
\Omega_{g}\subset V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
which depends on
g
uniquely.

Theorem 20
(Controllability with Respect to Generic Drift).

Let
{n_{u}}\geq 2
. There exists an open and dense (w.r.t. the Whitney topology) subset
\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Contr}}\subset C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n\times{n_{u}}})
, such that for every
(f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{u}})\in\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{Contr}}
, every vector field
f_{0}\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and every diffusion
g\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, the family of vector fields

\mathfrak{F}=\left\{F_{f_{0},g}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{u}}u_{i}F_{f_{i}}\ :\ u\in\mathcal{U}\right\}

is controllable on
V\times\textnormal{Sym}^{+}(n)
in free and fixed time.

Through the rank conditions provided above, one can deduce the structure of the reachable sets in free and fixed time of the statistical linearization, and thus generic accessibility and controllability properties for (
2
), by means of Lemma
4
–
5
.

The rest of the section is devoted to proving these results. In particular, we prove
the first part of
Theorem
18
for
{n_{u}}=1
(the cases
{n_{u}}\geq 2
trivially result from the latter), given that the proofs of
Theorem
20
and of the second part of Theorem
18
follow the same line, though they are less pedagogical. Moreover, since all our proofs develop along local analysis, without loss of generality we implicitly assume
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
in what follows.

3.1
Fundamental concepts from transversality theory

Before jumping into the proof of Theorem
18
, we recall some useful concepts from algebraic geometry and transversality theory which are key to achieve the aforementioned proof, for which we mainly refer to

Appendix A
Useful concepts from algebraic geometry

Let
\ell,r\in\mathbb{N}
. A semi-algebraic set
\mathcal{SA}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}
is a subset of the form

\mathcal{SA}=\bigcup^{\ell}_{i=1}\Big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:\ p^{i}_{1}(y)=\dots=p^{i}_{a_{i}}(y)=0,\ q^{i}_{1}(y)<0,\dots,q^{i}_{b_{i}}(y)<0\Big\},

(38)

for polynomials
p^{i}_{j},q^{i}_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Semi-algebraic sets enjoy the following properties:

•

finite unions of semi-algebraic sets are semi-algebraic sets;

•

closures (w.r.t. the natural topology) of semi-algebraic sets are semi-algebraic sets.

A simple induction argument on (
38
) shows that semi-algebraic sets are disjoint unions of open subsets of algebraic sets. Thus, Whitney’s stratification theorem readily yields the following representation result for semi-algebraic sets.

Theorem 25
(Whitney’s stratification for semi-algebraic sets
[
25
, Th. 2, p. 546]
).

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{SA}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}
be a semi-algebraic set. There exist
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and embedded submanifolds
\mathcal{M}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}
, which are mutually disjoint and such that

\mathcal{SA}=\bigcup^{\ell}_{i=1}\mathcal{M}_{i}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\dim\mathcal{M}_{i+1}<\dim\mathcal{M}_{i},\ i=1,\dots,\ell-1.

Thanks to Theorem
25
, given
r\in\mathbb{N}
and a semi-algebraic set
\mathcal{SA}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}
, we may define the dimension and the codimension of
\mathcal{SA}
as

\dim\mathcal{SA}=\underset{i=1,\dots,\ell}{\max}\dim\mathcal{M}_{i}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\textnormal{codim}\,\mathcal{SA}=r-\dim\mathcal{SA}.

Importantly, the topological closure does not modify the dimension, nor the codimension of a semi-algebraic set. Indeed, as a matter of example let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and

\mathcal{SA}=\big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:\ p(y)=0,\ q(y)<0\big\},

for polynomials
p,q:\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. By definition, we must have

\displaystyle\dim\overline{\mathcal{SA}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\dim\mathcal{SA}\ ,\ \dim\big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:\ p(y)=q(y)=0\big\}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\dim\mathcal{SA}\ ,\ \dim\big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:\ p(y)=0\big\}\Big\}=\dim\mathcal{SA},

where we infer the last equality from the fact that
\mathcal{SA}
is open in
p^{-1}(0)
, and thus

\dim\overline{\mathcal{SA}}=\dim\mathcal{SA}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\textnormal{codim}\,\overline{\mathcal{SA}}=\textnormal{codim}\,\mathcal{SA}.

(39)

The extension of this property to general semi-algebraic sets follows by iterating the previous argument through classical induction.

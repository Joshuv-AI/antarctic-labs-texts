Well-posedness and stability of a stochastic neural field in the form of a partial differential equation
José Antonio Carrillo, Pierre Roux, Susanne Solem

Abstract
A system of partial differential equations representing stochastic neural fields was recently proposed with the aim of modelling the activity of noisy grid cells when a mammal travels through physical space. The system was rigorously derived from a stochastic particle system and its noise-driven pattern-forming bifurcations have been characterised. However, due to its nonlinear and non-local nature, standard well-posedness theory for smooth time-dependent solutions of parabolic equations does not apply. In this article, we transform the problem through a suitable change of variables into a nonlinear Stefan-like free boundary problem and use its representation formulae to construct local-in-time smooth solutions under mild hypotheses. Then, we prove that fast-decaying initial conditions and globally Lipschitz modulation functions imply that solutions are global-in-time. Last, we improve previous linear stability results by showing nonlinear asymptotic stability of stationary solutions under suitable assumptions.

\tau_{c}\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\left[\left(\Phi\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x-y)\int_{0}^{+\infty}s\rho(y,s,t)dsdy+B(t)\right)-s\right)\rho(x,s,t)\right]+\sigma\dfrac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial s^{2}},

Remark 1.3

(Non-optimality of the existence results)
.

We want to emphasise that the regularity and the decay of the solutions we construct are by no means optimal. Optimal regularity for this problem is beyond the scope of this work. With higher regularity on

Therefore, although it is not necessary for construction of solutions, we choose to work with fast-decaying initial conditions.

The condition (

Well-posedness theory

Let us first remark that we can always assume without loss of generality that
\tau_{c}=1
and
\sigma=1
. Notice that it is possible to set these values by considering the change of variables:

\tilde{\rho}(x,s,t)=\sqrt{\sigma}\rho\left(x,\sqrt{\sigma}s,\tau_{c}\,t\right)\quad\mbox{or equivalently}\quad\rho(x,s,t)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\tilde{\rho}\left(x,\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{\sigma}},\frac{t}{\tau_{c}}\right).

The scaled density

satisfies (
1.1
) with
\tau_{c}=1

,
\tilde{\Phi}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\Phi

For the sake of clarity, we will always drop the tilde and write

2.1
Equivalence to a free boundary problem with Robin boundary condition

We first make the change of variables using selfsimilar variables for the linear Fokker-Planck equation followed by a change through characteristics as in

2.2
Duhamel formulae

In order to obtain a Duhamel formula for

, we use the heat kernel

G(z,\tau,\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi(\tau-\eta)}}e^{-\frac{(z-\xi)^{2}}{4(\tau-\eta)}}.

This kernel satisfies the Green identity

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(G\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\xi}-u\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}\right)-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\big(Gu\big)=0.

For each

, we integrate the identity in the domain
(\gamma(x,t),+\infty)\times(0,\tau)
; we thereby obtain

\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\gamma(x,\eta)}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(G\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\xi}\right)d\xi d\eta}_{I}-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\gamma(x,\eta)}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(u\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}\right)d\xi d\eta}_{II}-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\gamma(x,\eta)}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\big(Gu\big)d\xi d\eta}_{III}=0.

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\left[G(z,\tau,\xi,\eta)\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\xi}(x,\xi,\eta)\right]^{\xi=+\infty}_{\xi=\gamma(x,\eta)}d\eta=\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}G(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\xi}(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)d\eta

\displaystyle=\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}G(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)\Psi(x,\eta)u(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)d\eta.

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle II=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\left[u(x,\xi,\eta)\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(z,\tau,\xi,\eta)\right]^{\xi=+\infty}_{\xi=\gamma(x,\eta)}d\eta=\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)u(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)d\eta.

Let us assume for now that
\gamma
is smooth enough to be differentiated in time, which will be proved later; then

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma(x,\eta)}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\big(Gu\big)

\displaystyle d\xi d\eta\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\int_{\gamma(x,\eta)}^{+\infty}G(z,\tau,\xi,\eta)u(x,\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta\,+\,\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\eta}(x,\eta)G(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)u(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\int_{\gamma(x,\eta)}^{+\infty}G(z,\tau,\xi,\eta)u(x,\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta\,-\,\Psi(x,\eta)G(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)u(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta).

Last, using that in the limit

\displaystyle III\,=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\left[\int_{\gamma(x,\eta)}^{+\infty}G(z,\tau,\xi,\eta)u(x,\xi,\tau)d\xi\right]^{\eta=\tau}_{\eta=0}-\int_{0}^{\tau}G(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)\Psi(x,\eta)u(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)d\eta

\displaystyle\,u(x,z,\tau)-\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(z,\tau,\xi,0)u^{0}(x,\xi)d\xi-\int_{0}^{\tau}G(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)\Psi(x,\eta)u(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)d\eta.

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(z,\tau,\xi,0)u^{0}(x,\xi)d\xi+\int_{0}^{\tau}\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(z,\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)u(x,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)d\eta.

2.3
Existence of a local solution

A natural idea that is akin to the method used in

]
would be to look for a continuous solution of the closed system (

)-(

)-(

) by applying the Banach fixed point theorem in a closed bounded subspace of

\gamma

and derive some handy estimates on

along the way. Let us consider
\tau_{0}\in(0,1]

is arbitrary and serves the sole purpose of estimating the contribution of the exterior input function

&\gamma&\mapsto&\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma},\end{array}

where

\tau_{1}\in(0,1]
, define the space

small enough,

endowed witht the norm

\gamma
, the solution to (

If we choose

small enough with respect to

,
\beta
,

and

.

Let us prove now that

is a contraction for
\tau_{1}
small enough. Let

. The function
\Phi

such that for all

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{\bar{u}}(\gamma_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{\bar{u}}(\gamma_{2})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\tau}\big|\Phi(\alpha(\eta)W\ast[\bar{u}(x,\eta)-\gamma_{1}(x,\eta)]+\beta(\eta))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\Phi(\alpha(\eta)W\ast[\bar{u}(x,\eta)-\gamma_{2}(x,\eta)]+\beta(\eta))\big|d\eta

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\Phi}|W\ast[\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}](x,\eta)|d\eta

\displaystyle\leqslant\tau_{1}C_{\Phi}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau_{1}])}.

If
\tau_{1}
is small enough, then
\tau_{1}C_{\Phi}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}<1
and
\mathcal{T}_{\bar{u}}
is a contraction on

.
Hence, there exists a unique solution
\gamma
to (
2.10
) in

and it satisfies

. For all

\Psi

\displaystyle|\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\tau}\big|\Phi(\alpha(\eta)W\ast[\bar{u}_{1}(x,\eta)-\gamma_{1}(x,\eta)]+\beta(\eta))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\Phi(\alpha(\eta)W\ast[\bar{u}_{2}(x,\eta)-\gamma_{2}(x,\eta)]+\beta(\eta))\big|d\eta

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\Phi}|W\ast[\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}+\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}](x,\eta)|d\eta

\displaystyle\leqslant\tau C_{\Phi}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau])}+\tau C_{\Phi}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau_{1}])}.

Taking the supremum on

By the previous choice of

and use the constant

as a bound that is independent of

small enough (smaller than

Remark 2.2

(Blow-up of the free boundary)
.

In the previous lemma, we treated the case where the initial position of the boundary is not 0 and we can see in the proof that
\left\lVert\gamma^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
impacts the local existence time
\tau_{0}
. Note that, regardless of
\bar{u}
, it is easy to construct examples of blow-up of the free boundary: taking
\Phi(p)=p^{2}
,
\gamma^{0}
constant,
W(x)\equiv 1
,
\beta\equiv 0
, and
\bar{u}\equiv 0
, (
2.10
) reduces to
\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)=\alpha(\tau)^{3}\gamma(\tau)^{2}
. If the initial value of
\gamma^{0}
is spatially constant and large enough, this equation blows-up in finite time before
\alpha(\tau)
has the chance to dampen the dynamics.

An initial position of the boundary which is not uniformly 0 allows us to take a solution of the Stefan problem at some time
\tau_{0}
and construct a new local solution on
[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]
from there. However, the initial position of the boundary would lead to unecessary technical difficulties in the following proofs. Therefore, we assume without loss of generality that
\gamma^{0}\equiv 0
. This can be achieved by a change of variable
z^{\prime}=z-\gamma^{0}
and it comes with a minor modification of
u^{0}
. The effect of
\gamma^{0}
upon the local time of existence is not lost in this process, for the new initial average
\bar{u}^{0}(x)+\gamma^{0}(x)\left\lVert u^{0}(x)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\gamma^{0}(x),+\infty)}
now bears it. This change of variables has to be done at each final time of the intervals in which the extension of the solution is performed.

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume
\Phi

We proceed in modified variables by looking at the equation satisfied by

We eventually apply (

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume
\Phi
is globally Lipschitz,
B\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
W\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and that
\rho^{0}
is a compatible initial condition. Then,
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
) admits a unique global-in-time solution

1.4

Consider a local solution
\rho
over the maximal time interval

,

. If we consider

\rho
is non-negative. As a consequence,
\bar{\rho}(x,t)
and

must remain non-negative as well. In modified variables, due to the equivalence formulae above,
v(x,\tau)
is also positive but not necessarily

. Let us denote by

the Lipschitz coefficient of

Assume for the sake of contradiction that

is finite. Note that we can always choose
T^{*}
as small as we want without loss of generality by changing the initial time to
T^{*}-\varepsilon
and taking the new initial condition
\rho(\cdot,T^{*}-\varepsilon)
, for some small
\varepsilon
; then this solution blows-up at time
\varepsilon
. We proceed with tge proof in the following steps.

1.

Assume the average
\bar{\rho}
and the boundary value
\rho(\cdot,0,\cdot)
are uniformly bounded on
[0,T^{*})
and conclude that the free boundary position is then uniformly bounded.

2.

Prove a uniform bound on

for the average
\bar{\rho}
.

3.

Use Lemma
2.8
and step 1 to get bounds on all the quantities involved in condition (
2.12
) for the maximal time of existence.

Step 1. Conditional bound for the free boundary.-
Assume
\bar{\rho}
and
\rho(\cdot,0,\cdot)
(respectively
\bar{u}
and
v
) are uniformly bounded on
[0,T^{*})
(respectively
[0,\tau^{*})
,
\tau^{*}=\frac{1}{2}(e^{2\frac{T^{*}}{\tau_{c}}}-1)
). For all

\displaystyle|\gamma(x,\tau)|

\displaystyle=\left|\gamma^{0}(x)-\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\Phi\left(\alpha(\eta)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma}}W\ast[\bar{u}(x,\eta)-\gamma(x,\eta)]+\beta(\eta)\right)\alpha(\eta)d\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert\gamma^{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{T^{*}C_{\Phi}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\left(\frac{\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\left(\left\lVert\bar{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau^{*}))}+\left\lVert\gamma(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right)+\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,\tau^{*}])}\right).

Rearranging, taking the supremum on the left-hand side and choosing
T^{*}
small enough without loss of generality (as explained above), we obtain the uniform bound

\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau^{*}))}\leqslant\dfrac{\sqrt{\sigma}\left\lVert\gamma^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+T^{*}C_{\Phi}\left(\frac{\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\left\lVert\bar{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau^{*}))}+\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,\tau^{*}])}\right)}{\sqrt{\sigma}-T^{*}C_{\Phi}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}.

Step 2. Bound for the average
\bar{\rho}
.-
Consider a test function

satisfying

•

is increasing on
[0,+\infty)

•

;

•

Hence, there exist constants

Applying (
2.4

\forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(s)\rho(x,s,t)ds=\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(s)\rho^{0}(x,s)ds\\
\frac{1}{\tau_{c}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left[(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t^{\prime})-s)\dfrac{dh}{ds}(s)+\sigma\dfrac{d^{2}h}{ds^{2}}(s)\right]\rho(x,s,t^{\prime})dsdt^{\prime},

(2.33)

and applying the bounds on the derivative of

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(s)\rho(x,s,t)ds\leqslant\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(s)\rho^{0}(x,s)ds+\frac{1}{\tau_{c}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\dfrac{C_{h}^{1}}{L^{d}}|\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t^{\prime})|+\dfrac{\sigma C_{h}^{2}}{L^{d}}\right)dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(s)\rho^{0}(x,s)ds+\frac{1}{\tau_{c}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\dfrac{C_{h}^{1}C_{\Phi}}{L^{d}}(\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert\bar{\rho}(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}))}+\dfrac{\sigma C_{h}^{2}}{L^{d}}\right)dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(x,t)\leqslant\,

\displaystyle+\dfrac{T^{*}}{\tau_{c}L^{d}}\left(C_{h}^{1}C_{\Phi}\left(\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert\bar{\rho}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,t))}+\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}))}\right)+\sigma C_{h}^{2}\right).

Assuming

is small enough and taking the supremum on

in the left-hand side, we eventually get

\left\lVert\bar{\rho}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,t))}\leqslant\dfrac{\tau_{c}+\tau_{c}L^{d}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(s)\rho^{0}(x,s)ds+\sigma C_{h}^{2}T^{*}+C_{h}^{1}C_{\Phi}T^{*}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}))}}{\tau_{c}L^{d}-T^{*}C_{h}^{1}C_{\Phi}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}.

and obtain

2.5
Uniform bounds when
\Phi
is of constant sign

When we know the sign of the function
\Phi
, we can establish more precise bounds on the two main quantities
\bar{\rho}(x,t)
and
\rho(x,0,t)
. The case
\Phi\geqslant 0
is quite natural in computational neuroscience.

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume
\Phi
is globally Lipschitz,
B\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
W\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and
\rho^{0}
is a compatible initial condition. If
\Phi
is non-negative, then

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ \forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad\rho(x,0,t)\leqslant\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi\sigma}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}}}}.

(2.34)

For all
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
, there exists
\bar{C}_{T}
such that

\forall t\in[0,T],\ \forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad\bar{\rho}(x,t)\leqslant\bar{C}_{T}.

Proof.

Let us proceed in modified variables and prove a bound on
v(x,t)
. Note that the non-negativity of
\Phi
implies non-negativity of
\Psi
. Then, building on the fact that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(\gamma(x,\tau),\tau,\gamma(x,\eta),\eta)=\dfrac{\gamma(x,\tau)-\gamma(x,\eta)}{4\sqrt{\pi}(\tau-\eta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{(\gamma(x,\tau)-\gamma(x,\eta))^{2}}{4(\tau-\eta)}}=\dfrac{-\int_{\eta}^{\tau}\Psi(x,\zeta)d\zeta}{4\sqrt{\pi}(\tau-\eta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{(\gamma(x,\tau)-\gamma(x,\eta))^{2}}{4(\tau-\eta)}},

we rewrite the expression (
2.5
) for
v
in order to make signs salient:

\displaystyle v(x,\tau)=\,\,

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(\gamma(x,\tau),\tau,\xi,0)u^{0}(x,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{\tau}\dfrac{\int_{\eta}^{\tau}\Psi(x,\zeta)d\zeta}{4\sqrt{\pi}(\tau-\eta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{(\gamma(x,\tau)-\gamma(x,\eta))^{2}}{4(\tau-\eta)}}v(x,\eta)d\eta.

(2.35)

All the factors under the second integral being non-negative, and
u^{0}
having unit mass, we claim

0\leqslant v(x,\tau)\leqslant 2\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(\gamma(x,\tau),\tau,\xi,0)u^{0}(x,\xi)d\xi\leqslant\dfrac{1}{L^{d}\sqrt{\pi\tau}}.

In original variables (see formula (
2.28
)), this translates into the uniform bound

0\leqslant\rho(x,0,t)\leqslant\dfrac{e^{\frac{t}{\tau_{c}}}}{\sqrt{\sigma}L^{d}\sqrt{2\pi\left(e^{\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}}-1\right)}}=\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi\sigma}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}}}}.

By Lemma
2.7
, there is also a uniform bound over the average
\bar{\rho}(x,t)
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

Note that the bound over the boundary term is quantitative and universal, i.e. independent of the initial condition
\rho^{0}
, which is made possible by the fact that the upper bound tends to
+\infty
exponentially fast when time goes to
0
. However, the bound
\bar{C}_{T}
over the average can tend to
+\infty
when

tends to
+\infty
, as can be seen through the special case
\Phi(z)=z
,
W(x)\equiv 2
,
\tau_{c}=\sigma=1
,

,

,
\rho^{0}
constant in

:

\dfrac{\partial\bar{\rho}}{\partial t}(t)=\bar{\rho}(t)+\rho(0,t),

which admits the exponentially increasing subsolution

.

Remark 2.13

(Optimality of the bound (
2.34
))
.

The bound is saturated in the high noise regime under the application-relevant assumptions of
[
13
]
. Assume
\Phi
is an increasing function with
\Phi(x)=0
for
x\leqslant 0
, the external input is constant and positive (
B(t)=B>0
), and the connectivity kernel is average inhibitory:

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x)dx=W_{0}<0.

Then, by
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
, if

\sigma>\dfrac{L^{2d}\pi B^{2}}{2|W_{0}|^{2}},

there exists a unique

-independent stationary state

of the form

\rho_{\infty}(s)=\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi\sigma}}\,e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2\sigma}}.

We readily see that

\rho_{\infty}(0)=\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi\sigma}},

which corresponds exactly to the bound (

) in the limit

.

Lemma 2.14
.

Assume
\Phi
is globally Lipschitz,
B\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
W\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\rho^{0}
is a compatible initial condition. If
\Phi
is non-positive, then

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ \forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad\bar{\rho}(x,t)\leqslant\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}(1+\sigma-\sigma e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}})+e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{2}\rho^{0}(x,s)ds.

For all
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
, there exists
C_{T}
such that

\forall t\in[0,T],\ \forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad\rho(x,0,t)\leqslant C_{T}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Applying (
2.4
) to

yields

\tau_{c}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{2}\rho(x,s,t)ds=2\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t)\bar{\rho}(x,t)-2\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{2}\rho(x,s,t)ds+\dfrac{2\sigma}{L^{d}}.

Denoting

M_{2}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{2}\rho(x,s,t)ds

and using non-negativity of
\bar{\rho}
and non-positivity of
\Phi
, we come to

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(M_{2}(x,t)-\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}\right)\leqslant-\frac{2}{\tau_{c}}\left(M_{2}(x,t)-\dfrac{\sigma}{L^{d}}\right).

Hence, by Grönwall’s lemma applied to each point
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
separately, we have

M_{2}(x,t)\leqslant\dfrac{\sigma}{L^{d}}(1-e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}})+M_{2}(x,0)e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}},

which translates into the uniform bound

\forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad M_{2}(x,t)\leqslant\dfrac{\sigma}{L^{d}}(1-e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}})+\left\lVert M_{2}(\cdot,0)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}}.

We eventually notice that

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}s\rho(x,s,t)ds+\int_{1}^{+\infty}s\rho(x,s,t)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\rho(x,s,t)ds+\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{2}\rho(x,s,t)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho(x,s,t)ds+\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{2}\rho(x,s,t)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}+\dfrac{\sigma}{L^{d}}(1-e^{-2\frac{t}{\tau_{c}}})+\left\lVert M_{2}(\cdot,0)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau_{c}}}.

Thanks to Lemma
2.8
, we can lift this result to uniform bounds over the boundary term
\rho(x,0,t)
on all compact time intervals.

∎

Remark 2.15
.

The uniform estimate on
\bar{\rho}
is meaningful only when the second moment is finite for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, which is enforced by the fast-decay in Definitions
1.2
and
1.4
. For a solution which would not be fast-decaying, but only have linear decay, finite first moment, and linear derivative decay, a less precise uniform estimate can be obtained by applying (
2.4
) to the function
h
we used in the proof of Theorem
2.9
.

3
Stability of stationary states

We study the nonlinear stability of the stationary states of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) under the assumption of constant input
B
.

By setting the left hand side of (
1.1
) to zero and integrating with respect to
s
, and using the no-flux boundary condition (
1.2
), the stationary states satisfy

\sigma\partial_{s}\rho(x,s)=-\left(s-\Phi\left(W\ast\bar{\rho}(x)+B\right)\right)\rho(x,s).

Thus, the stationary states of (
1.1
) must solve

\rho(x,s)=\dfrac{1}{Z_{\rho}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{\big(s-\Phi\left(W\ast\bar{\rho}(x)+B\big)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma}\right),

(3.1)

where
Z_{\rho}
is the normalisation factor,

\displaystyle Z_{\rho}=L^{d}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-\dfrac{\big(s-\Phi\left(W\ast\bar{\rho}(x)+B\big)\right)^{2}}{2\sigma}\right)\textrm{d}s.

A full characterisation of the stationary solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfying (
3.1
) does not exist. It was shown in
[
11
, Prop. 3.1]
that when
W,B
, and
\Phi
satisfy:
\Phi
is an increasing function with
\Phi(x)=0
for
x\leqslant 0
, the external input is constant and positive (
B(t)=B>0
), and the connectivity kernel is average inhibitory, that is

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x)dx=W_{0}<0,

then (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) admits a unique spatially homogeneous stationary state for any
\sigma>0
.

Before embarking on the stability of stationary states of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), as a warm-up we investigate in what sense the noiseless (
\sigma=0
) dynamics are relaxed as time increases.

3.1
A Lyapunov functional for the noiseless case

Assume that
\Phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a positive and strictly increasing function. Following
[
21
]
and others, we define the functional

\mathcal{E}:\zeta\to\mathcal{E}[\zeta]=-\frac{1}{2L^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x-y)\zeta(y)\zeta(x)dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\zeta(x)}\left(\Phi^{-1}(\omega)-B\right)d\omega dx.

(3.2)

on
C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Given a solution
\bar{\rho}
to the problem

\dfrac{\partial\bar{\rho}}{\partial t}(x,t)=-\bar{\rho}(x,t)+\dfrac{\Phi(W\ast\bar{\rho}(x,t)+B)}{L^{d}},

(3.3)

and denoting

\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t)=\Phi(W\ast\bar{\rho}+B),\qquad\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}(x,t)=\Phi^{\prime}(W\ast\bar{\rho}+B),

we compute

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{E}[\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}]}{\partial t}(t)=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{L^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x-y)\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(y)\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}}{\partial t}(x,t)dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W\ast\bar{\rho}(x)\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}}{\partial t}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}}{\partial t}(x,t)\left(-W\ast\bar{\rho}(x,t)+W\ast\dfrac{\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t)}{L^{d}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}}{\partial t}(x,t)W\ast\dfrac{\partial\bar{\rho}}{\partial t}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Phi^{\prime}_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t)\left(W\ast\dfrac{\partial\bar{\rho}}{\partial t}(x,t)\right)^{2}dx\ \leqslant\ 0.

If
\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}
is uniformly bounded in time, so is
\mathcal{E}[\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}]
and then, owing to the compactness of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
the function
\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}
converges in time towards a function
\Phi_{0}
which is a local minimiser of
\mathcal{E}
. Hence, without noise, the (now first order) PDE (
1.1
) supplemented with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition has a firing rate
\Phi(W\ast\bar{\rho}+B)
that converges towards a constant-in-time
\Phi_{0}
, and hence this
\Phi_{0}
must be such that
\Phi_{0}=\Phi(W\ast\bar{\rho}_{\infty}+B)
for some stationary state
\bar{\rho}_{\infty}
. In summary, when
\sigma=0
, the nonlinear PDE (
1.1
) completely reduces, through the firing rate
\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t)
, to the dynamics of a classical deterministic Wilson–Cowan model
[
30
,
31
]
, which is already well understood.

3.2
Asymptotic stability of the stationary states for the system with noise

For simplicity, we assume that
\tau_{c}=L=1
in the following proofs. The results can be extended to any
\tau_{c},L>0
by an appropriate change of variables. Let
\rho_{\infty}
be a stationary state of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and define

\Phi_{0}(x)=\Phi(W\ast\bar{\rho}_{\infty}(x)+B),\qquad\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)=\Phi^{\prime}(W\ast\bar{\rho}_{\infty}(x)+B),

and
h=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\infty}}
. Let
G(h)
be a convex function of
h
. Then the relative entropy at position
x
with respect to
G
,

\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(h(x))\rho_{\infty}(x)ds,

satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(h)\rho_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}G^{\prime}(h)\left(-\partial_{s}\left(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}-s\right)\rho+\sigma\partial_{ss}\rho\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}G^{\prime\prime}(h)\partial_{s}h\left(-\left(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}-s\right)\rho+\sigma\partial_{s}\rho\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}G^{\prime\prime}(h)\partial_{s}h\left(-\left(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}-s\right)h+\frac{\sigma}{\rho_{\infty}}\partial_{s}\rho\right)\rho_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}G^{\prime\prime}(h)\left(\left(\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}h+\sigma|\partial_{s}h|^{2}\right)\rho_{\infty}ds,

by an integration by parts and then using the relation
\frac{\partial_{s}\rho}{\rho_{\infty}}=\partial_{s}h+\frac{1}{\sigma}(\Phi_{0}-s)h
.

Let us define the variance of the probability measure
L^{d}\rho_{\infty}(x,\cdot)
at each
x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
as

\displaystyle M_{\infty}(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(s-L^{d}\bar{\rho}_{\infty}(x))^{2}\rho_{\infty}(x,s)ds,

(3.4)

where
L
is set to 1 in the proofs.
Letting
G(h)={\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(h-1)^{2}
and utilising the Poincaré inequality with respect to the measure
\rho_{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\int_{0}^{+\infty}|g-\bar{g}|^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds\leqslant\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\partial_{s}g|^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds,

(3.5)

where
\bar{g}=\int g(s)\rho_{\infty}(s)ds,
see
[
25
,
27
]
, we can prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Phi
be Lipschitz,
W\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and
\rho_{\infty}
be a stationary state of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) given a
\sigma
. Assume that
\sigma
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\frac{\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}}{L^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(x)<\frac{\sigma}{2}\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}},

(3.6)

where
\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\gamma(\rho_{\infty}(x))
, and
\gamma(\rho_{\infty}(x))
is the Poincaré constant of (
3.5
) for
\rho_{\infty}(x)
, and
M_{\infty}
is defined in (
3.4
). Then, the
L^{1}
norm of the relative entropy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho(x,s,t)-\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}{\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}(x,s)dsdx,

decays exponentially fast whenever the compatible initial data
\rho_{0}
is close enough to the stationary state
\rho_{\infty}
in relative entropy.

Remark 3.2
.

Notice that there exist stationary states of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfying condition (
3.6
). Indeed, the bound
\gamma(\rho_{\infty}(x))\geqslant\frac{1}{\sigma}
can be generically ensured due to sharp Poincaré constants, see for example
[
27
, Thm. 3]
or the book
[
2
]
. As a consequence, stationary states satisfying

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\frac{\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}}{L^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(x)<\frac{\sigma^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}}{2},

(3.7)

are by Proposition
3.1
exponentially stable. In
[
13
, Lem. 2.3-2.4]
it was shown that

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma}=\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}\,g\left(\frac{\Phi_{0}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right),\qquad g(\eta):=1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\exp(-\eta^{2})}{1+\erf(\eta)}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\exp(-\eta^{2})}{1+\erf(\eta)}+\eta\right],

where
g
is an increasing function whose values lie in the interval
\big(1-\frac{2}{\pi},1\big)
. Thus, a sufficient condition for local asymptotic stability of all stationary states is

\frac{1}{L^{d}}\left\lVert\Phi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}<\frac{1}{2}.

(3.8)

It is an open problem to determine whether multiple stationary states can coexist under (
3.7
) and if there is only one stationary state under (
3.8
). Note also that the length of the domain
L
plays a special role: when the domain becomes smaller, the sufficient condition for stability requires that W becomes smaller in
L^{2}
norm. Another way to see this is to take
W\equiv w
, where
w\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant, then the condition is not on
w
but on the rescaled quantity
\tfrac{w}{L^{d}}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Assume
\Phi
is an increasing function with
\Phi(x)=0
for
x\leqslant 0
, the external input is constant and positive (
B(t)=B>0
), and the connectivity kernel is average inhibitory, that is

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x)\,dx=W_{0}<0.

Then, we can deduce that in the high-noise case
\sigma>\tfrac{\pi B^{2}}{2|W_{0}|^{2}}
,
the unique space-homogeneous stationary state

\rho_{\infty}(s)=\dfrac{1}{L^{d}}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi\sigma}}\,e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2\sigma}},

is locally asymptotically stable under under the sufficient condition

\left\lVert\Phi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant\frac{L^{d}}{2\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{2}{\pi}\right)}}.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let
G(h)={\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(h-1)^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}h\rho_{\infty}ds-\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\partial_{s}h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\partial_{s}h(h-1)\rho_{\infty}ds-\left(\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}ds-\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\partial_{s}h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left|\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\partial_{s}h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds-\left(\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}ds,

by applying the inequality
|ab|\leqslant{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(a^{2}+b^{2})
with
a=\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\infty}}{\sigma}}(h-1)(\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}})
and
b=\sqrt{\sigma\rho_{\infty}}\partial_{s}h
. We bound the difference
\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x-y)\int_{0}^{+\infty}s(\rho_{\infty}-\rho)(s,y)dsdy\right|

\displaystyle=\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x-y)\int_{0}^{+\infty}s(1-h)(s,y)\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right|

\displaystyle=\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(x-y)\int_{0}^{+\infty}(s-\bar{\rho}_{\infty})(1-h)(s,y)\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|W(x-y)|M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|W(x-y)|^{2}dy\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left[M_{\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds\right]dy\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}

\displaystyle=\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left[M_{\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds\right]dy\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}

\displaystyle=C(\Phi,W,\rho_{\infty})\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}},

by applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality twice, first in
s
, then in
y
. Summing up and by applying the Poincaré inequality (
3.5
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2\sigma}C^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+C\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right|\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\partial_{s}h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2\sigma}C^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(C-\frac{\sigma}{2}\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\partial_{s}h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}.

By assumption (
3.6
),
\tilde{C}:=C-\frac{\sigma}{2}\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}<0
. Thus, we can apply the Poincaré inequality (
3.5
) again, which results in

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy

\displaystyle\leqslant\left[\frac{C^{2}}{2\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy+\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\tilde{C}\right]\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy.

The expression between the brackets will be negative as long as we choose
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy
small enough at
t=0
and
\sigma
satisfies (
3.4
). Thus, we get exponential decay in time of the relative entropy under condition (
3.6
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The exponential decay rate obtained in Proposition
3.1
depends on the initial condition: we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx\leqslant e^{-2Kt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho^{0}-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx.

with

\displaystyle K=

\displaystyle\ \tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}-\frac{1}{L^{d}}\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}(x)}{2L^{d}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho^{0}-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx.

However, for all
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
, there exists
T_{\varepsilon}
, depending on
\rho^{0}
, such that for all
t\in(T_{\varepsilon},+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho(x,s,t)-\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}{\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}(x,s)dsdx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,e^{-2K_{\varepsilon}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho(x,s,T_{\varepsilon})-\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}{\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}(x,s)dsdx,

where
K_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}-\frac{1}{L^{\frac{d}{2}}}\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(x)\right)-\varepsilon
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Grant the assumptions of Proposition
3.1
. Assume further that either

•

\Phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),
and

-K:=\dfrac{2\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}}{\sigma}+\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\frac{\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}}{L^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(x)-\frac{\sigma}{2}\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\right)<0,\quad\mathrm{or}

•

W\leqslant 0
,
B\geqslant 0
,
\Phi
is non-negative and increasing, and

-K:=\dfrac{\Phi(B)^{2}}{\sigma}+\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\frac{\|W\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}}{L^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}M_{\infty}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(x)-\frac{\sigma}{2}\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\right)<0.

Then, for all compatible initial data
\rho_{0}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho^{0}(x,s)-\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}{\rho_{\infty}(x,s)}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}(x,s)dsdx<+\infty,

the
L^{1}
norm of the relative entropy satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx\leqslant e^{-2Kt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho^{0}-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx.

In particular, the stationary state
\rho_{\infty}
is the unique stationary state of the equation.

Proof.

Let
M
be either
\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{\infty}
or
\Phi(B)
. We notice that in both cases, for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}(x,t)\leqslant M
.
Then, coming back to (
3.9
), we now use the bound

\left|\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right|\leqslant 2M\ \ (\mathrm{first\ case})\qquad\mathrm{or}\qquad\left|\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}\right|\leqslant M\ \ (\mathrm{second\ case})

in the first term of the last inequality, while leaving the remainder of the proof of Proposition
3.1
unchanged. It yields, for example in the first case, the differential inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy

\displaystyle\leqslant\left[\frac{2M^{2}}{\sigma}+\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\tilde{C}\right]\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy.

\displaystyle\leqslant-2K\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}(h-1)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdy.

Since all stationary states have finite relative entropy, any other stationary state
\rho^{1}_{\infty}
converges towards
\rho_{\infty}
in relative entropy, and hence the uniqueness of the stationary state.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The second case of the corrollary applies to the setting of
[
13
]
with the additional assumption of a negative (fully inhibitory) connectivity kernel
W\leqslant 0
. With the same arguments as in the previous remarks, the sufficient condition can be simplified to the less sharp, but clearer,

\sigma>\frac{4\Phi(B)^{4}}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{L^{d}}\left\lVert\Phi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right)^{2}}>0\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\frac{1}{L^{d}}\left\lVert\Phi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}<\frac{1}{2},

which partially confirms the conjecture that in the high-noise case there is at most one stationary state which is homogeneous in time and globally asymptotically stable.

Although the above result applies to some stationary states, it is difficult to determine the full range of
\sigma
for which condition (
3.6
) actually holds. Therefore, building on the linear stability estimate in
[
11
]
, we establish below that the spatially homogeneous stationary states are nonlinearly asymptotically stable under similar conditions to the linearised case. In
[
11
]
it was shown that spatially homogeneous stationary states of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) are linearly exponentially stable if all the unnormalised Fourier modes of
W
,
\hat{W}_{k}
, defined as

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{k}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}W(\mathbf{x})\exp(2\pi k\cdot\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

satisfy the condition

\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{k}<\frac{\sigma}{M_{\infty}}.

(3.10)

This was done by carefully combining estimates for the time derivative of the relative entropy of each Fourier mode
(\hat{\rho}-\hat{\rho}_{\infty})_{k}(s,t)
of the perturbation
(\rho-\rho_{\infty})(x,s,t)
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(\hat{\rho}-\hat{\rho}_{\infty})_{k}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds

and the square of the Fourier modes of the perturbation of the mean,
(\hat{\bar{\rho}}-\hat{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty})_{k}
,
((\hat{\bar{\rho}}-\hat{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty})_{k})^{2}
, yielding exponential decay of the quantities

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{(\hat{\rho}-\hat{\rho}_{\infty})_{k}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds-\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\prime}}{\sigma}\hat{W}_{k}((\hat{\bar{\rho}}-\hat{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty})_{k})^{2},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

(3.11)

which where shown to be positive under condition (
3.10
). This was sufficient to prove that perturbations away from the spatially homogeneous state are linearly exponentially stable in relative entropy. Condition (
3.10
) was later found to be optimal in the sense that the condition leading to bifurcations along the branch of spatially homogeneous solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is exactly (
3.10
) with the inequality replaced by an equality
[
13
]
.

Motivated by the approach of the proof in
[
11
]
, we carefully obtain estimates for a combination of the time derivatives of the quantities

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\rho-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds,\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathcal{H}=\Phi^{\delta}\bar{\rho}^{\delta},

where
\Phi^{\delta}=\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}-\Phi_{0}
,
\bar{\rho}^{\delta}=\bar{\rho}-\bar{\rho}_{\infty}
. Linearizing
\Phi^{\delta}
, we get

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{E}-\frac{\mathcal{H}}{\sigma}\simeq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\rho-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds-\frac{\Phi^{\prime}_{0}}{\sigma}W\ast(\bar{\rho}-\bar{\rho}_{\infty})(\bar{\rho}-\bar{\rho}_{\infty}).

Integrating the previous expression in
x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}2\mathcal{E}-\frac{\mathcal{H}}{\sigma}dx\simeq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\rho-\rho_{\infty}}{\rho_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}_{0}}{\sigma}W\ast(\bar{\rho}-\bar{\rho}_{\infty})(\bar{\rho}-\bar{\rho}_{\infty})dx.

Notice that assuming
\rho_{\infty}
is spatially homogeneous and using the Parseval–Plancherel identity, we obtain the sum of the Fourier coefficients in (
3.11
).

Now, utilising the similarity between (
3.11
) and
2\mathcal{E}-\frac{\mathcal{H}}{\sigma}
, we show exponential decay of the latter in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
under conditions closely related to the linear stability condition (
3.10
), see Remark
3.8
for an ellaboration on the relation between the conditions. This will in turn give exponential decay of the relative entropy (
3.14
). To complete the proof, we will, in addition to the equality (
3.9
) and the Poincaré inequality (
3.5
), also need the time evolution of
\bar{\rho}^{\delta}={\bar{\rho}}-{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}({\bar{\rho}}-{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty})=\left(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}-\Phi_{0}\right)-\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}ds.

(3.12)

Equation (
3.12
) is derived from (
2.31
) and noting that

\displaystyle{\bar{\rho}}-{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}s(\rho-\rho_{\infty})ds=\int_{0}^{+\infty}s(h-1)\rho_{\infty}ds=\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{s-\Phi_{0}}{\sigma}(h-1)\rho_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle=-\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)\partial_{s}\rho_{\infty}ds=\sigma(\rho-\rho_{\infty})(0)+\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}ds.

We will also use the notation

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}=\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(x,s)^{2}\rho_{\infty}(x,s)ds\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\!\!,\qquad\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(x,s)^{2}\rho_{\infty}(x,s)dsdx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\!\!.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
W\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and
\rho_{\infty}
be a spatially homogeneous stationary state of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) given a
\sigma
. Assume that
W
is componentwise symmetric, and that for a suitably small
1>\alpha>0
,
\sigma
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\alpha)g^{2}(x)-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}_{g}(x)}{\sigma}g(x)\,dx>0,\quad g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

(3.13)

where

\Phi^{\delta}_{g}(x)=\Phi\big(M_{\infty}W\ast g(x)+W_{0}{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty}+B\big)-\Phi_{0}.

Then, the
L^{1}
norm of the relative entropy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho(x,s,t)-\rho_{\infty}(s)}{\rho_{\infty}(s)}\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}(s)dsdx,

(3.14)

decays exponentially fast whenever the compatible initial data
\rho_{0}
is close enough to the stationary state
\rho_{\infty}
in relative entropy.

Remark 3.8
.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\alpha)g^{2}-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}_{g}}{\sigma}g\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant(1-\alpha)\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}gdx\right)^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\delta}_{g}}{\sigma}g\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g(x)\left(1-\alpha-\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma}\tilde{\Phi}_{g}^{\prime}(x)W(x-y)\right)g(y)dydx,

(3.15)

with
\tilde{\Phi}_{g}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\Phi^{\delta}_{g}(x)}{W\ast g(x)}.
Requiring that the right hand side of the equality in (
3.8
) is positive is quite similar to the linear stability condition of
[
11
]
, see (
3.10
). In fact, assuming
\Phi
is linear, then
\Phi^{\delta}_{g}=M_{\infty}W\ast g
, and the right hand side of the equality of (
3.8
) is

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g(x)\left(1-\alpha-\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma}\Phi^{\prime}W(x-y)\right)g(y)dydx.

Thus, in the linear
\Phi
case requiring that the right hand side of (
3.8
) is positive, which is also known as requiring that
c(x)=1-\alpha-\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma}\Phi^{\prime}W(x)
is H-stable, see
[
10
]
, is identical to requiring that
c(x)
only has positive unnormalised Fourier modes. This is again identical to the linear condition (
3.10
) if we replace the numerator on the right hand side of (
3.10
) with
\sigma(1-\alpha)
. Lastly, if
\Phi
is linear and increasing, and
-W
itself is H-stable, then any spatially homogeneous stationary state is locally asymptotically stable in relative entropy. This is in accordance with the finding of
[
13
]
: there are no noise-driven bifurcations from the curve of homogeneous stationary states when
-W
is H-stable.

Proof of Proposition
3.7
.

Using (
3.9
) and (
3.12
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}2\mathcal{E}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathcal{H}\,dx=\,

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Phi^{\delta}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h(h-1)\rho_{\infty}dsdx-2\sigma\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int|\partial_{s}h|^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx

\displaystyle+3\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Phi^{\delta}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}dsdx-\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\Phi^{\delta})^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}\,\Phi^{\prime}_{\bar{\rho}}W\ast\left(\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}ds\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Phi^{\delta}\left(\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}ds\right)dx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Phi^{\delta}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h(h-1)\rho_{\infty}dsdx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}\,\Phi^{\prime}_{\bar{\rho}}W\ast\left(\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{s}h\rho_{\infty}ds\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\right)^{2}\rho_{\infty}dsdx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\right)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\right)\rho_{\infty}dsdx

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)\Phi^{\delta}\left(\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\right)\rho_{\infty}dsdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,I+II+III,

where the last equality follows by the symmetry of
W
and noting that
\Phi^{\delta}
does not depend on
s
and that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h-1)\rho_{\infty}ds=0
. Notice first that

I=-\frac{2}{\sigma}\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}^{2}\,.

Then, for for
II
,

\displaystyle II\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\big|\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}\big|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\big|dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\big|^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}},

and for
III
, we have

\displaystyle III\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Phi^{\delta}|\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}|\Phi^{\delta}|\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}2\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Phi^{\delta}|^{2}\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}},

yielding

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{E}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathcal{H}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{\sigma}\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}\left(2\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}-\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\big|^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.-2\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Phi^{\delta}|^{2}\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\right).

(3.16)

Let us denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathcal{H}\,dx.

We now sketch the strategy of the remaining steps of the proof. We first claim that
\mathcal{Q}
is equivalent to the relative entropy as

0\,\leqslant\,2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}dx\,\leqslant\,\mathcal{Q}\,\leqslant\,\left(1+\frac{\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}{\sigma}\|W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}M_{\infty}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}dx.

(3.17)

This will be shown in Step 2 below. Then, with the estimates

\displaystyle\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}\geqslant\sigma\gamma(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}

(3.18)

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\big|^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}+2\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Phi^{\delta}|^{2}\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\leqslant 2C_{4}\mathcal{Q},

(3.19)

where

is a positive constant, we show exponential decay of
\mathcal{Q}
, which then yields exponential decay of the relative entropy. The proofs of the estimates are postponed to Step 3 and 4.

Step 1. Exponential decay of
\mathcal{Q}
.-
Assuming (
3.17
)-(
3.19
), let us finalize the proof. From (
3.16
), we conclude

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{Q}\leqslant\,-2\gamma(\rho_{\infty})^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}\left(\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}-C_{4}\mathcal{Q}\right).

(3.20)

Thus, by choosing
\rho_{0}
close enough in relative entropy to
\rho_{\infty}
, we can ensure that the term between the parantheses in (
3.20
) is positive initially, and so
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{Q}\leqslant 0
, which then will keep the term between the parenthesis positive. This allows us to apply (
3.18
) again, such that we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\,-2\sigma\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\left(\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}-C_{4}\mathcal{Q}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\sigma\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\alpha\mathcal{Q}\left(\alpha-C_{4}\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}\right)

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leqslant-2\sigma\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\alpha\left(\alpha-C_{4}\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}_{0}\right)\mathcal{Q},

where
\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}_{0}=\mathcal{Q}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}(t=0)
, yielding exponential decay of
\mathcal{Q}
, and, due to (
3.17
), consequently of (
3.14
) whenever
\rho_{0}
is close enough in relative entropy to
\rho_{\infty}
.

Step 2. Positivity and equivalence to relative entropy of
\mathcal{Q}
.-
As in the proof of
[
11

, let

\displaystyle h(x,s,t)-1=V(x,s,t)+A(x,t)(s-{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty}),

(3.22)

where

is chosen such that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}V(s-{\bar{\rho}}_{\infty})\rho_{\infty}ds=0
for all

, i.e.

. We first rewrite

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}V^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds+M_{\infty}\left(A^{2}-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}}{\sigma}A\right)\,dx.

Due to (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}A^{2}dx\leqslant\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}A^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\Phi^{\delta}A\,dx\right).

This fact entails

\displaystyle 2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}dx

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}V^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds+M_{\infty}A^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\alpha\int_{0}^{+\infty}V^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds+M_{\infty}\left(A^{2}-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}}{\sigma}A\right)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}dx+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Phi^{\delta}||{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}|dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\frac{\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}{\sigma}\|W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}M_{\infty}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}dx,

and concludes the desired equivalence (
3.17
).

Step 3. Proof of (
3.18
).-

By the Poincaré inequality with respect to the measure
\rho_{\infty}
in (
3.5
) with

, at each
x

\displaystyle\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\sigma^{2}\left(\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}^{2}+\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma^{2}}(\Phi^{\delta})^{2}-\frac{2}{\sigma}\Phi^{\delta}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\sigma^{2}\left(2\mathcal{E}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathcal{H}+\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma^{2}}(\Phi^{\delta})^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\Phi^{\delta}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}\right).

(3.23)

Since

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{E}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathcal{H}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}V^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds+M_{\infty}A^{2}-\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma}\Phi^{\delta}A,

the terms between the brackets of (

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{E}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathcal{H}+\frac{M_{\infty}}{\sigma^{2}}(\Phi^{\delta})^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\Phi^{\delta}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}V^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds+M_{\infty}\left(A-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}}{\sigma}\right)^{2}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\left(A-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}}{\sigma}\right)^{2}\geqslant\begin{cases}A^{2}-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}}{\sigma}A,\quad&\sgn(\Phi^{\delta})\neq\sgn(A),\\
\alpha\left(A^{2}-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}}{\sigma}A\right),\quad&\sgn(\Phi^{\delta})=\sgn(A),|\Phi^{\delta}|\geqslant\sigma(1-\alpha)|A|,\\

and thus, we conclude that

\big\|\Phi^{\delta}-\sigma\partial_{s}h\big\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}}}^{2}\geqslant\sigma^{2}\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\alpha^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}V^{2}\rho_{\infty}ds+M_{\infty}\left(A^{2}-\frac{\Phi^{\delta}}{\sigma}A\right)dx\right)=\sigma^{2}\gamma(\rho_{\infty})\alpha^{2}\mathcal{Q},

).

).-

\displaystyle\left|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\right|

To summarise, replacing
h-1
and
{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}
by

and
A

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\big|^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}+2\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Phi^{\delta}|^{2}\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Phi^{\delta}-W\ast(\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})\big|^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}+2\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Phi^{\delta}|^{2}\|h-1\|_{L^{2}_{\rho_{\infty}(x)}}^{2}dx\right)^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{2}}}

\displaystyle\left|\Phi^{\delta}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W(y-x)\Phi_{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}(y){\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 2\|\Phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\|W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}({\bar{\rho}}^{\delta})^{2}dx+\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|{\bar{\rho}}^{\delta}(y)(W(x-y)-W(y-x))\right|dy,

where the first term is as in the proof above and the last term pops up due to the lack of symmetry of

. By a similar argument to the last paragraphs of the proof of Proposition

for the four population model (

4
Extension of the main results to a model for grid cells

The stochastic neural field PDE system (
1.1
) was first proposed in
[
11
]
in the following more general form, with four populations of neurons with an orientation preference:

\displaystyle\tau_{c}\frac{\partial\rho^{\beta}}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Bigg(\Big[\Phi^{\beta}(x,t)-s\Big]\rho^{\beta}\Bigg)+\sigma\frac{\partial^{2}\rho^{\beta}}{\partial s^{2}},

(4.1)

where
\Phi^{\beta}(x,t)
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\beta}(x,t)=\Phi\left(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\beta^{\prime}=1}^{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}W^{\beta^{\prime}}(x-y)\int_{0}^{\infty}s\rho^{\beta^{\prime}}(y,s,t)\,\textrm{d}s\textrm{d}y+B^{\beta}(t)\right),

(4.2)

where the orientation preference in physical space is represented by the index
\beta=1,2,3,4
(north, west, south, east), and with corresponding no-flux boundary and mass normalisation conditions. In the case
d=2
, the system (
4.1
) models a network of noisy grid cells
[
11
]
. The orientation preference is modelled by
\beta-
dependent shifts
r^{\beta}
and connectivity
W^{\beta}(x-y)=W(x-y-r^{\beta})
. The movement of an animal can be connected to this PDE system (
4.1
) through the orientation and time dependent input
B^{\beta}(t)
. The system was rigorously derived from a stochastic particle system in
[
8
]
and its noise-driven bifurcations from the homogeneous stationary state were rigorously characterised in
[
13
]
.

In the same fashion as in
[
13
]
, the main analytical results obtained on the solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be extended to the more general system (
4.1
). By checking the key points of the proof and without writing all the technical details, we first extend the local and global in time existence results of Theorem
1.5
to the four population system to a very general setting, before the asymptotic convergence results of Theorem
1.6
are extended to (
4.1
).

4.1
Global-in-time solutions for Lipschitz modulation functions

The proof of local and global existence of solutions for the four population model (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) being a direct extension of our work in Section
2
, we will only go through the main steps in order to check that no additional difficulty endangers the method.

We can apply the same change of variables as before to each population

y=e^{t}s\qquad\tau=\dfrac{1}{2}(e^{2t}-1),\qquad q^{\beta}(x,y,\tau)=\alpha(\tau)\rho^{\beta}\Big(x,y\alpha(\tau),-\log(\alpha(\tau))\Big),

and then we can apply separately to each population the change of variable

\displaystyle\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\Phi^{\beta}\left(\alpha(\eta)\sum_{\beta=1}^{4}W\ast\bar{q}^{\beta}(x,\eta)+\lambda^{\beta}(\eta)\right)\alpha(\eta)d\eta,

(4.3)

\displaystyle u^{\beta}(x,z,\tau)=q^{\beta}(x,y,\tau),\qquad\bar{u}^{\beta}=\int_{\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)}^{+\infty}zu^{\beta}(z,\tau)dz,\quad v^{\beta}(x,\tau)=u^{\beta}(x,\gamma(x,\tau),\tau).

(4.4)

Then, we obtain a similar free boundary Stefan problem with non-local Robin boundary condition: for
\beta\in\{1,2,3,4\}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial u^{\beta}}{\partial\tau}(x,z,\tau)&=&\dfrac{\partial^{2}u^{\beta}}{\partial z^{2}}(x,z,\tau),\qquad\qquad\qquad\ z\in(\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau),+\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)&=&-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\Psi^{\beta}(x,\eta)d\eta,\\
\dfrac{\partial u^{\beta}}{\partial y}(x,\gamma(x,\tau),\tau)&=&\Psi^{\beta}(x,\tau)u^{\beta}(x,\gamma(x,\tau),\tau),\\
\Psi^{\beta}(x,\tau)&=&\displaystyle\Phi\left(\alpha(\tau)\sum_{\beta=1}^{4}W^{\beta}\ast[\bar{u}^{\beta}(x,\tau)-\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)]+\lambda^{\beta}(\tau)\right)\alpha(\tau),\\
\displaystyle u^{\beta}(x,z,0)&=&u^{0,\beta}(x,z),\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad z\in\,(0,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

The exact same method that we used in Section
2
for the one population model allows us to derive Duhamel formulae for

.

For the solution:

u^{\beta}(x,z,\tau)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(z,\tau,\xi,0)u^{0,\beta}(x,\xi)d\xi+\int_{0}^{\tau}\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(z,\tau,\gamma^{\beta}(x,\eta),\eta)\,v^{\beta}(x,\eta)d\eta.

(4.6)

For the boundary value:

\displaystyle v^{\beta}(x,\tau)=

\displaystyle\,2\int_{0}^{+\infty}G(\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau),\tau,\xi,0)u^{0,\beta}(x,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\,+2\int_{0}^{\tau}\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau),\tau,\gamma^{\beta}(x,\eta),\eta)\,v^{\beta}(x,\eta)d\eta.

(4.7)

For the average:

\displaystyle\bar{u}^{\beta}(x,\tau)=

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)}^{+\infty}zG(z,\tau,\xi,0)dzu^{0,\beta}(x,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\,+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\gamma(x,\tau)}^{+\infty}z\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(z,\tau,\gamma^{\beta}(x,\eta),\eta)dz\,v^{\beta}(x,\eta)d\eta.

(4.8)

For the motion of the boundary:

\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\Phi\left(\alpha(\eta)\sum_{\beta=1}^{4}W^{\beta}\ast[\bar{u}^{\beta}(x,\eta)-\gamma^{\beta}(x,\eta)]+\lambda^{\beta}(\eta)\right)\alpha(\eta)d\eta.

Hence, if we denote in capital letters the vectors containing entries for all
\beta
, we can write the same kind of closed system as before

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}V(x,\tau)&=&F_{v}[V,\Gamma,\bar{U}](x,\tau)\\
\Gamma(x,\tau)&=&F_{\gamma}[V,\Gamma,\bar{U}](x,\tau)\\
\bar{U}(x,\tau)&=&F_{\bar{u}}[V,\Gamma,\bar{U}](x,\tau).\end{array}\right.

(4.10)

We can then consider the space

\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{0},m}=\{\textbf{w}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau_{0}])^{8}\ |\ \left\lVert\textbf{w}\right\rVert_{\infty}<m\},

where we denote

On this space, we consider the functional

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\mathcal{T}:&\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{0},m}&\to&C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\tau_{0}])^{8}\\
&(V,\bar{U})&\mapsto&(F_{v}[V,\Gamma,\bar{U}],F_{\bar{u}}[V,\Gamma,\bar{U}]),\end{array}

(4.11)

where

is the unique continuous solution of the coupled initial value problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dfrac{\partial\gamma^{\beta}}{\partial\tau}(x,\tau)&=&\displaystyle-\Phi\left(\alpha(\tau)\sum_{\beta=0}^{4}W^{\beta}\ast[\bar{u}^{\beta}(x,\tau)-\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)]+\lambda^{\beta}(\tau)\right)\alpha(\tau),\\
\gamma^{\beta}(x,0)&=&\gamma^{0,\beta}(x).\end{array}\right.

(4.12)

Like previously in Lemma

, we can prove that if
\Phi
is locally Lipschitz,

and

to (

). For all

,

like we did above for the one population case, and choose

Remark 4.2
.

An alternative route for the extension of existence to the four population system would be, denoting
\Lambda=(\Lambda^{1},\Lambda^{2},\Lambda^{3},\Lambda^{4})
a vector of four continuous functions of time, to introduce the following functional: consider for each
\beta
the solution of the one population problem (
2.3
) with exterior input
\Lambda^{\beta}
. Given the four solutions of these one population problems, we define
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
as the vector whose entries
\beta\in\{1,2,3,4\}
are

\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)_{\beta}=\sum_{\beta^{\prime}=1,\ \beta^{\prime}\neq\beta}^{4}W^{\beta}\ast[\bar{u}^{\beta}(x,\tau)-\gamma^{\beta}(x,\tau)]+\lambda^{\beta}(\tau).

From the regularities proved in the one population existence proof, it is possible to prove that this functional is a contraction on a suitable Banach space. Applications of the Banach fixed point theorem then yields a unique fixed point
(\Lambda^{1},\Lambda^{2},\Lambda^{3},\Lambda^{4})
. Using these four functions as exterior input in the one-population existence theorem and assembling these carefully crafted pieces, we recover the unique solution of the four population model.

Yet another way to frame the extension is to introduce, instead of the vector
\bar{U}=(\bar{u}^{1},\bar{u}^{2},\bar{u}^{3},\bar{u}^{4})
, the scalar variable

\bar{U}=\sum_{\beta^{\prime}=1}^{4}W^{\beta^{\prime}}\ast\bar{u}^{\beta^{\prime}},

and then to sum the Duhamel formulae for
\bar{u}^{\beta}
in order to include this quantity in the fixed point functional (
4.11
), thus symplifying the space upon which it is defined. This last extension framework was used in
[
13
]
to extend bifurcation results from the one population system to the model for grid cells.

The rigidity of minimal Legendrian submanifolds in the Euclidean spheres via eigenvalues of fundamental matrices
Pei-Yi Wu, Ling Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the rigidity problem for compact minimal Legendrian submanifolds in the unit Euclidean spheres via eigenvalues of fundamental matrices, which measure the squared norms of the second fundamental form on all normal directions. By using Lu’s inequality [ 19 ] on the upper bound of the squared norm of Lie brackets of symmetric matrices, we establish an optimal pinching theorem for such submanifolds of all dimensions, giving a new characterization for the Calabi tori. This pinching condition can also be described by the eigenvalues of the Ricci curvature tensor. Moreover, when the third large eigenvalue of the fundamental matrix vanishes everywhere, we get an optimal rigidity theorem under a weaker pinching condition.

Introduction

In 1968, J. Simons
[
28
]
proved a well-known rigidity theorem as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional compact minimal submanifold
in
S^{n+m}
, denote by
|B|^{2}
be the square norm of the second fundamental form

of

\int_{M}|B|^{2}\left[(2-\frac{1}{m})|B|^{2}-n\right]dM\geq 0.

(1.1)

As a corollary, the pinching condition
0\leq|B|^{2}\leq\frac{n}{2-\frac{1}{m}}
forces
|B|^{2}\equiv 0
or
|B|^{2}\equiv\frac{n}{2-\frac{1}{m}}

As shown by Chern-do Carmo-Kobayashi
[

[

]

is a Clifford torus or a Veronese surface in

.
By the Gauss equation, the scalar curvature

of

is completely determined by

(i.e.
R=n(n-1)-|B|^{2}
), so Simons’ theorem can be seen as an intrinsic
rigidity result on the scalar curvature. Based on this phenomenon, S. S. Chern
[

]
raised a well-known conjecture as follows,

[
40

Let

must lies in a discrete subset
of
{\tenmsb R}
.

From this viewpoint, Simons’ theorem describes the first gap of

. For the hypersurface cases (i.e.

[

[

independently got a rigidity theorem whose condition is weaker than Simons’ theorem:

.

Let

Given a submanifold

)

In the present paper, we study the rigidity properties of compact minimal Legendrian submanifolds via the fundamental matrices.
By utilizing Lu’s inequality (see Lemma
2.8
), we can establish the following pinching theorem, giving a new characterization
of the Calabi tori:

Main Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional compact minimal Legendrian submanifold in
S^{2n+1}

and
\lambda_{2}
be second large eigenvalue of the fundamental matrix at each point. If
|B|^{2}+\lambda_{2}\leq n+1
, then
M
is either a totally geodesic
subsphere (here
|B|^{2}+\lambda_{2}\equiv 0
) or a Calabi torus (here
|B|^{2}+\lambda_{2}\equiv n+1
). This pinching condition is equivalent to

n^{2}-n-2\leq R+\mu_{2}\leq n^{2}-1
, where
R
is the scalar curvature of
M
and
\mu_{2}
denotes the second small eigenvalue of
the Ricci curvature tensor.

This means the above pinching condition is optimal for all dimensions. Essentially, this conclusion gives an intrinsic obstruction
for each compact Riemannian manifold becoming a minimal Legendrian submanifold in the unit Euclidean sphere.

Moreover, we establish another rigidity theorem, which shows the pinching condition can be weakened under additional conditions,
e.g.
\lambda_{3}\equiv 0
.

Main Theorem 2
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional compact minimal Legendrian submanifold in
S^{2n+1}
with
n\geq 3
, such that

\lambda_{3}\equiv 0
. If
n\geq 4
, then
M
has to be totally geodesic. If
n=3
, then
|B|^{2}\leq\frac{16}{3}
(or
|B|^{2}\geq\frac{16}{3}
)
forces
|B|^{2}\equiv 0
or
|B|^{2}\equiv\frac{16}{3}
(or
|B|^{2}\equiv\frac{16}{3}
). Moreover,
|B|^{2}\equiv\frac{16}{3}
if and only if

\pi(M)
is the equivariant Lagrangian minimal
3
-sphere in
{\tenmsb CP}^{3}(4)
(see
[
18
]
), where
\pi
denotes the Hopf fibration.

This paper will be organized as follows.

In Section
2
, we introduce the conception of Legendrian submanifolds
in unit Euclidean spheres. From the second fundamental form, we can define a tri-linear symmetric tensor
\sigma
and the fundamental
matrix
(S_{ij})
at each point, whose eigenvalues directly determine the eigenvalues of Ricci curvature tensor, due to the Gauss equation.
Afterwards, the Simons-type identity on the second derivative of
\sigma
and Lu’s inequality on the upper bound of the squared norm
of Lie brackets of symmetric matrices shall be introduced, which play a crucial role in the following text.

To derive rigidity theorems, it is natural to compute the Laplacian of
\lambda_{1}
, i.e. the largest eigenvalue of the fundamental matrix,
but
\lambda_{1}
is not always smooth. To overcome this obstacle, we consider the smooth function

f_{m}:=\text{tr}(S^{m})=\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}^{m}
as in
[
19
]
. By calculating the Laplacian of
g_{m}:=f_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}
and letting
m\rightarrow\infty
,
we deduce a Simons-type integral inequality (see Proposition
3.9
). Based on this inequality, by carefully examining the conditions
when the equality holds, we establish a pinching theorem on
|B|^{2}+\lambda_{2}
, giving a new characterization of the Calabi tori. These are
what we shall do in Section
3
.

\lambda_{3}\equiv 0
is equivalent to saying that the rank of the Gauss map of
M
is
2
whenever
|B|^{2}\neq 0
. Thereby, the Gauss map
is a submersion of
M
onto a Riemannian surface. By studying the integrability conditions from the viewpoint of complex analysis, we establish a structure theorem for
this type of Legendrian submanifolds (see Theorem
4.13
), which immediately implies Main Theorem
2
. This completes
the whole paper.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Legendrian submanifolds in the unit spheres

Let
F
be an isometric immersion from an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M
into the
complex Euclidean

(n+1)
-space
{\tenmsb C}^{n+1}:=\{z=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n+1}):z^{k}\in{\tenmsb C}\}
, which is equipped with the canonical complex structure
J

and the Euclidean inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. If the position vector
F(p)
of each
p\in M
always lies
in the unit sphere
S^{2n+1}:=\{z\in{\tenmsb C}^{n+1}:\langle z,z\rangle=1\}
,
M
becomes a submanifold of
S^{2n+1}
and

{\tenmsb C}^{n+1}={\tenmsb R}F(p)\oplus T_{p}S^{2n+1}={\tenmsb R}F(p)\oplus T_{p}M\oplus N_{p}M

(2.1)

where
T_{p}M
and
N_{p}M
are the tangent space and the normal space of
M
at
p
, respectively. Moreover,

M
is called
Legendrian
if and only if

J(T_{p}M)\subset N_{p}M,JF(p)\subset N_{p}M.

(2.2)

Denote by
\nabla,\overline{\nabla}
and
\partial
the Levi-Civita connections on
M,S^{2n+1}
and
{\tenmsb C}^{n+1}
, then
for any tangent vector fields
X,Y
on
M
,

\partial_{X}Y=\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y-\langle Y,X\rangle F

(2.3)

and

\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+B(X,Y)

(2.4)

with
B
the
second fundamental form
of
M
in
S^{2n+1}
taking values in the normal bundle
NM
.
In conjunction with
\partial_{X}J=0
, we have

\displaystyle\langle B(X,Y),JF\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{X}Y,JF\rangle=-\langle Y,\partial_{X}(JF)\rangle

(2.5)

\displaystyle=-\langle Y,JX\rangle=0.

Define

\sigma(X,Y,Z):=\langle B(X,Y),JZ\rangle,\qquad\forall X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM).

(2.6)

then

\displaystyle\sigma(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{X}Y,JZ\rangle=-\langle Y,\partial_{X}JZ\rangle=-\langle Y,J(\partial_{X}Z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{X}Z,JY\rangle=\sigma(X,Z,Y).

Along with the symmetry of
B
, we observe that

\sigma
is a tri-linear symmetric tensor.

Let
\{E_{1},\cdots,E_{n}\}
be an arbitrary orthonormal frame field on
M
, then

S(X,Y):=\sum_{i,j}\sigma(E_{i},E_{j},X)\sigma(E_{i},E_{j},Y)

(2.7)

is a bilinear nonnegative definite symmetric tensor.
Define

\left(S_{ij}\right):=\left(S(E_{i},E_{j})\right)

(2.8)

to be the
fundamental matrices
of
M
and let

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}

(2.9)

be the eigenvalues of
\left(S_{ij}\right)
, then

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=\text{tr }S=\sum_{i,j,k}\sigma(E_{i},E_{j},E_{k})\sigma(E_{i},E_{j},E_{k})=|\sigma|^{2}=|B|^{2}.

(2.10)

Taking the trace of
B
gives the
mean curvature vector

H
of
M
, i.e.

H:=\sum_{i}B(E_{i},E_{i}).

(2.11)

Now we assume
M
is a
minimal submanifold
of
S^{2n+1}
, i.e.
H\equiv 0
everywhere on
M
. For each connection
\nabla
,
let

R_{XY}:=-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}+\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}+\nabla_{[X,Y]}

(2.12)

be the associated curvature tensor, then the
Gauss equation
says

\displaystyle\langle R_{XY}Z,W\rangle=\langle\overline{R}_{XY},Z,W\rangle+\langle B(X,Z),B(Y,W)\rangle-\langle B(X,W),B(Y,Z)\rangle

(2.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,W\rangle-\langle X,W\rangle\langle Y,Z\rangle+\sum_{i}\sigma(X,Z,E_{i})\sigma(Y,W,E_{i})-\sum_{i}\sigma(X,W,E_{i})\sigma(Y,Z,E_{i})

and taking the trace of
R
implies

\displaystyle\text{Ric}(X,Y):=\sum_{j}\langle R_{XE_{j}}Y,E_{j}\rangle

(2.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\langle X,Y\rangle+\sum_{i,j}\sigma(X,Y,E_{i})\sigma(E_{j},E_{j},E_{i})-\sum_{i,j}\sigma(X,E_{j},E_{i})\sigma(Y,E_{j},E_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\langle X,Y\rangle-S(X,Y).

i.e.
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of the fundamental matrix if and only if
n-1-\lambda
is an eigenvalue
of the Ricci curvature tensor of
M
at the considered point. Again taking the trace of both sides of
(
2.14
), we see the scalar curvature of
M
equals
n(n-1)-|B|^{2}
pointwisely.

The induced normal connection on the normal bundle
NM
is defined by

\nabla_{X}\nu=(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\nu)^{N}\qquad X\in\Gamma(TM),\nu\in\Gamma(NM),

(2.15)

whose corresponding curvature tensor is defined by
R^{\bot}
.
Then the
Ricci equation
says

\displaystyle\langle R^{\bot}_{XY}JZ,JW\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{R}_{XY}JZ,JW\rangle+\sum_{i}\langle B(X,E_{i}),JZ\rangle\langle B(Y,E_{i}),JW\rangle-\sum_{i}\langle B(X,E_{i}),JW\rangle\langle B(Y,E_{i}),JZ\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sigma(X,Z,E_{i})\sigma(Y,W,E_{i})-\sum_{i}\sigma(X,W,E_{i})\sigma(Y,Z,E_{i}).

Let

(\nabla_{X}B)(Y,Z):=\nabla_{X}(B(Y,Z))-B(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-B(Y,\nabla_{X}Z),

(2.16)

then

\displaystyle\langle(\nabla_{X}B)(Y,Z),JF\rangle

(2.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}(B(Y,Z)),JF\rangle=-\nabla_{X}\langle B(Y,Z),JF\rangle-\langle B(Y,Z),\partial_{X}(JF)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle B(Y,Z),JX\rangle=-\sigma(X,Y,Z).

and the
Codazzi equation
says

(\nabla_{X}B)(Y,Z)=(\nabla_{Y}B)(X,Z),

(2.18)

which implies

(\nabla_{X}\sigma)(Y,Z,W)=(\nabla_{Y}\sigma)(X,Z,W)=(\nabla_{X}\sigma)(Z,Y,W)=(\nabla_{X}\sigma)(Y,W,Z)

(2.19)

i.e.
\nabla\sigma
is a four-linear symmetric tensor.

2.2
The Simons type identity

The following Simons-type identity play a crucial part in the present paper (see e.g.
[
21
]
):

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume
M
is a minimal Legendrian submanifold in
S^{2n+1}
, then

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\sigma(X,Y,Z):=\sum_{i}(\nabla_{E_{i}}\nabla_{E_{i}}\sigma)(X,Y,Z)

(2.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\sigma(X,Y,Z)-\sum_{j}[S(X,E_{j})\sigma(E_{j},Y,Z)+S(Y,E_{j})\sigma(E_{j},Z,X)+S(Z,E_{j})\sigma(E_{j},X,Y)]

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i,j,k}\sigma(X,E_{j},E_{k})\sigma(Y,E_{k},E_{i})\sigma(Z,E_{i},E_{j}).

Consequently, let

\sigma_{l}:=\sigma(\cdot,\cdot,E_{l})

(2.21)

be the second fundamental form on the direction
JE_{l}
,
then in terms of matrix notations,
we have

\nabla^{2}\sigma_{l}=(n+1)\sigma_{l}-\sum_{j}\langle\sigma_{l},\sigma_{j}\rangle\sigma_{j}-\sum_{j}[\sigma_{j},[\sigma_{j},\sigma_{l}]].

(2.22)

Proof..

By the Codazzi equation and Ricci identity, a straightforward calculation shows

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\sigma(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{E_{i}}\nabla_{E_{i}}\sigma)(X,Y,Z)=(\nabla_{E_{i}}\nabla_{X}\sigma)(E_{i},Y,Z)

(2.23)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{E_{i}}\sigma)(E_{i},Y,Z)+(R_{X,E_{i}}\sigma)(E_{i},Y,Z)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\sigma)(E_{i},E_{i},Z)-\sigma(R_{X,E_{i}}E_{i},Y,Z)-\sigma(E_{i},R_{X,E_{i}}Y,Z)-\sigma(E_{i},Y,R_{X,E_{i}}Z)

\displaystyle=\text{Ric}(X,E_{j})\sigma(E_{j},Y,Z)-\langle R_{X,E_{i}}Y,E_{j}\rangle\sigma(E_{i},E_{j},Z)-\langle R_{X,E_{i}}Z,E_{j}\rangle\sigma(E_{i},Y,E_{j})

\displaystyle:=I-II-III.

(Here and in the sequel we use the summation convention.) According to (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), we get

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle[(n-1)\langle X,E_{j}\rangle-S(X,E_{j})]\sigma(E_{j},Y,Z)

(2.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\sigma(X,Y,Z)-S(X,E_{j})\sigma(E_{j},Y,Z),

\displaystyle II=

\displaystyle[\langle X,Y\rangle\delta_{ij}-\langle X,E_{j}\rangle\langle Y,E_{i}\rangle+\sigma(X,Y,E_{k})\sigma(E_{i},E_{j},E_{k})-\sigma(X,E_{j},E_{k})\sigma(E_{i},Y,E_{k})]\sigma(E_{i},E_{j},Z)

(2.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sigma(X,Y,Z)+S(Z,E_{k})\sigma(E_{k},X,Y)-\sigma(X,E_{j},E_{k})\sigma(Y,E_{k},E_{i})\sigma(Z,E_{i},E_{j})

and similarly

\displaystyle III=-\sigma(X,Y,Z)+S(Y,E_{k})\sigma(E_{k},Z,X)-\sigma(X,E_{k},E_{j})\sigma(Y,E_{j},E_{i})\sigma(Z,E_{i},E_{k}).

(2.26)

Substituting (

). Letting

where

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{l},\sigma_{j}\rangle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{t,s}\sigma_{l}(E_{t},E_{s})\sigma_{j}(E_{t},E_{s})

(2.28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t,s}\sigma(E_{t},E_{s},E_{l})\sigma(E_{t},E_{s},E_{j})=S_{lj}.

Finally, (

) immediately follows from (

),

[\sigma_{k},[\sigma_{k},\sigma_{l}]]=\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}-2\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}\sigma_{k}+\sigma_{l}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k}

(2.29)

and

\displaystyle(\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k})_{ts}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(E_{t},E_{i})\sigma_{k}(E_{i},E_{s})

(2.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(E_{t},E_{i},E_{k})\sigma(E_{i},E_{s},E_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{ts}.

2.3
On Lu’s inequality

For
2
real
(n\times n)
-matrices
A=(a_{ij})
and
B=(b_{ij})
, let

\langle A,B\rangle:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}b_{ij}=\text{tr }(AB^{T})

(2.31)

with
(\cdot)^{T}
denoting the transpose of a matrix, which induces the Hilbert-Schmidt norm:

\|A\|=\sqrt{\langle A,A\rangle}=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}}.

(2.32)

Z. Q. Lu
[
19
]
established the following matrix inequality, which is the main algebraic tool of the present paper.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{n}
be
(n\times n)
-symmetric matrices, such that

1.

\langle A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}\rangle=0
whenever
\alpha\neq\beta
;

2.

\|A_{1}\|=1
;

3.

\|A_{2}\|\geq\cdots\geq\|A_{m}\|
.

Then

\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\left\|[A_{1},A_{\alpha}]\right\|^{2}\leq\|A_{2}\|^{2}+\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\|A_{\alpha}\|^{2}

(2.33)

and the equality holds if and only if, after an orthonormal base change and up to a sign, we have

A_{k+2}=\cdots=A_{n}=0
,

\displaystyle=\nabla_{W}B(T,\overline{W})=(\nabla_{W}B)(T,\overline{W})+B(\nabla_{W}T,\overline{W})

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{T}B)(W,\overline{W})+\langle\nabla_{W}T,W\rangle B(\overline{W},\overline{W})+\langle\nabla_{W}T,\overline{W}\rangle B(W,\overline{W})

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{W}T,W\rangle B(\overline{W},\overline{W}).

This implies
\langle\partial_{W}W,T\rangle=\langle\nabla_{W}W,T\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{W}T,W\rangle=0
and hence

\langle W_{z},T\rangle=0.

(4.14)

Since

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{W}W,JF\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle W,\partial_{W}(JF)\rangle=-\langle W,J\partial_{W}F\rangle=-\langle W,JW\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{W}W,JT\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle B(W,W),JT\rangle=\langle B(T,W),JW\rangle=0,

and

\langle\partial_{W}W,J\overline{W}\rangle=\langle B(W,W),J\overline{W}\rangle=\langle B(W,\overline{W}),JW\rangle=0,

we have

\langle W_{z},JX\rangle=0,\quad\langle W_{z},JT\rangle=0,\quad\langle W_{z},JW\rangle=0.

(4.15)

In conjunction with

\langle W_{z},W\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle W,W\rangle_{z}=0,

(4.16)

we can write

W_{z}=hW+\mu J\overline{W}

(4.17)

with

h:=\langle W_{z},\overline{W}\rangle,\quad\mu:=\langle W_{z},JW\rangle.

(4.18)

Note that replacing
W
by
\hat{W}:=e^{i\theta}W
with suitable
\theta
makes sure
\hat{\mu}:=\langle\hat{W}_{z},J\hat{W}\rangle=e^{2i\theta}\langle W_{z},JW\rangle\in{\tenmsb R}^{+}
. Thereby, we can assume
\mu
takes positive real values everywhere.

Combining with (
4.11
) and
\pi_{*}\overline{W}=\bar{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}
implies
W_{\bar{z}}
is a vector field in
(\mathcal{D}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\bot})\otimes{\tenmsb C}
. Moreover,

\langle W_{\bar{z}},W\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle W,W\rangle_{\bar{z}}=0,

(4.19)

and

\langle W_{\bar{z}},\overline{W}\rangle=-\langle W,\overline{W}_{\bar{z}}\rangle=-\bar{h}

(4.20)

imply the existence of
U
in
\mathcal{D}\otimes{\tenmsb C}
, such that

W_{\bar{z}}=-\bar{h}W+U.

(4.21)

A straightforward calculation based on (
4.17
) and (
4.21
) shows

\displaystyle W_{z\bar{z}}

\displaystyle=h_{\bar{z}}W+hW_{\bar{z}}+\mu_{\bar{z}}J\overline{W}+\mu J\overline{W}_{\bar{z}}

(4.22)

\displaystyle=(h_{\bar{z}}-\mu^{2}-|h|^{2})W+(\mu_{\bar{z}}+\mu\bar{h})J\overline{W}+hU

and

\displaystyle W_{\bar{z}z}

\displaystyle=-\bar{h}_{z}W-\bar{h}W_{z}+U_{z}

(4.23)

\displaystyle=(-\bar{h}_{z}-|h|^{2})W-\mu\bar{h}J\overline{W}+U_{z}.

In conjunction with

\langle U_{z},JW\rangle=-\langle U,JW_{z}\rangle=0,

(4.24)

we have

\mu_{\bar{z}}+2\mu\bar{h}=0

(4.25)

and

U_{z}=(h_{\bar{z}}+\bar{h}_{z}-\mu^{2})W+hU,

(4.26)

which imply

(\log\mu)_{\bar{z}}=-2\bar{h},

(4.27)

\displaystyle\lambda:

\displaystyle=\langle U,\overline{U}\rangle=\langle W_{\bar{z}},\overline{U}\rangle=-\langle W,\overline{U}_{\bar{z}}\rangle

(4.28)

\displaystyle=-\bar{h}_{z}-h_{\bar{z}}+\mu^{2}=(\log\mu)_{z\bar{z}}+\mu^{2}

and

\langle U,U\rangle_{z}=2\langle U_{z},U\rangle=2h\langle U,U\rangle.

(4.29)

Combining (
4.25
) and (
4.29
) gives

\left(\mu\langle\overline{U},\overline{U}\rangle\right)_{\bar{z}}=-2\mu\bar{h}\langle\overline{U},\overline{U}\rangle+2\mu\bar{h}\langle\overline{U},\overline{U}\rangle=0.

(4.30)

Note that

\Psi:=\mu\langle\overline{U},\overline{U}\rangle dz^{3}=\langle W_{z},JW\rangle\langle\overline{W}_{z},\overline{W}_{z}\rangle dz^{3}

(4.31)

is independent of the choice of
W
and
z
, hence (
4.30
) means
\Psi
is a holomorphic
3
-form globally defined on
\Sigma
.

If
\Psi
does not vanish everywhere, then we can find an open subset of
\Sigma
which admits a complex coordinate
w
, such that

\Psi=dw^{3}=\langle W_{w},JW\rangle\langle\overline{W}_{w},\overline{W}_{w}\rangle dw^{3}
. Therefore, without loss of generality we can assume

\mu\langle\overline{U},\overline{U}\rangle=1

(4.32)

holds locally. By computing,

\displaystyle\langle U_{\bar{z}},W\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle U,W_{\bar{z}}\rangle=-\langle U,U\rangle=-\mu^{-1},

\displaystyle\langle U_{\bar{z}},\overline{W}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U,\overline{W}_{\bar{z}}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle U_{z\bar{z}},W\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U_{z},W\rangle_{\bar{z}}-\langle U_{z},W_{\bar{z}}\rangle=-h\mu^{-1},

\displaystyle\langle U_{\bar{z}z},W\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U_{\bar{z}},W\rangle_{z}-\langle U_{\bar{z}},W_{z}\rangle=-(\mu^{-1})_{z}-h\mu^{-1}.

This shows
\mu_{z}=0
, i.e.
\mu
is constant, then (
4.25
), (
4.26
) and (
4.28
) implies

h\equiv 0,\qquad U_{z}=-\mu^{2}W

(4.33)

and

\langle U,\overline{U}\rangle=\mu^{2}.

(4.34)

Differentiating both sides of (
4.32
) and (
4.34
), we can derive
\langle U_{\bar{z}},U\rangle=\langle U_{\bar{z}},\overline{U}\rangle=0
, hence

U_{\bar{z}}=-\mu^{-1}\overline{W}.

(4.35)

Comparing
U_{z\bar{z}}=-\mu^{2}W_{\bar{z}}=-\mu^{2}U
and
U_{\bar{z}z}=-\mu^{-1}\overline{W}_{z}=-\mu^{-1}\overline{U}
gives

\mu\equiv 0
, which causes a contradiction to
\mu>0
.

Therefore
\Psi\equiv 0
, i.e.
\langle U,U\rangle\equiv 0
. Since

\displaystyle\langle U_{\bar{z}},W\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle U,W_{\bar{z}}\rangle=-\langle U,U\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle U_{\bar{z}},\overline{W}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U,\overline{W}_{\bar{z}}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle U_{\bar{z}},U\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle U,U\rangle_{\bar{z}}=0,

\displaystyle\langle U_{\bar{z}},\overline{U}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U,\overline{U}\rangle_{\bar{z}}-\langle U,\overline{U}_{\bar{z}}\rangle=\lambda_{\bar{z}}-\lambda\bar{h},

we have

U_{\bar{z}}=\left((\log\lambda)_{\bar{z}}-\bar{h}\right)U.

(4.36)

Comparing

U_{z\bar{z}}=(-\lambda W+hU)_{\bar{z}}=(-\lambda_{\bar{z}}+\lambda\bar{h})W+\left(h_{\bar{z}}-\lambda+h(\log\lambda)_{\bar{z}}-|h|^{2}\right)U

with

U_{\bar{z}z}=\left[\left((\log\lambda)_{\bar{z}}-\bar{h}\right)U\right]_{z}=(-\lambda_{\bar{z}}+\lambda\bar{h})W+\left((\log\lambda)_{\bar{z}z}-\bar{h}_{z}+h(\log\lambda)_{\bar{z}}-|h|^{2}\right)U

yields

\displaystyle(\log\lambda)_{z\bar{z}}

\displaystyle=h_{\bar{z}}+\bar{h}_{z}-\lambda=-(\log\mu)_{z\bar{z}}-\lambda

(4.37)

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}-2\lambda.

Via Plücker coordinates, the Gauss map of
M
can be written as

\gamma(p)=JX\wedge JT\wedge JE_{1}\wedge JE_{2}=-\sqrt{-1}(JX\wedge JT\wedge JW\wedge J\overline{W}).

(4.38)

Thus

\displaystyle\gamma_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{-1}(JX\wedge JT\wedge JW_{z}\wedge J\overline{W}+JX\wedge JT\wedge JW\wedge J\overline{W}_{z})

(4.39)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\mu JX\wedge JT\wedge\overline{W}\wedge J\overline{W}

and

\langle\gamma_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\gamma_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}\rangle=\mu^{2}.

(4.40)

This means

g:=2\mu^{2}|dz|^{2}

(4.41)

is the induced metric on
\Sigma
, whose corresponding Gauss curvature is

K_{g}=-\frac{(\log\mu^{2})_{z\bar{z}}}{\mu^{2}}=\frac{2(\mu^{2}-\lambda)}{\mu^{2}}=2-\frac{2\lambda}{\mu^{2}}.

(4.42)

Noting that
\pi_{*}\overline{W}=\bar{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle U,X\rangle=\langle W_{\bar{z}},X\rangle=\bar{f}^{-1}\langle\partial_{\overline{W}}W,X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{f}^{-1}\langle W,\partial_{\overline{W}}X\rangle=-\bar{f}^{-1}.

Combining with
\langle U,U\rangle=0
, we can derive
U=-\bar{f}^{-1}(X+\pm\sqrt{-1}T)
, hence

\lambda=\langle U,\overline{U}\rangle=2|f|^{-2}

(4.43)

and

|B|^{2}=2|B(W,W)|^{2}=2|\langle B(W,W),JW\rangle|^{2}=2|f\mu|^{2}=\frac{4\mu^{2}}{\lambda}.

(4.44)

In conjunction with (
4.28
) and (
4.37
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\log|B|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}(\log|B|^{2})_{z\bar{z}}=\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}\left(2(\log\mu)_{z\bar{z}}-(\log\lambda)_{z\bar{z}}\right)

(4.45)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\lambda}{\mu^{2}}-6=\frac{32}{|B|^{2}}-6.

In summary, we get a structure theorem as follows:

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
M
be a minimal Legendrian submanifold in
S^{7}
, such that
\lambda_{3}=0
and
|B|^{2}\neq 0
everywhere. Then the Gauss map

\gamma
is a submersion from
M
onto a
2
-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma
of
\mathbb{G}_{4,4}
, the squared norm of the second fundamental form
|B|^{2}

takes the same value in each fibre, and

\displaystyle K_{g}

\displaystyle=2-\frac{8}{|B|^{2}},

(4.46)

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\log|B|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{32}{|B|^{2}}-6.

Here
g
is the induced metric on
\Sigma
,
K_{g}
and
\Delta_{g}
are the Gauass curvature and the Laplacian operator on
(\Sigma,g)
, respectively.

On the other hand, if
(\Sigma,g)
is a simply-connected
2
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, such that
K_{g}<2
and

\Delta_{g}\log(2-K_{g})=4K_{g}-2,

(4.47)

then there exists an isometric immersion
\psi:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{4,4}
and a minimal Legendrian submanifold
M\subset S^{7}
,
such that:

1.

The rank of the Gauss map
\gamma:M\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{4,4}
is 2 everywhere (i.e.
\lambda_{3}=0
and
|B|^{2}\neq 0
everywhere on
M
);

2.

The image manifold of the Gauss map is just
\Sigma
;

3.

|B|^{2}\equiv\frac{8}{2-K_{g}}
on each fibre of
\gamma
.

In conjunction with the results in
[
7
,
18
]
, we establish the following rigidity theorem:

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional compact minimal Legendrian submanifold in
S^{2n+1}
with
n\geq 3
, such that

\lambda_{3}\equiv 0
, then

1.

If
n\geq 4
, then
M
has to be totally geodesic.

2.

If
n=3
and
|B|^{2}\neq 0
everywhere, then
M
is diffeomorphic to
S^{3}
.

3.

If
n=3
and
|B|^{2}\leq\frac{16}{3}
, then
M
is either totally geodesic or
|B|^{2}\equiv\frac{16}{3}
(i.e.
|B|^{2}+\lambda_{2}\equiv 8
).
For the latter case,
M=S^{3}=\{(z,w)\in{\tenmsb C}^{2}:|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}=1\}
and
F:M\rightarrow{\tenmsb C}^{4}
is given as

F(z,w)=(z^{3}+3z\bar{w}^{2},\sqrt{3}(z^{2}w+w\bar{w}^{2}-2z\bar{z}\bar{w}),\sqrt{3}(zw^{2}+z\bar{z}^{2}-2w\bar{z}\bar{w}),w^{3}+3w\bar{z}^{2}).

4.

If
n=3
and
|B|^{2}\geq\frac{16}{3}
, then
|B|^{2}\equiv\frac{16}{3}
.

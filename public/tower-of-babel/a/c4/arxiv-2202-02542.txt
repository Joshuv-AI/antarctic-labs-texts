Abstract
. The article presents a formula expressing Macaulay constants of a numerical polynomial through its minimizing coefficients. From this, we have that Macaulay constants of Kolchin dimension polynomials do not decrease. For the minimal differential dimension polynomial ω 𝒢 / ℱ \omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}} (this concept was introduced by W.Sitt in [ 5 ] ) we will prove a criterion for Macaulay constants to be equal. In this case, as the example ( 2 ) shows, there are no bounds from above to the Macaulay constants of the polynomial ω ξ / ℱ \omega_{\xi/\mathcal{F}} for 𝒢 = ℱ ​ ⟨ ξ ⟩ \mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}\left<\xi\right> . Keywords: differential algebra, differential polynomials, Kolchin dimcnsion polynomial, minimizing coefficients.



1.
Introduction

One of the basic objects of stude in differential algebra is the
differential dimension polynomial introduced by E.Kolchin
[
1
]
.
This is an analogue of dimension in algebraic geometry,
and its role is similar to that of the Hilbert polynomial
in commutative algebra.

The dimension polynomial itself is not an
invariant of the differential field,
but contains some invariants, such as the degree and leading coefficient.
In this article, we are considering
another little studied invariant,
introduced by W.Sit
[
5
]
, namely a minimal
dimension polynomial.

Note that since the 80s of the last century, interest in computer algebra has increased.
Speaking of history,
one of the first scientists whose work laid to the foundation of
constructive theory of ideals in the ring of polynomials,
is F. Macaulay (20s of the 20th century).
He proved, in particular,
the criterion for a numerical function to be equal to
Hilbert function of a homogeneous ideal in the ring of commutative
polynomials.
In 1990 T.Dubé used in the proof of the bound of the
degree of elements
Gröbner
basis numbers named in honor of Macaulay
as Macaulay constants.

The Hilbert function for sufficiently large values becomes a polynomial,
which is discussed in this article.
Moreover, the set of all Hilbert polynomials
of homogeneous ideals coincides with the set of Kolchin
dimension polynomials.
In
[
3
]
(proposition 2.4.10) a criterion for the equality of a
numerical polynomial to some Hilbert polynomial is proved,
which does not use Macaulay’s results.
In this paper we present this result in terms of Macaulay constants.

3.
Basic results.

3.1.
Macaulay constants of an numerical polynomial.

The article
[
4
]
proves a criterion for the equality of
a numerical polynomial to
some Kolchin polynomial
\omega_{E}(s)
. Denote by

W
is the set of all possible polynomials

\{\omega_{E}(s):E\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m},m=1,\dots\}.

Theorem 1
.

(
[
3
]
, proposition 2.4.10)
A numerical polynom
\omega(s)
belongs to
W
, iff its
sequence of minimizing coefficients consists only of non-negative integers.

Note (see
[
4
]
) that the set of Kolchin polynomials is
closed under additions:

Theorem 2
.

(
[
3
]
, proposition 2.4.13.)
Let
\omega_{1}(s),\omega_{2}(s)\in W
. Then the polynomial

\omega(s)=\omega_{1}(s)+\omega_{2}(s)
also belongs to
W
.

As follows from these theorems, the differential
dimension polynomial

\omega_{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}/\mathcal{F}}(s)

has non-negative minimizing coefficients.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\omega(s)
is a numerical polynomial of degree
d
, and

b(\omega)=(b_{d},\dots,b_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
is a
sequence of its minimizing coefficients.
Then

\omega(s)=\binom{s+d+1}{d+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}\binom{s+i-1-c_{i}}{i}

(1)

where

c_{i}=\sum_{j=i-1}^{j=d}b_{i},\ i=0,\dots,d+1,

(the value of
c_{0}
can be set arbitrarily).

Proof.

We will prove this theorem by induction on
d
.
If
d=0
,
\omega(s)=b_{0}
, we need to check the equality

b_{0}=(s+1)-(\binom{s-1-c_{0}}{0}+\binom{s-c_{1}}{1})=(s+1)-(1+(s-c_{1}))
,
which follows from the definition
c_{1}=b_{0}
.

Let now
d>0
,
b(\omega)=(b_{d},\dots,b_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.
According to the definition
(
1
), we denote

v(s)=\omega(s+b_{d})-\binom{s+d+1+b_{d}}{d+1}+\binom{s+d+1}{d+1}.

(2)

Then
\operatorname{deg}v<d
,
b(v)=(b_{d-1},\dots,b_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

and by induction we can assume that

v(s)=\binom{s+d}{d}-\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{s+i-1-c^{\prime}_{i}}{i},

(3)

where

c^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{j=i-1}^{j=d-1}b_{i},\ i=0,\dots,d.

Let’s make the substitution
s^{\prime}=s+b_{d}
in the expression (
2
):

v(s^{\prime}-b_{d})=\omega(s^{\prime})-\binom{s^{\prime}+1+d}{d+1}+\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+d+1}{d+1}.

Then, taking into account the formula
(
3
) we will have:

\displaystyle\omega(s^{\prime})=\binom{s^{\prime}+d+1}{d+1}-\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+d+1}{d+1}+v(s^{\prime}-b_{d})=

\displaystyle\binom{s^{\prime}+d+1}{d+1}-\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+d+1}{d+1}+\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+d}{d}-\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+i-1-c^{\prime}_{i}}{i}=

\displaystyle\binom{s^{\prime}+d+1}{d+1}-(\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+d+1}{d+1}-\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+d}{d})-\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+i-1-c^{\prime}_{i}}{i}.

From equality

\binom{k+1}{l+1}-\binom{k}{l}=\binom{k}{l+1}
we have

\displaystyle\omega(s^{\prime})=\binom{s^{\prime}+d+1}{d+1}-\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+d}{d+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+i-1-c^{\prime}_{i}}{i}=

\displaystyle=\binom{s^{\prime}+d+1}{d+1}-\binom{s^{\prime}+d-c_{d+1}}{d+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{s^{\prime}-b_{d}+i-1-c^{\prime}_{i}}{i}=

\displaystyle\binom{s^{\prime}+d+1}{d+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}\binom{s^{\prime}+i-1-c_{i}}{i},

т.к.
c^{\prime}_{i}+b_{d}=c_{i}
для
i=0,\dots,d
,
c_{d+1}=b_{d}
.
∎

The values of
c_{i}
in the expression (
1
) are called

Macaulay constants

(see, for example,
[
2
]
, formula (*), p. 768).
The arbitrary value
c_{0}
for
w\in W
can be considered
equal to the smallest number,
for which the value of the Hilbert function is equal to the
value of the polynomial.
For
E=(e_{ij})\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m},i=1,\dots,n

this number equals to
\sum_{i=1}^{m}max_{j=1,\dots,n}e_{ij}
.

So, the theorem (
3
) establishes a relationship between
the values of the minimizing coefficients
polynomial and its Macaulay constants.

From here and from the theorem (
1
) we immediately have

Corollary 1
.

A numerical polynomial is a Kolchin dimension polynomial if and only
if the sequence of its Macaulay constants is non-decreasing.

Later in this article, since we will not use the value of Macaulay’s
constant

c_{0}
, we will consider only the values

c_{d+1},\dots,c_{1}
and, for convenience, number them,
as well as the minimizing coefficients, starting from zero:

(c_{d},\dots,c_{0})
, where

Example 1
.

Consider the following system of linear differential equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}\xi=0

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}\xi=0

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\partial_{3}^{2}\xi=0

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\partial_{3}\dots\partial_{m}^{2}\xi=0

Let’s find the dimension polynomial of this system and its Macaulay constants.

Since the equations are linear and form a Gröbner basis in the ring
of differential operators,
we need to calculate the dimension polynomial of the lower
triangular matrix

,
with 2 on the diagonal, and 1 below it:

\begin{matrix}200\dots 0\\

To calculate a dimension polynomial

]

Let

,

\omega_{E}(s)=\omega_{E\cup e}(s)+\omega_{H}(s-1),

(4)

is a matrix

\omega_{E}(s)=\omega_{e}(s)+\omega_{\begin{smallmatrix}10\\
01\end{smallmatrix}}(s-1)=(s+1)+1

In particular, the sequence of minimizing coefficients

Now suppose

.
Let

\begin{matrix}100\dots 0\\
020\dots 0\\
\dots\\
01\dots 12\end{matrix}

It is clear that the dimension polynomial of the matrix

By definition of minimizing coefficients (

From the theorem (

(1,2,\dots,m)

Let us now calculate the standard coefficients of the dimension polynomial.
Let for

the standard coefficients be the numbers

a(\omega_{E})=(a_{m-1},\dots,a_{0})
which means representation

\omega_{E}(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}\binom{s+i}{i}.

Denote the dimension polynomial of the matrix

by

\omega_{m}(s)
.
As shown above, for

the dimension polynomial

, so it’s

.
Let

Denote by

Taking into account the formula

is a polynomial whose Macaulay constants

and we can use the inductive assumption
to calculate standard coefficients

a_{i}(\omega_{m})=a_{i-1}(\omega_{m-1})
,

.
It remains for us to calculate the lowest standard coefficient,

.
We use the formula (

Substitute in this formula

3.2.
Macaulay constants of a minimal dimension polynomial.

According to Sit’s theorem (
[

If you need to compare sequences of different lengths,

The simplest example of a differential field extension, for which

Theorem 4

Let

Then for any prime differential component

(5)

and the sequence of Macaulay constants of the minimal

for each component is constant
(may be different for different prime components).
Conversly, if the Macaulay constants of the differential
dimension polynomial of the field extension

\omega_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}/\mathcal{F}}

are the same and
\operatorname{deg}(\omega_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}/\mathcal{F}})=|\Delta|-1
,
then this polynomial is minimal
\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}}
,
and a prime ideal, defining the field
\mathcal{G}

is the general component one differential polynomial

By the components theorem (
[

]

Denote

\omega_{\xi_{1}}(s)=\binom{s+m}{m}-\binom{s+m-d_{j}}{m},

where
\xi_{1}
is the common zero of

\omega_{P_{j}}(s)\leq\binom{s+m}{m}-\binom{s+m-d_{j}}{m}

Sequence of minimizing coefficients

(d,0,\dots,0)

According to the theorem (

Conversely, let all the Macaulay constants of the minimum dimension

\mathcal{G}

By Theorem (

\omega_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}/\mathcal{F}}=\binom{s+m}{m}-\binom{s+m-d}{m}

it follows that
\omega_{E_{j}}=0
for all
j
, except for the total
one (we can assume that for all

j>1
). Because
\omega_{E_{j}}=0
for
j>1
, the elements
\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{n}
are algebraic over
\mathcal{F}\left<\xi_{1}\right>
and
\mathcal{F}\left<\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\right>=\mathcal{F}\left<\xi_{1}\right>
.
Since

E_{1}
consists of one element, a prime ideal defining

\mathcal{F}\{y_{1}\}
, is the general component of some differential
polynomial.
∎

If degree

\omega_{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}/\mathcal{F}}(s)
is less than
m
, and the field
\mathcal{F}
contains

\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
(field of rational functions in unknowns

x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
), then (see
[
1
]
, part II, $8, assertion 9)

\mathcal{G}
has a
primitive element
\xi
(i.e. such that

\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}\left<\xi\right>
).
Moreover,
\xi
can be chosen in the form

\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\psi_{j}
,
\lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{F},j=1,\ldots,n
. With such a changing, the differential dimension
polynomial does not increase, i.e.
\omega_{\xi/\mathcal{F}}(s)\leqslant\omega_{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}/\mathcal{F}}(s)
for all sufficiently large
s
and so
the problem of finding the minimal differential dimension
polynomial is related to the search for a primitive element
fields (although it doesn’t solve it, see
[
3
]
, Example 5.7.7).
Both the problem of calculating the polynomial

\omega_{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}/\mathcal{F}}(s)
,
and the searching
\xi
are "in principle" solved
using the algorithm
Rosenfeld-Grebner, included in the diffag package of the system
Computer Algebra Maple.

Suppose a primitive element is found in the extension,

b(\omega_{\xi/\mathcal{F}})=(b_{d},b_{d-1},\dots.b_{0})
and
b_{i}\neq 0

for some
i<d
. We are interested in whether there are such values

b_{i}
that
b(\omega_{\mathcal{G}}/\mathcal{F})(s))=(b_{d},0,\dots,0)

is impossible.

The following example gives a negative answer to this question.

Example 2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{C}(x_{1},x_{2})
,
k>=2
and
\Delta
-extension
\mathcal{G}
be given by the system

\begin{cases}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}\varphi=0,&\\
x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}^{k}\partial_{2}\varphi-x_{1}\partial_{1}^{k}\varphi+x_{2}\partial_{1}^{k-1}\partial_{2}\varphi+\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\varphi-\partial_{1}^{k-1}\varphi=0.&\end{cases}

These equations form the Gröbner basis, so

\omega_{\varphi/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\omega_{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\
k&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)}(s)=2s+k
and
b(\omega_{\varphi/\mathcal{F}})=(2,k-1)
. Let

\psi=\varphi-x_{1}x_{2}(\partial_{1}^{k-1}\varphi-x_{2}\partial_{1}^{k-1}\partial_{2}\varphi)
. This change of variables is invertible,
since
\varphi=(1+x_{1}x_{2}\partial_{1}^{k-1})\psi
.
At the same time
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\psi=0
, hence

\omega_{\psi/\mathcal{F}}(s)=\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}}(s)=2s+1
and
b(\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}})=(2,0)
.

In the example (
2
), the field contains elements that are not constants. This condition, as the following statement shows,
is essential.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be defined by the system
\Sigma
of linear differential
equations in one variable with coefficients,
which are constants of the field
\mathcal{F}
. If

\omega_{\xi/\mathcal{F}}(s)!=\binom{s+m}{m}-\binom{s+m-d}{m}
, then

\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}}(s)!=\binom{s+m}{m}-\binom{s+m-d}{m}.

Proof.

Consider
\Omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}}
, the module of differentials (see, for example,

[
3
]
, p.38), and let
J=[\Sigma]
be the ideal of the ring
D=\mathcal{G}[\Delta]
of differential
operators. We have the exact sequence of
D
-modules:

0\to_{D}J\to D\to\Omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}}\to 0.

Suppose
\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}}(s)=\binom{s+m}{m}-\binom{s+m-d}{m}
.
By Theorem 5.7.8 (
[
3
]
), there exists
this exact sequence:

0\to_{D}D\to D\to\Omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{F}}\to 0.

It follows from these representations, that
{}_{D}J\oplus D=D\oplus D
.
Hence the ideal
J
is a projective
D
-module.
Under the conditions of the theorem, we can assume that the ring
D

is a ring of commutative polynomials over a field, and then

J
must be free (principal ideal in a polynomial ring).
Then, by the theorem (
4
), holds

\omega_{\xi/\mathcal{F}}=\binom{s+m}{m}-\binom{s+m-d}{m}
.
This is contrary to the condition.
∎

As follows from the proof, the example (
2
) represents the projective ideal
in non-commutative ring
D=\mathbb{C}(x_{1},x_{2})[\partial_{1},\partial_{2}]
, which is not free.

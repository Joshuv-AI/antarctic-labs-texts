The homology groups of finite cyclic covering of line arrangement complement
Yongqiang Liu, Wentao Xie

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the first homology group of finite cyclic covering of complex line arrangement complement. We show that this first integral homology group is torsion-free under certain condition similar to the one used by Cohen-Dimca-Orlik [ CDO03 , Theorem 1] . In particular, this includes the case of the Milnor fiber, which generalizes the previous results obtained by Williams [ Wil13 , Main Theorem 1] for complexified line arrangement to any complex line arrangement.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

An arrangement of hyperplanes
\mathcal{A}
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in a finite dimensional complex vector (affine, or projective) space. A fundamental problem in the theory of hyperplane arrangements is to decide whether various topological invariants of the complement of
\mathcal{A}
are determined by the combinatorial data (i.e., the poset of intersections of hyperplanes) of
\mathcal{A}
. For instance, the homology groups and the cohomology ring of the hyperplane arrangement complement are combinatorially determined (e.g., see
[
OT92
]
), while the fundamental group of the complement are not
[
Ryb11
]
. It is an open question whether
the homology groups of a finite cover of a hyperplane arrangement
complement are combinatorially determined. This includes the Milnor fiber of a central hyperplane arrangement.
See Papadima-Suciu’s work
[
PS17
]
for recent progress in this direction and also Suciu’s survey paper
[
Suc01
]
for an overview of the theory.

It is well-known that the homology groups of hyperplane arrangement complement are torsion-free. In fact, Dimca-Papadima
[
DP03
]
and Randell
[
Ran02
]
independently showed that the complement of a hyperplane arrangement is homotopy equivalent to a minimal CW complex. On the other hand, Cohen-Denham-Suciu
[
CDS03
, Theorem 1]
gave examples of arrangements with multiplicities whose first homology group of the
Milnor fiber has torsion. Later on Denham-Suciu
[
DS14
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that for every prime
p\geq 2
, there are examples of hyperplane arrangements whose higher degree homology of the Milnor fiber has
p
-torsion. This nice result resolved Dimca-Némethi
[
DN04
, Question 7.3]
and Randell’s question
[
Ran11
, Problem 7]
whether the Milnor fiber of central hyperplane arrangement always has torsion-free homology.
In particular, this gives examples of hyperplane arrangement whose Milnor fiber do not necessarily have a minimal CW structure. Yoshinaga
[
Yos20
, Theorem 1.2]
gave the first example of hyperplane arrangement whose first homology group of Minor fiber has
2
-torsion recently.

Denham-Suciu raised the following question:

Question 1.1
.

[
DS14
, Question 1.2]
Is the torsion in the homology of the Milnor fiber of a hyperplane
arrangement combinatorially determined?

Note that the Milnor fiber of central hyperplane arrangement is a finite cyclic covering of the complement of the associated projective hyperplane arrangement.
One can ask the same question for any finite cyclic covering of hyperplane arrangement complement.
In this paper, we focus on the line arrangement case regarding this question.

1.2.
Main results

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essential line arrangement of
n
lines in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with complement
\mathcal{U}
.
Let
Q(\mathcal{A})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{0},z_{1},z_{2}]
be the reduced defining homogeneous polynomial for
\mathcal{A}
, defined up to multiplication by a non-zero scalar. The Milnor fiber
F
of the projective arrangement
\mathcal{A}
is defined by
Q(\mathcal{A})=1
, which is a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Then

is a
n
-fold cyclic covering of
\mathcal{U}
.
We recall the following result due to Williams, which gives a partial answer to Question

Theorem 1.2
.

[
Wil13
, Main Theorem 1]
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a complexified real arrangement of
n
lines in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with the Milnor fiber

. Taking any line
H\in\mathcal{A}
, let
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{s}\}
denote the set of multiple points on
H
and let
m_{i}
denote the multiplicity of
P_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
.
Then, for any field
\mathbb{K}
, the first Betti number of F with respect to
\mathbb{K}
satisfies

(1)

b_{1}(F,\mathbb{K})\leq n-1+\sum^{s}_{i=1}(m_{i}-2)(\gcd(m_{i},n)-1).

Furthermore, if
\gcd(m_{i},n)=1
for every
m_{i}>2
(e.g.

is a prime number), then
H_{1}(F,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
is torsion-free.

For a full generalization beyond the Milnor fiber case, we use the following setup. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essential complex arrangement of
n
lines in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with complement
\mathcal{U}
. Fix any line
H\in\mathcal{A}
, let
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{s}\}
denote the set of multiple points on
H
and let
m_{i}
denote the multiplicity of
P_{i}
for

. Here since

We label the other lines according to their intersections with

Fix an epimorphism

with

\varphi(\alpha)=\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}
and

Here

Theorem 1.3
.

With the above notations and assumptions, consider the composed map

for any field

b_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi,N},\mathbb{K})\leq(n-1)+\sum^{s}_{i=1}(m_{i}-2)(\gcd(\varepsilon_{i},N)-1).

Remark 1.4
.

The setup we used here is to consider all finite cyclic covering determined by
\varphi
at once. This setup is convenient for our proof and can be used to prove a divisibility result for the Alexander polynomial of
\mathcal{U}
(see Proposition

), which partially generalizes

[
Eld21
, Theorem 6]
. If one only focuses on the
N
-fold covering, the assumption can be changed to

\epsilon\equiv 1\mod N
and
\varepsilon_{i}\neq 0\mod N
for all

.

When
N=2
, this corresponds to the CDO-condition considered by Sugawara in
[
Sug23
, Definition 2.1]
. In particular, it is already proved by the first author joint with Liu
[
LL23
, Corollary 1.7]
that the 2-fold covering of the complement has torsion-free homology groups using the results proved by Sugawara for complexified real arrangement case
[
Sug23
, Theorem 1.3]
and the first author joint with Maxim and Wang
[
LMW22
, Theorem 1.2]
.

In fact, the two conditions
\epsilon\neq 0
and
\varepsilon_{i}\neq 0
for all

are corresponding to the conditions used by Cohen-Dimca-Orlik in
[
CDO03
, Theorem 1]
. We have the condition
\epsilon=1
in Theorem
1.3
to make the computation manageable.

The following result is a direct application of Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.5
.

With the same assumptions and notations as in Theorem
1.3
,
if
m_{i}=2
for every multiple point
P_{i}
in
H
and
\epsilon=1
,
H_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi,N},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
is torsion-free.

Since the Milnor fiber is a special case satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
1.3
, we extend Theorem
1.2
to any complex line arrangement.

Corollary 1.6
.

Theorem
1.2
holds for any complex line arrangement.

As noted by Williams in
[
Wil13
, Section 4]
, the inequality (
1
) is quite similar to the one obtained by Cohen-Dimca-Orlik in
[
CDO03
, Theorem 13]
, which is linked to the direction of divisibility results for Alexander polynomials of hypersurface complements. This topic is initiated by Libgober
[
Lib82
,
Lib94
]
and developed by many other authors, see
[
DL06
,
Max06
,
DM07
,
CAF07

]
.
In particular, the recent work by Elduque
[

gives a divisibility result for twisted Alexander polynomial for line arrangement complement. She constructed a tubular neighborhood along one line in loc. cit., which
plays a key role in the proof of Theorem

. The key point in the proof is to compute the fundamental group of the boundary manifold of this neighbourhood. The computation is done by a method due to Hirzebruch
[
Hir95
]
. For related works, Westlund
[
Wes97
]
computed the fundamental group of the boundary manifold of the complement

[

[
Hi01
]
and Florens-Guerville-Marco
[

studied relationships between the fundamental groups of a line arrangement complement and that of the boundary manifold of such an arrangement. The computation in our case is similar but much simpler, since
our boundary manifold is only part of the boundary manifold

.

The boundary manifold of the tubular neighbourhood

In this section, we focus on the boundary manifold of the tubular neighbourhood along a line

.

2.1.
Construction

We follow Elduque’s construction presented in
[
Eld21
, Section 2, 3]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a line arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with
n
lines.
For a fixed line
H\in\mathcal{A}
, recall that
H
has the set of multiple points
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{s}\}
with the multiplicity
m_{i}
for
P_{i}
and

\mathcal{A}=\{H,H_{1,2},\cdots,H_{1,m_{1}},\cdots,H_{s,2},\cdots,H_{1,m_{s}}\}.

For each singular point
P_{i}
, we take a small enough ball
\mathbb{B}_{i}
centered at the point
P_{i}
.
Then we take a tubular neighbourhood of
H\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{B}_{i}(\frac{1}{2})
with a even smaller radius, denoted by
\Sigma
. Here
\mathbb{B}_{i}(\frac{1}{2})
is the ball with the same center as
\mathbb{B}_{i}
but half radius.
Set

W=\Sigma\cup(\cup_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{B}_{i}).

By rounding corners, its boundary manifold
X=\partial W
is a real 3-dimensional manifold, which can be described as follows.
Set
N=\big(H\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{B}_{i}\big)\times S^{1}
, which should be thought of as the boundary of
\Sigma
around the non-singular part of
H
. We have that

\partial N=\partial\big(H\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{B}_{i}\big)\times S^{1}.

Since
\partial(H\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{B}_{i})
is a union of disjoint
S^{1}
’s (one from every disk
H\cap\mathbb{B}_{i}
removed), then
\partial N
is a union of disjoint tori
T_{i}
.
Let
L_{i}
be the link of the singularity at the point
P_{i}
(which is a Hopf link with
m_{i}
components), and let
S_{i}^{3}
be the boundary of
\mathbb{B}_{i}
. Consider the space

X=N\cup_{\sqcup_{i}T_{i}}(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{s}S^{3}_{i}\setminus L_{i}),

where the gluing is done as follows.
Note that
L_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_{i}}L_{i,j}
, where
L_{i,j}
is the Hopf link which corresponds to
H_{i,j}
(here we regard
H
as
H_{i,1}
). A meridian around
L_{i,1}
is glued to
\text{\lx@text@lbrace point\lx@text@rbrace}\times S^{1}\subset N
and
L_{i,1}
is glued to the

corresponding to the boundary of

for all

The fundamental group of

(3)

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{i})

\displaystyle\cong\left\langle\beta_{i},\alpha_{i,1},\alpha_{i,2},\cdots,\alpha_{i,m_{i}}|\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i,1}\alpha_{i,2}\cdots\alpha_{i,m_{i}},[\beta_{i},\alpha_{i,j}],1\leq j\leq m_{i}\right\rangle

via the maps

\alpha_{i,1}=\alpha

Proposition 2.1
.

With the above assumptions and notations, we have

(4)

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(X)

\displaystyle\cong\left\langle\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}\alpha,&&\alpha^{1-s}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{s}=1,&\\
\beta_{i},&1\leq i\leq s,&\beta_{i}=\alpha\alpha_{i,2}\cdots\alpha_{1,m_{i}},&1\leq i\leq s\\
\alpha_{i,j},&2\leq j\leq m_{i}&[\alpha_{i,j},\beta_{i}],[\alpha,\beta_{i}],&2\leq j\leq m_{i}\end{array}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\cong\left\langle\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}\alpha,\beta_{i},&1\leq i\leq s&\alpha^{1-s}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{s}=1,&1\leq i\leq s\\

Proof.

We use Van-Kampen Theorem to compute
\pi_{1}(X)
. Note that
S^{3}_{i}\setminus L_{i}
is isomorphic to
S^{1}\times(\mathbb{CP}^{1}-\{m_{i}\text{ points}\})
, which is a trivial bundle. So

is constructed by pasting certain

-bundles along their boundaries, which are some disjoint tori. Using Van-Kampen Theorem, we see that

can be generated by the elements
\alpha,\beta_{i},\alpha_{i,j}

[\alpha,\beta_{i}]=1,[\alpha_{i,j},\beta_{i}]=1,1\leq i\leq s,2\leq j\leq m_{i}.

at all points

\widetilde{H}

in
\widetilde{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}

Consider the

\widetilde{H}
associated to the conormal bundle of

in
\widetilde{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}

\widetilde{H}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}

-bundle over

with Euler number

[

Since the blow-up map does not change
\mathcal{U}

X\subset\mathcal{U}\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}

The left relation relies on the Euler number of

bundle over
\widetilde{H}
. In fact, considering the product of the generators (total boundary) in the

gives the following relation

For a detailed discussion, see

Using the commutation relations,

2.3.
Fox calculus and the Alexander matrix

In this subsection, we use Fox calculus to compute the Alexander matrix.
We first briefly recall the basic properties of Fox calculus.
For more details, see
[
Fox56
]
and
[
CF77
, Chapter VII]
.

Given a finitely presented group
G=\left\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}|r_{1},\cdots,r_{l}\right\rangle
, let
\tilde{G}=\left\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\right\rangle
be a free group with
m
-generators. Let
\xi:\tilde{G}\xrightarrow{}G

and
\rho\colon G\xrightarrow{}\dfrac{G}{[G,G]}
denote the natural quotient morphisms, where
[G,G]
is the commutator subgroup of
G
. Note that
\rho
can be uniquely extended to a homomorphism on the corresponding group rings, still denoted by
\rho
by abuse of notations.

Define the
i
-th Fox derivative
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}:\tilde{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}G
for all
1\leq i\leq m
such that

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{j})=\delta_{ij}

and

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(g_{1}g_{2})=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(g_{1})+\xi(g_{1})\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(g_{2}),

where
\delta_{ij}
is Kronecker symbol,
g_{1},g_{2}
are elements of
\tilde{G}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The Alexander matrix of group
G
is a matrix
M=(c_{ij})
, where the size of
M
is
l\times m
and the entries are given by
c_{ij}=\rho(\dfrac{\partial r_{i}}{\partial x_{j}})
.

Now let us do the Fox calculus for
\pi_{1}(X)
. For the relations in
\pi_{1}(X)
, we have the following formulas:

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}(\alpha^{-1})=-\alpha^{-1},

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}(\alpha^{k})=\dfrac{\alpha^{k}-1}{\alpha-1},

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{i,j}}([\alpha_{i,j},\beta_{i}])=1-\alpha_{i,j}\beta_{i}\alpha_{i,j}^{-1}\overset{\rho}{\mapsto}1-\beta_{i},

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\beta_{i}}([\alpha_{i,j},\beta_{i}])=\alpha_{i,j}-\alpha_{i,j}\beta_{i}\alpha_{i,j}^{-1}\beta_{i}^{-1}\overset{\rho}{\mapsto}\alpha_{i,j}-1,

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\beta_{i}}(\alpha^{1-s}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{i})=\alpha^{1-s}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{i-1}\overset{\rho}{\mapsto}\alpha^{1-s}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{i-1}.

Then we get the Alexander matrix of
\pi_{1}(X)
:

(5)

M=\begin{pNiceMatrix},

where the empty entries are zero and
M_{i},V_{i},Q_{i}
are matrices with the following form:

(6)

\displaystyle M_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{pNiceMatrix}_{(m_{i}-1)\times(m_{i}-1)},

\displaystyle V_{i}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta_{i},0,\cdots,0)^{\mathsf{T}}_{1\times(m_{i}-1)},

\displaystyle Q_{i}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{1-s}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{i-1},0,\cdots,0)_{1\times(m_{i}-1)}.

Here
\mathsf{T}
denote the transpose of the matrix.

3.
Proof of the main results

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
First we list two equalities that will be used later:

(7)

\epsilon+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=2}^{m_{i}}\epsilon_{i,j}=(1-s)\epsilon+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\varepsilon_{i}=0

and

(8)

n=1+\sum_{i=1}^{s}(m_{i}-1)=1-s+\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}.

3.1.
Divisibility results for Alexander polynomial

Consider the inclusion map
X\to\mathcal{U}
. Note that
X
is connected, hence the map induced by inclusion
\pi_{0}(X)\to\pi_{0}(\mathcal{U})
is an isomorphism. By a Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem type argument, the map
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})
induced by inclusion is an epimorphism. In fact, we can take a generic line
L
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
, which is close enough to the line
H
such that
L
is contained in the tubular neighborhood we constructed in section 2. Then
the map
\pi_{1}(L\cap\mathcal{U})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})
is surjective by Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem (see
[
Dim92
, Proposition 4.3.1]
). Note that
L\cap\mathcal{U}\subset W\setminus H
and
W\setminus H
is homotopy equivalent to
X
. Putting all together, we get that the
the map
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})
induced by inclusion is an epimorphism.

Consider the composed maps
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})\overset{\varphi}{\to}\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
X^{\varphi}
denote the corresponding infinite cyclic covering space and
X^{\varphi,N}
denote the
N
-fold cyclic covering for any integer
N>1
induced by further composing with
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. Then we have the following result simply due to the fact that
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})
is an epimorphism.

Lemma 3.1
.

With the above assumptions and notations, the induced map
H_{1}(X^{\varphi,N},\mathbb{K})\to H_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi,N},\mathbb{K})
is surjective for any field
\mathbb{K}
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
, by lifting the cell structure of
X
to
X^{\varphi}
,
we obtain a free basis for the cellular chain complex of
X^{\varphi}
as
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
-modules.
Then the cellular chain complex
C_{*}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
is a bounded complex of finitely generated free
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
-modules.
The first Alexander module
H_{1}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
is the first homology of the complex
C_{*}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
, hence
H_{1}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
-modules. Note that
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
is a PID.

Definition 3.2
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
, the first Alexander polynomial of
X
with respect the covering space
X^{\varphi}
is defined
as the order of the torsion part of
H_{1}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
, denoted by
\Delta_{1}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
.

Consider the covering space
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}
associated to the map
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})\overset{\varphi}{\to}\mathbb{Z}
.
For any field
\mathbb{K}
, the first Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
of
\mathcal{U}
with respect to the covering space

can be defined similarly.

Proposition 3.3
.

With the above assumptions and notations, if
\epsilon\neq 0
and
\varepsilon_{i}\neq 0
for all
m_{i}>2
, then for any field
\mathbb{K}
,
H_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
is a torsion
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
-module and

\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})\mid\ (t-1)\cdot(t^{\epsilon}-1)^{s-2}\cdot\prod^{s}_{i=1}(t^{\varepsilon_{i}}-1)^{m_{i}-2}.

Proof.

We use Fox calculus to compute the first Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{1}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
.
In fact, it can be computed by the following Alexander matrix associated to
X^{\varphi}
:

(9)

\begin{pNiceMatrix}.

First, one can show the determinant of this matrix is 0 by cofactor expansion of the first row. Then
it is easy to see that this matrix has rank
n-1
if
\epsilon\neq 0
and
\varepsilon_{i}\neq 0
for all
m_{i}>2
(if
m_{i}=2
, one can see that the column for
\alpha_{i,2}
does not appear, hence the value of
\varepsilon_{i}
does not effect the rank in this case).
In particular,
H_{1}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
is a torsion
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
-module.

Using some elementary column and row transformations, we get the following new matrix:

\begin{pNiceArray}.

Set

f(t)=(t^{\epsilon}-1)^{s-2}\prod^{s}_{i=1}(t^{\varepsilon_{i}}-1)^{m_{i}-2}.

We consider some minors with size
n-1
.
Removing the row for
[\alpha,\beta_{i}]
and the first column, the column for
\beta_{i}
and the column for
\alpha_{i,j}
with
1\leq i\leq s
and
2\leq j\leq m_{i}-1
respectively, the corresponding minors are

(t^{\epsilon}-1)\cdot f(t)
,
(t^{\varepsilon_{i}}-1)\cdot f(t)
and
(t^{\epsilon_{i,j}}-1)\cdot f(t)

up to a unit of
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
. For the computation of these minors, one needs the following equality

0=1-t^{\epsilon(1-s)}+(1-t^{\varepsilon_{1}})t^{\epsilon(1-s)}+\cdots+(1-t^{\varepsilon_{s}})t^{\epsilon(1-s)+\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{s-1}},

which follows from (
7
).

Since
\varepsilon_{i}=\epsilon+\sum^{m_{i}}_{j=2}\epsilon_{i,j}
, we have the following equality

1-t^{\varepsilon_{i}}=(1-t^{\epsilon})+(1-t^{\epsilon_{i,2}})t^{\epsilon}+\cdots+(1-t^{\epsilon_{i,m_{i}}})t^{\epsilon+\epsilon_{i,2}+\cdots+\epsilon_{i,m_{i}-1}}.

Putting all these together, we have that the Alexander polynomial of
X^{\varphi}
divides

\gcd(t^{\epsilon}-1,t^{\epsilon_{1,2}}-1,\cdots,t^{\epsilon_{1,m_{i}}}-1,t^{\epsilon_{2,2}}-1,\cdots,t^{\epsilon_{s,m_{s}}}-1)f(t).

Note that
\varphi:\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is surjective,
which implies
\gcd(\epsilon,\epsilon_{1,2},\cdots,\epsilon_{s,m_{s}})=1
.
This fact implies that

\gcd(t^{\epsilon}-1,t^{\epsilon_{1,2}}-1,\cdots,t^{\epsilon_{1,m_{i}}}-1,t^{\epsilon_{2,2}}-1,\cdots,t^{\epsilon_{s,m_{s}}}-1)=t-1,

which can be proved by the similar trick for above equality of
1-t^{\varepsilon_{i}}
.
Hence the Alexander polynomial of
X^{\varphi}
divides
(t-1)f(t)
. One should check that the proof still works even if some
\epsilon_{i,j}=0
.

On the other hand, since
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U})
is surjective, it follows that the inclusion map induces an epimorphism

H_{1}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})\twoheadrightarrow H_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi},\mathbb{K}),

see e.g.
[
Eld21
, Proposition 2]
. Putting all together, we get that
H_{1}(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi},\mathbb{K})
is a torsion
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm}]
-module
and the Alexander polynomial of
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}
divides the Alexander polynomial of
X^{\varphi}
. Then the claim follows.
∎

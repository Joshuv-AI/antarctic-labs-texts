Abstract
. In the homogeneous manifold 𝔼 ⁡ ( − 1 , τ ) , \mathbb{E}(-1,\tau), for − 1 2 < H < 1 2 , -\tfrac{1}{2}<H<\tfrac{1}{2}, we define a new product compactification in which the slices { t = c } c ∈ ℝ \left\{t=c\right\}_{c\in\mathbb{R}} are rotational H H -surfaces. This product compatification is the natural setting where it makes sense to study the asymptotic Dirichlet Problem for the constant mean curvature equation. Indeed, for every rectifiable curve Γ \Gamma projecting bijectively onto ∂ ℍ 2 \partial\mathbb{H}^{2} we prove the existence of a unique entire H H -graph that is asymptotic to Γ \Gamma . We also find necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of H H -graphs over unbounded domains having prescribed, possibly infinite boundary data.

1.
Introduction

Constant mean curvature surfaces in simply connected homogeneous 3-manifolds have been a subject of extensive research by various authors over the past two decades. In particular, considerable attention has been devoted to 3-manifolds with an isometry group of dimension at least 4. These 3-manifolds, excluding the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, can be classified in a 2-parameter family denoted as
\mathbb{E}(\kappa,\tau)
, where
\kappa
and
\tau
are real numbers. This classification depends on the property of the
\mathbb{E}(\kappa,\tau)
-spaces to admit a Riemannian submersion
\pi\colon\mathbb{E}(\kappa,\tau)\to\mathbb{M}(\kappa)
over the complete simply connected Riemannian surface with constant curvature
\kappa
whose fibers are the integral curves of the unique unitary Killing vector field
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{E})
such that
\tau
is the bundle curvature (that is
\nabla_{X}\xi=\tau X\wedge\xi
for all vector fields
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{E}),
where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\mathbb{E}
and
\wedge
is the cross product in
\mathbb{E}
with respect to a chosen orientation, see
[
15
]
).

This geometric setup naturally prompts the question of investigating constant mean curvature graphs over specific domains of
\mathbb{M}(\kappa)
, i.e. sections of the Riemannian submersion obtained by deforming a fixed zero section through the flow lines.

In this paper we focus on the case
\kappa=-1
and investigate graphs with constant mean curvature
H
that will be referred as an
H
-graphs or, whenever
H
is not desired to be explicit, CMC graphs.

One of the pioneering works in this setting is due to Nelli and Rosenberg in
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{E}(-1,0)
(see
[
20
]
), who solved the following problems.

•

The existence of a unique entire minimal graph with arbitrarily prescribed asymptotic values, that is, the graph is asymptotic to a rectifiable curve that projects bijectively onto
\partial\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

•

The Jenkins–Serrin problem over relatively compact domains, that is, to find necessary and sufficient conditions that guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a solution for the Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface equation with possibly infinite boundary data.

So far, no result with an analogous statement as in
[
20
]
has been given for CMC graphs with prescribed asymptotic boundary value. This is also due to the fact that in the standard compactification for
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
, i.e. the model described in Section
2
, such a result cannot be achieved due to the Slab Theorem (see
[
12
]
), which implies that any entire function satisfying the constant mean curvature equation must diverge.

Concerning the Jenkins–Serrin problem, several extensions have been achieved.
A Jenkins–Serrin problem for CMC graphs in
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
over relatively compact domains of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
was proved by Hauswirth, Rosenberg and Spruck in
[
13
]
and by Eichmair and Metzger in
[
9
]
in a general product space
M\times\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, in
[
8
]
, the author, together with Manzano and Nelli, proves an analogous result for minimal graphs in a general Killing submersion, which include the space
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau).

In
[
17
]
Mazet, Rodriguez and Rosenberg proved in
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
a Jenkins–Serrin type result for minimal surfaces over unbounded domains of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, allowing the asymptotic boundary of such domains to contain open subsets of
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Later, Melo proved the same result in the space
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)

[
19
]
.
Finally, in
[
10
]
, Folha and Melo in
[
10
]
solved the Jenkins–Serrin problem for
H
-graphs in
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
with
0<H<1/2
over an unbounded domain whose asymptotic boundary does not contain open subsets of
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

In this paper we consider a new model for
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
in which the zero section has a fixed constant mean curvature
H\in\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
. Using this model, we can consider a new product compactification (which we call
H
-compactification) of
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
such that every family of
H
-surfaces that foliates the space
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
also foliates its asymptotic boundary
\pi^{-1}(\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{2})
, as it happens for the standard product compatification when we consider minimal surfaces (see
Remark

4.2
and
Figure

7
for a detailed description of the relation between the classical product compactification and the
H
-compactification). Furthermore, considering
|H|
to be strictly less then the critical value for the mean curvature ensures that the fibers of the submersion stay transversal to the foliation at the infinity reproducing the same properties of the classical product compactification in the minimal case (see
Section

4
for the details).

In particular, fixed
H\in\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
, we solve the following problems in the
H
-compatification of
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
:

•

Given a rectifiable curve
\Gamma\in\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{2}\times^{H}\mathbb{R}
that is the graph of a function of
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{2}
, we are able to construct an
H
-graph
\Sigma
that is asymptotic to
\Gamma
(see
Theorem

6.1
). In such a way, we have a direct control over the symmetries of the graph
\Sigma
. Indeed, by means of the Maximum Principle one can prove that
\Sigma
inherits the symmetries of the curve
\Gamma
. We cannot cover the case
H=1/2
because of the half-space property given in
[
22
,
16
]
. Moreover, due to our result, a half-space theorem for entire
H
-graphs with
H\in\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
cannot be proven.

•

We give the necessary and sufficient conditions that guaranty the existence of a solution to the Jenkins-Serrin problem for
H
-graphs over a larger set of unbounded domains of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, allowing them to contain parts of
\partial\mathbb{H}^{2}
in their asymptotic boundary (see
Theorem

7.5
).

Usually, the mean curvature is considered to be non-negative since, when
\tau=0
, we can solve the case
H\in(-1/2,0)
simply by applying a reflection with respect to the horizontal totally geodesic graph to a solution with positive
H
. In this article, we describe the new model and the upper and lower barriers in this setting for both
H>0,
and
H<0
. We then prove the main theorems under the assumption
H\in(0,1/2)
, as the other case follows by an analogous argument.

The paper is organized as follows.

Section

2
is a description of the basic properties of the space
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
and its compactification. In
Section

3
we recall the constant mean curvature graphs that are invariant with respect to a one-parameter group of isometries of
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
studied by Peñafiel in
[
23
]
and we study their asymptotic behaviour.
In
Section

4
we introduce the new model for
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
in which the plane
\left\{t=0\right\}
has constant mean curvature
H\in(-1/2,1/2)
.
In
Section

5
, we use the surfaces studied in
Section

3
to construct barriers for the Dirichlet Problem for the constant mean curvature equation. Finally, the main results of this paper are given in
Sections

6
and
7
, where, respectively, we prove the existence of entire
H
-graphs with prescribed asymptotic boundary and the Jenkins–Serrin problem.

2.
The space
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)

The space
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
is the homogeneous 3-dimensional manifold defined as
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, endowed with the metric

(2.1)

ds^{2}_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}\right)+\left[dt-2\tau\lambda\left(\left(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\right)_{y}dx-\left(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\right)_{x}dy\right)\right]^{2}.

where
\Omega=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\lambda(x,y)>0\right\}
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies
\Delta(\log(\lambda))-\lambda^{2}=0
.

Notice that
\left(\Omega,\lambda^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})\right)
is isometric to the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with a constant sectional curvature of
-1
and the Killing submersion is
\pi(x,y,t)=(x,y)
with
\partial_{t}
being the unitary Killing field satisfying the identity
\nabla_{X}\partial_{t}=\tau X\wedge\partial_{t}
for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{E})
. In this context, we call
vertical
every vector field parallel to
\partial_{t},
and horizontal every vector field orthogonal to
\partial_{t}
.
In this model, the mean curvature of the graph
\Sigma_{u}=(x,y,u(x,y))\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
of the function
u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Omega)
is given in the divergent form as

(2.2)

2H=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}}\right),

where
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|
are the divergence and norm of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
in the conformal metric
\lambda^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}),
and
Gu=\left(u_{x}-\tfrac{2\tau\lambda_{y}}{\lambda}\right)\frac{\partial_{x}}{\lambda^{2}}+\left(u_{y}+\tfrac{2\tau\lambda_{x}}{\lambda}\right)\frac{\partial_{y}}{\lambda^{2}}
is the generalized gradient.

Remark 2.1
.

Equation (
2.2
) follows from
2H=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits_{\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)}(N)=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\pi_{*}N)
, where
\pi\colon\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
is the projection on the first two coordinates and
N_{u}=\frac{\partial_{t}+Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}}
is upward-pointing unit normal to the Killing graph of
u
.

Depending on the choice of
\lambda,
we have two classical models to describe
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau):

•

If
\lambda(x,y)=\lambda_{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{y}
, we get the half-plane model for
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and the half-space model
\mathcal{H}
for
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau),
where the vertical boundary is
(\left\{y=0\right\}\cup\left\{\infty\right\})\times\mathbb{R}\equiv(\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{\infty\right\})\times\mathbb{R}.

•

If
\lambda(x,y)=\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\frac{2}{1-x^{2}-y^{2}}
, we obtain the Poincaré Disk model for
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and the cylindrical model
\mathcal{C}
for
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau),
with
\mathbb{S}(1)\times\mathbb{R}
as vertical boundary.

Using the complex coordinate
z=x+iy
, the isometries between this two models are given by lifting the Möbius transformation of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
(see
[
1
]
for more details):

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\psi\colon&\overline{\mathcal{H}}&\to&\overline{\mathcal{C}}\\
&(x,y,t)&\mapsto&\left(\frac{z-i}{z+1},t-4\tau\arctan\left(\frac{x}{y+1}\right)\right),\end{array}

and

(2.3)

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\psi^{-1}\colon&\overline{\mathcal{C}}&\to&\overline{\mathcal{H}}\\
&(x,y,t)&\mapsto&\left(\frac{i(z+1)}{1-z},t-4\tau\arctan\left(\frac{y}{1-x}\right)\right).\end{array}

Some isometries of
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau),
can be easily described using either
\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{H}:

•

Vertical translation: in both models

\varphi_{1}\colon(x,y,t)\mapsto(x,y,t+c),\,\forall c\in\mathbb{R}

•

Hyperbolic translation along an horizontal geodesic: using the half-space model
\mathcal{H},
the hyperbolic translation along the horizontal geodesics in
\left\{x=x_{0}\right\}
is given by the map

\varphi_{2}\colon(x,y,t)\mapsto(x_{0}+c(x-x_{0}),cy,t),\,\forall c\in\mathbb{R}.

•

Parabolic translation: using the half-space model
\mathcal{H},
the hyperbolic translation along the horocycle
\left\{y=y_{0}\right\}
is given by the map

\varphi_{3}\colon(x,y,t)\mapsto(x+c,y,t),\,\forall c\in\mathbb{R}.

•

Rotation: in the cylinder model
\mathcal{C},
rotations with respect to the
t
-axis are Euclidean rotations with respect to the
t
-axis, that are,

\varphi_{4}\colon(x,y,z)\mapsto(x\cos(\theta)-y\sin(\theta),x\sin(\theta)+y\cos(\theta),t),\,\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

In the cylinder model
\mathcal{C}
, the surface
\left\{t=0\right\}
is the rotational minimal surface
\mathcal{U}
known as umbrella; in the half-space model
\mathcal{H}
, the surface
\left\{t=0\right\}
is the minimal surface,
\mathcal{S},
invariant with respect to hyperbolic and parabolic translation.

Constant mean curvature surfaces invariant by a one-parameter group of isometries and their asymptotic behaviour

In this section, we provide a complete classification for CMC surfaces that are invariant under either rotations or hyperbolic translations and are vertical graphs of functions of
\Omega
, in either the cylinder or the half-space model. The study of such surfaces has been conducted previously in
[
26
]
and
[
25
]
for
\tau=0
and in
[
3
]
and
[
23
]
for
\tau\neq 0
. In particular, our interest focus on the asymptotic behaviour of these invariant surfaces with respect to the surface
\left\{t=0\right\}
in each model, and the relation between
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{U}
.

To describe the surfaces invariant by the rotation
\varphi_{4}
we will use the cylinder model
\mathcal{C}
and the geodesic polar coordinates

\begin{cases}x(\rho,\theta)=\tanh\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)\cos(\theta),\\
y(\rho,\theta)=\tanh\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)\sin(\theta),\end{cases}

of the base
\mathbb{H}^{2}
of the Killing submersion.
So, any
H
-surface invariant by
\varphi_{4}
is parametrized by

\mathcal{R}^{H}_{d}(\rho,\theta)=\left(\tanh\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)\cos(\theta),\tanh\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)\sin(\theta),v^{H}_{d}(\rho)\right),

where
v^{H}_{d}
is any solution of the differential equation

\displaystyle\left(\coth(\rho)-16\tau^{2}\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits^{3}(\rho)\sinh^{4}\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)+4\tau^{2}\coth(\rho)\tanh^{2}\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)+\coth(\rho)\dot{v}^{H}_{d}(\rho)\right)\dot{v}^{H}_{d}(\rho)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1+4\tau^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)\right)\overset{..}{v}^{H}_{d}(\rho)=2H\left(1+4\tau^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)+\dot{v}^{H}_{d}(\rho)^{2}\right)^{\tfrac{3}{2}}.

As it is shown in
[
23
]
, the solution is given by the one-parameter family of integral functions

(3.1)

v^{H}_{d}(\rho)=\int_{*}^{\rho}\frac{(2H\cosh(r)+d)\sqrt{1+4\tau^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\tfrac{r}{2}\right)}}{\sqrt{\sinh^{2}(r)-(2H\cosh(r)+d)^{2}}}dr,

and, depending on the choice of
d\in\mathbb{R},
we can obtain three type of surfaces:

•

if
d>-2H,

is the upper half of a proper embedded annulus symmetric with respect to the slice
\left\{t=0\right\}

•

if

then
\mathcal{R}^{H}_{d}(\rho,\theta)
is an entire vertical graph contained in

and tangent to
\left\{t=0\right\}

(see
Figure

•

if
d<-2H,

\mathcal{R}^{H}_{d}(\rho,\theta)

\left\{t=0\right\}

A new model for
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)

In this section we use the Killing Submersions theory to describe a new cylindrical model

(

\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau),
in which the slices
\left\{t=c\right\}_{c\in\mathbb{R}}
are rotational surfaces of fixed constant mean curvature

In general, if
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a simply connected domain endowed with the conformal metric
\lambda^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})
, with
\lambda\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})

is such that

is endowed with the metric

ds^{2}=\lambda^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})+[\lambda(a(x,y)dx+b(x,y)dy)+dt]^{2},

where

satisfy the bundle curvature equation

\frac{(\lambda b)_{x}-(\lambda a)_{y}}{2\lambda^{2}}=\tau.

For any fixed surface
\Sigma=(x,y,d(x,y))
in
\left(\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R},ds^{2}\right)
, described by the function
d\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}),
that is an entire graph over
\mathcal{D}
, the map

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\mathcal{I}_{d}\colon&\left(\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R},ds^{2}\right)&\to&\left(\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R},ds_{1}^{2}\right)\\
&(x,y,t)&\mapsto&(x,y,t-d(x,y))\end{array}

fixes the fibers of the submersion and send
\Sigma
to the slice
\left\{t=0\right\}
in
\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}
. Here, the metric
ds_{1}^{2}
is again of the form

ds_{1}^{2}=\lambda^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})+[\lambda(a^{\prime}(x,y)dx+b^{\prime}(x,y)dy)+dt]^{2}

with
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
satisfying

\frac{(\lambda b^{\prime})_{x}-(\lambda a^{\prime})_{y}}{2\lambda^{2}}=\tau.

The uniqueness result for Killing Submersions (see
[
14
, Theorem 2.6]
) implies that
\mathcal{I}_{d}
is an isometry and
a
and
a^{\prime}
and
b
and
b^{\prime}
satisfy the following relation:

b^{\prime}=b+\frac{d_{y}}{\lambda},\qquad a^{\prime}=a+\frac{d_{x}}{\lambda}.

We now apply this idea to
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau),
described with the cylindrical model
\mathcal{C}.
For any fixed
H\in\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right]

\Sigma

H

is isometric to

\begin{array}[]{lr}a_{\mathcal{C}}^{H}(x,y)=2y\tau+2Hx\sqrt{\frac{1+4(x^{2}+y^{2})\tau^{2}}{1-4H^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})}},&b_{\mathcal{C}}^{H}(x,y)=-2x\tau+2Hy\sqrt{\frac{1+4(x^{2}+y^{2})\tau^{2}}{1-4H^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})}}.\end{array}

H(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\frac{G^{H}u}{\sqrt{1+\|G^{H}u\|^{2}}}\right),

H
-graphs

In this section we consider the
H
-compactification of
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
in the
H
-cylinder model
\mathcal{C}_{H}
given by
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
endowed with the metric in (
4.2
), in which
\left\{t=0\right\}
is a rotational surface of constant mean curvature
H\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
and we prove the existence of
H
-graph having a prescribed asymptotic
H
-boundary. We point out that the existence of non-invariant subcritical CMC graphs in
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
was already proven in
[
11
]
using Scherk graphs over ideal domains. Our approach will allow us to have better control over the asymptotic behaviour of the
H
-graphs we will build.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable curve in
\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, that is the vertical graph of the function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}(\partial\Omega)
. Then, there exists an entire
H
-graph having
\Gamma
as asymptotic
H
-boundary. Such graph is unique.

Proof.

Assume first that
\Gamma
is differentiable. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
D_{n}\subset\Omega
be the disk centered at the origin with Euclidean radius
1-\tfrac{1}{n+1},
so that
\left\{D_{n}\right\}_{n}
is an exhaustion of
\Omega.
Since
\partial D_{n}
has geodesic curvature greater than
2H
for any
n\in\mathbb{N},
for any
g\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D_{n})
, there exists a
H
-graph
u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(D_{n})
such that
u\equiv g
in
\partial D_{n}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, or
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
). Let
\eta_{n}\colon\partial D_{n}\to\partial\Omega
be such that
\eta_{n}(x,y)=\left(\tfrac{n+1}{n}x,\tfrac{n+1}{n}y\right)
and
\varphi_{n}=\varphi\circ\eta_{n}.
Hence,
\varphi_{n}(\partial D_{n})
is a sequence of curves lying between
\left\{t=\min\,\varphi\right\}
and
\left\{t=\max\,\varphi\right\}
and converging to
\Gamma
for
n\to\infty.
Denote by
u_{n}
the solution of the Dirichlet problem for constant mean curvature
H
in
D_{n}
with
\varphi_{n}
as boundary values and by
M_{n}
its graph. Recall that in this setting the slice
\left\{t=c\right\}
is an
H
-surface for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the maximum principle implies that
\min\,\varphi\leq u_{n}\leq\max\,\varphi
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}.

The Compactness Theorem implies that for each
n>1
there exists subsequence
\left\{u^{n}_{m}\right\}\subset\left\{u^{n-1}_{m}\right\}
that converges to an
H
-graph
u^{n}
in
D_{n}
, where
u^{1}_{m}=u_{m}
. By construction, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
u^{n+1}
is the analytic extension of
u^{n}
from
D_{n}
to to
D_{n+1}
. In particular, it follows by a diagonal argument that the subsequence
\left\{u^{n}\right\}
of
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
converges to a solution
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
, and we denote by
M
its graph.

We have that
\Gamma
is contained in the asymptotic
H
-boundary
\partial M
of
M
by construction, so it remain to prove that if
p\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\Gamma,
then
p\notin\partial M.
So we want to show that for
p\notin\partial M,
there exists a surface that separates
p
from all the element of
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
. We need to distinguish two cases:
p
above
\Gamma
and
p
below
\Gamma
. Let us start with the first case. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
p=(1,0,\varphi(1,0)+2\varepsilon).
We want to use the upper barriers described in the previous section, so let
q=p=(1,0,\varphi(1,0)+\varepsilon)
and consider as upper barrier the function
\omega^{q}_{c}
described in
Proposition

5.2
. If
c
is sufficiently large,
f^{q}_{c}>\varphi
in all
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
and
\omega_{c}^{q}>u^{n}
in
D_{n}
.

If
p
is below
\Gamma
, without loss of generality we can assume
p=(1,0,\varphi(1,0)-2\varepsilon).
Let
\varepsilon_{1}>0
be such that
\underset{q\in\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}}{\max}(\varphi((1,0))-\varphi(q))<\varepsilon,
where
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}=\left\{(x,y)\in\partial\Omega\mid|y|<\varepsilon_{1},x>0\right\}.
Denote by
\varepsilon_{2}=\tfrac{\varphi_{1}}{3}
, by
\gamma_{\infty}=\left\{(x,y)\in\partial\Omega\mid|y|<\varepsilon_{1},x>0\right\}
and by
\gamma
the curve of constant geodesic curvature
2H
connecting the endpoints of
\gamma_{\infty}
such that the domain
D_{\gamma}
bounded by
\gamma\cup\gamma_{\infty}
and consider in
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(D_{\gamma})
described in the previous section whose graph is an hyperbolic invariant
H
-surface that diverges to
-\infty
along
\gamma
and to the function
f
along
\gamma_{\infty}
. After possibly a vertical translation and a rotation we can assume that
f
attains his maximum in
(1,0)
and that
f(1,0)=\varphi(1,0)-\varepsilon
. In this way, the maximum principle implies that
\omega^{-}<u_{n}
in
D_{\gamma}\cap D_{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and so
p
can not be contained in
\partial M
.

Now, if
\Gamma
is rectifiable, we consider two families of differentiable curves approximating
\Gamma
respectively from above and from below, and use the argument in
[
21
, Correction 4(a)]
and this conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

The result of this theorem is consistent with the Slab Theorem in
[
12
]
. Indeed, if we apply the inverse isometry
\mathcal{I}^{-1}
and move back to the classical cylinder model
\mathcal{C}
with
\left\{t=0\right\}

are asymptotic to the entire rotational

+\infty
approaching the asymptotic vertical boundary of

Furthermore, the previous result shows that a half-space Theorem analogous to
[

, Theorem 1]
and
[
16
, Theorem 3]
cannot be proven for
-\tfrac{1}{2}<H<\tfrac{1}{2}
and that the hypothesis of
[
18
, Theorem 2]
are sharp.

With an analogous argument we can prove the following result.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
D\subset\Omega
be a convex domain with
\partial D=\gamma\cup\gamma_{\infty}
is such that
\gamma_{\infty}\subset\partial\Omega
and
\gamma
has geodesic curvature greater or equal to
2H
with respect to

. Then, for any

piecewise continuous, there exists a solution to the Dirichlet Problem

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}H(u)=H&\textrm{in }D,\\

Remark 6.4
.

Using the Perron Process (see
[

for a detailed description) we extend this result and assume

to be non-convex with re-entrant corners, that is, we assume

7.
Asymptotic Jenkins–Serrin result

Again we consider the model that describe the
H
-compactification of
\mathbb{E}(-1,\tau)
given by
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
endowed with the metric in (
4.2
), in which
\left\{t=0\right\}
is a rotational surface of constant mean curvature
H\in\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right).
In this section we prove a Jenkins–Serrin type result for subcritical constant mean curvature graphs over unbounded domains of
\mathbb{H}^{2}.
When
\tau=0
this result was proven by Mazet, Rodriguez and Rosenberg (see
[
17
, Theorems 4.9, 4.12]
) for
H=0
and by Folha and Melo (see
[
10
, Theorems 3.1, 3.2]
) for
0<H<\tfrac{1}{2}
when the asymptotic boundary of the domain consists only of a finite number of isolated points of the asymptotic boundary of
\mathbb{H}^{2}.
We extend these results to the case
\tau\neq 0
allowing the asymptotic boundary of the domains to contain open arcs of the asymptotic boundary of
\mathbb{H}^{2}.

Definition 7.1

(Jenkins–Serrin domain)
.

A simply connected domain
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
is called a

(see
Figure

\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{D}
consists of a finite number of arcs
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
of constant geodesic curvature
2H
and
-2H
respectively with respect to
\mathcal{D}
, a finite number of arcs
C_{i}
of geodesic curvature

and a finite number of open arcs
D_{i}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{2},
together with their endpoints, which are called the vertices of
\mathcal{D}.

If
H>0
(resp.
H<0
), we say that a Jenkins–Serrin domain
\mathcal{D}
is
admissible
if

A)

neither two
A_{i}
or two

meets at a convex corner of
\mathcal{D},
nor at an asymptotic vertex;

B)

denoting by

(resp.
A_{i}^{*}

B_{i}
(resp.
A_{i}
) in
\mathbb{H}^{2}

(resp.

(resp.

(resp.

(resp.

of constant geodesic curvature

Remark 7.2
.

Condition (A) of admissibility is a necessary condition that follows by applying the Flux Argument. Condition (B), is necessary to define the extended Jenkins–Serrin domain, where is possible to solve a Dirichlet problem with finite boundary data. Condition (C) and (D) allows construction of the right upper/lower barriers, depending on the sign of
H
(see
Section

5

Notice also that condition (D) is trivially satisfied when
\mathcal{D}
does not admit re-entrant corners.

Without loss of generality we can assume
H>0

H<0
is the same under minor changes on the definition of the admissible domain (see
Definition

7.1
) and on the choice of the upper and lower barriers (see
Section

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an admissible Jenkins–Serrin domain. Assume that both

and
\left\{D_{i}\right\}
are empty. Then, there exists a solution to the Dirichlet problem

\alpha(\partial\mathcal{D})=\beta(\partial\mathcal{D})+2H\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}),

and for any inscribed

(7.2)

2\alpha(P)<\gamma(P)+2H\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}_{P})\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad 2\beta(P)>\gamma(P)-2H\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}_{P}).

General properties of graphs of constant mean curvature

Before, introducing the problem we recall a couple of results that will be necessary to prove the theorem.
Let
D\subset\Omega
be an open domain and
u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}(\partial D)
defining an
H
-graph
\Sigma_{u}
in the classical cylinder model
\mathcal{C}
, that is,
u
satisfy
H(u)=H\in\mathbb{R},
then, integrating
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}}\right)
gives

(7.3)

2H\mathcal{A}(D)=\int_{\partial D}\left\langle{\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle,

where
\mathcal{A}(D)
is the hyperbolic area of
D
and
\nu
is the outer normal to
\partial D.
The integral in the right side of the equation is called
flux
of
u
across
\partial D.
If
\gamma\subset\partial D
is diffeomorphic to a segment we can define the flux of
u
across
\gamma
as follows:

Definition 7.7
.

Let
\Gamma\subset D
any smooth curve such that
\gamma\cup\Gamma
bound a simply connected domain
\Delta_{\Gamma}.
Then we define the flux of
u
across
\gamma
as

\mathrm{Flux}_{u}\left(\gamma\right)=2H\mathcal{A}(\Delta_{\Gamma})-\int_{\Gamma}\left\langle{\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle

Theorem

. The proof is analogous to the case
\tau=0,
proved in

Lemma 7.8
.

Let

be such that
H(u)=H
in a bounded domain

\Gamma
be a piecewise
\mathcal{C}^{1}

\partial D
and denote by

\Gamma

2H\mathcal{A}(D)=\int_{\partial D}\left\langle{\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle\,\textrm{and }\left|\int_{\Gamma}\left\langle{\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle\right|\leq|\Gamma|.

Furthermore, if

is continuous on

\left|\int_{\Gamma}\left\langle{\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle\right|<|\Gamma|.

In

and diverges to

\int_{\Gamma}\left\langle{\frac{Gu}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle=\pm|\Gamma|.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
D
be a domain bounded in part by an arc
\gamma
and let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
be a sequence of solutions of
H(u_{n})=H
in

such that each

Then

(1)

if the sequence tends to

uniformly on compact subsets of
\gamma

we have

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\int_{\gamma}\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu_{n}\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle=|\gamma|;

(2)

we have

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\int_{\gamma}\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\|Gu_{n}\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle=-|\gamma|.

In the following theorem we prove a General Maximum Principle for unbounded domains that will be useful to study the case
\left\{D_{i}\right\}=\emptyset
. For
\tau=0
, this theorem was proved by Collin and Rosenberge
[
2
, Theorem 2]
, when
H=0
, and by Folha and Melo
[
10
, Theorem 4.2]
, when
H\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
. The following results can be deduced by the argument in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
, but we give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a domain such that
\partial\Omega_{0}
is an ideal
H
-polygon. Let
W\subset\Omega_{0}
be a domain and let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{W})
be two solution of
H(u^{i})=H
in
W
with
u_{1}<u_{2}
on
\partial W
. Suppose that for each vertex
p
of
\partial\Omega_{0}
,
\liminf\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2})\to 0
as one converges to
p
, where
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are the curves on
\partial\Omega_{0}
with
p
as vertex. Then
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
in
W
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists
W_{0}\subset W=\left\{p\in W|u_{1}(p)>u_{2}(p)\right\}
. The classical Maximum Principle implies that
W_{0}
cannot be compact. Furthermore,
u_{1}=u_{2}
on
\partial W_{0}
by continuity. Denote by
W_{0}^{n}
the intersection between
W_{0}
and the geodesic ball
B(p,n)
of radius
n
centered at a fixed point
p
outside
W_{0}
. Since
H(u_{1})=H(u_{2})=H
,
Lemma

7.8
implies that

\int_{\partial W_{0}^{n}}\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{1}\|^{2}}}-\frac{Gu_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{2}\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle=0

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\nu
is the unit normal of
\partial W_{0}
pointing inside
W_{0}
.
Furthermore,
Lemma

7.8
implies that

\left|\int_{\partial B(p,n)\cap W_{0}}\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{1}\|^{2}}}-\frac{Gu_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{2}\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle\right|\leq 2\mathop{\rm Length}\nolimits(\partial B(p,n)\cap W_{0}),

that goes to
0
for
n\to\infty
by hypothesis. So, the result follows by proving that

\int_{B(p,n)\cap\partial W_{0}}\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{1}\|^{2}}}-\frac{Gu_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{2}\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle

stays bounded away from zero. In particular, it is sufficient to prove that
\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{1}\|^{2}}}-\frac{Gu_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{2}\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle
does not change sign.

Since,
u_{1}-u_{2}>0
in
W_{0}
and
u_{1}=u_{2}
on
\partial W_{0}
, one has
Gu_{1}-Gu_{2}=\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})=\lambda\eta
, where
\eta
(the inner pointing conormal to
\partial W_{0}
in
W_{0}
) orients
\partial W_{0}
and
\lambda
is a strictly positive function (by the boundary maximum priciple). So,
\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{1}\|^{2}}}-\frac{Gu_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{2}\|^{2}}},\nu}\right\rangle
does not change sign if and only if
\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{1}\|^{2}}}-\frac{Gu_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{2}\|^{2}}},Gu_{1}-Gu_{2}}\right\rangle
does not change sign, but in
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
we can see that

\left\langle{\frac{Gu_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{1}\|^{2}}}-\frac{Gu_{2}}{\sqrt{1-\|Gu_{2}\|^{2}}},Gu_{1}-Gu_{2}}\right\rangle\geq 0

and that the equality holds only at points
q\in\partial W_{0}
where
\nabla(u_{1}(q)-u_{2}(q))=0
and this concludes the proof.
∎

.

Some differential inequalities and criteria for univalency in the unit disk
Milutin Obradović, Nikola Tuneski

Abstract
. In this paper, Jack lemma is used for obtaining several differential inequalities over analytic functions that later on, lead to new criteria for univalency in the unit disk.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
30C45

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
denote the class of functions
f
analytic in the open unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|<1\}
and normalized such that
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
, i.e.,

(1)

f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots.

Class
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
consists of univalent (one-on-one and onto) functions, and its subclasses of starlike and convex functions of order
\alpha
,
0\leq\alpha<1
, are defined respectively by

(2)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\alpha)&=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}:{\operatorname{Re\,}}\left[\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right]>\alpha,\,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\right\},\\[5.69054pt]
{\mathcal{K}}(\alpha)&=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}:{\operatorname{Re\,}}\left[1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]>\alpha,\,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\right\}.\end{split}

For
\alpha=0
we receive the classes of starlike and convex functions,
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}\equiv{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(0)
and
{\mathcal{K}}\equiv{\mathcal{K}}(0)
. The class of starlike functions is very wide containing most of the other classes of univalent functions (convex, for example), but not all. One class that is not contained, nor contain, the class
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
is the class of functions with bounded turning

(3)

{\mathcal{R}}=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}:{\operatorname{Re\,}}f^{\prime}(z)>0,\,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\right\}.

The geometrical characterisations of these classes correspond to their names. More details can be found in
[
1
,
4
]
.

In this paper we will obtain several differential inequalities over a function
p
analytic in the unit disk and such that
p(0)=1
, implying that it is a Caratheodory function, i.e., that
{\operatorname{Re\,}}p(z)>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Afterwards, varying the function
p
we will receive new criteria for a function
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
to be in some of the classes of univalent functions defined above. This is a commonly used strategy for obtaining criteria for univalency of a function normalized and analytic in the unit disk. Collection of the most important results in that direction is given in
[
3
]
.

For obtaining the results over the differential inequalities we will make use of the well known Jack lemma (
[
2
]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
\omega
be a nonconstant and analytic function in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
\omega(0)=0
. If
|\omega|
attains its maximum value on the circle
|z|=r
at
z_{0}
, we have
z_{0}\omega^{\prime}(z_{0})=k\omega(z_{0})
,
k\geq 1
.

2.
Main results and consequences

Theorem 1
.

Let
p
be analytic function on the unit dick
{\mathbb{D}}
, normalized such that
p(0)=1
. If

\left|zp^{\prime}(z)+p(z)+p^{2}(z)-2\right|<\frac{5}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
{\operatorname{Re\,}}p(z)>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

Let consider function
\omega
, such that

(4)

p(z)=\frac{1+\omega(z)}{1-\omega(z)}.

It is analytic in the unit disk and
\omega(0)=0
. It is enough to show that
|\omega(z)|<1
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

On a contrary, let assume that
|\omega(z_{0})|=1
for some
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Then, according to the Jack lemma
z\omega^{\prime}(z_{0})=k\omega(z_{0})
for some
k\geq 1
, and further, with
\omega(z_{0})=e^{i\theta}
and
t=\cos\theta
,

\begin{split}\left|z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})+p(z_{0})+p^{2}(z_{0})-2\right|&=\left|\frac{2z_{0}\omega^{\prime}(z_{0})}{[1-\omega(z_{0})]^{2}}+\frac{1+\omega(z_{0})}{1-\omega(z_{0})}+\left[\frac{1+\omega(z_{0})}{1-\omega(z_{0})}\right]^{2}-2\right|\\
&=2\cdot\left|\frac{\omega(z_{0})\left[k+3-\omega(z_{0})\right]}{[1-\omega(z_{0})]^{2}}\right|=2\cdot\frac{\left|k+3-\omega(z_{0})\right|}{|1-\omega(z_{0})|^{2}}\\
&=2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{(k+3)^{2}-2(k+3)\cos\theta+1}}{2-2\cos\theta}\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{(k+3)^{2}-2(k+3)t+1}}{1-t}\equiv\varphi(t,k).\end{split}

Since
k\geq 1
and
-1\leq t\leq 1
, we have that
\varphi_{k}(t,k)=\frac{3+k-t}{(1-t)\sqrt{(k+3)^{2}-2(k+3)t+1}}>0,
and so

\left|z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})+p(z_{0})+p^{2}(z_{0})-2\right|\geq\varphi(t,1)=\frac{\sqrt{17-8t}}{1-t},

which is an increasing function of
t
on the interval
[-1,1]
. Thus

\left|z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})+p(z_{0})+p^{2}(z_{0})-2\right|\geq\varphi(-1,1)=\frac{5}{2}.

This is in contradiction with the condition of the theorem, so
|\omega(z)|<1
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, implying
{\operatorname{Re\,}}p(z)>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.
∎

For a function
f
from
{\mathcal{A}}
, functions
\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
,
1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}
,
f^{\prime}(z)
, and
\frac{f(z)}{z}
, are analytic in the unit disk and map the origin into 1. So, they can be chosen to be
p(z)
in Theorem
1
, and lead to the following results.

Corollary 1
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

(
i
)

If

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\left[2+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]-2\right|<\frac{5}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
.

(
ii
)

If

\left|\frac{z^{2}f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+4\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|<\frac{5}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
f\in{\mathcal{K}}
.

(
iii
)

If

\left|zf^{\prime\prime}(z)+f^{\prime}(z)+f^{\prime 2}(z)-2\right|<\frac{5}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
{\operatorname{Re\,}}f^{\prime}(z)>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

(
iv
)

If

\left|f^{\prime}(z)+\left[\frac{f(z)}{z}\right]^{2}-2\right|<\frac{5}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
{\operatorname{Re\,}}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

In a similar way as Theorem
1
we prove the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p
be analytic function on the unit dick
{\mathbb{D}}
, normalized such that
p(0)=1
. If

\left|zp^{\prime}(z)+p(z)-p^{2}(z)\right|<\frac{1}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
{\operatorname{Re\,}}p(z)>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

With the same assumptions and notations as in the proof of Theorem
1
, and using similar technique, we again receive the conclusion by contradiction with the condition of the theorem:

\begin{split}\left|z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})+p(z_{0})-p^{2}(z_{0})\right|&=\left|\frac{2z_{0}\omega^{\prime}(z_{0})}{[1-\omega(z_{0})]^{2}}+\frac{1+\omega(z_{0})}{1-\omega(z_{0})}-\left[\frac{1+\omega(z_{0})}{1-\omega(z_{0})}\right]^{2}\right|\\
&=2\cdot\left|\frac{\omega(z_{0})\left[k-1-\omega(z_{0})\right]}{[1-\omega(z_{0})]^{2}}\right|\\
&=2\cdot\frac{\left|k-1-\omega(z_{0})\right|}{|1-\omega(z_{0})|^{2}}.\end{split}

The last is greater or equal to
\frac{1}{2}
, with equality for
k=1
and
\omega(z_{0})=-1
.
∎

As previously, by specifying functions
p
in Theorem
2
gives

Corollary 2
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

(
i
)

If

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\left[2-2\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right|<\frac{1}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
.

(
ii
)

If

\left|\frac{z^{2}f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-2\left[\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]^{2}\right|<\frac{1}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
f\in{\mathcal{K}}
.

(
iii
)

If

\left|zf^{\prime\prime}(z)+f^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime 2}(z)\right|<\frac{1}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
{\operatorname{Re\,}}f^{\prime}(z)>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

(
iv
)

If

\left|f^{\prime}(z)-\left[\frac{f(z)}{z}\right]^{2}\right|<\frac{1}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
{\operatorname{Re\,}}\frac{f(z)}{z}>0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

It is easy to verify that for
p(z)=\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
,

zp^{\prime}(z)+p(z)-p^{2}(z)=-zf(z)\left[\frac{z}{f(z)}\right]^{\prime\prime},

and so, Corollary
2
(i) can be rewritten in the following form.

Theorem 3
.

If
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
and

\left|f(z)\left[\frac{z}{f(z)}\right]^{\prime\prime}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
.

Remark 1
.

It is interesting that the condition

\left|f(z)\left[\frac{z}{f(z)}\right]^{\prime}\right|<1\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

which is equivalent to
\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-1\right|<1,

z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, also implies
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
.

If we repeat the proof of Theorem
2
, but instead of (
4
) work with function
\omega
defined by

p(z)=(1-\alpha)\frac{1+\omega(z)}{1-\omega(z)}+\alpha,

we receive

Theorem 4
.

Let
p
be analytic function on the unit dick
{\mathbb{D}}
, normalized such that
p(0)=1
, and let
0\leq\alpha<1
. If

\left|zp^{\prime}(z)+\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}p(z)-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}p^{2}(z)-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right|<\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

then
{\operatorname{Re\,}}p(z)>\alpha
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Remark 2
.

From the previous theorem we can obtain appropriate criteria as those in Corollary
1
and Corollary
2
. For example, for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, we have that

\left|zp^{\prime}(z)+3p(z)-2p^{2}(z)-1\right|<\frac{1}{4}\,\,(z\in{\mathbb{D}})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\operatorname{Re\,}}p(z)>\frac{1}{2}\,\,(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

and for
p(z)=\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
, we receive that

\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\left[4-3\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]-1\right|<\frac{1}{4}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

implies
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(1/2)
.

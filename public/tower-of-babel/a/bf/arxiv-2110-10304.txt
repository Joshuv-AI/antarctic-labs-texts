Operators which preserve a positive definite inner product
E. Andruchow

Abstract
Let ℋ {\cal H} be a Hilbert space, A A a positive definite operator in ℋ {\cal H} and ⟨ f , g ⟩ A = ⟨ A ​ f , g ⟩ \langle f,g\rangle_{A}=\langle Af,g\rangle , f , g ∈ ℋ f,g\in{\cal H} , the A A -inner product. This paper studies the geometry of the set ℐ A a := { adjointable isometries for ⟨ , ⟩ A } . {\cal I}_{A}^{a}:=\{\hbox{ adjointable isometries for }\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{A}\}. It is proved that ℐ A a {\cal I}_{A}^{a} is a submanifold of the Banach algebra of adjointable operators, and a homogeneous space of the group of invertible operators in ℋ {\cal H} , which are unitaries for the A A -inner product. Smooth curves in ℐ A a {\cal I}_{A}^{a} with given initial conditions, which are minimal for the metric induced by ⟨ , ⟩ A \langle\ ,\ \rangle_{A} , are presented. This result depends on an adaptation of M.G. Krein’s extension method of symmetric contractions, in order that it works also for symmetrizable transformations (i.e., operators which are selfadjoint for the A A -inner product).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let

. Denote by
{\bf T}
its (isometric) extension to
{\cal L}
. The following are equivalent:

1.

is
A
-adjointable;

2.

{\bf T}^{*}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
;

3.

is a compatible subspace.

4.

R(T^{*}A)=R(A)
;

5.

there exists

T^{*}A^{2}T\leq\lambda A^{2}.

Proof.

The equivalence between the first two conditions is clear: if
T
has an
A
-adjoint
S
, then
{\bf T}^{*}
and
S
coincide on
{\cal H}
. Then
{\bf T}^{*}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
and
S={\bf T}^{*}|_{\cal H}
. If
{\bf T}^{*}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
, then
{\bf T}^{*}|_{\cal H}
is the
A
-adjoint of

. Note that therefore the final projection

of

leaves

{\bf P}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
. Thus,

induces an (

in
{\cal H}
. Clearly,

, and

:

implies that

; on the other hand,
{\bf T}{\bf T}^{*}{\bf T}={\bf T}
, so that, restricting to

,
TST=PT=T
ans therefore

. Summarizing,

is the range of an
A
-symmetric projection, i.e., a compatible subspace.

Conversely, suppose that
R(T)
is compatible and denote by
P
the unique
A
-symmetric idempotent
with

. In particular,

is closed, and

.
Indeed,

is an isomorphism between Banach spaces,
thus it has a bounded inverse

S

S=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}T^{\prime}\hbox{ in }R(P)=R(T)\\
0\hbox{ in }N(P)\end{array}\right..

Then

equals the identity in

and is zero in

, i.e.,

. Note that

, and that

The equivalence with the last two conditions follows using Douglas’ result:

(i.e.,

, or equivalently, there exists

.

3
Wold decomposition of
A
-adjointable isometries

Recall the Wold decomposition of an isometry (see for instance

]
):

, there exists an orthogonal decomposition

such that

reduce

,
{\bf V}|_{{\cal L}_{0}}
is a unitary operator and
{\bf V}|_{{\cal L}_{1}}
is a shift

{\cal L}_{1}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\bf V}^{n}{\cal L}_{w}
,

{\cal L}_{w}={\cal L}\ominus{\bf V}{\cal L}
is the so called

of
{\bf V}

This decomposition is unique,
{\cal L}_{1}
is determined by
{\bf V}
,
as seen in the above formula; also
{\cal L}_{0}=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}{\bf V}^{n}{\cal L}
.

The next result shows that the Wold decomposition of
{\bf V}
in
{\cal L}
induces an analogous compatible decomposition for
V
in
{\cal H}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
and
{\bf V}
its extension to an isometry of
{\cal L}
, then the Wold decomposition
{\cal L}={\cal L}_{0}\oplus{\cal L}_{1}
of
{\bf V}
induces a (direct sum,
A
-orthogonal) decomposition

{\cal H}={\cal H}_{0}\oplus{\cal H}_{1},\ {\cal H}_{0}={\cal L}_{0}\cap{\cal H},\ {\cal H}_{1}={\cal H}\cap{\cal L}_{1},

of compatible subspaces.
V{\cal H}_{0}={\cal H}_{0}
,
V{\cal H}_{1}\subset{\cal H}_{1}
, so that
V|_{{\cal H}_{0}}
is invertible in
{\cal H}_{0}
.

Proof.

The wandering subspace
{\cal L}_{w}={\cal L}\ominus{\bf V}{\cal L}
is the orthogonal complement of the range of
{\bf V}
, therefore its intersection with
{\cal H}
is the
A
-orthogonal complement
R(V)^{\perp_{A}}
of
R(V)
(which are both compatible subspaces of
{\cal H}
). Note that for

n\geq 1
,

V^{n}(R(V)^{\perp_{A}})={\bf V}^{n}(R(V)^{\perp_{A}})\hbox{ is dense in }{\bf V}^{n}{\cal L}_{w}.

Thus the subspaces
V^{n}(R(V)^{\perp_{A}})
are in direct sum for different
n
, and their sum

\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V^{n}(R(V)^{\perp_{A}})\hbox{ is dense in }\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\bf V}^{n}{\cal L}_{w}.

Note also that since
V^{n}\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
,
V^{n}(R(V)^{\perp_{A}})
is a compatible subspace (in particular, closed). Therefore the
A
-orthogonal sum (and direct sum in
{\cal H}
)
\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V^{n}(R(V)^{\perp_{A}})
is a compatible subspace.

Next,
{\bf V}{\cal L}_{0}={\cal L}_{0}
, and thus
V({\cal H}_{0})\subset{\bf V}{\cal L}_{0}={\cal L}_{0}
, together with
V({\cal H}_{0})\subset{\cal H}
, implies
V({\cal H}_{0})\subset{\cal H}\cap{\cal L}_{0}={\cal H}_{0}
. By the same reason, since
V^{\sharp}={\bf V}^{*}|_{\cal H}
. Also
V^{\sharp}({\cal H}_{0})\subset{\cal H}_{0}
. Moreover,

{\cal H}_{0}=V^{\sharp}V({\cal H}_{0})\subset V^{\sharp}({\cal H}_{0})\subset{\cal H}_{0}.

Since clearly
{\cal H}_{0}\subset R(V)
, then
VV^{\sharp}=P_{V}
(the
A
-symmetric idempotent in
{\cal H}
with range equal to
R(V)
) satisfies
VV^{\sharp}{\cal H}_{0}={\cal H}_{0}
. Then

{\cal H}_{0}=VV^{\sharp}({\cal H}_{0})\subset V({\cal H}_{0})\subset{\cal H}_{0}.

That is,
V({\cal H}_{0})={\cal H}_{0}
, i.e.
V|_{{\cal H}_{0}}
is invertible.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In the above theorem it was shown that
V|_{{\cal H}_{0}}
is invertible in
{\cal H}_{0}
. The operator
A
does not necessarily leave
{\cal H}_{0}
invariant, nevertheless

induces an inner product in
{\cal H}_{0}

.

5
The extension method of M.G. Krein for

symmetrizable operators

In this section the extension method of M.G. Krein
[
11
]
is considered (see also Section 125 of the classic book
[
16
]
for a detailed exposition). Krein shows that if
S:{\cal L}_{0}\subset{\cal L}\to{\cal L}
is a contractive symmetric operator, then there exists a symmetric extension
S:{\cal L}\to{\cal L}
which is also a contraction. Later on, other authors elaborated on this problem (see for instance
[
8
]
were the solutions are parametrized, or the works of Parrott
[
15
]
). In this section, following the ideas of M.G. Krein with slight modifications, it will be shown that an
A
-symmetric operator defined on a
A
-compatible subspace
{\cal H}_{0}\subset{\cal H}
, which is contractive for the norm induced by
\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle_{A}
, can be extended to a contraction for this norm to the whole space
{\cal H}
. Or rather, and equivalently, we shall work in the bigger Hilbert space
{\cal L}
, extending to a symmetric contraction of
{\cal L}
which
leaves
{\cal H}
invariant.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be a selfadjoint operator in
{\cal L}
such that
{\bf X}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
, and let
{\bf P}
be a projection in
{\cal L}
with range
{\cal L}_{0}
, such that
P={\bf P}|_{\cal H}
is an idempotent in
{\cal H}
with range
{\cal H}_{0}
. Suppose that
\|{\bf X}{\bf P}\|=1
. Then there exists a selfadjoint operator
{\bf Z}
in
{\cal L}
such that
{\bf Z}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
,
{\bf Z}{\bf P}={\bf X}{\bf P}
and
\|{\bf Z}\|=1
.

Proof.

For
m>0
, let us denote by
{\bf X}_{m}=\frac{1}{m}{\bf X}
,
m
will be adjusted in the process. Denote by
{\cal H}_{0}={\bf P}({\cal H})
. Let
{\bf B}_{0}={\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}:{\cal L}_{0}\to{\cal L}_{0}
. Then
{\bf B}_{0}
extends to an operator in
{\cal L}
with the same norm:
\bar{{\bf B}}_{0}={\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}:{\cal L}\to{\cal L}_{0}
. Note that
\bar{{\bf B}}_{0}
leaves
{\cal H}
invariant, because
{\bf X}_{m}
and
{\bf P}
do.

Next extend
{\bf B}_{1}:={\bf P}^{\perp}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}:{\cal L}_{0}\to{\cal L}_{0}^{\perp}
to the whole space
{\cal L}
(not enlarging the norm of
{\bf B}_{1}
and leaving
{\cal H}
invariant). Consider the inner product

[\varphi,\gamma]:=\langle\varphi,\gamma\rangle_{\cal L}-\langle\bar{{\bf B}}_{0}\varphi,\bar{{\bf B}}_{0}\gamma\rangle_{\cal L}.

Note that

[\varphi,\gamma]:=\langle\varphi,\gamma\rangle_{\cal L}-\langle{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}\varphi,{\bf P}{\bf X}\gamma\rangle_{\cal L}=\langle(I-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})\varphi,\gamma\rangle_{\cal L}.

Take
m>0
so that
\|{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}\|<1
, then
I-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}
is (positive and) invertible. Then
({\cal L},[\ \ ,\ \ ])
is a Hilbert space, whose norm is equivalent to the original norm induced by
\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle_{\cal L}
. The operator
{\bf B}_{1}
is bounded by (for
\varphi={\bf P}\varphi\in{\cal L}_{0}
)

\|{\bf B}_{1}\varphi\|^{2}=\langle{\bf P}^{\perp}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}\varphi,{\bf P}^{\perp}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}=\langle{\bf X}_{m}(1-{\bf P}){\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}\varphi,{\bf P}\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}

=\langle{\bf X}_{m}^{2}{\bf P}\varphi,{\bf P}\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}-\langle{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}\varphi,{\bf P}\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}.

Since
\|{\bf X}{\bf P}\|=\frac{1}{m}
, the first term is bounded by

\langle{\bf X}_{m}^{2}{\bf P}\varphi,{\bf P}\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}=\langle{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}^{2}{\bf P}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}\leq\|{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}^{2}{\bf P}\|\|\varphi\|^{2}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\|\varphi\|^{2}.

Pick
m
so that
m\geq 1
, one has that

\|{\bf B}_{1}\varphi\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}}\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}-\langle{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}}\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\langle{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\cal L}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}[\varphi,\varphi].

Therefore
{\bf B}_{1}
induces an operator
{\cal B}:({\cal L}_{0},[\ \ ,\ \ ])\to({\cal L}_{0}^{\perp},\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle_{\cal L})
with norm less than or equal to
\frac{1}{m}
. Since at the set level, this mapping coincides with
{\bf B}_{1}
,
{\cal B}
maps
{\cal H}_{0}
into
{\cal L}_{0}^{\perp}\cap{\cal H}
. Let
\Pi:({\cal L},[\ \ ,\ \ ])\to({\cal L}_{0},[\ \ ,\ \ ])
denote the
[\ \ ,\ \ ]
-orthogonal projection. Since
[\ \ ,\ \ ]=\langle(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})\ \ ,\ \ \rangle_{\cal L}
, the
[\ \ ,\ \ ]
adjoint of an operator
{\cal T}
acting in

is given by
{\cal T}^{\sharp}=(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\cal T}^{*}{\bf X}_{m})^{-1}{\cal T}^{*}(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\cal T}^{*}{\bf X}_{m})
, with

the
\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle_{\cal L}
- adjoint of
{\cal T}
. It is known (see for instance
[

]
), that the orthogonal projection onto the range of an idempotent

P_{R(Q)}=Q(Q+Q^{*}-1)^{-1}.

In our case, this implies that

\Pi={\bf P}\{{\bf P}+(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})^{-1}{\bf P}(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})-1\}^{-1}.

If one further adjusts

. To shorten the writing, denote by

({\bf P}+Q_{m}-1)^{2}=1-({\bf P}-Q_{m})^{2},

(5)

J
is given by

\langle\varphi,J^{*}\eta\rangle_{\cal L}=[J\varphi,\eta]=\langle(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})\varphi,\eta\rangle_{\cal L}=\langle\varphi,(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})\eta\rangle_{\cal L},

i.e.,
J^{*}:({\cal L},[\ \ ,\ \ ])\to({\cal L},\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle_{\cal L})
is
J^{*}=(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})
.
The adjoint of

{\cal B}:({\cal L}_{0},[\ \ ,\ \ ])\to({\cal L}_{0}^{\perp},\langle\ \ ,\ \ \rangle_{\cal L})

is given by

[{\cal B}^{*}\varphi,\eta]=\langle\varphi,{\cal B}\eta\rangle_{\cal L}=\langle\varphi,{\bf P}^{\perp}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}\eta\rangle_{\cal L}=\langle(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})^{-1}(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})\varphi,{\bf P}^{\perp}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}\eta\rangle_{\cal L}

=[\varphi,(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})^{-1}{\bf P}^{\perp}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}\eta],

i.e.,
{\cal B}^{*}=(1-{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}{\bf X}_{m})^{-1}{\bf P}^{\perp}{\bf X}_{m}{\bf P}
, which leaves
{\cal H}
invariant for the appropriate election of
m
.

It follows that
{\bf Z}_{m}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
. Finally,
m
is disposed of:

{\bf Z}=m{\bf Z}_{m}
is selfadjoint, satisfies
{\bf Z}{\bf P}={\bf X}{\bf P}
,
{\bf Z}
leaves
{\cal H}
invariant, and

1=\|{\bf Z}{\bf P}\|\leq\|{\bf Z}\|\leq 1,

i.e.
\|{\bf Z}\|=1
.
∎

6
Minimality of curves in
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
with given initial conditions

In
[
2
]
, a metric was introduced in the set of isometries of a Hilbert space. If
{\cal I}
denotes the set of isometries in
{\cal L}
, a natural metric in
{\cal I}
is given by

d(V,W)=\inf\{\ell(\gamma):\gamma\hbox{ is a smooth curve in }{\cal I}\hbox{ joining }V\hbox{ and }W\},

where

\ell(\gamma)=\int_{I}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|dt.

Fix
V\in{\cal I}
. A vector
{\cal V}
tangent to
{\cal I}
at
V
is the velocity vector
\dot{\gamma}(0)
of a curve
\gamma\subset{\cal I}
with
\gamma(0)=V
. A smooth curve
\gamma
in
{\cal I}
can be lifted to smooth curve of unitary operators in
{\cal L}
:
\gamma(t)=\Gamma(t)V
for
\Gamma
in
{\cal U}({\cal L})
. It follows that
{\cal V}=iXV
, for
X^{*}=X\in{\cal B}({\cal L})
. The main result established in
[
2
]
(Theorem 2.2) states the following:

Suppose that
\|{\cal V}\|=1
, and let
P=VV^{*}
be the final projection of
V
. If
Z^{*}=Z
satisfies that
\|Z\|=1
and
ZP={\cal V}P
, then the curve

\delta(t)=e^{itZ}V

has minimal length along its path for
|t|\leq\pi
, among all smooth curves in
{\cal I}
which join the same endpoints as
\delta
.

Define the metric in
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
, by considering the norm of
{\cal B}({\cal L})
at every tangent space: if
T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
and
{\cal V}\in(T{\cal I}_{A}^{a})_{T}
, then

|{\cal V}|=\|\boldsymbol{{\cal V}}\|,

where
\boldsymbol{{\cal V}}
is the extension of
{\cal V}
to
{\cal L}
.
Formally, since one has considered the regular structure of
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
regarding it as a submanifold of
{\cal B}_{A}({\cal H})
, one is introducing a (constant) distribution of norms in the tangent bundle, consisting of the norm defined by the
A
-inner product. Or, if one does not care about the regular structure of
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
, one just regards
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
as a subset of the metric space
({\cal I},d)
.

A direct consequence of the result cited above and Lemma
5.1
is the following:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
T\in{\cal I}^{a}_{A}
, and
{\cal V}\in(T{\cal I}_{A}^{a})_{T}
with
|{\cal V}|=1
. Then there exists a symmetrizable operator
Z\in{\cal B}_{A}({\cal H})
such that the curve

\delta(t)=e^{it{\bf Z}}{\bf T}|_{\cal H}=e^{itZ}T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}

satisfies that
\delta(0)=T
,
\dot{\delta}(0)={\cal V}
and
\delta
has minimal length along its path for
|t|\leq\pi
, among all smooth curves in
{\cal I}^{a}_{A}
which join the same endpoints as
\delta
. In fact,
\delta
is minimal also in the bigger manifold
{\cal I}
.

Proof.

The tangent vector
{\cal V}
is of the form
{\cal V}=iXT
, for some
X\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
symmetrizable operator with
|{\cal V}|=\|{\bf X}{\bf T}\|=1
. We claim that there exists
{\bf Z}^{*}={\bf Z}
with
\|{\bf Z}\|=1
,
{\bf Z}{\bf T}={\bf X}{\bf T}
and
{\bf Z}({\cal H})\subset{\cal H}
. Indeed, note that
{\bf Z}{\bf T}={\bf X}{\bf T}
if and only if
{\bf Z}{\bf P}={\bf X}{\bf P}
, for
{\bf P}
the final projection of
{\bf T}
:

{\bf Z}{\bf T}={\bf X}{\bf T}\Longrightarrow{\bf Z}{\bf P}={\bf Z}{\bf T}{\bf T}^{*}={\bf X}{\bf T}{\bf T}^{*}={\bf X}{\bf P}\Longrightarrow{\bf Z}{\bf P}{\bf T}={\bf Z}{\bf T}={\bf X}{\bf P}{\bf T}={\bf X}{\bf P}.

Also note that

\|{\bf X}{\bf P}\|=\|{\bf X}{\bf T}{\bf T}^{*}\|\leq\|{\bf X}{\bf T}\|=\|{\bf X}{\bf T}{\bf T}^{*}{\bf T}\|\leq\|{\bf X}{\bf T}{\bf T}^{*}\|=\|{\bf X}{\bf P}\|,

i.e.
\|{\bf X}{\bf P}\|=1
.
If
Z={\bf Z}|_{\cal H}
, then
\delta(t)=e^{itZ}T
has minimal length along its path, due to Theorem 2.2 in
[
2
]
. Clearly
\dot{\delta}(0)=ZT=XT={\cal V}
.
∎

7
Components of
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
and the action of the restricted group

The connected components of the set of usual isometries in a Hilbert space are parametrized by the co-rank of the isometries: two isometries lie in the same connected component (i.e., are conjugate by the left action of the unitary group) if and only if they have the same co-rank
0\leq n\leq+\infty
. This fact follows easily and is a consequence of the connectedness of the unitary group of a Hilbert space. We do not know if the group
{\cal U}_{A}
is connected (or how the properties of the operator
A
determine the components of
{\cal U}_{A}
): we believe that it is an interesting problem. What does hold in general, both for usual or
A
-isometries, is that two isometries are conjugate by the group action if and only if their final projections are conjugate. The argument is essentially contained in the discussion before Theorem
4.3
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
T_{1},T_{2}\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
, with final
A
-orthogonal idempotents
P_{1}=T_{1}T_{1}^{\sharp}
,
P_{2}=T_{2}T_{2}^{\sharp}
.

1.

There exists
G\in{\cal U}_{A}
such that
GT_{1}=T_{2}
if and only if there exists
H\in{\cal U}_{A}
such that
HP_{1}H^{-1}=P_{2}
.

2.

T_{1}
and
T_{2}
lie in the same connected component of
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
if and only if
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
lie in the same component of
{\cal P}_{A}
.

Proof.

If there exists
G\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
such that
GT_{1}=T_{2}
, then

P_{2}=T_{2}T_{2}^{\sharp}=GT_{1}(GT_{1})^{\sharp}=GT_{1}T_{1}^{\sharp}G^{\sharp}=GP_{1}G^{-1}.

Conversely, if there exists
H\in{\cal U}_{A}
such that
HP_{1}H^{-1}=P_{2}
, then
T_{1}^{\prime}=HT_{1}
and
T_{2}
are
A
-isometries with the same final space
P_{2}
. Consider the operator
K
given by
K=T_{2}(T_{1}^{\prime})^{\sharp}+1-P_{2}
. Note that

KK^{\sharp}=(T_{2}(T_{1}^{\prime})^{\sharp}+1-P_{2})(T_{1}^{\prime}T_{2}^{\sharp}+1-P_{2})=T_{2}(T_{1}^{\prime})^{\sharp}T_{1}^{\prime}T_{2}^{\sharp}+1-P_{2},

because
T_{2}(T_{1}^{\prime})^{\sharp}(1-P_{2})=0=(1-P_{2})T_{1}^{\prime}T_{2}^{\sharp}
. Since

T_{2}(T_{1}^{\prime})^{\sharp}T_{1}^{\prime}T_{2}^{\sharp}=T_{2}T_{2}^{\sharp}=P_{2},

then
KK^{\sharp}=1
. Similarly
K^{\sharp}K=1
, i.e.
K\in{\cal U}_{A}
. Moreover

KT_{1}^{\prime}=(T_{2}(T_{1}^{\prime})^{\sharp}+1-P_{2})T_{1}^{\prime}=T_{2}(T_{1}^{\prime})^{\sharp}T_{1}=T_{2}.

Therefore,
T_{2}=KT_{1}^{\prime}=KHT_{1}
, with
KH\in{\cal U}_{A}
.

In order to prove part 2., one uses the fact that both

\pi_{T_{1}}:{\cal U}_{A}\to\{GT_{1}:G\in{\cal U}_{A}\},\ \pi_{T_{1}}(G)=GT_{1}

and

\pi_{P_{1}}:{\cal U}_{A}\to\{GP_{1}G^{-1}:G\in{\cal U}_{A}\},\ \pi_{P_{1}}(G)=GP_{1}G^{-1}

C
∞
submersions, and that the orbits
\{GT_{1}:G\in{\cal U}_{A}\}
and
\{GP_{1}G^{-1}:G\in{\cal U}_{A}\}
are unions of connected components of
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
and
{\cal P}_{A}
, respectively. Therefore a continuous path
T(t)\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
with
T(0)=T_{1}
and
T(1)=T_{2}
lifts to a continuous path
G(t)\in{\cal U}_{A}
, i.e.,
T(t)=G(t)T_{1}
. Then
P(t)=G(t)P_{1}G^{-1}(t)
is a continuous path in
{\cal P}_{A}
joining
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.

Similarly, if
P(t)
is a continuous path in
{\cal P}_{A}
, there exists a continuous path
H(t)\in{\cal U}_{A}
such that
P(t)=H(t)P_{1}H^{-1}(t)
. Then as in the proof of part 1.,

K(t)=T_{2}(H(t)T_{1})^{\sharp}+1-P_{2}\in{\cal U}_{A}

is a continuous path, and
T(t)=K(t)H(t)T_{1}
is a continuous path in
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
, joining
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.
∎

In the remaining of this section, the action of the restricted group

{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}:=\{G\in{\cal U}_{A}:G-1\hbox{ is compact}\}

on
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
is considered. In
[
3
]
it was shown that
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
is a
C^{\infty}
-Banach-Lie group, whose Banach-Lie group is

\mathfrak{u}_{A}^{\infty}=\{iX\in{\cal B}_{A}({\cal H}):X\hbox{ is symmetrizable and compact}\}.

Denote by
{\cal K}({\cal H})
the space of compact operators in
{\cal H}
. In
[
3
]
it was also proved that the usual exponential map
\exp(iX)=e^{iX}
,

\exp:\mathfrak{u}_{A}^{\infty}\to{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}

is surjective. This implies, in particular, that
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
is connected. Let us consider the action of the restricted group
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
on
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
. The first result characterizes the orbits of the restricted action. Note that these orbits are connected.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
T_{0}\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
. Then

\{GT_{0}:G\in{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}\}=\{T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}:T-T_{0}\in{\cal K}({\cal H})\}.

Proof.

If
T=GT_{0}
for
G\in{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
, then clearly
T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
and

T-T_{0}=GT_{0}-T_{0}=(G-1)T_{0}\in{\cal K}({\cal H}).

Conversely, suppose that
T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
satisfies that
T-T_{0}
is compact. Then, by a theorem of I.C. Gohberg and M.I. Zambickii
[
10
]
(see Remark
2.2
)
the extension
{\bf T}-{\bf T}_{0}
is compact in
{\cal L}
.
Then
{\bf T}^{*}({\bf T}-{\bf T}_{0})=1-{\bf T}^{*}{\bf T}_{0}
is compact, i.e.
{\bf T}^{*}{\bf T}_{0}
is a Fredholm operator in
{\cal L}
with zero index. Then, since
{\bf T}
is an isometric isomorphism between
{\cal L}
and
R({\bf T})=R({\bf T}{\bf T}^{*})
, and
{\bf T}_{0}^{*}
is an isometric isomorphism between
R({\bf T}_{0})=R({\bf T}_{0}{\bf T}_{0}^{*})
and
{\cal L}
, it follows that

{\bf T}{\bf T}^{*}{\bf T}_{0}{\bf T}_{0}^{*}:R({\bf T}_{0}{\bf T}_{0}^{*})\to R({\bf T}{\bf T}^{*})

is a Fredholm operator of zero index. In
[
4
]
(Theorem 6.3) it was shown this implies that
P_{T}=TT^{\sharp}
and
P_{T_{0}}=T_{0}T_{0}^{\sharp}
lie in the same connected component (same orbit) of the restricted Grassmannian: there exists
G\in{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
such that
GP_{T_{0}}G^{-1}=P_{T}
. As in Proposition
7.1
,
let
K=T(GT_{0})^{\sharp}+1-P_{T}\in{\cal U}_{A}
, which satisfies
KGT_{0}=T
. We claim that
K\in{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
. Indeed,

K-1=T(GT_{0})^{\sharp}-P_{T}=TT_{0}^{\sharp}G^{-1}-GT_{0}T_{0}^{\sharp}G^{-1}=(T-GT_{0})T_{0}^{\sharp}G^{-1},

and since
G\in{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
is of the form
G=1+C
with
C\in{\cal K}({\cal H})
,

T-GT_{0}=T-(1+C)T_{0}=T-T_{0}-CT_{0}\in{\cal K}({\cal H}).

∎

With a similar argument as the one used to prove that
{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
has differentiable structure, it can be shown that the orbit of
T_{0}
under the action on
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
is a
C^{\infty}
-manifold and a homogeneous space of this group.

Proposition 7.3
.

Given a fixed
T_{0}\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}
, the set
\{T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}:T-T_{0}\in{\cal K}({\cal H})\}
is a complemented
C^{\infty}
-submanifold of the affine space
T_{0}+{\cal B}_{A}({\cal H})\cap{\cal K}({\cal H})
, and the onto map

\pi^{\infty}_{T_{0}}:{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}\to\{T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}:T-T_{0}\in{\cal K}({\cal H})\},\ \pi^{\infty}_{T_{0}}(G)=GT_{0}

is a
C^{\infty}
-submersion

Proof.

We shall use Lemma
4.2
. Consider the Banach algebra
{\cal B}=\mathbb{C}1+{\cal B}_{A}({\cal H})\cap{\cal K}({\cal H})
, with the norm
|B|=\max\{\|B\|,\|B\|^{\sharp}\}
. The group
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
is a subgroup of the Banach-Lie group of invertible operators in
{\cal B}
. The element

K=T(GT_{0})^{\sharp}+1-P_{T}\in{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}

defines a map - in fact a cross section - on a neighbourhood of
T_{0}
in
\{T\in{\cal I}_{A}^{a}:T-T_{0}\in{\cal K}({\cal H})\}
, with values in
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
. Indeed, the operator
G
is also a
C^{\infty}
map in the argument
T
(because of the regular structure of the restricted orbit of
P_{T_{0}}
under the action of
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}

[
4
]
). Note that the map
T\mapsto P_{T}=TT^{\sharp}
is
C^{\infty}
, it is the restriction (to the orbit of
T_{0}
) of the global
C^{\infty}
map
B\mapsto BB^{\sharp}
of the ambient algebra
{\cal B}
. Clearly, then, the local cross section
T\mapsto KG
extends to a
C^{\infty}
map defined on a neighbourhood of
T_{0}
in
{\cal B}
.

The space on which
{\cal U}_{A}^{\infty}
acts by left multiplication, namely
\{X\in{\cal B}_{A}({\cal H}):X-T_{0}\in{\cal K}({\cal H})\}
, is not a Banach space. It is the
affine
Banach space
T_{0}+{\cal B}_{A}({\cal H})\cap{\cal K}({\cal H})
. Therefore the argument proceeds, with slight modifications.
∎

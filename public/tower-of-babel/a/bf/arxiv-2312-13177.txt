Lemma 5.1
.

f_{i}^{\prime}f_{j}^{\prime}=f_{j}^{\prime}f_{i}^{\prime}=f_{k}^{\prime}
where
(i,j,k)
is any permutation of
(1,2,3)
. Now we can use Lemma
5.7
to complete the proof. For simplicity, we will only show it for the case that
(f_{i}^{\prime})^{2}
is isotopic to
f_{0}^{\prime}=id
, and the left cases can be similarly proved.
Since
(f_{i}^{\prime})^{2}\mid_{N}=Id\mid_{N}
, one can automatically check that

(f_{i}^{\prime})^{2}\mid_{V}
is isotopic to
f_{0}^{\prime}\mid_{V}=id_{V}
. Then due to Lemma
5.7
,

(f_{i}^{\prime})^{2}
and
f_{0}^{\prime}=id
are isotopic on
M_{k}
.
∎

Now we are prepared enough to prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
5.8
, similar to the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we can build a surjective
homomorphism
\varphi:{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\to{\mathcal{F}}
by
\varphi((0,0))=[f_{0}]
,

\varphi((1,0))=[f_{1}]
,
\varphi((1,1))=[f_{2}]
and
\varphi((0,1))=[f_{3}]
where
{\mathcal{F}}=\{[f_{0}],[f_{1}],[f_{2}],[f_{3}]\}
. Further by Lemma
5.5
, we know that
\varphi

is also injective. Therefore
\varphi:{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\to{\mathcal{F}}
is an isomorphism.
Furthermore, by Lemma
5.3
, each self homeomorphism
f
on
M_{k}
is
isotopic to some
f_{i}
(
i=0,1,2,3
). Then following the above discussions, one can easily get that
\mbox{Mod}(M_{k})={\mathcal{F}}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

By Lemma
5.4
, in each isotopy class of
f_{i}
(
i=0,1,2,3
), there exists a self orbit equivalence of
X_{t}
(
f_{i}
if
i=0,2
and
F_{i}=\eta f_{i}
if
i=1,3
) in this class. This means that every element in
\mbox{Mod}(M_{k})
can be represented by a self orbit equivalence of
X_{t}
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.
∎

An asymptotic approximation for the Riemann zeta function revisited
R. B. Paris

Abstract
We revisit a representation for the Riemann zeta function ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) expressed in terms of normalised incomplete gamma functions given by the author and S. Cang in Methods Appl. Anal. 4 (1997) 449–470. Use of the uniform asymptotics of the incomplete gamma function produces an asymptotic-like expansion for ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) on the critical line s = 1 / 2 + i ​ t s=1/2+it as t → + ∞ t\to+\infty . The main term involves the original Dirichlet series smoothed by a complementary error function of appropriate argument together with a series of correction terms. It is the aim here to present these correction terms in a more user-friendly format by expressing then in inverse powers of ω \omega , where ω 2 = π ​ s / ( 2 ​ i ) \omega^{2}=\pi s/(2i) , multiplied by coefficients involving trigonometric functions of argument ω \omega . Mathematics subject classification (2010): 11M06, 33B20, 34E05, 41A60 Keywords: Riemann zeta function, critical line, asymptotic expansion, incomplete gamma function, modified complementary error function



1. Introduction

The computation of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line
s=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+it
,
t\geq 0
is normally carried out using the real function
Z(t)=e^{i\vartheta(t)}\zeta(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+it)
, where the phase angle

\vartheta(t)=\arg\,\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}it)-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}t\log\,\pi.

In
[
3
]
, a representation of
Z(t)
on the critical line was given in the form

Z(t)=2\Re e^{i\vartheta(t)}\biggl\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}Q(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\pi n^{2}i)-\frac{\pi^{s/2}e^{\pi is/4}}{s\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s)}\biggr\},

(1.1)

wherein
s=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+it
,
t\geq 0
and

Q(a,z)=\Gamma(a,z)/\Gamma(a)
is the normalised (upper) incomplete gamma function. The behaviour of
Q(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\pi n^{2}i)
for large
t
changes abruptly in the neighbourhood of its transition point
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s=\pi n^{2}i
; that is, when the summation index
n
roughly equals the Riemann-Siegel cut-off value
N_{t}
given by

N_{t}=[(t/2\pi)^{1/2}],

(1.2)

where square brackets denote the integer part. Then for large
t
, the function
|Q(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\pi n^{2}i)|\simeq 1
when
n\,\raisebox{-3.44444pt}{\mbox{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\sim}}$}}\,N_{t}
and decays (algebraically) to zero when
n\,\raisebox{-3.44444pt}{\mbox{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle>}}{{\sim}}$}}\,N_{t}
. As a consequence, the absolutely convergent sum
1

1.1

\Gamma(a,z)\sim z^{a-1}e^{-z}

|z|\to\infty

(-)^{n}/n^{2}

The uniform asymptotic expansion of
Q(a,z)
valid for
a\to\infty
when
|z|\in[0,\infty)

|\arg\,z|<\pi
and
|\arg\,(z/a)|<2\pi
was employed in
[

to derive an asymptotic approximation for

valid as

\omega

In

The purpose of the present paper is to revisit the expansion (

2. The asymptotic approximation for
Z(t)

To make this paper self-contained, we summarise in this section the main steps in the derivation of the asymptotic approximation derived from (
1.1
) and presented in
[
3
]
.
We employ the uniform asymptotic expansion of the normalised incomplete gamma function valid as
a\to\infty
in
|\arg\,a|<\pi
in the form
[
1

Q(a,z)=\frac{1}{2}\mbox{erfc}\,(\eta\sqrt{a/2})+\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}a\eta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}\biggl\{\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}c_{k}(\eta)a^{-k}+a^{-m}R_{m}(a,\eta)\biggr\}

The modified complementary error function is introduced by

Q(a,z)=\frac{1}{2}\mbox{erfc}\,(\eta\sqrt{a/2};m)+\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}a\eta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}\biggl\{\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-)^{k}W_{k}(\mu)}{\mu^{2k+1}}a^{-k}+a^{-m}R_{m}(a,\eta)\biggr\}.

(2.5)

We now substitute the expansion (
2.5
) into (
1.1

(2.6)

We also make use of the well-known expansion

\gamma_{r}

defined by

S_{k}(\omega):=2^{k-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{n}\omega^{k}}{(\omega^{2}-(\pi n)^{2})^{k}}\qquad k=1,2,\ldots

A_{r}(s)=2^{-2r}\omega^{2r+1}B_{r}(\omega),\qquad B_{r}(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{2r}(-)^{k}\alpha_{r,k}\,\frac{S_{2r+1-k}(\omega)}{(\omega/2)^{k}}.

(2.9)

4. Numerical results

In this section we describe computations using the expansion (
2.10
) with the correction term
T_{m}(\omega)
given in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). The terms in the main sum in (
2.10
) decay rapidly beyond
n\simeq N_{t}
. In the neighbourhood of the transition point of
Q(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\pi n^{2}i)
given by
\lambda_{n}=1
, we have

\eta_{n}\simeq\lambda_{n}-1=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\biggl(n^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\biggr)\simeq\frac{2\pi i}{s}\biggl(n^{2}-\frac{t}{2\pi}\biggr),

so that the argument of the modified complementary error function in (
2.10
) for large
t
when
n\simeq N_{t}
is

\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\eta_{n}\sqrt{s}\simeq\pi t^{-1/2}e^{\pi i/4}(n^{2}-N_{t}^{2})\simeq 2\pi t^{-1/2}e^{\pi i/4}N_{t}(n-N_{t})

\simeq\sqrt{2\pi i}\,(n-N_{t}).

From the asymptotic behaviour

\mbox{erfc}\,(z;m)\sim\frac{e^{-z^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{(-)^{m}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{m}}{z^{2m+1}}\qquad(|z|\to\infty,\ \ |\arg\,z|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}$}\pi),

it is then found that when
n=N_{t}+K
, where integer
K\ll N_{t}
, the magnitude of the terms in the main sum is given approximately by

|n^{-s}\mbox{erfc}\,(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\eta_{n}\sqrt{s};m)|=(4\pi K^{2})^{-m-1/2}O((t/2\pi)^{-1/4}).

The decay of the terms is therefore controlled by
K^{-2m-1/2}
together with a scaling factor depending weakly on
t
like
t^{-1/4}
. Thus if
K=10
, for example, the magnitude of the terms in the main sum is approximately of order

10^{-14}t^{-1/4}
when
m=4
.

5. Concluding remarks

We have revisited an expansion derived in
[
3
]
for
Z(t)
on the critical line
s=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+it
as
t\to+\infty
in
which the main sum is the original Dirichlet series smoothed by a complementary error function. The correction term in this expansion has been expressed as a series in inverse powers of
\omega
, where
\omega^{2}=\pi s/(2i)
, multiplied by coefficients involving
\csc\omega
and its derivatives. Numerical results are presented to illustrate the accuracy achievable with this expansion.

A difficulty arises in the use of the expansion (
2.10
) when
(t/2\pi)^{1/2}
is close to an integer i.e., at a discontinuity in
N_{t}
. This arises because

\frac{\omega}{\pi}=\biggl(\frac{t}{2\pi}\biggr)^{\!1/2}\biggl(1-\frac{i}{2t}\biggr)^{\!1/2},

so that
\csc\omega
and
\cot\omega
become large, but never singular as
\omega
always has a small imaginary part (that decreases with increasing
t
). This results in the term in the main sum and correction term corresponding to
\eta_{n}\simeq 0
becoming large. A means of overcoming this problem, by removing and combining these terms, is given in
[
3
, Section 5]
.

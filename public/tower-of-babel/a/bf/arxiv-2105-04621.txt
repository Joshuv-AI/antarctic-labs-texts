Joint k k -numerical ranges of operators
Jor-Ting Chan, Chi-Kwong Li, Yiu-Tung Poon

Abstract
Let ℋ {\mathcal{H}} be a complex Hilbert space and ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ) {\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}) the algebra of all bounded linear operators on ℋ {\mathcal{H}} . For a positive integer k k less than the dimension of ℋ {\mathcal{H}} and 𝐀 = ( A 1 , … , A m ) ∈ ℬ ​ ( ℋ ) m {\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})^{m} , the joint k k -numerical range W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) is the set of ( α 1 , … , α m ) ∈ ℂ m (\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m} such that α i = ∑ j = 1 k ⟨ A i ​ x j , x j ⟩ \alpha_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle A_{i}x_{j},x_{j}\rangle for an orthonormal set { x 1 , … , x k } \{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\} in ℋ {\mathcal{H}} . The geometrical properties of W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) and their relations with the algebraic properties of { A 1 , … , A m } \{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\} are investigated in this paper. First, conditions for W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) to be convex are studied, and some results for finite dimensional operators are extended to the infinite dimensional case. An example of 𝐀 {\mathbf{A}} is constructed such that W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) is not convex, but W r ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{r}({\mathbf{A}}) is convex for all positive integer r r not equal to k k . Second, descriptions are given for the closure of W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) and the closure of 𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐯 ​ W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) {\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) in terms of the joint essential numerical range of 𝐀 {\mathbf{A}} for infinite dimensional operators A 1 , … , A m A_{1},\dots,A_{m} . These lead to characterizations of W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) or 𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐯 ​ W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) {\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) to be closed. Moreover, it is shown that 𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐯 ​ W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) {\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) is closed whenever W k + 1 ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}}) or conv ​ W k + 1 ​ ( 𝐀 ) {\rm conv}W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}}) is. These results are used to study the connection between the geometric properties of W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) and algebraic properties of A 1 , … , A m A_{1},\dots,A_{m} . For instance, W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) is a polyhedral set, i.e., the convex hull of a finite set, if and only if A 1 , … , A k A_{1},\dots,A_{k} have a common reducing subspace 𝐕 {\mathbf{V}} of finite dimension such that the compression of A 1 , … , A m A_{1},\dots,A_{m} on the subspace 𝐕 {\mathbf{V}} are diagonal operators D 1 , … , D m D_{1},\dots,D_{m} and W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) = W k ​ ( D 1 , … , D m ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}(D_{1},\dots,D_{m}) . Characterization is also given to 𝐀 {\bf A} such that the closure of W k ​ ( 𝐀 ) W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}) is polyhedral. For finite rank operators the following two condition are equivalent: (1) { A 1 , … , A m } \{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\} is a commuting family of normal operators. (2) W k ​ ( A 1 , … , A m ) W_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m}) is polyhedral for every positive integer k k less than dim ℋ \dim{\mathcal{H}} . Results are also obtained for general non-compact operators. Open problems and future research topics are presented.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex Hilbert space
equipped with the inner product
{\langle}x,y{\rangle}
, and let
{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}

be the algebra of all bounded linear operators
on
\mathcal{H}
. If
\dim{\mathcal{H}}=n
is finite, we identify

{\mathcal{H}}
with
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
with
M_{n}
, and
{\langle}x,y{\rangle}=y^{*}x
.
The numerical range of
A\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
is defined as

W(A)=\{\langle Ax,x\rangle:\mbox{$x$ is a unit vector in
${\mathcal{H}}$}\},

which is a useful tool for studying operators
and matrices; see
[
15
,
16
]
and its references.
For instance, for
A\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
,
W(A)=\{\mu\}
if and only if
A=\mu I
;

A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
if and only if
A=A^{*}
; if
A
is normal then

{\bf cl}\,(W(A))={\bf conv}\,\sigma(A)
, the convex hull of the spectrum
\sigma(A)
of
A
.
Here and in the sequel,
we shall write
{\bf cl}\,X
and
{\bf conv}\,X

to denote the closure and convex hull of
X
respectively, for any subset
X
of a normed space.

Motivated by theory and applications, researchers consider different
generalizations of the classical numerical range.
For instance, for a positive integer
k\leq\dim{\mathcal{H}}
, Halmos
[
15
]

introduced the
k
-numerical range of
A\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
defined by

W_{k}(A)=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\langle}Ax_{j},x_{j}{\rangle}:\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\hbox{ is an orthonormal set}\right\}.

Evidently,
W_{1}(A)
reduces to the classical numerical range
W(A)
.
Researchers have extended the results on
W(A)
to
W_{k}(A)
, and showed
that
W_{k}(A)
can provide additional information about the operator;
see later sections,
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
24
,
26
,
27
]
and their references.
For instance, it is known that if
A\in M_{n}
is normal, then
W(A)
is a
polygon equal to
{\bf conv}\,\sigma(A)
. But the converse is not true.
Nevertheless, it was shown in
[
24
]
that
A\in M_{n}
is normal
if and only if
W_{k}(A)
is a polygon for some
k
with
|n-k/2|\leq 1
.

In the study of multiple operators

A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
, researchers consider the joint
k
-numerical range of

{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
defined and denoted by

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=\left\{\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\langle A_{1}x_{\ell},x_{\ell}\rangle,\dots,\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\langle A_{m}x_{\ell},x_{\ell}\rangle\right):\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\hbox{ is an orthonormal set}\right\}

for
k\leq\dim{\mathcal{H}}
. When
k=1
, we use the notation

W({\mathbf{A}})
. For simplicity, we often write
{\langle}{\mathbf{A}}x,x{\rangle}=({\langle}A_{1}x,x{\rangle},\dots,{\langle}A_{m}x,x{\rangle})
for
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Then elements of

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
are of the form
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{\langle}{\mathbf{A}}x_{\ell},x_{\ell}{\rangle}
for an
orthonormal set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
.
One may see some background for
W({\mathbf{A}})
in
[
2
,
4
,
6
,
21
,
23
,
17
,
29
]

and their references. Useful
information about
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
can be obtained from
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.
For instance, it was shown in
[
23
]
that

\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}\subseteq M_{n}
is a commuting family
of normal matrices if and only if there is
k
with
|n/2-k|\leq 1

such that
W_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m}))
is a polyhedral set, i.e., the
convex hull of a finite set in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
.

In this paper, we obtain additional results
connecting the geometrical properties of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
and the
algebraic properties of
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
as well as showing that
direct extensions of some
results on
W_{k}(A)
for a single operator
A
to
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is impossible.

We begin by collecting some basic results in Section 2.
In Section 3, we consider the convexity of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.
The celebrated Toeplitz-Hausdorff theorem asserts that

W(A)
is always convex. Berger extended the result to

W_{k}(A)
for any
k\leq\dim{\mathcal{H}}
; for example, see
[
15
]
.
However, for
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
,
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
may not be convex if
m\geq 2
.
We will extend
some results concerning the convexity of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})

for finite dimensional operators to the infinite dimensional case.
An example of
{\mathbf{A}}
is constructed such that

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is not convex, but
W_{r}({\mathbf{A}})
is convex for all positive integer
r

not equal to
k
. In particular, this shows that there are no implications between
the convexity of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
and
W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})
.
This is quite different from the other properties considered in the subsequent sections,
namely, special properties of
W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})
often induces similar properties on
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.

For infinite dimensional operator,
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
may not be closed.
In Section 4, we determine the closure of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})

in terms of the joint essential numerical
range of
{\mathbf{A}}
defined by

W_{{\rm ess}}({\mathbf{A}})=\cap\{{\bf cl}\,(W_{1}({\mathbf{A}}+{\mathbf{K}})):{\mathbf{K}}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})^{m}\},

(1.1)

where
{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
is the set of all compact operators in
{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
.
These lead to characterizations of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
or

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
to be closed. Moreover, it is shown that

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is closed whenever
W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})
or
{\bf conv}\,W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})

is.
The results extend those in
[
8
,
19
]
, and will be useful in
the subsequent sections, which concern
the interplay between the geometric
properties of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
and
algebraic properties of
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
.

In section 5, we show that
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is a polyhedral set in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

if and only if

A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
have a common reducing subspace
{\mathbf{V}}

of finite dimension
such that the compression of
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
on the subspace

{\mathbf{V}}
are diagonal operators
D_{1},\dots,D_{m}
and

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})
.
This extends the result in
[
23
]
.
Characterization is also
given to
{\bf A}
such that the closure of
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral.

For finite rank operators, one can show that the following two conditions are
equivalent:

(a)
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a commuting family of normal operators.

(b)
W_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
is polyhedral for every positive integer
k<\dim{\mathcal{H}}
.

This result is no longer true for compact operators. In Section 6, characterizations are given
for compact operators
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
satisfying (a) and (b), respectively.
Additional results concerning conditions (a) and (b) are obtained for general
non-compact operators in Section 7.

In Section 8, we describe some related results, open problems
and additional research topics.

In our discussion, we will
use
{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})},{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})},{{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})}
to denote the set of bounded linear operators, the set of
self-adjoint operators, and the set of compact operators acting on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces. A linear map
X:{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{2}

is an isometry if
X^{*}X=I_{{\mathcal{H}}_{1}}
.
Denote by
M_{m,n}
and
M_{n}
the set of
m\times n
matrices and the set of

n\times n
matrices.
We will use the convention that
W_{0}({\mathbf{A}})=\{(0,\dots,0)\}.

Suppose
\dim{\mathcal{H}}=n
is finite. Then

W_{n}({\mathbf{A}})=\{({\rm tr}\,A_{1},\dots,{\rm tr}\,A_{m})\}
, and

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=({\rm tr}\,A_{1},\dots,{\rm tr}\,A_{m})-W_{n-k}({\mathbf{A}}).

Unless it is specified otherwise, we always assume that

k
is a positive integer such that
1\leq k<\dim{\mathcal{H}}

when
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is considered. We note that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})

if and only if any of the following holds.

•

There is a rank
k
orthogonal projection
P
such that

(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})=({\rm tr}\,(A_{1}P),\dots,{\rm tr}\,(A_{m}P))
.

•

There is an isometry
X:{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
such that

(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})=({\rm tr}\,(X^{*}A_{1}X),\dots,{\rm tr}\,(X^{*}A_{m}X))
.

Clearly,
X:{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is an isometry such that

X^{*}AX=B
if and only if there is an orthonormal basis

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}\cup{\mathcal{B}}_{2}

of
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
B=({\langle}Ax_{j},x_{i}{\rangle})
.
In such a case, we will say that there is a unitary
U\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
such that

U^{*}AU=\begin{pmatrix}B&\star\cr\star&\star\end{pmatrix}.

With this notation, we see that

(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in W_{k}(A)
if and only if the following holds.

•

There is a unitary
U\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
such that

U^{*}A_{j}U=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{A}_{j}&\star\cr\star&\star\cr\end{pmatrix}

with
\tilde{A}_{j}\in M_{k}

such that
{\rm tr}\,\tilde{A}_{j}=a_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,m
.

In our study, it will be shown that if

(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
has some special properties, then
there will be a unitary

such that
U^{*}A_{j}U=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{A}_{j}&0\cr 0&\star\cr\end{pmatrix}
. Thus,

is a reducing subspace for
A_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,m
.
In such a case, we will say that
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
have a common reducing space

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
of
{\mathcal{H}}
of dimension

and

is an isometry
such that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})=({\rm tr}\,(X^{*}A_{1}X),\dots,{\rm tr}\,(X^{*}A_{m}X))
.

Basic properties

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
. Then
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is bounded.
If
\dim{\mathcal{H}}
is finite, then

is closed.

For any positive integer
\ell

I_{\ell}
and

be the
\ell\times\ell

Example 2.2
.

Let

.
It is easy to check that
W_{k}(A)=[0,k]

W_{k+1}(A)=(0,k+1]

In Section 4, we will consider the condition for

to be closed for

Let

These properties have direct extensions to

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}

Suppose

Remark 4.7
.

It is tempting to use an inductive argument on

as shown in the penultimate paragraph of the proof,

if

in reducing the problem to the case with one fewer operator.

In general,

is closed. For example, if

It is interesting to prove or disprove that

is closed
whenever

is closed
for
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
with
m>3
.

Note that by Theorem
4.6
, if

is convex and closed,
then

is closed.

5
Polyhedral properties

In this section, we determine
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
such that
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
or

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is a polyhedral set, i.e., the convex
hull of a finite set. It turns out that
these conditions are equivalent, and also equivalent
to that
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
have
a common reducing subspace of finite dimension such that the compression
of
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
onto the subspace are diagonal operators

D_{1},\dots,D_{m}
and
W_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})=W_{k}(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})
.
This extends the result in
[
23
]
.
It is remarkable that even if
{\mathcal{H}}
is inseparable,
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is determined
by the compression of
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
on a finite dimensional subspace if

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral. We will obtain similar
descriptions for
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
such that
{\bf cl}\,(W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
or

{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
is polyhedral.
We begin with the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(a)

The set
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is a singleton if and only if

A_{j}=\mu_{j}I
for any
j=1,\dots,m
.

(b)

The set
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is a subset of a line segment in

{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
if and only if there is
H\in{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}
and complex numbers

a_{1},\dots,a_{m},b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
such that

A_{j}=a_{j}I+b_{j}H
for
j=1,\dots,m
.

Proof.
(a) The sufficiency is clear.
Conversely, suppose
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is a singleton.
If
A_{1}
is not a scalar,

W_{k}(A_{1})=\{\mu:(\mu,\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{m})\in W({\mathbf{A}})\}

is not a singleton, which is a contradiction. Applying the same argument
to
A_{j}
for any
j
, the result follows.

(b) The sufficiency is clear. Conversely, suppose

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is a line segment. Then for any partial
isometry
X
of finite rank,

W_{k}(X^{*}A_{1}X,\dots,X^{*}A_{m}X)
is a line segment
so that

X^{*}A_{j}X=a_{j}(X)I+b_{j}(X)H(X)

for some scalar

.
Since this is true for any isometry, we conclude that

Let
{\mathcal{S}}

{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
(or
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

{\mathbb{R}}^{2m}
) and
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
With respect to the standard inner product
{\langle}\ ,\ {\rangle}
, a nonzero vector

if there are
m

at

with linearly

Clearly every extreme point of a polyhedral set is a conical point.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral for all

\ell=1,\dots,k
.

(b)

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral.

(c)

There is
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
r\geq 2k
and a unitary
U\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}

such that for each
j=1,\dots,m
,
U^{*}A_{j}U=D_{j}\oplus B_{j}
, where

D_{j}\in M_{r}
is a diagonal matrix, satisfying

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})
.

Proof.
We may assume that
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is clear.

Suppose (b) holds so that
{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral
with
q
vertices
{\bm{\nu}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{\nu}}_{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
For each
p=1,\dots,q
,
there is an orthonormal set

S_{p}=\{x_{1}^{(p)},\dots,x_{k}^{(p)}\}
such that

{\bm{\nu}}_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\langle}{\mathbf{A}}x_{j}^{(p)},x_{j}^{p)}{\rangle}
.
Let
V
be any finite dimensional subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
containing
{\rm span}\,S_{p}
(for all
p=1,\ldots,q
), and

\tilde{A}_{j}
be the compression of
A_{j}
onto
V
for

j=1,\dots,m
. Then for

\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=(\tilde{A}_{1},\dots,\tilde{A}_{m})

{\bm{\nu}}_{p}\in{\bf conv}\,W_{k}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\subseteq{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
so that
{\bm{\nu}}_{p}
is a conical point of
{\bf conv}\,W_{k}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})
.
By
[
23
, Theorem 4.2]
,
{\rm span}\,S_{p}
is a common reducing subspace for
\tilde{A}_{1},\dots,\tilde{A}_{m}
.
As this is true for any such subspace

,
{\rm span}\,S_{p}
is a common reducing subspace
for
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
. Let
W={\rm span}\,(S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{q})
. It is clearly an invariant subspace for all
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
.
But as they are self-adjoint,
W
is indeed a common reducing subspace. Now let
\tilde{A}_{j}
be the compression of
A_{j}
onto
W
for
j=1,\dots,m
. We have

{\bf conv}\,\{{\bm{\nu}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{\nu}}_{q}\}={\bf conv}\,W_{k}(\tilde{A}_{1},\dots,\tilde{A}_{m})={\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}).

By
[
23
, Theorem 5.1]
,
there is a unitary
\tilde{U}
on
W
such that

\tilde{U}^{*}\tilde{A}_{j}\tilde{U}=D_{j}\oplus B_{j}
,
where
D_{j}\in M_{r_{1}}
is a diagonal matrix
for
j=1,\dots,m
, and

W_{k}(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})=W_{k}(\tilde{A}_{1},\dots,\tilde{A}_{m})={\bf conv}\,W_{k}(\tilde{A}_{1},\dots,\tilde{A}_{m}).

The operator
U=\tilde{U}\oplus I_{W^{\perp}}
is unitary and satisfies our purpose.

Next, we show that
D_{j}\in M_{r}
can be chosen with
r\geq 2k
. To this end, let

\tilde{V}
be a subspace of dimension
2k+r_{1}
containing
W
.
Suppose
C_{j}
is the compression of

A_{j}
to
\tilde{V}
for
j=1,\dots,m
.
Then for
{\mathbf{C}}=(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
and
{\mathbf{D}}=(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})
,

W_{k}({\mathbf{D}})\subseteq W_{k}({\mathbf{C}})\subseteq W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}({\mathbf{D}}).

By
[
23
, Theorem 5.1]
again, we can choose
D_{j}\in M_{r}
with
r\geq 2k
. Hence (b)
\Rightarrow
(c).

Now, suppose (c) holds.
For every unit vector
{\mathbf{u}}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\max\{{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\bm{\xi}}:{\bm{\xi}}\in W_{k}({\mathbf{D}})\}

\displaystyle=

\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{\langle}D_{j}x_{\ell},x_{\ell}{\rangle}:\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\ \mbox{is an
o.n. set in ${\mathcal{H}}$}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\max\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left\langle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}D_{j}\right)x_{\ell},x_{\ell}\right\rangle:\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\ \mbox{is an
o.n. set in ${\mathcal{H}}$}\right\}

is the sum of the

k
largest eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathbf{u}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}D_{j}
.
Similarly,
if
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}A_{j}
, then

\max\{{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\bm{\xi}}:{\bm{\xi}}\in W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})\}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}})
.
So,
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}({\mathbf{D}})
implies that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{D}}_{\mathbf{u}})
.
Since
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}}
is unitarily similar to a direct sum of the form
{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathbf{u}}\oplus{\mathbf{B}}_{\mathbf{u}}
,

\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}})\geq\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{D}}_{\mathbf{u}})

for
j=1,\ldots,k
.
We see that
\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}})=\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{D}}_{\mathbf{u}})

is actually the
j
th largest eigenvalue of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}}
for each
j
.
It follows that for every
\ell=1,\dots,k
,

\max\left\{{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\bm{\xi}}:{\bm{\xi}}\in W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})\right\}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{A}}_{\mathbf{u}})=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{D}}_{\mathbf{u}})=\max\left\{{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\bm{\xi}}:{\bm{\xi}}\in W_{\ell}({\mathbf{D}})\right\}.

As this is true for all unit vectors

{\mathbf{u}}
,
{\bf conv}\,W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})
and
W_{\ell}({\mathbf{D}})

have the same support plane in all directions. Also, as
{\bf conv}\,W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})
is closed
by Theorem
4.6
,
the two sets are equal.
Thus,

W_{\ell}({\mathbf{D}})\subseteq W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})\subseteq{\bf conv}\,W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{\ell}({\mathbf{D}}),

and so,
W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{\ell}({\mathbf{D}})
is polyhedral. We have (c)
\Rightarrow
(a), and the proof is complete.
∎

We have an approximation result without assuming that

or

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

(c)

{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is clear.

To prove (b)

(c), suppose (b) holds so that

with
q
vertices
{\bm{\nu}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{\nu}}_{q}
.
By Theorem

{\bm{\nu}}_{j}={\bm{\mu}}_{j}+(k-\ell_{j}){\bm{\xi}}_{j}

For
{\mathbf{v}}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}={\mathbb{C}}^{p}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\subseteq

{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))={\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}))

It follows that

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})={\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))

By Theorem
5.2
,

\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
is unitarily similar to

{\mathbf{D}}\oplus{\mathbf{B}}
, where
{\mathbf{D}}=(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})

m

\ell
.
Now, decompose
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
as

,
where
\tilde{H}_{0}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r},w_{1},\dots,w_{r}\}^{\perp}

in
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}

\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{Q}\oplus\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{A}\oplus\tilde{\mathbf{B}}

\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{1}

{\mathbf{A}}

For each vertex
{\bm{\mu}}_{p}

.

{\bm{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})

is a conical point of
{\bf cl}\,(W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
and not in
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
, then there
is a sequence of isometries
X_{\ell}:{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}

for
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}

such that

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}({\rm tr}\,(X_{\ell}^{*}A_{1}X_{\ell}),\dots,{\rm tr}\,(X_{\ell}^{*}A_{m}X_{\ell}))={\bm{\mu}}.

Proof.
We may assume that
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
are self-adjoint.
(a) Suppose
{\bm{\mu}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\langle}{\mathbf{A}}x_{j},x_{j}{\rangle}\in W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})

is a conical point, where
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
is an orthonormal set.
Consider the operator matrices
A_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}C_{j}&Z_{j}\cr Z_{j}^{*}&B_{j}\cr\end{pmatrix}

for
j=1,\dots,m
,
where
C_{j}\in M_{k}
is the compression of
A_{j}
onto the space
spanned by
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
. We will show
that
Z_{j}=0
for all
j
and the result will follow.
Assume without loss of generality that
Z_{1}\neq 0
, and
by abuse of notation that the
(1,1)
entry of
Z_{1}
is nonzero.
Consider the leading principal submatrix
T\in M_{k+1}

of
A_{1}+iA_{2}
. Then

\mu_{1}+i\mu_{2}\in W(T)\subseteq\{\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2}:(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\in W({\mathbf{A}})\}

so that
\mu_{1}+i\mu_{2}
is a conical point of
W(T)
.
By the result in
[
26
]
,
T=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
with
T_{1}\in M_{k}
,
which is a contradiction.

(b) Suppose
{\bm{\mu}}
is a conical point of
{\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.
Then
{\bm{\mu}}
is an extreme point of
{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
.
Thus,
{\bm{\mu}}={\bm{\nu}}+(k-\ell){\bm{\xi}}
with

{\bm{\nu}}\in W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})
and
{\bm{\xi}}\in W_{{\rm ess}}({\mathbf{A}})
for some

\ell\in\{0,\dots,k\}
. Let
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}={\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bm{\xi}}I_{k-\ell}
.
Then
{\bm{\mu}}
is a conical point of
W_{k}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})
.
Using (a) and an argument similar to the proof of Theorem
5.3
,
we get the conclusion. ∎

6
Compact operators

In
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
, it was shown that
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}\subseteq M_{n}

is a commuting family of normal matrices if and only if
W_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})

is poyhedral for all
k=1,\dots,n-1
. In this section, we study conditions
for compact operators
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
satisfying one of the
following conditions.

(a)

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(b)

A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
are commuting normal operators.

One may see some results for the joint numerical range of compact operators
in
[
3
,
17
]
and their references.
There have been considerable interests
in the commutativity of compact self-adjoint (normal) operators in
terms of different kinds of joint spectra. See for example
[
9
]
,

[
12
]
and
[
25
]
. Our result gives an alternative
from the joint numerical range viewpoint, and use the joint eigenvalues
of operators defined as follows.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
. A point
{\bm{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}

is a
joint eigenvalue

of
{\mathbf{A}}
if there is a nonzero
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that

A_{j}x=\mu_{j}x
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
. The nonzero
x
is a common eigenvector corresponding to
{\bm{\mu}}
.
Define
\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
to be the set of all
{\bm{\mu}}_{1}+\cdots+{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
, where
{\bm{\mu}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
are joint eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{A}}
corresponding to
k
linearly independent common eigenvectors
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
.
This set could be empty; indeed,
{\mathbf{A}}
may not have any joint eigenvalue at all.
When
k=1
,
\Sigma_{1}({\mathbf{A}})
reduces to the joint
point spectrum of
{\mathbf{A}}
.
Suppose
{\bm{\mu}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
are joint eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{A}}
corresponding
to linearly independent common eigenvectors
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
.
One may apply Gram-Schmidt process to
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
to obtain an orthonormal
set
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}
. Then the matrix

B_{j}=({\langle}A_{j}u_{r},u_{s}\rangle)\in M_{k}
is in
upper triangular form, and

{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}=((B_{1})_{\ell\ell},\dots,(B_{m})_{\ell\ell})

for
\ell=1,\dots,k
.
Thus,
{\langle}{\mathbf{A}}u_{\ell},u_{\ell}{\rangle}={\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}
for
\ell=1,\dots,k

and
\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\bm{\mu}}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\langle}{\mathbf{A}}u_{j},u_{j}{\rangle}\in W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
. As a result,

\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})\subseteq W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.
Evidently, conditions (a) – (c) in Theorem
5.2
are
equivalent to the condition:

(d)
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}(A)
and
{\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}(A)
is polyhedral.

Suppose
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
are commuting compact
normal operators in
{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
and
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
.
As far as its
k
-numerical ranges are concerned, we may assume that
{\mathcal{H}}
is separable.
By
[
12
, Proposition 1]
, there is a unitary
U
such that

D_{j}=U^{*}A_{j}U={\rm diag}\,(d_{j}(1),d_{j}(2),\dots)

is a diagonal operator for
j=1,\dots,m
.
Let
{\mathbf{v}}(\ell)=(d_{1}(\ell),d_{2}(\ell),\dots,d_{m}(\ell))
for
\ell\geq 1
. Then
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{\mathbf{v}}(j_{\ell}):1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{k}\right\}.

For
k\geq 1
, let
{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
be the set of rank
k

orthogonal projections in
{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
, and

{\mathcal{D}}_{k}=\{{\rm diag}\,P:P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\}
. By
[
18
, Theorem 13]
,

{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
consists of all

(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)
such that

0\leq u_{j}\leq 1
for
j=1,\dots
and

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}=k
. Then we have

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=\left\{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}d_{1}(j),\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}d_{2}(j),\dots,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}d_{m}(j)\right):(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k}\right\}.

Suppose
\dim{\mathcal{H}}=N
is finite and
1\leq k\leq N
.
Let
{\mathbf{v}}=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k-{\rm terms}},0,\dots,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Then,

{\mathcal{D}}_{k}={\bf conv}\,\{P{\mathbf{v}}:P\mbox{ is an }N\times N\mbox{ permutation matrix}\},

for example see
[
28
]
.
Therefore, we have

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}}).

(6.1)

In fact, it is known that
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
for all

k=1,\dots,N-1
if and only if
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}

is a commuting family; see
[
23
]
and also
[
24
,
27
]
.
The result can be extended to compact operators
as in the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
is
an
m
-tuple of compact operators.
Then
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a commuting family of normal operators
if and only if

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})\qquad\hbox{ for all }\ k\geq 1.

Proof.
Suppose
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a commuting family of compact normal operators.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
{\mathcal{H}}
is separable,

A_{j}={\rm diag}\,(d_{j}(1),d_{j}(2),\dots)
for
j=1,\dots,m
and

{\mathbf{v}}(\ell)=(d_{1}(\ell),\dots,d_{m}(\ell))\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

for all
\ell\geq 1
. Let
{\bm{\mu}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}{\mathbf{v}}(j)
be a point in
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
, where
(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
.

If
u_{j}\neq 0
for only finitely many
j
’s, then by (
6.1
),
{\bm{\mu}}\in{\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
. Otherwise,
choose
N>k
such that
a=\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}u_{j}<1

and consider the sum

{\mathbf{u}}=\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}(u_{j}/a){\mathbf{v}}(j)
.
It is an infinite convex combination of the points
{\mathbf{v}}(j)
,
j=N+1,\ldots
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
By the result of
[
11
]
, there exists
\ell>0
such that
{\mathbf{u}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}{\mathbf{v}}(N+i)
for some

\alpha_{i}\geq 0
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}=1
. We have

{\bm{\mu}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}{\mathbf{v}}(j)+a{\mathbf{u}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}{\mathbf{v}}(j)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a\alpha_{i}{\mathbf{v}}(N+i)

with

0\leq u_{j},\,a\alpha_{i}\leq 1\ \mbox{ for all }1\leq j\leq N,\ 1\leq i\leq\ell\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}\ +\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a\alpha_{i}=k.

By (
6.1
) again,
{\bm{\mu}}\in{\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.

Conversely, suppose
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
for all

k\geq 1
.
Consider the first operator
A_{1}
. We have
W_{k}(A_{1})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}(A_{1})
for all
k
.
Write
A_{1}=H_{1}+iG_{1}
for self-adjoint
H_{1},G_{1}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
.
The compact self-adjoint
H_{1}
can be written as

H_{1}=\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{j}I_{{\mathcal{H}}_{j}}+\sum_{\ell\geq 1}b_{\ell}I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}},

where
a_{j}
’s are the positive, and
b_{\ell}
’s the negative, eigenvalues of
H_{1}
.
The eigenspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{j}
’s and
{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
’s are mutually orthogonal with
\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{j}=n_{j}
and

\dim{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}=m_{\ell}
all finite.
Either sum can be empty, finite, or infinite. An empty sum is taken to be 0.
We shall also assume that the eigenvalues are ordered as

a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots
and
b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots
.
We shall show that

{\mathcal{H}}_{j}
’s and
{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
’s are all reducing subspaces of
G_{1}
. It follows that
the null space of
H_{1}
is also a reducing subspace of
G_{1}
, and consequently,
H_{1}
and
G_{1}
commute,
and
A_{1}
is normal.

Starting with
a_{1}
, let
k=\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=n_{1}
.
Clearly,
\max{\rm Re}\,W_{k}(A_{1})=n_{1}a_{1}
.
Take an extreme point
\mu
of
{\bf cl}\,W_{k}(A_{1})
lying on the vertical line
{\rm Re}\,z=n_{1}a_{1}
.
Then
\mu\in W_{k}(A_{1})
. For otherwise
\mu=\mu_{1}+(k-\ell)0=\mu_{1}
for
\mu_{1}\in W_{\ell}(A_{1})
with

\ell<k
(Theorem
4.4
),
and so
{\rm Re}\,\mu={\rm Re}\,\mu_{1}<n_{1}a_{1}
, a contradiction.
Hence
\mu
is an extreme point of
W_{k}(A_{1})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}(A_{1})
.
We have
\mu\in\Sigma_{k}(A_{1})
.
Then,
\mu=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}
for eigenvalues
\mu_{j}
of
A_{1}
corresponding to linearly
independent (unit) eigenvectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
of
A_{1}
. Since
{\rm Re}\,\mu_{j}={\langle}H_{1}x_{j},x_{j}{\rangle}\leq a_{1}
for all
j
,

{\rm Re}\,\mu=n_{1}a_{1}
implies all
x_{j}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
.
We have
{\rm span}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}={\mathcal{H}}_{1}
. As a linear span of eigenvectors,

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
is invariant under
A_{1}
. It is also invariant under

G_{1}=-i(A_{1}-H_{1})
. It is indeed a reducing subspace because
G_{1}
is self-adjoint.

If
H_{1}
has another positive eigenvalue
a_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is also a reducing subspace of
H_{1}
.
This time, we take
k=n_{1}+n_{2}
. The same argument as above yields that
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is a invariant subspace of
A_{1}
. Consequently,
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is a reducing subspace of
G_{1}
.
Successively, we get that all
{\mathcal{H}}_{j}
’s are reducing subspaces of
G_{1}
.

The same can be done for the negative eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenspaces. The only difference is to consider
the left hand supporting line instead. For example, if
b_{1}
is the smallest negative eigenvalue of

B
, then for
k=m_{1}=\dim{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
,

\min\{{\rm Re}\,\lambda:\lambda\in W_{k}(A)\}=m_{1}b_{1}
. By considering an extreme point of
{\bf cl}\,W_{k}(A_{1})
lying on the vertical line
{\rm Re}\,\lambda=n_{1}b_{1}
, we get that

{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
, and then similarly, all other
{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
’s, are reducing subspaces of
G_{1}
.
As observed above,
A_{1}
is normal.

Similarly, all other
A_{2},\ldots,A_{m}
are normal. If
A_{j}=H_{j}+iG_{j}
for self-adjoint

H_{j}
and
G_{j}
, and if
a+ib
(
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
) is an eigenvalue of
A_{j}
with corresponding eigenvector
x
, then

G_{j}x=ax
and
H_{j}x=bx
. Thus we have

W_{k}(H_{1},G_{1},\ldots,H_{m},G_{m})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma(H_{1},G_{1},\ldots,H_{m},G_{m})
for all
k
.
For any two operators
B,C\in\{H_{1},G_{1},\ldots,H_{m},G_{m}\}
, we may apply the argument above to
B+iC

to conclude that
B
and
C
commute. It follows that the operators
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
commute.
∎

In contrast to the finite dimensional case,
the following example shows that
there are commuting compact self-adjoint operators
H,G
such that

W_{k}(H,G)\equiv W_{k}(H+iG)
is not polyhedral for all
k
.

Example 6.2
.

Let
A_{0}=-I+{\rm diag}\,(e^{i\pi/2},e^{-i\pi/2},e^{i\pi/3},e^{-i\pi/3},e^{i\pi/4},e^{-i\pi/4},\dots)
, and
for
k\geq 1

A_{k}={\rm diag}\,(i,-i,-1+i,-1-i)\oplus A_{k-1}
.
Then

{\bf cl}\,W(A_{0})
is not polyhedral as it has a smooth extreme point at 0.
For
k\geq 1
,
W_{k}(A_{k})
is not polyhedral because the extreme points

(k-1)i
and
(1-k)i
are smooth extreme points.

To determine a condition for

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
to be polyhedral for all
k
,
recall the following notations.
For
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
, let

{\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\ell}=(A_{1}\oplus 0_{\ell},\dots,A_{m}\oplus 0_{\ell})
,
where
0_{\ell}
is the zero matrix in
M_{\ell}
if
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We also write
0_{\infty}
as the zero operator on some infinite dimensional Hilbert space.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
be
an
m
-tuple of compact operators.
Then for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

{\bf cl}\,(W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))=W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{k})=W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty}),

and hence

{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))={\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{k})={\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty}).

(6.2)

Consequently, the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

{\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral.

(b)

For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
is polyhedral.

(c)

The set

\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a commuting family of normal operators,
and

{\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty})

is polyhedral for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,.

Proof.
The first assertion follows from Theorem
4.3
and
Theorem
4.4
.
One can readily adapt the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 4.4]

to the infinite dimensional operators and show that
for every rank
k
orthogonal projection

P=\begin{pmatrix}P_{11}&P_{12}\cr P_{21}&P_{22}\end{pmatrix}

with
P_{11},P_{22}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
,

P_{11}\oplus P_{22}
is a convex combination of
orthogonal projections of the form

Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}\oplus{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
, where

Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are orthogonal projections of rank
\ell
and
k-\ell

with
0\leq\ell\leq k
. Consequently,

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty})\subseteq{\bf conv}\,\bigcup_{0\leq\ell\leq k}(W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})+W_{k-\ell}({\bf 0}_{\infty}))={\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})).

Thus,
{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{k})\subseteq{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))

and (
6.2
) follows.

To prove the equivalence of conditions (a) – (c), we can focus on
self-adjoint operators
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
because
we can write
A_{\ell}=H_{\ell}+iG_{\ell}
for
self-adjoint
H_{\ell}
and
G_{\ell}
so that

(1)
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral if and only if

W_{k}(H_{1},G_{1},\ldots,H_{m},G_{m})
is, and

(2)
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a commuting family of
normal operators if and only if

\{H_{1},G_{1},\ldots,H_{m},G_{m}\}
is a commuting family
by Fuglede’s theorem.

The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is clear.
Suppose (b) holds.
For notational simplicity, we let
{\mathbf{A}}={\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty}\in{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
, and
show that the components of
{\mathbf{A}}
commute using an argument similar to those
in the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
Without loss of generality, we shall just show that
A_{1}A_{2}=A_{2}A_{1}
.
Now,
{\bf conv}\,W_{k}(A_{1},A_{2})
, as the projection
of
{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
onto
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
is polyhedral.
Since
A_{1}
is self-adjoint and compact, it can be written as

A_{1}=\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{j}I_{{\mathcal{H}}_{j}}+\sum_{\ell\geq 1}b_{\ell}I_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}},

where
a_{j}
’s are the positive, and
b_{\ell}
’s the negative, eigenvalues
of
A_{1}
, as in the proof of Theorem
6.1

We shall consider
A=A_{1}+iA_{2}
and use
[
26
, Theorem 1]

to show that
{\mathcal{H}}_{j}
’s and
{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
’s are all reducing subspaces
of
A_{2}
. It follows that
the null space of
A_{1}
is also a reducing subspace of
A_{2}
, and
consequently,
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
commute.

If
A_{1}
has at least one positive eigenvalue, we start with the largest
eigenvalue
a_{1}
and let
k=\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=n_{1}
.
For any unit vector
y\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}^{\perp}
, let
B=B_{1}+iB_{2}
be the
compression of

A=A_{1}+iA_{2}
onto the subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus\mbox{span}\{y\}
.
Clearly,

\max\{{\rm Re}\,\lambda:\lambda\in W_{k}(B)\}=n_{1}a_{1}=\max\{{\rm Re}\,\lambda:\lambda\in W_{k}(A)\}.

Let
\mu
be an extreme point of
{\bf cl}\,W_{k}(A)
lying on the vertical
line
{\rm Re}\,\lambda=n_{1}a_{1}
.
Then as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
,
\mu\in W_{k}(A)
.
Suppose
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\langle}Ax_{j},x_{j}{\rangle}
for orthonormal vectors

x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
. It is easy to see that
all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and so
\mu\in W_{k}(B)
. As

W_{k}(B)\subseteq{\bf cl}\,W_{k}(A)
, it follows that
\mu
is a conical
point of
W_{k}(B)
. By
[
26
, Theorem 1]
,

{\langle}Bx,y{\rangle}={\langle}By,x{\rangle}=0
, or,
{\langle}Ax,y{\rangle}={\langle}Ay,x{\rangle}=0
,
for all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
.
As this is true for all
y\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}^{\perp}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
is a reducing subspace
of
A
. It must also be a reducing subspace of
A_{2}
.

If
A_{1}
has another positive eigenvalue
a_{2}
, then we take
k=n_{1}+n_{2}
and for any unit vector

y\in({\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{2})^{\perp}
, consider
the compression of
A
onto
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\oplus\mbox{span}\{y\}
.
The same argument as above yields that
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is a reducing
subspace of
A_{2}
. Consequently,
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is a reducing subspace of
A_{2}
.
Successively, we get that all
{\mathcal{H}}_{j}
’s are reducing subspaces of
A_{2}
.
Similarly, every
{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
is also reducing for
A
.
Thus, we have
A_{1}A_{2}=A_{2}A_{1}
.
Therefore,
\{A_{1}\oplus 0_{\infty},\dots,A_{m}\oplus 0_{\infty}\}
, and consequently

\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
, are commuting families.
Condition (c) holds.

Suppose (c) holds. Since
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a
commuting family of normal operators, by Theorem
6.1
,

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is convex for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Now
\{A_{1}\oplus 0_{\infty},\dots,A_{m}\oplus 0_{\infty}\}
is also a
commuting family of normal operators. Therefore,

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty})

is polyhedral for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By (
6.2
),

{\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))=W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\bf 0}_{\infty})

is also polyhedral.
Hence, condition (a) holds. ∎

For finite rank operators,
the result resembles that of the finite dimensional case.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
be
an
m
-tuple of finite rank operators.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a commuting family of
normal operators, equivalently, there is a unitary operator
U
such that

U^{*}A_{j}U
is a diagonal operator for each
j
.

(b)

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf cl}\,(W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(c)

There is
k\geq r
such that

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})

is polyhedral, where
r
is the dimension of the sum
of the range spaces of
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
.

Proof.
Suppose (a) holds.
We may assume that
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
are real
diagonal operators, each has finite rank.
Then
\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is a finite set. By Theorem
6.1
,
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})={\bf conv}\,\Sigma_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral.
Hence
{\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.

The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(c)
is clear.

Suppose (c) holds. Then we may assume that

A_{j}=B_{j}\oplus 0_{\infty}
with
B_{j}\in M_{r}
for
j=1,\dots,m
. We may
further assume that all
B_{j}
’s are Hermitian. Let
C_{j}=B_{j}\oplus 0_{2k-r}
for

j=1,\dots,m
,
{\mathbf{B}}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{m})
and
{\mathbf{C}}=(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
.
By (
6.2
), we have

{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))={\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{B}}+{\bf 0}_{k})\subseteq{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{C}})\subseteq{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}).

Therefore,
{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{C}})={\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral.
For every
1\leq i<j\leq m
,
W_{k}(C_{i},C_{j})={\bf conv}\,W_{k}(C_{i},C_{j})
is
a polygon in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Since
C_{i},C_{j}\in M_{2k}
,
by Theorem
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
,
C_{i}C_{j}=C_{j}C_{i}
, which implies that
A_{i}A_{j}=A_{j}A_{i}
.
Hence, (a) holds.
∎

7
General operators

For any compact convex subset
{\mathcal{S}}
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, one can construct
diagonal operators
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
with
orthonormal basis
\{e_{\mu}:\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})\in{\mathcal{S}}\}

and
A_{j}e_{\mu}=(\mu_{j}/k)e_{\mu}
such that

W_{k}(I_{k}\otimes A_{1},\dots,I_{k}\otimes A_{m})={\mathcal{S}}
.
(Here and in the following, when we say
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
is an
m
-tuple of diagonal
operators, we always assume that they are diagonal operators relative to a fixed
orthonormal basis.)
Thus, there are a lot of flexibility in constructing commuting normal operators

{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
with prescribed
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.
Nevertheless, one can use the geometrical properties
of
W_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
to get useful information of
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
as
in Proposition
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
, and additional results were obtained for
compact operators in the last section.
However, the situation for general operators are more complicated
as one would expect. For example,
Theorem 6.3 is not true if the operators
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
are not compact.
As a counterexample, we exhibit a nonnormal operator whose
k
-numerical range
is a triangle for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Example 7.1
.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 0&0\cr\end{pmatrix}\oplus[I_{\infty}\otimes{\rm diag}\,(1,\omega,\omega^{2})]

with
\omega=e^{i2\pi/3}
.
It is not difficult to see that
W_{k}(A)={\bf conv}\,\{k,k\omega,k\omega^{2}\}

is the triangular region with vertices

k,k\omega,k\omega^{2}
. However
A
is not normal because of the first summand.

Despite the above example, we will obtain some results on

A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
if
W_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(b)

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(c)

There is a unitary
U\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
such that
U^{*}A_{j}U=D_{j}\oplus C_{j}
,
where
D_{j}={\rm diag}\,(d_{1}^{(j)},d_{2}^{(j)},\dots)
and

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})
is polyhedral for all

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.
We may assume that
(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
.
The implications (c)
\Rightarrow
(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) are clear.

Suppose (b) holds.
By Theorem
5.2
, we get for each
k
an orthonormal set of common
reducing eigenvectors
S_{k}=\{x_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,x_{n_{k}}^{(k)}\}
of

A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
such that
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}(D_{1}^{(k)},\dots,D_{m}^{(k)})
,
where
D_{j}^{(k)}
are the compressions of
A_{j}
onto
{\rm span}\,S_{k}
.
We may assume that
S_{k}\subseteq S_{k+1}

by arguing as follows. If
x_{1}^{(k+1)}
is orthogonal to
S_{k}
, then

S_{k}\cup\{x_{1}^{(k+1)}\}
is an orthonormal basis of

{\rm span}\,S_{k}\cup\{x_{1}^{(k+1)}\}
.
If
x_{1}^{(k+1)}
is not orthogonal to some of the vectors in
S_{k}
,
say,
x_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,x_{\ell}^{(k)}
for
\ell\leq n_{k}
,
then they are all eigenvectors corresponding to the same eigenvalue of

A_{j}
for each
j
.
It is because eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are
orthogonal.
Delete
x_{1}^{(k+1)}
if
x_{1}^{(k+1)}\in{\rm span}\,S_{k}
; otherwise
replace it by a unit vector
x
in

{\rm span}\,\{x_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,x_{\ell}^{(k)},x_{1}^{(k+1)}\}
so that

S_{k}\cup\{x\}
is an orthonormal basis of

{\rm span}\,S_{k}\cup\{x_{1}^{(k+1)}\}
.
This
x
is also a reducing eigenvector of each
A_{j}
corresponding to the
same eigenvalue as
x_{1}^{(k+1)}
.
Then either
S_{k}
or
S_{k}\cup\{x\}
is an orthonormal basis of

{\rm span}\,S_{k}\cup\{x_{1}^{(k+1)}\}
.
Going through all other vectors in
S_{k+1}
,
we get an orthonormal set
S_{k+1}^{\prime}
containing
S_{k}
such that

{\rm span}\,S_{k+1}^{\prime}={\rm span}\,S_{k}\cup S_{k+1}
.
Note that
S_{k+1}^{\prime}
contains all vectors in
S_{k+1}
that are
orthogonal to
S_{k}
, and for a vector that is not
orthogonal to
S_{k}
, it is either deleted or replaced.
A vector that is deleted can be regarded as replaced by an eigenvector in

S_{k}
corresponding to the same eigenvalue.
With the previous notations, if
x_{1}^{(k+1)},\ldots,x_{r}^{(k+1)}

belong to
{\rm span}\,\{x_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,x_{\ell}^{(k)}\}
so that they
are deleted, then
r\leq\ell
. There are always enough vectors for
replacement.
Renaming
S_{k+1}^{\prime}
as
S_{k+1}
, then we still have

W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k+1}(D_{1}^{(k+1)},\dots,D_{m}^{(k+1)})
.
Thus we may assume that the sets
S_{k}
are increasing. It follows that

S=\bigcup_{k}S_{k}
is an orthonormal set
of common reducing eigenvectors of
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
.
If
D_{j}
is the compression of
A_{j}
onto
{\rm span}\,S
,
then
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}(D_{1},\dots,D_{m})
is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence, condition (c) holds.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

{\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(b)

{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
is polyhedral
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(c)

There exists an
m
-tuple of diagonal operators

{\mathbf{D}}
of the form
(R_{1}\oplus S_{1},\dots,R_{m}\oplus S_{m})
,
where
{\mathbf{R}}=(R_{1},\dots,R_{m})

is the compression of
{\mathbf{A}}
on a separable reducing subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
,
and
{\mathbf{S}}=(S_{1},\dots,S_{m})
is an
m
-tuple of diagonal operators
whose diagonal entries are extreme points of

W_{\rm ess}({\mathbf{A}})
, such that

{\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})=W_{k}({\mathbf{D}})\quad\hbox{ is polyhedral
for every }\ k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Proof.
We may assume that
(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
.
The implications (c)
\Rightarrow
(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) are clear.

Suppose (b) holds. For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
{\mathbf{v}}_{1}^{k},\dots,{\mathbf{v}}_{n_{k}}^{k}

be the vertices of
{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
. For each vertex
{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{k}
,
1\leq j\leq n_{k}
,
by Theorem
4.4
, there exist
0\leq\ell\leq k
,
{\mathbf{w}}^{k}_{j}\in W_{\ell}({\mathbf{A}})
and

{\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j}\in W_{\rm ess}({\mathbf{A}})
such that

{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{k}={\mathbf{w}}^{k}_{j}+(k-\ell){\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j}
. Let

{\mathbf{S}}=\oplus_{k\geq 1}\oplus_{1\leq j\leq n_{k}}{\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j}I_{\infty}.

Then for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\mathbf{S}})={\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})

is polyhedral for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Theorem
7.2
,

{\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\mathbf{S}}
is unitarily similar to

{\mathbf{D}}\oplus{\mathbf{C}}
for an
m
-tuple of diagonal operators
{\mathbf{D}}
such that

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\mathbf{S}})=W_{k}({\mathbf{D}})
for each

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}=\{(x_{j},y_{j})\}
be an orthonormal set of common reducing eigenvectors
of
{\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\mathbf{S}}
corresponding to the diagonal entries of
{\mathbf{D}}
.
Then
{\mathcal{S}}
is at most countably infinite.
If
y_{j}=0
, then
x_{j}
is a common reducing eigenvector of
{\mathbf{A}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}=\{x_{j}:(x_{j},0)\in{\mathcal{S}}\}
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
is orthonormal.
Extending
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
to an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{H}}
, we have a unitary
U

in
{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
and
m
-tuple of
{\mathbf{R}}=(R_{1},\dots,R_{m})
of
diagonal operators acting on a separable subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}

such that

U^{*}A_{j}U=R_{j}\oplus B_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,m
. Note that the common
eigenvalue
{\mathbf{d}}_{j}
corresponding to each
x_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
is included in
{\mathbf{R}}
.
Also, it is easy to show that it suffices to use the extreme points
of
W_{{\rm ess}}({\mathbf{A}})
to construct the diagonal operator
{\mathbf{D}}
.
Then
{\mathbf{R}}
and
{\mathbf{S}}
satisfy condition (c). ∎

One may wonder whether
{\mathbf{R}}
or
{\mathbf{S}}
in
Theorem 7.3 can be replaced by finite dimensional operators.
By Proposition
2.7
,
for any
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}

\frac{1}{k+1}W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})\subseteq{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,\frac{1}{k}W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))\quad\hbox{ if }k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Hence,

{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,\frac{1}{k}W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))\rightarrow W_{\rm ess}({\mathbf{A}})\quad\hbox{as}\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

One may ask whether
{\bf cl}\,(W_{k}({\mathbf{A}}))
is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

will imply that
W_{{\rm ess}}({\mathbf{A}})
is polyhedral, and hence the distinct
diagonal entries of
{\mathbf{S}}
form a finite set.
The following example shows that the
answers for the above questions are both negative.

Example 7.4
.

Let
D_{1}={\rm diag}\,(1,e^{i\pi/2},e^{-i\pi/2},e^{i\pi/3},e^{-i\pi/3},\dots)
,

D_{2}=(1)\oplus{\rm diag}\,(1+1,1+1/2,1+1/3,\dots)
,
A_{1}=D_{1}\otimes I_{\infty}
and

A=A_{1}\oplus D_{2}
.
Then
W_{\rm ess}(A)={\bf conv}\,\{1,e^{i\pi/2},e^{-i\pi/2},e^{i\pi/3},e^{-i\pi/3},\dots\}

is not polyhedral. On the other hand, it is easy to see that

W_{1}(A)={\bf conv}\,(W_{1}(A_{1})\cup W_{1}(D_{2}))
is polyhedral. For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\frac{1}{k}W_{k}(A_{1})=W_{{\rm ess}}(A)={\bf conv}\,\{1,e^{i\pi/2},e^{-i\pi/2},e^{i\pi/3},e^{-i\pi/3},\dots\}
,
and
\frac{1}{k}W_{k}(D_{2})
is a line segment on the real axis of the form
[1,1+r]

for some
r>0
. One may use

[
23
, Theorem 4.4]

and mathematical induction
to show that
\frac{1}{k}W_{k}(A)
has only finitely many extreme points.

Theorems
7.2
and
4.3
characterize
{\mathbf{A}}
such that
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}({\mathbf{A}})

is polyhedral for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
,

{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
,
{\bf cl}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
, or
{\bf cl}\,({\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
.
In all cases, the condition depends on the
behavior of the compression of
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
on a certain
separable subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Even if
{\mathcal{H}}
is separable, Example
7.1
shows that
it is not easy to determine whether
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}

is a commuting normal family using the geometrical structure of

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We are only able to obtain a
result for some special families of
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
,
which can be deduced from the following.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
A\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
have a finite spectrum.
Then
A
is normal if any one of the following conditions holds.

(a)

For each
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
there is
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

W_{k}(A)
is polyhedral with a conical point

\lambda+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\mu_{j}\in\Sigma_{k}(A)
.

(b)

For each
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
there is
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

{\bf cl}\,W_{k}(A)
is polyhedral with a conical point

\lambda+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\mu_{j}+(k-\ell)\xi
, where
1\leq\ell\leq k
,

\lambda+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\mu_{j}\in\Sigma_{\ell}(A)
and
\xi\in W_{{\rm ess}}(A)
.

Proof.
Suppose
A\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}
has a finite spectrum
and (a) holds. Let
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
and

W_{k}(A)
be polyhedral with a vertex of the form

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\in\Sigma_{k}(A)
, where

\mu_{1}=\lambda
. We will show that
A
is unitarily
similar to
\lambda I\oplus B
such that
\lambda\notin\sigma(B)
.
By Theorem
5.2
,
A
is unitarily similar to
D\oplus B
for a
diagonal matrix
D\in M_{r}
such that
W(A)=W(D)
. In particular, we may
assume that
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
are diagonal entries of
D
.
If
\lambda\notin\sigma(B)
, then we are done.
Otherwise, assume that
\lambda\in\sigma(B)
. We may assume that
the first column (of the operator matrix) of
B
has the form

(\lambda,0,\dots)^{t}
. We claim that the first
row
B
is of the form
(\lambda,0,\dots)
.
If not, we may assume that the
(1,2)
entry of
B
is
\gamma\neq 0
.
Consider the leading principal submatrix
T\in M_{r+2}

of
D\oplus B
. Then the
(r+1,r+2)
entry of
T
is
\gamma
.
Note that

W_{k}(D)\subseteq W_{k}(T)\subseteq W_{k}(A)=W_{k}(D).

We can find
k
diagonal entries of
T
including the
(r+1,r+1)

entry, which is
\lambda
, summing up to
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}

is a vertex of
W_{k}(T)
. By Theorem
5.4
(a),
the
(r+1,r+2)
entry of
T
is zero, which is a contradiction.
So, if
\lambda\in\sigma(B)
, then
\sigma
is a reducing eigenvalue if
B
.
As a result,
B=\lambda I\oplus B_{1}
such that

\lambda\notin\sigma(B_{1})
. Repeating this argument for every
element in
\sigma(A)
, we see that
{\mathcal{H}}
is a direct sum of the eigenspaces
of
A
. Thus,
A
is normal.

Next, suppose (b) holds.
Let
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
, and
{\bf cl}\,W_{k}(A)
is polyhedral with a conical
point
\lambda+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\mu_{j}+(k-\ell)\xi
.
By Theorem
5.3
and its proof, we see that

{\bf cl}\,W_{\ell}(A)
is polyhedral with
\lambda+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\mu_{j}

as a conical point. We may use the argument in the preceding
paragraph to show that
\lambda
is a reducing eigenvalue of
A
.
Since this is true for all
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
, we see that
A
is normal.
∎

In Proposition
7.5
(a) and (b), one cannot just require
W_{k}(A)
to be polyhedral
for
k\leq\ell
for a finite value
\ell
using
the information of
\sigma(A)
.
For example, suppose
\sigma(A)=\{1,w,w^{2}\}
with
w=e^{i2\pi/3}
.
For any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we can let

A=[I_{\ell}\otimes{\rm diag}\,(1,w,w^{2})]\oplus\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 0&0\cr\end{pmatrix}

to be the non-normal operator
such that
W_{k}(A)={\bf conv}\,\{k,kw,kw^{2}\}

is a polygon for all
k=1,\dots,\ell
, but

W_{k+1}(A)
is not.

Corollary 7.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}
be an

m
-tuple of self-adjoint operators. Then

\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
is a commuting family if
for all choices of
1\leq p<q\leq m
,

A=A_{p}+iA_{q}
has a finite spectrum satisfying conditions
(a)
or
(b)

of Proposition
7.5
.

The following example shows that Corollary

7.6
will fail if one only assumes that

A_{1},A_{2}
are self-adjoint operators, where

A_{1},A_{2}
have finite spectrum, and for any eigenvalues

\lambda_{j}\in\sigma(A_{j})
for
j=1,2
,
there is
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
W_{k}(A_{1}+iA_{2})
is
polyhedral with a vertex of the form

(\lambda_{1}+i\lambda_{2})+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\mu_{j}\in\Sigma_{k}(A_{1}+iA_{2})
.

Example 7.7
.

Let
A_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&-1\cr\end{pmatrix}\oplus(I_{\infty}\otimes D_{1})
and

A_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\cr\end{pmatrix}\oplus(I_{\infty}\otimes D_{2})
with
D_{1}={\rm diag}\,(1,1,-1,-1)
,

D_{2}={\rm diag}\,(1,-1,1,-1)
.
Then
A_{1}A_{2}\neq A_{2}A_{1}
,

\sigma(A_{1})=\sigma(A_{2})=\{1,-1\}
. Let
A=A_{1}+iA_{2}
. Then for every
k\geq 1
,

W_{k}(A)
is the square with vertices
\{k(1+i),k(1-i),k(-1+i),k(-1-i)\}
. Note that
0\in\sigma(A_{1}+iA_{2})
but a point
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}
is a vertex of
W_{k}(A)
if and only if

\mu_{1}=\cdots=\mu_{k}\in\{1+i,1-i,-1+i,k-1-i\}
.

8
Open problems and related results

There are many interesting problems and related results in connection to our study.
We list some of them in the following.

•

In connection to Proposition
2.7
, it is interesting to prove or disprove that

W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})/(k+1)\subseteq W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})/k
.

•

In connection to Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.6
, it is interesting to
prove or disprove that

W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is closed whenever
W_{k+1}({\mathbf{A}})
is closed.

•

In connection to Example
4.5
, it is interesting to construct an
example
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{n}
such that
{\bf conv}\,W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is closed, but
W_{k}({\mathbf{A}})
is not
for
k>1
.

For any real vector
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
one can define the joint

c
-numerical range of
{\mathbf{A}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{m}

as the collection of
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

such that
a_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}{\langle}A_{j}x_{j},x_{j}{\rangle}
for an orthonormal set

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.
One may see
[
20
,
24
]
for some background
of the
c
-numerical range for a single operator.
Some of our results can be readily extended to
the setting of the joint
c
-numerical ranges. For example,
if
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
has distinct entries, then

Reconstruction of a surface from the category of reflexive sheaves
Agnieszka Bodzenta, Alexey Bondal

Abstract
. We define a normal surface X X to be codim-2-saturated if any open embedding of X X into a normal surface with the complement of codimension 2 is an isomorphism. We show that any normal surface X X allows a codim-2-saturated model X ^ \widehat{X} together with the canonical open embedding X → X ^ X\to\widehat{X} . Any normal surface which is proper over its affinisation is codim-2-saturated, but the converse does not hold. We give a criterion for a surface to be codim-2-saturated in terms of its Nagata compactification and the boundary divisor. We reconstruct the codim-2-saturated model of a normal surface X X from the additive category of reflexive sheaves on X X . We show that the category of reflexive sheaves on X X is quasi-abelian and we use its canonical exact structure for the reconstruction. In order to deal with categorical issues, we introduce a class of weakly localising Serre subcategories in abelian categories. These are Serre subcategories whose categories of closed objects are quasi-abelian. This general technique might be of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we address the problem of reconstructing
a normal surface
X
(a scheme, not an algebraic space) from the category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
of reflexive sheaves on it. The important issue here is that the category is considered as an additive category, not endowed with the (geometric) exact structure induced by the embedding into the category of coherent sheaves on
X
. In the case of smooth surfaces, reflexive sheaves are just vector bundles. The typical instance of the problem is how to recover a projective plane
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
from the category of vector bundles without using any additional data.

It is clear that the surface
X
cannot be uniquely reconstructed if no condition is imposed on it. For instance, one can remove a closed point from
X
and the category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
would not change.

For this reason, we reconstruct the maximally saturated model
\widehat{X}
of
X
.
The surface
X
is an open subset of
\widehat{X}
with complement of dimension zero and
\widehat{X}
is maximal with this property, i.e. it does not allow ‘adding’ one more closed point while keeping the surface normal and separated. We say that a normal surface is
codim-2-saturated
if it has this property. Any proper surface is such, as well as any surface which is proper over its affinisation, though the inverse implication does not hold.

The key point in our reconstruction is that
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
is
quasi-abelian
, hence it is endowed with the canonical exact structure. Remarkably, this exact structure is not geometric, but rather corresponds to a ‘surface’ with all closed points removed. We use the right abelian envelope of the canonical exact structure to categorically identify the Weil prime divisors of the surface.

To recover
\widehat{X}
from
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
we find those divisors whose complements are quasi-affine surfaces, using the fact that categories equivalent to
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
for
X
quasi-affine can be characterised intrinsically (we call them CM affine categories). Then
\widehat{X}
is basically glued from affinisations of the quasi-affine open pieces that cover
X
.

Actually, the construction of
\widehat{X}
out of
X
can be performed in purely geometric terms.
We prove that the process of adding closed points to a separated normal surface while preserving the property of it being normal and separated must terminate after a finite number of steps.
All these points can be added simultaneously giving the codim-2-saturated model
\widehat{X}
of
X
.
The explicit construction of
\widehat{X}
is via the affinisations:

(1)

\widehat{X}=\varinjlim_{U\in\mathcal{QA}(X)}U^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits},

where the colimit is taken over the category
\mathcal{QA}(X)

of quasi-affine open subsets
U
of
X
.

The canonical open embedding
X\to\widehat{X}
turns out to be the unit for a pair of adjoint functors.
We consider the category
\mathscr{D}
of normal surfaces and open embeddings with complement of codimension 2. Morphisms in
\mathscr{D}
form a multiplicative system, so we consider the groupoid

\widehat{\mathscr{D}}:=\mathscr{D}[\mathscr{D}^{-1}]
by inverting all morphisms in
\mathscr{D}
.
We prove

Theorem
.

(
cf.
Theorem
2.10
)

A normal surface

is codim-2-saturated if

is an isomorphism, or, equivalently, if any morphism in

with domain

As it was already mentioned above,
any normal surface which is proper over its affinisation is codim-2-saturated, but the converse is not true.

Actually, the converse holds in the category of algebraic spaces, but recovering a normal algebraic space of dimension two from its category of reflexive sheaves is beyond the scope of this paper. Instead, we characterize codim-2-saturated surfaces as open subschemes in proper surfaces via a geometric criterion on contractibility of connected components of the complement divisor.

The codim-2-saturated model
\widehat{X}
of

clearly determines
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)

yields an equivalence
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(\widehat{X})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)

\widehat{X}
can be recovered from the (additive) category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)

from

The canonical exact structure on the quasi-abelian category

In order to mimic formula (

.

(

We develop a general categorical set-up which
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
fits in as a subcategory of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
. We believe this is of independent interest and can be applied to other relevant (noncommutative) situations.

Given a Serre subcategory
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
, the subcategory

of
{\mathcal{B}}
-closed objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
is classically known. This category is fully faithfully embedded by the quotient functor into

. We say that

,

,

, and

the category of torsion free sheaves on

.

This paper can be regarded as the contemplation on exact structures on the category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
. The embedding
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\subset\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
as an extension closed subcategory induces the
geometric exact structure
on
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
in which conflations are complexes
F\to G\to H
such that
0\to F\to G\to H\to 0
is an exact sequence in
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
. For any non-isomorphism

in

the geometric exact structures on
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
induced by
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
and
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(Y)
are different, the one induced by

has less conflations. Hence, the geometric exact structure corresponding to a codim-2-saturated surface is minimal among possible geometric exact structures, while the canonical exact structure can be thought of as the maximal one among those, when all the closed points from the surface are removed. Note that there are other interesting exact structures which are related to various non-commutative resolutions of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
. We postpone the study of them to another publication.

We expect that the extension of ideas of this paper to higher dimensions will involve the machinery of the Minimal Model Program from Birational Geometry.

2.
The codim-2-saturated model of a normal surface

Throughout this section
X
is a normal surface, i.e. an irreducible two dimensional separated normal

-scheme of finite type.

Define the category
\mathscr{D}
whose objects are normal surfaces and morphisms are open inclusions with complement of codimension two.
We say that a normal surface

is

if one cannot add any closed points to it, i.e. if any morphism in

Any normal surface

admits a canonical morphism to a codim-2-saturated surface

which is the adjunction unit for the functor

is the groupoid obtained from

2.1.
The affinisation of a normal surface

We denote by

By

Proposition 2.2
.

We call an open subset
X\subset Y
a
non-contractible divisorial complement
if
Y\setminus X
is a divisor in
Y
and any connected component of
Y\setminus X
is not contractible.

Theorem 2.14
.

A normal surface
X
is codim-2-saturated if and only if
X
is isomorphic to a non-contractible divisorial complement in a normal surface
Y
proper over
\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits k
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{Y}
be a compactification of
X
as in (
6
). Let
\overline{Y}\setminus X=D\sqcup Z
be the decomposition of the complement of
X
into the divisorial part
D
and the Artinian part
Z
. If
Z\neq\emptyset
, let
U_{z}
be an affine open neighbourhood of
z\in Z
such that
V_{z}:=U_{z}\setminus\{z\}\subset X
. Then
V_{z}
is a quasi-affine open subset of
X
with affinisation
U_{z}
. By Proposition
2.7
,
U_{z}
is an open subset of
X
which contradicts the choice of
z\in\overline{Y}\setminus X
. Hence,
Z=\emptyset
, i.e.
X
is a divisorial complement in
\overline{Y}
.

Let
D\subset\overline{Y}\setminus X
be a negative definite connected component. If
g_{D}\colon\overline{Y}\to Y_{D}
is the contraction of
D
then the complement to
X
in
Y_{D}
has non-trivial Artinian part. Indeed, as
D
is a connected component of
\overline{Y}\setminus X
, the point
g_{D}(D)\notin X
does not lie on any of the divisorial components of
Y_{D}\setminus X
. Since
Y_{D}
is a compactification of
X
, an analogous argument as above yields a contradiction with the assumption that
X
is codim-2-saturated. Hence,
D
is not contractible.

In the opposite direction, assume that
X\subset\overline{Y}
is a non-contractible divisorial complement. In particular,
\overline{Y}
is a compactification of
X
as in (
6
). Let
\widehat{U}
be the affinisation of a quasi-affine open subset
U\subset X
and let
\overline{Y}_{U}
be the domain of the rational map
\overline{Y}\dashrightarrow\widehat{U}
which restricts to the identity on
U
. By Proposition
2.4
,
\overline{f}_{U}\colon\overline{Y}_{U}\to\widehat{U}
is proper.

Assume that, for a point
p\in\widehat{U}\setminus U
, the fiber of
\overline{f}_{U}
over
p
is of dimension one, i.e.
\overline{f}_{U}^{-1}(p)=D
is a proper connected curve contained in
\overline{Y}\setminus X
. Proposition
2.1
implies that
U\cup\{p\}
is an open subset of
\widehat{U}
. Hence,
X\cup D=\overline{f}_{U}^{-1}(U\cup\{p\})
is an open subset of
Y
. It follows that
D
is a connected component of
\overline{Y}\setminus X
. Indeed, if this is not the case, there exists a point on
D
whose any open neighbourhood is not contained in
X\cup D
. Hence,
\overline{f}_{U}
contracts a connected component of
\overline{Y}\setminus X
which contradicts the assumption on
X
.

It follows that, for any
p\in\widehat{U}\setminus U
the fiber of
\overline{f}_{U}
over
p
is a point. As
X\subset\overline{Y}
is a divisorial complement,
\overline{f}_{U}^{-1}(p)
is a point of
X
. Hence,
\widehat{U}\simeq\overline{f}_{U}^{-1}(\widehat{U})
is an open subset of
X
. By Proposition
2.7
,
X
is codim-2-saturated.
∎

Recall that a curve
D=\bigcup D_{i}
in a proper normal surface
Y
is said to be
negative definite
if the intersection matrix
|D_{i}\cdot D_{j}|
is negative definite.

In view of

Theorem 2.15
.

[
Sch00
, Theorem 3.3]

A connected negative definite curve
D\subset Y
is contractible if and only if there is a curve
A\subset Y
disjoint from
D
such that
A\cdot C>0
for every curve
C\subset Y
not supported by
D
.

Theorem
2.14
can be reformulated as the following geometric statement

Corollary 2.16
.

A normal surface
X
is codim-2-saturated if and only if
X
is isomorphic to an open subset in a normal surface
Y
proper over
\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits k
such that
Y\setminus X
is a divisor in
Y
and, for any negative definite connected component
D
of
Y\setminus X
and for any curve
A\subset Y
disjoint from
D
, there exists a curve
C\subset Y
not supported by
D
such that
A\cdot C\leq 0
.

3.
Closed objects for a Serre subcategory

Throughout this section we consider a Serre subcategory
{\mathcal{B}}
in an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
and the full subcategory

(8)

{\mathcal{E}}=\{A\in{\mathcal{A}}\,|\,\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}},A)=0=\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}({\mathcal{B}},A)\}

of
{\mathcal{B}}
-
closed objects

[
Gab62
, Lemme III.2.1.b]
.

We denote by

(9)

q\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{B}}=:{\mathcal{C}}

the quotient functor.

We show that
{\mathcal{E}}
is a full subcategory in
{\mathcal{C}}
and discuss when it is the torsion-free part of a torsion pair, i.e. when
{\mathcal{B}}
is weakly localising. To this end, we introduce the embedding condition (
13
) which allows us to induce a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{C}}
from a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{A}}
. We show that under further assumptions on
{\mathcal{A}}
the embedding condition is equivalent to the existence of a torsion pair
(\overline{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{E}})
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, where
{\mathcal{E}}
is the torsion free part. We describe also Ext-groups of objects in
{\mathcal{E}}
over the quotient category
{\mathcal{C}}
as colimits of Ext-groups over
{\mathcal{A}}
.

3.1.
Closed objects as a full subcategory of the quotient category

Recall that a Serre subcategory
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
yields a multiplicative system in
{\mathcal{A}}
:

{\mathcal{S}}=\{f\,|\,\ker f,\textrm{coker}f\in{\mathcal{B}}\}.

Since we are interested in
{\mathcal{B}}
-closed objects, it is more convenient to view
{\mathcal{S}}
as a right multiplicative system. Then morphisms in the quotient category
{\mathcal{C}}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}[{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}]

between two objects
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
in
{\mathcal{A}}
are equivalence classes of roofs

(10)

\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{A_{0}}
\textstyle{A_{1}}

with
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
. We denote (
10
) by
fs^{-1}:A_{0}\to A_{1}
. The equivalence relation on roofs is analogous to (
3
).

We check that the quotient functor (
9
) is fully faithful when restricted to the category
{\mathcal{E}}
of
{\mathcal{B}}
-closed objects (
8
). It allows us to consider
{\mathcal{E}}
as a full subcategory both in
{\mathcal{C}}
and in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Even more is true,

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
, the quotient functor induces an isomorphism:

(11)

q_{A,E}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,E)\simeq\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}(q(A),q(E)).

Proof.

Let
fs^{-1}\colon A\to E
be a morphism in
{\mathcal{C}}
, where
f:A^{\prime}\to E
is a morphism in
{\mathcal{A}}
and
s\colon A^{\prime}\to A
is in
{\mathcal{S}}
. We have an exact sequence:

(12)

0\to B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}\xrightarrow{s}A\to B\to 0,

where
B,B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Since
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-,E)
and
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-,E)
vanish on
{\mathcal{B}}
, the standard spectral sequence for
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{\bullet}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-,E)
, when applied to (
12
), implies
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,E)\simeq_{{\mathcal{A}}}\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits(A^{\prime},E)
, which gives a unique lifting of
f
to a morphism in
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,E)
. The lifting is independent of the choice of an element in the equivalence class of roofs.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Functor
q|_{\mathcal{E}}\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is fully faithful.

3.2.
Inducing a torsion pair in the quotient category

Given a full subcategory
\mathcal{G}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
we denote by
q({\mathcal{G}})\subset{\mathcal{C}}
the
essential image
of the quotient functor (
9
) restricted to
{\mathcal{G}}
. In other words,
q({\mathcal{G}})
is the (strictly) full subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}}
with objects
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
for which there exists
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
and an isomorphism
C\simeq q(G)
in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

We discuss sufficient conditions for a torsion pair
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
in
{\mathcal{A}}
to induce a torsion pair
(q({\mathcal{T}}),q({\mathcal{F}}))
in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
be a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then the subcategories
q({\mathcal{T}})
,
q({\mathcal{F}})
form a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}(q({\mathcal{T}}),q({\mathcal{F}}))=0
.

Proof.

We check that any object
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
is an extension of an object in
q({\mathcal{F}})
by an object in
q({\mathcal{T}})
. Indeed,
C
is isomorphic to
q(A)
, for some
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
0\to T\to A\to F\to 0
be the decomposition of
A
in the torsion pair
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
. Then
0\to q(T)\to q(A)\to q(F)\to 0
is a short exact sequence in
{\mathcal{C}}
with
q(T)\in q({\mathcal{T}})
and
q(F)\in q({\mathcal{F}})
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
be a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{A}}
with
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
a Serre subcategory. If
{\mathcal{B}}
is a subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
then
(q({\mathcal{T}}),q({\mathcal{F}}))
is a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
it suffices to check that
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}(q({\mathcal{T}}),q({\mathcal{F}}))=0
. For
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
and
F\in{\mathcal{F}}
, let
f\circ s^{-1}\colon T\to F
be a morphism in
{\mathcal{C}}
with
s\colon A\to T
in
{\mathcal{S}}
. Since
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is a Serre subcategory, the image of
s
is an object of
{\mathcal{T}}
. Since
A
is an extension of the image by the kernel of
s
and
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
, then
A
is an object of
{\mathcal{T}}
. Hence,
f\colon A\to F
is the zero morphism.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a full subcategory of the category
{\mathcal{E}}
of
{\mathcal{B}}
-closed objects (
8
). We say that a full subcategory
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the
embedding condition with respect to
{\mathcal{G}}
if

(13)

\forall\,F\in{\mathcal{F}}\,\exists\,G\in{\mathcal{G}}\,\exists\,B\in{\mathcal{B}},\,\textrm{and a short exact sequence }0\to F\to G\to B\to 0.

Remark 3.5
.

The short exact sequence (
13
) can be considered as an exact triangle

(14)

B[-1]\to F\to G\to B

in
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})
or any triangulated category with a
t
-struc
ture whose heart is equivalent to
{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{-1}_{{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})}(B[-1],G)\simeq 0\simeq\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})}(B[-1],G)
, the standard argument
[
BBD82
, Proposition 1.1.9]
shows that if (
14
) exists it is unique.

Proposition 3.6
.

Consider a torsion pair
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
in
{\mathcal{A}}
and a full subcategory
{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}\cap{\mathcal{E}}
. Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the embedding condition with respect to
{\mathcal{G}}
. Then
(q({\mathcal{T}}),{\mathcal{G}})
is a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Sequence (
13
) implies that any object
q(F)\in q({\mathcal{F}})
is equivalent to
q(G)
, for some
G\in{\mathcal{E}}\cap{\mathcal{F}}
. By Proposition
3.1
, the vanishing of
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(T,G)
, for any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
and
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
, implies the vanishing of
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}(q(T),q(F))=\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}(q(T),q(G))\simeq\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(T,G)
, for any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
,
F\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Hence,
(q({\mathcal{T}}),q({\mathcal{F}}))
is a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{C}}
by Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Consider a cotilting torsion pair
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
in
{\mathcal{A}}
(see Appendix
A.2
) and a full subcategory
{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}\cap{\mathcal{E}}
. Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the embedding condition with respect to
{\mathcal{G}}
. Then the torsion pair
(q({\mathcal{T}}),{\mathcal{G}})
in
{\mathcal{C}}
is cotilting.

Proof.

The existence of the torsion pair
(q({\mathcal{T}}),{\mathcal{G}})
follows from Proposition
3.6
.

An object
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
is isomorphic to
q(A)
, for some
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
\varphi\colon F\to A
be an epimorphism in
{\mathcal{A}}
with
F\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Condition (
13
) implies that
q(F)\simeq q(G)
, for some
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
. Hence,
q(\varphi)\colon q(F)\to C
is the required epimorphism with
q(F)\simeq q(G)\in q({\mathcal{G}})\simeq{\mathcal{G}}
.
∎

3.3.
Weakly localising Serre subcategories

Given a full subcategory
{\mathcal{G}}
of an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
we denote by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}^{\perp}=\{A\in{\mathcal{A}}\,|\,\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathcal{G}},A)=0\},

\displaystyle{}^{\perp}{\mathcal{G}}=\{A\in{\mathcal{A}}\,|\,\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits(A,{\mathcal{G}})=0\}

the full subcategories right, respectively left, orthogonal to
{\mathcal{G}}
. Clearly,
{\mathcal{G}}^{\perp}
is closed under extensions and subobjects while
{}^{\perp}{\mathcal{G}}
is closed under extensions and quotients.

Recall the subcategory
{\mathcal{E}}
of
{\mathcal{B}}
-closed objects (
8
).
We say that
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is
weakly localising
if
{\mathcal{E}}
is the torsion-free part of a torsion pair
(\overline{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{E}})
in the quotient category
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Remark 3.8
.

If
{\mathcal{B}}
is a localising Serre subcategory, i.e.
q
admits the right adjoint
r
, then
{\mathcal{E}}\simeq{\mathcal{C}}
is the torsion-free part of the trivial torsion pair
(0,{\mathcal{E}})
on
{\mathcal{C}}
. Indeed,
r\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
is essentially surjective by
[
Gab62
, Corollaire III.2]
and fully faithful by Corollary
3.2
.

We show that under the assumption that
{}^{\perp}{\mathcal{E}}
is the torsion part of a torsion pair
({}^{\perp}{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
in
{\mathcal{A}}
, the subcategory
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is weakly localising if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the embedding condition (
13
) with respect to
{\mathcal{E}}
.

If the category
{\mathcal{A}}
is Noetherian the torsion pair
({}^{\perp}{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
always exists:

Lemma 3.9
.

(cf.
[
Pol07
, Lemma 1.1.3]
)
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a Noetherian abelian category and
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
a subcategory closed under extensions and quotients. Then
{\mathcal{T}}
is the torsion part of a torsion pair in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Assume that the Serre subcategory
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is weakly localising and that category
{\mathcal{A}}
admits a torsion pair
({}^{\perp}{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the embedding condition with respect to
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\overline{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{E}})
be the torsion pair in the quotient category
{\mathcal{C}}
. Given
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
, the decomposition of
q(A)
yields an epimorphism
q(A)\to q(E)
, for some
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
. In particular, by Proposition
3.1
, we get a canonical morphism
\varphi_{A}\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,E)
. Let
K
, respectively
Q
, denote the kernel, respectively the cokernel of
\varphi_{A}
. Then
q(K)\in\overline{{\mathcal{T}}}
and

. Note that the condition

is equivalent to the condition

. Indeed, by Proposition
3.1

is an object of

if and only if

As subcategory

is closed under subobjects, for any

, the kernel of

is an object of

, i.e.
\varphi_{F}
is a monomorphism. Then

, with

is the required short exact sequence.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Assume that category
{\mathcal{A}}
admits a torsion pair
({}^{\perp}{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is weakly localising if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the embedding condition with respect to
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proof.

By construction,
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
. The statement follows from Propositions
3.6
and

.
∎

3.4.
Ext-groups between closed objects in the quotient category

A category
{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
satisfying the assumptions of Proposition
3.6
is closed under extensions in
{\mathcal{C}}
. We shall present
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(E_{1},E_{2})
, for
E_{1},E_{2}\in{\mathcal{G}}
, as a colimit of appropriate Ext-groups in the category
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Recall from Corollary
3.2
that the category
{\mathcal{E}}
of
{\mathcal{B}}
-closed objects (
8
) is a full subcategory both in
{\mathcal{A}}
and in
{\mathcal{C}}
. This allows us to consider a short exact sequence in
{\mathcal{C}}
with all terms in
{\mathcal{E}}
as a complex in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

A short exact sequence

(15)

0\to E_{2}\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}E_{1}\to 0

in
{\mathcal{C}}
with all terms in
{\mathcal{E}}
yields a 4-term exact sequence

(16)

0\to E_{2}\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}E_{1}\to B\to 0

in
{\mathcal{A}}
, with
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
.

Conversely, any 4-term sequence (
16
) in
{\mathcal{A}}
yields a short exact sequence (
15
) in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Since the restriction of the quotient functor
q|_{{\mathcal{E}}}\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is fully faithful by Corollary
3.2
, sequence (
15
) admits a lift to a complex
E_{2}\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}E_{1}
in
{\mathcal{A}}
with cohomology in
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Since there are no morphisms from
{\mathcal{B}}
to
{\mathcal{E}}
, the cohomology at
E_{2}
is zero. The cohomology
H
at
E
fits into a short exact sequence
0\to E_{2}\to\ker_{{\mathcal{A}}}g\to H\to 0
. Since
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}({\mathcal{B}},E_{2})=0
, this sequence splits.
Then
H
is a subobject of
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
, hence, as above,
H\simeq 0
.

The inverse statement is clear as
q(B)=0
.
∎

For an object
E\in{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
denote by
{\mathcal{A}}/^{{\mathcal{B}}}E
the category whose objects are pairs
(A,i)
of an object
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
and a monomorphism

in
{\mathcal{A}}
with the cokernel in
{\mathcal{B}}
. Morphisms
(A^{\prime},i^{\prime})\to(A,i)
in
{\mathcal{A}}/^{{\mathcal{B}}}E
are
\varphi\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A^{\prime},A)
such that
i\circ\varphi=i^{\prime}
.

Given
E_{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, consider a functor
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-,E_{2})\colon{\mathcal{A}}/^{{\mathcal{B}}}E^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\textrm{Ab}
which maps
(A,i)
to
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,E_{2})
. Given
f\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits((A,i),(A^{\prime},i^{\prime}))
, the map
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f,E_{2})\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A^{\prime},E_{2})\to\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,E_{2})
is the precomposition with
f
.

Proposition 3.13
.

Consider a full subcategory
{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
closed under extensions in
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then, for any
G_{1},G_{2}\in{\mathcal{G}}
,

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(G_{1},G_{2})=\varinjlim_{{\mathcal{A}}/^{{\mathcal{B}}}G_{1}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-,G_{2}).

Proof.

Consider
(A,i)\in{\mathcal{A}}/^{{\mathcal{B}}}G_{1}
and
\zeta\in\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,G_{2})
. The short exact sequence
0\to G_{2}\xrightarrow{f}G\xrightarrow{g}A\to 0
given by
\zeta
combined with the short exact sequence
0\to A\xrightarrow{i}G_{1}\to B\to 0
yields a 4-term exact sequence
0\to G_{2}\to G\to G_{1}\to B\to 0
, i.e. an element
\xi
of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(G_{1},G_{2})
(see Lemma
3.12
). Denote by
r_{A}\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(A,G_{2})\to\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(G_{1},G_{2})
the morphism which sends
\zeta
to
\xi
. It is straightforward to check that
r_{A}
is well-defined. Indeed, if
s\colon A\to G
is a section of
g
, then
q(s)\circ q(i)^{-1}
is a section of
q(g)
.

Let now
\varphi
be an element of
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits((A^{\prime},i^{\prime}),(A,i))
. The snake lemma for diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

4.
Reflexive sheaves on a surface and their right abelian envelope

Here we discuss the category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
of reflexive sheaves on a normal surface
X
. We prove that it is quasi-abelian and its right abelian envelope is the quotient of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
by the subcategory of Artinian sheaves. We show that the cotiliting torsion pair in
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
(see Proposition

) is canonical. We argue that

is the center of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
and
discuss the functor
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(U)
, for an open subset

.

4.1.

\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)

Denote by

We show that

is the right abelian envelope of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
endowed with its canonical exact structure.

It is well-known,

[

\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)=\{E\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)\,|\,\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{\leq 0}(X),E)=0=\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{X}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{\leq 0}(X),E)\}.

Lemma 4.1

Category

Proof.

For

is regular in codimension one, the cotorsion

∎

In view of Corollary

category

\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\subset\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
is closed under extensions and kernels of epimorphisms. Clearly, any locally free sheaf on
X
is an object of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
. By
[
SV04
, Theorem 2.1]
, any coherent sheaf on
X
is a quotient of a locally free sheaf. Hence, by Proposition
A.1
,
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
is the right abelian envelope of its full subcategory
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
endowed with the induced exact structure.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

[
Rou10
, Remark 4.5]

Consider a scheme
X
. Then
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
is the center of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
.

Proposition 4.15
.

Consider a normal surface
X
. Then
Z(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))=H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
4.11
,
4.13
and

,
Z(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))=Z({\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)))=Z(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X))=H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
.
∎

4.4.
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
and isomorphism of surfaces outside of dimension zero

We show that the categories of reflexive sheaves distinguish surfaces up to closed points.

Following
[
CP21
]
we say that schemes
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are
isomorphic outside of dimension

k-1
if there exist isomorphic open subsets
U_{1}\subset Z_{1}
,
U_{2}\subset Z_{2}
such that any point
p\in Z_{1}
of dimension greater than or equal to
k
is contained in
U_{1}
and, analogously, any point
q\in Z_{2}
of dimension greater than or equal to
k
is contained in
U_{2}
.
Clearly, normal surfaces
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are isomorphic outside of dimension zero if and only if they lie in the same connected component of the category
\mathscr{D}
.

Theorem 4.16
.

[
CP21
, Theorem 3.7]

Let
Z_{1}
,
Z_{2}
be schemes of finite type over an algebra
R
of finite type over a field. Then
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are isomorphic outside of dimension
k-1
if and only if
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq k}\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq k}(Z_{2})
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Given a normal surface
X
and an open
U\subset X
with complement of codimension two, the restriction functor
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(U)
is an equivalence.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that reflexive sheaves on normal schemes are normal in the sense of Barth,
[
Har80
, Proposition 1.6]
.
∎

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normal surfaces.
Then
X
and
Y
lie in the same connected component of the category
\mathscr{D}

if and only if there exists an equivalence
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(Y)
of additive categories.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.3
,
an equivalence
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(Y)
implies an equivalence
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(Y)
of the right abelian envelopes, i.e. an isomorphism of
X
and
Y
outside of dimension zero, see Theorem
4.16
.

By Proposition
4.17
, if
X
and
Y

lie in the same connected component of
\mathscr{D}
, then

\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(Y)
.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

A codim-2-saturated normal surface
X
can be uniquely reconstructed from the additive category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
of reflexive sheaves.

Remark 4.20
.

Since, by Corollary
4.3
, the right abelian envelope of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
is
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
and it’s derived category is the quotient of
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X))

4.5.
Reflexive sheaves under localisation

For a normal surface
X
, we discuss the restriction of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
to an open subset
U\subset X
. Let
D
be the divisorial part of
X\setminus U
. If
D=\emptyset
, then
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(U)
by Proposition
4.17
.
If
D\neq\emptyset
, let
D=\bigcup_{i\in I}D_{i}
be its presentation as a union of prime divisors
D_{i}\in X^{1}
. Consider subcategory
{\mathcal{S}}_{D}=\bigoplus_{I}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}_{D_{i}}(X)
in
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
.
Category
{\mathcal{S}}_{D}
is the subcategory of sheaves with support in
D
, hence it is a Serre subcategory in
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
.

Lemma 4.21
.

The quotient
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)/{\mathcal{S}}_{D}
is equivalent to
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(U)
.

Proof.

Let
Z
be the Artinian part of
X\setminus U
. By Theorem
4.16
,
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X\setminus Z)
, hence without loss of generality we can assume that
Z=\emptyset
. Then clearly
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(U)=(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)/\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{D}(X))/\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{\leq 0}(U)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)/\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{D}^{\geq 1}(X)=\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)/{\mathcal{S}}_{D}
(see
[
GD71
, Corollary I.9.4.8]
for the isomorphism
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)/\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{D}(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(U)
).
∎

Proposition 4.22
.

Let
U
be an open subset of a normal surface
X
and
D
the divisorial part of
X\setminus U
. Then
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(U)
is the essential image of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\subset\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
under the quotient functor
q\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)/{\mathcal{S}}_{D}
.

Proof.

As
\bigoplus_{D\in X^{1}}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}_{D}(X)
is a Serre subcategory in
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{D}
is its Serre subcategory, the torsion pair
(\bigoplus_{D\in X^{1}}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}_{D}(X),\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
in
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
descends to the quotient
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(U)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)/{\mathcal{S}}_{D}
, see Lemmas
3.4
and
4.21
.
For any prime divisor
E\subset X
whose generic point lies in
U
,
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{E}^{\geq 1}(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{E}^{\geq 1}(U)
as both are equivalent to
\textrm{mod--}{\mathcal{O}}_{X,E}
. Hence, the torsion part of the torsion pair on
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(U)
induced from
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
is equivalent the torsion part of the canonical torsion pair of Proposition
4.9
. Hence, the torsion-free parts
q(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
and
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(U)
are also equivalent.
∎

5.
Reconstruction of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\widehat{X})
from
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)

We give a categorical characterisation of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(U)
, for a quasi-affine normal surface
U
. This allows us to construct the category
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\widehat{X})
of coherent sheaves on the codim-2-saturated model of a normal surface
X
from the (additive) category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
. By Appendix, we can further reconstruct
\widehat{X}
from
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\widehat{X})
.

5.1.
CM affine surface is quasi-affine

Given a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
X
, the ring
\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)}({\mathcal{L}})
equals
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
. Since
{\mathcal{L}}
is an object of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
, Corollary
3.2
implies that

(19)

H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)}({\mathcal{L}}).

Since an open embedding
U\to X
with complement of codimension two induces an equivalence
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(U)
, equality (
19
) holds for any line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(U)
, i.e. for any line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(U)
,
\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)}({\mathcal{L}})=Z(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
, see Proposition
4.15
.

Denote by

q\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)

the quotient functor.

Object
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
can be recovered from
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\simeq{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
up to an auto-equivalence of the category:

Proposition 5.1
.

[
CP21
, Proposition 2.7, Theorem 4.1]

An object
E\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
is of the form
q(j_{*}{\mathcal{L}})
, for an open embedding
j\colon U\subset X
with complement of codimension two and a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(U)
, if and only if

(1)

for any simple object
P\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
,
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)}(E,P)
is a one-dimensional
\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)}(P)
-vector space,

(2)

E
is locally maximal with this property, i.e. for any
E^{\prime}\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
satisfying
(1)
any surjection
E^{\prime}\to E
is an isomorphism.

For any such
E
, there exists an auto-equivalence
\Phi
of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
such that
\Phi(E)\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

Since the center
Z({\mathcal{E}})
of an additive category is a commutative ring, the category
\textrm{CM}(Z({\mathcal{E}}))
of maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules over it is well-defined.

We say that an additive category
{\mathcal{E}}
is
CM affine
if there exists an object
{\mathcal{L}}\in{\mathcal{E}}
with
\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{E}}}({\mathcal{L}})=Z({\mathcal{E}})
such that
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{E}}}({\mathcal{L}},-)\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to\textrm{Mod--}Z({\mathcal{E}})
induces an equivalence
{\mathcal{E}}\simeq\textrm{CM}(Z({\mathcal{E}}))
.

We say that a normal surface
X
is
CM affine
if the category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
is.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
X
is a CM affine normal surface then
X
is quasi-affine.

Proof.

By
[
Sch00
, Corollary 6.3]
,
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}=\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits Z(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
(see Proposition
4.15
) is a normal scheme. If dimension of
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
is less than two, then
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
is regular and
\textrm{CM}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
is the category of torsion-free sheaves on
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
by
[
Yos90
, Proposition 1.5.1]
. In particular, if
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
is zero dimensional, then
\textrm{CM}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
is abelian, hence isomorphic to its right abelian envelope. As
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
, by Proposition
4.9
, we conclude that
\dim X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}\geq 1
. If
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
is a (regular) curve, then
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
admits a cotilting torsion pair
(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{\leq 0}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}),\textrm{CM}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}))
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\textrm{CM}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}))\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
, by Proposition
A.5
. In particular, simple objects in
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\textrm{CM}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}))
, i.e. structure sheaves of closed points, have endomorphisms algebras of finite dimension over
k
. As this is not the case for endomorphisms
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,D}/\mathfrak{m}_{X,D}
of simple objects
{\mathcal{O}}_{D}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))=\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{\geq 1}(X)
, we conclude that
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
is a normal surface. In particular,
\textrm{CM}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})

[
Yos90
, Proposition 1.5.3]
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
be such that
\Psi(-)=\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathcal{L}},-)\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
is an equivalence. It induces an equivalence
\overline{\Psi}\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
of the right abelian envelopes, by Corollary
4.3
. As the equivalence
\overline{\Psi}
maps
{\mathcal{L}}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}}
, and
{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}}
satisfies conditions
(1)
and
(2)
of Proposition
5.1
, so does
{\mathcal{L}}
. Hence, by the mentioned proposition, there exists an autoequivalence
\Phi
of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{\geq 1}(X)
such that
\Phi({\mathcal{L}})={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. Since any autoequivalence maps simple objects to simple objects,
\Phi
preserves the canonical torsion pair
(\bigoplus_{D\in X^{1}}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits_{D}^{\geq 1}(X),\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X))
of Proposition
4.9
. In particular,
\Phi
restricts to an autoequivalence of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
. Hence
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
satisfies the same property as
{\mathcal{L}}
, and we can assume that
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

We check that the equivalence
\overline{\Psi}\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
is given by
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},-)
. To this end, consider the geometric exact structure
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
on
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
induced by the inclusion
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})\subset\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}),{\mathcal{S}}_{g})\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
by Lemma
4.13
, and for the canonical exact structure
{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{can}}
, we have that
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}),{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{can}})\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
is the quotient of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
by the Serre subcategory of objects of finite length, according to Proposition
4.8
. Finally, any object of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
is the cokernel of a morphism in
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
,
[
SV04
, Theorem 2.1]
. Under the equivalence
\Psi^{-1}
all of the above holds for the right abelian envelopes of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{\Psi}
be the exact structure on
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
corresponding to
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
under
\Psi
. Functor
\Psi
induces an equivalence
\widetilde{\Psi}\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X),{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{\Psi})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
. To determine
\widetilde{\Psi}
on an object
T\in{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X),{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{\Psi})
we present it as the cokernel of
\varphi\colon R_{1}\to R_{2}
with
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
. Then
\widetilde{\Psi}(T)=\textrm{coker}(\Psi(\varphi))
. Note that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is projective in the exact structure
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{\Psi}
as
{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}}=\Psi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
is projective in
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
. Since
\widetilde{\Psi}(-)
and
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X),{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{\Psi})}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},-)
are both right exact functors which coincide on objects of
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
, it follows that they are equivalent.

Finally, as
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
is the quotient of
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X),{\mathcal{S}}^{\Psi}_{g})
by the Serre subcategory of objects of finite length and the equivalence
\widetilde{\Psi}
maps objects of finite length to objects of finite length, one concludes that
\widetilde{\Psi}
induces an equivalence
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},-)\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
.

Any divisor
D\subset X
yields a Serre subcategory
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}_{D}(X)
in
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
. Hence, the fact that
{\rho_{X}}_{*}\colon\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
is an equivalence implies that
{\rho_{X}}\colon X\to X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
does not contract any divisor, i.e.
\rho_{X}
is quasi-finite.

Since
X
is separated and of finite-type, so is
\rho_{X}
. Finally, by construction,
{\rho_{X}}_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}={\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}}
. By
[
GW10
, Theorem 12.83]
,
\rho_{X}
restricted to the open set
V=\{x\in X\,|\,\dim\rho_{X}^{-1}\rho_{X}(x)=0\}
is an open embedding. Hence, as
\rho_{X}
is quasi-finite, it is an isomorphism of
X
with an open subscheme of
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
. Since
\rho_{X}
induces an equivalence of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X)
and
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits^{\geq 1}(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
, the complement to
X
in
X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
is of codimension 2, see Theorem
4.16
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

A normal surface
X
is quasi-affine if and only if it is CM affine.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.2
if
X
is CM affine, it is quasi-affine.

By Proposition
2.1
, if
X
is quasi-affine then
\rho_{X}\colon X\to X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
is an open embedding with complement of codimension two. Hence,
\rho_{X}
induces an equivalence
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)\simeq\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits})
, see Theorem
4.18
, and
X
is CM affine.
∎

5.2.
Construction of
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\widehat{X})
from
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)

Recall that
X^{1}
denotes the set of prime divisors in
X
. For
I\subset X^{1}
, let
U_{I}\subset X
be the complement to the divisor
\bigcup_{D\in I}D
.

Lemma 5.4
.

The codim-2-saturated model
\widehat{X}
is isomorphic to
\varinjlim\widehat{U}_{I}^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
, where the colimit is taken over the category

(20)

\mathcal{D}\textrm{iv-}\mathcal{QA}(X)=\{I\subset X^{1}\,|\,I\textrm{ is finite, }U_{I}\textrm{ is quasi-affine}\}.

Proof.

By
[
KS06
, Proposition 2.5.2]
it suffices to check that the functor

\mathcal{D}\textrm{iv-}\mathcal{QA}(X)\to\mathcal{QA}(X)

which sends
I
to
U_{I}
is cofinal, i.e. that for any quasi-affine open subset
U\subset X
the category
(\mathcal{D}\textrm{iv-}\mathcal{QA}(X))^{U}
is connected. We show that even more is true, i.e. that
(\mathcal{D}\textrm{iv-}\mathcal{QA}(X))^{U}
has an initial object.
Let
D=\bigcup_{i\in I}D_{i}
be the divisorial part of
X\setminus U
. Clearly, any inclusion
U\to U_{J}
, for
J\in\mathcal{D}\textrm{iv-}\mathcal{QA}(X)
, factors via the inclusion

. By Proposition
2.9
,

is an open subset of
U^{\mathop{\textrm{aff}}\nolimits}
, hence it is quasi-affine, i.e.
I\in\mathcal{D}\textrm{iv-}\mathcal{QA}(X)
.
∎

Consider a quasi-abelian category
{\mathcal{E}}
and the right abelian envelope
{\mathcal{A}}
of
{\mathcal{E}}
with its canonical exact structure
{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{can}}
. We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
is

if the torsion part
{\mathcal{T}}={}^{\perp}{\mathcal{E}}
of the canonical torsion pair in
{\mathcal{A}}
(see Proposition
A.5
) is the Serre subcategory of objects of finite length in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

If
C
is a reduced curve then the category of torsion-free sheaves on

is height-one. By Corollary
4.3
and Proposition
4.9
, the category
\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)
, for a normal surface
X
, is height-one.

Given a finite set
I
of simple objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
and the Serre subcategory
{\mathcal{S}}_{I}
generated by them, we denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{I}\subset{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{S}}_{I}
the right orthogonal to
{\mathcal{T}}/{\mathcal{S}}_{I}\subset{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{S}}_{I}
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a height-one category and
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects in
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{can}})
. Recall that, for a category
{\mathcal{C}}
, its center is denoted by
Z({\mathcal{C}})
. Define the
saturated model
of
{\mathcal{E}}
by

(21)

\widehat{{\mathcal{E}}}:=\underleftarrow{\textrm{2-lim}}\,\textrm{mod}_{\textrm{fg}}\textrm{--}Z({\mathcal{F}}_{I}),

where the 2-limit in the 2-category of categories is taken over

(22)

\{I\subset{\mathcal{E}}^{1}\,|\,I\textrm{ is finite, }{\mathcal{F}}_{I}\textrm{ is CM-affine}\}.

Theorem 5.5
.

For a normal surface
X
, the saturated model
\widehat{\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}}\nolimits(X)}
is equivalent to
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\widehat{X})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
,
\widehat{X}=\varinjlim\widehat{U}
, where the colimit is taken over (

Appendix A
Abelian envelopes of quasi-abelian categories

We discuss exact categories and their right abelian envelopes. The main example is that of quasi-abelian categories endowed with their canonical exact structures. We recall the notion of cotilting torsion pairs and prove that, via the right abelian envelopes, they correspond to quasi-abelian categories.

A.1.
Exact categories and their right abelian envelopes

Recall

,

]

[

that an

Let

be a Noetherian scheme. Then

Evolution on Sets
Eduardo Santana

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of evolution on sets and study several sets endowed with this structure and obtain some results about this new notion.



1.
Introduction

When we observe the population of human beings, we know that it changes when some people are born or die. We can assume that in every moment there are people that are born and also people that die.

If we denote by
E
the set of human beings and by
E_{n}
the set of people in the
n-th
change, we have the following properties for the sequence
E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{n},\dots
of subsets of people:

(1)

E_{k}=\emptyset\implies E_{k+1}=\emptyset
;

it means that if the human beings are extinguished some day, they remain like that

(2)

E_{n},E_{n+1}\neq\emptyset\implies E_{n}\cap E_{n+1},E_{n}\backslash E_{n+1},E_{n+1}\backslash E_{n}\neq\emptyset
;

it means that from the
n-th
step to the
(n+1)-th
step some people die, some people are born and some people are in commom between them

(3)

e\in E_{n}\cap E_{n+k}\implies e\in E_{n+j},\forall j\leq k
;

it means that if some people live in two steps, then they live in the intermediate steps.

(4)

e\in E\implies\exists\,n,k\in\mathbb{N}\,\,\text{such that}\,\,e\in E_{n},e\not\in E_{n+k}
.

it means that every human being is born at some step and die at some step.

So, motivated by this observation, we introduce the notion of evolution on sets.

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 1
.

Let
E
be a set. A sequence
E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{n},\dots
of subsets of
E
is said to be an evolution on the set
E
if:

(1)

E_{k}=\emptyset\implies E_{k+1}=\emptyset
;

(2)

E_{n},E_{n+1}\neq\emptyset\implies E_{n}\cap E_{n+1},E_{n}\backslash E_{n+1},E_{n+1}\backslash E_{n}\neq\emptyset
;

(3)

e\in E_{n}\cap E_{n+k}\implies e\in E_{n+j},\forall j\leq k
;

(4)

e\in E\implies\exists\,n,k\in\mathbb{N}\,\,\text{such that}\,\,e\in E_{n},e\not\in E_{n+k}
.

We call
E_{k}
the
k-th

stage of the evolution
.

It is easy to see that for every
e\in E
there exists
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
e\in E_{n+j},\forall j\leq k
and
e\not\in E_{i},i\not\in\{n,\dots,n+k\}
.

We intend to interpret the set
E_{k}
as the set of ”focused points” in the set
E
at the moment
k
. Later on, we give examples where this notion is important.

Now we give simple examples of evolution.

Example 1
.

E=\mathbb{N},E_{n}=\{n,\dots,(n+1)^{2}\}
an evolution on the set
\mathbb{N}
.

We observe that the conditions
3,4
implies that for every point in
E
there exist moments when it appears and when it disappears forever. We can define two functions:

A:E\to\mathbb{N}\,\,\text{and}\,\,D:E\to\mathbb{N},

where

A(x)=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}\mid x\in E_{k}\}\,\,\text{and}\,\,D(x)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}\mid x\in E_{k}\}+1.

The following proposition shows that we can obtain an evolution from functions of appearing and disappearing.

Proposition 1
.

Let
E
be a set and

A:E\to\mathbb{N}\,\,\text{and}\,\,D:E\to\mathbb{N}\,\,\text{be surjective functions such that}\,\,A(x)+2\leq D(x),\,\,\forall x\in E.

Then, the sequence of subsets of
E
defined as

E_{k}=\{x\in E\mid A(x)\leq k<D(x)\}

is an evolution on
E
.

Proof.

Since the function
A
is surjective, we have that
E_{k}\neq\emptyset,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
. Also,
E=\displaystyle\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
and
x\in E_{k}
if
k>D(x)
, which shows codition
4
. It remains to show the conditions
2
and
3
.
We have that
x\in E_{k}
if
A(x)\leq k<D(x)
, which shows the condition
3
.
Also,

E_{k}\cap E_{k+1}\supset\{x\in E\mid A(x)=k\}\neq\emptyset

E_{k}\backslash E_{k+1}\supset\{x\in E\mid A(x)\leq k,D(x)=k+1\}\neq\emptyset

E_{k+1}\backslash E_{k}\supset\{x\in E\mid A(x)=k+1\}\neq\emptyset

It shows the condition
2
and completes the proof that the sequence is an evolution.
∎

We call
A
the
appearing function
and
D
the
disappearing function
of the evolution. It establishes a
chronology
on
E
.

Once we have a chronology on a set, we have an evolution. So, it is easy to find several evolutions on a given set. Also, as we can expect, the image of an evolution by a bijection is also an evolution:

Proposition 2
.

Let
f:F\to E
be a surjective function. If
E_{1},\dots,E_{k},\dots
is an evolution on
E
, then
f^{-1}(E_{1}),\dots,f^{-1}(E_{k}),\dots
is an evolution on
F
with the same appearing and disappearing functions.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the sequence
f^{-1}(E_{k})
satisfies the conditions to be an evolution because pre-images preserve intersections and differences of sets.
∎

We say that two sets
E
and
F
with evolutions
E_{k}
and
F_{k}
, respectively, is
isoevolved
if the exists a bijection
f:E\to F
such that
F_{k}=f(E_{k})
.

We say that an evolution
E_{k}
if
reducible
if there exists a subsequence
E_{k_{j}}
that is also an evolution. Otherwise, we say that the evolution is
irreducible
.

3.
Reproduction

Let
E_{*}\subset E
be a set such that
E=M\uplus F
is a decomposition where
M
(
male
) and
F
(
female
) and
E_{*}=M_{*}\uplus F_{*}
with
M_{*}\subset M,F_{*}\subset F
have same cardinality, and we consider
m:M_{*}\to F_{*}
a bijection, that we call
marriage function
and
\rho:E\to F
an arbitrary function, that we call
reproduction function
. We call the pair
(x,m(x))
a
couple
and
\rho^{-1}(m(x))
is the
set of children
of the couple
(x,m(x))
. It is clear that distinct couple have disjoint sets of children.

Remark 1
.

We observe that if the reproduction function is not surjective, then not every couple has a set of children.

Example 2
.

E=E_{*}=\mathbb{N},M=M_{*}=\{\text{odd numbers}\},F=F_{*}=\{\text{even numbers}\},m(x)=x+1,\rho(y)=2\times\pi(y)
, where
\pi(y)
denotes the number of primes less than
y
. If we take
x=9,m(x)=10
and the set of children of the couple
(9,10)
is

\rho^{-1}(10)=\{y\in\mathbb{N}\mid 2\times\pi(y)=10\}=\{y\in\mathbb{N}\mid\pi(y)=5\}=\{12,13,14,15,16\}.

Now, we would like to use the notion of reproduction to construct an evolution.

Proposition 3
.

Given a set
E
, a decomposition on
E
, a marriage function and a reproduction function, there exists an evolution on
E
such that the children of couples in
E_{k}
are in
E_{k+1}\backslash\cup_{i=0}^{k}E_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
\emptyset\neq M_{1}\subset M\backslash\rho^{-1}(F),\emptyset\neq F_{1}\subset F\backslash\rho^{-1}(F),G_{1}=M_{1}\cup F_{1}
and we denote

G_{k+1}=\bigcup_{x\in M_{k}}\rho^{-1}(m(x)),M_{k+1}=G_{k+1}\cap M\backslash\cup_{i=1}^{k}M_{i},F_{k+1}=G_{k+1}\cap F\backslash\cup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}.

We observe that the sequences
M_{k}
and
F_{k}
are composed by elements pairwise disjoints.

We claim that the sequence

E_{k}=M_{k}\cup F_{k}\cup M_{k+1}\cup F_{k+1},E_{0}=E_{1}\cup E\backslash\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}.

is the desired evolution. In fact, let us verify the four conditions:

(1)

If
E_{k}=\emptyset
then
M_{k},F_{k},M_{k+1},F_{k+1}=\emptyset
which implies
G_{k+2}=\emptyset
. Also,
M_{k+2},F_{k+2}=\emptyset
and finally
E_{k+1}=\emptyset
.

(2)

We have that
E_{n}\cap E_{n+1}=M_{n+1}\cup F_{n+1}
,
E_{n}\backslash E_{n+1}=M_{n}\cup F_{n}
and
E_{n+1}\backslash E_{n}=M_{n+2}\cup F_{n+2}
. All these sets are not empty.

(3)

If
e\in E_{n}\cap E_{n+k}
, we have that
k=1
and
E_{n}\cap E_{n+k}\neq\emptyset
and it implies that
k=1
. So,
e\in E_{n}\cap E_{n+j},j\leq k
.

(4)

By definition of
M_{k},F_{k},E_{k}
, it is easy to see that for every
e\in E
there exists
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
e\in E_{n},e\not\in E_{n+k}
.

∎

The previous proposition guarantees that there exists an evolution such that the children do not appear in the evolution before their parents. Moreover, we can not have cycles, which means that an element can not be a descendent of itself. Also, the evolution starts with the element
E_{1}
and is constructed by the reproduction, step by step.

4.
Evolution on Probability Spaces

With the above propositions, we know that there are several evolutions on a given set and we may ask what is the ”best” evolution on a set. If the set is a probability space, we could try to find an evolution for which all stages has positive measure.

Proposition 4
.

If
E
is a set with a probability
\mu
, such that there exists a sequence of two by two disjoint measurable subsets
G_{k}\subset E
with
\mu(G_{k})>0,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
and a measurable bijection
f:E\backslash\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}G_{k}\to E
, then there exists an evolution
E_{k}
on
E
such that
\mu(E_{k})>0,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
F_{k}
be an evolution on
E
. It is enough to show that
E_{k}=f^{-1}(F_{k})\uplus G_{k}
is an evolution on
E
. It follows from the fact that the sequence
f^{-1}(F_{k})
is an evolution on
E\backslash\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}G_{k}
(by the previous proposition). In fact, we have
E_{k}\neq\emptyset,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
because
\mu(G_{k})>0
. Also, we have

E_{k}\cap E_{k+1}=f^{-1}(F_{k})\cap f^{-1}(F_{k+1})\neq\emptyset\,\,\text{because}\,\,G_{k}\cap G_{k+1}=\emptyset,

E_{k}\backslash E_{k+1}=(f^{-1}(F_{k})\backslash f^{-1}(F_{k+1}))\cup G_{k}\neq\emptyset\,\,\text{because}\,\,G_{k}\cap G_{k+1}=\emptyset,

E_{k+1}\backslash E_{k}=(f^{-1}(F_{k+1})\backslash f^{-1}(F_{k}))\cup G_{k+1}\neq\emptyset\,\,\text{because}\,\,G_{k}\cap G_{k+1}=\emptyset,

Moreover,

e\in E_{k}\cap E_{k+n}\implies e\in f^{-1}(F_{k})\cap f^{-1}(F_{k+n})\implies e\in f^{-1}(F_{k})\cap f^{-1}(F_{k+j})\subset E_{k}\cap E_{k+j},\forall\,j\leq n

Finally,

E=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}\,\,\text{and}\,\,e\in E\implies\exists\,i\in\mathbb{N}\,\,\text{such that}\,\,e\not\in E_{i}.

It completes the proof that
E_{k}
is an evolution. Also,
\mu(E_{k})=\mu(f^{-1}(F_{k})\uplus G_{k})\geq\mu(G_{k})>0,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Let us assume that
E
is a measurable space, the sets
E_{k}
are measurable and
\mu
is a probability on
E
. Let
\phi_{k}:E\to\mathbb{R}
be a sequence of measurable functions. We intend to investigate the following limit:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi_{k}d\mu.

If
\phi_{k}\equiv\phi
, we could look for the value of

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi d\mu.

In particular, if
\phi\equiv 1
, then the value of

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(E_{k}).

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mu
be a probability on
E
and the sequence
E_{k}
of subsets of
E
an evolution. If there exists a sequence
d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
D(x)-A(x)\leq d_{k},\forall x\in E_{k},\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(E_{k})=0.

Proof.

If this limit is not equal to zero, there exist
\epsilon>0
and a subsequence
\mu(E_{k_{j}})\geq\epsilon
.
We have that
D(x)\leq A(x)+d_{k}\leq k+d_{k},\forall x\in E_{k}
. So,
E_{k_{j}}\cap E_{k_{j}+d_{k_{j}}+1}=\emptyset\implies E_{k_{j}}\cap E_{n}=\emptyset,\forall n\geq k_{j}+d_{k_{j}}+1
. It means that there exists a subsequence
E_{k_{j_{i}}}
two by two disjoint. This is a contradiction because we would have

\mu\bigg(\biguplus_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{k_{j_{i}}}\bigg)=\infty.

∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mu
be a probability on
E
,
E_{k}
an evolution on
E
and
\phi_{k}:E\to\mathbb{R}
a sequence of measurable functions. If there exist
C>0
and a sequence
d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
D(x)-A(x)\leq d_{k}
and
\mid\phi_{k}(x)\mid\leq C,\forall x\in E_{k},\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi_{k}d\mu=0.

Proof.

In fact, we have that

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}-Cd\mu\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi_{k}d\mu\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}Cd\mu=0,

because
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(E_{k})=0
by the previous proposition.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mu
be a probability on
E
,
E_{k}
an evolution on
E
and
\phi_{k}:E\to\mathbb{R}
a sequence of measurable functions. If there exists a sequence
d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
D(x)-A(x)\leq d_{k}
and we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi_{k}d\mu=L\neq 0,

then the following holds

\displaystyle\text{either}\,\,\limsup_{k}\bigg(\sup_{E_{k}}\phi_{k}\bigg)=+\infty\,\,\text{or}\,\,\liminf_{k}\bigg(\inf_{E_{k}}\phi_{k}\bigg)=-\infty

Proof.

It follows from the previous corollary because, otherwise, there would exist
C>0
such that
\mid\phi_{k}\mid<C
on
E_{k}
.
∎

Given an evolution
E_{k}
on
E
, we say that the sequence of functions
\phi_{k}:E\to\mathbb{R}
converges to
\phi:E\to\mathbb{R}

by the evolution

E_{k}
if

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi_{k}d\mu=\int_{E}\phi d\mu.

In particular, we intend to investigate when we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi d\mu=\int_{E}\phi d\mu.

or at least to know if there exists an evolution such that it holds. We already know that
\phi
must have both positive and negative values and be unbounded (unless
\int_{E}\phi d\mu=0
), since
\mu(E_{k})\to 0
under ”good” conditions.

Question: when
\phi_{k}
converges to
\phi
by all evolutions on
E
with positive measure stages?

Proposition 6
.

Let
E
be connected and
\phi:E\to\mathbb{R}
continuous, unbounded and assuming positive and negative values. There exists an evolution
E_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\phi d\mu=\int_{E}\phi d\mu.

Proof.

∎

5.
Evolution on Vector Spaces

Let
E
be an infinite dimensional vector space and fix a countable linearly independent subset
B=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k},\dots\}
. We can use evolutions on
\mathbb{N}
to obtain evolutions on a subset of
E
. Given
F\subset\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
[F]
the subspace generated by the vectors in
B
with index in
F
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
F_{k}
be an evolution on
\mathbb{N}
. Then the sequence
E_{k}=[F_{k}]
of subspaces of
E
is an evolution on
E^{\prime}=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}
with the same appearing and disappearing functions. If
E^{\prime}
is a subspace, then
F_{1}=\mathbb{N},F_{k}=\emptyset,k>2
.

Proof.

Firstly,

E_{k}=\emptyset\implies F_{k}=\emptyset\implies F_{k+1}=\emptyset\implies E_{k+1}=\emptyset.

Now, we have

E_{k}\cap E_{k+1}=[F_{k}\cap F_{k+1}]\neq\emptyset\,\,\text{because}\,\,F_{k}\cap F_{k+1}\neq\emptyset,

E_{k}\backslash E_{k+1}\supset[F_{k}\backslash F_{k+1}]\neq\emptyset\,\,\text{because}\,\,F_{k}\backslash F_{k+1}\neq\emptyset,

E_{k+1}\backslash E_{k}\supset[F_{k+1}\backslash F_{k}]\neq\emptyset\,\,\text{because}\,\,F_{k+1}\backslash F_{k}\neq\emptyset,

Also,

e\in E_{k}\cap E_{k+n}\implies e\in[F_{k}\cap F_{k+n}]\implies e\in[F_{k}\cap F_{k+j}]\,\forall\,j\leq n\implies e\in E_{k}\cap E_{k+j}\,\forall\,j\leq n.

Finally,

e\in E^{\prime}\implies\exists\,\,F_{i}\,\,\text{such that}\,\,e\not\in[F_{i}]=E_{i}.

∎

Proposition 8
.

Let
E
be a vector space and
f:E\to\mathbb{R}
a non trivial linear functional. If
F_{k}
is an evolution on
\mathbb{R}
, then
E_{k}=f^{-1}(F_{k})
is an evolution on
E
with the same appearing and disappearing functions. Each stage
E_{k}
is a union of hyperplanes.

Proof.

It follows from the fact that
f^{-1}(t)
is a hyperplane in
E
and that a non trivial linear functional is always surjective.
∎

Let
M_{n\times n}
be the space of
n\times n
matrices. Given
A\in M_{n\times n}
,
\det A
denotes the determinant of
A
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
F_{k}
be an evolution on
\mathbb{R}
. The following sequence is an evolution on
M_{n\times n}
:

E_{k}=\{A\in M_{n\times n}\mid\det A\in F_{k}\}.

Proof.

It follows from the fact that the function
\det:M_{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
is surjective.
∎

Proposition 10
.

Let
E
be a vector space with inner product
\langle\,,\rangle
and
F_{k}
be an evolution on
\mathbb{R}
. Fix
e_{0}\in E
. The following sequence is an evolution on
E
:

E_{k}=\{e\in E\mid\langle e,e_{0}\rangle\in F_{k}\}.

Proof.

It follows from the fact that the function
\langle\cdot,e_{0}\rangle:E\to\mathbb{R}
is surjective.
∎

If
E
is a normed vector space, we can find an evolution on
E
from the metric derived from the norm, as we will see in the next section.

6.
Evolution on Metric Spaces and Manifolds

If
E
is endowed with a metric
d
, we can obtain an evolution on
E
from an evolution on
\mathbb{N}
or
[0,\infty)
.

Proposition 11
.

Let
E
be a metric space, fix
E_{0}\subset E
and let
F_{k}
be an evolution on
[0,\infty)
. Let

\displaystyle E_{k}=\{e\in E\mid d(e,E_{0})\in F_{k}\}.

Then, the sequence
E_{k}
is an evolution on
E
with the same appearing and disappearing functions.

Proof.

We have that
E_{k}=f^{-1}(F_{k})
, where
f(e)=d(e,E_{0})
, which is a surjective function
f:E\to[0,\infty)
.
∎

Corollary 3
.

Let
E
be a metric space and fix
e_{0}\in E
and let
F_{k}
be an evolution on
[0,\infty)
. Let

\displaystyle E_{k}=\{e\in E\mid d(e,e_{0})\in F_{k}\}.

Then, the sequence
E_{k}
is an evolution on
E
with the same appearing and disappearing functions.

Proof.

It follows from the previous proposition, by taking
E_{0}=\{e_{0}\}
.
∎

Corollary 4
.

Fix
e_{0}\in E
and let
F_{k}
be an evolution on
\mathbb{N}
. Let

\displaystyle E_{k}=\bigcup_{n\in F_{k}}\{e\in E\mid n\leq d(e,e_{0})\leq n+1\}.

Then, the sequence
E_{k}
is an evolution on
E
with the same appearing and disappearing functions.

Proof.

It follows from the previous corollary, by taking
G_{k}=\cup_{j\in F_{k}}[j,j+1],k\geq 0
.
∎

Given a differentiable manifold
M
which is Hausdorff and with countable basis, we can endow
M
with a riemannian metric and
M
is also a metric space.

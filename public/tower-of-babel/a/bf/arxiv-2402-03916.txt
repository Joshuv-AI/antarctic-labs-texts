Can Large Language Models Detect Rumors on Social Media?
Qiang Liu, Xiang Tao, Junfei Wu, Shu Wu, Liang Wang

Abstract
In this work, we investigate to use Large Language Models (LLMs) for rumor detection on social media. However, it is challenging for LLMs to reason over the entire propagation information on social media, which contains news contents and numerous comments, due to LLMs may not concentrate on key clues in the complex propagation information, and have trouble in reasoning when facing massive and redundant information. Accordingly, we propose an LLM-empowered Rumor Detection (LeRuD) approach, in which we design prompts to teach LLMs to reason over important clues in news and comments, and divide the entire propagation information into a Chain-of-Propagation for reducing LLMs’ burden. We conduct extensive experiments on the Twitter and Weibo datasets, and LeRuD outperforms several state-of-the-art rumor detection models by 3.2 % 3.2\% to 7.7 % 7.7\% . Meanwhile, by applying LLMs, LeRuD requires no data for training, and thus shows more promising rumor detection ability in few-shot or zero-shot scenarios.

Introduction

With the development of social media, users can access to information more easily, and messages can be spread more rapidly.
However, at the same time, rumors can also be spread to the public more easily and widely.
It is necessary to conduct research on automatic rumor detection on social media
Castillo et al. 2011
;
Islam et al. 2020
, which is still very challenging.
Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs)
Zhao et al. 2023
have achieved success and applied widely.
This brings great convenience to people.
However, people can also use LLMs to intentionally generate great amount of unidentified information
Vykopal et al. 2023
;
Chen and Shu 2024
, which aggravate the propagation situation of rumor on social media.
Thus, the success of LLMs makes automatic rumor detection more challenging and urgent.

The key of rumor detection lies in the modeling of propagation patterns on social media, i.e., aggregating the features of original news and user comments.
Early approaches regards news and comments as sequences of texts, and applies recurrent networks
Ma et al. 2016
, convolutional networks
Yu et al. 2017
or attention networks
Liu et al. 2018
for feature extraction.
Then, rumor detection models based on graph neural networks
Lu and Li 2020
;
Bian et al. 2020
have been widely proposed and achieved state-of-the-art performances.

Recently, some research works attempt to apply LLMs in identifying the veracity of information
Chen and Shu 2023
.
They mostly use LLMs to extract knowledge from texts
Yang et al. 2023
or solely analyzing the news contents
Hu et al. 2024
.
The power of LLMs for analyzing the propagation information, which contains both news and comments, on social media has not been explored.
Meanwhile, as introduced by
Hu et al. 2024
;
Chen and Shu 2024
, solely relying on textual features of news, it is hard to obtain promising detection results by LLMs.
This requires us to investigate the ability of LLMs for modeling the entire propagation information on social media.

However, using LLMs for modeling the propagation information is not easy.
Firstly, we find LLMs can not concentrate on the key clues in the propagation information, and thus may produce wrong predictions (Sec.
3.2
and
3.3
).
Secondly, the amount of comments is usually large, which brings great burden for LLMs to reason (Sec.
3.4

;
Xie 2023

To teach LLMs to conduct reasoning over propagation information and overcome above shortcomings, we propose an

Firstly, we draw experience from previous rumor detection models

We design proper prompts to teach LLMs to concentrate on the writing styles and commonsense mistakes of news, as well as rebuttals or conflicts in comments.
Secondly, we divide the propagation information into a Chain-of-Propagation, which enables LLMs to reason step-by-step in time order with a reasonable number of comments.

Then, to evaluate the effectiveness of the LeRuD approach on detecting rumors, we conduct experiments on the Twitter and Weibo datasets (Sec.

Our main contributions can be listed as follows:

•

We propose to apply LLMs in detecting rumors on social media with the entire propagation information, which contains both news and comments.

•

We design proper prompts to teach LLMs to concentrate on the key clues in news and comments, which enable LLMs to conduct reasoning from multiple views.

•

We divide the propagation information of a piece of news into a Chain-of-Propagation, which enables LLMs to conduct reasoning step-by-step with reasonable amount of information in each time.

•

We conduct extensive experiments, in which several state-of-the-art rumor detection models are compared. Results demonstrate the superiority of our LeRuD approach.

Related Work

Rumor detection on social media is a major text mining task for automatically distinguishing untrustworthy information and maintaining the healthy development of the internet and society.
Extensive works have been done to detect rumors from different perspectives
Castillo et al. 2011
;
Islam et al. 2020
.

Early attempts on rumor detection mainly focus on extracting statistical features of the propagation process
Kwon et al. 2013
;
Ma et al. 2015
.
Then, deep neural networks for modeling sequential data have been widely applied in aggregating propagation information and detecting rumors on social media.
Among them, detection models based on recurrent neural networks
Ma et al. 2016
;
Ma et al. 2018
, convolutional neural networks
Yu et al. 2017
, attentive networks
Liu et al. 2018
and generative
adversarial networks
Ma et al. 2019
have been extensively studied.
Meanwhile, their combinations have also been investigated for rumor detection
Liu and Wu 2018
;
Yu et al. 2019
.
Recently, detection models based on graph neural networks
Bian et al. 2020
;
Lu and Li 2020
;
Nguyen et al. 2020
;
Xu et al. 2022
have achieved state-of-the-art performances.
Furthermore, some works investigate incorporate graph contrastive learning for better rumor detection performances
Sun et al. 2022b
;
Wu et al. 2023
.
For better interpretability, reasoning over subgraph has been proposed
Jin et al. 2022
;
Yang et al. 2022
.
Meanwhile, for capturing temporal characteristics, dynamic graph has been constructed and applied in rumor detection
Sun et al. 2022a
.
Nowadays, considering practical applications, rumor detection in debiasing, few-shot or zero-shot scenarios have drawn much attention
Zhu et al. 2022
;
Xu et al. 2023
;
Yue et al. 2023
;
Lin et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2023
.

As LLMs have achieved great success, they also bring opportunities and challenges to misinformation identification
Chen and Shu 2023
.
LLMs are able to easily generate misinformation
Vykopal et al. 2023
;
Chen and Shu 2024
, which is hard to be distinguished by human or LLMs solely based on textual features
Chen and Shu 2024
.
CICAN
Yang et al. 2023
uses GPT 3.5 as an auxiliary tool, for extracting entities from news and comments, and constructing relational graph.
ARG
Hu et al. 2024
uses LLMs for analyzing the news contents, and conclude that, LLMs are hard to beat fine-tuned small models on detecting fake news.
Thus, ARG incorporates insights generated by LLMs in small models for fake news detection.

3
Methodology

In this section, we detail our proposed LeRuD approach, which consists of prompts for reasoning over news and comments, as well as the Chain-of-Propagation reasoning process.
The whole procedure of LeRuD is demonstrated in Fig.
3
.

3.1
Problem Formulation

For a piece of news spreading on social media, we denote the news content as
n
, which is a paragraph of text.
During spreading, the news receives comments, denoted as
C=\{(c_{1},t_{1}),(c_{2},t_{2}),......\}
, in which
c_{i}
denotes the textual content of the
i
-th comment, and
t_{i}
means the relative posting time compared with the original news.
We aim to utilize LLMs for modeling both the news
n
and comments
C
, and detecting rumors, i.e., identifying whether the news is fake or real.

3.2
RP: Rational Prompts for News

We need design proper prompts to teach LLMs for analyzing news contents for rumor detection.
Here, we apply the widely-used GPT 3.5 as the LLM used in our proposed approach.
As shown in the two cases in Tab.
1
, we try the vanilla prompts, which directly ask the LLM to infer the credibility of the news contents.
However, without proper prompts, it is hard for the LLM to make guesses about veracity.
The LLM does not know how to reason over the content that it has no knowledge about the described fact.
This is mainly because that, the LLM can not concentrate on some key clues in the news contents.

Accordingly to some previous study, writing styles of news are highlighted for misinformation identification
Przybyla 2020
, and commonsense reasoning is essential for distinguish fake news
Hu et al. 2024
.
Thus, we need to teach LLMs to reason from these aspect.
Accordingly, we design
Rational Prompts
, which ask the LLM to answer “
Based on the writing style and the commonsense knowledge, estimate the credibility of the news.
”
As shown in Tab.
1
, with Rational Prompts, the LLM is able to find important clues in writing details and conduct commonsense reasoning, and give judgement on the veracity of news contents.
In the first case, the LLM correctly predicts the news is fake, by noticing that the event is not plausible from a commonsense perspective.
In the second case, the LLM correctly predicts the news is real, via noticing some accident details, and concluding that the writing style is consistent with typical reports.

3.3
CP: Conflicting Prompts for Comments

Furthermore, we need to design prompts to teach LLMs to reason over comments in propagation.
As shown in Tab.
2
, we try the vanilla prompts, which directly ask the LLM to verify the authenticity of news based on given comments.
However, with such prompts, the LLM tends to predict the news is fake when there lacks clear supporting materials.
In most cases, is it hard to have enough clear evidences, especially in the early stage of propagation.
This makes it easy for the LLM to mistakenly recognize real news as fake news.

Conflicts, rebuttals and skepticism in comments have been proven to be key features for distinguish misinformation
Ma et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2018
;
Ma et al. 2018
;
Lu and Li 2020
;
Yu et al. 2017
;
Yang et al. 2022
;
Jin et al. 2022
.
Considering the difficulty of having sufficient supporting materials, we design
Conflicting Prompts
, which ask the LLM to answer “
Based on the comments, analyze whether there are any rebuttals or conflicts, and accordingly verify the authenticity of the news.
”
As shown in Tab.
2
, with Conflicting Prompts, the LLM correctly predicts the news is real, via noticing that people are all discussing about the event itself, and no conflicting information is posted.

3.4
CoP: Chain-of-Propagation

Though Rational and Conflicting Prompts can teach LLMs to reason for rumor detection, LLMs still have trouble when facing too many comments.
In real social media scenarios, the number of comments is usually very large.
However, LLMs usually have limitations in input length.
And more seriously, LLMs can not well reason over long contexts or redundant information
Huang et al. 2023
;
Xie 2023
.
We test above Rational and Conflicting Prompts with GPT 3.5 on the Twitter and Weibo datasets (Sec.
4.1
), and results are shown in Fig.
2
.
When the input length of a sample exceeds the limit, we randomly select part comments belonging to the sample as input.
It is clear that, when a sample has too many comments, the detection accuracy drops sharply, which means the LLM can not well reason over excessive information.

In recent research of LLMs, a complex task, which is hard for LLMs, is divided into a series of easy tasks for LLMs to execute
Wei et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2023
;
Yao et al. 2023
;
Besta et al. 2024
.
This enhances LLMs’ ability for complex tasks.
Thus, we propose to divide the proposition information into a
Chain-of-Propagation (CoP)
, which enables LLMs to conduct reasoning more easily.
Considering comments in a time period usually share similar attitudes, we construct CoP in order of comments’ posting time, and select
k
comments in each reasoning step.
As shown in Fig.
2
, the LLM can deal with up to
100
comments easily, so we set
k=100
.
Reasoning steps in a CoP are run in the same session of LLM, so that latter reasoning steps can refer to the results of former reasoning steps.
Moreover, we use the result in the last step as the final estimation, considering the last step aggregates all the information.

Experiments

In this section, we conduct extensive experiments, to verify whether LLMs can detect rumors on social media with our efforts.

4.1
Data Preparation

Firstly, we introduce the data preparation process, which enables us to conduct sufficient evaluation.

Datasets
: For evaluation, we combine Twitter15 and Twitter16 as the
Twitter
dataset
Ma et al. 2017
, and also incorporate the
Weibo
dataset
Ma et al. 2018
.
They are collected from two popular social media platforms, i.e., Twitter and Weibo.
The average numbers of comments of each news in Twitter and Weibo are
232
and
816
respectively.
We only use the verified false rumors (fake news) and non-rumors (real news) in Twitter.

Data Filtering
: For smooth and low-cost usage of GPT 3.5, we filter samples with
ethics issues
(such as violence, pornography and discrimination) and
repeated news contents
.
We also filter samples containing mainly
subjective expressions
, for they are not factually real or fake.
Meanwhile, for fair comparison, we need to filter samples with
data leakage risks
raised by the usage of LLMs, in which GPT 3.5 already exactly knows about the facts in the news.
Thus, we direly ask GPT 3.5 to verify the veracity of each news with the Vanilla Prompts in Tab.
1
, and only keep samples in which GPT 3.5 has no idea about the facts and can not give answers.
In contrast, when GPT 3.5 knows about the fact in a piece of news, it will detail the fact and clearly judge the veracity.

Settings

Baselines

Metrics

Zero-shot Setting

4.3
Performance Comparison

In Tab.

Among the three graph-based detection models, DDGCN and GACL obviously outperform BIGCN.
With the help of GPT 3.5 for extracting entities, CICAN achieves similar or better performances compared to the graph-based models.
ARG considers only news contents, so it underperforms other detection models which capture both news and comments.
However, with insights generated by LLMs, ARG still achieves high accuracy of about
90\%
.
Meanwhile, our proposed LeRuD approach achieves the best performances on both dataset evaluated by all metrics.
Specifically, LeRuD outperforms the best baselines on Twitter and Weibo by

and
3.95\%
respectively.
These results strongly demonstrate the superiority of LeRuD.
Moreover,
LeRuD requires no data for training
, so it can consistently achieve promising rumor detection performances in few-shot or zero-shot scenarios, and thus is more suitable for real-world rumor detection.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate to use LLMs for rumor detection on social media, and propose a novel approach called LeRuD.
LeRuD designs proper prompts to teach LLMs to reason over important clues in news and comments.
Meanwhile, we divide the entire propagation information of a piece of news into a Chain-of-Propagation for leading LLMs to conduct reasoning step-by-step with reasonable amount of information in each time.
Then, we implement LeRud with the widely-used GPT 3.5, and conduct extensive experiments on the Twitter and Weibo datasets.
LeRud can outperform several state-of-the-art rumor detection models with large margins.
In summary, with our efforts, LLMs can well detect rumors on social media.

Limitations

In this paper, though we have taught LLMs to detect rumors on social media, our work still has limitations in the following two aspects.
Firstly, running LLMs for rumor detection requires relatively high costs, such as the usage of high-performance GPUs, or the calls of APIs, or inputs of web interfaces.
Secondly, graph neural networks have great ability for modeling structural data, while structural data is hard to be well described in texts.
Thus, using LLMs for analyzing complex propagation structures, or combining the abilities of LLMs and graph neural networks, has not been explored.
In the future, we shall put more efforts to overcome above limitations.

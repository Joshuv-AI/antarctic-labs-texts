Perverse filtrations and Fourier transforms
Davesh Maulik, Junliang Shen, Qizheng Yin

Abstract
. We study the interaction between Fourier–Mukai transforms and perverse filtrations for a certain class of dualizable abelian fibrations. Multiplicativity of the perverse filtration and the “Perverse ⊃ \supset Chern” phenomenon for these abelian fibrations are immediate consequences of our theory. We also show that our class of fibrations include families of compactified Jacobians of integral locally planar curves. Applications include the following: (a) we prove the motivic decomposition conjecture for this class (including compactified Jacobian fibrations), which generalizes Deninger–Murre’s theorem for abelian schemes; (b) we provide a new proof of the P = W P=W conjecture for GL r \mathrm{GL}_{r} ; (c) we prove half of the P = C P=C conjecture concerning refined BPS invariants for the local ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} ; (d) we show that the perverse filtration for the compactified Jacobian associated with an integral locally planar curve is multiplicative, which generalizes a result of Oblomkov–Yun for homogeneous singularities. Our techniques combine Arinkin’s autoduality for coherent categories, Ngô’s support theorem for the decomposition theorem, Adams operations in operational K K -theory, and Corti–Hanamura’s theory of relative Chow motives.

Introduction

0.1.
Overview

Throughout, we work over the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
. For a proper morphism
f:X\to Y
between nonsingular varieties, the perverse truncation functor
[
8
]
filters the derived direct image
Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\in D^{b}_{c}(Y)
, which yields an increasing filtration on the cohomology either for the total space
X
or for a closed fiber. Such a filtration is called the perverse filtration, which encodes important topological invariants of the map
f:X\to Y
.

In recent years, perverse filtrations have played a crucial role in the study of integrable systems, enumerative geometry, and geometric representation theory. For many interesting abelian fibrations (
e.g.
Hitchin systems), the associated perverse filtration is discovered to satisfy the following two mysterious properties:

(i)

it is
multiplicative
with respect to the cup-product, and

(ii)

the location of a tautological class in the filtration is determined by its
Chern grading
of the universal family.

Indeed, the
P=W
conjecture of de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini for
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
is equivalent to (i, ii) for the
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
-Hitchin system
[
12
,
13
]
, and was proven recently using special properties of Hitchin moduli spaces
[
38
,
26
]
. On the other hand, similar phenomena have also been found or conjectured for Lagrangian fibrations associated with compact hyper-Kähler manifolds
[
51
,
13
]
, compactified Jacobians associated with torus knots
[
47
,
48
]
, and moduli of
1
-dimensional stable sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

[
32
]
, and one would like to understand this beyond the Hitchin setting. Note that, for general proper morphisms, the perverse filtration will typically not be multiplicative even if the singularities of the fibers are mild
[
11
, Exercise 5.6.8]
.

The purpose of this paper is to systematically study the perverse filtrations associated with abelian fibrations, and give a uniform explanation for (i, ii) that applies in different geometric settings. We show that both (i) and (ii) are essentially consequences of the
duality
between derived categories of coherent sheaves and the
support theorem
in the decomposition theorem package. Our work also shows the motivic nature of the decomposition theorem associated with abelian fibrations.

0.2.
Dualizable abelian fibrations

In Section
1.4
, we introduce a class of proper morphisms, called
dualizable abelian fibrations
. They are modelled on Hitchin systems and compactified Jacobian fibrations.

Roughly, for nonsingular varieties
M
and
B
, we say that a proper morphism
\pi:M\to B
is a dualizable abelian fibration if it is an abelian fibration with a dual fibration
\pi^{\vee}:M^{\vee}\to B
, satisfying the following properties:

(a)

(Duality) the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
M
and
M^{\vee}
are related by Fourier–Mukai transforms with certain nice properties similar to those of dual abelian schemes;

(b)

(Support) every simple perverse summand in the decomposition theorem for
R\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}
has full support
B
.

We refer to Section
1.4
for more precise statements concerning (a, b). The Chern character of the Fourier–Mukai kernel of (a) induces a Fourier transform in cohomology:

\mathfrak{F}=\sum_{k}\mathfrak{F}_{k}:H^{*}(M^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\mathfrak{F}_{k}(H^{d}(M^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}}))\subset H^{d+2k-2g}(M,{\mathbb{Q}}),

where
g
is the relative dimension
\dim M-\dim B
. The Fourier inverse
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}=\sum_{k}{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{k}
behaves similarly. In order for the Fourier operators to have interesting homological consequences, we further impose a vanishing condition on the relative product
M^{\vee}\times_{B}M^{\vee}
. We say that a dualizable abelian fibration as above satisfies
the Fourier vanishing (FV)
, if

(FV)

\quad\quad{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{j}=0,\quad i+j<2g.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
\pi:M\to B
be a dualizable abelian fibration satisfying (FV). Then

(i)

(Multiplicativity) the perverse filtration associated with
\pi
is multiplicative,
i.e.
,

P_{k}H^{d}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})\times P_{k^{\prime}}H^{d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{~~\cup~~}P_{k+k^{\prime}}H^{d+d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{Q}});

(ii)

(Perverse
\supset
Chern
1
1

1

The term “Perverse
\supset
Chern” here means that the perversity (see Section
1.3.3
for the definition of perversity) of a class
\mathfrak{F}_{k}(\alpha)
is bounded by the Chern grading
k
associated with the Fourier transform.
) for any class
\alpha\in H^{*}(M^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}})
, we have

\mathfrak{F}_{k}(\alpha)\in P_{k}H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}}).

In fact, we prove a stronger version of Theorem
0.1
which is of a motivic nature and specializes to sheaf-theoretic statements; see Theorem
2.6
.

Before discussing applications, we make some remarks on the condition (FV). For an abelian scheme
\pi:A\to B
with its dual
\pi^{\vee}:A^{\vee}\to B
, the Fourier transform
{\mathfrak{F}}
was first considered by Beauville
[
7
]
. It induces a canonical (motivic) decomposition, called the
Beauville decomposition
, of the cohomology or the Chow ring of the total space
A
; see Section
1.2
for more discussions on this construction. Once we obtain the Beauville decomposition, the statements (i, ii) above for the abelian scheme
\pi:A\to B
are immediate consequences. A key step in Beauville’s theory is the observation that the operators
{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}
provide
projectors
, which follows from the vanishing

{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{j}=0,\quad i+j\neq 2g.

Clearly, this vanishing is stronger than (FV) in the case of abelian schemes. The reason why we do not pose this stronger condition is that it is hard to verify when the abelian fibration has singular fibers, and furthermore, it is unclear if the vanishing for
i+j>2g
holds in general.

On the other hand, the weaker condition (FV) is already sufficient to deduce the desired properties (i, ii) of Theorem
0.1
. Moreover, the condition (FV) is natural: in view of Arinkin’s work
[
5
,
6
]
, we will explain in Section
3
that (FV) holds essentially for the
same reason
as the fact that the normalized Poincaré line bundle and the Fourier–Mukai transforms can be extended over the singular fibers for certain abelian fibrations. The following theorem is our main source of applications.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
C\to B
be a flat family of integral projective curves which admits a section.
If the associated compactified Jacobian fibration
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
satisfies that

(i)

every curve in the family has at worst planar singularities, and

(ii)

the total space
\overline{J}_{C}
is nonsingular,

then
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
is a (self-)dualizable abelian fibration which satisfies (FV).

By promoting the Fourier theory from schemes to gerbes, we also prove a version of Theorems
0.1
and
0.2
(as well as Theorem
0.3
below) for certain twisted compactified Jacobian fibrations which are associated with families of curves admitting no section; see Corollaries
4.6
and
4.7
.

0.3.2.
The
P=W
conjecture

One of our motivations and applications comes from the
P=W
conjecture in non-abelian Hodge theory, which we briefly review.

For a projective curve
\Sigma
of genus
g\geq 2
and two coprime integers
r,n
, the non-abelian Hodge correspondence yields a diffeomorphism between the Dolbeault moduli space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
which parameterizes rank
r
and degree
n
stable Higgs bundles on
\Sigma
, and the Betti moduli space
M_{B}
which is the corresponding character variety. This further induces

(1)

H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}}).

In 2010, de Cataldo, Hausel, and Migliorini
[
12
]
proposed a relationship between the topology of the Hitchin system
h:M_{\mathrm{Dol}}\to B
and the Hodge theory of
M_{B}
via (
1
); more precisely, they conjectured that the perverse filtration associated with the Hitchin system is matched with the double indexed weight filtration for the character variety:

(2)

\text{``}P=W\text{''},\quad P_{k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=W_{2k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{B}},{\mathbb{Q}}).

This conjecture, known as the
P=W
conjecture, has now been proven by the first two authors
[
38
]
and Hausel–Mellit–Minets–Schiffmann
[
26
]
independently via different methods.

Theorem 0.4

(
[
38
,
26
]
)
.

The
P=W
conjecture (
2
) holds.

As we sketch now, our work in this paper provides a new proof of Theorem
0.4
.

The
P=W
conjecture can be decomposed into three identities:

(3)

P_{k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=C_{k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=C_{k}H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}})=W_{2k}H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}});

Here
C_{\bullet}
stands for the Chern filtration defined via the tautological classes of the moduli space.

The second and third identities have been established earlier by work of Markman
[
35
]
Hausel–Thaddeus
[
27
]
and Shende
[
52
]
respectively. As a result,
the
P=W
conjecture is reduced to the first identity — the “Perverse = Chern” phenomenon for the Hitchin system.
Indeed, both existing proofs
[
38
,
26
]
proceed by establishing the first identity using special features of the moduli space of Higgs bundles.

Moreover, in
[
41
]
Mellit proved the curious Hard Lefschetz for the character variety
M_{B}
, which further reduces the full
P=W
(
2
) to

(4)

P_{k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\supset C_{k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}}).

The present work grew out of an attempt to understand the geometric nature of (
4
). The surprising interaction between the perverse filtration and the Chern classes predicts the existence of a good theory of Fourier transforms for certain abelian fibrations with singular fibers. The discovery of an analogous conjecture for the
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
geometry (see Section
0.3.3
) further suggests that (
4
) should be a phenomenon beyond Hitchin systems. This leads us to Theorem
0.1
.

Conversely, we will show that (
4
) is a consequence of our Fourier theory; in combination with
an argument from
[
26
]
to reduce to integral spectral curves, as we explain in Section
5.4
, this yields Theorem
0.4
.

Remark 0.5
.

The above proof of
P=W
suggests that, over the locus of integral spectral curves, (
4
) is a property of compactified Jacobian fibrations associated with curves with planar singularities and does not rely on the representation theory perspectives of
[
38
,
26
]
. This approach gives a possible avenue for studying
P=W
phenomena beyond the Higgs setting. More precisely, for any dualizable abelian fibration as in Theorem
0.1
we may define the Chern filtration
C_{k}H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
as the span of the classes

{\mathfrak{F}}_{j}(\alpha)\in H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad j\leq k,\quad\alpha\in H^{*}(M^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}}).

This generalizes the Chern filtration defined in the
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
case, and does not rely on Markman’s generation result
[
35
]
. Therefore, in view of (
3
), the
P=W
phenomenon is reduced to understanding the interaction between the Fourier transform and the weight filtration on the Betti side. Speculatively, for other Hitchin moduli spaces, this interaction may be related to the Betti geometric Langlands correspondence.

0.3.3.
Enumerative geometry of local
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

A phenomenon similar to (
4

[

for the refined BPS invariants of local
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

Fix coprime integers

with

is the hyperplane class. The moduli space

admits a proper Hilbert–Chow morphism

where

stands for the Fitting support. This proper map induces a perverse filtration on the cohomology of

(5)

\text{``}P=C\text{''},\quad P_{k}H^{\leq 2(r-2)}(M_{r,\chi},{\mathbb{Q}})=C_{k}H^{\leq 2(r-2)}(M_{r,\chi},{\mathbb{Q}}).

0.3.4.
Compactified Jacobians

For an integral projective curve
C_{0}
with planar singularities, the compactified Jacobian
\overline{J}_{C_{0}}
is an integral projective variety. These varieties behave like local analogues of Hitchin systems; in particular, the singular cohomology
H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})
admits a canonical perverse filtration

(6)

P_{0}H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset P_{1}H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\cdots\subset H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})

by
[

,
42
]
. When
C_{0}
is a rational curve with a unique planar singular point, it was proposed by Shende that
\overline{J}_{C_{0}}
should serve as the Dolbeault side of a
P=W
conjecture with some “Betti moduli space” related to the link of the singularity. This proposal was motivated by the connection between algebro-geometric invariants associated with a planar singularity and topological invariants associated with the corresponding link; we refer to
[
36
,
46
,
45
,
53
,
55
,
10
]
for relevant work and recent developments. Such a
P=W
conjecture is wide open to the best of our knowledge; nevertheless, it predicts that the perverse filtration (
6
) has to be multiplicative with respect to the cup-product. For special singularities

with
(p,q)=1

[
47
,
48
]
using tautological generators and representations of the rational Cherednik algebra.

We establish the multiplicativity in full generality as a consequence of the sheaf-theoretic version of Theorem

together with Theorem

. This provides evidence for the

Theorem 0.7
.

For an integral projective curve
C_{0}
with planar singularities, the perverse filtration (

,

P_{k}H^{d}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})\times P_{k^{\prime}}H^{d^{\prime}}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{~~\cup~~}P_{k+k^{\prime}}H^{d+d^{\prime}}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}}).

[

, Rennemo constructed a decomposition

H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{k,d}D_{k}H^{d}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})

1.
Dualizable abelian fibrations

1.1.
Overview

In this section, we first review the Beauville decomposition for an abelian scheme as a motivating example. Then we introduce the notion of dualizable abelian fibrations which is a suitable framework for generalizing Beauville’s theory.

1.2.
The Beauville decomposition

To motivate our method, we start with a brief review of the Beauville decomposition
[
7
,
17
]
. We focus on the homological version for convenience.

Let
\pi:A\to B
be an abelian scheme of relative dimension
g
over a nonsingular base variety
B
. The “multiplication by
N
” map
[N]:A\to A
induces a canonical decomposition of the cohomology

(7)

H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{i}H^{*}_{(i)}(A,{\mathbb{Q}}),

which we call the (homological)
Beauville decomposition
. Here

H^{*}_{(i)}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})=\{\alpha\in H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})\,|\,[N]^{*}\alpha=N^{i}\alpha\}

is an eigenspace of the pullback map
[N]^{*}:H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The decomposition (
7
) is motivic (
i.e.
induced by projectors) and provides a splitting of the Leray filtration
2
2

2

Here
L_{k}H^{d}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})=L^{d-k}H^{d}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
with respect to the usual convention for the Leray filtration.

L_{\bullet}H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
associated with the fibration
\pi:A\to B
, where

L_{k}H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{i\leq k}H^{*}_{(i)}(A,{\mathbb{Q}}).

It is also multiplicative with respect to the cup-product,
i.e.
,

(8)

H^{*}_{(i)}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})\times H^{*}_{(i^{\prime})}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{~~\cup~~}H^{*}_{(i+i^{\prime})}(A,{\mathbb{Q}}).

In fact, since for
\alpha,\beta\in H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
we have
[N]^{*}(\alpha\cup\beta)=[N]^{*}\alpha\cup[N]^{*}\beta
, the multiplicativity follows simply by comparing the eigenvalues. For our purpose, we would like to extend (
7
) to abelian fibrations with singular fibers. We give another description of (
7
) using duality and Fourier transforms.

We consider the dual abelian scheme
\pi^{\vee}:A^{\vee}\to B
, and denote by
{\mathcal{L}}
the normalized Poincaré line bundle on
A^{\vee}\times_{B}A
. Then the correspondence given by the Chern character

\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{L}})=\exp(c_{1}({\mathcal{L}}))

induces the Fourier transform

\mathfrak{F}:H^{*}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\alpha\mapsto p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\alpha\cup\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{L}}))

with
p_{i}
the natural projections from
A^{\vee}\times_{B}A
. The inverse transform
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
is induced by
(-1)^{g}\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})
. Using the Fourier transform
\mathfrak{F}
and its inverse
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
, we obtain a canonical decomposition of the cohomology
H^{*}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
which recovers the decomposition (
7
). Indeed, for any class
\alpha\in H^{d}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
, we have

\mathfrak{F}^{-1}(\alpha)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{(i)}^{\vee},\quad\alpha^{\vee}_{(i)}\in H^{d+2g-2i}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}}).

This yields

(9)

\alpha={\mathfrak{F}}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}(\alpha)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i},\quad\alpha_{i}=\mathfrak{F}\left(\alpha^{\vee}_{(i)}\right)\in H^{d}_{(i)}(A,{\mathbb{Q}}).

As was explained in
[
17
]
, the decomposition (
9
) is motivic with projectors given by the Chern character of the normalized Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
. Consequently, the Beauville decomposition (
7
) is governed by the normalized Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
.

Moreover, the multiplicativity (
8
) can be seen from Fourier transforms together with (
9
). For this we use a new product structure on
H^{*}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}})
:

H^{d}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}})\times H^{d^{\prime}}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{~~*~~}H^{d+d^{\prime}-2g}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad(\alpha^{\vee},\beta^{\vee})\mapsto\mu_{*}(q_{1}^{*}\alpha^{\vee}\cup q_{2}^{*}\beta^{\vee}),

where
\mu:A^{\vee}\times_{B}A^{\vee}\to A^{\vee}
is the addition map and

are the natural projections. We shall call
*
the
convolution
since it is Fourier-dual to the cup product,

{\mathfrak{F}}(\alpha^{\vee}*\beta^{\vee})={\mathfrak{F}}(\alpha^{\vee})\cup{\mathfrak{F}}(\beta^{\vee}).

It is also known as the

since the definition involves the group structure of

. Now given two classes
\alpha\in H^{d}_{(i)}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\beta\in H^{d^{\prime}}_{(i^{\prime})}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})
, we have by (

{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}(\alpha)\in H^{d+2g-2i}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}(\beta)\in H^{d^{\prime}+2g-2i^{\prime}}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}}),

hence
{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}(\alpha)*{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}(\beta)\in H^{d+d^{\prime}+2g-2i-2i^{\prime}}(A^{\vee},{\mathbb{Q}})
. Applying (
9

\alpha\cup\beta={\mathfrak{F}}\left({\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}(\alpha)*{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}(\beta)\right)\in H^{d+d^{\prime}}_{(i+i^{\prime})}(A,{\mathbb{Q}})

Then (

which serves as a prototype for the “Perverse
\supset

1.4.
Dualizable abelian fibrations

Dualizable abelian fibrations are extensions of abelian schemes involving singular fibers, for which most properties given by the Fourier transform described in Section
1.2
may be generalized.

Definition 1.2

(Dualizable abelian fibration)
.

Let
\pi:M\to B
be an abelian fibration of relative dimension
g
,
i.e.

g=\dim M-\dim B
.
We say that
\pi:M\to B
is a dualizable abelian fibration, if it has a dual abelian fibration
\pi^{\vee}:M^{\vee}\to B
(see Section
1.3.1
) satisfying the following.

(a1)

(Poincaré) There is a normalized Poincaré complex
{{\mathcal{P}}}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee}\times_{B}M)
which admits an inverse
{{\mathcal{P}}}^{-1}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M\times_{B}M^{\vee})
,
i.e.
,

(12)

{{\mathcal{P}}}^{-1}\circ{{\mathcal{P}}}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta_{M^{\vee}/B}},\quad{{\mathcal{P}}}\circ{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta_{M/B}}.

(a2)

(Convolution) There is an object
{\mathcal{K}}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee}\times_{B}M^{\vee}\times_{B}M^{\vee})
of codimension
g
(see Section
1.3.2
for the definition of codimension), which satisfies

(13)

{{\mathcal{P}}}\circ{\mathcal{K}}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta^{\mathrm{sm}}_{M/B}}\circ({{\mathcal{P}}}\boxtimes{{\mathcal{P}}})\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee}\times_{B}M^{\vee}\times_{B}M).

Here
\Delta^{\mathrm{sm}}_{M/B}
is the small diagonal of the relative triple product
M\times_{B}M\times_{B}M
, and the notation
{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta^{\mathrm{sm}}_{M/B}}\circ({{\mathcal{P}}}\boxtimes{{\mathcal{P}}})
stands for composing
{\mathcal{P}}
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta^{\mathrm{sm}}_{M/B}}
via the first (resp. second) factor of
M\times_{B}M\times_{B}M
. We shall refer to
{\mathcal{K}}
as the
convolution kernel
as it induces a product structure on
D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee})
which is Fourier–Mukai-dual to the tensor product of
D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M)
.

(b)

(Support) The morphism
\pi
has full support.

Here (a1, a2) serve as the detailed version of (a) in Section
0.2
. We list some consequences of (a1, a2) in terms of Fourier–Mukai transforms. Using (a1), we can define the Fourier–Mukai transforms
\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee})\to D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M)
and its inverse

\mathrm{FM}^{-1}_{{\mathcal{P}}}=\mathrm{FM}_{{{\mathcal{P}}}^{-1}}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M)\to D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee}).

Next, we define a convolution product via
{\mathcal{K}}
in (a2):

\ast:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee})\times D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee})\to D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee}),\quad F_{1}\ast F_{2}=p_{3*}(p_{1}^{*}F_{1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}F_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{K}}).

Then (
13

\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}}(F_{1})\otimes\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}}(F_{2})\simeq\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}}(F_{1}\ast F_{2}),\quad F_{1},~F_{2}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M^{\vee}).

We also note that dualizable abelian fibrations extend abelian schemes.

Proposition 1.3
.

An abelian scheme
\pi:A\to B
is dualizable in the sense above.

Proof.

The abelian schemes
\pi:A\to B
and
\pi^{\vee}:A^{\vee}\to B
form dual abelian fibrations with
{\mathcal{P}}
given by the normalized Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
. The properties concerning the convolution were proven in
[

with fiber

\{(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})\in\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(A_{b})^{\times 3}\,|\,l_{1}\otimes l_{2}\simeq l_{3}\}.

This is clearly a codimension
g
locus over
B

R^{i}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{A}
is a local system with support

∎

2.
Multiplicativity and perversity

2.1.
Overview

In this section, we apply Fourier transforms to study the perverse filtrations for dualizable abelian fibrations. After the necessary preparations, we state our main result in Theorem

which is an extension of the Beauville decomposition and a motivic enhancement of the multiplicativity and the “Perverse

2.2.

Lemma 2.2

2.3.
Chern characters

As we will be working throughout with coherent sheaves on singular varieties, we need a systematic definition of Chern characters taking values in Chow groups. For convenience we use the Riemann–Roch theorem of Baum–Fulton–MacPherson in
[
22
, Chapter 18]
.

Let
K_{*}(-)
(resp.
K^{*}(-)
) denote the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves (resp. vector bundles). By
[
22
, Theorem 18.3]
, for any finite type scheme

there is a homomorphism

satisfying the following properties.

(i)

For
f:X\to Y
proper and
F\in K_{*}(X)
, we have
f_{*}\tau(F)=\tau(f_{*}F)
.

(ii)

For

, we have
\tau(E\otimes F)={\mathrm{ch}}(E)\cap\tau(F)
.

(iii)

If

is a closed embedding with

nonsingular, then for
F\in K_{*}(X)
,

(23)

\tau(F)={\mathrm{ch}}_{X}^{Y}(F_{\bullet})\cap{\mathrm{td}}(Y)\cap[Y]={\mathrm{td}}(i^{*}T_{Y})\cap{\mathrm{ch}}_{X}^{Y}(F_{\bullet})\cap[Y].

Here
{\mathrm{ch}}_{X}^{Y}(-)
is the localized Chern character in
[
22
, Section 18.1]
and
F_{\bullet}
is any locally free resolution of
i_{*}F
on
Y
.

(iv)

If
f:X\to Y
is an l.c.i. morphism and both
X
and
Y
admit closed embeddings in nonsingular varieties
8
8

8

The assumption on the embeddings was recently removed by
[
3
]
.
, then for
F\in K_{*}(Y)
,

\tau(f^{*}F)={\mathrm{td}}(T_{f})\cap f^{*}\tau(F).

Here
T_{f}\in K^{*}(X)
is the virtual tangent bundle of

.

Intuitively, one can think of
\tau
as a covariant (for proper morphisms), Todd-twisted Chern character for coherent sheaves on arbitrary varieties.

2.4.

\mathfrak{F}:={\mathrm{td}}(-T_{M^{\vee}\times_{B}M})\cap\tau({\mathcal{P}})\in\mathrm{CH}_{*}(M^{\vee}\times_{B}M),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{-1}

\displaystyle:={\mathrm{td}}(-(\pi\times_{B}\pi^{\vee})^{*}T_{B})\cap\tau({\mathcal{P}}^{-1})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{td}}(T_{M\times_{B}M^{\vee}})\cap{\mathrm{ch}}_{M\times_{B}M^{\vee}}^{M\times M^{\vee}}({\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{\bullet})\cap[M\times M^{\vee}]\in\mathrm{CH}_{*}(M\times_{B}M^{\vee}).

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{F}=[\Delta_{M^{\vee}/B}]\in\mathrm{Corr}^{0}_{B}(M^{\vee},M^{\vee}),\quad\mathfrak{F}\circ\mathfrak{F}^{-1}=[\Delta_{M/B}]\in\mathrm{Corr}_{B}^{0}(M,M).

{{\mathcal{P}}}^{-1}\circ{{\mathcal{P}}}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta_{M^{\vee}/B}}

(28)

\displaystyle\tau({\mathcal{P}}^{-1}\circ{\mathcal{P}})

\displaystyle=p_{13*}\tau(\delta^{*}({\mathcal{P}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}))

\displaystyle=p_{13*}\left({\mathrm{td}}(-p_{2}^{*}T_{M})\cap\delta^{!}\tau({\mathcal{P}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{P}}^{-1})\right)

By capping both (

\displaystyle[\Delta_{M^{\vee}/B}]

\displaystyle={\mathrm{td}}(-q_{1}^{\vee*}T_{M^{\vee}})\cap p_{13*}\left({\mathrm{td}}(-p_{2}^{*}T_{M})\cap\delta^{!}(\tau({\mathcal{P}})\times\tau({\mathcal{P}}^{-1}))\right)

\displaystyle=p_{13*}\left({\mathrm{td}}(-p_{1}^{*}T_{M^{\vee}})\cap{\mathrm{td}}(-p_{2}^{*}T_{M})\cap\delta^{!}(\tau({\mathcal{P}})\times\tau({\mathcal{P}}^{-1}))\right)

Remark 2.4
.

The seemingly asymmetric assignments for
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
are deliberate. For one thing
\mathfrak{F}
restricts to the Fourier transform in
[
17
]
over the open

where

\mathfrak{F}|_{U}={\mathrm{ch}}({\mathcal{L}})\cap[M^{\vee}_{U}\times_{U}M_{U}]=\exp(c_{1}({\mathcal{L}}))\cap[M^{\vee}_{U}\times_{U}M_{U}],

with no Todd contribution whatsoever. Another reason is the compatibility with tautological classes; see Section

We decompose both

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{F}_{i},\quad\mathfrak{F}_{i}\in\mathrm{Corr}_{B}^{i-g}(M^{\vee},M),

Now we can formulate the Fourier vanishing mentioned in Section

in terms of correspondences.

Definition 2.5

(Fourier vanishing)
.

With the notation as above, we say that the dualizable abelian fibration

(FV)

{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{j}=0\in\mathrm{Corr}_{B}^{i+j-2g}(M^{\vee},M^{\vee}),\quad i+j<2g.

The main theorem of this section is a motivic version of Theorem
0.1
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\pi:M\to B
be a dualizable abelian fibration of relative dimension

which satisfies (FV).

(i)

(Decomposition) For each

, the pair of self-correspondences

\mathfrak{p}_{k}:=\sum_{i\leq k}{\mathfrak{F}}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{2g-i}\in\mathrm{Corr}^{0}_{B}(M,M),\quad\mathfrak{q}_{k+1}:=\sum_{i\geq k+1}{\mathfrak{F}}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{2g-i}\in\mathrm{Corr}^{0}_{B}(M,M)

(ii)

(Realization) For each

together with the inclusion

is the natural morphism

{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}}\tau_{\leq k+\dim B}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}\to\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}.

Similarly, the homological realization of

\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}\to{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}}\tau_{\geq k+1+\dim B}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}.

(iii)

(Multiplicativity) For each pair of

(iv)

(Perverse
\supset

In view of the discussion in Section

2.5.1.

2.5.5.
Motivic decomposition

Finally, we note that our construction yields a motivic decomposition of
\pi:M\to B
which proves Corollary
2.7
.

One subtlety of the sequence of motives
P_{k}h(M)
is that we do not know if
P_{k}h(M)
is a summand of
P_{l}h(M)
for
k<l
. While the condition (FV) immediately implies

(41)

{\mathfrak{p}}_{l}\circ{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}={\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\quad k<l,

it is unclear if
{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\circ{\mathfrak{p}}_{l}={\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
. However, we can fix this issue by introducing a minor modification

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}:={\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathfrak{p}}_{2g}\in\mathrm{Corr}^{0}_{B}(M,M),\quad\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}:=\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}-\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k-1}\in\mathrm{Corr}^{0}_{B}(M,M).

Now it is easy to verify that both
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}
and
\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}
are projectors, and

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}\circ\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{l}=\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{l}\circ\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}=\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k},\quad k<l.

For instance, we have by (
41
)

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}\circ\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{l}={\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathfrak{p}}_{2g}\circ{\mathfrak{p}}_{l}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathfrak{p}}_{2g}={\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathfrak{p}}_{2g}=\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k},\quad k\leq l.

The resulting motives
\widetilde{P}_{k}h(M)=(M,\widetilde{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k},0)
and
h_{k}(M)=(M,\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k},0)
satisfy

\widetilde{P}_{k}h(M)=\widetilde{P}_{k-1}h(M)\oplus h_{k}(M)\in\mathrm{CHM}(B).

By induction, we also find that the
\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k}
are mutually orthogonal projectors giving rise to a motivic decomposition

(42)

h(M)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2g}h_{i}(M)\in\mathrm{CHM}(B),\quad h_{i}(M)=(M,\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{k},0).

The support argument of Section
2.5.2
then shows that the homological realization of (
42
) provides a decomposition

\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2g}{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{i+\dim B}(\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M})[-i-\dim B]\in D^{b}_{c}(B).

This finishes the proof of Corollary
2.7
. ∎

3.
Compactified Jacobians

3.1.
Overview

In this section, we treat the geometry of (degree
0
) compactified Jacobian fibrations; our main purpose is to prove Theorem
0.2
.

Throughout this section, we let
C\to B
be a flat family of integral projective curves of arithmetic genus
g
with planar singularities over an irreducible base
B
. We further assume that
C\to B
admits a section whose image is contained in the smooth locus of the fibers. We denote by
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
the compactified Jacobian fibration, and assume that the total space
\overline{J}_{C}
is nonsingular. In particular,
B
is nonsingular and
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
is flat with integral fibers of dimension
g
.

At the end of this section, we discuss compactified Jacobian fibrations of arbitrary degree still in the presence of a section to
C\to B
, in which case changes to the arguments are minimal. The assumption on the existence of a section will be removed in Section
4
using a stack version of our theory.

3.2.
Compactified Jacobians

Let
J_{C}\subset\overline{J}_{C}
be the Jacobian fibration parameterizing line bundles. It is an open dense subset of
\overline{J}_{C}
, which forms a nonsingular commutative group scheme over
B
.

The Fourier–Mukai theory of compactified Jacobian fibrations was developed by Arinkin
[
5
,
6
]
. More precisely, Arinkin constructed in
[
5
]
the normalized Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{P}}
over the product
J_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}
extending the standard normalized Poincaré line bundle for Jacobians associated with nonsingular curves. Then in
[
6
]
Arinkin showed that the sheaf
j_{*}{\mathcal{P}}
, where
j:J_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\hookrightarrow\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}
is the natural open embedding, is a Cohen–Macaulay coherent sheaf (which we still denote by
{\mathcal{P}}
for notational convenience) on
\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}
; he further proved that
{\mathcal{P}}
induces a derived auto-equivalence

\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{J}_{C})\xrightarrow{~~\simeq~~}D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{J}_{C}).

The inverse of this transform is induced by

{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}:={\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\pi}[g],\quad{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}:={{\mathcal{H}}}om({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}}).

Here
\omega_{\pi}
is the relative canonical bundle with respect to
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
, and
\pi_{2}:\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\to B
is the natural morphism.

Furthermore, the support theorem of Ngô
[
44
]
and the Severi inequality
[
37
, Lemma 4.1]
ensure that the decomposition theorem associated with
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
has full support. Therefore, in order to prove Theorem
0.2
, it remains to verify:

(i)

the convolution kernel

(43)

{\mathcal{K}}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C})

is of codimension
g
;

(ii)

the condition (FV) holds for
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
.

We will discuss (i), which is a result of Arinkin, in Section
3.3
, and will discuss (ii) in Section
3.5
.

We conclude this section by recalling the
canonical
perverse filtration constructed in
[
40
]
for the compactified Jacobian
\overline{J}_{C_{0}}
associated with
any
integral projective curve
C_{0}
with planar singularities; this is the filtration used in Theorem
0.7
. For such a
C_{0}
, we include it in a family of curves
C\to B
over an irreducible
B
such that the associated compactified Jacobian fibration
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
is nonsingular and
\overline{J}_{C_{0}}\subset\overline{J}_{C}
is the closed fiber over
0\in B
. Then the construction of Section
1.3.3
yields a perverse filtration

P_{0}H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset P_{1}H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\cdots\subset H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{0}},{\mathbb{Q}})

via the morphism
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
.

Theorem 3.1

(Maulik–Yun
[
40
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The perverse filtration defined above is independent of the choice of the family
C\to B
of curves.

Proof.

In
[
40
, Theorem 1.1]
, the family
C\to B
is chosen with certain assumptions. However, as pointed out in
[
40
, Proposition 2.15]
, the smoothness of
\overline{J}_{C}
suffices.
∎

In particular, Theorem
0.7
follows from Theorem
2.6
(iii) and Theorem
0.2
, where we include
C_{0}
in a family
\pi:C\to B
as a closed fiber.

3.3.
Arinkin’s dimension bound and the convolution kernel

We first give an expression of the convolution kernel (
43

\overline{J}_{C}^{n+1}
the
(n+1)
-th relative product. It carries natural projections

\pi_{n+1}:\overline{J}_{C}^{n+1}\to B,\quad p_{i}:\overline{J}_{C}^{n+1}\to\overline{J}_{C},\quad p_{i,j}:\overline{J}_{C}^{n+1}\to\overline{J}^{2}_{C},\quad\cdots.

We consider the object

{\mathcal{K}}_{n}:=p_{1,\dots,n,*}\left(p_{1,n+1}^{*}{\mathcal{P}}\otimes p_{2,n+1}^{*}{\mathcal{P}}\otimes\cdots\otimes p_{n,n+1}^{*}{\mathcal{P}}\right)\otimes\pi_{n}^{*}\omega_{\pi}[g]\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{J}_{C}^{n}).

Here the boundedness of
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is a consequence of the flatness of
{\mathcal{P}}
with respect to both factors
[
6
, Theorem A and Lemma 6.1]
. Let
\nu:\overline{J}_{C}\to\overline{J}_{C}
be the involution sending a sheaf to its dual, so that we can write

{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee}=(\mathrm{id}_{\overline{J}_{C}}\times_{B}\nu)^{*}{\mathcal{P}}.

Then by the defining equation

{\mathcal{K}}\simeq{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}\circ{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta_{\overline{J}_{C}/B}^{\mathrm{sm}}}\circ({\mathcal{P}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{P}})

obtained from (
13
), and again by the flatness of
{\mathcal{P}}
with respect to both factors, we may express the convolution kernel (

{\mathcal{K}}=\left(\mathrm{id}_{\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}}\times_{B}\nu\right)^{*}{\mathcal{K}}_{3}.

Therefore, the condition (i) of Section
3.2
is a special case of the following result of Arinkin
[
6
, Section 7.1]
.

Proposition 3.2

(Arinkin)
.

For any
n
, we have

\mathrm{codim}_{\overline{J}^{n}_{C}}(\mathrm{supp}({\mathcal{K}}_{n}))\geq g.

Note that
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is a classical object for low values of
n
. When
n=1
the proposition was a step towards establishing
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
concerning the Fourier–Mukai transform of the structure sheaf. When
n=2
, the proposition was used in
[
6
]
to show that
\mathrm{FM}_{\mathcal{P}}
is a derived equivalence. The same argument works for the general case, which we review as follows.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

We first fix some notation. For an integral projective curve
C_{b}
, we denote by
g_{a}(C_{b})
its arithmetic genus, and
\widetilde{C}_{b}
its normalization. We denote by
\delta(C_{b})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
the dimension of the maximal affine group of
\mathrm{Pic}(C_{b})
. Clearly, when
C_{b}
has planar singularities, we have

\delta(C_{b})=g_{a}(C_{b})-g_{a}(\widetilde{C}_{b}).

This yields a constructible function

\delta:B\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\quad b\mapsto\delta(C_{b})

associated with
C\to B
. For an irreducible subvariety
Z\subset B
, we define
\delta(Z)
to be the value of
\delta(b)
at a general point
b\in Z
.

Step 1.
Recall that the smoothness of
\overline{J}_{C}
yields the Severi inequality
[
37
, Lemma 4.1]

\mathrm{codim}_{B}(Z)\geq\delta_{Z}

for any irreducible
Z\subset B
. Therefore, it suffices to show that for any curve
C_{b}
in the family
C\to B
, we have

\mathrm{codim}_{\overline{J}_{C_{b}}}\left(\mathrm{supp}({\mathcal{K}}_{n})\cap\overline{J}_{C_{b}}\right)\geq g_{a}(\widetilde{C}_{b}).

Step 2.
Now we focus on a fixed curve
C_{b}
. For a point
F\in\overline{J}_{C_{b}}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{P}}_{F}
the restriction of
{\mathcal{P}}
to
\overline{J}_{C_{b}}=\overline{J}_{C_{b}}\times\{F\}
. If we further restrict this sheaf to the open subset
J_{C_{b}}\subset\overline{J}_{C_{b}}
parameterizing line bundles on
C_{b}
, it is a line bundle.

If
(F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{n})\in\overline{J}^{n}_{C_{b}}
lies in the support of
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
, then there is a nontrivial cohomology

(44)

H^{*}(\overline{J}_{C_{b}},{\mathcal{P}}_{F_{1}}\otimes{\mathcal{P}}_{F_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathcal{P}}_{F_{n}})\neq 0

By the same argument as in
[
6
, Proposition 7.2]
, (
44
) ensures that

(45)

\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}({\mathcal{P}}_{F_{i}}|_{J_{C_{b}}})\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{J_{C_{b}}}.

Step 3.
Finally, we claim that (
45

g_{a}(\widetilde{C}_{b})
condition for

(F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{n})\in\overline{J}^{n}_{C_{b}}.

This was explained in
[
6

. Indeed, pulling back the isomorphism (

(46)

\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}({F_{i}}|_{C^{\mathrm{reg}}_{b}})\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{C^{\mathrm{reg}}_{b}}.

Then the same argument as in
[
6

applied to the action morphism

\mu\times\mathrm{id}_{\overline{J}_{C_{b}}}:J_{C_{b}}\times\overline{J}^{n}_{C_{b}}\to\overline{J}^{n}_{C_{b}}

3.2

∎

We have thus verified (i) of Section
3.2
.

3.4.
Remarks on the Fourier vanishing for abelian schemes

We first discuss two proofs of (FV) for abelian schemes.

As in Section
1.2
, we let
\pi:A\to B
be an abelian scheme of relative dimension
g
with
\pi^{\vee}:A^{\vee}\to B
its dual. Then by Lemma
2.3
, for degree reasons we have for any
k\neq 2g
,

(47)

\sum_{i+j=k}{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{j}=0\in\mathrm{Corr}_{B}^{k-2g}(A^{\vee},A^{\vee}).

The idea of
[
7
,
17
]
is that, if we apply the pullback associated with the multiplication by
N
map on the first factor

[N]:A^{\vee}\times_{B}A^{\vee}\to A^{\vee}\times_{B}A^{\vee},

we may scale each individual term of (
47
) by a different constant
N^{j}
:

(48)

\sum_{i+j=k}N^{j}{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{j}=0.

Since this holds for every
N
, we obtain the desired vanishing

{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{i}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{j}=0,\quad i+j\neq 2g.

However, this argument does not work for abelian fibrations with singular fibers, since it relies on the facts that there is a multiplication by
N
map on the total space
A^{\vee}
and the normalized Poincaré complex is a line bundle. Both fail when there are singular fibers.

We now present another proof of the weaker version (FV) whose idea can be generalized. For convenience, we focus on the Jacobian fibration
\pi:J_{C}\to B
associated with a family of nonsingular projective curves
C\to B
of genus
g
. Recall that we have the normalized Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
. The discussion in Section
3.3
actually yields the dimension bound

\mathrm{codim}_{J^{2}_{C}}\left(\mathrm{supp}({\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\circ{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes N})\right)\geq g

for any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, if we take the Chern character of
{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\circ{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes N}
, we obtain the equation (
48
) only for
i+j<2g
. This yields (FV) for
\pi:J_{C}\to B
.

The second proof above relies on the following two ingredients:

(i)

the Arinkin-type dimension bound of Section
3.3
, and

(ii)

the
K
-theoretic operations
(-)^{\otimes N}
which help us to scale the Chern character of different degrees distinctly.

In the next section, we will generalize them to treat
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
where the second ingredient is replaced by the Adams operations from
K
-theory.

3.5.
Adams operations and Fourier vanishing

We now verify the condition (FV) for
\pi:\overline{J}_{C}\to B
, which proves (ii) of Section
3.2
. Consequently, this completes the proofs of Theorems
0.2
,
0.3
, and
0.7

3.5.1.
Dimension bound

We consider the closed embedding

(49)

i:\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\hookrightarrow\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C}.

For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, the object

(i_{*}{\mathcal{P}})^{\otimes N}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C})

is bounded, and is exact off
\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\subset\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C}
. Then we consider

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(N):={\mathcal{P}}^{-1}\circ(i_{*}{\mathcal{P}})^{\otimes N}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C}).

By definition (and by the flatness of
{\mathcal{P}}
with respect to both factors), it is constructed by pulling back the two objects
(i_{*}{\mathcal{P}})^{\otimes N},{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}
to the triple product

\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C},

taking their tensor product, and pushing forward to the first and the third factors.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
N
, the object
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(N)\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C})
is supported on a codimension
g
subset of
\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}
.

Proof.

The proof relies on Arinkin’s dimension estimate which goes through the following steps. It is essentially parallel to the proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Step 1.
We first show that the set-theoretic support of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(N)
is contained in
\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}
. By definition and the projection formula, the object
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(N)
is obtained via the pushforward of an object supported on

\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\hookrightarrow\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}

Therefore, it can be written as the pushforward through the following chain of maps

\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\rightarrow\overline{J}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{J}_{C}\hookrightarrow\overline{J}_{C}\times\overline{J}_{C}

where the first map is the projection to the first and the third factors, and the second map is the natural inclusion
i
. This proves the claim.

Step 2.
Now any closed point in the support of
\widetilde{K}(N)
can be represented by a pair of sheaves on
C_{b}\subset C
:

(50)

(F_{1},F_{2})\in\mathrm{supp}(\widetilde{K}(N))\cap\overline{J}_{C_{b}}.

As in Step 2 of the proof of Proposition
3.2
, it suffices to show that

(51)

\mathrm{codim}_{\overline{J}_{C_{b}}}\left(\mathrm{supp}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(N))\cap\overline{J}_{C_{b}}\right)\geq g_{a}(\widetilde{C}_{b})

where
\widetilde{C}_{b}
is the normalization of
C_{b}
.

Step 3.
For a pair
(F_{1},F_{2})
as in (

\mathrm{det}\left(p_{13*}(p^{*}_{12}{\mathcal{F}}^{e}\otimes p^{*}_{23}{\mathcal{F}}^{d})\right)\otimes\mathrm{det}\left(p_{13*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C}\times_{B}C\times_{B}\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}}\right)\otimes\mathrm{det}\left(p_{13*}p^{*}_{12}{\mathcal{F}}^{e}\right)^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{det}\left(p_{13*}p^{*}_{23}{\mathcal{F}}^{d}\right)^{\vee}

, where

are the natural projections from

{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}:={\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{F}}^{e},{\mathcal{F}}^{d})\in\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C})_{(d,e)}.

{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}^{-1}:={\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{om}_{\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C}\times_{B}\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}}({\mathcal{P}}_{e,d},\,p_{2}^{*}\omega_{\pi_{d}})[g]

is only nonzero on the following isotypic components:

\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C})_{(-d)}\rightarrow D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C})_{(e)}.

The next proposition is parallel to
[
1

and

Proposition 4.3
.

The objects
{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d},{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}
are inverse to each other as Fourier–Mukai kernels:

\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C})_{(-d)}\xrightarrow{~~\simeq~~}D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C})_{(e)},\quad\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}}=\mathrm{FM}^{-1}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}}.

Proof.

Recall that for
{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}
and
{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}
we always have the canonical adjunction morphisms

\mathrm{id}_{D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C})_{(-d)}}\to\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}}\circ\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}},\quad\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}}\circ\mathrm{FM}_{{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}}\to\mathrm{id}_{D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C})_{(e)}}.

It suffices to show that their cones in the derived categories are
0
; this is a property which can be checked étale locally over the base

below.
∎

4.3.
Étale local structures

In this section, we carry out the étale local calculation for the Fourier–Mukai transforms given by
{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d},{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}
. This leads to two consequences. First, the calculation completes the proof of Proposition
4.3
. Second, we will show as in Proposition
3.6
that the motivic decomposition induced by the Fourier transforms
\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
associated with
{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d},{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}
specializes to the (standard) perverse filtration; see Section
4.4
.

Now we work étale locally over the base
B
. Let
U
be an étale neighborhood of the base. We may assume that
C\to U
admits simultaneously a section and a multisection

U\subset C\to U,\quad D\subset C\to U.

They are independent and do not have any nontrivial relation. Due to the existence of the section, the relative compactified Jacobian
\overline{J}^{d}_{C}
are identified for any choices of
d
. As before, we denote by
\overline{J}_{C}
the degree
0
compactified Jacobian, and we want to compare
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}
on
C\times_{U}\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}
(which is trivialized along the multisection
D
) with the normalized universal sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
C\times_{U}\overline{J}_{C}
(which is trivialized along the section
U
).

Proposition 4.4
.

There is a
U
-morphism

\sigma_{d}:\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}\to\overline{J}_{C}

satisfying the following properties.

(i)

We have

{\mathcal{F}}^{d}\simeq(\mathrm{id}_{C}\times_{U}\sigma_{d})^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes p_{C}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(dU)\otimes p_{\mathcal{J}}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{d},\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{d}\in\mathrm{Pic}(\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}),

where
p_{C},p_{\mathcal{J}}
are the natural projections from
C\times_{U}\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}
.

(ii)

For any
b\in U
with
C_{b}
a nonsingular curve, the restriction of
{\mathcal{L}}_{d}
to the fiber
{\mathcal{J}}^{d}_{C_{b}}
has trivial first Chern class in
H^{2}({{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C_{b}},{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof.

The desired morphism
\sigma_{d}
can be constructed using the map from the Deligne–Mumford stack to its coarse moduli space, in view of the identification
\overline{J}^{d}_{C}\simeq\overline{J}_{C}
. For our purpose, we describe it in a more direct way as follows.

Note that
\overline{J}_{C}
is a fine moduli space. Therefore, describing a morphism from
\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}
is equivalent to constructing a family of rank
1
torsion-free sheaves of degree
0
trivialized along the section
U\subset C
, for which we may use
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}
. More precisely, we define

{\mathcal{G}}^{d}:={\mathcal{F}}^{d}\otimes p_{C}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(-dU)\otimes p_{\mathcal{J}}^{*}\left(({\mathcal{F}}^{d}\otimes p_{C}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(-dU))|_{U\times_{U}\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}}\right)^{\vee}.

Here the second factor adjusts the degree from
d
to
0
, and the third factor is included to trivialize the universal sheaf along the section. This yields a morphism
\sigma_{d}:\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}\to\overline{J}_{C}
satisfying

(\mathrm{id}_{C}\times_{U}\sigma_{d})^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\simeq{\mathcal{G}}^{d}.

This already proves (i).

To prove (ii), it suffices to show that, for a fixed nonsingular curve
C_{b}
with
s\in C_{b}
the intersection with the section
U\subset C
, the line bundle
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}|_{s\times{\mathcal{J}}_{C_{b}}}
has homologically trivial first Chern class. Assume
D\cap C_{b}
is given by
s_{1},\dots,s_{e}\in C_{b}
(counted with multiplicities). Then the condition that
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}
is trivialized along the multisection
D
implies

\sum_{i=1}^{e}c_{1}\left({\mathcal{F}}^{d}|_{s_{i}\times{\mathcal{J}}_{C_{b}}}\right)=0\in H^{2}({{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C_{b}},{\mathbb{Q}}).

This shows

c_{1}\left({\mathcal{F}}^{d}|_{s\times{\mathcal{J}}_{C_{b}}}\right)=0\in H^{2}({\mathcal{J}}^{d}_{C_{b}},{\mathbb{Q}})

as desired, since the first Chern class of the left-hand side does not depend on the point in
C_{b}
. The proof of (ii) is complete.
∎

Now we consider

\sigma_{e}\times_{U}\sigma_{d}:\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C}\times_{U}\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}\to\overline{J}_{C}\times_{U}\overline{J}_{C}.

The normalized Poincare sheaf
{\mathcal{P}}
and its inverse
{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}
are canonically defined on the target. The following is an immediate consequence of Proposition
4.4
as in Section
3.6
(using the argument of
[
1
, Proposition 3.1(i)]
). It provides an étale local description of the Poincaré sheaf
{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}
and its inverse
{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

We have

{\mathcal{P}}_{e,d}\simeq(\sigma_{e}\times_{U}\sigma_{d})^{*}{\mathcal{P}}\otimes\left({\mathcal{L}}_{e}^{\otimes d}\boxtimes{\mathcal{L}}_{d}^{\otimes e}\right),\quad{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}_{e,d}\simeq(\sigma_{d}\times_{U}\sigma_{e})^{*}{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}\otimes\left({\mathcal{L}}_{d}^{\vee\otimes e}\boxtimes{\mathcal{L}}_{e}^{\vee\otimes d}\right).

Here
{\mathcal{L}}_{d},{\mathcal{L}}_{e}
are line bundles on
\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C},\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{e}_{C}
respectively. Moreover, for any
b\in U
with
C_{b}
nonsingular, the restrictions of these line bundles to the fibers over
b
have homologically trivial first Chern classes.

4.4.
Multiplicativity and perversity

The goal of this section is to provide an extension of Theorem
2.6
and Corollary
2.7
for the degree
d
compactified Jacobian
\pi_{d}:\overline{J}^{d}_{C}\to B
. Note that we chose to first work out the untwisted case for purely expository reasons, so that the Fourier theory can be streamlined and separated from the language of stacks. The twisted case requires the following three additional ingredients.

(i)

Since we assume
\overline{J}^{d}_{C}
to be a quasi-projective variety, by
[
30
]
the Brauer map

\mathrm{Br}(\overline{J}^{d}_{C})\to H^{2}(\overline{J}^{d}_{C},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})_{\mathrm{tors}}

is an isomorphism. Then
[
21
, Theorem 3.6]
implies that the
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}
over
\overline{J}^{d}_{C}
is a quotient stack. This also applies to fiber products of
\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{d}_{C}
for various
d
over the base scheme
B
.

(ii)

The intersection theory of quotient stacks has been developed in a series of papers of Edidin and Graham; we refer to
[
18
]
for a summary of the theory and the references therein. In particular, the Riemann–Roch functor
\tau(-)
for quotient stacks was constructed in
[
20
]
and was shown to satisfy the same functorial properties as in Section
2.3
except for
the covariance under arbitrary proper morphisms. The Edidin–Graham functor
\tau(-)
is only covariant under proper
representable
morphisms of quotient stacks.

We also note that with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-coefficients, the Edidin–Graham Chow groups of a Deligne–Mumford quotient stack are canonically isomorphic (via pushforward) to the Chow groups of the coarse moduli space; see
[
19
,
56
]
.

(iii)

The
K
-theory of quotient stacks has been studied by Anderson, Gonzales, and Payne
[
4
,
3
]
in the style of Fulton and Edidin–Graham. For our purposes we shall need the construction of Adams operations and the scaling (
56
) for closed embeddings of (possibly singular) quotient stacks, both of which were established in
[
3
, Section 4]
.

We are now ready to run the entire argument of Sections
2
and
3
for
\pi_{d}:\overline{J}^{d}_{C}\to B
.

Step 1.
Choosing an arbitrary integer

and its inverse

5.4.3.
The reduction steps of Hausel–Mellit–Minets–Schiffmann

Finally, we complete the proof of Theorem
0.4
by reducing it to Corollary
5.7
. Clearly (
4
) can be deduced from the following analogue of Corollary
5.7
for the moduli space
M
:

(78)

c_{k}(\gamma)\cup{}:P_{i}H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})\to P_{i+k}H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}}).

If such a statement holds for a Hitchin type moduli space in Section
5.4.2
, we say that this moduli space satisfies
stronger
P\supset C
. For the moduli space
M
, the stronger
P\supset C
condition is equivalent to the weaker version (
4
) due to Markman’s generation result
[
35
]
. But for other spaces, these two conditions are not equivalent.

We now explain how the reduction steps of
[
26
, Section 8]
reduces (
78
) to Corollary
5.7
. The strategy is to keep track of the stronger
P\supset C
condition through (
76
) from the right end to the left end.

Step 1.
Since all the moduli spaces of (
76
) admit natural maps to
M^{\mathrm{mero}}
, we fix once and for all a normalized pair

({\mathbb{U}},\delta)\rightsquigarrow\Sigma\times M^{\mathrm{mero}},

whose pullback yields a normalized pair and the tautological classes
c_{k}(\gamma)
for each moduli space.

By Corollary
5.7
, the stronger
P\supset C
condition holds for
M^{\mathrm{parell}}
.

Step 2.
The stronger
P\supset C
condition holds for
M^{0}
.

This follows from Step 1 combined with the following facts:

(i)

the restriction map

\iota_{0}^{*}:H^{*}(M^{\mathrm{par}},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(M^{0},{\mathbb{Q}})

is a filtered isomorphism with respect to the perverse filteations and preserves tautological classes; see
[
26
, Proposition 8.18]
;

(ii)

the restriction map

\iota^{*}:H^{*}(M^{\mathrm{par}},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(M^{\mathrm{parell}},{\mathbb{Q}})

is a filtered injection with respect to the perverse filtrations and preserves tautological classes; see
[
26
, Proposition 8.16]
and the paragraph after that.

Step 3.
The stronger
P\supset C
condition holds for
M
, which completes the proof of (
78
).

To see this, we consider the morphism

\Gamma:=\widetilde{\iota}_{*}f^{*}:H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(M^{0},{\mathbb{Q}}).

We note that this is only a correspondence which does not preserve cup-products. By
[
26
, Section 8.6, before Theorem 8.20]

\Gamma
is injective with left inverse given by

\Gamma^{\prime}:=f_{*}\widetilde{\iota}^{*}:H^{*}(M^{0},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})

up to a nonzero constant.

14

14

In
[
26
, Section 8.6]
the correspondences
\Gamma

Since the pullback of the tautological classes

Effective equidistribution of rational points on horocycle sections in ASL ( 2 , ℤ ) \ ASL ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) (2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{ASL}(2,\mathbb{R})
Sam Pattison

Abstract
In this paper we prove an effective equidistribution result for both primitive and non-primitive points on certain expanding horocycle sections in ASL ​ ( 2 , ℤ ) \ ASL ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) \text{ASL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{ASL}(2,\mathbb{R}) . This provides an effective version of a result recently proven by the author and generalises recent work by Einsiedler, Luethi and Shah who prove similar results in the context of the unit tangent bundle of the modular surface.

Introduction

Let
G:=
ASL
(2,\mathbb{R})
be the semi-direct product SL
(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2}

with multiplication rule

(M,x)\cdot(M^{\prime},x^{\prime})=(MM^{\prime},xM^{\prime}+x).

Let

SL

\nu
by restricting the Haar measure on

to a fundamental domain for the left-action of
\Gamma
on

is invariant under the action of
G

is a measure preserving dynamical system

On the space

u(t):=\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\

\Phi(t):=\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}e^{t/2}&0\\

Orbits of

. In general, following

.

For such a horocycle section, we define
\Lambda(t)=\Lambda_{\xi}(t):=t\xi_{1}(t)+\xi_{2}(t)
. Such a horocycle section is called

, if there exists

such that
n(t+P)=\gamma n(t)
for all

. Moreover, we call such a horocycle section

if there are rationals

such that the Lebesgue measure of the set

In

Theorem 1

]

Let

\frac{1}{P}\int_{0}^{P}f(n(t)a(y))\;dt\to\int_{X}f\;d\nu.

as

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(n(t)a(y))\psi(t)\;dt=\int_{X}f\;d\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)\;dt+O(y^{\delta})

Preliminaries: Sobolev norms

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and a
C^{d}
function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
we define its
L^{p}
Sobolev norm of order
d
by

||\psi||_{W^{d,p}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{d}||\psi^{(j)}||_{p}\in[0,\infty].

As is well known from the theory of Sobolev spaces, this norm can be extended to a much larger class of functions. However, for the purposes of this paper, we merely remark that it is also defined for functions
\psi
which are piecewise
C^{d}
as such functions derivatives are defined almost everywhere.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. Every element
A\in\mathfrak{g}
defines a
differential operator on
C^{\infty}(X)
via the equation

Af(g):=\frac{d}{dt}f(g\exp(At))\big|_{t=0}.

Now, we choose the basis
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{5}
of
\mathfrak{g}
where

X_{1}=(X,\mathrm{0}),\;X_{2}=(Y,\mathbf{0}),\;X_{3}=(Z,\mathbf{0}),\;X_{4}=(\mathbf{0},(1,0)),\text{ and }X_{5}=(\mathbf{0},(0,1))\;

and

X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{d}
be the set of monomials
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{5}
of degree at most
d
. For a function
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
, we define its
(L^{\infty})
Sobolev norm of degree
d
by

||f||_{C_{b}^{d}}:=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}||Df||_{\infty}.

We let
C_{b}^{d}(X)
be the space of all
d
times differentiable functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
whose
||\cdot||_{C_{b}^{d}}
-norm is finite.

These Sobolev norms have the following properties, discussed in greater generality in
[
ELS21
]
[§3]:

•

(Nesting) For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in C_{b}^{d}(X)
we have that
f\in C_{b}^{d^{\prime}}(X)
for any
d^{\prime}\leq d
and

||f||_{C_{b}^{d^{\prime}}}\leq||f||_{C_{b}^{d}}

(7)

•

(Product Rule) For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{b}^{d}(X)
we have that
f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{b}^{d}(X)
with

||f_{1}f_{2}||_{C^{d}_{b}}\ll_{d}||f_{1}||_{C^{d}_{b}}||f_{2}||_{C^{d}_{b}}

(8)

•

(Distance bound) For all
d\geq 1
,
f\in C_{b}^{d}(X)
and
g\in G
we have that

||g\cdot f-f||_{\infty}\ll\rho(g,e)||f||_{C^{1}_{b}(X)}.

(9)

where
(g\cdot f)(x):=f(xg)
and
e\in G
is the identity.

•

(Adjoint bound) For all
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in C_{b}^{d}(X)
and
g\in G
we have that

||g\cdot f||_{C^{d}_{b}(X)}\ll_{d}|||g|||^{2d}||f||_{C^{d}_{b}(X)}

(10)

where
|||g|||
is the maximal value of the entries of
g
and
g^{-1}
.

Proof of properties.

Nesting (
7
) is immediate from the definition of Sobolev norms.

In terms of the product rule (
8
), we have that the standard product rule for differentiation tells us that, whenever
A\in\mathfrak{g}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{b}^{d}(X)

A(f_{1}f_{2})=A(f_{1})f_{2}+f_{1}A(f_{2}).

Therefore, for any
D\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
,
D(f_{1}f_{2})
is a sum of terms of the form
D_{1}(f_{1})D_{2}(f_{2})
where
D_{1},D_{2}
are monomials whose degrees sum to
d
. Hence,
||D(f_{1}f_{2})||_{\infty}\ll_{d}||f_{1}||_{C^{b}_{d}}||f_{2}||_{C^{b}_{d}}
and so, summing over all
D\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
, the claimed result follows.

For the distance bound (
9
), let
g\in G
and
w:[0,\rho(g,e)]\to G
be unit speed path of shortest length between
e
and
g
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, for any
h\in G
and
f\in C_{b}^{d}(X)
,

(g\cdot f)(h)-f(h)=\int_{0}^{\rho(g,e)}\frac{d}{ds}f(hw(s))|_{s=t}\;dt.

(11)

Now,
\frac{d}{dt}f(hw(s))|_{s=t}=[Af](hw(t))
for
A=A(t)=\frac{d}{ds}w(t)^{-1}w(t+s)|_{s=0}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since the path
w
is of unit speed,
\rho(w(t)^{-1}w(s+t),e)=s
. So,
A(t)
’s entries are bounded and hence it is a linear combination
\sum_{i=1}^{5}\alpha_{i}X_{i}
of the
X_{i}
with coefficients
\alpha_{i}
bounded uniformly in
t
and
g
. Thus,

\bigg|\frac{d}{ds}f(hw(s))|_{s=t}\bigg|\ll||f||_{C_{b}^{1}}

for all
t\in[0,\rho(g,e)]
. The result follows from plugging this absolute value bound into (
11
).

Finally, for the adjoint bound (
10
), note that whenever
A\in\mathcal{g}
,
f\in C_{b}^{1}(X)
and
g,h\in G
we have that

\displaystyle A(g\cdot f)(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}f(h\exp(tA)g)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}f(hg\exp(tg^{-1}Ag))

\displaystyle=(g\cdot[g^{-1}Ag]f)(h).

For
g\in\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{5}
, the size of the entries
g^{-1}Ag
will be bounded, up to a constant, by
|||g|||^{2}
. So, when
g^{-1}Ag
is written as a linear combination of the
X_{i}
, the coefficients will be bounded, up to a constant, by
|||g|||^{2}
. More generally, when
D=A_{1}\cdot\dotsc\cdot A_{d}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
, we have that

D(g\cdot f)=g\cdot[(g^{-1}A_{1}g)\cdot\dotsc\cdot(g^{-1}A_{d}g)]f

and
g^{-1}A_{1}g\cdot\dotsc\cdot g^{-1}A_{d}g
can be written as a sum of monomials in
\mathcal{M}_{d}
whose coefficients are bounded, up to a constant, by
(|||g|||^{2})^{d}=|||g|||^{2d}.

∎

The non-primitive case

In

Proposition 1
.

For any

\int_{X}(u(t)\cdot f)\;h\;d\nu=\int_{X}f\;d\nu\int_{X}h\;d\nu+O_{\epsilon}((1+t)^{-1+\epsilon}||f||_{C_{b}^{4}}||h||_{C_{b}^{4}})

Proof.

Let
f,h\in C_{b}^{4}(X)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
For

we set

k(\theta):=\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta)&-\sin(\theta)\\
\sin(\theta)&\cos(\theta)\end{pmatrix},(0,0)\bigg).

As in
[
Rat87
]
[Lemma 2.3], we can find
\theta_{1}=\theta_{1}(t),\theta_{2}=\theta_{2}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u(t)=k(\theta_{1})\Phi(2\sinh^{-1}(t))k(\theta_{2})
. Now, the flow given by the right action of the one-parameter subgroup

is exponentially mixing. Indeed, by

]
we know that

\int_{X}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x\Phi(s))\;d\nu(x)=\int_{X}f_{1}\;d\nu\int_{X}f_{2}\;d\nu+O(se^{-s/2}||f_{1}||_{C^{4}_{b}}||f_{2}||_{C^{4}_{b}})

for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{b}^{d}(X)
. Setting
f_{1}=k(-\theta_{1})\cdot h
,
f_{2}=k(\theta_{2})\cdot f
,
s=2\sinh^{-1}(t)
and using the right invariance of the measure

we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(xk(\theta_{1})\Phi(2\sinh^{-1}(t))k(\theta_{2}))h(x)\;d\nu(x)=\int_{X}f\;d\nu\int_{X}h\;d\nu

\displaystyle+O(\sinh^{-1}(t)e^{-\sinh^{-1}(t)}||k(-\theta_{1})\cdot h||_{C^{4}_{b}}||k(\theta_{2})\cdot f||_{C^{4}_{b}})

Note that, by the compactness of the group

, the implicit constant in the bound (

) for elements

can be made independent of the choice of

as

we get the desired error term.

(13)

provided that
(1+m)N^{-1/2}A(\xi)\leq c
.

Similarly, if
\alpha<\beta
,
s,t\in[\alpha,\beta]
and
N>0
then we have that

\rho(n(s)a(1/N),n(t)a(1/N))\ll N|s-t|+(1+|s-t|)A(\xi)N^{-1/2},

(14)

provided that
N|s-t|+(1+|s-t|)A(\xi)N^{-1/2}\leq c
.

Proof.

For the first bound, writing

\displaystyle n(t+m/N)a(1/N)

\displaystyle=(I,\xi(t+m/N))u(t+m/N)a(1/N)

\displaystyle n(t)a(1/N)u(m)

\displaystyle=(I,\xi(t))u(t+m/N)a(1/N)

and using left invariance, we have that

\displaystyle\rho(n(t)a(1/N)u(m),n(t+m/N)a(1/N))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho((I,\xi(t+m/N)-\xi(t))n(t+m/N)a(1/N)),n(t+m/N)a(1/N))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho((I,(\xi(t+m/N)-\xi(t))n(t+m/N)a(1/N)),(I,(0,0))).

We then calculate that

\displaystyle(\xi(t+m/N)-\xi(t))n(t+m/N)a(1/N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\xi_{1}(t+m/N)-\xi_{1}(t))N^{-1/2},

\displaystyle((t+m/N)(\xi_{1}(t+m/N)-\xi_{1}(t))+\xi_{2}(t+m/N)-\xi_{2}(t))N^{1/2}).

The first component has absolute value at most
2||\xi_{1}||_{L^{\infty}[\alpha,\beta]}N^{-1/2}
and the second, using the Lipschitz condition on
\xi_{2}
, has absolute value bounded above by
(2L_{\xi}+||\xi_{1}||_{L^{\infty}[\alpha,\beta]})mN^{-1/2}
.
Using that there is a neighbourhood of
(I,(0,0))\in G
around which
\rho
is Lipschitz equivalent to metric on
G
induced from, say, the
L^{\infty}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{6}\cong M_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
(
[
EW11
]
[Lemma 9.12]), we can find a constant
c>0
such that, provided that

||\xi_{1}||_{L^{\infty}[\alpha,\beta]}N^{-1/2}+(2L_{\xi}+||\xi_{1}||_{L^{\infty}[\alpha,\beta]})mN^{-1/2}\leq 2A(\xi)(1+m)N^{-1/2}\leq 2c

we have

\rho(n(t)a(1/N)u(m),n(t+m/N)a(1/N))\ll A(\xi)(1+m)N^{-1/2}.

The proof of the second bound is similar to the first. However, now we calculate that

\displaystyle\rho(n(s)a(1/N),n(t)a(1/N))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(u(N(s-t)),(\xi(s)-\xi(t))n(s)a(1/N)),(I,(0,0)).

So we find that

||(\xi(s)-\xi(t))n(s)a(1/N))||_{\infty}\ll N^{-1/2}(1+|s-t|)(||\xi_{1}||_{L^{\infty}[\alpha,\beta]}+L_{\xi}).

Together with the fact that, for any matrix norm
||\cdot||
we have

||n(N(s-t))-I||\ll N|s-t|,

the result follows as above.
∎

The primitive case

Turning now to the primitive case, our approach is going revolve around using Fourier analysis. Indeed, for a compactly supported function

has zero mean. This lemma
inspired by

Lemma 2

where

and

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
2

containing supp
(\psi)

\psi

Note that we can still apply Corollary

Let
F_{N}(t):=f(n(t)a(1/N))\psi(t)

Define

Let

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{N}((k+s)/N)e(c(k+s))\bigg)+O\bigg(\frac{T||f||_{\infty}||\psi||_{\infty}}{N}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}F_{N}((k+s)/N)e(c(k+s))\bigg)+O\bigg(\frac{T||f||_{\infty}||\psi||_{\infty}}{N}\bigg).

|I|\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}F_{N}((k+s)/N)e(c(k+s))\bigg|+O\bigg(\frac{T||f||_{\infty}||\psi||_{\infty}}{N}\bigg).

(28)

Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, expanding and rearranging we have that

\displaystyle|I^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{W+1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}F_{N}((k+s)/N)e(c(k+s)/N)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{W+1}{T^{2}}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{T-1}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{T-1}\frac{e(c(s_{1}-s_{2})/N)}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{N}((k+s_{1})/N)F_{N}((k+s_{2})/N)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{W+1}{T^{2}}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{T-1}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{T-1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{N}((k+s_{1})/N)F_{N}((k+s_{2})/N)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{W+1}{T^{2}}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{T-1}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{T-1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{N}(k/N)F_{N}((k+s_{2}-s_{1})/N)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{W+1}{T}\sum_{s\in[-T,T]_{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{N}(k/N)F_{N}((k+s)/N)\bigg|

where in the last sum we have let
s=s_{2}-s_{1}
.
Now, note for
s
in the above sum, we have that
(k+s)/N\in[\alpha-N^{-1/2},\beta+N^{-1/2}]
, and so by Lemma
1
and (
9
)

\displaystyle f(n((k+s)/N)a(1/N))=f(n(k/N)a(1/N)u(s))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O(TN^{-1/2}A_{N}^{\prime}(\xi)||f||_{C^{1}_{b}(X)}).

Therefore, using the notation
\psi_{a}(t):=\psi(t+a)
we have that
|I^{\prime}|^{2}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{W+1}{T}\sum_{s\in[-T,T]_{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle([u(s)\cdot f][f])(n(k/N)a(1/N))\psi_{s/N}(k/N)\psi(k/N)\bigg|

\displaystyle+O(TN^{-1/2}A^{\prime}(\xi)||f||_{C^{1}_{b}(X)}).

By Corollary
3
we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}([u(s)\cdot f][f])(n(k/N)a(1/N))\psi_{s/N}(k/N)\psi(k/N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}([u(s)\cdot f]f)\;d\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{s/N}(t)\psi(t)\;dt

(29)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O((W+1)C_{N}^{\prime}(\xi,L)||[u(s)\cdot f][f]||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}||\psi_{s/N}\cdot\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}N^{-\delta_{1}}).

(30)

As in the proof of Proposition
2
we have that

\int_{X}([u(s)\cdot f][f])\;d\nu\ll_{\epsilon}(1+|s|)^{-1+\epsilon}||f||_{C^{4}_{b}(X)}

and so we have an overall bound on (
29
) of

O_{\epsilon}((W+1)(1+|s|)^{-1+\epsilon}||f||^{2}_{C^{4}_{b}(X)}||\psi||^{2}_{\infty}).

Similarly, for
s\in[-T,T]
we have that
||[u(s)\cdot f][f]||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}\ll(1+|s|)^{2d^{\prime}}||f||^{2}_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}
. Applying the product rule then gives us a bound of

O((W+1)C_{N}^{\prime}(\xi,L)(1+|s|)^{2d^{\prime}}||f||^{2}_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}||\psi||^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}})N^{-\delta_{1}}

on (
30
).
This gives us the overall bound

|I^{\prime}|^{2}\ll_{\epsilon}(W+1)^{2}C_{N}^{\prime}(\xi,L)||f||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}^{2}||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}(T^{-1+\epsilon}+TN^{-1/2}+T^{2d^{\prime}}N^{-\delta_{1}}).

Choosing
T=N^{\delta_{1}/1+2d^{\prime}}
and combining plugging the resulting bound on
I^{\prime}
into (
28
) then gives the result for any
\delta_{2}<\delta_{1}/(2+4d^{\prime})
.

∎

Corollary 4
.

Suppose that
n(t)=(I,\xi(t))u(t)
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3
for some
\delta>0
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
C=C(\xi)
. Then there exists
\delta_{2}>0

such that for
d^{\prime}:=\max(d,4)
any
f\in C_{b}^{d^{\prime}}(X)
and any continuously differentiable compactly supported
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{Z}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n(p/q)a(1/q))\psi(p/q)=\int_{X}f\;d\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)\;dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O_{\epsilon}((W+1)C^{\prime}(\xi,L)^{1/2}||f||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}q^{-\delta_{2}+\epsilon})

where
L,W
and
C^{\prime}(\xi,L)
are defined as in Corollary
3
.

Proof.

We will suppose initially that supp
(\psi)\subset[a,b]
with
b-a=1
. We will also suppose, initially, that
f
has zero mean. As in the proof of Lemma
2
, let
F_{q}(t):=f(n(t)a(1/q))\psi(t)
.
Now, let

\hat{F}_{q}(k):=\int_{a}^{b}F_{q}(t)e(-kt)\;dt.

By the standard theory of Fourier series, we have that

F_{q}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{F}_{q}(k)e(kt)

whenever
t\in[a,b]
. Using this, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{Z}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n(p/q)a(1/q))\psi(p/q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{F}_{q}(k)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in[qa,qb]_{\mathbb{Z}}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(kp/q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{F}_{q}(k)S(q,k)

where

S(q,k):=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in[qa,qb]_{\mathbb{Z}}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(kp/q)=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq q-1:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(kp/q).

Since
S(q,k)
clearly only depends upon
k\pmod{q}
, we can write

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{F}_{q}(k)S(q,k)=\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}S(q,r)\sum_{k\equiv r\pmod{q}}\hat{F}_{q}(k).

(31)

At this stage, let us note the two following identities which hold for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
:

\frac{1}{q}\sum_{p=0}^{q-1}e(mp/q)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }q|m\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

(32)

and

\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=0\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q-1}e(mp/q)=\frac{\mu(q_{m})}{\phi(q_{m})}

(33)

where
\mu
is the Mobius function and
q_{m}:=q/(q,m)
.

Using (
32
) one sees that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\equiv r\pmod{q}}\hat{F}_{q}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\frac{1}{q}\sum_{p=0}^{q-1}e((k-r)p/q)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{p=0}^{q-1}e(-rp/q)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{F}_{q}(k)e(kp/q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{q}(p/q)e(-rp/q).

But, we see applying Lemma
2
that

\frac{1}{q}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{q}(p/q)e(-rp/q)\ll C^{\prime}(\xi,L)^{1/2}||f||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}q^{-\delta_{2}}

with implicit constant independent of
r
.

As a result, (
31
) tells us that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{F}_{q}(k)S(q,k)\ll\frac{C^{\prime}(\xi,L)^{1/2}||f||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}}{q^{\delta_{2}}}\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}|S(q,r)|.

So, it remains to estimate
\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}|S(q,r)|.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
we find that

\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\phi(m_{r})}=\sum_{d|m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq r\leq m-1\\
(r,m)=m/d\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(d)}\leq\sum_{d|m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq r\leq m-1\\
(m/d)|r\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(d)}.

Thus, using that
\phi(d)\gg_{\epsilon}d^{1-\epsilon}
and then that

\sum_{d|m}d^{\epsilon}\ll_{\epsilon}m^{2\epsilon}
we get that

\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\phi(m_{r})}\ll_{\epsilon}m^{\epsilon}.

Using (
33
) we therefore see that

\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}|S(q,r)|\ll_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}.

So, overall we have that

\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{Z}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n(p/q)a(1/q))\psi(p/q)\ll_{\epsilon}\frac{C^{\prime}(\xi,L)^{1/2}||f||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}}{q^{\delta_{2}-\epsilon}}

To deal with the case in which
f
has non-zero mean, we can perform the usual trick of replacing
f
by
f_{0}=f-\int_{X}f\;d\nu
. As a result, we are left to estimate

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{Z}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}\psi(p/q).

which is equal to
\int\psi(t)\;dt+O_{\epsilon}(||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}q^{-1+\epsilon}).

This is very similar to the above computation. To briefly outline, decomposing
\psi
as a Fourier series on the interval
[a,b]
, we write

\psi(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\psi}(k)e(kt)

and using (
33
), we arrive at

\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{Z}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}\psi(p/q)-\int_{X}\psi(t)\;dt\bigg|\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{|\hat{\psi}(k)|}{\phi(q_{k})}

Now, as above, we have that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{|\hat{\psi}(k)|}{\phi(q_{k})}\leq\sum_{d|q}\frac{1}{\phi(d)}\sum_{(q/d)|k}|\hat{\psi}(k)|=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{d|q}\frac{d}{\phi(d)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{1}{|k|}\bigg|\frac{kq}{d}\hat{\psi}\bigg(\frac{kq}{d}\bigg)\bigg|.

Using integration by parts, we see know that
k|\hat{\psi}(k)|\asymp|\hat{\psi^{\prime}}(k)|
and so, applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Parseval’s identity, we see that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{1}{|k|}\bigg|\frac{kq}{d}\hat{\psi}\bigg(\frac{kq}{d}\bigg)\bigg|\ll\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{\psi^{\prime}}(k)|^{2}\ll||\psi^{\prime}||_{2}

The claimed bound then follows from applying the same bounds on
\phi(d)
and the divisor sums we used previously.

Finally, to deal with the case where the width of the support of
\psi
is more than 1, we find a function
\Delta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with supp
(\Delta)\subset[0,1]
and which has the property that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta(t-j/2)=1

(34)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\Delta_{j}(t):=\Delta(t-j/2)
. Given that
\Delta_{j}\psi
always has support of width at most 1 and that, by the product rule,

||\psi\Delta_{j}||_{W^{1,\infty}}\ll||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{Z}:\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n(p/q)a(1/q))\psi(p/q)\Delta_{j}(p/q)=\int_{X}f\;d\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)\Delta_{j}(t)\;dt

(35)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{C^{\prime}(\xi,L)^{1/2}||f||_{C^{d^{\prime}}_{b}(X)}||\psi||_{W^{1,\infty}}}{q^{\delta_{2}-\epsilon}}\bigg)

Moreover, we have an exact equality here when the supports of
\Delta_{j}
and
\psi
don’t intersect. Given the number of
j
such that their supports do intersect is
\ll W+1
the result follows from summing (
35
) over all
j
and using (
34
).

∎

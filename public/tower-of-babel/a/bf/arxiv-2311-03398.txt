Generalized Werner’s formula and the connection between trigonometry with target sum problems
Hayato Isa

Abstract
This paper introduces the target sum function along with its characteristics. The target sum function takes a list of integers and a specific target integer as input values and expresses the number of ways to obtain the target sum by either adding or subtracting all of the integers from the given list. This function is rooted in the target sum problem which is explored in the fields of computer science and combinatorics. This paper, following the establishment of the generalized Werner’s formula, will propose a link between the target sum function and the definite integral of the product of sine and cosine functions based on the formula.



Keywords—
target sum problem, Werner’s formula, trigonometry

1
Introduction

1.1
Target sum problem

The target sum problem is a combinatorial problem that involves finding the number of ways to obtain a specific target integer by either adding or subtracting the elements from a given list of integers. The goal is to explore all possible combinations of additions and subtractions from the list to arrive at the target sum. For example, suppose we have the following list of integers: nums = [1, 2, 3] and the target integer: target = 0.

Then, possible combinations to obtain the target sum of 5 using the numbers in the ”nums” list are:

\displaystyle+1+2-3=0

\displaystyle-1-2+3=0

So, in this example, there are two ways to obtain the target sum of 0.

The known most optimized way to solve this problem is by using dynamic programming and it allows us to solve the problem in pseudo-polynomial time
O(ns)

(cf. [1]).

In this paper, for an arbitrary sequence
X_{n}=\{b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\}_{i=0}^{n}
,
T(X_{n})
represents the solution of a target sum problem with a target integer
b_{0}
and a list of integers [
b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{n}
]. Mathematically,
T(X_{n})
is the number of possible sign choices
\pm
that satisfy the equation
\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}=b_{0}
.

1.2
notation and main results

For an arbitrary sequence
X_{n}=\{b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\}_{i=0}^{n}
, define
T(X_{n})
as the number of possible sign choices
\pm
that satisfy the equation
\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}=b_{0}
.

Then the value of
T(X_{n})
can be computed as follows:

T(X_{n})=\frac{2^{n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)dx

For an arbitrary sequence
X_{n}=\{b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\}_{i=0}^{n}
, define
T_{e,m}(X_{n}),\hskip 9.24994pt-1\leq m\leq n
as the number of possible sign choices
\pm
that satisfy the equation
\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}=b_{0}
where the number of positive signs used for
b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{m}
is even. Then, we can simply express the definite integral of the product of sin and cos by using
T(X_{n}),T_{e,m}(X_{n})
.

For example, when
p+q\equiv 0\pmod{2},\hskip 9.24994ptp,q\in\mathbb{Z}

\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)\,dx=\begin{cases}0&m\equiv 0\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{n}}(p+q)\bigg(2T_{e,m}(X_{n})-T(X_{n})\bigg)&m\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
0&m\equiv 2\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}(p+q)\bigg(2T_{e,m}(X_{n})-T(X_{n})\bigg)&m\equiv 3\pmod{4}\\
\end{cases}

Theorem and Proof

Theorem 1
.

For all
X_{n}
, when we define
Y_{n}
as a sequence whose 0th term and kth term are swapped,
T(X_{n})=T(Y_{n})
.
(0<k\leq n)

Proof.

From the definition,
T(X_{n})
is the number of possible sign choices
\pm
that satisfy the equation
\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}=b_{0}
. Which means
T(X_{n})
is the number of possible sign choices
\pm
that satisfy the equation
b_{k}=b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{k-1}\pm b_{k+1}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}
or
-b_{k}=b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{k-1}\pm b_{k+1}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}
. Which implies
T(X_{n})
is also the number of possible sign choices
\pm
that satisfy the equation
b_{k}=\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{k-1}\pm b_{0}\pm b_{k+1}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}

∎

Lemma 2
.

For all
X_{n}

\int_{0}^{\pi}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)\,dx=T(X_{n})\pi

where the summation sums all
2^{n}
possible
\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)

Proof.

\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos{nx}\,dx=\begin{cases}\pi&\hskip 9.24994ptn=0\\
0&\hskip 9.24994ptn\neq 0\end{cases}

Therefore,
-b_{0}\pm b_{2}\pm...\pm b_{n}=0\iff\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)\,dx=\pi
. Also, since
T(X_{n})
is the number of possible sign choices
\pm
that satisfy the equation
\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm\ldots\pm b_{n}=b_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{2}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\bigg[\text{the number of ways to choose $\pm$ to make }\pm b_{1}\pm b_{2}\pm...\pm b_{n}=b_{0}\bigg]\cdot\pi

\displaystyle=T(X_{n})\pi

∎

Lemma 3
.

For all
X_{n}

\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)

where the summation sums all
2^{n}
possible
\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)

Proof.

When n=0, the equality holds.

When
n\geq 0
, suppose

\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)

Then,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n+1}\cos(b_{i}x)=\cos(b_{n+1}x)\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)\cos(b_{n+1}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\sum_{\pm}\bigg\{\cos\big(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x-b_{n+1}x\big)+\cos\big(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x+b_{n+1}x\big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n+1}\right)x\right)

Therefore, equality holds for all
X_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

”Generalized werner’s formula” For all
X_{n}
and
-1\leq m\in\mathbb{Z}

\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m+1}{2}}\rfloor}}{2^{n}}\begin{cases}\sum_{e\in S}\left(\cos\left(\left(b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)&\hskip 9.24994ptm\text{ is odd}\\
\sum_{e\in S}\left(\sin\left(\left(b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)&\hskip 9.24994ptm\text{ is even}\end{cases}

where the summation will sum all
2^{n}
possible
\cos\left(\left(b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)

Proof.

From Lemma 3, we can say for all
X_{n}

\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)

Considering applying
\prod_{i=0}^{m}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{i}}\right)
to both side of the equation

Then, when
m
is odd number

\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m+1}{2}}\rfloor}}{2^{n}}\sum_{e\in S}\left(\cos\left(\left(b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)

when
m
is even number

\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m+1}{2}}\rfloor}}{2^{n}}\sum_{e\in S}\left(\sin\left(\left(b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)

where
S=\{1,-1\}^{n}

∎

Theorem 5
.

For all
X_{n}

T(X_{n})=\frac{2^{n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)dx

Proof.

From Lemma 2, for all
X_{n}

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)dx=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{1}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)dx

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)dx=\frac{1}{2^{n}}T(X_{n})\pi\hskip 9.24994pt\text{($\because$ Lemma 2)}

\therefore T(X_{n})=\frac{2^{n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)dx

∎

Theorem 6
.

For all
X_{n}
, when
p+q\equiv 0\pmod{2},\hskip 9.24994ptp,q\in\mathbb{Z}

\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)\,dx=\begin{cases}0&m\equiv 0\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{n}}(p+q)\bigg(2T_{e,m}(X_{n})-T(X_{n})\bigg)&m\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
0&m\equiv 2\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}(p+q)\bigg(2T_{e,m}(X_{n})-T(X_{n})\bigg)&m\equiv 3\pmod{4}\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

From Lemma 4, substituting
b_{0}=-b_{0}
,

\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)\,dx=

\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m+1}{2}}\rfloor}}{2^{n}}\begin{cases}\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}-\sum_{e\in S}\left(\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)\,dx&\hskip 9.24994ptm\text{ is odd}\\
\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}-\sum_{e\in S}\left(\sin\left(\left(-b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)\,dx&\hskip 9.24994ptm\text{ is even}\end{cases}

Considering the case when m is odd

\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\cos{nx}\,dx=\begin{cases}(p+q)\pi&\hskip 9.24994ptn=0\\
0&\hskip 9.24994ptn\neq 0\end{cases}

Therefore, similar to lemma 2,

\displaystyle\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}-\sum_{e\in S}\left(\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}-\sum_{e\in S}\left(\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}+e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}\right)x\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\right)+\sum_{\pm}\cos\left(\left(-b_{0}\pm b_{2}\pm...\pm b_{n}\right)x\right)\,dx-(p+q)T(X_{n})\pi

\displaystyle=(p+q)\bigg[\text{two times the number of ways to choose $(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})$ to make }e_{1}b_{1}+...+e_{n}b_{n}=b_{0},\,\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}=-1\bigg]\pi

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-(p+q)T(X_{n})\pi

\displaystyle=(p+q)\cdot 2T_{e,m}(X_{n})\cdot\pi-(p+q)T(X_{n})\pi

\therefore\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)\,dx=\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m+1}{2}}\rfloor}}{2^{n}}(p+q)\bigg(2T_{e,m}(X_{n})-T(X_{n})\bigg)

Considering the case when m is even

\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\sin{nx}\,dx=\frac{1}{n}(\cos{nq\pi}-\cos{np\pi})

When
p+q\equiv 0\pmod{2}
,
(p,q)=(2k,2l),(2k+1,2l+1)
, where
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}

For both cases,
\cos{nq\pi}-\cos{np\pi}=0
, so

\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\sin{nx}\,dx=0

\therefore\int_{-q\pi}^{p\pi}\prod_{i=0}^{m}\sin(b_{i}x)\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}\cos(b_{i}x)\,dx=0

∎

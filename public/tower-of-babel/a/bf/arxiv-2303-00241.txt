Nonsymmetric q q -Cauchy identity and representations of the Iwahori algebra
Evgeny Feigin, Ievgen Makedonskyi, Daniel Orr

Abstract
. The t = 0 t=0 specialization of the Mimachi-Noumi Cauchy-type identity rewrites certain infinite product in terms of specialized nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials of type G ​ L n GL_{n} . We interpret the infinite product as a character of the space of functions on a certain matrix space. We show that the space of functions admits a filtration such that the graded pieces are isomorphic to the tensor products of certain generalized global Weyl modules of the Iwahori algebra. We identify the characters of the graded pieces with the terms of the specialized Mimachi-Noumi formula. We conjecture the existence of an analogous filtration on the space of functions on the Iwahori group for all simple Lie algebras and prove the conjecture for S ​ L n SL_{n} . Our construction can be seen as a current algebra extension of the van der Kallen filtration on functions on a Borel subgroup.



1.
Introduction

The celebrated Cauchy identity

\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}=\sum_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0)}s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})s_{\lambda}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

allows to rewrite the infinite product in the left hand side as a sum of products of
Schur functions (see
[
M1
]
). The identity has a vast number of applications and interpretations (see
e.g.
[
BC
,
BP
,
BW
,
Ok
,
OR
,
St
]
). In particular, one can understand the left hand side as the character of polynomial functions on the space of square matrices and decompose this space of functions into the
direct sum of bimodules over the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. The products of Schur functions
show up in this approach as characters of tensor products of irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
modules.
From the point of view of representation theory, the Cauchy identity is a close cousin of the
Peter-Weyl theorem (see
[
PW
,
TY
]
), since the latter decomposes the space of function on a simple group as
the direct sum of tensor products of irreducible representations.

The classical Cauchy identity admits a generalization in the setting of current algebras.
The corresponding numerical identity reads as (see
[
M1
]
)

\prod_{k\geq 0}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(1-x_{i}y_{j}q^{k})^{-1}=\sum_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0)}p_{\lambda}(x;q)p_{\lambda}(y;q)\prod_{a=1}^{n}(q)_{\lambda_{a}-\lambda_{a+1}}^{-1},

where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
p_{\lambda}(x;q)
denotes the
q
-Whittaker functions
and
(q)_{m}
stands for the
q
-Pochhammer symbol (we note that the
q
-Whittaker functions are equal to the
t=0
specializations of the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials
E_{w_{0}\lambda}(x;q,0)
, see
[
BF
,
Et
,
CO1
,
CO2
,
GLO
]
).
In
[
FKM
]
the authors suggested
to interpret the left hand side as the character of the space of polynomial functions on the space of square
matrices with coefficients in polynomial ring in one variable. The authors constructed
a filtration on this space of functions such that the homogeneous components of the associated
graded space are isomorphic to the tensor products (over the highest weight algebras) of global
Weyl modules. The paper
[
FKM
]
also contains a current algebras analogue of the Peter-Weyl
theorem, expressing the characters of the spaces of functions on current groups via products
of
t=0
specialized Macdonald polynomials.

The classical Cauchy identity has a non-symmetric version (see
[
CK
,
L
,
MN
]
)

\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}=\sum_{\lambda\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{n}}E_{\lambda}(x;0,0)E_{\lambda}(y;\infty,\infty).

Here
E_{\lambda}
stands for the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials of type
GL_{n}
,
E_{\lambda}(x;0,0)
are the
key polynomials (a.k.a. the characters of the Demazure modules) and

E_{\lambda}(y;\infty,\infty)
are the Demazure atoms (a.k.a. the characters of the van der Kallen modules – the
quotients of the Demazure modules by the sum of all smaller Demazure submodules). A
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-version of a theorem of van der Kallen
[
vdK
]
provides a representation-theoretic counterpart of the nonsymmetric
Cauchy identity. Namely, the left hand side is replaced by the character of the space
of polynomial functions on the upper triangular matrices. This space of functions admits
a filtration (indexed by
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
) such that the homogeneous pieces of the associated graded space are isomorphic
to the tensor products of the Demazure modules and the van der Kallen modules. The van der Kallen theorem holds true for all simple Lie groups provided the space of upper triangular
matrices is replaced by the Borel subgroup of the corresponding simple Lie group.

The nonsymmetric Cauchy identity has a
(q,t)
-version due to Mimachi-Noumi (see
[
MN
]
). The
t=0
specialization of the Mimachi-Noumi formula reads as

(1.1)

\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{1}{(qx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}\\
=\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(x;q,0)E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},\infty)

where
a_{\lambda}(q)
is the Cherednik norm of
E_{\lambda}
. Note that (
1.1
) reduces to the nonsymmetric Cauchy identity above when
q=0
. One easily sees that the left hand side of
(
1.1
) computes the character of the space of polynomial functions on the Iwahori-type
subspace of the space of polynomial-valued matrices.
The main goal of this paper is
to find the representation theoretic interpretation of formula (
1.1
) and to write down and prove an analogue of (
1.1
) for
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
We also formulate a conjecture for all simple Lie algebras. Let us describe our results in more details.

In what follows we fix an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
0
.
Let
M_{n}
be the space of square matrices of size
n
with coefficients in
\Bbbk
. Let

M_{n}[z]=M_{n}\otimes\Bbbk[z]
be the space of matrices whose entries are polynomials in one variable.
We denote by
M_{n}[z]^{+}
the direct sum of the subspace of uppertriangular matrices with the space

M_{n}\otimes z\Bbbk[z]
(so
M_{n}[z]^{+}
is the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
version of the Iwahori subalgebra). Then the left hand
side of (
1.1
) is naturally identified with the character of the space of polynomial functions

\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]
, where
q
is responsible for the
z
-degree and
x_{i}
,
y_{j}
correspond to the left
and right actions of diagonal torus. In order to obtain the summands in the right hand side we construct
a filtration
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{n}
on the restricted dual space
\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]^{\vee}
and describe the homogeneous pieces
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
. More precisely,
for a composition
\lambda
we consider certain
left module
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
and a right module
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}

of the Iwahori algebra of type
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Both modules are (
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-versions of) the generalized global Weyl modules with
characteristics (see
[
FMO
,
FKM
,
NS
,
NNS1
,
NNS2
]
). The modules
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}
are endowed
with the action of the graded (commutative) algebra
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}
(the so-called
highest weight algebra) commuting with the action of the current algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[z]

[
CFK
]
). Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

For any
\lambda\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{n}
,

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/{\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}}\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}.

We also show that the algebra
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}
acts freely on
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}
. In order to relate Theorem 1 with formula (
1.1
) we note that (see
[
FKM
,
I
,
OS
,
RY
,
Sa
]
)

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(x;q,0),\ {\mathop{\rm ch}}\,\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}=a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},\infty),\ {\mathop{\rm ch}}\,\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}=a_{\lambda}(q).

The nonsymmetric
q
-Cauchy identity (
1.1
) has an
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-version. We note that the
weight lattice
P
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
is obtained as a quotient of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

by the span of the vector
(1,\dots,1)
. Collecting terms in (
1.1
) with the same
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-weight, we obtain the
identity

(1.2)

(x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}y_{1}\dotsm y_{n};q)_{\infty}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{1}{(qx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}=\\
\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}}a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(x;q,0)E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},\infty),

where
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
is the subset of nonnegative integer vectors with at least one coordinate
equal to zero. We show that the left hand side coincides with the character of the space of polynomial
functions on the Iwahori group
{\mathbf{I}}\subset SL_{n}[z]
. Now our goal is to give a representation
theoretic realization of the right hand side of (
1.2
). To this end we note that

(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
is identified with the weight lattice
P
of the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
For an element
\lambda\in P
we consider the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}[z]
modules
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
and

\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}
, which are special cases of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
generalized global Weyl modules
with characteristics. Both modules admit an action of a polynomial algebra
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}
which commutes with the action of the current
algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}[z]
(we note that in the simply-laced case the
0
-specialization
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}}\Bbbk_{0}
is isomorphic to the corresponding level
1
affine Demazure module).
We introduce an increasing filtration
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
on the space of functions

\Bbbk[\mathbf{I}]
such that the following theorem holds:

Theorem 2
.

For any
\lambda\in P
,

(1.3)

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu}\simeq(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}}\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o})^{\vee},

where
\vee
in the upper index denotes the restricted dual space.

As in the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
case
algebra
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}
acts freely on
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}
. There is a natural map from the right-hand side to the left-hand side of (
1.3
). The numerical identity (
1.2
) is equivalent to the assertion that this map is an isomorphism.

The filtration
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
case can be seen as the generalization of the van der Kallen
result
[
vdK
]
to the Iwahori case. We conjecture that Theorem
2
holds true for arbitrary simple Lie algebra
(at the moment we are unable to prove it due to several technical difficulties). Let
P
be a weight lattice of a
simple Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
and let
\Delta=\Delta_{+}\sqcup\Delta_{-}
be the set of roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We also let
{\mathbf{I}}
denote
the Iwahori subgroup inside
G[z]
, where
G
is the Lie group of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Conjecture 1
.

The space of functions
\Bbbk[\mathbf{I}]
admits a filtration such that the associated graded space is isomorphic
to the direct sum over
\lambda\in P
of modules of the form
(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}}\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o})^{\vee}
for certain generalized global Weyl modules with characteristics
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}

and
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}
.

We are able to show that for all
{\mathfrak{g}}
the modules
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}
admit a free action of the highest weight algebra
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}
. The combinatorial counterpart of Conjecture
1
is given as follows.

Conjecture 2
.

One has an identity

\frac{\sum_{\lambda\in P}X^{\lambda}Y^{\lambda}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(1-X^{\alpha})\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}(qX^{\alpha};q)_{\infty}}=\\
\sum_{\lambda\in P}a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(X;q,0)E_{\lambda}(Y;q^{-1},\infty)

for
a_{\lambda}(q)
being the Cherednik norm of
E_{\lambda}
.

For
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, Conjecture
2
follows from (
1.2
), as we will show in Theorem
3.4
.
We note that a closely related question to Conjecture
1
is the existence of a BGG reciprocity theorem in the Iwahori setting (see
[
CI
,
CG
,
Kh
]
). We plan to address this question elsewhere.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect basic notation on representations of simple Lie algebras. In Section
3
we study the combinatorics of the nonsymmetric
q
-Cauchy identities for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
In Section
4
we introduce and study certain families of modules of the Iwahori algebra. These modules are used in Section
5
to derive a representation theoretic description of the nomsymmetric
q
-Cauchy identity and in Section
6
to prove an analogous theorem in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
case. Finally, in Appendix we consider the rank one example.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Simple Lie algebras

Suppose
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a simple Lie algebra over
{\Bbbk}
with Cartan decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}
. Let

\Delta=\Delta_{+}\sqcup\Delta_{-}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be the root system of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For each
\alpha\in\Delta_{+}
, we denote by
e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
the corresponding Chevalley generator of
\mathfrak{g}
. Similarly, for

\alpha\in\Delta_{-}
, we denote by
f_{\alpha}
the Chevalley generator of weight
\alpha
in
{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}
.
For
\alpha\in\Delta
, we write
\alpha>0
for
\alpha\in\Delta_{+}
and
\alpha<0
for
\alpha\in\Delta_{-}
. We denote by
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}
the simple roots and by

\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}
the fundamental weights. For a root
\alpha\in\Delta
, we denote by
\alpha^{\vee}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
the corresponding coroot.
For the canonical pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\times{\mathfrak{h}}\to{\Bbbk}
, we have
\langle\omega_{i},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
.

Let
Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice and
Q_{+}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i}
the positive root cone. Let
P=\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{i}
be the weight lattice and
P_{+}=\sum_{i\in I}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\omega_{i}
the dominant weight cone. Let
P_{-}:=-P_{+}
be the antidominant weight cone. Let
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\alpha\in P_{+}
.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For a root
\alpha\in\Delta
, the corresponding reflection is denoted by
s_{\alpha}\in W
. For each
i\in I
, we set
s_{i}:=s_{\alpha_{i}}
. Let
w_{0}
be the longest element in
W
.

For each
\lambda\in P
, let
\lambda_{-}\in P_{-}
be the unique antidominant weight belonging to the
W
-orbit of
\lambda
. Denote by
v(\lambda)
the unique element of
W
of minimal length such that
v(\lambda)\lambda=\lambda_{-}
. Define a partial order
\succeq
on
P
by
\lambda\succeq\mu
if and only if
\lambda_{-}-\mu_{-}\in-Q_{+}
and
v(\lambda)\leq v(\mu)
when
\lambda_{-}=\mu_{-}
.

2.2.
Characters

Let
\mathbb{Z}[P]
be the group algebra of
P
, which is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module with basis
\{X^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in P}
satisfying
X^{\lambda}X^{\mu}=X^{\lambda+\mu}
for all
\lambda,\mu\in P
. Denote by
\mathbb{Z}[[P]]
be the
\mathbb{Z}[P]
-module consisting of all (possibly infinite) formal sums
\sum_{\lambda\in P}c_{\lambda}X^{\lambda}
where
c_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Setting
X_{i}=X^{\omega_{i}}
for
i\in I
, we can also write
X^{\lambda}=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}^{\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle}
.

For any (left)
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module
M
and
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, we set

{}_{\lambda}M=\{m\in M:\text{$hm=\lambda(h)m$ for all $h\in{\mathfrak{h}}$}\}.

If
M
has a decomposition

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P}{}_{\lambda}M,\qquad\dim{}_{\lambda}M<\infty,

then we define the character of
M
as the formal sum

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,M=\sum_{\lambda\in P}(\dim{}_{\lambda}M)X^{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Z}[[P]].

Analogously, for any right
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module
M
, we set

M_{\lambda}=\{m\in M:\text{$mh=\lambda(h)m$ for all $h\in{\mathfrak{h}}$}\}.

If
M
has a decomposition

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P}M_{\lambda},\qquad\dim M_{\lambda}<\infty,

then we define the right character

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,M=\sum_{\lambda\in P}(\dim M_{\lambda})Y^{\lambda}

inside another copy of
\mathbb{Z}[[P]]
with basis elements denoted
\{Y^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in P}
.

Finally, for
M
an
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}})
-bimodule, we set
{}_{\lambda}M_{\mu}={}_{\lambda}M\cap M_{\mu}
for all
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
. If
M
has a decomposition

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda,\mu\in P}{}_{\lambda}M_{\mu},\dim{}_{\lambda}M_{\mu}<\infty,

then we define the bimodule character as

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,M=\sum_{\lambda,\mu\in P}(\dim{}_{\lambda}M_{\mu})X^{\lambda}Y^{\mu}

as an element of
\mathbb{Z}[[P\times P]]=\{\sum_{\lambda,\mu}c_{\lambda,\mu}X^{\lambda}Y^{\mu}:c_{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. The latter is a module over
\mathbb{Z}[P\times P]\cong\mathbb{Z}[P]\otimes\mathbb{Z}[P]
, whose elements are finite sums of the above form.

In the graded setting (see §
4.1
), we also consider the abelian group
(\mathbb{Z}[[P\times P]])[[q]]
consisting of power series in
q
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[[P\times P]]
, which is a module over the ring
(\mathbb{Z}[P\times P])[[q]]
.

2.3.
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}

We also consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Let
E_{ij}\ (i,j=1,\dots,n)
be the matrix units. We consider the Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
of diagonal matrices,
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{span}\{E_{11},\dots,E_{nn}\}
. We use the standard Cartan decomposition with
{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}=\mathrm{span}\{E_{ij}\}_{i<j}.

We denote by
P(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
the weight lattice
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_{i}
. The roots are the elements
\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
,
i\neq j
, with the positive roots given by
i<j
. We use the notation
h_{i}:=E_{ii}
and
e_{\alpha}=E_{ij}
and
f_{-\alpha}=E_{ji}
for any positive root
\alpha=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
,
i<j
.

We identify
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with the element
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\in P(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. Dominant weights then correspond to tuples
(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\dotsm\geq\lambda_{n}
. The Weyl group is the symmetric group
S_{n}
.

Elements of
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
are called compositions. In the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
case, we consider characters of
{\mathfrak{h}}
-modules whose weights are compositions. Thus, for a left
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module
M
such that

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}{}_{\lambda}M,\qquad\dim{}_{\lambda}M<\infty,

where
M_{\lambda}=\{m\in M:\text{$h_{i}m=\lambda_{i}m$ for $i=1,\dotsc,n$}\}
,
we define the character

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,M=\sum_{\lambda\in P}(\dim{}_{\lambda}M)x^{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Z}[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]]

where
x^{\lambda}=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\dotsm x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}
. The characters of right modules are defined in a similar way with variables
\{y_{i}\}
instead of
\{x_{i}\}
. Characters of bimodules are then defined using both sets of variables in the natural way, as above.

We realize the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
as the quotient
P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})=P(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})/\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{1}
, where
\mathbf{1}=\varepsilon_{1}+\dotsm+\varepsilon_{n}
, and we denote the quotient map
P(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\to P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
by
\lambda\mapsto\overline{\lambda}
. Each representation of the Cartan subalgebra, Borel subalgebra etc. in type
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
can be considered as representations of the corresponding subalgebras in type
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
. The restriction map on characters of finite-dimensional modules is achieved by the surjective ring homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{res}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{Z}[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\longmapsto X^{\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}}

Recalling the notation
X_{i}=X^{\omega_{i}}
, where
\omega_{i}\ (i=1,\dotsc,n-1)
are the fundamental weights of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, we can also describe this map as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{res}(x_{1})=X_{1},\quad\mathrm{res}(x_{i})=X_{i-1}^{-1}X_{i}(i=2,\dots,n-1),\quad\mathrm{res}(x_{n})\mapsto X_{n-1}^{-1}.

Note that
\mathbb{Z}[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]\cong\mathbb{Z}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsm,X_{n-1}^{\pm 1}]
is a Laurent polynomial ring, whereas for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
we have an ordinary polynomial ring because we only consider representations whose weights are compositions.

2.4.
q
-Pochammer symbols

Define
(a;q)_{m}=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(1-q^{i}a)
for
m\geq 0
,
(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-q^{i}a)
,
(q)_{m}=(q;q)_{m}
, and
(q)_{\infty}=(q;q)_{\infty}
.

3.
Nonsymmetric
q
-Cauchy identities in type
A

We recall the Cauchy identity of
[
MN
]
for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials and consider its
t=0
specialization. We establish a corresponding identity for specialized
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials. Note that we use the conventions of §
2.3
throughout this section.

3.1.
Macdonald polynomials

Fix a positive integer
n
and, as in §
2.3
, let
x=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n})
be variables. For any composition
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
, denote by
E_{\lambda}=E_{\lambda}(x;q,t)\in\mathbb{K}[x]
the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomial as defined in
[
Ch2
,
HHL
]
, where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
. The polynomial
E_{\lambda}(x;q,t)
is homogeneous in
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}
of total degree
|\lambda|:=\lambda_{1}+\dotsm+\lambda_{n}
.

The diagram of
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
is the set

\mathrm{dg}(\lambda)=\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\}.

We write
(i,j)\in\lambda
to mean
(i,j)\in\mathrm{dg}(\lambda)
. Following the conventions of
[
HHL
]
, we have we have the generalized arm and leg functions for
s=(i,j)\in\lambda
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{leg}_{\lambda}(s)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}-j

\displaystyle\mathrm{arm}_{\lambda}(s)

\displaystyle=|\{k<i:j\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{i}\}|+|\{k>i:j\leq\lambda_{k}+1\leq\lambda_{i}\}|.

We define

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{s\in\lambda}\frac{1-q^{\mathrm{leg}_{\lambda}(s)+1}t^{\mathrm{arm}_{\lambda}(s)+1}}{1-q^{\mathrm{leg}_{\lambda}(s)+1}t^{\mathrm{arm}_{\lambda}(s)}}.

The
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials
E_{\lambda}(X;q,t)\in\mathbb{K}[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]
(for
\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
) of
[

]
are related to those for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
via the restriction map
\mathrm{res}
of §

(extended
\mathbb{K}
-linearly). Specifically, for any
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}\subset P(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, one verifies directly from the definitions of these polynomials that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{res}(E_{\lambda}(x;q,t))=E_{\overline{\lambda}}(X;q,t).

3.2.
Nonsymmetric Cauchy identity

The nonsymmetric Cauchy identity of
[
MN
]
gives the expansion of the function

\displaystyle E(x,y;q,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1-tx_{i}y_{j}}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{(qtx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(qx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

into products of nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
MN
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

One has

(3.2)

\displaystyle E(x,y;q,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}a_{\lambda}(q,t)E_{\lambda}(x;q,t)E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},t^{-1}).

Proof.

Strictly speaking,
[
MN
, Theorem 2.1]
asserts that

\displaystyle E(x,y;q,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(q,t)\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(x;q,t)\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},t^{-1}),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=a_{w_{0}(\lambda)}(q,t)

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(x;q,t)

\displaystyle=w_{0}E_{w_{0}(\lambda)}(x;q^{-1},t^{-1}).

It is straightforward to see that the two formulas are equivalent.
∎

We regard (
3.2
) as an equality meromorphic functions. Both sides are regular for
|q|
,
|t|
,
|x_{i}|
,
|y_{i}|<1
. It is not immediate but well-known (see, e.g.,
[

.

3.3.
t=0
specialization

Setting
t=0
in (
3.2
), we obtain:

Corollary 3.2
.

We have

(3.3)

\displaystyle E(x,y;q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(x;q,0)E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},\infty)

where

\displaystyle E(x,y;q)

\displaystyle=E(x,y;q,0)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{1}{(qx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}

and

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(q)

\displaystyle=a_{\lambda}(q,0)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\lambda\\
\mathrm{arm}_{\lambda}(s)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-q^{\mathrm{leg}_{\lambda}(s)+1}}.

It follows from, e.g.,
[
HHL
]
that both of
E_{\lambda}(x;q,0)
and
E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},\infty)
have coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q]
. Moreover, by the homogeneity of
E_{\lambda}
, one sees that the number of summands on the right-hand side of (
3.3
) contributing to any fixed monomial
x^{\mu}y^{\nu}
is finite. In particular, both sides are convergent as formal power series and we may regard (
3.3
) as an identity in
\mathbb{Z}[[x,y,q]]=\mathbb{Z}[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n},y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n},q]]
.

The following lemma gives another expression for the quantity
a_{\lambda}(q)
:

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
, we have:

(3.4)

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(q)

\displaystyle=(q;q)_{(\lambda_{-})_{1}}^{-1}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\begin{cases}(q;q)_{-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle}^{-1}&\text{if $v(\lambda)^{-1}\alpha_{j}>0$ }\\
(q;q)_{-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle-1}^{-1}&\text{if $v(\lambda)^{-1}\alpha_{j}<0$}\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{-}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
and
v(\lambda)\in S_{n}
are as in §
2.1
.

Proof.

By
[
MN
, Lemma 2.5]
, we have the following for
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
:

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=a_{\lambda_{-}}(q,t)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha>0\\
\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>0\end{subarray}}\frac{(1-q^{\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}t^{-\langle\rho,v(\lambda)\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1})(1-q^{\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}t^{-\langle\rho,v(\lambda)\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1})}{(1-q^{\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}t^{-\langle\rho,v(\lambda)\alpha^{\vee}\rangle})^{2}}

Note that
\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>0
implies
\langle\lambda_{-},v(\lambda)\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>0
and hence
v(\lambda)\alpha<0
. Therefore, we may set
t=0
in the expression above to obtain

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(q,0)

\displaystyle=a_{\lambda_{-}}(q,0)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha>0\\
\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>0\\
\langle\rho,v(\lambda)\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=-1\end{subarray}}(1-q^{\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}).

Now, it is easy to see that the map
\alpha\mapsto-v(\lambda)\alpha
gives a bijection
between the sets

\{\alpha>0:\text{$\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>0$ and $\langle\rho,v(\lambda)\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=-1$}\}
and

\{\alpha_{j}:v(\lambda)^{-1}\alpha_{j}<0\}
.
Moreover, for
\alpha>0
and
\alpha_{j}=-v(\lambda)\alpha
belonging to these sets, we have
\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle.
Hence we can write

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(q)

\displaystyle=a_{\lambda_{-}}(q)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n-1\\
v(\lambda)^{-1}\alpha_{j}<0\end{subarray}}(1-q^{-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle}).

Finally, we consider

\displaystyle a_{\lambda_{-}}(q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\lambda_{-}\\
\mathrm{arm}_{\lambda_{-}}(s)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-q^{\mathrm{leg}_{\lambda_{-}}(s)+1}}

The cells
s\in\lambda_{-}
with
\mathrm{arm}_{\lambda_{-}}(s)=0
are precisely the following:
(j+1,(\lambda_{-})_{j}+1),\dotsc,(j+1,(\lambda_{-})_{j+1})
for
1\leq j<n
and
(1,1),\dotsc,(1,(\lambda_{-})_{1})
. These cells have
\mathrm{leg}_{\lambda_{-}}(s)+1
equal to
(\lambda_{-})_{j+1}-(\lambda_{-})_{j},\dotsc,1
for
1\leq j<n
and
(\lambda_{-})_{1},\dotsc,1
, respectively. Combining this with the preceding paragraph, we obtain the stated formula.
∎

3.4.
From
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
to
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}

Now, by considering the behavior of (
3.3
) under the projection
P(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\to P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
, we shall prove the following:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
E_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
be the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials. Then we have the following identity in
(\mathbb{Z}[[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\times P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]])[[q]]
:

(3.5)

\frac{\sum_{\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})}X^{\lambda}Y^{\lambda}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n-1}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1}{1-X^{\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}}Y^{\overline{\varepsilon}_{j}}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{1}{(qX^{\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}}Y^{\overline{\varepsilon}_{j}};q)_{\infty}}\\
=\sum_{\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})}{\mathop{\rm ch}}(\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda})E_{\lambda}(X;q,0)E_{\lambda}(Y;q^{-1},\infty).

where
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}
is the graded polynomial algebra defined in (
4.10
) below and
{\mathop{\rm ch}}(\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda})\in\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
is its Hilbert series.

Remark 3.5
.

Even though
(\mathbb{Z}[[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\times P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]])[[q]]
is not a ring, our proof will show that the product on the left-hand side of (
3.5
) is well-defined (when expanded formally).

Proof.

We begin by eliminating some redundancy on the right-hand side of (
3.3
). Let
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
be the subset of nonnegative integer vectors with at least one coordinate equal to
0
. Then every element of
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
can be written uniquely in the form
\lambda+m\mathbf{1}
for some
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
and
m\geq 0
. Moreover, each element of
P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
has a unique representative from
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
.

It is immediate from the definition of
E_{\lambda}
(e.g.,
[
HHL
, Def. 2.1.1]
) that

\displaystyle E_{\lambda+m\mathbf{1}}(x;q,t)

\displaystyle=(x_{1}\dotsm x_{n})^{m}E_{\lambda}(x;q,t).

Thus we can write the right-hand side of (
3.3
) as

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}}a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(x;q,0)E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},\infty)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}y_{1}\dotsm y_{n})^{m}}{(q;q)_{m}}.

By the
q
-binomial theorem, we can simplify the inner sum as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}y_{1}\dotsm y_{n})^{m}}{(q;q)_{m}}=\frac{1}{(x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}y_{1}\dotsm y_{n};q)_{\infty}}.

Moving this factor to the other side of (
3.3
), we arrive at

(3.6)

(x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}y_{1}\dotsm y_{n};q)_{\infty}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\frac{1}{(qx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}\\
=\sum_{\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}}a_{\lambda}(q)E_{\lambda}(x;q,0)E_{\lambda}(y;q^{-1},\infty).

The basic idea now is that we would like to consider the image of (
3.6
) under an extension of the projection
\mathrm{res}\otimes\mathrm{res}:x^{\lambda}y^{\mu}\mapsto X^{\overline{\lambda}}Y^{\overline{\mu}}
to infinite sums. However, this is not always defined and some care is required. For
F(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda,\mu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}c_{\lambda,\mu}x^{\lambda}y^{\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}[[x,y]]
, the sum

\displaystyle\overline{F}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\mu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}c_{\overline{\lambda},\overline{\mu}}X^{\overline{\lambda}}Y^{\overline{\mu}}\in\mathbb{Z}[[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\times P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]]

is well-defined
provided
that for each
\lambda,\mu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
there exist finitely many integers
k,l
such that
c_{\lambda+k\mathbf{1},\mu+l\mathbf{1}}\neq 0
.
Thus, for

F(x,y,q)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}(x,y)q^{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[[x,y,q]]=(\mathbb{Z}[[x,y]])[[q]],

we define

\displaystyle\overline{F}(X,Y,q)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{F}_{k}(x,y)q^{k}\in(\mathbb{Z}[[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\times P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]])[[q]]

precisely when each
F_{k}(x,y)
satisfies this finiteness condition.

We now apply these considerations to (
3.6
), beginning with the left-hand side.

Lemma 3.6
.

For

\displaystyle F(x,y)=\frac{1-x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-x_{i}y_{i})}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}

the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
character
\overline{F}(X,Y)
is well-defined and equal to

\displaystyle\overline{F}(X,Y)=\sum_{\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})}X^{\lambda}Y^{\lambda}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1}{1-X^{\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}}Y^{\overline{\varepsilon}_{j}}}.

Proof.

First, we have the following in
\mathbb{Z}[[x,y]]
, where we set
z_{i}=x_{i}y_{i}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1-x_{1}\dotsm x_{n}y_{1}\dotsm y_{n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-x_{i}y_{i})}

\displaystyle=(1-z_{1}\dotsm z_{n})\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}}z_{1}^{\nu_{1}}\dotsm z_{n}^{\nu_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}}z_{1}^{\nu_{1}}\dotsm z_{n}^{\nu_{n}}.

Thus we can write

(3.7)

\displaystyle F(x,y)=\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}}\sum_{\{m_{i,j}\}}x^{\nu+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}\varepsilon_{i}}y^{\nu+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}\varepsilon_{j}}

where the inner sum runs over all collections
\{m_{i,j}\}_{i<j}
of nonnegative integers. We need to show that for any fixed
\nu
and
\{m_{i,j}\}
as above, there exist finitely many other
\nu^{\prime}
and
\{m^{\prime}_{i,j}\}
such that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\nu+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}\varepsilon_{i}+k(1,\dotsc,1)=\nu^{\prime}+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}^{\prime}\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle\nu+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}\varepsilon_{j}+l(1,\dotsc,1)=\nu^{\prime}+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}^{\prime}\varepsilon_{j}

for some
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Taking the sum of all components on both sides, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle|\nu|+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}+kn=|\nu^{\prime}|+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle|\nu|+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}+ln=|\nu^{\prime}|+\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}^{\prime}

and these together imply that
k=l
. Now taking the difference of our original equations, we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})=\sum_{i<j}m_{i,j}^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}).

For fixed
\{m_{i,j}\}
, the number of
\{m_{i,j}^{\prime}\}
satisfying (
3.10
) is finite. Returning to (
3.8
), we now see that
k
is bounded above, since the smallest coordinate of the right-hand side is constrained by the requirement that
\nu^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
and the fact that only finitely many
\{m_{i,j}^{\prime}\}
are possible. Of course,
k
is also bounded below, because the left-hand side of (
3.9
) must be nonnegative. We conclude that there are only finitely many possibilities for
k
and therefore only finitely many
\nu^{\prime}
.

Knowing now that
\overline{F}(X,Y)
is well-defined, the stated formula follows from (
3.7
).
∎

The remaining factors on the left-hand side (
3.6
) actually belong to the subring
\mathbb{Z}[x,y][[q]]\subset\mathbb{Z}[[x,y,q]]
and hence map to elements of the ring
\mathbb{Z}[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\times P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})][[q]]
. From this one deduces that the left-hand side of (
3.6
) has well-defined projection to
\mathbb{Z}[[P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\times P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})]][[q]]
and that the result is given by the left-hand side of (
3.5
). Here one must observe that

(qX^{\mu}Y^{\mu};q)_{\infty}^{\pm 1}\sum_{\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})}X^{\lambda}Y^{\lambda}=(q;q)_{\infty}^{\pm 1}\sum_{\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})}X^{\lambda}Y^{\lambda}

for any
\mu\in P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
.

Finally, since the right-hand side of (
3.6
) is a sum of nonnegative terms its projection must also be well-defined in the sense above, and we can project each summand individually to arrive at (
3.5
). We use (
3.1
) to go from
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
to
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
Macdonald polynomials and then (
3.4
) to identify
a_{\lambda}(q)
for
\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
with the graded character of
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\overline{\lambda}}
given by (
4.10
).
∎

Lemma 4.4

are isomorphic to the level one Demazure modules. In particular,

(4.1)

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(D_{\lambda})=E_{\lambda}(X;q,0)

where
E_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
are the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials corresponding to the (untwisted) affinization of
{\mathfrak{g}}

[
Ch1
,
Ch2
]
.

Proof.

The defining relations for Demazure modules are found in
[
J
,
Ma
]
. For the Demazure modules for the affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras the relations were rewritten in
[
FL
]
. In the case of the level one case the relations from
[
FL
]
read as

\displaystyle(e_{\sigma(\alpha)}\otimes z^{s})^{k+1}d_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}=0,\ \sigma(\alpha)\in\Delta_{+},s\geq 0,k=\max(0,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-s),

\displaystyle(f_{\sigma(\alpha)}\otimes z^{s})^{k+1}d_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}=0,\ \sigma(\alpha)\in\Delta_{-},s>0,k=\max(0,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-s),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes z{\Bbbk}[z]d_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}=0,\quad(h\otimes 1)d_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}=\sigma(\lambda_{-})(h)d_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\ \forall h\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

One needs to show that the defining relations of the modules
D_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
are contained in the relations above and that the relations of

generate the ideal of relations of the Demazure module. To prove the first claim it suffices to note that
\max(0,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-s)=0
unless
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta_{+}
. The second claim follows from the standard computation in the

Finally, formula (

) in types

is proven in
[

,
I
]
.
∎

For the

Let

We consider the affine coroots

\mu\in P_{-}
.

For a positive root
\alpha

Definition 4.6

(
[

by the submodule generated by

\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}

) inside
\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
.

We need the following theorem from
[
FKM
]

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\lambda_{-},\mu,\lambda_{-}-\mu\in P_{-}
and let
\beta_{j}
be defined by (

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m)=\frac{{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,W_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m)}{(q)_{\lambda_{-}+\omega(m)}},

-\bar{\beta}_{i}=\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\text{ is simple}\end{subarray}}\omega_{j}.

Let us define the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m}

.

Let
\lambda_{-}\in P_{-}
,

and

is

Remark 4.9
.

Definition

\sigma(\lambda_{-})

\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m}

Lemma 4.10
.

We have an isomorphism of
z
-graded algebras

(4.6)

\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m}:=\Bbbk[h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\otimes z^{k}]|_{j\in I,~k=1,\dots,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle-|\{i=1,\dots,m:\ -\bar{\beta}_{i}=\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\}|}.

Proof.

Clearly
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a spanned by the vectors of the form
h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}
for

.
Therefore

\mathrm{U}(z\mathfrak{h}[z])=\Bbbk[h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\otimes z^{k}],j\in I,k=1,\dots.

By standard arguments based on the PBW-theorem the
\sigma(\lambda_{-})
-weight subspace of

is equal to

\mathrm{U}(z\mathfrak{h}[z])w_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}

By
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
computations for any positive

\Bbbk[h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\otimes z^{k},\ k=1,\dots,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle-|\{i=1,\dots,m:\ -\bar{\beta}_{i}=\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\}|].w_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})},

).
∎

For any

\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})w_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})fw_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}

\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m}
acts by endomorphisms on the module
\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m)
and we can consider
\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m)
as
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m})

Theorem 4.11
.

The right action of
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m}
is free on the
(\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I}),\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m})
-bimodule
\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m)

Proof.

By PBW theorem and Lemma

\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m)=\mathrm{U}(\widehat{\sigma}(z\mathfrak{n}_{-}[z]))w_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m}.

By definition we have the following isomorphism of the left

\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-}),m}}\Bbbk\simeq W_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(m).

Therefore the right

∎

4.4.
Identification with generalized Weyl modules with characteristics

We fix
\lambda_{-}\in P_{-}
, and we assume that
\sigma\in W
is the maximal length element in the class
\sigma\cdot{\rm stab}_{W}(\lambda_{-})
. We set
\lambda^{\prime}=w_{0}\sigma(\lambda_{-})
. Then,
v(\lambda^{\prime})=\sigma^{-1}w_{0}
is the shortest element such that

v(\lambda^{\prime})\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda_{-}
. In addition,
the factorizations
t_{\lambda_{-}}=v(\lambda^{\prime})u(\lambda^{\prime})

and
t_{\lambda_{-}}=\sigma u^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime})
refine to reduced decompositions (see e.g.
[
FKM
]
).

If we fix reduced expressions

v(\lambda^{\prime})=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{r}},\ u(\lambda^{\prime})=\pi s_{i_{r+1}}\dots s_{i_{M}},

then we obtain a reduced expression

(4.7)

t_{\lambda_{-}}=\pi s_{\pi^{-1}i_{1}}\dots s_{\pi^{-1}i_{r}}s_{i_{r+1}}\dots s_{i_{M}}.

In the similar way we fix reduced expressions

\sigma=s_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\dots s_{i_{r^{\prime}}^{\prime}},\ u^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime})=\pi^{\prime}s_{i^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}+1}}\dots s_{i^{\prime}_{M}},

and we obtain a reduced expression

(4.8)

t_{\lambda_{-}}=\pi^{\prime}s_{\pi^{\prime-1}i^{\prime}_{1}}\dots s_{\pi^{\prime-1}i^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}}s_{i^{\prime}_{r+1}}\dots s_{i^{\prime}_{M}}.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let the coroots
\beta_{j}
be defined by the reduced decomposition (
4.7
). Then

U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\simeq W_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(\ell(w_{0})-\ell(\sigma)),

\mathbb{U}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\simeq\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(\ell(w_{0})-\ell(\sigma)).

Let the coroots
\beta_{j}
be defined by the reduced decomposition (
4.8
). Then

D_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\simeq W_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(\ell(\sigma)),

\mathbb{D}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\simeq\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(\ell(\sigma)).

Remark 4.13
.

The modules
W_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
depend only on
\sigma(\lambda_{-})
, but not on
\sigma
and
\lambda_{-}

separately. We note that the choice of
\sigma
being maximal length element in the class
\sigma\cdot{\rm stab}_{W}(\lambda_{-})
gives the parametrization of the Weyl modules by such pairs
(\sigma,\lambda_{-})
.

Proof of Proposition
4.12
.

We have to prove that the defining relations (
4.5
) of
W_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(\ell(w_{0})-\ell(\sigma))
coincide
with the defining relations of
U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
. We set
r:=\ell(v(\lambda^{\prime}))=\ell(\sigma^{-1}w_{0})=\ell(w_{0})-\ell(\sigma)
,
which is the cardinality of the set
\Delta_{+}\cap\sigma^{-1}\Delta_{+}
.

It suffices to show that
\{-\bar{\beta}_{1}^{\vee},\dots,-\bar{\beta}_{r}^{\vee}\}=\Delta_{+}\cap\sigma^{-1}\Delta_{+}
. By definition, for
k=1,\dots,r
we
have

\displaystyle\beta_{k}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{i_{M}}\dots s_{i_{r+1}}s_{\pi^{-1}i_{r}}\dots s_{\pi^{-1}i_{k+1}}\alpha_{\pi^{-1}i_{k}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{-\lambda_{-}}\pi s_{\pi^{-1}i_{1}}\dots s_{\pi^{-1}i_{k-1}}s_{\pi^{-1}i_{k}}\alpha_{\pi^{-1}i_{k}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{-\lambda_{-}}s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{k-1}}(-\alpha_{i_{k}}^{\vee}).

The action of
t_{-\lambda_{-}}
on
\Delta^{a}
preserve the finite (bar) part of an affine coroot. Therefore, the negated finite parts of
\beta_{1}^{\vee},\dots,\beta_{r}^{\vee}
are exactly the positive roots which are mapped to negative roots by
w_{0}\sigma
. Therefore, the comparison of the defining equations yield

U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\simeq W_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(r),

\mathbb{U}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\simeq\mathbb{W}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}(r),

that is the first part of the assertion.

The proof of the second assertion is completely similar.
∎

We define
\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma({\lambda_{-}})}:=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma({\lambda_{-}}),\ell(w_{0})-\ell(\sigma)}
with respect to the reduced decomposition (
4.7
),
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\sigma({\lambda_{-}})}:=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma({\lambda_{-}}),\ell(\sigma)}

with respect to the reduced decomposition (
4.8
). Note that we have:

(4.9)

\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}=\Bbbk[h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\otimes z^{k}]_{j=1,\dots,n,~k=1,\dots,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle-\delta_{j,\sigma}},

where
\delta_{j,\sigma}=1
, if
\sigma(\alpha_{j})\in\Delta_{+}
and
0
otherwise.
Also we have:

(4.10)

\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}=\Bbbk[h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\otimes z^{k}]_{j=1,\dots,n,~k=1,\dots,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle-1+\delta_{j,\sigma}}.

Remark 4.14
.

One easily sees that the algebras
\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
and
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
depend on the weight
\lambda=\sigma(\lambda_{-})\in P
, but not on
\sigma
and
\lambda_{-}
separately.

Corollary 4.15
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma({\lambda_{-}})}
acts freely on
{\mathbb{U}}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
and

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\,{\mathbb{U}}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}={\mathop{\rm ch}}\,U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}{\mathop{\rm ch}}\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma({\lambda_{-}})}.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\sigma({\lambda_{-}})}

4.5.
Right modules

In this subsection we define right modules
U_{\lambda}^{o}
and
{\mathbb{U}}_{\lambda}^{o}
.

For an anti-dominant weight
\lambda_{-}
and an element
\sigma\in W
the right modules

U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}
and
{\mathbb{U}}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}
are defined as follows:

U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}
is the cyclic right
\mathcal{I}
-module with cyclic vector
u_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
-weight

\sigma(\lambda_{-})

subject to the relations:

\displaystyle u_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes z\Bbbk[z]=0,

\displaystyle u_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}(e_{\widehat{\sigma}(\alpha)+r\delta})=0,\ \alpha\in\Delta_{+},r\geq 0

\displaystyle u_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}(f_{\sigma(\alpha)}\otimes z)^{\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1}=0,\ \alpha\in\Delta_{-},\sigma\alpha\in\Delta_{-},

\displaystyle u_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}(e_{\sigma(\alpha)}\otimes 1)^{\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}=0,\ \alpha\in\Delta_{-},\sigma\alpha\in\Delta_{+}.

As before the definition of the
\mathcal{I}
-module
{\mathbb{U}}^{o}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
differs from the definition of the

U^{o}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
by removing the first line relation.

Proposition 4.16
.

We have an isomorphism of
z
-graded vector spaces
U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}\simeq U_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
. The right
\mathcal{I}
-module structure on the space
U_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
is induced by the minus identity map on the Iwahori algebra; this right module is isomorphic to
U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}
.

Proof.

The minus identity map induces a structure of the right
\mathcal{I}
-module on the space
U_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
. The
{\mathfrak{h}}
weight of the cyclic vector of this right module is equal to
-\sigma(\lambda_{-})=(\sigma w_{0})\lambda_{-,*}
, where

\lambda_{-,*}=-w_{0}\lambda_{-}\in P_{-}
. Now the defining relations of the right module
U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}
are obtained from the defining relations of the left module

U_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
taking into account the change of variables
\sigma\to\sigma w_{0}
,
\lambda_{-}\to\lambda_{-,*}
.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

The module
{\mathbb{U}}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o}
has a structure of
(\mathcal{A}^{U}_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})},\mathcal{I})
-bimodule which is free as the left
\mathcal{A}^{U}_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}
-module.

Corollary 4.18
.

For any
\lambda\in P
one has

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(U_{\lambda}^{o})=E_{\lambda}(Y;q^{-1},\infty).

Proof.

Proposition
4.16
implies

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o})={\mathop{\rm ch}}(U_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}|_{X_{i}\mapsto Y_{i}^{-1}}).

Hence we have

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(U_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}^{o})=w_{0}E_{-w_{0}\sigma(\lambda)}(X;q^{-1},\infty)_{X_{i}\mapsto Y_{i}^{-1}}.

Now it suffices to note that the right hand side is equal to
E_{\lambda}(Y;q^{-1};\infty)
(since the transformation
-w_{0}
is induced by an automorphism of the Dynkin diagram).
∎

4.6.
The
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
version

We recall the definition of
the global Weyl module over
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Let
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}
be a dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
We define the global Weyl module as follows. Let
\bar{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})\omega_{i}
be the corresponding

\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
weight and consider the global Weyl module
\mathbb{W}_{\bar{\lambda}}
for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.

Recall the notation
h_{\ell}=E_{\ell,\ell}
,
\ell=1,\dots,n
; in particular,

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[z]=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}[z]\oplus\Bbbk h_{\ell}[z]
.
In order to give the definition of the global Weyl module for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
we need one more piece of notation. We define

(4.11)

\varphi_{k}^{\ell}:\Bbbk[h_{\ell}z^{i}]_{i\geq 0}\to\Bbbk[s_{1},\dots,s_{k}],~h_{\ell}z^{i}\mapsto s_{1}^{i}+\dots+s_{k}^{i}.

Then the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[z]

\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
is defined as

(4.12)

\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{W}_{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\Bbbk[h_{n}z^{i}]_{i\geq 0}/\ker\varphi_{\lambda_{n}}^{n},

where
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}[z]
acts on the first tensor factor and
\Bbbk h_{n}[z]
acts on the second.

Now let us define the modules
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
. Let
\ell^{\prime}=1,\dots,n
be a number such that:

\lambda_{\ell^{\prime}}=\lambda_{\min}:=\min\{\lambda_{\ell}\}.

Then
we define

(4.13)

\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{U}_{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\Bbbk[h_{\ell^{\prime}}z^{i}]_{i\geq 0}/\ker\varphi_{\lambda_{\min}}^{\ell^{\prime}},

(4.14)

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{D}_{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\Bbbk[h_{\ell^{\prime}}z^{i}]_{i\geq 0}/\ker\varphi_{\lambda_{\min}}^{\ell^{\prime}}.

In a similar fashion one defines the right modules
\mathbb{U}^{o}_{\lambda}
.

The definitions of the local modules for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
do not differ much from their
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
analogues. More precisely, the defining relations for the local modules in type
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

are obtained from that in type
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
by adding the conditions that

kills the cyclic vector and the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

\lambda
. Clearly in type
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
the modules
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
,
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}
are free over its highest weight algebras. Moreover we have

(4.15)

{\mathop{\rm ch}}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{D}={\mathop{\rm ch}}\mathcal{A}_{\bar{\lambda}}^{D}(q;q)_{\lambda_{\min}}^{-1},~{\mathop{\rm ch}}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{U}={\mathop{\rm ch}}\mathcal{A}_{\bar{\lambda}}^{U}(q;q)_{\lambda_{\min}}^{-1}.

Recall that the characters of left weight modules in type
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
belong to
\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},q]
, the characters of the right ones belong to
\Bbbk[y_{1},\dots,y_{n},q]
.

Proposition 4.19
.

For a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight
\lambda
we have

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(D_{\lambda})=E_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q;0),

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(U_{\lambda}^{o})=E_{\lambda}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n};q^{-1};\infty).

As graded vector spaces the module
D_{\lambda}
(resp.,
U_{\lambda}^{o}
) is isomorphic to
D_{\bar{\lambda}}
(resp.,
U_{\bar{\lambda}}^{o}
). Now the desired character formulas are implied by Corollary
4.18
, Lemma
4.4
and the following equalities:

\displaystyle{\mathop{\rm ch}}(D_{\lambda+(1,\dots,1)})=x_{1}\dots x_{n}{\mathop{\rm ch}}(D_{\lambda}),\ {\mathop{\rm ch}}(U^{o}_{\lambda+(1,\dots,1)})=y_{1}\dots y_{n}{\mathop{\rm ch}}(D_{\lambda}),

\displaystyle E_{\lambda+(1,\dots,1)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q;t)=x_{1}\dots x_{n}E_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q;t).

∎

4.7.
Tensor bimodules

Proposition 4.20
.

There is a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\simeq\mathcal{A}^{D}_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}

Proof.

Recall (
4.9
), (
4.10
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}:=\Bbbk[h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\otimes z^{k}]_{j\in I,~k=1,\dots,\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle-\delta_{j,\sigma}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}=\Bbbk[h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\otimes z^{k}]_{j\in I,~k=1,\dots,-\langle\lambda_{-},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\rangle-1+\delta_{j,\sigma}}

and the notation
\lambda_{-}^{*}=-w_{0}(\lambda_{-})
,
\alpha_{j^{*}}=-w_{0}(\alpha_{j})
.
Note that
h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}=-h_{-\sigma(\alpha_{j})}
. Clearly
-\sigma(\lambda_{-})=\sigma w_{0}(-w_{0}(\lambda_{-}))=\sigma w_{0}(\lambda_{-}^{*})
and
\delta_{j,\sigma}=1-\delta_{j^{*},\sigma w_{0}}
. Thus the map
h_{\sigma(\alpha_{j})}\mapsto-h_{\sigma w_{0}(\alpha_{j^{*}})}
gives the isomorphism

\mathcal{A}^{U}_{\sigma(\lambda_{-})}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{D}_{-\sigma(\lambda_{-})}.

∎

The following
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodule plays a key role in this paper. We use the isomorphism from the previous proposition.

Definition 4.21
.

For a weight
\lambda\in P
we define the bimodule
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
in the following way:

\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{D}_{\lambda}}\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}.

Recall that for graded weight
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}})
-bimodules the characters belong to
\mathbb{Z}[P\times P][[q]]
.

Lemma 4.22
.

The character of
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
is equal to

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(D_{\lambda}){\mathop{\rm ch}}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{D}){\mathop{\rm ch}}(U_{\lambda}^{o})={\mathop{\rm ch}}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{D})E_{\lambda}(X;q,0)E_{\lambda}(Y;q^{-1},\infty).

Proof.

Let
d_{1},\dots,d_{l}
be a weight basis of
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
,
u_{1},\dots,u_{l^{\prime}}
be a weight basis of
\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}
as a free modules over
{\mathop{\rm ch}}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{D})
. We have the following isomorphisms of graded vector spaces:

\mathrm{span}\{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}\}\simeq D_{\lambda},\ \mathrm{span}\{u_{1},\dots,u_{l^{\prime}}\}\simeq U_{\lambda}^{o}.

Then

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}d_{i}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{D},\ \mathbb{U}_{\lambda}^{o}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l^{\prime}}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{D}u_{j}

This completes the proof.

Lemma 4.23
.

The bimodule
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
is the cyclic bimodule with the generator

and the following defining relations:

In the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
case one more adds the relation

\ker\varphi^{\ell}_{\lambda_{\min}}\bar{w}_{\lambda}=\{0\},\ \lambda_{\min}=\lambda_{\ell}.

Proof.

To prove the cyclicity we note that the
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})
span of the vector
d_{\lambda}\otimes u_{\lambda}
with respect to the right action coincides with

d_{\lambda}\otimes{\mathbb{U}}_{\lambda}^{o}
. Now applying
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})
on the left one gets the whole bimodule
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
.

We denote the module defined by relations (
4.16
)-(
4.20
) by
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}
.
Clearly the relations
(
4.16
)-(
4.20
) are satisfied on the bimodule
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}

\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\lambda}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}

By definition we have the morphism of the right modules:

Then the elements

Lemma 5.2

The
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodule

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1.

The following equality holds true:

\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{h}}[z])S=\mathrm{span}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ii}}v_{ii}^{(s_{ii}^{k})},\ s_{ii}^{k}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i}r_{ii}=N\right\}.

The proof of this fact is by induction on the number of nonzero exponents
s_{ii}^{k}
. Assume by induction that
\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{h}}[z])S

contains all elements of the form
x=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ii}}(v_{ii}^{(s_{ii}^{k})})

equal to
b<r_{jj}

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ii}}v_{ii}^{(s_{ii}^{k})}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{r_{jj}-1}v_{jj}^{(s_{jj}^{k})}\cdot v_{jj}^{{(a)}}.

This completes the induction step.

Step 2.
Next we prove the following claim:

(5.1)

\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{n}}_{+}[z])\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{h}}[z])S=\mathrm{span}\left\{\prod_{i\leq j}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ij}}v_{ij}^{(s_{ij}^{k})},s_{ij}^{k}\geq 0,\sum r_{ij}=N\right\}.

For a pair
i_{0}\leq j_{0}
we denote by
S_{i_{0},j_{0}}
be the linear span of the set of monomials
\prod_{i\leq j}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ij}}v_{ij}^{(s_{ij}^{k})}

subject to the condition

\text{ if }j>j_{0}\text{ or }j=j_{0},i<i_{0},\text{ then }r_{ij}=0\text{ provided }i\neq j.

We prove (
5.1

i_{0},j_{0}
((
5.1

(3,3)

(2,3)

Consider the increasing filtration on the space

G_{p}=\mathrm{span}\left\{\prod_{i\leq j}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ij}}v_{ij}^{(s_{ij}^{k})},r_{ij}=0~\text{for}~j>j_{0}~\text{or}~j=j_{0},i<i_{0},r_{i_{0}j_{0}}\leq p\right\}.

(5.2)

\mathrm{U}(\Bbbk E_{i_{0},i_{0}+1}[z]).\mathrm{span}\left\{\prod_{k=1}^{r_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}}v_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}^{(s_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}^{k})},\ s_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}^{k}\geq 0\right\}\\
=\mathrm{span}\left\{\prod_{k=1}^{r_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}}v_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}^{(s_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}^{k})}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{i_{0},j_{0}}}v_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(s_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{k})},\ s_{i_{0}+1,j_{0}}^{k},s_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{k}\geq 0\right\}

(see e.g.
[
FKhM
]

for all

In a similar way one proves

\prod_{i,j}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ij}}v_{ij}^{(s_{ij}^{k})}

\text{ if }j<j_{0}\text{ or }j=j_{0},i>i_{0},\text{ then }r_{ij}=0\text{ provided }i\leq j.

We prove (
5.3

Lemma 5.3

For

However:

\lambda_{j}(v_{jj}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{j}-1}v_{ij}^{(l)}\prod_{k\neq j}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}}=\\
\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+1}\left((v_{jj}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{j}-1}(v_{ii}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{i}+1}\prod_{k\neq i,j}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}}\right)E_{ij}z^{l}

and the similar equality holds for the second case. By definition of the order

\lambda+\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}\succ\lambda
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For
\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{j}
,

(5.5)

(5.6)

\prod_{k=1}^{n}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}}(E_{ji})^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}\in\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}.

For
\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{j}
,
i<j
:

(5.7)

(E_{ij})^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}+1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}}\in\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}.

\prod_{k=1}^{n}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}}(E_{ji}z)^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}+1}\in\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}.

Proof.

We have:

(E_{ij})^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}+1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}}\\

\displaystyle h_{i}z^{l}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}}=\lambda_{k}(v_{ii}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{i}-1}v_{ii}^{(l)}\prod_{k\neq i}(v_{kk}^{(0)})^{\lambda_{k}},

u_{ij}(s):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{ij}^{(k)}s^{k}
and the expansions

f_{l}(u_{ij}\mapsto u_{ij}(s))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{l}^{(k)}s^{k}.

Then

\Bbbk[G[z]]=\Bbbk[u_{ij}^{(k)}]/\langle f_{l}^{(k)}\rangle_{l=1,\dots,m,~k=0,1,\dots}.

Note that the space
\Bbbk[G[z]]
has a natural structure of
({\mathfrak{g}}[z],{\mathfrak{g}}[z])
bimodule, where

{\mathfrak{g}}[z]
is the Lie algebra of
G[z]
. Therefore the space
\Bbbk[G[z]]^{*}
also has the natural structure of
({\mathfrak{g}}[z],{\mathfrak{g}}[z])
-bimodule.

Now we consider the Iwahori subgroup
{\bf I}\subset G[z]
. The space

\Bbbk[{\bf I}]^{*}\subset\Bbbk[G[z]]^{*}
has the natural structure of
({\bf I},{\bf I})
-bimodule.
Given a monomial in variables
u_{ij}^{(k)}
we refer to the sum of upper indices of the variables
in the monomial as the
z
-degree of the monomial. The corresponding
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
grading
on the polynomial ring
\Bbbk[u_{ij}^{(k)}]
is called the
z
-grading. Note that all the relations
f_{l}^{(k)}
are homogeneous with respect to this grading. Therefore the rings
\Bbbk[G[z]]
and
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
inherit the
z
-grading.

6.2.
Character of the ring of functions

Let
u_{ij}^{(k)}\in(M_{n}[z]^{+})^{*}
be the coordinate functions on the space
M_{n}[z]^{+}
, i.e.
u_{ij}^{(k)}(v_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{(k^{\prime})})=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}
.
We denote

u_{ij}(s):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{ij}^{(k)}s^{k},

where
u_{ij}^{(0)}=0
if
i>j
.

Let

{\bf I}\subset SL_{m}[z]\subset M_{n}[z]^{+}

be the Iwahori sungroup.
As a variety
{\bf I}
is an affine subvariety
defined by the relations

\prod_{i=1}^{n}u_{ii}(s)-1=0.

Here the relation means that this variety is defined by the ideal generated by coefficients of the left hand side. Let
f^{(k)}
be the coefficients in the expansion

(6.1)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}u_{ii}(s)-1=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f^{(k)}s^{k}.

Lemma 6.1
.

The elements
f^{(k)}
,
k=0,1,\dots
form a regular sequence.

Proof.

Clearly
f^{(0)}
is not a zero divisor. Assume that
f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(k)}
is a regular sequence. Let
R
be the polynomial ring in all the variables
u_{ij}^{(l)}
and let
R_{k}\subset R
be the polynomial subring generated by
u_{ij}^{(l)}
with
l\leq k
.
Then
R=R_{k}[u_{ij}^{(l)},l>k]
.
The elements
f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(k)}
belong to
R_{k}
. Therefore

R\left/\langle f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(k)}\rangle\right.=(R_{k}\left/\langle f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(k)}\rangle\right.)[u_{ij}^{(l)},l>k].

Note that

f^{(k+1)}=u_{11}^{(k+1)}u_{22}^{(0)}\dots u_{nn}^{(0)}+u_{11}^{(0)}u_{22}^{(k+1)}\dots u_{nn}^{(0)}+\dots+u_{11}^{(0)}u_{22}^{(0)}\dots u_{nn}^{(k+1)}+a,

where
a\in R_{k}
. Assume that
f^{(k+1)}
is a zero divisor in the ring
R/\langle f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(k)}\rangle
, i.e.
f^{(k+1)}b=0
for some
b
. In particular, the highest degree term of
f^{(k+1)}b=0
with respect to the variable
u_{11}^{(k+1)}
vanishes; thus
u_{22}^{(0)}\dots u_{nn}^{(0)}
is a zero divisor in
R_{k}/\langle f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(k)}\rangle
. However

u_{22}^{(0)}\dots u_{nn}^{(0)}=\left(u_{11}^{(0)}\right)^{-1}\mod f^{(0)},

i.e. the momomial
u_{22}^{(0)}\dots u_{nn}^{(0)}
is invertible.
Therefore
f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(k)},f^{(k+1)}
is a regular sequence and we complete the proof by induction.
∎

Note that the elements
f^{(k)}
are homogeneous with respect to
z
-degree. Moreover they are left and right homogeneous with respect to the lattice
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}(\varepsilon_{1}+\dots+\varepsilon_{n})
.
The left and right weights of
f^{(k)}
are equal to
\varepsilon_{1}+\dots+\varepsilon_{n}
and its
z
-degree is equal to
k
. Recall the
q
-Pochhammer symbol

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}).

Proposition 6.2
.

The character of the space
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
is equal to the image of

\frac{(x_{1}\cdots x_{n}y_{1}\cdots y_{n};q)_{\infty}}{\prod_{i\leq j}(x_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}\prod_{i>j}(qx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}

with respect to the
\overline{\phantom{a}}
map, i.e., the left-hand side of (
3.5
).

Proof.

We have

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}])=\frac{1}{\prod_{i\leq j}(x_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}\prod_{i>j}(qx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

We consider the standard Koszul resolution (see e.g.
[
Eis
]
)

\dots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{0\leq k_{1}<\dots<k_{l}}f^{(k_{1})}\dots f^{(k_{l})}\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\rightarrow\\
\dots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{0\leq k_{1}}f^{(k_{1})}\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\rightarrow\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\rightarrow\Bbbk[{\bf I}],

because
f^{(0)},f^{(1)},\dots
is a regular sequence. Now the alternating sum of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
characters of components of this resolution produces the desired expression for the character of
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

{\mathop{\rm ch}}(\Bbbk[{\bf I}])=\sum_{\lambda\in P}{\mathop{\rm ch}}(\mathcal{A}^{D}_{{\lambda}})E_{\lambda}(X;q,0)E_{\lambda}(Y;q^{-1},\infty).

Proof.

It follows from equation (
3.6
).
∎

6.3.
Filtration on
\Bbbk{\bf[I]}

In this subsection we construct an increasing filtration
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}\subset\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
,
\lambda\in P
of the space of functions on the Iwahori group and describe the associated graded space.

Recall that the weight lattice
P=P(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
can be identified with the set
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
of nonnegative integer tuples with the smallest entry being zero (since the restriction of the natural surjection
\mathbb{Z}^{n}=P(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\to P
to
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
is one-to-one).
For a weight
\lambda\in P
we denote by
u_{\lambda}\in\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
the product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u_{ii}^{(0)})^{\mu_{i}}
, where
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}_{0}
corresponds to
\lambda
.

Lemma 6.4
.

The space
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
is cogenerated as a
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodule by vectors
u_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in P
.

Proof.

We first show that
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
is cogenerated by the subspace
\Bbbk[B]\subset\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
(
\Bbbk[B]
is contained in
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
as a subalgebra generated by
u_{ij}^{(0)}
).

Assume that there exists a function
\Psi\in\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
such that

(6.2)

\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})\Psi\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})\cap\Bbbk[B]=0.

We can (and will) assume that
\Psi
is homogeneous of strictly positive
z
-degree. We will also
assume that this degree is the smallest possible (i.e. there is no function with the property (
6.2
)
of
z
-degree smaller than that of
\Psi
).
Then equation (
6.2
) implies that

\Psi
is invariant with respect to the left-right action of the product of groups

\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])\times\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])
. In other words, for any
g_{z}\in{\bf I}
and
(a,b)\in\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])\times\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])

one has
\Psi(g_{z})=\Psi(ag_{z}b)
.
In fact, since
\Psi
is of positive
z
-degree and of the smallest
z
-degree with the property (
6.2
), we conclude

\mathrm{U}(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])\Psi\mathrm{U}(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])=0
, which is equivalent to the claim that
\Psi
is invariant with respect to the product
of groups
\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])\times\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])
.

We note that the set

\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])B\exp(z\mathfrak{g}[z])\subset{\bf I}

is open dense.
For an element
g_{z}\in\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])B\exp(z\mathfrak{g}[z])
we denote
g_{z}=ag_{0}b
,
a,b\in\exp(z\mathfrak{g}[z])
,
g_{0}\in B
.
Since the function
\Psi
is invariant with respect to
\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])\times\exp(z{\mathfrak{g}}[z])
, we obtain:

\Psi(g_{z})=\Psi(ag_{0}b)=\left((a^{-1}\times b^{-1})\Psi\right)(g_{0})=\Psi(g_{0})=0

because of positivity of
z
-degree of
\Psi
. Therefore
\Psi=0
.

We are left to show that
\Bbbk[B]
is cogenerated as a
(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b})
-bimodule by vectors
u_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in P
. This is easily seen since
\Bbbk[B]
is the span of products of the variables
u_{ij}^{(0)}
.
∎

We now define the filtration
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}\subset\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
as follows:

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}=\left\{\Psi\in\Bbbk[{\bf I}]:\ \mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})\Psi\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})\cap\mathrm{span}\{u_{\mu},\mu\in P\}\subset\mathrm{span}\{u_{\mu},\mu\preceq\lambda\}\right\}.

Thanks to Lemma
6.4
,
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
is an increasing filtration on
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
. Observe that, since the
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-action on
\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]
preserves the degree in variables
u_{ij}^{(k)}
, the intersection in the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
is finite-dimensional.

The following lemma is easy to show and we omit the proof.

Lemma 6.5
.

The quotient
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
has finite-dimensional homogeneous components with respect to the
z
-grading. The space
(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu})^{\vee}
has a natural structure of cyclic
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodule with cyclic vector
u^{\vee}_{\lambda}
.

Our goal is to show that
(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu})^{\vee}\simeq\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
(see Lemma
4.23
). We know that both
(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu})^{\vee}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
are cyclic
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodules and that

\sum_{\lambda\in P}{\mathop{\rm ch}}(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu})^{\vee}=\sum_{\lambda\in P}{\mathop{\rm ch}}\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}

(since both left and right hand sides are equal to the character of
\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
). Hence it suffices to show the existence of the surjection

(6.3)

\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\to(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu})^{\vee}.

6.4.
Dual space of functions

In order to construct the surjection (
6.3
) we define subspaces
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\subset\Bbbk[{\bf I}]^{*}
with the following properties:

•

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
if
\lambda\succeq\mu
,

•

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is a cyclic
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodule,

•

there exists a surjection of
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodules
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
,

•

there exists a surjection of
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodules
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\to(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu})^{\vee}
.

To define
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
and to prove the statements above we construct certain vectors
v_{\mu}\in\Bbbk[B]^{*}\subset\Bbbk[{\bf I}]^{*}
coming from the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
side of the story.

We have the surjective map

\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\twoheadrightarrow\Bbbk[{\bf I}]
and the
dual embedding

\Bbbk[{\bf I}]^{*}\hookrightarrow\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]^{*}
.
Recall the explicit realization

\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]=\Bbbk[u_{ij}^{(k)}:\ k\geq 0\text{ if }i\leq j,\ k\geq 1\text{ if }i>j].

One has the map

\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\twoheadrightarrow\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\left/\left\langle f^{(0)},f^{(1)},\dots\right\rangle\right.

and its dualization

(6.4)

\phi:\left(\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\left/\left\langle f^{(0)},f^{(1)},\dots\right\rangle\right.\right)^{*}\hookrightarrow\left(\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\right)^{*},

where
\sum_{k\geq 0}f^{(k)}s^{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}u_{ii}(s)-1
(see (
6.1
)).
We denote by
v_{ij}^{(k)}\in(M_{n}[z]^{+})^{\vee\vee}
the dual linear
forms to
u_{ij}^{(k)}\in(M_{n}[z]^{+})^{\vee}
(we note that
(M_{n}[z]^{+})^{\vee\vee}
is isomorphic to
M_{n}[z]^{+}
). Then we have the pairings between modules

S^{N}((M_{n}[z]^{+})^{\vee})
and
S^{N}(M_{n}[z]^{+})
defined on the bases by

\left(\prod_{i,j,k}(u_{ij}^{(k)})^{t_{ij}^{k}},\prod_{i,j,k}(v_{ij}^{(k)})^{s_{ij}^{k}}\right)=\prod_{i,j,k}\delta_{t_{ij}^{k},s_{ij}^{k}}\binom{N}{\{t_{ij}^{k}\}}^{-1}.

Hence we have the same pairing between
\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]
and
S(M_{n}[z]^{+})
.

For a composition
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
recall the notation
\overline{\nu}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\nu_{i}-\nu_{i+1})\omega_{i}
for the corresponding
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
weight. For an integral
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
weight
\mu\in P
we define

v_{\mu}:=\sum_{\nu:\ \overline{\nu}=\mu}\binom{\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n}}{\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(v_{ii}^{(0)})^{\nu_{i}}.

Lemma 6.6
.

For any
\mu\in P

v_{\mu}\in\phi\left(\Bbbk[M_{n}[z]^{+}]\left/\left\langle f^{(0)},f^{(1)},\dots\right\rangle\right.\right)^{*}.

One has
(v_{\mu},u_{\lambda})=\delta_{\mu,\lambda}
.

Proof.

Observe that
v_{\mu}
is automatically orthogonal to
\langle f^{(1)},f^{(2)},\dots\rangle
for degree reasons. Thus we only need to prove that, for any monomial
u=\prod_{i,j}\prod_{k=1}^{r_{ij}}u_{ij}^{(t_{ij}^{k})}
, the element
u(1-\prod_{i=0}^{n}u_{ii}^{(0)})
is orthogonal to
v_{\mu}
. Clearly the claim holds true if
r_{ij}\neq 0
for some
i\neq j
or
t_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
for some
k
. Hence it suffices to consider

u=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u_{ii}^{(0)})^{\eta_{i}}

for some composition
\eta
. One easily sees that for such monomials the scalar product of
u(1-\prod_{i=0}^{n}u_{ii}^{(0)})
with
v_{\mu}
vanishes unless
\bar{\eta}=\mu
.
However

u\left(1-\prod_{i=0}^{n}u_{ii}^{(0)}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u_{ii}^{(0)})^{\eta_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u_{ii}^{(0)})^{\eta_{i}+1}

and for
\eta
such that
\bar{\eta}=\mu
one has

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u_{ii}^{(0)})^{\eta_{i}},v_{\mu}\right)=1.

Thus
v_{\mu}\in\phi\left(\Bbbk[u_{ij}^{(k)}]_{i\leq j}\left/\left\langle f^{(0)},f^{(1)},\dots\right\rangle\right.\right)^{*}
.
Finally, the equality
(v_{\mu},u_{\lambda})=\delta_{\mu,\lambda}
follows from the explicit formulas.
∎

We define the following subspaces
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\subset\Bbbk[{\bf I}]^{*}
:

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu\succeq\lambda}\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I})v_{\nu}\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{I}).

We have two obvious properties:

•

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
whenever
\lambda\succ\mu
,

•

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is a cyclic
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodule with cyclic vector
v_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

There is a natural pairing between
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-bimodules:

\left(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu}\right)\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\right)\to\Bbbk,

which produces the surjective homomorphism

(6.5)

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\to(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu})^{\vee}.

Proof.

From the definitions of
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
one has

(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda},\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu})=0,\ (\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\mu},F_{\lambda})=0.

This produces the desired pairing and the homomorphism (
6.5
) sending the cyclic vector
v_{\lambda}
to the cyclic vector
u_{\lambda}^{\vee}
(where
u_{\lambda}^{\vee}
is dual to
u_{\lambda}
in the obvious sense). ∎

We will now show that there exists a surjective homomorphism

\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}/\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
.
Recall
\lambda=\sigma(\lambda_{-})
,
\lambda\in P
,
\lambda_{-}\in P_{-}
.

.

For all
\lambda\in P
the following relations are satisfied:

for
\alpha\in\Delta_{-}
,
l\geq 0

\displaystyle e_{\widehat{\sigma}(\alpha+\delta)}z^{l}v_{\lambda}\in\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu},

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}e_{\widehat{\sigma}(-\alpha)}z^{l}\in\sum_{\mu\succ\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu};

,

.

One has an isomorphism of of

-bimodules

FEICHTINGER CONJECTURES,
R

Abstract




Abstract
: Motivated from two decades old famous Feichtinger conjectures for frames,
R_{\varepsilon}
-conjecture and Weaver’s conjecture for Hilbert spaces (and their solution by Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava), we formulate Feichtinger conjectures for p-approximate Schauder frames,
R_{\varepsilon}
-conjecture, Weaver’s conjectures and Akemann-Weaver conjectures for Banach spaces. We also formulate conjectures on p-approximate Schauder frames based on the results of Casazza for frames for Hilbert spaces. We state conjectures and problems for p-approximate Schauder frames based on fundamental inequality for frames for Hilbert spaces and scaling problem for Hilbert space frames. Based on Kothe-Lorch theorem for Riesz bases for Hilbert spaces, we formulate a problem for p-approximate Riesz bases for Banach spaces. We formulate dynamical sampling problem for p-approximate Schauder frames for Banach spaces. We ask phase retrieval problem and norm retrieval problem for p-approximate Schauder frames for Banach spaces. We also formulate discretization problem for continuous p-approximate Schauder frames.

Keywords
: Feichtinger conjectures, p-approximate Schauder Frame, p-approximate Riesz basis, p-approximate Bessel sequence, p-orthonormal basis.

Definition 2.1
.

[
84
,
82
]

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
(dual of
\mathcal{X}
). The pair
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is said to be a
p-approximate Schauder frame (p-ASF)
for
\mathcal{X}
if the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

The map (
frame operator
)

\displaystyle S_{f,\tau}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto S_{f,\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{X}

is a well-defined bounded linear, invertible operator.

(ii)

The map (
analysis operator
)

\displaystyle\theta_{f}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto\theta_{f}x\coloneqq\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})

is a well-defined bounded linear operator.

(iii)

The map (
synthesis operator
)

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}:\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})\ni\{a_{n}\}_{n}\mapsto\theta_{\tau}\{a_{n}\}_{n}\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{X}

is a well-defined bounded linear operator.

Constants
a>0
and
b>0
satisfying

\displaystyle a\|x\|\leq\|S_{f,\tau}x\|\leq b\|x\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

are called as
lower frame bound
and
upper frame bound
, respectively. If
S_{f,\tau}x=x
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
, then we say that
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is a Parseval p-ASF for
\mathcal{X}
. If
S_{f,\tau}x=\lambda x
, for some nonzero scalar
\lambda
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
, then we say that
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is a tight p-ASF for
\mathcal{X}
. If we do not demand condition (i), then we say that
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is a
p-approximate Bessel sequence (p-ABS)
for
\mathcal{X}
. Constant
b
is called as
Bessel bound
.

Now we define the notion of p-orthonormal sequence and basis for Banach spaces. Our basic motivation is the notion of orthonormal basis for Hilbert spaces and the standard canonical Schauder basis for
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\mathbb{M}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{M}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{M}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
. The pair
(\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{M}},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{M}})
is said to be a
p-orthonormal sequence
for
\mathcal{X}
if the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

f_{n}(\tau_{m})=\delta_{n,m}
,
\forall n,m\in\mathbb{M}
.

(ii)

For each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\|x\|^{p}\geq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{M}}|f_{n}(x)|^{p}.

(iii)

For each
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{M}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{M})
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{{n\in\mathbb{M}}}a_{n}\tau_{n}\right\|^{p}=\sum_{{n\in\mathbb{M}}}|a_{n}|^{p}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
. The pair
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is said to be a
p-orthonormal basis
for
\mathcal{X}
if the following conditions hold.

(i)

\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}
is a Schauder basis for
\mathcal{X}
.

(ii)

f_{n}(\tau_{m})=\delta_{n,m}
,
\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
is the coordinate functionals associated with
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}

(iii)

For each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\|x\|^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(x)|^{p}.

(iv)

For each
\{a_{n}\}_{n}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\tau_{n}\right\|^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}.

It is clear that a p-orthonormal basis is a p-orthonormal sequence and every subset of p-orthonormal basis is a p-orthonormal sequence. Also note that condition (iv) in Definition
2.3
says that if
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is a p-orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{X}
, then
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}
is an unconditional Schauder basis for
\mathcal{X}
. Definition
2.3
give the following observations:

\displaystyle\|\tau_{n}\|=1=\|f_{n}\|,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\|\tau_{j_{1}}+\cdots+\tau_{j_{n}}\|=n^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall j_{1},\dots,j_{n}.

Let
\mathcal{H}

Like orthonormal basis for Hilbert spaces, we can characterize all p-orthonormal bases for Banach spaces.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
be a p-orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{X}
. Then all p-orthonormal bases for
\mathcal{X}
are precisely the family
(\{f_{n}V^{-1}\}_{n},\{V\tau_{n}\}_{n})
, where
V:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is an invertible isometry.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Let
(\{g_{n}\}_{n},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n})
be a p-orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{X}
. Define

\displaystyle V:\mathcal{X}\ni\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\omega_{n}\in\mathcal{X}

and

\displaystyle U:\mathcal{X}\ni\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(x)\omega_{n}\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(x)\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{X}.

Then
U
and
V
are well-defined and

\displaystyle UVx=U\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\omega_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)U\omega_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}=x,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle VUx=V\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(x)\tau_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(x)V\tau_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(x)\omega_{n}=x,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence
V
is invertible. Now

\displaystyle\|Vx\|=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\omega_{n}\right\|=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|x\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence
V
is isometry. We now have
V\tau_{n}=\omega_{n}
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\displaystyle f_{n}(V^{-1}x)=f_{n}(Ux)=f_{n}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g_{m}(x)\tau_{m}\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g_{m}(x)f_{n}(\tau_{m})=g_{n}(x),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Define
g_{n}\coloneqq f_{n}V^{-1},\omega_{n}\coloneqq V\tau_{n}
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. We claim that
(\{g_{n}\}_{n},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n})
is a p-orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{X}
. Since
V
is invertible,
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n}
is a Schauder basis for
\mathcal{X}
. Now we verify remaining three conditions:

\displaystyle g_{n}(\omega_{m})=f_{n}(V^{-1}V\tau_{m})=f_{n}(\tau_{m})=\delta_{n,m},\quad\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|g_{n}(x)|^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(V^{-1}x)|^{p}=\|V^{-1}x\|^{p}=\|x\|^{p},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\omega_{n}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}V\tau_{n}\right\|^{p}=\left\|V\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\tau_{n}\right)\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\tau_{n}\right\|^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p},\quad\{a_{n}\}_{n}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}).

∎

If
\mathcal{X}
admits a p-orthonormal basis, then

is isometrically isomorphic to

\displaystyle V:\mathcal{X}\ni\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)e_{n}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})

and

\displaystyle U:\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})\ni\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(x)e_{n}\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(x)\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{X}.

Other parts are similar to the proof of Theorem

∎

\mathcal{X}

Theorem 2.12
.

The following are equivalent.

(i)

The pair
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is a p-approximate Riesz basis for
\mathcal{X}

(ii)

The pair

is a p-ASF for
\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}=I_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}.

Proof.

(i)

\Rightarrow
(ii) Let

be bounded linear invertible operators such that
f_{n}=g_{n}U,~\tau_{n}=V\omega_{n},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

(\{g_{n}\}_{n},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n})

\mathcal{X}
. We then have

\theta_{\tau}=V\theta_{\omega}

which is invertible. Therefore

\mathcal{X}
. We now find

\displaystyle\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}\{a_{n}\}_{n}

\displaystyle=\theta_{g}U(VU)^{-1}V\theta_{\omega}\{a_{n}\}_{n}=\theta_{g}UU^{-1}V^{-1}V\theta_{\omega}\{a_{n}\}_{n}

\displaystyle=\theta_{g}\theta_{\omega}\{a_{n}\}_{n}=\theta_{g}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\omega_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\theta_{g}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g_{m}(\omega_{n})e_{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e_{n}=\{a_{n}\}_{n},\quad\forall\{a_{n}\}_{n}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}).

(ii)

\Rightarrow
(i) Let
(\{g_{n}\}_{n},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n})
be a p-orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{X}
. Define
U\coloneqq\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}
and
V\coloneqq\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g}
. Then

\displaystyle g_{n}(Ux)

\displaystyle=g_{n}(\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}x)=g_{n}\left(\theta_{\omega}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}f_{m}(x)e_{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=g_{n}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}f_{m}(x)\theta_{\omega}e_{m}\right)=g_{n}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}f_{m}(x)\omega_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}f_{m}(x)g_{n}(\omega_{m})=f_{n}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle V\omega_{n}=\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g}\omega_{n}=\theta_{\tau}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g_{m}(\omega_{n})e_{m}\right)=\theta_{\tau}e_{n}=\tau_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

To complete the proof, we have to show that both
U
and
V
are bounded invertible. First note that

\displaystyle\theta_{g}\theta_{\omega}\{a_{n}\}_{n}

\displaystyle=\theta_{g}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\omega_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\theta_{g}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g_{m}(\omega_{n})e_{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e_{n}

\displaystyle=\{a_{n}\}_{n},\quad\forall\{a_{n}\}_{n}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}).

Hence

(3)

\displaystyle\theta_{g}\theta_{\omega}=I_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}.

Now using Equation (
2
) and Equation (
3
) we find

\displaystyle U(S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g})=\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g}=\theta_{\omega}I_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\theta_{g}=\theta_{\omega}\theta_{g}=I_{\mathcal{X}},

\displaystyle(S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g})U=S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g}\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}=S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}I_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\theta_{f}=S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}\theta_{f}=I_{\mathcal{X}}

and

\displaystyle V(\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1})=\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g}\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}=\theta_{\tau}I_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}=\theta_{\tau}\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}=I_{\mathcal{X}},

\displaystyle(\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1})V=\theta_{\omega}\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}\theta_{g}=\theta_{\omega}I_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\theta_{g}=\theta_{\omega}\theta_{g}=I_{\mathcal{X}}.

Hence
U
and

are invertible.

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq n}c_{j}^{r}\leq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}^{r}

for some real
p,q,r>0
.

Theorem 2.47

Let

with bounds

Using the definition of

Definition 2.57
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space and
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
(dual of
\mathcal{X}
). The pair
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is said to be a
continuous p-approximate Schauder frame (continuous p-ASF)
for
\mathcal{X}
if the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

For each
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the map
\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto f_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{C}
is measurable.

(ii)

For each
u\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
, the map
\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto u(\alpha)\tau_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{X}
is measurable.

(iii)

The map (
continuous analysis operator
)

\displaystyle\theta_{f}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto\theta_{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu);\quad\theta_{f}x:\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto(\theta_{f}x)(\alpha)\coloneqq f_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{C}

is a well-defined bounded linear operator.

(iv)

The map (
continuous synthesis operator
)

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}:\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu)\ni u\mapsto\theta_{\tau}u\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}u(\alpha)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\in\mathcal{X}

is a well-defined bounded linear operator.

(v)

The map (
continuous frame operator
)

\displaystyle S_{f,\tau}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto S_{f,\tau}x\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\in\mathcal{X}

is a well-defined bounded linear, invertible operator.

Constants
a>0
and
b>0
satisfying

\displaystyle a\|x\|\leq\|S_{f,\tau}x\|\leq b\|x\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

are called as
lower frame bound
and
upper frame bound
, respectively. If
S_{f,\tau}x=x
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
, then we say that
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is a Parseval continuous p-ASF for
\mathcal{X}
. If
S_{f,\tau}x=\lambda x
, for some nonzero scalar
\lambda
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
, then we say that
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is a tight continuous p-ASF for
\mathcal{X}
. If we do not demand condition (v), then we say that
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is a
continuous p-approximate Bessel sequence (p-ABS)
for
\mathcal{X}
. Constant
b
is called as
Bessel bound
.

\displaystyle=\pi(x,y),\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\theta_{f}(x,y)\|

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(\theta_{f}(x,y))|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\alpha}(x,y))|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|x\cos\alpha+y\sin\alpha|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|x\cos\alpha|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|y\sin\alpha|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=|x|\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\cos\alpha|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+|y|\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\sin\alpha|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{p}}(|x|+|y|)=(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|(x,y)\|_{1},\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\|\theta_{\tau}u\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\alpha)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\alpha\right\|_{1}=\left\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\alpha)(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)\,d\alpha\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\alpha)\cos\alpha\,d\alpha,\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\alpha)\sin\alpha\,d\alpha\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\alpha)\cos\alpha\,d\alpha\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\alpha)\sin\alpha\,d\alpha\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u(\alpha)|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\cos\alpha|^{q}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u(\alpha)|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\sin\alpha|^{q}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u(\alpha)|^{p}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=2(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|u\|_{p},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{L}^{p}[0,2\pi].

Therefore
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is a continuous p-ASF for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Note that
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is also continuous 1-ASF.

Here is discretization problem for continous p-approximate Schauder frames for Banach spaces. For frames for Hilbert spaces this was asked by Ali, Antoine, and Gazeau

and solved by Fornasier, Rauhut, Freeman, and Speegle

Problem 2.59
.

(Discretization problem for continuous p-approximate Schauder frames for Banach spaces)

When a continuous p-approximate Schauder frame for a Banach space can be sampled to obtain a (discrete) p-approximate Schauder frame?

We now introduce the notion of continuous p-approximate Riesz basis for Banach spaces. Motivation comes from Theorem
2.12
.

Definition 2.60
.

A continuous p-ASF
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
for
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
continuous p-approximate Riesz basis
if

\displaystyle\theta_{f}S_{f,\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}=I_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu)}.

Definition 2.61
.

Let
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
(dual of
\mathcal{X}
). The pair
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is said to be a
continuous p-approximate Riesz family
for
\mathcal{X}
if
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is a continuous p-approximate Riesz basis for
\overline{span}\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
.

Now we can formulate most of the notions, conjectures and problems stated earlier for continuous p-approximate Schauder frames. Here are some samples. Throughout,
(\Omega,\mu)
is a measure space and
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is at least continuous p-approximate Bessel family whenever integrals appear.

Conjecture 2.62
.

(Feichtinger conjecture for continuous p-approximate Schauder frames)
Let
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
be a continuous p-ASF for
\mathcal{X}
such that

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{\alpha\in\Omega}\|\tau_{\alpha}\|\leq\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega}\|\tau_{\alpha}\|<\infty,

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{\alpha\in\Omega}\|f_{\alpha}\|\leq\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega}\|f_{\alpha}\|<\infty.

Then
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
can be partitioned into a finite union of continuous p-approximate Riesz families. Moreover, what is the number of partitions required?

Conjecture 2.63
.

(
Continuous Weaver’s conjecture for Banach spaces
)

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
d
-dimensional Banach space. There are universal constants
b\geq 2
and
b>\varepsilon>0
such that the following holds. Let
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a family in
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a family in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\|f_{\alpha}\|\leq 1,~\|\tau_{\alpha}\|\leq 1,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Omega

and

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right\|\leq b\|x\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Then there exists a measurable partition
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{M}
of
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Delta_{k}}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right\|\leq(b-\varepsilon)\|x\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},\forall 1\leq k\leq M.

Conjecture 2.64
.

(
Continuous Weaver’s conjecture for Banach spaces - strong form
)

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
d
-dimensional Banach space. There are universal constants

and

Conjecture 2.65
.

(
Continuous Akemann-Weaver conjecture for Banach spaces
)
Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space (finite or infinite dimensional). There exists a universal constant
c
such that the following holds. Let
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
be a continuous p-approximate Bessel family with Bessel bound 1 for
\mathcal{X}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega}\|f_{\alpha}\|^{q}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega}\|\tau_{\alpha}\|^{s}\leq\varepsilon,

for some
\epsilon>0
, for some
q,s>0
. Let
\{r_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be any family in [0,1]. Then there exists a measurable subset
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
and a
d>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Delta}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)-\int_{\Omega}r_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right\|\leq c\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d}}\|x\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Delta}f_{\alpha}(\cdot)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)-\int_{\Omega}r_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\cdot)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right\|\leq c\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d}}.

Definition 2.67
.

A continuous p-ASF
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})

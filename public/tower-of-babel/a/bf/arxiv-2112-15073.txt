The first question which probably comes to the reader’s mind is ‘‘why?’’. Why such a construction may be useful or interesting? Now we are going to explain our motivations.

Let
F:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}
be an endomorphism of the measure space
{\cal X},{\cal B},\mu

X\in{\cal B}

(the complete preimage) also lies in

\mu(X)=\mu(F^{-1}(X))

(1). Any such

(the Koopman operator):

(2) For any
F\in End({\cal X},\mu)
it is possible to compute the measure entropy (another name is the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy)
h(F)
.

Our question is as follows. Is it possible to determine in some ‘‘natural way’’ a real nonnegative function on the semigroup
Iso(L^{2}({\cal X},\mu))
so that the diagram

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&End({\cal X},\mu)&\\
h\swarrow&&\searrow\operatorname{Koop}\\
\mathbb{R}_{+}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{h}}}}{{\longleftarrow}}&Iso(L^{2}({\cal X},\mu))\end{array}

is commutative.

Recall the construction of the measure entropy of an endomorphism. Let
J_{N}
be the set of multiindices
j=(j_{0},\ldots,j_{N})

\{0,\ldots,K\}

We define

h_{F}(\chi,N+1)=-\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\mu({\bf X}_{j})\log\mu({\bf X}_{j}).

The function

h_{F}(\chi,n+m)\leq h_{F}(\chi,n)+h_{F}(\chi,m)
. This implies existence of the limit

h_{F}(\chi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}h_{F}(\chi,n).

Finally, the measure entropy is defined by

h(F)=\sup_{\chi}h_{F}(\chi).

Our idea is to construct the entropy of a unitary operator

We define

{\mathfrak{h}}_{U}(\chi,N+1)=-\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\|{\mathfrak{X}}_{j}\|_{\mu}^{2}\log\|{\mathfrak{X}}_{j}\|_{\mu}^{2}.

Other details are the same:

{\mathfrak{h}}_{U}(\chi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}{\mathfrak{h}}(\chi,n),\qquad{\mathfrak{h}}(U)=\sup_{\chi}{\mathfrak{h}}_{U}(\chi).

(1.8)

Corollary

(1.9)

2.1
\mu
-norm of
\pi_{X}

Lemma 2.1

For any
X\in{\cal B}

\|\pi_{X}\|_{\mu}^{2}=\mu(X).

(2.1)

Proof
. For any partition
\chi=\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{J}\}

{\cal M}_{\chi}(\pi_{X})=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mu(Y_{j})\|\pi_{X}\pi_{Y_{j}}\|^{2}=\sum_{\mu(Y_{j}\cap X)>0}\mu(Y_{j})\geq\mu(X).

This implies
{\cal M}_{\chi}(\pi_{X})\geq\mu(X)
.

On the other hand, for
\chi=\{X,Y\}
,
Y={\cal X}\setminus X
we have:

{\cal M}_{\chi}(\pi_{X})=\mu(X)\|\pi_{X}\|^{2}+\mu(Y)\|\pi_{X}\pi_{Y}\|^{2}=\mu(X).

Hence
\inf_{\chi}{\cal M}_{\chi}(\pi_{X})=\mu(X)
.

2.2
\mu
-norm and subpartitions

Lemma 2.2

Let
W
be a bounded operator and
\chi=\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{J}\}
a partition of the set

. Then

\mu(Y)\|W\pi_{Y}\|^{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mu(Y_{j})\|W\pi_{Y_{j}}\|^{2}.

(2.2)

Proof
. The following two simple remarks

\|W\pi_{Y_{j}}\|\leq\|W\pi_{Y}\|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mu(Y_{j})=\mu(Y)

imply (
2.2
).

Corollary 2.1

Lemma
2.2
implies that
{\cal M}_{\chi^{\prime}}(W)\leq{\cal M}_{\chi}(W)

if
\chi^{\prime}
is a subpartition of
\chi
.

2.3
Triangle inequality

Lemma 2.3

For any two bounded operators
W_{1}
and
W_{2}

\|W_{1}+W_{2}\|_{\mu}\leq\|W_{1}\|_{\mu}+\|W_{2}\|_{\mu}.

(2.3)

Proof
. First we show that for any partition
\chi=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{J}\}

\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{1}+W_{2})}\leq\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{1})}+\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{2})}.

(2.4)

We put

a_{j}=\|W_{1}\pi_{X_{j}}\|,\quad b_{j}=\|W_{2}\pi_{X_{j}}\|,\quad c_{j}=\|(W_{1}+W_{2})\pi_{X_{j}}\|,\quad\mu_{j}=\mu(X_{j}).

Then

0\leq c_{j}\leq a_{j}+b_{j}

(2.5)

and inequality (
2.4
) is equivalent to

\sqrt{\sum\mu_{j}c_{j}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\sum\mu_{j}a_{j}^{2}}+\sqrt{\sum\mu_{j}b_{j}^{2}}.

By (
2.5
) to prove this inequality, it is sufficient to check that

\sqrt{\sum\mu_{j}(a_{j}+b_{j})^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\sum\mu_{j}a_{j}^{2}}+\sqrt{\sum\mu_{j}b_{j}^{2}}.

This inequality is equivalent to

\Big(\sum\mu_{j}a_{j}b_{j}\Big)^{2}\leq\sum\mu_{j}a_{j}^{2}\cdot\sum\mu_{j}b_{j}^{2}

which follows from Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwartz.

To derive (
2.3
) from (
2.4
), we take a partition
\chi
such that

\|W_{1}\|_{\mu}>\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{1})}-\varepsilon,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|W_{2}\|_{\mu}>\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{2})}-\varepsilon.

Then by (
2.4
)

\displaystyle\|W_{1}+W_{2}\|_{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{1}+W_{2})}\leq\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{1})}+\sqrt{{\cal M}_{\chi}(W_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{1}\|_{\mu}+\|W_{2}\|_{\mu}+2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, (
2.3
) follows.

An equivalent form of inequality (
2.3
) is

\big|\|W_{2}\|_{\mu}-\|W_{1}\|_{\mu}\big|\leq\|W_{2}-W_{1}\|_{\mu}.

Corollary 2.2

By (
1.5
) we have:

\big|\|W_{2}\|_{\mu}-\|W_{1}\|_{\mu}\big|\leq\|W_{2}-W_{1}\|.

Therefore, the
\mu
-norm is a continuous function of
W
in the
L^{2}
operator topology.

7
Operators of diagonal type

7.1
Definition and properties

In this section we continue the study of bounded operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. Consider the operator

W=(W_{j,k})_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

f\mapsto F=Wf,\quad F_{j}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{j,k}f_{k},\qquad f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{k}e^{ikx},\quad F=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{k}e^{ikx}.

Let
\Lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be the distributions

\Lambda_{k}(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{k+j,j}e^{ijx}.

(7.1)

Then by (
6.2
)

\|\operatorname{Conv}_{\Lambda_{k}}\|=c_{k},\qquad c_{k}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|W_{k+j,j}|.

(7.2)

By using the distributions
\Lambda_{k}
, we obtain another form of
W
(see Remark
6.1
):

Wf=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ikx}\Lambda_{k}*f.

Definition 7.1

The operator
W
is said to be of diagonal type if

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}={{\bf c}}<\infty
. Below
{\cal DT}
denotes the space of such operators.
The sequence
\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
will be said to be the majorating sequence for
W
. We also put

\|W\|_{{\cal DT}}={\bf c}.

If
W\in{\cal DT}
then
|W_{j,k}|\leq c_{j-k}
. Hence, elements
W_{j,k}
which are not too close to zero are concentrated near the main diagonal of the matrix
W
. This motivates the terminology.

Examples
. 1. For any distribution (
6.1
) with finite
c_{\lambda}
the operator
\operatorname{Conv}_{\lambda}\in{\cal DT}
because all
c_{k}
vanish except
c_{0}=c_{\lambda}
.

2. Let
g:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
be a function with absolutely converging Fourier series. Then the multiplication operator
f\mapsto\widehat{g}f=gf
is of diagonal type. Indeed,

\widehat{g}_{j,k}=g_{j-k}
, where
g=\sum g_{k}e^{ikx}
. Hence,
c_{k}=|g_{k}|
.

We have the obvious estimate

\|g\|_{\infty}\leq\|\widehat{g}\|_{{\cal DT}}.

3. If
W\in{\cal DT}
then the conjugated operator
W^{*}
is also of diagonal type with the majorating sequence
\{c_{k}^{*}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{c_{-k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. This follows from the equation
\sup_{j}|W^{*}_{k+j,j}|=\sup_{j}|W_{-k+j,j}|
. Moreover, we have the equation
\|W^{*}\|_{{\cal DT}}=\|W\|_{{\cal DT}}
.

4. Linear combination of operators of diagonal type is an operator of diagonal type and

\|\lambda_{1}W_{1}+\lambda_{2}W_{2}\|_{{\cal DT}}\leq|\lambda_{1}|\|W_{1}\|_{{\cal DT}}+|\lambda_{2}|\|W_{2}\|_{{\cal DT}}.

Lemma 7.1

Product of two operators of diagonal type is also an operator of diagonal type and

\|W^{\prime}W^{\prime\prime}\|_{{\cal DT}}\leq\|W^{\prime\prime}\|_{{\cal DT}}\|W^{\prime}\|_{{\cal DT}}
.

Proof
. Suppose
W^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime\prime}
are of diagonal type. Let
c^{\prime}_{k}
and
c^{\prime\prime}_{k}
be the majorating sequences. Then

|(W^{\prime}W^{\prime\prime})_{jl}|=\Big|\sum_{k}W^{\prime}_{jk}W^{\prime\prime}_{kl}\Big|\leq\tilde{c}_{j-l},\qquad\tilde{c}_{j-l}=\sum_{k}c^{\prime}_{j-k}c^{\prime\prime}_{k-l}.

In particular, the majorating sequence
c_{k}
of the operator
W^{\prime}W^{\prime\prime}
satisfies the inequality
c_{k}\leq\tilde{c}_{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The obvious computation
\sum\tilde{c}_{s}=\sum_{s,k}c^{\prime}_{s-k}c^{\prime\prime}_{k}={\bf c}^{\prime}{\bf c}^{\prime\prime}
finishes the proof.

Lemma 7.2

The space
{\cal DT}
is closed w.r.t. the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal DT}}
.

Proof
. Suppose
\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence: for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

m,n>N\quad\mbox{implies}\quad\|W_{m}-W_{n}\|_{{\cal DT}}<\varepsilon.

(7.3)

Then for any
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
the matrix elements
\{(W_{n})_{jk}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
also form a Cauchy sequence. Hence, there exists a matrix
W=\lim_{n\to\infty}W_{n}
, where the limit is taken element-wise:

W_{jk}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(W_{n})_{jk},\qquad j,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

We put

d_{k}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|(W_{n})_{k+j,j}-W_{k+j,j}\big|,\quad d=\|W_{n}-W\|_{{\cal DT}}.

Then
d=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{k}
. There exists

Proposition 8.2

The second equation (

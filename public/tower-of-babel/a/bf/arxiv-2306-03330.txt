Complex saddles of Chern-Simons gravity
and dS

Abstract
We examine the black hole solutions of dS 3 gravity by applying the explicit dS 3 /CFT 2 correspondence. The gravity theory is described by Chern-Simons theory with complex gauge group SL ( 2 , ℂ ) (2,\mathbb{C}) , and the complexified theory is known to have too many saddle points. We determine the set of “allowable geometry” from dual CFT correlators. Concretely, we classify the possible complex solutions corresponding to dS 3 black holes from Liouville two-point functions. We extend the analysis to Liouville multi-point functions and among others we study geometry corresponding to two linked Wilson loops on S 3 S^{3} by the monodromy matrix of Liouville four-point function. Some parts of the results were presented in a previous letter but here they are explained in more details and extended in various ways. In particular, we generalize the results to the case with higher-spin gravity by focusing the effects of higher-spin charges.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &A=e^{-(\tilde{\rho}+\pi i/2)L_{0}}ae^{(\tilde{\rho}+\pi i/2)L_{0}}+L_{0}d\tilde{\rho}\,,\\
&\bar{A}=e^{(\tilde{\rho}+\pi i/2)L_{0}}\bar{a}e^{-(\tilde{\rho}+\pi i/2)L_{0}}-L_{0}d\tilde{\rho}\end{aligned}

(2.17)

with

\displaystyle a=a_{+}(x^{+})dx^{+}\,,\quad\bar{a}=\bar{a}_{-}(x^{-})dx^{-}\,.

(2.18)

Here
a_{+}(x^{+}),\bar{a}_{-}(x^{-})
are arbitrary functions of
x^{\pm}=it\pm\phi
(or
z=x^{+},\bar{z}=-x^{-}
) and the periodicity
\phi\sim\phi+2\pi
is assigned.
The bulk metric can be read off from

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\text{tr}(A_{\mu}-\bar{A}_{\mu})(A_{\nu}-\bar{A}_{\nu})\,.

(2.19)

Let us first consider the configuration of gauge fields of the form

\displaystyle a_{+}(x^{+})=L_{1}+\frac{2\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}L_{-1}\,,\quad\bar{a}_{-}(x^{-})=-L_{-1}-\frac{2\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}L_{1}\,,

(2.20)

then the metric (

\displaystyle\ell^{-2}ds^{2}=-d\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\frac{8\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}\sinh^{2}\tilde{\rho}dt^{2}+\frac{8\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}\cosh^{2}\tilde{\rho}d\phi^{2}\,.

(2.21)

Here we have performed a shift
\tilde{\rho}\to\tilde{\rho}+\ln\sqrt{2\pi{\mathcal{L}}/\kappa}
.
If we regard
\tilde{\rho}
as a time-like coordinate, then the geometry describes a cosmological universe.
Near the asymptotic future, the metric becomes

\displaystyle\ell^{-2}ds^{2}=-d\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\frac{8\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}e^{2\tilde{\rho}}(dt^{2}+d\phi^{2})\,,

(2.22)

where the boundary geometry is with the flat metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=dt^{2}+d\phi^{2}\,,\quad-\infty<t<\infty\,,\quad\phi\sim\phi+2\pi\,.

(2.23)

Notice that the infinities

are not included, thus the boundary topology is
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
, see, e.g.
[

]
.

The metric obtained above is outside the horizon of our static universe.
The static patch is obtained instead by an analytic continuation
\tilde{\rho}\to i\theta
as

\displaystyle\ell^{-2}ds^{2}=d\theta^{2}-\frac{8\pi{\mathcal{L}}}{\kappa}\sin^{2}\theta dt^{2}+\frac{8\pi{\mathcal{L}}}{\kappa}\cos^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\,.

(2.24)

A coordinate transformation

\displaystyle r=\sqrt{\frac{8\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}}\cos\theta\,,\quad\mathcal{L}=\frac{\ell r^{2}_{+}}{32\pi G_{N}}=\frac{\kappa r_{+}^{2}}{8\pi}

(2.25)

indeed leads to the metric in (

).
Considering a Euclidean time
it\to t_{E}
, the absence of conical singularity at the horizon requires the periodicity

t_{E}\sim t_{E}+\beta
with

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{i\beta}{2\pi}=\frac{i\kappa}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa\mathcal{L}}}\,.

(2.26)

As in the AdS
3
case, it is convenient to define
a holonomy matrix for

along the time cycle by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}e^{\oint A}=\mathcal{P}e^{\oint dt_{E}A_{+}}=e^{-(i\theta+\pi i/2)L_{0}}e^{\Omega}e^{(i\theta+\pi i/2)L_{0}}\,.

(2.27)

The eigenvalues of
\Omega
are computed as
(\pi i,-\pi i)
.

Let us examine more about the holonomy condition. The action of large gauge transformation changes the eigenvalues of
\Omega
as
(2\pi i(n+1/2),-2\pi i(n+1/2))
with integer

.

6

We may require that the holonomy matrix (

) is trivial. The center of gauge group

is

A configuration of gauge fields with the holonomy matrix is given by

\displaystyle a=-\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}}\sigma_{1}(d\phi+(2n+1)dt_{E})\,,\quad\bar{a}=-\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}}\sigma_{1}(d\phi-(2n+1)dt_{E})

with

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\

(2.29)

The metric from the configuration of gauge fields can be read off as

\displaystyle\ell^{-2}ds^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\frac{8\pi(2n+1)^{2}{\mathcal{L}}}{\kappa}\sin^{2}\theta dt^{2}_{E}+\frac{8\pi{\mathcal{L}}}{\kappa}\cos^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\,.

(2.30)

The classical action for the configuration is

\displaystyle-S\,(\equiv S^{(n)}_{\text{GH}})=4\pi(2n+1)\sqrt{2\pi\kappa\mathcal{L}}=(2n+1)\frac{\pi\ell\sqrt{1-8G_{N}E}}{2G_{N}}\,,

(2.31)

where

was introduced in (

See

for the case with conical defect geometry.
If we set

With an integer level and a compact gauge group, a large gauge transformation is a symmetry of Chern-Simons theory. However, now the level is not integer valued nor the gauge group is complex (non-compact), thus a large gauge transformation generates a different configuration, see, e.g.

2.2

In

We then read off all the saddle points of complexified Liouville field theory in the corresponding limit.

The action of Liouville field theory is given by

\displaystyle S_{\text{L}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{\tilde{g}}\left[\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{a^{\prime}}\phi\tilde{g}^{aa^{\prime}}+Q\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\phi+4\pi\mu e^{2b\phi}\right]\,.

Here and in the following, we follow the notation of

\tilde{g}_{aa^{\prime}}
as a reference metric with
\tilde{g}=\det\tilde{g}_{aa^{\prime}}

The theory is invariant under the combination of the Weyl transformation
\tilde{g}_{aa^{\prime}}\to\Omega(\sigma)\tilde{g}_{aa^{\prime}}
and the shift of field
\phi\to\phi-\frac{Q}{2}\ln\Omega(\sigma)
.
With the help of the symmetry, we may set the “physical” metric as

.
The vertex operators are defined by

\displaystyle V_{\alpha}=e^{2\alpha\phi}

(2.33)

with conformal weights
h=\bar{h}=\alpha(Q-\alpha)
. The Liouville central charge
c
is related to the background charge

Q=b+b^{-1}

\displaystyle c=1+6Q^{2}=1+6(b+b^{-1})^{2}\,.

(2.34)

We are interested in sphere amplitudes. We thus require that the Liouville field is regular everywhere on
S^{2}
. It is convenient to move to flat space with the reference metric

with

and
\bar{z}=\sigma_{1}-i\sigma_{2}
by making use of the symmetry of Liouville field theory. Then the regularity condition for the Liouville field on

is mapped to the boundary condition

\displaystyle\phi=-2Q\log|z|+\mathcal{O}(|z|^{0})

(2.35)

at the infinity
|z|\to\infty
. In order to remove the subtlety associated with the infinity
|z|\to\infty
, we consider a disc
D
with radius
R\to\infty
with the boundary
\partial D
. We then instead use the regularized action
[
38
]

\displaystyle S_{\text{L}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{D}d^{2}\sigma[\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{a}\phi+4\pi\mu e^{2b\phi}]+\frac{Q}{\pi}\oint_{\partial D}\phi d\theta+2Q^{2}\ln R\,,

(2.36)

where
\theta
represents the coordinate of the boundary
\partial D

We would like to study classical dS
3
gravity from the viewpoints of its dual CFT
2

[

(see also
[

)
7
7

7

The central charge

is an exact quantity including full quantum corrections since it is fixed by commutation relations among Virasoro generators. The relation to gravitational parameters may receive quantum corrections, but they should not violate the condition

as the physical gravitational parameters take real values.

\displaystyle c\,(\equiv ic^{(g)})=i\cdot 6\kappa=i\frac{3\ell}{2G_{N}}

(2.37)

as in the well-known case of AdS
3
by
[
53
]

We are interested in the leading order of
G_{N}
, which is dual to that of
1/c^{(g)}
. Solving (

\displaystyle b^{-2}=\frac{ic^{(g)}}{6}-\frac{13}{6}+\mathcal{O}((c^{(g)})^{-1})\,.

(2.38)

A unitary region of Liouville field theory is obtained by a real positive

has to take a complex value.
The Liouville field theory with a complex

In the current case, the central charge is large, so we need to set

At the leading order in

\displaystyle b^{2}S_{\text{L}}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{D}d^{2}\sigma[\partial_{a}\phi_{c}\partial_{a}\phi_{c}+16\lambda e^{\phi_{c}}]+\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial D}\phi_{c}d\theta+2\ln R+\mathcal{O}(b^{2})

(2.39)

with finite

and

The boundary condition is

\displaystyle\phi_{c}(z)=-4\ln|z|+\mathcal{O}(|z|^{0})

(2.40)

for

.
Since

is a complex number, the Liouville field is also assumed to take a complex value as emphasized in

\mu
as a real number, which makes
\lambda
to be complex. Here we rather set
\lambda
as a real number, which implies that
\mu
is complex. We will explain the reason of this choice later.

As mentioned above, we would like to determine the saddle points of gravity path integral from Liouville two-point function. The operator should have a large conformal dimension if it is dual to a bulk field back reacting to create a black hole geometry. Such an operator is usually called as a heavy operator.
At
b\sim 0
, the two-point function can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})V_{\alpha}(z_{2})\right\rangle\equiv\int\mathcal{D}\phi_{c}e^{-S_{\text{L}}}\exp\left(b^{-1}\alpha(\phi_{c}(z_{1})+\phi_{c}(z_{2}))\right)\,.

(2.41)

A heavy operator is defined with
\alpha=\eta/b
, where
\eta
is kept finite for
b\to 0
. The conformal dimension
h\equiv ih^{(g)}
of the heavy operator and the energy
E
to create a black hole geometry is related as

\displaystyle 2h\,(\equiv 2\alpha(Q-\alpha))=i\ell E\,,\quad 1-2\eta=\sqrt{1-8G_{N}E}\,.

(2.42)

In particular, a black hole exists only if the condition

\displaystyle 0<\eta<\frac{1}{2}

(2.43)

is satisfied. The upper bound is the same as so-called Seiberg bound
[
57
]
.

We regard the insertions of such heavy operators as a part of action. The equation of motion thus becomes:

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}\phi_{c}=2\lambda e^{\phi_{c}}-2\pi\eta[\delta^{(2)}(\sigma-\sigma_{1})+\delta^{(2)}(\sigma-\sigma_{2})]\,.

(2.44)

Notice that the equation is invariant under the constant shifts
\phi_{c}\to\phi_{c}+2\pi in
with integer
n
. Therefore, once
\phi_{c(0)}
is a
solution to the equation of motion, then the same is true for

\displaystyle\phi_{c(n)}=\phi_{c(0)}+2\pi in\,.

(2.45)

Near

, the heavy operator behaves as

\displaystyle\phi_{c}(z)\sim-4\eta|z-z_{1}|\,,

(2.46)

which implies that the physical metric is

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{1}{r^{4\eta}}(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2})

(2.47)

near
z\sim z_{1}
.
In the presence of heavy operators, we use the modified action
[
38
,
43

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} b^{2}\tilde{S}_{\text{L}}&=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{D-d_{1}-d_{2}}d^{2}\sigma[\partial^{a}\phi_{c}\partial_{a}\phi_{c}+16\lambda e^{\phi_{c}}]+\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial D}\phi_{c}d\theta+2\ln R\\
&\quad-\sum_{i}\left[\frac{\eta}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial d_{i}}\phi_{c}d\theta_{i}+2\eta^{2}\ln\epsilon\right]\,,\end{aligned}

(2.48)

where
d_{i}
is a small disk with radius
\epsilon
including

.
The modified action at a saddle point was evaluated in
[
43
]
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} b^{2}\tilde{S}_{\text{L}}&=2\pi i(n+1/2)(1-2\eta)+(2\eta-1)\ln\lambda+4(\eta-\eta^{2})\ln|z_{12}|\\
&\quad+2[(1-2\eta)\ln(1-2\eta)-(1-2\eta)]\,.\end{aligned}

(2.49)

The semi-classical limit of two-point function (
2.41
) is thus given by the sum of
\exp(-\tilde{S}_{\text{L}})
over some saddle points (
2.45
).

Even in a non-gravitational theory, it is difficult to determine the set of all semi-classical saddle points of path integral. Fortunately, the exact expression of Liouville two-point function is known as

\displaystyle\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})V_{\alpha}(z_{2})\rangle=|z_{12}|^{-4\alpha(Q-\alpha)}\frac{2\pi}{b^{2}}[\pi\mu\gamma(b^{2})]^{(Q-2\alpha)/b}\gamma\left(\frac{2\alpha}{b}-1-\frac{1}{b^{2}}\right)\gamma(2b\alpha-b^{2})\delta(0)\,.

(2.50)

Here we defined

\displaystyle\gamma(x)=\frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(1-x)}\,.

(2.51)

The delta function in the right hand side of (

\langle V_{\alpha}V_{\alpha^{\prime}}\rangle\propto\delta(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})

We then read off the set of semi-classical saddles from the exact expression. For

\displaystyle\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})V_{\alpha}(z_{2})\rangle\sim\delta(0)|z_{12}|^{-4\eta(1-\eta)/b^{2}}\lambda^{(1-2\eta)/b^{2}}\left[\frac{\gamma(b^{2})}{b^{2}}\right]^{(1-2\eta)/b^{2}}\gamma\left(\frac{2\eta-1}{b^{2}}\right)\,.

(2.52)

At the region of
b\sim 0
, we can easily see

Throughout this paper, we promise that

for

takes the principal value, satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} e^{-(\pi i/2)L_{0}}ae^{(\pi i/2)L_{0}}&=i\left(L_{1}-\frac{2\pi{\mathcal{L}}}{\kappa}L_{-1}-\frac{\pi{\mathcal{W}}}{2\kappa}W_{-2}\right)dz\\
&\quad-i\mu\left(W_{2}-\frac{4\pi{\mathcal{L}}}{\kappa}W_{0}+\frac{4\pi^{2}{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}{\kappa}W_{-2}+\frac{4\pi\mathcal{W}}{\kappa}L_{-1}\right)d\bar{z}\,.\end{aligned}

(4.22)

We define
\bar{a}
in a similar manner for non-rotating solution. The rule of replacement we use is

\displaystyle k\to i\kappa\,,\quad\mathcal{L}^{\text{AdS}}\to-i\mathcal{L}\,,\quad\mathcal{W}^{\text{AdS}}\to\mathcal{W}\,,\quad\mu^{\text{AdS}}\to-i\mu\,,\quad\tau^{\text{AdS}}\to i\tau\,.

(4.23)

As in the case of AdS
3
, we can transform the gauge field such that the metric is of the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=g_{\tilde{\rho}\tilde{\rho}}(\tilde{\rho})d\tilde{\rho}^{2}+g_{tt}(\tilde{\rho})dt^{2}+g_{\phi\phi}(\tilde{\rho})d\phi^{2}

(4.24)

by applying the gauge transformation similar to (
4.17
).
Moreover, by changing
\tilde{\rho}\to i\theta
, the metric becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\tilde{g}_{\theta\theta}(\theta)d\theta^{2}+\tilde{g}_{tt}(\theta)dt^{2}+\tilde{g}_{\phi\phi}(\theta)d\phi^{2}\,.

(4.25)

We may perform analytic continuations as
it\to t_{E}
with
t_{E}\sim t_{E}+\beta
. We then define the holonomy matrix for
a
along the thermal cycle

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}e^{\oint a}=e^{\Omega}\,,\quad\Omega=\beta(a_{z}+a_{\bar{z}})\,.

(4.26)

The eigenvalues of
\Omega
are gauge invariant,
in particular, they do not change under the gauge transformation of the form (
4.17
).
We require them to be the same as those for the dS black hole without any higher-spin charges, i.e.
(2\pi i,0,-2\pi i)
or equivalently

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\Omega^{2})=-8\pi^{2}\,,\quad\text{tr}(\Omega^{3})=0\,,

(4.27)

which become

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=-2048\pi^{2}\mu^{3}{\mathcal{L}}^{3}+576\pi\kappa\mu\mathcal{L}^{2}-864\pi\kappa\mu^{2}\mathcal{W}\mathcal{L}+864\pi\kappa\mu^{3}{\mathcal{W}}^{2}-27\kappa^{2}\mathcal{W}\,,\\
&0=256\pi^{2}\mu^{2}\mathcal{L}^{2}+24\pi\kappa\mathcal{L}-72\pi\kappa\mu\mathcal{W}-\frac{3\kappa^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\,.\end{aligned}

(4.28)

We may further define
\zeta
and
\gamma
by

\displaystyle\zeta=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{32\pi{\mathcal{L}}^{3}}}\mathcal{W}\,,\quad\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\mathcal{L}}{\kappa}}\mu\,,

(4.29)

and
C
is defined through the following relation:

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{C-1}{C^{3/2}}\,.

(4.30)

The entropy associated with the higher-spin black hole is

\displaystyle S_{\text{GH}}=2\pi\sqrt{2\pi\kappa\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{4C}}\,.

(4.31)

In above, we have assigned the condition that the eigenvalues of the holonomy matrix defined by (
4.26
) are the same as those of dS black hole without any higher-spin charges (
4.28
). However, it might be possible to consider the gauge configuration obtained by a large gauge transformation as in the case with
N=2
. We examine this issue for generic
N
. We can see that the eigenvalues of
\Omega
for the gauge configuration corresponding to dS black hole without any higher-spin charges are
2\pi i(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\cdots,\rho_{N})
, where

\displaystyle\rho_{j}=\frac{N+1}{2}-j\quad(j=1,2,\dots,N)\,.

(4.32)

As we will define below, these are the components of Weyl vector of SU
(N)
in the orthogonal basis. If we require only that the holonomy matrix
\exp\Omega
is the same as the one for dS black hole without any higher-spin charges, then the eigenvalues of
\Omega
can take

\displaystyle 2\pi i(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\ldots,\Lambda_{N})\,,\quad\Lambda_{j}=m_{j}+\rho_{j}\,,\quad m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}

(4.33)

satisfying
\sum_{j}m_{j}=0
.
As in the case of
N=2
, the saddle points of SL
(N)
Chern-Simons gravity may be labeled by
(n-1)
integers
m_{i}
or
\Lambda_{i}=m_{i}+\rho_{i}
.
Defining
[e_{ij}]_{kl}=\delta_{i,k}\delta_{j,l}
, the corresponding gauge configuration may be given in a diagonal form as

\displaystyle a=-i\sum_{j=1}^{N}e_{jj}((\rho-\eta)_{j}d\phi+\Lambda_{j}dt_{E})\,,\quad\bar{a}=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}e_{jj}((\rho-\eta)_{j}d\phi-\Lambda_{j}dt_{E})\,.

(4.34)

The action corresponding to the configuration can be evaluated as in
[
7
]

\displaystyle-S\,(\equiv S_{\text{GH}}^{(\Lambda)})=\frac{\pi}{3}c^{(g)}\frac{(\rho-\eta,\Lambda)}{(\rho,\rho)}\,,\quad\Lambda=\sum_{j}\Lambda_{j}\epsilon_{j}\,,

(4.35)

which takes a different value depending on the label
\Lambda
of the saddle points. In the next subsection, we read off the possible saddles from
\mathfrak{sl}(N)
Toda field theory by comparing the classical action at each saddle point as in the case with Liouville field theory.

4.2
Toda description

In this subsection, we extend the Liouville theory analysis in subsection
2.2
to that by
\mathfrak{sl}(N)
Toda field theory.
We first introduce notations for
\mathfrak{sl}(N)
Lie algebra. Let us denote the orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
\epsilon_{j}

(j=1,2,\ldots,N)
satisfying
(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
. Then, the simple roots are given by

\displaystyle e_{j}=\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{j+1}\quad(j=1,2,\ldots,N-1)\,,

(4.36)

which satisfy
(e_{i},e_{j})=K_{ij}
with
K_{ij}
being the Cartan matrix for
\mathfrak{sl}(N)
.
The fundamental weights
\omega_{j}

(j=1,2,\ldots,N-1)
satisfy
(\omega_{i},e_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
and given by

\displaystyle\omega_{j}=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\epsilon_{l}-\frac{j}{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\epsilon_{l}\,.

(4.37)

The Weyl vector
\rho
is the half of the sum over all positive root or equivalently the sum over fundamental weights as

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\omega_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rho_{j}\epsilon_{j}

(4.38)

with
\rho_{j}
defined in (
4.32
).

We consider the Toda field
\phi=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\phi^{j}e_{j}
or
\phi_{j}=(e_{j},\phi)
with
\phi_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}K_{ji}\phi^{i}
.
The action is given by

\displaystyle S_{\text{T}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{\tilde{g}}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{a}\phi,\partial_{a^{\prime}}\phi)\tilde{g}^{aa^{\prime}}+(Q,\phi)\tilde{\mathcal{R}}+4\pi\mu\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}e^{b(e_{j},\phi)}\right]\,.

(4.39)

The background charge here is

\displaystyle Q=\left(b+b^{-1}\right)\rho\,,

(4.40)

and the central charge is

\displaystyle c=N-1+12(Q,Q)=(N-1)(1+N(N+1)(b+b^{-1})^{2})\,.

(4.41)

We consider sphere amplitudes as in the Liouville case.
The theory is invariant under the combination of Weyl transformation
\tilde{g}_{aa^{\prime}}\to\Omega(\sigma)\tilde{g}_{aa^{\prime}}
and
\phi\to\phi-Q\ln\Omega(\sigma)
. Making use of it, we set
\tilde{g}_{aa^{\prime}}=\delta_{aa^{\prime}}
except at the infinity
|z|\to\infty
and the boundary condition

\displaystyle\phi=-Q\ln|z|+\mathcal{O}(1)

(4.42)

at
|z|\to\infty
.

We consider the vertex operators of the form

\displaystyle V_{\alpha}=e^{(\alpha,\phi)}\,,

(4.43)

whose conformal weights are

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}=\bar{h}_{\alpha}=\frac{(\alpha,2Q-\alpha)}{2}\,.

(4.44)

The correlation functions are defined in the path integral formulation as

\displaystyle\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}(z_{1})\cdots V_{\alpha_{n}}(z_{n})\rangle=\int\mathcal{D}\phi e^{-S_{\text{T}}}V_{\alpha_{1}}(z_{1})\cdots V_{\alpha_{n}}(z_{n})\,.

(4.45)

We use the normalization of two-point function as

\displaystyle\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})V_{2Q-\alpha}(z_{2})\rangle=\frac{\delta(0)}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\alpha}}}\,.

(4.46)

Performing a reflection relation, we obtain (see, e.g.,
[
39
]
)

\displaystyle\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})V_{\alpha^{*}}(z_{2})\rangle=\frac{\delta(0)R^{-1}(\alpha)}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\alpha}}}\,,

(4.47)

where the conjugate parameter is defined as

\displaystyle(\alpha,e_{j})=(\alpha^{*},e_{N-j})\,.

(4.48)

The coefficient is

\displaystyle R^{-1}(\alpha)=\frac{A(\alpha)}{A(2Q-\alpha)}

(4.49)

with

\displaystyle A(\alpha)=(\pi\mu\gamma(b^{2}))^{(Q-\alpha,\rho)/b}\prod_{e>0}\Gamma(1-b(Q-\alpha,e))\Gamma(-b^{-1}(Q-\alpha,e))\,,

(4.50)

where the product is over the positive roots. In the following, we set

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\alpha<\text{Re}\,Q

(4.51)

by performing a certain reflection to the vertex operator.

As in the Liouville case, we set
b\sim 0
,
\text{Re}\,b^{-2}<0
, and
\lambda\equiv\pi\mu b^{2}
real.
In the semi-classical limit with finite
N
,
the central charge behaves as
[
7
]

\displaystyle c=\frac{N(N^{2}-1)}{b^{2}}+N-1+\mathcal{O}(b)\,,

(4.52)

which implies

\displaystyle b^{-2}=\frac{ic^{(g)}}{N(N^{2}-1)}-\frac{1}{N(N+1)}+\mathcal{O}((c^{(g)})^{-1})\,.

(4.53)

We consider a two-point function of heavy operators with
\alpha=\eta/b
. The equations of motion reduce to

\displaystyle\partial^{a}\partial_{a}\phi^{j}=4\pi\mu b^{2}e^{\phi^{j}}-4\pi(\eta,e^{j})[\delta^{(2)}(\sigma-\sigma_{1})+\delta^{(2)}(\sigma-\sigma_{2})]\,.

(4.54)

As in the case of Liouville description, suppose there exists
\phi_{c(0)}
as a classical solution to the equations of motion, then we have multiple solutions

\displaystyle\phi_{c(n)}=\phi_{c(0)}+2\pi in

(4.55)

with
(e_{j},n)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
j
. In other words, there are different solutions labeled by
N-1
integers
(e_{j},n)
.

We can read off the semi-classical saddles of Toda field theory from the exact expression (
4.49
) with (
4.50
) by taking the limit of
b\to 0
with
\text{Re}\,b^{-2}<0
.
Near
b\sim 0
, the two-point function can be written as

\displaystyle R^{-1}(\alpha)\sim\lambda^{2(\rho-\eta,\rho)/b^{2}}\left[\frac{\gamma(b^{2})}{b^{2}}\right]^{2(\rho-\eta,\rho)/b^{2}}\prod_{e>0}\frac{\Gamma(b^{-2}(\eta-\rho,e))}{\Gamma(-b^{-2}(\eta-\rho,e))}\,.

(4.56)

Using the Stirling’s formula, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\gamma(b^{2})}{b^{2}}\right]^{2(\rho-\eta,\rho)/b^{2}}\sim\exp\left[-\frac{8(\rho-\eta,\rho)\ln b}{b^{2}}\right]

(4.57)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\Gamma(b^{-2}(\eta-\rho,e))}{\Gamma(-b^{-2}(\eta-\rho,e))}\sim\left(e^{-\pi i(\rho-\eta,e)/b^{2}}-e^{\pi i(\rho-\eta,e)/b^{2}}\right)\exp\left[\frac{2(\eta-\rho,e)}{b^{2}}(\ln(\rho-\eta,e)-2\ln b-1)\right]\,,\end{aligned}

(4.58)

the asymptotic behavior of two-point function is found to be

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} R^{-1}(\alpha)\sim\lambda^{2(\rho-\eta,\rho)/b^{2}}\prod_{e>0}\left(e^{-\pi i(\rho-\eta,e)/b^{2}}-e^{\pi i(\rho-\eta,e)/b^{2}}\right)\exp\left[\sum_{e>0}\frac{2(\eta-\rho,e)}{b^{2}}(\ln(\rho-\eta,e)-1)\right]\,.\end{aligned}

(4.59)

Taking the absolute value of two-point function of heavy operators, we find

\displaystyle\left|\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})V_{\alpha^{*}}(z_{2})\rangle\right|\sim\prod_{e>0}\left|e^{\frac{\pi}{6}c^{(g)}\frac{(\rho-\eta,e)}{(\rho,\rho)}}-e^{-\frac{\pi}{6}c^{(g)}\frac{(\rho-\eta,e)}{(\rho,\rho)}}\right|\,.

(4.60)

Here we consider the Weyl group of SL
(N)
denoted by
W
, such that an element
w\in W
exchanges the indices of orthogonal basis
\epsilon_{j}

(j=1,2,\dots,N)
. Then, the above expression can be rewritten as (see
[
74
]
for the modular S-matrix of Toda field theory)

\displaystyle\left|\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})V_{\alpha^{*}}(z_{2})\rangle\right|\sim\left|\sum_{w\in W}\epsilon(w)e^{\frac{\pi}{6}c^{(g)}\frac{(\rho-\eta,w(\rho))}{(\rho,\rho)}}\right|\sim|S_{0\alpha}|\,,

(4.61)

where
\epsilon(w)
is a sign related to
w
.
This is consistent with (
3.71
) even after including the sub-leading non-perturbative corrections.
Compared with (
4.35
), we find that the allowable set of saddle points is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda=w(\rho)\,.

(4.62)

The final answer is quite natural since the gauge configurations labeled by (
4.62
) can be mapped to each other by acting elements of Weyl group of SU
(N)
. Namely, the gauge configurations related by this way should represents the same higher-spin dS
3
black hole.

5
Probing higher-spin dS
3
black holes

In the previous section, we have constructed higher-spin dS
3
black holes and computed their partition functions (or Gibbons-Hawking entropy) both from the higher-spin theory and its dual Toda field theory. In particular, we have identified all geometries realized by path integral saddles. In this section, we examine in more details the properties of higher-spin dS
3
black holes realized as the dominant contributing saddle.
For the purpose, it is convenient to study the boundary-to-boundary two-point function of bulk scalar field on the black hole geometry.
If we view the dS
3
black hole arises due to the back reaction of a pair of heavy operators insertion on the boundary, we can alternatively view this computation as a special case of so-called heavy-heavy-light-light limit
[
75
]
.
However, it is known to be difficult to couple matter fields to higher-spin theory described by Chern-Simons gauge theory with finite dimensional group like SL
(N,\mathbb{C})
. We can avoid such a difficulty by working with the Prokushkin-Vasiliev theory on dS
3

[
30
]
instead of the Chern-Simons gravity with the finite dimensional group. In the next subsection, we introduce the Prokushkin-Vasiliev theory and compute the partition function of its black hole solution. In subsection
5.2
, we will compute the boundary-to-boundary two-point function of bulk scalar field and examine its properties. In this section, we mainly explain the analysis by the dual CFT description, see appendix
D
for details on the analysis from the bulk viewpoints.

5.1
Partition functions

The Prokushkin-Vasiliev theory contains an infinite tower of higher-spin gauge fields with
s=2,3,\ldots
and two complex scalar fields with mass

\displaystyle\ell^{2}m^{2}=1-\lambda^{2}\,.

(5.1)

Note that we need to replace
\ell_{\text{AdS}}\to i\ell
as in (
1.6
) in order to move from the case with negative cosmological constant to that with positive cosmological constant. The higher-spin gauge fields can be described by Chern-Simons gauge theory based on the infinite dimensional higher-spin algebra
\mathfrak{hs}[\lambda]
. The generators of the algebra can be expressed as

\displaystyle V_{n}^{s}\,,\quad s=2,3,\ldots\,,\quad n=-s+1,-s+2,\dots,s-1\,.

(5.2)

Here
V_{0}^{2}
and
V_{\pm 1}^{2}
form an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
sub-algebra, and the commutation relation with the other remaining generators are

\displaystyle[V^{2}_{m},V^{s}_{n}]=(-n+m(s-1))V^{s}_{m+n}\,.

(5.3)

See
[
76
,
77
]
for generic commutation relations. A feature of
\mathfrak{hs}[\lambda]
is that it can be truncated to
\mathfrak{sl}(N)
at
\lambda=\pm N
by dividing ideal formed.
We would like to construct a black hole solution as in the case with SL
(3,\mathbb{C})
Chern-Simons gravity analyzed in subsection
4.1
. Using
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\in\mathfrak{hs}[\lambda]
, the dS
3
black hole in subsection
2.1
can be embedded into the Prokushkin-Vasiliev theory. As in subsection
4.1
, we introduce a non-trivial spin-3 charge to the black hole. In the current infinite dimensional case, there are gauge fields with spin
s>3
. They have also induced charges, which can be evaluated by solving equations of motion.

We would like to compute the partition function of higher-spin black holes. In the AdS
3
case, the higher-spin black hole as a solution to the Prokushkin-Vasiliev theory was constructed in
[
47
]
as reviewed in appendix
D.1
. The gravity partition function of the black hole solution with the higher-spin charges was also computed in
[
47
]
as the following perturbative expansion:

\displaystyle\ln Z_{\text{AdSBH}}(\tau^{\text{AdS}},\alpha^{\text{AdS}})

(5.4)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{i\pi c}{12\tau^{\text{AdS}}}\left[1-\frac{4}{3}\frac{(\alpha^{\text{AdS}})^{2}}{(\tau^{\text{AdS}})^{4}}+\frac{400}{27}\frac{\lambda^{2}-7}{\lambda^{2}-4}\frac{(\alpha^{\text{AdS}})^{4}}{(\tau^{\text{AdS}})^{8}}-\frac{1600}{27}\frac{5\lambda^{4}-85\lambda^{2}+377}{(\lambda^{2}-4)^{2}}\frac{(\alpha^{\text{AdS}})^{6}}{(\tau^{\text{AdS}})^{12}}+\cdots\right]\,,

where
\tau^{\text{AdS}}
is the parameter related to mass of black hole given by (
2.8
).
The leading factor
\exp\frac{i\pi c}{12\tau^{\text{AdS}}}
represents (the holomorphic part of) the partition function without introducing higher spin charges. The parameter
\alpha^{\text{AdS}}=\bar{\tau}^{\text{AdS}}\mu^{\text{AdS}}
play the role of chemical potential for the spin-3 charge as in subsection
4.1.1
, see appendix
D.1
. The partition function is evaluated perturbatively in the dimensionless parameter
\alpha^{\text{AdS}}/(\tau^{\text{AdS}})^{2}
.
It was reproduced from the CFT computation in
[

]
by

\displaystyle Z_{\text{CFT}}=\text{Tr}\left(e^{-\beta^{\text{AdS}}H+2\pi i\alpha^{\text{AdS}}W_{0}}\right)

(5.5)

up to the order in (
5.4
).
In the gravity side, the partition function can be mapped to the one for dS black hole by changing the parameters as

\displaystyle c\to ic^{(g)}\,,\quad\tau^{\text{AdS}}\to i\tau\,,\quad\alpha^{\text{AdS}}=\bar{\tau}^{\text{AdS}}\mu^{\text{AdS}}\to\alpha\,,

(5.6)

as in (
4.23
), see also appendix
D.1
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle\ln Z_{\text{dSBH}}(\tau,\alpha)=\frac{i\pi c^{(g)}}{12\tau}\left[1-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\tau^{4}}+\frac{400}{27}\frac{\lambda^{2}-7}{\lambda^{2}-4}\frac{\alpha^{4}}{\tau^{8}}-\frac{1600}{27}\frac{5\lambda^{4}-85\lambda^{2}+377}{(\lambda^{2}-4)^{2}}\frac{\alpha^{6}}{\tau^{12}}+\cdots\right]

(5.7)

as in (
D.21
).
In order to obtain the gravity partition function, we have to multiply by a factor two, since we need to consider the square of wave functional of universe as in (
1.13
).
We also need to multiply the anti-holomorphic part as well. Putting (see (
2.25
) and (
2.26
))

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{i}{\sqrt{1-8G_{N}E}}\,,

(5.8)

the leading order in
\alpha
becomes
\frac{\pi}{6}c^{(g)}\sqrt{1-8G_{N}E}
by combining the anti-holomorphic part.
It reproduces
S_{\text{GH}}/2
, where
S_{\text{GH}}
is (
1.5
) with (
2.37
). In the rest of this subsection, we will derive this from its dual CFT.

We would like to extend the CFT analysis in
[
48
]
for AdS
3
to that for dS
3
. As argued above, the map from AdS
3
to dS
3
is not so difficult in the gravity side. What we have to do is just inserting
-1
and/or
i
in a proper way. However, it does not look straightforward in the dual CFT side. In the AdS/CFT correspondence, the dual CFT is supposed to live on the spatial boundary of AdS
3
black hole. The boundary of Euclidean BTZ black hole is given by a torus, thus the dual CFT is living on a torus. In the dS/CFT correspondence, the dual CFT is supposed to live on the future or past infinity. The geometry of the boundary is given by a cylinder as we saw in subsection
2.1
. Therefore, it seems impossible to directly map the result for AdS
3
to that for dS
3
as in the gravity analysis. However, we are only interested in thermodynamic quantities, which are obtained in the high temperature limit. We shall show that the difference between torus and cylinder amplitudes disappears at the high temperature limit.

In subsection
2.2
, we have reproduced the gravity partition function from the two-point function of heavy operators in Liouville field theory. The heavy operators create conical defects on
S^{2}
as explained around (
2.47
), thus the same quantity can be computed as the partition function on
S^{2}
with two conical defects as shown in appendix
E.1
. The discussion was done for the pure gravity (or Liouville field theory), but it can be extended for the Prokushkin-Vasiliev theory (or the ’t Hooft limit of Toda field theory) as argued in appendix
E.2
. We compute the CFT partition function in the expansion of
\alpha
, thus the insertions of spin-3 current can be treated perturbatively.
Therefore, we can compute the partition function on
S^{2}
with two conical defects and also with the deformation of spin-3 currents.
As mentioned before, the Toda field theory has the symmetry under the combination of the Weyl transformation and the shift of fields. Utilizing it, we can transform
S^{2}
with two conical defects to a cylinder with coordinates
(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
satisfying

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\sim\sigma_{0}+\beta\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma_{1}\leq L\,.

(5.9)

Here we have introduced an infra-red cut off
L
, which takes a very large value. Applying again the Weyl transformation and the shift of fields, we may set

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\sim\sigma_{0}+2\pi\tilde{\beta}\,,\quad\tilde{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{L}\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma_{1}\leq 2\pi\,.

(5.10)

We need to redefine another parameter
\alpha
.
Since the parameter has conformal
dimension
-2
, we have to rescale
\tilde{\alpha}=(2\pi/L)^{2}\alpha
.

Now the computation reduces to that of CFT partition function on the cylinder as

\displaystyle Z_{\text{CFT}}=\text{Tr}_{r}\left(e^{-\tilde{\beta}H+2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}W_{0}}\right)\,,

(5.11)

where
r
denote a representation.
We expand the partition function in the parameter
\tilde{\alpha}
, thus we compute the cylinder amplitude of the form at order
\tilde{\alpha}^{n}

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{r}\left(W_{0}^{n}e^{-\tilde{\beta}H}\right)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\oint\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\oint\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}F_{r}((W,z_{1}),\cdots,(W,z_{n});\tilde{\tau})

(5.12)

with

and
\tilde{\tau}=i\beta/(2\pi L)
.
Here we use the notation in (2.12) of
[
48
]
as

\displaystyle F_{r}((a^{1},z_{1}),\cdots(a^{n},z_{n});\tilde{\tau})=z_{1}^{h_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{h_{n}}\text{Tr}_{r}(V(a^{1},z_{1})\cdots V(a^{n},z_{n})\tilde{q}^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}})

(5.13)

with
h_{j}
as the conformal weight of
a^{j}
and
\tilde{q}=\exp(2\pi i\tilde{\tau})
.
Moreover, we set

\displaystyle V(a,z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}z^{-m-h}\,.

(5.14)

Performing the
S
-transformation, we obtain

\displaystyle(2\pi i)^{n}\text{Tr}_{r}\left(W_{0}^{n}e^{-\beta H}\right)\sim\tau^{2n}\int_{1}^{q}\frac{d\tilde{z}_{1}}{\tilde{z}_{1}}\cdots\int_{1}^{q}\frac{d\tilde{z}_{n}}{\tilde{z}_{n}}\langle 0|V(W,\tilde{z}_{1})\cdots V(W,\tilde{z}_{n})\hat{q}^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}|0\rangle\,.

(5.15)

Since
L
is a large number acting as an IR regulator,
\hat{\tau}=-1/{\tilde{\tau}}=iL/\beta
is now very large. Therefore, the dominant contribution comes from the amplitude where the in and out states are given by the identity one as above.

With very large
\hat{\tau}
, we can also use the fact that the dominant contribution comes from the primary state
|0\rangle
among the descendant states represented by
|\{k_{n}\}\rangle

In this paper, we examined solutions of Chern-Simons (higher-spin) gravity corresponding to dS

We first focused on the simplest case with pure gravity. The gravity theory is described by SL

We examined the saddle points of Liouville two-point function by following

We also extended the analysis to the higher-spin gravity on dS

It should be important to analyze gravity solutions dual to correlation functions of

Some CFT calculations

In the main context, we have analyzed partition function on

E.1

The metric of
S^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{4dzd\bar{z}}{|1+z\bar{z}|^{2}}=\frac{4(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\phi^{2})}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle ds^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\,.

(E.2)

The partition function is evaluated in (4.9) of

\displaystyle|Z_{\text{CFT}}|^{2}\sim e^{\frac{\pi}{3}c^{(g)}}\,.

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{4dzd\bar{z}}{|1+z\bar{z}|^{2}}=\frac{4(1-2\eta)^{2}(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\phi^{2})}{r^{4\eta}(1+r^{2-4\eta})^{2}}\,.

Using the relation (

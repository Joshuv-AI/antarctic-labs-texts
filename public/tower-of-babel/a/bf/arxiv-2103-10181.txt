Gradient Estimate for the Heat Kernel on Some Fractal-Like Cable Systems and Quasi-Riesz Transforms
Baptiste Devyver, Emmanuel Russ, Meng Yang

Abstract
We give pointwise upper estimate for the gradient of the heat kernel on some fractal-like cable systems including the Vicsek and the Sierpiński cable systems. Applications to L p L^{p} -boundedness of quasi-Riesz transforms are derived.

A natural question is to consider the matching upper estimate of the gradient of the heat kernel. However, gradient operator can not be easily defined using the classical Euclidean way due to the existence of too many “holes”. We turn to consider the corresponding fractal-like manifolds or fractal-like cable systems. Roughly speaking, given a fractal, by translating the small scale self-similar property, we obtain an infinite graph with self-similar property in the large scale. If we replace each edge of the graph by a tube and glue these tubes smoothly at each vertex, then we obtain a fractal-like manifold where the gradient operator is the standard one on a Riemannian manifold. If we replace each edge of the graph by an interval, then we obtain a fractal-like cable system where the gradient operator can be defined as the usual derivative on each interval (although only one-sided derivatives are well-defined at the endpoints of each interval, it does not matter since the set of all the endpoints has measure zero in our consideration).

Our second main result is the

-boundedness of the “quasi-Riesz transforms” for any

in the fractal setting. Recall that, under the volume doubling condition and the sub-Gaussian pointwise upper bound for the heat kernel, it was proved in
[

that the Riesz transform
\nabla\Delta^{-1/2}
is

. In
[
5

, the

was derived from an

-Poincaré inequality are assumed to hold (recall that, in this situation, pointwise Gaussian upper and lower bounds for the heat kernel hold). This contrasts with the
sub-Gaussian
context. Indeed, in the case of the Vicsek cable systems, the Riesz transform is

Motivated by situations where the heat kernel satisfies sub-Gaussian upper bound, consider a weaker form of the Riesz transform, namely the
quasi-Riesz transform
, where the exponent
{1}/{2}
is replaced by some
\varepsilon\in(0,{1}/{2})
. The quasi-Riesz transforms were introduced in
[

[

, say that the local Riesz transform is

\lVert|\nabla f|\rVert_{p}\lesssim\lVert(I+\Delta)^{{1}/{2}}f\rVert_{p}

and that the Riesz transform at infinity is

-bounded if and only if

\lVert|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f|\rVert_{p}\lesssim\lVert\Delta^{{1}/{2}}f\rVert_{p}.

Then the Riesz transform

Inspired by the aforementioned equivalence for the

\displaystyle\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2\Psi^{-1}(t))}\frac{1}{V(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)m(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right).

Moreover, by Equation (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2^{j})}|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{t}(x,z)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2^{j})}\left(\frac{1}{tV\left(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t)\right)}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(C_{2}d(x,z),t\right)\right)\right)^{p}m(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2^{j})}\left(\frac{1}{tV\left(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t)\right)}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)\right)^{p}m(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\frac{1}{tV\left(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t)\right)}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right).

For any
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}r]}\Phi(2^{j})=\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}r]}2^{j}\asymp 2^{[\log_{2}r]}\asymp r=\Phi(r),

while for any
r\in[1,+\infty)
,

\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}r]}\Phi(2^{j})=\sum_{j\leq-1}2^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{[\log_{2}r]}(2^{j})^{\alpha}\asymp 1+r^{\alpha}\asymp r^{\alpha}=\Phi(r).

Hence

\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}r]}\Phi(2^{j})\asymp\Phi(r)\text{ for any }r\in(0,+\infty),

and consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2^{j})}|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{t}(x,z)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV\left(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t)\right)}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right).

Finally, we obtain:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}p_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{t}\frac{1}{V(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV\left(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t)\right)}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV\left(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t)\right)}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right).

The proof is thus complete in the case
d(x,y)\geq 4\Psi^{-1}(t)
.

We now assume that
d(x,y)<4\Psi^{-1}(t)
. Then,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}p_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{t}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2\Psi^{-1}(t))}p_{t}(x,z)m(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2^{j})}|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{t}(x,z)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{t}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2\Psi^{-1}(t))}\frac{1}{V(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}m(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,2^{j})}\left(\frac{1}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\right)^{p}m(\mathrm{d}z)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{t}\frac{1}{V(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}+\sum_{j\leq[\log_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)]}\Phi(2^{j})\frac{1}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{t}\frac{1}{V(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}+\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))\frac{1}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)\exp\left(+\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)\exp\left(+\Upsilon\left(2C_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t),t\right)\right),

where

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(2C_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t),t\right)=\sup_{s\in(0,+\infty)}\left(\frac{2C_{2}\Psi^{-1}(t)}{s}-\frac{t}{\Psi(s)}\right)=\sup_{s\in(0,+\infty)}\left(2C_{2}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(t)}{\Psi^{-1}(s)}-\frac{t}{s}\right)

is bounded from above by some positive constant depending only on
C_{2}
and
\beta
(see Lemma
5.2
below), hence

|\nabla_{y}p_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}p_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-\Upsilon\left(C_{2}d(x,y),t\right)\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\begin{cases}\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t}\right),&\text{if }t<d(x,y),\\
\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-C_{2}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right),&\text{if }t\geq d(x,y),\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t}\right),&\text{if }t\in(0,1),\\
\frac{\Phi(\Psi^{-1}(t))}{tV(x,\Psi^{-1}(C_{1}t))}\exp\left(-C_{2}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right),&\text{if }t\in[1,+\infty),\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\asymp\begin{cases}\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{t}V(x,\sqrt{t})}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t}\right),&\text{if }t\in(0,1),\\
\frac{C_{1}}{t^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}V(x,t^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-C_{2}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right),&\text{if }t\in[1,+\infty),\\
\end{cases}

where in the third inequality, as in Subsection
2.2
, we use the facts that the function
\beta\mapsto\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}
is monotone decreasing if
d(x,y)>t
and monotone increasing if
d(x,y)\leq t
.
∎

The following lemma has been used in the above proof:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A\in(0,+\infty)
. Then there exists some positive constant
C
depending only on
A
and
\beta
such that

\sup_{t,s\in(0,+\infty)}\left(A\frac{\Psi^{-1}(t)}{\Psi^{-1}(s)}-\frac{t}{s}\right)\leq C.

Proof.

Denote

f(t,s)=A\frac{\Psi^{-1}(t)}{\Psi^{-1}(s)}-\frac{t}{s},\quad t,s\in(0,+\infty).

By considering separately the cases
t,s\in(0,1)
,
t,s\in[1,+\infty)
,
0<t<1\leq s
and
0<s<1\leq t
, one shows easily that

f(t,s)\leq A\max\left\{\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{1/2},\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{1/\beta}\right\}-\frac{t}{s}.

Since the function
(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\mapsto A\max\{x^{1/2},x^{1/\beta}\}-x
is bounded from above by some positive constant
C
depending only on
A
and
\beta
, one obtains

\sup_{t,s\in(0,+\infty)}f(t,s)\leq C.

∎

As a consequence, we obtain an
L^{p}
-upper estimate for the gradient of the heat semi-group.

Corollary 5.3
.

For any
p\in(1,+\infty)
, there exists
C\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\lVert|\nabla e^{-t\Delta}|\rVert_{p\to p}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}},&\text{if }t\in(0,1),\\
\frac{C}{t^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}},&\text{if }t\in[1,+\infty).\end{cases}

Proof.

We may assume that
t\in[1,+\infty)
since the proof for
t\in(0,1)
is similar. Taking
\gamma\in(0,+\infty)
, for any
f\in L^{p}(X;m)
, for
m
-a.e.
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla e^{-t\Delta}f(x)|\leq\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|\cdot|f(y)|m(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|\exp\left(\gamma\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{1/p^{\prime}}|f(y)|

\displaystyle\cdot\exp\left(-\gamma\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\frac{1}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{1/p^{\prime}}}m(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|^{p}\exp\left(\gamma p\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{p/p^{\prime}}|f(y)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}y)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{X}\exp\left(-\gamma p^{\prime}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\frac{1}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})}m(\mathrm{d}y)\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

By
VD
, as in
[
5
, p. 944, line -5]
and
[
18
, Equation (2.5)]
, we have

\int_{X}\exp\left(-\gamma p^{\prime}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\frac{1}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})}m(\mathrm{d}y)\lesssim 1.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla e^{-t\Delta}f(x)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{X}\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|^{p}\exp\left(\gamma p\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{p/p^{\prime}}|f(y)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}y)m(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\left(\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|^{p}\exp\left(\gamma p\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)m(\mathrm{d}x)\right)V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{p/p^{\prime}}|f(y)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}y).

By Theorem
1.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|^{p}\exp\left(\gamma p\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)m(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\frac{C_{1}^{p}}{t^{(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta})p}V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{p}}\exp\left(-pC_{2}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\exp\left(\gamma p\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)m(\mathrm{d}x).

Take
\gamma\in(0,C_{2})
, then
VD
implies that

\int_{X}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|^{p}\exp\left(\gamma p\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)m(\mathrm{d}x)\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta})p}V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{p-1}},

hence

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla e^{-t\Delta}f(x)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{X}\frac{1}{t^{(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta})p}V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{p-1}}V(y,t^{1/\beta})^{p/p^{\prime}}|f(y)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta})p}}\int_{X}|f(y)|^{p}m(\mathrm{d}y),

that is,

\lVert|\nabla e^{-t\Delta}f|\rVert_{L^{p}(X;m)}\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;m)}.

∎

(

DUHK(loc)
. By Lemma
6.1

is

If

PI(

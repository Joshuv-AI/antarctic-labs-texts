Definition 5.1
.

A prime number

•

In other words, a prime number

\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho},\mu_{p})

Let

be a trivial prime. The deformations of the trivial representation
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}

will be liftable, however, it will not be a deformation condition. Let
\alpha
be a root which shall be specified later. The root-subgroup

is the subgroup generated by the image of the root-subspace

under the exponential map. We let

be the subgroup of
\operatorname{GSp}_{2n}
consisting of elements which commute with
\text{U}_{\alpha}
.

Definition 5.2
.

[

, Definition 3.1]

Let

consist of the deformation classes of lifts such that some representative
\varrho
satisfies:

(1)

and
\varrho(\tau_{v})\in\text{U}_{\alpha}
,

(2)

under the composite

\varrho(\sigma_{v})

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose that
p>2
is a prime number and let
\Pi
be the a subgroup of
\operatorname{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that the quotient map
\Pi\rightarrow\operatorname{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})/\operatorname{U}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is surjective. Then
\Pi
is equal to
\operatorname{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Proof.

For
x,y\in\operatorname{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, set
\{x,y\}
to denote the commutator
xyx^{-1}y^{-1}
. As
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces, we have that

\operatorname{U}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})/\operatorname{U}_{k+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\operatorname{exp}(X_{L_{1}-L_{2}}),\operatorname{exp}(X_{2L_{2}})\rangle\text{ if }k=1,\\
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\operatorname{exp}(X_{L_{1}+L_{2}})\rangle\text{ if }k=2,\\
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\operatorname{exp}(X_{2L_{1}})\rangle\text{ if }k=3,\\
0\text{ if }k>3.\par\end{cases}

We check that if
x=\operatorname{exp}(X_{\lambda})
and
y=\operatorname{exp}(X_{\mu})
, for roots
\mu
and
\lambda
in
\Phi^{+}
, with height
k
and
l
respectively, then

\{x,y\}=\operatorname{exp}([X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}])\mod{\operatorname{U}_{k+l+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}.

We have the relations

(8.1)

[X_{L_{1}-L_{2}},X_{2L_{2}}]=X_{L_{1}+L_{2}}\text{ and }[X_{L_{1}-L_{2}},X_{L_{1}+L_{2}}]=2X_{2L_{1}}

and that
X_{\lambda}^{2}=0
for
\lambda\in\Phi^{+}
. We have therefore,

\begin{split}\{x,y\}=&(\operatorname{Id}+X_{\lambda})(\operatorname{Id}+X_{\mu})(\operatorname{Id}-X_{\lambda})(\operatorname{Id}-X_{\mu})\\
=&\operatorname{Id}+[X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}]+(X_{\mu}X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}-X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}X_{\lambda})+(X_{\lambda}X_{\mu})^{2}.\end{split}

Since
X_{\lambda}^{2}
and
X_{\mu}^{2}
are both equal to zero, we have that

X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}X_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}[[X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}],X_{\lambda}]

and thus
X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}X_{\lambda}\in(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})_{2k+l}
. Likewise, the same reasoning shows that

X_{\mu}X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}[[X_{\mu},X_{\lambda}],X_{\mu}]

and therefore,
X_{\mu}X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}\in(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})_{k+2l}
. Next, observe that
(X_{\lambda}X_{\mu})^{2}
is equal to
\frac{1}{2}([X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}])^{2}
. This too follows from the relations
X_{\lambda}^{2}=X_{\mu}^{2}=0
. From the relations
(
8.1
)
, we have that if
[X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}]
is nonzero, then,
\lambda+\mu
is a root and there is a constant
c
such that
[X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}]=cX_{\lambda+\mu}
. Since,
X_{\lambda+\mu}^{2}=0

, it follows that
(X_{\lambda}X_{\mu})^{2}=0
. Since
X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}X_{\lambda}\in(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})_{2k+l}
, and the maximal height of any root is
3
, it follows that either
X_{\lambda}X_{\mu}X_{\lambda}
is zero, or a constant multiple of
X_{2L_{1}}
. It may be checked that
X_{2L_{1}}X_{\lambda}=X_{\lambda}X_{2L_{1}}=0
for all
\lambda\in\Phi^{+}
. As a consequence, we arrive at the following relation:

\{x,y\}=\operatorname{exp}([X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}])\operatorname{exp}(-\frac{1}{2}[[X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}],X_{\lambda}])\operatorname{exp}(\frac{1}{2}[[X_{\mu},X_{\lambda}],X_{\mu}]).

Note that
\operatorname{exp}(-\frac{1}{2}[[X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}],X_{\lambda}])
and
\operatorname{exp}(\frac{1}{2}[[X_{\mu},X_{\lambda}],X_{\mu}])
are in
\operatorname{U}_{k+l+1}
. Therefore, we deduce that

\{x,y\}=\operatorname{exp}([X_{\lambda},X_{\mu}])\mod{\operatorname{U}_{k+l+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}.

We deduce from the relations
(
8.1
)
that the commutator
(x,y)\mapsto\{x,y\}
induces a surjective map:

\operatorname{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})/\operatorname{U}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\times\operatorname{U}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})/\operatorname{U}_{k+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\operatorname{U}_{k+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})/\operatorname{U}_{k+2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

It follows by ascending induction on
k
, that the quotient map

\Pi\cap\operatorname{U}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\operatorname{U}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})/\operatorname{U}_{k+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

is surjective for
k\geq 1
. By descending induction on
k
, we deduce that
\Pi\cap\operatorname{U}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\operatorname{U}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
for
k\geq 1
. In particular,
\Pi
is equal to
\operatorname{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and the proof is complete.
∎

H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}/\mathfrak{b})=0\text{ and }H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}/\mathfrak{b})(\bar{\chi}))=0.

Abstract
. We investigate the behavior of the Schrödinger equation under the influence of potentials, focusing on its relationship to quantum revivals and fractality. Our findings reveal that the solution displays fractal behavior at irrational times, while exhibiting regularity similar to the initial data at rational times. These extend the results of Oskolkov [ 16 ] and Rodnianski [ 18 ] on the free Schrödinger evolution to the general case regarding potentials.

1.
Introduction

In 1836, H. F. Talbot
[
20
]
observed an intriguing phenomenon in classical optics. When a plane wave passes through a periodic grating, the image of the diffracted wave is refocused and recovers the initial grating pattern with a specific periodicity. This phenomenon is now known as the Talbot effect. The distance between the grating and the point where the image is fully reconstructed is called the Talbot distance, denoted as
d_{\mathrm{T}}
. Lord Rayleigh
[
17
]
later derived a formula
d_{\mathrm{T}}=a^{2}/\lambda
, where
a
is the spacing of the grating and
\lambda
is the wavelength of the incident wave.

The Talbot effect, originally an optical experiment, can be described by using the Schrödinger evolution which leads us to mathematical studies of the physical consequences. One of such consequence is the phenomenon of quantum revivals, where a quantum wave packet is reconstructed at fractional multiples of a quantum period dependent on the Planck constant and energy levels
[
4
]
.
In particular, Berry and Klein
[
3
]
used the free Schrödinger evolution to model the Talbot effect. They found a dichotomy between “rational” and “irrational” times, where a finite copy of the grating pattern reappears at every
t\in d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{Q}
, but the wave function exhibits a fractal, nowhere-differentiable profile at every
t\notin d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{Q}
.

As the grating is periodic in
x
, many authors have studied intensively the periodic Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{xx}u=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z},\ t\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=f(x).\end{cases}

(1.1)

Assuming the initial function
f
is of bounded variation, Oskolkov
[
16
]
proved that
u(t,x)
, the wave function, is continuous but nowhere differentiable in
x
if
t\notin\pi\mathbb{Q}
, and necessarily contains (at most countable) discontinuities at every
t\in\pi\mathbb{Q}
if
f
has at least one discontinuity. Later, Kapitanski and Rodnianski
[
13
]
showed that
u(t,\cdot)
has better regularity for
t\notin\pi\mathbb{Q}
. For every
t\in\pi\mathbb{Q}
, Taylor
[
22
]
found that if
f
is the delta function,
u(t,\cdot)
is a finite linear combination of translated delta functions with coefficients being Gauss sums.

The Talbot effect has also led to the study of the Schrödinger equation, particularly in relation to Berry’s conjectures on the fractality of the graphs of
\mathrm{Re}\,u(t,\cdot)
,
\mathrm{Im}\,u(t,\cdot)
, and
|u(t,\cdot)|^{2}
. Berry
[
2
]
calculated the fractal dimension of the temporal, spatial, and diagonal restrictions of the graph of the density function
|u(t,x)|^{2}
for a step function
f
. Based on his calculations, he conjectured that for the
n
-dimensional linear Schrödinger equation confined in a box, the graphs of
\mathrm{Re}\,u(t,\cdot)
,
\mathrm{Im}\,u(t,\cdot)
, and
|u(t,\cdot)|^{2}
have fractal dimension
D=n+1/2
at almost all irrational times
t
.

Rodnianski
[
18
]
rigorously justified Berry’s conjecture in one dimension for the real and imaginary parts of the solution
e^{it\partial_{xx}}f
with almost all irrational times, showing that the graphs have fractal dimension
D=3/2
for every bounded variation
f
not in
\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
. Recently, Chousionis, Erdoğan, and Tzirakis
[
7
]
extended this result to initial data in the larger class
BV\setminus\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}H^{s_{0}+\epsilon}
,
\frac{1}{2}\leq s_{0}<\frac{3}{4}
, and settled Berry’s conjecture in one dimension for the density
|e^{it\partial_{xx}}f|^{2}
, showing that the dimension of the graph is
3/2
at almost all
t\notin\pi\mathbb{Q}
when
f
is a step function with jumps only at rational points.

Drawing inspiration from previous work on the free Schrödinger evolution, we investigate quantum revivals and fractality for the Schrödinger equation in the presence of potentials:

\begin{cases}iu_{t}+u_{xx}=V(x)u,\quad x\in\mathbb{T},\ t\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=f(x).\end{cases}

(1.2)

To state our results clearly, we introduce some notation. The space of functions of bounded variation on
\mathbb{T}
is denoted by
BV
. For a
2\pi
-periodic function
f
, we define its Fourier coefficient as
\widehat{f}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{r}^{r+2\pi}e^{-ikx}f(x)dx
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
s\geq 0
, we denote the Sobolev space on
\mathbb{T}
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}}:=\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(1.3)

by
H^{s}
, where
\langle k\rangle=1+|k|
. We also use the notation
H^{s+}:=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}H^{s+\epsilon}
and
H^{+}:=H^{0+}
. Moreover, throughout this paper, if a Banach space of functions
\mathcal{B}^{s}
is decreasing (in the sense of the set inclusion) with respect to a regularity index
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we use the following convenient notation:

\mathcal{B}^{s+}:=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\mathcal{B}^{s+\epsilon},\quad\mathcal{B}^{s-}:=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\mathcal{B}^{s-\epsilon}.

(1.4)

Examples of
\mathcal{B}^{s}
include the Sobolev space
H^{s}
, the Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}
, and the Hölder space
C^{s}
.

Our first result concerns the dichotomy in regularity at rational and irrational times.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
1.2
) with
f\in BV
and
V\in H^{+}
. If
f
is continuous, then
u(t,x)
is jointly continuous on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. When
f
is discontinuous,

•

if
t\notin\pi\mathbb{Q}
, then
u(t,x)
is continuous with respect to
x
,

•

if
t\in\pi\mathbb{Q}
, then
u(t,x)
is bounded and necessarily contains (at most countable) discontinuities.

We also determine the fractal dimension of the solution’s graph at irrational time slices, in relation to the regularity of the potentials and initial data.

Theorem 2
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
1.2
) with
f\in BV
and
V\in H^{+}
. Suppose that

\sigma_{0}:=\sup\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\colon f\in H^{\sigma}\}<3/4

(since
f\in BV
, we have
\sigma_{0}\geq 1/2
).
Let
r_{0}:=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon V\in H^{r}\}
.
Then, for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\pi\mathbb{Q}
:

(i)

The upper Minkowski dimension of the graphs of
\mathrm{Re}\,u(t,\cdot)
,
\mathrm{Im}\,u(t,\cdot)
, and
|u(t,\cdot)|^{2}
is less than or equal to
\max\big\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}-\sigma_{0}-r_{0}\big\}
.

(ii)

The upper Minkowski dimension of the graphs of
\mathrm{Re}\,u(t,\cdot)
and
\mathrm{Im}\,u(t,\cdot)
is greater than or equal to
\frac{5}{2}-2\sigma_{0}
, provided that
\sigma_{0}-\frac{1}{2}<r_{0}
.

2.
Proofs of the theorems

In this section, we prove Theorems
1
and
2

Let us first rearrange the equation in (
1.2

iu_{t}+\big(\partial_{xx}-\widehat{V}(0)\big)u=R(V,u),

where
R(V,u)
is the function on

R(V,u)(t,x)=\big(V(x)-\widehat{V}(0)\big)u(t,x).

Then, by Duhamel’s formula, the solution to the initial value problem (

u(t,x)=e^{it(\partial_{xx}-\widehat{V}(0))}f(x)-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})(\partial_{xx}-\widehat{V}(0))}R(V,u)(t^{\prime},x)dt^{\prime}.

We shall prove that the Duhamel term

\mathcal{P}(t,x):=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})(\partial_{xx}-\widehat{V}(0))}R(V,u)(t^{\prime},x)dt^{\prime}

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. For the purpose we obtain the following smoothing property for
\mathcal{P}(t,\cdot)
, which is the key ingredient in this paper. We provide the proof of the property in Section

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
V\in H^{r}
and
f\in H^{s}
for
r\geq 0
and
0<s<r+1

a\leq r\quad\text{and}\quad a<\min\{1+r-s,1/2\},

then
\mathcal{P}(t,\cdot)\in H^{s+a}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and is continuous in the time variable
t
.

For the evolution
e^{it(\partial_{xx}-\widehat{V}(0))}
, we make use of the following known result due to Oskolkov
[
16
, Proposition 14 and page 390]

Proposition 2
.

Suppose that
g\in BV
.

(i)

If
t\notin\pi\mathbb{Q}
, then
e^{it\partial_{xx}}g
is a continuous function of
x
. If
g
has at least one discontinuity on
\mathbb{T}
and
t\in\pi\mathbb{Q}
, then
e^{it\partial_{xx}}g
necessarily contains discontinuities.

(ii)

If
g
is continuous, then
e^{it\partial_{xx}}g
is jointly continuous in temporal and spatial variables.

.

The quantization results of Berry and Klein
[

]
and Taylor
[

(see

(2.3)

3.
Bilinear estimate in the Bourgain space

In this section, we obtain bilinear estimates for

in the Bourgain space, which are essential in proving Proposition

3.1.
The Bourgain space

For
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
X^{s,b}
the closure of the set of Schwartz functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})_{t}C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})_{x}
under the norm

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}:=\|\langle k\rangle^{s}\langle\tau+k^{2}\rangle^{b}\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}l^{2}_{k}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z})},

where
\widetilde{u}
denotes the space-time Fourier transform of
u
defined by

\widetilde{u}(\tau,k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau t}\widehat{u}(t,k)dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int^{2\pi}_{0}e^{-i(\tau t+kx)}u(t,x)dxdt,\quad(\tau,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}.

The
X^{s,b}
-space is also called the
Bourgain space
or
dispersive Sobolev space
. We also define, for a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, the restricted space
X^{s,b}_{I}
as the Banach space of functions on
I\times\mathbb{T}
equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{I}}=\inf\big\{\|w\|_{X^{s,b}}\colon w|_{I\times\mathbb{T}}=u\big\}.

We notice that
X^{s,b}_{\mathbb{R}}=X^{s,b}
.

The followings are some basic properties of the Bourgain space that we need to prove the smoothing estimate (Proposition
1
). The proofs of the properties, which we omit, can be obtained by routine adaptation of those of
[
21
, Corollary 2.10, Lemma 2.11 and Proposition 2.12]
in the spatially periodic setting and time translation argument.

Lemma 4
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
b>\frac{1}{2}
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
a closed interval. Then
X_{I}^{s,b}\hookrightarrow C(I;H^{s})
and

\sup_{t\in I}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{X_{I}^{s,b}},

where
C
is a constant depending only on
b
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
-\frac{1}{2}<b<b^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}
and
I
a closed interval of length
\delta
. Then

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{I}}\leq C\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}\|u\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}_{I}},

where the constant
C
depends only on
b
and
b^{\prime}
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\frac{1}{2}<b\leq 1
and
I=[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
for
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\delta\leq 1
. Then, for
t\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{i(t-t_{0})\partial_{xx}}f\|_{X^{s,b}_{I}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\Big\|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{xx}}F(t^{\prime},\cdot)dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{X^{s,b}_{I}}\leq C\|F\|_{X^{s,b-1}_{I}},

where
C
depends only on
b
.

3.2.
Bilinear estimate for
R(V,u)

In this section we estimate
R(V,u)=(V-\widehat{V}(0))u
in the
X^{s,b}
-space.

Proposition 3
.

Let
r\geq 0
and
0<s<1+r
, and suppose that

a\leq r,\quad a<1+r-s\quad\text{and}\quad a<1/2.

Then, for every interval
I
,

\|R(V,u)\|_{X_{I}^{s+a,b^{\prime}-1}}\lesssim\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|u\|_{X_{I}^{s,b}}

(3.1)

provided that
b,b^{\prime}\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon)
for an
\epsilon>0
small enough.

We first recall from
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
the following simple lemma which is used several times in proving the proposition.

Lemma 7
.

Let us define, for
\beta\geq 0
,

\phi_{\beta}(k):=\sum_{|n|\leq|k|}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{\beta}}\approx\begin{cases}1,&\beta>1,\\
\log(1+\langle k\rangle),&\beta=1,\\
\langle k\rangle^{1-\beta},&\beta<1.\end{cases}

(3.2)

If
\beta\geq\gamma\geq 0
and
\beta+\gamma>1
, then

\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\langle n-k_{1}\rangle^{\beta}\langle n-k_{2}\rangle^{\gamma}}\lesssim\frac{\phi_{\beta}(k_{1}-k_{2})}{\langle k_{1}-k_{2}\rangle^{\gamma}}.

Proof of Proposition
3
.

First, let us prove (
3.1
) by replacing the restricted spaces
X_{I}^{s+a,b^{\prime}-1}
and
X_{I}^{s,b}
with
X^{s+a,b^{\prime}-1}
and
X^{s,b}
, respectively. We write

\displaystyle\|R(V,u)\|_{X^{s+a,b^{\prime}-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}\langle k\rangle^{s+a}\langle\tau+k^{2}\rangle^{b^{\prime}-1}\widehat{V}(k-l)\widetilde{u}(\tau,l)\bigg|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}M(k,l,\tau)\langle k-l\rangle^{r}\widehat{V}(k-l)\langle l\rangle^{s}\langle\tau+l^{2}\rangle^{b}\,\widetilde{u}(\tau,l)\bigg|^{2}d\tau,

(3.3)

where

M(k,l,\tau):=\langle k\rangle^{s+a}\langle\tau+k^{2}\rangle^{b^{\prime}-1}\langle l\rangle^{-s}\langle k-l\rangle^{-r}\langle\tau+l^{2}\rangle^{-b}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz and Hölder inequalities (
3.3
) is bounded by

\left(\sup_{\tau,k}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}M(k,l,\tau)^{2}\right)\int\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}\langle k-l\rangle^{2r}|\widehat{V}(k-l)|^{2}\langle l\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau+l^{2}\rangle^{2b}|\widetilde{u}(\tau,l)|^{2}d\tau.

In the
\tau
-integration we replace the summation
\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}
with
\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and then apply Young’s inequality for the convolution, i.e.,
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k-l}b_{l}|\leq\|a\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\|b\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}
. Consequently we obtain the following estimate

\|R(V,u)\|_{X^{s+a,b^{\prime}-1}}^{2}\leq\left(\sup_{\tau,k}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}M(k,l,\tau)^{2}\right)\|V\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}^{2}.

Hence it remains to prove that

\sup_{\tau,k}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}M(k,l,\tau)^{2}<\infty.

From the assumption, it is clear that
2b\geq 2-2b^{\prime}
. Hence, by the easy inequality
\langle\tau+m\rangle\langle\tau+n\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\langle n-m\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}M(k,l,\tau)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}\frac{\langle k\rangle^{2s+2a}\langle l\rangle^{-2s}\langle k-l\rangle^{-2r}}{\langle\tau+k^{2}\rangle^{2-2b^{\prime}}\langle\tau+l^{2}\rangle^{2-2b^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}}\frac{\langle k\rangle^{2s+2a}\langle l\rangle^{-2s}\langle k-l\rangle^{-2r}}{\langle l^{2}-k^{2}\rangle^{2-2b^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{\pm k\}}\frac{\langle k\rangle^{2s+2a}\langle l\rangle^{-2s}\langle k-l\rangle^{-2r}}{\langle l+k\rangle^{2-2b^{\prime}}\langle l-k\rangle^{2-2b^{\prime}}}+\langle k\rangle^{2a-2r}.

(3.4)

Since
a\leq r
it is obvious that
\langle k\rangle^{2a-2r}\leq 1
, so we need only to show the summation in (
3.4
) is bounded uniformly in
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. That is, we have to prove that for some
\epsilon>0

S:=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{\pm k\}}\langle l\rangle^{-2s}\langle k-l\rangle^{-2r+\epsilon-1}\langle k+l\rangle^{\epsilon-1}\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-2(s+a)}.

Here, for the sake of brevity we renamed
\epsilon
as
\epsilon/2
, so
2b,2b^{\prime}\in(1,1+\epsilon)
.
Given
k\neq 0,
We also write simply
\phi_{\beta}
to mean
\phi_{\beta}(ck)
with
c
a constant because they have the same order of magnitude.

We can assume
k>0
by the symmetry and because for
k=0
the series converges for any
\epsilon<1/2
.
By using Lemma
7
, we have

S\lesssim\begin{cases}\langle k\rangle^{-2r-1+\epsilon-\min\{2s,1-\epsilon\}}\phi_{\max\{2s,1-\epsilon\}}\quad\textrm{if}\quad|l|\leq k/2,\\
\langle k\rangle^{-2s+\epsilon-1}\phi_{2r+1-\epsilon}+\langle k\rangle^{-2s+\epsilon-1-2r}\phi_{1-\epsilon}\quad\textrm{if}\quad k/2\leq|l|\leq 3k/2,\\
\langle k\rangle^{-2s-2r-1+2\epsilon}\quad\textrm{if}\quad 3k/2\leq|l|.\end{cases}

(3.5)

In the second case, the second term is negligible because
\langle k\rangle^{-2r}\phi_{1-\epsilon}\lesssim\phi_{2r+1-\epsilon}
is trivial for
r=0
and
0<\epsilon<2r
. In both situations,
\phi_{2r+1-\epsilon}\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{\epsilon}
and we deduce
S\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{2\epsilon-1-2s}
.
This bound also holds in the third case (because
r\geq 0
).
Hence
a<1/2
assures
S\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-2(s+a)}
when
\epsilon
is small enough.
It only remains to check the first case.
Since
\phi_{\max\{2s,1-\epsilon\}}\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{\epsilon}
for
s\geq 1/2
, we deduce
S\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-2r-2+3\epsilon}
which assure
S\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-2(s+a)}
if
a<1+r-s
and
\epsilon
is small enough.
For
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
, we have
\langle k\rangle^{-\min\{2s,1-\epsilon\}}\phi_{\max\{2s,1-\epsilon\}}\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-2s+\epsilon}
and
S\lesssim\langle k\rangle^{-2(s+a)}
if
a<1/2
and
\epsilon
is small enough.

We now prove (
3.1
). Let
\chi_{I}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\chi_{I}=1
on
I
. By the definition of the restricted Bourgain space and the estimate what we have obtained, we see that

\|R(V,u)\|_{X_{I}^{s+a,b^{\prime}-1}}\leq\|\chi_{I}R(V,u)\|_{X^{s+a,b^{\prime}-1}}\lesssim\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|\chi_{I}u\|_{X^{s,b}}.

Taking infimum over all functions
w
such that
w|_{I\times\mathbb{R}}=u
on the right side, we get the estimate (
3.1
).
∎

4.
Local well-posedness and smoothing estimate

In this section, we make use of Proposition
3
and employ the contraction mapping principle to establish local well-posedness in the Sobolev spaces for the initial value problem (
1.2
). We then prove Proposition
1
.

Let
u
be the solution to the equation (
1.2
) and set
v(t,x):=e^{it\widehat{V}(0)}u(t,x)
. Since

\displaystyle iv_{t}+v_{xx}

\displaystyle=e^{it\widehat{V}(0)}\big(iu_{t}-\widehat{V}(0)u+u_{xx}\big)

\displaystyle=e^{it\widehat{V}(0)}\big(V-\widehat{V}(0)\big)u=\big(V-\widehat{V}(0)\big)v=R(V,v),

the initial value problem (
1.2
) is equivalent to the following:

\begin{cases}iv_{t}+v_{xx}=R(V,v),\\
v(0,x)=f(x).\end{cases}

(4.1)

Let us recall the definition of
\mathcal{P}(t,x)
from (
2.1
) and note that

v(t,x)-e^{it\partial_{xx}}f=e^{it\widehat{V}(0)}\big(u(t,x)-e^{it(\partial_{xx}-\widehat{V}(0))}f\big)=e^{it\widehat{V}(0)}\mathcal{P}(t,x).

(4.2)

Since
\|\mathcal{P}(t,x)\|_{H^{s}}=\|e^{it\widehat{V}(0)}\mathcal{P}(t,x)\|_{H^{s}}
, in order to prove Proposition
1
it is enough to prove the smoothing estimate for
e^{it\widehat{V}(0)}\mathcal{P}(t,x)
instead of
\mathcal{P}(t,x)
. Therefore, in this section, we may and shall consider the equation (
4.1
) instead of (
1.2
).

4.1.
Local well-posedness

We now prove the initial value problem (
4.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}
. We use the notation
X^{s,b}_{\delta}:=X^{s,b}_{[0,\delta]}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
V\in H^{r}
for
r\geq 0
and suppose
0<s<1+r
and
\frac{1}{2}<b<\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
small enough.
For every
g\in H^{s}
, there exists a time
\delta>0
and a unique solution
v_{g}\in X_{\delta}^{s,b}
for the integral equation

v_{g}(t,x)=e^{it\partial_{xx}}g-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{xx}}R(V,v_{g})(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

(4.3)

Furthermore, the mapping
g\mapsto v_{g}
is Lipschitz continuous with the estimate

\|v_{g}\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

(4.4)

Remark 3
.

By Lemma
4
we have
X_{\delta}^{s,b}\hookrightarrow C([0,\delta],H^{s})
. Thus, the theorem establishes the local well-posedness of (
4.1
) (hence (
1.1
)) in
H^{s}
, in the sense of
[
21
, Definition 3.4]
.

Remark 4
.

In fact, as can be seen in the proof (see (
4.6
) below), the small time
\delta
is independent of the initial data
f\in H^{s}
. Since the initial value problem (
4.1
) is invariant under time translations, we can patch the solution (
4.3
) along
t\in\mathbb{R}
by repeatedly applying Theorem
3
to obtain the global solution
v\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{s})
to (
4.1
). Also, the estimate (
4.4
) combined with Lemma
4
implies the following global bound

\|v(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim e^{|t|}\|f\|_{H^{s}},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.5)

Indeed, by using Lemma
4
and (
4.4
) in turn, we have
\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\|v\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}}
,
and in the same way

\sup_{t\in[\delta,2\delta]}\|v\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|v(\delta)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C^{2}\|f\|_{H^{s}}
.
Repeating this process, we get

\sup_{(j-1)\delta\leq t\leq j\delta}\|v(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C^{j}\|f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\delta}C^{t/\delta}\|f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\delta}e^{t}\|f\|_{H^{s}}

for all integers
j\geq 1
. The case where
j\leq 0
follows from a similar argument.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

For each
g\in H^{s}
we define

\Gamma_{g}(v)(t,x):=e^{it\partial_{xx}}g-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{xx}}R(V,v)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

We aim to show that, for some small time
0<\delta<1
and
K>0
to be chosen later, the mapping
\Gamma_{g}
is a contraction on the set

X:=\big\{w\in X_{\delta}^{s,b}\colon\|w\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}\leq K\|g\|_{H^{s}}\big\}.

Let us first show that the mapping
\Gamma_{g}\colon X\to X
is well-defined. Lemmas
5
and
6
imply that if
w\in X
, then

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{g}(w)\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{it\partial_{xx}}g\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}+\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{xx}}R(V,w)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{H^{s}}+C\|R(V,w)\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{H^{s}}+C\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}\|R(V,w)\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b^{\prime}-1}}

whenever
0<\delta<1
and
\frac{1}{2}<b<b^{\prime}<1
. We then apply Proposition
3
(with
a=0
) to see that

\|\Gamma_{g}(w)\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{s}}+C\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|w\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}},

provided that
\frac{1}{2}<b<b^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
for
\epsilon>0
small enough. Since
w\in X
we get

\|\Gamma_{g}(w)\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}\leq C_{0}\big(1+K\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}\|V\|_{H^{r}}\big)\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

If we set
K\geq 2C_{0}
and take
0<\delta<1
so small that

\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}<1/(1+K\|V\|_{H^{r}}),

(4.6)

then it follows that

\|\Gamma_{g}(w)\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}\leq K\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

Therefore,
\Gamma_{g}(X)\subset X
for
g\in H^{s}
.

Secondly, let us prove that the map
\Gamma_{g}\colon X\to X
is a contraction. In a similar manner, by Lemmas
5
and
6
and Proposition
3
we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{g}(w_{1})-\Gamma_{g}(w_{2})\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}\|R(V,w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b^{\prime}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}},

which implies
\Gamma_{g}
is a contraction on
X
for
\delta
satisfies (
4.6
), if we set
K\geq 2C
here. By applying the contraction mapping principle, it follows that there exists a unique
v_{g}\in X
solving the equation
\Gamma_{g}(v_{g})=v_{g}
.

The continuity of the map
g\mapsto v_{g}
also follows from utilizing Lemmas
5
and
6
and Proposition
3
as in the above. Indeed, by (
4.3
) we see that for
g,h\in H^{s}
,

\displaystyle\|v_{g}-v_{h}\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{it\partial_{xx}}(g-h)\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}+\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{xx}}\big(R(V,v_{g})(t^{\prime})-R(V,v_{h})(t^{\prime})\big)dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g-h\|_{H^{s}}+C\delta^{b^{\prime}-b}\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|v_{g}-v_{h}\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g-h\|_{H^{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\|v_{g}-v_{h}\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}},

from which it follows that
\|v_{g}-v_{h}\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,b}}\lesssim\|g-h\|_{H^{s}}
.
∎

4.2.
Smoothing estimate: Proof of Proposition
1

In this final section, we prove Proposition
1
.

Let
f
and
V
be given as in the statement of Proposition
1
.
It is enough to prove that for every
t_{0}\geq 0
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\mathcal{P}(t_{0},x)\|_{H^{s+a}}\leq C\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

(4.7)

This gives regularity gain for
\mathcal{P}(t,\cdot)
compared to the global estimate (
4.5
) for the solution
v
. In order to prove (
4.7
), the bilinear estimate (Proposition
3
) as well as the local well-posedness (Theorem
3
) is crucial.

Let us invoke the
\delta>0
in Theorem
3
and pick
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(m-1)\delta\leq t_{0}<m\delta
. We also set

v_{j}(x)=v(j\delta,x)\quad\text{and}\quad I_{j}=[\delta j,\delta(j+1)].

(4.8)

Applying Theorem
3
with the initial data
v_{j}
, it is possible to write the solution
v
as

v(t)=e^{i(t-\delta j)\partial_{xx}}v_{j}-i\int_{\delta j}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{xx}}R(V,v)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},\quad t\in I_{j}.

By Lemmas
4
and
6
we have, for
t\in I_{j}
,

\displaystyle\|v(t)-e^{i(t-\delta j)\partial_{xx}}v_{j}\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\int_{\delta j}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{xx}}R(V,v)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{X_{I_{j}}^{s+a,b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|R(V,v)\|_{X_{I_{j}}^{s+a,b-1}}

whenever
\frac{1}{2}<b\leq 1
. Let us choose
b
sufficiently close to
\frac{1}{2}
, and then apply Proposition
3
(with
b^{\prime}=b
) to see that

\|R(V,v)\|_{X_{I_{j}}^{s+a,b-1}}\lesssim\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|v\|_{X_{I_{j}}^{s,b}}.

Hence, by the estimate (
4.5
), we conclude that

\|v(t)-e^{i(t-\delta j)\partial_{xx}}v_{j}\|_{H^{s+a}}\leq Ce^{t_{0}}\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|f\|_{H^{s}},\quad t\in I_{j},

(4.9)

for
j\in\{0,1,2,\cdots,m-1\}
. We recall (
4.2
) and write

\displaystyle e^{it_{0}\widehat{V}(0)}\mathcal{P}(t_{0},x)=v(t_{0})-e^{it_{0}\partial_{xx}}f

\displaystyle=v(t_{0})-e^{i(t_{0}-\delta(m-1))\partial_{xx}}v_{m-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}e^{i(t_{0}-\delta j)\partial_{xx}}\big(v_{j}-e^{i\delta\partial_{xx}}v_{j-1}\big).

Application of the estimate (
4.9
) gives

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v(t_{0})-e^{i(t_{0}-\delta(m-1))\partial_{xx}}v_{m-1}\|_{H^{s+a}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\|v_{j}-e^{i\delta\partial_{xx}}v_{j-1}\|_{H^{s+a}}

\displaystyle\leq Cme^{t_{0}}\|V\|_{H^{r}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

Therefore, we obtain the desired estimate (
4.7
).

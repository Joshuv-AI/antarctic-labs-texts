Global Controllability Criteria and Motion Planning of Regular Affine Systems With Drifts
Zhengping Ji, Xiao Zhang, Daizhan Cheng

Abstract
In this article, we give a condition for the global controllability of affine nonlinear control systems with drifts on Euclidean spaces. Under regularity assumptions, the condition is necessary and sufficient in the codimension-1 and codimension-2 cases, and holds for systems of higher codimensions under mild restrictions. We then investigate motion planning problems for codimension-1 affine systems, and give proof of the global existence of the lift to control curves for certain drifted systems using the homotopy continuation method.



I
Introduction

Finding necessary and sufficient conditions for the global controllability of affine control systems

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=f|_{x(t)}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}u^{i}(t)g_{i}|_{x(t)}

(1)

with smooth vector fields
f
,
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and measurable, essentially bounded controls
\{u^{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a basic problem in control theory. It has been investigated since the 1930s when Chow and Rachevskii provided bracket-generating conditions
[
1
]
for the global controllability of the driftless version of (
1
) as

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}u^{i}(t)g_{i}|_{x(t)},

(2)

where
\{u^{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
,
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are as in (
1
). The global controllability condition of (
1
) has been solved in some simple cases, such as when the drift vector field
f
lies in the Lie algebra generated by
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}

[
2
]
, for planar systems
[
19
]
, and for codimension-
1
systems under assumptions requiring global generating families
[
14
]
. The general case, however, remains open.

Suppose the system (
1
) is defined on a manifold
M
. For
\forall p\in M
, the reachable set of
p
is defined as

\mathcal{A}(p):=\bigcup_{T\geqslant 0}\mathcal{A}(p,T),

where

\mathcal{A}(p,T):=\Bigg\{x(T)\Bigg|\begin{array}[]{l}\exists u^{i}\in L^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}),i=1,\cdots,m,\\
(x(t),u(t))~\text{satisfying (\ref{0.1})},~x(0)=p\end{array}\Bigg\}.

When
\mathcal{A}(p)
contains an open set with respect to the topology of
M
, we say that the system (
1
) is strongly attainable at
p
. If for
\forall p\in M
,
\mathcal{A}(p)=M
, we call (
1
) globally controllable.

For globally controllable driftless affine systems, the motion planning problem has been widely considered. Its basic objective is: given any pair
(p,q)
in the state space of a control system, to design a control that yields an admissible trajectory steering
p
to
q
. Several methods have been proposed for this problem, such as the nilpotent approximation
[
11
]
, the loop method
[
18
]
, and steering with sinusoidal controllers
[
15
]
. However, there have been few investigations of motion planning problems for drifted affine systems due to the difficulty of dealing with drift and the lack of controllability conditions
[
23
]
.

Over the past thirty years, the homotopy continuation method (HCM) introduced by
[
21
]
has been applied to motion planning of affine systems and has shown good performance in numerical practice
[
3
]
. The basic idea of HCM for driftless affine systems is to lift a curve in
M
to one in the control space
{\cal U}
through the endpoint map
\mathcal{E}_{p}:{\cal U}\rightarrow M
,
u\mapsto\gamma_{u,p}(1)
, where
\gamma_{u,p}
is the solution of the control system (
2
) corresponding to
u
starting from
p\in M
. If a path
\gamma(t)
connecting
q_{0}
and
q
on
M
can be lifted through
\mathcal{E}_{p}
to a path
\zeta
on
\cal U
starting from
u_{0}
, satisfying
\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{0})=q_{0}
, that is,

\exists\zeta:[0,1]\rightarrow{\cal U},~{\text{s.t.}}~\gamma(t)=\mathcal{E}_{p}(\zeta(t)),~\forall t\in[0,1],

then the trajectory
\zeta(1):=(u_{1}^{*}(t),\cdots,u_{m}^{*}(t))\in\cal U
will give the required controls
u_{1}^{*}(t),\cdots,u_{m}^{*}(t)
driving system (
1
) from
p
to
q
by time
t=1
.

A sufficient condition for the curve
\gamma(t)
to be lifted is the existence of the solution to the following path-lifting equation (PLE):

\displaystyle D\mathcal{E}_{p}|_{\zeta(t)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\zeta(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}.

(3)

If (
3
) admits a global solution on
[0,1]
, then the motion planning problem is solved by
u(t)=\zeta(1)
(numerically, (
3
) can be solved by a finite-dimensional approximation of the control space
[
3
]
). Thus, the problem is reduced to avoiding singular points of
D\mathcal{E}_{p}
and finding conditions on the vector fields of (
2
) for the PLE to be solved globally on
[0,1]
. It was proved
[
10
]
that if the Moore-Penrose inverse of
D\mathcal{E}_{p}
at
u
, denoted by
P(u)
, has linear growth of
\|u\|
on any compact set, then (
3
) is globally solved on
[0,1]
. Such conditions are satisfied for some special types of driftless systems
[
10
,
22
]
, but so far there are no known results on the application of HCM to drifted systems. This is because the domain of the endpoint map should be changed to a product space for drifted cases, and due to the existence of the drift, the time parameter cannot be restricted to
[0,1]
as in the driftless case, which will also change the PLE consequently.

In this article, we consider affine systems defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonzero drifts. We will show that when the control Lie algebras of the corresponding driftless systems are regular and the controls are
L^{2}
, the sufficient conditions given in
[
8
, Theorem 6.1]
are also necessary for global controllability of systems of codimension
1
, and of systems of codimension
2
or higher under certain restrictions. We then discuss the motion planning problems of globally controllable affine systems. When a system of codimension
1
allows globally generating vector fields, we provide a simple steering algorithm; and for general drifted systems, we adopt the HCM and show the global existence of the lifting curve when the vector fields satisfy certain restrictions.

II
Necessary and Sufficient Conditions for Controllability: Codimension
1

Consider the system (
1
) defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. From now on we denote by
G:=\Lie\{g_{i}\}
the Lie algebra generated by
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, i.e. the smallest Lie algebra containing
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. We make the following assumption:

(A1)

G
is regular, i.e.
\dim G|_{x}=\Const
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
\varDelta
an involutive distribution on
M
(in this article we assume that all distributions are smooth). Given
x\in M
, denote by
\mathcal{I}_{\varDelta}(x)
the maximal integral submanifold passing through

corresponding to
\varDelta
. Under the assumption
(A1)
, we define the codimension of the system (
1
) as
n-\dim G
, and if the system is of codimension
k
, then every integral manifold
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is an
(n-k)
-dimensional injectively immersed edgeless submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, giving
\mathbb{R}^{n}
a codimension-
k
foliation structure
[
6
]
, where every maximal integral submanifold is a leaf. In fact,
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is the reachable set of
x
corresponding to the driftless system (
2
). This is the classical Chow-Rashevskii theorem
[
1
]
.

Given a manifold
M
and a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, denote by
\psi^{X}_{t}(x)
the flow of
X
starting from
x\in M
after time
t
. The critical question in the problem of controllability of affine nonlinear system (
1
) is whether
\{\psi_{t}^{f}(x)\}_{t<0}
can intersect the reachable set of
x
(we assume that all vector fields mentioned are complete).

Lemma II.1

Consider the control system (
1
) of codimension
1
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{f}(x)
the reachable set of a state
x\in M
with respect to (
1
). Consider the following system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=-f|_{x(t)}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}u^{i}(t)g_{i}|_{x(t)}

(4)

where
f
,
\{g_{i}\}
are vector fields defined as in (
1
). Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{-f}(x)
the reachable set of
x\in M
with respect to (
4
). Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

The system (
1
) is globally controllable;

2.

The system (
4
) is globally controllable;

3.

\mathcal{A}_{f}(p){\cap}\mathcal{A}_{-f}(p)\neq\varnothing
,
\forall p\in M
.

Proof:

\forall p,q\in M
,
q\in\mathcal{A}_{-f}(p)
is equivalent to
p\in\mathcal{A}_{f}(q)
, hence
\mathcal{A}_{-f}(p)=M
,
\forall p\in M
is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}_{f}(p)=M
,
\forall x\in M
.

On the other hand, if
\mathcal{A}_{f}(p){\cap}\mathcal{A}_{-f}(p)\neq\varnothing
, then there exists a control
u:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
s.t. the trajectory
\gamma_{u}:[0,T]\rightarrow M
corresponding to
u
satisfies
\gamma_{u}(0)=\gamma_{u}(T)=p
. Choose a neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that there exists a point
y\in\gamma_{u}([0,T])
satisfying
q\notin\mathcal{A}_{f}(p)\cap U
; then there is a neighbourhood
V
of
p
such that
V\subset\mathcal{A}_{f}(q)
. Also note that
A_{f}(q)\subset\mathcal{A}_{f}(p)
, one can see that
A_{f}(p)
contains an open neighborhood of
p
, hence by the well known fact that pointwise local controllability implies global controllability
[
13
]
, the system is globally controllable. ∎

Proposition II.2

Consider system (
1
) of codimension
1
. Suppose
\exists p\in M
such that there is a open neighbourhood
U_{p}
of
p\in M
such that
\mathcal{I}_{G}(p)
is dense in
U_{p}
, then the system is globally controllable from any point
q_{1}\in U_{p}
to another point
q_{2}\in U_{p}
, if and only if, the system is bracket-generating at each point in
U_{x}
.

Proof:

We need to show that
\mathcal{A}_{f}(q){\cap}\mathcal{A}_{-f}(q)\neq\varnothing
,
\forall q\in U_{p}
. Since
\mathcal{I}_{G}(p)
is dense in an open set of
M
,
\mathcal{A}_{f}(q)
and
\mathcal{A}_{-f}(q)
are both dense in
U_{p}
; also they both have nonempty interior with respect to the topology of
M
, so there are two open and dense subsets in
U_{p}
, and they must intersect. The conclusion follows by Lemma
II.1
.
∎

Therefore, for the case where (
1
) is defined over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we make another assumption:

(A2)

For any open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is not dense in
U
.

This is equivalent to say that
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is an embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
.

The following lemma is crucial for our proof of the controllability criteria.

Lemma II.3

A maximal integral manifold corresponding to a codimension-
1
involutive distribution satisfying
(A2)
separates
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into at least two connected components.

Proof:

According to
[
12
]
, consider an immersion
f:M\rightarrow N
, where
M
and
N
are edgeless manifolds of dimensions
n-1
and
n
, respectively, if the inverse images of compact sets are compact and
H_{1}(N;\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=0
, then
N\backslash f(M)
is not connected. In our context, since the codimension
1
integral manifolds satisfying
(A2)
are embeddings and
H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=0
, they satisfy these conditions and hence
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is not connected.
∎

We call a system (
1
) regular if its corresponding Lie algebra
G
satisfies both
(A1)
and
(A2)
. The key argument of the controllability criteria is based on the so-called shift of the vector fields of regular systems, defined as follows.

Definition II.4
(
[
8
]
)

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
\varDelta
a distribution on
M
. Given a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, by
X\in\varDelta
we mean that
X|_{x}\in\varDelta|_{x}
,
\forall x\in M
.

1.

Given
x,y\in M
and tangent vectors
\xi\in T_{x}M
,
\eta\in T_{y}M
, if
\exists g_{1},\cdots,g_{m}\in\varDelta
,
t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\geqslant 0
, s.t.

\displaystyle x=\varphi_{t_{m}}^{g_{m}}\cdots\varphi_{t_{1}}^{g_{1}}(y),~\xi=(\varphi_{t_{m}}^{g_{m}})_{*}\cdots(\varphi_{t_{1}}^{g_{1}})_{*}(\eta),

(5)

then
\xi
is called the
\varDelta
-shift of
\eta
from
y
, denoted by
\xi=\varDelta_{*}(y,x)(\eta)
.

2.

Given a subset
S\subset\mathfrak{X}(M)
, the
\varDelta
-shift of
S

is defined as

\displaystyle\varDelta_{*}S(x):=\{\varDelta_{*}(y,x)(S|_{y})|y\in\mathcal{I}_{\varDelta}(x)\}\subset T_{x}M.

(6)

3.

A vector field

is called
\varDelta
-invariant, if
\varDelta_{*}X(x)=X|_{x}

. This is equivalent to that

With the above notions, now we state the main theorem for codimension-
1

Theorem II.5

Consider the system (
1
) of codimension
1
satisfying
(A1)(A2)
. It is globally controllable if and only if

\displaystyle 0\in\Int\Con\{G_{*}f(x),G|_{x}\},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(7)

where
\Con
is the convex hull of vectors in
T_{x}M
.

Proof:

The sufficiency was proved in
[
8
, Theorem 6.1]
. Here we show the necessity.

Suppose the condition (
7
) is not satisfied, then
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists a non-vanishing
G
-invariant smooth vector field
\xi
on
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
such that
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f|_{x}\in G^{*}|_{x}
, where
G^{*}|_{x}
is the complement in
T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the connected component of
T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash G|_{x}
containing
\xi|_{x}
; which is denoted as
f\in G^{*}
for short.

Since according to Lemma
II.3
the whole space is separated by
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
, and
\xi
as a normal vector field actually gives an orientation of
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
, one can see that a trajectory of (
1
) starting from
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
cannot cross it in positive time, since
f\in G^{*}
means that the direction of
f
on
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is either zero or equal to the orientation normal vector field. So the system is not globally controllable.
∎

Remark II.6

The condition (
7
) aims to separate
\mathcal{A}_{f}(x)
and
\mathcal{A}_{-f}(x)
, the latter of which is the negative-time reachable set of (
1
).
By the Baker-Campell-Hausdorff formula, (

) is actually equivalent to

\displaystyle 0\in\Int\Con\{G|_{x},\mathrm{ad}_{G}f|_{x}\},~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(8)

Due to topological reasons, we cannot always find
n-1
non-vanishing smooth vector fields that pointwise generate a codimension-
1
distribution. But when this is achievable, the above criterion (

) can be simplified to a form that is easier to verify.

Corollary II.7

Consider a codimension-
1
system satisfying
(A1)(A2)
. If there exist

\tilde{g}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{g}_{n-1}
such that
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
G|_{x}=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{\tilde{g}_{1}|_{x},\cdots,\tilde{g}_{n-1}|_{x}\}
, construct the following criterion function

\displaystyle C(x):=\det(f|_{x},\tilde{g}_{1}|_{x},\cdots,\tilde{g}_{n-1}|_{x}),

(9)

then the system is globally controllable if and only if for
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\exists y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
, s.t.
C(y_{1})C(y_{2})<0
, i.e. the criterion function (

) changes its sign on each leaf.

The proof is straightforward by noticing that (
9
) aims to verify whether

gives an orientation of the submanifold
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
. This corollary this
corollary confirms partly the conjectures in
[
20
]
.

III
Conditions for Controllability: Codimension
k>1

Now consider systems of higher codimensions. As an analogue of
(A2)
, to ensure that integral manifolds are embeddings, we make the following assumption on the system (
1
).

(A2’)

\forall p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, given any
(n-k+1)
-dimensional cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{I}_{G}(p)
is not dense in it with respect to the

-dimensional Euclidean topology.

Remark III.1

In fact, one may change
(A2’)
to a weaker form: if for
\forall x
,
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is of finite depth
[
16
]
, then each of them is an embedding (while the converse is not always true).

We will show that Theorem
II.5
still holds for codimension-
2
systems under the assumption
(A2’)
, and the condition (
7
) is necessary and sufficient for global controllability of codimension
k
(

) systems under certain restrictions.

First, we define the supporting distribution of vector fields.

Definition III.2

Consider a codimension-
k
distribution
D
on a manifold
M
, denote its complement in
\mathfrak{X}(M)
by

, i.e.
D|_{x}\oplus D^{\perp}|_{x}=T_{x}M
,

. Given a
(k-1)
-dimensional distribution

, a set

is called

-supported along

is
D
-invariant and there exists a
D
-invariant non-vanishing smooth vector field
\xi\in H\backslash S
, such that
\forall x\in U
,
F|_{x}\subset S^{+}|_{x}
, where

is the complement in

of the connected component of
(D^{\perp}\backslash S)|_{x}
containing
\xi|_{x}
, and this is briefly denoted as

.

Since
\Con\{G_{*}f(x),G|_{x}\}
is an affine subspace in

, one can see that under the assumption
(A1)
any system (

) which does not satisfy (

) will allow a supporting distribution
S
along

. Further, we have the following statement on the necessity of (

Theorem III.3

Consider the system (
1
) of codimension

(A1)(A2’)
. If the condition (
7
) is not satisfied and for the supporting distribution

we have

\displaystyle f|_{x}\notin\Lie(S)|_{x},~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(10)

then the system is not globally controllable.

Proof:

If
f\notin\Lie(S)
, then
G\oplus S
is a codimension-
1
involutive distribution; therefore each of its maximal integral manifolds will separate
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into two components. By similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
II.5
, since

is
S
-supported along

, a trajectory starting from

will remain in one connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\mathcal{I}_{G\oplus S}(x)
and its boundary, preventing the system from being controllable.
∎

Since
1
-dimensional distributions are always involutive, we have the following corollary immediately.

Corollary III.4

For codimension-
2
systems satisfying
(A1)(A2’)
, the condition (

We give an example of codimension 3 to illustrate the necessity of the condition.

Example III.5

Consider a system (

) in

, where

\displaystyle g_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{6})

\displaystyle=(1,0,0,0,0,0)^{\mathrm{T}},

\displaystyle g_{2}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{6})

\displaystyle=(0,1,x_{1},0,0,0)^{\mathrm{T}},

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{6})

\displaystyle=(0,0,0,1,x_{1},x_{1}^{2})^{\mathrm{T}},

Remark III.6

Consider the control-affine system (

, we conjecture that the condition in theorem

Conjecture III.7

Consider (

. If (

Theorem III.8

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=f^{\sigma(t)}|_{x(t)}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}u^{i}(t)g_{i}^{\sigma(t)}|_{x(t)}

\displaystyle 0\in\Int\Con\{G_{*}F(x),G|_{x}\},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

1

, and when the system (

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the conditions for global controllability of affine systems with drifts. Under the assumptions of certain regularities, necessary and sufficient conditions have been proved for codimension
1
and
2
systems, and necessary conditions are given for specific systems of higher codimensions. Then, the motion planning problems for globally controllable drifting affine systems are investigated, improving the applicability of the HCM method.

The key point of the controllability condition is the separation property of foliations. The discussions can all be extended to systems on simply connected manifolds since Lemma
II.3
holds for any (edgeless) manifold
M
satisfying
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=0
. Further, if
M
is compact,
(A2)
can be replaced by that each leaf
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is of finite depth since this is equivalent to that each
\mathcal{I}_{G}(x)
is an embedded submanifold.

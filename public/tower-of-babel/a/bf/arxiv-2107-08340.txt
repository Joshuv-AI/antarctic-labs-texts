Abstract
. In order to construct solutions of the braid equation we consider bijective left non-degenerate set-theoretic type solutions, which correspond to regular q-cycle coalgebras. We obtain a partial classification of the different q-cycle coalgebra structures on the dual coalgebra of K ⁡ [ y ] / ⟨ y n ⟩ K[y]/\langle y^{n}\rangle , the truncated polynomial algebra. We obtain an interesting family of involutive q-cycle coalgebras which we call Standard Cycle Coalgebras. They are parameterized by free parameters { p 1 , … , p n − 1 } \{p_{1},...,p_{n-1}\} and in order to verify that they are compatible with the braid equation, we have to verify that certain differential operators ∂ j \partial^{j} on formal power series in two variables K ⁡ [ [ x , y ] ] K[[x,y]] satisfy the condition ( ∂ j G ) i = ( ∂ i G ) j (\partial^{j}G)_{i}=(\partial^{i}G)_{j} for all i, j, where G G is a formal power series associated to the given q-cycle coalgebra. It would be interesting to find out the relation of these operators with the operators given by Yang in the context with 2-dimensional quantum field theories, which was one of the origins of the Yang-Baxter equation.

1  Preliminaries

Let
C
be a cocommutative coalgebra and let
s\in\End_{\textbf{Coalg}}(C^{2})
, where
C^{2}
denotes the coalgebra
C\otimes C
. The maps

s_{1}\coloneqq(C\otimes\epsilon)\circ s
and
s_{2}\coloneqq(\epsilon\otimes C)\circ s
are the unique coalgebra morphisms such that

s=(s_{1}\otimes s_{2})\circ\Delta_{C^{2}}
. We will write
{}^{a}b\coloneqq s_{1}(a\otimes b)
and
a^{b}\coloneqq s_{2}(a\otimes b)
.

Definition 1.1
.

A coalgebra endomorphism
s
of
C^{2}
, is called
left non-degenerate
if there exists a map
\mathfrak{p}\colon C^{2}\to C
such that

(1.1)

a^{b_{(1)}}\cdot b_{(2)}=(a\cdot b_{(1)})^{b_{(2)}}=\epsilon(b)a,

where
a\cdot b\coloneqq\mathfrak{p}(a\otimes b)
. In the sequel we write
\mathfrak{d}(a\otimes b)=a\mathbin{:}b\coloneqq{}^{b\cdot a_{(1)}}a_{(2)}
. Note that
the map
\mathfrak{p}
is unique and that
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{d}
are coalgebra morphisms from
C^{2}
to
C
.

Definition 1.2
.

A
K
-linear map
s\colon C^{2}\to C^{2}
is a
solution of the braid equation
if

s_{12}\circ s_{23}\circ s_{12}=s_{23}\circ s_{12}\circ s_{23},

where
s_{12}\coloneqq s\otimes C
and
s_{23}\coloneqq C\otimes s
. If
s
is also a coalgebra endomorphism of
C^{2}
, then we say that
s
is
a
set-theoretic type
solution.

Definition 1.3
.

A triple
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq(C,\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{d})
, where
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{d}
are coalgebra morphisms from
C^{2}
to
C
,
is a
regular
q
-magma coalgebra over
C
if there exist coalgebra morphisms
a\otimes b\mapsto a^{b}
and
a\otimes b\mapsto a_{b}
, from
C^{2}
to
C
,
such that identities (
1.1
) are fulfilled, and

(1.2)

(a\mathbin{:}b_{(2)})_{b_{(1)}}=a_{b_{(2)}}\mathbin{:}b_{(1)}=\epsilon(b)a\quad\text{for all $a,b\in C$,}

where
a\cdot b\coloneqq\mathfrak{p}(a\otimes b)
and
a\mathbin{:}b\coloneqq\mathfrak{d}(a\otimes b)
. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
a
q
-cycle coalgebra
if and the following conditions hold for all
a,b,c\in C
:

1.

(a\cdot b_{(1)})\cdot(c\mathbin{:}b_{(2)})=(a\cdot c_{(2)})\cdot(b\cdot c_{(1)})
,

2.

(a\cdot b_{(1)})\mathbin{:}(c\cdot b_{(2)})=(a\mathbin{:}c_{(2)})\cdot(b\mathbin{:}c_{(1)})
,

3.

(a\mathbin{:}b_{(1)})\mathbin{:}(c\mathbin{:}b_{(2)})=(a\mathbin{:}c_{(2)})\mathbin{:}(b\cdot c_{(1)})
.

Remark 1.4
.

By
[

]
*Remarks 1.12 and 1.15 the maps
a\otimes b\mapsto a^{b}
and
a\otimes b\mapsto a_{b}

G_{p}(a\otimes b)\coloneqq a\cdot b_{(1)}\otimes b_{(2)}

If

Remark 3.23
.

It order to prove that

or equivalently, that

\partial^{j}F=\sum_{h=1}^{j}\left(F^{h}\right)_{j}\partial_{y}^{h}F,\quad\text{for all $j>0$.}

By Corollary

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{j}\left(F^{h}\right)_{j}\partial_{y}^{h}F

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{j}\left(F^{h}\right)_{j}\sum_{i\geq 1}\left(\overline{f}(y)^{i}\right)_{h}\tilde{\partial}_{y}^{i}F=\sum_{i\geq 1}\sum_{h=1}^{j}\left(\overline{f}(y)^{i}\right)_{h}\left(F^{h}\right)_{j}\tilde{\partial}_{y}^{i}F

Lemma 3.24

Proposition 3.25

We have

(3.29)

Set

Theorem 6.1

q

\mathfrak{p}_{11}^{1}=0
,
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{p}_{i0}^{1}=\delta_{1i}
and that
for all
j>0
,
\mathfrak{p}_{1j}^{1}=0
. Then

\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{1}=0,\quad\text{for all $i$.}

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{d}
, the braid equations reduce to (
5.1
). Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i0}^{1}=\delta_{1i}
,
when we consider the case
k=1
and
i,j>0
, the equality (
5.1
) reads

\sum_{a+b=j}\sum_{h=1}^{i}\mathfrak{p}_{ia}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{1b}^{1}\mathfrak{p}_{h1}^{1}=\sum_{h=1}^{i}\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{j}\mathfrak{p}_{j0}^{j}\mathfrak{p}_{hj}^{1}+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\mathfrak{p}_{i0}^{i}\mathfrak{p}_{j1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}_{il}^{1}.

By Proposition
1.15
we know that
\mathfrak{p}_{j1}^{j}=j\mathfrak{p}_{11}^{1}=0
, and by assumption
\mathfrak{p}_{1b}^{1}=\delta_{b0}
,
consequently this equality reduces to

\sum_{h=2}^{i}\mathfrak{p}_{ij}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{h1}^{1}=\sum_{h=2}^{i-1}\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{hj}^{1}+\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\mathfrak{p}_{j1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}_{il}^{1}.

By (
1.5
), we have

\mathfrak{p}_{ij}^{i}=\sum_{j_{1}+\dots+j_{i}=j}\prod_{s=1}^{i}\mathfrak{p}_{1,j_{s}}^{1}=0,

since
j>0
. Hence we arrive at

(7.6)

\sum_{h=2}^{i-1}\mathfrak{p}_{ij}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{h1}^{1}=\sum_{h=2}^{i-1}\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{hj}^{1}+\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\mathfrak{p}_{j1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}_{il}^{1}.

Now we prove that
\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{1}=0
by induction on
i
. For
i=1
this is true by assumption.
Let
t\geq 1
and assume that
\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{1}=0
for all
i\leq t
. Then
\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{h}=0
for
i-t<h
. In fact, by (
1.5
),

\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\dots+i_{h}=i\\
j_{1}+\dots+j_{h}=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{s=1}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{i_{s},j_{s}}^{1}=0,

since for some
s
we have
j_{s}=1
, and so
\mathfrak{p}_{i_{s},j_{s}}^{1}=\mathfrak{p}_{i_{s},1}^{1}=0
, because
1\leq i_{s}\leq i-h+1\leq t

(Note that
i-t\leq h-1
). So the equality (
7.6
) reads

\sum_{h=t+1}^{i-1}\mathfrak{p}_{ij}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{h1}^{1}=\sum_{h=2}^{i-t}\mathfrak{p}_{i1}^{h}\mathfrak{p}_{hj}^{1}+\sum_{l=1}^{j-t}\mathfrak{p}_{j1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}_{il}^{1}.

For
i=j=t+1
, only the second term of the right hand side survives, and we have

0=\sum_{l=1}^{j-t}\mathfrak{p}_{j1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}_{il}^{1}=\mathfrak{p}_{t+1,1}^{1}\mathfrak{p}_{t+1,1}^{1},

hence
\mathfrak{p}_{t+1,1}^{1}=0
, which completes the inductive step and concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

A rather surprising fact is that in order to have a
q
-cycle coalgebra that is equivalent to a
\scc

\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{d}

Example

-cycle coalgebra satisfying a) and c), but not b); and Example

gives a
q
-cycle coalgebra satisfying b) and c), but not a). Thus the three conditions are necessary.

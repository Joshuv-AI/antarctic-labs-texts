Efficient generation, unimodular element in a geometric subring of a polynomial ring
Sourjya Banerjee, Chandan Bhaumik and Husney Parvez Sarwar

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 13C10; Secondary 19A15

Abstract.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of dimension
d
. First, we define the “geometric subring”
A
of a polynomial ring
R[T]
of dimension
d+1
(the definition of geometric subring is more general, see (
1.2
)).
Then we prove that every locally complete intersection ideal of height
d+1
is a complete intersection ideal. Thus improving the general bound of Mohan Kumar
[
16
]
for an arbitrary ring of dimension
d+1
. Afterward, we deduce that
every finitely generated projective
A
-module of rank
d+1
splits off a free summand of rank one. This improves the general bound of Serre
[
26
]
for an arbitrary ring. Finally, applications are given to a set-theoretic generation of an ideal in the geometric ring
A
and its polynomial extension
A[X]
.

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of (Krull) dimension
d
. A classical
result of Serre
[
26
]
asserts that every finitely generated projective
R
-module
P
of rank
d
splits off a free summand of rank one. In general, it is the best possible result as it is evidenced by the tangent bundle over the real algebraic sphere. Therefore if the
rank(P)\leq d
, then the question of
P
to splits off a free summand, is subtle and critical. In the case
rank(P)=\text{\rm dim}(A)
, where
A
is a smooth affine algebra over an algebraically closed field, there is a
well-developed obstruction theory which is due to Murthy
[
20
, Theorem 3.7]
. One of the
crucial steps of Murthy
[
20
]
is the following beautiful theorem of Mohan Kumar
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be an affine algebra over an algebraically closed field of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
P
be a finitely generated projective
A
-module of rank
d
and
\phi\in{\rm Hom}_{A}(P,A)
such that the image ideal
\phi(P)
has height
d
. Then,
P
splits off a free summand of rank one if and only if
\phi(P)
is generated by
d
elements.

Now let us define the efficient generation of an ideal
I
in a ring
R

which has an apparent connection with the complete intersection ideal (see Remark
3.4
).
The ideal
I
is called efficiently generated if
\mu(I/I^{2})=\mu(I)
, where
\mu(*)
is the minimum number of generators of
*
as a
R/I
(respectively
R
) module. In
[
15
]
, Mohan Kumar proved that
\mu(I/I^{2})\leq\mu(I)\leq\mu(I/I^{2})+1
. Moreover, in
[
16
]
, it was shown by him that
I
is efficiently generated whenever
\mu(I/I^{2})\geq d+1
. It is well known that there exists a real maximal ideal of a real
n
-sphere which is not generated by
n
-many elements. This shows that the previously mentioned bound for efficient generation of an ideal is the best possible in general. Therefore, the question of efficient generation of an ideal
I
becomes interesting whenever
\mu(I/I^{2})=\text{\rm dim}(R)
. In the polynomial ring
R[T]
, due to the results of Sathaye
[
25
]
(for affine domains over infinite fields) and Mohan Kumar
[
16
]
, we know that any ideal
K\subset R[T]
with the property
\mu(K/K^{2})=d+1
is efficiently generated provided
\rm ht(K)\geq 1
. This was one of the crucial steps in their solutions of Eisenbud-Evans conjecture on the number of generators of a finite module.

Before we state our results on efficient generation, we offer the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
f\in R[T]
be a non-zero divisor.
A ring
A
is called a geometric
subring of
R[T,f^{n}]
if

(1)

A
is a Noetherian ring of dimension
d+1
such that
R\subset A\subset R[T,f^{n}]
;

(2)

there exists a non-zero divisor
s\in R
such that
A_{s}=R_{s}[T,f^{n}]
.

Remark 1.3
.

We call
A
a geometric subring because our major examples are Rees algebras
R[It]
and Rees-like algebras
R[It,t^{n}]
(
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
). These algebras appear naturally in algebraic geometry while blowing up a variety along a subvariety. Another class of examples are the Noethrian symbolic Rees algebras.

For the geometric subring
A
of
R[T,f^{n}]
, we have the following result
about the efficient generation of an ideal in
A
(for a proof, see Theorem
3.6
).
Let
A
be a ring as in (
1.2
) (need not be Noetherian). Let
I\subset A
be an ideal such that
\rm ht(I)\geq 2
and
\mu(I)<\infty
. Moreover, assume that
I=<f_{0},\ldots,f_{d}>+I^{2}
. Then there exist
F_{i}\in I
such that
I=<F_{0},\ldots,F_{d}>
with
F_{i}-f_{i}\in I^{2}

(i=0,\ldots,d).

More generally, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Theorem
3.2
)
Let
A
be a ring as in (
1.2
). Let
L
be a finitely generated projective
A
-module of rank one. Let
I\subset A
be an ideal such that
\rm ht(I)=\text{\rm dim}(A)
. Further, assume that there is a surjection
\omega_{I}:(L/IL)\oplus(A/I)^{d}\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}
. Then there exists a surjection
\phi:L\oplus A\twoheadrightarrow I
such that
\phi\otimes A/I=\omega_{I}
. In particular, the
(d+1)
-th Euler class group
E^{d+1}(A,L)
is trivial.

The efficient generation problem was solved for polynomial ring
R[T]

by Mohan Kumar
[
16
]
, for Laurent polynomial ring

R[T,T^{-1}]
by Mandal

[
18
]
, and for overrings for polynomial ring by Das–Zinna
[
11
]
.

As an amusing application, we first derive the following result from Theorem
1.4
.
Every finitely generated projective
A
-module of rank
d+1
splits off a free summand of rank one (Theorem
3.5
).

In the case of Rees algebras, this result was proved by Rao–Sarwar

[
24
]
. In the case of symbolic Rees algebras (need not be Noetherian), it is proved in
[
6
]
.
As we noted in the first paragraph that there is an obstruction to splits off a free summand in the case
{\rm rank}=\text{\rm dim}
. For instance, see
[
20
]
and more generally see
[
3
]
,
[

]
for a well-developed obstruction theory
to determine when a finitely generated projective module splits off a free summand of rank one.
However, when the ring

is “nice”, then every finitely generated projective

-module

splits off a free summand of rank one even if

. Examples of such rings are polynomial rings

(cf.
[
21
]
,
[
5
]
), Laurent polynomial rings

(cf.

,
[

]
), monoid rings

, where

[

]
,
[

]
).

An ideal
I
in a commutative ring

is called set-theoretically generated by
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in R
if the radical of the ideal
I
, i.e.
\sqrt{I}=\sqrt{<f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}>}=:
the radical of the ideal generated by

. In section

2.
Recollection of basic definitions and results

2.1.
Notation.

We assume that all the rings are commutative with the multiplicative identity
1
and modules are finitely generated. Also, we assume that the projective modules have a constant rank function.
Let
R
be a ring, and let
I,\,J
be two ideals of
R
. For any element
x\in R
,
<I,x>
(respectively
<I,J>
) denotes
the ideal generated by
I
and
x

(respectively by
I
and
J
).
Let
\sqrt{I}
denote the radical of the ideal
I
, that is,

\sqrt{I}=\{x\in R\,|\,x^{n}\in I\}
. Let
\rm ht(I)
denote the height of the ideal
I
. Let
P
be a projective
R
-module. Then throughout the paper,
P^{*}
denote the
R
-module
{\rm Hom}_{R}(P,R)
.
Let
{\operatorname{\rm Aut}}(P)
denote the group of all automorphisms of
P
.

We begin with the following lemma, which has an origin from a lemma due to Mohan Kumar
[
15
, Lemma]
, slightly recast to suit our needs. For the sake of completeness, we give the proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring and
I
be an ideal of
R
. Let
J,K
be
ideals of
A
contained in
I
such that
K\subset I^{2}
and
I=J+K
. Then there exists
e\in K
such that
e(1-e)\in J
and
I=<J,e>.

Proof.

Note that
(I/J)^{2}=(I^{2}+J)/J=I/J
(as
K\subset I^{2}
and
I=J+K
). Hence
I/J
is an idempotent ideal in the Noetherian ring
R/J
. Let ‘bar’ denote going modulo the ideal
J
. Since the image of
K
maps sujectively onto
I/J
, we get
\overline{K}\overline{I}=\overline{I}
. By Nakayama lemma there exists
e\in K
such that
(\overline{1}-\overline{e})\overline{I}=\overline{0}
. Therefore,
(1-e)I=J
, that is,
I+eI=J
. Thus going modulo
e
we get
I=J
, hence
J+<e>=I
. Since
e\in K\subset I
and
(1-e)I=J
, we get
e(1-e)\in J
.
∎

The following result, which is an application of Lemma
2.1
, is required to prove our main theorem.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Let
I,J\subset R
be two ideals. Suppose that there exists
e\in I^{2}
such that
I=<J,e>
. Moreover, assume that
e\in\sqrt{J}
. Then
J=I
.

Proof.

Because of the local-global principle, it is enough to prove that
J_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\rm Spec\,}(R)
. Since
J\subset I
, we have
V(I)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\rm Spec\,}(R):I\subset{\mathfrak{p}}\}\subset V(J)
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}\in V(J)
. Since
e\in\sqrt{J}
, we have
e\in{\mathfrak{p}}
. Therefore,
{\mathfrak{p}}\in V(I)
as
I=<J,e>
. Hence we have
V(I)=V(J)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\rm Spec\,}(R)
. If
{\mathfrak{p}}\not\in V(I)=V(J)
, then
J_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}.
Now assume that
{\mathfrak{p}}\in V(I)
. In the local ring
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
, we have
I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=<J,e>A_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=J_{{\mathfrak{p}}}+I^{2}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
as
e\in I^{2}
. Using Lemma
2.1
, there exists
s\in I^{2}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
such that
I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=<J,s>R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
s(1-s)\in J_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Now since
s\in I^{2}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is a local ring, the element
1-s
is a unit in
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Hence we have
s\in J_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Therefore, we get
I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=J_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following result is a consequence of a theorem by Eisenbud-Evans
[
12
]
. One can find a proof of the same in
[
3
, Corollary 2.8]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
P
be a projective
R
-module of rank
n
. Let
(\alpha,a)\in(P^{*}\oplus R)
. Then there exists an element
\beta\in P^{*}
such that
\rm ht(I_{a})\geq n
,
where
I=(\alpha+a\beta)(P).

The “free version” of the next lemma is due to Mohan Kumar
[
16
, Corollary 3]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of dimension
d
. Let
L
be a projective
R
-module of rank one. Let
I\subset R
be an ideal. Suppose that we are given a surjection
\omega:(L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d}\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}
. Then there exists a surjection
\Omega:L\oplus R^{d}\twoheadrightarrow I
such that
\Omega\otimes R/I=\omega
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:L\oplus R^{d}\to I
be a lift (might not be surjective) of
\omega
. Let
J=\alpha(L\oplus R^{d})
. Then note that
I=J+I^{2}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
there exists
e\in I^{2}
such that
I=<J,e>
and
e(1-e)\in J
. By using Theorem
2.3
, there exists
\beta\in(L\oplus R^{d})^{*}
such that
\rm ht(J^{\prime})_{e}\geq d+1>\text{\rm dim}(R)
, where
J^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}(L\oplus R^{d})
and
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha+e\beta
. Observe that, we have
I=<J,e>=<J^{\prime},e>
. Since
\text{\rm dim}(R)=d
, we get
J^{\prime}_{e}=R_{e}
. This implies that
e\in\sqrt{J^{\prime}}
. Therefore, using Lemma
2.2
we get
I=J^{\prime}
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.2.
A brief introduction to the Euler class group

The purpose of this part is to briefly recall some of the basic definitions and terminologies of the Euler class theory from
[
4
]
.
Before going to the Euler class theory, we recall the following definitions.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
R
be a ring and
P
a projective
R
-module.
An element
p\in P
is called a unimodular element of
P
if there exists a
R
-linear map
\phi:P\to R
such that
\phi(p)=1
. Observe that,
P
has a unimodular element if and only if
P
splits off a free summand of rank one.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
R
be a ring. Let
P
be a projective
R
-module such that either
P
or
P^{*}
has a unimodular element. We choose
\phi\in P^{*}(={\rm Hom}_{R}(P,R))
and
p\in P
such that
\phi(p)=0
. We define an endomorphism
\phi_{p}
as the composite
\phi_{p}:P\to R\to P
, where
R\to P
is the map sending
1\to p.
Then by a transvection we mean an automorphism of
P
, of the form
1+\phi_{p}
, where either
\phi\in\mbox{\rm Um\,}(P^{*})
or
p\in\mbox{\rm Um\,}(P)
. Let
{\operatorname{\rm E}}(P)
denote the subgroup of
{\operatorname{\rm Aut}}(P)
generated by all transvections.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
I\subset R
be an ideal such that
\mu(I/I^{2})=\rm ht(I)=d
. Let
L
be a rank one projective
R
-module. Let
\alpha,\beta:(L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}
be two surjections. We say
\alpha
and
\beta
are related if there
exists
\sigma\in{\operatorname{\rm E}}((L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1})
such that
\alpha\sigma=\beta
. This defines an equivalence relation on the set of all surjections
(L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}
. Let
[\alpha]
denote the
equivalence class of
\alpha
. We will call
[\alpha]
a “local orientation” of
I
with respect to
L
. With abuse of notation sometimes we will call
\alpha
a local orientation of
I
with respect to
L
instead of
[\alpha]
. The local orientation
[\alpha]
is said to be a “global orientation” of
I
with respect to
L
, if there exists a surjective map
\Gamma:L\oplus R^{n-1}\twoheadrightarrow I
such that
\Gamma\otimes R/I=\alpha
. In this situation, we call
\Gamma
a surjective lift of
\alpha
. Notice that, since the canonical map
{\operatorname{\rm E}}(L\oplus R^{d-1})\twoheadrightarrow{\operatorname{\rm E}}((L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1})
is surjective, if
\alpha
has a surjective lift, then so has any
\beta
equivalent to
\alpha
. Whenever
L\cong R
, we will say a local orientation (respectively global orientation) of
I
instead of a local orientation (respectively global orientation) of
I
with respect to
R
.

Let
G
be the free Abelian group on the set
B
of pairs
(I,\omega_{J})
, where:

(1)

I\subset R
is an ideal such that
\mu(I/I^{2})=\rm ht(I)=d
;

(2)

{\rm Spec\,}(R/I)
is connected;

(3)

\omega_{I}:(L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}

is an equivalence class of local orientations of
I
with respect to
L
.

Let
H
be the subgroup of
G
generated by the set
S
of pairs
(J,\omega_{J})
, where
\omega_{J}
is an equivalence class of global orientations of
J
with respect to
L
. Then the quotient group
G/H
is the “
d
-th Euler class group” of
R
with respect to
L
, denoted as
E^{d}(R,L)
. Whenever
L\cong R
, we will write
E^{d}(R)
instead of
E^{d}(R,R)
.

Let
P
be a projective
R
-module of rank
d
with determinant
L
. Let
\chi:\wedge^{d}(L\oplus R^{d-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\wedge^{d}(P)
be an isomorphism. Let
\lambda:P\twoheadrightarrow I
be a surjection, where
I
is an ideal of
R
of height

d
. Let ‘bar’ denote going modulo
I
. We obtain an induced surjection
\lambda\otimes(R/I):P/IP\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}
. Note that, since
P
has determinant
L
and

\text{\rm dim}(R/I)=0
, we have
P/IP\cong(L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1}
. We choose an isomorphism
\phi:(L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}P/IP
, such that
\wedge^{d}\phi=\chi\otimes R/I
. Let
\omega_{I}
be the surjection
(\lambda\otimes R/I)\circ\phi:(L/IL)\oplus(R/I)^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}
. We say that
(I,\omega_{I})
is an “Euler cycle” induced by the triplet

(P,\lambda,\chi)
.

Whenever the Euler cycle is independent of the choice of
\lambda
, that is, if
(J,\omega_{J})
is another Euler cycle induced by a triplet
(P,\lambda^{\prime},\chi)
, for some
\lambda^{\prime}\in P^{*}
, then
(I,\omega_{I})=(J,\omega_{J})
in
E^{d}(R,L)
, in this situation we shall call the class of
(I,\omega_{I})
in the Euler class group
E^{d}(R,L)
as the “Euler class” of the pair
(P,\chi)
, denoted as
e(P,\chi)
. It was proved in
[
4
]
, that whenever the ring contains an infinite field and either
R
is smooth or
\frac{1}{d!}\in R
, then the Euler class of such a pair
(P,\chi)
is well defined.

The following theorem is due to Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
4
, Theorem 4.2]
. This has been used crucially and frequently in the course of proving our main theorems.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
L
be a projective
R
-module of rank one. Let
(I,\omega_{I})\in E^{d}(R,L)
. Then
(I,\omega_{I})=0
in
E^{d}(R,L)
if and only if
\omega_{I}
is a global orientation of
I
with respect to
L
.

3.
Efficient generation and triviality of the Euler class group

The following lemma is a modification of a result due to Bhatwadekar-Sridharan
[
4
, Lemma 5.6]
, and it is crucial to our main theorem. For the proof, we shall follow their arguments with some necessary alterations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
L
be a projective
R
-module of rank one. Let
(I,\omega_{I})\in E^{d}(R,L)
such that
(I,\omega_{I})\neq 0
in
E^{d}(R,L)
. Further, assume that there exists a non-zero divisor
s\in R
such that the following hold:

(1)

\text{\rm dim}(R_{s})=d
;

(2)

\rm ht(I_{s})=d
;

(3)

(I,\omega_{I})_{s}=0
in
E^{d}(R_{s},L_{s})
, where
(I,\omega_{I})_{s}:=(I_{s},\omega_{I}\otimes(R/I)_{s})
.

Then there exists
(J,\omega_{J})\in E^{d}(R,L)
such that
(I,\omega_{I})=(J,\omega_{J})
in
E^{d}(R,L)
and
s\in\sqrt{J}
.

Proof.

We define
B:=\frac{R}{sR\cap I^{2}}
. Let
`bar^{\prime}
denote going modulo the ideal
sR\cap I^{2}
. Since
s
is a non-zero divisor, we have
\text{\rm dim}(B)\leq d-1
.
Therefore, in the ring
B
, we have

\overline{\omega_{I}}:(\overline{L}/\overline{I}\overline{L})\oplus(B^{d-1})\twoheadrightarrow\overline{I}/\overline{I}^{2}.

Using Lemma
2.4
, we get a surjective map
\overline{\Gamma_{I}}:\overline{L}\oplus B^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{I}
such that
\overline{\Gamma_{I}}\otimes B/\overline{I}=\overline{\omega}_{I}
. Let
\Gamma_{I}:L\oplus R^{d-1}\to I
be a lift of
\overline{\Gamma_{I}}
which a priory need not be surjective. Then we get
I=J_{\Gamma_{I}}+I^{2}\cap sR
, where
J_{\Gamma_{I}}=\Gamma_{I}(L\oplus R^{d-1})
. Using Lemma
2.1
, there exits
e\in I^{2}\cap sR
such that
I=<J_{\Gamma_{I}},e>
, and
e(1-e)\in J_{\Gamma_{I}}
. Moreover, using Theorem
2.3
replacing
{\Gamma_{I}}
by
{\Gamma_{I}+e\Gamma_{I}^{\prime}}
, for some suitably chosen
\Gamma_{I}^{\prime}\in(L\oplus R^{d-1})^{*}
, we have
\rm ht(({J_{\Gamma_{I}}})_{e})\geq d
, that is,
{(J_{\Gamma_{I}}})_{e}=R_{e}
or
\rm ht(({J_{\Gamma_{I}}})_{e})=d
. If
{(J_{\Gamma_{I}}})_{e}=R_{e}
, then
e\in\sqrt{{(J_{\Gamma_{I}}})}
. Hence using Lemma
2.2
, we have
J_{\Gamma_{I}}=I
. This gives us
\Gamma_{I}:L\oplus R^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow I
is a surjective lift of
\omega_{I}
. Hence using Theorem
2.7
we have
(I,\omega_{I})=0
in
E^{d}(R,L)
which is a contradiction.

Therefore, we can assume that
\rm ht((J_{\Gamma_{I}})_{e})=d
. Let
I_{1}=<J_{\Gamma_{I}},1-e>
. Then note that we have the following:

(a)

\Gamma_{I}:L\oplus R^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow I\cap I_{1}=J_{\Gamma_{I}}
is a surjection;

(b)

I_{1}+eR=I_{1}+sR=I_{1}+I=R
;

(c)

\rm ht(I_{1})=d
.

Since
I_{1}+I=R
, using Chinese remainder theorem,
we have
R/(I\cap I_{1})\cong R/I\oplus R/I_{1}
. Hence

\Gamma_{I}
induces local orientations of
I
and
I_{1}
with respect to
L
. Since
\Gamma_{I}
is a lift of
\omega_{I}
, any local orientation of
I
with respect to
L
induced by
\Gamma_{I}
, matches with
(I,\omega_{I})
in
E^{d}(R,L).
Let
\omega_{1}^{\prime}
be a local orientation of
I_{1}
with respect to
L
induced by
\Gamma_{I}
. Then by (a) we have

(3.1)

(I,\omega_{I})+(I_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime})=0.

As
I_{1}+sR=R
and
\rm ht(I_{1})=d
, we get
\rm ht((I_{1})_{s})=d
. These give us the class of
({I_{1}},\omega_{1}^{\prime})_{s}
is in
E^{d}(R_{s},L_{s})
. Since we have
(I,\omega_{I})_{s}=0
in
E^{d}(R_{s},L_{s})
, from
3.1
, we have
({I_{1}},\omega_{1}^{\prime})_{s}=0
in
E^{d}(R_{s},L_{s})
. Therefore, using Theorem
2.7
, we obtain a surjective lift

\tau^{\prime}:L_{s}\oplus{R_{s}}^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow(I_{1})_{s}

of
\omega_{1}^{\prime}\otimes(R/I_{1})_{s}
.

Since
L
is finitely generated, there exists an integer
k\geq 1
such that
(s^{2k}\tau^{\prime})(L_{s}\oplus{R_{s}}^{d-1})\subset I_{1}
. Let
\tau=(s^{2k}\tau^{\prime})\circ i:L\oplus R^{d-1}\to I_{1}
, where
i:L\oplus R^{d-1}\to L_{s}\oplus{R_{s}}^{d-1}
is the canonical map induced by the localization map. Since
s
is a unit modulo
I_{1}
, the map
\tau\otimes R/I_{1}:(L/I_{1}L)\oplus(R/I_{1})^{d-1}\twoheadrightarrow I_{1}/I_{1}^{2}
is surjective. That is,
\tau\otimes R/I_{1}
is a local orientation of
I_{1}
with respect to
L
. Let
\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\tau\otimes R/I_{1}
. By the construction of
\tau
we have
(I_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime})=(I_{1},u^{2}\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})
, for some
u\in(R/I_{1})^{*}
(here
u
will be some power of
s
). Using
[
4
, Lemma 5.4]
we get
(I_{1},u^{2}\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})=(I_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})
this implies that
(I_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime})=(I_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})
. Therefore, from
3.1
we get

(3.2)

(I,\omega_{1})+(I_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})=0.

Now adapting the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 5.6, paragraph 2]
, we get
(J,\omega_{J})\in E^{d}(R,L)
such that the followings hold:

(1)

(J)_{s}=R_{s}
;

(2)

I_{1}+J=R
;

(3)

(I_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})+(J,\omega_{J})=0
in
E^{d}(R,L)
.

Since
(J)_{s}=R_{s}
we have
s\in\sqrt{J}
. Now combining (
3.2
) and
(3)
, we have
(I,\omega_{I})=(J,\omega_{J})
in
E^{d}(R,L)
. This finishes the proof. ∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A
be a ring as in (
1.2
). Let
L
be a finitely generated projective
A
-module of rank one. Let
I\subset A
be an ideal such that
\rm ht(I)=\text{\rm dim}(A)=d+1
. Further, assume that there is a surjection
\omega_{I}:(L/IL)\oplus(A/I)^{d}\twoheadrightarrow I/I^{2}
. Then there exists a surjection
\phi:L\oplus A\twoheadrightarrow I
such that
\phi\otimes A/I=\omega_{I}
. In particular,
the
(d+1)
-th Euler class group
E^{d+1}(A,L)
is trivial.

Proof.

Let
S
be the multiplicatively closed subset of
R
consisting all non-zero divisors. Then it is easy to check that
S^{-1}(R)
is an Artinian ring and

S^{-1}A\cong S^{-1}R[T,f^{n}]
. To show that

S^{-1}L
is a free module, we can assume that
S^{-1}R[T,f^{n}]
is reduced. But then
S^{-1}R

is a product of fields, hence
S^{-1}R[T,f^{n}]

is a product of principal ideal domains.
So we conclude that
S^{-1}L
is a free
S^{-1}A
-module of rank one.

Since
L
is finitely generated, there exists
s_{1}\in S
such that
L_{s_{1}}
is a free
A_{s_{1}}
-module of rank one. Since
A
is a geometric ring, there exists a non-zero divisor
s_{2}\in R
such that
A_{s_{2}}=R_{s_{2}}[T,f^{n}]
. Let
s=s_{1}s_{2}
. Then note that
s\in R
is a non-zero divisor, such that
L_{s}\cong A_{s}
and
A_{s}=R_{s}[T,f^{n}]
. Now we prove the theorem in the following two steps.

Step 1.
In this step, we will show that we can assume that
s\in\sqrt{I}
.

Proof of the Step 1.
Note that
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s})\leq\text{\rm dim}(A)=d+1
. Therefore either
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s})=d+1
or
\leq d
. We shall handle these two cases separately.

Case 1.
Let us assume that
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s})=d+1
.

Recall that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the prime ideals of
A_{s}
with the prime ideals of the ring
A
, not containing
s
. Therefore, for any prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset A
,
s\not\in{\mathfrak{p}}
implies that
\rm ht({\mathfrak{p}}_{s})=\rm ht({\mathfrak{p}})
. Let
\sqrt{I}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
’s are prime ideals of the ring
A
. Since
\rm ht(I)=\rm ht(\sqrt{I})=d+1
and
\text{\rm dim}(A)=d+1
, we have
\rm ht({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})=d+1
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Hence we have either
\rm ht(I_{s})=d+1
or
I_{s}=A_{s}
. Now if
I_{s}=A_{s}
then
s\in\sqrt{I}
. So we have already achieved the claim of Step 1.

Therefore, we assume that
\rm ht(I_{s})=d+1
. Observe that,
A_{s}
is an overring of the polynomial ring
R[T]
. We have
\rm ht(I)=d+1
, and
\rm ht(I_{s})=d+1\geq 2
. Therefore, using
[
11
, Theorem 3.2 ]
, we have
(I_{s},\omega_{I}\otimes(A/I)_{s})=0
in
E^{d+1}(A_{s})
. Note that if
(I,\omega_{I})=0
in
E^{d+1}(A,L)
, then using Theorem
2.7
we get
\omega_{I}
is a global orientation of
I
with respect to
L
. Thus the theorem is proved. Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume that
(I,\omega_{I})\not=0
in
E^{d+1}(A,L)
. Hence by the previous reductions we may assume that the triplet
(A,(I,\omega_{I}),s)
satisfies all the hypotheses of Lemma
3.1
. Applying Lemma
3.1
, we get
(J,\omega_{J})\in E^{d+1}(A,L)
such that
(I,\omega_{I})=(J,\omega_{J})
and
s\in\sqrt{J}
. Note that to prove the theorem in view of Theorem
2.7
, it is enough to show that
(J,\omega_{J})=0
in
E^{d+1}(A,L)
. Therefore, replacing
(I,\omega_{I})
by
(J,\omega_{J})
we may assume that
s\in\sqrt{I}
. This completes the proof of Step 1 in the case whenever
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s})=d+1
.

Case 2.
Suppose that
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s})\leq d
.

Let
B=A/sA\cap I^{2}
. Let ‘bar’ denote going modulo the ideal
sA\cap I^{2}
. Since
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s})<d+1
, by Lemma
2.4
,
\overline{\omega_{I}}
is a global orientation of
\overline{I}
with respect to
\overline{L}
. Let
\Gamma:L\oplus A^{d}\to I
be a lift of
\overline{\omega_{I}}
. Then we have
I=K+I^{2}\cap sA
, where
K=\Gamma(L\oplus A^{d})
. Using Lemma
2.1
, there exists
e\in I^{2}\cap<s>
such that
I=<K,e>
, and
e(1-e)\in K
. Moreover, using Theorem
2.3
, we can make the assumption
\rm ht(K^{\prime}_{e})\geq d+1
, where
K^{\prime}=(\Gamma+e\lambda)(L\oplus A^{d})
, for some suitably chosen
\lambda\in(L\oplus A^{d})^{*}
. As we have
I=<K^{\prime},e>
and
e\in<s>
, these imply that
\rm ht(K^{\prime}_{s})\geq\rm ht(K^{\prime}_{e})\geq d+1
. Since
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s})<d+1
, we get
s\in\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\subset\sqrt{I}
. This finishes the proof of Step 1.

Step 2.
In this step, we show that
\omega_{I}
is a global orientation of
I
with respect to
L
.

Proof of Step 2.
Note that by Theorem
2.7
it is enough to show that
(I,\omega_{I})=0
in
E^{d+1}(A,L)
. Since
s
is a non-zero divisor, by Moving lemma
[
4
, Corollary 2.14]
we get an ideal
I^{\prime}\subset A
of height
d+1
such that
I^{\prime}+I=I^{\prime}+<s>=A
and a surjection
\beta:L\oplus A^{d}\twoheadrightarrow I\cap I^{\prime}
, with
\beta\otimes A/I=\omega_{I}
. Since
I^{\prime}+<s>=A
the map
\omega_{1}:=\beta\otimes A_{1+sA}:L_{1+sA}\oplus(A_{1+sA})^{d}\twoheadrightarrow I_{1+sA}\cap I^{\prime}_{1+sA}=I_{1+sA}
is surjective. Observe that
\omega_{1}\otimes(A_{1+sA}/I_{1+sA})=(\beta\otimes A/I)\otimes(A_{1+sA}/I_{1+sA})=\omega_{I}\otimes(A_{1+sA}/I_{1+sA})
.

In the ring
A_{s}
we have
I_{s}=A_{s}
, as
s\in\sqrt{I}
. Let
\omega_{2}:(A_{s})^{d+1}\twoheadrightarrow I_{s}
, which sends
e_{1}
to
1
and
e_{i}
to
0
for all
i>1
. Since
I_{s}=A_{s}
, the equality
\omega_{2}\otimes(A_{s}/I_{s})=\omega_{I}\otimes A_{s}
holds trivially. Consider the following fiber product diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L\oplus A^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A^{d+1})_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L\oplus A^{d})_{1+sA}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L\oplus A^{d})_{s(1+sA)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A^{d+1})_{s(1+sA)}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{s(1+sA)}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Note that
A_{s(1+sA)}=(R_{s}[T,f^{n}])_{1+sA}=S^{\prime-1}R^{\prime}[T]
, where
R^{\prime}=R_{s(1+sR)}
and
S^{\prime}
is some multiplicatively closed subset of non-zero divisors of
R^{\prime}[T]
. Therefore,
A_{s(1+sA)}
is an overring of the polynomial ring
R^{\prime}[T]
. Since
\text{\rm dim}(R^{\prime})\leq d-1
, we have
\text{\rm dim}(A_{s(1+sA)})\leq d
. Also observe that since
L_{s(1+sA)}\cong A_{s(1+sA)}
, we get
(\omega_{1})_{s},(\omega_{2})_{1+sA}\in\mbox{\rm Um\,}_{d+1}(A_{s(1+sA)})
. Hence by a theorem of Rao
[
23
, Theorem 5.1 (I)]
, there exists
\epsilon\in E_{d+1}(A_{s(1+sA)})
such that

. Since elementary matrices are homotopic to identity, using Quillen’s splitting theorem

the matrix
\epsilon
splits. Using the universal property of fiber product, we get a unique map

such that

and

. To prove that

is a surjective lift of

note that it is enough to check locally. Let
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset A
be a maximal ideal. Then
{\mathfrak{m}}
is either co-maximal with

. Therefore, in any case, in the ring

\omega_{1}
or
\omega_{2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

Let

be a commutative Noetherian ring of dimension

Continuing with the same notations as in Theorem

of rank

splits off a free summand of rank one. Note that here we are assuming the ring is Noetherian. For Rees algebras or extended Rees algebras this has been proved in

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A
be as in Theorem
3.2
.
Let
P
be a projective

-module such that

. Then

has a unimodular element.

Proof.

We deduce the theorem from Theorem
3.2
and Subtraction principle
[
4
, Corollary 3.4]
.
For this, let
L:=\wedge^{d+1}P
and
Q:=L\oplus A^{d-1}
.
Then
\wedge^{d}Q=L
.
Let
\chi:\wedge^{d+1}P\cong\wedge^{d+1}(Q\oplus A)(\cong L)
be an isomorphism. Let
(I,\omega_{I})\in E^{d+1}(A,L)
be a Euler cycle induced by the trilet

for some suitably chosen

. This means that there exists an isomorphism

, such that

we have a surjective lift
\beta:{L\oplus A^{d}}\twoheadrightarrow I
such that

. That is,
\beta\otimes A/I=(\lambda\otimes A/I)\circ\phi
. Now from
[
4
, Corollary 3.4]
, it follows that

has a unimodular element.
∎

.

be an ideal such that

. Moreover, any set of

S^{-1}(A[X])
, it is enough to lift
S^{-1}I=S^{-1}<F_{1},\ldots,F_{d+1}>+S^{-1}(I^{2})
to a set of generators of
S^{-1}I
. Observe that we have the following:

(1)

\text{\rm dim}(B)=\text{\rm dim}(R)=d
;

(2)

B\subset S^{-1}A[X]\subset B[T,f^{n}]
;

(3)

(S^{-1}A[X])_{s}=S^{-1}(A_{s}[X])=B_{s}[T,f^{n}]
;

(4)

S^{-1}I\subset S^{-1}A[X]
is an ideal such that
\rm ht(S^{-1}I)\geq 3
.

In particular,
S^{-1}A[X]
is a geometric subring of
B[T,f^{n}]
. Therefore, applying Theorem
3.6
we can lift
S^{-1}I=S^{-1}<F_{1},\ldots,F_{d+1}>+S^{-1}I^{2}
to a set of generators of
S^{-1}I
. This completes the proof in Case 1.

Case 2.
In this case we assume that
R
is an affine algebra over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Again as before, following the arguments given in Remark
3.3
, without loss of generality we may assume that
A
is a finitely generated
R-
algebra. In particular, this will imply
A
is an affine
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}-
algebra. Therefore, to prove the theorem, in view of
[
1
, Theorem 9.1]
it is enough to find a lift of
IA(X)=<f_{1},...,f_{d+1}>A(X)+I^{2}A(X)
to a set of generators of
IA(X)
. This can be achieved following the same arguments given in Case 1.
∎

As customary to the Euler class theoretic literature, we get the following result as a corollary to the previous theorem.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
R
and
A
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Moreover, assume that
A
is finitely generated
R
-algebra. Let
P
be a projective
A[X]
-module of rank
d+1
with a trivial determinant. Then
P
has a unimodular element.

Proof.

From Theorem
5.1
we get
E^{d+1}(A[X])=0
. Therefore, the result follows from
[
8
, Corollary 4.11]
and
[
1
, Theorem 4.11]
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Whenever
\mathbb{Q}\subset R
, altering the proof of Theorem
5.1
one can establish the fact that
E^{d+1}(A[X],L)
is trivial, where
L
is a projective
A[X]-
module of rank one. Therefore, using
[
10
]
, one can remove the hypothesis that
P
has a trivial determinant from Corollary
5.2
.

We now move towards proving a similar result to Theorem
4.2
, in the polynomial extensions. As a preparation, we need the following theorem due to Ferrand-Szpiro
[
27
]
(or see
[
19
, Theorem 6.1.3, page no 75]
).

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Let
I
be a locally complete intersection ideal of
R
of height
n\geq 2
with
\text{\rm dim}(R/I)\leq 1
. Then there is a locally complete intersection ideal
J\subset R
of height
n
such that

(1)

\sqrt{I}=\sqrt{J}
;

(2)

J/J^{2}
is a free
R/J-
module of rank
n
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
R
and
A
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Let
I\subset A[X]
be a local complete intersection ideal of height
\geq 3
such that
\text{\rm dim}(A[X]/I)\leq 1
and
\mu(I)<\infty
. Then
I
is set theoretically generated by
d+1
or lesser elements.

Proof.

Using Remark
3.3
, without loss of generality we may assume that
A
is finitely generated
R
-algebra. Let
\rm ht(I)=h
. It follows from the Theorem
5.4
that there exists an ideal
J\subset A[X]
such that the followings hold:

(1)

\sqrt{I}=\sqrt{J}
;

(2)

J/J^{2}

is a free
A[X]/J
-module of rank
h
.

If
h\leq d
, then from Lemma
2.1

J
is generated by at-most
d+1
elements. Therefore, we may assume that
h=d+1
. Let

J=<f_{1},...,f_{d+1}>+J^{2}
. Applying Theorem
5.1
, we see that
J
is generated by
d+1

elements. Therefore, from
(1)
, it follows that
I
is set theoretically generated by
d+1
elements. This completes the proof.
∎

The Galois coaction on ϕ 4 \phi^{4} periods
Erik Panzer, Oliver Schnetz

Abstract
. We report on calculations of Feynman periods of primitive log-divergent ϕ 4 \phi^{4} graphs up to eleven loops. The structure of ϕ 4 \phi^{4} periods is described by a series of conjectures. In particular, we discuss the possibility that ϕ 4 \phi^{4} periods are a comodule under the Galois coaction. Finally, we compare the results with the periods of primitive log-divergent non - ϕ 4 \phi^{4} graphs up to eight loops and find remarkable differences to ϕ 4 \phi^{4} periods. Explicit results for all periods we could compute are provided in ancillary files.

2.1.
Iterated integrals

In
[
31
]
Chen developed a theory of iterated path integration on general manifolds.
Here, we need only the elementary one-dimensional case of a punctured sphere
{\mathbbm{C}}\setminus\Sigma
for some finite set
\Sigma\subset{\mathbbm{C}}
.
Fix a weight
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, a path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathbbm{C}}\setminus\Sigma
from
a_{0}=\gamma(0)
to
a_{n+1}=\gamma(1)
and differential forms
\omega_{i}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{d}z/(z-a_{i})
with
a_{i}\in\Sigma
for
i\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,n\right\}
.
Then, the iterated integral of the word
a_{n}\!\ldots a_{1}
(considering
\Sigma
as an alphabet) along
\gamma
is defined by

I(a_{n+1},a_{n}\ldots a_{1},a_{0})_{\gamma}=\int_{1>t_{n}>\cdots>t_{1}>0}\gamma^{\ast}\omega_{n}(t_{n})\wedge\ldots\wedge\gamma^{\ast}\omega_{1}(t_{1}),

(2.1)

where the integration simplex is endowed with the standard orientation and
\gamma^{\ast}\omega_{i}
is the pullback of
\omega_{i}
along
\gamma
.
Iterated path integrals have the following properties:

I0:

I(a_{1},a_{0})_{\gamma}=1
(by definition).

I1:

I(a_{n+1},w,a_{0})_{\gamma}
depends only on the homotopy class of
\gamma
.

I2:

I(a_{0},w,a_{0})_{\gamma}=0
for the constant path
\gamma(t)=a_{0}
and non-empty words
w
.

I3:

Path reversal: For the reversed path
\gamma^{-1}(t)=\gamma(1-t)
,

I(a_{n+1},a_{n}\ldots a_{1},a_{0})_{\gamma^{-1}}=(-1)^{n}I(a_{0},a_{1}\ldots a_{n},a_{n+1})_{\gamma}.

I4:

Path concatenation:
If
\gamma=\gamma_{2}\star\gamma_{1}
is the composition of (first)
\gamma_{1}
and (second)
\gamma_{2}
meeting at
x=\gamma_{1}(1)=\gamma_{2}(0)\in{\mathbbm{C}}\setminus\Sigma
, then

I(a_{n+1},a_{n}\ldots a_{1},a_{0})_{\gamma}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}I(a_{n+1},a_{n}\ldots a_{k+1},x)_{\gamma_{2}}I(x,a_{k}\ldots a_{1},a_{0})_{\gamma_{1}}.

I5:

Shuffle product:
For
n=r+s
let
\mathfrak{S}_{r,s}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the
(r,s)
-shuffles

\mathfrak{S}_{r,s}=\left\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\colon\sigma^{-1}(1)<\ldots<\sigma^{-1}(r)\ \text{and}\ \sigma^{-1}(r+1)<\ldots<\sigma^{-1}(n)\right\},

a subset of the group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of permutations of
\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
. Then,

I(a_{n+1},a_{n}\ldots a_{r+1},a_{0})_{\gamma}I(a_{n+1},a_{r}\ldots a_{1},a_{0})_{\gamma}=\sum_{\mathclap{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r,s}}}I(a_{n+1},a_{\sigma(n)}\ldots a_{\sigma(1)},a_{0})_{\gamma}.

I6:

Chain rule: Every Möbius transformation
f
maps a word
w
by pull-back of the differential forms
\omega_{i}
to a linear combination
f^{\ast}w
of words in the alphabet
f^{-1}(\Sigma\cup\left\{\infty\right\})\setminus\left\{\infty\right\}
. The iterated integral transforms as

I(f(a_{n+1}),w,f(a_{0}))_{f(\gamma)}=I(a_{n+1},f^{\ast}w,a_{0})_{\gamma},

where the iterated integral on the right-hand side is linearly extended to linear combination of words.

Although by definition one assumes
a_{0}
,
a_{n+1}\notin\Sigma
, it is possible by a limiting procedure to define iterated integrals for the singular cases
a_{0}
,
a_{n+1}\in\Sigma
.
See
[
48
]
for a detailed discussion of iterated integrals in this special setup.

We only consider straight paths and suppress the subscript
\gamma
.
As a special case we obtain the multiple polylogarithms (MPLs) from
[
39
]
in the form

(-1)^{r}I(z,\underbrace{0\ldots 01}_{n_{r}}\ldots\underbrace{0\ldots 01}_{n_{1}},0)=\Li_{n_{r},\ldots,n_{1}}(z)=\sum_{k_{r}>\cdots>k_{1}\geq 1}\frac{z^{k_{r}}}{k_{r}^{n_{r}}\!\cdots k_{1}^{n_{1}}}.

(2.2)

By (
1.6

\Sigma=\left\{0,1\right\}
are MZVs. In this article we also need MPLs at other roots of unity.
In particular, we consider the three-letter alphabet

One can define motivic iterated integrals

4.
Methods

For a long time it was not known how to calculate Feynman periods. Now we have several methods at hand which, when combined, suffice to compute all periods up to seven loops, most graphs with eight loops and several graphs with nine, ten, or eleven loops. The method has four building blocks:

(1)

parametric integration,

(2)

graphical functions,

(3)

generalized single-valued hyperlogarithms and

(4)

Parametric integration was developed by F. Brown

5.11.
Beyond multiple polylogarithms.

With our methods we can only compute periods which can be expressed as linear combinations of multiple polylogarithms with algebraic coefficients and arguments. These are periods of mixed Tate motives and enjoy extra structure as compared to periods in general
[
24
]
, including:

•

They have a well-defined grading by integer weights in our MZV-inspired counting (which equal half the weights from Hodge theory).

•

The coaction acts unipotently, saying that a Galois conjugate of
x
under the pro-unipotent part of the Galois group equals
x
plus lower weight periods. In other words, the right-hand factors in
\Delta^{\prime}x
have weights strictly less than the weight of
x
.

For general periods, the weight is merely a filtration and we should expect to see half-integer weights. Furthermore, a period can have several Galois conjugates of the same weight.

Not a single non mixed Tate
\phi^{4}
period has so far been computed, but they are known to occur starting from
8
loops in graphs with modular
c_{2}
-invariants
[
26
,
25
]
. Concretely, there are four
8
loop
\phi^{4}
periods with modular
c_{2}
-invariants (
P_{8,37}
,
P_{8,38}
,
P_{8,39}
and
P_{8,41}

[
27
]
), and for
P_{8,37}
it is known that the framing given by the period (
1.3
) is not of mixed Tate type
[
25
]
.

Furthermore, the non mixed Tate contribution of
P_{8,37}
has weight
12
. In fact, a non mixed Tate contribution to a period
P(G)
necessarily has weight below
2h_{G}-3
. It is not excluded that the other
8
-loop periods with modular

-invariant contribute non mixed Tate periods to

in even smaller weights.
This is the reason why we had to restrict our statements about
{\mathcal{W}}_{\leq 11}\mathcal{P}_{\phi^{4}}
in Section

In view of the possibility to find several weight

Indeterminate Jacobi operators
Christian Berg, Ryszard Szwarc

Abstract
We consider the Jacobi operator ( T , D ⁡ ( T ) ) (T,D(T)) associated with an indeterminate Hamburger moment problem, i.e., the operator in ℓ 2 \ell^{2} defined as the closure of the Jacobi matrix acting on the subspace of complex sequences with only finitely many non-zero terms. It is well-known that it is symmmetric with deficiency indices ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) . For a complex number z z let 𝔭 z , 𝔮 z \mathfrak{p}_{z},\mathfrak{q}_{z} denote the square summable sequences ( p n ​ ( z ) ) (p_{n}(z)) and ( q n ​ ( z ) ) (q_{n}(z)) corresponding to the orthonormal polynomials p n p_{n} and polynomials q n q_{n} of the second kind. We determine whether linear combinations of 𝔭 u , 𝔭 v , 𝔮 u , 𝔮 v \mathfrak{p}_{u},\mathfrak{p}_{v},\mathfrak{q}_{u},\mathfrak{q}_{v} for u , v ∈ ℂ u,v\in\mathbb{C} belong to D ⁡ ( T ) D(T) or to the domain of the self-adjoint extensions of T T in ℓ 2 \ell^{2} . The results depend on the four Nevanlinna functions of two variables associated with the moment problem. We also show that D ⁡ ( T ) D(T) is the common range of an explicitly constructed family of bounded operators on ℓ 2 \ell^{2} .

Introduction

We shall consider the Jacobi matrix
J
associated with a moment sequence
s=(s_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of the form

s_{n}=\int x^{n}\,d\mu(x),\quad n=0,1,\ldots,

(1)

\mathbb{R}

J=\begin{pmatrix}b_{0}&a_{0}&0&\ldots\\

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix},

xp_{n}(x)=a_{n}p_{n+1}(x)+b_{n}p_{n}(x)+a_{n-1}p_{n-1}(x),n\geq 0,\quad a_{-1}:=0.

Here
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is the sequence of orthonormal polynomials associated with
\mu
, hence satisfying

\int p_{n}(x)p_{m}(x)\,d\mu(x)=\delta_{n,m},

and
p_{n}
is a real polynomial of degree
n
with positive leading coefficient. In this paper we follow the terminology of
[
17

It is clear that the proportional measures

The Jacobi matrix acts as a symmetric operator in the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}
of square summable complex sequences. Its domain
\mathcal{F}
consists of the complex sequences
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with only finitely many non-zero terms, and the action is multiplication of the matrix

(Jc)_{n}:=a_{n-1}c_{n-1}+b_{n}c_{n}+a_{n}c_{n+1},\quad n\geq 0.

(3)

Denoting

the standard orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}

\mathcal{F}=span\{e_{n}|n\geq 0\}.

Definition 1.1
.

The Jacobi operator associated with

(T,D(T))
of the symmetric operator
(J,\mathcal{F})

It is a classical fact that

\mathfrak{q}_{u}+\beta\mathfrak{q}_{v}=(r_{u}+\beta r_{v})+(r_{u}^{+}+\beta r_{v}^{+})\mathfrak{p}_{z_{0}}+(r_{u}^{-}+\beta r_{v}^{-})\mathfrak{p}_{\overline{z_{0}}},

so
\mathfrak{q}_{u}+\beta\mathfrak{q}_{v}\in D(T)
if and only if

which by (

C(u,\overline{z_{0}})+\beta C(v,\overline{z_{0}})=C(u,z_{0})+\beta C(v,z_{0})=0.

(23)

The determinant of this linear system is

{\mathcal{D}}_{1}:=C(u,\overline{z_{0}})C(v,z_{0})-C(u,z_{0})C(v,\overline{z_{0}})

and using Lemma

with

x=\begin{pmatrix}A(u)\\
C(u)\end{pmatrix},\;y=\begin{pmatrix}B(\overline{z_{0}})\\
D(\overline{z_{0}})\end{pmatrix},\;z=\overline{y},\;w=\begin{pmatrix}A(v)\\
C(v)\end{pmatrix}

we get from Corollary
5.2
combined with (
5.2
), (
5.2
), (
5.2
)

{\mathcal{D}}_{1}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}A(u)&\;A(v)\\
C(u)&\;C(v)\end{array}\right|\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}B(\overline{z_{0}})&\;B(z_{0})\\
D(\overline{z_{0}})&\;D(z_{0})\end{array}\right|=A(u,v)D(\overline{z_{0}},z_{0}).

As in case (i) we see that

{\mathcal{D}}_{1}=0
iff
A(u,v)=0
.
Therefore, if
\beta
is a solution to (
23

A(u,v)=0
. Suppose next that

. To see that (

\beta

If

Furthermore, in the case

so finally

. The case

Proof of (iii)
: By (

\mathfrak{p}_{u}+\gamma\mathfrak{q}_{v}=(s_{u}+\gamma r_{v})+(s_{u}^{+}+\gamma r_{v}^{+})\mathfrak{p}_{z_{0}}+(s_{u}^{-}+\gamma r_{v}^{-})\mathfrak{p}_{\overline{z_{0}}},

The determinant of this linear system is

{\mathcal{D}}_{2}:=D(u,\overline{z_{0}})C(v,z_{0})-D(u,z_{0})C(v,\overline{z_{0}})

and using Lemma

with

x=\begin{pmatrix}B(u)\\
D(u)\end{pmatrix},\;y=\begin{pmatrix}B(\overline{z_{0}})\\
D(\overline{z_{0}})\end{pmatrix},\;z=\overline{y},\;w=\begin{pmatrix}A(v)\\
C(v)\end{pmatrix}

we get from Corollary
5.2
combined with (

), (
5.2
), (
5.2
)

{\mathcal{D}}_{2}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}B(u)&\;A(v)\\
D(u)&\;C(v)\end{array}\right|\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}B(\overline{z_{0}})&\;B(z_{0})\\
D(\overline{z_{0}})&\;D(z_{0})\end{array}\right|=B(u,v)D(\overline{z_{0}},z_{0}).

As in case (i) we see that

.
Therefore, if

. Suppose next that

The case (ii) can be deduced from case (i) by using the observation that

3
Self-adjoint extensions of the Jacobi operator

As before
(s_{n})
is an indeterminate moment sequence with
s_{0}=1
. The corresponding Jacobi operator
(T,D(T))
has deficiency indices
(1,1)
and the self-adjoint extensions in

\ell^{2}
can be parametrized as the operators
T_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
with domain

D(T_{t})=D(T)\oplus\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{q}_{0}+t\mathfrak{p}_{0})\;\mbox{for}\;t\in\mathbb{R},\quad D(T_{\infty})=D(T)\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{p}_{0}

(25)

and defined by the restriction of
T^{*}
to the domain, cf.
[
17
, Theorem 6.23]
.
We recall that
\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathfrak{q}_{0}
are defined in (
4
).

The purpose of this section is to give some results about the domains
D(T_{t})
of the self-adjoint operators
T_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
we define the solutions to the moment problem

\mu_{t}(\cdot):=\langle E_{t}(\cdot)e_{0},e_{0}\rangle,

(26)

where
E_{t}(\cdot)
is the spectral measure of the self-adjoint operator
T_{t}
.

The measures
\mu_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
are precisely those measures
\mu\in V
for which the polynomials
\mathbb{C}[x]
are dense in
L^{2}(\mu)
according to a famous theorem of M. Riesz, cf.
[
15
]
, and they are called N-extremal in
[
1
]
and von Neumann solutions in
[
16
]
. They form a closed subset of
\ext(V)
, the set of extreme points of the convex set
V
. However,
\ext(V)
is known to be a dense subset of
V
. They are characterized by the formula

\int\frac{d\mu_{t}(x)}{x-z}=-\frac{A(z)+tC(z)}{B(z)+tD(z)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R},t\in\mathbb{R}^{*},

(27)

where
A,\ldots,D
are the entire functions given in (
11
), cf.
[
17
, Theorem 7.6]
. Recall that (
12
) holds.

We summarize some of the properties of
\mu_{t}
, which can be found in
[
1
]
and
[
17
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(i)

The solution
\mu_{t}
is a discrete measure with support equal to the countable zero set
\Lambda_{t}
of the entire function
B(z)+tD(z)
, with the convention that
\Lambda_{\infty}
is the zero set of
D
. In particular
\Lambda_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

(ii)

The support of two different N-extremal solutions are disjoint, and each point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
belongs to the support of a unique N-extremal measure
\mu_{t}
, where

t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
is given as
t=-B(x_{0})/D(x_{0})
if
D(x_{0})\neq 0
and
t=\infty
if
D(x_{0})=0
.

Let us consider the vector space

\mathcal{E}:=\{F_{c}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}p_{n}(z)\mid c\in\ell^{2}\}

(28)

of entire functions, cf. (
5
). It is a Hilbert space under the norm

||F_{c}||^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|c_{n}|^{2}=\int|F_{c}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu(x),

where
\mu\in V
can be arbitrary. It is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of functions with the reproducing kernel

K(u,v):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(u)p_{n}(v),\quad u,v\in\mathbb{C},

in the sense that

\int K(u,x)F_{c}(x)\,d\mu(x)=F_{c}(u),\quad\mu\in V,u\in\mathbb{C}.

Note that
(p_{n})
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{E}
, and the mapping
c\mapsto F_{c}

is a unitary operator of the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}
onto
\mathcal{E}
.

For each N-extremal measure
\mu_{t}
the mapping
c\mapsto F_{c}|_{\supp(\mu_{t})}
is a unitary operator of
\ell^{2}
onto
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
. The inverse mapping is given by

f\mapsto\left(\langle f,p_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\right)_{n\geq 0},\quad f\in L^{2}(\mu_{t}),

and

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f,p_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}p_{n}(x),\quad x\in\supp(\mu_{t}).

(29)

The series in (
29
) converges to
f
in
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
and converges also locally uniformly for
x\in\mathbb{C}
, but
f
is apriori only defined on
\supp(\mu_{t})
, so the equality holds pointwise for
x\in\supp(\mu_{t})
. The series represents a holomorphic extension of
f
to all of
\mathbb{C}
.

The self-adjoint operator
T_{t}
from
(
25
) is unitarily equivalent with the multiplication operator
M_{\mu_{t}}
on

L^{2}(\mu_{t})
given by

M_{\mu_{t}}f(x)=xf(x),\quad f\in L^{2}(\mu_{t}),x\in\supp(\mu_{t}).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mu_{t}
be an N-extremal measure and let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\supp\mu_{t}
.
Then

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t}),

(30)

where

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda):=\int{1\over x-\lambda}\,d\mu_{t}(x)=-{C(\lambda,x)\over D(\lambda,x)},\quad x\in\supp\mu_{t}.

(31)

In particular, the ratio
C(\lambda,x)/D(\lambda,x)

does not depend on
x\in\supp\mu_{t}.

Proof.

Since
\lambda\notin\supp\mu_{t}

the functions
(x-\lambda)^{-1}
and
x(x-\lambda)^{-1}
are bounded on
\supp\mu_{t}
and in particular they belong to
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
. Thus
(x-\lambda)^{-1}\in D(M_{\mu_{t}})
and we find

\int\frac{p_{n}(x)}{x-\lambda}\,d\mu_{t}(x)=w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)p_{n}(\lambda)+q_{n}(\lambda),

where
w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)
is given by the first equality of (
31
).
Moreover, by (
29
)

(x-\lambda)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)p_{n}(\lambda)+q_{n}(\lambda)]p_{n}(x),\quad x\in\supp\mu_{t}.

(32)

In view of the unitary equivalence of
T_{t}
and
M_{\mu_{t}}
we get (
30
).
Multiplying (
32
) sidewise by
x-\lambda
gives

1=w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)(x-\lambda)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(\lambda)p_{n}(x)+(x-\lambda)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}(\lambda)p_{n}(x),\quad x\in\supp(\mu_{t}).

By (
8
) and (
9
) we therefore get

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)D(\lambda,x)+C(\lambda,x)=0,\quad x\in\supp\mu_{t}.

Assume
D(\lambda,x)=0.
Then
C(\lambda,x)=0,
but this gives a contradiction to (
10
).
Hence
D(\lambda,x)\neq 0
and

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)=-{C(\lambda,x)\over D(\lambda,x)},\quad x\in\supp\mu_{t},

which gives the second part of (
31
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Using the formulas (
5.2
) and (
5.2
) in the last expression in (
31
), we get formula (
27
) with

t=-B(x)/D(x)
independent of
x\in\supp(\mu_{t})
if
D(x)\neq 0
, and
t=\infty
if
D(x)=0
.

Note also that
w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\notin D(T)
by Theorem
1.2
(iii) because
B(\lambda,\lambda)=-1
.

We know from Theorem
1.2
that
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda},\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\notin D(T)
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. We shall now clarify when
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda},\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}
belong to the domain of the self-adjoint extension
T_{t}
associated with the N-extremal measure
\mu_{t}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
we have

\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})\iff D(\lambda,x)=0\;\forall x\in\supp(\mu_{t})\iff\lambda\in\supp(\mu_{t}).

(33)

\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})\iff C(\lambda,x)=0\;\forall x\in\supp(\mu_{t}).

(34)

Proof.

We define the entire functions
g_{\lambda},h_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\mu_{t})
by

g_{\lambda}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(\lambda)p_{k}(x),\quad h_{\lambda}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q_{k}(\lambda)p_{k}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{C}.

If
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
then
(T_{t}-\lambda I)\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}=0
by (
13
), because
T_{t}
is a restriction of
T^{*}
. Furthermore, by the unitary equivalence between

T_{t}
and the multiplication operator
M_{\mu_{t}}
on
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
we have
xg_{\lambda}(x)\in L^{2}(\mu_{t})
and
D(\lambda,x)=(\lambda-x)g_{\lambda}(x)=0
in
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
. By discreteness of
\mu_{t}
we get
D(\lambda,x)=0
for all
x\in\supp(\mu_{t})
. The last equivalence of (
33
) follows from Remark
5.10
. On the other hand, it
is easy to see that the last two equivalent conditions of (
33
) imply
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
, because the zero-function
x\mapsto(\lambda-x)g_{\lambda}(x)
as well as
\lambda g_{\lambda}(x)
are in
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
, hence also
xg_{\lambda}(x)\in L^{2}(\mu_{t})
. Therefore
g_{\lambda}\in D(M_{\mu_{t}})
and finally

\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
.

If
\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
then
(T_{t}-\lambda I)\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}=e_{0}
by (
13
), because
T_{t}
is a restriction of
T^{*}
. This shows that
(x-\lambda)h_{\lambda}(x)=1
in
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
and hence
C(\lambda,x)=0
for all
x\in\supp(\mu_{t})
. On the other hand, if
C(\lambda,x)=0
for
x\in\supp(\mu_{t})
, we conclude that
xh_{\lambda}(x)\in L^{2}(\mu_{t})
, hence
\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
. This establishes (
34
).
∎

We know from Proposition
3.1
that
\supp(\mu_{t})
is the the zero set
of the entire function
B(z)+tD(z)
understood as
D(z)
if
t=\infty
. Using this we get the following Corollary about
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
. We get a similar result about
\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}
from (
5.2
).

Corollary 3.5
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
t=\infty
we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle B(\lambda)+tD(\lambda)=0,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle A(\lambda)+tC(\lambda)=0.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{\infty})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle D(\lambda)=0,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{\infty})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle C(\lambda)=0.

In particular
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}
only belong to
D(T_{t})
if

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
they belong to a unique
D(T_{t})
. Furthermore, they never belong to the same domain
D(T_{t})
.

Remark 3.6
.

Since
D(T)\subset D(T_{t})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
, it is clear that Corollary
3.5
implies that
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda},\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\notin D(T)
as stated in Corollary
2.2
.

We also have a kind of converse to Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume that
\lambda,\tau\in\mathbb{C}
are such that
\tau\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Then
\lambda\notin\supp(\mu_{t})
and

\tau=w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)
given by (
31
).

Proof.

Assume that
\lambda\in\supp(\mu_{t})
. By Theorem
3.4
we know that
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
and hence
\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
, contradicting Corollary
3.5
.

Having established
\lambda\notin\supp(\mu_{t})
, we get by Theorem
3.2
that
(\tau-w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda))\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\in D(T_{t})
, but since

\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\notin D(T_{t})
, we get

\tau-w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\supp(\mu_{t})
be given.
Then there exists a unique pair
(s,c)\in D(T)\times\mathbb{C}
depending on
t,\lambda
such that

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s+c(\mathfrak{q}_{0}+t\mathfrak{p}_{0}),&t\in\mathbb{R}\\
s+c\mathfrak{p}_{0},&t=\infty.\end{array}\right.

We have

c=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1/(B(\lambda)+tD(\lambda)),&t\in\mathbb{R}\\
-1/D(\lambda),&t=\infty,\end{array}\right.

and
s
is given by inserting the value of
c
.

Proof.

Recall that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\supp(\mu_{t})
if and only if
B(\lambda)+tD(\lambda)\neq 0
when
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\supp(\mu_{\infty})
if and only if

D(\lambda)\neq 0
. The existence and uniqueness of
(s,c)
follow from Theorem
3.2
and formula (
25
).

In case
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{q}_{\lambda}-c(\mathfrak{q}_{0}+t\mathfrak{p}_{0})\in D(T),

and fixing

in the open upper half-plane we have by Proposition 2.1

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)s_{\lambda}^{+}+r_{\lambda}^{+}-c(r_{0}^{+}+ts_{0}^{+})=w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)s_{\lambda}^{-}+r_{\lambda}^{-}-c(r_{0}^{-}+ts_{0}^{-})=0,

or equivalently by (
17
) and (
19
)

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)D(\lambda,\overline{z_{0}})+C(\lambda,\overline{z_{0}})-c(C(0,\overline{z_{0}})+tD(0,\overline{z_{0}}))=0,

(35)

and

w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)D(\lambda,z_{0})+C(\lambda,z_{0})-c(C(0,z_{0})+tD(0,z_{0}))=0.

(36)

From (
36
) and (
31
) we get for
x\in\supp(\mu_{t})

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{w_{\mu_{t}}(\lambda)D(\lambda,z_{0})+C(\lambda,z_{0})}{B(z_{0})+tD(z_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C(\lambda,x)D(\lambda,z_{0})-D(\lambda,x)C(\lambda,z_{0})}{(B(z_{0})+tD(z_{0}))D(\lambda,x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{D(z_{0},\lambda)C(\lambda,x)-B(z_{0},\lambda)D(\lambda,x)}{(B(z_{0})+tD(z_{0}))D(\lambda,x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{D(z_{0},x)}{(B(z_{0})+tD(z_{0}))D(\lambda,x)}=-\frac{1}{B(\lambda)+tD(\lambda)}.

Here we have first used (
54
) and next used (
5.2
) twice. Finally we recall that

for

, cf. Proposition

.

Note that (

) leads to the same expression for

.

The case

is treated in the same way.
∎

4
Parametrizations of the domain of the Jacobi operator

The Jacobi operator
(T,D(T))
in the indeterminate case is regular in the sense of
[
13
, p. 20]
, i.e., for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
there exists
d(z)>0
such that

||(T-zI)c||\geq d(z)||c||,\quad c\in D(T).

(37)

For
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
this is true with
d(z)=|{\rm Im}\,(z)|
, and for
z\in\mathbb{R}
let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
be such that
z\in\supp(\mu_{t_{0}})
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\setminus\{t_{0}\}
the distance

d_{t}(z):=\min\{|z-x|\mid x\in\supp(\mu_{t})\}>0,

can be used in (
37
), since we have

||(T-zI)c||^{2}=\int\left|(x-z)F_{c}(x)\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(x)\geq d_{t}(z)^{2}||c||^{2},

where
F_{c}
is given in (
5
).

We have the orthogonal decomposition in closed subspaces

\ell^{2}=(T-zI)(D(T))\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{p}_{\overline{z}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(38)

The operator
(T,D(T))
has no eigenvalues, has empty continuous spectrum, and the spectrum
\sigma(T)=\mathbb{C}
is equal to the residual spectrum, cf.
[
18
, p.209]
.

For
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
we have the orthogonal expansion

\frac{p_{n}(z)-p_{n}(z_{0})}{z-z_{0}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}(z_{0})p_{k}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}

(39)

of the polynomial
(p_{n}(z)-p_{n}(z_{0})/(z-z_{0})
of degree
n-1
, and it is easy to see that

a_{n,k}(z_{0})=\int\frac{p_{n}(x)-p_{n}(z_{0})}{x-z_{0}}p_{k}(x)\,d\mu(x)=q_{n}(z_{0})p_{k}(z_{0})-p_{n}(z_{0})q_{k}(z_{0}),

(40)

where
\mu\in V

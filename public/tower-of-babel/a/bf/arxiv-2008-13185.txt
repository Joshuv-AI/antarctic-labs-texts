Retarded resonance Casimir-Polder interaction of a uniformly rotating two-atom system
Saptarshi Saha, Chiranjeeb Singha, Arpan Chatterjee

Abstract
We consider here, a two-atom system is uniformly moving through a circular ring at an ultra-relativistic speed and weakly interacting with common external fields. The vacuum fluctuations of the quantum fields generate the entanglement between the atoms. Hence an effective energy shift is originated, which depends on the inter-atomic distance. This is commonly known as resonance Casimir-Polder interaction (RCPI). It is well known that, for a linearly accelerated system coupled with a massless scalar field, we get a thermal response when the local inertial approximation is valid. On the contrary, the non-thermality arises in the presence of the centripetal acceleration. We use the quantum master equation formalism to calculate the second-order energy shift of the entangled states in the presence of two kinds of fields. They are the massive free scalar field and the electromagnetic vector field. For both cases, we observe the non-thermal behavior. A unique retarded response is also noticed in comparison to the free massless case, which can be observed via the polarization transfer technique.

II
Dynamics of a two-atom system

We assume here, two atoms are rotating syn-chronically in a circular orbit and
the perpendicular distance in the rotating plane is defined as
L
, which is fixed
[
20
]
.
The angular speed is
\lambda_{0}
,
R
is the radius of the circular orbit. The tangential velocity
is
\mathcal{V}=\lambda_{0}R
. Now, we focus on the dynamics in the ultra-relativistic regime

[
18
]

The positions of the atoms in terms of proper time, are written by,

\displaystyle t_{1}(\tau)=\gamma\tau,\quad x_{1}(\tau)=R\cos\frac{\gamma\tau\mathcal{V}}{R},\quad y_{1}(\tau)=R\sin\frac{\gamma\tau\mathcal{V}}{R},\quad z_{1}(\tau)=0

\displaystyle t_{2}(\tau)=\gamma\tau,\quad x_{2}(\tau)=R\cos\frac{\gamma\tau\mathcal{V}}{R},\quad y_{2}(\tau)=R\sin\frac{\gamma\tau\mathcal{V}}{R},\quad z_{2}(\tau)=L~.

(1)

Here
\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-\mathcal{V}^{2}}
is the Lorentz factor. The centripetal acceleration is given by

a=\mathcal{V}^{2}\gamma^{2}/R
, which will provide a length scale in the dynamics. The atoms are
weakly coupled with the external quantum fields. The distance between the atoms is taken to be
smaller than the correlation length of the field. Total Hamiltonian of the system+field is written
as
[
29
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}+\mathcal{H}_{f}+\mathcal{H}_{sf}~.

\mathcal{H}_{s}
is the free Hamiltonian of the system, it is expressed as,

\mathcal{H}_{s}=\omega_{0}/2\big(\sigma_{z}\otimes\mathds{1}+\mathds{1}\otimes\sigma_{z}\big)
.
The system is taken to be isotropic.
\mathcal{H}_{f}
is the normal ordered field Hamiltonian.
Following the second quantization technique, it can be expressed as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{f}=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}E_{k}a^{\dagger}(k)a(k)~.

(2)

E_{k}
is the frequency of the field and
a,a^{\dagger}
is the creation and annihilation operator.
Exact expression of the operators are depend on the characteristics of the field.
The system-field coupling Hamiltonian is expressed as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{sf}=\alpha\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{3}\big[\sigma_{\mu}^{(1)}\otimes\phi_{\mu}(x_{1})+\sigma_{\mu}^{(2)}\otimes\phi_{\mu}(x_{2})\big]~,

(3)

\alpha
is the coupling strength.
\phi
is the field and
x_{1},\,x_{2}
are the individual trajectories
of two atoms which is defined earlier. We define,

\displaystyle\phi_{\mu}(x)=\sum^{N}_{a=1}\big[\chi^{a}_{\mu}\phi^{-}(x)\,+\,(\chi^{a}_{\mu})^{\dagger}\phi^{+}(x)\big]~,

(4)

\phi^{\mp}(x)
is the negative and positive field operator of the field and
\chi^{a}_{\mu}
are the
corresponding complex coefficients
[
29
]
. The system + field formed a closed system.
To find a dynamical equation of the system, the starting point is the “Von Neumann-Liouville” equation

[
22
]
. It is given by,

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i[\mathcal{H}_{s}+\mathcal{H}_{f}+\mathcal{H}_{sf},\rho(t)]~.

(5)

The Eq. (
5
) is further expanded to the second order of the perturbing Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{sf}

and after taking trace over the field variables the reduced dynamical equation of the two-atom system in proper time (
\tau
) can be
written as,

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{s}(\tau)}{d\tau}=-i[\mathcal{H}_{s}+\mathcal{H}_{lamb},\rho_{s}(\tau)]+\mathcal{L}\big(\rho_{s}(\tau)\big)~.

(6)

The above equation is called the Lindblad equation or the quantum master equation

[
27
,
28
]
. The time evolution operator is a one parameter
semi-group and the completely positivity and trace preservation holds. The initial correlation between
the system and field is ignored, it is called “Born-approximation”
[
23
]
.

\mathcal{H}_{lamb}
is the second order effective Hamiltonian. It produces the shift term in the dynamics.

\mathcal{L}
is called the dissipator, which generates the irreversibility in the reduced dynamics of the system.
The exact mathematical expression of this two quantity is written by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\big(\rho_{s}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a,b=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{3}\gamma^{ab}_{jk}\Big(\sigma_{b}^{k}\rho_{s}\sigma_{a}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{a}^{j}\sigma_{b}^{k},\rho_{s}\}\Big)~,

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{lamb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{2}\sum\limits_{a,b=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{3}\mathcal{S}_{jk}^{ab}\sigma_{a}^{j}\sigma_{b}^{k}~,

(8)

Here
\mathcal{S}_{jk}^{ab}
and
\gamma^{ab}_{jk}
is originated from the Fourier transform of
the two-point correlation function. They are Kramers-Kronig pairs to each-other.
Due to the common-environment effect, the atoms become entangled through the field correlation function.
This entanglement has an initial value dependency
[
29
]
.
The field induced shift term is calculated from the Hilbert transforms of the response function of the field.

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{ab}(\omega_{0})=\frac{\mathcal{P}}{\pi i}\int\limits^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\omega\frac{\mathcal{G}^{ab}(\omega)}{\omega-\omega_{0}}~.

(9)

\mathcal{P}
is the Cauchy principal value.
\mathcal{G}^{ab}(\omega)
is the response function of the field and it is defined as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{{G}}^{ab}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\Delta\tau~e^{i\omega\Delta\tau}~{G}^{ab}(\Delta\tau)~.

(10)

Here we assume
\chi^{a}_{\mu}
satisfies
\sum\limits^{N}_{a=1}\,\chi^{a}_{\mu}\big(\chi^{a}_{\nu}\big)^{\dagger}\,=\,\delta_{\mu\nu}.
So the field correlation functions are diagonal i.e.
G_{ij}^{ab}(x-y)=\delta_{ij}G^{ab}(x-y)
, which is given by,

\displaystyle G^{ab}(\Delta\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\Phi}(\tau,\mathbf{x}_{a}){\Phi}(\tau^{\prime},\mathbf{x}_{b})\rangle~,

(11)

Here
\Delta\tau=(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
. From the K.M.S condition, the detector response function of a linear accelerating
particle detector in a massless scalar field is given by

40
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\omega)=\frac{|\omega|}{2\pi}\big\{\theta(\omega)\frac{1}{e^{\omega/T}-1}+\theta(-\omega)\big(1+\frac{1}{e^{|\omega|/T}-1}\big)\big\}~.

(12)

T=a/2\pi
,
a
is the linear acceleration. The first term determine the absorption rate and the second term
determine the induced and spontaneous emission rate. The Planck factor in the Eq. (

particle detector perceive a thermal bath of temperature

. For a circularly rotating observer,

[

]
.
In the RCPI, only the spontaneous radiative process occurs hence there is no use of the number densities in calculation
of the response function
[

,
13

14
]
. Here we can write the form of

\mathcal{S}^{ab}_{jk}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{ab}_{jk}=A^{ab}\delta_{jk}-iB^{ab}\epsilon_{jkl}\delta_{3l}-A^{ab}\delta_{3j}\delta_{3k}~,

where the terms

\displaystyle A^{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\left[\mathcal{K}^{ab}(\omega_{0})+\mathcal{K}^{ab}(-\omega_{0})\right],

(14)

\displaystyle B^{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\left[\mathcal{K}^{ab}(\omega_{0})-\mathcal{K}^{ab}(-\omega_{0})\right]~.

(15)

The cross terms of the individual system-field Hamiltonian contribute in the off-diagonal elements of

) and anti-symmetric states

The expectation values of this two state is given by,

\displaystyle\delta E_{S_{LS}}=\langle E|\mathcal{H}_{lamb}|E\rangle

\displaystyle=-2~i\left[A_{2}+A_{1}\right]~,

\displaystyle\delta E_{A_{LS}}=\langle A|\mathcal{H}_{lamb}|A\rangle

Here,

III.1
The length dependence and retarded response of RCPI

The expression in Eq. (
21
) has a dependency on
aL
,

(aL=\frac{\mathcal{V}^{2}L}{(1-\mathcal{V}^{2})R})
. We analyze the
resonance interaction in two limiting cases. The condition,
aL<<1
can
be achieved when the inter-atomic separation is very small with
comparison with the radius of the circular path,
(L<<R)
. Basically
this set up is nearly equal to the linear acceleration case, where
inertial approximation is valid. In this limit, the expression can be
written as,

\displaystyle\delta E=\alpha_{1}^{2}\frac{\cos\big(\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-m^{2}}L\big)}{L}~.

(22)

In the small mass limit
(m<<\omega_{0})
, the phase lag
(\delta\eta)

in RCPI with respect to massless case is given by,

\displaystyle\delta\eta=\frac{m^{2}L}{2\omega_{0}}~.

(23)

In this limit, the length dependence of RCPI exactly matches with the linear case and
for
m=0
. It matches with the thermal response. For the other case,

aL>>1
, which is experimentally easier to achieve in the
ultra-relativistic limits. The RCPI is given by,

\displaystyle\delta E=3^{1/4}\alpha_{1}^{2}\frac{\cos\big(12^{1/4}\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-m^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{L}{a}}\big)}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{aL^{3}}}~.

(24)

Similarly in the small mass limit
(m<<\omega_{0})
, the phase lag w.r.t
massless one is given by,

\displaystyle\delta\eta=\frac{12^{1/4}m^{2}}{2\omega_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{L}{a}}~.

(25)

In this limit the local inertial approximation is violated so non-thermality
arises. The length dependency is also not equal with the linear acceleration
case
[
30
]
. The
l/L^{2}
dependence is replaced by

. Here
1/l
is the linear acceleration. The presence of

the

with the electromagnetic field. The Lagrangian of the EM field is obtained by

]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{uv}F^{uv}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{v}A^{v})^{2}~.

(26)

Here
F_{uv}=\partial_{u}A_{v}-\partial_{v}A_{u}
,
A_{u}
is the
electromagnetic vector potential,
A_{u}=(\phi,\vec{A})
. Here we use the

. Hence the photon operator can be written as,

\displaystyle A_{v}(\vec{x})=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2|\vec{p}|}}\sum\limits_{\lambda=0}^{3}\epsilon_{v}^{\lambda}(\vec{p})[a_{p}^{\lambda}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}+a_{p}^{\lambda\,\dagger}e^{-i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}]~.

\vec{d}=e\vec{r}

is the charge of the

\displaystyle\vec{d}=-\sigma^{-}\vec{\mathcal{Y}}^{*}-\sigma^{+}\vec{\mathcal{Y}}~.

Here
\vec{\mathcal{Y}}=e\langle E|\vec{r}|A\rangle

Two spins are identical, so

\displaystyle\langle 0|E_{i}(x)E_{j}(x^{\prime})|0\rangle=(\partial_{0}\partial_{0}^{\prime}\delta_{ij}-\partial_{i}\partial_{j})\langle 0|A_{i}(x)A_{j}(x^{\prime})|0\rangle~.

The two-point correlation function in the ultra-relativistic regime is given by,

\displaystyle G^{11}(\Delta\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{22}(\Delta\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\Delta\tau^{4}(1+a^{2}\Delta\tau^{2}/12)^{2}}~,

\displaystyle G^{12}(\Delta\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{21}(\Delta\tau)

V
Instantaneous polarization transfer using RCPI

In this section, we give a computation protocol to inspect the retardation effect using the
polarization transfer technique. In particular, as an example, we will discuss about the transfer of different magnetization modes in a system of two spin-1/2 magnetic dipoles.
For single spin system, there exist three possible
magnetization modes namely
\sigma_{x},\,\sigma_{y},\,\sigma_{z}
.
Following the same logic, for two spin system the number of possible
magnetization modes is fifteen. The trace preservation is a constraint in the
system. As the atoms has same energy levels, the number of the independent
magnetization modes reduces to nine. The modes are given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{s}\{(\sigma_{i}\otimes\mathds{1}+\mathds{1}\otimes\sigma_{i})\rho_{s}\}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{s}\{(\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{i})\rho_{s}\}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{s}\{(\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}+\sigma_{j}\otimes\sigma_{i})\rho_{s}\}~.

(39)

Here
i=x,y,z
. We consider here only those elements of the RCPI
Hamiltonian which is responsible for the creation of the inter-atomic
correlation. The form of the Hamiltonian in the Zeeman basis is given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ia}=\frac{\delta E}{2}(\sigma_{x}\otimes\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})~.

(40)

\mathcal{H}_{ia}
is the effective Hamiltonian of the resonance interaction. In the intermediate
time scale, the effect of dissipation can be ignored. Hence the dynamics is
pure unitary. So the magnetization modes are changing with time. The dynamical
equation in interaction frame is written as,

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho^{I}_{s}}{d\tau}=-i[\mathcal{H}_{ia}^{I},\rho_{s}^{I}]~.

(41)

\rho^{I}_{s}
is the density matrices in interaction frame. For simplicity we neglect the
I
in the superscript of the density matrices. The RCPI Hamiltonian is unchanged in the interaction frame. In terms of magnetization modes, it is given as,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}(\mathcal{F}_{x}-\mathcal{F}_{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta E}{2}(\mathcal{F}_{xz}+\mathcal{F}_{yz})~,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}(\mathcal{F}_{xz}+\mathcal{F}_{yz})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta E}{2}(\mathcal{F}_{x}-\mathcal{F}_{y})~.

(42)

Other modes are unchanged under the interaction. For initial magnetization,

\mathcal{F}_{x}(0)-\mathcal{F}_{y}(0)=N_{1}
and

\mathcal{F}_{xz}(0)+\mathcal{F}_{yz}(0)=0

42

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x}-\mathcal{F}_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{1}\cos\frac{\delta E\tau}{2}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{xz}+\mathcal{F}_{yz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{1}\sin\frac{\delta E\tau}{2}~.

(43)

We can distinguish the energy shift due to
RCPI for massive and EM field case with the massless case using the

\displaystyle t_{in}=\frac{\pi}{\delta E\,\sqrt{1-\lambda_{0}^{2}R^{2}}}

VI
Discussions

As a summary, in this paper, we explore the RCPI of a syn-chronically rotating
two-atom system in the circular storage ring. The atoms move through a
quantized field. They are kept in a ground state and excited state and become
entangled due to the interaction with the common external field, which is
called the resonance interaction. The quantum master equation is the essential
tool to calculate the energy shift due to the resonance interaction. In the
presence of the massless free scalar field, the thermalization theorem holds
for a uniformly accelerating particle detector. The thermal nature can be
observed when the inertial approximation is maintained. The non-thermal
characters arise when centripetal acceleration present in the system. We can
get back the thermal limit when the radius of the circle is much larger
than the inter-atomic distance. For interaction with the massive scalar field,
the length scale dependency is similar to the massless case. For
aL<<1
, it
has a
1/L
dependence and for
aL>>1
, it has a
1/\sqrt{aL^{3}}
dependence.
The major aspect of the massive scalar field case is the retarded response. It
is expected that RCPI shows a periodic response. In the presence of mass, we
get a retarded periodic response, which is basically governed by a
m^{2}

factor. When the inertial approximation is not valid, then the phase-lag is
also modified by the centripetal acceleration. If we consider an
electromagnetic-vector field, the response function is also corrected by a

a^{2}
term, and the non-Planck factor is also present. As a result, the RCPI
exhibits a non-thermal behavior, which is also independent of the inertial
approximation. In both the limits (
aL>>1,aL<<1
), the amplitude of RCPI
decays much faster than the massless case. The amplitude also depends on the
Zeeman energy of the atoms. A retarded response was also noticed in this case.
The oscillatory behavior of RCPI changes the nature of forces in each period of
the inter-atomic length scale. If we consider this kind of interaction, the
phase lag is present, which can alter the characteristics of RCPI
w.r.t

the mass-less case. It seems that an attractive RCPF for the mass-less case may
behave like a repulsive force for massive or EM field case and vice versa.
Surprisingly the effect of Zeeman frequency on the phase lag for massive and EM
field cases is different. Increasing the Zeeman frequency, the phase lag for a
massive case goes to zero, whereas it has a constant phase lag (
\delta\eta=\pi/2
) for the EM field. The characteristics of the interaction also can
be verified by using the polarization-transfer technique. The expectation value
of energy is different for mass-less, massive, and EM field case. The
magnetization transfer time should be different for them, as
t_{in}=\pi/(\gamma\delta E)
. Similarly, in the frequency domain, the energy peaks appear at different
points, which is also used as an important tool to distinguish the different
responses. Future experimental protocols can be designed by using NMR
spectroscopy to justify the Casimir effect in a circular storage ring.

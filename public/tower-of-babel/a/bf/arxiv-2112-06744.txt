Groups of p p -absolute Galois type that are not absolute Galois groups
Simone Blumer, Alberto Cassella, Claudio Quadrelli

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime. We study pro- p p groups of p p -absolute Galois type, as defined by Lam–Liu–Sharifi–Wake–Wang. We prove that the pro- p p completion of the right-angled Artin group associated to a chordal simplicial graph is of p p -absolute Galois type, and moreover it satisfies a strong version of the Massey vanishing property. Also, we prove that Demushkin groups are of p p -absolute Galois type, and that the free pro- p p product — and, under certain conditions, the direct product — of two pro- p p groups of p p -absolute Galois type satisfying the Massey vanishing property, is again a pro- p p group of p p -absolute Galois type satisfying the Massey vanishing property. Consequently, there is a plethora of pro- p p groups of p p -absolute Galois type satisfying the Massey vanishing property that do not occur as absolute Galois groups.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper,
p
will denote a prime number.
Given a field
\mathbb{K}
, let
\bar{\mathbb{K}}_{s}
denote the separable closure of
\mathbb{K}
, and let
\mathbb{K}(p)
denote the maximal

\mathbb{K}

The

is a profinite group, and the Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p):=\Gal(\mathbb{K}(p)/\mathbb{K})
, called the
maximal pro-
p
Galois group
of
\mathbb{K}
, is the maximal pro-
p
quotient of
G_{\mathbb{K}}
.
A major difficult problem in Galois theory is the characterization of profinite groups which occur as absolute Galois groups of fields, and of pro-
p
groups which occur as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups (see, e.g.,
[
12
, § 3.12]
and
[
24
, § 2.2]
).
Observe that if a pro-
p
group

does not occur as the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a field containing a root of 1 of order
p
, then it does not occur as the absolute Galois group of any field (see, e.g.,
[
37
, Rem. 3.3]
).
For this reason, the pursue of obstructions which detect effectively pro-
p
groups which do not occur as absolute Galois groups has great prominence in current research in Galois theory (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
3
,
8
,
37
]

The celebrated
Bloch-Kato Conjecture
— established by M. Rost and V. Voevodsky, with Ch. Weibel’s “patch”, and now called the Norm Residue Theorem (see
[
39
,
46
,
48
,
13

As a consequence, if
\mathbb{K}
is a field containing a root of 1 of order
p
, the structure of the
\mathbb{Z}/p
-cohomology algebra

\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)):=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{H}^{n}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{Z}/p),

endowed with the
cup-product

\smallsmile
, is determined by degrees 1 and 2.
This remarkable results provided new fuel — and new substantial results — to the research on maximal pro-
p
Galois groups of fields.

Subsequently, the paper by M. Hopkins and K. Wickelgren
[
15
]
kicked off a hectic research on
Massey products
in Galois cohomology.
Given a pro-
p
group
G
and an integer
n\geq 2
, the

-fold Massey product
is a multi-valued map which associates a (possibly empty) subset of
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
to a
n
-tuple of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
(if
n=2
it coincides with the cup-product).
Moreover,
G
is said to satisfy the
n
-Massey vanishing property
if every non-empty value of an
n
-fold Massey product contains 0. In
[
22
]
, E. Matzri proved that if

is a field containing a root of 1 of order
p
, then

has the 3-Massey vanishing property (see also
[
7
]
and
[
27
]
): this result produced new obstructions for the realization of pro-
p
groups as absolute Galois groups (see, e.g.,
[
29
, § 7]
); and Minač and Tân conjectured that such a

has the
n
-Massey vanishing property for
every

(see
[
25

Another cohomological property enjoyed by maximal pro-
p
Galois groups — and related to both the Norm Residue Theorem and Massey products — is the one which gives the title to this paper.
A pro-

Keeping this question in mind, we focus on the family of

Our first goal is to prove that every pro-

2.2.
Graphs and RAAGs

For the definition of simplicial graph we follow
[
4
, § 1.1]
.
A
simplicial graph
is a pair
\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
of sets such that
\mathcal{E}\subseteq[\mathcal{V}]^{2}
, i.e., the elements
of
\mathcal{E}
are 2-element subsets of
\mathcal{V}
(we always assume implicitly that

The elements of

are the

of

\mathcal{E}

One may realize geometrically a simplicial graph by drawing a dot for each vertex and joining two of these dots by a line if the corresponding two vertices form an edge.
Henceforth, we will always deal with finite simplicial graphs, i.e., with a finite number of vertices.

Remark 2.2
.

In
[

3.1.
Massey products in Galois cohomology

Let

be a pro-
p
group.
For

, the
n
-fold Massey product
on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
is a multi-valued map

\underbrace{\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)\times\ldots\times\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)}_{n\text{ times}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p).

Given an

n\geq 2

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
(with possibly
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}

which is the value of the
n

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}

\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
.
If
n=2
, then the 2-fold Massey product coincides with the cup-product, i.e., for
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
one has

(3.1)

\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle=\{\alpha\smallsmile\alpha_{2}\}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p).

For further details on this operation in the profinite and Galois-theoretic context, we direct the reader to
[

,
11
,
29
]

In particular, the definition of
n
-fold Massey products in the

p
groups may be found in
[
29
, Def. 2.1]
.
For the purposes of our investigation, the properties described below — and, in particular, the group-theoretic characterizations given by Proposition
3.4
— will be enough.

Given an
n

of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)

p

3.2.
Massey products and unipotent representations

Massey products for a pro-
p
group
G
may be translated in terms of unipotent upper-triangular representations of
G
as follows.
For
n\geq 2
let

\mathbb{U}_{n+1}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1,2}&\cdots&&a_{1,n+1}\\
&1&a_{2,3}&\cdots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&1&a_{n,n+1}\\
&&&&1\end{array}\right)\mid a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}/p\right\}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p)

be the group of unipotent upper-triangular
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrices over
\mathbb{Z}/p
.
Then
\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
is a finite
p
-group.
Moreover, let
I_{n+1}
and
E_{i,j}
denote respectively the identity
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrix and the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrix with 1 at entry
(i,j)
and 0 elsewhere, for
1\leq i<j\leq n+1
.
The center of
\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
is the subgroup

\mathrm{Z}(\mathbb{U}_{n+1})=I_{n+1}+\mathbb{Z}/p\cdot E_{1,n+1}=\left\{\>I_{n+1}+a\cdot E_{1,n+1}\>\mid\>a\in\mathbb{Z}/p\>\right\}

(cf., e.g.,
[
29
, § 3]
or
[
8
, p. 308]
).
Set
\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}=\mathbb{U}_{n+1}/\mathrm{Z}(\mathbb{U}_{n+1})
.
For a homomorphism of pro-
p
groups
\rho\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
, respectively
\bar{\rho}\colon G\to\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}
, and for
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\rho_{i,i+1}
, resp.
\bar{\rho}_{i,i+1}
, denote the projection of
\rho
, resp.
\bar{\rho}
, on the
(i,i+1)
-entry.
Observe that

\rho_{i,i+1}\colon G\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{\rho}_{i,i+1}\colon G\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p

are homomorphisms of pro-
p
groups, and thus we may consider
\rho_{i,i+1}
and
\bar{\rho}_{i,i+1}
as elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
.
One has the following “pro-
p
translation” of a result of W. Dwyer which interpretes Massey product in terms of unipotent upper-triangular represetations (cf., e.g.,
[
8
, Lemma 9.3]
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be an
n
-tuple of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
, with
n\geq 2
.

(i)

The
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
is defined if and only if there exists a continuous homomorphism
\bar{\rho}\colon G\to\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}
such that
\bar{\rho}_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.

(ii)

The
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
vanishes if and only if there exists a continuous homomorphism
\rho\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
such that
\rho_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Lemma 4.3

Proof.

Since

-vector space

.
∎

By Lemma
4.3
, and by duality,
\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\mathbb{Z}/p)
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(H_{\alpha},\mathbb{Z}/p)
have bases

\mathcal{Y}^{\ast}=\left\{\>\omega_{i}|_{H}\>\mid\>w_{i}\in\mathcal{Y}\>\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}^{\ast}=\left\{\>\omega_{i}|_{H_{\alpha}}\>\mid\>w_{i}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}\>\right\}

The next proposition gives a lower bound for the dimension of the image of the map
\mathrm{r}_{G_{\Gamma},H_{\alpha}}
restricted to the summand
\Lambda_{2}(\Gamma_{\alpha}^{\ast})

\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z}/p)

The idea is the following.
If

are vertices of
\Gamma_{\alpha}

.
We use this fact, together with the recursive procedure to construct a chordal graph via patching subgraphs along cliques, starting from cliques.

Proposition 4.4
.

If
\Gamma
is a chordal simplicial graph, then

(4.11)

\dim\left(\mathrm{r}_{G_{\Gamma},H_{\alpha}}(\Lambda_{2}(\Gamma_{\alpha}^{\ast}))\right)\geq|\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{\alpha})|-\dd(\Gamma_{\alpha})+\mathrm{r}(\Gamma_{\alpha}).

Proof.

We proceed as follows: first, we suppose that
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is complete, then we suppose that
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is connected, and finally we deal with the general case.

If
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is complete, then
[w_{i},w_{j}]=1
for every
1\leq i<j\leq m-1

H_{\alpha}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{m-1}
.
From M. Lazard’s work
[
20
]
, one knows that the
\mathbb{Z}/p
-cohomology algebra of a free abelian pro-
p
group is an exterior algebra (see, e.g.,
[
45

Thus,
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(H_{\alpha})
is the exterior algebra generated by

(4.12)

\dim\left(\mathrm{H}^{2}(H_{\alpha},\mathbb{Z}/p)\right)=\binom{m-1}{2}=\binom{m}{2}-m+1.

\mathrm{H}^{2}(H_{\alpha},\mathbb{Z}/p)=\mathrm{r}_{G_{\Gamma},H_{\alpha}}(\Lambda_{2}(\Gamma_{\alpha}^{\ast}))

\mathrm{H}^{2}(H_{\alpha},\mathbb{Z}/p)=\Lambda_{2}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(H_{\alpha},\mathbb{Z}/p))

4.11

Now suppose that
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is connected and not complete.
We use the recursive procedure to construct a chordal graph (cf. Proposition
2.5
): namely, we may find two proper induced subgraphs

of

(which are chordal as well), whose intersection is a clique

is the pasting of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
along

.
Up to renumbering, we may suppose that

with

, so that
\mathcal{V}(\Delta)=\{v_{m_{2}},\ldots,v_{m_{1}}\}
.
Let
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
and

generated, respectively, by

(4.14)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{r}_{G_{\Gamma},H_{\alpha}}\left(\Lambda_{2}(\Gamma_{\alpha}^{\ast})\right)}

&=1+1+1=3.\end{split}

The next proposition gives a lower bound for the dimension of the image of the map

restricted to the summand

of

The idea is the following.
If

is trivial: since

is a defining relation of
H
induced by the two defining relations
[v_{1},v_{3}]=[v_{2},v_{3}]=1
of
G_{\Gamma}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a simplicial graph.
Then

(4.16)

\dim\left(\mathrm{r}_{G_{\Gamma},H}(\Lambda_{1}(\Gamma^{\ast})\smallsmile V_{0})\right)\geq|\mathcal{E}_{0}|+\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{0,\alpha}}(e(v_{j})-1),

where
\mathcal{V}_{0,\alpha}
is defined as in Proposition
4.2
,
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in\mathcal{E}\>\mid\>m<i<j\leq d\}
, and
e(v_{j})
is the number of vertices of
\mathcal{V}(\Gamma_{\alpha})
which are joined to

Proof.

If
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in\mathcal{E}_{0}
, then
w_{i}=v_{i}
and
w_{j}=v_{j}
, and thus one has the relation
[w_{i},w_{j}]=1
in

On the other hand, for

be the vertices lying in

which are joined to

Then, by commutator calculus, for each

\begin{split}1&=\left[v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}^{-1},v_{j}\right]=[w_{j_{1}}\cdots w_{j_{2}-1},w_{j}]\\

&\;\vdots\\

&=[w_{j_{e(v_{j})-1}},w_{j}]\cdots[w_{j_{e(v_{j})}-1},w_{j}]\cdot y_{e(v_{j})-1}\\
\end{split}

for some

.

Now let
F
be the free pro-
p
group generated by
\mathcal{Y}
, and for every element

denote the image of

via the canonical projection

.
The list of all the relations above satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition

, as their images modulo

give rise to the subset

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{[w_{i},w_{j}]},\;\text{with }\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in\mathcal{E}_{0},\\
\sum_{k=j_{1}}^{j_{2}-1}\overline{[w_{k},w_{j}]},\>\ldots,\>\sum_{k=j_{e(v_{j})-1}}^{j_{e(v_{j})}-1}\overline{[w_{k},w_{j}]},\;\text{with }v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{0,\alpha}\end{array}\right\}\subseteq\Phi(F)/F^{(3)}

(here we use the additive notation for

\Gamma=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})

5.

p

5.1.
Free pro-
p
products

One knows that for every
n\geq 3
the
n
-Massey vanishing property is preserved by free pro-
p
products (cf.
[
29
, Prop. 4.5]
).
We show that also the property of being of
p
-absolute Galois type is preserved by free pro-
p
products.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be pro-
p
groups, and let
G
denote their free pro-
p
product
G_{1}\amalg G_{2}
.
If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are of
p
-absolute Galois type, then also
G
is of
p
-absolute Galois type.

Proof.

Since
G=G_{1}\amalg G_{2}
, one has an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{Z}/p
-algebras

(5.1)

\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)\simeq\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{1})\oplus\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{2}),

which implies that
\alpha_{1}\smallsmile\alpha_{2}=0
for every
\alpha_{1}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)
and
\alpha_{2}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)
(cf.
[
30
, Thm. 4.1.4–4.1.5]
).

By Theorem
3.6
–(i), it sufficies to show that (
1.1
) is exact at
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
for every non-trivial
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
.
By (
5.1
) we may write
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
, with
\alpha_{1}=\alpha|_{G_{1}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)
and
\alpha_{2}=\alpha|_{G_{2}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)
.
Set
N=\Ker(\alpha)
, and
N_{1}=\Ker(\alpha_{1})=N\cap G_{1}
and
N_{2}=\Ker(\alpha_{2})=N\cap G_{2}
.
By the Kurosh Subgroup Theorem for free pro-
p
products of pro-
p
groups (cf.
[
30
, Thm. 4.2.1]
), one has

(5.2)

N=N_{1}\amalg N_{2}\amalg\underbrace{\left(\coprod_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}xN_{1}x^{-1}\right)\amalg\left(\coprod_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}yN_{2}y^{-1}\right)\amalg F}_{H},

where
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
are finite subsets of non-trivial elements of
G
(in particular, one has

xN_{1}x^{-1}\neq N_{1}
and
yN_{2}y^{-1}\neq N_{2}
for every
x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
), and
F
is a (possibly trivial) free pro-
p
group.
Thus, by (
5.1
) one has

\mathrm{H}^{2}(N,\mathbb{Z}/p)=\mathrm{H}^{2}(N_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(N_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(H,\mathbb{Z}/p).

For
i=1,2
, let
c_{i}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z}/p)\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z}/p)
denote the map induced by cup-product with
\alpha_{i}
.
By (
5.1
), for every
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
one has

\begin{split}c_{\alpha}(\alpha^{\prime})&=\left(\alpha^{\prime}|_{G_{1}}+\alpha^{\prime}|_{G_{2}}\right)\smallsmile(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\\
&=(\alpha^{\prime}|_{G_{1}}\smallsmile\alpha_{1})+(\alpha^{\prime}|_{G_{2}}\smallsmile\alpha_{2})\\
&=c_{1}(\alpha^{\prime}|_{G_{1}})+c_{2}(\alpha^{\prime}|_{G_{2}}).\end{split}

Therefore,
\Img(c_{\alpha})=\Img(c_{1})\oplus\Img(c_{2})
.
On the other hand, by (
5.1
) for every
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
one has
\beta=\mathrm{r}_{G,G_{1}}(\beta)+\mathrm{r}_{G,G_{2}}(\beta)
, and since
N_{i}=N\cap G_{i}
for both
i=1,2
, one has

\begin{split}\mathrm{r}_{G,N}(\beta)&=\mathrm{r}_{G,N}\left(\mathrm{r}_{G,G_{1}}(\beta)+\mathrm{r}_{G,G_{2}}(\beta)\right)\\
&=\mathrm{r}_{G_{1},N_{1}}\left(\mathrm{r}_{G,G_{1}}(\beta)\right)+\mathrm{r}_{G_{2},N_{2}}\left(\mathrm{r}_{G,G_{2}}(\beta)\right).\end{split}

Consequently,
\Ker(\mathrm{r}_{G,N})=\Ker(\mathrm{r}_{G_{1},N_{1}})\oplus\Ker(G_{2},N_{2})
, which is equal to
\Img(c_{1})\oplus\Img(c_{2})
, as by hypothesis both
G_{1},G_{2}
are of
p
-absolute Galois type.
This concludes the proof.
∎

(ii)

\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p

(iii)

the cup-product induces a non-degenerate bilinear form

[

.

).
In particular, the only finite Demushkin group
G
occurs in case
p=2
and
\dim(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p))=1
, i.e.,
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2

[
30
, Prop. 3.9.10]
).
For more properties of Demushkin groups we direct the reader to
[

, Ch. III, § 9]

One knows that any Demushkin group has the strong
n
-Massey vanishing property for every

(cf.
[
31
, Thm. 3.5]

[

).
We show that, in addition, any Demushkin group is of
p

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
G
be a Demushkin group.
Then
G
is of
p
-absolute Galois type.

Proof.

Condition (iii) in the definition of Demushkin group implies that for any
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
,
\alpha\neq 0
, one has

(5.3)

\alpha\smallsmile\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)=\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p).

Put
N=\Ker(\alpha)
.
Then by (
5.3
), the map
c_{\alpha}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
is surjective.
Thus, by (
3.3
) one has
\Img(c_{\alpha})=\Ker(\mathrm{r}_{G,N})
, so that (
1.1
) is exact at
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
.
By Theorem
3.6
–(i), this is sufficient to show that
G
is of
p
-absolute Galois type.
Still, here we provide an explicit proof of the fact that, if
\dim(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p))
is even, the sequence (
1.1
) is exact at
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
.
(Recall that if
p\neq 2
then
\dim(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p))
is necessarily even.)

So, let
G
be a Demushkin group with
d=\dim(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p))
even, and pick
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
,
\alpha\neq 0
.
Since the cup-product induces a non-degenerate bilinear form,
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
decomposes as a direct sum of hyperbolic planes, and thus we may complete
\{\alpha\}
to a basis
\{\alpha_{1}=\alpha,\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\}
of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
such that

(5.4)

\alpha_{1}\smallsmile\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}\smallsmile\alpha_{4}=\ldots=\alpha_{d-1}\smallsmile\alpha_{d}\neq 0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha_{i}\smallsmile\alpha_{j}=0

for any other couple of
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq d
.
Thus,
\Ker(c_{\alpha_{1}})=\mathrm{Span}\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\}
.

Let
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}
be a minimal generating set of
G
such that
\alpha_{i}(y_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
Then by (
5.4
) one has an equivalence

(5.5)

[y_{1},y_{2}][y_{3},y_{4}]\cdots[y_{d-1},y_{d}]\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{pb_{i}}\equiv 1\mod F^{(3)},

for some
b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}/p
(cf.
[
30
, Prop. 3.9.13–(ii)]
).

Set
N=\Ker(\alpha_{1})
— so,
N
is generated as a normal subgroup of
G
by the set
\{y_{1}^{p},y_{2},\ldots,y_{d}\}
.
Then
N
is again a Demushkin group, with

\dim\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{Z}/p)\right)=2+p(d-1)

(cf.
[
30
, Thm. 3.9.15]
).
Moreover, (
5.5
) implies that
[y_{2},y_{1}]\equiv y_{1}^{pb_{1}}\bmod\Phi(N)
, and therefore the set

\mathcal{Y}=\left\{\>y_{1}^{p},\>y_{2},\>y_{1}^{\nu}y_{i}y_{1}^{-\nu}\>\mid\>3\leq i\leq d,\>0\leq\nu\leq p-1\>\right\}

is a minimal generating set of
N
.
Let
\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3,0},\psi_{3,1},\ldots,\psi_{d,p-1}\}
be the basis of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{Z}/p)
dual to
\mathcal{Y}
, and consider
\{1,y_{1},\ldots,y_{1}^{p-1}\}
as a set of representatives of the quotient
G/N
.
For every
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{Z}/p)
and every
x\in G
one has the formula

(5.6)

\mathrm{cor}_{N,G}^{1}(\alpha^{\prime})(x)=\sum_{h=0}^{p-1}\alpha^{\prime}\left(y^{-h^{\prime}}xy_{1}^{h}\right),

where
0\leq h^{\prime}\leq p-1
is such that
xy_{1}^{h}N=y_{1}^{h^{\prime}}N
(cf.
[
30
, Ch. I, § 5.4]
).
Then (
5.6
) implies that
\mathrm{cor}_{N,G}^{1}(\psi_{1})=\alpha_{1}
and
\mathrm{cor}_{N,G}^{1}(\psi_{i,\nu})=\alpha_{i}
for every
3\leq i\leq d
and
0\leq\nu\leq p-1
, while
\mathrm{cor}_{N,G}^{1}(\psi_{2})=0
.
Namely,
\Img(\mathrm{cor}_{N,G}^{1})=\Ker(c_{\alpha_{1}})
.

5.3.
Direct products

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group whose abelianization
G/G^{\prime}
is a free abelian pro-
p
group.
If
p=2
, then the natural map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/4)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)
is surjective, so that
\alpha\smallsmile\alpha=0
for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)
(cf., e.g.,
[
34
, Fact. 7.1]
; if
p>2
then one has
\alpha\smallsmile\alpha=0
trivially).

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group whose abelianization
G/G^{\prime}
is a free abelian pro-
p
group.
Then for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
and for every
n\geq 2
, the
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha,\ldots,\alpha\rangle
vanishes.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
–(i), we may suppose that
\alpha\neq 0
.
Let
\pi\colon G\to G/G^{\prime}
denote the canonical projection, and let
\bar{\alpha}\colon G/G^{\prime}\to\mathbb{Z}/p
be the morphism such that
\alpha=\bar{\alpha}\circ\pi
.
Moreover, pick
g\in G
such that
\alpha(g)=1
.
Then one has

G/G^{\prime}=\langle\>\pi(g)\>\rangle\times B,\qquad\text{for some }B\subseteq\Ker(\bar{\alpha}),

while
\langle\pi(g)\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Let
\rho^{\prime}\colon G/G^{\prime}\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
be the representation such that

\rho^{\prime}(\pi(g))=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
&1&1&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
&&&1&1\\
&&&&1\end{array}\right)

and
\rho^{\prime}|_{B}\equiv I_{n+1}
.
Then, the composition
\rho=\rho^{\prime}\circ\pi\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
is a homomorphism satisfying
\rho_{i,i+1}=\alpha
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
, and Proposition
3.4
yields the claim.
∎

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two pro-
p
groups, and let
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
be their direct product.
Then for the
\mathbb{Z}/p
-cohomology algebra of
G
one has the following:

(5.7)

\begin{split}\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)&=\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p),\\
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)&=\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)\oplus\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)\wedge\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)\right),\end{split}

(cf.
[
30
, Ch. II, § 4, Thm. 2.4.6 and Ex. 7]
).
In particular, if
\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{d_{1}}\}
and
\{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{d_{2}}\}
are bases of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)
respectively, then

\left\{\>\chi_{i}\smallsmile\psi_{j}\>\mid\>1\leq i\leq d_{1},\>1\leq j\leq d_{2}\>\right\}

is a basis of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)\wedge\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be two pro-
p
groups with torsion-free abelianization, and set
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
.

(i)

If both
G_{1},G_{2}
have the
n
-Massey vanishing property for every
n\geq 3
, then also
G
has the
n
-Massey vanishing property for every
n\geq 3
.

(ii)

If both
G_{1},G_{2}
have the
p
-cyclic Massey vanishing property, then also
G
has the
p
-cyclic Massey vanishing property.

Proof.

First of all, observe that by (
5.7
) if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
have trivial cup-product
\alpha\smallsmile\beta
, and
\alpha,\beta
do not lie in the same subgroup
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z}/p)
, then necessarily
\beta=a\alpha
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}/p
.
In this case, by Proposition
3.1
–(i) we may assume that
a=1
or
a=0
.
Moreover, obviously the abelianization
G/G^{\prime}\simeq G_{1}/G_{1}^{\prime}\times G_{2}/G_{2}^{\prime}
of
G
is a free abelian pro-
p
group.

(i) Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be a sequence of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
such that the
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
is defined — by Proposition
3.1
, we may assume that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Then
\alpha_{i}\smallsmile\alpha_{i+1}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, and by Proposition
3.4
–(i) there exists a representation
\bar{\rho}\colon G\to\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}
such that
\rho_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

If
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)
, then the
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1}|_{G_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{n}|_{G_{2}}\rangle
is defined in
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{1})
— indeed,
\bar{\rho}|_{G_{1}}\colon G_{1}\to\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}
is a representation satisfying
(\bar{\rho}|_{G_{1}})_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}|_{G_{1}}
— and thus, by hypothesis,
\langle\alpha_{1}|_{G_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{n}|_{G_{2}}\rangle
vanishes, yielding a representation
\rho^{\prime}\colon G_{1}\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
satisfying
\rho^{\prime}_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}|_{G_{1}}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Then, the representation
\rho\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
given by
\rho|_{G_{1}}=\rho^{\prime}
and
G_{2}\subseteq\Ker(\rho)
satisfies
\rho_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
, and thus
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
vanishes by Proposition
3.4
.
Analogously, if
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)
then
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
vanishes.

Otherwise, we may assume that
\alpha_{1}=\ldots=\alpha_{n}
, and the claim follows by Proposition
5.5
.

(ii) Pick two non-trivial elements
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/p)
such that
\alpha\smallsmile\beta=0
.

If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z}/p)
, then by hypothesis the
p
-fold Massey product

\langle\alpha|_{G_{1}},\ldots,\alpha|_{G_{1}},\beta|_{G_{1}}\rangle

vanishes in
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{1})
, as

(\alpha|_{G_{1}})\smallsmile(\beta|_{G_{1}})=\mathrm{r}_{G,G_{1}}(\alpha\smallsmile\beta)=0

(by the functoriality of the restriction map).
Thus, by Proposition
3.4
–(ii) there exists a representation
\rho^{\prime}\colon G_{1}\to\mathbb{U}_{p+1}
satisfying
\rho^{\prime}_{i,i+1}=\alpha
for
i=1,\ldots,p+1
, and
\rho^{\prime}_{p,p+1}=\beta
.
As done above, we may define a representation
\rho\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{p+1}
such that
\rho|_{G_{1}}=\rho^{\prime}
and
G_{2}\subseteq\Ker(\rho)
, so that
\rho_{i,i+1}=\alpha
for
i=1,\ldots,p-1
and
\rho_{p,p+1}=\beta
.
Therefore, the
p
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha,\ldots,\alpha,\beta\rangle
vanishes in
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)
.
Analogously, if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z}/p)
then the
p
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha,\ldots,\alpha,\beta\rangle
vanishes in
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)
.

Otherwise, if
\beta=a\alpha
for some
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
, then we may assume that
a=1
, and the
p
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha,\ldots,\alpha\rangle
vanishes in
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)
by Proposition
5.5
.
∎

The
Elementary Type Conjecture
on maximal pro-
p
Galois groups, formulated by I. Efrat, predicts that the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a field containing a root of 1 of order
p
may be constructible — if it is finitely generated — starting from free pro-
p
groups and Demushkin groups (and also the cyclic group of order 2, if
p=2
), and iterating free pro-
p
products and certain semidirect products with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(cf.
[
9
,
10
]
, see also
[
34
, § 7.5]
).
In case of fields containing
all
roots of 1 of
p
-power order, then a finitely generated maximal pro-
p
Galois group shoud be constructible starting from free pro-
p
groups and Demushkin groups
with torsion-free abelianization
, iterating free pro-
p
products and
direct
products with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Therefore, from Theorem
5.6
–(ii), together with the aforementioned results contained in
[
29
, § 4]
, one deduces the following.

Corollary 5.7
.

If the Elementary Type Conjecture holds true, then the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
of a field
\mathbb{K}
containing all roots of 1 of
p
-power order has the strong
n
-Massey vanishing property for every
n\geq 3
, provided that
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is finitely generated.

In other words, Efrat’s Elementary Type Conjecture implies a strengthened version of Minač-Tân’s conjecture
[

.

Let

be two pro-

Then the direct product

Suppose that

occurs as the maximal pro-
p

containing a root of 1 of order

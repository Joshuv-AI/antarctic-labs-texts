On a charged spinless point particle minimally coupled to a constant magnetic field in a noncommutative plane
S. Hasibul Hassan Chowdhury, Talal Ahmed Chowdhury

Abstract
In this paper, we provide a mathematically and physically consistent minimal prescription for a charged spinless point particle coupled to a constant magnetic field in a 2-dimensional noncommutative plane. It turns out to be a gauge invariant prescription in contrast to the widely and carelessly used naive minimal prescription in the context of 2-dimensional quantum mechanics in a noncommutative plane. Besides, we explore the noncommutative U(1) gauge theoretic structure of the underlying noncommutative system by explicitly computing the 1-parameter family of Seiberg-Witten maps.

II
Minimal prescription for quantum mechanics in noncommutative space

There are two equivalent ways of tackling the quantum mechanical problem of a charged spinless point particle placed in a noncommutative plane under the influence of a perpendicular constant magnetic field
B
(
[
10
]
). In one approach, one has the following set of commutation relations to start with

\begin{split}[\hat{X}^{s},\hat{\Pi}^{r,s}_{x}]=[\hat{Y}^{s},\hat{\Pi}^{r,s}_{y}]=i\hbar\hat{\mathbb{I}},\;\;[\hat{\Pi}^{r,s}_{x},\hat{\Pi}^{r,s}_{y}]=i\hbar B\hat{\mathbb{I}},\;\;[\hat{X}^{s},\hat{Y}^{s}]=i\vartheta\hat{\mathbb{I}}.\end{split}

(2.1)

Here,
\hat{X}^{s}
,
\hat{Y}^{s}
,
\hat{\Pi}^{r,s}_{x}
and
\hat{\Pi}^{r,s}_{y}
are some families of self-adjoint unbounded operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},dx\;dy)
representing the non-central generators of a nilpotent Lie group denoted by
G_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
(
[
3
]
). These families of operators are indexed by
2
parameters
r
and
s
in such a way (see equation (2.8) of
[
14
]
) that they disappear from the right side of the commutators given in (
2.1
). This is the reason the 2 parameters
r,s
were called gauge parameters in
[
14
]
. Certain values of the ordered pair
(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there, were seen to produce the symmetric and Landau gauge representations of
G_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
. Also,
\hat{\mathbb{I}}
is the identity operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},dx\;dy)
. In this approach, the magnetic field
B
appears as one of the 3 parameters (
\hbar,\vartheta,B
) labeling the unitary dual of
G_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
. This approach has been studied extensively in
[
14
]
. The 2 parameter (
r,s
) family of irreducible representations of
G_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
in terms of the self-adjoint operators
\hat{X}^{s}
,
\hat{Y}^{s}
,
\hat{\Pi}_{x}^{r,s}
,
\hat{\Pi}_{y}^{r,s}
satisfying the commutation relations
2.1
are easily seen to be gauge equivalent, i.e., operators corresponding to distinct ordered pairs
(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
are
unitarily equivalent
.

In the other approach, one starts with a noncommutative plane characterized by the following one parameter family of equivalent irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{g}_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
, the Lie algebra of
G_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
:

\begin{split}\hat{X}^{r}&=\hat{x}+\frac{(r-1)\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{y},\\
\hat{Y}^{r}&=\hat{y}+\frac{r\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{x},\\
\hat{\Pi}_{x}&=\hat{p}_{x},\\
\hat{\Pi}_{y}&=\hat{p}_{y}.\end{split}

(2.2)

The ordered triple
(\hbar,\vartheta,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the unitary dual of
G_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
determines the underlying equivalence class of irreducible unitary representations of
G_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}}
. Here, the quantum mechanical position operators
\hat{x}
and
\hat{y}
act on a generic element
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},dx\;dy)
in the following way:

\begin{split}(\hat{x}\psi)(x,y)&=x\psi(x,y),\\
(\hat{y}\psi)(x,y)&=y\psi(x,y),\end{split}

(2.3)

while the quantum mechanical momenta operators
\hat{p}_{x}
and
\hat{p}_{y}
act as derivations on a generic element
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},dx\;dy)
in the following way:

\begin{split}(\hat{p}_{x}\psi)(x,y)&=-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}(x,y),\\
(\hat{p}_{y}\psi)(x,y)&=-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}(x,y).\end{split}

(2.4)

The representation of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\hbox{\tiny{NC}}})
given in (
2.2
) obeys the following set of commutation relations:

\begin{split}&[\hat{X}^{r},\hat{Y}^{r}]=i\vartheta\hat{\mathbb{I}}\;,\;[\hat{X}^{r},\hat{\Pi}_{x}]=i\hbar\hat{\mathbb{I}},\\
&[\hat{Y}^{r},\hat{\Pi}_{y}]=i\hbar\hat{\mathbb{I}}\;,\;[\hat{\Pi}_{x},\hat{\Pi}_{y}]=0.\end{split}

(2.5)

Now, one couples the nonrelativistic spinless point particle of mass
m
situated in the noncommutative plane given by (
2.2
) to a constant magnetic field
B
minimally (
e
being the coupling) through the following 1-parameter family of representations:

\begin{split}&\hat{X}^{r}=\hat{x}+\frac{(r-1)\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{y},\\
&\hat{Y}^{r}=\hat{y}+\frac{r\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{x},\\
&\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{x}=\frac{2(1-r)e\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{y}+\left[1+\frac{2r(1-r)e\vartheta B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\right]\hat{p}_{x},\\
&\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{y}=\frac{-2re\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{x}+\left[1+\frac{2r(1-r)e\vartheta B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\right]\hat{p}_{y}.\end{split}

(2.6)

Now the minimal coupling of the noncommutative plane given by (
2.2
) to the electromagnetic field can be seen by a simple manipulation of (
2.6
):

\begin{split}&\hat{X}^{r}=\hat{x}+\frac{(r-1)\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{y},\\
&\hat{Y}^{r}=\hat{y}+\frac{r\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{x},\\
&\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{x}=\hat{p}_{x}+\frac{2e(1-r)\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\left(\hat{y}+\frac{r\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{x}\right),\\
&\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{y}=\hat{p}_{y}-\frac{2re\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\left[\hat{x}+\frac{(r-1)\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{y}\right].\end{split}

(2.7)

The representation (
2.7
) satisfy the following set of commutation relations:

\begin{split}&[\hat{X}^{r},\hat{Y}^{r}]=i\vartheta\hat{\mathbb{I}},\hskip 10.84006pt[\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{x},\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{y}]=ie\hbar B\hat{\mathbb{I}},\\
&[\hat{X}^{r},\hat{\Pi}_{x}^{r}]=\left[1+\frac{2(1-r)e\vartheta B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\right]i\hbar\hat{\mathbb{I}},\\
&[\hat{Y}^{r},\hat{\Pi}_{y}^{r}]=\left[1+\frac{2re\vartheta B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\right]i\hbar\hat{\mathbb{I}}.\end{split}

(2.8)

Writing
\hat{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{r}\equiv(\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{x},\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{y})
and
\hat{\mathbf{p}}\equiv(\hat{p}_{x},\hat{p}_{y})
, one obtains from the expressions of kinematical momenta provided by
(
2.7
) that

\hat{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{r}=\hat{\mathbf{p}}-e\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{r},

(2.9)

where the noncommutative U(1) gauge fields are given by

\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{r}=\left(\frac{-2(1-r)\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{Y}^{r},\frac{2r\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{X}^{r}\right).

(2.10)

The familiar symmetric and Landau gauges correspond to the following values of
r
:

r_{\hbox{\tiny{sym}}}=\frac{1}{2},\;\hbox{and}\;r_{\hbox{\tiny{Lan}}}=1.

The gauge field expressions can be read off using (
2.10
) as

\begin{split}&\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(-\frac{\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}+e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{Y}^{\frac{1}{2}},\frac{\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}+e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),\\
&\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{1}=(0,B\hat{x}).\end{split}

(2.11)

The representation (
2.6
) takes the following form in Landau gauge:

\begin{split}\hat{X}^{1}=\hat{x},\;\;\hat{Y}^{1}=\hat{y}+\frac{\vartheta}{\hbar}\hat{p}_{x},\;\;\hat{\Pi}_{x}^{1}=\hat{p}_{x},\;\;\hat{\Pi}_{y}^{2}=-eB\hat{x}+\hat{p}_{y},\end{split}

(2.12)

while in the symmetric gauge (
2.6
) looks as follow:

\begin{split}&\hat{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\hat{x}-\frac{\vartheta}{2\hbar}\hat{p}_{y},\\
&\hat{Y}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\hat{y}+\frac{\vartheta}{2\hbar}\hat{p}_{x},\\
&\hat{\Pi}_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{e\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}+e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{y}+\left[1+\frac{e\vartheta B}{2(\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}+e\hbar\vartheta B})}\right]\hat{p}_{x},\\
&\hat{\Pi}_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{e\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}+e\hbar\vartheta B}}\hat{x}+\left[1+\frac{e\vartheta B}{2(\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}+e\hbar\vartheta B})}\right]\hat{p}_{y}.\end{split}

(2.13)

Remark II.1

\partial_{x}\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{y}-\partial_{y}\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{x}-\frac{ie}{\hbar}[\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{x},\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{y}]=B\hat{\mathbb{I}}=:\hat{F}_{xy},

III
Star product approach to quantum mechanics on a noncommutative space

We consider the function space to be
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Now, we define
4
functions
x,y,\Pi_{x},\Pi_{y}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by means of each of their noncommutative
*
product with some other (sufficiently well-behaved) vector
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},dx\;dy)
as follow:

\begin{split}&x*^{r}\psi:=\hat{X}^{r}\psi=x\psi-i(r-1)\vartheta\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y},\\
&y*^{r}\psi:=\hat{Y}^{r}\psi=y\psi-ir\vartheta\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x},\\
&\Pi_{x}*^{r}\psi:=\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{x}\psi=\frac{2(1-r)e\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}y\psi-i\hbar\left[1+\frac{2r(1-r)e\vartheta B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\right]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x},\\
&\Pi_{y}*^{r}\psi:=\hat{\Pi}^{r}_{y}\psi=\frac{-2re\hbar B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}x\psi-i\hbar\left[1+\frac{2r(1-r)e\vartheta B}{\hbar+\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-4r(r-1)e\hbar\vartheta B}}\right]\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}.\end{split}

(3.1)

The last two equations in (
3.1
) merit elaboration: the
2
functions
\Pi_{x}
and
\Pi_{y}
are actually derived objects using the relations
\Pi_{x}=p_{x}-e\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{x}
and
\Pi_{y}=p_{y}-e\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{y}
. The 2 smooth functions
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
are given by the following star-products with sufficiently well-behaved wave function
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},dx\;dy)
:

p_{x}*\psi=\hat{p}_{x}\psi=-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x},\;\;\;\;p_{y}*\psi=\hat{p}_{y}\psi=-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}.

(3.2)

And, the vector valued function

N. Seiberg and E. Witten were the first to provide a set of formulae in

We will be using

instead of

for the sake of notational clarity to denote the redefined commutative gauge field, i.e., the 1-parameter family of commutative Seiberg-Witten gauge fields.

(4.11)

and

F_{xy}=\partial_{x}\mathbf{A}_{y}-\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}_{x}=\mathfrak{B}\mathbb{I}=-F_{yx},

(4.12)

are the entries of the following
2\times 2
antisymmetric real matrix
F
representing the commutative Seiberg-Witten field strength:

F=\begin{bmatrix}0&\partial_{x}\mathbf{A}_{y}-\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}_{x}\\
\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}_{x}-\partial_{x}\mathbf{A}_{y}&0\end{bmatrix}.

(4.13)

Also, from (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{y}

\displaystyle=r\frac{\hbar}{2r(1-r)e\vartheta}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{4r(r-1)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}}{\hbar}}}-1\right]x,

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{2(1-r)e\vartheta}\left[\left\{1+\frac{4r(r-1)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}}{\hbar}\right\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\right]x,

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar x}{2(1-r)e\vartheta}\left[-\frac{2r(r-1)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}}{\hbar}+\frac{6r^{2}(r-1)^{2}e^{2}\vartheta^{2}\mathfrak{B}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}-\cdots\right],

\displaystyle=r\mathfrak{B}x+\frac{3r^{2}(1-r)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}^{2}x}{\hbar}+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}),

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{y}+\frac{2e(r-1)}{\hbar}\theta^{yx}\mathbf{A}_{y}\partial_{x}\mathbf{A}_{y}+\frac{er}{\hbar}\theta^{yx}\mathbf{A}_{y}\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}_{x}+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}),

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{y}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\theta^{yx}\mathbf{A}_{y}\left[(2-3r)\partial_{x}\mathbf{A}_{y}+rF_{xy}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}).

It now remains to compare the noncommutative Seiberg-Witten field strength with its commutative counterpart. The noncommutative Seiberg-Witten field strength
F^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{xy}
is given by

F^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{xy}=\partial_{x}\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{y}-\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{x}-\frac{ie}{\hbar}[\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{x}\!\!\overset{\;\;*^{r}}{,}\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{y}]=B\mathbb{I},

(4.15)

with
\mathbb{I}
being the real valued constant map in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
that maps everything to
1
. Here, we used (
3.21
) to arrive at (
4.15
).

Now, using (
3.25
) in (
4.15
), one obtains

\displaystyle F^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{xy}

\displaystyle=\bar{B}\left[1-\frac{r(r-1)e\vartheta\bar{B}}{\hbar}\right]\mathbb{I},

(4.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{2r(1-r)e\vartheta}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{4r(r-1)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}}{\hbar}}}-1\right]\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{4r(r-1)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}}{\hbar}}}-1\right\}\right]\mathbb{I},

upon using (
4.4
). Let

\frac{4r(r-1)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}}{\hbar}=t

(4.17)

in (
4.16
) and simplify.

\displaystyle F^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{xy}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mathfrak{B}}{t}\left[1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t}}\right]\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t}}-1\right)\right]\mathbb{I},

(4.18)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{B}}{t}\left[1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t}}\right]\left[1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t}}\right]\mathbb{I},

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mathfrak{B}}{1+t}\right)\mathbb{I}.

Substituting back the value of
t
from (
4.17
) in (
4.18
), one finally obtains

\displaystyle F^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{xy}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}\left[1-\frac{4r(1-r)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}}{\hbar}\right]^{-1}\mathbb{I},

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}\mathbb{I}+\frac{4r(1-r)e\vartheta\mathfrak{B}^{2}}{\hbar}\mathbb{I}+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}),

\displaystyle=F_{xy}+\frac{4er(1-r)}{\hbar}\theta^{yx}F_{xy}F_{yx}+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}).

Here,
F_{xy}=-F_{yx}
are as given in (
4.12
). Equations (
4.10
), (
4.14
) and (
4.19
) tell us how noncommutative U(1) gauge field and Field strength are related with their commutative counterparts in the first order of the deformation parameter
\vartheta
in a chosen gauge parametrized by a real value of
r
. These equations take the following familiar forms in the symmetric gauge configuration corresponding to
r=\frac{1}{2}
:

\begin{split}\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{x}&=\mathbf{A}_{x}-\frac{e}{2\hbar}\theta^{xy}\mathbf{A}_{x}(\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}_{x}+F_{yx})+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}),\\
\mathbf{A}^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{y}&=\mathbf{A}_{y}-\frac{e}{2\hbar}\theta^{yx}\mathbf{A}_{y}(\partial_{x}\mathbf{A}_{y}+F_{xy})+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}),\\
F^{\hbox{\tiny{nc}}}_{xy}&=F_{xy}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\theta^{yx}F_{xy}F_{yx}+\mathcal{O}(\vartheta^{2}).\end{split}

(4.20)

We call the 1-parameter family of maps given by (
4.14
), (
4.10
) and (
4.19
), the
Seiberg-Witten maps
.

V
Conclusion and Future Perspectives

In this paper, we have undertaken noncommutative U(1) gauge theoretic study of a spinless point particle placed in a noncommutative plane minimally coupled to an external uniform magnetic field. In the process, we have seen how the gauge parameter
r
gives rise to
c
-equivalent star products
*^{r}
in the sense of
[
15
]
. We also have obtained an induced family of invertible (parametrized by the same gauge parameter
r
) maps that relate the noncommutative U(1) gauge field and the corresponding field strength with their commutative counterparts. These invertible maps for
r=\frac{1}{2}
agrees with the so-called Seiberg-Witten map constructed in
[
11
]
.

Geometrically, one has a 1-dimensional
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{A}
where the noncommutative
*
-algebra is
(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),*^{r})
. Connections on
\mathbb{M}
are then maps
\nabla:\hbox{Der}(\mathcal{A})\times\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
satisfying some nice properties (see appendix of
[
19
]
). There is a more interesting construction of deformation of principal G bundle
\hbox{pr}:\mathcal{P}\circlearrowleft G\rightarrow\mathcal{M}

[
20
,
21
]
where
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth manifold. According to the construction presented in
[
22
]
, if one has a
\star
product defined on
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.,
\star:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})[[\lambda]]\times C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})[[\lambda]]\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})[[\lambda]]
with
\lambda
being the deformation parameter, then one has an induced right
\star
-module structure
\bullet
on
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})[[\lambda]]
by

F\bullet f=F\hbox{pr}^{*}f+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{r}\varrho_{n}(F,f),

(5.1)

with
\varrho_{n}:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})\times C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is a bidifferential operator along the map
pr
for all
r\geq 1
such that one also has the
G
-equivariance

g^{*}(F\bullet f)=g^{*}F\bullet f.

(5.2)

Here,
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
,
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
g\in G
. Also,
\hbox{pr}^{*}:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
and the right action of the group
G
on
\mathcal{P}
induces a map
g^{*}:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.

We look forward to studying this construction in the present context where,
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\lambda=\vartheta
,
G=U(1)
and
\star=*^{r}
. We would like to define an equivalence class of
*^{r}
-module structures
\bullet^{r}
in the sense of
[
22
]
and study its resulting algebraic properties in a future publication.

Furthermore, the Landau problem is formulated for the particle moving in a two-dimensional field. However, it can be extended to the higher dimensional space as shown, for example, in
[
28
,
29
]
. Besides, the magnetic field can be non-uniform, as pointed out in
[
10
]
. It will be interesting to explore the noncommutative generalization of the higher dimensional case and the presence of a non-uniform magnetic field which we have set for a future publication.

Damped Euler system with attractive Riesz interaction forces
Young-Pil Choi, Jinwook Jung, Yoonjung Lee

Abstract
. We consider the barotropic Euler equations with pairwise attractive Riesz interactions and linear velocity damping in the periodic domain. We establish the global-in-time well-posedness theory for the system near an equilibrium state. We also analyze the large-time behavior of solutions showing the exponential rate of convergence toward the equilibrium state as time goes to infinity.

Primary: 76N10; Secondary: 35Q31

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we analyze the barotropic Euler equations with pairwise attractive Riesz interactions and linear damping in velocity. More precisely, let
\rho=\rho(x,t)
and
u=u(x,t)
be the density and velocity of the fluid at time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and position
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
, respectively. Then our main system reads as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad t>0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p=-\nu\rho u+\lambda\rho\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}(\rho-c),

\displaystyle p=p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma},\quad\gamma\geq 1,

supplemented with the initial data

(1.2)

(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=:(\rho_{0},u_{0}),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

The right-hand side of the momentum equations consists of the linear damping in velocity with
\nu>0
and the Riesz interactions, where the Riesz operator
\Lambda^{s}
is defined by
(-\Delta)^{s/2}
. For
\alpha
, we deal with the super Coulombic case:

\max\{d-2,0\}\leq\alpha<d.

In this case, we observe

\Lambda^{\alpha-d}(\rho-c)=c_{\alpha,d}K_{\alpha}*(\rho-c),\quad\mbox{with}\quad K_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{|x|^{-\alpha}}{\alpha}

for some
c_{\alpha,d}>0
. Here we use the convention
K_{\alpha}(x)=\log|x|
for
\alpha=0
. Note that the case
\alpha=d-2
corresponds to the Coulombic interactions when
d\geq 3
, and the potential
K
is called a pure Manev interaction potential when
\alpha=d-1
, see
[
1
,
7
,
11
,
12
]
.

The Riesz interaction force is attractive if
\lambda>0
and repulsive if
\lambda<0
.
In the current work, we consider the case of attractive interactions, i.e.,
\lambda>0
. The background state
c
is given as a fixed positive constant, and we impose the neutrality condition:

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho-c)\,dx=0.

Since the total mass is conserved in time, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\rho
is a probability density function, i.e.,
\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{1}}=1
for all
t\geq 0
, and subsequently, we set
c=1
.

In the present work, we study the global Cauchy problem for the system (
1.1
). To be more specific, we are concerned with the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of regular solutions to the system (
1.1
) and its large-time behavior under suitable assumptions on the initial data. To state our main results and compare them with existing results, inspired by
[
1
,
7
,
11
,
12
]
, we consider two cases

1.1.
Reformulations and notations

In order to present our main results, we first reduce the system (
1.1
) to a symmetric system. For the case
\gamma>1
, we consider the sound speed of
\rho
:

\displaystyle s(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho)}.

We let

h=N(s(\rho)-s(1))\quad\text{and}\quad N:=\frac{2}{\gamma-1}.

Setting
\sigma:=\gamma^{1/2}N
, we can write
h=\sigma(\rho^{1/N}-1)
, and the system (
1.1
) with initial data (
1.2
) can be rewritten as a symmetric form:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h+u\cdot\nabla h+\frac{1}{N}(h+\sigma)(\nabla\cdot u)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad t>0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\frac{1}{N}(h+\sigma)\nabla h=-\nu u+\sigma^{-N}\lambda\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\left\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\right\}

with initial data

(1.6)

(h,u)|_{t=0}=:(h_{0}:=\sigma(\rho_{0}^{1/N}-1),\,u_{0}),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Note that the neutrality condition (
1.3
) becomes

(1.7)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(h+\sigma)^{N}\,dx=\sigma^{N}.

On the other hand, when
\gamma=1
, we let

h:=\ln\rho
so that
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are transformed into the following system:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h+u\cdot\nabla h+\nabla\cdot u=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad t>0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla h=-\nu u+\lambda\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}(e^{h}-1),

with initial data

(h,u)|_{t=0}=:(h_{0}:=\ln\rho_{0},\,u_{0}),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Similarly, in this case, the neutrality condition becomes

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{h}dx=1.

Here we introduce our simplified notations. Throughout the paper, we write
\sigma=\gamma^{1/2}N
and
N=2/(\gamma-1)
. We frequently denote
\|\cdot\|_{X(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, and
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
by
\int
for simplicity.
We also denote
\|(h,u)(t)\|_{X}^{2}:=\|h(t)\|_{X}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{X}^{2}
,
We use
C
as a generic constant which does not depend on
\lambda
,
\nu
, and
t
,
and denote by
C_{\alpha,\beta,\cdots}
or
C(\alpha,\beta,\cdots)
a generic constant depending on
\alpha,\beta,...
.
We use the notation
A(t)\sim B(t)
in the sense that there exists a positive
C
independent of
t
such that
C^{-1}A(t)\leq B(t)\leq CA(t)
,
the notation
A\sim_{\alpha}B
in the sense
such that
C_{1}A\leq B\leq C_{2}A
for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
depending only on
\alpha
.

Remark 1.1
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the classical Sobolev spaces is defined under the norm

\|f\|_{H^{m}}^{2}:=\sum_{0\leq k\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for multi-index
\alpha
of order
|\alpha|=k
.
Here, one observes

\|f\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\sim_{d,m}\sum_{0\leq k\leq m}\|\Lambda^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq 0,

since

(1.8)

\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sim_{d,k}\|\Lambda^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad k\geq 1.

Indeed, we get

\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|n^{\alpha}|^{2}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}\leq d\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{2k}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}=d\|\Lambda^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

by Parseval’s theorem, and a direct calculation shows the reverse inequality

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{2k}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}\leq C_{d,k}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|n^{\alpha}|^{2}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}

from which, we derive (
1.8
).

1.2.
Main results

Our main results are two-fold. The first result concerns the global well-posedness of the system (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and the large-time behavior of the constructed solutions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma\geq 1
,
d\geq 1
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\max\{d-2,0\}\leq\alpha<d
.
For
m>1+d/2
, suppose that the initial data
(h_{0},u_{0})\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
satisfy

\|(h_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{m}}\leq\varepsilon

for some
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and
\lambda
is small enough. Then the system (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) admits a unique solution
(h,u)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.
Moreover, there exist
C,\tilde{\mu}>0
independent of
t
such that

(1.9)

\|(h,u)(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq Ce^{-C\tilde{\mu}t}

for
t\geq 0
.

Remark 1.2
.

Since the proof for the case
\gamma=1
is very similar to the case
\gamma>1
, we only present the details of the proof for the case
\gamma>1
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the case of pressureless and repulsive Riesz interaction, the solution space
(h,u)\in H^{m}\times H^{m+\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}
is considered in
[
9
]
. In our case, due to the presence of pressure, we cannot deal with solution space in which
h
and
u
have different regularities.

Remark 1.4
.

By the Sobolev embedding, Thereom
1.1
gives the existence and uniqueness of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-solutions
(h,u)
, and this together with Proposition
2.1
below yields the existence and uniqueness of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-solutions
(\rho,u)
to the system (
1.1
).

Our second main result is on the a priori temporal estimates on the lowest norm of the solution
(h,u)
. In the theorem above, the large time behavior of the classical solution
(h,u)
is obtained under the smallness assumption on initial data
(h_{0},u_{0})
. However, such a smallness condition is not necessary for the zeroth order norm of solutions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
(h,u)
be a global classical solution to (
1.5
) with sufficiently small
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
Assume that

(i)

\inf_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(x,t)+\sigma\geq h_{min}>0
and

(ii)

h,u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Then there exist
C,\mu>0
independent of
t
such that

(1.10)

\|(h,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-C\mu t}

for
t\geq 0
.

Remark 1.5
.

The lower bound assumption on
h+\sigma
in Theorem
1.2
corresponds to the assumption
\rho\geq\rho_{min}
for some
\rho_{min}>0
in the system (
1.1
). The condition (i) in Theorem
1.2
requires some smallness on the
L^{\infty}
norm of the negative part of
h
such that
\|h^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\sigma
.

Remark 1.6
.

The constants
C
and
\mu
appeared in Theorem
1.2
depend only on
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
h_{min}
. Formally speaking, it does not require any differentiability of solutions with respect to the spatial variable. Thus, one may use the argument used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
to show the large-time behavior of bounded solutions.

Remark 1.7
.

For the same reason as in Remark
1.2
, we only take into account the case
\gamma>1
for the proof of Theorem
1.2
. When
\gamma=1
, the condition (i) in Theorem
1.2
is not needed since
h\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
implies that
\rho=e^{h}\geq e^{-\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}}>0
.

1.3.
Ideas and strategies of the proof

Here, we give some technical ideas and strategies involved in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

1.3.1.
Global-in-time well-posedness and large-time behavior

As presented, we construct our solutions in the Sobolev space
H^{m}
. Due to the symmetric structure of the reformulated system (
1.5
), when we estimate
\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}
, the top order terms
(h+\sigma)(\nabla\cdot u)
and
(h+\sigma)\nabla h
are cancelled each other. Thus, as expected, it is important to analyze the Riesz interaction term. In the sub-Manev potential case, i.e.
\alpha\leq d-1
, roughly speaking,
\|\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\left\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\right\}\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}\lesssim\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}
under appropriate bound estimates, due to
m+1+\alpha-d\leq m
. This allows us to close the a priori estimates of solutions in
H^{m}
. However, in the super-Manev potential case, we get
m+1+\alpha-d>m
, and thus the interaction term cannot be handled by a similar energy estimate as in the sub-Manev potential case. To overcome this difficulty, we develop a novel technique to reduce the order of derivatives in the Riesz interaction term with the help of pressure. To be specific, we define a function

\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)=\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-(k+2)l}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

for any
0\leq k<m/l
and
0<l<1/2
with
l=(d-\alpha)/2
. Note that the term

\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)=\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int\Lambda^{m}u\cdot\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

appears in the estimate of
\|(h,u)\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}
. By an iteration argument, for
1\leq k_{0}<m/l
, we can derive

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)

\displaystyle\leq C\|(h,u)\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{k}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx,

where
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N^{2}
and
C=C(\lambda,\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}})>0
independent of
t
. In particular, here
C\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0
. Then we can close the
H^{m}
a priori estimate for the solution. On the other hand, for the global-in-time regularity of solutions, it is required to have appropriate dissipation rates for
h
and
u
. The linear velocity damping directly gives the dissipation term
\|u\|_{H^{m}}
in the estimate of
\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}
, however, the dissipation rate for
h
is not simply obtained from that estimate. For this, we employ a hypocoercivity-type estimate. We take into account the following integral:

\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx

with
s\leq m-1
which gives the dissipation rate for
\nabla h
in
H^{m-1}
. Note that for
\mu>0
small enough

\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\mu\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx\sim\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Then, together with adding the zeroth-order estimate of solutions and taking
\lambda>0
small enough, we derive

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{X}}_{m}+\xi{\mathcal{X}}_{m}\leq 0

for some
\xi>0
, where

{\mathcal{X}}_{m}:=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mu\int\nabla(\Lambda^{m-1}h)\cdot\Lambda^{m-1}u\,dx.

Since
{\mathcal{X}}_{m}\sim\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}
, we can have the uniform-in-time bound on
\|(h,u)(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}
, and moreover, it decays to zero exponentially fast as time goes to infinity.

1.3.2.
A priori large-time behavior

The proof of the large-time behavior estimate relies on the Lyapunov function method. To this end, we first consider the following modulated energy function
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(t)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:=

\displaystyle\int(h+\sigma)^{N}|u-m_{c}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{2}{N(N+2)}\int f\left(\,\frac{N+2}{N},(h+\sigma)^{N};\sigma^{N}\,\right)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{N}}\int|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}|m_{c}|^{2},

where
f(\gamma,r;r_{0})
and
m_{c}=m_{c}(t)
are given by

f(\gamma,r;r_{0}):=r\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{s^{\gamma}-r_{0}^{\gamma}}{s^{2}}\,ds

and

m_{c}:=\sigma^{-N}\int(h+\sigma)^{N}u\,dx,

respectively, for
r_{0}>0
,
\gamma\geq 1
, and
r\in[0,\tilde{r}]
with
\tilde{r}>0
. Then, we can show that
\mathcal{E}
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{D}=0,

where the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(t)
is given by

\mathcal{D}:=2\nu\int(h+\sigma)^{N}|u-m_{c}|^{2}\,dx+\nu|m_{c}|^{2}.

On the other hand, it follows from the symmetry of the operator
\Lambda^{\alpha-d}
that

m_{c}^{\prime}(t)=-\nu m_{c}(t),

and thus

m_{c}(t)\leq m_{c}(0)e^{-\nu t}.

Moreover, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, we can obtain

\mathcal{E}(t)\sim\|(h,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|m_{c}(t)|^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{D}(t)\sim\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|m_{c}(t)|^{2}.

Here we would like to remark that there is no dissipation rate for
h
in
\mathcal{D}
. Thus, to get the exponential decay of convergence of
\|(h,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we need to get some dissipation term
\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
in
\mathcal{D}
. Precisely, it suffices to have

\mathcal{D}(t)\gtrsim\|(h,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|m_{c}(t)|^{2}.

For this, motivated from
[
8
]
, we take into account a perturbed energy function
\mathcal{E}^{\mu}=\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(t)
:

\mathcal{E}^{\mu}:=\mathcal{E}+\mu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

for sufficiently small
\mu>0
, where the potential
W
is an even function explicitly written as

(1.11)

W(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle-c_{0}\log|x|+G_{0}(x)&\mbox{ when }&d=2,\\
c_{1}|x|^{2-d}+G_{1}(x)&\mbox{ when }&d\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

Here
c_{0}>0
and
c_{1}>0
are normalization constants and
G_{0}
and
G_{1}
are smooth functions over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(
d\geq 3
), respectively.
We notice that

\mathcal{E}(t)\sim\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(t)

for
\mu>0
small enough, and it is clear to get

(1.12)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(t)+\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t)=0,

where

\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t):=\mathcal{D}(t)-\mu\frac{d}{dt}\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx.

We then observe that the perturbed dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}^{\mu}
produces the required dissipation rate for
h
, in fact, we can show

\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t)\gtrsim\|(h,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|m_{c}(t)|^{2},

from which we have the result of Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.8
.

For any
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
\int g\,dx=0
,
U:=W*g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the unique function that satisfies the following condition:

\int U\,dx=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int\nabla U\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx=\int g\,\psi\,dx\quad\forall\,\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

i.e.
U
is the unique weak solution to
-\Delta U=g
.

2.1.
Local well-posedness

We first provide the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of regular solutions to the system (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
\max\{d-2,0\}\leq\alpha<d
.
For any
m>1+d/2
, suppose that
(h_{0},u_{0})\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
Then for any positive constants
\varepsilon_{1}<M_{0}
, there exists a positive constant
T_{0}>0
depending only on
\varepsilon_{1},M_{0}
such that if
\|(h_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{m}}<\varepsilon_{1}
, then the system (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) admits a unique solution
(h,u)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T_{0});H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
satisfying

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|(h,u)(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq M_{0}.

The above local well-posedness can be established by using a similar argument as in
[
6
]
, where the Euler-Riesz system is studied when
\rho
is integrable.

In the proposition below, we show a relation between two solutions
(\rho,u)
and
(h,u)
to the systems (
1.1
) and (
1.5
), respectively.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
\gamma>1
.
For any fixed
T>0
and given
\sigma=\gamma^{1/2}N
, if
(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T))
solves the system (
1.1
) with
\rho>0
,
then
(h,u)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T))
solves the system (
1.5
) with
h+\sigma>0
.
Conversely, if
(h,u)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T))
solves the system (
1.5
) with
h+\sigma>0
, then
(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T))
solves the system (
1.1
) with
\rho>0
.

In a straightforward way, we obtain the above proposition, where the positivity of the density
\rho
is obtained from the corresponding positivity of the density
(h+\sigma)
by using the method of characteristics. We refer to
[
16
]
for more details.

2.2.
Useful inequalities

In this part, we present the Moser-type and Sobolev-type inequalities in the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following relations hold.

(i)

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
f,g\in H^{k}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
then

(2.1)

\|\Lambda^{k}(fg)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{d,k}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{k}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}).

Moreover, if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, then there exists a positive constant
C=C(k,f)
such that

(2.2)

\|\Lambda^{k}(f\circ g)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{k-1}\|\Lambda^{k}g\|_{L^{2}}.

(ii)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
C_{s,\varepsilon}>0
depending only on
s,\varepsilon
such that

(2.3)

\|[\Lambda^{s}\partial_{x_{i}},g]f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{s,\varepsilon}(\|g\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1+s+\varepsilon}}\|f\|_{L^{2}})

and

(2.4)

\|[\Lambda^{s},g]f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{s,\varepsilon}(\|g\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{s}g\|_{L^{2}}).

Here,
[\cdot,\,\cdot]
denotes the commutator operator, i.e.
[A,B]:=AB-BA
.

Proof.

Since the inequality (
2.1
) is classical, we only deal with the commutator estimates appeared in (ii). The inequality (
2.3
) is obtained in
[
3
]
for the case
d=2
, and it can be readily checked that
it is also available for any
d\geq 1
. On the other hand, the proof of (
2.4
) follows a similar way to that in
[
3
]
, however, for completeness, we give details of the proof of (
2.4
).

By definition, we get

(2.5)

\widehat{[\Lambda^{s},g]f}(n)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(|n|^{s}-|n-m|^{s})\hat{f}(n-m)\hat{g}(m),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

We also observe

(2.6)

\displaystyle||n|^{s}-|n-m|^{s}|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dr}|nr+(n-m)(1-r)|^{s}\right|\leq|s|\int_{0}^{1}|nr+(n-m)(1-r)|^{s-1}|m|\,dr.

If
s\geq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\left||n|^{s}-|n-m|^{s}\right|

\displaystyle\leq s\left(|n|^{s-1}+|n-m|^{s-1}\right)|m|\leq\max\{s,2^{s-2}s\}(|n-m|^{s-1}|m|+|m|^{s})

due to

|n|^{s-1}\leq\max\{1,2^{s-2}\}(|n-m|^{s-1}+|m|^{s-1}).

By combining this with (
2.5
), we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{[\Lambda^{s},g]f}{(n)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big||n|^{s}-|n-m|^{s}\big|\,\big|\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big|\hat{g}(m)\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big||n-m|^{s-1}\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big|m\hat{g}(m)\big|+C\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big|\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big||m|^{s}\hat{g}(m)\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big|\widehat{\Lambda^{s-1}f}(n-m)\big|\,\big|\widehat{\nabla g}(m)\big|+C\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big|\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big|\widehat{\Lambda^{s}g}(m)\big|

\displaystyle=C(|\widehat{\Lambda^{s-1}f}|\ast|\widehat{\nabla g}|)(n)+C(|\hat{f}|\ast|\widehat{\Lambda^{s}g}|)(n).

By applying the Parseval theorem, (
2.7
) and then Young’s inequality in turn,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{s},g]f\|_{L^{2}}=\|\widehat{[\Lambda^{s},g]f}\|_{l^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\widehat{\Lambda^{s-1}f}\|_{l^{2}}\|\widehat{\nabla g}\|_{l^{1}}+C\|\hat{f}\|_{l^{1}}\|\widehat{\Lambda^{s}g}\|_{l^{2}}.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\Lambda^{k}f}(n)\|_{l^{1}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{k}\big|\widehat{f}(n)\big|\leq\Biggl(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|n|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+\varepsilon}}\Biggl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Biggl(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|(1+|n|^{2})|^{\frac{d}{2}+k+\varepsilon}\big|\widehat{f}(n)\big|^{2}\Biggl)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+k+\varepsilon}}

for any
k>0
. Thus, we use the Parseval theorem to obtain

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{s},g]f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{s,\varepsilon}\|\Lambda^{s-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\varepsilon}}+C_{s,\varepsilon}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{s}g\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, for
s<1
, from (
2.6
) we see that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{[\Lambda^{s},g]f}{(n)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big||n|^{s}-|n-m|^{s}\big|\,\big|\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big|\hat{g}(m)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|s|\int_{0}^{1}|nr+(n-m)(1-r)|^{s-1}\,\big|\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big|m\hat{g}(m)\big|\,dr.

Here we note for
r\in[0,1]
that

\displaystyle|nr+(n-m)(1-r)|^{s-1}

\displaystyle=|n-m+rm|^{s-1}

\displaystyle=(1+r)^{s-1}\Big|\frac{n-m}{1+r}+\frac{mr}{1+r}\,\Big|^{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq(1+r)^{s-1}\left(\frac{|n-m|^{s-1}}{1+r}+\frac{|m|^{s-1}r}{1+r}\,\right)

\displaystyle=(1+r)^{s-2}\left(|n-m|^{s-1}+|m|^{s-1}r\,\right)

from the fact that
|\cdot|^{s-1}
is convex for
s<1
.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{[\Lambda^{s},g]f}{(n)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|s|\,\big||n-m|^{s-1}\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big|m\hat{g}(m)\big|\int_{0}^{1}(1+r)^{s-2}\,dr

\displaystyle+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|s|\,\big|\hat{f}(n-m)\big|\,\big||m|^{s}\hat{g}(m)\big|\int_{0}^{1}r(1+r)^{s-2}\,dr,

which implies the second row in (
2.7
). This completes the proof.
∎

We next present some useful Sobolev type inequality on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
0<s_{1}\leq s_{2}
, then we have

(2.8)

\|\Lambda^{s_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\Lambda^{s_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, if

\int f\,dx=0
, then (
2.8
) holds for any
s_{1}\leq s_{2}
.

Proof.

The inequality (
2.8
) can be easily obtained as follows:

(2.9)

\|\Lambda^{s_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}|n|^{2s_{2}}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{2s_{2}}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}=\|\Lambda^{s_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

If
\int f\,dx=0
, then
the case
s_{1}=0
immediately follows from the Poincaré inequality.
Moreover,
\int f\,dx=0
implies
\hat{f}(n)=0
when
n=0
and then

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
in (
2.9
) can be replaced by
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}/\{0\}}

so that (
2.9
) is valid for any
s_{1}\leq s_{2}
.
∎

3.
A priori estimates

In this section, we provide a priori estimates of solutions for the system (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) in
\mathcal{C}([0,T);H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.

We start with
L^{2}
-estimates for the pair
(h,u)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\gamma>1
, and
m>1+d/2
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
. For
T>0
, let
(h,u)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T);H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
be a classical solution to system (
1.5
) satisfying

(3.1)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int|h|^{2}+|u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\int|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\|h\|_{H^{m}}+C\|u\|_{H^{m}}+C_{0}\lambda\right)\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(C\|h\|_{H^{m}}+C_{0}\lambda\right)\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where

(3.2)

C_{0}=C_{d}(2\sigma)^{-1}N\max\{2^{-(N-1)},2^{N-1}\}

with
C_{d}>1
.
Here,
C
is a positive constant depending on
m
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

We first see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int|h|^{2}+|u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\int|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int hu\cdot\nabla h\,dx-\int|u|^{2}\nabla\cdot u\,dx-\frac{1}{N}\int h(h+\sigma)(\nabla\cdot u)\,dx-\frac{1}{N}\int u\cdot(h+\sigma)\nabla h\,dx

\displaystyle+\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\left\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\right\}dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathsf{I}_{i}+\mathcal{R}_{0},

where we estimate

\mathsf{I}_{1}\leq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{I}_{2}\leq\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By using the integration by parts, we also get

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}_{3}+\mathsf{I}_{4}=\frac{1}{N}\int h\nabla h\cdot u\,dx\leq\frac{1}{N}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

To control the term
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, we notice that (
3.1
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{2}\leq h(t,x)+\sigma\leq\frac{3\sigma}{2}\quad\mbox{for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T].

This, together with Lemma
2.2
for
\alpha-d\leq 0
, gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sigma^{-N}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sigma^{-N}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|(h+\sigma)^{N-1}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{-1}N\max\{2^{-(N-1)},2^{N-1}\}\lambda\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{0}\lambda\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}

for
C_{0}>0
given as in (
3.2
)
Then, we use Young’s inequality to deduce

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int|h|^{2}+|u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\int|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2C_{0}\lambda\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{0}\lambda\right)\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{0}\lambda\right)\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
\gamma
.
Finally, we use

\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{m}\|u\|_{H^{m}},\quad\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{m}\|h\|_{H^{m-1}}\leq C_{m}\|h\|_{H^{m}}

to obtain the desired estimates.
∎

Before proceeding further, we present a technical lemma and a remark on the derivative estimates of
h
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
m>d/2+1
.
For given
T>0
and
\sigma>0
, let
h\in\mathcal{C}([0,T);H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
with

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}.

Then there exists a positive constant
C_{d,k}
such that

(3.3)

\|\Lambda^{k}(h+\sigma)^{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\max\left\{(\sigma/2)^{\beta-1},(2\sigma)^{\beta-1}\right\}|\beta|\|\Lambda^{k}h\|_{L^{2}}+C_{d,k}(1-\delta_{1,k})\|\Lambda^{k}h\|_{L^{2}}\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{k-2}

for any integer
k\leq m
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker’s delta.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. When
k=1
, we directly have

\|\nabla(h+\sigma)^{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\max\left\{(\sigma/2)^{\beta-1},(2\sigma)^{\beta-1}\right\}|\beta|\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}.

Now, suppose that (
3.3
) holds for
k\geq 1
. Then

\nabla^{k+1}(h+\sigma)^{\beta}=\beta(h+\sigma)^{\beta-1}\nabla^{k+1}h+\beta[\nabla^{k},(h+\sigma)^{\beta-1}]\nabla h.

Thus, we use Lemma
2.1
and induction hypothesis to deduce

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k+1}(h+\sigma)^{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{(\sigma/2)^{\beta-1},(2\sigma)^{\beta-1}\right\}|\beta|\|\nabla^{k+1}h\|_{L^{2}}+C\|[\nabla^{k},(h+\sigma)^{\beta-1}]\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{(\sigma/2)^{\beta-1},(2\sigma)^{\beta-1}\right\}|\beta|\|\nabla^{k+1}h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{k}(h+\sigma)^{\beta-1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla(h+\sigma)^{\beta-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{k}h\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{(\sigma/2)^{\beta-1},(2\sigma)^{\beta-1}\right\}|\beta|\|\nabla^{k+1}h\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\Lambda^{k+1}h\|_{L^{2}}\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{k-1},

and this yields the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Suppose that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}

holds for some
T>0
. Then we find

\|(h+\sigma)^{n}-\sigma^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|h\|_{L^{2}}

for any
n>0
.
Since Lemma
2.2
implies

\|h\|_{H^{m}}\sim\|h\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}},

with the help of Poincaré inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{m}}

\displaystyle\sim\|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla(h+\sigma)^{N}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

for
t\in[0,T]
. This shows

\|h\|_{H^{m}}\sim\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}.

Now, we provide the estimates of higher-order derivatives of solutions. Thanks to Remark
3.1
, it suffices to deal with the highest order.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
be satisfied. Then, depending on
\alpha
, we have the following estimates:

(i)

(Sub-Manev potential case) If
-2\leq\alpha-d\leq-1
, then we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int|\Lambda^{m}h|^{2}\,dx+\int|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\int|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(C\|h\|_{H^{m}}+C\|u\|_{H^{m}}+C_{0}\lambda+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(C\|h\|_{H^{m}}+C\|u\|_{H^{m}}+C_{0}\lambda+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where

C_{0}:=C_{d}(2\sigma)^{-1}N\max\{2^{-(N-1)},2^{N-1}\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad C_{d}>1.

(ii)

(Super-Manev potential case) If
-1<\alpha-d<0
, then we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int|\Lambda^{m}h|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\int|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left((1+\hat{\lambda})(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}})+\hat{\lambda}\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\left(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\hat{\lambda}\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\|h\|_{H^{m}})\right)\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where

\displaystyle C_{1}:=C_{d}\sigma^{-(2k_{0}+1)}

\displaystyle N^{2k_{0}+1}\max\{2^{-k_{0}(N-2)-N},2^{-(k_{0}-1)(N-2)},2^{(k_{0}+1)(N-2)}\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}:=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N^{2},\quad\hat{\lambda}:=\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda^{k},

and
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is a minimum value of
k
such that

\max\left\{1,\,\frac{2}{d-\alpha}-2\right\}\leq k<\frac{2m}{d-\alpha}.

Here
C=C(\gamma,m,\alpha,d)
is a positive constant.

For the proof of Proposition
3.1
, a crucial part is to control the following Riesz interaction term:

\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int\Lambda^{m}u\cdot\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx.

As mentioned in Section
1.3.1
, when
-2<\alpha-d\leq-1
(sub-Manev potential case), the total derivative order of the operator
\Lambda^{m+\alpha-d}\nabla
is roughly less than or equal to
m
, and hence, using Lemma
2.2
and
3.2
gives the desired result.
However, it does not work directly for the case
-1<\alpha-d<0
(super-Manev potential case).
To handle this issue, we shall use an iteration scheme to reduce the order of the derivative in the Riesz interaction. Let us recall

(3.6)

\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)=\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-(k+2)l}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

and consider a function

\mathcal{P}_{m}(k):=\frac{1}{N}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-kl}\{(h+\sigma)\nabla h\}\,dx

for any
0\leq k<m/l
and
0<l<1/2
, where
l=(d-\alpha)/2
.

In the following lemma, we show a relation between
\mathcal{P}_{m}(k)
and
\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)
for any
0\leq k<m/l
whose proof is postponed to Appendix
A
for the smoothness of reading.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
be satisfied and
0<l<1/2
. Then, for any integer
k\in[1,\,m/l)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{m}(k)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)+C(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx,

where
C=C(\gamma,m,k,l)
is a positive constant.

Together with the lemma above, the following is the core of the proof for Proposition
3.1
. By using them together, we can manipulate the term
\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)
into the term
\mathcal{R}_{m}(k+1)
with reduced order of the derivative on the Riesz interaction term.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
be satisfied and
0<l<1/2
. Then, for any integer
k\in[0,\,m/l-1)
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)-\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(k+1)\right|\leq C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N^{2}
and
C=C(\gamma,m,k,l)
is a positive constant.

Proof.

For notational simplicity, let us write
g_{k}:=(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}-\sigma^{k(N-2)}
in the proof.

We first show (
3.7
) in the case
k=0
. In this case, we observe

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N\int\Lambda^{m-l}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-l}\{(h+\sigma)^{N-1}\nabla h\}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N\int\Lambda^{m-l}u\cdot[\Lambda^{m-l},\,(h+\sigma)^{N-2}]\{(h+\sigma)\nabla h\}\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda\sigma^{-N}N}{2}\int\Lambda^{m-l}u\cdot[\Lambda^{m-l},\,(h+\sigma)^{N-2}]\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(1).

On the other hand, the following identity holds:

[\Lambda^{m-l},\,(h+\sigma)^{N-2}]=[\Lambda^{m-l},\,(h+\sigma)^{N-2}-\sigma^{N-2}]=[\Lambda^{m-l},\,g_{1}]

in the operator sense with a given constant
\sigma
. Thus, by using (
2.4
) with sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{m-l},g_{1}]\,\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g_{1}\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{m-l}(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{m-l}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g_{1}\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{H^{m-1}}\|\Lambda^{m}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

for some
C=C(\gamma,m,l)>0
. Here we used (
2.2
) to get

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m}(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{H^{m-1}}\leq C\|\nabla h\|_{H^{m-1}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}},\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|g_{1}\|_{H^{m}}\leq C\|h\|_{H^{m}}.

Thus, we combine (
3.8
) with (
3.9
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)-\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(1)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|\Lambda^{m-l}u\|_{L^{2}}\|[\Lambda^{m-l},\,(h+\sigma)^{N-2}-\sigma^{N-2}]\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

by Young’s inequality with a positive constant
C=C(\gamma,m,l)
.

Meanwhile, the case
k\geq 1
requires a more delicate analysis since the operator
\Lambda^{-l}
and
(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}
do not commute, i.e.
[\Lambda^{-l},\,(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}]\neq 0
for
k\geq 1
.
Thus we use the integration by parts twice as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)

\displaystyle=-\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int\left([\Lambda^{-l}\nabla\cdot,\,(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}]\Lambda^{m-kl}u\right)\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int\left(\nabla(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\cdot\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}u\right)\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int\left((h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}u\right)\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=:\mathsf{J}_{1}+\mathsf{J}_{2}+\mathsf{J}_{3}.

Then we use Lemma
2.1
and arguments in Remark
3.1
to obtain, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon<m-d/2-1
,

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{-l}\nabla\cdot,\,g_{k}]\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g_{k}\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}u\|_{L^{2}}+C\|g_{k}\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1-l+\varepsilon}}\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C\|g_{k}\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m}g_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}},

where
C=C(\gamma,m,d,\alpha,k)
is a positivie constant. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|[\Lambda^{-l}\nabla\cdot,\,g_{k}]\,\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For
\mathsf{J}_{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|\nabla(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for some
C=C(\gamma,m,d,\alpha,k)>0
.

We next rewrite
\mathsf{J}_{3}
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{3}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}\{(h+\sigma)^{N-1}\nabla h\}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}u\cdot[\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l},\,(h+\sigma)^{N-2}]\{(h+\sigma)\nabla h\}\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(k+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda\sigma^{-N}N}{2}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l}u\cdot[\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l},\,g_{1}]\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(k+1).

Similarly as in (
3.9
), we have

\|[\Lambda^{m-(k+1)l},g_{1}]\,\nabla(h+\sigma)^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}

for
k<m/l-1
and some
C=C(\gamma,m,k,d,\alpha)>0
.
Thus, it implies

\displaystyle|\mathsf{J}_{3}-\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(k+1)|\leq C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, we collect all the estimates for
\mathsf{J}_{i}
’s to yield

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{R}_{m}(k)-\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{m}(k+1)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

as desired.
∎

Now, we are ready to present the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let
m>1+d/2
. Through the integration by parts, we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int|\Lambda^{m}h|^{2}\,dx+\int|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\int|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int\Lambda^{m}h\,[\Lambda^{m},u\cdot]\,\nabla h\,dx-\int\Lambda^{m}u\cdot([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot]\,\nabla u)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\cdot u)(|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}+|\Lambda^{m}h|^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{m}h\,\Lambda^{m}\{(h+\sigma)(\nabla\cdot u)\}\,dx-\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{m}u\cdot\Lambda^{m}\{(h+\sigma)\nabla h\}\,dx

\displaystyle+\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int\Lambda^{m}u\cdot\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{5}\mathsf{K}_{i}+\mathcal{R}_{m}(0),

where
\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)
is given as in (
3.6
) for the case
k=0
.

We use the commutator estimate (
2.4
) with taking
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}(\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

\mathsf{K}_{2}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

along with Young’s inequality.
Here,
C>0
depends on
m
.
It is easy to get

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We use the integration by parts twice so that

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{4}+\mathsf{K}_{5}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{m}h\,\Lambda^{m}(h(\nabla\cdot u))\,dx-\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{m}u\cdot\Lambda^{m}(h\nabla h)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{m}h\,[\Lambda^{m},h](\nabla\cdot u)\,dx-\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{m}u\cdot[\Lambda^{m},h]\nabla h\,dx+\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{m}h\nabla h\cdot\Lambda^{m}u\,dx.

Then it follows from (
2.4
) with
\varepsilon>0
small enough that

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{4}+\mathsf{K}_{5}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}+C\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

thanks to the Young’s inequality. Here, a positive constant
C
depends on
\gamma
and
m
. Thus, we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int|\Lambda^{m}h|^{2}\,dx+\int|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\int|\Lambda^{m}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)

for some
C=C(\gamma,m)>0
.

Now we need to control the term
\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)
from the Riesz interaction.

\bullet
(Sub-Manev potential case:
-2\leq\alpha-d\leq-1
.) Here, we use Lemmas
2.2
and
3.2
to get

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sigma^{-N}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m+\alpha-d}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sigma^{-N}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{0}\lambda\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\lambda\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{0}\lambda\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

where
C_{0}
is given as

C_{0}=C_{d}(2\sigma)^{-1}N\max\{2^{-(N-1)},2^{N-1}\}.

\bullet
(Super-Manev potential case:
-1<\alpha-d<0
.) In this case, it is difficult to directly estimate the term
\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)
as we just previously did.
Instead, we shall use an iteration argument, which uses Lemmas
3.4
and
3.3
repeatedly, to reduce the order of derivative on the Riesz interaction term. Then, we arrive at

(3.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}\mathcal{R}_{m}(1)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{2}\lambda^{k}(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum_{k=1}^{2}\lambda^{k}\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{k}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}^{2}\mathcal{R}_{m}(2)

\displaystyle\leq\ \cdots

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda^{k}(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda^{k}\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{k}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\tilde{\lambda}^{k_{0}}\mathcal{R}_{m}(k_{0})

for
1\leq k_{0}<m/l
and recall that
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda\sigma^{-N}N^{2}
.
We now take
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
as a minimum value for
k
satisfying

\max\left\{1,\,\frac{1}{l}-2\right\}\leq k<\frac{m}{l}

which is valid when
0<l<1/2
. Then by applying Lemmas
2.2
and
3.2
, one obtains

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(k_{0})

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int(h+\sigma)^{k_{0}(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-k_{0}l}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla\Lambda^{1-(k_{0}+2)l}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sigma^{k_{0}(N-2)-N}\max\{2^{-k_{0}(N-2)},2^{k_{0}(N-2)}\}\|\Lambda^{m-k_{0}l}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{0}\lambda\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{0}\lambda}{2}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\widetilde{C}_{0}\lambda}{2}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

where
\widetilde{C}_{0}
is written as

\widetilde{C}_{0}:=C_{d}\sigma^{k_{0}(N-2)-1}N\max\{2^{-k_{0}(N-2)-(N-1)},2^{-k_{0}(N-2)+N-1},2^{k_{0}(N-2)+N-1}\}.

Thus, by (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda^{k}(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|h\|_{H^{m}})\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda^{k}\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{k}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+C\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

where
C=C(\gamma,m,\alpha,d)
is a positive constant and
C_{1}>0
is given as

C_{1}:=(\sigma^{-N}N^{2})^{k_{0}}2^{-1}\widetilde{C}_{0}\\
=C_{d}\sigma^{-(2k_{0}+1)}N^{2k_{0}+1}\max\{2^{-k_{0}(N-2)-N},2^{-(k_{0}-1)(N-2)},2^{(k_{0}+1)(N-2)}\}.

This yields

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}(0)

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\hat{\lambda}(\|u\|_{H^{m}}+\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|h\|_{H^{m}})+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}+C\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(C(\hat{\lambda}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2})\|h\|_{H^{m}}+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{k}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx.

Here
\hat{\lambda}=\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda^{k}>0
.

Hence, we combine (
3.10
) with (
3.11
) for the case
-2\leq\alpha-d\leq-1
and with (
3.14
) for the case
-1<\alpha-d<0
so that we conclude (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), respectively.
∎

We close this section by giving the dissipation estimate for
\nabla h
in the
H^{m-1}
norm. The proof is based on the hypocoercivity-type estimate. Precisely, we consider the time-derivative of the following integral

\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx

with
s\leq m-1
which gives an appropriate dissipation rate for
h
. Note that for
\mu>0
small enough

\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\mu\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx\sim\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
be satisfied. Then for
0\leq s\leq m-1
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx+\nu\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx+\frac{\sigma}{N}\int|\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\|u\|_{H^{m}}+C\|h\|_{H^{m}}+\frac{\sigma}{N}\right)\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(C\|u\|_{H^{m}}+C\|h\|_{H^{m}}+2C_{0}\lambda+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2})\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for some
C=C(\gamma,d,s,m)>0
, where
C_{0}
is appeared in Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

Let
0\leq s\leq m-1
. We integrate by parts to see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla h)\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\,dx+\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{s}\{(h+\sigma)(\nabla\cdot u)\}\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\,dx

\displaystyle-\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla u)\,dx-\frac{1}{N}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}\{(h+\sigma)\nabla h\}\,dx

\displaystyle-\nu\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx+\sigma^{-N}\lambda\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{5}\mathsf{L}_{i}+\widetilde{R}_{s},

where we use Lemma
2.1
to estimate the terms
\mathsf{L}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq 4
) as follows:

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla h)\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}})\|\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int\Lambda^{s}(h\nabla\cdot u)\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\,dx+\frac{\sigma}{N}\int|\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}})\|\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\sigma}{N}\|\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}+C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{N}\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{3}

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla u)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}(h\nabla h)\,dx-\frac{\sigma}{N}\int|\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}}(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}})-\frac{\sigma}{N}\int|\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}}-\frac{\sigma}{N}\int|\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)|^{2}\,dx

for some
C=C(s,\gamma)>0
.

Thus, we collect the estimates for
\mathsf{L}_{i}
’s to get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx+\nu\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx+\frac{\sigma}{N}\int|\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\sigma}{N})\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\widetilde{R}_{s}.

By using the neutrality condition (
1.7
), Lemmas
2.2
and
3.2
, we next estimate

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{s}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sigma^{-N}\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s+\alpha-d}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{-N}\lambda\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{0}\lambda\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\|\Lambda^{s}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx+\nu\int\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)\cdot\Lambda^{s}u\,dx+\frac{\sigma}{N}\int|\nabla(\Lambda^{s}h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\sigma}{N})\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}+2C_{0}\lambda+C\lambda\|h\|_{H^{m}}(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2})\|\nabla\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and applying the Sobolev inequality to the above yields the desired result.
∎

4.
Global existence of solutions: proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we present the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of the regular solutions
(h,u)
to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) by extending the local-in-time solution to the global one based on the classical continuation argument. For this, we obtain the uniform-in-time bound estimates of the solution by using a priori estimates established in the previous section.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T>0
,
m>1+d/2
, and
(h,u)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T);H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
be a classical solution to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).
Suppose that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m})}+\|h\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m})}\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\ll 1
so that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}.

Then there exists a positive constant
C^{\ast}
independent of
T
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|(h,u)(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\leq C^{\ast}\|(h_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Proof.

Since the proof of the sub-Manev potential case (
-2\leq\alpha-d\leq-1
) is rather parallel to that of the super-Manev potential case (
0<\alpha-d<-1
), we only provide the details of the proof for the latter case.

Note that

(1+\|h\|_{H^{m}})^{m-2}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})^{m-2}<C,

thus it follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\int|\Lambda^{m}h|^{2}\,dx\right)+\nu\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C(\varepsilon_{1}+\hat{\lambda}\varepsilon_{1}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\varepsilon_{1})+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(C(\varepsilon_{1}+\hat{\lambda}\varepsilon_{1}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\varepsilon_{1})+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\hat{\lambda}
,
\tilde{\lambda}
,
k_{0}
, and
C_{1}
are appeared in Proposition
3.1
.
With Lemma
3.5
multiplied by a constant
\mu/2>0
, we deduce that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\int|\Lambda^{m}h|^{2}\,dx+\mu\int\nabla(\Lambda^{m-1}h)\cdot\Lambda^{m-1}u\,dx\right)

\displaystyle+\nu\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mu\sigma}{2N}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mu\nu}{2}\int\nabla(\Lambda^{m-1}h)\cdot\Lambda^{m-1}u\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(C(\varepsilon_{1}(1+\mu)+\hat{\lambda}\varepsilon_{1}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\varepsilon_{1})+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}+\frac{\mu\sigma}{2N}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(C(\varepsilon_{1}(1+\mu)+(\hat{\lambda}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1})\varepsilon_{1}+\lambda\mu\varepsilon_{1})+C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}+C_{0}\mu\lambda\right)\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We also recall from Lemma
3.1
that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\left(\nu-C\varepsilon_{1}-C_{0}\lambda\right)\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left(C\varepsilon_{1}+C_{0}\lambda\right)\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then we combine (
4.1
) with (
4.2
) and use Young’s inequality to yield

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{X}}_{m}

\displaystyle\leq-(2\nu-C\varepsilon_{1}-2C_{0}\lambda)\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(2\nu-C\varepsilon_{1}(1+\mu)-C\varepsilon_{1}\hat{\lambda}-C\varepsilon_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}-2C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}-\mu\left(\frac{\sigma}{N}+\frac{N\nu^{2}}{2\sigma}\right)\right)\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\mu\sigma}{2N}-C\varepsilon_{1}(1+\mu)-C\varepsilon_{1}(\hat{\lambda}+\lambda^{k_{0}+1})-C\lambda\mu\varepsilon_{1}-2C_{0}\lambda(1+\mu)-2C_{1}\lambda^{k_{0}+1}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where

{\mathcal{X}}_{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mu\int\nabla(\Lambda^{m-1}h)\cdot\Lambda^{m-1}u\,dx.

Here, together with Remark
3.1
, we also note that

\|u\|_{H^{m}}\sim\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}.

Now we first take
\mu>0
small enough such that

2\nu>\mu\left(\frac{\sigma}{N}+\frac{N\nu^{2}}{2\sigma}\right)

and

{\mathcal{X}}_{m}\sim\|(h,u)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

We notice that we can choose
\lambda>0
sufficiently small compared to
\sigma
so that
\tilde{\lambda}\ll 1
,
C_{0}\lambda\ll 1
, and
C_{1}\lambda\ll 1
. This, together with taking
\varepsilon_{1}
small enough, yields that there exists
\xi>0
such that

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{X}}_{m}+\xi{\mathcal{X}}_{m}\leq 0.

Hence we have

(4.3)

\|(h,u)(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\leq C^{\ast}e^{-\xi t}\|(h_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all }t\in[0,T]

for some
C^{\ast}>0
independent of
T
. This concludes the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We set

(4.4)

\varepsilon_{2}^{2}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}}{2(1+C^{\ast})}

for
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
C^{\ast}>0
given in Proposition
4.1
.
By Theorem
2.1
, the following set is nonempty:

\mathcal{S}:=\left\{T>0\,:\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|(h,u)(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\right\},

and assume for a contradiction that
\sup\mathcal{S}=:T^{\ast}<\infty
.
Then by Proposition
4.1
and (
4.4
), we have

\varepsilon_{1}^{2}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{\ast}}\|(h,u)(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\leq C^{\ast}\|(h_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\leq C^{\ast}\varepsilon_{2}^{2}=\frac{C^{\ast}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}}{2(1+C^{\ast})}<\varepsilon_{1}^{2}

which is a contradiction. Hence, the solution exists in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and (
1.9
) directly follows from (
4.3
).
∎

5.
A priori estimate of large-time behavior: proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we investigate the a priori large-time behavior for the
L^{2}
-norm of the sufficiently regular solution
(h,u)
to the system (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) without any smallness assumptions on the initial data
(h_{0},u_{0})
.

To show the temporal decay estimate (
1.10
), we first introduce a function
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(t)
by

\mathcal{L}:=\int(h+\sigma)^{N}|u-m_{c}|^{2}\,dx+\int|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}|m_{c}|^{2},

where

m_{c}(t)=\sigma^{-N}\int(h(t)+\sigma)^{N}u(t)\,dx.

We recall that
m_{c}
decays to zero exponentially fast as time goes to infinity:

(5.1)

m_{c}(t)\leq m_{c}(0)e^{-\nu t}.

Since

\displaystyle h_{min}\leq h(t,x)+\sigma\leq\|h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sigma,\qquad(\sigma-\|h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}})^{-1}\leq(h(t,x)+\sigma)^{-1}\leq h_{min}^{-1}

for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
there exists a positive constant
C=C(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}},h_{min},\gamma)
such that

(5.2)

|(h(x,t)+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}|\leq C|h(x,t)|

by the mean value theorem and hence,

(5.3)

\int|u|^{2}+|h|^{2}\,dx\leq C\mathcal{L}+(C+\frac{1}{2})|m_{c}|^{2},

where a constant
C=C(\|h\|_{\infty},h_{min},\gamma)
may be different from the constant
C
in (
5.2
).
From (
5.3
) together with (
5.1
),
it is enough to show the exponential decay rate for
\mathcal{L}(t)
in the proposition below.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(h,u)
be a global classical solution to (
1.5
) with sufficient integrability.
Suppose that

(i)

\inf_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(x,t)+\sigma\geq h_{min}>0
and

(ii)

(h,u)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))^{d})
.

Then, for sufficiently small
\lambda>0
, we have

\mathcal{L}(t)\leq C\mathcal{L}(0)e^{-\mu t}

for
t\geq 0
, where
C>0
is independent of
t
.

For the proof of Proposition
5.1
. we start with the following auxiliary lemma whose proof can be found in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
r_{0},\tilde{r}>0
and
\gamma\geq 1
be given constants, and set

(5.4)

f(\gamma,r;r_{0})=r\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{s^{\gamma}-r_{0}^{\gamma}}{s^{2}}\,ds

for
r\in[0,\tilde{r}]
.
Then there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{C(\gamma,r_{0},\tilde{r})}(r-r_{0})^{2}\leq f(\gamma,r;r_{0})\leq C(\gamma,r_{0},\tilde{r})(r-r_{0})^{2}

for all
r\in[0,\tilde{r}]
.

Then we consider the following modulated energy function
\mathcal{E}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(h+\sigma)^{N}|u-m_{c}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{2}{N(N+2)}\int f\left(\,\frac{N+2}{N},(h+\sigma)^{N};\sigma^{N}\,\right)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{N}}\int|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}|m_{c}(t)|^{2},

where
f
is given in (
5.4
).
Then, it follows from Lemma
5.1
that

f\left(\frac{N+2}{N},(h(x)+\sigma)^{N};\sigma^{N}\right)\sim|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}|^{2},

and moreover, it is not difficult to check that

\int|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}|^{2}\,dx\sim\int|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}|^{2}-\lambda|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}|^{2}\,dx

by Lemma
2.2
with small
0<\lambda<1
. Thus we attain
\mathcal{E}(t)\sim\mathcal{L}(t)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(h,u)
be a global classical solution to (
1.5
) with sufficient integrability. Then we have

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)+\mathcal{D}(t)=0,

where

\mathcal{D}(t)=2\nu\int(h+\sigma)^{N}|u-m_{c}|^{2}\,dx+\nu|m_{c}|^{2}.

Proof.

From the system (
1.5
) with
\gamma=(N+2)/N
, we can easily find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{N}|u-m_{c}|^{2}\,dx+\nu\int(h+\sigma)^{N}|u-m_{c}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N(N+2)}\int u\cdot\nabla(h+\sigma)^{N+2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\nu\int(h+\sigma)^{N}m_{c}\cdot(u-m_{c})\,dx+\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{N}}\int(h+\sigma)^{N}(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathsf{I}_{i}.

For
\mathsf{I}_{1}
and
\mathsf{I}_{2}
, we obtain

\mathsf{I}_{1}=-\frac{1}{N(N+2)}\frac{d}{dt}\int f\left(\frac{N+2}{N},(h+\sigma)^{N};\sigma^{N}\right)\,dx

and

\mathsf{I}_{2}=\nu\sigma^{N}|m_{c}|^{2}-\nu|m_{c}|^{2}\int(h+\sigma)^{N}\,dx=0.

For
\mathsf{I}_{3}
, one uses the integration by parts to get

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int(h+\sigma)^{N}(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{N}}\int\partial_{t}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{N}}\int\partial_{t}\left(\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\right)\,\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{2\sigma^{N}}\frac{d}{dt}\int|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}|^{2}\,dx,

since

\displaystyle-\int\nabla\cdot((h+\sigma)^{N}m_{c})\,\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\cdot m_{c}\,\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}m_{c}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=0

by the symmetry of the operator
\Lambda^{\alpha-d}
and the fact
\nabla m_{c}=0
. On the other hand, one has

\frac{d}{dt}|m_{c}(t)|^{2}=2m_{c}(t)m^{\prime}_{c}(t)=-2\nu|m_{c}(t)|^{2},

and collect all the estimates to yield the desired result.
∎

Due to the absence of the dissipation rate for the density
h
in the term
\mathcal{D}(t)
, we need to consider the perturbed energy functional which is eventually equivalent to the total fluctuated energy functional
\mathcal{L}(t)
. Motivated from
[
8
]
, we consider the perturbed energy functional:

\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(t)=\mathcal{E}(t)+\mu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

for sufficiently small
\mu>0
which will be determined in Lemma
5.3
.
We note here that

\mathcal{E}(t)\sim\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(t)
.
Then it is clear to get

(5.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(t)+\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t)=0,

where

\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t):=\mathcal{D}(t)-\mu\frac{d}{dt}\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx.

Here the potential
W
is given as in (
1.11
). From (
5.5
) along with the equivalent relation
\mathcal{L}(t)\sim\mathcal{E}(t)\sim\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(t)
,
Proposition
5.1
is a direct result of the following.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(h,u)
be any global classical solutions to the system (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).
Under the same assumptions in Proposition
5.1
, there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t)\geq C\mathcal{L}(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

Proof.

Direct computation shows

\displaystyle-\mu\frac{d}{dt}\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

\displaystyle=-\mu\int u\otimes(u-m_{c}):\nabla^{2}W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx+\frac{\mu}{N}\int(h+\sigma)\nabla h\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

\displaystyle+\mu\nu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx-\mu\lambda\int\nabla\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

\displaystyle+\mu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[\nabla\cdot((h+\sigma)^{N}u)]\,dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{5}{\mathsf{J}_{i}}(t).

We then estimate each term
{\mathsf{J}_{i}},i=1,\dots,5
as follows.

For
\mathsf{J}_{2}
, we use the integration by parts to obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{N}\int(h+\sigma)\nabla h\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{2N}\int\{(h+\sigma)^{2}-\sigma^{2}\}\left((h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\mu\int\left|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\right|^{2}dx,

where
c_{1}=c_{1}(\gamma,\|h\|_{L^{\infty}},h_{min})
is a positive constant.

We next use the lower bound assumption on
h+\sigma
to estimate

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{3}

\displaystyle=\nu\mu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq-c_{2}\nu\mu\|(h+\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}}(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-c_{2}\nu\mu\|(h+\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}}(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{2}}\|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-c_{2}\nu\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(h+\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}}(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-c_{2}\nu\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}\|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}},

where
c_{2}=c_{2}(h_{min},\gamma)
is a positive constant.

For
\mathsf{J}_{4}
, similarly as the esitmate of
\mathsf{J}_{2}
, we find

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{4}

\displaystyle=-\mu\lambda\int\Lambda^{\alpha-d}\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq-\mu\lambda\|\Lambda^{\alpha-d}(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}}\|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-\mu\lambda\|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Finally, we observe

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{5}

\displaystyle=\mu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[\nabla\cdot((h+\sigma)^{N}(u-m_{c}))]\,dx

\displaystyle+\mu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[\nabla\cdot([(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]m_{c})]\,dx

and

\begin{aligned} \int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[\nabla\cdot([(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]m_{c})]\,dx=\int m_{c}\otimes(u-m_{c}):\nabla^{2}W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx\end{aligned}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{1}+\mathsf{J}_{5}

\displaystyle=-\mu\int(u-m_{c})\otimes(u-m_{c}):\nabla^{2}W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\,dx

\displaystyle+\mu\int(u-m_{c})\cdot\nabla W*[\nabla\cdot((h+\sigma)^{N}(u-m_{c}))]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq-\mu h_{min}^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|(h+\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}}(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{2}}\|(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}W*[(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle-\mu h_{min}^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|(h+\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}}(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla W*[\nabla\cdot((h+\sigma)^{N}(u-m_{c}))]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-C\mu^{1/2}(1+\mu^{1/2})\|(h+\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}}(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-c_{3}\mu^{3/2}\|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used Young’s inequality and
c_{3}=c_{3}(\gamma,m_{c}(0),\|u\|_{L^{\infty}},h_{min})
is a positive constant.

Hence, we collect all the estimates for
\mathsf{J}_{i}
’s to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t)\geq

\displaystyle\left(2\nu-C\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nu+\mu^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)\|(h+\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}}(u-m_{c})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\mu\left(c_{1}-(c_{2}\nu+c_{3})\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}-\lambda\right)\|(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu|m_{c}|^{2}.

We now take
\mu,\lambda>0
sufficiently small so that we find a positive constant
C
such that

\mathcal{D}^{\mu}(t)\geq C\mathcal{L}(t),

which implies the desired result.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Here, note that the condition

Proof of Lemma

In this appendix, we provide the details of the proof of Lemma

.

Let
0<l<1/2
. For any
k\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\nu\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{k(N-2)}{2}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)-1}\partial_{t}h\,|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx-\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-kl}(u\cdot\nabla u)\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-kl}\{(h+\sigma)\nabla h\}\,dx

\displaystyle+\lambda\sigma^{-N}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot\Lambda^{m-(k+2)l}\nabla\{(h+\sigma)^{N}-\sigma^{N}\}\,dx

\displaystyle=:\mathsf{I}_{1}+\mathsf{I}_{2}-\mathcal{P}_{m}(k)+\mathcal{R}_{m}(k),

where
\mathsf{I}_{1}
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{t}h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for some
C=C(\gamma,k)>0
.

For
\mathsf{I}_{2}
, we rewrite it as

(A.1)

\mathsf{I}_{2}=-\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot([\Lambda^{m-kl},u\cdot]\,\nabla u)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}u\cdot\nabla(|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2})\,dx.

We then use Lemma
2.1
to yield

\displaystyle-\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}\Lambda^{m-kl}u\cdot([\Lambda^{m-kl},u\cdot]\,\nabla u)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}\|[\Lambda^{m-kl},u\cdot]\,\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\varepsilon}}(\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\varepsilon>0
is a sufficiently small constant and
kl<m
. For the second term on the right-hand side of (
A.1
), we integrate by part to get

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}u\cdot\nabla(|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{k(N-2)}{2}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)-1}\nabla h\cdot u\,|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}(\nabla\cdot u)|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-kl}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for some
C=C(\gamma,k)>0
. Thus, we deduce

\mathsf{I}_{2}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining the above estimates gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx+\nu\int(h+\sigma)^{k(N-2)}|\Lambda^{m-kl}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|h\|_{H^{m}}\|u\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{P}_{m}(k)+\mathcal{R}_{m}(k),

where
C=C(\gamma,m,k,l)>0
. This together with using the fact
h+\sigma>0
concludes the desired estimate (
3.3
).

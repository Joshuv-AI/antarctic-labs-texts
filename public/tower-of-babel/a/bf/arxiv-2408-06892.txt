Symmetry reduction and reconstruction in contact geometry and Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations
Alexandre Anahory Simoes, Leonardo Colombo, Manuel de León, Modesto Salgado, Silvia Souto

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the reduction process of a contact Lagrangian system whose Lagrangian is invariant under a group of symmetries. We give explicit coordinate expressions of the resulting reduced differential equations, the so-called Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations. Our framework relied on the associated Herglotz vector field and its projected vector field, and the use of well-chosen quasi-velocities. Some examples are also discussed.



Keywords:
Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations, symmetry, reduction, reconstruction, connections on principal bundles.

MSC:
37J55, 53D10, 37C79, 37J37, 70H03, 70H05, 70H20

Introduction

Lagrangian and Hamiltonian contact systems have been the subject of intense activity in recent years. The fundamental difference with traditional mechanics is that, instead of possessing conservative properties, contact systems exhibit dissipation. The geometrical reasons for this different behaviour is that one case uses symplectic geometry (a closed, non-degenerate 2-form) while the latter case involves contact forms (a non-degenerate 1-form that is not closed). From the point of view of variations, symplectic mechanics is based on Hamilton’s principle, while contact mechanics is based on Herglotz’s principle, a generalisation that requires solving an implicit differential equation to define the action
[
13
,
7
,
11
,
12
]
. These Lagrangian functions are commonly called in Physics as action-dependent Lagrangians.

One of the most relevant applications of differential geometry to the study of mechanical systems are the procedures for the reduction of the dynamics when the system exhibits symmetries. The mechanisms are: the symplectic reduction theorem of Marsden and Weinstein via the momentum application (an extension of the renowned Noether theorem
[
16
,
2
]
) and its generalisation to the contact case
[
20
,
3
,
11
]
; the coisotropic reduction which in the symplectic case was developed by Weinstein
[
19
,
2
,
10
]
, and in the contact case by de León and Lainz
[
11
]
(see also the recent survey
[
14
]
); and the direct reduction by the Lie group of symmetries, which in the symplectic case has been developed by Mestdag and Crampin
[
17
,
9
]
. An alternative variational approach is the so-called Lagrangian reduction by stages developed by Cendra, Marsden and Ratiu
[
8
]
.

In a recent work
[
5
]
we have obtained the Euler–Poincaré–Herglotz equations and Lagrange–Poincaré–Herglotz equations, respectively (correspondingly Lie–Poisson–Jacobi equations and Hamilton–Poincaré–Herglotz equations in the Hamiltonian side) when the contact Lagrangian system is considered on a Lie algebroid, and, in particular a Atiyah algebroid if we are in presence of symmetries. Similar results are obtained in
[
6
]
, but in this case we are taking the methodology of prolongations of Lie algebroids, which permits to treat directly with contact structures and not general Jacobi structures as above. In
[
4
]
, the authors discussed reduction procedures for contact mechanical systems on Lie groups based on variational principles.

In this paper, we develop a procedure for the reduction and reconstruction of the dynamics of Lagrangian contact systems using the quasi-coordinate method, extending the results of
[
17
]
for conservative systems to systems that include dissipation. One of the necessary geometrical constructions is the prolongation of a connection in a principal fiber bundle using the Hessian of the Lagrangian (instead of a Riemannian metric as in the case of Marsden
[
15
]
).
The corresponding reduced dynamics are governed by the so-called Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations.

The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 is devoted to introduce some concepts on the geometry of the fiber bundle
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
, including Second Order Differential Equations (SODE for short), local frames and quasi-velocities. The main notions on contact Lagrangian systems are developed in Section 3, where the dynamics is provided by the so-called Herglotz SODE. In Section 4 we consider configuration manifolds that are principal bundles over a quotient
Q\to Q/G
, where
G
is a Lie group acting on
Q
; we also discuss connections and
G
-invariant vector fields, in order to get
G
-invariant local frames. Sections 5, 6 and 7 contain the main results of this paper. Indeed, in Section 5 we give a reduction procedure of the original dynamics to the space
TQ/G\times\mathbb{R}
, and so we obtain the so-called Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations. Section 6 is an auxiliary section for Section 7; in fact, in order to get a reconstruction procedure we need to construct a connection in the principal bundle

TQ\times\mathbb{R}\to TQ/G\times\mathbb{R}
using the nondegeneracy of the Lagrangian. We also include one example to illustrate our results as well as a final section of conclusions and further work.

Preliminaires

2.1
Connections and curvature

In what follows we will often use non-linear connections on different many fiber bundles. In order to unify notations, let us recall briefly some definitions. Let
p:E\to B
be a fiber bundle, that is,
p
is a surjective submersion. For
e\in E
, the vertical space
V_{e}E
at
e
is the kernel of
T_{e}p:T_{e}E\to T_{p(e)}B
. So, we obtain the so-called
vertical distribution

VE=\{V_{e}E|e\in E\}
. Next, we construct a short exact sequence of vector bundles over
E
as follow,

0\to VE\to TE\to E\times_{B}TB\to 0,

(1)

where the middle arrow
j:TE\to E\times_{B}TB
is given by
v_{e}\mapsto(e,Tp(v_{e}))
, and
E\times_{B}TB
denotes the fiber product of
E
and
TB
over
B
.

A
connection
on
p
is either given by a right splitting
\gamma:E\times_{B}TB\to TE
(i.e. a linear map satisfying
j\circ\gamma=id_{E\times_{B}TB}
), or by the corresponding left splitting
\omega=id_{TE}-\gamma\circ j:TE\to VE\subset TE
.

The above short exact sequence naturally extends to the level of sections of the corresponding bundles over
E
,

0\to Sec(VE)\to{\mathfrak{X}}(E)\to Sec(E\times_{B}TB)\to 0.

The notation
{\mathfrak{X}}(E)
stands for the Lie algebra of vector fields on
E
.
A splitting of (
1
) induces a splitting of the second sequence, and conversely. When we interpret
\omega:{\mathfrak{X}}(E)\to{\mathfrak{X}}(E)
as a (1,1)-tensor field on
E
, we will call it the
connection form
, or the
vertical projection
. The map
h:=id-\omega
is the
horizontal projection
of the connection. Since vector fields
T
on
B
can be thought as basic sections in
Sec(E\times_{B}TB)
, we may define the
horizontal lift
of the vector field
T
as the vector field
T^{h}
of
E
, given by
T^{h}(e)=\gamma(e,T(\pi(e)))
, for each
e\in E
.

The
curvature
of the connection is the (1,2)-tensor field on
E
, given by
(X,Y)\mapsto-\omega([hX,hY])
, for two vector fields
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{X}}(E)
. In what follows, however, we will also often use the word ‘curvature’ for the restriction of that map to two horizontal lifts and use the notation

K(T,S)=-\omega([T^{h},S^{h}])\in{\mathfrak{X}}(E)

when
T,S\in{\mathfrak{X}}(B)
.

Assume now that
p:E\to B
is a principal bundle. This means that there exists a Lie group
G
acting on
E
on the left such that
B
is naturally identified with the quotient manifold
E/G
. In addition, the fiber bundle is locally trivial, in the sense that for any point in
B
there exists a neighborhoopd
U
such that

\pi^{-1}(U)
is diffeomorphic to the product
U\times G
and this diffeomorphism preserves the action of
G
(the action of
G
on
U\times G

is the trivial one). We know that for any element
\xi
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
there exists a fundamental vector field
\xi_{E}
on
E
provided by the exponential of the group.

Consider now the left splitting
\omega_{e}:T_{e}E\to V_{e}E
as in (
1
), for any
e\in E
. Since

\omega_{e}(\xi_{E})(e)=\xi_{E}(e)

we can define a mapping

\overline{\omega}:T_{e}E\to\mathfrak{g}

by

\overline{\omega}_{e}(\xi_{E}(e))=\xi

This mapping is called the connection form, it is a 1-form taking values in
\mathfrak{g}
and if it has the property that

\Phi_{g}^{*}\overline{\omega}=Ad(g)\overline{\omega}

for any
g\in G
, where
\Phi_{g}
is the left action of the Lie group element
g
and
Ad
is the adjoint representation of the Lie group
G
, then it is called a principal connection.

2.3
Second-order differential equations

Definition 2.1
.

Consider a map
c_{s}\colon I\subset\mathbb{R}\to Q\times\mathbb{R}
with

c_{s}(t)=(c(t),s(t))\,,

where
c\colon I\subset\mathbb{R}\to Q
. The
first prolongation
of
c_{s}
to
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
is the curve
c_{s}^{(1)}\colon I\subset\mathbb{R}\to TQ\times\mathbb{R}
given by

c_{s}^{(1)}(t)=(\dot{c}(t),s(t))\,.

In local coordinates, if
c_{s}(t)=(c(t),s(t))=(q^{\alpha}(t),s(t))\,,

then we have

c_{s}^{(1)}(t)=(\dot{c}(t),s(t))=\left(q^{\alpha}(t),\dot{q}^{\alpha}(t),s(t)\right).

(9)

Definition 2.2
.

A vector field
\mathbf{\Gamma}
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
is a
second-order differential equation
(or a
SODE
) if
S(\Gamma)=\Delta
.

The local expression of a SODE is

\Gamma=u^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\alpha}}+f^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}}+g\frac{\partial}{\partial s},

(10)

for some local functions
f^{\alpha}
,
g

\in C^{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}(TQ\times\mathbb{R})
.

If
\sigma\colon I\subset\mathbb{R}\to TQ\times\mathbb{R}
, locally given by

, is an integral curve of a
sode

\mathbf{\Gamma}
, from Definition

\displaystyle\frac{dq^{\alpha}}{dt}\Big|_{t}=u^{\alpha}(t)\,,\qquad\displaystyle\frac{du^{\alpha}}{dt}\Big|_{t}=f^{\alpha}(\sigma(t))\,,\qquad\displaystyle\frac{ds}{dt}\Big|_{t}=g^{\alpha}(\sigma(t))\,.

Then, we have

\sigma(t)=(q^{\alpha}(t),\displaystyle\frac{dq^{\alpha}}{dt}\Big|_{t},s(t))\quad\displaystyle\frac{ds}{dt}\Big|_{t}=g^{\alpha}(\sigma(t))\,.

and hence
\sigma(t)=c_{s}^{(1)}(t)=(\dot{c}(t),s(t))
where

of

So, in coordinates a

\frac{d^{2}q^{\alpha}}{dt^{2}}=f^{\alpha}(q,\dot{q},s),\quad\frac{ds}{dt}=g(q,\dot{q},s).

2.4
Local frames and quasi-velocities

Consider
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
a
local frame
on
Q
, that is, a local basis of vector fields on
Q
. Then each vector field
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(Q)
can be written as

Z=Z^{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}

for some local functions
Z^{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(Q)
.

Likewise, each tangent vector
w_{q}\in T_{q}Q
can be descomposed as
w_{q}=v^{\alpha}(w_{q})Z_{\alpha}(q)
, for some real numbers
v^{\alpha}(w_{q})
.

This new fibre coordinates

, which are the components of

with respect to the basis

, are called

.

If we consider the standard frame
\{\partial/\partial q^{\beta}\}
on
Q
, then

Z_{\alpha}(q)=Z^{\beta}_{\alpha}(q)\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\beta}}\Big|_{q}.

Therefore,

w_{q}=v^{\alpha}(w_{q})Z_{\alpha}(q)=v^{\alpha}(w_{q})Z^{\beta}_{\alpha}(q)\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\beta}}\Big|_{q},

and we have

u^{\beta}(w_{q})=v^{\alpha}(w_{q})Z^{\beta}_{\alpha}(q)\,.

So, we can use
\left(q^{\alpha},v^{\alpha},s\right)
as (non-natural) coordinates in
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
,

v^{\alpha}(w_{q})=(Z_{\beta}^{\alpha}(q))^{-1}u^{\beta}(w_{q})

From the local expressions (
3
), we can easily conclude that:

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
is any local frame on
Q
, then
\{Z_{\alpha}^{C},Z_{\alpha}^{V},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\}
is a local frame on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We may express (locally) a second-order differential equation (SODE)
\Gamma\in\mathfrak{X}\left(TQ\times\mathbb{R}\right)
in the form

\Gamma=v^{\alpha}Z^{C}_{\alpha}+\Gamma^{\alpha}Z^{V}_{\alpha}+g\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s},

(13)

where
v^{\alpha}
are the quasi-velocities, and
\Gamma^{\alpha}
,

are some local functions on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
.

The proof is a consequence of the above Proposition

and the following properties:
T\tau_{Q}\circ Z_{\alpha}^{C}=Z_{\alpha}\circ\tau_{Q}
and

3
Contact Lagrangian systems

In this section, we recall the Lagrangian contact formalism and we introduce the notion of
G
-invariant vector fields associated with a connection. For a regular Lagrangian, the solution of the Herglotz equations is given by the integral curves of some vector field, the so-called
Herglotz SODE
.

A
contact manifold
is a pair
(M,\eta)
where
M
has odd dimension
2n+1
and
\eta
is a contact
1
-form, that is,
\eta\wedge d\eta^{n}=\hskip-9.95845pt/\hskip 5.69054pt0
. On these manifolds, there exist a unique vector field (called
Reeb vector field
)
\mathcal{R}
such that
i_{\mathcal{R}}\eta=1
and
i_{\mathcal{R}}d\eta=0
.

Consider now a Lagrangian function
L:TQ\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
L=L(q^{\alpha},u^{\alpha},s)
.
We will assume that
L
is regular. That is, its Hessian matrix with respect to the velocities
(W_{\alpha\beta})
, where

W_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial u^{\alpha}\partial u^{\beta}},

is regular.

Another way to read the regularity of the Lagrangian
L
in terms of a non-standard frame is the following:
a Lagrangian
L
is regular if, and only if, the matrix

\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(Z_{\beta}^{V}\left(L\right)\right)\right)

has maximal rank.

We are able to define the Lagrangian
1
-form on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}

\eta_{L}=ds-\theta_{L}=ds-dL\circ S=ds-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}}\,dq^{\alpha}

(14)

which is a contact form on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
if and only if
L
is regular. Indeed,

d\eta_{L}=-\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial s\partial u^{\alpha}}\,ds\wedge dq^{\alpha}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial q^{\beta}\partial u^{\alpha}}\,dq^{\beta}\wedge dq^{\alpha}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial u^{\beta}\partial u^{\alpha}}\,du^{\beta}\wedge dq^{\alpha}.

(15)

and then

\eta_{L}\wedge d\eta_{L}^{n}=det(W_{\alpha\beta})d^{n}q\wedge d^{n}u\wedge ds=\hskip-9.95845pt/\hskip 5.69054pt0.

The triple
\left(TQ\times\mathbb{R},\eta_{L},E_{L}\right)
is said to be a
contact Lagrangian system
.
The corresponding Reeb vector field, determined by the relations

i_{\mathcal{R}_{L}}\eta_{L}=1\,\,,i_{\mathcal{R}_{L}}d\eta_{L}=0,

is locally given by

\mathcal{R}_{L}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}-W^{\alpha\beta}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial s\partial u^{\beta}}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}},

(16)

The Lagrangian energy of the system is defined as

E_{L}=\Delta(L)-L=u^{\alpha}\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}}-L.

(17)

From a straightforward computation, we obtain

\mathcal{R}_{L}\left(E_{L}\right)=-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}.

We denote by
\flat_{L}
the vector bundle isomorphism for the contact
form
\eta_{L}
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
. That is,

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\flat_{L}:&\mathfrak{X}\left(TQ\times\mathbb{R}\right)&\longrightarrow&\Omega^{1}\left(TQ\times\mathbb{R}\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&Z&\longmapsto&\flat_{L}(Z)=i_{Z}d\eta_{L}+\eta_{L}(Z)\eta_{L}.\end{array}

(18)

The dynamics of the system is given by the Herglotz vector field
\Gamma
, which is the unique vector
field satisfying

\flat_{L}\left(\Gamma\right)=dE_{L}+\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}-E_{L}\right)\eta_{L}.

(19)

A direct computation from equation (
19
)
shows that
\Gamma
is a
sode
locally given by

\Gamma=u^{\alpha}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\alpha}}+\Gamma^{\alpha}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}}+L\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s},

(20)

where the components
\Gamma^{\alpha}
satisfy the equation

\Gamma^{\beta}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial u^{\beta}\,\partial u^{\alpha}}+u^{\beta}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial q^{\beta}\,\partial u^{\alpha}}+L\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial s\,\partial u^{\alpha}}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{\alpha}}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}}.

(21)

Let
\left(q^{\alpha}(t),u^{\alpha}(t),s(t)\right)
be an holonomic integral curve of
\Gamma
, that is
u^{\alpha}(t)=\displaystyle\frac{dq^{\alpha}}{dt}\Big|_{t}
, then by substituting its values in equation (
21
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}q^{\beta}}{dt^{2}}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial u^{\beta}\,\partial u^{\alpha}}+\displaystyle\frac{dq^{\beta}}{dt}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial q^{\beta}\,\partial u^{\alpha}}+\displaystyle\frac{ds}{dt}\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial s\,\partial u^{\alpha}}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{\alpha}}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}},\,\,\qquad\displaystyle\frac{ds}{dt}=L.

(22)

which are the
Herglotz equations
considered by Herglotz in
1930

[
13
]
, usually written as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}}\right)-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{\alpha}}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}}\,\,,\qquad\displaystyle\frac{ds}{dt}=L.

(23)

The
sode

\Gamma\in\mathfrak{X}(TQ\times\mathbb{R})
, solution to (
19
), is called the
Herglotz vector field
associated with the Lagrangian function
L
.

Next, we will show an alternative form to write Herglotz equations.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma\in\mathfrak{X}\left(TQ\times\mathbb{R}\right)
be a SODE and
L
a regular Lagrangian on

. The following conditions are equivalent:

1.

\Gamma
is Lagrangian vector field ,

2.

\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma}\eta_{L}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial{L}}{\partial{s}}\eta_{L}\,,\quad\eta_{L}(\Gamma)=-E_{L},

3.

For each vector field

,

\Gamma\left(Z^{V}\left(L\right)\right)-Z^{C}\left(L\right)=\displaystyle\frac{\partial{L}}{\partial{s}}\left(Z^{V}\left(L\right)\right);

or, equivalently, for each local frame
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
of vector fields on
Q
,

\Gamma\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(L\right)\right)-Z_{\alpha}^{C}\left(L\right)=\displaystyle\frac{\partial{L}}{\partial{s}}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(L\right)\right),\quad\alpha=1,\ldots,n.

(24)

In particular, if we take the standard frame
\{\partial/\partial q^{\alpha}\}
on
Q
, equations (
24
) become

\Gamma\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}}\right)-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{\alpha}}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

1.\Rightarrow 2.
Assume
\Gamma
is the unique solution of (
19
) whose local expression is given in (
20
). Then, by the isomorphism (
18
) we have

i_{\Gamma}d\eta_{L}+\eta_{L}(\Gamma)\eta_{L}=dE_{L}-\left(\mathcal{R}_{L}(E_{L})+E_{L}\right)

i.e.

i_{\Gamma}d\eta_{L}-dE_{L}=-\displaystyle\frac{\partial{L}}{\partial{s}}\eta_{L},

where we used that
\eta_{L}(\Gamma)=-E_{L}
.
Therefore,

\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma}\eta_{L}=i_{\Gamma}d\eta_{L}+di_{\Gamma}\eta_{L}=i_{\Gamma}d\eta_{L}-dE_{L}=-\displaystyle\frac{\partial{L}}{\partial{s}}\eta_{L}.

2.\Rightarrow 3.
If we apply the relation in
2
to a complete lift
Z^{C}
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}0&=&\left(\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma}\eta_{L}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\eta_{L}\right)\left(Z^{C}\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=&\Gamma\left(\eta_{L}\left(Z^{C}\right)\right)-\eta_{L}\left([\Gamma,Z^{C}]\right)-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\eta_{L}\left(Z^{C}\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=&-\Gamma\left(Z^{V}\left(L\right)\right)+Z^{C}\left(L\right)+\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\left(Z^{V}\left(L\right)\right).\end{array}

When applied to a vertical lift
Z^{V}
or to
\partial/\partial s
we obtain the identity
``0=0"
. So the result follows.

The implications
2.\Rightarrow 1.
and
3.\Rightarrow 2.
are easily proved.
∎

4
G-invariant vector fields

Throughout the paper we will assume that the configuration space
Q
of the Lagrangian system is equipped with a free and proper left Lie group action
\Phi:G\times Q\to Q
of a connected Lie group
G
, which eventually will be the symmetry group of the Lagrangian system under consideration.

The projection

\pi_{Q}:Q\to Q/G

on the set of equivalence classes gives
Q
the structure of a principal fibre bundle with structure group
G
.

Let
\Phi_{g}:Q\to Q
and
\Phi_{q}:G\to Q
be the maps

\Phi_{g}(q)=\Phi(g,q)=g\,q,\quad\Phi_{q}(g)=\Phi(g,q)=g\,q\,.

Definition 4.1
.

A vector field
W\in\mathfrak{X}(Q)
is said to be G-invariant if

\left(\Phi_{g}\right)_{*}(q)\left(W(q)\right)=W\left(\Phi_{g}(q)\right),

for all
g\in G
,
q\in Q
.

In that case, the relation

\breve{W}\circ\pi_{Q}=T\pi_{Q}\circ W

(25)

uniquely defines a
reduced vector field

\breve{W}\in\mathfrak{X}(Q/G)
.

That is,

\breve{W}\left(\pi_{Q}(q)\right)=\left(\pi_{Q}\right)_{*}(q)\left(W(q)\right),\quad q\in Q.

Likewise, if
F:Q\to\mathbb{R}
is an invariant function on
Q
(say,
F\circ\Phi_{g}=F
), it can be reduced to a function
f:Q/G\to\mathbb{R}
with
f\circ\pi_{Q}=F
. We also have that

W\left(F\right)=W(f\circ\pi_{Q})=\breve{W}(f)\circ\pi_{Q},

(26)

that is,
\breve{W}(f)
is the reduced function on
Q/G
of the invariant function
W(F)
on
Q
.

From these relations, we have the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\phi
is an integral curve of an invariant vector field
W\in\mathfrak{X}(Q)
, then
\breve{\phi}=\pi_{Q}\circ\phi
is an integral curve of the reduced vector field
\breve{W}\in\mathfrak{X}(Q/G)
.

We denote by
\xi_{Q}
the
fundamental vector field
on
Q
associated to an element
\xi
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
,

\xi_{Q}(q)=\left(\Phi_{q}\right)_{*}(e)(\xi).

We have,

•

A function
F\in C^{\infty}(Q)
is invariant if, and only if,
\xi_{Q}(F)=0
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.

•

A vector field
W\in\mathfrak{X}(Q)
is invariant if, and only if,
[W,\xi_{Q}]=0
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The Lie bracket
[X,Y]
of two
G
-invariants vector fields
X
and
Y
on
Q
is also
G
-invariant.

From now we will use local coordinates on
Q
defined as follows. Let
U\subset Q/G
be an open set over which
Q
is locally trivial, so that

(\pi_{Q})^{-1}(U)\simeq U\times G\,.

We will use coordinates
(q^{\alpha})=(q^{i},q^{a})
on a suitable open subset
(\pi_{Q})^{-1}(U)
(containing
U\times\,\{e\}
, where
e
is the neutral element of the group
G
) such that
(q^{i})
are coordinates on
U
, and
(q^{a})
are coordinates on the fibre
G
.

Then, the local expression of the projection
\pi_{Q}:Q\to Q/G
is:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\pi_{Q})^{-1}(U)\simeq U\times G&\longrightarrow&U\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr(q^{\alpha})=(q^{i},q^{a})&\longmapsto&(q^{i}),\end{array}

(27)

where
1\leq a\leq\mathop{dim}G=d
,
1\leq i\leq\mathop{dim}Q-\mathop{dim}G=n-d
.

The left action of
G
onto
(\pi_{Q})^{-1}(U)\simeq U\times G
is given
by

\Phi_{g}(q,h)=(q,gh),

so, in these coordinates, we have

\Phi_{g}(q^{i},q^{a})=\left(q^{i},(\Phi_{g})^{b}(q^{i},q^{a})\right).

Let

X=X^{i}{\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}}+{\tilde{X}}^{a}{\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{a}}}

(28)

be the local expression of a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(Q)
.
If
X
is
G
-invariant then
we can conclude that the functions
X^{i}
on
Q
are invariant and they can be identified with functions on
Q/G
.
The reduced vector field
\breve{X}
we had defined in (
25
) is given by

{\breve{X}}={\breve{X}}^{i}{\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}}
,
where
{\breve{X}}^{i}\circ\pi_{Q}=X^{i}.

4.1
Connections on
\pi_{Q}:Q\to Q/G
and G-invariant vector fields

Suppose we are given a principal connection on the principal bundle
\pi_{Q}:Q\to Q/G
. We will consider three sets of vector fields
\{X_{i}\}
,

\{\widetilde{E}_{a}\}
and
\{\widehat{E}_{a}\}
on
Q
.

The first set,
\{X_{i}\}
, is given by the horizontal lifts of a coordinate basis of
vector fields
{\partial}/{\partial q^{i}}
on
Q/G
by the given principal connection. These
vector fields are
G
-invariant by construction, and they form a basis of the
horizontal subspace at any point.

The other two sets of vector fields,
\{\widetilde{E}_{a}\}
and
\{\widehat{E}_{a}\}
, will both form a basis for the vertical space of
\pi_{Q}
at each point.

The vector fields
\{\widetilde{E}_{a}=(E_{a})_{Q}\}
are the fundamental vector fields on

Q
, associated to a basis
\{E_{a}\}
of the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
. They are in general not invariant vector fields. Since they are vertical by construction, we can write

\widetilde{E}_{a}=K^{b}_{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{q^{b}}},

(29)

for some non-singular matrix-valued function
(K^{b}_{a})
.

The quasi-velocities
corresponding to the basis
X_{i},\widetilde{E}_{a}
will be denoted by
(v^{i},v^{a})
, so for every tangent vector
v_{m}
,
v_{m}=v^{i}\,X_{i}+v^{a}\,\widetilde{E}_{a}
.

The vector fields
\widehat{E}_{a}
in the last set are defined as

\widehat{E}_{a}\left(q,g\right)=\left({Ad_{g}\,E_{a}}\right)_{Q}\left(q,g\right)=\widetilde{Ad_{g}\,E_{a}}(q,g)

(30)

where we are using the local trivialization (
27
), and
Ad
is the adjoint map and the notation
\xi_{Q}
refers again to the fundamental vector field of
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. One easily verifies that these vector fields are all invariant.

The relation between
\widehat{E}_{a}
and
\widetilde{E}_{a}
can be
expressed as

\widehat{E}_{a}(q,g)\,=\,A^{b}_{a}(g)\widetilde{E}_{b}(q,g),

(31)

where
(A^{b}_{a}(g))
is the matrix
representing
Ad_{g}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the basis
\{E_{a}\}
of

\mathfrak{g}
. In particular
A^{b}_{a}(e)=\delta^{b}_{a}
.

If we set

X_{i}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}-\gamma^{b}_{i}(q^{i},q^{a})\widehat{E}_{b}

(32)

the invariance of
X_{i}
implies that
\partial\gamma^{b}_{i}/\partial q^{a}=0
.

The quasi-velocities
corresponding to the basis
X_{i},\hat{E}_{a}
will be denoted by
(v^{i},w^{a})
, so for every tangent vector
v_{m}
,
v_{m}=v^{i}\,X_{i}+w^{a}\,\hat{E}_{a}
.

Let
C_{ab}^{c}
be the structure constants of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, that is
[E_{a},E_{b}]=C_{ab}^{c}E_{c}
, and
K^{a}_{ij}
be the components of the curvature of the principal connection (with respect to the vertical frame
{\widehat{E}}_{a}
),

K^{a}_{ij}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\gamma^{a}_{j}}}{\partial{q^{i}}}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\gamma^{a}_{i}}}{\partial{q^{j}}}+\gamma_{i}^{b}\gamma^{c}_{j}C^{a}_{bc}\,.

One easily verifies that the Lie brackets of the vector fields of interest are as follows (see e.g.
[
17
]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

\begin{array}[]{lll}(1)\,\,[\widetilde{E}_{a},\widetilde{E}_{b}]=-C_{ab}^{c}\widetilde{E}_{c},&(2)\,\,[\widehat{E}_{a},\widehat{E}_{b}]=C_{ab}^{c}\widehat{E}_{c},&(3)\,\,[X_{i},\widetilde{E}_{a}]=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\,(4)\,\,[X_{i},\widehat{E}_{a}]=\Upsilon^{b}_{ia}\widehat{E}_{b},&(5)\,\,[X_{i},X_{j}]=-K^{a}_{ij}\widehat{E}_{a},&(6)\,\,[\widetilde{E}_{a},\widehat{E}_{b}]=0,\end{array}

(33)

where
\Upsilon_{ia}^{b}=X_{i}(A_{a}^{c})\bar{A}_{c}^{b}
.

Proof.

We have that
(3)
and
(6)
are consequence of that
X_{i}
and
\widehat{E}_{b}
are
G
-invariant.

5
Symmetry reduction of a Herglotz vector field

In this section, we show that, if the contact Lagrangian is
G
-invariant, then so is its Lagrangian SODE. The integral curves of the reduced SODE will provide the contact Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations.

Suppose there is an action
\Phi:G\times Q\to Q
which is free and proper. It induces a lifted action
\Phi^{TQ}=T\Phi:G\times TQ\to TQ
on the tangent bundle
TQ
given by

\Phi^{TQ}\left(g,v_{q}\right)=T\Phi_{g}(v_{q})=\left(\Phi_{g}\right)_{*}(q)(v_{q}),

where
v_{q}\in TQ
and
g\in G
. The lifted action
\Phi^{TQ}
is also free and proper and, therefore,
\pi_{TQ}:TQ\to TQ/G
is a principal fiber bundle.

In order to give an action of
G
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
, we will consider the following induced action

\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}_{g}=\left(\Phi_{g}^{TQ},Id_{\mathbb{R}}\right):TQ\times\mathbb{R}\to TQ\times\mathbb{R},

so that,
G
acts on
\mathbb{R}
as the identity

\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}\left(g,(v_{q},s)\right)=(\Phi^{TQ}(g,v_{q}),s)=\left(\left(\Phi_{g}\right)_{*}(q)(v_{q}),s\right)

and then the coordinate function
s
is
G
-invariant. Therefore, the projection

\pi_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}:TQ\times\mathbb{R}\to\left(TQ\times\mathbb{R}\right)/G\simeq\displaystyle\frac{TQ}{G}\times\mathbb{R}

gives
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
the structure of a principal fiber bundle over
\frac{TQ}{G}\times\mathbb{R}
, provided that the action
\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}
is free and proper.

As before, we will denote by
\xi_{Q}
the fundamental vector field corresponding to
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. From the definition of the complete lift in Section
2.2
, it follows that the fundamental vector field
\xi_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}
given by the action
\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}
is actually the complete lift
\xi_{Q}^{C}
of the fundamental vector field
\xi_{Q}
given by the action on
Q
, that is,

\xi_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}=\xi_{Q}^{C}.

Now, from the properties in (
5
) and since
\partial/\partial s
is
G
-invariant (namely,
[\partial/\partial s,\xi_{Q}^{C}]=0
), we have the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

If the frame
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
on
Q
is invariant, then the frame
\left\{Z_{\alpha}^{C},Z_{\alpha}^{V},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right\}
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
is also invariant, with respect to the action
\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
,

Without loss of generality we may suppose that the local frame
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
on
Q
, consists of only invariant vector fields (for example, we can use the invariant frame
\{X_{i},{\widehat{E}}_{a}\}
that we had introduced in Section
4.1
).

We will use coordinates
(q^{\alpha})=(q^{i},q^{a})
on
Q
that are adapted to the principal fiber bundle structure
Q\to Q/G
, as explained in Section
4
.

The quasi-velocities
corresponding to the basis
X_{i},\widehat{E}_{a}
will be denoted by
(v^{i},w^{a})
, so for every tangent vector
v_{q}
,
v_{q}=v^{i}\,X_{i}+w^{a}\,\widehat{E}_{a}
,
then the set
(q^{i},q^{a},v^{i},w^{a},s)
represents coordinates on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
adapted to the basis

\{X_{i},\widehat{E}_{a},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\}
of vector fields.

The coordinate
s
is
G
-invariant, since
\xi_{Q}^{C}(s)=0
. The coordinates
q^{i}
are also
G
-invariant since the fundamental vector fields
\xi_{Q}
are vertical with
respect to the projection
\pi_{Q}:Q\to Q/G
, and therefore we have
0=\xi_{Q}(q^{i})=\xi_{Q}^{C}(q^{i})
.

Now we will see that the coordinate functions
v^{i},w^{a}
are also
G
-invariant functions on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
, in fact
we shall prove that

\xi_{Q}^{C}(v^{i})=0
and
\xi_{Q}^{C}(w^{a})=0.

Let
\theta
be a
1
-form on
Q
. We define the function
\overrightarrow{\theta}
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
as

\overrightarrow{\theta}(v_{q},s)=\theta(v_{q}).

In local coordinates
(q^{\alpha},u^{\alpha},s)
, if
\theta=\theta_{\alpha}\,dq^{\alpha}
,
then

\overrightarrow{\theta}=\theta_{\alpha}\,u^{\alpha}.

(34)

From Remark
2.2
, (
2
) and (
34
), we can conclude the following relations.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
Z
be a vector field on
Q
,
f
a function on

Q
, and
\theta
a
1
-form on
Q
. Then

Z^{C}(f^{V})=(Z(f))^{V},\quad Z^{V}(f^{V})=0,\quad Z^{C}(\overrightarrow{\theta})=\overrightarrow{({\mathcal{L}}_{Z}\theta)},\quad Z^{V}(\overrightarrow{\theta})=(\theta(Z))^{V}.

(35)

Lemma 5.3
.

If
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
is a local frame on
Q

and
\{\theta^{\alpha}\}
is its dual basis, then the local quasi-velocities

v^{\alpha}
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
given by

u^{\beta}=v^{\alpha}Z^{\beta}_{\alpha}

can in fact be represented by the linear functions
v^{\alpha}=\overrightarrow{(\theta^{\alpha})}
.

Proof.

It is a straightforward computation since

Z_{\alpha}=Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{q^{\beta}}},\qquad\theta^{\alpha}=\theta^{\alpha}_{\beta}dq^{\beta},\qquad\overrightarrow{\theta^{\alpha}}=\theta^{\alpha}_{\beta}u^{\beta}=\theta^{\alpha}_{\beta}v^{\gamma}Z^{\beta}_{\gamma}=v^{\alpha}.

∎

Now, from Lemma
5.2
we obtain

\xi_{Q}^{C}(v^{\alpha})=\xi_{Q}^{C}(\overrightarrow{\theta^{\alpha}})=\overrightarrow{({\mathcal{L}}_{\xi_{Q}}\theta^{\alpha})}.

Finally, we observe that

({\mathcal{L}}_{\xi_{Q}}\theta^{\alpha})(Z_{\beta})={\mathcal{L}}_{\xi_{Q}}(\theta^{\alpha}(Z_{\beta}))-\theta^{\alpha}({\mathcal{L}}_{\xi_{Q}}Z_{\beta})=\xi_{Q}(\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta})-\theta^{\alpha}([\xi_{Q},Z_{\beta}])=0,

since

is

\xi_{Q}^{C}(v^{\alpha})=0.

In particular, if

and

\xi_{Q}^{C}(v^{i})=0
and
\xi_{Q}^{C}(w^{a})=0
.

5.1
Invariant Herglotz SODES

For the remainder of the paper we will assume that the Lagrangian
L
is invariant under the action
\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}
, for a connected Lie group
G
. In view of what we said before this means that
\xi_{Q}^{C}(L)=0
, for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Given
L:TQ\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
a regular invariant Lagrangian, then the Herglotz SODE
\Gamma
associated with the Lagrangian function
L
is
G
-invariant, that is

0=[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},\Gamma].

Proof.

From (
13
) we know that if
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
is an invariant local frame on
Q
, the local expression of a Lagrangian SODE is

\Gamma=v^{\alpha}Z^{C}_{\alpha}+\Gamma^{\alpha}Z^{V}_{\alpha}+L\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}.

(36)

We only need to check that
[\tilde{E}_{a}^{C},\Gamma]=0
, where
\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}
is the fundamental vector field
(E_{a})_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}
of the action on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
.

From the invariance of the vector fields
Z_{\alpha}^{C}
,
Z_{\alpha}^{V}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
, and the functions
L
and
v^{\alpha}
we have

[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},\Gamma]=[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},v^{\alpha}Z^{C}_{\alpha}+\Gamma^{\alpha}Z^{V}_{\alpha}+L\frac{\partial}{\partial s}]=\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(\Gamma^{\alpha})Z_{\alpha}^{V},

(37)

so
\Gamma
is
G
-invariant if the functions
\Gamma^{\alpha}
are invariants.

We apply the vector field
\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}
to both sides of the equations (
24
)

\Gamma\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(L\right)\right)-Z_{\alpha}^{C}\left(L\right)=\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(L\right)\right).

and we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}\left(\Gamma\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(L\right)\right)-Z_{\alpha}^{C}\left(L\right)\right)&=&\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}\left(\Gamma\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(L\right)\right)\right)-\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{C}\left(L\right)\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=&[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},\Gamma]\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)\right)+\Gamma\left(\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)\right)\right)-[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},Z_{\alpha}^{C}](L)-Z_{\alpha}^{C}\left(\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(L)\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=&[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},\Gamma]\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)\right),\end{array}

because of
\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(L)=0
and the zero Lie brackets of
[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},Z_{\alpha}^{C}]
and
[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},Z_{\alpha}^{V}]
.

Since

0=[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}](L)=\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s})-\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}(\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(L))=\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s})

then

\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}\left(L\right)\right)\right)=\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\right)Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)+\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s}\,\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)\right)=0.

So, by making use of expression (
37
), we have

0=[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},\Gamma]\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)\right)=\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(\Gamma^{\beta})Z_{\beta}^{V}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)\right).

Given that the matrix
Z_{\beta}^{V}\left(Z_{\alpha}^{V}(L)\right)
has maximal rank for a regular Lagrangian
L
, the result follows.
∎

Now, since
\Gamma
is invariant, it reduces to a vector field
\breve{\Gamma}
on
(TQ\times\mathbb{R})/G=TQ/G\times\mathbb{R}
defined by

\breve{\Gamma}\left([v_{q}],s_{0}\right)=\left(\pi_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}\right)_{*}\left(v_{q},s_{0}\right)\Gamma\left(v_{q},s_{0}\right),

for
v_{q}\in TQ
,
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

The goal of the next few sections is to provide a coordinate expression of this vector field. To do so, we will need to invoke a principal connection on the bundle
Q\to Q/G
.

5.2
Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations

Suppose we are given a principal connection on the principal bundle
\pi_{Q}:Q\to Q/G
. We choose the invariant frame
\{X_{i},\widehat{E}_{a},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\}
(see Section
4.1
), and we will write
(q^{i},q^{a},v^{i},w^{a},s)
for the coordinates and the corresponding quasi-velocities on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
.

From Lemma
5.1
we know that
the frame

\{Z_{\alpha}^{C},Z_{\alpha}^{V},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\}=\{X_{i}^{C},X_{i}^{V},\widehat{E}_{a}^{C},\widehat{E}_{a}^{V},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\}

consists only of invariant vector fields on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
. Also the coordinate functions
(q^{i},v^{i},w^{a},s)
are
G
-invariant functions on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
and, therefore, they can be used as coordinates on

(TQ\times\mathbb{R})/G
.
In summary, we may say that the canonical projections are locally given by

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\pi_{Q}:Q&\to&Q/G\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr(q^{i},q^{a})&\mapsto&(q^{i})\end{array}\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{ccc}\pi_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}:TQ\times\mathbb{R}&\to&(TQ\times\mathbb{R})/G\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr(q^{i},q^{a},v^{i},w^{a},s)&\mapsto&(q^{i},v^{i},w^{a},s).\end{array}

(38)

Lemma 5.4
.

If we apply the vector fields
X_{i}^{C},X_{i}^{V},\widehat{E}_{a}^{C},\widehat{E}_{a}^{V},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
to the invariant functions

we obtain

\begin{array}[]{llll}X_{i}^{C}(q^{j})=\delta^{j}_{i}\,,&X_{i}^{C}(v^{j})=0\,,&X_{i}^{C}(w^{a})=-\Upsilon^{a}_{ib}w^{b}+K^{a}_{ij}v^{j}\,,&X_{i}^{C}(s)=0\,,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr X_{i}^{V}(q^{j})=0\,,&X_{i}^{V}(v^{j})=\delta^{j}_{i}\,,&X_{i}^{V}(w^{a})=0\,,&X_{i}^{V}(s)=0\,,\\

Illustrative example: Dissipative Wong’s equations

(Q,g_{Q})

\pi:Q\to Q/G

Q/G

g_{Q}(v_{q},u_{q})=\kappa(e)(\omega(v_{q}),\omega(u_{q}))+g(\pi(q))(\pi_{*}(q)(v_{q}),\pi_{*}(q)(u_{q})),

for
v_{q}
,
u_{q}\in T_{q}Q
and

\kappa
at the identity element

The fact that the symmetry group acts as isometries means that the fundamental vector fields

are Killing vector fields,

g_{Q}(X_{i},\widehat{E_{a}})=0

The further assumption about the vertical part of the metric has the following implications.
It means in the first place that

(since
\mathscr{L}_{\widetilde{E_{c}}}g_{Q}(\widehat{E_{a}},\widehat{E_{b}})=0

on

G

This tensor field is given by

\bar{\Omega}({\bf H}\,W)=0,\qquad\bar{\Omega}({\bf V}W)={\bf V}W

(55)

Let
W
be a vector field on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
, then

\begin{array}[]{ccl}{\mathcal{L}}_{\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}}\bar{\Omega}(W)&=&[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},\bar{\Omega}(W)]-\bar{\Omega}([\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},W])\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=&-\bar{\Omega}([\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},{\bf H}W])=-{\bf V}[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},{\bf H}W]\end{array}

where have used (
55
) and that
[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},{\bf V}W]
is vertical. Computing
[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},{\bf H}W]
we obtain that its vertical part is

{\bf V}[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},{\bf H}W]=W^{i}[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},H_{i}]

(56)

and thus
{\mathcal{L}}_{\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}}\bar{\Omega}(W)=0
if the vector fields
H_{i}
is invariant.

A direct computation shows that

{\bf V}[\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C},H_{i}]=\left(-\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(B^{d}_{i})+B^{b}_{i}C^{d}_{ab}\right)\widetilde{E}_{d}^{C}.

(57)

therefore
H_{i}
is invariant if

\widetilde{E}_{a}^{C}(B^{d}_{i})=B^{b}_{i}C^{d}_{ab}.

(58)

Now, using the identities (
50
) and that
L
is invariant one obtains

\widetilde{E}_{d}^{C}(g_{ab})=C^{e}_{da}g_{eb}+C^{e}_{db}g_{ae},\quad\widetilde{E}_{d}^{C}(g_{ib})=C^{e}_{db}g_{ie},\quad\widetilde{E}_{d}^{C}(g^{ec})=-\widetilde{E}_{d}^{C}(g_{ab})g^{ac}g^{eb},

(59)

and with these identities one deduce finally (
58
) .

7
Reconstruction

Now that we have derived the reduced form of the Herglotz-Euler-Lagrange equations it remains to
consider the problem of reconstruction: suppose we can find a solution of these equations, that is
an integral curve
\breve{\Gamma}
, how do we reconstruct from it a solution of the original equations, that is, an integral curve of
\Gamma
.

There is in fact a standard method for reconstructing integral curves of an invariant vector
field from reduced data, which makes use of connection theory
[
2
,
15
]
.

Let

\breve{c}(t)
be
an integral curve of
\breve{\Gamma}
on
TQ/G\times\mathbb{R}
and let
(v_{q_{0}},s_{o})
be a point of
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
in the fibre over
\breve{c}(0)
: we aim to find
the integral curve of
\Gamma
though
(v_{q_{0}},s_{o})
.

Let
\breve{c}^{H}(t)
be the horizontal lift through
(v_{q_{0}},s_{o})
of
\breve{c}(t)
with respect to the connection
\Gamma
on
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
, this is the unique curve in
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
projecting onto
\breve{c}(t)
such that

\Omega(\dot{\breve{c}}^{H}(t))=0
and
\breve{c}^{H}(0)=(v_{q_{0}},s_{o})
.

Since

\pi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}(c(t))=\pi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))=\breve{c}(t)

there exists a curve
g(t)
on
G
such that

c(t)=\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}(g(t),\breve{c}^{H}(t))\equiv g(t)\,\cdot\,\breve{c}^{H}(t)

(60)

with
g(0)=e
.

Now, we define a curve
\xi(t)
on
\mathfrak{g}
as follows

\xi(t)=(L_{g(t)^{-1}})_{*}(g(t))\dot{g}(t)

(61)

or, equivalently,

\dot{g}(t)=(L_{g(t)})_{*}(e)\xi(t)\qquad\mbox{with}\quad g(0)=e.

Now, using the following lemma

Lemma 7.1
.

For an action
\Phi:G\times M\to M
we have

(\Phi_{m})_{*}(g)v_{g}=(\Phi_{g})_{*}(m)\eta_{M}(m)\quad\eta=(L_{g^{-1}})_{*}(g)v_{g}\in\mathfrak{g}

and (
60
) and (
61
), we get

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\Gamma(c(t))=\dot{c}(t)&=&(\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}_{g(t)})_{*}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))\,\dot{\breve{c}}^{H}(t)\,+\,((\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}})_{\breve{c}^{H}(t)})_{*}(g(t))\dot{g}(t)\\
\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0pt\cr&=&(\Phi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}_{g(t)})_{*}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))\left[\dot{\breve{c}}^{H}(t)\,+\,(\xi(t))_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))\right]\in T_{c(t)}(TQ\times\mathbb{R})\end{array}

(62)

From equation (
62
) and since
\Gamma
is invariant, we deduce

\dot{\breve{c}}^{H}(t)+(\xi(t))_{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))=\Gamma(\breve{c}^{H}(t))

then, the reconstruction equation is

\Omega(\breve{c}^{H}(t))(\Gamma(\breve{c}^{H}(t)))=\xi(t)\,.

(63)

From (
42
) and Proposition
6.1
we have

\Omega(X_{i}^{C})=B^{a}_{i}\,E_{a},\quad\Omega(\hat{E}^{C}_{a})=A^{b}_{a}\,E_{b},\quad\Omega(X_{i}^{V})=\Omega(\widetilde{E}^{V}_{a})=0,\quad\Omega(\widetilde{E}^{C}_{a})=E_{a},\quad\Omega(\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{s}})=0.

and therefore

\Omega(\Gamma)=(A^{a}_{b}w^{b}+B^{a}_{i}v^{i})E_{a}\,.

(64)

The reconstruction equation is therefore

\begin{array}[]{rl}\xi(t)=&\left[A^{a}_{b}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))w^{b}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))+B^{a}_{i}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))v^{i}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))\right]E_{a}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr=&\left[A^{a}_{b}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))w^{b}(t)+B^{a}_{i}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))v^{i}(t)\right]E_{a}\,.\end{array}

(65)

since
v^{i}
and
w^{a}
are invariant, and then
v^{i}(t)
and
w^{a}(t)
are just their values on
\breve{c}(t)
.

Let us denote by

\breve{c}(t)=(q^{i}(t),v^{i}(t),w^{a}(t),s(t))\qquad\breve{c}^{H}(t)=(q^{i}(t),h^{a}(t),v^{i}(t),w^{a}(t),s(t))

the local coordinates of the corresponding of curves, where
h(t)=(h^{a}(t))
is a curve on
G
determined by
(v_{q_{0}},s_{o})
and the relation
\Omega(\dot{\breve{c}}^{H}(t))=0
.

The reconstruction equation (
63
) is therefore

\xi(t)=(A^{a}_{b}w^{b}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))+B^{a}_{i}v^{i}(\breve{c}^{H}(t)))E_{a}=(A^{a}_{b}w^{b}(t)+B^{a}_{i}v^{i}(t))E_{a}

We assume that we are able to calculate the integral curve

\breve{c}(t)=(q^{i}(t),v^{i}(t),w^{a}(t),s(t))

of the reduced vector fiel
\breve{\Gamma}
through
\pi^{TQ\times\mathbb{R}}(v_{q_{0}},s_{0})
.

The horizontal lift of

\breve{c}
is a curve in
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
of the form

\breve{c}^{H}(t)=(q(t),h(t),v^{i}(t),w^{a}(t),s(t))

where
h(t)
is a curve in
G
to be determined by

(v_{0},s_{0})
and by the relation

\Omega(\dot{\breve{c}}^{H})=0
.

The reconstruction equation can therefore be written as

\xi(t)=\left(A^{a}_{b}(h(t))\,w^{b}(t)+B^{a}_{i}(\breve{c}^{H}(t))\,v^{i}(t)\right)E_{a}

and the integral curve of

\Gamma
is just

c(t)=(q(t),g(t)h(t),v^{i}(t),w^{a}(t)).

7.1
A full example

Let
G
be the Lie group of invertible affine transformations on the real line. An element of this group is an affine map of the form
T_{(\theta,\phi)}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\ t\mapsto\exp(\theta)t+\phi

and can be represented by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\exp(\theta)&\phi\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The identity element is just
t\mapsto t
(the identity matrix) and multiplication on the left of
(\theta_{2},\phi_{2})
by
(\theta_{1},\phi_{1})
is given by the composition of the two affine maps, i.e. the element

(\theta_{1},\phi_{1})\ast(\theta_{2},\phi_{2})=(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2},\exp(\theta_{1})\phi_{2}+\phi_{1}).

The corresponding Lie algebra is the vector space spanned by the matrices

E_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }E_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Take now the manifold
Q
to be
G\times\mathbb{R}
and the action of
G
on
Q
given by the left translation on the first factor of
Q
. We will denote by
x
the coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
. Then
\pi:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a (trivial) principal fibre bundle and there is a trivial principal connection associated to the identification of
TQ
with the direct sum
TG\oplus T\mathbb{R}
.

A basis of fundamental vector fields is

\tilde{E}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi},\quad\tilde{E}_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi},

which span the vertical space
V=\ker T\pi=TG
. In addition, the horizontal space with respect to the trivial connection is spanned by the vector field
X=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
. Equivalently, the connection form is the map
\omega:TQ\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
given by

\omega(\gamma^{0}X+\gamma^{1}\tilde{E}_{1}+\gamma^{2}\tilde{E}_{2})=\gamma^{1}E_{1}+\gamma^{2}E_{2}.

The adapted coordinates
(v^{i},v^{a})
with respect to this basis are
v^{0}=\dot{x}
and
v^{1}=\dot{\theta},v^{2}=\dot{\phi}-\phi\dot{\theta}
.
The only non-vanishing Lie algebra structure cosntant is
C_{12}^{2}=-1
in virtue of the fact that
[\tilde{E}_{1},\tilde{E}_{2}]=-\tilde{E}_{2}
. The complete and vertical lifts of this basis are

\tilde{E}^{C}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}+\dot{\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\phi}},\quad\tilde{E}^{C}_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi},\quad\tilde{E}^{V}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\theta}}+\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\phi}},\quad\tilde{E}^{V}_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\phi}}.

Alternatively, an invariant basis of vector fields is given by
\{\hat{E}_{1},\hat{E}_{2},X\}
, where

\hat{E}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\quad\hat{E}_{2}=\exp(\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi},

and its adapted coordinates are
v^{0}=\dot{x},w^{1}=\dot{\theta}
and
w^{2}=\exp(-\theta)\dot{\phi}
. The complete and vertical lifts of the above basis are

X^{C}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\quad X^{V}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{x}},\quad\hat{E}^{C}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\quad\hat{E}^{C}_{2}=\exp(\theta)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}+\dot{\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right),

\hat{E}^{V}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\theta}},\quad\hat{E}^{V}_{2}=\exp(\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\phi}}.

In addition, the matrix
A
of change of basis
\hat{E}_{a}(x,g)=A^{b}_{a}(g)\tilde{E}_{b}(x,g)
, is

A(g)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\phi&\exp(\theta)\end{pmatrix}.

If we use the invariant fiber coordinates
(v^{0},w^{a})
, the induced action on
TQ
is

\psi_{TM}(\theta_{1},\phi_{1})(x,(\theta,\phi),\dot{x},w^{1},w^{2})=(x,(\theta_{1},\phi_{1})\ast(\theta,\phi),\dot{x},w^{1},e^{\theta_{1}}w^{2}).

Since the coordinates
(x,\dot{x},w^{1},w^{2})
can be interpreted as coordinates on
TQ/G=T\mathbb{R}\times TG/G=T\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{g}
,
G
-invariance of a Lagrangian function implies that the group variables
h=(\theta,\phi)
do not explicitly appear in the Lagrangian, when it is written in terms of the invariant fiber coordinates.

Consider the contact Lagrangian
L:TQ\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

L(x,h,\dot{x},\dot{h},s)=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\theta}^{2}+q\dot{x}\dot{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{2}+\ln(\exp(-\theta)\dot{\phi})-\gamma s,

where
q
is a constant. The Lagrangian is
G
-invariant since
\tilde{E}^{C}_{1}(L)=0=\tilde{E}^{C}_{2}(L)
and in the invariant fiber coordinates
(\dot{x},w^{1},w^{2})
, the Lagrangian is

L=\frac{1}{2}(w^{1})^{2}+q\dot{x}w^{1}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{2}+\ln(w^{2})-\gamma s.

The Hessian matrix in the basis
\{\tilde{E}_{a},X\}
is given by

g=\begin{pmatrix}1-\frac{\phi^{2}}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}&-\frac{\phi}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}&q\\
-\frac{\phi}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}&-\frac{1}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}&0\\
q&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The determinant of
g
is
(q^{2}-1)/\dot{\phi}^{2}
, so the Lagrangian is regular as long as
q^{2}=\hskip-9.95845pt/\hskip 5.69054pt1
. The upper left
(2,2)
matrix represents
(g_{ab})
. It is non-singular since its determinant is
-1/\dot{\phi}^{2}
. Its inverse is

(g^{ab})=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\phi\\
-\phi&\phi^{2}-\dot{\phi}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The Euler-Lagrange-Herglotz equations for the Lagrangian
L
are

q\ddot{\theta}+\ddot{x}=-\gamma(\dot{x}+q\dot{\theta}),\quad\ddot{\theta}+q\ddot{x}+1=-\gamma(\dot{\theta}+q\dot{x}),\quad-\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}=-\frac{\gamma}{\dot{\phi}}.

We will assume that
\dot{\phi}_{0}>0
. Then
\ddot{\phi}=\gamma\dot{\phi}
.

We now use the symmetry reduction and reconstruction to reobtain the same solutions. Since for the current example the connection coefficients
of the trivial connection vanish, the Lagrange-Poincare-Herglotz equations become

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial l}{\partial w^{b}}\right)=\frac{\partial l}{\partial w^{a}}C^{a}_{bd}w^{d}+\frac{\partial l}{\partial s}\frac{\partial l}{\partial w^{a}},

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial l}{\partial\dot{x}}\right)-\frac{\partial l}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial l}{\partial s}\frac{\partial l}{\partial\dot{x}}.

and substituting for the Lagrangian function we have that

\dot{w}_{1}+q\ddot{x}=-1-\gamma(w_{1}+q\dot{x}),\quad\dot{w}_{2}=-w_{1}w_{2}+\gamma w_{2},\quad q\dot{w}_{1}+\ddot{x}=-\gamma(\dot{x}+qw_{1}).

From the reduced solution
\check{v}(t)=(x(t),\dot{x}(t),w_{1}(t),w_{2}(t),s(t))
,
we could determine the remaining coordinates
(\theta(t),\phi(t))
directly from the relations
\dot{\theta}=w_{1}
and

\exp(-\theta)\dot{\phi}=w_{2}
. However, let us reproduce the reconstruction process in detail: first we calculate the horizontal lift of
\check{v}(t)
, and then we use it in the reconstruction
equation. In this way, we will see the effect of the connection in the reconstruction
equation.

The coordinates of the horizontal lift using the generalized mechanical connection are
\check{v}^{H}(t)=(x(t),\phi_{H}(t),\theta_{H}(t),\dot{x}(t),w_{1}(t),w_{2}(t),s(t))
, with respect to the invariant basis. They can be determined
by the relation
0=\Omega(\dot{\check{v}}^{H}(t))
, which in coordinates reads

\dot{\theta}_{H}=-q\dot{x},\quad\dot{\phi}_{H}=-q\dot{x}\phi_{H}+q\dot{x}\phi_{H}=0.

Therefore,
\theta_{H}(t)=-qx(t)+qx_{0}+\theta_{0}
and
\phi_{H}(t)=\phi_{0}
.
Now we determine the curve
g(t)=(\theta_{1}(t),\phi_{1}(t))
in
G
such that
v=g\check{v}^{H}
is the solution of
the Euler-Lagrange-Herglotz equations with the given initial values. This curve is the solution through
the identity of the reconstruction equation
g^{-1}\dot{g}=\Omega(\Gamma(\check{v}^{H}))
. The left-hand side is
\dot{\theta}_{1}E_{1}+\exp(-\theta_{1})\dot{\phi}_{1}E_{2}
. The right-hand side is
(w_{1}+q\dot{x})\tilde{E}^{C}_{1}\circ\check{v}^{H}+(w_{2}+q\phi_{H}\dot{x})\tilde{E}^{C}_{1}\circ\check{v}^{H}
. The
reconstruction equations are therefore

\dot{\theta}_{1}=w_{1}+q\dot{x},\quad\exp(-\theta_{1})\dot{\phi}_{1}=-\phi_{H}w_{1}+\exp(\theta_{H})w_{2}-q\phi_{H}\dot{x}.

Thus, we may write the solution
v(t)=(x(t),\phi(t),\theta(t),\dot{x}(t),w_{1}(t),w_{2}(t),s(t))
with respect to the invariant basis, where
\phi(t)
and
\theta(t)
are of the form
\theta(t)=\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{H}(t)
and
\phi(t)=\phi_{1}(t)+\exp{(\theta_{1}(t))}\phi_{H}(t)
, from where we deduce that
\dot{\theta}=w_{1}
and
\dot{\phi}=\exp(\theta)w_{2}
.

8
Conclusions and further work

In this paper we have developed a process for the reduction of Lagrangian contact systems in the presence of a group of symmetries. We have used the quasi-coordinate method and adapted local references to obtain the corresponding reduced equations, the so-called Lagrange-Poincaré-Herglotz equations. One of the necessary geometrical constructions is the prolongation of a connection in a principal fiber bundle using the Hessian of the Lagrangian.

In a future work, we propose the following objectives directly related to the research investigated in this paper:

•

Develop a reduction procedure for discrete contact Lagrangian systems with symmetries.

•

Explore the analog results of the present paper to the case of contact Lagrangian systems subject to nonholonomic constraints and symmetries.

•

Extend the above results for higher-order contact Lagrangian systems with symmetries.

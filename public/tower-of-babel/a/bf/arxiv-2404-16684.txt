Monolithic two-level Schwarz preconditioner for Biot’s consolidation model in two space dimensions
Stefan Meggendorfer, Guido Kanschat, Johannes Kraus

Abstract
This paper addresses the construction and analysis of a class of domain decomposition methods for the iterative solution of the quasi-static Biot problem in three-field formulation. The considered discrete model arises from time discretization by the implicit Euler method and space discretization by a family of strongly mass-conserving methods exploiting H div H^{\text{div}} -conforming approximations of the solid displacement and fluid flux fields. For the resulting saddle-point problem, we construct monolithic overlapping domain decomposition (DD) methods whose analysis relies on a transformation into an equivalent symmetric positive definite system and on stable decompositions of the involved finite element spaces under proper problem-dependent norms. Numerical results on two-dimensional test problems are in accordance with the provided theoretical uniform convergence estimates for the two-level multiplicative Schwarz method.

Introduction

Mathematical models describing the interaction of fluid flow with the mechanical deformation of porous media
go back to the pioneering works by Karl von Terzaghi and Maurice Anthony Biot in the first half of the last
century
[
Ter25
,
Bio41
,
Ter43
,
Bio55
]
.
Important application areas include, but are not limited to, petroleum and reservoir engineering, CO2 sequestration
and the mechanics of biological tissues. Partial saturation, the incorporation of two- or multi-phase flow, thermal
processes, and/or large deformations, and/or the consideration of multiple fluid networks lead to even more complex,
sometimes highly nonlinear mathematical models that require physically correct (structure-preserving) discretization
and robust iterative solution methods.

The well-posedness of the basic Biot model of consolidation on the continuous and discrete levels has
been established in the works of Zenisek and Showalter using semi-group theory and Galerkin discretization
methods
[
Že84a
,
Že84b
,
Sho00
]
.
The simplest linear model uses a two-field formulation of Biot’s equations for the solid displacement
\bm{u}
and pore
pressure
p
. Popular time discretizations of the quasi-static differential algebraic system are the backward Euler
or high-order strongly stable time integration methods, see, e.g.,
[
ABK15
]
.
A stability and convergence analysis of inf-sup stable finite element approximations of this two-field model has
first been presented in
[
ML92
,
ML94
]
in the setting of implicit Euler time stepping.
Discretizations based on three-field-formulation have originally been proposed
in
[
PW07a
,
PW07b
]
where standard continuous Galerkin
approximations of the displacement variable
\bm{u}
are coupled to a mixed finite-element method for the pore pressure
p

based on introducing the flux variable
\bm{v}
to the system, which obeys the Darcy law. This approach has also been extended
to discontinuous Galerkin approximations of
\bm{u}
in
[
PW08
]
and other nonconforming approximations,
e.g., using modified rotated bilinear elements
[
Yi13
]
, or Crouzeix-Raviart elements in
[
HRGZ17
]
.
Lately, the conservation of mass was identified essential for parameter robustness.
In
[
HK18
,
KR18
]
, families of strongly mass conserving discretizations based on the

H^{\text{div}}
-conforming discontinuous Galerkin discretization of
\bm{u}
have been suggested and proven stable independently
of model and discretization parameters (Lamé parameters, permeability and Biot-Willis parameter, storage coefficient, time step
and mesh size), see, e.g.
[
HK18
,
HKLP19
]
, for time-dependent error estimates, see
[
KR18
]
.
More recently, mass-conservation was achieved by finite elements based on enrichment in
[
GRH+19
,
LY23
]

and by mixed virtual finite elements in
[
WCWZ22
]
.

A discretization of a four-field formulation of Biot’s model has been proposed in
[
Yi14
]
. The method uses the
(effective) stress tensor
\bm{\sigma}
, the displacement
\bm{u}
, the fluid flux
\bm{v}
, and the pore pressure
p
as unknowns and
couples two standard mixed finite element methods, one for the mechanics and one for the flow subproblem, the former of which
is based on the Hellinger-Reissner formulation. Optimal a priori error estimates were shown for both semidiscrete and fully discrete
problems when the flux unknown is approximated in the Raviart-Thomas space and the stress tensor in the Arnold-Winther space.
The error analysis of this coupled mixed method has been complemented in
[
Lee16
]
by estimates in
L^{\infty}
-norm
in time and
L^{2}
-norm in space that are robust with respect to the Lamé parameters and do not require strict positivity of the
constrained storage coefficient
c_{s}
.

When it comes to the solution of the algebraic problems arising from discretization of Biot’s problem, preconditioning techniques
have been developed for various formulations and discretizations, in particular, in the framework of norm- or field-of-values-equivalent
operator preconditioners, see,
e.g.
[
BBDP16
,
AGH+19
]
for two-field,

[
ORB16
,
HRGZ17
,
LMW17
,
HK18
,
LPMR19
,
HKLP19
,
HKK+22
,
KLL+23
]
for three-field, and
[
Lee16
,
BLMW17
,
BKMRB21
]

for four-field formulations.
Here,
[
HKLP19
,
LPMR19
,
KLL+23
]
focus on multiple network extensions of Biot’s model and
[
HKK+22
]
on a generalization to Biot-Brinkman equations.
The key task in the development of such methods is the
stability analysis of the underlying saddle-point problems, which can be performed in the framework presented in
[
HKLP23
]
.

By a decomposition into physical subsystems, the coupled static problem can be solved implicitly, using a loose or explicit coupling,
or an iterative coupling. The latter, often provides an attractive alternative in terms of achieving high accuracy at reasonable computational
cost. The most popular procedures in this category are the undrained split, the fixed-stress split, the drained split and the fixed-strain split
iterative methods, which also generate preconditioners for Krylov methods such as the GMRES method. As shown in
[
KTJ11
]
,
in contrast to the drained split and the fixed-strain split methods, the undrained split and fixed-stress split methods are unconditionally stable.
The first convergence analysis of the latter methods has been presented in
[
MW13
]
for the quasi-static Biot system.
More recent works on fixed-stress type split methods have focused on the parameter-robustness of the scheme and the optimization of the stabilization/acceleration parameter
[
SBK+19
,
HKLW20
]
, extensions to heterogeneous poroelastic media
[
AKW23
]
, and generalizations to nonlinear poroelasticity models
[
KKLR23
]
.
For a parameter-robust convergence analysis of splitting methods that extends also to multiple network poroelastic theory (MPET) equations, see
[
HKLW20
,
HKLP20
]
.

However, no matter whether using classical block diagonal or block triangular preconditioners or splitting methods, the efficient solution of the subsystems for the individual physical fields remains an important issue. Domain decomposition and/or (algebraic) multigrid methods provide a feasible approach to solve the arising
H^{1}
and
H^{\text{div}}
subproblems although their parameter-robust performance, e.g, for nearly incompressible materials and/or low hydraulic conductivities can be quite challenging.
In any case, this approach requires to combine at least two (nearly) optimal solvers for subproblems, such as elasticity and Darcy flow,
in order to obtain an overall (nearly) optimal method.

A monolithic multigrid approach for a reduced-quadrature discretization has recently been developed in
[
AHH+23
]
where an additive Vanka relaxation method, based on overlapping vertex-patches, and an inexact Braess-Sarazin relaxation method is considered.
The methods rely on optimized relaxation factors depending on the Poisson ratio.
Their numerical tests confirm robustness of the method in the permeability coefficient and for moderately incompressible materials.
In comparison, our method is robust with respect to the Poisson ratio.

In this work we design a monolithic two-level Schwarz method based on overlapping vertex-patches for the quasi-static Biot problem in three-field formulation similar to
[
AHH+23
]
. However, instead of a reduced-quadrature discretization we apply the preconditioner to a family of strongly mass-conserving mixed finite element methods using an
H^{\text{div}}
-conforming discontinuous Galerkin ansatz for the displacement field, as proposed in
[
HK18
,
KR18
]
.
This allows us to prove convergence of the method by transforming the saddle-point problem into an equivalent singularly perturbed symmetric
positive definite problem, cf.
[
Sch99
,
LWXZ07
]
.
In particular, we obtain a robust method with standard choices of the relaxation parameters under the assumption of two-sided bounds on the permeability tensor.

we introduce Biot’s consolidation model and its conservative discretization in
Section

2
. The monolithic two-level algorithm is presented
in
Section

3
and analyzed in
Section

4
. In
Section

5
we provide numerical evidence of our theoretical findings.

2
Model and discretization

Biot’s consolidation model in three-field formulation couples the displacement field
\bm{u}
of the solid component, the seepage velocity
\bm{v}
of the fluid, and the fluid pressure
p
in a fully saturated porous medium at constant temperature.
It is posed on a computational domain
\Omega\times(0,T)
consisting of a spatial domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{1,2,3\}
, where in this work we focus on the case
d=2
, and a time interval
(0,T)
,
T\in\mathbb{R}
. In strong form, Biot’s quasi-static model of consolidation reads

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}-\operatorname{div}\left(2\mu\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})+\lambda\operatorname{div}(\bm{u})\bm{I}\right)&&&+&\alpha\nabla p&=&\bm{f}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&&\bm{v}&+&\bm{K}\nabla p&=&\bm{0}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
-\alpha\operatorname{div}\partial_{t}\bm{u}&-&\operatorname{div}\bm{v}&-&c_{s}\partial_{t}p&=&g\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\end{matrix}

with initial conditions
\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0}
, and
p(0)=p_{0}
at time
t=0
.
The set of equations describes the momentum balance, Darcy’s law, and the mass balance, respectively.
Here, the physical parameters
\lambda>0
and
\mu>0
denote the Lamé coefficients of linear elasticity,
\alpha>0
denotes the Biot-Willis constant,
\bm{K}
is the symmetric and positive definite permeability coefficient of the porous medium, and
c_{s}\geq 0
is the specific storage coefficient.
Further, the strain tensor is defined by
\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}):=(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{T})/2
.
For an introduction to the system of equations, as well as a discussion of the physical parameters we refer the reader to
[
DC93
,
Cou04
]
.

We apply a semi-discretization in time by the backward Euler method with time step size
\tau>0
, resulting in a
semi-discrete static problem of the form

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}-2\mu\operatorname{div}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})-\lambda\nabla\operatorname{div}\bm{u}&&&+&\alpha\nabla p&=&\bm{f}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
&&\bm{v}&+&\bm{K}\nabla p&=&\bm{0}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
-\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\operatorname{div}\bm{u}&-&\operatorname{div}\bm{v}&-&\frac{c_{s}}{\tau}p&=&\bar{g}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\end{matrix}

(1)

in every time step where
\bar{g}:=g-\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\operatorname{div}\bm{u}_{\rm old}-\frac{c_{s}}{\tau}p_{\rm old}
with
\bm{u}_{\rm old}
and

p_{\rm old}
taken from the previous time moment and all other quantities referring to the new time moment.
We point out that the specific time discretization is irrelevant for this article, since all implicit methods will result in the necessity
of solving systems of similar kind. Furthermore, we note that
\bm{u}/\tau
has the unit of speed and hence it is meaningful to add
the quantities
(\operatorname{div}\bm{u})/\tau
,
\operatorname{div}\bm{v}
, and
p/\tau
in the last equation.

Following
[
HKLP19
]
, by proper scaling and substitution of variables, we can transform (
1
)
into a symmetric problem.
First, from (
1
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}-\operatorname{div}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})-\frac{\lambda}{2\mu}\nabla\operatorname{div}\bm{u}&&&+&\frac{\alpha}{2\mu}\nabla p&=&\frac{1}{2\mu}\bm{f}&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
&&\frac{\alpha}{2\mu}\bm{K}^{-1}\bm{v}&+&\frac{\alpha}{2\mu}\nabla p&=&\bm{0}&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
-\operatorname{div}\bm{u}&-&\frac{\tau}{\alpha}\operatorname{div}\bm{v}&-&\frac{c_{s}}{\alpha}p&=&\frac{\tau}{\alpha}\bar{g}&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{matrix}

Next, by substituting

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{v}}=\frac{\tau}{\alpha}\bm{v},\quad\tilde{p}=\frac{\alpha}{2\mu}p,\quad\tilde{\bm{f}}=\frac{1}{2\mu}\bm{f},\quad\tilde{g}=\frac{\tau}{\alpha}\bar{g}-\operatorname{div}\bm{u}_{\rm old}-\frac{c_{s}}{\alpha}p_{\rm old},

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\mu\tau}\bm{K}^{-1},\quad\hat{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{2\mu},\quad\hat{c}_{s}=\frac{2\mu c_{s}}{\alpha^{2}},

(3)

we obtain the rescaled system

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}-\operatorname{div}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})+\hat{\lambda}\nabla\left(\operatorname{div}\bm{u}\right)&&&+&\nabla\tilde{p}&=&\tilde{\bm{f}}&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
&&\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1}\tilde{\bm{v}}&+&\nabla\tilde{p}&=&\bm{0}&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
-\operatorname{div}\bm{u}&-&\operatorname{div}\tilde{\bm{v}}&-&\hat{c}_{s}\tilde{p}&=&\tilde{g}&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{matrix}

(4)

Since this is the normalized system we build our analysis on, we will omit the tilde symbol from here on, but we will keep the hat to clarify the scaling of the parameters.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the scaled permeability tensor
\hat{\bm{K}}
is bounded such that

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\min}\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\left(\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1}\bm{v},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega}\leq\hat{R}_{\max}\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\quad\forall\bm{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2},

(5)

for two positive constants
\hat{R}_{\min}
and
\hat{R}_{\max}
.
Here,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\Omega}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
denote the
L^{2}
-norm
and
L^{2}
-inner product on
\Omega
, respectively.
Note that condition (
5
) follows, e.g., from the pointwise condition

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\min}\leq\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1})\leq\lambda_{\max}(\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1})\leq\hat{R}_{\max},\qquad\text{a. e. in }\Omega,

where
\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1})
and
\lambda_{\max}(\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1})
denote (pointwise) the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1}
, respectively.
As a matter of fact, the constants in the analysis below depend on
\hat{R}_{\max}
and
\hat{R}_{\min}
, so that it neither intends to cover high contrast coefficients nor the case of strong anisotropy.
In the simplest case, we consider a permeability tensor of the form
\hat{\bm{K}}=\hat{\kappa}\bm{I}
with a scalar
\hat{\kappa}
, constant over
\Omega
and constant over the time interval
(0,T)
.

This system is closed by proper boundary conditions. As usual, essential boundary conditions enter the definitions
of the function spaces, here
U\subset[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
V\subset H^{\text{div}}(\Omega)
for displacement and seepage velocity,
respectively.
We will choose a particular combination of boundary conditions in the presentation and analysis of the two-level algorithm
in Sections
3
and
4
, although one might also want to consider other combinations
for which certain arguments/basic estimates have to be adapted/exchanged accordingly.
The general assumptions that are required to conduct an analysis similar to the one presented in this paper are as follows.

Assumption 2.1
.

The boundary conditions on the spaces
U
and
V
are such that:

1.

The seepage velocity
\bm{v}
is uniquely determined.

2.

Korn’s inequality holds for
\bm{u}
in the continuous setting as well as for
\bm{u}_{h}
in the discrete setting. For sufficient conditions
in the continuous case we refer to
[
DV02
]
and the literature cited there. For the discrete case, see
[
Bre04
]
.

3.

The pressure space
Q
is chosen such that the operator
\operatorname{div}\colon V\to Q
is surjective. The boundary conditions on
U
are compatible such that also
\operatorname{div}\colon U\to Q
is surjective.

To give one example, which applies to the setting of the analysis presented in Section
4
, Korn’s inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{e}}\left\|\nabla\bm{u}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\left\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\left\|\nabla\bm{u}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

(6)

holds for all
\bm{u}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2}
.

In line with this, on the whole boundary we assume a homogeneous Dirichlet (no slip) boundary condition for
\bm{u}
and a Neumann boundary condition for
p
, which translates to an essential condition for
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}
.
Accordingly, we choose

\displaystyle U=[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2},\qquad V=H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad Q=L^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

where

\displaystyle H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{v}\in H^{\text{div}}(\Omega):\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\},

in the sense of traces of
H^{\text{div}}
and

\displaystyle L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}q\>dx=0\right\}.

The weak formulation of (
4
) is then:
find
(\bm{u},\bm{v},p)\in U\times V\times Q
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcccccc@{\hspace{3em}}l}e\!\left(\bm{u},\bm{\phi}\right)+\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u},\bm{\phi}\right)&&&-&b\!\left(p,\bm{\phi}\right)&=&\left(\bm{f},\bm{\phi}\right)_{\Omega}\hfil\hskip 30.00005pt&\forall\bm{\phi}\in U,\\
&&k\!\left(\bm{v},\bm{\psi}\right)&-&b\!\left(p,\bm{\psi}\right)&=&\bm{0}\hfil\hskip 30.00005pt&\forall\bm{\psi}\in V,\\
-b\!\left(q,\bm{u}\right)&-&b\!\left(q,\bm{v}\right)&-&\hat{c}_{s}\left(p,q\right)_{\Omega}&=&\left(g,q\right)_{\Omega}\hfil\hskip 30.00005pt&\forall q\in Q,\end{array}

with bilinear forms

\displaystyle e\!\left(\bm{u},\bm{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}),\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\phi})\right)_{\Omega},

\displaystyle d\!\left(\bm{u},\bm{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{div}\bm{u},\operatorname{div}\bm{\phi}\right)_{\Omega},

\displaystyle b\!\left(p,\bm{\psi}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(p,\operatorname{div}\bm{\psi}\right)_{\Omega},

\displaystyle k\!\left(\bm{v},\bm{\psi}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1}\bm{v},\bm{\psi}\right)_{\Omega}.

2.1
Discretization

The model problem (
2
) is discretized by a family of mixed strongly mass-conserving methods
as proposed in
[
HK18
,
KR18
,
HKLP19
]
, where the displacement and seepage velocity
fields are approximated in suitable
H^{\text{div}}
-conforming spaces
U_{h}
and
V_{h}
and the pressure space
Q_{h}

consists of piecewise polynomial functions discontinuous at element interfaces such that the conditions

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}U_{h}=Q_{h},\qquad\operatorname{div}V_{h}=Q_{h}

(10)

are satisfied.
The combined finite element space for the mixed method is then defined by
X_{h}:=U_{h}\times V_{h}\times Q_{h}
.
Since the space
U_{h}
is not
H^{1}
-conforming, a discrete interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin bilinear form

e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
is used to approximate
e\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
as detailed below.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a family of shape regular triangulations of the computational domain
\Omega
into mesh cells
T

with diameter
h_{T}=\operatorname{diam}(T)
where
h:=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{h_{T}}
denotes the mesh size.
Let
\Gamma_{I}
be the set of all interior faces (edges in two dimensions) of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and
\Gamma_{B}
be the set
of all faces on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
For every
F\in\Gamma_{I}
there are two neighboring cells
T_{+},T_{-}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that

F=\partial T_{+}\cap\partial T_{-}
. Let
\bm{n}
be the unit outward normal vector pointing from
T_{+}
to
T_{-}
.
Then on every face
F\in\Gamma_{I}
and for any
\bm{\phi}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
\bm{\tau}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2\times 2}
,
we define jump
[\![\,]\!]
and average

\{\!\!\{\,\}\!\!\}
by

\displaystyle[\![\bm{\phi}]\!]=\bm{\phi}_{+}-\bm{\phi}_{-},\qquad\{\!\!\{\bm{\tau}\bm{n}\}\!\!\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{\tau}_{+}+\bm{\tau}_{-}\right)\bm{n},

where
\bm{\phi}_{\pm}=\bm{\phi}|_{T_{\pm}}
and
\bm{\tau}_{\pm}=\bm{\tau}|_{T_{\pm}}
.
Further on the broken Sobolev space

\displaystyle[H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})]^{2}=\left\{\bm{\phi}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}\;\middle|\;\bm{\phi}|_{T}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{2}\right\}

we introduce the discrete norm

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{1,h}=\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|\nabla\bm{\phi}\right\|_{T}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{I}}\frac{1}{h}\left\|[\![\bm{\phi}]\!]\right\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{B\in\Gamma_{B}}\frac{1}{h}\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{B}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On
U_{h}\times U_{h}
we define the discrete bilinear form

\displaystyle e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}_{h}),\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\phi})\right)_{T}+\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{I}}\frac{\eta}{h}\left([\![\bm{u}_{h}]\!],[\![\bm{\phi}]\!]\right)_{F}

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{I}}\left(\{\!\!\{\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}_{h})\bm{n}\}\!\!\},[\![\bm{\phi}]\!]\right)_{F}-\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{I}}\left([\![\bm{u}_{h}]\!],\{\!\!\{\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\phi})\bm{n}\}\!\!\}\right)_{F}

(11)

\displaystyle+\sum_{B\in\Gamma_{B}}\frac{\eta}{h}\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)_{B}-\sum_{B\in\Gamma_{B}}\left(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}_{h})\bm{n},\bm{\phi}\right)_{B}-\sum_{B\in\Gamma_{B}}\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\phi})\bm{n}\right)_{B}.

Here, the penalty parameter
\eta>0
is chosen large enough to ensure coercivity of
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, i.e.,
there is a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle e_{h}\!\left(\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi}\right)\geq c\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\quad\forall\bm{\phi}\in U_{h}.

(12)

In addition we have continuity of
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
in the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1,h}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)\leq c\left\|\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,h}\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{1,h}\quad\forall\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\in U_{h}.

(13)

We note that since
U_{h}\subset H^{\text{div}}(\Omega)

the jumps in face terms in (
11
)
are zero in normal direction and hence can equivalently be defined using tangential components only.

The mass-conserving mixed method based on the finite element space
X_{h}
can then be represented as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\\
p_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathcal{F}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right),

(14)

where the discrete bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon X_{h}\times X_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\\
p_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)=

\displaystyle e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)+\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{\psi}\right)

(15)

\displaystyle-b\!\left(p_{h},\bm{\phi}+\bm{\psi}\right)-b\!\left(q,\bm{u}_{h}+\bm{v}_{h}\right)-\hat{c}_{s}\left(p_{h},q\right)_{\Omega}

and the right hand side is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\\
q\end{pmatrix}\right)=\left(\bm{f},\bm{\phi}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(g_{h},q\right)_{\Omega},

with
g_{h}
chosen as the
L^{2}
-projection
\Pi^{h}g
.
System (
14
) is consistent and uniformly well-posed, which has been proven in
[
KR18
,
HK18
]

and follows essentially by the special choice of
H^{\text{div}}
-conforming discretization spaces, coercivity (
12
),
continuity (
13
) and the discrete inf-sup conditions,
see, e.g.
[
BBF13
,
HL02
,
SST03
,
Bre74
]
)

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in Q_{h}}\sup_{\bm{\phi}\in U_{h}}\frac{\left(\operatorname{div}\bm{\phi},q\right)_{\Omega}}{\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{1,h}\left\|q\right\|_{\Omega}}\geq\gamma_{u}>0

and

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in Q_{h}}\sup_{\bm{\psi}\in V_{h}}\frac{\left(\operatorname{div}\bm{\psi},q\right)_{\Omega}}{\left\|\bm{\psi}\right\|_{H^{\text{div}}}\left\|q\right\|_{\Omega}}\geq\gamma_{v}>0,

where

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\psi}\right\|_{H^{\text{div}}}=\left(\left\|\bm{\psi}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\left\|\operatorname{div}\bm{\psi}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

3
Two-level algorithm

In this section, we define a monolithic two-level algorithm for the mixed problem (
14
).
The method differs from partitioned solvers which rely on and exploit the block structure of the system.

Let
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
be an overlapping covering of the domain
\Omega
into subdomains
\Omega_{j}
such that the following two standard assumptions are met, see
[
TW10
,
AFW97
,
FK01
]
.

Assumption 3.1

(Finite covering)
.

The partition
\left\{\Omega_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{J}
can be colored using at most
N^{c}
colors, in such a way that subregions with the same color are disjoint.

Assumption 3.2

(Sufficient overlap)
.

There is a constant
\delta>0
that measures the size of overlaps between the subdomains
\Omega_{j}
, for
j=1,...,J
, such that

\displaystyle c_{1}h\leq\delta\leq c_{2}H

with constants
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
.

In our experiments, we focus on the
vertex patch smoother
, where the subdomains
\Omega_{j}
can consist of all mesh cells sharing the vertex
j
, i.e., the vertex patch associated with vertex
j
.
In the special case of a uniform partition of the domain into rectangles this would be a union of
4
mesh cells for interior vertices.

With the subdomains
\Omega_{j}
we associate subspaces
X_{j}\subset X_{h}
.
To this end, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}U_{j}:=U_{h}\cap H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega_{j}),\quad V_{j}:=V_{h}\cap H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega_{j}),\quad Q_{j}:=Q_{h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{j}),\end{split}

continued by zero on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{j}
, and set

\displaystyle X_{j}:=U_{j}\times V_{j}\times Q_{j}.

Moreover, for
H>h
let
\mathcal{T}_{H}
be a triangulation of the coarse domain
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
such
that the associated subspace
X_{0}:=X_{H}\subset X_{h}
. Then,
X_{0}
plays the role of a global coarse space and
we can decompose
X_{h}
into an overlapping sum by

\displaystyle X_{h}=X_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}X_{j}.

For
j=1,\ldots,J
we introduce local bilinear forms
\mathcal{A}_{j}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon X_{j}\times X_{j}\to\mathbb{R}
as restrictions
of
\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
to
X_{j}
and define projections
\mathbb{P}_{j}\colon X_{h}\to X_{j}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{j}\!\left(\mathbb{P}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\\
p_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{j}\\
\bm{\psi}_{j}\\
q_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\\
p_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{j}\\
\bm{\psi}_{j}\\
q_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)\qquad\forall\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{j}\\
\bm{\psi}_{j}\\
q_{j}\end{pmatrix}\in X_{j}.

(16)

Additionally, on the coarse space
X_{0}=X_{H}
, we introduce a coarse bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{0}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon X_{0}\times X_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\mathcal{A}_{0}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)=\mathcal{A}_{H}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
and define
\mathbb{P}_{0}\colon X_{h}\to X_{0}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}\!\left(\mathbb{P}_{0}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\\
p_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{H}\\
\bm{\psi}_{H}\\
q_{H}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\\
p_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{H}\\
\bm{\psi}_{H}\\
q_{H}\end{pmatrix}\right)\qquad\forall\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{H}\\
\bm{\psi}_{H}\\
q_{H}\end{pmatrix}\in X_{0}.

(17)

Note, that the bilinear form
e_{H}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
is not inherited from
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
although
X_{H}\subset X_{h}
, because it differs in the face and boundary terms.
Thus,
\mathbb{P}_{0}
is not a projection, since consequently
\mathcal{A}_{0}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
differs from
\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
.

The bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon X_{h}\times X_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
given in (
15
) defines
an operator
\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{A}_{h}:X_{h}\rightarrow X^{\prime}_{h}
via the relation

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{A}\bm{x}_{h},\bm{y}_{h}\rangle_{X^{\prime}_{h}\times X_{h}}=\mathcal{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{x}_{h},\bm{y}_{h}\right)\qquad\forall\bm{x}_{h},\bm{y}_{h}\in X_{h},

where
X^{\prime}_{h}
denotes the dual space of
X_{h}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{\prime}_{h}\times X_{h}}
the corresponding duality pairing.
With this notation we define the two-level additive Schwarz preconditioner
\mathbb{P}_{\text{ad}}:X^{\prime}_{h}\rightarrow X_{h}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\text{ad}}=\omega\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathbb{P}_{j}\mathbb{A}^{-1},

(18)

where
\omega
is a relaxation factor depending on the overlap that is also used for tuning the method.
Further, the multiplicative two-level Schwarz preconditioner
\mathbb{P}_{\text{mu}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\text{mu}}

\displaystyle=\left(I-\mathbb{E}_{\text{mu}}\right)\mathbb{A}^{-1},\quad\mathbb{E}_{\text{mu}}=\left(I-\mathbb{P}_{J}\right)\cdots\left(I-\mathbb{P}_{1}\right)\left(I-\mathbb{P}_{0}\right).

(19)

Note, that in practice only local problems are solved and the global inverse
\mathbb{A}^{-1}
is actually never computed in the application of the preconditioners.

4
Two-level convergence analysis

We begin this section by stating the main results:

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
H\leq ch
for some positive constant
c
and that the bounds (
5
) on
\hat{\bm{K}}
hold.
Then, the additive Schwarz method (
18
) defines a uniform preconditioner for the system (
14
).
Consequently, the GMRES method converges uniformly, i.e.,
it achieves a prescribed accuracy within a number of iterations
that is bounded independently of the mesh size
h
and any other model parameters than
\hat{\bm{K}}
, when applied to solve a linear system with
\mathbb{P}_{\text{ad}}\mathbb{A}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

4.1
the stationary iterative method based on the multiplicative Schwarz
preconditioner (
19
) converges uniformly, i.e., with a residual contraction factor strictly less than
1
, independent of the mesh size
h
and any other model parameters than
\hat{\bm{K}}
.

Remark 4.3
.

The dependence of the presented two-level convergence estimates on the parameter
\hat{\bm{K}}
is caused by the same dependence of the constant in the stable decomposition of the divergence-free subspace
V_{h}^{0}
of
V_{h}
under the norm induced by the bilinear form
k\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, see (
42
) in the proof of Lemma
4.10
.
We will use a very crude upper bound at this point, which is given by the global contrast
\hat{R}_{\max}/\hat{R}_{\min}
for reasons of simplicity because this aspect is not the focus of the presented analysis.
Improved bounds for Darcy have been developed based on weighted Poincaré inequalities under the assumption of certain quasi-monotonicity properties of the coefficient function
\hat{\bm{K}}^{-1}
in
k\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, see
[
PS11
,
SVZ12
,
PS13
]
.

Due to the lack of an abstract convergence theory for subspace correction methods for indefinite problems,
we follow
[
Sch99
]
and transform the saddle point problem into an equivalent, singularly perturbed
symmetric positive definite (SPD) system, which is possible for any fixed storage coefficient
\hat{c}_{s}>0
, as
detailed in Section
4.1
.

The analysis of domain decomposition methods for this singularly perturbed problem requires a stable decomposition derived in subsections
4.2
and
4.3
.
Thus, the convergence with respect to the singularly perturbed problem follows by the theory for symmetric positive definite problems, which is proved in
Section

4.4
.
Finally, the proofs of
Theorems

4.1
and
4.2
can be found in subsection
4.5
. They rely on the fact that the estimates for the singularly perturbed problem are uniform for
\hat{c}_{s}\to 0
.

4.1
An equivalent, singularly perturbed problem

As shown in
[
HKLP19
]
, strong mass conservation can be recovered from system (
14
)
by using
H^{\text{div}}
-conforming discretizations for the displacement field and seepage velocity in combination with piecewise
polynomial approximations of the pressure if the polynomial degrees match. To be more precise, the discrete mass balance
equation

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}\bm{u}_{h}-\operatorname{div}\bm{v}_{h}-\hat{c}_{s}p_{h}=g_{h}

is fulfilled point-wise if the condition (
10
) is met.
This allows us to substitute

\displaystyle p_{h}=-\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}\left(\operatorname{div}\bm{u}_{h}+\operatorname{div}\bm{v}_{h}+g_{h}\right)

and obtain the equivalent, singularly perturbed system

\displaystyle e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)+\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{u}_{h}+\bm{v}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{f},\bm{\phi}\right)_{\Omega}-\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}\left(g_{h},\operatorname{div}\bm{\phi}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle k\!\left(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{\psi}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{u}_{h}+\bm{v}_{h},\bm{\psi}\right)

\displaystyle=-\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}\left(g_{h},\operatorname{div}\bm{\psi}\right)_{\Omega}

for all
(\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi})\in W_{h}=U_{h}\times V_{h}
.
We rewrite this system as

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathscr{F}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)

(20)

with

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathscr{E}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)+\mathscr{D}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right),

(21)

where the bilinear forms
\mathscr{E}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
and
\mathscr{D}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)

are defined on
W_{h}\times W_{h}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{\psi}\right),

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\phi}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{u}_{h}+\bm{v}_{h},\bm{\phi}+\bm{\psi}\right)

and the right hand side is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\left(\bm{f},\bm{\phi}\right)_{\Omega}-\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}\left(g_{h},{\operatorname{div}(\bm{\phi}+\bm{\psi})}\right)_{\Omega}.

Remark 4.4
.

The form
\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
is symmetric and coercive with respect to the weighted norm

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{W_{h}}=\left(\left\|\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}+\hat{\lambda}\left\|\operatorname{div}\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\left\|\hat{\bm{K}}^{-{1}/{2}}\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}\left\|\operatorname{div}(\bm{u}_{h}+\bm{v}_{h})\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
\bm{w}_{h}=(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\in W_{h}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix}\right)\geq c\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{W_{h}}^{2}.

Similar to
Section

3
we choose local subspaces
W_{j}\subset W_{h}
associated with the subdomains
\Omega_{j}
,
such that

\displaystyle W_{j}=U_{j}\times V_{j}

as well as a global coarse space
W_{0}=U_{H}\times V_{H}
. Hence, every
\bm{w}\in W_{h}
admits a decomposition of the form

\displaystyle\bm{w}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{w}_{j},\qquad w_{j}\in W_{j}.

(22)

For
j=1,\ldots,J
we introduce the local bilinear forms
\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon W_{j}\times W_{j}\to\mathbb{R}
as restrictions of

\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
to
W_{j}
and a coarse bilinear form
\mathscr{A}_{0}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon W_{0}\times W_{0}\to\mathbb{R}

as
\mathscr{A}_{0}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)=\mathscr{A}_{H}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
. Analogously to (
16
) and (
17
) we define
the projections
P_{j}\colon W_{h}\to W_{j}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(P_{j}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{j}\\
\bm{\psi}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{j}\\
\bm{\psi}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)\qquad\forall\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{j}\\
\bm{\psi}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{j}

and the projection-like operator
P_{0}\colon W_{h}\to W_{0}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{0}\!\left(P_{0}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{H}\\
\bm{\psi}_{H}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathscr{A}_{H}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{H}\\
\bm{\psi}_{H}\end{pmatrix}\right)\qquad\forall\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}_{H}\\

Furthermore, the bilinear form
\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon W_{h}\times W_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
given in (

an operator

\displaystyle\langle A\bm{x}_{h},\bm{y}_{h}\rangle_{W_{h}^{{}^{\prime}}\times W_{h}}=\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{x}_{h},\bm{y}_{h}\right),\quad\forall\bm{x}_{h},\bm{y}_{h}\in W_{h}.

Thus, for the singularly perturbed problem the two-level additive Schwarz preconditioner

is defined by

\displaystyle P_{\text{ad}}=\omega\sum_{j=0}^{J}P_{j}A^{-1},

(23)

and the multiplicative two-level Schwarz preconditioner

is defined by

\displaystyle P_{\text{mu}}

\displaystyle=\left(I-E_{\text{mu}}\right)A^{-1},\quad E_{\text{mu}}=\left(I-P_{J}\right)\cdots\left(I-P_{1}\right)\left(I-P_{0}\right).

(24)

4.2
Decomposition of the spaces according to
\ker(\mathscr{D})

Our goal is to prove that for any
\bm{w}\in W_{h}
any decomposition of the form (
22
)
is stable under the global and local bilinear forms
\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon W_{h}\times W_{h}\to\mathbb{R}

and
\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\colon W_{j}\times W_{j}\to\mathbb{R}
, respectively.
As a preparatory, step we decompose the space
W_{h}
into the orthogonal sum

\displaystyle W_{h}=\ker(\mathscr{D})\oplus\ker(\mathscr{D})^{\perp}

of the kernel of the summed divergence operator

\displaystyle W_{h}^{0}:=\ker(\mathscr{D})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{h}\middle|\;\mathscr{D}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)=0\quad\forall\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{h}\right\}

and its

-orthogonal complement

\displaystyle\ker(\mathscr{D})^{\perp}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{h}\middle|\;\mathscr{E}_{h}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)=0\quad\forall\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\in\ker(\mathscr{D})\right\},

cf.
[
AFW97
,
Hip97
]
.
To this end, we introduce the discrete Helmholtz-decompositions

\displaystyle U_{h}=U_{h}^{0}\oplus U_{h}^{\perp},\qquad V_{h}=V_{h}^{0}\oplus V_{h}^{\perp},

of
U_{h}
and
V_{h}
into their divergence free subspaces
U_{h}^{0}
and
V_{h}^{0}

and their
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
-orthogonal and
k\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
-orthogonal

\displaystyle U_{h}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{u}\in U_{h}\middle|\;e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u},\bm{\phi}\right)=0\quad\forall\bm{\phi}\in U_{h}^{0}\right\},

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{v}\in V_{h}\middle|\;k\!\left(\bm{v},\bm{\psi}\right)=0\quad\forall\bm{\psi}\in V_{h}^{0}\right\},

cf.
[
KM15

AFW10

Accordingly, we denote the Helmholtz-decompositions of the local and coarse spaces
U_{j}
and

-orthogonal complements by

\displaystyle U_{j}=U_{j}^{0}\oplus U_{j}^{\perp},\qquad V_{j}=V_{j}^{0}\oplus V_{j}^{\perp}.

The following lemma characterizes
W_{h}^{0}=\ker(\mathscr{D})\subset W_{h}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\hat{\lambda}>0
there holds

\displaystyle W_{h}^{0}:=\ker(\mathscr{D})=U_{h}^{0}\times V_{h}^{0},\qquad W_{h}^{\perp}:=\ker(\mathscr{D})^{\perp}=U_{h}^{\perp}\times V_{h}^{\perp}.

Proof.

Setting
\bm{\phi}=\bm{u}
and
\bm{\psi}=\bm{v}
in

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\phi}\\
\bm{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u},\bm{\phi}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{u}+\bm{v},\bm{\phi}+\bm{\psi}\right)

shows that a necessary condition for
(\bm{u},\bm{v})^{T}\in\ker(\mathscr{D})
is
\operatorname{div}\bm{u}=0
, i.e.,
\bm{u}\in U_{h}^{0}
.
Further, for any
\bm{u}\in U_{h}^{0}
, choosing
\bm{\phi}=\bm{u}
and
\bm{\psi}=-\bm{v}
shows that in this case
\bm{v}\in V_{h}^{0}

is necessary and sufficient to guarantee that
(\bm{u},\bm{v})^{T}\in W_{h}^{0}
.
∎

Note, that for all
\bm{w}_{h}^{0}=(\bm{u}_{h}^{0},\bm{v}_{h}^{0})\in W_{h}^{0}
and
\bm{\pi}_{h}=(\bm{\phi}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\in W_{h}
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}_{h}^{0},\bm{\pi}_{h}\right)=e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}_{h}^{0},\bm{\phi}_{h}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{v}_{h}^{0},\bm{\psi}_{h}\right).

(25)

4.3
Stable decompositions

The main result of this section is
Theorem

4.11
. Before we can prove it, we begin by recalling known facts from the literature
and showing some auxiliary results.
We start with the existence and some standard properties of a partition of unity for the overlapping covering
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
in

, see
[

, Lemma 3.4]

Proposition 4.6
.

Given
Assumptions

3.1
and
3.2
, there is a piecewise bilinear
partition of unity
\left\{\theta_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{J}
relative to the overlapping partition
\left\{\Omega_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{J}

\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{j})\subset\overline{\Omega_{j}}

x\in\overline{\Omega}

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\theta_{j}(x)=1,\quad\mbox{with }0\leq\theta_{j}(x)\leq 1,\quad\mbox{for all }j=1,2,\ldots,J.

Moreover,

\left\|\nabla\theta_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{\delta},

where
\delta
is the constant from
Assumption

3.2
.

In order to represent divergence free functions in the displacement
space
U_{h}^{0}
, we introduce the space

\displaystyle S(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})=\left\{s\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\;\middle|\;s_{|T}\in H^{2}(T)\right\},

which is equipped with the discrete norm

\displaystyle\left\|s\right\|_{2,h}=\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|\nabla^{2}s\right\|_{T}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{I}}\frac{1}{h}\left\|[\![\partial_{n}s]\!]\right\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{B\in\Gamma_{B}}\frac{1}{h}\left\|\partial_{n}s\right\|_{B}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(27)

Following
[
KS14

, the bilinear form

on the divergence free subspace is
algebraically equivalent to the bilinear form of a

-interior
penalty formulation of a corresponding biharmonic problem by assigning
velocities
\bm{u}_{h}=\operatorname{\bf curl}s
, where
\bm{u}_{h}
belongs to the discrete stream function space

Combining arguments from
[

and
[

\displaystyle\left\|\operatorname{\bf curl}s\right\|_{1,h}=\left\|s\right\|_{2,h},\quad\forall s\in S(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}),

(28)

where
\operatorname{\bf curl}=(\partial_{2},-\partial_{1})

Next, we recall a result showing that stream functions in

have a stable decomposition with respect to the norm (

Lemma 4.7
.

Every

admits a decomposition

with

\displaystyle\left\|s_{0}\right\|_{2,H}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|s_{j}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}\leq c\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)\left\|s\right\|_{2,h}^{2}

for some constant

.

Proof.

Compare the proof of Lemma 4.2 in
[
BW05
]
.
∎

The above results suffice to prove the stability of the decomposition of the divergence free subspace of
U_{h}
with respect to the elasticity energy norm.

Lemma 4.8
.

Every function
\bm{u}^{0}\in U_{h}^{0}
admits a decomposition of the form
\bm{u}^{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{u}^{0}_{j}
with
\bm{u}^{0}_{j}\in U_{j}^{0}
, which satisfies the bound

\displaystyle e_{H}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0}_{0},\bm{u}^{0}_{0}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{J}e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0}_{j},\bm{u}^{0}_{j}\right)\leq c\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0},\bm{u}^{0}\right)

for some constant
c>0
.

Proof.

For every
\bm{u}^{0}\in U_{h}^{0}
there exists a unique stream function
s\in S(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
, such that

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{0}=\operatorname{\bf curl}s.

Now we decompose
s=\sum_{j=0}^{J}s_{j}
and choose

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{0}_{j}=\operatorname{\bf curl}s_{j},

such that
\bm{u}^{0}_{j}\in U_{j}^{0}
and by linearity of the curl

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{u}^{0}_{j}.

Continuity of
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, the stability of the decomposition in (
29
), the identity (
28
),
and Korn’s inequality (
12
) yield

e_{H}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0}_{0},\bm{u}^{0}_{0}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{J}e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0}_{j},\bm{u}^{0}_{j}\right)\leq c\left(\left\|s_{0}\right\|_{2,H}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|s_{j}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}\right)\\
\leq c\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)\left\|s\right\|_{2,h}^{2}=c\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)\left\|\operatorname{\bf curl}s\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\\
\leq c\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0},\bm{u}^{0}\right).

∎

In order to show that also
U_{h}^{\perp}
has a stable decomposition with respect to the norm induced by the elasticity bilinear form, we will adapt the construction from
[
TW10
, Chapter10]
.

For this purpose, we recall the definition of the spaces

\displaystyle H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{\bm{v}\in H^{\text{div}}(\Omega):\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\},

\displaystyle H^{\operatorname{\bf curl}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{v\in H^{\operatorname{\bf curl}}(\Omega):v\cdot\bm{\tau}=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\},

and decompose them into

\displaystyle H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=H_{0}^{\operatorname{div},0}(\Omega)\oplus H_{0}^{\operatorname{div},\perp}(\Omega),

(30)

\displaystyle H^{\operatorname{\bf curl}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=H_{0}^{\operatorname{\bf curl},0}(\Omega)\oplus H_{0}^{\operatorname{\bf curl},\perp}(\Omega),

(31)

where
H_{0}^{\operatorname{div},\perp}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{\operatorname{\bf curl},\perp}(\Omega)
denote the orthogonal complements of the
divergence-free and curl-free subspaces

\displaystyle H_{0}^{\operatorname{div},0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{\bm{v}\in H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega):\operatorname{div}\bm{v}=0\},

\displaystyle H_{0}^{\operatorname{\bf curl},0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{v\in H^{\operatorname{\bf curl}}_{0}(\Omega):\operatorname{\bf curl}v={\bm{0}}\},

of
H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{\operatorname{\bf curl}}_{0}(\Omega)
in the
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{\text{div}}(\Omega)}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{\operatorname{\bf curl}}(\Omega)}
inner products, respectively.
Note that the decompositions (
30
) and (
31
) are both
L^{2}
-orthogonal as well.

Now, we introduce a new space
U^{+}
containing semicontinuous functions,
which can be characterized as the image of
U_{h}
under an orthogonal projection

\Theta^{\perp}\colon H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega)\to H_{0}^{\operatorname{div},\perp}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
U^{+}:=\Theta^{\perp}(U_{h})
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\Theta^{\perp}\bm{u}:=\bm{u}-\operatorname{\bf curl}w,

where
w\in H_{0}^{\operatorname{\bf curl},\perp}(\Omega)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\bf curl}w,\operatorname{\bf curl}v\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\bm{u},\operatorname{\bf curl}v\right)_{\Omega},\quad\forall v\in H_{0}^{\operatorname{\bf curl},\perp}(\Omega).

Further, we define the projection
P^{h}\colon H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega)\to U^{+}
by

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{div}\left(P^{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}\right),\operatorname{div}\bm{\phi}\right)_{\Omega}=0,\quad\forall\bm{\phi}\in U^{+}.

Then, for
P^{h}
there holds

\displaystyle\left\|P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{H^{\text{div}}}\leq c\left\|\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{H^{\text{div}}},\quad\forall\bm{u}^{\perp}\in U_{h}^{\perp}

with a constant
c
depending on
\Omega
.
According to
[
CKS05
, Proposition 4.6]

there is another constant
c
, independent of the mesh size, but depending on shape-regularity of the mesh and the polynomial degree, such that

\displaystyle\left\|P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}\leq c\left\|\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}.

(32)

Moreover, from
[
TW10
, Lemma 10.12]
for convex
\Omega
, we have the approximation property

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\perp}-P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{\Omega}\leq c\,h\left\|\operatorname{div}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{\Omega}\qquad\forall\bm{u}^{\perp}\in U_{h}^{\perp}.

(33)

For the
L^{2}
-projection
\Pi^{H}\colon L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\to U_{0}
onto the coarse space
U_{0}
there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{u}-\Pi^{H}\bm{u}\right\|_{\Omega}\leq c\,H\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{1,h}.

(34)

Additionally, we have
H^{1}
-stability of
\Pi^{H}
, that is,

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{H}\bm{u}\right\|_{1,h}\leq c\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{1,h}.

(35)

We are ready to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Every function
\bm{u}^{\perp}\in U_{h}^{\perp}
admits a decomposition of the form
\bm{u}^{\perp}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{u}^{1}_{j}
with
\bm{u}^{1}_{j}\in U_{j}
,
which satisfies the bound

\displaystyle e_{H}\!\left(\bm{u}^{1}_{0},\bm{u}^{1}_{0}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{J}e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{1}_{j},\bm{u}^{1}_{j}\right)\leq c\left(1+\frac{H^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)

(36)

for some constant
c>0
.

Proof.

We choose

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{H}P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp},

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{1}_{j}

\displaystyle=I^{h}\left(\theta_{j}\left(\bm{u}^{\perp}-\bm{u}^{1}_{0}\right)\right),

where
I^{h}
is the canonical interpolation into
U_{h}
and
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
the piecewise linear partition of unity defined
in (
26
).
We point at the fact that
\bm{u}^{1}_{j}\in U_{j}
is not necessarily a function in
U_{j}^{\perp}
.

Let
\tilde{\bm{u}}=\bm{u}^{\perp}-\bm{u}^{1}_{0}
.
Noting that on each cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the function
\theta_{j}\tilde{\bm{u}}
is polynomial, such that there holds

\displaystyle\left\|I^{h}\left(\theta_{j}\tilde{\bm{u}}\right)\right\|_{1,h}\leq c\left\|\theta_{j}\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{1,h}.

(37)

By continuity of the bilinear form
e_{H}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, the bounds (
35
) and (
32
),
and coercivity of
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, we get

\displaystyle e_{H}\!\left(\bm{u}^{1}_{0},\bm{u}^{1}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\Pi^{H}P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\leq c\left\|\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\leq c\,e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)

(38)

and

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\leq c\left(\left\|\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}+\left\|\Pi^{H}P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\right)\leq c\,e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right).

(39)

By (
33
), (
34
), and using
h\leq H
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{u}^{\perp}-P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{\Omega}+\left\|P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}-\Pi^{H}P^{h}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{\Omega}

(40)

\displaystyle\leq c\,h\left\|\operatorname{div}\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{\Omega}+c\,H\left\|\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq c\,H\left\|\bm{u}^{\perp}\right\|_{1,h}.

Using the continuity of
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, (
37
), the properties of
\theta_{j}
,
(
40
), (
39
), and the coercivity of
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, as well as the
finite covering
Assumption

3.1
, we get

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{J}e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{1}_{j},\bm{u}^{1}_{j}\right)&\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|I^{h}\left(\theta_{j}\tilde{\bm{u}}\right)\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\theta_{j}\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\\
&\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Biggl(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\left\|\nabla\theta_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}^{2}\left\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{T}^{2}+\left\|\theta_{j}\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{T}^{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{I}}\frac{1}{h}\left\|\theta_{j}[\![\tilde{\bm{u}}]\!]\right\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{B\in\Gamma_{B}}\frac{1}{h}\left\|\theta_{j}\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{B}^{2}\Biggr)\\
&\leq c\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\left\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}+c\left\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\right\|_{1,h}^{2}\\
&\leq c\left(1+\frac{H^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right).\end{split}

(41)

Finally, combining (
38
) and (
41
) results in the desired estimate (
36
).
∎

The last preparatory step is to show the stability of the decomposition of

W_{h}^{0}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}W_{j}^{0}
with respect to
\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Every
\bm{w}^{0}=(\bm{u}^{0},\bm{v}^{0})\in W_{h}^{0}
admits a decomposition of the form

\bm{w}^{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{w}^{0}_{j}
with
\bm{w}^{0}_{j}\in W_{j}^{0}
, which satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}^{0}_{j},\bm{w}^{0}_{j}\right)\leq c_{\hat{\bm{K}}}\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}^{0},\bm{w}^{0}\right),

where
c_{\hat{\bm{K}}}=c{\hat{R}_{\max}}/{\hat{R}_{\min}}
for some constant
c>0
independent of the model parameters and of
J
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.8
, every
\bm{u}^{0}\in U_{h}^{0}
has a decomposition
\bm{u}^{0}=\sum_{J=0}^{J}\bm{u}^{0}_{j}
with
\bm{u}^{0}_{j}\in U_{j}^{0}
, such that

\displaystyle e_{H}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0}_{0},\bm{u}^{0}_{0}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{J}e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0}_{j},\bm{u}^{0}_{j}\right)\leq c\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0},\bm{u}^{0}\right),

with
\bm{u}^{0}_{j}=\operatorname{\bf curl}s_{j}
, where
s_{j}\in S(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
is chosen as in the proof of
Lemma

4.8
.

Further, by a classical result in domain decomposition theory that goes back to
[
DW94
]
, every
r\in S(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})

has a decomposition
r=\sum_{j=0}^{J}r_{j}
, such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{1}^{2}\leq c\left(1+\frac{H}{\delta}\right)\left\|r\right\|_{1}^{2},

where
\left\|r\right\|_{1}=\left\|\nabla r\right\|_{\Omega}
.
By choosing
\bm{v}^{0}_{j}=\operatorname{\bf curl}r_{j}
, we get for the second term in (
25
)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{j=0}^{J}k\!\left(\bm{v}^{0}_{j},\bm{v}^{0}_{j}\right)&\leq\hat{R}_{\max}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left\|\operatorname{\bf curl}r_{j}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\hat{R}_{\max}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{1}^{2}\\
&\leq c\,\hat{R}_{\max}\left(1+\frac{H}{\delta}\right)\left\|r\right\|_{1}^{2}=c\,\hat{R}_{\max}\left(1+\frac{H}{\delta}\right)\left\|\operatorname{\bf curl}r\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\\
&\leq c\,\frac{\hat{R}_{\max}}{\hat{R}_{\min}}\left(1+\frac{H}{\delta}\right)k\!\left(\bm{v}^{0},\bm{v}^{0}\right)\end{aligned}

(42)

where we have used (
5
). The assertion of the lemma follows then
by choosing
\bm{w}^{0}_{j}=(\bm{u}^{0}_{j},\bm{v}^{0}_{j})=(\operatorname{\bf curl}s_{j},\operatorname{\bf curl}r_{j})\in W_{j}^{0}
for all
j=0,\ldots,J
.
∎

We are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.11

(Stable decomposition)
.

Every
\bm{w}\in W_{h}
admits a decomposition of the form
\bm{w}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{w}_{j}

with
\bm{w}_{j}\in W_{j}
, which satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{w}_{j}\right)\leq c_{\hat{\bm{K}}}\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w},\bm{w}\right),

where
c_{\hat{\bm{K}}}=c\max\left\{{\hat{R}_{\max}}/{\hat{R}_{\min}},\hat{R}_{\max}\right\}
for some constant
c>0

that is independent of the model parameters in the discrete bilinear form
\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
defined in (
21
) and independent of the number of subdomains
J
as well as of the discretization parameters
h
and
\tau
.

Proof.

For
\bm{w}={\bm{0}}
the result is trivial. So let
\bm{w}\in W_{h}
,
\bm{w}\neq{\bm{0}}
, be arbitrary.
For the sake of simplicity, we indicate dependencies of constants by subscripts.
To start with, we decompose
\bm{w}
in the form

\displaystyle\bm{w}:=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}^{0}\\
\bm{v}^{0}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}^{\perp}\\
\bm{v}^{\perp}\end{pmatrix},

with
(\bm{u}^{0},\bm{v}^{0})\in U^{0}\times V^{0}
and
(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{v}^{\perp})\in U^{\perp}\times V^{\perp}
.
Since we need to control the term
d\!\left(\bm{u}+\bm{v},\bm{u}+\bm{v}\right)
, we
define
\bm{\phi}=\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp}

and decompose it into
\bm{\phi}=\bm{\phi}^{0}+\bm{\phi}^{\perp}
,
\bm{\phi}^{0}\in V^{0}
, where
\bm{\phi}^{0}
and
\bm{\phi}^{\perp}
are orthogonal with respect to

k\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
.
Then, the decomposition of
\bm{w}
that we will use in our proof reads as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{w}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}^{0}\\
\bm{v}^{0}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}^{\perp}\\
-\bm{u}^{\perp}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}\\
\bm{\phi}\end{pmatrix}\\
=&\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}^{0}\\
\bm{v}^{0}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}\\
\bm{\phi}^{0}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}^{\perp}\\
-\bm{u}^{\perp}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}\\
\bm{\phi}^{\perp}\end{pmatrix}=:\bm{w}^{0}+\bm{w}^{1}+\bm{w}^{2}+\bm{w}^{3}.\end{aligned}

(43)

We decompose each component of each summand in (
43
) according to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{u}_{j}^{0}=\bm{u}^{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{v}_{j}^{0}=\bm{v}^{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{\phi}_{j}^{0}=\bm{\phi}^{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{u}_{j}^{1}=\bm{u}^{\perp},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{v}_{j}^{1}=\bm{v}^{\perp},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1}=\bm{\phi}^{\perp},

where
\bm{u}_{j}^{0}\in U_{j}^{0}
,
\bm{v}_{j}^{0}\in V_{j}^{0}
,
\bm{\phi}_{j}^{0}\in V_{j}^{0}
,
\bm{u}_{j}^{1}\in U_{j}
,
\bm{v}_{j}^{1}\in V_{j}
and
\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1}\in V_{j}
are specified below.
The superscript
1
of
\bm{u}_{j}^{1}
,
\bm{v}_{j}^{1}
and
\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1}
indicates that these terms are not orthogonal to
\bm{u}_{j}^{0}
,
\bm{v}_{j}^{0}
and
\bm{\phi}_{j}^{0}
with respect to the inner products
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
,
k\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
and
k\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, respectively.
Now, we define
\bm{w}_{j}:=\bm{w}_{j}^{0}+\bm{w}_{j}^{1}+\bm{w}_{j}^{2}+\bm{w}_{j}^{3}
, where

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{j}^{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{j}^{0}\\
\bm{v}_{j}^{0}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{j}^{0},\qquad

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{j}^{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}\\
\bm{\phi}_{j}^{0}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{j}^{0},

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{j}^{1}\\
-\bm{u}_{j}^{1}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{j},

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{j}^{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}\\
\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1}\end{pmatrix}\in W_{j},

and herewith estimate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{w}_{j}\right)&\leq c\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left(\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j}^{0},\bm{w}_{j}^{0}\right)+\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j}^{1},\bm{w}_{j}^{1}\right)\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 40.00006pt+\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j}^{2},\bm{w}_{j}^{2}\right)+\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j}^{3},\bm{w}_{j}^{3}\right)\right).\end{aligned}

(44)

For the decomposition in the orthogonal complement
W_{h}^{\perp}
, we will use the stability estimates

\displaystyle k\!\left(\bm{\phi}^{0},\bm{\phi}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq k\!\left(\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi}\right)=k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp}\right),

(45)

\displaystyle k\!\left(\bm{\phi}^{\perp},\bm{\phi}^{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle\leq k\!\left(\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi}\right)=k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp}\right).

(46)

Further, we note that

\displaystyle d\!\left(\bm{\phi}^{\perp},\bm{\phi}^{\perp}\right)=d\!\left(\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi}\right)=d\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp}\right).

(47)

Then, the stability of the
L^{2}
-decomposition (
42
) and the estimate (
45
) yield

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j}^{1},\bm{w}_{j}^{1}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}&k\!\left(\bm{\phi}_{j}^{0},\bm{\phi}_{j}^{0}\right)\leq c_{H,\delta,\hat{\bm{K}}}k\!\left(\bm{\phi}^{0},\bm{\phi}^{0}\right)\\
&\leq c_{H,\delta,\hat{\bm{K}}}\left(k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{v}^{\perp},\bm{v}^{\perp}\right)\right).\end{aligned}

(48)

Moreover, by
Lemma

4.9
and
[
TW10
,
AFW97
]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j}^{2},\bm{w}_{j}^{2}\right)&=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left(e_{j}\!\left(\bm{u}_{j}^{1},\bm{u}_{j}^{1}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{u}_{j}^{1},\bm{u}_{j}^{1}\right)+\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u}_{j}^{1},\bm{u}_{j}^{1}\right)\right)\\
&\leq c_{H,\delta}\left(e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)+\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)\right).\end{aligned}

(49)

Again, by
[
TW10
,
AFW97
]
, stability estimate (
46
), and equality (
47
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j}^{3},\bm{w}_{j}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left(k\!\left(\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1},\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1},\bm{\phi}_{j}^{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{H,\delta}\left(k\!\left(\bm{\phi}^{\perp},\bm{\phi}^{\perp}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{\phi}^{\perp},\bm{\phi}^{\perp}\right)\right)

(50)

\displaystyle\leq c_{H,\delta}\left(k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{v}^{\perp},\bm{v}^{\perp}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp}\right)\right).

Due to the special choice of the decomposition (
43
) the term
k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)
appears in (
50
),
which we further estimate using the Poincaré and Korn inequalities

\displaystyle k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)\leq c_{\Omega}\hat{R}_{\max}e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right).

(51)

Thus, by (
44
),
Lemma

4.10
and collecting the estimates
(
48
), (
49
), (
50
), and
(
51
), we obtain with
\bm{w}^{\perp}=(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{v}^{\perp})^{T}\in W_{h}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{w}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{H,\delta,\hat{\bm{K}}}\Bigl(e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{0},\bm{u}^{0}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{v}^{0},\bm{v}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)+k\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)+\hat{\lambda}d\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}\right)+\hat{c}_{s}^{-1}d\!\left(\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp},\bm{u}^{\perp}+\bm{v}^{\perp}\right)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{H,\delta,\hat{\bm{K}},\Omega}\Bigl(\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}^{0},\bm{w}^{0}\right)+\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}^{\perp},\bm{w}^{\perp}\right)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{H,\delta,\hat{\bm{K}},\Omega}\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w},\bm{w}\right)

where the constant
c_{H,\delta,\hat{\bm{K}},\Omega}
for a proper overlap
\delta
depends only on
\Omega
and the bounds
\hat{R}_{\min}
and
\hat{R}_{\max}
.
∎

Results for the singularly perturbed problem

We now prove convergence of the Schwarz methods for the singularly perturbed problem by applying the theory for symmetric positive definite problems as in
[
TW10
]
.
Therefore, we further establish a local stability estimate in
Lemma

4.12
and strengthened Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities in (
53
), which together with the stable decomposition in
Theorem

4.11
and
Assumptions

3.1
and
3.2
on the covering of the domain prove the results of this section, i.e.,
Theorems

4.14
and
4.15
.

Local stability, according to
[
TW10
]
, means that the estimates

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{w}_{j}\right)\leq C_{1}\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{w}_{j}\right)\qquad\forall\bm{w}_{j}\in W_{j}

hold true for all
0\leq j\leq J
with a constant
C_{1}>0
.
Since we have chosen exact bilinear forms on the local spaces, i.e.
\mathscr{A}_{j}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)=\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
restricted to
W_{j}
,
local stability is trivially fulfilled with
C_{1}=1
for all
1\leq j\leq J
.
Thus, it remains to consider the case
j=0
.

Lemma 4.12

(Local stability)
.

For each
\bm{w}_{0}\in W_{0}
it holds

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{w}_{0}\right)\leq\frac{H}{h}\mathscr{A}_{0}\!\left(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{w}_{0}\right).

Proof.

On the coarse space, we have chosen a non-inherited form for the approximation
of the elasticity bilinear form, namely
e_{H}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
instead of
e_{h}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, which differs in the face
and boundary terms because of the different cell size
H\geq h
. Due to the continuity of
the coarse displacement functions
\bm{u}_{0}=\bm{u}_{H}\in U_{0}
on every coarse cell, the jump terms
\left\|[\![\bm{u}_{H}]\!]\right\|_{F}

vanish for all faces
F
that lie in the interior of a coarse cell. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle e_{h}\!\left(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{0}\right)\leq\frac{H}{h}e_{H}\!\left(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{0}\right).

(52)

Since all other terms of the coarse space operator
\mathscr{A}_{0}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)

are chosen to be exact, the statement of
Lemma

4.12
follows by estimate (
52
).
∎

Remark 4.13
.

The convergence theory for multiplicative Schwarz methods in
[
TW10
]
requires that
0<C_{1}<2
.
In a typical application scenario, the coarse mesh size might be
H=2h
resulting in a factor
{H}/{h}=2
which violates the above condition.
Hence, an additional relaxation factor
\omega_{0}<1
has to be introduced in the definition of the coarse bilinear form, i.e.,

\mathscr{A}_{0}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)=\omega_{0}\mathscr{A}_{H}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
,
that scales
\mathscr{A}_{H}\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
such that
\omega_{0}{H}/{h}<2
.
However, we would like to point out that this scaling is not required in our experiments and we choose
\omega_{0}=1
in these cases.

By
Assumption

3.2
and standard arguments, see
[
TW10
]
, there hold the strengthened Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}_{i},\bm{w}_{j}\right)\right|\leq\epsilon_{ij}\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}_{i},\bm{w}_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w}_{j},\bm{w}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(53)

for
\bm{w}_{i}\in W_{i}
,
\bm{w}_{j}\in W_{j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq J
, and constants
0\leq\epsilon_{ij}\leq 1
.
Alongside
[
TW10
]
we denote the spectral radius of
\mathcal{E}=\left\{\epsilon_{ij}\right\}
by
\rho(\mathcal{E})
.

The maximal and minimal eigenvalues of
P_{\text{ad}}A
are characterized by the Rayleigh quotients

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}(P_{\text{ad}}A)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\bm{w}\in W_{h}}\frac{\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(P_{\text{ad}}A\bm{w},\bm{w}\right)}{\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w},\bm{w}\right)},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(P_{\text{ad}}A)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\bm{w}\in W_{h}}\frac{\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(P_{\text{ad}}A\bm{w},\bm{w}\right)}{\mathscr{A}_{h}\!\left(\bm{w},\bm{w}\right)}.

Theorem 4.14
.

The two-level additive Schwarz preconditioner (
23
) for the singularly perturbed system (
20
) satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(P_{\text{ad}}A)\geq\left(c_{\hat{\bm{K}}}\left(1+\frac{H^{4}}{\delta^{4}}\right)\right)^{-1},\qquad\lambda_{\max}(P_{\text{ad}}A)\leq\frac{H}{h}\left(\rho(\mathcal{E})+1\right),

\hat{R}_{\min}
and

By

[

[

. Hence, we can apply this result which concludes the proof.

Numerical experiments

For testing the numerical performance of the two-level Schwarz
preconditioners defined in
Section

3
, we follow the
setting in
[
HKLP19
, Section 6.1]
and construct a test case
with solution in

[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2}\times H^{\text{div}}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
For a more in depth investigation with different kinds of boundary
conditions, as well as a comparison to multilevel algorithms we refer the
reader to
[
Meg23
]
.
The implementation of the solvers is based on the finite element library
DEAL.II

[
ABF+22
]
.

Consider (
2
) with isotropic permeability

\hat{\bm{K}}=\hat{\kappa}I
and right hand side given by

\displaystyle\bm{f}=\begin{pmatrix}900\partial_{x}\phi-\partial_{y}^{3}\phi-\partial_{x}^{2}\partial_{y}\phi\\
900\partial_{y}\phi+\partial_{x}^{3}\phi+\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{2}\phi\end{pmatrix},\qquad g=900\hat{\kappa}\Delta\phi-\hat{c}_{s}(900\phi-1),

where
\phi
is defined on the square
\Omega=(0,1)\times(0,1)
by

\displaystyle\phi=x^{2}(x-1)^{2}y^{2}(y-1)^{2}.

Homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions are prescribed for the solid
displacement
\bm{u}
as well as for the normal direction of the
seepage velocity
\bm{v}
on the whole boundary
\partial\Omega
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bm{u}=\bm{0}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\qquad\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Here,
\bm{n}
denotes the unit outward normal vector. The solution is
defined only up to an additive constant for the pressure. Thus, we
search for a mean-value free solution in the pressure component
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p\,dx=0.

Then, the unique solution to this system is given by

\displaystyle\bm{u}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{y}\phi\\
-\partial_{x}\phi\end{pmatrix},\qquad p=900\phi-1,\qquad\bm{v}=-900\hat{\kappa}\nabla p.

The mesh consists of an equidistant subdivision of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
into rectangular cells.
The system is discretized with triplets
RT_{k}\times RT_{k}\times Q_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, of equal order Raviart-Thomas and discontinuous cell-wise polynomial finite element functions, such that the matching condition

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}RT_{k}=Q_{k}

is fulfilled.
To solve the algebraic equations we use the Krylov subspace method GMRES preconditioned by the multiplicative two-level Schwarz method (
19
), which combines a multiplicative subdomain solver with a multiplicative application of the coarse grid solver.
As stopping criterion we use a reduction of the unpreconditioned starting residual
measured in the
L^{2}
-norm by a factor
10^{8}
.
The subdomains
\Omega_{j}
form vertex patches, which are unions of four cells for all interior vertices with an overlap of size
\delta=h
.
Patches consisting of two cells for vertices at the boundary
\partial\Omega
and single cells for vertices in corners are excluded from the set of patches, since we could not observe any beneficial effects of their inclusion for calculations with homogeneous boundary conditions, cf.
[
Meg23
, Section 3.5]
.
To realize the homogeneous boundary conditions on the boundary
\partial\Omega_{j}
of the vertex patches, we build the patch matrices only out of interior degrees of freedom of each vertex patch
\Omega_{j}
.
The coarse space is always assembled one level below the actual level, i.e.,
H=2h
leading to a constant factor
\frac{H}{\delta}=2
.

The coarse and local problems are solved using a singular value decomposition in order to deal properly with the one-dimensional kernel of their operators caused by the nonuniqueness of the pressure solution on each subdomain.

6
Conclusion

In this article, we proposed two-level Schwarz methods to solve
H^{\text{div}}
-conforming discretizations of Biot’s quasi-static consolidation model.
We rigorously proved convergence of the monolithic algorithms by transforming the three-field formulation into an equivalent singularly perturbed symmetric positive definite system, for which a stable decomposition and local stability bounds form the basis for applying the abstract Schwarz convergence theory for noninherited forms.
Finally, independence with respect to the perturbation parameter resulted in the proof of convergence, robust with respect to the model parameters under the assumption that the permeability tensor can be bounded in the sense of (
5
).
The numerical tests demonstrated the performance and robustness of the multiplicative two-level Schwarz method for different polynomial degrees of the mixed finite element spaces and confirmed the theoretical results, even for nearly incompressible and low-permeable materials.
Beyond the theoretical results, a multilevel Schwarz approach showed comparable performance at lower cost.

The three-dimensional case as well as the investigation of high-frequency-high-contrast problems are the subjects of future work.

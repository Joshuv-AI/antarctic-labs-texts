Geometry of 𝑮 \boldsymbol{G} -Structures via the Intrinsic Torsion
Kamil NIEDZIAŁOMSKI

Abstract
We study the geometry of a G G -structure P P inside the oriented orthonormal frame bundle SO ⁡ ( M ) {\rm SO}(M) over an oriented Riemannian manifold M M . We assume that G G is connected and closed, so the quotient SO ⁡ ( n ) / G {\rm SO}(n)/G , where n = dim M n=\dim M , is a normal homogeneous space and we equip SO ⁡ ( M ) {\rm SO}(M) with the natural Riemannian structure induced from the structure on M M and the Killing form of SO ⁡ ( n ) {\rm SO}(n) . We show, in particular, that minimality of P P is equivalent to harmonicity of an induced section of the homogeneous bundle SO ⁡ ( M ) × SO ⁡ ( n ) SO ⁡ ( n ) / G {\rm SO}(M)\times_{{\rm SO}(n)}{\rm SO}(n)/G , with a Riemannian metric on M M obtained as the pull-back with respect to this section of the Riemannian metric on the considered associated bundle, and to the minimality of the image of this section. We apply obtained results to the case of almost product structures, i.e., structures induced by plane fields.

Introduction

Existence of a geometric structure on an oriented Riemannian manifold is equivalent to saying that the structure group
{\rm SO}(n)
of the oriented orthonormal frame bundle reduces to a certain subgroup
G
. For example, for
G
equal

\displaystyle{\rm SO}(m)\times{\rm SO}(n-m),\quad{\rm U}(n/2),\quad{\rm U}(n/2)\times 1,\quad{\rm Sp}(n/4){\rm Sp}(1),\quad{\rm G}_{2},\quad{\rm Spin}(7)

we have almost product, almost Hermitian, almost contact, almost quaternion-Kähler,
{\rm G}_{2}
and
{\rm Spin}(7)
structures, respectively. It is natural to ask if the holonomy group of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
is contained in given
G
. The list of possible irreducible Riemannian holonomies is limited by the Berger list
[
1
]
.

On the other hand, the defect of the Levi-Civita connection to be a
G
-connection measures the intrinsic torsion
\xi
, which is the difference of
\nabla
and a
G
-connection
\nabla^{G}
(with torsion),

\displaystyle\xi_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla^{G}_{X}Y.

If a
G
-structure is integrable, i.e., the intrinsic torsion vanishes, then the holonomy is contained in the structure group
G
. The study of possible intrinsic torsions, i.e., the decomposition of the space of intrinsic torsions into irreducible modules, was initiated by Gray and Hervella
[
13
]
in the case of almost Hermitian manifolds. Later, many authors considered other possible cases (see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
).

The other possible direction, initiated by Wood
[
20
]
and generalized to the general case by González-Dávila and Martín Cabrera
[
12
]
, is to consider differential properties of intrinsic torsion induced by condition of harmonicity of the unique section of the associated homogeneous bundle. More precisely, a
G
-structure
P\subset{\rm SO}(M)
, where
G
is closed and connected, induces the unique section
\sigma
of the homogeneous associated bundle
{\rm SO}(M)/G={\rm SO}(M)\times_{{\rm SO}(n)}{\rm SO}(n)/G
,

\displaystyle\sigma(\pi_{{\rm SO}(M)}(p))=[[p,eG]],\qquad p\in{\rm SO}(M),

where
\pi_{{\rm SO}(M)}
is the projection in the orthonormal frame bundle
{\rm SO}(M)
. Then the decomposition
\mathfrak{so}(n)=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the orthogonal complement to
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the Killing form, on the level of Lie algebras is reductive. Equip
{\rm SO}(M)/G
with the natural Riemannian metric induced from the Killing form on
\mathfrak{m}
and Riemannian metric
g
on
M
. Then we say that a
G
-structure
P
is harmonic if the section
\sigma
is harmonic. The correspondence of the notion of harmonicity with the intrinsic torsion follows from the fact that the intrinsic torsion
\xi
can be considered as a section of the bundle
T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{P}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{P}
is the adjoint bundle
P\times_{\operatorname{ad}G}\mathfrak{m}
. This follows from the observation that the
\mathfrak{m}
-component of the connection form
\omega
with respect to the reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{so}(n)=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
can be projected to the tangent bundle
TM
(see the next section for the details).

To seek for the ‘best’ possible non-integrable
G
-structures we consider the third possible approach. Namely, we focus on the minimality of a
G
-structure in the oriented orthonormal frame bundle
{\rm SO}(M)
. It is not surprising that minimality is related with the harmonicity of a
G
-structure. More generally, we use the concept of intrinsic torsion to obtain some results on the geometry of
G
-structures. The idea comes from the results obtained by the author
[
18
]
in the case of a single submanifold. We deal with the intrinsic and extrinsic geometry of a
G
-structure. To be more precise, we define the Riemannian metric on
{\rm SO}(M)
by inducing it from the Riemannian metric
g
on
M
and Killing form
{\bf B}
on the structure group
{\rm SO}(n)
. It is interesting that the Levi-Civita connection on
P
depends on the
G
-connection and Levi-Civita connection
\tilde{\nabla}
, which comes from a certain Riemannian metric
\tilde{g}
on
M
. This metric
\tilde{g}
depends on the intrinsic torsion and equals the pull-back of the Riemannian metric on the associated homogeneous bundle
{\rm SO}(M)/G
with respect to the section
\sigma
.

The main emphasis is put on the minimality of a
G
-structure
P
in the Riemannian structure
{\rm SO}(M)
. The main theorem can be stated as follows.

Theorem
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

1)

G
-structure
P
is minimal in
{\rm SO}(M)
,

2)

the induced section
\sigma\colon(M,\tilde{g})\to(N,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a harmonic map, where
\tilde{g}
is such that
\tilde{g}=\sigma^{\ast}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Notice, that these conditions are also equivalent, by the general fact concerning harmonic maps
[
6
]
, to the minimality of
\sigma(M)
in
N
. Moreover, the vanishing of the second fundamental form is the neccesary condition for the integrability of a
G
-structure.

Proposition
.

If a
G
-structure
P
is integrable, i.e., the intrinsic torsion vanishes, then
P
is totally geodesic in
{\rm SO}(M)
.

The article is organized as follows. In Sections
2
and
3
we recall the notion of the intrinsic torsion and state its main properties. The results in these sections are well known and can be found in the literature, for example, in
[
12
,
21

.

In Section
4

g
to the mentioned above
\tilde{g}
. Its properties are crucial in the main considerations. With the Riemannian metric
\tilde{g}
and the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of the
G
-structure
P
induced by the minimal
G
-connection
\nabla^{\prime}
, the projection
\pi_{P}\colon P\to M
becomes the Riemannian submersion.

Section
5
is the main section of the general outline and deals with the geometry of
G
-structures. Firstly, we consider intrinsic geometry focusing on the curvatures and, secondly, we consider extrinsic geometry, with the main result concerning minimality of a
G
-structure.

We end the article with some relevant examples. Examples considered here have been considered by other authors
[
10
,
11
,
14
]
, so we only list them and state relevant conditions.

Throughout the paper we will use the following notation and identification: For any associated bundle
E=P\times_{G}S
with the fiber
S
and induced by the principal bundle
P(M,G)
any element in
E
will be denoted by
[[p,s]]
. Moreover, we have
\Gamma(E)\equiv C^{\infty}(P,S)^{G}
, where
C^{\infty}(P,S)^{G}
is the space of equivariant functions

,

. The identification is the following

\displaystyle\Gamma(E)\ni\sigma\longleftrightarrow f\in C^{\infty}(P,S)^{G},\qquad\sigma(\pi_{P}(p))=[[p,f(p)]].

2
Intrinsic torsion

In this section we will review the basic facts concerning intrinsic torsion of a
G
-structure
[
12
,
21
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian manifold,
{\rm SO}(M)
its oriented orthonormal frame bundle. Denote by
\omega
the connection form induced by the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
M
. Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
be horizontal and vertical distributions on
{\rm SO}(M)
, respectively.

Assume that the structure group
{\rm SO}(n)
reduces to a closed and connected subgroup
G
. Then the quotient
{\rm SO}(n)/G
is a normal homogeneous space, i.e., the subspace
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{g}^{\bot}\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)
defines
\operatorname{ad}(G)
invariant decomposition
\mathfrak{so}(n)=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of
G
and the orthogonal part is taken with respect to the Killing form
{\bf B}
. Denote by
P\subset{\rm SO}(M)
the reduced subbundle. The
\mathfrak{g}
-component
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\omega
defines a connection form on
P
. Denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
the horizontal and vertical distributions on
P
with respect to
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
, respectively.

Moreover, consider the following associated bundles – the adjoint bundles over
M
:
\mathfrak{so}(n)_{P}=P\times_{\operatorname{ad}G}\mathfrak{so}(n)
,
\mathfrak{g}_{P}=P\times_{\operatorname{ad}G}\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{P}=P\times_{\operatorname{ad}G}\mathfrak{m}
.

Notice that
\mathfrak{so}(n)_{P}
can be realized as the bundle
\mathfrak{so}(M)
of skew-symmetric endomorphisms of the tangent bundle
TM
via the identification

\displaystyle[[p,A]]\mapsto p^{-1}\cdot A\cdot p,

where we treat element
p\in P_{x}
as a linear isomorphism
p\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to T_{x}M
. Analogously, we have the bundles
\mathfrak{g}(M)
and
\mathfrak{m}(M)
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{so}(n)_{P}
is isomorphic to the bundle
\mathcal{V}_{\rm equiv}
over
M
of equivariant vertical vector fields on
P
. Namely, the map

\displaystyle[[p,A]]\mapsto A^{\ast}_{p}

settles this isomorphism, where
A^{\ast}
denotes the fundamental vertical vector induced by the matrix
A\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
.

Let

\displaystyle\xi(X)=\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}\big(X^{h^{\prime}}\big),

where
\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}
denotes the
\mathfrak{m}
-component of
\omega
and
X^{h^{\prime}}
is the horizontal lift with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Since

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}\big(X^{h^{\prime}}_{pg}\big)=\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}\big(R_{g\ast}X^{h^{\prime}}_{p}\big)=\operatorname{ad}\big(g^{-1}\big)\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}\big(X^{h^{\prime}}_{p}\big),

it follows that
\xi(X)\in\mathfrak{m}_{P}
. Section
\xi\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{P})
is called the
intrinsic torsion
of a
G
-structure
P
. By above identifications
\xi(X)=\xi_{X}\in\mathfrak{m}(M)
. Let
\nabla^{\prime}
be the connection on
M
induced by
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
P
. We call
\nabla^{\prime}
the
minimal connection
of a
G
-structure. Intrinsic torsion
\xi
has the following properties:

\displaystyle X^{h^{\prime}}=X^{h}+(\xi_{X})^{\ast}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\xi_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla^{\prime}_{X}Y,

(2.1)

for
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. The decomposition
\mathfrak{so}(M)=\mathfrak{g}(M)\oplus\mathfrak{m}(M)
implies the following relations between intrinsic torsion and the curvature tensor
R
of
\nabla
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y})_{\mathfrak{g}}=[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\\
&(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y})_{\mathfrak{m}}=\nabla^{\prime}_{X}\xi_{Y}+[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}]_{\mathfrak{m}},\\
&R(X,Y)_{\mathfrak{g}}=R^{\prime}(X,Y)+[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\\
&R(X,Y)_{\mathfrak{m}}=\nabla^{\prime}_{X}\xi_{Y}-\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}\xi_{X}+[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}]_{\mathfrak{m}}-\xi_{[X,Y]}.\end{split}

(2.2)

Notice also that

\displaystyle R(X,Y)=R^{\prime}(X,Y)+(\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Y}-(\nabla_{Y}\xi)_{X}-[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}],

(2.3)

where
R^{\prime}
is the curvature tensor of
\nabla^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Y}=\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}-\xi_{\nabla_{X}Y}.

3
Harmonic
\boldsymbol{G}
-reductions

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian manifold,
\pi_{{\rm SO}(M)}\colon{\rm SO}(M)\to M
its orthonormal frame bundle with the connection form
\omega
inducing Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
M
. Let
G\subset{\rm SO}(n)
,
n=\dim M
, be a subgroup such that
{\rm SO}(n)/G
is a normal homogeneous space and let
P\subset{\rm SO}(M)
the reduced subbundle. Let
N={\rm SO}(M)/G={\rm SO}(M)\times_{{\rm SO}(n)}({\rm SO}(n)/G)
be the homogeneous bundle associated with
{\rm SO}(M)
. Denote by
\zeta\colon{\rm SO}(M)\to N
the natural projection. Then
\zeta
defines the
G
-principal bundle. Clearly
\zeta
is constant on
P
and hence there is a bijection between
G
-reductions of
{\rm SO}(M)
and sections of
N
. Denote by
\sigma\in\Gamma(N)
the section induced by
P
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{N}
be the adjoint bundle associated with
\zeta
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{m}_{N}={\rm SO}(M)\times_{\operatorname{ad}G}\mathfrak{m}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{N}
and
\mathcal{V}^{N}
be horizontal and vertical distributions on
N
, respectively, where
\mathcal{H}^{N}
is induced by
\omega
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{N}=\zeta_{\ast}\mathcal{H},\qquad\mathcal{V}^{N}={\rm SO}(M)\times_{{\rm SO}(n)}T({\rm SO}(n)/G),

where
{\rm SO}(n)
acts on
T({\rm SO}(n)/G)
by the differential of the natural action of
{\rm SO}(n)
on
{\rm SO}(n)/G
. Since
T({\rm SO}(n)/G)={\rm SO}(n)\times_{\operatorname{ad}G}\mathfrak{m}
it follows that
\mathcal{V}^{N}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{m}_{N}
.

Denote by
\varphi\colon TN\to\mathfrak{m}_{N}
the following map

\displaystyle\varphi([[p,[[g,A]]\,]])=[[p,A]],\qquad\varphi\big(X^{h,N}\big)=0,

i.e.,
\varphi
settles the described above isomorphism of
\mathcal{V}^{N}
onto
\mathfrak{m}_{N}
and is zero on the horizontal distribution. The Riemannian metric on
N
is induced by
g
and the Killing form on
\mathfrak{m}
, namely,

\displaystyle\langle V,W\rangle=g(\pi_{N\ast}V,\pi_{N\ast}W)+{\bf B}(\varphi(V),\varphi(W)),\qquad V,W\in TN,

(3.1)

where
{\bf B}(A,B)=-\operatorname{tr}(AB)
for
A,B\in\mathfrak{m}
. Decomposition

\displaystyle TN=\mathcal{H}^{N}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{N},

defines projections
{\bf h}\colon TN\to\mathcal{H}^{N}
and
{\bf v}\colon TN\to\mathcal{H}^{N}
. It can be shown that
[
12
]

\displaystyle{\bf v}\sigma_{\ast}(X)=\xi_{X}\in\mathfrak{m}_{N},\qquad X\in TM,

(3.2)

where
\xi_{X}
is the intrinsic torsion of a
G
-structure
P
.

In this section we will derive the formula for the derivative
\nabla\sigma_{\ast}
and the tension field of
\sigma
.

Equip
N
with the Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
given by (
3.1
) and
M
with a Riemannian metric
\tilde{g}
on
M
, which may vary from
g
. We will consider
\sigma
as a map

\displaystyle\sigma\colon\ (M,\tilde{g})\to(N,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle).

Assuming
M
is compact we may define the energy functional
E(\sigma)
given by

\displaystyle E(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\|\sigma_{\ast}\|^{2}\,d{\rm vol}_{M},

(3.3)

where the norm
\|\cdot\|
is taken with respect to
\tilde{g}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\ast}\|^{2}=\sum_{i}\langle\sigma_{\ast}(\tilde{e_{i}}),\sigma_{\ast}(\tilde{e_{i}})\rangle,

where
(\tilde{e_{i}})
is a
\tilde{g}
-orthonormal basis on
M
. In order to study harmonic sections it is convenient to study variations of the functional (
3.3
) in the class of all sections of
N
. We will consider that above functional in the class of all maps from
M
to
N
, thus we will study harmonicity as a map (not a section). Then, the Euler–Lagrange equation is given by the formula

\displaystyle\tau_{\tilde{g}}(\sigma)=\operatorname{tr}_{\tilde{g}}\nabla\sigma_{\ast}=\sum_{i}\nabla^{\sigma}_{\tilde{e_{i}}}\sigma_{\ast}\tilde{e_{i}}-\sigma_{\ast}(\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{e_{i}}}\tilde{e_{i}})=0,

(3.4)

where
\tilde{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\tilde{g}
and
\nabla^{\sigma}
is the pull-back connection in the bundle

\displaystyle\sigma^{\ast}TN=\sigma^{\ast}\mathcal{H}^{N}\oplus\sigma^{\ast}\mathcal{V}^{N}=TM\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{P}=TM\oplus\mathfrak{m}(M).

(3.5)

We call
\tau(\sigma)
the
tension field
of
\sigma
. We say that a
G
-structure is
harmonic as a map
if (
3.4
) holds for the induced section
\sigma
(here and furthermore we do not require
M
to be compact). Taking the decomposition of
\tau(\sigma)
with respect to (
3.5
), harmonicity of
\sigma
is equivalent to vanishing of
{\bf h}\tau(\sigma)
and
{\bf v}\tau(\sigma)
.

If
\tilde{g}=g
, then a
G
-structure induced by the section
\sigma
satisfying
{\bf v}\tau(\sigma)=0
is called a
harmonic

G
-
structure

[
12
]
.

Denote by
\Pi_{\tilde{g}}
and
\Pi_{g}
the differential
\nabla\sigma_{\ast}
with respect to
\tilde{g}
and
g
, respectively. Moreover, let
S
be the difference between
\tilde{\nabla}
and
\nabla
, i.e.,
S(X,Y)=\tilde{\nabla}_{X}Y-\nabla_{X}Y
. Then

\displaystyle\tau_{\tilde{g}}(\sigma)=\operatorname{tr}_{\tilde{g}}\Pi_{\tilde{g}}=\operatorname{tr}_{\tilde{g}}(\Pi_{g}-\sigma_{\ast}S).

We have
[
12
,
21
]

\displaystyle\varphi(\Pi_{g}(X,Y))=\frac{1}{2}((\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Y}+(\nabla_{Y}\xi)_{X})\in\mathfrak{m}(M),

(3.6)

\displaystyle g(\pi_{N\ast}\Pi_{g}(X,Y),Z)=\frac{1}{2}({\bf B}(\xi_{X},R_{\mathfrak{m}}(Y,Z))+{\bf B}(\xi_{Y},R_{\mathfrak{m}}(X,Z))),

(3.7)

where
(\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Y}=\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}-\xi_{\nabla_{X}Y}
.

In order to describe explicitly the condition for the harmonicity of
\sigma
let us introduce certain curvature operator
[
18
]
. For any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{so}(M)
let

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}(X)=\sum_{i}R(e_{i},\alpha(e_{i}))X\in TM,\qquad X\in TM.

(3.8)

Then
R_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{so}(M)
and the following formula holds

\displaystyle g(R_{\alpha}(X),Y)={\bf B}(\alpha,R(X,Y)),\qquad\alpha\in\mathfrak{so}(M),\qquad X,Y\in TM.

(3.9)

Indeed,

\displaystyle g(R_{\alpha}(X),Y)=\sum_{i}g(R(e_{i},\alpha(e_{i}))X,Y)=\sum_{i}g(\alpha(e_{i}),R(X,Y)e_{i})={\bf B}(\alpha,R(X,Y)).

Now, we can state the formula for the harmonicity of
\sigma
.

Proposition 3.1
.

G
-structure
\sigma\colon(M,\tilde{g})\to(N,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a harmonic map if and only if the following conditions hold

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\nabla_{\tilde{e_{i}}}\xi)_{\tilde{e_{i}}}-\xi_{S(\tilde{e_{i}},\tilde{e_{i}})}=0\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sum_{i}R_{\xi_{\tilde{e_{i}}}}(\tilde{e_{i}})-S(\tilde{e_{i}},\tilde{e_{i}})=0,

where
(\tilde{e_{i}})
is an orthonormal basis for
\tilde{g}
.

Proof.

Follows by (
3.2
), (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.9
).

4
Properties of certain transfer tensor

In this section we will introduce invertible tensor induced by the intrinsic torsion, the Riemannian metric defined by this tensor and state the properties of the Levi-Civita connection of this new metric. Results in this section are generalizations of the results obtained by the author in
[
18
]
.

Adopt the notation from the previous section. For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(M)
put

\displaystyle\xi\cdot\alpha=-\sum_{i}{\bf B}(\xi_{e_{i}},\alpha)e_{i}\in TM,

where we consider the intrinsic torsion as an element of
\mathfrak{m}(M)
. It is easy to show that

\displaystyle g(\xi\cdot\alpha,X)=-{\bf B}(\alpha,\xi_{X}),\qquad\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(M),\qquad X\in TM.

(4.1)

Let
L
be the endomorphism of the tangent bundle of the form

\displaystyle L(X)=X-\xi\cdot\xi_{X},\qquad X\in TM.

In order to derive some properties of
L
recall the definition of the Riemannian metric on the bundle
{\rm SO}(M)
induced by the metric
g
on
M
and by the Killing form
{\bf B}
on the structure group
{\rm SO}(n)
. We define the Riemannian metric
g_{{\rm SO}(M)}
on
{\rm SO}(M)
as follows

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h},Y^{h}\big)=g(X,Y),\qquad g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h},\alpha^{\ast}\big)=0,\qquad g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\alpha^{\ast},\beta^{\ast}\big)={\bf B}(\alpha,\beta),

where
X,Y\in TM
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{so}(M)=\mathfrak{so}(n)_{{\rm SO}(M)}
. Recall that we identify element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{so}(M)
with the equivariant vertical vector field denoted by
\alpha^{\ast}
. Then maps
\pi_{{\rm SO}(M)}\colon{\rm SO}(M)\to M
and
\zeta\colon{\rm SO}(M)\to N
are Riemannian submersions.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h^{\prime}},Y^{h^{\prime}}\big)=g(X,LY).

In particular,
L
is a symmetric and positive definite automorphism of
TM
. Moreover, the covariant derivative of
L
is related with the intrinsic torsion
\xi_{X}
by the formula

\displaystyle g((\nabla_{X}L)Y,Z)={\bf B}((\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Y},\xi_{Z})+{\bf B}((\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Z},\xi_{Y}).

Proof.

By (
4.1
) and (
2.1
),

\displaystyle g(X,LY)=g(X,Y)+{\bf B}(\xi_{X},\xi_{Y})=g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h}+\xi_{X},Y^{h}+\xi_{Y}\big)=g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h^{\prime}},Y^{h^{\prime}}\big).

Thus
L
is symmetric and positive definite. Moreover, by the fact that
\nabla
is metric for
{\bf B}
we have

\displaystyle g((\nabla_{X}L)Y,Z)=g(\nabla_{X}(LY),Z)-g(L(\nabla_{X}Y),Z)

\displaystyle\hphantom{g((\nabla_XL)Y,Z)}{}=Xg(LY,Z)-g(LY,\nabla_{X}Z)-g(\nabla_{X}Y,LZ)

\displaystyle\hphantom{g((\nabla_XL)Y,Z)}{}=Xg(Y,Z)+X{\bf B}(\xi_{Y},\xi_{Z})-g(Y,\nabla_{X}Z)-{\bf B}(\xi_{Y},\xi_{\nabla_{X}Z})

\displaystyle\hphantom{g((\nabla_XL)Y,Z)=}{}-g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-{\bf B}(\xi_{\nabla_{X}Y},\xi_{Z})

\displaystyle\hphantom{g((\nabla_XL)Y,Z)}{}={\bf B}(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}-\xi_{\nabla_{X}Y},\xi_{Z})+{\bf B}(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Z}-\xi_{\nabla_{X}Z},\xi_{Y}).

∎

By Proposition
4.1
the symmetric and bilinear form

5
Geometry of
\boldsymbol{G}
-structures

In this section we study the geometry of a
G
-structure
P
in
{\rm SO}(M)
. In this case we need to consider the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}
of the Riemannian metric
g_{{\rm SO}(M)}
. One can show
[
18

that

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^{h}}X^{h}=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{h}-\frac{1}{2}R(X,Y)^{\ast},\qquad

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{\alpha^{\ast}}Y^{h}=\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha}(Y)^{h},

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^{h}}\alpha^{\ast}=\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha}(X)^{h}+(\nabla_{X}\alpha)^{\ast},\qquad

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{\alpha^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}=-\frac{1}{2}[\alpha,\beta]^{\ast},

for

and

, or equivalently,

is defined by (

It is convenient to find orthogonal projections

\displaystyle T{\rm SO}(M)\mapsto TPq\quad\textrm{and}\qquad T{\rm SO}(M)\mapsto T^{\bot}P,

in order to derive the Levi-Civita connection and second fundamental form for
P
in
{\rm SO}(M)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X\in TM
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{so}(M)
.

1.

The orthogonal projection
T{\rm SO}(M)\mapsto TP
equals

\displaystyle X^{h}\mapsto L^{-1}(X)^{h^{\prime}},\qquad\alpha^{\ast}\mapsto\alpha_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}-L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h^{\prime}}.

2.

The orthogonal projection
T{\rm SO}(M)\mapsto T^{\bot}P
equals

\displaystyle X^{h}\mapsto-(\xi_{L^{-1}(X)})^{\ast}-(\xi\cdot\xi_{L^{-1}X})^{h},\qquad\alpha^{\ast}\mapsto\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}+(\xi_{L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})})^{\ast}+L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h}.

Proof.

We will use frequently (
2.1
) and (
4.1
). Recall that
TP=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{P}
. For any
Y\in TM

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h}-L^{-1}(X)^{h^{\prime}},Y^{h^{\prime}}\big)=g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h}-L^{-1}(X)^{h}-\xi_{L^{-1}(X)}^{\ast},Y^{h}+\xi_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^h-L^{-1}(X)^{h'},Y^{h'}\big)}{}=g\big(X-L^{-1}(X),Y\big)-{\bf B}\big(\xi_{L^{-1}(X)},\xi_{Y}\big)=0

and for any
\beta\in\mathfrak{g}_{P}

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(X^{h}-L^{-1}(X)^{h^{\prime}},\beta^{\ast}\big)=-{\bf B}\big(\xi_{L^{-1}(X)},\beta\big)=0.

Clearly
L^{-1}(X)
is tangent to
P
and
X^{h}-L^{-1}(X)^{h^{\prime}}=-(\xi_{L^{-1}(X)})^{\ast}-(\xi\cdot\xi_{L^{-1}X})^{h}
, which proves the desired decomposition for
X^{h}
. Analogously,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\ast}-\big(\alpha_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}-L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h^{\prime}}\big)=\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}+L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h^{\prime}},

and

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}+L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h^{\prime}},Y^{h^{\prime}}\big)={\bf B}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}},\xi_{Y})+g\big(L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}),Y\big)+{\bf B}\big(\xi_{L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})},\xi_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}+L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h'},Y^{h'}\big)}{}=-g(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}},Y)+g\big(L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}},Y)\big)\!-g\big(\xi\cdot\xi_{L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})},Y\big)\!

\displaystyle\hphantom{g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}+L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h'},Y^{h'}\big)}{}=0.

Moreover, for
\beta\in\mathfrak{g}_{P}

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}+L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h^{\prime}},\beta^{\ast}\big)={\bf B}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}},\beta)+{\bf B}\big(\xi_{L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})},\beta\big)=0.

Proposition follows from the fact that

\displaystyle L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h^{\prime}}=L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h}+\big(\xi_{L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})}\big)^{\ast}

and
\alpha_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}-L^{-1}(\xi\cdot\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}})^{h^{\prime}}
is tangent to
P
.
∎

5.1
Intrinsic geometry

For the intrinsic geometry we will compute the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{P}
of
(P,g_{{\rm SO}(M)})
, the curvature tensor, Ricci tensor and sectional and scalar curvatures. We will compare geometry of

Let us first introduce operator
Q_{\alpha}
and establish some relations. For
X\in TM
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{so}(M)
put

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}(X)=L^{-1}(R_{\alpha}(X)-\xi\cdot(\nabla_{X}\alpha)_{\mathfrak{m}}).

Moreover, it is easy to see that for any

we have the following relations with respect to the decomposition
\mathfrak{so}(M)=\mathfrak{g}(M)\oplus\mathfrak{m}(M)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\nabla_{X}\alpha_{\mathfrak{g}}=\nabla^{\prime}_{X}\alpha_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\xi_{X},\alpha_{\mathfrak{g}}],\\
&\nabla_{X}\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}=[\xi_{X},\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\big(\nabla^{\prime}_{X}\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}+[\xi_{X},\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}]_{\mathfrak{m}}\big).\end{split}

(5.1)

Theorem 5.2
.

The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{P}
of
P
with the induced Riemannian metric
g_{{\rm SO}(M)}
from
{\rm SO}(M)
is of the following form

\displaystyle\nabla^{P}_{X^{h^{\prime}}}Y^{h^{\prime}}=(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}Y)^{h^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}R^{\prime}(X,Y)^{\ast},\qquad

\displaystyle\nabla^{P}_{\alpha^{\ast}}Y^{h^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}Q_{\alpha}(Y)^{h^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\nabla^{P}_{X^{h^{\prime}}}\beta^{\ast}=\frac{1}{2}Q_{\beta}(X)^{h^{\prime}}+(\nabla^{\prime}_{X}\beta)^{\ast},\qquad

\displaystyle\nabla^{P}_{\alpha^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}=-\frac{1}{2}[\alpha,\beta]^{\ast},

where
X,Y\in TM
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{g}_{P}
.

Proof.

First, by (
4.1
) and (
3.9
) we have

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(Q_{\alpha}(X),Z)={\bf B}(R(X,Z),\alpha)+{\bf B}(\nabla_{X}\alpha,\xi_{Y}).

Thus, using (
2.3
) and (
4.2
)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(Q_{\xi_{X}}(Y)+Q_{\xi_{Y}}(X)+\xi\cdot\xi_{\nabla_{X}Y+\nabla_{Y}X},Z)

\displaystyle\qquad{}={\bf B}((\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Z}-(\nabla_{Z}\xi)_{X},\xi_{Y})+{\bf B}((\nabla_{Y}\xi)_{Z}-(\nabla_{Z}\xi)_{Y},\xi_{X})-{\bf B}([\xi_{X},\xi_{Z}],\xi_{Y})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-{\bf B}([\xi_{Y},\xi_{Z}],\xi_{X})+{\bf B}((\nabla_{Y}\xi)_{X}+(\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Y},\xi_{Z})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\tilde{g}(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}Y-\nabla_{X}Y,Z).

We have shown that

\displaystyle P(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\big(Q_{\xi_{X}}(Y)+Q_{\xi_{Y}}(X)+\xi\cdot\xi_{\nabla_{X}Y+\nabla_{Y}X}\big).

(5.2)

By the formula for the connection
\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}

\displaystyle\nabla^{P}_{X^{h^{\prime}}}Y^{h^{\prime}}=\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^{h}}Y^{h}+\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{(\xi_{X})^{\ast}}Y^{h}+\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^{h}}(\xi_{Y})^{\ast}+\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{(\xi_{X})^{\ast}}(\xi_{Y})^{\ast}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\nabla^P_{X^{h'}}Y^{h'}}{}=\left(\nabla_{X}Y+\frac{1}{2}R_{\xi_{Y}}(X)+\frac{1}{2}R_{\xi_{X}}(Y)\right)^{h}+\left(-\frac{1}{2}R(X,Y)+\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}-\frac{1}{2}[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}]\right)^{\ast}.

Since, by relations (
2.2
) between intrinsic torsion and curvature tensor,

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}R(X,Y)+\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}-\frac{1}{2}[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}]\right)_{\mathfrak{g}}=-\frac{1}{2}R^{\prime}(X,Y)

and

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}R(X,Y)+\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}-\frac{1}{2}[\xi_{X},\xi_{Y}]\right)_{\mathfrak{m}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}+\nabla_{Y}\xi_{X}+\xi_{[X,Y]}\right),

then, with the use of Proposition

\displaystyle\nabla^{P}_{X^{h^{\prime}}}Y^{h^{\prime}}=L^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{\xi_{Y}}(X)+\frac{1}{2}R_{\xi_{X}}(Y)-\frac{1}{2}\xi\cdot(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}+\nabla_{Y}\xi_{X})\right)^{h^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\nabla^P_{X^{h'}}Y^{h'}=}{}+L^{-1}\left(\nabla_{X}Y-\frac{1}{2}\xi\cdot\xi_{[X,Y]}\right)^{h^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}R^{\prime}(X,Y)^{\ast}.

Moreover

For the proof of the second formula we will use (

\displaystyle\nabla^{P}_{X^{h^{\prime}}}\beta^{\ast}=\left(\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^{h}}\beta^{\ast}+\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{(\xi_{X})^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}\right)^{\top}=\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{\beta}(X)^{h}+(\nabla_{X}\beta)^{\ast}-\frac{1}{2}[\xi_{X},\beta]^{\ast}\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\nabla^P_{X^{h'}}\beta^{\ast}}{}=L^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{\beta}(X)-\xi\cdot(\nabla_{X}\beta)_{\mathfrak{m}}+\frac{1}{2}\xi\cdot[\xi_{X},\beta]_{\mathfrak{m}}\right)^{h^{\prime}}+(\nabla_{X}\beta)^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{g}}

Proposition 5.3

The curvature tensor

The properties of the extrinsic geometry are encoded in the second fundamental form, which we will derive explicitly. Moreover, we will compute the mean curvature vector of

Adopt the notation from the previous sections. For

Theorem 5.8
.

The second fundamental form
\Pi^{P}
of

in

satisfies the following relations

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\Pi^{P}(X^{h^{\prime}},Y^{h^{\prime}}),\alpha^{+}\big)=\frac{1}{2}{\bf B}((\nabla_{X}\xi)_{Y}+(\nabla_{Y}\xi)_{X}-\xi_{R_{\xi_{X}}(Y)+R_{\xi_{Y}}(X)},\alpha),

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\Pi^{P}(\beta^{\ast},\gamma^{\ast}),\alpha^{+}\big)=0.

By the relations (

) (see also proof of Theorem

) we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^{h}}Y^{h}=\left(\nabla_{X}Y+\frac{1}{2}R_{\xi_{X}}(Y)+\frac{1}{2}R_{\xi_{Y}}(X)\right)^{h}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^h}Y^h =}{}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}+\nabla_{Y}\xi_{X}+\xi_{[X,Y]}\big)^{\ast}+\textrm{$\mathfrak{g}_{P}$-component}.

\displaystyle g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\Pi^{P}(X^{h^{\prime}},Y^{h^{\prime}}),\alpha^{+}\big)=g\left(\nabla_{X}Y+\frac{1}{2}(R_{\xi_{X}}(Y)+R_{\xi_{Y}}(X)),\xi\cdot\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{g_{{\rm SO}(M)}\big(\Pi^P(X^{h'},Y^{h'}),\alpha^{+}\big) =}{}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf B}\big(\nabla_{X}\xi_{Y}+\nabla_{Y}\xi_{X}+\xi_{[X,Y]},\alpha\big),

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\rm SO}(M)}_{X^{h^{\prime}}}\gamma^{\ast}=\frac{1}{2}R_{\gamma}(X)^{h}+\frac{1}{2}[\xi_{X},\gamma]^{\ast}+\textrm{$\mathfrak{g}_{P}$-component},

which proves the second equality. Since

, it follows that

-component of

.

If a

-structure

is integrable, i.e., the intrinsic torsion

is totally geodesic in

.

Now, we can state the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 5.10
.

A
G
-structure
P
is minimal in
{\rm SO}(M)
if and only the induced section
\sigma\colon M{\to}N
is a harmonic map with respect to Riemannian metrics
\tilde{g}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
M
and
N
, respectively.

Proof.

Recall, that by Proposition
3.1
section
\sigma\colon(M,\tilde{g})\to(N,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a harmonic map if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\nabla_{\tilde{e_{i}}}\xi)_{\tilde{e_{i}}}-\xi_{S(\tilde{e_{i}},\tilde{e_{i}})}=0,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}R_{\xi_{\tilde{e_{i}}}}(\tilde{e_{i}})-S(\tilde{e_{i}},\tilde{e_{i}})=0,

(5.5)

whereas, by Theorem
5.8
, minimality of
P
is equivalent to the following condition

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\nabla_{\tilde{e_{i}}}\xi)_{\tilde{e_{i}}}-\xi_{R_{\xi_{\tilde{e_{i}}}}(\tilde{e_{i}})}=0.

(5.6)

Clearly, (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) imply (
5.6
). Conversely, assume (
5.6
) holds. It suffices to show that (
5.5
) holds. By (
4.2
) and (

) we have

\displaystyle g\left(\sum_{i}R_{\xi_{\tilde{e_{i}}}}(\tilde{e_{i}})-S(\tilde{e_{i}},\tilde{e_{i}}),LZ\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=-\sum_{i}{\bf B}\big((\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi)_{e_{i}},\xi_{Z}\big)+{\bf B}\big((\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi)_{Z}-(\nabla_{Z}\xi)_{e_{i}},\xi_{e_{i}}\big)+\sum_{i}g\big(R_{\xi_{e_{i}}}(e_{i}),Z-\xi\cdot\xi_{Z}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=-\sum_{i}{\bf B}\big((\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi)_{Z}-(\nabla_{Z}\xi)_{e_{i}},\xi_{e_{i}}\big)+{\bf B}\big(R(e_{i},Z),\xi_{e_{i}}\big).

By the relations (
2.2
) and the fact that
{\rm SO}(n)/G
is a normal homogeneous space we get the desired equality.
∎

Let

. The reduction of the oriented orthonormal frame bundle
{\rm SO}(M)
to the subbundle
P
with the structure group
G
is equivalent to the existence of
m
-dimensional distribution
E
on
M
and, hence, its orthogonal complement
F=E^{\bot}
. We call
M
with the distinguished distribution
E
an almost product structure.

The connection
\nabla^{\prime}
induced by the connection form
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
\omega
is the connection form of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
, takes the form

\displaystyle\nabla^{\prime}_{X}Y=\big(\nabla_{X}Y^{\top}\big)^{\top}+\big(\nabla_{X}Y^{\bot}\big)^{\bot},

where the decomposition
X=X^{\top}+X^{\bot}
is taken with respect to
TM=E\oplus F
. In other words it is the sum of two connections induced by
\nabla
-connections in the vector bundles
E
and
F
over
M
. The intrinsic torsion of almost product structure equals

\displaystyle\xi_{X}Y=\big(\nabla_{X}Y^{\bot}\big)^{\top}+\big(\nabla_{X}Y^{\top}\big)^{\bot}.

The associated bundle
N={\rm SO}(M)/G={\rm SO}(M)\times_{{\rm SO}(n)}({\rm SO}(n)/G)
is the Grassmann bundle
\operatorname{Gr}^{\rm o}_{m}(TM)
of
m
-dimensional oriented subspaces of tangent spaces to
M
and the induced section
\sigma\colon M\to N
is just the Gauss map of the distribution
E
. Therefore the main result (Theorem
5.10
) states that a
{\rm SO}(m)\times{\rm SO}(n-m)
-structure
P\subset{\rm SO}(M)
is minimal if and only if the Gauss map
\sigma\colon(M,\tilde{g})\to(\operatorname{Gr}^{\rm o}_{m}(TM),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
of
E
is a harmonic map or, alternatively, if the image
E=\sigma(M)\subset\operatorname{Gr}^{\rm o}_{m}(TM)
is minimal in the oriented Grassmann bundle
\operatorname{Gr}^{\rm o}_{m}(TM)
.

Let us now give two examples (compare
[
10
,
11
,
14
]
).

Example 6.1
.

Let
(M,g,X_{0})
be a
K
-contact manifold with the Reeb vector field
X_{0}
, i.e.,
X_{0}
is a unit Killing vector field and there exist one-form
\eta
and endomorphism
\varphi
such that

\displaystyle\eta(X)=g(X,X_{0}),\qquad\varphi^{2}X=-X+\eta(X)X_{0},\qquad d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi Y),\qquad\iota_{X_{0}}d\eta=0

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
.

The one-dimensional distribution
E
tangent to
X_{0}
defines the almost product structure on
M
. Since
X_{0}
is geodesic vector field, the distribution
E
is totally geodesic. It can be shown, that
E\subset\operatorname{Gr}^{\rm o}_{n-1}(TM)
is a minimal submanifold or, in other words, the immersion
\sigma\colon(M,\tilde{g})\to(\operatorname{Gr}^{\rm o}_{n-1}(TM),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
,
\sigma(x)=E_{x}
,
x\in M
, is minimal. Therefore, the
{\rm SO}(n-1)\times{\rm SO}(1)
-structure
P
induced by
E
is minimal in the orthonormal frame bundle
{\rm SO}(M)
.

Example 6.2
.

Consider the sphere
S^{4n-1}
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
and let
I
,
J
,
K
be the usual quaternionic structure on
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
. The
3
-dimensional subspaces spanned by
IN
,
JN
,
KN
, where
N
is the unit outward vector field to
S^{4n-1}
, determine the Hopf distribution
E
on
S^{4n-1}
. Since the Hopf distribution defines the minimal immersion of
S^{4n-1}
into the Grassmann bundle
\operatorname{Gr}^{\rm o}_{3}(TS^{4n-1})
, it follows that the
{\rm SO}(3)\times{\rm SO}(4n-4)
-structure
P\subset{\rm SO}(S^{4n-1})
is minimal.

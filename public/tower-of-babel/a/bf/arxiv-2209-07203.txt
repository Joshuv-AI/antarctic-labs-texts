Biunit pairs in semiheaps and associated semigroups
Bernard Rybołowicz, Carlos Zapata-Carratalá

Abstract
. Biunit pairs are introduced as pairs of elements in a semiheap that generalize the notion of unit. Families of functions generalizing involutions and conjugations, called switches and warps, are investigated. The main theorem establishes that there is a one-to-one correspondence between monoids equipped with a particular switch and semiheaps with a biunit pair. This generalizes a well-established result in semiheap theory that connects involuted semigroups and semiheaps with biunit elements. Furthermore, diheaps are introduced as semiheaps whose elements belong to biunit pairs and they are shown to be non-isomorphic but warp-equivalent to heaps.

Introduction

The study of semiheaps can be traced back to the early work on semigroup theory by R. Baer
[
Bae29
]
, H. Prüfer
[
Prü24
]
and A.K. Suškevič
[
Suš37
]
. They investigated what were later known as heaps, certain kinds of ternary algebras arising from groups and semigroups satisfying some special conditions, see definitions
1.1
and
1.10
. Heaps and groups are closely related. From a heap
(H,[-,-,-])
, we can construct a group
\mathrm{G}(H)
and vice-versa. That is achieved by fixing an element
a\in H
and currying it in the middle argument of the ternary operation
[-,-,-]
to acquire a binary one
(x,y)\xmapsto{[-,a,-]}[x,a,y]
. Conversely, we associate a heap
\mathrm{H}(G)
with a group
(G,\cdot)
by taking the ternary bracket
[a,b,c]:=a\cdot b^{-1}\cdot c
, where
a,b,c\in G
. If we start with a ternary operation, these constructions compose to the original structure. In the other direction, starting with a group, we only obtain the group up to isomorphism as we forget which element is fixed, but we always find a so-called variant, see
[
Hic83
]
. In 1953, V. V. Wagner
[
Wag53
]
introduced semiheaps in the context of partial functions and relations on sets as a way to formalize the theory of coordinate charts on manifolds. A
semiheap
is a straightforward generalisation of a heap: a ternary algebra with the generalized associativity property satisfied by heaps but without any cancelling behaviour of elements, that is
(S,[-,-,-])
such that

[[a,b,c],d,e]=[a,[d,c,b],e]=[a,b,[c,d,e]]

for all
a,b,c,d,e\in S
. These ternary algebras have applications to Morita equivalence
[
Law11
]
, pseudogroups
[
Koc07
]
, affine structures
[
HS11
]

[
Bre+22
]
, Lie theory and quantum mechanics
[
Brz22
]

[
Bru22
]
, and hypermatrix theory
[
Abr+09
]

[
ZAB22
]
. In contrast to groups and heaps, there is no correspondence between semiheaps and semigroups. We can associate with every semiheap a semigroup by fixing an element. For different choices, however, semigroups do not have to be isomorphic anymore. The opposite direction is not unique either, more data is required. In his seminal paper
[
Wag53
]
, Wagner shows that there is a correspondence between involuted monoids and semiheaps with
biunit elements
, that is elements
e\in S
satisfying

[e,e,x]=x=[x,e,e]

for all
a\in S
. If there exists a biunit element, one can build an involuted monoid from a semiheap, and then reconstruct it from the monoid. Similarly, for monoids with involutive anti-homomorphism, we construct a semiheap with biunit from an involuted monoid, and then reconstruct the monoid up to isomorphism. Our contribution is to consider a more parsimonious generalization of biunit elements in semiheaps by considering a pair
(a,b)
such that:

[a,b,x]=x=[x,a,b]

which we call a
biunit pair
. The main purpose of our paper is to extend the classical result of Wagner
[
Wag53
]
to semiheaps with biunit pairs and determine the appropriate class of semigroups that establishes the correspondence.

We begin in Section

1.
Preliminaries

Throughout this work we will consider a set

In this paper we will mainly consider a particular class of ternars called semiheaps.

Definition 1.1
.

A

[[a,b,c],d,e]=[a,[d,c,b],e]=[a,b,[c,d,e]].

]

[

]

[

]

[

]
, type B associativity
[

]
and pseudo-associativity

Definition 1.2
.

Given a semiheap

Example 1.3
.

The classical example of a semiheap is the compositional structure of the set of binary relations between two arbitrary sets

given by the following ternary operation:

[R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}]_{\textsf{Rel}}:=R_{1}\circ R_{2}^{\top}\circ R_{3}

is an involution and a

Let

2.
Twists, Warps and Switches

In this section, we study the twistings of semiheaps. By that, we understand a method to deform a ternary operation to acquire a new semiheap. Furthermore, we research connections among semigroups, semiheaps and twisted semiheaps. Let us start by clarifying what we understand by twists.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a semiheap and

We will be particularly interested in semiheaps twisted by warps.

Let

Remark 2.9
.

It is worth noting that in general, a composition of warps is not a warp. Let
S
be a semiheap,
\varphi
be a warp on
S
and
\psi
be a warp on
S_{\varphi}
. Then
\psi\circ\varphi
is not necessarily a warp on
S
. One can easily show that if
\psi\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\psi
, then
\psi\circ\varphi
is a warp on
S
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(S,[-,-,-])
be a semiheap and
\eta
be a bijection and a warp. Then
\eta^{-1}
is also a warp.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in S
and observe that since
\eta
is a bijection there exist
e,d,f
such that
\eta(e)=a
,
\eta(b)=d
and
\eta(c)=f
.Then

\eta^{-1}([a,\eta^{-1}(b),c])=\eta^{-1}([\eta(e),\eta^{-1}(\eta(d)),\eta(f)])=\eta^{-1}(\eta([e,\eta(d),f]))=[\eta^{-1}(a),b,\eta^{-1}(c)].

Thus
\eta^{-1}
is a warp.
∎

Observe that since the inverse of the bijective warp is a warp, we get a criterion for the equivalence of semiheap structures on the same set.

Definition 2.11
.

We say that two semiheaps
S
and
H
are
warp equivalent
if there exist a sequence of bijective warps
(\varphi_{i})_{i\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,n\}}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
H=(((S_{\varphi_{1}})_{\varphi_{2}})...)_{\varphi_{n}}
, where
\varphi_{i}
is a warp of
S_{\varphi_{i-1}}
and
\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}
.

Indeed a relation
S\sim H
if and only if
S
is warp equivalent to
H
is an equivalence relation on the set of all twists of
S
. Indeed
S\sim S
as an identity is a warp. The relation is a symmetry by Lemma
2.10
. Transitivity follows trivially from the definition of warp equivalency.

Given the close connection between semigroups and semiheaps, see Example
1.4
and Theorem
1.6
, we are compelled to consider a warp analogue for a semigroup.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
(S,\cdot)
be a semigroup. We say that a function
\varphi:S\to S
is a
switch
if for all
a,b,c\in G
,

\varphi(a\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot c)=\varphi(c)\cdot b\cdot\varphi(a).

A semigroup equipped with a switch
(S,\cdot,\varphi)
is called a
switch semigroup
.

Example 2.13
.

Any involuted semigroup
(S,\cdot,*)
, in particular, any group taking
*=(-)^{-1}
, is a switch semigroup since

(a\cdot b^{*}\cdot c)^{*}=c^{*}\cdot b\cdot a^{*}.

We thus see that switch semigroups generalize involuted semigroups by relaxing the algebraic condition on the map
\varphi
from an antihomomorphic involution to the switch condition as defined above. The following lemma shows that the switch condition is, nevertheless, sufficient for a switch semigroup to induce a semiheap structure on the base set.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
(S,\cdot)
be a semigroup and
\varphi:S\to S
be a switch. Then
(S,[-,-,-]^{\varphi})
, where
[a,b,c]^{\varphi}=a\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot c
for all
a,b,c\in S
, is a semiheap.

Proof.

Let us check that associativity of semiheaps holds. Let
a,b,c,d,e\in S
,

[[a,b,c]^{\varphi},d,e]^{\varphi}=a\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot c\cdot\varphi(d)\cdot e=[a,b,[c,d,e]^{\varphi}]^{\varphi}

and

\displaystyle{}^{\varphi},d,e]^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=a\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot c\cdot\varphi(d)\cdot e=a\cdot\varphi(d\cdot\varphi(c)\cdot b)\cdot e=a\cdot\varphi([d,c,b]^{\varphi})\cdot e

\displaystyle=[a,[d,c,b]^{\varphi},e]^{\varphi}.

Thus
(S,[-,-,-]^{\varphi})
is a semiheap.
∎

Moreover, we can state the analogue of Lemma
2.5
for semigroups.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
(S,\cdot)
be a semigroup and
\varphi:S\to S
be a function. If there exist
l,r\in S
such that functions
s\xmapsto{l\cdot}l\cdot s
and
s\xmapsto{\cdot r}s\cdot r
are injective, then
(S,[-,-,-]^{\varphi})
, where
[a,b,c]^{\varphi}=a\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot c
for all
a,b,c\in S
, is a semiheap if and only if
\varphi
is a switch.

Proof.

If
\varphi
is a switch, then
(S,[-,-,-]^{\varphi})
is a semiheap by Lemma
2.14
.

In the opposite direction, let us assume
(S,[-,-,-]^{\varphi})
is a semiheap. Then by associativity of semiheap we get that for all
a,b,c\in S
,

l\cdot\varphi(c\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot a)\cdot r=[l,[c,b,a]^{\varphi},r]^{\varphi}=[[l,a,b]^{\varphi},c,r]^{\varphi}=l\cdot\varphi(a)\cdot b\cdot\varphi(c)\cdot r.

Now, since both maps
l\cdot,\cdot r:S\to S
are injective, we get that

\varphi(c\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot a)=\varphi(a)\cdot b\cdot\varphi(c).

Thus
\varphi
is a switch.
∎

Example 2.16
.

Observe that assumptions of the Lemma
2.15
are quite strong. If we consider semigroup
S
with a constant binary operation
(a,b)\xmapsto{\cdot}c
, for a fixed c, then any function
f:S\to S
is a switch. Even though multiplications by any elements are not injective, we still get a semiheap
(S,[-,-,-]^{f}).

Example 2.17
.

If the semigroup
S
is abelian, then an identity is a switch. That is
(S,[-,-,-]^{\mathrm{Id}})
is a semiheap.

.

Let

In an abelian case switches and warps coincide, for a particular choice of ternary operation.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
(S,\cdot)
be an abelian semigroup.Then
\varphi:S\to S
is a switch if and only if
\varphi
is a warp of
(S,[-,-,-]^{\mathrm{Id}})
.

Proof.

If
\varphi
is a switch and
[a,b,c]^{\mathrm{Id}}=a\cdot b\cdot c
, then

\varphi([c,\varphi(b),a])=\varphi(c\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot a)=\varphi(c)\cdot b\cdot\varphi(a)=[\varphi(c),b,\varphi(a)],

and
\varphi
is a warp.

If
\varphi
is a warp for
(S,[-,-,-]^{\mathrm{Id}})
, then

\varphi(a\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot c)=\varphi([a,\varphi(b),c])=\varphi([c,\varphi(b),a])=[\varphi(c),b,\varphi(a)]=\varphi(c)\cdot b\cdot\varphi(a),

where the second equality follows from the fact that
S
is an abelian semigroup. Thus
\varphi
is a switch
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
(S,[-,-,-])

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
(S,\cdot,1)
be a monoid,
\varphi:S\to S
a switch and
u\in S
be such that
\varphi(u)=1
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

A switch
\varphi
is an involutive antihomomorphism of monoids if and only if
u=1
.

(2)

The element
u\in S
is invertible with the inverse
\varphi(1)
.

(3)

For all
a,b\in S
,
\varphi(a\cdot b)=\varphi(b)\cdot u\cdot\varphi(a)
and
\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(b)=\varphi(b\cdot u^{-1}\cdot a)
.

(4)

For all
a\in S
,

\varphi(a)\cdot u^{-1}=\varphi(u^{-1}\cdot a),\qquad u^{-1}\cdot\varphi(a)=\varphi(a\cdot u^{-1}),

\varphi(u\cdot a)=\varphi(a)\cdot u,\qquad\varphi(a\cdot u)=u\cdot\varphi(a).

(5)

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\varphi(u^{n})=u^{n-1}.

(6)

\varphi
is a bijection.

Proof.

1
. Assume
\varphi(1)=1
, then for all
a,b\in S
,

\varphi(a\cdot b)=\varphi(a\cdot\varphi(1)\cdot b)=\varphi(a)\cdot 1\cdot\varphi(b)=\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(b)

and

\varphi(\varphi(b))=\varphi(1\cdot\varphi(b)\cdot 1)=\varphi(1)\cdot b\cdot\varphi(1)=b.

Thus,
\varphi
is an involutive antihomomorphism. Opposite follows by the fact that antihomomorphism of monoids preserve an identity.

2
. Observe that
\varphi(1)=u^{-1}
, since

u\cdot\varphi(1)=\varphi(u)\cdot u\cdot\varphi(1)=\varphi(u\cdot\varphi(u)\cdot 1)=\varphi(u)=1.

Analogously from the right side, so
\varphi(1)=u^{-1}
and
u
is invertible.

3
. Let
a,b\in S
, then

\varphi(a\cdot b)=\varphi(a\cdot 1\cdot b)=\varphi(a\cdot\varphi(u)\cdot b)=\varphi(b)\cdot u\cdot\varphi(a),

\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(b)=\varphi(a)\cdot 1\cdot\varphi(b)=\varphi(b\cdot\varphi(1)\cdot a)=\varphi(b\cdot u^{-1}\cdot a).

4
. Observe that by taking
b=1
in
3
, we get that

\varphi(a)\cdot u^{-1}=\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(1)=\varphi(u^{-1}\cdot a)\ \&\ u^{-1}\cdot\varphi(a)=\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(1)=\varphi(a\cdot u^{-1}),

Similarly, by taking
b=u
, we get

\varphi(a\cdot u)=\varphi(u)\cdot u\cdot\varphi(a)=u\cdot\varphi(a)\ \&\ \varphi(u\cdot a)=\varphi(a)\cdot u\cdot\varphi(u)=\varphi(a)\cdot u,

Thus
4
holds.

5
. Simply, by taking
a=u^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
in
4
, we get that

\varphi(u^{n})=\varphi(\underbrace{u^{sgn(n)}\cdot\ldots\cdot u^{sgn(n)}}_{|n|-times}\cdot 1)=\varphi(1)\cdot u^{sgn(n)|n|}=u^{-1}\cdot u^{n}=u^{n-1},

where
sgn(n)
is a sign of a number
n
and
|n|
is absolute value of
n
, that is
sgn(n)|n|=n
.

6
. Let us consider a function
a\xmapsto{\iota_{u}}u\cdot x\cdot u
. Then the conposition
\iota_{u}\circ\varphi
is the inverse function of
\varphi
. Indeed, for all
a\in S
,

\iota_{u}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi(a)=\iota_{u}(\varphi(1\circ\varphi(a)\circ 1))=\iota_{u}(\varphi(1)\cdot a\cdot\varphi(1))=u\cdot\varphi(1)\cdot a\cdot\varphi(1)\cdot u=u\cdot u^{-1}\cdot a\cdot u^{-1}\cdot u=a,

where second equality follows by the fact that
\varphi
is a switch and fourth follows by (
2
).
∎

3.
Biunit pairs

The main goal of this section, and paper, is to state and prove the generalized version of
[
Wag53
, Theorem 2.8]
for biunit pairs, see Theorem
3.16
. Let us start with the definiton of biunit pairs.

Definition 3.1
.

[Biunit Pair cf.
[
Wag53
,
Wag65
,
HL17
]
] Let
(S,[-,-,-])
be a semiheap and
a,b\in S
. We say that the pair
(a,b)
is a
left (right) biunit pair
if for all
x\in S

[a,b,x]=x\qquad([x,a,b]=x).

(3.1)

If a pair is left and right biunit, we call it a
biunit pair
.

Note that for an element
a\in S
such that
(a,a)
is a biunit pair we recover the definition of Mal’cev or biunit element.

Example 3.2
.

Let us consider a set of odd integers
\mathrm{Odd}:=\{2n+1\ |\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
together with a bracket
[-,-,-]:\mathrm{Odd}^{3}\to\mathrm{Odd}
given for all
a,b,c\in\mathrm{Odd}
by
[a,b,c]=a+b+c
. Obviously odd numbers are closed on the bracket operation. Moreover, an element is a biunit if and only if
a+a+b=b
, which implies
a=0
,however
0\not\in\mathrm{Odd}
. Now, observe that a pair
(a,-a)
is a biunit pair for all
a\in\mathrm{Odd}
.

Example 3.3
.

In the algebra of cubic matrices
[
ZAB22
]
, the ternary operation on
3
-index arrays of scalars (elements of a semiring in general) of size
N\times N\times N
defined by

[a,b,c]_{ijk}:=\sum_{p,q,r=1}^{N}a_{ijp}\cdot b_{qrp}\cdot c_{qrk}

is easily checked to be a semiheap operation. The pair of special cubic matrices generalizing the identity matrix defined by

I_{ijk}=\delta_{ijk}\qquad\iota_{ijk}=\delta_{ik},

form a (right) biunit pair since we can directly compute:

[a,I,\iota]_{ijk}=a_{ijk}=[a,\iota,I]_{ijk}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(S,[-,-,-])
be a semiheap. Then

(1)

If
(a,b)
is a left (right) biunit pair then
(b,a)
is a left (right) biunit pair.

(2)

For any
a\in S
, if there exists
b\in S
such that
(a,b)
is a biunit pair, then
b
is unique.

(3)

If
(a,b)
is a left (right) biunit pair. Then if
b
is a right (left) biunit, then
a
is a left (right) biunit.

Proof.

1
. Observe that if
(a,b)\in S\times S
is a left biunit pair then
[a,b,b]=b
, and for all
x\in S
,

[b,a,x]=[[a,b,b],a,x]=[a,[a,b,b],x]=[a,b,x]=x,

thus
(b,a)
is a left biunit pair. Similarly for the right case.

2
. Observe that if
(a,b)
and
(a,b^{\prime})
are biunit pairs, then

b^{\prime}=[[a,b,b^{\prime}],a,b]=[a,[a,b^{\prime},b],b]=[a,b,b]=b,

and

is unique.

3
. Let us assume that
(a,b)
is a biunit pair and

is a right biunit. Then for all

so

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S
be a semiheap with a biunit pair
(a,b)
, then

, for a bijective warp
\varphi

(a,c)

Let
(a,b)
be a biunit pair and

Observe that since
\varphi
is injective we get that

[x,a,c]_{\varphi}=[x,\varphi(a),c]=x,

and
(a,c)
is a biunit in

.

Let

(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.25264pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$c$}$}\hskip-6.25264pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,)
. Moreover,
\mu_{aa}:(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.73181pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.73181pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,)\to(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.25264pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$c$}$}\hskip-6.25264pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,)
is an anti-isomorphism of semigroups.

Proof.

Indeed, let
x,y\in S
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{ac}(x)\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,\mu_{ac}(y)

\displaystyle=[[a,x,c],a,[a,y,c]]=[a,[a,c,x],[a,y,c]]=[a,x,[a,y,c]]

\displaystyle=[a,[y,a,x],c]=\mu_{ac}(y\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,x),

where third and fifth follows by the associativity of semiheap and the fourth one follows by the fact that
(a,c)
is a biunit pair. Thus
\mu_{ac}
is an anti-endomorphism of the semigroup
(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,)
. Since
\mu_{ac}
is an involution, due to Lemma
3.8
,
\mu_{ac}
is an automorphism. Proof for the semigroup
(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.05267pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$c$}$}\hskip-6.05267pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,)
is analogous. The case of
\mu_{ca}
can be proven the same way.

The second statement is observation that for all
x,y\in S
,

\displaystyle\mu_{aa}(x\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,y)

\displaystyle=[a,[x,a,y],a]=[a,[x,[a,c,a],y],a]=[[a,y,[a,c,a]],x,a]=[[a,y,a],c,[a,x,a]]

\displaystyle=\mu_{aa}(y)\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.05267pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$c$}$}\hskip-6.05267pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,\mu_{aa}(x).

Thus,
\mu_{aa}
is an anti-homomorphism, and by Lemma
3.8
, it is an anti-isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

A set of all left (right) biunit pairs is a subsemiheap of a product semiheap
S\times S
with ternary operation given on coordinates, that is for all left biunit pairs
(a,b),(c,d),(e,g)

[(a,b),(c,d),(e,g)]:=([a,c,e],[b,d,g])
.

Proof.

Let
(a,b),(c,d)
and
(e,g)
be biunit pairs and
x\in S
, then

\displaystyle,[b,d,g],x]

\displaystyle=[[[a,c,e],g,d],b,x]=[[a,c,[e,g,d]],b,x]=[[a,c,d],b,x]=[a,[d,c,b],x]

\displaystyle=[a,b,x]=x.

Thus
([a,c,e],[b,d,g])
is a left biunit pair. Associativity, follows from the definition of a ternary operation on the product.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
(S,[-,-,-])

\mu_{bb}

(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.73181pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.73181pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,)

[x,y,z]=x\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.73181pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.73181pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,\mu_{bb}(y)\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.73181pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.73181pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,z

Corollary 3.14

If

. Thus,

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
S
be a semiheap with two different biunit pairs
(a,b)
and

(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.73181pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.73181pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,,b)

are related by equation

\lambda_{da}\circ\mu_{bb}=\mu_{dd}\circ\lambda_{ad}^{-1}.

Proof.

Let us check that

is an isomorphism of monoids. Indeed for all

,

\displaystyle\lambda_{da}(x)\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.05267pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$c$}$}\hskip-6.05267pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,\lambda_{da}(y)

\displaystyle=[[d,a,x],c,[d,a,y]]=[d,a,x],[a,d,c],y]=[[d,a,x],c,y]

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
(S,\cdot,1)
be a monoid and
\varphi
be a bijective switch. Then a pair
(\varphi^{-1}(1),1)
is a biunit pair in the semiheap
(S,[-,-,-]^{\varphi})
. Furthermore, every semiheap
S
with a biunit pair
(a,b)
is a monoid
(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.73181pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.73181pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,,b)
with a bijective switch
\mu_{bb}
.

Proof.

The second part is Lemma
3.13
and Corollary
3.14
. For the first part,
(S,[-,-,-]^{\varphi})
is a semiheap by Lemma
2.14
, so it is enough to check that
(\varphi^{-1}(1),1)
is a biunit pair. Indeed, observe that for all
c\in S
,

[\varphi^{-1}(1),1,c]^{\varphi}=\varphi^{-1}(1)\cdot\varphi(1)\cdot c=c=c\cdot\varphi(1)\cdot\varphi^{-1}(1)=[c,1,\varphi^{-1}(1)]^{\varphi},

where
\varphi(1)
is the inverse of
\varphi^{-1}(1)
by the Proposition
2.21
(
2
).
By Lemma
3.4
(
1
) it is enough to check just this one equality.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between semiheaps with a chosen biunit pair and monoids with a bijective switch:

\{\text{semiheap}+\text{biunit}\,\,\text{pair}\}\rightleftharpoons\{\text{monoid}+\text{bijective}\,\,\text{switch}\}

which is realized explicitly via the assignments:

(S,(a,b))\xmapsto{\Omega}(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.53331pt\hskip-6.73181pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.73181pt\hskip-10.53331pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.53331pt\hskip 10.53331pt}\,,b,\mu_{bb})\quad\text{and}\quad(M,\cdot,1,\varphi)\xmapsto{\Lambda}((M,[-,-,-]^{\varphi}),(\varphi^{-1}(1),1)).

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\Lambda=\Omega^{-1}
. Indeed, let us consider

\Lambda\circ\Omega(S,(a,b))=\Lambda(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,,b,\mu_{bb}).

Observe that for all
x,y,z\in S
,

[x,y,z]^{\mu_{bb}}=x\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,\mu_{bb}(y)\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,z=[[x,a,[b,y,b]],a,z]=[x,y,z]

and

\mu_{bb}^{-1}(b)=\mu_{aa}(b)=a,

where
[-,-,-]
is the ternary operation of
S
. Thus
\Lambda(S,\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.53185pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$a$}$}\hskip-6.53185pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,,b,\mu_{bb})=(S,(a,b))
, and
\Omega
is a right inverse of
\Lambda
.

For the left inverse, we have that
\Omega(\Lambda(M,\cdot,1,\varphi))=\Omega(M,[-,-,-]^{\varphi},(\varphi^{-1}(1),1))
. Let us denote
e:=\varphi^{-1}(1)
, observe that for all
x,y\in M
,

a\,\hbox{\hskip 10.2778pt\hskip-6.21703pt\hbox{$\scalebox{.8}{$e$}$}\hskip-6.21703pt\hskip-10.2778pt\raisebox{0.84723pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{1.2}{$\square$}$}}\hskip-10.2778pt\hskip 10.2778pt}\,b=a\cdot\varphi(e)\cdot b=a\cdot\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(1))\cdot b=a\cdot b,

1
is the identity from the definition of
\Omega
, and

\mu_{11}(x)=1\cdot\varphi(x)\cdot 1=\varphi(x).

Thus
\Omega((M,[-,-,-]^{\varphi}),(\varphi^{-1}(1),1))=(M,\cdot,1,\varphi)
, and
\Omega
is a right inverse to
\Lambda
. Therefore
\Lambda
is a bijection and we have one-to-one correspondence.
∎

These results motivate the definition of the family of semiheaps whose elements belong to biunit pairs.

Definition 3.18
.

A semiheap
(D,[-,-,-])
in which for every element
a\in D
there exists
b\in D
such that
(a,b)
is a biunit pair, is called a
diheap
.

Example 3.19
.

Any abelian group
(A,+)
has an associated abelian semiheap structure
(A,[-,-,-])
given by
[a,b,c]:=a+b+c
. The inverse map ensures that all elements
a\in A
are in a pair
(a,-a)
satisfying:

[a,-a,x]=a-a+x=x=x+a-a=[x,a,-a]

and thus
(A,[-,-,-])
is a diheap, that otherwise fails to be a heap in general.

It follows from this definition that diheaps generalize heaps. In fact, there is a deep connection between diheaps and heaps via twisting.

Proposition 3.20
.

A diheap
(D,[-,-,-])
is warp equivalent to a heap
D_{\psi}
, where
\psi
is an involutive semiheap automorphism, and thus a warp.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c,d\in D
and let us denote by
\hat{a}
, a second component of a biunit pair, that is an element of
D
such that
(a,\hat{a})
is a biunit pair. Then, from Lemma
3.4
we get that the map
\psi(a):=\hat{a}
is a well-defined function and an involution. The only thing to check is that
\psi
is a warp. Observe that

[[a,b,c],[\hat{a},\hat{b},\hat{c}],d]=[[a,b,[c,\hat{c},\hat{b}]],\hat{a},d]=d.

Thus
\widehat{[a,b,c]}=[\hat{a},\hat{b},\hat{c}]
and

\psi([a,\psi(b),c])=\widehat{[a,\hat{b},c]}=[\hat{a},\hat{\hat{b}},\hat{c}]=[\hat{a},b,\hat{c}]=[\psi(a),b,\psi(c)].

Therefore
\psi
is a warp, and one can easily check that every element in
D_{\psi}
is a biunit. Thus,
D
is warp equivalent to a heap.
∎

Note that if the diheap
(D,[-,-,-])
in Proposition
3.20
above is a heap, i.e. all biunit pairs are of the form
(a,a)
for
a\in D
, then the induced involutive semiheap automorphism is simply the identity
\psi=\text{id}_{D}
. This suggest that heaps and diheaps are distinct classes of semiheaps, as we can indeed confirm.

Proposition 3.21
.

Considered as semiheaps, a diheap is isomorphic to a heap if and only if it is, itself, a heap.

Proof.

Firstly, observe that any surjective homomorphism
\varphi:D\to S
, for some semiheaps
D
and
S
, preserve biunit pairs. Indeed, if
(a,b)
is a biunit pair in
D
, then for all
y\in S
exists
x\in D
such that
\varphi(x)=y
and

[\varphi(a),\varphi(b),y]=\varphi([a,b,x])=\varphi(x)=y=\varphi(x)=\varphi([x,a,b])=[y,\varphi(a),\varphi(b)].

Thus
(\varphi(a),\varphi(b))
is a biunit pair in
S
. Now, if
D
is a diheap which is not a heap, i.e. there exists at least one biunit pair
(a,b)
such that
a\neq b
, and
S
is a heap, it follows that
\varphi(a)=\varphi(b)
thus
\varphi
fails to be injective. Conversely, if diheap is a heap, it is isomorphic to itself.
∎

Diheaps thus appear as a well-motivated novel class of semiheaps that strictly generalizes heaps. This poses some intriguing lines of further enquiry. Although we have shown that diheaps are generally non-isomorphic to heaps, one can ask whether there exists a different category of semiheaps where warp equivalence corresponds to isomorphism. It seems plausible that the diheaps induced from invertible structures as in Example
3.19
are essentially the only class of non-heap diheaps. Following Theorem
3.16
, diheaps correspond to a particular class of switch monoids, it will be interesting to consider whether such class of algebras has been identified before or whether it may have some application in semigroup research.

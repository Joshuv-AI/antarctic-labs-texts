The Question of Arnold on classification of co-artin subalgebras in singularity theory
V. V. Bavula

Abstract
In [ 1 , Section 5, p.32] , Arnold writes: “Classification of singularities of curves can be interpreted in dual terms as a description of ‘co-artin’ subalgebras of finite co-dimension in the algebra of formal series in a single variable (up to isomorphism of the algebra of formal series).” In the paper, such a description is obtained but up to isomorphism of algebraic curves (i.e. this description is finer). Let K K be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. The aim of the paper is to give a classification (up to isomorphism) of the set of subalgebras 𝒜 {\cal A} of the polynomial algebra K ⁡ [ x ] K[x] that contains the ideal x m ​ K ​ [ x ] x^{m}K[x] for some m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 . It is proven that the set 𝒜 = ∐ m , Γ 𝒜 ⁡ ( m , Γ ) {\cal A}=\coprod_{m,\Gamma}{\cal A}(m,\Gamma) is a disjoint union of affine algebraic varieties (where Γ ​ ∐ { 0 , m , m + 1 , … } \Gamma\coprod\{0,m,m+1,\ldots\} is the semigroup of the singularity and m − 1 m-1 is the Frobenius number). It is proven that each set 𝒜 ⁡ ( m , Γ ) {\cal A}(m,\Gamma) is an affine algebraic variety and explicit generators and defining relations are given for the algebra of regular functions on 𝒜 ⁡ ( m , Γ ) {\cal A}(m,\Gamma) . An isomorphism criterion is given for the algebras in 𝒜 {\cal A} . For each algebra A ∈ 𝒜 ⁡ ( m , Γ ) A\in{\cal A}(m,\Gamma) , explicit sets of generators and defining relations are given and the automorphism group Aut K ​ ( A ) {\rm Aut}_{K}(A) is explicitly described. The automorphism group of the algebra A A is finite iff the algebra A A is not isomorphic to a monomial algebra, and in this case | Aut K ​ ( A ) | < dim K ​ ( A / 𝔠 A ) |{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)|<{\rm dim}_{K}(A/\mathfrak{c}_{A}) where 𝔠 A \mathfrak{c}_{A} is the conductor of A A . The set of orders of the automorphism groups of the algebras in 𝒜 ⁡ ( m , Γ ) {\cal A}(m,\Gamma) is explicitly described. Key Words: an algebraic curve, a singularity, normalization, an algebraic variety, the algebra of regular functions on an algebraic variety, moduli space, automorphism, isomorphism, generators and defining relations. Corollary 3.6

1.

Suppose that

and

(i.e. the algebra
A\in{\cal A}(m)

|{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)|\leq\begin{cases}m-3&\text{if }4\leq m\text{ is even},\\
m-4&\text{if }5\leq m\text{ is odd}.\\
\end{cases}

(a)

If char
(K)=0

(b)

If char
(K)=p>0

|{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)|\leq\begin{cases}\max\{i_{p}\,|\,1\leq i\leq m-3\}&\text{if }4\leq m\text{ is even},\\

\end{cases}

i_{p}
is the
p
-co-prime divisor of the natural number
i

(a)

\end{cases}

(m-3)
’rd root of unity,

is the
p
-co-prime divisor of

if

(b)

Remark.

in
{\cal A}(m)

in
\mathbb{S}(m)
(which is a purely combinatorial discrete object).

Proof
. Let
R
be the RHS of the equality in the theorem.

(i)
For all
\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}(m)
,
\mathbb{O}(m,\Gamma)\subseteq\begin{cases}\mathbb{L}(m,\Gamma)&\text{if char}(K)=0,\\
\mathbb{L}_{p}(m,\Gamma)&\text{if char}(K)=p>0.\\
\end{cases}

In particular,

\mathbb{O}(m)\subseteq R
:

Given an algebra
A\in{\cal A}(m)
with finite automorphism group
G={\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
. By Theorem
3.4

|G|=\begin{cases}{\rm gcd}(\overline{A})&\text{if char}(K)=0,\\
{\rm gcd}_{p}(\overline{A})&\text{if char}(K)=p>0.\\
\end{cases}

Let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{A}
and
\{1,f_{\gamma}=x^{\gamma}u_{\gamma}\,|\,\gamma\in\Gamma\}

\overline{A}
where
u_{\gamma}=1+\sum_{\delta\in C\Gamma(\gamma)}\lambda_{\gamma\delta}x^{\delta-\gamma}

(by Corollary

). Hence,

for some natural number

\mathbb{L}_{p}(m,\Gamma)&\text{if char}(K)=p>0,\\

(ii)

.(2) (resp., Proposition

\{1,f_{i}=x^{n(i)}(1+x^{l(i)}),x^{jn(i)}\,|\,2\leq j<\frac{m-1}{n(i)}\}
is the canonical basis of the algebra
\overline{A}_{i}
. Hence,
{\rm gcd}(A)=l(i)
, and statement 2 follows from Theorem
3.4
.(3).
\Box

By (
32
) and Lemma
3.13
, if char
(K)=0
then

\mathbb{L}(m)\supseteq\Bigg\{l(i)\,|\,2\leq i\leq\frac{1}{2}(m-1),\;i|m-1,\;n(i)\geq 2,\;\;n(i)\nmid m-1\;{\rm and}\;n(i)\nmid m-2\Bigg\}

(33)

where
l(i)=\frac{i-1}{i}(m-1)
and
n(i)=\frac{m-1}{i}-1
; and if char
(K)=p>0
then

\mathbb{L}(m)\supseteq\Bigg\{l(i)_{p}\,|\,2\leq i\leq\frac{1}{2}(m-1),\;i|m-1,\;n(i)\geq 2,\;n(i)\nmid m-1\;{\rm and}\;n(i)\nmid m-2\Bigg\}.

(34)

4
Generators and defining relations for the algebra
{\cal O}({\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
of regular functions on the algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)

In this section, the field
K
is an arbitrary field, i.e. not necessarily algebraically closed (unless it is not stated otherwise)
.

The aim of the section is to prove Lemma
4.1
, Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.3
which show that the set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is an affine algebraic variety and gives explicit sets of generators and defining relations for the algebra
{\cal O}({\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
of regular functions on
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
.

The action of the algebraic torus
\mathbb{T}
on
{\cal A}(m)
.
The algebraic torus
\mathbb{T}=\{t_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\in K^{\times}\}
acts on the set
{\cal A}(m)
by the rule:

\mathbb{T}\times{\cal A}(m)\rightarrow{\cal A}(m),\;\;(t_{\lambda},A)\mapsto t_{\lambda}(A).

The subsets
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
of
{\cal A}(m)
, where
\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}(m)
, are
\mathbb{T}
-stable (that is
\mathbb{T}{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)={\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
). By Theorem
3.1
.(1),
the set of
\mathbb{T}
-orbits of

{\cal A}(m)
,

\mathbb{A}(m):={\cal A}(m)/\mathbb{T}=\{\mathbb{T}A\,|\,A\in{\cal A}(m)\},

(35)

is the set of isomorphism classes of algebras

{\cal A}(m)
. By (
4
),

\mathbb{A}(m)=\coprod_{\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}(m)}\mathbb{A}(m,\Gamma)\;\;{\rm where}\;\;\mathbb{A}(m,\Gamma):={\cal A}(m,\Gamma)/\mathbb{T}

(36)

is the set of isomorphism classes of algebras in
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
.

The set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is an affine algebraic variety.
Let
A\in{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
.
Recall that
{\rm ind}(\Gamma)=\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{s}\}
and
f_{\nu_{i}}=x^{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}x^{j}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
.
The coefficients
\{\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}\,|\,i=1,\ldots,s;j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})\}
uniquely determined the algebra
A
. We treat them as regular functions on the algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
.

Lemma 4.1

1.

Suppose that
|{\rm ind}(\Gamma)|=1
, i.e.
{\rm ind}(\Gamma)=\{\nu_{1}\}
. Then the affine algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is isomorphic to the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{|C\Gamma(\nu_{1})|}
.

2.

Suppose that
s=|{\rm ind}(\Gamma)|\geq 2
and
{\rm dec}(\Gamma)_{\geq 2}=\emptyset
. Then the affine algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is isomorphic to the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{|C\Gamma(\nu_{1})|+\cdots+|C\Gamma(\nu_{s})|}
where
{\rm ind}(\Gamma)=\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{s}\}
.

Proof
. 1. Statement 1 follows from Lemma
2.14
.

2. Statement 2 follows from Corollary
2.16
.
\Box

So, it remains to consider the case when
s=|{\rm ind}(\Gamma)|\geq 2
and
{\rm dec}(\Gamma)_{\geq 2}\neq\emptyset
. The algebra of differential operators
{\cal D}(K[x])
on the polynomial algebra
K[x]
is equal to

{\cal D}(K[x])=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}K[x]\partial^{[n]}\;\;{\rm where}\;\;\partial^{[n]}:=\frac{\partial^{n}}{n!},\;\;\partial:=\frac{d}{dx}

and the action of the differential operators
\partial^{[n]}
on the polynomial algebra
K[x]
is given by the rule

\partial^{[n]}(x^{m})=\begin{cases}{m\choose n}x^{m-n}&\text{if }m\geq n,\\
0&\text{if }m<n.\\
\end{cases}

If the field
K
has characteristic zero then the algebra
{\cal D}(K[x])
is generated by the elements
x
and
\partial
that satisfy the defining relation
\partial x-x\partial=1
, and the algebra
{\cal D}(K[x])
is called the
Weyl algebra
.

Recall that for a polynomial
f\in K[x]
, we denoted by
c_{j}(f)
the coefficient of
x^{j}
. Clearly,

c_{j}(f)=\partial^{[j]}(f)|_{x=0}.

(37)

Recall that
A\in{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
;
s=|{\rm ind}(\Gamma)|\geq 2
and
{\rm dec}(\Gamma)_{\geq 2}\neq\emptyset
; for each element
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we fixed an element
a(\gamma)
, see (
12
);
{\rm ind}(\Gamma)=\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{s}\}
and
f_{\nu_{i}}=x^{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}x^{j}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. The elements
\{1,f^{a(\gamma)}\,|\,\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is a
K
-basis of the algebra
\overline{A}
.
For all
i=1,\ldots,s
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have the equality in the algebra
\overline{A}
,

f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}=f^{a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)}+\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma(\nu_{i}+\gamma)}\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}f^{a(\delta)},

(38)

where for each
i=1,\ldots,s
, the coefficients

\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma),\delta}
,
\delta\in\Gamma
, are the
unique solution
of the uni-triangular system of
|\Gamma|
linear equations
(the
|\Gamma|\times|\Gamma|
matrix of which has diagonal elements that are equal to 1):

\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}+\sum_{\delta^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\nu_{i}+\gamma,\delta)}\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta^{\prime}}c_{\delta}\Big(f^{a(\delta^{\prime})}\Big)=c_{\delta}\Big(f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}-f^{a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)}\Big),\;\;\gamma\in\Gamma.

(39)

Each element
\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}
,
\delta\in\Gamma
, is an
explicit polynomial
in the variables
\{\lambda_{\nu_{j},\gamma^{\prime}}\,|\,1\leq j\leq s,\;\gamma^{\prime}\in C\Gamma(\nu_{j}),\;\gamma^{\prime}\leq\delta\}
.
The elements
\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}
,
\delta\in\Gamma
, can be found recursively using (
39
).

If
a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)=e_{i}+a(\gamma)
then the equality (
38
) is the tautology
f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}=f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}
, i.e. all the coefficients

\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}
are equal to zero, and vice versa. In particular, if
\nu_{i}+\gamma\not\in{\rm dec}_{\geq 2}(\Gamma)
then
a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)=e_{i}+a(\gamma)
.

Theorem
4.2
shows that the
set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is an affine algebraic variety and gives an explicit set of its defining equations (see also Theorem
4.3
for another approach).

Theorem 4.2

We keep the notation as above (recall that for each element
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we fix an element
a(\gamma)
, see (
12
)). Suppose that
s=|{\rm ind}(\Gamma)|\geq 2
and
{\rm dec}(\Gamma)_{\geq 2}\neq\emptyset
.
Then the set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is an affine algebraic variety and the algebra of regular functions on it,
{\cal O}({\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
, is a factor algebra of the polynomial algebra
P(m,\Gamma):=K[\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}\,|\,i=1,\ldots,s;j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})]
in
n(m,\Gamma):=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|C\Gamma(\nu_{i})|
variables
\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}
by the defining relations: For each pair
(\nu_{i},\gamma)\in{\rm ind}(\Gamma)\times\Gamma
such that
\nu_{i}+a(\gamma)\neq a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)
and
j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i}+\gamma)
,

c_{j}\Big(f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}-f^{a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)}-\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma(\nu_{i}+\gamma)}\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}f^{a(\delta)}\Big)=0,\;\;{\rm i.e.}

\partial^{[j]}\Big(f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}-f^{a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)}-\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma(\nu_{i}+\gamma)}\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}f^{a(\delta)}\Big)|_{x=0}=0

where the polynomials
\eta_{\nu_{i},a(\gamma);\delta}
are defined in (
39
).

Proof
. By the very definition, the generators
\{\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}\}
of the algebra
{\cal O}({\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
satisfy the relations of the theorem.

Conversely, suppose that the
scalars

\{\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}\}
are a solution to the system of equations of the theorem. They determine the elements
f_{\nu_{i}}=x^{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}x^{j}
,
i=1,\ldots,s
of the algebra
K[x]/(x^{m})
.
We have to show that the subalgebra

\overline{A}^{\prime}
of
K[x]/(x^{m})
, which is generated by the elements
f_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,f_{\nu_{s}}
, is equal to

V:=K\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}Kf^{a(\gamma)}

(since then the subalgebra
A^{\prime}
of
K[x]
, which is generated by the elements

f_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,f_{\nu_{s}}
and the ideal
(x^{m})
of
K[x]
, would belong to
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
.

Clearly,
V\subseteq\overline{A}^{\prime}
(by the definition of the elements
f_{\nu_{i}}
). The defining relations of the theorem mean that the equality (
38
) holds for all pairs
(\nu_{i},\gamma)\in{\rm ind}(\Gamma)\times\Gamma
such that
\nu_{i}+a(\gamma)\neq a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)
. But for all pairs
(\nu_{i},\gamma)\in{\rm ind}(\Gamma)\times\Gamma
such that
a(\nu_{i}+\gamma)=\nu_{i}+a(\gamma)
the equality (
38
) holds automatically, it is simply the tautology
f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}=f_{\nu_{i}}f^{a(\gamma)}
. So, the equality (
38
) holds for all elements
(\nu_{i},\gamma)\in{\rm ind}(\Gamma)\times\Gamma
, i.e.
\overline{A}^{\prime}=V
, as required.
\Box

Let
\overline{A}^{\prime}
be a subalgebra of
K[x]/(x^{m})
which is generated by the elements
f_{\nu_{i}}=x^{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}x^{j}
,
i=1,\ldots,s
, where we treat the coefficients
\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}
as independent parameters (indeterminates). For each
\gamma\in{\rm dec}_{\geq 2}(\Gamma)
and
b\in{\rm Rel}(\gamma)\backslash\{a(\gamma)\}
, consider the equation (see Theorem
2.11
),

f^{b}=f^{a(\gamma)}+\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\gamma)}\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};b}f^{a(\gamma^{\prime})}.

The coefficients
\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};b}
are a
unique solution
to the uni-triangular system of
|\Gamma(\gamma)|
linear equations (the
|\Gamma(\gamma)|\times|\Gamma(\gamma)|
matrix of which has diagonal elements that are equal to 1):

\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};b}+\sum_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})}\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime\prime};b}\,c_{\gamma^{\prime}}\Big(f^{a(\gamma^{\prime\prime})}\Big)=c_{\gamma^{\prime}}\Big(f^{b}-f^{a(\gamma)}\Big)

(40)

where
\Gamma(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}):=\{\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma\,|\,\gamma<\gamma^{\prime\prime}<\gamma^{\prime}\}
. Each element
\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};b}
,
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\gamma)
, is an
explicit polynomial
in the variables
\{\lambda_{\nu_{j},\delta}\,|\,1\leq j\leq s,\;\delta\in C\Gamma(\nu_{j}),\;\delta\leq\gamma^{\prime}\}
. Theorem
4.3
also shows that the
set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is an affine algebraic variety and gives an explicit set of its defining equations. It also gives the lower bound for the dimension of the algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
.

Theorem 4.3

We keep the notation as above. Suppose that
s=|{\rm ind}(\Gamma)|\geq 2
and
{\rm dec}(\Gamma)_{\geq 2}\neq\emptyset
.
The set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is an affine algebraic variety, the algebra of regular functions on it,
{\cal O}({\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
, is a factor algebra of the polynomial algebra
P(m,\Gamma)=K[\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}\,|\,i=1,\ldots,s;j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})]
in
n(m,\Gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|C\Gamma(\nu_{i})|
variables
\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}
by the defining relations: For each
\gamma\in{\rm dec}_{\geq 2}(\Gamma)
and
b\in{\rm Rel}(\gamma)\backslash\{a(\gamma)\}
,

c_{j}(f^{b})=c_{j}(f^{a(\gamma)})+\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\gamma)}\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};b}\,c_{j}(f^{a(\gamma^{\prime})}),\;\;j\in C\Gamma(\gamma),

where the polynomials
\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};b}
are defined in (
40
).
The number of equations is

l(m,\Gamma):=\sum_{\gamma\in{\rm dec}_{\geq 2}(\Gamma)}\Big(|{\rm Rel}(\gamma)|-1\Big)\cdot|C\Gamma(\gamma)|.

In particular, the dimension of the variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is not smaller than
n(m,\Gamma)-l(m,\Gamma)
.

Proof
. By Theorem
2.11
, the generators
\{\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}\}
of the algebra
{\cal O}({\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
satisfy the relations of the theorem.

Conversely, suppose that the
scalars

\{\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}\}
are a solution to the equations of the theorem. They determine the elements
f_{\nu_{i}}=x^{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}x^{j}
,
i=1,\ldots,s
of the algebra
K[x]/(x^{m})
.
We have to show that the subalgebra

\overline{A}^{\prime}
of
K[x]/(x^{m})
, which is generated by the elements
f_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,f_{\nu_{s}}
, is equal to

V:=K\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}Kf^{a(\gamma)}

(since then the subalgebra
A^{\prime}
of
K[x]
, which is generated by the elements

f_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,f_{\nu_{s}}
and the ideal
(x^{m})
of
K[x]
, would belong to
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
.

Clearly,
V\subseteq\overline{A}^{\prime}
. On the other hand, the defining relations of the theorem and (
40
) mean that for each
\gamma\in{\rm dec}_{\geq 2}(\Gamma)
and
b\in{\rm Rel}(\gamma)\backslash\{a(\gamma)\}
,

f^{b}=f^{a(\gamma)}+\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\gamma)}\theta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};b}\,f^{a(\gamma^{\prime})}.

Now, by (
20
),
\overline{A}^{\prime}=K\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}Kf^{a(\gamma)}
.
\Box

Corollary 4.4

The set of irreducible components of the affine algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m)
is the union of irreducible components of the affine algebraic varieties
\{{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)\,|\,\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}(m)\}
.

Proof
. The affine algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m)=\coprod_{\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}(m)}{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
is a finite disjoint union of its closed subsets
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
(Theorem
4.2
or Theorem
4.3
) and the statement follows.
\Box

Recall that
f_{\nu_{i}}=x^{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}x^{j}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
, and the algebraic group
\mathbb{T}
acts on the algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
. The action of the algebraic group on the algebra

{\cal O}({\cal A}(m,\Gamma))
is given by the rule: For all
i=1,\ldots,s
,
j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})
and
\lambda\in K^{\times}
,

t_{\lambda}(\lambda_{\nu_{i},j})=\lambda^{-\nu_{i}+j}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}

(41)

since
t_{\lambda}(f_{\nu_{i}})=\lambda^{\nu_{i}}\Big(x^{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\lambda^{-\nu_{i}+j}\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}x^{j-\nu_{i}}\Big)
.

Recall that for each primitive
n
’th root of unity
\lambda_{n}
, the group
\mathbb{T}
contains the cyclic group of order
n
,
C_{n}=\langle t_{\lambda_{n}}\rangle
. The groups
\{C_{n}\}
are all the finite subgroups of
\mathbb{T}
.

Corollary 4.5

(
K
is an algebraically closed field). Given
n\in\mathbb{O}(m,\Gamma)
.

1.

The set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{n}}
of fixed points of the group
C_{n}=\langle t_{\lambda_{n}}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{T}
(where
\lambda_{n}
is a primitive
n
’th root of unity) is a non-empty closed subvariety of the affine algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
the defining equations of which are

\lambda_{\nu_{i},j}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
and
j\in C\Gamma(\nu_{i})
such that
n\nmid j-\nu_{i}
. The set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{n}}
contains precisely all the algebras
A
in
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
with
C_{n}\subseteq{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
.

2.

The set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{n}}/\mathbb{T}
is a set of isomorphism classes of algebras
A
in
\mathbb{A}(m,\Gamma)
with
C_{n}\subseteq{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
. If
n|n^{\prime}
then
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{n}}/\mathbb{T}\supseteq{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{n^{\prime}}}/\mathbb{T}
.

3.

The set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{n}}/\mathbb{T}\backslash\bigcup_{n\neq l\in\mathbb{O}(m,\Gamma),n|l}{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{l}}/\mathbb{T}
is a set of isomorphism classes of algebras
A
in
\mathbb{A}(m,\Gamma)
with
C_{n}={\rm Aut}_{K}(A)
.

4.

The set
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)/\mathbb{T}\backslash\Big\{\{A_{mon}(\Gamma)\}\cup\bigcup_{1\neq l\in\mathbb{O}(m,\Gamma)}{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{C_{l}}/\mathbb{T}\Big\}
is a set of isomorphism classes of algebras
A
in
\mathbb{A}(m,\Gamma)
with
{\rm Aut}_{K}(A)=\{e\}
. It is an open set of the algebraic variety
{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)
.

5.

{\cal A}(m,\Gamma)^{\mathbb{T}}=\{A_{mon}(\Gamma)\}
.

Proof
. 1. Statement 1 follows at once from
(
41
).

2. Statement 2 follows from statement 1.

3. Statement 3 follows from statement 2.

5. Statement 5 is obvious.

4. Statement 4 follows from statements 1, 3 and 5.
\Box

The CO-to-H 2 Conversion Factor in the Central Molecular Zone of the Milky Way using CO isotopologues
Mikito Kohno ∗, Yoshiaki Sofue

Abstract
We performed correlation analyses between the 12 CO and 13 CO J = J= 1-0 line intensities in order to derive the variability of the CO-to-H 2 conversion factor ( X CO , iso X_{\rm CO,{iso}} ) in the central molecular zone (CMZ) of our Galaxy. New high-resolution X CO , iso X_{\rm CO,{iso}} maps at a resolution of ∼ \timeform ​ 30 ​ " \sim\timeform{30"} and the longitude-velocity diagram (LVD) at resolution ∼ \timeform ​ 30 ​ " × 2 \sim\timeform{30"}\times 2 km s -1 are presented using the 12 CO and 13 CO archival survey data obtained by the Nobeyama 45 m telescope. We revealed the variation of X CO , iso X_{\rm CO,{iso}} in the CMZ within the range of X CO , iso ∼ ( 0.2 − 1.3 ) × 10 20 ​ cm − 2 ​ ( K ​ km ​ s − 1 ) − 1 X_{\rm CO,{iso}}\sim(0.2-1.3)\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}\ (K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}} , if we assume the normalization value of 0.59 × 10 20 ​ cm − 2 ​ ( K ​ km ​ s − 1 ) − 1 0.59\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}\ (K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}} . The mean value is obtained as X CO , iso = ( 0.48 ± 0.15 ) × 10 20 ​ cm − 2 ​ ( K ​ km ​ s − 1 ) − 1 X_{\rm CO,{iso}}=(0.48\pm 0.15)\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}\ (K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}} in the CMZ of our Galaxy.

1
Introduction

The Central Molecular Zone (hereafter CMZ) is the concentration of molecular gas within
\sim 300
pc in the Galactic Center (GC:
[
15
,
8
]
).
The total molecular mass in the CMZ has been derived to be
\sim 2.3\times 10^{7}\ M_{\odot}
assuming the CO-to-H
2
conversion factor of
X_{\rm CO}=0.51\times 10^{20}
cm
-2
(K km s
-1
)
-1

(
Sofue, 2022
)
, but it directly depends on the assumed value of
X_{\rm CO}
, which is still uncertain in the GC
(
Dahmen et al., 1998
)
.
Hence, the mass, the most fundamental parameter of the GC, is still uncertain by a factor of
\sim 2
to 4.
The conversion factor is defined by

X_{\rm CO}={N({\rm H_{2}})_{\rm 12X}\over I_{\rm{}^{12}CO}}\ {\rm[cm^{-2}\ (K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}]},

(1)

where
N({\rm H_{2}})_{\rm 12X}
is the H
2
column density and
I_{\rm{}^{12}CO}
is the integrated intensity of the
12
CO
J=
1-0 line. The standard value of the
X_{\rm CO}
factor is
2\times 10^{20}

{\rm cm^{-2}(K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}}
with the uncertainty of
\pm 30\%
in the Galactic disk
(
Bolatto et al., 2013
)
.

In the GC,
X_{\rm CO}
is suggested to be significantly smaller than the local value due to the higher metallicity
(
Arimoto et al., 1996
)
, decreasing by a factor of
\sim 3
–10
(
Bolatto et al., 2013
)
.
Lower
X_{\rm CO}
factors have been also pointed out comparison of the CO luminosity with the
\gamma
ray brightness
(
Blitz et al., 1985
;
Strong et al., 2004
)
, analysis of the infrared dust emission
(
Sodroski et al., 1995
;
Schultheis et al., 2014
)
, and Virial mass
(
Oka et al., 1998a
)
.

Recently, Sofue & Kohno (2020, hereafter: Paper I) and Kohno & Sofue (2024, hereafter: Paper II) revealed the variability of the
X_{\rm CO}
factor in the Galactic giant molecular clouds (GMC). These papers present the high-resolution (
\sim 15\arcsec
)
X_{\rm CO}
maps using CO isotopologues obtained by the FUGIN CO survey data
(
Umemoto et al., 2017
;
Torii et al., 2019
)
.

In this paper, we aim first at deriving the
X_{\rm CO}
variability in the GC at higher accuracy and at presenting detailed distributions of
X_{\rm CO}
in longitude-latitude

(l,b)
and longitude-velocity
(l,V_{\rm LSR})
planes at high angular and velocity resolutions using the CO-line data from the Nobeyama 45-m radio telescope.

3
Methods

In this study, we calculated the column densities of molecular hydrogen using the integrated intensity of
12
CO and
13
CO
J=
1-0. The H
2
column density is taken from the
12
CO integrated intensity by assuming the
X_{\rm CO}
factor from the equation (
1
).
We used the conversion factor of
{X_{\rm CO}}=0.59\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}\ (K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}}

(
Arimoto et al., 1996
)
currently obtained for the Galactic Center region as the normalization value in this article.
This value has been obtained by extrapolating the
X_{\rm CO}
values determined in the disc to the GC by assuming a scale radius of the Galactic disc,
r_{e}=6.2
kpc, and the distance of the GC from the Sun,
R_{0}=8.0
kpc
(
Reid et al., 2019
;
VERA Collaboration et al., 2020
)
.

Independent of the conversion factor, we derived the H
2
column density in the CMZ by the local thermal equilibrium (LTE) method described in Paper I, II, and
Pineda et al. (2008)
.
The brightness temperature (
T_{\rm B}
) of the CO line intensity with the excitation temperature (
T_{\rm ex}
) and the optical depth (
\tau
) is given by

T_{\rm B}=T_{0}\left(\frac{1}{e^{T_{0}/T_{\rm ex}}-1}-\frac{1}{e^{T_{0}/T_{\rm bg}}-1}\right)\left(1-e^{-\tau}\right)\ [{\rm K}],

(2)

where
T_{\rm bg}=2.73
K is the temperature of the cosmic-microwave background radiation.

T_{0}=h\nu/k
is the Planck temperature with
h
,
\nu
, and
k
being the Planck constant, rest frequency, and Boltzman constant, respectively.

If we assume that the
12
CO line is optically thick, the excitation temperature is given by

T_{\rm ex}=T_{0}^{115}\bigg/{\rm ln}\left(1+\frac{T_{0}^{115}}{T_{\rm B}({\rm{}^{12}CO})_{\rm max}+{0.836}}\right)\ [{\rm K}],

(3)

where
T_{\rm B}({\rm{}^{12}CO})_{\rm max}
and
T_{0}^{115}={5.53}
K correspond to the
12
CO peak brightness temperature and the Planck temperature at the rest frequency of
12
CO
J=
1-0, respectively. In the longitude-latitude (
l-b
) and longitude-velocity (
l-v
) space,
T_{\rm ex}
at each pixel is calculated using the peak value along the velocity-axis and latitude-axis, respectively. We assume that
T_{\rm ex}
is equal in the
12
CO and
13
CO line emissions, and express the optical depth as

\tau(^{13}{\rm CO})=-{\rm ln}\left(1-\frac{T_{\rm B}({\rm{}^{13}CO})_{\rm max}/T_{0}^{110}}{(e^{T_{0}^{110}/T_{\rm ex}}-1)^{-1}-{0.168}}\right),

(4)

where
T_{\rm B}({\rm{}^{13}CO})_{\rm max}
and
T_{0}^{110}={5.29}
K represent the
13
CO peak brightness temperature and the Planck temperature at the rest frequency of
13
CO
J=
1-0, respectively.
The
13
CO column density is given by

N_{\rm{}^{13}CO}=3.0\times 10^{14}~\frac{\tau}{1-e^{-\tau}}\frac{I_{\rm{}^{13}CO}}{1-e^{-T_{0}^{110}/T_{\rm ex}}}\ [{\rm cm^{-2}}],

(5)

where
I_{\rm{}^{13}CO}
is the
13
CO integrated intensity.
Then, we convert
N_{\rm{}^{13}CO}
to the H
2
column density using the abundance ratio of H
2
to
13
CO molecules given by

N({\rm H_{2}})_{\rm 13L}=Y_{\rm GC^{13}CO}N_{\rm{}^{13}CO}\ [{\rm cm^{-2}}].

(6)

Here,
Y_{\rm GC^{13}CO}
is adopted as
\sim 1\times 10^{6}
in Sagittarius B2
(
Lis & Goldsmith, 1989
)
.

5
Discussion

We investigated the radial variation of
X_{\rm CO,iso}
based on the results of Paper II.
Figure
6
shows the plot of
X_{\rm CO,iso}
as a function of the Galactocentric distance.
X_{\rm CO,iso}
of the GMCs in the Galactic disk were obtained by Paper II assuming the normalization value of
X_{\rm CO}=2\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}(K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}}
(
Bolatto et al., 2013
)
.
In this paper, we assumed the normalization in the GC of
X_{\rm CO}=0.59\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}(K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}}
(
Arimoto et al., 1996
)
.
Since the obtained value is proportional to this normalization factor if adopting a value of
2.0\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}(K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}}
of local molecular clouds, the whole results in our analysis must be multiplied by a factor of 3.39.

X_{\rm CO}
of
2.0\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}(K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}}
and
0.59\times 10^{20}\ {\rm cm^{-2}(K\ km\ s^{-1})^{-1}}
, respectively.

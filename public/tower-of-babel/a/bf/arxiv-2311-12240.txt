Sturm–Liouville systems for the survival probability in first-passage time problems
M. Dahlenburg 1,2 and G. Pagnini 1,3

Abstract
We derive a Sturm–Liouville system of equations for the exact calculation of the survival probability in first-passage time problems. This system is the one associated with the Wiener–Hopf integral equation obtained from the theory of random walks. The derived approach is an alternative to the existing literature and we tested it against direct calculations from both discrete- and continuous-time random walks in a manageable, but meaningful, example. Within this framework, the Sparre Andersen theorem results to be a boundary condition for the system.

Introduction

The probability for a random walker started at
a>0
to remain
in the initial half-axis after
n
steps is
called survival probability
[
1
]
.
If we denote it by
\phi_{n}(a)
,
for
z,\xi\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
, it holds

[
2
,
1
]

\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z-\xi)\,\phi_{n}(z)\,dz=\phi_{n+1}(\xi)\,,\quad n\geq 0\,,

(1)

with initial condition
\phi_{0}(\xi)=1
, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
,
where
k(x)
, with
x\in\mathbb{R}
, is the distribution of the
jumps.
A general solution to (
1
) is known in literature
with the name of Pollaczek–Spitzer formula
[
1
]
.
This name refers to a formula,
see
[
2
, theorem 5, formula (4.6)]
and

[
1
, formula (12)]
,
derived by F. Spitzer in 1957
[
2
, theorem 3, formula (3.1)]

on the basis of an auxiliary formula obtained by F. Pollaczek in 1952

[
3
, formula (8)]
but through a different method.
Here we derive the Sturm–Liouville system associated with
(
1
) for the calculation

of the survival probability
\phi_{n+1}(\xi)

as an alternative and
easier approach with respect to the Pollaczek–Spitzer formula.
Thus, first we show that
\phi_{n+1}(\xi)
is indeed
the solution of a differential equation
and later we check this solution against
direct calculations in a manageable, but meaningful,
case within both the framework of discrete-time random walk
and also that of continuous-time random walk (CTRW).

When
a=0
, the survival probability
\phi_{n}(a)
is indeed determined by
the celebrated Sparre Andersen theorem derived on the basis
of combinatorial arguments
[
4
]
.
Within the provided setting, the Sparre Andersen theorem (
2
)
actually is a boundary condition of the Sturm–Liouville system
of equations in order to have a unique and non-decreasing solution

\phi_{n+1}(\xi)
. Sparre Andersen theorem is a fundamental result
in the study of first-passage time problems for
Markovian symmetric random walks with discrete time-step and
starting at the origin.
Originally established in 1954

[
4
]
, the theorem states

\phi_{n}(0)=2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\pi}}\,,\quad n\to\infty\,,

(2)

where
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is the epoch of a one-dimensional
random walk
S_{n}=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}
started at the origin
S_{0}=0

with jumps i.i.d. random variables in
\mathbb{R}

and
\phi_{n}(0)
is
the probability for the first ladder epoch

\{\mathcal{T}=n\}=\{S_{1}\geq 0,\dots,S_{n-1}\geq 0,S_{n}<0\}

to be larger than
n
, i.e.,
\phi_{n}(0)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{T}>n)
.
Thus,
\mathcal{T}
is the epoch of the first entry of the walker
into the strictly negative half-axis.
See also F. Spitzer
[
5
]
and
W. Feller
[
6
, Section XII.7]
.
By adding a constant
a>0
to all terms,
we obtain a random walk starting at
a
.

In opposition to the present approach,
the derivation of the Sparre Andersen theorem
(
2
) from (
1
) in the limit
\xi\to 0
is
possible by taking into account the boundedness of
the survival probability at infinity as a consequence
of its physical interpretation, see, e.g.,

[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
By the way, the authors did not
calculate with that approach also the survival probability

[
7
,

,

]
.

[

Unfortunately, as reported by F. Spitzer in his proof

the so-called Pollaczek–Spitzer formula

[

requires
two conditions. The first condition

The non-decreasing limit function

\phi(\xi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{n}(\xi)}{\phi_{n}(0)}\,,\quad\xi>0\,,

(3)

satisfies the equation

\phi(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z-\xi)\phi(z)\,dz\,,\quad\xi>0\,,

2
The associated Sturm–Liouville system

We are now interested in solving (
1
) with a method that is
different from the Pollaczek–Spitzer formula.
First we observe that
z,\xi\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
while
x=(z-\xi)\in\mathbb{R}

and
\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{R}}k(x)dx=1}
.
Moreover, a noteworthy property of (
2
) is that it is independent
of the distribution of the jumps if a symmetric kernel
k(x)=k(|x|)

is considered.
Therefore, in spite of the fact that the kernel

k(z,\xi)=k(z-\xi)=k(|z-\xi|)

is assumed to be everywhere continuous in
z
,
its derivative with respect to
z

has a jump discontinuity at
z=\xi
, i.e.,

k^{\prime}_{z}(z,\xi)=\frac{dk(|z-\xi|)}{dz}=\sgn(z-\xi)\,\frac{dk(|x|)}{d|x|}\,,

(4)

and we define

-\frac{1}{p(\xi)}=k^{\prime}_{z}(\xi^{+},\xi)-k^{\prime}_{z}(\xi^{-},\xi)\,.

(5)

There are some general classes of boundary value problems
for differential equations that can be reduced to integral equations

[
14
]
.
More specifically, here we consider the reduction of the
Sturm–Liouville systems to
Fredholm integral equations of the second kind. Thus, by following F.G. Tricomi
[
15
, Chapter 3.13]
,
we introduce an operator
\mathcal{L}
such that

\mathcal{L}[k(z,\xi)]=\frac{d}{dz}\left[p(z)\frac{dk}{dz}\right]+q(z)k(z,\xi)=0\,,\quad z\neq\xi\,,

(6)

and

\mathcal{L}[\phi_{n+1}(z)]=\frac{d}{dz}\left[p(z)\frac{d\phi_{n+1}}{dz}\right]+q(z)\phi_{n+1}(z)=-\phi_{n}(z)\,,

(7)

for an appropriate function
q(z)

and for a source term
\phi_{n}(z)
,
together with the corresponding boundary conditions
at
z=0
and
z=\infty
of
k(z,\xi)
,
\phi_{n+1}(z)

and their first derivative.
Since the distribution
k(x)
of the jumps
is assumed to be known,
function
q(z)
is given by the choice of
k(x)
,
and
\phi_{n}(z)
is indeed iteratively determined from
the initial condition
\phi_{0}(z)=1
.
Then, from the subtraction

k(z,\xi)\mathcal{L}[\phi_{n+1}(z)]-\phi_{n+1}(z)\mathcal{L}[k(z,\xi)]=-k(z,\xi)\phi_{n}(z)

we remove
q(z)
and we have the Sturm–Liouville system

\frac{d}{dz}\left[p(z)\frac{d\phi_{n+1}}{dz}\right]=\frac{\phi_{n+1}(z)}{k(z,\xi)}\frac{d}{dz}\left[p(z)\frac{dk}{dz}\right]-\phi_{n}(z)\,.

(8)

We now show that (
8
) is
the Sturm–Liouville system associated with the Wiener–Hopf integral equation
(
1
) for proper boundary conditions.

We start by considering the integral

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z,\xi)\mathcal{L}[\phi_{n+1}(z)]\,dz

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{0}+\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z,\xi)q(z)\phi_{n+1}(z)\,dz\,,

where the second line follows from the LHS of (
7
) with

I_{0}=\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z,\xi)\frac{d}{dz}\left[p(z)\frac{d\phi_{n+1}}{dz}\right]\,dz\,.

(10)

From the RHS of (
7
) we also have that

I=-\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z,\xi)\phi_{n}(z)\,dz\,.

(11)

Integrating by parts
I_{0}
(
10
), it holds

I_{0}=\left.k\,p\,\frac{d\phi_{n+1}}{dz}\right|_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}k^{\prime}_{z}\,p\,\frac{d\phi_{n+1}}{dz}\,dz\,,

(12)

thus, by integrating again by parts the integral in the
RHS of (
12
) and reminding (
6
),
integral (
9
) turns into

I=\left.k\,p\,\frac{d\phi_{n+1}}{dz}\right|_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dz}\left[k^{\prime}_{z}\,p\,\phi_{n+1}\right]\,dz\,.

(13)

Because of jump discontinuity of
k^{\prime}_{z}
(
5
),
the integral term on the RHS of (
13
) gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\xi^{-}}+\int_{\xi^{+}}^{\infty}=\left.\frac{dk}{dz}\,p\,\phi_{n+1}\right|_{0}^{\infty}+\phi_{n+1}(\xi)\,,

(14)

and, finally, by comparing (
13
) and (
11
) we recover
(
1
) provided that

\left[p(z)\left(k(z,\xi)\frac{d\phi_{n+1}}{dz}-\phi_{n+1}(z)\frac{dk}{dz}\right)\right]_{0}^{\infty}=0\,.

(15)

Hence, the kernel
k(z,\xi)
and the survival probability
\phi_{n+1}(z)

which are related by the Wiener–Hopf integral equation (
1
) are indeed
the solutions of equations (
6
) and (
7
), or (
8
),
when boundary conditions (
15
) are met.
We highlight that in (
15
), the kernel
k(z,\xi)
is supposed to be
known from the process and
p(z)
is determined through (
5
).
Thus, boundary conditions (
15
) define indeed the boundary values
of
\phi_{n+1}(z)
and its first derivative.
In the general case when boundary conditions (
15
) are different
from
0
, this procedure reduces differential equations
to Fredholm integral equations of the second kind.
In our case (
15
),
this procedure reduces differential equation (
8
)
to a homogeneous Fredhdolm integral equation of second kind,
that is the Wiener–Hopf integral equation (
1
).

3
An example from the theory of discrete-time random walks

Wiener–Hopf integral equation (
1
)
provides the survival probability in the case of a discrete-time
random walk. In this section,
first we directly solve (
1
) for the generating function
by means of classical methods in complex analysis for the
meaningful and manageable example with
the exponential kernel
k(x)={\rm e}^{-|x|}/2
,
see, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
, and later
we solve the Sturm–Liouville system derived in section
2

for the same special case.

We introduce the generating function

\overline{\phi}(\xi,u)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u^{n}\phi_{n}(\xi)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u^{n}\phi_{n}(\xi)\,,

(16)

which solves the equation

\overline{\phi}(\xi,u)=1+u\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z-\xi)\overline{\phi}(z,u)\,dz\,.

(17)

Equation (
17
) is a Fredholm integral equation
of the second kind with respect to
z
and it can be solved
by methods developed for Wiener–Hopf integral equations

[
20
]
.
Thus, by following Wiener–Hopf technique,
we extend the interval of
\xi
to
\mathbb{R}
and we
refer to
\overline{\phi}_{+}(\xi,u)
and
\overline{\phi}_{-}(\xi,u)

for
positive
and
negative
values of
\xi
, respectively,
such that
\overline{\phi}=\overline{\phi}_{+}+\overline{\phi}_{-}
.
We are interested to find
\overline{\phi}_{+}(\xi,u)
that corresponds
to the physical solution of (
17
) while
\overline{\phi}_{-}(\xi,u)

is an auxiliary function.
We define the generalised Fourier transform with
\omega\in\mathbb{C}

and we have the pairs

\widehat{g}_{\pm}(\omega)=\pm\int_{0}^{\pm\infty}{\rm e}^{i\omega x}g_{\pm}(x)\,dx\,,

(18)

g_{\pm}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{L_{\pm}}{\rm e}^{-i\omega x}\widehat{g}_{\pm}(\omega)\,d\omega\,,

(19)

where
L_{\pm}
are proper integration paths in the complex plane.
By inverting the Fourier transform,
we explicitly obtain the generating function as follows

[
14
]

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{+}(\xi,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{L_{+}}{\rm e}^{-i\omega\xi}\,\widehat{\overline{\phi}}_{+}(\omega,u)\,d\omega\,,

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm Res}\left\{\frac{C\,\overline{\phi}_{-}(0,u)(i+\omega)\,{\rm e}^{-i\omega\xi}}{1+\omega^{2}-u}-\frac{(1+\omega^{2})\,{\rm e}^{-i\omega\xi}}{\omega\left(1+\omega^{2}-u\right)}\right\}\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-C\overline{\phi}_{-}(0,u)}{2}\left\{\frac{(\sqrt{1-u}-1)\exp(-\sqrt{1-u}\,\xi)}{\sqrt{1-u}}+\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-u})\exp(\sqrt{1-u}\,\xi)}{\sqrt{1-u}}\right\}+

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}+\frac{u\,\exp(-\sqrt{1-u}\,\xi)}{2(u-1)}+\frac{u\,\exp(\sqrt{1-u}\,\xi)}{2(u-1)}\,.

where
C
is a multiplicative constant that appears
in the solution of the homogeneous case.
Since the generating function is bounded because of its
physical meaning, i.e.,

\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\overline{\phi}_{+}(\xi,u)=\overline{\phi}_{\infty}(u)<\infty\,,

(21)

then from (
20
)
we have that the exponential term
\exp(\sqrt{1-u}\,\xi)
must disappear,
which gives

C\,\overline{\phi}_{-}(0,u)=\frac{\sqrt{1-u}-1}{\sqrt{1-u}}\,.

(22)

Finally, by plugging (
22
) in (
20
)
we obtain the unique generating function of the survival probability
for a discrete-time random walk in continuous space with
exponential jump distribution, which is

\overline{\phi}_{+}(\xi,u)=\frac{\sqrt{1-u}-1}{1-u}\exp(-\sqrt{1-u}\,\xi)+\frac{1}{1-u}\,,

(23)

that is consistent with formula (32) in reference

[
18
]
.
From formula (
23
)
we also have the boundary conditions:

\overline{\phi}_{+}(0,u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u}}\,,\quad\overline{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(0,u)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-u}}{\sqrt{1-u}}\,,

(24)

where
\overline{\phi}_{+}(0,u)

is in agreement with the Sparre Anderson theorem, and

\overline{\phi}_{\infty}(u)=\frac{1}{1-u}\,.

(25)

We show now the effectiveness of the discussed approach
based on the Sturm–Liouville systems in the same special case.
If we consider the special case
k(x)={\rm e}^{-|x|}/2
,
see, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
,
then from (
4
)
we have that
k^{\prime}_{z}(z,\xi)=-\sgn(z-\xi)\,{\rm e}^{-|z-\xi|}/2

and from (
5
) that

p(z)=1
, with
z\neq 0
.
Therefore,
by solving (
6
) with respect to
q(z)

in all points except
z=\xi

it results
q(z)=-1
.
This means that the survival probability
\phi_{n+1}(z)

from (
7
) is the solution of the equation

\frac{d^{2}\phi_{n+1}}{dz^{2}}-\phi_{n+1}(z)=-\phi_{n}(z)\,,

(26)

and the Sparre Andersen theorem (
2
) is a required boundary condition
in analogy with the literature studies
reported in Section
1
.
We introduce the generating function

\overline{\phi}(z,u)
, see definition in (
16
),
then from (
26
) we have that it solves the equation

\frac{d^{2}\overline{\phi}(z,u)}{dz^{2}}-\overline{\phi}(z,u)+1=-u\,\overline{\phi}(z,u)\,.

(27)

By applying the Laplace transform with respect to
z
,
where
\widetilde{\overline{\phi}}(s,u)
stands for the Laplace transform
of
\overline{\phi}(z,u)
with
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
formula (
27
) reads

s^{2}\,\widetilde{\overline{\phi}}(s,u)-s\overline{\phi}(0,u)-\overline{\phi}^{\prime}(0,u)-\widetilde{\overline{\phi}}(s,u)+\frac{1}{s}=-u\,\widetilde{\overline{\phi}}(s,u)\,,

(28)

such that

\widetilde{\overline{\phi}}(s,u)=\frac{s^{2}\,\overline{\phi}(0,u)+s\,\overline{\phi}^{\prime}(0,u)-1}{s(s^{2}+u-1)}\,.

(29)

By taking boundary conditions (
24
),
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\overline{\phi}}(s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}+s(1-\sqrt{1-u)}-\sqrt{1-u}}{s\,\sqrt{1-u}\,(s^{2}+u-1)}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s(s+1)-(s+1)\sqrt{1-u)}}{s\,\sqrt{1-u}\,(s^{2}+u-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s(s+1)-(s+1)\sqrt{1-u)}}{s\,\sqrt{1-u}\,(s+\sqrt{1-u})(s-\sqrt{1-u})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s+1}{s\,\sqrt{1-u}\,(s+\sqrt{1-u})}\,,

from which we recover formula (
23
)
after the Laplace inversion.

4
An example from the theory of the CTRW

We discuss now the feasibility
of the proposed approach based on the Sturm–Liouville system
through the same example but from the theory of the CTRW.
To this aim, we introduce the time variable
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

and random waiting-times between two consecutive jumps
\tau_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
,
with
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
such that if the process starts at
t=0
it holds

t=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}
.
The waiting-times
\tau_{j}
are assumed to be
i.i.d. random variables distributed according to
a density function
\psi(t)
.
Moreover, we introduce also the probability that a
given waiting interval between two consecutive jumps
is greater than or equal to
t
,
namely
\displaystyle{\Psi(t)=1-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(\tau)d\tau}
.
In this continuous-time setting,
we replace the notation of the survival probability
in discrete epochs
\phi_{n}(\xi)
with
the new notation
\Lambda(\xi,t)
and now (
1
) reads

[
19
, formula (16)]

\widetilde{\Lambda}(\xi,s)=\widetilde{\Psi}(s)+\widetilde{\psi}(s)\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z-\xi)\widetilde{\Lambda}(z,s)\,dz\,,

(31)

where
\widetilde{\Lambda}(\xi,s)
stands for the Laplace transform
of
\Lambda(\xi,t)
with
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
and it holds
s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)=1-\widetilde{\psi}(s)
. After Laplace inversion
and by setting
t=n\Delta t
and
\psi(\tau)=\delta(\tau-\Delta t)
,
then
\Psi(0)=1
and

\displaystyle{\Psi(t\geq\Delta t)=1-\int_{0}^{t}\delta(\tau-\Delta t)d\tau=0}
,
such that from (
31
) we recover (
1
) as expected.

Equation (
31
) is again a Fredholm integral equation
of the second kind with respect to
z
and it can be solved
by methods developed for Wiener–Hopf integral equations

[
20
,
14
]
.
Thus, by following Wiener–Hopf technique,
we extend the interval of
\xi
to
\mathbb{R}
and we
refer to
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(\xi,s)

for
positive
and
negative
values of
\xi
, respectively,
such that
\widetilde{\Lambda}=\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}+\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}
.
We are interested to find
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)
that corresponds
to the physical solution of (
31
) while
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(\xi,s)

is an auxiliary function. Therefore, we study the following equation

\widetilde{\Lambda}(\xi,s)=\widetilde{\Psi}(s)+\widetilde{\psi}(s)\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z-\xi)\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)\,dz\,,

(32)

with
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(\xi,s)=\widetilde{\psi}(s)\int_{0}^{\infty}k(z-\xi)\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)dz
.

We consider now, as a meaningful and manageable example,
the exponential kernel
k(x)={\rm e}^{-|x|}/2
, again,
see, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
By applying the Fourier transform (
18
) to (
32
) we have

\widehat{\widetilde{\Lambda}}_{+}(\omega,s)=-\frac{\omega(1-i\omega)\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(0,s)+i(1+\omega^{2})\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}{\omega(s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)+\omega^{2})}\,,

(33)

where by exploiting the exponential kernel we have

\widehat{k}(\omega)=1/(1+\omega^{2})
and from
the definition of
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(\xi,s)

it results
\widehat{\widetilde{\Lambda}}_{-}(\omega,s)=\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(0,s)/(1+i\omega)
.
We can invert (
33
)
through (
19
) by using the residue theorem at the
simple poles

\omega=0
,
\omega=i\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}
and
\omega=-i\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}
.
Finally, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)=\frac{1}{s}-\left[\frac{\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}(\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}+1)}{2s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(0,s)+\frac{1-s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}{2s}\right]{\rm e}^{\xi\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}}

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}(\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}-1)}{2s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(0,s)+\frac{1-s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}{2s}\right]{\rm e}^{-\xi\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}}\,.

(34)

Hence, the survival probability
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)
cannot be
determined form (
34
), and consequently from
(
31
) and (
32
), because
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(0,s)

is unknown.

From the theory of CTRW we also have
[
21
]

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)=\widetilde{\Psi}(s)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(\xi)\,\left[\widetilde{\psi}(s)\right]^{n}\,,

(35)

where now
n
counts the number of occurred jumps.
If we set
\xi=0
in (
35
),
then from (
2
) we can have
\phi_{n}(0)
.
Since (
2
) holds for Markovian random walks,
in the framework of CTRW we have that the process is Markovian
when
[
22
,
23
]

\psi(\tau)=\psi_{\rm{M}}(\tau)={\rm e}^{-\tau}\,,\quad\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)=\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)=\frac{1}{1+s}\,,

(36)

and it holds

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(0,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(0)\left[\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)\right]^{n}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}{\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s(1+s)}}\,.

In the long-time limit, i.e.,
s\to 0
, we have

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(0,s)\sim 1/\sqrt{s}
that after Laplace inversion gives

\Lambda_{+}(0,t)\sim 1/\sqrt{t}
for
t\to+\infty

[
24
]
.
The above limit is the continuous-time counter-part of (
2
).
This limit can be indeed obtained from (
37
)
with any distribution
\psi(\tau)
such that

\widetilde{\psi}(s)\sim 1-s
for
s\to 0
, that is a weak rearrangement of
Markovianity in the sense of the existence of a
finite time-scale that gives a finite mean for the waiting-times.
Finally, by equalling (
34
) with
\xi=0

and (
37
) we can determine
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(0,s)
as follows

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{-}(0,s)=\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)\,\frac{\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}-1}{\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}}=\frac{\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{1+s}}{\sqrt{s}(1+s)}\,.

(38)

We can now complete the calculation of
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)
in
the Markovian case that results to be

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)=\frac{1}{s}\left\{1-\left[1-\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}\right]{\rm e}^{-\xi\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}}\,\right\}\,,

(39)

with

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(0,s)=\frac{\sqrt{1-\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}}{s}\,,\quad\widetilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{+}(0,s)=\frac{\sqrt{1-\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}-1+\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}{s}\,,

(40)

and it is in agreement with a previous derivation on the basis of
probabilistic arguments
[
17
]
.
In fact, by introducing the quantity

\widetilde{\lambda}(\xi,s)=1-s\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(\xi,s)

we have

\widetilde{\lambda}(\xi,s)=\left[1-\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}\right]{\rm e}^{-\xi\sqrt{s\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}}\,,

(41)

that is the formula obtained by S.G. Kou and H. Wang
for the same simple case considered here

[
17
, Theorem 3.1, formula (3.1)]
.

In analogy with the previous section, we show that the proposed
Sturm–Liouville system can be used for calculating the survival probability also
for CTRW models. In this case, instead to consider the generating
function, we consider formula (
35
) that allows for
stepping from the discrete-time to the continuous-time setting.
By taking into account (
7
), we have in the most general case
that
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)
satisfies the equation

\frac{d}{dz}\left\{p(z)\,\frac{d}{dz}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)\right\}+q(z)\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)=-\widetilde{\psi}(s)\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)+q(z)\,\widetilde{\Psi}(s)\,.

(42)

Hence, in the special case
k(x)={\rm e}^{-|x|}/2
,
see, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
,
it holds
p(z)=1
and
q(z)=-1
and,
together with the Markovian assumption (
36
),
equation (
42
) reduces to

\frac{d^{2}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}}{dz^{2}}-\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)=-\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(z,s)-\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)\,.

(43)

To conclude, by applying the Laplace transform to
z
into
v

it holds

\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}}(v,s)=\frac{v\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{\prime}(0,s)+v^{2}\,\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}(0,s)-\widetilde{\Psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}{v\,\left(v^{2}-1+\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)\right)}\,,

(44)

and, after plugging boundary conditions (

),
we have

\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{+}}(v,s)=\frac{(v+1)\,\sqrt{1-\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}}{vs\,\left(v+\sqrt{1-\widetilde{\psi}_{\rm{M}}(s)}\right)}\,.

(45)

Finally, it is possible to check that, by the Laplace inversion in

5
Conclusion

In this paper we derived the Sturm–Liouville system of equations
that is associated with the Wiener–Hopf integral equation (
1
)
for the calculation of the survival probability in first-passage time
problems for random walks.
By studying a simple, but meaningful, example we showed the feasibility
of the proposed approach within the framework of
the discrete-time random walk and that of the CTRW.
This approach is an alternative to the
Pollaczek–Spitzer formula.

Summarising, we showed that
k(z,\xi)
and
\phi_{n+1}(z)

are indeed related accordingly to (
1
)
if and only if they solve (
6
) and (
7
), respectively,
and boundary conditions (
15
) are met.
Hence, from the derived Sturm–Liouville system (
8
)
the Sparre Andersen theorem (
2
) cannot be obtained
but it is indeed a necessary boundary condition.
In other words,
from the simple and concrete example here studied,
it follows that when the survival probability is calculated
by
the proposed Sturm–Liouville system (
8
)
an extra condition must be added, and
this extra condition is actually the statement of
the Sparre Andersen theorem (
2
).
As we discussed in the Introduction,
this is a limitation also in the use of the Pollaczek–Spitzer formula
in its original derivation
[
2
]
and analogue
to the assumption of the boundedness of the survival probability
in a more cumbersome derivation

[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

We end by underlying that the proposed approach
allows for deriving explicit formulae for the survival probability
in the discrete-time setting and also in the continuous-time one,
this last feasibility supports to extend its successful application
even to non-Markovian CTRW models.

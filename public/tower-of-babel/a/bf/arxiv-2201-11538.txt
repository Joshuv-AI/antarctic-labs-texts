Abstract
The optical fiber multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) channel with intensity modulation and direct detection (IM/DD) per spatial path is treated. The spatial dimensions represent the multiple modes employed for transmission and the cross-talk between them originates in the multiplexers and demultiplexers, which are polarization dependent and thus time-varying. The upper bounds from free-space IM/DD MIMO channels are adapted to the fiber case, and the constellation constrained capacity is constructively estimated using the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm. An autoencoder is then proposed to optimize a practical MIMO transmission in terms of pre-coder and detector assuming channel distribution knowledge at the transmitter. The pre-coders are shown to be robust to changes in the channel.



I
Introduction

Few-mode transmission in optical fibers is typically considered in long-haul communication links in order to go beyond the so-called nonlinear Shannon limit of single-mode fibers and enhance the data rate by employing spatial multiplicity
[
1
,
2
]
. On the other hand, for short-reach connections as the ones inside data centers (DCs), the problem is typically not capacity per fiber, but rather the cost of the transceivers, which have to employ multiple wavelengths to reach the target data rates. Such links typically operate with intensity-modulation and direct detection (IM/DD), with digital signal processing (DSP) functionalities kept at a minimum in order to reduce the cost w.r.t. the standard long-haul coherent transmission, and also to reduce latency which is critical for intra-DC communication.

The abundance and low cost of few-mode fibers (FMFs) and the lifted requirement for frequency stabilization of the lasers make few-mode multiplexing an attractive alternative to few-wavelength multiplexing. The challenge in employing FMF transmission is multiplexing and demultiplexing the transmission modes into and out of the same fiber. For that purpose, several types of mode multiplexers may be employed, e.g. air clad photonic lanterns
[
3
]
, demonstrating 100G transmission over up to 2 km for 2-mode multiplexing. Due to the cost requirement, multiple input multiple output (MIMO) processing and synchronization of the modes at the receiver is typically prohibitive. Instead, the multiplexer (MUX) and demultiplexer (DEMUX) are required to produce negligible cross-talk (XT), which sets a harsh requirement on their fabrication process.

Capacity and achievable rates for wireless IM/DD links have been studied
[
4
]
, more recently in
[
5
,
6
]
, including MIMO versions
[
7
,
8
]
, where the channel is assumed fixed and known at the transmitter and/or receiver. In general, the amplitude-constrained channel capacity study dates back to
[
9
]
, where a linear system was considered. Achievable rate regions for the MIMO multiple access channel with IM/DD were derived in
[
8
]
.

In this paper, the case of few-mode point-to-point optical fiber channel is considered with joint or independent mode processing at the receiver. The latter case falls into the category of MIMO broadcast channels with IM/DD, for which to our knowledge, no closed-form expressions exist for the channel capacity, especially in the case of fading. An upper bound on the capacity with perfect channel knowledge at the transmitter and receiver is adapted from the free-space case, and the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm (BAA) is used to estimate a tightly-approaching achievable rate. An autoencoder is then proposed to optimize a pre-coder and a detector for both cases of joint and independent processing at the receiver, under the constraint of only channel state distribution knowledge at the transmitter.

II
Channel model

A transmission system with independent data on each spatial path of the FMF is considered. The data bits modulate the waveform using pulse amplitude modulation (PAM), where the modulation alphabet is
{\cal{X}}={0,1,...M-1}
of size
|{\cal{X}}|=M
. The symbols are independent in time and the signal vector is denoted
\mathbf{x}=\left[x_{1},x_{2},...x_{N}\right]^{T}
, where
N
is the number of employed modes. The signal may then be pre-coded by a linear or nonlinear function
f
,
\mathbf{v}=f(\mathbf{x})
. A Mach-Zehnder modulator (MZM) is employed for modulating the intensity of optical carriers of wavelengths
\mathbf{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...\lambda_{N}\}
with the corresponding pre-coded symbols. The MZM is biased in quadrature, and its output intensity
s_{i}
as a function of the modulating voltage
v_{i}
can be modeled as

\displaystyle s_{i}=P_{i}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{v_{i}}{V_{\pi}}\right),

(1)

where
V_{\pi}
is the half-wave voltage of the modulator, and
P_{i}
is the optical power of the
i-
th carrier. The multiple fibers leading out of each MZM are combined in a mode MUX. An example of a MUX is the air clad photonic lantern made of single mode fibers, which are fused together and down tapered. The end of the taper is spliced to a few-mode fiber. After down tapering the surrounding air will act as the cladding. By use of single mode fibers with different core sizes mode selectivity is obtained
[
3
,
10
]
. At the end, a DEMUX is used to demultiplex the modes into independent spatial paths. Each mode is detected by a photodetector (PD), which translates light intensity to electrical current. The signal at the receiver is denoted
\mathbf{y}=\left[y_{1},y_{2},...y_{N}\right]^{T}
. A block diagram of the system is given in Fig.
1
. Relatively short fiber distance is assumed so that dispersion, loss and distributed XT along the fiber span can be neglected
[
10
]
. The channel is thus memoryless. Without loss of generality and for easier notation, the case of 2-mode transmission is assumed where the spatial modes
LP_{01}
and
LP_{11}
are excited for propagation. This case corresponds to the lanterns demonstrated in
[
3
]
.

Each of the MUX, DEMUX and splices can then be modeled by a MIMO matrix
\mathbf{H}_{MUX}
,
\mathbf{H}_{DEMUX}
and
\mathbf{H}_{SPL}
, respectively and has the form (for example for
\mathbf{H}_{MUX}
)

\mathbf{H}_{MUX}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-xt_{1})/\alpha_{1}&xt_{2}/\alpha_{2}\\
xt_{1}/\alpha_{1}&(1-xt_{2})/\alpha_{2}\end{bmatrix},

(2)

where
xt_{1}
and
xt_{2}
are the XTs from mode
LP_{01}
to mode
LP_{11}
(
LP_{11}
to
LP_{01}
, respectively), and
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are the losses of the corresponding modes in the component. The transfer matrices are subject to polarization-dependent XT. In this work, without loss of generality, this dependence is modeled by drawing the realization of each XT value in (
2
) for each time instant from a uniform distribution with a given mean
{\mathbb{E}}\left[XT\right]
and a range of
6
dB. The losses are assumed constant. Assuming the carriers are not coherent at the receiver (which is reasonable for cheap, independent lasers with significant phase noise and assuming large enough differential group delay, as well as proper wavelength selection for the carriers
[
11
]
), the beating between modes can be minimized, and the channel from transmitter to receiver can be modeled as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{H}_{DEMUX}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{SPL}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{MUX}\cdot\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{w},

(3)

where
\mathbf{s}=\left[s_{1},s_{2},...s_{N}\right]^{T}
. The noise terms
\mathbf{w}=\left[w_{1},w_{2},...w_{N}\right]^{T}
are independent, identically distributed samples from a Gaussian distribution with a variance
\sigma_{w}^{2}
. The noise is assumed dominated by the receiver thermal and quantization noise. At the receiver, independent or joint detection of the signal
\mathbf{y}
may be performed with the function
g(\mathbf{y})
.

The MZM transfer function imposes an amplitude constraint on the signal
0\leq v_{i}\leq V_{\pi}
, since outside of these boundaries the cosine transfer function becomes non-bijective. Equivalently, the channel in (
3
) can be considered the standard MIMO IM/DD channel
[
7
,
8
]
with input
\mathbf{s}
and amplitude constraint
0\leq s_{i}\leq P_{i}
.

In the case of fiber communications and for IM/DD links operating at short reach, there is no practical constraint (within reason) associated with the average power, unlike for example free-space optical wireless links, where safety and interference may be factors.

The signal to noise ratio (SNR) is defined as
SNR=10\log_{10}(\frac{\sum_{i}P_{i}}{2N\cdot\sigma_{w}})
, motivated by the fact that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[|Y_{i}-S_{i}|\right]=\sigma_{w}
and that for uniformly distributed
s_{i}
we have
{\mathbb{E}}\left[|S_{i}|\right]=P_{i}/2
. A total power constraint may be imposed in the form of
\sum_{i}P_{i}=P_{tot}
so that the SNR can be swept.

III
Capacity and capacity achieving distributions

Assuming full-rank, positive
\mathbf{H}
with well-behaved distribution, the ergodic capacity
{\cal{C}}
of the MIMO fading channel with perfect channel knowledge at the transmitter and receiver is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal{C}}=\max_{p_{\boldsymbol{X}}(\boldsymbol{X}),f({\boldsymbol{X}}),P_{\{1\dots N\}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{H}\left[{\cal{I}}(\boldsymbol{Y};\boldsymbol{X}|\mathbf{H},p_{\boldsymbol{X}}(\boldsymbol{X}))\right],

(4)

where the mutual information (MI)
{\cal{I}}(\cdot;\cdot)
between the input and output is maximized over the input distribution
p_{\boldsymbol{X}}(\boldsymbol{X})
, the pre-coder
f({\boldsymbol{X}})
and the power allocation. Assuming that the optimal pre-coder is applied, we have

\displaystyle{\cal{C}}=\max_{p_{\boldsymbol{S}}(\boldsymbol{S}),P_{\{1\dots N\}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{H}\left[{\cal{I}}(\boldsymbol{Y};\boldsymbol{S}|\mathbf{H},p_{\boldsymbol{S}}(\boldsymbol{S}))\right],

(5)

which is the ergodic capacity of the channel in (
3
) with ideal knowledge at the receiver. The latter can be upper bounded by splitting the channel into parallel IM/DD channels by using the QR decomposition of
\mathbf{H=QR}
, where
\mathbf{Q}
is unitary and
\mathbf{R}
is upper triangular. The receiver then pre-processes
\mathbf{y}
to
\hat{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{Q}^{T}\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{w}
, the interference from previously decoded channels is canceled on sub-sequent channels and the MIMO channel is equivalent to the set of parallel channels
\hat{y}_{i}=\mathbf{R}_{i,i}s_{i}+w_{i}
, where
\mathbf{R}_{i,i}
is the element on the
i-
th row and
i-
th column on
\mathbf{R}

[
7
]
. The capacity may then be upper-bounded by

\displaystyle{\cal{C}}\leq\max_{P_{\{1\dots N\}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{H}\left[\sum_{i}\bar{{\cal{C}}}_{i}(\mathbf{R}_{i,i},P_{i},\sigma)\right],

(6)

where
\bar{{\cal{C}}}_{i}(\mathbf{R}_{i,i},P_{i},\sigma)
is the upper bound (UB) on the scalar IM/DD channel with power
P_{i}
and a gain coefficient
\mathbf{R}_{i,i}
. The maximization over the power allocation can be done by a grid search for reasonable number of spatial modes with the above mentioned constraint of
\sum_{i}P_{i}=P_{tot}
. Two upper bounds are considered, UB1 (obtained from
[
6
, Theorem 1]
) and UB2 (obtaiend from
[
4
, Eq.
19
]
), where UB1 is tight at high SNR, and UB2 is superior at low SNR. The limitation of these UBs is that they do not provide the pre-coder function, since they are derived in the already pre-coded space.

3

p_{\boldsymbol{S}}^{opt}(\boldsymbol{S})=\sum_{k=1:|{\cal{S}}|}a_{k}\delta(S-{\cal{S}}_{k})

are the weights of the
k-
th mass point
{\cal{S}}_{k}
and

is the Dirac delta function, the following can be proven.

For a given power allocation
P_{\{1\dots N\}}
, an initialization of the BAA with a discrete uniform distribution of
M\rightarrow\infty
on the constrained hypercube produces

for which the MI

is the capacity of the channel in (
3
), and a lower bound on

Proof:

The following observations can be made:

1.

The capacity-achieving distribution of channel (
3
) is discrete and unique
[

]

[

]

2.

For discrete, memoryless channels the BAA achieves improvement on every iteration
[

]
until convergence (i.e., it always achieves a local optimum).

3.

The BAA is allowed to approach zero probability to individual mass points, effectively removing points from the support set of
\boldsymbol{S}
.

4.

Since the power constraint is ensured by the MZM transfer function (
1
) and the initialization of the mass points, the BAA is unconstrained (except for
\sum_{i}a_{i}=1

From Observations 3) and 4), for

the BAA can ’reach’ any discrete support set within the hypercube, making the corresponding local optimum from Observation 2) the global optimum due to the concavity in Observation 1), proving the first part of the Theorem. \mathbf{V}=f(X)=\mathbf{X}
and support set

obtained by inverting (

IV
Autoencoder based communication

As mentioned in the introduction, full MIMO processing may be too complex for the target communication system because of both hardware (in terms of synchronizing the receivers for each mode) and software (in terms of processing for channel estimation and multi-dimensional detection) requirements. However, partial channel knowledge may still be available in terms of the distribution of
\mathbf{H}
at the transmitter and receiver, which can be obtained e.g. from measurements statistics. For such cases, an auxiliary channel likelihood function must be employed for detection and MI estimation, which is subject to optimization. The likelihood function needs to be robust to the distribution of
\mathbf{H}
. At the same time, the knowledge of
\mathbf{H}
may allow to design a pre-coder which at least partially mitigates the mode interference. Both of these optimizations can be efficiently approached with an autoencoder (AE)
[
16
]
. The AE employs a neural network (NN) for pre-coding, another NN for detection, and wraps them around the channel model at hand. The most closely related to this work AE application is for the Rayleigh fading MIMO channel
[
17
]
, where the channel is linear. In this paper, both the MZM and the photodetection induce nonlinearities. Furthermore, in this paper, the AE is employed to also optimize the power allocation (instead of the grid-search performed in the previous Section). A basic schematic of the AE is given in Fig.
4
, and the following optimization scenarios are considered for the pre-coder:

The outputs of the detector NNs are auxiliary distributions
q(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{y})
for the conditional distribution
p(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{y})
, and the detectors thus provide an achievable rate
[

]
.
Det1
represents a more practical scenario. In order to simplify it even further, its performance is reported also for the case of a standard Gaussian auxiliary channel receiver, for which the auxiliary distribution is
q({\cal{X}}_{i}|y_{k})\propto p_{X_{i}}({\cal{X}}_{i})\cdot\exp\left[-0.5/\hat{\sigma}_{w_{i}}\left(y_{k}-\mu_{i}\right)^{2}\right]
. The parameters

of the auxiliary Gaussian function are estimated using training data

. The scenario with
Prec1

Prec2
and

and Gaussian detection, a NN is still employed at the receiver but only in order to facilitate training of the pre-coder using the cross-entropy (CE) cost function.

In the case of
Det1
, the AE is trained using the cost function
Cost=\max_{i=1\dots N}CE_{i}(X_{i},g(Y_{i}))
, where
CE_{i}(X_{i}|Y_{i})={\cal{H}}(X_{i}|Y_{i})
is the CE, which in this case is equivalent to the conditional entropy
{\cal{H}}(X|Y)

{\cal{I}}(X;Y)={\cal{H}}(X)-{\cal{H}}(X|Y)

Det2
is considered as a reference performance and for comparison to the bounds and rates of Section
III
. In this case, the cost function is
Cost=CE(\boldsymbol{X},g(\boldsymbol{Y}))
. For all detectors and pre-coders, the NN topologies were optimized by increasing their depth and width by factors of 2 until convergence of the cost function. The resulting topologies for

are given in Table

M
and

The Adam optimizer was employed for stochastic gradient descent optimization of all parameters. The batch size was 200, and a total of 1.000.000 symbols are used for training and independent 100.000 for testing. The channel is drawn from a uniform distribution as described above for every sample during training and testing. The result is an AE robust to the desired channel distribution. Observe, no explicit constraint is imposed on the encoder part. The AE is naturally forced to operate within the boundaries of the cosine transfer function of the MZM (

VI
Discussion

The BAA optimization can be thought of as probabilistic shaping, while the pre-coding can be thought of as geometric shaping. An obvious direction for future research is to explore joint optimization of both, since as it was seen above, each has its benefits.

In this work, uniform distribution of the cross-talk was arbitrarily chosen. The described methods directly support other types of distributions, e.g. Gaussian and or empirical, depending on the fit to the measured polarization drift.

The case of training independent detectors per mode with the target of maximizing the sum-rate instead of equalizing the rate per mode is well-within the realm of capabilities of the AE. It is relevant for cases where rate-adaptivity is permitted per mode. It was omitted here for space considerations.

In this paper,

[

VII
Conclusion

The capacity of the optical FMF MIMO channel was lower-bounded by means of an unconstrained BAA, and tightness was demonstrated to previous upper bounds, resulting in accurate capacity estimates. The lower bounds are shown to be achievable by means of trivial pre-coders. Non-trivial pre-coders and detectors were then designed based on an AE. The AE with independent mode processing at the receiver was shown to approach the BAA lower bound and at the same time to be robust against channel uncertainties and drifts. For a fixed channel, the pre-coders were shown to be superior to the BAA and further close the gap to capacity for constellations of practical size. Furthermore, up to
5
dB of cross-talk tolerance was reported by the AE w.r.t. standard techniques, which will enable cheaper multiplexers and demultiplexers to be employed for FMF transmission alternatives to standard wavelength division multiplexing of short reach optical links.

Abstract




Abstract
We provide several new characterizations of
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights,
originally introduced by A. Volberg
as matrix-valued substitutes of the classical
A_{\infty}
weights.
In analogy with the notion of
A_{p}
-dimension of matrix weights
introduced in our previous work, we introduce the concepts of
the lower and the upper dimensions of
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights,
which enable us to obtain sharp estimates related to their reducing operators.
In a follow-up work, these results will play a key role
in the study of function spaces with
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights,
which extends earlier results in the more restricted class of
A_{p}
-matrix weights.

1
Introduction

For a scalar-valued weight
w
,
it is well known that its membership in the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}

is the characterising condition for several inequalities
involving the boundedness of some operators (like the maximal operator
[
28
]
,
the Hilbert transform
[
21
]
, or more general singular integrals
[
6
]
)
in the weighted space
L^{p}(w)
.
On the other hand, the weaker condition
w\in A_{\infty}

is often enough for various other estimates in these spaces
(like the comparison of singular integrals with the maximal operators
[
6
]
).

The study of the space
L^{2}(W)
with a matrix weight
W
goes back to
Wiener and Masani
[
37
, §4]
.
Their work focused on the prediction theory for multivariate stochastic processes.
In order to address the problems related to
multivariate random stationary process and Toeplitz operators,
Treil and Volberg
[
34
]
introduced
A_{2}
-matrix weights
and proved that the Hilbert transform is bounded on
L^{2}(W)
if and only if
W\in A_{2}
.
Extensions to
L^{p}(W)
with
W\in A_{p}
for general
p\in(1,\infty)

were later found by Nazarov and Treil
[
31
]
and Volberg
[
35
]
.
For many other results on
A_{p}
-matrix weights and

L^{p}(W)
, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
16
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
30
]
.

In the same work already mentioned, Volberg
[
35
]
also introduced
an analogue of the
A_{\infty}
condition for matrix weights.
To be precise, for matrix-valued weights, this condition splits into
a family of conditions
A_{p,\infty}
,
p\in(0,\infty)
, but they all reduce to
the classical
A_{\infty}
, when the weight is scalar-valued.
In analogy with results in the scalar case, it was observed in
[
35
]
that the equivalence of certain matrix-weighted Triebel–Lizorkin norms remains valid for this larger class of weights.

Our interest in
A_{p,\infty}
weights is motivated by a more general class of similar applications in the theory of function spaces with matrix weights.
Following the development of matrix-weighted Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(W)
by
Frazier and Roudenko
[
13
,
14
,
32
,
33
]
and Bu et al.
[
4
]
,
and matrix-weighted Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(W)

by Frazier and Roudenko
[
15
]
and Wang et al.
[
36
]

(after some results in the special case
\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(W)

already in
[
31
,
35
]
and more recently in
[
25
]
),
the present authors
[
3
]
recently introduced and investigated
a rather general class of Besov-type and Triebel–Lizorkin-type spaces with matrix weights.
However, the theory of
[
3
]
was developed for
A_{p}
-matrix weights only.
Extending these results to the broader generality of
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights
motivated our systematic study of this interesting class of weights in this article.
Applications to Besov-type and Triebel–Lizorkin-type spaces
with
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights will be considered in a follow-up work,
but the present results should also have an independent interest
and many other applications involving matrix-valued extensions of
classical results dealing with
A_{\infty}
weights.

The organization of the remainder of this article is as follows.
In Section
2
,
we recall some basic concepts and results from the theory of matrix weights.
In Section
3
,
we introduce a new definition of the matrix
A_{p,\infty}
class
and prove its equivalence with Volberg’s definition,
together with several further characterisations.
In Section
4
,
we explore a certain self-improvement property of the original definition
and obtain a matrix-valued substitute of
the classical identity
A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{q\in[1,\infty)}A_{q}
.
Section
5
is concerned with reverse Hölder inequalities,
which are another manifestation of self-improvement.
In Section
6
,
we introduce and study the concepts of the upper
and the lower dimensions of
A_{p,\infty}
weights
and use them to estimate norms of the form
\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\|
,
where
A_{Q}
and
A_{R}
are the
reducing operators

related to
W\in A_{p,\infty}
on different cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Such estimates for
A_{p}
-matrix weights played a key role
in the development of the theory of matrix-weighted Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
in
[
3
,
15
]
, and will be extensively used in our follow-up work,
where we extend this theory to the larger class of
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights.
In the final Section
7
,
we illustrate the new concepts by several examples,
which also show the sharpness of the results obtained in Section
6
.

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The

B

\mathbb{R}^{n}

Q

For any

r_{+}

is defined as

.
For any
t\in(0,\infty)
,
\log_{+}t:=\max\{0,\log t\}
.
For any

,
a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\}
and

.
The symbol
C
denotes a positive constant which is independent
of the main parameters involved, but may vary from line to line.
The symbol
A\lesssim B
means that
A\leq CB
for some positive constant
C
,
while

means
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.
Let
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}:=(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{n}
.
We use
\mathbf{0}
to denote the

of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we use
\mathbf{1}_{E}
to denote its
characteristic function
.
For any
p\in(0,\infty]
, the
Lebesgue space

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

has the usual meaning, and the
local Lebesgue space

L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined to be the set of
all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that,
for any bounded measurable set

,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}:=\|f\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

In what follows, we denote
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

simply, respectively, by

and

.
For any measurable function

on

The
Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator

\mathcal{M}
is defined by setting,
for any
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.1)

\mathcal{M}(f)(x):=\sup_{\mathrm{ball}\,B\ni x}\fint_{B}|f(y)|\,dy.

Also, when we prove a proposition (or the like),
in its proof we always use the same symbols as in

2
Background on matrix weights

In this section, we recall some basic concepts and results
from the theory of matrix weights.
Let us begin with some basic concepts on matrices.

For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the set of all
m\times n
complex-valued matrices is denoted by
M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})

and
M_{m,m}(\mathbb{C})
is simply denoted by
M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
.
The zero matrix in
M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})
is denoted by
O_{m,n}

and
O_{m,m}
is simply denoted by
O_{m}
.
For any
A:=[a_{ij}]\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})
,
the conjugate transpose of
A
is denoted by
A^{*}
.
For any
A\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
, let

(2.1)

\|A\|:=\sup_{\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m},\,|\vec{z}|=1}|A\vec{z}|.

In what follows, we regard
\mathbb{C}^{m}
as
M_{m,1}(\mathbb{C})

and let
\vec{\mathbf{0}}:=(0,\ldots,0)^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
The matrix
A\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
is called a
Hermitian matrix
if
A^{*}=A
and
is called a
unitary matrix
if
A^{*}A=I_{m}
,
where
I_{m}
is the identity matrix.
For any
A:=[a_{ij}]\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})

and
\vec{z}:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,

\displaystyle[A\ \vec{z}]:=\begin{bmatrix}a_{11}&\cdots&a_{1,n}&z_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{m1}&\cdots&a_{m,n}&z_{m}\end{bmatrix}.

Now, we recall the concepts of positive definite matrices
and nonnegative definite matrices (see, for instance,
[
20
, (7.1.1a) and (7.1.1b)]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A matrix
A\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
is said to be
positive definite

if, for any
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}
,
\vec{z}^{*}A\vec{z}>0
,
and
A
is said to be
nonnegative definite
if,
for any
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
\vec{z}^{*}A\vec{z}\geq 0
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is well known that any nonnegative definite matrix is always Hermitian
(see, for instance,
[
20
, Theorem 4.1.4]
).

From Remark
2.2
and
[
20
, Theorem 5.6.2(d)]
,
we immediately deduce the following conclusion; we omit the details.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A,B\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be two nonnegative definite matrices.
Then
\|AB\|=\|BA\|
with the same norm
\|\cdot\|
as in (
2.1
).

Let
A\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be a positive definite matrix
and have eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
.
Due to
[
20
, Theorem 2.5.6(c)]
,
we find that there exists a unitary matrix
U\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
such that

(2.2)

A=U\operatorname{diag}\,(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})U^{*}.

Moreover, by
[
20
, Theorem 4.1.8]
,
we find
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset(0,\infty)
.
The following definition is based on these conclusions
and can be found in
[
19
, (6.2.1)]
(see also
[
18
, Definition 1.2]
).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be a positive definite matrix
and have positive eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
.
For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, define

A^{\alpha}:=U\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}^{\alpha},\ldots,\lambda_{m}^{\alpha}\right)U^{*},

where
U
is the same as in (
2.2
).

Remark 2.5
.

From
[
19
, p. 408]
, we deduce that
A^{\alpha}

is independent of the choices of the order of
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
U
,
and hence
A^{\alpha}
is well defined.

Now, we recall the concept of matrix weights (see, for instance,
[
31
,
34
,
35
]
).

Definition 2.6
.

A matrix-valued function
W:\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\to M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
is called
a
matrix weight
if
W
satisfies that

(i)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
W(x)
is nonnegative definite;

(ii)

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
W(x)
is invertible;

(iii)

the entries of
W
are all locally integrable.

A
scalar weight
is a matrix weight with
m=1
.

Next, we recall the concept of reducing operators (see, for instance,
[
35
, (3.1)]
).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
W
be a matrix weight,
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a bounded measurable set satisfying
|E|\in(0,\infty)
.
The matrix
A_{E}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
is called a
reducing operator
of order
p
for
W

if
A_{E}
is positive definite and,
for any
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,

(2.3)

\left|A_{E}\vec{z}\right|\sim\left[\fint_{E}\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{z}\right|^{p}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the positive equivalence constants depend only on
m
and
p
.

Remark 2.8
.

In Definition
2.7
, the existence of
A_{E}
is guaranteed by

[
16
, Proposition 1.2]
and
[
13
, p. 1237]
; we omit the details.

The following lemma is just
[
3
, Lemma 2.11]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
W
be a matrix weight,
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a bounded measurable set satisfying
|E|\in(0,\infty)
.
If
A_{E}
is a reducing operator of order
p
for
W
,
then, for any matrix
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle\|A_{E}M\|\sim\left[\fint_{E}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the positive equivalence constants depend only on
m
and
p
.

Corresponding to the classical weight class
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(see, for instance,
[
17
, Definitions 7.1.1 and 7.1.3]
),
we have the following concept of
A_{p}
-matrix weights
(see, for instance,
[
15
, p. 490]
).

Definition 2.10
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. A matrix weight
W
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is called an
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
-
matrix weight

if
W
satisfies that, when
p\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}:=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\mathop{\mathrm{\,ess\,sup\,}}_{y\in Q}\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx<\infty

or that, when
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}:=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\fint_{Q}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dy\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\,dx<\infty,

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.
The
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
-matrix weights
reduce to
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-weights when
m=1
.

If there is no confusion,
we will denote
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
simply by
A_{p}
.
The following lemma is contained in
[
3
, Lemma 2.15 and Corollary 2.16]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
, and matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
.
Then
\widetilde{W}:=W^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
and

(2.4)

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p}}\sim\left[W^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\right]_{A_{p^{\prime}}}^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}},

where the positive equivalence constants depend only on
m
and
p
.
Let
Q
be a cube and
A_{Q}
and
\widetilde{A}_{Q}
, respectively,
the reducing operators of order
p
for
W
and of order
p^{\prime}
for
\widetilde{W}
.
Then, for any
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
,

(2.5)

\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|\sim\left\|\widetilde{A}_{Q}M\right\|\sim\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

where the positive equivalence constants
depend only on
m
,
p
, and
[W]_{A_{p}}
.

3
Matrix
A_{\infty}
weights

Corresponding to
A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(see, for instance,
[
17
, Definitions 7.3.1]
),
we introduce the concept of
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights as follows.
A priori, this is different from the definition given by Volberg
[
35
]
,
but we will shortly prove that these definitions
(together with several further characterizations) are actually equivalent.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. A matrix weight
W
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is called an
A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
-
matrix weight

if
W
satisfies that, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\in L^{1}(Q)

and

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle:=[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)<\infty.

The
A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
-matrix weights
go back to
A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-weights when
m=1
.

If there is no confusion,
we will denote
A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
simply by
A_{p,\infty}
.
The following lemma gives the relations between scalar and matrix weights,
which follows immediately from their definitions; we omit the details.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
w
be a scalar weight and consider the matrix weight
W:=wI_{m}
,
where
I_{m}
is the identity matrix. Then

(i)

W\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
if and only if
w\in A_{\max\{1,p\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(ii)

W\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
if and only if
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We recall a well-known example of scalar weights (see, for instance,
[
17
, Example 7.1.7]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define the scalar weight
w_{a}(x):=|x|^{a}
.
Then the following three statements hold.

(i)

w_{a}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
a\in(-n,\infty)
;

(ii)

w_{a}\in A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
a\in(-n,0]
;

(iii)

For any
p\in(1,\infty)
,

w_{a}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
a\in(-n,n(p-1))
.

Remark 3.4
.

Lemma
2.11
does not hold for
A_{p,\infty}
.
Indeed, let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.
By Lemma
3.3
(i), we conclude for scalar weights that

\displaystyle w(\cdot):=|\cdot|^{n\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ but }w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(\cdot)=|\cdot|^{-n}\notin A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Combined with Lemma
3.2
, this implies for matrix weights that

\displaystyle W:=wI_{m}\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\text{ but }W^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}=w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}I_{m}\notin A_{p^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m}).

This defect leads to an essential difficulty that

\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\|
does not have a good enough equivalent form like (
2.5
)
and hence a series of related estimates no longer hold.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
W\in A_{p,\infty}
,
and
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be nonzero.
Then, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)M\right\|^{p}\right]\in L^{1}(Q)

and, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}\sim\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\},

where the positive equivalence constants
depend only on
m
,
p
, and
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}
.

Proof.

We first show

(3.3)

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)M\right\|^{p}\right]\in L^{1}(Q).

By
W\in A_{p,\infty}
and Lemma
2.9
,
we find that, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left[\left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)\right\|^{p}\right]\in L^{1}(Q).

From this and Lemma
2.3
, we infer that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)M\right\|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\log_{+}\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)A_{Q}\right\|^{p}\right]+\log_{+}\left[\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle=\log_{+}\left[\left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)\right\|^{p}\right]+\log_{+}\left[\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}\right]\in L^{1}(Q).

This finishes the proof of (
3.3
).

Next, we prove (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
For any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}\leq\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|^{p}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p},

which, together with (
3.3
), further implies that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}.

By Lemma
2.3
, Jensen’s inequality, and Lemma
2.9
, we have,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx\sim\left\|A_{Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}=1.

This, combined with (
3.4
), further implies that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle 0<\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}\lesssim\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}.

Using this and (
3.3
), we obtain (
3.1
).

On the other hand, from Lemmas
2.3
and
2.9

and the definition of
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}
, we deduce that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|^{p}\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dx\right\}\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p}\lesssim[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|^{p},

which, together with (
3.5
), further implies that (
3.2
).
This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Now, we recall
\widetilde{A}_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights
introduced by Volberg (see
[
35
, (2.2)]
).

Definition 3.6
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. A matrix weight
W

is called an
\widetilde{A}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
-
matrix weight

if there exists a positive constant
C
such that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{z}\right|\,dx\right)\leq C\sup_{\vec{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}}|(\vec{z},\vec{u})|\left[\fint_{Q}\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{u}\right|^{p}\,dx\right]^{-\frac{1}{p}}.

In what follows, we denote
\widetilde{A}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})

simply by
\widetilde{A}_{p,\infty}
.
The following proposition shows that Volberg’s condition is equivalent to ours;
it also gives several other equivalent variants of both conditions.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
W
be a matrix weight.
For any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
A_{Q}
be the reducing operator of order
p
for
W
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of cube
Q
,
such that the following conditions are all equivalent:

(i)

W\in\widetilde{A}_{p,\infty}
in the sense of Definition
3.6
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{z}\right|\,dx\right)\leq C\left|A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{z}\right|

for any
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\vec{v}\right|\,dx\right)\leq C

for any
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
|\vec{v}|=1
;

(iv)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}U\right\|\,dx\right)\leq C

for any
U\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
with
\|U\|=1
;

(v)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\,dx\right)\leq C
;

(vi)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|\,dx\right)\,dy\right)\leq C
;

(vii)

W\in A_{p,\infty}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
;

(viii)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|\,dx\right)\leq C\left\|A_{Q}^{-1}M\right\|

for any
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

(
i
)
\Leftrightarrow
(
ii
): Both conditions (
i
) and (
ii
) involve an estimate of the same left-hand side. Concerning the right-hand sides of these estimates,
by (
2.3
), we conclude that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\vec{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}}|(\vec{z},\vec{u})|\left[\fint_{Q}\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{u}\right|^{p}\,dx\right]^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\sim\sup_{\vec{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}}\frac{|(\vec{z},\vec{u})|}{|A_{Q}\vec{u}|}=\sup_{\vec{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}}\frac{|(\vec{z},A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{u})|}{|\vec{u}|}=\sup_{\vec{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}}\frac{|(A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{z},\vec{u})|}{|\vec{u}|}=\left|A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{z}\right|.

Thus, the right-hand sides of (
i
) and (
ii
)
are also comparable, and hence the two conditions are equivalent.

(
ii
)
\Rightarrow
(
iii
): We follow an argument contained in the proof of
[
35
, Lemma 3.1]
.
For any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
|\vec{v}|=1
,

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\vec{v}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\fint_{Q}\log\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\vec{v}\right|\,dx+\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left[\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\vec{v}\right|^{-1}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=:I(\vec{v})+II(\vec{v}).

By the assumption (
ii
), we obtain,
for any
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
|\vec{v}|=1
,

I(\vec{v})\leq\log\left(C\left|A_{Q}^{-1}A_{Q}\vec{v}\right|\right)=\log\left(C\left|\vec{v}\right|\right)=\log C,

where
C
is the same as in (
ii
). On the other hand,
since
W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)
and
A_{Q}
are Hermitian matrices
and by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we find that,
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for any
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
|\vec{v}|=1
,

1=\left|\vec{v}\right|^{2}=\left(W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\vec{v},W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{v}\right)\leq\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\vec{v}\right|\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{v}\right|.

This, combined with the elementary inequality
\log_{+}t<t
for
t\in(0,\infty)

and (
2.3
), further implies that,
for any
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
|\vec{v}|=1
,

\displaystyle II(\vec{v})

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{v}\right|\,dx=\frac{1}{p}\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left[\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{v}\right|^{p}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\fint_{Q}\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{v}\right|^{p}\,dx\sim\frac{1}{p}\left|A_{Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\vec{v}\right|^{p}=\frac{1}{p}.

Combining the bounds for
I(\vec{v})
and
II(\vec{v})
,
we find that (
iii
) holds and
C_{\eqref{W3}}\lesssim C_{\eqref{W2}}
,
where the implicit positive constant depends only on
m
and
p
.

(
iii
)
\Rightarrow
(
iv
): For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, let

\vec{e}_{i}:=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
Then, from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we infer that,
for any
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
and
\vec{z}:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,

\left|M\vec{z}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|z_{i}\right|\left|M\vec{e}_{i}\right|\leq\sqrt{m}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}\left|M\vec{e}_{i}\right|

and hence

\displaystyle\|M\|\leq\sqrt{m}\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}\left|M\vec{e}_{i}\right|.

By this, we conclude that, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

and any
U\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
with
\|U\|=1
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}U\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\log_{+}\left[\sqrt{m}\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}U\vec{e}_{i}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\log\sqrt{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\log_{+}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}U\vec{e}_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\log\sqrt{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\log_{+}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\frac{U\vec{e}_{i}}{|U\vec{e}_{i}|}\right|,

where the last step follows from the fact that

|U\vec{e}_{i}|\leq\|U\|\,|\vec{e}_{i}|=1
.
(If
U\vec{e}_{i}=0
, then the
i
-th term in the sum vanishes and can simply be ignored.)
Integrating both sides of (
3.6
) and using the assumption (
iii
)
with
U\vec{e}_{i}/|U\vec{e}_{i}|
in place of
\vec{v}_{i}

for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, we obtain

\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}U\right\|\,dx\leq\log\sqrt{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}C=\log\sqrt{m}+Cm,

where
C
is the same as in (
iii
).
Thus, (
iv
) holds and
C_{\eqref{W4}}\leq\log\sqrt{m}+C_{\eqref{W3}}m
.

(
iv
)
\Rightarrow
(
v
): This is obvious.

(
v
)
\Leftrightarrow
(
vi
):
Let us first change the integration variable in (
v
) to
y
.
Taking both sides of (
v
) to power
p
and using the usual rules,
we can further replace
\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q}\|
by
\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q}\|^{p}
.
On the other hand, we have

\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q}\right\|^{p}=\left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\sim\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx.

Observe that, if
A\leq cB
, where
c
is a positive constant,
then
\log_{+}A\leq\log_{+}c+\log_{+}B
. From these three observations,
the equivalence (
v
)
\Leftrightarrow
(
vi
) easily follows.

(
vi
)
\Rightarrow
(
vii
): This is immediate from the definition and the estimate
\log\leq\log_{+}
.

(
vii
)
\Rightarrow
(
viii
): This was proved in Lemma
3.5
.

(
viii
)
\Rightarrow
(
ii
):
This follows, with
C_{\eqref{W2}}\leq C_{\eqref{W5}}
,
from applying (
viii
) to
M=[O_{m,m-1}\ \vec{z}]
.

We have thus proved that
(
i
)
\Leftrightarrow
(
ii
)
\Rightarrow
(
iii
)
\Rightarrow
(
iv
)
\Rightarrow
(
v
)
\Leftrightarrow
(
vi
)
\Rightarrow
(
vii
)
\Rightarrow
(
viii
)
\Rightarrow
(
ii
),
and this shows that all these conditions are equivalent,
which completes the proof of Proposition
3.7
.
∎

The following proposition gives an alternative way of computing the
A_{p,\infty}
characteristic, which is sometimes useful.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
W
be a matrix weight.
Assume, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left(\int_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\in L^{1}(Q).

Then

(3.7)

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\sup_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{Q}:=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{H:\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\to M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\text{ measurable}:\ \fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy\neq 0,

\displaystyle\qquad\log_{+}\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)H(\cdot)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\in L^{1}(Q)\Bigg\}.

Proof.

Taking
H=W^{-\frac{1}{p}}
, we find that the right-hand side of (
3.7
)
is at least as big as
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}
.
Now, we show that they are equal.
By the definition of
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}

and Jensen’s inequality, we have,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
H\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)H(y)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy.

The proof of Proposition
3.7
is finished
after dividing both sides by the last factor
and taking supremum over all functions
H\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}
and all cubes
Q
.
∎

As a consequence of the previous result, we obtain the following distributional estimate.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
W\in A_{p,\infty}
,
and
\{A_{Q}\}_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}
be a family of
reducing operators of order
p
for
W
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C
,
depending only on
m
and
p
, such that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
M\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\left|\left\{y\in Q:\ \left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\geq e^{M}\right\}\right|\leq\frac{\log(C[W]_{A_{p,\infty}})}{M}|Q|.

Proof.

Let cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
M\in(0,\infty)
be fixed.
We make use of Proposition
3.8
with

\displaystyle H:=W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\mathbf{1}_{Q\cap E_{Q}}+A_{Q}^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{Q\setminus E_{Q}},

where

\displaystyle E_{Q}:=\left\{y\in Q:\ \left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\geq e^{M}\right\}.

We first prove that
H
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{Q}
defined in Proposition
3.8
.
By the definition of
H
, we obtain

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy=\frac{1}{|Q|}\left[|E_{Q}|+\int_{Q\setminus E_{Q}}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dy\right],

which, together with Definition
2.6
(ii) and Lemma
2.9
, further implies that

(3.8)

\displaystyle 0<\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy\leq 1+\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dy\sim 1+\left\|A_{Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\sim 1.

Moreover, for any
y\in Q
,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)H(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(y)\log_{+}\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbf{1}_{Q\setminus E_{Q}}(y)\log_{+}\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx\right),

where the first term is in
L^{1}(Q)
by the definition of
W\in A_{p,\infty}

and the second term is clearly in the same space as an indicator function times a constant.
These, combined with (
3.8
), further imply that
H\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}
.

From Lemma
2.9
and the definitions of
H
and
E_{Q}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)H(y)\right\|^{p}dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\sim\exp\left\{\fint_{Q}\log\left[\|A_{Q}H(y)\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\left\{\int_{E_{Q}}\log\left[\left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy+\int_{Q\setminus E_{Q}}\log\left(\left\|A_{Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\right)\,dy\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\exp\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{E_{Q}}M\,dy\right)=\exp\left(\frac{|E_{Q}|M}{|Q|}\right),

which, together with (
3.8
) and Proposition
3.8
, further implies that

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}\gtrsim\exp\left(\frac{|E_{Q}|M}{|Q|}\right),

from which the claimed bound follows by taking logarithms of both sides.
This finishes the proof of Corollary
3.9
.
∎

4
Self-improvement properties

The following proposition is another equivalent characterization of

A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights. It can be seen as a self-improvement property in the sense that the integrability of a logarithm, as in the equivalent conditions of Proposition
3.7
, already implies the integrability of a positive power of the same function.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
W
be a matrix weight.
Then
W\in A_{p,\infty}
if and only if
there exists
u\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|^{u}\,dx<\infty,

where
A_{Q}
is the reducing operator of order
p
for
W
.

Proof.

We prove that (
4.1
) is equivalent to condition (
v
) of Proposition
3.7
,
which is equivalent to
W\in A_{p,\infty}
by the said proposition.
From the elementary inequality
\log_{+}t\leq(es)^{-1}t^{s}
for any
t,s\in(0,\infty)
,
it is immediate that (
4.1
) implies condition (
v
) of Proposition
3.7
.

The converse implication is more delicate
and involves an argument somewhat in the spirit of the John–Nirenberg inequality
for the exponential integrability of BMO functions.
Condition (
v
) of Proposition
3.7
is clearly equivalent to

\displaystyle M:=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\,dx<\infty.

Let cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be fixed.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
Q
is a dyadic cube.
Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset Q
be its maximal dyadic subcubes such that,
for any
i\in I
,

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\,dx>2M.

This, combined with the definition of
M
, further implies that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}|Q_{i}|

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2M}\sum_{i\in I}\int_{Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2M}\int_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2M}M|Q|=\frac{1}{2}|Q|.

For any
i\in I
, let
\widehat{Q}_{i}:=\min\{R\in\mathscr{Q}:\ Q_{i}\subsetneqq R\}
.
By the maximality, we obtain, for any
i\in I
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\,dx\leq 2^{n}\fint_{\widehat{Q}_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\,dx\leq 2^{n+1}M.

We can then write

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q}\right\|=\mathbf{1}_{Q\setminus\bigcup_{i}Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q}\right\|+\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q}\right\|.

From the definition of
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem
(see, for instance,
[
17
, Corollary 2.1.16]
), we deduce that,
for any
x\notin\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_{i}
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\leq 2M.

On the other hand, for any
i\in I
and
x\in Q_{i}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\leq\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q_{i}}\right\|+\log_{+}\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}A_{Q}\right\|,

where, for any
y\in Q_{i}
,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}A_{Q}\right\|\leq\log_{+}\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|+\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q}\right\|.

Taking the average over
y\in Q_{i}

and using (
4.3
) for the second term, we obtain

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}A_{Q}\right\|\leq\fint_{Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|\,dy+2^{n+1}M.

Finally, we use again the elementary inequality
\log_{+}t\leq(ep)^{-1}t^{p}

for any
t,p\in(0,\infty)
and Lemmas
2.3
and
2.9
to find that

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|\,dy\lesssim\fint_{Q_{i}}\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy\sim\left\|A_{Q_{i}}^{-1}A_{Q_{i}}\right\|^{p}=1.

Combining the previous few estimates, we have checked that
there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{M}
such that,
for any
i\in I
and
x\in Q_{i}
,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q}\right\|\leq\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q_{i}}\right\|+\widetilde{M}.

This, together with (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), further implies that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2M\mathbf{1}_{Q\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_{i}}+\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}}\left(\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q_{i}}\right\|+\widetilde{M}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbf{1}_{Q}+\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q_{i}}\right\|,

where
C:=\max\{2M,\widetilde{M}\}
and
\sum_{i\in I}|Q_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|Q|
by (
4.2
).

Each term on the right-hand side of (
4.6
) has the same form as
the expression on the left-hand side,
and hence we can iterate this estimate. After infinitely many iterations, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{Q}\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q}\right\|\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{k}}=C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{k}\setminus\Omega_{k+1}},

where
\Omega_{0}=Q
and
\Omega_{1}=\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_{i}

are the cubes constructed in the first step with
|\Omega_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\Omega_{0}|

and, in general,
\Omega_{k}\subset\Omega_{k-1}
is a union of cubes
with
|\Omega_{k}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\Omega_{k-1}|\leq\cdots\leq 2^{-k}|Q|
.
From these, we infer that, if
u\in(0,\frac{\log 2}{C})
, then

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|^{u}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{Q}\exp\left(u\log_{+}\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}\right\|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{Q}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{uC(k+1)}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{k}\setminus\Omega_{k+1}}\,dx\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{uC(k+1)}2^{-k}<\infty.

This finishes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Now, we can show two basic properties of

A_{p}
-matrix weights and
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights.
Recall that, when
m=1
, we have
A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C})=A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C})=A_{\max\{1,p\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;
hence part (
ii
) of the following proposition
recovers the classical identity

A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\bigcup_{q\in[1,\infty)}A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for scalar weights
and can be seen as a generalisation of this identity to the case of matrix weights.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then the following two statements hold.

(i)

If
q\in(p,\infty)
, then
A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subset A_{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
,
depending only on
m
,
p
, and
q
, such that, for any matrix weight
W
,

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\leq C[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}.

(ii)

It holds that
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subsetneqq A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subset\bigcup_{q\in(0,\infty)}A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
,
depending only on
m
and
p
, such that, for any matrix weight
W
,

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\leq C[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})},

where
C=1
when
p\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

We first prove (i).
Let us begin by recalling a useful estimate.
By the proof of
[
27
, Lemma 2]

(in which the two exponents, used in reverse roles in
[
27
, Lemma 2]

compared to the present situation, satisfy
1\leq p<q<\infty
,
but its proof still works for any
0<p<q<\infty
),
we obtain, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\|W^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\right\|^{q}\lesssim\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}.

This, combined with Definition
3.1
, further implies that

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\lesssim[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}.

This finishes the proof of (i).

Now, we show (ii). We first prove

A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subset A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
To this end, we consider the following two cases on
p
.

Case 1)

p\in(0,1]
. In this case,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\displaystyle F(Q,y):=\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx.

Then, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log F(Q,y)\,dy\right)\leq\mathop{\operatorname{ess\,sup}}_{y\in Q}F(Q,y)

and hence
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\leq[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}
.
This finishes the proof of
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subset A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
in this case.

Case 2)

p\in(1,\infty)
. In this case, by Jensen’s inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log F(Q,y)\,dy\right)=\left\{\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left\{[F(Q,y)]^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\right\}\,dy\right)\right\}^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\left\{\fint_{Q}[F(Q,y)]^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\,dy\right\}^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}},

which, together with (
2.4
), further implies that

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\leq\left[W^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\right]_{A_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\sim[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}.

This finishes the proof of
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subset A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
in this case.

Next, we show
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\neq A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
We choose
q\in(n(p-1)_{+},\infty)
. From Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle w(\cdot):=|\cdot|^{q}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus A_{\max\{1,p\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This, combined with Lemma
3.2
, further implies that

\displaystyle W:=wI_{m}\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\setminus A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m}).

This finishes the proof of
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\neq A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.

Finally, we prove
A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subset\bigcup_{q\in(0,\infty)}A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
Let
W\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
By Proposition
4.1
and Lemmas
2.3
and
2.9
, we find that
there exists
u\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{Q}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right]^{u}\,dy<\infty.

This, together with (
2.4
), (
4.7
),
and Hölder’s inequality, further implies that,
for any
q\in(p,\infty)
with
\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}\leq u
,

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}

\displaystyle\sim\left[W^{-\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\right]_{A_{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\fint_{Q}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\right\|^{q}\,dx\right]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\,dy\right\}^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\fint_{Q}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\,dy\right\}^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\fint_{Q}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right]^{u}\,dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{u}}<\infty

and hence
W\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
This finishes the proof of
A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})\subset\bigcup_{q\in(0,\infty)}A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
and hence (ii),
which completes the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

5
Reverse Hölder inequalities

One of the most important properties of classical
A_{\infty}
weights is the reverse Hölder inequality that goes back to
[
6
]
.
To establish a reverse Hölder inequality for
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights,
we need several technical lemmas.
The following conclusion is the reverse Hölder inequality of scalar weights
(see, for instance,
[
23
, Theorem 2.3]
or
[
22
, Theorem 2.3(a)]
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
w
be a scalar weight satisfying

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}:=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\mathcal{M}(w\mathbf{1}_{Q})(x)\,dx<\infty,

where
\mathcal{M}
is the same as in (
1.1
).
Then, for any

\displaystyle r\in\left[1,1+\frac{1}{2^{n+1}[w]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}-1}\right]

and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\fint_{Q}[w(x)]^{r}\,dx\leq 2\left[\fint_{Q}w(x)\,dx\right]^{r}.

For any scalar weight
w
, from Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem
(see, for instance,
[
17
, Corollary 2.1.16]
), we deduce that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\mathcal{M}(w\mathbf{1}_{Q})(x)\,dx\geq\int_{Q}w(x)\,dx=w(Q)

and hence
[w]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}\in[1,\infty]
.

The following lemma is just
[
22
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a positive constant
\widetilde{C}
such that, for any scalar weight
w
,

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}\leq\widetilde{C}[w]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Next, we establish a useful relation
between scalar weights and matrix weights.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
W\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
Then, for any nonzero matrix
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
, the scalar function

\displaystyle w_{M}:=\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}M\right\|^{p}

satisfies
w_{M}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

[w_{M}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}
,
and

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}:=\sup_{M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{O_{m}\}}[w_{M}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}\leq\widetilde{C}[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})},

where
\widetilde{C}
is the same as in Lemma
5.2
.

Proof.

Let
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be nonzero.
By the definition of matrix weights, we find that
w_{M}
is a scalar weight.
From Definition
2.6
(ii), we infer that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, any
x\in Q
, and almost every
y\in Q
,

\displaystyle w_{M}(x)\leq\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)M\right\|^{p}

and hence

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}w_{M}(x)\,dx\leq\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)M\right\|^{p},

which further implies that

\displaystyle\log\left(\fint_{Q}w_{M}(x)\,dx\right)+\log\left\{[w_{M}(y)]^{-1}\right\}\leq\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right).

By this and
W\in A_{p,\infty}
,
we conclude that
\log_{+}(w_{M}^{-1})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and

\displaystyle[w_{M}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\fint_{Q}w_{M}(x)\,dx\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left\{[w_{M}(y)]^{-1}\right\}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}.

This, combined with Lemma
5.2
, further implies that

w_{M}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}\leq\widetilde{C}\sup_{M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{O_{m}\}}[w_{M}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\widetilde{C}[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})},

which completes the proof of Lemma
5.3
.
∎

A definition similar in form to
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}
can be found in

[
30
, (1.5)]
or
[
27
, p. 3087]
:
For any
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}
,
define the scalar weight
w_{\vec{z}}:=|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\vec{z}|^{p}
.
For any matrix weight
W
, define

\displaystyle\widetilde{[W]}_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}:=\sup_{\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}}\left[w_{\vec{z}}\right]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}.

The following conclusion shows that
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}

and
\widetilde{[W]}_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}
are equivalent.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
,
depending only on
m
and
p
, such that, for any matrix weight
W
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{[W]}_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}\leq[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}\leq C\widetilde{[W]}_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}.

Proof.

We first show the first inequality of (
5.1
).
Let
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{\mathbf{0}}\}
and

M_{\vec{z}}:=[O_{m,m-1}\ \vec{z}]
.
Then
M_{\vec{z}}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
is nonzero and
from (
2.1
), we deduce that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle w_{M_{\vec{z}}}(x)=\left\|\left[O_{m,m-1}\ W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{z}\right]\right\|^{p}=\left|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{z}\right|^{p}=w_{\vec{z}}(x).

This, together with the definition of

[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}
, further implies that

\displaystyle\left[w_{\vec{z}}\right]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}=\left[w_{M_{\vec{z}}}\right]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}\leq[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}},

which completes the proof of the first inequality of (
5.1
).

Next, we prove the second inequality of (
5.1
).
Let
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be nonzero.
Let
\{\vec{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be any orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
From [86, Lemma 3.2], we infer that, for any matrix
A\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle\|A\|\sim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|A\vec{e}_{i}\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This, combined with the definitions of
[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}

and
\widetilde{[W]}_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}
,
further implies that, for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle w_{M}(Q)\sim\int_{Q}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}(x)\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}(Q)

and

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\mathcal{M}(w_{M}\mathbf{1}_{Q})(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\sim\int_{Q}\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}\right)(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{M}(w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{Q})(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}\right]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{[W]}_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}(Q),

where
[w_{M\vec{e}_{i}}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}:=0
if
M\vec{e}_{i}=\vec{\mathbf{0}}
.
Therefore,
[w_{M}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}\lesssim\widetilde{[W]}_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}
.
This finishes the proof of Proposition
5.4
.
∎

The following conclusion is a simple application of Lemma
5.3
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then

(i)

for any
W\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, one has

[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\in[1,\infty)
;

(ii)

for any
W\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, one has

[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{O_{m}\}
.
Then, by Lemma
5.3
and
[
17
, Proposition 7.3.2(4)]
, we find that,
for any
W\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
,

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\geq[w_{M}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq 1.

This finishes the proof of (i).

Now, we show (ii).
Let
W\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
Using (i) and Proposition
4.2
(ii), we obtain, if
p\in(0,1]
, then

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\geq[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\geq 1.

On the other hand, if
p\in(1,\infty)
, then, from Hölder’s inequality, it follows that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x\in Q
,

\displaystyle\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dy\right]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

and hence

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|^{p}\,dx\leq[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy.

This further implies that
[W]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}\geq 1
,
which completes the proof of (ii) and hence Proposition
5.5
.
∎

Using Lemma
5.3
and the reverse Hölder inequality of scalar weights,
we obtain the following reverse Hölder inequality of
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights.
The case of
A_{p}
-matrix weights is well known, and the present proof is analogous; both results are based on the facts that the inequality holds for
A_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{sc}}
-matrix weights (cf.
[
29
, Lemma 2]
), and that both
A_{p}
and
A_{p,\infty}
are contained in
A_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{sc}}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
W\in A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, and
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then, for any

(5.2)

\displaystyle r\in\left[1,1+\frac{1}{2^{n+1}[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}-1}\right]

and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{pr}\,dx\leq 2\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)M\right\|^{p}\,dx\right]^{r}.

Proof.

If
M
is a zero matrix, then the present lemma is obvious,
so it is enough to consider the nonzero matrix
M
.
By Lemma
5.3
, we find that the scalar weight

\displaystyle w_{M}:=\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}M\right\|^{p}

satisfies
w_{M}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
[w_{M}]_{A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{*}\leq[W]_{A_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})}^{\mathrm{sc}}
.
From this and Lemma
5.1
, we infer that,
for any
r
in (
5.2
) and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}[w_{M}(x)]^{r}\,dx\leq 2\left[\fint_{Q}w_{M}(x)\,dx\right]^{r}.

This finishes the proof of Proposition
5.6
.
∎

The following corollary is also useful.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
W\in A_{p,\infty}
,
and
\{A_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}
be a sequence of
reducing operators of order
p
for
W
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

There exists a positive constant
C
,
depending only on
m
and
p
, such that, for any
r
as in (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{pr}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq C.

(ii)

There exists a positive constant
C
,
depending only on
m
,
p
, and
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}
,
such that, for any
r
as in (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\left[\fint_{Q}\sup_{R\in\mathscr{Q},\,x\in R\subset Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{R}^{-1}\right\|^{pr}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq C.

Proof.

From Proposition
5.6
and Lemma
2.9
, we deduce that,
for any
r
in (
5.2
)
and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{pr}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}<2\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx\sim\left\|A_{Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}=1.

This finishes the proof of (i).

Applying Lemma
3.10
and an argument similar to that used in
the proof of
[
4
, Lemma A.32]
(see also
[
26
, Lemma 3.6]
), we obtain (ii).
This finishes the proof of Corollary
5.7
.
∎

We conclude this section by indicating an application of Corollary
5.7

to the boundedness of certain pointwise multipliers
acting on certain subspaces of
L^{p}\ell^{q}
,
the space of sequences
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}

of measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\left\|\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{L^{p}\ell^{q}}:=\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{j}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L^{p}}<\infty,

with usual modification if
q=\infty
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathscr{Q}_{j}
be the collection of
dyadic cubes of edge length
2^{-j}
and,
for any nonnegative measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

or any
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, let

(5.3)

E_{j}(f):=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}_{j}}\left[\fint_{Q}f(x)\,dx\right]\mathbf{1}_{Q}

be the related conditional expectation.
Repeating an argument used in the proof of
[
15
, Corollary 3.8]

with
[
15
, Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3]
replaced by Corollary
5.7
,
we obtain the following conclusion; we omit the details.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty]
,
W\in A_{p,\infty}
,
and
\{A_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}
be a sequence of
reducing operators of order
p
for
W
.
For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}:=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}_{j}}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|\mathbf{1}_{Q}.

Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that,
for any sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
nonnegative measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

or
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
,

\left\|\left\{\gamma_{j}E_{j}\left(f_{j}\right)\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{L^{p}\ell^{q}}\leq C\left\|\left\{E_{j}\left(f_{j}\right)\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{L^{p}\ell^{q}},

where
E_{j}
for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
is the same as in (
5.3
).

Corollary
5.8
extends
[
15
, Corollary 3.8]

from
A_{p}
to the larger weight class
A_{p,\infty}
.
The said
[
15
, Corollary 3.8]
played a key role in the theory of
Triebel–Lizorkin spaces with
A_{p}
-matrix weights developed in
[
15
]

and in its extension to Besov-type and Triebel–Lizorkin-type spaces in
[

]
.
Corollary

will be similarly used in our follow-up work,
where this theory is extended to the larger class of
A_{p,\infty}
weights.

6
Upper and lower dimensions of
A_{p,\infty}
weights

As observed in
[
3
,
15
,
33
]
,
several results in the theory of matrix-weighted function spaces
depend at the technical level on the possibility of replacing a reducing operator
A_{Q}

related to one cube
Q
by a similar operator
A_{R}
for a different cube
R
;
this, in turn, depends on estimates for the norms
\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\|
.
Several results may be obtained abstractly, by postulating suitable upper bounds
for the said norms, but it is also of interest to be able to relate such bounds
to the
A_{p}
or the
A_{p,\infty}
conditions of the matrix weight
W
.
In
[
3
]
, we introduced the concept of
A_{p}
-dimension of a matrix weight
and obtained the sharp control of
\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\|
in terms of this quantity
when
W\in A_{p}
. Our goal in this section is to extend these ideas to
the larger class of
A_{p,\infty}
-matrix weights.

First, we give some useful equivalences among three quantities
on the composition of reducing operators and its related matrix weight.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
,
W\in A_{p,\infty}
,
and
\{A_{Q}\}_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}
be a family of
reducing operators of order
p
for
W
.
Then, for all cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\sim\fint_{Q}\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left[\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\sim\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right),

where the positive equivalence constants
depend only on
m
,
p
, and
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.9
,
2.3
, and
3.5
,
we conclude that, for any cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\sim\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{R}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx=\fint_{Q}\left\|A_{R}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\sim\fint_{Q}\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\fint_{Q}\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left[\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right)\,dx.

From Lemmas
2.3
,
3.5
, and
2.9
, we infer that,
for any cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|A_{R}^{-1}A_{Q}\right\|^{p}\sim\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q}\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left[\left\|A_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\sim\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right).

These finish the proof of Proposition
6.1
.
∎

To obtain a sharp estimate on
\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\|
,
we introduce the following concepts of lower and upper dimensions.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
d\in\mathbb{R}
.
A matrix weight
W
is said to have
A_{p,\infty}
-lower dimension
d
,
denoted by
W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
,
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that,
for any cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
Q\subset R
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)\leq C\left[\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right]^{d}.

A matrix weight
W
is said to have
A_{p,\infty}
-upper dimension
d
,
denoted by
W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
,
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that,
for any
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{Q}\log\left(\fint_{\lambda Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)\leq C\lambda^{d}.

In what follows, if there exists no confusion,
we denote
\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
simply by

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}
and

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{m})
simply by

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}
.
We have the following basic properties on
A_{p,\infty}
-lower dimensions.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

For any
d\in(-\infty,0)
, one has

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}=\emptyset
.

(ii)

\bigcup_{d\in[0,n)}\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}=A_{p,\infty}
.

(iii)

For any
d\in[n,\infty)
, one has

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}=A_{p,\infty}
.

(iv)

For any
d_{1},d_{2}\in[0,n]

with
d_{1}<d_{2}
, one has

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}\subsetneqq\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{2}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
.

Proof.

We first prove (i). Let
d\in(-\infty,0)
. Assume there exists

W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}
.
By Definition
2.6
(iii), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which, together with Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem
(see, for instance,
[
17
, Corollary 2.1.16]
),
further implies that there exist
x_{0}\in Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}

such that
W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})
is invertible
and a sequence of cubes
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}
such that
the center of every
Q_{k}
is
x_{0}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\ell(Q_{k})=0
and such that

\displaystyle\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}^{-1}\right\|^{p}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\fint_{Q_{k}}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx.

By this, Lemmas
2.9
and
6.1
,
and the assumption that
W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}
, we find that

\displaystyle\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}^{-1}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\sim\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|A_{Q_{k}}A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}^{-1}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\sim\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp\left(\fint_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}\log\left(\fint_{Q_{k}}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{k\to\infty}\left[\frac{\ell(Q_{0,\mathbf{0}})}{\ell(Q_{k})}\right]^{d}=0

and hence all entries of
W(x_{0})
are
0
,
which contradicts the invertibility of
W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})
.
Thus,
\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}=\emptyset
.
This finishes the proof of (i).

Next, we show (ii).
By Definition
6.2
with
Q=R
, we obtain, for any
d\in[0,n)
,

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}\subset A_{p,\infty}
and hence

(6.1)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{d\in[0,n)}\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}\subset A_{p,\infty}.

On the other hand, let
W\in A_{p,\infty}
.
From Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
5.6
with
r\in(1,\infty)
as in (
5.2
),
we deduce that, for any cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
Q\subset R
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{pr}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left[\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right]^{\frac{n}{r}}\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\left[\fint_{R}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{pr}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2^{\frac{1}{r}}\left[\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right]^{\frac{n}{r}}\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\fint_{R}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2^{\frac{1}{r}}[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}\left[\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right]^{\frac{n}{r}}

and hence
W
has
A_{p,\infty}
-lower dimension
\frac{n}{r}\in(0,n)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle A_{p,\infty}\subset\bigcup_{d\in[0,n)}\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}.

This, combined with (
6.1
), further implies (ii).

Now, we prove (iii). Let
d\in[n,\infty)
.
By Definition
6.2
with
Q=R
, we obtain

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}\subset A_{p,\infty}
.
On the other hand, for any
W\in A_{p,\infty}
,
from the definition of
[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}
, we infer that,
for any cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
Q\subset R
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left[\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right]^{n}\exp\left(\fint_{R}\log\left(\fint_{R}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}\left[\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right]^{d}

and hence
W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{lower}}=A_{p,\infty}
,
which completes the proof of (iii).

The inclusion “
\subset
” in Claim (iv) follows directly from Definition
6.2
.
That this is a strict inclusion “
\subsetneqq
” follows from the examples given in Lemma
7.5
below.
This completes the proof of Proposition
6.3
.
∎

Now, we can give the following basic properties of
A_{p,\infty}
-upper dimensions.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

For any
d\in(-\infty,0)
,

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}=\emptyset
.

(ii)

\bigcup_{d\in[0,\infty)}\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}=A_{p,\infty}
.

(iii)

For any
d_{1},d_{2}\in[0,\infty)
with
d_{1}<d_{2}
,

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{upper}}\subsetneqq\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{2}}^{\mathrm{upper}}
.

(iv)

For any
p\in(0,1]
,
A_{p}\subset\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,0}^{\mathrm{upper}}
.

Although we mostly deal with
A_{p,\infty}
weights here, the last claim is intentionally stated for
A_{p}
weights only.

Proof.

We first prove (i). Let
d\in(-\infty,0)
.
Assume that there exists

W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}
.
By Lemma
3.5
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\cdot)A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}\right\|^{p}\right]\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which, together with Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem
(see, for instance,
[
17
, Corollary 2.1.16]
),
further implies that there exist
x_{0}\in Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}

such that
W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})
is invertible
and a sequence of cubes
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}
such
that the center of every
Q_{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
is
x_{0}

and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\ell(Q_{k})=0
and such that

\displaystyle\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}\right\|^{p}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp\left(\fint_{Q_{k}}\log\left[\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right).

By this, Lemmas
2.3
and
2.9
,
and
W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}
, we find that

\displaystyle\left\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp\left(\fint_{Q_{k}}\log\left[\left\|A_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\right]\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\sim\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp\left(\fint_{Q_{k}}\log\left(\fint_{Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp\left(\fint_{Q_{k}}\log\left(\fint_{Q_{x_{0}}}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\lim_{k\to\infty}[\ell(Q_{k})]^{-d}=0,

where
Q_{x_{0}}
is centered at
x_{0}
, has edge length
\sim 1
, and
Q_{0,\mathbf{0}}\subset Q_{x_{0}}
.
Hence all entries of
W^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})
are
0
,
which contradicts the fact that this is the inverse matrix of
W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x_{0})
.
Thus,
\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}=\emptyset
.
This finishes the proof of (i).

Now, we show (ii).
By Definition
6.2
with
\lambda=1
, we obtain, for any
d\in[0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}\subset A_{p,\infty}
and hence

(6.2)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{d\in[0,\infty)}\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}\subset A_{p,\infty}.

It remains to prove
A_{p,\infty}\subset\bigcup_{d\in[0,\infty)}\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d}^{\mathrm{upper}}
.
Let
W\in A_{p,\infty}
and
M\in(0,\infty)
, where
M
is determined later.
For any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\displaystyle h_{Q}(x):=\begin{cases}M&\text{if }x\in Q,\\
1&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Q.\end{cases}

For any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
by Proposition
3.8
with
Q
and
H
replaced,
respectively, by
2Q
and
h_{Q}A_{Q}^{-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\fint_{2Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}[h_{Q}(x)]^{p}\,dx\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(\fint_{2Q}\log\left(\fint_{2Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}[h_{Q}(y)]^{p}\,dx\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=:I(Q)\times II(Q).

From Lemma
2.9
and the definition of
h_{Q}
, it follows that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle[I(Q)]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\fint_{2Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx+(M^{p}-1)2^{-n}\fint_{Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\sim\left\|A_{2Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}+M^{p}-1

and

\displaystyle II(Q)

\displaystyle=\left[\fint_{2Q}\left\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\,dx\right]\exp\left(\fint_{2Q}\log\left\{[h_{Q}(y)]^{p}\right\}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\sim\left\|A_{2Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\exp\left(\frac{\log(M^{p})}{2^{n}}\right).

Thus, there exists a constant
C\in(0,1]
, depending only on
m
and
p
, such that,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq C\frac{\|A_{2Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\|^{p}}{\|A_{2Q}A_{Q}^{-1}\|^{p}+M^{p}-1}\exp\left(\frac{\log(M^{p})}{2^{n}}\right).

Choosing
M\in(0,\infty)
such that
CM^{\frac{p}{2^{n}}}=2[W]_{A_{p,\infty}}
, then,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has

, (
7.8
) does not hold;

(f)

for
d=a_{+}
, (
7.8
) holds
if and only if
a\in(-\infty,0)
or
b\in[0,\infty)
.

These, together with Proposition
7.3
(ii), further imply (ii),
which then completes the proof of Lemma
7.6
.
∎

Finally, we prove that the estimate of
\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\|
in terms of the upper and lower
A_{p,\infty}
-dimensions, as given in Proposition
6.5
, is
sharp
.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
d_{1}\in[0,n)
,
d_{2}\in[0,\infty)
, and
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

If, for any
W\in A_{p,\infty}
satisfying that

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(W)=d_{1}
,

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{upper}}(W)=d_{2}
,

W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
,
and
W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{2}}^{\mathrm{upper}}
,
there exists a positive constant
C
such that,
for any cubes
Q,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(7.9)

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q}A_{R}^{-1}\right\|^{p}\leq C\max\left\{\left[\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right]^{a},\left[\frac{\ell(Q)}{\ell(R)}\right]^{b}\right\}\left[1+\frac{|c_{Q}-c_{R}|}{\ell(Q)\vee\ell(R)}\right]^{c},

where
\{A_{Q}\}_{\mathrm{cube}\,Q}
is a family of
reducing operators of order
p
for
W
, then

\displaystyle a\in[d_{1},\infty),\ b\in[d_{2},\infty),\text{ and }c\in[d_{1}+d_{2},\infty).

(ii)

If, for any
W\in A_{p,\infty}
satisfying that

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(W)=d_{1}
,

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{upper}}(W)=d_{2}
,

W\notin\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
,
and
W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{2}}^{\mathrm{upper}}
,
(
7.9
) holds, then

\displaystyle a\in(d_{1},\infty),\ b\in[d_{2},\infty),\text{ and }c\in(d_{1}+d_{2},\infty).

(iii)

If, for any
W\in A_{p,\infty}
satisfying that

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(W)=d_{1}
,

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{upper}}(W)=d_{2}
,

W\in\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
,
and
W\notin\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{2}}^{\mathrm{upper}}
,
(
7.9
) holds, then

\displaystyle a\in[d_{1},\infty),\ b\in(d_{2},\infty),\text{ and }c\in(d_{1}+d_{2},\infty).

(iv)

If, for any
W\in A_{p,\infty}
satisfying that

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(W)=d_{1}
,

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{upper}}(W)=d_{2}
,

W\notin\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
,
and
W\notin\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{2}}^{\mathrm{upper}}
,
(
7.9
) holds, then

(7.10)

\displaystyle a\in(d_{1},\infty),\ b\in(d_{2},\infty),\text{ and }c\in(d_{1}+d_{2},\infty).

Proof.

Due to similarity, we only show (iv).
Let
x_{0}:=(1,0,\ldots,0)
and matrix weight
W:=wI_{m}
,
where
I_{m}
is identity matrix and, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

w(x):=w_{1}(x)[w_{2}(x)]^{-1}:=\widetilde{w}_{-d_{1},1}(x)[\widetilde{w}_{-d_{2},1}(x-x_{0})]^{-1}.

Now, we prove that
W
satisfies all assumptions of (iv).
We first show that
d_{\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(w)=d_{1}
and

w\notin\mathbb{D}_{\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
To this end, from Proposition
7.3
(i), it follows that
we need only to prove that

(a)

for any
d\in(d_{1},\infty)
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that,
for any
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(7.11)

\displaystyle\frac{w(Q)}{w(\lambda Q)}\leq C\lambda^{d-n};

(b)

for
d=d_{1}
, (
7.11
) does not hold.

Next, we show (a). Let
d\in(d_{1},\infty)
.
By
[
12
, Lemma 2.3(v)]
, Lemma
7.6
(i),
and Proposition
7.3
(i), we conclude that, for any
i\in\{1,2\}
,

w_{i}\in A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and,
for any
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(7.12)

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}w_{1}(x)\,dx\left\|w_{1}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\lambda Q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda Q|}{|Q|}\frac{w_{1}(Q)}{w_{1}(\lambda Q)}\fint_{\lambda Q}w_{1}(x)\,dx\left\|w_{1}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\lambda Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{n}\frac{w_{1}(Q)}{w_{1}(\lambda Q)}[w_{1}]_{A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{d}.

From
w_{1},w_{2}\in A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the Jones factorization theorem,
we infer that
w\in A_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
This, combined with Lemma
3.2
, further implies that
W\in A_{p,\infty}
.
By
w\in A_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, (
2.5
), (
7.12
),
and the definition of
[w_{2}]_{A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, we find that,
for any
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\frac{w(Q)}{w(\lambda Q)}

\displaystyle\sim\lambda^{-n}\fint_{Q}w(x)\,dx\fint_{\lambda Q}[w(x)]^{-1}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-n}\fint_{Q}w_{1}(x)\,dx\left\|w_{1}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\lambda Q)}\fint_{\lambda Q}w_{2}(x)\,dx\left\|w_{2}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{d-n}[w_{2}]_{A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This finishes the proof of (a).

Now, we show (b).
By Corollary
7.8
, we obtain,
for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
c_{Q}=\mathbf{0}
and
\ell(Q)<\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle w(Q)\sim|Q|\fint_{Q}w_{1}(x)\,dx\sim[\ell(Q)]^{n-d_{1}}\log[2+\ell(Q)^{-1}].

Thus, for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
c_{Q}=\mathbf{0}
and
\ell(Q)=\frac{1}{3}
,

\displaystyle\frac{w(\lambda Q)}{w(Q)}\sim\lambda^{n-d_{1}}\frac{\log(2+3\lambda^{-1})}{\log 5},

where

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log(2+3\lambda^{-1})}{\log 5}=\infty

and hence (
7.11
) does not hold if
d=d_{1}
.
This finishes the proof of (b) and hence
d_{\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(w)=d_{1}
and

w\notin\mathbb{D}_{\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
From these and Lemma
7.2
,
it follows that
d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(W)=d_{1}
and

W\notin\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
.
Applying an argument similar to that used in the proofs of both

d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{lower}}(W)=d_{1}
and

W\notin\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{1}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
, we obtain
d_{p,\infty}^{\mathrm{upper}}(W)=d_{2}
and

W\notin\mathbb{D}_{p,\infty,d_{2}}^{\mathrm{upper}}
.
Therefore,
W
satisfies all assumptions of (iv).

Applying an argument similar to that used in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 2.47]
,
we obtain (
7.10
).
This finishes the proof of Lemma
7.9
.
∎

Abstract

Introduction

Throughout

Recently, many people are trying to describe the singularity categories for some special classes of algebras,

An approach to investigate singularity categories is to
compare these categories between algebras related to each another.
This was studied in the context of idempotent reduction

,

[

[

Recall that the Gorenstein defect category is a Verdier quotient of the singularity category by modulo

in general, a triangle equivalence on singularity category can not produce such an equivalence on Gorenstein defect category.
So the result of Lu motivates
the following natural questions: Can we weaken the hypotheses of Lu
to just get the desired equivalence on singularity category, but not on Gorenstein defect category?
or, under what conditions do we have an equivalence

D_{sg}(\Lambda)\cong D_{sg}(A)\coprod D_{sg}(B)
? We answer the second question by the following theorem,
which is listed as Theorem
3.3
in this paper.

Theorem I.

Let
\Lambda=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
M&B\end{array}\right]

be an Artin algebra and
{}_{B}M_{A}
a
B
-
A
-bimodule. If

{}_{B}M
is semi-simple and
M_{A}
is projective,
then there is a triangle equivalence
D_{sg}(\Lambda)\cong D_{sg}(A)\coprod D_{sg}(B)
.

Theorem I can be used to reduce the singularity categories of certain bounded quiver algebras.

\Lambda=kQ/I
be a bounded quiver algebra
over a field

. Suppose there exist two disjoint full subquivers
of

\Gamma_{0}

\bar{\Gamma}_{0}

the syzygy of

Lemma 3.2
.

Keep the notations as above. Then
\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(\mathrm{Im}\widetilde{j}_{*},\mathrm{Im}\widetilde{i}_{*})=0
.

Proof.

For any
X^{\bullet}\in D_{sg}(A)
, there exists some
n\in\mathbb{Z}

and
X\in\mathrm{mod}A
such that
X^{\bullet}\cong X[n]
in
D_{sg}(A)
,
see
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
. Similarly, any object
Y^{\bullet}\in D_{sg}(B)

is isomorphic to
Y[m]
, for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}

and
Y\in\mathrm{mod}B
. Therefore, we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(\widetilde{j}_{*}(Y^{\bullet}),\widetilde{i}_{*}(X^{\bullet}))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(\widetilde{j}_{*}(Y[m]),\widetilde{i}_{*}(X[n]))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(\widetilde{j}_{*}(Y),\widetilde{i}_{*}(X)[n-m])

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X)[n-m]).

Now we will claim
\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(\widetilde{j}_{*}(Y^{\bullet}),\widetilde{i}_{*}(X^{\bullet}))=0
under three cases.

Case 1.

n-m=0
. Then it follows from
[
9
, Proposition 2.3]

or
[
18
, Example 2.3]
that
\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X))\cong\underrightarrow{\mathrm{lim}}_{l\geq 0}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(\Omega^{l}(j_{*}(Y)),\Omega^{l}(i_{*}(X)))
. Clearly,
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X))=\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}((0,Y,0),(X,0,0))=0
, and Lemma
3.1
implies that

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(\Omega^{l}(j_{*}(Y)),\Omega^{l}(i_{*}(X)))\in\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(\mathrm{Im}j_{*},\mathrm{Im}i_{*})=0,
for any
l\geq 1
.
Therefore, we get that
\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X))=0
.

Case 2.

n-m>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X)[n-m])

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y)[-n+m],i_{*}(X))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(\Omega_{\Lambda}^{n-m}(j_{*}(Y)),i_{*}(X))

\displaystyle\cong\underrightarrow{\mathrm{lim}}_{l\geq 0}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(\Omega^{l+n-m}(j_{*}(Y)),\Omega^{l}(i_{*}(X))).

By Lemma
3.1
, we get
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(\Omega^{l+n-m}(j_{*}(Y)),\Omega^{l}(i_{*}(X)))\in\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(\mathrm{Im}j_{*},\mathrm{Im}i_{*})
, which is equal to zero.
Therefore,
\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X)[n-m])=0
.

Case 3.

n-m<0
. Then we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X)[n-m])\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),\Omega_{\Lambda}^{m-n}(i_{*}(X))),

and we can prove
\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{sg}(\Lambda)}(j_{*}(Y),i_{*}(X)[n-m])=0
in the same way as Case 2.

∎

.

Let

be a

-

with

semi-simple and

projective, and let

.
Then

Proof.

From the right recollement

4
Applications and examples

In this section, we will apply our main result to algebras given by quivers with relations.
Let
k
be an algebraically closed field. Then any finite dimensional
k
-algebra
is isomorphic to
kQ/I
, where
Q
is a finite quiver and
I
is an admissible ideal.
A quiver
Q
is a quadruples
(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
, where
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are finite sets of vertices and
arrows,
s
and
t
are functions sending any arrow in
Q_{1}
to its starting point and ending point,
respectively. For arbitrary two arrows
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
with
t(\alpha_{1})=s(\alpha_{2})
, the composition of

\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{1}
is denoted by
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
A=kQ_{A}/I_{A}
and
B=kQ_{B}/I_{B}
be two finite-dimensional
algebras,
(Q_{\Lambda})_{0}=(Q_{A})_{0}\cup(Q_{B})_{0}
, and
(Q_{\Lambda})_{1}=(Q_{A})_{1}\cup(Q_{B})_{1}\cup\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in J}
where

J
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha_{i}
is an arrow from
(Q_{B})_{0}
to
(Q_{A})_{0}
for any
i\in J
.
Let
\Lambda=kQ_{\Lambda}/I_{\Lambda}
with
I_{\Lambda}
being the ideal
generated by
I_{A}
,
I_{B}
and
\alpha\alpha_{i}
, for any
\alpha\in(Q_{B})_{1}

and
i\in J
. Then
D_{sg}(\Lambda)\cong D_{sg}(A)\coprod D_{sg}(B).

Proof.

Since there are no arrows from
(Q_{A})_{0}
to
(Q_{B})_{0}
, we have that
\Lambda=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
M&B\end{array}\right]
with
M=e_{B}\Lambda e_{A}

being a
B
-
A
-bimodule, where

e_{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1_{A}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]
and
e_{B}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&1_{B}\end{array}\right]
.
To get the desired assertion, in view of Theorem
3.3
, it suffices to show that

{}_{B}M
is semi-simple and
M_{A}
is projective. These facts
were mentioned in
[
3
, Section 1]
under the case of Morita context algebra, which is an extension of triangular matrix algebra.
Here, we give a proof for readers’ convenience.

First, note that
\mathrm{rad}(_{B}M)=(\mathrm{rad}B)M=(\mathrm{rad}B)e_{B}\Lambda e_{A}
,
which is equal to zero because
\alpha\alpha_{j}\in I_{\Lambda}
, for any
\alpha\in(Q_{B})_{1}

and
i\in J
. Hence we get that
\mathrm{rad}(_{B}M)=0
and thus
{}_{B}M
is semi-simple.

For any
i\in J
, let
t_{i}:=t(\alpha_{i})
and let
e_{t_{i}}
be the idempotent
of
A
corresponding to the vertex
t_{i}
.
Let
p_{i1}+I_{A}
,
\cdots
,
p_{ir_{i}}+I_{A}
be a basis of
e_{t_{i}}A
, and let
f_{i}:e_{t_{i}}A\rightarrow M=e_{B}\Lambda e_{A}
be the morphism of right
A
-modules mapping

p_{ij}+I_{A}
to
\alpha_{i}p_{ij}+I_{\Lambda}
, for any
j=1,\cdots,r_{i}
. Since
I_{A}\subseteq I_{\Lambda}

, we get that
f_{i}
is well-defined. Now we will claim

f=(f_{i})_{i\in J}:\ \oplus_{i\in J}(e_{t_{i}}A)\rightarrow M
is an isomorphism of right
A
-modules,
and then

M_{A}
is projective. Let
q
be a path in

Q_{\Lambda}
from
(Q_{B})_{0}
to
(Q_{A})_{0}
, which is not in
I_{\Lambda}
. Since
(\mathrm{rad}B)e_{B}\Lambda e_{A}=0
,
there is some
i\in J
such
that
q=\alpha_{i}p
, where
p
is a path in
Q_{A}
starting form
t_{i}
. So
f
is surjective.
For any
(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}p_{ij}+I_{A})_{i\in J}\in\oplus_{i\in J}(e_{t_{i}}A)

with
a_{ij}\in k
,
if
f((\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}p_{ij}+I_{A})_{i\in J})=0
, then
\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}p_{ij}\in I_{\Lambda}
. Note that
I_{\Lambda}=\langle I_{A},I_{B},\mathcal{S}\rangle
,
where
\mathcal{S}=\{\alpha\alpha_{i}|i\in J\ \mbox{and}\ \alpha\in(Q_{B})_{1}\}
.
So there exist
u\in\langle I_{A}\rangle
,
v\in\langle I_{B}\rangle

and
w\in\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
such that
\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}p_{ij}=u+v+w
. Since
u,v
and
w
are linear combinations of paths
from
(Q_{B})_{0}
to
(Q_{A})_{0}
, we may assume that
u=\sum_{i\in J}k_{i}\alpha_{i}u_{i}
,

v=\sum_{i\in J}b_{i}v_{i}\alpha_{i}p_{i}
and
w=\sum_{i\in J}c_{i}\beta_{i}\alpha_{i}q_{i}
,
where
k_{i},b_{i},c_{i}\in k
,
u_{i}\in I_{A}
,
v_{i}\in I_{B}
,
\beta_{i}\in(Q_{B})_{1}
,
and
p_{i}
,
q_{i}
are paths in
Q_{A}
starting from
t_{i}
. Therefore,

\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}p_{ij}=\sum_{i\in J}k_{i}\alpha_{i}u_{i}+\sum_{i\in J}b_{i}v_{i}\alpha_{i}p_{i}+\sum_{i\in J}c_{i}\beta_{i}\alpha_{i}q_{i}

in
kQ_{\Lambda}
. Since all paths form a basis of
kQ_{\Lambda}
, we get that

b_{i}=0=c_{i}
for any
i\in J
, and
\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}p_{ij}=\sum_{i\in J}k_{i}\alpha_{i}u_{i}
. Then we have
\sum_{i\in J}\alpha_{i}(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}p_{ij})=\sum_{i\in J}\alpha_{i}(k_{i}u_{i})
in
kQ
,
which implies that
\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}p_{ij}=k_{i}u_{i}\in I_{A}
, for any
i\in J
. In conclusion,
we get that
(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}a_{ij}p_{ij}+I_{A})_{i\in J}=0
,
and then
f
is injective.
∎

Example 4.2
.

Let
\Lambda=kQ/I
be the algebra where
Q
is the quiver

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}

and
I=\langle\alpha^{3},\gamma^{2},\gamma\beta\rangle
. Let
A=e_{1}\Lambda e_{1}=k[x]/\langle x^{3}\rangle

and
B=e_{2}\Lambda e_{2}=k[x]/\langle x^{2}\rangle
. Then both
A
and
B
are selfinjective algebras,
and the algebras
A
,
B
and
\Lambda
satisfy all the conditions in Corollary
4.1
.
So
D_{sg}(\Lambda)\cong D_{sg}(A)\coprod D_{sg}(B)\cong\underline{\mathrm{mod}}(k[x]/\langle x^{3}\rangle)\coprod\mathrm{mod}k
.
We remark that
\Lambda
is a monomial algebra, and it follows from

[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
that there are only
two indecomposable objects in the stable category

\underline{\mathrm{Gproj}}\Lambda
.
However,
\Lambda
is non-Gorenstein and thus
the embedding
\underline{\mathrm{Gproj}}\Lambda\hookrightarrow D_{sg}(\Lambda)

is not an equivalence.
Indeed, there are three indecomposable objects in
D_{sg}(\Lambda)
by our calculation.

Remark 4.3
.

In general, the singular equivalence in Corollary
4.1
can not induces
an equivalence on Gorenstein defect category. Consider the above example.
The Gorenstein defect categories of
A
and
B
are zero, but this is not true for
\Lambda

since
\Lambda
is non-Gorenstein. Therefore, the Gorenstein
defect category of
\Lambda
is not equivalent to the direct sum of those of
A
and
B
.
However, if we replace

I=\langle\alpha^{3},\gamma^{2},\gamma\beta\rangle
by
I=\langle\alpha^{3},\gamma^{2}\rangle
,
then we get a singular equivalence
D_{sg}(\Lambda)\cong D_{sg}(A)\coprod D_{sg}(B)

which induces
an equivalence on the corresponding Gorenstein defect categories, see

[
14
, Corollary 4.4]
and
[
29
, Corollary 4.1]
.

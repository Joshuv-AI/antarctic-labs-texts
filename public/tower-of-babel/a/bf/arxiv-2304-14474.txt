A Chain Rule for the Expected Suprema of Bernoulli Processes Thanks: This research was suppoorted in part by DARPA under the Learning with Less Labels (LwLL) program.
Yifeng Chu, Maxim Raginsky

Abstract
We obtain an upper bound on the expected supremum of a Bernoulli process indexed by the image of an index set under a uniformly Lipschitz function class in terms of properties of the index set and the function class, extending an earlier result of Maurer for Gaussian processes. The proof makes essential use of recent results of Bednorz and Latała on the boundedness of Bernoulli processes.

Introduction

Sharp bounds on the suprema of Bernoulli processes are ubiquitous in applications of probability theory. For example, in statistical learning theory, they arise as so-called
Rademacher complexities

Lemma 1

For any

Lemma 2

(

For any

to control

Definition 1
.

Given a sequence of sets
(B_{m})_{m\geq 0}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert B_{0}}}\right\rvert=1
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert{B_{m}}}}\right\rvert\leq 2^{2^{m}}

has admissible cardinality.

Definition 2
.

Given a set

(\mathcal{A}_{m})
of

, every set of
\mathcal{A}_{m+1}
is contained in a set of
\mathcal{A}_{m}
. Then
an admissible sequence of
T
is an increasing sequence
(\mathcal{A}_{m})
of
partitions of
T
such that each
\mathcal{A}_{m}
has admissible cardinality.

Definition 3
.

Given a metric space
(T,d)
, let

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(T,d):=\inf\sup_{t\in T}\sum_{m\geq 0}2^{m/2}\Delta(A_{m}(t),d),

where the infimum is taken over all admissible sequences of
(T,d)
, and

A_{m}(t)
is the unique subset of
\mathcal{A}_{m}
containing
t
.

Now we state a bound on expected suprema of stochastic processes that satisfy a certain increment
condition. Note that it can be recognized as a minor modification of the
generic chaining bound
(see

[

]

Lemma 4
.

Let
(T,d)
be a metric space. Let
(Y_{t})_{t\in T}
be a stochastic process indexed by

\kappa\geq 1
such that, for all

and all

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[Y_{s}-Y_{t}>u}}\right]\leq\kappa\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2d(s,t)^{2}}}}\right).

(4)

Then, for any
\tau\in T
and for each admissible sequence
(\mathcal{A}_{m})
of
T

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sup_{t\in T}Y_{t}-Y_{\tau}}}\right]\lesssim\sup_{t\in T}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{m/2}\Delta(A_{m}(t),d)+\Delta(T,d)\sqrt{\log\kappa}.

(5)

In particular, if we take

, and
Y\colon t\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}\xi_{i}t_{i}
,
we can apply the result above with

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(T)\lesssim\gamma_{2}(T,d_{2}).

(6)

It turns out that (

Theorem 3
.

There exists an admissible sequence
\mathcal{A}_{m}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in T}\sum_{m\geq 0}2^{m/2}\Delta_{2}(A_{m}(t))\lesssim\mathfrak{g}(T).

Turning now to Bernoulli averages, let us define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}^{*}(T):=\inf\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sup_{t\in T_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert t}}\right\rVert_{1}+\gamma_{2}(T_{2},d_{2}):T\subset T_{1}+T_{2}}}\right\},

where

is the Minkowski sum,

T_{1}+T_{2}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{t_{1}+t_{2}:t_{1}\in T_{1},t_{2}\in T_{2}}}\right\}

Since

The main difficulty when working with Bernoulli processes

We give two examples to illustrate the use of our chain rule. The first example is an improvement of a result from

4

, which was an
intermediate step in bounding Rademacher complexities of neural nets with a uniform upper bound on the spectral norm of their weight matrices. Given an
n

\mathscr{Z}

\hat{\mathscr{R}}_{n}
of a class
\mathscr{G}

is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\mathscr{G})\equiv\hat{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\mathscr{G};z^{n}):=\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}g(z_{i})}}\right],

see, e.g.,
[
1
]
. Then we have the following result, which improves on Theorem 4 in
[
4
]
:

Proposition 2
.

For some
R>0
,
let
\mathscr{G}
be a class of functions from
\mathscr{Z}
to
[-R,R]
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be the class
of
L
-Lipschitz functions from
[-R,R]
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then the Rademacher complexity of the composite class

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\mathscr{F}\circ\mathscr{G})\lesssim L\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt{n}}+\hat{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\mathscr{G})}}\right).

Proof.

The result follows from
Theorem

2
with

and
T=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{(g(z_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq n}:g\in\mathscr{G}}}\right\}
. ∎

Remark 3
.

For comparison, the bound in
[
4
]
has the form

[
3

\mathsf{X}=\mathscr{B}^{k}_{2}(\sqrt{k}R)

Remark 4
.

See the Remark following

2
for the motivation behind our choices of
\mathsf{X}
and
T
. Note, however, that we use

1
to prove the above proposition; although one can use existing bounds on the covering numbers for subsets of an RKHS (compactly embedded into a suitable space of real-valued continuous functions) in conjunction with
Theorem

2

Proof.

Let
K_{x}
denote the function

and all

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle K_{x}-K_{y},f}}\right\rangle_{K}\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert f}}\right\rVert_{K}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert K_{x}-K_{y}}}\right\rVert_{K}\leq\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert f}}\right\rVert_{K}}{\sigma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert x-y}}\right\rVert_{2}.

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S}}\right\rVert_{\infty,\infty}\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert T}}\right\rVert_{\infty,\infty}

Proposition 4
.

For some
u,\varrho,\zeta>0,w\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
let

\displaystyle p(u):=\sum_{m\geq 1}2^{2^{m+1+w}}\mathrm{e}^{-u^{2}2^{m-1}}\mbox{ and }q(u):=p(u)\wedge 1.

satisfies
\mathbf{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[Y>u\varrho+\zeta}}\right]\leq q(u)

Observe that

m\geq 1

\displaystyle u^{2}2^{m-1}\geq\frac{u^{2}}{2}+u^{2}2^{m-2}\geq\frac{u^{2}}{2}+2^{m+1+w},

\displaystyle q(u)=(p(u)\mathbf{1}_{\{u\geq C_{w}\}}+p(u)\mathbf{1}_{\{u<C_{w}\}})\wedge 1\leq c\mathbf{1}_{\{u\geq C_{w}\}}\mathrm{e}^{-u^{2}/2}+\mathbf{1}_{\{u<C_{w}\}}.

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}Y=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[Y>y}}\right]\mathrm{d}y=\int_{-\zeta/\varrho}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[Y>u\varrho+\zeta}}\right]\,\varrho\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\int_{-\zeta/\varrho}^{\infty}q(u)\,\varrho\,\mathrm{d}u\leq C_{w}\varrho+\zeta,

which concludes the proof.
∎

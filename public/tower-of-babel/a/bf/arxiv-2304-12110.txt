Exponential decay of the volume for Bernoulli percolation: a proof via stochastic comparison
Hugo Vanneuville

Abstract
Let us consider subcritical Bernoulli percolation on a connected, transitive, infinite and locally finite graph. In this paper, we propose a new (and short) proof of the exponential decay property for the volume of clusters. We do not rely on differential inequalities and rather use stochastic comparison techniques, which are inspired by several works including the paper An approximate zero-one law written by Russo in the early eighties.

Introduction

Let us consider
Bernoulli bond percolation
on a connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive and countably infinite graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
(e.g. the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
). ‘Locally finite’ means that the degree of each vertex is finite, and ‘vertex-transitive’ means that for all vertices
v,w
, there exists an automorphism of
\mathbb{G}
that maps
v
to
w
.

This model is defined as follows: each edge is declared
open
with some probability
p
and
closed
otherwise, independently of the other edges. We let
\mathbb{P}_{p}
denote the corresponding product probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{E}}
, where
1
means open and
0
means closed. In percolation theory, one is interested in the connectivity properties of the graph obtained by keeping only the open edges. We fix a vertex
o\in\mathbb{V}
once and for all, we let
\mathcal{C}_{o}
denote the cluster of
o
, i.e. the set of all vertices that are connected to
o
by an open path, and we let
|\mathcal{C}_{o}|
denote its cardinality. The
critical probability
is defined as follows:

p_{c}=\inf\big\{p\in[0,1]:\mathbb{P}_{p}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{o}|=+\infty\big]>0\big\}.

Let

\psi_{n}(p)=\mathbb{P}_{p}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{o}|\geq n\big],\quad\forall n\geq 0.

In this paper, we give a new proof of the following theorem, which states that the volume of
\mathcal{C}_{o}
has an exponential tail in the subcritical regime.

Theorem 1
.

For every
p<p_{c}
, there exist
c,C>0
such that

\forall n\geq 0,\quad\psi_{n}(p)\leq Ce^{-cn}.

To study the volume of clusters, it is relevant to introduce what is often referred to as a ‘ghost field’. By this, we just mean that, given some parameter
h\in(0,+\infty)
, we color every vertex
green

1-e^{-h}
, independently of the other vertices and of the bond percolation configuration. We let
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}

\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{E}}\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{V}}

1

\mathbb{P}_{p,h}=\big(p\delta_{1}+(1-p)\delta_{0}\big)^{\otimes\mathbb{E}}\otimes\big((1-e^{-h})\delta_{1}+e^{-h}\delta_{0}\big)^{\otimes\mathbb{V}}.

We let
\mathcal{G}
denote the set of green vertices and we consider the so-called
magnetization

m_{h}(p)=\mathbb{P}_{p,h}\big[\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}\neq\emptyset\big].

One can note that, for every

m_{h}(p)

\mathbb{P}_{p}[|\mathcal{C}_{o}|=+\infty]

Theorem 2
.

Let

\forall n\geq 0,\quad\psi_{n}(q)\leq\frac{1}{1-m_{h}(p)}\psi_{n}(p)e^{-hn}.

Proof of Theorem
1

Let

\psi_{n}(p)\overset{\text{Thm \ref{thm:exp}}}{\leq}\frac{1}{1-m_{h}(p^{\prime})}\psi_{n}(p^{\prime})e^{-hn}\leq Ce^{-hn},

Some ideas behind the proof

How can one control the volume of subcritical clusters? One can first notice that Theorem
1
is equivalent to the fact that decreasing
p
has the following
regularizing effect
:

If, for a given
p_{0}
,
|\mathcal{C}_{o}|
is finite
\mathbb{P}_{p_{0}}
-a.s., then for every
p<p_{0}
,
|\mathcal{C}_{0}|
has an exponential tail under
\mathbb{P}_{p}
.

To prove this, we will show that, given any
\varepsilon>0
and
p_{0}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{p_{0}}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{o}|=\penalty\ +\infty]=0
,
decreasing
p_{0}
by
\varepsilon
has more effect than conditioning on some well-chosen disconnection event
A
, essentially in the sense that
\mathbb{P}_{p_{0}-\varepsilon}
is stochastically smaller than
\mathbb{P}_{p_{0}}[\,\cdot\mid A]
. This disconnection event will be
A=\{\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\}
for some parameter
h
.

So the question is now: How can one compare the effect of conditioning on an event to that of slightly decreasing
p
? One can actually do this by exploring a configuration with law
\mathbb{P}_{p_{0}}[\,\cdot\mid A]
and estimating (at every step of the exploration) the probability that the next edge to be explored has some influence on
A
. If this probability is always small, then slightly decreasing
p
will have more effect than conditioning on
A
.

A. Explorations.

Let us fix a finite graph
G=(V,E)
and call
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{G}
and
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}
the analogues of
\mathbb{P}_{p}
and
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}
on the graph
G
.
3
3

3

I.e.
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{G}
is the product Bernoulli measure of parameter
p
on
\{0,1\}^{E}
and
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}
is the product Bernoulli measure on
\{0,1\}^{E}\times\{0,1\}^{V}
, of parameter
p
on the edges and
1-e^{-h}
on the vertices. The proofs in this section actually work in a more general setting, for instance by considering a probability space
(M,\mathcal{F},\rho)
and considering the measure
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{G}\otimes\rho
instead of
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}
.
We denote by
\omega
the elements of
\{0,1\}^{E}
and by
\eta
the elements of
\{0,1\}^{V}
. Moreover, we let
\vec{E}
be the set of all orderings
(e_{1},\dots,e_{|E|})
of

.

Definition 4
.

An

is a map

\begin{array}[]{rl}\textbf{{e}}:\{0,1\}^{E}&\longrightarrow\vec{E}\\
\omega&\longmapsto(\textbf{{e}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{e}}_{|E|})=\big(\textbf{{e}}_{1}(\omega),\dots,\textbf{{e}}_{|E|}(\omega)\big)\end{array}

such that
\textbf{{e}}_{1}
does not depend on
\omega

\textbf{{e}}_{k+1}

(\textbf{{e}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{e}}_{k})
and
(\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}},\dots,\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{k}})
.
4
4

4

This means that for every
k\in\{0,\dots,|E|-1\}
, there exists a function
\phi_{k}
such that
\forall\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E},\;\textbf{{e}}_{k+1}(\omega)=\phi_{k}\big(\textbf{{e}}_{1}(\omega),\dots,\textbf{{e}}_{k}(\omega),\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}(\omega)},\dots,\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{k}(\omega)}\big)
.
Given an exploration

,
(e,x)\in\vec{E}\times\{0,1\}^{E}
and

, we denote by
\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)
the event that

coincides with
(e,x)

\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)=\big\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}:\forall j\in\{1,\dots,k\},\textbf{{e}}_{j}=e_{j}\text{ and }\omega_{e_{j}}=x_{e_{j}}\big\}.

(In particular,
\textup{Expl}_{0}(e,x)=\{0,1\}^{E}
.) We also let
\textup{Expl}(e,x)=\textup{Expl}_{|E|}(e,x)
.

We recall that an

is a subset that satisfies (
\omega\in A

\forall e\in E,\quad\omega^{\prime}_{e}\geq\omega_{e}
)
\Rightarrow

\omega^{\prime}\in A
. Moreover, given two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\{0,1\}^{E}
, we say that
\mu

\mu[A]\leq\nu[A]
for every increasing subset

, and we denote this property by
\mu\preceq\nu
. Finally, we say that an edge
e\in E
is
pivotal
for a set
A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{E}\times\{0,1\}^{V}
and an element

if changing only the edge

in
\omega
(and not changing

.

B. The main intermediate lemma.

The key result of this section is the following lemma. We fix an exploration of
E
for the rest of the section.

Lemma 5
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
and
h\in(0,+\infty)
. Moreover, let
A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{E}\times\{0,1\}^{V}
be any non-empty set and let
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Assume that for every
(e,x)\in\vec{E}\times\{0,1\}^{E}
such that
A\cap\textup{Expl}(e,x)\neq\emptyset
, we have

\forall k\in\{0,\dots,|E|-1\},\quad\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}\big[\text{$e_{k+1}$ is pivotal for $A$}\;\big|\;A\cap\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]\leq\varepsilon.

Then,

\mathbb{P}_{p(1-\varepsilon)}^{G}\preceq\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}\big[\omega\in\cdot\;\big|\;A\big].

Remark 6
.

•

Recall that we use the letter
\omega
to denote the elements of
\{0,1\}^{E}
. So
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}[\omega\in\cdot\mid A]
denotes the marginal law on
\{0,1\}^{E}
of
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}[\,\cdot\mid A]
;

•

We have written
\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)
when we should have written
\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\times\{0,1\}^{V}
;

•

Let us take the opportunity of this remark to make the following observation that holds when
(e,x)
is in the image of
\omega\mapsto(\textbf{{e}}(\omega),\omega)
: In this case,
\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)=\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}:\forall j\in\{1,\dots,k\},\omega_{e_{j}}=x_{e_{j}}\}
. As a result,
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}[\,\cdot\mid\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)]
is the probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{E}\times\{0,1\}^{V}
that assigns the value
x_{e_{j}}
to
e_{j}
for every
j\leq k
and is still the product Bernoulli measure of parameter
p
on the other edges and of parameter
1-e^{-h}
on the vertices.

The proof of Lemma
5

Lemma 7
.

Let
q\in[0,1]
. Moreover, let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{E}
and assume that for every
(e,x)\in\vec{E}\times\{0,1\}^{E}
such that
\mu\big[\textup{Expl}(e,x)\big]>0
, we have

\forall k\in\{0,\dots,|E|-1\},\quad\mu\big[\omega_{e_{k+1}}=1\;\big|\;\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]\geq q.

Then,
\mathbb{P}_{q}^{G}\preceq\mu
.

Proof of Lemma
7
.

The result can be proven by induction on
|E|
. Throughout the proof, it is important to remember that
\textbf{{e}}_{1}
is constant. Let
A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{E}
be an increasing set. We want to prove that
\mu[A]\geq\mathbb{P}^{G}_{q}[A]
. We extend every
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\setminus\{\textbf{{e}}_{1}\}}
into two configurations
\omega^{0},\omega^{1}\in\{0,1\}^{E}
by setting
\omega^{i}_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}}=i
. We define
A^{0},A^{1}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{E\setminus\{\textbf{{e}}_{1}\}}
by
A^{i}=\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\setminus\{\textbf{{e}}_{1}\}}:\omega^{i}\in A\}
. Moreover, we define two probability measures
\mu^{0},\mu^{1}
on
\{0,1\}^{E\setminus\{\textbf{{e}}_{1}\}}
by
\mu^{i}=\mu[\omega_{|E\setminus\{\textbf{{e}}_{1}\}}\in\cdot\mid\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}}=i]
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by erasing
\textbf{{e}}_{1}
. By the induction hypothesis – applied to the explorations
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E\setminus\{\textbf{{e}}_{1}\}}\mapsto(\textbf{{e}}_{2}(\omega^{i}),\dots,\textbf{{e}}_{|E|}(\omega^{i}))
–, we have
\mu^{i}[A^{i}]\geq\mathbb{P}^{G^{\prime}}_{q}[A^{i}]
for every
i\in\{0,1\}
. As a result,

\mu[A]=\mu[\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}}=0]\mu^{0}[A^{0}]+\mu[\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}}=1]\mu^{1}[A^{1}]\\
\geq\mu[\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}}=0]\mathbb{P}_{q}^{G^{\prime}}\big[A^{0}\big]+\mu[\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}}=1]\mathbb{P}_{q}^{G^{\prime}}\big[A^{1}\big].

By the assumption of the lemma for
k=0
, we have
\mu[\omega_{\textbf{{e}}_{1}}=1]\geq q
. Moreover,
A^{0}\subseteq A^{1}
since
A
is increasing, so the above is larger than or equal to

(1-q)\mathbb{P}_{q}^{G^{\prime}}\big[A^{0}\big]+q\mathbb{P}_{q}^{G^{\prime}}\big[A^{1}\big]=\mathbb{P}^{G}_{q}[A].\qed

Proof of Lemma
5
.

Let us prove that the hypothesis of Lemma
7
holds with
\mu=\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}[\omega\in\cdot\mid A]
and
q=p(1-\varepsilon)
. Let
(e,x)
as in the statement of Lemma
5
. The third point of Remark
6
implies that, under
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}\big[\,\cdot\mid\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]
,
\omega_{e_{k+1}}
is independent of the event
A\cap\{\text{$e_{k+1}$ is not piv. for $A$}\}
(because the latter depends only on
\eta
and
\omega_{|E\setminus\{e_{k+1}\}}
). As a result,

\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}\big[\omega_{e_{k+1}}=1\;\big|\;A\cap\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]\\
\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}\big[\omega_{e_{k+1}}=1,A,\text{$e_{k+1}$ is not piv. for $A$}\;\big|\;\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]}{\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}\big[A\;\big|\;\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]}\\
=p\times\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G}\big[\text{$e_{k+1}$ is not piv. for $A$}\;\big|\;A\cap\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]\geq p(1-\varepsilon).

We end the proof by applying Lemma
7
.

Let
G_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})
denote the graph
\mathbb{G}
restricted to the ball of radius

around

(for the graph distance). We keep the notations
\mathcal{C}_{o}
and
\mathcal{G}
(the cluster of

. As in Section

\mathbb{P}_{p}^{G_{n}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}
are the analogues of
\mathbb{P}_{p}
and
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}
on the graph

\omega

Lemma 8
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
and
h\in(0,+\infty)
, and write
q=p(1-m_{h}(p))
. Then,

\forall n\geq 0,\quad\mathbb{P}_{q}^{G_{n}}\preceq\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[\omega\in\cdot\;\big|\;\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\big].

Proof.

Let us apply Lemma
5
, with
A=\{\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\}
and
\varepsilon=m_{h}(p)
. To this purpose, we define an exploration of
E_{n}
by fixing an arbitrary ordering of
E_{n}
and:

•

By first revealing
\mathcal{C}_{o}
(i.e. by revealing iteratively the edge of smallest index among all the edges that are connected to
o
by already revealed open edges). Here and below, ‘smallest index’ refers to the arbitrary ordering that we have fixed;

•

Then, by revealing all the other edges (once again by revealing iteratively the edge of smallest index).

Let
(e,x)\in\vec{E}_{n}\times\{0,1\}^{E_{n}}
such that
\{\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\}\cap\textup{Expl}(e,x)\neq\emptyset
. We observe that, if
\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)
holds and
e_{k+1}
is pivotal for
\{\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\}
, then one can write
e_{k+1}=\{v_{k+1},w_{k+1}\}
where:

•

v_{k+1}
is connected to

by open edges in
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
but
w_{k+1}
is not;

•

w_{k+1}
is connected to a green vertex by open edges that do not belong to
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k+1}\}
.

We let
B_{k+1}
denote the event of the second item. By this observation and then the Harris--FKG inequality
5
5

5

If one does not want to use the Harris–FKG inequality in this paper, one can continue to explore the configuration with the same rule as before, except that

is not revealed until no other unrevealed edge is connected to

(see for instance
[

, Theorem 2.4]
or
[

, Lemma 3]
) applied to
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[\,\cdot\mid\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]
(which is a product of Bernoulli laws by the third point of Remark

, we obtain that

\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[\text{$e_{k+1}$ is piv.\ for $\{\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\}$}\;\big|\;\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset,\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]\\
\leq\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[B_{k+1}\;\big|\;\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset,\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]\\
\overset{\text{Harris--FKG}}{\leq}\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[B_{k+1}\;\big|\;\textup{Expl}_{k}(e,x)\big]=\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[B_{k+1}\big]\leq m_{h}(p),

where in the equality we have used the third point of Remark

. We conclude by applying Lemma

.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We note that
\psi_{n}(q)=\mathbb{P}_{q}^{G_{n}}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{o}|\geq n\big]
. As a result, Lemma
8
implies that

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(q)\leq\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{o}|\geq n\;\big|\;\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\big]

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Bayes}}{=}\frac{\psi_{n}(p)\times\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\;\big|\;|\mathcal{C}_{o}|\geq n\big]}{1-\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}\neq\emptyset\big]}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt=\frac{\psi_{n}(p)\times\mathbb{E}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[e^{-h|\mathcal{C}_{o}|}\;\big|\;|\mathcal{C}_{o}|\geq n\big]}{1-\mathbb{P}_{p,h}^{G_{n}}\big[\mathcal{C}_{o}\cap\mathcal{G}\neq\emptyset\big]}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\leq\frac{\psi_{n}(p)}{1-m_{h}(p)}e^{-hn}.\qed

Some motivations and some perspectives

I am not proving new results in this paper: even the quantitative result (i.e. Theorem
2
) can be deduced from earlier works (see
[
Hut20
]
, where Hutchcroft combines OSSS and ghost field techniques).

My initial goal, in the (not for publication) work
[
Van22
]
and the present paper, was to propose a proof of sharpness
without differential inequalities
. As explained in
[
DMT21
]
(where the authors propose a new proof – also without differential inequalities – of some scaling relations for 2D Bernoulli percolation), finding approaches that “[do] not rely on interpretations (using Russo’s formula) of the derivatives of probabilities
of increasing events using so-called pivotal edges” is interesting because “such formulas are unavailable in most dependent percolation models”. I have to admit that on the one hand I have found a proof of exponential decay without differential inequalities, but I have not succeeded in extending the proof to other percolation models. Gradually, my aim became more to propose a new point of view on sharpness properties, and as simple a proof of a sharpness phenomenon as I could do. During discussions about such motivations, several colleagues asked me whether these techniques could also be used to prove new results for Bernoulli percolation. This was not my aim but the question is legitimate! I think that the answer is yes and I would like to propose four examples.

A. A new inequality for critical exponents.

In
[
Van22
]
, we use similar stochastic domination techniques in order to prove an analogue of Theorem
2
for the diameter. More precisely, we prove that

\forall n\geq m,\quad\theta_{2n}\big(p-2\theta_{m}(p)\big)\leq C\frac{\theta_{n}(p)}{2^{n/m}},

(1)

where
\theta_{n}(p)
is the probability that there is a path from
o
to the sphere of radius
n
around
o
(for the graph distance). Let
\eta_{1}
and
\nu
denote the one-arm and the correlation length exponents (so in particular
\eta_{1}
is the exponent that describes the quantity
\theta_{n}(p_{c})
, see for instance
[
DM23
]
for precise definitions and for other inequalities involving these exponents). (
1
) implies the following inequality:

\eta_{1}\nu\leq 1.

(2)

Similar inequalities for critical exponents that describe the volume of clusters can be proven by using Theorem
2
, and were already proven in
[
Hut20
]
. However, (
2
) is new to my knowledge, and the reason why previous techniques (such as those from
[
Hut20
]
) do not seem to imply (
2
) is that the calculations behind them only seem to work when the exponent of the connection probability is at most
1
(see e.g.
[
Hut20
, Section 4.1]
). The critical exponent that describes
\psi_{n}(p_{c})
(which is
1/\delta
in
[
Hut20
]
) is always at most
1
, but this is not the case of
\eta_{1}
, which is
2
on trees for instance.

C. A new inequality for arm events in the plane, and an application to exceptional times in dynamical percolation.

In this paragraph, we consider Bernoulli percolation on a planar (symmetric) lattice. Let
\alpha_{k}(n)
denote the probability of the
k
-arm event from
o
to distance
n
, at the critical parameter (see e.g.
[
GS14
, Chapter 2]
for this terminology). The OSSS inequality implies that

n^{2}\alpha_{4}(n)\alpha_{2}(n)\geq c.

(3)

(See for instance
[
GS14
, Chapter 12, Theorem 40]
.) I believe that – if one overcomes some technical difficulties related to the arm separation techniques – one can prove the following by using stochastic domination techniques similar to those proposed in the present paper:

n^{2}\alpha_{4}(n)\alpha_{2}(n)\geq cn^{c}.

(4)

The latter inequality is not only a very slight improvement of (
3
). Indeed, as explained in
[
TV22
]
, (
4
) implies that – if one considers dynamical percolation at the critical parameter – there exist
exceptional times
when there are both primal and dual unbounded components.

The inequality (
3
) is known for very general planar lattices whereas (
4
) is known (to my knowledge) only for site percolation on the regular triangular lattice (
[
SW01
]
) and bond percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(see
[
DMT21
]
, where the authors use parafermionic observables). I believe that one could prove (
4
) by combining the following two results/observations:

1.

As proven by Kesten
[
Kes81
]
, the size of the near-critical window for planar percolation is
1/(n^{2}\alpha_{4}(n))
;

2.

The size of the near critical window is much less than
\alpha_{2}(n)
. How can one prove this? Let us define an exploration of some percolation event by following an interface that separates a primal macroscopic cluster from a dual macroscopic cluster. I believe that results analogous to Lemma
5
(together with RSW techniques) imply that the near-critical window is less than the infimum – on every vertex
x
and on every path
\gamma
– of the probability of the
4
-arm event at
x
, conditionally on the event
\{x\text{ belongs to the interface}
and
\text{$\gamma$ is the interface stopped at $x$}\}
. This probability is less than the
2
-arm probability in a space that has the topology of the half-plane, which can be strongly expected to be much smaller than
\alpha_{2}(n)
.

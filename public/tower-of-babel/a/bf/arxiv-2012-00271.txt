Upper semi-continuity of random attractors and existence of invariant measures for nonlocal stochastic Swift-Hohenberg equation with multiplicative noise


Abstract
In this paper, we mainly study the long-time dynamical behaviors of 2D nonlocal stochastic Swift-Hohenberg equations with multiplicative noise from two perspectives. Firstly, by adopting the analytic semigroup theory, we prove the upper semi-continuity of random attractors in the Sobolev space H 0 2 ​ ( U ) H_{0}^{2}(U) , as the coefficient of the multiplicative noise approaches zero. Then, we extend the classical “stochastic Gronwall’s lemma”, making it more convenient in applications. Based on this improvement, we are allowed to use the analytic semigroup theory to establish the existence of ergodic invariant measures. Keywords: Nonlocal stochastic Swift-Hohenberg model; multiplicative noise; random attractor; asymptotic compactness; upper semi-continuity; Markovian transition semigroup; Feller property; ergodic invariant measures. Introduction

In the research of geophysical fluid flows in atmosphere, oceans and the earth’s mantle,
there often arise fluid convective phenomena resulting from the density variations.
A prototypical model to describe these phenomena is the Rayleigh-Bénard convection,
to forecast the spatio-temporal convection patterns.
The mathematical model for the Rayleigh-Bénard convection involves nonlinear Navier-Stokes
partial differential equations coupled with the temperature equation.
When the Rayleigh number approaches the onset of the convection, the Rayleigh-Bénard convection model
may be approximately reduced to an amplitude or order parameter equation as derived by
Swift and Hohenberg (
[
1

The SHE has featured in different branches of physics, ranging from hydrodynamics to nonlinear optics,
such as the Taylor-Couette flow (

viscous film flow, lasers(

The localized one-dimensional version (see
[

where cubic term

When reexamining the rationale for using the Swift-Hohenberg model as a
reliable model of the spatial pattern evolution in specific physical systems,

4
Existence of Ergodic Invariant Measures

In this section, we consider the existence of ergodic invariant measures for the nonlocal stochastic SHE (
1.2
) in
H=L^{2}(U)
.
First we introduce some necessary notations and results.

4.1
Preliminaries for ergodic invariant measures

Let
X
be a Banach space with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}(X)
.
We use
{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm b}(X)
to denote the space of bounded Borel measurable functions on
X
.
In the following we rewrite the
X
-valued stochastic process
{\mathcal{X}}(t,\omega,x)
as
{\mathcal{X}}(t,x)(\omega)
for notational convenience with initial datum
x\in X
and
\omega\in\Omega
.
For a set
\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
, we define the
transition functions

{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,\Gamma)=\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{X}}(t,x)\in\Gamma)\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }t\geqslant 0.

The
Markovian transition semigroup

{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
on
{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm b}(X)
is defined as

{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi(x)=\mathbb{E}\varphi({\mathcal{X}}(t,x))=\int_{X}\varphi(y){\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,{\rm d}y),\hskip 11.38092ptt\geqslant 0,\;\varphi\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm b}(X),\;x\in X.

Note also that
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,\Gamma)={\mathcal{P}}_{t}\mathds{1}_{\Gamma}(x)
.
The dual semigroup
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}
of
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
is defined on and into the set of Borel probability measures
\nu
on
X
by

{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}\nu(\Gamma)=\int_{X}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,\Gamma)\nu({\rm d}x),\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for each }\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).

The Markovian transition semigroup
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
(
t\geqslant 0
) is said to be
Feller
if for arbitrary
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm b}(X)

and
t\geqslant 0
, the mapping
x\mapsto{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi(x)
is continuous.
An
invariant measure
for the stochastic process
{\mathcal{X}}(t,x)
is a probability measure
\nu
on
X
, which is a fixed point for
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}
, that is to say,

\int_{X}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,\Gamma)\nu({\rm d}x)=\nu(\Gamma),\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for each }\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)\mbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Let
\nu
be an invariant measure for
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
.
We say that
\nu
is
ergodic
if

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi{\rm d}t=\int_{H}\varphi(x)\nu({\rm d}x)\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }\varphi\in L^{2}(X,\mu).

For the discussion next, we need the following “stochastic Gronwall lemma” (see
[
18
, Lemma 5.3]
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
T>0
and assume that
{\mathcal{X}}
,
{\mathcal{Y}}
,
{\mathcal{Z}}
,
{\mathcal{R}}:[0,T)\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are real valued, non-negative stochastic processes.
Let
\tau<T
be a stopping time so that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s<\infty.

Assume moreover that for some fixed deterministic constant
\kappa
,

\int_{0}^{\tau}{\mathcal{R}}{\rm d}s<\kappa,\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{a.s.}

Suppose that

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{ for all stopping times }0\leqslant\tau^{\prime}<\tau^{\prime\prime}\leqslant\tau,\\
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)\leqslant C_{0}\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right),\end{array}

(4.1)

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant independent of the choice of
\tau^{\prime}
,
\tau^{\prime\prime}
.
Then

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{0}^{\tau}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}{\mathcal{Z}}{\rm d}s\right),

where
C=C(C_{0},T,\kappa)
is a positive constant.

For the practical applications in the following, we sometimes need to relax the conditions given in Proposition
4.1
.
Actually, the relaxed conditions are equivalent to those in Proposition
4.1
, the details of which is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
4.1
, the condition (
4.1
) is equivalent to the following condition:

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{there is }\varepsilon>0\mbox{ such that for all stopping times }0\leqslant\tau^{\prime}<\tau^{\prime\prime}\leqslant\tau\mbox{ with }\tau^{\prime\prime}-\tau^{\prime}<\varepsilon,\\
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)\leqslant C_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right),\end{array}

(4.2)

where
C_{\varepsilon}
depends on
\varepsilon
, but does not depend on the specific distributions of
\tau^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}

Proof.

Obviously, the condition (
4.1
) implies (
4.2
) by arbitrary choice of
\varepsilon>0
.
Now we show the converse.

Let (
4.2
) hold true.
Select a finite sequence of stopping times

0=\tilde{\tau}_{0}<\tilde{\tau}_{1}<\cdots<\tilde{\tau}_{N}<\tilde{\tau}_{N+1}=T

such that
\tilde{\tau}_{k}-\tilde{\tau}_{k-1}<\varepsilon
.
Pick arbitrarily
\tau^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}
with
0\leqslant\tau^{\prime}<\tau^{\prime\prime}\leqslant\tau
.
Surely, we have
0\leqslant k_{1}\leqslant k_{2}\leqslant N
such that

\tau^{\prime}\in[\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}},\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+1})\cap[0,\tau]\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{and}\hskip 11.38092pt\tau^{\prime\prime}\in(\tilde{\tau}_{k_{2}},\tilde{\tau}_{k_{2}+1}]\cap[0,\tau].

We claim that

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)\leqslant\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}\right)^{k_{2}-k_{1}+1}\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right),

(4.3)

which implies (
4.1
) immediately.

We show the claim by induction.
Firstly, by (
4.2
), since
\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+1}-\tau^{\prime}<\varepsilon
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[\tau^{\prime},\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+1}]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+1}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)\leqslant\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}\right)\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+1}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right).

Assume that for
i\in[1,k_{2}-k_{1}]
,

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[\tau^{\prime},\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i}]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)\leqslant\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}\right)^{i}\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right).

Then by (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[\tau^{\prime},\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i+1}\wedge\tau^{\prime\prime}]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i+1}\wedge\tau^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}\right)^{i}\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i},\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i+1}\wedge\tau^{\prime\prime}]}{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i+1}\wedge\tau^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{Y}}{\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}\right)^{i}\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right)\!+\!C_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\left({\mathcal{X}}(\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i})+\int_{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i}}^{\tilde{\tau}_{k_{1}+i+1}\wedge\tau^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{Z}}){\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi(u_{10})-{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi(u_{20})|=|\mathbb{E}(\varphi(u(t,u_{10}))-\varphi(u(t,u_{20})))|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(\varphi(u(t,u_{10}))-\varphi(u(t,u_{20})))\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10},u_{20})<t\}}|

\displaystyle+|\mathbb{E}(\varphi(u(t,u_{10}))-\varphi(u(t,u_{20})))\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{\hat{\kappa}}(u_{10},u_{20})<t\}}|

\displaystyle+|\mathbb{E}(\varphi(u(t,u_{10}))-\varphi(u(t,u_{20})))\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10},u_{20})\geqslant t\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{\hat{\kappa}}(u_{10},u_{20})\geqslant t\}}|

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3},

(4.21)

where
\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}
and
\hat{\tau}_{\hat{\kappa}}
are defined as (
4.9
) and (
4.11
), respectively.

Let
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{u\in H}|\varphi(u)|
be the sup-norm of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm b}(H)
and the distance of the initial data satisfy
\|u_{10}-u_{20}\|<1
.
We first consider
P_{1}
.
By Lemma
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle P_{1}\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10},u_{20})<t)\leqslant 2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\left(\mathbb{P}(\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10})<t)+\mathbb{P}(\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{20})<t)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}t[1+\|u_{10}\|^{2}+(\|h\|^{2}+1)t].

(4.22)

For
P_{2}
, by Lemma
4.6
, we have for each
\kappa_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle P_{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}_{\hat{\kappa}}(u_{10},u_{20})<t)\leqslant 2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\left(\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}_{\hat{\kappa}}(u_{10})<t)+\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}_{\hat{\kappa}}(u_{20})<t)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\hat{\kappa}^{-1}C_{1}(t,\kappa_{2})\|\varphi\|_{\infty}[1+\|u_{10}\|^{2}+(\|h\|^{2}+1)t+\|h\|^{2}t^{2}]

\displaystyle+\kappa_{2}^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}t[1+\|u_{10}\|^{4}+(\|h\|^{4}+1)t].

(4.23)

For arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
, by taking
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}
sufficiently large and fixed and then
\hat{\kappa}
sufficiently large and fixed in (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), we can ensure that

P_{1}+P_{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(4.24)

Now that we have fixed
\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}
and
\hat{\kappa}
, next we can delve into
P_{3}
.
For this, we approximate
\varphi
given above by a Lipschitz function
\tilde{\varphi}
(to be chosen below).
Observe that on the set
\{\hat{\tau}_{\hat{\kappa}}(u_{10},u_{20})\geqslant t\}
, we have
\|\Delta u(t,u_{i0})\|^{2}\leqslant\hat{\kappa}/t
.
As a result,
P_{3}
can be bounded as follows,

\displaystyle P_{3}\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\sup_{u\in\overline{\bf B}_{V}\left((\hat{\kappa}/t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\|\varphi(u)-\tilde{\varphi}(u)\|+|\mathbb{E}(\tilde{\varphi}(u(t,u_{10}))-\tilde{\varphi}(u(t,u_{20})))\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10},u_{20})\geqslant t\}}|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\!\!\!\sup_{u\in\overline{\bf B}_{V}\left((\hat{\kappa}/t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\!\!\!\|\varphi(u)-\tilde{\varphi}(u)\|+L_{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathbb{E}\|\left(u(t,u_{10})-u(t,u_{20})\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10},u_{20})\geqslant t\}}\|,

(4.25)

where
\overline{\bf B}_{V}(r)
is the closed ball in
V
centered at
0
with radius
r
, and
L_{\tilde{\varphi}}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\tilde{\varphi}
.

By the compact embedding of
V
into
H
, we know the closed ball

\overline{\bf B}_{V}\left((\hat{\kappa}/t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
is compact in
H
.
By the density of Lipschitz functions in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm b}\left(\overline{\bf B}_{V}\left((\hat{\kappa}/t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)
, we can find a Lipschitz function
\tilde{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm b}\left(\overline{\bf B}_{V}\left((\hat{\kappa}/t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)
such that

\sup_{u\in\overline{\bf B}_{V}\left((\hat{\kappa}/t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\|\varphi(u)-\tilde{\varphi}(u)\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{8}.

(4.26)

Now the choice of
\tilde{\varphi}
fixes
L_{\tilde{\varphi}}
.
Then by Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
4.7
, we have

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathbb{E}\|\left(u(t,u_{10})-u(t,u_{20})\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10},u_{20})\geqslant t\}}\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{\varphi}}\left(\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau_{\kappa_{1}}^{1}(u_{10},u_{20})]}\|u(t,u_{10})-u(t,u_{20})\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{2}(t,\kappa_{1})L_{\tilde{\varphi}}\|u_{10}-u_{20}\|.

(4.27)

Hence, for the above
\varepsilon
, when
\|u_{10}-u_{20}\|
is sufficiently small, we can guarantee that

C_{2}L_{\tilde{\varphi}}\|u_{10}-u_{20}\|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

(4.28)

Combining the estimations from (
4.21
) to (
4.28
), we see that for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\overline{\delta}>0
such that whenever
\|u_{10}-u_{20}\|<\overline{\delta}
,

|{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi(u_{10})-{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi(u_{20})|<\varepsilon,

which proves the continuity.
∎

4.2.3
Existence of ergodic invariant measures

We first give the existence of invariant Borel probability measures of the original problem (
1.2
) – (
1.4
) in
H
.
Here we use the classical procedure – Krylov-Bogoliubov existence theorem (
[
24
, Corollary 11.8]
) and Prokhorov’s theorem (
[
24
, Theorem 2.3]
) to show our consequence.
This procedure reads as below at our disposal.

Lemma 4.9
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
is Feller on
{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm b}(H)
and for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,H)
, define a family of Borel probability measures on
H
,

\nu_{T}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(u,\cdot){\rm d}t,\hskip 11.38092ptT>0.

(4.29)

Suppose that the family
\{\nu_{T}\}_{T>0}
is tight.
Then each sequence
\{\nu_{T_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
T_{n}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
has a weakly convergent subsequence and the corresponding weak limit is an invariant measure for
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
.

For notational clarity, we denote the process given in (
3.1
) by
\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,u)
such that
\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,u)(\omega)=\Phi_{\epsilon}(t;\omega,u)
in the following discussion.
Now we show the existence of the invariant measures.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
0<\epsilon\leqslant 1
,
h\in H
and
G
satisfy the assumption (
Gp
).
Then there exists an invariant measure in
H
for the process
\Phi_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

Following Theorem
4.8
and Lemma
4.9
, we first show the tightness of the
\nu_{T}
defined as (
4.29
) for some suitable initial datum
u_{0}
, saying, given some
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,H)
, for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}\subset H
such that

\nu_{T}({\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon})\geqslant 1-\varepsilon,\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }T>0,

(4.30)

Indeed, if we set
u_{0}=0
, then due to the compact embedding of
V
into
H
and the fact
u(t,0)\in V
for all
t>0
, by Chebyshev’s inequality and Lemma
4.3
and choosing
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\bf B}_{V}({\mathcal{R}})
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{T}(H\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{V}({\mathcal{R}}))=

\displaystyle\nu_{T}(V\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{V}({\mathcal{R}}))=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{P}(\|\Delta u(t,0)\|>{\mathcal{R}}){\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta u(t,0)\|^{2}{\rm d}t\leqslant c\frac{\|h\|^{2}+1}{{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}<\varepsilon,

as long as
{\mathcal{R}}
is sufficiently large.
This assures the existence of
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}
for (
4.30
).
Therefore, Lemma
4.9
applies and the existence of invariant Borel probability measures is obtained.
∎

At last, we cope with the existence of ergodic invariant measures for the stochastic process
\Phi_{\epsilon}
generated by the problem (
1.2
) – (
1.4
),
for which, it is necessary for us to confirm the tightness of
{\mathcal{I}}
, that is defined to be the set of all the invariant measures for
\Phi_{\epsilon}
in
H
.
We first present a boundedness estimate for the moments with respect to each invariant measure.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\nu\in{\mathcal{I}}
.
Then for all
p\geqslant 1
, we have

\int_{H}\|u\|^{2p}\nu({\rm d}u)\lesssim\|h\|^{2p}+1.

(4.31)

Proof.

We adopt the property of invariant measures to carry on the proof.
Following (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}\|u(\varsigma)\|^{2p}+\|u(\varsigma)\|^{2p}{\rm d}\varsigma+2p\|u(\varsigma)\|^{2p-2}\|\Delta u(\varsigma)\|^{2}{\rm d}\varsigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\|u\|^{2p-2}\{2p[2(\Delta u,u)+a\|u\|^{2}+(u^{2},G*u^{2})+(h,u)]-[p(2p-1)\epsilon^{2}+1]\|u\|^{2}\}{\rm d}\varsigma

\displaystyle+2p\epsilon\|u\|^{2p}{\rm d}W_{\varsigma}.

(4.32)

Fix
\overline{\kappa}>0
and define for each
u_{0}\in H
and
p\geqslant 1
a third stopping time,

\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:\|u(t,u_{0})\|^{2p}>\overline{\kappa}\}.

Multiplying (
4.32
) by
{\rm e}^{\varsigma}
, integrating the obtained equality over
\varsigma\in[0,t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})]
and
according to the estimates above, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u(t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0}))\|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|^{2p}{\rm e}^{-t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}{\rm e}^{\varsigma-t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}[\|h\|^{2p}+c\|u\|^{2p}-\alpha\|u\|^{2p+2}]{\rm d}\varsigma

\displaystyle+2p\epsilon\int_{0}^{t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}{\rm e}^{\varsigma-t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}\|u\|^{2p}{\rm d}W_{\varsigma}.

(4.33)

Let
\overline{\kappa}_{0}>0
and
\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}
.
Taking expectation of (
4.33
), which vanishes the stochastic integral, and recalling that
c\|u\|^{2p}-\alpha\|u\|^{2p+2}
has an upper bound that is independent of
u
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|u(t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0}))\|^{2p}\mathds{1}_{\{\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}\}}\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|u_{0}\|^{2p}{\rm e}^{-t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}\mathds{1}_{\{\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}\}}

(4.34)

\displaystyle+c\left(\|h\|^{2p}+1\right)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}{\rm e}^{\varsigma-t\wedge\overline{\tau}^{p}_{\overline{\kappa}}(u_{0})}\mathds{1}_{\{\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}\}}{\rm d}\varsigma.

Observe that, given a fixed
\overline{\kappa}_{0}
, the integrands on the right-hand side of (
4.34
) are uniformly bounded with respect to
\kappa
, and the one on the left-hand side is non-negative.
Thus, we apply the dominated convergence theorem to the right-hand side and Fatou’s lemma to the left, so that

\mathbb{E}\|u(t)\|^{2p}\mathds{1}_{\{\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}\}}\leqslant{\rm e}^{-t}\mathbb{E}\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\mathds{1}_{\{\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}\}}+c\left(\|h\|^{2p}+1\right).

Then for all
\overline{\kappa}_{0}>0
, we can pick
t_{\overline{\kappa}_{0}}>0
large enough such that

\mathbb{E}\|u(t_{\overline{\kappa}_{0}})\|^{2p}\mathds{1}_{\{\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}\}}\leqslant 2c(\|h\|^{2p}+1).

(4.35)

Note by the property of invariant measures that, the distribution of
\|u(t_{ol{\kappa}_{0}})\|^{2p}
is
\nu
.
This indicates that there exists a function
\phi_{\overline{\kappa}_{0}}:H\rightarrow[0,1]
with
\phi_{\overline{\kappa}_{0}}(u)\rightarrow 1
for
\nu
-a.s. as
\overline{\kappa}_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, such that

\mathbb{E}\|u(t_{\overline{\kappa}_{0}})\|^{2p}\mathds{1}_{\{\|u_{0}\|^{2p}\leqslant\overline{\kappa}_{0}\}}=\int_{H}\|u\|^{2p}\phi_{\overline{\kappa}_{0}}(u)\nu({\rm d}u).

(4.36)

Combining (
4.35
) and (
4.36
) and letting
\overline{\kappa}_{0}
tend to the infinity, we obtain (
4.31
).
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

The invariant measure set
{\mathcal{I}}
is tight.

Proof.

We again follow the proof of tightness presented above.
Similarly, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we look for a compact subset
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\bf B}_{V}({\mathcal{R}})
with a sufficiently large
{\mathcal{R}}

such that

\nu(H\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{V}({\mathcal{R}}))<\varepsilon\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }\nu\in{\mathcal{I}}.

(4.37)

Indeed, for some fixed
t>0
, by the invariance and Lemma
4.6
, for every
\kappa>0
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(H\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{V}({\mathcal{R}}))=

\displaystyle\int_{H}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(u,H\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{V}({\mathcal{R}}))\nu({\rm d}u)=\int_{H}\mathbb{P}(\|\Delta\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,u)\|>{\mathcal{R}})\nu({\rm d}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{H}\mathbb{P}\left(t\|\Delta\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,u)\|^{2}>t{\mathcal{R}}^{2}\right)\nu({\rm d}u)\leqslant\int_{H}\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\tau}_{t{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}(u)<t\right)\nu({\rm d}u)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{t}\int_{H}\|u\|^{2}\nu({\rm d}u)+(\|h\|^{2}+1)+\|h\|^{2}t\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{ct}{\kappa}\left[\int_{H}\|u\|^{4}\nu({\rm d}u)+(\|h\|^{4}+1)t\right],

(4.38)

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(t,\kappa)
is given by Lemma
4.5
.
By Lemma
4.11
, we know both

\int_{H}\|u\|^{2}\nu({\rm d}u)\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{and}\hskip 11.38092pt\int_{H}\|u\|^{4}\nu({\rm d}u)\mbox{ are bounded.}

Therefore, for each
\varepsilon>0
, with
t
fixed, by choosing
\kappa
sufficiently large and fixed, we can make

\frac{ct}{\kappa}\left[\int_{H}\|u\|^{4}\nu({\rm d}u)+(\|h\|^{4}+1)t\right]<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Correspondingly
C_{1}
is thus fixed, and hence we can let
{\mathcal{R}}
be large enough such that

\frac{C}{{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{t}\int_{H}\|u\|^{2}\nu({\rm d}u)+(\|h\|^{2}+1)+\|h\|^{2}t\right]<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

for the
\varepsilon
given above.
By the choices above, we attain an
{\mathcal{R}}>0
in (
4.38
) such that (
4.37
) holds.
The proof is complete.
∎

Now we are prepared to verify the existence of ergodic invariant measures.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
0<\epsilon\leqslant 1
,
h\in H
and
G
satisfy the assumption (
Gp
).
Then there exists an ergodic invariant measure in
H
for
\Phi_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

By
[
24
, Proposition 11.12]
, we see that an invariant measure is ergodic if and only if it is an extreme point of
{\mathcal{I}}
.
We apply the Krein-Milman Theorem (see e.g.
[
39
, Theorem 7.4, Chapter V]
) to show the existence of extreme points of
{\mathcal{I}}
, i.e., to show that
{\mathcal{I}}
is nonempty, compact and convex.

By the discussion above, it is obvious that
{\mathcal{I}}\neq\emptyset
.
Note that
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}
is linear with respect to
\nu
, which implies that
{\mathcal{I}}
is convex.

For the compactness, we first show that
{\mathcal{I}}
is closed.
Indeed, given each weakly convergent sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
{\mathcal{I}}
such that
\nu_{n}
weakly converges to
\nu
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, by the invariance, we have for all
\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
,

\begin{array}[]{clll}{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}\nu_{n}(\Gamma)&=\displaystyle\int_{H}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(u,\Gamma)\nu_{n}({\rm d}u)&\rightarrow\displaystyle\int_{H}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(u,\Gamma)\nu({\rm d}u)&={\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}\nu(\Gamma)\\
\|&&&\\
\nu_{n}(\Gamma)&\rightarrow\nu(\Gamma),&\mbox{as }n\rightarrow\infty.&\end{array}

By the uniqueness of weak limit, we know that
\nu
is also an invariant measure.
Hence the closedness.

The compactness of
{\mathcal{I}}
is well assured by the tightness given by Lemma
4.12
and the closedness showed above.
The proof is eventually finished.
∎

5
Conclusions and remarks

In this paper, we first show the upper semi-continuity of the
{\mathcal{D}}
-random attractor
{\mathscr{A}}_{\epsilon}(\omega)

in the regular Sobolev space
H_{0}^{2}(U)
for the random dynamical system
\Phi_{\epsilon}
generated by 2D
nonlocal stochastic Swift-Hohenberg model (
1.2
) – (
1.4
) with multiplicative noise,
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
\omega\in\Omega
, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
Then we obtain the existence of ergodic invariant Borel probability measures in
L^{2}(U)
for the process
\Phi_{\epsilon}
.
What is more, both conclusions hold true for two sorts of the kernel,
i.e., the positive kernel (under assumption (
Gp
)) and the special non-negative kernel (under assumption (
Gn
)).

Firstly, we transform the original stochastic partial differential equation (
1.2
)
to a random partial differential equation (
2.7
) via a solution to an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck equation.
Then we apply the uniform estimates of the solution
v(t;\omega,v_{0})
of (
2.7
) – (
2.9
) in
H_{0}^{2}(U)

and
{\mathscr{D}}(A^{\mu})
for
\mu\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
to prove the upper semi-continuity we want.
For the estimates of
v(t;\omega,v_{0})
in
{\mathscr{D}}(A^{\mu})
, we take full advantage of the analytic semigroup theory to treat the fractional power
A^{\mu}
.

Secondly, we follow the Krylov-Bogoliubov procedure to show the existence of invariant measures on
L^{2}(U)
,
and use
[
24
, Proposition 11.12]
and the Krein-Milman Theorem to prove the existence of ergodic invariant measures.
Before these work, we need to assure the Feller property of the Markovian transition semigroup
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
, for which,
we apply the classical “stochastic Gronwall’s lemma”, Proposition
4.1
.
However, since
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, when we estimate the expectation of
H_{0}^{2}(U)
-norm, one can not apply
-\Delta
to (
1.2
) directly
and this prevents us using the Itô’s formula appropriately.
We are obliged to utilize the integral equation and the analytic semigroup theory to achieve our goal.
In this way, the classical “stochastic Gronwall’s lemma” can not directly apply at our disposal.
We hence in Lemma
4.2
develop the condition of Proposition
4.1
into an equivalent condition that is much easier to check.
This enables us to handle this difficulty.

Thirdly, as an extension, our approach of proving the existence of ergodic invariant measures can also apply to more general cases.
For example, the stochastic term
\epsilon u{\rm d}W_{t}
in (
1.2
) can be replaced by the formal expansion

\psi(u){\rm d}\widetilde{W}_{t}=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\psi_{k}(u){\rm d}W^{k}_{t},

where
\{W_{t}^{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is a sequence of independent one-dimensional Wiener process relative to some prescribed stochastic basis and

\psi
is required to be naturally compatible, bounded and Lipschitz continuous.
Also, our technique of the application of analytic semigroup theory enlightens us to deal with more general stochastic partial differential equations, such as non-autonomous ones.

At last, we would like to raise some interesting problems related to this paper.

(1)

We have seen the existences of
{\mathcal{D}}
-random attractors
{\mathscr{A}}_{\epsilon}(\omega)
, for
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
and the invariant measures for the process
\Phi_{\epsilon}
.
We know that for deterministic dynamical systems, the invariant measures are often supported on (global, pullback) attractors.
Then what is the relationship between the random attractors and invariant measures for stochastic dynamical system
\Phi_{\epsilon}
?

(2)

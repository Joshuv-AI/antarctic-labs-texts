Analyticity of stationary spacetimes from maximal hypersurfaces Thanks: Vienna preprint UWThPh 2022-9 Thanks: Email marc@usal.es
Piotr T. Chruściel

Abstract
The standard method of proving analyticity of stationary vacuum metrics invokes the quotient-space version of Einstein equations. We verify that the same conclusion can be obtained using the KID equations on maximal surfaces.



1
Introduction

A classical theorem of Morrey
[
15
]
asserts that solutions of a class of elliptic PDEs are real-analytic. We verify that this theorem applies to vacuum Lorentzian metrics with a Killing vector field which is transverse to a maximal surface if and only if the Killing vector is timelike.
This provides another example of usefulness of maximal hypersurfaces in general relativity.

For definiteness we restrict attention to the vacuum Einstein equations with cosmological constant, but the argument applies as is to all matter models where the field equations do not involve second derivatives of the metric and become elliptic when stationarity is assumed.

We note that from the point of view of general relativistic applications, e.g. to black hole uniqueness theorems, the usefulness of this result is unique continuation of solutions, which follows from analyticity, even though analyticity may not be necessary in some cases to obtain unique continuation;
see
[
7
]
and references therein.

ADM notation and Killing vector fields

Any Lorentzian metric
\mathfrak{g}
can be locally written in the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner form
(cf., e.g.,
[
13
, Equation (21.40), p. 507]
)

\mathfrak{g}=-N^{2}dt^{2}+g_{ij}(dx^{i}+Y^{i}dt)(dx^{j}+Y^{j}dt)\,,

(2.1)

where
g_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}
is a family of possibly
t
-dependent Riemannian metrics on a manifold
M
,
N
is a function on spacetime and
Y=Y^{i}\partial_{i}
is a family of possibly
t
-dependent vector fields on
M

Let
T
be the field of unit normals to the level sets of
t
, one finds

T=N^{-1}(\partial_{t}-Y^{i}\partial_{i})\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\partial_{t}=NT+Y\,.

(2.2)

Since

is tangent to
\mycal S
, the vector
\partial_{t}
is transverse to
\mycal S
if and only if
N
has no zeros.

It follows from (
2.1
) that

\mathfrak{g}_{tt}\equiv\mathfrak{g}(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})=-N^{2}+g_{ij}Y^{i}Y^{j}\equiv-N^{2}+g(Y,Y)\,,

(2.3)

which shows that

\partial_{t}
is spacelike if and only if the
g
-length of
Y
is larger than
N
.

We have

\det\mathfrak{g}=-N^{2}\det g\,,

(2.4)

which shows that the metric (
2.1
) is manifestly Lorentzian in the coordinate system above when
N
has no zeros and
g
is Riemannian. This holds regardless of the
g
-length of
Y
.

The extrinsic curvature tensor (second fundamental form) of the level sets of
t
is given by

K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\mycal L_{T}g_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}N^{-1}(\partial_{t}g_{ij}-\mycal L_{Y}g_{ij})\,,

(2.5)

and we note that sometimes an opposite sign convention is used in (
2.5
).

If all the functions appearing in (
2.1
) are
t
-independent the vector field
\partial_{t}
is a Killing vector field:

0=\partial_{t}\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}=\mycal L_{\partial_{t}}g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(2.6)

In this case (
2.5
) can be rewritten as

\mycal L_{Y}g_{ij}=-2NK_{ij}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad D_{(i}Y_{j)}=-NK_{ij}\,,

(2.7)

where
D
is the Levi-Civita covariant derivative associate with the Riemannian metric
g=g_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}
, and where parentheses over indices denote symmetrisation.

Given a Lorentzian metric
\mathfrak{g}
with a Killing vector field
X
one can construct local coordinates sastisfying (
2.1
) and the first equation in (
2.6
), this proceeds as follows: Let
\mycal S
be any spacelike hypersurface which is transverse to
X
. Let
t
be a function defined near
\mycal S
by solving the problem

\mycal L_{X}t=1\,,\qquad t|S=0\,.

(2.8)

Let
y^{i}
be any local coordinates on a coordinate patch
\mycal O\subset\mycal S
, we can define functions
x^{i}
near
\mycal O
by solving the equations

\mycal L_{X}x^{i}=0\,,\qquad x^{i}|S=y^{i}\,.

(2.9)

One checks that in these coordinates the metric can be written in the form (
2.1
). We have
X^{t}=X(t)=\mycal L_{X}t=1
and
X^{i}=X(x^{i})=\mycal L_{X}x^{i}=0
, which shows that
X=\partial_{t}
. Transversality of
X
to
\mycal O
is equivalent to the condition that
N
has no zeros on
\mycal O
.

Since the problem addressed is purely local, for our purposes here we can without loss of generality assume that
\mycal O=\mycal S
, and that the coordinate system above is global on
I\times\mycal S
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval containing

\displaystyle L(x,\lambda)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta N\\
\delta Y\\
\delta g\\
\end{array}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g^{k\ell}\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell}\delta N\\

Appendix A
pp-waves

The
pp
-waves provide examples of smooth non-analytic metrics with a null Killing vector. The metric takes the form

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=2dvdu+H(u,x,y)du^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}.

The vector field
X=\partial_{v}
is null, covariantly constant and nowhere zero, hence transversal to any spacelike hypersurface. We chose the orientation so that
\partial_{v}
is future. The metric
\mathfrak{g}
is
Ricci flat if and only if
H
satisfies
[
19
, Equation (24.43), p. 384]

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})H=0,

i.e. it is harmonic in the
x,y
coordinates. Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}
is locally flat if and only if
H
is a polynomial of degree one in the coordinates

x,y

[
19
, Equation (24.43), p. 384]
.

It is trivial to construct
non-analytic solutions. An example to be used below is

H=1+w(u)xy
, with
w(u)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

w(u)=0
in
u\in(-\infty,0)
, and
w(u)>0
for
u>0
. The metric is Minkowski in the domain
u<0
and has non-zero curvature for
u>0
, so there exists no coordinate system in which the metric is analytic near the point
u=x=y=0
.

Consider a hypersurface
\mycal S:=\{v=f(u,x,y)\}
.
To compute the fields
(N,Y,g)

on
\mycal S
we introduce adapted coordinates, as described in the main text. Let
t=v-f(u,x,y)
and let us write
\mathfrak{g}
in the coordinate system
\{t,u,x,y\}
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=2dtdu+\left(H+2\partial_{u}f\right)du^{2}+2\partial_{x}fdudx+2\partial_{x}fdudy+dx^{2}+dy^{2}.

(A.1)

The hypersurface
\mycal S
is
\{t=0\}
so the induced metric
(the first fundamental form) is

\displaystyle g=\left(H+2\partial_{u}f\right)du^{2}+2\partial_{x}fdudx+2\partial_{y}fdudy+dx^{2}+dy^{2}.

(A.2)

This is Riemannian (positive definite) provided that

\displaystyle F:=H+2\partial_{u}f-(\partial_{x}f)^{2}-(\partial_{y}f)^{2}>0,

(A.3)

which we assume from now on. To compute
N
and
Y
we compare
(
A.1
) with (
2.1
).
The absence of a
dt^{2}
term gives

N^{2}=g_{ij}Y^{i}Y^{j}
, which is simply a restatement of the fact that
X
is null everywhere. The cross-terms
dtdx^{i}
give
g_{ij}Y^{j}dx^{i}=du
, which can be solved for
Y
:

\displaystyle Y=\frac{1}{F}\left(\partial_{u}-f_{x}\partial_{x}-f_{y}\partial_{y}\right).

(A.4)

The
g
-norm of this vector is
g_{ij}Y^{i}Y^{j}=\frac{1}{F}=N^{2}
. Selecting the unit normal

T
to be future directed requires
N>0
(cf. (
2.2
)). Consequently,

\displaystyle N=\frac{1}{\sqrt{F}}.

(A.5)

One can now check by an explicit calculation that
(
3.1
)-(
3.2
) hold.

The metrics (
A.1
) provide an interesting example where the maximal surface equation reduces to a polynomial dynamical system with a source. Specifically, the trace of the
extrinsic curvature, as defined in (
2.5
), is given by

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm tr}\,_{g}K=-\partial_{u}N+\partial_{x}(N\partial_{x}f)+\partial_{y}(N\partial_{y}f).

As already pointed-out we choose
H=1+w(u)xy
, with
w(u)
as before,
and look for solutions of the maximal surface
equation
\mbox{\rm tr}\,_{g}K=0
of the form

\displaystyle f=f_{0}(u)+f_{1}(u)(x^{2}+y^{2})+f_{2}(u)xy.

The condition that
\mycal S
is maximal turns out to be equivalent
to the following system of ODEs, where a dot
denotes a derivative with respect to
u
:

\displaystyle\ddot{f_{0}}

\displaystyle=-4f_{1}(1+2\dot{f}_{0}),

\displaystyle\ddot{f_{1}}

\displaystyle=2f_{2}\dot{f_{2}}+2f_{1}(4f_{1}^{2}-f_{2}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}f_{2}w

\displaystyle\ddot{f_{2}}

\displaystyle=2f_{2}\left(4\dot{f}_{1}+4f_{1}^{2}-f_{2}^{2}\right)-2f_{1}w-\frac{1}{2}\dot{w}.

For
u\leq 0
we take
f_{0}(u)=f_{1}(u)=f_{2}(u)=0
and for
u\geq 0
we take the unique solution of this
system with initial data
f_{0}(0)=f_{1}(0)=f_{2}(0)=\dot{f}_{0}(0)=\dot{f}_{1}(0)=\dot{f}_{2}(0)=0
. Since
w(u)
is
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
but not analytic, the same holds for
f_{i}(u)
,
i=0,1,2
. It is immediate that
the condition (
A.3
) (i.e.,
\mycal S

being spacelike) is satisfied in a
neighbourhood of the point
u=x=y=0
. This provides an essentially explicit example
of a maximal hypersurface with transversal null Killing vector field and non-analytic metric.

The vanishing of

Smooth initial data lead, by evolution, to smooth spacetime metrics by general considerations. This is clear from (
B.2
)-(
B.3
) in any case except possibly at
r=0
, and can be directly verified at the axis of rotation by inspection of asymptotic expansions there.

Hence the set of smooth such metrics can be uniquely parameterised by the set of smooth rotation-invariant initial data for the
(2+1)
-dimensional wave equation in Minkowski space-time.

Consider a point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
at which we have

(-(\partial_{t}\psi)^{2}+(\partial_{r}\psi)^{2}-r^{-1}\partial_{r}\psi)|_{p}\neq 0\,.

(B.5)

One checks that the metric is
not flat
at
p
, and hence in a spacetime neighborhood of
p
. (This is a sufficient, but not necessary, condition).

As an example, let us take smooth rotation invariant initial data for
\psi
at
t=0
such that
\psi|_{t=0}\equiv 0
,
\partial_{t}\psi|_{t=0,\,r\leq 1}\equiv 0
, and
\partial_{t}\psi|_{t=0,\,r>1}>0
. Then (
B.5
) holds at
t=0
and
r>1
. This results, by evolution, in a smooth spacetime metric with two spacelike Killing vectors,
\partial_{\varphi}
and
\partial_{z}
, with a spacetime metric which is flat in a spacetime neighborhood of the solid cylinder
\{t=0,\,r<1\}
, but is not flat.
Unique continuation of the curvature tensor for real-analytic metrics shows that the spacetime metric cannot be real-analytic on a maximal hypersurface which is transverse to some linear combination of the spacelike Killing vectors and which intersects the cylinder
\{t=0,\,r=1\}.

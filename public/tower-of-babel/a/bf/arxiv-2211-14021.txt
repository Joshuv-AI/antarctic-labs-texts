Generalized Schwarzians


Abstract
In this paper we demonstrate that the different generalizations of the Schwarzians, supersymmetric or purely bosonic, can be easily constructed by using the nonlinear realizations technique.



Nikolay Kozyrev and Sergey Krivonos

Bogoliubov Laboratory of Theoretical Physics, JINR,
141980 Dubna, Russia

nkozyrev@theor.jinr.ru, krivonos@theor.jinr.ru

PACS numbers: 11.30.Pb, 11.30.-j

Keywords: Schwarzian, extended supersymmetry, (super)conformal symmetry

1
Introduction

The Schwarzian derivative (
2.1
), or just the Schwarzian appears in apparently unrelated fields of mathematics: from classical complex analysis to integrable systems
[
1
]
. In contrast, in the physics
the Schwarzian appears either in the transformation properties of the conformal (supersymmetric) stress-tensor
[
2
,
3
]
or arises as the low energy limit of the SYK model
[
4
]
.
Therefore, it is not strange that the possible generalizations of the Schwarzian are mainly related to its supersymmetric extensions where the supersymmetric Schwarzian naturally appears in the superconformal transformations of the super-current
[
5
,
6
,
3
,
7
]
. However, this generalization quickly stops at
{\cal N}=4
supersymmetry due to appearance of components with negative conformal dimension in the current superfield
J^{(N)}
for
{\cal N}>4
. In addition, the recent
construction of the “flat space” version of the Schwarzian
[
8
]
raised the question about
existence of the systematic way to build the generalized (bosonic ones or possessing higher,
{\cal N}>4
supersymmetries) Schwarzians.

The treatment of the supersymmetric Schwarzians as the anomalous terms in the transformations of the currents superfield
J^{({\cal N})}(Z)

[
3
]
leads to the conclusion that the structure of the (super)Schwarzians is completely defined by the conformal symmetry and, therefore, it should exist a different, probably purely algebraic, way to define the (super)Schwarzians. The main property of the (super)Schwarzians which defines their structure is their invariance with respect to (super)conformal transformations. The suitable way to construct (super)conformal invariants is the method of nonlinear realizations
[
9
,
10
]
equipped with the inverse Higgs phenomenon
[
11
]
. However, the natural invariant objects in the non-linear realization approach are the Cartan forms, which contain the differentials of the coordinates of the (super)space with non-trivial transformation properties. Thus, additional question concerns implementation of the inert (super)coordinates in the nonlinear realization approach.

This method was first applied to the
sl(2)
algebra in
[
12
]
to obtain the standard Schwarzian and then extended to different super-conformal algebras in
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. Later on, this approach has been applied to the cases of non-relativistic Schwarzians and Carroll algebra
[
17
]
. It should be noted that the constraints, proposed in these papers, looks like the results of illuminating guess. Moreover, in some practically interesting cases the proposed constraints are too strong to set the recovered supersymmetric Schwarzian a constant.

In two of our papers
[
18
,
19
]
the method proposed in
[
12
]
was modified in two directions.
Firstly, we introduced the “inert super-space” as the coordinates of the independent “inert” coset elements. Secondly, the constraints were imposed on the full Cartan forms by either nullifying them or identifying with the “inert super-space” forms. This last feature gives us the possibility to invoke into game the powerful method of the Maurer-Cartan equations to analyze the consequences of the constraints, which drastically simplified calculations.

In this paper, we apply the proposed approach to construct some new generalized Schwarzians.
After short review of the basic steps of our approach in the Section 2, we will construct

•

“Flat space” variant of the Schwarzian (Section 3),

•

Bosonic variant of the Schwarzian with
su(1,2)
symmetry (Section 4),

•

Schwarzians with
{\cal N}
extended supersymmetry (Section 5).

We conclude in the Section 6 with some interesting but unsolved at the moment questions and hypotheses.

2
Sketch of the idea

Before getting to the main results of our paper, let us illustrate how the method of nonlinear realizations works applied to the Schwarzians on two simpler examples: the standard bosonic Schwarzian (
{\cal N}{=}0
case) and one of supersymmetric Schwarzians (
{\cal N}{=}2
case).

2.1
{\cal N}{=}0
case

The Schwarzian derivative
\left\{t,\tau\right\}
is defined by the relation

\left\{t,\tau\right\}=\frac{\dddot{t}}{\dot{t}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\ddot{t}}{\dot{t}}\right)^{2},\;\;\dot{f}=\partial_{\tau}f.

(2.1)

Its famous property is the invariance with respect to
SL(2,R)
Möbius transformations, acting on
t
:

t^{\prime}=\frac{a\,t+b}{c\,t+d}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\left\{t^{\prime},\tau\right\}=\left\{t,\tau\right\}.

(2.2)

Note, the “time”
\tau
is invariant with respect to these
SL(2,R)
transformations.

The action of the bosonic Schwarzian mechanics (see e.g.
[
4
]
)

S_{schw}[t]=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\left\{t,\tau\right\}

(2.3)

leads to the following equation of motion

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left\{t,\tau\right\}=0\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\left\{t,\tau\right\}=2\,m^{2}=\mbox{const}.

(2.4)

As
SL(2,R)
transformations of
t
are involved, it is natural to look at this system from the nonlinear realization viewpoint. Indeed, one can consider the
sl(2,R)
algebra, spanned by the Hermitian generators
P,D,K

{\rm i\,}\left[D,P\right]=P,\quad{\rm i\,}\left[D,K\right]=-K,\quad{\rm i\,}\left[K,P\right]=2D

(2.5)

and parameterize the group element in the following way

g=e^{{\rm i\,}tP}e^{{\rm i\,}zK}e^{{\rm i\,}uD}.

(2.6)

This parameterization is similar to one used in construction of the conformal mechanics
[
20
]
, when
P
,
D
and
K
generate time translations, dilatations and conformal boosts, respectively.
Then the Cartan forms, invariant with respect to left multiplication
g^{\prime}=g_{0}g
, read

g^{-1}dg={\rm i\,}\omega_{P}P+{\rm i\,}\omega_{D}D+{\rm i\,}\omega_{K}K\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\omega_{P}=e^{-u}dt,\quad\omega_{D}=du-2zdt,\quad\omega_{K}=e^{u}\left(dz+z^{2}dt\right).

(2.7)

The infinitesimal
sl(2,R)
transformations

g_{0}=e^{{\rm i\,}\big(\tilde{a}P+\tilde{b}D+\tilde{c}K\big)}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\delta t=\tilde{a}+\tilde{b}t+\tilde{c}t^{2},\;\;\quad\delta u=\frac{d}{dt}\delta t,\quad\delta z=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\delta u-\frac{d}{dt}\delta t\,z

(2.8)

are just ones expected for
t
(
2.2
).

If one continues this way, treating
t
as time and
u
and
z
as functions of
t
, imposing covariant condition
\omega_{D}=0
would result in elimination of
z
as an independent variable,
z=\frac{1}{2}\frac{du}{dt}
. (This is manifestation of the Inverse Higgs phenomenon
[
11
]
). Then one can obtain the action of conformal mechanics as
[
20
]

S_{cf}=-\int\left(\omega_{K}+m^{2}\omega_{P}\right)=\int dt\left[\frac{1}{4}e^{u}\left(\frac{du}{dt}\right)^{2}-m^{2}e^{-u}\right]=\int dt\left[\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^{2}-\frac{m^{2}}{x^{2}}\right],\;\;x=e^{u/2}.

(2.9)

Note, the equation of motion which follows from the action (
2.9
)

-e^{u}\left[\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}u+\frac{1}{4}\,\left(\frac{du}{dt}\right)^{2}\right]+m^{2}e^{-u}=0

(2.10)

can be rewritten as the constraint on the Cartan forms
[
20
]

\omega_{K}-m^{2}\omega_{P}=0.

(2.11)

Thus the Schwarzian mechanics is essentially the conformal mechanics rewritten in the new coordinates.

The Cartan forms in (
2.7
) are invariant with respect to
sl(2,R)
transformations (
2.8
), while the time variable
t
transforms according to (
2.2
), (
2.8
). At this point one may impose our main condition
[
12
,
13
,
18
]

\omega_{P}=e^{-u}dt=d\tau,

(2.12)

where
\tau
is a new invariant “time” which is completely inert under
sl(2,R)
transformations. Treating now
t
,
u
,
z
as the functions of
\tau
, one can express the Goldstone fields
u
and
z
in terms of
\dot{t}
,
\ddot{t}

u=\log{\dot{t}},\;\;\omega_{D}=du-2\,z\,e^{u}\,d\tau=0\;\Rightarrow\;z=\frac{1}{2}e^{-u}\dot{u}=\frac{\ddot{t}}{2\,{\dot{t}}{}^{2}}.

(2.13)

Putting these relations into the remaining form
\omega_{K}
, one immediately obtains that it is proportional to the Schwarzian
\big\{t,\tau\big\}
:

\omega_{K}=\frac{1}{2}d\tau\left[\ddot{u}-\frac{1}{2}{\dot{u}}{}^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\dddot{t}}{\dot{t}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\ddot{t}}{\dot{t}}\right)^{2}\right]d\tau=\frac{1}{2}d\tau\,\big\{t,\tau\big\}.

(2.14)

The Schwarzian action is, obviously,
S_{schw}=-\int\omega_{K}
.

2.2
{\cal N}{=}2
case

This idea can be straightforwardly generalized to the supersymmetric case. To obtain supersymmetric Schwarzians, we should consider the proper superalgebra, which differs from (
2.5
) by the presence of supercharges
Q^{i}
, superconformal charges
S^{i}
and, possibly, internal symmetry generators
J^{ij}
. Then one should introduce the superconformally inert superspace coordinates
\tau
and
\theta
using the relations

\omega_{P}=\triangle\tau,\;\;\big(\omega_{Q}\big)^{i}=d\theta^{i},

(2.15)

where the forms
\triangle\tau
and
d\theta^{i}
are invariant with respect to standard superspace transformations
\delta\tau\sim\epsilon\theta
,
\delta\theta\sim\epsilon
. After imposing condition
\omega_{D}=0
also, one should obtain that the remaining forms are composed of supersymmetric Schwarzians and their derivatives. As one of the simplest examples, let us consider
{\cal N}=2
Schwarzian mechanics
[
13
,
18
]
.

In the case of
{\cal N}{=}2
supersymmetry we start from
{\cal N}{=}2
superconformal algebra
su(1,1|1)
with the following (anti)commutation relations

\displaystyle{\rm i\,}\left[D,P\right]=P,\quad{\rm i\,}\left[D,K\right]=-K,\quad{\rm i\,}\left[K,P\right]=2D,

\displaystyle\left\{Q,{\overline{Q}}{}\right\}=2P,\quad\left\{S,{\overline{S}}{}\right\}=2K,\quad\left\{Q,{\overline{S}}{}\right\}=-2D+2J,\left\{{\overline{Q}}{},S\right\}=-2D-2J,

\displaystyle{\rm i\,}\left[J,Q\right]=\frac{1}{2}Q,\;{\rm i\,}\left[J,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}{\overline{Q}}{},\quad{\rm i\,}\left[J,S\right]=\frac{1}{2}S,\;{\rm i\,}\left[J,{\overline{S}}{}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}{\overline{S}}{},

\displaystyle{\rm i\,}\left[D,Q\right]=\frac{1}{2}Q,\;{\rm i\,}\left[D,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]=\frac{1}{2}{\overline{Q}}{},\quad{\rm i\,}\left[D,S\right]=-\frac{1}{2}S,\;{\rm i\,}\left[D,{\overline{S}}{}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}{\overline{S}}{},

\displaystyle{\rm i\,}\left[K,Q\right]=-S,\;{\rm i\,}\left[K,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]=-{\overline{S}}{},\quad{\rm i\,}\left[P,S\right]=Q,\;{\rm i\,}\left[P,{\overline{S}}{}\right]={\overline{Q}}{}.

(2.16)

We parameterize the
SU(1,1|1)
group element in the following way

g=e^{{\rm i\,}tP}\,e^{\xi Q+{\bar{\xi}}{\overline{Q}}{}}\,e^{\psi S+{\bar{\psi}}{}{\overline{S}}{}}e^{{\rm i\,}zK}e^{{\rm i\,}uD}e^{\phi J}.

(2.17)

The Cartan forms

g^{-1}dg={\rm i\,}\omega_{P}P+\omega_{Q}Q+{\bar{\omega}}_{Q}{\overline{Q}}{}+{\rm i\,}\omega_{D}D+\omega_{J}J+\omega_{S}S+{\bar{\omega}}_{S}{\overline{S}}{}+{\rm i\,}\omega_{K}K

(2.18)

explicitly read

\displaystyle\omega_{P}\equiv e^{-u}\triangle t=e^{-u}\left(dt+{\rm i\,}(d{\bar{\xi}}\xi+d\xi{\bar{\xi}})\right),

\displaystyle\omega_{Q}=e^{-\frac{u}{2}+{\rm i\,}\frac{\phi}{2}}\left(d\xi+\psi\,\triangle t\right),\;\bar{\omega}_{Q}=e^{-\frac{u}{2}-{\rm i\,}\frac{\phi}{2}}\left(d{\bar{\xi}}+{\bar{\psi}}{}\,\triangle t\right),

\displaystyle\omega_{D}=du-2z\,\triangle t-2{\rm i\,}(d\xi{\bar{\psi}}{}+d{\bar{\xi}}\psi),\;\omega_{J}=d\phi-2\psi{\bar{\psi}}{}\,\triangle t+2(d{\bar{\xi}}\psi-d\xi{\bar{\psi}}{}),

\displaystyle\omega_{S}=e^{\frac{u}{2}+{\rm i\,}\frac{\phi}{2}}\left(d\psi-{\rm i\,}\psi{\bar{\psi}}{}d\xi+z\left(d\xi+\psi\,\triangle t\right)\,\right),

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{S}=e^{\frac{u}{2}-{\rm i\,}\frac{\phi}{2}}\left(d{\bar{\psi}}{}+{\rm i\,}\psi{\bar{\psi}}{}d{\bar{\xi}}+z\left(d{\bar{\xi}}+{\bar{\psi}}{}\,\triangle t\right)\right),

\displaystyle\omega_{K}=e^{u}\left(dz+z^{2}\triangle t-{\rm i\,}(\psi\,d{\bar{\psi}}{}+{\bar{\psi}}{}\,d\psi)+2{\rm i\,}z\,(d\xi\,{\bar{\psi}}{}+d{\bar{\xi}}\psi)\right).

(2.19)

Now we impose the following conditions on the forms
\omega_{P}
,
\omega_{Q}
,
\omega_{D}
(
2.2
):

\omega_{P}=\triangle\tau,\;\;\omega_{Q}=d\theta,\;\;{\bar{\omega}}_{Q}=d\bar{\theta},\;\;\omega_{D}=0.

(2.20)

Here,
\triangle\tau=d\tau+{\rm i\,}\big(d\theta\bar{\theta}+d\bar{\theta}\theta\big)
. The forms
\triangle\tau
,
d\theta
,
d\bar{\theta}
are invariant with respect to
{\cal N}{=}2
supersymmetry transformations

\delta\tau={\rm i\,}\big(\epsilon\bar{\theta}+\bar{\epsilon}\theta\big),\;\;\delta\theta=\epsilon,\;\;\delta\bar{\theta}=\bar{\epsilon}.

(2.21)

Covariant derivatives with respect to
\tau
,
\theta
,
\bar{\theta}
are

D=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-i\bar{\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},\;{\overline{D}}{}=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\overline{\theta}}}-i\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},\quad\quad\left\{D,{\overline{D}}{}\right\}=-2i\partial_{\tau}.

(2.22)

The constraints on the Cartan forms (
2.20
), expanded in projections
\triangle\tau
,
d\theta
,
d\bar{\theta}
with help of (
2.22
)

\displaystyle\dot{t}+{\rm i\,}\left(\dot{{\bar{\xi}}}\xi+\dot{\xi}{\bar{\xi}}\right)=e^{u},

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}+e^{u}\psi=0,\;D\xi=e^{\frac{1}{2}(u-{\rm i\,}\,\phi)},\;{\overline{D}}{}\xi=0,

\displaystyle\dot{{\bar{\xi}}}+e^{u}{\bar{\psi}}{}=0,\;{\overline{D}}{}{\bar{\xi}}=e^{\frac{1}{2}(u+{\rm i\,}\,\phi)},\;D{\bar{\xi}}=0.

(2.23)

Using (
2.2
), we express all Cartan forms in the terms of
{\cal N}{=}2
Schwarzian

{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}=\frac{D\dot{\xi}}{D\xi}-\frac{{\overline{D}}{}\dot{{\bar{\xi}}}}{{\overline{D}}{}{\bar{\xi}}}-2{\rm i\,}\frac{\dot{\xi}\dot{{\bar{\xi}}}}{D\xi{\overline{D}}{}{\bar{\xi}}}

(2.24)

as

\displaystyle\omega_{J}={\rm i\,}{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}\triangle\tau,\;\;\omega_{S}=-\frac{1}{2}{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}\,d\theta-\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}{\overline{D}}{}{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}\triangle\tau,\;\;{\bar{\omega}}_{S}=\frac{1}{2}{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}\,d{\bar{\theta}}+\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}D{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}\triangle\tau,

(2.25)

\displaystyle\omega_{K}=-\frac{1}{2}d\theta D{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}+\frac{1}{2}d\bar{\theta}{\overline{D}}{}{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}+\frac{1}{4}\triangle\tau\left({\rm i\,}\left[D,{\overline{D}}{}\right]{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}-{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}^{2}\right).

Note that the same conclusion about the structure of the forms can be achieved by the analysis of Maurer-Cartan equations the forms (
2.18
) satisfy. We will use such equations in Section 5 to study a system with
{\cal N}
supersymmetries.

The constructed
{\cal N}{=}2
Schwarzian (
2.24
) is invariant with respect to superconformal transformations which explicitly read

\displaystyle g^{\prime}=e^{\epsilon Q+\bar{\epsilon}{\overline{Q}}{}}e^{\varepsilon S+\bar{\varepsilon}{\overline{S}}{}}g\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\delta t={\rm i\,}\big(\bar{\epsilon}\xi+\epsilon{\bar{\xi}}\big)-{\rm i\,}t\big(\bar{\varepsilon}\xi+\varepsilon{\bar{\xi}}\big),\\
\delta\xi=\epsilon-\varepsilon t+{\rm i\,}\varepsilon\xi{\bar{\xi}},\\
\delta{\bar{\xi}}=\bar{\epsilon}-\bar{\varepsilon}t-{\rm i\,}\bar{\varepsilon}\xi{\bar{\xi}}.\end{array}\right.

Thus one can construct the supersymmetric Schwarzian action as

S_{N2schw}=-\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}\int d\tau\,d\theta\,d\bar{\theta}\,{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\omega_{J}\,\wedge\,\omega_{Q}\,\wedge\,{\bar{\omega}}_{Q}={\rm i\,}\int\omega_{P}\,\wedge\,\omega_{S}\,\wedge\,{\bar{\omega}}_{Q}=-{\rm i\,}\int\omega_{P}\,\wedge\,\omega_{Q}\,\wedge\,{\bar{\omega}}_{S}.

(2.30)

It is matter of a quite lengthy calculation to check that the equations of motion which follow from the action (
2.30
) can be written as

\frac{d}{d\tau}{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\cal S}_{{\cal N}=2}=const=-2\,m.

(2.31)

Looking at the Cartan forms (
2.25
), one may note that the equations (
2.31
) reduce them
to the forms on the subalgebra formed by the generators

R=P+m^{2}K-2{\rm i\,}mJ,\quad\Gamma=Q+{\rm i\,}mS,\quad\overline{\Gamma}={\overline{Q}}{}-{\rm i\,}m{\overline{S}}{},\quad\big\{\Gamma,\overline{\Gamma}\big\}=2R.

(2.32)

The reduction of the Cartan forms on the algebra
su(1,1|1)
to the forms on the subalgebra (
2.32
) is
the key ingredient of the covariant reduction used in
[
21
]
to construct
{\cal N}{=}2
superconformal mechanics. Thus, in the
{\cal N}=2
supersymmetric case the Schwarzian mechanics is nothing but the superconformal mechanics written in the superfields
\{t,\xi,{\bar{\xi}}\}
depending on the coordinates of the inert superspace
\{\tau,\theta,\bar{\theta}\}
. Unfortunately, this relation does not work beyond

{\cal N}=2
case with
m\neq 0
.

3
Flat space analogue of the Schwarzian

As the first example of the generalized Schwarzian in this Section we will consider the nonlinear realization of the Maxwell algebra which will result in so-called flat space analogue of the Schwarzian. The latter was discovered in
[
8
]
as a result of search of a holographic dual to the Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity
[
22
,
23
]
in flat space, much like the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model which is related to the standard Schwarzian and provides the holographic dual to the JT gravity in AdS space.

The Maxwell algebra contains the Hermitian generators of translation
P
, analogue of the dilatation – central charge generator
Z
, analogue of the conformal boost
K
, and the generator of
U(1)
rotations obeying the following relations

{\rm i\,}\left[J,P\right]=P,\quad{\rm i\,}\left[J,K\right]=-K,\quad{\rm i\,}\left[K,P\right]=2Z.

(3.1)

If we parameterize the Maxwell - group element
g
as

g=e^{{\rm i\,}t\left(P+qJ+m^{2}K\right)}e^{{\rm i\,}zK}e^{{\rm i\,}uZ}e^{{\rm i\,}\phi J},

(3.2)

then the Cartan forms

g^{-1}dg={\rm i\,}\omega_{P}P+{\rm i\,}\omega_{Z}Z+{\rm i\,}\omega_{K}K+{\rm i\,}\omega_{J}J

(3.3)

will read

\omega_{P}=e^{-\phi}dt,\quad\omega_{Z}=du-2zdt,\quad\omega_{K}=e^{\phi}\left(dz-qzdt+m^{2}dt\right),\quad\omega_{J}=d\phi.

(3.4)

The constraints

\omega_{P}=d\tau,\quad\omega_{Z}=0

(3.5)

result in the following relations

\dot{t}=e^{\phi},\quad z=\frac{\dot{u}}{2\dot{t}}\;.

(3.6)

Finally,

\omega_{K}=d\tau\,\dot{t}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\ddot{u}}{\dot{t}}-\frac{\dot{u}\ddot{t}}{\dot{t}^{2}}\right)+m^{2}{\dot{t}}-\frac{1}{2}q\,\dot{u}\right]\equiv d\tau{\cal S}_{flat}.

(3.7)

This is exactly the flat space analogue of the Schwarzian constructed in
[
8
]
. Unfortunately, the
simplest invariant action

{\cal S}\sim\int\omega_{K}

has no clear geometric interpretation.

4
Schwarzian with
su(1,2)
symmetry

In this Section we will consider the bosonic version of the
{\cal N}=2
superconformal mechanics – system with
su(1,2)
symmetry.

The
su(1,2)
algebra includes the following generators:

•

the generators
P,D,K
, forming
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
subalgebra

•

the generators
Q,{\overline{Q}}{}
, and
S,{\overline{S}}{}
- the bosonic analogs of the supersymmetric and conformal supersymmetry generators

•

U(1)
generator
U

The generators
P,D,K
and
U
are Hermitian, while the
Q
and
S
-generators obey the conjugation rules
\left(Q\right)^{\dagger}={\overline{Q}}{},\;\left(S\right)^{\dagger}={\overline{S}}{}
. The non-zero commutators read

\displaystyle{\rm i\,}\left[P,K\right]=-2D,\;{\rm i\,}\left[P,D\right]=-P,\;{\rm i\,}\left[K,D\right]=K,

\displaystyle{\rm i\,}\left[P,S\right]=-Q,\;{\rm i\,}\left[P,{\overline{S}}{}\right]=-{\overline{Q}}{},\quad{\rm i\,}\left[K,Q\right]=S,\;{\rm i\,}\left[K,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]={\overline{S}}{},

\displaystyle{\rm i\,}\left[D,Q\right]=\frac{1}{2}Q,\;{\rm i\,}\left[D,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]=\frac{1}{2}{\overline{Q}}{},\quad{\rm i\,}\left[D,S\right]=-\frac{1}{2}S,\;{\rm i\,}\left[D,{\overline{S}}{}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}{\overline{S}}{},

\displaystyle\left[U,Q\right]=Q,\;\left[U,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]=-{\overline{Q}}{},\quad\left[U,S\right]=S,\;\left[U,{\overline{S}}{}\right]=-{\overline{S}}{},

\displaystyle\left[Q,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]=-\gamma P,\;{\rm i\,}\left[Q,{\overline{S}}{}\right]=-\frac{3}{2}\gamma U-{\rm i\,}\gamma D,\quad\left[S,{\overline{S}}{}\right]=-\gamma K,\;{\rm i\,}\left[S,{\overline{Q}}{}\right]=\frac{3}{2}\gamma U-{\rm i\,}\gamma D.

(4.1)

We parameterize the group element in a standard way as

g=e^{{\rm i\,}tP}e^{{\rm i\,}\left(\phi Q+{\bar{\phi}}{\overline{Q}}{}\right)}e^{{\rm i\,}\left(vS+\bar{v}{\overline{S}}{}\right)}e^{{\rm i\,}zK}e^{{\rm i\,}uD}e^{{\rm i\,}\varphi U}.

(4.2)

The Cartan forms read

\displaystyle\omega_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-u}\left(dt+\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}\gamma\left(\phi d{\bar{\phi}}-{\bar{\phi}}d\phi\right)\right)\equiv e^{-u}\triangle t,

\displaystyle\omega_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle du-{\rm i\,}\gamma\left(\bar{v}d\phi-vd{\bar{\phi}}\right)-2z\triangle t,

\displaystyle\omega_{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{u}\left[dz+\left(z^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}v^{2}\bar{v}^{2}\right)\triangle t-{\rm i\,}\gamma z\left(vd{\bar{\phi}}-{\bar{v}}d\phi\right)+\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}\gamma\left(vd{\bar{v}}-{\bar{v}}dv\right)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}v{\bar{v}}\left(vd{\bar{\phi}}+{\bar{v}}d\phi\right)\right]

\displaystyle\omega_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{u}{2}-{\rm i\,}\varphi}\left[d\phi-v\triangle t\right],\quad{\bar{\omega}}{}_{Q}=e^{-\frac{u}{2}-{\rm i\,}\varphi}\left[d{\bar{\phi}}-{\bar{v}}\triangle t\right],

\displaystyle\omega_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{u}{2}-{\rm i\,}\varphi}\left[dv-\left(z+\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}\gamma v{\bar{v}}\right)\left(d\phi-v\triangle t\right)-{\rm i\,}\gamma v^{2}d{\bar{\phi}}\right],

\displaystyle{\bar{\omega}}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{u}{2}+{\rm i\,}\varphi}\left[d{\bar{v}}-\left(z-\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}\gamma v{\bar{v}}\right)\left(d{\bar{\phi}}-{\bar{v}}\triangle t\right)+{\rm i\,}\gamma{\bar{v}}^{2}d\phi\right],

\displaystyle\omega_{U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\varphi-\frac{3}{2}\gamma\left(vd{\bar{\phi}}+{\bar{v}}d\phi-v{\bar{v}}\triangle t\right).

(4.3)

The constraints we are going to impose are of three different types:

•

The constraint which introduce the inert “time”
\tau

{\omega}_{P}=d\tau

•

The constraints realizing the Inverse Higgs phenomenon
[
11
]
:

{\omega}_{D}={\omega}_{Q}={\bar{\omega}}_{Q}=0
,

•

The dynamical constraints which produced the equations of motion:

{\omega}_{K}={\omega}_{S}={\bar{\omega}}_{S}=0
.

As the results of two first constraints we have

\dot{t}+\frac{{\rm i\,}}{2}\gamma\left(\phi\dot{\bar{\phi}}-\bar{\phi}\dot{\phi}\right)=e^{u},\quad v=e^{-u}\dot{\phi},\;{\bar{v}}=e^{-u}\dot{\bar{\phi}},\quad z=\frac{1}{2}e^{-u}{\dot{u}}.

(4.4)

The dynamical constraints give the equations of motion:

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\dot{u}}\dot{\phi}+{\rm i\,}e^{-u}\gamma\dot{\phi}{}^{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}},\quad\ddot{\bar{\phi}}={\dot{u}}\dot{\bar{\phi}}-{\rm i\,}e^{-u}\gamma\dot{\phi}\dot{\bar{\phi}}{}^{2},

\displaystyle{\ddot{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left({\dot{u}}{}^{2}-e^{-2u}\gamma^{2}\dot{\phi}{}^{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}}{}^{2}\right).

(4.5)

To get the Schwarzian-like system one has to pass from the variable
u
to the “old” time
t
(
4.4
). The result

\frac{\dddot{t}+\frac{{\rm i\,}\gamma}{2}\big(\dot{\phi}\ddot{{\bar{\phi}}}-\dot{\bar{\phi}}\ddot{\phi}+\phi\dddot{{\bar{\phi}}}+{\bar{\phi}}\dddot{\phi}\big)}{\dot{t}+\frac{{\rm i\,}\gamma}{2}\big(\phi\dot{{\bar{\phi}}}-{\bar{\phi}}\dot{\phi}\big)}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\left(\ddot{t}+\frac{{\rm i\,}\gamma}{2}\big(\phi\ddot{{\bar{\phi}}}-{\bar{\phi}}\ddot{\phi}\big)\right)^{2}}{\left(\dot{t}+\frac{{\rm i\,}\gamma}{2}\big(\phi\dot{{\bar{\phi}}}-{\bar{\phi}}\dot{\phi}\big)\right)^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\gamma^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}}{\left(\dot{t}+\frac{{\rm i\,}\gamma}{2}\big(\phi\dot{{\bar{\phi}}}-{\bar{\phi}}\dot{\phi}\big)\right)^{2}}

(4.6)

is somewhat complicated, but evidently generalizes the equation of motion of the standard Schwarzian mechanics

\frac{\dddot{t}}{\dot{t}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\ddot{t}}{\dot{t}}\right)^{2}=2m^{2}.

(4.7)

5.4
Properties with respect to coordinate changes

The supersymmetric Schwarzian should possess a special property with respect to coordinate changes, known as the composition law. The coordinate changes are diffeomorphism transformations

\theta_{i}\rightarrow\theta^{\prime}_{i}=\tilde{\theta}_{i}(\tau,\theta),\;\;\tau^{\prime}=\tilde{\tau}(\tau,\theta),

(5.29)

constrained by
D_{i}\tilde{\tau}+{\rm i\,}D_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{j}\,\tilde{\theta}_{j}=0
, so that the derivative
D_{i}
transforms homogeneously,
D_{i}=D_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{j}D^{\prime}_{j}
. If the composition law holds for the Schwarzian, it should have the form

\Sigma_{ijk}\big[\zeta(\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime});\tau,\theta\big]=\Sigma_{ijk}\big[\tilde{\theta}(\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime});\tau,\theta\big]+M_{[ijk]}{}^{mnp}\Sigma_{mnp}\big[\zeta(\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime});\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\big]

(5.30)

with some matrix
M_{[ijk]}{}^{mnp}
. As the Schwarzian reads

\Sigma_{ijk}=\frac{{\rm i\,}{\cal N}}{2}\frac{D_{[i}D_{j}\zeta_{m}\,D_{k]}\zeta_{m}}{D_{p}\zeta_{q}D_{p}\zeta_{q}}.

(5.31)

As for
D_{i}\zeta_{j}
and
D_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{j}
the relations
D_{i}\zeta_{k}D_{j}\zeta_{k}\sim\delta_{ij}
and
D_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{k}D_{j}\tilde{\theta}_{k}\sim\delta_{ij}
hold, one can shortly obtain

D_{p}\zeta_{q}D_{p}\zeta_{q}=\frac{D_{k}\tilde{\theta}_{l}D_{k}\tilde{\theta}_{l}}{{\cal N}}D^{\prime}_{p}\zeta_{q}D^{\prime}_{p}\zeta_{q}.

(5.32)

Then, directly substituting
D_{i}\zeta_{k}=D_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{j}D^{\prime}_{j}\zeta_{k}
into (
5.31
), we obtain

\Sigma_{ijk}\big[\zeta(\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime});\tau,\theta\big]=\Sigma_{ijk}\big[\tilde{\theta}(\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime});\tau,\theta\big]+\frac{{\cal N}}{D_{r}\tilde{\theta}_{s}D_{r}\tilde{\theta}_{s}}D_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{m}D_{j}\tilde{\theta}_{n}D_{k}\tilde{\theta}_{p}\Sigma_{mnp}\big[\zeta(\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime});\tau^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\big].

(5.33)

Thus the Schwarzian transforms as in (
5.30
), as it should be.

It is clear that there is no hope to have the superfield actions for the theories with
{\cal N}

supersymmetry. However, the question of the equations of motion for such system is not trivial.
As we know, in the bosonic case the equations of motion of the Schwarzian mechanics reduces to the
condition

\mbox{Schwarzian}=const

It is interesting to understand whether this property can be extended to the supersymmetric case.
Another interesting continuation concerns supersymmetric Maxwell group, its analysis and possible
relation of the corresponding Schwarzians with the flat-space analogues of the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model.

Finally, there is a strong expectation that all models constructed in a such manner have to be integrable. It would be interesting to analyze the situation with integrability, at least for the simplest models.

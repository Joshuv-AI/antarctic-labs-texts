A note on the extended Bruinier-Kohnen conjecture
Mohammed Amin Amri, M’hammed Ziane

Abstract
Let f f be a cusp form of half-integral weight k + 1 / 2 k+1/2 , whose Fourier coefficients a ⁡ ( n ) a(n) not necessarily real. We prove an extension of the Bruinier-Kohnen conjecture on the equidistribution of the signs of a ⁡ ( n ) a(n) for the families { a ⁡ ( t ​ p 2 ​ ν ) } p , prime \{a(tp^{2\nu})\}_{p,\text{prime}} , where ν \nu and t t be fixed odd positive integer and square-free integer respectively.



Keywords:
Sign changes; Fourier coefficients of cusp forms; Sato-Tate Equidistribution.

Mathematics subject classification(2010):
11F03; 11F30; 11F37.

1
Introduction and statement of result

Over the recent years, the problem of sign changes of Fourier coefficients of various kind of automorphic forms has been studied by several authors
[
14
,
16
,
7
,
13
,
3
]
. The case of modular forms of half-integral weight was first considered by Bruinier and Kohnen in
[
6
]
, there, they proved sign change results for some subfamilies of Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight modular forms which are accessible via the Shimura-lift. Moreover, they conjectured that the signs of the sequence of Fourier coefficients of a cusp form which lies in the Kohnen’s plus space are equidistributed. Through this problem in
[
8
,
9
,
4
]
Arias, Inam and Wiese proved the Bruinier-Kohnen conjecture in the special case when the Fourier coefficients of a Hecke eigenforms of half-integral weight are indexed by
tn^{2}
with
t
a fixed square-free number and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In
[
1
]
, the results of
[
8
,
4
]
were generalized to Hecke eigenforms with not necessarily real Fourier coefficients, based on this and empirical evidence the first author generalized the Bruinier-Kohnen conjecture to cusp forms in Kohnen’s plus space with not necessarily real Fourier coefficients
a(n)
. More precisely, he conjectured that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{n\leq x\;:\;{\Re e\,}\{a(n)e^{-i\phi}\}\gtrless 0\}}{\#\{n\leq x\;:\;{\Re e\,}\{a(n)e^{-i\phi}\}\neq 0\}}=\frac{1}{2},

for each
\phi
belonging to
[0,\pi)
.

In the present note we address the above conjecture. Indeed, we shall prove that it holds for the families
\{a(tp^{2\nu})\}_{p\in\mathbb{P}}
where
\nu
and
t
be a fixed odd positive integer and a square-free integer. In order to state our result we need to introduce some notations. Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of all prime numbers, and let
k,N
be natural numbers, assume from now on that
k\geq 2
and
N
be an odd and square-free integer. Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
4N
, write
S_{k+1/2}(4N,\chi)
for the space of cusp forms of weight
k+1/2
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
with character
\chi
. In this set-up we have.

Theorem
.

Let
{\mathfrak{f}}\in S_{k+1/2}(4N,\chi)
be a cuspidal Hecke eigenform, and let the Fourier expansion of
{\mathfrak{f}}
at
\infty
be

{\mathfrak{f}}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a(n)q^{n}\quad q:=e^{2\pi iz}.

Let
\nu
be a positive odd integer, and
t
be a square-free integer such that
a(t)\neq 0
. Let
\phi\in[0,\pi)
, define the set of primes

P_{>0}(\phi,\nu):=\{p\in\mathbb{P}\;:\;{\Re e\,}(a(tp^{2\nu})e^{-i\phi})>0\},

and similarly
P_{<0}(\phi,\nu),P_{\neq 0}(\phi,\nu)
. Then the sequence
\{a(tp^{2\nu})\}_{p\in\mathbb{P}}
is oscillatory (in the sense of
[
10
]
), moreover the sets
P_{<0}(\phi,\nu)
and
P_{>0}(\phi,\nu)
have equal positive natural density, that is, both are precisely half of the natural density of the set
P_{\neq 0}(\phi,\nu)
.

The above theorem improves the result of the first author in
[
1
, Theorem 5, non-CM-case]
. The proof which will be given in the next section follows broadly the same lines of the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 5, non-CM-case]
and
[
2
, Theorem 1.4]
. The essential ingredients are the Shimura-lift
[
17
]
, and the Sato-Tate conjecture
[
5
, Theorem B]
.

2
Proof of the result

We start by recalling some basic properties of the Shimura-lift and introduce some notations. The Shimura correspondence
[
17
,
15
]
maps
{\mathfrak{f}}
to a Hecke eigenform
f_{t}
of weight
2k
for the group
\Gamma_{0}(2N)
with character
\chi^{2}
. Let the Fourier expansion of
f_{t}
be given by

f_{t}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{t}(n)q^{n}.

According to
[
17
]
, the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
f_{t}
is given by

A_{t}(n)=\sum_{d|n}\chi_{t,N}(d)d^{k-1}a\left(\frac{n^{2}}{d^{2}}t\right),

(2.1)

where
\chi_{t,N}
denotes the character
\chi_{t,N}(d):=\chi(d)\left(\frac{(-1)^{k}N^{2}t}{d}\right)
, we let
\chi_{0}(d):=\left(\frac{(-1)^{k}N^{2}t}{d}\right)
. Since
{\mathfrak{f}}
is a Hecke eigenform, then so is the Shimura lift. Indeed, we have
f_{t}=a(t)f
where
f
is a normalized Hecke eigenform, write

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda(n)n^{k-1/2}q^{n},

for its Fourier expansion at
\infty
. We shall assume that
a(t)=1
, in the general case we may apply the proof to
\frac{{\mathfrak{f}}}{a(t)}
. Since
2N
is square-free, it follows that
f
is a Hecke eigenform without complex multiplication.

Let
\zeta
be a root of unity belonging to
\mathrm{Im}(\chi)
, and let
p
be a prime number such that
\chi(p)=\zeta
, it is clear that
\frac{\lambda(p)}{\zeta}
is real. By Deligne’s bound we have
\left|\frac{\lambda(p)}{\zeta}\right|\leq 2
, thus we may write

\lambda(p)=2\zeta\cos(\theta_{p}),

(2.2)

for a uniquely defined angel
\theta_{p}\in[0,\pi]
. At this point we state the following theorem which will be crucial for our purpose.

Theorem 1
.

(Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, Taylor)
Assume the set-up above. The sequence
\{\theta_{p}\}_{p}
is equidistributed in
[0,\pi]
as
p
varies over primes satisfying
\chi(p)=\zeta,
with respect to the Sato-Tate measure
\mu_{\text{ST}}:=\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta
. In particular, for any sub-interval
I\subset[0,\pi]
we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{p\leq x\;:\;\chi(p)=\zeta,\;\theta_{p}\in I\}}{\#\{p\leq x\;:\;\chi(p)=\zeta\}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{I}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta.

We shall need the following technical lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\zeta
be a root of unity belonging to
\mathrm{Im}(\chi)
, then the set

\{p\in\mathbb{P}:\chi(p)=\zeta\},

have natural density equal to
\frac{1}{r_{\chi}}
, where
r_{\chi}
denotes the order of the character
\chi
.

Proof.

To establish the above lemma, it suffices to see

\{p\in\mathbb{P}:\chi(p)=\zeta\}=\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/4N\mathbb{Z})^*}{\chi(a)=\zeta}}\{p\in\mathbb{P}\;:\;p\equiv a\pmod{4N}\}.

Note that the number of the sets in the union is
\#\mathrm{ker}(\chi)=\frac{\phi(4N)}{r_{\chi}},
hence by Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{p\leq x:\chi(p)=\zeta\}}{\pi(x)}=\frac{\#\mathrm{ker}(\chi)}{\phi(4N)}=\frac{1}{r_{\chi}},

where here and subsequently
\pi(x)
denotes the prime counting function.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Assume the set-up above. Let
\nu
be an odd positive integer. Define the set of primes

\mathbb{P}_{>0}(\zeta,\nu):=\left\{p\in\mathbb{P}\;:\;\chi(p)=\zeta,\;\frac{a(tp^{2\nu})}{\zeta^{\nu}}>0\right\},

and similarly
\mathbb{P}_{\geq 0}(\zeta,\nu)
,
\mathbb{P}_{<0}(\zeta,\nu)
and
\mathbb{P}_{\leq 0}(\zeta,\nu)
. Then the sets

\mathbb{P}_{>0}(\zeta,\nu),\mathbb{P}_{\geq 0}(\zeta,\nu),\mathbb{P}_{<0}(\zeta,\nu),\mathbb{P}_{\leq 0}(\zeta,\nu)

have a natural density equal to
\frac{1}{2r_{\chi}}
, where
r_{\chi}
denotes the order of the character
\chi
.

Proof.

Denote by
\pi_{>0}(x,\zeta):=\#\{p\leq x:p\in\mathbb{P}_{>0}(\zeta,\nu)\}
and similarly
\pi_{<0}(x,\zeta)
,
\pi_{\leq 0}(x,\zeta)
,
\pi_{>0}(x,\zeta)
,
\pi_{\geq 0}(x,\zeta)
. Applying the Möbius inversion formula to (
2.1
), we derive that

a(tn^{2})=\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)\chi_{t,N}(d)d^{k-1}A_{t}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right).

Set
n=p^{\nu}
with
\chi(p)=\zeta
and normalizing by
p^{\nu(k-1/2)}\zeta^{\nu}
, the above equality specialises to

\dfrac{a(tp^{2\nu})}{p^{\nu(k-1/2)}\zeta^{\nu}}=\dfrac{\lambda(p^{\nu})}{\zeta^{\nu}}-\frac{\chi_{0}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\dfrac{\lambda(p^{\nu-1})}{\zeta^{\nu-1}}.

(2.3)

Since
f
is a Hecke eigenform its
p^{\nu}
-th Fourier coefficient with
\chi(p)=\zeta
is expressible (see
[
1
, Lemma 1]
) by the following trigonometric identity

\lambda(p^{\nu})=\dfrac{\sin((\nu+1)\theta_{p})}{\sin\theta_{p}}\zeta^{\nu},

(2.4)

for
\theta_{p}\in(0,\pi)
, and in the limiting cases when
\theta_{p}=0
and
\theta_{p}=\pi
respectively we have
\lambda(p^{\nu})=(\nu+1)\zeta^{\nu}
and
\lambda(p^{\nu})=(-1)^{\nu}(\nu+1)\zeta^{\nu}
, which can happen for at most finitely many primes
p
only (see
[
11
, Remark 2]
). Thus we may assume and do that
\theta_{p}\in(0,\pi)
. Altogether from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we have

\dfrac{a(tp^{2\nu})}{\zeta^{\nu}}>0\Longleftrightarrow\sin((\nu+1)\theta_{p})>\frac{\chi_{0}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\sin(\nu\theta_{p}).

(2.5)

Let
\epsilon>0
(small enough). Since for all
p>\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}
one has
\left|\frac{\chi_{0}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\sin(\nu\theta_{p})\right|<\epsilon
, then from (
2.5
) we have the inclusion of sets

\left\{p\leq x:\chi(p)=\zeta,\sin((\nu+1)\theta_{p})>\epsilon\right\}\subset\left\{p\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}:\chi(p)=\zeta\right\}\cup\{p\leq x:p\in\mathbb{P}_{>0}(\zeta,\nu)\}.

(2.6)

On the other hand we have

\sin((\nu+1)\theta_{p})>\epsilon\Longleftrightarrow\theta_{p}\in I_{\epsilon}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\left(\frac{(2j-2)\pi+\arcsin(\epsilon)}{\nu+1},\frac{(2j-1)\pi-\arcsin(\epsilon)}{\nu+1}\right),

Thus (
2.6
) is equivalent to

\left\{p\leq x:\chi(p)=\zeta,\theta_{p}\in I_{\epsilon}\right\}\subset\left\{p\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}:\chi(p)=\zeta\right\}\cup\{p\leq x:p\in\mathbb{P}_{>0}(\zeta,\nu)\}.

Consequently we have

\pi_{>0}(x,\zeta)+\pi_{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)\geq\#\left\{p\leq x:\chi(p)=\zeta,\theta_{p}\in I_{\epsilon}\right\},

where,
\pi_{\zeta}(x):=\#\{p\leq x:\chi(p)=\zeta\}
. Now divide the above inequality by
\pi_{\zeta}(x)
, we obtain

\dfrac{\pi_{>0}(x,\zeta)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}+\dfrac{\pi_{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}\geq\dfrac{\#\left\{p\leq x:\chi(p)=\zeta,\theta_{p}\in I_{\epsilon}\right\}}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}.

(2.7)

Since
\pi_{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)
is finite the term
\frac{\pi_{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}
tends to zero as
x\to\infty
. By Theorem
1
we have

\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{p\leq x:\chi(p)=\zeta,\theta_{p}\in I_{\epsilon}\right\}}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}=\mu_{ST}(I_{\epsilon}).

Hence a passage to the limit in (
2.7
) yields

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\pi_{>0}(\zeta,x)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}\geq\mu_{ST}(I_{\epsilon}).

Letting
\epsilon
goes to zero in the above inequality we find
\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\pi_{>0}(\zeta,x)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}\geq\mu_{ST}(I)
, where
I:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\left(\frac{(2j-2)\pi}{\nu+1},\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\nu+1}\right)
. Now from
[
12
]
we have
\mu_{ST}(I)=\frac{1}{2}
, therefore

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\pi_{>0}(\zeta,x)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

A similar reasoning yields
\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{\leq 0}(\zeta,x)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, in combination with
\pi_{\leq 0}(x,\zeta)=\pi_{\zeta}(x)-\pi_{>0}(x,\zeta)
, one sees
\limsup\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(\zeta,x)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, whence
\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(\zeta,x)}{\pi_{\zeta}(x)}
exists and equal to
\frac{1}{2}
. Now from Lemma
, we conclude

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{>0}(\zeta,x)}{\pi(x)}=\frac{1}{2r_{\chi}}.

Similar arguments apply to the other cases.
∎

Now we proceed to prove our main theorem. Fix
\phi\in[0,\pi)
. Let
\zeta
be a root of unity belonging to
\mathrm{Im}(\chi)
. If
p
a prime number satisfying
\chi(p)=\zeta
, from Lemma
we know that
\frac{a(tp^{2\nu})}{\zeta^{\nu}}
is real, thus we my write

{\Re e\,}(a(tp^{2\nu})e^{-i\phi})=\dfrac{a(tp^{2\nu})}{\zeta^{\nu}}{\Re e\,}(\zeta^{\nu}e^{-i\phi})\quad\text{with}\quad\chi(p)=\zeta.

(2.8)

If
\mathrm{arg}(\zeta^{\nu})\equiv\phi\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\pmod{2\pi}
, the sequence
\{{\Re e\,}(a(tp^{2\nu})e^{-i\phi})\}_{p,\chi(p)=\zeta}
is trivial. Assume
\mathrm{arg}(\zeta^{\nu})\not\equiv\phi\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\pmod{2\pi}
, in this case the sequence
\{{\Re e\,}(a(tp^{2\nu})e^{-i\phi})\}_{p,\chi(p)=\zeta}
is not trivial. Without restriction of generality we can assume
{\Re e\,}(\zeta^{\nu}e^{-i\phi})>0
. Thus for any fixed
\epsilon>0
(but small) from (
2.5
) we have

\left\{p\in\mathbb{P}:p>\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}},\;\chi(p)=\zeta,\;\theta_{p}\in I_{\epsilon}\right\}\subset\{p\in\mathbb{P}:\chi(p)=\zeta,\;{\Re e\,}(a(tp^{2\nu})e^{-i\phi})>0\},

(2.9)

and

\left\{p\in\mathbb{P}:p>\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}},\;\chi(p)=\zeta,\;\theta_{p}\in I^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right\}\subset\{p\in\mathbb{P}:\chi(p)=\zeta,\;{\Re e\,}(a(tp^{2\nu})e^{-i\phi})<0\},

(2.10)

where

I_{\epsilon}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\left(\frac{(2j-2)\pi+\arcsin(\epsilon)}{\nu+1},\frac{(2j-1)\pi-\arcsin(\epsilon)}{\nu+1}\right)\subset[0,\pi],

and

I^{\prime}_{\epsilon}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\frac{\nu+1}{2}}\left(\frac{(2j-1)\pi+\arcsin(\epsilon)}{\nu+1},\frac{2j\pi-\arcsin(\epsilon)}{\nu+1}\right)\subset[0,\pi].

From Theorem
1
we know that the sequence
\{\theta_{p}\}_{p,\chi(p)=\zeta}
is equidistributed in
[0,\pi]
with respect to the Sato-Tate measure
\mu_{ST}
, it follows that there are infinitely many primes
p
satisfying
\chi(p)=\zeta
such that
\theta_{p}\in I^{\prime}_{\epsilon}
and infinitely many primes
p
satisfying
\chi(p)=\zeta
such that
\theta_{p}\in I_{\epsilon}
, thus the sets in (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) are infinite. Hence for all root of unity
\zeta
belonging to
\mathrm{Im}(\chi)
the sequence
\{a(tp^{2\nu})\}_{p,\chi(p)=\zeta}
is oscillatory, then the sequence
\{a(tp^{2\nu})\}_{p\in\mathbb{P}}
so is.

What is left is to calculate the natural densities
\delta(P_{>0}(\phi,\nu))
and
\delta(P_{<0}(\phi,\nu))
of the sets
P_{>0}(\phi,\nu)
and
P_{<0}(\phi,\nu)
. From (
2.8
) one observes that

P_{>0}(\phi,\nu)=\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\zeta,\text{root of unity}}{\zeta\in\text{Im}(\chi) ,\re(\zeta^\nu e^{-i\phi})>0}}\mathbb{P}_{>0}(\zeta,\nu)\bigsqcup\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\zeta,\text{root of unity}}{ \zeta\in\text{Im}(\chi) ,\re(\zeta^\nu e^{-i\phi})<0}}\mathbb{P}_{<0}(\zeta,\nu),

and

P_{<0}(\phi,\nu)=\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\zeta,\text{root of unity}}{\zeta\in\text{Im}(\chi) ,\re(\zeta^\nu e^{-i\phi})<0}}\mathbb{P}_{>0}(\zeta,\nu)\bigsqcup\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\zeta,\text{root of unity}}{ \zeta\in\text{Im}(\chi) ,\re(\zeta^\nu e^{-i\phi})>0}}\mathbb{P}_{<0}(\zeta,\nu),

up to finitely many primes. Then the above formula combined with Lemma
, gives

\displaystyle\delta\left(P_{>0}(\phi,\nu)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\zeta,\text{root of unity}}{ \zeta\in\text{Im}(\chi) ,\re(\zeta^\nu e^{-i\phi})>0}}\dfrac{\pi_{>0}(x,\zeta)}{\pi(x)}+\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\zeta,\text{root of unity}}{\zeta\in\text{Im}(\chi) ,\re(\zeta^\nu e^{-i\phi})<0}}\dfrac{\pi_{<0}(x,\zeta)}{\pi(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\zeta,\text{root of unity}}{ \zeta\in\text{Im}(\chi) ,\re(\zeta^\nu e^{-i\phi})\neq 0}}\frac{1}{r_{\chi}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(P_{\neq 0}(\phi,\nu))}{2}.

Likewise we get
\delta\left(P_{<0}(\phi,\nu)\right)=\frac{\delta(P_{\neq 0}(\phi,\nu))}{2}
, which concludes the proof.

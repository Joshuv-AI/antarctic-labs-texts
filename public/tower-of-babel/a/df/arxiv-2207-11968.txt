Abstract
We present a search for dark matter particles with sub- GeV / c 2 \mathrm{GeV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2} masses whose interactions have final state electrons using the DarkSide-50 experiment’s 12 306 ± 184 kg d 12\,306\pm 184\text{\,}\mathrm{kg}\text{\,}\mathrm{d} low-radioactivity liquid argon exposure. By analyzing the ionization signals, we exclude new parameter space for the dark matter-electron cross section σ ¯ e \bar{\sigma}_{e} , the axioelectric coupling constant g A ​ e g_{\mathrm{A}e} , and the dark photon kinetic mixing parameter κ \kappa . We also set the first dark matter direct-detection constraints on the mixing angle | U e ​ 4 | 2 \left|U_{e4}\right|^{2} for keV / c 2 \mathrm{keV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2} sterile neutrinos.



The very nature of dark matter (
DM
) remains unknown despite cosmological and astronomical observations collecting evidence of its existence over the last several decades
Faber and Gallagher 1979
;
Refregier 2003
;
Clowe et al. 2006
;
Thompson et al. 2015
;
Planck Collaboration et al. 2016
.
Traditionally,
DM
particles with masses ranging from a
\mathrm{GeV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}
to
few
\mathrm{TeV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}
have been extensively searched for by experiments located in underground laboratories by detecting their interactions with baryonic matter via elastic scattering off atomic nuclei
Tan et al. 2016
;
Aprile et al. 2018
;
Akerib et al. 2017a
;
Agnes et al. 2018a
;
Ajaj et al. 2019
;
Agnese et al. 2014
;
Jiang et al. 2018
;
Abdelhameed et al. 2019
– usually called nuclear recoils (
NRs
).
Heavy
DM
can also scatter off electrons, but the energy of such interactions – called electron recoils (
ERs
) – is suppressed due to the small electron mass.
The lack of concrete evidence of direct
DM
detection motivates the search for other candidates and their possible interactions via scattering off, or absorption by, shell electrons, which may subsequently produce sufficiently large ionization signals in the detector
Essig et al. 2012
.

This Letter reports on the analysis of the
653.1\text{\,}\mathrm{\mbox{live-days}}
of data
collected with the
DarkSide-50
experiment (
DS-50
) to probe
DM
interactions in the form of light
DM
-electron scattering, absorption of bosonic
DM
(axion-like particles and dark photons), and sterile neutrino-electron scattering.
This analysis uses a more accurate calibration of the detector response
Agnes et al. 2021
, improved background modeling and determination of its systematic uncertainties
Agnes et al. 2022a
, and a larger data-set compared to the previous study
Agnes et al. 2018b
. The same analysis approach was also applied to improve existing limits on spin-independent WIMP-nucleon interactions for WIMP masses down to 1.2 GeV/c
2

Agnes et al. 2022a

2

Agnes et al. 2022b

DS-50
is a dual-phase time projection chamber (
TPC
) housed in Italy’s INFN Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso (
LNGS
).
The active volume consists of low-radioactivity underground liquid argon (
LAr
).
Construction and performance details regarding the
DS-50
detector are described in Ref.
Agnes et al. 2015
.
Two measurable signals can be observed: the light from scintillation in the liquid (
S1
) and ionization electrons, which are drifted using a
200\text{\,}\mathrm{V}\text{/}\mathrm{cm}
electric field in the
LAr
volume and extracted by a
2.8\text{\,}\mathrm{kV}\text{/}\mathrm{cm}
electric field into the gas phase, producing electroluminescence photons (
S2
)
under acceleration by a
4.2\text{\,}\mathrm{kV}\text{/}\mathrm{cm}
electric field in the
GAr
volume.
Two arrays of 19 3-in photomultiplier tubes (
PMT
s
), one above the anode and one below the cathode, detect photons.
The
DS-50

TPC
, enclosed in a stainless steel double-walled, vacuum-insulated cryostat, lies inside a
30\text{\,}\mathrm{t}
boron-loaded liquid scintillator veto instrumented by 110 8-in
PMT
s
– to actively reject neutrons
in situ
– surrounded by a
1\text{\,}\mathrm{kt}
water Cerenkov veto with 80
PMT
s
– to actively tag cosmic muons and act as a passive shield against external backgrounds.

The data selection criteria for this analysis aim to identify single-scatter, low-energy events in the form of paired
S1
and
S2
or
S2
-only pulses uncorrelated to any previous event.
Various quality and selection cuts based on the ratio of
S1
and
S2
,
S2
signal time profile, and
S2
distribution across the
PMT
arrays are implemented
Agnes et al. 2022a
.
These cuts remove pileup pulses, surface
\alpha

events inducing electrons from the cathode, and spurious trapped ionization electrons released
up to
20\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}
after the previous event.
Moreover, only events reconstructed in the fiducial volume are selected, defined by the seven central top
PMT
s

Agnes et al. 2022a
.
Veto detector signals are not used in the data selection since
S2
triggers are delayed with respect to the veto by the electron drift time in the
TPC
.

The background model accounts for the natural radioactivity present in the

39

In this Letter, we search for several
DM

DS-50

The candidates are assumed to be non-relativistic and comprise all of the galactic

.
While additional local sources for dark matter may be present (e.g. solar production of dark photons

Following the recommendations in Ref.

we assume a local

544\text{\,}\mathrm{km}\text{/}\mathrm{s}

238\text{\,}\mathrm{km}\text{/}\mathrm{s}

Moreover, the predicted ionization rates per unit mass (

Fermion or scalar boson light dark matter (
LDM
) candidates, with masses below a
\mathrm{GeV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}
, can interact with bound electrons via a vector mediator, resulting in the ionization of argon atoms.
The
LDM
-electron interaction’s dependence on the momentum-transfer
q
is encapsulated by a dark matter form factor
F_{\mathrm{DM}}(q)
.
The ionization rate for a
LDM
candidate of mass
m_{\chi}
is parametrized by a reference cross section
\bar{\sigma}_{e}
as
Agnes et al. 2018c
;
Essig et al. 2012
;
Essig et al. 2017
:

\frac{dR}{d\ln{E_{\mathrm{er}}}}=N_{T}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}}{m_{\chi}}\times\frac{\bar{\sigma}_{e}}{8\mu^{2}_{\chi e}}\times\\
\sum_{n\ell}\int\left|f^{n\ell}_{\mathrm{ion}}(k^{\prime},q)\right|^{2}\,\left|F_{\mathrm{DM}}(q)\right|^{2}\,\eta(v_{\mathrm{min}})\,q\,dq

(1)

where
N_{T}
is the number of target atoms per unit mass,

\mu_{\chi e}
is the
DM
-electron reduced mass,

f^{n\ell}_{\mathrm{ion}}(k^{\prime},q)
is the ionization form factor modeling the effects of the bound-electron in the
(n,\ell)
shell and outgoing final state,

k^{\prime}=\sqrt{2m_{e}E_{\mathrm{er}}}
is the electron recoil momentum,
and
\eta(v_{\mathrm{min}})=\int\frac{1}{v}f(v)\Theta(v-v_{\mathrm{min}})\,dv

is the inverse mean speed function that encodes the
DM
velocity profile for the minimum
DM
velocity (
v_{\mathrm{min}}
) required to eject an electron with
E_{\mathrm{er}}
given
q

Agnes et al. 2018c
.
Two benchmark interaction models are considered: a heavy mediator (mass
\gg\alpha m_{e}
) with
F_{\mathrm{DM}}=1
and a light mediator (mass
\ll\alpha m_{e}
) with
F_{\mathrm{DM}}=(\alpha m_{e}/q)^{2}
, where
\alpha
is the fine structure constant and
m_{e}
is the electron mass.

DS-50

The absorption rate per unit mass of galactic ALPs depends on the axion-electron coupling strength

R=N_{T}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}}{m_{\mathrm{A}}}\times\frac{3m_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}g_{\mathrm{A}e}^{2}}{16\pi\alpha m_{e}^{2}}\sigma_{\mathrm{pe}}(m_{\mathrm{A}}c^{2})c

(2)

while that of dark photons depends on the strength of the kinetic mixing
\kappa

Pospelov et al. 2008

R=N_{T}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}}{m_{\mathrm{A}^{\prime}}}\times\kappa^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{pe}}(m_{\mathrm{A}^{\prime}}c^{2})c

\sigma_{\mathrm{pe}}

Henke et al. 1993

A sterile neutrino
\nu_{s}
with a mass between
7\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}
and
36\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}

DM

Leo et al. 2017
;
Schneider et al. 2014
.
Sterile neutrinos interact via
\nu_{s}+e\rightarrow\nu_{e}+e
(and
\bar{\nu}_{s}+e\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{e}+e
)
Campos and Rodejohann 2016
, where a
\nu_{s}

\left|U_{e4}\right|^{2}

The ionization rate is governed by the cross section
\sigma_{n\ell}
between
\nu_{s}
and an electron in a given orbital
(n,\ell)

\frac{dR}{dE_{\mathrm{er}}}=N_{T}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}}{m_{\nu}}\times\\
\sum_{n\ell}2(2\ell+1)\int\frac{d\sigma_{n\ell}}{dE_{\mathrm{er}}}(v,m_{\nu},\left|U_{e4}\right|^{2})~f(v)\,v\,dv

(4)

where

We note that Eq. (
4

1

Additionally, we note that the scattering rates calculated in Ref.
Campos and Rodejohann 2016

DM

This analysis employs a binned Profile Likelihood Ratio (PLR) approach
Cowan et al. 2011
;
Moneta et al. 2011
to determine exclusion limits for each
DM
candidate.
The PLR includes a set of nuisance parameters representing the nominal values and uncertainties on the exposure, materials screening, theoretical energy spectra shape, and ionization energy scale.
Correlations among the different components are encoded in the likelihood definition.
See Ref.
Agnes et al. 2022a
for a complete description of the above. We also verified that the expected limits have negligible dependence on ER fluctuations by changing the Gaussian model described in Ref.
Agnes et al. 2022a
to a binomial one.

We have established the best direct-detection limits on dark matter-electron scattering in the mass range of
16\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}56\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}
for a heavy mediator and above
80\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}

These new
DS-50

and
7\text{\,}\mathrm{\mbox{$e^{-}$}}

Agnes et al. 2022a

Additional sensitivity gain comes from improved data selection, a more accurate detector calibration, improved background modeling, and a larger data-set.

We have also placed the first constraints on galactic axion-like particles and dark photons with an argon target.
Stronger direct-detection limits are placed on both

and
\kappa

0.03\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}
and
0.2\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}\text{/}{\mathrm{\mbox{$c$}}}^{2}

DS-50
is the first

Under the Standard Halo Model assumption, our results improve upon existing direct limits set by a high-precision measurement of the

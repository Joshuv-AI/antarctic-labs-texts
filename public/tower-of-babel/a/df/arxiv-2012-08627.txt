Conformal Minimal Foliations on Semi-Riemannian Lie Groups
Elsa Ghandour, Sigmundur Gudmundsson, Victor Ottosson

Abstract
. We study left-invariant foliations ℱ \mathcal{F} on semi-Riemannian Lie groups G G generated by a subgroup K K . We are interested in such foliations which are conformal and with minimal leaves of codimension two. We classify such foliations ℱ \mathcal{F} when the subgroup K K is one of the important SU ​ ( 2 ) \text{\bf SU}(2) , SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \text{\bf SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}) , SU ​ ( 2 ) × SU ​ ( 2 ) \text{\bf SU}(2)\times\text{\bf SU}(2) , SU ​ ( 2 ) × SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \text{\bf SU}(2)\times\text{\bf SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}) , SU ​ ( 2 ) × SO ​ ( 2 ) \text{\bf SU}(2)\times\text{\bf SO}(2) , SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) × SO ​ ( 2 ) \text{\bf SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})\times\text{\bf SO}(2) . This way we construct new multi-dimensional families of Lie groups G G carrying such foliations in each case. These foliations ℱ \mathcal{F} produce local complex-valued harmonic morphisms on the corresponding Lie group G G .

G

[

The authors classify the simply connected Lie groups
G

\mathcal{F}

2.
Conformal Semi-Riemannian Foliations

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian manifold,
\mathcal{V}
be an integrable distribution on
M
and denote by
\mathcal{H}
its orthogonal complement distribution.
As customary, we also use
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{H}
to denote the orthogonal projections onto the corresponding subbundles of
TM

and denote by
\mathcal{F}
the foliation tangent to
\mathcal{V}
. Then the second fundamental form for
\mathcal{V}
is given by

B^{\mathcal{V}}(E,F)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{H}(\nabla_{E}F+\nabla_{F}E)=\mathcal{H}(\nabla_{E}F)\qquad(E,F\in\mathcal{V}),

while the second fundamental form for
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

B^{\mathcal{H}}(E,F)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{V}(\nabla_{E}F+\nabla_{F}E)\qquad(E,F\in\mathcal{H}).

The foliation
\mathcal{F}
tangent to
\mathcal{V}
is said to be
conformal
if there exists a vector field
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that

B^{\mathcal{H}}=g\otimes V,

and
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
semi-Riemannian
if
V=0
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
minimal
if
\text{trace}\ B^{\mathcal{V}}=0
and
totally geodesic
if
B^{\mathcal{V}}=0
. This is equivalent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
being minimal and totally geodesic submanifolds of
M
, respectively.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian manifold,
\mathcal{V}
be an integrable distribution on
M
of codimension two and
\mathcal{H}
be its orthogonal complementary distribution. Then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
tangent to
\mathcal{V}
is conformal if and only if

\varepsilon_{X}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)-\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Y)=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,Y)=0,

for any local orthonormal frame
\{X,Y\}
for the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is conformal then it is semi-Riemannian if and only if

\varepsilon_{X}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Y)=0.

Proof.

The foliation
\mathcal{F}
is conformal if and only if there exists a vector field
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that
B^{\mathcal{H}}(E,F)=g(E,F)\otimes V
for all
E,F\in\mathcal{H}
. Let
\{X,Y\}
be a local orthonormal frame for
\mathcal{H}
and
\varepsilon_{X}
,
\varepsilon_{Y}
the corresponding causal charachters. Then

\varepsilon_{X}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Y)=(\varepsilon_{X}\cdot g(X,X)+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot g(Y,Y))\otimes V=2\,V

and hence
B^{\mathcal{H}}
has the following form

(2.1)

B^{\mathcal{H}}(E,F)=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot g(E,F)\otimes\bigl(\varepsilon_{X}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Y)\bigr).

This tells us that the conformality of
\mathcal{F}
implies
B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,Y)=0
and further

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)-\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot B^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot(\varepsilon_{X}\cdot(g(X,X)-\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot g(Y,Y))\otimes V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot(\varepsilon_{X}^{2}-\varepsilon_{Y}^{2})\otimes V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

The other direction of the first statement is an easy exercise left to the reader. The second statement is an immediate consequence of formula (
2.1
).
∎

3.
Lie Foliations of Codimension Two

Let
(G,g)
be a semi-Riemannian Lie group with subgroup
K
of codimension two and
\mathcal{F}
be the Lie foliation on
G
generated by the left-invariant translations of
K
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the integrable distribution, tangent to the fibres of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{H}
be its complementary orthogonal distribution. Further let
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{n},X,Y\}
be an orthonormal basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that
X
and
Y
generate
\mathcal{H}
.

For the rest of this paper, we shall make extensive use of the well-known Koszul formula, adapted to the particular situation of semi-Riemannian Lie groups. For left-invariant vector fields
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

2\cdot g(\hbox{$\nabla$\kern-3.00003pt\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{$X$}\kern-1.00006pt{$Y$}},Z)=g([Z,X],Y)+g([Z,Y],X)+g(Z,[X,Y]).

We also add the following standard calculations which turn out to be useful as we go along.

For the second fundamental form
B^{\mathcal{H}}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{V}
, of the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
, we see that for all left-invariant vector fields
E,F\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle B^{\mathcal{H}}(E,F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\bigl(\mathcal{V}\nabla_{E}F+\mathcal{V}\nabla_{F}E\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{V_{k}}\cdot\bigl(g(\nabla_{E}F,V_{k})+g(\nabla_{F}E,V_{k})\bigr)V_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{V_{k}}\cdot\bigl(g([E,V_{k}],F)+g([F,V_{k}],E)\bigr)V_{k}.

Similarly, we note that for the second fundamental form
B^{\mathcal{V}}:\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{H}
, of the vertical distribution
\mathcal{V}
, we have for all left-invariant vector fields
E,F\in\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle B^{\mathcal{V}}(E,F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\bigl(\mathcal{H}\nabla_{E}F+\mathcal{H}\nabla_{F}E\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\bigl(\varepsilon_{X}\cdot(g(\nabla_{E}F,X)+g(\nabla_{F}E,X))X

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot(g(\nabla_{E}F,Y)+g(\nabla_{F}E,Y))Y\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\bigl(\varepsilon_{X}\cdot\bigl(g([X,E],F)+g([X,F],E)\bigr)X

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot\bigl(g([Y,E],F)+g([Y,F],E)\bigr)Y\bigr).

For the completeness of our exposition, we now state the following result. This has already been applied in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, where the proving arguments can be found.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(G,g)
be a semi-Riemannian Lie group with a subgroup
K
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the integrable distribution tangent to
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{H}
be the orthogonal complementary distribution of dimension two. Then

\mathcal{H}[\,[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}],\mathcal{H}\,]=0.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(G,g)
be a semi-Riemannian Lie group with a semisimple subgroup
K
, of codimension two, generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. Then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is semi-Riemannian.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the integrable distribution tangent to
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{H}
be the orthogonal complementary distribution of dimension two. Since the subgroup
K
is semisimple we know that
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}]=\mathcal{V}
and it then follows from Theorem

3.1
that
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H}]=0
. This proves the statement.
∎

\text{\bf SU}(2)

Let
(G,g)
be a five dimensional semi-Riemannian Lie group with the

subgroup
\text{\bf SU}(2)

\mathcal{F}
on

. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{m}

\mathfrak{g}
of

\{A,B,C,X,Y\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{g}

A,B
and

generate the subalgebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
. Then the Lie bracket relations for
\mathfrak{su}(2)
are given by

[A,B]=2\,C,\quad[C,A]=2\,B,\quad[B,C]=2\,A.

Since the Lie group
\text{\bf SU}(2)
is simple and the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is conformal, we get from Theorem
3.1
that
\mathcal{H}[V,H]=0
, for all
V\in\mathcal{V},H\in\mathcal{H}
. The remaining bracket relations for
\mathfrak{g}
are thus given by

\displaystyle[A,X]=a_{11}A+a_{12}B+a_{13}C,\quad[A,Y]=a_{21}A+a_{22}B+a_{23}C,

\displaystyle[B,X]=b_{11}A+b_{12}B+b_{13}C,\quad[B,Y]=b_{21}A+b_{22}B+b_{23}C,

\displaystyle[C,X]=c_{11}A+c_{12}B+c_{13}C,\quad[C,Y]=c_{21}A+c_{22}B+c_{23}C,

\displaystyle[X,Y]=\rho X+\theta_{1}A+\theta_{2}B+\theta_{3}C,

for some, not necessarily independent, constant coefficients. By using the Jacobi identity we can now simplify this system.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(G,g)
be a five dimensional semi-Riemannian Lie group with the subgroup
\text{\bf SU}(2)

\mathcal{F}

. Let

of

be an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{g}
such that
A,B
and
C
generate the subalgebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
. Then the Lie bracket relations for
\mathfrak{g}
are given by

\displaystyle[A,B]=2C,\quad[C,A]=2B,\quad[B,C]=2A

\displaystyle[A,X]=-b_{11}B-c_{11}C,\quad[A,Y]=-b_{21}B-c_{21}C,

\displaystyle[B,X]=b_{11}A-c_{12}C,\quad[B,Y]=b_{21}A-c_{22}C,

\displaystyle[C,X]=c_{11}A+c_{12}B,\quad[C,Y]=c_{21}A+c_{22}B,

\displaystyle[X,Y]=\rho X+\theta_{1}A+\theta_{2}B+\theta_{3}C,

where the real coefficients
b_{11},b_{21},c_{11},c_{12},c_{21},c_{22},\rho
are arbitrary and

\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{1}\\
\theta_{2}\\
\theta_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\rho c_{12}+b_{11}c_{21}-b_{21}c_{11}\\
\phantom{-}\rho c_{11}+b_{11}c_{22}-b_{21}c_{12}\\
-\rho b_{11}+c_{11}c_{22}-c_{21}c_{12}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

We first consider the Jacobi identities involving the left-invariant vector fields
A,B,C,X
and yield

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[A,B],X]+[[X,A],B]+[[B,X],A]

\displaystyle\qquad 2\,((a_{13}+c_{11})A+(b_{13}+c_{12})B-(a_{11}+b_{12}-c_{13})C),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[A,C],X]+[[X,A],C]+[[C,X],A]

\displaystyle\qquad 2\,(-(a_{12}+b_{11})A+(a_{11}-b_{12}+c_{13})B-(b_{13}+c_{12})C),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[B,C],X]+[[X,B],C]+[[C,X],B]

\displaystyle\qquad 2\,((a_{11}-b_{12}-c_{13})A+(a_{12}+b_{11})B+(a_{13}+c_{11})C).

Since these all have to vanish, in order for
\mathfrak{g}
to be a Lie algebra, we obtain the following system of equations

\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}+b_{12}-c_{13}\\
a_{11}-b_{12}+c_{13}\\
a_{11}-b_{12}-c_{13}\\

This simplifies to

\begin{pmatrix}a_{12}\\

b_{13}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}b_{11}\\
c_{11}\\
c_{12}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}\\

Since the Lie bracket relations are symmetric in
X
and
Y
, with respect to
A,B
and
C
, we similarly obtain

\begin{pmatrix}a_{22}\\
a_{23}\\

c_{22}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}a_{21}\\

Next we consider the Jacobi identities involving both

\displaystyle[[A,X],Y]+[[Y,A],X]+[[X,Y],A]=(\rho b_{11}-c_{11}c_{22}+c_{21}c_{12}+2\theta_{3})B

\displaystyle\,+(\rho c_{11}+b_{11}c_{22}-b_{21}c_{12}-2\theta_{2})C,

\displaystyle[[B,X],Y]+[[Y,B],X]+[[X,Y],B]=-(\rho b_{11}-c_{11}c_{22}+c_{21}c_{12}-2\theta_{3})A

With the following result we give a complete answer to when the conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
is minimal and even totally geodesic. This shows that the general situation here is
different
from the special Riemannian case presented in Theorem 4.1 of
[
7
]
. Furthermore it shows that Conjecture
1.1
does not hold in the semi-Riemannian situation.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(G,g)
be a five dimensional semi-Riemannian Lie group with the subgroup
\text{\bf SU}(2)
generating a left-invariant conformal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{m}
be an orthogonal decomposition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
and let
\{A,B,C,X,Y\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{g}
such that
A,B
and
C
generate the subalgebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
. Then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is semi-Riemannian and minimal. It is totally geodesic if and only if the following conditions hold

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{B}-\varepsilon_{A})\,b_{11}=(\varepsilon_{B}-\varepsilon_{A})\,b_{21}=(\varepsilon_{C}-\varepsilon_{A})\,c_{11},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{C}-\varepsilon_{A})\,c_{21}=(\varepsilon_{C}-\varepsilon_{B})\,c_{12}=(\varepsilon_{C}-\varepsilon_{B})\,c_{22}.

Proof.

The foliation
\mathcal{F}
is minimal if and only if the trace of the second fundamental form
B^{\mathcal{V}}
vanishes. We now have

\displaystyle B^{\mathcal{V}}(A,A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot g([X,A],A)X+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot g([Y,A],A)Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot g(b_{11}B+c_{11}C,A)X+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot g(b_{21}B+c_{21}C,A)Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle B^{\mathcal{V}}(B,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot g([X,B],B)X+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot g([Y,B],B)Y=0,

\displaystyle B^{\mathcal{V}}(C,C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot g([X,C],C)X+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot g([Y,C],C)Y=0.

Consequently, we see that
\mathcal{F}
is minimal by default, independent of the choice of the metric
g
or the structure coefficients of the Lie algebra. The following confirms the last statement.

\displaystyle 2\,B^{\mathcal{V}}(A,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot(g([X,A],B)+g([X,B],A))X

\displaystyle+\,\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot(g([Y,A],B)+g([Y,B],A))Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot(g(b_{11}B+c_{11}C,B)+g(-b_{11}A+c_{12}C,A))X

\displaystyle+\,\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot(g(b_{21}B+c_{21}C,B)+g(-b_{21}A+c_{22}C,A))Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot(b_{11}\varepsilon_{B}-b_{11}\varepsilon_{A})X+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot(b_{21}\varepsilon_{B}-b_{21}\varepsilon_{A})Y,

\displaystyle 2\,B^{\mathcal{V}}(A,C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot(c_{11}\varepsilon_{C}-c_{11}\varepsilon_{A})X+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot(c_{21}\varepsilon_{C}-c_{21}\varepsilon_{A})Y,

\displaystyle 2\,B^{\mathcal{V}}(B,C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\cdot(c_{12}\varepsilon_{C}-c_{12}\varepsilon_{B})X+\varepsilon_{Y}\cdot(c_{22}\varepsilon_{C}-c_{22}\varepsilon_{B})Y.

∎

(b)

three 3-dimensional families, where the free variables are

(c)

one 1-dimensional family, where

\displaystyle 0=(x_{1}c_{12}+y_{1}c_{22})=(x_{2}c_{12}+y_{2}c_{22}),

\displaystyle 0=(x_{1}c_{11}+y_{1}c_{21})=(x_{2}c_{11}+y_{2}c_{21}),

\displaystyle 0=(x_{1}b_{11}+y_{1}b_{21})=(x_{2}b_{11}+y_{2}b_{21}).

Proof.

Here we can apply exactly the same technique presented in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

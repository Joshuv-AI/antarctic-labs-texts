1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the family of analytic functions in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, satisfying
f(0)=0=f^{\prime}(0)-1
, and having the form

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}\,z^{k},\quad a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}.

The subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of all univalent functions
f(z)
in
\mathbb{D}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}
. A domain
D
in
\mathbb{C}
is called starlike with respect to origin or simply starlike if every line segment joining the origin to any point in
D
lies completely inside
D
. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is called starlike if
f(\mathbb{D})
is a starlike domain. The subclass of starlike functions in
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{*}
and satisfies the differential inequality
\Re\left(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\right)>0
, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is convex if
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike with respect to every point inside the domain
f(\mathbb{D})
. The subclass of convex functions in
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}
and satisfies the inequality
\Re\left(1+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For a given
0\leq\beta<1
, a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is called a starlike function of order
\beta
and denoted by
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\beta)
if and only if
\Re\left(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\right)>\beta
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Similarly, for a given
0\leq\beta<1
, a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is called a convex function of order
\beta
, denoted by
f\in\mathcal{K}(\beta)
if and only if
\Re\left(1+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)\right)>\beta
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. A domain
D
is called convex in the direction
\theta\;(0\leq\theta<\pi)
if every line parallel to the line through
0
and
e^{i\theta}
has a connected or empty intersection with
D
. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is known to be convex in the direction
\theta\;(0\leq\theta<\pi)
if it maps
\mathbb{D}
to a domain convex in the direction
\theta
.

A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is close-to-convex in
\mathbb{D}
if
\mathbb{C}\setminus f(\mathbb{D})
can be represented as a union of non-crossing half lines. For every convex function
\psi
, a close-to-convex function
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfies the inequality
\Re\left(e^{i\theta}f^{\prime}(z)/\psi^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. The subset of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of close-to-convex functions is denoted by
\mathcal{C}
. It is well known that close-to-convex functions are univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, but not necessarily the converse. In particular, if
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
\Re(e^{i\theta}f^{\prime}(z))>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, then
f
is close-to-convex and hence univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. It is easy to note that
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}\subset\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{S}

[
1
]
. Now again, for a given
0\leq\beta<1
, a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is called close-to-convex of order
\beta
, denoted by
f\in\mathcal{C}(\beta)
, if
\Re\left(e^{i\theta}f^{\prime}(z)/\psi^{\prime}(z)\right)>\beta
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

A function
f
is subordinate to the function
\psi
, written as
f\prec\psi,
if there exists a Schwarz function
w
, with
w(0)=0
and
|w(z)|\leq|z|
, such that
f(z)=\psi(w(z))
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If
\psi\in\mathcal{S}
, then the following relation holds true:

f(z)\prec\psi(z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(0)=\psi(0)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;f(\mathbb{D})\subset\psi(\mathbb{D}).

Let
E\subset\mathcal{A}
and
P
denote the property of the image of
f\in E
. The largest
\gamma_{f}
such that
f\in E
has a property
P
in each disk
\mathbb{D}_{\rho}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z|<\rho\}
for
0<\rho\leq\gamma_{f}
is known as the radius of the property
P
of
f
. The number
\gamma:=\mbox{inf}\{\gamma_{f}:\;f\in E\}
is the radius of the property
P
of the class
E
. If
\mathcal{G}
is the class of all
f\in E
characterized by the property
P
, then
\gamma
is called the
\mathcal{G}
-radius of the class
E
.

The study of Umezawa
[
2
]
showed that a function
f
is locally univalent, having the form as in (
1.1
) and satisfying

(1.2)

\gamma>\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>-\;\dfrac{\gamma}{2\gamma-3},

where
\gamma
is a real number not less than
3/2
, then
f
is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover,
f
maps every subdisk of
\mathbb{D}
into a domain convex in one direction. Restricting
\gamma
to
3/2
(as many cases of (
1.2
) can be drawn by allowing different values of
\gamma\geq 3/2
), the subclass
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{S}
satisfying

(1.3)

\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)<\dfrac{3}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

is obtained. Ozaki
[
3
]
showed that functions in
\mathcal{G}
are univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Thereafter, Singh and Singh
[
4
, Theorem 6]
showed that functions in
\mathcal{G}
are starlike in
\mathbb{D}
.

The
n
-th section (partial sum) of
f\in\mathcal{A}
is the polynomial

(1.4)

s_{n}(z;f)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{n}a_{k}\,z^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The second section
s_{2}(z;f)
of the Koebe function
k(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}_{1/4}
. But as
s^{\prime}_{2}(-1/4;f)=0
, it is not univalent in any disk larger than
\mathbb{D}_{1/4}
. Proceeding from it, Szegö
[
5
]
proved that
s_{n}(z;f)
of
f\in\mathcal{S}
are univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{1/4}
, and the number
1/4
cannot be replaced by a larger one. Somewhat later, Robertson
[
6
]
showed that
s_{n}(z;f)
is univalent in
|z|<1-(3/n)\log n
for all
n\geq 5
. The Koebe function is an extremal for many subclasses of
f\in\mathcal{S}
, but Bshouty and Hergartner
[
7
]
showed that the Koebe function is not extremal for the radius of univalency of
s_{n}(z;f)
for
f\in\mathcal{S}
. As shown in
[
1
, p. 246]
, the exact (largest) radius of univalency of
s_{n}(z;f)
for
f\in\mathcal{S}
remains an open problem. Therefore, this is an area of interest.

Recently, Obradović and Ponnusamy
[
8
]
showed that each section
s_{n}(z;f)
of
f\in\mathcal{G}
is starlike in the disk
|z|\leq 1/2
for
n\geq 12
and
\Re(s_{n}^{\prime}(z))>0
holds in the disk
|z|\leq 1/2
for
n\geq 13
. Results for the best approximation of the generalized version of class
\mathcal{G}
have been obtained in
[
9
]
. Also, it is shown in
[
10
]
that the radius of convexity of class
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathbb{D}_{1/2}
and it is irreplaceable with a larger disk.

The generalized version of the class
\mathcal{G}
can be obtained from
[
11
]
, which is denoted as
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
, consisting of locally univalent functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
, which satisfies

(1.5)

\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)<1+\dfrac{\alpha}{2},\;\;z\in\mathbb{D},

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)\neq\emptyset
for
\alpha>0
, as the identity function satisfies (
1.5
) for
\alpha>0
. Here the class
\mathcal{G}\equiv\mathcal{G}(1)
. Many results, including sharp coefficient bounds for
f\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
, sharp bounds for the Fekete-Szegö functional for functions in
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
with complex parameters, a convolution characterization for functions
f\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
, and sufficient coefficient conditions for
f
to be in
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
, have been shown in
[
11
]
. Also, as shown in
[
11
]
, each section
s_{n}(z;f)
of
f\in\mathcal{G}
is starlike in the disk
|z|\leq 1/2
for
n\geq 11
and close-to-convex (and hence univalent) in the disk
|z|\leq 1/2
for
n\geq 11
.

As discussed recently in
[
9
]
,
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)\subset\mathcal{G}
whenever
\alpha\in(0,1]
. That is,
f\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
is starlike and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, whenever
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Kargar et al.
[
9
]
showed (with the help of an example) that
f\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
doesn’t contain only univalent functions in
\mathbb{D}
, when
\alpha\geq 2
. They
[
9
]
showed that the radius of univalence of
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
is at least
1/\alpha
when
\alpha\in[1,4.952)
. Also, they have established the best approximation of an analytic function by functions in a subclass of
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
.

Here we obtain radii of convexity, starlikeness, and close-to-convexity of order
\beta
for all sections (
s_{n}(z;f)
,
n\geq 2
) of functions in the class
\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
along with some properties of it.
Section 2
contains all known results that were used in our obtained results.
Section 3
is dedicated to all of our obtained results with their detailed proofs.

2.
Known results

We use the following known results in our obtained results:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
11
]

If
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
, then

|a_{n}|\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{n(n-1)},\quad n\geq 2.

The inequality is sharp and equality is attained for a function
f
, where
f^{\prime}(z)=(1-z^{n-1})^{\alpha/(n-1)},

n\geq 2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
11
,
9
]
If
f(z)\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
, then the following inequalities and subordination results hold:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\alpha f^{\prime}(z)}+\dfrac{|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right|\leq\;\dfrac{|z|}{1-|z|^{2}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(z)\prec\;(1-z)^{\alpha},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(z)}{z}\prec\;\dfrac{1-(1-z)^{1+\alpha}}{z(1+\alpha)},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\dfrac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\;\dfrac{(1+\alpha)(1-z)}{1+\alpha-z}.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\rho\in[0,1)
and
n\geq 2
, then the following sequences are decreasing:

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,\rho)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\dfrac{\rho^{k+n}}{(k+n)(k+n-1)},\quad S_{2}(n,\rho)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\dfrac{\rho^{k+n-1}}{k+n-1}.

Proof.

Note that,

\displaystyle S_{1}(n+1,\rho)-S_{1}(n,\rho)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho}{(k+n+1)(k+n)}-\dfrac{1}{(k+n)(k+n-1)}\right)\rho^{k+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{\rho(k+n-1)-(k+n+1)}{(k+n)\left((k+n)^{2}-1\right)}\right)\rho^{k+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{(1-\rho)(k+n)+\rho+1}{(k+n)\left((k+n)^{2}-1\right)}\right)\rho^{k+n}<0.

Hence,
S_{1}(n,\rho)
is a decreasing sequence for all
n\geq 2
. Similarly, we can show that
S_{2}(n,\rho)
is a decreasing sequence.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
and
\sigma_{n}(z;f)=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
. Then, for
n\geq 2
and
|z|=\rho<1
, the following inequalities hold:

(2.10)

\displaystyle|\sigma_{n}(z;f)|\;\leq

\displaystyle\;\;\alpha\left((1-\rho)\ln(1-\rho)+\rho-\dfrac{\rho^{2}}{2}\right),

(2.11)

\displaystyle|\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|\;\leq

\displaystyle\;\;-\alpha\left(\ln(1-\rho)+\rho\right),

(2.12)

\displaystyle|\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)|\;\leq

\displaystyle\;\;\dfrac{\alpha\,\rho}{1-\rho}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\sigma_{n}(z;f)|

\displaystyle\leq\;\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\dfrac{\alpha\,\rho^{k+n}}{(k+n)(k+n-1)}=\alpha\,S_{1}(n,\rho)\leq\alpha\,S_{1}(2,\rho)

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\dfrac{\rho^{k}}{k(k-1)}=\alpha\left((1-\rho)\ln(1-\rho)+\rho-\dfrac{\rho^{2}}{2}\right).

Similarly, we can find bounds on
|\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|
and
|\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)|
.
∎

3.
Main results

The following are our newly established results, along with their proofs.

3.1.
Radius properties for the sections

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
. Then
s_{n}(z;f)
is convex of order
\beta
in the disk
|z|<\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
for all
n\geq 2
, where
\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
is the least positive root of
I_{\alpha,\beta}(\rho)=0
in
[0,1)
for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\beta\in[0,1)
, where
I_{\alpha,\beta}(\rho)
is given by

(3.13)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha,\beta}(\rho)

\displaystyle=(1-\rho)^{\alpha}\left(\rho(1+\alpha-\beta)-(1-\beta)\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha\rho\left((2-\beta)\rho-(1-\beta)\right)-\alpha(1-\beta)(1-\rho)\ln(1-\rho).

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{G}(\alpha)
. Using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{2}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)\geq 1-\dfrac{2|a_{2}||z|}{1-2|a_{2}||z|}\geq 1-\dfrac{\alpha|z|}{1-\alpha|z|}=\dfrac{1-2\alpha\rho}{1-\alpha\rho}>\beta,

if

\rho<\dfrac{1-\beta}{\alpha(2-\beta)}:=\psi_{1}(\alpha,\beta).

Note that

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\psi_{1}(\alpha,\beta)=-\,\dfrac{1}{\alpha(2-\beta)^{2}}<0.

Therefore

\rho<\psi_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\psi_{1}(\alpha,0)=\dfrac{1}{2\alpha}:=\rho_{1}.

Hence,
\rho_{1}<1
if
\alpha\in(1/2,1]
and
\rho_{1}\geq 1
if
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. Thus,
s_{2}(z;f)
is convex of order
\beta
in the disk
|z|<\rho_{1}
if
\alpha\in(1/2,1]
and
s_{2}(z;f)
is convex of order
\beta
in whole
\mathbb{D}
if
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. Further, using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{3}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{3}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Re\left(\dfrac{1+4a_{2}z+9a_{3}z^{2}}{1+2a_{2}z+3a_{3}z^{2}}\right)=2-\Re\left(\dfrac{1-3a_{3}z^{2}}{1+2a_{2}z+3a_{3}z^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2-\left|\dfrac{1-3a_{3}z^{2}}{1+2a_{2}z+3a_{3}z^{2}}\right|\geq 2-\dfrac{1+3|a_{3}||z|^{2}}{1-2|a_{2}||z|-3|a_{3}||z|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2-\dfrac{2+\alpha|z|^{2}}{2-2\alpha|z|-\alpha|z|^{2}}=\dfrac{2-4\alpha\rho-3\alpha\rho^{2}}{2-2\alpha\rho-\alpha\rho^{2}}>\beta,

if

\rho<\dfrac{\sqrt{(2-\beta)^{2}+2\alpha(1-\beta)(3-\beta)}-\alpha(2-\beta)}{\alpha(3-\beta)}:=\psi_{2}(\alpha,\beta).

\psi_{2}(\alpha,\beta)
and
\alpha
, for different values of
\beta\in[0,1)
.

1
shows the graphs of
\psi_{2}(\alpha,\beta)
for different values of
\beta
, which shows that
\psi_{2}(\alpha,\beta)
is a decreasing function of
\beta
. Hence

\psi_{2}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\psi_{2}(\alpha,0)=\dfrac{\sqrt{4+6\alpha}-2\alpha}{3\alpha}:=\rho_{2}<1,

if
\alpha\in(0.5376,1]
. Note that
\rho_{2}\geq 1
if
\alpha\in(0,0.5376]
. Thus,
s_{3}(z;f)
is convex of order
\beta
in the disk
|z|<\rho_{2}
if
\alpha\in(0.5376,1]
, and
s_{3}(z;f)
is convex of order
\beta
in whole
\mathbb{D}
if
\alpha\in(0,0.5376]
.

Next, we consider the general case for all
n\geq 2
. To prove that
s_{n}(z;f)
is convex of order
\beta
in a disk
|z|<\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
, it is sufficient to prove that for
|z|<\rho_{\mathcal{K}},

s_{n}^{\prime}(z;f)\neq 0
and

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{n}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+\dfrac{\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)-z\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)}{f^{\prime}(z)-\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\left|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|-\dfrac{\left|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|\left|\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)\right|+\left|z\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)\right|}{\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|-\left|\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)\right|}>\beta,

where
\sigma_{n}(z;f)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n+1}\alpha_{k}z^{k}
. Using
2.6
, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\dfrac{\alpha\,\rho\,(1+\rho)}{1-\rho^{2}}=\dfrac{\alpha\,\rho}{1-\rho}.

Further, using (
2.7
) and (
2.11
) for
|z|=\rho
, we obtain

(3.16)

|f^{\prime}(z)|-|\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|\geq(1-\rho)^{\alpha}+\alpha(\ln(1-\rho)+\rho):=\psi_{3}(\alpha,\rho).

Since
\dfrac{\partial\psi_{3}(\alpha,\rho)}{\partial\alpha}<0
for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
, therefore

\psi_{3}(\alpha,\rho)\geq\psi_{3}(1,\rho)=1+\ln(1-\rho),

is positive when
|z|<\rho_{3},
where
\rho_{3}\approx 0.6321
. Thus,

(3.17)

|\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|<|f^{\prime}(z)|,\quad|z|<\rho_{3}.

Note that
\dfrac{\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)}{f^{\prime}(z)}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
since
f^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
. Hence, if (
3.17
) holds for
|z|=\rho_{\mathcal{K}},
then the same inequality holds for
0<|z|<\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
when
s_{n}^{\prime}(z;f)\neq 0
for
0<|z|<\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
. Consequently,
1+\dfrac{zs_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{n}^{\prime}(z;f)}
is analytic on
|z|\leq\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
, and if
\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{n}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)\geq\beta

, then
s_{n}(z;f)
is convex of order
\beta
in
|z|<\rho_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Now using inequalities (
2.11
), (
2.12
), (
3.15
), (
3.16
) in (

), we obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{n}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\dfrac{\alpha\rho}{1-\rho}+\dfrac{\dfrac{\alpha\rho}{1-\rho}\;\alpha(\ln(1-\rho)+\rho)-\dfrac{\alpha\rho^{2}}{1-\rho}}{(1-\rho)^{\alpha}+\alpha(\ln(1-\rho)+\rho)}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\dfrac{\alpha\rho}{1-\rho}+\dfrac{\alpha^{2}\rho\ln(1-\rho)-\alpha\rho^{2}(1-\alpha)}{(1-\rho)\left((1-\rho)^{\alpha}+\alpha(\ln(1-\rho)+\rho)\right)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(1-\rho)^{\alpha}(1-\rho(1+\alpha))+\alpha\rho(1-2\rho)+\alpha(1-\rho)\ln(1-\rho)}{(1-\rho)\left((1-\rho)^{\alpha}+\alpha(\ln(1-\rho)+\rho)\right)}

Theorem 3.4
.

(3.38)

\underset{|z|=0.6321}{\max}|\arg f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\sin^{-1}(0.6321)<39.206^{\circ}.

Using Lemma
2.6

Random Multiple Operator Integrals
Shih Yu Chang

Abstract
The introduction of Schur multipliers into the context of Double Operator Integrals (DOIs) was proposed by V. V. Peller in 1985. This work extends theorem on Schur multipliers from measurable functions to their closure space and generalizes the definition of DOIs to Multiple Operator Integrals (MOIs) for integrand functions as Schur multipliersconstructible by taking the limit of projective tensor product and by taking the limit of integral projective tensor product. According to such closure space construction for integrand functions, we demonstrate that any function defined on a compact set of a Euclidean space can be expressed by taking the limit of the projective tensor product of linear functions. We also generalize previous works about random DOIs with respect to finite dimensional operators, tensors, to MOIs with respect to random operators, which are defined from spectral decomposition perspectives. Based on random MOIs definitions and their properties, we derive several tail bounds for norms of higher random operator derivatives, higher random operator difference and Taylor remainder of random operator-valued functions.



Index terms—
Schur multiplier, Double Operator Integrals (DOIs), Multiple Operator Integrals (MOIs), random operators,
tail bounds.

We begin with the following theorem from

\mathfrak{P}_{k}^{m}

\displaystyle p(\bm{x})=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\sum\limits_{d=1}^{n_{i}}a_{i,d}\bm{x}^{\bm{j}_{i,d}},

where coefficients

is due to part (b) of Lemma
1
, and
\leq_{2}
comes from Markov inequality.

\hfill\Box

In the remainder of this section, we will establish continuity of random MOI. We need the following definition about the convergence in mean for random operators.

Definition 3

We say that a sequence of random operator
X^{(m)}
converges in the
r
-th mean towards the random operator
X
with respect to the operator norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
, if we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X^{(m)}\right\|^{r}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{exists,}

(52)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X\right\|^{r}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{exists,}

(53)

and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|X^{(m)}-X\right\|^{r}\right)=0.

(54)

We adopt the notatation
X^{(m)}\xrightarrow{r}X
to represent that random operators
X^{(m)}
converges in the
r
-th mean to the random operator
X
with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
.

Following theorem is about the continuity in the
r
-th mean of random MOI .

Theorem 6

For
i=1,2,\cdots,n+1
, we have random self-adjoint operators
A_{i}^{(m)},A_{i}
such that

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(m)}\xrightarrow{r}A_{i}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{as $m\rightarrow\infty$},

(55)

where
1\leq r<\infty
. Moreover, the norm for the real valued function
\overline{\psi}^{[n+1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}
and the norm for
X_{j}
for
j=1,2,\cdots,n
, are assumed bounded. Then, we have

\displaystyle T^{\underline{A^{(m)}}_{1}^{n+1}}_{\overline{\psi}^{[n]}_{\acute{\otimes}}}(\underline{X}_{1}^{n})\xrightarrow{r}T^{\underline{A}_{1}^{n+1}}_{\overline{\psi}^{[n]}_{\acute{\otimes}}}(\underline{X}_{1}^{n}).

(56)

Proof:

From the proof in Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T^{\underline{A^{(m)}}_{1}^{n+1}}_{\overline{\psi}^{[n]}_{\acute{\otimes}}}(\underline{X}_{1}^{n})-T^{\underline{A}_{1}^{n+1}}_{\overline{\psi}^{[n]}_{\acute{\otimes}}}(\underline{X}_{1}^{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{\psi}^{[n+1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}\right\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(\left\|A^{(m)}_{i}-A_{i}\right\|\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left\|X_{j}\right\|\right).

(57)

By raising the power
r
and taking the expectation at the both sides of the inequality provided by Eq. (
57
), we have proved this theorem by conditions given by Eq. (
55
) and the following inequality:

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}\right)^{r}\leq(n+1)^{r-1}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}^{r}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{given $a_{i}\geq 0$}.

(58)

5.1
Tail Bound for Higher Random Operator Derivative

In this section, we will derive tail bounds for higher random operato derivatives. We begin with a lemma to express the first derivative of an operator-valued function by double operator integrals.

Lemma 4

Let
X(t)
be self-adjoint operators indexed by
t
with bounded norm, i.e.,
\left\|X(t)\right\|<\infty
, and
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}
be differentiable on
\mathbb{R}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(X(t))}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{X(t),X(t)}_{\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}^{[1]}}\left(\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right).

(59)

Proof:

Suppose the function
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(x)
has the polynomial form as
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(x)=x^{m}
, by the chain rule of dervative, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(X(t))}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}X^{i}(t)\frac{dX(t)}{dt}X^{m-i-1}(t).

(60)

Since the operator
X(t)
can be represented by its spectrum as

\displaystyle X(t)=\int\limits_{\Lambda_{t}}\lambda dA_{t}(\lambda),

(61)

where
(\Lambda_{t},A_{t})
is the spectral measure at the instant
t
;by the spectral theorem, we can express Eq. (
60
) as

\displaystyle\frac{d\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(X(t))}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}\int\limits_{\Lambda_{t}}\lambda_{1}^{i}dA_{t}(\lambda_{1})\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\int\limits_{\Lambda_{t}}\lambda_{2}^{m-i-1}dA_{t}(\lambda_{2})

(62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Lambda_{t}}\int\limits_{\Lambda_{t}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{1}^{i}\lambda_{2}^{m-i-1}\right)dA_{t}(\lambda_{1})\frac{dX(t)}{dt}dA_{t}(\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Lambda_{t}}\int\limits_{\Lambda_{t}}\underbracket{\frac{\lambda_{1}^{m}-\lambda_{2}^{m}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}}_{(x^{m})^{[1]}}dA_{t}(\lambda_{1})\frac{dX(t)}{dt}dA_{t}(\lambda_{2}).

From MOIs defined by Eq. (
2
) as
m=2
(DOIs), we have Eq. (
59
) for
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(x)=x^{m}
. By linearity, this lemma is also true when
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(x)
is a polynomial function.

Suppose we can approximate
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}
by a sequence of polynomials
p_{n}(x)
, i.e.,
\left\|\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}-p_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T^{X(t),X(t)}_{\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}^{[1]}}\left(\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right)-T^{X(t),X(t)}_{p_{n}^{[1]}}\left(\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right)\right\|\leq Const\left\|\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}-p_{n}\right\|\left\|X(t)\right\|.

(63)

This lemma is proved due to we can approximate
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}
by polynomials.

\hfill\Box

From Lemma
4
, we are ready to have the following theorem about the tail bound for
\left\|\frac{d\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(X(t))}{dt}\right\|
.

Theorem 7

Let
X(t)
be self-adjoint operators indexed by
t
with bounded norm, i.e.,
\left\|X(t)\right\|<\infty
, and
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}
be differentiable on
\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\frac{d\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(X(t))}{dt}\right\|>\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Upsilon_{X}}{\theta}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\overline{\psi}^{[1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}\right\|\right].

(64)

Proof:

Because we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\frac{d\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(X(t))}{dt}\right\|>\theta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|T^{X(t),X(t)}_{\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}^{[1]}}\left(\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right)\right\|>\theta\right)

(65)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\overline{\psi}^{[1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}\right\|\left\|\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right\|>\theta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\overline{\psi}^{[1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}\right\|>\frac{\theta}{\left\|\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right\|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\overline{\psi}^{[1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}\right\|>\frac{\theta}{\Upsilon_{X}}\right),

where
\leq_{1}
comes from Lemma
1
. This lemma follows by Markov inequality.

(67)

\displaystyle\left.T^{\overbrace{A,\cdots,A}^{k}}_{\overline{\psi}^{[k-1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}}\left(\underbrace{B,\cdots,B}_{k-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{(k-1)!}{t}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(T^{\overbrace{A+tB,\cdots,A+tB}^{k-i},\overbrace{A,\cdots,A}^{i}}_{\overline{\psi}^{[k-1]}_{\acute{\otimes}}}\left(\underbrace{B,\cdots,B}_{k-1}\right)-\right.

\displaystyle=_{1}

\displaystyle k!T^{\overbrace{A+tB,\cdots,A+tB}^{k-i},\overbrace{A,\cdots,A}^{i+1}}_{\overline{\psi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes}}}\left(\underbrace{B,\cdots,B}_{k}\right),

This lemma is proved from Eq. (

\hfill\Box

From Lemma

5.2
Tail Bound for Higher Random Operator Difference

In this section, we will extend the operator first order difference, i.e.,
\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A)-\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A+B)
, to higher order difference. The higher opertor difference is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta^{k}_{B}\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-i}{k\choose i}\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A+iB),

(71)

where
A,B
are self-adjoint operators on Hilbert space. We will have
the following theorem about the tail bound for the higher opertor difference given by Eq. (
71
).

Theorem 9

Let
A
be a self-adjoint random operator,
B
be a deterministic self-adjoint operator and the eigenvalue for the operator
A+iB
is represented by
\lambda_{i+1}
for
i=0,1,\cdots,k
. We assume that
\max(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})<\kappa
for
j=1,2,\cdots,k

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\Delta^{k}_{B}\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A)\right\|>\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{k\kappa\left\|B\right\|^{k}}{\theta}\mathbb{E}(\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}).

(72)

Proof:

From the higher opertor difference provided by Eq. (
71
) and the divide difference definition, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{k}_{B}\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\Delta^{k-1}_{B}\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A)+(-1)^{k-1}\Delta^{k-1}_{B}\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A+B)

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\underbrace{\int\cdots\int}_{k+1}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})\overline{\psi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes}}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k+1})dP_{A}(\lambda_{1})BdP_{A+B}(\lambda_{2})B\right.

\displaystyle\left.\cdots BdP_{A+kB}(\lambda_{k+1})\right),

where
P_{A}(\lambda_{1}),\cdots,dP_{A+kB}(\lambda_{k+1})
are spectral measures.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\Delta^{k}_{B}\overline{\psi}_{\acute{\otimes}}(A)\right\|>\theta\right)

(74)

\displaystyle=_{1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\underbrace{\int\cdots\int}_{k+1}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})\overline{\psi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes}}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k+1})dP_{A}(\lambda_{1})B\cdots BdP_{A+kB}(\lambda_{k+1})\right)\right\|>\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{2}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\max(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})\left\|\overline{\psi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes}}\right\|\left\|B\right\|^{k}>\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{3}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(k\kappa\left\|\overline{\psi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes}}\right\|\left\|B\right\|^{k}>\theta\right),

where
=_{1}
comes from Eq. (
73
),
=_{2}
comes from Lemma
1
, and
\leq_{3}
comes from assumptions about
\max(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})
. The proof of this theorem is complete from Markov inequality.

5.3
Tail Bound for Taylor Remainder of Random Operator-valued Functions

In this section, we will derive tail bounds for Taylor remainder of random operator-valued functions.
We will consider two types of random operators: self-adjoint and unitary. Let us begin with
multi-index
notation definitions.

Multi-indices are represented by vector symbols as
\bm{a}
or
\bm{b}

\displaystyle\bm{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}),\penalty\ \bm{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n}),

(75)

where

. If
\bm{a}
is a multi-index, we have the following notation defnitions:

\displaystyle\left|\bm{a}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n},

\displaystyle\bm{a}!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}!a_{2}!\cdots a_{n}!,

\displaystyle\bm{X}^{\bm{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{1}^{a_{1}}X_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{a_{n}}\penalty\ \mbox{where $\bm{x}=(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n})$}.

(76)

5.3.1
Random Self-Adjoint Operators

All operators discussed in this section are assumed self-adjoint. Given a multi-variate operator-valued function
f(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n})
and perturbed operators
(H_{1},H_{2},\cdots,H_{n})
, we define the partial derivative
for
\partial^{\bm{a}}f(\bm{X}+\bm{t}\circ\bm{H})

\displaystyle\partial^{\bm{a}}f(\bm{X}+\bm{t}\circ\bm{H})=\frac{\partial^{\left|\bm{a}\right|}f(\bm{X}+\bm{t}\circ\bm{H})}{\partial t_{1}^{a_{1}}\partial t_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\partial t_{n}^{a_{n}}}\,

(77)

\bm{t}
is the vector of real variables
(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})

\circ
is the Hadamard product. Then, we define the Taylor remainder for

\displaystyle R_{k,f,\bm{X}}(\bm{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\bm{X}+\bm{H})-\sum\limits_{\left|\bm{a}\right|<k}\frac{\partial^{\bm{a}}f(\bm{X}+\bm{t}\circ\bm{H})}{\bm{a}!}\bigg|_{\bm{t}=\bm{0}},

(78)

where
\bm{t}
is the vector of real variables
(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})

(79)

(80)

(81)

Proof:

From Lemma
5

\displaystyle R_{k,\overline{\phi}_{\acute{\otimes},j},X_{j}}(H_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{X_{j}+H_{j},X_{j}}_{\overline{\phi}^{[1]}_{\acute{\otimes},j}}(H_{j})-\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{k-1}T^{X_{j},\cdots,X_{j}}_{\overline{\phi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes},j}}(\underbrace{H_{j},\cdots,H_{j}}_{k}).

(82)

2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{X_{j}+H_{j},X_{j},X_{j}}_{\overline{\phi}^{[2]}_{\acute{\otimes},j}}(H_{j})-\sum\limits_{\ell=2}^{k-1}T^{X_{j},\cdots,X_{j}}_{\overline{\phi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes},j}}(\underbrace{H_{j},\cdots,H_{j}}_{k}).

(83)

This lemma is proved by repeating this procedure
k-1

\hfill\Box

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T^{X_{j}+H_{j},\cdots,X_{j}}_{\overline{\phi}^{[k]}_{\acute{\otimes},j}}(\underbrace{H_{j},\cdots,H_{j}}_{k})\right\|>\theta\right)

where

Following lemma is given to express

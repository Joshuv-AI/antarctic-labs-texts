Maximum Likelihood Estimation of a Proportion from a Sample of Triplets
Rafael Weißbach, Eric Scholz

Abstract
When estimating a proportion and only a sample of triplets is given, dependencies within the triplets are to be accounted for. Without assuming a distribution for the success count of the triplet, together with the proportion, as second and third parameter the correlations of 1 st and 2 nd order enter the model. We apply maximum likelihood estimation, and derive consistency by using that the triplet count is multinomially distributed, combined with the continuous mapping theorem. The asymptotic normality follows with the delta-method, resulting in closed-form expressions for the standard errors. As application we study caries prevalence of pre-school children from a sample to nursing schools. We compare the standard errors with those for assuming erroneously independence within the nursing schools. As to be suspected, the design ‘inflates’ the standard error markedly.

Introduction

Occasionally, with interest on a proportion in a population, one may not draw units unrestrictedly as simple random sample (srs-design), but rather one has to draw clusters of units, of equal and unequal size (
Cochran 1977
,
Särndal et al. 1992
,
Donner and Klar 2000
,
Bland 2001
,
Wooldridge 2003
, or
Landesrechnungshof Mecklenburg-Vorpommern 2020
).

The intra-cluster correlation (ICC) can be of interest for its own, e.g. in finance when the cluster is a credit portfolio
(
Rosenow and Weißbach 2010
, see e.g.)
. More often the ICC is a nuisance parameter when estimating the proportion (or regression models build thereon
(see e.g.
Särndal et al. 1992
, Chapt. 8)
).
The design effect is long-known that of ’inflating’ the variance (VIF), and hence the standard error and the confidence interval, of the proportion
(
Lynn and Gabler 2005
, see e.g.)
. When cluster are larger than pairs
(
Weißbach et al. 2013
, for siblings see e.g.)
, correlation between more than two units are to be accounted for
(
Stefanescu and Turnbull 2003
, see e.g.)
. Here we study the case of three, i.e. for triplets, for a dichotomous outcome and apply maximum likelihood theory. In detail we use the Bahadur representation to parametrize the count of positive outcomes per triplet as a multinomial random variable, i.e. as a member of the Exponential family. We derive consistency thereof in a simplified proof and by the delta-method we derive asymptotic normality and the asymptotic variance of the estimator for the population proportion, and the correlations of 1
st
and 2
nd
order.

The sequence of likelihoods, seen as functions in the parameter
\boldsymbol{\rho}
, converges point-wise to the Kulback-Leibler loss by the LLN. When convergence is also uniform, the maxima of the functions will converge against the minimum of the loss, being the true parameter. Hence, for the consistency proof, we devote effort to establish uniform bounds.

Results in Section
2
are the closed-form expressions for the three maximum likelhood estimators in Subsection
2.1
. In Subsection
2.2
their asymptotic normality is shown following closely
van der Vaart 1998
. In Section
3
, after a short Monte Carlo simulation, we apply the confidence interval to the question of caries prevalence in the state of Hesse in Germany from a sample of nursing schools and compare it with the srs-design, to find that the VIF increases the confidence interval width by 10%.

2
Result of maximum likelihood estimation

2.1
Notation, parametrizations, estimators

Let
(X_{i1},X_{i2},X_{i3})^{T},\,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, be independent triplets of exchangeable binary random variables, with
X_{ik}=1
(success) or
X_{ik}=0
(failure),
k\in\{1,2,3\}
. We assume that

(A1)

X_{ik}
are identically distributed with
P\{X_{ik}=1\}=\pi\in[\xi_{\pi},1-\xi_{\pi}]
, where
\xi_{\pi}>0
is arbitrary small, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
k\in\{1,2,3\}
.

Note that the random variables within a triplet are not assumed to be independent. We define the model in Bahadur representation consisting of three probabilities
\boldsymbol{q}:=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})^{T}\in[\xi,1-\xi]^{3}
with some small
\xi<1/3
and
q_{s}:=P[S_{i}=s]
where
S_{i}:=\sum_{k=1}^{3}X_{ik},\,s\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.

Note that the numbers of triplets with
s
success are multinomial distributed, i.e. with probability mass distribution (see e.g.
Johnson et al. 1997
or
Bleymüller et al. 2020
):

f_{M}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=0\}},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=1\}},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=2\}},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=3\}}/n,q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\right):=\\
n!\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=0\}}\right)!\ldots\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=3\}}\right)!\right]^{-1}q_{0}^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=0\}}}\ldots q_{3}^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=3\}}}

with
\sum_{s=0}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{s_{i}=s\}}=n
and
\sum_{s=0}^{3}q_{s}=1
. Hence, the maximum likelihood estimator
\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{n}
has coordinates
\hat{q}_{n,h}:=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\{S_{i}=h\}}
, for
h\in\{1,2,3\}
.

By the invariance property of maximum likelihood estimation, it carries over to the second parametrization, called
\boldsymbol{\rho}
-parametrization, consisting of
\pi
, the well-known pairwise correlation
\rho_{1}
and the correlation of second order
\rho_{2}:=\mathrm{E}[(X_{1}-\mathrm{E}(X_{1}))(X_{2}-\mathrm{E}(X_{2}))(X_{3}-\mathrm{E}(X_{3}))]/[\pi(1-\pi)]^{3/2}
,
where we suppress the subscript
i
. By interchangeability we can simplify to

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\frac{\mathrm{E}(X_{1}X_{2})-\pi^{2}}{\pi(1-\pi)}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{E}(X_{1}X_{2}X_{3})-3\pi\mathrm{E}(X_{1}X_{2})+2\pi^{3}}{[\pi(1-\pi)]^{3/2}},

where
\mathrm{E}(X_{1}X_{2})=P\{X_{1}=1,X_{2}=1\}
. Note that, in terms of the Bahadur representation
(
Stefanescu and Turnbull 2003
, see e.g.)
, we have

\pi=3^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{3}sq_{s}
,

\mathrm{E}(X_{1}X_{2})=6^{-1}\sum_{s=2}^{3}s(s-1)q_{s}
and

\mathrm{E}(X_{1}X_{2}X_{3})=q_{3}
,
so that we can interpret
\boldsymbol{\rho}
as a function
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{q})
with

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}:=\begin{pmatrix}\pi\\
\rho_{1}\\
\rho_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{q})\\
\phi_{2}(\boldsymbol{q})\\
\phi_{3}(\boldsymbol{q})\end{pmatrix}=\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{q}):=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{3}q_{1}+\frac{2}{3}q_{2}+q_{3}\\
\\
\frac{q_{2}/3+q_{3}-(q_{1}/3+2q_{2}/3+q_{3})^{2}}{(q_{1}/3+2q_{2}/3+q_{3})[1-(q_{1}/3+2q_{2}/4+q_{3})]}\\
\\
\frac{q_{3}-(q_{1}+2q_{2}+3q_{3})(q_{2}/3+q_{3})+2(q_{1}/3+2q_{2}/3+q_{3})^{3}}{\{[(q_{1}/3+2q_{2}/3+q_{3})(1-(q_{1}/3+2q_{2}/3+q_{3})]\}^{3/2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(1)

Note that, in contrast to the enumeration of the coordinates in
\boldsymbol{q}
, the second coordinate of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is
\rho_{1}
.

The idea is now to use consistency and asymptotic normality for the Bahadur representation and afterwards defer from them asymptotic results for
\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{n}:=\boldsymbol{\phi}(\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{n})
. From now on we write short
X_{i}^{s}:=\mathbb{I}_{\{S_{i}=s\}}
for
s\in\{1,2,3\}
and
\boldsymbol{X}_{i}:=(X_{i}^{1},X_{i}^{2},X_{i}^{3})^{T}
. (Note that
\boldsymbol{X}_{i}\neq(X_{i1},X_{i2},X_{i3})^{T}
.) Then, divided by
n
, the logarithmic likelihood for
\boldsymbol{q}
is

M_{n}(\boldsymbol{q}):=\frac{1}{n}\log f_{M}\left(n-\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{1}+X_{i}^{2}+X_{i}^{3}\Bigr),\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{X}_{i}/n,q_{0},\boldsymbol{q}\right).

Thus, the estimator
\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{n}
will, (nearly) satisfy the system of equations
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{n}(\boldsymbol{q}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{i})=\mathbf{0}
,
with
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{n}:=\partial M_{n}(\boldsymbol{q})/\partial\boldsymbol{q}

(notation as in
van der Vaart 1998
, Chap. 5)
. The coordinates of
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}
are, for
h\in\{1,2,3\}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\boldsymbol{q},h}(\boldsymbol{X}_{i}):=\frac{1}{q_{h}(1-\sum_{j=1}^{3}q_{j})}\left[\left(1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq h\end{subarray}}^{3}q_{j}\right)X_{i}^{h}+q_{h}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq h\end{subarray}}^{3}X_{i}^{j}-1\right)\right].

(2)

2.2
Large sample properties and standard error

A useful prerequisite for asymptotic normality is consistency. The multinomial distribution is a member of the Exponential family, so that
\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{n}
is consistent
(see e.g.
Bickel and Doksum 2006
, Example 1.6.7+Theorem 5.2.2(i))
. Here we display a short and elementary proof and limit ourselves to the case of non-void categories,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{s}>0
, for all
s\in\{0,\ldots,3\}
.
Note that the true value
\boldsymbol{q}_{0}=(q_{0,1},q_{0,2},q_{0,3})^{T}
is a zero of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{q}):=\mathrm{E}_{\boldsymbol{q}_{0}}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{i})
with coordinates

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{\boldsymbol{q}_{0}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{q},h}(\boldsymbol{X}_{i})=\frac{q_{0,h}}{q_{h}}-\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{3}q_{0,j}}{1-\sum_{j=1}^{3}q_{j}},\quad h\in\{1,2,3\}.

(3)

Theorem 1

Assuming independence between triplets and
(A1)
,
\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\boldsymbol{q}_{0}
in probability.

Proof.
We apply Lemma 5.10 of
van der Vaart 1998
in its multivariate form.
First of all, we have to show that
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{n}(\boldsymbol{q})\to\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{q})
in probability for every
\boldsymbol{q}
. Therefore, since the random vectors
\boldsymbol{X}_{i}
are independent and identically distributed,
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{x})
is continuous for almost all
\boldsymbol{x}
, and the parameter space
\boldsymbol{Q}\subset[\xi,1-\xi]^{3}
is compact, we can apply the uniform law of large numbers
(
Newey and McFadden 1994
, see)
. We only need to show that
\lVert\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{x})\rVert\leq g(\boldsymbol{x})
for almost all
\boldsymbol{q}\in\boldsymbol{Q}
and a mapping
g(\boldsymbol{x})
with
\mathrm{E}_{\boldsymbol{q}_{0}}g(\boldsymbol{X}_{i})<\infty
. This is fulfilled - with proof in
A
- if we choose

g(\boldsymbol{x}):=\sqrt{3}(1-\xi)(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})\xi^{-2}.

(4)

Next, we have to verify that each map
\boldsymbol{q}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{q})
is continuous and has exactly one zero
\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{n}
. Because of non-void categories, this condition is fulfilled.

The proof is complete if
\boldsymbol{q}_{0}
is a point with
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{q}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon})>0>\Psi(\boldsymbol{q}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon})
for every
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}=(\epsilon,\epsilon,\epsilon)^{T}>\boldsymbol{0}
. By using (
3
), this can be verified directly. For example, it applies:

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{h}(\boldsymbol{q}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon})=\mathrm{E}_{\boldsymbol{q}_{0}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{q}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon},h}(\boldsymbol{X}_{i})=\frac{q_{0,h}}{q_{0,h}-3\epsilon}-\frac{1-q_{0,1}-q_{0,2}-q_{0,3}}{1-q_{0,1}-q_{0,2}-q_{0,3}+3\epsilon}>0

∎

We may now use the continuous mapping theorem and the Bahadur representation (
1
).

Under

4
Discussion

We study the saturated model for a simple sample of triplets. With one parameter less, the triplet count can assumed to be Negative Binomial to account for intra-triplet dependence by one over-dispersion parameter (see
Agresti 2002
or
Weißbach and Radloff 2020
).

Smaller models for triplets are one aspect, another practical aspect are equally sized clusters with more than three cluster units. In view of
Corollary
2
formulae for standard errors will then be unmanageable. Even for a sample of triplets, matrices in Corollary
2
, and hence standard errors, can be considered too tedious to compute. It appears important study further the conditions for a parametric bootstrap
(see
van der Vaart 1998
, Lemma 23.3)
, which appear to be fulfilled. This is especially tempting because of the simple expression
\hat{\pi}_{n}=\phi_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{n})
.
Further, for unequal clusters sizes,
Stefanescu and Turnbull 2003
proposes to use the maximal cluster size and think of smaller clusters to miss according outcomes and replace with the EM-algorithm
(see e.g.
Kremer and Weißbach 2013
;
Strohner and Weißbach 2016
, for similar applications)
. However, such maximal size
n
is then random, which is not covered by the ML theory as presented in
van der Vaart 1998
.

We here construct the bound for elements of the Hessian to the log-likelihood, called

. Since all second order derivatives of the form

\displaystyle|\psi_{\boldsymbol{q},1}^{1,1}(\boldsymbol{x})|

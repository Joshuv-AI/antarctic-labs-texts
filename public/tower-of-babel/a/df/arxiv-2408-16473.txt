Dispersive estimates of fourth order Schrödinger operators with scaling-critical magnetic potentials in dimension two
Haoran Wang

Abstract
. Dispersive estimate for the fourth order Schrödinger operator with a class of scaling-critical magnetic potentials in dimension two was obtained by the construction of the corresponding resolvent kernel and the stationary phase method.



Key Words: Dispersive estimate, Higher order Schrödinger operator, Scaling-critical magnetic potential, Resolvent kernel

AMS Classification:

42B37, 47A10, 35J10.

1.
Introduction

Consider the fourth order Schrödinger operator with a scaling-critical magnetic potential on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}=\left(-i\nabla+\frac{\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})}{|x|}\right)^{4},\quad\hat{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}\in\mathbb{S}^{1},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{A}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1};\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a vector function fulfilling the transversality condition

\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})\cdot\hat{x}\equiv 0,\quad\forall\hat{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

(1.2)

A typical example of
\mathbf{A}
is given by the Aharonov-Bohm potential

\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})=\alpha|x|^{-1}(-x_{2},x_{1}),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}.

If we write
\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})=(A_{1},A_{2})(\hat{x})
, then the usual second order operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}=(-i\nabla+\mathbf{A}/|x|)^{2}
represents the quantum Hamiltonian of a non-relativistic electron interacting with the singular magnetic field
B(x)

B(x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{A_{2}(\hat{x})}{|x|}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{A_{1}(\hat{x})}{|x|},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Since the potential
\frac{\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})}{|x|}
is singular at zero, we should extend the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}
to a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
so that the Schrödinger semigroup
e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}}
generated by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}
(in the spectral sense) is well-defined. To be specific, we regard
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}
as the Friedrichs extension of the symmetric operator
(-i\nabla+\mathbf{A}/|x|)^{2}
(initially defined on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
) throughout the whole paper. Hence, the spectrum of the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}
consists only of the absolutely continuous part
[0,+\infty)
.
If
\mathbf{A}\equiv\mathbf{0}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}
reduces to the free fourth order Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{0},4}=\Delta^{2}
.
By the Fourier transform, one can easily derive the sharp pointwise time-decay estimate for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see e.g.
[
2
]
)

|e^{-it\Delta^{2}}(x,y)|\lesssim(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{4}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{d}{3}}|t|^{-\frac{d}{4}},t\neq 0,

(1.3)

which, together with Young’s inequality, immediately yields the dispersive estimate for
e^{-it\Delta^{2}}

\|e^{-it\Delta^{2}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{4}},\quad t\neq 0.

(1.4)

The dispersive estimates for the second order Schrödinger operator (with the general form
(-i\nabla+\vec{A}(x))^{2}+V(x)
) have been extensively studied in all dimensions (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
20
,
21
,
25
,
22
,
29
,
37
]
and the references therein). For the higher order cases, however, the picture becomes rather unclear and there are a lot of situations to be considered. In fact, the investigations concerning the higher order Schrödinger operators are absolutely hot topics in harmonic analysis, at least in the last few years. In particular, several authors obtained various dispersive estimates for the higher order operators
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
with a decaying potential
V
in different dimensions recently. For example, the dispersive estimate (
1.4
) for
\Delta^{2}+V
was obtained via the Wiener inversion technique by Hill
[
26
]
in dimension one. Erdoğan-Goldberg-Green
[
13
]
proved the dispersive bounds for
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
with
m>1
in higher dimensions
d\in(2m,4m)
via the same method. For the fourth order operator
\Delta^{2}+V
, Feng-Soffer-Yao
[
19
]
established the Jensen-Kato bound
(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d}{4}}
in dimensions
d\geq 5
and the local dispersive bound
O(|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}})
in dimension three by the asymptotic expansion of the perturbed resolvent
R_{V}(z)=(\Delta^{2}+V-z)^{-1}
at the zero threshold provided that zero is a regular point. Based on the high energy estimates for the perturbed resolvent
R_{V}(z)
, the authors of
[
39
,
32
,
11
,
24
]
obtained the global dispersive estimates for
\Delta^{2}+V
in dimensions one, two, three and four, respectively, permitting the presence of a zero resonance or eigenvalue. Feng-Soffer-Wu-Yao
[
18
]
proved local dispersive estimates for
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
in dimensions
d>2m
with a decaying potential
|V(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>d
. Global dispersive estimate was obtained as a direct consequence of the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the associated wave operators by Erdoğan-Green
[
10
]
for
\beta>d+3
in odd dimension

and
[
14
]
for
\beta>d+4
in even dimension
d
. It turns out that certain measure of smoothness is indispensable to guarantee the validity of the global dispersive bound for
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
in high dimensions
d\geq 4m
(see
[
10
,
13
]
). Very recently, in a preprint
[
15
]
, Erdoğan-Goldberg-Green established the dispersive estimate (
1.4
) for
(-\Delta)^{m}+V(1<m\in\mathbb{N})
with scaling-critical potentials
V
in high dimensions
d\in(2m,4m)
again via the Wiener inversion theorem. For the second order operator
-\Delta+V
in dimension two, Erdoğan-Green obtained a weighted dispersive bound in
[
8
]
and dispersive estimates with obstructions at zero energy in
[
9
]
; Erdoğan-Goldberg-Green established the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the associated wave operators with threshold obstructions in
[
12
]
(for the regular case, see Schlag’s earlier paper
[
36
]
). Much more recently, Li-Soffer-Yao
[
32
]
proved the dispersive estimate (
1.4
) for
\Delta^{2}+V
with a decaying regular potential
V
in dimension two. For more considerations of fourth order operator
\Delta^{2}+V
in dimensions one and three,
we refer the interested readers to e.g.
[
26
,
33
,
34

35
,
39
]
.

In this paper, we show that the Schrödinger propagator
e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}}
satisfies the same dispersive estimate as (
1.4
) for a class of scaling-critical magnetic potentials in dimension two. On the one hand, the two-dimensional situation is quite special in the sense that
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the lowest dimensional Euclidean space for which the addition of a magnetic potential is nontrivial since the free operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{0},4}
is very sensitive to small perturbations. For example, the spectrum of the purely magnetic operator
(-i\nabla+\vec{A}(x))^{2}
can be quite general, ranging from the purely absolutely continuous spectrum
[0,\infty)
for the compactly supported magnetic field
B(x)
(e.g. the Aharonov-Bohm solenoid), through the discrete set of infinitely degenerated eigenvalues (Landau levels) for the constant field
B(x)\equiv B\neq 0
, to purely discrete spectrum for
|B(x)|\rightarrow\infty,|x|\rightarrow\infty
(magnetic bottles); we refer the interested reader to
[
17
]
on how to eliminate these eigenvalues. For the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
, Kato
[
30
]
established the absence of positive eigenvalues for a bounded decaying potential
V=o(|x|^{-1})
as
|x|\rightarrow+\infty
. For more about the absence of positive eigenvalues of Schrödinger operators, we refer to
[
16
,
27
,
31
,
38
]
. On the other hand, due to the degeneracy of the higher order free operator
\Delta^{m}(m\geq 1)
at zero threshold and in lower even dimension, it turns out that the asymptotic expansions of the perturbed resolvent
R_{V}(z)
at zero are more complicated than the usual Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
in dimension two (see
[
28
,
32
]
). For instance, there exists a compactly supported smooth potential
V
such that
\Delta^{2}+V
admits a positive eigenvalue and if the potential
V
is bounded and repulsive (i.e. satisfying
x\cdot\nabla\leq 0
), then
\Delta^{2}+V
does not admit positive eigenvalues (see e.g.
[
18
]
). Moreover, for a general self-adjoint operator
H
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, Costin-Soffer
[
6
]
proved that
H+\epsilon V
can kick off the eigenvalue located in a small interval for a generic small potential
\epsilon V
even if
H
only admits a simple embedded eigenvalue. Fortunately, the Schrödinger operator with a scaling-critical magnetic potential has neither negative nor embedded eigenvalues and the resolvent of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}
is regular at zero, although its resolvent kernel is a little more complicated than the free case (see e.g.
[
23
,
25
]
).
Throughout the whole paper, it will be convenient to write
X\lesssim Y
if there exists some constant
C>0
such that
X\leq CY
. Now we state the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}
be the self-adjoint Schrödinger operator with a scaling-critical magnetic potential given by (
1.1
), then we have the dispersive estimate

\|e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad t\neq 0.

(1.5)

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
is a natural generalization of the dispersive estimate (
1.4
) for the free operator
\Delta^{2}
. We do not include an additional rough potential
V
(i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}+V
with
|V(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-\beta}
) here since once we have proved the dispersive estimate (
1.5
) for the magnetic operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}
, then, viewing
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}+V
as a perturbation of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}
(similar to the treatment of
\Delta^{2}+V
), one may closely follow the steps of Li-Soffer-Yao
[
32
]
(with some obvious modifications) to obtain the dispersive estimate for
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}+V
. The paper
[
32
]
is quite lengthy (but the idea is the classical perturbation method), so we only consider the purely magnetic case
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}
to avoid repeating.
Apart from the case of an additional perturbation
V
, there are also many interesting situations to be considered. For example, one may consider the dispersive or smoothing estimates for the general higher order Schrödinger operators (with electromagnetic potentials) in two and higher dimensions.

2.1.
The magnetic flux

If we write
\hat{x}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
for
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then we have
\partial_{\theta}=\hat{x}_{1}\partial_{\hat{x}_{2}}-\hat{x}_{2}\partial_{\hat{x}_{1}}
and
\partial_{\theta}^{2}=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
. Set
x=r\hat{x}
, then the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},1}
(i.e. (
1.1
) with
m=1
) can be rewritten as

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},1}=-\partial_{r}^{2}-r^{-1}\partial_{r}+r^{-2}L_{\mathbf{A}},

where

L_{\mathbf{A}}=(-i\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}+\mathbf{A}(\hat{x}))^{2}=-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}+|\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})|^{2}+i\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})+2i\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.

Define
\alpha(\theta):[0,2\pi]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\alpha(\theta)=\mathbf{A}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\cdot(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),

then, in view of the transversality assumption (
1.2
), we have

\mathbf{A}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=(-\alpha(\theta)\sin\theta,\alpha(\theta)\cos\theta),\quad\theta\in[0,2\pi]

and thus

\begin{split}L_{\mathbf{A}}&=-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}+|\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})|^{2}+i\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})+2i\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\\
&=-\partial_{\theta}^{2}+|\alpha(\theta)|^{2}+i\alpha^{\prime}(\theta)+2i\alpha(\theta)\partial_{\theta}\\
&=(-i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha(\theta))^{2}.\end{split}

In what follows, the total magnetic flux
\alpha
is defined by

\alpha:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\alpha(\theta)d\theta.

(2.1)

2.2.
Resolvent kernel for
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}

To obtain the kernel of the resolvent
(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}-z)^{-1}
, we need the resolvent kernel of the second order operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}
(see
[
23
,
25
]
). The resolvent
(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}-z)^{-1}
is defined for
\lambda>0
by

R_{4}^{\pm}(\lambda^{4}):=(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}-(\lambda^{4}\pm i0))^{-1}=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}-(\lambda^{4}\pm i\epsilon))^{-1}.

Proposition 2.1

(Resolvent kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}
)
.

Let
\theta_{x},\theta_{y}
denote the angles between the positive real axis and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
respectively and define the partial magnetic flux
\alpha_{12}
by

\alpha_{12}:=\alpha(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\alpha(\vartheta)d\vartheta.

The Hankel function
H_{0}^{\pm}(z)
is defined for
z\in\mathbb{C}
by

H_{0}^{\pm}(z)=J_{0}(z)\pm iY_{0}(z),

where
J_{0}(z)
and
Y_{0}(z)
are the Bessel functions of the first kind and the second kind, respectively. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
s>0
, we define the vector
\mathbf{n}=(|x|+|y|,\sqrt{2|x||y|(\cosh s-1)})
, then the kernel of the resolvent
(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0))^{-1}
is given by

\begin{split}R_{2}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2})(x,y):&=(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},2}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0))^{-1}(x,y)\\
&=\pm\frac{i}{4\pi}\left(H_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)A_{\alpha}(\theta_{x},\theta_{y})+\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|\mathbf{n}|)B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{x},\theta_{y})ds\right)\end{split}

with

A_{\alpha}(\theta_{x},\theta_{y})=\frac{e^{i\alpha_{12}}}{4\pi^{2}}\left(1_{[0,\pi]}(|\theta_{x}-\theta_{y}|)+e^{-2\pi i\alpha}1_{[\pi,2\pi]}(|\theta_{x}-\theta_{y}|)\right)

and

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{x},\theta_{y})=-\frac{e^{-i\alpha(\theta_{x}-\theta_{y})-i\alpha_{12}}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg(\sin(\pi|\alpha|)e^{-s|\alpha|}

\displaystyle+\sin(\alpha\pi)\frac{(e^{-s}+\cos(\theta_{x}-\theta_{y}))\sinh(s\alpha)+i\sin(\theta_{x}-\theta_{y})\cosh(s\alpha)}{\cosh s+\cos(\theta_{x}-\theta_{y})}\bigg).

Here
\alpha
is the constant defined in (
2.1
).

Similar to the resolvent identity for the free operator
\Delta^{2}

\left(\Delta^{2}-z\right)^{-1}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{z}}\left((-\Delta-\sqrt{z})^{-1}-(-\Delta+\sqrt{z})^{-1}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

one obtains the kernel of the resolvent
R_{4}^{\pm}(\lambda^{4})=(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}-(\lambda^{4}\pm i0))^{-1}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
R_{4}^{\pm}(\lambda^{4})(x,y)
be the kernel function of the resolvent
(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}-(\lambda^{4}\pm i0))^{-1}
, then, using the notations of Proposition
2.1
, we have

\begin{split}R_{4}^{\pm}(\lambda^{4})(x,y)=&\pm\frac{iA_{\alpha}(\theta_{x},\theta_{y})}{8\pi\lambda^{2}}\left(H_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)-H_{0}^{\pm}(i\lambda|x-y|)\right)\\
&+\frac{\pm i}{8\pi\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(H_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|\mathbf{n}(s)|)-H_{0}^{\pm}(i\lambda|\mathbf{n}(s)|)\right)B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{x},\theta_{y})ds.\end{split}

To analysis the resolvent kernel
R_{4}^{\pm}(\lambda^{4})(x,y)

H_{0}^{\pm}(z)
(c.f.
[
1

Lemma 2.3
.

The Hankel function
H_{0}^{\pm}
is defined by

H_{0}^{\pm}(z)=J_{0}(z)\pm iY_{0}(z),

where

\begin{split}J_{0}(z)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{z^{2k}}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}},\quad z\rightarrow 0,\\
Y_{0}(z)&=\frac{2}{\pi}(\ln(z/2)+\gamma)J_{0}(z)+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j}\right)\frac{z^{2k}}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}},z\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

Here
\gamma
is the Euler constant. For
|z|>1
, one has

H_{0}^{\pm}(z)=e^{\pm iz}\omega_{\pm}(z),\quad|\omega_{\pm}^{(k)}(z)|\lesssim(1+|z|)^{-\frac{1}{2}-k},\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(2.2)

We also need the Van der Corput lemma from
[
40

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\phi,\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(a,b)
with
|\phi^{(k)}(x)|\geq 1
for all
x\in(a,b)
. If
k\geq 2
or
k=1
and
\phi^{\prime}(x)

\left|\int_{a}^{b}e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}\psi(x)dx\right|\lesssim_{k}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{k}}\left(|\psi(b)|+\int_{a}^{b}|\psi^{\prime}(x)|dx\right).

1.1

By the well-known Stone formula, we have

e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}}f=\frac{2}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{3}e^{-it\lambda^{4}}\left(R_{4}^{+}(\lambda^{4})-R_{4}^{-}(\lambda^{4})\right)fd\lambda.

(3.1)

2.2
, we get the kernel of

\tilde{F}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)=e^{\mp i\lambda|x-y|}F^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)
, then we have

\begin{split}K_{1,j}^{\pm}(t,x,y)=&2^{2j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma e^{-it2^{4j}\sigma^{4}\pm i2^{j}\sigma|x-y|}\\
&\quad\times\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\tilde{F}^{\pm}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)d\sigma.\end{split}

(3.10)

To prove (
3.12
), we consider two cases according to
|x-y|\leq 2^{-j}
and
|x-y|\geq 2^{-j}
.

Case 1.
For
|x-y|\leq 2^{-j}
, we further consider two subcases according to
|t|\leq 2^{-4j}
and
|t|\geq 2^{-4j}
. If
|t|\leq 2^{-4j}
, then, since the support of
\varphi_{0}
is contained in

If
|t|\geq 2^{-4j}
, then, by integration by parts, we have

K_{1,j}^{\pm}(t,x,y)=\frac{2^{2j}}{4it2^{4j}}\int_{1/4}^{1}e^{-it2^{4j}\sigma^{4}}\partial_{s}\left(\sigma^{-2}e^{\pm i2^{j}\sigma|x-y|}\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\tilde{F}^{\pm}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)\right)d\sigma.

Since the support of
\varphi_{0}
is contained in
[1/4,1]
, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
2.2
) that

|\tilde{F}^{\pm}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)|+|\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{F}^{\pm}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)|\lesssim 1,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.4
(Van der Corput’s lemma), we obtain

|K_{1,j}^{\pm}(t,x,y)|\lesssim 2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-1},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(3.11)

Case 2.
For
|x-y|\geq 2^{-j}
, we consider
K_{1,j}^{-}(t,x,y)
and
K_{1,j}^{+}(t,x,y)
separately. For
K_{1,j}^{-}(t,x,y)
, if
|t|\leq 2^{-4j}
, then we have

|K_{1,j}^{-}(t,x,y)|\lesssim 2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-1},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

If
|t|\geq 2^{-4j}
, then we write

K_{1,j}^{-}(t,x,y)=2^{2j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma e^{-i(t2^{4j}\sigma^{4}+2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)}\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\tilde{F}^{-}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)d\sigma.

Let
\phi_{\pm}(\sigma)=t2^{4j}\sigma^{4}\pm 2^{j}\sigma|x-y|
, then we have (remember that supp
\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\subset[1/4,1]
)

|\partial_{\sigma}\phi_{+}(\sigma)|=|4t2^{4j}\sigma^{3}+2^{j}|x-y||\gtrsim 1+2^{4j}|t|,

which implies that the phase function
\phi_{+}(\sigma)
is regular in
\sigma\in[1/4,1]
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have

|K_{1,j}^{-}(t,x,y)|\lesssim 2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-1},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

For
K_{1,j}^{+}(t,x,y)
, if
|t|\leq 2^{-4j}
, then, similarly, we have

|K_{1,j}^{+}(t,x,y)|\lesssim 2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-1},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

If
|t|\geq 2^{-4j}
, then we compute
\partial_{\sigma}\phi_{-}(\sigma)=4t2^{4j}\sigma^{3}-2^{j}|x-y|
, which implies that the phase function
\phi_{-}(\sigma)
of the oscillatory integral

K_{1,j}^{+}(t,x,y)=2^{2j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma e^{-i(t2^{4j}\sigma^{4}-2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)}\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\tilde{F}^{+}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)d\sigma

admits a critical point
\sigma_{0}
in
[1/4,1]
(i.e.
\sigma_{0}=2^{-j-\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt[3]{\frac{|x-y|}{t}}
).
Let
j_{0}=[\frac{1}{3}\log_{2}\frac{|x-y|}{|t|}]
, then we have
-2+j<\frac{1}{3}\log_{2}\frac{\Phi(z)}{t}<3+j
when
|j-j_{0}|<2
, which implies that such a critical point
\sigma_{0}
does exist.

One can check that for
\sigma\in[1/4,1]

\partial_{\sigma}^{2}\phi_{-}(\sigma)=12t2^{4j}\sigma^{2}\gtrsim 2^{4j}|t|,

so it follows by Lemma
2.4
that

|K_{1,j}^{+}(t,x,y)|\lesssim 2^{2j}(2^{4j}|t|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\varphi_{0}(1)\tilde{F}^{+}(2^{j}|x-y|)+\int_{1/4}^{1}\left|\partial_{\sigma}\left(\sigma\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\tilde{F}^{+}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)\right)\right|ds\right).

Since
\varphi_{0}(1)=0
and

|\tilde{F}^{+}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)|+|\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{F}^{+}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)|\lesssim 1,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

we obtain

|K_{1,j}^{+}(t,x,y)|\lesssim 2^{2j}(2^{4j}|t|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

When
|j-j_{0}|\geq 2
, the phase function
\phi_{-}(\sigma)
is regular in
[1/4,1]
. By Lemma
2.4
again, we obtain

|K_{1,j}^{+}(t,x,y)|\lesssim 2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Collecting
Case 1
and
Case 2

3.9
) for
\ell=1
.

) for

, i.e.

Let

Let
\tilde{G}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)=e^{\mp i\lambda|x-y|}G^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)
, then we have

\begin{split}K_{2,j}^{\pm}(t,x,y)=&2^{2j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma e^{-it2^{4j}\sigma^{4}\pm i2^{j}\sigma|x-y|}\\
&\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\tilde{G}^{\pm}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)d\sigma.\end{split}

(3.12)

By the elementary analysis, there exists a finite constant

2.1
, we know (see also

To prove (
3.12
), we consider two cases according to
|x-y|\leq 2^{-j}
and
|x-y|\geq 2^{-j}
.

Case 1.
For
|x-y|\leq 2^{-j}
, we further consider two subcases according to

and

. If

K_{2,j}^{\pm}(t,x,y)=\frac{2^{2j}}{4it2^{4j}}\int_{1/4}^{1}e^{-it2^{4j}\sigma^{4}}\partial_{\sigma}\left(\sigma^{-2}e^{\pm i2^{j}\sigma|x-y|}\varphi_{0}(\sigma)\tilde{G}^{\pm}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)\right)d\sigma.

\displaystyle|\tilde{G}^{\pm}(2^{j}\sigma|x-y|)|=\bigg|e^{\mp i2^{j}\sigma|x-y|}\int_{0}^{\infty}

Remark 3.2
.

The expected dispersive estimate (
1.5
) follows immediately from Lemma
3.1
. Indeed, from (
3.1
), (
3.7
),(
3.8
) and Proposition
2.2
, we have

\begin{split}K(x,y):&=e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A},4}}(x,y)\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(K_{1,j}^{\pm}(x,y)+K_{2,j}^{\pm}(x,y)\right).\end{split}

Now from Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\begin{split}|K(x,y)|&\leq\left(\sum_{|j-j_{0}|\leq 2}+\sum_{|j-j_{0}|\geq 2}\right)(K_{1,j}^{\pm}(x,y)+K_{2,j}^{\pm}(x,y))\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j_{0}|\leq 2}2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{|j|\geq j^{\prime}_{0}+1}2^{2j}(1+2^{4j}|t|)^{-1}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j_{0}|\leq 2}|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{j\leq j^{\prime}_{0}}2^{2j}+|t|^{-1}\sum_{j\geq j^{\prime}_{0}+1}2^{-2j}\\
&\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where
j^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
is a fixed constant satisfying
2^{4j^{\prime}_{0}}|t|\sim 1
.

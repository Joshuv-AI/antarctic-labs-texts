Simple Braids Tend toward Positive Entropy
Luke Robitaille, Minh-Tâm Quang Trinh

Abstract
. A simple braid is a positive braid that can be drawn so that any two strands cross at most once. We prove that as n → ∞ n\to\infty , the proportion of simple braids on n n strands that have positive topological entropy tends toward 100 % 100\% . Notably, such braids are either pseudo-Anosov or reducible with a pseudo-Anosov component. Our proof involves a method of reduction from simple braids to non-simple 3 3 -strand braids that may be of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
\mathit{Br}_{n}
be the braid group on
n
strands.
A braid
\beta\in\mathit{Br}_{n}
is
simple
iff, in some planar diagram for
\beta
, the crossings are all positive and any two strands cross at most once.
The subset of simple braids
E_{n}\subseteq\mathit{Br}_{n}
forms a generating set: the most natural one for the Garside theory of
\mathit{Br}_{n}

[
EM
]
.

At the same time,
\mathit{Br}_{n}
can be identified with the mapping class group of a disk with
n
marked points rel its boundary.
By
[
AKM
]
, every self-map of a compact topological surface can be assigned a nonnegative real number called its
topological entropy
, or
entropy
for short, roughly measuring the growth rate of its mixing of open covers.
The entropy of a mapping class is defined to be the infimum of the entropies of the maps it represents.
The goal of this note is to prove:

Theorem 1
.

The proportion of simple braids on
n
strands that have positive topological entropy tends to 100% as
n
tends to infinity.

In fact, we give a more precise version:
17
.
The idea of the proof is to reduce from studying simple braids on
n
strands to studying non-simple braids on
3
strands, whose positive entropy can be detected via the quotient homomorphism
\mathit{Br}_{3}\to\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
.
The reduction step, as well as the combinatorics that ensures that sufficiently many of the resulting
3
-strand braids have positive entropy, may be of independent interest.

1.2.

One motivation for
1
is the study of a different, but closely related, property of braids.
Recall that under the Nielsen–Thurston classification, a mapping class is either
periodic
,
reducible
, or
pseudo-Anosov
.
These options amount to the possible dynamics for its action on simple closed curves
[
T
]
.
The entropy and Nielsen–Thurston type of a mapping class constrain each other:
Namely, its entropy is zero if and only if it is either periodic or reducible with solely periodic components
[
BB
]
.

Caruso and Wiest showed that if
n\geq 3
, then in the Cayley graph of
(\mathit{Br}_{n},E_{n})
, the proportion of pseudo-Anosov braids in the ball of radius
\ell
tends to 100% as
\ell
tends to infinity
[
C
,
CW
]
.
This confirmed a folklore expectation dating to the work of Thurston.
Mahler and Sisto showed similar results, phrased in terms of random walks in non-elementary subgroups
[
M
,
S
]
.

The following sharpening of
1
, which we leave to future work, would be directly complementary to the work of Caruso and Wiest.

Conjecture 2
.

The proportion of simple braids on
n
strands that are pseudo-Anosov tends to 100% as
n
tends to infinity.

2.
Topological Entropy

In this section, we collect the only properties of topological entropy that we actually need.

2.1.

Let
S
be an compact topological surface, possibly with boundary, and
I\subset S
a finite set of points in its interior.
Let
M=\mathit{Mod}(S,I,\partial S)
be the mapping class group of
(S,I)
rel the boundary
\partial S
.
Explicitly,
M=\pi_{0}(\mathit{Homeo}^{+}(S,I,\partial S))
, where
\mathit{Homeo}^{+}(S,I,\partial S)
is the group of self-homeomorphisms of
S
that stabilize
I
and fix
\partial S
, endowed with the compact-open topology
[
FM
, §2.1]
.

2.2.

We define the
entropy
of a map
f:S\to S
to be its topological entropy
h(f)
in the sense of
[
AKM
]
.
We define the
entropy
of a mapping class
\phi\in M
to be

(2.1)

\displaystyle h(\phi)=\inf_{f\in\phi}h(f),

where the notation
f\in\phi
means
f
is a representative of
\phi
.

Lemma 3
.

The function
h:M\to\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}
has the following properties:

(1)

h
is constant along conjugacy classes.

(2)

For any
\phi\in M
and integer
k>0
, we have
h(\phi^{k})\leq kh(\phi)
.

Proof.

Parts (1) and (2) respectively follow from Theorems 1 and 2 in
ibid.

∎

2.3.

Suppose that
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
.
By construction, a mapping class
\phi\in M
can be lifted to
M^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\mathit{Mod}(S,I^{\prime},\partial S)
if and only if some, or equivalently any, representative of
\phi
stabilizes
I^{\prime}
.
We deduce that:

Lemma 4
.

If
\phi\in M
lifts to
\phi^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, then
h(\phi)\geq h(\phi^{\prime})
.

2.4.

Let
D
be a closed disk, and
I\subset D
a finite set of points in its interior.
Let

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathit{Br}_{I}=\pi_{1}(\mathit{Conf}^{|I|}(D),I),

where
\mathit{Conf}^{n}(D)
denotes the configuration space of
n
unordered points in
D
.
As explained in
[
FM
, §9.1.3]
, there is an explicit isomorphism

(2.3)

\displaystyle\beta\mapsto\phi(\beta):\mathit{Br}_{I}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathit{Mod}(D,I,\partial D).

At the same time, we can identify
\mathit{Br}_{I}
with the usual braid group on
|I|
strands, up to fixing an ordering of
I
.

We define the
entropy
of a braid
\beta
to be that of the corresponding mapping class:

h(\beta)=h(\phi(\beta))
.
Now we can rewrite
4
in terms of braids.
For any
\beta\in\mathit{Br}_{I}
and
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
, we say that
I^{\prime}
is
stable
under
\beta
iff we can delete strands from
\beta
to obtain an element of
\mathit{Br}_{I^{\prime}}
.
In this case, we denote the new braid by
\beta|_{I^{\prime}}
.
Since
\phi(\beta)
lifts to
\phi(\beta|_{I^{\prime}})
,
4
says that

(2.4)

\displaystyle h(\beta)\geq h(\beta|_{I^{\prime}}).

2.5.

For any integer
N>0
and
g\in\Mat_{N}(\mathbf{C}[t^{\pm 1}])
, the characteristic polynomial of
g
is a polynomial of degree
N
with coefficients in
\mathbf{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
.
For any complex number
z\neq 0
, let
\Spec(g(z))
be the eigenvalue spectrum of
g(z)\vcentcolon=g|_{t\to z}
, viewed as an unordered multiset of
N
complex numbers.
The
spectral radius
of
g
, which we will denote
\mathrm{rad}(g)
, is the maximum value of
|\lambda|
as we run over
z
on the unit circle and
\lambda\in\Spec(g(z))
.
The following result linking spectral radius to entropy was shown by Fried
[
F
]
and Kolev
[
K
]
independently:

Theorem 5

(Fried, Kolev)
.

Let
\rho:\mathit{Br}_{n}\to\GL_{n}(\mathbf{Z}[t^{\pm 1}])
be the unreduced Burau representation of
\mathit{Br}_{n}
, as in
[
K
, §2]
.
Then
\log\mathrm{rad}(\rho(\beta))\leq h(\beta)
for all
\beta\in\mathit{Br}_{n}
.

Corollary 6
.

In
5
, the same conclusion would hold were
\rho
the reduced, rather than unreduced, Burau representation.

Proof.

As a
\mathbf{Z}[t^{\pm 1}][\mathit{Br}_{n}]
-module, the unreduced representation is the direct sum of the reduced representation and the trivial representation
[
Tu
, §1.3]
.
∎

Corollary 7
.

Let
\gamma:\mathit{Br}_{3}\to\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
be the reduced Burau representation at
n=3
and
t=-1
.
Then
|{\tr(\gamma(\beta))}|>2
implies
h(\beta)>0
for all
\beta\in\mathit{Br}_{3}
.

Proof.

If
|{\tr(\gamma(\beta))}|>2
, then
\gamma(\beta)
must have an eigenvalue greater than
1
. ∎

3.
Simple Braids

3.1.

Following Artin, the braid group on
n
strands has the presentation

\displaystyle\mathit{Br}_{n}=\left\langle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}\,\middle|\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}&i=1,\ldots,n-1,\\
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}&|i-j|>1\end{array}\right\rangle,

where
\sigma_{i}
represents the positive simple twist of the
i
th and
(i+1)
th strands.
The
writhe
of a braid on
n
strands is its image under the quotient map
\ell:\mathit{Br}_{n}\to\mathbf{Z}
that sends
\ell(\sigma_{i})=1
for all
i
.

3.2.

Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group on
n
letters.
Let
s_{i}
be the simple transposition that swaps
i
and
i+1
.
There is a quotient homomorphism
\mathit{Br}_{n}\to S_{n}
given by
\sigma_{i}\mapsto s_{i}
.

The set of simple braids
E_{n}\subseteq\mathit{Br}_{n}
is the image of a right inverse to this quotient map.
Indeed, every permutation
w\in S_{n}
can be written as

(3.3)

\displaystyle w=(s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{1})(s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{2})\cdots(s_{i_{n}}\cdots s_{n})

for some uniquely determined
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}
such that
j-1\leq i_{j}\leq n-1
.
Let

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{w}=(\sigma_{i_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{1})(\sigma_{i_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{2})\cdots(\sigma_{i_{n}}\cdots\sigma_{n}).

Then
w\mapsto\sigma_{w}
is a right inverse of the quotient map
\mathit{Br}_{n}\to S_{n}
, and furthermore,
E_{n}=\{\sigma_{w}\mid w\in S_{n}\}

[
EM
]
.

3.3.

For any
w\in S_{3}
and integer
N>0
, let

(3.5)

\displaystyle P(w,N)=\{\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{N})\in S_{3}^{N}\mid w_{1}\cdots w_{N}=w\}.

For any
\vec{w}\in S_{3}^{N}
, let
\sigma_{\vec{w}}=\sigma_{w_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{w_{N}}
.
We now show that many
3
-strand braids of the form
\sigma_{\vec{w}}
for some
\vec{w}\in P(w,N)
are braids of positive entropy.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\in\mathit{Br}_{3}
be a list of
3
-strand braids such that:

(1)

They are all positive,
i.e.
, can be written without negative powers of the
\sigma_{i}
.

(2)

They are all pure,
i.e.
, map to the identity of
S_{n}
.

(3)

There is no matrix
g\in\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle|{\tr(g\gamma(\beta_{i}))}|\leq 2\quad\text{for all $i$}.

Let
L=\max_{i}\ell(\beta_{i})
, the maximum writhe among the
\beta_{i}
.
Then

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\{\vec{w}\in P(w,N)\mid h(\sigma_{\vec{w}})>0\}|\geq 6^{N-L-1}

for any integer
N\geq L+1
.

Proof.

By
7
, it suffices to give a lower bound on the number of
\vec{w}\in P(w,N)
such that
|{\tr(\gamma(\sigma_{\vec{w}}))}|>2
.

We have complete freedom to pick the first
N-L-1
entries of
\vec{w}
.
We pick the
(N-L)
th entry to ensure that the product of the first
N-L
entries of
\vec{w}
equals
w
.
By condition (3), there must be some
i
such that

(3.8)

\displaystyle|{\tr(\gamma(\sigma_{w_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{w_{N}-L})\gamma(\beta_{i}))}|>2.

Using condition (1), we can write
\beta_{i}=\sigma_{w_{N-L+1}}\cdots\sigma_{w_{N-L+k}}
for some
k\leq L
and
w_{N-L+1},\ldots,w_{N-L+k}\in S_{3}
.
For
j
such that
k<j\leq L
, we set
w_{N-L+j}=1
.
Finally, by condition (2), the product of all of the entries in the resulting tuple
\vec{w}
equals
w
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

In the setup of
8
, it is possible to choose the braids
\beta_{i}\in\mathit{Br}_{3}
so that
k=4
and
L=6
.
Explicitly,

(3.9)

\displaystyle(\beta_{i})_{i=1}^{4}=(1,\,\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}^{2},\,\sigma_{2}^{2}\sigma_{1}^{2},\,\sigma_{1}^{4}\sigma_{2}^{2}).

Proof.

Conditions (1)–(2) on the
\beta_{i}
are immediate; it remains to check condition (3).
Without loss of generality, we can normalize the reduced Burau representation so that the homomorphism
\gamma
in
7
takes the form

(3.10)

\displaystyle\gamma(\sigma_{1})=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),\quad\gamma(\sigma_{2})=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
-1&1\end{matrix}\right).

We compute that

(3.11)

\displaystyle(\gamma(\beta_{i}))_{i=1}^{4}=\left(\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}-3&2\\
-2&1\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}1&2\\
-2&-3\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}1&-2\\
4&-7\end{matrix}\right)\right).

So we must show that there cannot exist
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
such that

(3.12)

\displaystyle|a+d|

\displaystyle\leq 2,

(3.13)

\displaystyle|{-3}a-2b+2c+d|,|a-2b+2c-3d|

\displaystyle\leq 2,

(3.14)

\displaystyle|a+4b-2c-7d|

\displaystyle\leq 2.

(The structure of our argument will clarify why we group the inequalities in this way.)
In what follows, set
f=-2a+2b
.
First, by (
3.13
),

(3.15)

\displaystyle 4|a-d|\leq|{-3}a+f+d|+|a+f-3d|\leq 4,

from which we deduce

(3.16)

\displaystyle|a-d|\leq 1.

Next, by (
3.12
),
2|a|\leq|a-d|+|a+d|\leq 3
, from which
a\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

If
a=0
, then by (
3.16
),
d\in\{-1,0,1\}
.
If
d=0
, then (
3.13
) says
|f|\leq 2
, from which
-b+c\in\{-1,0,1\}
.
This contradicts the fact that
bc=ad-bc=1
.
If
d=1
, then (
3.13
) says
|f+1|,|f-3|\leq 2
.
This forces
f=1
, contradicting the fact that
f\in 2\mathbf{Z}
.
The argument when
d=-1
is similar, but with flipped signs.

If
a=1
, then by (
3.16
),
d\in\{0,1\}
.
If
d=0
, then the same argument as for
(a,d)=(0,1)
shows that
f=1
, contradicting
f\in 2\mathbf{Z}
.
If
d=1
, then (
3.13
) says
|f-2|\leq 2
, from which
-b+c\in\{0,1,2\}
.
But also,
1-bc=ad-bc=1
, from which
bc=0
.
Therefore,
(b,c)\in\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(-1,0),(-2,0)\}
, and each option contradicts (
3.14
).

Finally, the argument when
a=-1
is similar to that when
a=1
, except that in the
d=-1
subcase, the options for
(b,c)
have flipped signs in both entries, so we can again conclude using (
3.14
).
∎

3.4.

Next we explain how, using the sets
P(w,N)
above, we can pass from simple braids on many strands to non-simple braids on
3
strands that have equal or lower entropy.
For any integer
N>0
, let

(3.17)

\displaystyle C_{N}=\{w\in S_{3N}\mid\text{$w$ is a single cycle of length $3N$}\}.

We define a map

(3.18)

\displaystyle\vec{p}:C_{N}\to P(s_{1}s_{2},N)\sqcup P(s_{2}s_{1},N)

as follows.
First, for any
c\in C_{N}
and residue class
i
mod
3N
, let
a_{i}=a_{i}(c)
be the image of
1
under
c^{i}
, where we view
c^{i}
as a permutation of
\{1,2,\ldots,3N\}
.
In other words,
c
is the cycle
1=a_{0}\mapsto a_{1}\mapsto\cdots\mapsto a_{3N}=1
.
We define a permutation of
\{1,2,3\}
in three stages:

(1)

A bijection
\{1,2,3\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\{a_{i-1},a_{i-1+N},a_{i-1+2N}\}
sending
1
to the smallest element of the target,
2
to the next-smallest, and
3
to the largest.

(2)

A bijection
\{a_{i-1},a_{i-1+N},a_{i-1+2N}\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\{a_{i},a_{i+N},a_{i+2N}\}
sending

for all

.

(3)

A bijection
\{a_{i},a_{i+N},a_{i+2N}\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\{1,2,3\}
sending the smallest element of the domain to
1
, the next-smallest to
2
, and the largest to
3
.

For

, let

be the permutation of

resulting from the construction above.

Lemma 10
.

For all
c\in C_{N}
, the product

is a
3
-cycle, so (

\vec{p}(c)\vcentcolon=(p_{1}(c),\ldots,p_{N}(c))
.
Moreover,

(3.19)

\displaystyle|\{c\in C_{N}\mid\vec{p}(c)=\vec{w}\}|=\frac{|C_{N}|}{2\cdot 6^{N-1}}

for all
\vec{w}\in P(s_{1}s_{2},N)\sqcup P(s_{2}s_{1},N)
.
That is, the fibers of
\vec{p}

Proof.

In the notation of the discussion above, the product

is the permutation of
\{1,2,3\}
defined in stages by:

(1)

A bijection
\{1,2,3\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\{1,a_{N},a_{2N}\}
sending
1
to
1
,
2
to the next-smallest element, and
3
to the largest.

(2)

A permutation of
\{1=a_{0},a_{N},a_{2N}\}
sending
a_{k}\mapsto a_{k+N}
for all
k
.

(3)

A bijection
\{1,a_{N},a_{2N}\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\{1,2,3\}
sending
1
to
1
, the next-smallest element to
2
, and the largest to
3
.

We deduce that

is the
3
-cycle that sends

,
resp.

1\mapsto 3
, when

,
resp.

.
This proves the first assertion.

Next, observe that there are
\frac{|C_{N}|}{2\cdot 6^{N-1}}
ways to form an ordered

of sets of size

For any

.
Since the number of possible

,

,
resp.

|C_{N}|
, this will prove the second assertion.

It suffices to show that
\vec{w}
determines a way of assigning the elements of

bijectively to variables
a_{i-1},a_{i-1+N},a_{i-1+2N}
for every

, such that

.
We use induction:
If

, then the

determines whether we assign

Lemma 11
.

For all

, we have

\displaystyle h(\sigma_{c})\geq\frac{1}{N}h(\sigma_{\vec{p}(c)}).

3.5.

Combining

Lemma 12

For any

\displaystyle\frac{|\{c\in C_{N}\mid h(\sigma_{c})>0\}|}{|C_{N}|}\geq 6^{-6}.

For any

We arrive at the following result, reducing the proof of

Proposition 13

Let

be an equivalence class for the relation

Then

(3.23)

\displaystyle\frac{|\{w\in D\mid h(\sigma_{w})>0\}|}{|D|}\geq 1-(1-6^{-6})^{r}.

Proof.

Let

Lemma 16

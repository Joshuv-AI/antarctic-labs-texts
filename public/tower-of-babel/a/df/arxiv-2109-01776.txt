Abstract
. In this paper, we study the existence of Poisson metrics on flat vector bundles over noncompact Riemannian manifolds and discuss related consequence, specially on the applications in Higgs bundles, towards generalizing Corlette-Donaldson-Hitchin-Simpson’s nonabelian Hodge correspondence to noncompact Kähler manifolds setting.

,

(2.6)

\begin{split}\deg_{g}(S,D,K)=-\int_{M}\left(\tr(\pi_{K}\circ D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K})+\frac{1}{2}|D\pi_{K}|^{2}_{K}\right)\dvol_{g},\end{split}

where
\pi_{K}
is the orthogonal projection onto
S
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If a vector bundle
(E,D)
admits a Poisson metric
K
, it is an orthogonal direct sum of
K
-analytically stable bundles.

Proof.

For any nontrivial
D
-invariant sub-bundle
S\subset E
, the Chern-Weil formula yields

(2.7)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg_{g}(S,D,K)}{\rank(S)}&=\frac{-\int_{M}\left(\tr(\pi_{K}\circ D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K})+\frac{1}{2}|D\pi_{K}|^{2}_{K}\right)\dvol_{g}}{\rank(S)}\\
&=\frac{-\int_{M}\left(\frac{\rank(S)}{\rank(E)}\tr D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}+\frac{1}{2}|D\pi_{K}|^{2}_{K}\right)\dvol_{g}}{\rank(S)}\\
&\leq\frac{\deg_{g}(E,D,K)}{\rank(E)}.\end{split}

If the equality holds, we obtain
D\pi_{K}=0
. Let
S^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal complement of
S
in
E
with respect to
K
, then it is also a

D
-invariant sub-bundle such that
(E,D)\cong(S,D_{S})\oplus(S^{\perp},D_{S^{\perp}})
, where
D_{S}
and
D_{S^{\perp}}
are the induced connections.
Moreover, the induced metrics
K_{S}
and
K_{S^{\perp}}
both are Poisson metrics and we complete the proof by an easy induction.
∎

2.3.
Some useful calculations

We present some important calculations, it is emphasized that we are not working under the local flat frame and hence most of them need not the
assumption that the underlying connection is flat.

Given a vector bundle
E
over a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the exterior differential operator
D:\Omega^{p}(E)\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1}(E)

relative to a connection is given by

(2.8)

\begin{split}D\omega(e_{0},...,e_{p})&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{k}D_{e_{k}}(\omega(e_{0},e_{1},...,\hat{e_{k}},...,e_{p}))\\
&+\sum\limits_{k<l}(-1)^{k+l}\omega([e_{k},e_{l}],e_{1},...,\hat{e_{k}},...,\hat{e_{l}},...,e_{p}).\end{split}

Since the Levi-Civita connection on
TM
is torsion-free, we also have

(2.9)

\begin{split}D\omega(e_{0},...,e_{p})&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{k}(\tilde{\nabla}_{e_{k}}\omega)(e_{0},...,\hat{e_{k}},...,e_{p}),\end{split}

where
\tilde{\nabla}
is the induced connection on
\Omega^{\ast}(E)
.

For a metric
K
on
E
, we define the point-wise inner product

(2.10)

\begin{split}K(\omega,\theta)(x)=\sum\limits_{i_{1}<...<i_{p}}(\omega(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}}),\theta(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}}))_{K},\end{split}

where
\omega,\theta\in\Omega^{p}(E)
and
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\dim M}
is the orthogonal unit basis of
T_{x}M
. Then the decomposition
(\ref{decomposition})

is achieved by choosing
\psi_{K}
such that

(2.11)

\begin{split}K(\psi_{K}(X),Y)=\frac{1}{2}(K(DX,Y)+K(X,DY)-dK(X,Y)),\end{split}

for any
X,Y\in\Omega^{0}(E)
. With respect to the Riemannian structures of
E
and
TM
, the co-differential operator

D_{K}^{\ast}:\Omega^{p}(E)\rightarrow\Omega^{p-1}(E)
is characterized via the formula

(2.12)

\begin{split}\int_{M}K(D_{K}\omega,\theta)\dvol_{g}=\int_{M}K(\omega,D_{K}^{\ast}\theta)\dvol_{g},\end{split}

for any
\omega\in\Omega^{p-1}(E),\theta\in\Omega^{p}(E)
and either
\omega
or
\theta
is compactly supported. Then it holds

(2.13)

\begin{split}D_{K}^{\ast}\theta(e_{1},..,e_{p-1})=-\sum\limits_{i}(\nabla_{K,e_{i}}\theta)(e_{i},e_{1},...,e_{p-1}),\end{split}

where
\nabla_{K}
is the connection on
\Omega^{\ast}(E)
, induced by the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
and
D_{K}
.

If
H
be another metric on
E
, we define the positive endomorphism
h=K^{-1}H
by setting
H(\cdot,\cdot)=K(h(\cdot),\cdot)
.
A straightforward calculation, using
(\ref{psi})
, shows that

(2.14)

\begin{split}D_{H}&=D_{K}+\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\delta_{K}h=\frac{1}{2}(D_{K}+h^{-1}\circ D_{K}\circ h+\psi_{K}-h^{-1}\circ\psi_{K}\circ h),\end{split}

(2.15)

\begin{split}\psi_{H}&=\psi_{K}-\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\delta_{K}h=\frac{1}{2}(\psi_{K}+h^{-1}\circ\psi_{K}\circ h+D_{K}-h^{-1}\circ D_{K}\circ h),\end{split}

(2.16)

\begin{split}\delta_{H}&=\delta_{K}+h^{-1}\delta_{K}h=h^{-1}\circ\delta_{K}\circ h,\end{split}

where
\delta_{K}=D_{K}-\psi_{K}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
H(t)
is a one-parameter family of fiber metrics on the vector bundle
(E,D)
, then we have

(2.17)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial D_{H(t)}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}D_{H(t)}(h^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t})-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H}(t),h^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t}],\end{split}

(2.18)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\psi_{H(t)}}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{2}D_{H(t)}(h^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t})+\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H}(t),h^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t}],\end{split}

(2.19)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial D_{H(t)}^{\ast}\psi_{H(t)}}{\partial t}&=-\frac{1}{2}D_{H(t)}^{\ast}D_{H(t)}(h^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t})+\frac{1}{2}[D_{H(t)}^{\ast}\psi_{H(t)},h^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t}]\\
&-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}[\psi_{H(t)}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),[\psi_{H(t)}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}),h^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t}]],\end{split}

where
h(t)=K^{-1}H(t)
.

Proof.

It suffice to prove the
(\ref{DHt})
and
(\ref{DHstarpsiHt})
. By making use of
(\ref{DHDK})
,
(\ref{psiHpsiK})
, we deduce

(2.20)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial D_{H}}{\partial t}&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(h^{-1}D_{K}h-h^{-1}\circ\psi_{K}\circ h)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}h^{-1}D_{K}h+\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}D_{K}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}h^{-1}\psi_{K}h-\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\psi_{K}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}h^{-1}D_{K}h+\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}D_{K}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H},h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}]\\
&+\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(\psi_{H}-\psi_{K}-D_{K}+h^{-1}\circ D_{K}\circ h)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}(\psi_{H}-\psi_{K}-D_{K}+h^{-1}\circ D_{K}\circ h)h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}D_{H}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t})-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H},h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}]-\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}h^{-1}D_{K}h\\
&+\frac{1}{2}h^{-1}D_{K}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}[h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t},h^{-1}\circ D_{K}\circ h]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}D_{H}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t})-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H},h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}].\end{split}

On the other hand, let’s choose the local normal coordinate
(x^{1},...,x^{n})
at the considered point, using
(\ref{DHt})
and
(\ref{psiHt})
, we have

(2.21)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}}{\partial t}&=-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}},\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]\\
&=-[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}},\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]-[D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}[D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t})-[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}],\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]\\
&+\frac{1}{2}[D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}},D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t})-[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}]]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t})-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}]]\\
&-\frac{1}{2}[D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}),\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]-\frac{1}{2}[D_{H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}},[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}]]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}D_{H}^{\ast}D_{H}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t})+\frac{1}{2}[D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}]\\
&-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}]].\end{split}

∎

Under the local coordinate
(x^{1},...,x^{n})
, by using
(\ref{DHDK})
and
(\ref{psiHpsiK})
, it follows

(2.22)

\begin{split}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}&=D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}-\frac{1}{2}D_{K}^{\ast}(h^{-1}\delta_{K}h)-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}[h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h,\psi_{K}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}})]\\
&=D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}+\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}D_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}(h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}}h)-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}}h.\end{split}

From now on, the norms below are taken with respect to
K
unless indicated explicitly. Based on
(\ref{DHKstarpsiHK})
,
we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H
and
K
be two fiber metrics on the vector bundle
(E,D)
, it holds

(2.23)

\begin{split}(D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}-D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K},h)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\tr h-\frac{1}{2}|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{K}h|^{2},\end{split}

(2.24)

\begin{split}(D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}-D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},h^{-1})_{H}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\tr h^{-1}-\frac{1}{2}|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{H}h^{-1}|_{H}^{2},\end{split}

where
h=K^{-1}H
.

Proof.

We only proof
(\ref{keylemma1})
and
(\ref{keylemma2})
follows from
(\ref{keylemma1})
. Choose the local normal
coordinate
(x^{1},...,x^{n})
at the considered point, it follows

(2.25)

\begin{split}(D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}-D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K},h)&=-\frac{1}{2}(D_{K}^{\ast}(h^{-1}\delta_{K}h)+[h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h,\psi_{K}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})],h)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(D_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}(h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h),h)-\frac{1}{2}([h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h,\psi_{K}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})],h)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}(h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h,h)-\frac{1}{2}(h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h,D_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}(h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h,[\psi_{K}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),h])\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\tr\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h-\frac{1}{2}(h^{-1}\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h,\delta_{K,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}h)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\tr h-\frac{1}{2}|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{K}h|^{2}.\end{split}

∎

Following
[
13
]
, the Donaldson’s distance on the space of metrics is defined by

(2.26)

\begin{split}\sigma(K,H)=\tr(K^{-1}H)+\tr(H^{-1}K)-2\rank(E).\end{split}

It is obvious that
\sigma(H,K)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
H=K
.

By Lemma
2.2
, we get

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
H
and
K
be two harmonic metrics on the vector bundle
(E,D)
, then we have

(2.27)

\begin{split}\Delta\sigma(H,K)=|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{K}h|^{2}+|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{H}h^{-1}|_{H}^{2},\end{split}

where
h=K^{-1}H
. In particular, if
(E,D)
is simple and
(M,g)
satisfies the Assumption
2.1
, the mutually bounded harmonic metrics are unique up to scaling.

Now we consider the evolution equation

(2.28)

\begin{split}H^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial H(t)}{\partial t}=2D^{\ast}_{H(t)}\psi_{H(t)},\end{split}

a direct calculation yields

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(E,D)
be a vector bundle over
(M,g)
, assume
H(t)
and
K(t)
be two solutions of the heat flow
(\ref{outlineflow})
, then we have

(2.29)

\begin{split}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)\sigma(H(t),K(t))=-|\tilde{h}(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{K(t)}\tilde{h}(t)|^{2}_{K(t)}-|\tilde{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\delta_{H(t)}\tilde{h}^{-1}(t)|^{2}_{H(t)},\end{split}

where
\tilde{h}(t)=K^{-1}(t)H(t)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(E,D)
be a vector bundle over
(M,g)
, along the heat flow
(\ref{outlineflow})
it holds

(2.30)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}||\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}&=-2||D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}||_{H,2}^{2}-2\int_{M}d\eta_{H},\end{split}

(2.31)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}||D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}=-2||D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}-2||[\psi_{H},D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}]||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{M}d\tilde{\eta}_{H},\end{split}

(2.32)

\begin{split}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)|D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}&=-2|D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}-2|[\psi_{H},D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}]|_{H}^{2},\end{split}

where
\eta_{H}=H(D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},\ast\psi_{H})
,
\tilde{\eta}_{H}=H(D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},\ast D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H})
. Furthermore, if
D
is a flat connecton, we have

(2.33)

\begin{split}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)|\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}=-|[\psi_{H},\psi_{H}]|^{2}_{H}-2(\psi_{H}\circ\Ric,\psi_{H})_{H}-2|\nabla_{H}\psi_{H}|^{2}_{H},\end{split}

where
\Ric
is the Ricci transformation of
(M,g)
.

Proof.

Firstly, applying the equality
(\ref{psiHt})
yields

(2.34)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}||\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}&=2\int_{M}(-\frac{1}{2}D_{H}(H^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t})+\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H},H^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}],\psi_{H})_{H}\dvol_{g}\\
&=-2\int_{M}(D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},\psi_{H})_{H}\dvol_{g}\\
&=-2||D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}||_{H,2}^{2}-2\int_{M}d\eta_{H}.\end{split}

Next,
(\ref{DHstarpsiHt})
indicates that

(2.35)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}}{\partial t}=-D_{H}^{\ast}D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}-g^{ij}[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}),D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}]],\end{split}

as it is easy to check
D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}
is self-adjoint with respect to
H
, it then follows

(2.36)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}||D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}&=-2\int_{M}(D_{H}^{\ast}D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H})_{H}\dvol_{g}\\
&-2\int_{M}g^{ij}([\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}),D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}]],D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H})_{H}\dvol_{g}\\
&=-2||D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}-2||[\psi_{H},D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}]||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{M}d\tilde{\eta}_{H},\end{split}

and

(2.37)

\begin{split}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)|D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}&=-2(D_{H}^{\ast}D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H})_{H}\\
&-2g^{ij}([\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),[\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}),D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}]],D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H})_{H}\\
&+2(D_{H}^{\ast}D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H})_{H}-2|D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}\\
&=-2|D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}-2|[\psi_{H},D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}]|_{H}^{2}.\end{split}

On the other hand, we have

(2.38)

\begin{split}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)|\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}&=2(-\frac{1}{2}D_{H}(H^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t})+\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H},H^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}],\psi_{H})_{H}\\
&+2(\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\nabla_{H}\psi_{H},\psi_{H})_{H}-2|\nabla_{H}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}\\
&=-2(D_{H}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}-\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\nabla_{H}\psi_{H},\psi_{H})_{H}-2|\nabla_{H}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2},\end{split}

Now using the flatness of
D
:
D_{H}\psi_{H}=0
and
D_{H}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{H},\psi_{H}]
, it is equal to

(2.39)

\begin{split}&2g^{ij}(D_{H}^{2}(\cdot,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})(\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}))-\psi_{H}(R_{\nabla}(\cdot,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}})),\psi_{H})_{H}-2|\nabla_{H}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}\\
&=-(g^{ij}[[\psi_{H},\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})],\psi_{H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}})]+2\psi_{H}\circ\Ric,\psi_{H})_{H}-2|\nabla_{H}\psi_{H}|_{H}^{2}\\
&=-|[\psi_{H},\psi_{H}]|^{2}_{H}-2(\psi_{H}\circ\Ric,\psi_{H})_{H}-2|\nabla_{H}\psi_{H}|^{2}_{H}.\end{split}

∎

3.
Analytically stability and existence of Poisson metrics

Given a vector bundle
(E,D)
over a compact Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
, consider the following heat flow

(3.1)

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}H^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}=2D^{\ast}_{H}\psi_{H},\\
H(0)=K.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

And if
X
has nonempty smooth boundary
\partial X
, for any given compatible data
\tilde{H}
, we impose the boundary condition

H(t)|_{\partial X}=\tilde{H}
in the above system.

With the calculations in the last sections in hand and following
[
13
]
, we have

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(E,D)
be a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
(with possibly nonempty boundary),
then heat flow
(\ref{flow1})
admits unique solution defined on
[0,\infty)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
(E,D)
be a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
fiber metric
H_{\epsilon}
such that
||D_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let
H=H(t)
be the solution to
(\ref{flow1})
, defined on
[0,+\infty)
. Since
(\ref{flow1})
is the negative gradient flow
of the energy
||\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}
,
by
(\ref{timemodule})
we know
||D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}||_{H,L^{2}}^{2}\rightarrow 0
, as
t
goes infinity.
Then the proof can be finished by using
(\ref{modulet})
and parabolic Moser’s iteration.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(E,D)
be a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
with smooth boundary
\partial X
. Then for any given data
K
,
there exists a unique harmonic metric
H
on
(E,D)
such that
H=K
on
\partial X
.

Proof.

Due to Corollary
2.1
, it remain to prove the existence statement. By the standard elliptic equation theory,
let
u
be the solution to the following Dirichlet problem:

(3.2)

\begin{split}\Delta u=-|D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}|,u|_{\partial X}=0.\end{split}

Proposition
3.1
ensures the existence of long-time solution
H(t)
of
(\ref{flow1})
with boundary condition
H(t)|_{\partial X}=K
. Set

(3.3)

\begin{split}v(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}|D_{H(s)}^{\ast}\psi_{H(s)}|_{H(s)}ds-u,\end{split}

by
(\ref{modulet})
it is easy to see

(3.4)

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta)v(x,t)\leq 0,\\
v(x,0)=-u(x),\\
v(x,t)|_{\partial X}=0.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

Then the maximum principle gives

(3.5)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}|D_{H(s)}^{\ast}\psi_{H(s)}|_{H(s)}ds\leq\max\limits_{X}u.\end{split}

Now for any
t_{1}\leq t\leq t_{2}
and
\tilde{h}(t)=H^{-1}(t_{1})H(t),\hat{h}(t)=H^{-1}(t)H(t_{1})
, it is straightforward to check

(3.6)

\begin{split}&|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\tr\tilde{h}(t)|\leq 2|D_{H(t)}^{\ast}\psi_{H(t)}|_{H(t)},|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\tr\hat{h}(t)|\leq 2|D_{H(t)}^{\ast}\psi_{H(t)}|_{H(t)}.\end{split}

A simple integration yields

(3.7)

\begin{split}\sigma(H(t_{1}),H(t_{2}))\leq 2\rank(E)(e^{2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}|D_{H(s)}^{\ast}\psi_{H(s)}|_{H(s)}ds}-1).\end{split}

So by combining
(\ref{dirchlet1})
and
(\ref{dirchlet2})
, we know that
H(t)
converge in
C^{0}
-topology to
H_{\infty}

as

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(E,D)
be a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
with smooth boundary
\partial X
. Then for any given data

on

Proof.

\begin{split}D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}&=-\frac{1}{2}D_{\tilde{H}}^{\ast}(df\otimes\id_{E})=\frac{\tr D_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E}.\end{split}

Now we set

On each

Proposition 3.5

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,K}&=\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,H}+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}(h\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})\\
&=\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}D_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,H}(h\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial_{\theta,H}(h\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})\\
&=-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}D_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,H}(hD_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,H}^{c}h^{-1})+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}D_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,H}(h\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})\\
&-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial_{\theta,H}(h\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})\\
&=4\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}G_{H}^{\perp}-4\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}G_{K}^{\perp}+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}D_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,H}(h\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})\\
&-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial_{\theta,H}(h\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})\\
&=-2(D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K})^{\perp}+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial_{\theta,H}(h\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})\\
&+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}(h\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial_{\theta,H}(h\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1}),\end{split}

and

(4.28)

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial_{\theta,H}(h\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})&=\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}(\partial_{\theta,H}h\wedge\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}(\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h\wedge\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})\\
&-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}(\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h\wedge\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}(h\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})\\
&=\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}(\partial_{\theta,H}h\wedge\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}(\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h\wedge\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})\\
&-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,K}.\end{split}

where we have used
(\ref{sec428})
. We obtain from above two equalities that

(4.29)

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,K}&=-(D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K})^{\perp}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\Lambda_{\omega}(\partial_{\theta,H}h\wedge\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})\\
&+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\Lambda_{\omega}(\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h\wedge\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1})+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}(h\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial_{\theta,H}(h\partial_{\theta,H}h^{-1}),\end{split}

from which we conclude
|\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,K}|\in L^{1}
.

For any
\theta
-invariant sub-holomorphic bundle
V\subset E
, we have the orthogonal decomposition
E=V\oplus V^{\perp}
with
respect to
K
and the projection
\pi_{K}
onto
V
. We denote by
K_{V}
,
H_{V}
the metrics on

K
and
H
.
If

, by Chern-Weil formula
(\ref{ChernHiggs})
and
|\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,K}|\in L^{1}
, we know

(4.30)

\begin{split}\deg_{\omega}(V,K)=-\infty.\end{split}

If
|\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}\pi_{K}|\in L^{2}
, the same reason indicates
\deg_{\omega}(V,K)
is finite. It also holds

(4.31)

\begin{split}h_{V}=\pi_{K}\circ h\circ\pi_{K},\end{split}

(4.32)

\begin{split}\overline{\partial}_{V,\theta_{V}}h_{V}=\pi_{K}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h\circ\pi_{K}+\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}\pi_{K}\circ(\id_{E}-\pi_{K})\circ h\circ\pi_{K},\end{split}

where
h_{V}=K_{V}^{-1}H_{V}
and
\overline{\partial}_{V,\theta_{V}}
is the induced Higgs structure on

. Therefore

(4.33)

\begin{split}||\tr(h_{V}^{-1}\partial_{\theta_{V},K_{V}}h_{V})||_{L^{2}}&\leq||h_{V}^{-1}||_{L^{\infty}}||\overline{\partial}_{V,\theta_{V}}h_{V}||_{L^{2}}<\infty.\end{split}

So Simpson’s Lemma 5.2 in
[
39
]
(or Yau’s Lemma in

(4.34)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}\tr(h_{V}^{-1}\partial_{\theta_{V},K_{V}}h_{V})=0,\end{split}

where

is a sequence of exhaustion subsets.
And

(4.35)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\sqrt{-1}\tr F_{\overline{\partial}_{V},\theta_{V},H_{V}}&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\sqrt{-1}\tr F_{\overline{\partial}_{V},\theta_{V},K_{V}}\\
&+\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\sqrt{-1}\tr(\overline{\partial}_{V,\theta_{V}}(h_{V}^{-1}\partial_{\theta_{V},K}h_{V}))\\
&=\deg_{\omega}(V,K),\end{split}

then we have

(4.36)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\sqrt{-1}\tr F_{\overline{\partial}_{V},\theta_{V},H_{V}}&=\int_{X}\sqrt{-1}\tr F_{\overline{\partial}_{V},\theta_{V},H_{V}}=\deg_{\omega}(V,H).\end{split}

Since
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is
H
-analytically stable, we conclude by

,
(\ref{sec4211})
and
(\ref{sec4212})

-analytically stable.

Next assume

be another Hermitian-Einstein metric such that

and

for

\begin{split}D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}&=-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,K}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\Lambda_{\omega}(\partial_{\theta,H}h\wedge\overline{\partial}_{E,\theta}h^{-1})\\

4.3.
Vanishing theorems of characteristic classes

Associated with a vector bundle
(E,D)
, up to some normalization coefficients, the Kamber-Tondeur classes are defined by

(4.38)

\begin{split}\alpha_{2k+1}(E,D)=[\tr\psi_{H}^{2k+1}]\in H^{2k+1}_{DR}(M,\mathbb{R}),\end{split}

and the class is independent of the choice of fiber metrics, see
[
4
,
11
,
16
,
23
,
35
]
for the related study of
these characteristic classes.

By Proposition
4.4
, we know
\psi_{H}^{1,0}\wedge\psi_{H}^{1,0}=0
and following
[
24
]
, for any
k\geq 1
we deduce

(4.39)

\begin{split}\tr\psi_{H}^{2k+1}&=\tr\left((\psi_{H}^{1,0}\wedge\psi_{H}^{0,1})^{2k}\wedge\psi_{H}^{1,0}\right)+\tr\left((\psi_{H}^{0,1}\wedge\psi_{H}^{1,0})^{2k}\wedge\psi_{H}^{0,1}\right)\\
&=\tr\left(\psi_{H}^{1,0}\wedge(\psi_{H}^{1,0}\wedge\psi_{H}^{0,1})^{2k}\right)+\tr\left(\psi_{H}^{0,1}\wedge(\psi_{H}^{0,1}\wedge\psi_{H}^{1,0})^{2k}\right)\\
&=0.\end{split}

So we conclude the following vanishing theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a complete Kähler manifold with bounded Ricci curvature from below and satisfying the Assumptions
2.1
.
Assume
(E,D)
be a
K
-analytically stable flat bundle over
X
such that
|(D_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K})^{\perp}|\leq C\phi
for a constant
C

and
|\psi_{K}|\in L^{2}
. Then the characteristic classes
\alpha_{2k+1}(E,D)
vanish for all
k\geq 1
.

We mention that in the compact setting, this result was firstly proved in
[
36
]
.

On the curvature of the blowup of a point
Gunnar Þór Magnússon

Abstract
. We consider the blowup of a point of a compact Kähler manifold and a metric of the form μ ∗ ​ h + t ​ b \mu^{*}h+tb on it, where h h is a Kähler metric on the original manifold and b b is Hermitian form that looks like the Fubini–Study metric near the exceptional divisor. We calculate the curvature tensor of this metric on the exceptional divisor and show that its holomorphic sectional curvature is negative in some directions for all small enough t t , which torpedos a natural approach to showing that blowups of manifolds of positive holomorphic sectional curvature have positive curvature.



Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
Let
h
be a Kähler metric on
X
.
We write
D
for its Chern connection and

R(\alpha,\overline{\beta},\gamma,\overline{\delta})=h(\frac{i}{2}D^{2}_{\smash{\alpha,\overline{\beta}}}\gamma,\overline{\delta})
for its curvature tensor. The
holomorphic sectional curvature of
h
is

H(\xi)=R(\xi,\overline{\xi},\xi,\overline{\xi})/|\xi|^{4},

where
\xi
is a nonzero tangent field.
We say that
h
has positive holomorphic sectional curvature if
H>0
for all
tangent fields.
Examples of manifolds that carry such metrics are the complex projective space
with its Fubini–Study metric, the standard metric on a Grassmannian manifold,
and projectivized bundles over bases of positive holomorphic sectional curvature

[
2
]
.
Tsukamoto
[
6
]
showed that manifolds that admit such
metrics are simply connected, and recently Xiaokui Yang
[
9
]

proved that such manifolds are projective and rationally connected, answering a
question of Yau
[
10
, Problem 67]
.

A related question of Yau (again Problem 67) is whether the blowup of a compact
Kähler manifold of positive holomorphic sectional curvature along a smooth submanifold again has
positive holomorphic sectional curvature.
This is open; even for complex surfaces it is not known whether del Pezzo
surfaces admit positively curved metrics (aside from the del Pezzo surfaces of
degrees 8 and 9, where the nontrivial one admits such a metric because it’s
also a Hirzebruch surface and thus a projectivized bundle). In this note we
explain that the brute force approach to this problem does not work.

Prior art in this direction has focused on showing that projective bundles
over manifolds with positive holomorphic sectional curvature are also positive, along with more
general fibrations with positively curved fibers and base.
The common thread in the papers of
Hitchin
[
4
]
,
Sung
[
5
]
,
Álvarez
[
1
]
,
Álvarez,
Heier and Zheng
[
2
]
or Chaturvedi and
Heier
[
3
]

is to consider metrics of the form
\mu^{*}h+tb
, where
h
is a metric
on the base and
b
a fiberwise metric on the fibers and show they have
positive curvature for small enough
t
.
In this note we try this approach for the blowup of a point.
We calculate the full curvature tensor of
h_{t}
on a point on the exceptional
divisor and show that the metric
does not have positive holomorphic sectional curvature there, no matter what metric
h
we started with.
This of course doesn’t mean the blowup doesn’t have positive holomorphic sectional curvature, only that
the most straightforward method of constructing a metric on it fails to show that.

1.
Curvature

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
\dim_{\mathbf{C}}X=n

and let
h
be a Kähler metric on it.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mu:\widehat{X}\to X
be the blowup of
X
at a point
p
.
There exists a closed
(1,1)
-form
\beta
on
\widehat{X}
whose restriction to
the exceptional divisor is the Fubini–Study metric.

Proof.

This is proved for blowups of smooth submanifolds in
Voisin’s textbook
[
7
]
.
We can simplify that proof a little since we’re only blowing up points.
In that case the statement goes back at least to Kodaira’s proof of the
embedding theorem.

Locally around
p
, which may assume is the origin in a complex vector space

V
, the blowup is

\widehat{V}=\{(v,[w])\in V\times\mathbf{P}(V)\mid v\in\mathbf{C}w\}

and
\mu
is the projection onto the first factor.
Pick an inner product on
V
and let
B(r)\subset V
be a ball of radius
r

centered at
0
.
We pick
r
so that
B(r)
fits into the coordinate chart that implicitly lurks
in the background.
Let
\psi
be a bump function supported on that chart that is identically
1

on
B(r)
.
The
(1,1)
-form
\frac{i}{2}\partial\bar{\partial}\log|v|^{2}
on
V\setminus\{0\}
descends to
\mathbf{P}(V)
and defines the Fubini–Study metric.
If
p_{j}:V\times V\setminus\{0\}\to V
for
j=1,2
are the projections
onto the first and second factors then

\frac{i}{2}\partial\bar{\partial}(p_{1}^{*}\psi\log|p_{2}^{*}v|^{2})

defines a closed
(1,1)
-form on
V\times\mathbf{P}(V)
that restricts to the
pullback of the Fubini–Study metric by
p_{2}
on
B(r)\times\mathbf{P}(V)
.
It also extends to the rest of
X
by zero.
Its restriction to
\widehat{V}
is the form we want.
∎

Let
p\in X
be a point and blow it up to obtain
\mu:\widehat{X}\to X
.
Let
b
be the Hermitian form associated to the
(1,1)
-form
\beta
on
\widehat{X}

we constructed in Proposition
1
.
Then
h_{t}=\mu^{*}h+tb
is a Kähler metric on
\widehat{X}
for all
t
small
enough.
We’re going to calculate its curvature tensor at a point on the exceptional
divisor.

Recall that locally around
p
the blowup is

\widehat{V}=\{(v,[w])\in V\times\mathbf{P}(V)\mid v\in\mathbf{C}w\}.

If
f\in\GL V
then
f
acts on the blowup by
f(v,[w])=(f(v),[f(w)])
.
This is an isomorphism that maps the exceptional divisor to itself, and we can
map any point on the divisor to any other point on it.
Let
(0,[w])
be a point on
E
.
Let’s choose normal coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
centered at
p
.
There exists
f\in U(n)
so that
f(w)=(0\ldots,0,1)
, and the
coordinates obtained by applying
f
to the old ones are still centered at
p

and are normal there because
f\in U(n)
.
Picking the chart
\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbf{C}^{n}\mid y_{n}\not=0\}
for

\mathbf{P}(V)

we realize the blowup as

\widehat{X}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{n-1}\mid x_{j}y_{k}=x_{k}y_{j}\text{ for $j,k=1,\ldots,n$, where $y_{n}=1$}\}.

In these coordinates the point we want to calculate the holomorphic sectional curvature at is
(0,0)

and we have
D_{h,\xi}\partial/\partial x_{j}=0
at
0
for
j=1,\ldots,n
.

We also note that close to the exceptional divisor,
h_{t}=\mu^{*}h+tb
is
just the restriction of the product metric
p_{1}^{*}h\oplus tp_{2}^{*}b
on

V\times\mathbf{P}(V)
to
\widehat{X}
, where
p_{j}
are the projections onto the
factors and
b
is the Fubini–Study metric.

The only equations that give us any information at
(0,0)
are

x_{j}-y_{j}x_{n}=0,\quad j=1,\ldots,n-1.

Their differentials are

dx_{j}-y_{j}dx_{n}-x_{n}dy_{j}=0,\quad j=1,\ldots,n-1

and so the tangent fields

\xi_{j}=x_{n}e_{j}+f_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,n-1,\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}y_{j}e_{j}+e_{n}

are a basis for
the intersection of all the kernels of the differentials, that is, of
T_{\widehat{X}}
.
Here
e_{j}
is the tangent field corresponding to the coordinate
x_{j}

and
f_{k}
the one corresponding to
y_{k}
.
Note that the orthogonal complement of
T_{\smash{\widehat{X}}}
at
(0,0)
is spanned by

(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1})
.
Any holomorphic tangent field
\xi
on
\widehat{X}
near
(0,0)
can be written as

\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\xi_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(\alpha_{j}x_{n}+\alpha_{n}y_{j})e_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{j}f_{j}+\alpha_{n}e_{n},

where the
\alpha_{j}
are holomorphic functions.

While writing out the curvature tensor it will be useful to have the
semipositive Hermitian form
\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})=\langle\alpha,\bar{\beta}\rangle-\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}
at hand. This is the pullback of the standard inner product
by the projection onto the subspace spanned by the first
n-1
basis elements.

Proposition 2
.

Let

\alpha=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\xi_{j}
,

\beta=\sum_{k}\beta_{k}\xi_{k}
,

\gamma=\sum_{l}\gamma_{l}\xi_{l}

and
\delta=\sum_{m}\delta_{m}\xi_{m}

be holomorphic tangent fields close to
(0,0)
.
The curvature of
h_{t}
at the origin is

\hfil\displaystyle R_{h_{t}}(\alpha,\overline{\beta},\gamma,\overline{\delta})=H_{h}(e_{n})\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\gamma_{n}\bar{\delta}_{n}+t\bigl(\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})\tau(\gamma,\bar{\delta})+\tau(\alpha,\bar{\delta})\tau(\gamma,\bar{\beta})\bigr)\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill{}-\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\tau(\gamma,\bar{\delta})-\alpha_{n}\bar{\delta}_{n}\tau(\gamma,\bar{\beta})-\gamma_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\tau(\alpha,\bar{\delta})-\gamma_{n}\bar{\delta}_{n}\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta}).

Proof.

Recall that the curvature of
h_{t}
is

R_{h_{t}}(\alpha,\overline{\beta},\gamma,\overline{\delta})=p_{1}^{*}R_{h}(\alpha,\overline{\beta},\gamma,\overline{\delta})+tp_{2}^{*}R_{b}(\alpha,\overline{\beta},\gamma,\overline{\delta})-\langle\sigma(\alpha,\gamma),\overline{\sigma(\beta,\delta)}\rangle,

where
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)=\pi_{N}(D_{h\oplus tb,\alpha}\beta)
is
the second fundamental form, and the inner product is on the orthogonal complement
of the tangent bundle.

First,

p_{1}^{*}R_{h}(\alpha,\overline{\beta},\gamma,\overline{\delta})=H_{h}(e_{n})\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\gamma_{n}\bar{\delta}_{n}

at the origin. Second,

\displaystyle p_{2}^{*}R_{b}(\alpha,\overline{\beta},\gamma,\overline{\delta})

\displaystyle=\langle p_{2}(\alpha),\overline{p_{2}(\beta)}\rangle_{b}\langle p_{2}(\gamma),\overline{p_{2}(\delta)}\rangle_{b}+\langle p_{2}(\alpha),\overline{p_{2}(\delta)}\rangle_{b}\langle p_{2}(\gamma),\overline{p_{2}(\beta)}\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle=\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})\tau(\gamma,\bar{\delta})+\tau(\alpha,\bar{\delta})\tau(\gamma,\bar{\beta})

at the origin
because the Fubini–Study metric has constant holomorphic sectional curvature 2
and
\tau
is just the inner product on
\mathbf{C}^{n-1}
extended to
\mathbf{C}^{n}
.

Let’s now write
\pi_{N}
for the projection onto the orthogonal complement of

T_{\smash{\widehat{X}}}
in
T_{\smash{\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{n-1}|\widehat{X}}}
.
At the origin it is just the projection onto the first
n-1
coordinates.
Third, then,

\pi_{N}(D_{h_{t},\alpha}\gamma)=\pi_{N}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{\alpha}\gamma_{j}\,\xi_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}D_{\alpha}\xi_{j}\biggr).

Note that
\pi_{N}(\xi_{j})=0
for
j=1,\ldots,n
so the first term from that sum
is zero. The second term is

\pi_{N}(D_{\alpha}\xi_{j})=\pi_{N}(D_{\alpha}(x_{n}e_{j}+f_{j}))=\alpha_{n}e_{j}+x_{n}D_{\alpha}e_{j}=\alpha_{n}e_{j}

for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
at the origin because
D_{\alpha}e_{j}=0
there.
We also have

\pi_{N}(D_{\alpha}\xi_{n})=\pi_{N}\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\delta_{\alpha}y_{k}\,e_{k}+y_{k}D_{\alpha}e_{k}+D_{\alpha}e_{n}\biggr)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{k}e_{k}

at the origin.
Together we get

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}\pi_{N}(D_{\alpha}\xi_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{j}\alpha_{n}e_{j}+\gamma_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(\alpha_{n}\gamma_{j}+\gamma_{n}\alpha_{j})e_{j}.

And fourth,

\pi_{N}(p_{2}^{*}D_{g,\alpha}\gamma)=\pi_{N}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\delta_{\alpha}\gamma_{j}\,f_{j}+\gamma_{j}p_{2}^{*}D_{g,\alpha}f_{j}\biggr)=0

at the origin because there the argument to
\pi_{N}
takes values in the bundle
spanned by the
f_{j}
.
The second fundamental form then contributes

\displaystyle\langle\sigma(\alpha,\gamma),\overline{\sigma(\beta,\delta)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(\alpha_{n}\gamma_{j}+\gamma_{n}\alpha_{j})(\bar{\beta}_{n}\bar{\delta}_{j}+\bar{\delta}_{n}\bar{\beta}_{j})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\tau(\gamma,\bar{\delta})+\alpha_{n}\bar{\delta}_{n}\tau(\gamma,\bar{\beta})+\gamma_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\tau(\alpha,\bar{\delta})+\gamma_{n}\bar{\delta}_{n}\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})

to the curvature. Putting all of this together we get the result.
∎

A fairly natural strategy to proving that the blowup of a point of a manifold
that has a metric with positive holomorphic sectional curvature is also positively curved is to
show that
h_{t}
has positive holomorphic sectional curvature on a neighborhood around the exceptional
divisor for all small enough
t
. Then we could conclude that the blowup has
positive holomorphic sectional curvature by using Wu’s
[
8
]
characterization of the
holomorphic sectional curvature to show positivity outside of that neighborhood for
t
small enough.
The following corollary shows this strategy cannot work.

Corollary 3
.

The holomorphic sectional curvature of
h_{t}
is negative in
some tangent directions at the origin for all small enough
t
.

Proof.

Let
\xi=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\xi_{j}
be a holomorphic tangent field.
Note that
|\xi_{j}|_{h_{t}}^{2}=t
for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
and
|\xi_{n}|_{h_{t}}^{2}=1

at the origin.
We have

H_{h_{t}}(\xi)=\frac{|\alpha_{n}|^{4}H_{h}(e_{n})+2t\tau(\alpha,\bar{\alpha})^{2}-4|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\tau(\alpha,\bar{\alpha})}{(t\tau(\alpha,\bar{\alpha})+|\alpha_{n}|^{2})^{2}}.

First suppose that
H_{h}(e_{n})<0
. Picking
\alpha
with
|\alpha_{n}|=1
we have

\tau(\alpha,\bar{\alpha})=0
so
H_{h_{t}}(\xi)=H_{h}(e_{n})<0
.

Then suppose that
H_{h}(e_{n})\geq 0
.
We may assume that
|\alpha|=1
when checking for positivity and focus only on
the numerator. We’re then left considering the positivity of the polynomial

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=H_{h}(e_{n})x^{2}+2t(1-x)^{2}-4x(1-x)

\displaystyle=(H_{h}(e_{n})+2t+4)x^{2}-4(1-t)x+2t

for
0\leq x\leq 1
.
Its critical point is

x_{t}=\frac{2(1-t)}{H_{h}(e_{n})+2t+4}

where its value is

p_{t}(x_{t})=-\frac{4(1-t)^{2}}{H_{h}(e_{n})+2t+4}+2t,

which is negative for all small
t
.
∎

This corollary shows that
no
metric of the form
\mu^{*}h+tb
can have
positive holomorphic sectional curvature when
h
does, so we can’t prove that the blowup carries such
a metric in this way.
Note however that the blowup of the projective plane at a single point
is a Hirzebruch surface, thus a projective bundle, and thus carries
a metric of positive holomorphic sectional curvature.

Corollary 4
.

Let

\alpha=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\xi_{j}
,

\beta=\sum_{k}\beta_{k}\xi_{k}

be holomorphic tangent fields close to
(0,0)
.
The Ricci tensor of
h_{t}
is

r_{t}(\alpha,\overline{\beta})=H_{h}(e_{n})\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}+(n-1)\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})-(n-1)\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}/t

at the origin.

Proof.

The tangent fields that correspond to

v_{j}=(\delta_{1j}/\sqrt{t},\ldots,\delta_{n-1,j}/\sqrt{t},\delta_{nj})
are
orthonormal at the origin.
If
\xi
and
\eta
are the fields corresponding to
(\alpha_{j})
and
(\beta_{j})
we
first have

\sum_{j=1}^{n}H_{h}(e_{n})\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\delta_{jn}=H_{h}(e_{n})\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n},

and then

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})\tau(v_{j},\bar{v}_{j})+\tau(\alpha,\bar{v}_{j})\tau(v_{j},\bar{\beta})=n\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})/t.

The second fundamental form contributes

\hfil\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}\tau(v_{j},\bar{v}_{j})+\alpha_{n}\delta_{jn}\tau(v_{j},\bar{\beta})+\delta_{jn}\bar{\beta}_{n}\tau(\alpha,\bar{v}_{j})+\delta_{jn}^{2}\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})\hfill

\hfil\displaystyle\hfill{}=(n-1)\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}/t+\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})

because
\tau(\alpha,\bar{v}_{n})=0
.
Together we get

r_{t}(\alpha,\bar{\beta})=H_{h}(e_{n})\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}+(n-1)\tau(\alpha,\bar{\beta})-(n-1)\alpha_{n}\bar{\beta}_{n}/t.\qed

We see that the Ricci tensor can also be negative in some directions for
t

large enough, but is positive in other directions for all
t
.

Corollary 5
.

The scalar curvature of
h_{t}
is

s_{t}=H_{h}(e_{n})+(n-1)(n-2)/t

at the origin.

This is the same as Hitchin gets for the scalar curvature if we take his
expressions for
R_{0}
and
R_{1}
in
[
4
, Lemmas 5.15 and
5.16]
and evaluate them at
r=0
, which is
a nice sanity check.
With more work I think we could recover Hitchin’s full results.
We’ve made some attempts in that direction to see if we could extend his
result on positive scalar curvature to Hermitian metrics, but seem to run
into obstructions due to the nonvanishing of the connection at the origin.

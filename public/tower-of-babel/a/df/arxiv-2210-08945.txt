The universal coCartesian fibration
Denis-Charles Cisinski and Hoang Kim Nguyen

Abstract
Abstract. We give a new proof of the straightening/unstraightening correspondence by proving a generalization of the univalence property of the universal coCartesian fibration.



Introduction

A fundamental tool of
\infty
-category theory
is that there is an
\infty
-category
\mathit{Cat}
of small
\infty
-categories
so that coCartesian fibrations with small fibers of the form
X\to A
correspond
to functors
A\to\mathit{Cat}
. And this correspondence is implemented through an equivalence of categories.
This is
what is coined as straightening/unstraightening by Lurie in his foundational work.
Whether it is in his published monograph
Higher Topos Theory

[
Lur09
]

or in the
Kerodon

[
Lur
]
,
the
\infty
-category
\mathit{Cat}
of small
\infty
-categories is constructed
in two steps: we construct a category enriched in Kan complexes of small
\infty
-categories
and apply the homotopy coherent nerve to the latter. From this point of view,
proving the straightening/unstraightening
correspondence will thus necessarily involve dealing with interpreting at least some constructions
of
\infty
-category theory through the homotopy coherent nerve or its left adjoint.
We propose here an alternative construction in the setting of quasi-categories which does not use
any kind of homotopy coherent nerve. It is an adaptation of simpler version of
the straightening/unstraightening
correspondence in the case where the fibers are
\infty
-groupoids already developed in
[
Cis19
]
, which is itself an adaptation of the construction of a univalent universe of Kan complexes in the sense of homotopy type theory by Kapulkin, Lumsdaine and Voevodsky
[
KL21
]
, and
further generalized by Shulman
[
Shu15
,
Shu19
]
.
Similar constructions and proofs could also be performed directly in the language of complete Segal spaces,
completing the work of Rasekh
[
Ras21
]
. Whereas the point of view on the

\infty
-category of small
\infty
-categories as a homotopy coherent nerve has the advantage of giving rather quickly a concrete description on this fundamental object
(and this can be done rather efficiently, as explained for instance in
[
HHR21
]
) this kind of construction is not fully satisfying: it is now clear that the theory of
\infty
-categories goes much beyond being some form of theory of categories enriched in homotopy types and there is no reason to impose that we have a set (nor a
0
-truncated object) of objects. Indeed, we already have several
examples of new contexts (new topoi) in which we want to do mathematics, including applying homotopical methods such as
\infty
-category-theoretic constructions, e.g. equivariant stable homotopy theory, global homotopy theory, or condensed mathematics,
and a systematic study of the semantic interpretation of
\infty
-category theory in any
\infty
-topos is already developed in the work of
Martini and Wolf
[
Mar21
,
Mar22
,
MW21
,
MW22
]
.
There are already formal theories of
\infty
-category as well:
in the direction of cosmoi (from the point of view of categories enriched in quasi-categories), as in the work of Riehl and Verity
[
RV22
]

(with its own version of
straightening/unstraightening correspondence
[
RV18
]
), or in the direction of
directed type theory, as in the work of North
[
Nor19
]
and Riehl and Shulman
[
RS17
]
. Our contribution here is to provide a formulation of
the property of
directed univalence
which is a property which is
not obviously equivalent to the straightening/unstraightening
correspondence but seems to encode all the information needed to
deduce it, while it makes sense in any formal context in which
we can express the language of
\infty
-category theory. We hope this
can be useful in practice as well as to study the foundations of
directed type theory.

In this article, we discuss the universal coCartesian fibration
once again. We will mainly focus on its construction in the classical setting
of
\infty
-category theory which is nowadays the one of quasi-categories.
From this perspective, this may be seen
as a complement of the first name author’s book
[
Cis19
]
, and
as an addition to the general literature giving an alternative access to such a fundamental construction, in the spirit of
[
HHR21
]
, which we hope to be useful: we have tried our best to make these notes usable
by any reader eager to learn and/or use this theory in their own research.
But we also focus secretly on a reformulation
of the straightening/unstraightening correspondence
that could easily be formulated (or implemented) through a formal language
in any abstract directed type theory: directed univalence. The way we formulate
it is more general than the one considered by Licata and Weaver
[
LW20
]
: if
we take apart the constructive aspects of their work, what they do is
more in the spirit of
[
Cis19
, §5.2–5.3]
, since they focus on
left fibrations (coCartesian fibration with fibers in
\infty
-groupoids).
In the context of
\infty
-category theory,
we prove that the universal
coCartesian fibration defined tautologically (Definition
3.3
)
is directed univalent (Theorem
7.6
). More generally,
we define what it means for a coCartesian fibration
to be
directed univalent

(Definition
7.9
) and
prove that a coCartesian fibration is directed
univalent if and only if its classifying functor is a full embedding of its
codomain into the
\infty
-category theory of
\infty
-categories
(Theorem
7.10
). Since
\infty
-groupoids form
a full subcategory of the
\infty
-category theory of
\infty
-categories,
this gives yet another proof that the universal left fibration is directed
univalent, for instance (thus recovering some results of
[
Cis19
]
).
We also explain how to deduce the straightening/unstraightening correspondence
in the following form: given any small
\infty
-category
A
, there is
a canonical functor

{}_{A}\colon\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})

from the category
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}
of marked simplicial sets over
A

that exhibits
\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})
with the
\infty
-category theoretic
localization of
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}
at the covariant weak equivalences
(the class of weak equivalences of the covariant model structure, the
fibrant objects of which precisely are the coCartesian fibrations over

A
).

In this paper, we will start almost from scratch: we mainly
need basic results on the properties of coCartesian fibrations
that are explained in Lurie’s
Higher Topos Theory

[
Lur09
, §2.4]
.
Many of the arguments in this paper
are adaptations of arguments developed
in detail in
[
Cis19
, Chapter 5]
to the setting of marked
simplicial sets. In particular,
we will also use covariant model structures on marked simplicial sets
whose fibrant objects are the coCartesian fibrations.
Such model structures are described in
[
Lur09
, §3.1]

and are revisited by the
second named author in
[
Ngu19a
]
. Technical but
fundamental properties of these model structures (expressing
in secret exponentiability of coCartesian fibrations as well
as Beck-Chevalley properties of Kan extensions along coCartesian
fibrations) are furthermore developed in a companion paper by
the second named author
[
Ngu22
]
; such results will
be recalled explicitly when needed.
Only in the last section, in order to deduce
the straightening/unstraightening correspondence (Theorem
8.13
),
will we use less elementary results about localizations of
model structures from the last chapter of
[
Cis19
]
.
We also explain how to interpret the straightening/unstraightening correspondence
in a purely internal way (Theorem
8.16
).

1.5
.

We next recall the coCartesian model structure. This has been first constructued in
[
Lur09
]
. We follow the slightly more general treatment in
[
Ngu19a
]
. We denote by
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}
the category of marked simplicial sets. Its objects are given by pairs
(A,E_{A})
where
A
is a simplicial set and
E_{A}
is a subset of its 1-simplices containing all the degenerate 1-simplices. We will in general denote a marked simplicial set by
A^{+}
. The forgetful functor

U\colon\mathbf{sSet}^{+}\to\mathbf{sSet}

has both a left adjoint, denoted by
(A)^{\flat}
, and a right adjoint, denoted by
(A)^{\sharp}
. The simplicial set
(A)^{\flat}
has precisely the non-degenerate edges marked, while
(A)^{\sharp}
has all 1-simplices marked.

Definition 1.6
.

We define the class of
marked left anodyne extensions
to be the smallest saturated class containing the morphisms

(A1)

(\Lambda^{n}_{k})^{\flat}\to(\Delta^{n})^{\flat}
for
n\geq 2
and
0<k<n
,

(A2)

J^{\flat}\to J^{\sharp}
,

(B1)

(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\flat}\cup\{0\}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\to(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}
,

(B2)

(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\partial\Delta^{n})^{\flat}\cup\{0\}\times(\Delta^{n})^{\flat}\to(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{n})^{\flat}
.

Here, the simplicial set
J
is the nerve of the free-walking isomorphism.

Remark 1.7
.

The class we have described here differs slightly from Lurie’s definition of
marked anodyne morphisms
. Nevertheless, they define the same saturated class as his marked anodyne morphisms.

Definition 1.8
.

A
marked left fibration
is a map of marked simplicial sets having the right lifting property with respect to the class of marked left anodyne extensions.

Remark 1.9
.

Note that a map
(X,E_{X})\to A^{\sharp}
is a marked left fibration if and only if the underlying map of simplicial sets is a coCartesian fibration and the set
E_{X}
is precisely the set of coCartesian edges.

Remark 1.10
.

There is the ’dual’ notion of marked
right
anodyne extensions in which the sets (B1) and (B2) in Definition
1.6
are replaced by the sets

(B1’)

(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\flat}\cup\{1\}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\to(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}
,

(B2’)

(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\partial\Delta^{n})^{\flat}\cup\{1\}\times(\Delta^{n})^{\flat}\to(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{n})^{\flat}
.

Accordingly we have the notion of marked
right
fibration.

.

Let
A^{+}
be a marked simplicial set. Then there exists a left proper combinatorial model structure on
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{+}
with the following description.

•

The cofibrations are the morphisms of marked simplicial sets, whose underlying map of simplicial sets is a monomorphism.

•

The fibrant objects are the marked left fibrations with target

.

•

The fibrations between fibrant objects are precisely the marked left fibrations.

Proof.

This is
[
Ngu19a
, Theorem 4.29]
.

2
Extension properties

2.1
.

This section is the technical heart of the article. We prove various extension properties for coCartesian fibrations. Throughout this section we fix a regular cardinal
\kappa
and we call a map of simplicial sets
X\to A

\kappa
-small if the pullback along any map
\Delta^{n}\to A
is
\kappa
-small. We will call a map of marked simplicial sets
\kappa
-small if its underlying map of simplicial sets is
\kappa
-small.

2.2
.

The first extension property concerns extending an equivalence of coCartesian fibrations along a monomorphism. The proof is adapted almost word for word from
[
Shu15
]
and
[
KL21
]
. This extension property will imply univalence of the universal coCartesian fibration. An easy consequence of the extension property is that we can extend coCartesian fibrations along trivial cofibrations of the Joyal model structure which will imply fibrancy of the universe of
\kappa
-small coCartesian fibrations.

3
The universal coCartesian fibration

3.1
.

In this section we construct the universal coCartesian fibration, which classifies small coCartesian fibrations. The construction and proof that the codomain of the universal coCartesian fibration is an
\infty
-category first appeared in the second authors thesis
[
Ngu19b
]
. We give a new, more streamlined proof here.

3.2
.

We continue to fix a regular cardinal
\kappa
. We let
\mathbf{U}(\kappa)
be a universe classifying
\kappa
-small maps of simplicial sets and having the property that for any monomorphism
K\to L
and any diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{U}(\kappa)_{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{U}(\kappa)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}

in which the vertical arrows are
\kappa
-small and the squares are pullbacks, the dotted arrows exists such that the induced square is also a pullback. A construction of

can be found in
[
KL21
]
or
[
Cis19
, Definition 5.2.3]
. An
n
-simplex of

is roughly speaking given by a
\kappa
-small map

and some extra data such that

Definition 3.3

We define

and the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa,\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{U}(\kappa)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{U}(\kappa)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\mathrm{univ}}}

.

The map

-small coCartesian fibrations, i.e. for any coCartesian fibration

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\mathrm{univ}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

Proposition 3.5

The map

Proof.

Follows immediately by definition and the fact that being a coCartesian fibration can be checked on representables.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In particular, the map

is an isofibration
(i.e. a fibration of the Joyal model structure). This means that,

, we can drop the assumption that the codomain of

3.7

We refer to

as the universal coCartesian fibration. The main Theorem of this section is that the simplicial set

is fibrant.

Theorem 3.8

The simplicial set

Proof.

We want to solve the lifting property

3.10

We next characterize coCartesian fibrations whose classifying map factorizes over the maximal

if and only if

is also a Cartesian fibration
and any edge of

Suppose the classifying map factorizes over

. Note that the property of being a coCartesian and cartesian fibration with coCartesian edges precisely the cartesian edges and vice versa is stable under pullback. Also note that a coCartesian fibration over an
\infty
-groupoid satisfies this property by Lemma

. The assertion then follows since

is a pullback of a coCartesian fibration over an

For the converse we first assume that

. By assumption, this is a marked left as well as a marked right fibration. Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

in which

is marked left anodyne and

is a marked left fibration. Since

is a groupoid

is also a marked right fibration. We claim that the induced map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\natural}_{\Delta^{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}

is a coCartesian equivalence. In this case it is enough to show that it is a fiberwise equivalence. But since the inclusion of

into

as well as

is cellular marked left anodyne and

and

are marked right anodyne, it follows from Lemma

that the fibers of

and

over

are equivalent to

respectively in the coCartesian model structure over the point. Since

Now by Theorem

∎

4
The universal morphism classifier

4.1
.

The category of marked simplicial sets is locally cartesian closed. Given a marked simplicial set
A^{+}
and two maps
p\colon X^{+}\to A^{+}
and
q\colon Y^{+}\to A^{+}
, we denote the internal hom by

\pi_{X,Y}\colon\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{+}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})\to A^{+}.

A map

K^{+}\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A^{+}}^{+}(X^{+},Y^{+})

corresponds to the datum of a map
f\colon K^{+}\to A^{+}
and a map

f^{\ast}X^{+}\to f^{\ast}Y^{+}

over
K^{+}
. We denote by
\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}^{+}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})\subset\mathrm{Hom}^{+}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
the subobject spanned by the coCartesian equivalences.

Definition 4.2
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Hom}^{\sharp}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
and
\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}^{\sharp}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
the simplicial sets spanned by the marked edges of
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{+}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
and
\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}^{+}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
respectively.

4.3
.

In other words, a map of simplicial sets
K\to\mathrm{Hom}^{\sharp}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
corresponds by adjunction to a map
K^{\sharp}\to\mathrm{Hom}^{+}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
. This is equivalent to specifying a map
f\colon K^{\sharp}\to A^{+}
and a map
f^{\ast}X^{+}\to f^{\ast}Y^{+}
of marked simplicial sets over
K^{\sharp}
. In case
X
and
Y
are coCartesian fibrations, this amounts to specifying a map
K\to A
and a map
f^{\ast}X\to f^{\ast}Y
over
K
which preserves coCartesian edges. We have a factorization over
\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}^{\sharp}_{A^{+}}(X^{+},Y^{+})
if and only if the the map over
K
is a coCartesian equivalence.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p\colon X^{\natural}\to A^{\sharp}
and

be coCartesian fibrations. Then the map

Proof.

Let

be a Joyal trivial cofibration. The lifting problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{\sharp}_{A^{\sharp}}(X^{\natural},Y^{\natural})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

corresponds to the extension problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{K}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{K}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\natural}_{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\natural}_{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\sharp}}

which admits a solution by Proposition

.
∎

\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})\to\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}
as the universal equivalence classifier. Indeed a map
A\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})
corresponds to specifying a map of coCartesian fibrations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

preserving coCartesian edges. Similarly, a map to
\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})
corresponds to specifying a coCartesian equivalence between coCartesian fibrations.

Corollary 4.10
.

The universal morphism classifier is a bifibration.

5
The Grothendieck construction on homotopy categories

5.1
.

The goal of this section is to define the Grothendieck construction on homotopy categories. To this end consider a map of marked simplicial sets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{+}\times B^{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

Suppose we have a map
p\colon X^{+}\to A^{+}
, then we define the marked simplicial set
\mathrm{Map}_{A^{+}}^{B^{+}}(X^{+},W^{+})
as the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{A^{+}}^{B^{+}}(X^{+},W^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{+}(X^{+},W^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{+}(X^{+},A^{+}\times B^{+})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\ast}}

Here the bottom map is induced by the adjoint of

X^{+}\times B^{+}\xrightarrow{(p,id)}A^{+}\times B^{+}

If

is a marked left fibration, then

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{A^{+}}^{B^{+}}(X^{+},W^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{+}}

is also a marked left fibration by
[
Lur09

[

5.2
.

When

is simply the marked simplicial set of commuting triangles

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

We are mainly interested in the case where

are coCartesian fibrations. In this case we write

\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{+}(X,W):=\mathrm{Map}_{A^{\sharp}}^{\Delta^{0}}(X^{\natural},W^{\natural})

to keep notation simple. Furthermore we denote
\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,W)
the simplicial set spanned by the marked edges. Note that
\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,W)

\infty

5.3
.

Let
A
be a simplicial set and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})\times\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F,G)}

We define
\mathrm{Equiv}^{\sharp}_{A}(X,Y)
to be the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Equiv}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}

5.4
.

Let
B
be a simplicial set and
A
be the nerve of a small category. In
[
Ngu22
, Theorem 5.2]
it is shown that there is a Quillen equivalence

\lambda:\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/B^{\sharp})\leftrightarrow\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}\times B^{\sharp}:\rho

where the slice categories are endowed with the coCartesian model structure and the functor category is endowed with the projective model structure. The left adjoint

W^{+}\to A^{\sharp}\times B^{\sharp}
to the functor

with map to

5.5
.

Consider a coCartesian fibration
W\to\Delta^{1}\times A
. We obtain a map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}^{A^{\sharp}}((\Delta^{1}_{0/})^{\sharp},W^{\natural})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}^{A^{\sharp}}((\Delta^{1}_{1/})^{\sharp},W^{\natural})\cong W^{\natural}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\sharp}}

Since the inclusion
\Delta^{\{0\}}\to(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}
is marked left anodyne, we have a trivial fibration

\mathrm{Map}_{(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}^{A^{\sharp}}((\Delta^{1}_{0/})^{\sharp},W^{\natural})\to W_{0}^{\natural}

(5.1)

over
A^{\sharp}
, see
[
Ngu22
, Proposition 5.7]
, and choosing a section defines a map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{0}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{1}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\sharp}}

classified by a map
W\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})
.
Applying this construction to the coCartesian fibration
classified by the evaluation map

\Delta^{1}\times\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})\to\mathit{Cat}

yields the following lift (with

).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(s,t)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathit{ev}_{0},\mathit{ev}_{1})}

(5.2)

We get a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}

We will see that univalence of the universal coCartesian fibration is equivalent to the upper horizontal map being a categorical equivalence and we will define directed univalence to be a categorical equivalence of the middle horizontal map.

Proposition 5.6
.

The homotopy class of the lift (
5.2
) in the Joyal model structure over
\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}
is independent of the choice of section in (
5.1
.)

Proof.

Any two sections are
(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}
-homotopic over
A^{\sharp}
. Thus we get a commutative triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{0}^{\natural}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{1}^{\natural}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}

(5.3)

This is equivalent to a map

A\times\Delta^{1}\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})

We claim that this is a natural equivalence of functors, i.e. for each object
a
of
A
, the induced map

\Delta^{1}\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})

defines an equivalence in
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})
. Note that this is the case if and only if the induced map

\Delta^{1}\to\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}

defines an equivalence. Such a map corresponds to a pair of coCartesian fibrations over
\Delta^{1}
and by Proposition
3.11
this defines an equivalence if and only if every coCartesian edge is Cartesian and vice versa. Now taking fibers in (
5.3
) this is clearly the case.
∎

5.7
.

Let
A
be a simplicial set. Let
X\to A
be a coCartesian fibration classified by a functor

and
Y\to A
be a coCartesian fibration classified by

. Taking the fiber at

in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,h(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})\times\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})}

yields the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(A,\mathit{Cat})(F,G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Equiv}^{\sharp}_{A}(X,Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})(F,G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,Y)}

5.8
.

Recall that
\mathbf{coCart}(A)
denotes the homotopy category of
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}
endowed with the coCartesian model structure. The following Proposition can be viewed as a Grothendieck construction on homotopy categories. Here, we have to be careful about size: if
\kappa
denotes an inaccessible
cardinal defining
\mathit{Cat}=\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}
, we define
\mathbf{coCart}_{\kappa}(A)

as the full subcategory of
\mathbf{coCart}(A)
spanned by those objects
isomorphic to coCartesian
fibrations
X\to A
with
\kappa
-small fibers.
In the case where
A
is
\kappa
-small, we can identify
\mathbf{coCart}_{\kappa}(A)

with the localization of
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{+}
by the weak equivalence of
the model structure of Theorem
1.11
,
where
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{+}
denotes the full subcategory of
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{+}

spanned by coCartesian
fibrations with
\kappa
-small fibers.

Proposition 5.9
.

There is a canonical functor

ho(\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}))\to\mathbf{coCart}_{\kappa}(A)

sending a functor
A\to\mathit{Cat}
to the coCartesian fibration that it classifies.

Proof.

The Grothendieck construction on morphisms is given by taking
\pi_{0}
of the map

\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})(F,G)\to\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,Y)

We need to show that this defines a functor. A composition in
ho(\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}))
is represented by a coCartesian fibration

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{2}\times A}

and we need to show that the induced triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{0}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{1}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{2}^{\natural}}

commutes up to homotopy in
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}
. But this triangle is homotopic to the triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{(\Delta^{2})^{\sharp}}^{A^{\sharp}}((\Delta^{2}_{0/})^{\sharp},W^{\natural})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{(\Delta^{2})^{\sharp}}^{A^{\sharp}}((\Delta^{2}_{1/})^{\sharp},W^{\natural})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{(\Delta^{2})^{\sharp}}^{A^{\sharp}}((\Delta^{2}_{2/})^{\sharp},W^{\natural})}

which strictly commutes in
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

We will see that univalence is equivalent to the Grothendieck construction inducing an equivalence of maximal groupoids, while directed univalence is equivalent to the Grothendieck construction being an equivalence of categories.

6
Univalence

6.1
.

We show that the universal coCartesian fibration satisfies the univalence axiom. This generalizes previous results that the universal left fibration
[
Cis19
]
and the universal Kan fibration
[
KL21
]
satisfy the univalence axiom.

Proposition 6.2
.

The target map

t:\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})\to\mathit{Cat}

is a trivial fibration.

Proof.

We need to solve lifting problems of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}

which corresponds to completing the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{0}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{1}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{0}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{1}^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

such that the back square is a pullback,
w_{1}
is a coCartesian equivalence and
X_{1}^{\natural}\to L^{\sharp}
is a coCartesian fibration. This is precisely the content of Theorem
2.4
.
∎

Theorem 6.3

(Univalence)
.

The diagonal

\mathit{Cat}\to\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})

is a trivial cofibration of the Joyal model structure.

Proof.

We have the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}

The target map is a trivial fibration, hence by 2-out-of-3 the diagonal is a Joyal equivalence.
∎

6.4
.

We relate univalence to the Grothendieck construction.

Proposition 6.5
.

The map

\mathrm{Fun}(A,h(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat}))\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet}))

is a Joyal equivalence.

Proof.

The target map

h(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})\to\mathit{Cat}

is a trivial fibration by
[
Cis19
, Corollary 3.5.10]
and the target map

\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})\to\mathit{Cat}

is a trivial fibration by Proposition
6.2
. Therefore, since trivial fibrations are stable under exponentiation and by 2-out-of-3, the map

\mathrm{Fun}(A,h(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat}))\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet}))

is a Joyal equivalence.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

For any pair of coCartesian fibrations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

classified by functors
F,G\colon A\to Q
respectively, the induced map

k(A,\mathit{Cat})(F,G)\to\mathrm{Equiv}_{A}(X,Y)

is an equivalence of
\infty
-groupoids.

Proof.

The map is obtained by taking the fiber of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,h(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\underline{\mathrm{Eq}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})\times\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})}

which is a Joyal equivalence between Joyal fibration with target an
\infty
-category. Therefore the induced map on fibers is a Joyal equivalence.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

The Grothendieck construction induces an equivalence on maximal groupoids.

Proof.

It is clear that the Grothendieck construction is essentially surjective. Since

k(A,\mathit{Cat})(F,G)\to\mathrm{Equiv}_{A}(X,Y)

is an equivalence of
\infty
-groupoids, taking
\pi_{0}
implies that it is also fully faithful.
∎

7
Directed univalence

7.1
.

Univalence relates internally defined equivalences of functors
A\to\mathit{Cat}=\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}
with externally defined equivalence of coCartesian fibrations over
A
. A directed version of univalence then should relate internally defined morphisms in
\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})
with externally defined morphisms of coCartesian fibrations over
A
. The key ingredient here is the Grothendieck construction.

Proposition 7.2
.

The Grothendieck construction

ho(\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}))\to\mathbf{coCart}_{\kappa}(A)

is an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

We already know that it is essentially surjective, so we need to show that it is fully faithful. Let us first show that it is full. Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

be a morphism of coCartesian fibrations. By the Quillen equivalence mentioned in paragraph
5.4
there exists a coCartesian fibration

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times A^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

and a natural equivalence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{\natural}_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{\natural}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\natural}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}

in
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}
, where
u
and
v
are coCartesian equivalences. We already know that the Grothendieck construction induces an equivalence of maximal groupoids, Corollary
6.7
, so
u
and
v
are in the image of the Grothendieck construction. Therefore
f
is the image of
v\varphi u^{-1}
.

It remains to show faithful. Note that a homotopy of maps in
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp}
between maps of coCartesian fibrations can be represented by a triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\natural}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\natural}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(p,1)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(q,1)}

Under the Quillen equivalence of paragraph
5.4
there is a coCartesian fibration

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{\natural}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times(\Delta^{1})^{\sharp}\times A^{\sharp}}

which is constant in the first variable and induces the homotopic maps in the second variable.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

For any pair of coCartesian fibrations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

classified by functors
F,G\colon A\to Q
respectively, the induced map

\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})(F,G)\to\mathrm{Map}^{\sharp}_{A}(X,Y)

is an equivalence of
\infty
-groupoids.

Proof.

Let
K
be a simplicial set and denote

F_{K}:=K\times A\to K\xrightarrow{F}\mathit{Cat}

We then have

\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat})(F,G))\simeq\mathrm{Fun}(K\times A,\mathit{Cat})(F_{K},G_{K})

On the other hand we have

\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,Y))\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{K\times A}^{\sharp}(K\times X,K\times Y)

Thus Proposition
7.2
provides an isomorphism

\pi_{0}\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathrm{Map}_{A}^{\sharp}(X,Y))\simeq\pi_{0}\mathrm{Map}_{K\times A}^{\sharp}(K\times X,K\times Y)

functorial in all
K
which implies the assertion.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

The
\infty
-category
\mathit{Cat}
is (
\kappa
-small) cocomplete.

Proof.

Let
I
be a
\kappa
-small
\infty
-category. The canonical map
\pi\colon I\to\Delta^{0}
induces an adjunction

\mathbf{L}\pi_{!}:\mathbf{coCart}_{\kappa}(I\times A)\rightleftarrows\mathbf{coCart}_{\kappa}(A):\mathbf{R}\pi^{*}

By Proposition
7.2
, this induces an adjunction

ho(\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathit{Cat})))\rightleftarrows ho(\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}))

functorial in
A
. This implies by
[
Cis19
, Theorem 6.1.23]
that the constant functor

\mathit{Cat}\to\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathit{Cat})

has a left adjoint, hence
\mathit{Cat}
has colimits of shape
I
.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

The previous Corollary shows that the colimit of a functor
I\to\mathit{Cat}
is computed by inverting the coCartesian edges in the total space of the corresponding coCartesian fibration.

Theorem 7.6

(directed univalence)
.

The map (
5.2
)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}

is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories over
\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}
.

Proof.

By
[
Lur09
, Corollary 2.4.7.11]
the map

\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})\to\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}

is a bifibration and by Corollary
4.10
the map

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})\to\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}

is a bifibration. By
[

, Proposition 2.4.7.7]
it is therefore enough to show that we have a fiberwise equivalence. The map on fibers is precisely the map of Corollary

with
A=\Delta^{0}
.
∎

Remark 7.7
.

The same proof also shows that the universal left fibration is directed univalent.

7.8
.

Suppose

is a coCartesian fibration with small fibers. We have a pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}

induces a map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{B\times B}(E^{0},E^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\times B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}

Thus, for any coCartesian fibration with small fibers a comparison map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{B\times B}(E^{0},E^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\times B}

Definition 7.9
.

A coCartesian fibration
p\colon E\to B
with small fibers is called
directed univalent
if the above comparison map is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},B)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{B\times B}(E^{0},E^{1})\,.

Theorem 7.10
.

Let

be a coCartesian fibration of
\infty
-categories with small fibers. Then
p
is directed univalent if and only if its classifying
functor
B\to\mathit{Cat}
is fully faithful.

Proof.

Since it is a bifibration,
the map
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},B)\rightarrow\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{B\times B}(E^{0},E^{1})

is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories over
B\times B
if and only if it is a fiberwise equivalence. Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathit{Cat})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{B\times B}(E^{0},E^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}(\mathit{Cat}^{0}_{\bullet},\mathit{Cat}^{1}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\times B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}\times\mathit{Cat}}

Recall that the front square is a pullback square. Taking fibers at a point
(a,b)\colon\Delta^{0}\to B\times B
yields the commutative triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B(a,b)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathit{Cat}(a^{*}E,b^{*}E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}^{\sharp}(a^{*}E,b^{*}E)}

Since the universal coCartesian fibration is directed univalent, the right hand vertical map is an equivalence. Thus by 2-out-of-3
p
is directed univalent if and only if its classifying map is fully faithful.
∎

Corollary 7.12

The classifying map of the universal left fibration

8
Straightening/unstraightening as a consequence of directed univalence

8.1
.

Here, we assume that
\kappa
is inaccessible.
The goal of this section is to prove that for any simplicial set
A
, there is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories between the functor category
\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})
and the
\infty
-categorical localization of the category
\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp}
of
\kappa
-small simplicial sets over
A^{\sharp}
at the weak
equivalence from Theorem

.
In particular this implies that the
\infty
-category
\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}
is equivalent to the
\infty
-categorical localization of simplicial sets at the Joyal equivalences.

8.2
.

This section makes essential use of the theory of localizations of
\infty
-categories as developed in
[
Cis19
, Chapter 7]
. Given an
\infty
-category
C
together with a subcategory of weak equivalences, we will denote by
L(C)
the
\infty
-categorical localization of
C
at the weak equivalences. If
C
is an ordinary category with weak equivalences, the 1-categorical localization is obtained as
C\to ho(L(C))
. A key element will be the following Theorem (we will also write

J
for the nerve of any category
J
).

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
C
be a cocomplete model category and
I
be a small category. Then the
induced comparison functor
\mathrm{Fun}(I,C)\to\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(C))

defines an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

L(\mathrm{Fun}(I,C))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(C))

where the left hand side functor category is localized at the levelwise equivalences.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
[
Cis19
, Theorem 7.9.8 and Remark 7.9.7]
.
∎

8.4
.

Let
\lambda\geq\kappa
be any other inaccessible cardinal.
The theorem above implies that for any
\lambda
-small
category
I
and any
\lambda
-small simplicial set
A
, the canonical functor

\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\lambda}/A^{\sharp})\to ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\lambda}/A^{\sharp}))

(8.1)

is the (1-categorical) localization
of the functor category at the level wise coCartesian equivalences.

8.5
.

We give another construction of this localization. Recall from
[
Ngu22
, Theorem 5.2]
that we have for any small category
I
and any simplicial set
A
, a Quillen equivalence

\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/A^{\sharp})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbf{sSet}^{+}/I^{\sharp}\times A^{\sharp}

Taking the homotopy category of the target and restricting to
\lambda
-small simplicial sets over
A^{\sharp}
, we obtain

\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\lambda}/A^{\sharp})\to\mathbf{coCart}_{\lambda}(I\times A)

as the (
1
-categorical) localization of the functor category at the level wise coCartesian equivalences. Now recall from Proposition
7.2
that the Grothendieck construction on homotopy categories provides an equivalence of categories

\mathbf{coCart}_{\lambda}(I\times A)\simeq ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\lambda}))

To summarize, the composition

\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\lambda}/A^{\sharp})\to\mathbf{coCart}_{\lambda}(I\times A)\simeq ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\lambda}))

(8.2)

also defines the (1-categorical) localization of the functor category at the level wise coCartesian equivalences. Thus we have an equivalence of categories

ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\lambda}/A^{\sharp}))\simeq ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\lambda}))

Since
\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}
is a full subcategory of
\mathit{Cat}_{\lambda}
,
the functor category

\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}))
is the full subcategory of

\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\lambda}))
spanned by functors that take values in

\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}
. This implies that the composition

ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp}))\to ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\lambda}/A^{\sharp}))\simeq ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\lambda}))

factors through
ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})
, thus induces
a functor

\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})\to ho\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}))

(8.3)

8.6
.

Our next goal is to construct a comparison functor

L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})

To this end we consider
I=\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp}
and
\lambda>\kappa
so that
I

is
\lambda
-small.
The image of the identity under the map (
8.3
)
provides a functor

{}_{A}\colon\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp}\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})

Lemma 8.7
.

The functor
A
sends coCartesian equivalences to equivalences of the functor category.

Proof.

The functoriality of (
8.2
) in
I
implies that this localization functor is induced by post composition with
A
. In particular, this implies that
A
sends coCartesian equivalences to equivalences of the functor category.
∎

8.8
.

Let
A
be a
\kappa
-small simplicial set.
We get an induced functor

L{}_{A}\colon L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})

so that, for any
\kappa
-small category
I
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({}_{A})_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L{}_{A})_{\ast}}

commutes. Taking homotopy categories, the vertical arrow is the localization (
8.1
), while the horizontal arrow is the localization (
8.2
) for
\lambda=\kappa

Lemma 8.9
.

The functor

(L{}_{A})_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp}))\to\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}))

induces an equivalence on homotopy categories.

8.10
.

In particular we have that

L{}_{A}\colon L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})

induces an equivalence of homotopy categories. Our next goal is to show that it is actually an equivalence of
\infty
-categories. To this end, first note
that
L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})
is a cocomplete
\infty
-category since
it is the localization of a model category,
see for example
[
BHH17
, Theorem 2.5.9]

or
[
Cis19
, Proposition 7.7.4]
. On the other hand, the functor category
\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})
is cocomplete by Corollary
7.4
.

Lemma 8.11
.

The functor
L{}_{A}
preserves
\kappa
-small colimits.

Proof.

First note that by
[
Cis19
, Proposition 7.3.23]
,
it suffices to show that
L{}_{A}
preserves colimits indexed by (nerves of)

\kappa
-small categories.
Thus, let
I
be a
\kappa
-small category. We have the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(I,L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(I,\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L{}_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L{}_{A})_{\ast}}

(8.4)

where the vertical arrows are given by taking constant diagrams.
Since
L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})
and
\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})
are cocomplete, the vertical arrows admit left adjoints. The functor
L{}_{A}
preserves colimits if and only if the induced square after taking left adjoints commutes (up to equivalence). In other words, if and only if the square (
8.4
) satisfies the Beck-Chevalley condition. This can be checked by taking homotopy categories. By Lemma
8.9
, the horizontal arrows become equivalences after taking homotopy categories, so that the induced square does indeed satisfy the Beck-Chevalley condition, hence
L{}_{A}
preserves colimits.
∎

8.12
.

This already suffices to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 8.13
.

The map
L{}_{A}
induces an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

L(\mathbf{sSet}^{+}_{\kappa}/A^{\sharp})\cong\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathit{Cat}_{\kappa})\,.

By Lemma

is a colimit preserving functor between cocomplete

Therefore, by virtue of

[

\infty
-categories.
∎

Remark 8.17
.

The title of this section claims straightening as a consequence
of directed univalence, but we never explicitly make the connection.
Instead we use the Grothendieck construction, i.e. the fact that
we have a functorial equivalence on homotopy categories between
the cocartesian model structure and the functor category (Proposition
7.2
). In the previous section,
we used this to prove directed univalence (Theorem
7.6
),
but it is an easy exercise to
see that Proposition
7.2
follows straight away from Theorem
7.6
.
In other words, directed univalence is equivalent to Proposition
7.2
. Since
Theorem
7.6
is the poor man version of the Straightening/Unstraightening correspondence
(formulated as Theorem
8.13
or Theorem
8.16
), all this means that
directed univalence expresses nothing else than Straightening/Unstraightening.

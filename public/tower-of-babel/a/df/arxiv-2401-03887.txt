Global entropy solutions to a degenerate parabolic-parabolic chemotaxis system for flux-limited dispersal
Anna Zhigun

Abstract
Existence of global finite-time bounded entropy solutions to a parabolic-parabolic system proposed in [ 16 ] is established in bounded domains under no-flux boundary conditions for nonnegative bounded initial data. This modification of the classical Keller-Segel model features degenerate diffusion and chemotaxis that are both subject to flux-saturation. The approach is based on Schauder’s fixed point theorem and calculus of functions of bounded variation. Keywords : flux-limited diffusion; flux-limited chemotaxis; entropy solutions. Introduction

In this paper we study the initial-boundary value problem (IBVP) for the chemotaxis system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c=\nabla\cdot\left(D_{c}\frac{c\nabla c}{\sqrt{c^{2}+\frac{D_{c}^{2}}{C^{2}}|\nabla c|^{2}}}-\chi c\frac{\nabla v}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla v|^{2}}}\right)+f_{c}(c,v),

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=D_{v}\Delta v+f_{v}(c,v)

(1.1b)

equipped with no-flux boundary conditions. Equations

1.1

[

and serve to overcome several issues arising in connection with the classical Keller-Segel model for chemotaxis
[
33
,
34

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c=\nabla\cdot(D_{c}\nabla c-\chi c\nabla v),

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=D_{v}\Delta v-\gamma v+\alpha c.

(1.2b)

Both systems describe spatio-temporal evolution of cell density,
c
and concentration of chemoattractant,
v
. Functions

and

and constants

are assumed given.
Each of the

and

can be rewritten in the form of a continuity equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c=-\nabla\cdot(c(V_{d}+V_{c})),

where

are velocities due to diffusion and chemotaxis, respectively.
Whereas for

\displaystyle V_{d}=-D_{C}\frac{\nabla c}{c},\qquad V_{c}=\chi\nabla v

need not be finite everywhere, for
Eq.

1.1a
we have

\displaystyle V_{d}=-D_{c}\frac{\nabla c}{\sqrt{c^{2}+\frac{D_{c}^{2}}{C^{2}}|\nabla c|^{2}}},\qquad V_{c}=\chi\frac{\nabla v}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla v|^{2}}},

so that

and

are a priori bounded by

and

Thus, unlike

A justified expectation is that for such choices as in

15

38

2.4
Interpolation and discretisation of vector-valued functions

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
set

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\left\{\frac{k}{n}:\ k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

Let
X
be some Banach space. We use two standard ways of interpolating a function
u:\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap[0,T]\to X
: piecewise constant

\displaystyle\underline{\Pi}_{n}{u}(t):=u\left(\frac{k-1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in }\left(\frac{k-1}{n},\frac{k}{n}\right)\qquad\text{ for }k\in\{1,2,\dots,nT\},

\displaystyle{\overline{\Pi}_{n}}{u}(t):=u\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in }\left(\frac{k-1}{n},\frac{k}{n}\right)\qquad\text{ for }k\in\{1,2,\dots,nT\},

and piecewise linear

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}{u}(t):=

\displaystyle\frac{u\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)-u\left(\frac{k-1}{n}\right)}{\frac{1}{n}}\left(t-\frac{k}{n}\right)+u\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{n}-t}{\frac{1}{n}}u\left(\frac{k-1}{n}\right)+\frac{t-\frac{k-1}{n}}{\frac{1}{n}}u\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{n}-t}{\frac{1}{n}}\underline{\Pi}_{n}{u}(t)+\frac{t-\frac{k-1}{n}}{\frac{1}{n}}{\overline{\Pi}_{n}}{u}(t)\qquad\text{in }\left(\frac{k-1}{n},\frac{k}{n}\right)\qquad\text{ for }k\in\{1,2,\dots,nT\}.

(2.5)

For
\widetilde{u}\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,T];X)
, its
n
-th discretisation via Steklov averages is given by

\displaystyle P_{n}\widetilde{u}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right):=n\int_{\frac{k-1}{n}}^{\frac{k}{n}}\widetilde{u}(t)\,dt\qquad\text{for }k\in\{1,2,\dots,nT\}.

Further, we set

\displaystyle P_{n}\widetilde{u}(0):=P_{n}\widetilde{u}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Clearly, the introduced interpolation and discretisation operators are linear and preserve non-negativity: for a functional space
X

\displaystyle u\geq 0\text{ in }\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap[0,T]

\displaystyle\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\underline{\Pi}_{n}u,{\overline{\Pi}_{n}}u,\Lambda_{n}u\geq 0\text{ in }[0,T],

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}\geq 0\text{ in }[0,T]

\displaystyle\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad P_{n}\widetilde{u}\geq 0\text{ in }\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap[0,T].

Several other useful properties of these operators are provided in

\displaystyle\left(\left\|v^{(1)}-v^{(2)}\right\|_{C([0,1];C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}))}+\left\|v^{(1)}-v^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{1}((0,1);W^{2,1}(\Omega))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\|v^{(1)}-v^{(2)}\right\|_{W^{1,p_{0}}((0,T);L^{p_{0}}(\Omega))}+\left\|v^{(1)}-v^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}((0,T);W^{2,p_{0}}(\Omega))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{c}^{(1)}-\bar{c}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}((0,1);L^{p_{0}}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{c}^{(1)}-\bar{c}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1);L^{\infty}(\Omega))}^{1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left\|\bar{c}^{(1)}-\bar{c}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{1}((0,1);L^{1}(\Omega))}^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{c}^{(1)}-\bar{c}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{1}((0,1);L^{1}(\Omega))}^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

This means that
{\cal S}
is a (Hölder) continuous operator in
{\cal X}
.

∎

Lemma A.1
.

Let

,

(A.1a)

Lemma A.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
T\in\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}
, and
C\in(-\infty,n)
. Define

\displaystyle\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}:\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}_{0}\to(0,\infty),\qquad\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}(t):=\left(\left(1-\frac{C}{n}\right)^{-n}\right)^{t}.

Let
u,v:\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap[0,T]\to[0,\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Lambda_{n}u\leq C\overline{\Pi}_{n}u+\overline{\Pi}_{n}v\qquad\text{in }[0,T]\backslash\left(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{N}_{0}\right)

(A.14)

implies

\displaystyle\|\overline{\Pi}_{n}\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}^{-1}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq u(0)+\|\underline{\Pi}_{n}\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}^{-1}\overline{\Pi}_{n}v\|_{L^{1}((0,T))}.

(A.15)

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle a_{n}:=u\left(\frac{k}{n}\right),\quad g_{n}:=v\left(\frac{k}{n}\right),\quad\Delta t:=\frac{1}{n}.

In this notation, inequality
Eq.

A.14
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n+1}-a_{n}}{\frac{1}{n}}\leq Ca_{n+1}+g_{n+1}\qquad\text{for }n\in\{0,\cdots,nT-1\}.

Since
1-C/n>0
, a discrete version of Gronwall’s lemma
[
31
, Proposition 3.1]
can be applied in this setting, followed by division throughout by
\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}(k/n)
. The result is

\displaystyle\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}^{-1}u\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\leq

\displaystyle u(0)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}^{-1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)v\left(\frac{j+1}{n}\right)\qquad\text{for }k\in\{1,\dots,nT\}.

(A.16)

Since
v
is non-negative,
Eq.

A.16
implies

\displaystyle\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}^{-1}u\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\leq

\displaystyle u(0)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{nT-1}\operatorname{exp}_{n,C}^{-1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)v\left(\frac{j+1}{n}\right)\qquad\text{for }k\in\{1,\dots,nT\}.

(A.17)

Re-writing
Eq.

A.17
in terms of continuous-time interpolations gives
Eq.

A.15
.

∎

Abstract
. We study the entropy and Lyapunov exponents of invariant measures μ \mu for smooth surface diffeomorphisms f f , as functions of ( f , μ ) (f,\mu) . The main result is an inequality relating the discontinuities of these functions. One consequence is that for a C ∞ C^{\infty} surface diffeomorphisms, on any set of ergodic measures with entropy bounded away from zero, continuity of the entropy implies continuity of the exponents. Another consequence is the upper semi-continuity of the Hausdorff dimension on the set of ergodic invariant measures with entropy bounded away from zero. We also obtain a new criterion for the existence of SRB measures with positive entropy.

Introduction

Entropy and Lyapunov exponents play a major role in the study of differentiable dynamical
systems, and their dependence on the measure and the map is of great interest.
This dependence is sometimes continuous, but not always (for entropy, see

[
33
,
35
,
12
,
9
,
17
,

]
,
and for Lyapunov exponents, see
[

,

,
6
,

,

While there are many works relating the values of the entropy to the values of the Lyapunov exponents
[
43
,
39
,
31
,
27
]
,
the relation between the (dis)continuity of these objects as functions of the measure and the diffeomorphism has not yet been studied. The purpose of this work is to fill this gap, in the smooth two-dimensional case.
For instance, we show:

Theorem
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a compact surface without boundary. Let

\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots
be ergodic measures for
f
, which converge in the weak-
*
topology
to an ergodic measure
\mu
with positive entropy.

If the entropy of
\nu_{k}
converges to the entropy of
\mu
,
then the Lyapunov exponents of
\nu_{k}
converge to the Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
.

This has strong dynamical consequences, some of which we will discuss here, and some of which we will discuss in a companion paper.
For example, we have the following application to the problem of the existence of SRB measures.
Let
\delta^{u}(\mu)
denote the unstable dimension of
\mu
in the sense of Ledrappier and Young (see Section
1.5
).

Corollary
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a compact surface without boundary.
If there exist ergodic invariant probability measures
\nu_{k}
, with entropy uniformly bounded away from
0
, and such that

\delta^{u}(\nu_{k})\to 1
, then
f
admits an ergodic SRB measure with positive entropy.

For other consequences, including the upper semi-continuity of
the unstable dimension and of the Hausdorff dimension of ergodic measures with positive entropy, see Section
1.5
.

These results follow from inequalities between the multiplicative size of the defects in continuity of the entropy and the top Lyapunov exponent. These inequalities, which are the main results of this work, are described in detail in the next section.

1.
Main results

Throughout this paper,
M
is a two-dimensional compact
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold without boundary. Let
\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
denotes the class of
C^{r}
diffeomorphisms on
M
(see §
3.1
).

Suppose
\mu
is an
f
-invariant probability measure for some
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
. The
Kolmogorov-Sinaĭ entropy
of
\mu
will be denoted by

h(f,\mu)
.
Almost every

has two well-defined Lyapunov exponents
\lambda^{-}(f,x)\leq\lambda^{+}(f,x)
. The
Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
are defined by

\lambda^{-}(f,\mu):=\lambda^{-}_{\mu}:=\int\lambda^{-}(f,x)d\mu\ ,\ \lambda^{+}(f,\mu):=\lambda^{+}_{\mu}:=\int\lambda^{+}(f,x)d\mu.

We are interested in the regularity of
(f,\mu)\mapsto h(f,\mu)
and
(f,\mu)\mapsto\lambda^{\pm}(f,\mu)
.

In the
C^{\infty}
case, these functions are semi-continuous. Specifically,
suppose
f_{k},f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{\infty}(M)
,
\nu_{k}
are ergodic
f_{k}
-invariant measures,
f_{k}\to f
in
C^{\infty}
and
\nu_{k}\to\mu
weak-
*
. Then

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}h(f_{k},\nu_{k}

\displaystyle)\;\;\leq\;h(f,\mu),

(1)

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})\leq\lambda^{+}(f,\mu),

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{-}(f_{k},\nu_{k})\geq\lambda^{-}(f,\mu).

(See Section
1.6
for the history of these results.)

By (
1
) and Ruelle’s inequality, if
\limsup_{k}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})>0
, then
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)\geq h(f,\mu)\geq\limsup_{k}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})>0
, and

\frac{\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)}\ ,\ \frac{\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{h(f,\mu)}\in(0,1].

We call these quantities the
discontinuity ratios
, and think of them as measures for the difference between the two sides of the inequalities in (
1
).

We will provide inequalities relating the discontinuity ratio of the entropy to the discontinuity ratio of
\lambda^{+}
. (It is enough to consider
\lambda^{+}
, because
\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)=-\lambda^{+}(f^{-1},\mu)
.)

The ergodic

Our results are simplest and strongest when the maps are

–

If

1.4.6.
Entropy upper semi-continuity in the

It is well-known that for

1.5.
Applications to dimension theory and to SRB measures

The Hausdorff dimension of a Borel measure

\mu

Corollary 1.4
.

For every
k\geq 1
, let
f_{k}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{\infty}(M)
and
let
\nu_{k}
be an
f_{k}
-ergodic invariant measure. Suppose

f_{k}\to f

topology,
\nu_{k}\to\mu

*

\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})>0

is ergodic, then

\displaystyle{\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{HD}(\nu_{k})\leq\mathrm{HD}(\mu).}

Proof.

One can always take a subsequence such that

\lim_{k}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})>0
,
\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})>0
and
\lim_{k}\lambda^{-}(f_{k},\nu_{k})<0
exist,
and such that
\mathrm{HD}(\nu_{k})
converges to the limsup of the initial sequence.
In
[

, Young gives the following formula for the Hausdorff dimension:

\mathrm{HD}(\mu)=h(f,\mu)\left(1/\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)+1/|\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)|\right).

(6)

Writing

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{h(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})}=\left(\frac{h(f,\mu)}{\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)}\right)\biggl(\frac{\lim_{k}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{h(f,\mu)}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)}\biggr)^{-1}

\lim_{k}\frac{h(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})}\leq\frac{h(f,\mu)}{\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)}
. Working with

and

we obtain in a similar way that
\lim_{k}\frac{h(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{|\lambda^{-}(f,\nu_{k})|}\leq\frac{h(f,\mu)}{|\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)|}
.
∎

Suppose

. An
f
invariant probability measure is called a
Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen (SRB) measure

\lambda^{+}(f,x)>0

\mu

\mu

[
31

[

]

Suppose
\lambda^{+}(f,x)>0

\mu

\mu

By Ruelle’s inequality,

[

Next suppose

Corollary 1.7
.

Let

(i)

To see (ii)

f

\nu_{k}
with

and
P^{u}(f,\nu_{k})\to 0

(\nu_{k_{i}})_{i\geq 1}

\lambda^{+}:=\lim\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k_{i}})

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k_{i}})=\beta\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{1})\text{, and }\lim_{i\to\infty}h(f,\nu_{k_{i}})\leq\beta h(f,\mu_{1}).

It follows that
0=\lim_{i\to\infty}P^{u}(f,\nu_{k_{i}})\leq\beta P^{u}(f,\mu_{1})\leq 0
, where the last inequality is Ruelle’s inequality for
\mu_{1}
.
Since
\beta\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{1})=\lim\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k_{i}})=\lambda^{+}\neq 0
, it must be the case that
\beta\neq 0
, and
P^{u}(f,\mu_{1})=0
. If
\mu_{1}=\int\mu_{\xi}^{\prime}d\xi
is the ergodic decomposition of
\mu_{1}
, then
\int P^{u}(f,\mu_{\xi}^{\prime})d\xi=P^{u}(f,\mu_{1})=0
. By Ruelle’s inequality, the integrand is non-positive, so
P^{u}(f,\mu^{\prime}_{\xi})=0
for
\mu_{1}
-a.e. ergodic component. At the same time,
h(f,\mu_{1})\geq\beta^{-1}\lim h(f_{k},\nu_{k})>h^{\prime}
, so some of these ergodic components must have entropy
\geq h^{\prime}
. Thus

has ergodic components with entropy bigger than

h
, and (i) is proved.
∎

Notice that it is essential in this proof to be able to deal with non-ergodic limits, since we have no control of
\lim\nu_{k}
.

1.6.
Related works

In this paper we relate the continuity properties of the entropy to those of the Lyapunov exponents. The continuity of these objects has been studied separately before in several works, which we now recall.

Entropy.

In general, the entropy map
(f,\mu)\mapsto h(f,\mu)
is not lower semi-continuous, even in the uniformly hyperbolic case. For example, it is easy to construct sequences of atomic measures (with zero entropy) on a basic set, which converge to limits with positive entropy.

However, for
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms on compact manifolds, the entropy map is upper semi-continuous: This is due to Newhouse
[
35
]
.
For
C^{r}
diffeomorphisms with finite
r
, even upper semi-continuity may fail (for examples in dimension four see
[
33
]
, and for examples in dimension two see
[
14
]
). However,
the (additive) defect in semi-continuity:

D(f,\mu):=\limsup_{(g,\nu)\to(f,\mu)}h(g,\nu)-h(f,\mu)

can be bounded from above
by
\min(\lambda(f),\lambda(f^{-1}))/r
,
using Yomdin theory
[
35
,
12
,
10
,
11
]
.
A subject of more recent interest is the loss of semi-continuity due to non-compactness. This has been studied for countable Markov shifts
[
22
,
23
]
, geodesic flows on non-compact homogeneous spaces
[
18
,
25
]
, and geodesic flows on non-compact manifolds with negative sectional curvatures
[
21
,
42
]
.

Lyapunov exponents.

The top Lyapunov exponent map
(f,\mu)\mapsto\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)
varies continuously
for uniformly hyperbolic systems on surfaces. It even depends analytically on the diffeomorphism
f

[
44
]
. Moreover if
\mu_{\max}
is the unique measure of maximal entropy of a mixing Anosov surface diffeomorphism, then
[
24
]
(see also
[
41
,
45
]
) implies that
|\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\max})-\lambda^{+}(\nu)|\leq c\sqrt{|h(f,\mu_{\max})-h(f,\nu)|}
, where
c
only depends on
f
.

In the non-uniformly hyperbolic case, the situation is different. For example,
[
6
]
proves that among conservative systems
the Lyapunov exponents of the volume measure are discontinuous when the diffeomorphism varies in the
C^{1}
-topology, unless they vanish.

We are not aware of other general results on
the continuity of the Lyapunov exponents
for general non-uniformly hyperbolic surface diffeomorphisms.

By contrast, much is known on the continuity of Lyapunov exponents of random products of independent identically distributed
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
matrices, as functions of the underlying Bernoulli process, see
[
20
,
7
]
.
More general Hölder continuous matrix cocycles with holonomies are considered in
[
2
]
,
and a higher-dimensional extension has been announced in
[
47
]
.

Dimension.

L.-S. Young gave the famous formula (
6
) for the dimension of hyperbolic invariant measures in
[
49
]
in terms of the entropy and the Lyapunov exponents of the measure. For further dimension theoretic properties of hyperbolic invariant measures, see
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
.
The continuity of the dimension of invariant sets and measures for hyperbolic systems have been considered in numerous works, for instance
[
37
]
proves that
basic sets on surface have a Hausdorff dimension which varies continuously with the diffeomorphism,

[
5
]
proves that the supremum of the Hausdorff dimensions of ergodic measures on such a basic set
is attained by a measure of maximal dimension
and
[
3
]
discusses some non-uniformly hyperbolic cases.

2.
A heuristic overview of the proof

All our results follow from Theorem
C
, and the remainder of the paper is dedicated to the proof of this theorem.
Here we give a heuristic overview of the proof, in the special case when
f_{1}=f_{2}=\cdots=f

is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism.

2.1.
The origin of the discontinuities in
\lambda^{+}

As Furstenberg discovered, the Lyapunov exponents are easier to study in terms of the
projective dynamics

\widehat{f}(x,E)=(f(x),Df_{x}(E))
on the projective tangent bundle

\displaystyle\widehat{M}:=\{(x,E):x\in M,\ E\subset T_{x}M\text{ is a one-dimensional linear space}\}.

Indeed by Ledrappier’s work, a Lyapunov exponent of an
f
-ergodic measure
\mu
is simply the integral of the continuous function

\varphi(x,E):=\log\|Df_{x}|_{E}\|,

with respect to the lift of
\mu
to the bundle of the associated Oseledets spaces.

Suppose
\nu_{k}
are ergodic measures with positive entropy such that
\nu_{k}\to\mu
weak
∗
, and suppose for the moment that
\mu
is ergodic and with positive entropy.
Since
\dim(M)=2
,
\nu_{k}
have two simple Lyapunov exponents, and there are exactly two ergodic lifts
\widehat{\nu}^{+}_{k}
and
\widehat{\nu}^{-}_{k}
, one carried by the bundle
\mathcal{E}^{u}
of unstable Oseledets spaces, and the other carried by the bundle
\mathcal{E}^{s}
of the stable Oseledets spaces.
(The third bundle
\mathcal{E}^{0}
associated to the zero exponent has measure zero for all lifts of
\nu_{k}
.)
Hence,

\lambda^{\pm}(f,\nu_{k})=\int\varphi d\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{\pm}.

Suppose
\widehat{\nu}^{+}_{k}
converge weak-star on
\widehat{M}
to an
\widehat{f}
-invariant probability
measure
\widehat{\mu}
(this is true for a subsequence). Since
\varphi:\widehat{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\nu}_{k}(\varphi)=\widehat{\mu}(\varphi).

The limiting measure
\widehat{\mu}
is a lift of
\mu
, but this does not have to be the lift of
\mu
to
\mathcal{E}^{u}
,
\widehat{\mu}^{+}
. If
\widehat{\mu}(\varphi)\neq\widehat{\mu}^{+}(\varphi)
, then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})\neq\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)
.

It is certainly possible that
\widehat{\mu}\neq\widehat{\mu}^{+}
: The Oseledets bundle
\mathcal{E}^{u}
carrying the lifts
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}
is not necessarily bounded away from
\mathcal{E}^{s}
, and some mass
0\leq\rho\leq 1
on
\mathcal{E}^{u}
can escape to
\mathcal{E}^{s}
.

Escape of mass to
\mathcal{E}^{s}
is reflected in long stretches of time when
\nu_{k}
-typical orbits do not experience the exponential growth of
\mathcal{E}^{u}
-directions predicted by
\lambda^{+}
. Instead, they see, temporarily, exponential decay at rate
\lambda^{-}
, cancelling some of the previous growth.
If
\widehat{\mu}^{+},\widehat{\mu}^{-}
denote the two ergodic lifts. we must have

\widehat{\mu}=(1-\rho)\widehat{\mu}^{+}+\rho\widehat{\mu}^{-}
and thus,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})=(1-\rho)\widehat{\mu}^{+}(\varphi)+\rho\widehat{\mu}^{-}(\varphi)=\lambda^{+}_{\mu}-\rho(\lambda^{+}_{\mu}-\lambda^{-}_{\mu}).

In the language of Theorem
C
(and since
\mu=\mu_{1}
by ergodicity), the discontinuity ratio
\beta
is:

\beta:=\frac{\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)}=1-\rho(1-\lambda^{-}_{\mu}/\lambda^{+}_{\mu}).

(A different description of
\beta
will be given below.) So if
\mu
is ergodic, then
\beta
is a function of
\rho
, whence of the amount of mass which escapes to
\mathcal{E}^{s}
.

In the case where
\mu
is not ergodic, the different ergodic components of
\mu
have to be considered, and some of them may have zero Lyapunov exponents. The way in which
\nu_{k}
-typical orbits approximate those ergodic components determine the possible cancellations. So if
\mu
is not ergodic, then
\beta
may depend on the entire sequence
(\nu_{k})
, not just on its limit
\mu
.

2.2.
Neutral blocks, the decomposition of
\mu
, and the parameter
\beta

Recall the measurable
\widehat{f}
-invariant decomposition

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{E}^{s}\cup\mathcal{E}^{u}\cup\mathcal{E}^{0}
defined by the Oseledets theorem according to the sign of the limit
\tfrac{1}{n}\log\|Df^{n}_{x}|_{E}\|
. It has full measure with respect to any
\widehat{f}
-invariant measure.
To get quantitative estimates, we select compact subsets
K^{*}\subset\mathcal{E}^{*}
, for
*\in\{s,u,0\}
,
from which the contraction, expansion, or “central” behavior of the sequence
\|Df^{n}_{x}|_{E}\|
are uniformly controlled,
and such that the
\widehat{\mu}
-measure of
K:=K^{s}\cup K^{u}\cup K^{0}
is close to
1
. Since each
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is invariant, we can choose these compact sets to be nearly invariant: Points in a very small neighborhood stay close for a long time.

Hence, if
\widehat{x}_{0}
is a
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}
-typical point for some very large
k
, its orbit under
\widehat{f}
spends nearly all its time close to
K
and
every visit in a small neighborhood of
K^{s}\cup K^{0}
is the beginning of a long period of uniform contraction (or weak expansion/contraction). One expects no entropy creation not only during this period, but also during the “recovery period” which follows, i.e., until the expansion predicted by the Lyapunov exponent of
\nu_{k}
cancels this period of contraction (or weak expansion/contraction).

We select such long time intervals along the orbit of
\widehat{x}_{0}
in the following greedy way. Fixing
\alpha>0
small and
L
large, an
(\alpha,L)
–neutral block
is a maximal interval of integers

(n_{0},\dots,n_{0}+\ell)
such that
\ell\geq L
and

\|Df^{n}_{f^{n_{0}}(x_{0})}|_{E^{u}}\|\leq\exp(\alpha(n-n_{0}))\text{ for all }0<n\leq\ell.

We will check that indeed, there is very little if any entropy creation during neutral blocks.

Our estimates will be in terms of the distribution of these long neutral blocks.
Let
\widehat{x}_{k}=(x_{k},E^{u}(x_{k}))
be
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}
-generic points and let
\mathfrak{N}_{\alpha,L}(\widehat{x}_{k})
denote the union of all
(\alpha,L)
-neutral blocks of the orbit of
\widehat{x}_{k}
.
In Section
6
we show that it is possible to choose a subsequence
k_{i}\to\infty
so that following limits
make sense weak-
*
on
\widehat{M}
for
(\widehat{\nu}^{+}_{k_{1}}\times\widehat{\nu}^{+}_{k_{2}}\times\cdots)
–a.e.
(\widehat{x}_{k_{1}},\widehat{x}_{k_{2}},\ldots)
:

\displaystyle\widehat{m}_{0}:=\underset{L\to\infty}{\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}}\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\in[0,N)\cap\mathfrak{N}_{\alpha,L}(\widehat{x}_{k})}\delta_{\widehat{f}^{j}(\widehat{x}_{k_{i}})}\right)

\displaystyle\widehat{m}_{1}:=\underset{L\to\infty}{\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}}\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\in[0,N)\setminus\mathfrak{N}_{\alpha,L}(\widehat{x}_{k})}\delta_{\widehat{f}^{j}(x_{k_{i}})}\right)\,.

Notice that the sum of the two limits in the brackets is a.s.
\widehat{\nu}_{k_{i}}^{+}
, because this is the limit of the empirical measure of
\widehat{x}_{k_{i}}
, and
\widehat{x}_{k_{i}}
are all a.s.
\widehat{\nu}_{k_{i}}^{+}
–generic. So

\widehat{m}_{0}+\widehat{m}_{1}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}=\widehat{\mu}.

The measures
\widehat{m}_{0},\widehat{m}_{1}
are
\widehat{f}
-invariant. We will see that
\int\varphi d\widehat{m}_{0}=0
, and that
\widehat{m}_{1}
is carried by
\mathcal{E}^{u}
. The decomposition

\mu=\beta\mu_{1}+(1-\beta)\mu_{0}
in Theorem
C
is defined by

\beta:=1-\widehat{\mu}_{0}(\widehat{M}),\;(1-\beta)\mu_{0}:=\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\mu}_{0}),\text{ and }\beta\mu_{1}:=\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\mu}-\widehat{\mu}_{0}),

where
\widehat{\pi}:\widehat{M}\to M
is the natural projection.
Note that
\beta
is indeed the discontinuity ratio
\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})/\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{1})
and the quantity
1-\beta
coincides with the fraction of the time spent in maximal neutral blocks.
The measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
and
\beta
depend not just on
\widehat{\mu}
, but also on the way the measures
\widehat{\nu}^{+}_{k}
accumulate on
\widehat{\mu}
.

2.3.
Upper bound on the entropy

To complete the proof of the theorem it remains to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}h(f,\nu_{k})\leq\beta h(f,\mu_{1})
. This is the heart of the proof, and where most of the difficulties lie. We use Ledrappier-Young Theory and Yomdin Theory.

•

Ledrappier-Young theory
bounds
h(f,\nu_{k})
by the exponential rate of growth of the minimal number of
(n,\epsilon)
-balls needed to cover a definite fraction of a local unstable manifold
W^{u}_{loc}(x_{k})
,
where
x_{k}
is a fixed
\nu_{k}
-typical point and the scale
\varepsilon
tends to zero. The “fraction” is measured using the conditional measure
\nu^{u}_{x_{k}}
of
\nu_{k}
on
W^{u}_{loc}(x_{k})
. In particular, it suffices to follow points
x\in W^{u}_{\operatorname{loc}}(x_{k})
with
T_{x}W^{u}_{\operatorname{loc}}(x_{k})=\mathcal{E}^{u}(x)
.

•

Yomdin theory
provides tools for controlling the number of
(n,\varepsilon)
-balls needed for such covers, for
C^{r}
maps. Instead of working with
(n,\varepsilon)
-balls, one works with parametrized pieces of unstable manifolds which lie inside
(n,\varepsilon)
-balls and which have uniformly bounded
C^{r}
size, and Yomdin Theory allows to bound the number of such pieces. Here the regularity assumptions on
f
come into play. The expression
\frac{\lambda(f)+\lambda(f^{-1})}{r-1}
in (
3
) is due to Yomdin theory (see section
2.4
).

Let us sketch our argument for the upper bound on the entropy using the neutral blocks.
Since the unstable lift of
\nu_{x_{k}}^{u}
-almost every point is
\widehat{\nu}^{+}_{k}
-typical, neutral blocks represent roughly a fraction
1-\beta
of their time.
During a neutral block, typical points on a small piece of
f^{n}(W^{u}_{loc}(x_{k}))
do not separate much, therefore this piece remains small (or can be kept small by a subdivision into a small exponential number of pieces).
For the rest of the time, these subcurves follow the ergodic components of
\mu_{1}
, hence they experience entropic separation at an exponential rate given by
h(f,\mu_{1})
. Since the time outside neutral blocks is a proportion
\beta
of the total time, this leads to the bound

h(f,\nu_{k})\leq\beta h(f,\mu_{1}).

This argument explains the link between the entropy bound and the semicontinuity defect of the Lyapunov exponents.

This sketch glosses over several difficulties. We will only comment on the main issue: How to use non-expansion of the linearization
Df
at
(x_{k},E^{u}(x_{k}))
during a neutral block, to infer non-expansion of the map
f
itself on a small piece of
W^{u}_{loc}(x_{k})
during this neutral block. The difficulty is in controlling
Df
on
(x^{\prime}_{k},E^{u}(x^{\prime}_{k}))
for
x_{k}^{\prime}
close to
x_{k}
.

2.4.
Control of the expansion during neutral blocks

This is one of the most delicate points in the proof. To deduce the non-expansion of the small piece of the unstable manifold containing this point, we need to know that not only the diameter of this curve is small but that its tangent is almost constant too. This forces us to work with pieces of
W^{u}_{\operatorname{loc}}(x_{k})
whose lifts to
\widehat{M}
are also small: The size in the fiber of
\widehat{M}
measures the variability of the tangent directions.

How small is small enough? To use information on
Df
to control what happens on
W^{u}_{loc}(x_{k})
, we need the fluctuations of the tangent direction along any piece to be smaller than some
\widehat{\varepsilon}>0
, determined (mostly) by the modulus of continuity of
Df
. Using the uniform continuity of the measurable unstable bundle on a set of large measure, we find an
\varepsilon>0
such that if the diameter of the projection to
M
is less than
\varepsilon
, then the fluctuation of the tangent is smaller than
\widehat{\varepsilon}
.

The price we pay for this solution is that we need to work with different scales in
M
and along the fibers of the bundle
\widehat{M}\to M
. This leads us to introduce
fibered
(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-balls
, and to work with Yomdin theory for
\widehat{f}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
. When dealing with diffeomorphisms of finite regularity, there is an additional price to pay: If
f
is
C^{r}
, then
\widehat{f}
is only
C^{r-1}
, and this accounts for the extra term from Yomdin theory

\frac{\widehat{\lambda}(\widehat{f})}{r-1}\leq\frac{\lambda(f)+\lambda(f^{-1})}{r-1}\quad\text{ in \eqref{e.eqC2}}.

3.
Tangent dynamics and the semi-continuity of Lyapunov exponents

Let
M
be a smooth compact Riemannian surface without boundary.

3.1.
Review of the
C^{r}
size of maps

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Given
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we say that a map
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
C^{k}
if
for all
\omega\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{n}
such that
|\omega|:=\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n}=k
,
the partial derivative

\partial^{\omega}F:=\frac{\partial^{\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{n}}F}{\partial^{\omega_{1}}x_{1}\cdots\partial^{\omega_{n}}x_{n}}

exists and is continuous on
U
. For any compact subset
K\subset U
, we then define the
C^{k}

size

\|F\|_{C^{k},K}:=\max_{1\leq|\omega|\leq k}\max_{x\in K}\|\partial^{\omega}F(x)\|.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we say that a map
F
is
C^{\alpha}
if the following quantity is finite for any compact set
K\subset U
,

\|F\|_{C^{\alpha},K}:=\sup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{x,y\in K}\\
x\neq y\end{array}}\frac{\|F(x)-F(y)\|}{\|x-y\|^{\alpha}}.

Given
r>1
which is not an integer, we decompose it as
r=k+\alpha
, with
k=\lfloor r\rfloor
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
We say that
F
is
C^{r}
if it is
C^{k}
and each partial derivative
\partial^{\omega}F
,
|\omega|=k
is
C^{\alpha}
.
For any compact set
K\subset U
, we define the
C^{r}
size

\|F\|_{C^{r},K}:=\|F\|_{C^{k},K}+\max_{|\omega|=k}\|\partial^{\omega}F\|_{C^{\alpha},K}.

Let
\Omega
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is equal to the closure of its interior (we mostly need
[0,1]^{n}
). A map
F:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
C^{r}
if
F
has a
C^{r}
extension to an open neighborhood of
\Omega
.
In this case, the
C^{r}
size of
F
on
\Omega
is

\|F\|_{C^{r}}:=\;\sup_{K\subset\mathrm{int}(\Omega)}\;\|F\|_{C^{r},K}.

This (finite) quantity is independent of the extension of
F
to the neighborhood of
\Omega
. Notice that the
C^{r}
size of a constant function is zero.

A
C^{r}
structure
on a smooth manifold
N
is defined by a maximal atlas
\mathfrak{A}
with
C^{r}
changes of coordinates. A smooth manifold equipped with a
C^{r}
structure
\mathfrak{A}
is called a
C^{r}
manifold. A finite subset of
\mathfrak{A}
which covers
N
is called a
C^{r}
atlas of
N
.

Let
N_{1},N_{2}
be two compact
C^{r}
manifolds (later this will be
M
,
\widehat{M}
or the circle
S^{1}
), and let
\mathcal{A}_{i}
be finite
C^{r}
atlases of
N_{i}
.
Let
\Omega
be a compact subset of
N_{1}
equal to the closure of its interior.
We say that
f\colon\Omega\to N_{2}
is a
C^{r}
map
if
each map
\chi_{2}^{-1}\circ f\circ\chi_{1}
, where
\chi_{i}
ranges over
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, is
C^{r}
. The
C^{r}
size
of
f
is:

\|f\|_{C^{r}}:=\max_{\chi_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}}\|\chi_{2}^{-1}\circ f\circ\chi_{1}\|_{C^{r}}<\infty.

Again, the constant map has size zero.

The quantity
\|f\|_{C^{r}}
depends on the choice of atlases
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, but if
N_{i}
are compact, then finite atlases
induce equivalent
C^{r}
sizes. In case
N_{1}=S^{1}
, we will always use the Euclidean atlas.

Suppose
f_{k},f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
and
1\leq r<\infty
.
We will say that
f_{k}
converges to
f

uniformly in a
C^{r}
-bounded way
, if

f_{k}\to f
uniformly, and

\sup_{k\geq 1}\|f_{k}\|_{C^{r}}<\infty.

We write in this case

f_{k}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle r-\text{bd}}{\longrightarrow}f.

If
M
is compact,
f_{k},f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{\infty}(M)
, and
f_{k}\to f
in
C^{\infty}
, then
f_{k}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle r-\text{bd}}{\longrightarrow}f
for all
r
finite.

The Arzela-Ascoli theorem implies the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N_{1},N_{2}
be compact
C^{r}
manifolds, and
f,f_{1},f_{2},\dots\colon N_{1}\to N_{2}
be a collection of
C^{r}
maps such that
(f_{k})
converges to
f
uniformly, and
\sup_{k}\|f\|_{C^{r}}<\infty
.
Then
(f_{k})
converges to
f
in the
C^{\ell}
-topology for any
\ell<r
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Thus, if for some real
r>1
s.t.
r\not\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f,f_{1},f_{2},\dots\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
where
M
is a compact manifold, then
f_{k}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle r-\text{bd}}{\longrightarrow}f
implies that
f_{k}\to f
in the
C^{\lfloor r\rfloor}
-topology.

3.2.
The projective tangent bundle

Let

P_{x}M:=\{E:E\text{ is a one-dimensional linear subspace of }T_{x}M\}.

P_{x}M
is the quotient of
T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
by the equivalence relation

v\sim w

\iff

\exists\lambda\neq 0,\;v=\lambda w
.
It can also be viewed as the image of
\{v\in T_{x}M:\|v\|_{x}=1\}
by the two-to-one map
v\mapsto\mathrm{Span}\{v\}
. These identifications allow us to endow
P_{x}M
with a topology and with a smooth structure, and to identify the tangent spaces
T_{E}(P_{x}M)
with
\{w\in T_{x}M:w\perp E\}
.
We can also pull back the induced Riemannian inner product on
\{w\in T_{x}M:w\perp E\}
to an inner product on
T_{E}(P_{x}M)
. This endows
P_{x}M
with a Riemannian structure.
The resulting Riemannian distance on
P_{x}M
is simply
{\mathrm{dist}}(E_{1},E_{2})=|\measuredangle(E_{1},E_{2})|
.
With this structure,
P_{x}M
is isometric to the circle with perimeter
\pi
.

The
projective tangent bundle
(or just “projective bundle”) of
M
is the bundle

(\widehat{M},{\widehat{\pi}},M)
where
\widehat{\pi}:\widehat{M}\to M
is the natural projection
\widehat{\pi}(x,E)=x
, and

\widehat{M}:=\{(x,E):x\in M,\text{$E$ is a one-dimensional linear subspace of $T_{x}M$}\}=\bigsqcup_{x\in M}P_{x}M.

\widehat{M}
is a smooth compact three-dimensional manifold. We endow it with the Riemannian metric
\sqrt{ds^{2}+d\theta^{2}}
, where
ds
is the length element on
M
and
d\theta
is the length element on
P_{x}M
.
Points in
\widehat{M}
will be denoted by
\widehat{x}=(x,E)
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism. The
canonical lift
of
f
is the homeomorphism
\widehat{f}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
given by

\widehat{f}(x,E)=(f(x),Df_{x}(E)).

(7)

If
f
is of class
C^{r}
, then
\widehat{f}
is of class
C^{r-1}
.
Notice that

\widehat{\pi}\circ\widehat{f}=f\circ\widehat{\pi}
, and

\widehat{f}^{n}(x,E)=(f^{n}(x),(Df^{n})_{x}(E)).

Every
\widehat{f}
-invariant probability measure
\widehat{\nu}
on
\widehat{M}
projects to an
f
-invariant probability measure
\nu
on
M
given by

\nu:=\widehat{\pi}_{\ast}(\widehat{\nu}):=\widehat{\nu}\circ\pi^{-1}.

We call
\nu
the
projection
of
\widehat{\nu}
, and
\widehat{\nu}
a
lift
of
\nu
. In what follows, when we “lift”, we always mean an
\widehat{f}
-invariant lift. The following lemma is a well-known consequence of the compactness of
\widehat{M}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(1)

Every
f
-invariant probability measure
\nu
has at least one lift
\widehat{\nu}
.

(2)

If
\nu
is
f
-ergodic and
\widehat{\nu}
lifts
\nu
, then a.e. ergodic component of
\widehat{\nu}
is a lift of
\nu
.

Hence every ergodic
f
-invariant probability measure has at least one ergodic lift.

3.3.
Review of Lyapunov exponents

We review some facts on Lyapunov exponents in dimension two (see
[
46
, Theorems 3.12 and 3.14]
).
Suppose
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
and
\mu
is an
f
-invariant Borel probability measure.
Oseledets’ theorem asserts that for
\mu
-a.e.
x
,
\lim_{|n|\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|Df^{n}_{x}v\|
exists for all
v\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
. The possible values of the limit are called the
Lyapunov exponents
of
x
. There are at most two such values. We denote them by
\lambda^{+}(f,x)
,
\lambda^{-}(f,x)
, or

\lambda^{+}_{x}
,
\lambda^{-}_{x}
, with the convention

\lambda^{+}(f,x)\geq\lambda^{-}(f,x).

If
\lambda^{+}(f,x)\neq\lambda^{-}(f,x)
then
T_{x}M=E^{+}(x)\oplus E^{-}(x)
where

E^{\pm}(x):=\left\{v\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}:\lim_{|n|\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|Df^{n}_{x}v\|=\lambda^{\pm}(f,x)\right\}\cup\{0\}.

The decomposition
T_{x}M=E^{+}(x)\oplus E^{-}(x)
is called the
Oseledets splitting
.

If
\mu
is
ergodic
,
the functions
\lambda^{+}(f,x)\geq\lambda^{-}(f,x)
are equal
\mu
-almost everywhere to constants called the

Lyapunov exponents
of
\mu
and denoted by
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu),\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)
.
If
\mu
is not ergodic, the Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
are defined by

\lambda^{+}(f,\mu):=\int\lambda^{+}(f,x)d\mu(x),\;\lambda^{-}(f,\mu):=\int\lambda^{-}(f,x)d\mu(x).

In both cases,
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)\geq\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)
.

By the subadditive ergodic theorem, the largest Lyapunov exponent also satisfies

\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\|Df^{n}_{y}\|\,d\mu(y)=\inf_{n}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\|Df^{n}_{y}\|\,d\mu(y).

(8)

Throughout this paper, an ergodic invariant probability measure
\mu
is called
hyperbolic
if one of its Lyapunov exponents is positive, and the other is negative (sometimes this is called hyperbolic of saddle-type).
If
\mu
is hyperbolic, we sometimes write
\lambda^{u}=\lambda^{+}
,
\lambda^{s}=\lambda^{-}
,
E^{u}=E^{+}
and
E^{s}=E^{-}
.

3.4.
Semi-continuity of Lyapunov exponents

We will use the dynamics of the projective tangent bundle to study the semi-continuity properties of
(f,\mu)\mapsto\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)
(and by symmetry of
(f,\mu)\mapsto\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)
).
The principal tool is the function

\varphi:\widehat{M}\to{\mathbb{R}}\ ,\varphi(x,E)=\log\|Df_{x}|_{E}\|.

Notice that if
f
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism, then
\varphi
is bounded and uniformly continuous.
We will make frequent use of the following identity:

\log\|Df^{n}|_{E}\|=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(\varphi\circ\widehat{f}^{k})(x,E)\ \ \ ((x,E)\in\widehat{M}).

(9)

This is because
E
is a one-dimensional subspace of
T_{x}M
, and therefore by the chain rule

\|Df^{n}_{x}|_{E}\|=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\|Df_{f^{k}(x)}|_{Df^{k}_{x}(E)}\|=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\exp[(\varphi\circ\widehat{f}^{k})(x,E)]
. Equation (
9
) presents the subadditive cocycle
\log\|Df^{n}|_{E}\|
for
f
as an additive cocycle for
\widehat{f}
.
See
[
28
, Prop. 5.1 on p. 328]
for a proof of a more general fact (and
[
19
, Lemma 8.7]
for the first use of a related idea).

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
, and
\mu
is an ergodic
f
-invariant probability measure.
Then:

(1)

\lambda
is a Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
iff
\mu
has an
\widehat{f}
-ergodic lift
\widehat{\mu}
s.t.
\int\varphi d\widehat{\mu}=\lambda
;

(2)

If
\mu
has two different Lyapunov exponents, then it has exactly two ergodic
\widehat{f}
–invariant lifts:

\widehat{\mu}^{+}:=\int_{\widehat{M}}\delta_{(x,E^{+}(x))}d\mu(x)\text{, and }\widehat{\mu}^{-}:=\int_{\widehat{M}}\delta_{(x,E^{-}(x))}d\mu(x).

Moreover
\int\varphi d\widehat{\mu}^{\pm}=\lambda^{\pm}(f,\mu)
.

For any
\widehat{f}
-invariant probability measure, it will be convenient to denote

\widehat{\lambda}(\widehat{f},\widehat{\mu}):=\int_{\widehat{M}}\varphi d\widehat{\mu}.

When
\mu
is hyperbolic, the lifts
\widehat{\mu}^{+},\widehat{\mu}^{-}
are called the unstable and stable lifts of
\mu
.

Proof.

If
\mu
has equal Lyapunov exponents, (1) follows from eq. (
9
) and the ergodic theorem. Otherwise, by Oseledets theorem, there are two a.e. defined sections
x\mapsto E^{\pm}_{x}
s.t.
Df_{x}E^{\pm}_{x}=E^{\pm}_{f(x)}
.
Every
\widehat{f}
-invariant probability measure carried by the graph of an invariant section
x\mapsto E_{x}
is ergodic, and coincides with
\int_{M}\delta_{(x,E_{x})}\,d\mu(x)
.
So (1) and (2) follow from Lemma
3.2
and (
9
) (see
[
28
]
).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
and
\mu
is an
f
-invariant probability measure (not necessarily ergodic) s.t.
\lambda^{+}(f,x)>\lambda^{-}(f,x)
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in M
.
Then any
\widehat{f}
-invariant lift
\widehat{\mu}
of
\mu
is carried by
\mathrm{graph}(E^{+})\cup\mathrm{graph}(E^{-})
,
and there are unique
\widehat{f}
-invariant lifts
\widehat{\mu}^{+},\widehat{\mu}^{-}
s.t.
\widehat{\mu}^{+}(\mathrm{graph}(E^{+}))=1
,

\widehat{\mu}^{-}(\mathrm{graph}(E^{-}))=1
.

Proof.

By a general Borel construction, the graphs of
E^{+},E^{-}
are measurable. There are unique lifts
\widehat{\mu}^{+},\widehat{\mu}^{-}
of
\mu
to
\mathrm{graph}(E^{+}),\mathrm{graph}(E^{-})
, and it is easy to check using the identity
Df_{x}(E^{\pm}_{x})=E^{\pm}_{f(x)}
that
\widehat{\mu}^{\pm}
are
\widehat{f}
-invariant.

Now let
\widehat{\mu}
be an arbitrary lift of
\mu
and
consider its ergodic decomposition
\widehat{\mu}=\int\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}\,dm
. For almost every
\xi
, the ergodic measure
\mu_{\xi}=\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}\circ\widehat{\pi}^{-1}
has two different exponents, hence, by Lemma
3.3
, its lift
\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}
is some combination
a(\xi)\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{+}+(1-a(\xi))\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{-}
of its lifts
\widehat{\mu}^{\pm}_{\xi}
where
0\leq a(\xi)\leq 1
. Observe that
a(\xi)=\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}(\operatorname{graph}(E^{+}))
, hence the function
\xi\mapsto a(\xi)
is measurable.
It follows that

\widehat{\mu}=\int a(\xi)\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{+}+(1-a(\xi))\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{-}\,dm.

In particular, any lift
\widehat{\mu}
is carried by the union of the graphs of
E^{+}
and
E^{-}
.
∎

For each
n\geq 1
,
(f,\mu)\mapsto\int\log\|Df^{n}_{x}\|\,d\mu(x)
is continuous as a function on
\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)\times\{\text{probability measure on $M$}\}
.
Eq. (
8
) now give us the following “folklore” fact:

Theorem 3.5

(Upper semicontinuity)
.

Let
f_{k}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
be diffeomorphisms with ergodic invariant probability measures
\nu_{k}
.
If
f_{k}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle C^{1}}{\longrightarrow}f
and
\nu_{k}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mu
, then
the largest Lyapunov exponents
\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})
satisfy

\limsup_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})\leq\lambda^{+}(f,\mu).

The next result computes the defect in continuity
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\limsup\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})
in terms of the dynamics on the projective bundle. It relates the defect in continuity to the escape of some of the mass of the lifts to
\mathrm{graph}(E^{+})
to the vicinity of
\mathrm{graph}(E^{-})
.

Let
\widehat{\mu}
be an
\widehat{f}
-invariant probability measure with ergodic decomposition
\widehat{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}dm
. The projection
\mu
of
\widehat{\mu}
to
M
has ergodic decomposition
\mu=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{\xi}\,dm
, where
\mu_{\xi}=\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}\circ\pi^{-1}
. Note that in general, the map
\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}\mapsto\mu_{\xi}
is not injective.

We split the set of ergodic components
\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}

(\xi\in\Omega)
by considering whether they are carried by the invariant line bundles
E^{+}
or
E^{-}
or by a subset of
\widehat{M}
where these bundles are not defined:

\displaystyle\Omega^{-}:=\{\xi\in\Omega:\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})<\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})\ ,\ \widehat{\mu}_{\xi}=\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{-}\}

\displaystyle\Omega^{+}:=\{\xi\in\Omega:\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})<\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})\ ,\ \widehat{\mu}_{\xi}=\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{+}\}

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}:=\{\xi\in\Omega:\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})\}.

Theorem 3.6

(Defect in continuity)
.

Let
M
be a compact smooth boundaryless surface.
For each
k\geq 1
, fix
f_{k}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
and ergodic
f_{k}
-invariant measures
\nu_{k}
with
\lambda^{-}(f_{k},\nu_{k})<\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})
. Let
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}
be the ergodic lift to
\widehat{M}
carried by the bundle
E^{+}
.
Suppose

f_{k}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle C^{1}}{\longrightarrow}f
,
\nu_{k}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mu
, and
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\widehat{\mu}.

Considering the ergodic decompositions
\widehat{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}dm
,
\mu=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{\xi}\,dm

and defining
\Omega=\Omega^{+}\cup\Omega^{-}\cup\Omega^{0}
as above, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\int_{\Omega^{-}}[\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})-\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})]dm.

In the special case when
\mu
is ergodic, we have the following (see Lemma
3.3
):

(1)

If
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)=\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)
or
\widehat{\mu}=\widehat{\mu}^{+}
,
then

\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu).

(2)

If
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)<\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)
and
\widehat{\mu}\neq\widehat{\mu}^{+}
, then
\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})<\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)
.

More precisely, there is a unique
0<a\leq 1
such that

\widehat{\mu}=a\widehat{\mu}^{-}+(1-a)\widehat{\mu}^{+}
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-a(\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)).

If
\nu_{k}
and
\mu
are hyperbolic (of saddle type), then
a\neq 1
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.3
, we see that

\int\varphi d\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}=\begin{cases}\int\varphi d\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{+}=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})&\text{ if }\xi\in\Omega^{+}\\
\int\varphi d\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}^{-}=\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})&\text{ if }\xi\in\Omega^{-}\\
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})&\text{ if }\xi\in\Omega^{0},\end{cases}

(10)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int\varphi d\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}=\int\varphi d\widehat{\mu}=\iint\varphi d\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}dm.

(11)

Substituting (
10
) in (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{-}}\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})dm+\int_{\Omega^{+}\cup\Omega^{0}}\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})dm

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{-}}\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})dm+\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})dm-\int_{\Omega^{-}}\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})dm

\displaystyle=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\int_{\Omega^{-}}[\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\xi})-\lambda^{-}(f,\mu_{\xi})]dm,

which proves the theorem for general, possibly non-ergodic, limits
\mu
.

When
\mu
is ergodic, we apply equation (
11
) and Lemma
3.3
:

(1) If
\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)=\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)
or
\widehat{\mu}=\widehat{\mu}^{+}
, then
\int\varphi\,d\widehat{\mu}=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)
and item (1) follows.

(2) Otherwise,
\mu
has two different exponents and
m(\Omega^{+})<1
. Necessarily,
m(\Omega^{0})=0
,
a:=m(\Omega^{-})\in(0,1]
and the lift
\widehat{\mu}
can be written as
(1-a)\widehat{\mu}^{+}+a\widehat{\mu}^{-}
. So

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})=\int\varphi\,d\widehat{\mu}

\displaystyle=(1-a)\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)+a\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-a(\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\lambda^{-}(f,\mu))

\displaystyle<\lambda^{+}(f,\mu).

Finally, note that, if
a=1
and
\mu
is hyperbolic, then
\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})=\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)<0
, and
\nu_{k}
are not hyperbolic of saddle type for
k
large enough.
∎

Notice that the defect in continuity originates at
\Omega^{-}
, the set of ergodic components of
\lim\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}
which are carried by
\mathrm{graph}(E^{-})
. This confirms the heuristic that discontinuity in Lyapunov exponents is due to the asymptotic escape of mass from
\mathrm{graph}(E^{+})
, which carries
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}
, to
\mathrm{graph}(E^{-})
, which carries
\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}
for
\xi\in\Omega^{-}
.

3.5.
A bound for the asymptotic dilation of
\widehat{f}

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
C^{2}
diffeomorphism
f
of a surface
M
,
\lambda(\widehat{f})\leq\lambda(f)+\lambda(f^{-1})
.

Proof.

Working locally in charts, we identify the iterates
f^{n}
locally with diffeomorphisms
F_{i}\colon U_{i}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
defined on open subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We choose the charts so that the change of coordinates distorts the metric by a factor of less than
2
.
The lift
\widehat{f}^{n}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(\widehat{M})
is identified with:

\widehat{F}_{i}(x,v)=\left(F_{i}(x),\frac{(DF_{i})_{x}v}{\|(DF_{i})_{x}v\|}\right).

In what follows, we omit the first factor.

The differential of
\widehat{F}_{i}
can be computed in a straightforward way (writing
a\ast b
for the scalar product of two vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
):

\displaystyle(D\widehat{F}_{i})_{(x,v)}(y,w)=

\displaystyle\frac{(DF_{i})_{x}w}{\|(DF_{i})_{x}v\|}+\frac{(D^{2}F_{i})_{x}(y).v}{\|(DF_{i})_{x}v\|}

(12)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{(DF_{i})_{x}v}{\|(DF_{i})_{x}v\|}\ast\frac{(DF_{i})_{x}w+(D^{2}F_{i})_{x}(y).v}{\|(DF_{i})_{x}v\|}\right)\frac{(DF_{i})_{x}(v)}{\|(DF_{i})_{x}v\|}.

Thus,

\|(D\widehat{F}_{i})_{(x,v)}\|\leq 2\left(\|(DF_{i})_{x}\|\,\|(DF_{i}^{-1})_{f(x)}\|+\|(D^{2}F_{i})_{x}\|\,\|(DF_{i}^{-1})_{f(x)}\|\right).

We apply this to some iterate of
f
on
M
, remembering the distortion in the metric:

\|D\widehat{f}^{n}\|_{\sup}\leq 32\|Df^{n}\|_{\sup}\|Df^{-n}\|_{\sup}+16\|Df^{-n}\|_{\sup}\|D^{2}f^{n}\|_{\sup}

Let
0<\varepsilon<1/4
. Fix
n
an integer so large that
32\leq e^{\varepsilon n/4}
and

\|Df^{n}\|_{\sup}\leq e^{n(\lambda(f)+\varepsilon/8)}\text{ and }\|Df^{-n}\|_{\sup}\leq e^{n(\lambda(f^{-1})+\varepsilon/8)}.

Therefore,

\lambda(\widehat{f})\leq\lambda(f)+\lambda(f^{-1})+\varepsilon/2+\log\left(1+e^{-\lambda(f)}\|D^{2}f^{n}\|_{\sup}^{1/n}\right)

By dilating the metric on
M
, we can ensure that
\|D^{2}f^{n}\|_{\sup}^{1/n}\leq(\varepsilon/4)e^{\lambda(f)}
without changing the asymptotic dilations. Thus

\lambda(\widehat{f})\leq\lambda(f)+\lambda(f^{-1})+\varepsilon

As
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small, the claim follows.

∎

These computations allow the following control of the
C^{r-1}
size of the lift of a
C^{r}
diffeomorphism.

Lemma 3.8
.

For every real
2<r<\infty
, there is a constant
A=A(r)
with the following property. For any
g\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
with lift
\widehat{g}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r-1}(\widehat{M})
,

\|\widehat{g}\|_{C^{r-1}}\leq A\left(\|g\|_{C^{r}}\cdot\|Dg^{-1}\|_{\sup}\right)^{A}.

Proof.

We first consider the
k
th derivative of
f
in charts for the maximal integer
k\leq r
. A straightforward induction on the integer
k\geq 2
based on eq. (
12
) shows that the
(k-1)
th differential of
\widehat{F}
at some point
(x,v)\in\widehat{M}
can be written as a linear combination of terms:

\frac{1}{\|D_{x}F.v\|^{p}}(D^{\alpha_{1}}F*D^{\beta_{1}}F)\dots(D^{\alpha_{j}}F*D^{\beta_{j}}F)\cdot D^{\gamma}F

where
p,\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\dots,\alpha_{j},\beta_{j},\gamma
are integers, and
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\dots,\gamma\leq k
. The coefficients of this linear combination depend only on
k
.

If
k=r
, the claim is immediate. If
\alpha:=r-k>0
, recall that the
C^{r}
size is the sum of the
C^{k}
size and
\alpha
-Hölder size of the
k
-th derivative. A further computation using the above expression gives the required bound for the Hölder constant of order
\alpha
of
D^{k}\widehat{g}
.
∎

4.
Entropy formulas and reparametrizations

We saw in last section that the defect in continuity of
(f,\mu)\mapsto\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)
can be described in terms of the canonical lift
\widehat{f}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
. In this section we develop tools for studying the entropy map
(f,\mu)\mapsto h(f,\mu)
in terms of
\widehat{f}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{M}
.

Specifically, we will show that the entropy of hyperbolic measures on
M
can be studied in terms of the exponential rate of growth in
C^{r}
–complexity of
\widehat{f}^{n}\circ\widehat{\sigma}
, where
\widehat{\sigma}:[0,1]\to\widehat{M}
is the curve
\widehat{\sigma}(t)=(\sigma(t),\mathbb{R}.\sigma^{\prime}(t))
and
\sigma:[0,1]\to M
is a smooth parameterization of a local unstable manifold.

4.1.
Review of entropy and the ergodic decomposition

This section collects several classical facts on the entropy theory of non-ergodic measures. For proofs and details, see
[
15
, chap. 13]
.

Consider a compact metric space
X
together with a continuous map
T
preserving an invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
and the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{X}
of Borel subsets of
X
. The ergodic decomposition of
\mu
with respect to
T
is:

\mu=\int_{X}\mu_{x}\,d\mu(x)

where
\mu_{x}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq k<n}\delta_{T^{k}x}
(the weak-
*
limit exists almost everywhere by the ergodic theorem).

The map
x\mapsto\mu_{x}
is
\mu
-measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{I}
of invariant measurable subsets; for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
,
\mu_{x}
is a
T
-invariant and ergodic Borel probability measure and for every Borel
\mu
-absolutely integrable
u
,
x\mapsto\mu_{x}(u)
belongs to
L^{1}(\mu)
and
\mu(u)=\int_{X}\mu_{x}(u)\,d\mu(x)
.
The metric entropy of
\mu
and
\mu_{x}
are related by

h(T,\mu)=\int_{X}h(T,\mu_{x})d\mu(x).

Suppose
\xi
is a countable measurable partition of
X
with finite mean entropy
H_{\mu}(\xi):=-\sum_{A\in\xi}\mu(A)\log\mu(A)
.
Let
\xi_{n}:=\{\bigcap_{i=0}^{n-1}T^{-i}A_{i}:A_{i}\in\xi\}
.
It is a classical fact that

h(T,\mu,\xi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\xi_{n})

exists.

Similarly, one defines
h(T,\mu_{x},\xi)
.
The function
x\mapsto h(T,\mu_{x},\xi)
is defined
\mu
-a.e., is measurable with respect to the
\mu
-completion of
\mathcal{I}
.
Let
\xi_{n}(x)
denote the atom of
\xi_{n}
which contains
x
. The Shannon-McMillan-Breiman theorem, in its version for non-ergodic measures
[
15
, (13.4)]
, states that,

\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu\left(\xi_{n}(x)\right)=h(T,\mu_{x},\xi)\quad\text{both $\mu$-a.e. and in }L^{1}(\mu).

(13)

In addition, we have the following identity:
[
15
, (13.3)]
:

h(T,\mu,\xi)=\int_{X}h(T,\mu_{x},\xi)\,d\mu(x).

In particular,
h(T,\mu,\xi)\leq\overline{h}(T,\mu,\xi)
,
where

\overline{h}(T,\mu):=\operatorname{ess-sup}_{x\in X}h(T,\mu_{x}).

(14)

We call
\overline{h}(T,\mu)
the

essential supremum entropy
of
\mu

4.2.
Bowen and Katok entropy formulas

Let
T:X\to X
be a continuous map on a compact metrix space
X
.
An
(n,\epsilon)
-Bowen ball
is a set of the form

B_{T}(x,n,\varepsilon):=\{y\in X:\forall 0\leq k<n,\;d(T^{k}y,T^{k}x)<\varepsilon\}.

The
(n,\varepsilon)
-covering number
of a subset
Z\subset X
, is

r_{T}(n,\varepsilon,Z):=\min\{{|C|}:\bigcup_{x\in C}B_{T}(x,n,\varepsilon)\supset Z\}.

Bowen
[
8
]
defined the topological entropy of a (possibly non-invariant) set
Z\subset X
for
T
to be

h_{\operatorname{top}}(T,Z)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{\operatorname{top}}(T,Z,\varepsilon)\text{ with }h_{\operatorname{top}}(T,Z,\varepsilon)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{T}(n,\varepsilon,Z),

(15)

and showed that the topological entropy of
T
is
h_{\operatorname{top}}(T)=h_{\operatorname{top}}(T,X)
.

Katok gave a similar formula for the metric entropy of an invariant measure.
Let
\mu
be an invariant probability measure. For every
\gamma\in(0,1)
, let

r_{T}(n,\varepsilon,\mu,\gamma):=\inf\{r_{T}(n,\varepsilon,Z):Z\subset X\text{ measurable s.t. }\mu(Z)>\gamma\}.

He showed that if
\mu
is ergodic, then

\displaystyle{h}(T,\mu)=\lim_{\lambda\to 1}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{T}(n,\varepsilon,\mu,\gamma).

Katok’s proof in
[
26
]
also works in the non-ergodic case, if we replace the usual Shannon-McMillan-Breiman Theorem by (
13
). The result is that for a general (possily non-ergodic) invariant probability measure
\mu
,

\overline{h}(T,\mu)=\lim_{\lambda\to 1}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{T}(n,\varepsilon,\mu,\gamma).

(16)

Here
\overline{h}(T,\mu)
is the essential supremum entropy from (
14
).

4.3.
The lift to the projective tangent bundle preserves entropy

This is a standard consequence of the following theorem
[
30
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Ledrappier-Walters)
.

Let
(X,T)
,
(Y,S)
be continuous self-maps of compact metric spaces and let
\pi:(Y,S)\to(X,T)
be a topological factor map s.t.

\forall x\in X\qquad h_{\operatorname{top}}(S,\pi^{-1}(x))=0.

(17)

Then, for any
S
-invariant Borel probability measure
\nu
on
Y
,

h(S,\nu)=h(T,\pi_{*}\nu).

Thus by the variational principle, in the setup of the theorem,
h_{\operatorname{top}}(T)=h_{\operatorname{top}}(S)
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
and
\widehat{f}
is the canonical extension of
f
to the projective bundle
\widehat{M}
. Then

h_{\operatorname{top}}(\widehat{f})=h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)
, and for every
\widehat{f}
-invariant probability measure
\widehat{\nu}
with projection
\nu
,

h(\widehat{f},\widehat{\nu})=h(f,\nu)
and
\overline{h}(\widehat{f},\widehat{\nu})=\overline{h}(f,\nu)
.

Proof.

We check condition (
17
) for
T=f
,
S=\widehat{f}
, and apply the previous theorem.

\widehat{M}
is a topological bundle over
M
, and its fibers
P_{x}M
are homeomorphic to circles. The map
\widehat{f}:P_{x}M\to P_{f(x)}M
is a homeomorphism. For every
\varepsilon>0
, one can find partitions
\xi_{x}
of
P_{x}M
into a bounded number of arcs with diameter at most
\varepsilon
. It is easy to see that
\xi_{x}\vee\widehat{f}^{-1}\xi_{\widehat{f}\widehat{x}}\vee\dots\vee\widehat{f}^{-n+1}\xi_{\widehat{f}^{n-1}\widehat{x}}
has cardinality at most
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{Card}(\xi_{\widehat{f}^{k}\widehat{x}})=O(n)
. It follows that
h_{top}(\widehat{f},P_{x}M)=0
for all
x
.
∎

4.4.
Bowen and Katok entropy formulas on the bundle
\widehat{M}
.

We need a variant of the Bowen and Katok entropy formulas which uses a different type of Bowen balls, which are better adapted to the bundle structure of
\widehat{M}
.

Recall the natural projection
\widehat{\pi}:\widehat{M}\to M
,
\widehat{\pi}(x,E)=x
.
The
fibered ball
with
center

\widehat{x}\in\widehat{M}
and
scales

\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon}>0

B(\widehat{x},\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon}):=\{\widehat{y}\in\widehat{M}:\;d(\widehat{x},\widehat{y})<\widehat{\varepsilon}\text{ and }d(\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{x}),\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y}))<\varepsilon\}.

A set
S\subset\widehat{M}
has
fibered size

\leq(\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
if

for some
\widehat{x}
.

Suppose
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
and
\widehat{f}
is the canonical extension of
f
to
\widehat{M}
.
The
fibered
(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
–Bowen ball
with
center

\widehat{x}\in\widehat{M}
,
size

(\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
and
length

n
is the set

\begin{split}B_{\widehat{f}}(\widehat{x},n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon}):=\{\widehat{y}\in\widehat{M}:\;\forall 0\leq k<n,\;d(&\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{x}),\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{y}))<\widehat{\varepsilon}\\
&\text{ and }d(f^{k}(\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{x})),f^{k}(\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})))<\varepsilon\}.\end{split}

(18)

The
(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-covering number
of a subset
Z\subset\widehat{M}

(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-Bowen balls whose union contains

. It is denoted by

Clearly if
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{2}
and
\widehat{\varepsilon}_{1}\leq\widehat{\varepsilon}_{2}

r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon_{1},\widehat{\varepsilon}_{1},Z)\geq r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon_{2},\widehat{\varepsilon}_{2},Z)

Similarly, given an ergodic measure
\widehat{\mu}
of
\widehat{f}
and a number

-Bowen balls whose union has

-measure at least

. It is denoted by

If

, and

, then
r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon_{1},\widehat{\varepsilon}_{1},\widehat{\mu},\gamma_{1})\geq r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon_{2},\widehat{\varepsilon}_{2},\widehat{\mu},\gamma_{2})
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Fix
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1}(M)
with canonical lift
\widehat{f}
. We have:

(1)

Bowen’s formula: For every
\widehat{\varepsilon}>0
,

h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon},\widehat{M})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon},\widehat{M})

(2)

Katok’s formula: Suppose
\mu
is an
f
-ergodic invariant measure, and let
\widehat{\mu}
be an
\widehat{f}
-ergodic lift of
\mu
. Then for every
\widehat{\varepsilon}>0
and
0<\gamma<1
,

h(f,\mu)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon},\widehat{\mu},\gamma),

If
\mu
is
f
-invariant, but possibly not ergodic, then for every lift
\widehat{\mu}
,

\overline{h}(f,\mu)=\lim_{\gamma\to 1}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon},\widehat{\mu},\gamma),

Proof.

We
follow the proof of
[
8
, Thm 17]
. We need the following claim.

Claim 4.4
.

For every
\widehat{\varepsilon},\alpha>0
, there are
C,\varepsilon_{*}>0
such that

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{*}),\;\forall n\geq 1,\;\forall x\in M,\quad r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon},\widehat{\pi}^{-1}B_{f}(x,n,\varepsilon))\leq Ce^{\alpha n}.

Proof of the Claim.

Let
\widehat{\varepsilon},\alpha>0
.
Note first that

r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon},\widehat{\pi}^{-1}B_{f}(x,n,\varepsilon)){\leq}r_{\widehat{f}}(n,\widehat{\varepsilon},\widehat{\pi}^{-1}B_{f}(x,n,\varepsilon)),

hence it is enough to bound the latter. Since
h_{\operatorname{top}}(\widehat{f},\widehat{\pi}^{-1}(x))=0
,
for each
x\in M
there is a smallest integer
n_{x}\geq 1
such that
r_{\widehat{f}}(n_{x},\widehat{\varepsilon}/2,\widehat{\pi}^{-1}(x))\leq e^{\alpha n_{x}}
.

Recall that a set-valued function
F
from a topological space
X
to the set of subsets of a topological set
Y
is called
upper semi-continuous
, if for every
E\subset Y
closed,
\{x\in X:F(x)\cap E\neq\varnothing\}
is closed.
The continuity of
\widehat{\pi}
and the compactness of
\widehat{M}
implies that
x\mapsto\widehat{\pi}^{-1}(x)
is upper semi-continuous.

It follows that if
\widehat{\pi}^{-1}(x)
is contained in some open set
U
(say the union of a minimal cover by fibered Bowen balls), then
\widehat{\pi}^{-1}(y)
is contained in
U
for all

sufficiently close to

.
Hence there is an
r_{x}>0
such that

r_{\widehat{f}}(n_{x},\widehat{\varepsilon}/2,\widehat{\pi}^{-1}B(x,r_{x}))\leq r_{\widehat{f}}(n_{x},\widehat{\varepsilon}/2,\widehat{\pi}^{-1}(x))\leq e^{\alpha n_{x}}.

(19)

Using a compactness argument, we see that
n_{*}:=\sup\{n_{x}:x\in M\}
is finite and that one can arrange for
{\varepsilon_{*}}:=\inf\{r_{x}:x\in M\}
to be positive.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{*})
,
n\geq 1
and
x\in M
. Define
t_{0}(x):=0
and
t_{i+1}(x):=t_{i}(x)+n_{f^{t^{i}(x)}x}
. Choose
i\geq 0
maximal such that
t_{i}(x)\leq n
. Note that
0\leq n-t_{i}(x)<n_{*}
. Thus, setting
x_{j}:=f^{t_{j}(x)}x
and
n_{j}:=n_{x_{j}}
,

B_{f}(x,n,\varepsilon)=\bigcap_{j=0}^{i-1}f^{-t_{j}(x)}B_{f}(x_{j},n_{j},{\varepsilon})\cap f^{-t_{i}(x)}B(x_{i},n-t_{i}(x),{\varepsilon}).

Let
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{C}\}
be a
(n_{*}-1,\widehat{\varepsilon}/2)
-cover of
\widehat{M}
with
cardinality
C
.
For each
0\leq j<i
, let
\{y_{j1},\dots,y_{jm}\}
with
m\leq e^{\alpha n_{j}}
be the
(n_{j},\widehat{\varepsilon}/2)
-cover of
\widehat{\pi}^{-1}B(x_{j},n_{j},\varepsilon)
implied by eq. (
19
). Then
\widehat{\pi}^{-1}B_{f}(x,n,\varepsilon)
is contained in the following union:

\bigcup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}k,k_{0},\dots,k_{i-1}\\
k_{j}\leq\exp\alpha n_{j}\\
k\leq C\end{array}}\bigcap_{j=0}^{i-1}\widehat{f}^{-t_{j}(x)}B_{\widehat{f}}(y_{jk_{j}},n_{j},\widehat{\varepsilon}/2)\cap\widehat{f}^{-t_{i}(x)}B_{\widehat{f}}(z_{k},n-t^{i}(x),\widehat{\varepsilon}/2).

(n,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-separated.
So the cardinality of any

, there are

4.5.
Curves and
C^{r}
reparametrizations

The entropy of a diffeomorphism can be related to the exponential rate of growth in
C^{r}
complexity of the iterates of a local unstable manifold. To do this we need to control the curvature, and for this purpose it is useful to lift the curve to projective bundle
\widehat{M}
and study its iterations there. Here we develop the tools needed for doing this.

Definition 4.5
.

A
C^{r}
curve
\sigma:[0,1]\to M
is
regular
if its derivative
\sigma^{\prime}(t)
never vanishes.
In this case, it has a
canonical lift

\widehat{\sigma}:[0,1]\to\widehat{M}
defined by

\widehat{\sigma}(t):=(\sigma(t),{\mathbb{R}}.\sigma^{\prime}(t)).

Here and throughout,
\mathbb{R}.\sigma^{\prime}(t)\equiv\mathrm{span}\{\sigma^{\prime}(t)\}
is a linear subspace of
T_{x}M
. By regularity,
\mathbb{R}.\sigma^{\prime}(t)
is one-dimensional.

Definition 4.6
.

Fix
r\geq 2
and let
\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon}
be two positive numbers.
A regular
C^{r}
curve
\sigma:[0,1]\to M
has
C^{r}
size
(or just size) less than
(\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
if

\|\sigma\|_{C^{r}}{<}\varepsilon\text{ and }\|\widehat{\sigma}\,\|_{C^{r-1}}{<}\widehat{\varepsilon}.

The curve has
diameter
less than
(\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
if

{\operatorname{diam}}_{M}(\sigma([0,1]))<\varepsilon\text{ and }{\operatorname{diam}}_{\widehat{M}}(\widehat{\sigma}([0,1]))<\widehat{\varepsilon}.

To say that a curve has finite
C^{r}
size implies that it is regular and
C^{r}
.

Remark 4.7
.

If a curve
\sigma
is parametrized by length (i.e.,
\|\sigma^{\prime}(t)\|=1
for all
t
), then it has size at most
(\|\sigma\|_{C^{r}},C\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{C^{r-1}})
for some constant
C>0
which depends on the choice of
C^{r-1}
atlas of
\widehat{M}
used to define
C^{r-1}
size (see section
3.1
).

Suppose
\sigma:[0,1]\to M
is a curve. If we cut
[0,1]
into small intervals
[a_{i},b_{i}]
and reparametrize
\sigma|_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}
by
\sigma\circ\psi_{i}
where
\psi_{i}[0,1]\to[a_{i},b_{i}]
is an affine bijection, then
\|\sigma\circ\psi_{i}\|_{C^{r}}\leq\kappa\|\sigma\|_{C^{r}},\|\widehat{\sigma}\circ\psi_{i}\|_{C^{r}}\leq\kappa\|\widehat{\sigma}\|_{C^{r}}
, where
\kappa=|a_{i}-b_{i}|<1
. Cutting sufficiently finely, we can obtain covering by pieces with affine reparametrizations with
C^{r}
size as small as we wish.

Yomdin measured the
C^{r}
complexity of a curve (more generally a set) by counting how many reparametrized pieces with
C^{r}
size less than
1
are needed to cover it. We adapt this to the projective dynamics:

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\sigma:[0,1]\to M
be a
C^{r}
curve. A
reparametrization of
\sigma
is a non-constant affine map
\psi\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
.

A
family of reparametrizations of
\sigma
over a subset
T\subset[0,1]
is a collection
\mathcal{R}
of reparametrizations such that

T\subset\bigcup_{\psi\in\mathcal{R}}\psi([0,1])
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
and let
\sigma
be a regular
C^{r}
curve. We will be interested in families of reparametrizations which remain bounded in
C^{r}
size after application of
f^{n}
for certain
n
.
Specifically, fix numbers
\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon}>0
, an integer
N\geq 1
, and
T\subset[0,1]
.

Definition 4.9
.

A reparametrization
\psi
of
\sigma
is
(C^{r},f,N,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-admissible up to time
n
, if
there exists an increasing sequence
(n_{0},n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
such that

•

n_{0}=0
,
n_{\ell}=n
, and
n_{j}-n_{j-1}\leq N
for each
1\leq j\leq\ell
,

•

for each
0\leq j\leq\ell
the curve
f^{n_{j}}\circ\sigma\circ\psi
has
C^{r}
size less than
(\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
.

We call the integers
n_{j}
the admissible times.

A family
\mathcal{R}
of reparametrizations of
\sigma
over
T

is
(C^{r},f,N,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-admissible up to time

n
, if each
\psi\in\mathcal{R}
is
(C^{r},f,N,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-admissible up to time
n
.

Lemma 4.10

(Concatenation of reparametrizations)
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
,
r\geq 2
, and consider
a regular
C^{r}
curve
\sigma
on
M
, and
T\subset[0,1]
. Suppose that

\bullet

\mathcal{R}
is a family of reparametrizations of
\sigma
over
T
;

\bullet

\mathcal{R}
is

(C^{r},f,N,\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon})
-admissible up to time
n
;

\bullet

for each
\psi\in\mathcal{R}
, there is a family
\mathcal{R}_{\psi}
of reparametrizations of

.

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{\psi\in\mathcal{R}}\{\psi\circ\phi:\phi\in\mathcal{R}_{\psi}\}

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}

.

Since

\bigcup_{\psi\in\mathcal{R}}\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathcal{R}_{\psi}}(\psi\circ\phi)[0,1]=\bigcup_{\psi\in\mathcal{R}}\psi\bigl(\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathcal{R}_{\psi}}\phi[0,1]\bigr)\supseteq\bigcup_{\psi\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(\psi^{-1}(T))=\bigcup_{\psi\in\mathcal{R}}T\cap\psi[0,1]=T,

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}

over
T
.

To see that
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}

\psi\in\mathcal{R},\phi\in\mathcal{R}_{\psi}

\phi

We claim that

That the gaps are no larger than

<(\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon}),

and
\mathcal{R}_{\psi}

\phi^{\prime}=c

, we find that

\|f^{n_{j}}\circ\sigma\circ\psi\circ\phi\|_{C^{r}}\leq\|f^{n_{j}}\circ\sigma\circ\psi\|_{C^{r}}\cdot|c|\leq\|f^{n_{j}}\circ\sigma\circ\psi\|_{C^{r}}

,
because of the admissibility of
\mathcal{R}
.
Similarly
\|\widehat{f}^{n_{j}}\circ\widehat{\sigma}\circ\psi\circ\phi\|_{C^{r-1}}\leq\|\widehat{f}^{n_{j}}\circ\widehat{\sigma}\circ\psi\|_{C^{r-1}}<\widehat{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Lemma 4.11

(Length of reparametrizations)
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
,
r\geq 2
.
For any
\eta>0
and
N\geq 1

\varepsilon_{*}>0
and
a

-neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{*}
of

in
\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
with the following property.

Consider
g\in\mathcal{U}_{*}
,
\varepsilon,\widehat{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon_{*})
, a regular

curve
\sigma
, and a reparametrization
\varphi
of

\operatorname{Length}(g^{n}\circ\sigma\circ\varphi)<e^{\eta n/10}\|Dg^{n}_{x}|_{E}\|\operatorname{Length}(\sigma\circ\varphi).

Proof.

\widehat{M}
is compact, so one can find

and a small

-neighborhood

of

\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)

Let us consider

, a curve
\sigma
and a reparametrization
\varphi

. One gets for any

The reader who will compare this theorem to Yomdin’s original statement in

4.7.

In this section

Pesin’s Unstable Manifold Theorem says that

A measurable partition

[
29

Since

–a.e.

In this section we use the entropy theory of Ledrappier and Young

) to show that the entropy of

Theorem 4.15

(Y. Zang)
.

Let us consider

,

, and a system of conditional measures

-a.e.

.

5.

6.
The neutral decomposition

Let
f
be a homeomorphism on a compact metric space
X
.
We denote the point mass measure at
x\in X
by
\delta_{x}
. Given
{\mathfrak{N}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, let

\mu^{\mathfrak{N}}_{x,n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in[0,n)\cap{\mathfrak{N}}}\delta_{f^{j}(x)}.

The weak-
*
limit points of
(\mu^{\mathfrak{N}}_{x,n})_{n\geq 1}
are called the
{\mathfrak{N}}
-empirical measures
of
x
.

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose
\varphi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous,
\alpha>0
and
L\geq 1
.
An
(\alpha,L)
-neutral block
of
(x,f,\varphi)
is an interval of integers
(n_{0},n_{0}+1,\ldots,n_{1}-1)
s.t.

•

n_{1}-n_{0}\geq L
, and

•

\varphi(f^{n_{0}}(x))+\varphi(f^{n_{0}+1}(x))+\dots+\varphi(f^{n-1}(x))\leq\alpha\cdot(n-n_{0})
for all
n_{0}<n\leq n_{1}
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{N}_{\alpha,L}(x,f,\varphi)
the union of the
(\alpha,L)
-neutral blocks of
(x,f,\varphi)
.

Any interval of integers which is a union of two neutral blocks is still a neutral block.
Therefore, if
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{k}(x)),\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=-n}^{-1}{\varphi}(f^{k}(x))>\alpha
, then
\mathfrak{N}_{\alpha,L}(x,f,\varphi)
is a disjoint union of (finite) maximal neutral blocks.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
f,f_{1},f_{2},\dots
be homeomorphisms of a compact metric space
X
,
and let
\varphi,\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\dots
be continuous functions on
X
such that
f_{k}\to f
and
\varphi_{k}\to\varphi
uniformly.
For each
k
, let
\nu_{k}
be an ergodic probability for
f_{k}
such that
\int\varphi_{k}d\nu_{k}\geq 0
.
Then there exist a subsequence
(\nu_{k_{i}})
and (positive) measures
m_{0},m_{1}
such that:

(i)

Both
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
are
f
-invariant.

(ii)

The subsequence
(\nu_{k_{i}})
converges weak-
*
to
m_{0}+m_{1}
.

(iii)

For any neighborhoods
V=(V_{0},V_{1})
of
m_{0},m_{1}
, if
\alpha<\alpha_{*}(V)
,
L\geq L_{*}(V)
,
i\geq i_{*}(V,\alpha,L)
, then for
\nu_{k_{i}}
-a.e.
x
,
the
\mathfrak{N}_{\alpha,L}(x,f_{k_{i}},\varphi_{k_{i}})
-empirical measures belong to
V_{0}
, and
the
({\mathbb{N}}\setminus\mathfrak{N}_{\alpha,L}(x,f_{k_{i}},\varphi_{k_{i}}))
-empirical measures belong to
V_{1}
.

(iv)

\int\varphi dm_{0}=0
.

(v)

For
m_{1}
-almost every point
x
, the limit of

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{j}(x))
is positive.

Remark 6.3
.

The measures
m_{0},m_{1}
are not normalized, but there are
f
-invariant probabilities

\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
such that
m_{0}=(1-\beta)\mu_{0}
and

, where

.

Example 1.
The following constructions show that the decomposition
m_{0}+m_{1}
depends on the sequence
(\nu_{k})
and not just on its limit.

Let
X=\{-2,1,2\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
f_{k}=f=
the left shift, and
\varphi_{k}(x):=\varphi(x)=x_{0}
. For each

\displaystyle{\leq}n\cdot\widehat{m}_{1}^{\prime}(\widehat{M}\setminus\bigcup_{c^{\prime}}\widehat{U}_{1,c^{\prime}})+n\cdot\widehat{m}_{1}^{\prime}(\widehat{U}_{1,c})<n(\ell+2)\gamma^{2}+n[\widehat{m}_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1,c})+\gamma^{2}],

by eqs. (
52
) and (
56
).
By step 3 (d),
\widehat{m}_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1,c})<\beta(a_{c}+\gamma^{2})
.
Substituting this in the above and using (
50
) give

|\textsf{C}_{c}|\leq n\beta a_{c}+n(\ell+4)\gamma^{2}\leq n\beta a_{c}+n\gamma.

(b) Blank segments.
By definition, every blank segment is neutral, so
\textsf{B}\subset\textsf{Neut}
, and
|\textsf{B}|=|\textsf{Neut}|-|\textsf{Neut}\setminus\textsf{B}|
.
By (
52
) and the definition of
\widehat{m}_{0}^{\prime}
,

|\textsf{Neut}|=n\cdot\widehat{m}_{0}^{\prime}(\widehat{M})\geq n(\widehat{m}_{0}(\widehat{M})-\gamma^{2})=n(1-\beta-\gamma^{2}).

The set
\textsf{Neut}\setminus\textsf{B}
is the union of the maximal neutral orbit segments which visit
\widehat{U}_{0}
with frequency less than
1-\gamma
. Thus

\gamma\cdot|\textsf{Neut}\setminus\textsf{B}|<\widehat{m}_{0}^{\prime}(\widehat{M}\setminus\widehat{U}_{0})n
. By (
52
) and the bound

\widehat{\mu}_{0}(\widehat{U}_{0})>1-\gamma^{2}
in step 2,

\widehat{m}^{\prime}_{0}(\widehat{M}\setminus\widehat{U}_{0})<\widehat{m}_{0}(\widehat{M})-\widehat{m}_{0}(\widehat{U}_{0})+2\gamma^{2}<(1-\beta)-(1-\beta)(1-\gamma^{2})+2\gamma^{2}=\gamma^{2}(3-\beta),

so

|\textsf{Neut}\setminus\textsf{B}|\leq\gamma^{-1}\widehat{m}^{\prime}_{0}(\widehat{M}\setminus\widehat{U}_{0})n<\gamma(3-\beta)n.

It follows that
|\textsf{B}|>(1-\beta)n-4\gamma n.

(c) Fillers.

By construction, a filler is a segment of length one
(\widehat{y})
such that one of the following holds:

(i)

\widehat{y}
does not belong to a colored segment or to a segment in
\mathcal{S}_{neut}(\vartheta)
;

(ii)

\widehat{y}
belongs to a segment in
\mathcal{S}_{neut}(\vartheta)
, but this segment is not a blank segment;

(iii)

\widehat{y}
belongs to a
segment of length
n_{1,c}
which begins at
\widehat{U}_{1,c}
, but it fails to be a colored segment because it extends beyond the right endpoint of
\vartheta
;

(iv)

\widehat{y}
belongs to a
segment of length
n_{1,c}
which begins at
\widehat{U}_{1,c}
, but it fails to be a colored segment because it intersects an element of
\mathcal{S}_{neut}(\vartheta)
.

The fillers of type (i) belong to
\textsf{Neut}^{c}\setminus\bigcup_{c}\widehat{U}_{1,c}
, so their cardinality is bounded by eq. (
56
):

|\textsf{Neut}^{c}\setminus\bigcup_{c}\widehat{U}_{1,c}|=n\cdot\widehat{m}_{1}^{\prime}(\widehat{M}\setminus\bigcup_{c}\widehat{U}_{1,c})<(2+\ell)\gamma^{2}n<\gamma n

The fillers of type (ii) belong to
\textsf{Neut}\setminus\textsf{B}
. As we saw above this means that their cardinality is less than
\gamma(3-\beta)n<3\gamma n.

The number of fillers of type (iii) is clearly bounded by the maximum length of a colored segment
\max_{c}n_{1,c}=n_{1}+\ell^{\prime}\leq n_{1}+\ell
. This can be assumed to be less than
\gamma n
when
n
is large enough.

It remains to control the fillers of type (iv).
Fix
\vartheta_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{neut}(\vartheta)
, and suppose
\widehat{y}
belongs to a colored segment which intersects
\vartheta_{0}
. All colored segments have lengths at most
n_{1}+\ell^{\prime}
, therefore
\widehat{y}
must belong to one of two segments of length
n_{1}+\ell^{\prime}
adjacent to the endpoints of
\vartheta_{0}
. This gives the following bound for the number of fillers of type (iv):
2(n_{1}+\ell^{\prime})\cdot|\mathcal{S}_{neut}(\vartheta)|
.
Recalling that
\mathcal{S}_{neut}(\vartheta)
consists of disjoint sub-segments of
\vartheta
, each with length at least
L
, we find that

|\mathcal{S}_{neut}(\vartheta)|\leq\frac{n}{L}.

Thus by (
53
), the number of fillers of type (iv) is at most

\frac{2(n_{1}+\ell^{\prime})}{L}n<\gamma n.

It follows that the total length of the fillers is
|\textsf{F}|<6\gamma n
.

∎

Step 7 (A bound on the number of decomposition types)

In the previous step we decomposed orbit segments of typical points with length

Let

\theta=(t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{m})

H(t):=t\log\tfrac{1}{t}+(1-t)\log\tfrac{1}{1-t}\text{ for $0<t<1$}.

Claim 7.6
.

There exists
n_{H}:=n_{H}(\gamma)
such that the number of types of decompositions of all
\widehat{f}_{k}
–orbit segments as in Lemma

Proof.

A decomposition of an orbit segment with length

at most
\gamma n

–

at most
\gamma n

–

and at most

This gives a total of at most

So every type

Since

∎

We build

Step 10 (Cardinality of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
)

The families of reparametrizations obtained in step 9 satisfy

|\mathcal{R}(\psi,t_{i})|\leq\exp(\kappa_{i}(\theta)(t_{i}-t_{i-1})),

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}h_{c}+(r-1)^{-1}\lambda(\widehat{f})+2\eta&\text{if $t_{i}-t_{i-1}=n_{1,c}$},\quad\text{(case a)},\\
(r-1)^{-1}\lambda(\widehat{f})+\eta&\text{if $t_{i}-t_{i-1}\geq L$},\quad\text{(case b)},\\
\log(2\Upsilon)+\tfrac{r}{r-1}\log\|D\widehat{f}_{k}\|_{\sup}&\text{if $t_{i}-t_{i-1}=1$},\quad\text{(case c)}.\end{cases}

It follows that

|\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\theta}|\leq\exp\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_{i}(\theta)(t_{i}-t_{i-1})\biggr).

The total length of the blank segments is (trivially) less than
n
, and the total lengths of the segments with color

and fillers is respectively, less than
\beta a_{c}n+\gamma n
and

, by Lemma
7.5
.
Denoting the total length of colored segments with color

(resp. blank segments, fillers) by

(resp.

), we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_{i}(\theta)(t_{i}-t_{i-1})\leq\sum_{c}h_{c}N_{c}+\frac{\lambda(\widehat{f})}{r-1}\left(\sum_{c}N_{c}+N_{b}\right)+2\eta\sum_{c}N_{c}+\eta N_{b}

\displaystyle+\left(\log(2\Upsilon)+\frac{r}{r-1}\log\|D\widehat{f}_{k}\|_{{\sup}}\right)N_{f}.

Using the trivial bounds
\sum_{c}N_{c}+N_{b}=n-N_{f}\leq n
,
N_{b}\leq n
, and the bounds

,

from Lemma

, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_{i}(\theta)(t_{i}-t_{i-1})\leq n\sum_{c}h_{c}[\beta a_{c}+\gamma]+n\frac{\lambda(\widehat{f})}{r-1}+2\eta n

\displaystyle+6\gamma n\left(\log(2\Upsilon)+\frac{r}{r-1}\log\|D\widehat{f}_{k}\|_{{\sup}}\right).

Recall that we chose

and
\gamma
so that

–

\sum a_{c}h_{c}\leq h(f,\mu_{1})
by the choice of the decomposition of

in Step 1;

–

by eq. (
50
);

–

Hence,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\theta}|\leq\exp\biggl(\beta h(f,\mu_{1})n+\frac{\lambda(\widehat{f})}{r-1}n+4\eta n\biggr).

Recalling that

8.1.
Discontinuities: construction of the Example

Let us recall that two transitive hyperbolic sets

In this case there exists a transitive hyperbolic set that contains

and
K_{2}
.

If

is a periodic orbit, we will denote by

the invariant probability measure

converges weak-

We say that a

such that
for any diffeomorphism

The Newhouse domain is open by definition, and non-empty by
[

.

We prove the following more precise version of Example

:

Proposition 8.1
.

The Newhouse domain in
\operatorname{Diff}^{\infty}(M)
contains a dense G
δ
subset of diffeomorphisms

with the following property. For any pair of numbers

(\nu_{k})
converging weak-

Remark 8.2

One can choose for
\mu
any invariant probability measure with positive entropy, ergodic or not, and carried by the hyperbolic set

associated with the Newhouse domain of

.

Lemma 8.3

There is a dense G
δ

By an application of Baire’s argument, it is enough to find

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a locally maximal, hyperbolic compact set carrying an invariant probability measure

, not necessarily ergodic. Then there exist ergodic invariant probability measures

carried by

which converges to

*
topology and in entropy:
\lim_{k}h(f,m_{k})=h(f,m)

Sketch of proof.

This is routine, even if we could not locate an exact reference. Observe that it is enough to show this for a transitive subshift of finite type

8.2.
Variant inequality on Lyapunov exponents: proof of Corollary
1.3

By the Oseledets theorem,

\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)+\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)=\int\log|\det Df|d\mu,

which is continuous with respect to
(f,\mu)
in the
C^{1}\times
weak-
*
topology. Since the sum is continuous, the discontinuities in the summands must cancel out, whence

\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})=-\left(\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)-\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{-}(f_{k},\nu_{k})\right).

(57)

Now, Theorem
A
is equivalent to the statement

\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})\leq\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)\left(1-\frac{\lim_{k}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})}{h(f,\mu)}\right).

(58)

Applying this to
f_{k}^{-1}
and noting (
57
) and
\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)=-\lambda^{+}(f^{-1},\mu)
, we obtain (
4
).
Since
0<-\lambda^{-}(f,\mu)<\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)
, this is stronger than the conclusion of Theorem
A
.
∎

8.3.
Sequences of non-ergodic measures

We state and prove a version of Theorem
D
removing its assumption that the converging measures
\nu_{k}
are ergodic.

Corollary 8.5
.

Fix a real number
r>2
.
For every
k\geq 1
, let
f_{k}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
,

\nu_{k}
be an
f_{k}
-invariant measure, not necessarily ergodic, and
\widehat{\nu}_{k}
be a lift satisfying
\widehat{\lambda}(\widehat{f}_{k},\widehat{\nu}_{k})=\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})
. Let us assume that:

(1)

\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})
and
\lim_{k}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})
exist
and
\liminf_{k}\int\min(\lambda^{+}(f_{k},x),0)d\nu_{k}(x)=0,

(2)

f_{k}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle r-\text{bd}}{\longrightarrow}f
for some
f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)
,

(3)

\widehat{\nu}_{k}\overset{w^{*}}{\to}\widehat{\mu}
for some
\widehat{f}
-invariant probability measure
\widehat{\mu}
(perhaps non-ergodic) on
\widehat{M}
.

Then there exist
\beta\in[0,1]
, two

f
-invariant measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}

with
\widehat{f}
-invariant lifts
\widehat{\mu}_{0},\widehat{\mu}_{1}
s.t.

(a)

\widehat{\mu}=(1-\beta)\widehat{\mu}_{0}+\beta\widehat{\mu}_{1};

(b)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})=\beta\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{1});

(c)

\lim_{k\to\infty}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})-\tfrac{1}{r-1}\lambda(\widehat{f})\leq\beta\left(h(f,\mu_{1})+\tfrac{\lambda(f)}{r}\right);

(d)

if
\beta<1
,
\widehat{\lambda}(\widehat{f},\widehat{\mu}_{0})=0
;

(e)

if
\beta>0
, then
\widehat{\lambda}(\widehat{f},\widehat{\mu}_{1})=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{1})
and
\lambda^{+}(f,x)>0
for

\mu_{1}
-a.e.
x
.

Note that if
f_{k}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{\infty}(M)
and
f_{k}\to f
in
C^{\infty}
, then the corollary applies for all
r>2
, and Property (
c
) becomes
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})\leq\beta h(f,\mu_{1})
.

Note also that the conclusions (a)-(e) are the same as in Theorem
D
, except for the extra term
\lambda(f)/r
on the right hand side. See the following remark on this term.

Remark 8.6
.

Our proof relies on discretizing the ergodic decompositions of the measures
\nu_{k}
and applying Theorem
D
to the atoms thus defined and taking a limit.
This limiting process is responsible for the term
\lambda(f)/r
in the entropy estimate in Property (
c
).

Using the decomposition in the projective bundle is the key to avoid any such loss in the Lyapunov estimate eq. (
b
) and is therefore essential for our proof of this generalization.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{P}(M)
denote the set of Borel probability measures on
M
, and let
d
be the
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance over
{\mathbb{P}}(M)
. This distance is compatible with the weak-
*
topology and satisfies
d(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\mu_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\nu_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}d(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})
for all convex combinations.

We fix some
\varepsilon>0
and discretize the ergodic decompositions

\nu_{k}=\int_{X}\nu_{k,\xi}\,dP_{k}(\xi).

By compactness of
{\mathbb{P}}(M)
, there are
measurable partitions
X=X^{\varepsilon}_{k,1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup X^{\varepsilon}_{k,{N^{\varepsilon}}}
with number of elements
N^{\varepsilon}
independent of
k
, and with the following property for every
1\leq i\leq N^{\varepsilon}
:
There is an
\widehat{f}_{k}
-ergodic measure
\widehat{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{k,i}
with projection
\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i}
satisfying
\widehat{\lambda}(\widehat{f}_{k},\widehat{\nu}_{k,i}^{\varepsilon})=\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k,i}^{\varepsilon})

and, for
P_{k}
-a.e.
\xi\in X^{\varepsilon}_{k,i}
,

d(\nu_{k,\xi},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i})<\varepsilon,\;|h(f_{k},\nu_{k,\xi})-h(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i})|<\varepsilon,\text{ and }

(59)

\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k,\xi})-\tfrac{1}{k}\;<\;\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i})\;<\;\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k,\xi})+\varepsilon.

(60)

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume without loss of generality the existence of the following limits:

\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{i}:=\lim_{k}\widehat{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{k,i},\;\;\lim_{k}h(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i}),\;\;\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i}),\;\;\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{i}:=\lim_{k}P_{k}(X^{\varepsilon}_{k,i}).

For each
i=1,\dots,N
, we set
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{i}:=\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{i})
.
By inequalities (
60
) and
by our assumption
\liminf_{k}\int\min(\lambda^{+}(f_{k},x),0)d\nu_{k}(x)=0
,
it follows that either
\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{i}=0
or
\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i})\geq 0
.
One can thus apply Theorem
D

to the sequence
(f_{k},\widehat{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{k,i})_{k\geq 1}
converging to
(f,\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{i})
and obtain a decomposition

\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{i}=(1-\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i})\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}+\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i}\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1,i}

for some
0\leq\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i}\leq 1
and
\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{0,i},\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1,i}

\widehat{f}
-invariant measures such that

\lim_{k}h(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i})\leq\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i}h(f,\mu^{\varepsilon}_{1,i})+\frac{\lambda(\widehat{f})}{r-1}\text{ and }\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k,i})=\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i}\lambda^{+}(f,\mu^{\varepsilon}_{1,i})=\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i}\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1,i}(\varphi).

We collect all the pieces, setting:

\widehat{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\varepsilon}}\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{k,i}\widehat{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{k,i},\;\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\varepsilon}}\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{i}\,\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{i},\;\beta^{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\varepsilon}}\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{i}\beta^{\varepsilon}_{i},\;\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{s}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\varepsilon}}\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{i}\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{s,i}\text{ for }s=0,1.

We denote by
\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k},\mu^{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon}_{s}
the projections by
\widehat{\pi}
.
We have that:

\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}=(1-\beta^{\varepsilon})\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\beta^{\varepsilon}\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}.

By eq. (
59
),

d(\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k},\nu_{k})\leq\varepsilon,\;|h(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k})-h(f_{k},\nu_{k})|<\varepsilon,\;|\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k})-\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})|<\varepsilon.

Using that the entropy and average exponents are affine functions, we get

\lim_{k}h(f_{k},\nu_{k})\leq\liminf_{k}h(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k})+\varepsilon\leq\beta^{\varepsilon}h(f,\mu^{\varepsilon}_{1})+\frac{\lambda(\widehat{f})}{r-1}+\varepsilon

(61)

and

\left|\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})-\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{k})\right|\leq\varepsilon\text{ so }\left|\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(f_{k},\nu_{k})-\beta^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{+}(f,\mu^{\varepsilon}_{1})\right|\leq\varepsilon.

(62)

To conclude, we pick a sequence of numbers
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\geq 1}
decreasing to zero along which the three sequences
\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}
,
\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}
converge to measures
\widehat{\mu}
,
\widehat{\mu}_{0}
and
\widehat{\mu}_{1}
. Since
d(\mu^{\varepsilon},\mu)\leq\varepsilon
, we must have
\widehat{\pi}_{*}\widehat{\mu}=\mu
. We define
\mu_{s}:=\pi_{*}(\widehat{\mu}_{s})
for
s=0,1
. Property (
a
) follows by continuity.

By Yomdin theory (see the discussion after eq. (
5
)),

\lim_{j}h(f,\mu^{\varepsilon_{j}}_{1})\leq h(f,\mu_{1})+\lambda(f)/r,

yielding Property (
c
).
The decomposition converges in the projective bundle, hence, recalling that
\varphi(x,E):=\log\|D_{x}f|_{E}\|
is a continuous function,

\lim_{j}\lambda^{+}(f,\mu^{\varepsilon_{j}}_{1})=\lim_{j}\widehat{\mu}^{\varepsilon_{j}}_{1}(\varphi)=\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{1}).

b
) follows.

i

This gives the first part, assuming

for

would contain the same atom with uniform weight for large

. Let

We assume

\beta^{\prime}:=\beta(1-\widehat{\mu}_{1}(\widehat{Z})),\;(1-\beta^{\prime})\widehat{\mu}_{0}^{\prime}:=(1-\beta)\widehat{\mu}_{0}+\beta\frac{\widehat{\mu}_{1}(\,\cdot\cap\widehat{Z})}{\widehat{\mu}_{1}(\widehat{Z})},\;\beta^{\prime}\widehat{\mu}_{1}^{\prime}:=\beta\frac{\widehat{\mu}_{1}(\,\cdot\setminus\widehat{Z})}{1-\widehat{\mu}_{1}(\widehat{Z})}

obtaining a new decomposition
\widehat{\mu}=(1-\beta^{\prime})\widehat{\mu}^{\prime}_{0}+\beta^{\prime}\widehat{\mu}_{1}^{\prime}
.
Note that, setting
\mu^{\prime}_{1}:=\widehat{\pi}_{*}\widehat{\mu}^{\prime}_{1}
,

\beta^{\prime}h(f,\mu^{\prime}_{1})=\beta h(f,\mu_{1})\text{ and }\beta^{\prime}\lambda^{+}(f,\mu^{\prime}_{1})=\beta\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{1}).

since the probability measure
\frac{\mu_{1}(\,\cdot\cap Z)}{\widehat{\mu}_{1}(Z)}
has both zero entropy and zero top Lyapunov exponent.
This concludes the proof of the Corollary
8.5
.

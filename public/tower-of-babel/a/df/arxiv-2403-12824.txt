Well-posedness and no-uniform dependence for the Euler-Poincaré equations in Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
Yuanhua Zhong, Jianzhong Lu, Min Li, Jinlu Li

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem of the Euler-Poincaré equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with initial data belonging to the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. We prove the local-in-time unique existence of solutions to the Euler-Poincaré equations in
F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, we obtain that the data-to-solution of this equation is continuous but not uniformly continuous in these spaces.

Keywords:
Euler-Poincaré equations; Well-posedness; Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

MSC (2010): 35Q35

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following Cauchy problem of Euler-Poincaré equations:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}m+u\cdot\nabla m+\nabla u^{T}\cdot m+(\mathrm{div}u)m=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
m=(1-\Delta)u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(EP)

where
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{d})
denotes the velocity of the fluid,
m=(m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{d})
represents the momentum. Equivalently, we can write (
EP
) in components,

\partial_{t}m_{i}+\sum^{d}_{j=1}u_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}m_{i}+\sum^{d}_{j=1}(\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j})m_{j}+m_{i}\sum^{d}_{j=1}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{j}=0,~~~~~~~~i=1,2,\cdots,d.

According to Yan-Yin
[
32
]
, we can transform the first equation of (
EP
) into the following
nonlocal transport form:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u=Q(u,u)+R(u,u):=P(u,u),

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Q(u,v)=-(1-\Delta)^{-1}\mathrm{div}\Big(\nabla u\nabla v+\nabla u(\nabla v)^{T}-(\nabla u)^{T}\nabla v-(\mathrm{div}u)\nabla v+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}(\nabla u:\nabla v)\Big),\\
R(u,v)=-(1-\Delta)^{-1}\Big((\mathrm{div}u)v+(\nabla u)^{T}\cdot v\Big),\end{cases}

(1.2)

with

\displaystyle(\nabla u^{T})_{i,j}=\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j},\quad(u\cdot\nabla v)_{i}=\sum^{d}_{k=1}u_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}u_{i},\quad(\nabla u\nabla v)_{ij}=\sum^{d}_{k=1}\partial_{x_{i}}u_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}v_{j},

\displaystyle\nabla u:\nabla v=\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}v_{j},\quad\big((\nabla u)^{T}\cdot v\big)_{i}=\sum^{d}_{j=1}\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j}v_{j}.

The system (
EP
) was first introduced by Holm et al. in
[
19
,
18
]
as a higher dimensional generalization of the following Camassa-Holm equation (CH):

\displaystyle m_{t}+um_{x}+2u_{x}m=0,\ m=u-u_{xx}.

(CH)

Indeed, when
d=1
, the Euler-Poincaré equations reduce to (
CH
).
The celebrated Camassa-Holm equation (
CH
) was originally discovered by Camassa and Holm
[
4
]
as a bi-Hamiltonian system for modeling and analyzing the nonlinear shallow water waves. It is worth mentioning that the CH equation has peakon solutions of the form
Ce^{-|x-Ct|}\,(C>0)
, which aroused a lot of interest in physics; see for instance
[
8
,
29
]
. Nowadays, the research on the existence of solutions to the CH equation, their analytical and geometric properties, as well as the theory of well-posedness, has yielded fruitful results. The existence of global weak solutions and dissipative solutions was investigated in
[
2
,
3
,
31
]
and the references therein. Blow-up phenomena and global existence of strong solutions were discussed in
[
7
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, while the non-uniform continuity of the CH equation was considered in
[
15
,
16
,
21
,
27
]
. Moreover, in
[
10
,
13
,
14
]
,
the local well-posedness and ill-posedness for the Cauchy problem of the CH equation were studied.

The first rigorous analysis work of the Cauchy problem for the Euler-Poincaré equations (
EP
) is attributed to Chae and Liu
[
6
]
. They eatablished the local existence of weak solutions in
W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p>d
, and proved the local existence of unique classical solutions in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s>d/2+3
. Soon after, Yan and Yin
[
32
]
further discussed the local existence and uniqueness of the solutions to (
EP
) in Besov spaces. On the other hand, Li et al.
[
20
]
proved that the solutions to (
EP
) with a large class of smooth initial data blow up in finite time or exist globally in time, which settled an open problem raised by Chae and Liu
[
6
]
. Recently, Luo and Yin
[
28
]
obtained a new blow-up result in the periodic case by using the rotational invariant properties of the equation. For more results about Euler-Poincaré equations, we refer to
[
17
,
34
]
.

In recent years, as part of the well-posedness, the continuity properties of the data-to-solution map for Camassa-Holm type equations has received increasing interest from scholars. The related research was initiated by Li-Yu-Zhu in
[
21
]
. Later, Li et al.
[
22
]
proved that for the initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s>1+d/2
, the corresponding solution to (
EP
) is not uniformly continuous dependent. This non-uniformly continuous result was extended to Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),s>\max\{1+d/2,3/2\}
in
[
23
]
. Very recently, based on Galilean boost method and the symmetric structure, Li and Liu proved in
[
25
]
that the data-to-solution map of the Euler-Poincaré equations (
EP
) is not uniformly continuous on any open subset
U\subset B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s>\max\{1+d/2,3/2\}
.

In the present paper, inspired by
[
26
]
, we consider the Cauchy problem (
EP
) in Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and finally deduce the well-posedness and no-uniform dependence. To state our main result precisely, we first recall the notion of well-posedness in the sense of Hadamard:

Definition 1.1
.

(Local Well-posedness) The Cauchy problem (
EP
) is said to be locally well-posed in a Banach space
X
if the following three conditions hold

1.

(Local existence) For any initial data
u_{0}\in X
, there exists a short time
T=T(u_{0})
and a solution
u(t)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
to the Cauchy problem (
EP
);

2.

(Uniqueness) This solution
u(t)
is unique in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
;

3.

(Continuous Dependence) The data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
is continuous in the following sense: for any
T_{1}<T
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, such that if
\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{X}<\delta
, then
\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})
exists up to
T_{1}
and

\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,T_{1}],X)}<\epsilon.

Now we can present our main result in this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
1<p,r<\infty
. Assume that
u_{0}\in F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s>\max\{\frac{3}{2},1+\frac{d}{p}\}
. Then there exists some time
T>0
such that

1.

system (
1.1
) has a solution
u\in E^{s}_{p,r}(T):=\mathcal{C}([0,T];F^{s}_{p,r})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}{([0,T];F^{s-1}_{p,r})}
;

2.

the solutions of (
1.1
) are unique;

3.

the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
is continuous from any bounded
subset of
u_{0}\in F^{s}_{p,r}
into
\mathcal{C}([0,T],F^{s}_{p,r})
;

4.

the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
is not uniformly continuous from any bounded subset of
u_{0}\in F^{s}_{p,r}
into
\mathcal{C}([0,T],F^{s}_{p,r})
.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
33
]
, the existence and uniqueness of the CH equation have been studied well, while the continuous and non-uniform continuous dependence with respect to initial data seem empty. Note that when
d=1
, the Euler-Poincaré equations are just the Camassa-Holm equations. Therefore, our result in Theorem
1.1
extend and improve the corresponding result in
[
33
]
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some basic facts about Littlewood-Paley decomposition, nonhomogeneous Besov spaces, nonhomogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, and their useful properties. Also, we recall the transport equation theory on Besov spaces and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, which will be used in the sequel. For more details, the readers may consult monographs
[
1
,
30
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

There exists a couple of smooth functions
(\chi,\varphi)
valued in
[0,1]
, such that
\chi
is supported in the ball
\mathcal{B}\triangleq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
, and
\varphi
is supported in the ring
\mathcal{C}\triangleq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
. Moreover,

\forall\,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\ \chi(\xi)+{\sum\limits_{q\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-q}\xi)}=1,

\textrm{supp}\,\ \varphi(2^{-q}\cdot)\cap\textrm{supp}\,\ \varphi(2^{-p}\cdot)=\emptyset,\,\ if\,\ |q-p|\geq 2,

\textrm{supp}\,\ \chi(\cdot)\cap\textrm{supp}\,\ \varphi(2^{-q}\cdot)=\emptyset,\,\ if\,\ q\geq 1.

Then for all
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, we can define the nonhomogeneous dyadic blocks as follows. Let

\Delta_{q}{u}\triangleq 0,\,\ if\,\ q\leq-2,\quad\Delta_{-1}{u}\triangleq\chi(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),

\Delta_{q}{u}\triangleq\varphi(2^{-q}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi(2^{-q}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u),\,\ if\,\ q\geq 0.

Hence,
u={\sum\limits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{q}{u}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called the nonhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley decomposition of
u
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
s>0,1<p,q\leq\infty
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\triangleq\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):||f||_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\triangleq\big|\big|(2^{qs}||\Delta_{q}{f}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})_{q\geq-1}\big|\big|_{\ell^{q}}<\infty\},

and
B^{\infty}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\triangleq\cap_{s\in\mathbb{R}}B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Definition 2.2

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
s>0,1<p,r<\infty
. The nonhomogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin space
F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\triangleq\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+||f||_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\},

with

||f||_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\triangleq\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jsr}\left|\Delta_{j}f\right|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
s,t>0,1<p,r<\infty
and
1<q\leq\infty
. It should be emphasized that the following embedding will be often used implicity:

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{t}_{p,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{for}\;s>t\quad\text{or}\quad s=t,1<q\leq q^{\prime}\leq\infty,

\displaystyle F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow F^{t}_{p,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{for}\;s>t\quad\text{or}\quad s=t,1<r\leq r^{\prime}<\infty,

\displaystyle F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Lemma 2.1

(
[
1
,
30
]
)
.

Let
s>\frac{1}{p}
and
(p,r,q)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty]
. Then, there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle||f||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C||f||_{B^{s}_{p,q}},\qquad||f||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C||f||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
1
,
30
]
)
.

Let
s>0
and
(p,r,q)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty]
. There exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle||(1-\Delta)^{-1}f||_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq||f||_{F^{s-2}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\qquad||\nabla f||_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq||f||_{F^{s+1}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle||(1-\Delta)^{-1}f||_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq||f||_{B^{s-2}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\qquad||\nabla f||_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq||f||_{B^{s+1}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
1
,
5
]
)
.

Let
s>0
and
(p,r,q)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty]
. There exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{{B}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{{F}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|f\|_{{F}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{{F}_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{{F}_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|f\|_{{F}_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

Lemma 2.4

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(p,q)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty]
and
s>\max\big\{1+\frac{d}{p},\frac{3}{2}\big\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
s>0
and
(p,q)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty]
. Assume that
f_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,q}
,
g\in L^{1}(0,T;B^{s}_{p,q})
. If
f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{s}_{p,q})\bigcap\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}})
solves the following linear transport equation:

\quad\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla f=g,\quad f|_{t=0}=f_{0},

(2.3)

then there exists a constant
C=C(d,p,r,s)
such that the following statement holds:

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\|f(\tau)\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}\leq Ce^{CV(t)}\Big(\|f_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|g(\tau)\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big),

(2.4)

with

\displaystyle V(t)=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{B^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,\infty}\cap L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau,&\quad\mathrm{if}\;s<1+\frac{d}{p},\\
\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}\mathrm{d}\tau,&\quad\mathrm{if}\;s=1+\frac{d}{p},\\
\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,q}}\mathrm{d}\tau,&\quad\mathrm{if}\;s>1+\frac{d}{p}.\end{cases}

If
f=v
, the estimate (
2.4
) holds with

V(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}s.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
26
]
)
.

Let
s>0
and
(p,r)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty)
. Assume that
f_{0}\in F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
g\in L^{1}([0,T];F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. If
f\in L^{\infty}([0,T];F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\bigcap\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
solves the linear transport equation (
2.3
), then there exists a constant
C=C(d,p,r,s)
, such that

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{CV(t)}\|f_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(\tau)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C\int^{t}_{0}\left(\|f(\tau)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{F^{s-1}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla f(\tau)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau,

or

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{CV(t)}\|f_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(\tau)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C\int^{t}_{0}\left(\|f(\tau)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|f(\tau)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau,

here
V(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau
. Moreover, if
s>1+\frac{d}{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{{F}^{s}_{p,r}}\leq\left(\|f_{0}\|_{{F}^{s}_{p,r}}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-C\widetilde{V}(\tau)}\|g(\tau)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)e^{C\widetilde{V}(t)},

here
\widetilde{V}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\|v\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau
.

3
Local well-posedness and no-uniform dependence

This section is devoted to establishing the local well-posedness and no-uniform dependence of solutions for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. We divide the proof of Theorem
1.1
into six steps.

Step 1. Construction of the approximate system

Starting from
u^{0}\triangleq 0
, we define by induction a sequence
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of smooth functions by solving the following linear system:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u^{n+1}+u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}=Q(u^{n},u^{n})+R(u^{n},u^{n}),\\
u^{n+1}(0,x)=S_{n+1}u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(3.5)

Since
u_{0}\in F^{s}_{p,r}
, then all initial data
S_{n+1}u_{0}\in F^{\infty}_{p,r}
. By induction, for every
n\geq 1
and
T>0
, we can deduce from Lemma
2.6
that the system (
3.5
) has a unique solution
u^{n}
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];F^{\infty}_{p,r})
.

Step 2. Uniform estimates to the approximate solutions

By Lemma (
2.1
)-
2.3
and (
1.2
), we get

||Q(u^{n},u^{n})||_{F^{s}_{p,r}}+||R(u^{n},u^{n})||_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C||u^{n}||^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}.

(3.6)

Applying Lemma
2.6
to the system (
3.5
), we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{CU^{n}(t)}\left(\|S_{n+1}u_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}+C\int^{t}_{0}e^{-CU^{n}(\tau)}\|Q(u^{n},u^{n}),R(u^{n},u^{n})\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CU^{n}(t)}\|u_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}+C\int^{t}_{0}e^{CU^{n}(t)-CU^{n}(\tau)}\|u^{n}(\tau)\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau,

(3.7)

where
U^{n}(t)\triangleq\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{n}(\tau)\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau
and
C\geq 1
. We now fix a
T>0
such that
2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}T<1
. By induction, we gain

\displaystyle\|u^{n}(t)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\leq\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}}{{1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}t}},~~~\forall t\in[0,T],~~~\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

In fact, this is obvious for
n=0
since
u^{0}=0
. Suppose that it is valid for
n\geq 1
, we shall prove it holds for
n+1
.
Direct computations gives that

\displaystyle CU^{n}(t)-CU^{n}(t^{\prime})=C\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}\|u^{n}(\tau)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}\frac{C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\tau}\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}t^{\prime}}{1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}t}\right),

then from (
3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{{}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}}\left(1+\int^{t}_{0}\left(1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}t^{\prime}\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}}{{1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}t}}\leq\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}}{{1-2C^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{F^{s}_{p,r}}T}}.

Therefore,
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];F^{s}_{p,r})
. This entails that
u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];F^{s-1}_{p,r}).
As the right-hand side of (
3
) is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];F^{s}_{p,r}),
we can conclude that
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];F^{s}_{p,r})
.

Step 3. Convergence of the approximate solutions

Note that
F^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow B^{s}_{p,\infty}
. Now we claim that
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,\infty})
. Indeed, for all
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(\partial_{t}+(u^{n+m})\cdot\nabla\right)(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})=-(u^{m+n}-u^{n})\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}+P(u^{n+m},u^{n+m})-{P}(u^{n},u^{n}),\\
(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})|_{t=0}=(S_{n+m+1}-S_{n+1})u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

By the definition of
S_{n}
, we get

(S_{n+m+1}-S_{n+1})u_{0}(x)=\sum\limits_{q=n+1}^{n+m}\Delta_{q}u_{0}(x),

which leads to

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{q=n+1}^{n+m}\Delta_{q}u_{0}\right\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}

By Lemmas

-
2.4

\displaystyle||P(u^{n+m},u^{n+m})-P(u^{n},u^{n})||_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\leq C||u^{n+m}-u^{n}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}(||u^{n+m}||_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+||u^{n}||_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}),

(3.8)

According to Lemma

3.9

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CU^{n+m}(t)}\Big(\|(S_{n+m+1}-S_{n+1})u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-CU^{n+m}(\tau)}\|(u^{n+m}-u^{n})(\tau)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|(u^{n},u^{n+1},u^{n+m},u^{n+m+1})(\tau)\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big).

\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,\infty})

3.21
) implies

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{n}_{0})\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\geq ct||g_{n}\cdot\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n}||_{F^{s}_{p,r}}-Ct^{2}-C2^{-\frac{1}{2}n}.

(3.22)

Note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \mathcal{F}({g_{n}\cdot\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n}})\subset\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \frac{17}{12}2^{n}-1\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{17}{12}2^{n}+1\Big\}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(g_{n}\cdot\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n})=\begin{cases}g_{n}\cdot\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n},\quad j=n,\\
0,\qquad j\neq n.\end{cases}

Consequently,

\displaystyle||g_{n}\cdot\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n}||_{F^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle=2^{ns}||g_{n}\cdot\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n}||_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq||\phi^{2}(x_{1})\cos(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x_{1})\phi^{2}(x_{2})\cdots\phi^{2}(x_{d})||_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\quad-2^{-n}||\phi(x_{1})\phi^{\prime}(x_{1})\sin(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x_{1})\phi^{2}(x_{2})\cdots\phi^{2}(x_{d})||_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{17}{12}||\phi^{2}(x_{1})\cos(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x_{1})||_{L^{p}}||\phi||^{2(d-1)}_{L^{2p}}-C2^{-n}.

(3.23)

Using Riemann-Lebesgue’s lemma, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{n}\partial_{x_{1}}f_{n}\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\geq c,

which along with (
3.22
) yields

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{n}_{0})\|_{F^{s}_{p,r}}\gtrsim t\quad\text{for}\ t\ \text{small enough}.

This completes the proof of non-uniform continuous dependence.

Hanson-Wright Inequality for Random Tensors under Einstein Product
Shih Yu Chang

Abstract
The Hanson-Wright inequality is an upper bound for tails of real quadratic forms in independent subgaussian random variables. In this work, we extend the Hanson-Wright inequality for the maximum eigenvalue of the quadratic sum of random Hermitian tensors under Einstein product. We first prove Weyl inequality for tensors under Einstein product and apply this fact to separate the quadratic form of random Hermitian tensors into diagonal sum and coupling (non-diagonal) sum parts. For the diagonal part, we can apply Bernstein inequality to bound the tail probability of the maximum eigenvalue of the sum of independent random Hermitian tensors directly. For coupling sum part, we have to apply decoupling method first, i.e., decoupling inequality to bound expressions with dependent random Hermitian tensors with independent random Hermitian tensors, before applying Bernstein inequality again to bound the tail probability of the maximum eigenvalue of the coupling sum of independent random Hermitian tensors. Finally, the Hanson-Wright inequality for the maximum eigenvalue of the quadratic sum of random Hermitian tensors under Einstein product can be obtained by the combination of the bound from the diagonal sum part and the bound from the coupling (non-diagonal) sum part. In Appendix of this work, we also include the Hanson-Wright inequality under T-product tensor, which can be derived by the same method of establishing the Hanson-Wright inequality under Einstein product except changing the rule of tensors product operation.

). The other characterization by
=_{2}
in Eq. (
22
) can be proved similar. For every
\mathcal{X}\in S
with
\dim(\mathrm{S})=k
spanned by
\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{k}
(unitary orthogonal tensors), we can write
\mathcal{X}=\sum\limits^{k}_{j=1}c_{j}\mathcal{V}_{j}
. To show that the value
\lambda_{k}
is achievable, note that

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}{\sum\limits^{k}_{j=1}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}\geq\frac{\sum\limits^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{k}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}{\sum\limits^{k}_{j=1}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}=\lambda_{k}.

(23)

To verify that this is the maximum, since
T
has dimension
\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}-k+1
spanned by
\mathcal{V}_{k},\mathcal{V}_{k+1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}
, we have

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S\cap T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}.

(24)

Any such
\mathcal{X}\in S\cap T
can be expressed as
\mathcal{X}=\sum\limits^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}_{j=k}c_{j}\mathcal{V}_{j}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}_{j=k}\lambda_{j}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}{\sum\limits^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}_{j=k}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}\leq\frac{\sum\limits^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}_{j=k}\lambda_{k}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}{\sum\limits^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}_{j=k}c_{j}^{\ast}c_{j}}=\lambda_{k}.

(25)

Therefore, for all subspaces
S
of dimensions
k
, we have
\min\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}\leq\lambda_{k}
.

\hfill\Box

We then can apply Theorem
1
to prove Weyl inequality for Hermitian tensors
under Einstein product.

Theorem 2

Suppose
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
are Hermitian tensors with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}
and

\epsilon_{1}\geq\epsilon_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\epsilon_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}
, respectively. Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}
with eigenvalues
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}
. We then have:

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}+\epsilon_{1}\geq_{1}\mu_{k}\geq_{2}\lambda_{k}+\epsilon_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}.

(26)

Proof:

Due to Theorem
1
, we will prove the inequality
\geq_{1}
by
\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\\
\dim(T)=\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}-k+1\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}
only since the inequality
\geq_{2}
can be proved similarly from
\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\\
\dim(\mathrm{S})=k\end{subarray}}\min\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}
.

Because we have

\displaystyle\mu_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\\
\dim(T)=\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}-k+1\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\left(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\right)\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\\
\dim(T)=\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}-k+1\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\\
\dim(T)=\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}-k+1\end{subarray}}\left(\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}+\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\\
\dim(T)=\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}-k+1\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}+\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\\
\dim(T)=\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}+\epsilon_{1}.

Then, this theorem is proved.

\hfill\Box

2.2
Tensor Bernstein Bounds

Tensor Bernstein inequality is an important inequality to bound the sum of indepdenent, bounded random tensors by restricting the range of the maximum eigenvalue of each random tensors. Following theorem is proved at Theorem 6.2 in

Theorem 3
(Bounded
\lambda_{\max}

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}=0\mbox{\penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ }\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\leq T\mbox{\penalty\ \penalty\ almost surely.}

(28)

Define the total varaince
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}^{2}_{i}\right)\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-\theta^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta/3}\right);

(29)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-3\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{for $\theta\leq\sigma^{2}/T$};

(30)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-3\theta}{8T}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{for $\theta\geq\sigma^{2}/T$}.

(31)

We define a vector of random tensors

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{X}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{X}_{1}\\

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}_{i,j}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{j}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}_{i,i}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}_{i,i}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}_{i,i}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\right)

6
Conclusion

In this work, we generalize the Hanson-Wright inequality from the quadratic forms
in independent subgaussian random variables to the random Hermitian tensors. First, we apply Weyl inequality for tensors under the Einstein product and apply this fact to separate the quadratic form of random Hermitian tensors into the diagonal sum and the coupling (non-diagonal) sum parts. Second, we apply decoupling inequality to bound expressions with dependent random Hermitian tensors with independent random Hermitian tensors. Finally, the Hanson-Wright inequality can be obtained by utilizing Bernstein inequality to the diagonal sum part and the coupling sum part, respectively.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mbox{Diag}\left(\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}:i\in\{1,\cdots,p\}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}

k_{i}

is reduced as

\lambda_{\tilde{k}}
. The other is similar. Let
S_{i}

\lambda_{\tilde{k}}

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}}{\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\sum\limits^{k_{i}}_{j=1}\lambda_{i,j}c_{i,j}^{\ast}c_{i,j}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\sum\limits^{k_{i}}_{j=1}c_{i,j}^{\ast}c_{i,j}}

T_{\tilde{i}}

T

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S\cap T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}.

Any such

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}}{\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\sum\limits^{m}_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=k_{i}+1;i\neq\tilde{i}\\

(80)

Appendix B
Weyl Inequality for Symmetric T-product Tensors

We then can apply Theorem
5
to prove Weyl inequality for symmetric T-product tensors.

Theorem 6

Suppose
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m\times p}
are symmetric tensors with T-eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{mp}
and

, respectively. Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}
with T-eigenvalues
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{mp}
. We then have:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\tilde{k}}+\epsilon_{1}\geq_{1}\mu_{\tilde{k}}\geq_{2}\lambda_{\tilde{k}}+\epsilon_{mp},

(81)

where
1\leq\tilde{k}\leq mp
, where
\tilde{k}
is associated to
p
non-negative integers
k_{1},\cdots,k_{p}
between
0
and
m
inclusive.

Proof:

Due to Theorem
5
, we will prove the inequality
\geq_{1}
only based on

\displaystyle\lambda_{\tilde{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times p}\\
\dim(T)=\{m-k_{1},\cdots,m-k_{\tilde{i}-1},m-k_{\tilde{i}}+1,m-k_{\tilde{i}+1},\cdots,m-k_{p}\}\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle},

(82)

since the inequality
\geq_{2}
can be proved similarly from
\lambda_{\tilde{k}}=\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times p}\\
\dim(\mathrm{S})=\{k_{1},\cdots,k_{p}\}\end{subarray}}\min\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}
.

Because we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\tilde{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times p}\\
\dim(T)=\{m-k_{1},\cdots,m-k_{\tilde{i}-1},m-k_{\tilde{i}}+1,m-k_{\tilde{i}+1},\cdots,m-k_{p}\}\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times p}\\
\dim(T)=\{m-k_{1},\cdots,m-k_{\tilde{i}-1},m-k_{\tilde{i}}+1,m-k_{\tilde{i}+1},\cdots,m-k_{p}\}\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times p}\\

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times p}\\
\dim(T)=\{m-k_{1},\cdots,m-k_{\tilde{i}-1},m-k_{\tilde{i}}+1,m-k_{\tilde{i}+1},\cdots,m-k_{p}\}\end{subarray}}\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A}\star\mathcal{X}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\rangle}

\displaystyle\lambda_{\tilde{k}}+\epsilon_{1}.

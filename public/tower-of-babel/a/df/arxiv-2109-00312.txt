Convergence of Hermitian manifolds and the Type IIB flow


Abstract
The Type IIB flow is a flow of conformally balanced complex manifolds introduced by Phong, Picard, and Zhang, about whose singularities little is as yet known. We formulate convergence criteria for the Gromov-Cheeger-Hamilton convergence of sequences of Hermitian manifolds, and apply them to precompactness theorems and the existence of singularity models for the Type IIB flow, in analogy with Hamilton’s classic compactness theorems and classification of singularities for the Ricci flow.

Introduction

The earliest, and still most influential, geometric structure which emerged as a candidate for vacuum configuration of unified string theories was that of a Calabi-Yau manifold, equipped with a holomorphic vector bundle admitting a Hermitian-Einstein metric. This was proposed by Candelas, Horowitz, Strominger, and Witten
[
6
]
for the heterotic string, and it could rely on the fundamental existence theorems for such structures proved earlier by Yau
[
36
]
and Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
9
,
34
]
. But other geometric structures have emerged since, motivated by the other string theories, for which analogous existence theorems are not yet available. Such theorems can be expected to require the full theory of non-linear partial differential equations and, as advocated in
[
25
,
24
]
, notably the theory of geometric flows. In particular, flow approaches have been proposed in
[
25
]
for the Hull-Strominger system in heterotic string theory, in
[
24
,
19
]
for the Type IIB string, in
[
20
]
for the Type IIA string, and in
[
12
]
for
11
-dimensional supergravity.

These new flows share main features with the Ricci flow, with typically some additional structure, but they remain more complicated than, say, the Kähler-Ricci flow. A few known cases of convergence are in
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, but otherwise very little is known as yet about their possible singularities. It is a natural first step to establish the existence of singularity models, in analogy with the case of the Ricci flow. In this paper, we carry this out for the Type IIB flow.

We state now our main results, The definitions of all relevant notions are given in §2 below. The first two results are analogues for non-Kähler Calabi-Yau manifolds and for the Type IIB flow of Hamilton’s compactness theorems for the Ricci flow on Riemannian manifolds
[
16
]
:

Preliminaries

We begin by describing our notations and conventions.
Let
(M,g,J)
be a Hermitian manifold, and
\omega
be the Kähler form:,
\omega(X,Y)=g(X,JY)
. In local holomorphic coordinates, we write

\omega=ig_{j\bar{k}}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}

Let
V=V^{j}\partial_{j}
be a section of
T^{1,0}M
. The Chern connection
{\nabla}
is given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}V^{k}=\partial_{j}V^{k}+\Gamma^{k}_{js}V^{s},\quad\nabla_{\bar{j}}V^{k}=\partial_{\bar{j}}V^{k},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{k}_{js}=g^{k\bar{p}}\partial_{j}g_{s\bar{p}}.

(2.1)

The curvature tensor
Rm=R_{\bar{k}j}{}^{p}{}_{q}
is then given by

\displaystyle R_{\bar{k}j}{}^{p}{}_{q}=-\partial_{\bar{k}}\Gamma^{p}_{jq}

(2.2)

and the first and second Chern-Ricci curvatures are defined by

\displaystyle R_{\bar{k}j}=R_{\bar{k}j}{}^{p}{}_{p}=-\partial_{\bar{k}}\partial_{j}\,{\rm log}\,{\rm det}(g)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\bar{p}q}=g_{\bar{p}\ell}g^{j\bar{k}}R_{\bar{k}j}{}^{\ell}{}_{q}.

(2.3)

The torsion is defined by

\displaystyle T=i\partial\omega,~\bar{T}=-i\bar{\partial}\omega.

(2.4)

If we express
T
in components as
T={1\over 2}T_{\bar{l}kj}dz^{j}\wedge dz^{k}\wedge d\bar{z}^{l}
,
then
T_{\bar{l}kj}=\partial_{k}g_{\bar{l}j}-\partial_{j}g_{\bar{l}j}
.
We also define the contracted torsion
\tau=\Lambda i\partial\omega
which is a
(1,0)
-form.
In coordinates,
\tau=\tau_{j}dz^{j}
, with
\tau_{j}=g^{k\bar{l}}T_{\bar{l}kj}
.

2.1
Connections on Hermitian manifolds

For our purposes, the Chern connection may not be the most convenient Hermitian connection to use. The reason is that we would like to make use of convergence theorems of Riemannian manifolds. These require lower bounds on the injectivity radius with respect to the Levi-Civita connection, which is different from the injectivity radius defined with respect to the Chern connection.

Recall that, given a complex structure
J
and a Hermitian metric on the manifold, the Chen connection is only one connection in a whole line of Hermitian connections
\nabla^{t}
, known as the Gauduchon line, and given explicitly by
[
15
,
2
]
,

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{s}_{X}Y,Z)=g(\nabla^{g}_{X}Y,Z)+\frac{1-t}{4}d^{c}\omega(X,Y,Z)+\frac{1+t}{4}d\omega(JX,Y,Z).

(2.5)

Here
\nabla^{g}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection. We shall however be interested in the Bismut connection
{\nabla}^{+}
, which corresponds to
t=-1
. Explicitly, it is given by

\displaystyle\nabla^{+}_{j}V^{k}=\partial_{j}V^{k}+\Gamma^{k}_{mj}V^{m}=\nabla_{j}V^{k}+T^{k}_{mj}V^{m}

\displaystyle\nabla^{+}_{\bar{j}}V^{k}=\partial_{\bar{j}}V^{k}+g^{k\bar{m}}\bar{T}_{p\bar{j}\bar{m}}V^{p}.

(2.6)

Using the Chern and the Bismut connections as reference points, one can write the Gauduchon line as follows:

\displaystyle{\nabla}^{t}=(1-\kappa){\nabla}+\kappa{\nabla}^{+},\qquad\kappa={1-t\over 2}.

(2.7)

We are interested in the Bismut connection because of its following property:

Lemma 1

The notions of geodesic with respect to the Levi-Civita connection and with respect to the Bismut connection coincide. In particular, both connections define the same notion of injectivity radius.

Proof.
By definition, the geodesics for the Bismut connection are those curves
x(t)
, which satisfy the equation
\nabla^{+}_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}=0
. For any vector field
Z
we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{+}_{\dot{x}}\dot{x},Z)=g(\nabla^{g}_{\dot{x}}\dot{x},Z)+\frac{1}{2}d^{c}\omega(\dot{x},\dot{x},Z).

(2.8)

But
d^{c}\omega(\dot{x},\dot{x},Z)=(-Jd\omega)(\dot{x},\dot{x},Z)=0.

Hence
\nabla^{+}_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}=0
iff
\nabla^{g}_{\dot{x}}\dot{x}=0.

The lemma is proved. Q.E.D.

In view of this lemma, we can now introduce a notion of bounded Hermitian geometry formulated entirely in terms of the Bismut connection:

Definition 1

Let
\{(M_{j},g_{j},J_{j})\}_{j\in\bf N}
be a sequence of complete Hermitian manifolds. Denote by
Rm^{+}_{j}
, and
T^{+}_{j}
the curvature and torsion of the Bismut connection on
(M_{j},g_{j},J_{j})
. We say that this sequence has bounded Hermitian geometry, if the following holds:

(1)
There is a constant
\iota_{0}>0
, such that the injectivity radii of these manifolds are bounded below by
\iota_{0}
;

(2)
There are constants
C_{m}>0
, independent on
j
, such that

\displaystyle|({\nabla}^{+})^{m}Rm^{+}|_{g_{j}}\leq C_{m},\qquad|({\nabla}^{+})^{m}T^{+}|_{g_{j}}\leq C_{m}

(2.9)

for any
m\in\bf N
.

2.2
Basic facts about Hermitian Calabi-Yau manifolds

In this section, we collect some facts about the non-Kähler Calabi-Yau manifolds. Recall that a
Hermitian Calabi-Yau manifold is a 4-tuple
(M,g,J,\Omega)
, where
(M,g,J)
is a Hermitian manifold, and
\Omega
is a nonvanishing
(n,0)
-form.

A Hermitian Calabi-Yau manifold does not always possess a Kähler metric. There are many examples of such manifolds: Calabi-Yau manifolds obtained from the conifold transition (see
[
8
]
), hyperkähler fibrations over Riemannian surfaces (
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
), factors of Lie groups, etc. A less stringent notion than Kähler is that of balanced metric, introduced by Michelson

[
23
]
. These are metrics satisfying
d\omega^{n-1}=0
. Closely related is the notion of conformally balanced metric, defined by the condition

d(\|\Omega\|_{\omega}\omega^{n-1})=0
. The conformally balanced condition was recognized by Li and Yau
[
21
]
as one of the equations in the Hull-Strominger system in string theory. In terms of the metric
\eta=\|\Omega\|\omega
, it can also be expressed as
d(\|\Omega\|^{2}\eta^{n-1})=0
.

2.3
Pointed Gromov-Cheeger-Hamilton convergence

We recall the notion of convergence due to Hamilton
[
16
]
, which is an adaptation of a notion of
C^{\infty}
convergence of manifolds due to Gromov and Cheeger:

Definition 2

Let
\{(M_{j},g_{j},O_{j})\}
be a sequence of complete Riemannian manifolds, where
O_{j}\in M_{j}
is a point.
We say that this sequence converges to the complete pointed manifold
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},O_{\infty},q_{\infty})
in
C^{k}
-sense, if there is a sequence of open sets
\{U_{j}\}
in
M_{\infty}
with the following properties:

(1)
The sequence
\{U_{j}\}
is an exhaustion of
M_{\infty}
;

(2)
There are diffeomorphisms
\Phi_{j}:U_{j}\rightarrow V_{j}\subset M_{j}
such that
\Phi_{j}(O_{\infty})=O_{j}
and the sequence of metrics
\{\Phi_{j}^{*}g_{j}\}
converges to
g_{\infty}
uniformly in
C^{k}
-topology on compact subsets in
U_{j}
.

The following theorem of Hamilton
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
gives an important set of conditions implying the existence of a convergent subsequence:

Theorem 5

Let
\{M_{j},g_{j},O_{j}\}
be a sequence of complete pointed Riemannian manifolds and
\nabla_{j}
be the Levi-Civita connection on
(M_{j},g_{j})
. Assume that the following holds uniformly in
j
:

(1)
There are constants
C_{k}>0
, such that
|\nabla^{k}_{j}Rm_{j}|\leq C_{k}
;

(2)
There is a positive uniform lower bound
\iota_{0}
for the injectivity radii of
M_{j}
at
O_{j}
,
inj(M_{j},O_{j})\geq\iota_{0}
.

Then there is a subsequence of the initial sequence, which converges to a complete pointed Riemannian manifold
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},O_{\infty})
.

2.4
Curvature estimates

In this section, we fix a smooth compact manifold
M
with metric
g_{ij}
. Let
E
be a smooth vector bundle over
M
equipped with a metric
H_{\alpha\beta}
. Let
A_{1},A_{2}
be two connections on
E
, and
F_{1},F_{2}
be their curvatures. We are interested in obtaining bounds for the curvature of one of them from bounds for the curvature of the other.

Let
C=A_{1}-A_{2}
, which is a
End(E)
-valued 1-form. We have

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dA_{1}+F_{1}\wedge A_{1}=dA_{2}+A_{2}\wedge A_{2}+dC+[A_{2},C]+C\wedge C

(2.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{2}+d_{A_{2}}C+C\wedge C.

Here
d_{A_{2}}C
is the covariant derivative with respect to
A_{2}
. Let
x^{1},\dots,x^{n}
be a system of local coordinates on
M
.
Denote by
\nabla^{j}
the covariant derivative with respect to
A_{j}
. Then for any section
\psi
of
E
we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{j}_{a}\psi=\partial_{a}\psi+(A_{j})_{a}\psi.

(2.11)

For our endomorphism-valued 1-form
C=C_{a}dx^{a}
we obtain the following formula:

\displaystyle\nabla^{j}_{a}C_{b}=\partial_{j}C_{b}+[(A_{j})_{a},C_{b}].

(2.12)

In this notation,
d_{A_{j}}C=(\nabla^{j}_{a}C_{b}-\nabla^{j}_{b}C_{a})dx^{a}\wedge dx^{b}
. Let
F_{j}=(F_{j})_{ab}dx^{a}\wedge dx^{b}
for
j=1,2
. Combining all this, we obtain the following formula:

\displaystyle(F_{1})_{ab}=(F_{2})_{ab}+(\nabla^{2}_{a}C_{b}-\nabla^{2}_{b}C_{a})+[C_{a},C_{b}].

(2.13)

Assume now that we have bounds for all covariant derivatives of
F_{2}
and
C
with respect to
\nabla^{2}
. More presicely, for any natural
p
, there are constants
C_{p}>0
, such that
|(\nabla^{2})^{p}F_{2}|<C_{p}
and
|(\nabla^{2})^{p}C|<C_{p}
. We want to establish the estimates of
|(\nabla^{1})^{p}F_{1}|
in terms of
|(\nabla^{2})^{q}C|
and
|(\nabla^{2})^{p}F_{2}|
.

In order to do this, we recall some basic facts about the norms of tensors. First of all, for any two
E
-valued tensors
T_{1},T_{2}
on
M
, we have a pointwise inequality:

\displaystyle|T_{1}*T_{2}|\leq C_{1}|T_{1}||T_{2}|

(2.14)

where
\star
denotes any bilinear operation involving the metrics
g_{ij}
and
H_{\alpha\beta}
, and the constant
K_{1}=K_{1}(dim(M),rk(E))
is a universal constant depends only on the dimension
n
of

, the rank of
E
, and the type of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, but independent of
g_{ij}
and

. This can be proven by Linear Algebra.

Next, for any tensor
T
we have the following

\displaystyle|(\nabla^{2}+C)T|\leq|\nabla^{2}T|+|CT|\leq|\nabla^{2}T|+C_{1}|C||T|.

(2.15)

Iterating this process, we obtain the following inequality:

\displaystyle|(\nabla^{2}+C)^{p}T|\leq|(\nabla^{2})^{p}T|+\Sigma_{k=0}^{p-1}|C*\dots*(\nabla^{2})^{k}(C*\dots*C*T)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(\nabla^{2})^{p}T|+K_{p}\Sigma_{k_{1}+\dots+k_{p}=p-1}|(\nabla^{2})^{k_{1}}C|\dots|(\nabla^{2})^{k_{p-1}}C||(\nabla^{2})^{k_{p}}T|,

(2.16)

where
K_{p}
are universal, and independent of the metrics
g_{ij}
and
H_{\alpha\beta}

Let
M
be a smooth manifold, E is a vector bundle over

and independent on the metrics

and

,

and

\displaystyle g(C(X,Y),Z)

\displaystyle|({\nabla}^{g})^{m}J|_{g}\leq P_{m}\sum_{k_{1}+\dots+k_{m-1}=m-1}|{\nabla}^{k_{1}}T|_{g}\dots|{\nabla}^{k_{m-1}}T|_{g}|J|^{q(m)}_{g},

Let
\{(M_{j},g_{j}(t),J_{j})\}_{j\in\bf N}

, independent of

|({\nabla}^{g_{j}})^{m}Rm_{g_{j}}|_{g_{j}}\leq K_{m}
.

In general, the Bismut connection and the Levi-Civita connection are related by the following formula:

\displaystyle{\nabla}^{+}={\nabla}^{g}+{1\over 2}g^{-1}H

(2.22)

and
H=d^{c}\omega
. On the other hand,

. Applying to the case at hand, and omitting the dependence on

The desired statement follows now from Lemma

and

Lemma 6

Let
\{(M_{j},g_{j}(t),J_{j})\}_{j\in\bf N}
be a sequence of Hermitian manifolds, and

Proof.

Lemma 7

Let

2.5
The Type IIB flow

Let
M
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
n
with trivial canonical bundle
K_{M}={\rm det}(T^{(1,0)}M)^{*}
, and let
\Omega
be a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
(n,0)
-form.
The Type IIB flow is the flow of
(n-1,n-1)
-forms defined as follows
[
24

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\|\Omega\|_{\eta}\eta^{n-1})=i\partial\overline{\partial}\eta^{n-2}.

(2.26)

with conformally balanced intiial data
\omega_{0}
.
It can also be viewed as a special case with vanishing slope parameter
\alpha^{\prime}
of the Anomaly flow introduced in
[
25
]
. If we define the metric
\omega
by
\omega=\|\Omega\|_{\eta}\eta=ig_{j\bar{k}}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}
, then the above flow for
\eta
is equivalent to the following flow for
\omega
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\|\Omega\|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n-1})=i\partial\overline{\partial}(\|\Omega\|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n-2})

(2.27)

A motivating property for the Type IIB flow is the fact that it preserves the conformally balanced property of the initial data. For estimates, it is useful to express it as follows as a flow of
(1,1)
-forms
(see
[
18
]
for the detailed derivation)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{\bar{k}j}=-\tilde{R}_{\bar{k}j}-{1\over 2}T_{\bar{k}pq}\bar{T}_{j}^{~pq}

(2.28)

where
\tilde{R}_{j\bar{k}}=g_{s\bar{k}}g^{p\bar{q}}R^{s}_{~j\bar{q}p}
is the second Chern-Ricci curvature of the metric.
The Type IIB flow reduces to the Kähler-Ricci flow on Kähler manifolds and one can think about it as a particular generalization of the Kähler-Ricci flow to non-Kähler manifolds

1
1

1

Other generalizations have been considered by Streets and Tian
[
33
]
and Ustinovsky
[
35
]
.
.
In particular, the Type IIB flow shares many properties with the Kähler-Ricci flow.
As described in Theorem
4
, we can provide yet another formulation of the Type IIB flow in the spirit of Laplacian flows:

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Recall that for any (2,1)-form
\psi
the usual operator
\partial^{\dagger}
acts as follows (see
[
18
, Appendix D]
):

\displaystyle(\partial^{\dagger}\psi)_{\bar{\alpha}\beta}=-g^{\gamma\bar{j}}{\nabla}_{\bar{j}}\psi_{\bar{\alpha}\beta\gamma}+g^{\gamma\bar{j}}\bar{\tau}_{\bar{j}}\psi_{\bar{\alpha}\beta\gamma}-{1\over 2}\bar{T}_{\beta\bar{j}\bar{m}}\psi_{\bar{\alpha}\gamma\delta}g^{\gamma\bar{j}}g^{\delta\bar{m}}

(2.29)

The second summand appears from the integration by parts formula for the divergence of the Chern connection:

\displaystyle\int_{M}\nabla_{j}V^{j}\omega^{n}=\int_{M}\tau_{j}V^{j}\omega^{n}

(2.30)

for any vector field
V^{j}
. On the other hand, by the conformally balanced condition, we have
\nabla_{\|}\Omega\|^{2}_{\omega}=\|\Omega\|^{2}_{\omega}\tau_{j}
(see
[
26
, Lemma 4]
). This implies

\displaystyle\int_{M}\nabla_{j}V^{j}i^{n^{2}}\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}=\int_{M}(\nabla_{j}V^{j})||\Omega||^{2}_{\omega}{\omega^{n}\over n!}=0.

(2.31)

Hence, by the same argument as in
[
18
, Appendix D]
, we can show that for any (2,1)-form
\psi
we have

\displaystyle(\partial^{\dagger}_{\omega,\Omega}\psi)_{\bar{\alpha}\beta}=-g^{\gamma\bar{j}}{\nabla}_{\bar{j}}\psi_{\bar{\alpha}\beta\gamma}-{1\over 2}\bar{T}_{\beta\bar{j}\bar{m}}\psi_{\bar{\alpha}\gamma\delta}g^{\gamma\bar{j}}g^{\delta\bar{m}}.

(2.32)

In fact,
\partial^{\dagger}_{\omega,\Omega}=\partial^{\dagger}-\iota_{\tau}
, where
\iota_{\tau}
is the contraction with the vector field
g^{j\bar{k}}\bar{\tau}_{k}
.
The equivalence between the conformally balanced condition and the condition
\partial_{\omega,\Omega}^{\dagger}\omega=0
follows.
Next, let
\psi=T=i\partial\omega
. Then

\displaystyle(\partial^{\dagger}_{\omega,\Omega}T)_{\bar{\alpha}\beta}=-{\nabla}^{\gamma}T_{\bar{\alpha}\beta\gamma}-{1\over 2}\bar{T}_{\beta\bar{j}\bar{m}}T_{\bar{\alpha}\gamma\delta}g^{\gamma\bar{j}}g^{\delta\bar{m}}=-\tilde{R}_{\bar{\alpha}\beta}-{1\over 2}T_{\bar{\alpha}}^{\bar{j}\bar{m}}\bar{T}_{\beta\bar{j}\bar{m}}

(2.33)

where in the second equality we used the fact
\tilde{R}_{\bar{\alpha}\beta}=-{\nabla}^{\gamma}T_{\bar{\alpha}\beta\gamma}
(see
[
26
, Lemma 5,iii]
).
Combining all together, and using the fact that
\omega=ig_{\bar{k}j}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(ig_{\bar{k}j})=i(\partial^{\dagger}_{\omega,\Omega}i\partial\omega)_{\bar{k}j}=-(\partial^{\dagger}_{\omega,\Omega}\partial\omega)_{\bar{k}j}.

(2.34)

Q.E.D.

We observe that the evolution equation for the torsion was derived in

[

\displaystyle\partial_{t}T=\partial_{t}i\partial\omega=-i\partial\partial^{\dagger}_{\omega,\Omega}\partial\omega=-\partial\partial^{\dagger}_{\omega,\Omega}T.

(2.35)

3
Compactness theorem for the Type IIB flow

We give now the proof of Theorem

.
We break it into several steps.

Convergence of the metrics

The
C^{\infty}
convergence of the Riemannian manifolds

to
M_{\infty},g_{\infty}

for

Indeed, by Lemma

Convergence of the complex structures

Consider the forms
\omega_{j}(X,Y)=g_{j}(J_{j}X,Y)
. It is well known that the pair
g_{j},\omega_{j}
defines
J_{j}
. Let
\Phi_{j,k}
will be restriction
\Phi_{j+k}
on

. Denote

as

. It is easy to see that
g_{j,\infty}
is the restriction of
g_{\infty}
on
U_{j}
. Due to this, we omit the index

in

and write

everywhere. We need to show that the forms
\omega_{j,k}:=\Phi_{j,k}^{*}\omega_{j+k}
converge uniformly with all derivatives on

as

to some nondegenerate forms
\omega_{j,\infty}

Let

on

, the tensor

can be written as follows:

\displaystyle(A_{j,k})^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}={1\over 2}(g^{j,k})^{\alpha\delta}({\nabla}^{\infty}_{\beta}(v_{j,k})_{\delta\gamma}+{\nabla}^{\infty}_{\gamma}(v_{j,k})_{\beta\delta}-{\nabla}^{\infty}_{\delta}(v_{j,k})_{\beta\gamma}).

One can check this formula in the geodesic coordinates for
g_{\infty}
.

Since
g_{j,k}
converge to
g_{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}
, the norms

tends to zero for any

. The argument from
[

shows, that for any

there is a constant

, such that

.

Now we can obtain bounds for
\omega_{jk}
. These bounds follow from the boundedness of the covariant derivatives of
J_{j}
(which follows from the Lemma
4
), and the bounds for
A_{j,k}
. We have

\displaystyle{\nabla}^{g_{\infty}}\omega_{j,k}={\nabla}^{g_{j,k}}\omega_{j,k}+({\nabla}^{g_{\infty}}-{\nabla}^{g_{j,k}})\omega_{j,k}.

Since
g_{j,k}
converge to

with all derivatives, there is a constant
K_{\infty}

\displaystyle|{\nabla}^{g_{\infty}}\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{\infty}}\leq(K_{\infty})^{3\over 2}|{\nabla}^{g_{j}}\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{j,k}}+K_{0}|\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{j,k}}.

The quantity
|{\nabla}^{g_{j}}\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{j,k}}

are bounded. The quantity
|\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{j,k}}
is a constant.

Next, we obtain bounds for the higher derivatives of
{\nabla}_{j,k}
. As in
[
22
]

\displaystyle|({\nabla}^{g_{\infty}})^{l}\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Sigma_{m=0}^{l}|A_{j,k}|^{m}_{g_{\infty}}|({\nabla}^{g_{j,k}})^{m-l}\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{j,k}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad+C\Sigma_{m=1}^{l-1}|({\nabla}^{g_{\infty}})^{m}A_{j,k}|_{g_{\infty}}|({\nabla}^{g_{j,k}})^{m-l-1}\omega_{j,k}|_{g_{j,k}}

[

\omega_{j,\infty}
in
C^{\infty}
on compacts in

. The form
\omega_{j,\infty}
is compatible with
g_{\infty}
on
U_{j}
. This follows from the compatibility of

and

. In particular,

\displaystyle\omega_{j,\infty}^{n}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\omega_{j,k}^{n}=n!\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}d\mu_{g_{j,k}}=n!d\mu_{g_{\infty}},

(3.5)

hence

is nondegenerate.
The pair
(g_{j,\infty},\omega_{j,\infty})
defines an almost complex structure

, which is the
C^{\infty}
-limit of

are integrable, and

.

Let

. We see that

, and

. Since the set of smooth sections of any vector bundle on

\omega_{\infty}
on the whole

is
\omega_{j,\infty}
. Furthermore, the pair
(g_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})

(g_{\infty},\omega_{j,})

Convergence of the holomorphic forms

By

Let

\nabla^{+,g_{j,k}}\|\Omega_{j}\|_{g_{j,k}}\rightarrow{\nabla}^{+,g_{j,\infty}}f_{j,\infty}=f_{j,\infty}\tau(g_{j,\infty})

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{a}}\Omega_{\infty}={\nabla}^{+}_{\bar{a}}\Omega_{\infty}-\tau_{\bar{a}}\Omega_{\infty}={\nabla}^{+}_{\bar{a}}(f_{\infty}\phi_{\infty})-\tau_{\bar{a}}f_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}=\tau_{\bar{a}}f_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}-\tau_{\bar{a}}f_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}=0,

\displaystyle|\hat{\nabla}^{p}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}\leq N^{{}^{\prime}}_{p}

(3.13)

for all
p\geq 1
.

(3)
There are constants
N^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{p,q}>0
, such that

\displaystyle|\frac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}\hat{\nabla}^{p}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}\leq N^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{p,q}

(3.14)

Proof.

(1) For any vector
V
and for any solution
g
of the Type IIB flow we have the following identity

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g(V,V)=-\tilde{R}_{j\bar{k}}V^{j}\overline{V}^{k}-{1\over 2}(T\circ\bar{T})_{j\bar{k}}V^{j}\overline{V}^{k}.

(3.15)

From the definition we see that
|\tilde{R}_{j\bar{k}}V^{j}\overline{V}^{k}|\leq\sqrt{n}|Rm|g(V,V)
, and the norm of
T\circ\bar{T}
is bounded.

Hence, by the standard trick, we can write the following:

\displaystyle C|t_{1}-t_{0}|\geq\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|\partial_{t}\,{\rm log}\,(g(V,V))|dt\geq|\,{\rm log}\,\frac{g(t_{1})(V,V)}{g(t_{0})(V,V)}|

(3.16)

for some constant
C
, which depends on the dimension, and the bounds for
T
and
Rm
. Now we can take the exponent and obtain the desired bound with
N=e^{C(t_{1}-t_{0})}

(2) Throughout this section
{\nabla}^{m}
and
\Gamma_{m}
denote the Chern connection with respect to
g_{m}
.

We want to obtain bounds for
|\hat{{\nabla}}^{p}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}
for any
p
. First of all (see
[
19
]
for details),
h^{-1}_{m}\hat{{\nabla}}h_{m}={\nabla}^{m}h_{m}h^{-1}_{m}={\nabla}^{m}-\hat{{\nabla}}=g^{-1}\hat{{\nabla}}g
, where
h_{m}
is a relative endomorphism
(h_{m})^{a}_{b}=\hat{g}^{a\bar{s}}(g_{m})_{b\bar{s}}
. Next, since we proved the equivalence for
\hat{g}
and
g_{m}
, the norms on tensors, induced by these metrics are equivalent (see [Lemma 3.13]
[
7
]
for the proof).

We have the following identity:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{{\nabla}}g_{m}=\hat{{\nabla}}\dot{g}=-\hat{{\nabla}}(\tilde{Ric}_{m}+(T\circ\bar{T})_{m})=-{\nabla}(\tilde{Ric}_{m}+(T\circ\bar{T})_{m})+

\displaystyle({\nabla}-\hat{{\nabla}})(\tilde{Ric}_{m}+(T\circ\bar{T})_{m})

(3.17)

The first summand is bounded by our assumptions. The second summand is bounded by constant times
|\hat{{\nabla}}^{p}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}
. Altogether, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle|\hat{{\nabla}}^{p}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}\leq C(1+|\hat{{\nabla}}^{p}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}),

(3.18)

where
C
is the maximum of bounds for
\tilde{Ric}_{m}+(T\circ\bar{T})_{m}
and for
{\nabla}^{m}(\tilde{Ric}_{m}+(T\circ\bar{T})_{m})
.

For
p>1
we proceed by induction. First of all, for any
p\in\bf N
, by the same way as in
[
3
, Lemma 8.6]
, one can show that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{\nabla}^{p}g_{m}=\sum_{k_{1}+\dots+k_{q}=p}({\nabla}^{m})^{k_{1}}(Rm_{m}+(T*\bar{T})_{m})*\hat{{\nabla}}^{k_{2}}g_{m}*\dots*\hat{{\nabla}}^{k_{q}}g_{m}.

(3.19)

Suppose we have the estimate for
p-1
. Then, taking the time derivative of
|{\nabla}^{p}g_{m}|_{\hat{g}}
and incorporate bounds for the curvature, the torsion and for the lower derivatives, we obtain:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|{\nabla}^{p}g_{m}|_{\hat{g}}\leq C(1+|{\nabla}^{p}g_{m}|_{\hat{g}}),

(3.20)

where
C>0
depends only on bounds for the curvature, the torsion and for the lower derivatives. Hence,

\displaystyle\,{\rm log}\,(|{\nabla}^{p}g_{m}|_{\hat{g}}+1)\leq Ct

(3.21)

for some constant
D
. Now we take exponent and obtain the desired bound.

(3) We have

\displaystyle|\partial^{q}_{t}\hat{{\nabla}}^{p}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}=|\hat{{\nabla}}^{p}\partial^{q}_{t}g_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}=|\hat{{\nabla}}^{p}\partial^{q-1}_{t}\dot{g}_{m}(t)|_{\hat{g}}.

(3.22)

The Laplacian formulation of the Type IIB flow (see
1.2
) allows us write time derivatives of the metric
g_{m}
via the spatial derivatives. Iterating this process, we are able to estimate all mixed derivatives via spatial derivatives. However, we proved before the estimates for spatial derivatives. Q.E.D.

We can derive a useful consequence of this lemma.

Lemma 10

Let
(M,\hat{g},J,\Omega)
be a Hermitian Calabi-Yau manifold and
K\subset M
is compact. Assume there are complex structures
\{J_{j}\}
and holomorphic forms
\{\Omega_{j}\}
on
M
, which converge in
C^{\infty}
to
J
and
\Omega
respectfully. Suppose
g_{j}(t)
are the solutions of the Type IIB flow w.r.t.
J_{j}
and
\Omega_{j}
on
K\times[\tau_{0};\tau_{1}]
. Suppose all
g_{j}(t)
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
9
. Then the results of Lemma
9
also holds for
g_{j}(t)
, i.e. there are constants
N,N_{l,m}
, such that
|\partial^{l}_{t}\hat{{\nabla}}^{m}g_{j}(t)|_{\hat{g}}\leq N_{l,m}
and
N^{-1}\hat{g}\leq g_{j}\leq N\hat{g}
.

The difference between this Lemma and Lemma
9
is that here, the metrics
g_{j}(t)
are Hermitian with respect to the
different
complex structures on
K
.

Proof.

Define the sequence of the metrics
\hat{g}_{j}
by the formula
\hat{g}_{j}:={1\over 2}(\hat{g}(X,Y)+\hat{g}(J_{j}X,J_{j}Y))
. The metrics
\hat{g}_{j}
converge to
\hat{g}
in
C^{\infty}
because
J_{j}
converge to
J
in
C^{\infty}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that for any
j
there is a constant
P
independent on
j
, such that
P^{-1}\hat{g}\leq\hat{g}_{j}\leq P\hat{g}
. Applying the estimates from Part (1) of the previous lemma, we obtain the uniform equivalence between
g_{j}(t)
and
\hat{g}
.

Denote by
\hat{\Gamma}_{j}
and
\hat{\Gamma}
the Chern connections of
(\hat{g}_{j},J_{j})
and
(\hat{g},J)
respectfully. The difference
|\hat{\Gamma}_{j}-\hat{\Gamma}|_{\hat{g}}
between Chern connection
\hat{\Gamma}_{j}
of
(\hat{g}_{j},J_{j})
and
\hat{\Gamma}
of the pair
(\hat{g},J)
tends to zero as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Let
\hat{S}_{j}(X,Y,Z):=d\hat{\omega}_{j}(J_{j}X,Y,Z)
and
\hat{S}
has the same meaning for
(\hat{g},J)
. The difference of the Chern connections can be written as follows

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{j}-\hat{\Gamma}=({\nabla}^{\hat{g}_{j}}-{\nabla}^{\hat{g}})+(\hat{g}^{-1}_{j}S_{j}-\hat{g}^{-1}\hat{S}).

(3.23)

The quantity
|{\nabla}^{\hat{g}_{j}}-{\nabla}^{\hat{g}}|_{\hat{g}}
tends to zero as
j
tends to infinity due to
C^{\infty}
convergence of
\hat{g}_{j}
and
J_{j}
. The norm of the second summand is again tends to zero by the same reason.

The same is true for
|\hat{{\nabla}}^{l}(\hat{\Gamma}_{j}-\hat{\Gamma})|_{\hat{g}}
for any
l\in\bf N
. Hence
|\hat{{\nabla}}^{l}(\hat{\Gamma}_{j}-\hat{\Gamma})|_{\hat{g}}
is bounded by a constant, independent on
j
. Now we apply the previous lemma to
g_{j}(t)
and
\hat{g}_{j}
, and use the fact that
|\hat{{\nabla}}^{l}(\Gamma_{j}-\hat{\Gamma})|_{\hat{g}}\leq|\hat{{\nabla}}^{l}(\Gamma_{j}-\hat{\Gamma}_{j})|_{\hat{g}}+|\hat{{\nabla}}^{l}(\hat{\Gamma}_{j}-\hat{\Gamma})|_{\hat{g}}
. Hence, we obtain bounds for
|\hat{{\nabla}}^{l}g_{j}(t)|_{\hat{g}}
and
|\partial^{l}\hat{{\nabla}}^{m}g_{j}(t)|_{\hat{g}}
with respect to
\hat{g}
. Q.E.D.

Finally, we need to study the evolution of
\,{\rm log}\,\|\Omega\|^{2}
along the flow.

Lemma 11

Let
(M,g(t),J,\Omega)
be a compact conformally balanced Calabi-Yau manifold, and
g(t)
evolves according to the Type IIB flow:
\partial_{t}g_{\bar{k}j}=-\tilde{R}_{\bar{k}j}-{1\over 2}T_{\bar{k}pq}\bar{T}_{j}^{pq}
. Assume there is a constant
C>0
, such that
|Rm|_{g}+|{\nabla}T|_{g}+|T|^{2}_{g}\leq C
. Then there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}
and
C_{3}
such that the following holds:

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq\,{\rm log}\,\|\Omega\|^{2}_{g}(t)\leq C_{2}t+C_{3}.

(3.24)

Proof.

By a straightforward computation, we can show that
\tilde{R}=g^{j\bar{k}}\tilde{R}_{\bar{k}j}=g^{j\bar{k}}\partial_{\bar{k}}\partial_{j}\,{\rm log}\,\|\Omega\|^{2}=\Delta\,{\rm log}\,\|\Omega\|^{2}
and,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\,{\rm log}\,\|\Omega\|^{2}={1\over 2}|T|^{2}_{g}\geq 0

(3.25)

(see
[
19
, Section 3.1]
). By the maximum principle,
\,{\rm log}\,\|\Omega\|^{2}
achieves minimum at
t=0
, hence it bounded below.
On the other hand, by the assumption on
Rm
and
T
, there is constant
C_{2}
, such that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\,{\rm log}\,\|\Omega|\|^{2}=\tilde{R}+{1\over 2}|T|^{2}_{g}\leq C_{2}.

(3.26)

Now we can integrate it along
t
, and obtain the bound from above. Q.E.D.

We proceed as in the proof of Hamilton’s compactness theorem (see

By Theorem

, Shi-type estimates from

4
Singularity models for the Type IIB flow

Let
\omega(t)
be a solution for the Type IIB flow on a complex manifold on some time interval
[0,T)
. For each
t\in[0,T)
, define the function
f(t)
by

\displaystyle f(t):=\sup_{M}(|Rm|^{2}+|\nabla T|^{2}+|T|^{4}).

(4.1)

Definition 3
(Singularity types)

Let
(M,g(t),J,\Omega)
be a solution of the Type IIB flow on a compact Hermitian Calabi-Yau manifold
M
. Let
f(t)=\sup_{M}(|Rm|^{2}+|\nabla T|^{2}+|T|^{4})
as above, and
T
is the maximal time for which flow exists. We define the following type of singularities

[Type I]

\sup_{[0;T]}(T-t)f(t)=C<\infty
, and
T<\infty

[Type IIa]

\sup_{[0;T]}(T-t)f(t)=\infty
, and
T<\infty

[Type IIb]
\sup_{[0;T]}tf(t)=\infty
,
T=\infty

[Type III]

\sup_{[0;T]}tf(t)=C<\infty
,
T=\infty

We observe that we could
also try to define singularities in terms of
\|\Omega\|^{2}
, as suggested by examples (see
[
31
, Section 2.3.2]
for instance). However, as Lemma
11
shows, the bounds on
f(t)
imply bounds on
\|\Omega\|
.

Definition 4

We say that
(M,g(t),J,\Omega)
satisfies the injectivity radius estimate if the injectivity radius satisfy the following inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{inj}(M,g(t))\geq{c\over\sqrt{f(t)}}

(4.2)

for some positive constant
c
.

We give now the proof of Theorem
3
.
Denote
F(x,t):=\sqrt{|Rm|^{2}+|\nabla T|^{2}+|T|^{4}}
. Recall that
f(t)=\sup_{M}\sqrt{|Rm|^{2}+|\nabla T|^{2}+|T|^{4}}=\sup_{x\in M}F(x,t)
. Throughout the proof of the theorem we will use the sequence of rescaled Hermitian metrics
g_{j}(t)
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle g_{j}(t):=C_{j}g(t_{j}+C_{j}^{-1}t),

(4.3)

where the constants
C_{j}
will be defined later. We also rescale the holomorphic volume form

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}:=C_{j}^{-n\over 2}\Omega.

(4.4)

The case of Type I singularity

Let
C:=\sup_{t}f(t)
. . Pick a sequence
(x_{j},t_{j})\in M\times[0;T]
, such that
F(x_{j},t_{j})\rightarrow C
. Let
C_{j}:=F(x_{j},t_{j})
, and
(T-t_{j})F(x_{j},t_{j})\geq c>0
.
Consider the rescaled Hermitian metrics
g_{j}(t)
and forms
\Omega_{j}
. These metrics are again the solutions of the Type IIB flow on the interval
(-C_{j}t_{j};C_{j}(T-t_{j}))
. The curvature and torsion tensors of the metrics
g_{j}
satisfy the following bound:

\displaystyle\sqrt{|Rm|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|\nabla T|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|T|_{g_{j}}^{4}}\leq C_{j}^{-1}{C\over T-(t_{j}+C_{j}^{-1}t)}={C\over C_{j}(T-t_{j})-t}\leq{C\over c-t}.

(4.5)

By the compactness theorem for Type IIB flow, the sequence
(M,g_{j}(t),J,\Omega_{j},x_{j})
converge to the limit manifold
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),J,\Omega,\infty)
with
|Rm|_{g_{\infty}}^{2}+|\nabla T|_{g_{\infty}}^{2}+|T|_{g_{\infty}}^{4}\leq{C\over c-t}
. The case of Type I singularity is proved.

The case of Type IIa singularity

Pick a monotone increasing sequence
{T_{j}}
, such that
T_{j}\rightarrow T
and let
C_{j}=\sup_{t<T_{j}}f(t)
. Next, we pick a sequence of points
(x_{j},t_{j})
, such that
C^{2}_{j}=(T-t_{j})F(x_{j},t_{j})
. Now we take a sequence of rescaled metrics
g_{j}(t):=C_{j}g(t_{j}+C_{j}^{-1}t)
and the sequence of pointed manifolds
(M,g_{j}(t),J,\Omega_{j},x_{j})
again converge to the limit manifold
(M,g_{\infty}(t),J,\Omega,x_{\infty})
. The limit metric
g_{\infty}(t)
is the solution of the Type IIB flow on the whole line
(-\infty,\infty)
, as
C_{j}(T-t_{j})\rightarrow\infty
. The bound for the curvature is the following:

\displaystyle\sqrt{|Rm|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|\nabla T|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|T|_{g_{j}}^{4}}\leq{C_{j}\over C_{j}(T-t_{j})}\rightarrow 1.

(4.6)

Hence,
f(t)\leq 1
.

The case of Type IIb singularity

Pick a monotone sequence
T_{j}\rightarrow\infty
and a sequence of points
(x_{j},t_{j}),t_{j}\leq T_{j}
, such that:

t_{j}(T-t_{j})F(x_{j},t_{j})=\sup t(T_{j}-t)F(x,t)
. Let
C_{j}
be as above, and
A_{j}=t_{j}C_{j}

\displaystyle\sqrt{|Rm|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|\nabla T|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|T|_{g_{j}}^{4}}\leq C_{j}^{-1}{A_{j}\over t_{j}+C_{j}^{-1}t}={A_{j}\over A_{j}+t}\rightarrow 1

(4.7)

We again apply the compactness theorem and have a limit with the desirable property.

The case of Type III singularity

Let
A=\lim\sup tf(t)<+\infty
. This number is positive. Indeed, if
tf(t)<\varepsilon
, then the diameter
L
satisfies the following inequality:

\displaystyle{\partial L\over\partial t}\leq{C\varepsilon L\over t},

(4.8)

because the
f(t)
is bounded, hence
\tilde{R}_{\bar{k}j}+{1\over 2}T_{\bar{k}pq}\bar{T}_{j}^{pq}
is bounded. Hence,
\,{\rm log}\,L\leq C\varepsilon\,{\rm log}\,t
. Hence,
L\leq t^{C\varepsilon}
. Take
\varepsilon
smaller than
{1\over 2C}
. This choice implies
L^{2}f(t)\rightarrow 0
. On the other hand,
L\geq\mathrm{inj}(M,g(t))\geq{C\over\sqrt{f(t)}}
by the assumption of the theorem. This inequality is equivalent to the inequality
L^{2}f(t)\geq C
. This is a contradiction, hence
A>0
.

Let
C=\sup f(t)
, and
C_{j},(x_{j},t_{j})
as above. The metrics
g_{j}(t)
are solutions to the Type IIB flow on the interval
[-C_{j}t_{j};+\infty)
and, by our assumptions
C_{j}t_{j}\rightarrow A
. We have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\sqrt{|Rm|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|\nabla T|_{g_{j}}^{2}+|T|_{g_{j}}^{4}}\leq C^{-1}_{j}{A\over t_{j}+C_{j}^{-1}t}={A\over C_{j}t_{j}+t}\rightarrow{A\over A+t}.

(4.9)

Q.E.D.

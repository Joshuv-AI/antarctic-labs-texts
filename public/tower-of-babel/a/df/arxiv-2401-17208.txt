Inequalities and enumerative formulas for flags of Pfaff systems
Miguel Rodríguez Peña, Fernando Lourenço

Abstract
. In this work, we study inequalities and enumerative formulas for flags of Pfaff systems on ℙ ℂ n \mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}} . More specifically, we find the number of independent Pfaff systems that leave invariant a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation and deduce inequalities relating the degrees in the flags, which can be interpreted as the Poincaré problem for flags. Moreover, restricting to a flag of specific holomorphic foliations/distributions, we obtain inequalities involving the degrees. As a consequence, we prove stability results for the tangent sheaf of some rank two holomorphic foliations/distributions.



1.
Introduction

The concept of flag of holomorphic foliations is a recent topic and there are so many authors studying it, see for instance

[
3
,
16
,
23
]
and
[
28
]
. By a
flag
of holomorphic distributions on a complex manifold
M
,

\mathcal{D}_{k_{1}}\prec\mathcal{D}_{k_{2}}\prec\cdots\prec\mathcal{D}_{k_{m}}
, we mean a collection of distributions of dimensions

1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{m}<\mathrm{dim}(M)
such that, at each point
p\in M
where the distributions are regular,

\mathcal{D}_{k_{i},\,p}\subset\mathcal{D}_{k_{j},\,p}
, whenever
i<j
. In particular, when we have integrability, this implies that the
leaves of
\mathcal{D}_{k_{i}}
are contained in the leaves of the
\mathcal{D}_{k_{j}}
. All these concepts are explained in Section
2
.

In this paper we extended the concept of flag of holomorphic foliations/distributions to flag of Pfaff systems.
With this new approach, we study inequalities and enumerative problems associated with the flags. The inequalities that we present
in this work are motivated by the so-called Poincaré problem for foliations and also by a question raised by M. Brunella.

Before stating the results we recall that a
Pfaff system

\mathscr{F}
, on a complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, is defined by a nontrivial global section

\omega_{\mathscr{F}}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(l))
, where

\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}
is the sheaf of holomorphic
k
-forms on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. The number
k
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
is the codimension of the Pfaff system. A
holomorphic distribution
is a Pfaff system locally decomposable outside its singular set,
and a
holomorphic foliation
is a Pfaff system locally decomposable and integrable (Frobenius integrability).

The
degree
of
\mathscr{F}
, denoted by
\deg(\mathscr{F})
, is defined as the degree of the variety formed by the points of tangency between
\omega_{\mathscr{F}}
and a generic non-invariant linearly embedded subspace

i:H\simeq\mathbb{P}^{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then the degree of
\mathscr{F}
is given by

\mathrm{deg}(\mathscr{F})=-k-1+\ell
; see Section
2
.

Motivated by the question of algebraic integrability of foliations, Henri Poincaré studied in
[
32
]
the problem to decide whether a holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on the complex projective plane admits a rational first integral. Poincaré observed that, in order to solve this problem, it is sufficient to find a bound for the degree of the generic
\mathcal{F}
-invariants curves.
Raises the question of bounding the degree of an algebraic variety, invariant by a foliation on projective space, in terms of the degree of the foliation is known as the
Poincaré problem
.

On the other hand, the Poincaré problem can be interpreted in terms of the flag, namely, if we consider a flag of holomorphic foliations on projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
when we get to relate the degrees of the foliations involved into the flag? There are many topics which are closely related to flags and naturally appear in the theory of holomorphic foliation. For instance, we refer to a conjecture due to Marco Brunella, see
[
10
, p.443]
which says that a holomorphic foliation of dimension two on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
either admits an invariant algebraic surface, or it composes a flag of holomorphic foliations. Furthermore, we have a more general conjecture attributed to different authors, see
[
7
, p.2]
:

Any codimension one holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, either admits a transverse projective structure with poles on some invariant hypersurface, or it is the pull-back of a holomorphic foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by a rational map.

In the second alternative, the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are foliated by the levels of the rational map.

Next, we present some of the main results of this work, see also Section
6
.
The following theorem counts the number of independent Pfaff systems that leave a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation invariant.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation of degree
d
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that its singular set has only isolated singularities, then for each integer number
m
such that

d<m+1\leq 2d
, there are

\binom{m-d+n+1}{m-d+r+2}\binom{m-d+r+1}{r+1}

independent Pfaff systems of codimension
r
and degree
m
such that leaves
\mathcal{F}
invariant. Moreover,
if
m+1\leq d
, we do not have nontrivial Pfaff system of degree
m
that leaves
\mathcal{F}
invariant.

In the following corollary, we inquire about the correlation between the degrees of the Pfaff systems involved in the flag.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\prec\mathcal{G}
be a flag on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation which its singular set has only isolated singularities, and
\mathcal{G}
is a Pfaff system. Then

\deg(\mathcal{F})\leq\deg(\mathcal{G}).

Note that Theorem
1.1
gives a finitely many flags which is reaches the maximal quote in Corollary
1.2
, proving that this quote is sharp (see also Example
5.2
).

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a holomorphic distribution such that

T_{\mathcal{D}}\cong\bigoplus T_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
, where each
\mathcal{F}_{i}

is a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation which its singular set has only isolated singularities.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Pfaff system such that leaves
\mathcal{D}
invariant. Then

\deg(\mathcal{D})\leq\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{D})\cdot\deg(\mathcal{G}).

In
[
18
, Theorem 2]
(see also Theorem
1
), the authors show that the split condition of
T_{\mathcal{D}}

in Corollary
1.3
is an open condition for
n\geq 4
,
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{D})\leq n-2
, and

\mathrm{codim}\hskip-0.56905pt\big(\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{D})\big)\geq 3
.

Remark 1.4
.

We observe that a holomorphic distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is nonsingular if and only if
n
is odd,

{\rm{codim}}(\mathcal{D})=1
and
\deg(\mathcal{D})=0
, see
[

, p.36]
and
[

]
. In this work, we will assume that all distributions
have a nonempty singular set.

In the next result we consider a dual version of the above result in Theorem
1.1
, namely, if we fix a distribution of codimension one we count, under generic conditions, how many independent vector fields are tangent to the distribution.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

be a holomorphic distribution of codimension one and degree

invariants by

Note that for

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper we introduce a new approach of flag considering instead only holomorphic foliations, Pfaff systems (possibly non locally decomposable). For this propose, let us consider some notation.

2.1.
Pfaff systems on
\mathbb{P}^{n}

In this Subsection, we deal with Pfaff systems on the complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, for this denote
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}
the sheaf of germs of
k
-forms on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Given
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(l)

a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
we use the notation (twisted
k
-forms)

\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}(l):=\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(l).

Definition 2.1
.

A Pfaff system
\mathscr{F}
of codimension
k
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is induced by a
nontrivial section

\omega_{\mathscr{F}}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}(l)).

The singular set of the Pfaff system
\mathscr{F}
is naturally defined as

{\rm{Sing}}(\mathscr{F})=\{\,p\in\mathbb{P}^{n};\,\omega_{\mathscr{F}}(p)=0\}.

We remark that a
holomorphic distribution
of codimension
k
is a Pfaff system
\mathscr{F}
of codimension
k
locally decomposable outside its singular set, i.e., for
p\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus{\rm{Sing}}(\mathscr{F})
there is an open set
U
and holomorphic

1
-forms
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
on
U
satisfying

(1)

\omega_{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{k}.

We would like to note that for
k=2
, the decomposable condition can be revisited as follows
(see
[
8
, Proposition 1, p.5]
),

\omega|_{U}
is decomposable if and only if
\omega|_{U}\wedge\omega|_{U}=0
.

Again we observe that a
holomorphic foliation
of codimension
k
is a Pfaff system
\mathscr{F}
locally decomposable as in
(
1
) and integrable, where the integrability condition is given by

(2)

d\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{k}=0,

for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
For a background in holomorphic distribution and foliation, we refer
[
1
,
2
]
and
[
5
]
(and references therein).
Throughout the text, we consider reduced holomorphic foliations, i.e.,
holomorphic foliations whose singular set has codimension greater than
1
.

Given a Pfaff system
\mathscr{F}
of codimension
k
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, it can be determined by the following data, see
[
12
]
.

a)

an open covering
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n};

b)

holomorphic
k
-forms
\omega_{\alpha}\in H^{0}(U_{\alpha},\Omega_{U_{\alpha}}^{k})
, satisfying

\omega_{\alpha}=h_{\alpha\beta}\omega_{\beta}\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}\neq\emptyset,

where
h_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta})^{\ast}

determines a cocycle representing
\mathscr{F}
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a Pfaff system of codimension
k
induced by a twisted
k
-form

\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}(l))

and
i:H\simeq\mathbb{P}^{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a generic non-invariant linearly embedded subspace.
Then we get the non-trivial section as follow

i^{\ast}\omega\in H^{0}(H,\Omega_{H}^{k}(l))\simeq H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}(-k-1+l)).

The tangency set between
\omega
and
H
is denoted by
Z(i^{\ast}\omega)
.
The
degree of the Pfaff system

\mathscr{F}
, denoted by
\deg(\mathscr{F})
, is defined as the degree
of

in

and, therefore, is given by

\deg(\mathscr{F})=-k-1+l.

Then we can consider a Pfaff system
\mathscr{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of codimension
k
just by

\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}(m+k+1)),

where
m=\deg(\mathscr{F})
. The Euler sequence implies that a Pfaff system of codimension
k
and degree
m
can be induced by a polynomial differential
k
-form
\omega
with homogeneous coefficients of degree
m+1
on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that

, where
\vartheta
is the radial vector field. To general theory of Pfaff systems we refer
[
12
,
13
,
15
,
17
]
and
[
19
]

2.3.
Mumford-Takemoto stability

Let
L
be a very ample line bundle on a
n
-dimensional smooth projective variety
X
.
The slope of a torsion free coherent sheaf
E
on
X
is defined by

\mu_{L}(E)=\dfrac{c_{1}(E)\cdot L^{n-1}}{{\rm{rank}}(E)}.

Recall that
E
is said to be stable (respectively semistable) if every proper nontrivial subsheaf
F\subset E
with
{\rm{rank}}(F)<{\rm{rank}}(E)

satisfies
\mu_{L}(F)<\mu_{L}(E)
(respectively
\mu_{L}(F)\leq\mu_{L}(E)
).
We will say that a holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is stable (respectively semistable) when its tangent sheaf
T_{\mathcal{F}}

is stable (respectively semistable).

When
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we take
L=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
and write
\mu=\mu_{L}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation of codimension
k
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
given by
\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}(\deg(\mathcal{F})+k+1))
.
Since the singular set of
\mathcal{F}
has codimension at least two we obtain the adjunction formula

K_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}=K_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\det N^{\ast}_{\mathcal{F}},

where
K_{\mathcal{F}}=\left(\wedge^{n-k}\,T^{\ast}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)^{\ast\ast}
is the canonical bundle and
N_{\mathcal{F}}
the normal bundle of
\mathcal{F}
. Note that

\det(N_{\mathcal{F}})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\deg(\mathcal{F})+k+1)
.
In particular, we have

\mu(T_{\mathcal{F}})=\frac{\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{F})-\deg(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{F})}.

Therefore
\mathcal{F}
is stable (respectively semistable) if and only if for every distribution
\mathcal{D}
tangent to
\mathcal{F}

we have
\frac{\deg(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{F})}<\frac{\deg(\mathcal{D})}{\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{D})}
(respectively
\leq
).
For more details about stability and holomorphic foliations, see for instance
[
5
,
26
]
and
[
31
]
.

2.4.
Bott’s formula

To finish the preliminary part, we describe a little bit about the dimension of certain cohomology group, namely, the classical Bott’s formulas, see
[
16
]

[
31
]

n

and

k
and

t

h^{q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p}(k))=\mathrm{dim}H^{q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p}(k))\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,h^{s}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\bigwedge^{r}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(t))=\mathrm{dim}H^{s}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\bigwedge^{r}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(t)),

we have

h^{q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p}(k))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clccc}\binom{k+n-p}{k}\binom{k-1}{p}&q=0,\,0\leq p\leq n\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ k>p;\\
1&k=0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ 0\leq p=q\leq n;\\
\binom{-k+p}{-k}\binom{-k-1}{n-p}&q=n,\,0\leq p\leq n\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ k<p-n;\\

6.
Some inequalities for flags of foliations and distributions

In this section, we consider flags of specific holomorphic foliations and distributions on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and derive inequalities related to their degrees. These inequalities are motivated by the so-called Poincaré problem for foliations. In particular, we deduce stability results to tangent sheaf of some rank two holomorphic foliations. First, we are going to introduce some terminologies that will be necessary.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a subvariety. We denote by
\mathrm{reg}(X)
the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
X
.
Let us remember that the
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
is an invariant of arithmetic nature, a good measure of the complexity of
X
:
it is well-known that
X
is cut out by hypersurfaces with degree at most its regularity,
\mathrm{reg}(X)
.
However, when
X
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay, for example a complete intersection,
the regularity acquires a more geometric meaning: cut
X
by as many general hyperplanes as its dimension to obtain a set
S
of points;
then the regularity of
X
is the smallest integer
r
such that for each
p\in S
there is a hypersurface of degree
r-1

passing through all the points of
S\setminus\left\{p\right\}
. For example, the regularity of a hypersurface is its degree.
For more details about Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, see for instance
[
20
,
21
]
and
[
22
]
.

Finally, we say
X
is
nonsingular in codimension

1
if
\mathrm{codim}\hskip-0.56905pt\big(\mathrm{Sing}(X),X\big)\geq 2
.

Theorem 6.1
.

\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{L}(n,p,d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})

of degree

and

.

.

]
. Let

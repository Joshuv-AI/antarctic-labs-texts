Scalar field dark matter with time-varying equation of state
Gaspard Poulot, Elsa M. Teixeira, Carsten van de Bruck, and Nelson J. Nunes

Abstract
We propose a new model of scalar field dark matter interacting with dark energy. Adopting a fluid description of the dark matter field in the regime of rapid oscillations, we find that the equation of state for dark matter is non-zero and even becomes increasingly negative at late times during dark energy domination. Furthermore, the speed of sound of dark matter is non-vanishing at all length scales, and a non-adiabatic pressure contribution arises. The results indicate that there are still unexplored possible interactions within the dark sector that lead to novel background effects and can impact structure formation processes.

2
Model

Our setup is inspired by that of field theories of dark energy, such as coupled quintessence
[
55
,
83
,
81
]
, but with the role of the fields for DM and DE swapped. That is, we treat
\phi
in the action below as a dark matter scalar field, and the dark energy sector is coupled conformally to the DM sector, resulting in the following effective action for this model:

\displaystyle{\cal S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}{\cal R}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-U(\phi)\right)+{\cal S}_{\rm SM}+{\cal S}_{\rm DE}\,,

(1)

where
{\cal R}
is the Ricci-scalar and
M_{\rm Pl}\equiv 1/\sqrt{8\pi G}
is the reduced Planck mass. In the action above,
{\cal S}_{\rm SM}
denotes the Lagrangian containing the standard model fields propagating in geodesics of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. In what follows, we assume that the DE sector can be described by a slowly evolving scalar field
\chi
, described by the action

{\cal S}_{\rm DE}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-{\tilde{g}}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi-V(\chi)\right)~,

(2)

where the metric
{\tilde{g}}
is related to the metric
g
via a conformal transformation of the form
{\tilde{g}}_{\mu\nu}=C(\phi)g_{\mu\nu}
. Rewriting
{\cal S}_{\rm DE}
in terms of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
results in

{\cal S}_{\rm DE}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{C(\phi)}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi-C^{2}(\phi)V(\chi)\right)\,.

(3)

In contrast to models such as coupled quintessence, in which
C
depends on the dark energy field, the function
C
in this framework is dependent on
dark matter
properties

.

The equation of motion for the DE field
\chi
is derived from the corresponding variation of the action and reads

\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi-C\frac{dV}{d\chi}=-\frac{\nabla_{\alpha}C}{C}\nabla^{\alpha}\chi\,.

(4)

The equation for the DM field
\phi
is derived in an analogous manner and yields

\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi-\frac{\partial U}{\partial\phi}=Q\,,

(5)

where the coupling
Q
was defined as

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial L_{\chi}}{\partial(\nabla_{\mu}\phi)}\right)-\frac{\partial L_{\chi}}{\partial\phi}

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{,\phi}}{2C}\left[Cg^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\chi\nabla_{\beta}\chi+4C^{2}U\right]\,.

The Einstein equations result from variation of the action with the gravitational metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, resulting in

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=\kappa^{2}\left(T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}+T_{\mu\nu}^{(\chi)}+T_{\mu\nu}^{(\rm SM)}\right)\,,

(7)

where
R_{\mu\nu}
is the Ricci tensor,
\kappa\equiv 1/M_{\rm Pl}
and
T_{\mu\nu}^{(i)}
are the energy-momentum tensors for each
i
-th fluid, which for the dark sector scalar fields
\phi
and
\chi
read

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi\nabla_{\beta}\phi+V(\phi)\right)\,,

(8)

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{(\chi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\nabla_{\mu}\chi\nabla_{\nu}\chi-g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{C}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\chi\nabla_{\beta}\chi+C^{2}U(\chi)\right)\,,

(9)

respectively, and obey the following conservation equations:

\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}=Q\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,,\quad\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(\chi)}=-Q\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,,

(10)

which are physically equivalent to the Klein-Gordon equations.

For the background cosmology we consider the case of a flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker Universe, for which the field equations read

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+U_{,\phi}=\frac{1}{2}C_{,\phi}\dot{\chi}^{2}-2CC_{,\phi}V\,,

(11)

\ddot{\chi}+\left(3H+\frac{C_{,\phi}}{C}\dot{\phi}\right)\dot{\chi}+CV_{,\chi}=0\,,

(12)

where
H
is the Hubble expansion rate and over-dots indicate derivatives according to cosmic time
t
.
The energy densities
\rho_{\phi}
and
\rho_{\chi}
and the corresponding pressures
p_{\phi}
and
p_{\chi}
are given by

\rho_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+U(\phi),\ \ \ p_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-U(\phi)\,,

(13)

\rho_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}C\dot{\chi}^{2}+C^{2}V(\chi),\ \ \ p_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}C\dot{\chi}^{2}-C^{2}V(\chi)\,.

(14)

The Friedmann equations, which govern the expansion of the Universe and which can be derived from Einstein’s equations, Eq. (
7
), read

H^{2}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{3}\left[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+U(\phi)+\frac{1}{2}C(\phi)\dot{\chi}^{2}+C(\phi)^{2}V(\chi)\right]\,,

(15)

\dot{H}=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{3}\left[\dot{\phi}^{2}+C(\phi)\dot{\chi}^{2}\right]\,.

(16)

Finally, the Klein-Gordon equations for
\phi
and
\chi
, Eqs. (
73
) and (
73
), become

\dot{\rho}_{\phi}+3H\left(\rho_{\phi}+p_{\phi}\right)=-\frac{C_{,\phi}}{2C}\left(\rho_{\chi}-3p_{\chi}\right)\dot{\phi}\,,

(17)

\dot{\rho}_{\chi}+3H\left(\rho_{\chi}+p_{\chi}\right)=\frac{C_{,\phi}}{2C}\left(\rho_{\chi}-3p_{\chi}\right)\dot{\phi}\,.

(18)

Since we wish for
\phi
to behave as dark matter and
\chi
as dark energy, in this analysis we focus on the following self-interacting scalar field potentials:

V(\chi)=V_{0}e^{-\kappa\lambda\chi}\,,

(19)

U(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi^{2}\,.

(20)

The function
Q
defined in Eq. (
6
) can be written as

Q=\frac{C_{,\phi}}{2C}\left(\rho_{\chi}-3p_{\chi}\right)\,,

(21)

for a field dependent conformal function
C
.
Unlike standard interacting dark energy-dark matter models, we emphasise that the coupling in the present model depends on the DE energy density and pressure, rather than the DM density.

This concludes the derivation of the relevant equations for this theory, which will be used in the next section to derive a fluid-field description for the system.

3
Towards a fluid-field description

We now seek to solve the equations derived in the previous section to study the evolution of both the DM and DE species. The main difference and challenge of our model compared to interacting quintessence models is that the coupling described here by
Q(t)
depends on
\phi
and
\dot{\phi}
, which must be rapidly oscillating for
\phi
to behave like DM. Therefore, we have to perform an analysis based on the time-averaged character of the field over a period of oscillation to extract its average evolution and make the equations more manageable. For the particular conditions under study, the Klein-Gordon equation for
\phi
reads

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+m^{2}\phi=-Q(t)\,.

(22)

We now consider a conformal coupling function linearly dependent on
\phi
,
i.e.

C=1+2\kappa\beta\phi
, where
\beta
is a constant and
\kappa=1/M_{\rm Pl}
. This allows us to decompose the source term in Eq. (
22
) as

Q(t)=Q_{0}(t)+Q_{1}(t)\phi\,,

(23)

with

\displaystyle Q_{0}(t)\equiv-\kappa\beta(\dot{\chi}^{2}-4V)\,,\hskip 56.9055ptQ_{1}(t)\equiv 8\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}V\,,

(24)

such that the equation of motion for
\phi
can be recast into

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+m^{2}_{\rm eff}(t)\phi=-Q_{0}(t)\,,

(25)

where we have defined the effective mass

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\rm eff}(t)\equiv m^{2}+Q_{1}(t)\,.

(26)

Before solving these equations, we want to stress that the following analysis hinges on the form of Eq. (
25
) and any theory which predicts this form for the equation of motion for the DM field, will result in a similar DM phenomenology, as long as the functions
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are slowly varying (and it’s not even a necessary condition that these quantities must depend on the dark energy scalar field). What will potentially change is the dynamics of the DE field. We give another example of such a theory in Appendix B.

We will be interested in the case in which
m>H
and the scalar field is under-damped, allowing for the oscillations to begin before the DM dominated epoch begins. To solve Eq. (
25
) we employ an ansatz for
\phi
:

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\phi_{\rm osc}(t)+A(t)\,,

(27)

where
\phi_{\rm osc}
is the solution to the homogeneous version of the Klein-Gordon equation:

\ddot{\phi}_{\rm osc}+3H\dot{\phi}_{\rm osc}+m^{2}_{\rm eff}(t)\phi_{\rm osc}=0\,,

(28)

while
A(t)
solves

\ddot{A}+3H\dot{A}+m^{2}_{\rm eff}(t)A=-Q_{0}(t)\,.

(29)

On the basis that
A
is sourced by a slowly-evolving function,
Q_{0}
, we make the assumption that
A(t)
itself is slowly varying such that

\ddot{A},3H\dot{A}\ll m^{2}_{\rm eff}A
and thus, we can neglect the first two terms of the above equation. This results in an approximated analytical solution for
A
:

\displaystyle A(t)\approx-\frac{Q_{0}(t)}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}(t)}\,.

(30)

Making use of the Wentzel-Kramer-Brillouin (WKB) approximation, we find the following solution for
\phi_{\rm osc}
:

\phi_{\rm osc}(t)=\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\phi_{+}\sin{(m_{\rm eff}t)}+\phi_{-}\cos{(m_{\rm eff}t)}\right)\,,

(31)

where
m_{0}
is the effective mass taken at
t=t_{0}
, and
\phi_{+}
and
\phi_{-}
are constants. We can then average
\phi
over one oscillation period to get

\langle\phi\rangle=A\,,

(32)

and for its velocity

\langle\dot{\phi}\rangle=\dot{A}\,.

(33)

Likewise, the variance of these quantities becomes,

\langle\phi^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{3}+A^{2}\,,

(34)

\langle\dot{\phi}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}m_{\rm eff}^{2}(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{3}\,,

(35)

where in the last line we have ignored an
\dot{A}^{2}
term, since under our assumptions

\dot{A}\ll m_{\rm eff}A
.

The above expressions are crucial to the final results shown in this section. For standard uncoupled scalar field DM, both the field value and its derivative average out to zero. These equations encode the fact that, in our case, the field does not oscillate around zero, but rather, its averaged value is shifted by
A
due to the interaction. Therefore, the average energy density and pressure of the field are

\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle=\frac{1}{4}(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{3}(m_{\rm eff}^{2}+m^{2})+\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2}\,,

(36)

and

\langle p_{\phi}\rangle=\frac{1}{4}(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{3}(m_{\rm eff}^{2}-m^{2})-\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2}\,,

(37)

respectively. Here we see clearly that the interaction between DM and DE results in a change in the DM pressure, causing its departure from zero.
The pressure can also be expressed as

\langle p_{\phi}\rangle=\left(\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle-\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2}\right)\frac{m^{2}_{\rm eff}-m^{2}}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}+m^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2}\,,

(38)

in which case the equation of state becomes

w_{\phi}\equiv\frac{\langle p_{\phi}\rangle}{\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle}=\left(1-\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle}\right)\frac{m^{2}_{\rm eff}-m^{2}}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}+m^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle}\,.

(39)

Provided the kinetic energy of the dark energy field is small comparatively to its potential,
Q_{1}
is positive and
m_{\rm eff}>m
. This means that the equation of state of dark matter starts by being positive in its early stages. Around matter-dark energy equality, the equation of state begins to decrease and, in fact, becomes increasingly negative, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. The very late-time occurrence of this transition is ascribed to the dependence of the coupling on the dark energy density, implying that the coupling only has a significant impact on
w_{\phi}
when DE begins to dominate. Indeed, from Eq. (
39
), we see that the equation of state parameter for dark matter becomes negative when

\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle<\frac{1+Z}{Z}\,\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2}

(40)

where
Z=(m^{2}_{\rm eff}-m^{2})/(m^{2}_{\rm eff}+m^{2})\approx Q_{1}/2m^{2}
. As
A\approx-Q_{0}/m^{2}
, it turns out that
w_{\phi}<0
when
\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle
drops below

\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle<\frac{Q_{0}^{2}}{Q_{1}}\approx V\approx\rho_{\chi}\,.

(41)

Consequently, the DM pressure becomes appreciably negative only at very low redshift.

Differentiating the averaged density, Eq. (
36
), we obtain

\frac{d\langle{\rho_{\phi}}\rangle}{dt}+3H\left(\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle+\langle p_{\phi}\rangle\right)=\frac{3H}{4}(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{3}(m_{\rm eff}^{2}-m^{2})+m^{2}A\dot{A}\,,

(42)

or equivalently

\frac{d}{dt}\langle{\rho_{\phi}}\rangle+3H\left(\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle-\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2}\right)=m^{2}A\dot{A}\,.

(43)

Had we done the time-averaging of Eq. (
17
) directly, we would conclude that, in fact

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle{\rho_{\phi}}\rangle}{dt}=\langle{\dot{\rho}_{\phi}}\rangle\,.

(44)

We now define an effective equation of state parameter for DM as

w_{\rm eff}=-\frac{1}{\langle{\rho_{\phi}}\rangle}\left(\frac{m^{2}A^{2}}{2}+\frac{m^{2}A\dot{A}}{3H}\right)\,,

(45)

such that Eq. (
43
) becomes

\langle{\dot{\rho}_{\phi}}\rangle+3H\langle{\rho_{\phi}}\rangle(1+w_{\rm eff})=0\,.

(46)

The qualitative behaviour of
w_{\rm eff}
is shown in Fig.

[

]
code to account for the dynamics of the present model. From this, we see that the DM component effectively evolves like DM during matter domination and then transitions into a negative pressure fluid behaviour during DE domination.

Finally, and for completeness, the equation of motion for the dark energy field, obtained by substituting Eqs. (
32
) and (

) into Eq. (

\ddot{\chi}+\left(3H+\frac{2\kappa\beta\dot{A}}{1+2\beta\kappa A}\right)\dot{\chi}+V_{,\chi}\left(1+2\beta\kappa A\right)=0.

Before discussing cosmological perturbations in this setup, we emphasise once more that this framework is indeed more general than the setup presented. In particular, the slowly varying functions
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
might arise from interactions with non-scalar field dark energy sources. For example, if the DE field’s dynamics is negligible, our system reduces to DM coupled conformally to a cosmological constant. Another possibility motivated by string theory would be to have DM interacting with a 3-form DE field

4
Perturbations

We now turn to the study of linear perturbations for our model. This will lead to the derivation of the sound speed, which is crucial to solving the fluid-scalar field equations of motion.

We follow the approach developed in Ref.
[
85
]
to compute the sound speed, considering only scalar perturbations. We adopt the following conventions for the metric scalar perturbations in a general gauge:

\displaystyle\delta g_{00}

\displaystyle=-2\Phi\,,

(48)

\displaystyle\delta g_{i0}

\displaystyle=a\nabla_{i}B\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\delta g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2a^{2}(\delta_{ij}\Psi-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}E)\,,

(50)

where
\Phi
,
\Psi
,
B
and
E
are the four scalar degrees of freedom which can be expanded and decomposed in independently evolving Fourier modes
k
.
This results in the following equations of motion for the perturbations of the scalar fields
\delta\chi
and
\delta\phi
:

\displaystyle\delta\ddot{\chi}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(3H+\frac{C_{,\phi}}{C}\dot{\phi}\right)\delta\dot{\chi}+\left(\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+C(\phi)V_{,\chi\chi}\right)\delta\chi=\dot{\chi}\left(\dot{\Phi}+3\dot{\Psi}-\frac{k}{a}B+\dot{E}\right)

(51)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2C(\phi)V_{,\chi}\Phi-C_{,\phi}V_{,\chi}\delta\phi-\frac{C_{,\phi}}{C}\dot{\chi}\delta\dot{\phi}-\left(\frac{C_{,\phi}}{C}\right)_{,\phi}\dot{\phi}\dot{\chi}\delta\phi

and

\delta\ddot{\phi}+3H\delta\dot{\phi}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta\phi+m^{2}\delta\phi=\dot{\phi}\left(\dot{\Phi}+3\dot{\Psi}-\frac{k}{a}B+\dot{E}\right)+2(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi})\Phi-\delta Q\,,

(52)

Where we have defined
\delta Q
as the perturbation of the coupling function. As for the background evolution, we can expand it as

\delta Q=\delta Q_{0}+\phi\delta Q_{1}+\delta\phi Q_{1}\,,

(53)

such that Eq. (
52
) becomes

\delta\ddot{\phi}+3H\delta\dot{\phi}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta\phi+m^{2}_{\rm eff}\delta\phi=\dot{\phi}\left(\dot{\Phi}+3\dot{\Psi}-\frac{k}{a}B+\dot{E}\right)+2(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi})\Phi-\delta Q_{0}-\phi\delta Q_{1}\,.

(54)

Note that the
\delta Q
term encloses a term proportional to
\Phi
, arising due to the covariant derivatives in the definition of
Q
in Eq. (
6
). The exact form of
\delta Q_{0}
and
\delta Q_{1}
for the particular setup given by Eqs. (
19
)-(
21
) is the following:

\displaystyle\delta Q_{0}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\beta\left(2\dot{\chi}\delta\dot{\chi}-4V_{,\chi}\delta\chi-2\dot{\chi}^{2}\Phi\right)\,,

(55)

\displaystyle\delta Q_{1}

\displaystyle=8\kappa^{2}\beta^{2}V_{,\chi}\delta\chi\,.

(56)

The perturbed perfect fluid quantities are defined in terms of the time-averaged oscillating DM scalar field and its perturbation:

\delta\rho=\langle\dot{\phi}\delta\dot{\phi}-\dot{\phi}^{2}\Phi+m^{2}\phi\delta\phi\rangle\,,

(57)

\delta p=\langle\dot{\phi}\delta\dot{\phi}-\dot{\phi}^{2}\Phi-m^{2}\phi\delta\phi\rangle\,,

(58)

\frac{a}{k}(\rho+p)(v-B)=\langle\dot{\phi}\delta\phi\rangle\,,

(59)

where it should be understood that the fluid variables are themselves averaged quantities.
Bringing together Eqs. (
54
)-(
59
), we arrive at the following expressions for the perturbed continuity equations:

\delta\dot{\rho}+3H(\delta\rho+\delta p)=(\rho+p)\left(3\dot{\Psi}+\dot{E}-\frac{k}{a}v\right)-\langle\delta Q\dot{\phi}\rangle-\langle Q\delta\dot{\phi}\rangle\,,

(60)

\frac{1}{a^{4}(\rho+p)}\frac{d}{dt}[a^{4}(\rho+p)(v-B)]=\frac{k}{a}\Phi+\frac{k}{a(\rho+p)}[\delta p-\langle Q\delta\phi\rangle]\,.

(61)

For convenience and to simplify the calculations, we consider the axion-comoving gauge, which amounts to setting
B=v
. Thus, in this context, Eq. (
61
) reduces to

\Phi=-\frac{\delta p}{\rho+p}+\frac{\langle Q\delta\phi\rangle}{\rho+p}\,.

(62)

Analogous to background fluid-field treatment, we specify the following ansatz for the perturbation of the
\phi
-field:

\delta\phi(k,t)=\delta\phi_{+}(k,t)\sin(m_{\rm eff}t)+\delta\phi_{-}(k,t)\cos(m_{\rm eff}t)+\delta{\mathcal{A}}(t)\,.

(63)

It is important to note that
\delta{\mathcal{A}}(t)
is not just the perturbation of
A(t)
defined in Eq. (
30
), as will be shown explicitly below.

For the rest of the calculation, we will assume a quasi-static approximation following
[
85
]
, and will also be discarding derivatives of
A
and
\delta{\mathcal{A}}
on similar grounds. In comoving gauge we have
\langle\dot{\phi}\delta\phi\rangle=0
from Eq. (
59
), which implies

\delta\phi_{+}\phi_{-}=\delta\phi_{-}\phi_{+}\,.

(64)

Now we can solve Eq. (
54
) to leading order in
H/m
, since the field is oscillating if
m\gg H
. This implies assuming that metric perturbations vary only on cosmological time scales
t\sim H^{-1}\gg m^{-1}
and then grouping the terms in powers of
H/m
,

\delta\ddot{\phi}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta\phi+m_{\rm eff}^{2}\delta\phi=-2(m^{2}_{\rm eff}\phi+Q_{0})\Phi-\delta Q_{0}-\phi\delta Q_{1}\,.

(65)

Once again, splitting the equation and matching the non-oscillating terms, we get:

\delta\ddot{{\mathcal{A}}}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta{\mathcal{A}}+m^{2}_{\rm eff}\delta{\mathcal{A}}=-\delta Q_{0}-\delta Q_{1}A\,,

(66)

which, in the quasi-static approximation, yields

\delta{\mathcal{A}}=-\frac{\delta Q_{0}+\delta Q_{1}A}{\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+m^{2}_{\rm eff}}=-\frac{\delta Q_{0}-\frac{Q_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}^{2}}\delta Q_{1}}{\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+m^{2}_{\rm eff}}\,.

(67)

Substituting the ansatz in Eq. (
63
) into Eq. (
65
) we obtain:

\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{a}{a_{0}}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)^{-1/2}\frac{\delta\phi_{+}}{\phi_{+}}\frac{k^{2}}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}a^{2}}-\frac{1}{2m^{2}_{\rm eff}}\delta Q_{1}\,,

(68)

up to leading order in
H/m
. Replacing this expression into the perturbed fluid equations for
\delta p
and
\delta\rho
, Eqs. (
57
) and (
58
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\delta p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\frac{\delta\phi_{+}}{\phi_{+}}m^{2}_{\rm eff}\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}m^{2}_{\rm eff}}+\frac{Q_{1}}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}}\right]

(69)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle m^{2}A\delta{\mathcal{A}}+\frac{a^{-3}}{4}\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\delta Q_{1}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\frac{\delta\phi_{+}}{\phi_{+}}m^{2}_{\rm eff}\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}m^{2}_{\rm eff}}+2-\frac{Q_{1}}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}}\right]

(70)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle m^{2}A\delta{\mathcal{A}}+\frac{a^{-3}}{4}\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}}\right)(\phi_{+}^{2}+\phi_{-}^{2})\delta Q_{1}\,.

From here, and using Eq. (
68
), we can compute the pressure perturbation, given by

\displaystyle\delta p=c^{2}_{a}\delta\rho-m^{2}A\delta{\mathcal{A}}(1+c^{2}_{a})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho+p}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}}\delta Q_{1}(1-c^{2}_{a})\,.

(71)

where we have defined the effective sound-speed

c^{2}_{a}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}m^{2}_{\rm eff}}+\frac{Q_{1}}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}}}{\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}m^{2}_{\rm eff}}+2-\frac{Q_{1}}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}}}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}m^{2}}+\frac{Q_{1}}{m^{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}m^{2}}+2+\frac{Q_{1}}{m^{2}}}\,,

(72)

and the second equality follows from the definition of
m^{2}_{\rm eff}
in Eq. (
26
). It is important to note that this effective sound speed is akin to that of the non-interacting axion, presented in Refs.
[
85
,
86
]
. Accordingly, and as expected, Eq. (
72
) reduces to that case in the limit where the coupling
\beta
, and therefore
Q_{1}
, is zero. The effect of this coupling can be appreciated in Figs.
2
and
3
. At early times, the
k^{2}/a^{2}m^{2}
term dominates, and we get a similar behaviour to that of the non-interacting case. On the other hand, at late times, when
k^{2}/a^{2}m^{2}\ll Q_{1}
, the sound-speed becomes practically constant, according to
c^{2}_{a}\approx\frac{Q_{1}}{2m^{2}}
. As expected, in the absence of the coupling,
c^{2}_{a}
falls to zero at small redshifts.

Furthermore, according to Eq. (
71
), in contrast to the non-interacting case, the pressure perturbation is not precisely proportional to the density perturbation. This can be interpreted as a non-adiabatic contribution to the pressure perturbation caused by the interaction with the dark energy scalar field
[
87
,
88
,
81
,
82
]
.

5
Summary and Conclusions

In this study we have introduced a novel model of the dark sector, consisting of scalar field dark matter interacting with quintessence dark energy. Our approach involves a conformal coupling between DM and DE via a conformal coupling, with a unique twist: the conformal factor depends on the (fast oscillating) DM field rather than the slowly evolving DE field, as is typically the case. As a result, we have found an effective theory of interacting dark matter and dark energy in which the coupling term is linked to the energy density of DE rather than DM. Our framework not only offers a theoretical basis for many extensively studied models of interacting dark energy found in the existing literature,
e.g.
in Refs.
[
73
,
69
,
89
,
65
,
79
,
90
,
91
,
80
]
, but also introduces significant differences.
Namely, within our setup, the coupling between DM and DE serves to offset the oscillations of the scalar field, resulting in a non-zero average for its equation of state parameter. Upon averaging over these rapid oscillations, we have derived a fluid-field description of the system, revealing a non-zero average value for the field determined by the coupling.
Consequently, the effective DM fluid exhibits a non-zero physical pressure, starkly contrasting with standard IDE models. This physical pressure transitions from slightly positive at early times to negative after DM-DE equality. The main observational effect most likely comes from the change in the DM equation of state at very late times, at a redshift smaller than the redshift of the DE-DM equality. Thus, our work carries implications in the context of searches for non-standard CDM physics and provides theoretical support for such models (see
[
92
,
93
,
94
]
). Notably, the non-standard equation of state of DM impacts the analysis and interpretation of cosmological observational data, such as Pantheon+
[
95
]
or the recently published DESI data
[
96
]
.

At the linear perturbation level, we have computed the pressure perturbation and sound speed of the averaged DM fluid. Our findings reveal terms proportional to the density perturbation
\delta\rho
plus non-adiabatic pressure terms depending on the perturbation of the DE scalar field. Notably, due to the coupling, the adiabatic term deviates from the standard axion scalar field scenario. This results in an effective sound speed that remains non-zero across all scales, unlike the uncoupled case where it vanishes at small
k
. Consequently, we anticipate a slight power suppression in the matter power spectrum at all scales within the interacting scenario considered here.
Furthermore, the influence of non-adiabatic pressure contributions on observables in this context warrants further investigation. A comprehensive study of these points requires a full implementation of the theory in publicly available Boltzmann codes such as
CLASS
or
CAMB
3
3

3

https://github.com/cmbant/CAMB

[
97
]
, a task left for future work.

Appendix A

In the main body of the paper, we considered a conformal coupling function
C
, which depends only on the DM field value,
i.e.
we considered the case of
C(\phi)
. It is possible to consider a more general scenario, in which the
C
depends both on the field and its kinetic term
X=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi
. For completeness, in this appendix, we provide the equations for that case, that is,
C\equiv C(\phi,X)
. The equation of motion for the dark energy field
\chi
can be derived from the action in Eq. (
1
) and reads

\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi-C\frac{dV}{d\chi}=-\frac{\nabla_{\alpha}C}{C}\nabla^{\alpha}\chi\,.

(73)

In the same way, the equation for the DM field
\phi
is

\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi-\frac{\partial U}{\partial\phi}=Q\,,

(74)

where

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial L_{\chi}}{\partial(\nabla_{\mu}\phi)}\right)-\frac{\partial L_{\chi}}{\partial\phi}

(75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left[\nabla^{\mu}\phi\left(Cg^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\chi\nabla_{\beta}\chi+4C^{2}U\right)\frac{C_{,X}}{C}\right]+\frac{C_{,\phi}}{2C}\left[Cg^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\chi\nabla_{\beta}\chi+4C^{2}U\right]\,.

The energy momentum tensor for
\phi
and
\chi
are given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi\nabla_{\beta}\phi+V(\phi)\right)\,,

(76)

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{(\chi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\nabla_{\mu}\chi\nabla_{\nu}\chi-g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{C}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\chi\nabla_{\beta}\chi+C^{2}U(\chi)\right)

(77)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{C_{,X}}{2C}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi\left[Cg^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\chi\nabla_{\beta}\chi+4C^{2}U\right]\,.

We have the following conservation equations:

\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}=Q\nabla_{\nu}\phi;~~~\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(\chi)}=-Q\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,.

(78)

Note that

T^{\mu}_{(\chi)\mu}=-\left[C\nabla^{\mu}\chi\nabla_{\mu}\chi+4C^{2}U(\chi)\right]\left(1+\frac{C_{,X}}{2C}\nabla^{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\mu}\phi\right)\,,

(79)

and so the two terms in Eq. (
75
) are proportional to
T^{\mu}_{(\chi)\mu}
.

In full generality, these equations are difficult to analyse and to recast into a fluid-field description. Nevertheless, the sub-case of
C\equiv C(X)
might be worth investigating. We leave this for future work.

Appendix B

In the main body of the paper we have dealt with an interaction mediated by a conformal coupling which depends on the fast oscillating DM field. In the following we briefly present another example in which DE and DM couple, but not via a conformal coupling. The phenomenology turns out to be very similar to the model presented in Sections
3
and
4
.

The action we consider is

\displaystyle{\cal S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}{\cal R}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-U(\phi)-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi-V(\chi)\right)

(80)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\phi Q_{0}\left(\chi,X^{(\chi)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}Q_{1}\left(\chi,X^{(\chi)}\right)\right)+{\cal S}_{\rm SM}\,,

where
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are coupling functions, depending of the slowly varying DE field
\chi
and its kinetic term
X^{(\chi)}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi
. Let us focus here on the case in which
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
depend on
\chi
only. Then, in a flat FRW universe, the equation of motion for
\phi
is given by (using
U=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi^{2}
)

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+m^{2}\phi=-Q(t)\,.

(81)

where
Q(t)=Q_{0}(\chi)+Q_{1}(\chi)\phi
. This equation is of the same form as Eq. (
22
). Assuming that
\chi
evolves slowly as it represents DE, an analysis similar to that performed in Section
3
would apply for this theory, with the displacement
A
in (
30
) now given by

\displaystyle A(t)\approx-\frac{Q_{0}(\chi)}{m^{2}_{\rm eff}}\,,

(82)

and with an effective mass that is still given by Eq. (
26
).

The equation for the DE field reads

\ddot{\chi}+3H\dot{\chi}+V_{,\chi}+\phi Q_{0,\chi}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}Q_{1,\chi}=0\,.

(83)

We can now employ the time-averaged behaviour of the DM field. The end result is that the dynamics of the DE field is described by

\ddot{\chi}+3H\dot{\chi}+V_{,\chi}-\frac{Q_{0}}{m_{\rm eff}^{2}}Q_{0,\chi}+\frac{2\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle+\frac{Q_{0}^{2}}{m_{\rm eff}^{2}}}{2\left(m_{\rm eff}^{2}+m^{2}\right)}Q_{1,\chi}=0\,,

(84)

where we have used Eqs. (
34
) and (
36
).

At the linear perturbations level, the equation for the DM field is given by Eq. (
52
) and so the expression for the sound-speed is unchanged.

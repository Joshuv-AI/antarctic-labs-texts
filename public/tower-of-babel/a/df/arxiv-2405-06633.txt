Testing f ⁡ ( T ) f(T) cosmologies with HII Hubble diagram and CMB distance priors
Rodrigo Sandoval-Orozco, Celia Escamilla-Rivera, Rebecca Briffa, Jackson Levi Said

Abstract
In this work, we present independent determinations of cosmological parameters and new constraints on f ⁡ ( T ) f(T) cosmologies, employing two new catalogs related to HII galaxy Hubble and CMB distance priors, along with the local standard measurements, SNIa, H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) measurements, growth rate data (RSD), and BAO baselines. We found that the marginalised best-fit C.L. H 0 H_{0} and σ 8 \sigma_{8} parameters within these cosmologies can relax the current cosmological tensions using HIIG data. This produces a larger range of admissible values for the current Hubble constant, and when all baselines are considered, the uncertainty bands for H 0 H_{0} and the matter density parameter reduce significantly.

III.1
Baseline (low-z) local observations

•

H(z)
measurements
. We considered the observational measurements of the Hubble parameter through the Cosmic Chronometers (CC) in which the system of galaxies evolves in a cluster without interacting so small differences in redshift can be measured along with age estimates. The galactic spectra are used to obtain
\mathrm{d}t/\mathrm{d}z
using redshift and the age difference
(
Moresco:2016mzx
)
. The resulting sample contains 31 data points up to
z\sim 2
.

The corresponding
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{CC}}
is given by:

\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{CC}}=\Delta H(z_{i},\Theta)^{T}C^{-1}_{H(z)}\Delta H(z_{i},\Theta),

(17)

where
\Delta H(z_{i},\Theta)=H(z_{i},\Theta)-H_{obs}(z_{i})
and
C^{-1}_{\mathrm{CC}}
is the covariance matrix generated given in
(
Moresco:2020fbm
)
.

•

Pantheon+ SNIa dataset.

We use the 1701 data points provided by the
Pantheon+
sample
Scolnic:2021amr
that measures the apparent distance for 1550 distinct Supernovae Ia (SNIa) events in a redshift range
0.01<z<2.3
. The dataset for the Pantheon+ sample provides SNIa magnitudes corrected for the stretch and colour effects along with the maximum brightness, the mass of the host galaxy, and sky position bias, so to obtain a cosmological useful quantity we need to calculate the distance modulus
\mu=m-M
. The Pantheon+ dataset also includes SH0ES measurements of Cepheid distances to determine
H_{0}
and
M
at the same time without the necessity of including a prior in the parameters
Sandoval-Orozco:2023pit
. Hereafter, this data set will be referred to as PN
+
.

Thus, the
\chi^{2}_{\text{SN}}
for the Pantheon+ sample is given by

\chi^{2}_{\text{PN${}^{+}$}}=\Delta\mu(z_{i},\Theta)^{T}C^{-1}_{\text{PN${}^{+}$}}\Delta\mu(z_{i},\Theta)+\ln\quantity(\frac{S}{2\pi})-\frac{k^{2}(\Theta)}{S},

(18)

where
C^{-1}_{\text{PN${}^{+}$}}
is the total covariance matrix for the data,
S
is the sum of all components of the inverse of the matrix and
k(\Theta)=\Delta\mu(z_{i},\Theta)^{T}C^{-1}_{\text{PN${}^{+}$}}
. To use SH0ES Cepheids host distances, the SNIa, and the residuals will be calculated as
(
Brout:2022vxf
)
:

\Delta\mu(z_{i},\Theta)=\left\{\begin{matrix}\mu_{\text{obs}}(z_{i})-\mu_{i}^{\text{Cepheids}}&i\in\text{Cepheid hosts,}\\
\mu_{\text{obs}}(z_{i})-\mu(z_{i},\Theta)&\text{ in other case.}\end{matrix}\right.

(19)

For this cases, the distance modulus
\mu(z)
can be calculated as:

\mu(z_{i},\Theta)=5\log\quantity[D_L(z_i,\Theta)]+M,

(20)

and where
D_{L}(z_{i},\Theta)
is the luminosity distance given as:

D_{L}(z_{i},\Theta)=c(1+z_{i})\int_{0}^{z_{i}}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{H(z^{\prime},\Theta)},

(21)

where
c
is the speed of light and
H(z_{i},\Theta)
is the Hubble parameter.

•

Baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data.

We will consider a joint baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data set composed of several independent data points from different sources. This BAO data set includes measurements from the SDSS Main Galaxy Sample at
z_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.15

Ross:2014qpa
, the six-degree Field Galaxy Survey at
z_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.106

2011MNRAS.416.3017B
, and the BOSS DR11 quasar Lyman-alpha measurement at
z_{\mathrm{eff}}=2.4

Bourboux:2017cbm
. We will also use the angular diameter distances and
H(z)
measurements of the SDSS-IV eBOSS DR14 quasar survey at
z_{\mathrm{eff}}=\{0.98,1.23,1.52,1.94\}

Zhao:2018gvb
, along with the SDSS-III BOSS DR12 consensus BAO measurements of the Hubble parameter and the corresponding comoving angular diameter distances at
z_{\mathrm{eff}}=\{0.38,0.51,0.61\}

Alam:2016hwk
. For these two BAO data sets, we consider the full covariance matrix in our MCMC analyses as there is an overlap in the redshift slices.

To use the aforementioned datasets we need to compute additional distances as the angular diameter distance:

D_{V}(z)=\left[(1+z)^{2}D_{A}(z)^{2}\frac{cz}{H(z)}\right]^{1/3},

(22)

where
D_{A}(z)=(1+z)^{-2}D_{L}(z)
is the angular diameter distance. An additional quantity is necessary to perform the BAO analyses as it appears
r_{s,\mathrm{fid}}
and
r_{s}(z_{d})=r_{d}
. The value of the first one will be fixed according to the mentioned references as the different results correspond to a specific value. The
r_{d}
value will be a free parameter in the MCMC analysis.

The corresponding
\chi^{2}
for the BAO data is calculated using

\chi^{2}_{\text{BAO}}(\Theta)=\Delta G(z_{i},\Theta)^{T}C_{\text{BAO}}^{-1}\Delta G(z_{i},\Theta)

(23)

where
\Delta G(z_{i},\Theta)=G(z_{i},\Theta)-G_{\text{obs}}(z_{i})
using
G(z_{i},\Theta)
the corresponding vector for each of the observations used.
C_{\text{BAO}}
is the covariance matrix of all the considered BAO observations.

III.2
High-z Hubble diagram

•

HIIG and GHIIR Hubble Diagram (High-z HIIG)

The HII Galaxies or Giant Extragalactic HII regions GHIIR (that will be referred to as HII from now on) are known to satisfy a relation between the Luminosity in the Balmer Line of Hydrogen
L\mathrm{H}\beta
and the velocity dispersion
\sigma

(
Gonzalez-Moran:2021drc
)
. This relation is used as a cosmological distance indicator. HII regions are compact mass systems whose luminosity is composed almost entirely from a young burst of stellar formation with strong narrow emission lines that can be studied in detail
(
Chavez:2014ria
)
.
As we mentioned in the Introduction, the
L-\sigma
relation can be written

\log{L}=\alpha\log{\sigma}+\beta,

(24)

that will be used to calculate the observed distance modulus, which can be expressed from the complete set of observables as
(
Gonzalez-Moran:2019uij
)

\mu_{i,\mathrm{obs}}=2.5(\alpha+\beta\log{\sigma}-\log{f}-40.08),

(25)

where
\sigma
is the mentioned velocity dispersion of the object.
\alpha
and
\beta
are the intercept and slope of the
L-\sigma
relation and
\log{f}
is the measured flux around the continuum. The data points are provided with the flux, the Luminosity
L
and the dispersion
\sigma
.

The corresponding
\chi^{2}
for the HII sample is calculated using:

\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{HII}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\frac{\left[\mu_{i,\mathrm{obs}}-\mu(z_{i},\Theta)\right]^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},

(26)

where
\mu(z_{i},\Theta)
is the theoretical distance modulus.
\varepsilon
is the error associated with the measurements that include the statistical uncertainties for every mentioned parameter

\varepsilon^{2}=6.25(\varepsilon^{2}_{\log{f}}+\beta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}_{\log{\sigma}}+\varepsilon^{2}_{\beta}\log{\sigma^2}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{2}),

(27)

Although
\alpha
and
\beta
are free nuisance parameters, the variation between the numerical values of the parameters for the different models is too small to be considered in this analysis. We will employ
\alpha=33.268\pm 0.083
and
\beta=5.022\pm 0.058
consistent with the values reported in
(
Gonzalez-Moran:2019uij
;
Gonzalez-Moran:2021drc
;
Yang:2024epu
;
Cao:2023eja
)

A sample of 181 points is used in this analysis from
(
Gonzalez-Moran:2021drc
)
. Due to the bright nature of the star formation burst, HII composes a promising dataset as they reach
z\sim 4

and could help to complete the Hubble diagram at higher redshifts where SNIa is lacking data points.

•

Growth rate data (RSD)
. The growth rate catalog considered in this work is reported in
Alestas:2022gcg
. The catalog consists of measurements of the growth of cosmic structure, so-called Redshift Space Distortion (RSD). In standard scenarios, the peculiar velocity of galaxies causes cosmic high-density regions larger in the line-of-sight direction at small
z
, given by consequence maps of galaxies where distances are measured from spectroscopic redshifts show anisotropic deviations from the true galaxy distribution. From these results, this baseline can be important to test modified or extended theories of gravity. To derive the growth rate
f(z)
, we can use RSD cosmological probes to constrain cosmological models
Lambiase:2018ows
;
Gonzalez:2016lur
;
Gupta:2011kw
. However, instead of deriving the growth rate directly, Large Scale Surveys (LSS) report the density-weighted growth rate,
f\sigma_{8}\equiv f(z)\sigma_{8}(z)
.
This RSD baseline can be used to constrain cosmological parameters, in particular
\sigma_{8,0}
, e.g. the
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{min}}
, which is given by

\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{RSD}}=\Delta Q(z_{i},\Theta)^{T}C_{\text{RSD}}^{-1}\Delta Q(z_{i},\Theta)

(28)

where
Q(z_{i},\Theta)=(f\sigma_{8}(z_{i},\Theta)_{\mathrm{theo}}-f\sigma{{}_{8}{}_{\mathrm{obs}}}(z_{i}))
and
C_{\text{RSD}}^{-1}
is the inverse covariance matrix assumed to be a diagonal matrix. The total covariance matrix is given by

C_{\text{RSD}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}^{2}&0&0&\dots\\
0&C_{\text{WiggleZ}}&0&\dots\\
0&0&\dots&\sigma_{N}^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}\,.

(29)

Notice that
f\sigma_{8}(z_{i},\Theta)_{\mathrm{obs}}
is derived from the compiled observational baseline described above.

•

CMB (Planck 2018) distance priors.

The additional sample used for the high redshift universe will be the CMB distance priors (referred to only as CMB in the text). This is a dataset created to provide information on the CMB power spectrum without the calculation of the complete set of perturbations
Zhai:2019nad
;
Chen:2018dbv
.

The presented quantities for this dataset are the
shift parameter

R
that measures the peak spacing of the temperature in the power spectrum, the
acoustic scale

l_{\mathrm{A}}
that measures the temperature in the transverse direction and the combination
\Omega_{b}h^{2}

Chen:2018dbv
. Those quantities are known as the distance priors and are going to be calculated as:

l_{\mathrm{A}}=(1+z_{*})\frac{\pi D_{A}(z_{*})}{r_{s}(z_{*})},

(30)

where
z_{d}
is the redshift at photon the decoupling epoch. This redshift value will be fixed to
z=1089
in concordance with
(
Planck:2018vyg
)
. Additionally:

R=\frac{(1+z_{*})D_{A}(z_{*})\sqrt{\Omega_{m}H_{0}^{2}}}{c},

(31)

with
c
the light speed. Similar to the analyses performed in BAO,
r_{s}(z_{*})=r_{*}
will be used as an additional free parameter. We will use the distance priors as
R=1.7502\pm 0.0046,l_{\mathrm{A}}=301.471\pm 0.090,\Omega_{b}h^{2}=0.02236\pm 0.00015
with the correlation matrix
c_{ij}
given as

c_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.46&-0.66\\
0.46&1&-0.33\\
-0.66&-0.34&1\end{pmatrix},

(32)

that need to be transformed to a covariance matrix
C_{ij}
using
C_{ij}=c_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}
with the associated errors for every measurement. So, the
\chi^{2}
used for the CMB distance priors is

\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{CMB}}=\Delta V(\Theta)^{T}C_{\text{CMB}}^{-1}\Delta V(\Theta),

(33)

where
V(\Theta)=\left[R(\Theta),l_{\mathrm{A}}(\Theta),\Omega_{b}h^{2}\right]
and
V=(R,l_{\mathrm{A}},\Omega_{b}h^{2})
with
\Delta V=V(\Theta)-V
. The normalized Hubble parameter also appears as
h=H_{0}/100
km/s/Mpc.

V
Conclusions

In this study, we have presented the behaviour of parameters across various combinations of datasets. We assessed three prominent
f(T)
gravity models, evaluating their performance against five distinct combinations of observational datasets. Our combinations included CC+PN
+

Our study provides valuable insights into the behaviour of these
f(T)

Moving forward, the intention is to use these observations and extend the analysis to encompass the full comprehensive CMB power spectra, along with other early Universe datasets. This broader approach will allow for a more thorough assessment of the competitiveness of these models and their ability to accurately describe the Universe’s evolution.

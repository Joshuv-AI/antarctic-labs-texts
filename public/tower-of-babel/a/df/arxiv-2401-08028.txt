Improved regularity for minimizing capillary hypersurfaces

Otis Chodosh

Stanford University

Nick Edelen

University of Notre Dame

Chao Li

Courant Institute

New York University

Communicated by Guido De Philippis

Abstract.

We give improved estimates for the size of the singular set of minimizing capillary hypersurfaces: the singular set is always of codimension at least
4
, and this estimate improves if the capillary angle is close to
0
,
\frac{\pi}{2}
, or
\pi
. For capillary angles that are close to
0
or
\pi
, our analysis is based on a rigorous connection between the capillary problem and the one-phase Bernoulli problem.

Keywords.
Capillary hypersurfaces, Regularity, One-phase Bernoulli problem.

1.
Introduction

Given a smooth Riemannian
(n+1)
-manifold with boundary
(X^{n+1},h)
, functions
\sigma\in C^{1}(\partial X)
satisfying
\sigma\in(-1,1)
and
g\in C^{1}(X)
, and an open subset
E\subset X
, the Gauss’ free energy is

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}(E)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}E\cap\mathring{X})-\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial X}\sigma(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}+\int_{E}g(x)dx.

Stationary points (subject to a volume contraint) to the Gauss’ free energy model the equilibrium state of incompressible fluids. Indeed, letting
X
be the container and
E
the portion of the fluid, the three terms in (
1.1
) represent the free surface energy (proportional to area of the separating surface), the wetting energy (modeling the adhesion of the fluid with the wall of the container), and the gravitational energy, respectively. The interface,
M=\partial E\cap\mathring{X}
, is usually called a
capillary surface
. Formally, if
E
is a stationary point of
\mathcal{A}
, then the capillary surface
M
should have prescribed mean curvature
g
and boundary contact angle
\cos^{-1}(\sigma)
. However, it is well-known that minimizers of
\mathcal{A}
may have a singular set. The main result of this paper gives improved bounds for the size of this singular set.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that the following holds. Let
(X^{n+1},h)
be a smooth manifold with boundary,
\sigma\in C^{1}(\partial X)
,
-1<\sigma<1
, and
g\in C^{1}(X)
. Suppose that
E\subset X
is a Caccioppoli set that minimizes (
1.1
). Then
M=\spt(\partial E\cap\mathring{X})
is a smooth hypersurface of
M
away from a closed set
\sing(M)
.

(1)

We always have that
\dim(\sing(M)\cap\partial X)\leqslant n-4
;

(2)

Let
S_{1}=\{x\in\partial X:\sigma(x)\in(-1,-1+\varepsilon_{0})\cup(1-\varepsilon_{0},1)\}
. Then

\dim(\sing(M)\cap S_{1})\leqslant n-5.

(3)

Let
S_{2}=\{x\in\partial X:\sigma(x)\in(-\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1})\}
. Then

\dim(\sing(M)\cap S_{2})\leqslant n-7.

Previously, the best known partial regularity,
\dim\sing(M)\leqslant n-3
, was proven by Taylor in
[
26
]
. This was extended to the anisotropic case (extending the free surface energy to an elliptic integral depending on the surface unit normal) by De Philippis–Maggi in
[
10
]
. When
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, the surface
M
is usually called a free-boundary minimal surface, and satisfies the sharp estimate
\dim\sing(M)\leqslant n-7
by Grüter–Jost
[
16
]
. Capillary surfaces have shown potential applications in comparison geometry (see, e.g.
[
19
,
20
]
), where a major stumbling block was the lack a satisfactory boundary regularity in the form of Theorem
1.1
.

By the
\varepsilon
-regularity and compactness theorems due to De Philippis–Maggi
[
10
]
, the monotonicity formula of Kagaya-Tonegawa
[
18
]
(see also
[
9
]
), and Federer’s dimension reduction, Theorem
1.1
follows from the classification of capillary minimizing cones with an isolated singularity. Thus, for every open set
U\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
, let us consider the functional (recall that
\cos\theta=\sigma
by the first variation)

\mathcal{A}_{U}^{\theta}(\Omega)=|\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}\cap U|-\cos\theta|\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\partial\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}\cap U|.

We denote
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{R}^{n+1}}^{\theta}
simply by
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
, where
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x_{1}>0\}
is an open half space. We say that a Cacciopolli set
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
minimizes
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
, if for every pre-compact open set
U\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
,

\mathcal{A}_{U}^{\theta}(\Omega)\leqslant\mathcal{A}_{U}^{\theta}(\Omega^{\prime}),

for every Cacciopolli set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
such that
\Omega\Delta\Omega^{\prime}
is compactly contained in
U
.

Theorem
1.1
is thus a consequence of the following classification result for cones.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}>0
such that the following holds. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
be a minimizing cone for
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
and
M=\partial\Omega\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Assume that
M
is smooth away from
0
. Suppose one of the following holds:

(1)

n\leqslant 3
;

(2)

n=4
,
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0})\cup(\pi-\theta_{0},\pi)
;

(3)

4\leqslant n\leqslant 6
,
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{1},\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta_{1})
.

Then
M
is flat.

Since any
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
-minimizer
\Omega
gives a
\mathcal{A}^{\pi-\theta}
-minimizer
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}\setminus\Omega
, we may, without loss of generality, assume that
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. With this in mind, each of the three cases in Theorem
1.2
is treated differently. Case (1) follows from an Almgren-type argument as in
[
1
]
by analyzing the stability inequality on the spherical link
\Sigma=M\cap S^{n}(1)
, and a topological classification result for embedded capillary disks analogous to Fraser–Schoen
[
15
]
in the free-boundary case. Case (3) is analogous to the previous work by Li–Zhou–Zhu on the stable Bernstein theorem for capillary surfaces
[
21
, Appendix C]
, which follows from a careful Simons-type computation as in
[
25
]
, extending the one for stable free-boundary minimal hypersurfaces.

1.1.
Relationship with one-phase free boundary problem

The most interesting part of Theorem
1.2
is Case (2), and it relies on a novel application of the connection between the capillary problem and the one-phase Bernoulli free boundary problem. To motivate the discussion, let us assume, for simplicity, that
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x_{1}>0\}
, and
M
is contained in the graph over the
x_{1}
-direction of a Lipschitz function
u
- i.e. there exists
u\in\Lip(\mathbf{R}^{n}=\{x_{1}=0\})
such that

M=\{(u(x^{\prime}),x^{\prime}):x^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n},u(x^{\prime})>0\},

here
x^{\prime}=(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
. Set
v=\frac{u}{\tan\theta}
. Assuming that
|\Lip(v)|
is uniformly bounded as
\theta\to 0
, we compute that

(1.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}^{\theta}(\Omega)&=\int_{\{u>0\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}}\left(\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}-\cos\theta 1_{\{u>0\}}\right)dx^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{\{u>0\}}\left(\sqrt{1+\tan^{2}\theta|Dv|^{2}}-\cos\theta 1_{\{v>0\}}\right)dx^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{\{u>0\}}\left((1-\cos\theta)1_{\{v>0\}}+\frac{1}{2}\tan^{2}\theta|Dv|^{2}+O(\theta^{3})\right)dx^{\prime}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\tan^{2}\theta\int_{\{u>0\}}\left(|Dv|^{2}+1_{\{v>0\}}+O(\theta)\right)dx^{\prime}\end{split}

Thus, as
\theta\to 0
,
v=\frac{u}{\tan\theta}
should be an one-homogeneous minimizer of the functional

(1.3)

J(v)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\left(|Dv|^{2}+1_{\{v>0\}}\right)dx^{\prime}.

The functional (
1.3
) is the well-known one-phase Alt–Caffarelli functional, and the above argument shows heuristically that
J
should be the linearization of
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
as
\theta\to 0
.

There is a vast literature concerning regularity of minimizers of the Alt–Caffarelli functional; see, for example,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
27
]
. We also refer the readers to the book by Caffarelli–Salsa
[
7
]
for an excellent expository of the problem. We note that the idea of
J
being the linearization of
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
is well-known to the experts (see e.g.
[
4
,
14
]
and
[
13
, Section 5]
), but this seems the first time that the fine approximation (
1.2
) has been rigorously established and used to deduce geometric consequences.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\theta_{i}\to 0+
,
\Omega_{i}
be a sequence of cones minimizing
\mathcal{A}^{\theta_{i}}
with an isolated singularity at
0
, and
M_{i}=\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Then for
i
sufficiently large,
M_{i}
is contained in the graph of a Lipschitz function
u_{i}
over
\mathbf{R}^{n}=\partial\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Moreover, up to a subsequence,
\{\frac{u_{i}}{\tan\theta_{i}}\}
converges in
(W^{1,2}_{loc}\cap C^{\alpha})(\mathbf{R}^{n})
to an one-homogeneous minimizer
v
to the Alt–Caffarelli functional
J
for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and the free-boundaries
\partial\{u_{i}>0\}\to\partial\{v>0\}
in the local Hausdorff distance.

Therefore a smooth (away from
0
) capillary cone with small angle has a fine approximation in by
1
-homogenous minimizers to
J
. Additionally, in low dimensions (e.g.
n\leqslant 4
by
[
6
,
17
]
), it is known that an one-homogeneous minimizer to (
1.3
) is
(x\cdot\mathbf{n})_{+}
for some unit vector
\mathbf{n}
. In this case, we show that the improved convergence

\frac{u_{i}}{\tan\theta_{i}}\to v

holds actually in
C^{2}
away from the origin. We use this to prove case (2) of Theorem
1.2
. More generally, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Assume that the only one-homogeneous minimizers of
J
on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
are given by
(x\cdot\mathbf{n})_{+}
for some unit vector
\mathbf{n}
. Then there exists
\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}(n)>0
, such that any minimizing cone for
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with an isolated singularity at
0
is flat.

1.2.
Organization

In Section 2 we recall some preliminaries for capillary surfaces, minimal cones in the Euclidean space and the Alt-Caffarelli functional. In Section 3 we prove case (1) of Theorem
1.2
. We carry out the rigorous relation between the capillary problem and the one-phase Bernoulli problem in Section 4, and prove case (2) of Theorem
1.2
. Finally, we prove case (3) of Theorem
1.2
in Section 5.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

We take
(X,h)
to be a smooth Riemannian manifold with boundary,
E\subset X
an open set with
M=\partial E\subset\mathring{X}
smooth. Let
\nu
be the unit normal vector field of
M
in
E
that points into
E
,
\eta
be the unit normal vector field of
\partial M
in
M
that points out of
X
,
\overline{\eta}
be the unit normal vector field of
\partial X
in
X
that points outward
X
,
\bar{\nu}
be the unit normal vector field of
\partial M
in
\partial X
that points into
\partial E\cap\partial X
. Let
S:T_{M}\to T_{M}
denote the shape operator defined by
S(Y)=-\nabla_{Y}\nu
, and
A
the second fundamental form on
M
given as
A(Y,Z)=\langle S(Y),Z\rangle=\langle\nabla_{Y}Z,\nu\rangle
. Particularly, if
M
is unit sphere in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
viewed as the boundary of the unit ball
E
then we have that
S=\text{id}
and
A=g_{M}
.

We write
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}:x_{1}>0\}
,
\mathbf{R}^{n}=\partial\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x_{1}=0\}
, and let
\pi:\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
be the orthogonal projection. Given any set
S
, let
d(x,S)
be the Euclidean distance from
x
to
S
. We denote by
B_{r}(S)=\{x:d(x,S)<r\}
. Constants of the form
c
or
c_{i}
will always be
\geqslant 1
.

2.2.
First and second variation of capillary functional

Say
E
is stationary for the capillary functional (
1.1
), if
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\mathcal{A}(E_{t})\geqslant 0
for all variations
E_{t}
. Take
M=\partial E\cap\mathring{X}
. The stationary condition is equivalent to

H_{M}=g\text{ in }\mathring{X},\quad\langle\nu,\bar{\eta}\rangle=\cos\theta\text{ on }\partial M\cap\partial X.

E
is stable for (
1.1
), if
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}|_{t=0}\mathcal{A}(E_{t})\geqslant 0
for all variations
E_{t}
. The stability condition is equivalent to

(2.1)

\int_{M}\left(|\nabla_{M}f|^{2}-(|A_{M}|^{2}+\Ric(\nu,\nu))f^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n}-\cot\theta\int_{\partial M}A_{M}(\eta,\eta)f^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geqslant 0,

for all
f\in C_{0}^{1}(M)
. We refer the readers to
[
22
, Section 1]
for the deductions. Particularly, if
X=\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
, then stability gives

(2.2)

\int_{M}(|\nabla_{M}f|^{2}-|A_{M}|^{2}f^{2})d\mathcal{H}^{n}-\cot\theta\int_{\partial M}A_{M}(\eta,\eta)f^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geqslant 0.

We write
L_{M}=\Delta_{M}+|A_{M}|^{2}
the Jacobi operator.

We will often work with capillary stable (minimizing) cones
\Omega
in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. By definition,
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is stationary and stable (minimizing) for
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. We say
\Omega
is a
smooth cone
, if
M=\partial\Omega\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is smooth away from the origin.

2.3.
The Alt-Caffarelli functional

For an open set
U\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
u\in W^{1,2}(U)
, write

J_{U}(u)=\int_{U}|Du|^{2}+\chi_{U\cap\{u>0\}}dx

for the Alt–Caffarelli functional. We will say that
u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(U)
minimizes
J_{U}
if for every
U^{\prime}\subset\subset U
and every
w-u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(U^{\prime})
we have
J_{U^{\prime}}(u)\leqslant J_{U^{\prime}}(w)
. If
U=\mathbf{R}^{n}
we will simply write
J_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}=J
, and call any minimizer of
J_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
an entire minimizer of
J
.

We require the following basic fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u\in W_{\textnormal{loc}}^{1,2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
be an entire minimizer of
J
. Then there exists a constant
c_{0}(n)>0
such that

\frac{1}{c_{0}}d(x,\partial\{u>0\})\leqslant u(x)\leqslant d(x,\partial\{u>0\}).

Proof.

The lower bound follow from
[
2
, Corollary 3.6]
, and the sharp upper bound is a consequence of the estimate
|Du|\leqslant 1
(see, e.g.
[
17
, Section 2.2]
).
∎

We will also make important use of the classification of entire minimizers.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
17
]
)
.

Assume
n\leqslant 4
. Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
be an entire minimizer of
J
. Then
u=(x\cdot\textbf{n})_{+}
for some unit vector
n
.

Proof.

The classification of
1
-homogenous minimizers is due to
[
17
]
. The same classification holds for entire minimizers that are not a priori
1
-homogenous by an obvious blow-down argument and the Weiss monotonicity
[
28
]
.
∎

3.
The case
n=3

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is a cone, stationary and stable for
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
, and
M=\partial\Omega\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Assume that
M
has an isolated singularity so that
\Sigma=M\cap S^{n}(1)
is smooth. Then we have that

(3.1)

\inf_{f\in C^{1}(\Sigma)}\frac{\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla_{\Sigma}f|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}f^{2}\right)-\cot\theta\int_{\partial\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}(\eta,\eta)f^{2}}{\int_{\Sigma}f^{2}}\geqslant-\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

This follows from
[
5
, Lemma 4.5]
. Note that any homogeneous extension of the first eigenfunction with the capillary boundary condition (i.e.
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\eta}=\cot\theta A(\eta,\eta)f
) satisfies the same boundary condition.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
when
n=3
.

When
n=3
, (
3.1
) becomes

\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}f|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}f^{2})-\cot\theta\int_{\partial\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}(\eta,\eta)f^{2}\geqslant-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}f^{2},

for every
f\in C^{1}(\Sigma)
. Setting
f=1
and using the traced Gauss equation to write
|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}=1-K_{\Sigma}+\tfrac{1}{2}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}
, we have that

\int_{\Sigma}K_{\Sigma}-\cot\theta\int_{\partial\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}(\eta,\eta)\geqslant\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}.

Using
[
19
, (3.8)]
, one finds that
\cot\theta A_{\Sigma}(\eta,\eta)=-k_{g}
, where
k_{g}
is the geodesic curvature of
\partial\Sigma
with respect to the outward unit normal vector field. Thus, the above gives

\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma)\leqslant\int_{\Sigma}K_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}k_{g}=2\pi\chi(\Sigma).

In particular, we have that
\chi(\Sigma)>0
and thus
\Sigma
is a topological disk.

We then proceed exactly as in
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
. Precisely, let
u:D\to S^{n}_{+}
be the proper minimal embedding such that
\Sigma=u(D)
. Take the standard complex coordinates
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
, and consider the function

\phi(z)=A_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{2}.

Here
A_{\Sigma}(\partial_{z},\partial_{z})
is the second fundamental form in these coordinates, given by
A_{\Sigma}(\partial_{z},\partial_{z})=\langle\nabla_{du(\partial_{z})}du(\partial_{z}),\nu\rangle
. By
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
, we have that
\phi
is a holomorphic function in
D
. Now consider the boundary behavior of
\phi
. For this, take
(r,\theta)
the polar coordinates on
D
, so that
z=re^{i\theta}
. Set
w=\log z=\log r+i\theta
. Then
A(\partial_{z},\partial_{z})=\frac{1}{z^{2}}A(\partial_{w},\partial_{w})
. Along
\partial\Sigma
, we have that

du(\frac{\partial}{\partial r})=\lambda\eta=\lambda(\cos\theta\bar{\eta}+\sin\theta\bar{\nu}),\quad\nu=\cos\theta\bar{\nu}-\sin\theta\bar{\eta},

here
\lambda=|du(\frac{\partial}{\partial r})|
. Consequently, we conclude that

A_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)=\lambda\left\langle\nabla_{du(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta})}(\cos\theta\bar{\eta}+\sin\theta\bar{\nu}),\cos\theta\bar{\nu}-\sin\theta\bar{\eta}\right\rangle=0,

because
|\bar{\nu}|=|\bar{\eta}|=1
and
\partial S^{n}_{+}
is totally geodesic. Consequently, the same argument as in
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
shows that
z^{4}\phi(z)
vanishes identically on
D
, and that
\Sigma
is totally geodesic. ∎

4.
The case
n=4
with
\theta
close to
0

In this section, we consider
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
that minimize the functional

\mathcal{A}^{\theta}(\Omega)=|\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}|-\cos\theta|\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\partial_{+}\mathbf{R}^{n+1}|

in
B_{1}
or (later) in compact subsets of
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Here
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. We say
\Omega
is a smooth minimizer of
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
in
U
if
M:=\partial\Omega\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is a smooth hypersurface in
U
, which extends smoothly up to the solid boundary
\partial\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Most of our work in this Section is focused on proving the following convergence theorem, which is essentially a generalization/refinement of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\theta_{i}\to 0+
,
\Omega_{i}
be a sequence of minimizers of
\mathcal{A}^{\theta_{i}}
in
B_{1}
which are smooth (resp. conical with an isolated singularity at
0
), and
M_{i}=\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Then for a dimensional constant
\varepsilon(n)
and
i
sufficiently large,
M_{i}\cap B_{1/2}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
(resp.
M_{i}\cap B_{1/2}
) is contained in the graph of a Lipschitz function
u_{i}
over
B_{1/2}^{n}\equiv\partial\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}\cap B_{1/2}
. Moreover, up to a subsequence,
\theta_{i}^{-1}u_{i}
converges in
(W^{1,2}_{loc}\cap C^{\alpha}_{loc})(B_{1/4}^{n})
to a minimizer
v
(resp. one-homogenous minimizer
v
) to the Alt–Caffarelli functional
J
for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and the free-boundaries
\partial\{u_{i}>0\}\to\partial\{v>0\}
in the local Hausdorff distance in
B_{1/4}^{n}
.

If
n\leqslant 4
, then
v
is entirely regular (resp. linear), and the convergence
\theta_{i}^{-1}u_{i}\to v
is
C^{2,\alpha}_{loc}
in
B_{1/4}^{n}
. Near the free-boundary this is interpreted in the sense of the Hodograph transform.

Remark 4.2
.

More generally, in Theorem
4.1
(and Lemma
4.14
) instead of assuming
n\leqslant 4
one can assume
n+1\leqslant k^{*}
, where
k^{*}
is the smallest dimension in which there is a non-linear

-homogenous minimizer of
J
in
\mathbf{R}^{k^{*}}
. The value of
k^{*}
is not yet known, but by the works of
[
17
]
,
[
12
]
,

.

4.1.
Preliminaries

Our first Lemma says that if the interior surface
M:=\partial\Omega\cap\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is smooth (up to the boundary), then in a region uniform far away from the interface
\partial M\cap B_{1}\subset\partial\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
,
M
is mass-minimizing in the sense of boundaries.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth minimizer of
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
in
B_{1}
, and let

Theorem 4.4
.

The graph of

n

. Let us define the hypersurface

so that, because

\partial U=[\graph_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(u)\cap U]-[\graph_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(v)\cap U]+S

(here we equip the graphs with an orientation so its unit normal vectors point into positive

direction). Now since

, we can make the comparison

\mathcal{A}^{\theta}(\Omega)\leqslant\mathcal{A}^{\theta}(\{0<x_{1}<\min(u(\pi(x)),v(\pi(x)))\})

and thereby compute:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/4}^{n}\cap\{u>0\}}\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}-\cos\theta dx\leqslant\int_{\{u>0\}\cap B_{1/2}^{n}\setminus B_{1/4}^{n}}\sqrt{1+|D\min(u,v)|^{2}}-\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\graph_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(v)\cap U)-\mathcal{H}^{n}(\graph_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(u)\cap U)\leqslant\int_{\graph_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(v)\cap U}\omega-\int_{\graph_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(u)\cap U}\omega

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\int_{S}\omega=\int_{\partial B_{1/4}^{n}}\frac{\partial_{r}v}{\sqrt{1+|Dv|^{2}}}u\leqslant c(n)\varepsilon\theta\int_{\partial B_{1/4}^{n}}u.

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}-|Du|\theta-1

\displaystyle\geqslant\sqrt{4-\theta^{2}}-1

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-\theta^{2}/3

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-\theta^{2}/2+\theta^{4}/24

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n)\int_{B_{1/4}^{n}}(|Du|+u)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n)\theta^{-1}\int_{\{u>0\}\cap B_{1/4}^{n}}(|Du|\theta+1-\cos\theta)dx

4.2.

\text{either}\quad\frac{1}{2c_{0}}\tan\theta_{i}d(0,\partial M_{i})=z_{i,1}^{\prime}\quad\text{or}\quad z_{i,1}^{\prime}=2\tan\theta_{i}d(0,\partial M_{i}^{\prime}).

From (

∎

Lemma 4.14

(Curvature estimates)

There are absolute constants

Lemma 4.15

(Rigidity of almost-planar capillary cones with small angle)
.

There is a
\delta(n)
so that the following holds. Let

\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
making contact angle

(Note, though we don’t explicitly state it as a hypothesis,

Proof.

We first note the following trace-type inequality: provided

is small, we have for any non-negative

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}f

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{\partial\Sigma}f(-\eta\cdot e_{n+1})=2\int_{\Sigma}\Div_{\Sigma}(-fe_{n+1})

(4.52)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Sigma}\nabla_{\Sigma}f\cdot(-e_{n+1})+f\Div_{\Sigma}(-e_{n+1})\leqslant 2\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}f|+(n-1)f,

having used that
\Div_{\Sigma}(e_{n+1})=n-1
.

We now take Simons’ equation on
\Sigma
(see
[
25
, Theorem 5.3.1]
),

\Delta_{\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}=(n-1-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2})A_{\Sigma},

and inner product with
A_{\Sigma}
and integrate over

(4.53)

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+(n-1-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2})|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{\partial\eta}.

By
[
21
, Lemma C.2]
, we have

(4.54)

\left|\frac{\partial|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}}{\partial\eta}\right|\leqslant c(n)\cot\theta|A_{\Sigma}|^{3}.

Therefore, combining (

) with (

), and using (

) with our assumption

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+(n-1)|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n)\delta\int_{\partial\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{4}

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n)\delta\int_{\Sigma}2|A_{\Sigma}||\nabla_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}||+(n-1)|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\delta\theta\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n)\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2},

having used Kato’s inequality

∎

5.

In this section we prove case (3) of Theorem
1.2
. In fact, we prove the following slightly stronger result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
4\leqslant n\leqslant 6
. There exists
\theta_{1}=\theta_{1}(n)
such that if
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is a stable minimal cone for
\mathcal{A}^{\theta}
,

is smooth away from
0

\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{1},\frac{\pi}{2}]

is flat.

Remark 5.2
.

It will be clear from the proof that
\theta_{1}
can be explicitly computed depending on
n
(compare to
[
21
, Appendix C]
).

We first recall the following consequence of the Simons’s equation on a minimal cone.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M^{n}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
be a minimal cone. Then for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have that

(5.1)

\Delta(\tfrac{1}{2}|A|^{2})+|A|^{4}\geqslant 2\lambda|x|^{-2}|A|^{2}+\left[(1-\lambda)(1+\frac{2}{n})+\lambda\right]|\nabla|A||^{2}

on
M
.

Proof.

Fix a point
p\in M
and take normal coordinates
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
p
, then the Simons equation gives

\Delta(\tfrac{1}{2}|A|^{2})+|A|^{4}=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}A_{ij,k}^{2}.

We write the right hand side of the above as:

\Delta(\tfrac{1}{2}|A|^{2})+|A|^{4}=\lambda\left(\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}A_{ij,k}^{2}-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}|A|^{-2}(A_{ij}A_{ij,k})^{2}\right)\\
+(1-\lambda)\sum A_{ij,k}^{2}+\lambda\sum|A|^{-2}(A_{ij}A_{ij,k})^{2}.

Note that
\sum|A|^{-2}(A_{ij}A_{ij,k})^{2}=|\nabla|A||^{2}
. On one hand, since
M
is a cone, we have that (see
[
24
, B.9]
)

\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}A_{ij,k}^{2}-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}|A|^{-2}(A_{ij}A_{ij,k})^{2}\geqslant 2|x|^{-2}|A(x)|^{2}.

On the other hand, we have the following Kato type inequality (see
[
8
, (2.22)]
):

\sum A_{ij,k}^{2}\geqslant(1+\frac{2}{n})|\nabla|A||^{2}.

(
5.1
) follows from combining the above two estimates.
∎

Next, we establish the following trace type inequality on
M
. This was proven in
[
21
, Lemma C.3]
. We include it here for completeness. (Compare to Lemma
4.15
, where a similar trace type inequality holds when
\theta
is close to
0
).

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
M^{n}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is a smoothly immersed hypersurface, meeting
\partial\mathbf{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at constant angle
\theta
. Then for any
u\in W^{1,1}(M)
, we have

(5.2)

\int_{\partial M}u\leqslant\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\int_{M}|\nabla u|+|H_{M}u|.

Proof.

For
R>0
, let
\varphi_{R}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{R}
be a smooth function such that
\varphi_{R}(t)=1
when

,
\varphi_{R}(t)=0
when

, and
|\varphi_{R}^{\prime}|\leqslant\tfrac{2}{R}
. Consider the vector field
\xi=-\varphi_{R}(x_{1})\partial_{1}
. By assumption, we have that
\eta\cdot\xi=\sin\theta
along

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\int_{\partial M}u\eta\cdot\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\int_{M}\Div_{M}(u\xi^{T})\leqslant\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\int_{M}|\nabla u||\xi|+u\Div_{M}\xi+|uH_{M}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\int_{M}|\nabla u|+\frac{2}{R}u+|H_{M}u|.

Sending

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

For
p\in(\tfrac{1}{2},1)
to be chosen later, by (

), we have that (here and after we use

)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta(|A|^{2p})

\displaystyle\quad+p(2p-1)|A|^{2p-2}|\nabla|A||^{2}

\displaystyle 0

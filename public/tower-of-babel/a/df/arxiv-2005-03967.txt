Maximilian Janisch
*

Abstract
Using the approach of N. Etemadi for the Strong Law of Large Numbers ( SLLN ) in [ 1 ] and its elaboration in [ 5 ] , I give weaker conditions under which the SLLN still holds, namely for pairwise uncorrelated (and also for “quasi uncorrelated” ) random variables. I am focusing in particular on random variables which are not identically distributed. My approach here leads to another simple proof of the classical SLLN. Published in (English edition): Theory of Probability and its Applications , 2021, Volume 66, Issue 2, Pages 263–275. https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0040585X97T990381 . Doi: https://doi.org/10.1137/S0040585X97T990381 . Published in (Russian edition): Teoriya Veroyatnostei i ee Primeneniya (this is the Russian edition of Theory of Probability and its Applications ), 2021, Volume 66, Issue 2, Pages 327–341. (Submitted November 23, 2020; Accepted November 25, 2020). http://mi.mathnet.ru/tvp5459 . Doi: https://doi.org/10.4213/tvp5459 .

Introduction and results

In publication
[
1
]
from 1981, N. Etemadi weakened the requirements of Kolmogoroff’s first SLLN for identically distributed random variables and provided an elementary proof of a more general SLLN. I will first state the main Theorem of
[
1
]
after introducing some notations that I will use.

Notation
.

Throughout the paper,
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathsf{P})
is a probability space.
All random variables (usually denoted by
X
or
X_{n}
) are assumed to be measurable functions from
\Omega
to the set of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
. If
X
is a random variable, I write
\mathsf{E}X
for its expected value and
\mathsf{V}X
for its variance.

Etemadi’s Theorem (
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
pairwise
independent, identically distributed random variables with
\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{\left|X_{1}\right|}<\infty
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}+\dots+X_{n}}{n}=\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X_{1}}\quad\text{almost surely (a.s.)}.

(1)

Remark 1
.

Following Remark 2 from chapter 4.3 in Shiryaev’s book
[
4
]
, we notice that if
\mathsf{E}X_{1}=\infty
, then (
1
) still holds.

The main improvement of Etemadi’s Theorem compared to Kolmogoroff’s first SLLN is the replacement of
independence
by
pairwise independence
. The condition
“identically distributed”
still remains. There have been several extentions of this Theorem. For example, Chandra and Goswami gave a sufficient SLLN condition for non-identically distributed random variables. For convenience, I will from now on always use the notation
S_{n}\coloneqq X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}
.

Theorem 1

(Chandra, Goswami
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pairwise independent random variables such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsf{P}(|X_{n}|>t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}-\mathsf{E}S_{n}}{n}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

However, Etemadi’s arguments enable an elegant proof for large classes of non-identically distributed random variables.
First, let me introduce a few useful notions:

Definition 1
.

A sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of (arbitrarily distributed) random variables is said to

1.

Satisfy the Kolmogoroff condition
if and only if each one of the
X_{n}^{\prime}s,n\in\mathbb{N}
has finite variance
\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{n}
and

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{n}}{n^{2}}<\infty.

(2)

2.

Be quasi uncorrelated
if and only if each one of the
X_{n}^{\prime}s,n\in\mathbb{N}
has finite variance and there exists a positive constant
c
such that
\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{S}_{n}\leq c\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{k}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

3.

Satisfy the SLLN
if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S}_{n}}{n}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

Notice that if
a\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{n}
exists, this is (by Cesàro summation) equivalent to the relation
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}S_{n}=a
a.s.

Theorem 2

(SLLN under the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
))
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-negative, quasi uncorrelated random variables satisfying the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
) such that
A\coloneqq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S_{n}}}{n}<\infty
.
Then the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the SLLN.

Remark 2
.

After having finished the proof of this Theorem and the proof of Theorem
3
, I became aware of the paper by Korchevsky
[
2
]
, where the above Theorem
2
is formulated as Lemma 1. Korchevsky refers to the work of Chandra-Goswami
[
3
]
. For completeness, I will give a detailed proof of Theorem
2
, whereas the proof in
[
3
]
is only briefly outlined.

Remark 3
.

The condition
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{E}S_{n}}{n}<\infty
can be replaced by
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{E}S_{n}}{b_{n}}<\infty
for any unbounded, non-decreasing sequence of positive real numbers
b_{n}
. In that case, if the condition
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{V}X_{n}}{n^{2}}<\infty
is replaced by
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{V}X_{n}}{b_{n}^{2}}<\infty
, then the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
will satisfy
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}-\mathsf{E}S_{n}}{b_{n}}=0
a.s. During the proof I will make remarks that show how the proof has to be adapted in order to work for such
b_{n}
.

Remark 4
.

Of course Theorem
2
is also true if
non-negative
is replaced by
a.s. non-negative
. For my purposes here, these two notions can be seen as synonymous.

Remark 5
.

If the
X_{n}
’s are pairwise uncorrelated, we have the equality
\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{S}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{k}
(Bienaymé) and thus the
X_{n}
are, in particular, quasi uncorrelated.

Remark 6
.

If
X
is a random variable, I use the notation
\essinf X
for the essential infimum of
X
, i.e. the largest number
r
such that
X\geq r
a.s. Applying Theorem
2
to the transformation
\tilde{X}_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}-\essinf X_{n}
shows that the condition
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S_{n}}}{n}<\infty
can be replaced by the weaker condition

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S_{n}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\essinf X_{k}}{n}<\infty.

(3)

Since for any numerical sequence
a_{n}\geq 0
,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}=\infty
implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{k\geq n}a_{k}=\infty,

condition (
3
) is equivalent to

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S_{n}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\essinf X_{k}}{n}<\infty.

Remark 7
.

My proof of Theorem
2
relies heavily on the proof by Csörgo et al.
[
5
]
.
In their paper, it is also shown that the condition of non-negativity cannot be removed. More precisely, in their Theorem 4, the authors construct a sequence of pairwise uncorrelated random variables
X_{n}
satisfying the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
) and the condition

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{}|X_{k}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{k}|}{n}=0

and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|S_{n}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S}_{n}|}{n}=\infty.

If we require the
X_{n}
to be pairwise independent instead of just pairwise uncorrelated, the non-negativity assumption can be dropped:

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pairwise independent random variables satisfying the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
) and suppose further that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{}|X_{k}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{k}|}{n}<\infty.

(4)

Then the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the SLLN.

Remark 8
.

Thanks to linearity of
\mathsf{E}
, the non-negativity condition in Theorem
2
can be replaced by a.s. uniform boundedness of each
X_{n}
(or even of
X_{n}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{n}
) from below or above. For example, if each
X_{n}
is bounded from below by the real number
r
, then Theorem
2
can be applied to
\tilde{X}_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}-r
. In fact, one can always try to use the transformation
\tilde{X}_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}-\essinf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
and check if that transformation satiesfies all conditions of Theorem
2
.

Remark 9
.

Kolmogoroff’s classical SLLN is very similar to Corollary
2.1
, except that pairwise independence is replaced by independence but the auxilliary condition (
4
) is dropped. However, as is proven in
[
5
]
, Theorem 3, there is a sequence of pairwise independent random variables
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
) such that the SLLN is not satisfied. Hence, an auxilliary condition like (
4
) in Corollary
2.1
is necessary.

We can try to relax the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
) for the
X_{n}
using
truncation arguments
. For this, however, the condition that all
X_{n}
’s are pairwise uncorrelated has to be replaced by a condition of pairwise independence. The reason for this is that the truncations of the
X_{n}
’s are still pairwise independent if the
X_{n}
’s are pairwise independent, while the truncations need not be pairwise uncorrelated if the
X_{n}
’s are only pairwise uncorrelated.

More precisely, the truncations of
X_{n}
that I will look at are
Y_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}\cdot 1_{\{X_{n}\leq n\}}
, where
1_{\{X_{n}\leq n\}}
is the indicator function of the event
\{X_{n}\leq n\}
.

Remark 10
.

It should be noted that the truncated variables
Y_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
, do not have to satisfy the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
)! It depends on the
X_{n}
’s. For example, if the
X_{n}
’s are random variables with
\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{(}X_{n}=n)=\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{(}X_{n}=0)=\frac{1}{2}
, then
\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}Y_{n})=\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{n})=\frac{n^{2}}{4}
, which is too large for the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
) to hold. In this case, however, it is not surprising, because the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
also does not satisfy the SLLN. Interestingly, the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not even satisfy the Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN). Indeed, let
\tilde{X}_{n}=X_{n}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{n}
. Then
S_{n}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{X}_{k}
and
\mathsf{P}\left(\tilde{X}_{n}=-\frac{n}{2}\right)=\mathsf{P}\left(\tilde{X}_{n}=\frac{n}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}
, so that for
n\geq 2
,

\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{}\left(S_{n}-\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S}_{n}\geq\frac{n}{2}\right)=\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{X}_{k}\geq\frac{n}{2}\right)=\frac{\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tilde{X}_{k}\geq 0\right)+\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tilde{X}_{k}\geq n\right)}{2}\geq\frac{1}{4},

where the last inequality is true by the symmetry of
\tilde{X}_{k}
, i.e.
\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tilde{X}_{k}\geq 0\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark 11
.

Since the SLLN implies the WLLN, the sequence of random variables from Remark
10
satisfy neither the SLLN nor the WLLN. For completeness, I refer to examples 15.3 and 15.4 in
[
6
]
for sequences of random variables that satisfy the WLLN but not the SLLN.

Theorem 3

(SLLN for random variables with the Kolmogoroff condition for the truncations)
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-negative, pairwise independent random variables such that the truncated sequence
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
Y_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}\cdot 1_{\{X_{n}\leq n\}}
, satisfies the Kolmogoroff condition (
2
) as well as (
4
) and such that
X_{n}-Y_{n}
goes to
0
in
L^{1}
-sense and a.s. as
n\to\infty
. Then the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the SLLN.

Remark 12
.

Remark
8
also applies to Theorem
3
.

Of course, we can slightly modify the assumptions to avoid non-negativity. The following two standard notations,
X_{n}^{+}\coloneqq\max\{X_{n},0\}
and
X_{n}^{-}\coloneqq-\min\{X_{n},0\}
, will be used.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of random variables such that both sequences
(X^{+}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(X^{-}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3
. Then the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the SLLN.

Finally, I want to prove a (well-known) statement which shows that Etemadi’s result follows from Corollary
3.1
since identically distributed random variables
“behave nicely”
:

Theorem 4
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-negative, identically distributed, integrable random variables. Then the truncated sequence
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, defined by
Y_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}\cdot 1_{\{X_{n}\leq n\}}
, satisfies the Kolmogoroff condition, and, as
n\to\infty
,
X_{n}-Y_{n}\to 0
both in
L^{1}
-sense and a.s.

If we are given any identically distributed, pairwise independent random variables
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
with
\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{}|X_{1}|<\infty
, we can apply Theorem
4
to each one of the sequences
(X_{n}^{+})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(X_{n}^{-})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to see that all conditions of Corollary
3.1
are satisfied. As a consequence, we obtain the conclusion of Etemadi’s Theorem.

2
Examples

In order to illustrate my results I will now give two examples.

Example 1
.

Let
X
be a (continuous) uniformly distributed random variable over the interval
[-1,1]
. Define the sequence

X_{n}\coloneqq\cos(2\pi nX)\text{ for $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

(5)

Claim
.

The following three statements are true:

1.

The sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is pairwise uncorrelated.

2.

Each pair
(X_{i},X_{j})
for
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
is not independent.

3.

The sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the SLLN.

Remark 13
.

As will be seen in the proof, my Theorem
2
can be used to prove that
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the SLLN. Notice that, because of statement
2
, the original Theorem in
[
5
]
cannot be applied.

Proof of the statements.

Let me start with the most important statement,
3
. Consider
\tilde{X_{n}}\coloneqq 1+X_{n}
. Then (see (
5
)),
\mathsf{E}(\tilde{X_{n}})=1+\mathsf{E}(X_{n})=1
. Hence
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{E}(\tilde{X_{1}}+\dots+\tilde{X_{n}})}{n}=1<\infty
. Additionally, all of the random variables
\tilde{X_{n}}
are in the interval
[0,2]
, which implies that the Kolmogoroff condition is satisfied. Statement
1
guarantees that Theorem
2
can be applied to conclude that the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the SLLN.

To prove statement
1
, we need to show that
\mathsf{E}(X_{i}X_{j})=\mathsf{E}(X_{i})\mathsf{E}(X_{j})
for all
i\neq j
.
This is true by the following two simple computations:

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}(X_{i})=\int_{-1}^{1}\cos(2\pi ix)\,\mathrm{d}x=\

\displaystyle 0=\mathsf{E}(X_{j}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}(X_{i}X_{j})=\int_{-1}^{1}\cos(2\pi ix)\cos(2\pi jx)\,\mathrm{d}x=\

\displaystyle 0.

The last equality follows from the elementary trigonometric identity

\cos(2\pi ix)\cos(2\pi jx)=\frac{\cos(2\pi(i-j)x)+\cos(2\pi(i+j)x)}{2}.

To prove statement
2
, let
\varepsilon>0
and suppose that
X_{i}>1-\varepsilon
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
. This means that
\cos(2\pi iX)>1-\varepsilon
. Consider the
\arccos

“branch”
, defined by

\displaystyle\arccos_{i}:[-1,1]

\displaystyle\to[2i\pi,(2i+1)\pi],

(6)

\displaystyle\cos(x)

\displaystyle\mapsto x.

(7)

This function is continuous and thus there exist
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that

2\pi iX\in]2\pi k-\delta(\varepsilon),2\pi k+\delta(\varepsilon)[

for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\delta(\varepsilon)=0
.
This means that

X\in\bigcup_{k=-i}^{i}\left]\frac{k}{i}-\frac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{i},\frac{k}{i}+\frac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{i}\right[\overset{\text{Def.}}{=}D.

Each interval in the union has length
\frac{2\delta(\varepsilon)}{i}
and there are
2i
intervals. Hence, since
\cos
is
1
-Lipschitz, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\cos(2\pi jD)
has Lebesgue measure at most
4\delta(\varepsilon)
. If
\varepsilon
is small enough, we have
4\delta(\varepsilon)<2=\text{length}([-1,1])
. Hence in that case, as
\mathsf{P}(X_{j}\in[a,b])>0
for any
0\leq a<b\leq 1
,

\mathsf{P}(X_{j}\in[-1,1]\setminus\cos(2\pi jD)\mid X_{i}>1-\varepsilon)=0<\mathsf{P}(X_{j}\in[-1,1]\setminus\cos(2\pi jD))

and thus the pair
(X_{i},X_{j})
is not independent.
∎

Example 2
.

Let
Z_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
, be independent standard normal random variables (i.e. each
Z_{n}
has a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance 1). Let
W
be a random variable, independent of every
Z_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\mathsf{P}(W=0)=\mathsf{P}(W=1)=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. Then the statements
1
,
2
,
3
from Example
1
also hold for the random variables
X_{n}\coloneqq WZ_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

To prove
1
, note that, by symmetry of each
Z_{n}
and by the independence assumptions, for all
i\neq j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathsf{E}(X_{i})\mathsf{E}(X_{j})=\mathsf{E}(W)\mathsf{E}(Z_{i})\mathsf{E}(X_{j})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{E}(X_{i}X_{j})=\mathsf{E}(W^{2})\mathsf{E}(Z_{i})\mathsf{E}(Z_{j})=0.

To prove
2
, notice that if there exists any
i\in\mathbb{N}
for which
X_{i}>0
, then
W=1
. Hence, for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
i\neq j
,

\mathsf{P}(X_{j}>0\mid X_{i}>0)=\mathsf{P}(Z_{j}>0)=\frac{1}{2}>\mathsf{P}(Z_{j}>0\text{ and }W=1)=\frac{1}{4}=\mathsf{P}(X_{j}>0).

Finally I will prove
3
. Since
\frac{X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}}{n}=W\frac{Z_{1}+\dots+Z_{n}}{n}
, and
\frac{Z_{1}+\dots+Z_{n}}{n}
has a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance
\frac{1}{n}
, we see that the SLLN holds for the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in question.
∎

Remark 14
.

Example
2
also is an example of a sequence of random variables for which the SLLN holds, but for which the non-negativity/bounded-from-below assumption in Theorem
2
is not satisfied. If we try to treat this problem by applying Theorem

X_{n}^{+}\coloneqq\max\{X_{n},0\}

,

\mathsf{V}(X_{1}^{+}+\dots+X_{n}^{+})=n\,\mathsf{V}(X_{1}^{+})+\binom{n}{2}(\mathsf{E}(X_{1}^{+}X_{2}^{+})-\mathsf{E}(X_{1}^{+})\mathsf{E}(X_{2}^{+})),

(8)

while

\mathsf{V}(X_{1}^{+})+\dots+\mathsf{V}(X_{n}^{+})=n\mathsf{V}(X_{1}^{+}).

(9)

We can compute
1
1

1

See for instance

In particular, we see that
\mathsf{E}(X_{1}^{+}X_{2}^{+})>\mathsf{E}(X_{1}^{+})\mathsf{E}(X_{2}^{+})
, and hence by (

),

is bigger than some positive constant (independent of

.

3
Proofs

3.1
Tools for Theorem
2

Definition 2
.

From now on, fix real numbers
\alpha>1
and
\varepsilon>0
. The random variables
X_{n}
are as in Theorem
2
. Recall that, by assumption,
A\coloneqq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{S}_{n}}{n}<\infty
. Hence it is possible to define the integer
L\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{A}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor
, where
\lfloor\bullet\rfloor
denotes
“the largest integer smaller than, or equal to, •”
(also known as the
“floor part”
).

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
m(n)\coloneqq\lfloor\log_{\alpha}n\rfloor
. Then

\displaystyle m(n)\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha^{m(n)}\leq n<\alpha^{m(n)+1}\text{ for all }n.

For a non-negative random variable
X_{n}
, let
s(n)\in\{0,\dots,L\}

Proof of Theorem
4
.

I will first prove that the sequence
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the Kolmogoroff condition. For this, let us bound the tail sums of the convergent series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{2}}
. Recall that
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
, which is the famous Basel problem. I claim that

\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\text{ for any }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(27)

Notice that if
k=1
, then (
27
) is true (we have equality). For

, we have

\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{2}}<\int_{k-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{k-1}\leq\frac{2}{k}<\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

Now, using (
27
) and the abbreviation
g_{k}\coloneqq 1_{\{k<X_{1}\leq k+1\}}

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{V}\hskip 0.85355pt{Y}_{j}}{j^{2}}&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{}(Y_{j}^{2})}{j^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{1}^{2}\cdot 1_{\{X_{1}\leq j\}})}{j^{2}}\\
&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{j^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{1}^{2}\cdot g_{k})\right)\\
&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{1}^{2}\cdot g_{k})\sum_{j=k+1}^{N}\frac{1}{j^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{1}^{2}\cdot g_{k})}{k+1}\\
&=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{\{k<X_{1}\leq k+1\}}X_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{P}\\
&\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k+1}{k+1}\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(X_{1}\cdot g_{k})}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{1}<\infty.\end{split}

It remains to show that

as

in
L^{1}
-sense and a.s. First, I will prove the

\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{}|X_{n}-Y_{n}|=\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{n}-Y_{n})=\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{n}\cdot 1_{\{X_{n}>n\}})=\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{1}\cdot 1_{\{X_{1}>n\}})
. Since
\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{1}<\infty
by assumption,
\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{(}X_{1}\cdot 1_{\{X_{1}>n\}})
will converge to

, as

Now, regarding the a.s. convergence, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{(}X_{n}\neq Y_{n})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{(}X_{n}>n)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{(}X_{1}>n)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}\hskip 1.13791pt{(}X_{1}\geq x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.85355pt{X}_{1}<\infty.

Hence, by Borel-Cantelli, with probability

it is true that

for

∎

Stokesian Dynamics with odd viscosity Preprint: Draft for External Revision
Hang Yuan, Monica Olvera de la Cruz

Abstract
Stokesian Dynamics is a well-established computational method for simulating dynamics of many particles suspended in a conventional passive fluid medium. Active fluids composed of self-propelling particles with broken time reversal symmetry permit the emergence of a so-called odd viscosity. In this work, we extended the conventional Stokesian Dynamics formalism to incorporate the additional hydrodynamic effects due to odd viscosity, which enables simulating collective behaviors of many particles suspended in an active fluid medium with both even viscosity and odd viscosity.



I
Introduction

Understanding and predicting the macroscopic properties, such as the diffusion coefficient
Dhont1996
, thermal conductivity and rheology
Mewis2011
, of a suspension of particles from its micro-structural mechanics is a long-standing science and engineering problem. In order to address this problem, a vast number of different simulation techniques have been developed in past decades
PhysRevE.52.1734
;
Pozrikidis2002
;
Keaveny2014
;
Krueger2016
;
Fiore2019
;
Bolintineanu2014
. Stokesian Dynamics
Brady1988
is a well-known particle-based approach for dynamically simulating the collective behaviors of many particles suspended in a fluid medium. It has been successfully applied to a diverse range of particle suspensions for understanding phenomena such as sedimentation
Revay1992
;
Cichocki1993
;
Nakayama2005
, phase transition of colloidal gels
Dickinson2013
;
Varga2015
;
Landrum2016
and discontinuous shear thickening
PhysRevLett.111.218301
;
Mari2015
;
PhysRevLett.115.228304
, to name a few.

Hydrodynamic interactions have played an important role in describing suspensions of particles in viscous fluids for decades. In the 1990s, Aron and co-workers
Avron1995
;
avron1998odd
showed that the hydrodynamic equations in a system with broken time reversal symmetry are equipped with a non-dissipative viscosity coefficient, termed odd viscosity. Such odd viscosity was experimentally measured in a suspension of particles driven by an external field
Soni2019
. Active fluids composed of self-propelling particles
PhysRevE.99.042605
;
PhysRevE.101.022603
with broken time reversal symmetry have thus attracted great interest. The constant injection of external energy leads to non-equilibrium states of active particles, where diverse steady-state collective motions sharply contrast with those commonly observed in equilibrium systems
Elgeti2015
;
Zoettl2016
;
Sabrina2018
;
Shaebani2020
;
Qi2022
;
Lei2019b
;
Zhang2022
. Most of the research about active colloidal particles has focused on the active colloidal particles themselves and limited attention has been put on the medium, which mediates the hydrodynamic interactions between active colloidal particles. Nearly all the collective behavior of active colloidal particles is studied in a liquid environment. This makes hydrodynamic interactions essential
Marchetti2013
;
Zoettl2014
for understanding phenomena observed in active systems. More importantly, due to the constant input of external energy in active systems, the mechanical motions of embedded active units also constantly disturb the surrounding liquid. These induced active flows are absent in usual passive fluids.

In this work, we envision a collection of particles suspended in an active fluid, which possesses both even and odd viscosity. In an analogy to the development of traditional Stokesian Dynamics, we systematically derive an extended Stokesian Dynamics formalism to explore possible physical effects of odd viscosity
Khain2022
;
Samanta2022
;
Han2021a
;
PhysRevE.104.064613
. We first briefly review the concept of odd viscosity in section
II.1
. Then, the Stokes’ equations governing the conventional micro-hydrodynamics are generalized to include the additional effects caused by odd viscosity in section
II.2
and the Green’s function of the generalized Stokes’ equations is also derived in section
II.3
. By considering up to the first order effect of odd viscosity in section
III
, both near-field and far-field mobility tensors due to odd viscosity are calculated in section
III.2
and section
III.3
, respectively. In section
III.4
, those results are incorporated into the existing Stokesian Dynamics framework for simulating a population of particles suspended in an active fluid medium with both even and odd viscosity. The whole work concludes with a brief summary and discussions about possible future work.

II
Stokes’ equations with odd viscosity

II.1
Odd viscosity

Readers already familiar with odd viscosity can skip this section, where the concept of odd viscosity is briefly reviewed
avron1998odd
;
PhysRevE.103.042610
;
PhysRevLett.127.048001
;
Markovich2019
;
Banerjee2017
. Without loss of generality, the viscous stresses of any fluids in response to external disturbances can be written as

\sigma_{ij}=-p\delta_{ij}+\mu_{ijkl}\nabla_{l}u_{k}

(1)

where
\sigma_{ij}
is the stress tensor,
p
is the isotropic hydrostatic pressure and
\nabla_{l}u_{k}
is the velocity gradient tensor. Here,
\mu_{ijkl}
is the fourth order viscosity tensor, which describes resulting viscous stresses in response to velocity gradients. The above relation is usually termed as the linear constitutive relation of a fluid. Analogous to the case of a homogeneous isotropic linear elastic solid
Krempl2009
, the spatial symmetry properties of a homogeneous isotropic fluid simplifies the general viscosity tensor to

\mu_{ijkl}^{e}=\mu_{b}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\mu_{s}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{jk}\delta_{il})

(2)

where
\mu_{b}
and
\mu_{s}
are the bulk and shear viscosity, respectively. Such viscosity tensor
\mu_{ijkl}^{e}
only depends on two independent material properties, which play similar roles of bulk and shear modulus in linear elastic solids.

Furthermore, symmetry properties of the system enforce three important symmetry relations, which the viscosity tensor
\mu_{ijkl}^{e}
must fulfill. Firstly, the rotational invariance of system requires that
\mu^{e}_{ijkl}=\mu^{e}_{ijlk}
. Secondly, the conservation of local angular momentum (no external torques) requires that the stress tensor must be symmetric, that is,
\sigma_{ij}=\sigma_{ji}
, which enforces that
\mu^{e}_{ijkl}=\mu^{e}_{jikl}
. Thirdly, according to the Onsager reciprocal relations
PhysRev.37.405
;
Landau2013
, the time reversal symmetry requires that
\mu^{e}_{ijkl}=\mu^{e}_{klij}
. It is easy to verify that the above viscosity expression
\mu_{ijkl}^{e}
fulfills all three symmetry relations. The superscript
e
indicates that it is even under the time-reversal transformation (
ij\leftrightarrow kl
) and the corresponding viscosity
\mu_{ijkl}^{e}
is therefore termed as
even viscosity
.

As an extension of the above symmetry considerations, Avron et al.
avron1998odd
considered a system under an external magnetic field, which possesses a broken time-reversal symmetry. In such a scenario, the Onsager reciprocal relations can be further extended for systems with a broken time reversal symmetry
Groot1984
, which yields

\mu_{ijkl}^{o}=-\mu_{klij}^{o}

(3)

where the superscript
o
indicates that the viscosity is odd under the time-reversal transformation and
\mu_{ijkl}^{o}
is thus analogously termed as
odd viscosity
.

The most general expression of odd viscosity in a 3D system can be quite complex and may contain numerous components depending on the symmetry of system, which is systematically explored by Vitelli et al.
Khain2022
. Here, we restrict our attention to a more specific case by only considering the possible rotational degrees of freedom of microscopic fluid particles. Starting from a microscopic Hamiltonian, Lubensky et al.
PhysRevLett.127.048001
derived an expression of odd viscosity in a 3D system as,

\mu_{ijkl}^{o}=\ell_{n}(\varepsilon_{jln}\delta_{ik}+\varepsilon_{iln}\delta_{jk}+\varepsilon_{ikn}\delta_{jl}+\varepsilon_{jkn}\delta_{il})

(4)

where
\varepsilon_{ijk}
is the three-dimensional (3D) Levi-Civita symbol,
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta, and
\ell_{i}
is the local angular momentum density of constituting particles. Please be aware that the expression above derived in the work
PhysRevLett.127.048001
matches with the general expression derived in the work
Khain2022
by taking
\eta_{1}^{0}=-2\eta_{2}^{0}=\mu_{0}
in their notations. And similar to the case of even viscosity, the odd viscosity should fulfill three symmetry relations
\mu_{ijkl}^{o}=\mu_{ijlk}^{o}
,
\mu_{ijkl}^{o}=\mu_{jikl}^{o}
and
\mu_{ijkl}^{o}=-\mu_{klij}^{o}
. It can be easily verified that all three symmetric relations are fulfilled by Eqn.
4
. For a quasi-2D system where the local angular momentum density is constrained to be uni-axial such as
\mbox{\boldmath$\ell$}=\mu_{o}\mathbf{\hat{z}}
, the above general expression of odd viscosity in 3D (Eqn.
4
) can be further simplified to

\mu_{ijkl}^{o}=\mu_{o}(\epsilon_{jl}\delta_{ik}+\epsilon_{il}\delta_{jk}+\epsilon_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\epsilon_{jk}\delta_{il})

(5)

where
\varepsilon_{ij}
is the 2D Levi-Civita symbol defined as
\varepsilon_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ijz}
and the index of
\epsilon_{ij}
is extended to run over x, y and z. In such a simplified case, a single scalar material parameter — odd viscosity
\mu_{o}
— is sufficient to determine the whole odd viscosity tensor
\mu_{ijkl}^{o}
.

Finally, for a fluid involving both even and odd interactions under the time-reversal transformation, the general linear constitutive relation of such a fluid can be expressed as

\sigma_{ij}=-p\delta_{ij}+\left(\mu_{ijkl}^{e}+\mu_{ijkl}^{o}\right)\nabla_{l}u_{k}

(6)

where the explicit expressions of even and odd viscosity tensors are given by Eqn.
2
and
4
, respectively.

II.2
Odd Stokes’ equations

With the constitutive relation of a fluid including both even and odd viscosity (Eqn.
6
), the corresponding Stokes’ equations are ready to be derived via the balance of linear momentum. For simplicity, we only consider quasi-2D systems here and the overall viscous stress tensor is thus given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}=-p\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle+\mu_{b}\nabla_{k}u_{k}\delta_{ij}+\mu_{s}\left(\nabla_{j}u_{i}+\nabla_{i}u_{j}\right)

(7)

\displaystyle+\mu_{o}\left(\varepsilon_{jl}\nabla_{l}u_{i}+\varepsilon_{il}\nabla_{l}u_{j}+\nabla_{i}\varepsilon_{jl}u_{l}+\nabla_{j}\varepsilon_{il}u_{l}\right)

For an incompressible fluid,
\nabla_{j}u_{j}=0
, the balance of internal forces
\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\right)
and external forces (
\mathbf{f}
) yields the Stokes’ equations with odd viscosity,

-\nabla_{i}\left(p-\mu_{o}\epsilon_{jl}\nabla_{j}u_{l}\right)+\mu_{s}\nabla^{2}u_{i}+\mu_{o}\epsilon_{il}\nabla^{2}u_{l}=f_{i}

(8)

The above equations can also be rewritten in an equivalent matrix form as

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{s}&\mu_{o}&0\\
-\mu_{o}&\mu_{s}&0\\
0&0&\mu_{s}\end{pmatrix}\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}=\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\tilde{p}+\mathbf{f}

(9)

where the modified pressure
\tilde{p}
is defined as
\tilde{p}=p-\mu_{o}\epsilon_{jl}\nabla_{j}u_{l}
and
\mathbf{f}
denotes possible external volumetric forces such as gravity. The above equation reduces to the conventional Stokes’ equation when
\mu_{o}=0
and the odd viscosity
\mu_{o}
emerges only at off-diagonal elements, which introduces additional couplings between in-plane components (x and y components here, with angular momentum density
\ell
assumed to be along z axis).

II.3
Odd Oseen tensor

As the Stokes’ equations with odd viscosity (Eqn.
9
) are still linear equations, here we derive its corresponding Green’s function. Similarly to the Oseen tensor
SangtaeKim2005
;
Graham2018
derived for the conventional Stokes’ equations with only even viscosity, we start by considering a point force
-\mathbf{F}
applied at position
\mathbf{r}
Morse1953
, that is,

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{s}&\mu_{o}&0\\
-\mu_{o}&\mu_{s}&0\\
0&0&\mu_{s}\end{pmatrix}\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}=\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}p-\mathbf{F}\delta(\mathbf{r})

(10)

Here, the modified pressure
\tilde{p}
(Eqn.
9
) is denoted as
p
for brevity. Due to the linearity of the odd Stokes’ equations, both the pressure field
p
and flow field
\mathbf{u}
should be proportional to the applied point force
\mathbf{F}
. Since
p(\mathbf{r})
is a scalar field and
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r})
is a vector field, such linear relations can be expressed as

p(\mathbf{r})=P_{j}(\mathbf{r})F_{j},\quad u_{i}(\mathbf{r})=G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})F_{j}

(11)

where
P_{i}(\mathbf{r})
and
G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
are the Green’s function of the pressure field and flow fields, respectively. Applying the Fourier transformation to Eqn.
10
, its corresponding expressions in k-space can be computed as

-\mu_{ij}k^{2}\widehat{u}_{j}(\mathbf{k})=ik_{i}\widehat{p}(\mathbf{k})-F_{i}

(12)

where
\mathbf{k}
is the wave vector,
\widehat{p}(\mathbf{k})
and
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{k})
are the respective pressure field and flow fields in the k-space. For convenience, a second order auxiliary viscosity matrix
\mu_{ij}
is introduced,

\mu_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{s}&\mu_{o}&0\\
-\mu_{o}&\mu_{s}&0\\
0&0&\mu_{s}\end{pmatrix}=\mu_{s}\delta_{ij}+\mu_{o}\varepsilon_{ij}

(13)

Utilizing the linearity of odd Stokes’ equations (Eqn.
11
) and the incompressibility condition of fluids (
\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
), the Green’s functions of both pressure and flow field can be solved analytically in k-space as (see Appx.
A.1
):

\widehat{P}_{j}(\mathbf{k})=-\frac{ik_{j}}{k^{2}}+i\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{ik}k_{i}\widehat{G}_{kj}(\mathbf{k}),\quad\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=\widehat{G}_{(ij)}(\mathbf{k})+\widehat{G}_{[ij]}(\mathbf{k})

(14)

where
\widehat{P}_{j}(\mathbf{k})
and
\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
are the corresponding Green’s functions of pressure and flow fields in the k-space. The k-space Green’s function of flow fields
\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
can be split as the sum of its symmetric and anti-symmetric parts:

\widehat{G}_{(ij)}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\frac{1}{\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})}\left(-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{4}}+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{k^{2}}\right),\quad\widehat{G}_{[ij]}(\mathbf{k})=-\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\frac{\chi}{\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})}\varepsilon_{ijk}k_{k}\frac{k_{z}}{k^{4}}

(15)

where two auxiliary variables introduced are defined as:

\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})=1+\chi^{2}\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}},\quad\chi=\frac{\mu_{o}}{\mu_{s}}

(16)

Particularly, the dimensionless number
\chi
is the ratio between odd viscosity
\mu_{o}
and even viscosity
\mu_{s}
, which naturally characterizes the significance of odd viscosity related effects relative to even viscosity.

The corresponding real-space expressions of Green’s functions can be obtained via the inverse Fourier transformation of its k-space expressions (Eqn.
14
). However, the additional k-dependence introduced by
\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})
for non-vanishing odd viscosity makes the exact analytical calculation difficult. To avoid obscuring essential physics by tedious algebra, we only focus on the fluids with a weak odd viscosity
Soni2019
, that is,
\mu_{o}\ll\mu_{s}
. Then, the viscosity ratio
\chi\ll 1
serves as a natural expansion parameter for obtaining the leading effects of odd viscosity (see Appx.
A.2
). The real-space expressions of the Green’s functions can be similarly decomposed into symmetric and anti-symmetric parts,
G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})=G_{(ij)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{[ij]}(\mathbf{r})
, which can be systematically expanded in powers of
\chi
as

G_{(ij)}(\mathbf{r})=G_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{(ij)}^{(2)}(\mathbf{r})+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{4}),\quad G_{[ij]}(\mathbf{r})=G_{[ij]}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{[ij]}^{(3)}(\mathbf{r})+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{5})

(17)

where
G_{(ij)}(\mathbf{r})
and
G_{[ij]}(\mathbf{r})
are the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts of the real-space Green’s functions
G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
, respectively. The number inside the superscript parenthesis indicates powers of the viscosity ratio
\chi
. The explicit expressions of the symmetric Green’s functions are given by Eqn.
52a
and
60
and the explicit expressions of the anti-symmetric Green’s functions are given by Eqn.
52b
and
63
. Please refer to Appx.
A.2
for the detailed derivations.

III
Stokesian Dynamics with odd viscosity

With the Green’s function (Eqn.
17
) of the odd Stokes’ equations (Eqn.
9
) derived in previous sections, the flow fields induced by a point force
\mathbf{F}
at origin can be simply expressed as

u^{PF}_{i}(\mathbf{r})=G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})F_{j}=\left[G_{(ij)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{[ij]}(\mathbf{r})\right]F_{j}

(18)

For simplicity, only the first order effect of odd viscosity will be explored in this work but the formalism developed in this section in principle can be extended to arbitrarily higher orders. If we retain up to the first order terms of viscosity ratio
\chi
(Eqn.
52a
and
52b
), the first order approximation of the exact Green’s function is

\displaystyle G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})=G_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{[ij]}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{2})\approx\frac{1}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\left[J_{ij}(\mathbf{r})-\chi J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})\right]

(19)

where
J_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
is the conventional
Ossen tensor
(Eqn.
51
) derived from Stokes’ equations with only even viscosity
SangtaeKim2005
.
J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})
is the additional response due to the existence of odd viscosity, which in analogy to the conventional terminology used for even viscosity, we referred to it as
odd Oseen tensor
(Eqn.
53
). Hereafter, the shear viscosity
\mu_{s}
will be denoted as
\mu
for short. Comparing with the conventional passive fluids, the above Green’s function has one additional term due to the first order effect of the odd viscosity
-\chi J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
. By the linearity of the odd Stokes’ equations (Eqn.
9
), the total flow fields induced by a collection of
N
finite-sized rigid particles can be written as

u_{i}(\mathbf{x})-u_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{8\pi\mu}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\oint_{S^{\alpha}}\left[J_{ij}(\mathbf{r})-\chi J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})\right]f_{j}(\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}S(\mathbf{y})

(20)

where
u_{i}^{\infty}
(
i=x,y,z
) is the background linear velocity field,
S^{\alpha}
is the surface of
\alpha
-th particle (
\alpha=1,\cdots,N
),
\mathbf{y}
denotes any locations at the particle surface, and
f_{j}(\mathbf{y})
is the force density distributed over the particle surface.

Based on the above integral representation of odd Stokes’ equations, we will extend the conventional Stokesian Dynamics formalism to include the effects due to odd viscosity. Since the Stokesian Dynamics with only even viscosity has been extensively studied
Brady1988
, we will only focus on the additional flows contributed by odd viscosity, which is denoted as
u_{i}^{o}(\mathbf{x})
and

u_{i}^{o}(\mathbf{x})-u_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x})=-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\oint_{S^{\alpha}}J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})f_{j}(\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}S(\mathbf{y})

(21)

III.1
Multipole expansion

Commonly speaking, the detailed information of force density distribution on each particle’s surface is difficult to obtain and usually only moments of force density distribution, such as forces, torques and so on, are experimentally accessible. Therefore, induced flows in response to each force density moment are of more interest in practice. If we only consider up to the first order moments of force density distribution, the integral form expression of the induced odd flows (Eqn.
21
) can be systematically expanded via the multipole expansion

u_{i}^{o}(\mathbf{x})-u_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x})\approx-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\left[J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})F_{j}^{\alpha}+R^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})T_{j}^{\alpha}-K^{o}_{ijk}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})S_{jk}^{\alpha}\right]

(22)

where
\mathbf{F}^{\alpha}
,
\mathbf{T}^{\alpha}
and
\mathsfbfit{S}^{\alpha}
are the total forces, torques and stresslets acting on the
\alpha
-th particle, respectively, which are the zeroth and first order moments of force density distribution
Guazzelli2011
;
J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
,
R^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
and
K^{o}_{ijk}(\mathbf{r})
are the respective odd viscosity relevant propagators of forces, torques and stresslets, which are defined as

\displaystyle J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{ij3}}{r}-\frac{\varepsilon_{ijk}r_{k}r_{3}}{r^{3}}

(23a)

\displaystyle R_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ikl}\nabla_{k}J_{jl}^{o}

(23b)

\displaystyle K_{ijk}^{o}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\nabla_{k}J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})+\nabla_{j}J_{ik}^{o}(\mathbf{r})\right]

(23c)

The above multipole expansion (Eqn.
22
) is truncated at the first order although such expansion can proceed to higher order terms for a better accuracy. Particularly, for rigid spherical particles, such expansion terminates very quickly and even an exact solution can be obtained for isolated particles just like the case with only even viscosity
SangtaeKim2005
. Without loss of generality, the induced odd flow fields can be expressed as

u_{i}^{o}(\mathbf{x})-u_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x})\approx-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\left\{\mathcal{F}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\right]F_{j}^{\alpha}+\mathcal{T}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\right]T_{j}^{\alpha}+\mathcal{S}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\right]S_{jk}^{\alpha}\right\}

(24)

where
\mathcal{F}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
,
\mathcal{T}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
and
\mathcal{S}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
denote linear functions which describe the odd viscosity related responses to external forces, torques and stresslets, respectively. If the particles are considered ideal point particles, the explicit functional forms of those response functions are just the propagators of each force moment (Eqn.
23
). However, due to the fact that particles are of finite size, there are additional non-vanishing higher order force moments that need to be considered.

Next, we determine the exact functional forms of the response functions with higher order terms. We first extend the Lorentz reciprocal theorem and the Faxén laws to systems with odd viscosity. Then, the additional mobility tensors due to odd viscosity are systematically derived in both near and far fields for the development of Stokesian Dynamics with odd viscosity.

III.2
Near-field mobility tensors

III.2.1
Ambient flows in integral form

Here, we restrict our consideration to spherical rigid particles. Their spherical symmetry makes it reasonable to assume the force moments are uniformly distributed over the particle surface. Then, the integral form of ambient odd flows (odd flows induced by other particles excluding
\alpha
-th particle itself) can be written as

\displaystyle u_{i}^{o\prime}(\mathbf{x})-u_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu}\sum_{\beta=1,\beta\neq\alpha}^{N}\oint_{S^{\beta}}J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\left[\frac{F_{j}^{\beta}}{4\pi a^{2}}+\frac{3}{8\pi a^{3}}\epsilon_{jkl}T_{k}^{\beta}n_{l}\mathrm{d}S+\frac{3}{4\pi a^{3}}S_{jk}^{\beta}n_{k}\right]\mathrm{d}S

(25)

where
u_{i}^{o\prime}
denotes the ambient odd flows. Such an approximation is closely related to the idea of the Rotne-Prager-Yamakawa (RPY) approximation
Rotne1969
;
Yamakawa1970
;
Yamakawa1971
commonly used in conventional Stokesian Dynamics.
Then, the integral forms of response functions
\mathcal{F}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
,
\mathcal{T}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
and
\mathcal{S}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
can be defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y});S\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi a^{2}}\oint_{S}\widetilde{J}^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}S(\mathbf{y})

(26a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y});S\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8\pi a^{3}}\oint_{S}\widetilde{R}^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}S(\mathbf{y})

(26b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y});S\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4\pi a^{3}}\oint_{S}\widetilde{K}^{o}_{ijk}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}S(\mathbf{y})

(26c)

where integrals are evaluated at the particle surface
S
and integrands inside surface integrals of each force moments are defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})

(27a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=J^{o}_{ik}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\varepsilon_{kjl}n_{l}

(27b)

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{ijk}^{o}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})n_{k}+J^{o}_{ik}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})n_{j}\right]

(27c)

For spherical rigid particles, the single spherical surface integrals of response functions can be evaluated analytically, which yield

M^{o}_{Fij}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{8\pi\mu}\mathcal{F}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y});S^{\beta}\right]=\begin{cases}\left(1+\frac{a^{2}}{6}\nabla^{2}\right)\frac{J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta})}{8\pi\mu}&\left|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\right|>a\\
\frac{1}{12\pi\mu a}\varepsilon_{ij3}&\left|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\right|\leq a\end{cases}

(28a)

M^{o}_{Tij}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{8\pi\mu}\mathcal{T}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y});S^{\beta}\right]=\begin{cases}-\left(1+\frac{a^{2}}{10}\nabla^{2}\right)\frac{R_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta})}{8\pi\mu}&\left|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\right|>a\\
\frac{1}{8\pi\mu a^{3}}\left[-\frac{3}{5}\left(x_{l}-x^{\beta}_{l}\right)\delta_{lij3}\right.&\\
\left.-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}\left(z-z^{\beta}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{i3}(x_{j}-x^{\beta}_{j})\right]&\left|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\right|\leq a\end{cases}

(28b)

III.2.2
The Faxén laws in integral form

In the derivation of a traditional Stokesian Dynamics formalism, the Faxén laws
SangtaeKim2005
;
Guazzelli2011
provide a bridge for connecting the kinetic motions
(\mathbf{U}^{\alpha},\mathbf{\Omega}^{\alpha},\mathsfbfit{E}^{\alpha})
with corresponding mechanical quantities
(\mathbf{F}^{\beta},\mathbf{T}^{\beta},\mathsfbfit{S}^{\beta})
of each particles. However, the validity of the Faxén laws with even viscosity
SangtaeKim2005
relies on the Lorentz reciprocal theorem and symmetry properties of the Oseen tensor. Due to the existence of odd viscosity, we can derive the generalized Lorentz reciprocal theorem (Appx.
B

C
). Comparing with the conventional Faxén laws, there is an additional minus sign for the terms contributed by odd viscosity in the generalized Faxén laws.

With the integral forms of response functions (Eqn.

III.4
Stokesian Dynamics with odd viscosity

Finally, with both the near-field (Eqn.
30
) and far-field (Eqn.
35
)
odd
mobility tensors constructed, the traditional Stokesian Dynamics formalism can be modified to incorporate the leading order effects due to odd viscosity. Mathematically, the hydrodynamic interactions in Stokesian Dynamics with both even and odd viscosity can be written as

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{e}+\mathcal{M}^{o}

(37)

where
\mathcal{M}
is the full grand mobility tensor, which contains hydrodynamic interactions from both even and odd viscosity;
\mathcal{M}^{e}
is the grand mobility tensor due to even viscosity, which includes both near-field and far-field even mobility tensors
Brady1988
;
\mathcal{M}^{o}
is the grand mobility tensor due to odd viscosity, which includes both near-field (Eqn.
30
and Appx.
D.3
) and far-field (Eqn.
35
and Appx.
D.2
) odd mobility tensors derived in this work.

Due to the lack of lubrication theory with odd viscosity, it is important to be aware that the current near-field mobility tensors are not solved exactly like in the traditional Stokesian Dynamics framework
ONeill1967
;
Goldman1967
;
Goldman1967a
. It primarily serves as a convenient regularized near-field mobility tensors for numeric implementations. When the systems of interests are not very dense, the far-field mobility tensors should be able to capture dominant hydrodynamic interactions in the systems. Lubrication effects should be of more importance for dense suspensions, and current near-field mobility tensors should be avoided in such scenarios.

With the explicit expressions of odd mobility tensors, the extra mobility tensors due to odd viscosity can be readily incorporated into any numeric implementation of traditional Stokesian Dynamics, which enables large-scale simulations (but only practical when integrated into a highly-optimized parallel implementation) of collective motions of particles suspended in a fluid medium with both even and odd viscosity.

IV
Discussion and future works

In this work, we have extended the traditional Stokesian Dynamics simulation framework to further expand its capability to active fluids exhibiting odd viscosity effects. This enables simulations of the collective behaviors of many particles suspended in a fluid medium with both even and odd viscosity, which may help understanding and interpreting recent experimental advances in active systems.

Furthermore, there are still many open questions that need to be addressed in these non-equilibrium active fluid systems. First of all, we only consider a rather simple form of odd viscosity(Eqn.
5
) under restrictive assumptions. A more general expression of odd viscosity with multiple coefficients do and should exist
Khain2022
. Other coefficients may need to be included depending on the system setup. Besides, for the continual development of the Stokesian Dynamics with odd viscosity, the corresponding wall corrections
Blake1971
;
Swan2007
and lubrication theory
ONeill1967
;
Goldman1967
;
Goldman1967a
are still missing and worth investigating. For describing Brownian motions of particles, it is also very interesting to check whether the fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Chu2019
;
Burkholder2019
;
cengio2020fluctuationdissipation
;
PhysRevLett.107.130601
;
Junco2018
;
Sarracino2019
;
PhysRevX.9.021009
still holds in such active systems.

Experimentally, it is also worth designing active systems
Farhadi2018
;
Workamp2018
that isolate hydrodynamic interactions from other interactions for a systematic investigation of the effects of odd viscosity. An active control of odd viscosity measurements will enable the exploration and quantification of additional collective behaviors of particles in the presence of odd viscosity.

In terms of numerical technique development, it is helpful to automate the numeric implementation process for the Stokesian Dynamics community. Since Stokesian Dynamics relies on the analytic expressions of mobility tensors, which are ultimately derived from the Green’s function of the system, the whole theoretical derivations of Stokesian Dynamics formalism can be automated by a Symbolic Algebra System, such as SymPy. Then, the corresponding analytic expressions of mobility tensors can be generated automatically by simply changing the fundamental Green’s function, such as replacing the Oseen tensor with the odd Oseen tensor. Similar to some existing libraries such as lbmpy
Bauer2021a
, those symbolic expressions can be used for the generation of kernels on targeted devices (such as the CUDA code for GPU devices), where automatic code optimization can be applied to have highly-efficient device-specific implementations.

Appendix A
Green’s function of odd Stokes’ equations

A.1
Green’s function in k-space

In order to obtain the Green’s function of odd Stokes’ equations (Eqn.
9
), we need to find its solution in response to a point force (Eqn.
10
),

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{s}&\mu_{o}&0\\
-\mu_{o}&\mu_{s}&0\\
0&0&\mu_{s}\end{pmatrix}\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}=\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}p-\mathbf{F}\delta(\mathbf{r})

The above equations can be transformed into the k-space by applying the Fourier transformation to both pressure field and flow fields defined as

\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{k})\right]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{k})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}},\quad p(\mathbf{r})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{p}(\mathbf{k})\right]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}\widehat{p}(\mathbf{k})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}

(38)

where
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denotes the inverse Fourier transformation,
\mathbf{k}
is the wave vector and
\widehat{p}(\mathbf{k})
and
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{k})
are the corresponding pressure and flow fields in the k-space. Plugging above expressions back into the Stokes’ equations (Eqn.
10
) yields

-\mu_{ij}k^{2}\widehat{u}_{j}(\mathbf{k})=ik_{i}\widehat{p}(\mathbf{k})-F_{i}

where
\mu_{ij}=\mu_{s}\delta_{ij}+\mu_{o}\epsilon_{ij}
. Due to the linearity of odd Stokes’ equations, both induced pressure and flow fields should be proportional to external forces (Eqn.
11
), whose corresponding k-space expressions are

\widehat{p}(\mathbf{k})=\widehat{P}_{j}(\mathbf{k})F_{j},\quad\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{k})=\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})F_{j}

where
\widehat{P}_{j}(\mathbf{k})
and
\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
denote the Green’s functions of pressure field and flow fields in the k-space, respectively. Such linear relations enable the elimination of external forces
F_{i}
, which simplify Eqn.
12
to

-k^{2}\mu_{ik}\widehat{G}_{kj}(\mathbf{k})=ik_{i}\widehat{P}_{j}(\mathbf{k})-\delta_{ij}

(39)

For simplicity, we will restrict our consideration to incompressible fluids. The incompressibility condition (
\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
) in the k-space reads

\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})k_{i}=0

(40)

Taking the inner product of Eqn.
39
with
k_{i}
yields

-k^{2}\mu_{ik}k_{i}\widehat{G}_{kj}(\mathbf{k})=-k^{2}\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{ik}k_{i}\widehat{G}_{kj}(\mathbf{k})=ik^{2}\widehat{P}_{j}(\mathbf{k})-k_{j}

It gives the Green’s function of the pressure field in the k-space as

\widehat{P}_{j}(\mathbf{k})=-\frac{ik_{j}}{k^{2}}+i\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{ik}k_{i}\widehat{G}_{kj}(\mathbf{k})

(41)

where
\mu_{ij}=\mu_{s}\delta_{ij}+\mu_{o}\varepsilon_{ij}
by definition (Eqn.
13
) and the second term is the additional pressure contribution due to the odd viscosity. Plugging the above expression back into Eqn.
39
gives that

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{s}+\mu_{o}\frac{k_{x}k_{y}}{k^{2}}&\mu_{o}\left(1-\frac{k_{x}^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)&0\\
\mu_{o}(-1+\frac{k_{y}^{2}}{k^{2}})&\mu_{s}-\mu_{o}\frac{k_{y}k_{x}}{k^{2}}&0\\
\mu_{o}\frac{k_{y}k_{z}}{k^{2}}&-\mu_{o}\frac{k_{x}k_{z}}{k^{2}}&\mu_{s}\end{pmatrix}\widehat{G}_{kj}(\mathbf{k})=-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{4}}+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{k^{2}}

(42)

The right-hand side is exactly the k-space expression of the Oseen tensor
lisicki2013four
and the matrix on the left-hand side can be directly inverted, which gives the k-space expression of the Green’s function of the flow field as

\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\frac{1}{\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{k_{y}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}{k^{4}}&-\frac{k_{x}k_{y}}{k^{4}}-\chi\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{4}}&-\frac{k_{x}k_{z}}{k^{4}}+\chi\frac{k_{y}k_{z}}{k^{4}}\\
-\frac{k_{x}k_{y}}{k^{4}}+\chi\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{4}}&\frac{k_{x}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}{k^{4}}&-\frac{k_{y}k_{z}}{k^{4}}-\chi\frac{k_{x}k_{z}}{k^{4}}\\
-\frac{k_{x}k_{z}}{k^{4}}-\chi\frac{k_{y}k_{z}}{k^{4}}&-\frac{k_{y}k_{z}}{k^{4}}+\chi\frac{k_{x}k_{z}}{k^{4}}&\frac{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}{k^{4}}\end{pmatrix}

(43)

where two auxiliary variables are introduced as

\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})=1+\chi^{2}\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}},\quad\chi=\frac{\mu_{o}}{\mu_{s}}

(44)

As any second order tensor can be decomposed into symmetric and anti-symmetric parts, the above k-space Green’s function can be split as

\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=\widehat{G}_{(ij)}(\mathbf{k})+\widehat{G}_{[ij]}(\mathbf{k})

(45a)

\widehat{G}_{(ij)}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\frac{1}{\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})}\left(-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{4}}+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{k^{2}}\right)

(45b)

\widehat{G}_{[ij]}(\mathbf{k})=-\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\frac{\chi}{\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})}\varepsilon_{ijk}k_{k}\frac{k_{z}}{k^{4}}

(45c)

where indices in parentheses and brackets indicate symmetrizing and anti-symmetrizing the orginal tensor, respectively.

A.2
Green’s function in real-space

In principle, the corresponding real-space expressions of Green’s functions can be obtained via the inverse Fourier transformation, that is,

G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})\right],\quad P_{i}(\mathbf{r})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{P}_{i}(\mathbf{k})\right]

(46)

However, analytical calculation of above inverse Fourier transformations is not straightforward due to the additional k-dependence introduced by
\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})
for non-vanishing odd viscosity. For simplicity, we focus on the fluid with a small odd viscosity
Soni2019
, i.e.
\mu_{o}\ll\mu_{s}
. Then, the dimensionless viscosity ratio
\chi\ll 1
serves as a natural expansion parameter, which physically characterizes the activity level of the system. For the weak activity limit, that is,
\chi\to 0
, the inverse of
\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})
can be expanded as

\frac{1}{\Sigma^{2}(\mathbf{k})}=\frac{1}{1+\chi^{2}\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\chi^{2}\right)^{n}=1-\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\chi^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{4})

(47)

Then, the expansions of the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts of the k-space Green’s function (Eqn.
45c
) can be written as

\widehat{G}_{(ij)}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\chi^{2}\right)^{n}\left(-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{4}}+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{k^{2}}\right)=\left[\widehat{G}_{(ij)}^{(0)}+\widehat{G}_{(ij)}^{(2)}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{4})

(48a)

\widehat{G}_{[ij]}(\mathbf{k})=-\frac{\chi}{\mu_{s}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\chi^{2}\right)^{n}\left(\varepsilon_{ijk}k_{k}\frac{k_{z}}{k^{4}}\right)=\left[\widehat{G}_{[ij]}^{(1)}+\widehat{G}_{[ij]}^{(3)}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{5})

(48b)

where
n
-th order expansion of the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts are defined as

\widehat{G}_{(ij)}^{(2n)}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\mu_{s}}\left(\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\chi^{2}\right)^{n}\left(-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{4}}+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{k^{2}}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

(49a)

\widehat{G}_{[ij]}^{(2n+1)}(\mathbf{k})=-\chi\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\mu_{s}}\left(\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\chi^{2}\right)^{n}\left(\varepsilon_{ijk}\frac{k_{k}k_{z}}{k^{4}}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

(49b)

where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of natural numbers. The inverse Fourier transformation of the lowest order expressions is easy to compute

G_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{G}_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{k})\right]=\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{4}}+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{k^{2}}\right]=\frac{1}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{r}+\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{3}}\right)

(50a)

G_{[ij]}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{G}_{[ij]}^{(1)}(\mathbf{k})\right]=-\frac{1}{\mu_{s}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\varepsilon_{ijk}\frac{k_{k}k_{z}}{k^{4}}\right]=-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\left(\frac{\delta_{k3}}{r}-\frac{r_{k}r_{3}}{r^{3}}\right)

(50b)

Note the expression of the conventional Oseen tensor is

J_{ij}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{r}+\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{3}}

(51)

which enables rewriting the above equations as

G_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{8\pi\mu_{s}}J_{ij}(\mathbf{r})

(52a)

G_{[ij]}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})=-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}\left(\frac{\delta_{kl}}{r}-\frac{r_{k}r_{l}}{r^{3}}\right)=-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}J_{kl}^{s}(\mathbf{r})=-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu_{s}}J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})

(52b)

where an
odd
Oseen tensor is analogously defined as

J_{ij}^{o}(\mathbf{r})=\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}J_{kl}^{s}(\mathbf{r})=\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}\left(\frac{\delta_{kl}}{r}-\frac{r_{k}r_{l}}{r^{3}}\right)

(53)

For convenience, an auxiliary symmetric tensor
J_{ij}^{s}(\mathbf{r})
is also introduced, which only differs with the symmetric Oseen tensor
J_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
by a minus sign of the anisotropic term

J_{ij}^{s}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{r}-\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{3}}

(54)

As for the higher order terms, it can be constructed from the lower order solutions by noting the following properties of the Fourier transformation

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}f(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}(-k_{z}^{2})\widehat{f}(\mathbf{k})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}},\quad\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}(-k^{2})\widehat{f}(\mathbf{k})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}

(55)

Therefore, the next order solutions can be constructed as

G_{(ij)}^{(2)}(\mathbf{r})=-\chi^{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\widehat{G}_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{k})\right]=-\chi^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\nabla^{-2}\left[\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{G}_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{k})\right]\right]

(56a)

G_{[ij]}^{(3)}(\mathbf{r})=-\chi^{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\widehat{G}_{[ij]}^{(1)}(\mathbf{k})\right]=-\chi^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\nabla^{-2}\left[\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{G}_{[ij])}^{(1)}(\mathbf{k})\right]\right]

(56b)

where
\nabla^{-2}
is the inverse Laplace operator defined as

\nabla^{-2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}G(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),\quad G(\mathbf{r})=-\frac{1}{4\pi r}

(57)

Here,
G(\mathbf{r})
is the Green’s function of the 3D Laplace’s equation. Also please be aware that the inverse Laplace operator
\nabla^{-2}
is spherically symmetric but the double derivative in z-direction
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}
is not. Thus, it’s important to choose a proper order between two operators to maintain the desired symmetry for intermediate steps.

In the next, both symmetric parts
\left(G_{(ij)}^{(2)}(\mathbf{r})\right)
and anti-symmetric parts
\left(G_{[ij]}^{(3)}(\mathbf{r})\right)
will be calculated as an illustration and higher order terms can be computed in a similar manner.

•

the symmetric part
G_{(ij)}^{(2)}(\mathbf{r})

For the symmetric part, the inverse Laplace operator can be computed as:

\nabla^{-2}\left[\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[G_{(ij)}^{(0)}\right]\right]=\frac{1}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\nabla^{-2}\left[\frac{\delta_{ij}}{r}+\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{3}}\right]=\frac{1}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}G(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})J_{ij}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

(58)

The inverse Laplace operator of the Oseen tensor can be evaluated in spherical coordinates, which gives

\nabla^{-2}\left[J_{ij}(\mathbf{r})\right]=\frac{3}{4}r\delta_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r}

(59)

Then, the double derivative in z-direction is straightforward to calculate, which gives the real-space expression of the symmetric part as

\displaystyle G_{(ij)}^{(2)}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\chi^{2}}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{4}r\delta_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r}\right)

(60)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\chi^{2}}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\left[\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\frac{r_{3}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{r}\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{4}\left(1-3\frac{z^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{3}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\frac{r_{3}}{r^{3}}\left(\delta_{i3}r_{j}+r_{i}\delta_{j3}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{r}\delta_{i3}\delta_{j3}\right]

•

the anti-symmetric part
G_{[ij]}^{(3)}(\mathbf{r})

For the anti-symmetric part, the inverse Laplace operator can be calculated as

\displaystyle\nabla^{-2}\left[\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[G_{[ij]}^{(1)}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}\nabla^{-2}\left[\frac{\delta_{kl}}{r}-\frac{r_{k}r_{l}}{r^{3}}\right]

(61)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}\nabla^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}G(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})J^{s}_{ij}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

The inverse Laplace operator of
J^{s}_{ij}(\mathbf{r})
can be similarly evaluated in the spherical coordinates, which gives

\nabla^{-2}\left[J^{s}_{ij}(\mathbf{r})\right]=\frac{1}{4}r\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r}

(62)

Then, plugging it back and evaluate the double derivative in z-direction yields the real-space expression of the anti-symmetric part as

\displaystyle G_{[ij]}^{(3)}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi^{3}}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}r\delta_{kl}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{r_{k}r_{l}}{r}\right)

(63)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi^{3}}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{l3}\left[\frac{1}{4}\left(1-\frac{r_{3}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{r}\delta_{kl}-\frac{1}{4}\left(1-3\frac{z^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{r_{k}r_{l}}{r^{3}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{2}\frac{r_{3}}{r^{3}}\left(\delta_{k3}r_{l}+r_{k}\delta_{l3}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{r}\delta_{k3}\delta_{l3}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi^{3}}{8\pi\mu_{s}}\left[\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\frac{r_{3}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{r}\varepsilon_{ij3}-\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\frac{r_{3}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{r_{3}}{r^{3}}\varepsilon_{ijk}r_{k}\right]

Finally, the Green’s function in the real-space can be expressed as

G_{ij}(\mathbf{r})=G_{(ij)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{[ij]}(\mathbf{r})

(64a)

G_{(ij)}(\mathbf{r})=G_{(ij)}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{(ij)}^{(2)}(\mathbf{r})+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{4})

(64b)

G_{[ij]}(\mathbf{r})=G_{[ij]}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r})+G_{[ij]}^{(3)}(\mathbf{r})+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{5})

(64c)

where the explicit expressions of the symmetric Green’s functions are given by Eqn.
52a
and
60
; the explicit expressions of the anti-symmetric Green’s functions are given by Eqn.
52b
and
63
.

Appendix B
Generalized Lorentz reciprocal theorem

Suppose that
(\mathbf{u}^{(1)},\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(1)})
and
(\mathbf{u}^{(2)},\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(2)})
are two different solutions of the
odd
Stokes’ equations with the same fluid domain
V
but subjected to different boundary conditions at its bounding surface
S
. Due to the linearity of the viscosity tensor, the overall stress tensor can be separated into stresses contributed by even and odd viscosity, that is,
\mathsfbfit{\sigma}=\mathsfbfit{\sigma}_{e}+\mathsfbfit{\sigma}_{o}
. Then, the generalized Lorentz reciprocal theorem states that

\displaystyle\oint_{S}\mathbf{u}^{(1)}\cdot\left[\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(2)}_{e}+\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(2)}_{o}\right)\cdot\mathbf{\hat{n}}\right]\mathrm{d}S-\int_{V}\mathbf{u}^{(1)}\cdot\left[\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\cdot\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(2)}_{e}+\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(2)}_{o}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}V

(65)

\displaystyle=\oint_{S}\mathbf{u}^{(2)}\cdot\left[\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(1)}_{e}-\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(1)}_{o}\right)\cdot\mathbf{\hat{n}}\right]\mathrm{d}S-\int_{V}\mathbf{u}^{(2)}\cdot\left[\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\cdot\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(1)}_{e}-\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{(1)}_{o}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}V

The proof of the above generalized Lorentz reciprocal theorem is analogous to the proof of the conventional Lorentz reciprocal theorem with only even viscosity
Graham2018
;
Guazzelli2011
;
SangtaeKim2005
. By applying the divergence theorem, it is equivalent to prove that

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\left[u^{(1)}_{j}\sigma^{(2)}_{e\ ij}+u^{(1)}_{j}\sigma^{(2)}_{o\ ij}\right]-u^{(1)}_{i}\nabla_{j}\left[\sigma_{e\ ij}^{(2)}+\sigma_{o\ ij}^{(2)}\right]

(66)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\left[u^{(2)}_{j}\sigma^{(1)}_{e\ ij}-u^{(2)}_{j}\sigma^{(1)}_{o\ ij}\right]-u^{(2)}_{i}\nabla_{j}\left[\sigma_{e\ ij}^{(1)}-\sigma_{o\ ij}^{(1)}\right]

Note the following useful identities

\sigma^{(2)}_{ij}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(1)}=\nabla_{j}\left(\sigma_{ij}^{(2)}u_{i}^{(1)}\right)-u_{i}^{(1)}\nabla_{j}\sigma_{ij}^{(2)}=\nabla_{i}\left(u_{j}^{(1)}\sigma_{ij}^{(2)}\right)-u_{i}^{(1)}\nabla_{j}\sigma_{ij}^{(2)}

(67a)

\sigma^{(1)}_{ij}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(2)}=\nabla_{j}\left(\sigma_{ij}^{(1)}u_{i}^{(2)}\right)-u_{i}^{(2)}\nabla_{j}\sigma_{ij}^{(1)}=\nabla_{i}\left(u_{j}^{(2)}\sigma_{ij}^{(1)}\right)-u_{i}^{(2)}\nabla_{j}\sigma_{ij}^{(1)}

(67b)

where stress tensors are assumed to be symmetric, i.e.
\sigma_{ij}^{(1)}=\sigma_{ji}^{(1)}
and
\sigma_{ij}^{(2)}=\sigma_{ji}^{(2)}
. This holds for both even and odd viscous stress tensors as long as there is no external angular momentum in the system. Therefore, it further reduces to prove that

\sigma_{e\ ij}^{(2)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(1)}+\sigma_{o\ ij}^{(2)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(1)}=\sigma_{e\ ij}^{(1)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(2)}-\sigma_{o\ ij}^{(1)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(2)}

(68)

For a general linear constitutive relation of Newtonian liquids, the relation between the stress tensor and the velocity gradient tensor can be written as

\sigma_{ij}^{(1)}=\sigma_{e\ ij}^{(1)}+\sigma_{o\ ij}^{(1)}=\left(\mu^{e}_{ijkl}+\mu^{o}_{ijkl}\right)\nabla_{l}u_{k}^{(1)},\quad\sigma_{ij}^{(2)}=\sigma_{e\ ij}^{(2)}+\sigma_{o\ ij}^{(2)}=\left(\mu^{e}_{ijkl}+\mu^{o}_{ijkl}\right)\nabla_{l}u_{k}^{(2)}

(69)

Then, it’s equivalent to prove that

\mu_{ijkl}^{e}\nabla_{l}u_{k}^{(2)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(1)}+\mu_{ijkl}^{o}\nabla_{l}u_{k}^{(2)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(1)}=\mu_{ijkl}^{e}\nabla_{l}u_{k}^{(1)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(2)}-\mu_{ijkl}^{o}\nabla_{l}u_{k}^{(1)}\nabla_{j}u_{i}^{(2)}

(70)

For the above equation to hold everywhere in the fluid domain, it requires that

\mu^{e}_{ijkl}=\mu^{e}_{klij},\quad\mu^{o}_{ijkl}=-\mu^{o}_{klij}

(71)

These symmetry relations correspond to the time reversal symmetry of the system and are fulfilled by even and odd viscosity tensors respectively (Eqn.
2
and
4
), which completes the proof of the generalized Lorentz reciprocal theorem.

Appendix C
Generalized Faxén laws

The Faxén laws connect the kinetic motions
(\mathbf{U}^{\alpha},\mathbf{\Omega}^{\alpha},\mathsfbfit{E}^{\alpha})
with corresponding mechanical quantities
(\mathbf{F}^{\beta},\mathbf{T}^{\beta},\mathsfbfit{S}^{\beta})
of each particle. However, the validity of the Faxén laws with even viscosity relies on the Lorentz reciprocal theorem and symmetry properties of the Oseen tensor
SangtaeKim2005
. With the generalized Lorentz reciprocal theorem derived in Appx.
B
, the conventional Faxén laws will also be generalized here to include additional effects due to odd viscosity.

Following the proof of conventional Faxén laws described in
SangtaeKim2005
, let us consider a particle that translates in the fluid with velocity
\mathbf{U}_{1}
, which has a corresponding flow field
\mathbf{u}_{1}
, and another particle that is stationary but that there is a point-force
\mathbf{F}
applied at location
\mathbf{y}
outside the particle. Then, applying the generalized Lorentz theorem gives

\mathbf{U}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{2}-\mathbf{u}_{1}(\mathbf{y})\cdot\mathbf{F}=0

(72)

where
\mathbf{F}_{2}
is the force acting on the stationary particle due to the corresponding flow field
\mathbf{u}_{2}(\mathbf{x})
induced by the point force
\mathbf{F}
. Because of the linearity of the
odd
Stokes’ equations (Eqn.
9
), the ambient flow for the translating particle can be expressed as

\mathbf{u}_{1}(\mathbf{x})=\left\{\mathcal{F}_{e}\left[\frac{J_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})}{8\pi\mu}\right]+\mathcal{F}_{o}\left[\frac{-\chi J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})}{8\pi\mu}\right]\right\}\cdot\mathbf{U}_{1}

(73)

where
\mathcal{F}_{e}
and
\mathcal{F}_{o}
represent a linear response function due to even and odd viscosity, respectively;
\mathcal{F}_{e}
and
\mathcal{F}_{o}
may or may not have the same functional form. Here,
\xi
denotes the region where the source force densities are distributed. Plugging it back enables the elimination of
\mathbf{U}_{1}
, which yields

F^{2}_{i}=\left\{\mathcal{F}_{e}\left[\frac{J_{ji}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})}{8\pi\mu}\right]+\mathcal{F}_{o}\left[\frac{-\chi J^{o}_{ji}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})}{8\pi\mu}\right]\right\}F_{j}

(74)

Note the symmetry properties of both even and odd Oseen tensors

J_{ji}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=J_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),\quad J^{o}_{ji}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=-J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})

(75a)

J_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=J_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}-\mathbf{x}),\quad J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=J^{o}_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}-\mathbf{x})

(75b)

Applying those symmetry properties gives

F^{2}_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{e}\left[\frac{J_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}-\mathbf{y})}{8\pi\mu}F_{j}\right]+\mathcal{F}_{o}\left[\chi\frac{J^{o}_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}-\mathbf{y})}{8\pi\mu}F_{j}\right]

(76)

On the other hand, the flow fields contributed by even and odd viscosity induced by a point force
\mathbf{F}
are:

\mathbf{u}^{e}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=\frac{J_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}-\mathbf{y})}{8\pi\mu}F_{j},\quad\mathbf{u}^{o}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=-\chi\frac{J^{o}_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}-\mathbf{y})}{8\pi\mu}F_{j}

(77)

where
\mathbf{u}^{e}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})
and
\mathbf{u}^{o}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})
denote the flow contributed by the even and odd viscosity, respectively. Thus, the terms inside the brackets are identified as just the induced ambient flow, which gives

\mathbf{F}_{2}=\mathcal{F}_{e}\left[\mathbf{u}^{e}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\right]-\mathcal{F}_{o}\left[\mathbf{u}^{o}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\right]

(78)

It shows that there is just one additional minus sign for the flow contributed the odd viscosity in the generalized Faxén laws. Following the above construction, one can prove similar relations for torques and stresslets, that is,

\mathbf{T}=\mathcal{T}_{e}\left[\mathbf{u}^{e}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\right]-\mathcal{T}_{o}\left[\mathbf{u}^{o}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\right]

(79)

\mathsfbfit{S}=\mathcal{S}_{e}\left[\mathbf{u}^{e}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\right]-\mathcal{S}_{o}\left[\mathbf{u}^{o}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\right]

(80)

In this work, we will only consider additional new contributions due to the odd viscosity in the Faxén laws. The Faxén laws for even viscosity can be easily found in most micro-hydrodynamics books
SangtaeKim2005
;
Guazzelli2011
;
Graham2018
. For a collection of particles, the linear superposition enables writing the ambient
odd
flow field
\mathbf{u}^{o\prime}
induced by all other particles excluding the
\alpha
-th particle itself as

\displaystyle u_{i}^{o\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\chi}{8\pi\mu}\sum_{\beta=1,\beta\neq\alpha}^{N}\left\{\mathcal{F}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta})\right]F_{j}^{\beta}+\mathcal{T}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta})\right]T_{j}^{\beta}+\mathcal{S}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\beta})\right]S_{jk}^{\beta}\right\}

(81)

where
\mathcal{F}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
,
\mathcal{T}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
and
\mathcal{S}\left[J^{o}_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\right]
denote linear functions which describe the odd viscosity related responses to external forces, torques, stresslets respectively. Based on the results derived in Eqn.
78
,
79
and
80
,
the kinetic motions of
\alpha
-th particle due to the ambient
odd
flow
\mathbf{u}^{o\prime}
induced by other particles (
\beta\neq\alpha
) can be therefore written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} U_{i}^{\alpha}-U_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})&=-\mathcal{F}\left[u_{i}^{o\prime}(\mathbf{x});\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\right]\\
\Omega_{i}^{\alpha}-\Omega_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})&=-\mathcal{T}[u_{i}^{o\prime}(\mathbf{x});\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}]\\
E_{ij}^{\alpha}-E_{ij}^{\infty}(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})&=-\mathcal{S}[u_{i}^{o\prime}(\mathbf{x});\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}]\end{aligned}\right.

(82)

The above relations are the Faxén laws for the odd viscosity, which differ with the Faxén laws for the even viscosity by a minus sign.

A short note on spanning even trees
Jiangdong Ai, Zhipeng Gao, Xiangzhou Liu, Jun Yue

Abstract
We call a tree T T is even if every pair of its leaves is joined by a path of even length. Jackson and Yoshimoto [J. Graph Theory, 2024] conjectured that every r r -regular nonbipartite connected graph G G has a spanning even tree. They verified this conjecture for the case when G G has a 2 2 -factor. In this paper, we prove that the conjecture holds when r r is odd, thereby resolving the only remaining unsolved case for this conjecture.

Introduction

We refer to
[
1
]
for terminology and notation not introduced here. All graphs considered throughout this paper contain no loops. A tree is called
even
if all pairs of vertices of degree one are joined by a path of even length. For a graph
G
, we call a subgraph
H
, a
spanning
subgraph, of
G
if
V(H)=V(G)
. In particular, we say
H
is a
k
-factor of
G
if it is a
k
-regular spanning subgraph of
G
.

Motivated by Saito’s question regarding the existence of spanning even trees in regular connected graphs, Jackson and Yoshimoto proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
3
]

If
G
is an
r
-regular nonbipartite connected graph, then
G
contains a spanning even tree.

This conjecture was partially confirmed ([Theorem 1] in
[
3
]
) under the condition that
G
possesses a 2-factor. So sufficient conditions for an regular graph to have a 2-factor will be helpful. Especially, Petersen
[
5
]
showed that every
r
-regular graph where
r
is even admits a 2-factor, narrowing our focus to verifying the conjecture for odd integer
r
.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
3
]

Every regular nonbipartite connected graph which has a 2-factor has a spanning even tree.

In fact, following a conclusion established by Hanson et al.
[
2
]
, the authors
[
3
]
posed that the only remaining unsolved case is when
r
is odd and
G
has at least
r
bridges.

Following the same idea, we hope to find a 2-factor in an
r
-regular graph where
r
is odd. However, this does not always come true since we can easily obtain a counter-example as depicted in Figure
1
. It’s reasonable to assume that there exists a spanning subgraph similar to ‘2-factor’ in a regular graph, that is
\{1,2\}
-factor, which is defined in Definition
2
. There is a huge literature investigating graph factors and factorization, see more information on
[
6
]
.

In this short note, we address the final unsolved case for Conjecture
1.1
.

Additional Definitions and Useful Tools.

Definition 1
.

A spanning subgraph
F
of
G
is called an
\{a,b\}
-factor of
G
if
a\leq d_{F}(x)\leq b
for all
x\in V(G)
, and we usually call a
\{k,k\}
-factor a
k
-factor. A
\{k-1,k\}
-factor is called a good
\{k-1,k\}
-factor if each of whose components is regular.

Definition 2
.

An even tree
T
in a graph
G
is said to be good if
G\setminus V(T)
has a good
\{1,2\}
-factor.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
4
]

Let
r
and
k
be positive integers. If
k\leq 2(2r+1)/3
, then every
(2r+1)
-regular graph has a good
\{k-1,k\}
-factor.

Lemma 1.4
.

[
5
]

Every
2k
-regular graph contains a
2
-factor.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
G
be an
r
-regular connected graph where
r
is odd, then it has a good
\{1,2\}
-factor
F
with at least one
2
-regular component.

Proof.

Let
k=2
, then there is a good
\{1,2\}
-factor
F
by Theorem
1.3
. If
F
is not a perfect matching, the result holds clearly.
If
F
is a perfect matching, we can derive a spanning subgraph
G^{{}^{\prime}}
of
G
by deleting
E(F)
. Since
G^{{}^{\prime}}
is
(r-1)
-regular, Lemma
1.4
ensures the existence of a 2-factor of
G^{{}^{\prime}}
, which is also a 2-factor of
G
.

In
[
3
]
, there is a simple but valuable lemma.

Lemma 1.6
.

[
3
]

Let
G
be an
r
-regular nonbipartite connected graph and
Y\subset V(G)
with
E(Y)=\emptyset
. Then for any
X\subset V(G)\setminus Y
with
|X|\leq|Y|,E(Y,V(G)\setminus(X\cup Y))\neq\emptyset
.

2
Main Results

We want to point out that the main proof technique employed in this section is significantly similar to that of Theorem
4
in
[
3
]
of Jackson and Yoshimoto. Compared with their proofs, we work on the
\{1,2\}
-factor rather than the
2
-factor. This adjustment necessitates an additional discussion regarding the component that consists of a single edge. In the following proof, denote by
[a,b]
the set of
\{a,a+1,a+2,\ldots,b-1,b\}
. In particular, let
[b]=[1,b]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
G
is an
r
-regular nonbipartite connected graph where
r
is odd, then
G
has a good even tree.

Proof.

By Corollary
1.5
there is a good
\{1,2\}
-factor
F=F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup\cdots\cup F_{k}
of
G
such that
F_{i}
is a cycle for some
i
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
F_{1}
is a cycle. For any vertex
v
of
F_{i}
(
i\in[k]
),
we define two functions as follows:

e_{1}(v,F_{i})=\begin{cases}uv,u\in N_{F_{i}}(v)&\text{if $F_{i}$ is a cycle,}\\
\emptyset&\text{if $F_{i}$ is an edge.}\end{cases}

e_{2}(v,F_{i})=\begin{cases}u_{1}v\cup u_{2}v,u_{1},u_{2}\in N_{F_{i}}(v)&\text{if $F_{i}$ is a cycle,}\\
E(F_{i})&\text{if $F_{i}$ is an edge.}\end{cases}

If
F_{1}
is an odd cycle, then
T=F_{1}\setminus e_{1}(x,F_{1})
is a good even tree for any vertex
x
of
F_{1}
. Thus, we may assume that there is no odd cycle in
F
. Let
(X_{1},Y_{1})
be a bipartition of
F_{1}
. Since
F_{1}
is an even cycle, we have
|X_{1}|=|Y_{1}|
. If there is a chord
uv\in E(G)
such that
u,v\in Y_{1}
, then
T=(F_{1}\setminus e_{2}(v,F_{1}))\cup\{uv\}
is a good even tree. Therefore, we may assume that
E(Y_{1})=\emptyset
. Since
|X_{1}|=|Y_{1}|
, by Lemma
1.6
we have that
E(Y_{1},V(G)\setminus V(F_{1}))\neq\emptyset
.

We say that a bipartition
(X,Y)
of
F
is consistent if
X_{1}\subseteq X
and
Y_{1}\subseteq Y
. For any consistent bipartition of
F
, let
X_{i}=X\cap V(F_{i})
and
Y_{i}=Y\cap V(F_{i})
for all
i\in[2,k]
. Then
(X_{i},Y_{i})
is a bipartition of
F_{i}
for all
i\in[k]
. A sequence
F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{t}}
in
F
is a good sequence with respect to the bipartition
(X,Y)
if
E(Y_{i_{h}},X_{i_{h+1}})\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq h<t
. We say that
F_{i}
is good with respect to the bipartition
(X,Y)
if there is a good sequence from
F_{1}
to
F_{i}
.

Now we choose a consistent bipartition
(X,Y)
of
F
such that the number of
F_{i}
which is good with respect to
(X,Y)
is as large as possible. By symmetry, we may assume
F^{good}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{t}\}
.

Case 1.
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i})=\emptyset
.

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{t}|X_{i}|=\sum_{i=1}^{t}|Y_{i}|
, Lemma
1.6
implies that
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i},V(G)\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{t}F_{i})\neq\emptyset
. Then there is a vertex
v\in F_{j}
such that
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i},v)\neq\emptyset
, where
j>t
. Choose a bipartition
(X_{j}^{{}^{\prime}},Y_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
of
F_{j}
such that
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i},X_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset
(By choosing
X_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}
such that
v\in X_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}
). Then
F_{j}
becomes to be good with respect to the bipartition
X^{{}^{\prime}}=(X\setminus X_{j})\cup X_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
Y^{{}^{\prime}}=(Y\setminus Y_{j})\cup Y_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}
of
F
and all elements in
F^{good}\cup\{F_{j}\}
remain good with respect to
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
. This contradicts the choice of
(X,Y)
.

Case 2.
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i})\neq\emptyset
.

We can choose an edge
y_{p}y_{q}\in E(Y_{p},Y_{q})
for some
1\leq p\leq q\leq k
. Let

F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{r}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ F_{j_{1}},F_{j_{2}},\cdots,F_{j_{s}}

be good sequences with respect to
(X,Y)
with
i_{1}=1=j_{1}
,
i_{r}=p
and
j_{s}=q
. Choose an edge
y_{i_{h}}x_{i_{h+1}}\in E(Y_{i_{h}},X_{i_{h+1}})
for all
1\leq h<r
and an edge
y_{j_{h}}x_{j_{h+1}}\in E(Y_{j_{h}},X_{j_{h+1}})
for all
1\leq h<s
. Consider the following two subcases.

Subcase 2.1.
F_{q}\in\{F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{r}}\}
.

We have
F_{q}=F_{i_{a}}
for some
1\leq a\leq r
. Then

T=\left(\bigcup_{1\leq h\leq{r-1}}(y_{i_{h}}x_{i_{h+1}}\cup(F_{i_{h}}\setminus e_{1}(y_{i_{h}},F_{i_{h}})))\right)\cup\{y_{p}y_{q}\}\cup(F_{p}\setminus e_{2}(y_{p},F_{p}))

is a good even tree.

Subcase 2.2.
F_{q}\notin\{F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{r}}\}
.

Choose an integer
j_{b}
such that
F_{j_{b}}\in\{F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{r}}\}
and
b
is as large as possible. We have
1\leq b<s
since
j_{1}=i_{1}
and
F_{j_{s}}=F_{q}\notin\{F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{r}}\}
. Then
F_{j_{b}}=F_{i_{a}}
for some
1\leq a\leq r
and
\{F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{r}}\}\cap\{F_{j_{b+1}},F_{j_{b+2}},\cdots,F_{j_{s}}\}=\emptyset
. Then,

\displaystyle T=

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup_{1\leq h\leq{r-1}}(y_{i_{h}}x_{i_{h+1}}\cup(F_{i_{h}}\setminus e_{1}(y_{i_{h}},F_{i_{h}})))\right)\cup\{y_{p}y_{q}\}\cup(F_{p}\setminus e_{2}(y_{p},F_{p}))\cup

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup_{b+1\leq h\leq{s-1}}(y_{j_{h}}x_{j_{h+1}}\cup(F_{j_{h}}\setminus e_{1}(y_{j_{h}},F_{j_{h}})))\right)\cup\{y_{j_{b}}x_{j_{b+1}}\}\cup(F_{q}\setminus e_{1}(y_{q},F_{q}))

is a good even tree.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
G
is an
r
-regular nonbipartite connected graph where
r
is odd, then
G
has a spanning even tree.

Proof.

Let
T
be a good even tree such that
|V(T)|
is as large as possible, the existence of
T
is guaranteed by Theorem
2.1
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
V(T)<V(G)
. Let
(X_{0},Y_{0})
be the bipartition of
T
where
X_{0}
contains all leaves of
T
. Let
F=F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup\cdots\cup F_{m}
be a good
\{1,2\}
-factor of
G\setminus V(T)
.

Claim 1
.

E(X_{0},F)=\emptyset
and
E(Y_{0},F)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there is an edge
xu\in E(X_{0},F_{i})
for some
1\leq i\leq m
. If
F_{i}
is an even cycle, then
T^{{}^{\prime}}=T\cup\{xu\}\cup(F_{i}\setminus e_{1}(u,F_{i}))
is a good even tree, a contradiction to the maximality of
T
. If
F_{i}=uv_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{c}u
is an odd cycle, then
T^{{}^{\prime}}=T\cup\{xu\}\cup(F_{i}\setminus\{v_{1}v_{2}\})
is a good even tree, a contradiction to the maximality of
T
. If
F_{i}
is an edge then
T^{{}^{\prime}}=T\cup\{xu\}\cup F_{i}
is a good even tree, a contradiction to the maximality of
T
.

Hence
E(X_{0},F)=\emptyset
. The assertion that
E(Y_{0},F)\neq\emptyset
now follows from the assumptions that
G
is connected and
V(T)<V(G)
.

Thus, we suppose
yu\in E(Y_{0},F_{i})
for some
i\in[m]
. If
F_{i}
is an odd cycle, then
T^{{}^{\prime}}=T\cup\{yu\}\cup(F_{i}\setminus e_{1}(u,F_{i}))
is a good even tree, a contradiction to the maximality of
T
. Therefore,
F_{i}
is an even cycle or an edge.
Without loss of generality, let
F^{even}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{k}\}
be the set of all even cycles and edges in
F
. Clearly,
F^{even}\neq\emptyset
. Let
F^{{}^{\prime}}=\cup^{k}_{j=1}F_{j}
. Since
F^{even}
is the set of all even cycles and edges in
F
,
F^{{}^{\prime}}
is a balanced bipartite subgraph of
G
. Let
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
be a bipartition of
F^{{}^{\prime}}
, let
X_{i}=X^{{}^{\prime}}\cap V(F_{i})
and
Y_{i}=Y^{{}^{\prime}}\cap V(F_{i})
for all
i\in[k]
. Then
(X_{i},Y_{i})
is a bipartition of
F_{i}
for all
i\in[k]
. A sequence
F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{t}}
in
F^{even}
is an additive sequence with respect to the bipartition
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
if
E(Y_{i_{h}},X_{i_{h+1}})\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq h<t
and
E(Y_{0},X_{i_{1}})\neq\emptyset
. We say that
F_{j}\in F^{even}
is additive with respect to the bipartition
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
if there exists an additive sequence ending at it.

Now we choose a bipartition
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
of
F^{{}^{\prime}}
such that the number of elements in
F^{even}
which are additive with respect to
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
is as large as possible. Let
F^{add}
be the set of elements in
F^{even}
which are additive with respect to
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
. Then by Claim
1
and the definition of
F^{even}
, we obtain that
F^{add}\neq\emptyset
.
By symmetry, we may assume
F^{add}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{t}\}
.

Case 1.
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i})=\emptyset
.

Lemma
1.6
implies that
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i},V(G)\setminus V(F^{add}))\neq\emptyset
. Notice that

V(G)\setminus V(F^{add})=X_{0}\cup Y_{0}\cup\bigcup_{j=t+1}^{m}V(F_{j})

and by Claim
1
,
E(X_{0},F)=\emptyset
.
If there exists an edge
y_{p}y_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}\in E(Y_{p},Y_{0})
for some
1\leq p\leq t
. Let
F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\cdots,F_{i_{s}}
be an additive sequence with respect to
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
with
i_{s}=p
. Choose an edge
y_{i_{h}}x_{i_{h+1}}\in E(Y_{i_{h}},X_{i_{h+1}})
for all
1\leq h<s
and
y_{0}x_{i_{1}}\in E(Y_{0},X_{i_{1}})
. Then

T^{{}^{\prime}}=T\cup\{y_{0}x_{i_{1}}\}\cup\left(\bigcup^{s-1}_{h=1}(y_{i_{h}}x_{i_{h+1}}\cup(F_{i_{h}}\setminus e_{1}(y_{i_{h}},F_{i_{h}})))\right)\cup\{y_{p}y_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}\}\cup(F_{p}\setminus e_{2}(y_{p},F_{p}))

is a good even tree in
G
. This contradicts the maximality of
T
.

Hence there is an edge
y_{p}v_{j}\in E(Y_{p},F_{j})
for some
1\leq p\leq t
and some
t<j\leq m
. If
F_{j}
is an even cycle or an edge, then
F_{j}
is additive with respect to the bipartition
X^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=(X^{{}^{\prime}}\setminus X_{j})\cup Y_{j}
and
Y^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=(Y^{{}^{\prime}}\setminus Y_{j})\cup X_{j}
and all elements in

remain additive with respect to
(X^{{}^{\prime\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime\prime}})
. This contradicts the choice of
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
. Thus,

must be an odd cycle. Then

is a good even tree in

. This contradicts the maximality of

.

Case 2.
E(\cup^{t}_{i=1}Y_{i})\neq\emptyset
.

We can choose an edge
y_{p}y_{q}^{{}^{\prime}}\in E(Y_{p},Y_{q})
for some
1\leq p\leq q\leq t
. Let

be additive sequences with respect to
(X^{{}^{\prime}},Y^{{}^{\prime}})
with

. Choose edges

,

y_{j_{h}}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{j_{h+1}}^{{}^{\prime}}\in E(Y_{j_{h}},X_{j_{h+1}})

Subcase 2.1.

Subcase 2.2.

.

\displaystyle T^{{}^{\prime}}=

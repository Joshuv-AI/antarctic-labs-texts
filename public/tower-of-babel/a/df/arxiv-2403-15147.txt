Splitting methods for unbounded operators
Arieh Iserles, Karolina Kropielnicka

Abstract
This paper considers computational methods that split a vector field into three components in the case when both the vector field and the split components might be unbounded. We first employ classical Taylor expansion which, after some algebra, results in an expression for a second-order splitting which, strictly speaking, makes sense only for bounded operators. Next, using an alternative approach, we derive an error expression and an error bound in the same setting which are however valid in the presence of unbounded operators. While the paper itself is concerned with second-order splittings using three components, the method of proof in the presence of unboundedness remains valid (although significantly more complicated) in a more general scenario, which will be the subject of a forthcoming paper.



Keywords:
Operator splitting, unbounded operators, Duhamel’s formula, error bounds.

The problem

Splitting methods are a major methodology in modern numerical analysis of time-dependent differential equations
[
blanes24smd
,
mclachlan02sm
]
. Very often we wish to solve a problem of the form

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}u}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=f(u)+g(u),\qquad t\geq t_{0},

(1.1)

given with an initial condition at
t=t_{0}
and possibly suitable boundary conditions, while the solution of the problems

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}v}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=f(v)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=g(w)

(1.2)

is either known or can be approximated with great ease. The idea of a splitting method is to compose an approximation to the solution of (
1.1
) by leveraging our ability to solve (
1.2
). In particular, once both
f
and
g
are linear,
f(u)=Au
and
g(u)=Bu
(where
A
and
B
are, in general, linear operators), the exact solution of (
1.1
),
u(t_{0}+h)={\mathrm{e}}^{h\mathcal{L}}u(t_{0})
where
\mathcal{L}=A+B
, might be difficult to compute and popular approaches are the
Lie–Trotter splitting

u(t_{0}+h)\approx u_{\mathrm{LT}}(t_{0}+h)={\mathrm{e}}^{hA}{\mathrm{e}}^{hB}u(t_{0})

(1.3)

and the
Strang splitting

u(t_{0}+h)\approx u_{\mathrm{S}}(t_{0}+h)={\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{1}{2}hA}{\mathrm{e}}^{hB}{\mathrm{e}}^{\frac{1}{2}hA}u(t_{0}).

(1.4)

Provided that
A
and
B
, hence also
\mathcal{L}
, are bounded linear operators (for example, finite-dimensional matrices), we can expand in Taylor series and prove with great ease that

u_{\mathrm{LT}}(t_{0}+h)-u(t_{0}+h)+{\cal O}\!\left(h^{2}\right),\qquad u_{\mathrm{S}}(t_{0}+h)=u(t_{0}+h)+{\cal O}\!\left(h^{3}\right)

– the Lie–Trotter and Strang splittings are of
order
1 and 2, respectively. Likewise, it is possible to derive higher-order splittings, cf.
[
blanes24smd
,
mclachlan02sm
]
. This, however, becomes significantly more complicated once either
A
or
B
(or both) are unbounded operators, since Taylor expansion may no longer make sense . An example, that motivated much interest in this problem, is the
linear Schrödinger equation

{\mathrm{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u-V(x)u,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.5)

given with the initial condition
u(x,0)=u_{0}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Here
V
is the
interaction potential
of a quantum system,
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Note that, while
V
is a multiplication operator, which is bounded,
1
1

1

Its boundedness is contingent on
V
being a bounded function. There are instances, like the evolution of a hydrogen atom in quantum mechanics, where
V
is unbounded.
the Laplace operator
\Delta
is unbounded. Thus, while
{\mathrm{i}}t(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+V)
is unbounded it may generate a semigroup
{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}t(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+V)}
that is strongly continuous, but not necessarily have a Taylor expansion. The same applies to
{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\alpha t\Delta}
and
{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\alpha tV}
for any

. Therefore we cannot be assured that, say, the Lie–Trotter splitting (
1.3
) or the Strang splitting (
1.4
) are of order 1 or 2, respectively, just by using a Taylor expansion.

Another way of looking at the problem is that unboundedness places additional restrictions on the function class under consideration. Referring again to (
1.5
) and restricting the discussion to classical solutions, we need
u\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for

to make sense and
u\in\mathrm{H}^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the approximation
{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}t\Delta}u\approx\sum_{m=0}^{s}\frac{1}{m!}({\mathrm{i}}t\Delta)^{m}u
to be well defined. This means regularity well in excess of the minimal existence and uniqueness conditions for a classical solution of (
1.5
).

A good illustration of limitations on regularity is the function

u(x)=\left\{\hskip-9.0pt\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathrm{e}}^{-x^{\alpha}},&x>0,\\[4.0pt]
0,&x\leq 0,\end{array}\right.\hskip-9.0pt

where
\alpha>0
is non-integer. It is elementary to prove that
u\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(0,\infty)
if and only if
\alpha>s-\frac{1}{2}
. (If
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
then
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(0,\infty)

The minimal conditions consistent with an order-
p

u\in\mathrm{H}^{p}
(on the face of it, it might appear that we need
u\in\mathrm{H}^{p+1}
, to ensure that the error term is bounded, but we will see in the sequel that this is not necessary for
p=2
and it is safe to conjecture that this is the general state of affairs). The challenge is to close the gap between
p
and
2p
, specifically to prove that
u\in\mathrm{H}^{p}
, in tandem with formal order conditions, is sufficient for order
p

and
for an explicit form of the error.

Splittings of unbounded operators have been subject to significant research effort
[

,
jahnke00ebe
,
kropielnicka03fst
]
. Recently, the present authors introduced an elementary approach allowing for order analysis and the derivation of tight error bounds for three basic splittings, Lie–Trotter, parallel Lie–Trotter and Strang
[
iserles04eas
]

The splittings investigated in this paper are of the form

\mbox{\boldmath$S$\unboldmath}(t;P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}):={\mathrm{e}}^{tP_{1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{tP_{2}}\cdots{\mathrm{e}}^{tP_{n}}\approx{\mathrm{e}}^{t(P_{1}+P_{2}+\cdots+P_{n})},

(1.6)

where
\mathcal{L},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
are linear operators, which might be unbounded, and
\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{k}=\mathcal{L}
. We say that a splitting is

if

,

and each

is either a scalar multiple of
A
or a scalar multiple of
B
: the Lie–Trotter splitting (
1.3
) and the Strang splitting (
1.4
) are both canonical.

2

While a canonical splitting is defined formally also when
[A,B]=O
, in that case it bears no error, somehow missing the entire point of this paper.
In the sequel we do not require the splitting to be canonical unless otherwise stated.

The mechanism that allows to reduce regularity requirements for splittings is implicit in more general rules pertaining to commutators of differential operators. In our case
A=-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{i}}\Delta
(unbounded) and
B={\mathrm{i}}V
(bounded) – but

[A,B]=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta V+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla V)\cdot\nabla

is a bounded operator in

, while

[B,[A,B]]=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{i}}(\nabla V)^{\top}(\nabla V)

is a multiplication operator in
\mathrm{H}^{0}=\mathrm{L}_{2}
. This is just the simplest instance of a more general state of affairs
[

,
chodosh11imr
,
faou24dsp
]
. Thus, let
\mbox{\gothic F}=\mbox{\gothic F}(A,B)
be the free Lie algebra generated by the ‘letters’
A
and
B
. Given
T\in\mbox{\gothic F}
, it is composed of, say,
m\geq 0

A
s and
n\geq 0

B
s. If
n\geq m+2
then necessarily
T\equiv 0
, otherwise
Tu
is bounded for
u\in\mathrm{H}^{m+1-n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(and, if
n=m+1
is a multiplication operator). This is important because, as will be apparent in the sequel, the error expansion of a splitting (
1.6
) can be expressed using commutators in
\mbox{\gothic F}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
.

In Section 2 we consider the case
n=3
using canonical approach, expanding into Taylor series. This turns out to be surprisingly subtle. While establishing second-order conditions and writing the leading error term is in principle easy, its naive form adds little to our insight because it is seemingly not in
\mbox{\gothic F}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})
. It takes significant effort to show that actually it is composed solely of commutators. Second shortcoming of this approach is that, if any of the
P_{\ell}
s is unbounded, it tells us little about the error

\mbox{\boldmath$E$\unboldmath}(t)=\mbox{\boldmath$S$\unboldmath}(t;P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})-{\mathrm{e}}^{t(P_{1}+P_{2}+\cdots+P_{n})}

or even if it is bounded. This motivates the work of Section 3, where we employ a different approach, expanding upon the work of
[
iserles04eas
]
, which allows unboundedness while deriving second-order conditions and an upper bound of the error.

Note that
n=3
allows for order 2 at most: if we wish order 3 with a canonical splitting we need at least
n=7
. We hope to revisit more general splittings, employing the approach of Section 3, in a future paper. Note further that according to a theorem of Sheng
[
blanes24smd
]
any splitting of order greater than two must have some negative coefficients. This renders such splittings of little utility in the solution of diffusive phenomena, which are well posed only in positive direction. (The problem can be overcome by using complex-valued steps
[
hansen09hos
]
, an approach that we do not pursue in this paper.)

Classical approach

Bounding the zeros of polynomials using the Frobenius companion matrix partitioned by the Cartesian decomposition
Mohammad W. Alomari

Abstract
. In this work, some new inequalities for the numerical radius of block n n -by- n n matrices are presented. As an application, bounding of zeros of polynomials using the Frobenius companion matrix partitioned by the Cartesian decomposition method is proved and affirmed by several numerical examples showing that our approach of bounding zeros of polynomials could be very effective in comparison with the most famous and some recent results presented in the field.

Let

For a bounded linear operator
T

\mathscr{H}

W\left(T\right)
is the image
of the unit sphere of
\mathscr{H}
under the quadratic form
x\to\left\langle{Tx,x}\right\rangle

More precisely,

\displaystyle W\left(T\right)=\left\{{\left\langle{Tx,x}\right\rangle:x\in\mathscr{H},\left\|x\right\|=1}\right\}.

Also, the numerical radius is defined to be

\displaystyle w\left(T\right)=\sup\left\{{\left|\lambda\right|:\lambda\in W\left(T\right)}\right\}=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\left\|x\right\|=1}\left|{\left\langle{Tx,x}\right\rangle}\right|.

The spectral radius of an operator
T
is defined to be

\displaystyle r\left(T\right)=\sup\left\{{\left|\lambda\right|:\lambda\in\spe\left(T\right)}\right\}.

We recall that, the usual operator norm of an operator
T
is defined to be

\displaystyle\left\|T\right\|=\sup\left\{{\left\|{Tx}\right\|:x\in H,\left\|x\right\|=1}\right\},

2.
Numerical radius inequalities of
m\times m
matrix operator

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\bf{A}}=\left[A_{kj}\right]\in\oplus_{m}\mathscr{M}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
be an
m\times m
operator such that
P_{kj}+iQ_{kj}
is the corresponding Cartesian decomposition of
A_{kj}
. If
f
and
g
are nonnegative continuous functions on
\left[0,\infty\right)
satisfying
f(t)g(t)=t

(t\geq 0)
, then

(2.1)

\displaystyle w\left({\bf{A}}\right)\leq w^{1/2}\left(\left[c_{kj}\right]\right),

where

\displaystyle c_{kj}=m\cdot\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w\left({P_{kk}^{2}+Q_{kk}^{2}}\right),\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad j=k\\
\\
\frac{1}{4}\left\|{f^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+f^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)}\right\|^{2},\qquad j\neq k.\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
x=\left[{\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}{x_{1}}&{x_{2}}&\cdots&{x_{m}}\\
\end{array}}\right]^{T}\in\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathscr{M}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
with
\|x\|=1
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{2}}\left|{\left\langle{{\bf{A}}x,x}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\left|{\sum\limits_{k,j=1}^{m}{\left\langle{A_{kj}x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k,j=1}^{m}{\left|{\left\langle{A_{kj}x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}}\qquad\qquad{\rm{(by\,\,Jensen^{\prime}s\,\,inequality)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}{\left|{\left\langle{A_{kk}x_{k},x_{k}}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}}+\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{\scriptstyle j=1\hfill\atop\scriptstyle j\neq k\hfill}^{m}{\left|{\left\langle{A_{kj}x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}{\left({\left\langle{P_{kk}x_{k},x_{k}}\right\rangle^{2}+\left\langle{Q_{kk}x_{k},x_{k}}\right\rangle^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{\scriptstyle j=1\hfill\atop\scriptstyle j\neq k\hfill}^{m}{\left[\left\langle{f^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle{g^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left.{\left\langle{f^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle{g^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}{\left({\left\|{P_{kk}x_{k}}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{Q_{kk}x_{k}}\right\|^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{4m}\sum\limits_{\scriptstyle j=1\hfill\atop\scriptstyle j\neq k\hfill}^{m}\left\{\left\langle{\left[f^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)\right]x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\left[f^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)\right]x_{j},x_{k}}\right\rangle\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}{\left({\left\langle{P_{kk}^{2}x_{k},x_{k}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{Q_{kk}^{2}x_{k},x_{k}}\right\rangle}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{4m}\sum\limits_{\scriptstyle j=1\hfill\atop\scriptstyle j\neq k\hfill}^{m}\left\|{f^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+f^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)}\right\|^{2}\|x_{j}\|^{2}\|x_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}{\left\langle{\left({P_{kk}^{2}+Q_{kk}^{2}}\right)x_{k},x_{k}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{4m}\sum\limits_{\scriptstyle j=1\hfill\atop\scriptstyle j\neq k\hfill}^{m}\left\|{f^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+f^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)}\right\|^{2}\|x_{j}\|\|x_{k}\|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad({\text{since \,\,$\|x_{j}\|^{2}\leq\|x_{j}\|,\,\,\forall j$}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}{\left\|{P_{kk}^{2}+Q_{kk}^{2}}\right\|\left\|{x_{k}}\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{4m}\sum\limits_{\scriptstyle j=1\hfill\atop\scriptstyle j\neq k\hfill}^{m}\left\|{f^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{P_{kj}}\right|}\right)+f^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)+g^{2}\left({\left|{Q_{kj}}\right|}\right)}\right\|\|x_{j}\|\|x_{k}\|

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\left[{c_{kj}}\right]y,y}\right\rangle

where
y=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}{\left\|{x_{1}}\right\|}&{\left\|{x_{2}}\right\|}&\cdots&{\left\|{x_{m}}\right\|}\\
\end{array}}\right)^{T}
. Taking the supremum over
x\in\bigoplus_{m}\mathscr{M}_{n}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

3.
Applications for bounding zeros of polynomials

One of the most interesting and useful application of the numerical radius inequalities is to bound zeros of complex polynomials using a suitable partition of the well-known Frobenius companion matrix. Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle p\left(z\right)=z^{n}+a_{n}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{2}z+a_{1},

be any polynomial with
a_{1}\neq 0
. The general corresponding companion matrix is defined as:

\displaystyle C\left(p\right):=\left[{\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}{-a_{n}}&{-a_{n-1}}&\cdots&{-a_{2}}&{-a_{1}}\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\end{array}}\right].

It is well known that the eigenvalues of
C\left(p\right)
are exactly the zeros of
p\left(z\right)
, see
[
12
, p. 316]
.

Based on some numerical radius estimations of
C\left(p\right)
, several authors paid a serious attention to find various upper bounds of the zeros of
p\left(z\right)
, some famous upper bounds are listed as follow:
If
\lambda
is a zero of
p
, then

(1)

Cauchy
[
12
]
, obtained the following upper bound

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\right|\leq 1+\max\left\{\left|a_{k}\right|:k=1,2,,\cdots,n\right\}.

(2)

Carmichael and Mason
[
12
]
, provided the following estimate

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\right|\leq\sqrt{1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left|a_{k}\right|^{2}}}.

(3)

Montel
[
12
]
, proved the following estimate

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\right|\leq\max\left\{1,\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left|a_{k}\right|}\right\}.

(4)

Fujii and Kubo
[
9

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\right|\leq\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left|a_{k}\right|^{2}}\right).

(5)

Abdurakhmanov

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\left|{a_{n}}\right|+\cos\left({\frac{\pi}{n}}\right)+\sqrt{\left({\left|{a_{n}}\right|-\cos\left({\frac{\pi}{n}}\right)}\right)^{2}+\left({1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}{\left|{a_{k}}\right|^{2}}}\right)^{2}}}\right).

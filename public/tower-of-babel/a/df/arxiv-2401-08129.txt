Eigenvalue and pseudospectrum processes generated by nonnormal Toeplitz matrices with perturbation
Saori Morimoto, Makoto Katori, Tomoyuki Shirai

Abstract
Motivated by the recent study of non-Hermitian matrix-valued Brownian motions, we propose two kinds of dynamical processes of n × n n\times n matrices generated by nonnormal Toeplitz matrices. As a perturbation δ ​ J \delta J is added at time m ≤ n m\leq n , where δ > 0 \delta>0 and J J is the all-one matrix. First we report the complicated motions of the “eigenvalues” which are numerically calculated. Then we derive the equations which determine the exact eigenvalue processes. Comparison between the numerical results and the solutions of the equations, coexistence of eigenvalues and pseudospectra in the numerical results is clarified. We tried to characterize the numerical results using the symbol curves of the corresponding nonnormal Toeplitz operators without perturbation. Then we have observed new phenomenon in our second model such that at each time the outermost closed simple curve cut out from the symbol curve is realized as exact eigenvalues, but the inner part of symbol curve composed of several closed simple curves osculating each other is reduced in size and embedded as a complicated structure in the pseudospectrum. Keywords: Nonnormal Toeplitz matrices, Eigenvalue processes, Pseudospectrum processes, Symbol curves of Toeplitz operators

3
Exact Eigenvalue Processes

3.1
Model 1

For
model 1
,
we consider the
following eigenvalue-eigenvector equations,

S_{\delta J}(m)\bm{v}(m)=\lambda(m)\bm{v}(m),\quad m=1,2,\dots,n.

(3.1)

Let
{\bf 1}
be the all-one vector
and we introduce the Hermitian inner produce,

\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}\overline{v_{j}}
,

\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Define

\displaystyle\alpha(m)

\displaystyle:=\langle\bm{v}(m),{\bf 1}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}(m).

(3.2)

Let
1_{(\omega)}
be the indicator function
of the condition
\omega
;

1_{(\omega)}=1
if
\omega
is satisfied,
and
1_{(\omega)}=0
otherwise.
The following fact will be used.

Lemma 3.1

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\langle S^{\ell}{\bf 1},{\bf 1}\rangle=(n-\ell)1_{(1\leq\ell\leq n-1)}.

For
m=1,2,\dots,n
,
let

p_{1}=\left[\frac{n-1}{m}\right],

(3.3)

where
[x]
denotes the greatest integer
less than or equal to
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
The following is proved.

Theorem 3.2

For
m=1,2,\dots,n
,
there are
p_{1}+1
non-zero eigenvalues, which solve
the following equation,

\frac{1}{n\delta}z^{p_{1}+1}-\frac{1-z^{p_{1}+1}}{1-z}+\frac{m}{n}\frac{1}{1-z}\left\{p_{1}+1-\frac{1-z^{p_{1}+1}}{1-z}\right\}=0.

(3.4)

This equation is also written as

\frac{1}{n\delta}z^{p_{1}+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}\left\{1-(p_{1}-k)\frac{m}{n}\right\}z^{k}=0.

(3.5)

The corresponding eigenvectors satisfy
\alpha(m)\not=0
.
All other
n-p_{1}-1
eigenvalues degenerate at zero.
In this case,
the corresponding eigenvectors satisfy

\alpha(m)=0
, that is, they are orthogonal to
{\bf 1}
.

Proof

Equation (
3.1
) is written as

\displaystyle(zI-S^{m})\bm{v}(m)

\displaystyle=\delta\alpha(m){\bf 1},

where we noticed the equality,

J\bm{v}(m)=\alpha(m){\bf 1}.

(3.6)

We solve this equation as

\displaystyle\bm{v}(m)

\displaystyle=\delta\alpha(m)(zI-S^{m})^{-1}{\bf 1}=\delta\alpha(m)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{-(k+1)}S^{mk}{\bf 1},

where we used the expansion formula of an inverse matrix.
By taking inner products with
{\bf 1}
on both sides, we have

\langle\bm{v}(m),{\bf 1}\rangle=\delta\alpha(m)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{-(k+1)}\langle S^{mk}{\bf 1},{\bf 1}\rangle.

Since

mk\leq n-1,\quad m,k\in\mathbb{N}\iff k\leq p_{1},k\in\mathbb{N},

where
p_{1}
is defined by (
3.3
),
Lemma
3.1

gives

\displaystyle\alpha(m)=\delta\alpha(m)\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}z^{-(k+1)}(n-mk).

(3.7)

Here the fact
\langle\bm{v}(m),{\bf 1}\rangle=\alpha(m)
was used.
Assume that
\alpha(m)\neq 0
. Hence we have

\displaystyle 1=\delta\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}z^{-(k+1)}(n-mk)\iff\frac{1}{n\delta}-z^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}z^{-k}+\frac{m}{n}z^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}kz^{-k}=0.

(3.8)

Then we use the summation formulas,

\displaystyle z^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}z^{-k}

\displaystyle=z^{-1}\frac{1-z^{-(p_{1}+1)}}{1-z^{-1}}=z^{-(p_{1}+1)}\frac{1-z^{p_{1}+1}}{1-z},

\displaystyle z^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}kz^{-k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\sum_{k=0}^{p_{1}}z^{-k}=\frac{z^{-(p_{1}+1)}}{1-z}\left\{p_{1}+1-\frac{1-z^{p_{1}+1}}{1-z}\right\}.

(3.9)

Thus (
3.8
) is written as

\frac{1}{n\delta}-z^{-(p_{1}+1)}\frac{1-z^{p_{1}+1}}{1-z}+\frac{m}{n}\frac{z^{-(p_{1}+1)}}{1-z}\left\{p_{1}+1-\frac{1-z^{p_{1}+1}}{1-z}\right\}=0.

By multiplying
z^{p_{1}+1}
,
(
3.4
)
is obtained.
Using the formulas (
3.9
),
(
3.5
) is readily
derived from (
3.4
).
∎

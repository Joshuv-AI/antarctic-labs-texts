Self-consistent tight-binding calculations with extended Hubbard interactions in rhombohedral multilayer graphene
Dongkyu Lee, Wooil Yang, Young-Woo Son, Jeil Jung

Abstract
We study the mean-field broken symmetry phases of charge neutral multilayer rhombohedral graphene within tight-binding approximations including self-consistent extended Hubbard interactions. We used on-site and inter-site Hubbard interactions obtained from a newly developed first-principles calculation method. Our calculations for systems up to eight layers give rise to electron-hole asymmetries, band flatness, band gaps, and layer anti-ferromagnetic ground states in keeping with available experiments. By including the intersite Hubbard interactions up to the next-nearest neighboring sites, the band gaps are shown to open when the number of layers is larger than three, while the trilayer system maintains its metallic nature with two low energy density of state peaks near the Fermi energy whose separation increases with the range of inter-site Hubbard parameters. Within our framework, the calculated band gaps reflect mean-field ground states with extended Hubbard interactions, in closer agreement with experimental estimates. The tight-binding formulation further enables efficient treatment of large rhombohedral chiral systems, including twisted multilayer graphene.

The procedure is as follows. We start with the DFT+

We have carried out valley, spin, and inversion symmetry preserving self-consistent DFT+

+

Then, this symmetry preserving ground-state of

The Kohn-Sham (KS) equations of a DFT+
U
+
V

\hat{H}_{0}
of our self-consistent TB+

+
V

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\sigma}h^{\rm KS}_{ij}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{j\sigma}=\sum_{ij\sigma}\epsilon^{UV}_{i}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{i\sigma}+\sum_{ij\sigma}t^{UV}_{ij}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{j\sigma},

(1)

where
\epsilon^{UV}_{i}
is the onsite potential of the
i
-th site and
t^{UV}_{ij}
the hopping parameters
between
i
- and

For simplicity, all our calculations have started from the non-magnetic spinless form of

and use abbreviated notations for the density matrix

The

and

U
+
V

\displaystyle\epsilon^{UV}_{i\sigma}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\text{NI}}_{i\sigma}+\sum_{j\neq i}V^{H}_{ij}\rho^{0}_{j}+v_{xc,\,i\sigma}+U_{i}\rho^{0}_{i\tilde{\sigma}}~,

(2)

\displaystyle t^{UV}_{ij\sigma}

\displaystyle=t^{\text{NI}}_{ij\sigma}-V_{ij}\rho^{0}_{ij\sigma},

(3)

where the

density matrix elements corresponds to the DFT+
U
+
V

1

The second term on the right hand side of

Our self-consistent TB+

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{{\rm TB}0}[\rho_{0}]=

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{0}-\hat{H}^{UV}[\rho_{0}],

\displaystyle\sum_{i\sigma}(U_{i}\rho_{i\tilde{\sigma}}+\sum_{j\neq i}V^{H}_{ij}\rho_{j})\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{i\sigma}

\displaystyle-\sum_{ij\sigma}V_{ij}\rho_{ij\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{j\sigma}.~

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the TB+
U
+
V
model, a self-consistent tight-binding calculation framework to efficiently obtain DFT+
U
+
V
results using
U
and
V
previously obtained from
ab initio
calculations.
Our self-consistent TB+
U
+
V
calculation for the broken-symmetry solutions uses as the reference non-interacting tight-binding band Hamiltonian the corresponding one for DFT+
U
+
V
with unbroken spin symmetry, where we subtract the
U
+
V
contribution.
The increased efficiency of TB+
U
+
V
has allowed the calculation of the ground states of RnG structures drastically reducing the computation time that would be required in a direct DFT+
U
+
V
calculation.
We have applied our model to charge neutral RnG (
n=1,2,\dotsb,8
) systems to obtain the extended Hubbard interaction-driven broken symmetry electronic structures.
We find semimetallic phases for
n\leq 3
and LAF gapped phases for
n\geq 4
, in excellent agreement with experiments of RnG on hBN susbstrates for the band gap magnitudes and the transition temperatures to the metallic phase.
The results in this work are closer to experiments of RnG systems on substrates than previous calculations in the literature in particular for the reported band gap magnitudes
[
45
,
72
,
67
]
.
It is expected that similar TB+
U
+
V
approaches can be applied for other 2D materials in situations where more efficient computation is required, for instance in typical moiré materials with large supercells.

Appendix A
Self-consistent tight-binding Extended Hubbard model

The Hamiltonian corresponding to the DFT+
U
+
V
can be cast into the tight-binding Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{0}
in
Eq.

1
. This Hamiltonian serves as a reference point for the self-consistent TB+
U
+
V
calculation.
Here, we provide additional details for the derivation of the onsite and intersite hopping terms
\epsilon^{UV}
and
t^{UV}
of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{0}
stemming from the
DFT+
U
+
V
equations presented in Ref.
[
66
]
.

We use the localized orbital basis
\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r})
at atomic
i
sites for our tight-binding model. Hence, the density operators

\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{r})=\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\hat{\psi}(\mathbf{r})

such that the field operators

\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}

and
\hat{\psi}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{j}\phi_{j}(\mathbf{r})\hat{c}_{j}

are defined in terms of atomic orbital creation and annihilation operators.

The Kohn-Sham (KS) DFT+
U
+
V
Hamiltonian

\hat{H}^{\text{DFT}+U+V}=\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}^{\text{KS}}\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{j}

(8)

consists of matrix elements

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{\text{KS}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}r\,\phi_{i}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+V^{\text{DFT}}[\rho](\mathbf{r})+V^{UV}[\rho](\mathbf{r})\right]\phi_{j}(\mathbf{r})

where the kinetic term gives rise to the DFT interatomic hopping terms, and the KS potential is

\displaystyle V^{\text{DFT}}[\rho]({\bf r})

\displaystyle=V_{\rm ext}(\mathbf{r})+V^{\text{H}}[\rho](\mathbf{r})+v_{\text{xc}}[\rho](\mathbf{r}).

(9)

Apart from the standard KS terms a widely used way to deal with the double-counting corrected form of the
U
+
V
potential
[
90
]
is the so-called ‘fully localized limit’ (FLL). In the restriction for the single
p_{z}
orbitals at the carbon atoms

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{UV}=

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\text{Hub}}-\hat{V}^{\text{dc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\sigma}\frac{U_{i}}{2}\left(1-2\rho^{\sigma}_{ii}\right)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{i\sigma}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle,\sigma}V_{ij}\rho^{\sigma}_{ij}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{j\sigma},

where
\langle i,j\rangle
represents sums over all possible unequal sites within a given truncation range.
The inter-site interactions
V_{ij}
are assumed to be the same as the
Coulomb interaction averaged over all the states, just one orbital per atomic site in our case, such that

V_{ij}=\langle\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\mid V_{ee}\mid\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\rangle.
The same consideration applies when incorporating the Hubbard correction into hopping parameters obtained from DFT
[
91
]
.

In contrast, in this work, we employ hopping parameters obtained from DFT+U+V calculations rather than those obtained from the conventional DFT. By utilizing the parameters from which the double-counting interaction contained in
V^{\text{DFT}}
has been eliminated, we ensure that only the Hubbard-model-like potential term
V^{\text{Hub}}
corresponding to the variations in the density matrix need to be considered in subsequent tight-binding calculations. The DFT+U+V potential can be reformulated by decomposing it into a density-independent non-interacting part
\hat{V}^{\text{NI}}
, an onsite interaction
\hat{V}^{\text{on}}
, and an intersite interaction
\hat{V}^{\text{inter}}
.

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\text{DFT}+U+V}[\rho]=

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\text{DFT}}[\rho]+\hat{V}^{\text{Hub}}[\rho]-\hat{V}^{\text{dc}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\text{NI}}+\hat{V}^{\text{on}}[\rho]+\hat{V}^{\text{inter}}[\rho]

In DFT+U+V, the onsite component of
V^{\text{DFT}}
is effectively removed by the onsite
V^{\text{dc}}
, such that the remaining onsite term
V^{\text{on}}
corresponds to the Hubbard U term of the Hubbard potential
V^{\text{Hub}}
. Similarly, since the intersite
V^{\text{dc}}
corresponds to the Hartree part of
V^{\text{Hub}}
, the total intersite interaction can be expressed as the interaction part of
V^{\text{DFT}}
, equally
V^{\text{H}}+v_{xc}
, supplemented by the Fock (exchange) part of
V^{\text{Hub}}
:

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\text{on}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}U_{i}\rho^{\tilde{\sigma}}_{i}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{i\sigma}

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{\text{inter}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=(\sum_{ij}V^{H}_{ij}\rho_{j}+v_{xc,i\sigma})\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{i\sigma}-\sum_{ij\sigma}V_{ij}\rho^{\sigma}_{ij}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\hat{c}_{j\sigma}

Based on this, the tight-binding parameters from the DFT+U+V can be derived as follows:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{UV}_{i\sigma}:=

\displaystyle h^{\text{KS}}_{ii\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\text{NI}}_{i\sigma}+U_{i}\rho_{i\tilde{\sigma}}+\sum_{j}V^{\rm H}_{ij}\rho_{j}+v_{xc,i\sigma},

(10)

\displaystyle t^{UV}_{ij\sigma}:=

\displaystyle h^{\text{KS}}_{ij\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{\text{NI}}_{ij\sigma}-V_{ij}\rho_{ij\sigma},

(11)

where
\epsilon^{\text{NI}}
and
t^{\text{NI}}
represent the non-interacting parts of the onsite and hopping parameters, respectively. These are obtained from the kinetic term and the non-interacting potential
V^{\rm NI}
.

The

truncation
[

,

]
determines the hopping model to preserve the zeroth and first-order
\vec{k}\cdot\vec{p}
expansion coefficients in the vicinity of the Dirac cone. For a truncation order

t

where
\alpha

\beta

(c^{f}_{1},c^{f}_{2},\dots)=(-1,+2,+1,-5,-4,+7,\dots).

(13)

\alpha\beta=A_{1}A_{1},A_{1}B_{3},\dots
, they fall under the
G
category. For the cases, the coefficients are

(c^{g}_{0},c^{g}_{1},\dots)=(1,-3,+6,-3,-6,+6,\dots).

(14)

The
t_{0}
term in the
G
category represents the onsite potential for
\alpha=\beta
, while for
\alpha\neq\beta
, it denotes the perpendicular hopping.

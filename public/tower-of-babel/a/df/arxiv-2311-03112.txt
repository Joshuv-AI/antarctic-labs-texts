Recursive construction for expansions of tree Yang-Mills amplitudes from soft theorem
Chang Hu ab , Kang Zhou c

Abstract
In this paper, we have introduced a fundamentally different approach, based on a bottom-up methodology, to expand tree-level Yang-Mills (YM) amplitudes into Yang-Mills-scalar (YMS) amplitudes and Bi-adjoint-scalar (BAS) amplitudes. Our method relies solely on the intrinsic soft behavior of external gluons, eliminating the need for external aids such as Feynman rules or CHY rules. The recursive procedure consistently preserves explicit gauge invariance at every step, ultimately resulting in a manifest gauge-invariant outcome when the initial expression is already framed in a gauge-invariant manner. The resulting expansion can be directly analogized to the expansions of gravitational (GR) amplitudes using the double copy structure. When combined with the expansions of Einstein-Yang-Mills amplitudes obtained using the covariant double copy method from existing literature, the expansions presented in this note yield gauge-invariant BCJ numerators.

II.3
Recursive expansion of single-trace YMS amplitudes

The discussion for the expansion of tree level amplitudes in the previous subsection.

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(1,\cdots,n;\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\}|\sigma_{n+m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\,\big(\epsilon_{p}\cdot F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\cdot Y_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\big)\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(1,\{2,\cdots,n-1\}\shuffle\{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}},p\},n;\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\}\setminus\{p\cup\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}\}|\sigma_{n+m})\,,~~

\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\}
, and
\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}
are subsets of
\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\}\setminus p

\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\}\setminus p

\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}
is generated from
\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}

\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}

\displaystyle F^{\mu\nu}_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\equiv\big(f_{{\alpha}_{k}}\cdot f_{{\alpha}_{k-1}}\cdots f_{{\alpha}_{2}}\cdot f_{{\alpha}_{1}}\big)^{\mu\nu}\,,

Furthermore, we can also observe that in the recursive expansion (

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\,{k_{r}\cdot F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\cdot Y_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\over k_{r}\cdot k_{p_{1}\cdots p_{m}}}\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(1,\{2,\cdots,n-1\}\shuffle\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}},n;\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\}\setminus\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}|\sigma_{n+m})\,,~~

represents subsets of
{p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}}

III
Expand YM amplitudes to YMS ones

In this section, we derive the color-ordered Yang-Mills (YM) amplitudes as part of the formula for expanding YM amplitudes into Yang-Mills-scalar (YMS) amplitudes. The technique employed in this section is based on the analysis of the sub-leading-order soft behavior. It represents an improvement over the method utilized in
Zhou:2022orv
. In comparison with the previous method from
Zhou:2022orv
, the new approach allows us to construct the expanded formula recursively, starting from the lowest
3
-point amplitudes that can be uniquely determined through bootstrapping. This is achieved without the need to rely on other frameworks, such as Feynman rules or CHY formalism. However, it should be noted that the resulting expansion in this section does not exhibit gauge invariance for all polarizations. The manifestation of gauge invariance is the objective of the next section.

III.1

To ensure our construction is self-contained, without relying on Feynman rules or the CHY formula, we fix the 3-point color-ordered Yang-Mills (YM) amplitudes using a bootstrapping method. Our construction is primarily based on the following ansatz:

We can expand

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{1}\big)\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(1,3;2|1,2,3)

Then the double copy structure indicates the expansion for general

is an arbitrary ordering among legs in

V
Summery

In this paper, we have enhanced the recursive method based on the sub-leading soft theorem for external gluons, as previously employed in
Zhou:2022orv
. With our new approach, we have developed two types of expansions from YM amplitudes to YMS amplitudes. The first type does not exhibit manifest gauge invariance for each polarization, while the second type does. As detailed in Section
I
and Section
II
, the sub-leading soft theorem for gluons effectively ”grows” a soft gluon in a manifestly gauge-invariant manner. Consequently, as long as our recursive starting point is manifestly gauge-invariant, any intermediate result obtained during the recursion process remains explicitly gauge-invariant.

Classification of simple 0-dimensional isolated complete intersection singularities
Thuy Huong Pham, Gerhard Pfister, Gert-Martin Greuel

Abstract




Abstract

The aim of this article is the classification of simple 0-dimensional isolated complete intersection singularities in positive characteristic. As usual, a singularity is called simple or 0-modal if there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of singularities into which the given singularity can deform. The notion of simpleness was introduced by V. I. Arnold and the classification of low modality singularities has become a fundamental task in singularity theory. Simple complex analytic isolated complete intersection singularities (ICIS) were classified by M. Giusti. However, the classification in positive characteristic requires different methods and is much more involved. The final result is nevertheless similar to the classification in characteristic 0 with some additional normal forms in low characteristic.

The theoretical results in this paper mainly concern families of ICIS that are formal in the fiber and algebraic in the base (formal deformation theory is not sufficient). In particular, we give a definition of modality in this situation and prove its semicontinuity.

A criterion for non-simpleness

Definition 1.5

Lemma 1.6
.

Let

and

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
f\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{m}\cap I_{m,n}

Note that if

is quasi-homogeneous of type

then for all

Since

we have

Proposition 1.8
.

Proposition 1.14
.

(1) An ICIS is simple iff it is 0-modular.

(2) The

(2) It follows from Definition

The following result is the main general condition for a 0-dimensional ICIS being not simple.

Theorem 1.15
.

f=(f_{1},...,f_{n})\in I_{n,n}

\order(f)\geq 3

Assume by contradiction that
f
is simple. Since

is 0-modular, there is a Zariski neighborhood

of

in

T\geq 4
.

2.2.2.1)
b_{2}\neq 0
: by the proof of Proposition
2.5
,
g\sim H_{r+3}=(x^{2}+y^{r},xy^{2})
for some
r\geq 3
.

2.2.2.2)
b_{2}=0
: Set
Q=\min\{j\mid b_{j}\neq 0\}
. Then
3\leq Q\leq q.

2.2.2.2.1)
T\leq Q
: by Lemma
2.3
,
g\sim I^{0}_{2T-1}=(x^{2}+y^{3},y^{T})
.

2.2.2.2.2)
T=Q+1
: by the proof of Proposition
2.5
,
g\sim I^{0}_{2T-1}
if
p\nmid T
, and
g\sim(x^{2}+y^{3},x^{T}+xy^{T-1})=I^{1}_{2T-1}
if
p\mid T.

2.2.2.2.3)
T\geq Q+3
: by the proof of Proposition
2.5
,
g\sim I^{0}_{2Q+2}
.

2.2.2.2.4)
T=Q+2
: by the proof of Proposition
2.5
,
g\sim I^{0}_{2Q+2}=(x^{2}+y^{3},xy^{Q})
if
p\nmid 2Q+3
, and
g\sim I^{1}_{2Q+2}=(x^{2}+y^{3},xy^{Q}+y^{Q+2})
if
p\mid 2Q+3
.
∎

3
Simple isolated complete intersection singularities of
I_{2,2}
in characteristic 2

In this section, we assume that the field
K
has characteristic
p=2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in I_{2,2}
be such that
\order(f)=2
. Then either there is a
g_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
such that

f\sim(xy,g_{2})

or there are
h_{1}\in\mathfrak{m}^{3}
and
g_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
such that

f\sim(x^{2}+h_{1},g_{2}).

If
g_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}^{3}
then
f
is not simple.

Proof.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
. We may assume that
\order(f_{1})=2
. By Lemma
1.18
, either there is
\phi\in Aut(R)
such that
\phi(f_{1})=xy
or there is
\varphi\in Aut(R)
such that
\varphi(f_{1})=x^{2}+h_{1}
for some
h_{1}\in\mathfrak{m}^{3}
. Therefore, either
f\sim(xy,\phi(f_{2}))
or
f\sim(x^{2}+h_{1},\varphi(f_{2}))
.
Proposition
2.4
implies that
f
is not simple if
g_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}^{3}
.
This proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
g=(xy,g_{2})\in I_{2,2}
, where
g_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
. Then there are
m,n\geq 2
such that
g\sim(xy,x^{n}+y^{m})=F^{n,m}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to a part of the proof of Proposition
2.1
, which does not depend on characteristic.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
g=(x^{2}+h_{1},g_{2})\in I_{2,2}
, where
h_{1}\in\mathfrak{m}^{3}
and
j_{2}(g_{2})=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}\neq 0
, where
a,b,c\in K
.

a)

If
b=0
and
c\neq 0
then
g\sim(x^{2},y^{2})
.

b)

If
b\neq 0
and
c\neq 0
then
g\sim(x^{2},xy+y^{2})
.

Proof.

The proposed normal forms are of quasi-homogeneous type
({\bf d};{\bf a})=(2,2;1,1)
.

a) If
b=0
and
c\neq 0

then
g\sim(x^{2}+h_{1},y^{2}+h_{2})
,
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}^{3}
. Setting

f=(x^{2},y^{2}),\enskip h=(h_{1},h_{2}),

we have
v_{{\bf d},{\bf a}}(h)\geq 1
and

T^{1}(f)=R^{2}/\langle x^{2},y^{2}\rangle R^{2}
.
It is easy to check
T^{1}_{\nu}(f)=0
for all
\nu\geq 1
(see below). This implies

\sup\{i\mid T^{1}_{i}(H)\neq 0\}\leq 0<v_{{\bf d},{\bf a}}(h)
and by
Proposition
1.8
we deduce
f\sim f+h\sim g
.

b) If
b\neq 0
and
c\neq 0
then

g\sim(x^{2}+h_{1},xy+y^{2}+h_{2})
,
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}^{3}
. Setting

f=(x^{2},xy+y^{2}),\enskip h=(h_{1},h_{2}).

we have
v_{{\bf d},{\bf a}}(h)\geq 1
. Moreover,

T^{1}(f)=R^{2}/D,

with

D:=\langle(0,y),(0,x)\rangle+\langle x^{2},xy+y^{2}\rangle R^{2}.

Then
(x^{2},0)
,
(xy^{2},0)
,
(y^{3},0)
,
(0,xy^{2})
,
(0,y^{3})\in D.

We prove that for
\nu\geq 1

\left(\sum\limits_{i+j=2+\nu}c_{ij}x^{i}y^{j},\sum\limits_{h+k=2+\nu}d_{hk}x^{h}y^{k}\right)\in D.

Let
(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
be such that
i+j=2+\nu.
If
i\geq 2
then
(x^{i}y^{j},0)\in D
. If
i=1
then
j\geq 2
so that
(xy^{j},0)\in D
. If
i=0
then
j\geq 3
so that we get
(y^{j},0)\in D
. Similarly, let
(h,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
be such that
h+k=2+\nu.
If
h\geq 1

then
(0,x^{h}y^{k})\in D
. If
h=0
then
k\geq 3
so that we get
(0,y^{k})\in D.

This implies

T^{1}_{\nu}(f)=0
for all
\nu\geq 1
. By Proposition
1.8
,
f\sim f+h\sim g.
∎

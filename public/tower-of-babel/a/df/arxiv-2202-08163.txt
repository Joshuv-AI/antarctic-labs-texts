Abstract
. In this paper, we study graphon mean field games using a system of forward-backward stochastic differential equations. We establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions under two different assumptions and prove the stability with respect to the interacting graphons which are necessary to show propagation of chaos results. As an application of propagation of chaos, we prove the convergence of n n -player game Nash equilibrium for a general model, which is new in the theory of graphon mean field games.

More specially, we investigate a coupled system of
n
FBSDEs of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX_{t}^{i,n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\hat{B}(t,X_{t}^{i,n},X_{t}^{j,n},Y_{t}^{i,n})\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t}^{i/n}\\
dY_{t}^{i,n}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\hat{F}(t,X_{t}^{i,n},X_{t}^{j,n},Y_{t}^{i,n})\,dt+\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{t}^{i,j,n}\,dW_{t}^{j/n},\\
X_{0}^{i,n}=\xi^{i/n},\\
Y_{T}^{i,n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\hat{Q}(X_{T}^{i,n},X_{T}^{j,n}),\quad\quad i=1,\dotso,n,\end{cases}

where
\hat{B},\hat{F},\hat{Q}
are drifts and terminal,
(\xi^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}
,
(W^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}
are independent initial positions and Brownian motions respectively, and
G_{n}
is a sequence of grahpons characterizing interactions between
(X^{i,n})_{i=1,\dotso,n}
. As
n\to\infty
and
G_{n}\to G
, we show the propagation of chaos result that the above particle system converges to the following graphon interacting particle system

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX^{\lambda}_{t}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\lambda,\kappa)\hat{B}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},x,Y^{\lambda}_{t}\right)\mathcal{L}(X_{t}^{\kappa})(dx)\,d\kappa\,dt+\sigma\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
dY^{\lambda}_{t}=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\lambda,\kappa)\hat{F}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},x,Y^{\lambda}_{t}\right)\mathcal{L}(X_{t}^{\kappa})(dx)\,d\kappa\,dt+Z^{\lambda}_{t}\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
X^{\lambda}_{0}=\xi^{\lambda},\\
Y^{\lambda}_{T}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\lambda,\kappa)\hat{Q}(X_{T}^{\lambda},x)\,\mathcal{L}(X_{T}^{\kappa})(dx)\,d\kappa,\quad\quad\lambda\in[0,1],\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{L}(X^{\kappa}_{t})
denotes the law of
X^{\kappa}_{t}
. By the stochastic maximum principle, the solution of the graphon mean field game can be characterized using (
1.2
). One can conclude the convergence of
n
player game using the convergence
\eqref{eq:intro1}\Rightarrow\eqref{eq:intro2}
.

To carry out our analysis, first we study the existence and uniqueness of the limiting system (
1.2
). To properly define the interaction term

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\lambda,\kappa)\hat{B}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},x,Y^{\lambda}_{t}\right)\mathcal{L}(X_{t}^{\kappa})(dx)\,d\kappa\,dt,

we must show the measurability of
\lambda\to\mathcal{L}(X^{\lambda}_{t})
. Since we are only interested in the marginals
\mathcal{L}(X^{\lambda}_{t})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}
instead of the joint law
\mathcal{L}(X_{t})
, one can study another FBSDE system where all the
(X^{\lambda},Y^{\lambda})
are driven by the same Brownian motion. The marginal laws of these two systems are the same which is a useful observation in establishing measurability. Then under two commonly used monotonicity assumptions as in
[
10
]
, we show the existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
1.2

G
. As a natural notion of distance between two different graphons, the cut norm is widely used and is weaker than the
L^{1}
-norm; see e.g.
[
38
]
. To make an estimation involving the cut norm, we adopt the argument of
[
5

where the boundedness of solutions in the

norm for

(L^{p})_{p\geq 2}
spaces. Finally, we prove the propagation of chaos for FBSDEs, where the stability is essential. Assuming that the cut norm convergence of the interacting graphons
\lVert G_{n}-G\rVert_{\square}\to 0
, we show that
\eqref{eq:intro1}\Rightarrow\eqref{eq:intro2}
. Under a stronger condition that
G_{n}
is the uniform block sampling of

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove the unique existence of solutions of (
2.1

G

G

Notation

Denote by

and

\displaystyle PF^{p}:=\{\boldsymbol{\mu}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{C}\left([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})\right):\,\lambda\mapsto\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}\text{ is measurable}\}.

For any

For a family of random variables

We define spaces of processes and random variables

•

- progressively measurable real-valued process

•

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
- progressively measurable real-valued continuous process

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|^{p}\right]<+\infty
.

•

L^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}
to be the set of all
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable
p
-th integrable random variables.

•

\mathcal{M}L^{p,2}_{\mathcal{F}}
to be the set of all measurable functions
X
from
[0,1]
to

such that

For any

\displaystyle\lVert x\rVert^{I,p}_{k}:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,1]}\int_{0}^{T}e^{kt}\left(x^{\lambda}_{t}\right)^{p}\,dt\,d\lambda\right],

\displaystyle\lVert x\rVert^{p}_{k}:=

2.
Unique existence of solutions

In this section, we show the existence and uniqueness of solutions to a general FBSDE system (
2.1
) under two assumptions.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX^{\lambda}_{t}={B}_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},\mathcal{L}^{m}(X_{t}),Y^{\lambda}_{t}\right)\,dt+\sigma\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
dY^{\lambda}_{t}=-F_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},\mathcal{L}^{m}(X_{t}),Y^{\lambda}_{t}\right)dt+Z^{\lambda}_{t}\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
X^{\lambda}_{0}=\xi^{\lambda},\\
Y^{\lambda}_{T}=Q_{G}^{\lambda}\left(X^{\lambda}_{T},\mathcal{L}^{m}(X_{T})\right),\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,1].\end{cases}

The proof is very similar to that of McKean Vlasov FBSDE, except the verification of measurability of
\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{L}(X^{\lambda})
. Our main observation is that different types of players (
X^{\lambda})
are only interacting through their marginal laws
\mathcal{L}^{m}(X)
, and therefore by the weak uniqueness of FBSDE solutions, we can treat all processes
(X^{\lambda},Y^{\lambda},Z^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}
on one stochastic basis. Then we prove a stronger statement that
\lambda\mapsto X^{\lambda}
is measurable in the
L^{2}
sense, which implies the measurability of
\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{L}(X^{\lambda})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A family of processes
(X^{\lambda},Y^{\lambda},Z^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}
is said to be a solution of (
2.1
) if
\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{L}(X^{\lambda})
is measurable and
(X^{\lambda},Y^{\lambda},Z^{\lambda})
satisfies the FBSDE system (
2.1
) for each
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

For simplicity of notation, we suppress
G

.

(i)

is Lipschitz in

\displaystyle(x-x^{\prime})\cdot\left(B^{\lambda}(t,x,\boldsymbol{\eta},y)-B^{\lambda}(t,x^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\eta},y)\right)\leq-K_{1}(x-x^{\prime})^{2}

(ii)

is Lipschitz in

,

\displaystyle(y-y^{\prime})\cdot\left(F^{\lambda}(t,x,\boldsymbol{\eta},y)-F^{\lambda}(t,x,\boldsymbol{\eta},y^{\prime})\right)\leq-K_{2}(y-y^{\prime})^{2}

Lemma 2.1
.

Under Assumption
2.1

2.2
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between solutions to (

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX^{\lambda}_{t}=B_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},\mathcal{L}^{m}(X_{t}),Y^{\lambda}_{t}\right)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t},\\

2.1.
Contraction mapping

We will prove there exists a unique solution to (
2.3

2.1
with a constant
p\geq 2

Given any measurable
y\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}

\Psi(y):=x
as the unique solution to

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dx^{\lambda}_{t}=B^{\lambda}(t,x^{\lambda}_{t},\mathcal{L}^{m}(x_{t}),y^{\lambda}_{t})\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t},\\
x^{\lambda}_{0}=\xi^{\lambda},\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,1].\end{cases}

For any
x\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}
, define
\Phi(x):=y
to be the unique solution to backward stochastic equations

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dy^{\lambda}_{t}=-F^{\lambda}(t,x^{\lambda}_{t},\mathcal{L}^{m}(x_{t}),y^{\lambda}_{t})\,dt+z^{\lambda}_{t}\,dW_{t},\\
y^{\lambda}_{T}=Q^{\lambda}(x^{\lambda}_{T},\mathcal{L}^{m}(x_{T})),\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,1].\end{cases}

Lemma 2.2
.

For
y\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}
, there exists a unique solution
x
to (
2.5
) such that
\lambda\to x^{\lambda}
is measurable, and for

Proof.

The proof is based on Picard iterations.

Step 1:
For any
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in PF^{p}
, the following stochastic differential equations can be solved

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dx^{\lambda}_{t}=B^{\lambda}(t,x^{\lambda}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t},y^{\lambda}_{t})\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t},\\
x^{\lambda}_{0}=\xi^{\lambda},\end{cases}

via Picard iteration. Take
\widetilde{x}\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}
, and define

\displaystyle\overline{x}^{\lambda}_{t}=\xi^{\lambda}+\int_{0}^{t}B^{\lambda}(s,\widetilde{x}^{\lambda}_{s},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t},y_{t}^{\lambda})\,ds+\sigma W_{t}.

As a result of Lemma
A.4
and standard estimations of SDE, it is clear that

Take
\widetilde{x}\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\mathcal{L}^{m}(\widetilde{x})
into (
2.7

Step 2:
Take any
\widetilde{y}\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}
, denote
f^{\lambda}(t):=F^{\lambda}(t,x^{\lambda}_{t},\mathcal{L}^{m}(x_{t}),\widetilde{y}^{\lambda}_{t}),\,\forall\lambda\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\overline{y}^{\lambda}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{\lambda}(x^{\lambda}_{T},\mathcal{L}^{m}(x_{T}))-\int_{t}^{T}f^{\lambda}(s)\,ds\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

By a modification of
[
17

, it can be easily shown that
\widetilde{y}\mapsto\overline{y}
is a contraction under the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{p}_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha

2.6
). For the measurability of
\lambda\to\overline{y}^{\lambda}\in L^{2,c}_{\mathcal{F}}

\lambda\to\overline{y}^{\lambda}_{t}\in L^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{ks}ds\int_{[0,1]}\left(F^{\lambda}_{G}(s,x^{\lambda}_{s},\mathcal{L}^{m}(x_{s}),Y^{\lambda}_{s})-F^{\lambda}_{\widetilde{G}}(s,x^{\lambda}_{s},\mathcal{L}^{m}(x_{s}),Y^{\lambda}_{s})\right)^{2}d\lambda\right]

In step 3, we can replace (

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}\left(x_{s}^{i,n}-\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n}\right)\cdot\left(B_{0}(s,x_{s}^{i,n},y_{s}^{i,n})-B_{0}(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},y_{s}^{i,n})\right)ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}\left(x_{s}^{i,n}-\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{B}(s,x_{s}^{i,n},x_{s}^{j,n},y_{s}^{i,n})-\hat{B}(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n},y_{s}^{i,n})\right)ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}\left(x_{s}^{i,n}-\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{B}(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n},y_{s}^{i,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{B}\left(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},x,y_{s}^{i,n}\right)\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n})(dx)\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq(k-2K_{1}+3L_{1}+\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}|x^{i,n}_{s}-\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s}|^{2}\,ds+L_{1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}|x^{j,n}_{s}-\widetilde{x}^{j,n}_{s}|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{B}(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n},y_{s}^{i,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{B}\left(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},x,y_{s}^{i,n}\right)\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n})(dx)\right)\right)^{2}ds.

Taking expectations and the average over
i
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{kt}|x_{t}^{i,n}-\tilde{x}_{t}^{i,n}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(k-2K_{1}+4L_{1}+\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{ks}|x^{i,n}_{s}-\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s}|^{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{B}(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n},y_{s}^{i,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{B}\left(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},x,y_{s}^{i,n}\right)\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n})(dx)\right)\right)^{2}ds.

For the last line, we have the estimation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{B}(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n},y_{s}^{i,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{B}\left(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},x,y_{s}^{i,n}\right)\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n})(dx)\right)\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}^{2}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\mathbb{E}\left(\hat{B}(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n},y_{s}^{i,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{B}\left(s,\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n},x,y_{s}^{i,n}\right)\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n})(dx)\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n},

due to the boundedness of
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|\tilde{x}_{s}^{i,n}|^{2}]
and Lipschitz property of
\hat{B}
.
Therefore

(4.7)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{kt}|x_{t}^{i,n}-\tilde{x}_{t}^{i,n}|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{n}.

\displaystyle-2\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{ks}\left(y^{i,n}_{s}-\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{F}(s,x^{i,n}_{s},x^{j,n}_{s},y^{i,n}_{s})-\hat{F}(s,\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s},\widetilde{x}^{j,n}_{s},\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s})\right)ds

\displaystyle-2\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{ks}\left(y^{i,n}_{s}-\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{F}(s,\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s},\widetilde{x}^{j,n}_{s},\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{F}(s,\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s},x,\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s})\,\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n})(dx)\right)ds,

it can be easily seen that

(4.8)

\displaystyle e^{kt}\mathbb{E}|y^{i,n}_{t}-\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{t}|^{2}+k\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{ks}|y^{i,n}_{s}-\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s}|^{2}\,ds+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{ks}|Z^{i,j,n}_{s}-\delta_{ij}\widetilde{Z}^{i,n}_{s}|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +L_{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{ks}|x^{i,n}_{s}-\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s}|^{2}\,ds+L_{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{ks}|x^{j,n}_{s}-\widetilde{x}^{j,n}_{s}|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{ks}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{F}(s,\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s},\widetilde{x}^{j,n}_{s},\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{F}(s,\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{s},x,\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s})\,\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{s}^{j,n})(dx)\right)\right)^{2}ds.

Noting that

and also

\displaystyle e^{kT}\mathbb{E}\left|y_{T}^{i,n}-\widetilde{y}_{T}^{i,n}\right|^{2}\leq C\mathbb{E}\left(|x^{i,n}_{T}-\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{T}|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x^{i,n}_{T}-\widetilde{x}^{i,n}_{T}|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\right).

Therefore we conclude from (

) that

\displaystyle(k+2K_{2}-4L_{2}-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{ks}|y^{i,n}_{s}-\widetilde{y}^{i,n}_{s}|^{2}\,ds\right]\leq\frac{C}{n}.

,

and hence as

Remark 4.1
.

Proposition
4.1
provides a rate of convergence in terms of the
L^{2}
convergence
\|G_{n}-G\|_{2}\to 0
.
Such a convergence holds, for example, if
\|G_{n}-G\|_{\square}\to 0
and
G\in\{0,1\}
(see e.g.
[
37
, Proposition 8.24]
).
It also holds (by dominated convergence theorem) if
G
is continuous and
G_{n}
is sampled from
G
, namely
G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n}):=G(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})
.

The following is a more precise rate of convergence under Lipschitz conditions.

Corollary 4.1
.

Suppose Assumption
4.1
holds.
Suppose
G
is Lipschitz continuous,
\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{L}(\xi^{\lambda})
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
\mathcal{W}_{2}
, and
G_{n}
is sampled from
G
, namely
G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})=G(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})
.
Then
\|G_{n}-G\|_{2}\leq\frac{C}{n}
and hence

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{\lceil n\lambda\rceil,n}-X_{t}^{\lambda}|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}^{\lceil n\lambda\rceil,n}-Y_{t}^{\lambda}|^{2}\,ds\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\left.+\int_{0}^{T}|Z^{\lceil n\lambda\rceil,\lceil n\lambda\rceil,n}_{t}-Z^{\lambda}_{t}|^{2}\,dt\right)\,d\lambda\right]\leq\frac{C}{n},

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left[|X_{t}^{i,n}-X_{t}^{i/n}|^{2}+|Y_{t}^{i,n}-Y_{t}^{i/n}|^{2}\right]\,dt\leq\frac{C}{n},

(4.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\nu^{n}_{t},\nu_{t})\right]\,dt\leq C(n^{-1/2}+n^{-(p-2)/p}).

Proof.

The estimate
\|G_{n}-G\|_{2}\leq\frac{C}{n}
follows from the Lipschitz continuity of
G
.
Applying this to Proposition
4.1
gives (
4.10
).
Combining (
4.10
) and Proposition
3.3
, we have (
4.11
).
From (
4.11
) we have

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\nu_{t}^{n},\bar{\nu}_{t}^{n})\right]\leq\frac{C}{n}.

Using the independence and moment bound on
(X_{t}^{\lambda},Y_{t}^{\lambda})
(see e.g.
[
8

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\bar{\nu}_{t}^{n},\mathbb{E}\bar{\nu}_{t}^{n})\right]\leq C(n^{-1/2}+n^{-(p-2)/p}).

Combining these three displays gives (
4.12
).

Assumption 4.2

where

,

,
\theta=(x,y)
,
\widetilde{\theta}=(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})
.

\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{p}]<+\infty
, and
(B(\cdot,0),F(\cdot,0),Q)\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,2}_{\mathcal{F}}\times\mathcal{M}L^{p,2}_{\mathcal{F}}\times\mathcal{M}L^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}

\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{L}(\xi^{\lambda})

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose Assumption
4.2
holds and
\|G_{n}-G\|_{\square}\to 0
.
Then (
4.4
) holds.
If in addition
G
is continuous, then (
4.5
) and (
4.6

Proof.

We will use the same notation as above Theorem
4.1
.
That is, let
(\widetilde{X}^{n},\widetilde{Y}^{n},\widetilde{Z}^{n})
be the unique solution of the limiting system with graphon
G_{n}
and initial states
(\xi^{\lceil n\lambda\rceil})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}

Abusing notations, we write

\widetilde{Y}^{n}=(\widetilde{Y}^{i,n})_{i=1}^{n}=(\widetilde{Y}^{n,\lambda})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}

\widetilde{Z}^{n}=(\widetilde{Z}^{i,n})_{i=1}^{n}=(\widetilde{Z}^{n,\lambda})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}

Let

and

Let us compute

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta X_{T}^{i,n}\Delta Y_{T}^{i,n}]

\displaystyle\geq(k-\frac{3l}{2}-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{i,n}_{T})^{2}]-\frac{l}{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{j,n}_{T})^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{Q}(\widetilde{X}_{T}^{i,n},\widetilde{X}_{T}^{j,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{Q}(\widetilde{X}_{T}^{i,n},x)\,\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{X}_{T}^{j,n})(dx)\right)\right]^{2}.

Using Itô’s formula, we also obtain that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta X^{i,n}_{T}\Delta Y^{i,n}_{T}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-(k-\frac{5l}{2}-\epsilon)\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{i,n}_{t})^{2}+(\Delta Y^{i,n}_{t})^{2}]\,dt+l\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{j,n}_{t})^{2}]\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{B}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i,n},\widetilde{X}_{t}^{j,n},\widetilde{Y}_{t}^{i,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{B}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i,n},x,\widetilde{Y}_{t}^{i,n})\,\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{j,n})(dx)\right)\right]^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\left(\hat{F}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i,n},\widetilde{X}_{t}^{j,n},\widetilde{Y}_{t}^{i,n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{F}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i,n},x,\widetilde{Y}_{t}^{i,n})\,\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{j,n})(dx)\right)\right]^{2}dt.

Combining the above two inequalities (

4.14
) and averaging over

\displaystyle(k-\frac{7l}{2}-\epsilon)\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{i,n}_{T})^{2}]+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{i,n}_{t})^{2}+(\Delta Y^{i,n}_{t})^{2}]\,dt\right)\leq\frac{C}{n\varepsilon}\to 0

as

.
Combining this with (
4.13
) and (

) gives

\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{i,n}_{T})^{2}]+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta X^{i,n}_{t})^{2}+(\Delta Y^{i,n}_{t})^{2}]\,dt\leq\frac{C}{n}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.

Using the argument of
Step 4
in Theorem
3.2
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}|Z^{i,j,n}_{t}-\delta_{ij}\widetilde{Z}^{i,n}_{t}|^{2}\,dt\leq\frac{C}{n}.

By the argument of
Step 4
in Theorem
4.1
, we have the estimate (
4.9
) and hence the desired results.

Proposition 4.2
.

5.

n
-player game

Let
G:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a bounded graphon, and without loss of generality assume that
|G(\lambda,\kappa)|\leq 1,\,\forall(\lambda,\kappa)\in[0,1]^{2}
. Each
\lambda\in[0,1]
represents a type of population, which consists of continuum many players. Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
denote the distribution of population of type
\lambda
, and
\boldsymbol{\eta}
denote the collection
\{\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\lambda}:\,\lambda\in[0,1]\}
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}([0,1];\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex control space. Take functions

\displaystyle b_{1},f_{1},f_{2}

\displaystyle:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle b_{2},b_{3}

\displaystyle:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle q_{1},q_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

For any
(\lambda,x,\boldsymbol{\eta},a)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}([0,1];\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\times A
, we define

\displaystyle b^{\lambda}_{G}(t,x,\boldsymbol{\eta},a)

\displaystyle:=\int_{[0,1]}G(\lambda,\kappa)\,d\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}}b_{1}(t,z)\,\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\kappa}(dz)+b_{2}(t)x+b_{3}(t)a,

\displaystyle f^{\lambda}_{G}(t,x,\boldsymbol{\eta},a)

\displaystyle:=f_{1}(t,x)+\int_{[0,1]}G(\lambda,\kappa)\,d\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{2}(t,z)\,\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\kappa}(dz)+\frac{1}{2}a^{2},

\displaystyle q^{\lambda}_{G}(x,\boldsymbol{\eta})

\displaystyle:=q_{1}(x)+\int_{[0,1]}G(\lambda,\kappa)\,d\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{2}(z)\,\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\kappa}(dz).

Let
W^{\lambda}
be a family of independent standard Brownian motion, and
\sigma>0
be a constant volatility. Denote by
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}^{\lambda}(t)
the distribution of players of type
\lambda
at time
t
, and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t):=\{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}_{G}(t):\,\lambda\in[0,1]\}
,
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}:=\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t):\,t\in[0,T]\}
. Choosing
\alpha^{\lambda}(t)\in A

\lambda
controls the dynamic

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX^{\lambda}_{t}={b}_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t),\alpha^{\lambda}(t)\right)\,dt+\sigma\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
X^{\lambda}_{0}=\xi^{\lambda},\end{cases}

where
\xi^{\lambda}
is a square integrable random variable. The cost for the representative player
\lambda
is given by

(5.1)

\displaystyle J(\alpha^{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G})=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}{f}_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t),\alpha^{\lambda}(t)\right)\,dt+{q}_{G}^{\lambda}\left(X^{\lambda}_{T},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(T)\right)\right],

and each representative player chooses control
\alpha^{\lambda}(t)
to minimize
J(\alpha^{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G})
.

For each
\lambda\in[0,1]
, define the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,x,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t),y,a\right):={b}_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,x,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t),a\right)\cdot y+{f}_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,x,a,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t)\right),

and the minimizer

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}^{\lambda}_{G}(t,x,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t),y):=\lambdargmin_{a\in A}H_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,x,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t),y,a\right)=-b_{3}(t)y.

Given
\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t):\,0\leq t\leq T\}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dY^{\lambda}_{t}=-\partial_{x}H_{G}^{\lambda}\left(t,X^{\lambda}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(t),Y^{\lambda}_{t},-b_{3}(t)Y^{\lambda}_{t}\right)dt+Z^{\lambda}_{t}\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
Y^{\lambda}_{T}=\partial_{x}{q}_{G}^{\lambda}\left(X^{\lambda}_{T},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{G}(T)\right).\end{cases}

Since the law of
X^{\lambda}_{t}
should coincide with

, after simplification we obtain the FBSDE of the graphon field game

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX^{\lambda}_{t}=\left(b_{2}(t)X^{\lambda}_{t}-|b_{3}(t)|^{2}Y^{\lambda}_{t}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\lambda,\kappa)\,\mathbb{E}[b_{1}(t,X^{\kappa}_{t})]\,d\kappa\right)dt+\sigma\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
dY^{\lambda}_{t}=-\left(b_{2}(t)Y^{\lambda}_{t}+\partial_{x}f_{1}(t,X^{\lambda}_{t})\right)\,dt+Z^{\lambda}_{t}\,dW^{\lambda}_{t},\\
X^{\lambda}_{0}=\xi^{\lambda},\\
Y^{\lambda}_{T}=\partial_{x}q_{1}(X_{T}^{\lambda}),\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,1].\end{cases}

In order for coefficients of (
5.3

,
3.1
,
4.1
or

\displaystyle b^{i,n}(t,\underline{x},a^{i})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(i,j)b_{1}(t,x^{j})+b_{2}(t)x^{i}+b_{3}(t)a^{i},

\displaystyle:=f_{1}(t,x^{i})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(i,j)f_{2}(t,x^{j})+\frac{1}{2}(a^{i})^{2},

\displaystyle q^{i,n}(\underline{x})

\displaystyle:=q_{1}(x^{i})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_{n}(i,j)q_{2}(x^{j}).

Let us compute the FBSDE system of this
n
-player game. Each player has the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H^{i,n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}^{i},\underline{a})=b^{n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{a})\cdot\underline{y}^{i}+f^{i,n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{a}^{i}),

\displaystyle H^{i,n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}^{i},\hat{\alpha}^{n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}))\leq H^{i,n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}^{i},(a^{i},\hat{\alpha}^{n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y})^{-i})),

Note that when

\displaystyle\partial_{x^{i}}H^{i,n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}^{i},\underline{\hat{\alpha}}^{n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}))=

\displaystyle\partial_{x^{i}}q^{i,n}(\underline{x})=

\displaystyle\partial_{x^{j}}H^{i,n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}^{i},\underline{\hat{\alpha}}^{n}(t,\underline{x},\underline{y}))=

Theorem 5.1
.

Under Assumption
5.1
, if
\lVert G_{n}-G\rVert_{\square}\to 0
and

is continuous, then the Nash equilibrium of

-player game converges to the corresponding graphon field game, i.e., (

Proof.

Step 1:
Consider an auxiliary FBSDE system

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\widetilde{X}^{i,n}_{t}=\left(b_{2}(t)\widetilde{X}^{i,n}_{t}-|b_{3}(t)|^{2}\widetilde{Y}^{i,n}_{t}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}G_{n}(i,k)b_{1}(t,\widetilde{X}^{k,n}_{t})\right)dt+\sigma\,dW^{i/n}_{t},\\

\widetilde{Y}^{i,n}_{T}=\partial_{x}q_{1}(\widetilde{X}_{T}^{i,n}),\end{cases}

Invoking Theorem
4.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i,n}-X_{t}^{i/n}|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{i,n}_{t}-Y_{t}^{i/n}|^{2}\right]\to 0\ \ \text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Step 2:
We will show that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i,n}-X_{t}^{i,n}|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{i,n}_{t}-Y_{t}^{i,n}|^{2}\right]\to 0\ \ \text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Using the same computation as in Theorem
2.1
, we can prove that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}|X_{t}^{i,n}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}|Y^{i,j,n}_{t}|^{2}\,dt\right]\leq C,

where

is some constant uniformly for any

(5.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|X^{i,n}_{t}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}|Y^{i,j,n}_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C.

According to (
5

Appendix A
Measurability

Lemma A.1
.

\boldsymbol{\mu}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{C}\left([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})\right)
is measurable if and only if for any
t\in[0,T]
,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}(t)\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})\text{ is measurable}.

Proof.

The proof of ‘only if’ is trivial. For the proof of ‘if’ part, we note that with sup norm,
\mathcal{C}\left([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})\right)
is a topological subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{0}\left([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})\right)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, due to (
A.1
) we know that

\lambda\mapsto(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}(T/n),\dotso,\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}(T))\text{ is measurable}.

We construct
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}\left([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})\right)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}^{\lambda}(t):=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}\left(\frac{i}{n}\right),\,t\in\left(\frac{(i-1)T}{n},\frac{iT}{n}\right],\,i=1,\dotso,n.

Then it can be easily verified that
\lambda\to\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}^{\lambda}
is measurable. By the continuity of
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}(\cdot)
,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}^{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\lambda}
is measurable in
\lambda
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

A function
x:\lambda\mapsto x^{\lambda}\in L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}
if and only if
\lambda\mapsto x^{\lambda}_{t}\in L^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}
is measurable for any
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Note that
L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}\ni\boldsymbol{\eta}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\eta}_{t}\in L^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}
is continuous. Therefore it can be readily seen that the measurability of
\lambda\mapsto x^{\lambda}
implies the measurability of
\lambda\mapsto x^{\lambda}_{t}
for any
t\in[0,T]
.

Conversely, define
x^{N}\in\mathcal{M}L^{p,2}_{\mathcal{F}}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
as follows,

\displaystyle x^{N,\lambda}_{t}

\displaystyle:=x^{\lambda}_{nT/N},\quad\forall t\in[nT/N,(n+1)T/N),\,n=0,\dotso,N-2,\,\lambda\in[0,1],

\displaystyle x^{N,\lambda}_{t}

\displaystyle:=x^{\lambda}_{(N-1)T/N},\quad\forall t\in[(N-1)T/N,T],\,\lambda\in[0,1].

According to our hypothesis, it can be easily seen that

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto x^{N,\lambda}\in\left(L^{p,2}_{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\lVert\cdot\rVert}_{S}^{p}\right)

is measurable, and also the limit

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto x^{\lambda}\in\left(L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\lVert\cdot\rVert}_{S}^{p}\right).

∎

Lemma A.3
.

Take a polish space
\Omega
and a Borel probability measure
(\mathcal{F},P)
over
\Omega
. Take another measure space
(E,\Sigma,m)
. Suppose
\rho:E\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a real-valued function such that
x\mapsto\rho(e,x)
is continuous for any
e\in E
,
e\mapsto\rho(e,x)
is measurable for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
|\rho(e,x)|\leq C(1+|x|),\,\forall(e,x)\in E\times\mathbb{R}
for some positive constant
C
. Then given any measurable mapping
e\mapsto X(e)\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, the Banach-valued function

e\mapsto\rho(e,X(e))\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)

is also measurable.

Proof.

According to
[
24
, Proposition 3.4.5]
, the Banach space
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
is separable. Therefore as a result of Pettis measurability theorem, any measurable function
X:E\to L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
is also strongly measurable, i.e.,
X
can be written as a pointwise limit of simple functions

X^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{S_{m_{n}}}x_{m_{n}},

where
m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
,
S_{1},\dotso,S_{m_{n}}
is a finite collection of disjoint subsets of
E
, and
x_{1},\dotso,x_{m_{n}}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. It is then readily seen that

\displaystyle e\mapsto\rho(e,X^{n}(e))

is measurable, and thus
\rho(\cdot,X(\cdot))=\lim\limits_{n}\rho(\cdot,X^{n}(\cdot))
is measurable.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose
\rho:[0,1]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function such that
x\mapsto\psi(\lambda,t,x)
is continuous and grows at most linearly uniformly for
(\lambda,t)\in[0,1]\times[0,T]
. Given any measurable
\lambda\mapsto X^{\lambda}\in L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}
, we have that

(A.2)

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto\int_{0}^{\cdot}\psi(\lambda,s,X^{\lambda}(s))\,ds\in L^{p,c}_{\mathcal{F}}

is measurable.

Proof.

By our assumption, it is clear that
(\lambda,s)\mapsto X^{\lambda}(s)
is measurable. Applying Lemma
A.3
with
E=[0,1]\times[0,T]
, it is readily seen that

(A.3)

\displaystyle(\lambda,s)\mapsto\psi(\lambda,s,X^{\lambda}(s))

is measurable. The function (
A.3
) is also Bochner integrable due to our linear growth assumption in
x
. Thus by the Fubini theorem of Bochner theorem,

\lambda\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\psi(\lambda,s,X^{\lambda}(s))\,ds

is measurable for any
t\in[0,T]
. Now the measurable of (
A.2
) follows from Lemma
A.2
.

∎

Remark A.1
.

Using approximation of simple functions, one can easily verify that Bochner integral coincides with Lebesgue integral.

Appendix B
Weak uniqueness of FBSDE

The notion of weak existence and uniqueness for FBSDEs are almost the same to the ones considered for classical SDEs, see e.g.
[
1
,
26
,
39
]
.

Definition B.1
.

A five-tuple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P,W)
is said to be a standard set-up if
W
is a Brownian motion over the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P)
and
\mathbb{F}:=\{\mathcal{F}\}_{t\geq 0}
is complete and right continuous.

Consider an FBSDE

(B.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}B(s,X_{s},Y_{s})\,ds+\sigma\,W_{t},\\
Y_{t}=Q(X_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}F(s,X_{s},Y_{s})\,ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}\,dW_{s},\end{cases}

where
B,F,Q
are progressively measurable functions.

Definition B.2
.

A triple of processes
(X,Y,Z)
is said to be a weak solution of (
B.1
) if there exists a standard set-up
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P,W)
such that
(X,Y,Z)
are adapted to the filtration
\mathbb{F}
and satisfy (
B.1
) a.s. If
(X,Y,Z)
and
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z})
are two weak solutions of (
B.1
) on the same set-up, we say that pathwise uniqueness holds if

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[(X_{t},Y_{t})=(\widetilde{X}_{t},\widetilde{Y}_{t}),\,\forall t\in[0,T]\right]=1.

By Yamada-Watanabe Theorem for SDEs, pathwise uniqueness implies uniqueness in law. We have the same result for FBSDEs.

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose the pathwise uniqueness property holds for FBSDE (
B.1
). Then for any two weak solutions
(X,Y,Z)
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P,W)
and
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z})
on
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{F}},\widetilde{P},\widetilde{W})
, their distributions coincide.

Proof.

See
[
1
, Theorem 5.1]
.
∎

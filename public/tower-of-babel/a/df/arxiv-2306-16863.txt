On the mean field theory of Ensemble Kalman filters for SPDEs
Sebastian W. Ertel, TU Berlin

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the mathematical analysis of continuous time Ensemble Kalman Filters (EnKBFs) and their mean field limit in an infinite dimensional setting. The signal is determined by a nonlinear Stochastic Partial Differential Equation (SPDE), which is posed in the standard variational setting. Assuming global one-sided Lipschitz conditions and finite dimensional observations we first prove the well posedness of both the EnKBF and its corresponding mean field version. We then investigate the quantitative convergence of the EnKBF towards its mean field limit, recovering the rates suggested by the law of large numbers for bounded observation functions. The main tool hereby are exponential moment estimates for the empirical covariance of the EnKBF, which may be of independent interest. In the appendix of the paper we investigate the connection of the mean field EnKBF and the Stochastic Filtering Problem. In particular we derive the Feedback Particle Filter for infinite dimensional signals and show that the mean field EnKBF can viewed as its constant gain approximation.

Introduction

In recent years data driven methods have become increasingly relevant in scientific computing. In particular data assimilation, that is the (optimal) integration of real world data into mathematical models, has become a popular research topic for practitioners and mathematicians alike. As it shares similar objectives to stochastic filtering, which is essentially the discipline of Bayesian estimation of dynamic processes from noisy, potentially incomplete data, many algorithms originating in filtering are used for data assimilation tasks. Vice versa, algorithms for data assimilation of dynamical processes can be viewed through the lens of filtering, the mathematical model is then referred to as the signal and the available data as the observations.

One such algorithm is the Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF), which was introduced by Geir Evensen in 94
[

[

The EnKF has by now become one of the most widely used techniques for data assimilation in high dimensional settings, particularly popular amongst practitioners in the geosciences and numerical weather forecasting. Besides its usage for state estimation in dynamical systems, the EnKF and related algorithms have also been applied to parameter estimation in inverse problems

In the following, variance bounds for both the posterior (
P
) and the law of the EnKBF will play a crucial role in our analysis. This is why, besides the standard assumptions for the signal
3
and the observations
6
, we make the following additional assumption, which will give us a priori bounds for the signal variance.

Assumption 7

(Bounded signal diffusion)
.

There exists a constant
\beta<+\infty
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in\mathscr{V}}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathcal{B}(v)\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\left(\mathcal{B}(v)\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\prime}\right]\leq\beta.

Note that for constant
\mathcal{B}
, i.e. for additive noise in the signal (
S
), this assumption is fulfilled, as by the Parseval lemma

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathcal{B}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\left(\mathcal{B}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{k}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\nu_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}q_{l}\left\langle e_{l},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\nu_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}q_{l}\left\|\mathcal{B}e_{l}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}q_{l}\penalty\ \left\|\mathcal{B}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}\left(\mathscr{U};\mathscr{H}\right)}^{2}\leq\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathcal{Q}\right]\penalty\ \left\|\mathcal{B}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}\left(\mathscr{U};\mathscr{H}\right)}^{2}<+\infty.

Finally, to keep formulas short, let us make the following notational convention for conditional expectations that shall hold throughout this paper.

Definition 8
.

For any point in time
t\geq 0
and any (sufficiently integrable)
\mathfrak{F}
-adapted,
\mathscr{H}
-valued process
\left(v_{s}\right)_{s\geq 0}
we denote by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[v_{t}\right]:=\mathbb{E}\left[\penalty\ v_{t}\penalty\ \Big|\penalty\ Y_{s},\penalty\ s\leq t\penalty\ \right]

its conditional expectation w.r.t. past observations.

3
Analysis of the EnKBF

For the sake of brevity in formulas we make the following definitions.

Definition 9
.

For any
\mathfrak{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{N})\in\mathscr{H}^{N},\penalty\ N\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{v}\right]&:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v^{i},\penalty\ \mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{v}\right]:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}H(v^{i})\\
\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}[\mathfrak{v}]&:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(v^{i}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{v}\right]\right)\penalty\ \left(H(v^{i})-\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{v}\right]\right)^{\prime}.\end{split}

(9)

We use the normalization by
1/N
for the empirical covariance instead of the usual unbiased normalization by
1/(N-1)
just for notational convenience in the calculations that are to follow.

The EnKBF (
1
) can then be rewritten more compactly into

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}u^{i}_{t}&=\mathcal{A}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{i}_{t}\\
&\phantom{=}+\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-\frac{H(u^{i}_{t})+\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right),\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ i=1,\cdots,N\end{split}

(10)

where
(\bar{W}^{i})_{i=1,\cdots,N}
are independent copies of the Wiener process
W
, driving the signal (
S
). The initial conditions
u^{i}_{0}
are independent copies of
u_{0}
and we denote
\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}:=(u^{1}_{t},\cdots,u^{N}_{t})\in\mathscr{H}^{N}
.

While (
10
) is just a system of interacting ordinary SPDEs for which local one-sided Lipschitz conditions are enough to derive well posedness
[
33
]
, it does not seem to satisfy the usual growth conditions for unbounded observation functions
H
. In
[
32
]
the well posedness was proven for the finite dimensional setting by showing that blow ups do not occur in finite times. This is of course not sufficient to conclude well posedness in infinite dimensions. Instead we employ a partial stopping argument. A priori bounds for the empirical ensemble variance will play a key role in our proof, and so, to keep formulas simple, we make the following definitions¡.

Definition 10
.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and any ensemble
\mathfrak{v}=(v^{1},\cdots,v^{N})\in\mathscr{H}^{N}
of
N
elements of
\mathscr{H}
, we define the empirical variance
\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{v}]
by

\displaystyle\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{v}]:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|v^{i}_{s}-\mathbb{E}^{N}[\mathfrak{v}]\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}.

And similarly we define the empirical observed variance by

\displaystyle\sigma^{N,H}[\mathfrak{v}]:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|H(v^{i}_{s})-\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}[\mathfrak{v}]\right|^{2}.

3.1
Well posedness

Furthermore we make the following definition, which we will use to switch of parts of the dynamics of the (mean field) EnKBF.

Definition 11
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}
a smoothed version of the indicator function
\mathbb{1}_{[0,k]}
, such that
\mathbb{1}_{[0,k]}\leq\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\leq\mathbb{1}_{[0,k+1]}
.

We are now in the position to formulate and prove the following well posedness result.

Theorem 12
.

If the conditions in Assumption
3
and
7
are satisfied, there exists a unique (strong) solution
\mathfrak{u}^{N}=\left(u^{1},\cdots,u^{N}\right)
to the nonlinear EnKBF (
10
). I.e.
\mathfrak{u}^{N}
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-adapted,
\mathscr{H}^{N}
-valued process, such that for any
T>0
and any
i=1,\cdots,N

\displaystyle u^{i}\in L^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathscr{H}\right)\cap L^{\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathscr{V}\right)

and for any
v\in\mathscr{V}

\displaystyle\left\langle u^{i}_{T},v\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u^{i}_{0},v\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\int_{0}^{T}\prescript{}{\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}{\left\langle\mathcal{A}(u^{i}_{t}),v\right\rangle_{\mathscr{V}}}\mathrm{d}t+\left\langle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{i}_{t},v\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\left\langle\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\left(\left\|\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\|_{L(\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}},\mathscr{H})}^{2}\right)\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-\frac{H(u^{i}_{t})+\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right),v\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}.

Proof.

First we note that for any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the system

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}u^{i}_{t}&=\mathcal{A}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{i}_{t}\\
&\phantom{=}+\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\left(\left\|\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\|_{L(\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}},\mathscr{H})}^{2}\right)\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-\frac{H(u^{i}_{t})+\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right),\end{split}

(11)

for
i=1,\cdots,N
, satisfies the standard local one-sided Lipschitz and growth conditions and thus has a unique solution, see
[
33
]
. To keep formulas simple we will omit the argument of
\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\left(\left\|\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{d_{y}}}^{2}\right)
and instead simply write
\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}
in the rest of this proof.

We note that the ensemble mean
\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{A}(u^{j}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}(u^{j}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{j}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\penalty\ \mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\mathrm{d}t\right).

(12)

This gives us the evolution equation for the centered particles

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{A}(u^{i}_{t})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{A}(u^{j}_{t})\right)\mathrm{d}t+\left(\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{i}_{t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}(u^{j}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{j}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\penalty\ \mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\frac{H(u^{i}_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t.

Note that by Parseval one easily verifies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right],\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(H(u^{i}_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right)\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{k},u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(H(u^{i}_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right)\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]^{\mathrm{T}}\right].

Therefore, by Itô’s formula, we derive the following equation for the average deviation from the ensemble mean

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prescript{}{\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}{\left\langle\left(\mathcal{A}(u^{i}_{t})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{A}(u^{j}_{t})\right),u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathscr{V}}}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}^{N}_{t}\\
&\phantom{=}-\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\penalty\ \mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]^{\prime}\right]\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\left(\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\prime}\right]\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathcal{B}(u^{j}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\left(\mathcal{B}(u^{j}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\prime}\right]\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

(13)

where
\mathfrak{m}^{N}
denotes the local martingale given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}^{N}_{t}&:=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right],\left(\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{i}_{t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}(u^{j}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{j}_{t}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\\
&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right],\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}^{i}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}},\penalty\ \text{with}\penalty\ \mathfrak{m}^{N}_{0}=0.\end{split}

(14)

We note that in (
13
) we can replace
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{A}(u^{j}_{t})
by
\mathcal{A}\left(\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right)
. Thus by using the one-sided Lipschitz condition (
3
) and Assumption (
7
) as well as the positivity of the trace

\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]^{\prime}\right]
, we derive the inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]\leq\left(2\lambda\penalty\ \sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]+\beta\right)\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}^{N}_{t}.

(15)

Since
\mathfrak{m}^{N}
is a real valued local martingale we can deduce by the stochastic Grönwall Lemma
[
41
, Theorem 4]
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|u^{i}_{t}-m^{N}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\sqrt{\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]}\right]\leq(\pi+1)\sqrt{\beta}e^{\lambda T}.

(16)

Due to the Lipschitz continuity of
H
, this also gives a uniform bound for
\left\|\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{d_{y}}}^{2}
. Since (
11
) coincide with (
10
) on
\left\{\left\|\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{d_{y}}}^{2}\leq k\right\}
we have thus derived the well posedness of (
10
).

∎

3.2
Exponential moment bounds for the gain term

The key identity in the proof of Theorem
12
was (
15
), which gave a priori bounds for the empirical covariance operator. Later on we will show that the (conditional) covariance of its mean field limit satisfies a similar differential inequality that does not include the random fluctuations caused by the martingale
\mathfrak{m}^{N}
. If the EnKBF (
10
) is to be a good approximation of its mean field limit (
19
), one would expect these fluctations to become small as the number of particles is increased sufficiently. This is indeed the case, as due to Assumption
7
, we derive

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\left[\mathfrak{m}^{N}\right]_{t}&=\frac{2}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right],\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{B}(u^{i}_{t})^{\prime}(u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right])\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\frac{2\beta}{N}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|u^{i}_{t}-\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t=\frac{2\beta}{N}\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(17)

The last inequality is a consequence of the fact that the trace is invariant under the change of the orthonormal basis and that for every nonzero vector one can find an orthonormal basis that contains this vector. Since we were able to bound
\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]
uniformly in time, the quadratic variation of
\mathfrak{m}^{N}
will decrease to zero for
N\to\infty
.
For our convergence proof in section
5
we will need exponential moment bounds of the empirical variance
\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]
. Such bounds are a delicate matter as the ensemble
\mathfrak{u}^{N}
will likely show some Gaussian (tail) behaviour and thus
\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\exp\left(r\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]\right)\right]
might not be finite for all values of
r\geq 0
. However, as
N\to\infty
one would expect
\sigma^{N}
to become deterministic and as such any exponential moment should exist for
N
sufficiently large. We prove this fact in the following lemma by employing a Grönwall argument.

Proposition 14
.

Let
q\geq 0
be arbitrary. Then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N>2\beta q\penalty\ e^{(2\lambda+1)T}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\exp\left(q\penalty\ \sigma^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right)\right]\leq(\pi+1)\exp\left(\frac{q\left(e^{(2\lambda+1)T}-1\right)}{2(2\lambda+1)}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(2qe^{(2\lambda+1)T}\sigma^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{0}\right]\right)\right].

(18)

In particular the
q
-th exponential moment of the path of
\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}]
exists up to time
T
, if the
\left(2qe^{(2\lambda+1)T}\right)
-th exponential moment of the initial empirical variance
\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}]
exists.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}:=2\lambda+1
(see Assumption
3
) and
\mathfrak{b}:=qe^{\mathfrak{a}T}
. We define the process
\mathfrak{s}_{t}:=2\mathfrak{b}\penalty\ e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]
. Then, using inequality (
15
), we derive the inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{s}_{t}=2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]-2\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq(2\lambda-\mathfrak{a})2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}}\sigma^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}\beta e^{-\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{t}

\displaystyle=(2\lambda-\mathfrak{a})\mathfrak{s}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}\beta e^{-\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{t}.

Furthermore we derive from (
13
) the form of the quadratic variation of
\mathfrak{s}
, and from (
17
) the estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left[\mathfrak{s}\right]_{t}=(2\mathfrak{b})^{2}e^{-2\mathfrak{a}t}\mathrm{d}\left[\mathfrak{m}^{N}\right]_{t}\leq(2\mathfrak{b})^{2}e^{-2\mathfrak{a}t}\frac{2\beta}{N}\sigma^{N}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}]\mathrm{d}t=2\frac{2\beta\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}}{N}\mathfrak{s}_{t}\mathrm{d}t.

These inequalities, together with Itô’s formula, give us the following inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})

\displaystyle=\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{s}_{t}+\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\left[\mathfrak{s}\right]_{t}

\displaystyle\leq(2\lambda-\mathfrak{a})\mathfrak{s}_{t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}\beta e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{t}+\frac{2\beta\mathfrak{b}e^{-2\mathfrak{a}t}}{N}\mathfrak{s}_{t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\left(2\lambda-\mathfrak{a}+\frac{2\beta\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}}{N}\right)\mathfrak{s}_{t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}\beta e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{t}.

Due to our assumptions we have
\mathfrak{a}>2\lambda+\frac{2\beta\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}}{N}
and thus derive the stochastic inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\leq 2\mathfrak{b}\beta e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{t}.

Since
2\mathfrak{b}e^{-\mathfrak{a}t}\exp(\mathfrak{s}_{t})\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{t}
defines a local martingale, the stochastic Grönwall inequality
[
41
]
gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\exp\left(q\penalty\ \sigma^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{t}\right]\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\sqrt{\exp\left(\mathfrak{s}_{t}\right)}\right]\leq(\pi+1)\exp\left(q/2e^{\mathfrak{a}T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\mathfrak{a}s}\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\mathfrak{s}_{0}/2\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq(\pi+1)\exp\left(\frac{q\left(e^{(2\lambda+1)T}-1\right)}{2(2\lambda+1)}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(2qe^{(2\lambda+1)T}\sigma^{N}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{0}\right]\right)\right],

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 15
.

In the proof of Proposition
14
we used a standard testfunction for our Grönwall argument. Since we have good controls for the quadratic variation of
\mathfrak{m}
, we also could have just used the standard Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality in combination with a deterministic Grönwall Lemma. The usage of the stochastic Grönwall inequality is not necessary in our setting, however in

4
Analysis of the mean field EnKBF

Using the notational convention for conditional expectations of Definition
8
, the mean field EnKBF (
2
) can be written more compactly as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\bar{u}_{t}&=\mathcal{A}(\bar{u}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{B}(\bar{u}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}_{t}+\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}_{t},H(\bar{u}_{t})\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-\frac{H(\bar{u}_{t})+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(\bar{u}_{t})\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right).\end{split}

(19)

When taking the (formal) mean field limit
N\to\infty
for the EnKBF (
10
), one would expect it to converge in an appropriate sense to (
19
). Before we show this convergence rigorously in the next section
5
, let us first investigate the well posedness of the McKean–Vlasov equation (
19
).

Remark 16

(Literature)
.

The mean field EnKBF (
19
) does not seem to fit the standard well posedness theory for McKean–Vlasov equations, as found e.g. in
[
2
]
, due to only locally Lipschitz coefficients and missing growth conditions. In a finite dimensional setting well posedness of the mean field EnKBF (
19
) was shown in
[
16
]
for bounded signal dynamics and observation functions. For linear observation functions
[
22
]
showed well posedness of finite dimensional mean-field EnKBFs that may also include singular correction terms in the presence of correlated noise. This was done by a combination of a fixed point and a stopping argument with respect to the covariance
\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}_{t},H(\bar{u}_{t})\right]
. The main tool were a priori variance bounds, that were made robust with respect to the fixed point argument via stopping times.

In the infinite dimensional setting this argument does not work due to missing equivalence of norms.
While using a Galerkin argument would thus seem tempting, it would also not imply the desired uniqueness of solutions, which is a property that is difficult to show, and sometimes does not even hold for McKean–Vlasov equations under local Lipschitz conditions
[
41
]
.

So instead we use an adapted fixed point argument, that makes use of variance inequalities, similar to those used in the proof of Theorem
12
.

First we investigate the covariance structure in (
19
). We do this however in a more general form. Let
(h_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a given
R^{d_{y}}
-valued,
\mathfrak{F}
-adapted stochastic process with finite second moments. Furthermore assume that
\xi^{Y}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
-valued semimartingale that is adapted to the natural filtration generated by
Y
, and thus also to
\mathfrak{F}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau^{k}&:=\inf\left\{\penalty\ t\geq 0\penalty\ :\penalty\ \left|\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(u^{k}_{t})\right]\right|^{2}>k\penalty\ \right\}\\
\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}}&:=\inf\left\{\penalty\ t\geq 0\penalty\ :\penalty\ \mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left|H(u^{\mathrm{ref}}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]>l\penalty\ \right\},\end{split}

(32)

and note that both are stopping times with respect to the filtration generated by
Y
. This implies that for any stochastic process
(z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and any (suitably integrable) functions
f,g
, the identities

\displaystyle g\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[f(z_{\min\{\tau^{k},t\}})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\left.g\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[f(z_{s})\right]\right)\right|_{s=\min\{\tau^{k},t\}}

\displaystyle g\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[f(z_{\min\{\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}},t\}})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\left.g\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[f(z_{s})\right]\right)\right|_{s=\min\{\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}},t\}}

hold and therefore
\bar{u}^{k}
is a solution to the EnKBF (
19
) on the random time interval
[0,\min\{\tau^{k},\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}}\}]
. By the uniqueness of (
25
),
\bar{u}^{k}
and
\bar{u}^{k+1}
must even coincide on
[0,\min\{\tau^{k},\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}}\}]
. Thus we can construct a solution to (
19
) using the solutions to (
25
). In order to conclude existence and uniqueness of the EnKBF, we just have to show that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{k,l\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\tau^{k}>T\right\}\cap\left\{\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}}>T\right\}

defines a covering of the sample space almost surely.

To this end we first note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\prescript{}{\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}{\left\langle\mathcal{A}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}),\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{V}}}\mathrm{d}t+\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathcal{B}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathcal{B}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\left(\mathcal{B}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\prime}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+2\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y^{l}_{t}-\frac{H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right),\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right]\right].

Taking the conditional expectation thus gives us

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]\\
&=2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\prescript{}{\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}{\left\langle\mathcal{A}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}),\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{V}}}\right]\mathrm{d}t+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathcal{B}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\left(\mathcal{B}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\prime}\right]\right]\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y^{l}_{t}-\frac{H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]\\
&\phantom{=}+\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{l}\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left|H(u^{\mathrm{ref}}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{2}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right),\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right]\right]\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(33)

The first two terms on the right hand side can be bounded using the growth condition (
4
) and the diffusivity bound (
7
). The last term is just the squared shadow 2-norm of the operator
\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1/2}_{t}
, which we can estimate using Parseval and the robust variance bound (
22
) as

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right),\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right]\right]\leq\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[H\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right),\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\nu_{j},\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}},H\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[H\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right),\left\langle\nu_{j},\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\nu_{j},\bar{u}^{k}_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]\left|R^{-1}_{t}\right|\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left|H(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\right]\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right]\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]\left|R^{-1}_{t}\right|\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left|H(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\right]\right|^{2}\right]^{2}\leq\mathrm{Lip}(H)\left|R^{-1}_{t}\right|\beta^{2}e^{2\lambda t}.

Thus we can bound (
33
) by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]&=\left(2\alpha_{H}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]+2\alpha_{0}+\beta+\mathrm{Lip}(H)\left|R^{-1}_{t}\right|\beta^{2}e^{2\lambda t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathrm{d}Y^{l}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\\
&\phantom{=}-2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R_{t}^{-1}\frac{H^{k}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H^{k}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\right]}{2}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(34)

We use (
30
) and
\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\leq 1
to derive that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]R_{t}^{-1}\frac{H^{k}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H^{k}(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\right]}{2}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]+\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{k}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\right]\right|\right)^{2}\left\|\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]\right\|_{\mathrm{L}\left(R^{d_{x}};\mathscr{H}\right)}^{2}\left|R^{-1}_{t}\right|\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[|H(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(2+\mathrm{Lip}(H)^{3}\beta^{2}e^{2\lambda t}\left|R^{-1}_{t}\right|\right)\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]+\mathrm{Lip}(H)\beta^{2}e^{2\lambda t}\left|R^{-1}_{t}\right|\left|H(0)\right|^{2}.

Thus, we note that there exist constants
\kappa_{4}(T)
and
\kappa_{5}(T)
, only depending on the timeframe
T
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]&=\left(\kappa_{4}(T)\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]+\kappa_{5}(T)\right)\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]\right]R^{-1}_{t}\mathrm{d}Y^{l}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}},\end{split}

which by using the explicit form of the observations
Y
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]&=\left(\kappa_{4}(T)\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]+\kappa_{5}(T)\right)\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]\right]R^{-1}_{t}\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{l}\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left|H(u^{\mathrm{ref}}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]\right)H(u^{\mathrm{ref}}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+2\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\langle\bar{u}^{k}_{t},\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{k}_{t},H^{k}\left(\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right)\right]\right]R^{-1}_{t}\tilde{\mathbb{1}}_{l}\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left|H(u^{\mathrm{ref}}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]\right)\Gamma_{t}\mathrm{d}V_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}.\end{split}

(35)

Again using (
30
) and the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality we derive that there exist constants
\kappa_{6}(T)
, depending solely on
T
, and
\kappa_{7}(T,l)
, depending on
T
and
l
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]\right]&=\kappa_{6}(T)\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]+T\kappa_{7}(T,l).\end{split}

(36)

Thus, by the Grönwall Lemma, we derive that for fixed
l
and
T

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]\right]\leq\exp\left(T\kappa_{6}(T)\right)\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]+T\kappa_{7}(T,l)\right),

(37)

which, implies that almost surely there exists a
k
such that
\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]\leq k
. By the Lipschitz continuity of
H
and the inequality
\left|\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(\bar{u}^{k}_{t})\right]\right|\leq|H(0)|+\mathrm{Lip}(H)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left\|\bar{u}^{k}_{t}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\right]}
this then in turn implies that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\tau^{k}>T\right\}\cap\left\{\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}}>T\right\}=\left\{\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}}>T\right\}\penalty\ \text{almost surely.}

Since
\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\left|H(u^{\mathrm{ref}}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]
is finite almost surely, we can thus indeed conclude that there exists a solution to the EnKBF (
19
), defined on every event
\left\{\tau^{k}>T\right\}\cap\left\{\tau^{l}_{\mathrm{ref}}>T\right\}
by the sequence of
\bar{u}^{k}
.

Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the stopped dynamics (
24
).

Remark 18
.

Note that even though in the proof above we used the specific form of the observations
Y
, it actually does not matter that the true observation function and the modelled observation function coincide, i.e. if

. The continuous dependence on the signal parameters

,
\mathcal{B}
and the initial condition

can be shown as well. Only the robustness with respect to the observation stream

is a delicate matter due to the discontinuity of the Itô–Lyons map.

Proof.

We note that since

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle u^{i}_{s}-\bar{u}^{i}_{s},\left(\mathbb{C}^{N}_{H}\left[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{s}\right]-\mathbb{Cov}_{Y}\left[\bar{u}^{i}_{s},H(\bar{u}^{i}_{s})\right]\right)R^{-1}_{s}\Gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}V_{s}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

Next we note that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}[\mathfrak{u}^{N}_{s}]-\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(\bar{u}^{i}_{s})\right]\right|^{2}\leq 2\mathrm{Lip}(H)^{2}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|r^{i}_{s}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+2\left|\mathbb{E}^{N}_{H}\left[\bar{\mathfrak{u}}^{N}_{s}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H\left(\bar{u}_{s}\right)\right]\right|^{2}.

This is not the only connection of the EnKBF to the filtering problem, as in the linear Gaussian setting (see Definition
28
below) the law of its mean field limit (
2
) is a solution to the KSE and thus coincides with the posterior (
P
) (assuming that solutions to the KSE are unique).

Definition 28
.

[Linear Gaussian Setting]
When we speak of the linear Gaussian setting we mean that

•

\mathcal{A}:\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
is linear

•

\mathcal{B}\in\mathrm{L}\left(\mathscr{U};\mathscr{H}\right)
is a constant linear operator, i.e. it is independent of the state
u
.

•

H\in\mathrm{L}\left(\mathscr{H};\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}\right)
is a linear operator

•

the initial condition (of the signal and posterior)
u_{0}
is a Gaussian on
\mathscr{H}
, i.e.

\displaystyle u_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(m_{0},P_{0}\right)\penalty\ \text{with}\penalty\ m_{0}\in\mathscr{H}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ P_{0}\in\mathrm{L}\left(\mathscr{H};\mathscr{H}\right)\penalty\ \text{is symmetric positive semidefinite}.

In this linear Gaussian setting one can show (see e.g.
[
50
]
) that the solution of the KSE is a Gaussian
\mathcal{N}\left(m_{t},P_{t}\right)
, with mean
m
and covariance
P
given by the famous Kalman–Bucy equation

\mathrm{d}m_{t}=\mathcal{A}m_{t}\mathrm{d}t+P_{t}H^{\prime}R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-Hm_{t}\mathrm{d}t\right)

(50a)

\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathcal{A}P_{t}+P_{t}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}-P_{t}H^{\prime}R^{-1}_{t}HP_{t}+\left(\mathcal{B}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)\left(\mathcal{B}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\prime}.

(50b)

In our infinite dimensional variational setting these equations (
50
) were first studied by Bensoussan
[
4
, Théorème 3.1]
, proving existence and uniqueness of solutions. Now note that one can easily show that in the linear Gaussian setting of Definition
28
, the mean field EnKBF (
2
), which we write here again as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bar{u}_{t}=\mathcal{A}\bar{u}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{B}\mathrm{d}\bar{W}_{t}+\bar{P}_{t}H^{\prime}R^{-1}_{t}\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-H\frac{\bar{u}_{t}+\bar{m}_{t}}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right),

(51)

defines an (infinite dimensional) Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process. As one can easily verify with Itô’s formula, its (conditional) mean
\bar{m}
and covariance
\bar{P}
satisfy the Kalman–Bucy equations (
50
). Thus by the uniqueness of (
50
), the (conditional) law
\bar{\eta}_{t}
of
\bar{u}_{t}
has to coincide with the posterior (
P
)!

Appendix B
The Feedback Particle Filter

In the linear Gaussian setting (Definition
28
) the mean field EnKBF (
51
) describes a diffusion process with the remarkable property, that its (conditional) time-marginal laws are given by the desired posterior distribution. In the general setting this attribute is achieved by the Feedback Particle Filter (FPF). It is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\hat{u}_{t}&=\mathcal{A}(\hat{u}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{B}(\hat{u}_{t})\mathrm{d}\bar{W}_{t}\\
&\phantom{=}+\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-\frac{H(\hat{u}_{t})+\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[H(\hat{u}_{t})\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)+\frac{1}{2}\xi(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

(52)

where
\hat{\eta}_{t}
denotes the conditional distribution of
\hat{u}_{t}
. The so called gain term
\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t}):\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
is (not uniquely) determined by the weak differential equation

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]=\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\phi\left(H-\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[H\right]\right)^{\prime}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\penalty\ \text{for all Itô testfunctions}\penalty\ \phi.

(53)

Just as in section
2
we denote by
\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi
and
\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi
the first and second order Fréchét derivatives on the Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
.
The correctional drift term
\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t}):\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
is given by

\displaystyle\xi(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})

\displaystyle:=\left(\left\langle\mathfrak{K}\left(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t}\right),\nabla\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\mathfrak{K}\left(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t}\right)^{{}^{\prime}}\right)^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}\nu_{j},

where
\partial_{\nu_{k}}
denotes the derivative in direction of the basis vector
\partial_{\nu_{k}}
.

In the finite dimensional setting the FPF was first derived in
[
48
]
with an optimal control approach, independently and prior to this work a similar mean-field optimal filter for smoothed noise and finite dimensional signals has been found in
[
17
]
. In
[
38
]
various finite dimensional consistent mean field filters, among them the original FPF, have been derived by matching the strong Fokker–Planck equation of a diffusion process to the strong form of the KSE (
47
). Building on this work we now extend the FPF to infinite dimensions by showing that it describes the optimal filter, in the sense that the (conditional) law of (
52
) propagates in time exactly according to the KSE (
47
). However, since we are working in the infinite setting, we do so by matching the weak Fokker–Planck equation to the weak KSE (
47
).

Remark 29
.

The well posedness of the FPF (
52
) is an open problem, even in the much simpler finite dimensional case, and is thus just assumed in the following.

Lemma 30
.

Denote by
\left(\hat{\eta}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
the law of the FPF
\left(\hat{u}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, given by (
52
). We assume that for all times
t\geq 0
both
\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})
and
\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})
are well defined functions from
\mathscr{H}
into
\mathscr{H}
, that are integrable with respect to
\hat{\eta}_{t}
.

Then
\hat{\eta}
satisfies the KSE (
47
) for all Itô testfunctions (according to Definition
5
)
\phi:\mathscr{H}\to\mathbb{R}
, with the following properties

•

\phi
is integrable with respect to
\hat{\eta}_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

•

For all
v\in H
the Hessian
\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi(v)
is a self adjoint operator on
\mathscr{H}
.

•

The map
\hat{\Phi}:\mathscr{H}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
, defined by
\hat{\Phi}(v):=\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi(v),\mathfrak{K}(v,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}
, is an Itô function (componentwise) that is also integrable with respect to
\eta_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\phi:\mathscr{H}\to\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary, satisfying the properties specified above. We note by Itô’s formula, that the Kolmogorov forward equation describing the evolution of
\hat{\eta}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{t}[\phi]&=\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\mathcal{L}\phi\right]\mathrm{d}t+\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]\left(\mathrm{d}Y_{t}-\hat{\eta}_{t}[H]\mathrm{d}t\right)\\
&\phantom{=}+\frac{\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]+\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})R_{t}\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})^{\prime}\right]\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}-\frac{\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})(H-\hat{\eta}_{t}(H))\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]}{2}\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

(54)

Due to (
53
), the first line of (
54
) is exactly the KSE and thus, to show consistency, we only have to prove that the second line is zero. To this end we note that by Parseval we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})R_{t}\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{k},\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})R\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})^{\prime}\nu_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{k},\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}

Next we now that since
\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi
is self adjoint and by Parseval we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=\left\langle\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}.

This gives us by again using Parseval

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})R_{t}\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\left\langle\nu_{j},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\left\langle\partial_{\nu_{k}}\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right)R_{t}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}.

Using the product formula in Hilbert spaces
7
7

7

More precisely the formula for the directional derivative of the scalar product of two differentiable functions.
, we thus derive

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})R_{t}\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\partial_{\nu_{k}}\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}.

The map
\hat{\Phi}
, defined in the statement of the Lemma, then allows us to again use Parseval to derive

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}+\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})R_{t}\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\partial_{\nu_{k}}\hat{\Phi}\right)\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\hat{\Phi}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{y}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{y}}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\hat{\Phi}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\delta_{i}\delta_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle\nu_{k},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}^{\mathrm{T}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{y}}\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\hat{\Phi}\delta_{i},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\delta_{i},

where
\delta_{i},\penalty\ i=1,\cdots,d_{y}
denotes the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
. Thus, by the assumed regularity of
\hat{\Phi}
, we derive by using (
53
)

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\xi(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]+\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\mathrm{tr}_{\mathscr{H}}\left[\left(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi\right)\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})R_{t}\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})^{\prime}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{y}}\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\hat{\Phi}\delta_{i},\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]\delta_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{y}}\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\hat{\Phi}\delta_{i}\left(H-\hat{\eta}[H]\right)^{\prime}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{y}}\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\delta_{i}\left(H-\hat{\eta}[H]\right)^{\prime}\right]R^{-1}_{t}\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{y}}\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}R_{t}\delta_{i}\delta_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}R^{-1}_{t}\left(H-\hat{\eta}[H]\right)^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\hat{\eta}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi,\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\left(H-\hat{\eta}[H]\right)\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right],

which in turn lets us conclude that (
54
) coincides with the KSE (
47
) and thus the FPF is indeed consistent.
∎

The FPF is a true generalization of the EnKBF to general filtering problems and it even provides a connection between the EnKBF and the true posterior even in inconsistent setting as the following Lemma shows.

Lemma 31
.

Let again
\left(\hat{\eta}\right)_{t\geq 0}
be the (conditional) marginal laws to the FPF (
52
). Assuming integrability of
\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Y}\left[\mathfrak{K}(\hat{u}_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t})\right]=\hat{\eta}_{t}\left(\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right)=\mathbb{C}_{\hat{\eta}_{t}}\left[\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{H}},H\right]R^{-1}_{t}=\mathbb{C}_{Y}\left[\hat{u}_{t},H(\hat{u}_{t})\right]R^{-1}_{t}.

(55)

If
H
is linear and
\hat{\eta}_{t}
is Gaussian, one can even choose the gain term
K
such that
\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})=\mathbb{C}_{\hat{\eta}_{t}}\left[\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{H}},H\right]R^{-1}_{t}
. In the linear Gaussian setting the EnKBF is thus just a special case of the FPF.

Proof.

For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
we set
\phi_{i}(v):=\left\langle\nu_{i},v\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}
as a testfunction in the gain equation (
53
), then we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\nu_{i},\hat{\eta}_{t}\left(\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right)\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=\hat{\eta}_{t}\left(\left\langle\mathrm{D}^{1}_{\mathscr{H}}\phi_{i}\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \mathfrak{K}(\cdot,\hat{\eta}_{t})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right)=\hat{\eta}_{t}\left(\phi_{i}\left(H-\hat{\eta}[H]\right)^{\prime}\right)R^{-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nu_{i},\mathbb{C}_{\hat{\eta}_{t}}\left[\mathrm{id}_{H},H\right]R^{-1}_{t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}.

Since this holds for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
this indeed shows the validity of (
55
). The second claim follows from Gaussian integration by parts as in
[
12
]
.

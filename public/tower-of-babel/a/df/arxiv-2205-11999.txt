Dirac series of E 7 ​ ( − 5 ) E_{7(-5)}
Yi-Hao Ding, Chao-Ping Dong, Ping-Yuan Li

Abstract
. Using the sharpened Helgason-Johnson bound, this paper classifies all the irreducible unitary representations with non-zero Dirac cohomology of E 7 ​ ( − 5 ) E_{7(-5)} . As an application, we find that the cancellation between the even part and the odd part of the Dirac cohomology continues to happen for certain unitary representations of E 7 ​ ( − 5 ) E_{7(-5)} . Assuming the infinitesimal character being integral, we further improve the Helgason-Johnson bound for E 7 ​ ( − 5 ) E_{7(-5)} . This should help people to understand (part of) the unitary dual of this group.

1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in the representation theory of Lie groups is to understand the unitary dual
\widehat{G}
of a real reductive Lie group
G
. Here by
\widehat{G}
we mean the set of the equivalence classes of all the irreducible unitarizable
(\mathfrak{g},K)
modules. For simplicity, we assume that
G
is a connected semisimple Lie group with finite center. Given an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module
\pi
, it is highly possible that
\pi
is non-unitary, that is, it does not possess a positive definite invariant Hermitian form. Therefore, to arrive at the unitary dual of
G
from its admissible dual as classified by Langlands in 1970s, one needs an effective way to rule out the great many non-unitary representations.

Parthasarathy’s Dirac operator inequality
[
22
]
is a valuable tool on this aspect. Indeed, let
\theta
be a Cartan involution of
G
and assume that
K:=G^{\theta}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Let

(1)

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{0}

be the corresponding Cartan decomposition on the Lie algebra level. As usual, we will drop the subscripts to stand for the complexified Lie algebras. Fix a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{g}
, which is a scalar multiple of the Killing form on
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
so that each simple coroot has length
2
when
\mathfrak{g}
is simply-laced. Let
T_{f}
be a maximal torus of
K
, and put
A_{f}:=\exp(\mathfrak{a}_{f,0})
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{f,0}
is the centralizer of
\mathfrak{t}_{f,0}
in
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
. Then up to conjugation,
H_{f}:=T_{f}A_{f}
is the unique
\theta
-stable maximally compact Cartan subgroup of
G
. Namely,
H_{f}
is the fundamental Cartan subgroup of
G
. Let
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}_{f})
(resp.,
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
,
W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
) be the Weyl group of
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}_{f})
(resp.,
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
,
\Delta(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
). We fix a positive root system
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
once for all. Choose a positive restricted root system
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
containing the
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. Denote by
\rho
(resp.,
\rho_{c}
) the half sum of roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
(resp.,
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
).

Choose an orthonormal basis
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{m}
of
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
. Then the Dirac operator introduced by Parthasarathy
[
21
]
is

(2)

D:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{i}\otimes Z_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes C(\mathfrak{p}).

Here
C(\mathfrak{p})
is the Clifford algebra of
\mathfrak{p}
defined with respect to
B(\cdot,\cdot)|_{\mathfrak{p}}
. One sees easily that
D
is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Moreover,
D^{2}
is a natural Laplacian. Indeed,

(3)

D^{2}=-\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes 1+\Omega_{\mathfrak{k}_{\Delta}}+(\|\rho_{c}\|^{2}-\|\rho\|^{2})1\otimes 1.

Here
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
(resp.,
\Omega_{\mathfrak{k}_{\Delta}}
) is the Casimir operator of
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.,
\mathfrak{k}_{\Delta}
), and
\mathfrak{k}_{\Delta}
is a diagonal embedding of
\mathfrak{k}
into
U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes C(\mathfrak{p})
.

Let
S_{G}
be a spin module of
C(\mathfrak{p})
. Let
\widetilde{K}
be the subgroup of
K\times{\rm Pin}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})
consisting of the pairs
(k,s)
such that
{\rm Ad}(k)=p(s)
, where
{\rm Ad}:K\to O(\mathfrak{p}_{0})
is the adjoint action, and
p:{\rm Pin}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})\to O(\mathfrak{p}_{0})
is the double covering map.
Whenever
\pi
is unitary, by using the Hermitian form on
\pi\otimes S_{G}
and (
3
), one deduces Parthasarathy’s Dirac operator inequality:

(4)

\|\gamma+\rho_{c}\|\geq\|\Lambda\|.

Here
\Lambda
is the infinitesimal character of
\pi
, and
\gamma
is a highest weight of any
\widetilde{K}
-type of
\pi\otimes S_{G}
. Whenever there exists a
\gamma
such that (
4
) fails, one concludes that
\pi
is
not
unitary.

To sharpen the Dirac inequality, in 1997, Vogan introduced Dirac cohomology of
\pi
as

(5)

H_{D}(\pi)=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D/\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D\cap{\rm Im}\,D.

Since the operator
D
is invariant under the diagonal action of
K
given by adjoint actions on
U(\mathfrak{g})
and
C(\mathfrak{p})
, it follows that
\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D
,
{\rm Im}\,D
and thus
H_{D}(\pi)
are all
\widetilde{K}
modules.
Vogan conjectured that whenever non-zero, the Dirac cohomology
H_{D}(\pi)
should reveal the infinitesimal character of
\pi
. This conjecture was proven by Huang and Pandžić in 2002
[
16
]
. We regard
\mathfrak{t}_{f}^{*}
as a subspace of
\mathfrak{h}_{f}^{*}
by extending the linear functionals on
\mathfrak{t}_{f}
to be zero on
\mathfrak{a}_{f}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem 2.3 of
[
16
]
)

Let
\pi
be an irreducible (
\mathfrak{g}
,
K
) module with infinitesimal character
\Lambda
.
Assume that
H_{D}(\pi)\neq 0
, and let
\gamma\in\mathfrak{t}_{f}^{*}\subset\mathfrak{h}_{f}^{*}
be the highest weight of any
\widetilde{K}
-type in it. Then
\Lambda=w(\gamma+\rho_{c})
for some element
w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}_{f})
.

The above theorem establishes Dirac cohomology as an invariant of
\pi
. And a natural problem relevant to the study of the unitary dual is: Can we classify all the irreducible unitary representations of
G
with non-zero Dirac cohomology? Let us denote this set by
\widehat{G}^{d}
, and call it the
Dirac series
of
G
(after J.-S. Huang). It is worth noting that when
\pi
is unitary,
\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D\cap{\rm Im}\,D=\{0\}
and (
5
) simplifies as
H_{D}(\pi)=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D^{2}
. Moreover, it is exactly the members of
\widehat{G}
such that the Dirac inequality, which will be recalled in (
20
), becomes equality that consist of the Dirac series. Therefore,
\widehat{G}^{d}
cuts out the extreme members of
\widehat{G}
in the sense of Dirac inequality, and we expect the Dirac series to be interesting. For instance it contains the discrete series,
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
modules in the good range. Moreover, Example 6.3 of
[
8
]
suggests that Dirac series go beyond elliptic representations. Note that the latter have applications in endoscopy. In view of the research announcement by Barbasch and Pandžić
[
3
]
, Dirac series should have application in the theory of automorphic forms as well.

Recently, there has been progress on the classification of Dirac series. See
[
2
,
13
]
. The current paper is a continuation of this task on exceptional Lie groups. Here we study the group
E_{7(-5)}
, by which we actually mean the connected simple real exceptional Lie group
E7_q
in
atlas
. Its basic structure will be reviewed in Section
3
. Here
atlas

[
32
]
is a software which can now compute many questions pertaining to
(\mathfrak{g},K)
modules. In particular, it detects whether
\pi
is unitarizable or not based on the algorithm in
[
1
]
. We will give a brief account of it in Section
2.2
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module with
atlas
final parameter
p=(x,\lambda,\nu)
. We say that
\pi
is
fully supported
if the KGB element
x
has full support. That is, if
\texttt{support(x)}=[0,1,\dots,l-1]
, where
l=\mathop{\hbox{dim}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}}\,\mathfrak{h}_{f}
is the rank of
G
. Here
0,1,\dots,l-1
are the labels of certain simple roots (see Section
2.2
). Note that
atlas
always counts from zero. We say a member of
\pi\in\widehat{G}^{d}
is
FS-scattered
if it is fully supported. The Dirac series of
G
will be arranged into two parts: finitely many FS-scattered representations (which can not be cohomologically induced in the way of Theorem
2.1
), and finitely many
strings
(which are cohomologically induced from FS-scattered representations of certain Levi subgroups).
More careful explanation will be given in Section
2.3
. Now let us state our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The set
\widehat{E_{7(-5)}}^{d}
consists of
103
FS-scattered representations whose spin-lowest
K
-types are all unitarily small, and
1450
strings of representations. Moreover, each spin-lowest
K
-type of any Dirac series of
E_{7(-5)}
occurs exactly once.

The notions unitarily small
K
-type and spin-lowest
K
-type will be recalled in Section
3
. Note that besides
atlas
and the finiteness algorithm
[
11
]
for classifying Dirac series, the sharpened Helgason-Johnson bound
[
11
]
is a key tool as well.

The linear group
E7_q
studied here has a non-linear double cover
\widetilde{G}
. There should be interesting large families of genuine Dirac series of
\widetilde{G}
. Currently,
atlas
is unable to handle non-linear groups. But it is a valuable project for future to investigate the Dirac series of
\widetilde{G}
.

The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 collects some preliminaries for the entire paper. Section
3
reviews the structure of
E_{7(-5)}
. Section 4 classifies its Dirac series. Section 5 studies their Dirac indices, while Section
6
singles out the special unipotent representations. Section 7 further improves the Helgason-Johnson bound. We present all the FS-scattered representations of
E_{7(-5)}
in Section
8
.

2.
Preliminaries

This section collects necessary preliminaries. Note that by Theorem
1.1
, a necessary condition for
\pi
to have non-zero Dirac cohomology is that
\Lambda
is
real
in the sense of
[
28
, Definition 5.4.1]
. That is,
\Lambda\in i\mathfrak{t}_{f,0}^{*}+\mathfrak{a}_{f,0}^{*}
. If not stated otherwise, we will assume that
\Lambda
is real henceforth.

2.1.
Cohomological induction

Fix an element
H\in\mathfrak{t}_{f,0}
which is dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. Let
\mathfrak{l}
be the zero eigenspace of
{\rm ad}(H)
on
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\mathfrak{u}
be the positive eigenspaces.
Then

(6)

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{u}

is a
\theta
-stable parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. We choose a positive root system
(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
containing
\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
and
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. Let
L
be the normalizer of
\mathfrak{q}
in
G
. Then
L
is also connected and has a maximally compact subgroup
L\cap K
. Set

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t}_{f})=\Delta(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t}_{f})\cap(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f}).

Let
\rho^{L}
(resp.,
\rho(\mathfrak{u})
,
\rho^{\prime}
) be the half sum of roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
(resp.,
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
,
(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
). Then

\rho^{\prime}=\rho^{L}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}).

Cohomological induction is an important way of constructing representations of
G
from representations of
L
. Let
Z
be an irreducible
(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)
module with infinitesimal character

\Lambda_{Z}\in i\mathfrak{t}_{f,0}^{*}
. After
[
19
]
, we say that
Z
is in the
good range
(relative to
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{g}
) if

(7)

B(\Lambda_{Z}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha)>0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}_{f}).

If the above strict inequalities are replaced by
\geq
, we will say that
Z
is
weakly good
. Let
\mathfrak{z}
be the center of
\mathfrak{l}
. We say that
Z
is in the
fair range
if

(8)

B(\Lambda_{Z}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha|_{\mathfrak{z}})>0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}_{f}).

Similarly, we say that
Z
is
weakly fair
if the above strict inequalities are replaced by
\geq
.

Put
S
as the dimension of
\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{k}
. Cohomological induction functors (also called
Zuckerman functors
and
Bernstein functors
) lift
Z
to
(\mathfrak{g},K)
modules
\mathcal{L}_{j}(Z)
and
\mathcal{R}^{j}(Z)
, where
j
is a non-negative integer. We refer the reader to Chapter V of
[
19
]
for details. Here we simply recall that the most interesting case happens at the middle degree
S
. For instance, if
Z
is weakly good, then
\mathcal{L}_{j}(Z)
and
\mathcal{R}^{j}(Z)
vanish except for the degree
S
, while
\mathcal{L}_{S}(Z)\cong\mathcal{R}^{S}(Z)
is zero or irreducible with infinitesimal character
\Lambda_{Z}+\rho(\mathfrak{u})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}_{S}(Z)
is unitary provided
Z
is unitary. When
Z
is actually in the good range, we have that
\mathcal{L}_{S}(Z)
must be non-zero, and it is unitary if and only if
Z
is unitary.

In the special case that
Z
is a one dimensional unitary character
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
of
L
, we denote
\mathcal{L}_{S}(Z)
as
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
. This module has infinitesimal character
\lambda+\rho^{\prime}
. As shown by Salamanca-Riba in
[
26
]
, any irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module
\pi
with a strongly regular real infinitesimal character
\Lambda
must be isomorphic to an
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
module. Here
\Lambda
being
strongly regular
means that

B(\Lambda-\rho^{\prime},\alpha)\geq 0,\quad\forall\alpha\in(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f}).

In the next subsection, we will recall a canonical way of doing cohomological induction in the language of
atlas
.

2.3.
Organizing the Dirac series

Theorem
2.1
allows us to organize the irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
modules with a fixed infinitesimal character according to their supports. Then any
\pi
is either fully supported, or cohomologically induced within the weakly good range from a fully supported module on the Levi level.

It also hints us to organize the Dirac series of
G
in a similar way. As shown by Theorem A of
[
11
]
, the FS-scattered representations must be finitely many, and there is an algorithm to pin down them. Once some conditions have been verified for

(see Section
4.2
), those Dirac series which are
not
fully supported can be organized into finitely many strings, with each string being cohomologically induced from a FS-scattered representation of certain
L(x)
tensored with its unitary characters.

3.4.
Lambda norm, LKTs and infinitesimal character

The lambda norm of a
K
-type
\mu
was introduced by Vogan
[
28
]
. Choose an index
0\leq j\leq 62
such that
\mu+2\rho_{c}\in\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
.
Put

(16)

\lambda_{a}(\mu):=P(\mu+2\rho_{c}-\rho^{(j)}).

Here
P(\mu+2\rho_{c}-\rho^{(j)})
is the projection of
\mu+2\rho_{c}-\rho^{(j)}
onto the cone
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
, i.e., it denotes the unique point in the cone
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
which is closest to the point
\mu+2\rho_{c}-\rho^{(j)}
. It turns out that
\lambda_{a}(\mu)
is independent of the choice of an allowable index
j
. The
lambda norm
of
\mu
is defined as

(17)

\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}:=\|\lambda_{a}(\mu)\|.

The above geometric way of equivalently describing the lambda norm in
[
28
]
is due to Carmona
[
6
]
.

Now let
\pi
be an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module. A
K
-type
\mu
is called a
lowest
K
-type
(LKT for short) of
\pi
if
\mu
occurs in
\pi
and
\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}
attains the minimum among all the
K
-types of
\pi
. It is easy to see that
\pi
has finitely many LKTs.

Being important invariants of
\pi
, its infinitesimal character and LKTs are linked in the following way: Let
\mu
be one of the LKTs
\mu
of
\pi
, then a representative of the infinitesimal character of
\pi
can be chosen as

(18)

\Lambda=(\lambda_{a}(\mu),\nu)\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\mathfrak{t}^{*}+\mathfrak{a}^{*}.

Here, as in
[
28
]
,
G(\lambda_{a}(\mu))
is the isotropy group at
\lambda_{a}(\mu)
for the
G
action;
\mathfrak{h}
is the complexified Lie algebra of a maximally split
\theta
-stable Cartan subgroup
H=TA
of
G(\lambda_{a}(\mu))
. Note that
\nu
in (
18
) has the same norm as the
\nu
-part in the
atlas
final parameter

of
\pi
. We will not distinguish them by abusing the notation a bit.

3.5.
Spin norm, spin LKTs and Vogan pencil

Inspired by Vogan’s lambda norm, the second named author introduced spin norm in his 2011 HKUST thesis. See
[
9
]
. In the current setting, the
spin norm
of the
K
-type
E_{\mu}
specializes as

(19)

\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}=\min_{0\leq j\leq 62}\|\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}+\rho_{c}\|.

Note that
E_{\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}}
is the PRV component
[
24
]
of the tensor product of
E_{\mu}
with the contragredient
\mathfrak{k}
-type of
E_{\rho_{n}^{(j)}}
(which is just
E_{\rho_{n}^{(j)}}
in the current setting).

The spin norm of an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module
\pi
is defined as

\|\pi\|_{\rm spin}=\min\|\delta\|_{\rm spin},

where
\delta
runs over all the
K
-types of
\pi
.
If
\delta
attains
\|\pi\|_{\rm spin}
, we will call it a
spin-lowest
K
-type
of
\pi
. Again, it is easy to see that
\pi
has finitely many spin LKTs.

If
\pi
is further assumed to be unitary,
the original Dirac operator inequality (
4
) can be rephrased as

(20)

\|\pi\|_{\rm spin}\geq\|\Lambda\|.

Moreover, as shown in
[
9
]
, the equality happens in (
20
) if and only if
\pi
has non-zero Dirac cohomology, and in this case, it is exactly the spin-lowest
K
-types of
\pi
that contribute to

.

Since
E7_q
is not of Hermitian symmetric type, Lemma 3.4 of
[
27
]
says that the
K
-type
V_{\delta+n\beta}
must show up in
\pi
for any non-negative integer

provided that
V_{\delta}
occurs in
\pi
. We call them the
Vogan pencil
starting from

. Now it follows from (
20
) that

\|\delta+n\beta\|_{\rm spin}\geq\|\Lambda\|,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

In other words, whenever (
21
) fails, we can conclude that
\pi
is non-unitary. Distribution of spin norm along Vogan pencils has been discussed in Theorem C of
[
10
]
. In practice,
we will take
\delta
to be a lowest
K
-type of
\pi
and use the corresponding Vogan pencil to do non-unitarity test.

As noted in Section

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
G
be
E_{7(-5)}
, and let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module with infinitesimal character
\Lambda
which is given by (
18
). Assume that
\Lambda
is a non-negative integer combination of
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{7}

(26)

\|\nu\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{165}{2}},

then
\pi
is either trivial or minimal.

Note that
E_{7(-5)}
has a unique minimal representation, namely, the first entry of Table
12
. It has GK dim
17
, which equals to
B(\rho,\gamma_{7}^{\vee})
. Recall that
\gamma_{7}

defined in (
10
) is the highest root of
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
, and

Proof.

Take a LKT
\mu
of
\pi

). By the Dirac inequality (

\|\nu\|^{2}\leq\|\mu\|^{2}_{\rm spin}-\|\mu\|^{2}_{\rm lambda}.

As computed in Section 5.8 of
[

]
, we have that
\max\{A_{j}\mid 0\leq j\leq 62\}=82
. We refer the reader to Section 3 of
[

]
for the precise meaning of

. It follows that for any u-large

\mu
can

be u-large.

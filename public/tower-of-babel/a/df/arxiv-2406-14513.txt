On fibered Burnside rings, fiber change maps and cyclic fiber groups
Benjamín García, Alberto G. Raggi-Cárdenas

Abstract
Fibered Burnside rings appear as Grothendieck rings of fibered permutation representations of a finite group, generalizing Burnside rings and monomial representation rings. Their species, primitive idempotents and their conductors are of particular interest in representation theory as they encode information related to the structure of the group. In this note, we introduce fiber change maps between fibered Burnside rings, and we present results on their functoriality and naturality with respect to biset operations. We present some advances on the conductors for cyclic fiber groups, and fully determine them in particular cases, covering a wide range of interesting examples.

Burnside ring; fibered
G
-set; monomial representation

Introduction

The
A
-fibered Burnside ring of a finite group
G
, denoted by

, is defined as the Grothendieck ring of the category of finite
A
-fibered permutation representations of

, where
A
is an abelian group referred to as the fiber group. This ring was introduced by Dress in
[
7
]
as a generalization of the Burnside ring (where
A
is the trivial group) and the monomial representation ring (where
A=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
). It has been extensively studied as a commutative ring, as a Mackey functor, and as a global biset functor whose modules, known as fibered biset functors, are a source of important examples in the theory of biset functors (
[
3
]
,
[
12
]
).

The ring homomorphisms from
B^{A}(G)
to the field of complex numbers, known as the
species
of
B^{A}(G)
, as well as their respective primitive idempotents in
B^{A}_{\mathbb{K}}(G):=\mathbb{K}\otimes B^{A}(G)
for some splitting field
\mathbb{K}
, are particularly interesting since they are related to questions about solubility and nilpotency, among other aspects of the structure of the group
[
10
]
. The species and idempotents were determined by Boltje & Yılmaz in
[
4
]
under the assumption that the fiber group

has finite
\text{exp}(G)
-torsion. More precisely, the cyclotomic field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}[\zeta]
with

,

, is a splitting field for

; the primitive idempotents of

are in bijection with the species of
B^{A}(G)
, which are in one-to-one correspondence with the orbits of the set
\mathcal{D}^{A}_{G}=\left\{(H,\Phi)\;\middle|\;H\leq G,\Phi\in\text{Hom}(\text{Hom}(H,A),\mathbb{K}^{\times})\right\}
on which

acts by conjugation. The species and idempotent corresponding to the class of a pair

are denoted by

and

, respectively.

The conductor of the primitive idempotent

lies in

attached to the ring structure of

, hence preserved under isomophisms between fibered rings; they are known for

(
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
) and
D(G)

[
10
]

in

, play an important role in the computation of the conductors of

. More generally, if

, the ring

is often identified as a subring of

. In an even more general setting, any map of abelian groups

induces a

that happens to be a ring homomorphism preserving the standard basis.

The aim of this note is to present some results on fiber change maps and significant progress on the conductors of primitive idempotents for the case of a cyclic fiber group. We set up the background material in Sections 1 and 2. Section 1 is devoted to the study of the subgroups
G^{[n]}\unlhd G
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, defined as the minimal normal subgroup such that the quotient
G/G^{[n]}
is an
n
-torsion group, as well as their connection to the
n
-torsion of the Pontryagin dual
\text{Hom}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Section 2 is a summary of definitions and results on fibered Burnside rings and their standard bases, ghost rings, mark morphisms, and their biset functor structure.

In Section 3, we recall Boltje & Yılmaz’s formulae for species and idempotents, and we point out how they simplify for
A=C_{n}
, a cyclic group of order
n
. In this case, we take
\mathcal{D}^{C_{n}}_{G}
to be the set of all pairs
(H,hH^{[n]})
with
H\leq G
and

, whose corresponding species and primitive idempotent are denoted by

and

, respectively. Through Section 4, we study fiber change maps, and state some results about their functoriality and naturality with respect to biset operations in a wide generality. Concretely, we prove that fiber change is always a morphism of inflation functors, and that it is a morphism of biset functors if and only if

is injective on
\text{tor}(A)
. This last result will allow us to identify

as a subring of

commuting with all biset operations.

Finally, in Section 5 we focus on the conductors of

divides
[N_{G}(H,hH^{[n]}):H^{[n]}][H^{[n]}:H^{\prime}]_{0}

denotes the largest square-free divisor of

, and our main result states that these numbers are equal whenever
(n,\text{exp}(G)/n)=1
. Although the last condition might seem a bit too restrictive, it covers several cases of interest besides

and

is a field of positive characteristic

splits, the monomial

1
Abelianization and

Let

For a finite group

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

1.

G^{[exp(G)]}=G^{\prime}
,
G^{[1]}=G
;

2.

if
n
divides

, then
G^{[m]}\leq G^{[n]}
;

3.

if
H\leq G
, then
H^{[n]}\leq G^{[n]}
;

4.

(G/G^{\prime})^{[n]}=G^{[n]}/G^{\prime}
;

5.

G^{[n]}=G^{[n^{\prime}]}=G^{[n^{\prime\prime}]}
, where
n^{\prime}=(n,\text{exp}(G))
and
n^{\prime\prime}=(n,\text{exp}(G/G^{\prime}))
.

Proof.

Assertions 1 to 4 are straightforward. To prove 5, note that the containments
G^{[n]}\leq G^{[n^{\prime}]}\leq G^{[n^{\prime\prime}]}
are clear from part 2. Expressing

for some

, then for any

we have that

g^{n^{\prime\prime}}=(g^{a})^{n}(g^{b})^{\text{exp}(G/G^{\prime})}\equiv(g^{a})^{n}\;\text{mod }G^{\prime},

hence
G^{[n^{\prime\prime}]}\leq G^{[n]}
.
∎

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any abelian group
A
, there are natural isomorphisms

\text{Hom}(G,A)_{n}\cong\text{Hom}(G,A_{n})\cong\text{Hom}(G/G^{[n]},A_{n})\cong\text{Hom}(G/G^{[n]},A).

Proof.

The first isomorphism is clear as the operation in
\text{Hom}(G,A)
is pointwise multiplication of functions, thus
\text{Im}(\phi)\leq A_{n}
for all
\phi\in\text{Hom}(G,A)_{n}
. The second isomorphism follows by the universal property of

. The last isomorphism follows since the homomorphic image of an

∎

When

, the hom-set group

is the well-known

of

. The group

is isomorphic to the group

, for which

is cyclic of order

generated by a primitive

-th root of

. Moreover, for any field

, its group of torsion units
\text{tor}(\mathbb{K}^{\times})
can be embedded in
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
as a subgroup. This is clear if we consider the

-primary part of
\text{tor}(\mathbb{K}^{\times})
for each prime

, which is either cyclic of order a power of

or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
, the

-primary part of
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
n
a positive integer. Then

1.

Hom(G,C_{n})
and

are isomorphic;

2.

By the previous lemma,

Given a finite group

A

4
Fiber change maps

If
f:A\longrightarrow C
is a homomorphism of abelian groups, then
(H,f\circ\phi)\in\mathcal{M}^{C}_{G}
for all
(H,\phi)\in\mathcal{M}^{A}_{G}
. Moreover, if
(K,\psi)={{}^{g}(H,\phi)}
for some

f\circ\psi(k)=f(\phi(k^{g}))={{}^{g}(f\circ\phi)(k)}
for all

, proving that
(K,f\circ\psi)={{}^{g}(H,f\circ\phi)}
in
\mathcal{M}^{C}_{G}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
fiber change map induced by

is the application
f_{*}:B^{A}(G)\longrightarrow B^{C}(G)
sending
[H,\phi]_{G}
to
[H,f\circ\phi]_{G}
.

Fiber change maps are in fact ring homomorphisms, as we prove in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every homomorphism
f:A\longrightarrow C
of abelian groups, the fiber change map
f_{*}:B^{A}(G)\longrightarrow B^{C}(G)
is a ring homomorphism. Moreover, fixing a finite group
G
, the assignments
A\mapsto B^{A}(G)
and
f\mapsto f_{*}
define a functor from the category of abelian groups to the category of unital commutative rings.

Proof.

For all subcharacters
(H,\phi),(K,\psi)\in\mathcal{M}^{A}_{G}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{*}([H,\phi]_{G}[K,\psi]_{G})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in[H\backslash G/K]}[H\cap{{}^{g}K},f\circ(\phi|_{H\cap{{}^{g}K}}{{}^{g}\psi|_{H\cap{{}^{g}K}}})]_{G}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in[H\backslash G/K]}][H\cap{{}^{g}K},(f\circ\phi)|_{H\cap{{}^{g}K}}{{}^{g}(f\circ\psi)|_{H\cap{{}^{g}K}}}]_{G}

\displaystyle=f_{*}([H,\phi]_{G})f_{*}([K,\psi]_{G}),

and
f_{*}([G,1]_{G})=[G,1]_{G}
. The assertion on the functoriality is straightforward.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

The fiber change map
f_{*}:B^{A}(G)\longrightarrow B^{C}(G)
is injective if and only the restricted map

Suppose that

is injective on

is injective. Conversely, suppose that there is some

Recall that if

1.
Introduction

The terminology and notations used in this introduction can be found in Section 2.

The fact that the Walsh system is the group of characters of a compact abelian group connects Walsh analysis with abstract harmonic analysis was discovered independently by Fine
[
6
]
and Vilenkin
[
36
]
. For general references to the Haar measure and harmonic analysis on groups see Pontryagin
[
27
]
, Rudin
[
28
]
, and Hewitt and Ross
[
10
]
. In particular, Fine investigated the group
G
, which is a direct product of the additive groups
Z_{2}=:\{0,1\}
and introduced the Walsh system
\{{w}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
.

It is well-known that Walsh systems do not form bases in the
space
L_{1}.
Moreover, there is a function in the Hardy space
H_{1},

such that the partial sums of
f
are not bounded in the
L_{1}
-norm. Moreover, (see
[
34
]
) there
exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}\left(0<p<1\right),
such that

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\left\|S_{2^{n}+1}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

On the other hand, (for details see e.g. the books
[
29
]

and
[
37
]
and especially the newest one
[
26
]
) the subsequence
\{S_{2^{n}}\}
of partial
sums is bounded from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}
to the
space
H_{p},
for all
p>0,
that is the following inequality holds:

(1)

\left\|S_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{H_{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ \ p>0.

Weisz
[
38
]
proved that Fejér means of
Vilenkin-Fourier series are bounded from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}
to the
space
H_{p},
for
p>1/2.
Goginava
[
12
]
(see also
[
25
]
,
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
) proved that there exists a
martingale
f\in H_{1/2}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{1/2}=+\infty.

However, Weisz
[
38
]
(see also
[
22
]
) proved that for every
f\in H_{p},
there exists an absolute constant
c_{p},
such that the following inequality holds:

(2)

\left\|\sigma_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{H_{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ \ p>0.

Móricz and Siddiqi
[
16
]
investigated the approximation properties of some special Nörlund means of Walsh-Fourier series of
L_{p}
functions in
norm. Approximation properties for general summability methods can be found in
[
3
,
4
]
. Fridli, Manchanda and Siddiqi
[
9
]
improved and extended the results of Móricz and Siddiqi
[
16
]
to martingale Hardy spaces.
The case when
\left\{q_{k}=1/k:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
was
excluded, since the methods are not applicable to Nörlund logarithmic means. In
[
11
]
Gát and Goginava proved some
convergence and divergence properties of the Nörlund logarithmic means of
functions in the Lebesgue space
L_{1}.
In particular, they proved that there exists a function
f
in the space
L_{1},
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

In
[
5
]
(see also
[
17
]
) it was proved that there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p},\ \ (0<p<1)
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{p}=\infty.

A counterexample for
p=1
was proved in
[
24
]
.
However, Goginava
[
13
]
proved that for every
f\in H_{1},
there exists an absolute constant
c,
such that the following inequality holds:

(3)

\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{1}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

In
[
2
]
it was proved that for any
0<p<1,
there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

In
[
23
]
is was proved that for any non-decreasing sequence
(q_{k},k\in\mathbb{N})
satisfying the conditions

(4)

\frac{1}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\right)\text{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }q_{n}-q_{n+1}=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2-\alpha}}\right),\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty,

then, for every
f\in H_{p},
where
p>1/(1+\alpha),
there exists an absolute constant
c_{p},
depending only on
p,
such that the following inequality holds:

(5)

\left\|t_{n}f\right\|_{H_{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Boundedness does not hold from
H_{p}
to
weak-L_{p},
for
0<p<1/(1+\alpha).

As a consequence, (for details see
[
39
]
) we get that the Cesàro means
\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
H_{p}
to
L_{p},
for
p>1/(1+\alpha),
but they are not bounded from
H_{p}
to
weak-L_{p},
for
0<p<1/(1+\alpha).
In the endpoint case
p=1/(1+\alpha),
Weisz and Simon
[
31
]
proved that the maximal operator
\sigma^{\alpha,\ast}
of Cesàro means define by

\sigma^{\alpha,\ast}f:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|\sigma^{\alpha}_{n}f|

is bounded
from the Hardy space
H_{1/\left(1+\alpha\right)}
to the space
weak-L_{1/\left(1+\alpha\right)}.
Goginava
[
14
]
gave a counterexample,
which shows that boundedness does not hold for
0<p\leq 1/\left(1+\alpha\right).

In this paper we develop some methods considered in
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
(see also the new book
[
26
]
) and prove that for any
0<p<1,
there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|t_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Moreover, we prove that a big class of subsequence
\{t_{2^{n}}f\}
of Nörlund means with respect to the Walsh
system generated by non-increasing and convex sequences are not bounded from the martingale Hardy spaces
H_{p}
to the space
weak-L_{p}
for
0<p<1/(1+\alpha)
, where
0<\alpha<1
. Moreover, some new related inequalities are derived. As application, some well-known and new results are pointed out for well-known summability methods, especially for Nörlund logarithmic means and Cesàro means.

The main results in this paper are presented and proved in Section 4. Section 3 is used to present some auxiliary results, where, in particular, Lemma 2 is new and of independent interest. In order not to disturb our discussions later on some definitions and notations are given in Section 2.

2.
Definitions and Notations

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.
Denote by
Z_{2}
the discrete cyclic
group of order 2, that is
Z_{2}:=\{0,1\},
where the group operation is the
modulo 2 addition and every subset is open. The Haar measure on
Z_{2}
is
given so that the measure of a singleton is 1/2.

Define the group
G
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{2},

with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{2}
‘s.

The elements of
G

are represented by sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{j},...),\ \ \ \text{ where }\ \ \ x_{k}=0\vee 1.

It is easy to give a base for the neighborhood of
x\in G
namely:

I_{0}\left(x\right):=G,\text{ \ }I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G:y_{0}=x_{0},...,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\}\text{ }(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right),

\overline{I_{n}}:=G

\backslash

I_{n}
and

e_{n}:=\left(0,...,0,x_{n}=1,0,...\right)\in G,\ \ \text{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}.

If
n\in\mathbb{N},
then every
n
can be uniquely expressed as

n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{j}2^{j},
where
n_{j}\in Z_{2}\ (j\in\mathbb{N})

and only a finite numbers of
n_{j}
differ from zero. Let

\left|n\right|:=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\ n_{k}\neq 0\}.

The norms (or quasi-norms) of the spaces
L_{p}(G)
and
weak-L_{p}\left(G\right),

\left(0<p<\infty\right)
are, respectively, defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}^{p}:=\int_{G}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu\ \ \ \text{
and}\ \ \ \left\|f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}^{p}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{p}\mu\left(f>\lambda\right).

The
k
-th Rademacher function is defined by

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\left(-1\right)^{x_{k}}\text{\qquad}\left(\text{ }x\in G,\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now, define the Walsh system
w:=(w_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G
as:

w_{n}(x):=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\Pi}}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)=r_{\left|n\right|}\left(x\right)\left(-1\right)^{\underset{k=0}{\overset{\left|n\right|-1}{\sum}}n_{k}x_{k}}\text{
\qquad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

It is well-known that (see e.g.
[
29
]
) the Walsh system is orthonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G\right).
Moreover, for any
n\in\mathbb{N},

(6)

\displaystyle w_{n}\left(x+y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{n}\left(x\right)w_{n}\left(y\right).

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right)
we define the Fourier coefficients, partial sums and Dirichlet kernel by

\displaystyle\widehat{f}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{G}fw_{k}d\mu\,\,\,\,\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right),

\displaystyle S_{n}f

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)w_{k},\ \ \ D_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}w_{k\text{ }}\,\,\,\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

Recall that (for details see e.g.
[
29
]
):

(7)

D_{2^{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2^{n},&\,\text{if\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}x\in I_{n}\\
0,&\text{if}\,\,x\notin I_{n}\end{array}\right.

and

(8)

D_{n}=w_{n}\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{k}r_{k}D_{2^{k}}=w_{n}\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{k}\left(D_{2^{k+1}}-D_{2^{k}}\right),\text{ for }n=\overset{\infty}{\underset{i=0}{\sum}}n_{i}2^{i}.

Let
\left\{q_{k},\ k\geq 0\right\}
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers.
The Nörlund means for the Fourier series of
f
are defined by

t_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n-k}S_{k}f,\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}.

In this paper we consider convex
\left\{q_{k},\ k\geq 0\right\}
sequences, that is

q_{n-1}+q_{n+1}-2q_{n}\geq 0,\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

If the function
\psi(x)
is any real valued and convex function (for example
\psi(x)=x^{\alpha-1},\ 0\leq\alpha\leq 1
), then the sequence
\{\psi(n),\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is convex.

Since

q_{n-2}-q_{n-1}\geq q_{n-1}-q_{n}\geq q_{n}-q_{n+1}\geq q_{n+1}-q_{n+2}

we find that

q_{n-2}+q_{n+2}\geq q_{n-1}+q_{n+1}

and we also get that

(9)

q_{n-2}+q_{n+2}-2q_{n}\geq 0,\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

In the special case when
\{q_{k}=1,\ k\in\mathbb{N}\},
we have the Fejér means

\sigma_{n}f:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}f.

Moreover, if
q_{k}={1}/{(k+1)}
, then we get the Nörlund logarithmic means:

(10)

L_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{S_{k}f}{n+1-k},\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}.

The Cesàro means
\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}
(sometimes also denoted
\left(C,\alpha\right)
) is also well-known example of Nörlund means defined by

\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}f=:\frac{1}{A_{n}^{\alpha}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}A_{n-k}^{\alpha-1}S_{k}f,

where

A_{0}^{\alpha}:=0,\qquad A_{n}^{\alpha}:=\frac{\left(\alpha+1\right)\ldots\left(\alpha+n\right)}{n!},\qquad\alpha\neq-1,-2,\ldots

It is well-known that

(11)

A_{n}^{\alpha}=\overset{n}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}A_{n-k}^{\alpha-1},\ \ \ A_{n}^{\alpha}-A_{n-1}^{\alpha}=A_{n}^{\alpha-1}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ A_{n}^{\alpha}\sim n^{\alpha}.

We also define
U_{n}^{\alpha}
means as

U^{\alpha}_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}{(n+1-k)}^{(\alpha-1)}S_{k}f\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\alpha-1}.

Let us also define
V_{n}^{\alpha}
means as

V_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}{\ln(n+1-k)}S_{k}f\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\ln(k+1)}.

Let
f:=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
be a martingale with respect to
\digamma_{n}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right),
which are generated by the intervals
\left\{I_{n}\left(x\right):x\in G\right\}
(for details see e.g.
[
37
]
).

We say that this martingale belongs to the Hardy martingale spaces
H_{p}\left(G\right),
where
0<p<\infty,
if

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}:=\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{p}<\infty,\ \ \ \text{with}\ \ \ f^{\ast}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|f^{(n)}\right|.

In the case
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right),
the maximal functions are also
given by

M(f)\left(x\right):=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu\left(I_{n}\left(x\right)\right)}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)d\mu\left(u\right)\right|\right).

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right),
then it is easy to show that the sequence
F=\left(S_{2^{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a martingale and
F^{*}=M(f).

If
f=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a
martingale, then the Walsh-Fourier coefficients must be defined in a slightly
different manner:

\widehat{f}\left(i\right):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{G}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)w_{i}\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right).

A bounded measurable function
a
is
p
-atom, if there exists an interval
I,

such that

\text{ supp}\left(a\right)\subset I,\ \ \ \int_{I}ad\mu=0\text{ \ and \ }\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq\mu\left(I\right)^{-1/p}.

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}\backsim\inf\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{k}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

4.
The Main result

In previous Sections we have discussed a number of inequalities and sometimes their sharpness.
Our main result is the following new sharpness result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
,
\beta
be any non-negative real number and
t_{n}
be Nörlund means with convex and non-increasing sequence
\left\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
satisfying the condition

(13)

\frac{q_{1}-({3}/{2})q_{3}}{Q_{n}}\geq\frac{C}{n^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}n},

for some positive constant
C.
Then, for any
0<p<1/(1+\alpha)
there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|t_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Proof.

Let
0<p<1/(1+\alpha)
. Under condition (
13
) there exists a sequence
\left\{n_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
such that

\frac{2^{2n_{k}(1/p-1)}}{n_{k}Q_{2^{2n_{k}+1}}}\geq\frac{2^{2n_{k}(1/p-1-\alpha)}}{n_{k}^{\beta+1}}\to\infty,\ \ \text{as}\ \ k\to\infty.

Let
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subset\left\{n_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be an increasing sequence of
positive integers such that

(14)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{-p/2}<\infty,

(15)

\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{\left(2^{2\alpha_{\eta}}\right)^{1/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}<\frac{\left(2^{2\alpha_{k}}\right)^{1/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}

and

(16)

\frac{\left(2^{2\alpha_{k-1}}\right)^{1/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}<\frac{q_{1}-q_{3}-({3}/{2})q_{5}}{Q_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-3}}{\alpha_{k}}.

Let

f^{\left(n\right)}:=\sum_{\left\{k;\text{ }2\alpha_{k}<n\right\}}\lambda_{k}a_{k},

where

In a concrete case we get a result for Nörlund logarithmic means
\left\{L_{n}\right\}
proved in

]
:

Corollary 1
.

Let
0<p<1.
Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Proof.

It is easy to show that

q_{1}-({3}/{2})q_{3}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{8}>0,

and condition (
13
) holds true for
\alpha=\beta=0.

∎

We also get similar new result for the
V_{n}
means:

Corollary 2
.

Let
0<p<1.
Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|V_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Proof.

It is easy to show that

q_{1}-({3}/{2})q_{3}=\frac{1}{\ln 2}-\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\ln 4}=\log_{2}^{e}-(3/2)\frac{\log_{2}^{e}}{\log_{2}^{4}}=\log_{2}^{e}\left(1-\frac{3}{4}\right)>0,

and condition (

) holds true for
\alpha=\beta=0.

∎

We also get a corresponding new result for the Cesàro means
\sigma^{\alpha}_{2^{n}}.

Corollary 3
.

Let
0<p<1/(1+\alpha),
for some
0<\alpha\leq 0.56.
Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\sigma^{\alpha}_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Proof.

By a routine calculation we find that

q_{1}-({3}/{2})q_{3}=\alpha-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)}{4}=\alpha\cdot\frac{2-3\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{4}.

It is easy to show that when

this expression is positive.
Hence, condition (
13
) holds true for
\beta=0
and
0<\alpha<1.

∎

Corollary 4
.

Let
0<p<1/(1+\alpha),
for some
0<\alpha\leq 0.41.
Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|U^{\alpha}_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Proof.

By a straightforward calculation we find that

q_{1}-({3}/{2})q_{3}=2^{1-\alpha}-({3}/{2})4^{1-\alpha}=2^{1-\alpha}\left(1-3/2^{2-\alpha}\right).

It is easy to show that when

\beta=0

∎

Quasi-parametric rates for Sparse Multivariate Functional Principal Components Analysis
Ryad Belhakem

Abstract
This work aims to give non-asymptotic results for estimating the first principal component of a multivariate random process. We first define the covariance function and the covariance operator in the multivariate case. We then define a projection operator. This operator can be seen as a reconstruction step from the raw data in the functional data analysis context. Next, we show that the eigenelements can be expressed as the solution to an optimization problem, and we introduce the LASSO variant of this optimization problem and the associated plugin estimator. Finally, we assess the estimator’s accuracy. We establish a minimax lower bound on the mean square reconstruction error of the eigenelement, which proves that the procedure has an optimal variance in the minimax sense.



1
Introduction

We consider the
D-
multivariate setting and our object of interest is functional data observed on a fixed discretization grid defined as
\{t_{h}=\frac{h}{p-1};h=0,\dots,p-1\}
. For
D\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we assume the observations to be corrupted by random noise. Hence we consider the following statistical model: We have for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and all
h\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
:

\displaystyle\textbf{Y}_{i}(t_{h})

\displaystyle=(Y_{i,1}(t_{h}),\dots,Y_{i,D}(t_{h}))

\displaystyle=(Z_{i,1}(t_{h})+\epsilon_{i,1,h},\dots,Z_{i,D}(t_{h})+\epsilon_{i,D,h})

\displaystyle=\textbf{Z}_{i}(t_{h})+\bf{\epsilon}_{i,h},

(1)

meaning for each component
d\in\{1,\dots,D\}
and for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have:

Y_{i,d}(t_{h})=Z_{i,d}(t_{h})+\varepsilon_{i,d,h},

with the
\bf{\varepsilon}_{i,h}
’s being independent centered Gaussian vectors of errors with covariance
\sigma^{2}I_{D}
. The errors are assumed to be independent of the
\textbf{Z}_{i}
’s and the
\textbf{Z}_{i}
’s are independent and identically distributed with the same distribution as
Z
. Here, we assume that the grid is fixed and regular with
p
points, note that our approach could be generalized to the case where
|t_{h}-h/p|\leq 1/p
. With the modern ability to record multiple data streams at a very fine timescale, the need for new tools to perform analysis on all these streams at once has arisen. From a functional data analysis (FDA) perspective, these data can be considered as realizations of a multivariate random process on a dense grid. In this context, Multivariate functional principal components analysis (MfPCA) provides an interesting analysis framework.
As MfPCA is an extension of functional principal component analysis (fPCA) to the multivariate case, it inherits most of its properties
regarding representation. Thus as fPCA, it is used as a preprocessing tool. For example, we can cite the modeling and forecasting of multi-population mortality (see
Lam and Wang 2021
) and identification of biomarkers for accurate diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease in an early stage (see
Happ and Greven 2018
). The notable advantage of such an approach is its capacity to handle all the data presented at once, i.e., accounting for variability through time (intra-functional correlations) and space (correlations between functions).

1.1
Motivation and model

To the best of our knowledge, only a few articles investigate convergence rates. In
Happ and Greven 2018
a truncation approach was adopted; this can be formulated as a two-step procedure. The authors first fix
({\phi}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
an orthonormal basis of the space considered, then they choose
M
components, i.e.
\{\phi_{\lambda_{1}},\dots,\phi_{\lambda_{M}}\}
, and they project the curves on those
M
components. Finally they compute the eigenfunction after the projection step. The obtained rates using this approach are at best of the order of
O(\frac{M^{3}}{n})
.
In (
Li and Hsing 2010
and
Chiou et al. 2014
) a nonparametric approach was adopted, based on kernel estimation, assuming that the covariance is twice differentiable. This results in rates depending on the size of the bandwidth
h
of the order of
O_{p}(h^{4}+\frac{\log(n)}{nh})
.
In
Belhakem et al. 2021
we showed that in the univariate case, assuming
Z
to be a
\alpha-
Hölder continous function, the non-penalised estimator converges at minimax rates (
O(n^{-1})+O(p^{-2\alpha})
), however in the
D
-dimensional context, the same approach gives rates of order (
D^{2}n^{-1}+D^{2}p^{-2\alpha}
). So, in a high dimensional context where
D\gg n
, those rates are non-relevant.

The motivation of this work is to define an estimation process for the principal component that produces relevant results despite the high dimensional setting, and to investigate the effect of the noise (which is assumed to be Gaussian and i.i.d),
n
the number of replicates,
p
the size of the grid, the sparsity, and the regularization on the estimator of the first principal component in the multivariate setting.

In this article, we establish a minimax lower bound on the reconstruction error of the first eigenelement. Next, we extend the estimation process seen in
Belhakem et al. 2021
to the multivariate case. Once the estimator of the covariance operator is defined, we focus our research on establishing a new optimization problem that computes the first principal component. Finally, we base our methodology on a Lasso-penalized M-estimator. We show that any stationary point achieves the minimax optimal variance. The primary conditions we impose are: regularity of
Z
to be at least a
\alpha-
Hölder continuous function, sparsity in the first eigenfunction that cannot be larger than
\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log(pD)}
, and the dimension
D
cannot be larger than
p^{\alpha}
.

Definitions

In what follows, we define the space
\mathbb{H}
, the scalar product of
\mathbb{H}
and the norm associated with it and we explicit the form of the covariance function and the covariance operator.

Definition 1
.

Let
D\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we consider
\textbf{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{D})^{T},\text{with all $f_{d}$'s}\in L^{2}([0,1])

and
\textbf{g}=(g_{1},\dots,g_{D})^{T},\text{with all $g_{d}$'s}\in L^{2}([0,1])
, two vectors of functions. We define
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
such that:

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{f},\textbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{1}f_{d}(t)g_{d}(t)dt,

\displaystyle\|\textbf{f}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d=1}^{D}\|f_{d}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=\langle\textbf{f},\textbf{f}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},

and
\mathbb{H}
such that:

\mathbb{H}:=\big\{\textbf{f}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{D};\quad\|\textbf{f}\|_{\mathbb{H}}<\infty\big\}.

In the sequel we assume that
\textbf{Z}\in\mathbb{H}
and we assume that
Z
is a centered random continuous function such that
\mathbb{E}[\|\textbf{Z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}]\leq\infty
. Note that as defined
(\mathbb{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}})
is a separable Hilbert space.

Notations:

We denote
\|\cdot\|
the
\mathbb{L}^{2}:=L^{2}([0,1])
-norm and
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{2}}
the
\ell_{2}
-norm for a vector. For
P
a probability measure, we denote

\mathbb{E}
the associated expectation. We denote
P_{\textbf{Z}}
the distribution of the process
Z

and
\mathbb{E}_{\textbf{Z}}
the associated expectation. Also we define the norms on
\mathbb{H}
such that
\|\textbf{h}\|_{1}=\sum_{d=1}^{D}\|h_{d}\|_{1}
,
\|\textbf{h}\|_{\infty}=\max_{d\in\{1,\dots,D\}}\|h_{d}\|_{\infty}
and
\|\textbf{h}\|_{0}=\sum_{d=1}^{D}\mathbf{1}_{h_{d}\neq 0}
for any
\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H}
. We define
sign(u)=1_{\{u\geq 0\}}-1_{\{u<0\}}
for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2
.

We define
K
the covariance function of
Z
such that for all
(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2}
and for all
(d,d^{\prime})\in\{1,\dots,D\}^{2}
:

\displaystyle K_{d,d^{\prime}}(s,t):=(K(s,t))_{d,d^{\prime}}=\mathbb{E}(Z_{d}(s)Z_{d^{\prime}}(t)),

and

K(s,t)=\mathbb{E}(\textbf{Z}(s)\textbf{Z}(t)^{T})

with the associated integral operator
\Gamma
, such that for all
\textbf{f}\in\mathbb{H}
,

\displaystyle\quad\Gamma(\textbf{f})(\bullet)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}K(s,\bullet)\textbf{f}(s)ds,

We also define the Hilbert-Schmidt norm and the associated scalar product:
let
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\in L(\mathbb{H})
the space of linear operators on
\mathbb{H}
, when it makes sense,

\langle\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\rangle_{HS}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\langle\Gamma(\textbf{e}_{i}),\Gamma^{\prime}(\textbf{e}_{i})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},

denoting by
(\textbf{e}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{H}
. Note that the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{HS}
is independent of the choice of the basis,
and
\|\cdot\|_{HS}=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{HS}
is the associated norm.

The operator
\Gamma
is well defined since
\mathbb{E}[\|\textbf{Z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}]<\infty
. Also note that, as defined,
K
is symmetric, in the sense that we have:

\forall(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2}\quad K(s,t)=K(s,t)^{T},

which implies that the operator
\Gamma
is a linear self-adjoint operator.
Note that in this setting, Mercer’s theorem is still valid (see
Chiou et al. 2014
), i.e., there exists a sequence of orthonormal functions
(\textbf{f}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
and positive numbers
(\mu_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
(eigenfunctions and the associated eigenvalues) such that

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\mu_{\ell}\textbf{f}_{\ell}\otimes\textbf{f}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\textbf{g}_{\ell}\otimes\textbf{g}_{\ell},

where
\textbf{g}_{\ell}:=\sqrt{\mu_{\ell}}\textbf{f}_{\ell}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\otimes
is the tensorial product defined such that for any
\textbf{h},\textbf{f},\textbf{g}\in\mathbb{H}
:

\quad\textbf{h}\otimes\textbf{f}(\textbf{g})=\langle\textbf{h},\textbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\textbf{f}.

(2)

The eigenfunctions
(\textbf{f}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
are ordered such that the associated eigenvalues sequence is non-increasing. We also suppose that all the eigenvalues are distinct, i.e., for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\quad\mu_{\ell}>\mu_{\ell+1}
.

3
Definition of the smoothness class for the functional curve
Z

The convergence rates depend on the underlying smoothness of the process of interest.

Definition 3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
L>0
. We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}^{(D)}(L)=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbb{P}\text{ probability measure on }\mathbb{H}\text{ (with $\mathbb{P}_{d}$ its marginal on the d-th direction)}\right.

\displaystyle\text{such that}\left.\forall s,t\in[0,1],\int_{\mathbb{H}}\max_{d\in\{1,\dots,D\}}(z_{d}(t)-z_{d}(s))^{2}d\mathbb{P}_{d}(z)\leq L|t-s|^{2\alpha}\right\}.

The use of these regularity sets is natural. Indeed, we can, for instance, remark that
P_{\textbf{Z}}
, the distribution of
Z
, satisfies.

\mathbb{P}_{\textbf{Z}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}^{(D)}(L)\Rightarrow\max_{d\in\{1,\dots,D\}}\mathbb{E}_{\textbf{Z}}[(Z_{d}(t)-Z_{d}(s))^{2}]\leq L|t-s|^{2\alpha}\quad\forall s,t\in[0,1].

This regularity condition implies that the kernel
K
is bounded and it is a
\alpha-
Hölder continous function, for any
(s,s^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\in[0,1]^{4}
:

\mathbb{P}_{\textbf{Z}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}^{(D)}(L)\Rightarrow\max_{d,d^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,D\}}|K_{d,d^{\prime}}(s,t)-K_{d,d^{\prime}}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})|\leq(L\|K\|_{\infty})^{1/2}(|t^{\prime}-t|^{\alpha}+|s^{\prime}-s|)^{\alpha},

(4)

where
\|K\|_{\infty}=\max_{d,d^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,D\}}\|K_{d,d^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}
and
\|K_{d,d^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2}}|K_{d,d^{\prime}}(s,t)|
.

Remark
.

The class
R_{\alpha}^{(D)}(L)
can be seen as extension of
R_{\alpha}(L)
defined in
Belhakem et al. 2021
to the multivariate case. Thus it inherits all of the properties of
R_{\alpha}(L)
, i.e., Gaussian processes such as Brownian motion and fractional Brownian motion belong to
R_{\alpha}^{(D)}(L)
for
\alpha
and
L
well chosen.

Definition of the estimators of the eigenelements

Following the approach adopted in
Belhakem et al. 2021

\Gamma

\widetilde{Y}_{i,d}(t):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{M}}\widetilde{y}_{i,d,\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}(t),\quad\widetilde{y}_{i,d,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{h=0}^{p-1}Y_{i,d}(t_{h})\phi_{\lambda}(t_{h}),\quad t\in[0,1],

\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{i}\in\mathbb{H}
and the vectors
\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}

(\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{i})_{d}:=\widetilde{Y}_{i,d}\quad,(\widetilde{{y}}_{i,d})_{\lambda}:=\widetilde{y}_{i,d,\lambda}\quad\text{and }(\widetilde{\textbf{y}}_{i})_{d}:=\widetilde{y}_{i,d}.

Thus

is a smoothed version of the raw data. Then we define a natural estimator of the covariance kernel

as follows:

\widehat{K}_{\phi}(s,t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{i}(t)^{T}\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{i}(s),\quad(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2},

(5)

and derive from it an estimator of the covariance operator
\widehat{{\Gamma}}_{\phi}

\displaystyle\widehat{{\Gamma}}_{\phi}(\textbf{f})(\cdot)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{K}_{\phi}(s,\cdot)\textbf{f}(s)ds,\quad\textbf{f}\in\mathbb{H}.

Since the kernel
\widehat{K}_{\phi}
is symmetric, the operator
\widehat{\Gamma}_{\phi}
is self-adjoint and it is also a finite-rank hence compact operator since
{\rm Im}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\phi})\subset\text{span}\{\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}_{n}\}
. From the spectral theorem, we know that there exists a basis
(\widehat{\textbf{f}}_{\phi,\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
of
\mathbb{L}^{2}([0,1])

\widehat{\Gamma}_{\phi}

(\widehat{\mu}_{\phi,\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
the associated eigenvalues sorted in non-increasing order. Finally, with a slight abuse of notation, we denote the vectors containing all the observations by
(\textbf{Y}_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
, i.e., let
j\in\{1,\dots,pD\}
we denote by
q
and
r
the qotient and the rest of the euclidean division of

by
D
, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

(Y_{i})_{j}:=Y_{i,r}(t_{q}).

Optimization problem

Our approach mimics the one adopted by
Janková and van de Geer 2021
, which relies heavily on the Taylor expansion in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
of the function:

\beta\rightarrow\|\Sigma-\beta^{T}\beta\|_{F}^{2},\quad\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p},

where
\Sigma
is a covariance matrix and
\|\bullet\|_{F}
is the Frobenius norm. We first defines a functional counterpart of this function, for this purpose, recall the tensor product notation
\otimes
, given in
2
. Note that
\textbf{h}\otimes\textbf{f}
defines a linear operator verifying
\|\textbf{h}\otimes\textbf{f}\|_{HS}=\|\textbf{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\textbf{f}\|_{\mathbb{H}}<\infty
. We now introduce the objective function
R
such that

\quad R(\textbf{h}):=\|\Gamma-\textbf{h}\otimes\textbf{h}\|_{HS}^{2},\quad\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H}.

(6)

Extending this approach depends on our ability to use the Taylor expansion and the Taylor-Lagrange remainder in
\mathbb{H}
, however in

Zeidler 1985
Chapter 4 (theorem 4.5) an extension to Banach spaces of the Taylor expansion is defined, and since
R
is a real-valued function, we know that the mean value theorem is valid; thus, the existence of the Taylor-Lagrange remainder is guaranteed.

Theorem 2
.

Let
R:U\subset\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a map defined on an open convex set

U
of a Banach space
\mathbb{H}
. We suppose that
R
is
n-
times differentiable. Then the Taylor formula
holds true for all
(\textbf{u},\textbf{h})\in(U\times\mathbb{H})
such that
\textbf{h}+\textbf{u}\in U
:

R(\textbf{u}+\textbf{h})=R(\textbf{u})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{dR_{\textbf{u}}^{(k)}{(\textbf{h}^{k})}}{k!}+\Xi_{n},

where we set
dR_{\textbf{u}}^{(k)}(\textbf{h}^{k}):=dR_{\textbf{u}}^{(k)}(\textbf{u})\underbrace{(\textbf{h},\dots,\textbf{h})}_{k-\text{times}}
, and the remainder has the following form:

\Xi_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\tau)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}dR_{\textbf{u}+\tau\textbf{h}}^{(n)}(\textbf{h}^{n})d\tau.

Since
R
is a real-valued function, we can apply the mean value theorem on
\Xi_{n}
which means that there exists
\tau^{*}\in[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\tau)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}dR_{\textbf{u}+\tau\textbf{h}}^{(n)}(\textbf{h}^{n})d\tau

\displaystyle=dR_{\textbf{u}+\tau^{*}\textbf{h}}^{(n)}(\textbf{h}^{n})\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\tau)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{dR_{\textbf{u}+\tau^{*}\textbf{h}}^{(n)}(\textbf{h}^{n})}{n!}.

This allows us to deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
R:U\subset\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a map defined on an open convex set

U
of a Banach space
\mathbb{H}
. We suppose that
R
is
n-
times differentiable. Then the Taylor formula
holds true for all
(\textbf{u},\textbf{h})\in(U\times\mathbb{H})
such that
\textbf{h}+\textbf{u}\in U
. There exists
\tau^{*}\in[0,1]
such that:

R(\textbf{u}+\textbf{h})=R(\textbf{u})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{dR_{\textbf{u}}^{(k)}(\textbf{h}^{k})}{k!}+\Xi_{n},

(7)

where we set
dR_{\textbf{u}}^{(k)}(\textbf{h}^{k}):=dR_{\textbf{u}}^{(k)}(\textbf{u})\underbrace{(\textbf{h},\dots,\textbf{h})}_{k-\text{times}}
, and the remainder has the following form

\Xi_{n}:=\frac{dR_{\textbf{u}+\tau^{*}\textbf{h}}^{(n)}(\textbf{h}^{n})}{n!}.

Remark

In what follows we will use Corollary

One key element of the proof is the sub-differential of the
1

Definition 4
.

Let
\textbf{F}:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex function, a sub-differential at point

is a function denoted
\partial\textbf{F}(\textbf{h}_{0})

\forall\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H}\quad\textbf{F}(\textbf{h})-\textbf{F}(\textbf{h}_{0})\geq\langle\partial\textbf{F}(\textbf{h}_{0}),\textbf{h}-\textbf{h}_{0}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

In what follows we compute
\partial\|\bullet\|_{1}
. Let
\textbf{g},\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H}
. First we define the
Sign

\displaystyle Sign(\textbf{g})

\displaystyle=(sign(g_{1}),...,sign(g_{D})).

Note that we have the following equality for any

\displaystyle\|g\|_{1}=\max_{\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\langle g,h\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1])}=\langle sign(g),g\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1])}.

This implies that :

\displaystyle\|\textbf{g}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d=1}^{D}\|g_{d}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d=1}^{D}\max_{\|h_{d}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\langle g_{d},h_{d}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1])}

\displaystyle=\max_{\|\textbf{h}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\langle\textbf{g},\textbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\langle Sign(\textbf{g}),\textbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

\langle Sign(\textbf{g}),\textbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\|\textbf{g}\|_{1}

\|\textbf{g}\|_{1}=\max_{\|\textbf{h}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\langle\textbf{g},\textbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\langle Sign(\textbf{g}),\textbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

\displaystyle\forall\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H}\quad\|\textbf{h}\|_{1}-\|\textbf{g}\|_{1}\geq\langle Sign(\textbf{g}),\textbf{h}-\textbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

However since
\|\textbf{g}\|_{1}=\langle Sign(\textbf{g}),\textbf{g}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\forall\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H}\quad\|\textbf{h}\|_{1}\geq\langle Sign(\textbf{g})),\textbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},

{R}_{\phi}
the functions defined for any
\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H}
by

\widehat{R}_{\phi}(\textbf{h}):=\|\widehat{\Gamma}_{\phi}-\textbf{h}\otimes\textbf{h}\|_{HS}^{2},

(12)

and

{R}_{\phi}(\textbf{h}):=\|{\Gamma}_{\phi}-\textbf{h}\otimes\textbf{h}\|_{HS}^{2},

(13)

where
\Gamma_{\phi}=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Gamma}_{\phi}]
. We investigate the statistical properties of a LASSO variant of this optimization problem, i.e.:

\displaystyle\widehat{{\textbf{g}}}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\underset{\textbf{h}\in\mathcal{B}(\eta),\|\textbf{h}\|_{1}\leq T}{\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,\min}}}\widehat{R}_{\phi}(\textbf{h})+\lambda\|\textbf{h}\|_{1},

(14)

where
(T,\lambda)
are tuning parameters and for some

we define
\mathcal{B}(\eta):=\{\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{H},\|\textbf{h}-\textbf{g}_{1}\|\leq\eta\}
to be a small ball containing
\textbf{g}_{1}
. The constraint

might seem unnecessary or at least redundant with the penalty

, we impose it due to the non-convexity of

. From a theoretical point of view, it is essential to derive guarantees on
\widehat{\textbf{g}}
. In the sequel we will show that

is convenient. Thus, this constraint is not restrictive. Note that similar constraints were found in
Janková and van de Geer 2021
and
Loh and Wainwright 2015
.

Remark
.

This optimization problem can be seen as the functional counterpart of the multivariate PCA problem (see
Janková and van de Geer 2021
section 3.1). The Hilbert-Schmidt norm is considered in the literature as an extension of the Frobenius norm to the compact operators.

Remark
.

The existence and the form of
\mathcal{B}(\eta)
might seem problematic regarding the feasibility of this approach in practice. To overcome this difficulty we compute a pre-estimate
\textbf{g}_{init}
using an other algorithm (The one provided by
Hall et al. 2006
was shown to provide good estimate regardless of the dimension
D
). Finally, to create
\mathcal{B}(\eta)
, one would replace
\textbf{g}_{1}
by
\textbf{g}_{init}
in
\mathcal{B}(\eta)
.

The Co-Points of Rays are Cut Points of Upper Level Sets for Busemann Functions
Sorin V. SABAU

Abstract
We show that the co-rays to a ray in a complete non-compact Finsler manifold contain geodesic segments to upper level sets of Busemann functions. Moreover, we characterise the co-point set to a ray as the cut locus of such level sets. The structure theorem of the co-point set on a surface, namely that is a local tree, and other properties follow immediately from the known results about the cut locus. We point out that some of our findings, in special the relation of co-point set to the upper lever sets, are new even for Riemannian manifolds.

Introduction

Roughly speaking, a Busemann function is a function that measures the distance to a point at infinity on a complete boundaryless non-compact Riemannian or Finsler manifold. Originally introduced by H. Busemann for constructing a theory
of parallels for straight lines (see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
10
]
), the function plays a fundamental role in the study of complete non-compact Riemannian or Finsler manifolds (see
[
8
,
10
,
11
]
, etc).

In the present paper, we study the differentiability of the Busemann function in terms of co-rays and co-points to a ray in the general case of a forward complete non-compact Finsler manifold. We show that the notions of geodesic segments to a closed subset and the cut locus of such sets can be extremely useful in the study of co-rays and co-points to a ray, that is points where Busemann function is not differentiable.

The originality of our research is two folded. Firstly, the detailed study of Busemann functions, co-rays and co-points on Finsler manifolds is new. Secondly, in the special case of Riemannian manifolds, our main Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, first statement, are new and they lead to new elementary proofs of other results already known.

Let
(M,F)
be a forward complete boundaryless Finsler manifold. A unit speed globally minimising geodesic
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to M
is called a
(
forward
)
ray
. A ray
\gamma
is called

maximal
if it is not a proper sub-ray of another ray, i.e.,
for any
\varepsilon>0
its extension to
[-\varepsilon,\infty)
is not a ray
anymore. Moreover, let us assume that
(M,F)
is bi-complete, i.e., forward and backward complete.
A Finslerian unit speed globally minimising geodesic
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
is called a
straight line
. We point out that, even though for defining rays and straight lines we not need any completeness hypothesis, without completeness, introducing rays and straight lines would be meaningless.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to M
be a given forward ray and let

x
be a point on a non-compact forward complete
Finsler manifold
(M,F)
.
Then, a forward ray
\sigma\colon[0,\infty)\to M

is called a

forward co-ray
(
or a forward asymptotic ray
)
to
\gamma
if
there exists a sequence of minimal geodesics
\{\sigma_{j}\}
from
q_{j}:=\sigma_{j}(0)
to
\sigma_{j}(l_{j}):=\gamma(t_{j})
, for some divergent sequence of numbers
\{t_{j}\}
, such that
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}q_{j}=\sigma(0)
and
\dot{\sigma}(0)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dot{\sigma}_{j}(0)
.

A co-ray to
\gamma
is called

if
for any
\varepsilon>0
its extension to
[-\varepsilon,\infty)
is not an
co-ray to
\gamma
anymore.
The origin points of maximal co-rays of
\gamma
are
called the

to
\gamma
(a slightly stronger definition can be found in
[
8
]
).

Similarly, one can define
asymptotic straight lines
. If
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
is a straight line in a bi-complete Finsler manifold, then the straight line
\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
is an asymptotic straight line to
\gamma
if
\sigma|_{[0,\infty)}
is asymptotic ray to
\gamma|_{[0,\infty)}
, and
\bar{\sigma}|_{[0,\infty)}
is asymptotic ray to
\bar{\gamma}|_{[0,\infty)}
with respect to the

\bar{F}(x,y):=F(x,-y)
, where
\bar{\sigma}(t):=\sigma(-t)
and
\bar{\gamma}(t):=\gamma(-t)
,

are the reverse rays of
\sigma
and
\gamma
, respectively (see
[
8
]
for details). This definition makes sense because if
\sigma
is a geodesic for
F
, then the reverse curve
\bar{\sigma}(t)
is geodesic for
\bar{F}
.

If
\gamma
is a forward ray in the forward complete boundaryless non-compact Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, then the
Busemann function
is defined by

x\in M\mapsto\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(x):=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\{t-d(x,\gamma(t))\}
, where

is the Finsler distance function (see Section

Let us recall from
[
12
]
some notions that will be useful later.

Let
N\subset M
be a closed subset of
M

is called a

-segment

if
d(\alpha(t),N)=a-t
holds on

, where

for any

-segments from any

is called a

of

along the

-segment

, i.e.,
any geodesic extension

-segments. Observe that

If a point

We point out that in

Notice that, since we consider only boundaryless manifolds, any geodesic segment on a compact interval admits forward and backward local geodesic extensions even if the manifold

Here are the main results of our paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,F)
be a forward complete boundaryless Finsler manifold and let
\alpha\colon[0,a]

\to M
be a unit-speed geodesic.
The following three statements are equivalent.

1.

\alpha
is a subarc of a co-ray to
\gamma
.

2.

\alpha

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(\alpha(s))=s+\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(\alpha(0))

(1.1)

3.

\alpha

-segment, where

From here the relation between co-points to a forward ray and the cut points of a level set of Busemann function naturally follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,F)
be a forward complete boundaryless Finsler manifold, and
\gamma
a ray in
M

1.

For every
b\in\mathbb{R}
, the set of co-points of
\gamma

is exactly the cut locus of the subset

\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\cap\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(-\infty,b)={\mathcal{C}}_{N_{\gamma}^{b}}

Moreover,

{\mathcal{C}}_{N_{\gamma}^{b}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{N_{\gamma}^{c}}
, for any

.

2.

The Busemann function

is differentiable at a point

of

if and only if

admits a unique co-ray
\sigma
to
\gamma
emanating from

. In this case

.

Loosely speaking,

, where

denotes the cut locus of

These two main theorems make possible to apply known results about the

Seen in this light, the proof of the structure theorem for the co-point set on a Finsler surface, namely that is a local tree, becomes trivial.

(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma},\delta)

Busemann functions

Let
(M,F)
be a forward complete boundaryless non-compact Finsler manifold (see
[
1
,
9
]
for details on the completeness of Finsler manifolds).
In Riemannian geometry, the forward and backward completeness are equivalent, hence the words “forward” and “backward” are superfluous, but in Finsler geometry these are not equivalent anymore.

Definition 2.1
.

If
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to M
is a ray in a forward complete boundaryless non-compact Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, then the function

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}\colon\ M\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(x):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\{t-d(x,\gamma(t))\}

(2.1)

is called
the Busemann function with respect to

\gamma
, where
d
is the Finsler distance function.

The Busemann function for Finsler manifolds
was introduced and partially studied by Egloff
[
3
]
and
more recently by
[
8
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

1.

The limit in (
2.1
) always exists because the
function

t\mapsto t-d(x,\gamma(t))

is monotone nondecreasing and bounded above by
d(\gamma(0),x)
.

2.

Obviously
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(\gamma(t))=t
, for all
t\geq 0
.
Moreover, if
\gamma_{0}
is a sub-ray of the ray
\gamma
, then
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma_{0}}(x)=\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(x)-t_{0}
for any point
x\in M
, where
t_{0}\geq 0
is the parameter value on
\gamma
such that
\gamma_{0}(0)=\gamma(t_{0})
.

It follows that
a point
x
of
M
is an element of
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(a,\infty)
, for some real number

,
if and only if
t-d(x,\gamma(t))>a
for some

, and
hence we get

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbf{b}^{-1}_{\gamma}(a,\infty)=\bigcup_{t>0}B_{t}^{-}(\gamma(t+a))

holds, where
B_{t}^{-}(\gamma(t+a)):=\{x\in M\ |\ d(x,\gamma(t+a))<t\}
denotes the backward open ball centred at

of radius

.
In particular
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(a,\infty)
is arcwise connected for each

.

The triangle inequality implies

Lemma 2.4
.

The function
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}
is locally Lipschitz, i.e.,

\displaystyle-d(x,y)\leq\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(x)-\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(y)\leq d(y,x)

for any two points

.

The differentiability of Busemann function is fundamental for
the study of co-rays. Some results are already known (see for instance

is finite.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let

Then, the of co-point set

of

1.

2.

+\infty,\qquad\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

By Theorem

Theorem 2.14

Let

Theorem 3.1

Let

\gamma

.

If for some given

Proof.

For the number

given, we define the set

\displaystyle S_{c}:=\big\{q\in\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(c)\,|\,q\textrm{ belongs to some co-ray to }\gamma\textrm{ emanating from a point in }\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\big\}.

For later use we also define

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}:=\{x\in M\,|\,\textrm{there is a maximal co-ray }\sigma_{x}\colon(a,\infty)\to M,\textrm{ passing through }x,

\displaystyle\hphantom{\widetilde{M}:=\lx@text@lbrace}{}\textrm{ for some }a\in[-\infty,\infty)\}.

Remark that if

always in the definition of
\widetilde{M}
, but since we assume only forward completeness here, a finite value for

We will divide the proof in two steps.

Step 1.

Firstly, we prove that

\displaystyle S_{c}=\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(c).

In the case

, we prove this by showing the followings

(i)

the set

is non-empty,

(ii)

S_{c}
is open in
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(c)
,

(iii)

\widetilde{M}
is closed in

,

(iv)

\widetilde{M}
is open in
M
.

Proof of
(i). Firstly, we show that
S_{c}\neq\varnothing
. Indeed, taking into account the hypothesis, we can consider a point
p\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\cap\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(-\infty,c]
. If
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(p)=c
then

and there is nothing to prove.

We can therefore assume
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(p)<c
, that is there exists
l>0
such that
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(p)=c-l
.

Since
p\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
, we consider the maximal co-ray
\sigma\colon[0,\infty)\to M
from
p
to
\gamma
and let
q
be the point on
\sigma
such that

\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(\sigma(s))=s+\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(p)
implies
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(q)=d(p,q)+\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(p)=d(p,q)+c-l=c
and hence

and

. These show that

is non-empty and (i) is proved.

Proof of
(ii). Next, we prove by contradiction that

is open. Indeed, assuming by contradiction

\{q_{j}\}\subset\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(c){\setminus}S_{c}
such that

. We denote by
\sigma_{j}
and
\sigma
the co-rays passing through
q_{j}
and
q
, respectively. Let
x
be the initial point of
\sigma
, and by our assumption
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\cap\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(-\infty,c]
.

Consider now a scalar

and the forward closed ball
\overline{\mathcal{B}^{+}(q,r)}:=\{p\!\in\!M\,|\,d(q,p)\leq r\}
. Obviously
\overline{\mathcal{B}^{+}(q,r)}
is compact due to the forward complete hypothesis and the Hopf–Rinow theorem, and

.

Let
\sigma_{j}
denote a co-ray to
\gamma
emanating from

q_{j}=\sigma_{j}(0)
.
Since
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}^{+}(q,r)}
is compact and
q_{j}\notin S_{c}
, we can extend backward
\sigma_{j}
to some interval

with
d(q,\sigma_{j}(s_{j}))=r+\delta
.
Any limit geodesic of the sequence

\{\sigma_{j}\}
is a co-ray passing through

which
contains

It follows that

must be open set and (ii) is proved.

Proof of
(iii). Now we show that
\widetilde{M}
is closed in

. Indeed let

be a set of points in
\widetilde{M}
, such that
x_{j}\to x

M

. Obviously, such

.

By extracting some sub-sequence of

\sigma_{j}|_{[0,\infty)}
converges to some
\sigma_{x}|_{[0,\infty)}
.

We will show that

. As the sequence
\{x_{j}\}
was arbitrary, this would imply

closed. So assume by contradiction

x\in M{\setminus}\widetilde{M}
. This means that the domain of definition of the maximal co-ray extension of

is the interval

On the other hand, for

Thus, the (unique) maximal co-ray through

Proof of
(iv). Finally, we prove

Consider a sequence

, and consider the maximal co-rays

\sigma_{j}\colon[a_{j},\infty)\to M

and definition of
\widetilde{M}

Observe that the sequence of points

Using these we will build our argument as follows.

Reminding ourselves that a topological space

In other words, what we have proved in Step 1 is that

Using this we proceed to proving that

For each

From hypothesis 2 of the theorem, there exists a subsequence

2. The fact that

Remark 3.8

We recall that an

Here we prove

(M,F)

If
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}=\varnothing
, then
M
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(0)
.

2.

If
M
has at least three ends, then there are no differentiable Busemann functions on
M
.

Proof.

1. Since
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}=\varnothing
, it follows that
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}
is smooth everywhere and hence from each point there is a unique co-ray to
\gamma
. Thus, we can define the function

\varphi\colon M\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(0)
,
p\mapsto\varphi(\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}(p),h_{1}(p))
,
where
h_{1}(p)
is the intersection point of the co-ray from
p
with the level set

\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{-1}(0)
. From the bi-completness hypothesis it follows that
h_{1}(p)
always exists. We can easily see that this is a homeomorphism by using the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}
.

2. Due to statement 1 it follows that if
\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}
is differentiable, then
M
have at most two ends. Statement 2 follows by logical negation.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

It is known that the cut locus of a point in a Riemannian or Finsler manifold
M
is a closed subset of
M
(see
[
1
]
).
On the other hand, we have shown in
[
12
]
, by an example, that the cut locus of a closed subset in
M
is not closed in
M
anymore. A natural question is if the co-point set
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
is closed or not.
First to answer to this question is Nasu who constructed in
[
7
]
an example of Riemannian complete surface with a ray
\gamma
whose co-point set
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
is
not closed
.
Obviously, the same conclusion can be derived from our Theorem
1.2
and
[
12
]
.

Abstract
. We present a systematic algebraic approach for the weak coupling of Cauchy problems to multiple Lohe tensor models. For this, we identify an admissible Cauchy problem to the Lohe tensor (LT) model with a characteristic symbol consisting of four tuples in terms of a size vector, a natural frequency tensor, a coupling strength tensor and admissible initial configuration. In this way, the collection of all admissible Cauchy problems to the LT models is equivalent to the space of characteristic symbols. On the other hand, we introduce a binary operation, namely “ fusion operation ” as a binary operation between the characteristic symbols. It turns out that the fusion operation satisfies the associativity and admits the identity element in the space of characteristic symbols which naturally forms a monoid. By virtue of the fusion operation, the weakly coupled system of multiple LT models can be obtained by applying the fusion operation of multiple characteristic symbols corresponding to the LT models. As a concrete example, we consider a weak coupling of the swarm sphere model and the Lohe matrix model, and provide a sufficient framework leading to emergent dynamics to the proposed weakly coupled model.

A complex rank-
m
tensor can be visualized as a multi-dimensional array of complex numbers with multi-indices. The
“rank
” (or

[k]:=\{1,\cdots,k\}\subset\mathbb{N},\quad\mbox{for $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Let
T
be a rank-
m
tensor with a size
d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}
. Then, we denote
(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m})
-th component of
T
by
[T]_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}}
, and we also denote
\overline{T}
by the rank-
m
tensor whose components are simply the complex conjugate of the corresponding elements in
T
:

[\overline{T}]_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}}:=\overline{[T]_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}}},\quad\alpha_{i}\in[d_{i}],\quad i\in[m].

In other words, each component of
\overline{T}
is defined as the complex conjugate of the corresponding element of
T
. Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{m}(\mathbb{C};d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m})
be the collection of all rank-
m
tensors with size
d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}
and complex entries. Then, it is easy to see that
{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
is a complex vector space. Throughout the paper, for notational convenience, we use a handy notation:

{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}:={\mathcal{T}}_{m}(\mathbb{C};d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}).

Furthermore, we also introduce the following handy notation: for
T\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
and
A\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}\times d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
, we set

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &[T]_{\alpha_{*}}:=[T]_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{m}},\quad[T]_{\alpha_{*0}}:=[T]_{\alpha_{10}\alpha_{20}\cdots\alpha_{m0}},\quad[T]_{\alpha_{*1}}:=[T]_{\alpha_{11}\alpha_{21}\cdots\alpha_{m1}},\\
&[T]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}:=[T]_{\alpha_{1i_{1}}\alpha_{2i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{mi_{m}}},\quad[T]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}:=[T]_{\alpha_{1(1-i_{1})}\alpha_{2(1-i_{2})}\cdots\alpha_{m(1-i_{m})}},\\
&[A]_{\alpha_{*}\beta_{*}}:=[A]_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{m}\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{m}}.\end{aligned}

Moreover, we can endow an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\textup{F}}
, namely
“Frobenius inner product”
and its induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\textup{F}}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
: for
T,S\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
,

\langle T,S\rangle_{\textup{F}}:=\sum_{\alpha_{*}\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\{1,\cdots,d_{i}\}}[\overline{T}]_{\alpha_{*}}[S]_{\alpha_{*}},\quad\|T\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}:=\langle T,T\rangle_{\textup{F}}.

Let
A_{j}
be a
block skew-Hermitian
rank-
2m
tensor with size
(d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m})\times(d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m})
satisfying the relation:

[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=-[\overline{A}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}\alpha_{*0}}.

In other words, if two blocks with the first
m
-indices are interchanged with the rest
m
-indices, then it results in the negation of the complex conjugate of itself.

Now, we are ready to introduce the LT model on the finite ensemble
\{T_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
of rank-
m
tensors:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}[\dot{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}}=[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}\\
\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\frac{\kappa_{i_{*}}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left([T_{k}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}-[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}\right)~~t>0,\\
T_{j}(0)=T_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\cdots\times d_{m}},\quad\|T_{j}^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
A_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}\times d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
is a rank-
2m
natural frequency tensor satisfying the block skew-hermitian property:

[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=-[\overline{A}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}\alpha_{*0}},

and we used the Einstein summation convention for repeated indices in the right-hand side of (
1.1
).

Although the LT model (
1.1
) looks pretty complicated, coupling terms inside the parenthesis of (
1.1
) are designed to incorporate the coupling terms for the Kuramoto model, the swarm sphere model and the Lohe matrix model, and they certainly have “gain cubic term
-
loss cubic term” structure. These careful designs of couplings (or interactions) yield the existence of a constant of motion:

\|T_{j}(t)\|_{\textup{F}}=\|T_{j}(0)\|_{\textup{F}},\quad t\geq 0,\quad j\in[N].

In this paper, we are interested in the weak coupling of the LT models. For instance, the following question naturally arises:

“
How to couple two swarm sphere models or two Lohe matrix models weakly?

Here, the adverb “weakly” means that when we coupled two aggregation models, we do not change the underlying interaction structures, and the effect of other states is reflected on parameters such as coupling strengths or natural frequency tensors. As briefly discussed in Section
2
, weak couplings of two swarm sphere models and two Lohe matrix models are recently investigated in
[
27
]
and
[
15
]
under the names of “
double sphere model”
and “
double matrix model”
, respectively. In fact, the aforementioned weak couplings are for the LT models with the same rank structures. Then, how about the weak coupling of the swarm sphere model and the Lohe matrix model? We will see the affirmative answer for this question at the end of this paper.

In this paper, we address the following two simple questions:

•

(Q1): For a given Cauchy problem to the LT model (
1.1
), which set of parameters characterize the Cauchy problem?

•

(Q2): For a collection of Cauchy problems to the LT model, can we define a suitable weak coupling of two Cauchy problems to the LT models for possibly different rank tensors?

Next, we briefly outline the main results dealing with affirmative answers for (Q1) and (Q2) by developing an algebraic approach for the weak coupling of two Cauchy problems. First, we introduce
the characteristic symbol
denoted by
\mathfrak{C}
for each admissible Cauchy problem to the LT model (
1.1
) consisting of
four components
: a size vector
\mathbf{d}
, a coupling strength tensor
\mathfrak{K}
, set of natural frequency tensors
\{A_{j}\}
and initial configuration
\{T_{j}^{0}\}
:

\Big\{\mbox{Cauchy problems \eqref{LT} for the LT model}\Big\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{C}:=(\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{A_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\}).

If a solution
T_{j}
to (
1.1
) belongs to
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
, then one can associate a vector in
\mathbb{N}^{m}:=\underbrace{\mathbb{N}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{N}}_{m~\mbox{times}}
:

\mathbf{d}:=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m},

and we call it as the
size vector
of (
1.1
). For the LT model (
1.1
), there are
2^{m}
coupling strengths denoted
\{\kappa_{i_{*}}\}_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}
. Thus, we collect all the coupling strengths and named it as the
coupling strength tensor
:

\mathfrak{K}:=(\kappa_{i_{*}})_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}.

Then,
\mathfrak{K}
is a rank-
m
real tensor. Lastly, the sets of natural frequency tensors and initial configuration are included in the characteristic symbol (see Definition
3.2
). Thus, once the Cauchy problem to the LT model is given, then we can uniquely assign the associated characteristic symbol up to some equivalence relations. On the contrary, if the characteristic symbol is given, we can find the corresponding Cauchy problem to the LT model. In this way, we can naturally consider a bijective map between the collection of all admissible Cauchy problems to the LT models and the collection of characteristic symbols.

Second, we introduce an algebraic operation denoted by
\star
which we call the
fusion operation
on the set of characteristic symbols, and we show that the proposed fusion operation forms a monoid on its domain, i.e., it is associative and admits the identity element. However, an inverse element does not exist. Thus, the set of characteristic symbols equipped with
\star
forms a monoid. Thanks to this algebraic machinery on the space of characteristic symbols, we can weakly couple two Cauchy problems to the LT models for rank-
m_{1}
tensors
\{T_{j}^{1}\}
and rank-
m_{2}
tensors
\{T_{j}^{2}\}
. We call the resulting coupled system as
the double tensor model
where the interaction between
T_{j}^{1}
and
T_{j}^{2}
is involved in a classical way (see (
3.1
)). After tedious manipulation, we can also find a governing equation for
T_{j}:=T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}
(see (
3.4
)). Thus, we would say that the equation for
T_{j}
is obtained by
coupling
two LT models. Since the LT model corresponds to the unique characteristic symbol up to an equivalence relation, coupling two LT models allow us to define the fusion operation. For a detailed discussion, we refer the reader to Section
4.1
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section

In Section

Preliminaries

In this section, we discuss two concrete examples for the weak couplings of first-order aggregation models for low-rank tensors to motivate the subsequent sections. More precisely, we deal with weak couplings for the following two models:

•

Weak coupling of two swarm sphere models with distinct sets of free flows for rank-one real tensors with the same size.

•

Weak coupling of two Lohe matrix models with distinct sets of free flows for rank-two unitary tensors with the same size.

For each case, we present the corresponding weakly coupled systems and review their emergent dynamics and relation to the LT model (

2.1.
Weak coupling of two swarm sphere models

The swarm sphere model describes aggregation phenomenon on the unit sphere embedded in Euclidean space.
Consider two ensembles
\{u_{j}\}

\Omega_{j}^{\top}=-\Omega_{j},\quad\Lambda_{j}^{\top}=-\Lambda_{j},\quad j\in[N],

Weak coupling of two Lohe matrix models

(2)

(Orbital stability): there exist unitary matrices
X^{\infty}\in{\mathbf{U}}(d_{1})
and
Y^{\infty}\in{\mathbf{U}}(d_{2})
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{U}_{i}(t)-U_{i}(t)X^{\infty}\|_{\textup{F}}=0\quad\textup{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{V}_{i}(t)-V_{i}(t)Y^{\infty}\|_{\textup{F}}=0.

Similar to Proposition
2.2
, we finally state the relation between the double matrix model (
2.7
) and the LT model in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
15
]
Systems (
1.1
) and (
2.7
) are equivalent in the following sense:

(1)

Suppose
\{(U_{i},V_{i})\}
is a solution to (

) with the initial data

. Then, a rank-4 tensor

defined by

is a solution to (

) with a well-prepared free flow tensor

and the initial data

.

(2)

Suppose a rank-4 tensor
T_{i}
is a solution to (
1.1
) with a suitable

and quadratically separable initial data:

for rank-2 tensors

3.
Characteristic symbol for the Cauchy problem

In this section, we provide a concept of characteristic symbol for the Cauchy problem to the LT model (
1.1
) via system parameters and initial data.

Recall the Cauchy problem to the LT model for rank-
m
tensors:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}[\dot{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}}=[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}\\
+\displaystyle\sum_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\frac{\kappa_{i_{*}}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left([T_{k}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}-[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}\right),~t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
T_{j}(0)=T_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\cdots\times d_{m}},\quad\|T_{j}^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
A_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}\times d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
is a rank-
2m
natural frequency tensor satisfying block skew-hermitian property:

[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=-[\bar{A}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}\alpha_{*0}},\quad\alpha_{*0}=(\alpha_{10},\cdots,\alpha_{m0}),\quad\alpha_{*1}=(\alpha_{11},\cdots,\alpha_{m1})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}[d_{i}].

(3.2)

3.1.
Four components of characteristic symbol

In this subsection, we introduce four components consisting of characteristic symbol for the Cauchy problem (
3.1
)–(
3.2
). In what follows, we fix the number of particles
N
.

\bullet
(Size vector and rank): Since a solution
T_{j}
to (
3.1
) belongs to
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
, we can naturally associate
a size vector
and
a rank
to (
3.1
):

\displaystyle\mbox{(Size vector)}~~\mathbf{d}:=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m};

\displaystyle\mbox{(Rank)}~~m:=\textup{number of components in the size vector}.

For simplicity, we use the notation
m:=\textup{dim}(\mathbf{d})
. Here, we assume that
d_{i}\geq 2
for
i\in[m]
. Among
d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{m}
, if there is one index
k\in[m]
such that
d_{k}=1
, for instance,
d_{m}=1
, then we regard a rank
m
-tensor
T_{i}
as a rank
m-1
-tensor
\tilde{T}_{i}
by the natural reduction:

[\tilde{T}]_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}=[T]_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{m}}.

This basically holds since
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots d_{m-1}\times 1}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots d_{m-1}}
. Thus, there is no information loss from the reduction above.
In particular, when scalar is given, then we naturally set the rank to be zero, i.e.,
m=0
. Note that this is reminiscent of the relation
0!=1
.

\bullet
(Coupling strength tensor): since
\kappa_{i_{*}}
is defined for all
i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
, we call
\mathfrak{K}:=(\kappa_{i_{*}})_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}
as
a coupling strength tensor
associated to (
3.1
). Note that
\mathfrak{K}
is a rank-
m
tensor with
2^{m}
elements.

\bullet
(Set of natural frequency tensors): we denote
\{A_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
by
a set of natural frequency rank-
2m
tensors
.

\bullet
(Initial configuration): we set the
initial configuration
to be
\{T_{j}^{0}\}_{j=1}^{N}
.

For the simplicity of presentation, we define the index set and the set of natural frequency tensors.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{m}),~m)
be the pair of a size vector and a rank associated to (
3.1
).

(1)

The index set of
\mathbf{d}
is given as follows:

\mathfrak{I}(\mathbf{d}):=\{\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}:1\leq e_{i}\leq d_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq m\}.

(2)

The set of natural frequency tensors defined on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}\times d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}
is as follows:

\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{d}):=\{A\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}\times d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m}}:[A]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=-[\overline{A}]_{\alpha_{*1}\alpha_{*0}},\quad\alpha_{*0},~\alpha_{*1}\in\mathfrak{I}(\mathbf{d})\}.

Lastly, we denote the set of all admissible natural frequency tensors by

\mathfrak{F}:=\bigcup_{\mathbf{d}\in\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{N}^{k}}\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{d}).

Now, we are ready to define the characteristic symbol (
3.1
) for the Cauchy problem of the LT model consisting of a size vector, a coupling strength tensor, a set of natural frequency tensors and initial configuration.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{A_{j}\}
and
\{T_{j}^{0}\}
be the size vector, coupling strength tensor, set of natural frequency tensors and initial configuration associated to the Cauchy problem (
3.1
), respectively. Then, we call
{\mathfrak{C}}:=(\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{A_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\})
as the characteristic symbol for the Cauchy problem (
3.1
).

Since the characteristic symbol contains all the information of a given Cauchy problem for the LT model, Cauchy problem is uniquely determined (under some equivalent relation) by a characteristic symbol.

Definition 3.3
.

We define a set of all characteristic symbols with a size vector
\mathbf{d}
and a rank
m=\textup{dim}(\mathbf{d})
as follows: for
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
,

\mathfrak{D}_{m}^{\mathbf{d}}:=\left\{(\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{A_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\}):\mathfrak{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{m}},~\{A_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\in\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{d})^{N},~\{T_{j}^{0}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{d}}\right\}.

Then, it becomes a set of all characteristic symbols to the LT models with the size vector
\mathbf{d}
. We also denote

\mathfrak{D}_{m}:=\bigcup_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\mathfrak{D}_{m}^{\mathbf{d}}\quad\textup{and}\quad{\mathfrak{D}}:=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{D}_{m}.

Note that
\mathfrak{D}_{m}
is a set of all characteristic symbols with rank
m
, and
{\mathfrak{D}}
becomes the space of all characteristic symbols.

3.2.
Explicit examples

In this subsection, we provide explicit examples for the well-known low-rank models that can be reduced from the LT model such as the Kuramoto model, swarm sphere model, etc. It should be mentioned that the low-rank models are
not
the LT model as it is, and they are merely the variants of the LT model. Thus, their characteristic symbols would not be unique.

\bullet

The Lohe hermitian sphere model
: First, we begin with the Lohe hermitian sphere model
[
18
]
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, which is the LT model with rank-1 tensors, i.e.,
m=1
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\Omega_{j}z_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{0}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(z_{k}-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle z_{j})+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle)z_{j},\quad t>0,\\
z_{j}(0)=z_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\quad\|z_{j}^{0}\|=1,\quad j\in[N],\end{cases}

(3.3)

where
\Omega_{j}
is a
d\times d
skew-Hermitian matrix, and
\langle w,z\rangle
is a complex inner product between two complex vectors:

\langle w,z\rangle:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}[\overline{w}]_{\alpha}[z]_{\alpha},\quad\|\omega\|:=\sqrt{\langle\omega,\omega\rangle}.

Then, the characteristic symbol for (
3.3
) is given as follows:

\mathfrak{C}=(d,(\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}),\{\Omega_{j}\},\{z_{j}^{0}\}).

Note that since
z_{j}
is a rank-1 tensor with
d
components, the size vector becomes
d
, and the coupling strength tensor is also a rank-1 tensor with
2^{1}
elements.

\bullet

The Kuramoto model
: Recall the Kuramoto model which is an aggregation model on the space of rank-
0
tensors:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{j}=\nu_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin(\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}),\quad t>0,\\
\theta_{j}(0)=\theta_{j}^{0},\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(3.4)

In order to relate the Kuramoto model (
3.4
) with the LT model, we set in (
3.3
)

z_{j}=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}},\quad\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\Omega_{j}=\mathrm{i}\nu_{j}.

(3.5)

Then, one can check that (
3.3
) with (
3.5
) reduces to (
3.4
). Since
\theta_{j}
is scalar, the characteristic symbol of (
3.4
) is

\mathfrak{C}=\left(\emptyset,\frac{\kappa}{2},\{\mathrm{i}\nu_{j}\},\{e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}^{0}}\}\right).

\bullet

The swarm sphere model
: If we restrict our discussion to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
instead of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, then the coupling term involving with
\kappa_{1}
disappears due to the symmetry of inner product so that system (
3.3
) reduces to the swarm sphere model on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

[
32
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{x}_{j}=\Omega_{j}x_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{k}-\langle x_{k},x_{j}\rangle x_{j}),\quad t>0,\\
x_{j}(0)=x_{j}^{0},\quad\|x_{j}^{0}\|=1,\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(3.6)

We mention that there are many ways how (
3.6
) is derived from (
3.3
), and hence the characteristic symbol of (
3.6
) would not be unique. Here we introduce one simple reduction of (
3.3
) to (
3.6
). Although the initial data
\{z_{j}^{0}\}
belong to
\mathbb{C}^{d}
so that
z_{j}=z_{j}(t)
also lies in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we assume that

z_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\textup{$\Omega_{j}$ is a $d\times d$ skew-symmetric matrix with real entries},\quad j\in[N].

(3.7)

Then, it follows from the uniqueness of solutions to (
3.3
) that

z_{j}(t)\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0,\quad j\in[N].

Thus, one of the characteristic symbol for (
3.6
) is

\mathfrak{C}=(d,(\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}),\{\Omega_{j}\},\{z_{j}^{0}\}),

together with (
3.7
).

\bullet

The generalized Lohe matrix model
: Consider the LT model (
3.1
) for rank-2 tensors:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle\dot{T}_{j}=A_{j}T_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{00}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\textup{tr}(T_{j}^{*}T_{j})T_{k}-\textup{tr}(T_{k}^{*}T_{j})T_{j})+\frac{\kappa_{01}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(T_{k}T_{j}^{\dagger}T_{j}-T_{j}T_{k}^{\dagger}T_{j})\\
\hskip 56.9055pt+\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{10}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(T_{j}T_{j}^{\dagger}T_{k}-T_{j}T_{k}^{\dagger}T_{j})+\frac{\kappa_{11}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\textup{tr}(T_{j}^{*}T_{k}-T_{k}^{*}T_{j})T_{j},~t>0,\\
T_{j}(0)=T_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}},\quad\|T_{j}^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(3.8)

If we turn off the diagonal elements of the coupling strength tensors, i.e.,

\kappa_{00}=\kappa_{11}=0,

then (
3.8
) reduces to the generalized Lohe matrix model
[
17
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{T}_{j}=A_{j}T_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{01}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(T_{k}T_{j}^{\dagger}T_{j}-T_{j}T_{k}^{\dagger}T_{j})+\frac{\kappa_{10}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(T_{j}T_{j}^{\dagger}T_{k}-T_{j}T_{k}^{\dagger}T_{j}),~t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
T_{j}(0)=T_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}},\quad\|T_{j}^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\end{cases}

(3.9)

where
A_{j}
is a rank-4 tensor, and the term
A_{j}T_{j}
can be understood as a rank contraction:

[A_{j}T_{j}]_{\alpha\beta}:=[A_{j}]_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}[T_{j}]_{\gamma\delta}.

Then, again it follows from the previous argument that the characteristic symbol for (
3.9
) becomes

\mathfrak{C}=\left((d_{1},d_{2}),\begin{bmatrix}0&\kappa_{01}\\
\kappa_{10}&0\end{bmatrix},\{A_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\}\right).

(3.10)

\bullet

The Lohe matrix model
: We recall the Lohe matrix model
[
28
]
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{U}_{j}=-\mathrm{i}H_{j}U_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(U_{k}U_{j}^{\dagger}U_{j}-U_{j}U_{k}^{\dagger}U_{j}\Big),\\
U_{j}(0)=U_{j}^{0}\in\mathbf{U}(d),\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(3.11)

Note that there exists a
d\times d
Hermitian matrix
H_{j}
such that

-\mathrm{i}H_{j}T_{j}=A_{j}T_{j}.

(3.12)

If we assume the following relation in (
3.9
):

d:=d_{1}=d_{2},\quad 2\kappa:=\kappa_{01}+\kappa_{10},\quad T_{i}^{0}\in\mathbf{U}(d),

(3.13)

then (
3.9
) with (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) becomes (
3.11
). Thus, as in the swarm sphere model, the characteristic symbol for (
3.11
) would not be unique. One parameter family of characteristic symbols corresponding to (
3.11
) can be given as follows: for any
\tilde{\kappa}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathfrak{C}=\left((d,d),\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\kappa}{2}-\tilde{\kappa}\\
\tilde{\kappa}&0\end{bmatrix},\{H_{j}\},\{U_{j}^{0}\}\right).

4.
Weak Coupling of two Lohe tensor type models

In this section, we present a weak coupling of two Lohe tensor models for
different
rank tensors. Recall that two examples given in Section
2
dealt with the weak coupling of the LT-type models with the same rank tensors, say weak couplings between two ensembles of rank-1 tensors, or between two ensembles of rank-2 tensors, respectively. Now, we consider a weak coupling of two Lohe tensor type models for
different
rank tensors. To obtain an insight for the weak coupling, we review the weakly coupled model in
[
28
]
consisting of the Kuramoto model and the swarm sphere model, and then present
the double tensor model
for two Lohe tensor models. Finally, we introduce a binary operation between two characteristic symbols for the Cauchy problem of the LT model.

4.1.
Coupling of the Kuramoto model and the swarm sphere model

In this subsection, we discuss a weak coupling between the Kuramoto model and the swarm sphere model in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. For this, we begin with the Lohe matrix model on
\mathbf{U}(2)
:

{\mathrm{i}}{\dot{U}}_{i}U_{i}^{\dagger}=H_{i}+\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\kappa}{2N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(U_{j}U_{i}^{\dagger}-U_{i}U_{j}^{\dagger}\Big),\quad i\in[N].

(4.1)

In this case, we can use the parametrization of
2\times 2
unitary matrices
U_{i}
and
2\times 2
Hermitian matrices
H_{i}
via Pauli’s matrices
\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k=1}^{3}
:

U_{i}:=e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}\Big(\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{3}x_{i}^{k}\sigma_{k}+x_{i}^{4}I_{2}\Big),\quad H_{i}:=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\omega_{i}^{k}\sigma_{k}+\nu_{i}I,

where
I
and
\sigma_{i}
are the Pauli matrices and identity matrix defined by

I:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma_{1}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma_{2}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-i\\
i&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma_{3}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right).

If we denote

x_{i}=(x_{i}^{1},x_{i}^{2},x_{i}^{3},x_{i}^{4}),\quad\omega_{i}=(\omega_{i}^{1},\omega_{i}^{2},\omega_{i}^{3}),

then after straightforward manipulations, one can find the dynamics for
(\theta_{i},x_{i})
so that the Lohe matrix model (
4.1
) reduces to
5N
equations for
\theta_{i}
and
x_{i}
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{\theta}}_{i}=\nu_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x_{i},x_{k}\rangle\sin(\theta_{k}-\theta_{i}),\\
\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{i}=\Omega_{i}x_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\cos(\theta_{k}-\theta_{i})(x_{k}-\langle x_{i},x_{k}\rangle x_{i}),\quad i\in[N],\end{cases}

(4.2)

where
\Omega_{i}
is a real
4\times 4
skew-symmetric matrix given by

\Omega_{i}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&-\omega_{i}^{3}&\omega_{i}^{2}&-\omega_{i}^{1}\\
\omega_{i}^{3}&0&-\omega_{i}^{1}&-\omega_{i}^{2}\\
-\omega_{i}^{2}&\omega_{i}^{1}&0&-\omega_{i}^{3}\\
\omega_{i}^{1}&\omega_{i}^{2}&\omega_{i}^{3}&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad i\in[N].

For the emergent dynamics of (
4.2
), we refer the reader to
[
8
]
.

4.2.
The double tensor model

In this subsection, we present a weak coupling of two LT models for possibly different rank tensors. As discussed in previous section, we can associate a unique characteristic symbol for each Cauchy problem to the LT model. To fix the idea, we consider two characteristic symbols:

\mathfrak{C}_{1}=(\mathbf{d}^{1},\mathfrak{K}^{1},\{A^{1}_{j}\},\{(T_{j}^{1})^{0}\})\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathfrak{C}_{2}=(\mathbf{d}^{2},\mathfrak{K}^{2},\{A^{2}_{j}\},\{(T_{j}^{2})^{0}\}).

Now, let
\{T_{j}^{1}\}
and
\{T_{j}^{2}\}
be two solutions to the Cauchy problems of the LT models corresponding to
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle[\dot{T}_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*0}}=[A_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*0}\beta_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}+\sum_{j_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m_{1}}}\frac{\kappa_{j_{*}}^{1}}{N}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 48.36958pt\times\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left([T_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{j}^{1}}]_{\beta_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*(1-j_{*})}}-[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{k}^{1}}]_{\beta_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*(1-j_{*})}}\right),\quad t>0,\\
T_{j}^{1}(0)=(T_{j}^{1})^{0},\quad\|(T_{j}^{1})^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad j\in[N],\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\displaystyle[\dot{T}_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*0}}=[A_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*0}\gamma_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\gamma_{*1}}+\sum_{k_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m_{2}}}\frac{\kappa_{k_{*}}^{2}}{N}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 45.52458pt\times\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left([T_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{j}^{2}}]_{\gamma_{*1}}[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*(1-k_{*})}}-[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{k}^{2}}]_{\gamma_{*1}}[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*(1-k_{*})}}\right),\quad t>0,\\
T_{j}^{2}(0)=(T_{j}^{2})^{0},\quad\|(T_{j}^{2})^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(4.3)

Motivated by weakly coupled models (
2.3
) and (
2.7
) in Section
2
, we propose the double tensor model as follows: for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle[\dot{T}_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*0}}=[A_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*0}\beta_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}+\sum_{j_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m_{1}}}\frac{\kappa_{j_{*}}^{1}}{N}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\times\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\langle T_{j}^{2},T_{k}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{j}^{1}}]_{\beta_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*(1-j_{*})}}-\langle T_{k}^{2},T_{j}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{k}^{1}}]_{\beta_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*(1-j_{*})}}\Big),\\
\displaystyle[\dot{T}_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*0}}=[A_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*0}\gamma_{*1}}[T_{j}^{1}]_{\gamma_{*1}}+\sum_{k_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m_{2}}}\frac{\kappa_{k_{*}}^{2}}{N}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\times\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\langle T_{j}^{1},T_{k}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{j}^{2}}]_{\gamma_{*1}}[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*(1-k_{*})}}-\langle T_{k}^{1},T_{j}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\overline{{T}_{k}^{2}}]_{\gamma_{*1}}[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*(1-k_{*})}}\Big),\\
(T_{j}^{1},T_{j}^{2})(0)=((T_{j}^{1})^{0},(T_{j}^{2})^{0}),\quad\|(T_{j}^{1})^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad\|(T_{j}^{2})^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(4.4)

Next, we consider a larger tensor
T_{j}
given by the tensor product of
T_{j}^{1}
and
T_{j}^{2}
:

T_{j}:=T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}^{1}\times\cdots\times d_{m_{1}}^{1}\times d_{1}^{2}\times\cdots\times d_{m_{2}}^{2}}.

In what follows, we find a Cauchy problem for
T_{j}
. For this, note that

\dot{T}_{j}=\dot{T}_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}+T_{j}^{1}\otimes\dot{T}_{j}^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad[\dot{T}_{j}]_{\beta_{*0}\gamma_{*0}}=[\dot{T}_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*0}}[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*0}}+[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*0}}[\dot{T}_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*0}}.

(4.5)

We substitute (
4.4
) for
T_{j}^{1}
and
T_{j}^{2}
into the right-hand side of (
4.5

Definition 4.1
.

(i) (Fusion of size vectors)

for

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left([T_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[T_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}-[T_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[T_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}\right).

If we substitute
k_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}
, then we get

[T_{c}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}]_{\alpha_{*1}}-[T_{i}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{c}]_{\alpha_{*1}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle T_{i}^{2},T_{k}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}-\langle T_{k}^{2},T_{i}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}\right).

Similarly, if we substitue
j_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}
, then we get

[T_{c}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}]_{\alpha_{*1}}-[T_{i}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{c}]_{\alpha_{*1}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle T_{i}^{1},T_{k}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}-\langle T_{k}^{1},T_{i}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}\right).

Finally, we have the following results:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle T_{i}^{2},T_{k}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}-\langle T_{k}^{2},T_{i}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}\right)=0\quad\text{if }\kappa_{j_{*}}^{1}>0,\\
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle T_{i}^{1},T_{k}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}-\langle T_{k}^{1},T_{i}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}\right)=0\quad\text{if }\kappa_{k_{*}}^{2}>0.\end{cases}

We can compare these two terms and coupling terms in (
4.4
). Coupling terms of
\dot{T}_{j}^{1}
and
\dot{T}_{j}^{2}
can be expressed as follows:

\left(\frac{\kappa^{1}_{j_{*}}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle T_{i}^{2},T_{k}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}-\langle T_{k}^{2},T_{i}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{i}^{1}]_{\beta_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{1}]_{\beta_{*1}}\right)\right)[T_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*(1-j_{*})}}

and

\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}_{k_{*}}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle T_{i}^{1},T_{k}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}-\langle T_{k}^{1},T_{i}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{i}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*k_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*1}}\right)\right)[T_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*(1-k_{*})}},

respectively. From the previous results, we know that each coupling terms in (
4.4
) converge to zero as time goes to infinity. We can easily generalize this results to characteristic
\mathfrak{C}=\bs_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{C}^{i}
with each natural frequency tensor satisfies

A^{i}_{j}\equiv 0\quad\mbox{for all $i\in[n]$ and $j\in[N]$}.

Since each coupling term in (
5.12
) converges to zero, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=1\\
p\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle T_{j}^{p},T_{k}^{p}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{i}_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{i}_{*1}}-\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=1\\
p\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle T_{k}^{p},T_{j}^{p}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*j_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{k}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*1}}\Bigg)=0,

for all
\ell
and uncontracted indices when
\kappa^{\ell}_{j_{*}}>0
.

5.
Weak coupling of multiple tensor models

In this section, we extend the weak coupling of two LT models to the arbitrary number of LT models using the fusion operation summarized in Definition
4.2
. First, we study algebraic properties of the fusion operation on
\mathfrak{D}
, and it turns out to be a monoid on
\mathfrak{D}
; in other words, it is associative and
\mathfrak{D}
has the identity element under the fusion operation.

5.1.
Monoid structure of the fusion operation

In this subsection, we show that the fusion operation
\star
introduced in Definition
4.2
gives a monoid structure on
\mathfrak{D}
, i.e., it has the identity and satisfyies associativity relation.

5.1.1.
Associativity of
\star

: We set

\mathfrak{C}^{i}:=(\mathbf{d}^{i},\mathfrak{K}^{i},\{A_{j}^{i}\},\{(T_{j}^{i})^{0}\}),\quad m_{i}:=\mathrm{dim}(\mathbf{d}^{i}),\quad i=1,2,3.

Then, note that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star\mathfrak{C}^{2})\star\mathfrak{C}^{3}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\big((\mathbf{d}^{1}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{2})\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{3},(\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3},\{(A_{j}^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{2})\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{3}\},\{((T_{j}^{1})^{0}\otimes(T_{j}^{2})^{0})\otimes(T_{j}^{3})^{0}\}\big),\\
&\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star(\mathfrak{C}^{2}\star\mathfrak{C}^{3})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\big(\mathbf{d}^{1}\star_{s}(\mathbf{d}^{2}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{3}),\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}(\mathfrak{K}^{2}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}),\{A_{j}^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}(A_{j}^{2}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{3})\},\{(T_{j}^{1})^{0}\otimes((T_{j}^{2})^{0}\otimes(T_{j}^{3})^{0})\}\big).\end{aligned}

Thus for the associativity of
\star
, we need to show

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\mathbf{d}^{1}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{2})\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{3}=\mathbf{d}^{1}\star_{s}(\mathbf{d}^{2}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{3}),\quad(\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}=\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}(\mathfrak{K}^{2}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}),\\
&(A_{j}^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{2})\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{3}=A_{j}^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}(A_{j}^{2}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{3}),\\
&\{((T_{j}^{1})^{0}\otimes(T_{j}^{2})^{0})\otimes(T_{j}^{3})^{0}\}=\{(T_{j}^{1})^{0}\otimes((T_{j}^{2})^{0}\otimes(T_{j}^{3})^{0})\}.\end{aligned}

(5.1)

Since
\star_{s}
is a merely juxtaposition of size vectors and
\otimes
is just tensor product, one can easily verify the first and last relations:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\mathbf{d}^{1}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{2})\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{3}=\mathbf{d}^{1}\star_{s}(\mathbf{d}^{2}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{3}),\\
&((T_{j}^{1})^{0}\otimes(T_{j}^{2})^{0})\otimes(T_{j}^{3})^{0}=(T_{j}^{1})^{0}\otimes((T_{j}^{2})^{0}\otimes(T_{j}^{3})^{0}),\quad j\in[N].\end{aligned}

In the following two lemmas, we verify the second and third relations in (
5.1
) one by one.

Lemma 5.1
.

The fusion operation
\star_{c}
is associative:

(\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}=\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}(\mathfrak{K}^{2}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}),\quad\mathfrak{K}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{m_{i}}},\quad i=1,2,3.

(5.2)

Proof.

It suffices to show that (
5.2
) holds componentwisely. We recall from Definition
4.1
that for
(i_{*},j_{*},k_{*})\in\{0,1\}^{m_{1}}\times\{0,1\}^{m_{2}}\times\{0,1\}^{m_{3}}
, we use (
4.15
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &((\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3})_{(i_{*},j_{*},k_{*})}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=(\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})_{(i_{*},j_{*})}\delta_{k_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{3}}}+\mathfrak{K}^{3}_{k_{*}}\delta_{(i_{*},j_{*}),\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}+m_{2}}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\mathfrak{K}^{1}_{i_{*}}\delta_{j_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}\delta_{k_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{3}}}+\mathfrak{K}^{2}_{j_{*}}\delta_{i_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}}\delta_{j_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}+\mathfrak{K}^{3}_{k_{*}}\delta_{i_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}}\delta_{j_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}.\end{aligned}

(5.3)

Similarly, one finds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}(\mathfrak{K}^{2}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}))_{(i_{*},j_{*},k_{*})}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\mathfrak{K}^{1}_{i_{*}}\delta_{j_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}\delta_{k_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{3}}}+\mathfrak{K}^{2}_{j_{*}}\delta_{i_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}}\delta_{j_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}+\mathfrak{K}^{3}_{k_{*}}\delta_{i_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}}\delta_{j_{*},\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}.\end{aligned}

(5.4)

Thus, it follows from (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) that

(\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}=\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}(\mathfrak{K}^{2}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{3}).

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The fusion operation
\star_{\textup{F}}
is associative:

(A^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}A^{2})\star_{\textup{F}}A^{3}=A^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}(A^{2}\star_{\textup{F}}A^{3}),\quad A^{i}\in\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{d}^{i}),\quad i=1,2,3.

(5.5)

Proof.

Similar to Lemma
5.1
, it suffices to consider each component of both sides in (
5.5
). It follows from (
4.14
) in Definition
4.1
that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &[(A^{1}\star A^{2})\star A^{3}]_{\alpha_{*0}\beta_{*0}\gamma_{*0}\alpha_{*1}\beta_{*1}\gamma_{*1}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=[A^{1}\star A^{2}]_{\alpha_{*0}\beta_{*0}\alpha_{*1}\beta_{*1}}[A^{3}]_{\gamma_{*0}\gamma_{*1}}=[A^{1}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[A^{2}]_{\beta_{*0}\beta_{*1}}[A^{3}]_{\gamma_{*0}\gamma_{*1}}.\end{aligned}

(5.6)

Similarly, we have

[A^{1}\star(A_{j}^{2}\star A^{3})]_{\alpha_{*0}\beta_{*0}\gamma_{*0}\alpha_{*1}\beta_{*1}\gamma_{*1}}=[A^{1}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[A^{2}]_{\beta_{*0}\beta_{*1}}[A^{3}]_{\gamma_{*0}\gamma_{*1}}.

(5.7)

Finally, we combine (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) to derive the desired relation.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, we show that
\star
is associative on
\mathfrak{D}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The fusion operation
\star
is associative:

(\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star\mathfrak{C}^{2})\star\mathfrak{C}^{3}=\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star(\mathfrak{C}^{2}\star\mathfrak{C}^{3}),\quad\mathfrak{C}^{j}\in\mathfrak{D},\quad j=1,2,3.

Remark 5.1
.

By the result of Proposition
5.1
, associativity relation allows us to perform the following repeated operations for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star\mathfrak{C}^{2}\star\cdots\star\mathfrak{C}^{n}.

Thus, we simply denote the repeated fusion operations as

\bs_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{C}^{i}:=\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star\mathfrak{C}^{2}\star\cdots\star\mathfrak{C}^{n},

and it is well-defined.

5.1.2.
Identity element of
\star

: Next, we look for the identity element for the fusion operation
\star
denoted by
\mathfrak{C}_{e}\in\mathfrak{D}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{e}\star\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}\star\mathfrak{C}_{e}=\mathfrak{C},\quad\textup{for all}\quad\mathfrak{C}\in\mathfrak{D}.

(5.8)

To find the explicit representation for an identity
\mathfrak{C}_{e}
, we set

\mathfrak{C}_{e}:=(\mathbf{d}^{e},\mathfrak{K}^{e},\{A_{j}^{e}\},\{(T_{j}^{e})^{0}\}),\quad\mathfrak{C}=(\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{A_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\}),\quad m_{e}:=\mathrm{dim}(\mathbf{d}^{e}),\quad m=\mathrm{dim}(\mathbf{d}).

Below, we present a possible candidate for
\mathfrak{C}_{e}
one by one.

\bullet
Case A (Determination of
\mathbf{d}^{e}
): By the definition of
\star_{s}
, one has

\mathbf{d}^{e}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{d}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{e}=\mathbf{d}.

Thus,
\mathbf{d}^{e}
should not contain any components; otherwise,
\mathbf{d}
differs from
\mathbf{d}^{e}\star\mathbf{d}
. This shows that
\mathbf{d}^{e}
is a zero-dimensional vector:

\mathbf{d}^{e}=\emptyset.

Since the rank is defined as the number of components of
\mathbf{d}^{e}
(see Section
3.1
), we have

m_{e}=\mathrm{dim}(\mathbf{d}^{e})=0.

In addition, a solution
T_{j}^{e}
for the Cauchy problem corresponding to the characteristic symbol
\mathfrak{C}^{e}
is rank-
m_{e}
tensor, i.e.,
T_{j}^{e}
is a complex number.

\bullet
Case B (Determination of
\mathfrak{K}^{e}
): Since
m_{e}=0
, we have

\mathfrak{K}^{e}:\{0,1\}^{0}\to\mathbb{R},

and hence
\mathfrak{K^{e}}
can be considered as a real number. Our guess coincides with the fact that
\mathfrak{K}^{e}
is a rank-
m_{e}
tensor with
2^{m_{e}}
elements.

\bullet
Case C (Determination of
A_{j}^{e}
): since
A_{j}^{e}
is a rank-
2m_{e}
tensor and
m_{e}=0
, we can regard
A_{j}^{e}\in\mathbb{C}
as a complex number with following skew-hermitian property:

A_{j}^{e}=-\bar{A}_{j}^{e}.

Thus, there exists a real number
\nu_{j}
such that

A_{j}^{e}=\mathrm{i}\nu_{j}.

By Case A–Case C, one has a tentative ansatz for
\mathfrak{C}_{e}
:

\mathfrak{C}_{e}=(\emptyset,\mathfrak{K}^{e},\{\mathrm{i}\nu_{j}\},\{(T_{j}^{e})^{0}\}).

(5.9)

In the following proposition, we find the explicit representation for
\mathfrak{C}_{e}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The identity
\mathfrak{C}_{e}
is given as follows:

\mathfrak{C}_{e}=(\emptyset,0,\{0\},\{1\}).

Proof.

(i) We further identify
\mathfrak{K}^{e},\{\mathrm{i}\nu_{j}\}
and
(T_{j}^{e})^{0}
in (
5.9
) as follows.

\bullet
(Representation of
\mathfrak{K}^{e}
): since
m_{e}=0
, we have

(\mathfrak{K}^{e}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K})_{(\emptyset,i_{*})}=(\mathfrak{K}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{e})_{(i_{*},\emptyset)}=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{K}_{i_{*}}+\mathfrak{K}^{e}\quad&\text{if }i_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m},\\
\mathfrak{K}_{i_{*}}\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since the following relations should hold:

\mathfrak{K}^{e}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}=\mathfrak{K}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{e}=\mathfrak{K},\quad\textup{for all $\mathfrak{K}$},

\mathfrak{K}^{e}
satisfies
\mathfrak{K}_{i_{*}}+\mathfrak{K}^{e}=\mathfrak{K}_{i_{*}}

Thus, we have

\mathfrak{K}^{e}=0.

\bullet
(Representation of
A_{j}^{e}
): We observe that
A_{j}^{e}
should satisfy

\displaystyle[A_{j}^{e}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}]_{\emptyset\alpha_{*0}\emptyset\alpha_{*1}}=[A_{j}^{e}]_{\emptyset\emptyset}\delta_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}+\delta_{\emptyset\emptyset}[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=A_{j}^{e}\delta_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}+[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}},

\displaystyle[A_{j}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{e}]_{\alpha_{*0}\emptyset\alpha_{*1}\emptyset}=[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}\delta_{\emptyset\emptyset}+\delta_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[A_{j}^{e}]_{\emptyset\emptyset}=[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}+A_{j}^{e}\delta_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}.

Since the following relations below holds for all
A_{j}
:

A_{j}^{e}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}=A_{j}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{e}=A_{j},

one particularly has

A_{j}^{e}\delta_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}+[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}.

This yields

A_{j}^{e}=0.

\bullet
(Representation of
T_{j}^{e}
): since
(T_{j}^{e})^{0}
is a complex number whose Frobenius norm equal to 1, the initial data
(T_{j}^{e})^{0}
can be considered as a complex number with unit modulus. We set

c_{j}:=(T_{j}^{e})^{0}\in\mathbb{C},\quad|c_{j}|=1.

Since
(T_{j}^{e})^{0}
should satisfy

(T_{j}^{e})^{0}\otimes T_{j}^{0}=T_{j}^{0}\otimes(T_{j}^{e})^{0}=T_{j}^{0},\quad\textup{i.e.,}\quad c_{j}T_{j}^{0}=T_{j}^{0},

we have

c_{j}=(T_{j}^{e})^{0}=1.

In summary, we have shown that there exists a unique identity for the fusion operation
\star
on
\mathfrak{D}
, and the unique (left and right) identity of the fusion operation is given as follows:

\mathfrak{C}_{e}=(\emptyset,0,\{0\},\{1\}).

∎

Remark 5.2
.

Since a characteristic symbol uniquely corresponds to a Cauchy problem for the LT model, we are concerned with the Cauchy problem for
\mathfrak{C}^{e}=(\emptyset,0,\{0\},\{1\})
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{T}_{j}^{e}=0,\quad t>0,\quad j\in[N],\\
T_{j}^{e}(0)=(T_{j}^{e})^{0}=1,\end{cases}

which yields a trivial solution
T_{j}^{e}(t)\equiv 1.

5.2.
Multiple tensor model

In this subsection, we construct the multiple tensor model by repeatedly applying fusion operation in Definition
4.2
. For any given natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider
n
LT models whose characteristic symbols are given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}^{\ell}=(\mathbf{d}^{\ell},\mathfrak{K}^{\ell},\{A_{j}^{\ell}\},\{(T_{j}^{\ell})^{0}\}),\quad m_{\ell}=\textup{dim}(\mathbf{d}^{\ell}),\quad 1\leq\ell\leq n.

(5.10)

Since repeated fusion operation is well defined, we naturally consider
\bs_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathfrak{C}^{\ell}
and its corresponding Cauchy problem:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle[\dot{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}}=\left[{\bigstar_{\textup{F}}}_{\ell=1}^{n}A_{j}^{\ell}\right]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}+\sum_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\frac{\left({\bs_{c}}_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathfrak{K}^{\ell}\right)_{i_{*}}}{N}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left([T_{k}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}\overline{[{T}_{j}]}_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}-[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}\overline{[{T}_{k}]}_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}\right),\quad t>0,\\
\displaystyle T_{j}(0)=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}(T_{j}^{\ell})^{0},\quad\|(T_{j}^{\ell})^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad j\in[N],\end{cases}

(5.11)

where
m:=\sum_{l=1}^{n}m_{l}
is the sum of ranks. Similar to the double tensor model in Theorem
4.1
, we propose the multiple tensor model as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}^{1},\mathfrak{C}^{2},\cdots,\mathfrak{C}^{n}\in\mathfrak{D}
be the characteristic symbols of the Cauchy problems to the LT models. If
\{T_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
is a solution to the Cauchy problem associated to
\bs_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathfrak{C}^{\ell}
, then
T_{j}(t)
can be decomposed as

T_{j}(t)=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}T_{j}^{\ell}(t),\quad t>0,

where
\{(T_{j}^{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{n}\}_{j=1}^{N}
is a solution to the following system: for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle[\dot{T}_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*0}}=[A_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*0}\beta^{\ell}_{*1}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*1}}+\sum_{j_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m_{i}}}\frac{\kappa_{j_{*}}^{\ell}}{N}\\
\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=1\\
p\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle T_{j}^{p},T_{k}^{p}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{i}_{*j_{*}}}\overline{[{T}_{j}^{\ell}]}_{\beta^{i}_{*1}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*(1-j_{*})}}-\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=1\\
p\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{n}\langle T_{k}^{p},T_{j}^{p}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*j_{*}}}\overline{[{T}_{k}^{\ell}]}_{\beta^{\ell}_{*1}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*(1-j_{*})}}\Bigg),\\
T_{j}^{\ell}(0)=(T_{j}^{\ell})^{0},\quad\|(T_{j}^{\ell})^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad\ell\in[n],\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(5.12)

Proof.

For a proof, we use a mathematical induction and split the proof into two steps.

\bullet
Step A (Initial step): The case of
n=1
is trivial, and we have already verified the case of
n=2
in Theorem
4.1
.

\bullet
Step B (Induction step): We assume the statement holds for
n=q\geq 2
. Now, we prove the case when
n=q+1
.

Let characteristic symbols
\mathfrak{C}^{1},\mathfrak{C}^{2},\cdots,\mathfrak{C}^{q},\mathfrak{C}^{q+1}
be given, and we set
\{T_{j}\}
to be a solution to the Cauchy problem whose characteristic symbol is
\bs_{\ell=1}^{q+1}\mathfrak{C}^{\ell}
. Below, we show that
\{T_{j}^{\ell}\}
with
j\in[N]
and
\ell\in[q+1]
is a solution to the following system: for
t>0
,

\begin{cases}\displaystyle[\dot{T}_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*0}}=[A_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*0}\beta^{\ell}_{*1}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*1}}+\sum_{j_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m_{i}}}\frac{\kappa_{j_{*}}^{\ell}}{N}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\times\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=1\\
p\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{q+1}\langle T_{j}^{p},T_{k}^{p}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{k}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{i}_{*j_{*}}}\overline{[{T}_{j}^{\ell}]}_{\beta^{i}_{*1}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*(1-j_{*})}}-\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p=1\\
p\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{q+1}\langle T_{k}^{p},T_{j}^{p}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*j_{*}}}\overline{[{T}_{k}^{\ell}]}_{\beta^{\ell}_{*1}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{\ell}_{*(1-j_{*})}}\Bigg),\\
T_{j}^{\ell}(0)=(T_{j}^{\ell})^{0},\quad\|(T_{j}^{\ell})^{0}\|_{\textup{F}}=1,\quad\ell\in[q+1],\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(5.13)

We set

S_{j}=T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2},\quad j\in[N].

Then, it follows from the induction hypothesis on
n=q
that

T_{j}=S_{j}\otimes T_{j}^{3}\otimes T_{j}^{4}\otimes\cdots T_{j}^{q+1}.

Moreover, each dynamics of
S_{j}
and
T_{j}^{\ell}
with
3\leq\ell\leq q+1
can be represented in the following forms.

\diamond
(Dynamics of
S_{j}
): By induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\displaystyle[\dot{S}_{j}]_{\beta^{1}_{*0}\beta^{2}_{*0}}=[A_{j}^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{2}]_{\beta^{1}_{*0}\beta^{2}_{*0}\beta^{1}_{*1}\beta^{2}_{*1}}[S_{j}]_{\beta^{1}_{*1}\beta^{2}_{*1}}\\

&\hskip 42.67912pt-\left.\prod_{l=3}^{q+1}\langle T_{p}^{l},T_{j}^{l}\rangle_{\textup{F}}[S_{j}]_{\beta^{1}_{*j_{*}}\beta^{2}_{*k_{*}}}\overline{[{S}_{p}]}_{\beta^{1}_{*1}\beta^{2}_{*1}}[S_{j}]_{\beta^{1}_{*(1-j_{*})}\beta^{2}_{*(1-k_{*})}}\right).\end{aligned}

(5.14)

Let
\{T_{j}^{1},T_{j}^{2}\}
be a solution to (

\diamond

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} [\dot{T}_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{i}_{*0}}&=[A_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{i}_{*0}\beta^{i}_{*1}}[T_{j}^{\ell}]_{\beta^{i}_{*1}}\\

(5.15)

Since the following relation holds

5.3.
Weak coupling of the LT models for low-rank tensors

In this subsection, we discuss two weak couplings of the Kuramoto models and the swarm sphere models by means of the fusion operation.

5.3.1.
Same ranks

First, we review two Kuramoto models from Section
3.2
whose characteristic symbols are given as

\mathfrak{C}_{1}=\left(\emptyset,\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2},\{\mathrm{i}\nu^{1}_{j}\},\{e^{\mathrm{i}(\theta_{j}^{1})^{0}}\}\right),\quad\mathfrak{C}_{2}=\left(\emptyset,\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2},\{\mathrm{i}\nu^{2}_{j}\},\{e^{\mathrm{i}(\theta_{j}^{2})^{0}}\}\right).

Then, the corresponding Kuramoto models correspond to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{1}~\Longleftrightarrow~\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{\theta}^{1}_{j}=\nu_{j}^{1}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin(\theta^{1}_{k}-\theta^{1}_{j}),\\
\theta_{j}^{1}(0)=(\theta_{j}^{1})^{0},\end{cases}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathfrak{C}_{2}~\Longleftrightarrow~\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{\theta}^{2}_{j}=\nu_{j}^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin(\theta^{2}_{k}-\theta^{2}_{j}),\\
\theta_{j}^{2}(0)=(\theta_{j}^{2})^{0}.\end{cases}

Then, the weak coupling of two characteristic symbols
\mathfrak{C}:=\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}
is explicitly given as follows:

\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}=\left(\emptyset,\frac{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}{2},\{\mathrm{i}(\nu^{1}_{j}+\nu^{2}_{j})\},\{e^{\mathrm{i}\left((\theta_{j}^{1})^{0}+(\theta_{j}^{2})^{0}\right)}\}\right),

and the corresponding Cauchy problem becomes

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{\theta}^{1}_{j}+\dot{\theta}^{2}_{j}=(\nu_{j}^{1}+\nu_{j}^{2})+\frac{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin((\theta_{k}^{1}+\theta_{k}^{2})-(\theta_{j}^{1}+\theta_{j}^{2})),\\
(\theta_{j}^{1}+\theta_{j}^{2})(0)=(\theta_{j}^{1})^{0}+(\theta_{j}^{2})^{0}.\end{cases}

(5.16)

Now, we introduce new variables:

\varphi_{j}:=\theta_{j}^{1}+\theta_{j}^{2},\quad\nu_{j}:=\nu_{j}^{1}+\nu_{j}^{2},\quad\kappa:=\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}.

(5.17)

Then, system (
5.16
) can cast in terms of (
5.17
):

\dot{\varphi}_{j}=\nu_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{j}),

which is exactly the Kuramoto model. Thus, the weak coupling of two Kuramoto models is still the Kuramoto model.

Second, we consider the weak coupling of two swarm sphere models whose characteristic symbols are given by

\mathfrak{C}_{1}=(d_{1},(\kappa_{1},0),\{\Omega_{j}^{1}\},\{(x_{j}^{1})^{0}\}),\quad\mathfrak{C}_{2}=(d_{2},(\kappa_{2},0),\{\Omega^{2}_{j}\},\{(x_{j}^{2})^{0}\}),

with
\Omega_{j}^{1}
being a
d_{1}\times d_{1}
skew-symmetric matrix and
\Omega_{j}^{2}
being a
d_{2}\times d_{2}
skew-symmetric matrix. Furthermore,
(x_{j}^{1})^{0}
and
(x_{j}^{2})^{0}
are real-valued with unit norms. In particular, we set the second components of coupling strength tensors to be zero for simplicity. Then, the fusion of two characteristic symbols
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}
can be calculated as

\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}=\left((d_{1},d_{2}),\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa_{2}\\
\kappa_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},\{\Omega_{j}^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}\Omega_{j}^{2}\},\{(x_{j}^{1})^{0}\otimes(x_{j}^{2})^{0}\}\right),

(5.18)

which is exactly the same as (
3.13
) except for the natural frequency and the initial data. Thus, the weak coupling of two swarm sphere models is the generalized Lohe matrix model. However, it follows from
[
16
]
that the weakly coupled model corresponding to the characteristic symbol (
5.18
) is the double sphere model (
2.3
) (see Proposition
2.2
).

5.3.2.
Different ranks

So far, we have investigated the fusion of two characteristic symbols with the same ranks. Now, we return to the fusion of two characteristic symbols with
different
ranks. We recall the quantum Lohe model introduced in Section
4.1
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{j}=\nu_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x_{j},x_{k}\rangle\sin(\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{x}_{j}=\Omega_{j}x_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\cos(\theta_{k}-\theta_{j})(x_{k}-\langle x_{j},x_{k}\rangle x_{j}),\\
(\theta_{j}(0),x_{j}(0))=(\theta_{j}^{0},x_{j}^{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(5.19)

Recall that model (
5.19
) has been derived from the
\mathbf{U}(2)
parametrization of the Lohe matrix model. Our goal is to verify that model (
5.19
) can be obtained from the weak coupling of two models: the Kuramoto model and the swarm sphere model. First, we rewrite (
5.19
) in the form of the double sphere model. Precisely, for given
\theta_{j}
in (
5.19
), we introduce new variables in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
:

y_{j}=(\cos\theta_{j},\sin\theta_{j})^{\top}\in\mathbb{S}^{1},

Then, system (
5.19
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{y}_{j}=\nu_{j}Jy_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle x_{j},x_{k}\rangle y_{k}y_{j}^{\top}y_{j}-\langle x_{k},x_{j}\rangle y_{j}y_{k}^{\top}y_{j}\right),\quad J=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix},\\
\displaystyle\dot{x}_{j}=\Omega_{j}x_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\langle y_{j},y_{k}\rangle x_{k}x_{j}^{\top}x_{j}-\langle y_{k},y_{j}\rangle x_{j}x_{k}^{\top}x_{j}\right),\\
y_{j}(0)=(\cos\theta_{j}^{0},\sin\theta_{j}^{0})^{\top},\quad x_{j}(0)=x_{j}^{0}.\end{cases}

(5.20)

If the subsystems of (
5.20
) are decoupled, then we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{y}_{j}=\nu_{j}Jy_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(y_{k}y_{j}^{\top}y_{j}-y_{j}y_{k}^{\top}y_{j}\right),~t>0,\\
\displaystyle\dot{x}_{j}=\Omega_{j}x_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(x_{k}x_{j}^{\top}x_{j}-x_{j}x_{k}^{\top}x_{j}\right),\\
y_{j}(0)=(\cos\theta_{j}^{0},\sin\theta_{j}^{0})^{\top},\quad x_{j}(0)=x_{j}^{0}.\end{cases}

Thus, two characteristic symbols of two equations in (
4.3
) become

\mathfrak{C}_{1}=(2,\kappa_{1},\{\nu_{j}J\},\{(\cos\theta_{j}^{0},\sin\theta_{j}^{0})^{\top}\}),\quad\mathfrak{C}_{2}=(d,\kappa_{2},\{\Omega_{j}\},\{x_{j}^{0}\}).

Finally, we conclude that the characteristic symbol of (
5.20
) can be obtained as the fusion of
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
:

\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}=\left((2,d),\begin{bmatrix}0&\kappa_{1}\\
\kappa_{2}&0\end{bmatrix},\{\nu_{j}J\star\Omega_{j}\},\{(\cos\theta_{j}^{0},\sin\theta_{j}^{0})^{\top}\otimes x_{j}^{0}\}\right).

Note that the characteristic symbol of the quantum Lohe model is a special case of the double sphere model. So, we can conclude that the quantum Lohe model is the special case of the double sphere model.

5.4.
Gradient flow formulation

In this subsection, we discuss conditions in which the multiple tensor model can be represented as a gradient flow with a suitable analytical potential. Note that multiple sphere and multiple matrix models introduced in Section
2
can be written as gradient flows with total distance functionals as potentials.

Let
\{T_{j}\}
be a solution to the double tensor model (
3.4
). As in
[
15
,
16
]
, we consider the following potential function:

\mathcal{V}(\{T_{j}\})=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle T_{i},T_{j}\rangle_{\textup{F}}.

(5.21)

If there exist two tensors
T_{j}^{1}
and
T_{j}^{2}
such that
T_{j}=T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}
, then system (
5.21
) becomes

\mathcal{V}(\{T_{j}\})=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle T_{i}^{1},T_{j}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}\cdot\langle T_{i}^{2},T_{j}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}.

Thus, if a solution to the multiple tensor model is decomposed by using tensor products, then the the multiple tensor model becomes a gradient system.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}^{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}^{2}
be given characteristic symbols, and let
T_{j}^{1}
and
T_{j}^{2}
be the solutions to the LT models (
1.1
) corresponding to characteristic symbols
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
, respectively. Suppose that the systems for
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
are represented as gradient flows with the following potential functionals:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{1}(\{T_{j}^{1}\})=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle T_{i}^{1},T_{j}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}},\quad\mathcal{V}_{2}(\{T_{j}^{2}\})=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle T_{i}^{2},T_{j}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}},

on state spaces
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, respectively. Then, the weakly coupled system corresponding to the characteristic symbol
\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star\mathfrak{C}^{2}
can be expressed as a gradient flow with the following potential:

\mathcal{V}(\{T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}\})=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle T_{i}^{1},T_{j}^{1}\rangle_{\textup{F}}\cdot\langle T_{i}^{2},T_{j}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}},\quad T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}\in M_{1}\times M_{2}.

Remark 5.3
.

It is worthwhile to mention that Theorem
5.2
is consistent with the results of
[
15
,
16
]
. More precisely, let
\{T_{j}\}=\{T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}\}
be a solution to the LT model with characteristic symbol
\mathfrak{C}^{1}\star\mathfrak{C}^{2}
. For the case of rank-1 real tensors, then the LT model corresponds to the swarm sphere model, and
\{T_{j}\}
lies on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then, one finds

\dot{T}_{j}^{1}=-\nabla_{T_{j}^{1}}\mathcal{V}(\{T_{j}\})\Big|_{T_{T_{j}^{1}}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle T_{j}^{2},T_{k}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}\Big(T_{k}^{1}-\langle T_{k}^{1},T_{j}^{1}\rangle T_{j}^{1}\Big),

where
T_{T_{j}^{1}}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is the tangent space of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
at
T_{j}^{1}
. This exactly coincides with the double sphere model. On the other hand, if we consider rank-2 tensors and assume that
T_{j}
lies on
\mathbb{U}(d)
, then the model reduces to

\dot{T}_{j}^{1}=-\nabla_{T_{j}^{1}}\mathcal{V}(\{T_{j}\})\Big|_{T_{T_{j}^{1}}\mathbb{U}(d)}=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\langle T_{j}^{2},T_{k}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}T_{k}^{1}-\langle T_{k}^{2},T_{j}^{2}\rangle_{\textup{F}}T_{j}^{1}(T_{k}^{1})^{\dagger}T_{j}^{1}\Big).

Thus, we obtain the double matrix model.

By mathematical induction, Theorem
5.2
can be extended to the multiple tensor model. Since the proof is straightforward, we omit the proof.

Corollary 5.2
.

For
\ell\in[n]
, let
\mathfrak{C}^{\ell}
be the characteristic symbol of the system that can be represented as a gradient flow on a manifold

:

\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(\{T_{j}^{\ell}\})=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle T_{i}^{\ell},T_{j}^{\ell}\rangle_{\textup{F}},

on manifold

for all

. Then, system whose characteristic symbol is given by
\bs_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathfrak{C}^{\ell}
can be also represented as a gradient flow with the following potential function on the product manifold

:

\mathcal{V}\left(\left\{\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}T_{j}^{\ell}\right\}\right)=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\langle T_{i}^{\ell},T_{j}^{\ell}\rangle_{\textup{F}}\right).

5.5.
Index shuffling operator and induced transforms

In this section, we further investigate the structure of
(\mathfrak{D},\star)
. Heuristically, suppose that we consider a weakly coupled model from two LT models whose solutions are denoted by
\{T_{j}^{1}\}
and
\{T_{j}^{2}\}
. Then, a solution to the weakly coupled model becomes
T_{j}^{1}\otimes T_{j}^{2}
. One natural question is whether
T_{j}^{2}\otimes T_{j}^{1}
also becomes a solution to the same weakly coupled model. Our goal of this subsection is to provide an affirmative answer. For this purpose, we introduce the index shuffling operator (or a permutation)
\sigma:[m]\to[m]
on rank-
m
tensor, which is a one-to-one correspondence, and we set the collection of index shuffling operator on
[m]
to be

\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, which is the permutation group of
m
letters. For simplicity, we use the following handy notation: for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\sigma(*)}=\alpha_{\sigma(1)}\alpha_{\sigma(2)}\cdots\alpha_{\sigma(m)},\quad\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}=\alpha_{\sigma(1)0}\alpha_{\sigma(2)0}\cdots\alpha_{\sigma(m)0},

\displaystyle\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}=\alpha_{\sigma(1)1}\alpha_{\sigma(2)1}\cdots\alpha_{\sigma(m)1}.

The induced transform on a rank
m
-tensor
T
can be defined as follows:

[T^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)}}:=[T]_{\alpha_{*}},\quad\forall~\alpha_{*}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}[d_{i}].

We can easily check that
T^{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{\sigma(1)}\times d_{\sigma(2)}\times\cdots\times d_{\sigma(m)}}
, i.e.,

\alpha_{\sigma(i)}\in[d_{\sigma(i)}],\quad i\in[m].

5.5.1.
Size vector and rank

Since
\sigma
is an index shuffling operator, the rank of the tensor is not changed. However, it changes the size vector of the tensor
\mathbf{d}
as follows:

\mathbf{d}^{\sigma}=(d_{\sigma(1)},d_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,d_{\sigma(m)}).

Here,
\mathbf{d}^{\sigma}
can be interpreted as an induced transform of
\mathbf{d}
by the index shuffling operator
\sigma
.

5.5.2.
Natural frequency tensor

Consider a rank-
2m
natural frequency tensor
A
satisfying the block skew-Hermitian property:

[A]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=-[\bar{A}]_{\alpha_{*1}\alpha_{*0}}.

The induced transform on this rank
2m
-tensor
A
can be defined as follows:

[A^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}:=[A]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}.

Then, it is easy to check that
A^{\sigma}
satisfies

[A^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}=[A]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}=-[\bar{A}]_{\alpha_{*1}\alpha_{*0}}=-[\bar{A}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}},

and we conclude that
A^{\sigma}
is also a rank-
2m
natural frequency tensor of size
(d_{\sigma(1)}\times d_{\sigma(2)}\times\cdots\times d_{\sigma(m)})\times(d_{\sigma(1)}\times d_{\sigma(2)}\times\cdots\times d_{\sigma(m)})
.

5.5.3.
Coupling strength tensor

Finally, we introduce the induced transform of
\mathfrak{K}
with respect to the index shuffling operator
\sigma
as follows:

[\mathfrak{K}^{\sigma}]_{i_{\sigma(*)}}:=[\mathfrak{K}]_{i_{*}}\quad i_{*}=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m},

where
i_{\sigma(*)}=i_{\sigma(1)}i_{\sigma(2)}\cdots i_{\sigma(m)}
.

Below, we provide a brief summary of the induced transform of four components for the characteristic symbol by the index shuffling operator
\sigma
:

•

Size vector:

\mathbf{d}^{\sigma}=(d_{\sigma(1)},d_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,d_{\sigma(m)}).

•

Rank-
m
tensors:

[T^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)}}=[T]_{\alpha_{*}}.

•

Rank-
2m
block skew-Hermitian tensors:

[A^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}=[A]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}.

•

Coupling strength tensor:

[\mathfrak{K}^{\sigma}]_{i_{\sigma(*)}}=[\mathfrak{K}]_{i_{*}}.

Since the characteristic symbol of the LT model
\mathfrak{C}=(\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{A_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\})
is determined by the four components, we can also define the induced transform of the characteristic symbol with respect to the index shuffling operator
\sigma
as follows:

\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}:=(\mathbf{d}^{\sigma},\mathfrak{K}^{\sigma},\{A_{j}^{\sigma}\},\{(T_{j}^{0})^{\sigma}\}).

Now, we can find the Cauchy problem corresponding to the induced characteristic symbol
\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
\{T_{j}\}
is a solution to
\mathfrak{C}
, then
\{T_{j}^{\sigma}\}
is a solution to
\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}
for any index shuffling operator
\sigma
.

Proof.

First, we fix the index shuffling operator
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
and the characteristic symbol:

\mathfrak{C}=(\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{A_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\}).

Then,
\{T_{j}\}
is a solution to the Cauchy problem for
\mathfrak{C}
which reads as

\begin{cases}[\dot{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}}=[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}\\
+\displaystyle\sum_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\kappa_{i_{*}}\left([T_{c}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}-[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{c}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}\right),~t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
T_{j}(0)=T_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\cdots\times d_{m}},\end{cases}

(5.22)

where
T_{c}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}T_{k}
. In what follows, we will show that
\{T_{j}^{\sigma}\}
is a solution to the system defined by a characteristic
\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}
.

\bullet
(Initial data): it is easy to check that

(T_{j}^{\sigma})^{0}=T_{j}^{\sigma}(0)=(T_{j}^{0})^{\sigma}.

\bullet
(Dynamics): it directly follows from the dynamics (
5.22
) of
T_{j}
that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} [\dot{T}_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}}&=[\dot{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}}\\
&=[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\displaystyle\sum_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\kappa_{i_{*}}\left([T_{c}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}-[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{c}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}\right).\end{aligned}

(5.23)

\diamond
(Free flow): the free flow can be represented as

[A_{j}]_{\alpha_{*0}\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}=[A_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}[T^{\sigma}_{j}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}.

(5.24)

\diamond
(Coupling flow): For handy notation, we write

\displaystyle\alpha_{\sigma(*)i_{\sigma(*)}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\sigma(1)i_{\sigma(1)}}\alpha_{\sigma(2)i_{\sigma(2)}}\cdots\alpha_{\sigma(m)i_{\sigma(m)}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{\sigma(*)(1-i_{\sigma(*)})}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\sigma(1)(1-i_{\sigma(1)})}\alpha_{\sigma(2)(1-i_{\sigma(2)})}\cdots\alpha_{\sigma(m)(1-i_{\sigma(m)})}.

Then, the coupling term in the right-hand side of (
5.23
) can be represented as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{i_{*}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\kappa_{i_{*}}\left([T_{c}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{j}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}-[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*i_{*}}}[\bar{T}_{c}]_{\alpha_{*1}}[T_{j}]_{\alpha_{*(1-i_{*})}}\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\sum_{i_{\sigma(*)}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\kappa^{\sigma}_{i_{\sigma(*)}}\Big([T^{\sigma}_{c}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)i_{\sigma(*)}}}[\bar{T}_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}[T_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)(1-i_{\sigma(*)})}}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt-[T_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)i_{\sigma(*)}}}[\bar{T}_{c}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}[T_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)(1-i_{\sigma(*)})}}\Big).\end{aligned}

(5.25)

In (
5.23
), we combine (
5.24
) and (
5.25
) to find dynamical system for
T_{j}^{\sigma}
:

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}[T_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}}=[A_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)0}\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}[T^{\sigma}_{j}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum_{i_{\sigma(*)}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\kappa^{\sigma}_{i_{\sigma(*)}}\Big([T^{\sigma}_{c}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)i_{\sigma(*)}}}[\bar{T}_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}[T_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)(1-i_{\sigma(*)})}}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-[T_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)i_{\sigma(*)}}}[\bar{T}_{c}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)1}}[T_{j}^{\sigma}]_{\alpha_{\sigma(*)(1-i_{\sigma(*)})}}\Big),\quad t>0,\\
T_{j}^{\sigma}(0)=(T_{j}^{0})^{\sigma}.\end{cases}

Finally, we can conclude that
\{T_{j}^{\sigma}\}
is a solution to the Cauchy problem whose characteristic symbol is
\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\{T_{j}\}
be a solution to the Cauchy problem corresponding to the characteristic symbol
\mathfrak{C}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{C}
has the following symmetry:

\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}

for some permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
. Then, the solution
\{T_{j}\}
also has the same type of symmetry:

T_{j}(t)=T_{j}^{\sigma}(t),\quad t\geq 0,\quad j\in[N].

Since
\{T_{j}\}
and
\{T_{j}^{\sigma}\}
are just rearrangement of each other, two characteristic symbols
\mathfrak{C}
and
\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}
are essentially identical. To fix the idea, we define an equivalent relation
\sim
on
\mathfrak{D}
as follows:

\mathfrak{C}\sim\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma},\quad\mathfrak{C}\in\mathfrak{D}_{m},\quad\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Moreover, the equivalence class is defined as

[\mathfrak{C}]_{\sim}:=\{\mathfrak{C}^{\sigma}:\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}\}.

Now, we define the binary operation, denoted by
\star_{\sim}
, between two equivalence classes. In fact, such binary operation can be naturally defined from the fusion operation: for
\mathfrak{C}_{1}\in\mathfrak{D}_{m_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}_{m_{2}}
,

[\mathfrak{C}_{1}]\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}_{2}]:=[\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}].

In the following lemma, we show that the binary operation
\star_{\sim}
is well-defined.

Lemma 5.4
.

The binary operation
\star_{\sim}
is well-defined.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2}
be two characteristic symbols whose ranks are
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, respectively. In order to establish the well-definedness of
\star_{\sim}
, it suffices to show that

\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}\sim\mathfrak{C}_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}^{\sigma_{2}},\quad\textup{for all $\sigma_{1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{m_{1}}$ and $\sigma_{2}\in\mathfrak{S}_{m_{2}}$.}

(5.26)

To verify the relation (
5.26
), we need to construct an index shuffling operator (or a permutation) on
m_{1}+m_{2}
letters such that

(\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2})^{\sigma}=\mathfrak{C}_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}^{\sigma_{2}}.

(5.27)

In this regard, we define
\sigma:=\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2}\in\mathfrak{S}_{m_{1}+m_{2}}
as

\sigma(i)=\begin{cases}\sigma_{1}(i)\quad&\text{for }1\leq i\leq m_{1},\\
m_{2}+\sigma_{2}(i-m_{1})\quad&\text{for }m_{1}+1\leq i\leq m_{1}+m_{2}.\end{cases}

(5.28)

The first
m_{1}
components of
\sigma
come from
\sigma_{1}
and the last
m_{2}
terms come from
\sigma_{2}
. For
\sigma
defined in (
5.28
), we verify the relation (
5.27
) by considering four components of the characteristic symbols one by one.

(i) (Size vector): by straightforward calculation, one has

\displaystyle(\mathbf{d}^{1}\star_{s}\mathbf{d}^{2})^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=(d^{1}_{\sigma_{1}(1)},\cdots,d^{1}_{\sigma_{1}(m_{1})},d^{2}_{\sigma_{2}(1)},\cdots,d^{2}_{\sigma_{2}(m_{2})})=(\mathbf{d}^{1})^{\sigma_{1}}\star_{s}(\mathbf{d}^{2})^{\sigma_{2}}.

(ii) (Natural frequency tensors): again, we observe

\displaystyle[(A^{1}_{j}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{2})^{\sigma}]_{\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)0}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)0}\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)1}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=[A^{1}_{j}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{2}]_{\beta_{*0}\gamma_{*0}\beta_{*1}\gamma_{*1}}=[A_{j}^{1}]_{\beta_{*0}\beta_{*1}}\delta_{\gamma_{*0}\gamma_{*1}}+\delta_{\beta_{*0}\beta_{*1}}[A_{j}^{2}]_{\gamma_{*0}\gamma_{*1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=[(A_{j}^{1})^{\sigma_{1}}]_{\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)0}\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)1}}\delta_{\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)0}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)1}}+\delta_{\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)0}\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)1}}[(A_{j}^{2})^{\sigma_{2}}]_{\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)0}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=[(A_{j}^{1})^{\sigma_{1}}\star_{\textup{F}}(A_{j}^{2})^{\sigma_{2}}]_{\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)0}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)0}\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)1}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)1}}.

Hence, we see

(A_{j}^{1}\star_{\textup{F}}A_{j}^{2})^{\sigma}=(A_{j}^{1})^{\sigma_{1}}\star_{\textup{F}}(A_{j}^{2})^{\sigma_{2}}.

(iii) (Coupling strength tensors): it suffices to show the desired relation componentwise:

((\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})^{\sigma})_{(j_{\sigma_{1}(*)},k_{\sigma_{2}(*)})}=(\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})_{(j_{*},k_{*})}.

(5.29)

By the definition of
\star_{c}
, we observe

\displaystyle((\mathfrak{K}^{1}\star_{c}\mathfrak{K}^{2})^{\sigma})_{(j_{\sigma_{1}(*)},k_{\sigma_{2}(*)})}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\kappa_{j_{*}}^{1}\quad&\text{if }j_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}},\\
\kappa_{k_{*}}^{2}\quad&\text{if }j_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}},\\
\kappa_{\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}}^{1}+\kappa_{\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}^{2}\quad&\text{if }j_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}},\\
0\quad&\text{if }j_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(\kappa^{1})^{\sigma_{1}}_{j_{\sigma_{1}(*)}}\quad&\text{if }j_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}},\\
(\kappa^{2})^{\sigma_{2}}_{k_{\sigma_{2}(*)}}\quad&\text{if }j_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}},\\
(\kappa^{1})^{\sigma_{1}}_{\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}}+(\kappa^{2})^{\sigma_{2}}_{\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}}\quad&\text{if }j_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}=\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}},\\
0\quad&\text{if }j_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}\text{~and~}k_{*}\neq\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=((\mathfrak{K}^{1})^{\sigma_{1}}\star_{c}(\mathfrak{K}^{2})^{\sigma_{2}})_{(j_{\sigma_{1}(*)},k_{\sigma_{2}(*)})}.

Hence, we verify (
5.29
).

(iv) (Initial configuration): By direct observation, one has

\displaystyle[((T_{j}^{1})^{0}\star_{i}(T_{j}^{2})^{0})^{\sigma}]_{\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)}}

\displaystyle=[(T_{j}^{1})^{0}\star_{i}(T_{j}^{2})^{0}]_{\beta_{*}\gamma_{*}}=[(T_{j}^{1})^{0}]_{\beta_{*}}[(T_{j}^{2})^{0}]_{\gamma_{*}}

\displaystyle=[(T_{j}^{1})^{0}]_{\beta_{*}}[(T_{j}^{2})^{0}]_{\gamma_{*}}=[((T_{j}^{1})^{0})^{\sigma_{1}}]_{\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)}}[((T_{j}^{2})^{0})^{\sigma_{2}}]_{\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)}}

\displaystyle=[((T_{j}^{1})^{0})^{\sigma_{1}}\star_{i}((T_{j}^{2})^{0})^{\sigma_{2}}]_{\beta_{\sigma_{1}(*)}\gamma_{\sigma_{2}(*)}}.

This yields the desired relation:

((T_{j}^{1})^{0}\star_{i}(T_{j}^{2})^{0})^{\sigma}=((T_{j}^{1})^{0})^{\sigma_{1}}\star_{i}((T_{j}^{2})^{0})^{\sigma_{2}}.

By combining (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv), we obtain the desired relation (
5.27
) which gives (
5.26
).
∎

Since the operator
\star_{\sim}
is well-defined, we know that the quotient object
(\mathfrak{D}/\sim,\star_{\sim})
has also a monoid structure inherited from
(\mathfrak{D},\star)
. Indeed, we show that the quotient object
(\mathfrak{D}/\sim,\star_{\sim})
is a commutative monoid.

Theorem 5.3
.

(\mathfrak{D}/_{\sim},\star_{\sim})
is a commutative monoid.

Proof.

The fact that
(\mathfrak{D}/_{\sim},\star_{\sim})
becomes a monoid directly follows from the fact that
(\mathfrak{D},\star)
is a monoid. Thus, it suffices to show that for any
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
,

[\mathfrak{C}_{1}]\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}_{2}]=[\mathfrak{C}_{2}]\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}_{1}].

(5.30)

For (
5.30
), we claim that

\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}\sim\mathfrak{C}_{2}\star\mathfrak{C}_{1}.

(5.31)

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be the ranks of
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
, respectively. Then, we define the permutation on
m_{1}+m_{2}
letters as follows:

\sigma(i):=\begin{cases}i+m_{2},\quad&1\leq i\leq m_{1},\\
i-m_{1},\quad&m_{1}+1\leq i\leq m_{1}+m_{2}.\end{cases}

By similar arguments in Lemma
5.4
, we can see

(\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2})^{\sigma}=\mathfrak{C}_{2}\star\mathfrak{C}_{1}.

This gives (
5.31
). Then, we have

[\mathfrak{C}_{1}]\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}_{2}]=[\mathfrak{C}_{1}\star\mathfrak{C}_{2}]=[\mathfrak{C}_{2}\star\mathfrak{C}_{1}]=[\mathfrak{C}_{2}]\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}_{1}].

This shows that
(\mathfrak{D}/_{\sim},\star_{\sim})
is commutative.
∎

5.6.
Unique factorizability and separability

In Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.5
, we have studied the separability of a solution to the weakly coupled model. In what follows, we provide a relation between the separability of a solution and the unique factorizability of a commutative monoid.

Definition 5.1
.

(1)

Let
(M,*)
be a semigroup. Then, an element
a\in M
is left-cancellative (or right-cancellative) if for all
b,c\in M
,
a*b=a*c
(or
b*a=c*a
) always implies that
b=c
. An element is said to be cancellative if it is both left- and right-cancellative. If all elements in
M
are cancellative, then
M
is cancellative.

(2)

Let
(M,*)
be a commutative monoid with the identity element denoted by
e
. If
a\in M
has an inverse in
M
, i.e., there exists
b\in M
such that
ab=ba=e
, then we say that
a
is a unit of the given monoid.

(3)

Let
(M,*)
be a commutative monoid. For
a,b\in M
, we say that
a
divides
b
if there exists
c\in M
such that
b=a*c
, and we write
a|b
. Then, an equivalence relation
\sim
naturally arises, and we write
a\sim b
if
a|b
and
b|a
.

(4)

An element
a\in M
is invertible if there exists
b\in M
such that
a*b=1
. An element
p\in M
is a prime if it is not invertible(or it is not a unit) and

p|a*b\quad\textup{implies}\quad p|a\quad\textup{or}\quad p|b,\quad\forall a,b\in M.

An element
q\in M
is irreducible if
q
is not invertible and

q\sim a*b\quad\textup{implies}\quad q\sim a\quad\textup{or}\quad q\sim b,\quad\forall a,b\in M.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a characteristic symbol of the Kuramoto model (
3.4
), i.e.,

\mathfrak{C}=\left(\emptyset,\frac{\kappa}{2},\{\mathrm{i}\nu_{j}\},\{e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}^{0}}\}\right).

If we define
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
as

\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}=\left(\emptyset,-\frac{\kappa}{2},\{-\mathrm{i}\nu_{j}\},\{e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}^{0}}\}\right),

then we can easily check that

\mathfrak{C}\star\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}=\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}\star\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}_{e},

and this yields

[\mathfrak{C}]\star_{\sim}[\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}]=[\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}]\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}]=[\mathfrak{C}_{e}].

Finally, we can conclude that for any
\kappa
,
\{\nu_{j}\}
, and
\{\theta_{j}^{0}\}
, we know that
[\mathfrak{C}]
has an inverse in
(\mathfrak{D}/\sim,\star_{\sim})
. Furthermore,
[\mathfrak{C}]
is a unit of
(\mathfrak{D}/\sim,\star_{\sim})
.

Note that the set of nonnegative integers under addition is a cancellative monoid and the set of positive integers under multiplication is a cancellative monoid.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(M,*)
be a commutative cancellative monoid. If

p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{n_{1}}=q_{1}q_{2}\cdots q_{n_{2}},\quad\textup{where all $p_{i}$ and $q_{j}$ are primes},

then
n_{1}=n_{2}=n
, and
p_{i}=u_{i}q_{\sigma(i)}
for some permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and unit elements
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

Proof.

For a proof, we refer the reader to
[
22
]
.
∎

The following corollary directly follows from Lemma
5.5
in terms of the characteristic symbol.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a characteristic symbol. Then, there exists a unique factorization of
[\mathfrak{C}]
up to an order and unit. In other words, there exists characteristic symbols
\mathfrak{C}_{i}
for
i\in[n]
such that

[\mathfrak{C}]=[\mathfrak{C}_{1}]\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}_{2}]\star_{\sim}\cdots\star_{\sim}[\mathfrak{C}_{n}]

and at most one
[\mathfrak{C}_{i}]
can be a unit.
For the case of
n=1
and
[\mathfrak{C}_{1}]
not being a unit, then
[\mathfrak{C}]
is a prime.

Remark 5.5
.

(1)

It follows from Grothendieck’s theory that a commutative monoid can always be extended to an abelian group by introducing suitable inverses. In this case, such an abelian group is called the Grothendieck group. The easiest example of a Grothendieck group is the construction of
\mathbb{Z}
from
\mathbb{N}
. In this manner, we would extend
(\mathfrak{D}/_{\sim},\star_{\sim})
to an abelian group.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a characteristic symbol whose size vector is given as
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{m})
, and let
T_{j}
be a solution to the Cauchy problem corresponding to
\mathfrak{C}
. Then, the number of entries for
T_{j}
is
\#(\mathbf{d}):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}
. Thus, if the number
\#(\mathbf{d})
is larger, then the computational cost for numerical simulation would be higher. Thus, it is crucially required to reduce such numerical cost. Suppose that the characteristic symbol
\mathfrak{C}
can be decomposed as follows:

\mathfrak{C}=\bs_{j=1}^{n}\mathfrak{C}_{j},

and let
\mathbf{d}^{j}
be the size vector of
\mathfrak{C}_{j}
. Then, the number of all elements of tensors is
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\#(\mathbf{d}^{j})
. Since

\#(\mathbf{d})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\#(\mathbf{d}^{j}),

we have

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\#(\mathbf{d}^{j})\leq 2(n-1)+\frac{\#(\mathbf{d})}{2^{n-1}}.

Thus, we can roughly measure how the computational cost is reduced when the system is decomposed into subsystems. Precisely, the ratio of reduction is at least

\frac{2(n-1)}{\#(\mathbf{d})}+\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}.

In this way, our separability results would be applied to reduce the computational cost.

Emergent dynamics of weakly coupled models for low-rank tensors

In this section, we study the emergent dynamics of the weakly coupled model for low-rank tensors that can be derived from the double tensor model in a specific case. As introduced in Section
3.2
, the rank-0, 1, 2 would be related to the Kuramoto model, the swarm sphere model and the Lohe matrix model, respectively. Since coupled systems between two models of the same ranks have been investigated in literature, we study coupled systems between two models of different ranks. We recall that weakly coupled model (
4.2
) of the Kuramoto model and the swarm sphere model is introduced in Section
4.1
. We here focus on the weakly coupled model of the swarm sphere model and the Lohe matrix model. For this purpose, we consider the LT model whose characteristic symbol is given by the fusion operation of two characteristic symbols corresponding to the swarm sphere model and the Lohe matrix model:

\mathfrak{C}=(\mathbf{d},\mathfrak{K},\{\Omega_{j}\},\{T_{j}^{0}\})=(d,(\kappa,0),\{\Omega_{j}\},\{x_{j}^{0}\})\star\left((n,n),\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\kappa}{2}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\{\mathrm{i}H_{j}\},\{U_{j}^{0}\}\right),

(6.1)

where natural frequency tensors
\Omega_{j}
and
H_{j}
are
d\times d
skew-symmetric matrix and skew-hermitian matrix, respectively, and initial data

and
U_{j}^{0}
belong to
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
\mathbf{U}(n)
, respectively. Then, the corresponding Cauchy problem for (

) reads as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{j}=\Omega_{j}x_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\langle U_{j},U_{k}\rangle_{\textup{F}}x_{k}-\langle U_{k},U_{j}\rangle_{\textup{F}}\langle x_{k},x_{j}\rangle x_{j}\Big),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\

where

is an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix. However for the simplicity of calculation, we will consider

Let

Remark 6.1

It follows from the uniqueness of a solution to the LT model (

A_{j}

\mathfrak{C}

On the other hand, the following proposition can be directly deduced from the definition of the fusion operation
\star

6.1
and Remark
6.1

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\{\mathfrak{C}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{n}
be a set of real characteristic symbols. Then,
\displaystyle\bs_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathfrak{C}_{\ell}

Now, we consider the following Cauchy problem on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbf{SO}(n)
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{j}=\Omega_{j}x_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle U_{j},U_{k}\rangle_{\textup{F}}(x_{k}-\langle x_{k},x_{j}\rangle x_{j}),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\

6.1
or straightforward calculation that
U_{i}
and

remain in
\mathbf{SO}(n)
and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, respectively. Before we study the emergent dynamics of the whole ensemble, we recall several definitions in relation to the collective dynamics of (

).

Definition 6.2
.

(1)

System (
6.2
) exhibits complete aggregation if the following estimate holds:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\Big(|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|+\|U_{i}(t)-U_{j}(t)\|_{\textup{F}}\Big)=0.

(2)

\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\Big(|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|+\|U_{i}(t)-U_{j}(t)\|_{\textup{F}}\Big)=0.

(3)

The state
\{(x_{j},U_{j})\}
tends to a partially locked state if the following relation holds:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\Big(|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|\Big)=0\quad\textup{and}\quad\exists~~\lim_{t\to\infty}U_{i}(t)U_{j}^{\top}(t).

In the following subsections, we study the emergent dynamics of (

) depending on the dissimilarity of natural frequency tensors:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\textup{(i) Homogeneous ensemble:}\quad\Omega_{j}\equiv\Omega,\quad A_{j}\equiv A,\quad j\in[N].\\
&\textup{(ii) Heterogeneous ensemble:}\quad\Omega_{i}\neq\Omega_{j},\quad A_{i}\neq A_{j},\quad\textup{for some $i\neq j$.}\\
&\textup{(iii) Partially homogeneous ensemble:}\quad\Omega_{j}=\Omega,\quad A_{i}\neq A_{j},\quad\textup{for some $i\neq j$.}\end{aligned}

6.1.
Homogeneous ensemble

In this subsection, we consider a homogeneous ensemble:

\Omega_{i}=O,\quad A_{i}=O,\quad i\in[N].

In this case, system (
6.2
) with zero frequency tensors reads as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{j}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle U_{j},U_{k}\rangle_{\textup{F}}(x_{k}-\langle x_{k},x_{j}\rangle x_{j}),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle{\dot{U}}_{j}=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x_{j},x_{k}\rangle(U_{k}-U_{j}U_{k}^{\top}U_{j}),\vskip 5.69046pt\\
x_{j}(0)=x_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\quad U_{j}(0)=U_{j}^{0}\in{\mathbf{SO}}(n),\quad j\in[N].\end{cases}

(6.3)

For aggregation estimate, we set

h_{ij}:=\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle,\quad G_{ij}:=U_{i}U_{j}^{\top},\quad g_{ij}:=\langle U_{i},U_{j}\rangle_{\textup{F}}=\textup{tr}(G_{ij}),

(6.4)

and define the functionals:

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}):=\min_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|1-h_{ij}|,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\|U_{i}-U_{j}\|_{\textup{F}}.

Below, we provide a positively invariant region for (
6.3
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose initial data
\{(x_{i}^{0},U_{i}^{0}))\}_{i=1}^{N}
satisfy

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})>0,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}^{0})<\sqrt{2},

(6.5)

and let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{U})
be a solution to (
6.3
). Then, one has

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))>0\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}(t))<\sqrt{2},\quad t>0.

Proof.

It follows from (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\dot{h}}_{ij}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(g_{ik}(h_{kj}-h_{ik}h_{ij})+g_{jk}(h_{ik}-h_{kj}h_{ij})\Big),\\
&{\dot{G}}_{ij}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(h_{ik}(G_{kj}-G_{ik}G_{ij})+h_{jk}(G_{ik}-G_{ij}G_{kj})\Big).\end{aligned}

(6.6)

Since a solution to (
6.3
) is analytic and we assume (
6.5
), there exists a finite time
T_{1}>0
such that

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))>0,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}(t))<\sqrt{2},\quad t\in[0,T_{1}).

Moreover, there also exists a finite time
T_{2}>0
such that

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))=h_{i_{t}j_{t}}(t),\quad t\in[0,T_{2}).

We set

T_{*}:=\min\{T_{1},T_{2}\}.

Since the following relation holds:

\|U_{i}-U_{j}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}=2(d-g_{ij})<2,\quad\textup{or equivalently,}\quad g_{ij}>d-1,\quad t\in[0,T_{*}),

one has

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})&\geq\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(g_{i_{t}k}(h_{kj_{t}}-h_{i_{t}k}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))+g_{j_{t}k}(h_{i_{t}k}-h_{kj_{t}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))\Big)\\
&>\frac{\kappa(n-1)}{N}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-h_{i_{t}k}+1-h_{kj_{t}})>0,\quad t\in[0,T_{*}).\end{aligned}

On the other hand, we rewrite
\eqref{E-5}_{2}
as

\displaystyle{\dot{g}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}h_{ik}\Big(\|I_{n}-G_{ij}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}-\|I_{n}-G_{kj}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2})+h_{jk}(\|I_{n}-G_{ij}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}-\|I_{n}-G_{kj}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+(2-\|I_{n}-G_{ij}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2})\cdot\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(h_{ik}\|I_{n}-G_{ik}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}+h_{jk}\|I_{n}-G_{jk}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}\Big).

By the same argument, one has

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}&=-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(h_{i_{t}k}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}-\|I_{n}-G_{kj_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2})+h_{j_{t}k}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}-\|I_{n}-G_{ki_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2})\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-(2-\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2})\cdot\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(h_{i_{t}k}\|I_{n}-G_{ki_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}+h_{j_{t}k}\|I_{n}-G_{kj_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}\Big)\\
&<0,\quad t\in[0,T_{*}).\end{aligned}

Hence, if we continue the process above successively, then we obtain the desired assertion.
∎

Now, we are ready to provide a sufficient condition leading to complete aggregation for (
5.3
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose the initial data
(\mathcal{X}^{0},\mathcal{U}^{0})
satisfy

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})>0,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}^{0})<\sqrt{2},

and let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{U})
be a solution to (
6.3
). Then, system (
6.3
) exhibits complete aggregation:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}(t))+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}(t))\Big)=0.

Moreover, the convergence rate is exponential.

Proof.

Since we assume (
5.21
), we can apply Lemma
5.1
:

h_{ij}(t)>0,\quad g_{ij}(t)>d-1,\quad t>0,\quad i\neq j.

Note that

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}(t))^{2}=\max_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|^{2}=2(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))).

Now we choose extremal indices
(i_{t},j_{t})
such that

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))=\langle x_{i_{t}},x_{j_{t}}\rangle.

Then, one has

\displaystyle 1-h_{i_{t}k}+1-h_{j_{t}k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(|x_{i_{t}}-x_{k}|^{2}+|x_{j_{t}}-x_{k}|^{2})>\frac{1}{4}(|x_{i_{t}}-x_{k}|+|x_{j_{t}}-x_{k}|)^{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{4}|x_{i_{t}}-x_{j_{t}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})).

It follows from (
5.8
) and (
5.12
) that

\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})>\frac{\kappa(n-1)}{N}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-h_{i_{t}k}+1-h_{j_{t}k})>\frac{\kappa(n-1)}{2}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})).

Thus, we obtain the desired exponential convergence of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})
. On the other hand for
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})
, we use (
5.14
) to find

\displaystyle\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}<-\frac{\kappa}{4}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})(2-\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2})\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}.

This shows the desired assertion.
∎

6.2.
Heterogeneous ensemble

In this subsection, we consider a heterogeneous ensemble:

\Omega_{i}\neq\Omega_{j},\quad A_{i}\neq A_{j}\quad\textup{for some $i\neq j\in[N]$.}

(6.9)

Due to the dissimilarity between natural frequencies, we might not expect the emergence of complete aggregation. Instead, our goal is to provide a sufficient framework leading to practical aggregation.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose initial data and system parameters satisfy

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})>\frac{1}{2},\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}^{0})^{2}<1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}},\quad\kappa>\min\left\{\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})},\frac{8\mathcal{D}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})}\right\},

(6.10)

and let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{U})
be a solution to (
6.2
) with (
6.9
). Then, practical aggregation emerges asymptotically:

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}(t))\leq 1-\sqrt{1-\frac{8\mathcal{D}(\Omega)}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}(t))\leq 1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}}.

Proof.

By slightly modifying (
6.6
), one has

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\dot{h}}_{ij}=\langle x_{i},(\Omega_{j}-\Omega_{i})x_{j}\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(g_{ik}(h_{kj}-h_{ik}h_{ij})+g_{jk}(h_{ik}-h_{kj}h_{ij})\Big),\\
&{\dot{G}}_{ij}=A_{i}G_{ij}-G_{ij}A_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(h_{ik}(G_{kj}-G_{ik}G_{ij})+h_{jk}(G_{ik}-G_{ij}G_{kj})\Big).\end{aligned}

(6.11)

Now, we use the conditions on initial data:

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})>\frac{1}{2},\quad\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})>d-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}}\right)>0,

and the continuity of solutions to see that there exists a finite time
T_{*}>0
such that

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))>0,\quad\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}(t))>0,\quad t\in[0,T_{*}).

It follows from (
5.12
) and (
6.11
) that

\displaystyle\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})>\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))-\mathcal{D}(\Omega),\quad t\in[0,T_{*}).

At
t=0
, since
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})>\frac{1}{2}
, one has

\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})\biggl|_{t=0}>\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))-\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\biggl|_{t=0}=\frac{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})}{2}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0}))-\mathcal{D}(\Omega)>0.

Thus, we see that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})
begins to increase

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))>\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0}),\quad t\in[0,T_{**}).

For
t\in[0,T_{**})
, the diameter
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}=-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(h_{i_{t}k}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}-\|I_{n}-G_{kj_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2})+h_{j_{t}k}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}-\|I_{n}-G_{ki_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2})\Big)\\
&-(2-\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2})\cdot\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}h_{i_{t}k}\|I_{n}-G_{ki_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}+h_{j_{t}k}\|I_{n}-G_{kj_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2})+\textup{tr}(G_{i_{t}j_{t}}(A_{i}-A_{j}))\\
&<-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})(2-\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2})\cdot\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\|I_{n}-G_{ki_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}+\|I_{n}-G_{kj_{t}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}\Big)+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\\
&<-\frac{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}{2}(2-\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2})D(\mathcal{U})^{2}+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}).\end{aligned}

Since
\mathcal{D}({\mathcal{U}}^{0})
satisfies (
6.10
), then we have

\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}\biggl|_{t=0}<0,

which implies that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})
starts to increase and
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})
start to decrease. Thus, if we continue this process, then we conclude

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))>\frac{1}{2},\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}(t))^{2}<1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}},\quad t>0.

In addition, since
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})
satisfy for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})>\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))-\mathcal{D}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}<-\frac{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}{2}(2-\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2})D(\mathcal{U})^{2}+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}).

By dynamical systems theory, there exists a finite entrance time
T_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}(t))>\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{8\mathcal{D}(\Omega)}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})}}\right)\quad\textup{or}\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}(t))^{2}<1-\sqrt{1-\frac{8\mathcal{D}(\Omega)}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}(t))^{2}<1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}},\quad t>T_{1}.

This shows the desired assertion.
∎

6.3.
Partially homogeneous ensemble

In this subsection, we consider a partially homogeneous ensemble:

\Omega_{\ell}\equiv\Omega,\quad\ell=1,\cdots,N,\quad A_{i}\neq A_{j}\quad\textup{for some $i\neq j$.}

(6.12)

Although the natural frequencies
\{\Omega_{j}\}
are the same, complete aggregation for
\{x_{j}\}
can emerge. However, since
\{A_{j}\}
are different in general,
\{U_{j}\}
tends to locked state. Thus, our goal in this subsection is to find a sufficient framework such that the following relation holds:

\lim_{t\to\infty}|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|=0\quad\textup{and}\quad\exists~~\lim_{t\to\infty}U_{i}(t)U_{j}^{\top}(t).

First, we show that relative distances for
\{x_{j}\}
tend to zero and the maximal diameter
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})
can be controlled in terms of the coupling strength
\kappa
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose initial data and system parameters satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})>0,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}^{0})^{2}<1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}},\quad\kappa>\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})},

and let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{U})
be a solution to (
6.2
) with (
6.12
). Then, there exists
T_{*}>0
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}(t))=0,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}(t)^{2}<1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}},\quad t>T_{*}.

Proof.

We closely follow Theorem
6.3
to conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})>\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U}^{0})\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})),\\
&\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2}<-\frac{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}{2}(2-\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})^{2})D(\mathcal{U})^{2}+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}).\end{aligned}

(6.13)

Thus, inequalities (
6.13
) are verified. For the convergence of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})
, we use
\eqref{F-20}_{1}
to show that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})
tends to 1 and recall the relation
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})^{2}=2(1-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))
.
∎

In order to establish the desired convergence of
G_{ij}=U_{i}U_{j}^{\top}
, we adopt the strategy in
[
21
]
. Recall that
G_{ij}
satisfies

\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}G_{ij}=A_{i}G_{ij}-G_{ij}A_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(h_{ik}(G_{kj}-G_{ik}G_{ij})+h_{jk}(G_{ik}-G_{ij}G_{kj})\Big),

(6.14)

and it follows from Lemma
6.2
that all
h_{ij}
converges to 1. Thus, we rewrite (
6.14
) as

\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}G_{ij}=A_{i}G_{ij}-G_{ij}A_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big((G_{kj}-G_{ik}G_{ij})+(G_{ik}-G_{ij}G_{kj})\Big)+\kappa\varepsilon_{ij},

where the auxiliary functional
\varepsilon_{ij}
is defined as

\varepsilon_{ij}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big((h_{ik}-1)(G_{kj}-G_{ik}G_{ij})+(h_{jk}-1)(G_{ik}-G_{ij}G_{kj})\Big).

Let
\{(\tilde{x}_{j},\tilde{U}_{j})\}
be any solution to (
6.2
) with (
6.12
). Then, we write

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{ij}:=\tilde{U}_{i}\tilde{U}_{j}^{\top},\quad\tilde{h}_{ij}:=\langle\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{j}\rangle,

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}_{ij}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big((\tilde{h}_{ik}-1)(\tilde{G}_{kj}-\tilde{G}_{ik}\tilde{G}_{ij})+(\tilde{h}_{jk}-1)(\tilde{G}_{ik}-\tilde{G}_{ij}\tilde{G}_{kj})\Big).

Then, we observe

\displaystyle(G_{ij}-\tilde{G}_{ij})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=A_{i}(G_{ij}-\tilde{G}_{ij})-(G_{ij}-\tilde{G}_{ij})A_{j}+\varepsilon_{ij}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{ij}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big((G_{kj}-G_{ik}G_{ij})-(\tilde{G}_{kj}-\tilde{G}_{ik}\tilde{G}_{ij})

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+(G_{ik}-G_{ij}G_{kj})-(\tilde{G}_{ik}-\tilde{G}_{ij}\tilde{G}_{kj})\Big).

Define the diameter between
G_{ij}
:

d(\mathcal{U},\tilde{\mathcal{U}}):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\|G_{ij}-\tilde{G}_{ij}\|_{\textup{F}}=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\|U_{i}U_{j}^{\top}-\tilde{U}_{i}\tilde{U}_{j}^{\top}\|_{\textup{F}}.

Then, after algebraic manipulation, one finds a differential inequality for
d(\mathcal{U},\tilde{\mathcal{U}})
:

\frac{{\textup{d}}}{{\textup{d}t}}d(\mathcal{U},\tilde{\mathcal{U}})\leq-2\kappa(1-2\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}))d(\mathcal{U},\tilde{\mathcal{U}})+\|\varepsilon_{ij}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{ij}\|_{\textup{F}}.

Below, we recall an elementary lemma whose proof can be found in Lemma A.1 of
[
6
]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose that
f=f(t)\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is a positive and bounded function whose argument tends to zero, as
t\to\infty
, and let
y=y(t)
be a nonnegative
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-function satisfying

\begin{cases}\dot{y}\leq-ay+f,\quad t>0,\\
y(0)=y_{0},\end{cases}

where
a
is a positive constant. Then,
y=y(t)
tends to zero with an integrable convergence rate.

\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)=0.

Now, we are ready to state the main theorem.

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose initial data and system parameters satisfy

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})>0,\quad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U}^{0})^{2}<1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\kappa\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})}},\quad\kappa>\frac{4\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}^{0})},

and let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{U})
be a solution to (
6.2
) with (
6.12
). Then, a solution to system (
6.2
) tends to the partially locked state.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
6.2
that relative distances for
\mathcal{X}
tend to zero. Thus, it suffices to consider the dynamics of
\mathcal{U}
. From the arguments in the above, we show that
d(\mathcal{U},\tilde{\mathcal{U}})
tends to zero. As a next step, since our system is autonomous, for any
T>0
, we choose
\tilde{U}_{j}
and
\tilde{V}_{j}
as

\tilde{U}_{j}(t)=U_{j}(t+T).

By discretizing the time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
as
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and setting
T=m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we conclude that
\{U_{i}(n)U_{j}^{\top}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
becomes a Cauchy sequence in the complete spaces
\mathbf{SO}(n)
. Hence, there exists constant unitary matrices
U_{ij}^{\infty}\in\mathbf{SO}(n)
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|U_{i}(t)U_{j}^{\top}(t)-U_{ij}^{\infty}\|_{\textup{F}}=0.

This establishes the convergence of
U_{i}U_{j}^{\top}
.
∎

7.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have provided a systematic algebraic approach for the weak coupling of the Lohe tensor models with possibly different ranks and sizes. The Lohe tensor model incorporates well-studied first-order aggregation models such as the Kuramoto model, the swarm sphere model, the Lohe matrix model that have been extensively studied in previous literature. For the systematic modeling of the weak coupling of two models, we introduced a characteristic symbol corresponding to a given Cauchy problem to the Lohe tensor model, and it consists of four components, namely a size vector, a coupling strength tensor, set of natural frequency tensors and initial configuration, and then we also introduce a fusion operation
\star
which provides a binary operation on the space of all characteristic symbols. It is worthwhile to mention that such fusion operation can form a monoid structure. In this way, we can derive a weakly coupled model for multiple Lohe tensor models. As a concrete example, we presented a weakly coupled model for the swarm sphere model and the Lohe matrix model. For this weakly coupled model, we also provided several sufficient frameworks leading to collective emergent dynamics. Of course, there are many untouched issues in this work. For example, we have not studied the emergent dynamics of the multiple tensor model and did not investigate the solution structure of the proposed model. These interesting issues will be left for future work.

A gluing construction of D k D_{k} ALF gravitational instantons and existence of non-holomorphic minimal spheres
Xuwen Zhu

Abstract
. This note extends the construction of D k D_{k} ALF gravitational instantons in Singer–Schroers [ SS21 ] to a new case where the nonlinear superposition is given by the D 1 D_{1} Atiyah–Hitchin metric and k − 1 k-1 copies of A 0 A_{0} Taub-NUT metrics. We then give a general class of ALF spaces such that each of them contains a non-holomorphic minimal sphere. Together with Foscolo’s construction [ Fos19 ] this gives a large class of K ​ 3 K3 surfaces containing non-holomorphic minimal spheres.



1.
Introduction

A gravitational instanton is a complete noncompact 4-dimensional hyperKähler manifold with
L^{2}
curvature decay at infinity. There has been a long history in the study of gravitational instantons and a lot of recent developments have been made. Gravitational instantons arise as building blocks for degeneration of K3 surfaces
[
GW00
,
Fos19
,
HSVZ22
,
CVZ20
]
, which makes them powerful tools in probing
K3
surfaces near degeneration limits.

The gravitational instantons have been classified into six types, ALE / F / G / H / G* / H*, based on the end structure at infinity
[
Min10
,
CC21
,
SZ22
]
. In this paper we will discuss the ALF gravitational instantons, which is modeled at infinity by quotients of circle bundles over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and has cubic volume growth. There are two families of ALF spaces depending on the monodromy at infinity being cyclic or dihedral,
A_{k}
and
D_{k}
, labelled by non-negative integers
k
. In this paper we will discuss the construction of
D_{k}
type ALF spaces via a nonlinear superposition of
D_{1}
and
k-1
copies of
A_{0}
ALF spaces, and using this we will show the existence of non-holomorphic minimal spheres in these spaces as well as in
K3
surfaces.

This gluing construction extends the work by Schroers and Singer
[
SS21
]
, who obtained
D_{k}
ALF gravitational instantons by gluing the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{0}
of centered SU(2) monopoles (which is the only
D_{0}
ALF space) with
k
copies of Taub-NUT space (each of which is an
A_{0}
ALF space). As commented in their paper, this construction can be extended to other cases. In this paper we work on the case where the center piece is replaced by the Atiyah–Hitchin metric AH
[
AH88
]
, which is defined to be the double cover of
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{0}
and is a rotationally invariant
D_{1}
space. A rough statement of the theorem is given below and a more explicit description will be given at Theorem
3
.

Theorem 1
.

For any
k\geq 1
and any set
\{0,p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
of
k+1
distinct points, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
, such that for any
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
there exists a
D_{k+1}
ALF metric
g_{\epsilon}
which is a nonlinear superposition of the Atiyah–Hitchin metric centered at 0 and
k
copies of Taub-NUT metrics centered at
p_{i}/\epsilon,1\leq i\leq k
.

There has been a lot of progress in the analysis and construction of ALF spaces
[
Min09
,
Min10
,
CC19
,
BM11
]
. The
A_{k}
type ALF has been well understood as a multi-Taub-NUT space via gluing construction
[
Min11
,
GH78
]
. And
D_{k}
spaces have been constructed via twistor methods
[
Hit84
]
and desingularization
[
BM11
,
Auv18
,
Dan93
]
.
The construction in
[
SS21
]
gives an explicit gluing constructions of
D_{k}
type ALF based on Sen’s proposal
[
Sen97
]
, and the construction follows the physical interpretation of modeling electronically charged particles like protons and electrons
[
AMS12
]
. There are also works by Cherkis, Kapustin, Hitchin, Roček et.al
[
CH05
,
CK98
,
CK99
,
LRc88
,
IRc96
]
describing ALF spaces as moduli space of monopoles with prescribed singularities. The construction in
[
SS21
]
gave
D_{k}
ALF when the
k
Taub-NUT singularities are well separated from the origin, while the ones constructed in this paper can be viewed as the limit of these
D_{k}
spaces when one Taub-NUT singularity approaches zero.

It can be potentially further generalized, as suggested in
[
SS21
]
, to cases of composing a
D_{j}
ALF space with various
A_{\nu_{i}-1}
ALF to generate other
D_{k^{\prime}}
spaces, where
k^{\prime}=j+\sum\nu_{i}
, which would give other limiting cases when more TN singularities travel to zero or to each other. Since in this paper we are mainly interested in using the property of Atiyah–Hitchin space, we will only work on one generalization where the center piece is exactly given by AH.

Our construction follows the same procedure and the analysis is done following
[
SS21
]
, as the change of the center piece from
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{2}
to
AH
does not change the asymptotic behavior. The mapping properties of such metrics can be analyzed using the fiber boundary calculus, or
\phi-
calculus
[
MM98
,
Vai01
,
HHM04
]
, which captures the asymptotic metric structure of ALF spaces.

As a consequence of the construction, we obtain a family of
D_{k}
spaces that, with properly chosen marked points, degenerate to the Atiyah–Hitchin space as the Gromov Hausdorff limit. One special feature of the AH space is that it contains a stably minimal sphere that is not holomorphic with any of the complex structures in the hyperKähler rotation
[
MW93
]
. With this construction we can obtain such special sphere in all nearby
D_{k}
space.

Theorem 2

(same as Theorem
4
)
.

For any
k>1
, there is a family of
D_{k}
ALF metrics
\{g_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon)}
, such that each of them contains a minimal sphere that is not holomorphic with respect to any of the compatible complex structure.

In
[
Fos19
]
Foscolo constructed a collapsing sequence of K3 surfaces using ALF spaces as part of building blocks. As a corollary he used Atiyah–Hitchin space as the building block to show the existence of minimal non-holomorphic sphere in some K3 surface near the degeneration limit. Now with more ALF spaces constructed above with non-holomorphic spheres, we extend this result of Foscolo and show that there is a neighborhood in the moduli space of K3 surfaces such that each of these has a special sphere.

Corollary 1

(same as Corollary
3
)
.

There is an open neighborhood in the moduli space of hyperKähler metrics on
K3
, such that each metric in this neighborhood contains a stably minimal sphere that is not holomorphic with respect to any compatible complex structures.

This corollary is connected to one interpretation of the Weak Gravity Conjecture
[
AHMNV07
]
, which predicts the existence of “non-BPS” particles. In mathematics such particles can be interpreted as non-holomorphic submanifolds in Calabi–Yau manifolds in the large complex limit
[
HLM+20
,
DLMS20
,
LSVY23
]
. The corollary above can be seen as one of the examples.

The paper is organized in the following order: Section 2 describes the two building blocks, the multi-Taub-NUT space and the Atiyah-Hitchin metric, then gives the gluing construction and perturbative argument to find the hyperKähler metric. Section 3 proves the existence of non-holomorphic spheres in some
D_{k}
ALF spaces and
K3
surfaces.

2.
The gluing construction of
D_{k}
ALF spaces

The gluing construction is a modification from
[
SS21
]
, and we describe the construction below. The basic idea is to first construct an adiabatic Gibbons–Hawking Ansatz with symmetric Taub–NUT singularities which give the correct
D_{k}
asymptotic topology at infinity after the quotient of an involution action. After that we glue in a double branched cover of Atiyah Hitchin metric in the center. This produces an approximate hyperKähler metric after involution. Finally we solve for the actual hyperKähler metric by perturbation of hyperKähler triples. The set up is similar to
[
SS21
]
where the center piece is a double branched cover of
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{0}
. We also need to modify the coefficients in the adiabatic Gibbons–Hawking ansatz to be compatible with
AH
metric. On the other hand, the asymptotic behavior of
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{0}
and Atiyah Hitchin metric are similar since
AH
is the double cover of
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{0}
, the analysis used for perturbing the approximate triple to get the exact solution can be applied directly.

Now we describe the two pieces needed for gluing

2.1.
The multi-Taub-NUT space
M_{\epsilon}

Fix
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, and let
\epsilon\in(0,\delta^{2})
which will go to 0 in the limit later. Fix
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
sufficiently large, we consider a family of 4-manifolds
M_{\epsilon}
, each of which is a singular circle fibration over
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x|>2\epsilon\}
. We consider
the following adiabatic family of Gibbon–Hawking ansatz on
M_{\epsilon}

(1)

g_{\epsilon}=h_{\epsilon}\frac{|dx|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+h_{\epsilon}^{-1}\alpha^{2},\ \alpha=*_{\epsilon}dh_{\epsilon}

where
\alpha
is a connection 1-form of the circle bundle, and
*_{\epsilon}
is the Hodge star with respect to the flat metric
\frac{|dx|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}
on the base
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{|x|>2\epsilon}
; the singular harmonic function
h_{\epsilon}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{|x|>2\epsilon}
is given by

(2)

h_{\epsilon}=1-\frac{\epsilon}{|x|}+\sum_{v=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2|x+p_{v}|}+\frac{\epsilon}{2|x-p_{v}|}\right).

We take the singular fibration associated with the Gibbons–Hawking ansatz (
1
) by

(3)

\phi:M_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}_{|x|>2\epsilon}

and note the fibration is only singular at
P:=\{\pm p_{1},\dots,\pm p_{2}\}
.
This ansatz is modified from
[
SS21
, equation (1.4)]
by replacing the coefficients of
\frac{1}{|x|}
from
2\epsilon
to
\epsilon
which will match the the Atiyah–Hitchin metric in the center later. At
\pm p_{i}
this metric has a Taub-NUT type singularity, which is natural since
A_{k}
ALF spaces can be viewed as a multi-Taub-NUT space.

The involution action of
\iota:x\mapsto-x
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
applies to the fibration. Note that the asymptotic of
h_{\epsilon}
is given by

(4)

h_{\epsilon}=1+\frac{\epsilon(2k-2)}{2|x|}+O(\epsilon|x|^{-3}),\ |x|\gg 1

which, after quotient by the involution
\iota
, gives the asymptotic topology of a dihedral
D_{k+1}
ALF.

We recall one fact about the behavior of
g_{\epsilon}
when lifted to the compactified space, where the detailed proof was given in
[
SS21
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Definition 1
.

A compact manifold with boundary
(X,g)
has a fiber boundary structure, or
\phi
-structure, if there is a fibration
\phi:\partial X\rightarrow Y
where Y is a compact manifold without boundary and
F
is the fiber. The space of
\phi
-vector fields
\mathcal{V}_{\phi}
is given by b-vector fields (i.e. they are tangent to
\partial X
) which also satisfy

v|_{\partial X}\in C^{\infty}(\partial X,T(\partial X/Y)),

and

v(\rho)\in\rho^{2}C^{\infty}(X)

where
\rho
is a boundary defining function for
\partial X
.

Locally using coordinates
\phi(y_{1},\dots,y_{b},z_{1},\dots,z_{f})=(y_{1},\dots,y_{b})
, the
\phi
-vector fields are spanned by

\rho^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho},\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},\dots,\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{b}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{f}}.

And a
\phi
-metric is a smooth metric on the
\phi
-tangent bundle
T_{\phi}X
, locally given by

g_{\phi}=\frac{d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{4}}+\frac{\phi^{*}g_{Y}}{\rho^{2}}+g_{F}.

The fibration
\phi:M_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}_{|x|>2\epsilon}
can be lifted to the radially-compactified base
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\setminus\{|x|\leq 2\epsilon\}
. The radial compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is obtained by adding a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}=\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
at infinity. We use the inverted polar coordinates
\rho=1/|x|,y=\rho x
near the boundary, and the metric
g_{\epsilon}
in (
1
) lifts to a fiber boundary metric of the form

g_{\epsilon}=h_{\epsilon}(\frac{d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{4}}+\frac{|dy|^{2}}{\rho^{2}})+h_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bar{\alpha}^{2},

where
h_{\epsilon}
and
\bar{\alpha}=*_{\epsilon}dh_{\epsilon}
lift to be smooth on
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{|x|\leq 2\epsilon\}
(one can check this by changing variables in (
2
)).

2.2.
The branched cover
\widehat{AH}_{\epsilon}

We will glue the (double branched cover) of Atiyah–Hitchin metric into the center. Following the notation in
[
SS21
]
, we denote by
\widehat{AH}
the space
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\text{anti-diagonal}
, or in coordinates:

(5)

\widehat{AH}=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}:\ z_{1}\bar{w}_{1}+z_{2}\bar{w}_{2}\neq 0\}/\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}

This space can be identified via the space of X- and Y-ellipsis (see
[
SS21
, Appendix A]
for details). In the region near infinity, one can use coordinates
\{X\in\mathbb{C}^{3}|\sum_{i=1}^{3}X_{i}^{2}=1\}
. If we write
X=\tilde{x}+i\xi,\ \tilde{x},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then they satisfy

|\tilde{x}|^{2}=1+|\xi|^{2},\ \tilde{x}\cdot\xi=0.

There are two involution maps on
\widehat{AH}
:

\displaystyle s:(\tilde{x},\xi)\mapsto(-\tilde{x},-\xi)

\displaystyle r:(\tilde{x},\xi)\mapsto(\tilde{x},-\xi).

The relation between
\widehat{AH}
, the Atiyah–Hitchin space AH, and the
D_{0}
monopole moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{0}
is given by

(6)

AH=\widehat{AH}/s,\ \mathcal{M}_{2}^{0}=AH/r.

The relation implies that near infinity
\widehat{AH}
is a circle bundle
\psi:\widehat{AH}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{|x^{\prime}|>1\}

with the following asymptotic

(7)

g_{\widehat{AH}}=(1-\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}|})|dx^{\prime}|^{2}+(1-\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}|})^{-1}(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}+O(e^{-|x^{\prime}|})

where
x^{\prime}
is bijectively related to
\tilde{x}
in an implicit way. In particular,
AH
and its branched cover
\widehat{AH}
are both strongly ALF spaces by
[
SS21
, Definition 3.5]
and the asymptotic analysis at infinity can be applied the same way.

To connect with the adiabatic Gibbons–Hawking ansatz constructed before, we consider the following metric

(8)

g_{\widehat{AH},\epsilon}=h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|dx^{\prime}|^{2}+(h_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{-1}(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}

where

h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}=1-\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}|}+\sum_{v=1}^{k}\frac{\epsilon}{|p_{v}|},\ \alpha^{\prime}=*dh_{\epsilon}^{\prime}.

We call the associated circle fibration total space
\widehat{AH}_{\epsilon}
and the fibration

(9)

\psi:\widehat{AH}_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{|x^{\prime}|>1/\delta\}.

With the same compactification at infinity of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we can see that
g_{\widehat{AH},\epsilon}
also lifts to be a
\phi
-metric for each
\epsilon
.

2.3.
Gluing construction

The gluing is done on the connecting neck region

\{\frac{1}{\delta}<|x^{\prime}|<\frac{\delta}{\epsilon}\}\subset\widehat{AH}_{\epsilon}

and

\{\frac{\epsilon}{\delta}<|x|<\delta\}\subset M_{\epsilon}

we use the identification map
x^{\prime}=\kappa(x)=x/\epsilon,\kappa^{*}(\alpha_{0})=\alpha^{\prime}
to identify
g_{\epsilon}
and
g_{\widehat{AH},\epsilon}
, and note that under this map
h_{\epsilon}
and
h_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
are identified up to
O(\epsilon^{3})
.

However in order to apply the analysis, we need to modify the space.
The total space is glued from two parts:
\mathcal{W}_{0}
corresponding to the Atiyah-Hitchin metrics in the center, and
\mathcal{W}_{1}
corresponding to the multi-Taub-NUT singularities, where
\mathcal{W}_{0}
and
\mathcal{W}_{1}
are essentially
M_{\epsilon}
and
\widehat{AH}_{\epsilon}
with the additional steps of compactification at infinity and singularity resolution at
\epsilon=0
. The construction of
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{0}
are almost the same as in
[
SS21
]
, while some modification is needed.

The construction is first done on the double cover
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{i},\ i=0,1
, and the final space
\mathcal{W}
is the quotient by the involution
\iota
.
First we construct
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{0}
, which is defined as

(10)

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{0}=\{(z^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})\in\widehat{AH}_{\epsilon}\times[0,\epsilon_{0}):\frac{1}{\delta}<|\psi(z^{\prime})|<\frac{\delta}{\epsilon^{\prime}}\}.

The construction of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
is the same as in
[
SS21
, Section 4.1]
, obtained by pulling back the product of interval
[0,\delta^{2})_{\epsilon}\times M_{\epsilon}
to the resolved base
\widetilde{B}
. We briefly explain the construction of
\widetilde{B}
here. Define the blow up

(11)

\widetilde{B}=[\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{x}\times[0,\delta^{2})_{\epsilon};\{(0,0),\pm p_{i}\times\{0\},i=1,\dots,k\}]\cap\{|x|>\epsilon/\delta\}

which introduced
2k+1
front faces. We then pull back the circle fibration
\phi:M_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{|x|\leq\epsilon/\delta\}
to
\widetilde{B}
, such that when restricting to each
\epsilon>0
it is the same as the original fibration
\phi:M_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{\frac{\epsilon}{\delta}<|x|\leq\epsilon/\delta\}
. On the other hand, when restricting to
\epsilon=0
it lifts to a circle bundle over the blown up space
[\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{3};\{0,\pm p_{i}\}]
.

We construct the space gluing
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
into
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{0}
, identifying the torus neighborhood using the lift of the map
\kappa
described earlier, i.e. we identify the neighborhood

\{(z^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}):0\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\epsilon_{0},\delta^{-1}<|\psi(z^{\prime})|<\delta\epsilon^{-1}\}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{0}

and

\{(z,\epsilon):0\leq\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0},\epsilon\delta^{-1}\leq|\phi(z)|\leq\delta\}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{1}

via the lift of the map
\phi(z)=x:=\psi(z^{\prime})\epsilon,\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}
, and we denote this map by
\kappa
. The total space is defined as

(12)

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{1}\coprod_{\kappa}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{2},\ \mathcal{W}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}/\iota.

Here
\mathcal{W}
is a manifold with corners, where the boundary faces include:
I_{\infty}
which corresponds to the fibration over the boundary of the compactified
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of
\mathcal{W}_{1}
;
X_{0}
that is the lift of the fibration to the boundary face created by blowing up
(0,0)
;
X_{i},i=1,\dots k
, that are the lift of the fibration to the boundary faces created by blowing up
\{\pm p_{i},0\}
;
X_{ad}
the remaining part of
\epsilon=0
.
Here is a picture of the total space:

\mathcal{W}
obtained from gluing
\mathcal{W}_{0}
and
\mathcal{W}_{1}
, with boundary faces and associated boundary defining functions on
\pi^{-1}(0)

There is a projection map
\pi:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow[0,\epsilon_{0})_{\epsilon}
, such that for each nonzero
\epsilon
the fiber is an approximate ALF metric, while for
\epsilon=0
the fiber is a manifold with corners. We also note that
\epsilon
is the total boundary defining function, i.e. if we define
\sigma_{\nu}
to be the boundary defining function of
X_{\nu},\nu=0,1,\dots,k
, and
\rho
to be the boundary defining function of

(13)

\epsilon=\rho\sigma_{0}\dots\sigma_{k}.

2.4.
Solving for the hyperKähler triples

By the gluing construction discussed above, we arrived at a family of approximate metric, each living on the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\epsilon)
of
\mathcal{W}
. We will follow the set up in
[
SS21
]
to perturb these metrics and solve for the exact hyperKähler triples on each fiber. Recall that every hyperKähler metric on a 4-manifold gives a hyperKähler triple
\mathbf{\omega}=(\mathbf{\omega}_{1},\mathbf{\omega}_{2},\mathbf{\omega}_{3})
such that each
\omega_{i}
is a symplectic form and they satisfy the following equation
[
Don06
]

(14)

0=Q(\mathbf{\omega})_{jk}:=\mathbf{\omega}_{j}\wedge\mathbf{\omega}_{k}-\frac{1}{3}(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathbf{\omega}_{i}^{2})\delta_{jk}

Conversely a hyperKähler triple determines a hyperKähler metric
[
SS21
, Theorem 2.3]
.

For a adiabatic Gibbons-hawking ansatz in (
1
), the triple is given by the following
\mathbf{\omega}_{\epsilon}=(\mathbf{\omega}_{1},\mathbf{\omega}_{2},\mathbf{\omega}_{3})

[
SS21
, (2.21)]

(15)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega}_{1}=\alpha\wedge\frac{dx_{1}}{\epsilon}+h_{\epsilon}\frac{dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}}{\epsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega}_{2}=\alpha\wedge\frac{dx_{2}}{\epsilon}+h_{\epsilon}\frac{dx_{3}\wedge dx_{1}}{\epsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega}_{3}=\alpha\wedge\frac{dx_{3}}{\epsilon}+h_{\epsilon}\frac{dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}

which can be written as
\Omega_{\epsilon}+db
where
b\in O(|\epsilon|^{3})
where
\Omega_{\epsilon}
is given by replacing
h_{\epsilon}
by its leading term
1-\frac{\epsilon}{|x|}+\sum_{v=1}^{k}\frac{\epsilon}{|p_{v}|}
.
Similarly for the branched cover
\widehat{AH}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
the triple is given by
\mathbf{\omega}_{AH,\epsilon^{\prime}}=\Omega_{\epsilon^{\prime}}+da
where
a=O(e^{-|x^{\prime}|})
and
\Omega_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
is given by

(16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega}_{1}^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}\wedge dx_{1}^{\prime}+h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}dx_{2}^{\prime}\wedge dx_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega}_{2}^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}\wedge dx_{2}^{\prime}+h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}dx_{3}^{\prime}\wedge dx_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega}_{3}^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}\wedge dx_{3}^{\prime}+h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}dx_{1}^{\prime}\wedge dx_{2}^{\prime}

We will patch the two triples on each fiber to an initial approximation, then solve the equation and show that the solutions are uniformly controlled up to the
\pi^{-1}(0)
face. This will give a family of smooth hyperKähler triples hence the corresponding family of hyperKähler metrics. To give a more precise statement, we use the following space:

Definition 2
.

Let
T_{\phi}(\mathcal{W}/I)
denote the subspace of the total
\phi-
tangent space on
\mathcal{W}
that is spanned by
\phi
-tangent vectors along the
\epsilon-
fiber direction. Similarly the
T^{*}_{\phi}(\mathcal{W}/I)
and
\Omega^{2}_{\phi}(\mathcal{W}/I)
denote the fiberwise
\phi-
cotangent space and (smooth) 2-forms.

The symplectic forms will be in the space of
\Omega^{2}_{\phi}(\mathcal{W}/I)
, and the operator
Q
maps into the space
S_{0}^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\otimes\Lambda^{4}T_{\phi}^{*}(\mathcal{W}/I)
. With these notations, now we state the theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Fix
\{0,p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}
a set of distinct
k+1
points. Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
, such that on
\pi^{-1}([0,\epsilon_{0}))\subset\mathcal{W}
, there exists smooth triple
\mathbf{\omega}_{Se}=(\mathbf{\omega}_{1},\mathbf{\omega}_{2},\mathbf{\omega}_{3})
with
\mathbf{\omega}_{i}\in\Omega_{\phi}^{2}(\mathcal{W}/I)
satisfying
Q(\mathbf{\omega})=0
. The corresponding fiber metric
g_{Se,\epsilon}
satisfies

(1)

\{g_{Se,\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in I}
is a family of smooth fiberwise
\phi
-metric on
\mathcal{W}
;

(2)

g_{Se,0}|_{X_{0}}
restricts to be the Atiyah–Hitchin metric, and
g_{Se,0}|_{X_{i}}
restricts to be the Taub–NUT metric for
i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

The proof follows almost the same as in
[
SS21
]
. Such a strategy has also been used in
[
MZ18
,
MZ19
]
. We first construct an approximate solution
\zeta_{0}
for the hyperKähler triple by patching the two model metrics together using a cut off function
\chi
that is supported near
\mathcal{W}_{0}
:

\mathbf{\omega}_{\chi}:=\chi\mathbf{\omega}_{AH,\epsilon^{\prime}}+(1-\chi)\mathbf{\omega}_{\epsilon}

where the two hyperKähler triples are defined in (
15
) and (
16
). Note that the harmonic function in
\mathbf{\omega}_{AH,\epsilon^{\prime}}
and
\mathbf{\omega}_{\epsilon}
agree up to a closed form of order
\epsilon^{3}
, therefore the initial approximation solves the equation up to the following error

Q(\mathbf{\omega}_{\chi})\in\epsilon^{3}C^{\infty}(\mathcal{W},S_{0}^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\otimes\Lambda^{4}T_{\phi}^{*}(\mathcal{W}/I))

which also vanishes to infinite order away from
X_{\nu}
.

The second step is to solve the equation iteratively to
\epsilon^{\infty}
error. This step follows from the discussion in
[
SS21
, Section 5]
, where they solve the error iteratively near the corner of
X_{\nu}\cap X_{ad}
on the two faces. Recall that the linearized operator of
Q
is given by 12 copies of scalar Laplacians
[
SS21
]
. When restricting to the solvability near
X_{\nu}
, note that the replacement
AH
is still a strongly ALF metric hence the analysis carries through. The solvability near
X_{ad}
is the same as before. As a result we can iteratively construct a formal power series solution

\zeta=\mathbf{\omega}_{\chi}+dc
such that

Q(\zeta)\in\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{W},S_{0}^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\otimes\Lambda^{4}T_{\phi}^{*}(\mathcal{W}/I))

that is, the error vanishes to infinite order on every boundary face.

The third step is to show there is a bounded inverse of the linearized operator on
\mathcal{W}
. This step again relies on that one can patch inverses on
X_{\nu}
and
X_{ad}
. Since the only difference in the structure of
X_{\nu}
from
[
SS21
]
is that
X_{0}
is changed from
\overline{M_{0}^{2}}
by
\overline{AH}
, the inverse is constructed in the same way as in
[
SS21
, Theorem C.3]
for a strongly ALF space. On the other hand, on
X_{ad}
the inverse is constructed explicitly using Fourier series and Green’s function where the only difference is the coefficient in
h_{\epsilon}
which does not change the proof. As a result
\Delta_{\zeta}
is an invertible operator on the domain constructed in
[
SS21
, Theorem 6.6]
.

Finally by implicit function theorem we get the exact solution
\omega_{Se}
with
Q(\omega_{Se})=0
. The smoothness comes from the iterative series and invertibility of the linearized operator, and the restriction on
X_{\nu}
follows because AH and TN are the 0-th order terms in the metric expansion.
∎

As a corollary, such a family of metrics degenerates into the front face, and (under correct scaling and choice of base points) give Gromov-Hausdorff convergence to
AH
and
TN
respectively.

.

3.

In

D_{k+1}

D_{k+1}

Theorem 4
.

There exists
\epsilon_{0}
, such that for every
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
there exists a minimal sphere in

that is non-holomorphic with respect to any of the compatible complex structures.

Proof.

We start with the approximate metric
g_{0,\epsilon}
constructed via patching the
g_{\widehat{AH},\epsilon}
and
g_{\epsilon}
. Since the minimal sphere in
g_{\widehat{AH}}
is compact, so by shrinking
\epsilon_{0}
if necessary, there exists
\Sigma_{\epsilon}\subset(AH|_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta/\epsilon},g_{\widehat{AH},\epsilon})\subset(\pi^{-1}(\epsilon),g_{0,\epsilon})
for every
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
. Note that this minimal sphere
\Sigma
satisfies
[\Sigma]\cdot[\Sigma]=-4
which is not
2*\text{genus}(\Sigma)-2

[
Whi91
]
(which does not require the ambient manifold to be compact) to the actual hyperKähler metric

g_{0,\epsilon}

∎

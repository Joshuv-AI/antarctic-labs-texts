Asymptotic vanishing of cohomology in triangulated categories
Petter Andreas Bergh, David A. Jorgensen, Peder Thompson

Abstract
. Given a graded-commutative ring acting centrally on a triangulated category, our main result shows that if cohomology of a pair of objects of the triangulated category is finitely generated over the ring acting centrally, then the asymptotic vanishing of the cohomology is well-behaved. In particular, enough consecutive asymptotic vanishing of cohomology implies all eventual vanishing. Several key applications are also given.

The main result

Throughout this section, we fix a triangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Delta)
. Thus
\mathscr{C}
is an additive category equipped with an automorphism
\Sigma

\Delta
is a collection of triangles with respect to
\Sigma

[

instead of the more correct

we set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}(A,B)=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)

the

\mathbb{Z}
-graded abelian group
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}(A,A)
is a graded ring under composition products.

A
central ring action
on
\mathscr{C}
is a non-negatively graded ring

H=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{n}
which is graded-commutative, together with a ring homomorphism
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}(A,A)}
\varphi_{A}

of graded rings for every object

, such that for all

the left and right actions of

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}(A,B)

\varphi_{A}
and

are compatible. Specifically, for

\varphi_{B}(x)\cdot f=(-1)^{mn}f\cdot\varphi_{A}(x)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}(A,B)

Lemma 2.1
.

If
S
is a non-negatively graded ring which is graded-commutative, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

S
is Noetherian;

(2)

S^{\operatorname{ev}}
is Noetherian, and
S
is finitely generated as a module over this subalgebra.

Proof.

If (1) holds, consider the homogeneous ideal in
S
generated by the positive part of

S^{\operatorname{ev}}
. As
S
is Noetherian, this ideal is finitely generated, say by homogeneous elements

x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\in S^{\operatorname{ev}}
of positive degrees. A standard argument (cf. the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 10.7]
) now shows that
S^{\operatorname{ev}}
is finitely generated as an algebra over

S^{0}
by
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}
. Since
S^{0}\simeq S/S^{+}
is Noetherian, then so is
S^{\operatorname{ev}}
. Next, consider the odd part
S^{\operatorname{odd}}=\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}S^{2n-1}
of
S
; this is a module over

S^{\operatorname{ev}}
. As above, the homogeneous ideal in
S
generated by
S^{\operatorname{odd}}
is finitely generated, say by homogeneous elements

. These elements generate
S^{\operatorname{odd}}
as an

S^{\operatorname{ev}}
-module, hence
S=S^{\operatorname{ev}}\oplus S^{\operatorname{odd}}
is finitely generated as an
S^{\operatorname{ev}}
-module. Thus (2) holds, and the converse is obvious.
∎

The following lemma is a variant of
[

[

, Theorem 6.5]
. Given a homogeneous element

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a non-negatively graded Noetherian ring which is commutative in the ordinary sense, and

a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Suppose that
S^{0}
either contains an infinite field, or is local with an infinite residue field. Finally, choose homogeneous elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\in S
, of positive degrees, generating

as an algebra over
S^{0}
, and set

. Then there exists a homogeneous element

, with

, such that the multiplication map
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{n+d}}
\cdot x

is injective for
n\gg 0
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Lemma 2.5]
, there exists a positive integer
n_{0}
and a homogeneous element

, of positive degree, such that the corresponding multiplication map
M^{n}\longrightarrow M^{n+\deg(y)}
is injective for

. Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{t}
be the associated prime ideals of the (finitely generated)

-module

. As in the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.5]
,

is not contained in any of these prime ideals; if it were, say with

, then each generator

, and hence also the element

, would belong to
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. But this is impossible since

is regular on

. Therefore

is strictly contained in
S^{d}
for all

.

If

contains an infinite field

, then

is a proper

for all

\left(S^{d}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{1}\right)\cup\cdots\cup\left(S^{d}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{t}\right)

must also be strictly contained in

. The same conclusion follows if

is local with an infinite residue field. To see this, denote by

the maximal ideal of

. As above, the union

\left((V_{1}+\mathfrak{m}S^{d})/\mathfrak{m}S^{d}\right)\cup\cdots\cup\left((V_{t}+\mathfrak{m}S^{d})/\mathfrak{m}S^{d}\right)

is then strictly contained in

, so that the union of the

\left(S^{d}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{1}\right)\cup\cdots\cup\left(S^{d}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{t}\right)

.

It follows from what we have just proved that there exists an element
x\in S^{d}
which is not contained in the union
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathfrak{p}_{t}
. This union is the set of zero-divisors in

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let
H
be a graded-commutative and non-negatively graded ring acting centrally on
\mathscr{C}
. In the present passage we assume that for certain objects
A,B\in\mathscr{C}
the
H

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}(A,B)
is eventually Noetherian, that is, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

is Noetherian. Denote by
\mathfrak{a}
the annihilator ideal of

in

. This is a homogeneous ideal, and

is finitely generated as a module over the graded ring

. Moreover, this ring is Noetherian. To see this, let

be homogeneous elements that generate

over

, and consider the

(with

in the direct sum) given by

s\mapsto(sm_{1},\dots,sm_{t})
. The kernel is a homogeneous ideal of
H
, and must equal
\mathfrak{a}
, since the ring is graded-commutative. We therefore obtain an injective
H
-homomorphism

H/\mathfrak{a}\longrightarrow M\oplus\cdots\oplus M
, and so

is a Noetherian
H
-module. This implies that
H/\mathfrak{a}
is Noetherian as a ring. Let
E
denote the even part of
H/\mathfrak{a}
, that is,

E=(H/\mathfrak{a})^{\operatorname{ev}}

Then Lemma
2.1
says that

is module-finite over

and it follows that

is a finitely generated graded module over the commutative Noetherian graded ring
E
. Choose elements

x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\in E
of positive degrees generating
E
as an algebra over
E^{0}
and set

d=d(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})

to be the least common multiple of
the set
\{\deg(x_{1}),\dots,\deg(x_{t})\}
.

We now give the main theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Delta)
be a triangulated category with a central ring action from a graded-commutative non-negatively graded ring
H
, and
A,B\in\mathscr{C}
two objects such that the
H

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\geq n_{0}}(A,B)
is Noetherian for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Assume that one of the following holds:

(1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)
has finite length over
H^{0}
for
n\gg 0
;

(2)

H^{0}
contains an infinite field, or is local with an infinite residue field.

Then either
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)=0
for all
n\gg 0
, or there exist integers
d>0
and
m_{0}

such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)\neq 0
for all
n\geq m_{0}
with

n\equiv j\hskip 2.84526pt(\operatorname{mod}d)
, for at least one
j

0\leq j<d
.

Proof.

Suppose first that (1) holds. Without loss of generality we can assume that
n_{0}
satisfies

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\geq n_{0}}(A,B)
is Noetherian and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)
has finite length over

H^{0}
for
n\geq n_{0}
. It follows that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)
also has finite length over

(H/\mathfrak{a})^{0}
for
n\geq n_{0}
, where
\mathfrak{a}
is the annihilator of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\geq n_{0}}(A,B)
in
H
. We may now replace the object
B
by
\Sigma^{n_{0}}B
, since this does not change the conclusion, and assume that
n_{0}=0
. Thus we have that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\geq 0}(A,B)
is finitely generated over the Noetherian ring
H/\mathfrak{a}
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)
has finite length over
(H/\mathfrak{a})^{0}
for

n\geq 0
.

As in Remark
2.3
,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\geq 0}(A,B)
is finitely generated as a module over the Noetherian ring
E
, where
E
is the even part of
H/\mathfrak{a}
. Note that
E^{0}=(H/\mathfrak{a})^{0}
. By the classical Hilbert-Serre theorem (cf.
[
2
, Theorem 11.1]
), there is an equality of power series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\ell_{E^{0}}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)\right)z^{n}=\frac{g(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-z^{\deg(x_{i})}\right)}

where
g(z)
is a polynomial with integer coefficients,
\ell_{E^{0}}
denotes the length over
E^{0}
and the
x_{i}
are elements of
E^{>0}
defined in Remark
2.3
. Thus

x_{1},\dots,x_{t}
are elements of
E
of positive degrees generating
E
over
E^{0}
and

d=d(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})
is the least common multiple of the degrees of the

. We may now assume that

, that is, that the sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}
is not empty; otherwise

, and

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)=0

automatically. Write the right-hand side as

u(z)+\frac{v(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-z^{\deg(x_{i})}\right)}

for polynomials

with integer coefficients, and where the degree of

is strictly less than
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\deg(x_{i})
, that is, the degree of the denominator. By cancelling common factors in

and the denominator, we obtain an equality

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\ell_{E^{0}}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)\right)z^{n}=u(z)+\frac{p(z)}{q(z)}

where the fraction on the right-hand side is reduced, and the degree of

is strictly less than that of

. Moreover, every root

, since

It now follows from

that there exist polynomials

in

with the following property: for every

, there is an equality

\ell_{E^{0}}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)\right)=g_{i}(n)

when

. If all the

are zero, then

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,\Sigma^{n}B)=0
for all
n\gg 0
, and we are done. If not, let

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}
be the roots of the nonzero polynomials among
g_{1}(y),\dots,g_{d}(y)
, and take any integer
m_{0}
greater than the maximum of the

, and larger than the degree of

. Moreover, choose an index

with

nonzero. Then for any

, we see that

This proves the theorem when (1) holds.

Next, suppose that (2) holds. As before, we follow the notation from Remark
2.3
, letting

. If
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\geq n_{0}}(A,B)=0

of

. According to Remark

with the property that the multiplication map

such that multiplication by

and

. By injectivity of

with

3.
Applications

In this section, we apply the main result of Section
2
to the cohomology of modules over certain rings. We start with commutative local complete intersections. Recall that such a ring
A
is a commutative Noetherian local ring, whose completion (with respect to the maximal ideal) is of the form
S/(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
for some regular local ring
S
, and a regular sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{c}
contained in the maximal ideal of
S
. When this is the case, then there always exists such a regular sequence contained in the square of the maximal ideal of
S
, and the length
c
is then unique. This is the
codimension
of the local complete intersection
A
. Note that regular local rings are precisely the local complete intersections of codimension zero.

The following is a generalization of
[
29
, Lemma 3.2]
. We denote the Krull dimension of
A
by
\dim A
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
be a commutative local complete intersection ring, and
M,N
two finitely generated
A
-modules. Then either
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>\dim A
, or there exists a non-negative integer
m_{0}
with
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for all even or all odd integers
n\geq m_{0}
.

Proof.

Denote the codimension of
A
by
c
. By
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
, there exists a (faithfully) flat extension
A\longrightarrow B
of local rings, where
B
is a codimension
c
complete intersection with the following properties: the residue field of
B
is infinite, and
B=S/(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
for some regular local ring
S
, and a regular sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{c}
contained in the maximal ideal of
S
. The Krull dimension of
B
is the same as that for
A
, and it follows from
[
26
, Proposition 2.5.8]
that there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}_{B}^{n}(B\otimes_{A}M,B\otimes_{A}N)\simeq B\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)

for every
n
. We may therefore assume that
A
itself is of the form
S/(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
, and with an infinite residue field.

Consider the bounded derived category
\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
of
A
. As explained for example in
[
5
, Section 7.1]
or
[
8
, Section 11]
, there exists a polynomial ring
A[\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{c}]
of cohomology operators of degree two—called Eisenbud operators—acting centrally on
\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
, in such a way that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}^{\geq 0}(X,Y)
is finitely generated for all
X,Y\in\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
. Moreover, as
A
has an infinite residue field, we see that condition (2) in the statement of Theorem
2.4
is satisfied. Now use the theorem, Remark
2.5
(2), and the fact that there is an isomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}(M,\Sigma^{n}N)
for every
n\geq 1
, when we view
M
and
N
as stalk complexes in the derived category: either
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for
n\gg 0
, or there exists a non-negative integer
m_{0}
with
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for all even or all odd integers
n\geq m_{0}
. Finally, by
[
4
, Theorem III]
, if
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all higher

, then
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>\dim A
.
∎

There is also a homology version of the theorem, for
\operatorname{Tor}
; cf.
[
29
, Theorem 3.1]
. One must then assume that the tensor product

has finite length, in order to (in the proof) use Theorem

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,E_{A}))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N),E_{A})

is the injective hull of the residue field of
A
.

Let us now turn to finite-dimensional algebras. Given a field
k
and a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
A
, consider its Hochschild cohomology ring
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
. The degree
n
component of this ring is
\operatorname{Ext}_{A\otimes_{k}A^{\operatorname{op}}}^{n}(A,A)
, where
A^{\operatorname{op}}
is the opposite algebra of
A
. In other words, the elements of the degree
n
component
\operatorname{HH}^{n}(A)
correspond to
n
-fold bimodule extensions of
A
with itself. The multiplication is the Yoneda product, and it is well known that
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
is graded-commutative; cf.
[
25
]
. We now refer to
[
35
]
and
[
36
]
for details concerning what follows. Given a finitely generated left
A
-module
M
, the tensor product
-\otimes_{A}M
induces a homomorphism
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(M,M)}
\varphi_{M}

of graded rings. Thus if
N
is another finitely generated left
A
-module, then
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(M,N)
becomes a left
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
-module via
\varphi_{N}
, and a right
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
-module via
\varphi_{M}
. However, the two module structures coincide up to a sign, and so it does not matter whether we view
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(M,N)
as a left module or as a right module over
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
.

We can now state and prove the following asymptotic vanishing result for finite-dimensional algebras.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field, and
M,N
two finitely generated left
A
-modules with the property that the
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
-module
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{\geq n_{0}}(M,N)
is Noetherian for some integer
n_{0}
. Then either
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n\gg 0
, or there exist integers
d>0
and
m_{0}

such that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for all
n\geq m_{0}
with
n\equiv j\hskip 2.84526pt(\operatorname{mod}d)
, for at least one
j
with
0\leq j<d
.

Proof.

As explained in
[
36
, Section 10]
, the Hochschild cohomology ring
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
acts centrally on the bounded derived category
\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
of
A
. When we view
M
and
N
as stalk complexes in
\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
, then the action of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}^{\geq 0}(M,N)
corresponds to the above action on
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(M,N)
, via the two ring homomorphisms
\varphi_{M}
and
\varphi_{N}
. Here, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we use the fact that there is an isomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}(M,\Sigma^{n}N)
for every
n\geq 1
. As pointed out in
[
18
, Proposition 9.1]
, the isomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{\geq n_{0}}(M,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}^{\geq n_{0}}(M,N)
is one of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
-modules, hence the
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}^{\geq n_{0}}(M,N)
is Noetherian.

Since
\operatorname{HH}^{0}(A)
is just the center of
A
, and contains the ground field, we see that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}(M,\Sigma^{n}N)
has finite length over
\operatorname{HH}^{0}(A)
for all
n
. This means that condition (1) in the statement of Theorem
2.4
holds. Applying the theorem to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}^{\geq n_{0}}(M,N)
, we obtain what we want.
∎

For certain algebras one can give a more precise vanishing conclusion in the statement of the theorem, and also a more general version of the theorem itself. We explain all this in the following remark.

Remark 3.3
.

(1) We say that a finite-dimensional algebra
A
has
finitely generated cohomology
if
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
is Noetherian, and
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
-module for all finitely generated left
A
-modules
M
and
N
. Since all such
A
-modules have finite length, this is equivalent to the following:
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
is Noetherian, and
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(A/\mathfrak{r},A/\mathfrak{r})
is a finitely generated
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
-module, where
\mathfrak{r}
denotes the Jacobson radical of
A
.

If
A
has finitely generated cohomology, then it is actually a Gorenstein algebra, by
[
23
, Proposition 2.2]
. The injective dimension
\operatorname{id}_{A}A
of
A
as a left module over itself is then finite, say
\operatorname{id}_{A}A=s
. Given any finitely generated left
A
-module
M
, the syzygy module
M^{\prime}=\Omega_{A}^{s}(M)
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay, in the sense that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M^{\prime},A)=0
for
n\geq 1
. If
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all higher
n
, then the same is true for
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M^{\prime},N)
, and by combining this with
[
11
, Theorem 3.7]
we see that the vanishing conclusion in the statement of Theorem
3.2
can be sharpened considerably to the following:
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>2\operatorname{id}_{A}A
.

(2) There are situations where it is more convenient to work with a suitably nice subalgebra of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
, and Theorem
3.2
can be stated in this generality. Namely, let
H
be a graded subalgebra of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
. Given a finitely generated left
A
-module
M
, the homomorphism
\varphi_{M}
restricts to a homomorphism
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(M,M)}

of graded rings, and
H
acts centrally on
\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
. The proof of Theorem
3.2
now applies, with
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
replaced by
H
, and so we may replace
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
by
H
in the statement of the theorem.

If
H
is Noetherian and
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
H
-module for all finitely generated left
A
-modules
M
and
N
, then
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)}^{\geq 0}(X,Y)
is a finitely generated
H
-module for all
X,Y\in\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
. Note that, by
[
36
, Proposition 5.7]
, if there exists such a subalgebra
H
of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
, then
A
has finitely generated cohomology in the sense that we defined in the above remark, with respect to
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
. In
loc. cit.
, it is implicitly assumed that
H^{0}=\operatorname{HH}^{0}(A)
, but this is not needed.

Let us look at a large class of algebras for which the remark applies. Fix a field
k
, a nonzero element
q\in k
, and two positive integers
c\geq 1
and
a\geq 2
. The corresponding
quantum complete intersection

A_{c,q}^{a}
is the finite-dimensional
k
-algebra

A_{c,q}^{a}=k\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{c}\rangle/(x_{i}^{a},\{x_{i}x_{j}-qx_{j}x_{i}\}_{i<j})

It is commutative—and a local complete intersection—if
q=1
, but otherwise noncommutative when
c\geq 2
. We allow the case
c=1
here; in this case, the algebra is the commutative truncated polynomial ring
k[x]/(x^{a})
, and the commutator element
q
plays no role. Note that
A_{c,q}^{a}
is always selfinjective. The following is a non-commutative version of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
k
be a field, and
c,a
two positive integers with
c\geq 1
and
a\geq 2
. Furthermore, let
q\in k
be an element with
q^{a}=1
, and consider the corresponding quantum complete intersection
A=A_{c,q}^{a}
. Then, given two finitely generated left
A
-modules
M
and
N
, either
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>0
, or there exists a non-negative integer
m_{0}
with
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for all even or all odd integers
n\geq m_{0}
.

Proof.

If either
q=1
or
c=1
, then the algebra is isomorphic to the commutative local complete intersection ring
k\llbracket x_{1},\dots,x_{c}\rrbracket/(x_{1}^{a},\dots,x_{c}^{a})
, where
k\llbracket x_{1},\dots,x_{c}\rrbracket
denotes the power series ring. In this case, Theorem
3.1
applies, so from now on we assume that
q\neq 1
and
c\geq 2
.

The algebra
A
is local, with
k
as the simple left module. Moreover, by
[
16
, Theorem 5.5]
, it has finitely generated cohomology, so Theorem
3.2
and Remark
3.3
apply. However, we shall show that there is a polynomial subalgebra of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
, with generators in degree two, that does the job for us, as in Remark
3.3
(2).

First of all, it follows from
[
16
, Theorem 5.3]
that there exists a polynomial subalgebra
S=k[z_{1},\dots,z_{c}]
of the center of
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(k,k)
, with the latter a finitely generated
S
-module. Now consider the natural
\mathbb{Z}^{c}
-grading on
A
given by
\deg(x_{i})=e_{i}
, where
e_{i}
denotes the unit vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{c}
with
1
in the
i
th coordinate. This grading is passed down to
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(k,k)
, so that each
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(k,k)
is
\mathbb{Z}^{c}
-graded as well. As explained in
[
34
, Section 3]
, the element
z_{i}
belongs to
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{2,-ae_{i}}(k,k)
; in other words, it belongs to
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{2}(k,k)
, and has internal degree
-ae_{i}
.

We now follow the notation from
[
34
, Section 2]
, in particular from
[
34
, Technical notation, page 825]
. Consider the vector
\mathbf{d}=-ae_{i}
. Then
Q\mathbf{d}=(1,\dots,1)
, so that
(Q\mathbf{d})_{i}=1
for
1\leq i\leq c
. Moreover, since
a\geq 2
, the three subsets
I_{\rm{max}},I_{1}
and
I_{2}
of
\{1,\dots,c\}
, defined right before
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
and relative to our vector
\mathbf{d}
, are just given by

I_{\rm{max}}=I_{1}=\emptyset,\hskip 14.22636ptI_{2}=\{1,\dots,c\}

It therefore follows from
[
34
, Theorem 3.5]
that the polynomial subalgebra
S
of
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(k,k)
belongs to the image of the homomorphism
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(k,k)}
\varphi_{k}

Consequently, there exists a polynomial subalgebra
H=k[\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{c}]
of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
, with
\deg(\eta_{i})=2
for all
i
, and with the property that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(k,k)
is finitely generated when viewed as an
H
-module via
\varphi_{k}
.

As explained in Remark
3.3
(2), we may now apply Theorem
3.2
with
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
replaced by
H
in the statement, and with
n_{0}=0
since
A
has finitely generated cohomology. Moreover, since our algebra is selfinjective, we see from Remark
3.3
(1) that the vanishing conclusion actually becomes
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>0
. Finally, since the degree of each
\eta_{i}
is two, it follows from Remark
2.5
(2) that we may choose
d=2
in Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Exterior algebras are particular examples of quantum complete intersections for which Theorem
3.4
applies. We recored this in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
k
be a field,
c\geq 1
an integer, and
A
the corresponding exterior algebra

k\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{c}\rangle/(x_{i}^{2},\{x_{i}x_{j}+x_{j}x_{i}\}_{i\neq j})

Then, given two finitely generated left
A
-modules
M
and
N
, either
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>0
, or there exists a non-negative integer
m_{0}
with
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for all even or all odd integers
n\geq m_{0}
.

Our final application of the main result from Section
2
is the cohomology of modules over group algebras of finite groups. Let therefore
k
be a field and
G
a finite group. If the characteristic of
k
does not divide the order of
G
, then the group algebra
kG
is semisimple by Maschke’s theorem. Therefore, from a homological point of view, the only interesting setting for us is when
k
is of prime characteristic
p
, with
p
dividing
|G|
.

Recall that the cohomology ring
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
of
G
is the
\operatorname{Ext}
-algebra of the trivial
kG
-module, that is,
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)=\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{*}(k,k)
. Given left
kG
-modules
M
and
N
, the (Hopf algebra) tensor product
-\otimes_{k}M
induces a homomorphism
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{*}(M,M)}

of graded rings, turning
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{*}(M,N)
into a left and a right
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
-module. As with the Hochschild cohomology ring, the group cohomology ring is graded-commutative, and the left and right module actions on
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{*}(M,N)
coincide up to a sign; cf.
[
7
, Section 3.2]
. By a classical result of Evens and Venkov (cf.
[
24
]
,
[
38
]
and
[
39
]
), the cohomology ring is Noetherian, and
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{*}(M,N)
is finitely generated when
M
and
N
are finitely generated as
kG
-modules.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
k
be a field of prime characteristic
p
, and
G
a finite group with
p
dividing
|G|
. Furthermore, let
M
and
N
be two finitely generated left
kG
-modules. Then either
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>0
, or there exist integers
d>0
and
m_{0}

such that
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for all
n\geq m_{0}
with
n\equiv j\hskip 2.84526pt(\operatorname{mod}d)
, for at least one
j
with
0\leq j<d
.

Proof.

We denote the stable category of finitely generated left
kG
-modules by
\underline{\operatorname{mod}}kG
, and the group of morphisms between two objects
M,N
by
\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{kG}(M,N)
. Furthermore, we identify
\oplus_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{kG}(M,\Omega_{kG}^{-n}(N))
with
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{\geq 1}(M,N)
. The cohomology ring
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
acts centrally on
\underline{\operatorname{mod}}kG
, as explained in the paragraph preceding this theorem. Moreover, the result of Evens and Venkov implies that
\oplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{kG}(M,\Omega_{kG}^{-n}(N))
is a finitely generated
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
-module; in degree zero we have
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{0}(G,k)=k
, and
\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{kG}(M,N)
is finite-dimensional.

Since condition (1) in the statement of Theorem
2.4
holds, we can now apply the theorem with
n_{0}=0
: either
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all higher
n
, or there exist integers
d>0
and
m_{0}

such that
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for all
n\geq m_{0}
with
n\equiv j\hskip 2.84526pt(\operatorname{mod}d)
, for at least one
j
with
0\leq j<d
. Finally, since the group algebra
kG
is selfinjective, the arguments from Remark
3.3
(1) can be applied so that the vanishing conclusion becomes
\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n>0
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Choose homogeneous elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\in\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
, of positive degrees, generating the commutative even subalgebra
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{\operatorname{ev}}(G,k)
of
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
over
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{0}(G,k)
. It follows from Remark
2.5
(2) that we may take the
d
in Theorem
3.6
to be the least common multiple of the degrees of these generators. If the characteristic of the field
k
is two, so that the whole cohomology ring
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
is commutative, we may take generators for
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(G,k)
itself.

Let us look at an example with some of the symmetric groups.

Example 3.8
.

Let
S_{n}
denote the
n
th symmetric group and
k
the field with two elements. Furthermore, let
M
and
N
be two finitely generated left
kS_{n}
-modules. Note that since we are in characteristic two, the group cohomology ring
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(S_{n},k)

By
[

, Theorem 4.1]
, the cohomology ring
\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{*}(S_{4},k)

\operatorname{H}\nolimits^{0}(S_{4},k)
by elements

and

, respectively. The least common multiple of these degrees is

\operatorname{Ext}_{kS_{4}}^{m}(M,N)\neq 0

, then there exists a number

such that
\operatorname{Ext}_{kS_{4}}^{m}(M,N)\neq 0
for all

with

.

In

]
, the cohomology ring

. It starts rather gently: as with

\operatorname{Ext}_{kS_{8}}^{m}(M,N)\neq 0
for all

How to determine the local unitary equivalence of sets of generalized Bell states
Cai-Hong Wang 1, Jiang-Tao Yuan 1, Ying-Hui Yang 2

Abstract
Classification is a common method to study quantum entanglement, and local unitary equivalence (LU-equivalence) is an effective classification tool. The purpose of this work is to show how to determine the LU-equivalence of sets of generalized Bell states (GBSs) in an arbitrary dimensional bipartite quantum system ℂ d ⊗ ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d} ( d d is an integer no less than 3). The idea is that, for a given GBS set ℳ \mathcal{M} , try to find all the GBS sets that are LU-equivalent to ℳ \mathcal{M} , then we can determine whether another GBS set is LU-equivalent to ℳ \mathcal{M} by comparison. In order to accomplish this intention, we first reduce the LU-equivalence of two GBS sets to the unitary conjugate equivalence (UC-equivalence) of two generalized Pauli matrix (GPM) sets. Then we give the necessary and sufficient conditions for a 2-GPM set UC-equivalent to a special 2-GPM set { X a , Z b } \{X^{a},Z^{b}\} ( a , b a,b are nonnegative integers and factors of d d ). The general case, that is, the UC-equivalence of two general GPM sets, follows by the particular case. Moreover, these results are programmable, that is, we provide programs that can give all standard GPM sets that are UC-equivalent to a given GPM set, as well as programs that can determine all standard GPM sets that are unitary equivalent (U-equivalent) to a given GPM set, and then the U-equivalence of two arbitrary GPM sets (or LU-equivalence of two GBS sets) can be determined by comparison. To illustrate the role of the programs, we provide two examples, one showing that all 4-GBS sets (58905 items) in ℂ 6 ⊗ ℂ 6 \mathbb{C}^{6}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6} can be divided into 31 LU-equivalence classes, and the other providing a complete LU-equivalent classification of all 4-GBS sets in ℂ 4 ⊗ ℂ 4 \mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{4} .

I
Introduction

Quantum entanglement has always been a key role in the field of quantum information and quantum computation.
In a bipartite composite system
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we say two sets of quantum states are local unitary equivalence (LU-equivalence)
if they can be transformed into each other by local unitary operations.
Sets of quantum states which are LU-equivalent have the same effectiveness and generality in many quantum information processing tasks,
such as quantum secret sharing
raha2015pra
;
yang2015sci
.
And the local distinguishability of these sets is exactly the same

fan2004prl
;
nath2005jmp
;
band2011njp
;
zhang2014qip
;
wang2019pra
;
yang2018pra
.
Therefore an effective way to understand entanglement is to classify entanglement by using LU-equivalence.
Many nice results have been obtained on the LU-equivalence between two single entangled states
linden1999prl
;
kraus2010prl
;
mac2013jmp
;
martin2015pra
;
jing2016jmp
;
qiao2020ieee
.
However, it is much more difficult to determine the LU-equivalence between two sets of entangled states.

At present, the study on the LU-equivalence of orthogonal quantum state sets is limited to the sets of generalized Bell states (GBSs) in bipartite quantum systems.
Since there is a one-to-one correspondence between the maximally entangled state (MES) and the unitary matrix,
especially each GBS corresponds to a generalized Pauli matrix (GPM),
the LU-equivalence of the GBS sets can be expressed in terms of the unitary equivalence (U-equivalence) of the GPM sets.
Singal et al.
sing2017pra
were the first to consider the LU-equivalence of 4-GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{4}
,
all
C_{16}^{4}
(=1820) 4-GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{4}
are divided into 122 equivalence classes by means of some special LU-equivalence.
However, such a classification is not complete, that is, the different classes may be LU-equivalent.
Subsequently, Tian et al.
tian2016pra
divided all 4-GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{4}
into ten equivalence classes
using a number of LU-equivalence including Clifford operators
hos2005pra
;
fari2014jpa
.
This classification is thorough, i.e., different classes are not LU-equivalent to each other.
But the elements (4-GBS sets) of each equivalence class are not clearly given, which is not conducive to the use of the classification.

Wu et al.
wu-tian2018pra
discussed the local unitary classification of 3-GBS sets
in
\mathbb{C}^{p^{\alpha}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{p^{\alpha}}
(
p
is a prime number and
\alpha
is a positive integer)
using some more general unitary operators than Clifford operators.
Their discussions and the results are complex:
There are
\frac{\alpha+3}{6}p^{\alpha}+O(\alpha p^{\alpha-1})
local unitary inequivalent (LU-inequivalent) GBS triples
in the
\mathbb{C}^{p^{\alpha}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{p^{\alpha}}
quantum system for all prime
p
and positive integer
\alpha
,
and in particular, there are exactly
\lfloor\frac{5}{6}p^{2}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{p-2}{6}+(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{p}{3}\rfloor}\frac{p}{3}\rfloor+3

LU-inequivalent GBS triples in the
\mathbb{C}^{p^{2}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{p^{2}}
quantum system for prime
p\geq 3
.

Recently, in
yuan2022quantum
, the authors discussed the local discrimination of GBS sets and showed three results:
(a) Three practical and effective sufficient conditions for the local distinguishability of GBS sets in an
arbitrary dimensional space
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
are given.
(b) In
\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{4}
and
\mathbb{C}^{5}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{5}
,
some necessary and sufficient conditions for local discrimination of GBS sets are provided.
(c) In
\mathbb{C}^{4}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{4}
, by using Clifford operators on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
,
a list of all locally indistinguishable 4-GBS sets is obtained.
It should be pointed out that these results are based on the LU-equivalence classification of GBS sets
(see
sing2017pra
,
tian2016pra
,
wang2021jmp
).
That is, due to the large number of GBS sets, it is necessary to classify these GBS sets according to LU-equivalence,
and then consider the local discrimination of the representative elements of these equivalence classes.
Naturally, we may wish to consider the problem of local discrimination of GBS sets
in higher dimensional composite systems, such as in
\mathbb{C}^{6}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
.
But it is difficult for us to solve this problem according to two basic facts:
(a) In
\mathbb{C}^{6}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
, there are
C_{36}^{4}
(=58905) 4-GBS sets,

C_{36}^{5}
(=376992) 5-GBS sets and
C_{36}^{6}
(=1947792) 6-GBS sets.
That is, the number of sets to be considered is very large.
(b) In order to deal with a large number of GBS sets, in general,
it is necessary to classify these sets by using methods such as LU-equivalence.
However, as of now, such classification has not been completed yet.

Based on the above, the intention of this work is to give a simple and programmable method
to determine the LU-equivalence of two GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
Recall that the LU-equivalence of the GBS sets can be expressed in terms of the unitary equivalence (U-equivalence) of the GPM sets.
According to the characterization of the U-equivalence of two unitary matrix sets,
the main task of determining the U-equivalence of two GPM sets is to determine the unitary conjugate equivalence (UC-equivalence) of the two GPM sets.
In
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, the GPMs
X^{a}
and
Z^{b}
are crucial
since these GPMs are simple and all other GPMs can be expressed as the product of these GPMs.
We first show sufficient and necessary conditions for a general 2-GPM set UC-equivalent to
a given 2-GPM set
\{X^{a},Z^{b}\}
.
Next, we reduce the UC-equivalence of general GPM sets to the case of 2-GPM set
\{X^{a},Z^{b}\}
,
thus giving programs for determining the UC-equivalence and U-equivalence of any two general GPM sets.
To illustrate the role of the programs, two examples are given,
one saying that there are 31 LU-equivalence classes of all
C_{36}^{4}(=58905)
4-GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{6}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
,
and the other providing a complete U-equivalent classification (including UC-equivalence classification) of
all
C_{16}^{4}(=1820)
4-GPM sets on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section II, we review the notions of the LU-equivalence of two orthogonal MES set, Clifford operators and related conclusions.
In Section III, we consider the sufficient and necessary conditions for a general 2-GPM set to be UC-equivalent
to a special 2-GPM set
\{X^{a},Z^{b}\}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
In Section IV, the UC-equivalence between general GPM sets is discussed,
programs for determining whether two GPM sets are UC-equivalent or U-equivalent are given,
and an example is provided to illustrate the programs.
In Section V, the special case
d=p^{\alpha}
(
p
is a prime number and
\alpha
is a positive integer)
of the UC-equivalence or U-equivalence is considered,
programs for this special case is more clear and specific.
In the last section, we draw the conclusions.

II
Preliminaries

Consider a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space,
\{|j\rangle\}_{j=0}^{d-1}
is the computational basis
and
\mathbb{Z}_{d}=\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
.
Let
U_{m,n}=X^{m}Z^{n},m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
be generalized Pauli matrices (GPMs)
constituting a basis of unitary operators where
X|j\rangle=|j+1
mod
d\rangle
,
Z|j\rangle=\omega^{j}|j\rangle
and
\omega=e^{2\pi i/d}
,
which are generalizations of Pauli matrices.

In a bipartite quantum system
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
the canonical maximally entangled state
|\Phi\rangle
is
|\Phi_{00}\rangle=(1/\sqrt{d})\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|jj\rangle
.
It is known that
(I\otimes A)|\Phi\rangle=(A^{T}\otimes I)|\Phi\rangle
, where
A^{T}
means the transposition of matrix
A
.
Any MES can be written as
|\Psi\rangle=(I\otimes U)|\Phi\rangle
where
U
is unitary.
If
U=X^{m}Z^{n}
, the states

\displaystyle|\Phi_{m,n}\rangle=(I\otimes U_{m,n})|\Phi\rangle

are called generalized Bell states (GBSs).
Note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the MESs and unitaries as above,
and the corresponding unitaries of GBSs are GPMs.
It is convenient to denote a GBS set
\{(I\otimes X^{m_{i}}Z^{n_{i}})|\Phi\rangle\}
by
\{X^{m_{i}}Z^{n_{i}}\}
or
\{(m_{i},n_{i})\}
.

Let’s recall the LU-equivalence of two MES sets.

Definition 1

(
wu-tian2018pra
)
.

Let
\{|\phi_{1}\rangle,|\phi_{2}\rangle,\cdots,|\phi_{n}\rangle\}
and
\{|\psi_{1}\rangle,|\psi_{2}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{n}\rangle\}
be two sets of MESs.
Their corresponding unitary matrix sets are
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{n}\}
and
N=\{N_{1},N_{2},\cdots,N_{n}\}
.
If there exist two unitary operators
U_{A}
,
U_{B}
and a permutation

\sigma
over
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
such that
|\psi_{i}\rangle\approx(U_{A}\otimes U_{B})|\phi_{\sigma(i)}\rangle
,
where
\approx
denotes “equal up to some global phase”,
then these two MES sets are called LU-equivalent.
Meanwhile the corresponding unitary matrix sets are called
U
-equivalent, that is

\displaystyle L\mathcal{M}R\approx\mathcal{N},

where
L=U_{B}
and
R=U_{A}^{T}
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}\sim\mathcal{N}
.
Especially, when
R=L^{\dagger}
, the two sets
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are called unitary conjugate equivalent (UC-equivalent),
denoted by
\mathcal{M}\mathop{\sim}\limits^{L}\mathcal{N}
.

In general we can study LU-equivalence of two MES sets by studying the
U
-equivalence of their unitary matrix sets.
For convenience, we call a GBS set containing the standard MES
|\Phi\rangle
a standard GBS set,
and a GPM set containing the identity matrix
I
a standard GPM set.
Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{n}\}
and
\mathcal{N}=\{N_{1},N_{2},\cdots,N_{n}\}
be two GPM sets.
If
\mathcal{M}
is
U
-equivalent to
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}
is a standard GPM set, then there exist two unitaries
L
,
R
and a permutation
\sigma
over
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}

such that
LM_{i}R\approx N_{\sigma(i)}
, and
LM_{i_{0}}R\approx N_{\sigma({i_{0}})}=I
holds for some
i_{0}
.
Thus
M_{i_{0}}\approx L^{\dagger}R^{\dagger}
and for any
i
we have

\displaystyle N_{\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle\approx LM_{i}R=LM_{i}M_{i_{0}}^{\dagger}M_{i_{0}}R\approx L(M_{i}M_{i_{0}}^{\dagger})L^{\dagger}.

That is to say, the U-equivalence between a GPM set
\mathcal{M}
and a standard GPM set
\mathcal{N}
can be achieved by a right multiplication operation and a UC transformation.
Now we get a useful characterization of U-equivalence of GPM sets.

Lemma 1
.

For a given GPM set
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{n}\}
,
the standard GPM set
\mathcal{N}
that is U-equivalent to the set
\mathcal{M}
has the form
\mathcal{N}\approx\{L(M_{i}M_{i_{0}}^{\dagger})L^{\dagger}\}
where
L
is a unitary matrix.

Each GPM set is U-equivalent to a standard GPM set, so let’s assume that the GPM sets we consider are all standard GPM sets.
In order to determine whether two standard GPM sets
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are U-equivalent to each other,
according to Lemma
1
,
it is sufficient to find all standard GPM sets that are UC-equivalent to
the standard GPM sets
\mathcal{M}M_{i_{0}}^{\dagger}\triangleq\{M_{1}M_{i_{0}}^{\dagger},\cdots,M_{n}M_{i_{0}}^{\dagger}\}(i_{0}=1,\cdots,n)
,
and then check whether the standard GPM set
\mathcal{N}
is in these standard GPM sets.

In the next section, we give the necessary and sufficient conditions
for a GPM set UC-equivalent to a special 2-GPM set
\{X^{a},Z^{b}\}
(
a,b
are positive factors of
d
)
defined on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
(
d
is an integer no less than 3).

III
UC-equivalence of 2-GPM sets defined on
\mathbb{C}^{d}

In this section, we will consider the UC-equivalence of 2-GPM sets on
\mathbb{C}^{d}

where
d
is an integer no less than 3.

Recall that for an element
x
of a group, its order refers to the smallest positive integer
m
with the property
x^{m}=I

or the infinity if
x^{m}\neq I
for all
m\neq 0
. We often denote the order of
x
by
O(x)
.
Since the global phase does not affect the LU-equivalence between quantum states,
we introduce the so-called essential order and the essential power that are more suitable for the study of LU-equivalence.

Definition 2
.

Let
U
be a GPM defined on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
A positive integer
a
is said to be the essential order of
U
if it is the smallest positive integer such that
U^{a}\approx I
.
We often denote the essential order by
O_{e}(U)
.
When
U\not\approx I
, we call
d/O_{e}(U)
the essential power of
U
and denote it as
P_{e}(U)
.
When
U\approx I
, let
P_{e}(U)=0
.

For each GPM
X^{s}Z^{t}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, it is easy to know that its essential order
O_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})
should be
\frac{d}{gcd(s,t,d)}
and,
in turn, its essential power
P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})
is
gcd(s,t,d)
, where
gcd(s,t,d)
refers to the greatest common divisor of
s,t
and
d
.

Example 1
.

Let
X
and
Z
be Pauli matrices on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, then
O_{e}(X)=\frac{2}{gcd(1,0,2)}=2=O(X)
.
Since
(XZ)^{2}=-I
and
(XZ)^{4}=I
, we have
O_{e}(XZ)=2=\frac{2}{gcd(1,1,2)}
and
O(XZ)=4
.

Example 2
.

Let
X
and
Z
be GPMs on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
, then
O_{e}(XZ)=\frac{4}{gcd(1,1,4)}=4
.
Since
(XZ)^{4}=-I
and
(XZ)^{8}=I
, we have
O(XZ)=8
.
Similarly, it is easy to know that
O_{e}(XZ^{3})=4
,
(XZ^{3})^{4}=-I
and
O(XZ^{3})=8
.

Example 3
.

Let
X
and
Z
be GPMs on
\mathbb{C}^{6}
, then
O_{e}(XZ^{5})=6,(XZ^{5})^{6}=-I
and
O(XZ^{5})=12
.
Similarly, we have
O_{e}(X^{3}Z^{3})=2,(X^{3}Z^{3})^{2}=-I
and
O(X^{3}Z^{3})=4
.

Example 4
.

Let
X
and
Z
be GPMs on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, if
gcd(s,d)=1
or
gcd(t,d)=1
, then
P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})=gcd(s,t,d)=1
.
In particular, if
d=p^{\alpha}
(
p
is a prime number and
\alpha
is a positive integer) and
X^{s}Z^{t}\not\approx I

we have
P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})=gcd(s,t,p^{\alpha})=p^{\gamma}
, where
0\leq\gamma<\alpha
.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{n}\}
be a GPM set and
x=(P_{e}(U_{1}),P_{e}(U_{2}),\cdots,P_{e}(U_{n}))^{T}
be a
n
-dimensional real vector.
The essential power vector of
\mathcal{M}
is defined as
P_{e}(\mathcal{M})=x^{\uparrow}
,
where the symbol
\uparrow
means that the element in the vector taking ascending order.

For example, if
\mathcal{M}=\{X,X^{3}Z^{3},Z^{4},X^{2}\}
is a GPM set on
\mathbb{C}^{6}
,
then
P_{e}(X)=1
,
P_{e}(X^{3}Z^{3})=3
,
P_{e}(Z^{4})=2
,
P_{e}(X^{2})=2
, and
P_{e}(\mathcal{M})=(1,2,2,3)^{T}
.

Lemma 2
.

Two GPMs are UC-equivalent if and only if they have the same essential power.
In particular, two UC-equivalent GPM sets have the same essential power vector.

Proof
.
For each GPM
X^{s}Z^{t}

(\neq I)
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, let
gcd(s,t)=b,gcd(b,d)=a=P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})
,
then there exist integers
p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2}
such that

p_{1}s+q_{1}t=b,p_{2}d+q_{2}b=a.

Let
\overline{C}_{gcd(s,t)}
and
\overline{C}_{gcd(d,b)}
be two Clifford operators with the corresponding symplectic (or classical) representation

\displaystyle C_{gcd(s,t)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{llll}t/b&-s/b\\
p_{1}&q_{1}\end{array}\right],\ \ C_{gcd(d,b)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{llll}b/a&-d/a\\
p_{2}&q_{2}\end{array}\right],

we get
X^{s}Z^{t}\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\overline{C}_{gcd(s,t)}}Z^{b}\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\overline{C}_{gcd(d,b)}}Z^{a}=Z^{P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})}
.
Therefore two nontrivial GPMs
X^{s}Z^{t}
and
X^{s^{\prime}}Z^{t^{\prime}}
are UC-equivalent if and only if

Z^{P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})}
and
Z^{P_{e}(X^{s^{\prime}}Z^{t^{\prime}})}
are UC-equivalent.
Since
P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})
and
P_{e}(X^{s^{\prime}}Z^{t^{\prime}})
are factors of
d
,

Z^{P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})}
and
Z^{P_{e}(X^{s^{\prime}}Z^{t^{\prime}})}
are UC-equivalent
if and only if they have the same essential power, that is,
P_{e}(X^{s}Z^{t})=P_{e}(X^{s^{\prime}}Z^{t^{\prime}})
.
These indicate that the conclusion is valid.

We denote the composition
\overline{C}_{gcd(d,b)}\circ\overline{C}_{gcd(s,t)}
as
\overline{C}_{gcd(s,t,d)}
,
which is a Clifford operator that transforms
X^{s}Z^{t}
to
Z^{gcd(s,t,d)}
.
Lemma
2
shows that two GPM sets with different essential power vectors are not UC-equivalent.

Example 5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{X^{12},Z^{3},X^{3}Z^{4},X^{5}Z^{15}\}
and
\mathcal{N}=\{X^{4}Z^{6},X^{6}Z^{12},X^{2},X^{3}Z^{5}\}
be GPM sets on
\mathbb{C}^{30}
,
then the essential power vector
P_{e}(\mathcal{M})=(1,3,5,6)^{T}
,
P_{e}(\mathcal{N})=(1,2,2,6)^{T}\neq P_{e}(\mathcal{M})
,

\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are not UC-equivalent to each other.

For any two standard
n
-GPM sets
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
on
\mathbb{C}^{p}
(where
p
is an odd prime number),
since the essential power of each nontrivial GPM is 1,
it is obvious that they have the same essential power vector.
However, they are not necessarily UC-equivalent to each other, for example,
the
5
-GPM sets
\mathcal{M}=\{I,Z,Z^{2},Z^{3},Z^{4}\}
and
\mathcal{N}=\{I,Z,Z^{2},X,X^{4}\}
on
\mathbb{C}^{5}
are not UC-equivalent
(
wang2020jmp
, Theorem 4)
.

According to the definition, the essential power of a GPM and the commutation coefficient of two GPMs are invariant
through UC-transformations.
It is turn out that these two properties are essential features of UC-equivalence of 2-GPM sets.

Lemma 3
.

If
a
and
b
are both positive factors of
d
,
then two nontrivial GPMs
X^{a}
and
Z^{b}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
are UC-equivalent to
X^{ua}
and
Z^{b}
respectively through a unitary matrix
W

if and only if
gcd(u,d/a)=1
and
uab\equiv ab
(mod
d
).

Proof
.
Let
gcd(u,d/a)=1
and
uab\equiv ab
(mod
d
), and define a matrix
W
as follows

\displaystyle W|i+ja\rangle=|i+uja\rangle,0\leq i\leq a-1,0\leq j<d/a.

If
i+uja=i^{\prime}+uj^{\prime}a
(mod
d
), that is,
i-i^{\prime}\equiv u(j^{\prime}-j)a
(mod
d
),
then
i=i^{\prime},j=j^{\prime}
because of
gcd(u,d/a)=1
.
Thus
W
is a permutation matrix and is unitary.
Noting the condition
uab\equiv ab
(mod
d
), we have

\displaystyle WZ^{b}W^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime}}\omega^{b(i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}a)}|i+uja\rangle\langle i+ja|i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}a\rangle

\displaystyle\langle i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}a|i^{\prime\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}a\rangle\langle i^{\prime\prime}+uj^{\prime\prime}a|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\omega^{b(i+ja)}|i+uja\rangle\langle i+uja|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\omega^{b(i+uja)}|i+uja\rangle\langle i+uja|=Z^{b},

\displaystyle WX^{a}W^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime}}|i+uja\rangle\langle i+ja|i^{\prime}+(j^{\prime}+1)a\rangle

\displaystyle\langle i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}a|i^{\prime\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}a\rangle\langle i^{\prime\prime}+uj^{\prime\prime}a|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}|i+u(j+1)a\rangle\langle i+uja|=X^{ua}.

So the two GPMs
X^{a}
and
Z^{b}
are UC-equivalent to
X^{ua}
and
Z^{b}
respectively through the unitary matrix
W
.

Conversely, let
X^{a}\mathop{\sim}\limits^{W}X^{ua}
and
Z^{b}\mathop{\sim}\limits^{W}Z^{b}
,
since UC-equivalent GPMs have the same essential power,
we have
gcd(ua,d)=gcd(a,d)=a
and
gcd(u,d/a)=1

IV
Programs for determining the U-equivalence of GPM sets on
\mathbb{C}^{d}

In this section, we will show how to determine the LU-equivalence of GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
that is, we will provide a program for determining the U-equivalence of GPM sets on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
According to Lemma
1
, the key to determine the U-equivalence of GPM sets is to determine the UC-equivalence of the standard GPM sets.
Now let’s investigate the unitary conjugate transformations (UC-transformations) that transform one
(n+1)
-GPM set into another
(n+1)
-GPM set.
It is turned out that local Clifford operators on a given GPM set are determined by local Clifford operators on
two special GPMs with form
X^{a}
and
Z^{b}
.

Let

\mathcal{M}=\{I,X^{s_{1}}Z^{t_{1}},\cdots,X^{s_{n}}Z^{t_{n}}\}(0\leq s_{1},t_{1},\cdots,s_{n},t_{n}\leq d-1,\ n\geq 2)

be a standard
(n+1)
-GPM set on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
Without loss of generality,
assume that
1\leq P_{e}(X^{s_{1}}Z^{t_{1}})\leq\cdots\leq P_{e}({X^{s_{n}}Z^{t_{n}}})\leq d-1
,
then
\mathcal{M}
is UC-equivalent to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{I,Z^{gcd(s_{1},t_{1},d)},X^{s^{\prime}_{2}}Z^{t^{\prime}_{2}}\cdots,X^{s^{\prime}_{n}}Z^{t^{\prime}_{n}}\}

through the Clifford operator
\overline{C}_{gcd(s_{1},t_{1},d)}
,
that is,
\mathcal{M}\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\overline{C}_{gcd(s_{1},t_{1},d)}}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
and

1\leq gcd(s_{1},t_{1},d)\leq\cdots\leq gcd(s_{n},t_{n},d)\leq d-1
.
If the GPM set
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
satisfies the condition
b|t^{\prime}_{i}(i=2,\cdots,n)
,
its UC-equivalence is determined by two special GPMs
X^{a}
(

) and
Z^{b}
(

),
as described in the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{I,Z^{b},X^{s_{2}}Z^{t_{2}}\cdots,X^{s_{n}}Z^{t_{n}}\}
be a
(n+1)
-GPM set satisfying

and

then
U
is a local Clifford operator on the GPM set
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
if and only if

U
is a local Clifford operator on
X^{a}
(

) and
Z^{b}
.

Proof
.
Since
b|t_{i}
,
it is obvious that
U
is a local Clifford operator on
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}

if and only if
U
is a local Clifford operator on the
n
GPMs
Z^{b},X^{s_{2}}\cdots,X^{s_{n}}
.
If

is a local Clifford operator on two GPMs
X^{a}
and
Z^{b}
,

is a local Clifford operator on the
n

.
Conversely, if

is a local Clifford operator on the
n

such that

is a GPM.
So

is a local Clifford operator on
X^{a}
and
Z^{b}
.

For a
(n+1)
-GPM set
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
(as in Lemma

) that does not meet the condition

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, in order to show how to determine the LU-equivalence of GBS sets
in an arbitrary dimensional bipartite quantum system
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
we reduce the LU-equivalence of two GBS sets to the UC-equivalence of two GPM sets.
Then we show the necessary and sufficient conditions for a 2-GPM set
UC-equivalent to a special 2-GPM set
\{X^{p^{\gamma}},Z^{p^{\beta}}\}
(
0\leq\beta,\gamma<\alpha
),
and prove that the general case follows by the special case.
The fact shows that determining UC-equivalence class of a GPM set is the key to determining the U-equivalence between two GPM sets
(or the LU-equivalence between two GBS sets).
According to these results, we provide the programs
that can give all GPM sets UC-equivalent or U-equivalent to a given GPM set,
and then the U-equivalence or UC-equivalence of two arbitrary GPM sets can be determined.
The special case
d=p^{\alpha}
(
p
is a prime number and
\alpha
is a positive integer) of U-equivalence
is simpler, and the corresponding programs are more specific and detailed.
By using the programs, we illustrate that there are 31 LU-equivalent classes of 4-GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{6}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
,
and give a complete LU-equivalent classification of 4-GBS sets,
that is, we provide all of the 4-GPM sets U-equivalent or UC-equivalent to the 10 known GPM sets given by
tian2016pra
.
In other words, the results and programs in this work can be use to find out all GPM sets UC-equivalent to a given GPM set,
and then determine the LU-equivalence of two GBS sets in a bipartite system
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
This implies that LU-equivalence is a practical and effective classification tool,
which may be very useful for the research of quantum entanglement.
For example, for the problem of local discrimination of GBS sets, if all GBS sets are successfully classified via LU-equivalence,
then only the representative elements of LU-equivalent classes need to be considered for local discrimination.
Now it is proven that there are 31 LU-equivalent classes of 4-GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{6}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
,
which provides a good foundation for solving the problem of local discrimination of GBS sets in
\mathbb{C}^{6}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
.

X^{12a_{2}}Z^{12b_{2}}\approx Z^{12}.

So
6a_{1}\equiv 30
(mod
72
),
6b_{1}\equiv 0
(mod
72
),
12a_{2}\equiv 0
(mod
72
) and
12b_{2}=12
(mod
72
).
That is,
a_{1}=5+12s
,
b_{1}\equiv 0
(mod
12
),
a_{2}\equiv 0
(mod
6
) and
b_{2}=1+6t
for some integers
s,t
.
Substituting these values into the equation
a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}\equiv 1
(mod
72
) yields a congruence equation
12s+30t\equiv 68
(mod
72
).
This shows that the Clifford operator
W
can deduce that the congruence equation is solvable.
Since
gcd(12,30,72)=6\nmid 68
, we know that the congruence equation is unsolvable.
Therefore, the unitary matrix
W
is not a Clifford operator.

We need to prepare two lemmas to show the sufficiency of Theorem
1
in advance.

Lemma 5
.

Let
C^{nm}\triangleq C^{n}\otimes C^{m}
be a bipartite system,
then
X_{nm}^{m}=X_{n}\otimes I_{m}
,
Z_{nm}=Z_{n}\otimes\sum_{i}\omega_{mn}^{i}|i\rangle\langle i|

and
Z_{nm}^{n}=I_{n}\otimes Z_{m}
.

Proof
.
Let
|i+mj\rangle=|j\rangle\otimes|i\rangle(i\in\mathbb{Z}_{m},j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n})
be the computational basis of
C^{nm}
.
Then

X_{nm}^{m}|i+mj\rangle=|i+m(j+1)\rangle=X_{n}\otimes I_{m}|i+mj\rangle
, and

Z_{nm}|i+mj\rangle=\omega_{mn}^{i+mj}|i+mj\rangle=Z_{n}|j\rangle\otimes\omega_{mn}^{i}|i\rangle=(Z_{n}\otimes\sum_{i}\omega_{mn}^{i}|i\rangle\langle i|)|i+mj\rangle.

Lemma 6
.

Let

and

are both positive factors of

,

Proof
.
It is easy to see that

and

Z^{-a}
and
X^{b}
respectively
via the Clifford operator with its symplectic representation

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\{X^{a},Z^{b}\}\mathop{\sim}\{Z^{-a},X^{b}\}.

(12)

Pseudocycles for Borel-Moore Homology
Spencer Cattalani and Aleksey Zinger

Abstract
Pseudocycles are geometric representatives for integral homology classes on smooth manifolds that have proved useful in particular for defining gauge-theoretic invariants. The Borel-Moore homology is often a more natural object to work with in the case of non-compact manifolds than the usual homology. We define weaker versions of the standard notions of pseudocycle and pseudocycle equivalence and then describe a natural isomorphism between the set of equivalence classes of these weaker pseudocycles and the Borel-Moore homology. We also include a direct proof of a Poincaré Duality between the singular cohomology of an oriented manifold and its Borel-Moore homology.

Introduction

1.1
Main theorem

Constructions of some important gauge-theoretic invariants involve representing
cohomology classes on a smooth manifold
X
geometrically.
As the submanifolds in
X
and embedded cobordisms between them do not generally suffice
for representing the singular homology
H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
of
X
,

pseudocycles
have been used as a suitable replacement in the case of compact manifolds.
For example, pseudocycles are central to the constructions of Gromov-Witten invariants
for compact semi-positive symplectic manifolds
in
[
7
, Section 7.1]
and
[
10
, Section 1]
.

The
Borel-Moore

homology

H^{\textnormal{lf}}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
of a topological space
X
,
also known as the homology with closed supports and the homology based on locally finite chains,
is introduced from a sheaf-theoretic perspective in
[
1
]
.
If
X
is compact,
H^{\textnormal{lf}}_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is just the usual singular homology
H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.
On the other hand, a closed oriented
k
-submanifold

in a manifold

determines
a class
[M]_{X}
in

, even if
M
is not compact.
If
X
is an oriented
n
-manifold,

is Poincaré dual to
the singular cohomology
H^{n-k}(X;\mathbb{Z})
with respect to their pairing
with the compactly supported cohomology
H_{c}^{k}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.
The purpose of the present paper is to provide an analogue of pseudocycles for
the Borel-Moore homology of a non-compact manifold
X
and a geometric way of representing
all cohomology of
X
.
As indicated in
[
2
, Section 1]
and
[
3
, Section 1]
,
classes on non-compact manifolds can be relevant even if one is interested
only in compact manifolds.

A subset

of a manifold

is
of dimension at most k
,
which we write as

\dim\,Z\leq k\,,

if there exists a
k

a smooth map

such that
Z\!\subset\!h(Y)
.
If

is a continuous map between topological spaces,

the boundary of

is the subspace

\textnormal{Bd}~\!f\equiv\bigcap_{K\subset M\text{~cmpt}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\overline{f(M\!-\!K)}\subset X,

where the overline

denotes the closure in
X
.
If

is a closed compact subset disjoint from
\textnormal{Bd}~\!f
,
then

is compact.
A continuous map

.
If
\textnormal{Bd}~\!f\!=\!\emptyset

If

If

Definition 1.1
.

Let

be a smooth manifold.

(a)

A smooth map

is a

-pseudocycle
if

is an oriented
k
-manifold and
\dim\textnormal{Bd}~\!f\!\leq\!k\!-\!2
.

(b)

Two Borel-Moore

-pseudocycles

,

\displaystyle\dim Y_{0},\dim Y_{1}\leq k\!-\!1,\quad\partial\widetilde{M}=(M_{1}\!-\!Y_{1})\!\sqcup\!-(M_{0}\!-\!Y_{0}),

\displaystyle\dim\textnormal{Bd}~\!\widetilde{f}\leq k\!-\!1,\quad\widetilde{f}|_{M_{0}-Y_{0}}=f_{0}|_{M_{0}-Y_{0}},\quad\widetilde{f}|_{M_{1}-Y_{1}}=f_{1}|_{M_{1}-Y_{1}}.

(c)

The
k
-th Borel-Moore pseudocycle group
is the set
{\mathcal{H}}^{\textnormal{cl}}_{*}(X)
of
equivalence classes of Borel-Moore

-pseudocycles to

with the addition induced
by the disjoint union.

Example 1.2
.

If

is a Borel-Moore

-pseudocycle and

Let

There exist homomorphisms of graded abelian groups

Let

,

2.1
Standard simplicies

In order to set up notation for the standard simplices, their subsets, and maps between

For

, let

[k]=\big\{0,1,2,\ldots,k\big\}.

For a finite subset

, we denote by

the (

)

of

\begin{split}CH(A)&=\Big\{\sum_{v\in A}t_{v}v\!:t_{v}\!\in\![0,1];\,\sum_{v\in A}t_{v}\!=\!1\Big\}\qquad\hbox{and}\\
CH^{0}(A)&=\Big\{\sum_{v\in A}t_{v}v\!:t_{v}\!\in\!(0,1);\,\sum_{v\in A}t_{v}\!=\!1\Big\}.\end{split}

If

.

For each

, let

be the

-th coordinate vector in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Put

Denote by

\Delta^{k}=\textnormal{CH}\big(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\big)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\textnormal{Int}~\!\Delta^{k}=\textnormal{CH}^{0}\big(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\big)

-simplex
and its interior.
Let

For each

, let

\Delta^{k}_{p}=\textnormal{CH}\big(\big\{e_{q}\!:q\!\in\![k]\!-\!\{p\}\!\big\}\big)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\textnormal{Int}~\!\Delta_{p}^{k}=\textnormal{CH}^{0}\big(\big\{e_{q}\!:q\!\in\![k]\!-\!\{p\}\!\big\}\big)

denote the

-th face
of
\Delta^{k}

Define a linear map

\iota_{k;p}\!:\Delta^{k-1}\longrightarrow\Delta^{k}_{p}\subset\Delta^{k}\qquad\hbox{by}\qquad\iota_{k;p}(e_{q})=\begin{cases}e_{q},&\hbox{if}~q\!<\!p;\\

\widetilde{\pi}^{k}_{p}\!:\Delta^{k}\!-\!\{e_{p}\}\longrightarrow\Delta^{k}_{p}\qquad\hbox{by}\qquad\widetilde{\pi}^{k}_{p}\Big(\sum_{q=0}^{q=k}t_{q}e_{q}\Big)=\frac{1}{1\!-\!t_{p}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq q\leq k\\

Define a neighborhood of

\displaystyle\hbox{and}\qquad\textnormal{sign}~\!\tau_{r;(i_{1},p_{1}),(i_{2},p_{2})}=-(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}},

Since every Borel-Moore singular chain (

with

,

, and

(3.39)

Summing this equation with

from 1 to

(3.40)

Similarly to Lemma
3.5
, (
3.40
) implies that

a symmetric subset

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\iota}_{r},\widetilde{p}_{r})\!:K_{r}^{\textnormal{top}}\!\sqcup\!\mathcal{I}_{r}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{f},~~~\hbox{and}~~~\widetilde{\tau}_{r}\!:K_{r}^{\textnormal{top}}\!\sqcup\!\mathcal{I}_{r}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{k},~~i\longrightarrow\widetilde{\tau}_{(r,i)},

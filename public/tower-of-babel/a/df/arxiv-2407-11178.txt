Spacetime with prescribed hidden symmetry
Song He, Yi Li

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate spacetime characterized by a hidden symmetry defined by a given Killing tensor. To exhibit this hidden symmetry, the inverse metric must commute with the Killing tensor under the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket, which translates into a system of partial differential equations (PDEs) for the inverse metric. For some significant examples, we solve these PDEs directly, deriving spacetimes with prescribed hidden symmetries, including those specified by higher-rank Killing tensors. Utilizing the hidden symmetries, we study related problems such as null geodesics, photon region, and separation of variables of wave equations. Through this work, we aim to demonstrate that hidden symmetry is more accessible than previously believed.

III
Examples of spacetime with prescribed hidden symmetry

The typical physical scenario for a spacetime with prescribed hidden symmetry is as follows. We begin with a spacetime possessing strong explicit symmetry and then deform the metric to break some of these explicit symmetries, such as rotating a spherical black hole. Our focus is on deformed metrics that preserve a (reducible) Killing tensor of the original metric while breaking the explicit symmetries constituting the Killing tensor. The Killing tensor becomes irreducible and characterizes the hidden symmetry of the deformed metric. Despite the reduced explicit symmetry, the hidden symmetry allows for the exact solution of many problems in the deformed spacetime.

in terms of the radius and plug in the inequality from the third equation in (
13
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}-\csc^{2}\theta\mathcal{J}^{2}=\frac{r^{4}\dot{\theta}^{2}}{E^{2}}\geq 0

(16)

This inequality gives us the region where spherical photon orbits are allowed, namely the photon region. Taking the modified Lense-Thirring spacetime
[
35
]
for an example, we have
f(r)=h(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}},\omega(r)=a\frac{f(r)-1}{r^{2}}
and
s(r)=0
. By (
15
)
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{C}
are given by the spherical orbit radius
r
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=-\frac{l^{2}r^{4}\big(r(r-3M)+2q^{2}\big)}{a\big[r^{4}\big(r(r+3M)-2q^{2}\big)+l^{2}\big(2Mr^{2}(2r-3M)-q^{2}r(3r-7M)-2q^{4}\big)\big]}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\frac{4l^{2}r^{4}(3Mr-2q^{2})^{2}\big(r^{4}+l^{2}(q^{2}+r(r-2M))\big)}{\big[r^{4}(r(r+3M)-2q^{2})+l^{2}\big(2Mr^{2}(2r-3M)-q^{2}r(3r-7M)-2q^{4}\big)\big]^{2}}

(17)

The photon region is given by (
16
). It takes a simple form for the asymptotically flat and uncharged case (
l\to\infty,q=0
)

\displaystyle(\frac{r}{M})^{3}(\frac{r}{M}-3)^{2}\leq\frac{36a^{2}}{M^{2}}(\frac{r}{M}-2)\sin^{2}\theta

(18)

III.2
Hidden symmetry with broken planar symmetry

Consider a static plane-symmetric spacetime with the metric

\displaystyle g^{(0)}

\displaystyle=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{h(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}d\phi^{2})

\displaystyle=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{h(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})

(19)

We have Killing vectors
\partial_{x},\partial_{y},\partial_{\phi}
corresponding to the planar symmetry and
\partial_{t}
corresponding to the time translation symmetry. We have the “spatial momentum squared” as a reducible rank two Killing tensor

\displaystyle K=\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}=\partial_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{\phi}^{2}

(20)

Similar to the spherical case, we can show that the general deformed metric (modulo diffeomorphism) that preserves the Killing tensor
K
,
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{\phi}
but breaks the planar symmetry is

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{h(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\rho^{2}+\frac{r^{2}\rho^{2}}{1+s(r)\rho^{2}}(d\phi+\omega(r)dt)^{2}

(21)

As an application of the hidden symmetry in the deformed spacetime, we study the separation of variables of the Klein-Gordon equation. Formally, the separation of variables can be characterized by a set of commuting operators, including the one in the PDE, whose common eigenfunctions span the whole solution space to the PDE. For commuting Killing vectors and rank two Killing tensors, the operators constructed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=V^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha},\mathcal{K}=\nabla_{\alpha}K^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\beta}

(22)

are found to commute except for “anomaly” terms
[
77
,
78
]
. In our case, we have four operators constructed from the Killing tensors,
\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}
in the Klein-Gordon equation,
\mathcal{K}=\nabla_{\alpha}K^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\beta}
from the rank two Killing tensor,
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{\phi}
. They mutually commute except for the possible anomaly in
[\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha},\mathcal{K}]
, which vanishes only when
s(r)=s
is a constant. In this case, the separation of variables is relatively straightforward. We write a solution in the form

\displaystyle\Phi(t,r,\rho,\phi)=e^{-iEt+iJ\phi}\mathcal{F}(r)\mathcal{G}(\rho)

(23)

and the Klein-Gordan equation

\displaystyle(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}-m^{2})\Phi=0

(24)

is reduced to

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}\big(h^{{}^{\prime}}(r)+h(r)(\frac{4}{r}+\frac{f^{{}^{\prime}}(r)}{f(r)})\big)\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)}{2\mathcal{F}(r)}+\frac{r^{2}h(r)\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)}{\mathcal{F}(r)}

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{2}(E+J\omega(r))^{2}}{f(r)}-m^{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathcal{G}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{G}(\rho)}+\frac{\mathcal{G}^{{}^{\prime}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{G}(\rho)\rho(1+s\rho^{2})}-J^{2}\frac{(1+s\rho^{2})}{\rho^{2}}=0

(25)

Usually, the separation of variables above results in Sturm-Liouville eigenfunction equations for
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
. The eigenfunctions form a complete basis and justify the separation of variables method to solve the original linear PDE, namely solutions of the form (
23
), with variables separated, span the space of solutions to the Klein-Gordon equation. Now, we consider the case when
s(r)
is an arbitrary function. It’s convenient to change the coordinate
v=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}
and the metric now reads

\displaystyle g=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{h(r)}dr^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{4v^{3}}dv^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{v+s(r)}d\phi^{2}

(26)

Again, we write a solution in the form with variables separated

\displaystyle\Phi(t,r,v,\phi)=e^{-iEt+iJ\phi}\mathcal{F}(r)\mathcal{G}(v)

(27)

The Klein-Gordon equation is reduced to

\displaystyle a_{1}(r)+b_{1}(v)+(v+s(r))(a_{2}(r)+b_{2}(v))=0

(28)

where we define

\displaystyle a_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{r^{2}h(r)s^{{}^{\prime}}(r)\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)}{\mathcal{F}(r)}

\displaystyle b_{1}(v)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4v^{3}\mathcal{G}^{{}^{\prime}}(v)}{\mathcal{G}(v)}

\displaystyle a_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{2E^{2}r^{2}}{f(r)}-2m^{2}r^{2}-2J^{2}s(r)+\big(4rh(r)+\frac{r^{2}h(r)f^{{}^{\prime}}(r)}{f(r)}+r^{2}h^{{}^{\prime}}(r)\big)\frac{\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)}{\mathcal{F}(r)}+\frac{2r^{2}h(r)\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)}{\mathcal{F}(r)}

\displaystyle b_{2}(v)

\displaystyle=-2J^{2}v+\frac{12v^{2}\mathcal{G}^{{}^{\prime}}(v)}{\mathcal{G}(v)}+\frac{8v^{3}\mathcal{G}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(v)}{\mathcal{G}(v)}

(29)

Differentiating with respect to
v
, we find

\displaystyle b_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(v)+a_{2}(r)+b_{2}(v)+vb_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}(v)+s(r)b_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}(v)=0

(30)

We have assumed
s(r)
is not a constant function, so
b_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}(v)
must be a constant, say,
C_{1}
. Then
a_{2}(r)+C_{1}s(r)
must be another constant, say,
C_{2}
. A bit more computation leads us to the following separation of variables

\displaystyle b_{2}(v)=C_{1}v+C_{3},\quad b_{1}(v)=-C_{1}v^{2}-(C_{2}+C_{3})v+C_{4}

\displaystyle a_{2}(r)+C_{1}s(r)=C_{2},\quad a_{1}(r)+(C_{2}+C_{3})s(r)-C_{1}s(r)^{2}+C_{4}=0

(31)

where
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
are another two constants. These four ODEs, two second order and two first order, are clearly over-constraining compared to the original PDE. For example, from the second equation above and the boundary condition that
\mathcal{G}(v)
must be finite as
v\to 0
and
v\to\infty
, we find
\mathcal{G}(v)
is a constant. Therefore, solutions of the form with variables separated only span a very small subspace of the whole solution space of the Klein-Gordon equation, so separation of variables in terms of
t,r,\rho,\phi
coordinates is not possible. We knew
\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}
doesn’t commute with
\mathcal{K}
if
s(r)
is not a constant, our direct computation ensures that there is also no modification to
\mathcal{K}
to make the Klein-Gordon equation separable in
t,r,\rho,\phi
coordinates.

We have relatively few examples in the literature of irreducible higher rank Killing tensors. In fact, here we can solve for a deformed metric that preserves higher rank (
r\geq 3
) Killing tensors, for example

\displaystyle T=K\partial_{t}=\partial_{t}(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})

(32)

Looking for a solution to (
4
) that is independent on
\phi
, we find

\displaystyle(g^{\mu\nu})=\begin{pmatrix}G^{tt}(r)+4t^{2}a(r)-4tb(r)&G^{tr}(r)-2tG^{r\rho}(r)t&\rho(-2ta(r)+b(r))&\omega(r)-2tc(r)\\
G^{tr}(r)-2tG^{r\rho}(r)t&G^{rr}(r)&\rho G^{r\rho}(r)&G^{r\phi}(r)\\
\rho(-2ta(r)+b(r))&\rho G^{r\rho}(r)&G^{\rho\rho}(r)+\rho^{2}a(r)&\rho c(r)\\
\omega(r)-2tc(r)&G^{r\phi}(r)&\rho c(r)&\frac{G^{\rho\rho}(r)}{\rho^{2}}+s(r)\end{pmatrix}

An infinitesimal diffeomorphism that preserves
\partial_{\phi}
and
T
is of the form

\displaystyle V=(-2tW^{\rho}(r)+W^{t}(r))\partial_{t}+V^{r}(r)\partial_{r}+\rho W^{\rho}(r)\partial_{\rho}+V^{\phi}(r)\partial_{\phi}

(33)

Modulo this diffeomorphism, the general deformed inverse metric that preserves
T
and
\partial_{\phi}
but breaks
\partial_{t}
or
K
(thus making
T
irreducible) is given by

\displaystyle(g^{\mu\nu})=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{f(r)}+4t^{2}a(r)-4tb(r)&0&\rho(-2ta(r)+b(r))&-2tc(r)\\
0&h(r)&0&0\\
\rho(-2ta(r)+b(r))&0&\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\rho^{2}a(r)&\rho c(r)\\
-2tc(r)&0&\rho c(r)&\frac{1}{r^{2}\rho^{2}}\end{pmatrix}

The metric itself takes a complicated form. It becomes simpler if we look at a single mode of deformation, for example, with
a(r),b(r)
set to zero and only
c(r)
turned on

\displaystyle g=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{h(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\rho^{2}+\frac{r^{2}\rho^{2}}{1+c(r)^{2}(4t^{2}r^{2}\rho^{2}f(r)-r^{4}\rho^{4})}\big(d\phi-c(r)(2tf(r)dt+r^{2}\rho d\rho)\big)^{2}

(34)

IV
Discussion

In this paper, we study spacetimes with prescribed hidden symmetries and provide several examples. Beyond our examples, numerous spacetimes with hidden symmetries can be constructed in a similar manner and used to explore general questions related to hidden symmetry, such as the separation of variables in wave equations or the existence of photon surfaces
[
79
,
80
]
. An especially interesting question is how hidden symmetries can be realized for wave equations.

Our construction of spacetime is based solely on symmetry, without reference to any specific physical gravity theory. It is of great interest to investigate whether these spacetimes with hidden symmetries are approximate or exact solutions to relevant gravity theories, with or without matter; see
[
81
]
for work in this direction.

Mathematically, solving the defining equation (
4
) is central to our construction. It is particularly important to develop a qualitative understanding of this system of PDEs when the Killing tensor is of higher rank (
r\geq 3
).

Finally, our work can be extended to other Killing objects related to hidden symmetry, such as the Killing-Yano form
[
82
,
83
]
(see also the reviews
[
18
,
19
]
) and the generalized Killing tensor
[
84
]
.

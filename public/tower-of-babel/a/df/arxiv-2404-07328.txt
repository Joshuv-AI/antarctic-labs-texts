Affine super Yangians and deformed double current superalgebras
Nicolas Guay, Peggy Jankovic, Mamoru Ueda

Abstract
We extend to the super Yangian of the special linear Lie superalgebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 m | n \mathfrak{sl}_{m|n} and its affine version certain results related to Schur-Weyl duality. We do the same for the deformed double current superalgebra of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 m | n \mathfrak{sl}_{m|n} , which is introduced here for the first time.

Introduction

The study of quantum groups and of Lie superalgebras has found a lot of inspiration in theoretical physics, although both of those families of mathematical objects are of interest from a purely mathematical point of view. Their intersection is the theory of quantum superalgebras, which include, for instance, super Yangians and quantum loop superalgebras associated to classical Lie superalgebras like
\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}
, the special linear Lie superalgebra. It is also possible to consider quantum superalgebras associated to affine Lie superalgebras, one family being the affine super Yangians and another one the related quantum toroidal superalgebras. One goal of this paper is to increase our understanding of the super Yangians by extending to them some results known for the Yangian of the general linear Lie algebra, e.g. Schur-Weyl duality. 2.1
The Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}

Let us set
M_{k,l}(\mathbb{C})
as the set of
k\times l
matrices over
\mathbb{C}
. We define the Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
as follows;

\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}A\in M_{m,m}(\mathbb{C}),B\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C}),C\in M_{n,m}(\mathbb{C}),\text{ and }D\in M_{n,n}(\mathbb{C})\right\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\text{str}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}=\text{tr}(A)-\text{tr}(D)=0\right\},

where tr is the trace of a matrix and we set the commutator relation
\left[\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\
G&H\end{pmatrix}\right]
as

\displaystyle\left[\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\
G&H\end{pmatrix}\right]=\begin{pmatrix}AE-EA+(BG+FC)&AF+BH-(EB+FD)\\
CE+DG-(GA+HC)&DH-HD+(CF+GB)\end{pmatrix}.

We denote by
E_{i,j}
the matrix unit, that is, the matrix whose
(i,j)
component is
1
and other components are
0
. Then,
\{E_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq m+n}
becomes a basis of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
. By the definition of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}
, for
m\neq n
, we have a decomposition

\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}=\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}\oplus\mathbb{C}I_{m+n},

where
I_{m+n}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq m+n}E_{i,i}
.

The notion of the Cartan subalgebra works in the superalgebra setting as well. The Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}
is made up of all diagonal matrices.
Let the parity for an integer
i\in\{1,\dots,m+n\}
be defined as

|i|=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }1\leq i\leq m,\\
1&\text{ if }m+1\leq i\leq m+n.\end{cases}

The Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}
,
C=(a_{i,j})^{m+n-1}_{i,j=1}
, has entries

a_{i,j}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|i|}+(-1)^{|i+1|}&\text{ if }i=j,\\
-(-1)^{|i+1|}&\text{ if }j=i+1,\\
-(-1)^{|i|}&\text{ if }i=j+1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We can use the supertrace to define a non-degenerate supersymmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}
given by

(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}\times\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\ (E_{i,j},E_{k,l})=\text{str}(E_{i,j}E_{k,l})=\delta_{i,l}\delta_{j,k}(-1)^{|i|}.

If we restrict the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
to
\mathfrak{h}
, we obtain a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}
given by

(E_{i,i},E_{j,j})=\delta_{i,j}(-1)^{|i|}.

Denote by
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m+n}
the basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
dual to
\{E_{i,i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m+n}
. We can identify
\epsilon_{i}
with
((-1)^{|i|}E_{i,i},\cdot)
.
Then, the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{h}
induces a non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, which we can also denote
(\cdot,\cdot)
, given by

(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j})=\delta_{i,j}(-1)^{|i|}.

The root system of
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}
can be expressed as
\Phi=\Phi_{0}\cup\Phi_{1}
with

\Phi_{0}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}|i\neq j,|i|=|j|\},\ \Phi_{1}=\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})|i\leq m<j\}.

Let
\alpha_{i}
be
\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}
. The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}
has positive simple roots
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m+n-1}\},
where
\alpha_{m}
is odd and the rest are even.

2.2
Schur-Weyl Duality for
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
and the Symmetric group

In the classical case, the Schur-Weyl duality describes the relationship between representations of the general linear Lie group (or Lie algebra) and representations of the symmetric group.
This duality was generalized to the superalgebra setting by Sergeev in 1985
[

]

We set a super vector space

are even and
e_{m+1},\cdots,e_{m+n}

|v|

\displaystyle X\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{l}v_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|X|\sum_{j\leq i-1}|j|}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{i-1}v_{j}\right)\otimes Xv_{i}\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{k=i+1}^{l}v_{k}\right),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i,i+1}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{l}v_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|v_{i}||v_{i+1}|}\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{i-1}v_{j}\right)\otimes v_{i+1}\otimes v_{i}\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{k=i+1}^{l}v_{k}\right)

(2.2)

for a homogeneous element

and homogeneous elements

.
Let
\Phi_{l}:U(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n})\rightarrow\End(\mathbb{C}({m|n})^{\otimes l})
and

Lemma 2.1
.

[Lemma 8 in

Weyl’s Theorem provides a parametrization of irreducible representations of

Schur-Weyl duality for the Degenerate Affine Hecke algebra and the Super Yangian of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}

Classical Schur-Weyl duality was extended to the degenerate affine Hecke algebra of

and the Yangian of

[

3.1
Degenerate Affine Hecke Algebra

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
. The degenerate affine Hecke algebra
H^{\deg}_{\kappa}(S_{l})
is the associative algebra with generators

\sigma_{1}^{\pm 1},\sigma_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots,\sigma_{l-1}^{\pm 1},\mathsf{u}_{1},\mathsf{u}_{2},\dots,\mathsf{u}_{l}
, and the following defining relations:

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{i}=1,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\text{ if }|i-j|>1,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{u}_{i}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}\mathsf{u}_{i+1}-\kappa,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{u}_{i+1}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}\mathsf{u}_{i}+\kappa,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathsf{u}_{i}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}\mathsf{u}_{i}\text{ if }i\neq j,j+1,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathsf{u}_{i}\mathsf{u}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{u}_{j}\mathsf{u}_{i}.

(3.8)

We identify the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[S_{l}]
with the subalgebra of
H^{\deg}_{\kappa}(S_{l})
generated by
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{l-1}
. For
1\leq k\leq l
, we define elements

by

. The element
\mathsf{z}_{k}
has another presentation.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following relation holds:

\displaystyle\mathsf{z}_{k}=\mathsf{u}_{k}-\kappa\sum_{j<k}\sigma_{j,k}.

(3.9)

In paticular, we have
\mathsf{z}_{k}=\mathsf{u}_{k}+\sum_{1\leq j<k}\sigma_{j,k}
if
\kappa=-1
.

Proof.

We can prove the relation (
3.9
) by induction on
k
using

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,k+1}\mathsf{u}_{1}\sigma_{1,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k,k+1}\sigma_{1,k}\sigma_{k,k+1}\mathsf{u}_{1}\sigma_{k,k+1}\sigma_{1,k}\sigma_{k,k+1}=\sigma_{k,k+1}\sigma_{1,k}\mathsf{u}_{1}\sigma_{1,k}\sigma_{k,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k,k+1}\left(\mathsf{u}_{k}-\kappa\sum_{1\leq j<k}\sigma_{j,k}\right)\sigma_{k,k+1}

for

.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The elements
\mathsf{z}_{i}
satisfy

\displaystyle\sigma\mathsf{z}_{i}=\mathsf{z}_{\sigma(i)}\sigma,

\displaystyle[\mathsf{z}_{i},\mathsf{z}_{j}]=-\kappa(\mathsf{z}_{i}-\mathsf{z}_{j})\sigma_{i,j}

for all

and

.

The relation (

) can be proven by the induction on the length of

3.2
The RTT presentation of the Super Yangian

The super Yangian associated with
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n}
was first introduced in
[
20
]
. We will follow the notation used by Gow
[
11
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

The super Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{m|n})
is the associative superalgebra generated by the elements
t_{i,j}^{(r)}
for
1\leq i,j\leq m+n
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
satisfying:

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}(u),t_{k,l}(v)]=\frac{(-1)^{\nu(i,j;k,l)}}{(u-v)}\left(t_{k,j}(u)t_{i,l}(v)-t_{k,j}(v)t_{i,l}(u)\right),

(3.18)

\displaystyle\nu(i,j;k,l)=|i||j|+|i||k|+|j||k|,

(3.19)

where the generating power series
t_{i,j}(u)
is defined by

t_{i,j}(u)=\delta_{i,j}+\sum_{r\geq 1}\limits t^{(r)}_{i,j}u^{-r}

and the parity of
t^{(r)}_{i,j}
is
|i|+|j|
.
Expanding (
3.18
) yields useful explicit relations for the generators:

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}^{(r)},t_{k,l}^{(s)}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\nu(i,j;k,l)}\sum_{a=0}^{\textrm{min}(r,s)-1}\left(t_{k,j}^{(a)}t_{i,l}^{(r+s-1-a)}-t_{k,j}^{(r+s-1-a)}t_{i,l}^{(a)}\right),

(3.20)

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}^{(r+1)},t_{k,l}^{(s)}]-[t_{ij}^{(r)},t_{k,l}^{(s+1)}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\nu(i,j;k,l)}\left(t_{k,j}^{(r)}t_{i,l}^{(s)}-t_{k,j}^{(s)}t_{i,l}^{(r)}\right),

(3.21)

where we set
t^{(0)}_{ij}=\delta_{i,j}
.

Note the special case when
r=1
:

[t_{i,j}^{(1)},t_{k,l}^{(s)}]=\left(\delta_{k,j}(-1)^{|j|}t_{i,l}^{(s)}-\delta_{i,l}(-1)^{|i|}t_{k,j}^{(s)}\right).

This presentation of the super Yangian is called the RTT presentation, due to the generators

satisfying the following relation in

(3.22)

where

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{m+n}(-1)^{|b|}E_{a,b}\otimes E_{b,a}\in\End(\mathbb{C}(m|n))\otimes\End(\mathbb{C}(m|n)),

(3.23)

\displaystyle R_{1,2}(u)

\displaystyle=\id\otimes\left(1-\frac{P}{u}\right),

(3.24)

\displaystyle T(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m+n}(-1)^{|j|(|i|+1)}t_{i,j}(u)\otimes E_{i,j},

(3.25)

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m+n}(-1)^{|j|(|i|+1)}t_{i,j}(u)\otimes E_{i,j}\otimes\id,

(3.26)

\displaystyle T_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m+n}(-1)^{|j|(|i|+1)}t_{i,j}(u)\otimes\id\otimes E_{i,j}.

(3.27)

3.3
Schur-Weyl functor between the degenerate affine Hecke algebras and the super Yangian

Hereafter, let us denote

. The goal of this subsection is to produce the functor
SW
from the category of right

-modules to the category of left

for any right

-module

Fix a right

-module

R_{i,l+1}(u-\mathsf{u}_{i})=1-\frac{P_{i,l+1}}{u-\mathsf{u}_{i}},

(3.28)

By the definition of

\displaystyle P^{t_{k}}_{k,l+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{m+n}(-1)^{|b|}\textrm{id}_{M}\otimes\textrm{id}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes E_{a,b}^{t}\otimes\textrm{id}^{\otimes l-k}\otimes E_{b,a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{m+n}(-1)^{|a|+|b|+|a||b|}\textrm{id}_{M}\otimes\textrm{id}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes E_{b,a}\otimes\textrm{id}^{\otimes l-k}\otimes E_{b,a}.

Theorem 3.6

For a right

\displaystyle\quad\left((E_{a,b}\otimes E_{b,a}\otimes 1)(E_{c,d}\otimes 1\otimes E_{d,c})\right)^{t_{1}}=(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)(|c|+|d|)}(E_{a,b}E_{c,d})^{t_{1}}\otimes E_{b,a}\otimes E_{d,c}

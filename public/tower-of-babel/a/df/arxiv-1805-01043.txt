1.
Volterra–type integral operator

Let
\Delta:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and
H(\Delta)
be the class of all analytic functions in
\Delta
. In 1977 Pommerenke
[
16
]
introduced an integral operator, called Volterra–type operator as follows:

T_{g}:=T_{g}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(s)g^{\prime}(s){\rm d}s\quad(z\in\Delta).

He proved that
T_{g}
is a bounded operator on the Hardy space
H^{2}
if, and only if,
g
belongs to the class
BMOA
. Also, Aleman and Siskakis
[
1
]
proved that this characterization (boundedness) is valid on each
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
and that
T_{g}
is compact on
H^{p}
if, and only if,
g\in VMOA
.
An another natural integral operator is defined as follows:

J_{g}f(z):=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(s)g(s){\rm d}s\quad(z\in\Delta).

It is interesting to know this fact that

J_{g}f(z)+T_{g}f(z)=M_{g}f-f(0)g(0),

where
M_{g}
is the multiplication operator and is defined by

M_{g}f(z)=g(z)f(z)\quad(f\in H(\Delta),z\in\Delta).

Indeed, if
f
and
g
are two normalized analytic functions, i.e.
f,g\in H(\Delta),f(0)=g(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=1
, then

J_{g}f(z)+T_{g}f(z)=g(z)f(z).

We note that the integral operators
T_{g}
and
J_{g}
contain the well–known
integral operators in the analytic function theory and geometric function theory, such as the generalized Bernardi–Libera–Livingston linear integral operator (see
[
2
,
7
,
8
]
), Srivastava–Owa fractional derivative operators
[
11
,
12
]
and the Cesáro integral operator,
[
18
,
19
]
.

Recently many researchers have been studied the integral operators
T_{g}
and
J_{g}
. For example, Li and Stević
[
6
]
studied the boundedness and the compactness of
T_{g}
and
J_{g}
on the Zygmund space and the little Zygmund space. We note that there exist some extensions of the integral operators
T_{g}
, see for example
[
20
]
.

The basis of this article is to study of the integral operator

(1.1)

T_{g}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(s)g^{\prime}(s){\rm d}s=\int_{0}^{1}f(tz)zg^{\prime}(tz){\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{z}f{\rm d}g.

2.
Some Subclasses of Analytic Functions

In this section we recall some certain subclasses of analytic functions. Further, we denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of all analytic and normalized functions in the open unit disc
\Delta
. The subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of univalent (that is, one–to–one) functions is denoted by
\mathcal{U}
. We say that the function
f\in\mathcal{U}
is starlike of order
\alpha
,
0\leq\alpha<1
if, and only if

{\rm Re}\left\{z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta).

We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
the class of starlike functions of order
\alpha
. Also,
f\in\mathcal{U}
is convex of order
\alpha
,
0\leq\alpha<1
iff
zf^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
. The class of convex functions of order
\alpha
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
. Analytically,
f\in\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
iff

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta).

Let
A
and
B
be two complex numbers such that
|A|>1
and
|B|\leq 1
. We say that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to the class
S^{*}(A,B)
if it satisfies the following subordination relation:

(2.1)

z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}\quad(z\in\Delta).

We note that for real numbers
A
and
B
, where
-1\leq B<A\leq 1
, the function
f
that satisfies the above subordination relation (
2.1
) is called ”the Janowski starlike function”.
Also, we say that
f\in\mathcal{K}(A,B)
if, and only if,
zf^{\prime}(z)\in S^{*}(A,B)
. Indeed, if
f
belongs to the class
\mathcal{K}(A,B)
, then it satisfies

1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}\quad(z\in\Delta).

We remark that
\mathcal{K}(2,1)
and
\mathcal{K}(2,-1)
become to the Ozaki conditions. Moreover, by the Lindelöf subordination principle (this principle states that if
f(z)\prec g(z)
, then
|f^{\prime}(0)|\leq|g^{\prime}(0)|
and
f(\Delta_{r})\subset g(\Delta_{r})
), if
f\in\mathcal{K}(2,1)
, then we have

(2.2)

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}<\frac{3}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Also, if
f\in\mathcal{K}(2,-1)
, then

(2.3)

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>-\frac{1}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Ozaki proved that if
f\in\mathcal{A}
with

f(z)f′(z)/z\neq 0
, there, and if either (
2.2
) or (
2.3
) holds throughout
\Delta
, then
f
is univalent and convex in at least one
direction in
\Delta
, see
[
13
]
.

Let
\mathcal{LU}
denote the family of normalized locally univalent functions in
\Delta
.
For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the class

\mathcal{G}\left(\beta\right)
consisting of all functions

f\in\mathcal{LU}
which satisfy the condition

(2.4)

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}<1+\frac{\beta}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

We note that
\mathcal{G}(1)\subset\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{G}(1)\equiv\mathcal{K}(2,1)
. Also the
functions in the class
\mathcal{G}\left(1\right)
are starlike of order 0 in
\Delta
. The functions class
\mathcal{G}\left(\beta\right)
was studied extensively by Kargar
et al.

[
5
]
(see also
[
10
]
).

Let
Aut(\Delta)
be the class of holomorphic automorphisms in
\Delta
. Any
\phi\in Aut(\Delta)
has the following representation:

\phi(z)=e^{i\vartheta}\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\quad(\vartheta,a\in\mathbb{R},z\in\Delta).

The family
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{A}
is called a linear–invariant family (L.I.F.), if
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{LU}
and
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
\phi\in Aut(\Delta)

F_{\phi}(f)(z):=\frac{f(\phi(z))-f(\phi(0))}{f^{\prime}(\phi(0))\phi^{\prime}(0)}\in\mathcal{F}.

The class
\mathcal{U}
and the class
\mathcal{K}(0)
on
\Delta
are a L.I.F., but the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(0)
on
\Delta
is not a L.I.F..
Also, we define the order of the L.I.F.
\mathcal{F}
as

{\rm ord}f:=\sup\left\{\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2}\right|:f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}

and the universal linear invariant family of order
\gamma\geq 1
as

\mathcal{UL}_{\gamma}:=\{f\in\mathcal{F}:{\rm ord}f\leq\gamma\}.

We remark that
\mathcal{UL}_{1}\equiv\mathcal{K}(0)
and
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{UL}_{2}
. For more details about the L.I.F. see
[
4
, Chapter 5]
.

For fixed
k\geq 2
, Let
V_{k}
denote the class of normalized functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}\right|_{z=re^{i\theta}}{\rm d}\theta\leq k\pi.

Indeed, a function
f\in V_{k}
maps
\Delta
conformally onto an image domain
f(\Delta)
of boundary rotation at most
k\pi
.
The functions class
V_{k}
was introduced by Loewner

[
9
]
in 1917 and was developed by Paatero
[
14
]
who systematically developed their properties and made an exhaustive study of the class
V_{k}
. In association with the class
V_{k}
, Robertson proved that (see
[
17
, Theorem 1]
) the following inequlaity

(2.5)

\left|z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{2|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{k|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\quad(z\in\Delta,2\leq k<\infty)

holds true for each
f\in V_{k}
.

3.
Some Key Lemmas

In this section we recall some lemmas which help us in order to prove of main results.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
see
[
3
]
)

If
f\in\mathcal{UL}_{\gamma}
and
\gamma\geq 1
, then

\left|z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{2|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{2\gamma|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\qquad(z\in\Delta).

The next lemma is due to Pommerenke
[
15
]
, see also
[
4
, Lemma 5.1.3]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a linear–invariant family and
\delta={\rm ord}\mathcal{F}
. Then

(3.1)

\delta=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sup_{|z|<1}\left|-\overline{z}+\frac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|\quad(z\in\Delta).

The following lemma gives a basic estimate which leads to the distortion theorem for univalent functions.

Lemma 3.3
.

(
see
[
4
, p. 15]
)

If
f\in\mathcal{U}
, then

\left|z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{2r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{4r}{1-r^{2}}\qquad(|z|=r<1).

The estimate is sharp for rotation of Koebe function.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
see
[
10
]
)

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
be fixed. If
f\in\mathcal{G}(\beta)
, then

\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\beta}{1-|z|}\quad(z\in\Delta).

The result is sharp for the function
f^{\prime}(z)=(1-z)^{\beta}
.

In the present paper, we obtain the radius of convexity of the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
when the functions
f
and
g
belonging to the some certain subclasses of analytic functions which are defined in Section
2
.

4.
Main Results

We begin this section with the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be two complex numbers,
|A|>1
and
|B|\leq 1
and
T_{g}
be given by (
1.1
). Also, let
0\leq\alpha<1
be real number. If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(A,B)
and
g\in\mathcal{K}(A,B)
, then the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is convex of order
\alpha
in
|z|\leq r_{c}(A,B,\alpha)
, where

r_{c}(A,B,\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\hskip-42.67912pt\frac{2-\alpha}{2|A|}&\displaystyle{B=0}\\
\frac{|B-A|-|(\alpha-1)B-A|}{\alpha|B|^{2}-2{Re}\{A\overline{B}\}}&\displaystyle{B\neq 0}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Using the definition of convexity of order
\alpha
, where
0\leq\alpha<1
it is enough to find out the largest number
0<r<1
for which

\min_{|z|=r}{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}-\alpha\geq 0.

Further, from (
1.1
) we have

(4.1)

1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}-\alpha=z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\alpha+z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}+1.

On the other hand, since
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(A,B)
,
by the Lindelöf subordination principle, we get

(4.2)

\left|z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\frac{1-A\overline{B}r^{2}}{1-|B|^{2}r^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{|B-A|r}{1-|B|^{2}r^{2}}\quad(|z|=r<1).

From the above inequality (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left\{z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}-\alpha

\displaystyle\geq{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{1-A\overline{B}r^{2}}{1-|B|^{2}r^{2}}\right\}-\frac{|B-A|r}{1-|B|^{2}r^{2}}-\alpha

(4.3)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\alpha-|B-A|r-\left({\rm Re}\{A\overline{B}\}-\alpha|B|^{2}\right)r^{2}}{1-|B|^{2}r^{2}}.

Also
g\in\mathcal{K}(A,B)
and the above same method imply that

(4.4)

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right\}\geq\frac{1-|B-A|r-{\rm Re}\{A\overline{B}\}r^{2}}{1-|B|^{2}r^{2}}.

Now from (
4.1
), (
4
) and (
4.4
) we get

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}-\alpha\geq\frac{2-\alpha-2|B-A|r-\left(2{\rm Re}\{A\overline{B}\}-\alpha|B|^{2}\right)r^{2}}{1-|B|^{2}r^{2}}>0

provided
\phi(r):=2-\alpha-2|B-A|r-\left(2{\rm Re}\{A\overline{B}\}-\alpha|B|^{2}\right)r^{2}>0
. A simple calculation gives that the roots of
\phi(r)
are

\frac{|B-A|\pm|(\alpha-1)B-A|}{\alpha|B|^{2}-2{\rm Re}\{A\overline{B}\}}.

Also, it is clear that if
B=0
, then
\phi(r)
yields that
\phi(r)=2-\alpha-2|A|r
and it will be positive if
r<(2-\alpha)/2|A|
. This completes the proof.
∎

Putting
A=2
and
B=1
in Theorem
4.1
, we get.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
{\rm Re}\{zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\}<3/2
where
z\in\Delta
and
g
satisfies the condition (
2.2
). Then the radius of convexity of order
\alpha
of the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is
\frac{\alpha-2}{\alpha-4}
where
(0\leq\alpha<1)
.

If we take
A=2
and
B=-1
in Theorem
4.1
, then we have the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
{\rm Re}\{zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\}>-1/2
where
z\in\Delta
and
g
satisfies the condition (
2.3
). Then the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is convex of order
\alpha

(0\leq\alpha<1)
in the disc
|z|\leq(2-\alpha)/(4+\alpha)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
and
\gamma\geq 1
. If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
and
g\in\mathcal{LU}_{\gamma}
, then the radius of convexity of the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is

(4.5)

r^{-}_{c}(\alpha,\gamma)=\frac{\gamma-\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\gamma^{2}-1}}{1-\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
(
0\leq\alpha<1
) and
g
belongs to the family
\mathcal{LU}_{\gamma}
(
\gamma\geq 1
). From (
1.1
) we get

(4.6)

1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}=z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}+1.

Since
g\in\mathcal{LU}_{\gamma}
, Lemma
3.1
implies that

(4.7)

{\rm Re}\left\{z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right\}\geq\frac{2r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}-\frac{2\gamma r}{1-r^{2}}.

Now by definition of starlikeness of order
\alpha
and using the above inequality (
4.7
), and also applying the relation (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}+1\right\}

\displaystyle>\frac{1+\alpha-2\gamma r+(1-\alpha)r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}>0,

provided
\varphi(r)=1+\alpha-2\gamma r+(1-\alpha)r^{2}>0
. It is easy to see that the roots of
\varphi(r)
are

r^{\pm}_{c}(\alpha,\gamma)=\frac{\gamma\pm\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\gamma^{2}-1}}{1-\alpha}.

Also we see that if
0\leq\alpha<1
and
\gamma\geq 1
, then
0<r^{-}_{c}(\alpha,\gamma)<1
where
r^{-}_{c}(\alpha,\gamma)
is defined in (
4.5
). This is the end of proof.
∎

Putting
\alpha=0
and
\gamma=1
in Theorem
4.2
, we get.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be starlike and convex univalent functions in the open unit disc
\Delta
, respectively. Then the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is convex univalent function in
\Delta
, too.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
. If
f
is starlike function of order
\alpha
and
g\in\mathcal{F}
with
{\rm ord}\mathcal{F}=1
, then the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is convex univalent in the open unit disc
\Delta
.

Proof.

Since
f
is starlike function of order
\alpha
, we have

(4.8)

{\rm Re}\left\{z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta).

Also since
g\in\mathcal{F}
, by using the relation (
3.1
) and with a little calculation we get

(4.9)

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right\}\geq\frac{1-2\delta r+r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\quad(|z|=r<1).

Now, from (
4.6
), (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) and since
{\rm ord}\mathcal{F}=1
, we get

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}\geq\frac{1+\alpha-2r+(1-\alpha)r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}

and for
z\in\Delta
one deduces that

{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>0\quad(z\in\Delta).

Thus by definition we conclude that
T_{g}
is convex univalent in the open unit disc
\Delta
. This is the end of proof.
∎

The next theorem is the following.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f
be starlike function of order
\alpha
where
0\leq\alpha<1
and
g
be univalent function. Then the radius of convexity of the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is

r^{-}_{c}(\alpha)=\frac{2-\sqrt{3+\alpha^{2}}}{1-\alpha}\quad(0\leq\alpha<1).

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
where
0\leq\alpha<1
and
g\in\mathcal{U}
. From Lemma
3.3
and by (
4.6
), we get

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}+1\right\}

\displaystyle>\frac{1+\alpha-4r+(1-\alpha)r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}>0,

when
|z|\leq(2-\sqrt{3+\alpha^{2}})/(1-\alpha)
and concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Taking
\alpha=0
in Theorem
4.4
, we see that if
f
is starlike univalent function and
g\in\mathcal{U}
, then the radius of convexity of the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is
2-\sqrt{3}
. Indeed, in this case the radius of convexity the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is equal to familiar radius of convexity for the class
\mathcal{U}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 2.2.22]
).

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f
be starlike function of order
\alpha
where
0\leq\alpha<1
and
g
be locally univalent function which satisfies the inequality (
2.4
) where
0<\beta\leq 1
. Then the radius of convexity of the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is

r_{c}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1+\alpha}{1+\alpha+\beta}.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
and
g\in\mathcal{G}(\beta)
, where
0\leq\alpha<1
and
0<\beta\leq 1
. By Lemma
3.4
and from (
4.6
) we have

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}

\displaystyle>\alpha+1-\frac{\beta|z|}{1-|z|}\geq 0,

where
|z|\leq(1+\alpha)/(1+\alpha+\beta)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Finally we have.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
f
be starlike function of order
\alpha
where
0\leq\alpha<1
and
g\in V_{k}
where
k\geq 2
. Then the radius of convexity of the Volterra–type integral operator
T_{g}
is

r_{c}(\alpha,k)=\frac{k-\sqrt{k^{2}-4(1-\alpha^{2})}}{2(1-\alpha)}.

Proof.

From the definition of starlikeness of order
\alpha
, inequality (
2.5
) and (
4.6
), one deduces that

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left\{1+z\frac{T_{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{T_{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+z\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}+1\right\}

\displaystyle>\frac{(1-\alpha)r^{2}-kr+1+\alpha}{1-r^{2}}=\frac{\lambda(r)}{1-r^{2}}

where

\lambda(r):=(1-\alpha)r^{2}-kr+1+\alpha\quad(k\geq 2,0\leq\alpha<1).

It is easy to see that
\lambda(r)>0
if

r>\frac{k+\sqrt{k^{2}-4(1-\alpha^{2})}}{2(1-\alpha)}\geq 1\quad(k\geq 2,0\leq\alpha<1)

or

r<\frac{k-\sqrt{k^{2}-4(1-\alpha^{2})}}{2(1-\alpha)}\leq 1\quad(k\geq 2,0\leq\alpha<1).

This completes the proof.
∎

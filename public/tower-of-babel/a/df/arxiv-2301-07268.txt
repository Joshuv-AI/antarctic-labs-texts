1.
Introduction

This is one of two papers concerned with the construction of cluster structures on braid varieties. In the present paper, we work in the setting of a general simple algebraic group
G
and construct cluster seeds using algebraic geometry. In the companion paper
[
GLSBS22
]
, joint with David Speyer, we give an alternative proof in the special case
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
, using the combinatorics of plabic graphs and surfaces. The current work is logically independent of
[
GLSBS22
]
, which, however, ultimately produces the same cluster structure in the case
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
.

Let

be a complex, simple, simply-connected algebraic group,

opposing Borel subgroups,
U_{\pm}
their unipotent radicals,
H:=B_{+}\cap B_{-}
the torus,
I
the vertex set of the Dynkin diagram,
W
the Weyl group with simple generators
s_{i},i\in I
, and denote by
\dot{w}
the lift of
w\in W
to
G
as in (
2.1
). Let
w_{\circ}\in W
denote the longest element and
i\mapsto i^{*}
the action of
w_{\circ}
on
I
. Let
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\omega_{i}
for
i\in I
denote the simple roots, simple coroots, fundamental weights, respectively, and let
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
be the Cartan matrix given by
a_{ij}:={\langle}\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}_{j}{\rangle}
. Denote
d_{i}:=2/(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})
so that
d_{i}a_{ij}=d_{j}a_{ji}
.

1.2.
The seed

We give an informal summary of our construction; see
Section

2
for details and
[
GLSBS22
,
Gal23
]
for examples. We introduce an open dense algebraic torus
T_{\beta}\subset\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
called the
Deodhar torus
, so named for its relation to the Deodhar decomposition of Richardson varieties
[
Deo85
,
MR04
]
. It is defined by requiring the weighted flags
X_{c},Y_{c}
to be weakly
w_{c}
-related, where
w_{c}\in W
is maximal possible subject to (
1.2
) (for each
c=0,1,\dots,m
). The complement
\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}\setminus T_{\beta}
is a union of irreducible
mutable Deodhar hypersurfaces

\{V_{c}\mid c\in J_{\beta}^{{\rm mut}}\}
.
We define a partial compactification of
\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
so that the complement of
T_{\beta}
in it also includes
frozen Deodhar hypersurfaces

\{V_{c}\mid c\in J_{\beta}^{{\rm fro}}\}
. We let
J_{\beta}:=J_{\beta}^{{\rm fro}}\sqcup J_{\beta}^{{\rm mut}}
.
The following definition, suggested by David Speyer, is key to our approach.

Proposition-Definition 1.3
.

For
c\in J_{\beta}
, define the
cluster variable

x_{c}
to be the unique character of
T_{\beta}
that vanishes to order one on
V_{c}
and has neither a pole nor a zero on
V_{e}
for
e\in J_{\beta}\setminus\{c\}
. We denote the cluster by
{\mathbf{x}}_{\beta}=\{x_{c}\}_{c\in J_{\beta}}
.

We show that the cluster variables form a basis of the character lattice of
T_{\beta}
, and that they extend to regular functions on
\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
.
We consider a
2
-form
\omega_{\beta}
on
\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
, defined in terms of certain generalized minors
as a sum of local contributions for each letter of
\beta
. This is similar in spirit to
[
BFZ05
,
FG06
,
Ing19
,
SW21
]
. We introduce integers
\mathbf{d}=(d_{i})_{i\in J_{\beta}}
and expand
\omega_{\beta}
in the basis of cluster variables:

(1.3)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:intro:omcoef_{d}fn}\omega_{\beta}=\sum_{c,e\in J_{\beta}:\ c\leq e}d_{e}{\tilde{B}}_{ce}{\rm dlog}x_{c}\wedge{\rm dlog}x_{e}=\sum_{c,e\in J_{\beta}:\ c\leq e}d_{c}{\tilde{B}}_{ec}{\rm dlog}x_{e}\wedge{\rm dlog}x_{c}.

The coefficients
{\tilde{B}}_{ce}
define a
J_{\beta}\times J_{\beta}^{{\rm mut}}
integer matrix
{\tilde{B}}:=({\tilde{B}}_{ce})
. The principal
J_{\beta}^{{\rm mut}}\times J_{\beta}^{{\rm mut}}
part of the matrix
{\tilde{B}}
is skew-symmetrizable, with symmetrizer
{\rm diag}(d_{c}\mid c\in J_{\beta}^{{\rm mut}})
.
Therefore
\Sigma_{\beta}:=({\mathbf{x}}_{\beta},{\tilde{B}})
is a seed of a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{\beta})
of geometric type. The content of
Theorem

1.1
is that
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{\beta})={\mathbb{C}}[\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}]
.

1.3.
Overview of the proof

The proof that
\Sigma_{\beta}
provides a cluster structure for
\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
is obtained in two steps. First, we develop a notion of deletion-contraction for cluster seeds in
Section

3.3
. We apply this in the case when
\beta=ii\beta^{\prime}
starts with a repeated letter; the braids
i\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
correspond to deletion and contraction, respectively. Using deletion-contraction, we deduce that if
Theorem

1.1
holds for
i\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
then it holds for
\beta
.

Second, in
Section

4
, we describe moves
\beta\sim\beta^{\prime}
on double braid words that induce natural isomorphisms
\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}\cong\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta^{\prime}}
. In
Theorem

4.2
, we show that the isomorphisms
\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}\cong\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta^{\prime}}
give rise to a sequence of mutations connecting the corresponding seeds
\Sigma_{\beta}
and
\Sigma_{\beta^{\prime}}
.
Theorem

1.1
then follows by induction. In
Section

4
, we prove
Theorem

4.2
in the simply-laced case (i.e., for
G
of type
A
,
D
,
E
);
the seeds
\Sigma_{\beta}
and
\Sigma_{\beta^{\prime}}
either coincide or are related by a single mutation.
The proof of
Theorem

4.2
in the multiply-laced case is achieved via folding in
Sections

5
and
6
; the seeds
\Sigma_{\beta}
and
\Sigma_{\beta^{\prime}}
are related by a sequence of mutations. This generalizes a result of Fock and Goncharov
[
FG06
, Theorem 3.5]
.
Finally, in
Section

7
, we give an algorithm, implemented in
[
Gal23
]
, for computing our seeds using only root-system combinatorics.

2.1.
Background

For each

\phi_{i}:\operatorname{SL}_{2}\to G,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\

[

Let
\Phi
be the root system of

, with positive roots
\Phi^{+}
corresponding to
B_{+}
. Let

be the

of

and

of

\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset X^{\ast}(H)
(resp.,
\{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset X_{\ast}(H)

\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset X^{\ast}(H)

We have a natural pairing

.

Let the Weyl group

have simple generators

, length function
\ell(\cdot)
, and identity

, we set

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:dw_{d}fn}\dot{w}=\overline{w}:=\overline{s_{i_{1}}}\cdot\overline{s_{i_{2}}}\cdots\overline{s_{i_{l}}},\quad\overline{\overline{w}}:=\overline{\overline{s_{i_{1}}}}\cdot\overline{\overline{s_{i_{2}}}}\cdots\overline{\overline{s_{i_{l}}}}.

2.2.
Weighted flags

Recall from
Section

1.1

is an element
F=gU_{+}\in G/U_{+}
. Associated to a weighted flag

The following elementary facts can be found in e.g.
[

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F,F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime}
be weighted flags. Suppose
v,w\in W
with
\ell(vw)=\ell(v)+\ell(w)
.

(1)

F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\operatorname{id}}\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime}
if and only if
F=F^{\prime}
.

(2)

If
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime\prime}
, then
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle vw\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime\prime}
.

(3)

If
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle vw\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime\prime}
, then there exists a unique
F^{\prime}
such that
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime\prime}
. If
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle vw\ }}{{\Longrightarrow}}F^{\prime\prime}
then there exist unique
F^{\prime}_{1},F^{\prime}_{2}
such that
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\ }}{{\Longrightarrow}}F^{\prime\prime}
and
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\ }}{{\Longrightarrow}}F^{\prime}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime\prime}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose

and say

. Then there exists a unique

such that
F^{\prime}=gz_{i}(t)U_{+}
. Similarly, if
F^{\prime}=g^{\prime}U_{+}
, there exists a unique
t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
F=g^{\prime}\bar{z}_{i}(t^{\prime})U_{+}
. The maps
(g,F^{\prime})\mapsto t
and
(g^{\prime},F)\mapsto t^{\prime}
are regular on the appropriate subvarieties of

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\ }}{{\Longrightarrow}}gU_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{i}\ }}{{\longrightarrow}}gz_{i}(t)U_{+}
and
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\ }}{{\Longrightarrow}}gz_{i}(t)U_{+}

. If

Fix
\beta=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{m}\in(\pm I)^{m}

[m]:=\{1,2,\dots,m\}

[0,m]:=\{0,1,\dots,m\}

Recall the notation
s_{i}^{+}
and
s_{i}^{-}
from (

Given two elements

with

in the Bruhat order, we write
\max(u,v):=v
and
\min(u,v):=u
. We also define

The

of

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:dem}\pi(\beta):=s_{i_{m}}^{-}*s_{i_{m-1}}^{-}*\cdots*s_{i_{1}}^{-}*s_{i_{1}}^{+}*s_{i_{2}}^{+}*\cdots*s_{i_{m}}^{+}\quad\in W.

From now on, we assume that

Definition 2.4
.

A
w_{\circ}
-subexpression
of
\beta
is a sequence
{\mathbf{u}}=(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\in W^{m+1}
such that
u_{0}={\operatorname{id}}
,
u_{m}=w_{\circ}
, and such that for each

, we have either
u_{c-1}=u_{c}
or
u_{c-1}=s_{i_{c}}^{-}u_{c}s_{i_{c}}^{+}
. Since
\pi(\beta)=w_{\circ}

. It is given by
u_{m}:=w_{\circ}
and

u_{c-1}:=\min(u_{c},s_{i_{c}}^{-}u_{c}s_{i_{c}}^{+})

for all
c=m,m-1,\dots,1
.

We also define
w_{c}:=w_{\circ}u_{c}
for
c\in[0,m]
and
{\mathbf{w}}=w_{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}:=(w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{m})

.
We set
J_{\beta}:=\{c\in[m]\mid u_{c}=u_{c-1}\}
. We refer to the indices in

.

The

3.
Cluster algebras

Cluster algebras were discovered by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
FZ02
]
. We consider skew-symmetrizable cluster algebras of geometric type, relying on formalism similar to
[
FG09
]
.

3.1.
Background

Definition 3.1
.

A rank
n
and dimension
n+m

(abstract) seed
is a quadruple
\Sigma=(T,{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{d},\omega)
, where

(1)

T
is a complex algebraic torus of dimension
{n+m}
,

(2)

{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{{n+m}})
is an ordered basis of
X^{\ast}(T)
, where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
(resp.,
x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{{n+m}}
) are mutable (resp., frozen) variables,

(3)

\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{{n+m}})
is a collection of positive integers,

(4)

\omega
is a 2-form on
T
of the form

(3.1)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:om_{d}fn_{c}luster}\omega=\sum_{i\leq j}d_{j}{\tilde{B}}_{ij}{\rm dlog}x_{i}\wedge{\rm dlog}x_{j}=\sum_{i\leq j}d_{i}{\tilde{B}}_{ji}{\rm dlog}x_{j}\wedge{\rm dlog}x_{i},

where
{\tilde{B}}_{ii}=0
for
i\in[n+m]
and
{\tilde{B}}_{ij}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
i,j\in[{n+m}]
.

We say that
\Sigma
is
integral
if
{\tilde{B}}_{ij}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
i\in[{n+m}]
and
j\in[n]
.

The matrix

{\tilde{B}}=({\tilde{B}}_{ij})_{(i,j)\in[{n+m}]\times[n]}

is the usual
({n+m})\times n

extended exchange matrix
in the theory of cluster algebras.
We have
d_{j}{\tilde{B}}_{ij}=-d_{i}{\tilde{B}}_{ji}
for
i,j\in[{n+m}]
; in particular, the top
n\times n

principal part

B
of
{\tilde{B}}
is skew-symmetrizable.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma=(T,{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{d},\omega)
be a seed and
k
a mutable index. We say that
\Sigma
is
integral at
k
if
{\tilde{B}}_{jk}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
j\in[{n+m}]
. In this case, we define

(3.2)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:mut_{d}fn}x_{k}^{\prime}:=\frac{\prod_{{\tilde{B}}_{jk}>0}x_{j}^{{\tilde{B}}_{jk}}+\prod_{{\tilde{B}}_{jk}<0}x_{j}^{-{\tilde{B}}_{jk}}}{x_{k}}.

The
mutation
of
\Sigma
in the direction
k
is the seed
\mu_{k}(\Sigma)=(T^{\prime},{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime},\mathbf{d},\omega^{\prime})
where
T^{\prime}
is the algebraic torus with basis of characters
{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{k},\ldots,x_{{n+m}})
and the 2-form
\omega^{\prime}
on
T^{\prime}
is the pullback of
\omega
via the natural rational map
T^{\prime}\to T
; see
[
FG09
]
.

For the rest of this subsection, we assume that all seeds are integral. Following
[
LS16
, Section 5.1]
, a seed
\Sigma
is
really full rank
if the columns of
{\tilde{B}}
span
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. We will prove this for the seeds
\Sigma_{\beta}
from
Section

2
in
Corollary

6.7
.

Let
X
be an irreducible complex algebraic variety of dimension
{n+m}
. A
seed on
X
is an abstract seed
\Sigma=(T,{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{d},\omega)
together with an identification
T\subset X
of
T
with an open dense subset of
X
. The inclusion
T\hookrightarrow X
induces an identification of the field
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
of rational functions on
X
with the field of rational functions
{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbf{x}}):={\mathbb{C}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+m})
in the initial cluster variables
{\mathbf{x}}
. In practice, we abuse notation and write
{\mathbf{x}}
for a tuple of elements in
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
.

The
cluster algebra

{\mathcal{A}}(\Sigma)
is the subring of
{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbf{x}})
generated by all cluster variables together with inverses of frozen variables. We let
\mathcal{X}(\Sigma):=\operatorname{Spec}({\mathcal{A}}(\Sigma))
denote the
cluster variety
. We say that
(X,\Sigma)
is a cluster variety
if
X
is an affine variety and the coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathcal{X}]
is identified with
{\mathcal{A}}(\Sigma)
under the identification
{\mathbb{C}}(X)\cong{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbf{x}})
.

We will need the following property of cluster variables.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
GLS13
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Each cluster variable is an irreducible element of
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be an abstract seed of rank
n
and dimension
{n+m}
, and let
I\subset[n]
. The
freezing of
\Sigma
at
I
, denoted
\Sigma^{\backslash I}
, is the seed obtained from
\Sigma
by declaring the variables
\{x_{c}\}_{c\in I}
to be frozen.
It is a seed of rank
n-|I|
and dimension
{n+m}
. For
k\in[n]
, we denote
\Sigma^{\backslash k}:=\Sigma^{\backslash\{k\}}
.

To a seed
\Sigma
we associate a directed graph
\tilde{\Gamma}(\Sigma)
with vertex set
[n+m]
and an arrow
i\to j
whenever
{\tilde{B}}_{ij}>0
. We let
\Gamma:=\Gamma(\Sigma)
be the
mutable part
of
\tilde{\Gamma}(\Sigma)
, i.e., the induced subgraph of
\tilde{\Gamma}(\Sigma)
with vertex set
[n]
. We say that a mutable index
s\in[n]
is a

if it has no outgoing arrows in
\Gamma
. Let
N^{\operatorname{in}}_{s}(\Gamma)
denote the set of vertices of
\Gamma
having an arrow to
s
, and denote
\widehat{N}^{\operatorname{in}}_{s}(\Gamma):=N^{\operatorname{in}}_{s}(\Gamma)\cup\{s\}
. The following definition is a variation of locally-acyclic seeds
[
Mul13
]
and Louise seeds
[
LS16
]
; see also
[
GL22
, Section 5.4 and Remark 5.14]
.

Definition 3.5
.

The class of
sink-recurrent seeds
is defined recursively as follows.

—

Any seed
\Sigma
such that
\Gamma(\Sigma)
has no arrows is sink-recurrent.

—

Any seed that is mutation equivalent to a sink-recurrent seed is sink-recurrent.

—

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a seed with a sink
s\in[n]
such that the seeds
\Sigma^{\backslash s}
and
\Sigma^{\backslash\widehat{N}^{\operatorname{in}}_{s}(\Gamma(\Sigma))}

is sink-recurrent.

The

[
BFZ05
]

\mathcal{U}(\Sigma)\subset{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbf{x}}^{\pm 1}]
is the intersection
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbf{x}}^{\pm 1}]\cap\bigcap_{k\in[n]}{\mathbb{C}}[\mu_{k}({\mathbf{x}})^{\pm 1}]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a sink-recurrent seed. Then
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{U}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

It follows from induction and
[
Mul13
, Lemma 5.3]
that sink-recurrent seeds are locally acyclic in the sense of
[
Mul13
,
Mul14
]
. By
[
Mul14
, Theorem 2]
, we have
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{U}(\Sigma)
.
∎

3.2.
Quasi-equivalence

Definition 3.7
.

Two seeds
\Sigma=(T,{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{d},\omega)
and
\tilde{\Sigma}=(\tilde{T},\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{d}},\tilde{\omega})
of rank
n
and dimension
{n+m}
are

, denoted
\Sigma\sim\tilde{\Sigma}
, if the following conditions are satisfied:

—

T=\tilde{T}
,
\mathbf{d}=\tilde{\mathbf{d}}
,
\omega=\tilde{\omega}
;

—

the sublattice of

spanned by the frozen variables

coincides with the sublattice spanned by
\tilde{x}_{n+1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{{n+m}}
;

—

for each
k\in[n]
, we have
\tilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}M_{k}
, where
M_{k}
is a Laurent monomial in
x_{n+1},\dots,x_{{n+m}}
.

It is easy to see that if
\Sigma
is integral and
\Sigma\sim\tilde{\Sigma}
then
\tilde{\Sigma}
is integral.
The following is also straightforward to check.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\Sigma
and
\tilde{\Sigma}
are quasi-equivalent seeds then
\mu_{k}(\Sigma)\sim\mu_{k}(\tilde{\Sigma})
for all mutable
k
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose two seeds
\Sigma,\tilde{\Sigma}
are quasi-equivalent.
Then they define the same cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(\Sigma)={\mathcal{A}}(\Sigma^{\prime})\subset{\mathbb{C}}(T)
.

Proof.

It follows from
Lemma

3.8
that each cluster variable in
{\mathcal{A}}(\Sigma)
differs from the corresponding cluster variable in
{\mathcal{A}}(\tilde{\Sigma})
by a factor equal to a Laurent monomial in the frozen variables.
∎

Assumption 3.10

Theorem 3.12

(Deletion-contraction recurrence)
.

Let
X
be an affine, normal, irreducible, complex algebraic variety, and let
\Sigma=(T,{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{d},\omega)
be a seed on
X
with a sink
s
satisfying

. Assume that all cluster variables in

are regular on
X
. Define subvarieties
W:=\{x_{s}\neq 0\}
and
V:=\{x_{s}=0\}

. Suppose we have isomorphisms

and
V\cong\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[x^{\prime}_{s}])\times\mathcal{X}(\Sigma^{/\penalty s})\cong{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathcal{X}(\Sigma^{/\penalty s})

\Sigma^{\backslash s}
, resp.
\Sigma^{/\penalty s}

\Sigma
under the inclusion
\iota_{W}:W\hookrightarrow X
, resp.
\iota_{V}:V\hookrightarrow X

Proof.

First,
\Sigma^{\backslash s}
is integral by assumption. Since
\Sigma
is integral at

, this implies

\Sigma
is also integral.

Let

be a mutable index. Clearly, the (pullback under
\iota_{W}
of the) exchange relation for

in
\Sigma
coincides with the exchange relation for
x_{j}
in
\Sigma^{\backslash s}
. Thus, the mutated variable

is regular on

. Next, assume that

. By assumption,

is not connected to

in
\Gamma
, and thus the terms involving
{\rm dlog}x_{j}
are unchanged when passing from
\omega
to
\omega^{/\penalty s}

under
\iota_{V}
is still the exchange relation for
x_{j}
in
\Sigma^{/\penalty s}
, and therefore the mutated variable
x_{j}^{\prime}
is regular on

must also be regular on

is a frozen variable. It follows that for all

is a regular function on

since it is regular on both

and

is regular on
X
by assumption.

Next, we show that

\Sigma

. For the tori

(cf.

4.
Double braid moves

In this section, we study natural isomorphisms between braid varieties corresponding to
double braid moves
, and determine the effect of these isomorphisms on seeds. Double braid moves are defined as follows:

(B1)

ij\leftrightarrow ji
if
i,j\in\pm I
have different signs;

(B2)

ij\leftrightarrow ji
if
i,j\in\pm I
have the same sign and
(s_{|i|}s_{|j|})^{2}=1

(B3)

\underbrace{iji\dots}_{\text{$m_{ij}$ letters}}\leftrightarrow\underbrace{jij\dots}_{\text{$m_{ij}$ letters}}

(B5)

and
\beta_{0}\in(\pm I)^{m-1}

\beta

(B5)

The exchange matrices for our seeds
\Sigma_{\beta}
are defined via Deodhar geometry. Let

and

. We give an algorithm to compute the order of vanishing of
\Delta_{c}
on
V_{e}
, which determines our cluster algebras.

The function

of
Section

2.4
is an
H
-valued character on
T_{\beta}
. We may thus write
h^{\pm}_{c}=\prod_{e\in J_{\beta}}\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta,c,e}(x_{e})
, where
\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta,c,e}
are cocharacters of
H
satisfying
\gamma^{-}_{\beta,c,e}=u_{c}\cdot\gamma^{+}_{\beta,c,e}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

(1)

Suppose
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by removing the first
c-1
letters. Then
\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta,c,e}=\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta^{\prime},1,e-c+1}
.

(2)

Suppose
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by doing non-mutation moves
(B1)
–
(B4)
involving indices greater than
c
, and let
e^{\prime}
be the image of
e
under the resulting identification of cluster seeds. Then we have
\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta,c,e}=\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta^{\prime},c,e^{\prime}}
.

(3)

Suppose
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by removing solid crossings greater than
e
. Then
\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta,c,e}=\gamma^{\pm}_{\beta^{\prime},c,e}
.

Proof.

Part (
1
) follows from
Lemma

2.25
, and part (
2
) is immediate from the definitions.
We prove (
3
). Suppose
\beta
has a solid crossing
{e^{\prime}}>e
. Using (
1
) and (
2
), we may assume that
e\in J_{\beta}^{{\rm mut}}
, that
{e^{\prime}}
is the largest solid crossing, and that
i_{e^{\prime}}=i_{{e^{\prime}}+1}\in I
. Let
\beta^{\prime\prime}
be obtained from
\beta
by removing the letter
i_{e^{\prime}}
from
\beta
. Let
W\subset\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
be the open subset obtained by removing the Deodhar hypersurface
V_{e^{\prime}}
, if
{e^{\prime}}
is mutable; otherwise, let
W:=\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
. The projection
\pi:W\to\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}
given by forgetting the flags
(X_{e^{\prime}},Y_{e^{\prime}})
is a fiber bundle with fiber
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. We have
\pi^{*}(\Delta_{c}^{\beta^{\prime\prime}})=\Delta_{c}^{\beta}
and
\pi
maps
V^{\beta}_{e}\cap W
surjectively onto
V^{\beta^{\prime\prime}}_{e}
. (Here we use the superscript to refer to the braid variety on which
\Delta_{c}
and
V_{e}
are defined.) Since both
V^{\beta}_{e}\subset\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta}
and
V^{\beta^{\prime\prime}}_{e}\subset\accentset{\circ}{R}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}
are hypersurfaces, it follows that the order of vanishing of
\Delta_{c}^{\beta}
on
V^{\beta}_{e}
is equal to that of
\Delta_{c}^{\beta^{\prime\prime}}
on
V^{\beta^{\prime\prime}}_{e}
. Repeating this argument, we obtain (
3
).

.

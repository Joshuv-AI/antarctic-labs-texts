Curvature of minimal graphs
David Kalaj

Abstract




Abstract

We consider the Gaussian curvature conjecture of a minimal graph
S
over the unit disk. First of all we reduce the general conjecture to the estimating the Gaussian curvature of some Scherk’s type minimal surfaces over a quadrilateral inscribed in the unit disk containing the origin inside.
As an application, we obtain the best estimates of the Gaussian curvature so far at the point above the center of the unit disk. Further we obtain an optimal estimate of the Gaussian curvature at the point
\mathbf{w}
over the center of the disk, provided
\mathbf{w}
satisfies certain ”symmetric” conditions. The result extends a classical result of Finn and Osserman in 1964. In order to do so, we construct a certain family
S^{t}
,
t\in[t_{\circ},\pi/2]
of Scherk’s type minimal graphs over the isosceles trapezoids inscribed in the unit disk. Then we compare the Gaussian curvature of the graph
S
with that of
S^{t}
at the point
\mathbf{w}
over the center of the disk.

Keywords
conformal minimal surface, minimal graph, curvature

53A10, 32B15, 32E30, 32H02

1.  Introduction

Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
be a minimal graph lying over the unit disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
.
Let
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}):\mathbb{D}\to M
be a conformal harmonic parameterization of
M
with
w(0)=0
.
Its projection
(w_{1},w_{2}):\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a harmonic diffeomorphism of the disc which may be assumed
to preserve the orientation. Let
z
be the complex variable in
\mathbb{D}
, and write

w_{1}+\mathfrak{i}w_{2}=f
in the complex notation.
We denote by
f_{z}=\partial f/\partial z
and
f_{\bar{z}}=\partial f/\partial\bar{z}
the Wirtinger derivatives of
f
.
The function
\omega
defined by

(1.1)

\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}=\omega f_{z}

is called the
second Beltrami coefficient
of
f
, and the above is the

second Beltrami coefficient
with
f
as a solution. Observe that
\bar{f}_{z}=\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}
and this notation will be used in the sequel.

Orientability of
f
is equivalent to
\mathrm{Jac}(f)=|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}|^{2}>0
, hence to

|\omega|<1
on
\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, the function
\omega
is holomorphic whenever

f
is harmonic and orientation preserving. (In general, it is meromorphic when
f
is harmonic.)
To see this, let

(1.2)

u+\mathfrak{i}v=f=h+\overline{g}

be the canonical decomposition of the harmonic map
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
,
where
h
and
g
are holomorphic functions on the disc. Then,

(1.3)

f_{z}=h^{\prime},\quad\ f_{\bar{z}}=\overline{g}_{\bar{z}}=\overline{g^{\prime}},\quad\ \omega=\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}/f_{z}=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}.

In particular, the second Beltrami coefficient
\omega
equals the meromorphic function
g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}

on
\mathbb{D}
. In our case we have
|\omega|<1
, so it is holomorphic map
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
.

We now consider the Enneper–Weierstrass representation of the minimal graph

\varpi=(u,v,T):\mathbb{D}\to M\subset\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
over
f
, following Duren
[
2
, p. 183]
. We have

\displaystyle u(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re f(z)=\Re\int_{0}^{z}\phi_{1}(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle v(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Im f(z)=\Re\int_{0}^{z}\phi_{2}(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle T(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{0}^{z}\phi_{3}(\zeta)d\zeta

where

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(u)_{z}=2(\Re f)_{z}=(h+\bar{g}+\bar{h}+g)_{z}=h^{\prime}+g^{\prime},

\displaystyle\phi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(v)_{z}=2(\Im f)_{z}=\mathfrak{i}(\bar{h}+g-h-\bar{g})_{z}=\mathfrak{i}(g^{\prime}-h^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\phi_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(T)_{z}=\sqrt{-\phi_{1}^{2}-\phi_{2}^{2}}=\pm 2\mathfrak{i}\sqrt{h^{\prime}g^{\prime}}.

The last equation follows from the identity
\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2}+\phi_{3}^{2}=0
which
is satisfied by the Enneper–Weierstrass datum
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})=2\partial w

of any conformal minimal (equivalently, conformal harmonic) immersion
w:D\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

from a conformal surface
D
. Let us introduce the notation
p=f_{z}
. We have that

(1.4)

p=f_{z}=(\Re f)_{z}+\mathfrak{i}(\Im f)_{z}=\frac{1}{2}(h^{\prime}+g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}-g^{\prime})=h^{\prime}.

By using also
\omega=\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}/f_{z}=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
(see (
1.3
)), it follows that

\phi_{1}=h^{\prime}+g^{\prime}=p(1+\omega),\quad\phi_{2}=-\mathfrak{i}(h^{\prime}-g^{\prime})=-\mathfrak{i}p(1-\omega),\quad\phi_{3}=\pm 2\mathfrak{i}p\sqrt{\omega}.

From the formula for
\phi_{3}
we infer that
\omega
has a well-defined holomorphic square root:

(1.5)

\omega=q^{2},\qquad q:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}\ \ \text{holomorphic}.

In terms of the Enepper–Weierstrass parameters
(p,q)
given by (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) we obtain

(1.6)

\phi_{1}=p(1+q^{2}),\quad\phi_{2}=-\mathfrak{i}p(1-q^{2}),\quad\phi_{3}=-2\mathfrak{i}pq.

(The choice of sign in
\phi_{3}
is a matter of convenience; since we have two choices of sign for

q
in (
1.5
), this does not cause any loss of generality.) Hence,

\varpi(z)=\left(\Re f(z),\Im f(z),\Im\int_{0}^{z}2p(\varsigma)q(\varsigma)dt\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The curvature
\mathcal{K}
of the minimal graph
M
is expressed in terms of
(h,g,\omega)
(
1.3
), and in terms
of the Enneper–Weierstrass parameters
(p,q)
, by

(1.7)

\mathcal{K}=-\frac{|\omega^{\prime}|^{2}}{|h^{\prime}g^{\prime}|(1+|\omega|)^{4}}=-\frac{4|q^{\prime}|^{2}}{|p|^{2}(1+|q|^{2})^{4}},

where
p=f_{z}
and
\omega=q^{2}=\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}/f_{z}
. (See Duren
[
2
, p. 184]
.)

1.1.  Non-parametric minimal surface equation

Assume that
S=\{(u,v,\mathbf{f}(u,v)):(u,v)\in\mathbb{D}\}
is a minimal surface, where
\mathbb{D}
is the unit disk. Then we call such a surface minimal surface above the unit disk. The minimal surface equation is

f_{uu}(1+f_{v}^{2})-2f_{u}f_{v}f_{uv}+f_{vv}(1+f_{u}^{2})=0.

2.  The Heinz-Hopf-Finn-Osserman problem

We are interested in the following problem.

Problem 2.1
.

What is the supremum of
|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|
over all minimal graphs lying over
\mathbb{D}
? Is

(2.1)

|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|<\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}

the precise upper bound? Here
\mathbf{w}
is the point above the center of the unit disk and we call it
centre
.

The previous conjecture has been also formulated by Duren in his monograph
[
2
, Conjecture 2. p. 185]
.

The first result on this topic has been given by E. Heinz on 1952 in
[
6
]
who introduced the constant
c_{0}
which is the best constant in the inequality
|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|\leq c_{0}
, for all minimal graphs over the unit disk with the centre
\mathbf{w}
. Further this result has been improved by E. Hopf in 1953 in
[
8
]
, who introduced the constant
c_{1}
which is the best constant in the inequality

W^{2}|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|\leq c_{1},

where
W=\sqrt{1+\mathbf{f}_{u}^{2}+\mathbf{f}_{v}^{2}}
. So a similar problem to be consider is the following

Problem 2.2
.

What is the supremum of
W^{2}|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|
over all minimal graphs lying over
\mathbb{D}
? Is

(2.2)

W^{2}|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|<\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}

the precise upper bound? Here
\mathbf{w}
is the
centre
of minimal surface.

It was shown by Finn and Osserman
[
3
]
in 1964 that the
upper bound in (
2.1
) is indeed sharp if
q(0)=0
, which means that
the tangent plane
T_{0}M=\mathbb{C}\times\{0\}
being horizontal (and hence
f
is conformal at
0
).
Although there is no minimal graph lying over the whole unit disc
\mathbb{D}
whose centre curvature equals

\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}
, there is a sequence of minimal graphs whose centre curvatures converge to
\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}
,
and the graphs converge to the Scherk’s surface lying over square inscribed into the unit disc.
The associated Beltrami coefficient of the Scherk’s surface is
\omega(z)=z^{2}
, with
q(z)=z
.
We refer to Duren
[
2
, p. 185]
for a survey of this subject. We also refer to the monograph by J. C. C. Nitsche
[
10
]
for earlier results.

Let us recall a path to obtain a weaker upper bound on
|\mathcal{K}|
which holds for every value

|q(0)|<1
. This is explained in
[
2
, pp. 184–185]
.

Hall proved in
[
4
]
(1982) the following estimate

(2.3)

|f_{z}(0)|^{2}+|f_{\bar{z}}(0)|^{2}\geq\frac{27}{4\pi^{2}}

for any harmonic diffeomorphism
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
.
This estimate is sharp in general, but is not sharp if the second Beltrami coefficient

\omega
is the square of a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}
.
Applying Hall’s estimate and noting that

|f_{z}(0)|^{2}+|f_{\bar{z}}(0)|^{2}=|f_{z}(0)|^{2}(1+|q(0)|^{4})

gives

|f_{z}(0)|^{2}\geq\frac{27}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+|q(0)|^{4})}.

By using also the Pick-Schwarz inequality
|q^{\prime}(0)|<1-|q(0)|^{2}
, we obtain

(2.4)

|\mathcal{K}|=\frac{4|q^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{|f_{z}(0)|^{2}(1+|q(0)|^{2})^{4}}\leq\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}\frac{\bigl(1-|q(0)|^{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigl(1+|q(0)|^{4}\bigr)}{(1+|q(0)|^{2})^{4}}.

So we have the following inequality

(2.5)

|\mathcal{K}|\leq\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}\approx 5.84865.

The above constant is better than the constant
5.98
obtained by Finn and Osserman in
[
3
]
.

Further if the minimal surface has its non-parametric parameterization
z=\mathbf{f}(u,v)
, and denoting

W=\sqrt{1+\mathbf{f}_{u}^{2}+\mathbf{f}_{v}^{2}},

then
(
2.4
), in view of (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) below implies that

(2.6)

|\mathcal{K}|\cdot W^{2}\leq\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}\frac{\bigl(1+|q(0)|^{4}\bigr)}{(1+|q(0)|^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}.

It follows from (
2.4
), that the Heinz constant
c_{0}<\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}
, while (
2.6
) implies that the Hopf constant
c_{1}<\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}
.

We will give better estimate of both constants in Corollary
3.10
.

The estimate (
2.5
) is not sharp as it has been proved by R. Hall in
[
5
]
by obtaining a very small improvement of about
10^{-5}
.
As said before, the sharp estimate (
2.1
) in the case
q(0)=0
was given by
Finn and Osserman
[
3
]
(see also
[
10
]
).

3.  The main results

We first formulate the following general result

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
w\in\mathbb{D}
, there exist four different points
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\mathbf{T}
and then there is a harmonic mapping
f
of the unit disk onto the quadrilateral
Q(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
that solves the Beltrami equation

(3.1)

\bar{f}_{z}(z)=\left(\frac{w+\frac{\mathfrak{i}\left(1-w^{4}\right)z}{\left|1-w^{4}\right|}}{1+\frac{\mathfrak{i}\overline{w}\left(1-w^{4}\right)z}{\left|1-w^{4}\right|}}\right)^{2}f_{z}(z),

|z|<1
and satisfies the initial condition
f(0)=0
,
f_{z}(0)>0
. It also defines a Scherk’s type minimal surface
S^{\diamond}:\zeta=\mathbf{f}^{\diamond}(u,v)
over the quadrilateral
Q(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
, with the centre
\mathbf{w}=(0,0,0)
so that its Gaussian normal is

\mathbf{n}^{\diamond}_{\mathbf{w}}=-\frac{1}{1+|w|^{2}}(2\Im w,2\Re w,-1+|w|^{2}),

and
D_{uv}\mathbf{f}^{\diamond}(0,0)=0
. Moreover, every other non-parametric minimal surface
S:

z=\mathbf{f}(u,v)
over the unit disk, with a centre
\mathbf{w}
, with
\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{w}}=\mathbf{n}^{\diamond}_{\mathbf{w}}
and
D_{uv}\mathbf{f}(0,0)=0
satisfies the sharp inequality

|\mathcal{K}_{S}(\mathbf{w})|<|\mathcal{K}_{S^{\diamond}}(\mathbf{w})|,

or what is the same

W^{2}_{S}|\mathcal{K}_{S}(\mathbf{w})|<W^{2}_{S^{\diamond}}|\mathcal{K}_{S^{\diamond}}(\mathbf{w})|.

Further we have

(3.2)

\mathcal{K}_{S^{\diamond}}(\mathbf{w})=-\frac{4\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{4}|f_{z}(0)|^{2}}.

Remark 3.2
.

It follows from the result of Jenkins and Serrin that such a minimal surface described in Theorem
3.1
is unique
[
9
]
, so
Q=Q(w)
depends only on
w
and also
f=f^{w}
depends only on
w
. It also follows from Theorem
3.1
(i.e. from (
3.2
)) that the Heinz and the Hopf constants can be defined as

(3.3)

c_{0}=\sup_{w}\frac{4\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{4}|f^{w}_{z}(0)|^{2}}

(3.4)

c_{1}=\sup_{w}\frac{4}{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{2}|f^{w}_{z}(0)|^{2}}.

In particular, when
w
from Theorem
3.1
is an imaginary number (in view of Remark
4.3
), then we precisely describe the quadrilaterals, which appear to be isosceles trapezoids (Section
4.1
, Proposition
4.1
). In this case we give the precise bound of the curvature.

Further, if we consider the mapping

\tilde{f}(z)=f\left(\frac{\mathfrak{i}\left|1-w^{4}\right|}{1-w^{4}}\frac{(w-z)}{(1-z\overline{w})}\right),

then
\tilde{f}
satisfies the Beltrami equation
\overline{\tilde{f}}_{z}=z^{2}\tilde{f}_{z}
with the initial conditions
\tilde{f}(w)=0
and
\mathfrak{i}(1-w^{4})\tilde{f}_{z}(w)>0
.

In order to formulate our next results, which are extensions of the Finn-Osserman results, we give the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

We call
\zeta\in D
a symmetric point of a double differentiable real function
\mathbf{f}:D\to\mathbf{R}
if there is some vector
h\in\mathbf{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}
so that the equalities hold

(3.5)

\nabla^{2}_{h,\mathfrak{i}h}\mathbf{f}(\zeta)=\nabla_{h}\mathbf{f}(\zeta)=0.

We call also that point
\zeta
,
h-
symmetric. A point
\mathbf{w}=(\zeta,\mathbf{f}(\zeta))
on the graph of a function
\mathbf{f}
is symmetric if
\zeta
is symmetric for
\mathbf{f}
.

Remark 3.4
.

The motivation for this definition comes from the following observation. Assume that
\mathbf{f}
is a symmetric real function w.r.t. imaginary axis, i.e. assume that
\mathbf{f}(-u,v)=\mathbf{f}(u,v)
. Then
D_{u}\mathbf{f}(-u,v)=-D_{u}\mathbf{f}(u,v)
. So
D_{u}\mathbf{f}(0,v)=0
. Further
D_{uv}\mathbf{f}(0,v)=0
for every
v
. This implies that
\nabla^{2}_{e_{1},e_{2}}\mathbf{f}(0,0)=0
. By using the translation and rotation of the coordinate system, we get a similar fact for functions that are symmetric at some point w.r.t to an arbitrary line, or more general w.r.t. a small segment.

An example of a symmetric point is any stationary point of the function.

Example 3.5
.

Prove that if
\nabla\mathbf{f}(0,0)=0
, then
z=(0,0)
is a symmetric point of
\mathbf{f}
. Namely if
h=e^{ic}
and
\mathbf{f}^{c}(z)=\mathbf{f}(e^{ic}z)
, then

\mathbf{f}^{c}_{u}(0,0)=\cos c\,\mathbf{f}_{u}(0,0)+\sin c\,\mathbf{f}_{v}(0,0)=\nabla_{h}\mathbf{f}(0,0).

Further

\mathbf{f}^{c}_{uv}(0,0)=\cos(2c)\mathbf{f}_{uv}(0,0)+\cos(c)\sin(c)\left(-\mathbf{f}_{vv}(0,0)+\mathbf{f}_{uu}(0,0)\right)=\nabla^{2}_{h,\mathfrak{i}h}\mathbf{f}(0,0).

Since
\mathbf{f}^{\pi/2}_{uv}(0,0)=-\mathbf{f}_{uv}(0,0)
, there is
c
so that
\mathbf{f}^{c}_{uv}(0,0)=0
.

In the sequel we give two additional examples of symmetric points of classical minimal surfaces and one counterexample.

Example 3.6
.

a) Assume that
w=\cosh^{-1}\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}
,
|w|=\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}>1
. Then this function defines the catenoid. Moreover

w_{u}=\frac{u}{\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}\sqrt{-1+\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}}\sqrt{1+\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}}}

and

w_{uv}=\frac{uv\left(1-2u^{2}-2v^{2}\right)}{\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)^{3/2}\left(-1+\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\left(1+\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}\right)^{3/2}}.

So every point
(u,0)
and
(0,v)
is a symmetric point of this surface. Since it is rotation invariant, it follows that every point of this surface is symmetric.

b) Assume that
w=\log\frac{\cos v}{\cos u}
. Then
w_{u}=\tan u
and
w_{uv}=0
. So every point
w=\mathfrak{i}v=(0,v)
is a symmetric point of Scherk’s saddle surface.

c) Assume that
w=\tan^{-1}\frac{v}{u}
, where
u\neq 0
. Then this function defines the helicoid. Then
w_{u}=\frac{v}{u^{2}+v^{2}}
and
w_{uv}=\frac{(u-v)(u+v)}{\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)^{2}}
. It follows that this surface has not any symmetric point.

We give a partial solution of Problem
2.1
and extend Finn-Osserman result by proving the following theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume that
S
is a non-parametric minimal surface above the unit disk and assume that the point
\mathbf{w}
over the center of the disk is symmetric. Then the Gaussian curvature
\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})
satisfies the sharp inequalities

(3.6)

|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|<\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}.

and

(3.7)

W^{2}|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|<\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}.

Remark 3.8
.

After we wrote this paper we realized that the statement of Theorem
3.7
is not new for symmetric minimal surfaces. An approach different from our approach has been given by Nitsche in
[
11
]
.

Further we prove the following theorem

Theorem 3.9
.

There is a decreasing diffeomorphism
\Psi:[0,\pi/2]\to[0,\pi^{2}/2]
with the following property.
Assume that
S
is a non-parametric minimal surface above the unit disk with a
h-
symmetric point
\mathbf{w}
above
0
. Assume that
\theta
is the angle of the tangent plane
TS_{\mathbf{w}}
at
\mathbf{w}
with
h
. Then the Gaussian curvature
|\mathcal{K}|
at
\mathbf{w}
satisfies the sharp inequality

(3.8)

|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|<\Psi(\theta)(<\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}),

and for every
0\leq\phi<\Psi(\theta)
there is a non-parametric minimal surface
S_{\phi}
above the unit disk, whose point above the center of the unit disk is
h-
simmetric and whose tangent plane at
\mathbf{w}
makes the angle
\theta
with
h
so that

\mathcal{K}_{S_{\phi}}(\mathbf{w})=\phi.

As a corollary of our results, we obtain the following improvement of the Hall upper bound of Gaussian curvature (i.e. of Heinz and Hopf constants) without any condition on the centre.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
S:z=\mathbf{f}(u,v)
be a minimal surface over the unit disk and assume that
\mathbf{w}
is its centre. Then the Gaussian curvature

\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})<5.7.

Moreover if
W=\sqrt{1+\mathbf{f}_{u}^{2}+\mathbf{f}_{v}^{2}}
, then

\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})<\frac{5.8}{W^{2}}.

4.  Proof of main results

This section contains the proof of our results. At the begging we describe the family of Scherk’s type minimal surfaces over isosceles trapezoids inscribed in the unit disk. On account of Theorem
3.1
we know that a similar family depending on two parameters exists and such a family would solve the general conjecture, provided it can be explicitly expressed.

4.1.  Scherk’s type minimal surfaces with 4 sides and auxiliary results

We are going to find a harmonic mapping of the unit disk onto a quadrilateral
inscribed in the unit disk that produces a minimal surface. Let
a_{1}=1
,
a_{2}=e^{\mathfrak{i}t}
,
a_{3}=e^{\mathfrak{i}s}
,
a_{4}=e^{\mathfrak{i}(t+s)}
, where
s=\arccos\frac{3\cos t-1}{1+\cos t}.

Let

F(\sigma)=\begin{array}[]{ll}\Bigg\{&\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\sigma\in[0,\pi/2]\\
e^{\mathfrak{i}t}&\sigma\in[\pi/2,\pi]\\
e^{\mathfrak{i}s}&\sigma\in[\pi,3\pi/2]\\
e^{\mathfrak{i}(t+s)}&\sigma\in[3\pi/2,2\pi].\end{array}\end{array}

Let

f_{1}(z)=P[F](z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1-r^{2}}{1+r^{2}-2r\cos(\varsigma-\sigma)}F(\sigma)d\sigma,z=re^{\mathfrak{i}\varsigma}.

Then
f_{1}
maps the unit disk onto the trapezoid
\mathcal{T}
with the vertices
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}
,
a_{k}=a_{1}
. Moreover

f_{1}(0)=\frac{1}{4}\left(1+e^{\mathfrak{i}t}\right)\left(1+e^{\mathfrak{i}\cos^{-1}\left[\frac{-1+3\cos t}{1+\cos t}\right]}\right).

Further, by
[
2
, p. 63]
,

f_{1}(z)=g(z)+\overline{h(z)},

where

g^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathfrak{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{(a_{k}-a_{k+1})}{z-\mathfrak{i}^{k}}

and

h^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathfrak{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{(\overline{a_{k}-a_{k+1}})}{z-\mathfrak{i}^{k}}.

Thus

g^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{(1+\mathfrak{i})\left(\mathfrak{i}+e^{\mathfrak{i}t}\right)\left(-1+\cos t+2\mathfrak{i}\sqrt{\cos t}\sin\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]\right)\left(1+\frac{z\sqrt{\cos t}}{\cos\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]+\sin\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]}\right)^{2}}{\pi\left(z^{4}-1\right)(1+\cos t)},

and

h^{\prime}(z)=\frac{(1+\mathfrak{i})\left(1-\cos t+2\mathfrak{i}\sqrt{\cos t}\sin\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]\right)(e^{-\mathfrak{i}t}\left(\mathfrak{i}+e^{\mathfrak{i}t}\right))\left(z+\frac{\sqrt{\cos t}}{\cos\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]+\sin\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]}\right)^{2}}{\pi\left(z^{4}-1\right)(1+\cos t)}.

Thus we get

\omega_{1}=\frac{h^{\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}=e^{-\mathfrak{i}\left(t+s-\pi\right)}\frac{\left(z+\frac{\sqrt{\cos t}}{\cos\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]+\sin\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]}\right)^{2}}{\left(1+\frac{z\sqrt{\cos t}}{\cos\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]+\sin\left[\frac{t}{2}\right]}\right)^{2}},

where
s=\cos^{-1}\frac{3\cos t-1}{1+\cos t}
.
So
\omega_{1}=q_{1}^{2}
, where

Proof of Corollary
3.10
.

Let
S:\zeta=\mathbf{f}(u,v)
be a non-parametric minimal surface over the unit disk and assume that

\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{w}}=-\frac{1}{1+|w|^{2}}(2\Im w,2\Re w,-1+|w|^{2}),

is its Gaussian normal at the
centre
. Let
f=f_{w}
be the solution that is provided to us by Theorem
3.1
that produces the Scherk type minimal surface
S^{\diamond}
. Let
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{S^{\diamond}}(\mathbf{w})
. In view of Theorem
3.1
, we only need to estimate the curvature
\mathcal{K}
. Now we have the estimate

(4.27)

\begin{split}|\mathcal{K}|&=\frac{4(1-|w|^{2})}{|f_{z}(0)|^{2}(1+|w|^{2})^{4}}\\
&\leq\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}\frac{\bigl(1-|w|^{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigl(1+|w|^{4}\bigr)}{(1+|w|^{2})^{4}}.\end{split}

Write
|w|=r
and consider the function

(4.28)

G(r)=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}\frac{\bigl(1-r^{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigl(1+r^{4}\bigr)}{(1+r^{2})^{4}},\quad 0\leq r\leq 1.

Note that (
4.27
) can be written in the form

(4.29)

|\mathcal{K}|\leq G(r).

Now the proof of Theorem
3.1
implies that,
f
is a limit of a sequence
f_{n}
satisfying Corollary
3.10
. In view of (
3.2
) and (
4.26
) and harmonic Schwarz lemma:
|f(w)|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\tan^{-1}(|w|)
, we get

\begin{split}|\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})|&=\frac{4\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{4}|f_{z}(0)|^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{4\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{4}|\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\frac{(1-|f(-w)|)}{1-|w|^{2}}|^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\frac{\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{4}}{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{4}(1-|f(-w)|)^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\frac{\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{4}}{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{4}(1-\frac{4}{\pi}\tan^{-1}(|w|))^{2}}:=H(|w|).\end{split}

From the previous relations and (
4.29
) we conclude that

\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})\leq\max_{r\in[0,1]}\min\{G(r),H(r)\}.

Let

be the solution of the equation

where

is defined in (

and
G

(0,1)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})<G(r_{\diamond})\approx 5.6918
.

Further, by using (
4.12
) and (
4.13

W^{2}=1+\mathbf{f}^{2}_{u}+\mathbf{f}^{2}_{v}=\frac{\left(1+|w|^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{2}},

where
w=|q(0)|
. Therefore we get that

\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w})W^{2}\leq\frac{16\pi^{2}\left(1+r_{\diamond}^{4}\right)}{27\left(1+r_{\diamond}^{2}\right)^{2}}\approx 5.79608.

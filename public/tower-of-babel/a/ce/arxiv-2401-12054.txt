Abstract
: In this paper, we study asymptotic symmetries and algebraically special exact solutions in the Newman-Penrose formalism. Removing the hypersurface orthogonal condition in the well studied Newman-Unti gauge, we obtain a generic asymptotic solution space which includes all possible origins of propagating degree of freedom. The asymptotic symmetry of the generalized system extends the Weyl-BMS symmetry by two independent local Lorentz transformations with non-trivial boundary charges, which reveals new boundary degrees of freedom. The generalized Newman-Unti gauge includes algebraically special condition in its most convenient form. Remarkably, the generic solutions satisfying the algebraically special condition truncate in the inverse power of radial expansions and the non-radial Newman-Penrose equations are explicitly solved at any order. Hence, we provide the most general algebraically special solution space and the derivation is self-contained in the Newman-Penrose formalism. The asymptotic symmetry with respect to the algebraically special condition is the standard Weyl-BMS symmetry and the symmetry parameters consist only the integration constant order. We present the Kerr solution and Taub-NUT solution in the generalized Newman-Unti gauge in a simple form.

5.2
Asymptotic symmetries and transformation law

The extra conditions
\Psi_{1}=\Psi_{0}=\sigma=0
yield that
\omega^{23}_{0}=0=\omega^{24}_{0}
. The asymptotic symmetries are in closed form as

\begin{split}&\xi^{u}=f(u,z,\bar{z}),\quad Y^{z}=Y(z),\quad Y^{\bar{z}}=\bar{Y}(\bar{z}),\quad\xi^{r}=-\partial_{u}fr,\\
&\omega^{12}=\partial_{u}f,\quad\omega^{13}=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\eth$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{n}\partial_{u}f,\quad\omega^{14}=-\eth_{n}\partial_{u}f,\quad\omega^{34}=\Lambda(u,z,\bar{z}).\end{split}

(79)

The symmetry algebra recovers precisely the Weyl-BMS algebra
[
17
]
. The asymptotic symmetries have the following transformation law on the solution space,

\displaystyle\delta M^{0}=f\partial_{u}M^{0}+Y^{A}\partial_{A}M^{0}+M^{0}\partial_{u}f+\Lambda M^{0}-\eth_{n}f,

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma=f\partial_{u}\Sigma+Y^{A}\partial_{A}\Sigma,

\displaystyle\delta\Psi_{2}^{0}=f\partial_{u}\Psi_{2}^{0}+Y^{A}\partial_{A}\Psi_{2}^{0}+3\Psi_{2}^{0}\partial_{u}f.

Note that the residual gauge transformation can not turn off
\Sigma
. Hence, the twist in this system must not be pure gauge from the point of view of both propagating and boundary degrees of freedom. It is amusing to notice that the inhomogeneous piece in the transformation of
M^{0}
is only supertranslation relevant as
-\eth_{n}f
which is in a similar form as the transformation law of
\sigma^{0}
in the twist-free case
[
24
,
17
]
. The difference of them is only from their different spin weight. The shear of the generator of the null infinity
\lambda^{0}
is completely fixed by
\tau^{0}
in (
64
). Hence, in the algebraically special case,
\tau^{0}
is effectively the news tensor of this system, which indicates the propagating degree of freedom. The NP variables
M^{0}
and
\tau^{0}
in the algebraically spacial case are the analogue of
\sigma^{0}
and
\lambda^{0}
of the twist-free case.

The next step will be the computation of the asymptotic charges. As we have shown that the solution space and asymptotic symmetry parameters are all in truncated forms. We expect that the charges are in closed form which indicates that the BMS symmetry becomes symplectic symmetry similar to three dimensional theories
[
40
]
, see also
[
41
]
. Surprisingly, there are propagating degree of freedom in the present case. This will be the first example of enhancement from asymptotic symmetry or near horizon symmetry to symplectic symmetry in the system with propagating degree of freedom. We will present the full charge analysis elsewhere.

6
Exact solutions with twist

It is very important for the applications of asymptotic symmetry and the associated charges to have exact solutions in the proposed system. In this section, we will present the exact forms of Kerr and Taub-NUT solution in the generalized NU system. Both solutions are given in a very simple forms in the generalized NU gauge.

6.1
Kerr

The Kerr line-element in the Kerr-Schild coordinate
(u,r,\theta,\phi)
is

ds^{2}=\frac{\vartriangle}{\varrho\bar{\varrho}}(du-a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi)^{2}-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varrho\bar{\varrho}}[adu-(r^{2}+a^{2})d\phi]^{2}\\
+2dr(du-a\sin^{2}\theta d\phi)-\varrho\bar{\varrho}(d\theta)^{2},

(80)

where we define

\vartriangle=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2},\quad\varrho=r+ia\cos\theta,\quad\bar{\varrho}=r-ia\cos\theta.

(81)

The inverse metric is given by

g^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\varrho\bar{\varrho}}\begin{pmatrix}-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta&r^{2}+a^{2}&0&-a\\
r^{2}+a^{2}&-\vartriangle&0&a\\
0&0&-1&0\\
-a&a&0&-1/\sin^{2}\theta\end{pmatrix}

(82)

We choose the tetrad system as

\begin{split}&l=\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\quad\quad n=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{Mr}{\varrho\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\varrho$\kern 0.0pt}}}})\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\\
&m=\frac{e^{i\phi}}{\sqrt{2}\bar{\varrho}}\left(ia\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial u}-ia\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+i\csc\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right).\end{split}

(83)

The non-vanishing spin coefficients and Weyl scalars are

\begin{split}&\rho=-\frac{1}{\varrho},\quad\alpha=\frac{e^{-i\phi}\cot\frac{\theta}{2}}{2\sqrt{2}\varrho},\quad\beta=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\alpha$\kern 0.0pt}}},\\
&\mu=-\frac{1}{2\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\varrho$\kern 0.0pt}}}}+\frac{Mr}{\bar{\varrho}\varrho^{2}},\quad\gamma=\frac{M}{2\varrho^{2}},\quad\nu=\frac{iaMe^{-i\phi}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}\varrho^{3}},\\
&\Psi_{2}=-\frac{M}{(\varrho)^{3}},\quad\Psi_{3}=-\frac{3iaMe^{-i\phi}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}\varrho^{4}},\quad\Psi_{4}=\frac{3a^{2}Me^{-2i\phi}\sin^{2}\theta}{\varrho^{5}}.\end{split}

(84)

In the complex stereographic coordinates
(z,\bar{z})
, where
\theta=2\text{arccot}\sqrt{z\bar{z}}
and
\phi=-\frac{i}{2}\ln\frac{z}{\bar{z}}
, the tetrad system becomes

\begin{split}&l=\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\quad n=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{Mr}{\varrho\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\varrho$\kern 0.0pt}}}})\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\\
&m=\frac{1}{\bar{\varrho}}\left(\frac{iaz}{P_{S}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}-\frac{iaz}{P_{S}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+P_{S}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}\right),\quad\quad P_{S}=\frac{1+z\bar{z}}{\sqrt{2}}.\end{split}

(85)

The non-vanishing spin coefficients and Weyl scalars are now

\begin{split}&\rho=-\frac{1}{\varrho},\quad\alpha=\frac{\bar{z}}{2\sqrt{2}\varrho},\quad\beta=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\alpha$\kern 0.0pt}}},\\
&\mu=-\frac{1}{2\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\varrho$\kern 0.0pt}}}}+\frac{Mr}{\bar{\varrho}\varrho^{2}},\quad\gamma=\frac{M}{2\varrho^{2}},\quad\nu=\frac{iaM\bar{z}}{P_{S}\varrho^{3}},\\
&\Psi_{2}=-\frac{M}{\varrho^{3}},\quad\Psi_{3}=-\frac{3iaM\bar{z}}{P_{S}\varrho^{4}},\quad\Psi_{4}=\frac{6a^{2}M\bar{z}^{2}}{P_{S}^{2}\varrho^{5}}.\end{split}

(86)

Taub-NUT

The Taub-NUT solution in the complex stereographic coordinates adapted to the NP conventions is

\text{d}s^{2}=f(r)\left(\text{d}t+2iN\frac{z\text{d}\bar{z}-\bar{z}\text{d}z}{1+z\bar{z}}\right)^{2}-\frac{\text{d}r^{2}}{f(r)}-(r^{2}+N^{2})\frac{4\text{d}z\text{d}\bar{z}}{(1+z\bar{z})^{2}},

Applications and future directions

There are a number of interesting applications and open questions that should be addressed in the future. We list some of them here.

•

The most direct one is to find new exact solutions of vacuum Einstein gravity from the three constraints of

and

•

It will be very meaningful to check if the investigation of the algebraically spacial solution can be extended to the case with a cosmological constant.

Another extension is to include electromagnetic fields or other types of matter fields.

It is interesting to see if one can proof some kind of uniqueness theorem from the truncated algebraically special solution space when further constraint is required, such as stationary or axial symmetric, see, e.g.,

•

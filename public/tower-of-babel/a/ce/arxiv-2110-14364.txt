Einstein Hypersurfaces of Warped Product Spaces
R. F. de Lima, F. Manfio and J. P. dos Santos

Abstract
. We consider Einstein hypersurfaces of warped products I × ω ℚ ϵ n , I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}, where I ⊂ ℝ I\subset\mathbb{R} is an open interval and ℚ ϵ n \mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n} is the simply connected space form of dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and constant sectional curvature ϵ ∈ { − 1 , 0 , 1 } . \epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}. We show that, for all c ∈ ℝ c\in\mathbb{R} (resp. c > 0 c>0 ), there exist rotational hypersurfaces of constant sectional curvature c c in I × ω ℍ n I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{H}^{n} and I × ω ℝ n I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{R}^{n} (resp. I × ω 𝕊 n I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{S}^{n} ), provided that ω \omega is nonconstant. We also show that the gradient T T of the height function of any Einstein hypersurface of I × ω ℚ ϵ n I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n} (if nonzero) is one of its principal directions. Then, we consider a particular type of Einstein hypersurface of I × ω ℚ ϵ n I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n} with non vanishing T T — which we call ideal — and prove that, for n > 3 , n>3, such a hypersurface Σ \varSigma has either precisely two or precisely three distinct principal curvatures everywhere. We show that, in the latter case, there exist such a Σ \varSigma for certain warping functions ω , \omega, whereas in the former case Σ \varSigma is necessarily of constant sectional curvature and rotational, regardless the warping function ω . \omega. We also characterize ideal Einstein hypersurfaces of I × ω ℚ ϵ n I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n} with no vanishing angle function as local graphs on families of isoparametric hypersurfaces of ℚ ϵ n . \mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider Einstein hypersurfaces
of warped products
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval, and

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
stands for the simply connected space form of dimension

n\geq 2
and constant sectional curvature
\epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}.

Recall that a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called
Einstein
if

{\rm Ric}_{M}=\varLambda g,

where
{\rm Ric}_{M}
is the Ricci tensor of
(M,g)
and

\varLambda
is a constant. More specifically, we call such an
M
a

\varLambda
-Einstein manifold.

Riemannian manifolds of constant sectional curvature (CSC, for short)
are the simplest examples of Einstein manifolds, which we call
trivial
.
It was shown by P. Ryan
[
14
]
that,
for
\epsilon\leq 0,
any Einstein hypersurface
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n+1}
is trivial, and also that there
exist nontrivial Einstein hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}.

Inspired by this result, the main question we address here is the following:

Under which conditions an Einstein hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is necessarily trivial
?

For
\omega
constant and
\epsilon\neq 0,
it was proved in
[
11
]
that any Einstein hypersurface of

\mathbb{R}\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is trivial.
On the other hand, this is not true for certain nonconstant warping functions
\omega.

Indeed, writing
\mathbb{S}^{2}(1/\sqrt{2})
for the
2
-sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of radius
1/\sqrt{2},

it is known that the product
\mathbb{S}^{2}(1/\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(1/\sqrt{2})

— endowed with its canonical Riemannian metric —
is an Einstein manifold of nonconstant sectional curvature which
is naturally embedded in
\mathbb{S}^{5}-\{p,-p\}=(0,\pi)\times_{\sin t}\mathbb{S}^{4},

where
p
is a suitable point of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{5}\subset\mathbb{R}^{6}.

It should be noticed that, in answering the above question, one may
conclude that certain manifolds cannot be isometrically immersed
in
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.

For instance, from the aforementioned result in
[
11
]
,
there is no isometric immersion
of the product

\mathbb{S}^{2}(1/\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(1/\sqrt{2})
into
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{4},

\epsilon\neq 0.

A major property of Einstein hypersurfaces of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

we establish here is that they have the gradient
T

of their height functions (when nonzero) as principal directions.
Hypersurfaces satisfying this condition, which we call the
T
-
property
, have been considered in
several works on constant curvature (mean and sectional)
hypersurfaces of product spaces
\mathbb{R}\times M
,
M
a Riemannian manifold
(see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
).
We add that, in product spaces
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
the class of hypersurfaces
with the
T
-property is abundant and includes all rotational ones.

Considering our purposes here, a primary concern
should be to prove existence of trivial Einstein hypersurfaces in

I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.
This is accomplished in our first result, as stated below.
It should be mentioned that we conceive rotational hypersurfaces
as in
[
5
]
, dividing them into three types:
spherical, parabolic, and hyperbolic (see Section
4
for details).

Theorem 1
.

Given an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R},
let
\omega:I\rightarrow\omega(I)
be a diffeomorphism
such that
|\omega^{\prime}|>1
on
I.
Then, the following assertions
hold:

i)

For all
c\in\mathbb{R},

there exists a rotational spherical-type hypersurface
of constant sectional curvature
c
in
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
where
\epsilon\in\{0,-1\}
.

ii)

For all
c>0,
there exists a rotational spherical-type hypersurface
of constant sectional curvature
c
in
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{S}^{n}.

iii)

For all
c<0,
there exist in
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{H}^{n}

a rotational parabolic-type hypersurface and a rotational
hyperbolic-type hypersurface which are both of constant sectional curvature
c.

iv)

There exists a flat rotational parabolic-type hypersurface in

I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{H}^{n}.

We remark that Theorem
1

is of local nature. So,
there is no loss of generality in assuming the (nonconstant)
warping function
\omega
to be
a diffeomorphism over its image.
By the same token,
we can assume that, on
I,

|\omega^{\prime}|
is bounded below by a positive constant.
Hence, up to scaling, one has
|\omega^{\prime}|>1.

Considering these facts, Theorem
1
shows that,
for virtually any nonconstant warping function
\omega,

the class of local rotational CSC hypersurfaces of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is abundant.

To each warped product
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
we associate two real functions
\alpha
and
\beta

defined on
I,
which arise from the expression of the curvature tensor of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
in terms of the curvature
tensor of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
and the unit tangent
\partial_{t}
to the horizontal factor
I\subset\mathbb{R}.

Then, assuming
n>3,
we show that
a CSC hypersurface
\varSigma
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is essentially rotational.
More precisely, we have the following result
(
\xi
always denotes the height function of the hypersurface).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varSigma\subset I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

(n>3)
be a hypersurface with constant sectional
curvature on which
(\beta\circ\xi)T
never vanishes.
Then,
\varSigma
is rotational.

Next, we turn our attention to Einstein hypersurfaces of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

focusing on our main question.
Firstly, as stated in the next two theorems, we characterize them
according to whether
(\beta\circ\xi)T
vanishes everywhere or nowhere on
the hypersurface.

Theorem 3
.

For
n>3,
let
\varSigma
be a connected
\varLambda
-Einstein hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
such
that
(\beta\circ\xi)T
vanishes on
\varSigma.

Then,
\alpha\circ\xi
is constant. Furthermore, defining

\sigma:=\varLambda+(n-1)(\alpha\circ\xi),

the following assertions hold:

i)

If
\sigma>0,

\varSigma
is trivial and totally umbilical (with constant umbilical function).

ii)

If
\sigma=0,

\varSigma
is trivial and its shape operator has rank at most one.

iii)

If
\sigma<0,

\varSigma
is nontrivial and has precisely
two distinct nonzero constant principal curvatures with opposite signs, both with constant
multiplicity
\geq 2.
In particular,
\varSigma
has constant mean curvature.

Connected Einstein hypersurfaces on which
(\beta\circ\xi)T
never vanishes
have many interesting properties. For this reason, we shall call them
ideal.

The following result supports our claim.

Theorem 4
.

For
n>3,
let
\varSigma
be an ideal Einstein hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.

Then,
\varSigma
has the
T
-property. In addition,
the following assertions are equivalent:

i)

\varSigma
is trivial.

ii)

Any vertical section
\varSigma_{t}=\varSigma\mathrel{\text{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\smash{$-$}\crcr$\pitchfork$\crcr}}}}(\{t\}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n})
is
totally umbilical.

iii)

\varSigma
is rotational.

The second fundamental property of ideal Einstein hypersurfaces
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
we establish here is that they are
local horizontal graphs of functions
\phi=\phi(s)

whose level hypersurfaces
f_{s}
are parallel and isoparametric (i.e, have constant
principal curvatures) in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.
We call such a hypersurface a
(\phi,f_{s})
-
graph
.

Theorem 5
.

For
n>3,
let
\varSigma
be an oriented ideal Einstein hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
with unit normal

N
and non vanishing angle function
\varTheta:=\langle N,\partial_{t}\rangle.

Under these conditions, the following hold:

i)

\varSigma
is locally a
(\phi,f_{s})
-graph in
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.

ii)

The parallel family
\{f_{s}\}
of any local
(\phi,f_{s})
-graph of
\varSigma

is isoparametric, and each
f_{s}
has at most two distinct principal curvatures.

iii)

\varSigma
is trivial, provided that the principal curvature associated to

T
vanishes everywhere on
\varSigma.

Theorem
3
-(iii) opens the possibility of existence of
nontrivial Einstein hypersurfaces in
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.

As we have seen, they are easily obtained in

\mathbb{S}^{n+1},
which turns out to be
a space form that can be expressed as a warped product.
However, nontrivial examples
of Einstein hypersurfaces of general warped products
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

are not easy to find. Here, we consider this problem restricting ourselves to the class
of constant angle hypersurfaces of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
obtaining the following result.

Theorem 6
.

For
n>3,
let
\varSigma
be a connected oriented
\varLambda
-Einstein
hypersurface (not necessarily ideal) of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
with constant
angle function
\varTheta\in[0,1).

Then, the following assertions hold:

i)

If
\varTheta\neq 0,

\varSigma
is trivial.

ii)

If
\varTheta=0,

\omega
is necessarily a solution of the ODE:

(\omega^{\prime})^{2}+\dfrac{\varLambda}{n-1}\omega^{2}+c=0,\,\,c\in\mathbb{R},

on an open subinterval of
I,
and
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
has
nonconstant sectional curvature if
c+\epsilon\neq 0.
Regarding
\varSigma,

we distinguish the following cases, according to the sign of
c+\epsilon:

a)

c+\epsilon=0:

\varSigma
is trivial and its shape operator has rank at most one.

b)

c+\epsilon<0:

\varSigma
is trivial and constitutes a cylinder over
a totally umbilical hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}.

c)

c+\epsilon>0:

\varSigma
is nontrivial and constitutes a
cylinder over a product
of two spheres of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(so that
\epsilon
is necessarily
1
in this case).

We point out that Theorem
6
-(ii)(c)
provides examples of nontrivial Einstein hypersurfaces in
warped products
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{S}^{n}
which are not space forms.

Putting together Theorems
4
–
6
with classical
results on classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces in space forms,
we obtain the following characterization of ideal Einstein hypersurfaces
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\varSigma
be an ideal Einstein hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},

n>3.

Then,
\varSigma
has a constant number of distinct principal curvatures —
which is two or three — all with constant multiplicities.
Moreover,
\varSigma
is trivial
if and only if it has two distinct principal curvatures everywhere.
If so,
\varSigma
is necessarily rotational.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we set some notation and formulae. In Section
3
, we establish three key lemmas.
In Section
4
, we consider rotational CSC hypersurfaces of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
and prove Theorems
1
and
2
.
Section
5
is reserved for the proofs of Theorems
3
and
4
. In Section
6
,
we introduce the
(\phi,f_{s})
-graphs we mentioned, establishing some
of its fundamental properties. In the final Section
7
, we prove
Theorems
5
–
7
.

]
, which overlaps with ours on some parts.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Ricci tensor

Given a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
with curvature
tensor
R
and tangent bundle
TM,

recall that its
Ricci tensor
is defined as:

{\rm Ric}\,(X,Y):={\rm trace}\,\{Z\mapsto R(Z,X)Y\},\,\,\,\,\,(X,Y)\in TM\times TM.

We say that a frame
\{X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}\}\subset TM

diagonalizes
the Ricci tensor
of
(M,\langle\,,\,\rangle)
if

{\rm Ric}\,(X_{i},X_{j})=0\,\forall i\neq j\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

Notice that, when
M
is an Einstein manifold, any orthogonal
frame
\{X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}\}
in
TM
diagonalizes its Ricci tensor.

2.2.
Hypersurfaces

Let
\varSigma
be an oriented hypersurface of a Riemannian manifold
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1}
whose
unit normal field we denote by
N.
Let
A
be the shape operator of
\varSigma
with respect to
N,
that is,

AX=-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N,\,\,X\in T\varSigma,

where
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1}.

For such a
\varSigma
, and for
X,Y,Z,W\in T\varSigma
, one has the
Gauss equation
:

(1)

\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle=\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X,Y)Z,W\rangle+\langle AX,W\rangle\langle AY,Z\rangle-\langle AX,Z\rangle\langle AY,W\rangle,

where
R
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
denote the curvature tensors of
\varSigma
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1},
respectively.

Let
\lambda_{1}\,,\dots,\lambda_{n}
be the principal curvatures of
\varSigma
, and

\{X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}\}
the corresponding orthonormal frame of principal directions, i.e.,

AX_{i}=\lambda_{i}X_{i}\,,\,\,\,\,\langle X_{i},X_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\,.

In this setting, for
1\leq k\leq n,
the Gauss equation yields

(2)

\langle R(X_{k},Y)Z,X_{k}\rangle=\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X_{k},Y)Z,X_{k}\rangle+\lambda_{k}\langle AY,Z\rangle-\lambda_{k}^{2}\langle X_{k},Z\rangle\langle X_{k},Y\rangle.

Thus, for the Ricci tensor
{\rm Ric}
of
\varSigma
one has

(3)

{\rm Ric}(Y,Z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X_{k},Y)Z,X_{k}\rangle+H\langle AY,Z\rangle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{2}\langle X_{k},Z\rangle\langle X_{k},Y\rangle,

where
H={\rm trace}\,A
is the (non normalized) mean curvature of
\varSigma.

In particular, if we set
Y=X_{i}
and
Z=X_{j}
, we have

(4)

{\rm Ric}(X_{i}\,,X_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X_{k},X_{i})X_{j},X_{k}\rangle+H\delta_{ij}\lambda_{i}-\delta_{ij}\lambda_{i}^{2}\,.

2.3.
Hypersurfaces in
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

In the above setting, let us consider the particular case when
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1}
is the warped product

I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval,

\omega
is a positive differentiable function defined on
I,

and
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is one of the simply connected space forms of constant curvature

\epsilon\in\{0,1,-1\}
:
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
\epsilon=0
),

\mathbb{S}^{n}
(
\epsilon=1
), or
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(
\epsilon=-1
).

Recall that the Riemannian metric
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
, to be denoted by
\langle\,,\,\rangle,
is:

dt^{2}+\omega^{2}ds_{\epsilon}^{2},

where
dt^{2}
and
ds_{\epsilon}^{2}
are the standard Riemannian metrics of
\mathbb{R}
and

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
respectively.

We call
horizontal
the fields of
T(I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n})
which are tangent to
I,
and

vertical
those which are tangent to
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.
The gradient of
the projection
\pi_{I}
on the first factor
I
will be denoted by
\partial_{t}
.

Setting
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and
\widetilde{\nabla}
for the Levi-Civita connections of

I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
respectively,
for any
vertical
fields
X,Y\in T\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},

the following identities hold
(see
[
1
, Lema 7.3]
):

(5)

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y-({\omega^{\prime}}/{\omega})\langle X,Y\rangle\partial_{t}\,.

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\partial_{t}}X=({\omega^{\prime}}/{\omega})X.

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle=0.

The curvature tensor
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
of
I\times_{\omega}\,\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is given by (cf.
[
10
]
)

(6)

\displaystyle\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X,Y)Z,W\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\circ\pi_{I})(\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,W\rangle-\langle X,W\rangle\langle Y,Z\rangle)

\displaystyle+(\beta\circ\pi_{I})(\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle W,\partial_{t}\rangle-\langle Y,Z\rangle\langle X,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle W,\partial_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle X,W\rangle\langle Y,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle+\langle Y,W\rangle\langle X,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle),

where
X,Y,Z,W\in T(I\times_{\omega}\,\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}),
and
\alpha
and
\beta
are the following functions on
I
:

(7)

\alpha:=\frac{(\omega^{\prime})^{2}-\epsilon}{\omega^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta:=\frac{\omega^{\prime\prime}}{\omega}-\alpha.

It is easily seen from (
6
) that
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
has
constant sectional curvature if and only if
\alpha
is constant and
\beta
vanishes on
I.

Also, a direct computation yields:

(8)

\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{2\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}\beta.

Given a hypersurface
\varSigma
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},

the
height
function
\xi
and the
angle
function
\varTheta
of
\varSigma

are defined as

\xi:=\pi_{\scriptscriptstyle I}|_{\varSigma}\quad\text{and}\quad\varTheta(x):=\langle N(x),\partial_{t}\rangle,\,\,x\in\varSigma.

The gradient of
\xi
on
\varSigma
is denoted by
T,
so that

(9)

T=\partial_{t}-\varTheta N.

Definition 1
.

A connected Einstein hypersurface
\varSigma
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
on which

(\beta\circ\xi)T
never vanishes will be called
ideal
.

Regarding the gradient of
\varTheta,
let us notice first that,
from the equalities (
5
), for all
X\in T(I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}),
one has

(10)

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}\partial_{t}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X-\langle X,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}}\partial_{t}=\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}\left(X-\langle X,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}\right),

where, by abuse of notation, we are writing
\omega\circ\xi=\omega
and

\omega^{\prime}\circ\xi=\omega^{\prime}
.
Therefore, for any
X\in T\varSigma,
we have

X(\varTheta)=\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N,\partial_{t}\rangle+\langle N,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}\partial_{t}\rangle=-\langle AX,T\rangle-\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}\varTheta\langle T,X\rangle=-\langle AT,X\rangle-\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}\varTheta\langle T,X\rangle.

Hence, the gradient of
\varTheta
is

(11)

\nabla\varTheta=-\left(A+\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}\varTheta\,{\rm Id}\right)T,

where
{\rm Id}
stands for the identity map of
T\varSigma.

The following concept will play a fundamental role in this paper.

Definition 2
.

We say that a hypersurface
\varSigma
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
has the
T
-
property
if
T
is a principal direction
of
\varSigma,
i.e.,
T
never vanishes and there is a differentiable real function
\lambda
on
\varSigma
such that

AT=\lambda T
, where
A
is the shape operator of
\varSigma.

Finally, it follows from (
6
) that, for any hypersurface

\varSigma
of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
the
Gauss equation (
1
) takes the form:

(12)

\displaystyle\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\circ\xi)(\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,W\rangle-\langle X,W\rangle\langle Y,Z\rangle)

\displaystyle+(\beta\circ\xi)(\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,T\rangle\langle W,T\rangle-\langle Y,Z\rangle\langle X,T\rangle\langle W,T\rangle

Three Key Lemmas

Given a hypersurface
\varSigma\subset I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
any nonempty transversal intersection

\varSigma_{t}:=\varSigma\mathrel{\text{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\smash{$-$}\crcr$\pitchfork$\crcr}}}}(\{t\}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n})

is a hypersurface of
(\{t\}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},ds_{\epsilon}^{2})

which we call the
vertical section
of
\varSigma
at height
t.
If
\varSigma
is oriented with
unit normal
N,
it is easily seen that

N-\varTheta\partial_{t}
is tangent to

\{t\}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
and orthogonal to
\varSigma_{t}
, so that

\varSigma_{t}
is orientable as well.
Denoting also by
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{\epsilon}
the Riemannian metric
ds_{\epsilon}^{2}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
one has

\omega^{2}\langle N-\varTheta\partial_{t},N-\varTheta\partial_{t}\rangle_{\epsilon}=\langle N-\varTheta\partial_{t},N-\varTheta\partial_{t}\rangle=1-\varTheta^{2}=\|T\|^{2}.

Hence, a unit normal
to
\varSigma_{t}
in
(\{t\}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},ds_{\epsilon}^{2})
is given by

(13)

N_{t}=-\frac{\omega(t)(N-\varTheta\partial_{t})}{\|T\|}\,\cdot

We shall denote by
A_{t}
the
shape operator of
\varSigma_{t}
with respect to
N_{t}\,,

that is,

A_{t}X=-\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{t}\,,\,\,\,X\in T\varSigma_{t}\subset T\varSigma,

where
\widetilde{\nabla}
stands for the Levi-Civita connection of

(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},ds_{\epsilon}^{2}).

Our first lemma establishes the relation between the shape operator

A
of a hypersurface
\varSigma\subset I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
with the shape operator
A_{t}
of a vertical section

\varSigma_{t}\subset(\{t\}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},ds_{\epsilon}^{2}).

Lemma 1
.

For
n\geq 2,
let
\varSigma
be a hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
and let

\varSigma_{t}
be one of its vertical sections (considered as a hypersurface of
(\{t\}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},ds_{\epsilon}^{2})
).
Then, the following identity holds:

(14)

\langle A_{t}X,Y\rangle=-\frac{\omega}{\|T\|}\left(\langle AX,Y\rangle+\frac{\varTheta\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}\langle X,Y\rangle\right)\,\,\,\,\forall X,Y\in T\varSigma_{t}\subset T\varSigma.

Consequently, if
T
is a principal direction of
\varSigma
along
\varSigma_{t}

with corresponding principal curvature
\lambda_{1},
the principal curvatures
\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}

of
\varSigma
are given by

(15)

\lambda_{i}=-\frac{\|T\|\lambda_{i}^{t}+\omega^{\prime}\varTheta}{\omega}\,,\,\,\,\,\,i=2,\dots,n,

where
\lambda_{i}^{t}
denotes the
i
-th principal curvature of
\varSigma_{t}.

Proof.

Given
X\in T\varSigma_{t},
it follows from the first identity (
5
) that

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{t}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N_{t},
for
\langle X,N_{t}\rangle=0.

However,
from (
13
), we have

-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N_{t}=X({\omega}/{\|T\|})(N-\varTheta\partial_{t})+({\omega}/{\|T\|})(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N-X(\varTheta)\partial_{t}-\varTheta\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}\partial_{t}).

Thus, since
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}\partial_{t}=(\omega^{\prime}/\omega)X
and
AX=-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N,
for all
Y\in T\varSigma_{t}
, one has

\langle A_{t}X,Y\rangle=-\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{t},Y\rangle=-\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N_{t},Y\rangle=-\frac{\omega}{\|T\|}\langle AX+\varTheta(\omega/\omega^{\prime})X,Y\rangle,

as we wished to prove.

Now, assuming that
T
is a principal direction of
\varSigma

along
\varSigma_{t},
we have that
T\varSigma_{t}
is invariant by
A.

This, together with (
14
), then yields

A_{t}=-\frac{\omega}{\|T\|}\left(A+\varTheta({\omega^{\prime}}/{\omega}){\rm Id}\right){|_{T\varSigma_{t}}}\,,

where
\rm{Id}
denotes the identity map of
T\varSigma_{t}.

From this last equality,
one easily concludes that any principal direction
X_{i}
of
\varSigma_{t}
with
corresponding principal curvature
\lambda_{i}^{t}
is a principal direction of

\varSigma
with corresponding principal curvature
\lambda_{i}
as in
(
15
).
∎

Now, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition
for an arbitrary hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
to have the
T
-property.

Lemma 2
.

For
n>2,
let
\varSigma
be a hypersurface of
I\times_{\omega}\,\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
on which

never vanishes. Then,
\varSigma
has the

T
-property if and only if the principal directions

of
\varSigma
diagonalize its Ricci tensor.
In particular, if
\varSigma
is Einstein, it has the

-property.

Proof.

Choosing

with
i\neq j\neq k\neq i,
we have from (

\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X_{k},X_{i})X_{j},X_{k}\rangle=-\beta\langle X_{i},T\rangle\langle X_{j},T\rangle.

Combining this equality with (

∎

Finally, as a direct consequence of equation (

Lemma 3
.

For

let
\varSigma
be a hypersurface of

The proof is straightforward considering the identity (

for

∎

Remark 1

It is a well known fact that, for any

contains open dense subsets which can

any conformally flat Einstein manifold has constant sectional curvature.
Another classical result on this subject states that, for

any hypersurface of a

.

\left(\dfrac{\varrho^{\prime}}{\varrho}\right)^{\prime}=-\epsilon+\left(\dfrac{1-n_{\lambda}}{n-3}\right)(\lambda^{s})^{2}+\left(\dfrac{1-n_{\mu}}{n-3}\right)(\mu^{s})^{2}.

Finally, Cartan’s formula
\lambda^{s}\mu^{s}=-\epsilon
and equalities
(
52
)–(
54
) yield

\dfrac{(n-1)(1-n_{\lambda})(1-n_{\mu})}{(n-3)^{2}}\left((\lambda^{s})^{2}+(\mu^{s})^{2}+2\epsilon\right)=0.

Since, from (
50
), we have
n_{\lambda}>1

and
n_{\mu}>1,
the above equation reduces to
(\lambda^{s})^{2}+(\mu^{s})^{2}+2\epsilon=0.

Again from
\lambda^{s}\mu^{s}=-\epsilon,
we have
(\lambda^{s}-\mu^{s})^{2}=0,

which yields
\lambda^{s}\equiv\mu^{s},
so that
\lambda\equiv\mu.

Therefore, all vertical sections of
\varSigma
are totally umbilical. So,
Theorem
4
applies and gives that
\varSigma
is trivial.
This shows (iii) and finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Since

\displaystyle(n-1)(\beta+\alpha)+\varLambda

Remark 2
.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
6
that, except for the case (ii)-(a),
the Einstein hypersurface
\varSigma
in its statement is necessarily ideal.

In the next proof, we shall consider the fact that
the isoparametric hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
having at most two distinct
principal curvatures are completely classified. They are
the totally umbilical hypersurfaces, in the case of a single principal curvature,
and the tubes around totally geodesic submanifolds,
in the case of two distinct principal curvatures. In Euclidian space, for instance,
they constitute hyperplanes, geodesic spheres, and generalized cylinders

\mathbb{S}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k-1},

k\in\{1,\dots,n-2\}
(see
[
3
,
7
]
for more details).

Proof of Theorem
7
.

Keeping the notation of the proof of Theorem
4
, we have that
\varSigma
has at most
three distinct principal curvatures,
\lambda_{1},\lambda
and
\mu.
Since
(\beta\circ\xi)T
never vanishes on

\varSigma,
the principal curvature
\lambda_{1}
cannot satisfy both equations in Lemma
3
. Thus,

\lambda\neq\lambda_{1}\neq\mu,
i.e.,
\lambda_{1}
has multiplicity one, so that

\varSigma
has at least two distinct principal curvatures.
Let us define the set

C:=\{x\in\varSigma\,;\,\lambda(x)=\mu(x)\}.

If
\varSigma=C,
we are done. Analogously, if

C=\emptyset,
we have that

\varSigma
has three distinct principal curvatures everywhere. Also, since

\{T\}^{\perp}
is invariant by the shape operator
A
of
\varSigma,

and
\lambda
and
\mu
are the (distinct) eigenvalues of
A|_{\{T\}^{\perp}},

we have from
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
that both
\lambda
and
\mu

have constant multiplicity.

Now, suppose that
C
and
\varSigma-C
are both nonempty and let

x
be a boundary point of
\varSigma-C.
Since
C
is closed, one has

x\in C,
so that the vertical section
\varSigma_{t}\owns x
is umbilical at
x

(by (
15
) in Lemma
1
).
Furthermore, there is no open set
\varSigma^{\prime}
of
\varSigma
containing
x

such that
\varTheta
vanishes on
\varSigma^{\prime}.
Indeed, assuming otherwise,
we have that
\varSigma^{\prime}
correspond to
one of the cases (b) or (c)
of Theorem
6
-(ii) (see Remark
2
).
However, in any of them,

\lambda=\mu
or
\lambda\neq\mu

everywhere on
\varSigma^{\prime},
which contradicts the fact that

x
is a boundary point of
\varSigma-C.

It follows from the above considerations that, in any open set
\varSigma^{\prime}
of

\varSigma
containing
x,
there exists

y\in(\varSigma-C)\cap\varSigma^{\prime}
such that
\varTheta(y)\neq 0.

Then, from Theorem
5
, there exists a local
(\phi,f_{s})
-graph

\varSigma(y)\owns y
in
\varSigma-C

whose parallel family
\{f_{s}\}
is isoparametric in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.
In this case,
each parallel
f_{s}
has precisely two distinct principal curvatures everywhere,
so that they are tubes around totally geodesic submanifolds.

Now, we can choose
y
as above in such a way that

x
is a boundary point of
\varSigma(y).

Then, denoting by
\pi
the vertical projection of
I\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},

one has that an open neighborhood

in
\pi(\varSigma_{t})\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is the limit set of a
family of parallel open subsets of tubes of the family
\{f_{s}\}.
In this case,
it is easily seen that

itself must be part of a

\{f_{s}\}
. However,
U
is umbilical at
\pi(x),
and tubes have no umbilical points.
This contradiction shows that either
C
or
\varSigma-C
is empty, proving the
first part of the theorem. As for the last part, just apply Theorem
4
.
∎

Abstract
This paper conducts sensitivity analysis of random constraint and variational systems related to stochastic optimization and variational inequalities. We establish efficient conditions for well-posedness, in the sense of robust Lipschitzian stability and/or metric regularity, of such systems by employing and developing coderivative characterizations of well-posedness properties for random multifunctions and efficiently evaluating coderivatives of special classes of random integral set-valued mappings that naturally emerge in stochastic programming and stochastic variational inequalities.

Introduction

Sensitivity analysis has drawn a strong attention of many researchers and users in the areas of optimization, variational analysis, and related disciplines who are interested in understanding the behavior of feasible and optimal solutions
under perturbations of the initial data. Such perturbations should be taken into account due to “always present” errors in the given data. The literature on sensitivity analysis for various classes of optimization-related problems in deterministic frameworks is enormous; see, e.g.,
bs
;
m06
and the bibliographies therein. To the best of our knowledge, much less has been done in this direction for stochastic problems; we refer the reader to
sdr
for a very recent account.

Variational analysis offers natural approaches to the study of sensitivity of parametric sets of feasible and optimal solutions to optimization-related and equilibrium problems with respect to parameter perturbations. Among such approaches, we emphasize those based on
generalized differentiation
of usually
set-valued
parameter-dependent solution maps. Efficient results of this type for
robust Lipschitzian stability
of solution maps associated with
deterministic
constraint and variational systems were developed in
m06
;
m18
based on the
coderivative
concept for set-valued mappings (multifunctions) introduced in
m80
. This approach is based on the
complete characterization
of robust Lipschitzian stability of general closed-graph multifunctions (and equivalent properties of
metric regularity
and
linear openness
of inverse mappings) obtained in
m93
and known as the
Mordukhovich criterion

rw
. Due to the well-developed calculus rules and computations of coderivatives, this criterion has been broadly applied to the sensitivity analysis of various deterministic constraint and variational systems; see
m06
;
m18
and the references therein.

The situation is much more involved for
random multifunctions
in all the aspects: coderivative calculus, a variety of Lipschitzian properties, and their coderivative characterizations. Various results in this directions for general classes of
expected-integral
mappings have been recently obtained in our papers
mp2
;
mp3
. For the reader’s convenience, the major results obtained therein, which are needed in what follows, will be briefly reviewed in the next section.

The
main goal
of this paper is to elaborate and further develop the aforementioned results for general random multifunctions in order to apply them to the sensitivity analysis of
structured random constraint
and
variational systems
that appear in various models of
stochastic optimization
and
stochastic variational inequalities
with applications to
well-posedness
of such systems in the sense of their robust Lipschitzian stability and/or metric regularity.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we
overview
the major constructions and results of variational analysis, generalized differentiation, random measurable multifunctions and their integration, which are largely used in the formulation and derivation of the main results of the paper.

Section
3
is devoted to various
Lipschitzian
properties of
random normal integrands
and the corresponding
expected integral multifunctions
. Using coderivatives, we establish efficient conditions ensuring these properties, which are significant for the subsequent material.

In Section
4
we conduct a
coderivative-based sensitivity analysis
for a general class of
stochastic constraint systems
as well as for their various specifications. Our approach is based on coderivative evaluations (upper estimates) for such parametric systems in terms of the initial data with further applications of coderivative conditions ensuring their
well-posedness properties
such as
Lipschitzian stability
and
metric regularity
.

Section
5
concerns
stochastic variational systems
, which are described by
stochastic generalized equations
involving expected integral multifunctions and encompass, in particular,
stochastic variational inequalities
. We first evaluate coderivatives of solution maps to such systems in terms of their initial data and then use these calculations of derive efficient conditions for their well-posedness properties based on coderivative characterizations.

Section
6
provides a coderivative-based sensitivity analysis of solution maps to stochastic constraint and variational systems in the case where the random integrand enjoys the
integrable Lipschitz-like
property being also single-valued at the reference point. Finally, in Section
7
we apply the obtained results to explicit coderivative evaluations for
stationary point maps
in stochastic programming and to the derivation of necessary optimality conditions in
stochastic mathematical programs with equilibrium constraints
.

2.1
Variational analysis and generalized differentiation

Here we recall some major notions of generalized differentiation for sets, set-valued mappings/multifunctions, and extended-real-valued functions that are broadly employed in the paper.

Given
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, the (Fréchet)
regular normal cone
to
x\in C
is defined by

\widehat{N}(x;C):=\Big\{x^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\Big|\;\limsup\limits_{u\overset{C}{\to}x}\Big\langle x^{\ast},\frac{u-x}{\|u-x\|}\Big\rangle\leq 0\Big\},

(2.1)

where the symbol ‘
y\overset{C}{\to}x
’ means that
y\to x
with
y\in C
. We put
\widehat{N}(x;C):=\emptyset
if
x\notin C
. The (Mordukhovich)
limiting normal cone
to
C
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is

N(x;C):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{u\to x}\widehat{N}(u;C),

(2.2)

where for any multifunction
F:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;\mathbb{R}^{m}
the symbol ‘
\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}
’ stands for the (Painlevé-Kuratowski)
outer limit
of
F
at
x
defined by

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{u\to x}F(u):=\big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\big|\;\exists\,u_{k}\to x,\,y_{k}\to y,\;y_{k}\in F(u_{k})\;\mbox{as}\;k\in{\rm I\!N}\big\}.

(2.3)

The set
C
is called
normally regular
at
x\in C
if
\widehat{N}(x;C)=N(x;C)
.

Given a multifunction
F:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;\mathbb{R}^{m}
, denote its
graph
and
kernel
by

\operatorname{gph}F:=\big\{(v,w)\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\;\big|\;w\in F(x)\big\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\mbox{\rm ker}\,F:=\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\big|\;0\in F(x)\big\}.

The
regular coderivative
and
limiting coderivative
of
F
at
(x,y)\in\operatorname{gph}F
are defined for all
y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
via the corresponding normal cone (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) to the graph of
F
by, respectively,

\widehat{D}^{\ast}F(x,y)(y^{\ast}):=\big\{x^{\ast}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\big|\;(x^{\ast},-y^{\ast})\in\widehat{N}\big((x,y);\operatorname{gph}F\big)\big\},

(2.4)

{D}^{\ast}F(x,y)(y^{\ast}):=\big\{x^{\ast}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\big|\;(x^{\ast},-y^{\ast})\in N\big((x,y);\operatorname{gph}F\big)\big\},

(2.5)

where
y
is dropped if
F
is a singleton at
x
, i.e.,
F(x)=\{y\}
. From (
2.2
) we have the limiting representation

\displaystyle{D}^{\ast}F(x,y)(y^{\ast})=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{{\scriptstyle{(v,w)\overset{\operatorname{gph}F}{\longrightarrow}(x,y)}\atop\scriptstyle{w^{\ast}\to y^{\ast}}}}\widehat{D}^{\ast}F(v,w)(w^{\ast}),\quad y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(2.6)

Given now an extended-real-valued function
f:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, assume in what follows that it is
proper
, i.e.,
f(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and its domain
\mbox{\rm dom}\,f:=\{x\in X\;\big|\;f(x)<+\infty\}
is nonempty. Considering the epigraph
\mbox{\rm epi}\,f:=\{(x,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}\;|\;f(x)\leq\alpha\}
of
f
, we define its (limiting)
subdifferential
at
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
geometrically by

\partial f(x):=\big\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;(x^{\ast},-1)\in N\big((x,f(x));\mbox{\rm epi}\,f\big)\big\},

(2.7)

while referring the reader to
m06
;
m18
;
rw
for various limiting analytic representations of (
2.7
) as well as comprehensive
calculus rules
for this subdifferential and the associated limiting constructions (
2.2
) and (
2.5
). Observe that if a mapping
F\colon{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is single-valued and locally Lipschitzian around
x
, then we have the coderivative
scalarization formula
held for all
y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

{D}^{\ast}F(x)(y^{\ast})=\partial\langle y^{\ast},F\rangle(x)\text{ with }\langle y^{\ast},F\rangle(\cdot):=\langle y^{\ast},F(\cdot)\rangle.

(2.8)

Along with the (first-order) subdifferential (
2.7
), consider the
second-order subdifferential
(or
generalized Hessian
) of
f:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
at
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
relative to
x^{\ast}\in\partial f(x)
introduced in
m92
by

\partial^{2}f(x,x^{\ast})(v^{\ast})=\left(D^{\ast}\partial f\right)(x,x^{\ast})(v^{\ast}),\quad v^{\ast}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

(2.9)

Dealing with extended-real-valued functions of two variables
f(x,z):{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we use the
partial second-order subdifferential
of
f
with respect to
x
at
(\bar{x},\bar{z})
relative to
\bar{y}\in\partial_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{z})
defined by

\partial_{x}^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{w},\bar{y})(u^{\ast}):=\left(D^{\ast}\partial_{z}f\right)(\bar{x},\bar{w},\bar{y})(u^{\ast}),

(2.10)

where
\partial_{x}f(x,z):=\partial f_{z}(x)
with
f_{z}:=f(\cdot,z)
; see
mr
. Note that both second-order constructions (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) enjoy well-developed calculus rules and are efficiently computed for broad classes of functions encountered in variational analysis and optimization; see
m06
;
m18
;
mr
and the references therein.

Next we recall the two interrelated
well-posedness
properties of multifunctions, which play a fundamental role in many aspects of variational analysis, optimization, and applications. They both are studied and largely used in the paper. We say that
F\colon{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
Lipschitz-like
around
(x,y)\in\operatorname{gph}F
(or satisfies the
Aubin pseudo-Lipschitz property
around this point) if
there exist a constant
\ell\geq 0
and neighborhoods
U
of
x
and
V
of
y
such that

F(u)\cap V\subseteq F(v)+\ell\|u-v\|\mathbb{B}\;\text{ for all }\;u,v\in U.

(2.11)

A complete characterization of the Lipschitz-like property of closed-graph multifunctions
F
around
(x,y)
and the precise calculations of the exact bound
\mbox{\rm lip}\,F(x,y)
, i.e., the infimum of moduli
\ell
over neighborhood
U
and
V
in (
2.11
), are given by the
Mordukhovich coderivative criterion

D^{*}F(x,y)(0)=\{0\}\;\mbox{ with }\;\mbox{\rm lip}\,F(x,y)=\|D^{*}F(x,y)\|

(2.12)

in terms of the limiting coderivative (
2.5
) and its norm as a positive homogeneous mapping of
y^{*}
. Another well-posedness property used in this paper is the
metric regularity
of
F
around
(x,y)\in\operatorname{gph}F
defined as

\mbox{\rm dist}\,\big(u;F^{-1}(v)\big)\leq\kappa\,\mbox{\rm dist}\,\big(v;F(u)\big)\;\text{ for all }\;u\in U\;\text{ and }\;v\in V

(2.13)

for some constant
\kappa\geq 0
and neighborhoods
U
of
x
and
V
of
y
with
\mbox{\rm reg}\,F(x,y)
standing for the exact bound (supremum) of
\kappa
over
U
and
V
in (
2.13
). As well known in variational analysis, the Lipschitz-like property of
F
around
(x,y)
is
equivalent
to the metric regularity of the inverse mapping
F^{-1}
around
(y,x)
with the exact bound relationship
\mbox{\rm lip}\,F(x,y)\cdot\mbox{\rm reg}\,F^{-1}(y,x)=1
. Thus the coderivative criterion (
2.12
) yields the
metric regularity characterization
:

\mbox{\rm ker}\,D^{*}F(x,y)=\{0\}\;\mbox{ with }\;\mbox{\rm reg}\,F(x,y)=\|D^{*}F(x,y)\|^{-1}.

(2.14)

Since the coderivatives in (
2.12
) and (
2.14
) are
robust
and possess
full calculus
, both these characterizations are broadly used in variational analysis, optimization, and their applications; see
m06
;
m18
;
rw
for more details and references.

2.2
Measurable multifunctions and expected-integral mappings

In the second part of this section, we review some required classical notions and results on measurable multifunctions and their selections, as well as more recent ones dealing with expected-integral functionals and set-valued mappings.
Throughout the paper,
({T},\mathcal{A},\mu)
is a complete finite measure space. To avoid confusion, we use special font (e.g.,
\mathpzc{v},\mathpzc{w},\mathpzc{x},\mathpzc{y},\mathpzc{z}
, etc.) to designate functions defined on
T
. For any
p\in[1,+\infty]
, denote by
\textnormal{L}^{p}({T},\mathbb{R}^{n})
the sets of all (equivalence classes by the relation ‘equal almost everywhere’) measurable functions
\mathpzc{x}
such that
\|\mathpzc{x}(\cdot)\|^{p}
is integrable for
p\in[1,+\infty)
and measurable essentially bounded functions for
p=\infty
. The norm in
\textnormal{L}^{p}(T,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. We identify points in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
with constant functions in
\textnormal{L}^{p}(T,{\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and so have

\displaystyle\|x-\mathpzc{x}\|_{p}:

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{T}\|x-\mathpzc{x}(t)\|^{p}\mu(dt)\right)^{1/p}\text{as }\;p\in[1,+\infty),

\displaystyle\|x-\mathpzc{x}\|_{\infty}:

\displaystyle=\esssup\limits_{t\in T}\|x-\mathpzc{x}(t)\|\;\mbox{ for $x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}$ and $\mathpzc{x}\in\textnormal{L}^{p}(T,{\mathbb{R}^{n}})$}.

Recall that a set-valued mapping
M:T\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is

measurable
if for every open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
the set

M^{-1}(U):=\{t\in T\;|\;M(t)\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
is measurable, i.e.,
M^{-1}(U)\in\mathcal{A}
. The mapping
M
is
graph measurable
if
\operatorname{gph}M\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra, i.e., the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all open sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since the space
(T,\mathcal{A},\mu)
is complete, any multifunction
M
with closed values is measurable if and only if
\operatorname{gph}M\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

For a multifunction
M:{T}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}
(not necessarily measurable) and a measurable set
A\in\mathcal{A}
, define the
the Aumann integral
of
M
over
A
by

\int\limits_{A}M(t)\mu(dt):=\Bigg\{\int\limits_{A}\mathpzc{x}^{*}(t)\mu(dt)\;\Bigg|\;x^{*}\in{\textnormal{L}}^{1}({T},{\mathbb{R}^{n}})\textnormal{ and }\mathpzc{x}^{*}(t)\in M(t)\text{ a.e.}\Bigg\}.

An extended-real-valued function
f:T\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is called a
normal integrand
if the multifunction
t\mapsto\mbox{\rm epi}\,f_{t}
is measurable with closed values. By
(
rw
, Corollary 14.34)
, this amounts to saying that
f
is
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-measurable and that for every
t\in{T}
the function
f_{t}:=f(t,\cdot)
is lower semicontinuous function (l.s.c.). In addition, we say that
f
is
proper
if
f_{t}
is proper for all
t\in T
. If
f_{t}
is convex for all
t\in T
, then
f
is known as a
convex normal integrand
; see
cv
;
rw
.

We say
mp2
that
\Phi:T\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a
set-valued normal integrand
, or a
random multifunction
, if for all
t\in T
the mapping
\Phi_{t}:=\Phi(t,\cdot)
is of closed graph, and the graph of
\Phi
belongs to
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
. When
\Phi
is single-valued, it is called a
vector-valued normal integrand
. Since the measure space is complete, we have that the definition of a set-valued normal integrand is equivalent to requiring that
t\mapsto\operatorname{gph}\Phi_{t}
is a measurable multifunction with closed values. It is natural to say that
\Phi:T\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
locally single-valued
around
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
if there exists
\eta>0
and
\widehat{T}\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mu(T\backslash\widehat{T})
such that

\Phi_{t}(x)\text{ is single-valued for all }x\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x})\text{ and all }t\in\widehat{T}.

The term that
\Phi
is
continuously differentiable
around
\bar{x}
is defined similarly.

3
Lipschitzian properties for random multifunctions

This section is devoted to the study of various
Lipschitzian
properties
of
random
set-valued mappings that are largely used in our sensitivity analysis in what follows. These properties were introduced in
mp3
with establishing relationships between them and their applications to coderivative calculus of random multifunctions. First we recall the formulations.

Definition 1

Let
\Phi:T\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a set-valued normal integrand.

(i)

We say that
\Phi
is integrably locally Lipschitzian at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\Phi
if there exists
\eta>0
,
\ell\in\textnormal{L}^{1}(T,\mathbb{R})
, and
\widehat{T}\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mu(T\backslash\widehat{T})
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(x)\subseteq\Phi_{t}(x^{\prime})+\ell(t)\|x-x^{\prime}\|\mathbb{B}\text{ for all }t\in\widehat{T}\text{ and }x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x}).

(3.1)

(ii)

Pick
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\operatorname{gph}\mathrm{E}_{\Phi}
with
\operatorname{gph}\mathrm{E}_{\Phi}
defined in (
2.16
) and take
\bar{\mathpzc{y}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\Phi}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
for
{\cal S}_{\Phi}
from (
2.18
). We say that
\Phi
is integrably quasi-Lipschitzian around
(\bar{x},\bar{\mathpzc{y}})
if there exist
\eta>0
and
\ell\in\textnormal{L}^{1}(T,\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

\sup\big\{\|x^{\ast}\|\;\big|\;x^{\ast}\in{D}^{\ast}\Phi_{t}\big(\mathpzc{x}(t),\mathpzc{y}(t)\big)\big(\mathpzc{y}^{\ast}(t)\big)\big\}\leq\ell(t)\|\mathpzc{y}^{\ast}(t)\|

(3.2)

for a.e.
t\in T
and all

\mathpzc{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x})
,
\mathpzc{y}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{\mathpzc{y}})\cap\Phi(\mathpzc{x})
,

For the next result we need to recall another subdifferential construction for extended-real-valued functions

\widehat{\partial}f(x):=\Big\{x^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\Big|\;\liminf_{u\to x}\frac{f(u)-f(x)-\langle x^{*},u-x\rangle}{\|u-x\|}\geq 0\Big\}.

(4.29)

The following lemma establishes relationships between

Lemma 3

Let
g:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be an l.s.c. function such that
g(\bar{x},\cdot)
is convex. Then we have
z^{\ast}\in{\partial}_{z}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
whenever
(x^{\ast},z^{\ast})\in\widehat{\partial}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
. If in addition
g(x^{\prime},\cdot)
is convex and
g(\cdot,z^{\prime})
is continuous at
\bar{x}
for all
(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})
sufficiently close to
(\bar{x},\bar{z})
, then
z^{\ast}\in{\partial}_{z}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
whenever
(x^{\ast},z^{\ast})\in{\partial}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
.

Proof

Pick any
(x^{\ast},z^{\ast})\in\widehat{\partial}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
and fix an arbitrary number
\varepsilon>0
. Using definition (
4.28
), find
\eta>0
such that for all
(x,z)\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
we have

\displaystyle\langle x^{\ast},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\langle z^{\ast},z-\bar{z}\rangle\leq g(x,z)-g(\bar{x},\bar{z})+\varepsilon(\|x-\bar{x}\|+\|z-\bar{z}\|).

Fix
z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
and employ the above inequality with
x:=\bar{x}
and
z:=\bar{z}+\alpha(z^{\prime}-\bar{z})
for
\alpha>0
sufficiently small. Then we get

\displaystyle\alpha\langle z^{\ast},z^{\prime}-\bar{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq g(\bar{x},\bar{z}+\alpha(z^{\prime}-\bar{z}))-g(\bar{x},\bar{z})+\varepsilon\alpha(\|z^{\prime}-\bar{z}\|)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\left(g(\bar{x},z^{\prime})-g(\bar{x},\bar{z})+\varepsilon\|z^{\prime}-z\|\right),

where the latter inequality is due to the convexity of
g
with respect to
z
. Therefore, for all
\varepsilon>0
and
z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\langle z^{\ast},z^{\prime}-\bar{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq g(\bar{x},z^{\prime})-g(\bar{x},\bar{z})+\varepsilon\|z^{\prime}-z\|.

Since this holds for each
\varepsilon>0
, it tells us that
z^{\ast}\in\widehat{\partial}_{z}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
, and hence

(x^{\ast},z^{\ast})\in\widehat{\partial}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})\Longrightarrow z^{\ast}\in\widehat{\partial}_{z}g(\bar{x},\bar{z}).

To prove the last statement of the lemma, take any
(x^{\ast},z^{\ast})\in{\partial}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
and then find by (
4.29
) sequences
(x^{\ast}_{k},z_{k}^{\ast})\in\widehat{\partial}g(x_{k},z_{k})
with
(x_{k},z_{k},x^{\ast}_{k},z_{k}^{\ast})\overset{g}{\to}(\bar{x},\bar{z},x^{\ast},z^{\ast})
as
k\to\infty
. This gives us for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
that

\displaystyle\langle z_{k}^{\ast},z^{\prime}-z_{k}\rangle\leq(x_{k},z^{\prime})-g(x_{k},z_{k})\text{ whenever }z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}.

(4.30)

Choose
\gamma>0
such that for all
z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}(\bar{z})
the function
g(\cdot,z^{\prime})
is continuous at
\bar{x}
. Passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
in (
4.30
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\langle z^{\ast},z^{\prime}-\bar{z}\rangle\leq g(\bar{x},z^{\prime})-g(\bar{x},\bar{z})\text{ for all }z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}(\bar{z}),

which ensures by the assumed convexity of
g(\bar{x},\cdot)
that
z^{\ast}\in\partial_{z}g(\bar{x},\bar{z})
. This therefore completes the proof of the lemma.

7
Applications to problems of stochastic optimization

The concluding section of this paper addresses applications of the coderivative-based sensitivity analysis of general stochastic variational systems developed above to some important aspects of stochastic optimization. We split this section into two subsections. The first one deals with
stationary solution maps
for general problems of
constrained stochastic programming
, while in the second subsection we derive new coderivative-type
necessary optimality conditions
for problems of
stochastic programming with equilibrium constraints
.

7.1
Coderivatives of solution maps in stochastic programming

Given a normal integrand
f:T\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and an extended-real-valued l.s.c. function
\psi:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, consider the following class of
parametric stochastic programs
defined in the unconstrained format

\displaystyle\min\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\mathrm{E}_{f}(x,z)+\psi(x,z)

(7.1)

with the parameter
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Since
\psi
is an extended-real-valued function, the model in (
7.1
) implicitly encompasses problems of
constrained
stochastic programming defined in the form

\displaystyle\min\mathrm{E}_{f}(x,z)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ subject to }\;g(x,z)\in\mathcal{C},

(7.2)

where
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a closed set, and where
g\colon{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a continuous function. Indeed, the latter problem reduces to (
7.1
) with
\psi:=\delta_{\mathcal{C}}\circ g
. In particular, programs of type (
7.2
) include problems of
stochastic conic programming
, where
C
is a closed convex cone; see, e.g.,
bs
;
sdr
and the references therein.

Applying the subdifferential Fermat and sum rules from
m18
to local minimizers of (
7.1
) under appropriate qualification conditions and then using the basic subdifferential Leibniz rule from
mp3
for the expected-integral functional
\mathrm{E}_{f}
, we get a collection of stationary points of the stochastic programming problem (
7.1
) formalized as the
parametric stationary point map

\displaystyle S(x):=\Bigg\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\;\Bigg|\;0\in\int_{T}\partial_{z}f_{t}(x,z)\mu(dt)+\partial_{z}\psi(x,z)\Bigg\}.

(7.3)

Note that for
convex
stochastic programs (
7.1
), the stationary point map (
7.3
) equivalently describes the parametric set of
optimal solutions
to (
7.1
), which is not the case for general stochastic problems of our interest here. Observe furthermore that
(
mp3
, Proposition 7.1)
(see also
chp1
;
chp2
;
chp3
for related results) ensures under some additional assumptions that there exists
\eta>0
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mathrm{E}_{f}(x,z)=\int_{T}\partial_{z}f_{t}(x,z)\mu(dt),\text{ for all }(x,z)\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x},\bar{z}).

This tells us that (
7.3
) describes the set of
M-stationary points
for (
7.1
) that are important for various applications; see, e.g.,
hr
with other references.

To proceed with the study of the stationary point map (
7.3
) by reducing it to the stochastic variational system (
5.1
) investigated in Section
5
, we first formulate the following assumptions on the initial data of (
7.1
) ensuring the fulfillment of those in (
4.2
):
there exist
\eta>0
and
\kappa\in\textnormal{L}^{1}(T,\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}f_{t}(x,z)

\displaystyle\text{ is convex for all }(x,z)\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\text{ and }t\in T_{na},

(7.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}f_{t}(x,z)

\displaystyle\subseteq\kappa(t)\mathbb{B}\text{ for all }(x,z)\in\mathbb{B}_{\rho}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\text{ and all }t\in T,

where

signifies the nonatomic part of
\mu

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{f}(x,z,y):=\Bigg\{\mathpzc{y}\in\textnormal{L}^{1}(T,\mathbb{R}^{m})\;\Bigg|\;\int_{T}\mathpzc{y}(t)\mu(dt)=y\text{ and }\mathpzc{y}(t)\in\partial_{z}f_{t}(x,z)\text{ a.e.}\Bigg\}.

Our goal here is to present an efficient evaluation of the limiting coderivative of the stationary point map (
7.3
) in terms of its initial data. The obtained results allow us to make a conclusion on the Lipschitz-like property of (
7.3
), which we skip for brevity. Recall that the
subdifferential structure
of the mapping

According to Theorem
5.1

, while indicating the possibility of using also the integrable Lipschitz-like property (

for all

7.2
Stochastic programming with equilibrium constraints

Various versions of
stochastic mathematical programs with equilibrium constraints
(stochastic MPECs) have been formulated and investigated in the literature with numerous applications to practical models; see, e.g.,
hr
;
pw
;
sdr
and the references therein. the underlying feature of stochastic MPECs in the presence among constraints stochastic generalized equations of type (
5.1
) and their specifications, which can be interpreted as certain equilibrium conditions. In this subsection we consider a general class of such problems given by

\displaystyle\min\varphi(x,z)\text{ subject to }z\in S(x),\;x\in\mathcal{C},

(7.6)

where
S(x)
is a stochastic parametric variational system taken from (
5.1
) in the setting described therein, where

\varphi:T\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is an l.s.c. cost function, and where
{\cal C}
is a closed set. Our goal here is to derive
necessary optimality conditions
for such stochastic MPECs by using the basic machinery of variational analysis and the coderivative evaluation for the stochastic system (
5.1
) obtained in Section
5
.

To formulate desired necessary optimality conditions for the stochastic MPEC (
7.6
) in full generality, we need to recall yet another subdifferential construction for extended-real-valued function. Given

f\colon{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
finite at
x
, its
singular subdifferential
at this point is defined geometrically via the limiting normal cone (
2.2
) to the epigraph of
f
by

\partial^{\infty}f(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\big|\;(x^{*},0)\in N\big((x,f(x);\mbox{\rm epi}\,f\big)\big\}.

(7.7)

We refer the reader to the books
m06
;
m18
;
rw
and the bibliographies therein for various properties, analytic representations, and calculus rules for the singular subdifferential (
7.7
). Note that if
f
is l.s.c. around
x
, then
\partial^{\infty}f(x)=\{0\}

if and only if

f
is locally Lipschitzian around this point.

Now we are ready to establish the aforementioned necessary optimality conditions for the stochastic MPEC (
7.6
).

Let

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\ast}\\
z^{\ast}\end{array}\right)+D^{\ast}G(\bar{x},\bar{z},-\bar{y})(y^{\ast})+\int_{T}D^{\ast}\Phi_{t}\big(\bar{x},\bar{z},\mathpzc{y}(t)\big)(y^{\ast})\mu(dt)+\begin{pmatrix}N(\bar{x};\mathcal{C})\\

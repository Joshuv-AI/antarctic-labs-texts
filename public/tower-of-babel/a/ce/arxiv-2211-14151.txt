Double parton distributions in the nucleon on the lattice: Flavor interference effects
Daniel Reitinger

Abstract
Information about double parton distributions (DPDs) can be obtained by calculating four-point functions on the lattice. We continue our study on the first DPD Mellin moment of the unpolarized proton by considering interference effects w.r.t. the quark flavor. In our simulation we employ an n f = 2 + 1 n_{f}=2+1 ensemble with inverse coupling β = 3.4 \beta=3.4 , and pseudoscalar masses of m π = 355 ​ MeV m_{\pi}=355~\mathrm{MeV} and m K = 441 ​ MeV m_{K}=441~\mathrm{MeV} . The results are converted to the MS ¯ \overline{\mathrm{MS}} -scheme at the scale μ = 2 ​ GeV \mu=2~\mathrm{GeV} . We analyze the dependence of the considered Mellin moments on the quark polarization and compare our results with quark model predictions.

Introduction

In LHC experiments double parton scattering (DPS) contributions are non-negligible. Especially after the high-luminosity upgrade the sensitivity to them will be enhanced. In the last decade, significant progress has been made in studying the theory of DPS and of double parton distributions (DPDs), see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

The DPS contribution is proportional to the integral over the transverse distance
\boldsymbol{y}
of the two scattering quarks of a product of DPDs
F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})
:

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{y}\;F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})\,F_{b_{1}b_{2}}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y})\,,

(1)

where
x_{i}
denote the two longitudinal momentum fractions and
a_{i}
labels the quark polarization. Since DPDs are poorly known from experiments, as well as in theory, they are often approximated by:

\displaystyle F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle\overset{?}{=}f_{a_{1}}(x_{1})\,f_{a_{2}}(x_{2})\,G(\boldsymbol{y})\,.

(2)

f_{a_{i}}
are ordinary parton distribution functions (PDFs). This leads to the famous pocket formula
[
6
]
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{DPS},ij}=\frac{1}{C}\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{SPS},i}\ \sigma_{\mathrm{SPS},j}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,,

(3)

where
C
is a combinatorical factor and the effective cross section
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}
is introduced for dimensional consistency. From the assumption (
2
), one can derive that
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}
it is a fixed constant that does not depend on the DPS process.

Access from first principles to DPDs can be provided by lattice QCD simulations. In the past we published several studies for the case of the pion
[
7
]
, as well as for the proton
[
8
,
9
]
. One of our main results was the flavor dependence of the transverse distribution
G(\boldsymbol{y})
appearing in (
2
) in the case of the proton, so that (
3
) cannot be fulfilled.

In the current work we want to continue our research regarding DPDs on the lattice by extending our calculation on the contributions from flavor interference. These are usually considered to be suppressed.

2
Double parton distributions and two-current matrix elements

The definition of (collinear) DPDs for unpolarized protons is given by the following integral of proton matrix elements of two light-cone currents
[
4
]
:

\displaystyle F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})=2p^{+}\int\mathrm{d}y^{-}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}z^{-}_{1}}{2\pi}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z^{-}_{2}}{2\pi}\,

\displaystyle e^{i\mskip 1.5mu(x_{1}z_{1}^{-}+x_{2}z_{2}^{-})\mskip 1.5mup^{+}}

\displaystyle\times\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\lambda}\bra{p,\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{a_{1}}(y,z_{1})\,\mathcal{O}_{a_{2}}(0,z_{2})\ket{p,\lambda}\,,

(4)

including the helicity average
\sum_{\lambda}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda}
. This equation involves the light-cone operators:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{a}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\left.\bar{q}\left(y-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z\right)\,\Gamma_{a}\,q^{\prime}\left(y+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z\right)\right|_{z^{+}=y^{+}=0,\,\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{0}}\,,

(5)

where the Dirac matrix
\Gamma_{a}
selects the quark polarization

\displaystyle\Gamma_{(qq^{\prime})}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{+}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Delta(qq^{\prime})}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\delta(qq^{\prime})}^{j}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}i\sigma^{j+}\gamma_{5}\quad(j=1,2)\,,

(6)

corresponding to unpolarized, longitudinally polarized and transversely polarized quarks. Applying symmetry arguments, we can give a decomposition of the DPDs
F(x_{i},\boldsymbol{y})
in terms of transversely rotationally invariant functions
f(x_{i},y^{2})
. For flavor diagonal channels these are given by:

\displaystyle F_{q_{1}q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=f_{q_{1}q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2})\,,

\displaystyle F_{\Delta q_{1}\Delta q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=f_{\Delta q_{1}\Delta q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2})\,,

\displaystyle F_{\delta q_{1}q_{2}}^{j_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{j_{1}k}\boldsymbol{y}^{k}\,mf_{\delta q_{1}q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2})\,,

\displaystyle F_{q_{1}\delta q_{2}}^{j_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{j_{2}k}\boldsymbol{y}^{k}\,mf_{q_{1}\delta q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2})\,,

\displaystyle F_{\delta q_{1}\delta q_{2}}^{j_{1}j_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\delta^{j_{1}j_{2}}f_{\delta q_{1}\delta q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl(2\boldsymbol{y}^{j_{1}}\boldsymbol{y}^{j_{2}}-\delta^{j_{1}j_{2}}\boldsymbol{y}^{2}\bigr)\mskip 1.5mum^{2}f^{\mskip 1.5mut}_{\delta q_{1}\delta q_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2})\,.

(7)

This is the same for flavor interference. In that case, we indicate the quark flavors belonging to one operator by parentheses. For instance, we denote the unpolarized DPD for
ud
-interference as
F_{(du)(ud)}
and the corresponding longitudinally polarized DPD as
F_{\Delta(du)\Delta(ud)}
.

DPDs cannot be calculated directly on the lattice, since the operators (
5
) involve light-like quark field separations. Instead, we consider Mellin moments of DPDs, where the light-cone operators are reduced to local operators, which are well understood on the lattice. We denote the corresponding rotationally invariant Mellin moments as:

\displaystyle I_{a_{1}a_{2}}(y^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\;f_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2})\,.

(8)

The matrix elements to be calculated have the following form:

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\lambda}\bra{p,\lambda}J^{\mu_{1}\cdots}_{q_{1}q_{1}^{\prime},i_{1}}(y)\,J^{\mu_{2}\cdots}_{q_{2}q_{2}^{\prime},i_{2}}(0)\ket{p,\lambda}\,,

(9)

where we consider three kinds of local currents:

\displaystyle J_{qq^{\prime},V}^{\mu}(y)

\displaystyle=\bar{q}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\gamma^{\mu}\mskip 1.5muq^{\prime}(y)\,,

\displaystyle J_{qq^{\prime},A}^{\mu}(y)

\displaystyle=\bar{q}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,q^{\prime}(y)\,,

\displaystyle J_{qq^{\prime},T}^{\mu\nu}(y)

\displaystyle=\bar{q}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mskip 1.5muq^{\prime}(y)\,.

(10)

In analogy to (
2
) we can decompose the matrix elements (
9
) in terms of Lorentz invariant functions
A(py,y^{2})
,
B(py,y^{2})
,
\dots
and a suitable set of basis tensors, so that there is a one-to-one correspondence to the rotationally invariant DPDs. More details on these decompositions can be found in
[
8
]
. For the leading-twist contributions in (
2
) we can identify:

\displaystyle I_{a_{1}a_{2}}(y^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}(py)\,A_{a_{1}a_{2}}(py,y^{2})\,,

\displaystyle I^{t}_{\delta q\delta q^{\prime}}(y^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}(py)\,B_{\delta q\delta q^{\prime}}(py,y^{2})\,.

(11)

DPDs can be approximated in terms of GPDs by inserting a complete set of eigenstates between the two light-cone operators and assuming that the hadron state with lowest energy dominates. For flavor diagonal contributions this leads to a convolution of two impact parameter distributions:

\displaystyle F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle\overset{?}{=}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{b}\;f_{a_{1}}(x_{1},\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{y})\,f_{a_{2}}(x_{2},\boldsymbol{b})\,.

(12)

For the flavor interference DPD, we get an analogous expression, involving transition distributions
f_{ud}(x,\boldsymbol{y})
,
f_{du}(x,\boldsymbol{y})
, since the flavor changing operators turn the proton into a neutron. On the level of Lorentz invariant functions
A(py,y^{2})
the convolution turns into an integral of Pauli and Dirac form factors
F_{1,2}(t)
, which we shall consider later in this work.

3
Lattice simulations

In order to obtain the two-current matrix elements (
9
) on the lattice, we have to evaluate four-point functions, which we define as:

\displaystyle C^{ij,\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}_{\mathrm{4pt}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,t,\tau)

\displaystyle:=a^{6}\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu^{\prime},\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu}e^{-i\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu(\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu^{\prime}-\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu)}\ \left\langle\operatorname{tr}\left\{P_{+}\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu^{\prime},t)\ J_{i}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau)\ J_{j}(\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau)\ \overline{\mathcal{P}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu,0)\right\}\right\rangle\,,

(13)

where
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}
are the proton creation and annihilation operators and the sum over
\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu
,
\vec{\mskip 0.5muz}\mskip 1.5mu^{\prime}
together with the subsequent phase projects on a definite proton momentum. The two quark currents are placed on the same Euclidean time slice.
P_{+}
projects onto the required state with positive parity.

The local two-current matrix element itself is obtained by calculating the ratio of four-point functions and the proton two-point function:

\displaystyle 2V\sqrt{m^{2}+\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu^{2}}\left.\frac{C^{ij,\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}_{\mathrm{4pt}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,t,\tau)}{C^{\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}_{\mathrm{2pt}}(t)}\right|_{0\ll\tau\ll t}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\sum_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\bar{u}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(p)P_{+}u^{\lambda}(p)\ \bra{p,\lambda}J_{i}(y)\ J_{j}(0)\ket{p,\lambda^{\prime}}}{\sum_{\lambda}\bar{u}^{\lambda}(p)P_{+}u^{\lambda}(p)}\right|_{y^{0}=0}

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\lambda}\bra{p,\lambda}J_{i}(y)\,J_{j}(0)\ket{p,\lambda}\,,

(14)

5
Conclusions

We extended our previous analyses of DPDs of the nucleon on the lattice by calculating flavor interference contributions. It was observed that the size of these contributions is comparable to the flavor diagonal
dd
channel. Moreover, we considered ratios of DPDs for certain flavor combinations which can also be calculated within a
SU(6)
quark model. On the level of invariant functions, these ratios were compared to the corresponding data obtained on the lattice, where we observed poor agreement, in particular for small
y
. Finally, we checked (again on the level of invariant functions) to what extent the DPDs can be factorized in terms of nucleon form factors. Like for the flavor diagonal channel, we observed that the correct order of magnitude is predicted, however discrepancies are slightly larger. Our next step will be to study the continuum limit.

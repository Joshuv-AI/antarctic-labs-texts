1.
Introduction

In this paper we obtain, under some ergodicity assumptions on the involved transformations, about the multicorrelation sequence
I_{\vec{f},d}(n)
which is defined as follows. Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a probability space, let
T_{1},\dots,T_{d}:X\rightarrow X
be commuting, invertible, measure preserving transformations, and let
\vec{f}=(f_{0},\dots,f_{d})\in(L^{\infty}(\mu))^{(d+1)}
. Then we put

(1.1)

I_{\vec{f},d}(n):=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{n}f_{d}\ d\mu.

When
d=1
, Herglotz-Bochner’s theorem implies that the correlation sequence
I_{\vec{f},1}(n)
is given by the Fourier coefficients of some finite complex measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
K
]
and
[
KovN
]
). Decomposing
\sigma
into its atomic part,
\sigma_{a}
, and continuous part,
\sigma_{c}
, we get

(1.2)

I_{\vec{f},1}(n)=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\ d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{2\pi inx}\ d\sigma(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{2\pi inx}d\sigma_{a}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{2\pi inx}d\sigma_{c}(x)=\psi(n)+\nu(n).

In formula (
1.2
), the sequence
\psi(n)
is
almost periodic
, which, equivalently, means that there exists a compact abelian group
G
, a continuous function
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, and
a\in G
such that
\psi(n)=\phi(a^{n})
.

On the other hand, the sequence,
\nu(n)
is a nullsequence in the sense of the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
a:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a bounded sequence. We say that
a
is a
null-sequence
if

(1.3)

\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}|a(n)|^{2}=0.

It is natural to inquire whether for general

the multicorrelation sequence
I_{\vec{f},d}(n)
can be represented as a sum of a “generalized almost periodic sequence” and a nullsequence. A very satisfactory answer to this question was obtained by Bergelson, Host and Kra in
[
BHK
]
for the case
T_{i}=T^{i}
. Before formulating their result, we need to introduce some definitions pertaining to systems on nilmanifolds:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a Lie group. For a positive integer
d
, we denote by
G_{d}
the
d
-th commutator subgroup of
G
.

•

The group
G
is a
d
-
step nilpotent
Lie group if
G_{d+1}=\{e\}
.

•

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete cocompact subgroup of
G
. The quotient
X=G/\Gamma
is a
d
-step nilmanifold if
G
is a
d
-step nilpotent Lie group.

•

Let
X=G/\Gamma
be a
d
-step nilmanifold and
a\in G
. We call the map
T:X\rightarrow X
given by
T(g\Gamma)=(ag)\Gamma
a niltranslation.

•

Finally, let
X
be a
d
-step nilmanifold,
f\in C(X)
,
T
a niltranslation, and
x\in X
. The sequence
a(n)=f(T^{n}x)
is called a
d
-
step nilsequence
.

In
[
BHK
]
it was proved that

(1.4)

\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot Tf_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T^{d}f_{d}\ d\mu=a_{\textrm{st}}(n)+\nu(n),

where
a_{\textrm{st}}(n)
, the “structured part”, is a uniform limit of
d
-step nilsequences and
\nu(n)
is a nullsequence (see Theorem 1.9 in
[
BHK
]
).

In
[
Lei
]
, Leibman showed that a similar decomposition holds for multicorrelation sequences of the form
b(n)=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T^{p_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T^{p_{d}(n)}f_{d}\ d\mu
, where

. He proved that

can be decomposed as a sum of a uniform limit of
r
-step nilsequences and a nullsequence. (The number

.)

For general commuting transformations, the following result was proved by Frantzikinakis in
[

]
:

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
F1
]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a probability space and

be commuting measure preserving transformations of
X
. Let

and

in (

I_{\vec{f},d}(n)=a_{\textrm{st}}(n)+a_{\textrm{er}}(n),

where

is a uniform limit of

-step nilsequences, and

, the so-called error sequence, is a bounded sequence with

.

Juxtaposing equations (

[

, Host and Frantzikinakis posed the following question:

Problem 1.4
.

[Problem 1 in
[
FH
]
] Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a probability space, and let

be measure preserving transformations that commute with one another. Let

defined by

a(n):=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{n}f_{2}\ d\mu,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

can be decomposed as

where

is a uniform limit of

-step nilsequences and

Under some extra ergodicity asssumptions, we shall establish the following theorem as a partial answer to Question

:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a probability space, and

be commuting, invertible measure preserving transformations. Let

, and suppose that the transformations

It is sensible to ask whether Leibman’s decomposition for polynomial multicorrelation sequences generalizes to the case of commuting transformations. A result in this direction was provided by Host and Frantzikinakis in Theorem 2.5 in
[
FH
]
, where the authors showed that for any

, any polynomial multicorrelation sequence of the form

\tilde{a}(n):=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{p_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{p_{d}(n)}f_{d}\ d\mu

admits a decomposition

, where

is a uniform limit of
\ell

, and the error term satisfies

. While removal of
\varepsilon
in

in the appropriate generality remains a challenge, under certain ergodicity conditions, our methods can also be pushed to prove that the
\varepsilon
can be removed. Indeed, we will show

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a probability space and let
T_{1},\dots,T_{d}:X\rightarrow X
be measure preserving transformations. Assume that the measure preserving transformations
T_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{a_{d}}
are ergodic for all
(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\neq\vec{0}
. Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be polynomial functions such that
p_{i},p_{i}-p_{j}
are not constant for all

and for all

. Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then the sequence

a(n):=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{p_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{p_{k}(n)}f_{k}\ d\mu

is a sum of a uniform limit of
\ell=\ell(d,p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
-step nilsequences (
a_{\textrm{st}}
) and a nullsequence
(a_{\textrm{er}}
), where if

, we write

to mean

.

We remark that the number

depends on the number of applications of the van der Corput trick, essential in the PET induction introduced in
[

]
(see also
[

]
, where it is used for commuting transformations). It is worth point out that

and the highest degree of the polynomials in

The ergodicity assumptions enable us to use the results on characteristic factors for commuting transformations that was established in the work of Frantzikinakis and Kra
[
FK
]
. Their work provides an essential tool in establishing the desired decomposition of the multicorrelation sequence

. The main strategy of the proof builds, then, on previous work of Bergelson, Host and Kra in
[
BHK
]
and Leibman in
[

]
, adapted for commuting transformations.

Another purpose of this article is to prove two more results that showcase how natural extra ergodicity assumptions on a measure preserving system make “expected” results on “large intersections” hold. The first new result we establish on large intersections is the Theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a measure space and let
T_{1},T_{2}:X\rightarrow X
be invertible measure preserving transformations such that
T_{1},T_{2},T_{1}T_{2}^{-1}
are ergodic. Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
A\in\mathcal{B}
, the set

(1.8)

\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu(A\cap T_{1}^{-n}A\cap T_{2}^{-n}A)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\}

is syndetic (i.e., has bounded gaps in
\mathbb{Z}
).

Theorem
1.7
should be compared and contrasted with the results of Chu in
[
C
]
: Chu showed that for an arbitrary ergodic measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
one has that
\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu(A\cap T_{1}^{-n}A\cap T_{2}^{-n}A)>\mu(A)^{4}-\varepsilon\}
is syndetic, but that one cannot improve the exponent
4
to
3
in the general setting. In fact, more is true: the exponent
4
cannot be improved to any exponent strictly smaller than
4
(see Theorem 1.2 in
[
DS
]
). Theorem
1.7
gives some natural directional ergodicity conditions that allow to replace
\mu(A)^{4}
with
\mu(A)^{3}
.

For arbitrarily large
d
, one certainly cannot hope for the set
\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu(A\cap T_{1}^{-n}A\cap\dots\cap T_{d}^{-n}A)>\mu(A)^{d}-\varepsilon\}
to be syndetic even with the mild ergodicity conditions that make Theorem
1.5
work. Indeed, examples in the appendix with I. Rusza in
[
BHK
]
show that we can at most hope for such a result to hold if
d\leq 3
. Theorem 1.3 in
[
DS
]
shows that in fact, even
d=3
is too much to hope for, as they provide an example of a measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},T_{2},T_{3})
with each
T_{i}
ergodic and a set
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
\mu(A)>0
such that for every
\ell>0
,

(1.9)

\mu(A\cap T_{1}^{-n}A\cap T_{2}^{-n}A\cap T_{3}^{-n}A)<\mu(A)^{\ell},\textrm{ for all }n\neq 0,

so there is no more room for improvement in that direction.

To prove Theorem
1.7
we use the results on seminorms of Host
[
H
]
and the structural work in Frantzikinakis and Kra
[
FK
]
to establish a limiting formula (see Theorem
5.1
below), as well as a version of a trick of Frantzikinakis (
[
F2
]
, Proposition 5.1) which was employed in Section 8 of
[
BTZ
]
.

Lastly, we also prove a variant of Theorem
1.7
for shifted primes (i.e.
\mathbb{P}\pm 1
), using techniques from
[
L
]
,
[
FHK
]
and
[
DMLS
]
combined with our previous decomposition result, namely Theorem
1.5
. In the statements and proofs, we will stick to
\mathbb{P}-1
for convenience, but one easily checks that the same results hold for
\mathbb{P}+1
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system with
T,S,TS^{-1}
totally ergodic. Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
A\in\mathcal{B}
, the set

\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu(A\cap T^{-(p_{n}-1)}A\cap S^{-(p_{n}-1)}A)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\}

has positive lower density.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the necessary notation and terminology that will be used throughout the paper, and recall a few basic results from Ergodic Theory. In Section 3 we obtain some auxiliary results that naturally extend the previously known facts pertaining to a single transformation.

In Section 4 we give the proof of Theorem

and Theorem
1.8

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

2.1.
Basic facts from Ergodic Theory

Systems.
A measure preserving system is a tuple
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
, where
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
is a standard Lebesgue probability space (see Definition 2.3 in
[
W
]
), and
T_{1},\dots,T_{d}
are commuting invertible measure preserving selfmaps of
X
.

Factors.
A
homomorphism
from
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
to
(Y,\mathcal{C},\nu,S_{1},\dots,S_{d})
is a measurable map
\pi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
with
X^{\prime}
a
T_{i}
-invariant set of full measure and
Y^{\prime}
an
S
-invariant set of full measure (for all
i
), that intertwines
T_{i}
and
S_{i}
for all
i
. When such a map
\pi
exists, we say that
(Y,\mathcal{C},\nu,S_{1},\dots,S_{d})
is a factor of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
. A factor can be identified with the
T_{i}
-invariant (for all
i
) sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{Y})
, and one can show that any
T_{i}
-invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{B}
defines a factor. Alternatively, a factor can also be thought of as a
T_{i}
-invariant subalgebra
\mathcal{F}
of
L^{\infty}(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
. In the sequel we will make extensive use of the
Kronecker factor
of a system which is the smallest factor that makes all the
L^{2}(\mu)
-eigenfunctions measurable.

Conditional expectation.
If
\mathcal{Y}
is a
T
-invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{B}
and
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
, we write
\mathbb{E}[f|\mathcal{Y}]
for the conditional expectation of
f
with respect to
\mathcal{Y}
. We will make use of the identities

\int_{X}\mathbb{E}[f|\mathcal{Y}]\ d\mu=\int_{X}f\ d\mu\textrm{ and }T\mathbb{E}[f|\mathcal{Y}]=\mathbb{E}[Tf|\mathcal{Y}]\textrm{ (this equality holds for a.e. }x\in X).

Characteristic factors
Let
(F_{N})
be a Følner sequence in
\mathbb{Z}
(i.e. a sequence of finite sets of
\mathbb{Z}
with
\frac{|(F_{N}+k)\Delta F_{N}|}{|F_{N}|}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
) and let
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a family of essentially distinct polynomials (i.e., such that
p_{i}-p_{j}
is not constant for any
i\neq j
). We say that a sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{B}
is a characteristic factor of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
if for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}T_{p_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{p_{k}(n)}f_{k}-\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}T_{p_{1}(n)}\mathbb{E}[f_{1}|\mathcal{D}]\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{p_{k}(n)}\mathbb{E}[f_{k}|\mathcal{D}]\right\}=0,

where the equality takes place in
L^{2}(\mu)
. We recall here that throughout the paper, if
p_{r}(n)=(p_{r,1}(n),\dots,p_{r,d}(n))
, we write
T_{p_{r}(n)}f_{r}
to mean
\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{i}^{p_{r,i}(n)}f_{r}

Inverse limits.
We say that
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
is an
inverse limit sequence of factors

(X,\mathcal{B}_{j},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
if
(\mathcal{B}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of
T_{i}
-invariant (for all
i
)sub-
\sigma
-algebras such that
\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}_{j}=\mathcal{B}
(here
\bigvee_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}_{j}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the union of the
\mathcal{B}_{j}
) up to sets of measure zero.

Ergodicity and the ergodic decomposition.

Given a measure preserving transformation
T:X\rightarrow X
, we denote by
\mathcal{I}(T)=\{A\in\mathcal{B}:\mu(A\Delta T^{-1}A)=0\}
the algebra of invariant sets. We say that
T
is ergodic if any set in
\mathcal{I}(T)
has measure
0
or
1
. The pointwise ergodic theorem states that if
T
is ergodic, then for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)=\int_{X}f\ d\mu.

There exists a map
x\mapsto\mu_{x}
that is
\mathcal{I}(T)
-measurable and such that for every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
we have

\mathbb{E}[f|\mathcal{I}(T)](x)=\int_{X}f\ d\mu_{x},\textrm{ for }\mu\textrm{ almost every }x\in X.

The
ergodic decomposition
for the measure
\mu
is given as an integral of
\mu_{x}
with respect to a probability measure
\lambda
sitting on the space of extreme points of
T
-invariant measures, denoted by
\Omega

\mu:=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{\omega}\ d\lambda(\omega).

Moreover, the measures
\mu_{x}
are such that for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
, the system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu_{x},T)
is ergodic.

\bar{z},\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The factors
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
.
For every

, the factors

denote the invariant
\sigma
-algebras on

\textrm{ for }f\in L^{\infty}(\mu),\ \mathbb{E}[f|\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}]=0\textrm{ if and only if }|||f|||_{k}=0.

See

2.3.
Structure theory and nilsystems

A
nilmanifold
is a homogeneous space
X=G/\Gamma
where
G
is a nilpotent Lie group, and
\Gamma
is a discrete cocompact subgroup of
G
. Let
G_{k}
be the
k
-th commutator subgroup of
G
. If
G_{k+1}=\{e\}
, we say that
G
is a
k
-step nilpotent Lie group. We say that
X=G/\Gamma
is a
k
-
step nilmanifold
if
G
is a
k
-step nilpotent Lie group.

A
k
-step nilpotent Lie group
G
acts on
G/\Gamma
by left translations, where the translation by a fixed element
a\in G
is given by
T_{a}(g\Gamma)=(ag)\Gamma
. By
m_{X}
we denote the unique probability measure on
X
that is invariant under the action of
G
by left translations, and by
\mathcal{G}/\Gamma
we denote the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
G/\Gamma
. Fixing an element
a\in G
, we call the measure preserving system
(G/\Gamma,\mathcal{G}/\Gamma,m_{X},T_{a})
a
k
-
step nilsystem
.

If
X=G/\Gamma
is a
k
-step nilmanifold,
a\in G
,
x\in X
, and
f\in C(X)
, we call the sequence
(f(a^{n}x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a
k
-
step nilsequence
. We caution the reader that it is possible to find slightly different usage of the terminology we just presented in the literature for nilsequences. Regarding the previously introduced nullsequences, we will use the following equivalent formulations:
First, since
a
is bounded, it follows that the square in (
1.3
) is immaterial, so
a
is a nullsequence if and only if

\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}|a(n)|=0.

Second, an equivalent condition to (
1.3
) is

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}|a(n)|=0

for all Følner sequences
(F_{N})
in
\mathbb{Z}
.

We will need later the following fundamental result of Host and Kra:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
HK1
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be an ergodic measure preserving system and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{Z}_{k},\mu,T)
is a (measure theoretic) inverse limit of
k
-step nilsystems.

2.4.
Gowers seminorms and
o
-notation

We will use below the notation
o_{N\to\infty,a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}(1)
, which denotes a quantity depending on
N,a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
that goes to
0
as
N\to\infty
if we fix the quantities
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
. This convergence is assumed to be uniform on all other quantities that do not explicitly appear in the subindex.

In order to define the Gowers seminorms, we find it convenient to use expectation on finite sets as follows: if
A
is a finite set, the expectation of a function
f:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is defined as the following average:

\mathbb{E}_{n\in A}f(n)=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{n\in A}f(n).

The Gowers seminorms of
f:\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are inductively defined as follows:

||f||_{U_{1}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})}:=|\mathbb{E}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}f(n)|

and

||f||_{U_{d+1}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})}:=\left(\mathbb{E}_{h\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}||f_{h}\cdot\bar{f}||_{U_{d}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})}^{2^{d}}\right)^{1/2^{d+1}},

where
f_{h}(n):=f(n+h)
for all
h\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. Gowers showed in
[
G
]
that
||\cdot||_{U_{d}}
is a seminorm for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

3.
Technical results

In this section we introduce a natural variant of Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms for the case of commuting transformations and establish some basic facts which will be needed for the proofs in subsequent sections.

We begin by reviewing a few key concepts that appeared first in Host’s article
[

in an abridged form.

3.1.
Notation

We will assume all functions in the following subsections to be measurable and complex-valued. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the points in
X^{2^{k}}
are written

x=(x_{\varepsilon}:\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}).

Our convention is to write elements in
\{0,1\}^{k}
without commas and parentheses, so that we can append a

1

\{0,1\}^{k+1}
.
If

are functions on

by

\left(\bigotimes_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}f_{\varepsilon}\right)(x):=\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}f_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}).

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and put

. For
1\leq i\leq d
, let
T_{i}^{\Delta}=T_{i}\times\dots\times T_{i}
, the diagonal transformation of

(T_{i}^{\Delta}x)_{\varepsilon}=T_{i}x_{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}

The

T_{i}^{*}
of

are given by

\textrm{ for every }\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d},\quad(T_{i}^{*}x)_{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}T_{i}x_{\varepsilon}&\textrm{ if }\varepsilon_{i}=0;\\
x_{\varepsilon}&\textrm{ if }\varepsilon_{i}=1.\end{cases}

3.2.
The box measure and its associated seminorm

The box measure
\mu^{*}
on
X^{*}
is constructed inductively as follows. First, define a measure on
X\times X
by

\mu_{T_{1}}:=\mu\times_{\mathcal{I}(T_{1})}\mu,

and then, for all
k\leq d-1
, define the measures
\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{k+1}}
on
X^{2^{k+1}}
inductively by

\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{k+1}}=\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}}\times_{\mathcal{I}(T_{k+1}^{[k]})}\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{k}}.

Finally, set
\mu^{*}=\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}
on
X^{*}
. One can check that
\mu^{*}
is invariant under the diagonal and side transformations of
X^{*}
as defined above.

The ergodic theorem and downwards induction imply the following result:

Lemma 3.1

(see
[
H
]
)
.

Let
f_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}
be functions in
L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then,

\int\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\mathcal{C}^{|\varepsilon|}(f_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}))\ d\mu^{*}=\lim_{N_{d}\to\infty}\frac{1}{|I_{N_{d}}|}\sum_{n_{d}\in I_{N_{d}}}\dots\lim_{N_{1}\to\infty}\frac{1}{|I_{N_{1}}|}\sum_{n_{1}\in I_{N_{1}}}\int\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\mathcal{C}^{|\varepsilon|}(T_{1}^{(1-\varepsilon_{1})n_{1}}\dots T_{d}^{(1-\varepsilon_{d})n_{d}}f_{\varepsilon})\ d\mu.

for any sequences of intervals
(I_{N_{j}})_{N_{j}\in\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq j\leq d
with lengths going to infinity.

Host then shows that for every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
one has

(3.1)

\int\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\mathcal{C}^{|\varepsilon|}(f(x_{\varepsilon}))\ d\mu^{*}\geq 0.

Alternatively, one can show the inequality (
3.1
) using an argument similar to that in the proof of Theorem 0.5 in
[
BL2
]
. The inequality (
3.1
) then allows the definition

Definition 3.2
.

For
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
let

|||f|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}:=\left(\int\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\mathcal{C}^{|\varepsilon|}(f(x_{\varepsilon}))\ d\mu^{*}\right)^{1/2^{d}}.

It is easily checked that

|||f|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}^{2^{d}}=\lim_{N_{d}\to\infty}\frac{1}{|I_{N_{d}}|}\sum_{n\in I_{N_{d}}}|||T_{d}^{n}f\cdot\bar{f}|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d-1}}^{2^{d-1}},

where
(I_{N_{d}})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is any Følner sequence in
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 3.3

(Host
[
H
]
)
.

(i)

For
f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
for all
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}
we have

\left|\int\bigotimes_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\mathcal{C}^{|\varepsilon|}f_{\varepsilon}\ d\mu^{*}\right|\leq\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}|||f_{\varepsilon}|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}},

(ii)

|||\cdot|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}
is a seminorm on
L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

3.3.
Technical results on seminorms for commuting transformations

In this subsection we formulate technical results which will be needed in the sequel:

Proposition 3.4

(Host
[
H
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,S_{1},\dots,S_{d})
be a measure preserving system. Let
T_{1}=S_{1}
and
T_{i}=S_{1}S_{i}^{-1}
for
2\leq i\leq d
. Then, for every
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
||f_{i}||_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq 1
for
2\leq i\leq d
we have

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}S_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot S_{d}^{n}f_{d}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq|||f_{1}|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}},

for every Følner sequence
(F_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 3.5

(Corollary 3
[
H
]
)
.

The seminorm
|||\cdot|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}
remains unchanged if the transformations
T_{1},\dots,T_{d}
are permuted.

The following lemma is given for complex valued functions, as the argument does not require significant changes:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
be a measure preserving system. Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then,

|||f\otimes\bar{f}|||_{T_{1}\times T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\times T_{d}}\leq|||f|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d},T_{i}}^{2},

for all
1\leq i\leq d
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
d=1
. We are going to show that

|||f\otimes\bar{f}|||_{T_{1}\times T_{1}}\leq|||f|||_{T_{1},T_{1}}^{2}
. Consider the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
with respect to
T_{1}
:

(3.2)

\mu=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{\omega}\ d\lambda(\omega).

We have

|||f\otimes\bar{f}|||_{T_{1}\times T_{1}}^{2}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{X^{2}}(T_{1}^{n}f\otimes T_{1}^{n}\bar{f})\bar{f}\otimes f\ d\mu\ d\mu=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\int_{X}\bar{f}T_{1}^{n}f\ d\mu\right|^{2}=

(using (
3.2
))

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{X}\bar{f}T_{1}^{n}f\ d\mu_{\omega}\ d\lambda\right|^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\int_{X}\bar{f}T_{1}^{n}f\ d\mu_{\omega}\right|^{2}\ d\lambda,

where we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality first, then linearity of
\int_{\Omega}
and the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Next, rewriting the expression in the right hand side of the formula above we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{X^{2}}(T_{1}^{n}f\otimes T_{1}^{n}\bar{f})\bar{f}\otimes f\ d\mu_{\omega}\ d\mu_{\omega}\ d\lambda

which equals

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}\int_{X^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\omega}\otimes\mu_{\omega}}[f\otimes\bar{f}\mid\mathcal{I}(T_{1}\times T_{1})\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\omega}\otimes\mu_{\omega}}[\bar{f}\otimes f\mid\mathcal{I}(T_{1}\times T_{1})]\ d\mu_{\omega}\ d\mu_{\omega}\ d\lambda

(using the ergodic theorem, and properties of conditional expectation). Finally, we notice that since for a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,
\mu_{\omega}
is ergodic, then, the definition of the seminorms simplifies a little bit, so by Proposition 18 in Chapter 8 of
[
HK2
]
we can rewrite equation (
3.3
) as

\int_{\Omega}|||f|||_{\mu_{\omega},T_{1},T_{1}}^{4}\ d\lambda=|||f|||_{T_{1},T_{1}}^{4}.

So we showed
|||f\otimes\bar{f}|||^{2}_{T_{1}\times T_{1}}\leq|||f|||_{T_{1},T_{1}}^{4}
. Taking square roots completes the proof of the base case.

So now suppose that the result in question holds for
d=d_{0}\geq 1
, and consider

|||f\otimes\bar{f}|||_{T_{1}\times T_{1},\dots,T_{d_{0}+1}\times T_{d_{0}+1}}^{2^{d_{0}+1}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|||T_{d_{0}+1}^{n}f\cdot\bar{f}\otimes T_{d_{0}+1}^{n}\bar{f}\cdot f|||^{2^{d_{0}}}_{T_{1}\times T_{1},\dots,T_{d_{0}}\times T_{d_{0}}}\leq

(using the inductive hypothesis)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|||T_{d_{0}+1}^{n}f\cdot\bar{f}|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d_{0}},T_{i}}^{2^{d_{0}+1}}=|||f|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d_{0}},T_{i},T_{d_{0}+1}}^{2^{d_{0}+2}}

Finally, note that seminorms for commuting transformations do not depend on the order of the transformations involved (see Corollary 3 in
[
H
]
), so that permuting them in the expression above, gives the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
be a measure preserving system. Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Suppose that the measure preserving transformations
T_{1},\dots,T_{d}
are ergodic. Then, we have

(3.4)

|||f|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}=|||f|||_{\underbrace{T_{i},T_{i},\dots,T_{i}}_{d}},

for all
1\leq i\leq d
.

Proof.

First, notice that by Theorem
3.5
, it suffices to show this result in the case where
i=1
, and then use symmetry, so let
i=1
. Next, to show (
3.4
) it suffices to prove that
\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}=\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{1}}
. Recall that the measures
\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}
are defined inductively so that

\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}=\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d-1}}\times_{\mathcal{I}(T_{d}^{[d-1]})}\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d-1}},

(see the subsection on the construction of
\mu^{*}
) so we can change
\mathcal{I}(T_{d}\times\dots\times T_{d})
for
\mathcal{I}(T_{1}\times\dots\times T_{1})
(see Proposition 29 of Chapter 8 in
[
HK2
]
), so we get

\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}=\mu_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d-1},T_{1}}.

From this, it follows that
|||f|||_{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}}=|||f|||_{T_{1},T_{1},\dots,T_{d-1}}
.

Repeating this argument using our ergodicity assumptions, we are allowed to change all the other transformations for
T_{1}
, which implies the result.
∎

Next, we give an essential theorem that characterizes the
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
factors.

Definition 3.8
.

We say that a measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
is

if it is isomorphic to
(G/\Gamma,\textrm{Borel}(G/\Gamma),\mu_{\textrm{Haar}},T_{a_{1}},\dots,T_{a_{d}})

is a

a cocompact subgroup, and the transformations

act by ergodic niltranslations by commuting elements

Definition 3.9
.

We say that a measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
is
of order

if

and each

is ergodic. (Note that the notion

is independent of the transformations

because of the ergodicity assumptions made on

Theorem 3.10

(Frantzikinakis-Kra
[

]
)

Let

be a measure preserving system of order

of toral systems of order

. Moreover, these toral systems of order

We note that the sequence of factors in Theorem

as the number of commuting transformations involved do not change them.

Let

4.
Removal of
\varepsilon
under some ergodicity assumptions

We now move to the proof of Theorem
1.5
and its generalization to polynomial multicorrelation sequences, which we state here again for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
a measure preserving system. Let
f_{0},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, and suppose that the transformations
T_{1},\dots,T_{d}
and

are ergodic (for

). Then, the multicorrelation sequence

a(n):=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{n}f_{d}\ d\mu

can be decomposed as a sum of a uniform limit of
d
-step nilsequences (
a_{\textrm{st}}
) and a nullsequence (
a_{\textrm{er}}
).

Proof.

We follow and adapt the proof strategy in Section
3
of Leibman’s paper
[
Lei
]
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
||f_{i}||_{\infty}\leq 1
for all
i
. Let
\Phi_{N}
be a Følner sequence in
\mathbb{Z}

3.4
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the fact that
||f_{0}||_{\infty}\leq 1

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}\left|\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{n}f_{d}\ d\mu\right|^{2}=

(4.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}\int_{X^{2}}f_{0}\otimes\bar{f_{0}}\cdot(T_{1}\times T_{1})^{n}(f_{1}\otimes\bar{f_{1}})\cdot\dotso\cdot(T_{d}\times T_{d})^{n}(f_{d}\otimes\bar{f_{d}})\ d\mu\ d\mu\leq\\
\lim_{N\to\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}(T_{1}\times T_{1})^{n}(f_{1}\otimes\bar{f_{1}})\cdot\dotso\cdot(T_{d}\times T_{d})^{n}(f_{d}\otimes\bar{f_{d}})\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq|||f_{i}\otimes\bar{f_{i}}|||_{T_{i}\times T_{i},((T_{i}\times T_{i})^{-1}(T_{j}\times T_{j}))_{i\neq j}},

for all

. Thus, given our ergodicity assumptions, and using Lemma

|||f_{i}|||_{T_{i},T_{i},(T_{i}^{-1}T_{j})_{i\neq j}}^{2}=|||f_{i}|||_{T_{i},\dots,T_{i}}^{2}=|||f_{i}|||_{d+1,T_{i}}^{2}.

Therefore, for
1\leq i\leq d
we have

(4.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}\int_{X^{2}}f_{0}\otimes\bar{f_{0}}\cdot(T_{1}\times T_{1})^{n}(f_{1}\otimes\bar{f_{1}})\cdot\dotso\cdot(T_{d}\times T_{d})^{n}(f_{d}\otimes\bar{f_{d}})\ d\mu\ d\mu\leq|||f_{i}|||_{d+1,T_{i}}^{2}

The bound (
4.2
) and Theorem
3.10
imply that the sequence

(4.3)

a(n)-\int_{Z_{d}(X)}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[f_{1}\mid Z_{d}(X)]\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{n}\mathbb{E}[f_{k}\mid Z_{d}(X)]\ d\mu_{Z_{d}(X)}

is a null-sequence.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By Theorem
3.10
,
Z_{d}(X)=(X_{d},\textrm{Borel}(X_{d}),\mu_{X_{d}},T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
is an inverse limit of nilsystems. Thus, there exists a factor of
Z_{d}(X)
with the structure of a

-step nilsystem
(\tilde{X},\textrm{Borel}(\tilde{X}),\mu_{\tilde{X}},T_{1},\dots,T_{d})

acts by the niltranslation by an element
a_{i}\in\tilde{X}
, such that for
\tilde{f_{i}}=\mathbb{E}[f_{i}\mid\tilde{X}]
we have

\left|\int_{X_{d}}f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[f_{1}\mid Z_{d}(X)]\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{n}\mathbb{E}[f_{d}\mid Z_{d}(X)]\ d\mu_{X_{d}}-\int_{\tilde{X}}\tilde{f_{0}}\cdot a_{1}^{n}\tilde{f_{1}}\cdot\dotso\cdot a_{d}^{n}\tilde{f_{k}}\ d\mu_{\tilde{X}}\right|<\varepsilon

for all

. Therefore, there exists a nullsequence
\lambda
such that

(4.4)

\left|\left|a(n)-\left(\int_{\tilde{X}}\tilde{f_{0}}\cdot a_{1}^{n}\tilde{f_{1}}\cdot\dotso\cdot a_{d}^{n}\tilde{f_{k}}\ d\mu_{\tilde{X}}+\lambda(n)\right)\right|\right|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}<\varepsilon.

A standard approximation argument allows us to assume without loss of generality that
\tilde{f_{1}},\dots,\tilde{f_{d}}\in C(\tilde{X})
in (
4.4
). Applying Theorem 2.5 in
[
Lei
]
to the nilmanifold
\tilde{X}^{k}
, the diagonal subnilmanifold
\{(x,\dots,x):x\in\tilde{X}\}
, the linear polynomial sequence
(a_{1}^{n},\dots,a_{d}^{n})
and the function
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\tilde{f_{1}}(x_{1})\cdot\dotso\cdot\tilde{f_{d}}(x_{d})\in C(\tilde{X}^{k})
, we obtain that the sequence

\int_{\tilde{X}}\tilde{f_{0}}\cdot a_{1}^{n}\tilde{f_{1}}\cdot\dotso\cdot a_{d}^{n}\tilde{f_{k}}\ d\mu_{\tilde{X}}

is a sum of a
d
-step nilsequence and a nullsequence.

Therefore, for each
\varepsilon>0
we can find a
d
-step nilsequence
\psi
, a nullsequence
\lambda
and a bounded sequence
\delta
with
||\delta||_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\varepsilon
such that

(4.5)

a(n)=\psi(n)+\lambda(n)+\delta(n).

For each
l\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the decomposition
a=\psi_{l}+\lambda_{l}+\delta_{l}
, where
||\delta_{l}||_{\ell^{\infty}}<\frac{1}{l}
. For
r\neq l
, we have

(4.6)

|\psi_{l}(n)-\psi_{r}(n)|=|(\lambda_{l}(n)-\lambda_{r}(n))+(\delta_{l}(n)-\delta_{r}(n))|.

Now,
\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M}^{N}|\lambda_{l}(n)-\lambda_{r}(n)|=0
and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\delta_{r}(n)-\delta_{l}(n)|\leq\frac{1}{l}+\frac{1}{r}
. Therefore,

(4.7)

|\psi_{l}(n)-\psi_{r}(n)|\leq\frac{1}{l}+\frac{1}{r}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
except potentially a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
with
\mathbb{1}_{A}(n)
a nullsequence. For each
l,r\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
\psi_{l}(n)-\psi_{r}(n)
is a nilsequence, so it follows that inequality (
4.7
) must, in fact, hold for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, the sequence
(\psi_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
that consists of
d
-step nilsequences, and since we already showed that
(\delta_{r})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
converging to a nullsequence, we are done.
∎

The following theorem extends Theorem
4.1
to more general polynomial multicorrelation sequences, at the cost of more stringent ergodicity assumptions on the measure preserving system:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
be a measure preserving system. Assume that the measure preserving transformations
T_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{d}^{a_{d}}
are ergodic for all
(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\neq\vec{0}
. Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be polynomial functions such that
p_{i},p_{i}-p_{j}
are not constant for all
i
and for all
j\neq i
. Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then the sequence

a(n):=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T_{p_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot T_{p_{k}(n)}f_{k}\ d\mu

is a sum of a uniform limit of
\ell=\ell(d,p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
-step nilsequences (
a_{\textrm{st}}
) and a nullsequence
(a_{\textrm{er}}
).

Proof.

We can argue similarly to the proof of Theorem
4.1
. First, we reduce to the case (without loss of generality) where
||f_{i}||_{\infty}\leq 1
for all
i
. Then, observe that for any Følner sequence
(F_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(4.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}|a(n)|^{2}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\int_{X^{2}}f_{0}\otimes\bar{f_{0}}\cdot(T_{p_{1}(n)}\times T_{p_{1}(n)})f_{1}\otimes\bar{f_{1}}\cdot\dotso\cdot(T_{p_{k}(n)}\times T_{p_{k}(n)})f_{k}\otimes\bar{f_{k}}\ d\mu\ d\mu\\
\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}(T_{p_{1}(n)}\times T_{p_{1}(n)})f_{1}\otimes\bar{f_{1}}\cdot\dotso\cdot(T_{p_{k}(n)}\times T_{p_{k}(n)})f_{k}\otimes\bar{f_{k}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

By Theorem
3.11
, the last limit in (
4.8
) is bounded by a seminorm of the form
|||f_{i}\otimes\bar{f_{i}}|||_{\underbrace{T_{i}\times T_{i},\dots,T_{i}\times T_{i}}_{r(i)}}\leq|||f_{i}|||^{2}_{\underbrace{T_{i},\dots,T_{i}}_{r(i)+1}}
, for some
r_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, by Theorem
3.11
. Therefore, there is a common
\ell=\ell(d,p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
, big enough, such that if any of the norms
|||f_{i}|||_{\ell}=0
, then the averages (
4.8
) converge to
0
. (These seminorms are well defined because of our extra ergodicity assumptions).

This implies that we can proceed as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
but now using the factor
Z_{\ell}(X)
instead of
Z_{d}(X)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists an
\ell
-step nilsystem
\tilde{X}
, a factor of
X
, on which the transformations
T_{i}
act by niltranslations by commuting elements
a_{i}\in\tilde{X}
, there exist
\tilde{f_{0}},\tilde{f_{1}},\dots,\tilde{f_{k}}\in C(\tilde{X})
and a nullsequence
\lambda\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

\left|a(n)-\left(\lambda(n)+\int_{\tilde{X}}\tilde{f_{0}}\cdot\vec{a}_{p_{1}(n)}\tilde{f}_{1}\cdot\dotso\cdot\vec{a}_{p_{k}(n)}\tilde{f}_{k}\ d\mu_{\tilde{X}}\right)\right|<\varepsilon,

where
\vec{a}_{p_{i}(n)}=a_{1}^{p_{1,i}}(n)\cdot\dotso\cdot a_{d}^{p_{d,i}(n)}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

From this point, we proceed exactly as in the remainder of the proof of Theorem
4.1
, simply changing the polynomial sequence from
(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})^{n}
to
(\vec{a}_{p_{1}(n)},\dots,\vec{a}_{p_{k}(n)})
, when applying Theorem 2.5 of
[
Lei
]
. We are done.
∎

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem

.

5.1.
Large “linear” returns

We begin with the following Theorem, which establishes a limit formula for averages of two commuting transformations with some ergodicity assumptions:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system such that
T,S,TS^{-1}
are ergodic. Let
Z
be a compact abelian group such that the Kronecker factor of
X
, denoted by
Z_{1}(X)
, is isomorphic to
(Z,\textrm{Borel}(Z),\mu_{\textrm{Haar}},T,S)
. Take
\alpha,\beta\in Z
so that the map
R_{\alpha}z:=z+\alpha
on
Z
corresponds to the action of
T
on
Z
, and the map
R_{\beta}z:=z+\beta
corresponds to the action of
S
on
Z
. Let
Y_{T,S}:=\overline{\{(n\alpha,n\beta):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}}
, and denote by
\nu_{Y_{T,S}}
the Haar probability measure on
Y
. Then, for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and any Følner sequence
(F_{N})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
we have

(5.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}T^{n}f_{1}S^{n}f_{2}=\int_{Y_{T,S}}\tilde{f_{1}}(z+u)\tilde{f_{2}}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v)\textrm{ (with respect to the }L^{2}(\mu)\textrm{-norm)},

where
\tilde{f_{i}}=\mathbb{E}[f_{i}|Z_{1}(X)]
is the conditional expectation of
f_{i}
onto
Z_{1}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
(F_{N})
be a Følner sequence in
\mathbb{Z}
. We consider the expression on the left hand side of (
5.1
). By Proposition
3.4
, together with Lemma
3.7
, we have

(5.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}T^{n}f_{1}S^{n}f_{2}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq|||f_{i}|||_{T_{i},T_{i}},

for
i=1,2
, where we put
T_{1}=T,T_{2}=S
. In particular, the structure theory afforded by Theorem
3.10

it is bounded by
\min\{|||f_{1}|||_{2},|||f_{2}|||_{2}\}
(the seminorms agree, independently of the chosen transformations). Thus, since
|||f_{i}|||_{2}=0
if and only if
\mathbb{E}[f_{i}|Z_{1}(X)]=0
, it follows that

(5.3)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}T^{n}f_{1}(x)S^{n}f_{2}(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\tilde{f_{1}}(z+n\alpha)\tilde{f_{2}}(z+n\beta),

with respect to the
L^{2}(\mu)
-norm, and where the averages on the right hand side of (
5.3
) take place in the factor
Z_{1}(X)
(in other words, the map
x\mapsto z
denotes the factor map from
X
to
Z
). Now, by the ergodic theorem applied to the ergodic action
(z,w)\mapsto(z+\alpha,w+\beta)
defined on
Y_{T,S}
we get the following
L^{2}(\mu)
limit formula:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}F(z_{1}+n\alpha,z_{2}+n\beta)=\int_{Y_{T,S}}F(u,v)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v),

for all
(z_{1},z_{2})\in Z^{2}
, and all
F\in L^{2}(Y_{T,S})
. Thus, (
5.1
) follows by setting
F=\tilde{f_{1}}\otimes\tilde{f_{2}}
.
∎

We will use Theorem
5.1
along with the notation introduced therein to show the next lemma:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system with
T,S,TS^{-1}
ergodic. Let
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
(F_{N})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be a Følner sequence. Then, for every continuous function
\eta:Y_{T,S}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
we have

(5.4)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\eta(n\alpha,n\beta)\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T^{n}f_{1}\cdot S^{n}f_{2}\ d\mu=\int_{Z}\int_{Y_{T,S}}\eta(u,v)f_{0}(z)\tilde{f_{1}}(z+u)\tilde{f_{2}}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v)\ d\nu_{Z}(z),

where
\tilde{f_{i}}(z)=\mathbb{E}[f_{i}|Z_{1}(X)](z)
is the projection onto the Kronecker factor.

Proof.

We begin by observing that since
Y_{T,S}
is closed, we can extend
\eta
to a continuous map
\eta_{0}:Z^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Now, by a standard approximation argument using Stone-Weierstrass’ theorem, it is enough to show the result for
\eta_{0}(u,v)=\chi_{1}(u)\chi_{2}(v)
, where
\chi_{i}
is a character on
Z
. Now put
g_{0}(x):=\overline{\chi_{1}(z)\chi_{2}(z)}f_{0}(x)
,
g_{1}(x):=\chi_{1}(z)f_{1}(x)
and
g_{2}(x):=\chi_{2}(z)f_{2}(x)
with the understanding that we write
z
for the projection of
x
onto
Z
.

By Theorem
5.1
, we can evaluate the limit of the averages in question:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\eta(n\alpha,n\beta)\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T^{n}f_{1}\cdot S^{n}f_{2}\ d\mu=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\eta_{0}(n\alpha,n\beta)\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T^{n}f_{1}\cdot S^{n}f_{2}\ d\mu=

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\int_{X}g_{0}\cdot T^{n}g_{1}\cdot S^{n}g_{2}\ d\mu=\int_{X}g_{0}(x)\int_{Y_{T,S}}\tilde{g_{1}}(z+u)\tilde{g_{2}}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v)\ d\mu(x)=

\int_{Z}\tilde{g_{0}}(z)\int_{Y_{T,S}}\tilde{g_{1}}(z+u)\tilde{g_{2}}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v)\ d\nu_{Z}(z)=

\int_{Z}\int_{Y_{T,S}}\eta_{0}(u,v)\tilde{f_{0}}(z)\tilde{f_{1}}(z+u)\tilde{f_{2}}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Z}(z)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v)=

\int_{Z}\int_{Y_{T,S}}\eta(u,v)\tilde{f_{0}}(z)\tilde{f_{1}}(z+u)\tilde{f_{2}}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Z}(z)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v),

as desired.
∎

With this we can now prove Theorem
1.7
:

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system such that
T,S,TS^{-1}
are ergodic. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
A\in\mathcal{B}
. Then, the set

(5.5)

\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\}

is syndetic.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction, so assume that the set (
5.5
) is not syndetic. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
,
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
\mu(A)>0
and we can find a Følner sequence
(F_{N})
such that

(5.6)

\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A)\leq\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon

for all
n\in\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}F_{N}
. Let
f=\mathbb{1}_{A}
and write
\tilde{f}
, for the projection of
f
onto the Kronecker factor of
X
. We know that
0\leq\tilde{f}\leq 1
, so by Jensen’s inequality we have

(5.7)

\int_{Z}\tilde{f}(z)\tilde{f}(z)\tilde{f}(z)\ d\nu_{Z}(z)\geq\left(\int_{Z}\tilde{f}(z)\ d\nu_{Z}(z)\right)^{3}=\mu(A)^{3}.

Consequently, for any
(u,v)
in a small neighborhood of the identity of
Z^{2}
, we have that

(5.8)

\int_{Z}\tilde{f}(z)\tilde{f}(z+u)\tilde{f}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Z}(z)\geq\mu(A)^{3}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By Urysohn’s lemma we can find a continuous function
\eta:Y_{T,S}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
\int_{Y_{T,S}}\eta\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}=1
satisfying

(5.9)

\int_{Y_{T,S}}\int_{Z}\eta(u,v)\tilde{f}(z)\tilde{f}(z+u)\tilde{f}(z+v)\ d\nu_{Z}(z)\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}(u,v)\geq\mu(A)^{3}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Now, Lemma
5.2
and (
5.9
) give

(5.10)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\eta(n\alpha,n\beta)\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A)\geq\mu(A)^{3}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

But (
5.6
) implies that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\eta(n\alpha,n\beta)\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A)\leq(\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon)\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{n\in F_{N}}\eta(n\alpha,n\beta)=

(\mu^{3}(A)-\varepsilon)\int_{Y_{T,S}}\eta\ d\nu_{Y_{T,S}}=\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon,

a contradiction.
∎

5.2.
Large returns along shifted primes

In this subsection we will establish Theorem
1.8
. We begin by introducing the
von Mangoldt function

\Lambda:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, given by

\Lambda(n):=\begin{cases}\log p,\textrm{ if }n=p^{m},\textrm{ for some }m\in\mathbb{N},p\in\mathbb{P}\\
0\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
w\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. Put
W:=\prod_{p<w,p\in\mathbb{P}}p
and
\Lambda^{\prime}(n):=\Lambda(n)\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)
. Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
the modified von Mangoldt function is defined by

\Lambda^{\prime}_{w,r}(n):=\frac{\phi(W)}{W}\Lambda^{\prime}(Wn+r),

where
\phi
is the Euler totient function. We begin with a classical lemma (cf. Lemma 2.1 in
[
FHK
]
):

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of complex numbers with
|a_{n}|\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

(5.11)

\left|\frac{1}{\pi(N)}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P},p<N}a_{p}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Lambda^{\prime}(n)a_{n}\right|=o_{N\to\infty}(1),

where
\pi(N)
is the number of primes less than or equal to
N
.

The next result is taken from
[
FHK
]
. We put .

Proposition 5.5

(cf. Proposition 3.6 in
[
FHK
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system. Let
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then,

\lim_{w\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\max_{(r,W)=1,r\leq W}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\Lambda_{w,r}^{\prime}(n)-1)T^{n}fS^{n}g\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

In particular, from the proof of Proposition 3.6 in
[
FHK
]
, we see that letting
B_{W,r}(N):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{Wn+r}fS^{Wn+r}g
we have

(5.12)

\max_{r<W,(r,W)=1}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Lambda^{\prime}_{w,r}(n)T^{Wn+r}fS^{Wn+r}g-B_{W,r}(n)\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=o_{N\to\infty,w}(1)+o_{w\to\infty}(1).

In particular, this implies that the Kronecker factor is characteristic in the following setting:

Lemma 5.6

(Adapted from
[
L
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system with
T,S,TS^{-1}
totally ergodic. Let
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then, if
|||f|||_{2}=0
or if
|||g|||_{2}=0
, then

(5.13)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{p_{n}-1}fS^{p_{n}-1}g=0\textrm{ in }L^{2}(\mu).

Proof.

First notice that letting
a(n):=T^{n}fS^{n}g
it suffices to show that
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a(p_{n})
converges to
0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
as
N\to\infty
if
|||f|||_{2}=0
or
|||g|||_{2}=0
. Now, by Proposition
5.5

(5.14)

\left|\left|\frac{1}{W}\sum_{1\leq r\leq W,(r,W)=1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Lambda^{\prime}_{W,r}(n)a(Wn+r)-\frac{1}{W}\sum_{r=1,(r,W)=1}^{W}B_{W,r}(n)\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=o_{N\to\infty,w}(1)+o_{w\to\infty}(1).

But the first average in (
5.14
) is equal to
\frac{1}{WN}\sum_{n=1}^{WN}\Lambda^{\prime}_{W,r}(n)a(n)
. It follows from Lemma
5.2
, that the Kronecker factor is characteristic for the averages
B_{W,r}(N)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{Wn+r}fS^{Wn+r}g
, and thus,
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}B_{W,r}(n)=0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

It was shown in
[
FHK
]
that the limit
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Lambda^{\prime}(n)a(n)
exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Equation (
5.14
) implies that it must be equal to
0
, so we are done.
∎

Proposition
5.5
and Lemma
5.6
allow us to generalize Theorem 1.1 in
[
L
]
and obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system with
T,S,TS^{-1}
totally ergodic. Let
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and consider the multicorrelation sequence

a(n):=\int_{X}f_{0}\cdot T^{n}f_{1}\cdot S^{n}f_{2}\ d\mu.

Let
a(n)=a_{\textrm{st}}(n)+a_{\textrm{er}}(n)
be the decomposition of the multicorrelation sequence
a(n)
obtained in Theorem
4.1
. Then,

\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}|a_{\textrm{er}}(p_{n}-1)|=0.

Proof.

We start by noticing that Proposition
5.5
and Lemma
5.6
imply that the Kronecker factor (which is a special case of a nilfactor) is characteristic for the
L^{2}(\mu)
-limit of the averages

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{p_{n}-1}f_{1}S^{p_{n}-1}f_{2}

for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Combining this with the equidistribution results obtained in
[
L
]
(Corollary 1.4), we see that for commuting
a_{1},a_{2}
in the Kronecker factor
Z
,
(a_{1}^{p_{n}-1},a_{2}^{p_{n}-1})
is equidistributed with appropriate weights on the connected components of
Z
. Using uniqueness of the decomposition into a nilsequence and a nullsequence of the multicorrelation function
a(n)
, it follows that putting

a(n)=a_{\textrm{st}}(n)+a_{\textrm{er}}(n)

(as obtained in Theorem
4.1
), the sequence
a_{\textrm{er}}
is still a nullsequence along primes, namely

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{\textrm{er}}(p_{n}-1)|=0.

∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.8
:

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T,S)
be a measure preserving system with
T,S,TS^{-1}
totally ergodic. Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
A\in\mathcal{B}
, the set

\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu(A\cap T^{-(p_{n}-1)}A\cap S^{-(p_{n}-1)}A)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\}

has positive lower density.

Proof.

We follow the method described in
[
DMLS
]
. Let
\phi(n):=\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S^{-n}A)
, then by Theorem
4.1
,
\phi(n)=a_{\textrm{st}}(n)+a_{\textrm{er}}(n)
, where
a_{\textrm{st}}(n)
is a uniform limit of
2
-step nilsequences and, by Theorem
5.7
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{\textrm{er}}(n)|=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{\textrm{er}}(p_{n}-1)|=0.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
a_{\textrm{st}}(n)
is a uniform limit of
2
-step nilsequences it can be approximated by a nilsequence
F(\tau^{n}\Gamma)
, where
Y=G/\Gamma
is a
2
-step nilmanifold,
F\in C(Y)
, and
\tau

for all

\Gamma\in Y

Note that in Theorem
5.3
if one strengthens the assumptions to

being totally ergodic, then the set
S_{d}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\mu(A\cap T^{-dn}A\cap S^{-dn}A)=\phi(dn)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\}
is syndetic. Thus,

(5.15)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\{1,\dots,N\}\cap S_{d}|}\sum_{0\leq n<N,n\in S_{d}}|a_{\textrm{er}}(dn)|=0,\textrm{ whence}

(5.16)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\{1,\dots,N\}\cap S_{d}|}\sum_{0\leq n<N,n\in S_{d}}|\phi(dn)-F(\tau^{dn}\Gamma)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

This, in turn, implies that there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
for which
F(\tau^{dn}\Gamma)>\mu^{3}(A)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Since
\tau^{dn}\Gamma\in Y_{0}
, we can find an open set
U

. Hence, the set

. Moreover, the set of

0

R\setminus R^{\prime}

Conflict-Free Colouring of Subsets
Bruno Jartoux, Chaya Keller, Shakhar Smorodinsky, Yelena Yuditsky

Abstract
We introduce and study conflict-free colourings of t t -subsets in hypergraphs. In such colourings, one assigns colours to all subsets of vertices of cardinality t t such that in any hyperedge of cardinality at least t t there is a uniquely coloured t t -subset. The case t = 1 t=1 , i.e., vertex conflict-free colouring, is a well-studied notion that has been originated in the context of frequency allocation to antennas in cellular networks. It has caught the attention of researchers both from the combinatorial and algorithmic points of view. A special focus was given to hypergraphs arising in geometry. Already the case t = 2 t=2 (i.e., colouring pairs) seems to present a new challenge. Many of the tools used for conflict-free colouring of geometric hypergraphs rely on hereditary properties of the underlying hypergraphs. When dealing with subsets of vertices, the properties do not pass to subfamilies of subsets. Therefore, we develop new tools, which might be of independent interest. (i) For any fixed t t , we show that the ( n t ) \binom{n}{t} t t -subsets in any set P P of n n points in the plane can be coloured with O ⁡ ( t 2 ​ log 2 ​ n ) O(t^{2}\log^{2}n) colours so that any axis-parallel rectangle that contains at least t t points of P P also contains a uniquely coloured t t -subset. (ii) For a wide class of “well behaved” geometrically defined hypergraphs, we provide near tight upper bounds on their t t -subset conflict-free chromatic number. For t = 2 t=2 we show that for each of those “well -behaved” hypergraphs H H , the hypergraph H ′ H^{\prime} obtained by taking union of two hyperedges from H H , admits a 2 2 -subset conflict-free colouring with roughly the same number of colours as H H . For example, we show that the ( n 2 ) \binom{n}{2} pairs of points in any set P P of n n points in the plane can be coloured with O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) colours such that for any two discs d 1 , d 2 d_{1},d_{2} in the plane with | ( d 1 ∪ d 2 ) ∩ P | ≥ 2 |(d_{1}\cup d_{2})\cap P|\geq 2 there is a uniquely (in d 1 ∪ d 2 d_{1}\cup d_{2} ) coloured pair. (iii) We also show that there is no general bound on the t t -subset conflict-free chromatic number as a function of the standard conflict-free chromatic number already for t = 2 t=2 .

Introduction

A
hypergraph

H
is a pair
(V,\mathcal{E})
where
\mathcal{E}\subseteq 2^{V}
. The elements of
V
and
\mathcal{E}
are referred to as
vertices
and
hyperedges
respectively.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a
k
-colouring
of the vertices of
H
is a function
\varphi
from
V
to a set of cardinality
k
e.g., the set
\{1,\ldots,k\}
.
The colouring is
proper
if for every
h\in\mathcal{E}
with
|h|\geq 2
there exist
x,y\in h
with
\varphi(x)\neq\varphi(y)
. This extends the definition of the classical proper colouring of a graph.
Denote by
\chi(H)
the least integer
k
such that
H
admits a proper
k
-colouring.

The following notion of CF-colouring is a further restriction of proper colouring:

Definition 1
.

A conflict-free colouring (CF-colouring for short) of
V
is a colouring
\varphi
such that for any nonempty
h\in\mathcal{E}
at least one vertex
x\in h
is uniquely coloured, meaning
\varphi(y)\neq\varphi(x)
for all
y\in h\setminus\{x\}
. Denote by
\chi_{CF}(H)
the least integer
k
such that
H
admits a CF-colouring with
k
colours. Clearly
\chi_{CF}(H)\geq\chi(H)
.

This notion was introduced to model radio frequencies allocations to antennas while avoiding interference
[
11
,
17
]
. This spawned a new area of research in combinatorics and computational geometry, with dozens of followup papers and theses. For more on CF-colouring and its applications see the survey
[
16
]
and the references therein.

In this paper we study an extension of the notion of CF-colouring to subsets of vertices.

Definition 2
.

Let
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
be a hypergraph and let
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
A
t
-subset-CF-colouring
of
H
with
k
colours is a function
\varphi
from
\binom{V}{t}
to a
k
-element set such that for every hyperedge
h\in\mathcal{E}
with
|h|>t
there exists a
t
-subset
s\in\binom{h}{t}
whose colour
\varphi(s)
is distinct from all colours of other
t
-subsets in
\binom{h}{t}
. The
t
-subset-CF-chromatic number

\chi^{t}_{CF}(H)
is the least integer
k
such that
H
admits a
t
-subset-CF-colouring with
k
colours.

1.1
Sparse hypergraphs

We show that in hypergraphs
H
with
n
vertices exhibiting a certain kind of sparsity property, as is the case in many geometrically defined hypergraphs we have
\chi^{t}_{CF}(H)=O(\log n)
where the constant of proportionality in the big-Oh notation depends on
t
and the the sparsity parameter. To make this statement precise
we need the following definitions:

The
Delaunay graph
of a hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
is the graph
\Del(H)=(V,\{h\in\mathcal{E}\colon|h|=2\})
. For a subset
V^{\prime}\subset V
the
induced sub-hypergraph

H[V^{\prime}]
is the hypergraph
(V^{\prime},\{h\cap V^{\prime}:h\in\mathcal{E}\})
.

Definition 3
.

A hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
has the Hereditary Linear Delaunay (HLD) property with parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
if for every subset of vertices
V^{\prime}\subset V
the graph
\Del(H[V^{\prime}])
has at most
c|V^{\prime}|
edges.

Theorem 4
.

Let
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
be a hypergraph. If
H
has the HLD property with parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
then
\chi_{CF}^{t}(H)=O(ct^{2}\log|V|)
. Moreover, there are such hypergraphs for which
\chi_{CF}^{t}(H)=\Omega(\log|V|)
.

In order to prove
\cref
thm:linear-delaunay we first need to prove a common generalization of previous results on so-called
strong CF-colouring

[
1
,
12
]
. Then, we use it as an auxiliary colouring for subset-CF-colouring.

Next, we prove the following result on hypergraphs which are derived from “well-behaved” hypergraphs by allowing union of hyperedges:
Given a hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
, define a new hypergraph on the same vertex set by
H^{\cup}=(V,\{e\cup f\colon e,f\in\mathcal{E}\})
.

Theorem 5
.

If
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
has the HLD property for some parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then
\chi^{2}_{CF}(H^{\cup})=O(c\log|V|)
.

Given two families of sets
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
, the
intersection hypergraph
of
\mathcal{B}
with respect to
\mathcal{C}
is the hypergraph
H(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})
on the vertex-set
\mathcal{B}
, in which any
c\in\mathcal{C}
defines a hyperedge
\{b\in\mathcal{B}\colon b\cap c\neq\emptyset\}
.

Many geometrically defined hypergraphs (also called
range spaces
)
admit the HLD property. This includes
H(P,\mathcal{D})
where
P
is a set of points and
\mathcal{D}
is the set of all discs in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(since
\Del(H(P,\mathcal{D}))
is then the standard Delaunay graph, which is planar). This was also extended to
H(D,\mathcal{D})
for a finite family of discs
D

[
13
]
. Another example is
H(P,\mathcal{H})
where
P
is a set of points and
\mathcal{H}
is the set of all halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(by
[
15
, Lem. 3.1]
, combined with the fact that the union of two planar graphs on
n\geq 3
vertices has at most
6n-12
edges).

Pseudo-disc families are another example:

Definition 6
.

A finite set of simple closed Jordan regions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a
family of pseudo-discs
if the boundaries of any two regions in the family intersect at most twice.

Theorem 7

(
[
14
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are families of pseudo-discs, then
\Del(H(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}))
is planar.

In particular,
H(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})
has the HLD property with
c=3
.

For all these hypergraphs, denoting by
n
the number of vertices, Theorem
4
and Theorem
5
give
\chi^{t}_{CF}(H)=O(t^{2}\log n)
and
\chi^{2}_{CF}(H^{\cup})=O(\log n)
.

1.2
Axis-parallel rectangles

Consider
H=H(P,\mathcal{R})
where
P
consists of
n
points and
\mathcal{R}
of axis-parallel rectangles, in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The problem of bounding
\chi_{CF}(H)
as a function of
n
remains elusive after almost two decades of research; there is an exponential gap between the best known upper and lower bounds of
O(n^{0.368})
and
\Omega(\frac{\log n}{\log^{2}\log n})

[
6
,
8
]
.

In strong contrast to this for
t\geq 2
we prove the following result:

Theorem 8
.

Let
t\geq 2
be an integer and let
P
be a set of
n
points and
\mathcal{R}
a set of axis-parallel rectangles in the plane. Then
\chi^{t}_{CF}(H(P,\mathcal{R}))=O(t^{2}\log^{2}n)
.

Since such hypergraphs do not have linear Delaunay graphs, our proof of Theorem
8
uses different approach and is more involved technically.

1.3
General hypergraphs

Contrary to the above results, in general there is no quantitative relation between
\chi^{t}_{CF}
and
\chi_{CF}
. For example, We show that there is no function
f
such that
\chi^{t}_{CF}(H)\leq f(\chi_{CF}(H))
.

Theorem 9
.

For any
t\geq 2
there exists a sequence of hypergraphs
(H_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\chi_{CF}(H_{i})=2
and
\lim_{i\to+\infty}\chi^{t}_{CF}(H_{i})=+\infty
.

Notice also that there is no function
f
such that
\chi_{CF}(H)\leq f(\chi^{t}_{CF}(H))
already for
t=2
. One can take for example, the complete graph
K_{n}
and easily verify that
\chi^{t}_{CF}(K_{n})=1
and
\chi_{CF}(K_{n})=n
for any
n
. We also show that
\chi_{CF}(H)
can be much larger than
\chi^{t}_{CF}(H)
, even in non-trivial geometrically-defined hypergraphs.

Theorem 10
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
with
|V|=n
such that
\chi^{2}_{CF}(H)=O(\log n)
and
\chi_{CF}(H)=n
.

The phenomenon witnessed by Theorem
10
can be realised by geometric intersection hypergraphs defined with respect to points and halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
(
\cref
subsec:interval_union).

, we have to consider two types of hyperedges: those consisting of a single integer interval (of the form
\llbracket i\ldots j\rrbracket
with
i<j
) and those consisting of two disjoint intervals (of the form
\llbracket i\ldots j\rrbracket\cup\llbracket k\ldots l\rrbracket
where
i\leq j<k\leq l
). In the first case,
\psi
has a unique maximum
m
over
\llbracket i\ldots j\rrbracket
and hence at least one of the pair-colours
(m,0)
and
(m,1)
is present exactly once in
\phi
. In the second case,
\psi
attains respective maxima
m
and
m^{\prime}
on each of the intervals. If
m\neq m^{\prime}
then at least one of the colours
(\max\{m,m^{\prime}\},0)
and
(\max\{m,m^{\prime}\},1)
appears exactly once, whereas if
m=m^{\prime}
then the colour
(2m,0)
is present exactly once in the hyperedge.
∎

In
\cref
subsec:comparing we described a hypergraph whose
t
-subset CF-chromatic number is much larger than its CF-chromatic number. The hypergraph
H^{\cup}
in
\cref
thm:union_intervals demonstrates the opposite situation:
\chi^{2}_{CF}(H^{\cup})=O(\log n)
, while
clearly
\chi_{CF}(H^{\cup})=n
and hence proves Theorem
10
.

However, this is not very informative since the underlying lower bound
is due to the hyperedges of size
2
, which we disregard for the pairs-CF colourings. It is therefore natural to consider the CF-chromatic number of
H^{\cup}
restricted to its hyperedges of size at least
3
.

\cref

thm:LBunion below shows that even if we consider only hyperedges of size at least 3 the same phenomenon holds – the pairs-CF chromatic number of
H^{\cup}
is still at most logarithmic in the CF-chromatic number of
H^{\cup}
.

Theorem 26
.

Let
H^{\cup}
be as in
\cref
thm:union_intervals. The sub-hypergraph
H^{\prime}\subset H^{\cup}
containing all hyperedges of size at least
3
has

\chi_{CF}(H^{\prime})\geq\sqrt{n-1}.

Proof.

Consider a CF-colouring of
H^{\prime}
with
\chi_{CF}(H^{\prime})
colours. For every ordered pair of colours
(a,b)
there is at most one pair of consecutive vertices that are coloured with
a
and
b
in this order, for otherwise there would be a hyperedge of size
4
where both colour
a
and
b
appear twice.
Since there are
n-1
consecutive pairs of vertices and
\chi_{CF}(H^{\prime})^{2}
ordered pairs of colours, we have
\chi_{CF}(H^{\prime})^{2}\geq n-1
and the inequality follows.
∎

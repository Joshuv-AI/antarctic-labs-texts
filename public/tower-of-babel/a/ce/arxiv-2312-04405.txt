A universal scheme to self-test any quantum state and extremal measurement
Shubhayan Sarkar, Alexandre C. Orthey, Jr, Remigiusz Augusiak

Abstract
The emergence of quantum devices has raised a significant issue: how to certify the quantum properties of a device without placing trust in it. To characterise quantum states and measurements in a device-independent way, up to some degree of freedom, we can make use of a technique known as self-testing. While schemes have been proposed to self-test all pure multipartite entangled states (up to complex conjugation) and real local rank-one projective measurements, little has been done to certify mixed entangled states, composite or non-projective measurements. By employing the framework of quantum networks, we propose a scheme for self-testing (up to complex conjugation) arbitrary extremal measurements, including the projective ones, but also in an indirect way any quantum states, including the mixed ones. The quantum network considered in this work is the simple star network, which is implementable using current technologies. For our purposes, we also construct a scheme that can be used to self-test the two-dimensional tomographically complete set of measurements with an arbitrary number of parties.



Introduction.—
Demonstrating the strength of quantum systems over classical ones in tasks like computation and cryptography has been one of the most important advancements in quantum information theory. These quantum protocols are more efficient and secure when compared to the classical ones. To implement these protocols, it is thus necessary to certify the
quantumness
of a given device. The traditional methods of certifying a quantum state or a measurement rely on trusting the devices involved in the scheme, which are referred to as device-dependent schemes. For instance, any quantum state can be device-dependently verified if one has complete knowledge of the measurement apparatuses. However, from a practical perspective, it is unreasonable to completely trust the device. Therefore, it is highly desirable to devise certification schemes where the assumptions made on the devices are minimal, also referred to as device-independent (DI) certification schemes. DI schemes allow one to verify the non-trivial quantum properties of an unknown device by only observing the statistical data it generates. The essential resources for any DI scheme are Bell nonlocal correlations that cannot be reproduced via any local (or simply classical) hidden variable models
Bell 1964
;
Bell 1966
;
Brunner et al. 2014
. For instance, observation of the violation of a Bell inequality certifies in a DI manner that the device operates on an entangled state.

Interestingly, the observation of Bell nonlocality in the data produced by a given device can provide a lot more information about its internal working than just the presence of entanglement. The strongest and most complete form of Bell nonlocality-based certification, known as self-testing
Mayers and Yao 1998
;
Mayers and Yao 2004
, allows for almost full characterization of the underlying quantum state and measurements performed on it.

In recent years, a plethora of schemes have been proposed to self-test pure quantum states or quantum measurements (see, e.g., Refs.
McKague et al. 2012
;
Reichardt et al. 2013
;
McKague 2014
;
Wu et al. 2014
;
Bamps and Pironio 2015
;
Wang et al. 2016
;
Šupić et al. 2016
;
Coladangelo et al. 2017
;
Kaniewski et al. 2019
;
Mančinska et al. 2021
;
Tavakoli et al. 2021
;
Sarkar et al. 2021
;
Sarkar and Augusiak 2022
;
Šupić et al. 2023
;
Sarkar et al. 2023a
). In particular, Refs.
Coladangelo et al. 2017
and
Šupić et al. 2023
propose self-testing strategies for any bipartite or multipartite pure entangled states, where the second method is based on the quantum networks scenario. The only scheme to self-test a mixed entangled state, in particular, a bound entangled state, was only recently proposed in Ref.
Sarkar et al. 2023a
(see nevertheless Refs.
Baccari et al. 2020a
;
Frérot and Acín 2021
). As for quantum measurements, apart from a bunch of schemes allowing to certify various examples of quantum measurements
Tavakoli et al. 2021
;
Kaniewski et al. 2019
;
Woodhead et al. 2020a
;
Sarkar et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2023
, including the composite ones
Renou et al. 2018
;
Zhou et al. 2022
;
Šupić and Brunner 2022
;
Sarkar et al. 2023a
, a general method to certify any real local rank-one projective measurement has only very recently been designed in Ref.
Chen et al. 2023
. Despite this progress, there exist no single unified scheme
that allows for certification of quantum measurements and quantum states (pure or mixed). Moreover, no general scheme for quantum networks-based certification of composite measurements has been proposed.

The main aim of this work is to address the above challenges and provide a universal scheme that can be used to self-test (up to complex conjugate) any extremal generalized quantum measurement (POVM) acting on arbitrary finite-dimensional Hilbert space Our scheme also includes all projective measurements as any projective measurement in extremal. Interestingly, our approach allows also for an indirect certification of any, even mixed quantum state. For our purposes, we utilise the framework of quantum networks consisting of an arbitrary number of parties and sources (as depicted in Fig.
1
and explained later in the text). We first propose a Bell inequality that can be used to self-test the two-dimensional tomographically complete set of measurements with arbitrary number of parties. Based on this, we are then able to certify any extremal POVM and eventually any quantum state. It is worth mentioning that, unlike the standard self-testing statements, our scheme requires additional causality constraints that are natural in quantum networks, in particular, that the sources are statistically independent. Our scheme is simple and can be implemented using the current infrastructure of quantum networks
Wu et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2023
;
Gu et al. 2023
.

Self-testing.—
Let us introduce the idea of self-testing by referring back to the scenario shown in Fig.
1
. First, we assume that the measurements conducted by the parties and the states
\rho_{A_{i}E_{i}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E_{i}})

It is worth pointing out already now that since the dimensions of the local Hilbert spaces

6

Main results.—
We are now ready to present our scheme for self-testing of composite measurements and quantum states. It is divided into three major parts. The first one involves self-testing of the two-dimensional tomographically complete set of Pauli measurements in the measurements of the external parties and the two-qubit Bell states in the states generated by all the sources. The second one involves self-testing any quantum measurement performed by the central party using the states and external parties’ measurements as certified in the above first part of the scheme. Lastly, the third part is concerned with applying the first two parts for self-testing the post-measurement state at the external parties’ laboratories. As a result, our schemes enable self-testing any quantum state (pure or mixed) distributed between the external parties by Eve’s measurements, while also facilitating the self-testing of quantum measurements performed by the central party.

is thus certified to be

\displaystyle U_{A}\,\rho_{A}\,U^{\dagger}_{A}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k}p(k,1)}\sum_{k}p(k,1)U_{A}\,\rho_{A}^{(k,1)}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k}M_{(k,1)}\right)\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=\rho_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}},

(12)

or its complex conjugate,
which is exactly what we promised. Let us say that the external parties want to perform a task with the state
\rho
. Then, they perform their task with all the post-measurement states of Eve and after the task is done, just like Bell’s scenario Eve announces the outcomes. Now all the parties only consider the statistics when Eve obtains
(k,1)
and discard the rest of the statistics.

Discussion.
After completing our work, we have identified several follow-up issues. While we have provided a proof-of-principle scheme for self-testing any quantum state and extremal measurement, it is important to note that in actual experiments, the exact statistics mentioned may not be attainable, and instead, values that are close to them will be obtained. One crucial follow-up question is whether our scheme is resilient to experimental imperfections. Additionally, our current scheme requires highly entangled states and measurements, which can be costly to generate. Therefore, it would be interesting to explore the possibility of self-testing quantum states and measurements using partially entangled states and measurements. On the other hand, it would be interesting to generalize this scheme to any quantum measurement. Lastly, as we have utilised the framework of quantum networks, it would be valuable to investigate if our approach can be utilised for multiparty quantum cryptography or key distribution.

A.1

Let us consider the following

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{1,0}=\frac{A_{1,0}-A_{1,1}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad\tilde{A}_{1,1}=\frac{A_{1,0}+A_{1,1}}{\sqrt{2}}.

Here,

Fact 1
.

The maximal classical value of the Bell expressions
\mathcal{I}_{l}
for any
l
is
\beta_{C}=(\sqrt{2}+1)(N-1)
.

Proof.

To compute the maximal classical value of
\mathcal{I}_{l}
it is enough to optimize
it over local deterministic strategies for which all the expectation values are products of the local ones, and, moreover, the local expectation values equal either
1
or
-1
. Let us begin by noting that the maximal value of the last sum in Eq. (
13
) over such strategies is
N-1
and it is independent of the choice of the maximal values of the first two term. To determine the latter we can
to consider two cases:
A_{1,0}=A_{1,1}
and
A_{1,0}\neq A_{1,1}
. In the first one, the maximal value of the first term in Eq. (
13

\sqrt{2}(N-1)
, whereas the second term vanishes. In the second case, the first term in
\mathcal{I}_{l}
vanishes, whereas the maximal value of the second term
is again
\sqrt{2}(N-1)
. Thus,
\beta_{C}=(\sqrt{2}+1)(N-1)
.
∎

Fact 2
.

The maximal quantum value of the Bell expression
\mathcal{I}_{l}
is
3(N-1)
and it is achieved by the following observables

\displaystyle A_{1,0}=\frac{X+Z}{\sqrt{2}},\quad A_{1,1}=\frac{X-Z}{\sqrt{2}},\quad A_{1,2}=Y,\quad A_{i,0}=Z,\quad A_{i,1}=X,\quad A_{i,2}=Y\quad(i=2,\ldots,N)

Proof.

The following proof is inspired by Refs.
Baccari et al. 2020b
;
Sarkar et al. 2023a

(

j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}A_{j,1}.

(17)

Now, for each

\displaystyle 2\left[3(N-1)\,\mathbbm{1}-\mathcal{\hat{I}}_{l}\right]\geq(N-1)P_{l_{1}}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}R_{i,l_{1},l_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}Q_{i,l_{i}}^{2},

(18)

(19a)

(19b)

Q_{i,l_{i}}=(-1)^{l_{i}}A_{1,2}+A_{i,2}\otimes\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}A_{j,1}.

(19c)

Notice that by expanding the terms on the right-hand side of the above decomposition (

\displaystyle(N-1)\left(\tilde{A}_{1,1}^{2}+\tilde{A}_{1,0}^{2}+A_{1,2}^{2}\right)+(N-1)\bigotimes_{i=2}^{N}A_{i,1}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}A_{i,2}^{2}\otimes\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}A_{j,1}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}A_{i,0}^{2}-2\ \mathcal{\hat{I}}_{l}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\ 2\left[3(N-1)\ \mathbbm{1}-\ \mathcal{\hat{I}}_{l}\right],

(20)

where to arrive at the last line we used the fact that

. Thus,
one can conclude from (
A.1

is an upper bound on

Putting the state (

\overline{l}_{i}

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}}\left[|\overline{l}_{1}\ldots\overline{l}_{N}\rangle|\phi_{l_{1}\ldots l_{N}}\rangle+|l_{1}\ldots l_{N}\rangle|\phi_{\overline{l}_{1}\ldots\overline{l}_{N}}\rangle\right]=|l_{1}\ldots l_{N}\rangle|\phi_{l_{1}\ldots l_{N}}\rangle+|\overline{l}_{1}\ldots\overline{l}_{N}\rangle|\phi_{\overline{l}_{1}\ldots\overline{l}_{N}}\rangle.

This implies that

\displaystyle|\phi_{\overline{l}_{1}\ldots\overline{l}_{N}}\rangle=(-1)^{l_{1}}|\phi_{l_{1}\ldots l_{N}}\rangle,

which after substituting into Eq. (

the state

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|l_{1}l_{2}\ldots l_{N}\rangle+(-1)^{l_{1}}|\overline{l}_{1}\ldots\overline{l}_{N}\rangle\right)_{A^{\prime}}\otimes|\phi_{l}\rangle_{A^{\prime\prime}G},

where we have also included the normalization factor.

\displaystyle\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}U_{A_{i}}\right)\rho^{l}_{{A}}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}U_{A_{i}}^{\dagger}\right)=|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}}.

(58)

where
\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
denotes the auxiliary state acting on
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}^{\prime\prime}}

Putting it back into Eq. (
26

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=2^{N}\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes R_{l}\right)\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}|\tilde{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle\!\langle\tilde{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}|\right],

where

B. States generated by the source

Our aim now is to characterize the states produced by the sources

and to this aim we follow the approach of Ref.
Sarkar et al. 2023a

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{d_{i}-1}\alpha_{j}|e_{j}\rangle_{A_{i}}|f_{j}\rangle_{E_{i}}

(60)

such that

denotes the dimension of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}}
and
\{|e_{j}\rangle_{A_{i}}\}
and

denotes the local orthonormal bases of the subsystem
A_{i}
and
E_{i}

is even for any

as the Hilbert space of any external party decomposes as
\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}}=\mathbbm{C}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}^{\prime\prime}}

\alpha_{j}>0

Let us now consider a unitary operation
U_{E_{i}}:\mathcal{H}_{E_{i}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{E_{i}}

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=U_{E_{i}}|\tilde{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{d_{i}-1}\alpha_{j}|e_{j}\rangle_{A_{i}}|e_{j}^{*}\rangle_{E_{i}}

Let us now define a matrix
P_{E_{i}}
of rank
d_{i}
as,

\displaystyle P_{E_{i}}=\sum_{j=0}^{d_{i}-1}\alpha_{j}|e_{j}^{*}\rangle\!\langle e_{j}^{*}|,

using which we rewrite the state (

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=\sqrt{d_{i}}\ \mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\otimes P_{E_{i}}|\phi^{+,d_{i}}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle\qquad\forall i.

Here

denotes the maximally entangled state of local dimension

, that is,

\displaystyle|\phi^{+,d_{i}}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{i}}}\sum_{j=0}^{d_{i}-1}|e_{j}\rangle_{A_{i}}|e_{j}^{*}\rangle_{E_{i}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{i}}}\sum_{j=0}^{d_{i}-1}|j\rangle_{A_{i}}|j\rangle_{E_{i}}.

(64)

Putting it back into the condition (
59

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=2^{N}\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes\overline{R}_{l,E}\right)\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}|\phi^{+,d_{i}}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{+,d_{i}}_{A_{i}E_{i}}|\right]

where

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{l,E}=D_{N}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}P_{E_{i}}U_{E_{i}}\right)\ R_{l}\ \left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}U_{E_{i}}^{\dagger}P_{E_{i}}\right),

(66)

such that
D_{N}=\Pi_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}
.
Now, notice that the state

a singe maximally entangled state between the external parties
A
and
E

\displaystyle\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}|\phi^{+,d_{i}}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=|\phi^{+,D_{N}}_{AE}\rangle,

\displaystyle|\phi^{+,D_{N}}_{A|E}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D_{N}}}\sum_{j=0}^{D_{N}-1}|j\rangle_{A}|j\rangle_{E}.

Plugging this state back to the condition (
65

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=2^{N}\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left[\overline{R}^{T}_{l,A}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}|\phi^{+,D_{N}}_{A|E}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{+,D_{N}}_{A|E}|\right].

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{2^{N}}{D_{N}}\overline{R}^{T}_{l,A}.

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l,T}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=2^{N}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}P_{A_{i}}U_{{A_{i}}}\right)\ R_{l}\ \left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}U_{{A_{i}}}^{\dagger}P_{{A}_{i}}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{2^{N}-1}|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l,T}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=2^{N}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}P_{A_{i}}^{2}.

Now, taking a partial trace over the subsystems

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}}{2}}=P_{A_{1}}

where,

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{2^{N}-1}\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left(\tilde{\rho}^{l,T}_{A^{\prime\prime}}\right).

In a similar way we can determine from Eq. (

\displaystyle P_{A_{i}}=\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}^{\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{A_{i}^{\prime\prime}}}{2}}\qquad(i=1,\ldots,N),

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=\sqrt{d^{\prime}_{i}}\ \left(\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}E_{i}^{\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{\sigma_{E_{i}^{\prime\prime}}}\right)|\phi^{+,d_{i}}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle\qquad\forall i.

Using then again the fact that

\displaystyle(U_{A_{i}}\otimes U_{E_{i}})|\psi_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=|\overline{\psi}_{A_{i}E_{i}}\rangle=|\phi^{+}_{A_{i}^{\prime}E_{i}^{\prime}}\rangle|\xi_{A_{i}^{\prime\prime}E_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\qquad\forall i.

where the auxiliary state

\displaystyle|\xi_{A_{i}^{\prime\prime}E_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle=\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{d^{\prime}_{i}\sigma_{E_{i}^{\prime\prime}}}\right)|\phi^{+,d^{\prime}_{i}}_{A^{\prime\prime}_{i}E^{\prime\prime}_{i}}\rangle.

(92)

Now, considering the general case

and sandwiching the above expression with the state

Let us consider the relation in Eq. (

for

) and (

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}U_{A_{s}}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}\right)\left(\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}U_{A_{s}}\right)\otimes R_{l}\right\rangle_{\psi_{AE}}=f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}.

\displaystyle d_{1}^{\prime}\ldots d_{N}^{\prime}\left\langle\left(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\otimes\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}^{T}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}\right\rangle_{\phi^{+}_{2^{N}D}}=f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}},

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{l}=\left(\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}U_{\overline{A}_{s}}\right)R_{l}\left(\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}U_{\overline{A}_{s}}^{\dagger}\right).

After tracing Eve’s subsystems the expression in Eq. (

) can further be simplified to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\otimes\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}^{T}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=2^{N}f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}.

(108)

Now, we can exploit the fact that

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{l}^{T}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}=0}^{3}\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\otimes\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left(\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma^{T}_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}\right]=f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}\qquad\forall i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}.

(110)

The rest of the proof follows the same lines as the proof of Theorem 6 of Ref.
Sarkar et al. 2023b
. We first decompose the state
\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma^{T}_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}
in its eigenbasis, let’s say
\{|b\rangle\}
with eigenvalues
p_{b}
which allows us to obtain from Eq. (
110

\displaystyle\sum_{b}p_{b}\langle b|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|b\rangle=f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}.

(111)

Next, we introduce a collection of POVMs
\{R_{l}^{b}\}_{l}
for any
b

\displaystyle R_{l}^{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes|b\rangle\!\langle b|_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]

(112)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{}i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}=0}^{3}\langle b|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|b\rangle\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}.

Since
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\geq 0
it directly follows from the above equation that

for any
l
and

. Moreover,
\sum_{l}\overline{R}_{l}^{T}=\mathbbm{1}

\sum_{l}R_{l}^{b}=\mathbbm{1}
for any

\{R_{l}^{b}\}_{l}
are proper quantum measurements for all

These additional POVMs, through Eq. (

\{N_{l}\}
as
N_{l}=\sum_{b}p_{b}R_{l}^{b}
.
As the ideal POVM is extremal, it can not be decomposed in terms of other POVM’s. Consequently, we have that

\displaystyle\forall b\qquad R_{l}^{b}=N_{l},

(113)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\langle b|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|b\rangle=f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}\qquad\forall b.

(114)

Let us now consider the following vectors:

\displaystyle|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|s\rangle\pm\mathbbm{i}^{a}|t\rangle\right),

(115)

such that
|s\rangle
and
|t\rangle
are vectors that belong to the eigenbasis
\{|k\rangle\}
of
\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma^{T}_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}

and
a=0,1
. Let us look at the quantity

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{a,s,t}|_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}=0}^{3}\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\ \mathrm{Tr}(|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{a,s,t}|_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}),

(116)

which with the aid of the explicit form of the vector (
115
)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{a,s,t}|_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=N_{l}\pm\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}})\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right],

(117)

where
L_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}^{a}=(\mathbbm{i}^{a}/2)\left(|t\rangle\!\langle s|+(-1)^{a}|s\rangle\!\langle t|\right)
. Using the fact that
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}
is positive semi-definite, which implies that the left-hand side of the above
relation is positive semi-definite too, we conclude that

\displaystyle N_{l}\geq\pm\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}})\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=:\pm\Omega_{l}^{a}.

(118)

As we show at the end of the proof, the above operator inequality can be satisfied only if

\mathrm{supp}(\Omega_{l}^{a})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(N_{l})
for any
l
and for both values of
a=0,1
. Let us then denote by
|\phi_{l,m}\rangle

(m=1,\ldots,r_{l})
as the eigenvectors of
N_{l}
, where
r_{l}
denotes the rank of
N_{l}
. Due to the fact that
\mathrm{supp}(\Omega_{l}^{a})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(N_{l})
, each operator
\Omega_{l}^{a}
can be written using the eigenvectors of
N_{l}
as

\Omega_{l}^{a}=\sum_{m,n=1}^{r_{l}}\gamma_{m,n}^{(l,a)}|\phi_{l,m}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l,n}|,

(119)

where
\gamma_{m,n}^{(l)}
are some complex coefficients; recall that
\Omega_{l}^{a}
are Hermitian.

Let us now notice that it follows from Eq. (
118
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\Omega_{l}^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}})\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]

(120)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}})\right]=0,

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}
sum up to the identity, whereas the third equality is a consequence of the fact that the

operators are traceless.
We thus have that for any
a
,

\sum_{l}\Omega_{l}^{a}=0.

(121)

After plugging Eq. (
119
), we obtain the following equation

\sum_{l,m,n}\gamma_{m,n}^{(l,a)}|\phi_{l,m}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l,n}|=0

(122)

Let us now exploit the fact that the measurement
\{N_{l}\}
is extremal.
As proven in Ref.
D'Ariano et al. 2005
it follows from the latter that
the operators

are linearly independent for all

,

and

. It thus follows from Eq. (

that
\gamma

\Omega_{l}^{a}=\mathrm{Tr}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}})\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=0

(123)

for any
a
and any
l
. Using now the explicit form of the operators

, the above
implies the following conditions for the
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}
operators,

\displaystyle\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\left(\langle s|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|t\rangle+\langle t|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|s\rangle\right)=0,

(124)

for

\displaystyle\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\left(\langle t|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|s\rangle-\langle s|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|t\rangle\right)=0

(125)

for

, where we used the fact that

are linearly
independent for all

. The only possible solution to these equations
is that

\langle s|\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}|t\rangle=0\qquad(s\neq t).

(126)

Combining this with Eq. (

\tilde{R}^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}=f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}^{l}\mathbbm{1},

(127)

which implies the desired relation
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}=N_{l}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\ldots A_{N}^{\prime\prime}}

for all
l
.

Let us finally prove that
\mathrm{supp}(\Omega_{l}^{a})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(N_{l})
for any
l
and
a
. For this purpose, let us pick a particular

and consider its eigenvectors

|\psi_{i}\rangle

(i=1,\ldots,r_{l})
, where
r_{l}
is the rank of

. We then construct an orthonormal basis
\{|\phi_{i}\rangle\}_{i}
with

in the

-qubit Hilbert space such that

for

.

Sandwiching then inequalities (

) with
|\phi_{i}\rangle
and
|\phi_{j}\rangle
,
one obtains

\displaystyle\forall_{i,j}\qquad\langle\phi_{i}|N_{l}|\phi_{j}\rangle\geq\pm\langle\phi_{i}|\Omega_{l}^{a}|\phi_{j}\rangle.

(128)

Now, for any pair

(i.e., either

\displaystyle 0\geq\pm\langle\phi_{i}|\Omega_{l}^{a}|\phi_{j}\rangle,

(129)

which directly implies that

for any pair

.

B.1

Here we present an alternative proof in the case when we want to certify Eve’s second measurement

to be a particular reference projective measurement

\mathbbm{1}_{2^{N}}

is from the beginning defined on the

Let us then decompose every measurement

as

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{l|1}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}=0}^{3}f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}\sigma_{1,i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\sigma_{N,i_{N}},

\displaystyle f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}^{l}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\mathrm{Tr}[(\sigma_{1,i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\sigma_{N,i_{N}})R^{\prime}_{l|1}].

(132)

Let us now move on to our self-testing scheme and consider again the quantum network scenario presented in above. Let us then assume that apart from the conditions imposed in Theorem
1
, the correlations observed in the network satisfy the following additional set of constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}=0}^{3}f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}\left\langle\tilde{A}_{1,i_{1}}\otimes\bigotimes_{k=2}^{N}A_{k,i_{k}}\otimes(R_{l|1})_{E}\right\rangle_{|\psi_{AE}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{l}}{2^{N}}\quad\forall l,

(133)

where

.

Let us suppose that the correlations generated in the quantum network satisfy the assumptions of Theorem

and the additional conditions in Eq. (

). Then, for any

we conclude that

\displaystyle U_{E}R_{l|1}\,U_{E}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(R^{\prime}_{l|1})^{*}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime\prime}},\quad\mathrm{if}\quad A_{i,2}=Y\otimes\mathbbm{1}\quad\forall i,\quad\text{or}\quad

\displaystyle U_{E}R_{l|1}\,U_{E}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime\prime}}\quad\ \ \ \mathrm{if}\quad A_{i,2}=-Y\otimes\mathbbm{1}\quad\forall i

throughout the proof. Let us first consider the relation in Eq. (
133

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}=0}^{3}f^{l}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k,i_{k}}\otimes\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma^{T}_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=r_{l}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left(R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma^{T}_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=r_{l}.

)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{r_{l}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left(\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma^{T}_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\tilde{R}_{m,m}\right]=r_{l}.

Now, due to the fact that the density matrices

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{l}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,m^{\prime}=1\\

imposes that the
first three sums in the above representation must vanish. To this aim, let us consider a product vector

|\delta_{m}\rangle|\xi\rangle
for any
m=1,\ldots,r_{l}

and any vector
|\xi\rangle
, and act on it with
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}=R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}+\mathbbm{L}_{l}
.
This gives

\overline{R}_{l}^{T}|\delta_{m}\rangle|\xi\rangle=|\delta_{m}\rangle|\xi\rangle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2^{N}}|\delta_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\otimes\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}|\xi\rangle,

(148)

which further leads to the following condition

\langle\delta_{m}|\langle\xi|(\overline{R}_{l}^{T})^{2}|\delta_{m}\rangle|\xi\rangle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2^{N}}\langle\xi|\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}^{\dagger}\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}|\xi\rangle.

(149)

Let us observe now that
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\leq\mathbbm{1}
implies
(\overline{R}_{l}^{T})^{2}\leq\mathbbm{1}
, which after applying to the above equation allows one to conclude that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}=1\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2^{N}}\langle\xi|\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}^{\dagger}\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}|\xi\rangle\leq 0.

(150)

Therefore,
\langle\xi|\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}^{\dagger}\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}|\xi\rangle=0
for any vector
|\xi\rangle
, which in turn means that
\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}=0
for any
m^{\prime}\neq m
.
Since the same argument can be repeated for any
|\delta_{m}\rangle
with
m=1,\ldots,r_{l}
one then has that all the matrices

\tilde{R}_{m^{\prime},m}=0

for any
m=1,\ldots,r_{l}
and any
m^{\prime}\neq m
.
This finally implies that the
\mathbbm{L}_{l}
operator
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathbbm{L}_{l}=\sum_{m=r_{l}+1}^{2^{N}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=r_{l}+1}^{2^{N}}|\delta_{m}\rangle\!\langle\delta_{m^{\prime}}|\otimes\tilde{R}_{m,m^{\prime}},

(151)

and thus it acts on a subspace of the corresponding Hilbert space which is orthogonal to that supporting

R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
. Due to the fact that
the measurement operator satisfies
\overline{R}_{l}\geq 0
the latter clearly implies
that
\mathbbm{L}_{l}\geq 0
.

It is important to stress that the same reasoning can be applied to

\overline{R}_{l}^{T}
for any
l
, and thus
every
\overline{R}_{l}^{T}
decomposes into a direct sum

\overline{R}_{l}^{T}=R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}+\mathbbm{L}_{l},

(152)

of two positive semi-definite operators
R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
and
\mathbbm{L}_{l}
.
Now, after summing (
152
) over all outcomes,
one obtains

\sum_{l}\mathbbm{L}_{l}=0,

(153)

where we also exploited the fact that both
\overline{R}_{l}
and
R^{\prime}_{l|1}
sum up to the identities.
Taking into account that
\mathbbm{L}_{l}\geq 0
, one deduces from the condition (
153
) that

_{l}=0
for any
l
and thus

\overline{R}_{l}^{T}=R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}

(154)

or, equivalently,

\overline{R}_{l}=(R^{\prime}_{l|1})^{*}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}.

(155)

Similarly, one can consider the other case of
A_{s,2}=-Y\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{s^{\prime\prime}}
for any
s
and obtain an analogous relation to Eq. (

) which reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left((R^{\prime}_{l|1})^{*}\otimes\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}\sigma^{T}_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right)\overline{R}_{l}^{T}\right]=r_{l},\qquad(l=1,\ldots,K).

(156)

Following exactly the same steps as above, we find that
\overline{R}_{l}=R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
for
l=1,\ldots,K
, thus completing the proof.

Appendix C: Self-testing any quantum state

Using the Theorems
2
proven above, we can now show how it is possible to probabilistically self-test arbitrary pure or mixed
N
-qubit quantum state. For this purpose, let us first certify the post-measurement state when eve chooses
e=1
and obtains an outcome
l
.

Corollary 1
.

Assume that the states are certified as in Eq. (
23
) and Eve’s measurement
E_{1}
is certified as in Eq. (
2
). Consequently, when Eve observes the
l-
th outcome of her measurement, the post-measurement state with the external parties is given by

\displaystyle U_{A}\,\rho_{A}^{l}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{Tr}R^{\prime}_{l|1}}R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}},\quad\mathrm{if}\quad A_{i,2}=Y\otimes\mathbbm{1}\quad\forall i,\quad\text{or}\quad

\displaystyle U_{A}\,\rho_{A}^{l}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{Tr}R^{\prime}_{l|1}}(R^{\prime}_{l|1})^{*}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}}\quad\ \ \ \mathrm{if}\quad A_{i,2}=-Y\otimes\mathbbm{1}\quad\forall i,

(157)

where
U_{A}=\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}U_{A_{s}}
and the unitaries
U_{A_{s}}
are the same as in Eq.
(
23
).

Proof.

The post-measurement state when Eve observes the
l-
th outcome of her measurement
E_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{l}=\frac{1}{\overline{P}(l|e=1)}\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes R_{l|1}\right)\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}|\psi_{A_{s}E_{s}}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{A_{s}E_{s}}|\right].

(158)

Then, substituting the states
|\psi_{A_{s}E_{s}}\rangle
from Eq. (
22
) and the measurement elements
R_{l|1}
from Eq. (
23
), we get that

\displaystyle U_{A}\,\rho_{A}^{l}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\overline{P}(l|e=1)}\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes(R^{\prime}_{l|1})^{*}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime\prime}}\right)\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}|\phi^{+}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{+}|_{A_{s}^{\prime}E_{s}^{\prime}}\otimes|\xi_{s}\rangle\!\langle\xi_{s}|_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}E_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right].

(159)

Again using the identity
(\mathbbm{1}\otimes Q)|\phi^{+}\rangle=(Q^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1})|\phi^{+}\rangle
, we obtain

\displaystyle U_{A}\,\rho_{A}^{l}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\overline{P}(l|e=1)}\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left[\left(R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}\right)\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}|\phi^{+}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{+}|_{A_{s}^{\prime}E_{s}^{\prime}}\otimes|\xi_{s}\rangle\!\langle\xi_{s}|_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}E_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right],

(160)

where we also used the fact that

\left((R^{\prime}_{l|1})^{*}\right)^{T}=R^{\prime}_{l|1}
. After tracing the
E
subsystem we arrive at

\displaystyle U_{A}\,\rho_{A}^{l}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2^{N}\overline{P}(l|e=1)}R^{\prime}_{l|1}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}},

(161)

where
\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{E^{\prime\prime}}\left[\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N}|\xi_{s}\rangle\!\langle\xi_{s}|_{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}E_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right]
. It is straightforward to observe now that
\overline{P}(l|e=1)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(R^{\prime}_{l|1}\right)/2^{N}
giving us the desired result.
∎

Now, to certify an arbitrary pure state
|\psi\rangle\in(\mathbbm{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}
among the external parties it is enough that the second Eve’s reference measurement
E_{1}^{\prime}
is projective and one of its elements, say
R^{\prime}_{0|1}
, is a projection onto
|\psi\rangle
,
R^{\prime}_{0|1}=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
.

For mixed states the situation is a bit more complicated. Consider a mixed state acting on
\mathbbm{C}^{d}
:

\rho=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|.

(162)

It follows that one can construct an extremal
3d-
outcome POVM in the Hilbert space
\mathbbm{C}^{2d}
that can be performed by Eve on her share of the joint state
|\psi_{AE}\rangle
to create
\rho
at the external parties’ labs with the aid of post-processing. To construct this POVM, we first define two sets of
d
mutually orthogonal vectors
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{|\psi_{k}\rangle\}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}=\{|\phi_{k}\rangle\}
such that
\langle\psi_{k}|\phi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle=0
for any
k,k^{\prime}
. Then, one considers the pair of the states
\{\psi_{k},\phi_{k}\}
to construct the trine POVM that acts on
\mathbbm{C}^{2}
[cf. Ref.
Woodhead et al. 2020b
] as

\displaystyle M_{k,1}=p_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|,\qquad M_{k,2}=\frac{2-p_{k}}{2}|\tau_{k,2}\rangle\!\langle\tau_{k,2}|,\qquad M_{k,3}=\frac{2-p_{k}}{2}|\tau_{k,3}\rangle\!\langle\tau_{k,3}|,

(163)

where for all
k
,

\displaystyle|\tau_{k,2}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{k}}{2-p_{k}}}|\psi_{k}\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{1}{2-p_{k}}}|\phi_{k}\rangle,\qquad|\tau_{k,3}\rangle=-\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{k}}{2-p_{k}}}|\psi_{k}\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{1}{2-p_{k}}}|\phi_{k}\rangle.

(164)

Notice that for any
k
,

M_{k,0}+M_{k,1}+M_{k,2}=|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|+|\phi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{k}|,

(165)

and therefore the three-element set
\{M_{k,l}\}_{l}
can also be understood as a three-outcome rank-one POVM in
\mathbbm{C}^{2}
. Moreover, taking a sum over
k
in Eq. (D8) one obtains that

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(M_{k,0}+M_{k,1}+M_{k,2})=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|+|\phi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{k}|)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2d}}

(166)

which implies that
M_{k,l}
form a valid
3d
-outcome rank-one POVM in
\mathbbm{C}^{2d}
which we denote
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{k,l}\}
.

Let us now demonstrate that
\mathcal{M}
is an extremal POVM. To this end, we first notice that for any
k
the elements
M_{k,l}

(l=0,1,2)
are linearly independent and, moreover, the sets
\{M_{k,0},M_{k,1},M_{k,2}\}
and
\{M_{k^{\prime},0},M_{k^{\prime},1},M_{k^{\prime},2}\}
for any pair
k\neq k^{\prime}
are orthogonal in the sense that any element from the first set is orthogonal to any element in the second set. This means that all
M_{k,l}
forming our POVM are linearly independent which together with the fact that
M_{k,l}
are rank-one
implies that
\mathcal{M}
is extremal [cf. Corollary 5 in Ref.
D'Ariano et al. 2005
].

Let us now go back to our scheme of probabilistic self-testing of an arbitrary mixed state
\rho
and observe that after performing
\mathcal{M}
on her shares of the joint state, Eve obtains the outcomes
l\equiv(k,1)
with probability
p(k,1|e=1)p_{l}/2^{N}
. Whenever she obtains the outcome
(k,1)
, the post-measurement state with the external parties is certified from Corrollary
1
. Now, one can clearly see that as the average state with the external parties when Eve obtains
(k,1)
for all
k
is certified from Corrollary
1
to be

\displaystyle U_{A}\,\rho_{A}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k}p(k,1)}\sum_{k}p(k,1)U_{A}\,\rho_{A}^{(k,1)}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}=\left(\sum_{k}M_{(k,1)}\right)\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}}=\rho_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A^{\prime\prime}},

(167)

or its complex conjugate, which is exactly as we promised. The above state occurs with a probability
1/2^{N}
.

Orthogonal projections in the plane over prime order fields
Ben Lund, Thang Pham, Le Anh Vinh

Abstract
We give an upper bound on the number of exceptional orthogonal projections of a small set of points in a plane over a prime order field, which makes progress toward a conjecture of Chen (2018). This theorem relies on a new upper bound on the number of incidences between an arbitrary set of lines and a set of points contained in a reasonably small pencil of lines. We also prove a new upper bound on the number of incidences between points and lines when both are Cartesian products in an appropriate sense.

Introduction

In this paper, we study orthogonal projections in vector spaces over finite fields. The study of projections (over
\mathbb{R}
) has a long history in geometry measure theory, and we refer the reader to
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
5
]
for well-known results and recent progress.

We first start by introducing some notation.

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}
denotes the
d
-dimensional vector space over the field with
q
elements, where
q
is a prime power.
For
0<k<d
, the Grassmannian
G(k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d})
is the space parameterizing
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
.
For
x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}
, denote

x\cdot y=x_{1}y_{1}+\ldots+x_{d}y_{d}.

Vectors
x,y
are orthogonal if
x\cdot y=0
, and the orthogonal complement of
V\in G(k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d})
is the maximal subspace
V^{\perp}\in G(d-k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d})
such that if
x\in V
and
y\in V^{\perp}
, then
x\cdot y=0
.

Given
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, the orthogonal projection of
E
onto
V
is defined to be

\pi_{V}(E):=\{x+V^{\perp}\colon(x+V^{\perp})\cap E\neq\emptyset,~x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\}.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be a set with
|E|=q^{a}

(0<a<d)
. For
s\in(0,a)
, define

\displaystyle T_{s}^{k,d}(E)

\displaystyle=\{V\in G(k,\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}):\#\pi_{V}(E)<q^{s}\}

to be the set of small projections of
E
onto
k
-dimensional subspaces.

Using discrete Fourier analysis, Chen
[
4
]
proved the following theorem.
He stated his result over prime fields, but it can be extended to arbitrary finite fields using the same method.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with
|E|=q^{a}
, we have:

1.

if
0<s<k<a
then

|T_{s}^{k,d}(E)|\ll q^{\max\{k(d-k)+s-a,0\}},\text{ and}

(1)

2.

if
0<s<a<k
, then

|T_{s}^{k,d}(E)|\ll q^{k(d-k)+s-k}.

(2)

Here and throughout the paper, the notation
\ll
is used to absorb a multiplicative constant, not depending on any other parameters.

Bright and Gan
[
3
]
recently obtained the following refinement of Chen’s bound.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with
|E|=q^{a}
. Denote

M:=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\sum_{V\in T_{s}^{k,d}(A)}1_{V}(\xi).

Then

|T_{s}^{k,d}(E)|\lesssim Mq^{d-k+s-a},
where
1_{V}
is the characteristic function of
V
.

Here and throughout the paper, the notation
\lesssim
is used to absorb an arbitrarily small polynomial factor,
i.e.

f(q)\lesssim g(q)
means that, for any
\epsilon>0
there is a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that
f(q)\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}g(q)
for all
q>0
.

Since the number of
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
that contain a fixed nonzero-vector is
O(q^{(k-1)(d-k)})
, this is a the trivial bound on
M
in

1.2
, which immediately implies the first statement in Chen’s theorem.
Bright and Gan also made an observation that if there is a
(d-1)
-dimensional hyperplane with at least
q^{s}
elements from
E
, then
|T_{s}(E)|
can be bounded by the size of
G(d-k,d-1)
.
When each hyperplane contains at most
q^{s}
points from
E
, then they partition
E
into disjoint subsets in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}
and apply Theorem
1.2
for a set in one lower dimensional vector space with an appropriate upper bound of
M
.
As a consequence, they obtain the following result for large
E
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Given
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
T_{s}^{k,d}(E)
is defined as above. If
s<(a+2k-d)/2
, then

|T_{s}^{k,d}(E)|\lesssim q^{\max\{k(d-k)+2(s-a),~(k-1)(d-k)\}}.

In particular, if
|E|=q^{a}>p^{d-1}
and
0<s<\frac{a+d-2}{2}
, then

|T_{s}^{d-1,d}(E)|\lesssim q^{d-2}.

As noted in
[
3
]
that this result is sharp in its range, i.e.
s<\frac{a+d-2}{2}
. As in the continuous setting, understanding the sharpness of Theorem
1.1
or providing plausible conjectures is a difficult task. Moreover, the problem for prime fields is quite different than for fields that have subfields. This is explained in more detail below.

Our main result concerns orthogonal projections in the two dimensional plane over a prime field. Before stating the result, we discuss the following conjecture due to Chen
[
4
]
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
a/2\leq s\leq a\leq 1
, let
p
be prime, and let and

Then

One important implication of this conjecture is that if
a/2\leq s<a\leq 1

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(a,s)>0

|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|=|\{\ell\in G(2,1)\colon|\pi_{\ell}(E)|\leq p^{s}\}|\leq p^{s-\epsilon}.

(3)

The continuous version of

3

[

There are two reasons to believe that Conjecture

First, Bright and Gan
[

constructed a set

Second, Orponen
[

The hypothesis that

In more detail, assume

1.1
, the following sharp result is obtained.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
q
be a prime power, and let
0<a\leq 2
and
a/2\leq s<\min(1,a)
.
Let
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
with
|E|=q^{a}
.
Then,

|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|=|\{\ell\in G(2,1)\colon|\pi_{\ell}(E)|\leq q^{s}\}|\ll\min(q^{1+s-a},q^{s}).

The upper bound

is tight when

is the size of a sub-field of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and

. For other ranges of

, the situation is not so clear, and it would be interesting to classify all possibilities.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

For
a/2\leq s\leq a\leq 1
and
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
with
|E|=p^{a}
. Then

|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|\ll\min\left\{p^{\frac{5s}{2}-a},~p^{6s-3a},~p^{s}\right\}.

The term
p^{6s-3a}
is effective when
a/2\leq s\leq 4a/7
, the term
p^{5s/2-a}
is effective when
4a/7\leq s\leq 2a/3
, and the term
p^{s}
is effective when
2a/3\leq s\leq a

Theorem

1.6
matches

or

.
For

1.6
improves

3

We note that, in higher dimensions, an inductive argument of Bright and Gan in

with the base case (Theorem

Sketch of main ideas:

The main tool in our proof of Theorem
1.6
is a new incidence bound between an arbitrary set of lines and a collection of points contained in the union of a relatively small number of parallel lines. To see how the problem is reduced to an incidence problem, we proceed as follows.
Let
Z^{*}=\bigcup_{\ell\in T_{s}(E)}\pi_{\ell}(E)
.
Note that
Z^{*}
consists of
|T_{s}(E)|
families of parallel lines with at most
p^{s}
lines each.
Hence, we can choose a projective duality that maps
Z^{*}
to a set
Z
of points that is contained in the union of
|T_{s}(E)|
parallel lines, and each of these lines contains at most
p^{s}
points of
Z
. Moreover, we also have
I(Z,E^{*})=I(Z^{*},E)=|T_{s}(E)|\,|E|
. Thus, to bound the size of
T_{s}(E)
from above, we need to estimate the number of incidences between
Z
and
E^{*}
.
From here, one could apply a known upper bound
[
16
]
on the number of incidences between arbitrary small sets of points and lines in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, but this leads to a weaker bounds than that of Theorem
1.6
.
In order to improve this approach, we prove the following incidence bound to take advantage of the special structure arising in this incidence problem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
p
be prime, let
X>0
be an integer, and let
Y\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
For each
y\in Y
, let
X_{y}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
|X_{y}|\leq X
.
Let
Z=\bigcup_{y\in Y}X_{y}\times\{y\}
.
Let
L
be a set of lines in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
.
Suppose that
|Y|\,|L|\ll p^{2}
.
Then,

I(Z,L)\ll\min(|Y|^{9/10}X^{3/5}|L|^{7/10},|Y|^{7/9}X^{5/9}|L|^{7/9})+|Y|\,X^{1/2}\,|L|^{1/2}+|Z|^{2/3}|L|^{2/3}+|L|+|Z|.

We also prove an independently interesting bound on the number of incidences between points and lines when both sets are highly structured.

Given
C\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we say a set

of lines is of the form

Theorem 1.8
.

Let

with
|A|\leq|B|
,

, and

To see how good this theorem is, we first compare it with a result of Steven and De Zeeuw

(4)

Let
R^{\prime}
be the set of elements of
R
for which
a\neq a^{\prime}
, and note that

R^{\prime}=\{((a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}),y)\in E\times E\times Y:a\neq a^{\prime},y=(b-b^{\prime})(a-a^{\prime})^{-1}\}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|R^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in Y}|\{((a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\in E\times E:y=(b-b^{\prime})(a-a^{\prime})^{-1}\}|

\displaystyle\leq|Y|^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}|\{((a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\in E\times E:\lambda=(b-b^{\prime})(a-a^{\prime})^{-1}\}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(14)

For each
(a,b)\in E
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, denote the number of
(c,d)\in E\setminus\{(a,b)\}
such that
(d-b)/(c-a)=\lambda
by
N_{a,b}(\lambda)
.
For a fixed
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,

|\{((a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\in E\times E:\lambda=(b-b^{\prime})(a-a^{\prime})^{-1}\}|=\sum_{(a,b)\in E}N_{(a,b)}(\lambda).

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\sum_{(a,b)\in E}N_{(a,b)}(\lambda)\right)^{2}\leq|E|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{(a,b)\in E}N_{a,b}(\lambda)^{2}.

(15)

Note, if
(d-b)/(c-a)=\lambda
, then
(c,d)
is contained in the line with slope
-\lambda
that passes through the point
(a,b)
.
From this, it is easy to see that, for any fixed
(a,b)\in E
, we have that
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}N_{a,b}(\lambda)^{2}
is equal to the number of collinear triples in
E
that contain
(a,b)
, including those triples of the form
((a,b),(c,d),(c,d))
.
An easy consequence of the point-line incidence bound Theorem 3 in
[
17
]
is that the number of collinear triples of distinct points in
E
is bounded by
O(|E|^{3}/q+q^{2}|E|)
.
Combining this with
eqs.

14
and
15
leads to the upper bound

|R|\ll|Y|\,|E|+|Y|^{1/2}|E|^{2}/q^{1/2}+|Y|^{1/2}|E|q.

Combining this with the previously obtained lower bound
eq.

13
leads immediately to the result.
∎

Proof of the orthogonal projection theorem

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
with
2p^{s}\leq|E|
, we have
|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|\leq 2p^{s}
.

Proof.

We use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to bound the size of the set
\Pi_{\ell}=\{x,y\in E:x-y\in\ell,x\neq y\}
for each
\ell
such that
|\pi_{\ell}(E)|\leq p^{s}
as follows:

\displaystyle|E|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{t\in\pi_{\ell}(E)}|\{x\in E:x\in\ell+t\}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{s}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}|\{x\in E:x\in\ell+t\}|^{2}

\displaystyle=p^{s}(2|\Pi_{\ell}|+|E|).

The sets
\Pi_{\ell}
and
\Pi_{\ell^{\prime}}
are disjoint for
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
, hence

\binom{|E|}{2}\geq\sum_{\ell\in T_{s}^{1,2}(E)}|\Pi_{\ell}|\geq 2^{-1}|T_{s}^{1,2}|(|E|^{2}p^{-s}-|E|)\geq 4^{-1}|T_{s}^{1,2}||E|^{2}p^{-s}.

In the last inequality, we use the assumption that
|E|\geq 2p^{s}
.
Rearranging, this implies that
|T|\leq 2p^{s}
, as claimed.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
and let
p^{s}\ll|E|
with
|E|p^{s}\ll p^{2}

Then,

|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|\ll\min(p^{5s/2}|E|^{-1},p^{6s}|E|^{-3}).

Proof.

Let

.
Note that

consists of
|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|
families of parallel lines with at most
N
lines each.
Hence, we can choose a projective duality that maps
Z^{*}
to a set
Z
of points that is contained in the union of
|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|
parallel lines, and each of these lines contains at most
p^{s}
points of
Z
.
We will apply
theorem

1.7
to bound
I(Z,E^{*})=I(Z^{*},E)
.

proposition

3.1
, we can assume that
|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|\leq p^{s}
, and hence
|E|\,|T_{s}^{1,2}(E)|\ll p^{2}
, so the hypotheses of

1.7
are satisfied.
We combine this upper bound with the observation that each point of

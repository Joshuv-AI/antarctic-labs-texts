Partial orders are the free conservative cocompletion of total orders
Calin Tataru

Abstract
We show that the category of partially ordered sets 𝖯𝗈𝗌 \Pos is equivalent to the free conservative cocompletion of the category of finite non-empty totally ordered sets Δ \Delta , which is also known as the simplex category.



1
Introduction

Colimits are an important tool in category theory, allowing us to glue together objects in a category in a universal way.
However, most categories do not have all colimits.
We can get around this, for a small category
\mathcal{C}
, by considering its free cocompletion
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}=[\mathcal{C}^{\op},\Set]
, which is cocomplete and contains
\mathcal{C}
as a full subcategory via the Yoneda embedding:

\displaystyle\yo:\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathcal{C}(-,c)

Moreover, it satisfies the following universal property
[
4
]
.
For every cocomplete category
\mathcal{D}
and functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, there is an essentially unique cocontinuous functor extending
F
along the Yoneda embedding:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\yo}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{F}}

However, the category
\mathcal{C}
will often have some colimits to start with, but the Yoneda embedding will not, in general, preserve those colimits.
This motivates the idea of the free conservative cocompletion.
We recall the definition from
[
12
]
.

Definition 1
.

The
free conservative cocompletion
of a category
\mathcal{C}
consists of:

•

a cocomplete category
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
, and

•

a fully faithful cocontinuous functor
I:\mathcal{C}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
.

such that for every cocomplete category
\mathcal{D}
and cocontinuous functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, there exists an essentially unique cocontinuous functor
\widetilde{F}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{D}
such that:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{F}}

There is a well-known way to characterise the free conservative cocompletion of a small category, which is guaranteed to exist, due to Kelly
[
3
, Theorem 6.23]
.
See
[
2
, Theorem 11.5]
for a simpler description of the result without proof.

Proposition 2
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a small category, the free conservative cocompletion
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
is equivalent to the full subcategory of
[\mathcal{C}^{\op},\Set]
whose objects are the continuous presheaves (i.e. presheaves that take colimits in
\mathcal{C}
to limits in
\Set
).

While this description is useful, it is not always easy to work with.
In general, obtaining a concrete description of the free conservative cocompletion of a given category is not straightforward.
In this paper, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 3
.

The category of partially ordered sets
\Pos
is the free conservative cocompletion of the category of finite non-empty totally ordered sets
\Delta
.

The proof makes use of the nerve of the inclusion
\Delta\hookrightarrow\Pos
, which is a functor

N:\Pos\to[\Delta^{\op},\Set]

We first show that the inclusion is cocontinuous, so the image of the nerve is contained in the category of continuous simplicial sets.
We then show that the inclusion is dense, so the nerve is fully faithful.
Finally, we show that the nerve is essentially surjective onto the category of continuous simplicial sets.
Therefore
\Pos
is equivalent to the free conservative cocompletion of
\Delta
by
Proposition

2
.

Preliminaries

We first recall some basic definitions and facts from order theory.

Definition 4
.

A
partial order
on a set
X
is a relation
\leq
that is:

•

reflexive
, i.e.
x\leq x
for all
x\in X
,

•

transitive
, i.e. if
x\leq y
and
y\leq z
then
x\leq z
for all
x,y,z\in X
, and

•

antisymmetric
, i.e. if
x\leq y
and
y\leq x
then
x=y
for all
x,y\in X
.

A
total order
is a partial order such that either
x\leq y
or
y\leq x
for all
x,y\in X
.

We write
\Pos
for the category of partially ordered sets and monotone maps,
\Tos
for the full subcategory of totally ordered sets, and
\Delta
for the full subcategory of finite non-empty totally ordered sets, also known as the
simplex category
:

\Delta\hookrightarrow\Tos\hookrightarrow\Pos

For convenience, we will often work with a skeletal presentation of the simplex category
\Delta
, where the objects are given by the finite non-empty ordinals

[n]=\{0,1,\dots,n\}

and the morphisms are generated by two families of monotone maps:

•

face maps

\delta_{i}:[n-1]\to[n]
skipping an element
i\in[n]
, i.e.

\delta_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}j&\text{if }j<i\\
j+1&\text{if }j\geq i\end{cases}

•

degeneracy maps

\sigma_{i}:[n+1]\to[n]
duplicating an element
i\in[n]
, i.e.

\sigma_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}j&\text{if }j\leq i\\
j-1&\text{if }j>i\end{cases}

subject to the following equations, which are known as the
simplicial identities
:

\displaystyle\delta_{j}\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i}\delta_{j-1}

\displaystyle(i<j)

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j+1}

\displaystyle(i\leq j)

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\delta_{j}\sigma_{i-1}

\displaystyle(i<j)

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\id

\displaystyle(i=j\text{ or }i=j+1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\delta_{j-1}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle(i>j+1)

The category
\Pos
is cocomplete, with its colimits obtained as follows: take the colimit in
\Set
, endow it with the smallest preorder
\leq
making all maps monotone, and then take the quotient under the smallest equivalence relation
\sim
such that:
1
1

1

The equivalence classes of
\sim
are the strongly connected components of the preorder
\leq
.

x\leq y\text{ and }y\leq x\implies x\sim y

On the other hand, the category
\Delta
is
not
cocomplete (e.g. it has no coproducts).
However, we will see later that the inclusion
\Delta\hookrightarrow\Pos
is cocontinuous, and in fact, the colimits in
\Delta
are computed in the same way as the colimits in
\Pos
.

Definition 5
.

A
linear extension
of a partial order
\leq
is a total order
\preceq
on the same set such that
\leq
is contained in
\preceq
, i.e.
x\leq y
implies
x\preceq y
for all
x,y
.

Proposition 6

(Order extension principle
[
8
]
)
.

Every partial order has a linear extension, and moreover, it is the intersection of all of its linear extensions.

Note that for infinite sets, this requires Zorn’s Lemma (which is equivalent to the axiom of choice).
However, for finite sets, it can be proved without choice.

2.2
Nerve, dense functors

Definition 8
.

Any functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
induces a functor
N_{F}:\mathcal{D}\to[\mathcal{C}^{\op},\Set]
, called the
nerve
of
F
, given by the restricted Yoneda embedding:

\mathcal{D}\xrightarrow{\yo}[\mathcal{D}^{\op},\Set]\xrightarrow{(-)\circ F^{\op}}[\mathcal{C}^{\op},\Set]

In particular, it sends every object
d\in\mathcal{D}
to the presheaf
N_{F}(d)\coloneqq\mathcal{D}(F(-),d)
.

Recall the following proposition due to Ulmer
[
11
, Lemma 1.7]
.

Proposition 9
.

For every functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, the following are equivalent:

•

Every object
d\in\mathcal{D}
is a colimit of objects in the image of
F
:

d\cong\colim(F\downarrow d\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{F}\mathcal{D})

Here
F\downarrow d
is the comma category whose objects are morphisms
F(c)\xrightarrow{f}d
and whose morphisms are morphisms
\alpha:c_{1}\to c_{2}
making this commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(c_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(c_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}

Also the functor
\pi_{\mathcal{C}}
is the canonical projection sending
F(c)\xrightarrow{f}d
to
c
.

•

The nerve
N_{F}:\mathcal{D}\to[\mathcal{C}^{\op},\Set]
is fully faithful.

A functor satisfying the conditions of
Proposition

9
is called
dense
.

Example 10
.

The Yoneda embedding
\yo:\mathcal{C}\to[\mathcal{C}^{\op},\Set]
is dense.
To see that, note that any presheaf is a colimit of representables, and the nerve is the identity.

Main results

We begin by showing that the inclusion
i:\Delta\hookrightarrow\Pos
is cocontinuous.
We break it down into two steps: (1) we show that the inclusion
\Delta\hookrightarrow\Tos
is cocontinuous, and (2) we show that the inclusion
\Tos\hookrightarrow\Pos
is cocontinuous.

Proposition 11
.

Let
f:N\to T
be an injective monotone map between totally ordered sets with
N
finite and non-empty.
Then
f
is a split monomorphism.

Proof.

Write
N=\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}
.
We define
g:T\to N
as follows:

g(t)=\begin{cases}x_{0}&\text{if }t<f(x_{0})\\
x_{i}&\text{if }f(x_{i})\leq t<f(x_{i+1})\\
x_{n}&\text{if }t\geq f(x_{n})\end{cases}

It is easy to see that
g\circ f=\id
, so
f
is a split monomorphism.
∎

Proposition 12
.

The inclusion

is cocontinuous.

Proof.

Let

We claim that

is non-empty since

We claim that it is also finite.
Now for every finite non-empty subset

Hence

is a cocone in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi[D]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{j}}

Note that

.

Proposition 14
.

The inclusion

Proof.

.

The inclusion

Corollary 24
.

\Pos
is the free conservative cocompletion of
\Delta
.

Therefore

satisfies the universal property of

That is, for every cocomplete category

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pos}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{F}}

According to Kelly

]
, the functor

can be given by a Left Kan extension:

\widetilde{F}(P)\coloneqq\colim(i\downarrow P\xrightarrow{\pi_{\Delta}}\Delta\xrightarrow{F}\mathcal{C})

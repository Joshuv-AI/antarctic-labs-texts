{summary}

Register automaton (RA) is an extension of
finite automaton for dealing with data values
in an infinite domain.
RA has good properties such as
the decidability of the membership and emptiness problems.
In the previous work,
we proposed
disjunctive
\mu^{\downarrow}
-calculus (
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus),
which is a subclass of
modal
\mu
-calculus with the freeze quantifier,
and showed that it has the same expressive power as RA.
However,

{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus is defined as a logic
on finite words,
whereas temporal specifications in model checking are
usually given in terms of infinite words.
In this paper,
we re-define the syntax and semantics of
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus
to be suitable for infinite words and
prove that the obtained temporal logic,
called
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus,
has the same expressive power as Büchi RA.
We have proved the correctness of
the equivalent transformation
between
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus
and Büchi RA
with a proof assistant software Coq.

1
Introduction

Register automaton (RA)
[
1
]
is an extension of
finite automaton (FA)
that has registers
for dealing with data values.
RA preserves good properties of FA such as
the closure property and
the decidability of the membership and
emptiness problems
[
2
,
3
]
.
RA has been used as a formal model
in verification of systems
[
4
]
and
reactive synthesis
[
5
]
.
Model checking is a technique
to verify whether every run of a model
\mathcal{M}

of a system
satisfies a given specification
\varphi
.
For a finite-state model of a system and a linear temporal logic (LTL)
specification,
the model checking
can be reduced to the emptiness problem of
a Büchi automaton
[
6
]
.
LTL model checking has also been shown to be decidable
for pushdown systems (PDS)
[
7
]

and register pushdown systems (RPDS)
[
8
]
.
These model-checking methods rely on the fact that
every LTL formula can be transformed into
an equivalent Büchi automaton.
To extend these model-checking methods to models with registers,
we need an appropriate temporal logic to give a specification,
which can be transformed into an equivalent Büchi RA.

In the previous work,
we proposed
disjunctive
\mu^{\downarrow}
-calculus
(
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus)
[
9
]
,
which is a subclass of
modal
\mu
-calculus with the freeze quantifier
(
\mu^{\downarrow}
-calculus)
[
10
]

and is shown to have the same expressive power as RA.
Since the class of RA is not closed under complement
and an RA does not have the ability to simulate logical conjunction,

{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus imposes restrictions on the usage of negation and conjunction.
On the other hand,
by a system of equations
for representing recursive properties,

{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus can
represent an arbitrary cycle in an RA,
which cannot be represented by
temporal operators such as

\mathord{\mathsf{X}}
(next),

\mathbin{\mathsf{U}}
(until) and
\mathord{\mathsf{G}}
(global) in LTL.
We can say that
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus is
one of the most expressive temporal logic
that can be equivalently transformed into RA.
However,
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus is defined as a logic
on finite words, whereas temporal specifications in model checking are
usually given in terms of infinite words.

In this paper,
we re-define the syntax and semantics of
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus
to be suitable for infinite words, and we
prove that the obtained temporal logic,
called
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus,
has the same expressive power as Büchi RA.
We have proved the correctness of
the equivalent transformation
between
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus
and Büchi RA
with a proof assistant software Coq.

The main difference between

{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}}
-calculus and
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus is that
the former requires only
the least solution of a system of equations
while the latter has to consider both
the least and greatest solutions of systems of equations.
For example,
a proposition
“every (resp. some)
odd position in a given infinite word
satisfies a given atomic proposition
p
,”
which can be represented by Büchi RA,
is represented
in
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus as
the greatest (resp. least)
solution of
a recursive equation
V=p\land\mathord{\mathsf{X}}\mathord{\mathsf{X}}V

(resp.
V=p\lor\mathord{\mathsf{X}}\mathord{\mathsf{X}}V
).
To deal with both the least and greatest solutions,

\mu^{\downarrow}
-calculus in
[
10
]
classifies
each variable (i.e. the left-hand side of each equation)
into two types,
\mu
and
\nu
.
A
\mu
-type (resp.
\nu
-type)
variable represents its least (resp. greatest) solution.
Moreover,

\mu^{\downarrow}
-calculus has a syntactic restriction that
a
\mu
-type variable and a
\nu
-type one cannot
mutually depend on each other.
Similar restriction is imposed in
usual modal
\mu
-calculus
[
11
]

that has
\mu
and
\nu
constructs
instead of systems of equations;
any two constructs cannot be mutually a subformula
of each other.
We do not follow these restrictions in
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus,
because they prevent simple correspondence
between states of a Büchi RA and
variables in
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus equations.
Focusing on the fact that we only
need the same expressive power as Büchi RA,
we define the semantics carefully and
keep the syntax and semantics of
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus simple
without introducing restrictions
on the dependence between variables.

.

A sequence

.

For an infinite data word

.
We also let

and

\mathrm{snd}((b,d))=d

.
Let

An

k

k
-tuple

\theta\in D^{k}
.
The value of

.
For

\theta[R\mathbin{\leftarrow}d](r)=d

3
Disjunctive
\mu
-calculus with the freeze quantifier
(
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus)

In the proposed logic named
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus,
each specification is given as a system of equations
and each equation is given by a formula in
the disjunctive LTL with the freeze quantifier
(
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
) defined as follows.

Definition 1

{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formulas
over
a set
\mathit{Var}
of variables, a set
\mathit{At}
of atomic propositions,
and
k
registers
are defined by the following
\psi
,
where

V\in\mathit{Var}
,
p\in\mathit{At}
,

R\subseteq[k]
and

\displaystyle\psi::={}

\displaystyle V\mid\psi\lor\psi\mid{\mathord{\downarrow}_{R}}\mathord{\mathsf{X}}\psi\land\phi\mid\mathtt{tt}\,,

\displaystyle\phi::={}

\displaystyle\mathtt{tt}\mid\mathtt{ff}\mid p\mid\neg p\mid\mathord{\uparrow}_{r}\mid\neg\mathord{\uparrow}_{r}\mid\phi\land\phi\,.

Basic
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formulas
are
formulas defined by the above

Let
\Phi_{k}
denote the set of basic
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formulas.

Temporal operator
\mathord{\mathsf{X}}
(next) has the same meaning
as in the usual LTL.
The freeze operator
\mathord{\downarrow}_{R}
represents the storing of
an input data value into registers specified by
R

The look-up operator
\mathord{\uparrow}_{r}
represents the proposition
that the stored value of the
r
-th register equals
the input data value.
Formal semantics of
these operators will be given later.
Since the class of RA is not closed under complement
and an RA does not have the ability to simulate logical conjunction,
negation can be applied to only atomic basic formulas,
and for a conjunction
\psi_{1}\land\psi_{2}
,
at least one of
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
should
be a basic
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formula.
Moreover, while the freeze operator
\mathord{\downarrow}_{R}
can
simultaneously update any number of registers
with the same data value,
the operator must be followed by
\mathord{\mathsf{X}}
.
We can consider
\mathord{\mathsf{X}}\psi
is an
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formula
since it is equivalent to
\mathord{\downarrow}_{\emptyset}\mathord{\mathsf{X}}\psi\land\mathtt{tt}
.
Similarly,
we can consider
\phi\in\Phi_{k}
is
also an
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formula
since
\phi
is equivalent to
\mathord{\downarrow}_{\emptyset}\mathord{\mathsf{X}}\mathtt{tt}\land\phi
.

A basic
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formula describes a proposition
on a single position in an infinite data word.
The semantics for a basic
{\mathrm{LTL}^{\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{d}\omega}
formula

\phi\in\Phi_{k}

is provided by the relation

w,i,\theta\models\phi
,
which intuitively means that
a position
i
of an infinite data word
w

satisfies
\phi
when the contents of registers
equal
\theta
.

Definition 2

For an infinite data word
w

,

\displaystyle w,i,\theta

\displaystyle\models p\quad

\displaystyle{}:\Leftrightarrow p\in\mathrm{fst}(w_{i}),

\displaystyle w,i,\theta

\displaystyle{}:\Leftrightarrow\theta(r)=\mathrm{snd}(w_{i}),

\displaystyle w,i,\theta

\displaystyle\models\mathtt{tt}

\displaystyle\;\text{always holds},

Formally,

satisfies
x

w

\sigma(V)
holds at position

\theta

If we were considering only the least solution

-calculus in

Instead of considering an infinite substitution

To handle such a sequence, we use 5-tuple

Definition 10

A

(

over

A transition rule whose second element is
\varepsilon

\varepsilon
-rule.
For each
\varepsilon
-rule

must be
\emptyset
.

The second element
\phi
of a transition rule is called

guard condition
of that rule,
which is either an element of
\Phi_{k}
or
\varepsilon
.
An expression
\phi\in\Phi_{k}
represents the condition
under which
a transition rule can be applied to an element of a data word.
For example,

can be

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(q,\phi,R)\mathbin{\rightarrow}q^{\prime}\in\delta\quad w,i,\theta\models\phi\\

Conclusion

In this paper,
we proposed

{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus,
which is a subclass of
\mu
-calculus with the freeze quantifier and
has the same expressive power as
Büchi RA.
We showed a transformation from a system of equations in
{\mu^{\downarrow}}_{\!\mathrm{d}\omega}
-calculus
to an equivalent Büchi RA as well as its reverse.
We proved the correctness of the transformations.
We have also provided formal proofs of those theorems
using the Coq proof assistant.

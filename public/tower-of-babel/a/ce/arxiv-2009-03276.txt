Ray–Singer Torsion and the Rumin Laplacian on Lens Spaces
Akira KITAOKA

Abstract
We express explicitly the analytic torsion functions associated with the Rumin complex on lens spaces in terms of the Hurwitz zeta function. In particular, we find that the functions vanish at the origin and determine the analytic torsions. Moreover, we have a formula between this torsion and the Ray–Singer torsion.

Introduction

Let
(M,H)
be a compact contact manifold of dimension
2n+1

and
E
be the flat vector bundle with unitary holonomy on
M
.
Rumin
[
12
]
introduced a complex

(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E),{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet})
,
which is
a subquotient of the de Rham complex of
E
.
A specific feature of the complex is that
the operator
D={\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{n}\colon\mathcal{E}^{n}(M,E)\to\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(M,E)
in ‘middle degree’ is a second-order,
while

{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{k}\colon\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E)\to\mathcal{E}^{k+1}(M,E)
for
k\not=n
are first order which are induced by
the exterior derivatives.
Let
a_{k}=1/\sqrt{|n-k|}
for
k\not=n
and
a_{n}=1
.
Then,
(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E),{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
,
where
{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}=a_{k}{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{k}
,
is also a complex.
We call
(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E),{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
the
Rumin complex
.
In virtue of the rescaling,
{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}
satisfies Kähler-type identities on Sasakian manifolds
[
13
, equation (34)]
, which include the case of lens spaces.

Let
\theta
be a contact form of
H

and

J
be an almost complex structure on
H
.
Then we may define a Riemann metric
g_{\theta,J}
on
TM
by extending the Levi metric
{\rm d}\theta(-,J-)
on
H

(see Section
2.1
).
Following
[
12
]
, we define the Rumin Laplacians
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}
associated with
(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E),{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
and the metric
g_{\theta,J}
by

\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}:=\begin{cases}({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}{}^{\dagger})^{2}+({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}{}^{\dagger}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}})^{2},&k\not=n,\,n+1,\\
({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}{}^{\dagger})^{2}+D^{\dagger}D,&k=n,\\
DD^{\dagger}+({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}{}^{\dagger}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}})^{2},&k=n+1.\end{cases}

Rumin showed that
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}
has discrete eigenvalues with finite multiplicities.

We next introduce the analytic torsion and metric of the Rumin complex
(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E),{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
by following
[
2
,
8
,
14
]
.
We define the
contact analytic torsion function
associated with
(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E),\allowbreak{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
by

\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}(M,E,g_{\theta,J})(s):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}(n+1-k)\zeta\big(\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}\big)(s),

(1.1)

where
\zeta\big(\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}\big)(s)
is the spectral zeta function of
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}
.
Since the Rumin Laplacians
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the Rockland condition by
[
12
, p. 300]
,
the spectral zeta function
\zeta\big(\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}\big)(s)
extends to a meromorphic function
on
\mathbb{C}
which is holomorphic at zero by
[
9
, Section 4]
.
Here, we use
0^{s}=1
.
We define the
contact analytic torsion

T_{\mathcal{E}}
by

2\log T_{\mathcal{E}}(M,E,g_{\theta,J})=\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}(M,E,g_{\theta,J})^{\prime}(0).

Let
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet},{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
be the cohomology of the Rumin complex.
We define the contact metric on
\det H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet},{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
by

\|\ \|_{\mathcal{E}}(M,E,g_{\theta,J})=T_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(M,E,g_{\theta,J})|\ |_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet})},

where
the metric

|\ |_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet})}
is induced by
L^{2}
metric on
\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E)

via identification of the cohomology classes by the harmonic forms on
\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E)
.

Rumin and Seshadri
[
14
]
defined another analytic torsion function
\kappa_{\mathrm{R}}
from

(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E),{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet})
, which is different from
\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}
except in dimension 3.
In dimension 3, they showed that

\kappa_{\mathrm{R}}(M,E,\allowbreak g_{\theta,J})(0)
is a contact invariant,
that is, independent of the metric
g_{\theta,J}
.
Moreover, on 3-dimensional Sasakian manifolds with
S^{1}
-action,
\kappa_{\mathrm{R}}(M,E,g_{\theta,J})(0)=0
.
Here,
Sasakian manifolds with
S^{1}
-action means Sasakian manifolds whose Reeb vector filed generates the circle action
S^{1}
.
3-dimensional Sasakian manifolds with
S^{1}
-action
are CR Seifert manifolds.
Furthermore, they showed that this analytic torsion and the Ray–Singer torsion
T_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,E,g_{\theta,J})
equal for flat bundles with unitary holonomy on 3-dimensional Sasakian manifolds with
S^{1}
action.

To extend the coincidence,
with
{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}
instead of
{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}
,
the author
[
8
]
showed that
T_{\mathcal{E}}\big(S^{2n+1}\!,\underline{\mathbb{C}},g_{\theta,J}\big)\\
=n!T_{\mathrm{dR}}\big(S^{2n+1},\underline{\mathbb{C}},g_{\theta,J}\big)
on the standard CR spheres
S^{2n+1}\big({\subset}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\big)
,
where
\underline{\mathbb{C}}
is the trivial line bundle.
Here the
standard CR sphere
is triple
\big(S^{2n+1},\theta,J\big)
,
where

\theta
is given the contact form by
\theta=\sqrt{-1}\big(\bar{\partial}-\partial\big)|z|^{2}

and

J
is an almost complex structure
J
induced from the complex structure of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
It is simply denoted by
S^{2n+1}
.
Moreover, Albin and Quan
[
1

showed the difference between the Ray–Singer torsion and the contact analytic torsion is given by some integrals of universal polynomials in the local invariants of the metric
on contact manifolds.

In this paper,
we extend this coincidence on lens spaces
and determine explicitly the analytic torsion functions associated with the Rumin complex
in terms of the Hurwitz zeta function.
Let
g_{\mathrm{std}}
be the standard metric on
S^{2n+1}

and we note that
g_{\theta,J}=4g_{\mathrm{std}}
.
Let
\mu,\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n+1}
be integers such that the
\nu_{j}
are coprime to
\mu

Let
\Gamma
be the subgroup of

generated by

\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n+1}):=\big(\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}\nu_{1}/\mu\big),\dots,\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}\nu_{n+1}/\mu\big)\big).

We denote the lens space by

K:=S^{2n+1}/\Gamma.

Let
\underline{\mathbb{C}}
be the trivial line bundle on
K
.
Fix
u\in\mathbb{Z}
and consider the unitary representation
\alpha_{u}\colon\pi_{1}(K)=\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(1)
, defined by

\alpha_{u}\big(\gamma^{\ell}\big):=\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}u\ell/\mu\big)\qquad\text{for}\ \ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let
E_{\alpha}
be the flat vector bundle associated with the unitary representation
{\alpha\colon\pi_{1}(K)=\Gamma\!\to\!\mathrm{U}(r)}
, and
E_{\alpha_{u}}=E_{u}
.
The sections of this bundle correspond to
\alpha_{u}
-equivariant functions on
S^{2n+1}
.

Our main result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be the lens space with
the contact form and the almost complex structure which are induced by the action
\Gamma
on the standard CR sphere

.

(1)

The contact analytic torsion function
of
(K,\underline{\mathbb{C}})

is given by

\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}(K,\underline{\mathbb{C}},g_{\theta,J})(s)=-(n+1)\bigr(1+2^{2s+1}\mu^{-2s}\zeta(2s)\bigl),

(1.2)

where

is the Riemann zeta function.
In particular, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}(K,\underline{\mathbb{C}},g_{\theta,J})(0)=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{E}}(K,\underline{\mathbb{C}},g_{\theta,J})=\left(\frac{4\pi}{\mu}\right)^{n+1}.

(1.4)

(2)

of
(K,E_{u})

for

is given by

\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}(K,E_{u},g_{\theta,J})(s)=-2^{2s}\mu^{-2s}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\bigl(\zeta(2s,A_{\mu}(u{\tau}_{j})/\mu)+\zeta(2s,A_{\mu}(-u{\tau}_{j})/\mu)\bigr),

.
In particular, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}(K,E_{u},g_{\theta,J})(0)=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{E}}(K,E_{u},g_{\theta,J})=\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\big|{\rm e}^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}u{\tau}_{j}/\mu}-1\big|.

(1.7)

The equations (
1.2
) and (

) extend the following results of
\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}
the spheres to on lens spaces.
Rumin and Seshadri
[

showed (

The author

From (

2
The Rumin complex

2.1
The Rumin complex on contact manifolds

We call
(M,H)
an orientable contact manifold of dimension
2n+1

if
H
is a subbundle of
TM
of codimension
1

and
there exists a 1-form
\theta
,
called a contact form,
such that

{\Ker(\theta\colon TM\!\to\!\mathbb{R})=H}

and
\theta\wedge({\rm d}\theta)^{n}\not=0
.
The Reeb vector field of
\theta
is the unique vector field
T
satisfying
\theta(T)=1
and
\Int_{T}{\rm d}\theta=0
, where
\Int_{T}
is the interior product with respect to
T
.

For
H
and
\theta
,
we call
J\in\End(TM)

an almost complex structure associated with
\theta
if

J^{2}=-\Id
on
H
,
JT=0
, and the Levi form
{\rm d}\theta(-,J-)
is positive definite on
H
.
Given
\theta
and
J
, we define a Riemannian metric
g_{\theta,J}
on
TM
by

\displaystyle g_{\theta,J}(X,Y):={\rm d}\theta(X,JY)+\theta(X)\theta(Y)\qquad\text{for}\ X,Y\in TM.

Let
*
be the Hodge star operator on
\wedge^{\bullet}T^{*}M
with respect to
g_{\theta,J}
.

Let
M
be a manifold,
\widetilde{M}
be the universal covering of
M
,

\pi_{1}(M)
be the fundamental group of
M
.
For each unitary representation
\alpha\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{U}(r)
,
we denote the flat vector bundle associated with
\alpha
by

E_{\alpha}:=\widetilde{M}\times_{\alpha}\mathbb{C}^{r}\to M.

Let
\nabla_{\alpha}
be the flat connection on
E_{\alpha}
induced from the trivial connection on
\widetilde{M}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
,
and
{\rm d}^{\nabla_{\alpha}}
be the exterior covariant derivative of
\nabla_{\alpha}
.

The Rumin complex
[
12
]
is defined on contact manifolds as follows.
We set
L:={\rm d}\theta\wedge

and
\Lambda:=*^{-1}L*
,
which is the adjoint operator of
L
with respect to the metric
g_{\theta,J}
at each point.
We set

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigwedge_{\prim}^{k}H^{*}:=\big\{v\in\textstyle\bigwedge^{k}H^{*}\,\big|\,\Lambda v=0\big\},

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigwedge_{L}^{k}H^{*}:=\big\{v\in\textstyle\bigwedge^{k}H^{*}\,\big|\,Lv=0\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha}):=\begin{cases}C^{\infty}\big(M,\bigwedge_{\prim}^{k}H^{*}\otimes E_{\alpha}\big),&k\leq n,\\
C^{\infty}\big(M,\theta\wedge\bigwedge_{L}^{k-1}H^{*}\otimes E_{\alpha}\big),&k\geq n+1.\end{cases}

We embed
H^{*}
into
T^{*}M
as the subbundle
\{\phi\in T^{*}M\,|\,\phi(T)=0\}
so that
we can regard

\displaystyle\Omega_{H}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha}):=C^{\infty}\big(M,\textstyle\bigwedge^{k}H^{*}\otimes E_{\alpha}\big)

as a subspace of
\Omega^{k}(M)
, the space of
k
-forms.
We define
{\rm d}_{b}\colon\Omega_{H}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})\to\Omega_{H}^{k+1}(M,E_{\alpha})
by

{\rm d}_{b}\phi:={\rm d}^{\nabla_{\alpha}}\phi-\theta\wedge\big(\Int_{T}{\rm d}^{\nabla_{\alpha}}\phi\big),

and then
D\colon\mathcal{E}^{n}(M,E_{\alpha})\to\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(M,E_{\alpha})
by

D=\theta\wedge\big(\mathcal{L}_{T}+{\rm d}_{b}L^{-1}{\rm d}_{b}\big),

where
\mathcal{L}_{T}
is the Lie derivative with respect to
T
,
and we use the fact that
L\colon\textstyle\bigwedge^{n-1}H^{*}\to\textstyle\bigwedge^{n+1}H^{*}
is an isomorphism.

Let
P\colon\textstyle\bigwedge^{k}H^{*}\to\textstyle\bigwedge_{\prim}^{k}H^{*}
be the fiberwise orthogonal projection with respect to
g_{\theta,J}
, which also defines a projection
P\colon\Omega^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})\to\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})
.
We set

{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{k}:=\begin{cases}P\circ{\rm d}^{\nabla_{\alpha}}&\text{on }\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha}),\ k\leq n-1,\\
D&\text{on }\mathcal{E}^{n}(M,E_{\alpha}),\\
{\rm d}^{\nabla_{\alpha}}&\text{on }\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha}),\ k\geq n+1.\end{cases}

Then
(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E_{\alpha}),{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet})
is a complex.
Let
{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}=a_{k}{\rm d}_{\mathrm{R}}^{k}
, where

a_{k}=1/\sqrt{|n-k|}
for
k\not=n
and

a_{n}=1
.
We call
(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E_{\alpha}),{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
the Rumin complex.

We define the
L^{2}
-inner product on
\Omega^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})
by

(\phi,\psi):=\int_{M}g_{\theta,J}(\phi,\psi)\,{\rm d}\vol_{g_{\theta,J}}

and the
L^{2}
-norm on
\Omega^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})
by
\|\phi\|:=\sqrt{(\phi,\phi)}
.
Since the Hodge star operator
*
induces a bundle isomorphism
from
\textstyle\bigwedge_{\prim}^{k}H^{*}
to
\theta\wedge\textstyle\bigwedge_{L}^{2n-k}H^{*}
,
it also induces a map
\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})\to\mathcal{E}^{2n+1-k}(M,E_{\alpha})
.
We note that

\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})=\big\{\phi\in\mathcal{E}^{k}\big(\widetilde{M},\mathbb{C}^{r}\big)\,|\,t_{*}\phi=\alpha(t)^{-1}\phi\text{ for }t\in\pi_{1}(M)\big\}.

Let
{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}
and
D^{\dagger}
denote the formal adjoint of
{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}
and
D
, respectively for the
L^{2}
-inner product.
We define the fourth-order Laplacians
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})
by

\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}:=\begin{cases}\big({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{k-1}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{k-1}{}^{\dagger}\big)^{2}+\big({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}{}^{\dagger}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}\big)^{2},&k\not=n,n+1,\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\big({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{n-1}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{n-1}{}^{\dagger}\big)^{2}+D^{\dagger}D,&k=n,\\
DD^{\dagger}+\big({\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{n+1}{}^{\dagger}{\rm d}_{\mathcal{E}}^{n+1}\big)^{2},&k=n+1.\end{cases}

We call it the Rumin Laplacian
[
12
]
.
Since
*
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}
commute,
to determine the eigenvalue on
\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}(M,E_{\alpha})
,
it is enough to compute them on
\mathcal{E}^{k}(M,E_{\alpha})
for
k\leq n
.

2.2
The Rumin complex on the CR spheres

Let
S:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\,|\,|z|^{2}=1\}

and

\theta:=\sqrt{-1}\big(\bar{\partial}-\partial\big)|z|^{2}
.
(We will omit the dimension from
S^{2n+1}
for the simplicity of the notation.)
Let
g_{\mathrm{std}}
be the standard metric on
S
.
Then,
g_{\theta,J}
coincides with
4g_{\mathrm{std}}

With respect to the standard almost complex structure

\displaystyle H^{*1,0}:=\big\{v\in\mathbb{C}H^{*}\,|\,Jv=\sqrt{-1}v\big\},

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigwedge^{i,j}H^{*}:=\textstyle\bigwedge^{i}H^{*1,0}\otimes\textstyle\bigwedge^{j}H^{*0,1},

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigwedge_{\prim}^{i,j}H^{*}:=\big\{\phi\in\textstyle\bigwedge^{i,j}H^{*}\,\big|\,\Lambda\phi=0\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{i,j}:=C^{\infty}\big(S,\textstyle\bigwedge_{\prim}^{i,j}H^{*}\big).

We decompose
\mathcal{E}^{i,j}
into a direct sum of irreducible representations of the unitary group

Recall that

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{m}

To simplify the notation, we introduce the following notation:

,
a_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

,
(\underline{a_{1}}_{k_{1}},\dots,\underline{a_{l}}_{k_{l}})
denotes the

whose first

, whose next

For example,

(\underline{1}_{3},\underline{0}_{2},\underline{-1}_{2})=(1,1,1,0,0,-1,-1).

We note that
\underline{a}_{1}
is
a
, and
\underline{a}_{0}
is the zero tuple.

In
[
7
]
, it is shown that
the multiplicity of

in
\mathcal{E}^{s,t}

is at most one.
Thus we may set

\Psi_{(q,j,i,p)}^{(s,t)}:=\mathcal{E}^{s,t}\cap V(q,\underline{1}_{j},\underline{0}_{n-1-i-j},\underline{-1}_{i},-p).

Proposition 2.1

(
[
7
, Section 4(b)]
)
.

Given
(q,j,i,p)
, we list up all
(s,t)
such that

and
\Psi_{(q,j,i,p)}^{(s,t)}\not=\{0\}
as the following:

Case I:

For

and

,
the space is

\Psi_{(0,0,0,0)}^{(0,0)}.

Case II:

For
i+j\leq n-2
,
p\geq 1
and
q\geq 1

\kappa_{3}(K,E_{u},g_{\theta,J})(s)=-2^{2s}\mu^{-2s}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\zeta\bigl(2s,A_{\mu}(u{\tau}_{j})/\mu\bigr).

(3.17)

From (

), (

), (
3.16
) and (

),

\kappa_{\mathcal{E}}(K,E_{u},g_{\theta,J})(s)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-(n+1)\bigl(1+2^{2s+1}\mu^{-2s}\zeta(2s)\bigr),\quad u=0.\\
\displaystyle-2^{2s}\mu^{-2s}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\big(\zeta\bigl(2s,A_{\mu}(u{\tau}_{j})/\mu\bigr)+\zeta\bigl(2s,1-A_{\mu}(u{\tau}_{j})/\mu\bigr)\big),\quad u\not=0.\end{cases}

It is known that

Using the above equations,

4
Ray–Singer torsion of the trivial bundle

We compute the analytic torsion of the trivial bundle on lens spaces.
We define the Ray–Singer torsion function associated with
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,E),{\rm d}^{\nabla}\big)
by

\kappa_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,E,g)(s):=\sum_{k=0}^{2n+1}(-1)^{k}k\zeta\big(\Delta_{\mathrm{dR},g}^{k}\big)(s),

where
\zeta\big(\Delta_{\mathrm{dR},g}^{k}\big)(s)
is the spectral zeta function of the
k
-th Hodge–de Rham Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathrm{dR},g}^{k}

with respect to
g
.
We define the Ray–Singer torsion
T_{\mathrm{dR}}

by

2\log T_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,E,g):=\kappa_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,E,g)^{\prime}(0).

Following the derivation of
[
10
, equation (3)]
, we have

2\log T_{\mathrm{dR}}(K,\underline{\mathbb{C}},g_{\mathrm{std}})=\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu-1}\sum_{j=0}^{2n}(-1)^{j+1}\zeta^{\prime}\big(0;j,\gamma^{\ell}\big),

where
\lambda_{m}
is the
m
-th eigenvalue of
{\rm d}^{\dagger}{\rm d}

and

\displaystyle\zeta\big(s;j,\gamma^{\ell}\big)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}^{-s}\Tr\big(\gamma^{\ell}\big\rvert_{X_{j,m}}\big),

\displaystyle X_{j,m}:=\big\{\phi\in\Omega^{j}(K)\,|\,{\rm d}^{\dagger}{\rm d}\phi=\lambda_{m}\phi\big\}.

We recall from
[
10
, p. 123]

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2n}(-1)^{j+1}\zeta\big(s;j,\gamma^{\ell}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\big(\Gamma(s)\big)^{-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2s-1}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(u(1-u)\bigr)^{s-1}\big(f_{0}(t,u)+f_{1}(t,\ell\nu/\mu)\big)\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}t+\mathcal{O}\big(s^{2}\big),

where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n+1})
and for
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,

\displaystyle f_{0}(t,u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}\left(\frac{2\sinh tu\sinh t(1-u)}{\sinh t}\right)^{j}-\frac{{\rm e}^{-(2n+1)tu}}{2\sinh tu}-\frac{{\rm e}^{-(2n+1)t(1-u)}}{2\sinh t(1-u)},

\displaystyle f_{1}(t,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}^{n+1}\left(1-\frac{\sinh t}{\cosh t-\cos 2\pi\sigma_{k}}\right).

We set

\displaystyle h_{0}(s):=\big(\Gamma(s)\big)^{-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2s-1}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(u(1-u)\bigr)^{s-1}f_{0}(t,u)\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle h_{1}\big(s,\gamma^{\ell}\big):=\big(\Gamma(s)\big)^{-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2s-1}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(u(1-u)\bigr)^{s-1}f_{1}(t,\ell\nu/\mu)\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}t.

From
[
10
, p. 125]
,
it is seen that

h_{1}\big(s,\gamma^{\ell}\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu-1}\Tr\big(\gamma^{j\ell}\big)\bigl(\zeta(2s,j/\mu)+\zeta(2s,1-j/\mu)\bigr)-2(n+1)\mu^{-2s}\zeta(2s),

where

\Tr\big(\gamma^{\ell}\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\ell\nu_{j}/\mu}+{\rm e}^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\ell\nu_{j}/\mu}\big).

Taking the average of
h_{1}(s,\gamma^{\ell})
, we have

\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu-1}h_{1}\big(s,\gamma^{\ell}\big)=-2(n+1)\mu^{-2s}\zeta(2s)

Using
\zeta^{\prime}(0)=-\log(2\pi)/2
, we get

\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu-1}h_{1}^{\prime}\big(0,\gamma^{\ell}\big)=2(n+1)\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mu}\right).

(4.1)

We recall the Ray–Singer torsion on spheres,

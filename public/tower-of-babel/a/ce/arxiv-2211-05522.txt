Abstract
We consider multi-group multicast precoding designs for cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems. To optimize the transmit and receive beamforming strategies, we focus on minimizing the sum of the maximum mean squared errors (MSEs) over the multicast groups, which is then approximated with the sum MSE to simplify the computation and signaling. We adopt an iterative bi-directional training scheme with uplink and downlink precoded pilots to cooperatively design the multi-group multicast precoders at each base station and the combiners at each user equipment in a distributed fashion. An additional group-specific uplink training resource is introduced, which entirely eliminates the need for backhaul signaling for channel state information (CSI) exchange. We also propose a simpler distributed precoding design based solely on group-specific pilots, which can be useful in the case of scarce training resources. Numerical results show that the proposed distributed methods greatly outperform conventional cell-free massive MIMO precoding designs that rely solely on local CSI.



I
Introduction

Cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) is a promising beyond-5G technology that aims at providing a uniform service over its coverage area
[
1
,
2
]
. In a cell-free massive MIMO system, a large number of base stations (BSs) are distributed across the network and are connected to a central processing unit (CPU) via backhaul links to exchange the channel state information (CSI) and the user-specific data
[
3
]
. All the BSs jointly serve all the user equipments (UEs) simultaneously, which eliminates the inter-cell interference and enhances the spectral efficiency. Cell-free massive MIMO systems can outperform traditional cellular massive MIMO, and small-cell networks in many practical scenarios
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Moreover, their performance can be considerably improved by allowing coordination among the BSs
[
4
]
.

The demand for multicasting transmissions is increasing on account of applications such as vehicular communications and augmented/mixed reality
[
1
,
5
]
. In multicasting, the same data is transmitted to a group of UEs with a group-specific precoder. The multicast precoding design framework was presented in
[
6
]
and extended to the multi-group setting in
[
7
]
. Recently, multi-group multicasting in massive MIMO has been studied in
[
8
,
9
]
. A few works have considered multi-group multicasting via cell-free massive MIMO, which adopts conjugate beamforming for the data transmission
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. In
[
10
]
, equal transmit powers are allocated to all the UEs to avoid any backhaul signaling for CSI exchange. On the other hand,
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
tackle the max-min fairness problem to compute the transmit powers at the UEs and guarantee the same rate within each multicast group at the cost of extra backhaul signaling. While
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
consider single-antenna UEs,
[
13
]
assumes multi-antenna UEs and antenna-specific pilots for the CSI acquisition.

Considering multi-group multicasting via cell-free massive MIMO with multi-antenna UEs, we aim to cooperatively design the multi-group multicast precoders at each BS and the combiners at each UE in a distributed fashion. To this end, we initially target the minimization of the sum of the maximum mean squared errors (MSEs) over the multicast groups, which we refer to in the following as the
sum-group MSE
. While the sum-group MSE minimization achieves absolute fairness within each multicast group, the resulting distributed precoding design depends on slowly varying dual variables, which results in slow convergence. Instead, we propose to solve a simpler sum MSE minimization problem, which also provides a degree of in-built fairness among all the UEs. This approach provides a good approximation for the sum-group MSE minimization, especially at a medium-to-high signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR). Then, we propose a novel distributed precoding design for multi-group multicasting based on iterative bi-directional training
[
14
]
. Differently from our previous works
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
on the unicasting scenario, we introduce an additional group-specific uplink training resource that entirely eliminates the need for backhaul signaling for CSI exchange in multi-group multicasting. We also present a simpler precoding design that relies uniquely on group-specific pilots
[
18
]
, which can be useful when the training resources are scarce. Numerical results show that the proposed distributed methods bring substantial gains over conventional cell-free massive MIMO precoding designs that utilize only local CSI.

II
System Model

Consider a cell-free massive MIMO system where a set of BSs
\mathcal{B}\triangleq\{1,\ldots,B\}
serves a set of UEs
\mathcal{K}\triangleq\{1,\ldots,K\}
in the downlink. Each BS and UE is equipped with
M
and
N
antennas, respectively. The UEs are divided into a set of non-overlapping multicast groups
\mathcal{G}\triangleq\{1,\ldots,G\}
. We use
\mathcal{K}_{g}
to denote the set of UEs in group
g\in\mathcal{G}
, whereas
g_{k}
represents the index of the group that contains UE
k
. The BSs transmit a single data stream to each multicast group, i.e., all the UEs in
\mathcal{K}_{g}
are intended to receive the same data symbol
d_{g}
. Assuming time division duplex (TDD) mode, let
\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times N}
denote the uplink channel matrix between UE
k\in\mathcal{K}
and BS
b\in\mathcal{B}
, and let
\mathbf{w}_{b,g}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times 1}
be the BS-specific precoder used by BS
b
for group
g
. We use
\mathbf{H}_{k}\triangleq[\mathbf{H}_{1,k}^{\mathrm{T}},\ldots,\mathbf{H}_{B,k}^{\mathrm{T}}]^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{BM\times N}
and
\mathbf{w}_{g}\triangleq[\mathbf{w}_{1,g}^{\mathrm{T}},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{B,g}^{\mathrm{T}}]^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{BM\times 1}
to denote the aggregated uplink channel matrix of UE
k
and the aggregated precoder used for group
g
, respectively, which imply
\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{g}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{b,g}
. We assume the per-BS transmit power constraints
\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\mathbf{w}_{b,g}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}},\penalty\ \forall b\in\mathcal{B}
, where
\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}}
denotes the maximum transmit power at each BS. Hence, the received signal at UE
k
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{k}\triangleq\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\!\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{b,g_{k}}d_{g_{k}}\!+\!\sum_{\bar{g}\neq g_{k}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\!\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{b,\bar{g}}d_{\bar{g}}\!+\!\mathbf{z}_{k}\!\in\!\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times 1},

where
\mathbf{z}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N})
is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN). Upon receiving
\mathbf{y}_{k}
, UE
k
applies the combiner
\mathbf{v}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times 1}
to obtain a soft estimate of
d_{g}
and the resulting SINR can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{SINR}_{k}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{b,g_{k}}|^{2}}{\sum_{\bar{g}\neq g_{k}}|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{b,\bar{g}}|^{2}+\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{k}\|^{2}}.

(2)

Finally, the sum of the rates over the multicast groups, which we refer to in the following as the
sum-group rate
, is given by

\displaystyle R\triangleq\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\log_{2}(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k})\quad\textrm{[bps/Hz]}.

(3)

Note that (
3
) represents an upper bound on the system performance that is attained in presence of perfect global CSI.

In Section
IV
, we propose distributed precoding designs based on iterative bi-directional training to cooperatively compute the multi-group multicast precoders at each BS and the combiners at each UE. We also present the centralized precoding design along with the problem formulation in Section
III
.

II-A

Bi-Directional Training and Channel Estimation

The proposed distributed precoding designs rely on iterative bi-directional training, where uplink and downlink precoded pilots are transmitted at each iteration
[
14
]
. On the other hand, the centralized precoding design requires each BS to estimate the antenna-specific uplink channels. We now describe the different types of pilot-aided channel estimation that will be used in Sections
III
and
IV
.

Antenna-specific uplink channel estimation.
The estimation of the channel matrix
\mathbf{H}_{b,k}
involves
N
antenna-specific uplink pilots for UE
k
. Let
\mathbf{P}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{\tau\times N}
be the pilot matrix assigned to UE
k
, with
\|\mathbf{P}_{k}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=\tau N
and
\mathbf{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{P}_{k}=\tau\mathbf{I}_{N}
. Moreover, let
\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}
denote the maximum transmit power at each UE. Each UE
k
synchronously transmits its pilot matrix, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\triangleq\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}}\mathbf{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times\tau},

(4)

where the power scaling factor
\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\triangleq\frac{\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}}{N}
ensures that
\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
complies with the UE transmit power constraint. Then, the received signal at BS
b
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}

\displaystyle\ \triangleq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}+\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}

(5)

\displaystyle=\ \sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\mathbf{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}+\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times\tau},

(6)

where
\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
is the AWGN with i.i.d.
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}}^{2})
elements.

Finally, the least-squares (LS) estimate of
\mathbf{H}_{b,k}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{b,k}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{\tau\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}}}\mathbf{Y}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\mathbf{P}_{k}

(7)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{b,k}+\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{\bar{k}\neq k}\mathbf{H}_{b,\bar{k}}\mathbf{P}_{\bar{k}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{P}_{k}+\frac{1}{\tau\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}}}\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\mathbf{P}_{k}.

(8)

UE-specific effective uplink channel estimation.
Let
\mathbf{h}_{b,k}\triangleq\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\mathbf{v}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times 1}
denote the effective uplink channel between UE
k
and BS
b
, and let
\mathbf{p}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{\tau\times 1}
be the pilot assigned to UE
k
, with
\|\mathbf{p}_{k}\|^{2}=\tau
. Each UE
k
synchronously transmits its pilot
\mathbf{p}_{k}
using its combiner
\mathbf{v}_{k}
as precoder, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}\triangleq\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}}\mathbf{v}_{k}\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times\tau},

(9)

where the power scaling factor
\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}
ensures that
\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}
complies with the UE transmit power constraint. Then, the received signal at BS
b
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}+\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}

(10)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{h}_{b,k}\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}+\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times\tau},

(11)

where
\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}
is the AWGN with i.i.d.
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}}^{2})
elements. Finally, the LS estimate of
\mathbf{h}_{b,k}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{b,k}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{\tau\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}}}\mathbf{Y}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}\mathbf{p}_{k}

(12)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{h}_{b,k}+\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{\bar{k}\neq k}\mathbf{h}_{b,\bar{k}}\mathbf{p}_{\bar{k}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{p}_{k}+\frac{1}{\tau\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}}}\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-1}}}\mathbf{p}_{k}.

(13)

Group-specific effective uplink channel estimation.
Let
\mathbf{f}_{b,g}\triangleq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\mathbf{v}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times 1}

\mathcal{K}_{g}

b
, and let
\mathbf{p}_{g}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{\tau\times 1}
be the pilot assigned to group
g
, with
\|\mathbf{p}_{g}\|^{2}=\tau
. Each UE
k
synchronously transmits its pilot
\mathbf{p}_{g_{k}}
using its combiner
\mathbf{v}_{k}
as precoder, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}\triangleq\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}}\mathbf{v}_{k}\mathbf{p}_{g_{k}}^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times\tau},

(14)

where the power scaling factor
\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}
ensures that
\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}
complies with the UE transmit power constraint. Then, the received signal at BS
b
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\mathbf{X}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}+\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}

(15)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{f}_{b,g}\mathbf{p}_{g}^{\mathrm{H}}+\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times\tau},

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{b,g}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{\tau\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}}}\mathbf{Y}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}\mathbf{p}_{g}

(17)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}_{b,g}+\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{\bar{g}\neq g}\mathbf{f}_{b,\bar{g}}\mathbf{p}_{\bar{g}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{p}_{g}+\frac{1}{\tau\sqrt{\beta^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}}}\mathbf{Z}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL-2}}}\mathbf{p}_{g}.

\mathbf{g}_{k}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{\tau}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\mathbf{p}_{g_{k}}

III
Problem Formulation

In this section, we present the problem formulation for the proposed multi-group multicast precoding design focusing on the centralized method, where the aggregated precoders are computed at the CPU. In doing so, we first target the sum-group MSE minimization in Section
III-A
and then propose to solve a simpler sum MSE minimization problem in Section
III-A
.

III-A

Sum-Group MSE Minimization

The sum-group MSE minimization achieves absolute fairness within each multicast group through the min-max MSE criterion and can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\underset{{\{\mathbf{w}_{g},\mathbf{v}_{k}\}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\max_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\mathbf{E}_{b}\mathbf{w}_{g}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}},\quad\forall b\in\mathcal{B},\end{array}

where
\mathrm{MSE}_{k}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{MSE}_{k}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{y}_{k}-d_{g_{k}}|^{2}\big]

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{g}|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}[\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{g_{k}}]

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\ +\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{k}\|^{2}+1,

(27)

and
\mathbf{E}_{b}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{M\times BM}
is such that
\mathbf{E}_{b}\mathbf{w}_{g}=\mathbf{w}_{b,g}
. The problem in (
III-A
) is convex with respect to either the precoders or the combiners. Hence, we use
alternating optimization
, whereby the precoders are optimized for fixed combiners and vice versa in an iterative best-response fashion. Before describing each step of the alternating optimization, let us define
t_{g}\triangleq\max_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}
and rewrite (
III-A
) as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\underset{{\{t_{g},\mathbf{w}_{g},\mathbf{v}_{k}\}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}t_{g}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&\displaystyle\mathrm{MSE}_{k}\leq t_{g},\quad\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\penalty\ \forall g\in\mathcal{G}\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\mathbf{E}_{b}\mathbf{w}_{g}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}},\quad\forall b\in\mathcal{B}.\end{array}

Optimization of the combiners.
For a fixed set of precoders, the combiners
\{\mathbf{v}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
are obtained by solving

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\underset{{\{t_{g},\mathbf{v}_{k}\}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}t_{g}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&\displaystyle\mathrm{MSE}_{k}\leq t_{g},\quad\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\penalty\ \forall g\in\mathcal{G}.\end{array}

Specifically, the optimal
\mathbf{v}_{k}
can be obtained by computing the stationary point of the Lagrangian of (
III-A
), which yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{k}=\bigg(\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{g}\mathbf{w}_{g}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{k}+\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N}\bigg)^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{g_{k}}.

(33)

Optimization of the precoders.
For a fixed set of combiners, the precoders
\{\mathbf{w}_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
are obtained by solving

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\underset{{\{t_{g},\mathbf{w}_{g}\}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}t_{g}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&\displaystyle\mathrm{MSE}_{k}\leq t_{g},\quad\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\penalty\ \forall g\in\mathcal{G}\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\mathbf{E}_{b}\mathbf{w}_{g}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}},\quad\forall b\in\mathcal{B}.\end{array}

Specifically, the optimal
\mathbf{w}_{g}
can be obtained by computing the stationary point of the Lagrangian of (
III-A
), which yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{g}=\bigg(\!\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\!\nu_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\!+\!\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\!\lambda_{b}\mathbf{E}_{b}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{E}_{b}\!\bigg)^{-1}\!\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\!\nu_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k},

(37)

where
\nu_{k}
and
\lambda_{b}
are the dual variables corresponding to the first and second constraints in (
III-A
), respectively. Note that
\nu_{k}
and
\lambda_{b}
can be updated using the sub-gradient and ellipsoid methods, respectively.

The combiner in (
33
) can be computed locally at each UE. However, the local computation of the precoder in (
37
) at each BS requires BS-specific CSI from all the other BSs
[
15
]
. Moreover, the sub-gradient update of the dual variables
\{\nu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
significantly slows down the convergence. To simplify the distributed precoding design, we thus propose to replace the sum-group MSE minimization with the sum MSE minimization, as described next.

III-B

Sum MSE Minimization

To circumvent the shortcomings of the sum-group MSE minimization, we propose to tackle the (weighted) sum MSE minimization, which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\underset{{\{\mathbf{w}_{g},\mathbf{v}_{k}\}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mu_{k}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\mathbf{E}_{b}\mathbf{w}_{g}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{BS}}},\quad\forall b\in\mathcal{B},\end{array}

where
\mu_{k}
is the weight of UE
k
. This choice stems from the fact that (
III-B
) provides a degree of in-built fairness among all the UEs, especially at high SINR. More specifically, at high SINR, the sum MSE minimization corresponds to maximizing the minimum SINR across all the UEs and well approximates the sum-group MSE minimization in (
III-A
). This is formalized in Proposition
1
, whose proof is omitted due to the space limitations and will be presented in the longer version of this paper.

Proposition 1
.

At high SINR, both the sum-group MSE minimization in (
III-A
) and the sum MSE minimization in (
III-B
) maximize the minimum SINR across all the UEs.

Note that, regardless of the high-SINR assumption, (
III-B
) becomes equivalent to (
III-A
) if
\mu_{k}=\nu_{k},\penalty\ \forall k
at each iteration of the alternating optimization. However, optimally tuning
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
at each iteration leads to the same complexity and signaling requirements as the sum-group MSE minimization. To simplify the distributed precoding design, we fix
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
to the same value at all the iterations. Though slightly suboptimal, this approach leads to much simpler computation and signaling as well as faster convergence. In this context, the optimal
\mathbf{w}_{g}
can be obtained by computing the stationary point of the Lagrangian of (
III-B
) for a fixed set of combiners, which yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{g}=\bigg(\!\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\!\mu_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\!+\!\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\!\lambda_{b}\mathbf{E}_{b}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{E}_{b}\!\bigg)^{-1}\!\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\!\mu_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}.

(40)

Furthermore, the optimal
\mathbf{v}_{k}
of (
III-B
) for a fixed set of precoders corresponds to (
33
). Hereafter, we consider the sum MSE minimization to design the multi-group multicast precoders.

At this stage, we briefly illustrate the centralized precoding design with pilot-aided channel estimation. The algorithm begins with the antenna-specific uplink channel estimation described in Section
II-A
, whereby each BS
b
obtains
\{\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{b,k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
and forwards them to the CPU via backhaul signaling. Then, the CPU computes the combiners
\{\mathbf{v}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
and the aggregated precoders
\{\mathbf{w}_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}

VI
Conclusions

We proposed multi-group multicast precoding designs for cell-free massive MIMO systems. Initially, the sum-group MSE minimization was considered to achieve absolute fairness among the UEs within each multicast group. Subsequently, to simplify the computation and signaling, the sum-group MSE was approximated with the sum MSE. This approximation is accurate in the medium-to-high SINR regime. We utilized iterative bi-directional training with an extra uplink training resource to cooperatively design the precoders at each BS and the combiners at each UE in a distributed fashion. We also proposed a simpler distributed precoding design considering only group-specific pilots, which is useful when the training resources are limited. The proposed distributed precoding designs greatly outperform conventional cell-free massive MIMO precoding techniques such as local MMSE precoding.

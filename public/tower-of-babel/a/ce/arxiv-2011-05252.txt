Addendum. The spaces
L^{2}
on
T_{+}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\setminus\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})

A.1. The principal series of unitary representations
of the groups
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
.

Denote by
\Lambda
the set of pairs
\lambda|\lambda^{\prime}
, where

\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\lambda=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\lambda^{\prime}
. In other words, we consider pairs
of the type

\lambda|\lambda^{\prime}=\tfrac{k+is}{2}\Bigl|\tfrac{-k+is}{2},\qquad\text{where $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, $s\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

For
\lambda|\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
we have a well defined ’generalized power’
of any nonzero
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
:

z^{\lambda|\lambda^{\prime}}=z^{\lambda}\overline{z}^{\,\lambda^{\prime}}:=(z/|z|)^{k}\cdot|z|^{is}.

Consider the subgroup
\mathrm{B}_{+}(n):=\mathrm{P}_{+}(1,\dots,1)\subset\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
consisting of all upper triangular
matrices,

C:=\begin{pmatrix}c_{11}&c_{12}&\dots&c_{1n}\\
0&c_{22}&\dots&c_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&c_{nn}\end{pmatrix},

(A.1)

A
signature

{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a collection
of the form

{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}:=(\lambda_{1}|\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}|\lambda^{\prime}_{n}),\qquad\text{where $\lambda_{j}|\lambda^{\prime}_{j}\in\Lambda$}.

For such
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
denote by
\chi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(A)
the character
of
\mathrm{B}_{+}(n)
defined by

\chi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(A):=\prod c_{jj}^{\lambda_{j}|\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}.

(A.2)

By
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
we denote the representation
of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
unitary induced in the sense of Mackey (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
, Subsect. 13.2,
[
1
]
, Sect. 16, Sect.19.1) from a one-dimensional
representation
\chi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
of the subgroup
\mathrm{B}_{+}(n)
.
Such representations are called representations of the
unitary
nondegenerate principal series
, see, e.g.,
[
1
]
, Sect. 19.3.
Representations
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}

are equivalent iff a collection
\{\mu_{k}|\mu_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k=1,\dots,n}
is obtained
from a collection
\{\lambda_{j}|\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j=1,\dots,n}
by a permutation.
Denote by
\Sigma_{n}
the set of all signatures determined upto a permutation.

Next, consider the action of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
on
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})

by left and right multiplications,
g\mapsto h_{1}^{-1}gh_{2}
. This determines
the left-right regular representation of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
in

L^{2}\bigl(\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\bigr)
. According Gelfand and Naimark, see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
Sect. 14.4.A, this representation
decomposes as a direct integral

\int_{\Sigma_{n}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\otimes\rho_{-{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\,d{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

(A.3)

of representations of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
.

A.2. The space
L^{2}(\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})\setminus\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\bigr)
.

Let us decompose a quasiregular representation of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
in
L^{2}(\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})\setminus\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\bigr)
.
This problem has an additional symmetry.
Indeed, the subgroup
\mathrm{P}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})
normalizes

\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})
. Therefore the quotient group

\mathrm{P}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})/\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{p}\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{i},{\mathbb{C}})

acts on
\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})\setminus\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
by left multiplications.
So we get a unitary representation of the group

G:=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{i},{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})

(A.4)

in our
L^{2}
.

For elements
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{j}\in\Sigma_{\alpha_{j}}
denote by

{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}\in\Sigma_{n}

the row obtained by concatenation of rows
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{j}
.

Theorem A.1.

The decomposition of
L^{2}(\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})\setminus\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\bigr)

under the action of the group
G=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{i},{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})

is multiplicity-free and has the form

\int\limits_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}\in\Sigma_{\alpha_{1}}}\dots\int\limits_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}\in\Sigma_{\alpha_{p}}}\Bigl(\rho_{-{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\rho_{-{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}\Bigr)\otimes\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}\,d{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}\dots d{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}.

Proof.
We have a space homogeneous with respect to the group
G
. The stabilizer

G_{0}

of the initial point consists of tuples

\left\{b_{1}\in\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{1}),\dots,b_{p}\in\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{p}),\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&a_{12}&\dots&a_{1n}\\
0&b_{2}&\dots&a_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&b_{p}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{P}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})\right\}.

By definition our representation is induced from the trivial representation
of the stabilizer
G_{0}
. Consider a lager group

G_{0}^{*}=\prod\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{i},{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{P}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}).

We apply inducing in stages (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
, Subsect.13.1,
[
1
]
, Sect. 16.2),
first from
G_{0}
to
G_{0}^{*}
, second from
G_{0}^{*}
to
G
.

On the first step we have the same normal subgroup
\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})

in both
G_{0}
to
G_{0}^{*}
, since the initial representation of
G_{0}
is trivial, the induced representation
is trivial on
\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})
. In fact we have the induction from

G_{0}/\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})\qquad\text{to}\qquad G_{0}^{*}/\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}).

The second group is the double
\prod\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}})\times\prod\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}})
, the first
group is
\prod\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}})
embedded to the double as the diagonal. So the induced representation
is the left-right representation of the double. Clearly, it is equivalent to the tensor product
of the left-right regular representations of the factors
\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}})
,

L^{2}\Bigl(\prod\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}})\Bigr)=\bigotimes_{j}L^{2}\bigl(\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}})\bigr).

We decompose spaces
L^{2}(\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j},{\mathbb{C}}))

according (A.3).

In this way, we come to a direct integral of irreducible representations of
G_{0}^{*}

having the form

\Bigl(\rho_{-{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}}(b_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes\rho_{-{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}(b_{p})\Bigr)\otimes\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1},\dots,{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}},

(A.5)

where
\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1},\dots,{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}
is the representation of
\mathrm{P}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})
,
which is trivial on
\mathrm{T}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})
and is defined by

\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1},\dots,{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}&\dots&a_{1n}\\
0&a_{22}&\dots&a_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&a_{pp}\end{pmatrix}=\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}}(a_{11})\otimes\dots\otimes\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}(a_{pp}).

Notice, that
\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1},\dots,{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}
itself is an induced
representation. It is induced from a one-dimensional representation of
\mathrm{B}_{+}(n)
, see
(A.1), namely from the character given by the formula

\zeta(C)=\prod_{j=1}^{\alpha_{1}}c_{jj}^{\lambda_{j}^{1}\bigl|(\lambda_{j}^{1})^{\prime}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{\alpha_{2}}c_{(\alpha_{1}+j)(\alpha_{1}+j)}^{\lambda_{j}^{2}\bigr|(\lambda_{j}^{2})^{\prime}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{\alpha_{3}}c_{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+j)(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+j)}^{\lambda_{j}^{3}\bigr|(\lambda_{j}^{3})^{\prime}}\cdot\,\dots=\\
=\chi_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}(C),

(2.17)

where
\chi\dots(C)
is determined by (A.1).

Next, we consider the representation of
G
induced from an irreducible representation
(A.5)
of
G_{0}^{*}
. Since the factor
\prod\mathrm{GL}(\alpha_{j})
is present in both groups
G_{0}^{*}
and
G
,
actually we have the induction from
\mathrm{P}_{+}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p})
to

\mathrm{GL}(n)
(formally, we can refer to
[
1
]
, Sect.16.2.D, Theorem 3). But the representation
\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1},\dots,{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}
itself
is induced from the character (A.6) of
\mathrm{B}_{+}(n)
. Applying inducing in stages
we get that our representation of
\mathrm{GL}(n)
is induced from the character (A.6)
of
\mathrm{B}_{+}(n)
. But this a representation
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{p}}
of the principal series.

L-values of elliptic curves twisted by cubic characters
David Kurniadi Angdinata

Abstract
Given a rational elliptic curve E E of analytic rank zero, its L-function can be twisted by an even primitive Dirichlet character χ \chi of order q q , and in many cases its associated central algebraic L-value ℒ ​ ( E , χ ) \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)} is known to be integral. This paper derives some arithmetic consequences from a congruence between ℒ ​ ( E , 1 ) \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)} and ℒ ​ ( E , χ ) \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)} arising from this integrality, with an emphasis on cubic characters χ \chi . These include q q -adic valuations of the denominator of ℒ ​ ( E , 1 ) \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)} , determination of ℒ ​ ( E , χ ) \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)} in terms of Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer invariants, and asymptotic densities of ℒ ​ ( E , χ ) \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)} modulo q q by varying χ \chi .

Introduction

The Hasse–Weil L-function
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,s\right)}
of an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
can be twisted by a primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
to get a twisted L-function
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
. The algebraic and analytic properties of these L-functions are studied extensively in the literature, and they are the subject of many problems in the arithmetic of elliptic curves. Classically, the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture relates
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,s\right)}
to certain algebraic invariants of
E
, which encode important global arithmetic information of
E
. When
E
is base changed to a cyclotomic extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
, Artin’s formalism for L-functions says that
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{(E/K,s)}
decomposes into a product of twisted L-functions
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
over all Dirichlet characters
\chi
that factor through
K
, so the behaviour of
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{(E/K,s)}
, and hence the arithmetic of
E/K
, is completely governed by these twisted L-functions.

By the modularity theorem,
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
admits an analytic continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
, so its value at
s=1
is well-defined. When it does not vanish,
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,1\right)}
can be normalised by periods to get an algebraic twisted L-value
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
. Under some mild hypotheses on
E
and
\chi
, Wiersema–Wuthrich proved that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
for non-trivial primitive Dirichlet characters
\chi
of order
q

[
WW22
, Theorem 2]
, by expressing
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
in terms of Manin’s modular symbols. Parts of their argument can be adapted to obtain a congruence between
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
and
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}
modulo the prime
\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
in
\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
above
q
, and this turns out to have arithmetic consequences for
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
. This paper derives a few consequences for when
q=3
.

As one consequence, this addresses a problem of Dokchitser–Evans–Wiersema, in an attempt to obtain a Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer-type formula for twisted L-values
[
DEW21
]
. Under standard arithmetic conjectures, they expressed the norm of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
in terms of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer quotients
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}
, where
K
is the number field that
\chi
factors through, and deduced furthermore that there are only finitely many possibilities for the ideal generated by
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}

[
DEW21
, Theorem 38]
. A subsequent refinement by Burns–Castillo recovers the ideal factorisation of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
in terms of the Galois module structure of the Tate–Shafarevich group
[
BC21
, Remark 7.4]
, but the precise value of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
is only predicted up to a unit in
\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
. In particular, there are pairs of elliptic curves with identical Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer quotients but with distinct twisted L-values
[
DEW21
, Section 4]
. The following result determines this unit for cubic characters, and hence explains their examples, under an assumption on the Manin constant
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
5.2
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of conductor
N
, and let
\chi
be a cubic Dirichlet character of odd prime conductor
p\nmid N
such that

. Assume that Stevens’s conjecture holds for

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}=u\cdot\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}}\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}},

for some sign

In particular, all of the arithmetically identical pairs of elliptic curves considered by Dokchitser–Evans–Wiersema have distinct twisted L-values simply because their number of points modulo
p
are distinct modulo
3

As another consequence, this explains some of the patterns in Kisilevsky–Nam, where they made heuristic predictions on the asymptotic distribution of twisted L-values
[
KN22
]

of analytic rank zero and five positive integers

\!\mathinner{\left(E,q\right)}

\!\mathinner{\left(E,q\right)}

\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\!\mathinner{\left\{\chi\in X_{E,q}^{<n}\ \middle|\ \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\lambda\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle\right\}}}{\#X_{E,q}^{<n}},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

\!\mathinner{\left(\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(0\right)},\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(1\right)},\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(2\right)}\right)}

This classification builds upon the fact that
\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq-1

\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}

\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
in its numerator, so
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq-1
for any prime
q>3
. On the other hand, he noted that there is an explicit family of elliptic curves with

but with no such cancellations

Background and conventions

This section establishes some relevant background on Galois representations and L-functions of elliptic curves, as well as some notational conventions that might be deemed less standard in the literature.

\mathrm{Nm}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{n}\right)}\to\mathbb{Q}

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\neq 1
in general. Nevertheless, every modular parameterisation by
\mathrm{X}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
factors through a modular parameterisation by the modular curve
\mathrm{X}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
associated to the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}

[
Ste89
, Theorem 1.9]
, and an analogous construction using
\mathrm{X}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
yields the Manin constant
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
with the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2.1

(Stevens)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve. Then
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
.

The L-function
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,s\right)}
of
E
is defined to be the Euler product of
\mathrm{L}_{p}\!\mathinner{\bigl(\rho_{E,q}^{\vee},p^{-s}\bigr)}^{-1}
over all primes
p
, where
\rho_{E,q}^{\vee}
is the dual of the Artin representation associated to
\rho_{E,q}
for some prime number
q\neq p
. Here, for an Artin representation
\rho
, the local Euler factors are given by
\mathrm{L}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(\rho,T\right)}\coloneqq\det\!\mathinner{\bigl(1-T\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{p}^{-1}\mid\rho^{p}\bigr)}
, where
\rho^{p}
is the subrepresentation of
\rho
fixed by the inertia subgroup at
p
. The modularity theorem says that
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,s\right)}
is the Hecke L-function of
f_{E}
, so its order of vanishing at
s=1
is well-defined.

The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture predicts this order of vanishing and its leading term in terms of arithmetic invariants as follows. Let
\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\mathrm{rk}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
denote the torsion subgroup and the rank of the Mordell–Weil group
E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)}
respectively. Let
\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
denote the real period given by
\int_{E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}\omega_{E}
, with orientation chosen such that
\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}>0
. Let
\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
denote the Tamagawa product of local Tamagawa numbers
\mathrm{Tam}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
at each prime
p
. Let
\mathrm{Reg}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
denote the elliptic regulator defined in terms of the Néron–Tate pairing
\!\left\langle P,Q\right\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{h}_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(P+Q\right)}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{h}_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(P\right)}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{h}_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(Q\right)}
, where
\mathrm{h}_{E}
is the canonical height on
E
. Finally, let
\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
denote the Tate–Shafarevich group, which is implicitly assumed to be finite in this paper.

Conjecture 2.2

(Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve. Then the order of vanishing
r
of
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,s\right)}
at
s=1
is equal to
\mathrm{rk}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, and its leading term satisfies

\lim_{s\to 1}\dfrac{\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,s\right)}}{\!\mathinner{\left(s-1\right)}^{r}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}=\dfrac{\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\cdot\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\cdot\mathrm{Reg}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}{\#\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{2}}.

Here, the left hand side is the algebraic L-value of
E
, which will be denoted
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, and the right hand side is the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer quotient of
E
, which will be denoted
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
. By the combined works of Gross–Zagier
[
GZ86
, Theorem 7.3]
and Kolyvagin
[
Kol88
, Corollary 2]
, it is known that
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\neq 0
implies that
\mathrm{rk}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=0
and
\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
is finite. In this setting,
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
is clearly rational since
\mathrm{Reg}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
, and Proposition
3.3
shows that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
is also rational. If
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\!\mathinner{\left\{\infty\right\}}
denotes the
q
-adic valuation for some prime
q
, the conjecture that
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
is called the
q
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. For the base change
E/K
of
E
to an extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
, the analogous quantities
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}
and
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}
can be defined as in the paper by Dokchitser–Evans–Wiersema
[
DEW21
, Section 1.5]
.

Throughout, a
character
will always refer to a non-trivial even primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
of conductor
n
coprime to
N
and order
q>1
, which means that
\chi\!\mathinner{\left(-1\right)}=1
, and furthermore
q
divides the Euler totient of
n
. The L-function
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
of
E
twisted by
\chi
is defined to be the Euler product of
\mathrm{L}_{p}\!\mathinner{\bigl(\rho_{E,q}^{\vee}\otimes\overline{\chi},p^{-s}\bigr)}^{-1}
over all primes
p
, so that in particular
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}=\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
. The modularity theorem says that
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
is the Hecke L-function of a weight two cusp form of level
\Gamma_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
twisted by
\chi

[
Shi71
, Theorem 3.66]
, so its order of vanishing at
s=1
is again well-defined. The analogous algebraic twisted L-value is given by

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\coloneqq\lim_{s\to 1}\dfrac{\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}}{\!\mathinner{\left(s-1\right)}^{r}}\cdot\dfrac{n}{\tau\!\mathinner{\left(\chi\right)}\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}},

where
r
is the order of vanishing of
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
at
s=1
and
\tau\!\mathinner{\left(\chi\right)}
is the Gauss sum of
\chi
.

Remark 2.3
.

The definitions of L-values and Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer invariants in this section agree with those in Wiersema–Wuthrich
[
WW22
, Section 7]
and those in Dokchitser–Evans–Wiersema
[
DEW21
, Section 1.5]
, except for one notable difference for twisted L-functions. In this paper, the Dirichlet series of
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
is
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}\mathrm{a}_{n}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}n^{-s}
, and
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
is defined in terms of
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
. Wiersema–Wuthrich gives two definitions for twisted L-functions, namely an automorphic one that agrees with
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
, and a motivic one that coincides with
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\overline{\chi},s\right)}
instead of
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,s\right)}
. However, their algebraic twisted L-value is defined motivically, so it coincides with
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\overline{\chi}\right)}
instead of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
. Dokchitser–Evans–Wiersema follows the motivic convention, so their twisted L-functions and algebraic twisted L-values coincide with
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\overline{\chi},s\right)}
and
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\overline{\chi}\right)}
respectively.

Remark 2.4
.

Except for generalities on modular symbols in the next section, characters will have prime conductor and prime order, but many results can be generalised to prime power orders with a bit more work.

3
Modular symbols

This section recalls some classical facts on modular symbols. Most of the arguments here are well-known since the time of Manin
[
Man72
]
, with a few recent integrality results by Wiersema–Wuthrich
[
WW22
]
, but they are provided for reference. Nevertheless, the main tool is the congruence in Corollary
3.7
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\mathrm{S}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
denote the space of weight two cusp forms on the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}\coloneqq\!\mathinner{\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}\ \middle|\ N\mid c\right\}},

which acts on the extended upper half plane
\mathcal{H}
by fractional linear transformations. A smooth path between two points in the same
\Gamma_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
-orbit projects onto a closed path in the quotient
\mathrm{X}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=\mathcal{H}/\Gamma_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
, which defines an integral homology class
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{X}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)},\mathbb{Z}\right)}
. This is independent of the smooth path chosen because
\mathcal{H}
is simply connected, and any integral homology class
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{X}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)},\mathbb{Z}\right)}
arises in such a way. On the other hand, any cusp form
f\in\mathrm{S}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
induces a differential
2\pi if\!\mathinner{\left(z\right)}\mathrm{d}z
on
\mathrm{X}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
, and integrating this over the closed path
\gamma
gives a complex number
\int_{\gamma}2\pi if\!\mathinner{\left(z\right)}\mathrm{d}z
called a
modular symbol
. A general definition for paths with arbitrary endpoints is given by Manin
[
Man72
, Section 1.2]
, but for the purposes of this paper, it suffices to consider the modular symbol associated to the path from
0
to cusps
q\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\!\mathinner{\left\{\infty\right\}}
. When the denominator of
q\in\mathbb{Q}
is coprime to
N
, the image of any smooth path between
0
and
q
is always closed
[
Man72
, Proposition 2.2]
, so it makes sense to write the modular symbol

\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{q}2\pi if\!\mathinner{\left(z\right)}\mathrm{d}z\in\mathbb{C}.

The key example for
f
will be the normalised cuspidal eigenform
f_{E}\in\mathrm{S}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
associated to an elliptic curve
E
of conductor
N
. In this case, it turns out that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, as well as
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
for any character
\chi
of conductor coprime to
N
, can be written as sums of
\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}\coloneqq\mu_{f_{E}}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
for some
q\in\mathbb{Q}
. Furthermore, the terms in these sums can be paired up in a way that guarantees integrality, using the following trick.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{Q}
with denominator coprime to
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
f\in\mathrm{S}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
. Then

\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}+\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(1-q\right)}=2\Re\!\mathinner{\left(\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}\right)}.

In particular, if
E
is an elliptic curve, then
\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}+\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(1-q\right)}
is an integer multiple of
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
.

Proof.

This is similar to the proof in Wiersema–Wuthrich
[
WW22
, Lemma 4]
, but the argument is repeated here for reference. Note that
\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(1-q\right)}-\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(-q\right)}
is the integral of
2\pi if\!\mathinner{\left(z\right)}
along the smooth path between
-q
and
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\!\mathinner{\left(-q\right)}
, which is zero
[
Man72
, Proposition 1.4]
, so
\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(1-q\right)}=\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(-q\right)}
. The change of variables
z\mapsto-\overline{z}
then transforms
\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(-q\right)}
into
\overline{\mu_{f}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}}
, so the first statement follows. Now let
\gamma
be the image of any smooth path from
0
to
q
under the modular parameterisation
\phi_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathrm{X}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}\twoheadrightarrow E
associated to
E
. Then
\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}=\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}\int_{\gamma}\omega_{E}
by definition, so the second statement follows from the first statement and the fact that
\gamma
is necessarily an integer multiple of the generator of
\mathrm{H}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{C}\right)},\mathbb{Z}\right)}
that defines
\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

When the denominator of
q\in\mathbb{Q}
is coprime to
N
, these modular symbols
\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
coincide precisely with the modular symbols
\mu\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
defined in Wiersema–Wuthrich
[
WW22
, Section 2]
.

For this exact reason, the modular symbols
\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
can be normalised to be integers. More precisely, for an elliptic curve
E
of conductor
N
with normalised cuspidal eigenform
f_{E}\in\mathrm{S}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}
, define

\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}\coloneqq\dfrac{\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}{\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}\!\mathinner{\left(\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}+\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(1-q\right)}\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}.

The integrality of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
is now a formal consequence of the Hecke action on the space of modular symbols.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
coprime to
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{n}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}-\sigma_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}+\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}\right)}\sigma_{0}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}\right)}=\sum_{m\mid n}\sum_{a=1}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{m}\right)},

where
\sigma_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}
is the sum of divisors of
n
and
\sigma_{0}^{+}
is the number of even divisors of
n
. In particular, both sides lie in
\mathbb{Z}
, and if
p\nmid N
is an odd prime, then

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}=\sum_{a=1}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{p-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{p}\right)}.

Proof.

Integrality and the final statement are immediate consequences from Lemma
3.1
and the first statement. To prove the first statement, assume that
2\nmid N
. The Hecke action
[
Man72
, Theorem 4.2]
says

\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{n}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}-\sigma_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}\right)}=\sum_{m\mid n}\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{m}\right)},

so in particular
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}\right)}=-\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}
. By dividing by
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, the first statement reduces to

\sum_{m\mid n}\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{m}\right)}-\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}\sigma_{0}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}=\sum_{m\mid n}\sum_{a=1}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\!\mathinner{\left(\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{m}\right)}+\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(1-\tfrac{a}{m}\right)}\right)},

which is simply a rearrangement of sums. Now assume that
2\mid N
, so that
2\nmid n
by assumption. Then the divisors
m\mid n
are all odd, so
\sigma_{0}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}=0
and this follows again from the Hecke action.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The assumption that
N
is coprime to
n
is crucial, and removing this may cause integrality to fail, such as for the elliptic curve 11a1 where
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
and
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=\tfrac{1}{5}
, but
\mathrm{a}_{11}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
and
\sigma_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(11\right)}=12
.

The same argument can be adapted for the integrality of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
using Birch’s formula.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
, and let
\chi
be a character of order
q
and conductor
n
coprime to
N
. Then

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}=\sum_{a=1}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}}\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{n}\right)}.

In particular, both sides lie in
\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
. Furthermore, if
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
, then
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
.

Proof.

Integrality follows immediately from Lemma
3.1
and the first statement. To prove the first statement, a modification of Birch’s formula
[
WW22
, Lemma 6]
yields

\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi,1\right)}=\dfrac{\tau\!\mathinner{\left(\chi\right)}}{n}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}}\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{n}\right)},

noting that the automorphic and motivic definitions of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
agree by the assumption that
n
is coprime to
N

[
WW22
, Lemma 18]
. By dividing by
n^{-1}\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}\tau\!\mathinner{\left(\chi\right)}\Omega\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, the first statement reduces to

\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}}\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{n}\right)}=\sum_{a=1}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}}\!\mathinner{\left(\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{n}\right)}+\mu_{E}\!\mathinner{\left(1-\tfrac{a}{n}\right)}\right)}.

Now
\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}}=\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(n-a\right)}}
since
\chi
is even, and
\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)}}=0
when
2\mid n
, so this is again a rearrangement of sums. The final statement is an analogous argument with
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}

[
WW22
, Proposition 8]
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The assumption that
n
is coprime to
N
can be weakened slightly to
n\nmid N
for the first two statements
[
WW22
, Proposition 7]
, and to
n\nmid N
for the final statement
[
WW22
, Proposition 8]
. Removing either of these conditions may cause integrality to fail, such as for the elliptic curve 50b1 satisfying
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
and the unique quadratic character of conductor
5
, where
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}=\tfrac{1}{3}
.

Observe that the right hand sides of Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.5
are highly similar. More precisely, since
\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}}\equiv 1\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
except when
\gcd\!\mathinner{\left(a,n\right)}\neq 1
, the right hand sides are congruent modulo
\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
, up to a small error term consisting of extraneous modular symbols
\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{m}\right)}
for each proper divisor
m
of
n
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
, and let
\chi
be a character of order
q
and conductor
n
coprime to
N
. Then

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{n}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}-\sigma_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}+\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}\right)}\sigma_{0}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}\right)}-\epsilon_{E,n}\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle,

where

\epsilon_{E,n}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
\gcd\!\mathinner{\left(a,n\right)}\neq 1\end{subarray}}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{n}\right)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\sum_{a=1}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{m}\right)}.

In particular, if
p\nmid N
is an odd prime, then
\epsilon_{E,p}=0
and

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv-\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle,

and if furthermore
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, then

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv-\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle,

where the denominators of both sides are inverted modulo
\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
.

Remark 3.8
.

By stating the congruence at this generality, some interesting parity results for algebraic L-values twisted by quadratic characters can also be derived. For instance, if
\chi
is a quadratic character of conductor
n=p_{1}p_{2}
, where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are two distinct primes coprime to
N
, then

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{p_{1}}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{a}_{p_{2}}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}-n-p_{1}-p_{2}-1\right)}\mod 2.

When
2\nmid N
, something similar occurs for the unique quadratic character
\chi
of conductor
n=8
, where

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}+1\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}+2\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{2}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}-3\right)}\mod 2.

In both cases, it can be verified explicitly that
\epsilon_{E,n}\equiv 0\mod 2
, and the remaining terms are obtained from recurrences for Fourier coefficients of eigenforms. Neither of these will be explored further in the paper.

Remark 3.9
.

Without having
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, both integrality results and the congruence easily fail in trivial ways, but
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
is a relatively mild assumption, since
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\neq 1
seems to be relatively rare.

Remark 3.10
.

Algebraic twisted L-values
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
satisfy Deligne’s period conjecture
[
BD07
, Theorem 2.7]
, so in particular they are Galois equivariant, in the sense that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\sigma\circ\chi\right)}=\sigma\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\right)}
for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{q}\right)}/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)}
. With this property,
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
can be expressed in terms of the sum of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
for all characters
\chi
of a given conductor and order. For instance, when
\chi
is a cubic character of conductor
n
,

1+\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}+\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}}=\begin{cases}3&\text{if}\ a\in\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{\times 3}\\
1&\text{if}\ a\in\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\right)}\setminus\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{\times}\\
0&\text{if}\ a\in\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{\times}\setminus\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{\times 3}\end{cases},

so the identities in Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.5
combine to yield

\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}+\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\overline{\chi}\right)}+\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{a}_{n}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}-\sigma_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}+\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}\right)}\sigma_{0}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(n\right)}\right)}=3\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
a\in\!\mathinner{(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})}^{\times 3}\end{subarray}}^{\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{n}\right)}+\epsilon_{E,n}.

By Galois equivariance, the first two terms combine to
2\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\Re\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\right)}
, so this expresses
\Re\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\right)}
in terms of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
up to a few error terms consisting of modular symbols. By reducing modulo
3
, this recovers the congruence in Corollary
3.7
, but also shows that the congruence would not a priori hold modulo
9
, unless the extraneous modular symbols
\mu_{E}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{a}{n}\right)}
for each cubic residue
a
modulo
n
sum to a multiple of
3
.

4
Denominators of L-values

This section proves a few results on the
q
-adic valuations of denominators of algebraic L-values, where
q
is an odd prime, which may be of independent interest. Since
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
q
-adic valuation of
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\in\mathbb{Q}
can be bounded from below by the
q
-adic valuation of
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
, which is in turn controlled by
\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
in the denominator of
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
. When
q\neq 3
, under the
q
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, such a lower bound follows from Lorenzini’s result that
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\#\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\leq\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
with finitely many exceptions
[
Lor11
, Proposition 1.1]
, but the case
q=3
requires more work.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve without complex multiplication such that
E
has a point of order
3
but
3\nmid\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
. Then
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,3}}
is a Borel subgroup.

Proof.

By the assumption that
E
has a point of order
3
,
E
is isomorphic either to
y^{2}+cy=x^{3}
for some cube-free
c\in\mathbb{N}
, which has complex multiplication by
\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{3}\right]}
, or to

E_{1,\pm b/a}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}+xy\pm\dfrac{b}{a}y=x^{3},

for some coprime
a,b\in\mathbb{N}

[
BR22
, Proposition 2.4]
. If
3\nmid\mathrm{ord}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(a\right)}
for some prime
p
, then
3\mid\mathrm{Tam}_{p}\!\mathinner{\bigl(E_{1,\pm b/a}\bigr)}

[
BR22
, Theorem 3.5]
. This contradicts the assumption that
3\nmid\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, so
a=d^{3}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
coprime to
b
. The change of variables
\!\mathinner{\left(x,y\right)}\mapsto\!\mathinner{\left(x/d^{2},y/d^{3}\right)}
yields an isomorphism from
E_{1,\pm b/a}
to

E_{d,\pm b}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}+dxy\pm by=x^{3},

which has discriminant
\Delta=\pm b^{3}\!\mathinner{\left(d^{3}-27b\right)}
. Now let
p\mid b
, so that
p\mid\Delta
and
\mathrm{ord}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(d^{3}-27b\right)}=0
by coprimality. By step
2
of Tate’s algorithm, since
T^{2}+dT
splits in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
E_{d,\pm b}
has Kodaira symbol
\textbf{I}_{\mathrm{ord}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(\Delta\right)}}
and has split mutiplicative reduction at
p
, so
\mathrm{Tam}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(E_{d,\pm b}\right)}=\mathrm{ord}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(\Delta\right)}=3\mathrm{ord}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(b\right)}
. This forces
b=1
by the assumption that
3\nmid\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, so the j-invariant of
E_{d,\pm b}=E_{d,\pm 1}
is given by

\dfrac{d^{3}\!\mathinner{\left(d^{3}\mp 24\right)}^{3}}{\pm d^{3}-27}=27\dfrac{\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{27}{\pm d^{3}-27}+1\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{27}{\pm d^{3}-27}+9\right)}^{3}}{\!\mathinner{\left(\tfrac{27}{\pm d^{3}-27}\right)}^{3}},

which implies that
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,3}}
is the Borel subgroup 3B.1.1
[
Zyw15
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

Assuming the
3
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, a divisibility result for
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
can be derived from the integrality of
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
, via a case-by-case analysis on
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,3}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
such that
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\neq 0
and
\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
. Assume that
\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\leq\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
. Then
3\mid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
.

Proof.

Assume that
3\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
. By Proposition
3.3
and the assumptions, for an odd prime
p
,

\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}{9}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\right)}\geq\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\right)}\geq 0,

so it suffices to find an odd prime
p\nmid N
such that
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 3\mod 9
. By Chebotarev’s density theorem, this reduces to finding a matrix
\mathrm{M}_{E,3}\in\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,9}}
such that
1+\det\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{M}_{E,3}\right)}-\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{M}_{E,3}\right)}=3
. By inspecting Table
A
, such matrices exist for all
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,3}
except for the two
3
-adic Galois images 9.72.0.1 and 9.72.0.5, so these have to be handled separately. If
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,3}
is 9.72.0.1, then
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,3}}
is 3Cs.1.1 and not 3B.1.1, so Lemma
4.1
implies that
3\mid\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
. Otherwise
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,3}
is 9.72.0.5, then
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,9}}
fixes a subspace of the group of
9
-torsion points of
E
, so
E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}
, which contradicts the assumption that
E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The conclusion of Proposition
4.2
was already observed by Melistas
[
Mel23
, Example 3.8]
, where the elliptic curves 27a3, 27a4, and 54a3 all have
E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
but
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=3
. By the work of Lorenzini, it is generally expected that the factors in
\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
would cancel
\#\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, but in this case it is necessary to consider
\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
as well, such as in the elliptic curve 1638j3 where
E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
but
\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=9
. Note also that the statement is false for
\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
but
\mathrm{rk}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}>0
, such as for the elliptic curve 91b1 where
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
.

A lower bound on the
q
-adic valuation of
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
then follows under the
q
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, but when
E
has no rational
q
-isogeny the bound can be made unconditional.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
such that
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\neq 0
, and let
q
be an odd prime.

1.

If
E
has no rational
q
-isogeny, then
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq 0
.

2.

Assume that
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
. Then
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq-1
.

Proof.

For the first statement, by Proposition
3.3
, it suffices to find an odd prime
p\nmid N
such that
q\nmid\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
, so by Chebotarev’s density theorem this reduces to finding a matrix
M\in\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
such that
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}\neq 1+\det\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}
. Suppose otherwise that
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}=1+\det\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}
for all
M\in\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
, so in particular
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}=2
for all
M\in\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}\cap\mathrm{SL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
. In this case, by inspecting the order in each conjugacy class of
\mathrm{SL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
,
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}\cap\mathrm{SL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
is necessarily a
q
-group, so in particular
q\mid\#\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
. Then either
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
is contained in a Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
or
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
contains
\mathrm{SL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}

[
Ser72
, Proposition 15]
. The former contradicts the assumption that
E
has no rational
q
-isogeny, and the latter is impossible by comparing orders.

For the second statement, the assumption on the
q
-adic valuations reduces the statement to proving that
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq-1
. By Mazur’s torsion theorem, it suffices to consider
\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
being one of

\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z},

since
q
is odd. If
E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, then a case-by-case analysis of
q
yields
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\#\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
except for the elliptic curve 11a3 with
q=5
, and the elliptic curves 14a4 and 14a6 with
q=3

[
Lor11
, Proposition 1.1]
, but these exceptions all have
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=1
and
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=-2
. If
E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, then Proposition
4.2
implies that
\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{Tam}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#\Sha\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}-2\geq-1
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The assumption on the
q
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in the second statement can be slightly weakened, by only requiring that
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
for all
E
, except for when
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,3}
is 9.72.0.1, where the assumption
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\leq\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
is also needed to proceed with the argument in Proposition
4.2
. The second statement might also be provable without appealing to the
q
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture when
q>3
, by finding a matrix
M\in\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,q}
such that
1+\det\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}-\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}\equiv q\mod q^{2}
along the same lines as the proof of Proposition
4.2
. In general, this would need a case-by-case analysis of
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,q}
for when
E
has no rational
q
-isogeny, which seems to be unsolved.

Remark 4.6
.

A lower bound for the
2
-adic valuation of
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
should also be achievable by arguing along the same lines, using the
n=2
version of Proposition
3.3
and the classification of
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,2}
if necessary. This will not be needed for the rest of the paper and will not be discussed further.

The following is an easy result on the
q
-adic valuation of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
. The factors arising from the denominator of the rational number
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
could a priori cancel the factors appearing in
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, but the congruence of L-values says that this should not happen under Stevens’s conjecture that
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
, and let
p\nmid N
and
q
be odd primes such that
p\equiv 1\mod q
. Assume that
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
. Then

q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\qquad\iff\qquad q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\bigl(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\bigr)}=0.

Proof.

Assume first that
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
but
q\mid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
. By the assumption that
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
, Proposition
3.5
says that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
for any character
\chi
of conductor
p
and order
q
, so
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv 0\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
, which contradicts
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
by Corollary
3.7
. Thus
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, so that
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\bigl(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\bigr)}=0
also follows, while the converse is immediate noting that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\neq 0
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Assuming Stevens’s conjecture, Proposition
4.7
yields an immediate proof that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
is integral at
q
if
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\leq 1
. This condition seems to hold for all elliptic curves in the LMFDB
[
Col
]
, but a proof remains elusive. On the other hand, assuming the
q
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, there might be a direct proof that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
is integral at
q
, by arguing that
1+\det\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}-\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}
cancels
\#\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{2}
for every matrix
M
lying in every possible
\mathrm{im}\rho_{E,q}
. Again, this will be omitted here.

5
Units of twisted L-values

Under standard arithmetic conjectures, Dokchitser–Evans–Wiersema computed the norm of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
in terms of
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}
, where
K
is the degree
q
subfield of
\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{p}\right)}
cut out by the kernel of
\chi

[
DEW21
, Theorem 38]
. Some of their results can be summarised in the notation of this paper as follows.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
, and let
\chi
be a character of odd prime conductor
p\nmid N
and odd prime order
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
. Assume that
\mathrm{c}_{1}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=1
, and that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}=\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}
. Furthermore, set
\zeta\coloneqq\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}^{\!\mathinner{\left(q-1\right)}/2}
.

1.

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
generates an ideal invariant under complex conjugation, and has norm

\mathrm{Nm}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\right)}=\dfrac{\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}}{\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}.

2.

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\cdot\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}^{+}
, and has norm

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathrm{Nm}_{q}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\cdot\zeta\right)}\right\rvert}=\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}}}.

In particular, if
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}
, then there is a unit
u\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}=u\cdot\zeta^{-1}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.7
, under the arithmetic conjectures, the assumption that
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
reduces to
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\bigl(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\bigr)}=0
. In particular

6
Residual densities of twisted L-values

For a fixed elliptic curve
E
of conductor
N
, a natural problem is to determine the asymptotic distribution of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
as
\chi
varies over characters of some fixed prime order
q
but arbitrarily high odd prime conductor
p\nmid N
. However, for each such
p
, there are
q-1
characters
\chi
of conductor
p
and order
q
, giving rise to
q-1
conjugates of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
, so a uniform choice of
\chi
for each
p
has to be made for any meaningful analysis. One solution is to observe that the residue class of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
modulo
\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
is independent of the choice of
\chi
, so a simpler problem would be to determine the asymptotic distribution of these residue classes instead. As in the introduction, let
X_{E,q}^{<n}
be the set of equivalence classes of characters of odd order
q
and odd prime conductor
p\nmid N
less than
n
, where two characters in
X_{E,q}^{<n}
are equivalent if they have the same conductor. Define the residual densities
\delta_{E,q}
of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
to be the natural densities of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
modulo
\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
, namely

\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\!\mathinner{\left\{\chi\in X_{E,q}^{<n}\ \middle|\ \mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\lambda\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle\right\}}}{\#X_{E,q}^{<n}},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

if such a limit exists. When
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, this can be computed for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
directly using Corollary
3.7
, with the only subtlety being the possible cancellations between
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}
. In the generic scenario when
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
is maximal, there is a clean description in terms of Legendre symbols.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve such that
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\neq 0
, and let
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
be an odd prime.

1.

If
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}>0
, then
\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(0\right)}=1
and
\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}=0
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

2.

If
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\leq 0
, then set
m\coloneqq 1-\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
and

\mathrm{G}_{E,q^{m}}\coloneqq\!\mathinner{\left\{M\in\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q^{m}}}\ \middle|\ \det\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}\equiv 1\mod q\right\}}.

Then for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}=\dfrac{\#\!\mathinner{\left\{M\in\mathrm{G}_{E,q^{m}}\ \middle|\ 1+\det\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}-\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}\equiv-\lambda\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}\mod q^{m}\right\}}}{\#\mathrm{G}_{E,q^{m}}}.

In particular, if
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\leq 0
and
\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
is surjective, then set

\lambda_{E,q}\coloneqq\!\mathinner{\left(\dfrac{\lambda\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}}{q}\right)}\!\mathinner{\left(\dfrac{\lambda\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}+4}{q}\right)}.

Then for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{q-1}&\text{if}\ \lambda_{E,q}=1\\
\dfrac{q}{q^{2}-1}&\text{if}\ \lambda_{E,q}=0\\
\dfrac{1}{q+1}&\text{if}\ \lambda_{E,q}=-1\end{cases}.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.7
,
\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}
is just the natural density of
-\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv\lambda\mod q
. If
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}>0
, then only
\lambda=0
gives a non-zero natural density, otherwise this is equivalent to
1+p-\mathrm{a}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\equiv-\lambda\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}\mod q^{m}
, noting that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}
is well-defined and non-zero modulo
q^{m}
by definition. By Chebotarev’s density theorem, this occurs with the proportion of matrices
M\in\mathrm{G}_{E,q}
with
\det\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}=p
and
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}=\mathrm{a}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, so the second statement follows. If
\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
is surjective, then Theorem
4.4
.1 yields
m=1
, so
\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}
is the proportion of matrices
M\in\mathrm{SL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
such that
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}\equiv 2-\lambda\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}^{-1}\mod q
. The final statement then follows by
\#\mathrm{SL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}=\!\mathinner{\left(q-1\right)}q\!\mathinner{\left(q+1\right)}
and by inspecting the trace in each conjugacy class of
\mathrm{SL}\!\mathinner{\left(q\right)}
, noting that
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}=x+x^{-1}
for some
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\!\mathinner{\left\{\pm 1\right\}}
precisely when
x^{2}-4
is a quadratic residue modulo
q
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Without the assumption
q\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, the same argument can be used to compute the residual density of
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
instead, by adding a factor of
\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
to every instance of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
in the statement and proof of Proposition
6.1
. On the other hand, Proposition
3.5
predicts that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
under Stevens’s conjecture, so both sides of the congruence are divisible by
q
and the statement becomes vacuous.

Remark 6.3
.

Under standard arithmetic conjectures, Proposition
5.1
says that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\cdot\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
, so
\mathrm{Nm}_{q}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\cdot\zeta\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since the norm is multiplicative and
\zeta\equiv 1\mod\!\left\langle 1-\zeta_{q}\right\rangle
, the asymptotic distribution of the residue class of
\mathrm{Nm}_{q}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\cdot\zeta\right)}
modulo
q
essentially boils down to computing
\delta_{E,q}
.

Assuming the
q
-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, Theorem
4.4
.3 says
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}\geq-1
, so non-trivial
\delta_{E,q}
are only visible when
\mathrm{ord}_{q}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=0,-1
. Once this is determined, computing
\delta_{E,q}
then reduces to identifying
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q}}
or
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,q^{2}}}
, and then weighing the proportion of matrices with a certain determinant and trace. To illustrate this in action, the next result describes the possible ordered triples
\!\mathinner{\left(\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(0\right)},\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(1\right)},\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(2\right)}\right)}
of residual densities, which is only made possible thanks to the classification of
3
-adic Galois images by Rouse–Sutherland–Zureick-Brown
[
RSZB22
, Corollary 1.3.1 and Corollary 12.3.3]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve such that
3\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\neq 0
. Assume that
\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}
. Then precisely one of the following holds.

1.

If
\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}>0
, then
\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(0\right)}=1
and
\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(1\right)}=\delta_{E,3}\!\mathinner{\left(2\right)}=0
.

2.

If
\mathrm{ord}_{3}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\right)}=0
and
3\mid\#\mathrm{tor}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
, then

7
Twisted L-values of Kisilevsky–Nam

The computation of residual densities was originally motivated by the statistical data in Kisilevsky–Nam
[
KN22
, Section 7]
. They numerically computed millions of algebraic twisted L-values by fixing the elliptic curve and varying the character, but considered an alternative normalisation given by

\mathcal{L}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}&\text{if}\ \chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=1\\
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\cdot\!\mathinner{\left(1+\overline{\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}}\right)}&\text{if}\ \chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}\neq 1\end{cases},

in contrast to the normalisation factor
\zeta
in Proposition
5.1
. Under the implicit assumption that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}
, they showed that
\mathcal{L}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}\!\mathinner{\left[\zeta_{q}\right]}^{+}

[
KN22
, Proposition 2.1]
, so that
\mathrm{Nm}_{q}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\right)}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Fixing six elliptic curves
E
and five small orders
q
, they varied the character
\chi
over millions of conductors
p
, empirically determined the greatest common divisor
\gcd_{E,q}
of all the integers
\mathrm{Nm}_{q}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\right)}
, and considered

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\coloneqq\dfrac{\mathrm{Nm}_{q}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{L}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\right)}}{\gcd_{E,q}}.

Remark 7.1
.

The definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
is equivalent to that of
A_{\chi}
defined by Kisilevsky–Nam when
q
is odd and
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\neq 0
, since
\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=-1
never occurs and the global root number is always
1

[
KN22
, Section 2.2]
. Their definition of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
has an extra factor of
2
, but this is cancelled out after division by
\gcd_{E,q}
.

Remark 7.2
.

In the interpretation of Proposition
5.1
, the integer
\gcd_{E,q}
is predicted to arise from contributions by the greatest common divisors of
\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{(E/K)}/\mathrm{BSD}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
ranging over various number fields

of degree

over
\mathbb{Q}
coming from characters of order
q
, but this will not be discussed here.

As their normalisation differs from that in Proposition

[

, Remark 1]
, the resulting residual densities are skewed. More precisely, define
X_{E,q}^{<n}
as before, and define the analogous residual densities

of

to be the natural densities of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}
modulo

, or in other words

\delta_{E,q}\!\mathinner{\left(\lambda\right)}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\!\mathinner{\left\{\chi\in X_{E,q}^{<n}\ \middle|\ \widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\lambda\mod q\right\}}}{\#X_{E,q}^{<n}},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

if such a limit exists. In the simplest case where

and

, there is no norm, and so

which becomes amenable to a similar computation to that of Proposition

provided

depends completely on

due to a shared action of Frobenius in

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
with no rational
3
-isogeny such that the splitting field
F
of
X^{3}-N
lies in the splitting field
K
of the
3
-division polynomial
\psi_{E,3}
, and let
\chi
be a cubic character of odd prime conductor
p\nmid N
. Then
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,3}}=\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}
and
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathrm{PGL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}
. Furthermore, if
p
does not split completely in
K
, then
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 2\mod 3
if and only if
\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=1
. Otherwise, if
p
splits completely in
K
, then
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\not\equiv 2\mod 3
and
\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=1
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the extension of
K
obtained by adjoining the
Y
-coordinates of
E\!\mathinner{\left[3\right]}
. By the assumption that
E
has no rational
3
-isogeny and the classification of
\mathrm{im}\overline{\rho_{E,3}}
, if
\overline{\rho_{E,3}}
were not surjective, then
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(L/\mathbb{Q}\right)}
is either 3Nn or 3Ns. Neither of this could occur, since by the assumption that
F\subseteq K
, there are inclusions

\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\subseteq F\subseteq K\subseteq L,

so in particular
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(L/\mathbb{Q}\right)}
surjects onto
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(F/\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathcal{S}_{3}
, which forces
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(L/\mathbb{Q}\right)}\cong\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}
. On the other hand,
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\right)}
permutes the roots of the degree
4
polynomial
\psi_{E,3}
, so it must be the quotient group
\mathrm{PGL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}\cong\mathcal{S}_{4}
. Its subgroup
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\right)}\cong\mathcal{A}_{4}
surjects onto
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(F/\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\right)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, with kernel the unique subgroup
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/F\right)}\cong\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)}^{2}
of index
4
consisting precisely of all elements of
\mathcal{A}_{4}
of order
1
or
2
.

Now
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}\in\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\right)}
acts on the residue field of a prime
\pi
of
F
above
p
by

\mathrm{Fr}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\equiv\zeta_{3}^{p}\mod\pi,\qquad\mathrm{Fr}_{p}\!\mathinner{\bigl(\sqrt[3]{N}\bigr)}\equiv\sqrt[3]{N}^{p}\mod\pi.

Clearly
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}
fixes
\zeta_{3}
, so that
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}\in\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\right)}
. Claim that, when
p
does not split completely in
K
, the condition
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}\in\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/F\right)}
is equivalent to
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 2\mod 3
and to
\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=1
. On one hand, this means that
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}
fixes
\sqrt[3]{N}
, or equivalently that
\sqrt[3]{N}^{p-1}\equiv 1\mod p
, which is precisely the condition that
\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=1
. On the other hand, this also means that
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}^{2}=1
in
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\right)}
, which is equivalent to
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}
having order exactly
2
in
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\right)}
. By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, these are precisely the trace
0
matrices in
\mathrm{PGL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}
, or equivalently the trace
0
matrices in
\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}
, which proves the equivalence with
\mathrm{a}_{p}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}=0
. If
p
splits completely in
K
, then
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}=1
in
\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{\left(K/\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{\left(\zeta_{3}\right)}\right)}
, but these never have trace
0
in
\mathrm{PGL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}
or
\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{\left(3\right)}
.
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
with no rational
3
-isogeny such that
3\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
3\nmid\gcd_{E,3}
, and the splitting field
F
of
X^{3}-N
lies in the splitting field
K
of the
3
-division polynomial
\psi_{E,3}
, and let

be a cubic character of odd prime conductor

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\begin{cases}0\mod 3&\text{if}\ \#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 0\mod 3\\
2\mod 3&\text{if}\ \#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 1\mod 3\ \text{and}\ p\ \text{splits completely in}\ K\\
1\mod 3&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

In particular,

\delta_{E,3}^{\prime}\!\mathinner{\left(0\right)}=\dfrac{9}{24},\qquad\delta_{E,3}^{\prime}\!\mathinner{\left(1\right)}=\dfrac{15}{24},\qquad\delta_{E,3}^{\prime}\!\mathinner{\left(2\right)}=\dfrac{1}{24}.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.7
and the assumptions that

and
3\nmid\gcd_{E,3}
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv\begin{cases}2\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\gcd_{E,3}&\text{if}\ \chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=1\\
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}\gcd_{E,3}&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{cases}.

Clearly
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}\!\mathinner{\left(E,\chi\right)}\equiv 0\mod 3
when
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 0\mod 3
. By Lemma
7.3
,
\chi\!\mathinner{\left(N\right)}=1
occurs either when
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 1\mod 3
but
p
splits completely in
K
or when
\#E\!\mathinner{\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)}\equiv 2\mod 3
but

does not split completely in

, the only remaining case being when

and

. The first statement then follows by substituting the residues of

modulo

, and noting that

cancels out the factors in

by definition. For the final statement, the description of the groups in Lemma

implies that

with
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{\left(M\right)}=2-\lambda

in

∎

Remark A.1
.

Note that many of the
3
-adic Galois images seemingly do not represent any elliptic curves with
b\neq 0
, in the sense that a search through the LMFDB
[
Col
]
yields no examples satisfying
3\nmid\mathrm{c}_{0}\!\mathinner{\left(E\right)}
and
\mathrm{L}\!\mathinner{\left(E,1\right)}\neq 0
, but current results a priori do not rule out their existence. To rule out examples for a specific
3
-adic Galois image, one could consider the explicit family of Weierstrass equations parameterised by the associated modular curve, and then investigate the divisibility of Tamagawa numbers as in Lemma
4.1
. Such is the case for the last six
3
-adic Galois images arising from elliptic curves with complex multiplication, where their associated modular curves have effectively computable finite sets of rational points.

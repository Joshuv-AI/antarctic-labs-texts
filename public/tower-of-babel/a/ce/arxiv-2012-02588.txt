Explicit Relations between Multiple Zeta Values and Related Variants
Ce Xu

Abstract




Abstract
In this paper we present some new identities for multiple polylogarithms (abbr. MPLs) and multiple harmonic star sums (abbr. MHSSs) by using the methods
of iterated integral computations of logarithm functions. Then, by applying these formulas obtained, we establish some explicit relations between Kaneko-Yamamoto type multiple zeta values (abbr. K-Y MZVs), multiple zeta values (abbr. MZVs) and MPLs. Further, we find some explicit relations between MZVs and multiple zeta star values (abbr. MZSVs). Furthermore, we define an Apéry-type variant of MZSVs
\zeta^{\star}_{B}({\bf k})
(called multiple zeta
B
-star values, abbr. MZBSVs) which involve MHSSs and central binomial coefficients, and establish some explicit connections among MZVs, alternating MZVs and MZBSVs by using the method of iterated integrals. Finally, some interesting consequences and illustrative examples are presented.

Keywords
: Multiple harmonic (star) sums, (alternating) multiple zeta (star) values, multiple polylogarithms, Kaneko-Yamamoto type multiple zeta values, iterated integration.

In this section, we establish several iterated integral identities of multiple polylogarithms and parametric multiple harmonic star sums.
We begin with some basic notations.
For any index

\displaystyle{\bf m}_{j}:=(m_{1},m_{2}+1,\ldots,m_{j}+1),\ {\bf m}_{1}:=(m_{1}),

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{{\bf m}}_{i,j}:=(m_{i},m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{j}),

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{{\bf m}}_{i,j}:=(m_{j},m_{j-1},\ldots,m_{i}),

and

\displaystyle|{\bf m}|_{j}:=m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{j},

where

, then we let
\overleftarrow{{\bf m}}_{i,j}:=\emptyset
. In definition of
{\bf m}_{j}

Moreover, for two index

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{({\bf m+n})}_{i,j}:=(m_{i}+n_{i},\ldots,m_{j-1}+n_{j-1},m_{j}+n_{j}),

2.1
Identities for Multiple Polylogarithms

According to the definition of multiple polylogarithms, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}{\mathrm{Li}}_{{{k_{1}},{k_{2}},\cdots,{k_{r}}}}(x)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}{\mathrm{Li}}_{{{k_{1}-1},{k_{2}},\cdots,{k_{r}}}}(x){,\ \ k_{1}>1,}\\
{\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-x}{\mathrm{Li}}_{{{k_{2}},\cdots,{k_{r}}}}(x),\;\;\;k_{1}=1.}\\
\end{array}}\right.

Hence, applying (
2.1
) we obtain the following iterated integral expression

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Li}}_{{{k_{1}},{k_{2}},\cdots,{k_{r}}}}\left(x\right)=\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{x}\underbrace{\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\cdots\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}}_{k_{1}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\underbrace{\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\cdots\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}}_{k_{2}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\underbrace{\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\cdots\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}}_{k_{r}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t},

(2.13)

where

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{x}f_{1}(t)dtf_{2}(t)dt\cdots f_{r}(t)dt:=\displaystyle\int\limits_{0<t_{r}<\cdots<t_{1}<x}f_{1}(t_{1})f_{2}(t_{2})\cdots f_{r}(t_{r})dt_{1}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{r}.

Note the fact that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{x_{1}<t_{r}<\cdots<t_{1}<x_{2}}\displaystyle\frac{dt_{1}\cdots dt_{r}}{t_{1}\cdots t_{r}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{r!}\log^{r}\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right).

Thus, (

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Li}}_{{{k_{1}},{k_{2}},\cdots,{k_{r}}}}\left(x\right)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(k_{j})}\displaystyle\int\limits_{0<t_{r}<\cdots<t_{1}<t_{0}=x}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{\log^{k_{j}-1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{j-1}}{t_{j}}\right)}{1-t_{j}}dt_{j}\right\}.

(2.14)

For convenience, we use the following notations

\displaystyle\Omega_{p}^{k}(x):=\displaystyle\frac{\log^{k-1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{x}{1-t_{p}}\right)}{t_{p}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\Omega_{p,p-1}^{k}:=\displaystyle\frac{\log^{k-1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1-t_{p}}{1-t_{p-1}}\right)}{t_{p-1}},

where

\Omega_{p}(x)\equiv\Omega^{1}_{p}(x)
and
\Omega_{p,p-1}\equiv\Omega_{p,p-1}^{1}

Therefore, in (

gives

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Li}}_{{{k_{1}},{k_{2}},\cdots,{k_{r}}}}\left(x\right)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(k_{j})}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{r}(1-x)}\Omega_{r}^{k_{1}}(x)\Omega_{r,r-1}^{k_{2}}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{k_{r}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{r}.

(2.15)

Theorem 2.1

For

, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{k,\{1\}_{r-1}}(x)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\displaystyle\frac{\log^{k-1-j}(x)}{(k-1-j)!}\left\{\zeta(r+1,\{1\}_{j-1})-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{r-1}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}\log^{i}(1-x){\rm Li}_{r+1-i,\{1\}_{j-1}}(1-x)\right\}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{r}\displaystyle\frac{\log^{k-1}(x)\log^{r}(1-x)}{(k-1)!r!}.

Proof.

in (

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{D_{x,{\bf t}}}\Omega_{r}^{k}(x)\Omega_{r,r-1}^{1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{1}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{r}

(2.25)

2.2
Identities for Parametric Multiple Harmonic Star Sums

For positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{p}
and real
x\in[-1,1]
, we define a
parametric multiple harmonic star sum
(PMHSS for short)
\zeta^{\star}_{n}(m_{1},\cdots,m_{p-1},m_{p};x)
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1},\cdots,m_{p-1},m_{p};x):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{p}\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n_{p}}}{n^{m_{1}}_{1}\cdots n^{m_{p-1}}_{p-1}n^{m_{p}}_{p}},

where
\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\emptyset;x):=x^{n}
.

It is clear that according to definition, by a direct calculation, we have the relations

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1},\cdots,m_{p-1},m_{p};x)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}\ \ \ \quad\quad\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1},\cdots,m_{p-1},m_{p};x){,\ \ \ \quad\quad\quad m_{p}>1,}\\
{\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1},\cdots,m_{p-1})-\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1},\cdots,m_{p-1};x)}{1-x},\;\;\;m_{p}=1.}\\
\end{array}}\right.

Definition 2.1

For an index
{\bf m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})
, its Hoffman dual is
the index
{\bf m}^{\vee}=(m^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}})
determined by

|{\bf m}|:=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{p}=m^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+m^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}}
and

\{1,2,\ldots,|{\bf m}|-1\}=\{m_{1},m_{1}+m_{2},\ldots,m_{1}+\cdots+m_{p-1}\}\amalg\{m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}+m^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+m^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}-1}\}.

For example, we have

\displaystyle({1,1,2,1})^{\vee}=(3,2)\quad\text{and}\quad({1,2,1,1})^{\vee}=(2,3).

Theorem 2.7

For positive integer
p
and real
x\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}({\bf m}_{p}^{v};x)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}n}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(x)}\Omega_{p}^{m_{p}+1}(1-x)\Omega_{p,p-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-j}\zeta_{n}^{\star}({\bf m}_{j}^{v}){\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p}}(1-x),

(2.31)

where
{\bf m}_{p}=(m_{1},m_{2}+1,\ldots,m_{p}+1)
and
m_{1}\geq 1,\ m_{j}\geq 0\ (j=2,3,\ldots,p)
.

Proof.
The proof is by induction on
p
. Note the fact that the case
p=1
is well known (see
[
24
]
). For
p=2
, if
m_{2}=0
, by (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},1)^{v};x)=\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1}^{v};x)}{x}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{n}{m_{1}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{2}(x)}\Omega_{2}(1-x)\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}dt_{2}-\zeta(m_{1}^{v}){\rm Li}_{1}(1-x)+\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},1)^{v})\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1}^{v};x)}{x}.

Hence, we know that

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},1)^{v};x)=\displaystyle\frac{n}{m_{1}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{2}(x)}\Omega_{2}(1-x)\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}dt_{2}-\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1}^{v}){\rm Li}_{1}(1-x)+\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},1)^{v})+C_{1},

where
C_{1}
is a constant. If letting
x\rightarrow 1
, then we get
C_{1}=0
.
Thus, the formula (
2.7
) holds for
p=2
and
m_{2}=0
. Similarly, if
m_{2}\geq 1
, we assume (
2.7
) holds for
(m_{1},m_{2})
, then from (
2.2
) we deduce

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2}+1)^{v};x)=\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2})^{v})-\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2})^{v};x)}{1-x}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{n}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{2}(x)}\Omega_{2}^{m_{2}+1}(1-x)\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}dt_{2}-\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1}^{v}){\rm Li}_{m_{2}+1}(1-x)+\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2}+1)^{v})\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2})^{v})-\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2})^{v};x)}{1-x}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2}+1)^{v};x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{n}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{2}(x)}\Omega_{2}^{m_{2}+1}(1-x)\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}dt_{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\zeta_{n}^{\star}(m_{1}^{v}){\rm Li}_{m_{2}+1}(1-x)+\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2}+1)^{v})+C_{2},

where
C_{2}
is a constant. Further, setting
x\rightarrow 1
yields
C_{2}=0
. So, for
p=2
, the formula (
2.7
) holds. Now, we assume the formula (
2.7
) holds for index
(m_{1},m_{2}+1,\ldots,m_{p-1}+1)^{v}
. If
m_{p}=0
, by direct calculations, we find that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(({\bf m}_{p-1},1)^{v};x)=\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}((m_{1},m_{2}+1,\ldots,m_{p-1}+1)^{v};x)}{x}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}n}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p-1}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(x)}\Omega_{p}(1-x)\Omega_{p,p-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}\\
+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-j}\zeta_{n}^{\star}({\bf m}_{j}^{v}){\rm Li}_{1,\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p-1}}(1-x)+\zeta_{n}^{\star}(({\bf m}_{p-1},1)^{v})\end{array}\right\}.

Then by the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}(({\bf m}_{p-1},1)^{v};x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}n}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p-1}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(x)}\Omega_{p}(1-x)\Omega_{p,p-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-j}\zeta_{n}^{\star}({\bf m}_{j}^{v}){\rm Li}_{1,\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p-1}}(1-x)+\zeta_{n}^{\star}(({\bf m}_{p-1},1)^{v}).

Further, if
m_{p}\geq 1
, we assume the formula (
2.7
) holds for index
(m_{1},m_{2}+1,\ldots,m_{p-1}+1,m_{p})^{v}
. By (
2.2
), it is easy to obtain that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\zeta_{n}^{\star}({\bf m}_{p}^{v};x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}n}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(x)}\Omega_{p}^{m_{p}+1}(1-x)\Omega_{p,p-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}\\
\quad+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-j}\zeta_{n}^{\star}({\bf m}_{j}^{v}){\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p}}(1-x)\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(({\bf m}_{p-1},m_{p})^{v})-\zeta_{n}^{\star}(({\bf m}_{p-1},m_{p})^{v};x)}{1-x}.

Hence, the desired evaluation is obtained.
\square

3.2
Two Special Cases

In this subsection, we will prove the conclusion that the K-Y MZVs

\zeta(\{1\}_{r}\circledast(1,\{\{1\}_{m-1},2\}_{p-1},\{1\}_{m})^{\star})

and

\zeta((\{1\}_{m},2,\{1\}_{b})\circledast(1,\{\{1\}_{m-1},2\}_{p-1},\{1\}_{m})^{\star})

can be expressed in terms of the products of Riemann zeta values.

For any index
{\bf m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{p})
with
m_{1}>1
, we define the following
multiple Hurwitz zeta function

\displaystyle\zeta_{HZ}(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{p};a+1):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{p}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n_{1}+a)^{m_{1}}(n_{2}+a)^{m_{2}}\cdots(n_{p}+a)^{m_{p}}},

where
a\neq-1,-2,-3,\ldots.
For convenience, we set

\zeta_{HZ}(\emptyset;a+1):=1.

It is clear that if
a=0
, then

\displaystyle\zeta_{HZ}(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{p};1)=\zeta(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{p}).

Theorem 3.4

For
k,m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\ (j=1,2,\ldots,p-1)
and
m_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{p}=k,\atop i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{p}\geq 0}\left\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\binom{m_{j}+i_{j}}{i_{j}}\right\}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+i+1})}_{1,p}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\lim\limits_{a\rightarrow 0}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\zeta_{HZ}\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p};a+1\right)\right\},

(3.41)

Proof.
We note that the right hand side of formula (
3.4
) (not limit) is equal to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\zeta_{HZ}\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p};a+1\right)\right\}

(3.42)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\zeta_{HZ}(m_{p}+1,\ldots,m_{2}+1,m_{1}+1;a+1)\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{p}>0}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n_{1}+a)^{m_{p}+1}\cdots(n_{p-1}+a)^{m_{2}+1}(n_{p}+a)^{m_{1}+1}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{p}>0}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{p}=k\atop i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{p}\geq 0}\displaystyle\frac{k!}{i_{1}!i_{2}!\cdots i_{p}!}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{i_{j}}}{\partial a^{i_{j}}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n_{p+1-j}+a)^{m_{j}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{p}=k,\atop i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{p}\geq 0}\left\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\binom{m_{j}+i_{j}}{k_{j}}\right\}\zeta_{HZ}\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+k+1})}_{1,p};a+1\right),

(3.43)

where we used the general Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}f_{j}\right)^{(k)}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{p}=k,\atop k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{p}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\displaystyle\frac{k!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{p}!}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}(f_{j})^{(k_{j})}.

Hence, letting
a\rightarrow 0
in (
3.42
) yields the desired result.
\square

Theorem 3.5

For
k,m,p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\}_{p};a+1)\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{c_{1}+2c_{2}+\cdots+pc_{p}=p,\atop c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{p}\geq 0}\left\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{(j-1)c_{j}}}{c_{j}!j^{c_{j}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{{|\bf c|}_{p}}=k,\atop k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{{|\bf c|}_{p}}\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\prod\limits_{j_{i}={|\bf c|}_{i-1}+1}^{{|\bf c|}_{i}}\binom{im+i-1+k_{j_{i}}}{k_{j_{i}}}\zeta(im+i+k_{j_{i}};a+1),

(3.44)

where
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{0}(\cdot):=1
and
{|\bf c|}_{i}:=c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{i},\quad{|\bf c|}_{0}:=0.

Proof.
In
[
24

, we gave the recurrence formula

\displaystyle\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\}_{p};a+1)=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{p}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}\zeta_{HZ}((p-i)(m+1);a+1)\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\}_{i};a+1).

(3.45)

According to
[
22

satisfy the recurrence

\displaystyle Y_{0}=1\,,\quad Y_{p}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{j}x_{p-j}Y_{j}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{j})\,,\quad n\geq 1\,,

(3.46)

and

\displaystyle Y_{p}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p})=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{c_{1}+2c_{2}+\cdots+pc_{p}=p,\atop c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{p}\geq 0}\displaystyle\frac{p!}{c_{1}!c_{2}!\cdots c_{p}!}\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\right)^{c_{1}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\right)^{c_{2}}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{p}}{p!}\right)^{c_{p}}.

Letting

\displaystyle\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\}_{p};a+1)=\displaystyle\frac{Y_{p}\left(0!\zeta_{HZ}(m+1;a+1),\ldots,(-1)^{p-1}(p-1)!\zeta_{HZ}(p(m+1);a+1)\right)}{p!}.

\displaystyle\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\}_{p};a+1)=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{c_{1}+2c_{2}+\cdots+pc_{p}=p,\atop c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{p}\geq 0}\left\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{(j-1)c_{j}}\zeta_{HZ}^{c_{j}}(j(m+1);a+1)}{c_{j}!j^{c_{j}}}\right\}.

(3.49)

Thus, differentiating (
3.49
)
k
times with respect to
a
and using general Leibniz rule, we deduce the desired result.
\square

In particular, we compute the three cases

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\};a+1)\right\}=\binom{m+k}{k}\zeta_{HZ}(m+k+1;a+1),

(3.50)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\}_{2};a+1)\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k,\atop k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\binom{m+k_{1}}{k_{1}}\binom{m+k_{2}}{k_{2}}\zeta_{HZ}(m+k_{1}+1;a+1)\zeta_{HZ}(m+k_{2}+1;a+1)

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\binom{2m+k+1}{k}\zeta_{HZ}(2m+k+2;a+1),

(3.51)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial a^{k}}\left\{\zeta_{HZ}(\{m+1\}_{3};a+1)\right\}

r=1
and
k_{1}=k
then

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{B}({k}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{k}4^{n}}.

For any index
{\bf m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})
, we define the classical multiple polylogarithm function with
r
-complex variables by

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{\bf m}({\bf x})\equiv{{\rm Li}}_{{{m_{1}},{m_{2}},\cdots,{m_{p}}}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{p}\geq 1}{\displaystyle\frac{{{x_{1}^{{n_{1}}}}}x_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots x_{p}^{n_{p}}}{{n_{1}^{{m_{1}}}n_{2}^{{m_{2}}}\cdots n_{p}^{{m_{p}}}}}}.

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})
with
|x_{1}\cdots x_{j}|\leq 1\ (1\leq j\leq p)
and
(x_{1},m_{1})\neq(1,1)
.

It is clear that if
x_{j}=\pm 1
then
{\rm Li}_{\bf m}({\bf x})
becomes to (alternating) MZVs. According to definition, we deduce

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{\bf m}({\bf x})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\displaystyle\frac{\log^{m_{j}-1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{j-1}}{t_{j}}\right)}{(x_{1}\cdots x_{j})^{-1}-t_{j}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p},

(4.60)

where
t_{0}:=1
.

Now, we prove two formulas of iterated integrals.

Theorem 4.1

For nonnegative integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{p-1}
and
m_{p}>0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}\Omega_{p}^{m_{p}+1}(1)\Omega_{p,p-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}\right)dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle=m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}}\log(2)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p}}\left(\sigma_{p},\cat_{i=1}^{p-1}\{\sigma_{p+1-i}\sigma_{p-i}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},1}\left(\sigma_{p},\cat_{i=1}^{p-1}\{\sigma_{p+1-i}\sigma_{p-i}\},-\sigma_{1}\right),

(4.61)

where
\cat_{i=j}^{k}\{a_{i}\}
abbreviates the concatenated argument sequence
a_{j},a_{j+1},\ldots,a_{k}
, with the convention
\cat_{i=j}^{k}\{a_{i}\}:=\emptyset
for
k<j
.

Proof.
Let
A
denote the left hand side of (
4.1
). Applying the change of variables
t_{j}=1-t_{p+1-j}\ (j=1,2,\ldots,p)
, we have

\displaystyle A=\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{t_{p}}}\right)}{(1-t_{1})(1-t_{2})\cdots(1-t_{p})}dt_{1}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{p}.

Then, letting
t_{j}\rightarrow t_{j}^{2}\ (j=1,2,\ldots,p)
yields

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=2^{p+|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}t_{1}\cdots t_{p}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+t_{p}}\right)}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})\cdots(1-t_{p}^{2})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle=2^{p+|{\bf m}|_{p}}\log(2)\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}t_{1}\cdots t_{p}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})\cdots(1-t_{p}^{2})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle\quad-2^{p+|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}t_{1}\cdots t_{p}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}\log\left({1+t_{p}}\right)}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})\cdots(1-t_{p}^{2})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle=2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}}\log(2)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}}{(\sigma_{p}^{-1}-t_{1})(\sigma_{p-1}^{-1}-t_{2})\cdots(\sigma_{1}^{-1}-t_{p})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle\quad-2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}\log(1+t_{p})}{(\sigma_{p}^{-1}-t_{1})(\sigma_{p-1}^{-1}-t_{2})\cdots(\sigma_{1}^{-1}-t_{p})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}.

Then, using (
4.60
) and noting that

\log(1+t_{p})=\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t_{p}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+t_{p+1}}dt_{p+1},

we obtain the desired evaluation by a direct calculation.
\square

Theorem 4.2

For nonnegative integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{p-1}
and
m_{p}>0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}\Omega_{p}^{m_{p}+1}(1)\Omega_{p,p-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1+\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}{2\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}\right)}{\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle=m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|{\bf i}|_{p}=1\atop i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{p}\geq 0}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\binom{m_{l}+i_{l}}{i_{l}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p}\sigma_{1}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+i+1})}_{1,p}}\left(\sigma_{p},\cat_{i=1}^{p-1}\{\sigma_{p+1-i}\sigma_{p-i}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}}\log(2)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p}\sigma_{1}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p}}\left(\sigma_{p},\cat_{i=1}^{p-1}\{\sigma_{p+1-i}\sigma_{p-i}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p}\sigma_{1}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},1}\left(\sigma_{p},\cat_{i=1}^{p-1}\{\sigma_{p+1-i}\sigma_{p-i}\},-\sigma_{1}\right).

(4.62)

Proof.
The proof of (
4.2
) is similar as the proof of (
4.1
). Let
B
denote the left hand side of (
4.2
). By an elementary calculation, we can find that

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=2^{p+|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}t_{1}\cdots t_{p-1}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1+t_{p}}{2t_{p}}\right)}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})\cdots(1-t_{p}^{2})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle=2^{p+|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}t_{1}\cdots t_{p-1}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}\log\left(1+t_{p}\right)}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})\cdots(1-t_{p}^{2})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle\quad-2^{p+|{\bf m}|_{p}}\log(2)\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}t_{1}\cdots t_{p-1}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})\cdots(1-t_{p}^{2})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle\quad+2^{p+|{\bf m}|_{p}}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(0)}t_{1}\cdots t_{p-1}\displaystyle\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{m_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{m_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{m_{1}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{p}}\right)}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})\cdots(1-t_{p}^{2})}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}.

We note the fact that

\displaystyle\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\cdots\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{j-1}}{t_{j}}\right)\quad\quad(t_{0}:=1)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{p}=1\atop i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{p}\geq 0}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right)^{i_{p}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right)^{i_{p-1}}\cdots\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{t_{p-1}}{t_{p}}\right)^{i_{1}}.

Hence, with the help of formula (
4.60
) we may easily deduce the desired result.
\square

Next, we prove two recurrence relations for Apéry-type multiple zeta values
\zeta^{\star}_{B}(\cdots)
. First, by integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p+r}n^{r+1}}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p+r}(x)}\Omega_{p+r}^{m_{p}+1}(1-x)\Omega_{p+r,p+r-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{r+2,r+1}^{m_{1}+1}\Omega_{r+1,r}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}t_{1}^{n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p+r}dt_{j}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}n}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p}(x)}\Omega_{p}^{m_{p}+1}(1-x)\Omega_{p,p-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}^{m_{1}+1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}

\displaystyle\quad-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}n^{r+1-j}(-1)^{p+r-j}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r-j}}(1-x).

Hence, the (
2.7
) can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\bf m}_{p}^{v};x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p+r}n^{r+1}}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p+r}(x)}\Omega_{p+r}^{m_{p}+1}(1-x)\Omega_{p+r,p+r-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{r+2,r+1}^{m_{1}+1}\Omega_{r+1,r}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}t_{1}^{n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p+r}dt_{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}n^{r+1-j}(-1)^{p+r-j}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r-j}}(1-x)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-j}\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\bf m}_{j}^{v}){\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p}}(1-x).

(4.63)

Theorem 4.3

For integers
m_{2},\ldots,m_{p-1},r\geq 0
and
m_{1},m_{p}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{j-1}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p}\right)\zeta^{\star}_{B}(r+2,{\bf m}_{j}^{v})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{r-j}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{(\bf m+1)}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r-j}\right)\zeta^{\star}_{B}(j+1)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{r}2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}+1}\log(2)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p+r}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r}}\left(\sigma_{p+r},\cat_{i=1}^{p+r-1}\{\sigma_{p+r+1-i}\sigma_{p+r-i}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}+1}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p+r}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r+1}}\left(\sigma_{p+r},\cat_{i=1}^{p+r-1}\{\sigma_{p+r+1-i}\sigma_{p+r-i}\},-\sigma_{1}\right).

(4.64)

Proof.
Setting
x=0
in (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{j-1}\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\bf m}_{j}^{v})\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{r}n^{r+1}}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{p}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p+r}(0)}\Omega_{p+r}^{m_{p}+1}(1)\Omega_{p+r,p+r-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{r+2,r+1}^{m_{1}+1}\Omega_{r+1,r}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}t_{1}^{n}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p+r}

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}n^{r+1-j}(-1)^{r-j}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{(\bf m+1)}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r-j}\right).

(4.65)

Multiplying it by
\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{r+2}4^{n}}
and summing with respect to
n
, and noting that the well-known formula (see
[
5
,
16
]
)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^{n}n}t^{n}=2\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-t}}\right),

we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{j-1}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p}\right)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\bf m}_{j}^{v})}{n^{r+2}4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{2(-1)^{r}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}m_{j}!}\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{E_{p+r}(0)}\Omega_{p+r}^{m_{p}+1}(1)\Omega_{p+r,p+r-1}^{m_{p-1}+1}\cdots\Omega_{r+2,r+1}^{m_{1}+1}\Omega_{r+1,r}\cdots\Omega_{2,1}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}\right)dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p+r}

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{r-j}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{(\bf m+1)}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r-j}\right)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{j+1}4^{n}}.

Then by (
4.1
), the desired formula can be established directly.
\square

Theorem 4.4

For integers
m_{2},\ldots,m_{p-1},r\geq 0
and
m_{1},m_{p}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{j-1}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{j+1,p}\right)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\bf m}_{j}^{v})H_{n}}{n^{r+1}4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{r-j}\zeta\left(\overleftarrow{(\bf m+1)}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r-j}\right)\zeta^{\star}_{B}(j,1)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{r}2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}+1}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|{\bf i}|_{r}+|{\bf j}|_{p}=1\atop i_{1},\ldots,i_{r},j_{1},\ldots,j_{p}\geq 0}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\binom{m_{l}+j_{l}}{j_{l}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p+r}\sigma_{1}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+j+1})}_{1,p},\overleftarrow{({\bf i+1})}_{1,r}}\left(\sigma_{p+r},\cat_{i=1}^{p+r-1}\{\sigma_{p+r+1-i}\sigma_{p+r-i}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{r}2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}+1}\log(2)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p+r}\sigma_{1}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r}}\left(\sigma_{p+r},\cat_{i=1}^{p+r-1}\{\sigma_{p+r+1-i}\sigma_{p+r-i}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{r}2^{|{\bf m}|_{p}+1}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\atop j=1,2,\ldots,p+r}\sigma_{1}{\rm Li}_{\overleftarrow{({\bf m+1})}_{1,p},\{1\}_{r+1}}\left(\sigma_{p+r},\cat_{i=1}^{p+r-1}\{\sigma_{p+r+1-i}\sigma_{p+r-i}\},-\sigma_{1}\right),

(4.66)

where
H_{n}=\zeta_{n}(1)
stands for the classical harmonic number.

Proof.
The proof of (
4.4
) is similar as the proof of (
4.3
). Multiplying (
4
) by
\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{r+1}4^{n}}
and summing with respect to
n
, and noting that the formula (see Chen
[
5
]
)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}\binom{2n}{n}}{4^{n}}t^{n}=\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-t}}\log\left(\displaystyle\frac{1+\sqrt{1-t}}{2\sqrt{1-t}}\right),

with the help of (
4.2
), we prove the desired result.
\square

From (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we can get the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5

For an index
{\bf m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})
and positive integer
r
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{B}^{\star}(r,{\bf m}_{p}^{v})\in\mathbb{Q}[\text{\rm Alternating MZVs with depth}\leq p+r].

From Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
, by direct calculations, we deduce two specific examples:

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{B}(2,2,1)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\star}_{n}(2,1)}{n^{2}4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}

\displaystyle=2\zeta^{2}(2)\log(2)+4\zeta(2)(\zeta(\bar{2},1)+\zeta(2,\bar{1}))

\displaystyle\quad-8\log(2)\left(\zeta(2,2)+\zeta(2,\bar{2})+\zeta(\bar{2},\bar{2})+\zeta(\bar{2},2)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-8\left(\zeta(2,2,\bar{1})+\zeta(2,\bar{2},1)+\zeta(\bar{2},\bar{2},\bar{1})+\zeta(\bar{2},2,1)\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{75}{8}\zeta(5)-4\zeta(4)\log(2)-3\zeta(2)\zeta(3)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{H^{2}_{n}}{n^{2}4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}=2\zeta^{\star}_{B}(2,1,1)-\zeta^{\star}_{B}(2,2)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2)\zeta^{\star}_{B}(1,1)-8(\zeta(3,1)-\zeta(3,\bar{1})+\zeta(\bar{3},\bar{1})-\zeta(\bar{3},1))

\displaystyle\quad-4(\zeta(2,2)-\zeta(2,\bar{2})+\zeta(\bar{2},\bar{2})-\zeta(\bar{2},2))

\displaystyle\quad+4\log(2)(\zeta(2,1)-\zeta(2,\bar{1})+\zeta(\bar{2},\bar{1})-\zeta(\bar{2},1))

\displaystyle\quad+4(\zeta(2,1,\bar{1})-\zeta(2,\bar{1},1)+\zeta(\bar{2},\bar{1},\bar{1})-\zeta(\bar{2},1,1))

\displaystyle=32{\rm Li}_{4}(1/2)-14\zeta(4)+7\zeta(3)\log(2)-8\zeta(2)\log^{2}(2)+\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\log^{4}(2),

where we used Au’s Mathematica package [1,

\leq 8

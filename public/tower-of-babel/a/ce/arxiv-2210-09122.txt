Oblique scattering from non-Hermitian optical waveguides
Tal Goldstein, Gal Shmuel

Abstract
A judicious design of gain and loss leads to counterintuitive wave phenomena that are inaccessible by conservative systems. Notably, such designs can give rise to laser-absorber modes and anisotropic transmission resonances. Here, we analyze the emergence of these phenomena in an optical scatterer with sinusoid gain-loss modulation that is subjected to monochromatic oblique waves. We derive an analytical solution to the problem, with which we show how the scatterer parameters, and specifically the modulation phase and incident angle, constitute a real design space for these phenomena.

A judicious design of gain and loss in physical systems in general

The bulk of the works is on systems that are invariant to combined
parity-time (
\mathcal{PT}
) transformations
[

,
16
,

25
,
44
,

,
which translates to the condition
\epsilon\left(-z\right)=\epsilon^{*}\left(z\right)

for the dielectric coefficient of a medium that
is modulated along

. Depending on the parameters of such systems,
their spectrum can be real, in spite of the fact that they are governed
by non-Hermitian operators
[
38
,
37
]
.
This domain in the parameter space is called the
\mathcal{PT}
-exact phase,
and the rest of the domain, at which the eigenvalues are complex,
is called the
\mathcal{PT}
-broken phase. The transition points between the
two phases are a type of
exceptional points
(EPs)
[
5
,
22
,
21
,
34
,
36
]
,
at which the eigenvalues and eigenvectors become degenerate, and are
the source of the counterintuitive phenomena mentioned earlier
[
1
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
30
,
33
,
41
,
42
,
45
,

,
48
,
53
]
.

Of particular relevance to this work is the phenomenon of unidirectional
invisibility, which was discovered by
Lin et al. 2011
, when analyzing
a
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric scatterer with sinusoid modulation. Using the
rotating wave approximation, they have found that at the EPs of the
scattering matrix, the reflection vanishes from one side only, while
the transmission is unity; they termed it later as anisotropic transmission
resonance (ATR)
[
17
]
. In addition, the transmission also has zero phase,
hence the scatterer is unidirectionally invisible. Later on,
Longhi 2011
,

Jones 2012
and
Uzdin and Moiseyev 2012
used exact solutions to
analyze the scattering properties beyond the limitations of the rotating
wave approximation.

A second pioneering work that motivated the study to follow is by

Longhi 2010
. He showed that a waveguide with uniform grating
and two symmetric layers of gain and loss can simultaneously act as
a laser oscillator, emitting coherent waves
[
47
]
,
and as a coherent perfect absorber (CPA), completely absorbing particular
incoming waves
[
6
]
.
Chong et al. 2011
have identified
the CPA-laser states as special solutions in the
\mathcal{PT}
-broken phase,
where a pole and a zero of the scattering matrix

The pioneering works in Refs.
[
31
,
28
]
led
to various studies whose objective is to control the phase transition
and scattering singularities by different means, such as the incident
angle of oblique waves and the chirality in a single gain-loss bilayer

[
11
,
27
]
; or the sinusoid modulation
properties
[
29
]
. Here, we extend the study
to the problem of oblique waves that are scattered by waveguides of
different sinusoid gain-loss modulations. We derive an exact analytical
solution to the problem, with which we characterize how the scattering
properties, and specifically the EPs, depend on the parameters of
the system, such as the incident angle (
\theta
); driving frequency
(
\omega
); and the wavelength- (
\beta
), amplitude- and phase
(
z_{0}
) of the modulation. For two particular cases where the modulation
yields a
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric medium, we characterize the
\mathcal{PT}
phase
diagram in the
\left(\theta,\omega\right)
and
\left(\theta,\beta\right)

parameter spaces. We also calculate the phase diagram that defines
the ATRs in the
\left(\theta,\beta\right)
space. We show that
there is a range of modulation amplitudes and wavelengths at which
these ATRs coincide with (or reside very close to) Fabry-Pérot resonance frequencies.
By analyzing the resultant structure of the phase diagram, we also
gain insights on how to access bidirectional zero-reflection states.
We conclude the study with an analysis of the poles and zeros of the
scattering matrix. We show that the modulation phase and incident
angle constitute together real (rather than complex) design space
for these singularities, and specifically for quasi CPA-laser states.

Our results are presented in the following order. Sec.
2

contains the mathematical formulation of the problem, together with
our derivation of its analytical solution. In Sec.
3
,
we recall the two definitions of the scattering matrix and their connection
to EPs. We further derive a useful relation between the poles and
zeros of the two matrix definitions, for the particular family of
modulations that we analyze. We carry out a parametric study in Sec.
4
,
and conclude this paper with a summary of our main results in Sec.
5
.

We consider a nonmagnetic medium at
0\leq z\leq L
, whose dielectric
coefficient is modulated according to

\epsilon\left(z\right)=\epsilon_{h}+\epsilon_{p}e^{i2\beta\left(z+z_{0}\right)}.

(1)

where
\beta=\pi/L
is half the wavenumber of the modulation.
Note that when
z_{0}=0
or
L/2
, the dielectric coefficient
satisfy the necessary condition for
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry, namely,
\epsilon\left(-z\right)=\epsilon^{*}\left(z\right)
.
(The particular case of
z_{0}=0
was considered in Refs.
[
28
,
26
,
32
,
50
]
,
for normal incident waves.) Two homogeneous nonmagnetic waveguides
with a dielectric constant
\epsilon_{h}
are connected at
z\leq 0

and
z\geq L
, guiding oblique monochromatic waves to- and from
the modulated medium (Fig.
1
). We focus on TE (or

H
) modes
[
49
]
, such that the electric field is
in the
y
direction, and propagates in the
xz
plane. The
governing equation for these modes is

\left[\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{z}^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\epsilon\left(z\right)\right]E\left(x,z\right)=0,

(2)

where
c
is the speed of light, and we consider time dependency
of the form
e^{-i\omega t}
with an angular frequency
\omega
. The
solution in the homogeneous waveguides is given by

E\left(x,z\right)=\begin{cases}A_{1}e^{ik_{h}\left(x\cos\theta+z\sin\theta\right)}+B_{1}e^{ik_{h}\left(x\cos\theta-z\sin\theta\right)}&z\leq 0,\\
A_{2}e^{ik_{h}\left[x\cos\theta-\left(z-L\right)\sin\theta\right]}+B_{2}e^{ik_{h}\left[x\cos\theta+\left(z-L\right)\sin\theta\right]}&z\geq L,\end{cases}

(3)

where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
(
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
) are the amplitudes of
the incoming (outgoing) waves,
k_{h}=\omega\sqrt{\epsilon_{h}}/c
,
and
\theta
is the angle between the
xy
plane and the waves
in these waveguides.

Inside the modulated medium, we seek solutions in the form

E\left(x,z\right)=e^{ik_{h}x\cos\theta}Z\left(z\right),

(4)

where the
x
dependency is enforced by the continuity of
E

at
z=0
and
L
. By substituting the ansatz (
4
)
into Eq. (
2
), we obtain the following equation for

Z\left(z\right)

\left[\partial_{z}^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\left(\epsilon_{h}+\epsilon_{p}e^{i2\beta\left(z+z_{0}\right)}\right)-k_{h}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\right]Z\left(z\right)=0,

(5)

which can rearranged as

\left[\partial_{z}^{2}+k_{h}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\left(1+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\frac{\epsilon_{p}}{\epsilon_{h}}e^{i2\beta\left(z+z_{0}\right)}\right)\right]Z\left(z\right)=0.

(6)

Our next step is to rewrite Eq. (
6
) as a Bessel equation. To this end, we perform a change of variable, namely,

\zeta=\sqrt{\frac{k_{h}^{2}\epsilon_{p}}{-\beta^{2}\epsilon_{h}}}e^{i\beta\left(z+z_{0}\right)},

(7)

such that

\partial_{z}\zeta=i\beta\zeta,\quad\partial_{z}^{2}\zeta=-\beta^{2}\zeta,

(8)

which together with the chain rule
\partial_{z}Z=\partial_{\zeta}Z\partial_{z}\zeta
allows us to replace Eq. (
6
) with

\left[\zeta^{2}\partial_{\zeta}^{2}+\zeta\partial_{\zeta}+\left(\zeta^{2}-\frac{k_{h}^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta\right)\right]Z=0.

(9)

Eq. (
9
) is solved exactly using the Bessel functions

J_{\pm\nu}\left(\zeta\right)\eqqcolon\psi_{\pm}\left(z\right)
,
where
\nu=\left(k_{h}/\beta\right)\sin\theta
is assumed to
be a non-integer number. Essentially, Eq. (
9
) and
its solution are generalizations of the results in Refs.
[
32
,
26
,
50
]

to oblique waves. The electric field in the modulated medium is
a linear combination of these solutions, such that

E\left(x,0\leq z\leq L\right)=C_{1}e^{ik_{h}x\cos\theta}\psi_{+}\left(z\right)+C_{2}e^{ik_{h}x\cos\theta}\psi_{-}\left(z\right).

(10)

Our next objective is to related the amplitudes of the waves inside the modulated medium to the amplitudes in the homogeneous waveguides. To this end, we assemble the two quantities that are continuous along
z
, i.e.,
E
and
\partial_{z}E
, into a column vector which we denote by
\mathsf{s}
. In the modulated medium, this so-called state vector can be written as

\mathsf{s}\left(0\leq z\leq L\right)=\mathsf{Q}\left(z\right)\mathsf{c},

(11)

where

\displaystyle\text{$\mathsf{Q}$}\left(z\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{+}\left(z\right)&\psi_{-}\left(z\right)\\
\partial_{z}\psi_{+}\left(z\right)&\partial_{z}\psi_{-}\left(z\right)\end{array}\right),\mathsf{c}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}C_{1}\\
C_{2}\end{array}\right).

Similarly, the state vector in the right- and left ends of the left- and right homogeneous waveguides is

\mathsf{s}\left(0^{-}\right)=\mathsf{Q}_{h}\mathsf{l},\quad\mathsf{s}\left(L^{+}\right)=\mathsf{Q}_{h}\mathsf{r},

(12)

respectively, where

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
ik_{h}\sin\theta&-ik_{h}\sin\theta\end{array}\right),\mathsf{l}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{1}\\
B_{1}\end{array}\right),\mathsf{r}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{2}\\
B_{2}\end{array}\right).

It now follows that
from the continuity of the state vector at
z=0

and
L
, we obtain

\mathsf{Q}_{h}\mathsf{l}=\text{\text{$\mathsf{Q}$}$\left(0\right)$}\mathsf{c},\ \mathsf{Q}_{h}\mathsf{r}=\text{\text{$\mathsf{Q}$}$\left(L\right)$}\mathsf{c}.

(13)

Accordingly, the transfer matrix
\mathsf{M}
that relates between the
wave amplitudes of the two homogeneous waveguides is

\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{Q}_{h}^{-1}\text{$\mathsf{Q}$}\left(L\right)\text{$\mathsf{Q}$}^{-1}\left(0\right)\mathsf{Q}_{h},\ \text{such\ that}\ \mathsf{r}=\mathsf{M}\mathsf{l};

(14)

this completes the solution for the electric waves, given any amplitude
of incoming waves. Using the Bessel’s continuation rule we have that

\text{$\mathsf{Q}$}\left(L\right)=\text{$\mathsf{Q}$}\left(0\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{ik_{h}\sin\theta L}&0\\
0&e^{-ik_{h}\sin\theta L}\end{array}\right)
, which together with Eq. (
14
) yields
\det\mathsf{M}=1
.

3
Scattering analysis

We can now relate the incoming and outgoing waves in terms of the
components of
\mathsf{M}
. One way to do that is

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}B_{1}\\
B_{2}\end{array}\right)=\mathsf{S}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\end{array}\right),\ \mathsf{S}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{12}/M_{22}&M_{22}^{-1}\\
M_{22}^{-1}&-M_{21}/M_{22}\end{array}\right).

(15)

If we prescribe an incoming wave with unitary amplitude from the left
(right), such that
A_{1}=1
and
A_{2}=0
(
A_{1}=0
and
A_{2}=1
),
it follows that the amplitude of the transmitted wave is
S_{21}

(
S_{12}
), and the amplitude of the reflected wave is
S_{11}

(
S_{22}
). Accordingly, the components of
\mathsf{S}
are identified
with the reflection and transmission coefficients, such that

\mathsf{S}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{L}&t\\
t&r_{R}\end{pmatrix}.

(16)

The eigenvalues of
\mathsf{S}
are

\lambda^{\left(\mathsf{S}\right)}_{1,2}=\frac{r_{L}+r_{R}}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{\left(r_{L}-r_{R}\right)^{2}}{4}+t^{2}};

(17)

they become degenerate, together with their eigenvectors, when
\left(r_{L}-r_{R}\right)/t=\pm 2i
.
These exceptional points of
\mathsf{S}
for a
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric scatterer
are linked to the degeneracies in its eigenmodes, if it was bounded

[
2
]
.

There is an alternative scattering matrix, denoted here
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
, that is obtained if we interchange the two entries of the column vector in the left-hand-side of Eq. (
15
). By doing so, we obtain

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}B_{2}\\
B_{1}\end{array}\right)=\tilde{\mathsf{S}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\end{array}\right),\ \tilde{\mathsf{S}}=\begin{pmatrix}t&r_{R}\\
r_{L}&t\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

Both Eq. ,(
15
) and Eq. (
18
) deliver the same relations between the incoming and outgoing waves, however the corresponding scattering matrices have different eigenvalues. Specifically, the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
are

\lambda^{\left(\tilde{\mathsf{S}}\right)}_{1,2}=t\pm\sqrt{r_{L}r_{R}};

(19)

they become degenerate when either
r_{L}
or
r_{R}
vanish. When the scatterer is
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric,
the exceptional points of
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
reflect anisotropic transmission
resonances (ATRs), at which the reflection may vanish only from one
side while the transmittance is unity, since the scattering coefficients
for
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric system satisfy

\left|T-1\right|=\sqrt{R_{L}R_{R}},

(20)

where
T\coloneqq\left|t\right|^{2}
is the transmittance, and

R_{L,R}\coloneqq\left|r_{L,R}\right|^{2}
are the two reflectances.

The zeroes of
\mathsf{S}
, which correspond to a zero eigenvalue, reflect
a perfectly absorbing medium, while the poles of
\mathsf{S}
, which correspond
to an infinite eigenvalue, reflect a lasing oscillator which emits outgoing
coherent waves. It is follows that if
\mathsf{S}
has a zero, then from
Eq. (
17
) we have
t^{2}=r_{L}r_{R}
, thus
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
has
a zero too. In our case, it also follows that if
\mathsf{S}
has a pole
then
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
has a pole too. To show this, we note that

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\mathsf{S}^{*}\left(-z_{0}\right)\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{S}^{-1}\left(z_{0}\right),\tilde{\mathsf{S}}^{*}\left(-z_{0}\right)=\tilde{\mathsf{S}}^{-1}\left(z_{0}\right),

(21)

where
\sigma_{1}
is the first Pauli matrix, since
\epsilon\left(z\right)

satisfies Eq. (
1
). As a result, the product of
the modulus of the eigenvalues of
\mathsf{S}\left(-z_{0}\right)
and
\mathsf{S}\left(z_{0}\right)

must be
1
, and the same holds for
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
. Therefore if
\mathsf{S}\left(z_{0}\right)

has a pole then
\mathsf{S}\left(-z_{0}\right)
has a zero, and hence
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}\left(-z_{0}\right)

has a zero too, which finally implies that
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}\left(z_{0}\right)

has a pole. In the next section, we analyze the dependency of the zeroes,
poles and EPs of
\mathsf{S}
and
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
, and the scattering properties, on the
system parameters, and specifically the incident angle.

5
Summary

We derived an exact solution to the problem of monochromatic oblique
TE waves that are scattered by a waveguide with sinusoid gain-loss
modulation. We have analyzed a family of modulations that are parametrized
by their phase, amplitude and wavelength. We have investigated how
these parameters, together with the frequency and incident angle of
the waves, affect the emergence of lasing, perfect absorption, and
anisotropic transmission resonances. For two particular modulations
that satisfy
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry, we have evaluated the diagram of the
exact- and broken
\mathcal{PT}
phases in the parameter space, and the phase
diagram that defines the anisotropic transmission resonances. The two modulations have different diagrams, one of which is much richer, exhibiting multiple re-entries to the broken region. With regard to the diagrams of the anisotropic transmission resonances, we have showed that there is a regime in the parameter space where these resonances
reside very close to Fabry-Pérot resonances. A further investigation of this property has
provided guidelines on how to design bidirectional
zero-reflection states. Finally, we have analyzed the poles and
zeros of the scattering matrix, which correspond, respectively, to
lasing and perfect absorption states. We showed that the the modulation
phase and incident angle constitute a design space for these singularities
when the scatterer is subjected to monochromatic waves. Specifically, we have identified quasi CPA-laser states, when a zero and pole are very close but not coincide. These states correspond to scatterers that can simultaneously absorb almost completely incoming coherent waves and significantly enhance certain incoherent excitations.

Appendix. Approximated solution near the first Fabry-Pérot resonance

Lin et al. 2011
have showed that for normal incident waves

\left(\theta=\pi/2\right)
impinging on a
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric Bragg scatterer, the scattering matrix
\tilde{\mathsf{S}}
exhibits
an EP at the Bragg point. Here, using a multiple scale expansion, we
show a similar result for oblique waves, impinging on a non-Hermitian scatterer exhibiting one period of modulation, where the EP occur at the first Fabry-Pérot resonance, i.e.,
\beta=k_{h}\sin\theta

With these assumptions at hand, we introduce the variables

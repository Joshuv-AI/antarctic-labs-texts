(Markov’s inequality)
.

If

\mathbb{P}(X\geqslant a)\leqslant\frac{\mathbb{E}(X)}{a}.

Lemma 4

(Chernoff bound)

Let

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X\geqslant(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}(X))

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp(-\varepsilon^{2}np/3),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X\leqslant(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}(X))

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp(-\varepsilon^{2}np/2).

\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=1\textnormal{ for all }i\in S)\leqslant\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=1).

Proof

For motivation we first consider a naïve application of the Lovász local lemma. Suppose
G
is a linear
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph. Colour
G
with
k\coloneqq\lfloor 100(\Delta/(d+1))^{1/r}\rfloor
colours uniformly at random. For each set
F
of
d+1
edges all containing a common vertex, let
B_{F}
be the event that the vertex set of
F
is monochromatic. Then, since

is linear,
p\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(B_{F})=k^{-r(d+1)}
. For a fixed
F
, the number of
F^{\prime}
sharing a vertex with
F
is at most
D\coloneqq(r(d+1)+1)\Delta(r+1)\binom{\Delta}{d}
; here we have specified the vertex shared with
F
, the edge containing that vertex, the common vertex of the edges in

, and finally the remaining

. Now

and so

\displaystyle 4pD

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\cdot 100^{-r(d+1)}\bigl(\tfrac{d+1}{\Delta}\bigr)^{d+1}\cdot 3r^{2}e^{d}d^{-d+1}\Delta^{d+1}

\displaystyle=12r^{2}e^{d}\cdot 100^{-r(d+1)}\cdot d(d+1)\bigl(\tfrac{d+1}{d}\bigr)^{d}

\displaystyle\leqslant 24r^{2}d^{2}e^{d+1}\cdot 100^{-r(d+1)}\leqslant 1.

Hence, by the Lovász local lemma, there is a colouring in which no
B_{F}
occurs; that is, there is a

-defective

-colouring of
G
. It was crucial in this argument that

was linear so that the powers of
\Delta
in

and

cancelled out exactly. For non-linear

, the number of neighbours of a vertex

is not determined by the degree of

and so

may be larger without a corresponding decrease in

. A more involved argument is required.

2.1
First Steps

Here we outline our colouring strategy before diving into the details. We are given an
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph
G
with maximum degree
\Delta
and wish to colour its vertices so that every vertex is in at most
d
monochromatic edges. For a fixed colouring
\phi
, the
monochromatic degree
of a vertex

\deg_{\phi}(v)
, is the number of monochromatic edges containing

(which must have colour
\phi(v)
).

First we colour the vertices of
G
uniformly at random with
k

k=\lfloor 49(\frac{\Delta}{d+1})^{1/r}\rfloor
. Say a vertex is
bad
if its monochromatic degree is greater than
d
and
good
otherwise. We are aiming for a colouring in which every vertex is good. The expected monochromatic degree of a vertex
v
in such a colouring is
k^{-r}\deg(v)\leqslant k^{-r}\Delta\leqslant 49^{-r}(d+1)
. In particular, each individual vertex has small (certainly, by Markov’s inequality, at most
49^{-r}
) probability of being bad. However, the goodness of a vertex
v
depends on the colours assigned to vertices in the neighbourhood of
v
and so
49^{-r}
is not a sufficiently small probability to conclude (by, say, the Lovász local lemma) that there is a particular colouring for which all vertices are good.

Instead of colouring all of
G
with a single-uniform colouring, we do so over many rounds. After a round (where we coloured a hypergraph
G
), any good vertices will keep their colours and be discarded (they have been coloured appropriately). Let
G^{\prime}
be the subhypergraph of
G
induced by the bad vertices. In the next round we uniformly and randomly colour the vertices of
G^{\prime}
with a new palette of colours completely disjoint from those used in previous rounds. If the palettes all have the same size and the process runs for too many rounds, then we will end up using too many colours. However, if
\Delta(G^{\prime})\leqslant 2^{-r}\Delta(G)
, then we can use half the number of colours in the next round and so use
\mathcal{O}((\frac{\Delta}{d+1})^{1/r})
colours across all the rounds. Thus, our aim is to prove the following nibble-style lemma from which
Theorem

1
easily follows.

Fix non-negative integers

with

(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph

\Delta(G^{\prime})\leqslant 2^{-r}\Delta
.

A vertex

Let

Fix distinct colours

Let

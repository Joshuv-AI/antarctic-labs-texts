Nonstationary frames of translates and frames from the Weyl–Heisenberg group and the extended affine group
Divya Jindal, Lalit Kumar Vashisht ∗

Abstract
. In this work, we analyze Gabor frames for the Weyl–Heisenberg group and wavelet frames for the extended affine group. Firstly, we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of nonstationary frames of translates. Using these conditions, we give the existence of Gabor frames from the Weyl–Heisenberg group and wavelet frames for the extended affine group. We present a representation of functions in the closure of the linear span of a Gabor frame sequence in terms of the Fourier transform of window functions. We show that the canonical dual of frames of translates has the same structure. An approximation of inverse of the frame operator of nonstationary frames of translates is presented. It is shown that a nonstationary frame of translates is a Riesz basis if it is linearly independent and satisfies approximation of the inverse frame operator. Finally, we give equivalent conditions for a nonstationary sequence of translates to be linearly independent.

Frames for separable Hilbert separable spaces were introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer in

Wavelet analysis corresponds to unitary irreducible representations of the affine group, while Gabor analysis corresponds to unitary irreducible representations of the Weyl-Heisenberg group. Whereas Gabor analysis yields a time-frequency representation of signals, wavelet analysis provides a time-scale representation. There is huge literature on Gabor analysis and wavelet analysis and its applications in both pure mathematics and engineering science, see

In 1998, Dai and Liang proved that if
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are two affine orthonormal MRA wavelets then there exists a continuous map
A:[0,1]\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
such that
A(0)=\psi_{1}
,
A(1)=\psi_{2}
, and

is an affine orthonormal MRA wavelet for all

[

[

[

and Torresani in
[

39

established a connection between Gabor analysis and wavelet analysis by exhibiting both the affine group and the Weyl–Heisenberg group as subgroups of the four dimensional affine Weyl–Heisenberg group. They also contracted the corresponding coherent state families, resolutions of the identity, and tight frames. One of the key insights in this paper is the realization that the affine group and the Weyl–Heisenberg group have different dimensions. They overcame this problem by introducing the extended affine group and an additional parameter,

\varepsilon

\varepsilon=0
yields a Gabor frame and
\varepsilon=1
yields a wavelet frame.

Inspired by the above works we study the characterization of Gabor frames in

with compact support and several generators (and in particular, Parseval frames). We apply our results in the context of group representations, and in particular to those systems associated with the Weyl–Heisenberg group and extended affine group, using the unitary irreducible representation. Whereas authors of

and

They also proved a Paley-Wiener type perturbation results for frames with several generators. Extension of frame conditions from

[

and Christensen and Hasannasab

As usual
\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{R}^{+}

\mathbb{R}^{*}

\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{I}
denotes a countable (finite or infinite) index set. The
support
of a function
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}
is closure of the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}:f(x)\neq 0\}
. Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{H}\neq\{0\}
denotes a separable Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\mathcal{H}

\|f\|=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}

2.1.
Hilbert frames and Riesz Bases:

A collection of vectors
\mathcal{F}:=\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
in
\mathcal{H}
is called a
frame
(or
Hilbert frame
) of
\mathcal{H}
if for some
\alpha_{o}
,
\beta_{o}\in(0,\infty)
the following inequality holds

(2.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{o}\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle f,\varphi_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq\beta_{o}\|f\|^{2}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Inequality (
2.1
) is called the
frame inequality
. The positive scalars
\alpha_{o}
and
\beta_{o}
, obviously not unique, are known as
lower frame bound
and
upper frame bound
of
\mathcal{F}
. The frame
\mathcal{F}
is
tight
if
\alpha_{o}=\beta_{o}
, it is
Parseval
, if
\alpha_{o}=\beta_{o}=1
. If
\mathcal{F}
fulfills the upper inequality in (
2.1
), then we say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
Bessel sequence
with
Bessel bound

\beta_{o}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is a Bessel sequence, then the map
S:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by
Sf=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{I}}\langle f,\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}
is called the
frame operator
of the frame
\mathcal{F}
. The frame operator
S
is bounded and linear. It is invertible on
\mathcal{H}
if
\mathcal{F}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. In that case
\mathcal{F}
gives the following reconstruction formula,

f=SS^{-1}f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{I}}\langle f,S^{-1}\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}
,
f\in\mathcal{H}
. The scalars
\langle f,S^{-1}\varphi_{k}\rangle
are known as
frame coefficients
. The supremum over all lower frame bounds is called the
optimal lower frame bound
, and the infimum over all upper frame bounds is called the
optimal upper frame bound
. The optimal bounds in terms of the frame operator are given in the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
4
, p. 121]

The lower optimal frame bound
\alpha_{\text{Opt}}
and the upper optimal frame bound
\beta_{\text{Opt}}
of a frame
\mathcal{F}
with frame operator
S
are given by
\alpha_{\text{Opt}}=\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}
,
\beta_{\text{Opt}}=\|S\|
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
4
, Definition 7.3.1]

A sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
in
\mathcal{H}
is a
Riesz frame
of
\mathcal{H}
if every subsequence of
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is a frame for its closed linear span, with the same frame bounds
\alpha
,
\beta
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
4
, Definition 3.6.1]

Let
\Xi
be a bounded, linear and bijective operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
and
\{\chi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. A sequence of the form
\{\Xi\chi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is called a
Riesz basis
for
\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
4
, Theorem 7.1.1]

A frame
\mathcal{F}=\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
of
\mathcal{H}
is a Riesz basis for
\mathcal{H}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}
has a biorthogonal sequence
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
, that is,

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{i},\psi_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{if}\ i=j;\\
0,\quad\text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

Every Riesz basis is a basis and hence linearly independent. For other types of independence of frames we refer to Chapter 7 of

2.2.
Gabor System and Wavelet System

As is standard,
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx):=\Big\{f:\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|^{2}dx<\infty\Big\}
, the space of square integrable (in the sense of Lebesgue) functions over
\mathbb{R}
.
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
is a Hilbert space with respect to the standard inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx
.
The Fourier transform of a function
f
, denoted by
\widehat{f}
, defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\gamma}dx,\ \ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

Mathematically, a Gabor system and wavelet system are defined by using the following three classes of operators which act unitarily on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
. For
a
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
define translation operator, modulation operator and dilation operator, respectively, on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
by

\displaystyle T_{a}f(x)

\displaystyle\mapsto f(x-a),\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)\quad\text{(Translation by a);}

\displaystyle E_{b}f(x)

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{2\pi ibx}f(x),\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)\quad\text{(Modulation by b);}

\displaystyle D_{c}f(x)

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}f\Big(\frac{x}{c}\Big),\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)\quad\text{(Dilation by c).}

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
be a non-zero function. A collection of functions of the form
\mathcal{G}(a,b,\phi):=\{E_{mb}T_{na}\phi\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}

=\{e^{2\pi imbx}\phi(x-na)\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called the
Gabor system
; and the collection
\mathcal{W}(c,b,\phi):\{T_{kbc^{j}}D_{c^{j}}\phi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{c^{\frac{-j}{2}}\phi(c^{-j}x-kb)\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called the
wavelet system
. A frame of the form
\mathcal{G}(a,b,\phi)
and
\mathcal{W}(a,b,\phi)
for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
is called the
Gabor frame
and the
wavelet frame
, respectively. Christensen
[
4
]
and Heil
[
19
]
are good texts for fundamental properties of Gabor frames and wavelet frames.

The following fundamental properties of above operators will be used throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
4
, p. 65]

For any
0\neq a\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{T_{a}f}=E_{-a}\widehat{f},\quad\widehat{E_{a}f}=T_{a}\widehat{f},\quad\widehat{D_{a}f}=D_{\frac{1}{a}}\widehat{f}.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
4
, p. 65]

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),a,b\in\mathbb{R},c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the following commutator relations hold.

\displaystyle T_{a}E_{b}f(x)=e^{-2\pi iba}E_{b}T_{a}f(x),\quad T_{a}D_{c}f(x)=D_{c}T_{a/c}f(x),\quad D_{c}E_{b}f(x)=E_{b/c}D_{c}f(x).

The following result is direct consequence of Parseval’s equation and can be found in any standard text on analysis, for instance
[
19
,
45
]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume that for
\mu>0
and
f\in L^{2}([0,\mu],dx)\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle\mu\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k}|^{2},\quad\text{where}\ c_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\frac{k}{\mu}x}dx.

3.
Frames for the Weyl–Heisenberg Group

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a locally compact group with left Haar measure
\mu
. A unitary representation of
\mathcal{G}
is a homomorphism
\pi
from
\mathcal{G}
into the group
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
that is continuous with respect to the strong operator topology, that is, a map
\zeta:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
that satisfies
\zeta(xy)=\zeta(x)\zeta(y)
and
\zeta(x^{-1})=\zeta(x)^{-1}=\zeta(x)^{*}
, and for which
x\to\pi(x)u
is continuous from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
for any
u\in\mathcal{H}
. Also,
\zeta
is said to be irreducible if
\pi
admits only trivial (means,
=\{0\}
or
\mathcal{H}
) invariant subspaces.

The Weyl–Heisenberg group
\mathcal{W}
is the outer semidirect product
\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{R}
, where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Aut(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by
\Phi_{\zeta}\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}+\zeta a_{2}\\
a_{2}\end{pmatrix}
for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The product of
(s,\vec{u})
and
(t,\vec{v})
in
\mathcal{W}
is given by

\displaystyle(s,\vec{u})(t,\vec{v})=(s+t,\Phi_{\zeta}(\vec{u}+\vec{v})).

For every
A\in\mathbb{R}^{*},B\in\mathbb{R}
, the unitary irreducible representation of
\mathcal{W}
is given by

\displaystyle\Theta^{A,B}\colon\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)

\displaystyle(\Theta^{A,B}(c,(y_{1},y_{2}))f)(x)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i[A(y_{1}+xy_{2})+By_{2}]}f(c+x).

All unitary irreducible representations of this group
\mathcal{W}
are unitarily equivalent, see
[
30
,
32
,
33
,
36
]
for technical details.
We refer to the
[
22
,
37
]
for technical details about applications of the Weyl–Heisenberg group in the construction of Gabor frames.

For
j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, where
N
is some strictly positive number, let
p_{0}^{(j)}
and
q_{0}^{(j)}
be real numbers such that
|p_{0}^{(j)}q_{0}^{(j)}|<1
, and consider the discrete subset of
\mathcal{W}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{q_{0}^{(j)},p_{0}^{(j)}}=\bigg\{\bigg(nq_{0}^{(j)},\bigg(\frac{nlq_{0}^{(j)}p_{0}^{(j)}}{2},lp_{0}^{(j)}\bigg)\bigg)|n,l\in\mathbb{Z}\bigg\}.

For each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, let
\Phi_{j}
be a non-zero function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
(also known as “window function”). For
n,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

\displaystyle\Phi_{(n,l,j)}^{A,B}=\Theta^{A,B}\bigg(nq_{0}^{(j)},\bigg(\frac{nlq_{0}^{(j)}p_{0}^{(j)}}{2},lp_{0}^{(j)}\bigg)\bigg)\Phi_{j},

Then

\displaystyle\Big\{\Phi_{(n,l,j)}^{A,B}(x)\Big\}_{\underset{j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}}

\displaystyle=\Big\{e^{2\pi i[(1/2)Anlp_{0}^{(j)}q_{0}^{(j)}+Blp_{0}^{(j)}]}e^{2\pi iAlp_{0}^{(j)}x}\Phi_{j}(x+nq_{0}^{(j)})\Big\}_{\underset{j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}}

\displaystyle=\Big\{e^{2\pi i[(1/2)Anlp_{0}^{(j)}q_{0}^{(j)}+Blp_{0}^{(j)}]}E_{Alp_{0}^{(j)}}T_{-nq_{0}^{(j)}}\Phi_{j}(x)\Big\}_{\underset{j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}},

which is a Gabor system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
.

Now we are ready to give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of Gabor frames for the Weyl–Heisenberg group.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
be a finite collection of non-zero functions such that support of each
\widehat{\Phi_{j}}
is contained in an interval of length
\lambda
. For each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, let
p_{0}^{(j)}
and
q_{0}^{(j)}
be real numbers such that

|p_{0}^{(j)}q_{0}^{(j)}|<1
and
q_{0}^{(j)}=\frac{1}{\lambda}
. Then,
\big\{\Phi_{(n,l,j)}^{A,B}\big\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z},j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame bounds
\alpha_{o}
,
\beta_{o}
if and only if

\displaystyle\alpha_{o}\leq\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\Phi_{j}}(\gamma-Ap_{0}^{(j)}l)|^{2}\leq\beta_{o}\ \ \text{for almost all}\ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

For the proof of Theorem
3.1
, first we state and prove the following result which gives necessary and sufficient conditions for nonstationary frames of translates in terms of a series of the Fourier transforms of window functions. Frames of translates (or frames for shift-invariant subspaces) first considered in
[
35
]
by Ron and Shen.

Theorem 3.2
.

For each
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\phi_{l}
be a non-zero function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
such that support of each
\widehat{\phi_{l}}
is contained in an interval of length
\lambda
and
q^{(l)}=\frac{1}{\lambda}
. Then,
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a nonstationary frame of translates for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame bounds
\alpha
,
\beta
if and only if

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\leq\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)|^{2}\leq\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\ \ \text{for almost all}\ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Assume first that (
3.1
) holds. Then, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\widehat{f}|\widehat{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\widehat{f}|E_{-nq^{(l)}}\widehat{\phi_{l}}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(\gamma)E_{nq^{(l)}}\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}d\gamma\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(\gamma)e^{2\pi inq^{(l)}\gamma}\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}d\gamma\bigg|^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(\gamma)e^{2\pi in\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}}\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}d\gamma\bigg|^{2}.

Using Theorem
2.8
in (
3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}\bigg|^{2}d\gamma

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}\big|\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}\big|^{2}d\gamma

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}\big|^{2}d\gamma.

Thus, by Ineq. (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}d\gamma\leq\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\leq\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}d\gamma,

or

\displaystyle\alpha\|\hat{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\leq\beta\|\hat{f}\|^{2}.

That is

\displaystyle\alpha\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\leq\beta\|f\|^{2}\ \ \text{for all}\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx).

is a nonstationary frame of translates for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with the desired frame bounds.

To prove the opposite implication, let

be a frame of translates for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame bounds
\alpha,\beta
. Then, using (
3
), for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha\|f\|^{2}\leq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}\big|^{2}d\gamma\leq\beta\|f\|^{2},

which entails

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}d\gamma\leq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\overline{\widehat{\phi_{l}}(\gamma)}\big|^{2}d\gamma\leq\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{f}(\gamma)\big|^{2}d\gamma

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Frames for the Extended Affine Group

The affine group
A
realized as the upper half plane

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\{(\alpha,\beta)\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\beta\in\mathbb{R}\}.

The multiplication law in
A
is given by

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)(x,y)=(\alpha x,\alpha y+\beta)

for all points
(\alpha,\beta)
,
(x,y)\in A
.

Let
V
denote the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
consisting of all the functions whose Fourier transform vanish on the negative half of
\mathbb{R}
.
Recall that for any
d\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
, the map

\displaystyle\rho^{d}\colon A

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(V)

\displaystyle(\rho^{d}(\alpha,\beta)f)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}f\Big(\frac{x+d\beta}{\alpha}\Big),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

The extended affine group, denoted by
EA
, is the direct product of the affine group
A
with the additive group of real numbers. That is,
EA=A\oplus\mathbb{R}
. The product of
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
and
(x,y,z)
in
EA
is given by

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)(x,y,z)=(\alpha x,\alpha y+\beta,\gamma+z).

For
c\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\chi_{c}(x)=e^{icx}
be a unitary character of
\mathbb{R}
. For every
d\in\mathbb{R}^{*},c\in\mathbb{R}
, the unitary irreducible representation of
EA
acting on
V
is the map

\displaystyle\beth^{c,d}=\rho^{d}\otimes\chi_{c}\colon EA

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(V)

\displaystyle(\beth^{c,d}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)f)(x)

\displaystyle=e^{ic\gamma}\frac{1}{\alpha}f\Big(\frac{x+d\beta}{\alpha}\Big).

We now study wavelet frames associated with the extended affine group. We fix a representation
\Theta^{A,B}
of the Weyl–Heisenberg group on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. For
j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, where
N
is some strictly positive number, consider the discrete subset of
EA
that is defined by
EA_{q_{0}^{(j)},p_{0}^{(j)}}=\{(\alpha_{l},\beta_{nl},\gamma_{nl})\colon n,l\in\mathbb{Z}\},
where

\displaystyle\alpha_{l}

\displaystyle=e^{-lq_{0}^{(j)}},

\displaystyle\beta_{nl}

\displaystyle=\frac{-nl(q_{0}^{(j)})^{2}}{d\hskip 1.42262pt\mathrm{ln}\hskip 1.42262pt(\alpha_{l})}=\frac{nq_{0}^{(j)}}{d},

\displaystyle\gamma_{nl}

\displaystyle=\beta_{nl}\frac{\mathrm{ln}(\alpha_{l})}{\alpha_{l}-1}.

For
\Phi_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)\backslash\{0\}
and
j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, define

\displaystyle\Phi_{(n,l,j)}^{c,d}=\beth^{c,d}(\alpha_{l},\beta_{nl},\gamma_{nl})\Phi_{j},

where
n,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\Phi_{(n,l,j)}^{c,d}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{ic\mathrm{ln}(q_{0}^{(j)})^{2}}{d(e^{-lq_{0}^{(j)}}-1)}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{e^{-lq_{0}^{(j)}}}}\Phi_{j}(\frac{x+nq_{0}^{(j)}}{e^{-lq_{0}^{(j)}}})

(4.1)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{ic\mathrm{ln}(q_{0}^{(j)})^{2}}{d(e^{-lq_{0}^{(j)}}-1)}}D_{e^{-lq_{0}^{(j)}}}T_{\frac{-nq_{0}^{(j)}}{e^{-lq_{0}^{(j)}}}}\Phi_{j}(x),\ \text{for}\ n,l\in\mathbb{Z},j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\},

[

3.1

(4.2)

5.
The Canonical Dual of Nonstationary Frames of Translates

Recently, the structure of the canonical dual of different types of frames studied by many authors, see
[
6
,
10
,
34
]
and references therein. Chui and Shi showed in
[
6
]
that the canonical dual of a wavelet frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
need not have a wavelet structure. The authors of
[
10
]
proved that the canonical dual of a discrete wavelet frame for the unitary space
\mathbb{C}^{N}
has the same structure. Further, the canonical dual of a discrete Gabor frame has the same structure, see Proposition 6.1 of
[
34
]
. In this section, we show that the canonical dual of nonstationary frames of translates has the same structure.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a nonstationary frame of translates for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame operator
\mathcal{S}
. Then, the canonical dual frame of
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\mathcal{S}^{-1}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

First we show that frame operator
\mathcal{S}
commutes with translation operator.
For any
n^{\prime},l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\psi_{l^{\prime}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we compute

\displaystyle T_{n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}}\mathcal{S}\psi_{l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=T_{n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}}\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\psi_{l^{\prime}},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\psi_{l^{\prime}},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\psi_{l^{\prime}},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}+nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\psi_{l^{\prime}},T_{nq^{(l)}-n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\psi_{l^{\prime}},T_{-n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle T_{-n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}}\psi_{l^{\prime}},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}T_{-n^{\prime}q^{(l^{\prime})}}\psi_{l^{\prime}}.

Therefore, the frame operator
\mathcal{S}
commutes with translation operator.

Now

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=(T_{nq^{(l)}}^{-1}\mathcal{S})^{-1}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=(T_{-nq^{(l)}}\mathcal{S})^{-1}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}T_{-nq^{(l)}})^{-1}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle=T_{-nq^{(l)}}\mathcal{S}^{-1}\phi_{l},\ n,l\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence, the canonical dual frame of
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
\big\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\mathcal{S}^{-1}\phi_{l}\big\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

is a frame for its span, see
[
4
]
. Then, the family
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{1},l\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}
is a frame for
\mathcal{V}=\text{span}\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{1},l\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{1}=\{-s,\dots,s\},\mathbb{F}_{2}=\{-t,\dots,t\}
for
s,t\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote its frame operator by
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}
. Then,
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}\colon\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l},

and its frame decomposition is given by
f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}\langle f,\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}
,
f\in\mathcal{V}
. Since a nonstationary frames of translates are multivariate frames, it would be interesting to approximate the inverse of frame operator (of a multivariate frame) in some sense.
In this direction, the following result gives a necessary and sufficient condition for approximation of inverse of the frame operator of nonstationary frames of translates in the weak sense.

Theorem 6.1
.

For each
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
q^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Suppose
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a nonstationary frame of translates for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with upper bound
\beta
. Then, for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and for all
n,l\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\big\langle f,\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\big\rangle\rightarrow\big\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\big\rangle\ \text{as}\ s,t\rightarrow\infty,

if and only if, for all
i
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
c_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|T_{iq^{(j)}}\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}\phi_{j}\|=\|\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}\phi_{j}\|\leq c_{i,j}\ \text{for all}\ \ s\geq i,\ t\geq j.

Proof.

Suppose first that
(\ref{s2})
holds. For
i
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
define functions
\psi_{s,t}
as follows.

\displaystyle\psi_{s,t}=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}-\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}.

We need to prove that for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\langle f,\psi_{s,t}\rangle\to 0
as
s,t\to\infty
.

Note that for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}f+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}.

We will use this to obtain an alternative formula for
\psi_{s,t}
.

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\psi_{s,t}=\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}-T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}.

Therefore, using Eq.
(\ref{s3})
on
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\psi_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}-\ T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}.

It follows that for
s\geq i
and
t\geq j
,

\displaystyle\psi_{s,t}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}.

Therefore, for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle|\langle f,\psi_{s,t}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\Big|^{2}

(6.4)

\displaystyle+\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle\Big|^{2}\Big).

Using Cauchy-Schwartz’ inequality in (
6
), we compute

\displaystyle|\langle f,\psi_{s,t}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}|\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j},T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta\|\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}\|^{2}\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2\beta\|T_{iq^{(j)}}\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}\phi_{j}\|^{2}\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2\beta\|\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}\phi_{j}\|^{2}\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,\mathcal{S}^{-1}T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\bigg)

(6.5)

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta c_{i,j}^{2}\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}|\langle\mathcal{S}^{-1}f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\mathcal{S}^{-1}f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\bigg).

Since
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame, we have

(6.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}}|\langle\mathcal{S}^{-1}f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{1}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\mathcal{S}^{-1}f,T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}\to 0\ \text{as}\ s,t\to\infty.

From (
6
) and (
6.6
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\langle f,\psi_{s,t}\rangle\to 0\ \text{as}\ s,t\to\infty

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

To prove the converse, assume that
(\ref{s1})
holds. For any
i
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the functionals

\displaystyle F_{s,t}

\displaystyle\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle F_{s,t}f

\displaystyle=\langle f,\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}\rangle,\quad s\geq i,\ t\geq j.

Each
F_{s,t}
is bounded and linear. By condition
(\ref{s1})
, the family of functionals
\{F_{s,t}\}_{s\geq i,t\geq j}
is pointwise convergent. By invoking the Principal of Uniform Boundedness, the sequence
\{\|F_{s,t}\|\}_{s\geq i,t\geq j}
is bounded. Therefore, there is constant
c_{i,j}>0
such that

\displaystyle c_{i,j}\geq\|F_{s,t}\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}T_{iq^{(j)}}\phi_{j}\|=\|T_{iq^{(j)}}\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}\phi_{j}\|=\|\mathcal{S}_{s,t}^{-1}\phi_{j}\|\ \text{for all}\ s\geq i,\ t\geq j.

This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Linear Independence of Nonstationary Sequences of Translates

In this section, we discuss linear independence of a nonstationary sequence of translates
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, where
q^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
for each
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Christensen and Hasannasab in
[
5
]
proved an equivalent criteria for a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in a separable Hilbert space to be linearly independent in terms of a linear operator on span
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that iterated action of that operator on an element
f_{1}
in
\mathcal{H}
gives the sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. They proved the following result.

Proposition 7.1
.

[
5
, Proposition 2.3]

Consider any sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{H}
for which
span\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is infinite dimensional. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is linearly independent.

(ii)

There exists a linear operator
L\colon\text{span}\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{H}
such that
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}=\{L^{n}f_{1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

Now we give an equivalent criteria of a nonstationary sequence
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
to be linearly independent in terms of a linear operator defined on span
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that iterative action of that operator on
T_{nq^{(1)}}\phi_{1}
gives the whole sequence
T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}
when
\text{span}\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{N}}
is infinite dimensional. This extends Proposition
7.1
to nonstationary sequences of translates in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Suppose
\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a nonstationary sequence of translates in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
such that
\text{span}\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{N}}
is infinite dimensional. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is linearly independent.

(ii)

There exists a linear operator
L\colon\text{span}\{T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
such that

\displaystyle\big\{L^{m,k}T_{q^{(1)}}\phi_{1}\big\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\big\{T_{(m+1)q^{(k+1)}}\phi_{k+1}\big\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

For convenience, write

\phi_{n,l}=T_{nq^{(l)}}\phi_{l}
,
n
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

\eqref{eqi0}\implies\eqref{eqii}
: Assume that
\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is linearly independent. Hence, every subset of
\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is also linearly independent. For a fixed
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

\displaystyle L^{1,0}\phi_{n,l}=\phi_{n+1,l},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Extend
L^{1,0}
to an operator on span
\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
linearly. Then, for a fixed
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(7.1)

\displaystyle\{L^{m,0}\phi_{1,l}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{\phi_{m+1,l}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Similarly, for fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(7.2)

\displaystyle\{L^{0,k}\phi_{n,1}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{\phi_{n,k+1}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Using
(\ref{i1})
and
(\ref{i2})
, there exists a linear operator on span
\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\{L^{m,k}\phi_{1,1}\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{\phi_{m+1,k+1}\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Thus,
(
i
)
implies
(
ii
)
.

\eqref{eqii}\implies\eqref{eqi0}
: By (
ii
) there exists a linear operator
L\colon\text{span}\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
such that

\displaystyle\{L^{m,k}\phi_{1,1}\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{\phi_{m+1,k+1}\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Assume on the contrary that
\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is linearly dependent. Then, there exist
R
,
S>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-R}^{R}\sum_{l=-S}^{S}c_{n,l}\phi_{n,l}=0

for some coefficients
\{c_{n,l}\}_{n\in\{-R,\dots,R\},l\in\{-S,\dots,S\}}
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
c_{R,S}\neq 0
. Then, we can write

(7.3)

\displaystyle\phi_{R,S}=\sum_{n=-R}^{R-1}\sum_{l=-S}^{S-1}c^{\prime}_{n,l}\phi_{n,l}+\sum_{n=-R}^{R-1}c^{\prime}_{n,S}\phi_{n,S}+\sum_{l=-S}^{S-1}c^{\prime}_{R,l}\phi_{R,l}

for some coefficients
c^{\prime}_{n,l}
.

Write

\displaystyle V:=\text{span}\Big[\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n\in\{-R,\dots,R-1\},\atop l\in\{-S,\dots,S-1\}}\bigcup\{\phi_{n,S}\}_{n\in\{-R,\dots,R-1\}}\bigcup\{\phi_{R,l}\}_{l\in\{-S,\dots,S-1\}}\Big].

Then,
\phi_{R,S}\in V
. Now, for any
v\in V
, we have

\displaystyle L^{1,0}v=L^{1,0}\bigg(\sum_{n=-R}^{R-1}\sum_{l=-S}^{S-1}d_{n,l}\phi_{n,l}+\sum_{n=-R}^{R-1}d^{\prime}_{n,S}\phi_{n,S}+\sum_{l=-S}^{S-1}d^{\prime\prime}_{R,l}\phi_{R,l}\bigg)

for some coefficients
d_{n,l},d^{\prime}_{n,S}
and
d^{\prime\prime}_{R,l}
. Therefore, for any
v\in V
, we have

\displaystyle L^{1,0}v=\sum\limits_{n=-R+1}^{R}\sum\limits_{l=-S}^{S-1}d_{n,l}\phi_{n,l}+\sum\limits_{n=-R+1}^{R}d^{\prime}_{n,S}\phi_{n,S}+\sum\limits_{l=-S}^{S-1}d^{\prime\prime}_{R,l}\phi_{R,l},

Thus,
L^{1,0}v\in V
. This implies that
V
is invariant under
L^{1,0}
. Therefore,
L^{m,0}v\in V
for
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Similarly,
L^{0,k}v\in V
for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Now for any
v\in V
and for any
m,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle L^{m,k}v

\displaystyle=L^{m,0}(L^{0,k}v)

\displaystyle=L^{m,0}(v^{\prime})\in V\ \ \big(\text{where}\ v^{\prime}=L^{0,k}v\in V\big).

Thus, (
ii
) implies that
V=\text{span}\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n\in\{-R,\dots,\infty\},\atop l\in\{-S,\dots,\infty\}}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
\{\phi_{n,l}\}_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is linearly independent.
This completes the proof.
∎
